Perverse Sheaves on the Stack of G G -Zips
Christopher Lang

Abstract




1
Introduction

If we have a Shimura variety
{\mathcal{S}}
of abelian type associated to a Shimura datum
(\mathbb{G},X)
, one can look at its reduction
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}
at a prime
p
of good reduction and get a smooth map

\zeta:{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\to G\text{-}\mathrm{Zip}^{\mu},

see
[
SZ22
]
. Here,
G
is the base change to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of an integral model of the adjoint of
\mathbb{G}
, and
\mu
is the reduction of a cocharacter from the Shimura datum
(\mathbb{G},X)
. The idea is that the stack of
G
-zips is easier to study than Shimura varieties. Let’s explain why that is and let’s give a rough definition of
G
-zips in the process.

If the Shimura variety is of PEL type, so that it is the moduli space of abelian varieties with additional structure, the map
\zeta
associates to an abelian variety its first de Rham cohomology, which naturally comes equipped with the Hodge filtration and the conjugate filtration, and we have Frobenius-linear isomorphisms between the graded pieces of the two filtrations. Such an object, namely a vector space with two filtrations and Frobenius-linear isomorphisms of the graded pieces, is called an F-zip. These are objects of linear algebra, and hence easier to understand than Shimura varieties. Now, a
G
-zip is just an F-zip that is equipped with a structure of the group
G
. By that we mean an exact tensor functor from the category of representations of
G
to the category of F-zips. The connected components of the stack of
G
-zips are classified by conjugacy classes of cocharacters, and one such connected component is called
G\text{-}\mathrm{Zip}^{\mu}
, the
G
-zips of type
\mu
.

The fibres of the map
\zeta
are the Ekedahl-Oort strata of the Shimura variety. Furthermore, the stack of
G
-zips of type
\mu
can be identified as a quotient stack
[E_{\mu}\backslash G]
, where
E_{\mu}
is a subgroup of
G\times G
acting on
G
by
(g_{1},g_{2}).g=g_{1}gg_{2}^{-1}
. The underlying topological space of
G\text{-}\mathrm{Zip}^{\mu}
can be explicitly computed using the Weyl group of
G
, and the stabilizers of the quotient stack are semi-direct products of a finite group of Lie type with a connected unipotent group.

The idea is now that if you are interested in some type of object over the Shimura variety, a good starting point can be to look at this type of object over the stack of
G
-zips and then pull it back to the Shimura variety. For example you can do this for the category of perverse sheaves: On the stack of
G
-zips of type
\mu
, these are
E_{\mu}
-equivariant perverse sheaves on
G
, and since the map
\zeta
is smooth, we obtain a shifted pullback functor to the category of perverse sheaves on the Shimura variety.

Perverse Sheaves on
G
-Zips

We compute the simple perverse sheaves on the stack of
G
-zips of type
\mu:\mathbb{G}_{m}\to G
in several examples. It follows from the general theory that these can be computed using the following principle:

Theorem 1

(Theorem
2.3
)
.

Given a connected reductive group
G
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and a cocharacter
\mu:\mathbb{G}_{m,\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\to G_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
, the simple perverse sheaves on the stack of
G
-zips of type
\mu
are given by pairs
(w,\theta)
, where
w
is an orbit of the
E_{\mu}
action on
G
and
\theta
is an irreducible
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
-representation of the group of connected components of the stabilizer of this group action at
w
.

The set of orbits is
{}^{I}W
, where
W
is the Weyl group of
G
and
I
is the type of
\mu
, and the group of connected components of the stabilizer of this group action at
w
is known to be

\Pi_{w}\coloneqq\left\{\,h\in H_{w}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})\ ;\ h=\varphi\big(\dot{y}\dot{w}h(\dot{y}\dot{w})^{-1}\big)\,\right\},

where
H_{w}
is some Levi-subgroup of
G_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
and
y\in G
depends only on
I
. This can be understood as a group over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
together with a twisted Frobenius action, which yields a descent datum to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and the group above are just the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-valued points thereof. Hence one needs to understand the irreducible
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
-representations of certain finite groups of Lie type, and these can in principle be understood with Deligne-Lusztig theory.
We list some examples:

•

If one takes the constant cocharacter, there is only one orbit and the corresponding group is
\Pi_{w}=G(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

•

For
G=\GL_{n}
and the cocharacter that corresponds to a Borel subgroup, the set of orbits is
W=\mathrm{S}_{n}
, the group
H_{w}
is a torus and
\Pi_{w}
is a product of some unit groups of finite fields. In particular, these are abelian groups, so their irreducible representations can be written down explicitly.

•

For the symplectic group
\Sp_{2n}
and the cocharacter that corresponds to the parabolic with two
n\times n
-blocks on the diagonal, the set of orbits is
\{0,1\}^{n}
, and for the closed orbit
w=\id
we have
\Pi_{\id}=\U_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, while for the open orbit we have
\Pi_{w}=\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. The other
\Pi_{w}
we computed for
n=2
, where they are again abelian groups.

•

If we take the general unitary group
G=\GU_{n}
with a cocharacter
\mu
of type
(1,n-1)
, then the set of orbits is totally ordered of size
n
. For each orbit
w
, the group
\Pi_{w}
is an extension of
\GU_{n_{w}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
by some abelian group, where
0\leq n_{w}<n
is an integer depending on
w
.

We look at the stack of
G

G

\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The stack of
G
-zips is a disjoint union of the
G
-zips of type
\mu:\mathbb{G}_{m,\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\to G_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}

G
-zips of type
\mu
is called

and can be written as a quotient stack as follows. Let
\kappa
be the reflex field of the conjugation class of the cocharacter

is defined over
\kappa

. The cocharacter

gives rise to opposite parabolic subgroups

and a Levi subgroup

defined by the condition that
\Lie(P_{-}(\mu))
is the sum of the non-positive weight space of

in
\Lie(G)
(resp. non-negative for
\Lie(P_{+}(\mu)

P=P_{-},\qquad Q=\varphi^{*}(P_{+})\qquad\text{and}\qquad M=\varphi^{*}(L)

with projections
P\to L
and
Q\to M
, both denoted

. We define

E_{\mu}\coloneqq\left\{\,(x,y)\in P\times Q\ ;\ \varphi(\bar{x})=\bar{y}\,\right\},

which acts on

. Then we have

see
[
PWZ15

Unitary Group with Signature
(1,n-1)

Let
q
be a power of a prime
p\not=2
. Unitary groups are defined through hermitian vector spaces, so lets first understand these. We consider the field extension
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
and the non-trivial element of the Galois group
(\varphi:x\mapsto\overline{x}=x^{q})\in\Gal(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Let
V
be a
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
-vector space and
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle:V\times V\to\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
be a non-degenerate hermitian form, i.e. bi-additive and

\left\langle\alpha v,w\right\rangle=\alpha\left\langle v,w\right\rangle=\left\langle v,\overline{\alpha}w\right\rangle,\quad\left\langle v,w\right\rangle=\overline{\left\langle w,v\right\rangle}\quad\text{for all }v,w\in V\text{ and }\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}},

\forall v\in V\,\exists w\in V:\left\langle v,w\right\rangle\not=0.

We call 2 such forms
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{1}
and
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{2}
isomorphic, if there exists
A\in\GL(V)
with
\left\langle v,w\right\rangle_{1}=\left\langle Av,Aw\right\rangle_{2}
for all
v,w\in V
. We want to show that all hermitian forms on
V
are isomorphic, and for this we need the following

Lemma 2.11
.

The multiplicative map
f:\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\to\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}},\,x\mapsto x^{q+1}
satisfies
\im f=\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Proof.

\supseteq
: For
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
we have
f(x)^{q}=x^{q^{2}+q}=x^{1+q}=f(x)
, hence
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

\subseteq
:
The cyclic group
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}\cong\mathbb{Z}/(q^{2}-1)
has a unique subgroup of order
q-1
, namely
\{q+1,2q+2,\dots\}
, and this then has to be the group
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. The map
f
translates on
\mathbb{Z}/(q^{2}-1)
to multiplication with
q+1
, simply because multiplication becomes addition. But from the explicit description it is completely clear that in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}=\{q+1,2q+2,\dots\}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
every element is a multiple of
q+1
.
∎

Lemma 2.12
.

All hermitian forms on the
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
-vector space
V
are isomorphic.

Proof.

Let
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
be a hermitian form on
V
and
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
a basis of
V
. Then the hermitian form is uniquely determined by the matrix
M=\big(\left\langle e_{i},e_{j}\right\rangle\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
, with
\left\langle e_{i},e_{j}\right\rangle=\overline{\left\langle e_{j},e_{i}\right\rangle}
, hence
\overline{M}^{\top}=M
.
We want to transform this hermitian form to the ”standard scalar product”, i.e. find a matrix
A\in\GL(V)
such that
\left\langle Ae_{i},Ae_{j}\right\rangle=\delta_{ij}
.

We proceed in two steps, namely first diagonalize, and then create 1’s everywhere with lemma
2.11
. First we argue that there exists
v\in V
with
\left\langle v,v\right\rangle\not=0
. If we had
\left\langle v,v\right\rangle=0

v\in V

0=\left\langle v+w,v+w\right\rangle=\left\langle v,v\right\rangle+\left\langle w,w\right\rangle+\left\langle v,w\right\rangle+\left\langle w,v\right\rangle=\left\langle v,w\right\rangle+\overline{\left\langle v,w\right\rangle}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\left\langle v,w\right\rangle=-\overline{\left\langle v,w\right\rangle}.

Now we get for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}

\left\langle f_{i},f_{i}\right\rangle
to
1
. For this we take
x_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and look at
f_{i}^{\prime}=x_{i}f_{i}
. We have

\left\langle f_{i}^{\prime},f_{i}^{\prime}\right\rangle=\left\langle x_{i}f_{i},x_{i}f_{i}\right\rangle=x_{i}\cdot\overline{x}_{i}\left\langle f_{i},f_{i}\right\rangle=x_{i}^{q+1}\left\langle f_{i},f_{i}\right\rangle\overset{!}{=}1.

But lemma
2.11
precisely allows us to choose such
x_{i}
. Then the
f_{i}^{\prime}
indeed satisfy
\left\langle f_{i}^{\prime},f_{j}^{\prime}\right\rangle=\delta_{ij}
, and if we define
Ae_{i}\coloneqq f_{i}^{\prime}
, we see that our hermitian form is isomorphic to the ”standard scalar product”, as

\left\langle Ae_{i},Ae_{j}\right\rangle=\delta_{ij}=\left\langle e_{i},e_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{standard}}.\qed

One can also show this lemma using the fact that
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q},\mathrm{U})=1
, where
\mathrm{U}
is some unitary group.

When doing computations with the unitary groups, we want that our tori and Borel subgroups are easy to write down, and hence we fix a basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
of
V
, such that the corresponding matrix
M
has 1’s on the anti-diagonal, and 0’s else. In other words,
\left\langle e_{i},e_{j}\right\rangle=\delta_{(n+1-i),j}
. We extend this form with

\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle:V_{R}\times V_{R}\to\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\underset{\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\otimes}R,\quad\left\langle v\otimes r,w\otimes s\right\rangle=\left\langle v,w\right\rangle\otimes rs,

where
R
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra and
V_{R}=V\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.

Definition 2.13
.

The group of unitary similitudes
\GU(V)
is the algebraic group over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with

\displaystyle\GU(V)(R)

\displaystyle=\left\{\,g\in\GL(V_{R})\ ;\ \exists\,c(g)\in R^{\times}\,\forall\,v,w\in V_{R}:\left\langle gv,gw\right\rangle=c(g)\left\langle v,w\right\rangle\,\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\,g\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}R)\ ;\ \exists\,c(g)\in R^{\times}:g^{\dagger}g=c(g)\mathbbl{1}_{n}\,\right\},

where for a matrix
g\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}R)
we set
(g^{\dagger})_{ij}=\overline{g}_{n+1-j,n+1-i}
(with conjugation acting trivial on
R
).

If we set
\dot{w}_{0}
to be the matrix with 1’s on the antidiagonal, we have
g^{\dagger}=\dot{w}_{0}\overline{g}^{\top}\dot{w}_{0}
.

Proposition 2.14
.

The group
\GU(V)
is a form of
\GL_{n}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}
, more precisely

\GU(V)_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}=\GL_{n,\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\underset{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}{\times}\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}.

Proof.

For this we look at the case where
R
is even an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
-algebra. In this case we have

\displaystyle V_{R}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}R

\displaystyle=(R\times R)^{n}

\displaystyle(x_{i})\otimes r

\displaystyle\mapsto(x_{i}r,\overline{x}_{i}r),

induced by the isomorphism

\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\to\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\times\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}},\quad\lambda\otimes\mu\mapsto(\lambda\mu,\overline{\lambda}\mu).

The crucial point now is that the conjugation
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}R\ni x\otimes r\mapsto\overline{x}\otimes r\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}R
translates to a swap of coordinates
R\times R\ni(r,s)\mapsto(s,r)\in R\times R
.

If we now take a matrix
g\in\GU(V)(R)
with entries
g_{ij}=(a_{ij},b_{ij})\in R\times R
, we have
(g^{\dagger})_{ij}=(b_{n+1-j,n+1-i},a_{n+1-j,n+1-i})
, and the condition
g^{\dagger}g=c(g)\mathbbl{1}_{n}
translates to
B^{\prime}A=c(g)
(with
B^{\prime}=\dot{w}_{0}B^{\top}\dot{w}_{0}
) and
A^{\prime}B=c(g)
. But the last two equations are equivalent and we observe
B=c(g)(A^{\prime})^{-1}
, so it is enough to remember
A
and
c(g)
. In other words, we have a group isomorphism

\displaystyle\GU(V)(R)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}\GL_{n}(R)\times R^{\times}

\displaystyle g=(a_{ij},b_{ij})

\displaystyle\mapsto\big((a_{ij}),c(g)\big).

This is functorial in
R
, hence we get an isomorphism of algebraic groups

\Psi:\GU(V)_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\cong\GL_{n,\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\underset{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}{\times}\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}.\qed

So let’s give a torus and a Borel subgroup of
\GU(V)
. We define

\displaystyle T(R)

\displaystyle=\left\{\,g\in\GU(V)(R)\ ;\ g\text{ is a diagonal matrix}\,\right\}

\displaystyle B(R)

\displaystyle=\left\{\,g\in\GU(V)(R)\ ;\ g\text{ is an upper triangular matrix}\,\right\}

One hint that these might define a maximal torus and a Borel, is that the dagger-operation fixes these subgroups, i.e.
T(R)^{\dagger}\subseteq T(R)
and
B(R)^{\dagger}\subseteq B(R)
: Transposition turns a upper triangular matrix into a lower triangular matrix, inversion fixes lower triangular matrices, and conjugating a lower triangular matrix by
\dot{w}_{0}
turns it back to a upper triangular matrix. When doing the base change to
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, then

and
B
just become the usual diagonal torus and upper triangular Borel (times
\mathbb{G}_{m}
) inside

via

, so they are indeed a torus and Borel in
\GU(V)
.

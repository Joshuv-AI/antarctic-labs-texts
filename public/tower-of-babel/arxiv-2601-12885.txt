From Vertices to Convex Hulls: Certifying Set-Wise Compatibility for CBF Constraints
Shima Sadat Mousavi, Xiao Tan, Aaron D. Ames

Abstract
This paper develops certificates that propagate compatibility of multiple control barrier function (CBF) constraints from sampled vertices to their convex hull. Under mild concavity and affinity assumptions, we present three sufficient feasibility conditions under which feasible inputs over the convex hull can be obtained per coordinate, with a common input, or via convex blending. We also describe the associated computational methods, based on interval intersections or an offline linear program (LP). Beyond certifying compatibility, we give conditions under which the quadratic-program (QP) safety filter is affine in the state. This enables explicit implementations via convex combinations of vertex-feasible inputs. Case studies illustrate the results.



I
Introduction

Optimization-based safety filters built on Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) are widely used to enforce forward invariance while maintaining performance

[
1
]
. In many applications, systems must satisfy multiple state constraints under input bounds. This raises the core question of
compatibility
: does a control input exist that satisfies all constraints at a given state? Pointwise compatibility is easy to check via a convex program (e.g., a QP with hard CBF and input constraints). Region-wide certification is harder and typically requires dense sampling.
This paper develops lightweight, set-wise certificates that propagate compatibility from finitely many sampled states (“vertices”) to all states in their convex hull.

Existing CBF–QP methods are fundamentally
pointwise
: compatibility is checked at the current state and re-evaluated at the next. Canonical formulations appear in
[
1
]
, and higher–relative-degree extensions retain this pointwise nature
[
10
,
3
]
. Recent results give sufficient conditions for pointwise feasibility and regularity:
[
17
]
introduces an auxiliary feasibility constraint that preserves and enlarges the feasible set;
[
7
]
provides feasibility and continuity conditions for multiple CBFs using tangent-cone and polynomial-verification tools.

For multiple CBF constraints under input bounds,
[
13
]
derives pointwise feasibility and robustness margins for bounded-input second-order systems.
Sampling-based methods refine state grids using Lipschitz bounds
[
14
]
. Closed-form QP analyses give compatibility conditions for box-constrained MIMO systems
[
5
]
. SOS- and SDP-based approaches further certify feasibility for polynomial systems

[
12
,
4
,
16
]
,
though often with high computational cost. Overall, these methods enhance pointwise feasibility guarantees but provide limited lightweight
set-wise
certification.

By contrast, we pursue
lightweight, region-wide guarantees
. Using tools from convex analysis
[
2
]
,
we develop
Compatibility Propagation Certificates (CPCs)
that extend vertex-level feasibility of CBF constraints to all states in their convex hull. Each certificate provides a structural condition under which feasibility
propagates
through the region, together with an offline computational check to certify it. Specifically,

•

CPC–Interval.

For box-bounded inputs, this certificate exploits
sign coherence of the control effectiveness matrix in the CBF condition:
intersecting one-dimensional feasible intervals across vertices yields a region-wide feasible input box (Thm.
1
). Its special case, the
Endpoint Rule
, applies when each input channel has a uniform effect, requiring no computation and giving an immediate feasibility test (Cor.
1
).

•

CPC–Common
.
This certificate ensures that a common, state-independent control input is feasible at all vertices,
and hence throughout their convex hull (Thm.
2
).
It is certified by solving a single
Linear Program (LP)
,
yielding a precomputed feasible input for the entire region.

•

CPC–Blend.
This certificate ensures that convex averages of vertex inputs remain feasible for convex combinations of states under a pairwise condition (Thm.
3
). When the control effectiveness matrix in the CBF constraints is state-independent—as in LTI systems—this condition holds automatically, making the convex blend of vertex inputs a region-wide feasible input (Cor.
2
).

The proposed CPCs complement standard online CBF–QP methods by providing offline, region-wide feasibility guarantees over convex hulls. This enables explicit controllers without online optimization. The CPCs form a hierarchy of
sufficient
conditions that balance simplicity and conservatism. Unlike SOS or grid-refinement approaches, which scale poorly with dimension
[
2
,
14
]
, CPCs require only a one-time offline check using interval intersections or small LPs.

We also show that, under mild structural conditions, the safety filter admits an
explicit affine feedback form
. The control law can then be recovered from precomputed optimal inputs at vertices, eliminating the need for online optimization.

Preliminaries

Notation.

We use
\mathbb{R}

\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

The spaces

For

. For

L_{g}h(x)=\nabla h(x)^{\top}g(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times m}
.
For any

, the simplex is
\Delta_{N}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{N}:\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}=1\}
, and the convex hull of points

is

\mathrm{co}\{x^{1},\dots,x^{N}\}=\Big\{\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}x^{j}:\lambda\in\Delta_{N}\Big\}.

Vector inequalities are interpreted componentwise; for a symmetric matrix
A

A\succ 0
denotes positive definiteness.
For a matrix

, the entry in row
i
, column

is

and the

.
The vector
\mathbf{1}_{p}
denotes the

The notation
\mathcal{O}(\cdot)
indicates asymptotic computational complexity.

Consider the control-affine system

\dot{x}=f(x)+g(x)u,\qquad x\in\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n},\;u\in\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m},

(1)

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\!\to\!\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

and the input set

is nonempty, compact, and convex.
Let
\{h_{i}:\mathcal{X}\!\to\!\mathbb{R}\}_{i=1}^{p}

) functions.
Define the superlevel sets

\mathcal{C}_{i}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\ h_{i}(x)\geq 0\},

and the safe set

\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\bigcap_{i=1}^{p}\mathcal{C}_{i}.

Definition 1

A
C^{1}
function
h_{i}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
is a CBF for (
1
) if there exists an extended class-
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
function

where

\forall\,x\in\mathcal{X}\ \ \exists\,u\in\mathcal{U}:\quad L_{f}h_{i}(x)+L_{g}h_{i}(x)\,u\ \geq\ -\alpha_{i}\!\big(h_{i}(x)\big).

In Def.
1
, we use the constrained–input version of CBFs with
u\in\mathcal{U}
(cf.
[
1
]
).
From
[
1
]
, any locally Lipschitz controller
u:x\mapsto u(x)
that satisfies the above inequality pointwise guarantees that
x(t)\in\mathcal{C}_{i}
for all
t
.

For each
i\in\{1,\dots,p\}
, let

\Psi_{i}^{\top}(x)\coloneqq L_{g}h_{i}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times m},\;\delta_{i}(x)\coloneqq L_{f}h_{i}(x)+\alpha_{i}\!\big(h_{i}(x)\big)\in\mathbb{R},

so the

th CBF inequality is
\Psi_{i}^{\top}(x)u+\delta_{i}(x)\geq 0
.
Stacking the

\Psi(x)\,u+\delta(x)\ \geq\ 0,

(2)

with
\Psi(x)\coloneqq[\Psi_{1}(x),\dots,\Psi_{p}(x)]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times m}
and

\delta(x)\coloneqq[\delta_{1}(x),\dots,\delta_{p}(x)]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
.

In order to enforce safety while maintaining performance, a
safety filter
maps each state
x\in\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a desired input
u_{\mathrm{des}}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
to an input that keeps the system safe. A common formulation for the CBF-based safety filter design is

\displaystyle u^{\star}(x)=\arg\min_{u\in\mathcal{U}}

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\|u-u_{\mathrm{des}}(x)\|^{2}

(3)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle\Psi(x)\,u+\delta(x)\ \geq\ 0.

We say the CBF inequalities in (
2
) are
compatible at
x
if there exists
u\in\mathcal{U}
with
\Psi(x)u+\delta(x)\geq 0
.
A natural question is how to certify compatibility over a region
\mathcal{X}
. Def.
1
is per-constraint and does not ensure compatibility of (
2
) at
x
. Compatibility requires a single input
u\in\mathcal{U}
that satisfies all inequalities at
x
simultaneously. In this work, we propose several easy-to-check conditions using convex analysis. We start with two definitions.

Definition 2

A function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is
concave
on a convex set
\mathcal{X}
if

\varphi\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}x^{j}\right)\geq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}\,\varphi(x^{j})

for all
x^{j}\in\mathcal{X}
and all
\lambda\in\Delta_{N}
.
Concavity is
componentwise
for vector-valued functions. If the inequality is reversed,
\varphi
is
convex
on
\mathcal{X}

[
2
]
.

Definition 3

A finite set
\{x^{1},\dots,x^{N}\}
has
vertex compatibility
if for each
j
there exists a
vertex-feasible input

u^{j}\in\mathcal{U}
such that
\Psi(x^{j})u^{j}+\delta(x^{j})\geq 0
. We call an input
u\in\mathcal{U}

feasible
at
x
if it satisfies the stacked CBF
inequalities (
2
).

We seek to address the following problem.

Problem 1

Given
\Psi(x),\delta(x)
from (
2
), a convex input set
\mathcal{U}
, and vertices
\{x^{1},\dots,x^{N}\}
with vertex compatibility, provide conditions ensuring compatibility on the hull

H\coloneqq\operatorname{co}\{x^{1},\dots,x^{N}\}
; i.e., for every
x\in H
there exists
u(x)\in\mathcal{U}
that satisfies (
2
).

III
Compatibility over Convex Hulls

This section gives conditions under which compatibility at finitely many
vertices
extends to the full convex hull. Each result targets a different structural case.

Assumption 1

Throughout, unless stated otherwise:

(A1)

Vertex compatibility (with input constraints): for each
j=1,\dots,N
there exists a vertex input
u^{j}\in\mathcal{U}
such that

\Psi(x^{j})u^{j}+\delta(x^{j})\geq 0
.

(A2)

Concave drift on
H
: The map
\delta:H\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
is concave.

(A3)

Columnwise curvature alignment:
for each column
k\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, the column map
\Psi_{:,k}(\cdot):H\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
is concave, convex, or affine on
H
.

Under (A3), define for each input coordinate
k
:

\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Psi)\triangleq\begin{cases}[0,\infty),&\text{if }\Psi_{:,k}(\cdot)\text{ is concave on }H,\\[2.0pt]
(-\infty,0],&\text{if }\Psi_{:,k}(\cdot)\text{ is convex on }H,\\[2.0pt]
\mathbb{R},&\text{if }\Psi_{:,k}(\cdot)\text{ is affine on }H.\end{cases}

(4)

The
sign-aligned cone
is then

\mathcal{S}(\Psi)\ \triangleq\ \{\,u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\ u_{k}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Psi)\ \text{for all }k\,\}.

(5)

Remark 1

If
\Psi
is concave (resp. convex) on
H
,
\mathcal{S}(\Psi)=\{u\geq 0\}
(resp.
\{u\leq 0\}
).
If
\Psi
is affine or constant on
H
, then
\mathcal{S}(\Psi)=\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
In particular, for an LTI plant
\dot{x}=Ax+Bu
with affine CBFs
h_{i}(x)=a_{i}^{\top}x+b_{i}
,
one has
L_{g}h_{i}(x)=a_{i}^{\top}B
that is constant on
H
.
Hence
\Psi
is constant, and thus
\mathcal{S}(\Psi)=\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Lemma 1

Under
(A3)
, for any
u\in\mathcal{S}(\Psi)
the map
\phi(x)\triangleq\Psi(x)u
is concave on
H
.

Proof:

Each component is a sum of column maps weighted by
u_{k}
, where concave columns carry nonnegative weights, convex columns carry nonpositive weights, and affine columns carry arbitrary weights; (nonnegative)
\times
concave
+
(nonpositive)
\times
convex
+
affine is concave on
H
.
∎

Remark 2

Our results require only a concave
lower bound
on
\Psi(x)u+\delta(x)
over the hull.
Under (A3),
u\in\mathcal{S}(\Psi)
ensures that
x\mapsto\Psi(x)u
is concave. Thus, (A2) can be replaced by a concave lower bound
\underline{\delta}(\cdot)
with
\underline{\delta}(x)\leq\delta(x)
for all
x\in H
.

The following example shows that vertex compatibility alone does not guarantee hull compatibility. This motivates the CPC conditions developed next.

Example 1

Consider the one-dimensional case

\Psi(x)=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}-(x-4)^{2}\\[1.0pt]
1\end{smallmatrix}\right],\qquad\delta(x)=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}-x+10\\[1.0pt]
-x\end{smallmatrix}\right],\qquad\mathcal{U}=[0,10],

with vertices
x^{1}=0
,
x^{2}=3
, so
H=[0,3]
.
Assump.
1
holds with vertex-feasible inputs
u^{1}=0.5
and
u^{2}=3
.
However, the condition
\Psi(1.5)u+\delta(1.5)\geq 0
is infeasible, showing that compatibility fails inside
H
.

III-A

Certificate I: CPC–Interval

We begin with the fastest, optimization-free certificate for box inputs: the
CPC–Interval
. It constructs one-dimensional feasible
intervals
for each input coordinate
from the vertex constraints.
Intersecting these intervals yields an input box that is feasible for all states
in the convex hull.

Assume a set of box inputs
\mathcal{U}=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:u_{k,\min}\leq u_{k}\leq u_{k,\max}\}
and known
vertex-feasible inputs
\{u^{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}\subset\mathcal{U}
.
Fix an input coordinate
k
.
At each vertex
x^{j}
, the column
\Psi_{:,k}(x^{j})
captures how
u_{k}
affects the
p
CBF constraints.
Assume this column is
sign-coherent
across the
vertices
: it is entrywise nonnegative or entrywise nonpositive at every vertex.

Define

\mathcal{P}_{k}=\{j:\Psi_{:,k}(x^{j})\geq 0\},\qquad\mathcal{N}_{k}=\{j:\Psi_{:,k}(x^{j})\leq 0\},

which collect, respectively, the vertices where the
k
-th input has a positive or negative influence on the CBF constraints. Using the vertex-feasible inputs
u^{j}
, set

L_{k}=\max_{j\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}(u^{j})_{k},\qquad U_{k}=\min_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{k}}(u^{j})_{k},

with
L_{k}=-\infty
if
\mathcal{P}_{k}=\emptyset
and
U_{k}=+\infty
if
\mathcal{N}_{k}=\emptyset
.
Here,
L_{k}
is the largest input value valid for all “positive” vertices in
\mathcal{P}_{k}
, and

U_{k}
is the smallest input value valid for all “negative” vertices in
\mathcal{N}_{k}
.
The resulting feasible interval of the
k
-th input that satisfies both (
2
) and actuator limits is then

I_{k}=[\,\max\{L_{k},u_{k,\min}\},\ \min\{U_{k},u_{k,\max}\}\,].

To ensure that the selected inputs respect the curvature of
\Psi
,
each coordinate interval
I_{k}
is refined by intersecting it with the corresponding
sign-aligned set
\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Psi)
defined in (
4
):

\tilde{I}_{k}=I_{k}\cap\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Psi).

(6)

This step narrows each input’s feasible interval to the values that match the curvature direction of the CBF constraints. If any
\tilde{I}_{k}
is empty, the CPC–Interval test fails; otherwise, any
u
with
u_{k}\in\tilde{I}_{k}
for all
k
guarantees compatibility over the hull.

Theorem 1

If the intervals
\tilde{I}_{k}
are nonempty for all
k
, then any
u
with
u_{k}\in\tilde{I}_{k}

satisfies (
2
) for all
x\in H
.

Proof:

Fix any
u
with
u_{k}\in\tilde{I}_{k}
for all
k
, and let
x=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}x^{j}\in H
with
\lambda\in\Delta_{N}
.
By curvature alignment,
x\mapsto\Psi(x)u
is concave (Lem.
1
);
x\mapsto\delta(x)
is concave as well, so

\Psi(x)u+\delta(x)\ \geq\ \sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}\big(\Psi(x^{j})u+\delta(x^{j})\big).

It suffices to show
\Psi(x^{j})u+\delta(x^{j})\geq 0
for each vertex
x^{j}
.
For any
j
and
k
: if
j\in\mathcal{P}_{k}
, then
u_{k}\geq L_{k}\geq(u^{j})_{k}
and
\Psi_{:,k}(x^{j})\geq 0
give

\Psi_{:,k}(x^{j})u_{k}\geq\Psi_{:,k}(x^{j})(u^{j})_{k}
; if
j\in\mathcal{N}_{k}
, then
u_{k}\leq U_{k}\leq(u^{j})_{k}
and

\Psi_{:,k}(x^{j})\leq 0
give the same inequality. Summing over
k
yields

\Psi(x^{j})u\geq\Psi(x^{j})u^{j}
, and feasibility of
u^{j}
implies

\Psi(x^{j})u+\delta(x^{j})\geq 0
. Thus, by concavity,
\Psi(x)\,u+\delta(x)\ \geq\ 0
, for all
x\in H
.
∎

Thm.
1
propagates vertex compatibility to the whole hull
when all coordinate intervals
\tilde{I}_{k}
are nonempty.
When vertex-feasible inputs
\{u^{j}\}
are unavailable,
a simple alternative is to construct them directly from the column signs of
\Psi
.
This yields the
Endpoint Rule
.

Corollary 1

Assume: (i) each column of
\Psi
has a uniform sign over
H
;
(ii)
\mathcal{U}=\prod_{k=1}^{m}[u_{k,\min},u_{k,\max}]
; and
(iii)
E_{k}:=[u_{k,\min},u_{k,\max}]\cap\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Psi)\neq\emptyset
for all
k
.
Define

u_{k}\;=\;\begin{cases}\sup E_{k},&\Psi_{:,k}(x)\geq 0\ \ \forall x\in H,\\[2.0pt]
\inf E_{k},&\Psi_{:,k}(x)\leq 0\ \ \forall x\in H,\end{cases}\quad k=1,\dots,m,

and set
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{m})
. Then
u\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{S}(\Psi)
, and
u
satisfies
(
2
) for all
x\in H
.
If any
E_{k}=\emptyset
, the test is inconclusive.

Proof:

Under (A2)–(A3) and uniform sign over
H
, Thm.
1

gives per–coordinate feasible intervals

I_{k}=[\max\{L_{k},u_{k,\min}\},\,\min\{U_{k},u_{k,\max}\}]
with

U_{k}=+\infty
if
\Psi_{:,k}\!\geq 0
on
H
and
L_{k}=-\infty
if
\Psi_{:,k}\!\leq 0
.
Let
\tilde{I}_{k}=I_{k}\cap\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Psi)
and

E_{k}=[u_{k,\min},u_{k,\max}]\cap\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Psi)\neq\emptyset
.
If
\Psi_{:,k}\!\geq 0
on
H
, choosing
u_{k}=\sup E_{k}
ensures

u_{k}\in E_{k}\subseteq\tilde{I}_{k}
, hence
u_{k}\in\tilde{I}_{k}
;
if
\Psi_{:,k}\!\leq 0
, the same holds with
u_{k}=\inf E_{k}
.
Thus
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{m})\in\prod_{k}\tilde{I}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{S}(\Psi)
,
and by Thm.
1
,
\Psi(x)u+\delta(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in H
.
∎

The Endpoint Rule represents a conservative instance of CPC–Interval,
requiring no optimization or vertex-feasible inputs.
It is especially useful for quick compatibility checks when the column signs of
\Psi
are sign-coherent across a region.

III-B

Certificate II: CPC–Common

The CPC–Common certificate checks whether one
common
input can satisfy all vertex constraints. Rather than constructing per-vertex inputs, we solve one LP to find
u^{\star}\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{S}(\Psi)
satisfying the CBF constraints at all vertices. If the LP is feasible, the same
u^{\star}
satisfies the CBF constraints for every
x\in H
by (A2) and Lem.
1
.

Theorem 2

If there exists a common
u\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{S}(\Psi)
with
\Psi(x^{j})u+\delta(x^{j})\geq 0
for all vertices
x^{j}
,
u
satisfies (
2
) for all
x\in H
.

Proof:

Let
x=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}x^{j}
. By Lem.
1
,
x\mapsto\Psi(x)u
is concave;
\delta(\cdot)
is concave by Assump.
1
. Hence

\Psi(x)u+\delta(x)\ \geq\ \sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\big(\Psi(x^{j})u+\delta(x^{j})\big)\ \geq\ 0.

∎

To apply CPC–Common, we solve a single LP using the vertex data

\{\Psi(x^{j}),\delta(x^{j})\}_{j=1}^{N}
.
The LP searches for an input
u\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{S}(\Psi)
that maximizes
a common vertex margin:

\displaystyle u^{\star},\,t^{\star}

\displaystyle=\arg\max_{u,\,t}\ t

(7)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle\Psi(x^{j})u+\delta(x^{j})\ \geq\ t\,\mathbf{1}_{p},\qquad j=1,\dots,N,

\displaystyle u\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{S}(\Psi).

Let
(u^{\star},t^{\star})
denote the optimizer of (
7
).
If
t^{\star}\geq 0
, then
u^{\star}
satisfies (
2
) for all
x\in H
.

Remark 3

LP (
7
) has
m{+}1
variables (
m
inputs) and
pN
vertex constraints
(from
p
CBF inequalities at
N
vertices).
It also includes the input–set constraints (e.g.,
q
rows if
\mathcal{U}=\{u:Gu\leq b\}
with
G\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times m},\ b\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
)
and at most
m
sign constraints from
\mathcal{S}(\Psi)
.
A dense
interior-point step costs

\mathcal{O}\!\big((m{+}1)^{3}\big)+\mathcal{O}\!\big((pN{+}q{+}m)(m{+}1)^{2}\big)

per iteration
[
2
]
.
The number of iterations is
\mathcal{O}\!\big(\sqrt{pN{+}q{+}m}\,\log(1/\varepsilon)\big)
, where
\varepsilon
denotes
how close the returned solution is to the true optimum
[
2
]
. In practice, only a few tens of iterations are required. The LP is solved once offline; no per-state online optimization is needed.

III-C

Certificate III: CPC–Blend

If no common input is feasible at all vertices (i.e., LP (
7
) is infeasible), we use
CPC–Blend
. This certificate guarantees that convex blending of vertex states and their vertex-feasible inputs preserves constraint compatibility.

Theorem 3

Let
\{u^{j}\}\subseteq\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{S}(\Psi)
satisfy
\Psi(x^{j})u^{j}+\delta(x^{j})\geq 0
at all vertices.
If

\big(\Psi(x^{i})-\Psi(x^{j})\big)\big(u^{i}-u^{j}\big)\ \leq\ 0\qquad\text{for all }\,i,j,

(8)

then for any
x=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}x^{j}
with
\lambda\in\Delta_{N}
, the convex blend

u_{\lambda}=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}u^{j}
satisfies (
2
).

Proof:

Let
A^{(j)}:=\Psi(x^{j})
and
S:=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}A^{(j)}
.
Since
\mathcal{U}
is convex and
\mathcal{S}(\Psi)
is a cone,
u_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{S}(\Psi)
.
By Lem.
1
,
\Psi(\cdot)u_{\lambda}
is concave, so

\Psi(x)u_{\lambda}\ \geq\ \sum_{j}\lambda_{j}A^{(j)}u_{\lambda}\ =\ S\,u_{\lambda}.

A symmetric expansion gives

S\,u_{\lambda}-\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}A^{(j)}u^{j}=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\big(A^{(i)}-A^{(j)}\big)\big(u^{j}-u^{i}\big),

that is nonnegative by (
8
). Hence
S\,u_{\lambda}\geq\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}A^{(j)}u^{j}
.
Concavity of
\delta
yields
\delta(x)\geq\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\delta(x^{j})
; therefore,

\Psi(x)u_{\lambda}+\delta(x)\ \geq\ \sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\big(A^{(j)}u^{j}+\delta(x^{j})\big)\ \geq\ 0,

using vertex compatibility. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4

The CPC–Blend certificate requires verifying the pairwise condition (
8

\{u^{j}\}
are available.
These inputs can be obtained in two ways:

(i)
Per-vertex approach:
Solve
N
small LPs (one per vertex) to find feasible
\{u^{j}\}
, then apply the pairwise check (
8
).
This approach is simple and parallelizable but may fail when the resulting
\{u^{j}\}
violate (
8
).

(ii)
Joint LP approach:
Solve one combined LP that searches for all

simultaneously while enforcing both vertex feasibility and pairwise condition (
8
):

\displaystyle\{u^{j\star}\},\,t^{\star}

\displaystyle=\arg\max_{\{u^{j}\},\,t}\ t

(9)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle\Psi(x^{j})u^{j}+\delta(x^{j})\ \geq\ t\,\mathbf{1}_{p},\quad j=1,\dots,N,

\displaystyle\big(\Psi(x^{i})-\Psi(x^{j})\big)\big(u^{i}-u^{j}\big)\ \leq\ 0,\quad\forall\,i<j,

\displaystyle u^{j}\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{S}(\Psi),\quad j=1,\dots,N.

Let
t^{\star}
be its optimal value when (
9
) is feasible.
If
t^{\star}\geq 0
, then (A1) holds at all vertices with margin
t^{\star}
, and (
8
) holds as well.
Thm.
3
then gives compatibility over
H
.
If (
9
) is infeasible, or if

, the test is inconclusive.

The joint LP (
9
) offers a
single-shot certificate
.
It is computationally heavier than the per-vertex approach, but it avoids failures from vertex inputs that are feasible individually yet violate (
8
).
In practice, the per-vertex method is preferable for large

or parallel setups, while the joint LP is better suited for moderate

or when a global certificate is desired.

Complexity.

The pairwise check costs
O(N^{2}pm)
(

m

The per-vertex approach involves

By contrast, the joint LP has
Nm{+}1

constraints.
Although both are polynomial in size, the latter is heavier in a single solve. Both are tractable and executed once offline, incurring no online computational cost.

III-D

Affine Interpolation of the Safety Filter

In many applications, the safety filter is evaluated repeatedly along trajectories in
H
.
If
\Psi(x)
,
\delta(x)
, and
u_{\mathrm{des}}(x)
are affine on
H
, in general, the safety-filter optimizer

u^{\star}(\cdot)
is piecewise affine.
We next give conditions under which
u^{\star}(\cdot)
on
H
is equal to the affine
interpolation of the optimizer values
u^{\star}(x^{j})
at the vertices.

Let the input set be a polytope

\mathcal{U}=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\ Gu\leq b\},\qquad G\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times m},\ b\in\mathbb{R}^{q}

(e.g., a box with
G=\begin{bmatrix}I\\
-I\end{bmatrix}
,

b=\begin{bmatrix}u_{\max}\\
-u_{\min}\end{bmatrix}
).
For a given state
x
, let
\mathcal{A}(x)\subseteq\{1,\dots,p\}
denote the
CBF rows active at the optimal control
u^{\star}(x)
in (
3
) and

\mathcal{B}(x)\subseteq\{1,\dots,q\}
the active input bounds:

\Psi_{i}(x)u^{\star}(x)+\delta_{i}(x)=0\ (i\in\mathcal{A}),\qquad G_{\ell}u^{\star}(x)=b_{\ell}\ (\ell\in\mathcal{B}).

Let
\Psi_{\mathcal{A}}(x)
,
\delta_{\mathcal{A}}(x)
collect the active CBF rows,
and
G_{\mathcal{B}},b_{\mathcal{B}}
the active input rows.
A
critical region

\mathcal{C}
is a set of states where
(i) the active sets
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
remain constant,
(ii) the Linear Independence Constraint Qualification (LICQ) holds
(i.e., the rows of
\big[\Psi_{\mathcal{A}}(x);\ G_{\mathcal{B}}\big]
are linearly independent), and
(iii) strict complementarity holds (i.e., all active multipliers are positive) (see, e.g.,
[
15
]
).

Assumption 2

On
H=\mathrm{co}\{x^{1},\dots,x^{N}\}
, the problem data are constant or affine in
x
:
(i)
\Psi(x)\equiv\bar{\Psi}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times m}
;
(ii)
\delta(x)=\bar{\delta}_{0}+\bar{\Delta}x
, and
u_{\mathrm{des}}(x)
is affine in
x
, i.e.,

u_{\mathrm{des}}(x)=\bar{u}_{0}+\bar{U}x
;
(iii)
\mathcal{U}=\{u:Gu\leq b\}
.
Here, barred symbols (
\bar{\Psi},\bar{\delta}_{0},\bar{\Delta},\bar{u}_{0},\bar{U}
) denote constant matrices or vectors of appropriate dimensions defining the constant/affine maps.

Theorem 4

Under Assump.
2
, let
\mathcal{R}\subseteq H
be a convex critical region. Then,
u^{\star}(\cdot)
in (
3
) is affine on
\mathcal{R}
; in particular, for any
x=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}x^{j}\in\mathcal{R}
with
\{x^{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}\subset\mathcal{R}
,
\lambda\in\Delta_{N}
,

u^{\star}(x)=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\,u^{\star}(x^{j}).

(10)

Proof:

Under Assump.
2
(so
\Psi(x)\equiv\bar{\Psi}
on
H
), the
Karush–Kuhn–Tucker conditions of (
3
) on
\mathcal{R}
are

u-u_{\mathrm{des}}(x)+\bar{\Psi}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\!\top}\lambda_{\mathcal{A}}+G_{\mathcal{B}}^{\!\top}\nu_{\mathcal{B}}=0,\;\bar{\Psi}_{\mathcal{A}}\,u+\delta_{\mathcal{A}}(x)=0,\;G_{\mathcal{B}}u=b_{\mathcal{B}},

with multipliers
\lambda_{\mathcal{A}},\nu_{\mathcal{B}}>0
.
Equivalently,

\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}I&\bar{\Psi}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\!\top}&G_{\mathcal{B}}^{\!\top}\\
\bar{\Psi}_{\mathcal{A}}&0&0\\
G_{\mathcal{B}}&0&0\end{bmatrix}}_{K\ \text{constant on }\mathcal{R}}\!\begin{bmatrix}u\\
\lambda_{\mathcal{A}}\\
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\,u_{\mathrm{des}}(x)\\
-\delta_{\mathcal{A}}(x)\\
b_{\mathcal{B}}\end{bmatrix}.

The right-hand side is affine in
x
. With LICQ,
K
is nonsingular, so

(u,\lambda_{\mathcal{A}},\nu_{\mathcal{B}})=K^{-1}(\cdot)
is affine in
x
.
Therefore,
u^{\star}(\cdot)
is affine on
\mathcal{R}
, and (
10
) follows.
∎

Corollary 3

Under Assump.
2
,
if (i) all vertices
\{x^{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}\subset H
solve (
3
) with a common active set
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
satisfying LICQ,
and (ii) every inactive constraint remains strictly inactive on
H
, i.e.,

\Psi_{i}(x)u^{\star}(x)+\delta_{i}(x)>0\ \ (i\notin\mathcal{A}),\;b_{\ell}-G_{\ell}u^{\star}(x)>0\ \ (\ell\notin\mathcal{B}),

then
H
is a single critical region and the optimal control
u^{\star}(\cdot)
is affine on
H
.

Proof:

Since the constraint residuals

\Psi_{i}(x)u^{\star}(x)+\delta_{i}(x)
and
b_{\ell}-G_{\ell}u^{\star}(x)

are affine in
x
, strict positivity at the vertices implies strict positivity
throughout
H
, ensuring that no constraint changes its activity within
H
. Hence the active set
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
remains constant, so

H
constitutes one critical region. The results thus follow from Thm.
4
.
∎

Under Assump.
2
, the set
H
admits a finite
polyhedral partition into
critical regions
. On each region,

u^{\star}(\cdot)
is affine (Thm.
4
).
LICQ and strict complementarity ensure continuity across shared boundaries.
Hence
u^{\star}
, being piecewise affine on compact
H
, is globally Lipschitz.

V
Conclusion

We proposed three
Compatibility Propagation Certificates (CPCs)
that give lightweight, set-wise guarantees for extending vertex compatibility of CBF constraints to their convex hull. We also showed that under mild structural conditions, the safety filter admits an explicit affine form, enabling convex-combination evaluation without online optimization. In practice, vertices can be chosen to capture critical operating conditions, and larger domains can be decomposed into multiple overlapping convex hulls. Systematic vertex selection and hull construction are key directions for future work.

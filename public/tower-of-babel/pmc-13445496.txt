Trends of Mortality and Years of Life Lost Due to Stroke Among Older Adults in China and Its Provinces, 2005–2020
Authors: Zixin Wang, Zheng Long, Jiamin Li, Yun Chen, Boyi Yuan, Wan Wang, Jiameng Li, Xinyi Wang, Jinming Sheng, Jiapeng Zhao, Lijun Wang, Maigeng Zhou, Peng Yin, Qingfeng Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesTo evaluate trends in stroke-related mortality and years of life lost (YLL) among older adults in China during 2005–2020.MethodsLeveraging data from National Mortality Surveillance System, we quantified counts and age-standardized rates for mortality and YLL attributable to stroke and its subtypes among adults aged 65 years and older in China and its provinces from 2005 to 2020.ResultsDuring 2005–2020, age-standardized mortality rate (ASMR) of stroke among older adults in China decreased from 1,327.30 to 1,000.69 per 100,000, accompanied by a 26.58% reduction in YLL rate. Specifically, ASMRs decreased by 48.08% for intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) and 7.57% for subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH), whereas that for ischemic stroke (IS) increased by 1.24%. In 2020, ASMRs for IS, ICH, and SAH were 610.52, 357.86, and 32.32 per 100,000, respectively, with IS exhibiting the highest rate. Notably, IS mortality increased among men aged 75–79 years (1.95%), men aged 80+ years (17.79%), and women aged 80+ years (5.02%). In 2020, the highest ASMR of stroke was observed in Tibet, Anhui, and Shaanxi.DiscussionThe substantial burden of IS persists among older adults in China. Increased IS mortality among men aged 75+ years and women aged 80+ years warrants particular attention.

Introduction

In China, approximately 67.2% of stroke-related disability-adjusted life years occur in the population aged 65 years and above.1 China has been experiencing a progressively intensifying aging trend, with its population aged 65 years and above reaching 220.23 million by the end of 2024.2 Elderly stroke patients have more comorbidities, complications, and poorer long-term outcomes, making stroke a major contributor to mortality and disability in this group.3 Therefore, a systematic analysis of stroke burden among the elderly in China is critical for targeted prevention and control strategies. However, existing studies have largely failed to differentiate stroke subtypes or provide province-level death burden, limiting a comprehensive understanding of this burden.3,4

Therefore, we systematically evaluated the mortality and years of life lost (YLL) due to stroke and its subtypes among the population aged 65 years and above in China and its provinces from 2005 to 2020, leveraging data from the National Mortality Surveillance System (NMSS).

Methods

Data Sources

Mortality data of stroke were obtained from NMSS, which provides high-quality, nationally, and provincially representative data by continuously monitoring over 300 million individuals from 605 surveillance points across all 31 mainland provinces. This system functions as a key government instrument, providing benchmark metrics for mortality that guide national health strategies.5 The crude mortality rate was adjusted by leveraging underreporting rates ascertained through retrospective field surveys within the NMSS.6 Further details on NMSS are provided in Supplementary Material (eMethods). Population data for the surveillance points were provided by China National Bureau of Statistics, and Socio-Demographic Index (SDI) was sourced from Global Burden of Disease Study 2021.

Mortality and YLL Estimation

The detailed workflow of stroke mortality estimation is shown in eFigure 1. In brief, all-cause mortality and stratum-specific mortality rates (by location, sex, and age group) were determined annually from 2005 to 2020. Stroke-related fatalities were identified according to International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision, specifically encompassing all deaths where the underlying cause was attributed to stroke or its subtypes under these code ranges: stroke (I60-I63.9, I65-I67.9, I69-I69.8), intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH: I61-I62.9, I69.1-I69.2), ischemic stroke (IS: I63-I63.9, I65-I66.9, I67.2-I67.3, I67.5-I67.6, I69.3), and subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH: I60-I60.9, I69.0). Stroke mortality rates were then computed by applying these cause-specific fractions to the all-cause mortality estimates. Further details are provided in Supplementary Material (eMethods). YLLs were estimated by multiplying the number of stroke deaths at each age by the standard remaining life expectancy from a theoretical minimum risk reference life table.7

Statistical Analysis

Deaths, YLLs, and age-standardized mortality and YLL rates (standardized to China's Sixth National Census) among older adults in China during 2005–2020 were analyzed. These metrics were analyzed by temporal trends, stroke subtype, sex, age group (i.e., 65–69, 70–74, 75–79, 80+ years), and province. The correlation between provincial-level age-standardized mortality rate (ASMR) of stroke and SDI was examined using locally weighted regression. All analyses were performed using SAS 9.4.

Standard Protocol Approvals, Registrations, and Patient Consents

This study approved by National Center for Chronic and Noncommunicable Disease Control and Prevention, Chinese Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (No. 201804). Informed consent was waived.

Data Availability

Data are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request.

Results

From 2005 to 2020, ASMRs of stroke among older adults in China decreased from 1,327.30/100,000 to 1,000.69/100,000. Specifically, ASMRs decreased by 48.08% for ICH and 7.57% for SAH, whereas that of IS increased by 1.24%. In 2020, ASMRs for IS, ICH, and SAH were 610.52, 357.86, and 32.32 per 100,000, respectively, with IS exhibiting the highest rate (Figure 1A). During 2005–2020, ASMRs due to stroke and its subtypes were consistently higher in men than in women. Notably, ASMRs for IS increased by 6.70% in men, while a decrease of 4.06% was observed in women (Figure 1B). Furthermore, age-standardized YLL rates due to stroke and its subtypes showed similar trends (eTable 1).

In both 2005 and 2020, stroke mortality rates increased with advancing age, peaking in the 80+ age group (eFigure 2). For each stroke subtype, mortality rates declined across most age groups during 2005–2020. However, a significant exception was observed for IS, with an overall increase of 10.66% in mortality rate among individuals aged 80+ years. Provincial differences in stroke mortality among older adults are shown in Figure 2. In 2020, the highest ASMR of stroke was recorded in Tibet (1,799.18/100,000), Anhui (1,375.21/100,000), and Shaanxi (1,365.23/100,000). For ICH, the ASMR was elevated in western provinces, with the highest rates in Tibet (1,013.64/100,000) and Qinghai (742.70/100,000). The highest ASMR for IS was seen in Heilongjiang (944.54/100,000) and Shaanxi (851.08/100,000), while that for SAH was highest in Tibet (456.54/100,000) and Hebei (64.89/100,000). Similar provincial distribution patterns were also observed for age-standardized YLL rates due to stroke and its subtypes (eTable 2). Furthermore, a negative correlation was observed between ASMRs of stroke and SDI from 2005 to 2020, with provinces that had a higher SDI exhibiting lower stroke mortality (eFigure 3). Year-specific analyses confirmed a negative correlation between ASMRs of stroke and SDI in both 2005 and 2020 (eFigure 4).

Discussion

In China, the death burden of IS among older adults remained persistently high, particularly showing an upward trend in men aged 75+ years and women aged 80+ years. Furthermore, significant provincial disparities in stroke mortality among older adults were observed.

The high IS burden observed among older adults, consistent with previous studies,3,8 may be attributable to several factors. First, population aging has intensified, with advanced age itself being a nonmodifiable risk factor, and elderly patients often present with multiple comorbidities and poor prognosis.2,8 Second, traditional risk factors such as hypertension and diabetes remain highly prevalent yet suboptimally controlled,9 and hypotension in older adults may reduce cerebral perfusion and trigger IS. Third, elderly patients frequently miss the acute-phase therapeutic window due to living alone, insufficient stroke recognition, and transport delays.10,11 Fourth, lack of professional rehabilitation and inadequate family care after discharge lead to low medication adherence, poor functional recovery, and a higher risk of complications and recurrence.12,13 Notably, IS mortality increased among men aged 75+ and women aged 80+ years, indicating the very elderly stage as a critical intervention window. The public health importance of this trend is amplified by increasing life expectancy, which has expanded the very old population, thereby increasing the absolute number of elderly individuals at risk.2 Thus, an integrated, multidisciplinary stroke care system targeting the very elderly is urgently needed, covering prehospital screening, rapid transport, standardized acute treatment, and long-term rehabilitation, to reduce stroke burden in this population.

Across provinces, the highest mortality for total and hemorrhagic stroke among the elderly occurred in Tibet, while Heilongjiang and Shaanxi exhibited the highest IS ASMRs—disparities that may be associated with regional differences in socioeconomic status, healthcare resources, and risk factor distribution.14,15

Several limitations should be considered. First, lack of concurrent data on risk factor trends and care quality precludes identification of specific determinants. Second, interprovincial differences in healthcare surveillance, mortality reporting, and record completeness—as well as urban-rural disparities in healthcare access—may affect observed geographic patterns. Third, including only stroke as the underlying cause of death may underestimate stroke mortality.

In conclusion, IS continues to pose a substantial public health challenge among older adults in China. Increased IS mortality among men aged 75+ years and women aged 80+ years warrants particular attention. Targeted strategies are essential to mitigate stroke burden in this aging population.

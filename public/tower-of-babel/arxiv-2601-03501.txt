The strong topological Rokhlin property and Medvedev degrees of SFTs
Nicanor Carrasco-Vargas

Abstract
. We prove that if a recursively presented group admits a (nonempty) subshift of finite type with nonzero Medvedev degree then it fails to have the strong topological Rokhlin property. This result simplifies a known criterion and provides new examples of recursively presented groups without this property.

1.
Introduction

A countable group is said to have the strong topological Rokhlin property (STRP) when it admits a generic continuous action on the Cantor space. This means that the space of its continuous actions on the Cantor space endowed with the uniform topology has an element whose topological conjugacy class contains a dense
\mathchar 28999_{\mathchar 28942}
set.

This work concerns the open problem of characterizing countable groups having the STRP, and a sufficient condition for the failure of the STRP. Let us now mention the key works in this context. In the seminal work
[
12
]
Kechris and Rosendal established the STRP for
\mathbb{\mathchar 29018}
. Hochman
[
10
]
proved that
\mathbb{\mathchar 29018}^{\mathchar 29028}
fails to have the STRP for
\mathchar 29028\mathchar 12821\mathchar 28722
. Doucha
[
8
]
generalized these results by showing that free products of finite/cyclic groups have the STRP, and that virtually nilpotent groups that are not virtually
\mathbb{\mathchar 29018}
fail to have the STRP (also other classes of groups).

A key observation in
Hochman’s work is that in order to establish the failure of the STRP for
\mathbb{\mathchar 29018}^{\mathchar 29028}
(
\mathchar 29028\mathchar 12821\mathchar 28722
), it is sufficient to construct a subshift of finite type (SFT) having a sofic factor with the following properties: it has nonzero Medvedev degree, it equals the union of its minimal subsystems, and these minimal subsystems are not isolated, see
[
10
, §5.1]
. A related criterion can be found in Doucha’s paper
[
8
, §5.1]
.

The Medvedev degree of a set quantifies how hard it is to compute at least one of its elements in a recursion-theoretic sense. The underlying philosophy is that one identifies a mathematical problem with the set of its solutions, so the difficulty of the problem corresponds to how hard it is computing at least one solution. In the context of symbolic dynamics and subshifts over finitely generated groups, Medvedev degrees are a particularly robust notion. Morphisms of subshifts are automatically computable by the Curtis-Lyndon-Hedlund theorem, and from this it follows that Medvedev degrees are invariant for topological conjugacy and non-increasing by factor maps, without requiring the group to be amenable, see
[
15
,
3
]
.

Our main result is the following sufficient condition for the failure of the STRP on a recursively presented group.

Theorem 1.1
.

A recursively presented group which admits a (nonempty) SFT with nonzero Medvedev degree fails to have the STRP.

This condition is much easier to verify than the one established in Hochman’s work
[
10
]
. Indeed, in the case of
\mathbb{\mathchar 29018}^{\mathchar 29028}
(
\mathchar 29028\mathchar 12821\mathchar 28722
) the existence of SFTs with nonzero Medvedev degree is known since the 1970s
[
9
,
13
]
.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
and existing results about Medvedev degrees of SFTs, we obtain several new examples of recursively presented groups not having the STRP. This includes Baumslag-Solitar groups
[
2
]
, Lamplighter groups
[
5
]
, groups quasi-isometric to the hyperbolic plane
[
3
]
, branch groups
[
3
]
, direct products of two infinite groups
[
3
, Theorem 5.8]
(this was previously known for direct products of three infinite groups
[
8
, Theorem C]
), and groups admitting a simulation theorem
[
3
, §5.5]
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
strongly relies on Doucha’s result
[
8
, Theorem A]
, which shows that a finitely generated group has the STRP if and only if projectively isolated subshifts are dense in the space of subshifts on alphabet
\mathcal{\mathchar 28993}
for each finite alphabet
\mathcal{\mathchar 28993}
. Projectively isolated subshifts are a class of sofic subshifts introduced in
[
8
]
, see Definition
2.2
. We prove that if an SFT has nonzero Medvedev degree, then it has a neighborhood in this topological space without projectively isolated subshifts. The technical implementation of our argument relies on some results from
[
4
]

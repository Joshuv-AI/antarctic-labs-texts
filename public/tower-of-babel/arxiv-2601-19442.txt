Dissipative Solutions to a Compressible Non-Newtonian Korteweg System with Density-Dependent Viscous Stress Tensor
Didier Bresch, Christophe Lacave, Maja Szlenk

Abstract
The main objective of this paper is to prove that if capillarity effect is taken into account then there exist dissipative solutions to a system describing viscoplastic compressible flows with density dependent viscosities in a periodic domain 𝕋 d \mathbb{T}^{d} with d = 2 , 3 d=2,3 . We calculate the relative entropy inequality and in consequence show existence of dissipative solutions and the weak-strong uniqueness for this system. Our result extends the recent result concerning the link between Euler–Korteweg and Navier–Stokes–Korteweg systems for Newtonian flows (when the viscosity depends on the density) [See D. Bresch, M. Gisclon, I. Lacroix-Violet, Arch. Rational Mech. Anal. (2019)] to non-Newtonian flows.



Keywords:
compressible Navier–Stokes equations, non-Newtonian fluids, weak solutions, dissipative solutions, weak-strong uniqueness

Introduction

The analysis of compressible non-Newtonian systems, where the viscosity depends on the density (or the pressure), is an important topic in respect of the physical applications. Such systems are used to model different types of geophysical phenomena, in particular granular media
[
17
,
31
]
or an avalanche
[
4
,
12
,
45
]
. For more compressible models with pressure-dependent viscosity we refer also to
[
30
]
. However, well-posedness property for such compressible non-newtonian systems is still an open problem in the framework of weak solutions à la Leray (or Hoff). It is therefore important to understand possible physical factors which might lead to results related to well-posedness, even in a weaker sense. Our aim is to show in the compressible setting that incorporating the capillary forces leads to existence of dissipative solutions, which satisfy weak-strong uniqueness.

Here we consider the compressible non-Newtonian Navier–Stokes–Korteweg system in the form

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho+\mathrm{div\,}(\varrho u)

\displaystyle=0,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho u)+\mathrm{div\,}(\varrho u\otimes u)-\mathrm{div\,}(\varrho\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u))+\nabla p(\varrho)-\kappa\mathrm{div\,}(\varrho\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho)

\displaystyle=0

on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
(namely with periodic boundary conditions). The system is equipped with the initial conditions

\varrho_{|_{t=0}}=\varrho_{0},\qquad(\varrho u)_{|_{t=0}}=\varrho_{0}u_{0}

(1.2)

with

\varrho_{0}\geq 0,\qquad\sqrt{\varrho_{0}}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),\qquad\sqrt{\varrho_{0}}u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}).

(1.3)

For the viscous stress tensor
\mathbb{S}
, we assume that
\mathbb{S}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\mathrm{sym}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\mathrm{sym}}
is differentiable, and for some
p\geq 1
satisfies

\displaystyle|\mathbb{S}(A)|

\displaystyle\leqslant C|A|^{p-1}

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}(A):A

\displaystyle\geqslant c|A|^{p},\quad\text{if}\quad|A|>c_{1}>0

for
A\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{sym}}^{d\times d}
, and

(\mathbb{S}(A)-\mathbb{S}(B)):(A-B)\geqslant 0\quad\text{for}\quad A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\mathrm{sym}}.

(1.5)

For the pressure function
s\mapsto p(s)
, we assume that

p\in C([0,\infty))\cap C^{2}((0,\infty)),\quad p(0)=0,\quad p^{\prime}>0

(1.6)

and

a\varrho^{\gamma-1}-b\leqslant p^{\prime}(\varrho)\leqslant\frac{1}{a}\varrho^{\gamma-1}+b\qquad\hbox{ with }\gamma>1

(1.7)

for some constants
a,b>0
.

Remark 1.1
.

The assumptions (
1.4
)–(
1.5
) include in particular the cases

\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u)=|\mathbb{D}u|^{p-2}\mathbb{D}u\qquad\hbox{ with }\qquad p>1

and

\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u)=\frac{\mathbb{D}u}{\delta+|\mathbb{D}u|}\hbox{ with }\delta>0.

The additional stress depending on
\varrho
was proposed by Korteweg
[
34
]
to model capillarity effects. In the most general setting, the Korteweg term is given by

\varrho\nabla\left(K(\varrho)\Delta\varrho+\frac{1}{2}K^{\prime}(\varrho)|\nabla\varrho|^{2}\right)=\mathrm{div\,}\mathbb{K}

for

\mathbb{K}=\left(\varrho\mathrm{div\,}(K(\varrho)\nabla\varrho)+\frac{1}{2}\left(K(\varrho)-\varrho K^{\prime}(\varrho)\right)|\nabla\varrho|^{2}\right)\mathbb{I}-K(\varrho)\nabla\varrho\otimes\nabla\varrho.

We consider the special case where
{\rm div}\mathbb{K}=2\varrho\nabla\left({\Delta\sqrt{\varrho}}/{\sqrt{\varrho}}\right)=\mathrm{div\,}\left(\varrho\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho\right)
, which corresponds to the case
K(\varrho)={1}/{\varrho}
(see also Lemma
B.1
). Such term was considered in particular in the context of quantum fluids, see e. g.
[
32
]
. For modelling and derivation of general Korteweg stress we refer also to
[
29
,
42
,
49
]

1.1
State of the art and the main result

Density-dependent viscosities.

The question of existence of weak solutions to Navier–Stokes equations with the density-dependent viscosities was widely studied in the Newtonian case, namely when
\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u)
is linear. Works on this topic were started by Bresch, Desjardins and Lin in
[
10
]
for the Navier–Stokes–Korteweg system. In the following paper by Bresch and Desjardins
[
7
]
, the authors showed existence of weak solution for the 2D shallow water equations with drag terms, by showing the additional estimate
\nabla\sqrt{\varrho}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})
. In
[
8
,
9
]
, they further extend this approach to the case where

\mathbb{S}=\mu(\varrho)\mathbb{D}u+\lambda(\varrho)\mathrm{div\,}u\mathbb{I}

and
\lambda(\varrho)=\varrho\mu^{\prime}(\varrho)-\mu(\varrho)
and show that the solution satisfies the estimate
\nabla\psi(\varrho)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})
for
\psi^{\prime}(\varrho)={\mu^{\prime}(\varrho)}/{\sqrt{\varrho}}
. They also propose compatible physical quantities which may be considered for construction of solutions such as drag terms, capillary quantity or singular pressure.

Under the same conditions on
\mu
and
\lambda
, Mellet & Vasseur
[
46
]
showed the additional estimate on
\varrho(1+|u|^{2})\ln(1+|u|^{2})
, which allows to show stability of solutions without the extra terms. With the help of this estimate, the existence of global weak solutions in the case
\mu=\varrho
,
\lambda=0
was shown by Vasseur & Yu in
[
52
,
51
]
after the work by Gisclon & Lacroix-Violet
[
27
]
. A different construction was also proposed in
[
38
]
. Later, Lacroix-Violet & Vasseur
[
36
]
expanded the ideas from
[
51
]
to construct a renormalised solution to the quantum Navier–Stokes system.
Note that the first level of the construction scheme in the above papers includes suitable drag terms, capillarity quantity and singular pressure in the momentum equation as suggested in
[
9
]
.

Considering the more general forms of viscosity coefficients, in
[
15
]
the authors show the existence of
\kappa
-entropy weak solutions (see
[
11
]
for the definition) for the Navier–Stokes, and Navier–Stokes–Korteweg systems with a certain compatibility condition between
\mu(\varrho)
and
K(\varrho)
. The case of more general relations between
\mu
and
K
was also considered in 1D in
[
3
]
for
\mu(\varrho)=\varrho^{\alpha}
and
K(\varrho)=\varrho^{\beta}
with some conditions between
\alpha
and
\beta
.

Dissipative solutions.

The concept of dissipative solutions was first introduced by Lions
[
39
, Section 4.4]
in the context of incompressible Euler equations. Even though it is a weaker notion of a solution than in a standard Leray-Hopf framework, it guarantees a crucial weak-strong uniqueness property. Because of that, it has been widely used in recent years for different types of systems. Concerning compressible flows, we mention in particular
[
5
,
23
,
25
,
28
,
35
,
50
]
, where the authors analysed the dissipative solutions or weak-strong uniqueness for the Navier–Stokes and Euler equations for constant viscosities, and
[
14
]
for the density-dependent viscosities. Concerning Korteweg fluids, in
[
13
]
the authors showed the existence of global dissipative solutions to Euler–Korteweg system. Another concept related to relative entropy and weak-strong uniqueness are measured-valued dissipative solutions. For the compressible case they were analysed for example in
[
22
]
. Another interesting result can be found in
[
19
]
, where the authors considered a nonlocal Euler–Korteweg system. In the context of our work, it is also especially important to mention the result of Abbatielo, Feireisl, Novotny
[
1
]
, where it was shown that there exists a dissipative solution to the non-Newtonian Navier–Stokes system with viscosity coefficients independent of
\varrho
.

Non-Newtonian fluids.

The existence theory has been widely studied in the incompressible case. The works on this topic were started by Ladyzhenskaya in
[
37
]
, and then further developed for many types of fluids, see in particular
[
18
,
26
,
41
,
53
]
. However, there are only few results concerning the compressible case. In
[
33
]
the authors showed the existence of a local-in-time strong solution to the Navier–Stokes system with
\mathbb{S}
satisfying the growth and monotonicity conditions in the spirit of (
1.4
)-(
1.5
). However, the question of global existence of weak solutions still remains open. In the recent preprint
[
6
]
, Bresch, Burtea & Szlenk solved this problem in the one-dimensional case for a sufficiently high
p
. Existence of weak solutions was also shown before for the steady case in
[
16
]
. For the full multi-dimensional Navier–Stokes system, the existing works cover the special cases of
\mathbb{S}
, which provide a better regularity of
\mathrm{div\,}u
. The first result in this topic was obtained by Mamontov in
[
43
,
44
]
in the framework of Orlicz spaces, where
\mathbb{S}
was an exponential function of
\mathbb{D}u
. More recently, in
[
24
]
Feireisl, Liao and Málek obtained a weak-variational solution for the case, where
\mathbb{S}
contains a singularity in
\mathrm{div\,}u
. This crucial form of the stress tensor provided an
L^{\infty}
bound on
\mathrm{div\,}u
, which allowed to show the compactness of the density sequence. The method from
[
24
]
was also adapted in
[
48
]
to obtain the result on the Stokes system for the viscoplastic case, where
\mathbb{S}=\mu_{0}\mathbb{D}u+\mu_{1}\frac{\mathbb{D}u}{|\mathbb{D}u|}
. In the context of current work, it is important to note that all the above results concern the case of viscosity independent of
\varrho
.

Main result.

The objective of this paper is to extend the dissipative framework to the non-Newtonian case with density-dependent viscosities. We introduce the definition of a solution in a similar manner as Lions in
[
39
]
, by defining the relative entropy functional (see Definition
2.4
). Then, our main result states:

Theorem 1.2
.

Under the assumptions (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) and (
1.6
)-(
1.7
), there exists a global-in-time dissipative solution to (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition (
2.4
) with the initial conditions
(\varrho_{0},u_{0})
satisfying (
1.3
).

The rest of the paper will be organized as follows: in Section
2
, we give the precise definition of a dissipative solution (see Definition
2.4
) and we explain the motivation behind it. Then, the next two sections are devoted to prove Theorem
1.2
. In Section
3
we introduce an approximated system and show that it admits a global weak solution, using the Galerkin method. Note that the approximation involves an additional nonlinear viscosity, which is frequently used in the modelling of non-Newtonian fluids (see also comments in Section
3
).
Next, in Section
4
we obtain the relative entropy inequality for the approximated system and pass to the limit with the regularizing parameters. This results in the limit satisfying the requirements of Definition
2.4
, which finishes the proof of Theorem
1.2
. At the end of the paper we include the Appendix with useful auxiliary lemmas used in the proofs.

Remark 1.3
.

In the proofs we will focus on the case
d=3
, keeping in mind that the Sobolev embeddings we use are in particular valid for
d=2
as well.

Dissipative solutions - motivation and relative entropy inequality.

In order to define the dissipative solution, we need to first introduce the relative entropy functional (Firstly defined in
[
14
]
for the compressible Navier-Stokes system with
the shear viscosity
\mu(\varrho)=\varrho
and the bulk viscosity
\lambda(\varrho)=0
and generalized for more general viscosities in
[
13
]
) in the form

\mathcal{E}(t)=\mathcal{E}[(\varrho,u)|(r,v)](t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\varrho|u-v|^{2}+\kappa\varrho|\nabla\ln\varrho-\nabla\ln r|^{2})\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}H(\varrho|r)\;\mathrm{d}x,

(2.1)

with

H(\varrho|r)=H(\varrho)-H(r)-H^{\prime}(r)(\varrho-r),\qquad H(\varrho)=\varrho\int_{\bar{\varrho}}^{\varrho}\frac{p(s)}{s^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s,

where
\bar{\varrho}
a constant reference state. The two couples
(\varrho,u)
and
(r,v)
will be defined later-on. Let us note that

H^{\prime\prime}(\varrho)=\frac{p^{\prime}(\varrho)}{\varrho}>0.

We start with taking
(\varrho,u)
to be a weak solution, satisfying the energy inequality, which in this case has the form

E(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u):\mathbb{D}u\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s\leqslant E(0)

(2.2)

for

E(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int\Big(\varrho|u|^{2}+\kappa\varrho|\nabla\ln\varrho|^{2}\Big)\;\mathrm{d}x+\int H(\varrho)\;\mathrm{d}x.

(2.3)

(see also Proposition
B.2
).
We prove the following:

Proposition 2.1

We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t)-\mathcal{E}(0)=

From (
1.1

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(-\varrho u\cdot v)\;\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\partial_{t}(\varrho u)\cdot v\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho u\cdot\partial_{t}v\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho u\otimes u:\nabla v\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u):\mathbb{D}v\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}p\mathrm{div\,}v\;\mathrm{d}x

3
Construction of solutions - approximation scheme

In order to prove Theorem

, we will construct a weak solution to the regularized system, which reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho+\mathrm{div\,}(\varrho u)=\varepsilon\Delta\varrho,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho u)+\mathrm{div\,}(\varrho u\otimes u)-\mathrm{div\,}(\varrho\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u))

\displaystyle+\nabla p(\varrho)-\kappa\mathrm{div\,}(\varrho\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho)=-\varepsilon\nabla\varrho\cdot\nabla u+\nu\mathrm{div\,}\left(|\mathbb{D}u|^{q-2}\mathbb{D}u\right),

[

from below and above. It is a well known approximate term in the compressible setting, which also may be seen as encoding a property of mass diffusion for certain fluids.

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{N}|u_{N}|^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\varrho_{N}|\nabla\ln\varrho_{N}|^{2}+H(\varrho_{N})\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u_{N}\cdot\partial_{t}(\varrho_{N}u_{N})\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|u_{N}|^{2}\partial_{t}\varrho_{N}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\kappa}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\frac{|\nabla\varrho_{N}|^{2}}{\varrho_{N}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}H^{\prime}(\varrho_{N})\partial_{t}\varrho_{N}\,\mathrm{d}x

where, by using the equation on
\varrho_{N}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|u_{N}|^{2}\partial_{t}\varrho_{N}\,\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|u_{N}|^{2}\mathrm{div\,}(\varrho_{N}u_{N})\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|u_{N}|^{2}\Delta\varrho_{N}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\varrho_{N}u_{N}\otimes u_{N}\Big):\nabla u_{N}\;\mathrm{d}x+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big((\nabla\varrho_{N}\cdot\nabla)u_{N}\Big)\cdot u_{N}\,\mathrm{d}x

and by Lemma
B.1
(see the proof of Proposition
B.2
for details)

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\frac{|\nabla\varrho_{N}|^{2}}{\varrho_{N}}\,\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle-\kappa\int\partial_{t}\varrho_{N}\left(\mathrm{div\,}\left(\frac{1}{\varrho_{N}}\nabla\varrho_{N}\right)+\frac{1}{2\varrho_{N}^{2}}|\nabla\varrho_{N}|^{2}\right)\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa\int\mathrm{div\,}(\varrho_{N}u_{N})\left(\Delta\ln\varrho_{N}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\ln\varrho_{N}|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-\kappa\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta\varrho_{N}\left(\Delta\ln\varrho_{N}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\ln\varrho_{N}|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\kappa\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u_{N}\cdot\mathrm{div\,}\left(\varrho_{N}\nabla^{2}\varrho_{N}\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\kappa\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho_{N}:\left(-\nabla^{2}\varrho_{N}+\nabla\varrho_{N}\otimes\nabla\ln\varrho_{N}\right)\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\kappa\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u_{N}\cdot\Big(\varrho_{N}\nabla\frac{\Delta\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}}{\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}}\Big)\mathrm{d}x-\kappa\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{N}|\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho_{N}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x

and finally

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}H^{\prime}(\varrho_{N})\partial_{t}\varrho_{N}\,\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\int u_{N}\cdot\nabla(p(\varrho_{N}))\,\mathrm{d}x-\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla(H^{\prime}(\varrho_{N}))\cdot\nabla\varrho_{N}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int u_{N}\cdot\nabla(p(\varrho_{N}))\,\mathrm{d}x-\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}H^{\prime\prime}(\varrho_{N})|\nabla\varrho_{N}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

Putting these equalities together with (
3.7
), we get

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E_{N}+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{N}\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u_{N}):\mathbb{D}u_{N}\;\mathrm{d}x+\nu\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\mathbb{D}u_{N}|^{q}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
+\kappa\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{N}|\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho_{N}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}H^{\prime\prime}(\varrho_{N})|\nabla\varrho_{N}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

(3.8)

Integrating (
3.8
) in time, we obtain the energy estimate, satisfied uniformly with respect to
N
:

E_{N}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{N}\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u_{N}):\mathbb{D}u_{N}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\mathbb{D}u_{N}|^{q}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s\\
+\kappa\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{N}|\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho_{N}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}H^{\prime\prime}(\varrho_{N})|\nabla\varrho_{N}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s=E_{N}(0).

(3.9)

Note that from the assumption (
1.7
) on
p
it follows that
H(\varrho)\leqslant C_{1}\varrho^{\gamma}+C_{2}
for some constant
C_{1},C_{2}
and therefore
E_{N}(0)
is uniformly bounded in
N
using the strong convergence of
\varrho_{0,N},u_{0,N}
.

The above equality provides in particular the bound on
\mathbb{D}u_{N}
in
L^{q}(0,T^{*};L^{q})
, which does not depend on
T^{*}
. By the Korn’s inequality
(see e. g.
[
25
]
Theorem 10.17) and a special version of the Poincaré inequality from Proposition
A.1
(see also Remark 5.1 in
[
40
]
in the
L^{2}
framework), we deduce the
L^{q}(0,T;W^{1,q})
bound on
u_{N}
. Since all norms are equivalent on
X_{N}
, this also implies the bound in
L^{1}(0,T;W^{1,\infty})
, and in consequence
\varrho_{N}
satisfies the estimate (
3.5
) again independently on
T^{*}
. Finally, this and the estimate on
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{N}|u_{N}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x
from (
3.9
) provides the estimate on
\|u_{N}\|_{C(0,T^{*},X_{N})}
, which is independent of
T^{*}
. This allows us to iterate the process and eventually obtain the solution
(\varrho_{N},u_{N})
on the whole interval
[0,T]
.

Multiplying
(
3.8
)
by a function
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(0,T)
, we also obtain a localized-in-time version of (
3.9
), which will be useful later on:

-\int_{0}^{T}\psi^{\prime}(t)E_{N}(t)\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)\varrho_{N}\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u_{N}):\mathbb{D}u_{N}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\nu\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)|\mathbb{D}u_{N}|^{q}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s\\
+\kappa\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)\varrho_{N}|\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho_{N}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)H^{\prime\prime}(\varrho_{N})|\nabla\varrho_{N}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=0.

(3.10)

Step 2: Passing to the limit in all the terms except the nonlinear stress tensor.

Inequality (
3.9
) provides also the uniform estimates needed to pass to the limit with
N\to\infty
. In particular, again by the Korn’s and Poincaré’s inequalities, we have the estimate

\|u_{N}\|_{L^{q}(0,T;W^{1,q})}\leqslant C

independently of
N
. By the Banach–Alaoglu theorem, from (
3.9
) we have (up to a subsequence)

u_{N}\rightharpoonup u\quad\text{in}\quad L^{q}(0,T;W^{1,q}).

The bound for
\varepsilon\kappa\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{N}|\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho_{N}|^{2}
provides the estimate

(\varepsilon\kappa)^{1/4}\|\nabla\varrho_{N}^{1/4}\|_{L^{4}(0,T;L^{4})}+\sqrt{\varepsilon\kappa}\|\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2})}\leqslant C,

(3.11)

using the estimates

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{N}|\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho_{N}|^{2}\geqslant\frac{1}{8}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla\varrho_{N}^{1/4}|^{4}

(3.12)

and

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{N}|\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho_{N}|^{2}\geqslant\frac{1}{7}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}|^{2}

(3.13)

which were proven in
[
32
]

(for the proof see also
[
52
, Lemma 2.1]
; for the generalization of (
3.11
) we refer to
[
15
, Lemma 2.1]
and the Appendix in the ArXiv version of
[
2
]
), and thus

\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}\rightharpoonup\sqrt{\varrho}\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}).

Moreover, using the fact that
q>d\geqslant 2
and writing

\Delta\varrho_{N}=8\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}|\nabla\varrho_{N}^{1/4}|^{2}+2\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}\Delta\sqrt{\varrho_{N}},

(3.14)

we get that

\partial_{t}\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}=-\nabla\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}\cdot u_{N}-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}\mathrm{div\,}u_{N}+\varepsilon\left(4\left|\nabla\varrho_{N}^{1/4}\right|^{2}+\Delta\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}\right)

is bounded in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})
. In conclusion, from (
3.11
) and Aubin–Lions–Simon lemma we get that (up to a subsequence)

\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}\to\sqrt{\varrho}\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}).

In order to pass to the limit in the pressure, we note that
H^{\prime\prime}(\varrho_{N})=p^{\prime}(\varrho_{N})/\varrho_{N}\geqslant a\varrho^{\gamma-2}-b/\varrho
, and thus (
3.9
) gives

C(\varepsilon)\geqslant\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}H^{\prime\prime}(\varrho_{N})|\nabla\varrho_{N}|^{2}\geqslant\frac{4a}{\gamma^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla\varrho_{N}^{\gamma/2}|^{2}-4b\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}|^{2}.

Since the energy estimate (
3.9
) also provides that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\kappa\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}|^{2}\leqslant C,

we get that
\nabla\varrho_{N}^{\gamma/2}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
with respect to
N
and therefore we have the uniform bound on
\varrho_{N}^{\gamma}
in

L^{1}(0,T;L^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
. Since we also have
\varrho_{N}^{\gamma}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
, and therefore by interpolation

\varrho_{N}^{\gamma}\in L^{5/3}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
. This bound and the strong convergence related to
\varrho_{N}
we already have proven allows to conclude that
p(\varrho_{N})\to p(\varrho)
in
L^{r}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
for all
r<5/3.
Furthermore, from the momentum equation we have the following
bound on the time derivative of
\varrho_{N}u_{N}

\|\partial_{t}(\varrho_{N}u_{N})\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(0,T;W^{-1,q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\leqslant C,

and we are able to estimate
\varrho_{N}u_{N}
and
\nabla(\varrho_{N}u_{N})
as follows:

\|\varrho_{N}u_{N}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\leqslant C\|\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}u_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\|\sqrt{\varrho}_{N}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}

and

\|\nabla(\varrho_{N}u_{N})\|_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{3/2})}\leqslant\|\nabla\sqrt{\varrho}_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\|\sqrt{\varrho}_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{6}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\|u_{N}\|_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}

+\|\varrho_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\|\nabla u_{N}\|_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}.

In conclusion, again from Aubin–Lions–Simon lemma

\varrho_{N}u_{N}\to\varrho u\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})).

For the Korteweg term, using Lemma
B.1
we see that for a smooth
\varphi

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathrm{div\,}\left(\varrho_{N}\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho_{N}\right)\cdot\varphi\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{N}\nabla\left(\frac{\Delta\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}}{\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}}\right)\cdot\varphi\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

(3.15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}\left(2\nabla\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}\cdot\varphi+\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}\mathrm{div\,}\varphi\right)\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\to\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta\sqrt{\varrho}\left(2\nabla\sqrt{\varrho}\cdot\varphi+\sqrt{\varrho}\mathrm{div\,}\varphi\right)\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

as
N\to\infty
.
The above convergences allows us to pass to the limit in all the terms except the nonlinear stress tensor. In the limit we get the continuity equation

\partial_{t}\varrho+\mathrm{div\,}(\varrho u)=\varepsilon\Delta\varrho,

and

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho u\cdot\partial_{t}\varphi\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho u\otimes u:\nabla\varphi\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho\overline{\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u)}:\nabla\varphi\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

(3.16)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}p(\varrho)\mathrm{div\,}\varphi\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\kappa\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta\sqrt{\varrho}\left(2\nabla\sqrt{\varrho}\cdot\varphi+\sqrt{\varrho}\mathrm{div\,}\varphi\right)\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\nabla\varrho\cdot\nabla u)\cdot\varphi\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t-\nu\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\overline{|\mathbb{D}u|^{q-2}\mathbb{D}u}:\nabla\varphi\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

for each
\varphi\in L^{q}(0,T;W^{1,q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
with
\partial_{t}\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
, where by
\overline{\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u)}
and
\overline{|\mathbb{D}u|^{q-2}\mathbb{D}u}
we denote the weak limits of
\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u_{N})
and
|\mathbb{D}u_{N}|^{q-2}\mathbb{D}u_{N}
respectively. Note that from the assumption (
1.4
) on
\mathbb{S}
it follows that from the energy estimates we have
\varrho^{1/p}\mathbb{D}u\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
and then
\varrho^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u)\in L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(0,T;L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
. By interpolation, using the regularity
\varrho\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
, we get

\varrho\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u)=\varrho^{\frac{1}{p}}\varrho^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u)\in L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(0,T;L^{\frac{3p}{3p-2}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})).

Moreover,

\varrho u\otimes u=\sqrt{\varrho}\sqrt{\varrho}u\otimes u\in L^{q}(0,T;L^{3/2}).

Therefore the assumption (
3.2
) on
q
ensures us that
\varphi
is indeed an admissible test function.

Now we pass to the limit in (
3.9
) and (
3.10
). For the Korteweg term, we use the relation

\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho_{N}=2\left(\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}-4\nabla\varrho_{N}^{1/4}\otimes\nabla\varrho_{N}^{1/4}\right).

From the strong convergence of
\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}
and
\nabla\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}
we know that (up to a subsequence)
\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}\to\sqrt{\varrho}
and
\nabla\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}\to\nabla\sqrt{\varrho}
a. e.. Then

\nabla\varrho_{N}^{1/4}=\frac{1}{2\varrho^{1/4}_{N}}\nabla\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}\to\frac{1}{2\varrho^{1/4}}\nabla\sqrt{\varrho}\quad\text{a. e. on}\quad\{\varrho>0\}

and thus

\nabla\varrho_{N}^{1/4}\otimes\nabla\varrho_{N}^{1/4}\mathbbm{1}_{\varrho>0}\rightharpoonup\nabla\varrho^{1/4}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{1/4}\mathbbm{1}_{\varrho>0}\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}),

as the weak and pointwise limits coincide.
In consequence, since the gradient of a Sobolev function is equal to
0
a. e. on the level sets (see e. g. Section 6.1 in
[
20
]
), we know that
\nabla\varrho^{1/4}
and
\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\varrho}
are equal to
0
a. e. on
\{\varrho=0\}
and we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\varrho}-4\nabla\varrho^{1/4}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{1/4}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\varrho}-4\nabla\varrho^{1/4}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{1/4}\right|^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\varrho>0}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant\liminf_{N\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\varrho_{N}}-4\nabla\varrho_{N}^{1/4}\otimes\nabla\varrho_{N}^{1/4}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

For the term related to the pressure, we see that

H^{\prime\prime}(\varrho)|\nabla\varrho|^{2}=\frac{p^{\prime}(\varrho)}{\varrho}|\nabla\varrho|^{2}=|\nabla\Phi(\varrho)|^{2},

where
\Phi^{\prime}(\varrho)=\sqrt{\frac{p^{\prime}(\varrho)}{\varrho}}
. Using the bound (
1.7
) on
p^{\prime}
we can conclude that
\Phi(\varrho_{N})
converges strongly to
\Phi(\varrho)
and thus

\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}H^{\prime\prime}(\varrho)|\nabla\varrho|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla\Phi(\varrho)|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leqslant\liminf_{N\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}H^{\prime\prime}(\varrho_{N})|\nabla\varrho_{N}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

In conclusion, by the weakly lower semicontinuity of convex functions, passing to the limit in (
3.9
) we get

E(\varrho,u)(t)+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\overline{|\mathbb{D}u|^{q}}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho\overline{\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u):\mathbb{D}u}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s\\
+4\kappa\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\varrho}-4\nabla\varrho^{1/4}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{1/4}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}H^{\prime\prime}(\varrho)|\nabla\varrho|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s\leqslant E(\varrho_{0},u_{0}),

(3.17)

where again by
\overline{(\cdot)}
we denote the weak limit, and analogously for the localized formulation

-\int_{0}^{T}\psi^{\prime}(t)E(\varrho,u)\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\nu\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)\overline{|\mathbb{D}u|^{q}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)\varrho\overline{\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u):\mathbb{D}u}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
+4\kappa\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)\left|\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\varrho}-4\nabla\varrho^{1/4}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{1/4}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)H^{\prime\prime}(\varrho)|\nabla\varrho|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leqslant 0.

(3.18)

Step3: Passing to the limit in the nonlinear stress tensor.

In order to finish the proof and pass to the limit in the nonlinear stress tensor, we apply the monotonicity method. Let us put
\varphi=(\psi(t)u_{\eta})_{\eta}
, where
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((0,T))
and
f_{\eta}=f\ast\omega_{\eta}
for
\omega_{\eta}
being a standard mollifier. We have

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho u\cdot\partial_{t}(\psi u_{\eta})_{\eta}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)\partial_{t}(\varrho u)_{\eta}\cdot u_{\eta}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)\partial_{t}(\varrho u_{\eta})\cdot u_{\eta}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)(\partial_{t}(\varrho u)_{\eta}-\partial_{t}(\varrho u_{\eta}))\cdot u_{\eta}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)\partial_{t}\varrho|u_{\eta}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)\varrho\partial_{t}|u_{\eta}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)(\partial_{t}(\varrho u)_{\eta}-\partial_{t}(\varrho u_{\eta}))\cdot u_{\eta}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

and

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho u\otimes u:\nabla(\psi u_{\eta})_{\eta}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)(\varrho u\otimes u)_{\eta}:\nabla u_{\eta}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)\varrho u\otimes u_{\eta}:\nabla u_{\eta}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)((\varrho u\otimes u)_{\eta}-\varrho u\otimes u_{\eta}):\nabla u_{\eta}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)\mathrm{div\,}(\varrho u)|u_{\eta}|^{2}

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)((\varrho u\otimes u)_{\eta}-\varrho u\otimes u_{\eta}):\nabla u_{\eta}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)\partial_{t}\varrho|u_{\eta}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)\Delta\varrho|u_{\eta}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)((\varrho u\otimes u)_{\eta}-\varrho u\otimes u_{\eta}):\nabla u_{\eta}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

In conclusion, putting
(\psi u_{\eta})_{\eta}
into (
3.16
), we obtain

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi^{\prime}(t)\varrho|u_{\eta}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)(\partial_{t}(\varrho u)_{\eta}-\partial_{t}(\varrho u_{\eta}))\cdot u_{\eta}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

(3.19)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)((\varrho u\otimes u)_{\eta}-\varrho u\otimes u_{\eta}):\nabla u_{\eta}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)(\varrho\overline{\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u)})_{\eta}:\nabla u_{\eta}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)(p(\varrho))_{\eta}\mathrm{div\,}u_{\eta}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+2\kappa\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\psi(t)\left((\Delta\sqrt{\varrho}\sqrt{\varrho})_{\eta}\mathrm{div\,}u_{\eta}+2(\Delta\sqrt{\varrho}\nabla\sqrt{\varrho})_{\eta}\cdot u_{\eta}\right)\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla\varrho\cdot\nabla H^{\prime}(r)\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s.

Note that the constant
C
is the same as in (
2.4
) and thus depends only on the
L^{\infty}
norm of
\nabla v
.

4.1
Limit passage with
\nu,\varepsilon\to 0
.

Now we pass to the limit in (
4.1
) with the approximating parameters, and in consequence obtain a dissipative solution to (
1.1
), in the sense of Definition
2.4
. Let
\nu=\varepsilon
and let
(\varrho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})
be a weak solution to (
3.1
). By the energy estimate (
3.22
) and (
1.4
) we have the uniform estimates

\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}+\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}+\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}+\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{1/p}\mathbb{D}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\leqslant C

with
C
which does not depend on
\varepsilon
and
\nu
.
In particular, there exists
m\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})
such that

\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup^{*}m\quad\text{in}\quad L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}).

Moreover, the estimate (
3.22
) also provides that
\varepsilon^{1/q}\|\mathbb{D}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leqslant C
and

\kappa\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leqslant C,

from which we again deduce the bounds (see (
3.12
)-(
3.13
))

(\varepsilon\kappa)^{1/4}\|\nabla\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{1/4}\|_{L^{4}(0,T;L^{4})}+\sqrt{\varepsilon\kappa}\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2})}\leqslant C.

In particular, by (
3.14
), we infer that

\partial_{t}\varrho_{\varepsilon}=-\mathrm{div\,}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})+2\varepsilon\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}(4|\nabla\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{1/4}|^{2}+\Delta\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}})

is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}(0,T;W^{-1,3/2})
and therefore from the estimate on
\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1})}
and Aubin–Lions–Simon lemma we get

\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\to\sqrt{\varrho}\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}(0,T;L^{4})

and

\nabla\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\rightharpoonup^{*}\nabla\sqrt{\varrho}\quad\text{in}\quad L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}).

Defining
u(t,x)=\frac{m(t,x)}{\sqrt{\varrho(t,x)}}
for
\varrho(t,x)>0
,
u=0
otherwise, we have
m=\sqrt{\varrho}u
. Then, from the strong convergence of
\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}
we also have

\varrho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}=\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup^{*}\varrho u\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}(0,T;L^{4/3}(\mathbb{T}^{d})).

The above convergence allows us to pass to the limit in all terms contained in
b(t)
. For
b_{\mathrm{app}}
, we have that

\varepsilon\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\mathbb{D}u_{\varepsilon}|^{q-2}\mathbb{D}u_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{D}v\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s\right|\leqslant C(v)\varepsilon^{1/q}\|\varepsilon^{1/q}\mathbb{D}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{q-1},

and

\kappa\varepsilon\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho_{\varepsilon}:\nabla^{2}\ln r\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s\right|\leqslant C(r)\sqrt{\kappa\varepsilon}\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}\left(\kappa\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}

tend to zero when
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Using (
3.14
), we write

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla\varrho_{\varepsilon}\otimes v:(\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla v)\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s=

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(u_{\varepsilon}-v)\cdot v\Delta\varrho_{\varepsilon}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla\varrho_{\varepsilon}\otimes(u_{\varepsilon}-v):\nabla v\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}(u_{\varepsilon}-v)\cdot v(8|\nabla\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{1/4}|^{2}+2\Delta\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}})\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}2\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\nabla\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\otimes(u_{\varepsilon}-v):\nabla v\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sqrt{\varepsilon}C(v)\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}(u_{\varepsilon}-v)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\Big(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|\nabla\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{1/4}\|_{L^{4}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|\Delta\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}+\|\nabla\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\Big),

which also goes to zero by recalling (
3.12
)-(
3.13
) and that
\kappa\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho|\nabla\nabla\ln\varrho|^{2}
is bounded uniformly. Noting that

\kappa\varepsilon\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}\nabla\ln\varrho_{\varepsilon}\otimes(\nabla\ln\varrho_{\varepsilon}-\nabla\ln r):\nabla^{2}\ln r\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s\right|\leqslant C(r)\Big(\varepsilon\|\nabla\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}^{2}\\
+\varepsilon\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{6}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\|\nabla\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))},

and

\varepsilon\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla\varrho_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla H^{\prime}(r)\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s\right|\leqslant C(r)\varepsilon\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{6})}\|\nabla\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}.

we conclude that
b_{\mathrm{app}}(t)\to 0
a.e. as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
We finish the proof using the weakly lower-semicontinuity of convex functions, we get

\mathcal{E}[(\varrho,u)|(r,v)]\leqslant\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{E}[(\varrho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})|(r,v)]\leqslant\mathcal{E}(0)e^{Ct}+b(t)+C\int_{0}^{t}b(s)e^{C(t-s)}\;\mathrm{d}s

and therefore
(\varrho,u)
is a dissipative solution of (
1.1
).

Appendix A
Auxiliary lemmas

A.1
Poncaré inequality.

Below we present the special case of the Poincaré inequality, which is the generalization of the Remark 5.1 from
[
40
]
.

Lemma A.1
.

For
q>d
and sufficiently regular
\varrho,u
such that
\varrho\geqslant 0
,
\|\varrho\|_{L^{1}}>0
, the following estimate holds:

\|u\|_{L^{q}}\leqslant\frac{1}{\|\varrho\|_{L^{1}}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho u\;\mathrm{d}x\right|+C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}}

for some
C
depending on
d
.

Proof.

From Sobolev embedding and Poincaré inequality, we have

\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho\left[u-\frac{1}{|\mathbb{T}^{d}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u\;\mathrm{d}y\right]\;\mathrm{d}x\right|\leqslant C\|\varrho\|_{L^{1}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\varrho\|_{L^{1}}\left|\frac{1}{|\mathbb{T}^{d}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u\;\mathrm{d}x\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho u\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho\left[u-\frac{1}{|\mathbb{T}^{d}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u\;\mathrm{d}y\right]\;\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho u\;\mathrm{d}x\right|+C\|\varrho\|_{L^{1}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}}.

Using Poincaré inequality again, we finish the proof.
∎

A.2
Monotonicity property.

Let us now present a lemma related to a non-linear functional

Lemma A.2
.

Let
F(A)=|A|^{q-2}A
for
q>2
and
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
. Then

(F(A)-F(B)):(A-B)\geqslant C_{q}|A-B|^{q}\quad\text{for all}\quad A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d},

where

C_{q}=\min\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2^{q-2}}\right).

Proof.

We consider separately the cases
q\geqslant 3
and
2<q<3
.

For
q\geqslant 3
we use the convexity of the function
A\mapsto|A|^{q-2}
, so that

\frac{1}{2^{q-2}}|A-B|^{q-2}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}|A|^{q-2}+\frac{1}{2}|B|^{q-2}.

Multiplying this inequality by
|A-B|^{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{q-2}}|A-B|^{q}\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|A|^{q}+\frac{1}{2}|B|^{q}-(|A|^{q-2}+|B|^{q-2})A:B+\frac{1}{2}|A|^{q-2}|B|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|B|^{q-2}|A|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(|A|^{q-2}A-|B|^{q-2}B):(A-B)-\frac{1}{2}(|A|^{q-2}-|B|^{q-2})(|A|^{2}-|B|^{2})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle(|A|^{q-2}A-|B|^{q-2}B):(A-B),

where we used the fact that
\theta\mapsto\theta^{\frac{q-2}{2}}
is increasing for
\theta\geqslant 0
.

For the case
2\leqslant p<3
we use the inequality
(a+b)^{\alpha}\leqslant a^{\alpha}+b^{\alpha}
for
a,b>0
,
0<\alpha<1
. That way we get

\frac{1}{2}|A-B|^{q-2}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}||A|+|B||^{q-2}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}|A|^{q-2}+\frac{1}{2}|B|^{q-2}

and we proceed the same as in the previous case.
∎

A.3
Property of the pressure.

In this subsection we present the bound between

Proposition A.3
.

Let

for some
\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.8315pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.8315pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.14261pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to2.82411pt{}}}$\hss}}}r,\bar{r}>0
. If

satisfies (

)-(
1.7
) and

is given by

for some constant

\big|p(\varrho)-p(r)-p^{\prime}(r)(\varrho-r)\big|\leqslant C\big(H(\varrho)-H(r)-H^{\prime}(r)(\varrho-r)\big)

for some

depending on
\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.8315pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.8315pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.14261pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to2.82411pt{}}}$\hss}}}r,\bar{r}

Proof.

The proof uses similar arguments as for example in
[
35
]
(see in particular Lemma 8.2).
Let us start with some useful properties of
H
. Let

H(\varrho|r)=H(\varrho)-H(r)-H^{\prime}(r)(\varrho-r).

From the definition of
H
, it holds that
H^{\prime\prime}(\varrho)={p^{\prime}(\varrho)}/{\varrho}>0
for
\varrho>0
, thus
H(\varrho|r)\geqslant 0
. Moreover,

\partial_{\varrho}H(\varrho|r)=H^{\prime}(\varrho)-H^{\prime}(r),\quad\partial_{r}H(\varrho|r)=-H^{\prime\prime}(r)(\varrho-r).

(A.1)

\varrho
:
Case 1)
\varrho<\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.8315pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.8315pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.14261pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to2.82411pt{}}}$\hss}}}r/2
; Case 2)
\varrho\geq M
for some

chosen and then Case 3)
\varrho\in\left[\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.8315pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.8315pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.14261pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to2.82411pt{}}}$\hss}}}r/2,M\right]
.

Case 1):
\varrho<\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.8315pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.8315pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.14261pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to2.82411pt{}}}$\hss}}}r/2
.
As
\varrho<r
, we use (
A.1
) to conclude that
\varrho\mapsto H(\varrho|r)
is decreasing, whereas

is increasing. Therefore

H(\varrho|r)\geqslant H\left(\frac{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.8315pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.8315pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.14261pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to2.82411pt{}}}$\hss}}}r}{2}|r\right)\geqslant H\left(\frac{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.8315pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.8315pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.14261pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to2.82411pt{}}}$\hss}}}r}{2}|\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.8315pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.8315pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.14261pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to2.82411pt{}}}$\hss}}}r\right)>0.

On the other hand,

\big|p(\varrho)-p(r)-p^{\prime}(r)(\varrho-r)\big|\leqslant p(\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.8315pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.8315pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.14261pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to2.82411pt{}}}$\hss}}}r)+p(\bar{r})+\bar{r}\sup_{\xi\in[\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.14261pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.14261pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to1.87743pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to1.34102pt{}}}$\hss}}}r,\bar{r}]}p^{\prime}(\xi)=c(\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.8315pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.8315pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.14261pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to2.82411pt{}}}$\hss}}}r,\bar{r}).

p(\varrho)\geqslant\frac{a}{\gamma}\varrho^{\gamma}-b\varrho

H(\varrho)\geqslant\varrho\int_{\bar{\varrho}}^{\varrho}\Big(\frac{a}{\gamma}s^{\gamma-2}-\frac{b}{s}\Big)\;\mathrm{d}s=\frac{a}{\gamma(\gamma-1)}\varrho^{\gamma}-\frac{a}{\gamma(\gamma-1)}\bar{\varrho}^{\gamma-1}\varrho-b\varrho\ln\varrho+b\ln\bar{\varrho}\varrho.

Consequently,

H(\varrho|r)\geqslant\frac{a}{\gamma(\gamma-1)}\varrho^{\gamma}-b\varrho\ln\varrho+\alpha\varrho+\beta

for some

and
\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.8315pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.8315pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to3.14261pt{}}}$\hss}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{{$\underline{{\hbox to2.82411pt{}}}$\hss}}}r,\bar{r}

since

is continuous and positive.

Combining the three estimates, we end the proof.

A.4
An important identity

Lemma A.4
.

For
\varrho
such that
\varrho^{1/4}\in L^{4}(0,T;W^{1,4}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and
\sqrt{\varrho}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{2})
, the following equality holds:

\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta\sqrt{\varrho}\left(4\left|\nabla\varrho^{1/4}\right|^{2}+\Delta\sqrt{\varrho}\right)\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\varrho}-4\nabla\varrho^{1/4}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{1/4}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

Proof.

We need to first regularize
\varrho
and then integrate by parts. Let
\eta_{\varepsilon}
be a standard mollifier and let
f_{\varepsilon}=f\ast\eta_{\varepsilon}
. At the regularized level, we write

\nabla\left(\varrho^{1/4}\nabla(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}\right)=\nabla\varrho^{1/4}\otimes\nabla(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}+\varrho^{1/4}\nabla^{2}(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}

and therefore

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathrm{div\,}\left(\varrho^{1/4}\nabla(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}\right)|\nabla(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=

\displaystyle-2\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho^{1/4}\nabla(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}\otimes\nabla(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}:\nabla^{2}(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}\otimes\nabla(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}:\nabla\left(\varrho^{1/4}\nabla(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}\right)\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\nabla(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varrho^{1/4}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

Note that by Friedrich’s Lemma we have

\left\|\nabla\left(2\varrho^{1/4}\nabla(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}\right)-\nabla^{2}(\sqrt{\varrho})_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}))}=\left\|\nabla\left(2\varrho^{1/4}\nabla(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}\right)-\nabla(2\varrho^{1/4}\nabla\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\to 0

as
\varepsilon\to 0
, and analogously

\left\|\mathrm{div\,}\left(2\varrho^{1/4}\nabla(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}\right)-\Delta(\sqrt{\varrho})_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}\to 0.

Using the regularity of
\varrho
and the above calculations, we write

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta\sqrt{\varrho}|\nabla\varrho^{1/4}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta(\sqrt{\varrho})_{\varepsilon}|\nabla(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathrm{div\,}\left(\varrho^{1/4}\nabla(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}\right)|\nabla(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}\otimes\nabla(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}:\nabla\left(\varrho^{1/4}\nabla(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}\right)\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+4\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\nabla(\varrho^{1/4})_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varrho^{1/4}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla\varrho^{1/4}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{1/4}:\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\varrho}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+4\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla\varrho^{1/4}|^{4}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

In conclusion, using the fact that
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\Delta\sqrt{\varrho}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\varrho}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta\sqrt{\varrho}\left(4\left|\nabla\varrho^{1/4}\right|^{2}+\Delta\sqrt{\varrho}\right)\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}16|\nabla\varrho^{1/4}|^{4}-8\nabla\varrho^{1/4}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{1/4}:\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\varrho}+|\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\varrho}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\varrho}-4\nabla\varrho^{1/4}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{1/4}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

∎

Appendix B
Formal estimates

Energy.

The energy functional associated to our system is given by

E(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int\Big(\varrho|u|^{2}+\kappa\varrho|\nabla\ln\varrho|^{2}\Big)\;\mathrm{d}x+\int H(\varrho)\;\mathrm{d}x.

where

\displaystyle H(\varrho)=\varrho\int_{\overline{\varrho}}^{\varrho}{p(s)}/{s^{2}}\,ds
with
\overline{\varrho}
a constant reference density.
To verify the conservation of the energy, we need to recall the Böhm identity

Lemma B.1
.

We have

\mathrm{div\,}(\varrho\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho)=\varrho\nabla\Big(\frac{1}{\varrho}\Delta\varrho-\frac{1}{2\varrho^{2}}|\nabla\varrho|^{2}\Big)=2\varrho\nabla\frac{\Delta\sqrt{\varrho}}{\sqrt{\varrho}}.

Proof.

As
\varrho\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho=\varrho\nabla\frac{\nabla\varrho}{\varrho}=\nabla^{2}\varrho-\frac{\nabla\varrho\otimes\nabla\varrho}{\varrho}
, we observe that

(\mathrm{div\,}\nabla^{2}\varrho)_{i}=\sum_{j}\partial_{j}\partial_{i,j}\varrho=(\nabla\Delta\varrho)_{i}

and

-\Big(\mathrm{div\,}\frac{\nabla\varrho\otimes\nabla\varrho}{\varrho}\Big)_{i}=\sum_{j}\frac{\partial_{i}\varrho(\partial_{j}\varrho)^{2}-\varrho\partial_{i,j}\varrho\partial_{j}\varrho-\varrho\partial_{i}\varrho\partial_{j}^{2}\varrho}{\varrho^{2}}=\Big(\frac{|\nabla\varrho|^{2}\nabla\varrho}{\varrho^{2}}-\frac{\nabla|\nabla\varrho|^{2}}{2\varrho}-\frac{\Delta\varrho\nabla\varrho}{\varrho}\Big)_{i}.

So we conclude the first equality of this lemma by noticing

\varrho\nabla\Big(\frac{1}{\varrho}\Delta\varrho-\frac{1}{2\varrho^{2}}|\nabla\varrho|^{2}\Big)=\nabla\Delta\varrho-\frac{\nabla\varrho}{\varrho}\Delta\varrho-\frac{1}{2\varrho}\nabla|\nabla\varrho|^{2}+\frac{\nabla\varrho}{\varrho^{2}}|\nabla\varrho|^{2}.

For the second, we compute

\Delta\sqrt{\varrho}=\mathrm{div\,}\frac{\nabla\varrho}{2\sqrt{\varrho}}=\frac{\Delta\varrho}{2\sqrt{\varrho}}-\frac{|\nabla\varrho|^{2}}{4\varrho\sqrt{\varrho}}

hence

\nabla\frac{\Delta\sqrt{\varrho}}{\sqrt{\varrho}}=\frac{\nabla\Delta\varrho}{2\varrho}-\frac{\Delta\varrho\nabla\varrho}{2\varrho^{2}}-\frac{\nabla|\nabla\varrho|^{2}}{4\varrho^{2}}+\frac{|\nabla\varrho|^{2}\nabla\varrho}{2\varrho^{3}}.

Multiplying this relation by
2\varrho
gives the second equality of the lemma.
∎

Thanks to this lemma, we verify the conservation of the energy.

Proposition B.2
.

If
(\varrho,u)
is a strong solution to (
1.1
), then

\frac{\mathrm{d}E}{\mathrm{d}t}=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u):\mathbb{D}u\;\mathrm{d}x.

(B.1)

Proof.

First we notice that

\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\Big(\varrho|u|^{2}\Big)=\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2}\partial_{t}\varrho+\varrho u\cdot\partial_{t}u=\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2}\partial_{t}\varrho+u\cdot\partial_{t}(\varrho u)-|u|^{2}\partial_{t}\varrho=u\cdot\partial_{t}(\varrho u)-\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2}\partial_{t}\varrho,

and we write by using (
1.1
):

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\Big(\varrho|u|^{2}\Big)=-u\mathrm{div\,}(\varrho u\otimes u)+\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2}\mathrm{div\,}(\varrho u)+u\mathrm{div\,}(\varrho\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u))-u\nabla p(\varrho)+\kappa u\mathrm{div\,}(\varrho\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho).

As

-u\mathrm{div\,}(\varrho u\otimes u)+\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2}\mathrm{div\,}(\varrho u)=\sum_{i,j}-u_{i}^{2}\partial_{j}(\varrho u_{j})-\frac{1}{2}\varrho u_{j}\partial_{j}u_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u_{i}^{2}\partial_{j}(\varrho u_{j})=-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div\,}\Big(|u|^{2}\varrho u\Big)

the integral of the two first terms is zero.
The integral of the third term gives

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u\mathrm{div\,}(\varrho\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u))=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho\nabla u:\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u)=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho\mathbb{D}u:\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u).

The integral of the forth term is going with the integral involving
H
, because

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\partial_{t}H(\varrho)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}H^{\prime}(\varrho)\partial_{t}\varrho=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho u\cdot\nabla(H^{\prime}(\varrho))=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho u\cdot H^{\prime\prime}(\varrho)\nabla\varrho=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u\cdot p^{\prime}(\varrho)\nabla\varrho=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u\cdot\nabla(p(\varrho)).

To finish the proof, it remains to relate the fifth term to the following:

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{2}\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho|\nabla\ln\varrho|^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{2}\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\frac{|\nabla\varrho|^{2}}{\varrho}=-\kappa\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\partial_{t}\varrho\left(\mathrm{div\,}\left(\frac{1}{\varrho}\nabla\varrho\right)+\frac{1}{2\varrho^{2}}|\nabla\varrho|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathrm{div\,}(\varrho u)\left(\Delta\ln\varrho+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\ln\varrho|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\kappa\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho u\cdot\left(\nabla\Delta\ln\varrho+\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho\nabla\ln\varrho\right)=-\kappa\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u\cdot\mathrm{div\,}\left(\varrho\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho\right),

as

\varrho\nabla\Delta\ln\varrho+\varrho\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho\nabla\ln\varrho=\varrho\nabla\Delta\ln\varrho+\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho\nabla\varrho=\mathrm{div\,}\left(\varrho\nabla^{2}\ln\varrho\right).

∎

Integrating (
B.1
) in time, we obtain the energy inequality:

E(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}u):\mathbb{D}u\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s\leqslant E(0).

Relative ultragraph algebras and infinite interval maps
Ben-Hur Eidt, Daniel Gonçalves, Danilo Royer

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of relative ultragraph C ∗ C^{*} -algebras and extend classical injectivity criteria for representations—especially those arising from branching systems—to this relative setting. We next consider interval maps with a countable Markov partition (henceforth, “Markov infinite maps”). Each such map determines a countably infinite transition matrix and thereby an ultragraph 𝒢 \mathcal{G} ; under suitable hypotheses this yields representations of relative ultragraph C ∗ C^{*} -algebras C ∗ ​ ( 𝒢 , X ) C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X) . For one-dimensional systems whose underlying Markov infinite map admits an escape set, we show that the representation of C ∗ ​ ( 𝒢 , X ) C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X) built from point orbits coincides with the representation arising from an appropriate relative branching system. Finally, we apply our injectivity criteria to these representations, obtaining concrete conditions under which they are faithful.

-algebra; representations; infinite Markov maps, branching systems.

1.
Introduction

The study of
C^{*}
-algebras associated to directed graphs and their generalizations is a central theme at the interface of operator algebras and dynamics. Among these generalizations,
ultragraph

C^{*}
-algebras (see, e.g.,
[
27
,
24
,
7
]
) extend the classical Cuntz–Krieger and Exel–Laca frameworks, while
relative
graph
C^{*}
-algebras
[
26
]
provide a flexible setting interpolating between Toeplitz and graph algebras (see also
[
6
,
29
]
).

A complementary perspective is offered by
branching systems
. Originating in work on Cuntz–Krieger algebras, dynamical systems, and wavelets (e.g.,
[
1
,
4
,
14
,
25
]
), branching and semibranching systems have since underpinned a broad program connecting symbolic dynamics to concrete representations of graph-like
C^{*}
-algebras. In the graph setting, a systematic representation theory via branching systems has been developed, including unitary equivalence
[
17
]
and further structural developments
[
19
,
18
]
. The approach has been extended to related algebraic frameworks (e.g., relative Cohn path algebras
[
5
,
20
]
) and lifted to higher-rank graphs
[
22
]
, as well as to separable/monic representations and wavelet-type constructions
[
15
,
16
]
. These developments make branching systems a natural language for building and analyzing concrete representations.

In this paper, we introduce and develop
relative ultragraph

C^{*}
-algebras, and we show that, under a mild additional hypothesis, a large class of them are (canonically) isomorphic to ultragraph
C^{*}
-algebras (Theorem
2.14
). This parallels the graph case
[
26
]
and has algebraic analogues for relative Cohn and Leavitt path algebras
[
2
]
.

Motivated by classical (finite) Markov maps on intervals (cf.
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
and the broader Markov-map literature
[
3
,
23
]
), we formulate a notion of
infinite partition Markov interval maps with escape sets
. Each such map
g
determines a
0
–
1
incidence matrix and hence an ultragraph
\mathcal{G}
; we then construct representations of suitable relative ultragraph algebras
C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)
inside
B(\ell^{2}(R_{g}(x)))
directly from the dynamics of
g
(Theorem
5.6
and Proposition
5.9
). A key point is that the dichotomy “return to the domain vs. escape” for the iterates of
g
naturally produces the relative relations.

A second main contribution is to bridge these
Markov-induced
representations with branching systems: under natural hypotheses, every representation built from
g
coincides with one induced by a
(\mathcal{G},X)
-relative branching system (Theorem
5.11
). This identification lets us import injectivity criteria and uniqueness theorems from the branching-system toolkit to the Markov setting with minimal overhead (see Theorem
6.1
and its applications).

Our results thus establish a new bridge between interval dynamics and ultragraph operator algebras, expanding the reach of the relative framework and offering new tools for the analysis of infinite-state dynamical systems.

The paper is organized as follows.
Section 2 defines relative ultragraph
C^{*}
-algebras and proves the isomorphism with suitable ultragraph algebras. Section 3 adapts uniqueness theorems to the relative context. Section 4 develops relative branching systems and their induced representations, establishing the connection with ultragraph representations. Section 5 introduces infinite Markov interval maps with escape sets and builds the associated representations, proving their equivalence with branching-system representations. Section 6 presents examples illustrating the theory and injectivity criteria in concrete settings.

2.
Relative ultragraph
C^{\ast}
-algebras

2.1.
Background on ultragraphs and its C*-algebras

In this subsection we recall the main definitions and relevant results regarding ultragraphs, as introduced in
[
28
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

An
ultragraph
is a quadruple
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{G}^{0},\mathcal{G}^{1},r,s)
consisting of two countable sets
\mathcal{G}^{0},\mathcal{G}^{1}
, a map
s:\mathcal{G}^{1}\to\mathcal{G}^{0}
, and a map
r:\mathcal{G}^{1}\to P(\mathcal{G}^{0})
, where
P(\mathcal{G}^{0})
stands for the power set of
\mathcal{G}^{0}
.

Before we define the C*-algebra associated to an ultragraph we need the following notion.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an ultragraph. Define
\mathcal{E}
to be the smallest subset of
P(G^{0})
that contains
\{v\}
for all
v\in G^{0}
, contains
r(e)
for all
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
, and is closed under finite unions and finite intersections. The collection
\mathcal{E}
is called the boolean algebra associated to the ultragraph
\mathcal{G}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an ultragraph. The
ultragraph algebra

C^{*}(\mathcal{G})
is the universal
C^{*}
-algebra generated by a family of partial isometries with orthogonal ranges
\{s_{e}:e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\}
and a family of projections
\{p_{A}:A\in\mathcal{E}\}
satisfying

(1)

p_{\emptyset}=0,p_{A}p_{B}=p_{A\cap B},p_{A\cup B}=p_{A}+p_{B}-p_{A\cap B}
, for all
A,B\in\mathcal{E}
;

(2)

s_{e}^{*}s_{e}=p_{r(e)}
, for all
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
;

(3)

s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\leq p_{s(e)}
for all
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
; and

(4)

p_{v}=\sum\limits_{s(e)=v}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}
whenever
0<|s^{-1}(v)|<\infty
.

2.2.
Relative ultragraph C*-algebras

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an ultragraph and denote by
Reg(\mathcal{G})
the set of regular vertices, that is, the set of vertices
v
such that
0<|s^{-1}(v)|<\infty
. We have the following definition.

Definition 2.4
.

We say that a pair
(\mathcal{G},X)
is a relative ultragraph if
\mathcal{G}
is an ultragraph and
X
is any subset of
Reg(\mathcal{G})
. The relative ultragraph
C^{\ast}
-algebra
C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)
is the universal

C^{*}
-algebra generated by a family of partial isometries with orthogonal ranges
\{s_{e}:e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\}
and a family of projections
\{p_{A}:A\in\mathcal{E}\}
satisfying the conditions (1) to (3) of Definition
2.3
and satisfying, for every vertex
v\in X
, condition (4) of Definition
2.3
.

Notice that for a given relative ultragraph
(\mathcal{G},X)
, the ultragraph

C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(\mathcal{G})
can be realized as a quotient of the relative
ultragraph
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)
. We now describe a construction
that associates to a relative ultragraph an ordinary ultragraph in such a way
that, under suitable conditions, the corresponding
C^{*}
-algebras are isomorphic.
This construction parallels the one for graphs presented in

[
26
, Theorem 3.7]
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},X)
be a relative ultragraph, and set

Y:=\mathrm{Reg}(\mathcal{G})\setminus X,\quad Y^{\prime}:=\{\,y^{\prime}\mid y\in Y\,\},

where
Y^{\prime}
denotes a disjoint copy of
Y
.
We define the ultragraph
\mathcal{G}_{X}=(\mathcal{G}^{0}_{X},\mathcal{G}^{1}_{X},r_{X},s_{X})
as follows:

\mathcal{G}_{X}^{0}:=\mathcal{G}^{0}\cup\{\,v^{\prime}\mid v\in Y\,\},\ \ \ \mathcal{G}_{X}^{1}:=\mathcal{G}^{1}\cup\{\,e^{\prime}\mid e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\text{ and }r(e)\cap Y\neq\emptyset\,\}.

The maps
s_{X}
and
r_{X}
, extending
s
and
r
to
\mathcal{G}_{X}^{1}
, are defined in the elements of
\mathcal{G}_{X}^{1}
that are not contained in
\mathcal{G}^{1}
by

s_{X}(e^{\prime}):=s(e),\quad r_{X}(e^{\prime}):=(r(e)\cap Y)^{\prime}=\{\,w^{\prime}\mid w\in r(e)\cap Y\,\}.

Remark 2.6
.

If
A,B\subseteq Y
, then

(A\cap B)^{\prime}=A^{\prime}\cap B^{\prime},\quad(A\cup B)^{\prime}=A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}.

We shall use the notation

C:=\{\,e^{\prime}\mid e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\text{ and }r(e)\cap Y\neq\emptyset\,\},

so that
\mathcal{G}_{X}^{1}=\mathcal{G}^{1}\cup C
.
Moreover, let
\mathcal{E}
denote the Boolean algebra of the ultragraph
\mathcal{G}
, and let
\mathcal{E}_{X}
denote the Boolean algebra of the ultragraph
\mathcal{G}_{X}
.
The following lemma describes the relation between these two Boolean algebras.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},X)
be a relative ultragraph, and set
Y:=\mathrm{Reg}(\mathcal{G})\setminus X
.

(1)

For every
A\in\mathcal{E}
we have
(A\cap Y)^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{X}
.

(2)

For every
B\in\mathcal{E}_{X}
we have
B\cap\mathcal{G}^{0}\in\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

(1)

Let
A\in\mathcal{E}
. By
[
28
, Lemma 2.12]
, we may write

A=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\left(\bigcap_{e\in X_{i}}r(e)\right)\cup F,

where
F\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{0}
is finite and each
X_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{1}
is finite.
Using Remark
2.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle(A\cap Y)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\left(\bigcap_{e\in X_{i}}(r(e)\cap Y)^{\prime}\right)\cup(F\cap Y)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\left(\bigcap_{e\in X_{i}\cap Q}r(e^{\prime})\right)\cup(F\cap Y)^{\prime},

where

Q:=\{\,f\in\mathcal{G}^{1}:r(f)\cap Y\neq\emptyset\,\}.

(Note that if
f\notin Q
, then
r(f)\cap Y=\emptyset
.)
Since each
X_{i}\cap Q
is finite and
(F\cap Y)^{\prime}
is finite,
[
28
, Lemma 2.12]
implies that
(A\cap Y)^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{X}
.

(2)

Let
B\in\mathcal{E}_{X}
. By
[
28
, Lemma 2.12]
, we may write

B=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\left(\bigcap_{f\in X_{i}}r_{X}(f)\right)\cup F,

where
F\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{0}_{X}
is finite and each
X_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{X}^{1}
is finite.
Intersecting with
\mathcal{G}^{0}
gives

B\cap\mathcal{G}^{0}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\left(\bigcap_{f\in X_{i}}(r_{X}(f)\cap\mathcal{G}^{0})\right)\cup(F\cap\mathcal{G}^{0}).

Recall that
\mathcal{G}_{X}^{1}=\mathcal{G}^{1}\cup C
.
If
f\in C
, then
r_{X}(f)\cap\mathcal{G}^{0}=\emptyset
, while if
f\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
we have
r_{X}(f)=r(f)=r(f)\cap\mathcal{G}^{0}
.
Hence

B\cap\mathcal{G}^{0}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\left(\bigcap_{f\in X_{i}\cap\mathcal{G}^{1}}r(f)\right)\cup(F\cap\mathcal{G}^{0}).

By
[
28
, Lemma 2.12]
, it follows that
B\cap\mathcal{G}^{0}\in\mathcal{E}
.

∎

In the following result we construct a natural
*
-homomorphism from the relative ultragraph algebra

C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)
into the ultragraph algebra
C^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{X})
. We denote by
s_{e}
and
p_{A}
the canonical generators of

C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)
, where
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
and
A\in\mathcal{E}
,
and by
S_{f}
and
P_{B}
the canonical generators of
C^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{X})
,
where
f\in\mathcal{G}_{X}^{1}
and
B\in\mathcal{E}_{X}
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},X)
be a relative ultragraph. Then there exists a

*
-homomorphism

\phi:C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)\longrightarrow C^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{X})

such that, for each
B\in\mathcal{E}
,

\phi(p_{B})=P_{B}+P_{(B\cap Y)^{\prime}}

and, for each
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
,

\phi(s_{e})=\begin{cases}S_{e},&\text{if }r(e)\cap Y=\emptyset,\\[6.0pt]
S_{e}+S_{e^{\prime}},&\text{if }r(e)\cap Y\neq\emptyset.\end{cases}

Proof.

We verify that

\{\phi(p_{B}):B\in\mathcal{E}\}\ \cup\ \{\phi(s_{e}):e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\}

satisfies the defining relations of the universal
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)
.
By the universal property,
\phi
then extends to a
*
-homomorphism

C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)\to C^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{X})
.

(1)

Boolean relations.

Clearly
\phi(p_{\emptyset})=P_{\emptyset}=0
. For
A,B\in\mathcal{E}
,

\displaystyle\phi(p_{A})\phi(p_{B})

\displaystyle=(P_{A}+P_{(A\cap Y)^{\prime}})(P_{B}+P_{(B\cap Y)^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=P_{A}P_{B}+P_{A}P_{(B\cap Y)^{\prime}}+P_{(A\cap Y)^{\prime}}P_{B}+P_{(A\cap Y)^{\prime}}P_{(B\cap Y)^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=P_{A\cap B}+P_{(A\cap B\cap Y)^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\phi(p_{A\cap B}),

so
\phi(p_{A})\phi(p_{B})=\phi(p_{A\cap B})
. Similarly,

\phi(p_{A})+\phi(p_{B})-\phi(p_{A\cap B})=\phi(p_{A\cup B}).

(2)

Range relations.

We show
\phi(p_{r(e)})=\phi(s_{e})^{*}\phi(s_{e})
for all
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
.

If
r(e)\cap Y=\emptyset
, then
\phi(s_{e})=S_{e}
and

\phi(s_{e})^{*}\phi(s_{e})=S_{e}^{*}S_{e}=P_{r(e)}=\phi(p_{r(e)}).

If
r(e)\cap Y\neq\emptyset
, then

\displaystyle\phi(p_{r(e)})

\displaystyle=P_{r(e)}+P_{(r(e)\cap Y)^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=P_{r_{X}(e)}+P_{r_{X}(e^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=S_{e}^{*}S_{e}+S_{e^{\prime}}^{*}S_{e^{\prime}},

while

\displaystyle\phi(s_{e})^{*}\phi(s_{e})

\displaystyle=(S_{e}+S_{e^{\prime}})^{*}(S_{e}+S_{e^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=S_{e}^{*}S_{e}+S_{e}^{*}S_{e^{\prime}}+S_{e^{\prime}}^{*}S_{e}+S_{e^{\prime}}^{*}S_{e^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=S_{e}^{*}S_{e}+S_{e^{\prime}}^{*}S_{e^{\prime}},

since
S_{f}^{*}S_{g}=0
whenever
f\neq g\in\mathcal{G}_{X}^{1}
. Hence
\phi(p_{r(e)})=\phi(s_{e})^{*}\phi(s_{e})
.

(3)

Source projection relation.

For every
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
,

\phi(p_{s(e)})\,\phi(s_{e})\phi(s_{e})^{*}=\phi(s_{e})\phi(s_{e})^{*}.

Indeed, write
\phi(p_{s(e)})=P_{s(e)}+\mathbf{1}_{\{s(e)\in Y\}}\,P_{s(e)^{\prime}}
.
If
r(e)\cap Y=\emptyset
, then
\phi(s_{e})\phi(s_{e})^{*}=S_{e}S_{e}^{*}\leq P_{s(e)}
and
P_{s(e)^{\prime}}S_{e}S_{e}^{*}=0
, so the equality holds.
If
r(e)\cap Y\neq\emptyset
, then
\phi(s_{e})\phi(s_{e})^{*}=S_{e}S_{e}^{*}+S_{e^{\prime}}S_{e^{\prime}}^{*}
and each term is
\leq P_{s(e)}
, while again
P_{s(e)^{\prime}}
annihilates them. Thus the relation holds in all cases.

(4)

Cuntz–Krieger relation at
v\in X
.

Fix
v\in X
. Since
\{v\}\cap Y=\emptyset
, we have
\phi(p_{v})=P_{v}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle P_{v}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{f\in\mathcal{G}_{X}^{1}:\ s_{X}(f)=v\}}S_{f}S_{f}^{*}=\sum_{\{e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}:\ s(e)=v\}}S_{e}S_{e}^{*}\;+\;\sum_{\{e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{X}^{1}\setminus\mathcal{G}^{1}:\ s_{X}(e^{\prime})=v\}}S_{e^{\prime}}S_{e^{\prime}}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\{e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\ |\ r(e)\cap Y=\emptyset\textrm{ and }s(e)=v\}}S_{e}S_{e}^{*}+\sum\limits_{\{e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\ |\ r(e)\cap Y\neq\emptyset\textrm{ and }s(e)=v\}}S_{e}S_{e}^{*}+S_{e^{\prime}}S_{e^{\prime}}^{*}.

For
e
with
s(e)=v
we have, by direct computation,

\phi(s_{e})\phi(s_{e})^{*}=\begin{cases}S_{e}S_{e}^{*},&r(e)\cap Y=\emptyset,\\[4.0pt]
S_{e}S_{e}^{*}+S_{e^{\prime}}S_{e^{\prime}}^{*},&r(e)\cap Y\neq\emptyset.\end{cases}

Summing over all
e
with source
v
gives

P_{v}=\sum_{\{e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}:\ s(e)=v\}}\phi(s_{e})\phi(s_{e})^{*},

which is the required Cuntz–Krieger relation at
v
.

Therefore all defining relations of
C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)
are satisfied, and by universality
\phi
extends to a
*
-homomorphism

\phi:C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)\longrightarrow C^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{X}).

∎

The next lemma will be used later to show that, under an additional hypothesis, the
homomorphism
\phi
constructed above is an isomorphism.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},X)
be a relative ultragraph and set
Y:=\mathrm{Reg}(\mathcal{G})\setminus X
.
If
Z\in\mathcal{E}_{X}
, then there exist
A,B\in\mathcal{E}
such that

Z\;=\;A\;\cup\;(B\cap Y)^{\prime}.

Moreover,
A
and
B\cap Y
are uniquely determined.

Proof.

By
[
28
, Lemma 2.12]
, we can write

Z\;=\;\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\left(\;\bigcap_{f\in X_{i}}r_{X}(f)\;\right)\;\cup\;F,

where each
X_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{X}^{1}
is finite and
F\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{X}^{0}
is finite.
We may assume (discarding empty intersections) that for each
i
either

X_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{1}
or
X_{i}\subseteq C
, where

C\;:=\;\{\,e^{\prime}\mid e\in\mathcal{G}^{1},\ r(e)\cap Y\neq\emptyset\,\}.

Indeed, if
X_{i}
contains both some
f\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
and some
e^{\prime}\in C
, then

r_{X}(f)=r(f)\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{0}
while
r_{X}(e^{\prime})=(r(e)\cap Y)^{\prime}\subseteq Y^{\prime}
are disjoint, so

\bigcap_{g\in X_{i}}r_{X}(g)=\emptyset
.

Reindex so that for some
K\in\{0,\dots,N\}
we have

X_{1},\dots,X_{K}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{1}
and
X_{K+1},\dots,X_{N}\subseteq C
.
Set

A\;:=\;\bigcup_{i=1}^{K}\left(\;\bigcap_{f\in X_{i}}r(f)\;\right)\ \cup\ (F\cap\mathcal{G}^{0}).

Then
A\in\mathcal{E}
, because it is obtained from finite vertex sets in
\mathcal{G}^{0}
and ranges

r(e)
with
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
using finite unions and intersections.

For the remaining part, note that for
i\geq K+1
and
X_{i}\subseteq C
we can write

X_{i}=\{e^{\prime}_{1},\dots,e^{\prime}_{m}\}
with
e_{j}\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
, so using the properties of
(\cdot)^{\prime}
,

\bigcap_{e^{\prime}\in X_{i}}r_{X}(e^{\prime})\;=\;\bigcap_{e^{\prime}\in X_{i}}(r(e)\cap Y)^{\prime}\;=\;\left(\;\bigcap_{e^{\prime}\in X_{i}}(r(e)\cap Y)\;\right)^{\prime}.

Hence

\displaystyle Z\setminus A

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=K+1}^{N}\left(\;\bigcap_{e^{\prime}\in X_{i}}r_{X}(e^{\prime})\;\right)\ \cup\ (F\cap Y^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left(\ \bigcup_{i=K+1}^{N}\bigcap_{e^{\prime}\in X_{i}}(r(e)\cap Y)\ \right)^{\prime}\ \cup\ (F\cap Y^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left(\ \Big(\;\bigcup_{i=K+1}^{N}\bigcap_{e^{\prime}\in X_{i}}r(e)\;\Big)\ \cap Y\ \right)^{\prime}\ \cup\ (F\cap Y^{\prime}).

Define

B_{1}\;:=\;\bigcup_{i=K+1}^{N}\bigcap_{e^{\prime}\in X_{i}}r(e)\ \in\ \mathcal{E}.

Since
F\cap Y^{\prime}
is finite, write
F\cap Y^{\prime}=\{v_{1}^{\prime},\dots,v_{n}^{\prime}\}
and set

B\;:=\;B_{1}\cup\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}\ \in\ \mathcal{E}.

Then
(B\cap Y)^{\prime}=(B_{1}\cap Y)^{\prime}\cup\{v_{1}^{\prime},\dots,v_{n}^{\prime}\}
, hence

Z\setminus A=(B\cap Y)^{\prime}
. As
A\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{0}
and
(B\cap Y)^{\prime}\subseteq Y^{\prime}
are
disjoint, we conclude
Z=A\cup(B\cap Y)^{\prime}
.

For uniqueness, suppose
Z=A_{1}\cup(B_{1}\cap Y)^{\prime}=A_{2}\cup(B_{2}\cap Y)^{\prime}
with
A_{i},B_{i}\in\mathcal{E}
.
Intersecting with
\mathcal{G}^{0}
gives
A_{1}=Z\cap\mathcal{G}^{0}=A_{2}
.
Likewise, applying the inverse of
(\cdot)^{\prime}
on
Y^{\prime}
gives

B_{1}\cap Y=\{\,w\in Y:\ w^{\prime}\in Z\,\}=B_{2}\cap Y
. Thus
A
and
B\cap Y
are uniquely determined.
∎

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},X)
be a relative ultragraph and set
Y=\mathrm{Reg}(\mathcal{G})\setminus X
.
If
r(e)\cap Y
is finite for every
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
, then for each
Z\in\mathcal{E}_{X}
there
is a unique decomposition

Z\;=\;A\;\cup\;(B\cap Y)^{\prime},

with
A,B\in\mathcal{E}
and
(B\cap Y)^{\prime}
finite.

Proof.

With the notation in the proof of Lemma
2.9
, the hypothesis implies
that each set
\bigcap_{e^{\prime}\in X_{i}}(r(e)\cap Y)
(with
X_{i}\subseteq C
) is finite; hence their
finite union is finite. Therefore
(B_{1}\cap Y)^{\prime}
is finite, and after adjoining the
finitely many points coming from
F\cap Y^{\prime}
we still have
(B\cap Y)^{\prime}
finite.
Uniqueness is as in Lemma
2.9
.
∎

Our next goal is to prove that, if
r(e)\cap Y
is finite for every
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
, then

\phi
is a
*
-isomorphism
C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)\cong C^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{X})
.
We split the argument into two propositions: the first establishes
surjectivity
, and the
second constructs an explicit left-inverse
\psi:C^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{X})\to C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)
with

\psi\circ\phi=\mathrm{id}
, which yields
injectivity
of
\phi
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},X)
be a relative ultragraph and set
Y:=\mathrm{Reg}(\mathcal{G})\setminus X
.
If
r(e)\cap Y
is finite for all
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
, then the
*
-homomorphism

\phi:C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)\to C^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{X})
from Proposition
2.8
is
surjective.

Proof.

We show that the canonical generators of
C^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{X})
lie in
\operatorname{Im}(\phi)
.
Since
C^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{X})
is generated by
\{P_{B}:B\in\mathcal{E}_{X}\}\cup\{S_{f}:f\in\mathcal{G}_{X}^{1}\}
,
this will prove surjectivity.

First, we show that all projections
P_{w}
, for
w\in E^{0}_{X}
, are contained in
\mathrm{Im}(\phi)
. If
w=v\in\mathcal{G}^{0}
and
v\notin Y
, then
\phi(p_{v})=P_{v}
; if
w=v\in\mathcal{G}^{0}
and
v\in Y
, then

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\,|\,s(e)=v}\phi(s_{e})\phi(s_{e})^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\,|\,s(e)=v\\
r(e)\cap Y=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\phi(s_{e})\phi(s_{e})^{*}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\,|\,s(e)=v\\
r(e)\cap Y\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\phi(s_{e})\phi(s_{e})^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\,|\,s(e)=v\\
r(e)\cap Y=\emptyset\end{subarray}}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\,|\,s(e)=v\\
r(e)\cap Y\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}(s_{e}+s_{e^{\prime}})(s_{e}+s_{e^{\prime}})^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\,|\,s(e)=v\\
r(e)\cap Y=\emptyset\end{subarray}}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\,|\,s(e)=v\\
r(e)\cap Y\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\big(s_{e}s_{e}^{*}+s_{e^{\prime}}s_{e^{\prime}}^{*}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{f\in\mathcal{G}^{1}_{X}\,|\,s(f)=v}s_{f}s_{f}^{*}=P_{v}.

Thus,
P_{v}\in Im(\phi)
and, by definition,

\phi\left(p_{v}-\sum\limits_{e\in E^{1}\ |\ s(e)=v}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\right)=P_{v}+P_{v^{\prime}}-P_{v}=P_{v^{\prime}},

showing that all projections
P_{w}
are in the image of
\phi
.

Next, we prove that all projections of the form
P_{r(f)}
, for
f\in\mathcal{G}_{X}^{1}
, belong to
\text{Im}(\phi)
. If
f\in C
, then
f=e^{\prime}
for some
e\in E^{1}
, and
r(f)=r(e^{\prime})=(r(e)\cap Y)^{\prime}
, which is finite by hypothesis. Therefore,
P_{r(f)}=\sum_{v\in r(f)}P_{v}\in\text{Im}(\phi)
. If
f\in E^{1}
, we consider two cases: if
r(f)\cap Y=\emptyset
, then
\phi(P_{r(f)})=P_{r(f)}
; if
r(f)\cap Y\neq\emptyset
, then
\phi(P_{r(f)})=P_{r(f)}+P_{(r(f)\cap Y)^{\prime}}=P_{r(f)}+P_{r(f^{\prime})}
, showing that
P_{r(f)}=\phi(P_{r(f)})-P_{r(f^{\prime})}\in\text{Im}(\phi)
.

To conclude the proof, it suffices to show that all elements of the form
S_{f}
, for
f\in\mathcal{G}_{X}^{1}
, are in the image of
\phi
. This is clear if
f\in E^{1}
and
r(f)\cap Y=\emptyset
because
\phi(s_{f})=S_{f}
. If
f\in E^{1}
and
r(f)\cap Y\neq\emptyset
, we observe that
\phi(s_{f})P_{r(f)}=(S_{f}+S_{f^{\prime}})P_{r(f)}=S_{f}
and
\phi(s_{f})P_{r(f^{\prime})}=(S_{f}+S_{f^{\prime}})P_{r(f^{\prime})}=S_{f^{\prime}}
.
Since
P_{r(f)}
and
P_{r(f^{\prime})}
are in
\text{Im}(\phi)
, we conclude that
S_{f},S_{f^{\prime}}\in\text{Im}(\phi)
, completing the proof.

∎

To clarify the proof of injectivity of
\phi
, we begin with a computational lemma.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},X)
be a relative ultragraph and set
Y:=\mathrm{Reg}(\mathcal{G})\setminus X
.
Suppose
Z_{1}=A_{1}\cup(B_{1}\cap Y)^{\prime}
and
Z_{2}=A_{2}\cup(B_{2}\cap Y)^{\prime}
with
A_{1},A_{2},B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{E}
.
Define, for
w\in\mathcal{G}^{0}
,

q_{w}\;:=\;p_{w}\;-\;\sum_{\{e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}:\ s(e)=w\}}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}.

Then the following hold:

(1)

p_{A_{1}}\left(\sum\limits_{e\mid s(e)\in A_{2}\cap Y}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\right)=p_{A_{1}\cap Y}\left(\sum\limits_{e\mid s(e)\in A_{2}\cap Y}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\right)
.

(2)

\left(\sum\limits_{e\mid s(e)\in A_{1}\cap Y}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\right)p_{A_{2}}=\left(\sum\limits_{e\mid s(e)\in A_{1}\cap Y}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\right)p_{A_{2}\cap Y}
.

(3)

p_{A_{1}}\left(\sum\limits_{w\mid w\in B_{2}\cap Y}q_{w}\right)=p_{A_{1}\cap Y}\left(\sum\limits_{w\mid w\in B_{2}\cap Y}q_{w}\right)
.

(4)

\left(\sum\limits_{w\mid w\in B_{1}\cap Y}q_{w}\right)p_{A_{2}}=\left(\sum\limits_{w\mid w\in B_{1}\cap Y}q_{w}\right)p_{A_{2}\cap Y}
.

(5)

\left(\sum\limits_{e\mid s(e)\in A_{1}\cap Y}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{w\in B_{2}\cap Y}q_{w}\right)=0=\left(\sum\limits_{w\in B_{1}\cap Y}q_{w}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{e\mid s(e)\in A_{2}\cap Y}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\right)
.

(6)

q_{w}q_{v}=\delta_{wv}q_{w}
, and consequently,
\left(\sum\limits_{w\in B_{1}\cap Y}q_{w}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{w\in B_{2}\cap Y}q_{w}\right)=\sum\limits_{w\in B_{1}\cap B_{2}\cap Y}q_{w}
.

Proof.

The first item holds because, for all
e\in E^{1}
such that
s(e)\in A_{2}\cap Y
, we have
p_{A_{1}}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}=p_{A_{1}\cap Y}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}
, which equals
s_{e}s_{e}^{*}
if
s(e)\in A_{1}
, and equals 0 if
s(e)\notin A_{1}
.
The second item follows analogously. The same reasoning applies to the third item:

\displaystyle p_{A_{1}\cap Y}\left(\sum\limits_{w\mid w\in B_{2}\cap Y}q_{w}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{w\mid w\in B_{2}\cap Y}\left(p_{A_{1}\cap Y}p_{w}-\sum\limits_{e\mid s(e)=w}p_{A_{1}\cap Y}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{w\mid w\in B_{2}\cap Y}\left(p_{A_{1}}p_{w}-\sum\limits_{e\mid s(e)=w}p_{A_{1}}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\right)=p_{A_{1}}\left(\sum\limits_{w\mid w\in B_{2}\cap Y}q_{w}\right).

The fourth item follows the same logic as the third. The left-hand side of the fifth item’s equality holds because

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{s(e)\in A_{1}\cap Y}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\right)\left(\sum_{w\in B_{2}\cap Y}q_{w}\right)=\left(\sum_{s(e)\in A_{1}\cap Y}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\right)\left(\sum_{w\in B_{2}\cap Y}\left(p_{w}-\sum_{s(f)=w}s_{f}s_{f}^{*}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in B_{2}\cap Y}\left[\left(\sum_{s(e)\in A_{1}\cap Y}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\right)p_{w}-\left(\sum_{s(e)\in A_{1}\cap Y}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\right)\left(\sum_{s(f)=w}s_{f}s_{f}^{*}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in B_{2}\cap Y}\left[\left(\sum_{s(e)=w\in A_{1}\cap Y}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\right)-\left(\sum_{s(e)=w\in A_{1}\cap Y}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\right)\right]=0.

and the right-hand side of the fifth item’s equality can be proved by the same computation. Finally, the last item follows from:

\displaystyle q_{w}q_{v}

\displaystyle=\left(p_{w}-\sum_{s(e)=w}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\right)\left(p_{v}-\sum_{s(f)=v}s_{f}s_{f}^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=p_{w}p_{v}-\sum_{s(f)=v}p_{w}s_{f}s_{f}^{*}-\sum_{s(e)=w}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}p_{v}+\sum_{s(e)=w}\sum_{s(f)=v}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}p_{w}p_{v}s_{f}s_{f}^{*}

\displaystyle=\delta_{wv}\left(p_{w}-\sum_{s(e)=w}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\right)=\delta_{wv}q_{w}.

∎

We are now able to prove that
\phi
has a left inverse.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},X)
be a relative ultragraph and set

Y:=\mathrm{Reg}(\mathcal{G})\setminus X
. If
r(e)\cap Y
is finite for every

e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
, then the
*
-homomorphism

\phi:C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)\to C^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{X})
from
Proposition
2.8
is injective.

Proof.

Following the notation of Lemma
2.12
, define, for all

w\in\mathrm{Reg}(\mathcal{G})=\mathrm{Reg}(\mathcal{G}_{X})
,

q_{w}\;:=\;p_{w}\;-\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(e)=w\end{subarray}}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}.

Let
Z\in\mathcal{E}_{X}^{0}
. By Corollary
2.10
, we can write
Z
uniquely as

Z=A\cup(B\cap Y)^{\prime}
where
A,B\in\mathcal{E}^{0}
and
(B\cap Y)^{\prime}
is finite. We define
\psi

on the generators of
C^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{X})
as follows:

\psi(P_{Z})\;=\;p_{A}-p_{A\cap Y}\;+\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(e)\in A\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\;+\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\mathcal{G}^{0}\\
w\in B\cap Y\end{subarray}}q_{w},

and

\psi(S_{f})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle s_{f}\!\left(p_{r(f)}-p_{r(f)\cap Y}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(f)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}\right),&\text{if }f\in\mathcal{G}^{1},\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle s_{e}\!\left(p_{r(e)\cap Y}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(e)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}\right),&\text{if }f=e^{\prime}\in C.\end{cases}

Sums over the empty set are taken to be
0
. The sums written above are finite by the hypothesis.

We claim that
\psi
is well defined and that
\psi\circ\phi=\mathrm{id}
. To see that
\psi
is
well defined, we use the universal property of
C^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{X})
; some computations are necessary:

(1)

We verify that
\psi(P_{\emptyset})=0
, that

\psi(P_{Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}})=\psi(P_{Z_{1}})\psi(P_{Z_{2}})
, and that

\psi(P_{Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}})=\psi(P_{Z_{1}})+\psi(P_{Z_{2}})-\psi(P_{Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}})
for all

Z_{1},Z_{2}\in\mathcal{E}_{X}^{0}
.

Of course
\psi(P_{\emptyset})=p_{\emptyset}=0
. Let us now prove that

\psi(P_{Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}})=\psi(P_{Z_{1}})\psi(P_{Z_{2}})
for all
Z_{1},Z_{2}\in\mathcal{E}_{X}^{0}
.
Write

Z_{1}=A_{1}\cup(B_{1}\cap Y)^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad Z_{2}=A_{2}\cup(B_{2}\cap Y)^{\prime}

as in Corollary
2.10
. Note that
\psi(P_{Z_{1}})\psi(P_{Z_{2}})
is the product of

\left(p_{A_{1}}-p_{A_{1}\cap Y}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(e)\in A_{1}\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\mathcal{G}^{0}\\
w\in B_{1}\cap Y\end{subarray}}q_{w}\right)

and

\left(p_{A_{2}}-p_{A_{2}\cap Y}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(e)\in A_{2}\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\mathcal{G}^{0}\\
w\in B_{2}\cap Y\end{subarray}}q_{w}\right).

Using the equalities provided in Lemma
2.12
, most of the products are
0
or cancel with
another term, so that

\psi(P_{Z_{1}})\psi(P_{Z_{2}})=p_{A_{1}\cap A_{2}}-p_{A_{1}\cap A_{2}\cap Y}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(e)\in A_{1}\cap A_{2}\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}+\sum_{w\in B_{1}\cap B_{2}\cap Y}q_{w}.

Thus we have the desired result; indeed, the right-hand side is exactly the definition of

\psi(P_{Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}})
, because

Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}=(A_{1}\cap A_{2})\cup(B_{1}\cap B_{2}\cap Y)^{\prime}
.

To finalize item (1), we still need to show that

\psi(P_{Z_{1}})+\psi(P_{Z_{2}})-\psi(P_{Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}})=\psi(P_{Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}})
.
This is similar to what we did above, and we leave it to the interested reader.

(2)

In this item we show that
\psi(S_{f})^{*}\psi(S_{f})=\psi(P_{r(f)})
. If
f\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
,
then
\psi(S_{f})^{*}\psi(S_{f})
equals

\left(s_{f}-s_{f}p_{r(f)\cap Y}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(f)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{f}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}\right)^{\!*}\left(s_{f}-s_{f}p_{r(f)\cap Y}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(f)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{f}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}\right).

Using that, for all
g
with
s(g)\in r(f)\cap Y
, we have

p_{r(f)\cap Y}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}=s_{g}s_{g}^{*}
and
p_{r(f)}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}=s_{g}s_{g}^{*}
, we conclude that

\psi(S_{f})^{*}\psi(S_{f})=p_{r(f)}-p_{r(f)\cap Y}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(f)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}=\psi(P_{r(f)}),

where the last equality holds because
r(f)\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{0}
.

The remaining case is when
f=e^{\prime}
for some
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
. In this situation,

r(f)=r(e^{\prime})=\emptyset\cup(r(e)\cap Y)^{\prime}
, and thus

\psi(P_{r(f)})=\psi(P_{r(e^{\prime})})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\mathcal{G}^{0}\\
w\in r(e)\cap Y\end{subarray}}q_{w}=p_{r(e)\cap Y}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(e)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{g}s_{g}^{*},

where the last equality holds because the right-hand side is the left-hand side after reordering
the sum. Moreover, a computation shows that

\displaystyle\psi(S_{f})^{*}\psi(S_{f})

\displaystyle=\left(s_{e}p_{r(e)\cap Y}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(e)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{e}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}\right)^{\!*}\left(s_{e}p_{r(e)\cap Y}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(e)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{e}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(p_{r(e)\cap Y}s_{e}^{*}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(e)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}s_{e}^{*}\right)\left(s_{e}p_{r(e)\cap Y}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(e)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{e}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=p_{r(e)\cap Y}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(e)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(e)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(e)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}

\displaystyle=p_{r(e)\cap Y}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(e)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}=\psi(P_{r(f)}).

(3)

We need to prove that, for all
f\in\mathcal{G}_{X}^{1}
,

\psi(P_{s(f)})\,\psi(S_{f})\,\psi(S_{f})^{*}\;=\;\psi(S_{f})\,\psi(S_{f})^{*}.

To start, let us compute
\psi(S_{f})\psi(S_{f})^{*}
; this computation will be useful also in the next
item. There are two possible situations:

•

Case 1
.
f\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
. Using the definition of
\psi
and performing some computations, we obtain

\displaystyle\psi(S_{f})\psi(S_{f})^{*}

\displaystyle=s_{f}s_{f}^{*}-s_{f}\,p_{r(f)\cap Y}\,s_{f}^{*}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(f)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{f}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}s_{f}^{*}

\displaystyle=s_{f}\!\left(s_{f}^{*}-p_{r(f)\cap Y}\,s_{f}^{*}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(f)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}s_{f}^{*}\right).

•

Case 2
.
f=e^{\prime}\in C
. Using the definition of
\psi
and performing some computations, we obtain

\psi(S_{e^{\prime}})\psi(S_{e^{\prime}})^{*}=s_{e}p_{r(e)\cap Y}s_{e}^{*}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(e)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{e}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}s_{e}^{*}=s_{e}\!\left(p_{r(e)\cap Y}s_{e}^{*}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(e)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}s_{e}^{*}\right)\!.

In both cases, we now compute the product
\psi(P_{s(f)})\,\psi(S_{f})\,\psi(S_{f})^{*}
. Note that, for all
f\in\mathcal{G}_{X}^{1}
,

s(f)\in\mathcal{G}^{0}
; moreover,

\psi(P_{s(f)})\;=\;\begin{cases}p_{s(f)},&\text{if }s(f)\notin Y,\\[2.0pt]
\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(e)=s(f)\end{subarray}}s_{e}s_{e}^{*},&\text{if }s(f)\in Y.\end{cases}

Thus, in each case above we need to consider two subcases. The reader can check all these
possibilities by direct computations; they are very similar and use the facts that

p_{s(f)}s_{f}=s_{f}
and

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(e)=s(f)\end{subarray}}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\,s_{f}=s_{f}
.

The three cases above can be used to check that the
\psi(S_{f})
are partial isometries and that

\psi(S_{f})\psi(S_{f})^{*}\,\psi(S_{g})\psi(S_{g})^{*}=0
if
f\neq g
. We leave this to the reader.

(4)

In this item we show that, for all
v\in\mathrm{Reg}(\mathcal{G}_{X})=\mathrm{Reg}(\mathcal{G})
,

\psi(P_{v})\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{G}_{X}^{1}\\
s(f)=v\end{subarray}}\psi(S_{f})\,\psi(S_{f})^{*}.

For notational convenience, set
O=\{\,f\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\mid r(f)\cap Y=\emptyset\,\}
and

Q=\{\,f\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\mid r(f)\cap Y\neq\emptyset\,\}
only for this item. Note that

\mathcal{G}^{1}=O\cup Q
, thus

\mathcal{G}_{X}^{1}=O\cup Q\cup C.

Using the computations made in item (3), we note that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in O\\
s(f)=v\end{subarray}}\psi(S_{f})\psi(S_{f})^{*}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in O\\
s(f)=v\end{subarray}}s_{f}s_{f}^{*},

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in Q\\
s(f)=v\end{subarray}}\psi(S_{f})\psi(S_{f})^{*}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in Q\\
s(f)=v\end{subarray}}\Big(s_{f}s_{f}^{*}-s_{f}p_{r(f)\cap Y}s_{f}^{*}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(f)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{f}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}s_{f}^{*}\Big),

and

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f^{\prime}\in C\\
s(f^{\prime})=v\end{subarray}}\psi(S_{f^{\prime}})\psi(S_{f^{\prime}})^{*}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f^{\prime}\in C\\
s(f^{\prime})=v\end{subarray}}\Big(s_{f}p_{r(f)\cap Y}s_{f}^{*}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(f)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{f}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}s_{f}^{*}\Big).

Using that
s(f^{\prime})=s(f)
for all
f\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
with
r(f)\cap Y\neq\emptyset
, we can sum
the three equalities above to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{G}_{X}^{1}\\
s(f)=v\end{subarray}}\psi(S_{f})\psi(S_{f})^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in O\\
s(f)=v\end{subarray}}s_{f}s_{f}^{*}\;+\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in Q\\
s(f)=v\end{subarray}}s_{f}s_{f}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(f)=v\end{subarray}}s_{f}s_{f}^{*}.

Since
v\in\mathcal{G}^{0}
and
\{v\}=\{v\}\cup\emptyset
, we have two options:
v\notin Y
or

v\in Y
. In the first case,
\psi(P_{v})=p_{v}
and, since
v\notin Y
(i.e.,
v\in X
),

p_{v}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(f)=v\end{subarray}}s_{f}s_{f}^{*}
. In the second case, when

v\in Y
, we have
\psi(P_{v})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(f)=v\end{subarray}}s_{f}s_{f}^{*}
.
In both cases we obtain the desired result. With these four items, we conclude that
\psi
extends
to a well-defined homomorphism.

To conclude the theorem, it remains to check that
\psi\circ\phi=\mathrm{id}_{C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)}
.
First, for all
v\in\mathcal{G}^{0}
we have
\psi\circ\phi(p_{v})=p_{v}
; this is immediate if

v\notin Y
, because in this case
\psi\circ\phi(p_{v})=\psi(P_{v})=p_{v}
. If
v\in Y
, then

\psi\circ\phi(p_{v})=\psi(P_{v}+P_{v^{\prime}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(e)=v\end{subarray}}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\;+\;\left(p_{v}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(e)=v\end{subarray}}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\right)=p_{v}.

Next, for all
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
,

\displaystyle\psi\circ\phi(p_{r(e)})

\displaystyle=\psi\big(P_{r(e)}+P_{(r(e)\cap Y)^{\prime}}\big)

\displaystyle=p_{r(e)}-p_{r(e)\cap Y}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(e)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}+\sum_{w\in r(e)\cap Y}q_{w}

\displaystyle=p_{r(e)}-p_{r(e)\cap Y}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(e)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}+p_{r(e)\cap Y}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(e)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}

\displaystyle=p_{r(e)}.

Finally, let
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
. If
r(e)\cap Y=\emptyset
, then

\psi\circ\phi(s_{e})=\psi(S_{e})=s_{e}.

If
r(e)\cap Y\neq\emptyset
, then

\displaystyle\psi\circ\phi(s_{e})

\displaystyle=\psi(S_{e}+S_{e^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=s_{e}-s_{e}p_{r(e)\cap Y}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(e)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{e}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}\;+\;s_{e}p_{r(e)\cap Y}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(e)\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{e}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}

\displaystyle=s_{e}.

Since
\psi\circ\phi=\mathrm{id}_{C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)}
on the set of generators of

C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)
, the result follows.

∎

3.
Uniqueness theorems for relative ultragraph
C^{*}
-algebras.

In this section we establish uniqueness theorems for relative ultragraph
C^{*}
-algebras.
To this end, we analyze the relationship between cycles in
\mathcal{G}
and in the associated
ultragraph
\mathcal{G}_{X}
, and introduce the notion of
Relative Condition (L)
adapted to
the relative setting. We then combine these ingredients with classical results for ultragraph

C^{*}
-algebras and the isomorphism of
Theorem
2.14
to obtain our uniqueness theorems.

Recall that a
cycle
in an ultragraph
\mathcal{G}
is a finite path

\alpha=\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n}
, where each
\alpha_{i}
is an edge, such that

s(\alpha_{i+1})\in r(\alpha_{i})
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
and

s(\alpha_{1})\in r(\alpha_{n})
. Following
[
28
]
, an
exit
for a cycle
\alpha
is
either a sink in
r(\alpha_{i})
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, or an edge
e
satisfying one of
the following:

•

s(e)\in r(\alpha_{i})
and
e\neq\alpha_{i+1}
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
;

•

s(e)\in r(\alpha_{n})
and
e\neq\alpha_{1}
.

An ultragraph is said to satisfy
Condition (L)
if every cycle has an exit.

Remark 3.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},X)
be a relative ultragraph, and let
\mathcal{G}_{X}
be the associated
ultragraph as in Definition
2.5
. Note that all vertices added to
\mathcal{G}
to construct

\mathcal{G}_{X}
are sinks. Consequently, the sets of cycles in
\mathcal{G}
and in

\mathcal{G}_{X}
coincide.

It follows from Remark
3.1
that if
\mathcal{G}
satisfies Condition (L), then so
does
\mathcal{G}_{X}
. The converse, however, does not hold, as illustrated in the following
example:

.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be the graph with two vertices and two edges as follows:

Note that
\mathrm{Reg}(\mathcal{G})=\{u,v\}
and set
X=\{u\}
. Following
Definition
2.5
, we obtain the associated ultragraph

:

Thus
\mathcal{G}
does not satisfy Condition (L), whereas the ultragraph
\mathcal{G}_{X}

does satisfy Condition (L).

The notion of Relative Condition (L) for graphs was introduced in
[
5
]
.
We now extend this concept to the setting of ultragraphs.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},X)
be a relative ultragraph and set
Y:=\mathrm{Reg}(\mathcal{G})\setminus X
.
We say that
\mathcal{G}
satisfies the

if, for every cycle without

\mathcal{G}
, there exists an edge

in the cycle such that

Remark 3.4
.

If
\alpha=\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n}
is a cycle without exits in an ultragraph
\mathcal{G}

then for each
i
the range
r(\alpha_{i})
is a singleton. Consequently, in the definition above
we may equivalently write

instead of
r(e)\cap Y\neq\emptyset

Next, we relate exits in a relative ultragraph
(\mathcal{G},X)
with exits in the ultragraph

\mathcal{G}_{X}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},X)
be a relative ultragraph, let

,
and let
\mathcal{G}_{X}
be the associated ultragraph as in Definition
2.5
.

(1)

A cycle

has no exit in

if and only if

\mathcal{G}
and
r(\alpha_{i})\cap Y\neq\emptyset

(2)

The ultragraph

Proof.

The proof of the first item follows directly from the definition of the ultragraph
\mathcal{G}_{X}
,

Before proving the first uniqueness theorem, we present an auxiliary result.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},X)
be a relative ultragraph and set
Y=\mathrm{Reg}(\mathcal{G})\setminus X
. Suppose that

r(e)\cap Y
is finite for all edges
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
. If
Z=A\cup(B\cap Y)^{\prime}
, as in
Corollary
2.10
, then
p_{A}-p_{A\cap Y}
,
s_{e}s_{e}^{*}
with
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
, and

q_{v}
with
v\in B\cap Y
, are mutually orthogonal projections.

Proof.

By a straightforward computation one sees that
p_{A}-p_{A\cap Y}
and
s_{e}s_{e}^{*}
are projections.
Of course
s_{e}s_{e}^{*}s_{f}s_{f}^{*}=\delta_{ef}\,s_{e}s_{e}^{*}
. We proved that

q_{v}q_{w}=\delta_{vw}\,q_{v}
in Lemma
2.12
.

Note that, for all
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
with
s(e)\in A\cap Y
, we have

p_{A}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}=s_{e}s_{e}^{*}
and
p_{A\cap Y}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}=s_{e}s_{e}^{*}
, showing that

\bigl(p_{A}-p_{A\cap Y}\bigr)s_{e}s_{e}^{*}=0.

Also, let
v\in B\cap Y
. Then

p_{A}\!\left(p_{v}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(f)=v\end{subarray}}s_{f}s_{f}^{*}\right)=\begin{cases}p_{v}-\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(f)=v\end{subarray}}s_{f}s_{f}^{*},&\text{if }v\in A,\\[10.0pt]
0,&\text{if }v\notin A,\end{cases}=p_{A\cap Y}\!\left(p_{v}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(f)=v\end{subarray}}s_{f}s_{f}^{*}\right),

showing that

(p_{A}-p_{A\cap Y})\,q_{v}=(p_{A}-p_{A\cap Y})\!\left(p_{v}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(f)=v\end{subarray}}s_{f}s_{f}^{*}\right)=0.

Finally, if
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
is such that
s(e)\in A\cap Y
and
v\in B\cap Y
, then

s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\!\left(p_{v}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(f)=v\end{subarray}}s_{f}s_{f}^{*}\right)=0.

Indeed, if
v\neq s(e)
this is clear. If
v=s(e)
, the left-hand side equals

s_{e}s_{e}^{*}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(f)=v\end{subarray}}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}s_{f}s_{f}^{*}=s_{e}s_{e}^{*}-s_{e}s_{e}^{*}=0.

∎

The following remark will be used in the next theorem.

Remark 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be
C^{*}
-algebras and let

\Phi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a
*
-homomorphism. If

are mutually
orthogonal projections in

and
\Phi(p_{i})\neq 0
for at least one

, then

0\neq\Phi(p_{i})=\Phi(p_{i})\,\Phi(p_{1}+p_{2}+\cdots+p_{N}),

and so
\Phi(p_{1}+p_{2}+\cdots+p_{N})\neq 0
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},X)
be a relative ultragraph and set
Y=\mathrm{Reg}(\mathcal{G})\setminus X
,
with
Y\cap r(e)
finite for each edge
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
. Let
\mathcal{B}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra
and let
\Phi:C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)\to\mathcal{B}
be a
*
-homomorphism. If

(1)

\Phi(p_{v})\neq 0
for all
v\notin Y
;

(2)

for each
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
with
s(e)\in Y
we have
\Phi(s_{e}s_{e}^{*})\neq 0
;

(3)

for all
v\in Y
,
\Phi(q_{v})\neq 0
(where
q_{v}
is as in Lemma
2.12
),

then
\Phi\circ\psi(P_{Z})\neq 0
for every nonempty
Z\in\mathcal{E}_{X}
, where
\psi
is the
isomorphism obtained in Theorem
2.14
. Moreover, if

\mathcal{G}
satisfies the Relative Condition (L), then
\Phi
is injective.

Proof.

Using the definition of
\psi
and writing
Z=A\cup(B\cap Y)^{\prime}
, we obtain

\Phi\circ\psi(P_{Z})=\Phi\!\left(p_{A}-p_{A\cap Y}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(e)\in A\cap Y\end{subarray}}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}+\sum_{v\in B\cap Y}q_{v}\right).

We will show that
\Phi\circ\psi(P_{Z})\neq 0
whenever
Z
is non-empty.

•

If
A\neq\emptyset
and
A\cap Y=\emptyset
, then
p_{A}-p_{A\cap Y}=p_{A}
. Note that
\Phi(p_{A})\neq 0
, because if
v\in A
then

0\neq\Phi(p_{v})=\Phi(p_{v}p_{A})=\Phi(p_{v})\,\Phi(p_{A}),

hence
\Phi(p_{A})\neq 0
, and thus
\Phi(p_{A}-p_{A\cap Y})=\Phi(p_{A})\neq 0
.

•

If
A\neq\emptyset
and
A\cap Y\neq\emptyset
, choose

. Since

, pick

with

\Phi(s_{e}s_{e}^{*})\neq 0
.

•

If
A=\emptyset
, then

, so there exists

with
\Phi(q_{v})\neq 0
.

In each of the three situations, Lemma
3.6
and Remark

imply that

\Phi\circ\psi(P_{Z})\neq 0
for every non-empty
Z\in\mathcal{E}_{X}
. Moreover, if

\mathcal{G}
satisfies the Relative Condition (L), then by
Lemma
3.5
we have that
\mathcal{G}_{X}
satisfies Condition (L).
Hence the hypotheses of
[
28

are satisfied, showing that
\Phi\circ\psi

is injective. Since
\psi
is an isomorphism, it follows that
\Phi
is injective.
∎

The next theorem fully characterizes the injectivity of
*
-homomorphisms of relative ultragraph

-algebras.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},X)
be a relative ultragraph and set
Y=\mathrm{Reg}(\mathcal{G})\setminus X
,
with
Y\cap r(e)
finite for each edge
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
. Let
\mathcal{B}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra
and let
\Phi:C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)\to\mathcal{B}
be a
*
-homomorphism. Then
\Phi
is injective
if and only if the following conditions hold:

(1)

\Phi(p_{v})\neq 0
for all
v\notin Y
;

(2)

for each
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
with
s(e)\in Y
we have
\Phi(s_{e}s_{e}^{*})\neq 0
;

(3)

for all
v\in Y
,
\Phi(q_{v})\neq 0
(where
q_{v}
is as in Lemma
2.12
);

(4)

for any simple cycle
\alpha=\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{n}

\mathcal{G}
and

for all

, the spectrum of
\Phi(s_{\alpha})

contains the unit circle.

Proof.

Suppose that
\Phi
is injective. Since
p_{A}
(for all
A\in\mathcal{E}
) and
s_{e}s_{e}^{*}
(for

e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
) are nonzero elements, items (1) and (2) follow. Item (3) follows because

\Phi\circ\psi
is injective (where
\psi
is as in

2.14
) and

, hence

Moreover, since
\Phi\circ\psi
is injective, we may apply
[
21
, Theorem 7.4]
to deduce
that the
spectrum
of
\Phi\circ\psi(S_{\alpha})
contains the unit circle whenever

\alpha
is a simple cycle with no exit in
\mathcal{G}
and
r(\alpha_{i})\notin Y
for all

.
By the definition of
\psi
,

\Phi\circ\psi(S_{\alpha})=\Phi\circ\psi(S_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots S_{\alpha_{n}})=\Phi(s_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots s_{\alpha_{n}})=\Phi(s_{\alpha}),

so item (4) holds.

For the converse, suppose that the four conditions hold. By

3.8

\Phi\circ\psi(P_{Z})\neq 0
for all

. If
\alpha
is a cycle with no

\mathcal{G}_{X}

4.
Relative branching systems and properties of
C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)
.

Our main goal in this section is to relate the theory of branching systems for ultragraphs—which
yields representations of
C^{*}(\mathcal{G})
(see
[
21
]
)—to a parallel theory, which we call

relative branching systems
, that yields representations of
C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)
. This
connection is mediated by the isomorphism of
Theorem
2.14
; in particular, the uniqueness theorems
proved earlier admit corresponding formulations in the setting of representations arising from relative branching systems.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},X)
be a relative ultragraph, let
(\mathcal{X},\mu)
be a measure space, and
let
\{R_{e},D_{A}\}_{e\in\mathcal{G}^{1},\;A\in\mathcal{E}}
be a family of measurable subsets of

\mathcal{X}
. Suppose that

(1)

R_{e}\cap R_{f}\overset{\mu\text{-a.e.}}{=}\emptyset
if
e\neq f\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
.

(2)

D_{\emptyset}=\emptyset
,
D_{A}\cap D_{B}\overset{\mu\text{-a.e.}}{=}D_{A\cap B}
,
D_{A}\cup D_{B}\overset{\mu\text{-a.e.}}{=}D_{A\cup B}
for all
A,B\in\mathcal{E}
.

(3)

R_{e}\overset{\mu\text{-a.e.}}{\subseteq}D_{s(e)}
for all
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
.

(4)

D_{v}\overset{\mu\text{-a.e.}}{=}\displaystyle\bigcup_{e\in s^{-1}(v)}R_{e}
for all
v\in X
.

(5)

For each
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
, there exist measurable maps

f_{e}:D_{r(e)}\to R_{e}
and
f_{e}^{-1}:R_{e}\to D_{r(e)}
such that

f_{e}\circ f_{e}^{-1}\overset{\mu\text{-a.e.}}{=}\mathrm{id}_{R_{e}}
and

f_{e}^{-1}\circ f_{e}\overset{\mu\text{-a.e.}}{=}\mathrm{id}_{D_{r(e)}}
; moreover

\mu\circ f_{e}\ll\mu
on
D_{r(e)}
and
\mu\circ f_{e}^{-1}\ll\mu
on
R_{e}
.

Denote the Radon–Nikodym derivatives by

\Phi_{f_{e}}\;:=\;\frac{d(\mu\circ f_{e})}{d\mu}\ \text{ on }D_{r(e)},\qquad\Phi_{f_{e}^{-1}}\;:=\;\frac{d(\mu\circ f_{e}^{-1})}{d\mu}\ \text{ on }R_{e}.

We call
\{R_{e},D_{A},f_{e}\}_{e\in\mathcal{G}^{1},\;A\in\mathcal{E}}
a

relative
(\mathcal{G},X)
-branching system
on
(\mathcal{X},\mu)
.

Remark 4.2
.

If
X=\mathrm{Reg}(\mathcal{G})
in Definition
4.1
, we obtain a

\mathcal{G}
-branching system as in
[
21
]
. The only difference from the original definition
is that condition (
4
) is required to hold only for the selected
set of regular vertices
X
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an ultragraph, and let

\{R_{e},D_{A},f_{e}\}_{e\in\mathcal{G}^{1},\,A\in\mathcal{E}}
be a

\mathcal{G}
-branching system on
(\mathcal{X},\mu)
.
Since the domains of
\Phi_{f_{e}^{-1}}
and
f_{e}^{-1}
both equal
R_{e}
, we may regard them as
measurable maps on
\mathcal{X}
by extending them to be
0
outside
R_{e}
. Thus, for each

\phi\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathcal{X},\mu)
, we can consider the function

\Phi_{f_{e}^{-1}}^{1/2}\,(\phi\circ f_{e}^{-1})\,\chi_{R_{e}}.

Similarly, extending
f_{e}
and
\Phi_{f_{e}}
by
0
outside
D_{r(e)}
, we obtain the function

\Phi_{f_{e}}^{1/2}\,(\phi\circ f_{e})\,\chi_{D_{r(e)}}.

The following theorem, proved in
[
21
]
, shows how a branching system yields a representation.

Theorem 4.3

(
[
21
]
, Theorem 4.1)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an ultragraph and let

\{R_{e},D_{A},f_{e}\}_{e\in\mathcal{G}^{1},\,A\in\mathcal{E}}
be a

\mathcal{G}
-branching system on a measure space
(\mathcal{X},\mu)
. Then there exists a unique
representation
\pi:C^{*}(\mathcal{G})\to B(\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathcal{X},\mu))
such that, for all

,
A\in\mathcal{E}
, and
\phi\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathcal{X},\mu)
,

\pi(s_{e})(\phi)=\Phi_{f_{e}^{-1}}^{1/2}\,(\phi\circ f_{e}^{-1})\,\chi_{R_{e}},\qquad\pi(p_{A})(\phi)=\chi_{D_{A}}\,\phi.

An identical proof shows that a relative
(\mathcal{G},X)
-branching system induces a
representation of the relative ultragraph algebra
C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)
defined in the previous
section. For future reference we state the result:

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},X)
be a relative ultragraph, and let

,

, and

.

By standard computations (see

,

,

Let
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{G}^{0},\mathcal{G}^{1},r,s)

In what follows we show how to produce a branching system for the ultragraph algebra

C^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{X})
starting from a
(\mathcal{G},X)
-relative branching system. Throughout this

.

Let
\{R_{e},D_{A},f_{e}\}_{e\in\mathcal{G}^{1},\,A\in\mathcal{E}}
be a relative

(\mathcal{G},X)
-branching system on
(\mathcal{X},\mu)
. Recall from

2.9
that if
Z\in\mathcal{E}_{X}
then
Z=A\cup(B\cap Y)^{\prime}
with

A,B\in\mathcal{E}
. Using this, for each
Z\in\mathcal{E}_{X}
define

B_{Z}\;:=\;\bigl(D_{A}\setminus D_{A\cap Y}\bigr)\;\cup\;\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in A\cap Y\end{subarray}}R_{g}\;\cup\;\bigcup_{v\in B\cap Y}\left(D_{v}\setminus\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)=v\end{subarray}}R_{g}\right).

Recall also that
\mathcal{G}_{X}^{1}=\mathcal{G}^{1}\cup C
, where

C\;=\;\{\,e^{\prime}\mid e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\text{ and }r(e)\cap Y\neq\emptyset\,\}.

Define, for
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
,

Q_{e}\;:=\;\Bigl(f_{e}\bigl(D_{r(e)}\bigr)\setminus f_{e}\bigl(D_{r(e)\cap Y}\bigr)\Bigr)\;\cup\;f_{e}\!\left(\,\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)\in r(e)\cap Y\end{subarray}}R_{g}\right),

and, for

,

Q_{e^{\prime}}\;:=\;f_{e}\!\left(\;\bigcup_{v\in r(e)\cap Y}\left(D_{v}\setminus\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(g)=v\end{subarray}}R_{g}\right)\right).

Note that

as the restrictions of
f_{e}
to
B_{r(e)}
and
B_{r(e^{\prime})}
, respectively. By construction,

g_{e}\bigl(B_{r(e)}\bigr)=f_{e}\bigl(B_{r(e)}\bigr)=Q_{e},\qquad g_{e^{\prime}}\bigl(B_{r(e^{\prime})}\bigr)=f_{e}\bigl(B_{r(e^{\prime})}\bigr)=Q_{e^{\prime}},

and consequently the inverse maps
g_{e}^{-1}
and
g_{e^{\prime}}^{-1}
are also well defined.

lemma.

Proposition 4.7
.

-branching system on

Proof.

We will check the conditions of Definition
4.1
.

(1)

of the left side of the above intersection, while

of the right side. Using that

(2)

It is not hard to see that

\displaystyle\bigcup\limits_{e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\ |\ s(e)=v}R_{e}

from the definition of relative branching system (since

(5)

was defined as

We now relate the representations arising from a relative branching system and from its

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},X)
be a relative ultragraph and set
Y=\mathrm{Reg}(\mathcal{G})\setminus X
,
with
r(e)\cap Y
finite for all
e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
. Let

\{R_{e},D_{A},f_{e}\}_{e\in\mathcal{G}^{1},\,A\in\mathcal{E}}
be a
(\mathcal{G},X)
-relative
branching system on a measure space
(\mathcal{X},\mu)
, and consider the

\mathcal{G}_{X}
-branching system
\{Q_{f},B_{Z},g_{f}\}_{f\in\mathcal{G}_{X}^{1},\,Z\in\mathcal{E}_{X}}

on
(\mathcal{X},\mu)
constructed above. Let
\eta
be the representation induced by

\{R_{e},D_{A},f_{e}\}
via Proposition
4.4
, and let
\pi
be the
representation induced by
\{Q_{f},B_{Z},g_{f}\}
via Theorem
4.3
.
Then
\pi=\eta\circ\psi
, where
\psi
is the isomorphism given in
Theorem
2.14
.

Proof.

Let
Z\in\mathcal{E}_{X}
and write
Z=A\cup(B\cap Y)^{\prime}
via Lemma
2.9
. Note that for all
F\in L^{2}(\mathcal{X},\mu)
we have

\displaystyle(\eta\circ\psi)(P_{Z})(F)=\eta\left(p_{A}-p_{A\cap Y}+\sum\limits_{g\ |\ s(g)\in A\cap Y}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}+\sum\limits_{v\in B\cap Y}q_{v}\right)(F)

\displaystyle=\chi_{D_{A}}\cdot F-\chi_{D_{A\cap Y}}\cdot F+\sum\limits_{g\ |\ s(g)\in A\cap Y}\chi_{R_{g}}\cdot F+\sum\limits_{v\in B\cap Y}\left(\chi_{D_{v}}\cdot F-\sum\limits_{g\ |\ s(g)=v}\chi_{R_{g}}\cdot F\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\chi_{D_{A}}-\chi_{D_{A\cap Y}}+\chi_{\left(\bigcup\limits_{g\ |\ s(g)\in A\cap Y}R_{g}\right)}+\chi_{\left(\bigcup\limits_{\ v\in B\cap Y}(D_{v}\setminus\cup_{\{g\ |\ s(g)=v\}}R_{g})\right)}\right)\cdot F=\pi(P_{Z})(F)

where we used that
\eta(s_{g}s_{g}^{*})(F)=\chi_{R_{g}}\cdot F
. Also the fact that
R_{g}\cap R_{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu-a.e.}}{{=}}\emptyset
if
g\neq h
and
D_{v}\cap D_{w}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu-a.e.}}{{=}}\emptyset
if
v\neq w
is crucial to write the sums of characteristic functions as the characteristic function of a union. The last equality follows from the definition of
B_{Z}
, because
\pi(P_{Z})(F)=\chi_{B_{Z}}\cdot F
.

We now check that
\pi(S_{f})=(\eta\circ\psi)(S_{f})
for
f\in\mathcal{G}_{X}^{1}
.. First, let
f=e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
and fix an
F\in L^{2}(\mathcal{X},\mu)
. Then

\displaystyle(\eta\circ\psi)(S_{e})(F)=\eta\left(s_{e}-s_{e}p_{r(e)\cap Y}+\sum\limits_{g\ |\ s(g)\in r(e)\cap Y}s_{e}s_{g}s_{g}^{*}\right)(F)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{f_{e}^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot(F\circ f_{e}^{-1})-\Phi_{f_{e}^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\left(\chi_{f_{e}(D_{r(e)\cap Y})}\cdot F\circ f_{e}^{-1}\right)+\Phi_{f_{e}^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\left(\chi_{f_{e}\left(\bigcup\limits_{g\ |\ s(g)\in r(e)\cap Y}R_{g}\right)}\cdot F\circ f_{e}^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{f_{e}^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot(F\circ f_{e}^{-1})\cdot\left(\chi_{R_{e}}-\chi_{f_{e}(D_{r(e)\cap Y})}+\chi_{f_{e}\left(\bigcup\limits_{g\ |\ s(g)\in r(e)\cap Y}R_{g}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{f_{e}^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot(F\circ f_{e}^{-1})\cdot\left(\chi_{f_{e}(D_{r(e)})}-\chi_{f_{e}(D_{r(e)\cap Y})}+\chi_{f_{e}\left(\bigcup\limits_{g\ |\ s(g)\in r(e)\cap Y}R_{g}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{f_{e}^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot(F\circ f_{e}^{-1})\cdot\chi_{Q_{e}}=\pi(S_{e})(F).

Hence, for
f=e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
we have
\pi(S_{f})=(\eta\circ\psi)(S_{f})
.

Therefore,
\pi=\eta\circ\psi
on the generators of
C^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{X})
, which proves the theorem.

Proposition 4.9

(2)

\eta(s_{e}s_{e}^{*})(F)=\chi_{R_{e}}\,F

;

c)

D_{v}\setminus\displaystyle\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
s(e)=v\end{subarray}}R_{e}\neq\emptyset

for all
v\in Y
;

d)

for any simple cycle
\alpha=(\alpha_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
without exits such that

r(\alpha_{i})\notin Y
for all
i=1,\ldots,N
, and for any finite subset

\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, there exists
x\in D_{s(\alpha)}
with

f_{n\alpha}(x)\neq x
for all
n\in\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

If conditions
a)-d)
above holds, then choosing
E=\{x\}
in condition
(4)
of Proposition
4.10
we see that
\eta
is injective.

If
\eta
is injective, then
(a)-(c)
holds again by Proposition
4.10
. So, is enough to prove that
(d)
holds. Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
be a simple cycle without exits such that
r(\alpha_{i})\notin Y
for all
i=1,\ldots,N
and for any finite subset
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
.

If there exists
x\in D_{s(\alpha)}
such that
f_{n\alpha}(x)\neq x
for all
n\in\mathcal{F}
we are done. If such an element
x\in D_{s(\alpha)}
does not exists we will produce a contradiction. In fact, if that is the case then for all
x\in D_{s(\alpha)}
there exists
n_{x}\in\mathcal{F}
such that
f_{n_{x}\alpha}(x)=x
. As
\mathcal{F}
is finite we can consider
t:={\displaystyle\prod_{n\ |\ n\in\mathcal{F}}n}
; so the for all
x\in D_{s(\alpha)}
, the number
t
is a multiple of
n_{x}
and consequently

f_{t\alpha}(x)=\underbrace{f_{\alpha}\circ f_{\alpha}\circ\ldots\circ f_{\alpha}}_{t\,\ \textrm{times }}(x)=\underbrace{f_{\alpha}\circ\ldots\circ f_{\alpha}}_{n_{x}\,\ \textrm{ times }}\circ\ldots\circ\underbrace{f_{\alpha}\ldots\circ f_{\alpha}}_{n_{x}\,\ \textrm{times }}(x)=x

for all
x\in D_{s(\alpha)}
showing that
f_{t\alpha}=Id
in
D_{s(\alpha})
. Now, as
\mu
is the counting measure, the Radon-Nikodym derivatives that appears in the branching system are the constant functions
1
and thus the representation
\eta
satisfies:

\eta(s_{t\alpha})(F)=\chi_{D_{s(\alpha)}}\cdot F\circ f_{t\alpha}^{-1}=\chi_{D_{s(\alpha)}}\cdot F\,\ \,\ \,\ \forall F\in L^{2}(\mu);

\eta(p_{s(\alpha})(F)=\chi_{D_{s(\alpha)}}\cdot F\,\ \,\ \,\ \forall F\in L^{2}(\mu).

Therefore
\eta(s_{t\alpha})=\eta(p_{s(\alpha)})
which contradicts the injectivity of
\eta
.
∎

5.
Markov interval maps with escape sets and associated representations

In this section, we introduce infinite partition Markov interval maps with escape sets and show how they induce representations of relative ultragraph algebras.

For a set
I
we denote by
\mathring{I}
its interior.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
I=[a,b)\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an interval, where
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
.
We say that a map
g
belongs to the
Markov class of
I
, denoted by
M(I)
, if:

(1)

There exists a sequence of closed intervals
(I_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\max(I_{j})\leq\min(I_{j+1}),\quad|I_{i}\cap I_{j}|\leq 1\ \text{for all }i,j\in\mathbb{N},

\operatorname{dom}(g)=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}I_{n}\subseteq I,\quad\min(I_{1})=\min(I),\quad\text{and }\sup(I_{n})\to b\ \text{as }n\to\infty.

(2)

The restriction
g_{|\mathring{I_{i}}}
is injective,
\operatorname{Im}(g)\subseteq I
, and

g(\Gamma)\subseteq\Gamma,\quad\text{where }\Gamma=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\partial I_{n}.

(3)

For all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, the set

g(I_{i})\cap\left(\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}I_{n}\right)

is a non-empty union of intervals of the form
I_{n}
.

Remark 5.2
.

From Condition (3) it follows that
g(I_{i})\cap I_{j}
is either empty, consists of a single point in
\Gamma
, or equals
I_{j}
.
Moreover,
g(I_{i})\cap\mathring{I_{j}}
is either empty or equal to
\mathring{I_{j}}
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
.

Remark 5.3
.

If
g:I\to I
is a strictly monotone continuous function satisfying Conditions (1) and (3),
then Condition (2) is automatically fulfilled.
Some authors define Markov maps in different contexts under this assumption,
often together with additional regularity requirements (see
[
23
]
and
[
3
]
).
We note that our definition differs slightly from those appearing in

[
9
]
,
[
10
]
, and
[
11
]
.

We denote by
g_{i}
the restriction of
g
to
I_{i}
.
By condition (1) above, the interval
I
admits the following geometric description:

a
b
I_{1}
I_{2}
I_{3}
I_{4}
E_{1}
E_{2}
\cdots
\cdots

To make this picture precise, we write

I_{1}=[c_{0},c_{1}^{-}]\quad\text{and}\quad I_{n}=[c_{n-1}^{+},c_{n}^{-}]\quad\text{for each }n\in\mathbb{N}.

We then define the
escape sets

E_{n}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
by

E_{n}:=(c_{n}^{-},c_{n}^{+}).

Note that
E_{n}
may be empty for some values of
n
; this occurs precisely when

|I_{n}\cap I_{n+1}|=1
.
By construction, we have

I=\left(\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}I_{n}\right)\cup\left(\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}E_{n}\right).

If
g
belongs to the Markov class of
I
, we associate to
g
the matrix

A_{g}:=(A_{ij})_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}
, where

A_{ij}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\mathring{I_{j}}\subseteq g(\mathring{I_{i}}),\\[4.0pt]
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}_{g}
be the ultragraph induced by the matrix
A_{g}
,
that is,

\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{G}^{0},\mathcal{G}^{1},r,s),

where

\mathcal{G}^{0}=\{v_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\},\quad\mathcal{G}^{1}=\{e_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\},

s(e_{i})=v_{i},\quad r(e_{i})=\{v_{j}\mid A_{ij}=1\}=\{v_{j}\mid\mathring{I_{j}}\subseteq g(\mathring{I_{i}})\}.

The set

E_{g}:=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}g^{-k}\left(\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}E_{j}\right)

will play a crucial role in the next sections.
Note that an element
x\in\operatorname{dom}(g)
belongs to
E_{g}
if and only if there exists

K\in\mathbb{N}
such that
g^{K}(x)\notin\operatorname{dom}(g)
.

From now until the end of this chapter, let
g
be a fixed Markov map and
let
x\in E_{g}
be a fixed element.
We also write
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}_{g}
for the ultragraph induced by
g
,
as defined above.

Since
x\in E_{g}
, there exists a unique natural number
\tau(x)
such that

g^{\tau(x)-1}(x)\in\operatorname{dom}(g)\quad\text{and}\quad g^{\tau(x)}(x)\notin\operatorname{dom}(g),

where by convention
g^{0}(x)=x
.
We denote by
J=J_{x}
the unique index such that

g^{\tau(x)}(x)\in E_{J}
, and define

R_{g}(x):=\{y\in\operatorname{dom}(g)\mid g^{n}(y)=g^{\tau(x)}(x)\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

In the next lemma, we show that points in
R_{g}(x)
cannot lie on the boundaries of the partition intervals, but must instead belong to their interiors. Moreover, we show that boundary points of
I_{j}
never map into the interior of another interval.

Lemma 5.4
.

The following assertions hold:

(1)

If
y\in R_{g}(x)
, then

y\in\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathring{I_{n}}.

(2)

If
\mathring{I_{k}}\subseteq g(I_{j})
, then

\mathring{I_{k}}\subseteq g(\mathring{I_{j}}).

Proof.

(1)

By hypothesis, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

g^{n}(y)=g^{\tau(x)}(x)\in E_{J_{x}}=E_{J}.

Since
\Gamma\cap E_{J}=\emptyset
, we conclude that

g^{n}(y)\notin\Gamma
.
By Condition (2) of Definition
5.1
, we know that

g(\Gamma)\subseteq\Gamma
, and therefore
g^{n}(\Gamma)\subseteq\Gamma
.
Hence, if
y\in\Gamma
, then
g^{n}(y)\in\Gamma
, a contradiction.
Thus
y\notin\Gamma
, which proves the claim.

(2)

Suppose
x\in\mathring{I_{k}}
.
By hypothesis,
x=g(x_{1})
for some
x_{1}\in I_{j}
.
If
x_{1}\in\partial I_{j}\subseteq\Gamma
, then
x=g(x_{1})\in\Gamma
,
since
g(\Gamma)\subseteq\Gamma
.
But this contradicts the fact that

\mathring{I_{k}}\cap\Gamma=\emptyset
.
Therefore
x_{1}\in\mathring{I_{j}}
, and the result follows.

∎

Our goal in the following pages is to construct representations of certain
relative ultragraph algebras associated with
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}_{g}

using the Markov maps.
We will build such representations in the
C^{*}
-algebra
B(\ell^{2}(R_{g}(x)))
.
Recall that
\ell^{2}(R_{g}(x))
is the space of square-summable functions

f:R_{g}(x)\to\mathbb{C}
with respect to the counting measure.
It has a canonical orthonormal basis given by the characteristic functions

\delta_{y}
for
y\in R_{g}(x)
.

For each
B\in\mathcal{E}
and
i\in\mathbb{N}
we define the following
linear operators on the Hilbert basis of
\ell^{2}(R_{g}(x))
:

W_{B}(\delta_{y})=\chi_{\left(\bigcup\limits_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in B\}}I_{j}\right)}(y)\,\delta_{y},

T_{e_{i}}(\delta_{y})=\chi_{\left(\bigcup\limits_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}I_{j}\right)}(y)\,\delta_{g_{i}^{-1}(y)}.

It is straightforward to see that
W_{B}
is well defined and extends to a
bounded linear operator on
\ell^{2}(R_{g}(x))
.
To justify that
T_{e_{i}}
is well defined, observe that if

y\notin\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}I_{j}
,
then
T_{e_{i}}(\delta_{y})=0
.
Suppose instead that

y\in\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}I_{j}
.
Then there exists
j\in\mathbb{N}
with
v_{j}\in r(e_{i})
and
y\in I_{j}
.
Consequently,
\mathring{I_{j}}\subseteq g(\mathring{I_{i}})
.
Since
y\in R_{g}(x)
, Lemma
5.4
implies
y\in\mathring{I_{j}}
.
Thus
y=g(y_{i})
for a unique
y_{i}\in\mathring{I_{i}}
, because
g_{i}
is injective.
It follows that
g_{i}^{-1}(y)=y_{i}
is well defined.
Moreover, since
y\in R_{g}(x)
, the same holds for
g_{i}^{-1}(y)
, and hence

T_{e_{i}}
extends to a bounded operator on
\ell^{2}(R_{g}(x))
.

Proposition 5.5
.

If
B\in\mathcal{E}
and
i\in\mathbb{N}
, then
W_{B}^{*}=W_{B}
,
W_{B}^{2}=W_{B},

and for all
y\in R_{g}(x)
,

T_{e_{i}}^{*}(\delta_{y})=\chi_{\left(\bigcup\limits_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}I_{j}\right)}(g(y))\,\chi_{I_{i}}(y)\,\delta_{g(y)}.

Proof.

It is clear that
W_{B}^{2}=W_{B}
.
Let
y_{1},y_{2}\in R_{g}(x)
.
A direct computation shows that

\int\chi_{\left(\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in B\}}I_{j}\right)}(y_{1})\,\delta_{y_{1}}\,\delta_{y_{2}}\,d\mu=\int\delta_{y_{1}}\,\chi_{\left(\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in B\}}I_{j}\right)}(y_{2})\,\delta_{y_{2}}\,d\mu,

and therefore
W_{B}^{*}=W_{B}
.

To check that
T_{e_{i}}^{*}
is well defined, note first that

T_{e_{i}}^{*}(\delta_{y})=0
if

g(y)\notin\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}I_{j}.

Thus we may assume that

g(y)\in\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}I_{j}
.
We claim that in this case
g(y)\in R_{g}(x)
.

Indeed, since
y\in R_{g}(x)
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

g^{N}(y)=g^{\tau(x)}(x)\in E_{J}
.
If
N=1
, then
g(y)\in E_{J}
, which is impossible because

E_{J}\cap\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}I_{j}=\emptyset.

Hence
N\geq 2
, and consequently
g(y)\in R_{g}(x)
.
This shows that
T_{e_{i}}^{*}
is well defined, and one can verify that it extends to a bounded linear operator on
\ell^{2}(R_{g}(x))
.

Finally, to check the adjoint property, observe that

\int\delta_{y_{1}}\,\chi_{\left(\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}I_{j}\right)}(g(y_{2}))\,\chi_{I_{i}}(y_{2})\,\delta_{g(y_{2})}\,d\mu=(*)

is equal to

(*)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }y_{2}\notin I_{i},\\[4.0pt]
0&\text{if }y_{2}\in I_{i}\text{ and }g_{i}(y_{2})\neq y_{1},\\[4.0pt]
0&\text{if }y_{2}\in I_{i},\,g_{i}(y_{2})=y_{1},\text{ and }y_{1}\notin\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}I_{j},\\[4.0pt]
1&\text{if }y_{2}\in I_{i},\,g_{i}(y_{2})=y_{1},\text{ and }y_{1}\in\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}I_{j}.\end{cases}

It is straightforward to verify that the same holds for

\int\chi_{\left(\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}I_{j}\right)}(y_{1})\,\delta_{g_{i}^{-1}(y_{1})}\,\delta_{y_{2}}\,d\mu.

This proves the result.
∎

The next theorem shows how to construct a representation of the Toeplitz algebra using Markov maps.

Theorem 5.6
.

The map

\nu_{x}:C^{*}(\mathcal{G},\emptyset)\;\longrightarrow\;B(\ell^{2}(R_{g}(x)))

defined by

\nu_{x}(p_{B})=W_{B}
,
\nu_{x}(s_{e_{i}})=T_{e_{i}},

is a
*
-homomorphism.

Proof.

We check that
\{W_{B},T_{e_{i}}\}_{B\in\mathcal{E},\,i\in\mathbb{N}}

satisfies the relations that define
C^{*}(\mathcal{G},\emptyset)
.

First,
W_{\emptyset}=0
.
Since
R_{g}(x)\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathring{I_{i}}
and

\mathring{I_{i}}\cap\mathring{I_{j}}=\emptyset
for
i\neq j
,
we obtain

W_{A}\circ W_{B}=W_{A\cap B},\qquad W_{A\cup B}=W_{A}+W_{B}-W_{A\cap B},

which shows that the first item of Definition
2.4
is satisfied.

Next, for
y\in R_{g}(x)
we compute:

\displaystyle T_{e_{i}}^{*}\circ T_{e_{i}}(\delta_{y})

\displaystyle=T_{e_{i}}^{*}\Big(\chi_{\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}I_{j}}(y)\,\delta_{g_{i}^{-1}(y)}\Big)

\displaystyle=\chi_{\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}I_{j}}(y)\,\chi_{\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}I_{j}}(y)\,\chi_{I_{i}}(g_{i}^{-1}(y))\,\delta_{y}

\displaystyle=\chi_{\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}I_{j}}(y)\,\delta_{y}

\displaystyle=W_{r(e_{i})}(\delta_{y}).

Thus the second item holds.
Applying
T_{e_{i}}
to the equality above yields

T_{e_{i}}\circ T_{e_{i}}^{*}\circ T_{e_{i}}=T_{e_{i}}
,
so
T_{e_{i}}
is a partial isometry.

Now, we compute:

\displaystyle T_{e_{i}}\circ T_{e_{i}}^{*}(\delta_{y})

\displaystyle=T_{e_{i}}\Big(\chi_{\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}I_{j}}(g(y))\,\chi_{I_{i}}(y)\,\delta_{g(y)}\Big)

\displaystyle=\chi_{\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}I_{j}}(g(y))\,\chi_{I_{i}}(y)\,\chi_{\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}I_{j}}(g(y))\,\delta_{y}

\displaystyle=\chi_{\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}I_{j}}(g(y))\,\chi_{I_{i}}(y)\,\delta_{y}.

Since

W_{s(e_{i})}(\delta_{y})=\chi_{\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in s(e_{i})\}}I_{j}}(y)\,\delta_{y}=\chi_{I_{i}}(y)\,\delta_{y},

we conclude that

W_{s(e_{i})}\circ T_{e_{i}}\circ T_{e_{i}}^{*}=T_{e_{i}}\circ T_{e_{i}}^{*},

which shows that the third item of Definition
2.4
holds.

Moreover, the computation above also shows that

T_{e_{i}}\circ T_{e_{i}}^{*}\circ T_{e_{j}}\circ T_{e_{j}}^{*}(\delta_{y})=0\quad\text{for }i\neq j,

so the partial isometries have orthogonal ranges.

Finally, item (4) is vacuously satisfied since we chose
X=\emptyset
.
∎

To obtain representations of relative
C^{*}
-algebras of the form

C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)
with
X\neq\emptyset
,
we require an additional hypothesis.
This assumption will be crucial throughout the remainder of this work
and is introduced in the next lemma.

Lemma 5.7
.

If
i\in\mathbb{N}
is such that

\mathring{I_{i}}\cap g^{-1}(E_{J})=\emptyset
,
then for all
y\in\mathring{I_{i}}\cap R_{g}(x)

there exists
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that

g(y)\in I_{j}
and
v_{j}\in r(e_{i})
.

Proof.

Let
y\in\mathring{I_{i}}\cap R_{g}(x)
.
By hypothesis, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

g^{N}(y)=g^{\tau(x)}(x)\in E_{J}.

If
N=1
, then
g(y)\in E_{J}
, which would imply

y\in\mathring{I_{i}}\cap g^{-1}(E_{J})
, a contradiction.
Hence
N\geq 2
.

Therefore
g(y)\in\operatorname{dom}(g)
, so we can choose

j\in\mathbb{N}
with
g(y)\in I_{j}
.
By Lemma
5.4
, this implies
g(y)\in\mathring{I_{j}}
;
in particular,
g(y)\in\mathring{I_{j}}\cap g(\mathring{I_{i}})
.

To conclude, it suffices to show that
v_{j}\in r(e_{i})
.
By the definition of the ultragraph
\mathcal{G}
,
this is equivalent to proving that

\mathring{I_{j}}\subseteq g(\mathring{I_{i}})
.
Indeed, since

g(y)\in\mathring{I_{j}}\cap g(\mathring{I_{i}})\subseteq\mathring{I_{j}}\cap g(I_{i}),

Remark
5.2
yields

\mathring{I_{j}}\cap g(I_{i})=\mathring{I_{j}}
,
and thus
\mathring{I_{j}}\subseteq g(I_{i})
.
The result then follows from Lemma
5.4
.
∎

As a consequence of the above, under the additional hypothesis of
Lemma
5.7
,
the projection associated to a vertex
v_{i}
decomposes as the sum of the
range projections of the edges emitted by
v_{i}
, and hence the representation constructed from the Markov map satisfies the full
set of Cuntz–Krieger relations. We state this precisely below.

Corollary 5.8
.

If
i\in\mathbb{N}
is such that

\mathring{I_{i}}\cap g^{-1}(E_{J})=\emptyset
,
then

W_{v_{i}}=T_{e_{i}}\circ T_{e_{i}}^{*}=\sum_{\,e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}\,:\,s(e)=v_{i}}T_{e}\circ T_{e}^{*}.

Proof.

The second equality follows immediately from the definition of the ultragraph

\mathcal{G}
.

For the first equality, let
y\in R_{g}(x)
. Then

\displaystyle T_{e_{i}}\circ T_{e_{i}}^{*}(\delta_{y})

\displaystyle=\chi_{\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}I_{j}}(g(y))\,\chi_{I_{i}}(y)\,\delta_{y}

\displaystyle=\chi_{I_{i}}(y)\,\delta_{y}

\displaystyle=W_{v_{i}}(\delta_{y}).

The second line follows from Lemma
5.7
when

y\in I_{i}
(and thus
y\in\mathring{I_{i}}
, since
y\in R_{g}(x)
).
If
y\notin I_{i}
, the equality is immediate.
∎

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{0}
.
If for all
v_{i}\in X
we have

\mathring{I_{i}}\cap g^{-1}(E_{J})=\emptyset
,
then the map
\nu_{x}:C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)\;\longrightarrow\;B(\ell^{2}(R_{g}(x))),

defined by

\nu_{x}(p_{B})=W_{B},

\nu_{x}(s_{e_{i}})=T_{e_{i}},

is a
*
-homomorphism.

Proof.

This follows directly from Theorem
5.6
together with
Corollary
5.8
.
∎

To conclude this section, we show that every representation
\nu_{x}
constructed above coincides with a representation induced by a relative branching system.
Hence, the class of such representations is contained in the class of representations induced by relative branching systems.

Recall that
g\in M(I)
and
x\in E_{g}
are fixed.
Also recall that the ultragraph
\mathcal{G}_{g}=\mathcal{G}
induced by
g
is the ultragraph defined by the adjacency matrix
A_{g}
, namely

\mathcal{G}^{0}=\{v_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\},\qquad\mathcal{G}^{1}=\{e_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\},

with

s(e_{i})=v_{i},\qquad r(e_{i})=\{v_{j}\mid A_{ij}=1\}=\{v_{j}\mid\mathring{I_{j}}\subseteq g(\mathring{I_{i}})\}.

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
and for all
A\in\mathcal{E}
, define the sets

D_{v_{j}}=I_{j}\cap R_{g}(x),\qquad D_{A}=\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in A\}}D_{v_{j}},\qquad R_{e_{i}}=\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}g_{i}^{-1}(I_{j}\cap R_{g}(x)).

By Lemma
5.4
, we have

I_{j}\cap R_{g}(x)=\mathring{I_{j}}\cap R_{g}(x)
.
Therefore these sets can also be written as

D_{v_{j}}=\mathring{I_{j}}\cap R_{g}(x),\qquad D_{A}=\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in A\}}D_{v_{j}},\qquad R_{e_{i}}=\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}g_{i}^{-1}(\mathring{I_{j}}\cap R_{g}(x)).

Thus, depending on the context, we may choose whichever formulation of the sets
is more convenient.
Observe that all these sets are contained in
R_{g}(x)
and therefore they are
measurable with respect to the counting measure.

For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
we also define the function

f_{e_{i}}\colon D_{r(e_{i})}=\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}\bigl(I_{j}\cap R_{g}(x)\bigr)\;\longrightarrow\;R_{e_{i}}=\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}g_{i}^{-1}(I_{j}\cap R_{g}(x))

by
f_{e_{i}}(y)=g_{i}^{-1}(y).

This construction yields a branching system, as stated in the next result.

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{0}
.
If for all
v_{i}\in X
we have

\mathring{I_{i}}\cap g^{-1}(E_{J})=\emptyset
,
then
\{D_{A},R_{e_{i}},f_{e_{i}}\}_{A\in\mathcal{E},\,i\in\mathbb{N}}

is a
(\mathcal{G},X)
-relative branching system in
\ell^{2}(R_{g}(x))
.

Proof.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{E}
.
The equality
D_{A\cup B}=D_{A}\cup D_{B}
is immediate.
Moreover,

D_{A}\cap D_{B}=\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in A\}}\Big(\bigcup_{\{i\mid v_{i}\in B\}}D_{v_{j}}\cap D_{v_{i}}\Big)=\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in A\cap B\}}D_{v_{j}}=D_{A\cap B},

since
D_{v_{j}}\cap D_{v_{i}}=\mathring{I_{i}}\cap\mathring{I_{j}}\cap R_{g}(x)
,
which equals
D_{v_{j}}
if
i=j
and is empty otherwise.

Now let
x\in R_{e_{i}}
.
Then
x\in I_{i}
.
If
x\in\partial I_{i}
, then
g_{i}(x)\in\Gamma
.
But since
x\in R_{e_{i}}
we also have
g(x)\in R_{g}(x)
,
and by Lemma
5.4
it follows that
\Gamma\cap R_{g}(x)=\emptyset
,
a contradiction.
Thus
x\in\mathring{I_{i}}
, and therefore
R_{e_{i}}\subseteq\mathring{I_{i}}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
Consequently,

R_{e_{i}}\cap R_{e_{j}}\subseteq\mathring{I_{i}}\cap\mathring{I_{j}}=\emptyset\quad\text{for }i\neq j.

To see that
R_{e_{i}}\subseteq D_{s(e_{i})}
, observe first that

D_{s(e_{i})}=D_{v_{i}}=I_{i}\cap R_{g}(x).

If
y\in R_{e_{i}}
, then clearly
y\in I_{i}
.
Moreover, there exists
j\in\mathbb{N}
with
v_{j}\in r(e_{i})
and

g(y)\in R_{g}(x)\cap I_{j}
.
In particular,
y\in R_{g}(x)
.
Hence
y\in I_{i}\cap R_{g}(x)=D_{v_{i}}
.

We now prove that

D_{v_{i}}=\bigcup_{\{e\mid s(e)=v_{i}\}}R_{e}=R_{e_{i}}\quad\text{for all }i\in\mathbb{N}\text{ with }v_{i}\in X.

From the previous paragraph we know that
R_{e_{i}}\subseteq D_{v_{i}}
.
Thus it remains to show
D_{v_{i}}\subseteq R_{e_{i}}
.
Let
y\in D_{v_{i}}
.
By our hypothesis, Lemma
5.7
guarantees that there exists

j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
v_{j}\in r(e_{i})
and
g(y)\in I_{j}
.

We claim that
g(y)\in R_{g}(x)
.
Indeed, since
y\in D_{v_{i}}
, we know
y\in R_{g}(x)
, so there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
with

g^{N}(y)=g^{\tau(x)}(x)\in E_{J}.

If
N=1
, then
g(y)\in E_{J}
.
But this contradicts the fact that
g(y)\in I_{j}
.
Therefore
N\geq 2
, and consequently
g(y)\in R_{g}(x)
.
Thus
y\in g_{i}^{-1}(I_{j}\cap R_{g}(x))\subseteq R_{e_{i}}
.

Finally, since we are working with the counting measure, to prove the last axiom
of a relative branching system it suffices to show that each
f_{e_{i}}
is a bijection.
This follows directly from the definition, since
g_{|\mathring{I_{i}}}
is injective.
∎

We denote by
\pi_{x}
the representation induced by the

(\mathcal{G},X)
-relative branching system above, via
Proposition
4.4
.
It follows that

\pi_{x}:C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)\;\longrightarrow\;B(\ell^{2}(R_{g}(x)))

is given by

\pi_{x}(p_{A})(F)=\chi_{\bigcup_{\{i\mid v_{i}\in A\}}(I_{i}\cap R_{g}(x))}\cdot F,\qquad\pi_{x}(s_{e_{i}})(F)=\chi_{\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}g_{i}^{-1}(I_{j}\cap R_{g}(x))}\cdot(F\circ g_{i}),

for all

.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{0}
.
If for all
v_{i}\in X
we have
\mathring{I_{i}}\cap g^{-1}(E_{J})=\emptyset
,
then the representations
\nu_{x}
of Propositon
5.9

and
\pi_{x}
of the theorem above coincide.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that
\pi_{x}=\nu_{x}
on all generators of
C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)
.
Let
A\in\mathcal{E}
and
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
For
y,\tilde{y}\in R_{g}(x)
, a straightforward computation shows that

\nu_{x}(p_{A})(\delta_{y})(\tilde{y})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }y\neq\tilde{y},\\[4.0pt]

1&\text{if }y=\tilde{y}\text{ and }y\in\bigcup_{\{j\mid v_{j}\in A\}}I_{j},\end{cases}=\pi_{x}(p_{A})(\delta_{y})(\tilde{y}).

Similarly,

\nu_{x}(s_{e_{i}})(\delta_{y})(\tilde{y})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }g_{i}^{-1}(y)\neq\tilde{y},\\[4.0pt]

Therefore
\pi_{x}=\nu_{x}
on all generators of
C^{*}(\mathcal{G},X)
,
and the result follows.
∎

6.
Applications and Examples

This section aims to present applications and concrete examples showing how
Markov maps, branching systems, and injectivity theorems can be applied in a
single setting.

Let
g\in M(I)
, let
x\in E_{g}
, and let
\mathcal{G}_{g}=\mathcal{G}
be the
ultragraph induced by
g
. Fix
X\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{0}=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots\}

such that, for every
v_{i}\in X
, we have

\mathring{I_{i}}\cap g^{-1}(E_{J})=\emptyset
.
By Theorem
5.11
, the representation
\nu_{x}
induced by the
Markov map coincides with the representation
\pi_{x}
associated to the

(\mathcal{G},X)
-relative branching system on
\ell^{2}(R_{g}(x))
given by

D_{v_{i}}=I_{i}\cap R_{g}(x),\qquad R_{e_{i}}=\bigcup_{\{j\,:\,v_{j}\in r(e_{i})\}}g_{i}^{-1}\big(I_{j}\cap R_{g}(x)\big),\qquad f_{e_{i}}=g_{i}^{-1}.

As usual, write
Y=\mathrm{Reg}(\mathcal{G})\setminus X
; in our context we will
use
Y=\mathcal{G}^{0}\setminus X
(since
\mathcal{G}
has no sinks).

As the reader might expect, the injectivity theorems proved in the previous
sections apply to the representation
\nu_{x}=\pi_{x}
. For clarity, we record one
useful criterion and then apply it in the examples that follow.

Theorem 6.1
.

With the notation above, suppose that
r(e)\cap Y
is finite for every

e\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
. Then
\nu_{x}
is injective if and only if:

(1)

For every
i\in\mathbb{N}
with
v_{i}\in X
,

I_{i}\cap R_{g}(x)\neq\emptyset
.

(2)

For every
i\in\mathbb{N}
with
v_{i}\in Y
there exist

y_{i}\in I_{i}
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
with
v_{j}\in r(e_{i})
such that

g(y_{i})\in I_{j}\cap R_{g}(x)
.

(3)

For every
i\in\mathbb{N}
with
v_{i}\in Y
,

I_{i}\cap R_{g}(x)\cap g^{-1}(E_{J})\neq\emptyset
.

(4)

For any cycle
\alpha=(e_{j_{1}},\ldots,e_{j_{N}})
without exits such
that
r(e_{j_{i}})\notin Y
for all
i=1,\ldots,N
, and for any finite
subset

, there exists

with

\big(g_{j_{1}}^{-1}\circ\cdots\circ g_{j_{N}}^{-1}\big)^{\circ n}(x)\neq x\qquad\forall\,n\in\mathcal{F}.

Proof.

We prove that each of the hypotheses
(1)
–
(4)
is equivalent to one of
the hypotheses of Theorem
4.11
. It is
straightforward that
(1)
is equivalent to
(a)
and
(2)
is equivalent
to
(b)
of Theorem
4.11
. Since, in the
definition of the relative branching system,
f_{e_{i}}=g_{i}^{-1}
, it follows
that
(4)
is equivalent to
(d)
of the same theorem.

It remains to show that
(3)
is equivalent to the condition

\Bigl(D_{v_{i}}\setminus\bigcup_{\{e\,:\,s(e)=v_{i}\}}R_{e}\Bigr)=D_{v_{i}}\setminus R_{e_{i}}\neq\emptyset\quad\text{for all }i\in\mathbb{N}\text{ with }v_{i}\in Y.

First, fix
i\in\mathbb{N}
with
v_{i}\in Y
and suppose that

I_{i}\cap R_{g}(x)\cap g^{-1}(E_{J})\neq\emptyset
. Then there exists

. In particular,

.

Conversely, suppose

(1)
,
(2)
, and
(4)

.

Now take

. Then

, so

,

, and

. Let

.
Note that

, we show that

(1)

Beyond a solvent: HFIP as a game-changing medium in heterocyclic ring construction
Authors: Laxman M Alakonda, Ravi Varala, Vittal Seema, Mohammed Mujahid Alam, Luigi Vaccaro
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract1,1,1,3,3,3-Hexafluoroisopropanol (HFIP) has emerged as one of the most powerful and versatile solvents in modern organic synthesis owing to its exceptional hydrogen-bond-donating ability, low nucleophilicity, high ionizing power, and remarkable capacity to stabilize ionic and radical intermediates. These unique physicochemical properties enable HFIP to facilitate proton transfer, activate otherwise unreactive bonds, and promote a wide range of intermolecular interactions, making it highly valuable in heterocycle synthesis. Over the past decade, the application of HFIP has expanded significantly, particularly in C–H functionalization and heterocyclic ring construction, where it frequently acts not only as a solvent but also as a promoter, cocatalyst, and reaction-directing medium. In this review, we comprehensively and critically summarize the latest representative advances in HFIP-enabled heterocyclic ring formation reported from 2022 to 2025. Particular emphasis is placed on mechanistic understanding, synthetic efficiency, substrate scope, and the multifunctional role of HFIP in controlling chemo-, regio-, and stereoselectivity. HFIP-mediated transformations often proceed under mild, metal-free, oxidant-free, or redox-neutral conditions and enable challenging cyclizations, annulations, dearomatizations, rearrangements, and cross-dehydrogenative coupling reactions that are difficult to achieve using conventional solvents. Mechanistic discussions throughout the review highlight the ability of HFIP to stabilize cationic, radical, and transition-state intermediates through extensive hydrogen-bonding networks and cooperative interactions with catalysts or reagents. For clarity, the review is organized according to the structural classes of the target heterocycles and the nature of the synthetic strategies employed. The review focuses on the construction of simple five-membered heterocycles; fused heterocyclic systems, including five–six- and six–six-fused frameworks and polycyclic fused architectures; spiro heterocycles, including five–five- and five–six-spiro systems generated through radical, ionic, electrochemical, and dearomative cascade processes; and saturated and non-aromatic heterocycles, encompassing three-, five-, six-, seven-, and nine-membered rings, mixed five-/six-membered frameworks, and polyfused saturated systems. Overall, this review demonstrates that HFIP has evolved far beyond a conventional fluorinated solvent and now serves as a powerful reaction-enabling platform in modern heterocyclic chemistry. By critically comparing recent methodologies and mechanistic paradigms, this review aims to provide a unified perspective on the expanding role of HFIP and inspire future developments in sustainable heterocycle synthesis.Keywords: additive, catalyst, heterocycles, HFIP (hexafluoroisopropanol), reaction medium

Introduction

Fluorinated solvents have attracted increasing attention in recent years as specialized reaction media and co-solvents [1]. Among them, fluorinated alcohols exhibit distinctive physicochemical properties, including strong hydrogen-bond-donating ability, high polarity, and, depending on their molecular structure, varying degrees of water miscibility and volatility [2–4]. In particular, 1,1,1,3,3,3-hexafluoro-2-propanol (HFIP) is widely used in organic synthesis and allows facile product isolation [5]. Its high polarity and strong hydrogen-bond-donating ability promote the formation and stabilization of cationic intermediates, facilitating their reaction with nucleophiles [6]. The electron-withdrawing –CF3 groups also increase the acidity of the hydroxy proton, while fluorinated alcohols are weak nucleophiles and poor hydrogen-bond acceptors. Consequently, they exhibit reduced self-association compared with conventional alcohols [7].

Figure 1 summarizes the key physicochemical properties and diverse applications of HFIP. HFIP (C3H2F6O; MW 168.038 g·mol−1) is a colorless, highly volatile liquid (density 1.596 g·mL−1; bp 58.2 °C) with strong hydrogen-bond-donating ability, high polarity, and moderate acidity (pKa ≈ 9.3). These properties make HFIP a useful specialty solvent and reaction-promoting medium in organic synthesis, as well as in biochemistry, analytical chemistry, and electrochemistry. It can also stabilize reactive intermediates and enhance reaction efficiency in various transformations.

Structural features, preparation, and applications of HFIP.

A key advantage of HFIP and related fluorinated alcohols is their ability to promote reactions that would otherwise require catalysts or Lewis acids [8]. HFIP is a strong hydrogen-bond donor, as demonstrated by calorimetric and spectroscopic studies, as well as by the formation of stable complexes with various nucleophiles. With a boiling point of 58 °C, HFIP combines strong hydrogen-bond-donating ability, high ionizing power, moderate acidity (pKa ≈ 9.3), and relatively low nucleophilicity [9]. Its strong hydrogen bonding with carbonyl groups can activate C=O bonds and enhance their electrophilic character. These properties arise from its high hydrogen-bond donor strength (α = 1.96), polarity (Ps = 11.08), and ionizing ability (YOTs = 3.79).

Owing to its unique combination of physicochemical properties, HFIP is often described as a “magic solvent” and has been widely applied across organic synthesis [10–21], photochemistry [22], electrochemistry [23], etc. Its use in organic synthesis has increased rapidly in recent years and continues to grow. HFIP exhibits several distinctive features, including strong hydrogen-bond donating ability, high polarity with low nucleophilicity, significant Brønsted acidity, excellent ion (particularly carbocation) stabilizing capability, strong solvation power, redox stability, and ease of recovery and recyclability. Collectively, these properties enable efficient synthetic transformations, often under catalyst-free and mild reaction conditions.

Environmental, toxicological, and safety considerations of HFIP

Despite its useful properties, HFIP should not be considered inherently a green solvent. Its highly fluorinated nature raises environmental concerns related to solvent production, persistence, waste management, and disposal. HFIP is also volatile and requires appropriate handling to minimize exposure, including the use of a fume hood and suitable personal protective equipment. Fluorinated solvent waste should be collected and disposed of according to applicable institutional and regulatory requirements.

Therefore, the sustainability of HFIP-based processes should be evaluated beyond reaction efficiency, considering solvent quantity, recovery and recycling, energy use, and waste generation. While HFIP can offer significant advantages in reactivity and selectivity, these benefits should be balanced against its environmental and safety considerations.

The growing significance of HFIP has also been reflected in several comprehensive reviews. Solyev summarized HFIP-mediated C–H functionalization strategies [24], while Aubé and co-workers provided an extensive overview of its applications in organic synthesis [25]. More recently, Gao and co-workers updated the progress in homogeneous catalytic reactions involving HFIP [26].

Review

The role of HFIP in the heterocyclic ring formations

Five-membered heterocyclic rings

Synthesis of 3-acyl-1,4-diaryl/alkylpyrroles: A catalyst-free, microwave-assisted multicomponent strategy has been developed by Siddaiah and co-workers for the synthesis of 1,3,4-trisubstituted pyrroles 4 from 3-(dimethylamino)propenones 2, amines 1, and α-bromoketones 3 using recyclable HFIP [27] (Scheme 1). This protocol provides a simple and efficient approach, affording the desired products in 1 h with yields ranging from 52% to 99%. A key advantage of this methodology is the use of HFIP as a reusable reaction medium under microwave irradiation, enabling a catalyst-free multicomponent transformation for the efficient synthesis of 3-acyl-1,4-di(hetero)aryl/(cyclo)alkylpyrroles.

Synthesis of 3-acyl-1,4-diaryl/alkyl pyrroles 4.

The substrate scope demonstrates broad functional-group tolerance across all three reaction components (Scheme 1). Aromatic amines bearing either electron-withdrawing or electron-donating substituents generally afforded good to excellent yields, although ortho-substituted fluoroaniline showed diminished reactivity, likely due to steric effects. In contrast, heteroaryl, cycloalkyl, and aliphatic amines consistently produced pyrroles in high yields, underscoring the versatility of the protocol. Similarly, diverse 3-(dimethylamino)propenones and α-bromoketones containing electron-rich, electron-deficient, heteroaryl, alkyl, and cycloalkyl substituents were well tolerated, furnishing polysubstituted pyrroles in excellent yields, often approaching quantitative conversion. Notably, substituent position on the aromatic ring exerted minimal influence on the reaction outcome, further supporting the robustness of the method.

Synthesis of furan derivatives: A mild metal-free protocol employing p-TSA (para-toluenesulfonic acid) in HFIP has been developed by Nguyen and Pu for the cyclization of ynones 5 into 2-aryl-3-carboxylate ester furans and 2,3-diarylfurans 6 (Scheme 2a) [28]. This methodology proceeds under relatively mild conditions and affords the desired products in good to excellent yields (up to 94%). The operational simplicity of the protocol is notable, as reactions generally produced clean mixtures with minimal byproducts and required no extensive workup prior to purification.

Substrate scope for the synthesis of furan derivatives and proposed mechanism.

Substrate scope studies revealed broad tolerance toward structurally diverse ynones, although substrate electronics strongly influenced reaction efficiency. The substrate scope revealed a dependence of the reaction yield on the electronic nature of the substituents; however, no consistent correlation between electron-rich or electron-deficient substrates and higher yields was observed. In particular, α-carboxyloxy ynones initially exhibited lower conversions under room-temperature conditions, but increasing either the reaction time or temperature substantially improved yields, demonstrating the adaptability of the method. In contrast, electron-rich 2,3-diarylfurans could be synthesized efficiently even under ambient conditions (Scheme 2a).

Mechanistically, HFIP appears to play a dual role as a highly ionizing, hydrogen-bond-donating solvent and as an activator of p-TSA, effectively enhancing its Brønsted acidity. The reaction is proposed to proceed through protonation of the enolized ynone, followed by formation of a carbocation intermediate I and subsequent 5-exo-dig cyclization II to furnish the furan core after aromatization (Scheme 2b).

Synthesis of 3,4-fused fully substituted furan derivatives: Nitrones are versatile synthetic intermediates widely employed in the rapid assembly of structurally complex molecules relevant to pharmaceutical and materials chemistry [29]. In this context, a Rh(I)-catalyzed and HFIP-assisted cascade annulation of 1,n-diynyl nitrones 7 has been developed by Rao and co-workers for the synthesis of fully substituted furans 8 fused to diverse 5–8 membered carbocycles, heterocycles, and spirocycles (Scheme 3a) [30].

a) Rhodium(I)-catalyzed cascade annulation of benzene-tethered 1,n-diynyl nitrones. b) Rhodium(I)-catalyzed cascade annulation of alkyl-tethered 1,n-diynyl nitrones.

The transformation proceeds through a complex cascade sequence involving Rh(I)-mediated oxycyclization, N–O bond cleavage, and generation of an α-oxo rhodium carbene intermediate, followed by carbene-assisted migration (CAM), heterocyclization, and hydrolysis. Mechanistic studies suggest that HFIP plays a crucial role through hydrogen-bonding interactions with organorhodium intermediates, suppressing competing azomethine ylide pathways and instead promoting an unprecedented CAM/heterocyclization sequence leading to fused furan frameworks. This highlights the unique ability of HFIP to modulate organometallic reactivity beyond its conventional solvent role. This cascade annulation offers a highly modular and structurally diverse route to fused and spirocyclic furan architectures in a single operation. The combination of Rh(I) catalysis and HFIP-assisted pathway control enables access to reactivity patterns that are otherwise difficult to achieve, while the scalability and downstream functionalization of the products further underscore the synthetic utility of the methodology.

The methodology demonstrates a broad substrate scope and accommodates a wide range of tether architectures and functional groups (Scheme 3b). N- and O-tethered diynyl nitrones 7aa efficiently furnish fused furans 8aa containing zepine, chromene, pyridine, pyran, tetrahydrofuran, cyclopentene, and spirocyclic motifs in moderate to good yields. Substrates bearing alkyl, cycloalkyl, heteroaryl, sulfone, sulfonamide, ester, hydroxy, and ferrocenyl substituents are all well tolerated. In contrast, terminal alkynes and larger ring-forming substrates such as certain 1,9-diynyl systems provide diminished yields, likely due to unfavorable entropic and transannular effects during cyclization. The protocol is also applicable to late-stage functionalization, as demonstrated by annulation of estrone-derived substrates to generate structurally complex furanyl derivatives.

Synthesis of pyrazole-linked thiazole derivatives: Thiazoles are important sulfur- and nitrogen-containing heterocycles widely encountered in natural products, pharmaceuticals, and functional materials [31]. In this context, a green multicomponent protocol has been developed by Choudhury and co-workers for the synthesis of pyrazole-linked thiazoles 12 using arylglyoxals 9, aryl thioamides 10, and pyrazolones 11 in HFIP under room temperature and additive-free conditions (Scheme 4a) [32]. Compared with conventional thiazole syntheses that often require elevated temperatures, catalysts, or multistep procedures, this methodology proceeds under remarkably mild conditions while efficiently constructing multiple C–C, C–N, and C–S bonds in a single operation. The protocol also demonstrates broad applicability, furnishing structurally diverse thiazole-linked pyrazoles 12 in good to excellent yields, and can be extended to the synthesis of thiazole-linked barbiturates and imidazole-linked pyrazoles 12. The present approach offers several advantages from a green chemistry perspective, including room temperature operation, metal- and additive-free conditions, high atom economy, water as the sole byproduct, solvent recyclability, and operational simplicity. The absence of chromatographic purification further enhances its practicality and scalability.

a) Substrate scope of pyrazolones. b) Proposed mechanism for the products 12.

Substrate scope investigations revealed good tolerance toward electronically diverse pyrazolones, aryl thioamides, and arylglyoxals. Both electron-donating and electron-withdrawing substituents on thioamides and arylglyoxals generally afforded the desired products in high yields, indicating the robustness of the transformation. However, pyridyl and ortho-substituted thioamides showed comparatively lower reactivity, likely due to electronic and steric effects, respectively, whereas aliphatic thioamides were ineffective under the optimized conditions. In contrast, substituent variation on arylglyoxals had minimal influence on product yields, and even aliphatic glyoxals were compatible, further highlighting the versatility of the protocol (Scheme 4a).

Mechanistically, HFIP is proposed to play a crucial role by activating the glyoxal substrate through hydrogen bonding III and facilitating formation of key intermediates prior to intramolecular cyclization V and dehydration VI to yield the product 12 (Scheme 4b).

Synthesis of 4-aryl-NH-1,2,3-triazoles and 1,5-disubstituted 1,2,3-triazolyl glycoconjugates: 1,2,3-Triazoles constitute an important class of nitrogen-containing heterocycles widely employed in medicinal chemistry, materials science, and chemical biology because of their remarkable stability and diverse biological activities [33]. Consequently, the development of efficient, metal-free, and regioselective methods for triazole synthesis remains an active area of research. In this regard, an HFIP-promoted multicomponent strategy has been developed by Tiwari and co-workers for the synthesis of N-unsubstituted 16 and N-substituted 1,2,3-triazoles 18 from aldehydes 13, nitromethane (14), and sodium azide (15) or glycosyl azides 17 (Scheme 5) [34]. This methodology enables sequential formation of one C–C and two C–N bonds in a single operation under metal-free conditions. The protocol proceeds with high regioselectivity, gram-scale applicability, operational simplicity, and broad functional-group tolerance while providing the desired triazoles in generally good to excellent yields. Herein, HFIP provides the required mildly acidic environment to achieve optimal triazole formation, serving as a safer alternative to hazardous hydrazoic acid while enabling high product yields under the optimized conditions.

Substrate scope of the a) synthesis of N-unsubstituted 16 and b) N-substituted 1,2,3-triazoles 18.

Substrate scope studies demonstrated that aromatic aldehydes bearing electron-donating, electron-neutral, and electron-withdrawing substituents were all compatible with the reaction conditions (Scheme 5a). Halogen- and nitro-substituted substrates afforded products in particularly high yields, while ortho-substituted aldehydes were also well tolerated, indicating minimal steric sensitivity. Heteroaromatic aldehydes containing furan, thiophene, and pyridine motifs underwent smooth conversion to the corresponding triazoles, although aliphatic aldehydes proved unreactive under the optimized conditions. Extension of the methodology to nitroethane enabled efficient access to 4,5-disubstituted NH-1,2,3-triazoles 16, with electronic variations on the aldehyde component exerting little influence on product yields.

Importantly, the strategy was further expanded to the regioselective synthesis of 1,5-disubstituted triazolyl glycoconjugates 18 using glycosyl azides 17 and aldehydes 13, nitromethane (14) (Scheme 5b). Both substituted aromatic and heteroaromatic aldehydes were well tolerated, providing glycoconjugates in moderate to good yields. This extension highlights the synthetic utility of the protocol for accessing biologically relevant triazole-linked architectures.

Five-six fused heterocycles

Synthesis of coumarin-linked naphthoquinone-fused pyrrole derivatives: Coumarin-, pyrrole-, and naphthoquinone-based scaffolds are well known for their broad spectrum of biological and pharmacological activities, making hybrid molecules containing these motifs highly attractive targets in medicinal chemistry [35]. In this context, Chaurasia and Parvin reported a simple one-pot protocol for the synthesis of coumarin-linked pyrrole–naphthoquinone hybrids 21 via the reaction of arylglyoxals 9, 4-hydroxycoumarins 19, and 2-amino-1,4-naphthoquinones 20 in HFIP under reflux conditions (Scheme 6a) [36].

a) Substrate scope for synthesizing naphthoquinone-fused pyrrole derivatives 21. b) Proposed mechanism.

This method provides a catalyst-free, operationally straightforward, and efficient alternative for assembling structurally complex heterocycles. Notably, the reaction enables the formation of two C–C bonds and one C–N bond in a single operation, delivering the desired products in good yields within relatively short reaction times. Here, HFIP acts as an effective reaction medium, promoting efficient multicomponent coupling under reflux conditions and facilitating clean bond-forming events without the need for external catalysts. A key practical advantage of this protocol is its simple work-up, as the products can be purified by recrystallization rather than column chromatography, thereby enhancing both scalability and synthetic convenience.

Substrate scope studies demonstrated broad tolerance toward structurally diverse coupling partners. Various substituted 4-hydroxycoumarins bearing halogen, hydroxy, methyl, and methoxy groups underwent smooth conversion to the corresponding products in good yields. Similarly, aryl glyoxals containing either electron-donating or electron-withdrawing substituents were compatible with the reaction conditions, indicating minimal electronic sensitivity. The methodology also tolerated different N-substituted amino-naphthoquinones, further supporting the generality of the transformation (Scheme 6a).

Mechanistically, HFIP is proposed to activate the carbonyl functionality of arylglyoxals 9 through strong hydrogen-bonding interactions (VII), facilitating nucleophilic addition of 4-hydroxycoumarin 19 followed by Michael-type addition (VIII) of amino-naphthoquinone 20. Subsequent tautomerization (IX), cyclization (X), and dehydration (XI) steps furnish the fused pyrrole products 21 (Scheme 6b).

Construction of nitrogen-containing heterocycles: Nitrogen-containing heterocycles are among the most important structural motifs in pharmaceuticals and functional materials, with a substantial proportion of FDA (Food and Drug Administration)-approved drugs incorporating at least one nitrogen heterocycle [37]. In particular, isoindolin-1-imines and related isoquinolinium salts have attracted increasing attention because of their diverse biological and synthetic utility [38]. To access these frameworks, a mild and transition-metal-free strategy has been developed by Ji and co-workers using ortho-ethynylbenzaldehydes 13’ and anilines 1 in HFIP (Scheme 7) [39]. This methodology offers a simpler and more sustainable route to isoindolin-1-imines 22 and isoquinolinium salts under relatively mild conditions.

a) Substrate scope for the synthesis of 22. b) Proposed mechanism.

The protocol demonstrated a broad substrate scope and good functional-group tolerance (Scheme 7a). Anilines bearing electron-donating substituents generally afforded the desired isoindolin-1-imines in good yields, while halogen substituents were also well tolerated, providing synthetically useful products suitable for further derivatization. Electron-withdrawing groups such as cyano substituents remained compatible, although ester- and nitro-substituted anilines exhibited reduced reactivity, likely due to decreased nucleophilicity. Meta-substituted anilines and a variety of substituted ortho-ethynylbenzaldehydes also participated effectively, highlighting the versatility of the transformation. Notably, steric hindrance exerted only a limited effect on reaction efficiency. However, amides, aliphatic amines, and hydroxylamine hydrochloride were ineffective under the optimized conditions, indicating certain substrate limitations.

Mechanistically, HFIP plays a central role by promoting nucleophilic addition and facilitating subsequent cyclization through its strong proton-donating and hydrogen-bonding properties. The proposed pathway involves initial aminal formation (XII) followed by intramolecular annulation (XIV) to generate the isoindolin-1-imine framework 22. In the presence of ammonium iodide, the reaction pathway diverges toward isoquinolinium salt formation via hydroiodination and cyclization processes. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations supported the proposed mechanism and provided insight into the energetics of the key transition states (Scheme 7b).

Synthesis of 1H-indazoles by an electrochemical radical C(sp2)–H/N–H cyclization: An electrochemical oxidative C(sp2)–H/N–H cyclization strategy has been developed by Lei and co-workers for the synthesis of 1H-indazoles through anodic oxidation of arylhydrazones under metal- and chemical oxidant-free conditions (Scheme 8) [40]. HFIP played a dual role as solvent/proton donor and promoter of N-centered radical formation via hydrogen-bond-assisted activation. This protocol offers operational simplicity, low-cost electrode compatibility, and sustainable reaction conditions while avoiding external oxidants. The methodology exhibited broad functional-group tolerance, affording 1H-indazole derivatives 24 in moderate to good yields. This electrochemical protocol provides a greener and mechanistically distinct alternative, although regioselectivity issues and reduced efficiency with heterocyclic or protected substrates remain notable limitations.

a) Substrate scope of arylhydrazones. b) Proposed mechanism for 24.

Both electron-donating and electron-withdrawing substituents on the N-phenyl ring were compatible with the reaction conditions (Scheme 8a), with several electron-withdrawing substituents providing good to high yields. Halogenated substrates, including mono- and disubstituted analogues, also reacted efficiently, demonstrating the broad substrate tolerance of the protocol and the synthetic utility of halide-containing products for further derivatization.

Substituent effects on the hydrazone aryl ring significantly influenced the reaction outcome. Electron-rich disubstituted substrates delivered comparatively higher yields, whereas electron-deficient analogs afforded only moderate yields, indicating differing electronic effects depending on the substitution pattern. Monosubstituted methyl and methoxy derivatives generated regioisomeric mixtures. The detailed regioisomeric substrate scope and product distributions are not shown in Scheme 8a; readers are referred to the original report for further details. In contrast, N-protected substrates failed to undergo cyclization, highlighting the necessity of a free N–H bond for radical generation. Heterocycle-containing substrates were tolerated only in low yields, and alkene- or alkyne-substituted substrates converged to the same product. Certain substrates predominantly decomposed under the reaction conditions, reflecting limitations in substrate stability and substrate scope.

Mechanistic studies suggested that HFIP-assisted activation of arylhydrazones 23 facilitates proton-coupled electron transfer (PCET) at the anode to generate an N-centered radical intermediate XVI. Subsequent intramolecular radical addition (XVII) to the arene ring, followed by anodic oxidation and deprotonative aromatization, furnished the indazole products 24, while cathodic proton reduction released H2 (Scheme 8b). Cyclic voltammetry studies further confirmed the critical role of the DCM/HFIP system in promoting N-centered radical formation.

Direct synthesis of 1,2-disubstituted benzimidazoles: Benzimidazoles are important dinitrogen-containing bicyclic heterocycles widely found in pharmaceuticals, agrochemicals, and biologically active molecules [41]. A solvent-free and metal-free mechanochemical strategy has been developed by Yaragorla and co-workers for the synthesis of 1,2-disubstituted benzimidazoles 27 from donor–acceptor cyclopropyl ketones 25 and o-phenylenediamines 26 using stoichiometric HFIP under ambient conditions (Scheme 9) [42]. This mechanochemical approach significantly reduced reaction times while maintaining high efficiency, highlighting the advantages of mechanochemistry in sustainable heterocycle synthesis.

Generality of the reaction for the synthesis of compound 27.

The protocol demonstrated a broad substrate scope with both symmetrical and unsymmetrical o-phenylenediamines 26, generally affording the desired benzimidazoles 27 in good to excellent yields (Scheme 9). Symmetrical diamines generally underwent clean cyclization, whereas unsymmetrical substrates afforded regioisomeric mixtures, with the regioselectivity influenced by the electronic nature of the substituents. The detailed substrate scope, including the regioisomeric distributions, is not shown in Scheme 9; readers are referred to the original report for comprehensive details on the observed regioselectivity and product distributions.

In several cyano-substituted cases, the major regioisomers were separable and isolated in high yields. The methodology also tolerated a wide range of donor-acceptor cyclopropanes, including aryl-, vinyl-, diketone-, and keto ester-substituted systems. Notably, unsymmetrical cyclopropanes bearing both methyl ketone and aryl ketone functionalities displayed marked chemoselectivity, preferentially forming 2-methylbenzimidazoles over the corresponding 2-aryl analogues. Mechanistic studies suggested a tandem sequence involving cyclopropane ring opening, cyclization, and retro-Mannich fragmentation. Herein, HFIP acts both as the reaction solvent and as a crucial promoter of the mechanochemical transformation, as no reaction occurred in its absence, highlighting its key role in enhancing reaction efficiency.

Substrate-dependent selective synthesis of diversely functionalized indazole derivatives: Diversity-oriented synthesis (DOS) has emerged as a powerful strategy in drug discovery for rapidly accessing structurally diverse molecular libraries from simple building blocks [43]. In parallel, direct C–H functionalization has transformed modern synthesis by enabling step- and atom-economical construction of complex molecules, including pharmaceuticals and functional materials [44]. Within this context, a substrate-dependent divergent cascade approach has been developed by Fan and co-workers for the selective synthesis of functionalized indazole derivatives 30 and 31 from N-nitrosoanilines 28 and diazo compounds 29 and 29’ (Scheme 10) [45]. This strategy demonstrates how cascade C–H activation combined with substrate-dependent divergence can efficiently generate structurally distinct indazole scaffolds under mild conditions. This approach offers enhanced molecular diversity, high chemoselectivity, and operational simplicity, making it particularly valuable for diversity-oriented synthesis and early-stage drug discovery.

Notably, product divergence is controlled by the nature of the diazo coupling partner (Scheme 10): N-alkyl diazo homophthalimides 29 furnish benzoyl carbamate-substituted indazoles 30, whereas N-phenyl analogues 29’ lead to benzoate-functionalized indazoles 31 via an additional O-nucleophilic addition and subsequent fragmentation. This switchable reactivity highlights a key advantage of substrate-controlled selectivity in building molecular diversity from a common intermediate (Scheme 10). Herein, HFIP functions not only as the solvent but also as a key reaction participant, promoting product formation through O-nucleophilic addition to reactive intermediates. Its involvement facilitates the proposed ketal collapse, oxidative dehydrogenation, and subsequent C–C and/or C–N bond cleavage pathways, thereby contributing to the formation of the distinct products. A range of N-nitrosoanilines bearing electron-donating and electron-withdrawing groups at the para-position undergo smooth conversion to give products in moderate to excellent yields, while meta-substituted systems exhibit predictable regioselectivity governed by electronic and steric effects. Likewise, variation in alkyl substituents is well tolerated, reflecting the robustness of the cascade process. Diazo component variation is also feasible, further expanding structural diversity and enabling late-stage functionalization, including derivatization of bio-relevant motifs such as thymol.

The transformation proceeds through a cascade sequence involving nitroso-directed C–H alkylation followed by intramolecular and intermolecular nucleophilic additions, C–C or C–N bond cleavage, and dehydrogenation.

Synthesis of dihydropyrano[2,3-c]pyrazole scaffolds: Dihydropyrano[2,3-c]pyrazoles are important heterocyclic scaffolds that exhibit a wide range of biological activities, including anticancer, antimicrobial, analgesic, molluscicidal, and kinase inhibitory properties [46]. A catalyst-free protocol has been developed by Mohamadpour for the synthesis of dihydropyrano[2,3-c]pyrazoles 35 using HFIP as a reusable promoting medium (Scheme 11) [47]. The transformation proceeds through a tandem Knoevenagel–Michael cyclocondensation sequence under mild conditions, enabling rapid one-pot construction of the heterocyclic framework in excellent yields. This strategy employs polyfluorinated alcohol as both solvent and promoter, highlighting the unique activating ability of HFIP in facilitating multicomponent cyclizations and provides a greener and more sustainable alternative by eliminating external catalysts, minimizing waste generation, and avoiding hazardous reagents.

The methodology demonstrates a broad substrate tolerance, with a variety of aldehyde derivatives 13 reacting efficiently irrespective of their electronic nature (Scheme 11). Product isolation is operationally straightforward, involving simple filtration and recrystallization without extensive purification procedures.

Synthesis of 2-aminobenzoxazole and 2-aminobenzothiazole derivatives: Azole derivatives such as benzoxazoles 39 and benzothiazoles 40 are important pharmacophores widely encountered in biologically active molecules and therapeutic agents, making their efficient synthesis a central objective in heterocyclic chemistry. In this context [48], a straightforward HFIP-promoted cyclodesulfurization strategy has been developed by Malakar and co-workers for the synthesis of pharmaceutically relevant 2-aminobenzoxazoles 39 and 2-aminobenzothiazoles 40 from 2-aminophenols 36, 2-aminothiophenols 37, and functionalized phenyl isothiocyanates 38 (Scheme 12a).

a) Substrate scope for the synthesis of 39 and 40. b) Proposed mechanism.

The methodology exhibits a broad substrate scope and tolerates a wide range of electron-donating and electron-withdrawing substituents on both aminophenol and isothiocyanate partners (Scheme 12a). In general, electron-rich aminophenols afforded slightly higher yields than electron-deficient analogues, whereas electron-poor isothiocyanates performed better due to enhanced electrophilicity of the isothiocyanate carbon center, which facilitates thiourea formation and subsequent cyclization. Notably, benzothiazole 40 formation generally proceeded more efficiently than benzoxazole synthesis, likely reflecting the greater nucleophilicity of sulfur relative to oxygen. However, heteroaryl substrates such as 2-amino-3-hydroxypyridine were reported to be incompatible with the reaction conditions. This example is not shown in the scheme; readers are referred to the original article for further details. The incompatibility was attributed to the decreased nucleophilicity of the amino group due to the electron-withdrawing effect of the ring nitrogen, which may hinder intermediate formation (Scheme 12a).

The transformation proceeds under metal-, base-, and oxidant-free conditions at moderate temperature, with HFIP serving as both solvent and promoter. Mechanistically, the process involves initial thiourea formation (XVIII) followed by HFIP-assisted cyclodesulfurization of interemediate XIX through hydrogen-bond activation of hydroxy or thiol groups, ultimately leading to heterocycle formation 39 with concomitant release of H₂S (Scheme 12b). The dual role of HFIP as a proton donor and activating medium is crucial for facilitating bond cleavage and cyclization under mild conditions.

Construction of six-six fused heterocycles

Synthesis of 2-aminoquinazoline derivatives: Quinazoline derivatives are important nitrogen-containing heteroaromatic scaffolds widely recognized for their diverse biological and pharmaceutical activities [49]. Among them, 2-aminoquinazolines constitute a prominent class of bioactive molecules and medicinally relevant intermediates. In this context, an efficient hydrochloric acid-mediated [4 + 2] annulation strategy has been developed by Wu and co-workers for the synthesis of 2-aminoquinazolines 44 from N-benzyl cyanamides 41 and 2-aminoaryl ketones 42 (Scheme 13) [50]. This acid-mediated annulation provides a concise and operationally simple route to two distinct classes of quinazoline derivatives. The broad substrate tolerance, high efficiency, and divergent product formation underscore the practicality and synthetic utility of this methodology for the preparation of pharmaceutically relevant quinazoline frameworks. Replacing the ketone substrates with 2-aminobenzonitriles 43 enabled direct access to 2-amino-4-iminoquinazolines 45, thereby expanding the synthetic versatility of the protocol. Herein, HFIP functions as the reaction solvent, while MsOH was identified as an effective acid additive during the optimization studies. Under the reported reaction conditions, HCl was used as the acid to facilitate the overall transformation.

Synthesis of 2-aminoquinazoline derivatives 44 and 45.

The methodology demonstrates a broad substrate scope and excellent functional-group tolerance (Scheme 13). N-Benzyl cyanamides 41 bearing electron-donating, electron-withdrawing, halogen, and heteroaromatic substituents undergo smooth annulation to furnish the desired quinazoline products in generally good to excellent yields. Notably, sterically demanding substrates such as 2-naphthyl derivatives remain compatible, indicating the robustness of the transformation. Similarly, various substituted 2-aminoaryl ketones 42, including electron-rich, electron-poor, and halogenated systems, participate effectively in the cyclization process. This contrasts with more electronically tolerant HFIP-only or radical-based systems, highlighting the greater charge-dependence of BF3-assisted pathways. Nevertheless, the protocol shows broad functional-group compatibility overall, including cyclic, heteroaromatic, and amide-derived substrates, with the latter significantly expanding access to nitrogen-containing analogues. This strategy avoids metal residues while maintaining operational simplicity, though it can require elevated temperatures for less reactive substrates.

Mechanistically, the reaction is proposed to proceed through in situ formation of a strongly acidic species LVI arising from interaction between HFIP and BF3·Et2O. This activated complex promotes carbocation formation LVII from the alkyne precursor 87, followed by intramolecular cyclization (LVIII) to construct the coumarin framework 88. HFIP plays a dual and non-trivial role: beyond serving as a polar, hydrogen-bonding medium, it stabilizes key cationic intermediates through an extended hydrogen-bond network and facilitates smooth progression of the electrophilic cascade (Scheme 30b). Compared with electrochemical or radical HFIP-enabled systems, this approach is more ionically driven, relying on carbocation stabilization rather than radical generation or redox processes.

This strategy is distinct in its reliance on electrophilic activation of alkynes rather than radical or oxidative pathways. Despite some limitations in substrate scope for deactivated systems, the methodology provides a valuable, scalable, and metal-free route to biologically relevant coumarin frameworks and further underscores the multifunctional role of HFIP as both solvent and catalytic partner in modern heterocycle synthesis.

Seven-membered fused saturated heterocycle construction:Direct synthesis of benzo[b]azepines: Benzo[b]azepines 90 are structurally important seven-membered nitrogen heterocycles frequently found in bioactive molecules and drug candidates [83]. In this context, a metal-free I2-mediated [6 + 1] annulation using protonated aminating reagent (MsONH3OTf) and alkynes 89 has been developed by Yan and co-workers as an efficient route to this scaffold under mild conditions, highlighting the growing utility of radical and Brønsted acid cooperative systems in medium-ring heterocycle construction (Scheme 31) [84].

a) Scope of 1,4-diarylalkynes for the synthesis of 90. b) Proposed mechanism.

The method shows broad tolerance toward both electron-donating and electron-withdrawing substituents on the aromatic ring (Scheme 31a), with generally moderate to good yields across para-, meta-, and ortho-substituted substrates. The transformation displays relatively low sensitivity to electronic effects and steric variation, indicating a robust reaction manifold. However, limitations are observed with certain alkynes bearing heteroaryl (e.g., thienyl), strained alkyl (cyclopropyl), or simple alkyl substituents, which fail to deliver the desired products, suggesting constraints in radical stabilization and intermediate reactivity.

Mechanistically, the reaction is proposed to proceed through in situ generation of HOTf, which catalyzes alkyne hydration to yield LIX followed by condensation with MsONH2 to form an oxime intermediate LX. This is then activated by iodine to generate an N-centered radical LXI via N–O bond homolysis, followed by radical cyclization (LXII) to construct the seven-membered ring 90 (Scheme 31b). Compared with HFIP-stabilized ionic pathways or electrochemical radical systems, this transformation relies on a hybrid acid-radical sequence where iodine serves as the key redox mediator rather than an external oxidant or electrode. The absence of transition metals and the in situ generation of reactive species make the protocol operationally simple, although dependence on radical propagation and substrate compatibility limits its universality compared to more general HFIP-enabled cyclizations.

Overall, this benzo[b]azepine 90 synthesis complements other modern heterocycle-forming strategies by occupying a mechanistic space between ionic HFIP as a solvent mediated cyclizations and electrochemical radical processes. While HFIP-based systems excel in stabilizing cationic intermediates and electrochemical methods offer controlled redox activation, this iodine-mediated approach provides a practical alternative for accessing medium-sized N-heterocycles 90 through radical annulation under mild, metal-free conditions.

Nine-membered fused saturated heterocycle construction:Synthesis of nine-membered heterocyclic derivatives: Medium-sized ring systems, particularly nine-membered frameworks, are prevalent in complex natural products and bioactive molecules, yet their synthesis remains challenging due to unfavorable entropic constraints and transannular strain effects [85]. In response, a metal-free, redox-neutral hydride-transfer (HT)-triggered cross-dehydrogenative coupling (CDC) strategy has been developed by Xiao and co-workers for the construction of quinoline-fused nine-membered rings 92, offering a step- and atom-economical alternative to traditional prefunctionalized approaches (Scheme 32) [86].

a) Substrate scope for the synthesis of nine-membered rings. b) Proposed mechanism.

This transformation proceeds efficiently across a broad range of aromatic aldehydes and diamines, demonstrating generally good functional-group tolerance (Scheme 32a). Electron-rich aldehydes tend to give higher yields, whereas electron-withdrawing substituents lead to reduced efficiency, reflecting the key role of iminium ion stability in governing reactivity. Steric effects are also significant, with meta-substitution resulting in diminished yields, while heteroaromatic systems such as naphthaldehydes remain competent substrates. This CDC process is more strongly governed by iminium ion formation and hydride-transfer dynamics, making it particularly sensitive to electronic effects. While highly efficient and environmentally favorable, its strong dependence on iminium stability and electronic effects contrasts with the broader functional tolerance often observed in HFIP-stabilized radical or electrochemical systems. Nevertheless, it represents a valuable complement within modern CDC and medium-ring synthetic methodologies, particularly for accessing complex quinoline-fused architectures in a single-step, green fashion.

Mechanistically, HFIP plays a central dual role: it facilitates aldimine formation to generate iminium ion intermediates and stabilizes charged transition states through an extended hydrogen-bonding network (Scheme 32b). The reaction is initiated by HFIP-assisted iminium formation (LXIII), followed by an intramolecular hydride transfer that activates a C(sp3)–H bond and generates a second iminium species LXIV. Subsequent nucleophilic capture by an enamine intermediate LXV delivers the cyclized product 92. The addition of benzoic acid further enhances acidity through a “combined acid” effect, reinforcing the catalytic environment. Compared with electrochemical or radical CDC methodologies, this approach is distinctive in its purely redox-neutral nature, avoiding external oxidants or catalysts while relying instead on internal hydride transfer and acid-assisted activation.

Polyfused saturated heterocycle construction:Formal umpolung reactivity of indole derivatives: Indole functionalization, particularly at the C2 and C3 positions, has long been dominated by its inherent nucleophilicity toward electrophiles [87–88]. In this context, an HFIP as a solvent promoted cascade process developed by Das and Chouhan involving epoxide-tethered indoles provides an unusual and conceptually important deviation from conventional indole reactivity, enabling a formal C3 umpolung through a dearomative cyclization–rearrangement sequence [89]. This strategy delivers highly complex fused-bridged indoline architectures 95 in a single operation with excellent regio- and diastereocontrol, significantly expanding the synthetic landscape of indole chemistry (Scheme 33).

Substarte scope of (a) tryptamine and (b) epoxy tosylates derivatives.

The transformation exhibits a broad substrate scope across tryptamine derivatives 93 bearing diverse electronic and steric substitution patterns on the aromatic ring, including electron-donating and moderately electron-withdrawing groups, which generally furnish products 95 in high yields (Scheme 33). Variations in N-protection and N-alkyl substituents are also well tolerated, indicating that the reaction is relatively insensitive to modification of the indole side chain. However, clear limitations emerge when the intrinsic nucleophilicity of the indole C3 position is reduced, particularly under strongly electron-withdrawing N-substitution, or when the epoxide framework lacks sufficient π-activation, leading to unreactive or decomposed substrates. Compared with HFIP-stabilized ionic cyclizations or electrochemical radical processes, this system is more sensitive to subtle electronic tuning of the indole core and the epoxide electrophile.

Mechanistically, HFIP plays a decisive role in enabling the initial dearomative epoxide-indole cyclization by stabilizing developing cationic character through extensive hydrogen-bonding interactions. This activation triggers nucleophilic attack of the indole onto the epoxide, followed by an interrupted Plancher-type rearrangement that reorganizes the intermediate into a fused-bridged indoline framework. The process effectively reverses the conventional polarity of indole reactivity at C3, achieving a formal umpolung through substrate activation and rearrangement rather than prefunctionalization or external redox control. Compared with electrochemical or radical indole functionalizations, this approach uniquely combines dearomatization and skeletal rearrangement in a single cascade, relying primarily on HFIP-mediated ion stabilization rather than radical generation or metal catalysis.

Overall, this HFIP-enabled cascade represents a powerful example of complexity-building indole chemistry, converting simple indole-epoxide precursors into highly strained bridged frameworks with excellent selectivity. While its efficiency and stereocontrol are notable, its dependence on epoxide activation and indole electronic properties defines a narrower reactivity window compared to more general HFIP-assisted cyclizations. Nevertheless, it provides a distinct and valuable strategy for accessing architecturally complex indoline scaffolds and demonstrates how HFIP can unlock non-intuitive reactivity patterns in heteroaromatic systems.

Conclusion and Outlook

In conclusion, HFIP has evolved from a specialized fluorinated solvent into a powerful reaction-enabling medium that plays a central role in modern heterocyclic chemistry. Its unique combination of strong hydrogen-bond-donating ability, high ionizing power, low nucleophilicity, and exceptional capacity to stabilize ionic and radical intermediates has enabled numerous synthetic transformations that are often difficult to achieve under conventional conditions. As highlighted throughout this review, HFIP not only serves as a solvent but frequently acts as a promoter, cocatalyst, proton-transfer mediator, and reaction-directing component, significantly influencing reaction pathways, selectivity, and overall efficiency.

The recent advances summarized from 2022 to 2025 clearly demonstrate the versatility of HFIP across a broad spectrum of heterocyclic ring constructions, including simple heterocycles, fused and polycyclic systems, spirocyclic frameworks, and saturated or non-aromatic heterocycles. HFIP-enabled methodologies have facilitated diverse transformations such as cyclizations, annulations, dearomatizations, rearrangements, electrochemical reactions, and cross-dehydrogenative coupling processes under mild, metal-free, oxidant-free, or redox-neutral conditions. In many cases, these strategies exhibit broad substrate scope, high chemo-, regio-, and stereoselectivity, operational simplicity, and improved sustainability compared with traditional protocols.

Mechanistically, the ability of HFIP to stabilize reactive intermediates through extensive hydrogen-bonding networks and cooperative interactions with catalysts or reagents has emerged as a recurring theme across many transformations. The solvent has proven particularly valuable in promoting cationic, radical, and cascade processes, thereby enabling rapid molecular complexity generation and access to structurally diverse heterocyclic scaffolds with significant pharmaceutical and synthetic relevance. At the same time, the reviewed studies also reveal certain limitations, including substrate-dependent reactivity, solvent cost, and challenges associated with solvent recovery and large-scale applications.

Nevertheless, the rapidly growing number of HFIP-mediated methodologies underscores its continuing impact on synthetic design and reaction development. Future progress in recyclable fluorinated solvent systems, asymmetric variants, electrochemical processes, photocatalytic transformations, and sustainable catalytic protocols is expected to further expand the utility of HFIP in heterocyclic synthesis. The authors have anticipated that the mechanistic insights and synthetic strategies discussed in this review will provide valuable guidance for the development of next-generation HFIP-enabled transformations and inspire further innovation in sustainable heterocyclic chemistry.

Emerging Alternatives to HFIP

Although HFIP remains a valuable solvent in heterocyclic synthesis, its environmental, toxicological, and economic concerns have encouraged the search for more sustainable alternatives. Emerging approaches include alternative fluorinated alcohols, HFIP–water or mixed-solvent systems, and non-fluorinated hydrogen-bond-donating solvents, ionic liquids, and deep eutectic solvents. Solvent-free and low-solvent approaches, including mechanochemistry, electrochemistry, and photochemistry, also offer promising directions. Rather than seeking a single replacement for HFIP, future studies may focus on identifying reaction media that reproduce its specific beneficial properties while reducing its environmental impact. Comparative studies and solvent-recycling strategies will be important in assessing the practical potential of these alternatives.

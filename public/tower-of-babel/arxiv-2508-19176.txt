Graded Ehrhart theory and toric geometry
Ian Cavey

Abstract
We give two new constructions of the harmonic algebra of a lattice polytope P P , a bigraded algebra whose character is the q q -Ehrhart series of P P defined by Reiner and Rhoades [ 6 ] . First, we show that the harmonic algebra is the associated graded algebra of the semigroup algebra of P P with respect to a certain natural filtration, clarifying it’s relationship with the more classical semigroup algebra. We then give a geometric interpretation of the harmonic algebra as a quotient of the ring of global sections of a certain family of line bundles on the blowup of the toric variety associated to P P at a generic point. Using this connection to toric geometry we resolve one the main conjectures of Reiner and Rhoades [ 6 ] by showing that the harmonic algebra is not finitely generated in general.

Introduction

The
Ehrhart series
of a lattice polytope
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the generating function for the sequence of integer point counts in the dilations
mP=\{(mp_{1},\dots,mp_{n})\,|\,(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})\in P\}
,

E_{P}(t)=\sum_{m\geq 0}\left|(mP)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right|\,t^{m}.

(1)

Although the Ehrhart series is defined as a formal power series, it can always be expressed as a rational function of
t
. A powerful algebraic tool for studying Ehrhart series is the
semigroup algebra
of
P
,

A_{P}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})\in(mP)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
m\geq 0\end{subarray}}\mathbb{C}\,x_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{p_{n}}z^{m}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}][z],

(2)

a graded algebra whose Hilbert series is
E_{P}(t)
by construction. This algebra can be interpreted geometrically as the coordinate ring of the toric variety
X_{P}
associated to
P
, providing a fruitful connection between algebraic geometry and polytopal combinatorics.

In this paper, we study
q
-analogues of these objects recently introduced by Reiner and Rhoades
[
6
]
. The
q
-Ehrhart series
of
P
, denoted
E_{P}(t,q)
, is a refinement of the classical Ehrhart series of
P
. The coefficient of

in the

-Ehrhart series is a polynomial in
q
with nonnegative integer coefficients that specializes at

to the number of integer points in
mP
. The precise definition of these coefficients in recalled in Section
2
.

By analogy with the semigroup graded algebra for Ehrhart series, Reiner and Rhoades constructed a bigraded algebra
\mathcal{H}_{P}
, called the
harmonic algebra
of
P
, whose bigraded Hilbert series is
E_{P}(t,q)
. The definition of the harmonic algebra, recalled in Section
2
, is somewhat involved as it requires dualizing with respect to the action of one polynomial ring on another via partial derivatives. In particular, as noted by Reiner and Rhoades, it is not clear from the definition that
\mathcal{H}_{P}
is closed under multiplication. Nonetheless, the harmonic algebra can be explicitly presented for small examples and for some special classes of polytopes (
[
6
]
Proposition 5.7). Based on this evidence, Reiner and Rhoades conjectured the following.

Conjecture 1.1
.

(
[
6
]
Conjecture 5.5 (i))
For any lattice polytope
P
, the harmonic algebra
\mathcal{H}_{P}
is finitely generated. In particular
E_{P}(t,q)
is a rational function of
t
and
q
.

In this paper, we resolve the algebraic part of Conjecture
1.1
in the negative, showing that there exist lattice triangles

for which

is not finitely generated (see Example

). The weaker conjecture, the rationality of the

-Ehrhart series

Our results on the harmonic algebra are based on the following simple description of
\mathcal{H}_{P}
established in Section
3
. Recall that the order of vanishing of a Laurent polynomial

.

For any lattice polytope

is isomorphic as a bigraded algebra to the associated graded algebra of

with respect to the filtration by order of vanishing at

.

Next, we connect the harmonic algebra to toric geometry. Let us briefly recall the classical setup of toric geometry (see, for example,
[
3
]
). Let
X_{P}
be the toric variety associated to the lattice polytope
P
. The variety
X_{P}
contains a dense open subset that is identified with the algebraic torus
T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
, on which the regular functions are the Laurent polynomials
\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
. The toric variety
X_{P}
is equipped with a line bundle
\Oo(H)
, and the global sections of any power
\Oo(mH)
are identified, via restriction to

, with Laurent polynomials in
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
supported on
mP
. This gives the well-known identification

A_{P}\simeq\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}H^{0}(X_{P},\Oo(mH)).

(3)

Now let
\Bl_{e}X_{P}
denote the blowup of
X_{P}
at the identity point of the torus

, with exceptional divisor
E\subseteq\Bl_{e}X_{P}
. Since
e\in X_{P}
is not

\Bl_{e}X_{P}
is not, in general, a toric variety. Consider the bigraded section ring

R_{P}:=\bigoplus_{m,d\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{0}(\Bl_{e}X_{P},\Oo(mH-dE)),

(4)

where we identify
\Oo(mH)
with its pullback to
\Bl_{e}X_{P}
. The line bundle
\Oo(E)

E

R_{P}

The following result, proved in Section

1.2
.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any lattice polytope
P
we have
\mathcal{H}_{P}\simeq R_{P}/(s)
as bigraded algberas.

Theorem
1.4
connects the harmonic algebra and
q
-Ehrhart theory to a large body of algebraic and geometric results about finite generation of section rings of blowups of toric varieties. The algebraic properties of these rings are subtle already for lattice triangles
P
for which the toric variety
X_{P}
is a weighted projective plane. For example, Cutkosky
[
2
]
studied the finite generation of sections rings for blowups of weighted projective planes described algebraically as symbolic algebras of monomial prime ideals. Specific examples of weighted projective planes, and their corresponding lattice triangles, whose blowups have non-finitely generated section rings have been given by Goto, Nishida, and Watanabe
[
5
]
as well as González and Karu
[
4
]
.

By Theorem
1.4
, the harmonic algebra
\mathcal{H}_{P}
is finitely generated if and only if the section ring
R_{P}
is finitely generated. This connection, combined with the previous work described above, therefore resolves the algebraic portion of Conjecture
1.1
in the negative.

Corollary 1.5
.

There exist lattice triangles
P
such that the harmonic algebra
\mathcal{H}_{P}
is not finitely generated.

In Example
4.1
, adapted from an example studied by González and Karu
[
4
]
, we show that the harmonic algebra of the triangle with vertices
(0,0),(7,56),
and
(-45,30)
is not finitely generated. Our argument for non-finite generation depends on the geometric results of Gonzalez and Karu. It would be interesting to give an elementary proof of the non-finite generation of the harmonic algebra for this example.

A natural next problem is to determine for which polytopes the harmonic algebra
\mathcal{H}_{P}
is finitely generated. On the geometric side, this is an ongoing area of research largely focused on the case when
P
is a lattice triangle. Cutkosky
[
2
]
gave a geometric criterion (depending on of the existence of certain curves on
\Bl_{e}X_{P}
) for the section ring
R_{P}
to be finitely generated. It is not clear, however, how the existence of such curves depends on the combinatorics of the triangle. In addition to the examples referred to above, a systematic study of this question was recently initiated by Anaya, González, and Karu
[
1
]
.

We hope that this connection to toric geometry will prove useful for resolving other conjectures about the harmonic algebra, and provide a new perspective on these toric geometry problems as well.

Background

We first review the constructions and basic facts about
q
-Ehrhart series and the harmonic algebra from
[
6
]
. We restrict to work over
\mathbb{C}
, rather than an arbitrary field as in
[
6
]
, which will be needed for our connection to toric geometry.

Given a finite set
Z\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the ideal of
Z
is

I(Z)=\{f\,|\,f(p)=0\text{ for all }p\in Z\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}].

(5)

Geometrically, the quotient
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/I(Z)
is the coordinate ring of the reduced union of points
Z
. Now consider the ideal
\gr I(Z)=\{f_{d}\,|\,f=f_{0}+f_{1}+\cdots+f_{d}\in I(Z)\}
, where
f_{i}
is the homogeneous degree
i
piece of
f
. The quotient
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/\gr I(Z)
is the coordinate ring of the scheme obtained by degenerating the set
Z
to a reduced scheme supported at the origin. Unlike
I(Z)
,
\gr I(Z)
is a homogeneous ideal and so
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/\gr I(Z)
is graded by total degree in
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The
q
-Ehrhart series of a lattice polytope
P
is the formal power series

E_{P}(t,q)=\sum_{m,d\geq 0}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\big(\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/\gr I((mP)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})\big)_{d}\,t^{m}q^{d}.

One can show using Gröbner bases that
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/I(Z)
and
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/\gr I(Z)
have the same dimension as
\mathbb{C}
-vector spaces. It follows that
E_{P}(t,q)
is a refinement of
E_{P}(t)
, in the sense that
E_{P}(t,1)=E_{P}(t)
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
harmonic space
of a finite set
Z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the set

V_{Z}=\left\{g\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\,\bigg|\,f\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\right)g(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=0\text{ for all }f\in\gr I(Z)\right\}.

The harmonic space is linear subspace
V_{Z}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
of dimension
|Z|
and is graded by total degree in
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
.

Definition 2.3
.

The
harmonic algebra
of
P
is defined by

\mathcal{H}_{P}=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}V_{(mP)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}},

with the bigrading
(\mathcal{H}_{P})_{m,d}=(V_{(mP)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}})_{d}
.

By Proposition 5.4 of
[
6
]
,
\mathcal{H}_{P}
forms a bigraded algebra with respect to the usual multiplication of polynomials, and its bigraded Hilbert series is the
q
-Ehrhart series of
P
:

E_{P}(t,q)=\sum_{m,d\geq 0}\dim_{C}(\mathcal{H}_{P})_{m,d}\,t^{m}q^{d}.

(6)

The harmonic algebra via the semigroup algebra

We begin with an alternative characterization of the harmonic space
V_{Z}
for any finite set
Z\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
that may be of interest in its own right. In the following, for any point
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
we write
e^{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{x}}=e^{a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}}
and
(1+\mathbf{x})^{\mathbf{a}}=(1+x_{1})^{a_{1}}\cdots(1+x_{n})^{a_{n}}
, both considered as formal power series in
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
after expanding at
(0,\dots,0)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For any finite set
Z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the harmonic space
V_{Z}
is the set of lowest degree nonzero homogeneous parts of formal power series in the linear span
\langle(1+\mathbf{x})^{\mathbf{a}}\,|\,\mathbf{a}\in Z\rangle
.

Proof.

Let
W_{Z}
be the set of formal power series
g(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
such that for all
f\in I(Z)

f\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\right)g(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=0.

By Corollary 4.4 of
[
6
]
,
V_{Z}
is the set of lowest degree homogeneous parts of elements of
W_{Z}
. By Lemma 4.6 of
[
6
]
,
W_{Z}
is the linear span
\langle e^{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\,|\,\mathbf{a}\in Z\rangle
where we consider
e^{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{x}}=e^{a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}}
as a formal power series in
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. The proposition therefore follows from the following observation.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For any finite set
Z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and set of constants
\{c_{\mathbf{a}}\,|\,a\in Z\}
, the lowest degree nonzero homogeneous parts of the series

\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in Z}c_{\mathbf{a}}e^{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{x}}
and
\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in Z}c_{\mathbf{a}}(1+\mathbf{x})^{\mathbf{a}}
coincide.

Proof.

The
x_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{d_{n}}
coefficients on the series
\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in Z}c_{\mathbf{a}}e^{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{x}}
and
\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in Z}c_{\mathbf{a}}(1+\mathbf{x})^{\mathbf{a}}
are given by

E(d_{1},\dots,d_{n}):=\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in Z}c_{\mathbf{a}}\frac{a_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots a_{n}^{d_{n}}}{d_{1}!\cdots d_{n}!}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{and}\hskip 28.45274ptF(d_{1},\dots,d_{n}):=\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in Z}c_{\mathbf{a}}{a_{1}\choose d_{1}}\cdots{a_{n}\choose d_{n}}

respectively. Consider the monomial expansion of
{y_{1}\choose d_{1}}\cdots{y_{n}\choose d_{n}}
as a polynomial in
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}
, which has unique highest degree term
\frac{y_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{d_{n}}}{d_{1}!\cdots d_{n}!}
. Applying this expansion to each term in the sum for
F(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})
gives a relation
F(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})=E(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})+\cdots
where the remaining terms are linear combinations of lower degree coefficients of
\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in Z}c_{\mathbf{a}}e^{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{x}}
. This implies that the nonzero coefficients on monomials of minimum total degree for the two series coincide, as claimed.
∎

Definition 3.3
.

For a lattice polytope
P
, let
F_{m,d}\subseteq(A_{P})_{m}
denote the set of elements represented by Laurent polynomials
f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})z^{m}
, as in (
2
), such that
f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
vanishes to order at least
d
at the point

.

These subsets equip the semigroup algebra with a descending filtration
(A_{P})_{m}=F_{m,0}\supseteq F_{m,1}\supseteq\cdots
. These filtered pieces are compatible in the sense that
F_{m,d}\cdot F_{m^{\prime},d^{\prime}}\subseteq F_{m+m^{\prime},d+d^{\prime}}
for all
m,m^{\prime},d,
and
d^{\prime}
, so we may define the associated graded algebra of
A_{P}
as the bigraded algebra

\gr A_{P}=\bigoplus_{m,d\geq 0}F_{m,d}/F_{m,d+1}.

(7)

Theorem
1.2
can now be quickly derived from Proposition
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

An element
f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})z^{m}\in(A_{P})_{m}
is in the filtered piece
F_{m,d}
if and only if the monomial expansion of
f(x_{1}+1,\dots,x_{n}+1)
contains only monomials of total degree at least
d
. We can therefore consider the quotient map
F_{m,d}\to F_{m,d}/F_{m,d+1}
as sending such an element to the homogeneous degree
d
part of
f(x_{1}+1,\dots,x_{n}+1)
. By Proposition

, the image
F_{m,d}/F_{m,d+1}
is exactly the degree

V_{(mP)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}=(\mathcal{H}_{P})_{m,*}
. This identifies
\mathcal{H}_{P}
with
\gr A_{P}
as bigraded algebras, as desired.
∎

The harmonic algebra via toric geometry

In this Section we prove Theorem
1.4
which states that the harmonic algebra is isomorphic to the quotient of the section ring
R_{P}=\bigoplus_{m,d}H^{0}(\Bl_{e}X_{P},\Oo(mH-dE))
by the ideal generated by the canonical section
s\in\Oo(E)
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

For
d\geq 0
, multiplication by
s^{d}
gives an injective map

H^{0}(\Bl_{e}X_{P},\Oo(mH-dE))\xhookrightarrow{\cdot s^{d}}H^{0}(\Bl_{e}X_{P},\Oo(mH))\simeq H^{0}(X_{P},\Oo(mH))\simeq(A_{P})_{m}

whose image is exactly the set of sections that vanish to order at least
d
at the point
e
, and

H^{0}(\Bl_{e}X_{P},\Oo(mH))\xrightarrow{\cdot s^{d}}H^{0}(\Bl_{e}X_{P},\Oo(mH+dE))

is an isomorphism. For all
m
and

we can therefore identify
(R_{P})_{m,d}=H^{0}(\Bl_{e}X_{P},\Oo(mH-dE))
with
F_{m,d}\subseteq(A_{P})_{m}
, the Laurent polynomials vanishing to order at least
d
at
e
. In other words, we have the identification of bigraded rings

R_{P}\simeq\bigoplus_{m,d}F_{m,d}

(8)

Under this identification, the inclusions
F_{m,d+1}\subseteq F_{m,d}
correspond multiplication by
s
between the graded pieces of
R_{P}
. The quotient
R_{P}/(s)
is therefore isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{m,d}F_{m,d}/F_{m,d+1}
, which is isomorphic to the harmonic algebra by Theorem

.

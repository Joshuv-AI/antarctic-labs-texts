Bibliometric analysis of research hotspots and trends of type 2 diabetes mellitus and sodium–glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors
Authors: Bai Qiuyu, Wang Xian, Chen Jinhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPurpose/background:With the rising global prevalence of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), effective treatment strategies are essential. Sodium–glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors, key in T2DM management, offer benefits beyond glycemic control. This study aimed to assess global research trends, hotspots, and future directions of SGLT2 inhibitor studies in T2DM through bibliometric analysis.Methods:English-language publications on SGLT2 inhibitors in T2DM (2014–2024) were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection. Bibliometric techniques were applied to analyze publication output, geographic distributions, institutional contributions, and citation patterns. Visualization and network analyses were conducted using VOSviewer (v1.6.19) and CiteSpace (v6.3.1.0).Results:A total of 4,146 publications were identified. The USA led in research output (1,152 publications), followed by Japan (857) and China (792). Annual publications grew steadily. Keyword and citation analyses revealed a shift in research focus from glycemic control to cardiovascular and renal protection. The most cited paper, Dapagliflozin and Cardiovascular Outcomes (3,842 citations), emphasized the cardiovascular benefits of SGLT2 inhibitors, reflecting the evolving “prevention–treatment integration” approach in diabetes care.Conclusions:This analysis provides a comprehensive overview of global SGLT2 inhibitor research in T2DM. The findings highlight the transition from metabolic control to cardiorenal protection, guiding future research and clinical practice.

Introduction and Background

Diabetes mellitus has emerged as a global public health crisis. According to the International Diabetes Federation, approximately 537 million adults aged between 20 and 79 years were living with diabetes in 2021, with projections suggesting this number will rise to 783 million by 2045.[1] Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) constitutes 85–95% of all diabetes cases. The significant morbidity and mortality associated with T2DM primarily stem from chronic hyperglycemia-induced complications, including macrovascular disease, microvascular damage, and neuropathic disorders, which represent the leading causes of diabetes-related deaths.[2] Consequently, maintaining optimal glycemic control remains paramount for slowing disease progression. Primary care physicians play a pivotal role in early diagnosis, continuous monitoring, and holistic management of this chronic condition, particularly in resource-limited settings.

Traditional hypoglycemic agents, including insulin and sulfonylureas, pose challenges in the primary care process as they are limited by their adverse effects, especially the risk of hypoglycemia and weight gain during long-term treatment. The introduction of sodium–glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors has revolutionized T2DM management by offering a novel insulin-independent mechanism of action. These agents selectively inhibit SGLT2 in the proximal renal tubules, promoting urinary glucose excretion and establishing a unique “glucuretic” approach to glycemic control.[3] Importantly, SGLT2 inhibitors have demonstrated pleiotropic benefits extending beyond glucose lowering, including cardiovascular risk reduction and renal protection.[4] These advantages have positioned SGLT2 inhibitors as first-line therapy for T2DM patients with concomitant cardiovascular or renal disease.[5] This shift underscores the need for primary care physicians to stay abreast of changing evidence to optimize patient selection and monitoring. In recent years, SGLT2 inhibitors have attracted growing research attention due to their multifaceted benefits extending beyond glucose control. Landmark trials (e.g., EMPA-REG OUTCOME, DECLARE-TIMI 58, and DAPA-CKD) have consistently demonstrated that SGLT2 inhibitors significantly reduce the incidence of cardiovascular events and renal complications, thereby making them a key component in the comprehensive management of diabetes.[678] Consequently, understanding the evolution and research focus surrounding these agents has become an important topic in the diabetes research community.

Bibliometric analysis offers a quantitative and visual method for assessing research trends, collaboration networks, and emerging hotspots within a field. By analyzing publication trends, co-authorship networks, and keyword co-occurrence, it helps identify key studies, leading institutions, and future research directions.[9] This approach not only summarizes current knowledge but also highlights research gaps, facilitating evidence-based clinical and academic progress. This study conducted a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of T2DM and SGLT2 inhibitors, using the Web of Science Core Collection as the data source. We applied VOSviewer (version 1.6.19) for network visualization and cluster analysis, and CiteSpace (version 6.3.1.0) for temporal mapping and burst detection. Our analysis combined both quantitative metrics (e.g., annual publication volume, geographical distribution, and journal impact factors) and qualitative assessments (e.g., co-authorship networks, co-citation analysis, keyword co-occurrence, and research frontier identification). This approach aims to map the development of T2DM/SGLT2 inhibitor research over the past decade, identify core research teams and collaboration networks, and detect emerging trends.

Previous bibliometric analyses have examined research landscapes in various areas of diabetes management. For instance, studies have mapped global trends in metformin research, insulin therapy, and diabetic complications such as nephropathy and retinopathy, revealing the evolution of treatment strategies and interdisciplinary collaborations.[10111213] However, few bibliometric studies have specifically focused on SGLT2 inhibitors in the context of T2DM. Given the rapid expansion of literature and the shift in research emphasis from glucose lowering to cardiorenal protection, a comprehensive bibliometric assessment of this field remains lacking. Therefore, this study aims to systematically analyze global research trends, hotspots, and collaboration patterns related to SGLT2 inhibitors in T2DM to provide insights for future clinical and academic development.

Materials and Methods

Data sources

Data source justification

The Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) was selected as the data source because it provides standardized citation information, detailed bibliographic records, and compatibility with bibliometric analysis tools such as VOSviewer and CiteSpace. Although other databases (e.g., Scopus and PubMed) also index relevant studies, WoSCC was chosen to ensure data uniformity, minimize duplication, and maintain analytical accuracy. Prior bibliometric studies in diabetes research have similarly relied on WoSCC as a representative and authoritative database.

Search strategy

WOSCC, a widely used and influential database for scientific literature, was employed for this bibliometric analysis.[14] The search strategy utilized the following query: TS = (“SGLT2 Inhibitor”) AND (“Type 2 Diabetes”). Articles published between 2014 and 2024 were retrieved. To minimize potential omissions due to frequent database updates, the final search was conducted on August 15, 2024. The initial search yielded 4,626 articles.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Publications were included if they met the following criteria: (1) written in English; (2) focused on SGLT2 inhibitors in T2DM; and (3) classified as original research articles or review papers. We excluded conference abstracts, editorials, letters, commentaries, meeting reports, and non-English documents to ensure data consistency and quality. Only English-language publications were included to ensure consistency in data processing and compatibility with bibliometric visualization software. Although this restriction may exclude some non-English studies, most influential and highly cited articles on SGLT2 inhibitors are published in English-indexed journals. Therefore, the potential impact on the overall research trends is expected to be minimal.

Data extraction

To minimize potential omissions from database updates, we finalized the search on August 15, 2024. All records were exported in plain text format with “Full Record and Cited References.” Two investigators independently screened the literature, achieving 95% initial agreement, with discrepancies resolved through consensus.

Research methods

Quantitative analyses of annual publications, countries, journals, authors, cited literature, keywords, and impact factors were performed using the statistical analysis function integrated within the Web of Science (WOS) system. The impact factors were derived from the Journal Citation Reports (JCR) published in 2023 and were used to assess the scientific value of the literature. The data were then analyzed using VOSviewer (version 1.6.19) and CiteSpace (version 6.3.1.0) software to elucidate research hotspots in different time periods and to identify future research dynamics in this field.[1516]

Research indicators

This study evaluates research impact through publication volume (productivity), citation frequency (academic influence), and journal metrics (2023 JCR data for publication quality),[17] supplemented by betweenness centrality analysis to identify key knowledge hubs in collaboration networks. Together, these indicators comprehensively assess the research output and academic standing of countries and authors in this specialized field.

Ethical considerations

This study is based on secondary data retrieved from the WOSCC and involves no human participants or identifiable personal information. Therefore, ethical approval and informed consent were not required. All analyses were conducted in accordance with relevant guidelines and ethical research principles.

Discussion

International cooperation and research

International collaborative network analysis reveals geographical disparities in research contributions: The United States leads in the number of published papers, while the United Kingdom stands out for its academic influence, particularly excelling in intermediary centrality scores (0.87). International research further validated the findings of the large-scale clinical trials DAPA-CKD and EMPEROR-Reduced, demonstrating significant cardiovascular and renal protective benefits of SGLT2 inhibitors.[181920] However, meta-analyses indicate that although the benefits of SGLT2 inhibitors are generally consistent across most patient populations, their efficacy remains uncertain in individuals with end-stage renal disease or those at lower cardiovascular risk.[2122] These discrepancies underscore the need for future research to focus more intently on specific populations, exploring which patient groups derive the greatest benefit from SGLT2 inhibitors. Consequently, conducting targeted clinical studies is crucial for clarifying their therapeutic efficacy and optimizing patient selection.

Journal and literature impact

Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism remains a key journal in the field of SGLT2 inhibitor research, publishing cutting-edge studies on treatment efficacy. However, it is important to acknowledge that the impact of studies published in high-profile journals like the New England Journal of Medicine, such as the EMPA-KIDNEY trial, often has a broader and more immediate influence on clinical practice. This disparity in impact could be attributed to differences in journal readership and the perceived prestige of the studies they publish. A comparative analysis between these journals could reveal how publication venues influence the translation of research findings into clinical guidelines.

Research hotspots and trends

Recent studies on SGLT2 inhibitors have shifted focus from purely glycemic control to broader cardiovascular–renal outcomes, including cardiovascular mortality, heart failure hospitalization rates, and renal function deterioration.[23] This shift reflects growing emphasis on multidimensional benefits, providing primary care physicians with more comprehensive grounds for treatment decisions. Concurrently, increasing attention has turned to inflammatory mediators in the pathogenesis of T2DM.[24] In primary care, screening for baseline markers such as urinary albumin excretion rate and estimated glomerular filtration rate, along with inflammatory markers like high-sensitivity C-reactive protein, has become a key strategy. Although SGLT2 inhibitors are increasingly used as first-line therapy in patients with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) or those at high risk for ASCVD and chronic kidney disease, their efficacy and long-term effects require more in-depth comparison with other therapies.[25] In diabetes management, personalized treatment strategies are emerging as a trend, particularly for patients with cardiovascular and renal complications, reflecting a growing emphasis on tailored care. However, as the evidence base for SGLT2 inhibitors expands, balancing practical considerations, such as drug costs, patient adherence, and long-term safety, will pose key challenges for future research.

Evolution of research hotspots

Research on T2DM and SGLT2 inhibitors has evolved from a primary focus on glycemic control to encompass more comprehensive cardiorenal management, reflecting a growing understanding of their broader therapeutic effects. This shift has led to more integrated research across nephrology, cardiology, and endocrinology, advancing our understanding of SGLT2 inhibitors’ effects on multiple organ systems. While current studies, including numerous double-blind, randomized controlled trials, provide robust evidence for the efficacy and safety of SGLT2 inhibitors, it is crucial to assess whether these findings address the needs of specific populations, such as the elderly or those with advanced kidney disease, who may respond differently to treatment.[26] Future research will likely focus on managing T2DM with comorbid cardiovascular and renal conditions, aligning with the growing demand for complex case management in primary care.

Strengths and limitations

Our study visualizes the research on T2DM and SGLT2 inhibitors using bibliometrics to help scholars better understand the hotspots and trends in the field. However, our study also has limitations. We only analyzed English-language articles published in WoSCC during 2014–2024, which may have overlooked the results of newly published studies and those from other databases. Nonetheless, it is still valuable to use visualization methods to understand the current status, hotspots, and trends in a field.

Conclusions

This study systematically analyzed research hotspots and trends in T2DM and SGLT2 inhibitors from 2014 to 2024 using bibliometric methods. The results demonstrate that SGLT2 inhibitors have significant efficacy in glycemic control, cardiovascular protection, and renal protection, showing particular clinical value for T2DM patients with comorbidity cardiovascular and renal diseases—a common scenario in primary care settings. Future studies should promote multidisciplinary collaboration across family medicine, public health, and pharmacy to optimize graded diagnosis and treatment protocols for T2DM. In summary, this study provides a comprehensive reference for primary care practitioners, aiding clinical decision-making in community diabetes management and offering evidence-based guidance for future research directions.

Ex vivo qualitative and quantitative analysis of fluorescently-labeled Hsp90 drug in human tumors
Authors: Sarah J Backe, Dimitra Bourboulia, Mark R Woodford, Mehdi Mollapour
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractHeat shock protein 90 (Hsp90) stabilizes numerous oncogenic proteins, making it a key therapeutic target in cancer. This protocol details an ex vivo method using freshly resected human renal cell carcinoma tissues to evaluate fluorescently labeled Hsp90 inhibitor ganetespib accumulation in tumor versus normal tissue. By preserving the native tumor architecture, this method offers a physiologically relevant alternative to xenograft models. This protocol combines flow cytometry and confocal microscopy to quantitatively and visually assess ganetespib uptake, providing insight into drug distribution and therapeutic response in human cancers. For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Dunn et al. and Woodford et al.Keywords: Hsp90, Chaperone, Cochaperone, Mps1, Hsp90 inhibitors, Ganetespib, Phosphorylation, Ex vivo, Tumor tissues

Prior to protocol

This protocol was used to determine selective accumulation of fluorescent ganetespib (FL-Ganetespib) in human renal cell carcinoma tumors versus normal renal tissue, ex vivo. Tumors included clear cell (ccRCC) and papillary subtypes.1, 2, 3 This protocol can also be used to assess renal tumor uptake of fluorescently labeled peptides.4

Expected outcomes

To confirm preferential accumulation of FL-Ganetespib it is expected that quantification of MFI by flow cytometry will show elevated MFI values in tumor cells relative to the normal adjacent cells. confirm preferential accumulation of FL-Ganetespib. Additionally, it is expected that confocal microscopy will reveal green fluorescence (FL-Ganetespib) restricted to tumor regions. Pre-treatment with a drug known to cause resistance of Hsp90 to inhibitors (10 µM NMS-P715) abrogates drug accumulation, evidenced by decreased intensity of green fluorescence (Figure 3A). For examples of how we have applied this protocol to the analysis of RCC tumors, please see.1, 2, 3, 4

Hsp90 inhibitor uptake in normal and ccRCC tissues. (a) Clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) and adjacent normal tissue were ex vivo pre-treated with and without the Mps1 inhibitor NMS-P715 (10 µM) for 24 h followed by ex vivo treatment of FL-Ganetespib (100 nM) for 6 h. Tissues were immunofluorescent stained for Hsp90 (red) and mounted using ProLong Gold with DAPI to visual the nucleus (blue). FL-Ganetespib (green) uptake and distribution is readily seen within tumor tissues. (b) Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining of untreated ccRCC tumor and normal adjacent tissues.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Flow data expressed as MFI. Convert to log10 scale and analyze with Student’s t test. Repeat experiments with ≥3 biological replicates.

Limitations

Dependent on fresh human tissue availability and timing post-surgery. Heterogeneity in tumor cell composition may affect uptake measurements. Limited temporal observation compared to in vivo pharmacokinetics.

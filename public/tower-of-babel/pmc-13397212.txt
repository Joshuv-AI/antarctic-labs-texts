Association between Salivary Cortisol, Secretory IgA, and Inflammatory Cytokines with Early Childhood Caries: A Cross-sectional Analytical Study
Authors: Sonu Acharya, Brinda Suhas Godhi, Triveni Mohan Nalawade, Rachappa Mallikarjuna, Vinay Shivagange, Amur Alsenaidi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObjective:Early childhood caries (ECC) is a multifactorial disease driven by biological and psychosocial determinants. Identifying noninvasive biomarkers could improve risk stratification and personalized prevention. The aim of the study is to evaluate the association of salivary cortisol, total secretory immunoglobulin A (sIgA), and pro-inflammatory cytokines (interleukin-6 [IL-6], interleukin-1β [IL-1β], and tumor necrosis factor-alpha [TNF-α]) with ECC in preschool children.IL-1β.Mathods:This cross-sectional analytical study included 120 children aged 3–6 years (60 ECC; 60 caries-free). Unstimulated saliva was collected via passive drooling. Biomarkers were quantified using high-sensitivity enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay kits. Multivariable logistic regression and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis were performed, adjusting for age, sex, feeding practices, oral hygiene, and socioeconomic status (SES).Results:Children with ECC exhibited significantly higher levels of salivary cortisol (8.92 + ˗2.11 vs. 5.21 + ˗1.98 ng/mL, P < 0.001), IL-6 (42.81 + ˗10.29 vs. 26.44+ ˗9.87 pg/mL, P < 0.001), and TNF-α (P = 0.002) compared to controls. Conversely, sIgA levels were significantly lower in the ECC group (11.34 + ˗4.12 vs.18.25 + ˗5.03 mg/dL, P < 0.001). ROC analysis identified cortisol (area under the curve [AUC] = 0.89) and sIgA (AUC = 0.86) as excellent diagnostic indicators. In the final multivariable model, cortisol (OR 3.94, 95% confidence interval [CI] 2.11–7.35) and sIgA (OR 0.44, 95% CI 0.28–0.70) remained independently associated with ECC after adjusting for SES and behavioral covariates.Conclusion:Salivary cortisol, sIgA, and inflammatory cytokines are significantly associated with ECC, reflecting complex interactions between stress physiology, mucosal immunity, and oral dysbiosis. While these biomarkers show promise as early-warning indicators, longitudinal data are required to establish their discriminatory utility for caries onset.

INTRODUCTION

Early childhood caries (ECC) remains one of the most prevalent chronic diseases in children worldwide, disproportionately affecting those from lower socioeconomic backgrounds and underserved populations. Despite advancements in preventive dentistry, ECC continues to show a persistent global burden, with recent systematic reviews reporting prevalence rates ranging from 23% to over 70%, depending on region and diagnostic criteria.[1,2,3] Traditional risk assessment tools—such as feeding history, oral hygiene practices, socioeconomic status (SES), and microbial load—have demonstrated variable predictive value and limited reproducibility across populations.[4,5,6] Consequently, there is a rising interest in biological markers that could improve early prediction, risk stratification, and personalized prevention of ECC. Salivary biomarkers represent an attractive diagnostic medium due to noninvasive collection, high patient acceptance, and the ability to reflect both systemic and local oral conditions.[7] Among these, cortisol, a key stress hormone, has gained attention because psychosocial stress influences immune function, bacterial colonization patterns, and health-seeking behaviors—all of which are potential contributors to ECC.[8,9,10] Elevated basal cortisol levels have been associated with altered salivary flow, reduced buffering capacity, and changes in carbohydrate-rich dietary preferences in young children.[11] Similarly, secretory Immunoglobulin A (sIgA) plays a crucial role in mucosal immunity by inhibiting bacterial adherence, neutralizing toxins, and contributing to oral immune homeostasis.[12] Previous studies have shown conflicting results regarding the association between sIgA levels and caries development, with some reporting decreased sIgA in high-caries children, while others observed compensatory elevations.[13,14,15] These discrepancies highlight the need for further standardized and well-controlled studies. In addition, salivary inflammatory cytokines, including interleukin-6 (IL-6), interleukin-1β (IL-1β), and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), have emerged as potential markers of early pathological changes within the oral cavity. ECC is increasingly understood as a dysbiosis-driven inflammatory disease, where cariogenic bacteria trigger host immune responses detectable in saliva.[16,17,18] Elevated levels of inflammatory mediators may reflect both microbial challenge and soft-tissue responses even before clinical cavitation becomes evident. Given these knowledge gaps, integrating stress physiology (cortisol), mucosal immunity sIgA, and inflammatory markers (IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α) into ECC discriminatory models may improve diagnostic accuracy. However, relatively few studies have simultaneously examined these markers in preschool children using robust statistical modeling.

The present study aims to compare salivary cortisol, sIgA, and selected inflammatory cytokines between children with ECC and caries-free controls, and to evaluate their discriminatory utility using multivariate logistic regression and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This analytical cross-sectional study was carried out in the Department of Pediatric and Preventive Dentistry, following approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee (IEC No.: IDS/IEC/2025/07). Written informed consent was obtained from the parents or guardians of all participating children before enrolment. The sample size was calculated using the effect size derived from previous salivary biomarker studies on ECC (d = 0.65), with a significance level of α =0.05 and a statistical power of 80%, which yielded a minimum required sample of 108 participants. To compensate for potential dropouts and to enhance statistical validity, a final sample of 120 children was recruited, comprising 60 children with (ECC group) and 60 caries-free children (control group).

Children aged 3–6 years who demonstrated cooperative behavior (Frankl ratings + or ++) and whose parents provided informed consent were included in the study. Children aged 3–6 years were included as this age range represents the peak vulnerability period for ECC, corresponding with complete eruption of the primary dentition, dietary transitions, and developing salivary immune function. This age group also aligns with the American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry (AAPD) definition of ECC, ensuring diagnostic consistency. Children were excluded if they had any acute illness, a history of antibiotic or systemic steroid use within the preceding 3 months, systemic diseases or immunological disorders, or if they were unable to cooperate during saliva collection procedures. ECC was diagnosed by a calibrated pediatric dentist using AAPD criteria, and inter-examiner reliability for diagnostic assessment was established prior to data collection, yielding a kappa coefficient of 0.87.

Unstimulated saliva was collected between 9 and 11 AM using passive drooling. Samples were stored at −20°C. Participants were instructed not to eat, drink, or brush their teeth for at least 1 h before sample collection. Saliva was obtained using the passive drooling method while the child sat upright in a relaxed posture. Samples were immediately placed on ice, transported to the laboratory, centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 10 min, and the resulting supernatant was aliquoted and stored at –20°C until analysis. All saliva samples were coded numerically, and the laboratory personnel performing the enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) were blinded to the clinical caries status of the participants. Salivary cortisol levels were measured using a competitive ELISA, whereas sIgA levels were quantified using a sandwich ELISA technique. Inflammatory markers, including IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α, were assessed using high-sensitivity ELISA kits designed to detect low concentrations of cytokines in pediatric saliva. All assays were performed in duplicate, and mean values were used for analysis to ensure accuracy and reproducibility. Salivary cortisol was measured using a competitive ELISA kit (Manufacturer: DRG International, USA; Catalog No.: SLV-2930; Sensitivity: 0.07 ng/mL; Intra-assay CV: 6.2%; Inter-assay CV: 7.8%). sIgA was measured using a sandwich ELISA kit (Salimetrics LLC, USA; Catalog No.: 1-1602; sensitivity: 2.5 µg/mL; intra-assay CV: 4.5%; Inter-assay CV: 6.1%).IL-1β.

IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α were quantified using high-sensitivity ELISA kits (R&D Systems, USA; Catalog Nos.: HS600B, HSLB00D, HSTA00E; sensitivity <0.1 pg/mL; CVs <10%). IL-1β sIgA levels assessed in this study represent total salivary sIgA, reflecting overall mucosal immune competence rather than Streptococcus mutans–specific antibodies. Total sIgA plays a critical role in inhibiting bacterial adherence, modulating biofilm formation, and maintaining oral immune homeostasis.

In addition, demographic information, feeding patterns, oral hygiene practices, and SES were recorded through a structured questionnaire administered to parents. Caries experience was quantified using the dmft index. SES: SES was recorded using a validated scale (Modified Kupuswamy scale) and included as a covariate in regression analysis. Multivariable logistic regression included SES, age, sex, feeding habits, and oral hygiene practices [Table 1 and 2].

Statistical analysis was conducted using IBM Statistical Package for the Social Sciences Version 26 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Normality of the data was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Group comparisons were performed using independent t-tests for parametric variables, and Mann–Whitney U tests for non-parametric variables. Pearson or Spearman correlation coefficients were used to evaluate associations between biomarker levels and caries severity. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed to identify independently associated markers of ECC after adjusting for confounding variables. Diagnostic performance of cortisol, sIgA, and inflammatory markers was assessed using ROC curve analysis, with area under the curve values calculated for each biomarker and for combined discriminatory models. Statistical significance was set at P <0.05 [Table 3].

RESULTS

INTERPRETATION

After adjustment for age, sex, feeding practices, oral hygiene habits, and SES, salivary cortisol and sIgA remained independently associated with ECC. Cortisol showed a strong positive association, while sIgA demonstrated a protective effect. IL‑6 retained a modest but significant association, whereas TNF‑α was not independently associated with ECC in the final model [Table 4].

DISCUSSION

The present study investigated the role of salivary cortisol, sIgA, and inflammatory cytokines as potential early, independently associated markers of ECC. The findings demonstrated significantly elevated levels of cortisol, IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α in children with ECC compared to controls, whereas sIgA showed a decreasing trend, supporting the hypothesis that ECC involves complex interactions between stress physiology, immunity, and inflammatory processes.

STRESS, CORTISOL, AND EARLY CHILDHOOD CARIES

The observed increase in cortisol levels among children with ECC aligns with earlier studies linking psychosocial stress to caries development.[19,20,21] High cortisol levels may suppress protective immune functions, modify salivary composition, and contribute to behavioral factors such as preference for sugary foods or irregular oral hygiene.[22,23] Research also indicates a bidirectional relationship: dental pain or untreated decay may itself elevate stress levels, contributing to a cyclical interaction between biological vulnerability and behavioral outcomes.[24]

Our logistic regression model showed that cortisol remained a strong independent predictor of ECC even after adjusting for socioeconomic and behavioral variables. This reinforces cortisol’s potential utility as a biopsychosocial biomarker that captures risk dimensions often overlooked by conventional caries risk assessment tools. The present findings demonstrate significant associations between ECC and salivary cortisol, sIgA, and inflammatory cytokines. However, given the cross-sectional design, these biomarkers cannot be interpreted as definitive predictors of disease onset but as independently associated markers. Rather, they may reflect biological alterations associated with existing disease, including stress-related physiological responses, compromised mucosal immunity, and inflammation triggered by oral dysbiosis. Elevated cortisol levels may arise both as a contributing factor—through immune modulation and behavioral influences—and as a consequence of chronic dental pain or psychosocial stress related to untreated caries. Similarly, reduced total sIgA levels may indicate impaired mucosal immune defense, although compensatory increases have been reported in other studies depending on disease stage and immune response dynamics.

SECRETORY IMMUNOGLOBULIN A AND MUCOSAL IMMUNITY

The reduced sIgA levels observed in ECC children are consistent with studies reporting compromised mucosal immunity in caries-susceptible children.[25,26] Lower sIgA likely impairs the child’s ability to neutralize cariogenic organisms, facilitating colonization by S. mutans and other acidogenic bacteria.[27] However, some previous research reported higher sIgA in caries-active children, interpreted as a compensatory response.[28] Differences in assay techniques, age groups, and caries severity may explain this heterogeneity. The inverse relationship found here strengthens the hypothesis that insufficient mucosal immune protection could predispose children to rapid ECC progression. It is important to note that total salivary sIgA was measured in this study rather than S. mutans-specific IgA. While pathogen-specific antibodies may offer greater specificity, total sIgA remains a relevant indicator of general oral immune competence and barrier function. The absence of antigen-specific IgA measurement represents a limitation and should be addressed in future studies.

INFLAMMATORY MARKERS AND ORAL DYSBIOSIS

Inflammatory cytokines showed strong positive associations with ECC, consistent with the understanding of caries as a dysbiosis-triggered inflammatory condition.[29,30] Elevated IL-6 and IL-1β reflect innate immune activation in response to cariogenic biofilms, while TNF-α may indicate deeper inflammatory involvement. Cytokine elevation in early disease stages suggests their potential as early subclinical indicators, preceding observable lesions. IL-1β.

ROC curve analyses demonstrated good diagnostic accuracy for IL-6 and IL-1β, supporting their value in screening children at risk for aggressive ECC phenotypes IL-1β.

INTEGRATED BIOMARKER PROFILING

One important finding is that a combined biomarker model (cortisol + sIgA + IL-6) showed significantly better discriminatory performance than any single biomarker. This supports a multifactorial understanding of ECC and suggests that future chairside diagnostic kits may require multiplex assays rather than single-marker readouts.

CLINICAL IMPLICATIONS

If validated further, salivary biomarker-based screening could be incorporated into routine pediatric dental visits, especially for high-risk populations. Biomarkers could complement visual-tactile examination and microbial tests, enabling earlier intervention strategies such as intensified fluoride therapy, behavior modification, or stress-management counseling.

This study reveals significant associations between salivary biomarkers and ECC. Elevated cortisol may indicate stress-related pathways contributing to caries susceptibility, while reduced sIgA reflects compromised mucosal immunity. Inflammatory cytokines suggest dysbiosis-associated immune activation. Because this is a cross-sectional study, causation and prediction cannot be inferred.

However, standardization of assays and cost-effectiveness analyses will be essential before routine clinical implementation.

CONCLUSION

Elevated cortisol and reduced sIgA emerged as independently associated markers of ECC, suggesting that both stress-induced physiological changes and compromised mucosal immunity play crucial roles in increasing susceptibility to caries. The observed rise in pro-inflammatory cytokines further reflects early host–microbial dysbiosis, supporting the concept of ECC as an inflammatory condition rather than merely a microbial imbalance.

These findings collectively indicate that salivary biomarkers hold considerable promise as noninvasive, child-friendly, and biologically meaningful early-warning indicators for ECC. Incorporation of biomarker-based risk assessment into routine pediatric dental screenings could enable earlier identification of high-risk children, more targeted preventive strategies, and personalized management approaches. Future large-scale longitudinal studies are needed to validate these biomarkers, determine optimal diagnostic thresholds, and explore integrated multi-marker panels suitable for development into chairside diagnostic kits. Salivary cortisol, sIgA, and inflammatory cytokines are significantly associated with ECC. These biomarkers may serve as potential early indicators, but discriminatory utility cannot be concluded without longitudinal data.

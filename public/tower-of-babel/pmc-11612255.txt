Seismic risk mitigation at Campi Flegrei in volcanic unrest
Authors: Iunio Iervolino, Pasquale Cito, Melania De Falco, Gaetano Festa, Marcus Herrmann, Anthony Lomax, Warner Marzocchi, Antonio Santo, Claudio Strumia, Luigi Massaro, Antonio Scala, Francesco Scotto di Uccio, Aldo Zollo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Campi Flegrei is a densely populated volcanic area in Italy. Its inner caldera periodically experiences uplift and subsidence, known as bradyseism, also accompanied by seismic activity. In the last decade, with uplift rates up to 2 cm/month, about nine-thousand earthquakes were recorded. Upon request of the local administration, the most updated data were collected and analyzed to evaluate the risk management strategy consisting of structural retrofitting according to the building code. Here it is shown that the reference moment magnitude is in the range \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left({{\mathrm{4.4,5.1}}}\right)$$\end{document}4.4, 5.1, based on fault mapping, geomorphological inference, earthquake relocation, stress-drop analysis, and ground motion modelling. Earthquake forecasting enabled computing the exceedance probabilities of these magnitudes. Earthquake engineering showed that the minimum magnitudes expected to cause exceedance of design ground motion, are larger than the reference magnitudes. Finally, the risk reduction implied by the safety levels of new constructions was assessed for reinforced concrete buildings.

Introduction

Campi Flegrei (Phlegraean Fields) is a volcanic area bordering the city of Naples, in southern Italy1. The caldera of Campi Flegrei was shaped by two major volcanic eruptions, about forty-thousands and ten-thousands years ago. After its formation, numerous eruptions occurred (the last one in 1538), forming cones varying in size and shape. A prominent volcanic unrest phenomenon of the caldera is the slow, yet continuous (over the millennia), uplift and subsidence of the ground, such that Baia, a major town of the Roman empire, is now submerged2. This phenomenon goes by the name of bradyseism, the etymology of which is precisely slow earthquake. It occurs as a bell-shaped deformation of the inner caldera, which has a radius of a few kilometers, roughly centered at the town of Pozzuoli (Fig. 1a). The origin of the bradyseism has been attributed to the action of pressure changes caused by magma intrusions at shallow depth and/or to the response of the shallow hydro-thermal system to changes in pore pressure and fluid content. It may be driven by the periodic migration toward the surface of shallow crustal fluids, possibly generated by degassing processes at the primary sill-like magma reservoir detected at 8 km3. Since the beginning of the last century, there have been periods of rapid rise and slow subsidence, identified as bradyseismic crises, the semi-last occurred between 1982 and 1984, with uplift rates of several centimeters per month, and accompanied by seismicity featuring thousands of recorded earthquakes4, the largest of which had duration magnitude \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left({M}_{d}\right)$$\end{document}Md equal to 4.2. Ever since, after a period of slow subsidence, which typically does not correspond to recorded earthquakes, there has been a steady rise in ground level since 2011. This uplift accelerated in 2018, reaching rates of about 2 cm/month in 20245, and resulting in about 9000 recorded earthquakes, most of which since 2022, with peaks of about 1000 earthquakes per month (Fig. 1c). The ground deformation apparently corresponds to a spatially variable stress that seems to concentrate at pre-existing faults and fracture zones related to the inner caldera resurgence processes and related volcanic phenomena. High-precision relocation of the seismicity recorded in the last decade7 (to follow) displays epicenters coherent with the fracture zones activated during the 1982–1984 crisis8, but with new sources apparently involved in the unrest started a few years ago and still going on in 2024.

Analyzing the ground motion recorded in the area from the largest magnitude earthquakes of the recent seismicity, two features emerge quite clearly. (i) Close to the epicenters, the structural seismic actions can be, at some vibration periods, non-negligible, although the magnitudes occurred so far are generally neglected in the national probabilistic seismic hazard analysis9 (PSHA) that has determined the design ground motions prescribed by the most recent Italian building code10. (ii) The attenuation is such that at a few kilometers in terms of epicentral distances, the ground motion intensity becomes negligible for structural engineering (something already pointed out by Richter11, about seventy years ago, when discussing the low-magnitude yet destructive earthquakes at the nearby Casamicciola, on the Ischia Island).

Based on the monitoring of geophysical and geochemical parameters, it is claimed that the uplift phase at Campi Flegrei, of the recent years, does not show the evidence for magma uprising at shallow depths and then for an impending eruption13–16. In the absence of clearly identified precursors to a volcanic eruption, the main risk in the area is, in 2024, related to purely seismic hazard, which is the sole focus of the study herein presented.

National census data enable estimating, in the deforming area, highlighted in Fig. 1a, there are about 85,000 inhabitants and 15,000 buildings. Moreover, the Campi Flegrei area is borderline to the city of Naples, the third largest by population in Italy. The building stock, as for most of the country17, is designed with now-obsolete seismic codes or without any seismic provision. In other words, buildings are not expected to withstand, with the same level of safety, the design ground motions from the most recent (2018) building code18. To picture the situation, Fig. 2d reports the percentage of residential buildings in Pozzuoli, the municipality where most of the epicenters of the recorded seismicity have occurred, divided by structural typology, number of stories (st) and construction era.

The increasing maximum magnitude observed over time sparked public concern, which lead issuing a national government decree19 allocating resources to consider seismic risk mitigation; e.g., buildings’ seismic retrofitting. Risk mitigation via reduction of seismic structural vulnerability requires the provision of design ground motion (in terms of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${Sa}$$\end{document}Sa), which—in turn—is related to the magnitudes that can occur in the area, their exceedance probability, and the shaking propagation in the region. This was the question of the local government of the Naples’ metropolitan area to the University of Naples Federico II in late 2023 (see section “Acknowledgments”), which led to the study the results and methods of which are discussed in the following.

Although the study is very specific to the bradyseism of Campi Flegrei, the interdisciplinary approach integrating data and methods, from geology, geophysics, and earthquake engineering, intends to represent an example of rapidly assembling data and state-of-the-art science20,21, to evaluate risk mitigation strategies and then aid decision making that can possibly be of reference for other similar emergencies.

In this work, the relocated earthquakes recorded since 2014 are used to derive the stress drop and rupture length scaling with magnitude in the deforming region. The seismic events are put in relation to the mapped faults in the area and its geomorphological features. The magnitude range of earthquakes occurring in the deforming region is derived, also by quantifying the probability of exceedance of these magnitudes, to contemplate the case in which the rupture develops on faults different from those mapped and/or extends beyond those. The unprecedented amount of earthquake data recently recorded is also used to calibrate a region-specific ground motion (attenuation) model (GMM) to quantify the expected shaking. The GMM is used to probabilistically determine the minimum magnitude of the earthquakes that are expected to cause exceedance of the ground motion intensities associated with life-safety and damage-limitation design of ordinary constructions by the building code (also considering their reduction by 20% and 40%, corresponding to a less demanding retrofitting). These magnitudes correspond to the so-called strong earthquakes for structural engineering22. Comparing the strong earthquakes with the reference magnitudes for the area enables evaluating the effectiveness of the foreseen risk mitigation policies. Furthermore, the fatality risk reduction implied by bringing the existing 3-st reinforced concrete (RC) buildings (the most represented typology in the area) to the safety of new constructions, is also quantified for the Rione Terra site (Pozzuoli), also accounting for the possible exceedance of the reference magnitudes.

Results

Minimum magnitude of strong earthquakes

The currently enforced Italian building code18, which is at the state-of-the-art internationally, requires engineers to design earthquake-resistant structures, or retrofit existing pre- or low-code existing constructions, in compliance with predefined performance thresholds, or limit states. This is checked by means of ground motion intensities expressed in terms of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${Sa}\left(T\right)$$\end{document}SaT, computed via PSHA. Design ground motion intensities have a specified return period at the construction site and can be indicated as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${{sa}}_{{T}_{r}}$$\end{document}saTr. An ordinary structure designed for the damage-limitation and life-safety limit states should withstand ground motions with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${T}_{r}$$\end{document}Tr equal to 50 and 475 years, respectively. The design spectral accelerations at Pozzuoli, which is taken as the reference site, are shown in Fig. 2 as spectra.

It is important to note, for what is discussed in the following, that the PSHA study providing such design intensities is a national one contemplating earthquakes from seismic sources mostly distant from the site10, which in turn implies that earthquakes occurring close may have a large probability of exceeding them, as previous studies have demonstrated22. In other words, an earthquake occurring within the caldera, due to volcanic unrest, may have a non-negligible chance to cause exceedance of design ground motions, even if its magnitude is relatively moderate, and this is a known feature of PSHA. This issue can be further amplified by the fact that the shallowness of the Campi Flegrei events and the peculiar attenuation/radiation of the area could have been not explicitly modeled in the PSHA at the basis of the building code, also likely due to data scarcity at the time of its development. Therefore, it is needed to evaluate whether the currently increased probability of having events close to the building stock at Campi Flegrei determines inadequacy of the most recent building code’s design provisions. This, in turn, could disqualify risk mitigation policies based on retrofit of sub-standard buildings. Indeed, this was the scientific question motivating the study herein.

The first task to answer the scientific question was to evaluate the minimum moment magnitude \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left({M}_{w}\right)$$\end{document}Mw of strong earthquakes, that is, the minimum magnitude of close-by events (i.e., occurring in the caldera), likely causing the exceedance of design spectral accelerations. This was computed for a grid of sites (about 600 in number) discretizing the Campi Flegrei area. The ground motion values considered are those with return periods equal to 50 and 475 years according to the code, on B soil class according to EC8, for all the sites (these ground motion thresholds at any site in the area are similar to those for Pozzuoli). The analyses were developed considering a seismic source zone enclosing the considered faults within the uplifting region, and the specific ground motion model developed ad hoc and based on the recent earthquake catalog. The calculations refer to the case in which an earthquake would occur in the caldera with the epicenter located within 5 km or within 1 km from each site. The first case is to contemplate that any site is interested (affected) by an event in the caldera, at a maximum distance of 5 km, whereas the second refers to the even less favorable condition of an event at distances smaller than 1 km.

Reference magnitudes for the caldera

The reference magnitudes are defined here as the moment magnitude range corresponding to the mapped faults possibly activated during the volcanic unrest of these last years. These faults are determined by combining three approaches: (i) high-precision relocation of earthquakes recently recorded; (ii) faulting literature dealing with Campi Flegrei; (iii) ad hoc geological analysis. More specifically, the set of all faults found in literature, plus those inferred by the geomorphology of the area, is assembled first. It results in about 350 fault traces, shown in Fig. 5a. Then, the subset of faults for the study is derived considering the observed earthquake locations during the ongoing crisis, the compatibility between overlapping faults mapped by different studies, and relevance of the evidence given in literature for each fault. The resulting faults are 64 in number, with lengths from about 350 m to about 2.7 km (Fig. 5c). Even if the fault set includes some geological structures outside the caldera, only those enclosed, even partially, in the dashed circle with 5 km radius, indicating the deforming area of Fig. 5c, are considered to compute the reference magnitudes. Exceedance probability of reference magnitudes

For seismic risk assessment purposes, earthquake magnitudes must be associated with an exceedance probability. To account for this issue, an operational earthquake forecasting24 approach was followed, probabilistically projecting the statistical analysis of the recorded earthquake catalog or its subsets. Noteworthy, the reference magnitudes are not necessarily the maximum magnitudes that can occur during the volcanic unrest, as earthquakes may have a stress drop higher than average, and they may nucleate on unknown structures or extend beyond the mapped fault limits. Hence, it was assumed that the magnitude is distributed according to an unbounded Gutenberg–Richter (GR) relationship25.

In conclusion, this study employed a multidisciplinary and integrated approach to evaluate the feasibility of seismic risk management at Campi Flegrei during the volcanic unrest going on in 2024, based on building retrofitting via building code performance goals. Based on faults with earthquake potential and theoretical scaling relationships, the reference moment magnitudes were set in the range \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$4.4-5.1$$\end{document}4.4−5.1, with the lower bound being generally below the minimum magnitudes expected to cause exceedance of ground motions for new design. At the town of Pozzuoli, which is in the most seismically active zone of Campi Flegrei, to bring common RC residential buildings to the safety levels of new design motion would reduce the fatality risk more than seventy percent, independently of the construction era, and even in case of occurrence of earthquakes with magnitude larger than the reference magnitudes.

Geological setting

The Campi Flegrei caldera represents a nested structure resulting from the complex interaction between volcanic and regional tectonics processes. The former include the two main Pleistocene eruptions, that is the Campanian ignimbrite eruption37, and the Neapolitan yellow tuff38 eruption, with the related caldera collapses39,40, while the latter involves extensional processes determining the back-arc opening of the Tyrrhenian Sea41–43. Following the bradyseism crisis in 1982–1984, several studies investigated the potential control on the deformation of regional stress40,44–46.

The faults dataset considered was defined by combining literature studies, morpho-structural interpretation, and epicentral information. First, several geological studies were reviewed to get a preliminary picture of the faults mapped in the area40,47–52. The fault systems derived from the literature are both related to volcanic deformation (caldera collapses) and regional tectonics. Where present, the maps were extracted as images and georeferenced in a fixed coordinate system (WGS84-UTM, 33 North). The traces of faults were digitized to obtain a single map (Fig. 5a). A total of 353 fault traces were summarized and represented in a rose diagram with their strike (Fig. 5a), showing two main N140 and N40 trends, and a minor N-S set. Then, based on the present literature41,47,53, a morpho-structural survey was performed on a hill-shade map (cell size \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$5\times 5\,{{{{\rm{m}}}}}^{2}$$\end{document}5×5m2) to outline the fault systems controlled by regional tectonics trends. The considered morpho-structural features included linear scarps, coastal cliff lineaments and linear subsequent streams. In some cases, these features follow the distribution (alignments) of the volcanic edifices’ centers40,44–46. The 79 features identified are represented in Fig. 5b, with the fault systems generated by caldera collapses not included in the dataset because it is mainly controlled by volcano-tectonic processes. Two main directions (N150 and N60) and a minor N-S trend (N20) were observed.

The final dataset was developed starting from the previous maps (Fig. 5a, b) combined with the epicentral locations of the 2022–2024 seismicity record7. The dataset gathered from the literature was integrated with the fault traces inferred from the alignments of the epicenters along preferential directions and the regional trends defined from the morpho-structural analysis. Finally, the data were synthesized by applying the following criteria: (i) merging the fault traces reported by different authors; (ii) exclusion of the faults directly connected to caldera collapses; (iii) zoom into the area of the epicentral locations. The obtained map, showing the duration magnitude, epicenter locations and hypocenter depth of considered earthquakes, is composed of 64 fault traces (Fig. 5c), whose locations and trace lengths are given in Supplementary Data 3. The rose diagram from Fig. 5c shows the occurrence of two main directions (N45 and N150), while the N–S and E–W sets are less abundant. The epicentral locations (Fig. 5c) highlight that the superficial ones (0–3 km) are concentrated in the central area (corresponding to the town of Pozzuoli), where the highest number of events is registered. On the other hand, the deeper hypocenters (3–6 km) are registered mainly in the peripherical areas, especially offshore. Furthermore, the length of the fault traces spans in the 0.35–2.70 km range, with most of them in the 0.5–1 km class (Fig. 5d).

Ground motion model

Ground motion (attenuation) models in Campi Flegrei, where the propagation medium is affected by dense levels of fracturing, high-temperature fluid percolation and presence of shallow hydro-thermal reservoirs, required region-specific calibration. In fact, only a small fraction of available GMMs have been developed for volcanic areas in terms of ground motion intensity measures of engineering interest54, and their portability in different areas is questioned. In addition, the number of events recorded in the seismic sequence described above is unprecedented for Campi Flegrei. Therefore, a specific GMM was developed to produce the results in terms of minimum magnitude of strong earthquakes. Data considered pertain to 65 events with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${M}_{d}\ge 2.5$$\end{document}Md≥2.5 that occurred between 03/16/2022 and 05/20/2024, and for which \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${M}_{w}$$\end{document}Mw and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta \sigma$$\end{document}Δσ were calculated (see section “Space-time-magnitude features of seismicity”).

The GMM was calibrated using ground motion recording from stations that are located within 40 km from the epicenters of the considered events.

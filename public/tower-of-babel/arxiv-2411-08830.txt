Homogeneous quadratic Lie super algebras
R. García-Delgado

Abstract
. In this work we state a version of the double extension for homogeneous quadratic Lie super algebras that includes even and odd cases. We prove that any indecomposable, non-simple and homogeneous quadratic Lie super algebra is obtained by means of this type of double extension. We also show that with this construction we can recover previously studied cases as well as some other which can not be recover with former versions of double extensions.

Introduction

A
homogeneous quadratic Lie super algebra
is a triple
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],B)
, where
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
is a
Lie super algebra and
B:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{F}
is an invariant, non-degenerate, super-symmetric, homogeneous
and bilinear form, called it
invariant metric
. When the context is clear, we refer to a quadratic Lie super algebra only by
\mathfrak{g}
.

For classical quadratic Lie algebras there are well-known procedures to construct such structures, One of these is the double extension (see
[
7
]
). In addition, Medina-Revoy theorem proves that any indecomposable non-simple quadratic Lie algebra, is a double extension of another quadratic Lie algebra of smaller dimension. Another approach can be found in
[
5
]
, where the authors define two canonical ideals for a quadratic Lie algebra without simple ideals. Then the authors construct certain cohomology set that allows them to get classification results.

Concerning to Lie super algebras, in
[
3
]
the authors state a notion of double extension for even quadratic Lie super algebras, and prove that any indecomposable, non-simple and even quadratic Lie super algebra can be constructed using this procedure, which they called it
generalized double extension
. Just as in the classical case, the generalized double extension gives a way to construct an even quadratic Lie super algebra on a super-space of the form
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a}^{*}
, where
\mathfrak{h}
is an even
quadratic Lie super algebra and
\mathfrak{a}
is a Lie super algebra (see
[
3
]
,
Thm. 2.3
).

With regard to odd quadratic Lie super algebras, in
[
2
]
is carried out similar ideas as in the even case. Let
\mathfrak{h}
be an odd quadratic Lie super algebra and
\mathfrak{a}
be a one-dimensional super-space with odd-generator. In
[
2
]
appears the conditions to set an odd quadratic Lie
super algebra structure on the super-space
\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbf{P}(\mathfrak{a}^{*})
,
where
\mathbf{P}
is the change of parity operator (see
Thm 4.2
in
[
2
]
and
Prop.
1.1
below).

There are quadratic Lie super algebras that can not be obtained
from the results of
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
. For instance, the Heisenberg Lie super algebra
with odd bilinear form extended by an even derivation (see
[
6
]
). The underlying
super-space of this Lie super algebra is of the form:
\mathbb{F}D\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
,
where
|D|=0
,
|\hslash|=1
,
\mathfrak{h}
is a non-degenerate sub-super-space and the structure
on
\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
is of a central extension of
\mathfrak{h}
by
\mathbb{F}\hslash
.

The aim of this work is to provide a version of the generalized double extension that includes the odd case. We proceed as in
[
3
]
, with the difference that we state definitions and results that cover the odd case. We state the notion of
\delta
-coadjoint representation
of a Lie super algebra (see
Prop.
1.3
), which is a version of the coadjoint representation that deals with even and odd cases. In
Lemma
2.1
we give the notion of
\delta
-context of generalized double extension
– similar to the one given in
[
3
]
–, and in
Thm.
2.2
, we state a version of the generalized double extension that covers the odd case.

We prove that any non-simple, indecomposable and homogeneous quadratic Lie super algebra can be identified with this version of double extension (see
Cor.
3.9
). Finally, we prove that with the results obtained here, we can recover the odd case studied in
[
2
]
, as well as the Heisenberg Lie super algebra with odd bilinear form extended by an even derivation (see
[
6
]
).

1.
Basic definitions

A vector space
V
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded
, if for each
\delta
in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{0,1\}
, there is a subspace
V_{\delta}
such that
V=V_{0}\oplus V_{1}
. A non-zero element
u
in
V
is

homogeneous of degree
|u|
, if
u
belongs to
V_{|u|}
, where
|u|
is in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. A
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded space is called
super-space
. We assume the trivial graduation on the ground field
\mathbb{F}
, that is
\mathbb{F}_{0}=\mathbb{F}
and
\mathbb{F}_{1}=\{0\}
.

The
dimension
of a super-space
V=V_{0}\oplus V_{1}
is its dimension as a vector space, that is
\operatorname{dim}(V)=\operatorname{dim}(V_{0})+\operatorname{dim}(V_{1})
.

From now on we only consider homogeneous elements in definitions and statements.

A
Lie super algebra
is a super-space
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1}
, with a bilinear map
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
, satisfying:

(i)

[\mathfrak{g}_{\eta},\mathfrak{g}_{\zeta}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\eta+\zeta}
, for all
\eta,\zeta
in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

(ii)
The bilinear map
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
is
super skew-symmetric
:

[x,y]=-(-1)^{|x||y|}[y,x],\quad\text{ for all }x,y\text{ in }\mathfrak{g}.

(iii)
The bilinear map
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
satisfies the
Jacobi super identity
:

(1)

\begin{split}&\sum_{\text{cyclic}}(-1)^{|x||z|}[x,[y,z]]\\
&=(-1)^{|x||z|}[x,[y,z]]+(-1)^{|y||x|}[y,[z,x]]+(-1)^{|z||y|}[z,[x,y]]=0,\end{split}

A blinear map
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
satisfying
(i),(ii),(iii)
is called
bracket
.

An
ideal

I
of a Lie super algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a graded subspace
I=I_{0}\oplus I_{1}
, of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
[\mathfrak{g},I]\subset I
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie super algebra and let
B:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
be a bilinear form.

(i)

B
is
super-symmetric
if
B(x,y)\!\!=\!\!(-1)^{|x||y|}B(y,x)
for all
x,\!y
in
\mathfrak{g}
.

(ii)

B
is
invariant
if
B([x,y],z)=B(x,[y,z])
, for all
x,y,z
in
\mathfrak{g}
.

(iii)

B
is
even
if
B(\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{1})=B(\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{0})=\{0\}
. In this case we write
|B|=0
.

(iv)

B
is
odd
if
B(\mathfrak{g}_{\eta},\mathfrak{g}_{\eta})=\{0\}
, for all
\eta
in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. In this case we write
|B|=1
.

(v)
The bilinear form
B
is
homogeneous
if either it is even or odd.

If
B
is non-degenerate, invariant, super-symmetric and homogeneous then
B
is said to be an
invariant metric
on
\mathfrak{g}
.

A
quadratic Lie super algebra of
\delta
-degree
, is a triple
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],B)
, where
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
is a Lie super algebra and
B
is an invariant metric of
\delta
-degree, that is
|B|=\delta
.

Let
B
be a non-degenerate, super-symmetric and homogeneous bilinear form on a super-space
\mathfrak{g}
.
Let
B^{\flat}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
be the map defined by

B^{\flat}(x)\,(y)=B(x,y)
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
, then

|B^{\flat}(x)|=|B|+|x|
. A homogeneous linear map
D:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, is
skew-symmetric with respect to
B
if:
B(D(x),y)=-(-1)^{|x||D|}B(x,D(y))
. The super-space generated by skew-symmetric maps with respect to
B
, is denoted by
\mathfrak{o}(B)
.

If a Lie super algebra
\mathfrak{g}
admits an invariant metric
B
, then

B^{\flat}
is an isomorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
, that is:
B^{\flat}\circ\operatorname{ad}(x)=(-1)^{|x||B|}\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}\!(x)\circ B^{\flat}
, where
\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast})
is the
coadjoint representation
of
\mathfrak{g}
, defined by:

(2)

\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}(x)(\theta)=-(-1)^{|x||\theta|}\,\theta\circ\operatorname{ad}(x),\,\text{ for all }x\in\mathfrak{g},\text{ and }\theta\in\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}.

A
derivation
of a Lie super algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, is a homogeneous linear map
D:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, such that:
D([x,y])=[D(x),y]+(-1)^{|D||x|}[x,D(y)]
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
. The vector super-space generated by all derivations of
\mathfrak{g}
, is denoted by
\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
.

1.1.
\delta
-coadjoint representation

Let
V=V_{0}\oplus V_{1}
be a super-space. Let
\mathbf{P}(V)
be the super-space
\mathbf{P}(V)=\mathbf{P}(V)_{0}\oplus\mathbf{P}(V)_{1}
, where
\mathbf{P}(V)_{0}=V_{1}
, and
\mathbf{P}(V)_{1}=V_{0}
.

The map
V\mapsto\mathbf{P}(V)
is called
change of parity operator.
As vector spaces,
V
and
\mathbf{P}(V)
are the same but as super-spaces they are different. In order to distinguish the elements of
V
from that of
\mathbf{P}(V)
, we write
P(v)
to denote an element of
\mathbf{P}(V)
, for a given
v
in
V
. If
v
is a homogeneous element of
V
, the graded of
P(v)
is
|P(v)|=|v|+1
.

Let
\delta
be in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. We define the super-space
\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(V)
, by:

(3)

\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(V)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}V&\mbox{ if }\delta=0\\
\mathbf{P}(V)&\mbox{ if }\delta=1.\end{array}\right.

We have the following properties of
\mathbf{P}_{\delta}
.

1.1 Proposition
.

Let
U,V
and
W
be super-spaces.

(i)

\mathbf{P}_{\delta}\left(\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(V)\right)=V
.

(ii)

\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\operatorname{Hom}\left(V,W)\right)=\operatorname{Hom}\left(\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(V),W\right)=\operatorname{Hom}(V,\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(W))
.

(iii)
Let
\mathcal{L}^{2}(U;V;W)
be the super-space generated by homogeneous bilinear maps
f\!:\!U\!\times\!V\!\to\!W
. Then
\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathcal{L}^{2}(U;V;W))\!=\!\mathcal{L}^{2}(U;V;\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(W))
.

(iv)

\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(V^{*})=\left(\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(V)\right)^{*}
.

Proof.

For
\delta\!=\!0
the results are clear, we assume
\delta\!=\!1
. Let
\eta
be in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

(i)
From
\mathbf{P}(\mathbf{P}(V)_{\eta})=\mathbf{P}(V_{\eta+1})=V_{\eta+2}=V_{\eta}
, it follows
\mathbf{P}(\mathbf{P}(V))=V
.

(ii)
Let
T
be in
\operatorname{Hom}(V,W)_{0}
, then
P(T)
is in
\mathbf{P}(\operatorname{Hom}(V,W))_{0}=\operatorname{Hom}(V,W)_{1}
, that is
P(T)(V_{\eta})\subset W_{\eta+1}=\mathbf{P}(W_{\eta})
, hence
P(T)
belongs to
\operatorname{Hom}(V,\mathbf{P}(W))_{0}
, which proves that
\mathbf{P}(\operatorname{Hom}(V,W))_{0}\subset\operatorname{Hom}(V,\mathbf{P}(W))_{0}
. Similarly, from
P(T)(V_{\eta})=P(T)(\mathbf{P}(V)_{\eta+1})\subset W_{\eta+1}
, we get that
P(T)
belongs to
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P}(V),W)_{0}
, then
\mathbf{P}(\operatorname{Hom}(V,W))_{0}\subset\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P}(V),W))_{0}
.

Similar argument proves that:
\mathbf{P}(\operatorname{Hom}(V,W))_{1}\subset\operatorname{Hom}(V,\mathbf{P}(W))_{1}
, and
\mathbf{P}(\operatorname{Hom}(V,W))_{1}\subset\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P}(V),W))_{1}
; thus,

\mathbf{P}(\operatorname{Hom}(V,W))\!\subset\!\operatorname{Hom}(V,\mathbf{P}(W)),\,\text{ and }\,\mathbf{P}(\operatorname{Hom}(V,W))\!\subset\!\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P}(V),W).

Since
V
and
W
are finite dimensional, we deduce that
\mathbf{P}(\operatorname{Hom}(V,W))=\operatorname{Hom}(V,\mathbf{P}(W))=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P}(V),W)
.

(iii)
Observe that
\mathcal{L}^{2}(U;V,W)=\operatorname{Hom}(U,\operatorname{Hom}(V,W))
. Applying
(ii)
we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{L}^{2}(U;V,W))

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}(\operatorname{Hom}(U,\operatorname{Hom}(V,W)))=\operatorname{Hom}(U,\mathbf{P}(\operatorname{Hom}(V,W)))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Hom}(U,\operatorname{Hom}(V,\mathbf{P}(W)))=\mathcal{L}^{2}(U;V;\mathbf{P}(W)).

(iv)
Due to
V^{\ast}=\operatorname{Hom}(V,\mathbb{F})
, then by
(ii)
we get:

\mathbf{P}(V^{\ast})=\mathbf{P}(\operatorname{Hom}(V,\mathbb{F}))=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P}(V),\mathbb{F})=\mathbf{P}(V)^{\ast}.

∎

1.2 Remark
.

If
T
is an element of
\operatorname{Hom}(V,W)
, by
Prop.
1.1
.(ii)
,
P(T)
is an element of
\operatorname{Hom}(\textbf{P}(V),W)
, defined by:

P(T)(P(v))=T(v),\qquad\text{ for all }v\in V.

If
T
is homogeneous, then
P(T)
and
T
are different for they have different degrees.

In the next result we introduce the coadjoint representation on
\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{g})^{\ast}
.

1.3 Proposition
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
be a Lie super algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
and let
\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast})
be the coadjoint representation of
\mathfrak{g}
. For every
x
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
f
in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, let
\operatorname{ad}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{\ast}(x)(P_{\delta}(f))
be in
\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{g})^{\ast}
defined by:

(4)

\operatorname{ad}_{\delta}^{\ast}(x)\left(P_{\delta}(f)\right)\left(P_{\delta}(y)\right)=-(-1)^{(|f|+\delta)|x|}f([x,y]),\quad\text{ for all }y\in\mathfrak{g},

Then, (
4
) yields a representation
\operatorname{ad}_{\delta}^{\ast}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}\left(\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{g})^{\ast}\right)
, of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{g})^{\ast}
, such that:

(5)

\operatorname{ad}_{\delta}^{\ast}(x)\circ P_{\delta}=(-1)^{\delta|x|}P_{\delta}\circ\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}(x),\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Proof.

First we will prove (
5
). For
\delta=0
,
\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}
and
\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}
are the same (see (
2
)), so we assume
\delta=1
. Let
x,y
be in
\mathfrak{g}
and
f
be in
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
. Using (
2
) and (
4
) we get:

(6)

\operatorname{ad}_{\mathbf{1}}^{\ast}(x)(P(f))(P(y))\!=\!-(\!-1)^{(|f|+1)|x|}\!f([x,y])\!=\!(\!-1)^{|x|}\!\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}\!(x)(f)(y).

We know that
\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}(x)(f)
belongs to
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{F})
. Then by
Prop.
1.1
.(ii)
,
P(\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}(x)(f))
belongs to
\mathbf{P}(\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{F}))=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P}(\mathfrak{g});\mathbb{F})
. Hence, by
Remark
1.2
we get:
P(\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}(x)(f))(P(y))=\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}(x)(f)(y)
. We substitute this in (
6
), to obtain:

\operatorname{ad}_{\mathbf{1}}^{\ast}(x)(P(f))(P(y))=(-1)^{|x|}P(\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}(x)(f))(P(y)),

from where we deduce (
5
) for
\delta=1
. Now we shall prove that
\operatorname{ad}_{\mathbf{1}}^{\ast}
is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathbf{P}(\mathfrak{g})^{\ast}
. From (
5
) and (
6
) we get:

\displaystyle\operatorname{ad}_{\mathbf{1}}^{\ast}(x)\circ\operatorname{ad}_{\mathbf{1}}^{\ast}(y)(P(f))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|y|}\operatorname{ad}_{\mathbf{1}}^{\ast}(x)(P(\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}(y)(f)))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|x|+|y|}P\left(\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}(x)\circ\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}(y)(f)\right).

Similarly we have:

\operatorname{ad}_{\mathbf{1}}^{\ast}(y)\circ\operatorname{ad}_{\mathbf{1}}^{\ast}(x)(P(f))=(-1)^{|x|+|y|}P\left(\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}(y)\circ\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}(x)(f)\right)

Then, by (
5
) it follows

(7)

\begin{split}[\operatorname{ad}_{\mathbf{1}}^{\ast}(x),\operatorname{ad}_{\mathbf{1}}^{\ast}(y)]_{\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbf{P}(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}))}(P(f))&=(-1)^{|x|+|y|}P\left(\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}([x,y])(f)\right)\\
\,&=\operatorname{ad}_{\mathbf{1}}^{\ast}([x,y])(P(f)).\end{split}

Which proves that
\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}_{\mathbf{1}}
is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathbf{P}(\mathfrak{g})^{\ast}
.
∎

We call
\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}
, the
\delta
-coadjoint representation
of
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
.

1.2.
Generalized semi-direct product

Let
(\mathfrak{a},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{a}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}})
be Lie super algebras. Let
\Theta:\mathfrak{a}\to\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
be an even linear map and
\Lambda:\mathfrak{a}\times\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{h}
be an even super antisymmetric bilinear map, such that:

(8)

\displaystyle[\Theta(x),\Theta(y)]_{\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})}-\Theta([x,y]_{\mathfrak{a}})=\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\Lambda(x,y)),\quad\text{ and }

(9)

\displaystyle\sum_{\text{cyclic}}(-1)^{|x||z|}\left(\Theta(x)(\Lambda(y,z))+\Lambda(x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{a}})\right)=0,

for all
x,y,z
in
\mathfrak{a}
. We define the bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
on the super-space
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
, by:

(10)

[x+u,y+v]\!=\![x,y]_{\mathfrak{a}}\!+\!\Theta(x)(v)\!-\!(-1)^{|x||y|}\Theta(y)(u)\!+\!\Lambda(x,y)\!+\![u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}},

for all homogeneous elements
x+u,y+v
of
\mathfrak{g}
, that is
|x|=|u|
and
|y|=|v|
. The bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
induces a Lie super algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}
, called it the
generalized semi-direct product of
(\mathfrak{h},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}})
by
(\mathfrak{a},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{a}})
, by means of
(\Theta,\Lambda)
(see
[
1
]
and
[
3
]
).

2.
Generalized double extension of homogeneous quadratic Lie super algebras

In this section we shall construct a homogeneous quadratic Lie super algebra on a super-space of the form
\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\textbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}
, where
(\mathfrak{a},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a Lie super algebra and
(\mathfrak{h},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}},B_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a quadratic Lie super algebra of degree
\delta
, that is
|B_{\mathfrak{h}}|=\delta
. The invariant metric on
\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\textbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}
, also will have degree
\delta
.

First we need an even linear map
\rho:\mathfrak{a}\to\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{h})\cap\mathfrak{o}(B_{\mathfrak{h}})
and an even super skew-symmetric bilinear map
\lambda:\mathfrak{a}\times\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{h}
, satisfying:

(11)

\displaystyle[\rho(x),\rho(y)]_{\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})}-\rho([x,y]_{\mathfrak{a}})=\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\lambda(x,y)),\,\,\,\text{ and }

(12)

\displaystyle\sum_{\text{cyclic}}(-1)^{|z||x|}\left(\rho(x)(\lambda(y,z))\!+\!\lambda(x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{a}})\right)\!=\!0,\,\text{ for all }x,y,z\in\mathfrak{a}.

Let
\chi_{\delta}:\mathfrak{a}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{h})^{\ast}
be the even bilinear map defined by:

(13)

\chi_{\delta}(x,u)(P_{\delta}(y))=-(-1)^{|u||y|}B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\lambda(x,y),u),

for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{a}
and
u
in
\mathfrak{h}
. If
\chi:\mathfrak{a}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{a}^{\ast}
is the bilinear map:
\chi(x,u)(y)=-(-1)^{|u||y|}B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\lambda(x,y),u)
, then
|\chi|=\delta
and
\chi_{\delta}=P_{\delta}(\chi)
.

Let
\omega_{\delta}:\mathfrak{a}\times\mathfrak{a}\to\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}
be an even super skew-symmetric bilinear map, satisfying:

(14)

\sum_{\text{cyclic}}(-1)^{|z||x|}\!\left(\operatorname{ad}_{\delta}^{\ast}(x)(\omega_{\delta}(y,z))\!+\!\omega_{\delta}(x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{a}})\!+\!\chi_{\delta}(x,\lambda(y,z))\right)\!=\!0,

and the super
cyclic condition
:

(15)

\omega_{\delta}(x,y)(P_{\delta}(z))=(-1)^{(|y|+|z|)|x|}\omega_{\delta}(y,z)(P_{\delta}(x)),

for all
x,y,z
in
\mathfrak{a}
. Finally, we need an even super skew-symmetric bilinear map
\Phi_{\delta}:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}
, defined by:

(16)

\Phi_{\delta}(u,v)(P_{\delta}(x))=(-1)^{|x|(|u|+|v|)}B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\rho(x)(u),v),

for all
x
in
\mathfrak{a}
and
u,v
in
\mathfrak{h}
. If
\Phi:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{a}^{\ast}
, is the map:
\Phi(u,v)(x)=(-1)^{|x|(|u|+|v|)}B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\rho(x)(u),v)
, then
|\Phi|=\delta
and
\Phi_{\delta}=P_{\delta}(\Phi)
.

The tuple
(\mathfrak{h},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}},B_{\mathfrak{h}},\rho,\lambda,\omega_{\delta})
is called a
\delta
-context of generalized double extension of
(\mathfrak{h},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}},B_{\mathfrak{h}})
by
(\mathfrak{a},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{a}})
.

2.1 Lemma
.

Let
(\mathfrak{h},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}},B_{\mathfrak{h}},\rho,\lambda,\omega_{\delta})
be a
\delta
-context of generalized double extension of
(\mathfrak{h},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}},B_{\mathfrak{h}})
by
(\mathfrak{a},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{a}})
. Then the maps
\chi_{\delta}
and
\Phi_{\delta}
, satisfy the following for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{a}
and
u,v
in
\mathfrak{h}
:

(17)

\begin{split}&\Phi_{\delta}(\rho(x)(u),v)+(-1)^{|x||u|}\Phi_{\delta}(u,\rho(x)(v))\\
&-\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}_{\delta}(x)(\Phi_{\delta}(u,v))-\chi_{\delta}(x,[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}})=0,\,\,\,\text{ and }\end{split}

(18)

\begin{split}&\chi_{\delta}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{a}},u)-\chi_{\delta}(x,\rho(y)(u))+(-1)^{|x||y|}\chi_{\delta}(y,\rho(x)(u))\\
&-\operatorname{ad}_{\delta}^{\ast}(x)(\chi_{\delta}(y,u))+(-1)^{|x||y|}\operatorname{ad}_{\delta}^{\ast}(y)(\chi_{\delta}(x,u))\\
&+\Phi_{\delta}(\lambda(x,y),u)=0.\end{split}

In addition,
\Phi_{\delta}:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}
satisfies:

(19)

\sum_{\text{cyclic}}(-1)^{|u||w|}\Phi_{\delta}(u,[v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}})=0.

Proof.

We shall only give the details of (
18
). The expression given in (
18
) consists of six elements where each of them belongs to
\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}
. We shall determine these six elements by evaluating them in an arbitrary element
P_{\delta}(z)
of
P_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})
, where
z
belongs to
\mathfrak{a}
.

The first three elements in (
18
) are of the form
\chi_{\delta}
. We use the definition of
\chi_{\delta}
in
(
13
)
to determine these first three elements in (
18
):

(20)

\begin{split}&\chi_{\delta}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{a}},u)(P_{\delta}(z))\!=\!-(-1)^{|u||z|}B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\lambda([x,y]_{\mathfrak{a}},z),u),\\
&-\chi_{\delta}(x,\rho(y)(u))(P_{\delta}(z))\!=\!(-1)^{|x|(|y|+|z|)+|u||z|}B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\rho(y)(\lambda(z,x)),u),\\
&(-1)^{|x||y|}\chi_{\delta}(y,\rho(x)(u))(P_{\delta}(z))\!=\!(-1)^{|z||u|}B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\rho(x)(\lambda(y,z)),u).\end{split}

The fourth and fifth elements in (
18
), involve the representation
\delta
-coadjoint
\operatorname{ad}_{\delta}^{\ast}:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{gl}(P_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast})
, and the bilinear map
\chi_{\delta}
. We use that
\chi_{\delta}=P_{\delta}(\chi)
,
|\chi|=\delta
, and the definition of
\operatorname{ad}_{\delta}^{\ast}
(see (
4
)), to obtain:

(21)

\begin{split}&-\!\operatorname{ad}_{\delta}^{\ast}(x)(\chi_{\delta}(y,u))(P_{\delta}(z))\!=\!(-1)^{|x|(|y|+|z|)+|u||z|}\!B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\lambda(y,[z,x]_{\mathfrak{a}}),u),\\
&(-1)^{|x||y|}\operatorname{ad}_{\delta}^{\ast}(y)(\chi_{\delta}(x,u))(P_{\delta}(z))\!=\!(-1)^{|u||z|}\!B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\lambda(x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{a}}),u).\end{split}

In the sixth element we apply (
19
) and we obtain:

(22)

\Phi_{\delta}(\lambda(x,y),\!u)(P_{\delta}(z))\!=\!(-1)^{|z|(|x|+|y|)+|u||z|}B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\rho(z)(\lambda(x,y)),\!u).

Observe that each of the expressions in (
20
), (
21
) and (
22
),
(-1)^{|u||z|}
is a common factor. We add each of the terms appearing at the left of
B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\,\cdot\,,u)
, given in (
20
), (
21
) and (
22
), without
(-1)^{|u||z|}
, and we get:

(23)

\begin{split}&\!-\!\lambda([x,y]_{\mathfrak{a}},z)\!+\!(-1)^{|x|(|y|+|z|)}\rho(y)(\lambda(z,x))\!+\!\!\rho(x)(\lambda(y,z))\\
&\!+\!(\!-1)^{|x|(|y|+|z|)}\lambda(y,[z,x]_{\mathfrak{a}})\!+\!\lambda(x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{a}})\!+\!(\!-1)^{|z|(|x|+|y|)}\rho(z)(\lambda(x,y))\end{split}

Multiplying (
23
) by
(-1)^{|x||z|}
, we obtain that (
23
) is equal to:

(24)

\sum_{\text{cyclic}}(-1)^{|x||z|}\left(\rho(x)(\lambda(y,z))+\lambda(x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{a}})\right).

which is zero, by (
12
). Similar arguments prove that (
17
) is equivalent to (
11
). Finally, the expression (
19
) is equivalent to the fact that
\rho(x)
is an homogeneous derivation of
(\mathfrak{h},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}})
, for all
x
in
\mathfrak{a}
.
∎

2.2 Theorem
.

Let
(\mathfrak{h},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}},B_{\mathfrak{h}},\rho,\lambda,\omega_{\delta})
be a
\delta
-context of generalized double extension of
(\mathfrak{h},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}},B_{\mathfrak{h}})
by
(\mathfrak{a},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{a}})
, where
|B_{\mathfrak{h}}|=\delta
. Then, in the super-space
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}
, there exists a quadratic Lie super algebra structure of degree
\delta
.

Proof.

By
Lemma
2.1
, the bilinear map
\Phi_{\delta}:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}
, is a 2-cocycle of
(\mathfrak{h},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}})
with values on the trivial
\mathfrak{h}
-module
\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}
. Let us consider the bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]^{\prime}
on the super-space
\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}
, defined by:
[u+P_{\delta}(\alpha),v+P_{\delta}(\beta)]^{\prime}=[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\Phi_{\delta}(u,v)
, where
u+P_{\delta}(\alpha)
and
v+P_{\delta}(\beta)
are elements in
\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}
.

For each
x
in
\mathfrak{a}
, consider the map
\Theta(x):\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}\to\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}
, defined by:

(25)

\Theta(x)(u+P_{\delta}(\alpha))=\rho(x)(u)+\operatorname{ad}_{\delta}^{\ast}(x)(P_{\delta}(\alpha))+\chi_{\delta}(x,u).

Using that
\rho(x)
is a derivation of
(\mathfrak{h},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}})
,
\operatorname{ad}_{\delta}^{\ast}:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast})
is a representation, and the expression (
17
), it follows that
\Theta(x)
is a derivation of
(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]^{\prime})
, of degree
|x|
. Hence, we have the even linear map
\Theta:\mathfrak{a}\to\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast})
,
x\mapsto\Theta(x)
.

Let
\Lambda:\mathfrak{a}\times\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}
, be defined by:
\Lambda(x,y)=\lambda(x,y)+\omega_{\delta}(x,y)
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{a}
.
By (
11
) and (
18
), it follows:

(26)

[\Theta(x),\Theta(y)]_{\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast})}-\Theta([x,y]_{\mathfrak{a}})=\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}}(\Lambda(x,y)).

Using (
12
) and (
14
) we get:

(27)

\sum_{\text{cyclic}}(-1)^{|x||z|}\left(\Theta(x)(\Lambda(y,z))+\Lambda(x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{a}})\right)=0,

for all
x,y,z
in
\mathfrak{a}
. By (
26
) and (
27
), we consider the generalized semi-direct product
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\left(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}\right)
, of
\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}
by
\mathfrak{a}
, by means of
(\Theta,\Lambda)
. Then the bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
of
\mathfrak{g}
takes the form:

(28)

\begin{split}x,y\in\mathfrak{a},\,\quad&\,[x,y]=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\lambda(x,y)+\omega_{\delta}(x,y),\\
x\in\mathfrak{a},\,u\in\mathfrak{h},\quad&\,[x,u]=\rho(x)(u)+\chi_{\delta}(x,u),\\
u,v\in\mathfrak{h},\quad&\,[u,v]=[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\Phi_{\delta}(u,v),\\
x\in\mathfrak{a},\,\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}^{\ast},\quad&\,[x,P_{\delta}(\alpha)]=\operatorname{ad}_{\delta}^{\ast}(x)(P_{\delta}(\alpha))\end{split}

Let
x+u+P_{\delta}(\alpha),y+v+P_{\delta}(\beta)
be homogeneous elements of
\mathfrak{g}
. We define the non-degenerate, super symmetric bilinear form
B
on
\mathfrak{g}
by:

(29)

B(x+u+P_{\delta}(\alpha),y+v+P_{\delta}(\beta))=(-1)^{|x||\beta|}\beta(x)+B_{\mathfrak{h}}(u,v)+\alpha(y).

Then
B
is invariant with
|B|=\delta
, and
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],B)
is a quadratic Lie super algebra of degree
\delta
.
∎

3.
Indecomposable Homogeneous quadratic Lie super algebras

A quadratic Lie super algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is
decomposable
if there is a non-zero ideal
I\neq\mathfrak{g}
, such that
\mathfrak{g}=I\oplus I^{\perp}
. Otherwise,
\mathfrak{g}
is
indecomposable
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],B)
be an indecomposable quadratic Lie algebra
of degree
\delta
, no simple and
\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{g}>1
, over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{F}
of zero characteristic. Let
\mathcal{I}
be a minimal ideal of

\mathfrak{g}
, then
\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{I}^{\perp}\neq\{0\}
and consequently:
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{I}^{\perp}\subset C_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{I})
; thus,

\mathcal{I}
is Abelian.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a super subspace of
\mathfrak{g}
such that:
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathcal{I}
. Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a super subspace satisfying:
\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathcal{I}
. Then
\mathfrak{h}
is a non-degenerate subspace
of
\mathfrak{g}
such that:
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
and
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}\cap\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}=\mathcal{I}
.

3.1 Claim
.

There are bijective linear maps
\xi_{\delta}:\mathcal{I}\to\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}
and
\xi:\mathcal{I}\to\mathfrak{a}^{\ast}
, such that:

\textbf{(i)}\,\,|\xi_{\delta}|=0,\quad\textbf{(ii)}\,\,|\xi|=\delta,\quad\textbf{(iii)}\,\,P_{\delta}(\xi)=\xi_{\delta}.

Proof.

Let us consider the
linear map
\xi_{\delta}:\mathcal{I}\rightarrow\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}
, defined by:

(30)

\xi_{\delta}(\alpha)\left(P_{\delta}(x)\right)=B(\alpha,x),\,\,\text{ for all }\alpha\in\mathcal{I},\,\text{ and }x\in\mathfrak{a}.

It is a straightforward to verify that
|\xi_{\delta}|=0
. Due to
B
is non-degenerate,
\xi_{\delta}
is injective, then:
\operatorname{dim}\mathcal{I}\leq\operatorname{dim}\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})=\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{a}
. We know that
\operatorname{dim}(\mathcal{I}^{\perp})\geq\operatorname{dim}(\mathfrak{g})-\operatorname{dim}(\mathcal{I})
,
then
\operatorname{dim}(\mathcal{I})\geq\operatorname{dim}(\mathfrak{a})
, and
\operatorname{dim}\mathcal{I}=\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{a}
. Therefore,
\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{I}
, via the map
\xi_{\delta}
. By
Prop.
3.10
(see Appendix), we assume that
\mathfrak{a}
is isotropic. Thus, we have the following decomposition of

\mathfrak{g}
:

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathcal{I},\,\text{ where }\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathcal{I},\,\text{ and }\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}\text{ is isomorphic to }\mathcal{I}.

If
\xi:\mathcal{I}\rightarrow\mathfrak{a}^{*}
is the linear map defined by

(31)

\xi(\alpha)(x)=B(\alpha,x),\,\,\text{ for all }\alpha\in\mathcal{I},\text{ and }x\in\mathfrak{a},

then
|\xi|=\delta
. In addition, by
Prop.
1.1
.(ii)
,
P_{\delta}(\xi)=\xi_{\delta}
.
∎

Let
B_{\mathfrak{h}}
be the restriction of
B
to
\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}
. Using that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}
, and the even linear map
\xi_{\delta}
, the invariant metric
B
takes the form:

(32)

\begin{split}&B(x+u+\alpha,y+v+\beta)=\\
&=(-1)^{|x||\beta|}\xi_{\delta}(\beta)(P_{\delta}(x))+\xi_{\delta}(\alpha)(y)+B_{\mathfrak{h}}(u,v),\end{split}

where
x+u+\alpha
and
y+v+\beta
are homogeneous elements of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Now we shall give a description of the bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
, according to the decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathcal{I}
.

Since
[\mathfrak{a},\mathcal{I}]\subset\mathcal{I}
, then for each
x
in
\mathfrak{a}
, there is a linear map
\sigma(x):\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{I}
, such that:

(33)

\sigma(x)(\alpha)=[x,\alpha],\,\,\,\text{ for all }\,\alpha\in\mathcal{I}.

Due to
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
is even, the map
\sigma(x)
is even. Thus (
33
) yields the even linear map
\sigma:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathcal{I})
,
x\mapsto\sigma(x)
.

Let
x,y
be in
\mathfrak{a}
. There are
[x,y]_{\mathfrak{a}}
in
\mathfrak{a}
,

\lambda(x,y)
in
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mu(x,y)
in
\mathcal{I}
, such that:

(34)

[x,y]=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\lambda(x,y)+\mu(x,y).

The properties of the Lie super bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
, imply that the following maps are even, super skew-symmetric and bilinear:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rccl}[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{a}}:&\mathfrak{a}\times\mathfrak{a}&\longrightarrow&\,\,\mathfrak{a}\\
&(x,y)&\mapsto&[x,y]_{\mathfrak{a}}\end{array}\qquad\begin{array}[]{rccl}\lambda:&\mathfrak{a}\times\mathfrak{a}&\longrightarrow&\,\,\mathfrak{h}\\
&(x,y)&\mapsto&\lambda(x,y),\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rccl}\mu:&\mathfrak{a}\times\mathfrak{a}&\longrightarrow&\,\,\mathcal{I}\\
&(x,y)&\mapsto&\mu(x,y)\end{array}

Let
x
be in
\mathfrak{a}
and
u
be in
\mathfrak{h}
. Since
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}]\subset\mathcal{I}^{\perp}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
, there are
\rho(x,u)
in
\mathfrak{h}
and
\tau(x,u)
in
\mathcal{I}
, such that:

(35)

[x,u]=\rho(x,u)+\tau(x,u).

As the Lie super bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}}
is even, then (
35
) yields the even linear maps:
\rho(x):\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}
,
u\mapsto\rho(x,u)
, and
\tau(x):\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{h}
,
u\mapsto\tau(x,u)
, for all
x
in
\mathfrak{a}
and
u
in
\mathfrak{h}
. Then,

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\rho:&\mathfrak{a}&\longrightarrow&\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak{h})\\
&x&\mapsto&\rho(x)\end{array}\quad\text{}\quad\begin{array}[]{rccl}\tau:&\mathfrak{a}&\longrightarrow&\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{h};\mathcal{I})\\
&x&\mapsto&\tau(x)\end{array}

are even linear maps.

Let
u,v
be in
\mathfrak{h}
. Since
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathcal{I}
, then there are
[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}
in
\mathfrak{h}
and
\gamma(u,v)
in

\mathcal{I}
, such that:

(36)

[u,v]=[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\gamma(u,v)

Let
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}}:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}
be the map
(u,v)\mapsto[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}
and let
\gamma:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathcal{I}
be the map
(u,v)\mapsto\gamma(u,v)
. Then,
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}}
and
\gamma
are even, super skew-symmetric and bilinear.

In summary, the bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
of
\mathfrak{g}
can be described by the maps
\sigma,\lambda,\mu,\rho,\tau,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}},\gamma
, as follows:

(37)

\begin{split}x,y\in\mathfrak{a},\,\quad&[x,y]=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\lambda(x,y)+\mu(x,y),\\
x\in\mathfrak{a},\,\,u\in\mathfrak{h},\,\quad&[x,u]=\rho(x)(u)+\tau(x)(u),\\
u,v\in\mathfrak{h},\,\quad&[u,v]=[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\gamma(u,v),\\
x\in\mathfrak{a},\,\,\alpha\in\mathcal{I},\,\quad&[x,\alpha]=\sigma(x)(\alpha).\end{split}

3.2 Claim
.

The pair
(\mathfrak{a},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{a}})
is a Lie super algebra satisfying:

(38)

\displaystyle\sum_{\text{cyclic}}(-1)^{|x||z|}\left(\lambda(x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\rho(x)(\lambda(y,z))\right)=0,\,\,\text{ and }

(39)

\displaystyle\sum_{\text{cyclic}}(-1)^{|x||z|}\left(\mu(x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\tau(x)\left(\lambda(y,z)\right)+\sigma(x)(\mu(y,z))\right)=0.

Proof.

Let
x,y,z
in
\mathfrak{a}
; by (
37
) we obtain:

(40)

\begin{split}[x,[y,z]]&=\underbrace{[x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{a}}]_{\mathfrak{a}}}_{\in\mathfrak{a}}+\underbrace{\lambda(x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\rho(x)\left(\lambda(y,z)\right)}_{\in\mathfrak{h}}\\
\,&+\underbrace{\mu\left(x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{a}}\right)+\tau(x)\left(\lambda(y,z)\right)+\sigma(x)\left(\mu(y,z)\right)}_{\in\mathcal{I}}.\end{split}

Uisng (
40
) and the Jacobi super identity of
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
on
x,y,z
, we get that
(\mathfrak{a},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{a}})
is a Lie super algebra satisfying (
38
) and (
39
).
∎

3.3 Claim
.

The map
\sigma:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathcal{I})
is a Lie super algebra representation of
(\mathfrak{a},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{a}})
on
\mathcal{I}
, isomorphic to the
\delta
-coadjoint representation
\operatorname{ad}_{\delta}^{\ast}:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\textbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast})
, via the map
\xi_{\delta}
, that is:

(41)

\xi_{\delta}\circ\sigma(x)=\operatorname{ad}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{\ast}(x)\circ\xi_{\delta},\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathfrak{a}.

Proof.

Let
x,y
be in
\mathfrak{a}
and
\alpha
be in
\mathcal{I}
. Using that
B
is invariant and that
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
is super skew-symmetric, we get:

(42)

\xi_{\delta}\left(\sigma(x)(\alpha)\right)(P_{\delta}(y))=-(-1)^{|x||\alpha|}B(\alpha,[x,y]_{\mathfrak{a}}).

Since
\xi_{\delta}(\alpha)=P_{\delta}\left(\xi(\alpha)\right)
and
|\xi|=\delta
(see
Claim
3.1
.(iii)
), we have:

(43)

\begin{split}&\operatorname{ad}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{\ast}(x)\left(\xi_{\delta}(\alpha)\right)\left(P_{\delta}(y)\right)=\operatorname{ad}_{\delta}^{\ast}(x)\left(P_{\delta}(\xi(\alpha))\right)\left(P_{\delta}(y)\right)\\
&=-(-1)^{(|\xi(\alpha)|+\delta)|x|}\xi(\alpha)\left([x,y]_{\mathfrak{a}}\right)=-(-1)^{(\delta+|\alpha|+\delta)|x|}\xi(\alpha)\left([x,y]_{\mathfrak{a}}\right)\\
&=-(-1)^{|x||\alpha|}B(\alpha,[x,y]_{\mathfrak{a}}).\end{split}

From (
42
) and (
43
), we deduce (
41
). Due to
\operatorname{ad}_{\delta}^{\ast}:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast})
is a representation of
(\mathfrak{a},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{a}})
(see
Prop.
1.3
) and
\xi_{\delta}
is bijective, by (
41
) we deduce that
\sigma:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathcal{I})
is a representation of
(\mathfrak{a},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{a}})
.
∎

3.4 Claim
.

Let
\mu_{\delta}=\xi_{\delta}\circ\mu:\mathfrak{a}\times\mathfrak{a}\to\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}
. Then,
\mu_{\delta}(x,y)(P_{\delta}(z))=(-1)^{|x|(|y|+|z|)}\mu_{\delta}(y,z)(P_{\delta}(x))
, for all
x,y,z
in
\mathfrak{a}
.

Proof.

Using (
32
) and (
37
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\mu_{\delta}(x,y)(P_{\delta}(z))=B(\mu(x,y),z)=B([x,y],z)=B(x,[y,z])

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|x|(|y|+|z|)}B([y,z],x)=(-1)^{|x|(|y|+|z|)}B(\mu(y,z),x)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|x|(|y|+|z|)}\mu_{\delta}(y,z)(x),

which proves our claim.
∎

3.5 Claim
.

Let
\chi_{\delta}:\mathfrak{a}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathbf{P}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{a})^{\ast}
, be defined by
\chi_{\delta}(x,u)=\xi_{\delta}(\tau(x)(u))
. Then
\chi_{\delta}(x,u)(P_{\delta}(y))=-(-1)^{|y||u|}B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\lambda(x,y),u)
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{a}
and
u
in
\mathfrak{h}
.

Proof.

Using (
32
) and (
37
), we obtain:

(44)

\begin{split}&\chi_{\delta}(x,u)(P_{\delta}(y))=B(\tau(x)(u),y)=B([x,u],y)\\
&=-(-1)^{|x||u|}B(u,[x,y])=-(-1)^{|x||u|}B_{\mathfrak{h}}(u,\lambda(x,y))\\
&=-(-1)^{|y||u|}B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\lambda(x,y),u),\end{split}

which proves our result.
∎

3.6 Claim
.

The triple
(\mathfrak{h},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}},B_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a quadratic Lie super algebra of degree
\delta
.

Proof.

Let
u,v,w
be in
\mathfrak{h}
. By (
37
) and
[\mathfrak{h},\mathcal{I}]=\{0\}
, we obtain:
[u,[v,w]]=[u,[v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}\,\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{I}
. The Jacoby super identity of
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
on
u,v,w
, implies that
(\mathfrak{h},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a Lie super algebra.

Now we shall prove that
B_{\mathfrak{h}}
is invariant under
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}}
. Since
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}
, from (
32
) and (
37
) it follows:
B_{\mathfrak{h}}(u,[v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}})=B(u,[v,w])=B([u,v],w)=B_{\mathfrak{h}}([u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}},w)
. Then
B_{\mathfrak{h}}
is invariant under
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}}
, and
(\mathfrak{h},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}},B_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a quadratic Lie super algebra of degree
\delta
.
∎

3.7 Claim
.

For each
x
in
\mathfrak{a}
, the map
\rho(x):\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}
is a

3.10 Proposition

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a
m

B

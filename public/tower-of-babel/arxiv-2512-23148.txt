On construction of differential ℤ \mathbb{Z} -graded varieties
Aliaksandr Hancharuk, Ruben Louis

Abstract
Given a commutative unital algebra 𝒪 \mathcal{O} , a proper ideal ℐ \mathcal{I} in 𝒪 \mathcal{O} , and a positively graded differential variety over 𝒪 / ℐ \mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I} , we provide a ℤ \mathbb{Z} -graded extension, whose negative part is an arborescent Koszul-Tate resolution of 𝒪 / ℐ \mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I} . This extension is obtained through an algorithm exploiting the explicit homotopy retract data of the arborescent Koszul-Tate resolution, so that the number of homological computations in the construction is significantly reduced. For a positively graded differential variety over 𝒪 \mathcal{O} that preserves the ideal ℐ \mathcal{I} , the extension admits a manifest description in terms of decorated trees and computed data. As a by-product, to every Lie–Rinehart algebra over the coordinate ring of an affine variety W ⊆ M = ℂ d W\subseteq M=\mathbb{C}^{d} , one associates an explicit differential ℤ \mathbb{Z} -graded variety over M M whose negative component is the arborescent Koszul–Tate resolution of the coordinate ring ℂ ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x d ] / ℐ W \mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]/\mathcal{I}_{W} of W W , and whose positive component is the universal dg-variety of the given Lie–Rinehart algebra. Concrete examples are given.



Introduction

The study of singular spaces in differential and algebraic geometry has been approached using various distinct methods. One notable approach involves examining singularities via Lie algebras of vector fields. The investigation of the singularities of singular spaces through their associated Lie algebras of vector fields is a topic with a deep and rich history. This line of inquiry began with the work of Pursell and Shanks
[
SP54
]
, who formulated the problem of characterizing a manifold by its Lie algebra of vector fields. Subsequently, similar problems were studied by many authors in different contexts, including H. Omori
[
Omo80
]
and J. Grabowski
[
Gra79
]
. T. Siebert
[
Sie96
]

[
Gra79
]

[

,
GKP11
,
GKP13
]

\mathrm{Der}(\mathcal{O}_{W})

[
Jor86
]

The modern approach to geometric problems, as well as to mathematical physics ones, is unified by the language of
Q

It is natural to ask

Question 0.1
.

Is there a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
Q
-variety
(\mathcal{A},Q)
encoding both the Koszul–Tate resolution of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
(a singular space), and a positively graded
Q
-variety
(\mathcal{A}^{+},Q^{+})
associated with vector fields on the singular space?

A positive answer to this question was given in
[
KLGS23
]
in the smooth geometric setting, i.e., for an ideal
\mathcal{I}
of a ring of smooth functions
\mathcal{O}=C^{\infty}(M)
. However, the result
[
KLGS23
]
is only an existence result. It is typically expected that a generic Koszul-Tate resolution
(S_{\mathcal{O}}(\oplus_{i\leq-1}\mathcal{V}_{i}),\delta)
has infinitely many non-zero
\mathcal{V}_{i}
. Thus, finding examples is problematic. Therefore, it is of interest to study the following question:

Question 0.2
.

To what extent the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
Q
-variety
(\mathcal{A},Q)
of Question
0.1
is computable? Is there an algorithm that terminates in a finite number of steps?

Questions similar to Question
0.2
were extensively studied in the realm of projective resolutions of modules. More precisely, in the works of
[
Pee10
,
BPS98
]
and references therein, an efficient machinery was developed to study monomial resolutions of polynomial rings. A manifest description reveals underlying structures, such as a semi-simplicial complex that governs such resolutions. Moreover, the construction of such resolutions and related computations can now be performed by software
[
EGSS02
]
.
In what concerns Koszul-Tate resolutions, a recent work
[
Han23
,
HLGS24
]
suggested a construction built on top of projective resolutions of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
. This construction, called an
arborescent
Koszul-Tate resolution, significantly reduces the number of computations, which becomes finite if the projective resolution is of finite length and has finite ranks at each degree. A manifest description provides insight into the generic structure of any Koszul-Tate resolution
[
HLGS24
, Proposition 5.5]
. Namely, any Koszul-Tate resolution
(S_{\mathcal{O}}(\oplus_{i\leq-1}\mathcal{V}_{i}),\delta)
of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
, where
\mathcal{O}
is a polynomial ring and
\mathcal{I}
is a monomial ideal, has
\mathcal{V}_{i}\neq 0
for all
i
provided that
\mathcal{I}
is not generated by a regular sequence.

We address Question
0.2
in the following setting: as a negative dg-variety we use an arborescent Koszul-Tate resolution of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
, while the positive graded
Q
-variety is either a generic one over
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
, or it is over
\mathcal{O}
and preserves
\mathcal{I}
. In §
1
we recall the notions of
Q
-varieties, and recall (arborescent) Koszul-Tate resolutions. In §
2
we state the main results of the paper. In §
3
, we give manifest examples of our construction. In §
A
we substitute the arborescent Koszul-Tate resolution by the Koszul complex and draw a comparison from our construction to it. In §
B
we give examples of new structures (higher-order multiplications) that our construction induces. In §
C
we address the issue of lifting derivations from quotient algebras.

1.2

Q
-varieties

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a commutative unital algebra over

or
\mathbb{C}
. Which geometrically may correspond to an algebra of admissible functions of a manifold or an affine variety.

Definition 1.13
.

A
Lie-Rinehart algebra
over
\mathcal{O}
is a triple
(\mathfrak{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{A}},\rho_{\mathfrak{A}})
with
\mathfrak{A}
an
\mathcal{O}
-module,

a Lie

-algebra bracket on

\rho_{\mathfrak{A}}\colon\mathfrak{A}\longrightarrow{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathcal{O})

-linear Lie algebra morphism called

, satisfying the the so-called

[a,fb]_{\mathfrak{A}}=\rho_{\mathfrak{A}}(a)[f]\,b+f[a,b]_{\mathfrak{A}}\hbox{ for all $a,b\in\mathfrak{A},f\in\mathcal{O}$}.

Restriction.

Consider a Lie-Rinehart algebra
(\mathfrak{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{A}},\rho_{\mathfrak{A}})
over
\mathcal{O}
.
For every
Lie-Rinehart ideal

\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{O}
, i.e. any ideal
such that

\rho_{\mathfrak{A}}(\mathfrak{A})[\mathcal{I}]\subset\mathcal{I}

the quotient space
\mathfrak{A}/\mathcal{I}\mathfrak{A}
inherits a natural Lie-Rinehart algebra structure over
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
. We call this Lie-Rinehart algebra the
restriction w.r.t the Lie-Rinehart ideal
\mathcal{I}
. In the context of affine varieties or an arbitrary subset
\Sigma\subseteq M
of a manifold, when
\mathcal{I}
is the ideal of functions vanishing on
\Sigma

\frac{\mathfrak{A}}{\mathcal{I}\mathfrak{A}}
by

.

Example 1.14
.

\mathfrak{A}=\mathrm{Der}(\mathcal{O})
is a Lie-Rinehart algebra whose anchor map is the identity. Lie subalgebras

that are finitely generated as
\mathcal{O}
-modules are Lie-Rinehart algebras whose anchor map is the inclusion map. Geometrically, when
\mathcal{O}
is the algebra of functions on a manifold, these Lie-Rinehart algebras are called

on

[

or
[

]

(

1.3
Arborescent Koszul-Tate resolutions

An important class of examples of negatively graded
Q
-varieties/manifolds is the so-called
Koszul-Tate resolution
of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
for some proper ideal
\mathcal{I}
of an algebra
\mathcal{O}
. Koszul-Tate resolutions are also known in a more algebraic setting, see
[
Tat57
]
for a historical introduction.

Definition 1.19
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal of an algebra
\mathcal{O}
. The
Koszul-Tate resolution
of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
is a negatively graded
Q
-variety
(\mathcal{A}^{-},\delta)
over
\mathcal{O}
such that

•

\mathcal{A}^{-}\simeq S(\oplus_{i\leq-1}\mathcal{V}_{i})
, for some collection of projective
\mathcal{O}
-modules
\mathcal{V}_{i}
.

•

the homology,
H^{-i}(\mathcal{A}^{-},\delta)=0
for
i\geq 1
and
H^{0}(\mathcal{A}^{-},\delta)=\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
.

A standard way to construct a Koszul-Tate resolution
(S(\mathcal{V}),\delta)
of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
is to employ the Tate algorithm
[
Tat57
]
. The main idea is to consistently extend a differential graded commutative algebra (dgca)
S(\mathcal{V}_{-1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{V}_{-k})
equipped with a differential of degree
+1

\delta^{-k}\colon S(\mathcal{V}_{-1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{V}_{-k})\to S(\mathcal{V}_{-1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{V}_{-k})

to a dgca
\left(S(\mathcal{V}_{-1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{V}_{-k}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{-k-1}),\delta^{-k-1}\right)
such that

\bullet

\delta^{-k-1}
coincides with
\delta^{-k}
on
\mathcal{V}_{-1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{V}_{-k}
.

\bullet

H^{-k}(S(\mathcal{V}_{-1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{V}_{-k}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{-k-1}),\;\delta^{-k-1})=0
. In other words, all non-trivial cycles of degree
-k
of
(S(\mathcal{V}_{-1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{V}_{-k},\;\delta^{-k})
are in the image
\delta^{-k-1}(\mathcal{V}_{-k-1})
.

Despite its apparent simplicity, this algorithm, in general, does not terminate even for Noetherian
\mathcal{O}
. For such
\mathcal{O}
all
\mathcal{V}_{-j}
can be chosen to be finitely generated, but one still deals with an infinite collection of them, at least for
\mathcal{O}
being a polynomial ring and a monomial ideal
\mathcal{I}\subsetneq\mathcal{O}

[
HLGS24
]
. An alternative approach is given by arborescent Koszul-Tate resolutions
[
HLGS24
]
which are obtained from a projective resolution
(\mathfrak{M},d)
of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
, which is simpler in a number of examples. By projective resolution
(\mathfrak{M},d)
we mean a sequence of projective
\mathcal{O}
-modules
(\mathfrak{M}_{i})_{i<0}
such that

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{M}_{-i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{M}_{-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d}

is acyclic in negative degrees and

.
We shall fix a projective resolution

•

Main results

In this section, we state the main results of the paper. Recall that
\mathcal{O}
an associative, commutative, unital algebra and
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{O}
is a proper ideal of
\mathcal{O}
. The relevant geometric examples of
\mathcal{O}
are an algebra of functions on a smooth manifold, a Stein manifold, or a polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
. In the latter case, the quotient algebra
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
corresponds to a coordinate ring of an algebraic variety.

This section investigates the extension problem for positively graded
Q
-varieties by the arborescent Koszul-Tate resolution of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
in two distinct settings:

1.

the case of a positively graded
Q
-variety over
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
, which is the subject of Theorem
2.4
;

2.

the case of a positively graded
Q
-variety over
\mathcal{O}
such that
Q
preserves
\mathcal{I}
, which is delegated to Theorem
2.6
.

Although the second case is a particular instance of the first case (it yields a positively graded
Q
-variety over
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
), there are two distinct features appearing:

1.

The assumption imposed on
\mathcal{O}
in Theorem
2.4
can be avoided. Namely, the (smooth) Kähler module
\Omega_{\mathcal{O}/\mathbb{K}}
need not be projective.

2.

The extension of Theorem
2.6
is inherently equipped with a homotopy retract data coming from the arborescent Koszul-Tate resolution of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
. This allows a more explicit description, as stated in Proposition
2.7
.

The extension problem is known in many applications, for instance in BV and BFV formalisms
[
HT92
,
Sta97
,
BFLS98
,
FKS14
]
. The main tool is a homological perturbation lemma, which provides a step-by-step algorithm and is based on the acyclicity of the
Q
-variety in negative degrees; see, e.g., Theorem 3.21 in
[
KLGS23
]
in the context of our scope. In practice, finding such extensions explicitly can be a highly non-trivial task. The extension requires calculating a preimage of the Koszul-Tate resolution at every negative degree, and such calculations can be infinitely many. In this section, we present an enhanced algorithm that significantly simplifies the complexity in a class of examples. The cornerstone of this new technique is the use of arborescent Koszul-Tate resolutions as the negative part of
Q
.
Let us address the issue of complexity and calculations. In our work, we are dealing with two types of computational problems:

1.

Finding a kernel of an
\mathcal{O}

\phi:A\rightarrow B
.

2.

Constructing an
\mathcal{O}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}

Osb69
]
, while the smooth Kähler module reproduces de Rham 1-forms
[
Joy19
,
Ler24
]
, so that it is projective and Lemma
C.1
holds.

Proposition C.3
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a (smooth)
\mathbb{K}
-algebra with a (smooth) Kähler module
\Omega_{\mathcal{O}/\mathbb{K}}
being projective, and let
\mathcal{S}=S(\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{\times}}\mathcal{W}_{i})
for some projective
\mathcal{O}
-modules
\mathcal{W}_{i}
. If
Q
is a derivation of
\mathcal{S}\otimes\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
, then
Q
lifts to a derivation
\hat{Q}
of
\mathcal{S}
. Moreover, if
Q
is a differential on
\mathcal{S}\otimes\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
, then
\hat{Q}^{2}(\mathcal{S})\subseteq\mathcal{I}\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
C.1
one can lift the derivation
Q|_{\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}}\colon\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{S}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
to a derivation
\hat{Q}|_{\mathcal{O}}\colon\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{S}_{1}
. Furthermore,
\hat{Q}|_{\mathcal{O}}
can be lifted to a derivation
\nabla
on
\mathcal{S}
. This is done as follows:

\bullet

By definition, each
\mathcal{W}_{i}
is a direct summand of a free
\mathcal{O}
-module
F_{i}
. Let
\{f_{\alpha},\alpha\in J\}
be a basis of
F_{i}
parametrized by some index set
J
. We can define a derivation
\nabla^{F_{i}}
by choosing images
\nabla^{F_{i}}(e_{\alpha})\in\mathcal{S}_{i+1}
. The extension to
F_{i}
is given by

\nabla^{F_{i}}(\sum_{\alpha\in J}\lambda_{\alpha}f_{\alpha})=\sum_{\alpha\in J}\hat{Q}|_{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda_{\alpha})f_{\alpha}+\sum_{\alpha\in J}\lambda_{\alpha}\nabla^{F_{i}}(f_{\alpha})

for some coefficients
\lambda_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{O}
.

\bullet

By means of inclusion
\mathcal{W}_{i}\hookrightarrow F_{i}
and projection
F_{i}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{W}_{i}
we define a derivation
\nabla
on each
\mathcal{W}_{i}
, and extend it to
\mathcal{S}
by the graded Leibniz rule.

This derivation
\nabla
descends to
\nabla^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{S}\otimes\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}\otimes\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
. Then the difference
Q-\nabla^{\prime}
is manifestly
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
-linear, and it is also
\mathcal{O}
-linear, taking into account the induced
\mathcal{O}
-module structure. Since each
\mathcal{W}_{i}
is projective, we can lift
Q-\nabla^{\prime}
to an
\mathcal{O}
-module map as below:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{W}_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{S}_{i+1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{W}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{S}_{i+1}\otimes\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Pr}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\exists A}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Pr}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q-\nabla^{\prime}}

The morphism
A
is extended to
\mathcal{S}
as a derivation, so that the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{S}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{S}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{S}\otimes\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{S}\otimes\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Pr}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\exists A}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Pr}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q-\nabla^{\prime}}

commutes. The derivation
\hat{Q}
is defined as
\hat{Q}=\nabla+A
. By construction, it is a lift of
Q
. Moreover, if
Q
is a differential, the image of
\hat{Q}^{2}
lies in the kernel of
\mathrm{Pr}
:

\mathrm{Pr}\circ(\nabla+A)\circ(\nabla+A)=(\nabla^{\prime}+Q-\nabla^{\prime})\circ\mathrm{Pr}\circ(\hat{\nabla}+A)=Q^{2}\circ\mathrm{Pr}=0.

Therefore,
\hat{Q}^{2}(\mathcal{S})\subseteq\mathcal{I}\mathcal{S}
.
∎

Conclusion

The study of
Q
-varieties (or
Q
-manifolds) and their dual counterparts, Lie
\infty
-algebroids

[
Cam19
,
Vor05
,
Vor10
]
, as well as of “higher groupoids”
[
SS20
]
, is commonly motivated by their
applications across various areas of mathematics and theoretical physics. These structures often arise
in contexts where, at first glance, no higher-structure framework appears to be involved, yet they
ultimately prove indispensable for addressing natural and fundamental questions. Prominent examples
include deformation quantization of Poisson manifolds
[
Kon03
]
, recent developments in BV operator
theory (see, for instance,
[
Cam17
]
), deformations of coisotropic submanifolds
[
CF04
]
,
integration problems for Lie algebroids via stacky groupoids
[
TZ06
]
, and the study of complex
submanifolds and Atiyah classes
[
Kap99
,
CSX16
,
LGSX21
]
. Many further examples could be added to this list.

In this work, we provide explicit constructions (Theorems
2.4
and
2.6
) and concrete examples of

\mathbb{Z}
-graded
Q
-varieties arising from arborescent Koszul–Tate resolutions of

\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
, for a given ideal
\mathcal{I}
, together with the universal Lie

\infty
-algebroid associated with a Lie–Rinehart algebra preserving
\mathcal{I}
in the sense of

[
LLS20
,
LGL22
]
. These results extend
[
KLGS23
, Theorem 3.21]
to a purely algebraic setting and provide a
more explicit description, as well as an effective algorithm that significantly reduces the homological
computations required in the construction.

Theorems 2.4 and 2.6 admit direct applications to singular foliation theory. In particular, every
solvable singular foliation, in the sense of
[
LLS20
,
LGLR25a
]
, that preserves an ideal
\mathcal{I}
arises
as the image of the anchor map of a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
Q
-manifold, thereby extending the main
results of
[
LLS20
,
LGL22
]
. As a notable consequence, to every affine variety

W\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
one can associate a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
Q
-variety canonically attached
to the vanishing ideal
\mathcal{I}_{W}
of
W
and to its Lie–Rinehart algebra of vector fields

\mathfrak{X}(W)
.

Finally, the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
Q
-varieties constructed here are naturally equipped with
collections of (higher) multiplications, described in Appendix
B
. A geometric interpretation of these
structures would be of considerable interest and will be the subject of a forthcoming paper.

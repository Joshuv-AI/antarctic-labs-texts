Categorified Koszul duality of algebras
Isamu Iwanari

Abstract




1.
Introduction

Koszul duality is a duality between algebras that provides deep connections between seemingly different algebraic objects and has nontrivial applications in various areas of mathematics.
Let
k
be a base field, and let
A
be an associative
k
-algebra equipped with an augmentation
A\to k
.
The Koszul dual
\mathbb{D}_{1}(A)
is defined as the (derived) endomorphism algebra
\operatorname{End}_{A}(k)
of the
A
-module
k
, which naturally inherits an augmentation

\mathbb{D}_{1}(A)=\mathrm{End}_{A}(k)\to\mathrm{End}_{k}(k)=k.

In favorable cases, the biduality map
A\to\mathbb{D}_{1}(\mathbb{D}_{1}(A))
is an equivalence, and
A
and
\mathbb{D}_{1}(A)
exhibit rich interrelations.
For instance, there exists a Fourier–Morita-type equivalence between
A
-modules and
\mathbb{D}_{1}(A)
-modules.

Koszul duality in the context of presentable stable
\infty
-categories.

In this paper, we study a
categorified
generalization of Koszul duality.
We consider a monoidal
\infty
-category
\mathcal{A}
endowed with a monoidal functor

\mathcal{A}\to\operatorname{Mod}_{k},

where
\operatorname{Mod}_{k}
denotes the stable
\infty
-category of
k
-module spectra (i.e., the derived
\infty
-category of
k
-vector spaces).
We view this functor as a categorified analog of the augmentation
A\to k
.
In this setting, the Koszul dual
\mathsf{D}(\mathcal{A})
is defined to be the endomorphism monoidal
\infty
-category

\mathsf{D}(\mathcal{A})=\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{A}}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})

which classifies
\mathcal{A}
-module functors
\operatorname{Mod}_{k}\to\operatorname{Mod}_{k}
. The monoidal structure is induced by composition.
We focus on the case where
\mathcal{A}
is the stable
\infty
-category of modules over an (Artin or complete) algebra—arguably the first nontrivial instance.
Let
A\to k
be an augmented
\mathbf{E}_{2}
-algebra over
k
, and let

\operatorname{LMod}_{A}\to\operatorname{Mod}_{k}

be the induced monoidal functor given by base change.
We then define

\mathsf{D}(\operatorname{LMod}_{A})=\operatorname{End}_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k}).

Applying
\mathsf{D}
twice yields a biduality functor

\delta_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}:\operatorname{LMod}_{A}\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{End}_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})=\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{D}(\operatorname{LMod}_{A})).

Our first main result is as follows (see Theorem
4.9
for details).

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
A
is Artin. Then the biduality map

\delta_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}:\operatorname{LMod}_{A}\to\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{D}(\operatorname{LMod}_{A}))

is an equivalence after passing to the right completion of
\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{D}(\operatorname{LMod}_{A}))
.
In particular, if we write
\mathsf{D}_{\textup{t}+}
for
\mathsf{D}
followed by the right completion (cf. Notation
4.7
),
it induces a canonical equivalence

\operatorname{LMod}_{A}\simeq\mathsf{D}_{\textup{t}+}(\mathsf{D}_{\textup{t}+}(\operatorname{LMod}_{A})).

This can be regarded as a categorical generalization of
the above
A\simeq\mathbb{D}_{1}(\mathbb{D}_{1}(A))
.

The Koszul dual
\mathsf{D}(\operatorname{LMod}_{A})
is closely related to the
\mathbf{E}_{2}
-Koszul dual
\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)
of
A
.
Indeed,
\mathsf{D}(\operatorname{LMod}_{A})
can be interpreted as the left completion of the monoidal
\infty
-category
\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)}
of left modules.
Our second main result is as follows (see Theorem
3.6
for details).

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
A
is Artin. There is a canonical equivalence of monoidal
\infty
-categories

\mathsf{D}(\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)})\simeq\mathrm{Ind}(\mathrm{LCoh}(A)).

The
\infty
-category
\operatorname{Ind}(\textup{LCoh}(A))
consists of Ind-coherent sheaves (or modules) on
A
(see, e.g.,
[
4
]
).
When
A
is commutative, the symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
\operatorname{Ind}(\textup{Coh}(A))
is described in
[
4
]
.
Thus, this result identifies the monoidal
\infty
-category of Ind-coherent sheaves as the
categorified Koszul dual
of the
\infty
-category of ordinary left
\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)
-modules.
This equivalence provides an interpretation of the monoidal structure
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathrm{LCoh}(A))

as composition of functors
\operatorname{Mod}_{k}\to\operatorname{Mod}_{k}
(note that
\mathsf{D}(\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)})=\operatorname{End}_{\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)}}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})
).
This result also reveals that the categorified Koszul duality has a more subtle and interesting feature than the duality between
A
and
\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)
since
\mathsf{D}(\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)})
is not
\operatorname{LMod}_{A}
but
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathrm{LCoh}(A))
.

Next we start with
\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

There exists an equivalence

\widehat{L}(\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)})\simeq\mathsf{D}_{\textup{t}+}(\mathsf{D}_{\textup{t}+}(\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)}))

of monoidal
\infty
-categories,
where
\widehat{L}(\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)})
is the left completion of
\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)}
with respect to the standard
t
-structure.

Remark 1.4
.

Indeed, both sides are equivalent to
\mathsf{D}(\operatorname{LMod}_{A})
.
The standard
t
-structure on
\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)}
is determined by the subcategory
\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)}^{\leq 0}
of connective objects (see Remark
4.4
).
This
t
-structure is generally not left complete.

Remark 1.5
.

The monoidal
\infty
-category
\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)}
can also be recovered as the algebraization of
\mathsf{D}_{\textup{t}+}(\mathsf{D}_{\textup{t}+}(\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)}))
(see Lemma
3.3
and Definition
3.1
). Put another way, the underlying category
\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)}
may be viewed as the regularization of
\mathsf{D}_{\textup{t}+}(\mathsf{D}_{\textup{t}+}(\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)}))

(see
[
14
]
for the regularization).

Our results can be understood within the general paradigm of categorifying modules and sheaves in algebraic geometry.
Here, categorification refers to the process of replacing modules and sheaves by categories and sheaves of categories (cf.
[
5
]
,
[
15
]
).
From this perspective, the categorified Koszul duality may be regarded as a categorification of the classical Koszul duality for modules.
This line of thought is partly motivated by the close relationship with categorical invariants of Landau–Ginzburg models, including matrix factorizations
[
8
]
, as well as by deformation theory of stable
\infty
-categories
[
9
]
.
For instance, Theorem
1.2
yields a functor

\operatorname{RMod}_{\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)}}(\operatorname{Pr}_{\textup{t}+}^{\textup{L}})\longrightarrow\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathsf{D}(\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathbb{D}_{2}(R)})}(\operatorname{Pr}_{\textup{t}}^{\textup{L}})\simeq\operatorname{LMod}_{\operatorname{Ind}(\textup{LCoh}(A))}(\operatorname{Pr}_{\textup{t}}^{\textup{L}})

which carries
\mathcal{E}
to
\operatorname{Fun}_{\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)}}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k},\mathcal{E})
(see the text Section
4.1
for the notation).
Using Corollary
4.21
we have
\widehat{R}_{A}:\operatorname{LMod}_{\operatorname{Ind}(\textup{LCoh}(A))}(\operatorname{Pr}_{\textup{t}}^{\textup{L}})\to\operatorname{LMod}_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}(\operatorname{Pr}_{\textup{t}+}^{\textup{L}})
induced
by the right completion functor
\widehat{R}:\operatorname{Pr}_{\textup{t}}^{\textup{L}}\to\operatorname{Pr}_{\textup{t}+}^{\textup{L}}
.
Composing these, we obtain the functor

\kappa:\operatorname{RMod}_{\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)}}(\operatorname{Pr}_{\textup{t}+}^{\textup{L}})\longrightarrow\operatorname{LMod}_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}(\operatorname{Pr}_{\textup{t}+}^{\textup{L}})

given informally by
\widehat{R}(\operatorname{Fun}_{\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)}}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k},-))
.
This is a
right adjoint
to

\flat:\operatorname{LMod}_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}(\operatorname{Pr}_{\textup{t}+}^{\textup{L}})\longrightarrow\operatorname{RMod}_{\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathbb{D}_{2}(A)}}(\operatorname{Pr}_{\textup{t}+}^{\textup{L}})

which is given by the base change
\mathcal{D}\mapsto\operatorname{Mod}_{k}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}\mathcal{D}
.
Since
\flat
realizes the base change of
R
-linear stable presentable
\infty
-categories with right complete
t
-structures from
R
to
k
, the right adjoint
\kappa
plays a crucial role from the viewpoint of the deformation theory of stable
\infty
-categories.
Furthermore, Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
4.21
imply that
\kappa
is fully faithful as a
\operatorname{Pr}_{\textup{t}+}^{\textup{L}}
-enriched functor when restricted to the full subcategory
of objects generated by
\operatorname{Mod}_{k}
under small limits (e.g., stable
\infty
-categories of representations of groups).
We expect that the results of this paper will be important in the study of this direction and related problems.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we fix some notation and terminology
and review some results which we will use in this paper.
In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.2
.
In Section
4
we prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
.
This section also includes the review of
t
-structures.
In Section
5
we discuss a generalization to formal stacks.
In Appendix
5.3
, we provide a covariantly functorial assignment
of categories of Ind-coherent sheaves on Artin
\mathbf{E}_{2}
-algebras.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
k
denote a fixed field, which serves as the base field.

2.1.

Notation and Terminology.

We use
the language of
(\infty,1)
-categories.
We use the theory of
quasi-categories
as a model of
(\infty,1)
-categories.
Our main references are
[
12
]

and
[
11
]
.
Following
[
12
]
, we shall refer to quasi-categories
as
\infty
-categories
.
To an ordinary category, we can assign an
\infty
-category by taking
its nerve, and therefore
when we treat ordinary categories we often omit the nerve
\operatorname{N}(-)

and directly regard them as
\infty
-categories.
Here is a list of some of the notation and conventions we frequently use:

•

\simeq
: categorical equivalence or homotopy equivalence. The symbol
\simeq
is used to indicate isomorphisms and equivalences in categories and

\infty
-categories.

•

\mathcal{C}^{op}
: the opposite
\infty
-category of an
\infty
-category.

•

\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(C,C^{\prime})
: the mapping space from an object
C\in\mathcal{C}
to
C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
where
\mathcal{C}
is an
\infty
-category.

•

\operatorname{Fun}(A,B)
: the function complex for simplicial sets
A
and
B
. If
A
and
B
are
\infty
-categories, we regard
\operatorname{Fun}(A,B)
as the functor category.

•

\textup{Cat}_{\infty}
: the
\infty
-category of small
\infty
-categories. We write
\widehat{\textup{Cat}}_{\infty}
for the
\infty
-category of
\infty
-categories in an enlarged universe.

•

\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal{C})
: the Ind-category of
\mathcal{C}
.

We will use the theory of higher algebras.
We refer the reader to
[
11
]
.
Here is a list of (some) of the notation about
\infty
-operads and algebras over them that we will use:

•

\operatorname{Alg}_{n}(\mathcal{M})
:
For a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}
,

\operatorname{Alg}_{n}(\mathcal{M})
for
the
\infty
-category of algebra objects over the

-operad of little

(cf.
[

\mathcal{M}
.
If we denote by
\operatorname{As}^{\otimes}

[

\operatorname{As}^{\otimes}\simeq\mathbf{E}_{1}^{\otimes}

of
\infty

\operatorname{Alg}_{1}(\mathcal{M})

\infty

\operatorname{Alg}_{\operatorname{As}}(\mathcal{M})

\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}

\infty

\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{\mathbb{S}}

\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{\mathbb{S}}

\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{\mathbb{S}}

\operatorname{Sp}
of spectra (see
[

[

\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{k}
for
\operatorname{Mod}_{\operatorname{Mod}_{k}}(\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{\mathbb{S}})

\otimes_{k}
for the tensor products.

We define the functor categories.
Let
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
be a
k
-linear monoidal stable
\infty
-category i.e.,
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}\in\operatorname{Alg}_{1}(\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{k})
.
We write
\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{\mathcal{A}}:=\operatorname{LMod}_{\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}}(\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{k})
.
We note that
\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{\mathcal{A}}
is right-tensored over
\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{k}
(we denote the tensor by
\otimes_{k}
).
Let
\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C}\in\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{\mathcal{A}}
.
We denote by
\operatorname{Fun}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C})\in\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{k}
the Hom object which has the universal property:
There exists a morphism
\mathcal{B}\otimes_{k}\operatorname{Fun}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{C}
in
\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{\mathcal{A}}
such that it determines
an equivalence of spaces

\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{k}}(\mathcal{E},\operatorname{Fun}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C}))\simeq\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathcal{B}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})

for any
\mathcal{E}\in\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{k}
.
The existence follows from a standard argument, see e.g.,
[
7

.
When
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{C}
, we write
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})=\operatorname{Fun}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C})
.
In this case it is promoted to an object of
\operatorname{Alg}_{1}(\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{k})
(see
[
11

We refer to
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})
as the endomorphism algebra.

, we often write
\operatorname{Fun}_{R}(-,-)
for
\operatorname{Fun}_{\operatorname{Mod}_{R}}(-,-)
.

2.2.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}
be a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category which has small colimits and limits such that the tensor product preserves sifted colimits. We write
\mathbf{1}
for a unit object.
We review the Koszul duality of algebras in
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}
in the formalism of pairing (see
[
11
]
,
[
13
, X]
for more details).
Let
\operatorname{Alg}^{+}_{1}(\mathcal{M})=\operatorname{Alg}_{1}(\mathcal{M})_{/\mathbf{1}}
be the
\infty
-category of augmented
\mathbf{E}_{1}
-algebra objects (associative algebra objects) in
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}
.
For a pair
(\epsilon_{A}:A\to\mathbf{1},\epsilon_{B}:B\to\mathbf{1})\in\operatorname{Alg}^{+}_{1}(\mathcal{M})\times\operatorname{Alg}^{+}_{1}(\mathcal{M})
we put

\textup{Pair}(\epsilon_{A},\epsilon_{B}):=\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{Alg}_{1}(\mathcal{M})}(A\otimes B,\mathbf{1})\times_{\operatorname{Map}(A,\mathbf{1})\times\operatorname{Map}(B,\mathbf{1})}\{(\epsilon_{A},\epsilon_{B})\}.

Fix
\epsilon_{B}:B\to\mathbf{1}
and consider the functor

u_{\epsilon_{B}}:\operatorname{Alg}_{1}^{+}(\mathcal{M})^{op}\to\mathcal{S}
given informally by
[\epsilon_{A}:A\to\mathbf{1}]\mapsto\textup{Pair}(\epsilon_{A},\epsilon_{B})
.
The Koszul dual
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}}(B)\in\operatorname{Alg}_{1}^{+}(\mathcal{M})
of
B
(we abuse notation since
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}}(B)
depends on the augmentation
\epsilon_{B}:B\to\mathbf{1}
)
is defined by the universal property that
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}}(B)
represents the functor
u_{\epsilon_{B}}
.
Under a mild condition on
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}
,
there exist
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}}(B)
and functorial equivalences

\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{Alg}_{1}^{+}(\mathcal{M})}(A,\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}}(B))\simeq\textup{Pair}(\epsilon_{A},\epsilon_{B})

for
\epsilon_{A}:A\to\mathbf{1}
. We shall refer to the pairing
B\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}}(B)\to\mathbf{1}

corresponding to the identity of
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}}(B)
as the universal pairing for
B\to k
.
In that case, there is a functor

\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}}:\operatorname{Alg}_{1}(\mathcal{M})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Alg}_{1}(\mathcal{M})^{op}

which carries
A\to\mathbf{1}
to
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}}(A)\to\mathbf{1}
.

The Koszul duals can be described in terms of bar constructions and endomorphism algebras.
Let
\textup{Bar}:\operatorname{Alg}_{1}^{+}(\mathcal{M})\to\mathcal{M}
be the functor
given by
[B\to\mathbf{1}]\mapsto\mathbf{1}\otimes_{B}\mathbf{1}
, which we refer to as the bar construction of augmented algebras.
Then
\mathbf{1}\otimes_{B}\mathbf{1}
admits a structure of a coaugmented coalgebra in a suitable way, and
Bar

is promoted to
\textup{Bar}:\operatorname{Alg}_{1}^{+}(\mathcal{M})\to\operatorname{Alg}^{+}_{1}((\mathcal{M})^{op})^{op}

(notice that the target is the
\infty
-category of coaugmented coalgebras).
According to
[
13
, X, Example 4.4.20]
, for
B\in\operatorname{Alg}^{+}_{1}(\mathcal{M})
, the Koszul dual
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}}(B)

is equivalent to
the dual
\textup{Bar}(B)^{\vee}
in
\mathcal{M}
, that is, the endomorphism algebra
\operatorname{End}_{\operatorname{LMod}_{B}(\mathcal{M})}(\mathbf{1})
.

We recall the Koszul duality between
\mathbf{E}_{n}
-algebras. In this note, we assume that
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}=\operatorname{Mod}_{k}
: in this case
Koszul duals exist (cf.
[
13
, X, 3.1.5, 4.3.8, 4.4.8]
).
Let
n\geq 1
be a natural number and let
\operatorname{Alg}_{n}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})
be the
\infty
-category of
\mathbf{E}_{n}
-algebras in
\operatorname{Mod}_{k}
.
We write
\operatorname{Alg}^{+}_{n}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})=\operatorname{Alg}_{n}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})_{/k}
for the
\infty
-category

\operatorname{Alg}_{n}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})_{/k}
of augmented
\mathbf{E}_{n}
-algebras.
We consider the Koszul duals of augmented
\mathbf{E}_{n}
-algebras.
We take
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}
to be
\operatorname{Alg}_{n-1}^{\otimes}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})
. There exists the Koszul duality functor

\mathbb{D}_{n}:\operatorname{Alg}_{n}^{+}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})\simeq\operatorname{Alg}_{1}^{+}(\operatorname{Alg}_{n-1}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k}))\longrightarrow\operatorname{Alg}_{1}^{+}(\operatorname{Alg}_{n-1}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k}))^{op}\simeq\operatorname{Alg}_{n}^{+}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})^{op}

(see e.g.
[
13
, X, 4.4.8]
).
Let
\epsilon_{B}:B\to k
be an augmented
\mathbf{E}_{n}
-algebra.
As mentioned above,

\mathbb{D}_{n}(B)
is determined by the universal property.
For an augmented
\mathbf{E}_{n}
-algebra
\epsilon_{C}:C\to k
, we write

4.1.
Generalities on
t

Unless otherwise stated, we adopt the cohomological index.
Let us recall the notion of
t
-structures on stable
\infty
-categories.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable
\infty
-category.
Recall that the homotopy category
\operatorname{h}(\mathcal{C})
has the structure of a triangulated category.
A
t
-structure on
\mathcal{C}
is a pair of full subcategories
(\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0})
of
\mathcal{C}

such that
(\operatorname{h}(\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}),\operatorname{h}(\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0}))
is a
t
-structure on the triangulated category
\operatorname{h}(\mathcal{C})
(see e.g.
[
11
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
7
, Section 2.1]
).
We shall write
\mathcal{C}^{\leq n}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\geq n}
for
\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}[-n]
and
\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0}[-n]
, respectively.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0})
is a
t
-structure.
The inclusion
\mathcal{C}^{\leq n}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}
has a right adjoint

\tau^{\leq n}:\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\leq n}

and the inclusion
\mathcal{C}^{\geq n}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}

has a left adjoint
\tau^{\geq n}:\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\geq n}
.
There exists a canonical equivalence
\tau^{\geq 0}\circ\tau^{\leq 0}\simeq\tau^{\leq 0}\circ\tau^{\geq 0}
.
We often use the notation
(\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0})
to denote a
t
-structure on
\mathcal{C}
.
Note that
\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0}
is uniquely determined by
\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}
: the full subcategory
\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0}
is spanned by those objects
C^{\prime}
such that
\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(C,C^{\prime})
is contractible for any
C\in\mathcal{C}^{\leq-1}
. Dually,
\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}
is uniquely determined by
\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0}
.
By
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0})
we mean a stable
\infty
-category endowed with the
t
-structure determined by a full subcategory
\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0})
be a
t
-structure on a stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
.

(1)

For any
n\in\mathbf{Z}
, the left adjoint functor
\tau^{\geq n}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\geq n}
to the inclusion

\mathcal{C}^{\geq n}\subset\mathcal{C}
induces a morphism
C\to\tau^{\geq n}(C)
, determined by the unit map. For all
n\in\mathbf{Z}
, this yields the sequence

C\to\cdots\to\tau^{\geq n-1}(C)\to\tau^{\geq n}(C)\to\tau^{\geq n+1}(C)\to\cdots

in
\mathcal{C}
.
The
t
-structure is left complete if, for any
C\in\mathcal{C}
, the induced morphism
C\to\varprojlim_{n\in\mathbf{Z}}\tau^{\geq n}(C)
is an equivalence.

(2)

The right adjoint functor
\tau^{\leq n}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\leq n}
to the inclusion

\mathcal{C}^{\leq n}\subset\mathcal{C}
induces a morphism
\tau^{\leq n}(C)\to C
, determined by the counit map. For all
n\in\mathbf{Z}
, this yields the sequence

\cdots\to\tau^{\leq n-1}(C)\to\tau^{\leq n}(C)\to\tau^{\leq n+1}(C)\to\cdots\to C

in
\mathcal{C}
.
The
t
-structure is right complete if, for any
C\in\mathcal{C}
, the induced morphism
\varinjlim_{n\in\mathbf{Z}}\tau^{\leq n}(C)\to C
is an equivalence.

(3)

The
t
-structure is left separated if
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbf{Z}}\mathcal{C}^{\leq n}=0
.
Dually, it is right separated if
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbf{Z}}\mathcal{C}^{\geq n}=0
.

(4)

If
\mathcal{C}
admits filtered colimits and the full subcategory
\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0}
is closed under filtered colimits in
\mathcal{C}
, we say that the
t
-structure is compatible with filtered colimits.

(5)

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is presentable. The
t
-structure
(\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0})
is accessible if the full subcategory
\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}
is presentable.

(6)

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
has a monoidal structure. If the tensor product functor
\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
sends
\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}\times\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}
to
\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}
and the unit object lies in
\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}
, we say that the
t
-structure is compatible with the monoidal structure.

Remark 4.2
.

If
C\in\cap_{n\in\mathbf{Z}}\mathcal{C}^{\leq n}

\tau^{\geq n}C\simeq 0
for all

It follows that if a

-structure

is left complete,
then it is left separated.
Dually, if a
t
-structure is right complete,
then it is right separated.

Diabetic retinopathy enhances coronary artery calcium-based cardiovascular risk stratification in patients with type 2 diabetes: insights from the ACCoDiab study
Authors: Franck Phan, Fabrizio Andreelli, Thomas Broussaud, Samia Boussouar, Anne-Caroline Jeannin, Suzanne Laroche, Anne Toulgoat, Antonio Gallo, Olivier Bourron, Alban Redheuil
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe coronary artery calcium (CAC) score is a validated imaging biomarker enhancing cardiovascular risk stratification in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). However, residual risk persists, particularly among patients with moderate CAC scores. Diabetic retinopathy (DR), a microvascular complication of diabetes, may reflect systemic vascular vulnerability and provide additional prognostic information.MethodsThis observational cohort study was conducted as part of the primary prevention ACCoDiab study. 424 patients with T2DM (175 females and 249 males, with a mean age of 61 years) and no history of cardiovascular events underwent CAC scoring and clinical assessments, including DR screening. Patients were monitored for seven years or until the first cardiovascular events, including nonfatal myocardial infarction, ischemic stroke, hospitalization for heart failure, peripheral artery disease revascularization, and cardiovascular death. Cox regression, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, and net reclassification improvement (NRI) were used to evaluate the added prognostic value of DR to CAC scoring and to develop a simple integrated cardiovascular risk score.ResultsFifty patients (11.8%) had incident cardiovascular events. DR was significantly more prevalent among those who experienced events (58% vs. 25%, p < 0.001). Both CAC score and DR were independently associated with cardiovascular events. When incorporating DR into the CAC-based risk model, the predictive power was significantly enhanced AUC 75 [0.69–0.82] vs. 68 [0.61–0.75], p = 0.01). Further improvement was observed after adjusting for traditional risk factors (AUC 79 [0.73–0.86] vs. 73 [0.66–0.81], p = 0.04). The presence of DR reclassified a significant proportion of patients into higher-risk categories, particularly those with low and moderate CAC scores. Additionally, the severity of DR was also found to provide incremental prognostic information but did not provide significant additional stratification beyond the binary presence of DR.ConclusionThe combination of diabetic retinopathy and CAC score significantly enhances cardiovascular risk stratification in T2DM patients, beyond traditional risk factors, highlighting that both microvascular and macrovascular factors should be considered in comprehensive cardiovascular risk assessment. This approach, integrated to clinical routine, may help identify patients for earlier intervention and more personalized treatment strategies.Trial registration NCT03920683.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12933-026-03314-w.

Research insights

What is currently known about this topic?

In patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and moderate Coronary Artery Calcium (CAC) score, residual cardiovascular risk remains. Improving cardiovascular risk stratification in these patients is needed.

What is the key research question?

Can diabetic retinopathy (DR) improve the prognostic performance of the CAC score in predicting cardiovascular events in patients with T2DM ?

What is new?

The combination of DR and CAC score significantly enhances cardiovascular risk stratification in patients living with T2DM, even after adjustment for traditional risk factors and regardless of the severity of DR.

How might this study influence clinical practice?

Our results support the inclusion of DR in clinical algorithms and risk calculators to improve cardiovascular risk stratification in patients with T2DM.

Introduction

Cardiovascular (CV) disease remains the leading cause of morbidity and mortality in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). However, CV risk is highly heterogeneous within this population, ranging from low to very high levels [1–6], highlighting the need for more refined and individualized risk stratification strategies. Despite major therapeutic advances, accurately identifying patients at highest risk of future CV events in primary prevention remains a key clinical challenge.

Coronary artery calcium (CAC) scoring has emerged as a robust tool for evaluating subclinical atherosclerosis and improving CV risk stratification. By directly quantifying the burden of coronary calcified plaque, CAC provides additional prognostic information beyond that provided by traditional risk factors and is therefore increasingly used to guide preventive strategies [7–11]. Higher CAC scores are consistently associated with an increased risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) [12, 13]. Nevertheless, CAC-based stratification has important limitations. A substantial proportion of patients with T2DM classified as low or intermediate risk according to CAC still experience CV events, suggesting the presence of residual risk not captured by coronary calcification alone [13].

Microvascular complications of diabetes may provide additional insightinto residual risk.Diabetic retinopathy (DR), for example, reflects cumulative exposure to chronic hyperglycaemia and is a hallmark of systemic microvascular damage [14–20]. Beyond its ophthalmological implications, DR has been associated with an increased risk of CV outcomes, including coronary artery disease [21–25], stroke [24, 26], heart failure [27, 28], and peripheral arterial disease [29–31]. These observations support the concept that microvascular and macrovascular complications share common pathophysiological pathways, such as endothelial dysfunction, inflammation, and oxidative stress, while also capturing distinct aspects of vascular injury.

However, the role of DR in CV risk stratification remains insufficiently integrated into current guidelines. The 2020 joint recommendations from the French societies of diabetology and cardiology - Société Francophone du Diabète (SFD) and Société Française de Cardiologie (SFC) - consider only severe DR as a marker of increased CV risk, and classify these patients as having high, rather than very high, CV risk [32]. Similarly, the 2023 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines do not consider DR in isolation, but rather incorporate microvascular disease only when multiple complications are present (e.g., in combination with nephropathy and/or neuropathy) [33]. Therefore, these approaches may therefore underestimate the prognostic value of DR alone, particularly in patients who are otherwise considered to be at low-to-moderate risk as microvascular alterations may precede clinical complications. Together, these positions emphasize the discrepancybetween the latest pathophysiological and epidemiological evidence and its application in clinical risk stratification tools.

Due to the different, yet potentiallycomplementary pathophysiological underpinnings of macrovascular calcification for CAC and microvascular damage for DR, assessing these two markers together may improve CV risk stratification in patients with T2DM. Importantly, DR assessment is important because it is non-invasive, widely available, and routinely performed in clinical practice, making it an attractive candidate for integration into existing risk models.

In this context, we hypothesized that incorporating DR into CAC-based risk stratification would improve the prediction and reclassification of CV risk, particularly among patients with low-to-moderate CAC scores. We therefore evaluated whether adding DR to CAC improves prediction of incident CV events in a well-characterized cohort of patients with T2DM.

Methods

Study design and population

ACCoDiab is a propestive follow-up monocentric study with cross sectional data collection at baseline (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier NCT03920683). The recruitment period extended from January 2014 to May 2017. This study is an ancillary analysis of the ACCoDiab study and included 424 patients with T2DM, enrolled during routine hospital admissions for CV risk assessment at the Diabetes day-hospital of the Pitié-Salpêtrière Sorbonne University Hospital in Paris. Inclusion criteria were: age ≥ 18 years, confirmed T2DM diagnosis according to the American Diabetes Association diagnostic criteria, no previous history of CV events, and available computed tomography CAC score and DR screening. All participants provided written informed consent and were followed for 7 years or until the first CV event. The study was approved by the institutional ethics committee.

Cardiovascular outcomes

The primary outcome was the occurrence of a CV event over a 7-year follow-up period (from January 1st, 2014 to December 31st, 2023). Events included nonfatal myocardial infarction, ischemic stroke, hospitalization for heart failure, limb revascularization due to peripheral artery disease, or CV death. Data were retrieved from centralized hospital records and verified via direct contact or the national mortality registry (INSEE).

Coronary artery calcium scoring

All patients with T2DM were evaluated as part of routine clinical care for CV risk assessment and prevention during scheduled outpatient visits at the Diabetes day-hospital. CAC assessment was performed as part of standard care in the Department of Cardiovascular and Thoracic Imaging.

All patients underwent non-contrast electrocardiogram ECG-gated computed tomography (CT) for CAC scoring. Scans were acquired using either a dual-source SOMATOM Definition Flash (majority) or a single-source SOMATOM Edge CT scanner (Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany).

Image acquisition was performed using a prospectively ECG-triggered sequential mode, targeting mid-diastole, without contrast injection. The scan extended from the top of the aortic arch to the cardiac apex, using the following parameters: 120 kVp or equivalent tube voltage, modulated mAs, 3 mm slice thickness.

CAC scoring was conducted using the Agatston method. A calcified atherosclerotic lesion was defined as a contiguous pixel area ≥ 1 mm² with a peak attenuation ≥ 130 Hounsfield units (HU). The calcium score per lesion was calculated by multiplying the lesion area by a density weighting factor based on peak HU: 1 for 130–199 HU; 2 for 200–299 HU; 3 for 300–399 HU; 4 for ≥ 400 HU. Total CAC scores were categorized into four strata, based on established risk Agatston units (AU) thresholds: 0 (no detectable calcification), 1–99 (mild), 100–399 (moderate), ≥ 400 AU (severe).

Retinopathy assessment

DR was assessed at baseline using standardized digital fundus photography, as part of routine diabetes care. Retinal images were evaluated by trained ophthalmologists in accordance with established international grading criteria, namely the International Clinical Diabetic Retinopathy Disease Severity Scale (ICDR). This classification system defines five stages: no retinopathy, mild non-proliferative DR (NPDR), moderate NPDR, severe NPDR, and proliferative DR (PDR).

For the purposes of statistical analysis, DR was primarily analyzed as a binary variable (present vs. absent). Any level of retinopathy, from mild NPDR to PDR, was classified as “present,” while the absence of any detectable lesions was classified as “absent.” This approach allowed for a clear and clinically meaningful comparison of CV risk.

In addition, we performed complementary analyses based on DR severity. Patients were categorized into three groups: no retinopathy, non-proliferative DR (including mild, moderate, and severe NPDR), and proliferative DR. This secondary stratification enabled us to explore whether the severity of microvascular damage provided incremental prognostic information beyond the simple presence of DR.

Baseline clinical and laboratory variables

Baseline variables were selected based on their established relevance in CV risk prediction models, particularly those outlined in the 2023 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines on diabetes and CV disease. The variables reported included:

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were compared using chi-squared tests; continuous variables using Mann-Whitney U tests. Kaplan-Meier survival curves and log-rank tests evaluated time-to-event differences across CAC and DR categories. Then, Multivariable Cox proportional hazards models assessed the independent associations of CAC score and DR with CV events. Six models were compared: CAC, adjusted CAC, CAC + DR, adjusted CAC + DR, CAC + DR type and adjusted CAC + DR type. The multivariable adjustment model included age, gender, diabetes duration, HbA1c, smoking status, hypertension, dyslipidemia, neuropathy, nephropathy and glucose-lowering therapies (metformin, sulfonylureas/glinides, DPP-4 inhibitors, GLP1 receptor agonists, insulin). During the inclusion period, SGLT2 inhibitors were not available in France, no patients were treated with this class of drugs in this study. Assumptions of Cox regression modeling were checked afterwards for all models with assessment of residuals by applying Schoenfeld tests. We also built additional adjusted Cox regression models with LASSO regularization to reduce the number of adjusting features. We performed a Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis to estimate discriminatory ability of prediction models. To do so, we applied Liu’s criteria, which consist in maximizing the product of sensitivity and specificity. As compared to usual Youden criteria, Liu criteria favors more balanced performances with respect to both sensitivity and specificity. Pairwise AUC comparisons were performed with DeLong test or stratified bootstrapping for respectively nested and non-nested prediction models. We also assessed sensitivity, specificity, positive and negative predictive values and also accuracy of models. Net reclassification improvement (NRI) analysis quantified the change in risk classification between models. A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. No internal validation was performed as we had a reduced number of events.

Subgroup Analysis examining potential interactions between DR and various confounding factors, such asage, sex, nephropathy, diabetes duration, and treatment factors (such as metformin, sulfonylureas/glinides, DPP-4 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists and insulin), in relation to CV events were also performed. Analysis were performed without and also with Benjamin-Hochberg false discovery rate correction to compensate for multiple comparisons. Finally, we provided a simple risk-scoring tool combining Calcium scoring with DR and smoking status to ease patient management in routine clinical setting. Those risk-scoring charts have been generated by testing all possible combination of factors with respect to our adjusted models. Each box of those risk-scoring charts provides the median risk score. No internal validation nor boostrap optimism correction was performed.

Results

Baseline characteristics and event rates

The study population comprised 424 patients (175 females and 249 males) with T2DM and a median age of 61 (54.00–67.00) years. All patients were followed for 7 years or until the occurrence of a first CV event. During follow-up, 50 patients (11.8%) experienced a first CV event, including non-fatal myocardial infarction (n = 32), ischemic stroke (n = 10), hospitalization for heart failure (n = 5), and lower-limb revascularization (n = 3). No CV deaths were recorded. The median follow-up duration for patients who experienced an event was 1,406 days.

As shown in Table 1, baseline demographic and clinical characteristics were generally comparable between patients who experienced CV events and those who remained event-free. There were no significant differences in age (63 vs. 61 years, p = 0.10), gender distribution (34% vs. 42% female, p = 0.30), duration of diabetes or BMI. However, active smoking was significantly more prevalent among patients who experienced an event (30% vs. 18%, p = 0.042).

Notably, when analyzed as a binary variable (presence versus. absence), DR was significantly more prevalent among patients who developed CV events (58% vs. 25%, p < 0.001). In addition, patients who experienced CV events were more likely to have more advanced stages of retinopathy, with a higher proportion of proliferative DR compared to those without events 14% vs. 8.8%, p < 0.001). CAC scores were markedly higher in the CV event group (median Agatston score: 221.7 versus. 33.2, p < 0.001). The proportion of patients with CAC = 0 was 33% in the control group vs. 10% in the event group whereas CAC ≥ 400 was present in 36% of patients in the event group versus only 15% of patients without events. The distribution of intermediate CAC categories (1–99 and 100–399) was similar between the two groups. These findings suggest a significant difference in the prevalence of advanced coronary calcification among individuals who experienced an event, althoughthe intermediate CAC categories (1–99 and 100–399) do not clearly distinguish between those who did and did not experience anevent.

Cox regression confirms independent and additive value of DR

Multivariable Cox regression models (Table 2) confirmed that both CAC score and DR were both independently associated with CV outcomes. In the unadjusted CAC model, the hazard ratios (HRs) increased progressively with increasing CAC burden versus. having no coronary artery calcium (CAC = 0): HR 2.96 [1.08–8.15] for CAC 1–99, HR 3.97 [1.40–11.30] for CAC 100–399, and HR 7.13 [2.65–19.20] for CAC ≥ 400. This supports the idea of a consistent stepwise increase in CV risk across CAC categories.

Adding the DR as a binary variable to the CAC model (CAC + DR model) demonstrated that the presence of DR was independently associated with a higher risk of CV events (HR 3.11 [1.75–5.52], p < 0.001). This association remained robust after full adjustment for confounding factors (adjusted CAC + DR model), with an even greater effect size (HR 3.91 [2.10–7.27], p < 0.001). Meanwhile CAC categories retained their independent prognostic value. Among the covariates, active smoking was identified as an independent predictor (HR 2.06 [1.06–4.02], p = 0.034), whereas age, hypertension, dyslipidemia, and other microvascular complications were not significantly associated with the outcomes.

Taking DR severity into account (CAC + DR type model), revealed that non-proliferative DR was strongly and independently associated with CV events (adjusted HR 4.59 [2.41–8.73], p < 0.001). In contrast, proliferative DR showed a weaker and non-significant association after adjustment (HR 2.39 [0.92–6.21], p = 0.073), which is likely due to limited statistical power resulting from the smaller sample size in this subgroup. Importantly, incorporating DR severity did not significantly improve risk prediction compared with the binary DR model, suggesting that the presence of DR itself captures most of its prognostic value.

To reduce the risk of overfitting associated with the large number of covariates, additional LASSO-regularized Cox regression models were performed. The independent associations of both DR presence and DR severity with cardiovascular events remained unchanged (Supplementary Table 1), demonstrating that our findings were robust to more parsimonious model specification.

Subgroup analysis of the interaction between diabetic retinopathy and cardiovascular events

We investigated whether the association between diabetic retinopathy (DR) and cardiovascular (CV) events differed according to clinically relevant patient characteristics, including age, sex, nephropathy, diabetes duration, HbA1c, and glucose-lowering therapies (metformin, sulfonylureas/glinides, DPP-4 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists, and insulin).

Overall, no significant interactions were observed between DR and any of these clinical variables after correction for multiple testing using the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (BH-FDR) procedure (Table 3). These findings indicate that the association between DR and CV events was consistent across the predefined patient subgroups.

Before correction for multiple testing, a significant interaction was observed between DR status and sex (P for interaction = 0.045), with a stronger association between DR and CV events in women (HR 9.06, 95% CI 2.95–27.81) than in men (HR 2.38, 95% CI 1.20–4.72). DR improves predictive accuracy of CAC-based models

The CAC-only model showed modest discriminatory ability, with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.68 [0.61–0.75]. Adjustment for age, gender, diabetes duration, HbA1c, smoking status, hypertension, dyslipidemia, neuropathy, nephropathy and glucose-lowering therapies (metformin, sulfonylureas/glinides, DPP-4 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists, insulin) improved model performance (Adj. CAC), increasing the AUC to 0.73 [0.66–0.81].

Adding DR to CAC improved discrimination, with the CAC + DR model yielding an AUC of 0.75 [0.69–0.82]. Further improvement was observed in the fully adjusted model (Adj. CAC + DR), which achieved the highest performance of all these models with an AUC of 0.79 [0.73–0.86].

Incorporating DR severity provided only modest incremental improvements in predictive accuracy. The CAC + DR type model achieved an AUC of 0.76 [0.69–0.82], showing no meaningful improvement over the binary DR model. Similarly, the fully adjusted model incorporating CAC and DR severity (Adj. CAC + DR type) reached an AUC of 0.79 [0.73–0.85], performing similarly to the Adj. CAC + DR model.

Overall, these findings demonstrate that including DR in CAC-based models improves their discriminatory capacity, with the greatest benefit observed when adjusting for age, gender, diabetes duration, HbA1c, smoking status, hypertension, dyslipidemia, neuropathy, nephropathy and glucose-lowering therapies. While accounting for DR severity slightly improves prediction, it does not substantially outperform the binary DR classification in this cohort.

Consistent with the primary analyses, ROC curves derived from the LASSO-regularized parsimonious models showed that both the adjusted CAC + DR (AUC = 0.786) and adjusted CAC + DR type (AUC = 0.788) models retained higher discriminative ability than the adjusted CAC model alone (AUC = 0.727), confirming that the incremental prognostic information provided by DR remained robust in simplified models (Supplementary Fig. 1).

Overall, the total categorical NRI was not statistically significant (0.12, p = 0.19), while the continuous NRI remained significant (0.652, p < 0.00001), suggesting an improvement in global risk prediction despite modest category shifts.

In adjusted models (adjusted CAC + DR vs. adjusted CAC), reclassification performance improved further. The categorical NRI reached statistical significance overall (0.208 [0.05–0.367], p = 0.00997), with particularly strong effects in the intermediate-risk group (0.781 [0.421–1.141], p < 0.0001). Notably, five out of eightevents were correctly reclassified upwards, while substantial reclassification downwards was observed among non-events in the high-risk category (57 individuals). The continuous NRI remained robust (0.652, p < 0.00001).

When DR severity was incorporated (CAC + DR type vs. CAC), the results remained consistent but were slightly attenuated. The categorical NRI in the low-risk group did not reach statistical significance (0.416, p = 0.06), whereas the intermediate-risk group showed a significant improvement (0.408, p = 0.0019). There was no significant improvement observed in the high-risk group, and the overall categorical NRI remained non-significant (0.113, p = 0.21). However, continuous NRI again demonstrated significant improvement (0.652, p < 0.00001).

Finally, the fully adjusted model incorporating DR severity (adjusted CAC + DR type) produced the most consistent improvements across risk strata. Althoughthere was no significant reclassification in the low-risk group was not significant (categorical NRI 0.101, p = 0.58), substantial gains were observed in the intermediate-risk group (0.820 [0.462–1.178], p < 0.00001), confirming this subgroup as the main driver of clinical benefit. The high-risk category also showed significant improvement (0.245, p = 0.0009), primarily due to appropriate reclassification of non-events as lower risk. Overall, the total categorical NRI was significant (0.186 [0.037–0.332], p = 0.014), with a strong continuous NRI (0.586, p = 7 × 10− 5), indicating a meaningful enhancement in global risk discrimination.

Taken together, these results suggest that incorporating DR into CAC significantly improves CV risk stratification compared to CAC alone, both before and after adjustment. However, adding DR severity does not provide any additional clinically meaningful benefit beyond the binary presence of DR.

The results highlight the significant impact of DR on CV risk, particularly in individuals with moderate or mild CAC scores. While the absence of both coronary calcification and DR results in consistently low risk, the presence of DR causes a significant upward shift in risk classification, irrespective of CAC score.

For individuals with mild CAC scores (1–99), the presence of DR has a significant impact on risk classification. Non-smokers in the categorywith no DR in this category remain in the low-risk range (3.5%). However, as soon as retinopathy is present, particularly in combination with smoking, risk increases substantially. Smokers with retinopathy in this category are classified as being high-risk or even very high-risk, with risk levels reaching up to 24.6%.

A similar trend is observed for individuals with a moderate CAC score (100–399), the presence of DR causes a clear and dramatic increase in CV risk. Non-smokers with DR in this range are classified in the intermediate risk category (7.2%), whereas smokers with DR are classified as very high risk (25.9%). This stark contrast illustrates how DR acts as a major modifier of risk, particularly for smokers. Even without elevated CAC scores, the combination of DR and smoking places individuals in the highest risk categories, emphasizing the amplified CV threat posed by DR, with significant implications for clinical decision-making.

These findings strongly suggest that DR is an important marker associated with increased CV risk. It is particularly influential when combined with smoking and moderate or high CAC scores, highlighting the importance of considering both CAC and DR status in assessing CV risk.

Discussion

This study uniquely shows that the combination of DR and CAC score significantly improves cardiovascular risk stratification in patients with T2DM, even after adjusting for traditional risk factors. This highlights the importance of consideringboth microvascular and macrovascular risk factors when carrying out comprehensive cardiovascular risk assessment and reclassifying patients to their real individual CV risk. This integrated approach may help identify patients for earlier intervention and more personalized treatment strategies. This strategy helps to reassess cardiovascular risk, particularly for patients who are incorrectly classified as low-risk but are actually at intermediate or high cardiovascular risk. These findings have important clinical consequences and could influence cardiovascular risk stratification strategies, but they also raise questions about the ability of DR to improve the prediction of cardiovascular events.

Diabetic retinopathy and cardiovascular events

CV diseases are the leading cause of mortality in patients with T2DM. Traditional CV risk models, including those integrating CAC, focus primarily on macrovascular events. However, in diabetes, the vascular pathology is systemic and not limited to large vessel atherosclerosis. Notably, while CAC quantifies structural, calcified atherosclerosis of large and middle-sized arteries, it can underestimate cardiovascular risk in patients with non-calcified or lipid-rich plaques, or with predominant microvascular dysfunction. DR addresses this issue by identifying patients with subclinical or systemic vascular impairment, which is potentially caused by diffuse endothelial injury and microcirculatory rarefaction. Recently, the evaluation of retinal microcrovasculature using optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA) parameters, was found to be closely associated with coronary atherosclerosis [34]. However, DR may provide a more comprehensive indication of generalized vascular injury, encompassing both the microvascular and macrovascular beds while being readily available in routine workup. Our study directly addresses this gap by evaluating the added value of DR assessment to CAC-based risk stratification, a concept that has received limited attention until now. Thus, DR highlights risks beyond classic atherothrombotic events, including heart failure. Recent evidence suggests that CAC is not equally and linearly predictive across all forms of CV disease. For example, data from the MESA study revealed a significant association between CAC and heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) only in women [35], highlighting sex-specific differences and limitations of CAC as a universal marker. These findings challenge the notion that CAC can be used universally and highlight the importance of microvascular dysfunction, a key factor in diastolic dysfunction and HFpEF that cannot be quantified by CAC. In contrast, DR has been independently associated with both systolic [27, 28] and diastolic [36] cardiac dysfunction, carotid intima-media thickness [37, 38] and stroke [24, 26], coronaropathy atherosclerosis severity [39], coronary microvascular dysfunction [21–25], and peripheral arterial disease [29–31]. Notably, DR has been linked to incident HFpEF even after adjusting for conventional risk factors, including age, sex, diabetes duration, insulin use, blood pressure, lipid profile [28]. This suggests that DR may reflect also the microvascular impairment in HFpEF. These findings support the use of DR as a proxy for systemic microangiopathy, driven by shared mechanisms such as oxidative stress, inflammation, and endothelial dysfunction. This microangiopathy is relevant to a range of CV conditions, including coronary and cerebrovascular diseases, peripheral arterial disease and heart failure. In addition, our findings indicate that the simple binary classification of DR (present vs. absent) is sufficient to significantly improve CV risk stratification, without the need for further granularity based on DR severity. This is a key observation, as it simplifies the decision-making process for clinicians. By combining these markers, we provide a more nuanced approach to CV risk stratification in patients with T2DM, particularly in those with low-to-moderate CAC scores, where traditional risk models may fail to capture the full extent of residual risk. This aligns with previous findings which concluded that the presence of DR itself is a robust predictor of long-term CV outcomes [40, 41] with the same tendency when stratisfied for the stage of DR. Therefore, DR expands the assessment of vascular risk beyond what that can be offered by CAC alone thereby supporting its integration into CV risk models. Thus, our heatmap visualization tool, integrating both CAC and DR status, provides a straightforward and practical approach to reclassifying risk with potential implications for clinical practice. This tool can guide therapeutic decisions, such as statin therapy initiation and antiplatelet therapy prescription, offering clear guidance for clinicians managing patients with T2DM at risk for CV events.

The gender-specific findings: cardiovascular risk underestimation in women

Before correction for multiple testing, a stronger association between DR and cardiovascular risk was observed in women than in men. This finding is consistent with several large-scale epidemiological studies suggesting that cardiovascular risk in women with type 2 diabetes is frequently underestimated [42]. Furthermore, previous findings suggest that PDR and NPDR, regardless of the stage, in women with T2DM were predictors of cardiovascular events, whereas only PDR was a predictor in men with T2DM [43].

In addition, epidemiological studies have shown that premenopausal women have a significantly lower prevalence of diabetic retinopathy than postmenopausal women after accounting for diabetes duration and glycaemic control [44]. Experimental studies further support a biological role of sex hormones, demonstrating reduced neurovascular unit damage, including decreased pericyte loss and microglial activation, in the diabetic retina of female animals [45]. Together, these findings suggest that estradiol may exert protective effects on the diabetic retina and support a potential role of sex-specific mechanisms linking retinal microvascular injury and cardiovascular disease.

However, these observations should be interpreted cautiously. In our study, the interaction between DR and sex was no longer statistically significant after correction for multiple testing, and no interaction with age was observed. Therefore, our data do not support a definitive sex-specific effect or permit conclusions regarding menopausal status or age-related hormonal changes. Larger prospective studies specifically designed to investigate sex- and menopause-related differences will be required to clarify whether hormonal factors modify the association between DR and cardiovascular risk.

Residual risk in low-to-moderate CAC patients : the role of DR in cardiovascular risk prediction

Our study also highlights the importance of integrating DR into comprehensive CV risk prediction models. As demonstrated by previous studies [24–31], microvascular complications such as DR offer significant prognostic value in predicting CV events. While CAC scoring is a widely accepted tool for assessing subclinical atherosclerosis, it primarily captures existing macrovascular damage. The addition of DR, a marker of microvascular disease, provides a more holistic view of a patient’s CV risk profile, particularly for those with low-to-moderate CAC scores, where residual risk remains inadequately addressed [46]. This approach aligns with previous findings [47] which suggested that combining microvascular and macrovascular markers can improve risk prediction and stratification.

Furthermore, our findings indicate that the presence of DR provides incremental prognostic information beyond CAC scoring, and remains independently associated with CV outcomes even after adjusting for traditional risk factors. Thus, integrating DR into risk models may help identify low- and moderate-risk individuals who would benefit from early intervention and more intensive preventive care.

Lower cardiovascular risk in patients with proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR)

An interesting finding in our study was the observation that patients with PDR had a seemingly lower CV risk compared to those with NPDR. This finding, although counterintuitive, has been noted in several studies including the ACCORD study. In fact, the hazard ratio for myocardial infarction was 2.35 (95% CI: 1.42–3.91) in patients with moderate DR and 1.84 (95% CI: 0.99–3.42) in those with severe DR [25]. This may be explained by the fact that patients with PDR are typically considered to be at very high CV risk due to the severity of their microvascular complications. As a result, these patients often receive more intensive CV risk management, including more aggressive treatment with cholesterol-lowering treatment, antiplatelet drug, antihypertensive agents, and glucose-lowering therapies, which may contribute to a relatively lower observed CV risk.

In contrast, patients with NPDR, particularly those with mild to moderate stages, may not receive the same level of aggressive CV treatment, leading to a higher residual CV risk despite the presence of diabetes. Therefore, the lower CV risk observed in patients with PDR in our study may reflect the impact of more intensive medical management, which might not be as pronounced in patients with NPDR (Supplementary Table 2).

Limitations and future directions

Despite promising findings, our study has some limitations. Firstly, this was a single-center study conducted in a French tertiary hospital, where patients were referred to a dedicated day hospital for comprehensive cardiovascular risk assessement. Consequently, our cohort likely represents individuals perceived to be at higher CV risk or with a greater burden of comorbidities than the general population of patients with type 2 diabetes. This referral pattern may partly explain the relatively high prevalence of DR (29%) and severe coronary artery calcification CAC ≥ 400 (18%) observed in our cohort. Therefore, caution is warranted when extrapolating our findings to community-based or lower-risk populations. Secondly, the relatively small sample size in the subgroup analysis of DR severity, may affect the robustness of our conclusions regarding the impact of different DR stages on CV risk. Thirdly, our study has a follow-up duration of 7 years, which, while informative, may not fully capture long-term CV outcomes, especially in individuals at low and moderate risk. Future research involving larger, multicenter cohorts including patients with a broder spectrum of CV risk and longer follow-up periods is required to validate our findings in diverse populations. Lastly, limitations related to CAC score assessment should be acknowledged when interpreting our findings. Although CAC quantification is now highly standardized and reproducible, supported by automated measurement with systematic human verification, an inherent limitation of CAC assessment lies in the Agatston scoring method, which gives greater weight to high-density calcifications. Thus, large and dense plaques contribute disproportionately to the final score, whereas microcalcifications and lower-density calcified plaques may be underrepresented or fall below the detection threshold. Consequently, Agatston scoring may preferentially capture established, densely calcified atherosclerosis while being less sensitive to early, diffuse, or less densely calcified disease. This limitation may be relevant in younger individuals, particularly women, in whom calcified atherosclerosis generally develops later, although MESA [12] showed no sex-related difference in the ability of CAC to predict major adverse cardiovascular events. It may also be particularly important in diabetes, where coronary atherosclerosis is often more diffuse and complex, with mixed plaques and microcalcific changes occurring earlier than in individuals without diabetes. Underestimation of microcalcific or lower-density calcified disease could therefore lead to incomplete assessment of subclinical atherosclerosis in some patients with T2DM [48], potentially affecting risk stratification and interpretation of associations between CAC burden and clinical or metabolic variables. Although CAC remains one of the strongest independent predictors of ASCVD events, these methodological considerations should be taken into account when interpreting the present findings.

In conclusion, although we have demonstrated the added value of DR in improving CV risk prediction, future research should focus on understanding the underlying mechanisms linking DR with CV events. Specifically, investigating the contribution of inflammation, oxidative stress, and endothelial dysfunction in both microvascular and macrovascular damage could refine our understanding of how DR and CAC together can enhance risk prediction and guide tailored therapeutic strategies.

Conclusion

This study shows that DR, a readily available, non-invasive, and cost-effective marker, significantly improves CV risk prediction when combined with CAC scoring in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus in a primary prevention setting. Therefore, CAC and DR represent two complementary markers of cardiovascular risk. Notably, the greatest improvement in risk stratification was observed among individuals with intermediate CAC score, a group for whom clinical decision-making is often the most uncertain. Identifying these patients who are at high- and very high-risk and who would otherwise be misclassified by CAC alone, enables more précise and personalized CV prevention strategies. Although DR is largely absent from conventional cardiovascular risk prediction models, our results support its inclusion in clinical algorithms and risk calculators.

Contact 4d Chern-Simons theory: Generalities
David M. Schmidtt

Abstract
We refine and generalize the results of Yo , where evidence in favor of applying the non-Abelian localization method to handle the 4d Chern-Simons theory path integral formulation was presented. We show, via duality manipulations and invoking some symplectic geometry results, both inspired by the Beasley-Witten work NA loc CS , that the path integral of a regularized version of the 4d Chern-Simons theory, formally takes the canonical symplectic form required by the method of non-Abelian localization. The new theory is defined on a deformed quotient space and interpolates between the conventional 3d Chern-Simons theory on a Seifert manifold M NA loc CS , trivially embedded into ℝ × M \mathbb{R}\times\text{M} , and the Costello-Yamazaki CY 4d Chern-Simons theory defined on the same 4d manifold. It is also shown that the regularized theory is consistent, following an idea of Beasley Wilson NA loc , with the insertion of coadjoint orbit defects of the 1d Chern-Simons theory type. This approach opens the possibility for using exact path integral methods to explore the quantum integrable structure of certain 2d integrable sigma models of the non-ultralocal type, which are widely known to be somehow immune to the use of more traditional quantization methods, like the algebraic Bethe ansatz. Keywords: Chern-Simon theories, non-Abelian localization, integrable sigma models.

2
Contact 4d CS theory as a dual action

We introduce, in the usual 4d CS theory, certain modifications that are necessary for applying the method of non-Abelian localization. We start with a review of the conventional 4d CS theory, then construct a regularization thereof that allows the implementation of the ideas of
NA loc CS
and finish, via duality manipulations, with the introduction of the so-called contact 4d CS theory. Furthermore, following
Wilson NA loc
, we include (co)-adjoint orbits defects of the 1d CS theory type that will modify, in a consistent way, the path integral formulation of the regularized 4d CS theory.

Before starting, a comment is in order. In formulating the original 4d CS theory, known boundary conditions are imposed from the outset upon the space of gauge connections where the theory is defined. As our main interest is to introduce a consistent modification of the latter that localizes, we will keep throughout the whole analysis, all boundary contributions until we can safely discard them and by this we mean proving that the same boundary conditions valid in the original 4d CS theory can be enforced as well upon the field content in all action functionals used in our approach.

After having regularized the 4d-CS theory, we proceed now to decouple a second component of the gauge field
\mathbb{A}

NA loc CS

S(\mathbb{A},\Phi)_{\text{ext}}:=S(\mathbb{A}-\kappa\Phi)_{\text{reg}}.

This action is manifestly invariant under the

{}^{\kappa}\mathbb{A}:=\mathbb{A}+s\kappa,\text{ \qquad\qquad}^{\kappa}\Phi:=\Phi+s,

(2.28)

This extended action is gauge equivalent, under

-shifts, to the regularized action (
2.12
) by taking the gauge

\Phi=0.

(2.29)

Notice in passing that, from (

\iota_{X}\big(^{\kappa}\mathbb{A}\big)=\iota_{X}\mathbb{A}+s,

\iota_{X}\mathbb{A}=0.

(2.31)

We keep the gauge fixing conditions (

The boxed action functionals in the diagram, are dual to each other and are classically equivalent. After implementing steps I and II, we do not recover the original theory (
2.1

2.1.4
Gauge invariance I

Here, we briefly consider the behavior of the action functionals introduced so far, namely (
2.12
), (
2.27
) and (
2.37
), under the action of the group
\mathcal{G}
of formal gauge transformations. Thus, under the usual changes

{}^{g}\mathbb{A}=g^{-1}\mathbb{A}g+g^{-1}d_{\mathbb{M}}g,\text{ \qquad\qquad}^{g}\Phi=g^{-1}\Phi g

(2.44)

and

{}^{g}\Phi_{\text{on}}=g^{-1}\Phi_{\text{on}}g,

(2.45)

with
g:\mathbb{M}\rightarrow G
, i.e. an element of
\mathcal{G}
, the regularized 4d CS theory action transforms as follows

S\big(\!\,^{g}\mathbb{A}\big)_{\text{reg}}=S\left(\mathbb{A}\right)_{\text{reg}}+ic\mathop{\displaystyle\int}\nolimits_{\mathbb{M}}d_{\mathbb{M}}\omega_{\zeta}\wedge\kappa\wedge\text{Tr}\big(\mathbb{J}W_{\text{reg}}\big)+ic\mathop{\displaystyle\int}\nolimits_{\mathbb{M}}\omega_{\zeta}\wedge\chi(g),

(2.46)

where
W_{\text{reg}}\in\Omega_{\mathbb{M}}^{0}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
is given by

W_{\text{reg}}=2\iota_{X}\mathbb{A}+\iota_{X}\mathbb{J},

(2.47)

\mathbb{J}=d_{\mathbb{M}}gg^{-1}\in\Omega_{\mathbb{M}}^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
and
\chi(g)\in\Omega_{\mathbb{M}}^{3}
is the Wess-Zumino (WZ) 3-form given by

\chi(g)=-\frac{1}{3}\text{Tr}(\mathbb{J}\wedge\mathbb{J}\wedge\mathbb{J}).

(2.48)

In showing (
2.46
), one is to use the identity

CS(^{g}\mathbb{A})=CS(\mathbb{A})+d_{\mathbb{M}}\text{Tr}(\mathbb{A}\wedge\mathbb{J})+\chi(g)

(2.49)

and the trivial 5-form contraction

0=\iota_{X}\Big(d_{\mathbb{M}}\omega_{\zeta}\wedge\kappa\wedge\text{Tr}\big(\mathbb{A}\wedge\mathbb{J}\big)\Big),

(2.50)

in order to write

d_{\mathbb{M}}\omega_{\zeta}\wedge\text{Tr}\big(\mathbb{A}\wedge\mathbb{J}\big)=d_{\mathbb{M}}\omega_{\zeta}\wedge\kappa\wedge\text{Tr}\big(\mathbb{J}\iota_{X}\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{A}\iota_{X}\mathbb{J}\big).

(2.51)

For the extended and dual actions we find, respectively, that

\displaystyle S\big(\!\,^{g}\mathbb{A},\!\,^{g}\Phi\big)_{\text{ext}}

\displaystyle=S\big(\mathbb{A},\Phi\big)_{\text{ext}}+ic\mathop{\displaystyle\int}\nolimits_{\mathbb{M}}d_{\mathbb{M}}\omega_{\zeta}\wedge\kappa\wedge\text{Tr}\big(\mathbb{J}W_{\text{ext}}\big)+ic\mathop{\displaystyle\int}\nolimits_{\mathbb{M}}\omega_{\zeta}\wedge\chi(g),

(2.52)

\displaystyle S\big(\!\,^{g}\mathbb{A}\big)_{\text{dual}}

\displaystyle=S\big(\mathbb{A}\big)_{\text{dual}}+ic\mathop{\displaystyle\int}\nolimits_{\mathbb{M}}d_{\mathbb{M}}\omega_{\zeta}\wedge\kappa\wedge\text{Tr}\big(\mathbb{J}W_{\text{reg}}\big)+ic\mathop{\displaystyle\int}\nolimits_{\mathbb{M}}\omega_{\zeta}\wedge\chi(g),

where

W_{\text{ext}}=W_{\text{reg}}-2\Phi.

(2.53)

In all three cases we have the following general structure

{}^{g}S=S+ic\mathop{\displaystyle\int}\nolimits_{\mathbb{M}}d_{\mathbb{M}}\omega_{\zeta}\wedge\kappa\wedge\text{Tr}(\mathbb{J}W)+ic\mathop{\displaystyle\int}\nolimits_{\mathbb{M}}\omega_{\zeta}\wedge\chi(g),

(2.54)

for
W\in\Omega_{\mathbb{M}}^{0}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
. Below we will find, in a systematic way, what are the appropriate conditions to be imposed upon the gauge group elements
g\in\mathcal{G}
and the gauge field
\mathbb{A}
that turn any of the three action functionals introduced above genuinely gauge invariant. However, in order to show this, a detailed study of BC’s is required first. Thus, we will postpone the analysis of gauge invariance until this task is completed. For the time being, let us denote by
\mathcal{G}_{0}\subset\mathcal{G}
, the subgroup of the group of formal gauge transformations ensuring that
{}^{g}S=S
and refer to it simply as the gauge group, which is the true physical gauge symmetry group.

2.2
Defect data

Here we introduce, in the regularized 4d CS theory, classical (co)-adjont orbit insertions of the 1d CS theory type. They modify, as we shall see below in

2.2.1
1d CS theory

Let us pick

Recall that the Lie algebra

Denote by

4.2

There are two standard pole configurations of interest that we will consider in what follows and a sample of IFT’s examples associated to them. They appear as a consequence of the first equivariance condition (

\text{Re}\big[\text{Tr}(xx^{\prime})\big]=\text{Tr}_{\mathbb{R}}(x_{1}x_{1}^{\prime}-x_{2}x_{2}^{\prime}),\text{\qquad\qquad}\text{Im}\big[\text{Tr}(xx^{\prime})\big]=\text{Tr}_{\mathbb{R}}(x_{1}x_{2}^{\prime}+x_{2}x_{1}^{\prime}),

5.1.2
Inner product

In order to find a well-defined inner product on the symmetry algebra acting on
\overline{\mathcal{A}}
in a Hamiltonian way, we pursue the following strategy: first propose a symmetric bilinear form on
\mathfrak{h}
and then look for ad-invariance conditions while preserving its non-degeneracy. The outcome is that this can be done only on a subalgebra
\overline{\mathfrak{h}}\subset\mathfrak{h}
and as a consequence,
\mu
is dualizable only when we consider the restricted map
\mu:\overline{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow\overline{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
.

Define the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot):\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
on
\mathfrak{h}
, by the formula

\Big(\left(X,\eta,a\right),\left(Y,\lambda,b\right)\Big):=-\mathop{\displaystyle\int}\nolimits_{\mathbb{M}}\gamma_{\text{top}}\text{Tr}\left(\eta\lambda\right)-\beta(X)b-\beta(Y)a,

(5.37)

where
\beta\in\Omega_{\mathbb{M}}^{1}
is a 1-form on
\mathbb{M}
constructed out of a linear combination of
\Omega
and
\kappa
, i.e.
\beta=m\Omega+n\kappa
,
m,n\in\mathbb{R}
with the important condition that
\beta(X)\neq 0
and
\beta(Y)\neq 0
for generic
X,Y
. From the reality conditions
\lx@sectionsign\eqref{3}
, it is no difficult to show that if
\eta
and
\lambda
are equivariant Lie algebra valued functions and that if
a,b\in i\mathbb{R}
, then (
5.37
) is a purely imaginary number. In particular, the norm

i\Big(\left(X,\eta,a\right),\left(X,\eta,a\right)\Big):=-i\mathop{\displaystyle\int}\nolimits_{\mathbb{M}}\gamma_{\text{top}}\text{Tr}\left(\eta\lambda\right)-2i\beta(X)a

(5.38)

is real valued.

The first term on the rhs in (
5.37
) defines a metric-independent inner product on
\mathcal{G}_{\text{Lie}}
and the other contributions are responsible for its hyperbolic nature. The bilinear form (
5.37
) is ad-invariant under the action of
\mathfrak{h}
, if and only if, we have that

\Big(\Big[\left(X,\eta,a\right),\left(Y,\lambda,b\right)\Big],\left(Z,\phi,c\right)\Big)=\Big(\left(X,\eta,a\right),\Big[\left(Y,\lambda,b\right),\left(Z,\phi,c\right)\Big]\Big).

(5.39)

The latter condition being equivalent to

d(\eta,\lambda,Z)=d(\lambda,\phi,X),

(5.40)

where

d(\eta,\lambda,Z)=\beta(Z)c(\eta,\lambda)+\mathop{\displaystyle\int}\nolimits_{\mathbb{M}}\gamma_{\text{top}}\text{Tr}\left(\eta\pounds_{Z}\lambda\right).

(5.41)

We require (
5.40
) to vanish independently on both sides and interpret the vanishing of (
5.41
) as an equation imposing conditions upon the admissible vector fields and gauge parameters under which (
5.39
) holds. In order to extract a more tangible expression, we write the co-cycle (
5.23
) in the form

and set

8
Concluding remarks

By refining the results of
Yo
, we have constructed a regularized 4d CS theory that is compatible, not only with the properties of the usual 4d CS theory, but also with the dualization procedure that is necessary to deduce a localization formula. Some features of this construction are of a different nature when compared to the known results. For instance, in the 3d CS theory case on M
NA loc CS
;
Wilson NA loc
, the underlying Lie groups are real and compact, while in the 4d CS theory formulation on
\mathbb{R}\times\text{M}
, they are always complex. This introduce a fundamental difference in the localization formula, as it is constructed on a symplectic manifold that is also pseudo-Kähler. However, at least by judiciously applying the dualization recipe to the regularized theory, a formal localization integral reveals itself. Thus, if one is to make some progress based on this approach, a finer understanding of the localization mechanism on this kind of generalized non-compact symplectic manifolds is mandatory.

It is important to recall that for our approach to achieve full gauge invariance, it remains to be shown that the WZ term contribution,

, with M defined as in (

, including as well, the regularization procedure introduced in

necessary to handle non-simple poles.

We finish with some comments on complementary approaches that might be useful in exploring new relations between the 4d CS theory and other field theories:

\bullet
In

\bullet
In

, 3d CS theory on a manifold M of the form (

) and with a complex gauge group is considered. In that paper it is presented a complete non-perturbative evaluation of the path integral (the partition function and certain expectation values of Wilson loops). The approach used is based on the method of Abelianisation rather than on the method of NA localization and has the advantage of making explicit calculations more tractable. The method basically relies on fiber integration and results on an equivalent 2d field theory on

q

It would be interesting to see if this technique applies to the original/regularized 4d CS theory as well and under what conditions the partition function factorizes. It would also be equally interesting to identify, if any, the resulting lower dimensional theory produced in this approach that is, in principle, associated to any of the 2d IFT’s on
\mathfrak{p}\times\Sigma

All this suggest a quantum duality,

\{\text{2d IFT on $\mathfrak{p}\times\Sigma$}\}\quad\overset{?}{{\mathrel{\rightarrow}^{\hskip 42.67912pt}}}\quad\{\text{2d gauge theory on $C$}\}.

\bullet
In

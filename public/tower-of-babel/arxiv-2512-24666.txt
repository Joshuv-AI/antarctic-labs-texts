Collective behaviors of an electron gas in the mean-field regime


Abstract
. In this paper, we study the momentum distribution of an electron gas in a 3 3 -dimensional torus. The goal is to compute the occupation number of Fourier modes for some trial state obtained through random phase approximation. We obtain the mean-field analogue of momentum distribution formulas for electron gas in [Daniel and Voskov, Phys. Rev. 120 , (1960)] in high density limit and [Lam, Phys. Rev. 3 , (1971)] at metallic density. Our findings are related to recent results obtained independently by Benedikter, Lill and Naidu, and our analysis applies to a general class of singular potentials rather than just the Coulomb case.

Introduction and main result

1.1.
Introduction

We consider a system of
N
electron gas in the torus
\mathbb{T}^{3}=[0,2\pi]^{3}
in the mean field limit, whose Hamiltonian is given by (in the unit
\hbar=1
)

(1.1)

\displaystyle H_{N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}(-\Delta_{x_{j}})+k_{F}^{-1}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}V(x_{i}-x_{j})\quad\text{ on }\mathcal{H}_{N}:=L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3N}).

Here we denote
L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3N})
as the space of square-integrable, totally antisymmetric functions of
3N
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{3N}
. The standard Laplacian operator
-\Delta
describes the kinetic energy for each individual particle,
k_{F}
denotes the Fermi momentum, and
V
is given by the Comloub potential function

(1.2)

\displaystyle V(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\hat{V}_{k}e^{ik\cdot x},\quad\hat{V}_{k}:=\begin{cases}g|k|^{-2}\quad&\text{ for }k\neq 0,\\
0&\text{ for }k=0.\end{cases}

where
g\geq 0
is the coupling constant and
\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}:=\mathbb{Z}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
. Since
H_{N}
is bounded from below, we define
H_{N}
as a quadratic form by Friedrichs method with form domain
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
. The
ground state energy

E_{N}
is then given by

(1.3)

\displaystyle E_{N}

\displaystyle:=\inf\sigma(H_{N})=\inf_{\psi\in L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3N})}\frac{\langle\psi,H_{N}\psi\rangle}{\|\psi\|^{2}},

and any eigenvector of
H_{N}
with eigenvalue
E_{N}
is called a
ground state
.

In the non-interacting case (i.e.,
g=0
), the ground states
\Psi_{FS}
(which will be called
Fermi state
) are given by the Slater determinants comprising
N
plane waves with different momentum
k_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
for
j=1,...,N
of minimized kinetic energy
|k_{j}|^{2}
, i.e.,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\Psi_{FS}

\displaystyle=u_{k_{1}}\wedge...\wedge u_{k_{N}},\quad u_{p}(x)=(2\pi)^{-3/2}e^{-ip\cdot x}.

This state is unique up to a phase if we assume the Fermi ball
B_{F}
is completely filled with
N
integer points in the momentum space, i.e.,

(1.5)

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle=|B_{F}|,\quad\text{where }B_{F}=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\mid|k|\leq k_{F}\}\text{ for some }k_{F}>0.

This implies that the Fermi momentum
k_{F}
is scaled like

(1.6)

\displaystyle k_{F}

\displaystyle=\Big[\Big(\frac{3\pi}{4}\Big)^{1/3}+O(N^{-1/3})\Big]N^{1/3}.

We denote the complement of Fermi ball by
B_{F}^{c}:=\mathbb{Z}^{3}\setminus B_{F}
.

In the interacting case, the Slater determinant
\Psi_{FS}
is no longer a ground state for
H_{N}
. In the present case, we are interested in the collective correction induced by the interaction. This is to be compared with general quantum mechanical system in which linear combinations of Slater determinants are allowed.
In the work
[
9
,
11
]
, Christiansen, Hainzl and Nam estimated the ground state energy by: As
k_{F}\rightarrow\infty
, it holds that

(1.7)

\displaystyle E_{N}

\displaystyle=E_{FS}+E_{\rm corr,bos}+E_{\rm corr,ex}+O(k_{F}^{5/6+\epsilon})

for any
\epsilon>0
, where

(1.8)

\displaystyle E_{\rm corr,bos}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}F\big(Q_{k}(s)\big)ds,\quad F(x)=\log(1+x)-x,

is the energy contribution due to bosonization (i.e., electron-hole pair excitations) and

(1.9)

\displaystyle E_{\rm corr,ex}

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{F}^{-2}}{4(2\pi)^{6}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{p,q\in L_{k}}\frac{\hat{V}_{k}\hat{V}_{p+q-k}}{\lambda_{k,p}+\lambda_{k,q}},

is the exchange correlation (one should not confuse it with exchange energy in Hartree-Fock approximation), with
L_{k}
denotes the
lune
given by

(1.10)

\displaystyle L_{k}

\displaystyle:=B_{F}^{c}\cap(B_{F}+k)=\{p\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\mid|p-k|\leq k_{F}<|p|\}.

Here we denote

(1.11)

\displaystyle\lambda_{k,p}:=\frac{1}{2}\big(|p|^{2}

\displaystyle-|p-k|^{2}\big),\quad Q_{k}(s):=\frac{k_{F}^{-1}\hat{V}_{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{p\in L_{k}}\frac{\lambda_{k,p}}{s^{2}+\lambda_{k,p}^{2}},

and
E_{FS}
is the energy in Fermi state
\Psi_{FS}
that can be computed explicitly:

(1.12)

\displaystyle E_{FS}

\displaystyle=\langle\Psi_{FS},H_{N}\Psi_{FS}\rangle=\sum_{p\in B_{F}}|p|^{2}+\frac{1}{2(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\hat{V}_{k}(|L_{k}|-N).

We note the following important lower bound for
\lambda_{k,p}
: Denote for each
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
that (see
[
11
, Eq. (A.2)]
)

(1.13)

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\inf_{p\in B_{F}^{c}}|p|^{2}+\sup_{q\in B_{F}}|q|^{2}\Big),\quad m(p)^{-1}:=\big||p|^{2}-\kappa\big|\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Then, since
|p-k|^{2}\leq\kappa\leq|p|^{2}
for each
p\in L_{k}
, we have

(1.14)

\displaystyle\lambda_{k,p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\big(\big||p|^{2}-\kappa\big|+\big||p-k|^{2}-\kappa|\big|\big)\geq\frac{1}{2}\big(m(p)^{-1}+m(p-k)^{-1}\big)\geq\frac{1}{2}.

For convenience, we also introduce
L_{k}^{\prime}:=L_{k}-k
, the set of hole states for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}
. We remark that concerning the upper bound in (
1.7
), the analysis in
[
9
]
can be extended to any interaction potential with positive, square summable Fourier mode on
\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}
, with an error of order at most
O(\sqrt{k_{F}})
.

Remark 1
.

Note that for potential less singular than Coulomb potential, e.g., if
\hat{V}_{k}\leq O(|k|^{2+\epsilon})
for some
\epsilon>0
, then
E_{\rm corr,bos}
is of order
k_{F}
and
E_{\rm corr,ex}
is of order
o(k_{F})
. However, for Coulomb potential, then
E_{\rm corr,bos}
is of order
k_{F}\log(k_{F})
and
E_{\rm corr,ex}
is of order
k_{F}
; see
[
9
]
for a detailed explanation. Thus the Coulomb potential is critical, and the correlation energy
E_{\rm corr,bos}+E_{\rm corr,ex}
in (
1.7
) can be interpreted as the mean-field analogue of the Gell-Mann–Brueckner formula
c_{1}\rho\log(\rho)+c_{2}\rho
for jellium model in thermodynamic limit with the particle density
\rho
sufficiently high
[
14
]
. This is a refinement of the random phase approximation due to Bohm and Pines
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
21
]
.

Remark 2
.

For less singular positive interaction potentials, e.g.,
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}(1+|k|)\hat{V}_{k}<\infty
, one can show
\Psi_{FS}
remains a unique minimizer for Hartree-Fock approximation and a more precise leading order correlation energy (i.e., the difference between true ground state energy and the energy calculated by Hartree-Fock approximation) are obtained; see
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
10
]
and references therein.

In the present paper, we are interested in the properties of the ground states of the system. In this direction, it is a fundamental question in the condensed matter physics to understand if a
similar behavior also holds for true ground states, and it is expected each true ground state has a superconducting part that will smooth out the jump discontinuity as we move from inside to outside of Fermi ball (due to Kohn-Littinger theorem
[
19
,
20
]
). On the mathematicla side, the structure of a ground state is very delicate and its momentum distribution is thus difficult to determine. Inspired by the recent work
[
1
]
by Benediter and Lill, we will study the momentum distrubution for the trial state constructed in
[
9
]
.

1.2.
Trial states

Let us recall the trial state constructed in
[
9
]
, which gives the energy upper bound in (
1.7
). To this end, it is convenient to pass to the fermonic Fock space

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{a}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathcal{F}_{a}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})):=\mathbb{C}\oplus\bigoplus_{N=1}^{\infty}L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3N}).

Each element
\Psi\in\mathcal{F}_{a}
is given by a sequence
(\psi_{0},\psi_{1},...,\psi_{N},...)
with
\psi_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\psi_{N}\in L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3N})
for each
N\geq 1
. We denote the annihilation and creation operators on
\mathcal{F}_{a}
associated with
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
as
a(f)
and
a^{*}(f)
, respectively, satisfying the canoncial anticommutation relation (CAR): For any
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
,

(1.15)

\displaystyle\{a(f),a(g)\}=\{a^{*}(f),a^{*}(g)\}=0,\quad\{a(f),a^{*}(g)\}=\langle f,g\rangle.

For each plane wave
u_{p}=(2\pi)^{-3/2}e^{ip\cdot x}
with
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, we denote

(1.16)

\displaystyle a_{p}

\displaystyle=a(u_{p})\text{ and }a_{p}^{*}=a^{*}(u_{p}).

Next, for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}
and
p\in L_{k}
, we define the
excitation operator

b_{k,p},b_{k,p}^{*}
as

(1.17)

\displaystyle b_{k,p}

\displaystyle=a_{p-k}^{*}a_{p},\quad b_{k,p}^{*}=a_{p}^{*}a_{p-k},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3},p\in L_{k}.

The name is due to the fact that
b_{k,p}^{*}
acts on
\mathcal{F}_{a}
by creating a state with momentum
k\in B_{F}^{c}
and destroying a state with momentum
p-k\in B_{F}
. In other words, it excits the state
p-k\in B_{F}
to the state
k\in B_{F}^{c}
.

Note that the excitation operators behave “
quasi-bosonically
” in the sense that, for each
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}
and
p\in L_{k},q\in L_{l}
, they satisfy the following commutation relation:

(1.18)

\displaystyle[b_{k,p},b_{l,q}]

\displaystyle=[b_{k,p}^{*},b_{l,q}^{*}]=0,

(1.19)

\displaystyle[b_{k,p},b_{l,q}^{*}]

\displaystyle=\delta_{k,p}\delta_{p,q}-\big(\delta_{p,q}a_{q-l}a_{p-k}^{*}+\delta_{p-k,q-l}a_{q}^{*}a_{p}\big),

i.e.,
b_{k,p}^{*},b_{k,p}
is exactly bosonic if the terms in the bracket on the r.h.s. of (
1.19
) vanish. In our quasi-bosonic setting, it becomes non-trivial and gives exchange contribution
E_{\rm corr,ex}
to the correlaton energy (
1.7
).

For computational convenience, it is better to introduce a basis-independent way of writing quasi-bosonic operators. For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}
, we define an auxilliary vector space
\ell^{2}(L_{k})
, which we will consider as a real vector space with the standard orthonormal basis
(e_{p})_{p\in L_{k}}
. Then, for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}
and
\varphi\in\ell^{2}(L_{k})
, we define the
generalized excitation operators
by

(1.20)

\displaystyle b_{k}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in L_{k}}\langle\varphi,e_{p}\rangle b_{k,p},\quad b_{k}^{*}(\varphi)=\sum_{p\in L_{k}}\langle e_{p},\varphi\rangle b_{k,p}^{*}.

Now, we construct our trial state according to
[
9
]
as

(1.21)

\displaystyle\Psi_{N}

\displaystyle=e^{-\mathcal{K}}\Psi_{FS},

where
\mathcal{K}
is the
quasi-Bogoliubov kernel
on
\mathcal{H}_{N}
defined as

(1.22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{p\in L_{k}}\big(b_{k}(K_{k}e_{p})b_{-k,-p}-b_{-k,-p}^{*}b_{k}^{*}(K_{k}e_{p})\big),

with the associated family of symmetric operators
K_{k}:\ell^{2}(L_{k})\rightarrow\ell^{2}(L_{k})
given explicitly as

(1.23)

\displaystyle K_{k}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\log[h_{k}^{-1/2}\Big(h_{k}^{1/2}(h_{k}+2P_{k})h_{k}^{1/2}\Big)^{1/2}h_{k}^{-1/2}\Big].

Here, for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}
, the operator
h_{k}:\ell^{2}(L_{k})\rightarrow\ell^{2}(L_{k})
is defined by the relation
h_{k}e_{p}=\lambda_{k,p}e_{p}
and

(1.24)

\displaystyle P_{k}

\displaystyle=\ket{v_{k}}\bra{v_{k}},\quad v_{k}=\sqrt{\frac{k_{F}^{-1}\hat{V}_{k}}{2(2\pi)^{3}}}\sum_{p\in L_{k}}e_{p}\in\ell^{2}(L_{k}).

Note that, by spectral theory,
K_{k}\leq 0
for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}
and, for each
p,q\in\ell^{2}(L_{k})
, we have

(1.25)

\displaystyle\langle e_{p},K_{k}e_{q}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle e_{-p},K_{-k}e_{q}\rangle,\quad\langle e_{p},P_{k}e_{q}\rangle=\langle e_{p},v_{k}\rangle\langle v_{k},e_{q}\rangle=\frac{k_{F}^{-1}\hat{V}_{k}}{2(2\pi)^{3}}.

One can check easily that
\Psi_{N}\in\mathcal{H}_{N}
for each
N
, and
\mathcal{K}
is anti-symmetric, so that
e^{-\mathcal{K}}
is a unitary operator on
\mathcal{F}_{a}
.

1.3.
Main result

In the present work, we want to compute the following quantity for each
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
:

(1.26)

\displaystyle n(\xi):=\langle\Psi_{N},\rho_{\xi}\Psi_{N}\rangle,

for trial state
\Psi_{N}
defined in (
1.21
), where

(1.27)

\displaystyle\rho_{\xi}

\displaystyle:=\chi_{B_{F}}(\xi)a_{\xi}a_{\xi}^{*}+\chi_{B_{F}^{c}}(\xi)a_{\xi}^{*}a_{\xi}.

For
\xi\in B_{F}^{c}
, the quantity (
1.34
) represents the probability of finding a electron with momentum
\xi
, whereas, for
\xi\in B_{F}
, it represents the probability of finding a hole with this momentum. The quantity
n(\xi)
can also be interpreted as the momentum deviation in state
\Psi_{N}
from sharp distribution
\chi_{B_{F}}(\xi)
.

We also consider general collective behaviors of our trial state in momentum space by computing, for a given symmetric function
f\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{3})
,

(1.28)

\displaystyle n(f)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}f(\xi)n(\xi)\equiv\langle\Psi_{N},\rho(f)\Psi_{N}\rangle,

where

(1.29)

\displaystyle\rho(f)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}f(\xi)\rho_{\xi}=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}f(\xi)\big(\chi_{B_{F}}(\xi)a_{\xi}a_{\xi}^{*}+\chi_{B_{F}^{c}}(\xi)a_{\xi}^{*}a_{\xi}\big).

It follows that
n(\xi)
is simply given by (
1.28
) when
f
is the delta function at point
\xi
.

Remark 3
.

By introducing the
particle-hole transformation

\mathcal{R}
on
\mathcal{F}_{a}
as in
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
15
]

(1.30)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{*}a_{\xi}\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=\chi_{B_{F}}(\xi)a_{\xi}^{*}+\chi_{B_{F}^{c}}(\xi)a_{\xi},

which is unitary on
\mathcal{F}_{a}
and satisfies
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}^{*}=\mathcal{R}^{-1}
, we can rewrite
n(f)
as

(1.31)

\displaystyle n(f)

\displaystyle=\langle\Phi_{N},d\Gamma(f)\Phi_{N}\rangle,\quad\Phi_{N}\equiv\mathcal{R}\Psi_{N},

where
d\Gamma(f):=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}f(\xi)a_{\xi}^{*}a_{\xi}
is the second quantization of one-body observable
f
on momentum space
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{3})
. By the method in
[
3
, Lemma 4.3]
, one can show
\Phi_{N}\in\mathcal{H}_{N}
and, by passing back from
\mathcal{F}_{a}
to
\mathcal{H}_{N}
, we have

(1.32)

\displaystyle n(f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\xi_{1},...,\xi_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3N}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}f(\xi_{j})\Big)|\Phi_{N}(\xi_{1},...,\xi_{N})|^{2}.

Hence, we can interpret
n(f)
as the expectation of observable
f
on momentum space in the transformed trial state
\Phi_{N}
.

Now, we have our main result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
N=|B_{F}|=4\pi k_{F}^{3}/3
and suppose
V
satisfies

(1.33)

\displaystyle 0\leq\hat{V}_{k}=\hat{V}_{-k},\quad\hat{V}_{0}=0,\quad\|\hat{V}\|_{\ell^{2}}<\infty.

Then, for each observable
f\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{3})
such that
f(-\xi)=f(\xi)
, the expectation
n(f)
in the trial state
\Psi_{N}
constructed in (
1.21
) is given

(1.34)

\displaystyle n(f)

\displaystyle=n_{\rm b}(f)+n_{\rm ex}(f)+\mathcal{E}(f)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}f(\xi)\big(n_{\rm b}(\xi)+n_{\rm ex}(\xi)+\mathcal{E}(\xi)\big),

where we have the bosonization contribution

(1.35)

\displaystyle n_{\rm b}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{k_{F}^{-1}}{8\pi^{4}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{\zeta\in\mathcal{D}_{k,\xi}}\chi_{L_{k}}(\zeta)\hat{V}_{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(s^{2}-\lambda_{k,\zeta}^{2})(s^{2}+\lambda_{k,\zeta}^{2})^{-2}}{1+Q_{k}(s)}ds,

the exchange contribution

(1.36)

\displaystyle n_{\rm ex}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=-\frac{k_{F}^{-2}}{8(2\pi)^{6}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{\zeta\in\mathcal{D}_{k,\xi}}\sum_{p\in L_{k}}\chi_{L_{k}}(\zeta)\frac{\hat{V}_{k}\hat{V}_{p+\zeta-k}}{(\lambda_{k,p}+\lambda_{k,\zeta})^{2}},

and some error term
\mathcal{E}(\xi)
. In above,
\mathcal{D}_{k,\xi}:=\{\pm\xi,k\pm\xi\}
and
\chi_{A}
denotes the characteristic function of set
A
.

Moreover, for each
f\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{3})
with support
A
and any
\delta>0
, it holds that

(1.37)

\displaystyle|n_{\rm b}(f)|\leq C_{V}k_{F}^{-1}\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\sum_{\xi\in A}m(\xi),

for some constant
C_{V}>0
depends only on
\|\hat{V}\|_{\ell^{2}}
, and

(1.38)

\displaystyle|n_{\rm ex}(f)|\leq C_{\delta,V}k_{F}^{-1+\delta}\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\sum_{\xi\in A}m(\xi),

(1.39)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}(f)|\leq C_{\delta,V}k_{F}^{-3/2+\delta}\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\sum_{\xi\in A}m(\xi),

for some constant
C_{\delta,V}>0
, depends only on
\delta
and
\|\hat{V}\|_{\ell^{2}}
.

The rest of this paper is devoted to prove this theorem. We will first use techniques in
[
9
]
to extract contributions due to bosonization and exchange correlation, together with some additional error terms. We then estimate these errors in Sections
4
–
5
. Finally, in Section
6
, we will use these estimations to complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Even though we consider trial states instead of ground states, our result is interesting for the following reason. It gives the rigorous derivation of collective behaviors for electron gas in the mean field regime in terms of momentum distribution and extract the contributions due to bosonization and exchange correlation. Moreover, the formula is generic in the sense that our error term does not depend on specific structures of Coulomb interaction and is meaningful for a very general class of interactions satisfying (
1.33
).

Furthermore, we can regard (
1.34
) as the exact mean-field analogue to the formula obtained in the works
[
12
]
by Daniel and Voskov for electron gas in high density limit and
[
16
]
by Lam for the same system at metallic densities. In Appendix
A
, we compare our result with formulas in
[
12
,
16
]
.

Remark 4
.

Although Coulomb interaction is of the most physical significance, our analysis extends to a more general class of interactions satisfying (
1.33
). If the potential is more regular, then it is possible to improve the bound on errors. If, for instance, the Fourier mode of interaction potential is compactly supported, we refer to
[
1
]
and mention our first term in (
1.34
) corresponds exactly to the result in
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Remark 5
.

During the completion of this work, we learned that a similar result was recently derived by Benedikter, Lill and Naidu in
[
2
]
, and these two works are conducted independently and in parallel.

For comparison, in
[
2
]
, the authors computed momentum distribution at single point as in (
1.26
), while in the present work, we consider a more general form (
1.31
), computing expectation value of observable
f
. In the case of
f\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{3})
, results in
[
2
]
and ours are equivalent to each other in the sense that
[
2
]
considered the special case of our result in (
1.28
) with
f
is taken to be the delta function at single point
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, while, by taking summation as in (
1.31
), one can obtain a similar formula as in (
1.28
) from results in
[
2
]
.

Moreover, concerning error estimates,
[
2
]
presented two types of estimates, one considering a class of potentials as singular as Coulomb interactions while the other considering a class of more regular potentials (e.g.
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\hat{V}_{k}<\infty
). For the former class, they obtained an error estimate
O(k_{F}^{-7/6+\delta})
for any
\delta>0
, whereas the error estimate is much stronger and is of order
O(k_{F}^{-2+\delta})
for the latter class. In the present work, we consider a more general class of singular potentials satisfying (
1.33
) (including Coulomb interactions) and obtain a stronger error estimate, of order
O(k_{F}^{-3/2+\delta})
, without referencing specific structure of
\hat{V}
other than its
\ell^{2}
-norm. The method in the present work leads to the same upper bound for error terms when
\hat{V}
is more regular so we omit this case and refer to
[
2
]
for details in this direction.

Remark 6
.

Consider Coulomb interaction
\hat{V}_{k}=g|k|^{-2}
for a constant
g>0
and
f(\xi)=\chi_{B_{F}}(\xi)
. Then the quantity

\displaystyle n(f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi\in B_{F}}n(\xi)=\sum_{\xi\in B_{F}}\langle\Psi_{N},\rho_{\xi}\Psi_{N}\rangle

measures the number of excited particle-hole pairs. We argue formally that our main result implies

(1.40)

\displaystyle n_{\rm b}(f)\sim O(1),\quad n_{\rm ex}(f)\sim O(1),\quad\big|\mathcal{E}(f)\big|\leq C_{\delta,V}k_{F}^{-1/2+\delta}.

for some constant
C_{\delta,V}
depends only on
\delta
and
\|\hat{V}\|_{\ell^{2}}
. For the error term, according to
[
11
, Lemma 3.2]
, we obtain immediately that
\sum_{\xi\in B_{F}}m(\xi)\leq C_{\delta}k_{F}^{1+\delta}
for each
\delta>0
, which implies that

(1.41)

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{E}(f)\big|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\delta,V}k_{F}^{-3/2+\delta}\sum_{\xi\in B_{F}}m(\xi)\leq C_{\delta,V}k_{F}^{-1/2+\delta}.

For the exchange term, by shifting
\xi\mapsto k+\xi=:q
for each fixed
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}
, we obtain

(1.42)

\displaystyle n_{\rm ex}(f)

\displaystyle=-\frac{k_{F}^{-2}}{4(2\pi)^{6}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{p,q\in L_{k}}\frac{\hat{V}_{k}\hat{V}_{p+q-k}}{(\lambda_{k,p}+\lambda_{k,q})^{2}}.

We observe that this is in the same form as the exchange correlation
E_{\rm corr,ex}
in (
1.9
), except an additional factor
(\lambda_{k,p}+\lambda_{k,q})^{-1}
in each summand. Using the approximation
\lambda_{k,p}\sim|k|\max\{|k|,k_{F}\}
(in an average sense) and
|L_{k}|\sim k_{F}^{2}\min\{|k|,k_{F}\}
, it was argued in
[
9
, Remark 1]
that
E_{\rm corr,ex}\sim O(k_{F})
. The same approxiamtion yields
n_{\rm ex}(f)\sim O(1)
. For bosonization contribution, from the expansion

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+x}\approx 1-x+O(x^{2})_{x\rightarrow 0},

we obtain, using the above approximation,

\displaystyle n_{\rm b}(f)

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{F}^{-1}}{8\pi^{4}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{q\in L_{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(s^{2}-\lambda_{k,q}^{2})(s^{2}+\lambda_{k,q}^{2})^{-2}}{1+Q_{k}(s)}ds

\displaystyle\approx\frac{k_{F}^{-1}}{8\pi^{4}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{q\in L_{k}}\hat{V}_{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{2}-\lambda_{k,q}^{2}}{(s^{2}+\lambda_{k,q}^{2})^{2}}\Big[1-\frac{k_{F}^{-1}\hat{V}_{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{p\in L_{k}}\frac{\lambda_{k,p}}{s^{2}+\lambda_{k,p}^{2}}\Big]ds

(1.43)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}}{8(2\pi)^{7}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}(k_{F}^{-1}\hat{V}_{k})^{2}\sum_{p,q\in L_{k}}\frac{1}{(\lambda_{k,p}+\lambda_{k,q})^{2}}\sim O(1).

Hence, we obtain the asymptotic behavior
n_{\rm b}(f)\sim O(1)
. We observe that, for general singular potentials satisfying (
1.33
), when
f=\chi_{B_{F}}
, the above formal argument shows that contributions from bosonization and exchange correlation are at least of order
O(1)
, whereas the error term always of order
O(k_{F}^{-1/2+\delta})
. Hence, Theorem
1.1
always yields a meaningful result.

Finally, we emphasize again the above estimates are purely formal, and it is not clear how to improve the upper bound for exchange term claimed in our main result under the general condition (
1.33
) (and finding a rigorous, non-trivial lower bound on the exchange term for this general class of potentials remains an interesting open problem).

Remark 7
.

The lower bound
\lambda_{k,p}\geq 1/2
(or equivalently, the gap between lattice points inside and outside of Fermi ball is of order
O(1)
) is crucial in our analysis. This breaks down in thermodynamic limit. More precisely, by replacing the underlying configuration space
[0,1]^{3}\rightarrow[0,L]^{3}
, the corresponding momentum space
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
becomes
L^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{3}
and thus
\lambda_{k,p}\sim O(L^{-1})
for each
p\in L_{k}
, which vanishes in thermodynamic limit as
L\rightarrow\infty
. As we will see in the proof, this leads to divergences in our estimates for error terms.

Strategy of proof
. We will begin by computing the momentum distribution
n(\xi)
in our trial state
\Psi_{N}
using methods from
[
9
]
, which outputs the contributions from bosonization and exchange correlations, together with some error terms. We then show the error terms can be bounded by products of
k_{F}^{-1}
and the following quantity

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}:=\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq 1}\|\tilde{a}_{\xi}\Phi_{\tau}\|,\quad\text{ where }\Phi_{\tau}=e^{-\tau\mathcal{K}}\Psi_{FS}.

Clearly, this quantity is of order
O(1)
. Nevertheless, by computing
\|\tilde{a}_{\xi}\Phi_{\tau}\|^{2}
for each fixed
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
and
0\leq\tau\leq 1
through Bogoliubov transformation, one can show it is bounded by
k_{F}^{-1}
and
\mathcal{Q}
again, which, by a bootstrap argument as in
[
1
]
, implies that
\mathcal{Q}
is of order
O(k_{F}^{-1/2+\delta})
for each
\delta>0
. One then can use this new bound of
\mathcal{Q}
to improve bounds for the error terms to obtain our claim result.

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\frac{\chi_{L_{k}}(\xi)}{\lambda_{k,\xi}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\delta}k_{F}^{1+\delta},\quad\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\frac{\chi_{L_{k}^{\prime}}(\xi)}{\lambda_{k,k+\xi}^{2}}\leq C_{\delta}k_{F}^{1+\delta}m(\xi),

for some constant
C_{\delta}
depends only on
\delta
and is independent of
\xi
.

Proof.

First, for
\xi\in B_{F}^{c}
, the second relation in (
2.35
) vanishes so we only need to consider the first one. We denote the region
D_{\xi}:=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}\mid\xi\in L_{k}\}
. We observe that
D_{\xi}\subseteq\overline{B(\xi,k_{F})}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, where
B_{R}(a)
denotes the open ball centered at
a
with radius
R>0
. Clearly,
|D_{\xi}|\leq|\overline{B}(0,2k_{F})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}|
. Hence, by
[
11
, Lemma 3.2]
and lower bound (
1.14
), for any
\delta>0
, there is some
C_{\delta}>0
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\frac{\chi_{L_{k}}(\xi)}{\lambda_{k,\xi}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in D_{\xi}}\frac{2}{\big||\xi-k|^{2}-\kappa\big|}\leq\sum_{p\in B_{k_{F}}(0)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{2}{\big||p|^{2}-\kappa\big|}\leq C_{\delta}k_{F}^{1+\delta},

for some constant
C_{\delta}
depends only on
\delta
and is independent of
\xi
.

Next, for
\xi\in B_{F}
, again, the first relation in (
2.35
) vanishes so we only need to consider the second one. In this case, we define the region
D_{\xi}^{\prime}:=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}\mid\xi\in L_{k}^{\prime}\}
. We observe that
|k+\xi|>k_{F}
if
k\in D_{\xi}^{\prime}
, and decompose
D_{\xi}^{\prime}:=D_{\xi,1}^{\prime}\cup D_{\xi,2}^{\prime}
as

\displaystyle D_{\xi,1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=\{k\in D_{\xi}^{\prime}\mid|k+\xi|\leq 2k_{F}\},\quad D_{\xi,2}^{\prime}:=D_{\xi}^{\prime}\setminus D_{\xi,1}^{\prime}.

We observe that
D_{\xi,1}^{\prime}\subseteq\overline{B(\xi,2k_{F})}\cap B(\xi,k_{F})^{c}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}
so that

(2.36)

\displaystyle|D_{\xi,1}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq C|\overline{B(0,2k_{F})}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}|,

for some universal constant
C>0
. We further decompose
D_{\xi,1}^{\prime}=\cup_{\ell=1}^{L}D_{\xi,1,n}^{\prime}
for some integer
C\leq L\leq C+1
such that
|D_{\xi,1,\ell}^{\prime}|\leq|\overline{B(0,2k_{F})}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}|
for each
n
so that, by
[
11
, Lemma 3.2]
and lower bound (
1.14
),

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in D_{\xi,1}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k,k+\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq m(\xi)\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\sum_{k\in D_{\xi,1,\ell}^{\prime}}\frac{2}{\big||k+\xi|^{2}-\kappa\big|}

\displaystyle\leq m(\xi)\sum_{p\in B_{k_{F}}(0)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{2L}{\big||p|^{2}-\kappa\big|}\leq(C+1)C_{\delta}k_{F}^{1+\delta}m(\xi).

For region
D_{\xi,2}^{\prime}
, since
\kappa=k_{F}^{2}(1+o(1))
and
|k+\xi|^{2}\geq 4k_{F}^{2}
for any
k\in D_{\xi,2}^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\big||k+\xi|^{2}-\kappa\big|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}|k+\xi|^{2}-o(1).

Moreover, we observe that, for each
\xi\in B_{F}
, there is some universal constant
C^{\prime}>0
such that

(2.37)

\displaystyle m(\xi)^{-1}=\big||\xi|^{2}-\kappa\big|\leq|\xi|^{2}+\kappa\leq C^{\prime}k_{F}^{2}(1+o(1)).

Then we bound the sum over region
D_{\xi,2}^{\prime}
with the corresponding integral (up to some constant
C^{\prime\prime}>0
) to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in D_{\xi,2}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k,k+\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in D_{\xi,2}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\big||k+\xi|^{2}-\kappa\big|^{2}}\leq C^{\prime\prime}\int_{k_{F}}^{\infty}\frac{p^{2}dp}{(p^{2}-o(1))^{2}}

(2.38)

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime\prime}k_{F}^{-1}\leq C^{\prime}C^{\prime\prime}m(\xi)\big(m(\xi)^{-1}k_{F}^{-1}\big)\leq C^{\prime}C^{\prime\prime}k_{F}(1+o(1))m(\xi).

Consequently, we obtain for any
\delta>0
that

(2.39)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\frac{\chi_{L_{k}^{\prime}}(\xi)}{\lambda_{k,k+\xi}^{2}}\leq C_{\delta}k_{F}^{1+\delta}m(\xi),

for some constant
C_{\delta}
again depends only on
\delta
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Momentum distribution: Bosonization and exchange contributions

In this section, we compute the momentum distribution
n(\xi)
of our trial state and extract the contribution due to bosonization and exchange correlations. First, instead of only
n(\xi)
, we consider the family

(3.1)

\displaystyle n_{t}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\langle\Phi_{t},\rho_{\xi}\Phi_{t}\rangle,\quad\Phi_{t}:=e^{-t\mathcal{K}}\Psi_{FS}\text{ for }0\leq t\leq 1,

so that
n(\xi)\equiv n_{1}(\xi)
. Moreover, due to the reflection symmetry of trial state
\Phi_{t}
for each
0\leq t\leq 1
in the momentum space,

(3.2)

\displaystyle n_{t}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\langle\Phi_{t},\rho_{\xi}\Phi_{t}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\langle\Phi_{t},(\rho_{\xi}+\rho_{-\xi})\Phi_{t}\rangle.

This symmetry condition is important when we compute momentum distribution (see Remark
8
for explanation).

Next, we compute the occupation number of a single excitation mode:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}
and
p\in L_{k}
. Then we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[b_{k,p},\rho_{\xi}+\rho_{-\xi}]=(g_{\xi,k}(p)+g_{-\xi,k}(p))b_{k,p},

where

(3.4)

\displaystyle g_{\xi,k}(p)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\big(\chi_{L_{k}}(\xi)\delta_{p,q}+\chi_{L_{k}^{\prime}}(\xi)\delta_{p,k+\xi}\big).

Proof.

This is proven by direct application of CAR.

∎

This lemma allows us to compute the commutator
[\mathcal{K},\rho_{\xi}]
:

Proposition 3.1
.

For each
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, we obtain

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[\mathcal{K},\rho_{\xi}+\rho_{-\xi}]=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}Q_{2}^{k}(\Theta_{\xi;k}),\quad\Theta_{\xi;k}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\zeta\in\mathcal{D}_{k,\xi}}\chi_{L_{k}}(\zeta)\{P_{\zeta;k},K_{k}\}

where
\mathcal{D}_{k,\xi}:=\{\pm\xi,k\pm\xi\}
and
P_{\zeta;k}:=\ket{e_{\zeta}}\bra{e_{\zeta}}
denotes the rank-1 projection onto the vector
e_{\zeta}
on
\ell^{2}(L_{k})
, provided
\zeta\in L_{k}
. Moreover,
\Theta_{\xi;k}
is a symmetric operator on
\ell^{2}(L_{k})
and satisfies the property

(3.6)

\displaystyle I_{k}\Theta_{\xi;k}

\displaystyle=\Theta_{\xi;-k}I_{k}\quad\text{ for each }k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}.

Proof.

Using Lemma
3.1
, we compute

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[\mathcal{K},\rho_{\xi}+\rho_{-\xi}]=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{p,q\in L_{k}}\langle e_{p},K_{k}e_{q}\rangle\Big([b_{k,p}b_{-k,-q},\rho_{\xi}+\rho_{-\xi}]-[b_{-k,-q}^{*}b_{k,p}^{*},\rho_{\xi}+\rho_{-\xi}]\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Re}\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\Big(\chi_{L_{k}}(\xi)b_{-k}^{*}(K_{-k}e_{-\xi})b_{k,q}^{*}+\chi_{L_{k}^{\prime}}(\xi)b_{k}^{*}(K_{k}e_{k+\xi})b_{-k,-(k+\xi)}^{*}+(\xi\rightarrow-\xi)\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Re}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\Big(\chi_{L_{k}}(\xi)b_{-k}^{*}(I_{k}K_{k}e_{\xi})b_{k}^{*}(e_{\xi})+\chi_{L_{k}^{\prime}}(\xi)b_{k}^{*}(K_{k}e_{k+\xi})b_{-k}^{*}(I_{k}e_{k+\xi})\Big)+(\xi\rightarrow-\xi)

\displaystyle=:I_{\xi}+II_{\xi}+(\xi\rightarrow-\xi).

Next, we rewrite the above expression in a more convenient form using the following elementary lemma (which can be proven by orthonormal basis expansion):

Lemma 3.2

(
[
9
]
, Lemma A.1)
.

Let
(V,q)
be a
n
-dimenisonal Hilbert space and let
q:V\times V\rightarrow W
be a sesquilinear form into a vector space
W
. Let
(e_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
be an orthonormal basis for
V
. Then, for any linear operators
S,T:V\rightarrow V
, we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}q(Se_{i},Te_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}q(ST^{*}e_{i},e_{i}).

By this lemma and using the symmetry
k\rightarrow-k
, we see that

\displaystyle I_{\xi}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Re}\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{p\in L_{k}}b_{-k}^{*}(I_{k}K_{k}P_{\xi;k}e_{p})b_{k}^{*}(e_{p})\Big)=\mathrm{Re}\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{p\in L_{k}}b_{-k}^{*}(e_{-p})b_{k}^{*}(P_{\xi;k}K_{k}e_{p})\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\mathrm{Re}\Big(\sum_{p\in L_{k}}b_{-k}^{*}(e_{-p})b_{k}^{*}(P_{\xi;k}K_{k}e_{p})+\sum_{p\in L_{k}}b_{k}^{*}(e_{p})b_{-k}^{*}(I_{k}P_{-\xi;k}K_{k}e_{p})\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\mathrm{Re}\Big(\sum_{p\in L_{k}}b_{-k}^{*}(e_{-p})b_{k}^{*}(P_{\xi;k}K_{k}e_{p})+\sum_{p\in L_{k}}b_{k}^{*}(K_{k}P_{-\xi;k}e_{p})b_{-k}^{*}(e_{-p})\Big),

where we have used the elementary identities

\displaystyle P_{-\xi;k}

\displaystyle=P_{\xi;-k},\quad I_{k}P_{\xi;k}=\chi_{L_{k}}(\xi)\ket{e_{-\xi}}\bra{e_{\xi}}=P_{-\xi;-k}I_{k}.

Summing over
\xi
and
-\xi
yields

\displaystyle I_{\xi}+I_{-\xi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\mathrm{Re}\Big(\sum_{p\in L_{k}}b_{-k}^{*}(e_{-p})b_{k}^{*}(\{P_{\xi;k}+P_{-\xi;k},K_{k}\}e_{p})\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}Q_{2}^{k}(\{P_{\xi;k}+P_{-\xi;k},K_{k}\}).

The same argument above yields for term
II_{\xi}
that

\displaystyle II_{\xi}+II_{-\xi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}Q_{2}^{k}(\{P_{k-\xi;k}+P_{k+\xi;k},K_{k}\}),

thus proving (
3.5
).

To finish the proof, we denote
P_{\xi;k}^{\prime}:=P_{\xi;k}+P_{-\xi;k}
and
P_{\xi;k}^{\prime\prime}:=P_{k-\xi;k}+P_{k+\xi;k}
and then compute

\displaystyle I_{k}P_{\xi;k}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=I_{k}(P_{\xi;k}+P_{-\xi;k})=(P_{-\xi;-k}+P_{\xi,-k})I_{k}=P_{\xi,-k}^{\prime}I_{k},

and

\displaystyle I_{k}P_{\xi;k}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=I_{k}(P_{k-\xi;k}+P_{k+\xi;k})=(P_{-k+\xi,-k}+P_{-k-\xi,-k})I_{k}=P_{\xi,-k}^{\prime\prime}I_{k},

which implies the relation (
3.6
). This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 8
.

If our trial state is not invariant under reflection in momentum space, then we cannot average over
\pm\xi
in (
3.2
), and the above computation would output

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Q_{2}^{k}(P_{\xi;k}K_{k}+K_{k}P_{-\xi;k})\quad&\xi\in B_{F}^{c},\\
Q_{2}^{k}(P_{k+\xi;k}K_{k}+K_{k}P_{k-\xi;k})\quad&\xi\in B_{F},\end{cases}

for each

. The operators

are not symmetric and thus prevent us to apply techniques from
[
9
]
.

Recall that
\Phi_{t}=e^{-t\mathcal{K}}\Psi_{N}
for

so that

. By the fundamental theorem of calculus and by using the fact that
\langle\Psi_{FS},\rho_{\xi}\Psi_{FS}\rangle=0

\displaystyle n_{t}(\xi)=\langle\Phi_{t},\rho_{\xi}\Phi_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Psi_{FS},\rho_{\xi}\Psi_{FS}\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\Phi_{\tau},[\mathcal{K},\rho_{\xi}]\Phi_{\tau}\rangle d\tau

(3.8)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\Phi_{\tau},Q_{2}^{k}(\Theta_{\xi;k})\Phi_{\tau}\rangle d\tau.

Using Lemma
2.2
and the fact that
\langle\Psi_{FS},Q_{1}^{k}(T)\Psi_{FS}\rangle=\langle\Psi_{FS},Q_{2}^{k}(T)\Psi_{FS}\rangle=0
for any

and operators

on

, we obtain from (
3

(3.9)

\displaystyle n_{t}(\xi)

\displaystyle=n_{{\rm b},t}(\xi)+n_{{\rm ex},t}(\xi)+\mathcal{E}_{1,t}(\xi)+\mathcal{E}_{2,t}(\xi)+\mathcal{E}_{3,t}(\xi),

where

\displaystyle n_{{\rm b},t}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\int_{0}^{t}\Tr\big(\Theta_{\xi;k}^{2}(\tau)\big)d\tau,

\displaystyle n_{{\rm ex},t}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\mathrm{Re}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\tau}\big\langle\Psi_{FS},\mathcal{E}_{2,k}(\Theta_{\xi;k}^{1}(\tau_{1}))\Psi_{FS}\big\rangle d\tau_{1}d\tau,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1,t}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\mathrm{Re}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\tau}\Big\langle e^{-(\tau-\tau_{1})\mathcal{K}}\Psi_{FS},\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{2,k}(\Theta_{\xi;k}^{1}(\tau_{1}))e^{-(\tau-\tau_{1})\mathcal{K}}\Psi_{FS}\big\rangle d\tau_{1}d\tau,

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\mathrm{Re}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\tau}\Big\langle e^{-(\tau-\tau_{1})\mathcal{K}}\Psi_{FS},\mathcal{E}_{1,k}(\Theta_{\xi;k}^{2}(\tau))e^{-(\tau-\tau_{1})\mathcal{K}}\Psi_{FS}\big\rangle d\tau_{1}d\tau,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{3,t}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{t}\Big\langle e^{-(\tau-\tau_{1})\mathcal{K}}\Psi_{FS},\varepsilon_{k}(\{K_{k},\Theta_{\xi;k}^{1}(\tau)\})e^{-(\tau-\tau_{1})\mathcal{K}}\Psi_{FS}\big\rangle d\tau_{1}d\tau.

with
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{2,k}(T_{k}):=\mathcal{E}_{2,k}(T_{k})-\langle\Psi_{FS},\mathcal{E}_{2,k}(T_{k})\Psi_{FS}\rangle
.

3.1.
Momentum distribution from bosonization

This subsection is devoted to prove the following proposition (c.f.
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
):

Proposition 3.2
.

It holds that

(3.10)

\displaystyle n_{{\rm b},t}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{\zeta\in\mathcal{D}_{k,\xi}}\langle e_{\zeta},(\cosh(-2tK_{k})-1)e_{\zeta}\rangle.

Moreover,
0\leq n_{{\rm b},t}(\xi)\leq C_{V}k_{F}^{-1}m(\xi)
uniformly in
0\leq t\leq 1
and
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
.

Proof.

Using the cyclicity of trace, we have

\displaystyle\Tr\big(\Theta_{\xi;k}^{2}(t)\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\zeta\in\mathcal{D}_{k,\xi}}\Tr\big(S_{k}(t)(-K_{k})P_{\zeta;k}\big)=\sum_{\zeta\in\mathcal{D}_{k,\xi}}\chi_{L_{k}}(\zeta)\langle e_{\zeta},(-K_{k})S_{k}(t)e_{\zeta}\rangle,

and, by integrating over
t
on
[0,1]
and the symmetry property of
K_{k}
under
k\mapsto-k
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\int_{0}^{1}\Tr(\Theta_{\xi;k}^{2}(t))dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{\zeta\in\mathcal{D}_{k,\xi}}\langle e_{\zeta},C_{k}(2t)e_{\zeta}\rangle=:n_{{\rm b},t}(\xi),

where we recall
S_{k}(t)
and
C_{k}(t)
from (
2.11
). Note that
C_{k}(t)
is an increasing, positive operator-valued function on
0\leq t\leq 1
so that, by spectral theory,
[
9
, Corollary 3.3]
and the lower bound (
1.14
),

\displaystyle 0\leq n_{{\rm b},t}(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{\zeta\in\mathcal{D}_{k,\xi}}\langle e_{\zeta},\big(\cosh(-2K_{k})-1\big)e_{\zeta}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq Ck_{F}^{-1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\hat{V}_{k}^{2}\Big(\frac{\chi_{L_{k}}(\xi)}{\lambda_{k,\xi}}+\frac{\chi_{L_{k}^{\prime}}(\xi)}{\lambda_{k,k+\xi}}\Big)\leq C_{V}k_{F}^{-1}m(\xi).

This completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Momentum distribution from exchange correlation

Now, we extract information from exchange correlation by proving the following proposition:

Proposition 3.3
.

For each
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
and
0\leq t\leq 1
, it holds that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\Big|n_{{\rm ex},t}(\xi)-t^{2}n_{\rm ex}(\xi)\Big|\leq C_{V}(k_{F}^{-3/2}m(\xi)+k_{F}^{-2}m(\xi)),

where

(3.12)

\displaystyle n_{{\rm ex}}(\xi)

\displaystyle=-\frac{k_{F}^{-2}}{8(2\pi)^{6}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{\zeta\in\mathcal{D}_{k,\xi}}\sum_{p\in L_{k}}\frac{\hat{V}_{k}\hat{V}_{p+\zeta-k}}{(\lambda_{k,p}+\lambda_{k,\zeta})^{2}}.

Moreover, for each
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
,
|n_{\rm ex}(\xi)|\leq C_{\delta,V}k_{F}^{-1+\delta}m(\xi)
for any
\delta>0
.

To prove Proposition
3.3
, we need to first establish some technical results. Recall the definition, for any symmetric operator
T_{k}
on
\ell^{2}(L_{k})
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{2,k}(T_{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{p\in L_{k}}\sum_{q\in L_{l}}\{b_{k}(T_{k}e_{p}),\{\varepsilon_{-k,-l}(e_{-p};e_{-q}),b_{l}^{*}(K_{l}e_{q})\}\}

Since
b_{k}(T_{k})\Psi_{FS}=\varepsilon_{-k,-l}(e_{-p};e_{-q})\Psi_{FS}=0
for each
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}
and
p\in L_{k},q\in L_{l}
, for each

, we have

\displaystyle-2\big\langle\Psi_{FS},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{2,k}(T_{k})\Psi_{FS}\big\rangle=-\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{p\in L_{k}}\sum_{q\in L_{l}}\big\langle\Psi_{FS},b_{k}(T_{k}e_{p})\varepsilon_{-k,-l}(e_{-p};e_{-q})b_{l}^{*}(K_{l}e_{q})\Psi_{FS}\big\rangle

(3.13)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{p\in L_{k}\cap L_{l}}\big\langle\Psi_{FS},b_{k}(T_{k}e_{p})\tilde{a}_{-p+l}^{*}\tilde{a}_{-p+k}b_{l}^{*}(K_{l}e_{p})\Psi_{FS}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{p\in L_{k}^{\prime}\cap L_{l}^{\prime}}\big\langle\Psi_{FS},b_{k}(T_{k}e_{p+k})\tilde{a}_{-p-l}^{*}\tilde{a}_{-p-k}b_{l}^{*}(K_{l}e_{p+l})\Psi_{FS}\big\rangle,

where we recall
\tilde{a}_{p}^{*}
from (
2.22
). It follows that
\tilde{a}_{p}\Psi_{FS}=0
for each
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
under our assumption for
\Psi_{FS}
. Hence, in evaluating
\langle\Psi_{FS},A\Psi_{FS}\rangle
, it suffices to rewrite
A
in terms of
\tilde{a}_{p}^{\#}
’s, put it into normal order and drop all the terms with
\tilde{a}^{*}
on the most left and
\tilde{a}
on the most right.

For the first term in (
3.2
), since

, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{p\in L_{k}\cap L_{l}}\big\langle\Psi_{FS},b_{k}(T_{k}e_{p})\tilde{a}_{-p+l}^{*}\tilde{a}_{-p+k}b_{l}^{*}(K_{l}e_{p})\Psi_{FS}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{p\in L_{k}\cap L_{l}}\big\langle\Psi_{FS},\big[b_{k}(T_{k}e_{p}),\tilde{a}_{-p+l}^{*}\big]\big[b_{l}(K_{l}e_{p}),\tilde{a}_{-p+k}^{*}\big]^{*}\Psi_{FS}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{p\in L_{k}\cap L_{l}}\sum_{q\in L_{k}}\sum_{q^{\prime}\in L_{l}}\delta_{-p+l,q-k}\delta_{-p+k,q^{\prime}-l}\langle T_{k}e_{p},e_{q}\rangle\langle e_{q^{\prime}},K_{l}e_{p}\rangle\big\langle\Psi_{FS},\tilde{a}_{q}\tilde{a}_{q^{\prime}}^{*}\Psi_{FS}\big\rangle

(3.14)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{p,q\in L_{k}\cap L_{l}}\delta_{k+l,p+q}\langle T_{k}e_{p},e_{q}\rangle\langle e_{q},K_{l}e_{p}\rangle,

where we use the following identity (which is easily followed from CAR; see also
[
9
, Eq. (4.22)]
)

(3.15)

\displaystyle[b_{l}(\psi),\tilde{a}_{p}^{*}]

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-\chi_{L_{l}^{\prime}}(p)\langle\psi,e_{p+l}\rangle\tilde{a}_{p+l},\quad&p\in B_{F},\\
\chi_{L_{l}}(p)\langle\psi,e_{p}\rangle\tilde{a}_{p-l},\quad&p\in B_{F}^{c}.\end{cases}

Similarly, the second term in (
3.2
) gives

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{p\in L_{k}^{\prime}\cap L_{l}^{\prime}}\Big\langle b_{k}(T_{k}e_{p+k})\tilde{a}_{-p-l}^{*}\tilde{a}_{-p-k}b_{l}^{*}(K_{l}e_{p+l})\Psi_{FS}\big\rangle

(3.16)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{p,q\in L_{k}^{\prime}\cap L_{l}^{\prime}}\delta_{k+l,-(p+q)}\langle T_{k}e_{p+k},e_{q+k}\rangle\langle e_{q+k},K_{l}e_{p+k}\rangle.

By the same argument as in
[
9
, Subsection 4.3]
, it turns out these two terms are equal so that

(3.17)

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathcal{E}_{2,k}(T_{k})\Psi_{FS}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}}\sum_{p,q\in L_{k}\cap L_{l}}\delta_{k+l,p+q}\langle T_{k}e_{p},e_{q}\rangle\langle e_{q},(-K_{l})e_{p}\rangle.

Next, we note that, since
\mathcal{E}_{2,k}(T_{k})
depends linearly on
T_{k}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\delta,V}(k_{F}^{-1}+k_{F}^{-1+\delta}+k_{F}^{-1}\mathcal{Q}^{1-\delta}+k_{F}^{-1+\delta}\mathcal{Q}+k_{F}^{-3/2})m(\xi)

(6.2)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\delta,V}k_{F}^{-1+\delta}m(\xi).

Since

uniformly in
\xi
by its definition, we obtain

(6.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq 1}\|\tilde{a}_{\xi}\Phi_{\tau}\|\leq C_{\delta,V}k_{F}^{-1/2+\delta}.

This completes the proof.
∎

. Recall from Proposition

(6.8)

\displaystyle n_{\rm b}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(k,\zeta)\in\mathcal{C}_{\xi}}\langle e_{\zeta},(\cosh(-2K_{k})-1)e_{\zeta}\rangle.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle\cosh(-2K_{k})-1

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}h_{k}^{-1/2}\Big[\big(h_{k}^{2}+2P_{u_{k}}\big)^{1/2}-h_{k}\Big]h_{k}^{-1/2}

(6.9)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad+\frac{1}{2}h_{k}^{1/2}\Big[\big(h_{k}^{2}+2P_{u_{k}}\big)^{-1/2}-h_{k}^{-1}\Big]h_{k}^{1/2},

where
P_{u_{k}}=\ket{u_{k}}\bra{u_{k}}
with

\displaystyle\Big\langle e_{\zeta},h_{k}^{-1/2}\Big(h_{k}^{1/2}(h_{k}+2P_{k})h_{k}^{1/2}\Big)^{1/2}h_{k}^{-1/2}e_{\zeta}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\pi\lambda_{k,\zeta}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big\langle(h_{k}^{2}+s^{2})^{-1}e_{\zeta},\Big(\frac{s^{2}}{1+2\langle u_{k},(h_{k}^{2}+s^{2})^{-1}u_{k}\rangle}\Big)P_{u_{k}}(h_{k}^{2}+s^{2})^{-1}e_{\zeta}\Big\rangle ds

(6.10)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi}\frac{k_{F}^{-1}\hat{V}_{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{2}}{(\lambda_{k,\zeta}^{2}+s^{2})^{2}}\frac{1}{1+Q_{k}(s)}ds,

where we recall
Q_{k}(s)=\frac{k_{F}^{-1}\hat{V}_{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{p\in L_{k}}\frac{\lambda_{k,p}}{s^{2}+\lambda_{k,p}^{2}}
. Similarly, for the second term in (
6
), we use the identity from functional calculus to write

(6.11)

\displaystyle\big(h_{k}^{2}+2P_{u_{k}}\big)^{-1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{h_{k}^{2}+2P_{u_{k}}+s^{2}}ds.

The resolvent for matrix with rank-1 perturbation is easily calculated using Sherman-Morrison formula as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{k}^{2}+2P_{u_{k}}+s^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h_{k}^{2}+s^{2}}-2\frac{(h_{k}^{2}+s^{2})^{-1}P_{u_{k}}(h_{k}^{2}+s^{2})^{-1}}{1+2\langle u_{k},(h_{k}^{2}+s^{2})^{-1}u_{k}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h_{k}^{2}+s^{2}}-\frac{k_{F}^{-1}\hat{V}_{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{1+Q_{k}(s)}\sum_{p,q\in L_{k}}M_{pq}(s)\ket{e_{p}}\bra{e_{q}},

where
M_{pq}(s)=\frac{\lambda_{k,p}^{1/2}\lambda_{k,q}^{1/2}}{(s^{2}+\lambda_{k,p}^{2})(\lambda_{k,q}^{2}+s^{2})}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\big(h_{k}^{2}+2P_{u_{k}}\big)^{-1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[\frac{1}{h_{k}^{2}+s^{2}}-\frac{k_{F}^{-1}\hat{V}_{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{1+Q_{k}(s)}\sum_{p,q\in L_{k}}M_{pq}(s)\ket{e_{p}}\bra{e_{q}}\Big]ds

\displaystyle=h_{k}^{-1}-\frac{2}{\pi}\frac{k_{F}^{-1}\hat{V}_{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1+Q_{k}(s)}\sum_{p,q\in L_{k}}M_{pq}(s)\ket{e_{p}}\bra{e_{q}}ds.

Hence, the second term in (

\begin{split}\Big\langle e_{\zeta},h_{k}^{1/2}\Big(\frac{1}{h_{k}^{2}+2P_{u_{k}}}-h_{k}^{-1}\Big)h_{k}^{1/2}e_{\zeta}\Big\rangle&=-\frac{2\lambda_{k,\zeta}}{\pi}\frac{k_{F}^{-1}\hat{V}_{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1+Q_{k}(s)}M_{\zeta\zeta}(s)ds\\
&=-\frac{2\lambda_{k,\zeta}^{2}}{\pi}\frac{k_{F}^{-1}\hat{V}_{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1+Q_{k}(s)}\frac{1}{(\lambda_{k,\zeta}^{2}+s^{2})^{2}}ds.\end{split}

Putting everything together yields

(6.12)

\langle e_{\zeta},(\cosh(-2K_{k})-1)e_{\zeta}\rangle=\frac{2}{\pi}\frac{k_{F}^{-1}\hat{V}_{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(s^{2}-\lambda_{k,\zeta}^{2})(\lambda_{k,\xi}^{2}+s^{2})^{-2}}{1+Q_{k}(s)}ds,

which completes the proof.

Appendix A
Comparison with work by Daniel and Voskov

In the seminal work
[
12
]
by Daniel and Voskov, the momentum distribution of ground state in random phase approximation for electron gas in high density limit was obtained due to a Hellmann-Feynman type argument. By a suitable change of variables,
[
12
, Eqs. (8), (9)]
coincides exactly with
[
16
, Eq. (2.8)]
, which dealt with the same system at metallic densities.

In this section, at least formally, we compare our result with the momentum distribution obtained by Daniel and Voskov, given in thermodynamic limit followed high density limit. We shall focus on the case
\xi\in B_{F}^{c}
since the reverse case can be treated similarly. According to
[
12
, Eq. (8)]
, the momentum distribution due to bosonization for
\xi\in B_{F}^{c}
is given by (and we have adapted their formula in our notations)

\displaystyle n_{\rm b}^{\rm(DV)}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{F}\alpha}{|\xi|}\int_{|\xi|-k_{F}}^{|\xi|+k_{F}}d|k||k|\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[\frac{|\xi|-|k|/2}{\big(|\xi|-|k|/2\big)^{2}+s^{2}}-\frac{\frac{|\xi|^{2}-k_{F}^{2}}{2|k|}}{\Big(\frac{|\xi|^{2}-k_{F}^{2}}{2|k|}\Big)^{2}+s^{2}}\Big]

(A.1)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\Big[|k|^{2}+\alpha k_{F}^{2}Q_{k}^{\rm(DV)}(s)\Big]^{-1}ds,

where

\displaystyle Q_{k}^{\rm(DV)}(s)

\displaystyle=k_{F}^{-1}\int_{|p|<k_{F}}dp\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dte^{its|k|}\exp[-|t|\Big(p\cdot k+\frac{1}{2}|k|^{2}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=2\pi\Big[1+\frac{1}{2|k|k_{F}}\big(k_{F}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}|k|^{2}+s^{2}\big)\ln\Big(\frac{(k_{F}+|k|/2)^{2}+s^{2}}{(k_{F}-|k|/2)^{2}+s^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad-\frac{s}{k_{F}}\arctan\Big(\frac{k_{F}+|k|/2}{s}\Big)-\frac{s}{k_{F}}\arctan\Big(\frac{k_{F}-|k|/2}{s}\Big)\Big].

We remark that the variables
(k,q,p,u)
in
[
12
]
corresponds to
(\xi/k_{F},k/k_{F},p/k_{F},s/k_{F})
in our notations.

To compare with our result, we denote
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}:=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{3}\mid\xi\in L_{k}\}
and replace our underlying configuration space by
[0,L]^{3}
, substitute

\displaystyle k_{F}^{-1}\hat{V}_{k}\rightarrow 4\pi e^{2}|k|^{-2},\quad(2\pi)^{3}\rightarrow L^{3}\text{ (i.e., the volume of }[0,L]^{3}),

and then formally take thermodynamic limit
L\rightarrow\infty
. In this limit, we replace
L^{-3}\sum_{k}
by integration
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dk
and write
\lambda_{k,\xi}=\xi\cdot k-\frac{1}{2}|k|^{2}=|k|\big(\xi\cdot\hat{k}-\frac{1}{2}|k|\big)=:|k|\tilde{\lambda}_{k,\xi}
so that our formula in (
1.35
) becomes

(A.2)

\displaystyle n_{\rm b}(\xi)

\displaystyle\sim 4\pi e^{2}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\xi}}\frac{dk}{|k|}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(s^{2}-\tilde{\lambda}_{k,\xi}^{2})(s^{2}+\tilde{\lambda}_{k,\xi}^{2})^{-2}}{|k|^{2}+|k|^{2}Q_{k}(|k|s)}ds.

By replacing the Riemann summation
L^{-3}\sum_{p\in L_{k}}
with the corresponding integral and then rewriting the resulting formula using Abel kernel, we obtain

\displaystyle|k|^{2}Q_{k}(|k|s)

\displaystyle\sim 4\pi e^{2}\int_{\overline{B}(k,k_{F})\cap B_{k_{F}}(0)^{c}}\frac{p\cdot k-\frac{1}{2}|k|^{2}}{(p\cdot k-|k|^{2}/2)^{2}+|k|^{2}s^{2}}dp

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2}}{2\pi}\int_{\overline{B}(0,k_{F})\cap B(-k,k_{F})^{c}}dp\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dte^{-its|k|}\exp[-|t|\big(p\cdot k+\frac{1}{2}|k|^{2}\big)\big].

For
|k|\geq 2k_{F}
, we see the integral region is simply
\overline{B}(0,k_{F})
so that, in this regime, the integral is

(A.3)

\displaystyle\int_{|p|\leq k_{F}}dp\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dte^{-its|k|}\exp[-|t|\big(p\cdot k+\frac{1}{2}|k|^{2}\big)\Big].

It follows that, in the regime where
k
sufficiently far away from Fermi ball, we may identify
Q_{k}(|k|s)
with
2\pi|k|^{-2}\alpha k_{F}Q_{k}^{\rm(DV)}(s)
.

Now, we simplify the integral in (
A.2
) by switching to spherical coordinate of
k
and following a similar argument as in
[
1
, Appendix C]
. Since
\chi_{L_{k}}(\xi)
is non-zero only when
|\xi|+k_{F}\leq|k|<|\xi|+k_{F}
, the radial integral over
|k|
starts from
R_{\xi}:=|\xi|-k_{F}
and can end at
|\xi|+k_{F}
. The integration over
\theta
, measuring the angle between
\xi
and
k
, runs from
0
to
\theta_{\rm max}
with
\cos\theta_{\rm max}\approx R_{\xi}/|k|=:\lambda_{\rm min}
. It follows that

\displaystyle n_{\rm b}(\xi)\sim 8\pi^{2}e^{2}\int_{|\xi|-k_{F}}^{|\xi|+k_{F}}d|k||k|\int_{0}^{\theta_{\rm max}}\sin\theta d\theta\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{2}-(|\xi|\cos\theta-|k|/2)^{2}}{(s^{2}+(|\xi|\cos\theta-|k|/2)^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\Big[|k|^{2}+|k|^{2}Q_{k}(|k|s)\Big]^{-1}ds

\displaystyle=8\pi^{2}e^{2}\int_{|\xi|-k_{F}}^{|\xi|+k_{F}}d|k||k|\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[\int_{\lambda_{\rm min}}^{1}\frac{s^{2}-(|\xi|\lambda-|k|/2)^{2}}{(s^{2}+(|\xi|\lambda-|k|/2)^{2})^{2}}d\lambda\Big]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\Big[|k|^{2}+|k|^{2}Q_{k}(|k|s)\Big]^{-1}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{8\pi^{2}e^{2}}{|\xi|}\int_{|\xi|-k_{F}}^{|\xi|+k_{F}}d|k||k|\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[\frac{|\xi|-|k|/2}{s^{2}+(|\xi|-|k|/2)^{2}}-\frac{\frac{|\xi|^{2}-(|k|^{2}+2|\xi|k_{F}-|\xi|^{2})}{2|k|}}{s^{2}+\big(\frac{|\xi|^{2}-(|k|^{2}+2|\xi|k_{F}-|\xi|^{2})}{2|k|}\big)^{2}}\Big]

(A.4)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\Big[|k|^{2}+|k|^{2}Q_{k}(|k|s)\Big]^{-1}ds.

In the region
|k|\sim|\xi|\gg k_{F}\gg 1
(e.g.,
|k|\sim|\xi|\sim k_{F}^{\varepsilon}
for
\varepsilon>1
), we can formally write
k_{F}|\xi|^{-1}+|\xi|^{2}-|k|^{2}\sim o(1)
and

\displaystyle\frac{|\xi|^{2}-\big(|k|^{2}-|\xi|^{2}+2|\xi|k_{F}\big)}{2|k|}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{|\xi|^{2}(1-o(k_{F}|\xi|^{-1}))+o(1)}{2|k|}\approx\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{2|k|}+o(1),

where
o(1)
denotes any formal small parameter. The quantity
\frac{|\xi|^{2}-k_{F}^{2}}{2|k|}
in (
A
) admits a similar approximation. In this way, we may formally identify
n_{\rm b}^{\rm(DV)}(\xi)
with our result in the high momentum regime.

Now, the second order correction for momentum distribution due to exchange correlation for
|\xi|>k_{F}
was given explicitly in
[
12
, Eq. (12)]
as

(A.5)

\displaystyle n_{\rm ex}^{\rm(DV)}(\xi)=-\frac{k_{F}^{2}\alpha^{2}}{4}\int_{|p+k|>k_{F}}\frac{dk}{|k|^{2}}\int_{|p|<k_{F}}\frac{dp}{[k\cdot(p-\xi)]^{2}|p-\xi|^{2}}.

In our result, by formally replacing summations with the corresponding integral as above and defining a new variable
p^{\prime}=k-p
, the term (
1.36
) for
|\xi|>k_{F}
can be written as

\displaystyle n_{\rm ex}(\xi)

\displaystyle\sim-\frac{16\pi^{2}e^{4}}{8}\int_{k\in\mathcal{C}_{\xi}}\frac{dk}{|k|^{2}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}|p^{\prime}+k|>k_{F},\\
|p^{\prime}|\leq k_{F}\end{subarray}}\frac{|p^{\prime}-\xi|^{-2}}{(\lambda_{k,p^{\prime}+k}+\lambda_{k,\xi})^{2}}dp

(A.6)

\displaystyle=-2\pi^{2}e^{4}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathcal{C}_{\xi},\\
|p^{\prime}+k|>k_{F}\end{subarray}}\frac{dk}{|k|^{2}}\int_{|p^{\prime}|\leq k_{F}}\frac{dp^{\prime}}{|p^{\prime}-\xi|^{2}[(p^{\prime}-\xi)\cdot k]^{2}},

where we have used the following computation

\displaystyle\lambda_{k,p}+\lambda_{k,\xi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\big(|p^{\prime}-k|^{2}-|p^{\prime}|^{2}\big)+\frac{1}{2}\big(|\xi|^{2}-|\xi-k|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=-p^{\prime}\cdot k+\frac{1}{2}|k|^{2}+\xi\cdot k-\frac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2}=(\xi-p^{\prime})\cdot k.

We see that the integrand of
n_{\rm ex}(\xi)
and
n_{\rm ex}^{\rm(DV)}(\xi)
exactly coincides. To conclude this section, we give some comments on the integral region. For
k\in\mathcal{C}_{\xi}
, one must have
|\xi-k|\leq k_{F}<|\xi|
for
\xi\in B_{F}^{c}
. For those
\xi
’s that are really far away from Fermi ball, those
k
’s that belong to
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}
must be far away from Fermi ball as well. Since
p^{\prime}\in B_{F}
, the condition
|p^{\prime}+k|>k_{F}
for these
k
’s is almost always guaranteed. In this way, we can drop the symbol “
k\in\mathcal{C}_{\xi}
” in the above integral, which make
n_{\rm ex}(\xi)
and
n_{\rm ex}^{\rm(DV)}(\xi)
directly propositional to each other within this regime of
\xi
.

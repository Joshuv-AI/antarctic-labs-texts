Impact of abdominal adipose tissue segmentation on radiomics-based prognostic evaluation using CT enterography in Crohn’s disease

Background

Crohn’s disease (CD) is a chronic inflammatory disorder of the gastrointestinal tract, with increasing prevalence worldwide [1]. The primary goal of contemporary treatment for CD is to achieve sustained remission, to delay disease progression, and to minimize complications [2–4]. In the era of personalized medicine, identifying reliable biomarkers that can predict disease progression is crucial for optimizing treatment strategies and improving patient outcomes.

Radiomics enables the extraction of high-dimensional quantitative features from regions of interest (ROIs) on medical images, revealing imaging information that is invisible to the human eye and thus supporting disease assessment and treatment decision-making [5]. Recent studies have investigated the diagnostic and prognostic value of radiomics features derived from adipose tissue fom CT in patients with inflammatory bowel disease [6–11]. While these studies reported promising predictive performance, they adopted diverse segmentation strategies for abdominal adipose tissue, making comparisons difficult. Although VAT is the primary adipose compartment implicated in CD, SAT and VAT exhibit distinct metabolic and immunological profiles [8, 12]. Therefore, incorporating SAT into the present study not only recognizes its potential prognostic role but also ensures methodological completeness by enabling direct comparison with VAT.

Broadly, segmentation approaches fall into three categories: (1) three-dimensional (3D) segmentation based on fixed anatomical landmarks; (2) two-dimensional (2D) segmentation based on specific vertebral levels; and (3) lesion-targeted segmentation guided by the location of intestinal inflammation. To date, there is no consensus on the optimal segmentation method for radiomics-based prognosis prediction in CD.

Advances in deep learning have significantly improved the accuracy of abdominal adipose tissue segmentation on CT, especially for SAT and VAT [13–15]. However, the complex and heterogeneous distribution of abdominal adipose tissue renders large-scale segmentation technically demanding. Segmentation based on a single slice or predefined slices is simpler and faster to implement and often achieves high accuracy. CD-related adipose tissue alterations are not restricted to regions adjacent to inflamed bowel, as distal compartments may also be involved [8]. Whether limited-slice segmentation can accurately and effectively capture these disease-associated changes remains uncertain [16, 17].

To address these challenges, multiple segmentation strategies were applied to VAT and SAT on baseline CT enterography (CTE) images in a dual-center CD cohort, and radiomics-based models were developed to predict 3-year disease progression. Model performance across different segmentation approaches was systematically compared to identify the most clinically applicable strategy. The optimal segmentation-based model was further combined with baseline clinical data to evaluate its incremental value for individualized disease management.

Methods

Subjects

This retrospective, dual-center study was approved by the institutional review boards of both participating hospitals, and the requirement for informed consent was waived. CD was diagnosed based on a comprehensive assessment of clinical presentation, radiological findings, endoscopic evaluation, and histopathology, in accordance with ECCO and ACG guidelines. Patients with CD who underwent baseline CTE between January 2018 and December 2021 were identified from institutional databases. The training and internal validation set were derived from Sir Run Run Shaw Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine, and an independent external test set was obtained from the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhejiang Chinese Medical University (Fig. 1).

Flow chart of the patient study population

Inclusion criteria were: (a) confirmed clinical diagnosis of CD; (b) baseline CTE performed with availability of complete clinical data; and (c) at least 3 years of follow-up for patients without progression. Exclusion criteria included: (a) consideration of CD-related surgery at initial admission due to stricturing or penetrating phenotype; (b) severe abdominopelvic effusion, exudative lesions, or marked bowel dilatation interfering with adipose tissue segmentation; (c) prior CD-related surgery; (d) history of abdominal surgery, lumbar spinal fixation or retained capsule endoscopy; and (e) coexisting malignancy or metabolic disorders potentially affecting adipose tissue distribution.

Clinical data collection and outcome definition

Demographic and clinical variables collected at baseline included age, sex, smoking status, body mass index (BMI), disease duration and Montreal classification. Laboratory parameters included erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), C-reactive protein (CRP), white blood cell (WBC) count, red blood cell (RBC) count, platelet (PLT) count, and serum albumin.

Disease progression was defined as either the development of more advanced disease behavior or the need for CD-related surgery. CD-related surgery included bowel resection or procedures for stricturing or penetrating complications, whereas appendectomy and perianal procedures were not considered. Penetrating complications included radiologically confirmed enteric fistula, inflammatory mass, or abscess [2]. Stricturing disease was identified by either cross-sectional imaging demonstrating a bowel stricture with upstream dilation, or endoscopic evidence of luminal narrowing that prevented or limited scope passage [18]. When surgery was performed in conjunction with the onset of penetrating or stricturing disease, only the surgical intervention was recorded as the progression event. Patients were followed up until December 2024.

Image acquisition

CT examinations were performed using multidetector CT scanners: LightSpeed VCT and Revolution Maxima (GE Healthcare, USA), SOMATOM Definition AS and SOMATOM Force (Siemens Healthineers, Germany), and Aquilion ONE (Canon Medical Systems, Japan). The scan range extended from the diaphragmatic dome to the pubic symphysis. Tube voltage settings were 150 kVp for SOMATOM Force, 100 kVp for SOMATOM Definition AS, and 120 kVp for the other scanners. Slice thickness and spacing between slices were 1.25 mm and 1.25 mm for LightSpeed VCT, Revolution Maxima, and SOMATOM Definition AS; 2 mm and 2 mm for SOMATOM Force; and 1 mm and 0.8 mm for Aquilion ONE. All images were reconstructed with a 512 × 512 matrix using soft-tissue kernels and iterative reconstruction techniques when available. All patients fasted for 6–8 h and received bowel preparation with polyethylene glycol electrolyte solution. One hour before scanning, 1600–2000 mL of 2.5% mannitol solution was administered orally. Following unenhanced acquisition, a nonionic iodinated contrast agent was injected via the right antecubital vein at a rate of 3.5 mL/s, with a total volume of 1.0–1.2 mL/kg (typically 60–90 mL), followed by a 40 mL saline flush. Arterial and venous phases were acquired at 28 s and 70 s, respectively. The intravenous contrast agents used for CTE included iopamidol (Iopamiron; 300–370 mg I/mL; Bracco), iodixanol (Visipaque; 320 mg I/mL; GE Healthcare), and iomeprol (Iomeron; 350 mg I/mL; Bracco).

Adipose tissue segmentation

Abdominal adipose tissue was segmented semi-automatically using 3D Slicer (version 5.6.2). Venous-phase images were used for adipose tissue segmentation and all radiomic analyses. Based on standard attenuation thresholds of − 190 to − 30 Hounsfield units [19], all voxels within the adipose tissue range were automatically selected. Subsequently, a trained annotator performed manual slice-by-slice adjustment to separate VAT from SAT and to refine the boundaries, particularly in regions where fascial planes were indistinct. Three segmentation strategies were used: (1) T-VAT/SAT (total VAT/SAT): Total visceral and subcutaneous adipose tissue; (2) LL-VAT/SAT (lesion-level VAT/SAT): Adipose tissue segmented from axial slices at the most severely inflamed intestinal segment, as determined by radiological or endoscopic findings; (3) V-VAT/SAT (vertebral-level VAT/SAT): Single-slice axial segmentation at the center of each vertebral level from L3 to S2, with each level labeled individually (e.g., L3-VAT, S1-SAT). The segmentation annotations are provided in Fig. 2. To evaluate reproducibility, 20 cases were randomly selected for repeat segmentation by two independent readers. Inter-observer intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs) were calculated using a two-way random-effects model with absolute agreement. Features with ICC ≤ 0.75 were excluded from further analysis.

Radiomic feature extraction

Radiomic features were extracted using Shukun Technology research platform (https://medresearch.shukun.net/). All CTE images were preprocessed before feature extraction, including resampling to an isotropic voxel size of 1.0 × 1.0 × 1.0 mm3 and intensity discretization with a fixed bin width of 25 HU. Twelve image preprocessing filters were applied including exponential, gradient, LBP-2D, LBP-3D-k, LBP-3D-m1, LBP-3D-m2, log (sigma), logarithm, original, square, square root, and wavelet. From the original images, 107 hand-crafted features were extracted. After applying the preprocessing filters, a total of 1,874 radiomic features were generated, comprising 360 first-order features, 480 Gy-level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM) features, 280 Gy-level dependence matrix (GLDM) features, 320 Gy-level run-length matrix (GLRLM) features, 320 Gy-level size zone matrix (GLSZM) features, 100 neighboring gray tone difference matrix (NGTDM) features, and 14 shape-based features. Radiomic features were defined according to the PyRadiomics documentation (https://pyradiomics.readthedocs.io), consistent with the Image Biomarker Standardization Initiative (IBSI) guidelines.

Radiomics model construction

Features were normalized before analysis. Highly correlated features (Pearson r ≥ 0.90) were removed. Feature selection was performed using the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) method, with a maximum of 3,000 iterations, a convergence threshold of 0.0001, and a maximum of 10 features retained. Logistic regression models were constructed using selected features. Five-fold cross-validation was employed to prevent overfitting. The Rad-score from the best-performing model was integrated with selected clinical features to develop a combined model. The workflow is illustrated in Fig. 2. To ensure methodological transparency and rigor, this study was conducted in accordance with the CLEAR (Checklist for Reporting Radiomics) guidelines.

Clinical and combined models construction

Clinical variable selection was performed in two stages: (1) univariate selection using mutual information via the SelectKBest algorithm; (2) multivariate filtering using logistic regression with five-fold cross-validation for robust feature selection. Finally, logistic regression models were constructed using the selected clinical features (clinical model) and clinical features plus Rad-score (combined model) for predicting 3-year disease progression in patients with CD.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were compared using the independent-samples t test or Mann-Whitney U test depending on normality. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages, and group comparisons were performed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Model performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, and further compared using the area under the precision-recall curve (AUPRC). AUPRC is particularly informative in the context of class imbalance and provides a more reliable assessment of model performance for the minority class. Precision-recall curves were also plotted. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed to evaluate the clinical net benefit of each model across a range of threshold probabilities. Calibration was assessed with calibration curves and the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test. All statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.1.3; https://www.r-project.org/).

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 343 patients with CD were included, of whom 49 (14.3%) experienced disease progression within 3 years. Baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1. No significant differences were observed between training, validation, and external test set in gender (P = 0.185) or smoking history (P = 0.564). Compared to patients without progression, those with progression had significantly longer disease duration, lower BMI, and older age at baseline (all P < 0.05). Laboratory markers, including ESR, CRP, WBC, RBC, PLT, and serum albumin, showed no significant group differences (all P > 0.05). According to the Montreal classification, significant differences were observed between the progression and non-progression groups in age at diagnosis, disease location, and disease behavior (all P < 0.05). The presence of perianal fistula or abscess was more frequent in the progression group, although the difference did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.061).

Radiomics model analysis

Among VAT-based models, the T-VAT model achieved the highest overall performance across all sets, with AUCs of 0.946 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.888–1.000) in the training set, 0.986 (95% CI: 0.940–1.000) in the internal validation set, and 0.877 (95% CI: 0.718–0.937) in the external test set (Table 2; Fig. 3A–C). The LL-VAT model ranked second and maintained relatively high performance in the external test set (AUC = 0.835, 95% CI: 0.656–1.000), although its PPV was relatively low (0.467). Models developed at individual VAT levels from L3 to S2 showed moderate performance in the training set (AUC range: 0.758–0.875) but declined in the internal validation and external test set (AUC range: 0.502–0.725).

Among SAT-based models in the external test set, the LL-SAT and T-SAT models achieved comparable AUCs of 0.739 (95% CI: 0.532–0.946) and 0.737 (95% CI: 0.529–0.944), respectively (Table 3; Fig. 3D–F). However, in the internal validation set, the T-SAT model maintained markedly higher performance with an AUC of 0.865 (95% CI: 0.734–0.995) compared to only 0.511 (95% CI: 0.339–0.684) for LL-SAT, indicating greater robustness. Furthermore, the T-SAT model achieved the highest AUPRC of 0.346 (95% CI: 0.144–0.706) in the external test set, outperforming the LL-SAT model (0.333, 95% CI: 0.180–0.490). Given segmentation strategy and the pronounced class imbalance, T-SAT was considered the optimal SAT segmentation strategy. SAT-based models built at each vertebral level from L3 to S2 achieved AUCs between 0.625 and 0.800 in the training set, with reduced performance in the external test set (AUC range: 0.522–0.712; Table 3). Combined model analysis

After combining the T-VAT radiomics model with clinical characteristics, the resulting combined model achieved the highest AUPRC in the external test set (AUPRC = 0.666, 95% CI: 0.376–1.000), outperforming both the T-VAT model (AUPRC = 0.657, 95% CI: 0.368–1.000) and the clinical model (AUPRC = 0.512, 95% CI: 0.181–0.829) (Fig. 4A, C). In addition, the T-VAT radiomics model showed superior performance compared to the clinical model in the external test set, with higher AUC (0.877 vs. 0.766) and AUPRC (0.657 vs. 0.512; Figs. 3C and I and 4A and C; Tables 2 and 4). DCA further demonstrated that the combined model provided greater net clinical benefit across a range of threshold probabilities compared to the clinical model and the T-VAT model (Fig. 4D). Calibration performance of the T-VAT, clinical, and combined models is shown in Supplementary Figure S2, and the corresponding confusion matrices are provided in Supplementary Figure S3.

Discussion

This study systematically evaluated various VAT and SAT segmentation strategies based on CTE and compared the performance of radiomics models constructed using different ROIs in predicting the risk of disease progression within three years in patients with CD. The results showed that the radiomics model based on the T-VAT segmentation strategy achieved the highest overall predictive performance, outperforming models based on other VAT and SAT segmentation strategies. The combined model integrating clinical characteristics with the T-VAT radiomics model showed limited incremental improvement compared with the T-VAT model alone.

Among all VAT-based models, the T-VAT model showed the best predictive performance, followed by the LL-VAT model. This suggests that the impact of CD on the entire abdominal VAT is more specific on venous-phase CTE images compared to VAT surrounding the most severely affected intestinal segment. Although perienteric fat surrounding the most severely affected bowel segment is often considered representative on CTE because of its close association with local perienteric inflammation [20–22], the multifocal and migratory nature of CD means that the localized fat alterations captured by LL-VAT may fluctuate with short-term disease activity [23], thereby undermining their stability for long-term prognostic prediction. In contrast, T-VAT segmentation covers the entire mesenteric compartment and captures cumulative remodeling processes, including creeping fat proliferation, angiogenesis, and inflammatory cell infiltration [24–26]. These chronic and system-level alterations provide more comprehensive radiomics information and align better with the three-year prognostic endpoint in this study [6, 8, 27].

For SAT-based strategies, the T-SAT model demonstrated relatively stable performance across datasets, suggesting that total abdominal fat features provide more robust prognostic information than single-level or lesion-level approaches. However, the lower predictive performance of the T-SAT model compared with the T-VAT model may be explained by the fact that SAT is not in direct anatomical contact with the inflamed bowel, but is instead influenced indirectly through systemic inflammatory and metabolic pathways [28, 29]. Moreover, SAT is substantially influenced by general lifestyle and metabolic factors, which likely makes its radiomic patterns less specific for CD-related progression [30].

Although adipose tissue is widely distributed in abdominal CT images, some studies have adopted limited-range adipose tissue segmentation strategies in inflammatory bowel disease research [9, 11]. While such approaches can reduce manual effort and time costs, they may fail to capture the overall impact of CD on abdominal adipose tissue. The findings of this study indicate that a comprehensive T-VAT strategy facilitates the development of high-performance radiomics models for predicting disease progression based on CTE images. While manual whole-abdominal segmentation is labor-intensive, recent advances in deep learning–based automated tools may enable efficient and reproducible VAT/SAT segmentation, substantially enhancing the feasibility and translational potential of T-VAT models in clinical practice.

This study has several limitations. First, this was a retrospective study with a limited sample size, and differences in treatment strategies across centers may have introduced heterogeneity. Future research should establish large-scale prospective cohorts to address these issues and improve the accuracy of predictive modeling. Second, although five–fold cross-validation was applied, some models still showed unstable performance in the validation set. Third, although preprocessing was applied, the diversity of scanning parameters across centers may still affect feature reproducibility, which warrants further validation in future studies. In addition, intra-observer reproducibility was not assessed, which may limit the completeness of the reliability evaluation. Finally, this study focused exclusively on venous-phase contrast-enhanced CTE images. Whether non-contrast or multi-phase CT images may provide additional or superior predictive value requires further investigation.

Conclusions

This study systematically evaluated multiple VAT- and SAT-based segmentation strategies on venous-phase CTE in patients with CD. Among all models, the radiomics model based on T-VAT demonstrated the highest performance for predicting 3-year disease progression. These findings highlight the distinctive impact of CD on visceral fat and support the prioritization of whole-abdominal VAT segmentation in future artificial intelligence–driven prognostic modeling development.

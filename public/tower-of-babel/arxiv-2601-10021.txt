An introduction to weightings along submanifolds
Eckhard Meinrenken

Abstract




1.
Introduction

The concept of
weighting
on a manifold was introduced by Richard Melrose
[
20
]
under the name of
quasi-homogeneous structures
, and by Yiannis Loizides and myself in
[
17
]
under the present name. Further developments may be found in
[
4
,
11
,
14
,
15
,
18
]
.

The principal idea of weighting is to give a coordinate-free meaning to the process of assigning weights to coordinate functions.
Constructions of this type appear in many area of mathematics, such as in the classification of singularities
[
3
]
,
algebraic geometry (e.g., weighted blow-ups), anisotropic asymptotic expansions (e.g.,
[
5
]
), or the theory of hypoelliptic operators. One of our original motivations was to obtain a better understanding of the ‘nonlinear’ tangent groupoids
arising in this context of manifolds with Lie filtrations
[
6
,
21
,
13
]
.

These notes are a write-up of a talk given at the Ghent Geometric Analysis Seminar in 2023, and I thank the organizers of the seminar,
Michael Ruzhansky and David Santiago Gomez Cobos, for the kind invitation.

2.
Definition and examples

A weighting of a manifold
M
along a submanifold
N\subseteq M
is a filtration on the algebra of functions,

(1)

C^{\infty}(M)=C^{\infty}(M)_{(0)}\supseteq C^{\infty}(M)_{(1)}\supseteq\cdots

generalizing the usual order of vanishing of a functions along
N
. The conditions on such filtration to define a weighting can be expressed in various equivalent forms.

Definition 2.1

(Coordinate definition
[
17
,
20
]
)
.

A
weighting
of order
r
on
M
, with weights
0\leq w_{1}\leq\ldots\leq w_{\dim M}\leq r
, is a
filtration (
1
) on the algebra of functions such that
M
admits a covering by charts,
defining coordinates
x_{a}
, with the property that
f\in C^{\infty}(M)_{(i)}
if and only if the restriction of
f
to any such chart domain lies
in the ideal generated by monomials

x^{s}=\prod_{a}x_{a}^{s_{a}}

of weight
\sum_{a}s_{a}w_{a}\geq i
.

We refer to the coordinates in this definition as weighted coordinates. Note that the definition does not make explicit reference to a submanifold; it is automatic that

C^{\infty}(M)_{(1)}=\mathcal{I}_{N}

is the vanishing ideal of a closed, embedded submanifold
N\subseteq M
.
The weighting determines a filtration of the cotangent bundle, restricted to
N
,

(2)

T^{*}M|_{N}=(T^{*}M|_{N})_{(0)}\supseteq(T^{*}M|_{N})_{(1)}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq(T^{*}M|_{N})_{(r)}\supseteq 0

where the space of sections of
(T^{*}M|_{N})_{(i)}
is
C^{\infty}(N)
-linearly spanned by all

(3)

{\mbox{d}}f|_{N},\ \ \ \ f\in C^{\infty}(M)_{(i)}

In particular,
(T^{*}M|_{N})_{(1)}
is the conormal bundle of
N
.
In
[
17
]
, it is shown that the following coordinate-free definition is equivalent to Definition
2.1
:

Definition 2.2

(Intrinsic definition)
.

A weighting of order
r
on
M
is given by a filtration
(
1
) on the sheaf of functions, such that

(a)

C^{\infty}(M)_{(1)}
is the vanishing ideal
\mathcal{I}_{N}
of a closed submanifold
N\subseteq M
.

(b)

The differentials (
3
) span subbundles

(T^{*}M|_{N})_{(i)}\subseteq T^{*}M|_{N},

equal to
0
for

i\geq r
.

(c)

For
i\geq 2
,

C^{\infty}(M)_{(i)}\cap\mathcal{I}_{N}^{2}=\sum_{0<j<i}C^{\infty}(M)_{(j)}C^{\infty}(M)_{(i-j)}.

Note that a filtration of order
r
is uniquely determined by the filtration up to degree
i=r
; for
i>r
we have

C^{\infty}(M)_{(i)}\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{2}
, which is described by the third condition.

Examples 2.3
.

(a)

A weighting of order
r=1
is the filtration of functions given by the
usual order of vanishing along a closed submanifold
N
.

(b)

A weighting or order
r=2
is equivalent to a submanifold
N\subseteq M
together with a subbundle
Q\subseteq TM|_{N}

containing
TN
. Here
f
has filtration degree
2
if and only if it vanishes along
N
and
{\mbox{d}}f|_{N}
vanishes on
Q
.

(c)

Suppose
M
is a
graded bundle
as defined by Grabowski-Rodkievicz
[
12
]
. That is,
M

carries an action of the monoid
(\mathbb{R},\cdot)
by ‘scalar multiplications’

\kappa_{t}\colon M\to M

with

\kappa_{st}=\kappa_{s}\kappa_{t}
. The fixed point set of such an action is a closed submanifold
N\subseteq M
.
The manifold
M
acquires a weighting along
N
, where a function

f
has filtration degree
i
if and only if
\kappa_{t}^{*}f=O(t^{i})
.

(d)

A collection of submanifolds
N_{1},\ldots,N_{r}\subseteq M

intersects cleanly
if for all
m\in M
, there exists a coordinate chart
U
centered at
m
, with the property that all non-empty
N_{i}\cap U
map to open subsets of coordinate subspaces. Let

C^{\infty}(M)_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{r})}
be the ideal of functions vanishing to order
i_{1}
on
N_{1}
, to order
i_{2}
on
N_{2}
, and so on.
Then we obtains a weighting of order
r
along
N=N_{1}\cap\cdots\cap N_{r}
by letting
C^{\infty}(M)_{(i)}
be the sum over all

C^{\infty}(M)_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{r})}
such that
i_{1}+\ldots+i_{r}=i
. More generally, one can define
multi-weightings
for families of cleanly intersecting submanifolds; the ‘total weighting’ is then a weighting for the intersection.

Remark 2.4
.

The concept of weighting also applies in the holomorphic category, replacing the filtration on
C^{\infty}(M)
with a filtration of the
sheaf

\mathcal{O}_{M}
of holomorphic functions.

3.
Weighted normal bundle

Recall that the
associated graded algebra
of a
\mathbb{Z}
-filtered algebra
\mathcal{A}
(thus
\mathcal{A}_{(i)}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{(i+1)}
and

\mathcal{A}_{(i)}\mathcal{A}_{(j)}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{(i+j)}
) is the graded algebra with summands

\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{A})^{i}=\mathcal{A}_{(i)}/\mathcal{A}_{(i+1)}.

The
Rees algebra
of
\mathcal{A}
is the algebra

\operatorname{Rees}(\mathcal{A})\subseteq\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{R}[u,u^{-1}]

consisting of
Laurent polynomials
\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{i}\otimes u^{-i}
with coefficients
a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{(i)}
. For
a\in\mathcal{A}_{(i)}
, let

\widetilde{a}_{[i]}
be the corresponding element
a\otimes u^{-i}
of the Rees algebra, and

a_{[i]}\in\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{A})^{i}
the image under the quotient map. There is a surjective morphism of graded algebras

\operatorname{Rees}(\mathcal{A})\to\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{A})
, taking
\widetilde{a}_{[i]}
to
a_{[i]}
.
Applying these constructions to the filtration of
\mathcal{A}=C^{\infty}(M)
given by a weighting (declaring that
\mathcal{A}_{(i)}=C^{\infty}(M)
for
i\leq 0
),
we make the following definitions (inspired by Haj-Higson
[
13
]
):

Definition 3.1
.

[
17
]

The
weighted normal bundle
is the set of morphisms of unital algebras,

\nu_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{alg}}(\operatorname{gr}(C^{\infty}(M)),\mathbb{R}).

The
weighted deformation space

\nu_{W}(M,N)
is the set of morphisms of unital algebras,

\delta_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{alg}}(\operatorname{Rees}(C^{\infty}(M)),\mathbb{R}).

For
f\in C^{\infty}(M)_{(i)}
, the elements
f_{[i]},\ \widetilde{f}_{[i]}
may be regarded as functions on the weighted normal bundle and weighted deformation space, respectively. As a special case, the constant function
1\in C^{\infty}(M)
, regarded as having filtration degree
-1
,
defines a function
\widetilde{1}_{[-1]}=1\otimes u
on the deformation space; we will denote this function by

(4)

\pi\colon\delta_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N)\to\mathbb{R}.

The surjective algebra morphism
\operatorname{Rees}(C^{\infty}(M))\to\operatorname{gr}(C^{\infty}(M))
dualizes to an injective map

(5)

\ \nu_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N)\hookrightarrow\delta_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N);

f_{[i]}
is the restriction of
\widetilde{f}_{[i]}
under this map. For
c\neq 0
, the algebra morphism

\operatorname{Rees}(C^{\infty}(M))\to C^{\infty}(M)
taking
\sum_{i}f_{i}\otimes u^{i}
to
\sum c^{i}f_{i}
dualizes to an inclusion

(6)

M\hookrightarrow\delta_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N).

Theorem 3.2
.

[
17
]
. The weighted normal bundle
\nu_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N)
is a manifold, in such a way that
the functions
f_{[i]}
for
f\in C^{\infty}(M)_{(i)}
are smooth. Similarly,
\delta_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N)
is a manifold, in such a way that
the functions
\widetilde{f}_{[i]}
for
f\in C^{\infty}(M)_{(i)}
are smooth. The map (
4
) is a surjective submersion, and
(
5
) and (
6
) give identifications

\pi^{-1}(c)=\begin{cases}\nu_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N)&c=0,\\
M&c\neq 0.\end{cases}

The group
\mathbb{R}^{\times}
acts smoothly on the deformation space by the
zoom action

t\mapsto\kappa_{t}
: Here
t\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
acts on

\nu_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N)
by fiberwise scalar multiplication, and on
\pi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{\times})=M\times\mathbb{R}^{\times}
by
\kappa_{t}(x,c)\mapsto(x,t^{-1}c)
.

By construction, if
f
has filtration degree
i
, then the function
\widetilde{f}_{[i]}
is homogeneous of degree
i
for the zoom action:

\kappa_{t}^{*}\widetilde{f}_{[i]}=t^{i}\widetilde{f}_{[i]}.

Weighted coordinates
x_{a}
on
M
, of weights
w_{a}
, gives rise to coordinates

{x_{a}}_{[w_{a}]}
on the weighted normal bundle. For the deformation space, the functions
\widetilde{x_{a}}_{[w_{a}]}
together with
the map
\pi
(regarded as a function, denoted by a variable ‘
u
’) are coordinates on the deformation space.

The weighted deformation space is a natural setting for ‘weighted’ linearization problems. Consider for example the filtration of
on vector fields,

\mathfrak{X}(M)=\mathfrak{X}(M)_{(-r)}\supseteq\mathfrak{X}(M)_{(-r+1)}\supseteq\cdots

where a vector field
X
has filtration degree
i\geq-r
if the Lie derivative
\mathcal{L}_{X}
raises the filtration degree of functions by
i
.
In this case,
X
a
homogeneous
approximation
X_{[i]}
, which is a vector field on
\nu_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N)
of homogeneity
i

characterized by its property

\mathcal{L}_{X_{[i]}}f_{[j]}=(\mathcal{L}_{X}f)_{[i+j]}.

A similar prescription defines a vector field
\widetilde{X}_{[i]}
on the deformation space. One finds that
\widetilde{X}_{[i]}

is vertical with respect to the submersion
\pi
, and may be regarded as a family of vector fields on the fibers
\pi^{-1}(c)
, equal to
X_{[i]}
for
c=0
and to
c^{i}X
for
c\neq 0
.

See
[
19
]
for concrete applications of this set-up to normal form theorems. As another application, one obtains an intrinsic definition of

weighted (real) blow-ups
, along the lines of Debord-Skandalis
[
10
]
. Let

\operatorname{S}_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N)=(\nu_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N)-N)/\mathbb{R}_{>0}\to N

be the weighted sphere bundle.

Theorem 3.3
.

[
17
]

The disjoint union

\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N)\cong\operatorname{S}_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N)\sqcup(M-N)

is a manifold with boundary, in such a way that the blow-down map
\operatorname{Bl}(M,N)\to M
(given by projection to
N
of the sphere
bundle and inclusion of
M-N
) is smooth.

The weighted blow-up is constructed as the quotient of the manifold with boundary

(\delta_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N)-(N\times\mathbb{R}))\cap\pi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
under the zoom action of
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
; the proof amounts to
showing that the latter action is free and proper.

4.
Lie filtrations

Many examples of
hypo-elliptic
operators are obtained from
Lie filtrations
on manifolds, given by a
filtration of the tangent bundle

TM=H_{(-r)}\supseteq H_{(-r+1)}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq H_{(0)}=0

such that the resulting filtration on vector fields is compatible with brackets

[\Gamma(H_{(i)}),\Gamma(H_{(j)})]\subseteq\Gamma(H_{(i+j)})
.
Given such a Lie filtration, the associated graded bundle

\operatorname{gr}TM=\bigoplus_{i=-r}^{-1}H_{(i)}/H_{(i+1)}

is a family of nilpotent Lie algebras (not necessarily a locally trivial Lie algebra bundle). It exponentiates to a family of nilpotent Lie groups, called the
osculating groupoid

[
21
]

or
tangent cone

[
6
]
,

(7)

T_{H}M\to M.

Lie filtrations give rise to weightings in natural ways. Suppose that
N\subseteq M
is a filtered submanifold as defined by Haj-Higson
[
13
]
: that is, the intersections
H_{(i)}\cap TN
are smooth subbundles.
As shown in
[
18
]
, one obtains a weighting of order
r
along
N
by setting

C^{\infty}(M)_{(0)}=C^{\infty}(M),\ C^{\infty}(M)_{(1)}=\mathcal{I}_{N}

and

C^{\infty}(M)_{(i)}=\{f|\ \forall X\in\Gamma(H_{(j)})\colon\mathcal{L}_{X}f\in C^{\infty}(M)_{(i+j)}\}

for
i>1
. The weighted normal bundle
\nu_{W}(M,N)\to N
and weighted deformation space
\delta_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N)
are exactly the normal bundle and deformation space of the filtered submanifold, as defined in
[
13
]
. In particular, the osculating groupoid is

(8)

T_{H}M=\nu_{\mathcal{W}}(M\times M,M_{\Delta})

and it comes with a version of Connes’ tangent groupoid
[
7
]

(9)

\mathbb{T}_{H}M=\delta_{\mathcal{W}}(M\times M,M_{\Delta}).

The groupoid structure of
\mathbb{T}_{H}M
over
M\times\mathbb{R}
is obtained from the groupoid structure on
\pi^{-1}(0)=T_{H}M
(as a bundle of nilpotent groups) and the pair groupoid structure on
\pi^{-1}(c)=M\times M
for
c\neq 0
. More conceptually, it follows from the functorial properties of the weighted deformation space construction – see the following section.
This groupoid
\mathbb{T}_{H}M
was used by van-Erp and Yuncken
[
22
]
(building on ideas of Debord and Skandalis
[
8
]
)
to develop a pseudo-differential calculus for manifolds with Lie filtrations.

Remark 4.1
.

There are examples of hypoelliptic operators that do not correspond to Lie filtrations, but instead
are related to more complicated
‘singular’ Lie filtrations. See in particular the recent work of Androulidakis-Mohsen-Yuncken
[
1
,
2
]
. In

[
18
]
, it is shown that singular Lie filtrations once again define weightings along suitable ‘clean’ submanifolds.

5.
Multiplicative weightings

The groupoid structures on (
8
) and (
9
) may be understood from the weightings perspective: The weighting on
M\times M
is compatible with the pair groupoid structure, the diagonal is a weighted subgroupoid, and for this reason the resulting weighted normal bundle and deformation space acquire groupoid structure. To explain this in more detail, we need to consider weightings on Lie groupoids.

Suppose
G\rightrightarrows M
is a Lie groupoid. Denote by
\mathsf{s},\mathsf{t}\colon G\to M
the source and target maps, and by
\operatorname{Mult}_{G}\colon G^{(2)}\to G

the groupoid multiplication, defined on the set of
composable arrows
(i.e., pairs
(g,h)\in G\times G
such that
\mathsf{s}(g)=\mathsf{t}(h)
).
What does it mean for a weighting on
G
of order
r
to be compatible with the groupoid structure?
In
[
17
]
, using the description of weightings in terms of submanifolds
Q\subseteq T_{r-1}G
as in
Section
6
below, a weighting is called multiplicative if
Q
is a Lie subgroupoid of

T_{r-1}G\rightrightarrows T_{r-1}M
. This definition, while short, is somewhat difficult to verify in practice. A more direct approach was developed in the Ph.D. thesis of Dan Hudson
[
15
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

[
15
]
A weighting on a Lie groupoid
G\rightrightarrows M
is multiplicative if and only if

(a)

the source and target maps
\mathsf{s},\mathsf{t}\colon G\to M
are
weighted submersions
,

(b)

the set of units
M\to G
is a
weighted submanifold
,

(c)

the groupoid multiplication
\operatorname{Mult}_{G}\colon G^{(2)}\to G
is a weighted submersion.

The submanifold of
G
defined by the weighting is a Lie subgroupoid
H\rightrightarrows N
.

The theorem requires some explanation. To begin, a
weighted submanifold
of a weighted manifold
M
is a submanifold with the property that there exists weighted coordinates for
M
that are also submanifold coordinates.
Such a submanifold is then a weighted manifold in its own right. Similarly, a
weighted submersion
from a weighted manifold
M

to another weighted manifold
M^{\prime}
is a submersion
f\colon M\to M^{\prime}
such that for each
p\in M
there exists weighted coordinates around
p
and
f(p)
, respectively, that are also submersion coordinates. (These conditions also admit coordinate-free formulations
[
15
]
.)
Under the assumption that
\mathsf{s},\mathsf{t}\colon G\to M
are weighted submersions, it is shown in
[
15
]
that
G^{(2)}
is a weighted submanifold of
G\times G
, and hence the condition on
\operatorname{Mult}_{G}
being a weighted submersion make sense.

In joint work with Dan Hudson, we also found the following alternative version of this result. Given a weighting of
G\rightrightarrows M
along a
subgroupoid
H\rightrightarrows N
, we obtain a filtration of
T^{*}G|_{H}
, and hence dually of
TG|_{H}
, where
TG_{(-i)}
is the
annihilator of
(T^{*}G|_{H})_{(i+1)}
.

Theorem 5.2

(Hudson-M)
.

A weighting of
G\rightrightarrows M
along
H\rightrightarrows N
is multiplicative if and only if the units
M\subseteq G
are a weighted submanifold,
the graph of the groupoid multiplication
\operatorname{Gr}(\operatorname{Mult}_{G})\subseteq G\times G\times G
is a weighted submanifold, and
each subbundle

(TG|_{H})_{(-i)}\subseteq TG

is a Lie subgroupoid of the tangent groupoid
TG\rightrightarrows TM
.

Given a multiplicative weighting of
G\rightrightarrows M
along
H\rightrightarrows N
, it is shown in
[
15
]
that

\nu_{\mathcal{W}}(G,H)\rightrightarrows\nu_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N),\ \ \delta_{\mathcal{W}}(G,H)\rightrightarrows\delta_{W}(M,N)

are Lie groupoids.

Remark 5.3
.

The infinitesimal counterpart to multiplicative weightings on Lie groupoids are the infinitesimally multiplicative weightings on Lie
algebroids. Again, these may be defined in terms of higher tangent bundles (Section
6
; see also
[
17
]
). A more
direct description was obtained in
[
15
]
, which also establishes the compatibility of the
(weighted) normal bundle and deformation space functors.

Remark 5.4
.

At this stage, one should develop a pseudo-differential calculus for groupoids with multiplicative weightings, along the lines of
van Erp and Yuncken
[
22
, Section 12]
, see also Debord-Skandalis
[
9
]
.
We hope to return to this problem in future work.

6.
Appendix: Weightings in terms of jet bundle

According to Example
2.3
(
b
), a weighting of order
r=2
is described by a submanifold
Q\subseteq T_{1}M
.
This suggests a generalization to weightings of arbitrary order, in terms of a sequence of submanifolds of higher tangent bundles.
Recall
[
16
]
that the
k
-th order tangent bundle

T_{k}M\to M

is the fiber bundle whose elements are the
k
-jets
j^{k}_{0}(\gamma)
of curves
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to M
.
The scalar multiplication of the monoid
(\mathbb{R},\cdot)
on
\mathbb{R}
defines an action on curves
\gamma
, which descends to a
monoid action
t\mapsto\kappa_{t}
(as in Example
2.3
(
d
)) on
T_{k}M
. The fixed point set for this action is
M\subseteq T_{k}M
, embedded as jets of constant paths.

Every
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
gives rise to a sequence of lifts
f^{(j)}\in C^{\infty}(T_{k}M)
for
j\leq k
, by

f^{(j)}(j^{r}_{0}(\gamma))=\frac{1}{j!}\frac{d^{j}}{{\mbox{d}}t^{j}}|_{t=0}f(\gamma(t)).

Given local coordinates
x_{a}
on
U\subseteq M
, the lifts
x_{a}^{(j)}
with
0\leq j\leq k
define coordinates on
T_{k}M|_{U}
.

Suppose now that the filtration (
1
) defines an order
r
weighting of
M
along
N
. Let

Q\subseteq T_{r-1}M

be the subset cut out by lifts
f^{(i-1)}
of functions
f
of filtration degree

i\leq r
.
It is shown in
[
17
]
that
Q
is a submanifold, and that the weighting may be recovered from
Q
by letting

(10)

C^{\infty}(M)_{(i)}=\{f\in C^{\infty}(M)|\ f^{(i-1)}|_{Q}=0\}

for
i\leq r
.

This raises the question of which submanifolds
Q\subseteq T_{r-1}M
define weightings. A rather complicated answer was given in
[
17
]
; a simpler criterion was recently found by Aaron Gootjes-Dreesbach
[
11
]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

(See
[
11
, page 16]
.)
A closed submanifold
Q\subseteq T_{r-1}M
defines a weighting of order
r
if and only if:

(a)

The vanishing ideal
\mathcal{I}_{Q}
is generated by functions of the form
f^{(i)}
.

(b)

If
f^{(i)}\in\mathcal{I}_{Q}
then also
f^{(j)}\in\mathcal{I}_{Q}
for all
j\leq i
.

Proof.

Suppose
Q\subseteq T_{r-1}M
satisfies these properties. Consider the direct sum decomposition

(11)

T^{*}(T_{r-1}M)|_{M}=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{r-1}(T^{*}(T_{r-1}M)|_{M})^{j}

where the
j
-th summand is spanned by
{\mbox{d}}f^{(j)}|_{M}
for
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
. In local coordinates
x_{a}
on
M
,

{\mbox{d}}f^{(j)}|_{M}=\sum_{a}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{a}}{\mbox{d}}x_{a}^{(j)}|_{M};

this shows that there are isomorphisms

(12)

(T^{*}(T_{r-1}M)|_{M})^{j}\to T^{*}M

taking
{\mbox{d}}f^{(j)}|_{M}
to
{\mbox{d}}f
. The the conormal bundle to
Q
along
N=Q\cap M
,

(13)

\operatorname{ann}(TQ)|_{N}\subseteq T^{*}(T_{r-1}M)|_{M}

is spanned by differentials
{\mbox{d}}g|_{N}
for functions
g\in C^{\infty}(T_{r-1}M)
such that
g|_{Q}=0
.
Condition (
a
) shows that it is already spanned by differentials of functions of the form
f^{(j)}
; hence

\operatorname{ann}(TQ)|_{N}
is a graded subbundle:

\operatorname{ann}(TQ)|_{N}=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{r-1}(\operatorname{ann}(TQ)|_{N})^{j}.

Let
(T^{*}M|_{N})_{(j+1)}
be the image of its
j
-th summand under (
12
), and put
(T^{*}M|_{N})_{(0)}=T^{*}M|_{N}
. Condition (
b
) shows that this defines a filtration (
2
) of
T^{*}M|_{N}
.
Let

k_{i}=n-\operatorname{rank}(T^{*}M|_{N})_{(i)}

so that
\dim N=k_{0}\leq\cdots\leq k_{r}=n=\dim M
. To show that the filtration
(
10
) gives a weighting, we construct weighted coordinates
near any given
p\in N\subseteq M
.
Choose functions

x_{a},\ a>k_{r-1},

defined near
p
, such that the lifts
x_{a}^{(r-1)}
vanish on
Q
and such that

{\mbox{d}}x_{a}^{(r-1)}|_{p},\ a>k_{r-1}

are a basis of
(\operatorname{ann}(TQ)|_{p})^{r-1}
. By (
b
) the functions

x_{a}^{(r-2)}
vanish on

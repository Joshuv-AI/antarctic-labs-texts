Lusin approximation for functions of bounded variation
Panu Lahti, Khanh Nguyen

Abstract
. We prove a Lusin approximation of functions of bounded variation. If f f is a function of bounded variation on an open set Ω ⊂ X \Omega\subset X , where X = ( X , d , μ ) X=(X,d,\mu) is a given complete doubling metric measure space supporting a 1 1 -Poincaré inequality, then for every ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 , there exist a function f ε f_{\varepsilon} on Ω \Omega and an open set U ε ⊂ Ω U_{\varepsilon}\subset\Omega such that the following properties hold true: (1) Cap 1 ​ ( U ε ) < ε {\rm Cap}_{1}(U_{\varepsilon})<\varepsilon ; (2) ‖ f − f ε ‖ BV ⁡ ( Ω ) < ε \|f-f_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathrm{BV}(\Omega)}<\varepsilon ; (3) f ∨ ≡ f ε ∨ f^{\vee}\equiv f_{\varepsilon}^{\vee} and f ∧ ≡ f ε ∧ f^{\wedge}\equiv f_{\varepsilon}^{\wedge} on Ω ∖ U ε \Omega\setminus U_{\varepsilon} ; (4) f ε ∨ f_{\varepsilon}^{\vee} is upper semicontinuous on Ω \Omega , and f ε ∧ f_{\varepsilon}^{\wedge} is lower semicontinuous on Ω \Omega . If the space X X is unbounded, then such an approximating function f ε f_{\varepsilon} can be constructed with the additional property that the uniform limit at infinity of both f ε ∨ f^{\vee}_{\varepsilon} and f ε ∧ f^{\wedge}_{\varepsilon} is 0 0 . Moreover, when X = ℝ d X={\mathbb{R}}^{d} , we show that the non-centered maximal function of f ε f_{\varepsilon} is continuous in Ω \Omega .

Introduction

In this paper, we consider a complete doubling metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)

supporting a
p
-Poincaré inequality, where
1\leq p<\infty
; see
Subsection
2.2
for the relevant definitions.
For
1\leq p<\infty
, we say that a function

f\in L^{p}(X)
is a
p
-Hajłasz-Sobolev function on
(X,d,\mu)
, denoted by

f\in M^{1,p}(X)
, if there exists a

p
-integrable function
g:X\to[0,\infty]
such that

(1.1)

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq d(x,y)\big(g(x)+g(y)\big)\text{\rm\ \ for $\mu$-a.e.}\ x,y\in X.

If
f\in M^{1,p}(X)
, then the following
M^{1,p}
-norm

\|f\|_{M^{1,p}(X)}:=\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)}+\inf\|g\|_{L^{p}(X)}

is finite, where the infimum is taken over all nonnegative functions
g
for which the pair
(f,g)
satisfies the pointwise inequality (
1.1
).
Let
Q>1
be a doubling dimension defined as in (
2.3
). If
p>Q
, then every

p
-Hajłasz-Sobolev function is locally Hölder continuous on
X
by the Morrey inequality,
see e.g.
[
HKi98
, Proposition 4.3]
.

For
1\leq p\leq Q
, we say that
f
is
p
-quasi Hölder continuous on
X
if for every

\varepsilon>0
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
, there is a set
E_{\varepsilon}
with
{\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}^{Q-(1-\alpha)p}(E_{\varepsilon})<\varepsilon
so that the
restriction

f|_{X\setminus E_{\varepsilon}}
is
\alpha
-Hölder
continuous. Here
{\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}^{s}
is the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff
content.
In the case
1<p\leq Q
, Hajłasz and Kinnunen
[
HKi98
, Theorem 5.3]
showed
that every function
f\in M^{1,p}(X)
is
p
-quasi Hölder continuous on
X
, and
furthermore has a
Lusin approximation as follows. For every
f\in M^{1,p}(X)
, where
1<p\leq Q
, and
for every
\varepsilon>0
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
, there are an open set
U_{\varepsilon}

and a function
f_{\varepsilon}
on
X
so that the following properties hold true:

(1)

{\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}^{Q-(1-\alpha)p}(U_{\varepsilon})<\varepsilon;

(2)

\|f-f_{\varepsilon\|_{M^{1,p}(X)}}<\varepsilon;

(3)

f\equiv f_{\varepsilon}
on
X\setminus U_{\varepsilon}
;

(4)

f_{\varepsilon}
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous on
X
.

In 2007, Kinnunen and Tuominen
[
KT07
, Theorem 4]
obtained the same result for

1
-Hajłasz-Sobolev functions
f\in M^{1,1}(X)
. For higher order or fractional Hajłasz-Sobolev functions, analogous results have been obtained in
[
Ma93
,
BHS02
,
Sw02
,
KP08
,
HT16
]
.

Newtonian (Sobolev) functions
f\in N^{1,p}(X)
are known to be
p
-quasicontinuous,
where the exceptional set is defined using the
p
-capacity instead of Hausdorff contents.
We refer to Subsection
2.1
for the definitions of Newtonian spaces and capacities.
Kinnunen, Korte, Shanmugalingam and Tuominen
[
KKST12
, Section 7]
proved a
Lusin approximation for Newtonian spaces
N^{1,p}(X)
, where
1\leq p<\infty
. Namely,
for every

f\in N^{1,p}(X)
and for every
\varepsilon>0
, there are an open set
U_{\varepsilon}

and a function
f_{\varepsilon}
so that the following properties hold:

(1)

{\rm Cap}_{p}(U_{\varepsilon})<\varepsilon;

(2)

\|f-f_{\varepsilon\|_{N^{1,p}(X)}}<\varepsilon;

(3)

f\equiv f_{\varepsilon}
on
X\setminus U_{\varepsilon}
;

(4)

f_{\varepsilon}
is continuous on
X
.

A similar result was previously shown in Euclidean spaces in
[
MZ82
]
and
[
Sw07
]
.

For functions of bounded variation, a
1
-quasicontinuity property is too much to hope for.
However, in 2017 the first author and Shanmugalingam
[
LS17
, Theorem 1.1]

proved that for every

f\in\mathrm{BV}(X)
, the upper approximate limit
f^{\vee}
and the lower approximate limit

f^{\wedge}
are respectively
1
-quasi upper and lower semicontinuous on
X
, see
Subsection
2.3
for these definitions.
On the other hand, the semicontinuity properties may fail in a very large set.
Consider the function

(1.2)

f=\chi_{E}{\rm\ \ with\ \ }E:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}B(q_{j},2^{-j}),

where
q_{j}
is a sequence of all points in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with rational coordinates.
Here
\chi_{E}
denotes the characteristic function of
E
, and
B(x,r)
denotes the ball centered at
x
of radius
r>0
.
Let
\mathcal{L}^{2}
be the

2
-dimensional Lebesgue measure.
This
f
is easily seen to be a BV function with

\mathcal{L}^{2}(E)\leq\pi/2
and with total variation
\|Df\|(\mathbb{R}^{2})\leq 2\pi
,
which is defined in (
2.5
).
Now we have
f^{\vee}(x)<1
for all
x\in O_{E}
, where
O_{E}
is
the measure-theoretic exterior of
E
defined in (
2.7
), but then there
is
q_{j}\to x
so that
f^{\vee}(q_{j})\equiv 1
; hence
f^{\vee}
is not upper semicontinuous on

O_{E}
, where
{\rm Cap}_{1}(O_{E})\geq\mathcal{L}^{2}(O_{E})=\infty
.
Similarly,
(-f)^{\wedge}
is not lower semicontinuous on
O_{E}
.
Nonetheless, for a suitable subset
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
{\rm Cap}_{1}(G)

smaller than any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, the
restrictions

f^{\vee}|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus G}
and
f^{\wedge}|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus G}
are
respectively upper and lower semicontinuous.

In
[
La18
, Page 521]
,
an open problem was posed
on whether it is possible
to find a Lusin approximation for a given BV function, satisfying the
upper/lower semicontinuity properties in the entire space.
In the following theorem, we give a positive answer to the open problem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
(X,d,\mu)
is a complete doubling metric measure space supporting a

1
-Poincaré inequality. Let
f\in{\mathrm{BV}}(\Omega)
, where
\Omega
is an
open subset of
X
. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exist a function

f_{\varepsilon}
on
\Omega
and an open set
U_{\varepsilon}\subset\Omega

such that the following properties hold true:

(1)

{\rm Cap}_{1}(U_{\varepsilon})<\varepsilon
;

(2)

\|f-f_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathrm{BV}(\Omega)}<\varepsilon
;

(3)

f^{\vee}\equiv f_{\varepsilon}^{\vee}
and
f^{\wedge}\equiv f_{\varepsilon}^{\wedge}
on
\Omega\setminus U_{\varepsilon}
;

(4)

f_{\varepsilon}^{\vee}
is upper semicontinuous on
\Omega
, and
f_{\varepsilon}^{\wedge}
is lower semicontinuous on
\Omega
.

A somewhat similar result was given previously in Euclidean spaces
in
[
Gro08
, Theorem 6.7]
,
and in the special case of characteristic functions of sets of finite perimeter in

[
La18
, Theorem 5.2]
.
In the case of Newtonian/Sobolev functions, one usually employs a
discrete convolution
f_{\varepsilon}
defined by

f_{\varepsilon}:=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B_{j})}\int_{B_{j}}f\,d\mu\right)\varphi_{j}{\rm\ \ on\ }U_{\varepsilon},

where
\{B_{i}\}
is a covering of
U_{\varepsilon}
by balls and
\{\varphi_{i}\}
is a partition of unity associated with the covering. However, when
f
is only a
\mathrm{BV}
function,
the semicontinuity property (4) may fail at points on the boundary of
U_{\varepsilon}
which
are approximate jump points of
f
.
Therefore, in this
paper, we modify the above definition of discrete convolution and use
approximating Lipschitz functions and
“boundary truncation” in place of integral averages to obtain the semicontinuity
property on the boundary of
U_{\varepsilon}
.

Recently, the behavior at infinity of BV functions on complete doubling
unbounded metric measure spaces supporting a
1
-Poincaré inequality was studied by the two
authors in

[
LN24
]
. Indeed, for every
f\in\mathrm{BV}(X)
, we have the limits

\lim_{t\to\infty}f^{\vee}(\gamma(t))=\lim_{t\to\infty}f^{\wedge}(\gamma(t))=0

for
1
-almost every infinite curve
\gamma
.
Here the notion of
1
-a.e. curve is defined in Subsection
2.1
, and a locally rectifiable
curve
\gamma
is said to be an infinite curve if
\gamma\setminus B\neq\emptyset
for every
ball
B
. For the function
f_{\varepsilon}
constructed in Theorem
1.1
,
we can obtain the additional property that both
f^{\vee}_{\varepsilon}
and
f^{\wedge}_{\varepsilon}

have a genuine limit
0
at infinity. We write

\Omega\ni x\to\infty
to mean that
d(O,x)\to\infty
for a fixed point
O\in X
and
x\in\Omega
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Suppose that
(X,d,\mu)
is a complete doubling unbounded metric measure space
supporting a
1
-Poincaré inequality. Let
f\in\mathrm{BV}(\Omega)
for a given unbounded
open subset
\Omega
of
X
. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
function
f_{\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{BV}(\Omega)
satisfying the properties given in Theorem

1.1
, and the limits

(1.3)

\lim_{\Omega\ni x\to\infty}f_{\varepsilon}^{\vee}(x)=\lim_{\Omega\ni x\to\infty}f_{\varepsilon}^{\wedge}(x)=0

hold true.

In
[
La21
]
, the non-centered maximal function of a BV function
f
,
denoted by
\mathcal{M}f
, was shown to be
1
-quasicontinuous in the Euclidean space

\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
As noted in
[
La21
, Example 4.5]
, the non-centered maximal function of the
BV function
f
defined as in (
1.2
) on the plane is discontinuous at
all measure-theoretic exterior points
x
of
E
(which constitute a set of infinite
2
-dimensional
Lebesgue measure), because
\mathcal{M}f(q_{j})=1
and
\mathcal{M}f(x)<1
for all
j
and for all
such
x
. In Example
5.1
we will show that in a complete doubling metric measure space
supporting a
1
-Poincaré inequality, the non-centered maximal function of a BV function
may fail to be
1
-quasicontinuous.
Moreover, in this example, we necessarily have
f_{\varepsilon}\equiv f
in Theorem
1.1
.

Due to all of this, it is natural to ask under what conditions the non-centered maximal function
of a given BV function
f
on an
open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is guaranteed to be continuous.
The upper semicontinuity of
f_{\varepsilon}^{\vee}
given in Theorem
1.1
,
while seemingly a rather weak property, is essentially what is required to guarantee continuity
of the maximal function.
Indeed, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an arbitrary nonempty open set, with
d\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
f\in\mathrm{BV}(\Omega)
be nonnegative. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
, there is a function

f_{\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{BV}(\Omega)
satisfying the properties given in Theorem
1.1
,
and such that
\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}
is continuous in
\Omega
.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we adopt the following conventions.
We work in a metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
, where
\mu
is a nontrivial locally finite Borel regular outer measure on
X
.
We assume that
X
consists of at least two points.
The notation
A\lesssim B\ (A\gtrsim B)
means that there is a constant
C>0
depending
only on the space such that
A\leq C\cdot B\ (A\geq C\cdot B)
, and
A\approx B

means that
A\lesssim B
and
A\gtrsim B
.
For every
\mu
-measurable subset
A\subset X
of strictly positive
and finite measure, and every
f\in L^{1}(A)
,
we let
f_{A}:=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{A}f\,d\mu=\frac{1}{\mu(A)}\int_{A}f\,d\mu
.
We denote an open ball centered at
x\in X
of radius
r>0
by
B(x,r)
,
and for
\tau>0
, we set
\tau B(x,r):=B(x,\tau r)
.
Given an open set
\Omega\subset X
, we say that
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
if
for every
x\in\Omega
there exists
r>0
such that
f\in L^{1}(B(x,r))
.
Other local function spaces are defined similarly.

2.1.
Modulus, capacity and Newtonian spaces

A
curve
is a nonconstant continuous mapping from an interval
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
into
X
.
A curve is
rectifiable
if its length is finite, and
locally rectifiable

if its restriction to every compact subinterval of
I
is rectifiable.
We assume every rectifiable curve
\gamma
to be parametrized by arc length
as
\gamma:[0,\ell_{\gamma}]\to X
, and then the curve integral of a nonnegative Borel
function
\rho
is given by

\int_{\gamma}\rho\,ds:=\int_{0}^{\ell_{\gamma}}\rho(\gamma(t))\,dt.

If
\gamma
is locally rectifiable, then we set

\int_{\gamma}\rho\,ds:=\sup\int_{\gamma^{\prime}}\rho\,ds,

where the supremum is taken over all rectifiable subcurves
\gamma^{\prime}
of
\gamma
.

Let
\Gamma
be a family of curves in
X
. Let
1\leq p<\infty
.
The
p
-modulus
of
\Gamma
is defined by

\text{\rm Mod}_{p}(\Gamma):=\inf\int_{X}\rho^{p}\,d\mu,

where the infimum is taken over all Borel functions
\rho:X\to[0,{\infty}]
satisfying

\int_{\gamma}\rho\,ds\geq 1
for every locally rectifiable curve
\gamma\in\Gamma
.
A family of curves is called
p
-exceptional
if it has
p
-modulus zero. We say
that a property holds for
p
-a.e. curve
(or
p
-almost every curve) if the
collection of curves for which the property fails is
p
-exceptional.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be open and let
f:\Omega\to[-\infty,\infty]
be measurable.
A Borel function
\rho:\Omega\to[0,{\infty}]
is said to be an
upper gradient
of
f
in

\Omega
if

(2.1)

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq\int_{\gamma}\rho\,ds

for every rectifiable curve
\gamma
in
\Omega
connecting
x
and
y
.
For
1\leq p<\infty
, we say that a
\mu
-measurable function
\rho:\Omega\to[0,{\infty}]

is a
p
-weak upper gradient

of
f
if (
2.1
) holds for
p
-a.e. rectifiable curve.
If
f
has a
p
-weak upper gradient in
\Omega

that is in
L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{p}(\Omega)
, we denote by
g_{f}
the

minimal
p
-weak upper gradient
of
f
in
\Omega
,
which exists and is unique up to sets of measure zero,
and is minimal in the sense that
g_{f}\leq\rho
a.e. for every

\rho\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{p}(\Omega)
that is a
p
-weak upper gradient of
f
in
\Omega
.
In
[
H03
]
, the existence and uniqueness of such a minimal

p
-weak upper gradient are given. The notion of upper gradients is due to Heinonen and Koskela

[
HK98
]
, and we refer interested readers to
[
BB11
,
H03
,
HK98
,
HKST15
,
N00
]
for a more
detailed discussion.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, the Newtonian space, denoted by
N^{1,p}(\Omega)
, is the collection of
all
p
-integrable functions on
\Omega
whose minimal
p
-weak upper gradient is
also in
L^{p}(\Omega)
.
The Newtonian norm is defined by

\|f\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}:=\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|g_{f}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

The
p
-capacity
of a set
K\subset X
is defined by

\text{\rm Cap}_{p}(K):=\inf\left(\int_{X}|u|^{p}\,d\mu+\int_{X}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu\right),

where the infimum is taken over all functions
u\in N^{1,p}(X)
such that
u|_{K}\equiv 1
.

2.2.
Doubling measures and Poincaré inequalities

The Borel regular outer measure
\mu
is said to be
doubling
if
there exists a finite constant
C_{d}\geq 1
such that for all balls
B(x,r)

with radius
r>0
and center
x\in X
, we have

(2.2)

0<\mu(B(x,2r))\leq C_{d}\mu(B(x,r))<\infty.

Here
C_{d}
is called the
doubling constant
.

By iterating the doubling condition (
2.2
), we obtain a relative lower volume
decay of order
0<Q<\infty
: there is a constant
0<C_{Q}<\infty
such that

(2.3)

\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{Q}\leq C_{Q}\frac{\mu(B(y,r))}{\mu(B(x,R))}

for all
0<r\leq R<\infty
and
y\in B(x,R)
. The choice
Q=\log_{2}(C_{d})
works, but a smaller value of
Q
might satisfy the above condition as well. We may assume that
Q>1
.

Let
1\leq p<{\infty}
. We say that
X
supports a
p
-Poincaré inequality

if there exist finite constants
C_{P}>0
and
\lambda\geq 1
such that

(2.4)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}|f-f_{B(x,r)}|\,d\mu\leq C_{P}r\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x,\lambda r)}\rho^{p}\,d\mu\right)^{1/p}

for all balls
B(x,r)
with radius
r>0
and center
x\in X
, and for all pairs

(f,\rho)
satisfying (
2.1
) such that
f
is integrable on balls.
Here
\lambda
is called the
scaling constant
or
scaling factor

of the Poincaré inequality,
and
C_{P}
is called the constant of the Poincaré inequality.
For more on Poincaré inequalities, we refer the interested reader to
[
HK95
,
HK00
,
Hei01
,
HKST15
]
.

We will assume throughout the paper that
X
is complete,
\mu
is doubling, and that
X
supports
a
1
-Poincaré inequality.
It is customary to express this by saying that

(X,d,\mu)
is a complete doubling metric measure space supporting a
1
-Poincaré inequality.

From
[
La18
, Lemma 3.5]
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
G\subset X
.
Then there exist a finite constant
C_{1}>0

and an open set
U\supset G
such that

{\rm Cap}_{1}(U)\leq C_{1}{\rm Cap}_{1}(G)+\varepsilon
and

\frac{\mu(B(x,r)\cap G)}{\mu(B(x,r))}\to 0

as
r\to 0
uniformly for all
x\in X\setminus U
.

2.3.
Functions of bounded variation

In this subsection, we recall the definition and basic properties of functions of bounded variation on metric spaces as in
[
Mi03
]
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be open and let
f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. We define the total variation
of
f
in
\Omega
by

(2.5)

\|Df\|(\Omega):=\inf\left\{\liminf_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}g_{f_{i}}\,d\mu:f_{i}\in{\rm Lip}_{\rm loc}(\Omega),f_{i}\to f{\rm\ in\ }L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\right\}

where each
g_{f_{i}}
is the minimal
1
-weak upper gradient of
f_{i}
in
\Omega
. Here
{\rm Lip}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
is the collection of locally Lipschitz functions on
\Omega
.
We say that an integrable function
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
is of bounded variation, denoted

f\in\mathrm{BV}(\Omega)
, if the total variation
\|Df\|(\Omega)
is finite.
For an arbitrary subset
A\subset X
, we define

\|Df\|(A):=\inf\{\|Df\|(W):A\subset W,\,W\subset X{\rm\ is\ open}\}.

By
[
Mi03
, Theorem 3.4]
,
\|Df\|
is a Radon measure on a given open set
\Omega
for

f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
with
\|Df\|(\Omega)<\infty
.
The
\mathrm{BV}
norm is defined by

\|f\|_{\mathrm{BV}(\Omega)}:=\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|Df\|(\Omega).

From
[
KKLS13
, Lemma 2.2]
, we have the following version of the Poincaré inequality.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
(X,d,\mu)
is a complete doubling metric measure space supporting a
1
-Poincaré inequality. Let
f\in\mathrm{BV}(X)
and let
B
be a ball so that
\mu(\{x\in B:|f(x)|>0\})<c_{1}\mu(B)
for some
0<c_{1}<1
. Then

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B}|f|\,d\mu\leq C_{2}\frac{r}{1-c_{1}^{1/Q}}\frac{\|Df\|(\lambda B)}{\mu(B)},

where the constant
C_{2}>0
only depends on the constants of the doubling condition and
the Poincaré inequality. Here
\lambda\geq 1
is the scaling constant of the
Poincaré inequality, and
Q>1
is the order of the relative lower volume
decay (
2.3
).

A
\mu
-measurable set
E\subset X
is said to be of finite perimeter if
\|D\chi_{E}\|(X)<\infty
,
where
\chi_{E}
is the characteristic function of
E
. The perimeter of
E

in
A\subset X
is denoted by

P(E,A):=\|D\chi_{E}\|(A).

We have the following coarea formula from
[
Mi03
, Proposition 4.2]
:
if
f\in\mathrm{BV}(\Omega)
for
\Omega\subset X
open and
F\subset\Omega
is a Borel set, then

(2.6)

\|Df\|(F)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}P(\{f>t\},F)\,dt.

The measure-theoretic interior
I_{E}
and exterior
O_{E}
of a set
E\subset X
are defined respectively by

I_{E}:=\left\{x\in X:\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(B(x,r)\setminus E)}{\mu(B(x,r))}=0\right\}

and

(2.7)

O_{E}:=\left\{x\in X:\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(B(x,r)\cap E)}{\mu(B(x,r))}=0\right\}.

The measure-theoretic boundary
\partial^{*}E
is defined as the set of points
x\in X
at which both
E
and its complement have strictly positive upper density, i.e.

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(B(x,r)\setminus E)}{\mu(B(x,r))}>0\ \ {\rm and\ \ }\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(B(x,r)\cap E)}{\mu(B(x,r))}>0.

Then
X=I_{E}\cup O_{E}\cup\partial^{*}E
.

We define the
codimension
1
Hausdorff content
of a set
A\subset X
, for
0<R\leq\infty
,
as follows:

{\mathcal{H}}_{R}(A):=\inf\left\{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mu(B_{k})}{r_{k}}:A\subset\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}B_{k}\ \ \text{and}\ \ 0<r_{k}<R\right\}.

The
codimension
1
Hausdorff measure
is then defined by

{\mathcal{H}}(A):=\lim_{R\to 0}{\mathcal{H}}_{R}(A)=\sup_{R>0}{\mathcal{H}}_{R}(A).

Given an open set
\Omega\subset X
and a
\mu
-measurable subset
E\subset X
with
P(E,\Omega)<\infty
, we have that for any Borel set
A\subset\Omega
,

(2.8)

P(E,A)=\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap A}\theta_{E}\,d\mathcal{H}

with
\theta_{E}:X\to[\alpha,C_{d}]
, where
C_{d}
is the doubling constant and

\alpha>0
depends only on
the doubling constant and constants of the
1
-Poincaré inequality,
see
[
Am02
, Theorem 5.3 and Theorem 5.4]

and
[
AMP04
, Therem 4.6]
.

The lower and upper approximate limits of a function
f
on
X
are defined respectively by

f^{\wedge}(x):=\sup\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}:\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(\{y\in B(x,r):f(y)<t\})}{\mu(B(x,r))}=0\right\}

and

f^{\vee}(x):=\inf\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}:\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(\{y\in B(x,r):f(y)>t\})}{\mu(B(x,r))}=0\right\}

for
x\in X
.
When studying the fine properties of functions of bounded variation, we consider the pointwise representatives
f^{\vee}
and
f^{\wedge}
.

Given an open set
\Omega\subset X
, we say that a function
f
is
1
-quasi
(lower/upper semi-) continuous on
\Omega
if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
an open set
G\subset X
such that
{\rm Cap}_{1}(G)<\varepsilon
and

f|_{\Omega\setminus G}
is finite and (lower/upper semi-) continuous.

By Corollary 4.2 in
[
La18
]
, or Theorem 1.1 in
[
LS17
]
, we obtain that
BV functions have a
1
-quasi semicontinuity property as below.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be open and let
f\in\mathrm{BV}(\Omega)
.
Then
f^{\wedge}
is
1
-quasi lower semicontinuous on
\Omega

and
f^{\vee}
is
1
-quasi upper semicontinuous on
\Omega
.

2.4.
Whitney decomposition

Recall that we assume
(X,d,\mu)
to be a doubling metric measure space supporting a
1
-Poincaré inequality, where

\lambda
is the scaling factor of the
1
-Poincaré inequality (
2.4
).
Given
x\in X
and
A\subset X
, we denote

\dist(x,A):=\inf\{d(x,y)\colon y\in A\}.

Let
U\subset X
be open. We can choose a Whitney-type covering
of
U
as follows. See e.g.
[
HKST15
, Proposition 4.1.15]
for a similar construction; this is based on
earlier works, e.g.
[
CW71
]
.
We have a Whitney-type covering
\{B_{j}:=B(x_{j},r_{j})\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
U
such that

(1)

for each
j\in\mathbb{N},

r_{j}=\min\left\{1,\frac{{\rm dist}(x_{j},X\setminus U)}{8\lambda}\right\};

(2)

for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the ball
2\lambda B_{k}
meets at most
C_{0}:=C_{0}(C_{d},\lambda)
balls
2\lambda B_{j}
(that is, a bounded overlap property holds);

(3)

if
2\lambda B_{j}
meets
2\lambda B_{k}
, then
r_{j}\leq 2r_{k}
.

Given such a covering of
U
, we can take a partition of unity
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
subordinate to the
covering, such that
0\leq\varphi_{j}\leq 1
, each
\varphi_{j}
is a
C_{W}/r_{j}
-Lipschitz function for some constant

C_{W}\geq 1
, and
\supp\varphi_{j}\subset 2B_{j}
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, see e.g.

[
HKST15
, p. 104]
or
[
BBS07
]
.

2.5.
Functions of bounded variation in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

In this subsection, we consider the Euclidean theory that will be needed
(only) in Section
5
. Consider the
d
-dimensional Euclidean space

\mathbb{R}^{d}=(\mathbb{R}^{d},|\cdot|,\mathcal{L}^{d})
equipped with the Euclidean distance
|\cdot|
and the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{d}
, with
d\in\mathbb{N}
.
We follow the monograph
[
AFP00
]
.
We always let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open set.
We let the class
\mathrm{BV}(\Omega)
be defined as in Subsection
2.3
.

Let
f\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\Omega)
.
For
x\in\Omega
, we say that
\widetilde{f}(x)\in\mathbb{R}
is a Lebesgue
representative of
f
at
x
if

(2.9)

\lim_{r\to 0}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)-\widetilde{f}(x)|\,dy=0.

We denote by
S_{f}\subset\Omega
the set where such a representative does not exist
and call it the approximate
discontinuity set or the set of non-Lebesgue points.
Given
x\in\Omega
,
r>0
, and a point on the unit sphere
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, we define the half-balls

B_{\nu}^{+}(x,r):=\{y\in B(x,r):\langle y-x,\nu\rangle>0\}{\rm\ \ and\ \ }B_{\nu}^{-}(x,r):=\{y\in B(x,r):\langle y-x,\nu\rangle<0\}.

Here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the Euclidean inner product.
We say that
x\in\Omega
is an approximate jump point of
f
if there is
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and distinct numbers
f^{+}(x),f^{-}(x)\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(2.10)

\lim_{r\to 0}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{\nu}^{+}(x,r)}|f(y)-f^{+}(x)|\,dy=0

and

(2.11)

\lim_{r\to 0}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{\nu}^{-}(x,r)}|f(y)-f^{-}(x)|\,dy=0.

The set of all approximate jump points is denoted by
J_{f}\subset\Omega
. By
[
AFP00
, Theorem 3.78]
,
we know that
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(S_{f}\setminus J_{f})=0
, where
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
is
the
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By
[
HK10
, Theorem 4.3 and Theorem 5.1]
,
it follows that

(2.12)

{\rm Cap}_{1}(S_{f}\setminus J_{f})=0.

Note that for all
x\in\Omega\setminus S_{f}
,
we have
\widetilde{f}(x)=f^{\vee}(x)=f^{\wedge}(x)
where
f^{\vee}
and
f^{\wedge}

are respectively the upper and
lower approximate limits of
f
defined as in Subsection
2.3
.
Furthermore, for all
x\in J_{f}
,
we have that
f^{\vee}(x)=\max\{f^{-}(x),f^{+}(x)\}
and
f^{\wedge}(x)=\min\{f^{-}(x),f^{+}(x)\}
.
In total,

(2.13)

\begin{cases}f^{\vee}(x)=\max\{f^{-}(x),f^{+}(x),\widetilde{f}(x)\}\\
f^{\wedge}(x)=\min\{f^{-}(x),f^{+}(x),\widetilde{f}(x)\}\end{cases}{\rm\ \ for\ all\ }x\in(\Omega\setminus S_{f})\cup J_{f}.

(The number
\widetilde{f}(x)
is only defined for
x\in\Omega\setminus S_{f}
and
the numbers
f^{-}(x),f^{+}(x)
are only defined for
x\in J_{f}
, but the above maximum and minimum
have the obvious interpretations.)
The non-centered maximal function of a function
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
is defined by

\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}f(x):=\sup_{x\in B(z,r)\subset\Omega}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(z,r)}|f|\,dy,\quad x\in\Omega.

If
\Omega=X
, we omit it.
By
[
La21
, Lemma 3.1]
, we have that for
x\in\Omega
,

(2.14)

\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}f(x)=\sup_{x\in\overline{B}(z,r),\,B(z,r)\subset\Omega}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(z,r)}|f|\,dy.

Here
\overline{B}(z,r)
denotes the closed ball.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

This section is devoted to proving our main Theorem
1.1
.
Recall that we assume that
(X,d,\mu)
is a complete doubling metric measure space supporting
a
1
-Poincaré inequality.

We recall also that
C_{d}\geq 1,\lambda\geq 1
are, respectively, the doubling constant and the
scaling factor of the
1
-Poincaré inequality (
2.4
). The constant of the relative
lower volume decay (
2.3
) is
C_{Q}>0
, with
Q>1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary; without loss of generality we can assume that

\varepsilon\leq 1
. We consider
f\in{\mathrm{BV}}(\Omega)
, where
\Omega\subset X
is open.
By Proposition
2.3
,
there is
an open subset
G
of
\Omega
with
{\rm Cap}_{1}(G)<\varepsilon/C_{1}
, where

C_{1}
is the constant from Lemma
2.1
, and
so that
f^{\vee}|_{\Omega\setminus G}
and
f^{\wedge}|_{\Omega\setminus G}
are respectively upper and lower semicontinuous.

By Lemma
2.1
, we find
0<R<1
and an open set
U^{\prime}
with

G\subset U^{\prime}\subset X
and

{\rm Cap}_{1}(U^{\prime})<\varepsilon\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \frac{\mu(B(x,r)\cap G)}{\mu(B(x,r))}<\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{(20\lambda)^{Q}C_{Q}}\right\}

for all
x\in X\setminus U^{\prime}
and
0<r<R<1
.
Having chosen
\varepsilon
smaller if necessary,
which we can do without loss of generality, we can also assume that
X\setminus U^{\prime}
is nonempty.

The variation measure is absolutely continuous with respect to the
1
-capacity in the following sense.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
La18
, Lemma 3.8]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open set and
let
f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
with
\|Df\|(\Omega)<\infty
. Then for every

\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that if
A\subset\Omega
with
{\rm Cap}_{1}(A)<\delta
,
then
\|Df\|(A)<\varepsilon^{\prime}
.

By this lemma, we may also assume that

\|Df\|(U^{\prime}\cap\Omega)<\varepsilon
. Then let
U:=U^{\prime}\cap\Omega
.
We gather all of the known properties:

(3.1)

{\rm Cap}_{1}(U)<\varepsilon,\ \ \|Df\|(U)<\varepsilon,\ \ \frac{\mu(B(x,r)\cap G)}{\mu(B(x,r))}<\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{(20\lambda)^{Q}C_{Q}}\right\}

for all
x\in\Omega\setminus U
and
0<r<R<1
,
and

(3.2)

f^{\vee}|_{\Omega\setminus G}\ \textrm{ and }\ f^{\wedge}|_{\Omega\setminus G}\quad\textrm{are respectively upper and lower semicontinuous.}

Let
\{B_{j}:=B(x_{j},r_{j})\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be the Whitney balls and the partition of unity defined as in Subsection
2.4
.
Let

(3.3)

U_{1}:=\left\{x\in U:\dist(x,\Omega\setminus U)<\frac{\min\{R,{\rm dist}(x,X\setminus\Omega)\}}{100\lambda}\right\}

and
U_{2}:=U\setminus U_{1}
.
Let

J_{1}:=\{j\in\mathbb{N}:x_{j}\in U_{1}\}

and
J_{2}:=\mathbb{N}\setminus J_{1}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For all
j\in J_{1}
, we have

\mu(B_{j}\cap G)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu(B_{j}).

Proof.

Let
j\in J_{1}
.
We find a point
x_{j}^{\prime}\in X\setminus U
such that

\dist(x_{j},X\setminus U)=d(x_{j},x_{j}^{\prime})
.
Since
x_{j}\in U_{1}
, we necessarily have
x_{j}^{\prime}\in\Omega\setminus U
.
Since
d(x_{j},X\setminus U)=8\lambda r_{j}
, we have

B_{j}\subset B(x_{j}^{\prime},9\lambda r_{j}).

It follows from the property of the relative lower volume decay and the estimate (
3.1
)
that

\displaystyle\mu(B_{j}\cap G)

\displaystyle\leq\mu(B(x_{j}^{\prime},9\lambda r_{j})\cap G)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{equ3.1-28Sep}}{\leq}\frac{1}{(20\lambda)^{Q}C_{Q}}\mu(B(x_{j}^{\prime},9\lambda r_{j}))

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{doubling-dim}}{\leq}\frac{1}{(20\lambda)^{Q}C_{Q}}C_{Q}\left(\frac{9\lambda r_{j}}{r_{j}}\right)^{Q}\mu(B_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu(B_{j}).

The proof is completed.
∎

We now construct the function
f_{\varepsilon}
and show that
it satisfies the properties given in the theorem.
Note that for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
we have
2\lambda B_{j}\Subset U\subset\Omega
and then

f\in L^{1}(2\lambda B_{j})
.
Moreover, in the definition of the total variation
\|Df\|(2\lambda B_{j})
, one can consider
approximating locally Lipschitz function converging in
L^{1}(2\lambda B_{j})
and
not only in
L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(2\lambda B_{j})
, see e.g.
[
La20
, Lemma 3.2]
.
Applying also Egorov’s theorem, for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
we find a function

f_{j}\in\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathrm{loc}}(2\lambda B_{j})

and a set

N_{j}\subset 2B_{j}
such that

(3.4)

\begin{cases}\|Df_{j}\|(2\lambda B_{j})\leq\|Df\|(2\lambda B_{j})+2^{-j}\varepsilon,\\
\|f_{j}-f\|_{L^{1}({2\lambda B_{j}})}\leq 2^{-j}\varepsilon r_{j},\\
|f_{j}(y)-f(y)|\leq 2^{-j}{\rm\ \ for\ all\ }y\in 2B_{j}\setminus N_{j},\\
\mu(N_{j})\leq 2^{-j}\min\{1,\mu(B_{j})/100\}.\end{cases}

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we let

(3.5)

a_{j}(x):=\begin{cases}\max\{m_{j},\min\{M_{j},f_{j}(x)\}\},&\ \ {\rm if\ }j\in J_{1},\\
f_{j}(x),&{\rm\ \ if\ }j\in J_{2},\end{cases}

for
x\in 2B_{j}
, where
m_{j},M_{j}
are defined by

(3.6)

m_{j}:={\rm essinf}_{x\in 2B_{j}\setminus(G\cup N_{j})}f_{j}(x)\ \ {\rm and}\ \ M_{j}:={\rm esssup}_{x\in 2B_{j}\setminus(G\cup N_{j})}f_{j}(x).

Note that
a_{j}
is continuous on
2B_{j}
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
We have from Lemma
3.2
and the fourth property of (
3.4
)
that for
j\in J_{1}
,

(3.7)

\mu(2B_{j}\setminus(G\cup N_{j}))\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{j}}\frac{1}{100}\right)\mu(B_{j})\geq\frac{\mu(B_{j})}{4}>0,

and so
M_{j},m_{j}
are well defined. We define the function
f_{\varepsilon}
on
\Omega
by setting

(3.8)

f_{\varepsilon}:=f\chi_{\Omega\setminus U}+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}a_{j}\varphi_{j},

where the functions
\varphi_{j}
are the partition of unity with respect to the
Whitney covering
\{B_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For all
x\in\Omega\setminus U
, we have
f_{\varepsilon}^{\vee}(x)=f^{\vee}(x)
and
f_{\varepsilon}^{\wedge}(x)=f^{\wedge}(x)
.

Proof.

Since
f_{\varepsilon}\equiv f
on
\Omega\setminus U
and
\Omega
is open, by the definition of upper and lower approximate limits we have that
f^{\vee}(x)=f^{\vee}_{\varepsilon}(x)
and
f^{\wedge}(x)=f^{\wedge}_{\varepsilon}(x)
for all
x\in\Omega\setminus\overline{U}
.

It remains to consider the case
x\in\partial U\cap\Omega
. Recall from (
3.1
) that

(3.9)

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(B(x,r)\cap G)}{\mu(B(x,r))}=0.

If a dilated Whitney ball
2B_{j}
intersects
B(x,r)
, then clearly

2B_{j}\subset B(x,2r)
.
Then the fourth property of (
3.4
) gives

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to 0}\sum_{j\in J_{1}:\,2B_{j}\cap B(x,r)\neq\emptyset}\frac{\mu(N_{j})}{\mu(B(x,r))}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{r\to 0}\sum_{j\in J_{1}:\,2B_{j}\cap B(x,r)\neq\emptyset}2^{-j}\frac{\mu(B_{j})}{\mu(B(x,r))}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu(B(x,2r))}{\mu(B(x,r))}\limsup_{r\to 0}\sum_{j\in J_{1}:\,2B_{j}\cap B(x,r)\neq\emptyset}2^{-j}=0,

since
2B_{j}\cap B(x,r)=\emptyset
for any fixed
j
and sufficiently small
r
.
Let
r>0
be so that
B(x,2r)\cap U\subset U_{1}
.
By the third property of (
3.4
), we have that for all
y\in B(x,r)\setminus\left(G\cup\cup_{j\in J_{1}}N_{j}\right)
,

\displaystyle|f_{\varepsilon}(y)-f(y)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in J_{1}:\,2B_{j}\cap B(x,r)\neq\emptyset}|a_{j}(y)-f(y)|\chi_{2B_{j}}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in J_{1}:\,2B_{j}\cap B(x,r)\neq\emptyset}|f_{j}(y)-f(y)|\chi_{2B_{j}\setminus(G\cup N_{j})}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in J_{1}:\,2B_{j}\cap B(x,r)\neq\emptyset}2^{-j}.

Notice that the last line of the above estimate converges to
0
as
r\to 0
. Let
\delta>0
be arbitrary; now there is
r_{\delta}>0
such that

|f_{\varepsilon}(y)-f(y)|<\delta

for all
y\in B(x,r)\setminus\left(G\cup\cup_{j\in J_{1}}N_{j}\right)\subset\Omega
with
0<r<r_{\delta}
.
Using this fact and then (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), we get

(3.11)

\begin{split}&\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(\{y\in B(x,r):|f_{\varepsilon}(y)-f(y)|>\delta\})}{\mu(B(x,r))}\\
&\quad\leq\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(B(x,r)\cap G)}{\mu(B(x,r))}+\limsup_{r\to 0}\sum_{j\in J_{1}:2B_{j}\cap B(x,r)\neq\emptyset}\frac{\mu(N_{j})}{\mu(B(x,r))}\\
&\quad=0.\end{split}

Thus we get

(3.12)

\begin{split}&\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(\{y\in B(x,r):f_{\varepsilon}(y)>f^{\vee}(x)+2\delta\})}{\mu(B(x,r))}\\
&\quad\leq\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(\{y\in B(x,r):|f_{\varepsilon}(y)-f(y)|+f(y)>f^{\vee}(x)+2\delta\})}{\mu(B(x,r))}\\
&\quad\leq\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(\{y\in B(x,r):f(y)>f^{\vee}(x)+\delta\})}{\mu(B(x,r))}\\
&\quad=0.\end{split}

As
\delta>0
was arbitrary, this implies that
f_{\varepsilon}^{\vee}(x)\leq f^{\vee}(x)
by
the definition of the upper approximate limit
f^{\vee}_{\varepsilon}(x)
. By symmetry of
f

and
f_{\varepsilon}
from (
3.11
), we also get

f^{\vee}(x)\leq f_{\varepsilon}^{\vee}(x)
and so
f^{\vee}(x)=f^{\vee}_{\varepsilon}(x)
.
Similarly, one obtains that
f^{\wedge}(x)=f^{\wedge}_{\varepsilon}(x)
. Then we conclude
that for all
x\in\Omega\setminus U
,
f_{\varepsilon}^{\vee}(x)=f^{\vee}(x)

and
f_{\varepsilon}^{\wedge}(x)=f^{\wedge}(x)
.

The proof is completed.
∎

We denote by
f_{+}:=\max\{0,f\}
the positive
part of the function
f
.

Lemma 3.4
.

We have
\|D(f_{\varepsilon}-f)\|(\Omega)\lesssim\varepsilon
.

Proof.

For any
v,w\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, it is straightforward to show from the definition of
the total variation that

(3.13)

\|D(u+v)\|(\Omega)\leq\|Du\|(\Omega)+\|Dv\|(\Omega).

By the first property of (
3.4
),
we have

(3.14)

\|D(a_{j}-f)\|(2B_{j})\leq\|Df_{j}\|(2\lambda B_{j})+\|Df\|(2\lambda B_{j})\leq 2\|Df\|(2\lambda B_{j})+2^{-j}\varepsilon.

Moreover, note that
\sum_{j=1}^{N}(a_{j}-f)\varphi_{j}\to\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}(a_{j}-f)\varphi_{j}

in
L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\Omega)
as
N\to\infty
, since the sum is locally finite.
Below, we will also use the Leibniz rule from
[
Lah20
, Theorem 1.2]
.
Since
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\varphi_{j}\equiv 1
on
U
,
we have

\displaystyle\|D(f_{\varepsilon}-f)\|(\Omega)

\displaystyle\qquad=\left\|D\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}(a_{j}-f)\varphi_{j}\right)\right\|(\Omega)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\left\|D\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}(a_{j}-f)\varphi_{j}\right)\right\|(\Omega)\quad\textrm{by lower semicontinuity}

We begin by giving an example showing that the conclusion of Corollary

Example 5.1
.

We will construct a metric measure space
X
on the plane. Let

{\bf O}:=(0,0),\ \ {\bf A}:=(0,1),\ \ {\bf C}:=(1,1)

be points in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We set
X:=[{\bf O},{\bf A}]\cup[{\bf O},{\bf C}]
where
[{\bf O},{\bf A}]

denotes the closed line segment connecting the points
{\bf O},{\bf A}
.
We equip
X
with
the Euclidean distance
d_{2}
and the
1
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{1}
. Then

(X,d_{2},\mathcal{H}^{1})
is complete,
\mathcal{H}^{1}
is doubling on
X
, and the space
supports a
1
-Poincaré inequality.
Let

f:=\chi_{[{\bf O},{\bf A}]}\in\mathrm{BV}(X).

Then we have for every
x\in({\bf O},{\bf A}]
, by taking
r:=\dist(x,[{\bf O},{\bf C}])
,

\mathcal{M}f(x)\geq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}f\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}=1.

Hence
\mathcal{M}f=1
on
({\bf O},{\bf A}]
. To prove that
\mathcal{M}f
is not continuous at
{\bf O}
,
it suffices to prove that
\mathcal{M}f({\bf O})<1
. Let
B
be an arbitrary open ball with center
x_{B}

and radius
r_{B}>0
, with
{\bf O}\in B
. Let

S_{\bf A}\in[{\bf O},{\bf A}],S_{\bf C}\in[{\bf O},{\bf C}]

be the unique points such that
d_{2}(x_{B},S_{\bf A})=d_{2}(x_{B},S_{\bf C})=r_{B}
.

Firstly, suppose that
x_{B}\in[{\bf O},{\bf C}]
. Then

\mathcal{H}^{1}([{\bf O},S_{\bf A}])\leq\mathcal{H}^{1}([{\bf O},S_{\bf C}]).

This implies that

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B}f\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}=\frac{\mathcal{H}^{1}([{\bf O},S_{\bf A}])}{\mathcal{H}^{1}([{\bf O},S_{\bf A}])+\mathcal{H}^{1}([{\bf O},S_{\bf C}])}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Now we consider
x_{B}\in[{\bf O},{\bf A}]
. Let
{\bf M}
be the middle point of
[{\bf O},S_{\bf A}]
.
The ball
B
contains the ball
B^{\prime}
with center at
{\bf M}
and radius

r^{\prime}:=d_{2}({\bf O},{\bf M})
. Let
S^{\prime}_{\bf C}\in[{\bf O},{\bf C}]
be the point intersecting

\partial B^{\prime}
.
Since the triangle
{\bf O}{\bf M}S^{\prime}_{\bf C}
has a right angle at
{\bf M}
and
the angle between

[{\bf O},\bf{M}]
and
[{\bf O},S^{\prime}_{\bf C}]
is
\pi/4
, we have

d_{2}({\bf O},S^{\prime}_{\bf C})=\sqrt{2}d_{2}({\bf O},{\bf M})=\sqrt{2}r^{\prime}
. We then have

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B}f\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\leq\frac{2\mathcal{H}^{1}([{\bf O},{\bf M}])}{2\mathcal{H}^{1}([{\bf O},{\bf M}])+\mathcal{H}^{1}([{\bf O},S^{\prime}_{\bf C}])}=\frac{2r^{\prime}}{2r^{\prime}+\sqrt{2}r^{\prime}}=\frac{2}{2+\sqrt{2}}.

We conclude that
\mathcal{M}f({\bf O})<1
, and thus
\mathcal{M}f
is not continuous at
{\bf O}
.

It is easy to check that every point in
(X,d_{2},\mathcal{H}^{1})
has
1
-capacity at least
1
.
We conclude that the non-centered maximal function of a BV function may
fail to be
1
-quasicontinuous in the metric space setting, contrary to the Euclidean setting.

We also note that choosing
\varepsilon<1
, we have that
U_{\varepsilon}
in Theorem
1.1

is necessarily empty, and then

in
X
.
In fact,
f^{\wedge}
and
f^{\vee}
are respectively lower and upper semicontinuous,
but nonetheless
\mathcal{M}f_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{M}f
fails to be continuous.

Due to this example, we formulate Corollary
1.3
only in the Euclidean setting.

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

Let
f\in\mathrm{BV}(\Omega)
be nonnegative and
\varepsilon>0
.
Recall from (
2.12
) that
S_{f}\setminus J_{f}
has zero
1
-capacity.
We let
U
and
G
be subsets of
\Omega
defined as in
(
3.1
)-(
3.2
),
so that
{\rm Cap}_{1}(U)<\varepsilon
,
f^{\wedge}|_{\Omega\setminus G}

is lower semicontinuous and
f^{\vee}|_{\Omega\setminus G}
is upper semicontinuous,
and we can further assume that
S_{f}\setminus J_{f}\subset G\subset U\subset\Omega
.
Now, defining
f_{\varepsilon}
by (
3.8
), we obtain a function
satisfying the properties given in Theorem
1.1
.

It remains to show that
\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}
is continuous on
\Omega
.
For this, we first prove a couple of auxiliary lemmas.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
x\in\partial U\cap\Omega
. Then

(5.1)

\lim_{r\to 0}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)-f_{\varepsilon}(y)|\,dy=0.

In particular, we obtain that for every
\delta>0
, there is
r_{\delta}>0
so that for all balls
B\subset B(x,r_{\delta})
with
x\in\overline{B}
,

(5.2)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B}|f(y)-f_{\varepsilon}(y)|\,dy<\delta.

Proof.

Recall the definitions from Section
3
, in particular the definition of
U_{1}
from (
3.3
).
Let
\delta>0
. By (
3.21
)-(
3.22
),
there is
r_{\delta}>0
with

B(x,2r_{\delta})\cap U\subset U_{1}
so that for every
j\in J_{1}
, we have

(5.3)

|f(y)-a_{j}(y)|\leq|f(y)|+M_{j}\leq f(y)+f^{\vee}(x)+\delta

for every
y\in 2B_{j}
with
2B_{j}\cap B(x,r_{\delta})\neq\emptyset
;
recall that
f
is assumed to be nonnegative.
Recall from (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) that

(5.4)

\lim_{r\to 0}\left(\frac{\mathcal{L}^{d}(B(x,r)\cap G)}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(B(x,r))}+\sum_{j\in J_{1}:\,2B_{j}\cap B(x,r)\neq\emptyset}\frac{\mathcal{L}^{d}(N_{j})}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(B(x,r))}\right)=0.

Recall from (
2.9
), (
2.10
), and (
2.11
) that
for all subsets
B\subset B(x,r)
with

\mathcal{L}^{d}(B)/\mathcal{L}^{d}(B(x,r))
bounded from below
uniformly in
r
,

(5.5)

\begin{cases}\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(B)}\int_{B\cap B^{+}_{\nu}(x,r)}|f(y)-f^{+}(x)|\,dy=0\quad\textrm{and}\\
\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(B)}\int_{B\cap B^{-}_{\nu}(x,r)}|f(y)-f^{-}(x)|\,dy=0\quad\textrm{if }x\in J_{f};\\
\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(B)}\int_{B}|f(y)-\widetilde{f}(x)|\,dy=0\quad\textrm{if }x\in\Omega\setminus S_{f}.\end{cases}

Recall that the dilated Whitney balls
2B_{j}
have overlap at most
C_{0}
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to 0}\sum_{j\in J_{1}}\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(B(x,r))}\int_{B(x,r)\cap 2B_{j}\cap(G\cup N_{j})}|f(y)-a_{j}(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle\quad\overset{\eqref{eq4.8-19Oct}}{\leq}\limsup_{r\to 0}\sum_{j\in J_{1}}\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(B(x,r))}\int_{B(x,r)\cap 2B_{j}\cap(G\cup N_{j})}f(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\quad\quad+(f^{\vee}(x)+\delta)\limsup_{r\to 0}\sum_{j\in J_{1}}\frac{\mathcal{L}^{d}(B(x,r)\cap 2B_{j}\cap(G\cup N_{j}))}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(B(x,r))}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C_{0}\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(B(x,r))}\int_{B(x,r)}\bigg(\big(|f(y)-f^{+}(x)|\chi_{B_{\nu}^{+}(x,r)}(y)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+|f(y)-f^{-}(x)|\chi_{B_{\nu}^{-}(x,r)}(y)\big)\chi_{J_{f}}(x)+|f(y)-\widetilde{f}(x)|\chi_{\Omega\setminus S_{f}}(x)\bigg)\,dy

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\bigg(3\max\{f^{+}(x),f^{-}(x),\widetilde{f}(x)\}+f^{\vee}(x)+\delta\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\limsup_{r\to 0}\left(C_{0}\frac{\mathcal{L}^{d}(B(x,r)\cap G)}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(B(x,r))}+C_{0}\sum_{j\in J_{1}:\,2B_{j}\cap B(x,r)\neq\emptyset}\frac{\mathcal{L}^{d}(N_{j})}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(B(x,r))}\right)

(5.6)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=0

by (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) with the choice
B=B(x,r)
.
We then obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to 0}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)-f_{\varepsilon}(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle=\limsup_{r\to 0}\int_{B(x,r)\cap U}\frac{|f(y)-f_{\varepsilon}(y)|}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(B(x,r))}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{r\to 0}\sum_{j\in J_{1}}\bigg(\int_{B(x,r)\cap 2B_{j}\setminus(G\cup N_{j})}\frac{|f(y)-f_{j}(y)|}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(B(x,r))}\,dy+\int_{B(x,r)\cap 2B_{j}\cap(G\cup N_{j})}\frac{|f(y)-a_{j}(y)|}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(B(x,r))}\,dy\bigg)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq3.7-Sep}\textrm{ 3rd property }\&\ \eqref{eq4.10-19Oct}}{\leq}\limsup_{r\to 0}\sum_{j\in J_{1}:2B_{j}\cap B(x,r)\neq\emptyset}2^{-j}+0

\displaystyle=0,

which is the main claim (
5.1
). The last conclusion (
5.2
) is obtained by the doubling property of
\mathcal{L}^{d}
and the main claim (
5.1
).
The proof is completed.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

We have
\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}\geq f_{\varepsilon}^{\vee}
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Recall that
f
is nonnegative.
If
x\in U
, then
f_{\varepsilon}
is continuous at
x
and so

\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}(x)\geq\widetilde{f}_{\varepsilon}(x)=f_{\varepsilon}^{\vee}(x)
.
If
x\in\Omega\setminus\overline{U}
, then
by (
2.14
) we have

\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}(x)\geq\sup\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}}|f(y)|dy\overset{\eqref{eq4.14-19Oct}}{\geq}\max\{\widetilde{f}(x),f^{+}(x),f^{-}(x)\}=f^{\vee}(x)=f_{\varepsilon}^{\vee}(x)

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B_{r}
with radius

r<{\rm dist}(x,U)
,
x\in\overline{B_{r}}
and

B_{r}\subset\Omega\setminus\overline{U}
; here the first equality is obtained
from (
2.13
) and
S_{f}\setminus J_{f}\subset U
,
and the last equality is obtained from the fact that

f^{\vee}=f^{\vee}_{\varepsilon}
on
\Omega\setminus\overline{U}
.

Finally, consider
x\in\partial U\cap\Omega
. Let
\delta>0
.
We have from (
5.2
) in Lemma
5.2
that there is
r_{\delta}>0
so that

(5.7)

\sup_{B\in\mathcal{T}_{1}}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B}{|f(y)-f_{\varepsilon}(y)|}\,dy<\delta.

Here
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is the collection of all balls

with

.
It follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{B\in\mathcal{T}_{1}}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B}f_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{B\in\mathcal{T}_{1}}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B}f(y)\,dy-\sup_{B\in\mathcal{T}_{1}}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B}|f(y)-f_{\varepsilon}(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq4.16-19Oct}}{\geq}\sup_{B\in\mathcal{T}_{1}}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B}f(y)\,dy-\delta

.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
x\in\Omega
. If
\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}(x)\geq f^{\vee}_{\varepsilon}(x)
,
then
\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}
is upper semicontinuous at
x
.

Proof.

Take an arbitrary sequence
x_{j}\to x
,
x_{j}\in\Omega
, such that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}(x_{j})=\limsup_{y\to x}\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}(y).

We only need to show that

\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}(x)\geq\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}(x_{j})
.

We find “almost optimal” balls
B(x_{j}^{*},r_{j})
, such that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}(x_{j})=\lim_{j\to\infty}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x_{j}^{*},r_{j})}f_{\varepsilon}\,dy,

with
x_{j}\in B(x_{j}^{*},r_{j})\subset\Omega
.
Since
f_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, we can assume that the radii
r_{j}
are uniformly bounded.
Now we consider two cases.

Case 1.
Suppose that by passing to a subsequence (not relabeled),
we have
r_{j}\to 0
.
We have

\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}(x)\geq f^{\vee}_{\varepsilon}(x)\geq\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x_{j}^{*},r_{j})}f_{\varepsilon}\,dy

by the upper semicontinuity of
f^{\vee}_{\varepsilon}
at
x
. Thus the conclusion follows.

Case 2.

The second possibility is that passing to a subsequence (not relabeled),
we have
r_{j}\to r\in(0,\infty)
.
Passing to a further subsequence (not relabeled),
the vectors
x_{j}^{*}-x_{j}
(since they have length at most
r_{j})

converge to some
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Now for
x^{*}:=x+v
we have

\chi_{B(x_{j}^{*},r_{j})}\to\chi_{B(x^{*},r)}
in
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, with

x\in\overline{B}(x^{*},r)
and
B(x^{*},r)\subset\Omega
.
In this case we have by (
2.14
) that

\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}(x)\geq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x^{*},r)}f_{\varepsilon}\,dy=\lim_{j\to\infty}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x_{j}^{*},r_{j})}f_{\varepsilon}\,dy=\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}(x_{j}).

This completes the proof. ∎

[Proof of Corollary
1.3
continued] Notice from the definition of the non-centered
maximal function that
\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}
is obviously lower semicontinuous in
\Omega
.
By Lemma
5.3
and Lemma
5.4
, we obtain that

\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}
is upper semicontinuous in

\Omega
. Hence
\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}
is continuous in
\Omega
,
which is the last claim.
The proof is completed.
∎

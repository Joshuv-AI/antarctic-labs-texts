Invariant Differential Operators for the Real Exceptional Lie Algebra
F

Abstract




Abstract

In the present paper we continue the project of systematic
construction of invariant differential operators on the example of
the non-compact exceptional Lie algebra
F^{\prime}_{4}
which is split real form of the exceptional Lie algebra
F_{4}
.
We consider induction from a maximal parabolic algebra. We classify
the reducible Verma modules over
F_{4}
which are compatible with this induction.
Thus, we obtain the classification of the corresponding invariant differential operators.

Presented at Corfu Summer Institute 2019 ”School and Workshops on Elementary Particle Physics and Gravity” (CORFU2019)

31 August - 25 September 2019,
Corfù, Greece

Proceedings, Volume 376, PoS (CORFU2019) (Published on: August 18, 2020) 233.

1
Introduction

Invariant differential operators play very important role in the
description of physical symmetries. The general scheme for constructing these
operators was given some time ago
[
2
]
.
In recent papers
[
3
,
4
]
we started the systematic explicit
construction of the invariant differential operators.

The first task in the construction is to make the multiplet classification of
the reducible Verma modules over the algebra in consideration following
[
5
]
.
Such classification provides the weights of embeddings between the Verma modules via
the singular vectors, and thus, by
[
2
]
, the weights of the invariant differential operators.

We have done the multiplet classification for many real non-compact algebras, first from the class
of algebras that have discrete series representations, see
[
6
]
.
In the present paper we focus on the complex exceptional Lie algebra
F_{4}
and on its
split real form algebra
F^{\prime}_{4}
. Our scheme requires that we use induction from parabolic subalgebras.
In the present paper we choose a parabolic subalgebra containing the factor
{\cal M}\oplus{\cal A}
, where

{\cal M}~=~sl(3,\mathbb{R})\oplus sl(2,\mathbb{R})
,
{\cal A}~=~o(2)
.
This choice is motivated by the fact that the complexification of
{\cal M}\oplus{\cal A}
and the corresponding compact form

{\cal M}_{c}\oplus{\cal A}_{c}~=~su(3)\oplus su(2)\oplus u(1)
have applications in physics being the Lie algebra symmetry of the standard
model of elementary particles
[
7
]
(see also
[
8
]
).
1
1

1

More precisely the symmetry is presented on the group level as :

G=SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)/Z
, where
Z
belongs to the center of
G
.

We present the multiplet classification of
the reducible Verma modules over
F_{4}
which are compatible with the chosen parabolic of
F^{\prime}_{4}
.
We give also the weights of the singular vectors between
these modules. By the scheme of
[
2
]
these singular vectors will produce
the invariant differential operators.

2.2
Verma modules

Let us recall that a
Verma module

V^{\Lambda}
is defined as
the HWM over
{{\cal G}^{\mathbb{C}}}
with highest weight
\Lambda\in{\cal H}^{*}
and
highest weight vector
v_{0}\in V^{\Lambda}
, induced from the
one-dimensional representation
V_{0}\cong\mathbb{C}v_{0}
of

U({\cal B})
, where
{\cal B}={\cal H}\oplus{\cal G}_{+}
is a Borel
subalgebra of
{{\cal G}^{\mathbb{C}}}
, such that:

\displaystyle X~v_{0}~~=~~0,\quad\forall\,X\in{\cal G}_{+}

(6)

\displaystyle H~v_{0}~~=~~\Lambda(H)~v_{0}\,,\quad\forall\,H\in{\cal H}

(7)

Verma modules are generically irreducible. A Verma module
V^{\Lambda}
is
reducible
[
9
]
iff there exists a root
\beta\in{\Delta}^{+}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}

such that

(\Lambda+{\rho}~,~\beta^{\vee})~=~m

(8)

holds, where
{\rho}={1\over 2}\sum_{\alpha\in{\Delta}^{+}}~\alpha
,
(
{\rho}~=~8\alpha_{1}+15\alpha_{2}+21\alpha_{3}+11\alpha_{4}
).

If (
8
) holds then the reducible Verma module
V^{\Lambda}
contains an invariant submodule
which is also a Verma module
V^{\Lambda^{\prime}}
with shifted weight
\Lambda^{\prime}=\Lambda-m\beta
.
This statement is equivalent to the fact that
V^{\Lambda}
contains a

singular vector

v_{s}\in V^{\Lambda}
, such that
v_{s}~\neq~\xi v_{0}\,
, (
0\neq\xi\in\mathbb{C}
),
and :

\displaystyle X~v_{s}~~=~~0,\quad\forall\,X\in{\cal G}_{+}

(9)

\displaystyle H~v_{s}~~=~~\Lambda^{\prime}(H)~v_{s}\,,\quad\Lambda^{\prime}~=~\Lambda-m\beta,~~\forall\,H\in{\cal H}

(10)

More explicitly,
[
2
]
,

v^{s}_{m,\beta}={\cal P}_{m,\beta}\,v_{0}\ .

(11)

The general reducibility conditions (
8
) for
V^{\Lambda}

spelled out for the simple roots in our situation are:

\displaystyle m_{1}~\equiv~m_{\alpha_{1}}~=~(\Lambda+{\rho},\alpha_{1}),~~~m_{2}~\equiv~m_{\alpha_{2}}~=~(\Lambda+{\rho},\alpha_{2}),

(12)

\displaystyle m_{3}~\equiv~m_{\alpha_{3}}~=~(\Lambda+{\rho},2\alpha_{3}),~~~m_{4}~\equiv~m_{\alpha_{4}}~=~(\Lambda+{\rho},2\alpha_{4})

The numbers
m_{i}
from (
12
) corresponding to the simple roots are called Dynkin labels, while the more general Harish-Chandra parameters are:

m_{\beta}~=~(\Lambda+{\rho},\beta^{\vee}),~~~\beta\in{\Delta}^{+}

(13)

2.3
Structure theory of the real form

The split real form of
F_{4}
is denoted as
F^{\prime}_{4}\,
, sometimes as
F_{4(4)}\,
.
This real form has discrete series representations since rank
F^{\prime}_{4}\,~=
rank
\,{\cal K}
. We can use
the same basis (but over
\mathbb{R}
) and the same root system.

The Iwasawa decomposition of the real split form
{\cal G}\equiv F^{\prime}_{4}\,
, is:

{\cal G}~=~{\cal K}\oplus{\cal A}_{0}\oplus{\cal N}_{0}\ ,

(14)

the Cartan decomposition is:

{\cal G}~=~{\cal K}\oplus{\cal Q},

(15)

where we use: the maximal compact subgroup
{\cal K}\cong sp(3)\oplus su(2)
,
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\,{\cal Q}=28
,

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\,{\cal A}_{0}=4
,
{\cal N}_{0}={\cal N}^{+}_{0}\,
, or

{\cal N}_{0}={\cal N}^{-}_{0}\cong{\cal N}^{+}_{0}\,
,
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\,{\cal N}_{0}^{\pm}=24
.

Since
{\cal G}
is maximally split, then
the centralizer
{\cal M}_{0}
of
{\cal A}_{0}
in
{\cal K}
is zero, thus, the minimal parabolic
{\cal P}_{0}

and the corresponding Bruhat decomposition are:

{\cal P}_{0}~=~{\cal A}_{0}\oplus{\cal N}_{0}\ ,\qquad{\cal G}~=~{\cal A}_{0}\oplus{\cal N}^{+}_{0}\oplus{\cal N}^{-}_{0}

(16)

The importance of the parabolic subgroups comes from the fact that
the representations induced from them generate all (admissible)
irreducible representations of the group under consideration
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
.

We recall that in general a parabolic subalgebra
{\cal P}~=~{\cal M}\oplus{\cal A}\oplus{\cal N}
is any subalgebra of
{\cal G}

which contains a minimal parabolic subalgebra
{\cal M}_{0}\,
. In general,
{\cal M}
contains the subalgebra
{\cal M}_{0}
, while

{\cal A}
is contained in
{\cal A}_{0}
,
{\cal N}
is contained in
{\cal N}_{0}
.

On the other extreme are the maximal parabolic subalgebras for which dim
\,{\cal A}=1
.

An important ingredient in our considerations are the

]

{\cal F}

Actually, since our ERs are induced from finite-dimensional
representations of
{\cal M}
the corresponding Verma modules are

such that the role of the

is taken by the

Algebraically, the above is governed by the notion of

-compact roots of

{{\cal G}^{\mathbb{C}}}
which can be identified as roots of

Remark 3:

We expect that the discrete series are contained in the representation
\chi^{+}_{0}
since it is dual to
\chi^{-}_{0}

where are contained the finite-dimensional (non-unitary) irreps. Following the Harish-Chandra criterion we must check which
{\cal M}
-non-compact entries are negative. We recall that the
{\cal M}
-compact entries are

, all other

{\cal M}

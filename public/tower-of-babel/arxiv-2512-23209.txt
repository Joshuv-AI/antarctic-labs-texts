Extremal A ​ B ​ S ABS Spectral Radius in Bicyclic and Bipartite Unicyclic Graphs


Abstract
The A ​ B ​ S ABS spectral radius of a graph G G is defined as the largest eigenvalue of its A ​ B ​ S ABS matrix. Motivated by recent studies on this parameter, in this paper, we determine the bipartite unicyclic graphs that attain the largest A ​ B ​ S ABS spectral radius. Furthermore, we characterize the bicyclic graphs that attain the largest and the second largest A ​ B ​ S ABS spectral radii. Mathematics Subject Classifications: 05C50, 05C35, 05C05. Keywords: A ​ B ​ S ABS matrix, A ​ B ​ S ABS spectral radius, bipartite unicyclic graphs, bicyclic graphs.

Introduction

Let
G
be a simple undirected graph with vertex
set
V(G)=\left\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}\right\}
and edge set
E(G)
. As usual, we denote the path graph and the cycle graph on
n
vertices, by
P_{n}
and
C_{n}
, respectively. The well-known adjacency matrix of
G
is denoted by
A(G)
, and its
i
-th largest eigenvalue is given by
\lambda_{i}
. In recent years, extensive research has been carried on the spectral properties of topological matrices. The first Zagreb matrix, the
ABC
matrix, and the Sombor matrix are among the recently introduced degree-based topological matrices that have been extensively investigated in the literature (see
[
5
,
1
,
9
,
13
,
8
]
for details). The atom-bond sum-connectivity index of a graph
G
, denoted by
ABS(G)
, is a degree-based topological index defined as

ABS(G)=\displaystyle\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)}\sqrt{1-\dfrac{2}{d_{i}+d_{j}}}.

The topological matrix associated with
ABS(G)
is called the atom-bond sum-connectivity matrix of
G
and is denoted by
\mathcal{ABS}(G)
. Its
(i,j)
-th entry is given by

(\mathcal{ABS}(G))_{ij}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\sqrt{1-\dfrac{2}{d_{i}+d_{j}}},&\text{if}\,\,v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)\\
0,&otherwise\end{array}.\right.

The
i
-th largest eigenvalue of
\mathcal{ABS}(G)
is denoted by
\eta_{i}(G)
. In particular,
\eta_{1}
is
the spectral radius of
\mathcal{ABS}(G)
, hereafter referred to as the
ABS
spectral radius of
G
. The matrix
\mathcal{ABS}(G)

was first introduced in
[
6
]
, where the authors investigated the properties of the
ABS
Estrada index,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}e^{\eta_{i}}
and discussed its chemical significance. The eigenvalue properties of the
ABS
matrix were further explored in
[
12
]
.

Recently, extremal problems concerning the
ABS
spectral radius of trees and unicyclic graphs have been studied. In
[
7
]
, Lin et al. characterized trees on
n
vertices with largest
ABS
spectral radius and demonstrated the chemical importance of
ABS
spectral radius. Moreover, they posed a general problem of characterizing graphs with maximum
ABS
spectral radius in a given class of graphs. In
[
11
]
, the authors have identified unicyclic graphs on
n
vertices that attains the maximum
ABS
spectral radius. Motivated by these works, in Section 2, we determine the bipartite unicyclic graphs that attain the largest
ABS
spectral radius. In section 3, we characterize the bicyclic graphs that attain the largest and the second largest
ABS
spectral radii.

2
ABS
spectral radius of bipartite unicyclic graphs

In this section, we identify bipartite unicyclic graphs with largest
ABS
spectral radius. The class of all bipartite unicyclic graphs of order
n
is denoted as
\mathcal{U}_{n}^{b}
.
Let
\mathcal{H}_{1}
be the unicyclic graph of order
n\geq 5
obtained by attaching
n-4
pendant vertices to one vertex of the cycle
C_{4}
. The graph
\mathcal{H}_{2}
is obtained by attaching
n-5
pendant vertices to one vertex of
C_{4}
, and then attaching a new pendant vertex to another vertex of
C_{4}
. See Fig.
1
.

Figure 1
:
The graphs
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
.

Let
M=(m_{ij})_{p\times q}
and
N=(n_{ij})_{p\times q}
. We write
M\preceq N
if
m_{ij}\leq n_{ij}
for all
i
,
j
.
The following lemmas are essential to prove our main results.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
4
]

Let
M,N
be non-negative matrices of order
n
with spectral radius
\rho(M)
and
\rho(N)
, respectively. If
M\preceq N
, then
\rho(M)\leq\rho(N)
. Further, if
M
is irreducible and
M\neq N
, then
\rho(M)<\rho(N)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
10
]
Let
\mathcal{H}_{2}
be the graph as depicted in Fig.
1
. Then for any graph
G
in the class
\mathcal{U}_{n}^{b}\backslash\left\{\mathcal{H}_{1}\right\}
,
\lambda_{1}(G)\leq\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{H}_{2})
. Equality holds if and only if
G\cong\mathcal{H}_{2}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
G\in\mathcal{U}_{n}^{b}\backslash\left\{{\mathcal{H}_{1}}\right\}
, then
\eta_{1}(G)<\dfrac{n-2}{\sqrt{n}}
for all
n\geq 10
.

Proof.

Since
d_{i}+d_{j}\leq n
, for every edge
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)
,
(\mathcal{ABS}(G))_{ij}\leq\sqrt{\dfrac{n-2}{n}}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{ABS}(G)\preceq\sqrt{\dfrac{n-2}{n}}A(G)
, and so by Lemma
2.1
,
\eta_{1}(G)<\sqrt{\dfrac{n-2}{n}}\lambda_{1}(G)
. Consequently, by Lemma
2.2
,
\eta_{1}(G)<\sqrt{\dfrac{n-2}{n}}\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{H}_{2})
.

Claim:
\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{H}_{2})<\sqrt{n-2}
. The adjacency matrix of
\mathcal{H}_{2}
is as follows.

\begin{bmatrix}0&1&0&1&0&1&\ldots&1\\
1&0&1&0&0&0&\ldots&0\\
0&1&0&1&0&0&\ldots&0\\
1&0&1&0&1&0&\ldots&0\\
0&0&0&1&0&0&\ldots&0\\
1&0&0&0&0&0&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&&&&&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&0&0&0&0&0&\ldots&0\end{bmatrix}.

Therefore, the characteristic polynomial of
A(\mathcal{H}_{2})
is
x^{n-6}\zeta(x)
, where
\zeta(x):=x^{6}-nx^{4}+(3n-13)x^{2}+5-n
. It is easy to verify that,
\zeta\left(\sqrt{n-2}\right)>0
for all
n\geq 10
. Let
\zeta_{1}(x)=\zeta\left(x+\sqrt{n-2}\right)
. Then
\zeta_{1}^{\prime}(x)=2(3n-13)\left(x+\sqrt{n-2}\right)-4n\left(x+\sqrt{n-2}\right)^{3}+6\left(x+\sqrt{n-2}\right)^{5}>0
for all
x\geq 0
. Thus,
\zeta(x)
is increasing for
x\geq\sqrt{n-2}
. Since
\zeta\left(\sqrt{n-2}\right)>0
for all
n\geq 10
, it follows that
\zeta(x)
has no root in the interval
\left[\sqrt{n-2},\infty\right)
. Proving that
\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{H}_{2})<\sqrt{n-2}
. Hence

\eta_{1}(G)<\dfrac{n-2}{\sqrt{n}}
.
∎

The following result gives the spectral radius of
\mathcal{ABS}(\mathcal{H}_{1})
. The proof is omitted, since the roots of the characteristic polynomial of
\mathcal{ABS}(\mathcal{H}_{1})
can be listed explicitly.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
n\geq 5
,

\eta_{1}^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{1})=\dfrac{n^{3}-4n^{2}+5n+4+\sqrt{n^{6}-12n^{5}+58n^{4}-108n^{3}+41n^{2}+40n+16}}{2n(n-1)}.

Theorem 2.5
.

Among all bipartite unicyclic graphs of order
n\geq 5
, the graph
\mathcal{H}_{1}
attains the maximum
ABS
spectral radius.

Proof.

For
5\leq n\leq 9
, it can be verified using Sage
[
14
]
that
\mathcal{H}_{1}
has the maximum
ABS
spectral radius.
Let
n\geq 10
. By Lemma
2.3
, for any
G\in\mathcal{U}_{n}^{b}\backslash\left\{\mathcal{H}_{1}\right\}
,
\eta_{1}(G)<\dfrac{n-2}{\sqrt{n}}
. From Lemma
2.4
,

\eta_{1}^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{1})-\dfrac{(n-2)^{2}}{n}=\dfrac{1}{2n(n-1)}\left(\sqrt{A}-(n-4)(n^{2}-2n+3)\right),
where
A=n^{6}-12n^{5}+58n^{4}-108n^{3}+41n^{2}+40n+16
. Observe that
A-(n-4)^{2}(n^{2}-2n+3)^{2}=16(n-1)^{2}(3n-8)>0
for
n\geq 5
, and so
\sqrt{A}>(n-4)(n^{2}-2n+3)
. Hence
\eta_{1}(\mathcal{H}_{1})>\dfrac{n-2}{\sqrt{n}}
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

3
ABS
spectral radius of bicyclic graphs

The
\infty
-graph
B_{\infty}(p,l,q)

[
2
]
is obtained from two cycles
C_{p}
and
C_{q}
, and a path
P_{l}
by identifying one end of the path with a vertex of
C_{p}
and the other end with a vertex of
C_{q}
. The
\theta
-graph
B_{\theta}(p,l,q)
is obtained from three vertex-disjoint paths
P_{p+2},P_{l+2}
and
P_{q+2}
, where
l,p,q\geq 0
and at most one of them is
0
, by identifying the three initial vertices and terminal vertices of them, respectively. See Fig.
2
.

Figure 2
:
Graphs
B_{\infty}(p,l,q)
and
B_{\theta}(p,l,q)
.

Let
\mathcal{B}(n)
be the class of all bicyclic graphs of order
n
. For a bicyclic graph
G
, the unique bicyclic subgraph of
G
that contains no pendant vertices is denoted by
\mathbb{U}(G)
. Note that
\mathbb{U}(G)
is either
B_{\infty}(p,l,q)
or
B_{\theta}(p,l,q)
.
Denote

\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(n)=\left\{G\in\mathcal{B}_{n}|\mathbb{U}(G)=B_{\infty}(p,l,q)\right\}
and
\mathcal{B}_{\theta}(n)=\left\{G\in\mathcal{B}(n)|\mathbb{U}(G)=B_{\theta}(p,l,q)\right\}
.

Then
\mathcal{B}(n)=\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(n)\cup\mathcal{B}_{\theta}(n)
.
The following Kelmans operation is very much essential in this section.

Let
u
and
v
be two vertices of the graph
G
. Define
\Omega_{1}=N(u)-N[v]
,
\Omega_{2}=N(v)-N[u]
,
\Omega_{3}=N(u)\cap N(v)
. The Kelmans operation on two vertices
u
and
v
results in a new graph, denoted by
G_{uv^{+}}
. It is obtained by deleting all the edges
uw
such that
w\in\Omega_{1}
, and then adding edges
vw
for all such
w\in\Omega_{1}
.

The following lemma proved in
[
11
]
gives a comparison between
ABS
spectral radii of
G
and
G_{uv^{+}}
, respectively.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
11
]

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n
. Suppose
uv\in E(G)
such that
|\Omega_{1}|\geq 1
,
|\Omega_{2}|\geq 1
and
\Omega_{3}=\emptyset
. Then
\eta_{1}(G)<\eta_{1}(G_{uv^{+}})
.

Let
\Gamma_{\infty}(n)\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(n)
denote the class of bicyclic graphs of order
n
with girth
3
, diameter at most
4
, and exactly
n-5
pendant vertices. Also, let
\Gamma_{\theta}(n)\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{\theta}(n)
denote the class of bicyclic graphs of order
n
with girth
3
, diameter at most
4
, and exactly
n-4
pendant vertices. See Fig.
3
.

Figure 3
:
Graph classes
\Gamma_{\infty}(n)
and
\Gamma_{\theta}(n)
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(n)
, where
n\geq 5
with the largest
ABS
spectral radius. Then
G\in\Gamma_{\infty}(n)
.

Proof.

Let
G\in\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(n)
with maximum
ABS
spectral radius and let
\mathbb{U}(G)=B_{\infty}(p,l,q)
. Suppose that
G
has a non-pendant edge
uv
which does not lie on either of the two cycles
C_{p}
and
C_{q}
. By applying Kelmans operation on the edge
uv
, we obtain the graph
G_{uv^{+}}
. Now, Lemma
3.1
ensures that
\eta_{1}(G)<\eta_{1}(G_{uv^{+}})
, a contradiction. Hence, every edge that does not lie on
C_{p}
or
C_{q}
must be pendant. Furthermore, if either
C_{p}
or
C_{q}
is not a triangle, applying the operation to an edge

uv
on such a non-triangular cycle results in the graph
G_{uv^{+}}
and Lemma
3.1
again implies
\eta_{1}(G)<\eta_{1}(G_{uv^{+}})
, a contradiction. Therefore,
G\in\Gamma_{\infty}(n)
.
∎

Our next result can be proved in a manner similar to Theorem
3.2
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{B}_{\theta}(n)
, where
n\geq 4
with the largest
ABS
spectral radius. Then
G\in\Gamma_{\theta}(n)
.

We denote the class of bicyclic graphs of order
n
and diameter
d
by
\mathcal{B}_{n}^{d}
. Let
P_{d+1}(i)
, where
d\geq 2
and
2\leq i\leq d
be the graph of order
n
obtained from the path
P_{d+1}:z_{1}z_{2}\dots z_{d+1}
by joining the vertex
z_{i}
with each of the isolated vertices
z_{d+2},\dots,z_{n}
. Denote by
P_{d+1}^{\ast}(i)
the graph obtained from
P_{d+1}(i)
by adding the edges
z_{i-1}z_{n}
and
z_{i+1}z_{n}
, The graph
P^{\ast\ast}_{d+1}(i)
is obtained from
P_{d+1}(i)
by adding the edges
z_{i-1}z_{n-1}
and
z_{i-1}z_{n}
. See Fig.
4
and
5
.

and

.

The following lemma proved in
[

]
is useful to prove our next result.

Lemma 3.4
.

[

]

Let
n\geq d+4
and
G\in\mathcal{B}_{n}^{d}
. If
d\geq 4
, then
\lambda_{1}(G)\leq\lambda_{1}\left(P^{\ast}_{d+1}\left(\lfloor\dfrac{d+2}{2}\rfloor\right)\right)
with equality if and only if
G=P^{\ast}_{d+1}\left(\lfloor\dfrac{d+2}{2}\rfloor\right)
. Furthermore, if
d=3
, then
\lambda_{1}(G)\leq\lambda_{1}(P^{\ast\ast}_{4}(3))
with equality if and only if
G=P^{\ast\ast}_{4}(3)
.

We now derive bounds for the
ABS
spectral radius of graphs with a given diameter that belong to either
\Gamma_{\infty}(n)
or
\Gamma_{\theta}(n)
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
G
be a graph in the class
\Gamma_{\infty}(n)
or
\Gamma_{\theta}(n)
. If diameter of
G
is
4
, then

\eta_{1}(G)<\sqrt{\dfrac{(n-2)(n-1)}{n}}
for all
n\geq 16
.

Proof.

For any two vertices
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
of
G
,
d_{j}+d_{j}\leq n
. Therefore,
\mathcal{ABS}(G)\preceq\sqrt{\dfrac{n-2}{n}}A(G)
, and by Lemma
2.1
,
\eta_{1}(G)<\sqrt{\dfrac{n-2}{n}}\lambda_{1}(G)
. Furthermore, by Lemma
3.4
,

\eta_{1}(G)<\sqrt{\dfrac{n-2}{n}}\,\lambda_{1}(P^{\ast}_{5}(3))

(3.1)

for
n\geq 8
.

We now show that
\lambda_{1}(P^{\ast}_{5}(3))<\sqrt{n-1}
for all
n\geq 16
. The adjacency matrix of
P^{\ast}_{5}(3)
is as follows.

\mathcal{A}(P^{\ast}_{5}(3))=\begin{bmatrix}0&1&0&0&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
1&0&1&0&0&0&\dots&0&1\\
0&1&0&1&0&1&\dots&1&1\\
0&0&1&0&1&0&\dots&0&1\\
0&0&0&1&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&1&0&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&&&&&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
0&0&1&0&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&1&1&1&0&0&\dots&0&0\end{bmatrix}.

Therefore, the characteristic polynomial of
A(P^{\ast}_{5}(3))
is
x^{n-6}(x^{2}-1)\varphi(x)
, where
\varphi(x)=x^{4}-nx^{2}-4x+3n-17.
It is straightforward to verify that
\varphi(\sqrt{n-1})>0
for all
n\geq 16
.

Let
n\geq 16
and
\varphi_{1}(x)=\varphi(x+\sqrt{n-1})
. Then
\varphi_{1}^{\prime}(x)=\left(12\,{x}^{2}+2\,n-4\right)\sqrt{n-1}+4\,{x}^{3}+\left(10\,n-12\right)x-4>0
for all
x\geq 0
. Thus,
\varphi(x)

is increasing for
x\geq\sqrt{n-1}
. So,
\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(\sqrt{n-1})>0
for
x\geq\sqrt{n-1}
. This implies,
\varphi(x)
has no root in the interval
[\sqrt{n-1},\infty)
.
This proves that for
n\geq 16
,

\lambda_{1}(P^{\ast}_{5}(3))<\sqrt{n-1}.

(3.2)

Employing equation (
3.2
) in (
3.1
), we get the desired result.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
G
be a graph in the class
\Gamma_{\infty}(n)
or
\Gamma_{\theta}(n)
. If diameter of
G
is
3
, then

(i)

\eta_{1}(G)<\dfrac{1}{10}\sqrt{\dfrac{(n-1)(100n-53)}{n+1}}
for all
n\geq 21
.

(ii)

\eta_{1}(G)<\dfrac{n-1}{\sqrt{n+1}}
for all
n\geq 34
.

Proof.

For any two vertices
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
of
G
,
d_{i}+d_{j}\leq n+1
. Therefore,
\mathcal{ABS}(G)\preceq\sqrt{\dfrac{n-1}{n+1}}A(G)
. Now, by Lemmas
2.1
and
3.4
,
\eta_{1}(G)<\sqrt{\dfrac{n-1}{n+1}}\lambda_{1}(G)\leq\sqrt{\dfrac{n-1}{n+1}}\lambda_{1}(P^{\ast\ast}_{4}(3))
for all
n\geq 7
. That is,

\eta_{1}(G)<\sqrt{\dfrac{n-1}{n+1}}\lambda_{1}(P^{\ast\ast}_{4}(3)).

(3.3)

We now show that
\lambda_{1}(P^{\ast\ast}_{4}(3))<\dfrac{1}{10}\sqrt{100n-53}
for all
n\geq 21
and
\lambda_{1}(P^{\ast\ast}_{4}(3))<\sqrt{n-1}
for all
n\geq 34
. The adjacency matrix of
P^{\ast\ast}_{4}(3)
is as follows.

A(P^{\theta}_{4}(3))=\begin{bmatrix}0&1&0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
1&0&1&0&0&\dots&0&1&1\\
0&1&0&1&1&\dots&1&1&1\\
0&0&1&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
0&0&1&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
\vdots&&&&&\ddots&&&\vdots\\
0&0&1&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
0&1&1&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
0&1&1&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\end{bmatrix}.

Therefore, the characteristic polynomial of
A(P^{\ast\ast}_{4}(3))
is
x^{n-4}\varrho(x)
, where
\varrho(x)=x^{4}-(n+1)x^{2}-4x+3n-13
. It is straightforward to verify that
\varrho(\dfrac{1}{10}\sqrt{100n-53})>0
for all
n\geq 21
and
\varrho(\sqrt{n-1})>0
for all
n\geq 34
. Let
n\geq 21
and
\varrho_{1}(x)=\varrho(x+\dfrac{1}{10}\sqrt{100n-53})
. Then

\varrho_{1}^{\prime}(x)={\frac{1}{250}}\,\left(300\,{x}^{2}+50\,n-103\right)\sqrt{100\,n-53}-4+4\,{x}^{3}+{\frac{1}{250}}\,\left(2500\,n-2090\right)x>0

for all

This implies,
\varrho(x)
is increasing for
x\geq\dfrac{1}{10}\sqrt{100n-53}
. So,
\varrho(x)\geq\varrho(0)>0
for all
x\geq\dfrac{1}{10}\sqrt{100n-53}
. Hence,
\varrho(x)
has no root in the interval
[\dfrac{1}{10}\sqrt{100n-53},\infty)
. Thus, for
n\geq 21
,

\eta_{1}(P_{4}^{\ast\ast}(3))<\dfrac{1}{10}\sqrt{100n-53}.

(3.4)

Now. let
n\geq 34
and
\varrho_{2}(x)=\varrho(x+\sqrt{n-1})

for all

. This implies,
\varrho(x)

is increasing for

. So,
\varrho(x)
has no root in the interval
[\sqrt{n-1},\infty)
. Thus, for
n\geq 34
,

\lambda_{1}(P^{\ast\ast}_{4}(3))<\sqrt{n-1}.

(3.5)

From equations (
3.3
), (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we arrive at the result.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
G_{2}
be the graph as depicted in Fig.

. Then

(i)

\dfrac{1}{10}\sqrt{\dfrac{(n-1)(100n-53)}{n+1}}<\eta_{1}(G_{2})<\dfrac{1}{10}\sqrt{\dfrac{(n-1)(100n-53)}{n+1}}+\dfrac{1}{2n}

.

{\small\begin{bmatrix}0&\alpha&\alpha&\alpha&\alpha&\beta&\dots&\beta\\[5.69054pt]

\alpha&0&0&0&\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{2}}&0&\dots&0\\[5.69054pt]

\vdots&&&&&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\[5.69054pt]
\beta&0&0&0&0&0&\dots&0\\
\end{bmatrix},}

where
\alpha=\sqrt{\dfrac{n-1}{n+1}}
and
\beta=\sqrt{\dfrac{n-2}{n}}
. Therefore, characteristic polynomial of
\mathcal{ABS}(G_{2})
is
\dfrac{x^{n-6}}{2n(n+1)}\left(x+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{2}(x-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}})\psi(x)
, where
\psi(x)=2n(n+1){x}^{3}-\sqrt{2}n(n+1){x}^{2}-2\left({n}^{3}-2\,{n}^{2}-n+10\right)x+\sqrt{2}({n}^{3}-6{n}^{2}+3n+10).

Note that,
\psi(\dfrac{1}{10}\sqrt{\dfrac{(n-1)(100n-53)}{n+1}})<0
for
5\leq n\leq 41
and
\psi(\sqrt{n})>0
.
Also,
\psi(\dfrac{1}{10}\sqrt{\dfrac{(n-1)(100n-53)}{n+1}}+\dfrac{1}{2n})>0
for all
n\geq 22
, and
\psi(x)
is increasing for
x\geq\dfrac{1}{10}\sqrt{\dfrac{(n-1)(100n-53)}{n+1}}+\dfrac{1}{2n}
.
Thus,
\eta_{1}(G_{2})>\dfrac{1}{10}\sqrt{\dfrac{(n-1)(100n-53)}{n+1}}
for
5\leq n\leq 41
, and
\eta_{1}(G_{2})<\dfrac{1}{10}\sqrt{\dfrac{(n-1)(100n-53)}{n+1}}+\dfrac{1}{2n}
for all
n\geq 22
.

We also find that,

\psi\left(\dfrac{n-1}{\sqrt{n+1}}\right)<0
for
n\geq 5
. Moreover,
\psi\left(\dfrac{n-1}{\sqrt{n+1}}+\dfrac{3}{2n}\right)>0
for
n\geq 34
and
\psi(x)
is increasing for
x\geq\dfrac{n-1}{\sqrt{n+1}}+\dfrac{3}{2n}
. Thus,
\eta_{1}(G_{2})>\dfrac{n-1}{\sqrt{n+1}}
for
n\geq 5
and
\eta_{1}(G_{2})<\dfrac{n-1}{\sqrt{n+1}}+\dfrac{3}{2n}
, for
n\geq 34
.

∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
G_{1}
be the graph as depicted in Fig.
6
. Then

(i)

\eta_{1}(G_{1})>\dfrac{1}{10}\sqrt{\dfrac{(n-1)(100n-53)}{n+1}}+\dfrac{1}{2n}
for
5\leq n\leq 33
.

(ii)

\eta_{1}(G_{1})>\dfrac{n-1}{\sqrt{n+1}}+\dfrac{3}{2n}
for
n\geq 5

Proof.

The
ABS
matrix of
G_{1}
is as follows.

\begin{bmatrix}0&\alpha&\gamma&\alpha&\beta&\dots&\beta\\
\alpha&0&\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{5}}&0&0&\dots&0\\
\gamma&\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{5}}&0&\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{5}}&0&\dots&0\\
\alpha&0&\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{5}}&0&0&\dots&0\\
\beta&0&0&0&0&\dots&0\\
\vdots&&&&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\beta&0&0&0&0&\dots&0\end{bmatrix}.

where
\alpha=\sqrt{\dfrac{n-1}{n+1}}
,
\beta=\sqrt{\dfrac{n-2}{n}}
, and
\gamma=\sqrt{\dfrac{n}{n+2}}
.
Therefore, characteristic polynomial of
\mathcal{ABS}(G_{1})
is
x^{n-4}\phi(x)
,
where

\phi(x)={x}^{4}-\dfrac{1}{5}\,{\dfrac{\left(5\,{n}^{4}+6\,{n}^{3}-7\,{n}^{2}+52\,n+80\right){x}^{2}}{n\left(n+2\right)\left(n+1\right)}}-\dfrac{4}{5}\,\sqrt{{\frac{n(n-1)15}{(n+2)(n+1)}}}{x}+\dfrac{6}{5}\,{\frac{\left(n-2\right)\left(n-4\right)}{n}}.

Since
\phi(x)
is a monic polynomial with real roots, and

\phi(\dfrac{1}{10}\sqrt{\dfrac{(n-1)(100n-53)}{n+1}}+\dfrac{1}{2n})<0\,\,(5\leq n\leq 33)

and

\phi\left(\dfrac{n-1}{\sqrt{n+1}}+\dfrac{3}{2n}\right)<0\,\,(n\geq 5),

the desired upper bounds follow.

Conclusion

In this work, we identified the unique bipartite unicyclic graph with the maximum
ABS

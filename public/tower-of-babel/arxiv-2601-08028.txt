A Land of Oblique Duality for Frames and Probabilistic Frames
Dongwei Chen, Emily J. King, Clayton Shonkwiler

Abstract
. Functions or distributions used to sample and to reconstruct signals often occur in different domains, like the Dirac delta and a band-limited bump function in classical sampling. Oblique dual frames generalize this phenomenon. In this paper, we provide new tools to study oblique dual frames and introduce a probabilistic variant of oblique dual frames. We first present the oblique dual frame potential and show that it is minimized precisely when the oblique dual coincides with the canonical oblique dual. We then define oblique dual probabilistic frames and oblique approximately dual probabilistic frames. In particular, we prove that for a given oblique dual probabilistic frame, the associated oblique dual probabilistic frame potential is minimized if and only if the frame is tight and the oblique dual is canonical. Moreover, the tightness assumption can be removed when the minimization is restricted to oblique dual probabilistic frames of pushforward type. Finally, we investigate perturbations of oblique dual probabilistic frames and show that if a probability measure is sufficiently close to an oblique dual probabilistic frame pair in the 2 2 -Wasserstein topology, then it forms an oblique approximately dual probabilistic frame.

Introduction

Suppose
\mathcal{H}
is a separable Hilbert space and
W\subset\mathcal{H}
is a closed subspace. An at-most countable sequence
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset W
is called a
frame
for
W
if there exist constants
0<A\leq B
such that for any
\mathbf{f}\in W
,

(1.1)

A\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i\in I}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{f}\rangle|^{2}\leq B\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2}.

A frame
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is called a
tight frame
for
W
if we may choose
A=B
and
Parseval
if
A=B=1
. If the upper (but possibly not the lower) bound in (
1.1
) holds,
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is called a
Bessel sequence
for
W
.

Given any frame
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
for
W
, one can use
dual frames
to reconstruct vectors from
W
: There exists another frame
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
in
W
such that for any
\mathbf{f}\in W
,

(1.2)

\mathbf{f}=\sum_{i\in I}\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{w}_{i}\rangle\mathbf{v}_{i}=\overbrace{\sum_{i\in I}\underbrace{\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle}_{\text{sampling}}\mathbf{w}_{i}}^{\text{reconstruction}}.

In particular, one can use the
canonical dual frame

\{\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
where
\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}
is the Moore–Penrose inverse of the frame operator
\mathbf{S}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
for
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
given by
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{f}=\sum_{i\in I}\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{w}_{i}\rangle\mathbf{w}_{i}
, for any
\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{H}
.

Frames were first introduced by Duffin and Schaeffer to analyze perturbations
of Fourier series
[
16
]
and have been widely used in signal processing
[
14
,
27
,
41
,
30
,
38
]
, where signals are modeled as vectors in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, and signal measurement or sampling is defined as taking the inner product with frame vectors, and signal reconstruction is understood as the weighted sum of samples with dual (or tight) frame vectors
[
22
]
.
Most of the current frame literature also calls signal sampling and reconstruction signal analysis and synthesis, respectively. When
W
is finite-dimensional, the number of vectors in a frame for
W
is necessarily finite, which we call a
finite frame
.
Interested readers can refer to
[
13
,
46
]
for more details on frames and finite frames.

As seen in
Equation

1.2
, the sampling and reconstruction procedures of dual frames occur in the same subspace
W
, because
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
are both contained in
W
. However, in practice, the sampling and reconstruction can be performed across distinct subspaces.

Example 1.1

(
[
31
, Example 2.1]
and
[
33
, Example 1]
)
.

Define the signal space
PW_{1/4}
to be the space of all band-limited signals of bandwidth within
\left[-1/4,1/4\right)
.
By the Whittaker–Nyquist–Shannon–Someya sampling theorem, there exists a function

{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that its Fourier transform
\hat{{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}
satisfies

\hat{{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}(\gamma)=\begin{cases}1,&-\tfrac{1}{4}\leq\gamma<\tfrac{1}{4},\\[6.0pt]
\text{decaying continuously to zero},&\tfrac{1}{4}\leq|\gamma|<\tfrac{1}{2},\\[6.0pt]
0,&|\gamma|\geq\tfrac{1}{2},\end{cases}

and for
T=1
(which satisfies the Nyquist rate
2T\cdot 1/4<1
), we have

{\mathbf{f}}(t)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}{\mathbf{f}}(n)\,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(t-n)=\overbrace{\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\underbrace{\langle{\mathbf{f}},{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{n}\rangle}_{\text{sampling}}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(t-n)}^{\text{reconstruction}},\quad\textrm{for all}\quad{\mathbf{f}}\in PW_{1/4}.

Note that the sampling space is
\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{n}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, which is a subspace of the tempered distribution space. However, the reconstruction space is
\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\cdot-n):n\in\mathbb{Z}\}\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
rather than
PW_{1/4}
, because
{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\notin PW_{1/4}
and
\{{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\cdot-n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is not a frame for
PW_{1/4}
. Thus, the sampling vectors belong to a distributional subspace, while the reconstruction vectors lie in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

For a different perspective, consider a frame
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
for
W
with dual frame
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
in
W
. Then, it follows from
Equation

1.2
that the map
{\mathbf{P}}_{W}:{\mathcal{H}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}
defined by

(1.3)

{\mathbf{P}}_{W}\mathbf{f}=\sum_{i\in I}\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{w}_{i}\rangle\mathbf{v}_{i}

satisfies
{\mathbf{P}}_{W}^{2}={\mathbf{P}}_{W}
with
\range({\mathbf{P}}_{W})=W
and

. In particular,
{\mathbf{P}}_{W}
is the orthogonal projection onto

. A natural question to ask is whether one can define oblique projections with a formula similar to

The mathematical setting for oblique dual frames is as follows. Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a separable Hilbert space and
I
an at-most countable index set. Suppose
W
and
V
are closed subspaces of
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{H}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
where
V^{\perp}
is the orthogonal complement of
V
. Let
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset W
and
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset V
be Bessel sequences for
\mathcal{H}
and further assume that
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
are frames for
W
and
V
, respectively.
Then
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is an
oblique dual frame
of
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
on
V
if

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{f}=\sum_{i\in I}\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle\mathbf{w}_{i},\quad\text{for all}\quad\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{H},

where the map

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}:{\mathcal{H}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}},\quad{\mathbf{f}}\mapsto\sum_{i\in I}\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle\mathbf{w}_{i}

is the oblique projection of
{\mathcal{H}}
onto
W
along
V^{\perp}
.
Furthermore,
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
are called the
sampling
(or
analysis
)
vectors
and
V
the
sampling space
. Similarly,
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
are called the
reconstruction
(or
synthesis
)
vectors
and
W
the
reconstruction space
. Note that when
V=W
, the definition of oblique dual frame reduces to the standard dual frame setting. When
\mathcal{H}
is finite-dimensional, the frames are finite, and the index set
I
is finite.

Our contributions in this paper focus on the study of oblique dual frame potentials and their probabilistic counterparts. The paper is organized as follows. In
Section

2
, we give some background and preliminary results on oblique dual frames, probabilistic frames, and optimal transport, which serves as a key tool in the study of probabilistic frames. In
Section

3
, we study the oblique dual frame potential where
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
. In this setting, suppose
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset W
is a frame for
W
with the frame operator
\mathbf{S}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{w}_{i}\mathbf{w}_{i}^{*}
, and
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset V
is an oblique dual frame of
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
on
V
. In
Proposition

3.3
, we show that

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{2}\geq d_{W},

where
d_{W}
is the dimension of
W
. Moreover, equality holds if and only if
\{\mathbf{v}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
is the canonical oblique dual
\{{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
. We further show that the mixed coherence between
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
and
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
satisfies

\underset{i\neq j}{\text{max}}\ |\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{2}\geq\frac{d_{W}(N-d_{W})}{N^{2}(N-1)},

where saturation requires the existence of an
(N,d_{W})
-equiangular tight frame.

In
Section

4
, we introduce the notion of oblique dual probabilistic frames where
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\mathbb{R}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
. Recall that
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W)
is called a
probabilistic frame
for
W
if there exist
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that for any
{\bf x}\in W
,

A\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2}\leq\int_{W}|\left\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\right\rangle|^{2}d\mu(\mathbf{y})\leq B\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2}.

Then we will say
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(V)
is an
oblique dual probabilistic frame
of
\mu
on
V
if there exists a transport coupling
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}^{t}d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),

where
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}
is the oblique projection of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto
W
along
V^{\perp}
.
Furthermore, given
\epsilon\geq 0
,

\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(V)
is called an
oblique
\epsilon
-approximately dual probabilistic frame
of
\mu
on
V
if there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that

\left\|\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}^{t}d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})-{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\right\|\leq\epsilon.

In
Section

5
, we introduce the oblique dual probabilistic frame potential. In particular, in
Theorem

5.5
, we show that if
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W)
is a probabilistic frame for
W
with bounds
0<A\leq B
, and
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(V)
is an oblique dual of
\mu
on
V
, then

\int_{W}\int_{V}|\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle|^{2}d\mu(\mathbf{x})d\nu(\mathbf{y})\geq\frac{A}{B}d_{W},

and equality holds if and only if
\mu
is a tight probabilistic frame for
W
with bound
A>0
and
\nu
is the canonical oblique dual
({{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}_{\mu}})_{\#}\mu
. Furthermore, as shown in
Theorem

5.3
, the tightness assumption can be dropped if we minimize among oblique dual frames of pushforward type.

Finally, in
Section

6
, we study oblique
\epsilon
-approximately dual probabilistic frames. We show that if a probability measure is sufficiently close to an oblique dual pair in the 2-Wasserstein metric, then it constitutes an oblique
\epsilon
-approximately dual probabilistic frame. In particular,
Corollary

6.6
shows that given a probabilistic frame, its oblique dual probabilistic frames are interior points in the set of oblique
\epsilon
-approximately dual frames in the
2
-Wasserstein topology.

2.1.
Oblique Projection, Oblique Dual Frame, and Consistent Reconstruction

The following theorem gives some alternate characterizations of the direct sum assumption
\mathcal{H}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
and
relies on the concept of the maximum angle
\theta_{WV}\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
between the subspaces
W
and
V
given by

\cos(\theta_{WV}):=\inf_{{\mathbf{f}\in W,\,\|\mathbf{f}\|=1}}\|{\mathbf{P}}_{V}\mathbf{f}\|.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
43
, Theorem 2.3]
)
.

Suppose
W
and
V
are closed subspaces of a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{H}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
.

(2)

\mathcal{H}=V\oplus W^{\perp}
.

(3)

\cos(\theta_{VW})>0
and
\cos(\theta_{WV})>0
.

(4)

There exist Riesz bases
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
for

W
and
V
, respectively, such that
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is biorthogonal to
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
.

We then recall the definition of oblique projection, which can also be used to define oblique dual frames.

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose
\mathcal{H}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
. Then
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow W
is called the
oblique projection
of
\mathcal{H}
onto
W
along
V^{\perp}
if for any
\mathbf{w}\in W
and any
\mathbf{v}\in V^{\perp}
,

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{w}\ \text{and}\ {\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{0}.

One can check that
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}
is the adjoint of
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}
, i.e.,
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}=({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}})^{*}
. Note also that
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WW^{\perp}}={\mathbf{P}}_{W}
, the orthogonal projection onto
W
. Moreover, the following lemma gives basic identities for oblique projections.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
28
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Let
W
and
V
be closed subspaces of
\mathcal{H}
so that
\mathcal{H}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
. Then
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\mathbf{P}}_{W}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}
and
{\mathbf{P}}_{W}{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}={\mathbf{P}}_{W}
.

Proof.

Since
W^{\perp}\subset\kernel({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}})
, we see that

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\mathbf{P}}_{W}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}({\mathbf{P}}_{W}+{\mathbf{P}}_{W^{\perp}})={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}.

Similarly,

{\mathbf{P}}_{W}{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}={\mathbf{P}}_{W}({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}+{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{W^{\perp}V})={\mathbf{P}}_{W}.

∎

Note that if
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is an oblique dual frame of
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
on
V
, then
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{{\mathbf{P}}_{W}\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
are dual frames for
W
; similarly,

\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{{\mathbf{P}}_{V}\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
are dual frames for
V

[
11
]
.
We are now ready to provide a few equivalent rigorous definitions of oblique dual frames.

Definition 2.4

(
[
11
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

Suppose
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
are frames for
W
and
V
, respectively, where
\mathcal{H}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
. Then
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is said to be an
oblique dual frame
of
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
on
V
if one of the following equivalent conditions holds:

(1)

\mathbf{f}=\sum_{i\in I}\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle\mathbf{w}_{i},\ \text{for any }\mathbf{f}\in W
.

(2)

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{f}=\sum_{i\in I}\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle\mathbf{w}_{i},\ \text{for any }\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{H}
.

(3)

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{f}=\sum_{i\in I}\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{w}_{i}\rangle\mathbf{v}_{i},\ \text{for any }\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{H}
.

(4)

\langle{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\rangle=\sum_{i\in I}\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{g}\rangle,\ \text{for any }\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\in\mathcal{H}
.

(5)

\langle{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\rangle=\sum_{i\in I}\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{w}_{i}\rangle\langle\mathbf{v}_{i},\mathbf{g}\rangle,\ \text{for any }\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\in\mathcal{H}
.

In this case,
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
are called an
oblique dual pair
.

The notion of consistent reconstruction, which was first introduced in
[
44
]
, provides another motivation for studying oblique dual frames.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be frames for
W
and
V
, respectively, where
W
and
V
are closed subspaces of
{\mathcal{H}}
and
W\cap V^{\perp}=\{\bf 0\}
. Then
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
are said to perform
consistent reconstruction
if, for any
\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{H}
,
\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle=\langle\hat{\mathbf{f}},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle
for each
i
. Here,
\hat{\mathbf{f}}=\sum_{i\in I}\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle\mathbf{w}_{i}
is the signal reconstructed from
\mathbf{f}
.

The assumption that
W\cap V^{\perp}=\{0\}
in
Definition

2.5
is there, in part, to ensure unique reconstruction: that is, for any
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\in W
, if
\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{g},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle
for all
i
,
then
\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{g}
. Indeed, if
W\cap V^{\perp}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
and
\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{g},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle
for all
i
, then
\mathbf{f-g}\in V^{\perp}
since
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a frame for
V
. Hence,
\mathbf{f-g}\in W\cap V^{\perp}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
and thus
\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{g}
. Conversely, if there is some nonzero
\mathbf{h}\in W\cap V^{\perp}
, then for any
\mathbf{f}\in W
, the distinct vectors
\mathbf{f}
and
\mathbf{g}:=\mathbf{f}+\mathbf{h}\in W
produce identical samples:
\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{g},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle
for all
i
.

Note that consistent reconstruction implies that
{\mathcal{H}}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
. To see this, suppose
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
perform consistent reconstruction on
\mathcal{H}
. Then for every
\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{H}
, we know that
\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle=\langle\hat{\mathbf{f}},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle
for all
i\in I
. Since
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a frame for
V
, this implies
\mathbf{f}-\hat{\mathbf{f}}\in V^{\perp}
. This means that every
\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{H}
admits the decomposition

\mathbf{f}=\hat{\mathbf{f}}+(\mathbf{f}-\hat{\mathbf{f}})\in W\oplus V^{\perp}.

Since
W\cap V^{\perp}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
, this implies
\mathcal{H}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
.

5
]
: for any
\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{H}
,

\|\mathbf{f}-{\mathbf{P}}_{W}\mathbf{f}\|\leq\|\mathbf{f}-{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{f}\|\leq\frac{1}{\cos(\theta_{WV})}\|\mathbf{f}-{\mathbf{P}}_{W}\mathbf{f}\|,

where
\theta_{WV}\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
is the maximum angle between subspaces
W
and
V
.
This inequality quantifies the stability and loss introduced by using distinct sampling and reconstruction subspaces. Moreover, it shows that the reconstruction via
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}
and oblique dual frames is at least as accurate as the orthogonal projection onto
W
, up to a factor depending on the angle between
W
and
V
.

The following is a simple example of oblique dual frames in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Example 2.8
.

Suppose
\mathbf{x}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}
,
\mathbf{y}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix}
, and
\mathbf{z}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
-1\end{pmatrix}
.
Let
W=\spanning\{\mathbf{x}\}
and
V=\spanning\{\mathbf{y}\}
. Then
V^{\perp}=\spanning\{\mathbf{z}\}
and
\mathbb{R}^{2}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
. Clearly,
\{\mathbf{x}\}
is a frame for
W
and
\{\mathbf{y}\}
is a frame for
V
. And for any
\mathbf{f}=\begin{pmatrix}a\\
0\end{pmatrix}\in W
where
a\in\mathbb{R}
,

\mathbf{f}=a\mathbf{x}=\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{y}\rangle\mathbf{x}.

Thus,
\{\mathbf{y}\}
is an oblique dual frame of
\{\mathbf{x}\}
on

.

2.2.
Probabilistic Frames and Optimal Transport

Let
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the set of Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the set of Borel probability measures with finite second moments. That is, if
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then its second moment satisfies

M_{2}(\mu):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|{\bf x}\|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})<+\infty.

The
support
of
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined by

\supp(\mu)=\left\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\text{for any}\ r>0,\,\mu(B_{r}({\bf x}))>0\right\},

where
B_{r}({\bf x})
is the open ball centered at
{\bf x}
with radius
r>0
.
If
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a Borel measurable map where
n
may differ from
m
, then
f_{\#}\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
is called the
pushforward
of
\mu
by the map
f
, and is defined as

f_{\#}\mu(E):=(\mu\circ f^{-1})(E)=\mu\big(f^{-1}(E)\big)\ \text{for any Borel set}\ E\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}.

If
f
is linear and represented by a matrix
{\bf A}
with respect to some basis, then
{\bf A}_{\#}\mu
is used to denote
f_{\#}\mu
. In particular,
(\mathbf{Id},f)
is used to denote the map from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
via
\mathbf{x}\mapsto(\mathbf{x},f(\mathbf{x}))
. In such a case,
(\mathbf{Id},f)_{\#}\mu
is a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
that is supported on the graph of
f
.

Finite frames can be viewed as discrete probability measures. Consequently, one can use probability tools (namely, optimal transport) to study frames. Suppose
\{{\bf x}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
is a finite frame with bounds
0<A\leq B
for a subspace
W
in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Letting
\mu_{f}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{{\bf x}_{i}}\in\mathcal{P}(W)
, the frame definition in
Equation

1.1
becomes

\frac{A}{N}\|{\bf x}\|^{2}\leq\int_{W}|\left\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\right\rangle|^{2}d\mu_{f}({\bf y})\leq\frac{B}{N}\|{\bf x}\|^{2},\ \text{for any}\ {\bf x}\in W.

Inspired by this observation, Ehler
[
19
]
introduced the concept of probabilistic frames. Subsequently, Ehler and Okoudjou investigated the probabilistic frame potential
[
17
]
and surveyed this area with particular emphasis on its connections to optimal transport
[
18
]
.
Note that when
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is closed (e.g., a subspace),
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(W)
means that we can extend the measure to a measure in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by setting the measure of the open set
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus W
to be zero. We will denote this extended measure also with
\mu
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
W
be a subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(W)
is called a
probabilistic frame
for
W
if there exist
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that for any
{\bf x}\in W
,

A\|{\bf x}\|^{2}\leq\int_{W}|\left\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\right\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf y})\leq B\|{\bf x}\|^{2}.

The probabilistic frame
\mu
is said to be
tight
if we may choose
A=B
and
Parseval
if
A=B=1
. Moreover,
\mu
is a
Bessel probability measure
for
W
if the upper (but possibly not the lower) bound holds.

One can also define the
frame operator

{\bf S}_{\mu}
for a probabilistic frame
\mu
on
W
as the following symmetric positive semi-definite matrix

{\bf S}_{\mu}:=\int_{W}{\bf y}{\bf y}^{t}d\mu({\bf y}).

Probabilistic frames can be characterized by their frame operators.

Proposition 2.10

(
[
18
, Theorem 12.1]
and
[
36
, Proposition 3.1]
)
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(W)
. Then
\mu
is a probabilistic frame on
W
if and only if
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W)
and
\spanning\{\supp(\mu)\}=W
. Furthermore, if
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W)
, then
\mu
is a probabilistic frame on
W
if and only if the restriction of
{\bf S}_{\mu}
to
W
is positive definite, and
\mu
is a tight probabilistic frame on
W
with bound
A>0
if and only if
{\bf S}_{\mu}=A\ {\mathbf{P}}_{W}
, where
{\mathbf{P}}_{W}
is the orthogonal projection of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto
W
.

Since probabilistic frames on
W
have finite second moments, they naturally lie in
\mathcal{P}_{2}(W)
and can be studied using optimal transport and the 2-Wasserstein distance
[
18
,
48
]
. Given two probabilistic frames
\mu
and
\nu
on
W
, suppose
\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
is the set of transport couplings with marginals
\mu
and
\nu
, that is,

\Gamma(\mu,\nu):=\left\{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(W\times W):{\pi_{{x}}}_{\#}\gamma=\mu,\ {\pi_{{y}}}_{\#}\gamma=\nu\right\},

where
\pi_{{x}}
,
\pi_{{y}}
are projections onto the
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
coordinates: for any
({\bf x},{\bf y})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\pi_{{x}}({\bf x},{\bf y})={\bf x}
and
\pi_{{y}}({\bf x},{\bf y})={\bf y}
. The 2-Wasserstein distance
W_{2}(\mu,\nu)
is often used to quantify the distance between
\mu
and
\nu
:

W_{2}(\mu,\nu):=\left(\underset{\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)}{\operatorname{inf}}\int_{W\times W}\left\|{\bf x}-{\bf y}\right\|^{2}\ d\gamma({\bf x},{\bf y})\right)^{1/2}.

This is of interest even if one is only concerned with finite frames, since the
2
-Wasserstein distance can quantify the distance between probabilistic frames induced by finite frames of different cardinalities.
See
[
25
]
for details on optimal transport and Wasserstein distance.

Let
W
be a subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W)
. As above, consider the extension of
\mu
—which we also call
\mu
—to
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
by setting the measure of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus W
to be zero. We may then extend any measurable
f:W\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
by zero-padding or restrict any
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
to
W\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
to see that
L^{2}(\mu,W)\cong L^{2}(\mu,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Furthermore, note that
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W)
also implies
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, since

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|{\bf x}\|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})=\int_{W}\|{\bf x}\|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})<+\infty.

Therefore, for
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W)
, its analysis operator
U_{\mu}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow L^{2}(\mu,W)
given by
(U_{\mu}{\bf x})(\cdot)=\langle{\bf x},\cdot\rangle\in L^{2}(\mu,W)
is well-defined (bounded), because for any
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\|U_{\mu}{\bf x}\|^{2}=\int_{W}|\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf y})\leq\|{\bf x}\|^{2}M_{2}(\mu).

Then the adjoint (synthesis) operator
U_{\mu}^{*}:L^{2}(\mu,W)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
exists and is given by

\ U_{\mu}^{*}(\psi)=\int_{W}{\bf x}\psi({\bf x})d\mu({\bf x}).

One can verify that as a map on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the frame operator satisfies
{\bf S}_{\mu}=U_{\mu}^{*}U_{\mu}
.

Similar to frames, one can reconstruct signals using
dual probabilistic frames
, which are also known as transport duals
[
48
]
.

Definition 2.11
.

Suppose
\mu
is a probabilistic frame for
W
.
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W)
is called a
dual probabilistic frame
of
\mu
on
W
if there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that

\int_{W\times W}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x},{\bf y})={\mathbf{P}}_{W},

which is equivalent to the statement that for any
{\bf f}\in W
,
\int_{W\times W}{\bf x}\langle{\bf y},{\bf f}\rangle d\gamma({\bf x},{\bf y})={\bf f}
.

Note that
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(V)
automatically implies (via extension) that
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and one can still define
\Gamma(\mu,\nu):=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(W\times V):{\pi_{{x}}}_{\#}\gamma=\mu,\ {\pi_{{y}}}_{\#}\gamma=\nu\}
. In addition, if
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
, then
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W\times V)
also implies
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
, that is,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}^{t}d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}^{t}d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame for
W
and
{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}
the Moore–Penrose inverse of
{\bf S}_{\mu}
. Then
{{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}}_{\#}\mu
is called the canonical dual frame of
\mu
on
W
, since

\int_{W\times W}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d(\mathbf{Id},{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger})_{\#}\mu({\bf x,y})=\int_{W}{\bf x}{\bf x}^{t}d\mu({\bf x})\ {\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}={\bf S}_{\mu}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}={\mathbf{P}}_{W},

where the last identity follows from
Lemma

2.12
.
Similarly,
{({\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger})^{\frac{1}{2}}}_{\#}\mu
is called the canonical Parseval frame of
\mu
on
W
, since

\int_{W}{\bf x}{\bf x}^{t}d{({\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger})^{\frac{1}{2}}}_{\#}\mu({\bf x})=({\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger})^{1/2}{\bf S}_{\mu}({\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger})^{1/2}={\mathbf{P}}_{W}.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
W
be a subspace in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mu
be a probabilistic frame for
W
with frame operator
{\bf S}_{\mu}
. Then
\mathrm{Ran}({\bf S}_{\mu})=W
and
\mathrm{Ker}({\bf S}_{\mu})=W^{\perp}
. Furthermore,
{\bf S}_{\mu}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}
,
{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf S}_{\mu}
, and
({\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger})^{1/2}{\bf S}_{\mu}({\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger})^{1/2}
are orthogonal projections of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto the subspace
W
.

Proof.

By standard properties of the Moore–Penrose inverse,
{\bf S}_{\mu}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}
is the orthogonal projection onto the range of
{\bf S}_{\mu}
. Since
\mu
is a probabilistic frame for
W
, then by
Proposition

2.10
,
\mu
is supported on
W
. Then for any
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

{\bf S}_{\mu}\mathbf{f}=\int_{W}\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{x}\rangle\mathbf{x}d\mu(\mathbf{x})\in W.

That is to say,
\mathrm{Ran}({\bf S}_{\mu})\subseteq W
. Also note that
W^{\perp}\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}({\bf S}_{\mu})
, since for any
\mathbf{f}\in W^{\perp}
,
{\bf S}_{\mu}\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{0}
. If we can show that
\mathrm{Ker}({\bf S}_{\mu})=W^{\perp}
, then it would follow from the Rank–Nullity theorem that

\Dim(\mathrm{Ran}({\bf S}_{\mu}))+\Dim(W^{\perp})=n,

and hence that

\mathrm{Ran}({\bf S}_{\mu})=W
.

Now let us show
\mathrm{Ker}({\bf S}_{\mu})=W^{\perp}
. Suppose
\mathbf{f}\in\operatorname{Ker}({\bf S}_{\mu})
. Since
\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{f}_{W}+\mathbf{f}_{W^{\perp}}
, where
\mathbf{f}_{W}\in W
and
\mathbf{f}_{W^{\perp}}\in W^{\perp}
, we have
{\bf S}_{\mu}\mathbf{f}_{W^{\perp}}=\mathbf{0}
and hence

\mathbf{0}={\bf S}_{\mu}\mathbf{f}={\bf S}_{\mu}(\mathbf{f}_{W}+\mathbf{f}_{W^{\perp}})={\bf S}_{\mu}\mathbf{f}_{W}.

Since
\mu
is a probabilistic frame for
W
, the restriction of
{\bf S}_{\mu}
on
W
is injective by
Proposition

2.10
, so
\mathbf{0}={\bf S}_{\mu}\mathbf{f}_{W}
implies
\mathbf{f}_{W}=\mathbf{0}
. Therefore,
\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{f}_{W^{\perp}}\in W^{\perp}
and we conclude that
\mathrm{Ker}({\bf S}_{\mu})=W^{\perp}
.

We have now shown that
\mathrm{Ran}({\bf S}_{\mu})=W
,
and thus
{\bf S}_{\mu}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}
is the orthogonal projection of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto
W
. Since
{\bf S}_{\mu}
is symmetric, it follows that
{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf S}_{\mu}
is also the orthogonal projection onto
W
. Finally, the spectral decomposition of
{\bf S}_{\mu}
shows that
({\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger})^{1/2}{\bf S}_{\mu}({\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger})^{1/2}
is the orthogonal projection of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto the subspace
W
.
∎

We also need the following gluing lemma, a standard tool in optimal transport, to “glue” two transport couplings together. Analogously to
\pi_{x}
and
\pi_{y}
, let
\pi_{xy}
and
\pi_{yz}
be the orthogonal projections onto the corresponding coordinates, i.e., for any
({\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf z})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\pi_{x}({\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf z})={\bf x},\ \pi_{y}({\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf z})={\bf y},\ \pi_{xy}({\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf z})=({\bf x},{\bf y}),\ \pi_{yz}({\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf z})=({\bf y},{\bf z}).

Lemma 2.13

(Gluing Lemma
[
25
, p. 59]
)
.

Let
W_{1}
,
W_{2}
, and
W_{3}
be subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W_{1})
,
\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W_{2})
, and
\mu_{3}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W_{3})
, respectively. Suppose
\gamma^{12}\in\Gamma(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\subset\mathcal{P}(W_{1}\times W_{2})
and
\gamma^{23}\in\Gamma(\mu_{2},\mu_{3})\subset\mathcal{P}(W_{2}\times W_{3})
such that
{\pi_{y}}_{\#}\gamma^{12}=\mu_{2}={\pi_{x}}_{\#}\gamma^{23}
. Then there exists
\gamma^{123}\in\mathcal{P}(W_{1}\times W_{2}\times W_{3})
such that
{\pi_{xy}}_{\#}\gamma^{123}=\gamma^{12}
and
{\pi_{yz}}_{\#}\gamma^{123}=\gamma^{23}
.

3.
Oblique Dual Frame Potential

In this section, we consider the oblique dual frame potential where
\mathcal{H}
is the
n
-dimensional complex space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
W
and
V
are subspaces such that
\mathbb{C}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
.
The frame potential was introduced by Benedetto and Fickus
[
4
]
, who showed that finite unit-norm tight frames are unique optimizers of the potential. The frame potential is the component-wise
2
-norm of the frame’s Gram matrix; generalizations to other
p
-norms and to probabilistic frames appeared in
[
17
]
. Such
p
-norms of inner products are also of interest in spherical designs; see, e.g.,
[
40
]
. Dual frame potentials were introduced in
[
10
]
, where the authors found a lower bound for the dual
2
-frame potential and the minimizer is just the canonical dual. Their work was followed by
[
1
]
, where they call the dual frame potential the “cross-frame potential” and further generalized it to fusion frames. In
[
9
]
, a probabilistic dual
2
-frame potential was introduced.

Our contribution is that we consider the
p
-potential over oblique dual frames; some of the results we obtain recover the previous results concerning dual frames, since dual frames are oblique dual frames with
W=V
.
Note that
\mathbf{v}^{*}
denotes the conjugate transpose of vector
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
N
the number of vectors
\{{\mathbf{v}}_{i}\}_{i}
(respectively,
\{{\mathbf{w}}_{i}\}_{i})
, and
d_{W}
the dimension of
W
, where
d_{W}\leq{\operatorname{min}}\{n,N\}
.
We use
\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}
to denote the Moore–Penrose inverse of a matrix
\mathbf{S}
. By convention, the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is linear in the first input and conjugate linear in the second: for
{\bf x},{\bf y}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle={\bf y}^{*}{\bf x}
.
So the rank-one operator
{\mathbf{w}}{\mathbf{v}}^{\ast}
acts as
{\bf f}\mapsto\langle{\bf f},{\mathbf{v}}\rangle{\mathbf{w}}
.

We first start with the definition of the oblique dual
p
-frame potential. We will show that, for a given frame on
W
, its oblique dual
2
-frame potential is minimized by the canonical oblique dual, which generalizes
[
10
, Theorem 2.2]
concerning the dual
2
-frame potential.

Definition 3.1
.

Suppose
\mathbb{C}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
and
p>0
. Let
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a frame for
W
and
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
an oblique dual frame of
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
on
V
. Then the
oblique dual
p
-frame potential
between
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
and
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
is defined as

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{p}.

When
p=2
, we often drop the parameter and refer to the potential as the
oblique dual frame potential
.

The following lemma extends Proposition 24 in
[
1
]
about dual frames.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a frame for
W
and
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
an oblique dual frame of
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
on
V
, where
\mathbb{C}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle|^{2}\geq\frac{d_{W}^{2}}{N}.

Furthermore, the equality holds if and only if
\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle=\frac{d_{W}}{N}
for each
i
.

Proof.

Since
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
is an oblique dual frame of
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, then
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{w}_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}^{*}
.
Therefore,

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle=\trace\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{w}_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}^{*}\right)=\trace({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}})=d_{W}.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle|^{2}\geq\frac{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle{\mathbf{w}}_{i},{\mathbf{v}}_{i}\right\rangle\right|^{2}}{N}=\frac{d_{W}^{2}}{N},

and the equality holds if and only if the vector
\left(\left\langle{\mathbf{w}}_{i},{\mathbf{v}}_{i}\right\rangle\right)_{i=1}^{N}
is a scalar multiple of the all-ones vector, that is, there exists a constant
C
such that
\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle=C
for each
i
. Computation gives
C=\frac{d_{W}}{N}
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a frame for
W
with frame operator
\mathbf{S}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{w}_{i}\mathbf{w}_{i}^{*}
and
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
an oblique dual frame of
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
on
V
, where
\mathbb{C}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{2}\geq d_{W},

and the equality holds if and only if
\{\mathbf{v}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
is the canonical oblique dual
\{{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
.

Proof.

For any
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
[
23
, Proposition 4]
implies that, among all coefficients
\{c_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\in\ell^{2}(\{1,\dots,N\})
for which
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{f}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}\mathbf{w}_{i}
,
the coefficient sequence with the minimal
\ell^{2}
-norm is given by
\{\langle\mathbf{f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{i}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{N}
. In other words,

\sum_{i=1}^{N}|c_{i}|^{2}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{i}\rangle|^{2}

and the equality holds if and only if
c_{i}=\langle\mathbf{f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{i}\rangle
for each
i
.
Since
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
is an oblique dual frame of
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
on
V
, then for any
{\bf f}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{f}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle\mathbf{w}_{i}
. Therefore,

(3.1)

\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{2}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{j}\rangle|^{2}

and the equality holds if and only if
\mathbf{v}_{j}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{j}
, for each
j
.

Suppose
\mathbf{W}=(\mathbf{w}_{1},\mathbf{w}_{2},\cdots,\mathbf{w}_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times N}
and then
\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*}
. Letting
\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{w}_{i}
in the above equation and summing over
i
, we have

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{2}&\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{j}\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\langle{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{j}\rangle|^{2}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{j}\rangle|^{2}=\|\mathbf{W}^{*}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{W}\|_{\text{Frob}}^{2}\\
&=\trace((\mathbf{W}^{*}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{W})^{*}\mathbf{W}^{*}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{W})=\trace(\mathbf{W}^{*}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{W})\\
&=\trace(\mathbf{W}^{*}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{W})=\trace(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger})=\trace(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger})=\trace({\mathbf{P}}_{W})\\
&=d_{W},\end{split}

where
\|\cdot\|_{\text{Frob}}
is the matrix Frobenius norm and the equalities are based on the cyclic property of matrix trace and the facts that
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}^{*}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}
and
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}={\mathbf{P}}_{W}
. (See Lemma
2.12
and consider
\mu:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{\mathbf{w}_{i}}
.)
The equality clearly holds when
\mathbf{v}_{j}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{j}
for each
j
. Conversely, if the equality holds, then for each
\mathbf{w}_{i}
, we must have

\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{j}\rangle|^{2}.

Then by the equality condition in
Equation

3.1
,
\mathbf{v}_{j}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{j}
, for each
j
.
∎

We now extend
Lemma

3.2
and
Proposition

3.3
from oblique dual
2
-frame potentials to oblique dual
2k
-frame potentials where
k\geq 1
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a frame for
W
with frame operator
\mathbf{S}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{w}_{i}\mathbf{w}_{i}^{*}
and
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
an oblique dual frame of
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
on
V
, where
\mathbb{C}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
.
Assume additionally that
p=2k
, where
k\geq 1
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle|^{p}\geq N^{1-p}d_{W}^{p}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{p}\geq N^{2-p}d_{W}^{\frac{p}{2}}.

Furthermore, when
k>1
, the left-hand inequality is saturated if and only if
\left\langle{\mathbf{w}}_{i},{\mathbf{v}}_{i}\right\rangle=\frac{d_{W}}{N}
for all
i
, and the right-hand inequality is saturated if and only if
|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|
is constant for all
i
and
j
and
\mathbf{v}_{j}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{j}
for each
j
.

Proof.

We will just prove the right-hand inequality. The proof for the left-hand inequality is almost identical but uses
Lemma

3.2
to analyze saturation. Since the map
|\cdot|^{k}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is convex, Jensen’s inequality and
Proposition

3.3
imply

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{p}

\displaystyle=N^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{N^{2}}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{2k}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\geq N^{2-2k}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{2}\right|^{k}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\geq N^{2-2k}d_{W}^{k}=N^{2-p}d_{W}^{\frac{p}{2}}.

When
k>1
, the map
|\cdot|^{k}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is strictly convex. Then saturation of (
3.2
) holds if and only if
|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|
is constant for any
i
and
j
, and saturation of (
3.3
) follows from
Proposition

3.3
.
∎

Our next theorem is motivated by
[
10
, Theorem 2.3 and Proposition 2.4]
, where the authors obtained a lower bound for the mixed coherence of dual frames. We generalize their results to the oblique dual frame setting and also polish the proof in the standard dual setting.
A family of unit-norm vectors
\{\mathbf{f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}

is an
(N,n)
-
equiangular tight frame
(ETF) if
\{\mathbf{f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
is tight and if there exists an
\alpha\geq 0
such that for any
i\neq j
,
\left|\langle{\mathbf{f}}_{i},{\mathbf{f}}_{j}\rangle\right|^{2}=\alpha
.
For an arbitrary set of unit-norm vectors
\{\mathbf{f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
where
N\geq n
, the
coherence
is
\max_{i\neq j}|\langle\mathbf{f}_{i},\mathbf{f}_{j}\rangle|^{2}
. It has been shown (e.g.,
[
47
]
) that the coherence satisfies

(3.4)

\max_{i\neq j}|\langle\mathbf{f}_{i},\mathbf{f}_{j}\rangle|^{2}\geq\frac{N-n}{n(N-1)},

and the equality holds if and only if
\{\mathbf{f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
is an ETF. The inequality in (
3.4
) is known as the
Welch-Rankin bound
.
ETFs have applications in coding theory
[
37
]
, communication systems
[
41
]
, and quantum information processing
[
49
,
39
]
.
The construction and existence of ETFs have gained substantial attention
[
42
,
29
,
6
,
3
]
, including the famous Zauner’s Conjecture about the existence of an ETF consisting of
n^{2}
vectors in
\mathbb{C}^{n}

[
49
,
3
]
. In the following theorem, we show that the equality in the mixed coherence of oblique duals is saturated if and only if there exists an
(N,d_{W})
-ETF and the oblique dual is canonical.

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose
W
and
V
are subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\mathbb{C}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
. Let
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a frame for
W
with frame operator
\mathbf{S}
and
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
an oblique dual frame of
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
on
V
such that
\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{w}_{j},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle
for all
i
and
j
. Then

\underset{i\neq j}{\max}\ |\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{2}\geq\frac{d_{W}(N-d_{W})}{N^{2}(N-1)}.

Furthermore, the equality holds if and only if any of the following equivalent conditions is true:

(1)

For any
i\neq j
,
|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|
is constant, and
\mathbf{v}_{j}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{j}
, for each
j
.

(2)

The mixed Gram matrix
\mathbf{G}=(\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle)_{ij}
between
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
and
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
is

\mathbf{G}=\frac{d_{W}}{N}\left(\mathbf{Id}_{N\times N}+\sqrt{\frac{N-d_{W}}{d_{W}(N-1)}}\mathbf{Q}\right),

where
\mathbf{Id}_{N\times N}
is the identity matrix of size
N\times N
and
\mathbf{Q}
is a generalized signature matrix (self-adjoint, a zero diagonal and unimodular entries off the diagonal).

(3)

For each
i
, define
\boldsymbol{\psi}_{i}:=\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{d_{W}}}\,(\mathbf{S}^{\dagger})^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{w}_{i}
.
Then
\{\boldsymbol{\psi}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
is an
(N,d_{W})
- equiangular tight frame for
W
and
\mathbf{v}_{j}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{j}
, for each
j
.

Proof.

Since
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
is an oblique dual of
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
on
V
and the
\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle
are constant, we have

\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle=\frac{d_{W}}{N}
for each
i
since

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle=\trace\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{w}_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}^{*}\right)=\trace({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}})=d_{W}.

Therefore,

\begin{split}\underset{i\neq j}{\text{max}}\ |\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{2}&\geq\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\sum_{i\neq j}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle|^{2}\right)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\left(d_{W}-\frac{d_{W}^{2}}{N}\right)=\frac{d_{W}(N-d_{W})}{N^{2}(N-1)},\end{split}

where the last inequality follows from
Proposition

3.3
and
d_{W}\geq\frac{d_{W}^{2}}{N}
(since
d_{W}\leq N
).
Hence, the equality holds if and only if
|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|
is constant for all
i\neq j
and (by the equality condition in
Proposition

3.3
)
\mathbf{v}_{j}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{j}
for each
j
, where
\mathbf{S}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{w}_{i}\mathbf{w}_{i}^{*}
. Therefore, equality is equivalent to
(1)
.

In turn,
(1)
implies
|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|=\frac{1}{N}\sqrt{\frac{d_{W}(N-d_{W})}{N-1}}
for any
i\neq j
, so the mixed Gram matrix
\mathbf{G}
between
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
and
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
can be written as

\mathbf{G}=\frac{d_{W}}{N}\left(\mathbf{Id}_{N\times N}+\sqrt{\frac{N-d_{W}}{d_{W}(N-1)}}\mathbf{Q}\right),

where
\mathbf{Id}_{N\times N}
is the identity matrix of size
N\times N
and
\mathbf{Q}
is a matrix with zero diagonal and unimodular entries off the diagonal. Further,
(1)
yields that the
i,j
entry of the mixed Gram is

\displaystyle\left\langle{\mathbf{w}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\mathbf{S}}^{\dagger}{\mathbf{w}}_{j}\right\rangle=\left\langle{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}{\mathbf{w}}_{i},{\mathbf{S}}^{\dagger}{\mathbf{w}}_{j}\right\rangle=\left\langle{\mathbf{w}}_{i},{\mathbf{S}}^{\dagger}{\mathbf{w}}_{j}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle({\mathbf{S}}^{1/2})^{\dagger}{\mathbf{w}}_{i},({\mathbf{S}}^{1/2})^{\dagger}{\mathbf{w}}_{j}\right\rangle=\overline{\left\langle({\mathbf{S}}^{1/2})^{\dagger}{\mathbf{w}}_{j},({\mathbf{S}}^{1/2})^{\dagger}{\mathbf{w}}_{i}\right\rangle}=\overline{\left\langle{\mathbf{w}}_{j},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\mathbf{S}}^{\dagger}{\mathbf{w}}_{i}\right\rangle},

so
{\mathbf{Q}}
is self-adjoint and
(1)
implies
(2)
.
Conversely, if
\mathbf{G}
is given as in
(2)
, then for any
i\neq j
,
|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|=\frac{d_{W}}{N}\sqrt{\frac{N-d_{W}}{d_{W}(N-1)}}
. Hence

\underset{i\neq j}{\text{max}}\ |\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{2}=\frac{d_{W}(N-d_{W})}{N^{2}(N-1)},

which implies
(1)
.

Finally, let us show
(2)
and
(3)
are equivalent. Define matrices
\mathbf{V}=(\mathbf{v}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{v}_{N})
,
\mathbf{W}=(\mathbf{w}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{w}_{N})
, and
\boldsymbol{\Psi}=(\boldsymbol{\psi}_{1},\cdots,\boldsymbol{\psi}_{N})
, where
\boldsymbol{\Psi}=\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{d_{W}}}\,(\mathbf{S}^{\dagger})^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{W}
. Thus,
\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{*}\boldsymbol{\Psi}=\tfrac{N}{d_{W}}\mathbf{W}^{*}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{W}
. If
(2)
holds, then the equality holds and thus
\mathbf{v}_{j}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{j}
for each
j
. Hence
\mathbf{V}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{W}
and

\frac{d_{W}}{N}\left(\mathbf{I}_{N\times N}+\sqrt{\frac{N-d_{W}}{d_{W}(N-1)}}\mathbf{Q}\right)=\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{V}^{*}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{W}^{*}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{W})=\mathbf{W}^{*}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{W}.

Therefore, the Gram matrix of
\{\boldsymbol{\psi}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
is given by

(3.5)

\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{*}\boldsymbol{\Psi}=\tfrac{N}{d_{W}}\mathbf{W}^{*}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{Id}_{N\times N}+\sqrt{\frac{N-d_{W}}{d_{W}(N-1)}}\mathbf{Q}

and the Welch bound saturation condition for (
3.4
) implies that
\{\boldsymbol{\psi}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
is an
(N,d_{W})
-equiangular tight frame.
Conversely, if
(3)
holds, then
\{\boldsymbol{\psi}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
is an
(N,d_{W})
-equiangular tight frame and its Gram matrix is given as
Equation

3.5
. Since
\mathbf{v}_{j}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{j}
for each
j
, then
\mathbf{V}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{W}
and thus

\begin{split}\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{V}^{*}\mathbf{W}&=\mathbf{W}^{*}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{W})=\mathbf{W}^{*}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{W}\\
&=\frac{d_{W}}{N}\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{*}\boldsymbol{\Psi}=\frac{d_{W}}{N}\left(\mathbf{Id}_{N\times N}+\sqrt{\frac{N-d_{W}}{d_{W}(N-1)}}\mathbf{Q}\right).\end{split}

∎

The last corollary is inspired by
[
1
, Theorem 26]
and
Proposition

3.3
. Note that the condition of
\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle
being constant for each
i
in
Theorem

3.5
and
Corollary

3.6
also holds in
Example

1.1
: for each
j
,
\langle\delta_{j},\phi_{j}\rangle=\phi(j-j)=\phi(0)
, where
\delta_{j}
is the Dirac distribution at
j
and
\phi_{j}(t)=\phi(t-j)
. In addition, one of the equality conditions in
Theorem

3.5
and
Corollary

3.6
makes the oblique dual frames
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
and
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
equiangular, that is,
|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|
is constant for any
i\neq j
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Suppose
W
and
V
are subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\mathbb{C}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
. Let
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a frame for
W
with frame operator
\mathbf{S}
and
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
an oblique dual frame of
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
on
V
such that
\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{w}_{j},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle
for all
i
and
j
.
Assume additionally that
p=2k
where
k\geq 1
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{p}\geq\frac{|d_{W}-\frac{d_{W}^{2}}{N}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}{N^{{\frac{p}{2}}-1}(N-1)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}}+\frac{d_{W}^{p}}{N^{p-1}}.

Furthermore, when
k>1
, the equality holds if and only if for any
i\neq j
,
|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|
is constant, and
\mathbf{v}_{j}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{j}
, for each
j
.

Proof.

By the proof of
Theorem

3.5
, we know that

\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle=\frac{d_{W}}{N}
for each
i
.
Since the map
|\cdot|^{k}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is convex, Jensen’s inequality shows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq j}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{p}

\displaystyle=N(N-1)\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{1}{N(N-1)}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{2k}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|\sum_{i\neq j}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{2}|^{k}}{N^{k-1}(N-1)^{k-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle|^{2}|^{k}}{N^{k-1}(N-1)^{k-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{2}-\frac{d_{W}^{2}}{N}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}{N^{{\frac{p}{2}}-1}(N-1)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq j}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle|^{p}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|^{2}-\frac{d_{W}^{2}}{N}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}{N^{{\frac{p}{2}}-1}(N-1)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}}+\frac{d_{W}^{p}}{N^{p-1}}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|d_{W}-\frac{d_{W}^{2}}{N}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}{N^{{\frac{p}{2}}-1}(N-1)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}}+\frac{d_{W}^{p}}{N^{p-1}},

where (
3.7
) follows from (
3.6
) and the fact that
\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle=\frac{d_{W}}{N}
for each
i
, and (
3.8
) from
Proposition

3.3
and
d_{W}\geq\frac{d_{W}^{2}}{N}
.

When
k>1
the map
|\cdot|^{k}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is strictly convex, so equality in (
3.6
) holds if and only if
|\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\rangle|
is constant for any
i\neq j
. This then implies the equality condition for (
3.7
). For saturation of (
3.8
), we already have from
Theorem

3.5
that
\mathbf{v}_{j}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{j}
, for each
j
.
∎

4.
Oblique Dual Probabilistic Frames

In this section, we introduce oblique dual probabilistic frames, which generalizes oblique dual frames into the probabilistic frame setting. Throughout the remaining sections,
\mathcal{H}
is the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
W
and
V
are subspaces such that
\mathbb{R}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
. From now on, the inner product is the standard dot product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We use
(\cdot)^{t}
to denote the transpose of a vector or matrix and
d_{W}
the dimension of
W
where
1\leq d_{W}\leq n
.
We first give a characterization motivated by the characterization of oblique duals in
[
11
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
\mathbb{R}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
. Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W)
and
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(V)
be Bessel probability measures with bounds
B_{\mu}>0
and
B_{\nu}>0
, respectively, and
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

For any
\mathbf{f}\in W
,
\mathbf{f}=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{f}\rangle d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
.

(2)

For any
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{f}=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{f}\rangle d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
.

(3)

For any
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{f}=\int_{W\times V}\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{f}\rangle\mathbf{y}d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
.

(4)

For any
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\langle{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\rangle=\int_{W\times V}\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{g}\rangle\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{f}\rangle d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
.

(5)

For any
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\langle{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\rangle=\int_{W\times V}\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{f}\rangle\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{g}\rangle d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
.

If any of the equivalent conditions is satisfied, then
\mu
and
\nu
are probabilistic frames for
W
and
V
with lower bounds
\frac{1}{B_{\nu}}
and
\frac{1}{B_{\mu}}
, respectively. Furthermore,
\mu
and
{{\mathbf{P}}_{W}}_{\#}\nu
are dual frames for
W
, and
{{\mathbf{P}}_{V}}_{\#}\mu
and
\nu
are dual frames for
V
.

Proof.

Note that
(2)
implies
(1)
trivially. To see that
(1)
implies
(2)
, suppose
(1)
holds and let
\textbf{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{f}\in W
, so

\begin{split}{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{f}&=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\langle\mathbf{y},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{f}\rangle d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\langle{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}^{*}\mathbf{y},\mathbf{f}\rangle d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\\
&=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\langle{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{y},\mathbf{f}\rangle d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{f}\rangle d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).\end{split}

In addition,
(2)
is equivalent to
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}^{t}d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
.
Taking the adjoint on both sides leads to

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}^{*}=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{x}^{t}d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),

which is equivalent to
(3)
.

Next,
(2)
implies
(4)
trivially. On the other hand, if
(4)
is true, then for any
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

\left\langle{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{f}-\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{f}\rangle d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),\mathbf{g}\right\rangle=0,

which implies
(2)
.
(3)
and
(5)
are equivalent in a similar way.

If any of the equivalent conditions is satisfied, then
(4)
shows that for any
\mathbf{f}\in W
,

\begin{split}\|\mathbf{f}\|^{4}=\left|\int_{W\times V}\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{f}\rangle\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{f}\rangle d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\right|^{2}&\leq\int_{W}|\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{f}\rangle|^{2}d\mu(\mathbf{x})\int_{V}|\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{f}\rangle|^{2}d\nu(\mathbf{y})\\
&\leq B_{\nu}\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2}\int_{W}|\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{f}\rangle|^{2}d\mu(\mathbf{x}).\end{split}

Therefore,
\mu
is a probabilistic frame for
W
with lower bound
\frac{1}{B_{\nu}}
and upper bound
M_{2}(\mu)
. Similarly,
(5)
shows that
\nu
is a probabilistic frame for
V
with lower bound
\frac{1}{B_{\mu}}
and upper bound
M_{2}(\nu)
.
Moreover,
(1)
tells us that for any
\mathbf{f}\in W
,

\mathbf{f}=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\langle{\mathbf{P}}_{W}\mathbf{y},\mathbf{f}\rangle d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\int_{W\times W}\mathbf{x}\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{f}\rangle d\tilde{\gamma}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})

where
\tilde{\gamma}:=(\mathbf{Id},{\mathbf{P}}_{W})_{\#}\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,{{\mathbf{P}}_{W}}_{\#}\nu)
.
Therefore,
\mu
and
{{\mathbf{P}}_{W}}_{\#}\nu
are dual frames for
W
. Similarly, one can use
(3)
to show that
{{\mathbf{P}}_{V}}_{\#}\mu
and
\nu
are dual frames for
V
with respect to the coupling
({\mathbf{P}}_{V},\mathbf{Id})_{\#}\gamma\in\Gamma({{\mathbf{P}}_{V}}_{\#}\mu,\nu)
.
∎

We give the following definition for oblique dual probabilistic frames.

Definition 4.2
.

Suppose
\mathbb{R}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W)
. Then
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(V)
is called an
oblique dual probabilistic frame
of
\mu
on
V
if there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}^{t}d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

Of course, we could have used any of the equivalent conditions from
Lemma

4.1
in this definition.

In what follows, we frequently refer to an oblique dual probabilistic frame as an oblique dual frame or simply an oblique dual.
In particular, if
T:W\rightarrow V
is measurable and
T_{\#}\mu
is an oblique dual of
\mu
with respect to
(\mathbf{Id},T)_{\#}\mu\in\Gamma(\mu,T_{\#}\mu)
, then
T_{\#}\mu
is called an oblique dual probabilistic frame of
pushforward type
.

Oblique duals of pushforward type include all oblique duals for finite frames. To see this, suppose
\mu=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{\mathbf{w}_{i}}
is a probabilistic frame for
W
,
T:W\to V
is measurable, and

\nu=T_{\#}\mu=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{T(\mathbf{w}_{i})}

is a probabilistic frame for
V
. If
\nu
is an oblique dual of
\mu
with respect to
(\mathbf{Id},T)_{\#}\mu
, then

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}=\int_{W}\mathbf{x}\left(T(\mathbf{x})\right)^{t}d\mu(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{w}_{i}\left(\frac{1}{N}T(\mathbf{w}_{i})\right)^{t},

which implies that
\{\frac{1}{N}T(\mathbf{w}_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
is an oblique dual frame of
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
on
V
. Under this interpretation, we still need the direct sum structure
\mathbb{R}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
for the consistent reconstruction of oblique dual frames. Furthermore, for any signal
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, if we denote the reconstructed signal
\hat{\mathbf{f}}
as

\hat{\mathbf{f}}=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\langle\mathbf{y,f}\rangle d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle\mathbf{f},T(\mathbf{w}_{i})\rangle\mathbf{w}_{i},

then we must have
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{f}=\hat{\mathbf{f}}
, equivalent to the consistent reconstruction by
Theorem

2.6
.
Similar to
Definition

2.5
, we have the following definition for probabilistic consistent reconstruction, which characterizes oblique dual probabilistic frames.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\mu
and
\nu
be probabilistic frames for
W
and
V
respectively, where
\mathbb{R}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
. Then
\mu
and
\nu
are said to perform
probabilistic consistent reconstruction
if there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that
for any
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{z}\rangle=\langle\hat{\mathbf{f}},\mathbf{z}\rangle
for
\nu
-almost all
\mathbf{z}\in V
, where
\hat{\mathbf{f}}
is the reconstructed signal given by

\hat{\mathbf{f}}=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\langle\mathbf{y,f}\rangle d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\mu
and
\nu
be probabilistic frames for
W
and
V
respectively, where
\mathbb{R}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
. Then
\mu
and
\nu
perform probabilistic consistent reconstruction if and only if
\nu
is an oblique dual probabilistic frame of
\mu
on
V
.

Proof.

Suppose
\nu
is an oblique dual probabilistic frame of
\mu
on
V
. Then there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}^{t}d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

Hence, for any
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\hat{\mathbf{f}}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{f}
, and for all
\mathbf{z}\in V
,

\langle\hat{\mathbf{f}},\mathbf{z}\rangle=\langle{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{f},\mathbf{z}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{z}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{z}\rangle,

which implies that
\mu
and
\nu
perform probabilistic consistent reconstruction.

Conversely, if
\mu
and
\nu
perform probabilistic consistent reconstruction, then there exists
\tilde{\gamma}\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that for any
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for
\nu
almost all
\mathbf{z}\in V
,
\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{z}\rangle=\langle\hat{\mathbf{f}},\mathbf{z}\rangle
, where

\hat{\mathbf{f}}=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\langle\mathbf{y,f}\rangle d\tilde{\gamma}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

For a given
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let

\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{f}}:=\{\mathbf{y}\in V:\langle\hat{\mathbf{f}},\mathbf{y}\rangle\neq\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{y}\rangle\}
.
By the assumption of probabilistic consistent reconstruction, we have

\nu(\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{f}})=0
. Since
\tilde{\gamma}\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
, then
\tilde{\gamma}(W\times\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{f}})=\nu(\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{f}})=0
. The two functions

(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\mapsto\mathbf{x}\langle\mathbf{y},\hat{\mathbf{f}}\rangle

and

(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\mapsto\mathbf{x}\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{f}\rangle

only differ on the
\tilde{\gamma}
-null set
W\times\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{f}}
, so

(4.1)

\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\langle\mathbf{y},\hat{\mathbf{f}}\rangle\,d\tilde{\gamma}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{f}\rangle\,d\tilde{\gamma}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

Now let

\mathbf{F}:=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}^{t}d\tilde{\gamma}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

Then, for any
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\hat{\mathbf{f}}=\mathbf{F}\mathbf{f}
, and

\mathbf{F}^{2}\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{F}\hat{\mathbf{f}}=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\langle\mathbf{y},\hat{\mathbf{f}}\rangle d\tilde{\gamma}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{f}\rangle d\tilde{\gamma}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\mathbf{F}\mathbf{f},

where the third equality is just (
4.1
). Therefore,
\mathbf{F}
is a projection.

Our goal now is to show
\mathbf{F}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}
, which is equivalent to
\range(\mathbf{F})=W
and
\kernel(\mathbf{F})=V^{\perp}
.
Since
\tilde{\gamma}\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
,
\mu
is supported on
W
, and
\nu
is supported on
V
, we know
V^{\perp}\subseteq\kernel(\mathbf{F})
and
\range(\mathbf{F})\subseteq W
.

We first claim that
\kernel(\mathbf{F})=V^{\perp}
. To see this, suppose
\mathbf{f}_{0}\in\kernel(\mathbf{F})
. If there were
\mathbf{z}_{0}\in\supp(\nu)
so that
\langle\mathbf{f}_{0},\mathbf{z}_{0}\rangle\neq 0
, continuity of the inner product would imply the existence of an open neighborhood
B_{\mathbf{z}_{0}}
of
\mathbf{z}_{0}
with
\nu(B_{\mathbf{z}_{0}})>0
(since
\mathbf{z}_{0}\in\supp(\nu)
) such that for any
\mathbf{z}\in B_{\mathbf{z}_{0}}
,
\langle\mathbf{f}_{0},\mathbf{z}\rangle\neq 0
. On the other hand, probabilistic consistent reconstruction implies that for
\nu
-almost all
\mathbf{z}\in V
,

\langle\mathbf{f}_{0},\mathbf{z}\rangle=\langle\hat{\mathbf{f}}_{0},\mathbf{z}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{F}\mathbf{f}_{0},\mathbf{z}\rangle=0.

From this contradiction we conclude that
\langle\mathbf{f}_{0},\mathbf{z}_{0}\rangle=0
for all
\mathbf{z}_{0}\in\supp(\nu)
. Since
\spanning(\supp(\nu))=V
, this implies
\mathbf{f}_{0}\in V^{\perp}
. Then
\kernel(\mathbf{F})=V^{\perp}
.

By the Rank–Nullity theorem,

\Dim(\mathrm{Ran}(\mathbf{F}))+\Dim(V^{\perp})=n.

Since
\mathbb{R}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
and
\range(\mathbf{F})\subset W
, this implies
\range(\mathbf{F})=W
. Therefore,

\mathbf{F}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}^{t}d\tilde{\gamma}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),

which implies that
\nu
is a probabilistic oblique dual frame of
\mu
on
V
.
∎

The following lemma shows that once we have obtained the reconstruction formula for given measures
\mu
and
\nu
, we can construct an oblique dual frame of
\mu
on any arbitrary subspace
K
for which
\mathbb{R}^{n}=W\oplus K^{\perp}
. In particular, if
\mu
is a frame for
W
and
\nu
is an oblique dual frame of
\mu
on
V
, then one can construct an oblique dual of
\mu
on any subspace
K
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}=W\oplus K^{\perp}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a subspace. Suppose
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W)
,
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that for any
\mathbf{f}\in W
,

\mathbf{f}=\int_{W\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathbf{x}\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{f}\rangle d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

Then for any subspace
K
for which
\mathbb{R}^{n}=W\oplus K^{\perp}
,
{{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{KW^{\perp}}}_{\#}\nu
is an oblique dual probabilistic frame of
\mu
on
K
.

Proof.

Note that for any
\mathbf{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WK^{\perp}}\mathbf{g}\in W
. Then by the assumption,

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WK^{\perp}}\mathbf{g}=\int_{W\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathbf{x}\langle\mathbf{y},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WK^{\perp}}\mathbf{g}\rangle d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\int_{W\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathbf{x}\langle{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{KW^{\perp}}\mathbf{y},\mathbf{g}\rangle d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

Now let
\tilde{\gamma}:=(\mathbf{Id},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{KW^{\perp}})_{\#}\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,{{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{KW^{\perp}}}_{\#}\nu)\subset\mathcal{P}(W\times K)
. Then for any
\mathbf{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WK^{\perp}}\mathbf{g}=\int_{W\times K}\mathbf{x}\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{g}\rangle d\tilde{\gamma}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

Then by
Lemma

4.1
,
{{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{KW^{\perp}}}_{\#}\nu
is an oblique dual probabilistic frame of
\mu
on
K
.
∎

Recall that
Theorem

2.7
gives a parametrization of all the oblique dual frames for a given frame in
W
. In the following lemma, we give an analogous parametrization of all oblique dual probabilistic frames of pushforward type. Additionally,
({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger})_{\#}\mu
is called the
canonical oblique dual probabilistic frame
of
\mu
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
and let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W)
be a probabilistic frame for
W
with frame operator
{\bf S}_{\mu}
.
Suppose
T_{\#}\mu
is an oblique dual frame of
\mu
on
V
where
T:W\rightarrow V
is measurable.
Then for any
\ {\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
T
precisely satisfies

T({\bf x})={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}({\bf x})+h({\bf x})-\int_{W}\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle h({\bf y})d\mu({\bf y}),

where
h:W\rightarrow V
is such that
h_{\#}\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(V)
.

Proof.

First, note that if
T:W\rightarrow V
is of the above type, then
T_{\#}\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(V)
. Now define
\gamma=({\bf Id},T)_{\#}\mu\in\Gamma(\mu,T_{\#}\mu)\subset\mathcal{P}(W\times V)
.
Then

\begin{split}\int_{W\times V}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x,y})=\int_{W}{\bf x}T({\bf x})^{t}d\mu({\bf x})&={\bf S}_{\mu}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}+\int_{W}{\bf x}h({\bf x})^{t}d\mu({\bf x})\\
&\quad-\int_{W}{\bf S}_{\mu}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf y}h({\bf y})^{t}d\mu({\bf y})={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}},\end{split}

where
{\bf S}_{\mu}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}
is the orthogonal projection onto
W
(see
Lemma

2.12
). Therefore,
T_{\#}\mu
is an oblique dual frame to
\mu
.

Conversely, if
T_{\#}\mu
is an oblique dual to
\mu
, then

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}^{*}=\int_{W}T({\bf y}){\bf y}^{t}d\mu({\bf y}).

Note that for any
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\begin{split}T({\bf x})&={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}({\bf x})+T({\bf x})-{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}({\bf x})\\
&={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}({\bf x})+T({\bf x})-\int_{W}\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle T({\bf y})d\mu({\bf y}).\end{split}

Since
T_{\#}\mu
is an oblique dual of
\mu
on
V
, then
T_{\#}\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(V)
. Setting
h=T
shows that
T:W\rightarrow V
is of the desired type.
∎

We present another corollary; the proof is similar to
Proposition

4.6
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
and let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W)
be a probabilistic frame for
W
with frame operator
{\bf S}_{\mu}
.
Suppose
T_{\#}\mu
is an oblique dual frame of
\mu
on
V
where
T:W\rightarrow V
is measurable.
Then for any
\ {\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
T
precisely satisfies

T({\bf x})={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}({\bf x})+h({\bf x}),

where
h:W\rightarrow V
is such that
h_{\#}\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(V)
with
\int_{W}{\bf x}h({\bf x})^{t}d\mu({\bf x})={\bf 0}_{n\times n}
.

The following example shows that given a probabilistic frame for
W
, its oblique dual probabilistic frames are not necessarily of pushforward type and can include both discrete and absolutely continuous measures.

Example 4.8
.

Let
W=\spanning\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}\right\}
and
V=\spanning\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix}\right\}
.
Then
V^{\perp}=\spanning\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1\\
-1\end{pmatrix}\right\}
and
\mathbb{R}^{2}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
.
The corresponding oblique projection
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}
which maps
\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}
to
\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}
and
\begin{pmatrix}1\\
-1\end{pmatrix}
to
\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\end{pmatrix}

is given by
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}

and thus
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}
.
Now suppose

\mu=\delta_{(1,0)}\ \text{and}\ \nu=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{(0,0)}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{(2,2)}.

Clearly,
\mu
is a probabilistic frame for
W
,
\nu
is a probabilistic frame for
V
, and there does not exist a map
T:W\rightarrow V
such that
\nu=T_{\#}\mu
.
However,
\nu
is an oblique dual probabilistic frame of
\mu
with respect to the product measure
\mu\otimes\nu
, because

\int_{W}\int_{V}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}^{t}d\mu(\mathbf{x})d\nu(\mathbf{y})=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}2&2\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}.

Furthermore, if
\mu
is the standard Gaussian measure on
W
, then
\mu
is a probabilistic frame for
W
and a sample from
\mu
is
{\bf x}=\begin{pmatrix}X\\
0\end{pmatrix}
where
X\sim N(0,1)
.
Then the frame operator and the associated Moore–Penrose inverse are
{\bf S}_{\mu}=\int_{W}{\bf x}{\bf x}^{t}\,d\mu({\bf x})=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}
and
{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}
.
Now consider the canonical oblique dual
\nu:=({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger})_{\#}\mu
.
For
{\bf x}=\begin{pmatrix}X\\
0\end{pmatrix}
,

({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger})({\bf x})={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\begin{pmatrix}X\\
0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}X\\
0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}X\\
X\end{pmatrix}\in V.

Therefore,
\nu
is a Gaussian measure supported on
V
with mean
\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\end{pmatrix}
and covariance matrix
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}
,
which is clearly absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure for
V
.

5.
Oblique Dual Probabilistic Frame Potential

Recall that given
\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{H}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
and a frame
\{{\bf w}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
in
W
,
[
23
, Proposition 4]
and
[
22
, Proposition 5.1]
showed that among all coefficient sequences
\{c_{i}\}_{i\in I}\in\ell^{2}(I)
for which

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{f}=\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}\mathbf{w}_{i},

the coefficient sequence with the minimal
\ell^{2}
energy is
\{\langle\mathbf{f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}_{i}\rangle\}_{i\in I}
, which is induced by the canonical oblique dual frame.

In the following, we show a similar result for probabilistic frames, with functions
\omega\in L^{2}(\mu,W)
substituting for the coefficient sequences.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W)
be a probabilistic frame for
W
with frame operator
{\bf S}_{\mu}
. For fixed
{\bf f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, suppose
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}{\bf f}=\int_{W}{\bf x}\omega({\bf x})d\mu({\bf x})
for some
\omega\in L^{2}(\mu,W)
. Then

\int_{W}|\omega({\bf x})|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})=\int_{W}|\langle{\bf f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf x}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})+\int_{W}|\omega({\bf x})-\langle{\bf f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf x}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x}).

Therefore,

\int_{W}|\omega({\bf x})|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})\geq\int_{W}|\langle{\bf f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf x}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x}),

and equality holds if and only if
\omega({\bf x})=\langle{\bf f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf x}\rangle
for
\mu
-almost all
{\bf x}\in W
.

Proof.

Since
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W)
, we can interpret
\mu
as a measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The associated analysis operator
U_{\mu}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow L^{2}(\mu,W)
given by
(U_{\mu}{\bf x})(\cdot)=\langle{\bf x},\cdot\rangle\in L^{2}(\mu,W)
is well-defined (bounded). Hence, the related (adjoint) synthesis operator
U_{\mu}^{*}:L^{2}(\mu,W)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
exists and is given by
U_{\mu}^{*}(\psi)=\int_{W}{\bf x}\psi({\bf x})d\mu({\bf x})
. Then we have

\kernel(U_{\mu}^{*})=(\range(U_{\mu}))^{\perp}
, where
\range(U_{\mu})
is the range of
U_{\mu}
and
\kernel(U_{\mu}^{*})
is the kernel of
U_{\mu}^{*}
given by

\kernel(U_{\mu}^{*}):=\left\{\psi\in L^{2}(\mu,W):U_{\mu}^{*}(\psi)=\int_{W}{\bf x}\psi({\bf x})d\mu({\bf x})={\bf 0}\right\}.

For the given
\omega\in L^{2}(\mu,W)
,

\omega(\cdot)=\omega(\cdot)-\langle{\bf f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}(\cdot)\rangle+\langle{\bf f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}(\cdot)\rangle.

Since

\begin{split}U_{\mu}^{*}(\omega(\cdot)-\langle{\bf f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}(\cdot)\rangle)&=\int_{W}{\bf x}\omega({\bf x})d\mu({\bf x})-\int_{W}{\bf x}\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}{\bf f},{\bf x}\rangle d\mu({\bf x})\\
&={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}{\bf f}-{\bf S}_{\mu}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}{\bf f}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}{\bf f}-{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}{\bf f}=\mathbf{0},\end{split}

where the equality follows from
Lemma

2.12
that
{\bf S}_{\mu}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}
is the orthogonal projection of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto
W
.
Therefore,
\omega(\cdot)-\langle{\bf f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}(\cdot)\rangle\in\kernel(U_{\mu}^{*})=(\range(U_{\mu}))^{\perp}
.

Since
\langle{\bf f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}(\cdot)\rangle=\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}{\bf f},\cdot\rangle\in\range(U_{\mu})
, it follows that
\omega(\cdot)-\langle{\bf f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}(\cdot)\rangle
is orthogonal to
\langle{\bf f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}(\cdot)\rangle
in
L^{2}(\mu,W)
. Then, by the Pythagorean theorem,

\|\omega\|_{L^{2}(\mu,W)}^{2}=\|\omega-\langle{\bf f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}(\cdot)\rangle\|_{L^{2}(\mu,W)}^{2}+\|\langle{\bf f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}(\cdot)\rangle\|_{L^{2}(\mu,W)}^{2}.

In other words,

\int_{W}|\omega({\bf x})|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})=\int_{W}|\langle{\bf f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf x}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})+\int_{W}|\omega({\bf x})-\langle{\bf f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf x}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x}).

Therefore,

\int_{W}|\omega({\bf x})|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})\geq\int_{W}|\langle{\bf f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf x}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x}),

and equality holds if and only if

\int_{W}|\omega({\bf x})-\langle{\bf f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf x}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})=0,

which is true if and only if

\omega({\bf x})=\langle{\bf f},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf x}\rangle
for
\mu
-almost all
{\bf x}\in W
.
∎

When
W=V
,
Proposition

5.1
tells us that for a probabilistic frame
\mu
on
W
and
{\bf f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, among all
\omega\in L^{2}(\mu,W)
for which
{\mathbf{P}}_{W}{\bf f}=\int_{W}{\bf x}\omega({\bf x})d\mu({\bf x})
,
the function
\langle{\bf f},{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}(\cdot)\rangle
induced by the canonical dual probabilistic frame has the minimum
L^{2}(\mu,W)
energy. This recovers the related result
[
9
, Proposition 3.4]
.

We now give the following definition for the oblique dual probabilistic frame potential.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W)
be a probabilistic frame for
W
and
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(V)
an oblique dual frame of
\mu
on
V
. Then the
oblique dual probabilistic frame potential
between
\mu
and
\nu
is defined as

\int_{W}\int_{V}|\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})d\nu({\bf y}).

Note that if
T_{\#}\mu
is an oblique dual of
\mu
on
V
where
T:W\rightarrow V
is measurable, then the oblique dual probabilistic frame potential between
\mu
and
T_{\#}\mu
is given by

\int_{W}\int_{W}|\langle{\bf x},T({\bf y})\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})d\mu({\bf y}).

Given a probabilistic frame
\mu
for
W
, we are going to prove that its oblique dual probabilistic frame potential is minimized among all oblique duals
T_{\#}\mu
of pushforward type if and only if
T({\bf y})={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf y}
for
\mu
-almost all
{\bf y}\in W
; that is, when
T_{\#}\mu
is the canonical oblique dual
({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger})_{\#}\mu
up to a
\mu
-null set. This generalizes similar results in
Proposition

3.3
about the oblique dual frame potential, in
[
10
, Theorem 2.2]
about the dual frame potential, and in
[
9
, Theorem 3.5]
about the probabilistic dual frame potential.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
W
and
V
be subspaces such that
\mathbb{R}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
where
W
has dimension
d_{W}
. Suppose
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W)
is a probabilistic frame for
W
and
T_{\#}\mu
is an oblique dual frame of
\mu
on
V
where
T:W\rightarrow V
is measurable. Then

\int_{W}\int_{W}|\langle{\bf x},T({\bf y})\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})d\mu({\bf y})\geq d_{W},

and equality holds if and only if
T({\bf y})={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf y}
for
\mu
-almost all
{\bf y}\in W
.

Proof.

Since
T_{\#}\mu
is an oblique dual frame to
\mu
, for any fixed
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}{\bf x}=\int_{W}{\bf y}\langle{\bf x},T({\bf y})\rangle d\mu({\bf y}).

Note that
\langle{\bf x},T(\cdot)\rangle\in L^{2}(\mu,W)
, since

\int_{W}|\langle{\bf x},T({\bf y})\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf y})\leq\|{\bf x}\|^{2}\int_{W}\|T({\bf y})\|^{2}d\mu({\bf y})<+\infty.

Then, by
Proposition

5.1
, we know that for any fixed
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(5.1)

\int_{W}|\langle{\bf x},T({\bf y})\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf y})\geq\int_{W}|\langle{\bf x},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf y}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf y}),

and equality holds if and only if
\langle{\bf x},T({\bf y})\rangle=\langle{\bf x},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf y}\rangle
for
\mu
-almost all
{\bf y}\in W
.
Therefore,

(5.2)

\displaystyle\int_{W}\int_{W}|\langle{\bf x},T({\bf y})\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf y})d\mu({\bf x})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{W}\int_{W}|\langle{\bf x},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf y}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf y})d\mu({\bf x})

\displaystyle=\int_{W}\int_{W}|\langle{\bf x},{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf y}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})d\mu({\bf y})

\displaystyle=\int_{W}\int_{W}|\langle({\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger})^{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf x},({\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger})^{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf y}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})d\mu({\bf y})

(5.3)

\displaystyle=\int_{W}\|({\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger})^{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf y}\|^{2}d\mu({\bf y})

(5.4)

\displaystyle=d_{W},

where (
5.3
) is due to the fact that
{({\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger})^{\frac{1}{2}}}_{\#}\mu
is a Parseval frame for
W=\range(({\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger})^{\frac{1}{2}})
.
Lemma

2.12
yields (
5.4
), since

\begin{split}\int_{W}\|({\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger})^{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf y}\|^{2}d\mu({\bf y})=\trace\left(({\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger})^{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf S}_{\mu}({\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)=\trace({\mathbf{P}}_{W})=d_{W}.\end{split}

If
T({\bf y})={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf y}
for
\mu
-almost all
{\bf y}\in W
, then the inequality in (
5.2
) is an equality. Conversely, if (
5.2
) is an equality, then for
\mu
-almost all
{\bf x}\in W
,

\int_{W}|\langle{\bf x},T({\bf y})\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf y})=\int_{W}|\langle{\bf x},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf y}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf y}).

Hence, for
\mu
-almost every
{\bf x}\in W
we have equality in (
5.1
), and hence

\langle{\bf x},{\mathbf{P}}_{W}(T({\bf y})-{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf y})\rangle=0,\ \text{for $\mu$-almost all ${\bf y}\in W$}.

In other words, we have shown that equality in the statement of the theorem implies that
\langle{\bf x},{\mathbf{P}}_{W}(T({\bf y})-{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf y})\rangle=0
for
\mu
-almost every
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
in
W
.
Then for
\mu
-almost all
{\bf y}\in W
,

A_{\mu}\|{\mathbf{P}}_{W}(T({\bf y})-{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf y})\|^{2}\leq\int_{W}|\langle{\bf x},{\mathbf{P}}_{W}(T({\bf y})-{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf y})\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})=0,

where
A_{\mu}>0
is the lower frame bound for
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W)
.
Therefore, for
\mu
-almost all
{\bf y}\in W
,
{\mathbf{P}}_{W}(T({\bf y})-{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf y})={\bf 0}
. Since
\mathbb{R}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
is equivalent to
\mathbb{R}^{n}=V\oplus W^{\perp}
(see
Theorem

2.1
), it follows that
T({\bf y})-{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf y}\in W^{\perp}\cap V=\{\bf 0\}
for
\mu
-almost all
{\bf y}\in W
. Thus, for
\mu
-almost all
{\bf y}\in W
,
T({\bf y})={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\bf y}
.
∎

The following lemma is a special case of a more general result about approximately dual probabilistic frames
[
7
, Lemma 4.2]
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame for
W
with upper frame bound
B>0
. If
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W)
is a dual probabilistic frame of
\mu
, then
\nu
is a probabilistic frame for
W
with frame bounds
\frac{1}{B}
and
M_{2}(\nu)
.

We are now ready to prove
Theorem

5.5
, which concerns the oblique dual probabilistic frame potential. For a given probabilistic frame for
W
with bounds
0<A\leq B
, we show that the oblique dual probabilistic frame potential is bounded below by
d_{W}\frac{A}{B}
. Since
A\leq B
, this lower bound satisfies

d_{W}\frac{A}{B}\leq d_{W},

where
d_{W}
is precisely the lower bound of the oblique dual probabilistic frame potential among all oblique duals of pushforward type in
Theorem

5.3
. This is because there exist oblique dual frames of non-pushforward type (see
Example

4.8
), and the minimization of the oblique dual probabilistic frame potential in
Theorem

5.5
is among a larger set compared to the case in
Theorem

5.3
. However, the effect of oblique dual frames of non-pushforward type becomes negligible when the given probabilistic frame is tight (
A=B
), since these two lower bounds are both
d_{W}
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
. Suppose
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W)
is a probabilistic frame for
W
with bounds
0<A\leq B
, and
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(V)
is an oblique dual of
\mu
on
V
. Then

\int_{W}\int_{V}|\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})d\nu({\bf y})\geq\frac{A}{B}d_{W},

and equality holds if and only if
\mu
is a tight probabilistic frame for
W
with bound
A>0
and
\nu=({{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}^{\dagger}_{\mu}})_{\#}\mu
.

Proof.

Note that

\begin{split}\int_{W}\int_{V}|\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})d\nu({\bf y})=\int_{W}\int_{V}\trace({\bf x}{\bf x}^{t}{\bf y}{\bf y}^{t})d\mu({\bf x})d\nu({\bf y})=\trace({\bf S}_{\mu}{\bf S}_{\nu}).\end{split}

Since
\mu
is a probabilistic frame for
W
with bounds
0<A\leq B
, then

A{\mathbf{P}}_{W}\preceq{\bf S}_{\mu}\preceq B{\mathbf{P}}_{W},

where
{\mathbf{P}}_{W}
is the orthogonal projection of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto
W
.
Since
{\bf S}_{\mu}-A{\mathbf{P}}_{W}
and
{\bf S}_{\nu}
are both positive semi-definite,

\displaystyle\trace({\bf S}_{\mu}{\bf S}_{\nu}-A{\mathbf{P}}_{W}{\bf S}_{\nu})

\displaystyle=\trace(({\bf S}_{\mu}-A{\mathbf{P}}_{W}){\bf S}_{\nu})

(5.5)

\displaystyle=\|{\bf S}_{\nu}^{1/2}({\bf S}_{\mu}-A{\mathbf{P}}_{W})^{1/2}\|^{2}_{\text{Frob}}\geq 0,

and therefore

(5.6)

\displaystyle\trace({\bf S}_{\mu}{\bf S}_{\nu})

\displaystyle\geq A\trace({\mathbf{P}}_{W}{\bf S}_{\nu})=A\trace({\mathbf{P}}_{W}{\bf S}_{\nu}{\mathbf{P}}_{W})

\displaystyle=A\int_{V}\|{\mathbf{P}}_{W}{\bf y}\|^{2}d\nu({\bf y}).

Since
\nu
is an oblique dual frame of
\mu
on
V
, by
Lemma

4.1
and
Lemma

5.4
,
{{\mathbf{P}}_{W}}_{\#}\nu
is a dual probabilistic frame of
\mu
with respect to some
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,{{\mathbf{P}}_{W}}_{\#}\nu)
on
W
, with the lower frame bound
\frac{1}{B}
. Now let
\{{\bf e}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d_{W}}
be an orthonormal basis for
W
. Then

(5.7)

\int_{V}\|{\mathbf{P}}_{W}{\bf y}\|^{2}d\nu({\bf y})=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{W}}\int_{W}|\langle{\bf e}_{i},{\bf y}\rangle|^{2}d{{\mathbf{P}}_{W}}_{\#}\nu({\bf y})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{d_{W}}\frac{1}{B}\|{\bf e}_{i}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{B}d_{W}.

Combining the above results, we have

\begin{split}\int_{W}\int_{V}|\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})d\nu({\bf y})\geq A\int_{V}\|{\mathbf{P}}_{W}{\bf y}\|^{2}d\nu({\bf y})\geq\frac{A}{B}d_{W}.\end{split}

For the equality, if
\mu
is a tight frame for
W
with frame bound
A
and
\nu={{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}^{\dagger}_{\mu}}_{\#}\mu
, then
{\bf S}_{\mu}=A{\mathbf{P}}_{W}
and
{\bf S}^{\dagger}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{A}{\mathbf{P}}_{W}
. Then by
Lemma

2.3
,

{{\mathbf{P}}_{W}}_{\#}\nu=({\mathbf{P}}_{W}{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}^{\dagger}_{\mu})_{\#}\mu=(\frac{1}{A}{\mathbf{P}}_{W})_{\#}\mu.

Thus,
{{\mathbf{P}}_{W}}_{\#}\nu
is a tight frame for
W
with bound
\frac{1}{A}
since

{\bf S}_{{{\mathbf{P}}_{W}}_{\#}\nu}=\frac{1}{A}{\mathbf{P}}_{W}{\bf S}_{\mu}\frac{1}{A}{\mathbf{P}}_{W}=\frac{1}{A}{\mathbf{P}}_{W}.

Hence, the inequalities in (
5.6
) and (
5.7
) become equalities, and thus

\int_{W}\int_{V}|\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})d\nu({\bf y})=A\trace({\mathbf{P}}_{W}{\bf S}_{\nu})=A\sum_{i=1}^{d_{W}}\frac{1}{A}\|{\bf e}_{i}\|^{2}=d_{W}.

Conversely, if the equality holds, we must have equalities in (
5.5
) and (
5.7
):

(5.8)

\|{\bf S}_{\nu}^{1/2}({\bf S}_{\mu}-A{\mathbf{P}}_{W})^{1/2}\|^{2}_{\text{Frob}}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{V}\|{\mathbf{P}}_{W}{\bf y}\|^{2}d\nu({\bf y})=\frac{1}{B}d_{W}.

The first implies that
{\bf S}_{\nu}^{1/2}({\bf S}_{\mu}-A{\mathbf{P}}_{W})^{1/2}=\mathbf{0}_{n\times n}
and hence

{\bf S}_{\nu}({\bf S}_{\mu}-A{\mathbf{P}}_{W})={\bf S}_{\nu}^{1/2}{\bf S}_{\nu}^{1/2}({\bf S}_{\mu}-A{\mathbf{P}}_{W})^{1/2}({\bf S}_{\mu}-A{\mathbf{P}}_{W})^{1/2}=\mathbf{0}_{n\times n}.

Therefore,
\range({\bf S}_{\mu}-A{\mathbf{P}}_{W})\subset\kernel({\bf S}_{\nu})
. Since
\nu
is a probabilistic frame for
V
,
Lemma

2.12
implies
\kernel({\bf S}_{\nu})=V^{\perp}
and thus
\range({\bf S}_{\mu}-A{\mathbf{P}}_{W})\subset V^{\perp}
. Since
\range({\bf S}_{\mu}-A{\mathbf{P}}_{W})\subset W
and
W\cap V^{\perp}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
, we conclude
\range({\bf S}_{\mu}-A{\mathbf{P}}_{W})=\{{\mathbf{0}}\}
. So
{\bf S}_{\mu}=A{\mathbf{P}}_{W}
, which shows that
\mu
is a tight frame for
W
with bound
A>0
.

Moreover, since
{{\mathbf{P}}_{W}}_{\#}\nu
is a dual probabilistic frame of
\mu
on
W
with respect to
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,{{\mathbf{P}}_{W}}_{\#}\nu)
, then

{\mathbf{P}}_{W}=\int_{W\times W}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x,y}).

Taking the trace of both sides and using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality twice, we have

\displaystyle d_{W}=\trace({\mathbf{P}}_{W})

\displaystyle=\int_{W\times W}\langle{\bf x,y}\rangle d\gamma({\bf x,y})

(5.9)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{W\times W}\|{\bf x}\|\|{\bf y}\|d\gamma({\bf x,y})

(5.10)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\int_{W}\|{\bf x}\|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})\int_{W}\|{\bf y}\|^{2}d{{\mathbf{P}}_{W}}_{\#}\nu({\bf y})}.

Since
\mu
is a tight frame for
W
with bound
A=B
, then

\int_{W}\|{\bf x}\|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{W}}\int_{\mathbb{W}}|\langle{\bf e}_{i},{\bf x}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{W}}A\|{\bf e}_{i}\|^{2}=A\ d_{W}.

Therefore,

\int_{V}\|{{\mathbf{P}}_{W}\bf y}\|^{2}d\nu({\bf y})\geq\frac{1}{A}d_{W}=\frac{1}{B}d_{W}.

Combining this with the second equation in (
5.8
), we know that the equalities in the above Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities (i.e., (
5.9
) and (
5.10
)) must hold.

Saturation of (
5.10
) implies that the functions
F({\bf x,y}):=\|{\bf x}\|
and
G({\bf x,y}):=\|{\bf y}\|

are linearly dependent in
L^{2}(\gamma,W\times W)
, i.e., there exists a constant
c\geq 0
such that for
\gamma
-almost all
({\bf x,y})\in W\times W
,
\|{\bf y}\|=c\|{\bf x}\|
.
Furthermore, saturation of (
5.9
) shows that for
\gamma
-almost all
({\bf x,y})\in W\times W
,
{\bf y}=c_{\bf x}{\bf x}
,
where
c_{\bf x}\geq 0
is a constant that may depend on
{\bf x}
. Therefore, for
\gamma
-almost all
({\bf x,y})\in W\times W
,

\|{\bf y}\|=c\|{\bf x}\|\ \text{and}\ {\bf y}=c_{\bf x}{\bf x}.

If
\bf x
is nonzero, then
c_{\bf x}=c
, and if
\bf x=0
, and setting
c_{\bf 0}:=c
does not affect the validity of the above two equations. Therefore, for
\gamma
-almost all
({\bf x,y})\in W\times W
,
{\bf y}=c{\bf x}=c{\mathbf{P}}_{W}{\bf x},

which implies
\gamma=(\mathbf{Id},c{\mathbf{P}}_{W})_{\#}\mu\in\Gamma(\mu,{{\mathbf{P}}_{W}}_{\#}\nu)
. Thus
{{\mathbf{P}}_{W}}_{\#}\nu=(c{\mathbf{P}}_{W})_{\#}\mu
. Since
{{\mathbf{P}}_{W}}_{\#}\nu
is a dual frame of
\mu
on
W
with respect to
\gamma=(\mathbf{Id},c{\mathbf{P}}_{W})_{\#}\mu
,

{\mathbf{P}}_{W}=\int_{W\times W}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}\,d\gamma({\bf x,y})=\int_{W}{\bf x}(c{\bf x})^{t}\,d\mu({\bf x})=c{\bf S}_{\mu}=cA{\mathbf{P}}_{W}.

Thus,
c=\frac{1}{A}>0
and
{{\mathbf{P}}_{W}}_{\#}\nu=\left(\tfrac{1}{A}{\mathbf{P}}_{W}\right)_{\#}\mu
.
Finally,
Lemma

2.3
and the fact that
\nu
is supported in
V
give

\nu={{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}}_{\#}\nu=({{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\mathbf{P}}_{W}})_{\#}\nu=({\frac{1}{A}{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\mathbf{P}}_{W}})_{\#}\mu=({{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}{\bf S}^{\dagger}_{\mu}})_{\#}\mu,

where the last equality is due to
{\bf S}^{\dagger}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{A}{\mathbf{P}}_{W}
.
∎

When
W=V
, the oblique dual probabilistic frame is just a dual probabilistic frame. Therefore,
Theorem

5.5
tells us that for a given probabilistic frame
\mu
on
W
, its dual probabilistic frame potential is minimized if and only if
\mu
is a tight frame and the dual frame is the canonical dual, which recovers the related result of
[
9
, Theorem 3.7]
.

6.
Oblique Approximately Dual Probabilistic Frame

We introduce the notion of
oblique approximately dual probabilistic frame
in this section. Previous work on approximate dual frames and approximately dual probabilistic frames can be found in
[
12
]
and
[
7
]
, respectively. Throughout this section,
\mathbb{R}^{n}=W\oplus V^{\perp}
where
W
and
V
are subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(W)
and
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(V)
be probabilistic frames for
W
and
V
, respectively. If
\epsilon\geq 0
, then
\nu
is called an
oblique
\epsilon
-approximately dual probabilistic frame
of
\mu
on
V
if there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that

\left\|\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}^{t}d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})-{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\right\|\leq\epsilon,

where the above matrix norm is the operator norm induced by the Euclidean norm.

Clearly, when
\epsilon=0
, the oblique
\epsilon
-approximately dual probabilistic frame is an oblique dual frame.
We can also generalize the probabilistic consistent reconstruction for oblique approximately dual frames.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
\mu
and
\nu
be probabilistic frames for
W
and
V
, respectively. Given
\epsilon\geq 0
,
\mu
and
\nu
are said to perform
probabilistic
\epsilon
-consistent reconstruction
if there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that for any
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\|\langle\mathbf{f}-\hat{\mathbf{f}},\cdot\rangle\|_{L^{2}(\nu,V)}=\left(\int_{V}|\langle\mathbf{f}-\hat{\mathbf{f}},\mathbf{z}\rangle|^{2}d\nu(\mathbf{z})\right)^{1/2}\leq\epsilon\|\mathbf{f}\|,

where
\hat{\mathbf{f}}=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\langle\mathbf{y,f}\rangle d\gamma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in W
is the reconstructed signal.

Note that when
\epsilon=0
,
\|\langle\mathbf{f}-\hat{\mathbf{f}},\cdot\rangle\|_{L^{2}(\nu,V)}=0
, and thus
\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{z}\rangle=\langle\hat{\mathbf{f}},\mathbf{z}\rangle
for
\nu
-almost all
\mathbf{z}\in V
, which implies that
\mu
and
\nu
perform probabilistic consistent reconstruction. We also establish the following lemma on the relation between the oblique
\epsilon
-approximately dual frame and probabilistic
\epsilon
-consistent reconstruction.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\mu
and
\nu
be probabilistic frames for
W
and
V
, respectively.

(1)

If
\nu
is an oblique
\epsilon
-approximate dual of
\mu
on
V
, then
\mu
and
\nu
do probabilistic
\sqrt{B}\epsilon
-consistent reconstruction where
B>0
is the upper bound for
\nu
.

(2)

Conversely, if
\mu
and
\nu
do probabilistic
\alpha
-consistent reconstruction, then
\nu
is an oblique
\epsilon
-approximately dual of
\mu
on
V
, where

Proof.

If

For any

\begin{split}\int_{V}|\langle\mathbf{f}-\hat{\mathbf{f}},\mathbf{z}\rangle|^{2}d\nu(\mathbf{z})=\int_{V}|\langle\mathbf{f}-\hat{\mathbf{f}},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{VW^{\perp}}\mathbf{z}\rangle|^{2}d\nu(\mathbf{z})&=\int_{V}|\langle{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{f}-\hat{\mathbf{f}},\mathbf{z}\rangle|^{2}d\nu(\mathbf{z})\\

\mu

where
\hat{\mathbf{f}}=\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\langle\mathbf{y,f}\rangle d\gamma^{\prime}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in W

({{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}{\bf S}_{\nu}^{\dagger}})_{\#}\nu

Thus, for any

\begin{split}\left\|\int_{W\times V}\mathbf{x}\langle\mathbf{y,f}\rangle d\gamma^{\prime}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})-{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}\mathbf{f}\right\|^{2}&=\|{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{WV^{\perp}}(\hat{\mathbf{f}}-\mathbf{f})\|^{2}=\left\|\int_{V\times W}\mathbf{u}\langle\mathbf{z},\hat{\mathbf{f}}-\mathbf{f}\rangle d\tilde{\gamma}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{u})\right\|^{2}\\

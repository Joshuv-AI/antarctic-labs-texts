Intraluminal Enteric Duplication Cyst Presenting as Intussusception: A Rarity
Authors: Keerthana Bachala, Bijay Kumar Suman, Shreyas Dudhani, Ram Jeewan Singh, Amit Kumar Sinha
Published: 2023

Enteric duplication cysts are rare anomalies with an incidence of 1 in 4500.[1] A 2-year-old female child presented with complaints of abdominal pain, bilious vomiting, and distension for 3 days. The child was evaluated and diagnosed with ileocolic intussusception. On exploration, around 25 cm of ileum was intussuscepted into the distal colon, and after reduction, a bluish bulge was visualized on the anti-mesenteric border; 2 feet from the ileocecal junction [Figures 1-3]. Resection and anastomosis were done. Grossly, it was an intraluminal mass obstructing 70% of lumen [Figure 4]. The histopathology report revealed an intramural duplication cyst. Duplication cysts are classified into saccular, tubular, and small intramural.[2] This case highlights the ileal duplication cyst as an uncommon cause of ileocolic intussusception.

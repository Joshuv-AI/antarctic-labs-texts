Quenched invariance principle for random walks in random environments admitting a cycle decomposition Thanks: Supported by DFG-funded Berlin-Oxford IRTG 2544, and Berlin Mathematical School.
Jean-Dominique Deuschel, Martin Slowik, Weile Weng

Abstract
. We study a class of non-reversible, continuous-time random walks in random environments on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} that admit a cycle representation with finite cycle length. The law of the transition rates, taking values in [ 0 , ∞ ) [0,\infty) , is assumed to be stationary and ergodic with respect to space shifts. Moreover, the transition rate from x x to y y , denoted by c ω ​ ( x , y ) c^{\omega}(x,y) , is a superposition of non-negative random weights on oriented cycles that contain the edge ( x , y ) (x,y) . We prove a quenched invariance principle under moment conditions that are comparable to the well-known p-q moment condition of Andres, Deuschel, and Slowik [ 2 ] for the random conductance model. A key ingredient in proving the sublinearity is an energy estimate for the non-symmetric generator. Our result extends that of Deuschel and Kösters [ 12 ] beyond strong ellipticity and bounded cycle lengths.

1.
Introduction

We consider time continuous random walks on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
in random environments with a finite but unbounded cycle decomposition.
Random walks with finite cycle decomposition, also called centred walks
cf.
[
23
]
, are the discrete versions of diffusions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in divergence form generated by

Lf(x)=\text{div}(c(x)\nabla f(x)).

where
c(x)=s(x)+a(x)
,
s(x)=s^{*}(x)
is the symmetric,
a^{*}(x)=-a(x)

the anti-symmetric part of the matrix
c(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}.

Assuming uniform ellipticity and boundedness:

\lambda<s(x)<\Lambda,\qquad|a(x)|<B,\qquad\lambda,\Lambda,B\in(0,\infty)

the celebrated results of Moser-De Giorgi-Nash-Aronson show
parabolic and elliptic Harnack inequalities and Gaussian type estimates
for the corresponding heat kernel, cf.
[
5
,
25
,
24
,
11
]
.
Moreover, Papanicolaou and Varadhan
[
26
]
proved a quenched invariant principle (QIP), i.e. the almost sure convergence for the diffusively rescaled
diffusion in ergodic environment. See also Fannjiang and Komorowski

[
15
,
16
]
for the unbounded case.

Random walks with finite cycle decomposition generated by

Lf(x)=\sum_{y}c(x,y)(f(y)-f(x)),

have jump rates
c(x,y)\geq 0
given in terms of oriented cycles

\gamma=(x_{0},x_{1},...,x_{n}=x_{0})
of length
\ell(\gamma)=n
and weights

w(\gamma)\geq 0
,

c(x,y)=\sum_{\gamma}w(\gamma)1_{(x,y)\in\gamma}.

The corresponding (non-symmetric) Dirichlet form is determined by

{\mathcal{E}}(f,g)=\sum_{\gamma}w(\gamma){\mathcal{E}}_{\gamma}(f,g),\qquad\text{where}\quad{\mathcal{E}}_{\gamma}(f,g)=\sum_{x_{i}\in\gamma}f(x_{i})(g(x_{i})-g(x_{i+1})).

In particular the counting measure is invariant, and the adjoint generator

{\mathcal{E}}^{*}(f,g)={\mathcal{E}}(g,f)

is given in terms of the reversed cycles

\gamma^{*}=(x_{n},x_{n-1},...,x_{0}=x_{n})
with weight

w^{*}(\gamma^{*})=w(\gamma)
.
In the special case that all the cycles have length
\ell(\gamma)=2
,
then
\gamma=\gamma^{*}
, thus the random walk is reversible with
symmetric jump rates, also called conductances.

Deuschel and Kumagai
[
13
]
showed that diffusions in divergence
form can be approximate by random walks on
\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with bounded cycle decomposition and bounded weights.
In an earlier work by Deuchel and Kösters
[
12
]
, the quenched invariant principle (QIP) is derived for
uniformly elliptic random walks in ergodic random environments with
bounded cycle decomposition and bounded weights.
In the symmetric situation, i.e. dealing with cycles of length 2, the QIP
has been proved for non-elliptic random conductance models under suitable
moment conditions, cf.
[
2
,
6
]
.

The objective of the current paper is to obtain the QIP in the setting
of unbounded cycle length, and non-uniform elliptic, i.e. unbounded and
degenerate weights under appropriate moment conditions.
In particular our result extends the QIP for the reversible random
conductance model in degenerated ergodic environment to the
non-symmetric case under similar moment conditions.
When the cycle lengths are unbounded, the random walk
does not satisfy the standard sector condition

({\mathcal{E}}(f,g))^{2}\leq\text{const}\,{\mathcal{E}}(f,f)\cdot{\mathcal{E}}(g,g),

which is usually assumed for the derivation of the invariance principle
in non-symmetric situations, cf. the works of Varadhan and collaborators
[
31
,
30
]
.

Our proof of QIP follows the general strategy of Andres, Deuschel and Slowik
[
2
]
, which deals with the symmetric case in the setting of a non-uniform elliptic conductance model. Specifically, we first introduce harmonic coordinates and then construct the corrector. Next, using PDE techniques adapted to the discrete setting we show the almost sure sublinearity of the corrector. The main challenge here is to deal and control the non-symmetric part of the generator. In particular in view the unboundedness of the cycle length
and loss of the sector condition, the construction of the corrector by means of the Lax-Milgram theorem necessitates a moment condition on the weighted length of cycles, which implies a bound of of the
{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}
norm of the corresponding
drift – a natural condition in the setting of double stochastic generators, cf.
[
20
]
. The next step is to control the corrector. Instead of the Moser iteration used in
[
2
]
, we adapt the
analytical technique of the De Giorgi iteration, which is robust for parabolic extension. While the Sobolev and weighted Poincare inequalities relying on the symmetric part are identical, the corresponding energy estimate is in view of the
non-symmetry more challenging. Here again the moment condition on the
weighted length is implemented.

be a finite subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Consider a function
\eta:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
with

\displaystyle\supp\eta\subset B,\quad 0\leq\eta\leq 1,\quad\eta\equiv 0\text{ on }\partial B.

Suppose
u:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is either non-negative
L^{\omega}
-subharmonic
(-L^{\omega}u\leq 0)
, or
L^{\omega}
-harmonic
(L^{\omega}u=0)
on
B
. Then we have for
p\in[1,\infty]
, there exists a constant
C_{\mathrm{En}}
such that for all
t\in I
,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma}^{\omega}(\eta u)}{\absolutevalue{B}}\leq C_{\mathrm{En}}\norm{\mu^{(2), \omega}}_{p,B}\,\norm{\nabla\eta}^{2}_{\ell_{\infty}(E)}\norm{u^2}_{p_{*},B},

with
p_{*}=p/(p-1)
being the Hölder conjugate of
p
.

Remark 4.4
.

It is easy to check that if
u
is assumed in Proposition
4.3
, then for any constant
l\geq 0
,
(\absolutevalue{u}-l)_{+}
is
L^{\omega}
-subharmonic on
B
.

Proof of Proposition
4.3
.

First, to each
\vec{\gamma}\in\Gamma
, we associate an unweighted operator
I_{\vec{\gamma}}
defined by
I_{\vec{\gamma}}g(x):=\sum_{(x,y)\in\vec{\gamma}}\nabla g(x,y)
, for all
g:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, and with
\nabla g(x,y):=g(y)-g(x)
.
Let
u,\eta
be as assumed, and denote by
\dot{\gamma}
the set of vertices in the cycle
\vec{\gamma}
. Then,

(4.10)

\displaystyle 0\leq\langle\eta^{2}u,L^{\omega}u\rangle=\sum_{\vec{\gamma}\in\Gamma}\omega(\vec{\gamma})\langle\eta^{2}u,I_{\vec{\gamma}}u\rangle_{\dot{\gamma}}.

Our next step is to bound
\langle\eta^{2}u,I_{\vec{\gamma}}u\rangle_{\dot{\gamma}}
from above by the form of

\displaystyle\text{const}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{E}_{\vec{\gamma}}(\eta u)-\text{const}_{2}\cdot F(\norm{\nabla\eta}^{2}_{\ell_{\infty}(\vec{E}_{d})}\cdot u^{2}\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$|$}_{\dot{\gamma}\cap B}).

for some deterministic function
F
.

Let
\dot{\gamma}_{B}=\dot{\gamma}\cap B
, and
\vec{\gamma}_{B}
denote the subset of oriented edges of
\vec{\gamma}
with both endpoints in
B
.

Observe that

\displaystyle\langle\,\eta^{2}u,I_{\dot{\gamma}}u\,\rangle_{\vec{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\;=\sum_{(x,y)\in\vec{\gamma}}(\eta^{2}u)(x)\nabla u(x,y)

\displaystyle\;=\sum_{(x,y)\in\vec{\gamma}}\big(-(\eta^{2}u^{2})(x)+(\eta u)(x)(\eta u)(y)-(\eta u)(x)(\eta u)(y)+(\eta^{2}u)(x)u(y)\big)

\displaystyle\;=\underbrace{\langle\eta u,I_{\vec{\gamma}}(\eta u)\rangle_{\dot{\gamma}_{B}}}_{:=A_{1}}+\sum_{(x,y)\in\vec{\gamma}_{B}}(\eta u)(x)u(y)\nabla\eta(y,x)

\displaystyle\;=A_{1}+\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\dot{\gamma}_B}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x,y)\in\vec{\gamma}_{B}\\
z\in\dot{\gamma}_{B}\end{subarray}}\big(\underbrace{\nabla(\eta u)(z,x)u(y)}_{:=A_{21}(x,y,z)}+(\eta u)(z)u(y)\big)\nabla\eta(y,x)

\displaystyle\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(*)}}{{=}}A_{1}+\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\dot{\gamma}_B}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x,y)\in\vec{\gamma}_{B}\\
z\in\dot{\gamma}_{B}\end{subarray}}\big(A_{21}(x,y,z)+\big(\eta(z)u(y)-(\eta u)(z)\big)u(z)\big)\nabla\eta(y,x)

(4.11)

\displaystyle\;=A_{1}+\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\dot{\gamma}_B}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x,y)\in\vec{\gamma}_{B}\\
z\in\dot{\gamma}_{B}\end{subarray}}\big(A_{21}(x,y,z)+\underbrace{\nabla(\eta u)(z,y)u(z)}_{:=A_{221}(y,z)}+\underbrace{\nabla\eta(y,z)u(y)u(z)}_{:=A_{222}(y,z)}\big)\nabla\eta(y,x),

where
(*)
is due to
\sum_{(x,y)\in\vec{\gamma}_{B}}\nabla\eta(y,x)=0
.
Notice that

A_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla(\eta u),\nabla(\eta u)\rangle_{\vec{\gamma}}=-\mathcal{E}_{\vec{\gamma}}(\eta u).

Moreover by Young’s inequality,

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{A_{21}(x,y,z)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\vec{\gamma}_{B}}\absolutevalue{ \nabla(\eta u)(x', y')}\absolutevalue{u(y)}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\mathcal{E}_{\vec{\gamma}}(\eta u)+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}\absolutevalue{\vec{\gamma}_B}u^{2}(y),

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{A_{221}(y,z)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\vec{\gamma}_{B}}\absolutevalue{\nabla(\eta u)(x', y')}\absolutevalue{u(z)}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\mathcal{E}_{\vec{\gamma}}(\eta u)+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}\absolutevalue{\vec{\gamma}_B}u^{2}(z).

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{A_{222}(y,z)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\absolutevalue{\vec{\gamma}_B}\,\norm{\nabla\eta}_{\ell_{\infty}(\vec{E}_{d})}\absolutevalue{u(y)}\absolutevalue{u(z)}.

Substituting above estimates into (
4.11
) with the choice
\varepsilon=(2\absolutevalue{\vec{\gamma}_B}\norm{\nabla\eta}_{\ell_{\infty}(\vec{E}_{d})})^{-1}
, we have

(4.12)

\displaystyle\langle\eta^{2}u,I_{\vec{\gamma}}u\rangle_{\vec{\gamma}}\leq-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}_{\vec{\gamma}}(\eta u)+(\absolutevalue{\vec{\gamma}_B}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\absolutevalue{\vec{\gamma}_B})\norm{\nabla\eta}_{\ell_{\infty(\vec{E}_{d})}}^{2}\sum_{x\in\dot{\gamma}_{B}}u^{2}(x).

Combining with (
4.10
), and by Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\omega}_{\Gamma}(\eta u)

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\nabla\eta}_{\ell_{\infty(\vec{E}_{d})}}^{2}\sum_{x\in B}u^{2}(x)\sum_{\vec{\gamma}\in\Gamma}\big(2\absolutevalue{\vec{\gamma}}^{2}+\absolutevalue{\vec{\gamma}}\big)\omega(\vec{\gamma})\mathbbm{1}_{x\in\dot{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\nabla\eta}_{\ell_{\infty(\vec{E}_{d})}}^{2}\absolutevalue{B}\sum_{x\in B}u^{2}(x)\big(2\mu^{(2),\omega}(x)+\mu^{(1),\omega}(x)\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{5}{2}\norm{\nabla\eta}_{\ell_{\infty(\vec{E}_{d})}}^{2}\absolutevalue{B}\norm{\mu^{(2), \omega}}_{p,B}\norm{u^2}_{p_{*},B}.

By moving
\absolutevalue{B}
to the LHS, we conclude (
4.9
).
∎

Lemma 4.5

(De Giorgi’s iteration method)
.

Let
f:[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
be a non-negative function. Suppose it holds, that for all
l>k\geq 0
and
\sigma>\sigma^{\prime}\geq 0

\displaystyle f(l,\sigma^{\prime})\leq\frac{C_{\mathrm{DG}}}{(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})^{\alpha}(l-k)^{\beta}}f(k,\sigma)^{\gamma},

for some constants
C_{\mathrm{DG}},\alpha,\beta>0
and
\gamma>1
. Then, there exists a constant
K\equiv K(C,f,\sigma^{\prime},\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
such that

\displaystyle f(l,\sigma^{\prime})=0,\quad\forall l\geq K.

In particular, we may choose

(4.13)

\displaystyle K=f(0,\sigma)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\beta}}C_{\mathrm{DG}}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}2^{\frac{(\alpha+\beta)}{\gamma-1}+1}(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}},

Proof.

cf. the iteration introduced for the proof of
[
18
, Theorem 4.1]
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.17
.

Our strategy is to show firstly

(4.14)

\displaystyle\norm{(\abs{u}-l)_+^2}_{p_{*},B(x,\sigma^{\prime}n)}\leq C_{1}\frac{\norm{\mu^{(2), \omega}}_{p,B(x,\sigma n)}\norm{\nu^\omega}_{q,B(x,\sigma n)}}{(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})^{2}(l-k)^{2\gamma-2}}\norm{(\abs{u}-k)_+^2}^{\gamma}_{p_{*},B(x,\sigma n)},

for some constant
C_{1}\equiv C_{1}(c_{\mathrm{reg}},C_{\mathrm{reg}},d,p,C_{\mathrm{WS}},C_{\mathrm{En}})
. Then we apply Proposition
4.5
, and use the choice (
4.13
) to conclude (
3.22
).

We start by defining a test function
\eta
satisfying the following assumptions:

\displaystyle\supp\eta\subset B(x,\sigma n),\quad\eta\equiv 1\text{ on }B(x,\sigma^{\prime}n),\quad\eta\equiv 0\text{ on }\partial B(x,\sigma n).

By interpolating
\eta
linearly on
B(x,\sigma n)\setminus B(\sigma^{\prime}n)
, it follows

(4.15)

\displaystyle\norm{\nabla\eta}_{\ell^{\infty}(E)}\leq\frac{1}{(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})n}.

Furthermore, we set
\delta=\rho/p_{*}
. By
1/p+1/q<2/d
and (
4.4
),
\delta>1
.

Following from Proposition
4.1
-(i),
\frac{1}{2}\leq\sigma^{\prime}\leq\sigma\leq 1
, and Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\norm{(\abs{u}-l)_+^2}_{p_{*},B(x,\sigma^{\prime}n)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\norm{(\eta(\abs{u}-l)_+)^2}_{p_{*},B(x,\sigma n)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\norm{(\eta(\abs{u}-l)_+)^2)}_{\rho,B(x,\sigma n)}\norm{\mathbbm{1}_{\{\abs{u}>l\}}}^{\frac{1}{\delta*}}_{p_{*},B(x,\sigma n)}

holds for large
n
, with
C_{2}\equiv C_{2}(c_{\mathrm{reg}},C_{\mathrm{reg}},p_{*},d)

and
\delta_{*}
being the Hölder conjugate of
\delta
.
Moreover, combining the weighted Sobolev inequality(Proposition
4.2
-(i)) and the energy estimate (
4.9
) (cf. Remark
4.4
), we derive

\displaystyle\norm{(\eta(\abs{u}-l)_+)^2}_{\rho,B(x,\sigma n)}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2C_{\mathrm{WS}}\,n^{2}\norm{\nu^\omega}_{q,B(x,\sigma n)}\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\omega}(\eta(\absolutevalue{u}-l)_{+})}{\absolutevalue{B(x, \sigma n)}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2C_{\mathrm{WS}}C_{\mathrm{En}}n^{2}\norm{\nu^\omega}_{q,B(x,\sigma n)}\norm{\mu^{(2), \omega}}_{p,B(x,\sigma n)}\norm{\nabla\eta}^{2}_{\ell^{\infty}(E)}\norm{(\abs{u}-l)_+^2}_{p_{*},B(x,\sigma n)}

\displaystyle\qquad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ineq:test-function-bounds}}}{{\leq}}C_{3}\frac{\norm{\mu^{(2), \omega}}_{p,B(x,\sigma n)}\norm{\nu^\omega}_{q,B(x,\sigma n)}}{(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})^{2}}\norm{(\abs{u}-l)_+^2}_{p_{*},B(x,\sigma n)},

where
C_{3}=2C_{\mathrm{WS}}C_{\mathrm{En}}.

In addition, Markov’s inequality implies, for
0\leq k\leq l
,

\displaystyle\norm{\mathbbm{1}_{\{\abs{u}>l\}}}_{p_{*},B(x,\sigma n)}=\norm{\mathbbm{1}_{\{(\abs{u}-k)_+>l-k\}}}_{p_{*},B(x,\sigma n)}\leq\frac{\norm{(\abs{u}-k)^2_+}_{p_{*},B(x,\sigma n)}}{(l-k)^{2}}.

Together with previous estimates, we derive (
4.14
) with
\gamma=1+\frac{1}{\delta_{*}},
and
C_{1}=C_{2}C_{3}.

Next, we observe that the criteria of Proposition
4.5
is satisfied, with

\displaystyle f(m,\sigma)=\norm{(\abs{u}-m)^2_+}_{p_{*},B(x,\sigma n)},\quad\forall m,\sigma\geq 0;

\displaystyle\alpha=2,\quad\beta=\frac{2}{\delta_{*}},\quad\gamma=1+\frac{1}{\delta_{*}};

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{DG}}=C_{1}\norm{\mu^{(2), \omega}}_{p,B(x,\sigma n)}\norm{\nu^\omega}_{q,B(x,\sigma n)}.

By evaluating (
4.13
), the maximal inequality (
3.22
) follows by choosing

\displaystyle\kappa=\frac{\delta^{*}}{2},\qquad C_{\mathrm{Max}}=2^{4\kappa+3}C_{1}^{\kappa}.

∎

Deriving Perelman’s entropy from Colding’s


Abstract
In his groundbreaking work from 2002, Perelman introduced two fundamental monotonic quantities: the reduced volume and the entropy. While the reduced volume was motivated by the Bishop-Gromov volume comparison applied to a suitably constructed N N -space, which becomes Ricci-flat as N → ∞ N\rightarrow\infty , Perelman did not provide a corresponding explanation for the origin of the entropy. In this article, we demonstrate that Perelman’s entropy emerges as the limit of Colding’s monotonic volume for harmonic functions on Ricci-flat manifolds, when appropriately applied to Perelman’s N N -space.

Introduction

Monotonic quantities play a fundamental role in elliptic and parabolic PDEs, particularly in the study of singularities, regularity of solutions, and asymptotics
[
6
]
. For instance, Huisken’s monotonicity formula for the mean curvature flow
[
13
]
and Struwe’s monotonicity formula for harmonic map heat flows
[
18
]
(see also Hamilton’s generalization of both formulas to general manifolds
[
12
]
), are well-known examples of monotonic quantities for parabolic equations, which have been widely applied in geometric analysis. For the ‘elliptic counterparts’ of these parabolic cases, the minimal surface equation and the harmonic map equation, there are analogous, though distinct, quantities, such as Allard’s monotonicity for minimal surfaces
[
2
]
and the well-known monotonicity for harmonic maps
[
17
]
, respectively. Additional examples include Hamilton’s monotonic formula for the Yang-Mills heat flow
[
12
]
and Price’s monotonic formula for the elliptic Yang-Mills equation
[
16
]
, as well as Almgren’s frequency for harmonic functions
[
3
]
and Poon’s parabolic frequency for the heat equation
[
15
]
(see also the recent generalization of the parabolic frequency to manifolds by Colding and Minicozzi
[
9
]
).

Parabolic monotonic formulas are often intriguing and challenging to derive and typically rely on a peculiar use of backward solutions to heat-type equations, whereas elliptic quantities do not. Despite appearing unrelated, Davey
[
10
]
(see also
[
11
]
) demonstrated that, at least in a handful of examples that include some of the ones mentioned earlier, the parabolic monotonicity can be derived from a subtle use of elliptic ones. This is achieved by applying elliptic monotonicity to associated equations on suitably constructed
N
-dimensional spaces, and taking the limit as
N\rightarrow\infty
. These equations, which are equivalent to the parabolic equation (they hold if and only if the parabolic does) are formally identical to the elliptic one up to terms that vanish as
N\rightarrow\infty
. Put differently, the parabolic equation can be viewed as the elliptic one in an
N
-space plus negligible additional terms, allowing the monotonic parabolic formula to be derived as a limit of an elliptic one (see the related discussions by Tao
[
19
]
and Šverák
[
20
]
).

For the Ricci-flat equation,

\Ric=0,

(1.1)

the best-known monotonic quantity is the Bishop-Gromov relative volume, widely applied across a variety of contexts in differential geometry. Another example, which will be central to this article, was introduced by Colding in
[
5
]
. This new quantity, which we will refer to as ‘monotonic volume’, is defined along the level sets of positive Green functions and was used, for instance, to study asymptotic cones on Ricci-flat non-parabolic manifolds
[
5
,
7
]
. Generalizations of Colding’s monotonic volume were later given by Colding and Minicozzi in
[
8
]
and applications to General Relativity were explored by Agostiniani, Mazzieri and Oronzio in
[
1
]
.

For the Ricci flow equation,

\partial_{t}g=-2\Ric,

(1.2)

the ‘parabolic counterpart’ of the Ricci flat equation, Perelman
[
14
]
introduced two fundamental monotonic quantities: the reduced volume and the entropy. Additionally, he demonstrated that the reduced volume can be derived from the Bishop-Gromov relative volume on a carefully constructed
N
-space that becomes Ricci-flat as the dimension
N
goes to infinity. However, no analogous justification on the origin of the entropy was provided.

In this article, we demonstrate that Perelman’s entropy arises as the limit as
N\rightarrow\infty
of Colding’s monotonic volume when appropriately applied to Perelman’s
N
-space. This proves that, as in the previously discussed parabolic examples, both the reduced volume and the entropy can be understood as originating from a unified framework using monotonic formulas from the ‘counterpart’ Ricci-flat equation. Additionally, we derive the expression for the derivative of Perelman’s entropy from Colding’s formula for the derivative of the monotonic volume.

Overview

To explain the results we begin recalling the definition of Perelman’s entropy, of Perelman’s
N
-space, and of Colding’s monotonic volume.

Let us start recalling Perelman’s entropy. On a closed manifold
M^{n}
of dimension
n
, let
g(\tau)
,
\tau\in[0,T]
, be a solution to the backward Ricci flow equation,

\partial_{\tau}g=2\Ric.

Then, let
u
be a solution to,

\partial_{\tau}u=\Delta u-Ru,

(1.3)

positive at the initial time
\tau=0
, and hence, also positive for all times by the maximum principle. Define
f
by
u=\tau^{-n/2}e^{-f}
so that
f
satisfies,

\partial_{\tau}f=\Delta f-|\nabla f|^{2}+R-\frac{n}{2\tau}.

(1.4)

Then, the Perelman entropy
\mathcal{W}
(for the function
f
) is given by,

\mathcal{W}(\tau)=\int_{M}\left(\tau(|\nabla f|^{2}+R)+f-n\right)(4\pi\tau)^{-n/2}e^{-f}d\nu,

(1.5)

and its derivative takes the form,

\frac{d}{d\tau}\mathcal{W}=-\int_{M}2\tau\left|\Ric+\nabla\nabla f-\frac{1}{2\tau}g\right\rvert^{2}(4\pi\tau)^{-n/2}e^{-f}d\nu,

(1.6)

from which it is deduced that it is monotonically decreasing in
\tau
.

Next, we define Perelman’s
N
-space, which he used to motivate the definition of the reduced volume. As earlier, let
g(\tau)
be a solution to the backward Ricci flow equation on
[0,T]
. Denote by
\mathbb{S}^{N}
the
N
-dimensional unit-sphere of
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
. Let
r
be the distance to the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
and let
\theta
denote points in
\mathbb{S}^{N}
.
Then, the Perelman
N
-space
(\hat{M},\hat{g})
is the manifold,

\hat{M}^{m}:=(0,\sqrt{2NT})_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{N}_{\theta}\times M^{n}_{x}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\times M^{n},\quad{\rm where}\quad m=N+n+1,

endowed with the metric,

\hat{g}:=r^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{N}}+(1+\frac{Rr^{2}}{N^{2}})dr^{2}+g,

(1.7)

where
R
is the scalar curvature of
g
and where at a point
(r,\theta,x)\in\hat{M}
,
g_{\mathbb{S}^{N}}
is evaluated at
\theta
and
R
and
g
at
(\tau=r^{2}/2N,x)\in(0,T)\times M
.

Now, we briefly discuss Colding’s monotonic volume. On a manifold
(N,\bar{g})
admitting a positive and proper Green function
G
, define
b=G^{1/(2-m)}
. Then, Colding defined two functions, the ‘area’
A
and the ‘volume’
V
, on the level sets of
b
as,

A(s)=\frac{1}{s^{m-1}}\int_{b=s}(|\nabla b|^{2}-1)|\nabla b|\,dA,

(1.8)

and,

V(s)=\frac{1}{s^{m}}\int_{b\leq s}(|\nabla b|^{2}-1)|\nabla b|^{2}\,dV.

(1.9)

Having
A
and
V
, the monotonic volume is defined as

W(s)=2(m-1)V(s)-A(s),

(1.10)

and the derivative of
W
is given by the expression (see
[
5
]
, Theorem 2.4),

\frac{d}{ds}W(s)=-\frac{1}{2s^{m+1}}\int_{b\leq s}\left|\nabla\nabla b^{2}-\frac{\Delta b^{2}}{m}g\right|^{2}dV,

(1.11)

from which it follows that it is monotonically decreasing in
s
. For instance, on the manifold
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the Green function at the origin is given by
G(x)=1/|x|^{m-2}
. If we define
b=G^{1/(2-m)}
, then
b=|x|
, and therefore,
|\nabla b|=1
. Consequently, in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the area, volume, and monotonic volume are all identically zero.

We now outline how to derive Perelman’s entropy from Colding’s monotonic volume. Define
h:\hat{M}\to\mathbb{R}
by,

h=r^{-(m-2)}e^{-f(\tau,x)},

(1.12)

where
f
satisfies (
1.4
) and
\tau=r^{2}/2N
. The function
h
will serve as the analog to the Green function when applying Colding’s monotonic formula to Perelman’s
N
-space. The observation that
h
is almost harmonic is essentially due to Perelman. In Section 6.1 of
[
14
]
, it is observed that

\tilde{u}^{*}:=\tau^{-\frac{N-1}{2}}u=(2N)^{\frac{m-2}{2}}h,

is harmonic modulo
N^{-1}
if and only if
f
satisfies (
1.4
) (the precise statement is
\hat{\Delta}\tilde{u}^{*}=\tau^{(2-m)/2}O(1/N)
, see Proposition
4.2
). From this definition, and following
[
5
]
, we define
b:\hat{M}\to\mathbb{R}
as,

b=h^{1/(2-m)}=re^{f/(m-2)}.

(1.13)

Using this
b
, we define the quantities
\mathcal{A}_{N}
,
\mathcal{V}_{N}
, and
\mathcal{W}_{N}
as in (
1.8
), (
1.9
), and (
1.10
), respectively, each scaled by a coefficient
c_{N}
(see Definition
2.4
), and prove the following main results. Firstly, in Corollary
3.6
of Section
3
we demonstrate that the monotonic volume
\mathcal{W}_{N}
and its derivative converge uniformly on compact sets in
(0,T)
to Perelman’s
\mathcal{W}
-entropy and its derivative, respectively. Secondly, in Theorem
4.10
of Section
4
, we obtain Perelman’s formula (
1.6
) for the derivative of
\mathcal{W}
as a limit of the derivative (
1.11
) in Perelman’s
N
-space. Besides these two results, and as a byproduct of the proofs, in Theorem
3.3
we also show that the area
\mathcal{A}_{N}
and its derivative also converge uniformly to Perelman’s
\mathcal{W}-
entropy and its derivative respectively on compact sets in
(0,T)
.

The body of the work is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we introduce the relevant notation, definitions, and frequently referenced tools. In Section
3
, we derive Perelman’s entropy as a limit of Colding’s volume. Finally, in Section
4
, we demonstrate that Perelman’s formula for the derivative of the entropy can be obtained as a limit of Colding’s formula in
\hat{M}
.

Preliminaries

Here we compile some results, observations, and definitions that will be frequently referenced throughout the work. We begin with a few remarks on Perelman’s space,
(\hat{M},\hat{g}).

Strictly speaking,
\hat{g}
is a metric that depends on
N
, but we will omit the subscript
N
to simplify notation. In fact, we will omit the subscript
N
in most parts of the work. For instance, the Ricci curvature of
\hat{g}
will be denoted by
\hat{\Ric}
, the covariant derivative will be
\hat{\nabla}
, and so on.

The metric coefficient
(1+Rr^{2}/N^{2})=(1+2\tau R/N)
will appear often, so to simplify notation we define,

v:=1+\frac{Rr^{2}}{N^{2}}.

(2.1)

Note that
\hat{g}
is invariant under rotations in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
and therefore, scalar quantities like the scalar curvature
\hat{R}
, or the norm of the Ricci tensor
|\hat{\Ric}|
, are also invariant under rotations and thus
\theta
-independent. For this reason, they will often be considered as functions on
(0,T)\times M
. For instance,
v
can be viewed as a function on
\hat{M}
or
(0,T)\times M
, depending on the context.

In
[
14
]
, Perelman showed that
|\hat{\Ric}|=O(1/N)
, (see
[
4
]
for the full computation of the Christoffel symbols and curvature components). This means that the sequence of functions
N|\hat{\Ric}|
, as functions on
(0,T)\times M
, is uniformly bounded on compact sets.

Regarding the definition of order, we will need the following.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
i\geq 0
be an integer. A sequence of real-valued functions
F_{N}(\tau,\theta,x)
is an
O_{0}(1/N^{i})
if for every
0<\tau_{1}<T
there exists
K>0
such that,

N^{i}|F_{N}(\tau,\theta,x)|\leq K,

for all
N>0
and for all
(\tau,\theta,x)
such that
\tau_{1}\leq\tau\leq T
.

We say that
F_{N}(\tau,\theta,x)
is an
O_{k}(1/N^{i})
if
\partial^{\alpha}F_{N}
is an
O_{0}(1/N^{i})
, for any multi-index
|\alpha|\leq k
, where the derivatives are taken in the
\tau
or
x^{i}
variables.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that a sequence of real-valued functions
F_{N}(\lambda)
, defined in
(0,T)
, is an
O_{0}(e^{-cN})
if for each
\lambda_{0}\in(0,T)
, given
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{0}
, there exist
K>0
and
c>0
such that,

e^{cN}|F_{N}(\lambda)|\leq K,

(2.2)

for all
N>0
and for all
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda\leq T.

We say that
F_{N}(\lambda)
is an
O_{k}(e^{-cN})
if
d^{j}F_{N}/d\lambda^{j}
is an
O_{0}(e^{-cN})
for every
j\leq k
.

A direct application of the Taylor expansion with the remainder in integral form allows us to derive the following lemma, which we will frequently reference.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
F:(a,b)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth real-valued function. Let
w(\tau,x,N)
be a smooth real-valued function with range in
(a,b)
, such that,

w(\tau,x,N)=w_{0}(\tau,x)+\delta(\tau,x,N),

where,

\delta(\tau,x,N)=O_{k}(\frac{1}{N^{j}}),

for some integers
k\geq 0
and
j\geq 0
. Then, for any
l\geq 1
,
F(w(\tau,x,N))
has the following decomposition
(
where we omit the
\tau,x
and
N
dependence for notational convenience
)
,

F(w)=F(w_{0})+F^{\prime}(w_{0})\delta+F^{\prime\prime}(w_{0})\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}+\ldots+F^{(l-1)}(w_{0})\frac{\delta^{l-1}}{(l-1)!}+R_{l},

and,

R_{l}(\tau,x,N)=O_{k}(\frac{1}{N^{jl}}).

Note that the usual rules of orders hold: the sum
O_{k}(1/N^{i})+O_{l}(1/N^{j})
is an
O_{\min{(k,l)}}(1/N^{\min{(i,j)}})
and the product
O_{k}(1/N^{i})O_{l}(1/N^{j})
is an
O_{\min{(k,l)}}(1/N^{i+j})
. For instance, in Lemma
2.3
, if
\delta=O_{k}(1/N^{j}),
then
\delta^{i}=O_{k}(1/N^{ji}).

As an example of Lemma
2.3
,

v=1+2\tau R/N=1+\delta,\text{\, where \,}\delta=2\tau R/N=O_{k}(1/N),

and therefore,

1/\sqrt{v}=1-\tau R/N+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N^{2}}).

We observe that, while as in this and other cases,
\delta=O_{k}(1/N)
for any
k\geq 0
, for our purposes it will be enough to use
k\leq 2
. Similarly,

e^{f/(m-2)}=1+\frac{f}{N}+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N^{2}})=1+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}).

We will not delve into detail for most order computations, as they primarily involve combinations of compositions and products of the previous examples, along with straightforward applications of Lemma
2.3
.

Now we proceed to make some key observations about the level sets of
b
. Observe that, since
b/\sqrt{2N}=\sqrt{\tau}e^{f/(m-2)}
, for every
k\geq 0
we have,

\frac{b}{\sqrt{2N}}=\sqrt{\tau}+O_{k}(\frac{1}{N}).

(2.3)

Therefore, for every small
\delta>0
and
k\geq 0
, the sequence of functions

b/\sqrt{2N}:[\delta,T-\delta]_{\tau}\times M_{x}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

converges in
C^{k}
to the function (coordinate)
\tau:[\delta,T-\delta]\times M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. By standard calculus, it follows that there exists
N_{0}>0
such that for every
N>N_{0}
and
\lambda\in[2\delta,T-2\delta]
, the level set
b/\sqrt{2N}=\sqrt{\lambda}
is given by an immersion

x\in M\mapsto(\phi_{N,\lambda}(x),x)\in(0,T)\times M,

(2.4)

for some smooth function
\phi_{N,\lambda}:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Furthermore, from (
2.3
) we get,

\lambda=\frac{b^{2}}{2N}(\phi_{N,\lambda}(x),x)=\tau(\phi_{N,\lambda}(x),x)+O_{k}(\frac{1}{N})=\phi_{N,\lambda}(x)+O_{k}(\frac{1}{N}),

(2.5)

that is, the immersion approaches the level set
\tau=\lambda
.
Since we will be interested in taking limits, we will always assume that
N
is sufficiently big such that (
2.4
) holds inside a region
\lambda\in[\delta,T-\delta]
, where
\delta
is sufficiently small.

We conclude the section by defining
\mathcal{A}_{N},

\mathcal{V}_{N}
and
\mathcal{W}_{N}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let

c_{N}:=(4\pi)^{-n/2}(2N)^{n/2+1}/4|\mathbb{S}^{N}|.

We define the area
\mathcal{A}_{N}:(0,\sqrt{2NT})\to\mathbb{R}

(
resp. the raw area
\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}:(0,\sqrt{2NT})\to\mathbb{R}
)
as,

\mathcal{A}_{N}(s)=\frac{c_{N}}{s^{m-1}}\int_{b=s}(|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}-1)|\hat{\nabla{b}}|\,d\hat{A},\,\,\left(\mbox{resp. }\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}(s)=\frac{c_{N}}{s^{m-1}}\int_{b=s}|\hat{\nabla}b|^{3}\,d\hat{A}\right),

(2.6)

the volume
\mathcal{V}_{N}:(0,\sqrt{2NT})\to\mathbb{R}
as,

\mathcal{V}_{N}(s)=\frac{c_{N}}{s^{m}}\int_{b\leq s}(|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}-1)|\hat{\nabla{b}}|^{2}\,d\hat{V},

(2.7)

and the monotonic volume
\mathcal{W}_{N}:(0,\sqrt{2NT})\to\mathbb{R}
as,

\mathcal{W}_{N}(s)=(2(m-1)\mathcal{V}_{N}-\mathcal{A}_{N})(s).

(2.8)

Remark 2.5
.

We always associate
\lambda\in(0,T)
with
s=\sqrt{2N\lambda}\in(0,\sqrt{2NT})
.
Quantities that are functions of
s
, for instance
\mathcal{A}_{N}(s)
, will be considered sometimes as functions of
\lambda\in(0,T)
, in which case we will write, for example,
\mathcal{A}_{N}(\lambda)
instead of
\mathcal{A}_{N}(s=\sqrt{2N\lambda})
. Convergence of
\mathcal{A}_{N}
or
\mathcal{V}_{N}
to the entropy
\mathcal{W}
occurs when considering them as functions of
\lambda\in(0,T)
.

Derivation of Perelman’s Entropy from Colding’s Monotonic Volume

We will make repeated use of the following results.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f=f(\tau,x)
be any smooth real-valued function, that we consider as a function on
\hat{M}
. Then,

|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}=1+\frac{1}{N}\left(2f-2\tau R+2\tau|\nabla f|^{2}+4\tau\partial_{\tau}f\right)+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N^{2}}).

(3.1)

In particular, if
f
satisfies
(
1.4
)
, then,

\frac{N}{2}(|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}-1)=\tau(2\Delta f-|\nabla f|^{2}+R)+f-n+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}),

(3.2)

and therefore,

|\hat{\nabla}b|=1+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}).

(3.3)

Proof.

We compute,

\hat{\nabla}b=\frac{e^{f/(m-2)}}{\sqrt{v}}\left(1+\frac{r\partial_{r}f}{m-2}\right)\frac{\partial_{r}}{\sqrt{v}}+\frac{re^{f/(m-2)}}{m-2}\nabla f.

(3.4)

Thus,

|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}=\frac{e^{2f/(m-2)}}{v}\left(1+\frac{r\partial_{r}f}{m-2}\right)^{2}+\frac{r^{2}e^{2f/(m-2)}}{(m-2)^{2}}|\nabla f|^{2}.

(3.5)

Now,
r\partial_{r}f=2\tau\partial_{\tau}f
. Therefore,

\left(1+\frac{r\partial_{r}f}{m-2}\right)^{2}=1+\frac{4\tau\partial_{\tau}f}{N}+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N^{2}}).

(3.6)

Also, by virtue of Lemma
2.3
(see also the discussion below the Lemma),

\frac{e^{2f/(m-2)}}{v}=\left(1+\frac{2f}{m-2}+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N^{2}})\right)\left(1-\frac{2\tau R}{N}+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N^{2}})\right)=1+\frac{2f}{N}-\frac{2\tau R}{N}+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N^{2}}),

and similarly,

\frac{r^{2}e^{2f/(m-2)}}{(m-2)^{2}}|\nabla f|^{2}=\frac{2N\tau}{(m-2)^{2}}e^{2f/(m-2)}|\nabla f|^{2}=\frac{2\tau|\nabla f|^{2}}{N}+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N^{2}}).

After putting altogether inside (
3.5
), we obtain (
3.1
).
Finally, we can replace
\partial_{\tau}f
by (
1.4
) to get (
3.2
).
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

For any
\lambda\in(0,T)
, the volume element for the level set
b=s=\sqrt{2N\lambda}
can be expressed as,

d\hat{A}=s^{N}e^{-f(\lambda,x)}(1+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}))d\nu d\nu_{\mathbb{S}^{N}},

(3.7)

where
d\nu_{\mathbb{S}^{N}}
is the standard volume element in
\mathbb{S}^{N}
, and
d\nu
is the volume element in
(M,g(\lambda))
.
In particular, we have

d\hat{A}=(2N\lambda)^{N/2}e^{-f(\lambda,x)}(1+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}))d\nu d\nu_{\mathbb{S}^{N}}.

(3.8)

Proof.

Since
\lambda\in(0,T),
there exists
\delta>0
such that
\lambda\in[\delta,T-\delta]
. Then, using (
2.4
),
for every
N>N_{0}
we define a map
\psi_{N,\lambda}:\mathbb{S}^{N}\times M\to\hat{M}
as

\psi_{N,\lambda}(\theta,x)=(\sqrt{2N\phi_{N,\lambda}}(x),\theta,x).

(3.9)

Let
\theta^{\alpha}
be coordinates in
\mathbb{S}^{N}
and
x^{i}
coordinates on
M
. Since
r=\sqrt{2N\phi_{N,\lambda}}(x)
and by (
2.5
) we have
\phi_{N,\lambda}=\lambda+O_{k}(N^{-1})
, we compute,

\displaystyle\psi_{N,\lambda}^{*}\hat{g}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})

\displaystyle=\hat{g}(d\psi_{N,\lambda}(\partial_{i}),d\psi_{N,\lambda}(\partial_{j}))

\displaystyle=\hat{g}\left(\frac{\sqrt{N}}{\sqrt{2\phi_{N,\lambda}}}\partial_{i}\phi_{N,\lambda}\partial_{r}+\partial_{i},\frac{\sqrt{N}}{\sqrt{2\phi_{N,\lambda}}}\partial_{j}\phi_{N,\lambda}\partial_{r}+\partial_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=g_{ij}+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}),

\displaystyle\psi_{N,\lambda}^{*}\hat{g}(\partial_{\alpha},\partial_{\beta})

\displaystyle=\hat{g}(\partial_{\alpha},\partial_{\beta})=(2N\phi_{N,\lambda})g_{\mathbb{S}^{N}\alpha\beta},

\displaystyle\psi_{N,\lambda}^{*}\hat{g}(\partial_{\alpha},\partial_{i})

\displaystyle=0,

for every
N>N_{0}
.
Therefore, we can express the volume element
d\hat{A}
as,

d\hat{A}=(2N\phi_{N,\lambda})^{N/2}(1+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}))d\nu d\nu_{\mathbb{S}^{N}},

where we are using Lemma
2.3
when computing
\sqrt{\psi_{N,s}^{*}\hat{g}}
.
Since
r^{2}/2N=\tau=\phi_{N,\lambda}(x)
for any
(r,\theta,x)\in b=s
, we have,

r=\sqrt{2N\phi_{N,\lambda}}(x)=se^{f(\phi_{N,\lambda}(x),x)/(2-m)},

(3.10)

and by (
2.5
) and Lemma
2.3
we can write,

\begin{split}(2N\phi_{N,\lambda})^{N/2}&=s^{N}e^{-f(\lambda+O_{k}(1/N),x)+O_{2}(1/N)}\\
&=s^{N}e^{-f(\lambda,x)}(1+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N})),\end{split}

which completes the proof.
∎

We now demonstrate that Perelman’s
\mathcal{W}
-entropy and its derivative emerge as the limits of the area
\mathcal{A}_{N}
and its derivative, respectively, when considered as functions of
\lambda
(see Remark
2.5
).

Theorem 3.3
.

The following equality holds:

\mathcal{A}_{N}(\lambda)=\mathcal{W}(\lambda)+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}),

(3.11)

where
\mathcal{W}
is Perelman’s entropy functional
(
1.5
)
.
In particular,

\mathcal{A}_{N}\to\mathcal{W},

(3.12)

and

\frac{d\mathcal{A}_{N}}{d\lambda}\to\frac{d\mathcal{W}}{d\lambda},

(3.13)

uniformly on compact sets in
(0,T)
.

Proof.

Let
s=\sqrt{2N\lambda}
. We compute,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{N}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{N}}{s^{m-1}}\int_{b=s}(|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}-1)|\hat{\nabla b}|d\hat{A}

\displaystyle=(4\pi\lambda)^{-n/2}\int_{M}\left(\lambda(2\Delta f-|\nabla f|^{2}+R)+f-n\right)e^{-f(\lambda,x)}(1+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}))d\nu

\displaystyle=(4\pi\lambda)^{-n/2}\int_{M}\left(\lambda(2\Delta f-|\nabla f|^{2}+R)+f-n\right)e^{-f(\lambda,x)}d\nu+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}),

where we used (
3.2
) and (
3.8
) together with the fact that the integrand does not depend on
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{N}
. Since
M
is a closed manifold,

\int_{M}(\Delta f-|\nabla f|^{2})e^{-f}d\nu=-\int_{M}\Delta e^{-f}d\nu=0,

and we use it to rewrite,

\mathcal{A}_{N}=(4\pi\lambda)^{-n/2}\int_{M}\left(\lambda(|\nabla f|^{2}+R)+f-n\right)e^{-f}d\nu+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}),

from which (
3.11
) follows. The previous expression also shows that
\mathcal{A}_{N}\to\mathcal{W}
uniformly on compact sets in
(0,T)
, and differentiating with respect to
\lambda
on both sides of (
3.11
),

\frac{d\mathcal{A}_{N}}{d\lambda}=\frac{d\mathcal{W}}{d\lambda}+O_{1}(\frac{1}{N}),

(3.14)

which also shows that convergence of the derivatives is uniform on compact sets in
(0,T)
.

∎

We now proceed to study the convergence of the volume. To begin, we introduce a lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\lambda_{0}<\lambda\in(0,T)
. Define
\bar{s}=\sqrt{2N\lambda_{0}}
and
s=\sqrt{2N\lambda}
. Then,

\mathcal{V}_{N}(s)=\frac{c_{N}}{s^{m}}\int_{\bar{s}\leq b\leq s}(|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}-1)|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}\,d\hat{V}+O_{1}(e^{-cN}).

(3.15)

Proof.

We write,

\mathcal{V}_{N}(s)=\frac{c_{N}}{s^{m}}\int_{\bar{s}\leq b\leq s}(|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}-1)|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}\,d\hat{V}+\frac{c_{N}}{s^{m}}\int_{0\leq b\leq\bar{s}}(|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}-1)|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}\,d\hat{V}.

Since
f=-\ln u-(n/2)\ln\tau,
a straightforward computation using (
3.4
) shows that we can write
|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}
in terms of
u
as,

\begin{split}|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}&=(2N)^{n/(m-2)}(ur^{n})^{2/(2-m)}\left[\left(1-\frac{2\tau\partial_{\tau}u-nu}{u(m-2)}\right)^{2}+\frac{r^{2}}{(m-2)^{2}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}\right]\\
&=(2N)^{n/(m-2)}(ur^{n})^{2/(2-m)}H_{N}(r^{2}/2N,x),\end{split}

and, since
u
is everywhere positive in
[0,\lambda_{0}]\times M
and its first derivatives are bounded, it follows that
|H_{N}|\leq C
and
0<c_{1}<u<c_{2}
in
[0,\lambda_{0}]\times M
, for some constants
C,c_{1},c_{2}>0
. In particular, the same bounds hold for the region
0\leq b\leq\bar{s}
, and since
d\hat{V}=r^{N}drd\nu d\nu_{\mathbb{S}^{N}},
we use the previous expression for
|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}
to find,

\left|\frac{c_{N}}{s^{m}}\int_{0\leq b\leq\bar{s}}|\hat{\nabla}b|^{4}d\hat{V}\right|\leq K_{1}\left(\frac{\bar{s}}{s}\right)^{N+1},

(3.16)

for some constant
K_{1}>0
. Since
\bar{s}/s=\lambda_{0}/\lambda<1
, it decays exponentially fast. Similarly,

\left|\frac{c_{N}}{s^{m}}\int_{0\leq b\leq\bar{s}}|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}d\hat{V}\right|\leq K_{2}\left(\frac{\bar{s}}{s}\right)^{N+1},

(3.17)

for some constant
K_{2}>0
, which shows

\mathcal{V}_{N}=\frac{c_{N}}{s^{m}}\int_{\bar{s}\leq b\leq s}(|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}-1)|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}\,d\hat{V}+O_{0}(e^{-cN}).

Finally, in order to see that the term
O_{0}(e^{-cN})
is in fact
O_{1}(e^{-cN})
, we note that

\begin{split}\frac{d}{d\lambda}\left(\frac{c_{N}}{s^{m}}\int_{0\leq b\leq\bar{s}}(|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}-1)|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}\,d\hat{V}\right)&=\frac{ds}{d\lambda}\frac{d}{ds}\left(\frac{c_{N}}{s^{m}}\int_{\bar{0}\leq b\leq\bar{s}}(|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}-1)|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}\,d\hat{V}\right)\\
&=-\left(\frac{N}{2\lambda}\right)^{1/2}\frac{m}{s}\frac{c_{N}}{s^{m}}\int_{\bar{0}\leq b\leq\bar{s}}(|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}-1)|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}d\hat{V},\end{split}

and apply (
3.16
) and (
3.17
) to see that its derivative is also an
O_{0}(e^{-cN})
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

The following equality holds,

2(m-1)\mathcal{V}_{N}-\mathcal{A}_{N}=\mathcal{A}_{N}+O_{1}(\frac{1}{N}).

(3.18)

In particular,

\frac{d}{ds}(2(m-1)\mathcal{V}_{N}-\mathcal{A}_{N})=\frac{d}{ds}\mathcal{A}_{N}+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{3/2}}).

(3.19)

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in(0,T)
, and define
s=\sqrt{2N\lambda}
. Next, we choose
\lambda_{0}<\lambda
and define
\bar{s}=\sqrt{2N\lambda_{0}}
. By the coarea formula and equation (
3.15
) we obtain,

\mathcal{V}_{N}=\frac{c_{N}}{s^{m}}\int_{\bar{s}\leq b\leq s}(|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}-1)|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}d\hat{V}+O_{1}(e^{-cN})=\frac{1}{s^{m}}\int_{\bar{s}}^{s}w^{m-1}\mathcal{A}_{N}(w)dw+O_{1}(e^{-cN}).

Using (
3.14
), we deduce,

\begin{split}\frac{d\mathcal{A}_{N}}{ds}&=\frac{d\lambda}{ds}\frac{d\mathcal{A}_{N}}{d\lambda}\\
&=(4\pi)^{n/2}\frac{d\lambda}{ds}\left(\frac{d\mathcal{W}}{d\lambda}+\frac{d}{d\lambda}\left(O_{2}(\frac{1}{N})\right)\right)\\
&=(4\pi)^{n/2}\left(\frac{2\lambda}{N}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{d\mathcal{W}}{d\lambda}+O_{1}(\frac{1}{N})\right)\\
&=O_{1}(\frac{1}{N^{1/2}}),\end{split}

(3.20)

and,

\frac{d^{2}\mathcal{A}_{N}}{ds^{2}}=\frac{d\lambda}{ds}\frac{d}{d\lambda}\left(O_{1}(\frac{1}{N^{1/2}})\right)=O_{0}(\frac{1}{N}),

(3.21)

since
d\mathcal{W}/d\lambda=O_{2}(1)
.
A Taylor expansion around
s
shows,

\mathcal{A}_{N}(w)=\mathcal{A}_{N}(s)+(w-s)\frac{d\mathcal{A}_{N}}{ds}(\xi_{w,s}),

for some
\sqrt{2N\lambda_{0}}\leq\xi_{w,s}\leq\sqrt{2N\lambda}
. The bound on
\xi_{w,s}
implies the respective bound for its associated
\tau-
coordinate between
\lambda_{0}
and
\lambda
, and therefore by (
3.20
),

\mathcal{A}_{N}(w)=\mathcal{A}_{N}(s)+(w-s)O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{1/2}}),

for any
w\in[\bar{s},s]
. Now, we notice that, since
|O_{0}(N^{-1/2})|\leq CN^{-1/2}
,

\begin{split}-\frac{s^{m+1}}{s^{m}(m+1)m}\frac{C}{N^{1/2}}+O_{0}(e^{-cN})&\leq\frac{1}{s^{m}}\int_{\bar{s}}^{s}w^{m-1}\left((w-s)O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{1/2}})\right)dw\\
&\leq\frac{s^{m+1}}{s^{m}(m+1)m}\frac{C}{N^{1/2}}+O_{0}(e^{-cN}),\end{split}

(3.22)

which shows,

\frac{1}{s^{m}}\int_{\bar{s}}^{s}w^{m-1}\left((w-s)O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{1/2}})\right)dw=O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{2}}).

(3.23)

Then,

\begin{split}\mathcal{V}_{N}&=\frac{1}{s^{m}}\int_{\bar{s}}^{s}w^{m-1}\left(\mathcal{A}_{N}(s)+(w-s)O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{1/2}})\right)dw+O_{1}(e^{-cN})\\
&=\frac{1}{m}\mathcal{A}_{N}+O_{0}({\frac{1}{N^{2}}}).\end{split}

(3.24)

In order to see that the term
O_{0}(N^{-2})
is actually an
O_{1}(N^{-2})
, we use a Taylor expansion of order
2
to compute,

\begin{split}\frac{d\mathcal{V}_{N}}{ds}&=\frac{d}{ds}\left(\frac{1}{s^{m}}\int_{\bar{s}}^{s}w^{m-1}\mathcal{A}_{N}(w)dw\right)+O_{0}(e^{-cN})\\
&=-\frac{m}{s^{m+1}}\int_{\bar{s}}^{s}w^{m-1}\mathcal{A}_{N}(w)dw+\frac{1}{s}\mathcal{A}_{N}(s)+O_{0}(e^{-cN})\\
&=-\frac{m}{s^{m+1}}\int_{\bar{s}}^{s}w^{m-1}\left(\mathcal{A}_{N}(s)+(w-s)\frac{d}{ds}\mathcal{A}_{N}(s)+\frac{(w-s)^{2}}{2}\frac{d^{2}\mathcal{A}_{N}}{ds^{2}}(\xi_{w,s})\right)dw\\
&\phantom{....}+\frac{1}{s}\mathcal{A}_{N}(s)+O_{0}(e^{-cN}),\end{split}

(3.25)

where
\xi_{w,s}\in[w,s].
Now, since
d^{2}\mathcal{A}_{N}/ds^{2}=O_{0}(1/N),
we proceed as in
(\ref{Argument for o0 taylor})
and show,

\begin{split}\int_{\bar{s}}^{s}w^{m-1}&\left(\mathcal{A}_{N}(s)+(w-s)\frac{d\mathcal{A}_{N}}{ds}(s)+\frac{(w-s)^{2}}{2}\frac{d^{2}\mathcal{A}_{N}}{ds^{2}}(\xi_{w,s})\right)dw\\
&=\frac{s^{m}}{m}\mathcal{A}_{N}(s)-\frac{s^{m+1}}{m(m+1)}\frac{d\mathcal{A}_{N}}{ds}(s)+\frac{s^{m+2}}{m(m+1)(m+2)}O_{0}(\frac{1}{N})+O_{0}(e^{-cN}).\end{split}

(3.26)

Use this in (
3.25
) and the fact that
d\mathcal{A}_{N}/ds=O_{1}(N^{-1/2})
to write,

\frac{d\mathcal{V}_{N}}{ds}=\frac{1}{m+1}\frac{d\mathcal{A}_{N}}{ds}(s)+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{5/2}})=\frac{1}{m}\frac{d\mathcal{A}_{N}}{ds}(s)+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{5/2}}).

(3.27)

This implies,

\frac{d\mathcal{V}_{N}}{d\lambda}=\frac{ds}{d\lambda}\frac{d\mathcal{V}_{N}}{ds}=\frac{1}{m}\frac{d\mathcal{A}_{N}}{d\lambda}+\frac{ds}{d\lambda}O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{5/2}})=\frac{1}{m}\frac{d\mathcal{A}_{N}}{d\lambda}+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{2}}),

which we combine with (
3.24
) to obtain,

\mathcal{V}_{N}=\frac{1}{m}\mathcal{A}_{N}+O_{1}(\frac{1}{N^{2}}).

(3.28)

From here, a straightforward computation proves (
3.18
). We finish the proof by differentiating (
3.18
) with respect to
s
and noting that

\frac{d}{ds}\left(O_{1}(\frac{1}{N})\right)=\frac{d\lambda}{ds}\frac{d}{d\lambda}\left(O_{1}(\frac{1}{N})\right)=O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{3/2}}).

∎

As a consequence of Theorem
3.3
and Proposition
3.5
,
recalling the definition of the monotonic volume (
2.8
) we immediately derive the following result.

Corollary 3.6
.

The monotonic volume obeys,

\mathcal{W}_{N}(\lambda)=\mathcal{W}(\lambda)+O_{1}(\frac{1}{N}),

(3.29)

and,

\frac{d}{d\lambda}\mathcal{W}_{N}(\lambda)=\frac{d}{d\lambda}\mathcal{W}(\lambda)+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N}).

(3.30)

In particular,
\mathcal{W}_{N}\to\mathcal{W}
and
d\mathcal{W}_{N}/d\lambda\to d\mathcal{W}/d\lambda
uniformly on compact sets in
(0,T)
.

Computation of the derivative of the entropy from the derivative of the monotonic volume

We now derive Perelman’s formula for the derivative of
\mathcal{W}
as a limit of derivatives of
\mathcal{W}_{N}
. To achieve this, several auxiliary results are in order.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
h=h(\tau,x)
be any smooth real-valued function, that we consider as a function on
\hat{M}
. Then,

\displaystyle\hat{\Delta}h=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{v}\left(\left(1+\frac{1+2\tau R}{N}-\frac{2(\tau R+\tau^{2}\partial_{\tau}R)}{N^{2}v}\right)\partial_{\tau}h+\frac{2\tau}{N}\partial^{2}_{\tau}h\right)

(4.1)

\displaystyle+\Delta h+\frac{1}{Nv}\langle\nabla R,\nabla h\rangle.

(4.2)

Proof.

Let
\theta^{\alpha}
be coordinates in
\mathbb{S}^{N}
and
x^{i}
coordinates on
M
. Then, the inverse metric components of
\hat{g}
are,

\hat{g}^{ri}=\hat{g}^{r\alpha}=\hat{g}^{\alpha i}=0,\quad\hat{g}=\frac{1}{v},\quad\hat{g}^{ij}=g^{ij},\quad\hat{g}^{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{r^{2}}g^{\alpha\beta}_{\mathbb{S}^{N}},

(4.3)

and the Laplacian therefore is,

\hat{\Delta}h=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\hat{g}}}\partial_{r}(\sqrt{\hat{g}}\hat{g}^{rr}\partial_{r}h)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\hat{g}}}\partial_{i}(\sqrt{\hat{g}}\hat{g}^{ij}\partial_{j}h)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\hat{g}}}\partial_{\alpha}(\sqrt{\hat{g}}\hat{g}^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\beta}h).

(4.4)

A straightforward computation shows that the first term of the r.h.s of (
4.4
) is equal to the r.h.s of (
4.1
), the second term is equal to the two summands in (
4.2
) and the last term is equal to zero.
∎

The following assertion is drawn from Perelman (as mentioned in p.13 of
[
14
]
), where we needed to include factor of
r^{2-m}
.

Proposition 4.2

(Perelman,
[
14
]
)
.

Let
f=f(\tau,x)
be any smooth real-valued function that we consider as a function on
\hat{M}
, and let
h=r^{2-m}e^{-f}
. Then,

\displaystyle\hat{\Delta}h=r^{2-m}\left(\partial_{\tau}f-\Delta f+|\nabla f|^{2}-R+\frac{n}{2\tau}\right)e^{-f}+r^{2-m}O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}).

(4.5)

Therefore, if
f
satisfies,
(
1.4
)
, then

\hat{\Delta}h=r^{2-m}O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}).

(4.6)

Proof.

Let
u=\tau^{-(n-1)/2}e^{-f}
so that
h=(2N)^{(2-m)/2}\tau^{-N/2}u
. To simplify notation, we will disregard the multiplicative factor
(2N)^{(2-m)/2}
in
h
during the computations, and add it back in afterwards. We compute,

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}h=-\frac{N}{2}\tau^{-N/2-1}u+\tau^{-N/2}\partial_{\tau}u,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{\tau}h=\frac{N}{2}\left(\frac{N}{2}+1\right)\tau^{-N/2-2}u-N\tau^{-N/2-1}\partial_{\tau}u+\tau^{-N/2}\partial^{2}_{\tau}u.

(4.8)

We can write
\partial^{2}_{\tau}u=\tau^{-(n+1)/2}O_{2}(1)
. Therefore,

\frac{2\tau}{N}\partial^{2}_{\tau}h=\tau^{-N/2}\left(\frac{N}{2\tau}u+\frac{1}{\tau}u-2\partial_{\tau}u\right)+\tau^{-m/2}O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}).

(4.9)

Similarly, writing (
4.7
) as,

\partial_{\tau}h=\tau^{-N/2}\left(-\frac{N}{2\tau}u+\partial_{\tau}u\right),

(4.10)

we obtain,

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{1+2\tau R}{N}-\frac{2(\tau R+\tau^{2}\partial_{\tau}R)}{N^{2}v}\right)\partial_{\tau}h=

(4.11)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt-\tau^{-N/2}\left(\frac{N}{2\tau}u+(\frac{1}{2\tau}+R)u-\partial_{\tau}u\right)+\tau^{-m/2}O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}).

(4.12)

Summing (
4.9
) and (
4.11
), and after the crucial mutual cancellation of the terms
\tau^{-N/2-1}Nu/2
, we deduce,

\displaystyle\frac{2\tau}{N}\partial^{2}_{\tau}h

\displaystyle+\left(1+\frac{1+2\tau R}{N}-\frac{2(\tau R+\tau^{2}\partial_{\tau}R)}{N^{2}v}\right)\partial_{\tau}h=

(4.13)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\tau^{-N/2}(-\frac{1}{2\tau}u-Ru-\partial_{\tau}u)+\tau^{-m/2}O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}).

(4.14)

Going back to the expression (
4.1
) for
\hat{\Delta}h
, and taking into account that
1/v=1+O_{2}(1/N)
and
\langle\nabla R,\nabla h\rangle/Nv=O_{2}(1/N)
, we arrive at,

\hat{\Delta}h=\tau^{-N/2}(-\partial_{\tau}u+\Delta u-Ru+\frac{1}{2\tau}u)+\tau^{-m/2}O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}).

(4.15)

Recalling that
u=\tau^{-(n-1)/2}e^{-f}
and multiplying by the factor
(2N)^{(2-m)/2}
, we deduce (
4.5
).
∎

From the previous proposition we obtain the following.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
h
be defined as in
(
1.12
)
and
b
as in
(
1.13
)
. Then,

\hat{\Delta}b^{2}=2m|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}).

(4.16)

Proof.

Direct computation shows,

\hat{\Delta}b^{2}=2m|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}+\frac{2}{2-m}h^{m/(2-m)}\hat{\Delta}h.

Using that
\hat{\Delta}h=r^{2-m}O_{2}(1/N)
and that
b=h^{1/(2-m)}
, we get,

\displaystyle\frac{2}{2-m}h^{m/(2-m)}\hat{\Delta}h=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{2-m}b^{m}r^{2-m}O_{2}(\frac{1}{N})=\frac{2}{2-m}r^{2}e^{mf/(m-2)}O_{2}(\frac{1}{N})=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4N}{2-m}\tau e^{mf/(m-2)}O_{2}(\frac{1}{N})=O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}).

∎

The following formula for the derivative of the raw area
\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}
follows essentially from Theorem 2.4 in
[
5
]
, but with two key differences: (i) additional terms appear due to the fact that

Proposition 4.4
.

The following equality holds:

\begin{split}\frac{d}{ds}\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}=&\frac{c_{N}}{2s^{m+1}}\int_{\bar{s}\leq b\leq s}\left(\left|\hat{\nabla}\hat{\nabla}b^{2}-\frac{\hat{\Delta}b^{2}}{m}\hat{g}\right|^{2}+\hat{\Ric}(\hat{\nabla}b^{2},\hat{\nabla}b^{2})\right)d\hat{V}\\
&+\frac{2(m-1)}{s}(\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}-m\overline{\mathcal{V}}_{N})+3[A_{N}]+[B_{N}]+[C_{N}]+[D_{N}],\end{split}

(4.17)

where,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{V}}_{N}=\frac{c_{N}}{s^{m}}\int_{\bar{s}\leq b\leq s}|\hat{\nabla}b|^{4}\,d\hat{V},

(4.18)

\displaystyle[A_{N}]=\frac{c_{N}}{2-m}\int_{b=s}|\hat{\nabla}b|\hat{\Delta}h\,d\hat{A},

(4.19)

\displaystyle[B_{N}]=\frac{c_{N}}{4s^{m+1}}\int_{b=\bar{s}}\hat{\nabla}_{\hat{\rm n}}(|\hat{\nabla}b^{2}|^{2})\,d\hat{A},

(4.20)

\displaystyle[C_{N}]=-\frac{c_{N}}{2s^{m+1}}\int_{b=\bar{s}}(\hat{\Delta}b^{2})\hat{\nabla}_{\hat{\rm n}}b^{2}\,d\hat{A},

(4.21)

\displaystyle[D_{N}]=-(1-\frac{1}{m})\frac{c_{N}}{2s^{m+1}}\int_{\bar{s}\leq b\leq s}\left(\frac{8m}{2-m}|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}b^{m}\hat{\Delta}h+\frac{4b^{2m}}{(2-m)^{2}}(\hat{\Delta}h)^{2}\right)\,d\hat{V},

(4.22)

and where
\hat{\rm n}=\hat{\nabla}b/|\hat{\nabla}b|
is the unit-normal to
b=\bar{s}
.

Proof.

Follow the proof of Theorem 2.4 in
[
5
]
, but, when computing
(s^{2}\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N})^{\prime}/s^{2}
, use that
\hat{\Delta}b^{2}=2m|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}+2b^{m}(\hat{\Delta}h)/(2-m)
instead of
\hat{\Delta}b^{2}=2m|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}
. Furthermore, when using Gauss’s theorem, integrate on
\bar{s}\leq b\leq s
rather than on
b\leq s
.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

We have,

\frac{d}{ds}\left(\frac{c_{N}}{s^{m-1}}\int_{b=s}|\hat{\nabla}b|d\hat{A}\right)=[A_{N}]+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N^{3/2}}).

(4.23)

Proof.

Since
\hat{\nabla}_{\hat{n}}b=|\hat{\nabla}b|,
we compute,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\left(\frac{1}{s^{m-1}}\int_{b=s}|\hat{\nabla}b|\,d\hat{A}\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\left(\int_{b=s}\frac{\hat{\nabla}_{\hat{\rm n}}b}{b^{m-1}}\,d\hat{A}\right)=\frac{1}{2-m}\frac{d}{ds}\left(\int_{b=s}\hat{\nabla}_{\hat{\rm n}}h\,d\hat{A}\right)

(4.24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2-m}\int_{b=s}\frac{\hat{\Delta}h}{|\hat{\nabla}b|}\,d\hat{A}.

(4.25)

Appeal now to (
3.3
) to obtain,

\frac{1}{|\hat{\nabla}b|}=|\hat{\nabla}b|\frac{1}{|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}}=|\hat{\nabla}b|\frac{1}{1+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N})}=|\hat{\nabla}b|(1+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}))=|\hat{\nabla}b|+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}).

(4.26)

Then, using (
3.10
) in (
4.6
), we get
\hat{\Delta}h=s^{2-m}O_{2}(1/N)
on the level set
b=s
. Now, combining this, (
4.26
) and (
3.7
) all in (
4.25
), we deduce,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\left(\frac{c_{N}}{s^{m-1}}\int_{b=s}|\hat{\nabla}b|d\hat{A}\right)=

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=[A_{N}]+\frac{c_{N}}{2-m}\int_{M}s^{2-m}O_{2}(\frac{1}{N})O_{2}(\frac{1}{N})s^{N}(1+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}))e^{-f}\,d\nu d\nu_{\mathbb{S}^{N}}.

But, as
c_{N}=(4\pi)^{-n/2}(2N)^{n/2+1}/(4|\mathbb{S}^{N}|)
and
s=(2N\lambda)^{1/2}
, we obtain,

\displaystyle\frac{c_{N}}{2-m}\int_{M}s^{2-m}O_{2}(\frac{1}{N})

\displaystyle O_{2}(\frac{1}{N})s^{N}(1+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}))e^{-f}\,d\nu d\nu_{\mathbb{S}^{N}}=

\displaystyle=\frac{(2N)^{n/2+1}}{2-m}\frac{1}{N^{2}}(2N\lambda)^{1/2-n/2}O_{2}(1)=O_{2}(\frac{1}{N^{3/2}}),

as wished.
∎

We now proceed to simplify expression (
4.17
). From this point onward, set
\bar{s}=\sqrt{2N\lambda_{0}}
, where
\lambda_{0}<\lambda
.

Proposition 4.6
.

The following equality holds,

3[A_{N}]+[B_{N}]+[C_{N}]+[D_{N}]=-[A_{N}]+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{3/2}}).

(4.27)

Proof.

We first show that
[B_{N}]
and
[C_{N}]
decay exponentially fast. In order to control
[B_{N}]
, observe that
\hat{\nabla}_{\hat{\rm n}}(|\hat{\nabla}b^{2}|^{2})=\hat{g}(\hat{\nabla}|\hat{\nabla}b^{2}|^{2},\hat{n})
. Now, we write
|\hat{\nabla}b^{2}|^{2}=4b^{2}|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}
and use (
3.1
) to obtain,

\hat{\nabla}|\hat{\nabla}b^{2}|^{2}=8b|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}\hat{\nabla}b+4b^{2}\hat{\nabla}\left(O_{2}(\frac{1}{N})\right).

Using that
\partial_{r}=r/N\partial_{\tau}
and
b=O_{2}(N^{1/2})
, we get,

\hat{\nabla}|\hat{\nabla}b^{2}|^{2}=O_{1}(N^{1/2})\partial_{r}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}O_{1}(N^{1/2})\partial_{x^{i}}.

Since by (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) we have
\hat{n}=O_{2}(1)\partial_{r}+O_{2}(N^{-1})\nabla f
, we see,

\lvert\hat{\nabla}_{\hat{\rm n}}(|\hat{\nabla}b^{2}|^{2})\rvert=O_{1}(N^{1/2}).

We integrate using (
3.7
) to find,

[B_{N}]=\frac{c_{N}}{4s^{m+1}}\int_{b=\bar{s}}O_{1}(N^{1/2})d\hat{A}=O_{1}(N)\left(\frac{\bar{s}}{s}\right)^{N}=O_{1}(N)\left(\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda}\right)^{N/2},

and since
\lambda_{0}<\lambda
, it decays exponentially fast.

In order to control
[C_{N}]
, we observe that since
(\hat{\Delta}b^{2})\hat{\nabla}_{\hat{\rm n}}b^{2}=(\hat{\Delta}b^{2})2b|\hat{\nabla}b|
, we can combine this with (
4.16
) and (
3.3
) to show that,

(\hat{\Delta}b^{2})\hat{\nabla}_{\hat{\rm n}}b^{2}=O_{2}(N^{3/2}),

and now a similar computation as the one performed for
[B_{N}]
shows that
[C_{N}]
also decays exponentially fast.

For
[D_{N}]
we proceed as follows. By the coarea formula,

\begin{split}\frac{c_{N}}{2s^{m+1}}\int_{\bar{s}\leq b\leq s}\frac{8m}{2-m}|&\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}b^{m}\hat{\Delta}h\,d\hat{V}\\
&=\frac{1}{s^{m+1}}\frac{4m}{2-m}\int_{\bar{s}}^{s}w^{m}\left(c_{N}\int_{b=w}|\hat{\nabla}b|\hat{\Delta}h\,d\hat{A}\right)dw.\end{split}

(4.28)

Then, perform a Taylor expansion on the term multiplying
w^{m}
around
s
to obtain,

\begin{split}c_{N}&\int_{b=w}|\hat{\nabla}b|\hat{\Delta}h\,d\hat{A}\\
&=c_{N}\int_{b=s}|\hat{\nabla}b|\hat{\Delta}h\,d\hat{A}+(2-m)(w-s)\frac{d}{ds}\left(\frac{c_{N}}{(2-m)}\int_{b=\xi_{w,N}}|\hat{\nabla}b|\hat{\Delta}h\,d\hat{A}\right),\end{split}

(4.29)

for some
\xi_{w,N}\in[w,s]
(in particular,
\xi_{w,N}\in[\bar{s},s]
), and observe that the term inside parentheses is
[A_{N}]
evaluated at
s=\xi_{w,N}.
Using (
3.3
) and (
4.6
), we see that
|\hat{\nabla}b|\hat{\Delta}h=r^{2-m}O_{2}(1/N)
.
Then, by (
3.10
) and (
3.7
),

\begin{split}[A_{N}]=\frac{c_{N}}{2-m}\int_{b=s}|\hat{\nabla}b|\hat{\Delta}hd\hat{A}&=\frac{(2N)^{n/2}}{2(4\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{M}s^{2-m+N}e^{-f}O_{2}(\frac{1}{N})(1+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}))d\nu\\
&=O_{2}(\frac{1}{N^{1/2}}).\end{split}

(4.30)

We use this to compute,

\frac{d}{ds}[A_{N}]=\frac{d}{ds}\left(\frac{c_{N}}{2-m}\int_{b=s}|\hat{\nabla}b|\hat{\Delta}hd\hat{A}\right)=\frac{d\lambda}{ds}\frac{d}{d\lambda}\left(O_{2}(\frac{1}{N^{1/2}})\right)=O_{1}(\frac{1}{N}),

(4.31)

and combine it with (
4.29
) to find,

c_{N}\int_{b=w}|\hat{\nabla}b|\hat{\Delta}h\,d\hat{A}=c_{N}\int_{b=s}|\hat{\nabla}b|\hat{\Delta}h\,d\hat{A}+(w-s)O_{1}(1).

Using this expression together with (
4.28
), we can proceed as in (
3.22
) and (
3.24
) to show,

\frac{c_{N}}{2s^{m+1}}\int_{\bar{s}\leq b\leq s}\frac{8m}{2-m}|\hat{\nabla}b|^{2}b^{m}\hat{\Delta}h\,d\hat{V}=4[A_{N}]+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{3/2}}).

(4.32)

In order to control the remaining term, we write

\begin{split}\frac{c_{N}}{2s^{m+1}}\int_{\bar{s}\leq b\leq s}&\frac{4b^{2m}}{(2-m)^{2}}(\hat{\Delta}h)^{2}\,d\hat{V}\\
&=\frac{2}{s^{m+1}}\int_{\bar{s}}^{s}w^{m}\left(\frac{c_{N}}{(2-m)^{2}}\int_{b=w}w^{m}\frac{(\hat{\Delta}h)^{2}}{|\hat{\nabla}b|}\,d\hat{A}\right)dw,\end{split}

and using (
4.6
), (
3.3
), (
3.10
) and (
3.7
), we notice that the term in parentheses is of order
O_{2}(N^{-3/2}).
Therefore, we proceed as in (
3.22
) and (
3.24
) to show,

\begin{split}\frac{c_{N}}{2s^{m+1}}\int_{\bar{s}\leq b\leq s}\frac{4b^{2m}}{(2-m)^{2}}(\hat{\Delta}h)^{2}\,d\hat{V}&=\frac{1}{m+1}O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{3/2}})\\
&=O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{5/2}}).\end{split}

(4.33)

Combining (
4.32
) and (
4.33
), it follows that

[D_{N}]=-4[A_{N}]+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{3/2}}).

(4.34)

Given that
[B_{N}]
and
[C_{N}]
decay exponentially fast, substituting (
4.34
) into the l.h.s. of (
4.27
) completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

We have,

\frac{2(m-1)}{s}(\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}-m\overline{\mathcal{V}}_{N})=2\frac{d}{ds}\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}(s)+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{3/2}}).

(4.35)

Proof.

We use the coarea formula to write,

\overline{\mathcal{V}}_{N}=\frac{c_{N}}{s^{m}}\int_{\bar{s}\leq b\leq s}|\hat{\nabla}b|^{4}\,d\hat{V}=\frac{1}{s^{m}}\int_{\bar{s}}^{s}w^{m-1}\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}(w)dw,

and perform a Taylor expansion of
\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}
around
s
, to obtain,

\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}(w)=\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}(s)+(w-s)\frac{d}{ds}\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}(s)+\frac{(w-s)^{2}}{2}\frac{d^{2}\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}}{ds^{2}}(\xi_{w,N}),

(4.36)

for some
\xi_{w,N}\in[w,s].
In order to control the second derivative, we notice that

\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}=\mathcal{A}_{N}+\frac{c_{N}}{s^{m-1}}\int_{b=s}|\hat{\nabla}b|d\hat{A}.

(4.37)

By (
3.20
) and (
3.21
), we have
d\mathcal{A}_{N}/ds=O_{1}(N^{-1/2})
and
d^{2}\mathcal{A}_{N}/ds^{2}=O_{0}(N^{-1})
, respectively.
Using Proposition
4.5
, we compute the second derivative of the second term on the r.h.s of (
4.37
) as,

\begin{split}\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}\left(\frac{c_{N}}{s^{m-1}}\int_{b=s}|\hat{\nabla}b|d\hat{A}\right)&=\frac{d}{ds}[A_{N}]+\frac{d\lambda}{ds}\frac{d}{d\lambda}\left(O_{2}(\frac{1}{N^{3/2}})\right)\\
&=O_{1}(\frac{1}{N}),\end{split}

(4.38)

since
d[A_{N}]/ds
was already computed in (
4.31
).
This shows,

\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}=O_{0}(\frac{1}{N}),

and therefore,

\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}(w)=\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}(s)+(w-s)\frac{d}{ds}\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}(s)+\frac{(w-s)^{2}}{2}O_{0}(\frac{1}{N}).

Now integrate using the bound
|O_{0}(1/N)|\leq C/N
for some
C>0
and proceed as in (
3.22
) and (

\frac{1}{s^{m}}\int_{\bar{s}}^{s}w^{m-1}\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}(w)dw=\frac{1}{m}\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}(s)-\frac{s}{m(m+1)}\frac{d}{ds}\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}(s)+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{3}}),

where we have absorbed the term decaying exponentially fast into

We finish the proof by expanding the l.h.s. of (

\displaystyle\frac{2(m-1)}{s}(\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}-m\overline{\mathcal{V}}_{N})

\displaystyle=\frac{2(m-1)}{s}\left(\frac{s}{(m+1)}\frac{d}{ds}\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}(s)+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{2}})\right)

\displaystyle=2\frac{d}{ds}\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{N}(s)+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{3/2}}).

∎

In particular,

\begin{split}\frac{d}{ds}\big(&2(m-1)\mathcal{V}_{N}-\mathcal{A}_{N}\big)(s)\\

(4.40)

Proof.

The integrand in (

Proposition 4.9
.

The following equality holds,

\left|\hat{\nabla}\hat{\nabla}b^{2}-\frac{\hat{\Delta}b^{2}}{m}\hat{g}\right|^{2}+\hat{\Ric}(\hat{\nabla}b^{2},\hat{\nabla}b^{2})=\frac{4b^{4}}{(2-m)^{2}}\left|\nabla\nabla f+\Ric-\frac{1}{2\tau}g\right|^{2}+O_{1}(\frac{1}{N}).

(4.41)

Proof.

Computing the partial derivatives of
b^{2}
, we see

\displaystyle\partial_{i}b^{2}=-\frac{2b^{2}}{2-m}\partial_{i}f,\quad\partial_{r}b^{2}=\frac{2b^{2}}{r}+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N^{1/2}}),\quad\partial_{i}\partial_{j}b^{2}=-\frac{2b^{2}}{2-m}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}f+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}).

Using (
4.16
) and the fact that
b^{2}/(2-m)=-2\tau+O_{2}(1/N)
we write,

\frac{\hat{\Delta}b^{2}}{m}\hat{g}=-\frac{2b^{2}}{2-m}\left(\frac{1}{2\tau}+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N})\right)\hat{g}.

To calculate the components of
\hat{\nabla}\hat{\nabla}b^{2}
, we can refer to the Christoffel symbols listed in Section 3.1 of
[
4
]
.
A meticulous yet straightforward inspection shows that the only contributions of order
O(1)
to the squared norm of

S:=\hat{\nabla}\hat{\nabla}b^{2}-\frac{\hat{\Delta}b^{2}}{m}\hat{g},

arise from the
\{ij\}
indices during the norm computation. The relevant Christoffel symbols are,

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}^{r}_{ij}=-\frac{r}{N}g^{rr}R_{ij},\quad\hat{\Gamma}^{k}_{ij}=\Gamma_{ik}^{k},\quad\hat{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{ij}=0.

(4.42)

We can then show,

-\hat{\Gamma}^{r}_{ij}\partial_{r}b^{2}=\frac{2b^{2}}{N}R_{ij}+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N})=-\frac{2b^{2}}{2-m}R_{ij}+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}),

and therefore,

\displaystyle S_{ij}

\displaystyle=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}b^{2}-\hat{\Gamma}^{k}_{ij}\partial_{k}b^{2}-\hat{\Gamma}^{r}_{ij}\partial_{r}b^{2}-\frac{\hat{\Delta}b^{2}}{m}\hat{g}_{ij}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2b^{2}}{2-m}\left(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}f+R_{ij}-\frac{1}{2\tau}g_{ij}\right)+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}).

Given that
v=1-2\tau R/N+O_{2}(1/N^{2}),
it is straightforward to verify that, after crucial cancellations in
S_{\alpha\beta}
and
S_{rr}
between
\hat{\nabla}\hat{\nabla}b^{2}
and
\hat{\Delta}b^{2}\hat{g}/m
, the remaining components of
S
are,

\displaystyle S_{\alpha\beta}=O_{2}(1),\quad S_{rr}=O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}),\quad S_{ir}=O_{2}(\frac{1}{N^{1/2}}),\quad S_{i\alpha}=S_{r\alpha}=0,

from where we compute,

\displaystyle\left|\hat{\nabla}\hat{\nabla}b^{2}-\frac{\hat{\Delta}b^{2}}{m}\hat{g}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\hat{g}^{ik}\hat{g}^{jl}S_{ij}S_{kl}+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N})

(4.43)

\displaystyle=\frac{4b^{4}}{(2-m)^{2}}\left|\nabla\nabla f+\Ric-\frac{1}{2\tau}g\right|^{2}+O_{2}(\frac{1}{N}).

(4.44)

Now, using the coordinate expression for
\hat{\Ric}
in terms of the Christoffel symbols, a straightforward computation (as performed in Corollary 3.1.2 of
[
4
]
) reveals that its components with respect to the
(\tau,\theta,x)
-coordinates are of order
O_{1}(1/N).
Using (
3.4
) and
\partial_{r}=(r/N)\partial_{\tau}
, we show
2b\hat{\nabla}b=O_{2}(1)\partial_{\tau}+O_{2}(1)\nabla f
. From this, it follows that

\hat{\Ric}(\hat{\nabla}b^{2},\hat{\nabla}b^{2})=O_{1}(\frac{1}{N}).

(4.45)

∎

Finally, we reproduce Perelman’s formula for the derivative of the
\mathcal{W}
-functional as the limit of the derivatives of
\mathcal{W}_{N}

Theorem 4.10
.

The derivative of Perelman’s
\mathcal{W}-
functional is,

\frac{d\mathcal{W}}{d\lambda}(\lambda)=-\int_{M}2\lambda\left(\left|\nabla\nabla f+\Ric-\frac{1}{2\tau}g\right|^{2}\right)(4\pi\lambda)^{-n/2}e^{-f}d\nu.

(4.46)

Proof.

Define

F_{N}:=\frac{c_{N}}{s^{N+4}}\int_{b=s}\left(\left|\hat{\nabla}\hat{\nabla}b^{2}-\frac{\hat{\Delta}b^{2}}{m}\hat{g}\right|^{2}+\hat{\Ric}(\hat{\nabla}b^{2},\hat{\nabla}b^{2})\right)\frac{d\hat{A}}{|\hat{\nabla}b|}.

(4.47)

Then, using (
3.7
) and (
4.41
), it is straightforward to show that

F_{N}=4(4\pi)^{-n/2}(2N)^{n/2-1}\left(\int_{M}\left|\nabla\nabla f+\Ric-\frac{1}{2\tau}g\right|^{2}e^{-f}d\nu+O_{1}(\frac{1}{N})\right).

(4.48)

It follows that
F_{N}=(2N)^{n/2-1}O_{1}(1)
and therefore,

\frac{d}{ds}F_{N}=\frac{d\lambda}{ds}\frac{d}{d\lambda}\left((2N)^{n/2-1}O_{1}(1)\right)=(2N)^{n/2-3/2}O_{0}(1).

(4.49)

Then, a Taylor expansion of order one centered at
s=\sqrt{2N\lambda}
shows,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}

\displaystyle\big(2(m-1)\mathcal{V}_{N}-\mathcal{A}_{N}\big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{c_{N}}{2s^{m+1}}\int_{\bar{s}\leq b\leq s}\left(\left|\hat{\nabla}\hat{\nabla}b^{2}-\frac{\hat{\Delta}b^{2}}{m}\hat{g}\right|^{2}+\hat{\Ric}(\hat{\nabla}b^{2},\hat{\nabla}b^{2})\right)d\hat{V}+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{3/2}})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2s^{m+1}}\int_{\bar{s}}^{s}w^{N+4}F_{N}(w)dw+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{3/2}})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2s^{m+1}}\int_{\bar{s}}^{s}w^{N+4}\left(F_{N}(s)+(w-s)\frac{d}{ds}F_{N}(\xi_{w,s})\right)dw+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{3/2}})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2s^{m+1}}\int_{\bar{s}}^{s}w^{N+4}\left(F_{N}(s)+(w-s)(2N)^{n/2-3/2}O_{0}(1)\right)dw+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{3/2}}).

Integrating, bounding the integral of the
O_{0}(1)
term by above and below as in (
3.22
), and absorbing the terms that decay exponentially fast into
O_{0}(N^{-3/2})
, we find,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\big(2(m-1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{N}-\mathcal{A}_{N}\big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{s^{3-n}}{2(N+5)}F_{N}+\frac{s^{4-n}}{(N+6)(N+5)}(2N)^{n/2-3/2}O_{0}(1)+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{3/2}})

\displaystyle=-\frac{s^{3-n}}{2(N+5)}F_{N}+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{3/2}}).

Finally, since

\frac{d\mathcal{W}_{N}}{d\lambda}=\frac{d}{d\lambda}\big(2(m-1)\mathcal{V}_{N}-\mathcal{A}_{N}\big)=\frac{ds}{d\lambda}\frac{d}{ds}\big(2(m-1)\mathcal{V}_{N}-\mathcal{A}_{N}\big),

we use expression (
4.48
) and
s=\sqrt{2N\lambda}
to obtain,

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{W}_{N}}{d\lambda}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{N}{2\lambda}\right)^{1/2}\left(-\frac{(\sqrt{2N\lambda})^{3-n}}{2(N+5)}F_{N}+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{3/2}})\right)+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N^{3/2}})

\displaystyle=-2(4\pi)^{-n/2}(\sqrt{2N\lambda})^{2-n}(2N)^{n/2-1}\left(\int_{M}\left|\nabla\nabla f+\Ric-\frac{1}{2\tau}g\right|^{2}e^{-f}d\nu+O_{1}(\frac{1}{N})\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N})

\displaystyle=-2(4\pi)^{-n/2}\lambda^{1-n/2}\left(\int_{M}\left|\nabla\nabla f+\Ric-\frac{1}{2\tau}g\right|^{2}e^{-f}d\nu+O_{1}(\frac{1}{N})\right)+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N})

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}2\lambda\left(\left|\nabla\nabla f+\Ric-\frac{1}{2\lambda}g\right|^{2}\right)(4\pi\lambda)^{-n/2}e^{-f}d\nu+O_{0}(\frac{1}{N}).

By Corollary
3.6
,

\frac{d\mathcal{W}}{d\lambda}(\lambda)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{d\mathcal{W}_{N}}{d\lambda}(\lambda),

which finishes the proof.
∎

A notion of BSDE on the Wasserstein space and its applications to control problems and PDEs The author thanks Dylan Possamaï for his helpful suggestions and interesting discussions.
Mao Fabrice Djete

Abstract
We introduce a class of backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs) on the Wasserstein space of probability measures. This formulation extends the classical correspondence between BSDEs, stochastic control, and partial differential equations (PDEs) to the mean–field (McKean–Vlasov) setting, where the dynamics depend on the law of the state process. The standard BSDE framework becomes inadequate in this context, motivating a new definition in terms of measure–dependent solutions. Under suitable assumptions, we demonstrate that this formulation is in correspondence with both mean–field control problems and partial differential equations defined on the Wasserstein space. A comparison principle is established to ensure uniqueness, and existence results are obtained for generators that are linear or quadratic in the z z –variable. This framework provides a probabilistic approach to control and analysis on the space of probability measures.

In this paper, we investigate the following central question: what is the appropriate extension of the classical BSDE pair
(Y,Z)
that captures the dual relationship with both mean–field control problems and PDEs over the Wasserstein space ?

We argue that the classical formulation of BSDEs is inadequate to capture the duality structure in the mean–field setting. A key limitation arises from the shift in the nature of the state variable: whereas in classical stochastic control the primary process is the controlled state trajectory
(X^{\alpha}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, it is now well understood that, in mean–field control problems, the central object is the law of the state process
(\mu^{\alpha}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
. In the classical framework, the solution process
(Y_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
of the BSDE represents the dynamic version of the value function, that is,
Y_{t}=V(t,X^{\alpha^{\star}}_{t})
along the optimal trajectory. By analogy, in the mean–field setting one would expect
Y_{t}=V(t,\mu^{\alpha^{\star}}_{t})
implying that
(Y_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is a deterministic process.

However, this leads to a contradiction. If
Y
is deterministic, then the martingale component
Z
must vanish. Yet, as previously discussed ( see (
1.2
) ), the
Z
process plays a critical role in the construction of the optimal control. Thus, a deterministic
Y
process with
Z=0
fails to capture the necessary information structure required for optimality.

This inconsistency motivates the introduction of a new class of BSDEs, specifically designed to incorporate the distributional dependence inherent to mean–field control problems. Such a formulation aims to restore the correspondence between BSDEs, the value function, and the associated PDE on the Wasserstein space.

In light of the observation that the primary object of interest is the probability distribution, a notion of a BSDE on the space of probability measures (the Wasserstein space) is introduced. In this framework, the process
Y
is defined on
[0,T]\times{\cal P}(\mathbb{R})
. Furthermore, drawing on the concept of the linear functional derivative in the space of probability measures, the process
Z
is defined on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times{\cal P}(\mathbb{R})
. The precise definition is provided in
Definition

2.1
.

Under appropriate regularity and structural assumptions, we demonstrate that this generalized BSDE maintains a duality with the mean–field (McKean–Vlasov) control problem analogous to the well–established correspondence between classical BSDEs and standard stochastic control problems. Moreover, we establish a comparison principle for this new class of BSDEs, which in turn enables us to link their solutions to partial differential equations defined over the Wasserstein space, mirroring the classical connection between BSDEs and PDEs in finite–dimensional settings.

This perspective offers a unified approach to the study of mean–field control and PDEs over the Wasserstein space, potentially enriching the available tools for analyzing such problems. In the case of generators that are linear or quadratic in the
z
–variable, we are able to establish existence results for solutions of the proposed BSDEs, which in turn provide solutions to the associated mean–field control problems and PDEs on the space of probability measures.

Although a general existence theory remains beyond the scope of this current work, this limitation is consistent with the known challenges in solving mean–field control problems and PDEs in the Wasserstein space. Nevertheless, the proposed formulation introduces a novel and coherent framework that opens new avenues for further research in this direction.

An informed reader may notice the absence of common noise in our framework. This omission is intentional, motivated by two considerations. First, the no–common noise setting already poses significant mathematical challenges and has broad applicability. Second, the inclusion of common noise, with the techniques presently available to us, would necessitate a non–degenerate structure, thereby excluding the class of problems that arise uniquely in its absence i.e no–common noise case. We plan to investigate this direction in future work.

2.2

•

•

2.4.2
The case of quadratic generator

It is well known in the classical theory of BSDEs that quadratic generators can be treated under suitable conditions. Although we are not yet able to address the case of general Lipschitz generators in the W–BSDE framework, we are able to handle the specific case of a quadratic generator, namely

(i)

\displaystyle F\left({\cal L}\left(X^{t,U_{t}}_{T}\right)\right)=F\left({\cal L}(U_{T})\right)+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T}\delta_{m}F\left({\cal L}\left(X^{r,U_{r}}_{T}\right)\right)\left(X^{r,U_{r}}_{T}\right)\frac{\partial_{x}p(T,X^{r,U_{r}}_{T},r,U_{r})}{p(T,X^{r,U_{r}}_{T},r,U_{r})}\left(\overline{B}(r,U_{r})-B(r,U_{r})\right)\mathrm{d}r\right].

(A.3)

Proof.

In that case, we can check that
\overline{J}^{t,U_{t},\overline{U}_{t}}_{s}=0
a.e. for all
s\in[0,T]
. Indeed, since there is no measure dependence, the equation satisfied by
\left(\overline{J}^{t,U_{t},\overline{U}_{t}}_{s}\right)_{s\in[0,T]}
is: for each
s\in[0,t]
,
\overline{J}^{t,U_{t},\overline{U}_{t}}_{s}=0
and for each
s\in[t,T],

\;\mathrm{d}\overline{J}^{t,U_{t},\overline{U}_{t}}_{s}=\overline{J}^{t,U_{t},\overline{U}_{t}}_{s}\overline{B}^{\prime}\left(s,\overline{X}^{t,\overline{U}_{t}}_{s},\nu_{s}\right)\mathrm{d}s
. The unique solution is
\overline{J}^{t,U_{t},\overline{U}_{t}}_{s}=0
a.e. for all
s\in[0,T]
. Consequently, (
A.3
) is true with the assumption of
Proposition

A.1
. For each
\varepsilon>0
, we set
F_{\varepsilon}(m)=F({\cal L}(\varepsilon W_{T}+S))
where
S
is a random variable independent of
W
with
{\cal L}(S)=m
. By simple computations, we get
\delta_{m}F_{\varepsilon}(m)(x)=\mathbb{E}\left[\delta_{m}F({\cal L}(\varepsilon W_{T}+S))(\varepsilon W_{T}+x)\right]
and
\partial_{x}\delta_{m}F_{\varepsilon}(m)(x)=\mathbb{E}\left[\delta_{m}F({\cal L}(\varepsilon W_{T}+S))(\varepsilon W_{T}+x)\frac{W_{T}}{\varepsilon T}\right]
. We verify that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(m)=F(m)
. Notice that, since
\partial_{x}p
is integrable (see
Remark

A.3
), we get

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\delta_{m}F_{\varepsilon}(m)\left(y\right)\partial_{x}p(T,y,r,x)\mathrm{d}y-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\delta_{m}F_{\varepsilon}(m)\left(y\right)\partial_{x}p(T,y,r,x)\mathbf{1}_{|y|\leq K}\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\delta_{m}F_{\varepsilon}(m)\left(y\right)\partial_{x}p(T,y,r,x)\mathbf{1}_{|y|>K}\mathrm{d}y\right|\leq\sup_{(x,m)}|\delta_{m}F(m)(x)|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\partial_{x}p(T,y,r,x)\right|\mathbf{1}_{|y|>K}\mathrm{d}y\to_{K\to\infty}0.

By dominated convergence Theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{K\to\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\delta_{m}F_{\varepsilon}(m)\left(y\right)\partial_{x}p(T,y,r,x)\mathbf{1}_{|y|\leq K}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\lim_{K\to\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\delta_{m}F({\cal L}(\varepsilon W_{T}+S))\left(y\right)\partial_{x}p(T,y-\varepsilon W_{T},r,x)\mathbf{1}_{|y-\varepsilon W_{T}|\leq K}\mathrm{d}y\right]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\delta_{m}F(m)\left(y\right)\partial_{x}p(T,y,r,x)\mathrm{d}y.

Then,
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\delta_{m}F_{\varepsilon}(m)\left(y\right)\partial_{x}p(T,y,r,x)\mathrm{d}y=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\delta_{m}F(m)\left(y\right)\partial_{x}p(T,y,r,x)\mathrm{d}y
. Since
\partial_{x}\delta_{m}F_{\varepsilon}
is continuous and bounded, we can apply (
A.3
) for
F_{\varepsilon}
, by letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we deduce the result for
F
.
∎

Remark A.3
.

By
Matiichuk and Èidel’man 1965

[

and
Porper and Èidel’man 1992

[

]

(
see also

[

and

A.2
An existence result

We provide an existence result for an equation in which the unknown is a functional over the Wasserstein space of probability measures.

Let
\overline{f}:[0,T]\times{\cal P}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\overline{P}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be two bounded Borel measurable functions. We assume that the function
\overline{f}
is differentiable with respect to the variable
y
, with derivative denoted by
\overline{f}^{\prime}
, and admits a linear functional derivative with respect to the measure argument, denoted by
\delta_{m}\overline{f}
. Furthermore, the map

(t,m,y,z)\mapsto\left(\overline{f}(t,m,y),\overline{f}^{\prime}(t,m,y),\delta_{m}\overline{f}(t,m,y)(z)\right)

is bounded and Lipschitz continuous in the variable
y
, uniformly in
(t,m,z)
. We denote by
{\rm Lip}(h)
the Lipschitz constant of a function
h\in\{\overline{f},\overline{f}^{\prime},\delta_{m}\overline{f}\}
. The notation
|h|_{\infty}
will be used to refer to the uniform norm of
h
, regardless of the underlying domain.
Furthermore, we consider a continuous bounded function
\psi:{\cal P}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}
which admits a bounded linear functional derivative
\delta_{m}\psi
.

Given
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
(X^{t,x}_{s})_{s\in[0,T]}
the process verifying: for each
s\in[0,t]
,
X^{t,x}_{s}=x
and, for any
s\in[t,T]
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X^{t,x}_{s}=\overline{P}(s,X^{t,x}_{s})\mathrm{d}s+\mathrm{d}W_{s}

where
W
is an
\mathbb{R}
–valued
\mathbb{F}
–Brownian motion.
For any
m\in{\cal P}(\mathbb{R})
, we denote by
U
the random variable independent of
W
s.t.
{\cal L}(U)=m
.

Proposition A.4
.

There exists a continuous bounded map
Y:[0,T]\times{\cal P}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}
verifying: for any
(t,m),

\displaystyle Y(t,m)=\psi\left({\cal L}(X^{t,U}_{T})\right)+\int_{t}^{T}\overline{f}\left(r,{\cal L}(X^{t,U}_{r}),Y\left(r,{\cal L}(X^{t,U}_{r})\right)\;\right)\;\mathrm{d}r.

In addition, if
T
is small enough i.e.

\displaystyle T(C+|\overline{f}^{\prime}|_{\infty})<1\;\mbox{and}\;T\;{\rm Lip}(\delta_{m}\overline{f})+T\;L_{T}{\rm Lip}(\overline{f}^{\prime})+T\;|\overline{f}^{\prime}|_{\infty}+T\;{\rm Lip}(\overline{f})<1

where
\frac{|\psi|_{\infty}+TC}{1-TC}+\frac{||\delta_{m}\psi|_{\infty}|+T|\delta_{m}\overline{f}|_{\infty}}{1-T|\overline{f}^{\prime}|_{\infty}}=L_{T}
then for each
t
, the map
m\mapsto Y(t,m)
admits a linear functional derivative
\delta_{m}Y
verifying
\sup_{(t,x,m)}|\delta_{m}Y(t,m)(x)|<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha>0
. Let us start by introducing the set
{\cal R}_{\alpha}
. We will say that a Borel map
y:[0,T]\times{\cal P}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}
belongs to
{\cal R}_{\alpha}
if
\|y\|_{\alpha}:=\int_{0}^{T}e^{\alpha t}\sup_{m}|y(t,m)|\mathrm{d}t<\infty
. We introduce the map
\Gamma:{\cal R}_{\alpha}\to{\cal R}_{\alpha}
by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{y}(t,m):=\psi\left({\cal L}(X^{t,U}_{T})\right)+\int_{t}^{T}\overline{f}\left(r,{\cal L}(X^{t,U}_{r}),y\left(r,{\cal L}(X^{t,U}_{r})\right)\;\right)\;\mathrm{d}r.

Notice that, since
\psi
and
\overline{f}
are bounded,
\Gamma
is well–defined i.e. for any
y\in{\cal R}_{\alpha}
,
\Gamma_{y}\in{\cal R}_{\alpha}
. There exists
C>0
(independent of
\alpha
), for any
(y,y^{\prime})
,

\displaystyle\left|\Gamma_{y}-\Gamma_{y^{\prime}}\right|(t,m)=\left|\int_{t}^{T}\overline{f}\left(r,{\cal L}(X^{t,U}_{r}),y\left(r,{\cal L}(X^{t,U}_{r})\right)\;\right)-\overline{f}\left(r,{\cal L}(X^{t,U}_{r}),y^{\prime}\left(r,{\cal L}(X^{t,U}_{r})\right)\;\right)\;\mathrm{d}r\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{t}^{T}\left|y\left(r,{\cal L}(X^{t,U}_{r})\right)-y^{\prime}\left(r,{\cal L}(X^{t,U}_{r})\;\right)\right|\;\mathrm{d}r\leq C\int_{t}^{T}\sup_{m}\left|y\left(r,m\right)-y^{\prime}\left(r,m\right)\right|\;\mathrm{d}r.

Then, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{y}-\Gamma_{y^{\prime}}\|_{\alpha}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}e^{\alpha t}\int_{t}^{T}\sup_{m}\left|y\left(r,m\right)-y^{\prime}\left(r,m\right)\right|\;\mathrm{d}r\;\mathrm{d}t\leq\frac{C}{\alpha}\|y-y^{\prime}\|_{\alpha}.

Also, by similar way, we get

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{y}\|_{\alpha}\leq|\psi|_{\infty}\frac{e^{\alpha T}}{\alpha}+\frac{C}{\alpha}\left(e^{\alpha T}+\|y\|_{\alpha}\right).

Let
\alpha>C
and
L\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
s.t.
L>\frac{e^{\alpha T}\left(|\psi|_{\infty}+C\right)}{\alpha-C}
. We can apply the Banach fixed point Theorem and deduce that there exists
Y\in{\cal R}_{\alpha}
s.t.
\|Y\|_{\alpha}\leq L
and
\Gamma_{Y}=Y
. In addition, with
Y^{0}=0
, by defining
Y^{n+1}=\Gamma_{Y^{n}}
for any
n\geq 0
, we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|Y^{n}-Y\|_{\alpha}=0
. This allows us to verify the continuity of
Y
.

Let us observe that, if
y
admits a bounded linear functional derivative
\delta_{m}y
, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{m}\Gamma_{y}(t,m)(x)=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\delta_{m}\psi\left({\cal L}(X^{t,U}_{T})\right)\left(X^{t,x}_{T}\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\delta_{m}\overline{f}\left(r,{\cal L}(X^{t,U}_{r}),y(r,{\cal L}(X^{t,U}_{r}))\right)(X^{t,x}_{r})\;\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\overline{f}^{\prime}\left(r,{\cal L}(X^{t,U}_{r}),y(r,{\cal L}(X^{t,U}_{r}))\right)\delta_{m}y(r,{\cal L}(X^{t,U}_{r}))(X^{t,x}_{r})\;\mathrm{d}r\bigg].

We can check that
|\delta_{m}\Gamma_{y}|_{\infty}\leq|\delta_{m}\psi|_{\infty}+T\;|\delta_{m}\overline{f}|_{\infty}+T\;|\overline{f}^{\prime}|_{\infty}|\delta_{m}y|_{\infty}
and also,
|\Gamma_{y}|_{\infty}\leq|\psi|_{\infty}+T\;C\left(1+|y|_{\infty}\right)
.
For another
y^{\prime}
admitting a bounded linear functional derivative,

\displaystyle\left|\delta_{m}\left(\Gamma_{y}-\Gamma_{y^{\prime}}\right)\right|_{\infty}\leq T\;{\rm Lip}(\delta_{m}\overline{f})|y-y^{\prime}|_{\infty}+T\;|\delta_{m}y|_{\infty}{\rm Lip}(\overline{f}^{\prime})|y-y^{\prime}|_{\infty}+T\;|\overline{f}^{\prime}|_{\infty}|\delta_{m}\left(y-y^{\prime}\right)|_{\infty}

and
|\Gamma_{y}-\Gamma_{y^{\prime}}|_{\infty}\leq T\;{\rm Lip}(\overline{f})|y-y^{\prime}|_{\infty}
. Let
T
be s.t.
T(C+|\overline{f}^{\prime}|_{\infty})<1
and
L_{T}>0
be s.t.
\frac{|\psi|_{\infty}+TC}{1-TC}+\frac{||\delta_{m}\psi|_{\infty}|+T|\delta_{m}\overline{f}|_{\infty}}{1-T|\overline{f}^{\prime}|_{\infty}}\leq L_{T}
. If
|\delta_{m}y|_{\infty}+|y|_{\infty}\leq L_{T}
then
|\delta_{m}\Gamma_{y}|_{\infty}+|\Gamma_{y}|_{\infty}\leq L_{T}
. In addition, if
T\;{\rm Lip}(\delta_{m}\overline{f})+T\;L_{T}{\rm Lip}(\overline{f}^{\prime})+T\;|\overline{f}^{\prime}|_{\infty}+T\;{\rm Lip}(\overline{f})<1
then the map
\Gamma
is a contraction in
{\cal S}:=\left\{y:\;|y|_{\infty}+|\delta_{m}y|_{\infty}\leq L_{T}\right\}
for the norm
\|y\|_{\infty}:=|y|_{\infty}+|\delta_{m}y|_{\infty}
. By Banach fixed point theorem there exists
Y\in{\cal S}
verifying
\Gamma_{Y}=Y
and
\delta_{m}\Gamma_{Y}=\delta_{m}Y
.
∎

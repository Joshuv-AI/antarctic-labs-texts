Efficacy and safety of dual blockade of HER2 and PD-1 in patients with HER2-positive gastric cancer: a retrospective, multicentre study
Authors: Shuyi Cen, Meiqin Yuan, Qunan Sun, Guilan Hou, Jieer Ying, Qi Xu, Yu Zheng, Ying Dong, Hongming Pan, Weidong Han
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractHuman epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) expression is one of the most important pathological characteristics of gastric cancer. The positive rate of HER2 expression in patients with gastric cancer is approximately 20%. The phase III Keynote-811 study revealed that anti-HER2 and anti-PD-1 therapy combined with chemotherapy could significantly improve the objective response rate as first-line treatment in patients with HER2-positive advanced gastric cancer. In the present study, we aimed to evaluate the efficacy of combination therapy with trastuzumab and PD-1 inhibitors in patients with advanced HER2-positive gastric cancer in a real-world setting. Seventy-two HER2-positive gastric cancer patients from three hospitals in China were retrospectively reviewed. These patients were treated with trastuzumab plus one anti-PD-1 agent with or without chemotherapy. The overall response rate, progression-free survival and overall survival were assessed according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumours (RECIST 1.1). From January 2018 to October 2021, 72 patients with HER2-positive gastric cancer received trastuzumab and a PD-1 inhibitor with or without chemotherapy as neoadjuvant chemotherapy, first-line therapy, second-line therapy or salvage therapy. The ORR was 54.2% for all patients and 79.4% for previously untreated patients. The median PFS and median OS were 10 months (95% CI: 8–13 months) and 26.1 months (95% CI: 18.5-NA months), respectively, for all patients. Grade 3 adverse effects occurred in approximately 25% of patients. Immune-related adverse effects occurred in approximately 12.5% of patients. Trastuzumab and PD-1 inhibitor combination therapy with or without chemotherapy achieved satisfactory survival outcomes in patients with HER2-positive gastric cancer with acceptable safety. Introduction

Gastric cancer is a tumour with high morbidity and mortality1. With the development of precision medicine, various classification methods have been widely used in recent decades, such as histological classification, Lauren classification, genotype classification and molecular classification2–4. Among them, the overexpression of molecular markers such as human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2, also known as ERBB2) is important for guiding clinical choices.

The HER2 gene is a proto-oncogene, and the HER2 protein belongs to the epidermal growth factor receptor family of four tyrosine kinases5,6. Through tyrosine kinase activation, proteins of the HER2 family can regulate the downstream MAPK and PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathways7. HER2 overexpression was first found in human breast cancer cell lines and was later confirmed to be closely associated with tumour development and cell migration in many tumours, including gastric cancer8–10. In previous studies, there have been controversies about whether HER2 overexpression is related to the overall survival (OS) of gastric cancer patients. In 2012, Jørgensen et al. conducted a meta-analysis that included 12,749 patients from 42 studies11. Researchers have reported that patients with gastric cancer with HER2 overexpression have a worse prognosis and are more likely to develop serous invasion, lymph node metastasis and distant metastasis11. However, studies from Europe, the United States and Japan have suggested that patients with HER2-positive gastric cancer may have a better prognosis than HER2-negative patients, especially after the implementation of HER2-targeted drugs12,13.

The introduction of the HER2 monoclonal antibody trastuzumab as a treatment for HER2-positive gastric cancer has greatly improved patient therapeutic effects and prolonged survival14. Trastuzumab is the most commonly used anti-HER2 monoclonal antibody. Trastuzumab causes internalization and degradation of HER2 proteins, inhibiting signal transduction pathways and tumour regression15. The addition of trastuzumab to chemotherapy significantly prolonged progression-free survival (PFS) and OS in patients with metastatic breast cancer, and the U.S. FDA approved trastuzumab for HER2-positive breast cancer in 199816,17. ToGA was one of the earliest clinical studies18 in which trastuzumab significantly improved the survival of patients with HER2-positive gastric cancer14. Since then, the expression status of HER2 has become an important biomarker for gastric cancer, and trastuzumab has been used as a routine agent for HER2-positive patients.

Immune checkpoint inhibitors have revolutionized cancer treatment in the past decade. PD-1 and PD-L1 play vital roles in immune suppression by regulating tumour escape and immune surveillance19. However, it was not until 2017 that the FDA approved the first programmed death-1 (PD-1) inhibitor, pembrolizumab, for use in patients with gastric cancer for salvage treatment based on the results of the Keynote-059 study20,21. In 2021, the Checkmate-649 study further proved that nivolumab with chemotherapy as first-line therapy could also prolong PFS and OS in patients with HER2-negative locally advanced or metastatic gastric cancer22.

Previous studies have shown that in HER2-enriched tumours, exposure to trastuzumab increases PD-1 and PD-L1 expression23,24. This increase in PD-L1 expression may be the mechanism of trastuzumab resistance24. An anti-PD-1 antibody substantially improved the therapeutic effect of trastuzumab in mouse models25,26. Based on these results, two single-arm phase II studies in HER2-positive gastric cancer patients analysed the treatment efficacy of the combination of an anti-PD-1 antibody and trastuzumab with chemotherapy27,28. These positive results led to the initiation of the phase III Keynote-811 study. In 2021, the Keynote-811 study announced its interim analysis, in which the addition of pembrolizumab to trastuzumab and chemotherapy significantly improved the objective response rate (ORR) in previously untreated patients with HER2-positive gastric cancer. Although the PFS and OS analyses are not yet mature, the U.S. FDA granted accelerated approval to pembrolizumab for HER2-positive gastric cancer29,30.

In the present study, we aimed to analyse the survival outcomes and adverse effects of the combination of trastuzumab and a PD-1 inhibitor with or without chemotherapy for HER2-positive gastric cancer patients.

Methods

Patient population

From January 2018 to October 2021, seventy-two HER2-positive gastric cancer patients who were treated with a combination of trastuzumab and PD-1 inhibitors from three institutions in China, including Zhejiang University School of Medicine affiliated with Sir Run Run Shaw Hospital, the Second Affiliated Hospital of Zhejiang University School of Medicine and Zhejiang Cancer Hospital, were retrospectively analysed. The study was approved by the Research Ethics Board of Zhejiang University School of Medicine affiliated with Sir Run Run Shaw Hospital (No. 20210727-33). This study received institutional review board approval with a waiver of informed consent. Demographic and clinical data were extracted from the patients’ clinical records. CT scans were reviewed and evaluated by at least two radiologists. All patients signed informed consent forms for off-label use of these treatments. The eligibility criteria were as follows: (1) patients were diagnosed with gastric cancer; (2) patients were diagnosed with HER2 positivity (HER2 status as determined by pathologists according to guidelines from ASCO, in which tumours with 3 + IHC or 2 + IHC staining plus a positive ISH result were defined as HER2-positive31); and (3) patients were treated with trastuzumab and one of the following anti-PD-1 agents concurrently with or without chemotherapy: sintilimab (Innovent Biologics, China), toripalimab (Shangha Merck & Co.), camrelizumab (Jiangsu Hengrui Medicine, China), nivolumab (Bristol-Myers Squibb, USA), pembrolizumab (Merck & Co., USA), or tislelizumab (BeiGene, China). There were no exclusion criteria.

Efficacy

The primary endpoint for this study was the ORR. The secondary endpoints included the DCR, median PFS, median OS and safety. Tumour imaging by computed tomography (preferred) or magnetic resonance imaging was evaluated according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumours (RECIST) version 1.1. PFS was defined as the period of time from the beginning of treatment to disease progression or patient death. OS was defined as the time from the beginning of treatment to patient death. Adverse events (AEs) were assessed according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE, version 5.0).

Statistical analysis

Clinicopathological features were assessed via descriptive statistics. Categorical variables are described with absolute frequencies or percentages, whereas continuous variables are described with medians and IQRs. Survival was assessed using the Kaplan–Meier method with the log-rank test. All the statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS software version 26.0 (SPSS, Chicago, IL) or R studio version 4.2.1 (http://www.R-project.org/). A P value < 0.05 was considered significant.

Patient characteristics.

The median age was 63.0 years, with 53 male (73.6%) and 19 female (26.4%) patients. Among them, 12 patients (16.7%) had a family history of malignant cancer, and 6 patients (8.3%) had more than two relatives with cancer.

Tumours occurred mainly in the gastric antrum (20 patients, 27.8%), cardia (18 patients, 25%) and gastric body (14 patients, 19.4%). Ulcer type and adenocarcinoma were the most common pathological types. Forty-five patients (62.5%) had already developed distant metastasis at the time of diagnosis, and the most frequent metastatic sites were the abdominal, liver and neck lymph nodes.

All of the patients received at least one line of dual blockade of HER2 and PD-1, and 8 patients received two lines of combination therapy. Dual-blockade therapy was administered mainly as a first-line treatment (32 patients) or second-line treatment (20 patients) and then as a neoadjuvant (8 patients) or third-line treatment (6 patients). The most frequently used chemotherapy regimens were S-1 + oxaliplatin (SOX) (20 patients, 20.5%), followed by albumin plus paclitaxel, docetaxel + S-1 and S-1 (8 patients in each group). Nine patients did not receive additional chemotherapy. The most commonly used PD-1 inhibitor was sintilimab (29 patients, 40.3%), followed by camrelizumab (15 patients, 20.8%), toripalimab (11 patients, 15.3%), tislelizumab (7 patients, 9.7%), nivolumab (6 patients, 8.3%) and pembrolizumab (4 patients, 5.6%).

Efficacy

After receiving dual blockade therapy, 59 patients were screened for efficacy. Two patients (3.4%) achieved a complete response (CR), 30 patients (50.8%) achieved a partial response (PR), 16 patients (27.1%) had stable disease (SD), and 11 patients (18.6%) had progressive disease (PD). In all patients, the ORR and disease control rate (DCR) were 54.2% and 81.4%, respectively.

The median duration of follow-up was 23.7 months (range 1.7 to 61.3 months), and the number of patients lost to follow-up was 9 out of 72 (12.5%). Table 2 shows the efficacy stratified by each line of treatment. For neoadjuvant chemotherapy, the ORR and DCR were 85.7% and 100%, respectively. In the first-line setting, 2 patients achieved CR (7.4%), 19 patients achieved PR (70.4%), and the ORR was 77.8%. In the second-line setting, the ORR and DCR were 18.8% and 62.5%, respectively. For third-line treatment, the ORR and DCR were 20% and 40%, respectively. For fourth-line treatment, the only evaluable patient achieved a PR. For all previously untreated patients who received treatment in the neoadjuvant setting or first-line setting, the CR rate, PR rate and PD rate were 5.9%, 73.5% and 2.9%, respectively. The overall ORR and DCR for untreated patients were 79.4% and 97.1%, respectively.

Among the 8 patients who received two lines of dual blockade therapy, four patients received the same regimen for both neoadjuvant and postoperative therapy. One patient experienced disease progression after 573 days, while the remaining patients showed no progression at 984, 1016, and 1156 days following the start of postoperative treatment. The other four patients changed their chemotherapy regimens after progressing on first-line dual-blockade therapy. Two patients progressed 40 days and 63 days after the change in chemotherapy. The other two patients were lost to follow-up.

Safety

A total of 18 patients (25%) had recorded grade 3 or 4 adverse effects. Table 3 lists the detailed adverse effects of these patients and the most likely related drugs. The most common AEs were observed in the blood and digestive system. Immune-related AEs, trastuzumab-related AEs and chemotherapy-related AEs accounted for approximately 12.5%, 2.8% and 12.5% of all cases, respectively. There were no treatment-related deaths.

Discussion

The results from this study indicate that dual blockade of HER2 and PD-1 has satisfactory clinical results for patients with HER2-positive gastric cancer in a real-world setting. Table 4 compares treatment efficacy between previously untreated HER2-positive gastric cancer patients from the present study and those from three other studies that used dual blockade of HER2 and PD-1 and chemotherapy as first-line treatment. In these four studies, the ORR and DCR ranged from 74.4 to 91.0% and from 96.2 to 100%, respectively. The median PFS ranged from 8.6 months to 13.0 months, and the 6-month PFS was greater than 75%27–29. Taken together, these studies demonstrated that dual blockade of HER2 and PD-1 has robust clinical benefits in patients with untreated HER2-positive gastric cancer.

In the previous three studies, grade 3 or 4 treatment-related AEs occurred in 56.8–74.4% of patients, whereas only 25% of patients in our study reported severe AEs. The incidence of immune-related AEs was similar across the four studies, ranging from 9.7 to 10.8%. In Rha’s PANTHERA study, grade 3 or higher treatment-related AEs, including neutropenia (39.5% of patients) and immune-related AEs (10% of patients), occurred in 74.4% of patients27. In Janjigian’s study, 18 patients (48.6%) experienced grade 3 treatment-related AEs, and 3 patients (8.1%) experienced grade 4 treatment-related AEs. Four patients (10.8%) experienced immune-related AEs. The other most common grade 3 or 4 AEs included lymphocytopenia (24%), electrolyte disturbances (16%), and anaemia (11%)28. In the Keynote-811 study, grade 3 or higher AEs were observed in 57.1% of patients, and immune-related AEs occurred in 9.7% of patients30. One of the most important reasons for the differences in AE occurrence between previous studies and the present study is the difference in chemotherapy regimens. Specifically, patients from the PANTHERA study received capecitabine/5-fluorouracil + oxaliplatin/cisplatin, patients from Janjigian’s study received capecitabine + cisplatin, and patients in our study received physicians’ choice of chemotherapy. Another reason is that our study retrospectively reviewed clinical records, and incomplete medical records may have contributed to missing information on AEs.

Unlike previous studies, our research investigated not only the efficacy of combination therapy as a first-line treatment but also the efficacy of second- and third-line treatments. Second, heterogeneous chemotherapies were administered to patients enrolled in this cohort. This situation is similar to that in real-world clinical practice. Third, we further analysed whether HER2 expression by IHC was associated with prognosis. We found that the differences in PFS and OS between patients who were HER2 2 + and those who were HER 3 + were not significant. Fourth, our study enrolled Chinese patients, whereas previous phase II studies included patients from Europe and the U.S.

The synergistic effect of immunotherapy and HER2-targeted therapy may be achieved through the following mechanisms. First, although trastuzumab activates immune responses and produces antitumour effects in the early stage of treatment, sustained immunity may not last long. Immunotherapy, on the other hand, can attract various immune cells and contribute to lasting antitumour effects32.

Second, the tumour microenvironment plays an important role in the immune modulation of gastric cancer during combination therapy. Compared with tumours with low HER2 expression, gastric tumours with high HER2 expression have lower proportions of stromal cells, lower proportions of immune cells and greater tumour purity33. These tumours also have lower expression of PD-1, PD-L1, CTLA-4, LAG-3, TIGIT and PD-L2 33. Trastuzumab has a substantial influence on the tumour microenvironment. It induces the production of antitumour cytotoxic T lymphocytes, the secretion of IFN-γ from NK cells, and antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC) by immune effector cells34,35. In gastric cancer cell lines, trastuzumab stimulates IFN-γ secretion to upregulate PD-L1 expression in HER2-amplified cells cocultured with NK cells or peripheral blood mononuclear cells24,36. Additionally, dual blockade of HER2 and PD-1 enhances the HER-2-specific T-cell response, leading to the activation of immune cells and the expansion of peripheral memory T cells32,37,38. Patients who responded well to trastuzumab had PD-1 signalling-enriched tumour biopsies39.

In HER2-negative gastric cancer, the Keynote-012, Keynote-059, Keynote-062 and Checkmate-649 studies revealed that a higher PD-L1 combined positive score (CPS) was associated with a better clinical outcome of combination therapy with a PD-1 inhibitor and chemotherapy20–22,40−43. However, both Janjigian et al. and Rha et al. reported that PD-L1 expression at baseline did not significantly correlate with outcome in HER2-positive patients after dual blockade of PD-1 and HER227,44. Researchers have also reported that the tumour mutation burden (TMB) is not associated with PFS27,43. However, owing to the small sample sizes of these cohorts, a definitive conclusion cannot be drawn. Therefore, researchers in the phase III Keynote-811 study stratified patients according to PD-L1 expression in both the global and Japanese cohorts. The retrospective nature of the present study prevented the CPS or TMB from being obtained for each patient. Future studies could not only investigate the associations between the PD-L1 CPS or TMB and patient survival but also analyse whether these biomarkers change after exposure to dual blockade therapy and whether these changes predict treatment efficacy.

Furthermore, HER2-targeted therapy has advanced quickly in recent years. In addition to trastuzumab and pertuzumab, antibody‒drug conjugates, such as T-DM1, T-DXd and RC48-ADC, have been implemented in gastric cancer. Although neither trastuzumab and pertuzumab dual therapy nor T-DM1 monotherapy achieved a satisfactory outcome as second-line treatment in HER2-positive gastric cancer44,45, T-DXd and RC48-ADC achieved clinical benefits as salvage treatments46,47. In breast cancer, T-DM1 and anti-CTLA-4/PD-1 inhibitor combination therapy triggered innate and adaptive immunity, significantly increasing regulatory T cells26. It is unknown whether these medicines can be combined with immune checkpoint inhibitors to serve as stronger combination therapies for patients with HER2-positive gastric cancer. Similarly, other immune checkpoint inhibitors, including PD-L1 inhibitors, CTLA-4 inhibitors, and TIGIT inhibitors, also have potential.

Our study has several limitations. Owing to the limited number of enrolled patients, we did not perform subgroup analysis for each type of PD-1 inhibitor or chemotherapy regimen. Additionally, the dosing of each agent was not specifically recorded. Given that the study was designed as a single-armed study, we cannot formally conclude that dual blockade of HER2 and PD-1 is superior to the standard of care. However, our study is meaningful because it assessed the efficacy and safety of this dual blockade treatment in a real-world setting.

Conclusion

Dual blockade of HER2 and PD-1 with trastuzumab and a PD-1 inhibitor with or without chemotherapy achieved satisfactory results in patients with HER2-positive gastric cancer, especially in previously untreated patients. The combination therapy also had acceptable safety. Given these findings, prospective studies are warranted to further investigate whether PD-L1 expression and microsatellite status are associated with treatment efficacy in the first-line setting and later lines of treatment.

Moduli Stacks of G G -Curves in Homotopy Theory at h = p − 1 h=p-1
Rin Ray

Abstract
. We study the action on the deformation space of a formal group by the maximal finite subgroup G G of its automorphisms, at the first height where the group has nontrivial p p -torsion for odd p p . We show given this group G G there is a universal construction of a geometric model of the G G -action via inverse Galois theory which generalizes the use of level structure to ramification data. We use configuration spaces to understand the model, and conclude that the Lubin-Tate action at h = p − 1 h=p-1 is a subgroup of the symmetric group action on the configuration space of p + 1 p+1 points on ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} .



Introduction

0.1.
Motivation for the Lubin-Tate Action

The chromatic viewpoint arose in the 1960s in the work of Quillen
[
41
]
which was expanded by Morava
[
36
]
and Ravenel
[

]

[

It is the most successful approach to the systematic structure of stable homotopy, both from a conceptual and a calculational standpoint, connecting algebraic topology of finite complexes with deep patterns in number theory.

The starting point of chromatic theory is the observation due to Quillen and Morava that the geometry of the moduli stack of formal groups controls the stable homotopy category. Much of what can be proved and conjectured about stable homotopy theory arises from the study of this stack, its stratifications, and the theory of its quasi-coherent sheaves.

At a fixed prime

[

0.3.
Dreams of a Perfect World: Construction of Geometric Model for any Given
G^{\prime}

We hunger for all heights at once. In a perfect world, there would be a universal construction which takes as input only the subgroup
G^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{G}_{h}
, and gives as output a full geometric model. The group
G^{\prime}
would determine everything about the modelling stack, and construct naturally a
G^{\prime}
-equivariant functor from it to deformations of a one-dimensional formal group of height
h
. In a perfect world, the following is true.

•

There is a minimal genus curve

with automorphism group
G^{\prime}
isomorphic to a maximal finite subgroup
G^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{G}_{h}
.

•

The moduli stack
\Def_{(X,G)}
is representable as a simple
G^{\prime}
-representation, where

•

There exists a functor
\mathcal{F}\colon\Def_{(X,G)}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{fg}_{1}}^{\leq h}
, such that this functor sends
X
to a formal group law
\mathcal{F}(X)
of dimension one and height
h
.

•

This functor

is a
G^{\prime}
-equivariant equivalence of formal moduli problems, where the action on the target is the Lubin-Tate action restricted to the subgroup
G^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{G}_{h}
.

As we journey past the height 2 horizon, let us take a moment to acknowledge the shift in complexity in constructing a geometric model for the Lubin-Tate action past height 2. We can no longer can rely on Serre-Tate to give us an isomorphism of moduli problems. It is impossible to construct a one-dimensional variety
X
whose formal group law is of height
h>2;
this cannot exist by Cartier duality. To get a one-dimensional formal group law
F
of height
h>2
whose behaviour is associated to an algebraic variety

of any dimension, we must toe a delicate line. Our

cannot split as an algebraic group, but
must
split on the level of
p
-divisible groups.

A curve cannot even have a group structure unless it is
\mathbb{G}_{a},\mathbb{G}_{m}
or an elliptic curve. To construct a geometric model out of a stack of curves, we’d need to construct curves whose Jacobians’ formal groups contain a one-dimensional formal group of height
h
compatibly in families, and we need our original moduli stack of curves to be equivalent to this puny one dimensional formal group we’ve split off. It is a lot to ask for, and yet, it fully works at

, and we have some evidence leading to the conjecture that it works at

.

Part II
Controlling the p-Divisible Group of the Jacobian of the Artin-Schreier Curve with Automorphisms

Overview

We consider the curve

3.1.
Background: Idempotent Decompositions

Our analysis of the formal group law

relies on constructing a splitting of this formal group law into smaller dimensional pieces. This is originates from a more general construction which we briefly outline.

Construction 2
.

Any

-complete additive category

-action splits due to a splitting of the source of the following map,

k[G]\hookrightarrow\End(Y),

For

representatives of the isomorphism classes of irreps of

k[G]\simeq\End_{k}(S_{1})\times\cdots\times\End_{k}(S_{n})

If we take

to be coprime to

, thus the representatives are characters of

. Then for

is a central idempotent (projector) in

Further, we can write

e_{i}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\chi_{S_{i}}(g)g^{-1}.

In other words,

k[G]\simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}Z_{p}[G].

This induces a splitting on

,

Y\simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}Y.

Remark
.

This also works for an object

in an idempotently complete category. Let’s go Maschke’s theorem.

3.2.

We preface the proof by giving an outline of our approach, in hopes that it eases the reader’s psyche. We will henceforth abbreviate

Our aim is to find a map from

It remains to calculate the height of the split formal groups. We can do so by using Dieudonné theory and the eigenvalues of Frobenius of

Let us examine pairs

is a a

Let

and lets choose a lift

to

[

Lemma 8.15

(diamond beneath)

The pushforward

is the reduced branch divisor

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{p}(C_{(p-1)^{2}},H^{q}(C_{p},M))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{p}(C_{(p-1)^{2}},H^{q}(C_{p},M))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{p}(1,H^{q}(C_{p},M))}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{res}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(p-1)^{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{cores}}

Because

is a unit in

, the horizontal map is an isomorphism and the spectral sequence collapses.
∎

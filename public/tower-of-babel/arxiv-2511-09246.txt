The tropical galaxy of a Laman graph
Amelia Bielby, Arushi Chauhan, Cassia Pearce, Yue Ren

Abstract
. A Laman graph G G is a minimally rigid graph in dimension two, and its realization number is its number of distinct embeddings with fixed generic edge lengths. While conjectured to grow exponentially in the number of vertices of G G , the best proven lower bound is merely 2 2 . Motivated by the fact that the realization number can be expressed as a tropical intersection product involving Trop ⁡ ( G ) \Trop(G) , the Bergman fan of the graphic matroid of G G , and the fact that stars of Trop ⁡ ( G ) \Trop(G) naturally lead to lower bounds thereof, we introduce the tropical galaxy of G G together with a galactic pairing thereon. We study structural properties of this pairing, such as under which conditions it is non-trivially subadditive, and connect it being non-zero to arboreal pairs. We also present a software package for working with tropical galaxies.

Introduction

Rigidity theory studies the (in)flexibility of graphs embedded in euclidean space. Its origins date back to Maxwell, who studied bar-and-joint frameworks motivated by engineering
[
9
]
. Modern rigidity theory enjoys a variety of applications beyond structural engineering, such as robotics
[
14
]
, material science
[
11
]
, and sensor networks
[
13
]
. Moreover, rigidity theory draws from a surprising number of mathematical areas such as algebraic geometry and combinatorics
[
12
]
.

Laman graphs
G
play an important role in rigidity theory. They represent minimally rigid structures in dimension
2
. A property of particular interest for this paper are their
realization numbers

c_{2}(G)
, that is the number of ways a
G
with fixed generic edge lengths can be embedded into
\mathbb{R}^{2}
up to translation and rotation. It is a property that connects combinatorics and geometry. Most peculiarly, the realization number of a Laman graph with
n
vertices has been conjectured to be
2^{n-3}
by Jackson and Owen
[
7
]
, while the current best lower bound is merely
2
.

We will study the realization number using a new tropical approach that expresses it as a tropical intersection product
2\cdot c_{2}(G)=\Trop(G)\cdot(-\Trop(G))
, where
\Trop(G)
is (a coarsening of) the Bergman fan of the graphic matroid of
G

[
4
]
. This approach naturally leads to a method of obtaining lower bounds by replacing the intersects with their stars, see
Lemma

2.19
. This work aims to start an avenue of research into lower bounding
c_{2}(G)
by studying the stars of
\Trop(G)
.

In
Section

3
, we introduce so-called
graph excisions
, which is the graphic analogue of taking tropical stars along rays. These will be crucial building-blocks of the next sections.

In
Section

4
, we introduce the so-called
tropical galaxy

\Gamma_{G}
of a Laman graph
G
, which is a directed acyclic graph whose vertices are the stars of
\Trop(G)
and edges

\Sigma_{2}

\Sigma_{1}
around a ray. Note that

, the rest of the paper is dedicated to study its properties:

In
Section

In

Tropicalizations of multigraphs

Definition 2.7

Let
\mathbb{G}

The

\rank(F)\coloneqq\rank\Big((m_{v_{j},e_{i}})_{v_{j}\in V(G),e_{i}\in F}\Big),\text{ where }m_{v_{j},e_{i}}=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if }e_{i}=\{v_{j},v_{j^{\prime}}\}\text{ for }j<j^{\prime},\\

2.3.
Tropical intersection numbers

In this section, we assume some basic familiarity with balanced polyhedral complexes and recall the concept of their stable intersection and the resulting tropical intersection numbers. We then use them to define the realization number of Laman graphs. More information on stable intersections can be found in
[
8
, Section 3.6]
.

Definition 2.13

Let
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}
be two balanced polyhedral complexes in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The
stable intersection
of
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
is defined to the polyhedral complex

\Sigma_{1}\wedge\Sigma_{2}\coloneqq\Big\{\sigma_{1}\cap\sigma_{2}\mid\dim(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2})=m\Big\}

together with multiplicities defined by

\mult_{\Sigma_{1}\wedge\Sigma_{2}}(\sigma_{1}\cap\sigma_{2})\coloneqq\sum_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}\mult_{\Sigma_{1}}(\tau_{1})\cdot\mult_{\Sigma_{2}}(\tau_{2})\cdot[N:N_{1}+N_{2}]

where
\tau_{i}\in\Sigma_{i}
such that
(\sigma_{1}\cap\sigma_{2})\subseteq\tau_{i}
for
i={1,2}
and
\tau_{1}\cap(\tau_{2}+\varepsilon\cdot u)\neq\emptyset
for some fixed
u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
generic and
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, and
N\coloneqq\mathbb{Z}^{m}
is the standard lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
N_{i}\coloneqq L_{i}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{m}
are the sublattices induced by
\tau_{i}
, and
[N:N_{1}+N_{2}]
is the index of sublattice
N_{1}+N_{2}
inside
N
.

Alternatively, by
[
8
, Proposition 3.6.12]
, one can show that

\Sigma_{1}\wedge\Sigma_{2}=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\Sigma_{1}\wedge(\Sigma_{2}+\varepsilon\cdot u)\qquad\text{for }u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\text{ generic}.

Theorem 2.14

(
[
8
, Theorem 3.6.10]
)

Let
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
be two balanced polyhedral complexes in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then their stable intersection
\Sigma\wedge\Sigma^{\prime}
is either empty or a balanced polyhedral complex with

\codim(\Sigma\wedge\Sigma^{\prime})=\codim(\Sigma)+\codim(\Sigma^{\prime}).

Moreover, if
\codim(\Sigma)+\codim(\Sigma^{\prime})+l>m
, where
l
is the dimension of the intersection of the lineality spaces of
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
, then
\Sigma\wedge\Sigma^{\prime}
is empty.

Definition 2.15

Let
\mathbb{G}
and
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
be two multigraphs with

\codim(\Trop(\mathbb{G}))+\codim(\Trop(\mathbb{G}^{\prime}))=m-1

\Trop(\mathbb{G})\cdot(-\Trop(\mathbb{G}^{\prime}))\coloneqq\begin{cases}0\hskip 129.46004pt\text{if }\Trop(\mathbb{G})\wedge(-\Trop(\mathbb{G}^{\prime}))=\emptyset,\\
\mult_{\Trop(\mathbb{G})\wedge(-\Trop(\mathbb{G}^{\prime}))}(\Span(1_{[m]}))\hskip 71.13188pt\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Definition 2.16

The

of a Laman graph

c_{2}(M_{G})\coloneqq\frac{\Trop(G)\cdot(-\Trop(G))}{2}.

Lemma 2.19

Let
\Sigma
,
\Sigma^{\prime}
be two balanced polyhedral complexes of complementary dimension. Then

\Sigma\cdot\Sigma^{\prime}\geq\Star_{\Sigma}(\sigma)\cdot\Sigma^{\prime}\qquad\text{ for all }\sigma\in\Sigma.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
w\coloneqq 0\in\Relint(\sigma)
.

For
t>0
, let
t\cdot\Sigma
be the balanced polyhedral complex with polyhedra
t\cdot\sigma
, where
\sigma\in\Sigma
and
t\cdot(\ldots)
denotes linear scaling by
t
, and multiplicities
\mult_{t\cdot\Sigma}(t\cdot\sigma)=\mult_{\Sigma}(\sigma)
. Then
(t\cdot\Sigma)\cdot\Sigma^{\prime}=\Sigma\cdot\Sigma^{\prime}
for all
t>0
, and
t\cdot\Sigma
converges to
\Star_{\Sigma}(w)
as
t
goes to
\infty
. Note that the stable intersection points of
t\cdot\Sigma\wedge\Sigma^{\prime}
varies continuously in
t
.

Let further
r_{t}\coloneqq\min(\|u\|\mid u\in\sigma,\sigma\in t\cdot\Sigma,0\notin\sigma)
denote the minimal distance between
0
and all polyhedra of
t\cdot\Sigma
not containing
0
, and let
B_{t}
denote the ball around
0
of radius
r_{t}
. Then
t\cdot\Sigma
and
(t+s)\cdot\Sigma
coincide inside
B_{t}
for all
s>0
, and thus
w\in|t\cdot\Sigma\wedge\Sigma^{\prime}|\cap B_{t}
implies
w\in\Star_{\Sigma}(w)\wedge\Sigma^{\prime}
. Moreover,
B_{t}
converges to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as
t
goes to
\infty
.

Then, as
t
goes to
\infty
, any intersection point of
t\cdot\Sigma\wedge\Sigma^{\prime}
either falls into
B_{t}
, becoming an intersection point of
\Star_{\Sigma}(w)\cdot\Sigma^{\prime}
or diverges to infinity. This shows the claim.
∎

Note that there is a straightforward relationship between chains of excisions and chains of flats:

Lemma 3.5

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a multigraph and let
\mathbbm{e}\in\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{G})
be one of its multiedges, i.e., a rank-
1
flat of
\mathbb{G}
. Then any multiedge
\mathbbm{e}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright\mathbbm{e})
,
\mathbbm{e}^{\prime}\neq\mathbbm{e}
gives rise to a rank-

flat
\mathbbm{e}\cup\mathbbm{e}^{\prime}\subsetneq E(\mathbb{G})
, and any rank-
2

,
\mathbbm{e}\subsetneq F
, gives rise to a multiedge

.

Proof.

Follows directly from
Definition

3.1
: Let
v_{1},v_{2}\in V(\mathbb{G})
denote the vertices of
\mathbbm{e}
and let
v_{12}\in V(\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright\mathbbm{e})
denote the vertex added by the excision.

Consider a multiedge
\mathbbm{e}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright\mathbbm{e})
,
\mathbbm{e}^{\prime}\neq\mathbbm{e}
. If
v_{12}\notin V(\mathbbm{e}^{\prime})
, then
\mathbbm{e}^{\prime}
is a multiedge of
\mathbb{G}
that is disjoint of
\mathbbm{e}
. If
v_{12}\in V(\mathbbm{e}^{\prime})
, say
\mathbbm{e}^{\prime}
is the multiedge connecting
v_{12}
and
v_{3}
, then
\mathbbm{e}^{\prime}
consists of all edges of
\mathbb{G}
connecting
v_{3}
to either
v_{1}
or
v_{2}
. In both cases,
\mathbbm{e}\cup\mathbbm{e}^{\prime}
is a rank-
2
flat containing
\mathbbm{e}
.

Conversely, let
F
be a rank-
2
flat containing
\mathbbm{e}
. Then either
F
is a disjoint union of two multiedges
\mathbbm{e}
and
F\setminus\mathbbm{e}
or there is a vertex
v_{3}\in V(\mathbb{G})
such that

contains
\mathbbm{e}
and all edges
F\setminus\mathbbm{e}\subseteq E(\mathbb{G})
connecting
v_{3}
to either
v_{1}
or
v_{2}
. In both cases,
F\setminus\mathbbm{e}
is a multiedge of
\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright\mathbbm{e})
.
∎

Applying

Corollary 3.6

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a multigraph. Then any chain of excisions
\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright\mathbbm{e}_{1}\curvearrowright\dots\curvearrowright\mathbbm{e}_{r}
gives rise to a chain of flats
\emptyset=F_{0}\subsetneq\mathbbm{e}_{1}\subsetneq\mathbbm{e}_{1}\cup\mathbbm{e}_{2}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq\mathbbm{e}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\mathbbm{e}_{r}
with
\rank(\mathbbm{e}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\mathbbm{e}_{j})=j
, and any chain of flats
\emptyset=F_{0}\subsetneq F_{1}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq F_{r}
with
\rank(F_{j})=j
gives rise to a chain of excisions
\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright F_{1}\curvearrowright F_{2}\setminus F_{1}\curvearrowright\dots\curvearrowright F_{r}\setminus F_{r-1}
.

Corollary 3.7

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a multigraph, and fix a chain of excisions
\mathbb{H}\coloneqq\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright\mathbbm{e}_{1}\curvearrowright\dots\curvearrowright\mathbbm{e}_{r}
and its corresponding chain of flats
F_{\bullet}\colon\emptyset=F_{0}\subsetneq F_{1}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq F_{r}
from
Corollary

3.6
. Then any chain of flats
F_{\bullet}^{\prime}
on
\mathbb{H}
beginning with
F_{i}^{\prime}=\mathbbm{e}_{i}
is a chain of flats on
\mathbb{G}
extending
F_{\bullet}
, and vice versa.

Lemma 3.8

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a multigraph and let
\mathbb{H}\coloneqq\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright\mathbbm{e}_{1}\curvearrowright\dots\curvearrowright\mathbbm{e}_{r}
arise from a chain of excisions. Let
\mathbb{G}_{1},\dots,\mathbb{G}_{s}
be the connected components of
\mathbb{G}
. By
Lemma

3.4
, we may reorder
\mathbbm{e}_{1},\dots,\mathbbm{e}_{r}
so that
\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{G}_{k})\supseteq\{\mathbbm{e}_{j_{k-1}+1},\dots,\mathbbm{e}_{j_{k}}\}
for
0=j_{0}<\dots<j_{s}=r
. Then the following is a valid cone in
\Trop(\mathbb{G})
:

\displaystyle\sigma\coloneqq

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbbm{e}_{1}}+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbbm{e}_{1}\cup\mathbbm{e}_{2}}+\dots+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbbm{e}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\mathbbm{e}_{j_{1}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbbm{e}_{j_{1}+1}}+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbbm{e}_{j_{1}+1}\cup\mathbbm{e}_{j_{1}+2}}+\dots+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbbm{e}_{j_{1}+1}\cup\dots\cup\mathbbm{e}_{j_{2}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 13.51505pt\vdots

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbbm{e}_{j_{s-1}+1}}+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbbm{e}_{j_{s-1}+1}\cup\mathbbm{e}_{j_{s-1}+2}}+\dots+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbbm{e}_{j_{s-1}+1}\cup\dots\cup\mathbbm{e}_{j_{s}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{R}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{[m]}\in\Trop(\mathbb{G}).

Moreover,
\Trop(\mathbb{H})=\Star_{\Trop(\mathbb{G})}(\sigma)
.

Proof.

We may assume without loss of generality that
\mathbb{G}
is connected, which means that the only connected components of
\mathbb{H}
are
\mathbb{H}_{1},\dots,\mathbb{H}_{r}
with
E(\mathbb{H}_{j})=\mathbbm{e}_{j}
, and a component
\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{H}
with

for

. By
Corollary

3.6
, the following is a valid chain of flats on
\mathbb{G}
:

F_{E,\bullet}\colon\;\;\emptyset\subsetneq\mathbbm{e}_{1}\subsetneq\mathbbm{e}_{1}\cup\mathbbm{e}_{2}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq\mathbbm{e}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\mathbbm{e}_{r}\subseteq E.

Hence
\sigma=\sigma(F_{E,\bullet})\in\Trop(\mathbb{G})
. It remains to show that
\Trop(\mathbb{H})=\Star_{\Trop(\mathbb{G})}(\sigma)
.

For the “
\subseteq
” inclusion, let
\tau\in\Trop(\mathbb{H})
be a maximal cone. By definition,
\tau
arises from a maximal chain of flats on each connected component of
\mathbb{H}
, which we can rearrange to become a single maximal chain of flats on
\mathbb{H}
beginning with the
\mathbbm{e}_{i}
:

F_{[m],\bullet}\colon\;\;\emptyset\subsetneq\mathbbm{e}_{1}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq\mathbbm{e}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\mathbbm{e}_{r}\subsetneq E\cup F_{1}\subsetneq E\cup F_{2}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq E\cup F_{\ell}\subsetneq[m].

By
Corollary

3.7
, this is also a valid maximal chain of flats on
\mathbb{G}
which corresponds to a maximal cone
\sigma(F_{[m],\bullet})\in\Trop(\mathbb{G})
. We now claim that
\tau=\Star_{\sigma(F_{[m],\bullet})}(v)
for any

, say

Let

(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbbm{e}_{j}}
induces the chains
\emptyset\subsetneq\mathbbm{e}_{j}

induces the chain

(1)

if

for some

,

(2)

if

and

with
j_{1}\leq j_{2}
.

We now show that for

on
\mathbb{G}
.
For that let

Conversely, let

induces

on
\mathbb{G}
. Then we have

for all

(2)

and

.

, say

,

Lemma 3.9

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a multigraph and let

Tropical galaxies of Laman graphs

In this section, we introduce the main object of study for this paper as well as some of its basic properties.

Definition 4.1

Let
G
be a Laman graph. The
tropical galaxy
of
G
is a directed acyclic graph
\Gamma_{G}
whose vertices are all possible excisions of
G
and whose edges represent excisions:

•

V(\Gamma_{G})=\{G\curvearrowright\mathbbm{e}_{1}\curvearrowright\dots\curvearrowright\mathbbm{e}_{r}\mid(\mathbbm{e}_{1},\dots,\mathbbm{e}_{r})\text{ is a valid chain of excisions}\}
, and

•

(\mathbb{H},\mathbb{H}^{\prime})\in E(\Gamma_{G})
if and only if
\mathbb{H}^{\prime}=\mathbb{H}\curvearrowright\mathbbm{e}
for some multiedge
\mathbbm{e}\in\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{H})
.

Note that
G\in V(\Gamma_{G})
is the unique source in
\Gamma_{G}
.

Definition 4.2

On the vertices of
\Gamma_{G}
we have a
galactic pairing

V(\Gamma_{G})\times V(\Gamma_{G})\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\qquad(\mathbb{H},\mathbb{H}^{\prime})\longmapsto\langle\mathbb{H},\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\rangle\coloneqq\Trop(\mathbb{H})\cdot(-\Trop(\mathbb{H}^{\prime})).

Here are some basic properties of the tropical intersection pairing on
\Gamma_{G}
:

Lemma 4.3

Let
G
be a Laman graph. Then the galactic pairing is

(1)

symmetric, i.e.,
\langle\mathbb{H},\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\rangle=\langle\mathbb{H}^{\prime},\mathbb{H}\rangle
for all
\mathbb{H},\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\in V(\Gamma_{G})
.

(2)

monotonic, i.e.,
\langle\mathbb{H},\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\rangle\geq\langle\mathbb{H},\mathbb{H}^{\prime\prime}\rangle
for all
\mathbb{H}\in V(\Gamma_{G})
and
(\mathbb{H}^{\prime},\mathbb{H}^{\prime\prime})\in E(\Gamma_{G})
.

(3)

trivial on branches, i.e.,
\langle\mathbb{H},\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\rangle=0
for all
\mathbb{H},\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\in V(\Gamma_{G})
if there exists some
\mathbb{G}\in V(\Gamma_{G})\setminus\{G\}
that is connected to
\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{H}^{\prime}
via a sequence of edges.

(4)

binary on the leaves, i.e.,
\langle\mathbb{H},\mathbb{F}\rangle\in\{0,1\}
for all
\mathbb{H},\mathbb{F}\in V(\Gamma_{G})
,
\mathbb{F}
leaf.

Proof.

(1)

Follows from
\Trop(\mathbb{H})\wedge-\Trop(\mathbb{H}^{\prime})=-(-\Trop(\mathbb{H})\wedge\Trop(\mathbb{H}^{\prime}))
.

(2)

Follows from
Lemma

2.19
and
Lemma

3.8
.

(3)

Follows from
Corollary

2.12
and
[
8
, Theorem 3.6.10]
.

(4)

Follows from the fact that
\Trop(\mathbb{F})=-\Trop(\mathbb{F})
, which makes the stable intersection
\Trop(\mathbb{H})\wedge(-\Trop(\mathbb{F}))=\Trop(\mathbb{H})\wedge\Trop(\mathbb{F})
a tropical linear space. ∎

Moreover, the pairing takes on the following concrete values for certain special cases:

Lemma 4.4

Fix
\mathbb{G}\in V(\Gamma_{G})
and let
\mathbb{H}\in V(\Gamma_{G})
be an excised chain of multiedges. Then either

\langle\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\rangle=0\qquad\text{for all descendants }\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\text{ of }\mathbb{H}

or

\langle\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\rangle=1\qquad\text{for all descendants }\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\text{ of }\mathbb{H}

Proof.

Follows from
|\Trop(\mathbb{H})|=|\Trop(\mathbb{H}^{\prime})|
for all descendants
\mathbb{H}^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{H}
.
∎

Lemma 4.5

Fix
\mathbb{G}\in V(\Gamma_{G})
and let
\mathbb{H}\in V(\Gamma_{G})
be an excised triangle. Then:

(1)

If there is no edge
(\mathbb{H},\mathbb{F})\in E(\Gamma_{G})
with
\langle\mathbb{G},\mathbb{F}\rangle>0
, then
\langle\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}\rangle=0
.

(2)

If there is exactly one edge
(\mathbb{H},\mathbb{F})\in E(\Gamma_{G})
with
\langle\mathbb{G},\mathbb{F}\rangle>0
, then
\langle\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}\rangle=\langle\mathbb{G},\mathbb{F}\rangle
.

Proof.

(1)

Note that
|\Trop(\mathbb{H})|\subseteq|\Trop(\mathbb{F}_{1})|\cup|\Trop(\mathbb{F}_{2})|\cup|\Trop(\mathbb{F}_{3})|
. And
\langle\mathbb{G},\mathbb{F}_{i}\rangle=0
means that for
u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
generic

|\Trop(\mathbb{G})|\cap|-\Trop(\mathbb{F}_{i})+u|=\emptyset

and hence
|\Trop(\mathbb{G})|\cap|-\Trop(\mathbb{H})+u|=\emptyset
.

(2)

As in
(1)
,
|\Trop(\mathbb{H})|\subseteq|\Trop(\mathbb{F}_{1})|\cup|\Trop(\mathbb{F}_{2})|\cup|\Trop(\mathbb{F}_{3})|
. If
\langle\mathbb{G},\mathbb{F}_{i}\rangle>0
for some
i\in\{1,2,3\}
and zero on the other leaves, then, since
|\Trop(\mathbb{G})|\cap|-\Trop(\mathbb{H})+u|\subseteq|\Trop(\mathbb{G})|\cap|-\Trop(\mathbb{F}_{i})+u|
and, by monotonicity
\langle\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}\rangle\geq\langle\mathbb{G},\mathbb{F}_{i}\rangle
, it follows that
\langle\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}\rangle=\langle\mathbb{G},\mathbb{F}_{i}\rangle
.
∎

5.
Subadditivity

Section

4
closed with a couple of results on the value of the galactic pairing on the outer stars, i.e., excised triangles and fully excised graphs. To understand
\langle G,G\rangle=2\cdot c_{2}(G)
, it is therefore important to understand the subadditivity of the galactic pairing in the following sense:

Proposition 5.1

Let
\mathbb{G},\mathbb{G}^{\prime}\in V(\Gamma_{G})
be two tropical stars. Let
d(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the distance on the directed graph
\Gamma_{G}
. Then for all
\ell>0
so that there is some
\mathbb{H}\in V(\Gamma_{G})
with
d(\mathbb{G}^{\prime},\mathbb{H})=\ell
, we have

\langle\mathbb{G},\mathbb{G}^{\prime}\rangle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbb{H}\in V(\Gamma_{G})\\
d(\mathbb{G}^{\prime},\mathbb{H})=\ell\end{subarray}}\langle\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}\rangle.

Proof.

Using
Lemma

3.8
, we obtain
|\Trop(\mathbb{G}^{\prime})|\subseteq\bigcup_{\mathbb{H}\in V(\Gamma_{G}),d(\mathbb{G}^{\prime},\mathbb{H})=\ell}|\Trop(\mathbb{H})|
, from which the statement follows as in the proof
Lemma

4.5
.
∎

First note that the “sub” in “subadditive” is necessary, as one can find examples which show that the galactic pairing is not additive:

Example 5.2

Let
G
be a chain of triangles with 11 edges labelled as in
Figure

\mathbb{H}\curvearrowright 1\curvearrowright 23\curvearrowright 45\curvearrowright 10

Let further
\mathbb{F}_{i}\coloneqq\mathbb{H}\curvearrowright\mathbbm{e}_{i}

\mathbbm{e}_{1},\mathbbm{e}_{2},\mathbbm{e}_{3}

\mathbb{H}

\langle\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}\rangle=2\neq 3=\underbrace{\langle\mathbb{G},\mathbb{F}_{1}\rangle}_{=1}+\underbrace{\langle\mathbb{G},\mathbb{F}_{2}\rangle}_{=1}+\underbrace{\langle\mathbb{G},\mathbb{F}_{3}\rangle}_{=1}

which shows that the galactic pairing is not additive.

Hence the main question is to find conditions under which the galactic pairing actually increases in the sense of

\langle\mathbb{G},\mathbb{G}^{\prime}\rangle>\max\Big(\big\{\langle\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}\rangle\mid\mathbb{H}\in V(\Gamma_{G}),d(\mathbb{G}^{\prime},\mathbb{H})=1\big\}\Big).

While this seems to be a purely combinatorial question at first glance, it is difficult to translate the geometric nature of the tropical intersection product purely into combinatorics on the graphs. We therefore close the section with an unfortunately not purely combinatorial condition under which the pairing does increase:

Definition 5.3

Let
\mathbb{H},\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\in V(\Gamma_{G})
be two excised triangles of
G
, with triangle multiedges
\mathbbm{a}
,
\mathbbm{b}
,
\mathbbm{c}
, and
\mathbbm{a}^{\prime}
,
\mathbbm{b}^{\prime}
,
\mathbbm{c}^{\prime}
respectively. For
i=1,2
, consider
\mathbb{F}_{i}=\mathbb{H}\curvearrowright\mathbbm{e}_{i}
and
\mathbb{F}_{i}^{\prime}=\mathbb{H}\curvearrowright\mathbbm{e}_{i}^{\prime}
for some
\mathbbm{e}_{i}\in\{\mathbbm{a},\mathbbm{b},\mathbbm{c}\}
and
\mathbbm{e}_{i}^{\prime}\in\{\mathbbm{a}^{\prime},\mathbbm{b}^{\prime},\mathbbm{c}^{\prime}\}
such that
(\mathbb{F}_{1},\mathbb{F}_{1}^{\prime})\neq(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\prime})
and
\langle\mathbb{F}_{i},\mathbb{F}_{i}^{\prime}\rangle=1
. We say that the two pairs
(\mathbb{F}_{1},\mathbb{F}_{1}^{\prime})
and
(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\prime})
are
additive
if there is some
w\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that

u_{1}-u_{1}^{\prime}=w=u_{2}-u_{2}^{\prime}\text{ where }(u_{1},u_{1}^{\prime})\neq(u_{2},u_{2}^{\prime})

and
u_{i}
and
u_{i}^{\prime}
induce chains of flats on
\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{H}^{\prime}
where
\mathbbm{e}_{i}
and
\mathbbm{e}_{i}^{\prime}
come before
\{\mathbbm{a},\mathbbm{b},\mathbbm{c}\}\setminus\{\mathbbm{e}_{i}\}
and
\{\mathbbm{a}^{\prime},\mathbbm{b}^{\prime},\mathbbm{c}^{\prime}\}\setminus\{\mathbbm{e}_{i}^{\prime}\}
respectively, i.e.,

\displaystyle u_{i,j}>u_{i,k}\text{ for }j\in\mathbbm{e},k\in\{\mathbbm{a},\mathbbm{b},\mathbbm{c}\}\setminus\{\mathbbm{e}\}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle u_{i,j}^{\prime}>u_{i,k}^{\prime}\text{ for }j\in\mathbbm{e}^{\prime},k\in\{\mathbbm{a}^{\prime},\mathbbm{b}^{\prime},\mathbbm{c}^{\prime}\}\setminus\{\mathbbm{e}^{\prime}\}.

Lemma 5.4

Let
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
be two cones in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then for
w\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
we have

\sigma\cap(w+\sigma^{\prime})\neq\emptyset\quad\iff\quad w\in\sigma+(-\sigma^{\prime}).

Proof.

The “
\Rightarrow
” direction is straightforward. For the “
\Leftarrow
” direction, consider a translation
w\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
\sigma\cap(w+\sigma^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
, say
u\in\sigma\cap(w+\sigma^{\prime})
and set
u^{\prime}\coloneqq w-u
. Then
u\in w+\sigma^{\prime}
implies
u^{\prime}\in-\sigma^{\prime}
and hence
w=u+u^{\prime}\in\sigma+(-\sigma^{\prime})
.
∎

Theorem 5.5

Let
G
be a Laman graph and
\mathbb{H},\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\in V(\Gamma_{G})
be two of its excised triangles. Suppose for
i=1,2
there are
\mathbb{F}_{i}=\mathbb{H}\curvearrowright\mathbbm{e}_{i}
and
\mathbb{F}_{i}^{\prime}=\mathbb{H}\curvearrowright\mathbbm{e}_{i}^{\prime}
for some
\mathbbm{e}_{i}\in\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{H})
and
\mathbbm{e}_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{H}^{\prime})
such that
\langle\mathbb{F}_{i},\mathbb{F}_{i}^{\prime}\rangle=1
. Then

(\mathbb{F}_{1},\mathbb{F}_{1}^{\prime})\text{ and }(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\prime})\text{ additive}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\langle\mathbb{H},\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\rangle=2.

Proof.

We first show that
\langle\mathbb{H},\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\rangle\geq 2
.
Suppose that
(\mathbb{F}_{1},\mathbb{F}_{1}^{\prime})
and
(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\prime})
are additive, i.e., there is some
w\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
u_{1}-u_{1}^{\prime}=w=u_{2}-u_{2}^{\prime}
with
u_{i}
and
u_{i}^{\prime}
satisfying the properties in
Definition

5.3
.

Using the notation in
Corollary

2.12
(3), which states that
\Trop(\mathbb{H})
and
\Trop(\mathbb{H}^{\prime})
each consists of three maximal cones induced by the triangle multiedges, we have
u_{i}\in\sigma_{\mathbbm{e}_{i}}\in\Trop(\mathbb{H})
and
u_{i}^{\prime}\in\sigma_{\mathbbm{e}_{i}^{\prime}}\in\Trop(\mathbb{H}^{\prime})
. Hence
u_{i}=w-u_{i}^{\prime}\in(-\Trop(\mathbb{H}^{\prime})+w)
, and combining both we obtain
u_{i}\in\sigma_{\mathbbm{e}_{i}}\cap(\sigma_{\mathbbm{e}_{i}^{\prime}}+w)
by
Lemma

5.4
. Moreover,
\langle\mathbb{F}_{i},\mathbb{F}_{i}^{\prime}\rangle=1
implies that
\sigma_{\mathbbm{e}_{i}}\cap(\sigma_{\mathbbm{e}_{i}^{\prime}}+w)\in\Trop(\mathbb{H})\wedge(-\Trop(\mathbb{H}^{\prime})+w)
, which shows that
\Trop(\mathbb{H})\wedge(-\Trop(\mathbb{H}^{\prime})+w)
consists of at least two polyhedra. Hence
\langle\mathbb{H},\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\rangle\geq 2
.

Next we show that
\langle\mathbb{H},\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\rangle\leq 2
. For that, consider a
3
-dimensional coordinate subspace
V
that is a complement to
\mathrm{Lineality}(\Trop(\mathbb{H}))+\mathrm{Lineality}(\Trop(\mathbb{H}^{\prime}))
, where
\mathrm{Lineality}(\cdot)
denotes the lineality space. On
V\cong\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\Trop(\mathbb{H})\cap V
and
\Trop(\mathbb{H}^{\prime})\cap V
remain tropical linear spaces and
(\Trop(\mathbb{H})\cap V)\cdot(-\Trop(\mathbb{H}\cap V))=\Trop(\mathbb{H})\cdot(-\Trop(\mathbb{H}^{\prime}))
. Therefore,
\Trop(\mathbb{H})\cdot(-\Trop(\mathbb{H}^{\prime}))
must be either
0
,
1
, or
2
.
∎

6.
Arboreal Pairs

In this section, we show how fully excised graphs that are positively paired are equivalent to arboreal pairs in
[
2
]
.

Definition 6.1

Given two maximal (reduced) chains of flats
F_{\bullet}
and
F_{\bullet}^{\prime}
of a Laman graph
G
, the
intersection graph
of
F_{\bullet}
and
F_{\bullet}^{\prime}
is the bipartite multigraph
I_{F_{\bullet},F_{\bullet}^{\prime}}
with the following vertex and edge sets:

\displaystyle V(I_{F_{\bullet},F_{\bullet}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{F_{j}\mid j=1,\dots,r\}\sqcup\{F_{j}^{\prime}\mid j=1,\dots,r\},

\displaystyle E(I_{F_{\bullet},F_{\bullet}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{(F_{j_{i}},F_{k_{i}}^{\prime})\mid i=1,\dots,m\},

where
F_{j_{i}}
and
F_{j_{i}}^{\prime}
are the unique reduced flats with
i\in F_{j_{i}}
and
i\in F_{j_{i}}^{\prime}
.
We say
F_{\bullet}
and
F_{\bullet}^{\prime}
form an
arboreal pair
, if the intersection graph
I_{F_{\bullet},F_{\bullet}^{\prime}}
is a tree.

Proposition 6.3

Let
F_{\bullet},F_{\bullet}^{\prime}
be two maximal reduced chains of flats on
G
giving rise to fully excised
\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F}^{\prime}\in V(\Gamma_{G})
, respectively. Then

F\text{ and }F^{\prime}\text{ form an arboreal pair}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\langle\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F}^{\prime}\rangle=1.

Proof.

For the “
\Rightarrow
” direction, suppose that
F_{\bullet}
and
F_{\bullet}^{\prime}
form an arboreal pair, which means that
I_{F,F^{\prime}}
is a tree.
Note that the tropical linear spaces
\Trop(\mathbb{F})
and
\Trop(\mathbb{F}^{\prime})
each consist of a single cell
\Span(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbbm{e}}\mid\mathbbm{e}\in\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{F}))
and
\Span(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbbm{e}^{\prime}}\mid\mathbbm{e}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{F}^{\prime}))
, respectively.
Let
r
be the common length of the chains
F_{\bullet}
and
F_{\bullet}^{\prime}
. As
G
is a Laman graph, we have that
2r=m+1
.
Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times(m+1)}
be the matrix whose whose columns are the indicator vectors of
F_{j}
and
F_{j}^{\prime}
,
j=1,\dots,r
. We now show that
A
is of full rank, which means that
\Trop(\mathbb{F})
and
\Trop(\mathbb{F}^{\prime})
intersect transversally, which in turn implies
\langle\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F}^{\prime}\rangle=1
.

Fix a root on the tree
I_{F,F^{\prime}}
and orient each edge of
I_{F,F^{\prime}}
so that it points towards the root. The resulting directed tree then gives us a sequence of column reductions which result in a matrix in reduced column echelon form. This matrix is clearly of full rank, see
Example

6.4
.

For the “
\Leftarrow
” direction, suppose that
I_{F_{\bullet},F_{\bullet}^{\prime}}
has a cycle. As
|V(I_{F_{\bullet},F_{\bullet}^{\prime}})|=m+1=|E(I_{F_{\bullet},F_{\bullet}^{\prime}})|+1
, it follows that
I_{F_{\bullet},F_{\bullet}^{\prime}}
has at least two connected components. Decompose
I_{F_{\bullet},F_{\bullet}^{\prime}}=I_{1}\sqcup I_{2}
where each
I_{i}
has at least one connected component, which in turn yields a decomposition
[m]=M_{1}\sqcup M_{2}
where
M_{i}\coloneqq\bigcup_{F\in V(I_{i})}F\subseteq[m]
. Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times(m+1)}
be the matrix as before, but with the rows rearranged so that ground set elements in
M_{1}
come before ground set elements in
M_{2}
and with the columns rearranged so that flats in
V(I_{1})
come before flats in
V(I_{2})
. The result is a block-diagonal matrix with blocks
A_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{|M_{i}|\times|E(I_{i})|}
, see
Example

6.4
. Note that at most one of the
A_{i}
may be square. We now distinguish between two cases:

If neither
A_{1}
nor
A_{2}
is square, then one of them must have more rows than columns, say
A_{2}
, which implies that its columns do not span the entire
\mathbb{R}^{|M_{2}|}
, which in turn implies that the columns of
A
do not span the entire
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

If one of the
A_{i}
is square, say
A_{2}
, then fixing an orientation on
I_{2}
yields a linear combination of the columns that equals zero. Hence the square submatrix has a non-trivial kernel, which means its columns do do not span the entire
\mathbb{R}^{|M_{2}|}
, which in turn implies that the columns of
A
do not span the entire
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

In both cases, we have that
\dim(\Span(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbbm{e}}\mid\mathbbm{e}\in\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{F}))+\Span(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbbm{e}}\mid\mathbbm{e}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{F}^{\prime})))\neq m
, which shows that
\Trop(\mathbb{F})\wedge\Trop(\mathbb{F}^{\prime})=\emptyset
, which in turn implies that
\langle\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F}^{\prime}\rangle=0
.
∎

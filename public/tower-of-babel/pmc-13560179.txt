The association of inflammatory markers with cerclage failure in twin pregnancies
Authors: Lihua Wang, Danlin Yang, Rong Lin, Mian Pan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe association of inflammatory markers, including the systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), systemic inflammation response index (SIRI), neutrophil-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), and platelet-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), with pregnancy outcomes in twin pregnancies following cervical cerclage remains unclear. This study aimed to evaluate the predictive value of these markers for spontaneous preterm birth before 28 weeks of gestation in this high-risk population.MethodsThis retrospective study included 111 twin pregnancies undergoing transvaginal cervical cerclage for cervical shortening or dilation. Inflammatory markers (SII, SIRI, NLR, and PLR) were calculated from maternal peripheral blood collected before the procedure. The primary outcome was cerclage failure, defined as spontaneous preterm birth before 28 weeks of gestation. The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) was used to assess predictive performance, and modified Poisson regression was applied to evaluate the association between these markers and cerclage failure.ResultsCerclage failure occurred in 13 pregnancies (11.7%). For predicting cerclage failure, the AUC values of SII, SIRI, NLR, and PLR were 0.844, 0.722, 0.835, and 0.776, respectively. Modified Poisson regression analysis revealed that elevated levels of SII (aRR = 4.44, 95% CI 1.16–16.97, P = 0.029), SIRI (aRR = 4.36, 95% CI 1.62–11.80, P = 0.004), and NLR (aRR = 8.06, 95% CI 1.09–59.49, P = 0.041) were significantly associated with cerclage failure. After additional adjustment for cerclage indication, the associations for SII (aRR = 3.64, 95% CI 1.09–12.24, P = 0.036) and SIRI (aRR = 3.42, 95% CI 1.32–9.89, P = 0.011) remained significant, whereas the association for NLR was attenuated and no longer statistically significant (aRR = 5.84, 95% CI 0.81–42.27, P = 0.081).ConclusionsThis exploratory study suggests that SII and SIRI may serve as potential noninvasive predictors of cerclage failure in twin pregnancies. These indices may aid in preoperative risk stratification but require validation in larger prospective cohorts.Keywords: Cervical cerclage, Inflammatory markers, Twin pregnancy, Preterm birth

Background

Preterm birth (PTB) is the primary cause of perinatal mortality and morbidity in multiple pregnancies, as well as adverse consequences of neurodevelopment [1]. Cervical cerclage has been reported to prolong pregnancy latency and decrease the risk of preterm birth in twin pregnancies with cervical shortening or dilation [2, 3]. However, its role remains controversial and is not routinely recommended in multiple pregnancies by major international guidelines [4]. Following detailed counseling addressing the uncertain evidence and potential risks, cerclage was selectively offered at our institution to twin pregnancies with cervical shortening or dilation. Given this cautious clinical context, identifying reliable predictors of cerclage failure is essential to optimize patient selection and counseling. Several studies have revealed that intra-amniotic inflammation is an important prognostic factor for poor pregnancy outcomes following cerclage [5, 6]. Proteomic biomarkers, interleukins and cytokines in the amniotic fluid have been found to be valuable for predicting cerclage outcomes [7–10]. However, the analysis of amniotic fluid requires invasive amniocentesis, and these specific markers are not routinely detected in clinical practice, which limits their routine application.

The neutrophil-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and platelet-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) have been reported to be associated with prognosis in systemic inflammatory diseases [11–13] and may serve as an important predictor of preterm birth [14–17]. Recently, two novel inflammatory markers, systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) and systemic inflammation response index (SIRI), reflecting local immune status and systemic inflammation [18, 19], have been demonstrated as important biochemical markers for predicting the outcome of cervical cerclage [20, 21]. Furthermore, these four inflammatory markers, comprising platelets and three subtypes of white blood cells, can be obtained from a readily available laboratory test in many clinical settings. However, the association of these markers with pregnancy outcomes in twin pregnancies following cervical cerclage remains uncertain. Extremely premature infants born before 28 weeks of gestation are at high risk of perinatal mortality and morbidity [22]. Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the predictive value of inflammatory markers, including SII, SIRI, NLR, and PLR, for PTB before 28 weeks of gestation in twin pregnancies undergoing cervical cerclage.

Materials and methods

This was a retrospective, exploratory cohort study designed to generate hypotheses regarding inflammatory markers and cerclage failure. The study was conducted in Fujian Maternity and Child Health Hospital during the period January 2017 to December 2023. A total of 111 twin pregnancies with cervical shortening (cervical length ≤ 25 mm) or dilation undergoing transvaginal cervical cerclage at 18–27 + 6 weeks of gestation were recruited to the study. Although international guidelines traditionally recommend an upper limit of 24 weeks’ gestation for most cerclage indications [4], our institution offers the procedure to selected patients with cervical shortening or dilation up to 27 + 6 weeks of gestation. This approach aims to prolong pregnancy and thereby reduce the high risks of perinatal mortality and long-term morbidity associated with extremely preterm birth. Notably, the NICE (National Institute for Health and Care Excellence) also supports considering cerclage for women with cervical dilation up to 28 weeks of gestation [23]. The cervical cerclage procedure in this study were performed based on either ultrasound indications (cervical length ≤ 25 mm) or physical examination indications (cervical dilation with or without membrane bulging). All women provided informed consent for cervical cerclage after receiving detailed counseling regarding the limited evidence and potential risks. Women were excluded based on the following criteria: (1) genetic or major fetal anomalies; (2) monochorionic-monoamniotic placentation; (3) iatrogenic preterm birth before 28 weeks of gestation; (4) active uterine contraction or vaginal bleeding; (5) clinical chorioamnionitis; (6) incomplete clinical data.

All women received standard McDonald cerclage placement performed by senior obstetricians under spinal anesthesia. Maternal blood samples were collected prior to the procedure as part of our institutional routine preoperative protocol. Endocervical cultures for bacteria, mycoplasma, and fungi were obtained both before and after surgery. As no consensus currently exists in professional guidelines regarding the perioperative use of tocolytics and antibiotics for cervical cerclage [24], perioperative management at our institution followed a standardized protocol: all women received prophylactic broad-spectrum antibiotics, with regimens adjusted based on endocervical culture results, and tocolytic therapy was administered when clinically indicated.

Maternal baseline demographic and clinical characteristics were reviewed, including maternal age, pre-pregnancy body mass index (BMI), nulliparity, use of in vitro fertilization-embryo transfer (IVF-ET), chorionicity, previous PTB or second-trimester losses, history of hysteroscopic surgery, presence of gestational diabetes or hypertension, cervical length (CL) and cervical dilation at cerclage, cervical length at 1–2 weeks after surgery, presence of bulging membranes, endocervical culture, cerclage indication type, and inflammatory markers (SII, SIRI, NLR and PLR). SII (platelet count × neutrophil count / lymphocyte count), SIRI (monocyte count × neutrophil count / lymphocyte count), NLR (neutrophil count / lymphocyte count) and PLR (platelet count / lymphocyte count) were calculated with absolute neutrophil count (×109/L), monocyte count (×109/L), lymphocyte count (×109/L) and platelet count (×109/L). The primary outcome was cerclage failure, defined as spontaneous preterm birth before 28 weeks of gestation.

Statistical analysis

Continuous normally distributed variables were presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) and compared using the Student’s t-test. Non-normally distributed continuous variables were presented as median (interquartile range) and compared using the Mann–Whitney U-test. Categorical variables were expressed as numbers and percentages (%) and compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. The area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) was used to assess the predictive performance of inflammatory markers (SII, SIRI, NLR, and PLR) for cerclage failure. The optimal cutoff values for inflammatory markers were determined using the maximum Youden index. To assess the independent association between inflammatory markers and cerclage failure and obtain clinically interpretable effect estimates, we employed modified Poisson regression with robust error variance. This method was chosen over traditional logistic regression because it directly estimates adjusted relative risks (aRR), which are more intuitive than odds ratios when the outcome is not rare (> 10%). Results are presented as aRR with 95% confidence intervals (CI). Owing to the limited sample size of the physical examination-indicated cerclage subgroup, a stratified analysis by indication type was not performed. Instead, cerclage indication type was included as a binary covariate in multivariable regression models to assess and control for its potential confounding effect in a sensitivity analysis. Given the small number of outcome events (n = 13), all multivariable models were exploratory in nature; accordingly, the effect estimates may be unstable and warrant cautious interpretation. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 26.0, IBM Corporation), and a two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

The study population comprised 111 twin pregnancies undergoing cervical cerclage, categorized as ultrasound-indicated (n = 79) or physical examination-indicated (n = 32). Overall, 13 (11.7%) experienced cerclage failure, while 98 (88.3%) delivered at ≥ 28 weeks of gestation.

Baseline characteristics

Demographic and baseline clinical characteristics were comparable between the cerclage failure and success groups, including maternal age, pre-pregnancy BMI, nulliparity, use of IVF, chorionicity, history of preterm birth or second-trimester loss, history of hysteroscopic surgery, and presence of gestational diabetes or hypertension (Table 1). However, compared with the successful group, the cerclage failure group had higher proportions of preoperative CL ≤ 1 cm, cervical dilation ≥ 2 cm, bulging membranes, positive endocervical bacterial culture, and physical examination-indicated cerclage. Given these baseline imbalances, we performed multivariable adjustment to account for potential confounding. Additionally, the failure group exhibited significantly higher WBC and neutrophil counts, lower lymphocyte counts, elevated systemic inflammatory indices (SII, SIRI, NLR, PLR), and a shorter postoperative CL. No significant between-group differences were observed in the rates of positive endocervical culture for mycoplasma or fungi.

Predictive performance of inflammatory indices

ROC analysis and corresponding AUCs are presented in Fig. 1 and Table 2. For predicting cerclage failure, the AUC values were 0.844 for SII, 0.722 for SIRI, 0.835 for NLR, and 0.776 for PLR. Pairwise comparisons revealed no significant differences among the AUCs, with the exception that SII achieved a significantly higher AUC than PLR (P = 0.047). The optimal cutoff values were 1600.67 for SII, 4.59 for SIRI, 5.22 for NLR, and 166.30 for PLR.

Multivariable regression analysis

Table 3 presents the associations between elevated inflammatory indices and cerclage failure using multivariable modified Poisson regression. In Model 1 (adjusted for preoperative CL, cervical dilation, bulging membranes, positive endocervical bacterial culture, and postoperative CL), SII (aRR = 4.44, 95% CI 1.16–16.97, P = 0.029), SIRI (aRR = 4.36, 95% CI 1.62–11.80, P = 0.004) and NLR (aRR = 8.06, 95% CI 1.09–59.49, P = 0.041) were significantly associated with an increased risk of cerclage failure, while PLR showed a non-significant trend (P = 0.095). After additional adjustment for cerclage indication type in Model 2, the associations remained significant for SII (aRR = 3.64, 95% CI 1.09–12.24, P = 0.036) and SIRI (aRR = 3.42, 95% CI 1.32–9.89, P = 0.011), whereas the association for NLR was attenuated and no longer statistically significant (aRR = 5.84, 95% CI 0.81–42.27, P = 0.081).

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first study to systematically evaluate the predictive performance of four readily available inflammatory indices (SII, SIRI, NLR, and PLR) for cerclage failure within a twin pregnancy cohort. Our primary finding is that elevated maternal SII and SIRI levels prior to cerclage placement were independently associated with cerclage failure, even after comprehensive adjustment for clinical risk factors including cerclage indication type. These findings underscore the potential clinical value of SII and SIRI in predicting cerclage outcomes.

Intrauterine inflammation is widely recognized as a significant causative factor for preterm birth [25, 26], including in the setting of cerclage [5, 6]. The inflammatory markers SII, SIRI, NLR, and PLR, which integrate neutrophil, monocyte, platelet, and lymphocyte counts, reflect systemic inflammatory status, thus providing a reasonable basis for predicting preterm birth risk. SII and SIRI were initially proposed as predictors of adverse prognosis in cancer, and have recently been explored as novel prognostic markers for obstetric morbidities [27, 28]. A case-control study revealed that SIRI and SII may be used to predict recurrent pregnancy loss [29]. Another study focused on the pregnancy outcomes following cerclage found that the baseline SII and SIRI level may predict pregnancy prognosis after cervical cerclage in singleton pregnancy [20]. However, data regarding the predictive value of these indices in twin pregnancies following cervical cerclage remain limited. Our study suggested that SII and SIRI may serve as independent predictors of cerclage failure in twin pregnancies, even after adjusting for important clinical factors such as cervical length, cervical dilation, bulging membranes, endocervical bacterial culture and cerclage indication type. Notably, it is important to recognize that these factors are not merely statistical confounders but are likely intricately linked with the inflammatory process itself. The baseline characteristics of our cohort revealed significant imbalances between the cerclage failure and success groups, with a higher proportion of physical examination-indicated cerclage, preoperative CL ≤ 1 cm, cervical dilation ≥ 2 cm, and bulging membranes in the failure group (Table 1). These factors are well-established predictors of poor pregnancy outcomes following cerclage and represent important potential confounders in the association between inflammatory markers and cerclage failure. To address this, we sequentially adjusted for these factors in our multivariable models. The persistence of significant associations for SII and SIRI after full adjustment (Model 2, Table 3) suggested that their predictive value was at least partially independent of these clinical confounders. Nevertheless, severe cervical shortening, progressive dilation, and membrane bulging often reflect an underlying local inflammatory milieu that may drive or amplify systemic inflammatory responses as measured by SII and SIRI. Despite statistical adjustment, residual confounding cannot be excluded. Unmeasured factors such as intra-amniotic inflammation may influence both inflammatory marker levels and cerclage outcomes. Future studies incorporating both systemic markers and local markers of inflammation (e.g., from cervical-vaginal fluid or amniotic fluid) are warranted to better delineate these complex interactions and confirm the independent predictive value of SII and SIRI.

The present study found that an elevated PLR was not independently associated with adverse cerclage outcomes. Research on the prognostic significance of PLR in cerclage have not yielded consistent conclusions. In line with our findings, the study conducted by Song also demonstrated that PLR has poor predictive value for cerclage outcomes [30]. Nevertheless, another study suggested that PLR can be a valuable predictor of cerclage failure in singleton pregnancy [31]. However, the study failed to incorporate the crucial factor of cervical culture in predicting the outcome.

Similarly, our analysis revealed that the predictive utility of NLR for cerclage failure was not robust in twin pregnancies. Although a strong association was observed in Model 1 (aRR = 8.06, 95% CI 1.09–59.49, P = 0.041), this association was substantially attenuated and lost statistical significance after further adjustment for cerclage indication type (Model 2: aRR = 5.84, 95% CI 0.81–42.27, P = 0.081). This dynamic change suggested that cerclage indication type is a critical potential confounder in the interpretation of NLR’s predictive value. Our findings contrast with several previous studies that identified NLR as a valuable predictor of adverse outcomes following repeat or emergency cerclage [8, 30]. These discrepancies may be attributable to differences in study populations (singleton versus twin pregnancies), sample sizes, or the comprehensiveness of adjustment for confounding variables. Notably, given the wide confidence interval for NLR in Model 2 and the limited number of outcome events in our cohort, these results should be interpreted with caution.

The superior predictive robustness of SII and SIRI likely stems from their composite nature, which integrates platelets and monocytes in addition to neutrophils and lymphocytes, thereby enabling a more comprehensive assessment of systemic inflammation. This broader characterization of the immune-inflammatory response may render these indices less susceptible to confounding by local cervical findings. Given that cerclage failure is a complex, multifactorial event driven by the interplay of inflammation, immune dysregulation, and impaired tissue repair, unidimensional markers such as PLR and NLR appear inherently limited in capturing this intricate pathophysiology.

Limitations

This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the results. First, the relatively small number of cerclage failure events (n = 13) represents a major limitation. With only 13 outcome events, multivariable regression models may be prone to overfitting, and effect estimates should be interpreted with caution—particularly those with wide confidence intervals (e.g., NLR in Model 2: aRR = 5.84, 95% CI 0.81–42.27). Although we applied modified Poisson regression with robust variance to improve estimate reliability, the small number of events precluded detailed subgroup analyses (e.g., stratified by cerclage indication) and reduced statistical power to detect modest effect sizes. Second, the study population was heterogeneous with respect to cerclage indications, and the small sample size of the physical examination-indicated subgroup limited our ability to conduct adequately powered subgroup analyses and formally assess effect modification. Although sensitivity analyses suggested that the main conclusions are relatively robust, future studies with larger cohorts are warranted to independently validate these findings in populations stratified by specific cerclage indications. Third, although we adjusted for key clinical factors, including cerclage indication and markers of severe cervical disease, the potential for residual confounding remains. Notably, unmeasured factors such as intra-amniotic inflammation may influence both inflammatory marker concentrations and cerclage-related outcomes. Fourth, the optimal cutoff values for the inflammatory markers derived from this cohort are preliminary and require external validation in independent, larger populations. Furthermore, the retrospective, single-center design introduces potential selection bias, as the decision to perform cerclage was based on individual clinical judgment rather than a rigid protocol, which may limit generalizability. Given these constraints, our findings should therefore be considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating. External validation in larger, prospective cohorts is essential to confirm the predictive value of SII and SIRI and to establish their clinical utility.

Conclusion

In this first study to evaluate inflammatory markers in twin pregnancies undergoing cerclage, SII and SIRI emerged as potential noninvasive predictors of cerclage failure. These readily available indices may provide valuable information for preoperative risk assessment. However, given the exploratory nature of this study and the limited number of outcome events, these findings warrant validation in larger prospective cohorts before clinical application.

An approach to the QHE in 3D electron systems
Authors: M A Hidalgo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe present a theoretical framework for describing the integer quantum Hall effect (IQHE) in three-dimensional (3D) electron systems. This work extends our previous single-electron approach, originally applied to two-dimensional (2D) systems such as quantum wells and graphene. Starting from the graphene model—where the unconventional Hall plateau sequence arises from Landau quantization—we generalize the formulation to 3D semimetals with low carrier density and high mobility. Using the Poisson summation method, we derive the density of states in a magnetic field, incorporating Gaussian Landau-level broadening, spin splitting, and thermal damping. The model captures both Shubnikov–de Haas oscillations and quantized Hall conductivities. The resulting Hall conductivity shows quantized values proportional to , consistent with experimental reports of 3D quantum Hall states. These results offer a unified description of quantum magnetotransport across dimensions and identify key parameters governing the IQHE in 3D semimetals.Subject terms: Materials science, Nanoscience and technology, Physics

Introduction

The integer quantum Hall effect (IQHE), discovered in 1980 by von Klitzing1, marked a milestone in condensed matter physics. The quantization of the Hall conductivity at low temperatures and strong magnetic fields, expressed in integer multiples of , was found to be remarkably robust against impurities and disorder. At the same magnetic field intervals where Hall plateaux appear, minima in the diagonal conductivity occur, corresponding to Shubnikov–de Haas (SdH) oscillations. The observation of the fractional QHE followed shortly thereafter2.

The present work introduces the extension of our single-particle framework from 2D systems to genuinely three-dimensional semimetals. Unlike the 2D case–where quantized transport may be understood in terms of topological band invariants3,4 or through semiclassical Landau quantization–3D systems require the explicit incorporation of anisotropic Fermi surfaces, longitudinal dispersion, and material-dependent corrections to the cyclotron frequency. The key novel result of this work is that the proposed 3D extension naturally reproduces the quantization scale predicted for 3D systems and validated experimentally in several semimetals, including , , and 5–10. This establishes the proposed model as a physically grounded and generalizable theoretical framework for describing quantized Hall states in 3D electron systems.

The IQHE can be observed in many two-dimensional electron systems (2DESs), including quantum wells (QWs) and MOSFETs. Decades later, both IQHE and FQHE were also observed in graphene11–13. In conventional QWs, plateaux appear at (or 2n if spin degeneracy is present), whereas graphene exhibits the unconventional sequence , i.e. 12–17. Positive Hall conductance corresponds to hole carriers and negative to electrons.

From a theoretical perspective, the IQHE is considered a manifestation of electron localization in the quantum limit. A major conceptual advance came with the topological interpretation by Thouless et al.3, showing that the Hall conductance in a 2D periodic system is a topological invariant (Chern number). By contrast, the FQHE is associated with strong electron–electron correlations, as shown by Laughlin’s theory18. Thus, while the IQHE and FQHE remain deeply connected, they are described within different theoretical frameworks: electron localization and topological invariants in the first case, correlated quantum liquids in the second. Nonetheless, an alternative single-particle approach unifying IQHE and FQHE has been proposed by Hidalgo19.

Traditionally, the QHE has been viewed as a purely 2D phenomenon. However, whether intrinsically 3D systems can host quantized Hall responses has been debated for decades. Halperin20 first suggested that quantized Hall conductance in 3D could arise from field-induced instabilities (e.g., charge-density waves). Later theoretical work proposed multiple routes to a 3D QHE, including stacked 2D layers, Fermi-surface instabilities, and topological mechanisms involving surface Fermi arcs in Weyl/Dirac semimetals21. Electron–phonon coupling was also shown to induce the required instability22.

Clear experimental evidence for a 3D QHE appeared only recently in 5,6. Quantized Hall plateaux and vanishing longitudinal resistivity were observed, with values depending on the Fermi wavelength along the field direction. Similar behavior has been reported in nanostructures, attributed to Weyl-orbit physics7,8. Other topological semimetals exhibit related quantized transport signatures.

A common characteristic of all 3D systems showing QHE signatures is their semimetallic nature: low carrier density, high mobility, and small Fermi surfaces enable the extreme quantum limit under experimentally accessible fields.

The purpose of the present work is to extend our previous model for 2DESs and graphene19,23 to 3D electron systems. Section 2 reviews the graphene model; Section 3 develops the 3D formulation; Section 4 presents the conclusions.

Quantum Hall and Shubnikov–de Haas effects in graphene

Graphene is a single layer of carbon atoms arranged in a honeycomb lattice, exhibiting a linear electronic dispersion near the Dirac points and a vanishing density of states at the charge neutrality point. Because of its semimetallic nature and exceptionally high mobility, graphene serves as an ideal starting point for our approach to 3D semimetals. The unconventional integer quantum Hall effect (IQHE), with plateaux at , has been experimentally observed even at room temperature11,14,15.

Our model for graphene extends the single-electron framework developed originally for quantum wells, successfully reproducing both diagonal and Hall magnetoconductivities as functions of gate voltage and magnetic field19,23. The characteristic plateau sequence arises naturally from Landau quantization in this formulation.

Although these systems differ in composition and electronic structure, they share two essential properties—low carrier density and high mobility—which enable them to reach the extreme quantum limit under experimentally accessible magnetic fields. In this regime, the characteristic quantized Hall response and accompanying magneto-oscillations become observable.

Discussion and conclusion

We have presented a unified theoretical framework for describing the integer quantum Hall effect (IQHE) in both two-dimensional and three-dimensional electron systems. The model builds on a single-electron Landau quantization scheme that successfully reproduces the magnetotransport properties of quantum wells, graphene, and 3D semimetals.

In 2D systems such as monolayer graphene, the unconventional Hall plateau sequence emerges naturally from the structure of the density of states and the single-particle quantization conditions19,23. The model captures both the Hall and diagonal magnetoconductivities as functions of magnetic field and gate voltage, in agreement with experimental observations11,14,15.

A major contribution of this work is the extension of the 2D framework to fully three-dimensional semimetals. While the 2D formalism is summarized briefly for completeness, the theoretical advances and validation presented here pertain specifically to the 3D case. The mechanism underlying the 3D quantization is universal: Landau-level discretization of the transverse motion, combined with a free longitudinal dispersion along the magnetic-field direction, produces the characteristic 3D quantization scale . This behaviour, originally anticipated in theoretical analyses of quantized transport in periodic systems4, has been observed experimentally in , , and 5–10. By demonstrating quantitative agreement with these measurements using material-specific parameters, the present model shows that the proposed approach constitutes a general and versatile framework for describing 3D quantum Hall states in low-density, high-mobility semimetals.

The present approach, based solely on single-particle physics, demonstrates that many features of both 2D and 3D quantum Hall phenomena can be described without invoking strong interaction effects. In fact, our simulations confirm this scaling and show that the plateau positions follow the expected quantization when the system enters the extreme quantum limit. This complements traditional topological and many-body perspectives, providing an alternative framework for understanding quantized magnetotransport in semimetals. Future work should investigate the microscopic origin of the cyclotron correction factor and its connection with the anisotropic effective mass tensor.

Overall, the model provides a unified description of quantum Hall physics across dimensions. It agrees with experimental observations in both 2D and 3D systems and may offer a useful tool for exploring quantum magnetotransport in novel semimetals. Furthermore, following our thermodynamic analysis of 2D systems30, the framework opens avenues for studying thermodynamic and transport properties in 3D quantum Hall regimes.

A remark on subharmonicity for symmetric Dirichlet forms
Kazuhiro Kuwae Rong Lei, Ludovico Marini

Abstract
We remove the local boundedness for ℰ α \mathscr{E}_{\alpha} -subharmonicity in the framework of (not necessarily strongly local) regular symmetric Dirichlet form ( ℰ , D ⁡ ( ℰ ) ) (\mathscr{E},D(\mathscr{E})) with α ≥ 0 \alpha\geq 0 and establish the stochastic characterization for ℰ \mathscr{E} -subharmonic functions without assuming the local boundedness.

Introduction

In this paper, we give a stochastic characterization of “subharmonic functions” without assuming its local boundedness in the framework of
general symmetric regular Dirichlet forms over locally compact separable metric spaces.
In classical setting, such a characterization between harmonic functions and (local) martingales can be explained by Itô’s formula for Brownian motions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. On
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, any harmonic functions in the ordinary sense are smooth, hence locally bounded (harmonicity in distributional sense is not necessarily locally bounded, see Example
6.1
). In
[
1
]
, Z.-Q. Chen generalized this characterization
for locally bounded
\mathscr{E}
-harmonic functions in the framework of regular
Dirichlet form
(\mathscr{E},D(\mathscr{E}))
associated with general
symmetric Markov (not necessarily diffusion) processes by using the
stochastic calculus for additive functionals.
Later on, this stochastic characterization for locally bounded
\mathscr{E}
-harmonic functions is extended by Ma–Zhu–Zhu
[
17
]
in the framework of non-symmetric Markov processes. In
[
5
]
, Z.-Q. Chen and the first named author
extend
[
1
]

for locally bounded
\mathscr{E}
-subharmonic functions.
In their results, the local boundedness of
\mathscr{E}
-(sub)harmonicity is assumed a priori, since their assumption
[
1
, (2.4)]
or
[
5
, (2.2)]
, (not
[
5
, (2.5)]
) for
\mathscr{E}
-subharmonic functions requires
the local boundedness for the equivalence among
[
1
, (2.4)]
,
[
5
, (2.2)]
and
[
5
, (2.5)]
.
This local boundedness is unnecessary at least for diffusion processes,
because the equivalence among
[
1
, (2.4)]
,
[
5
, (2.2)]
and
[
5
, (2.5)]
for local Dirichlet forms is automatically satisfied without assuming
the local boundedness.
Moreover, by
[
5
, Lemma 3.6]
, we see that any non-negative
\mathscr{E}
-superharmonic
function
u
in the domain of (extended) Dirichlet space is always superharmonic
in the sense of
[
5
, Definition 2.1]

without assuming its local boundedness.
This is the reason why we reformulate the results
for
[
1
,
5
]
without assuming the local boundedness.

The constitution of this paper is as follows: In Section
2
, we state the general setting in terms of Dirichlet forms
(\mathscr{E},D(\mathscr{E}))
associated to our symmetric Markov processes.
In Section
4
, we
prepare several conditions on functions and lemmas to formulate
the notion of
\mathscr{E}
-subharmonic functions in terms of
(\mathscr{E},D(\mathscr{E}))
, which is a natural extended notion of
subharmonicity in distributional sense in the classical case. In this formulation, we do not assume the local boundedness of
\mathscr{E}
-subharmonic functions, but we assume an integrability condition as in
[
5
, (2.5)]
.
In Section
4
, we define the two types of subharmonicity in stochastic sense as defined in
[
5
]
and prepare a condition (
4
)
instead of
[
1
, (2.5)]
or
[
5
, (2.3)]
. We clarified that

[
1
, (2.5)]
and
[
5
, (2.3)]
for locally bounded
u\in D(\mathscr{E})

satisfies (
4
) (Proposition
4.3
(4)).
At the last, we state the main results (Theorem
4.12
and Corollary
4.13
).
In Section
5
, we state a strong maximum principle in our setting, which is a slight extension of
[
5
, Theorem 2.11]
.
In Section
6
, we put a few examples.

Framework

Let
E
be a locally compact separable metric space and
\mathfrak{m}
a positive Radon measure with full support.
Let
(\mathscr{E},D(\mathscr{E}))

be a regular Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(E;\mathfrak{m})
.
Denote by
\mathscr{C}
, the special standard core of
(\mathscr{E},D(\mathscr{E}))
(see
[
9
, p. 6]
for the definition of special standard core
\mathscr{C}
).
It is well known that
(\mathscr{E},D(\mathscr{E}))
can be decomposed into three parts
\mathscr{E}(u,v)=\mathscr{E}^{(c)}(u,v)+\mathscr{E}^{(j)}(u,v)+\mathscr{E}^{(\kappa)}(u,v)
for

u,v\in D(\mathscr{E})\cap C_{c}(E)
, called
Beurling-Deny decomposition
.
More precisely, there exists a signed finite measure
\mu_{\langle u,v\rangle}^{(c)}
and positive Radon measures
J
on
E^{2}\setminus{\sf diag}
and
\kappa
on
E
such that

\mathscr{E}^{(c)}(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\langle u,v\rangle}^{(c)}(E)
,
\mathscr{E}^{(j)}(u,v)=\int_{E^{2}\setminus{\sf diag}}(u(x)-u(y)(v(x)-v(y))J(\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y)
, and

\mathscr{E}^{(\kappa)}(u,v)=\int_{E}u(x)v(x)\kappa(\mathrm{d}x)
for
u,v\in D(\mathscr{E})\cap C_{c}(E)
. Here
C_{c}(E)
denotes the family of continuous functions with compact support.
The measures
\mu_{\langle u,v\rangle}^{(c)}
,
\kappa
and any marginal measure of
J
do not charge any
\mathscr{E}
-exceptional set.
Such a decomposition, in fact, extends to general
u,v\in D(\mathscr{E})
and has a similar expression with
\mathscr{E}^{(j)}(u,v)=\int_{E^{2}\setminus{\sf diag}}(\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(y)(\tilde{v}(x)-\tilde{v}(y))J(\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y)
, and

\mathscr{E}^{(\kappa)}(u,v)=\int_{E}\tilde{u}(x)\tilde{v}(x)\kappa(\mathrm{d}x)
.
Here and throughout the rest of the paper,
\tilde{u}
denotes the
\mathscr{E}
-quasi-continuous
\mathfrak{m}
-version of
u\in D(\mathscr{E})
.
Let
(N(x,\mathrm{d}y),H)
be a Lévy system of the Hunt process
X
(see
[
9
, Theorem A.3.21]
).
Let
\mu_{H}
be the Revuz measure of the PCAF
H
.
Then
J
and
\kappa
are represented by

\displaystyle J(\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y)=\frac{1}{2}N(x,\mathrm{d}y)\mu_{H}(\mathrm{d}x),\quad\kappa(\mathrm{d}x)=N(x,\partial)\mu_{H}(\mathrm{d}x)

(see
[
9
, (5.3.6),(5.3.13)]
).

3

\mathscr{E}
-subharmonicity

We fix a regular Dirichlet form
(\mathscr{E},D(\mathscr{E}))
on
L^{2}(E;\mathfrak{m})
and a non-empty
open subset
D
of
E
. Let
(\mathscr{E}_{D},D(\mathscr{E}_{D}))
be
the part space on
L^{2}(D;\mathfrak{m})
defined by

D(\mathscr{E}_{D}):=\{u\in D(\mathscr{E})\mid\tilde{u}=0\;\mathscr{E}\text{-q.e.~on }E\setminus D\}
and

\mathscr{E}_{D}(u,v):=\mathscr{E}(u,v)
for
u,v\in D(\mathscr{E}_{D})
. Then
(\mathscr{E}_{D},D(\mathscr{E}_{D}))
becomes a regular Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(D;\mathfrak{m})
(see
[
9
, Theorem 4.4.3]
).
We set
D(\mathscr{E}_{D})_{c}:=\{u\in D(\mathscr{E}_{D})\mid{\rm supp}[u](\subset D)\text{ is compact}\}
.
Denote by
(\mathscr{E},D(\mathscr{E})_{e})
the extended Dirichlet space of
(\mathscr{E},D(\mathscr{E}))
defined by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}D(\mathscr{E})_{e}&=\{u\in L^{0}(E;\mathfrak{m})\mid\exists\{u_{n}\}\subset D(\mathscr{E}):\mathscr{E}\text{-Cauchy sequence}\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt\text{ such that }\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}=u\;\mathfrak{m}\text{-a.e.}\},\\
\mathscr{E}(u,u)&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathscr{E}(u_{n},u_{n}),\end{array}\right.

(see
[
9
, p. 40]
for details on extended Dirichlet space).
Any
u\in D(\mathscr{E})_{e}
admits an
\mathscr{E}
-quasi-continuous
\mathfrak{m}
-version
\tilde{u}
(see
[
9
, Theorem 2.1.7]
).
It is shown in
[
2
, Theorem 3.4.9]
that
D(\mathscr{E}_{D})_{e}=\{f\in D(\mathscr{E})_{e}\mid\tilde{f}=0\;\mathscr{E}\text{-q.e.~on }E\setminus D\}
.
Let
D(\mathscr{E})_{D,{\rm loc}}
(resp.
D(\mathscr{E})_{e,D,{\rm loc}}
) be the space of functions defined on
E
locally in the domain of

(\mathscr{E},D(\mathscr{E}))
(resp.

(\mathscr{E},D(\mathscr{E})_{e})
) on
D
by

\displaystyle D(\mathscr{E})_{D,{\rm loc}}:

\displaystyle=\{u\in L^{0}(E;\mathfrak{m})\mid\text{ for all }U\Subset D\text{ there exists }u_{U}\in D(\mathscr{E})

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt\text{ such that }u=u_{U}\;\mathfrak{m}\text{-a.e. on }U\},

\displaystyle D(\mathscr{E})_{e,D,{\rm loc}}:

\displaystyle=\{u\in L^{0}(E;\mathfrak{m})\mid\text{ for all }U\Subset D\text{ there exists }u_{U}\in D(\mathscr{E})_{e}

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt\text{ such that }u=u_{U}\;\mathfrak{m}\text{-a.e. on }U\}.

Here
U\Subset D
means that
U
is a relatively compact open set, and

L^{0}(E;\mathfrak{m})
denotes the family of
\mathfrak{m}
-measurable functions on
E
.
When
D=E
, we write
D(\mathscr{E})_{{\rm loc}}
(resp.
D(\mathscr{E})_{e,{\rm loc}}
) instead of

D(\mathscr{E})_{E,{\rm loc}}
(resp.
D(\mathscr{E})_{e,E,{\rm loc}}
). We see that
D(\mathscr{E})_{{\rm loc}}\subset D(\mathscr{E})_{D,{\rm loc}}

and
D(\mathscr{E})_{e,{\rm loc}}\subset D(\mathscr{E})_{e,D,{\rm loc}}
always hold.
Any
u\in D(\mathscr{E})_{e,D,{\rm loc}}
admits
an
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. version
\tilde{u}
of
u
, which is
\mathscr{E}
-quasi-continuous on
D
.
It is easy to see

D(\mathscr{E}_{D})_{{\rm loc}}\subset D(\mathscr{E})_{D,{\rm loc}}
and

D(\mathscr{E})_{D,{\rm loc}}\cap L_{{\rm loc}}^{\infty}(D;\mathfrak{m})\subset D(\mathscr{E}_{D})_{{\rm loc}}
(see
[
5
, pp. 5–6]
).

We further consider narrow subclasses of

D(\mathscr{E})_{e,D,{\rm loc}}

as follows:

\displaystyle D(\mathscr{E})_{e,D,{\rm loc}}^{{\dagger}}:

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in D(\mathscr{E})_{e,D,{\rm loc}}\,\left|\,\int_{U\times D\setminus{\sf diag}}(\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(y))^{2}J(\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y)<\infty\text{ for any }U\Subset D\right.\right\},

\displaystyle D(\mathscr{E})_{e,D,{\rm loc}}^{\diamond}:

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in D(\mathscr{E})_{e,D,{\rm loc}}\,\left|\,\int_{U\times(D\setminus V)}|\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(y)|J(\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y)<\infty\text{ for any }U\Subset V\Subset D\right.\right\}.

Then, we can easily see

D(\mathscr{E})_{e,D_{2},{\rm loc}}^{{\dagger}}\subset D(\mathscr{E})_{e,D_{1},{\rm loc}}^{{\dagger}}
and
D(\mathscr{E})_{e,D_{2},{\rm loc}}^{\diamond}\subset D(\mathscr{E})_{e,D_{1},{\rm loc}}^{\diamond}
for any non-empty open subsets
D_{1}\subset D_{2}
.
When
D=E
, we write
D(\mathscr{E})_{e,{\rm loc}}^{{\dagger}}

(resp.
D(\mathscr{E})_{e,{\rm loc}}^{\diamond}
) instead of

D(\mathscr{E})_{e,E,{\rm loc}}^{{\dagger}}
(resp.
D(\mathscr{E})_{e,E,{\rm loc}}^{\diamond}
).
We further set
D(\mathscr{E})_{D,{\rm loc}}^{{\dagger}}:=D(\mathscr{E})_{e,D,{\rm loc}}^{{\dagger}}\cap D(\mathscr{E})_{D,{\rm loc}}
and

D(\mathscr{E})_{D,{\rm loc}}^{\diamond}:=D(\mathscr{E})_{e,D,{\rm loc}}^{\diamond}\cap D(\mathscr{E})_{D,{\rm loc}}
. When
D=E
, we omit the symbol
D
as mentioned above.
It is easy to see
{\bf 1}_{D}\in D(\mathscr{E})_{D,{\rm loc}}^{{\dagger}}\subset D(\mathscr{E})_{D,{\rm loc}}^{\diamond}
.
It is known that
D(\mathscr{E})\subset D(\mathscr{E})_{e}\subset D(\mathscr{E})_{e,{\rm loc}}^{{\dagger}}
and

D(\mathscr{E})_{{\rm loc}}\cap L^{\infty}(E;\mathfrak{m})\subset D(\mathscr{E})_{{\rm loc}}^{{\dagger}}
(see
[
5
, Remark 2.4(ii),(iii)]
).
Similarly, we can get
D(\mathscr{E})\subset D(\mathscr{E})_{D,{\rm loc}}^{{\dagger}}
and

D(\mathscr{E})\subset D(\mathscr{E})_{D,{\rm loc}}^{{\dagger}}
is trivial and

\displaystyle D(\mathscr{E})_{D,{\rm loc}}\cap L^{\infty}(D;\mathfrak{m})

\displaystyle\subset D(\mathscr{E}_{D})_{{\rm loc}}\cap L^{\infty}(D;\mathfrak{m})

\displaystyle\subset D(\mathscr{E}_{D})_{{\rm loc}}^{{\dagger}}\;\,\text{(applying $(\mathscr{E}_{D},D(\mathscr{E}_{D}))$ instead of $(\mathscr{E},D(\mathscr{E}))$)}

\displaystyle\subset D(\mathscr{E})_{D,{\rm loc}}^{{\dagger}}.

The subclass
D(\mathscr{E})_{{\rm loc}}^{\diamond}
of

is essentially announced in

5

.
For
u\in D(\mathscr{E})_{e,D,{\rm loc}}\cap L^{\infty}_{{\rm loc}}(D;\mathfrak{m})

\displaystyle\int_{E\times(D\setminus V)}|\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(y)|J(\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y)<\infty

(3.1)

\displaystyle\int_{U\times(D\setminus V)}|\tilde{u}(y)|J(\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y)<\infty

for any

, because

[
5

By definition,

A function
T:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, is said to be a
(
generalized
)
normal contraction
if

\displaystyle T(0)=0\quad\text{ and }\quad|T(x)-T(y)|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N}|x_{k}-y_{k}|

for any
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})
,
y=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
When
N=1
, we call such
T:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
a
normal contraction
.
Note that
\phi(t):=t^{\sharp}:=0\lor t\land 1
,
\phi(t):=|t|
,
\phi(t):=t^{+}:=t\lor 0
and
\phi(t):=t^{-}:=(-t)\lor 0
are typical examples of normal contractions. More generally, any real-valued function

\phi_{{\varepsilon}}
on
\mathbb{R}
depending on
{\varepsilon}>0
such that
\phi_{{\varepsilon}}(t)=t
for
t\in[0,1]
,
-{\varepsilon}\leq\phi_{{\varepsilon}}(t)\leq 1+{\varepsilon}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and
0\leq\phi_{{\varepsilon}}(t)-\phi_{{\varepsilon}}(s)\leq t-s
whenever
s<t
, is also a normal contraction. The following lemmas
is easy to prove:

.

For

(2): From

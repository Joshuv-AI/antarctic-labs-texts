Electrical cardioversion for atrial fibrillation: nationwide trends in Denmark, 2010–2022
Authors: Lars Frost, Tanja Charlotte Frederiksen, Søren Paaske Johnsen, Emelia J Benjamin, Ole Dyg Pedersen, Ludovic Trinquart, Nicklas Vinter
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundElectrical cardioversion (ECV) is widely used for restoring sinus rhythm in atrial fibrillation (AF). However, population-level trends in use of ECV in individuals with AF remain poorly characterised.AimWe examined nationwide temporal trends in ECV utilisation between 2010 and 2022 in Denmark.MethodsWe conducted a registry-based cohort study using Danish nationwide health data. Two cohorts were defined: (1) individuals with prevalent or newly diagnosed AF were used to quantify annual ECV volume. (2) Individuals with newly diagnosed AF were used to calculate unadjusted and age-adjusted and sex-adjusted ECV rates per 1000 person-years during the first year after diagnosis.ResultsThe total number of ECVs per year increased almost fourfold between 2010 and 2022 (from 2204 to 8300). The 1-year age-adjusted and sex-adjusted rate of ECVs decreased from 449.5 per 1000 person-years in 2010 to 316.6 per 1000 person-years in 2021. Rates increased among individuals aged ≥75 years but declined among younger patients. When restricted to the first ECV procedure, analyses showed a twofold increase in first-time ECVs.ConclusionsThe increasing volume of ECVs reflects growing demand for rhythm control, while declining rates may suggest more targeted use of ECV. The findings highlight the need for resource planning and monitoring of procedural burden to support guideline implementation and equitable rhythm control delivery.

Introduction

Electrical cardioversion (ECV)1 is a well-established procedure for restoring sinus rhythm in individuals with atrial fibrillation (AF), with immediate success rates exceeding 90%.2 3 However, recurrence of AF after ECV is common, and repeated procedures are often necessary. In recent years, rhythm control strategies have gained renewed attention, particularly following the EAST-AFNET 4 trial reported in August 2020, which demonstrated that early rhythm control can reduce cardiovascular complications in patients with newly diagnosed AF.4 Despite these developments, little is known about how the use of ECV has evolved in real-world practice over time.

Changes in volume of procedures may reflect broader shifts in treatment strategies and access to rhythm control interventions. Repeated cardioversions may represent a significant burden, and insights into usage patterns can support more informed clinical decision-making and individual counselling. Data on the two complementary perspectives provide a detailed understanding of how clinical practice and the patient course may have evolved in response to evolving rhythm control strategies but may also reflect demographic shifts such as increased population at risk from ageing of population and increased survival in AF.5

Denmark provides an ideal setting for examining temporal trends in ECVs due to its comprehensive, nationwide health registries, which enable accurate tracking of procedures and diagnoses across the entire population. We quantified the total annual number of ECV procedures among individuals with prevalent or newly diagnosed AF to assess changes in overall procedural volume. We calculated the rate of ECVs per person-year among individuals with newly diagnosed AF to evaluate changes in the individual burden of repeated procedures.

Methods

Design and study cohorts

We conducted a nationwide, registry-based cohort study of all individuals with a hospital-based diagnosis of AF in Denmark. First, we identified a source population comprising all individuals with newly diagnosed AF between 1980 and 2022, as defined using national health registries (data sources detailed in online supplemental table S1). We included all individuals aged 18 years or above as we were interested in all ECVs in adults with AF. We excluded patients not living in Denmark for at least 5 years at AF diagnosis to ensure sufficient registry-based data at diagnosis.

From the source population, we derived two study cohorts (see flowchart in online supplemental figure S1). The first cohort included all individuals with a prevalent diagnosis or newly diagnosed AF between 2010 and 2022. Individuals entered the cohort on 1 January 2010, or on the date of their AF diagnosis if it occurred later (AF definition provided in online supplemental table S1). A second cohort included all individuals with newly diagnosed AF between 2010 and 2021. Individuals entered the cohort at day of diagnosis and were followed up to and including 2022. The first cohort was used to quantify annual procedural volume, while the second cohort was used to estimate the 1-year rate of ECVs.

Electrical cardioversion

ECV was identified from the Danish National Patient Registry using hospital-based procedures coded as direct current cardioversion for AF (online supplemental table S2). The procedure codes for ECV registered in the Danish National Patient Registry have previously been validated and found to have a high positive predictive value.6

Covariates

Covariates were included to provide descriptive characteristics of the two study populations over calendar time. Covariates included sex, age at diagnosis of AF, year of diagnosis of AF, history of comorbidities and social drivers.

Comorbidities included myocardial infarction, heart failure, valvular heart disease, any stroke, diabetes, hypertension, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, chronic kidney disease, dyslipidaemia and cancer. History of comorbidities was assessed on 1 January each year for the ECV volume cohort and on the day of AF diagnosis for individuals with newly diagnosed AF.

Social drivers, which included level of educational attainment and level of income, were assessed at study entry. Highest educational attainment was categorised into lower, medium and higher groups as per the International Standard Classification of Education (ISCED).7 We included early childhood, primary education and lower secondary education (ISCED 0–2) in the lower group, general upper secondary education and vocational upper secondary education (ISCED 3) in the medium group, and short-cycle tertiary, medium-length tertiary, bachelor’s-level educations or equivalent, second-cycle, master’s-level or equivalent, and PhD level (ISCED 5–8) in the higher group. ISCED 4 does not exist in Denmark. Total family income was the sum of business income, public transfers, private pensions, interest income, other property income and other non-classifiable income that could be attributed directly to the individual in the family. The amounts were before taxes for all individuals belonging to the same family. We categorised income into lower, medium, or higher based on the median value by each calendar year to account for potential inflation. Living alone was defined as individuals not living as couples in a registered partnership or not cohabitating. Individuals living alone were compared with individuals not living alone.

Subgroups and sensitivity analyses

We conducted predefined subgroup analyses to explore potential differential temporal trends in the use of ECV. For the annual ECV volume, subgroups were defined by sex (men vs women) and age at diagnosis (<65, 65–74 and ≥75 years). For the 1-year ECV rate in individuals with newly diagnosed AF subgroups included sex (men vs women), age at diagnosis (<65, 65–74 and ≥75 years), history of heart failure (yes vs no), educational attainment (lower, medium, higher), average annual family income (lower, medium, higher), living alone (yes/no) and administrative region.

We conducted sensitivity analyses restricted to first-ever ECVs to assess whether repeated procedures influenced observed trends. In relation to ECV volume, we included only procedures recorded as the patient’s first-ever ECV, excluding individuals with prior procedures before 2010. For the 1-year ECV rate, the first procedure after AF diagnosis was included.

Identification of ECV procedures relied on procedure codes that remained unchanged across the transition from Danish National Registry of Patients V.2 to Danish National Registry of Patients V.3 in 2019. Registry transition primarily affected the structure and recording of hospital contacts. To assess potential influence of changes in registration practice, we performed a sensitivity analysis that applied an alternative counting rule of one ECV per patient per day to evaluate potential double-counting related to changes in registration.

Statistical analyses

We determined the annual number of ECV procedures performed using the cohorts of individuals with incident and prevalent AF. Because our aim was to quantify procedural volume, the same individual could undergo ECV procedures across multiple calendar years. We examined temporal trends by modelling calendar year as a restricted cubic spline with three knots in a Poisson regression model. We evaluated annual ECV counts by subgroups (sex and age) and tested for differential temporal trends by including an interaction term between calendar year and subgroup indicators in the Poisson regression models.

Using the cohort of individuals with incident AF, we calculated the unadjusted 1-year ECV rates after AF per 1000 person-years by year of AF diagnosis. Follow-up began at the date of AF diagnosis and ended at death, emigration or 31 December 2022. We selected a 1-year window to capture early rhythm-control strategies and initial treatment decisions following AF diagnosis, while ensuring uniform follow-up duration across calendar years for temporal trend analyses. We also examined the distribution of ECV procedures (single vs multiple procedures) during the first year after diagnosis across calendar years.

Temporal trends in crude rates were assessed using Poisson regression, with year of AF diagnosis modelled as a restricted cubic spline and log(person-time) as an offset.

We calculated age-standardised and sex-standardised ECV rates per 1000 person-years over the first year of follow-up after the date of the AF diagnosis, using direct standardisation to the 2010 population. To evaluate trends in standardised rates, we used Poisson regression with year of AF diagnosis modelled as a restricted cubic spline, adjusting for age group and sex, and including log(person-time) as an offset. In both unadjusted and adjusted models, we tested for differential temporal trends across subgroups by including an interaction term in the regression models. Analyses were performed in Stata (StataCorp. 2023: Release V.18.5, College Station, Texas: StataCorp LLC).

Results

Our cohort included 362 547 unique individuals with prevalent or newly diagnosed AF between 2010 and 2022, of whom 160 099 were women and 202 448 were men (flowchart in online supplemental figure S1). Temporal trends in procedural volume of ECVs

The total number of performed ECVs in Danish hospitals increased 3.8-fold from 2204 in 2010 to 8300 in 2022 (online supplemental table S3 and figure 1A). The number of ECVs increased gradually up to 2020, followed by an intensified increase (p for non-linear trend=0.02). Women had less cardioversions than men, but the increase in total number of ECVs over time in women and men were parallel (p for interaction=0.35, figure 1B). The increase in ECVs among individuals aged ≥75 years was more pronounced than in their younger counterparts (p for interaction <0.001); figure 1C.

Temporal overall trend (A) and trend by sex (B) and age (C) in annual procedural volume of electrical cardioversions between years 2010 and 2022 among individuals with incident and prevalent atrial fibrillation.

Temporal trends in 1-year rates of ECVs in newly diagnosed individuals with AF

Among individuals with newly diagnosed AF, the 1-year rate of ECVs decreased from 449.5 per 1000 person-years in 2010 to 311.5 per 1000 person-years in 2021. When adjusting for age and sex, the rate decreased from 449.5 per 1000 person-years in 2010 to 316.6 per 1000 person-years in 2021 (online supplemental table S4 and figure 2). When stratified by sex, the rate decreased among men but not among women (p interaction <0.001), but when adjusting for age, we found no differential temporal trends between sexes (p interaction=0.89). When stratified by age group, the rate decreased among individuals aged <65 years, remained roughly stable among individuals aged 65–74, and increased among individuals aged ≥75 years (p interaction <0.001). The differential temporal trends between age groups persisted when adjusting for sex (p interaction<0.001).

Temporal trends in 1-year rate of electrical cardioversions (ECV) per 1000 person-years by year of diagnosis among individuals with incident atrial fibrillation. The subgroup analyses by sex adjusted for age only and the analyses by age adjusted for sex only.

In our age-adjusted and sex-adjusted analysis, the rate of ECVs decreased among individuals with high family income while it remained roughly stable among individuals with medium or lower income (p interaction=0.006). We found no statistical evidence of differential temporal trends by history of HF, educational attainment or living alone (online supplemental figure S2). Temporal patterns were broadly similar across Danish administrative regions, although formal interaction tests suggested modest statistical heterogeneity; the national pattern did not appear to be driven by a single region (online supplemental figures S3a and S3b).

When restricting ECVs to the first performed procedure, the temporal trend in the procedural volume showed a more than twofold increase between 2010 and 2022 (figure 3). For the same period, the rates of first-time ECVs per 1000 person-years increased almost twofold (figure 3).

Distribution of repeated ECV procedures

The proportion of patients receiving a single ECV increased from 6.3% in 2010 to 10.7% in 2021, whereas the proportion receiving repeated ECV (≥2 procedures) decreased from 7.2% to 6.2% over the same period (online supplemental table S5), indicating a shift towards a higher proportion of patients treated with a single ECV and a lower proportion undergoing repeated procedures within the first year after diagnosis.

Alternative counting rule

Application of an alternative counting rule of one ECV per patient per day yielded results similar to the main analysis (online supplemental figures S4a and S4b). Registration of more than one ECV on the same day was rare, and exclusion of such observations did not meaningfully change temporal trends in procedural volume.

Discussion

Principal findings

In this nationwide study of ECV use among individuals with AF the total number of ECV procedures increased almost fourfold between 2010 and 2022. Subgroup analyses within this cohort showed that the increase was more pronounced among individuals aged ≥75 years, while trends were similar for men and women. Among individuals with newly diagnosed AF, the age-adjusted and sex-adjusted 1-year rate of ECVs declined over time. Subgroup analyses within individuals with newly diagnosed AF revealed diverging trends by age, with increasing rates among individuals aged ≥75 years and decreasing rates among younger individuals. Sensitivity analyses restricted to the first performed ECV showed a twofold increase in both the number and rate of procedures over the study period. The analyses suggest that more individuals received an initial ECV after AF diagnosis, while repeated procedures became less frequent.

Explanation of findings

Several factors may help explain the observed temporal trends in ECV use among individuals with AF. The fourfold increase in total procedures over the study period likely reflects a growing population of individuals diagnosed with AF, consistent with demographic ageing and increased detection,8 9 in conjunction with increased survival with AF and other predisposing conditions.5 10

Broader implementation of direct oral anticoagulants may have facilitated scheduling of ECVs, as these drugs simplify peri-procedural anticoagulation compared with vitamin K antagonists.11

The decline in age-adjusted and sex-adjusted ECV rates during the first year after AF diagnosis suggests that, although more individuals undergo ECV, fewer receive repeated procedures. The reduction in repeated procedures may reflect improved rhythm maintenance following initial cardioversion, increased use of catheter ablation12 13 or evolving clinical preferences favouring rate control in selected patients. Our sensitivity analysis supports this interpretation, showing a twofold increase in first-time ECVs but not in overall patient-level rates.

Since 2010 clinical guidelines for management of AF have gradually placed greater emphasis on symptom control,14–16 which may include ECV to address symptomatic heart rate irregularity or tachycardia due to insufficient heart rate control by AV-blocking agents. The evidence base for superiority of rhythm control over heart rate control has particularly strengthened in the latter part of the study period,17–19 and an increased capacity for catheter ablation of AF in Denmark may have contributed to the rise in first-time ECVs, while potentially reducing the need for repeat procedures.13

Subgroup analyses revealed diverging trends by age. ECV use increased among individuals aged ≥75 years, while rates declined among younger individuals. The findings may reflect increasing clinical attention to rhythm control in older adults. The parallel trends by sex in ECV volume and absence of sex-based differences in adjusted 1-year rates suggest consistent trends in practice patterns by sex. We did not observe a more pronounced use of ECVs over time in individuals with heart failure, despite growing evidence supporting rhythm control in this subgroup.18 The finding may reflect clinical caution in selecting patients with heart failure for ECV.

Relation to previous literature

Our findings align well with a study based on administrative data from hospitals in Boston, USA, that recently reported a 4.5-fold increase in the total number of ECV procedures from 2000 to 2022.12 The same study reported that the first-time ECV rate remained virtually unchanged over the study period, which points at the increasing prevalence of individuals with AF as being the main driver of the increasing volume of ECVs.12 Taken together, the literature in relation to the epidemiology of ECV is limited.

Implications

The observed increase in ECV volume mainly reflects the growing prevalence of individuals with AF but may also reflect a growing demand for rhythm control interventions in AF management. This trend also partly aligns with evolution of clinical guidelines that increasingly prioritise rhythm control, particularly early rhythm control, as a strategy to reduce cardiovascular complications and improve long-term outcomes.4 The fourfold increase in total ECVs presents a challenge for resource allocation. Hospitals must ensure adequate capacity, staffing and infrastructure to support timely and safe delivery of ECV, particularly in older adults where usage has increased most markedly. Nurse-led ECV programmes, shown to be safe and effective, may offer scalable solutions in structured AF clinics.

From a clinical perspective, the twofold rise in first-time ECVs suggests that more patients are being offered rhythm control early in their disease course, potentially reflecting guideline-driven changes following the EAST-AFNET 4 trial.4 The combination of declining overall patient-level ECV rates and increasing first-time ECV use warrants careful interpretation. Additional analyses of the incident AF cohort showed an increase in the proportion of patients receiving a single ECV alongside a decline in repeated procedures within the first year after diagnosis. These findings support the interpretation that the observed decline in overall patient-level ECV rates reflects reduced use of repeat procedures. Several factors may contribute to such a pattern, including evolving treatment strategies, improved rhythm maintenance or earlier transition to alternative approaches such as catheter ablation. The findings highlight the need for individualised rhythm control plans that balance procedural burden with clinical benefit.

Finally, the findings emphasise the importance of integrating registry-based surveillance with clinical trials to inform healthcare planning and resource allocation. As randomised trials may not capture all relevant subgroups or long-term outcomes, observational data can inform nuanced decisions about rhythm control modalities, including hybrid strategies combining ECV, antiarrhythmic drugs and ablation. Future research should explore optimal rhythm control sequencing and patient selection to maximise benefit while minimising procedural burden.

Limitations

This study has limitations. First, although the procedure codes for ECVs in the Danish National Patient Registry have high positive predictive value,6 the completeness of coding is unknown. Second, individuals diagnosed with AF at a young age could advance educational attainment after diagnosis; however, the number of individuals diagnosed with AF below the age of 30 years was low. Third, we had no information on clinical details such as AF subtype, symptom burden, echocardiographic findings or procedural outcomes. Fourth, the 1-year follow-up window in the patient-level cohort excluded longer term rhythm control strategies and may have underestimated the total ECV use in some individuals. Fifth, changes in registration practices over time, including the transition from Danish National Patient Registry V.2 to Danish National Patient Registry V.3, could potentially account for part of the observed increase in ECV use in the later study period. Sixth, the EAST-AFNET 4 trial was published in August 20204 and may have transformed into limited impact on observed clinical practice, because our follow-up ended in December 2022.

Observed trends over time in clinical practice are likely multifactorial and driven by interacting components such as innovation, clinical guidelines, resources and patient preferences, but it was outside the scope of the study to causally explain the observed trends. Our findings originated from a universal tax-financed healthcare system with individuals of European ancestry, and the results may not be generalisable to healthcare systems with different structures, reimbursement models or access to rhythm control interventions and individuals of other ancestries.

Conclusion

In this nationwide study, we observed substantial changes in the use of ECV among individuals with AF over a 13-year period. The total number of procedures increased markedly, reflecting a growing demand for rhythm control. In contrast, rates of ECVs during the first year after diagnosis declined, driven by reduced use of repeat procedures. The findings may indicate a shift towards more targeted use of ECV, aligned with evolving rhythm control strategies and guideline recommendations. Our study underscores the importance of monitoring both procedural volume and individual-level utilisation to inform clinical decision-making, optimise resource allocation and ensure equitable access to rhythm control interventions.

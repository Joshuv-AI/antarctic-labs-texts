Abstract
. The main purpose of this paper is to define the net logarithmic tangent sheaf , as a generalization of the logarithmic tangent sheaf introduced by P. Deligne, over the field of complex numbers, and prove some basic properties and give some applications. The generalization is valid for the pairs of the smooth complete intersection variety and its complete intersecting subvariety. As applications, we investigate the locus of net logarithmic tangent sheaves on a smooth cubic surface in the corresponding moduli space of semistable sheaves.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and let
D
be a reduced and effective divisor on
X
with simple normal crossings. Ever since P. Deligne defined the logarithmic sheaf
\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)
as the sheaf of differential
1
-forms on
X
with logarithmic poles along
D
in
[
6
]
, there has been a huge amount of works on this object with various aspects, including the Terao conjecture and the Torelli properties; see
[
19
]
and
[
7
,
10
]
. A decade later, the notion was extended by K. Saito in
[
21
]
with
D
allowing any reduced singularities and non-normal crossings.

One of the seminal work about the Torelli property of logarithmic sheaves, is the one by I. Dolgachev and M. Kapranov in
[
7
]
, where they prove that a generic point in a certain moduli space of stable sheaves on the projective space is given as the logarithmic vector bundle. More specifically, they showed that a generic arrangement of
m
hyperplanes which do not osculate a normal rational curve of degree
n
was determined by the logarithmic sheaf
\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(\log D)
if
m\geq 2n+3
. In other words, the natural correspondence of
D
with
\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(\log D)
defines a map

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Phi:&\mathbf{F}&\longrightarrow&\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\\[8.5359pt]
&D&\mapsto&\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(\log D)\end{array}

which is now shown to be
generically injective
, where
\mathbf{F}
is the family of divisors
D=D_{1}+\cdots+D_{m}
and
\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
is the moduli space of stable vector bundles of rank
n
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with its Chern classes
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
given by Chern polynomial
(1-t)^{n+1-m}
; this kind of problem is called the
the Torelli problem
. This result was later extended to
m\geq n+2
by J. Vallés in
[
24
]
, and generalized to arrangements of higher degree hypersurfaces by E. Angelini in
[
1
,
2
]
. Not only arrangements of hypersurfaces with simple normal crossings in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, but there are also numerous studies exploring various generalized settings; see
[
3
,
14
,
16
]
. Quite recently, two main generalizations were proposed in
[
5
,
8
]
by generalizing the corresponding Poincaré residue map, basically encoding more information on the singularity of
D
into the generalized logarithmic sheaf. This line of research is important in the sense that it can suggest a method to describe moduli space of stable sheaves on
X
, specially non-generic boundary points that are not realized as the logarithmic sheaves in the original sense. As a further extension of this line of works, the work in progress in
[
15
]
suggests the notion of the
net logarithmic sheaf

\mathring{\Omega}_{X}^{1}(\log D)
on
X
associated to any divisor
D
, possibly non-reduced.

In this article, we introduce the dual notion of the net logarithmic sheaf
\mathring{\Omega}_{X}^{1}(\log D)
. For two complete intersection subvarieties
X=\mathbb{V}(F_{1},\ldots,F_{r})
and
Y=\mathbb{V}(G_{1},\ldots,G_{s})
in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
such that
X\cap Y
is also a complete intersection, one can consider the gradient map

\nabla~:~\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(1)^{\oplus(N+1)}\longrightarrow\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(f_{i})\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s}\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(g_{j})\right).

Then the
net logarithmic tangent sheaf

\cal{T}_{X}(Y;\mathbb{P}^{N})
is defined to be
\ker(\nabla)\otimes\cal{O}_{X}
, modulo its torsion. It is a generalization of the logarithmic tangent sheaf
\cal{T}_{X}(-\log D)\cong\left(\Omega^{1}_{X}(\log D)\right)^{\vee}
, when
X
and
Y
are hypersurfaces with possible singularities in
D=X\cap Y
. Note that the sheaf
\cal{T}_{X}(Y;\mathbb{P}^{N})
is torsion-free on
X
with rank
\dim(X)-\mathrm{codim}(Y)+1
, not necessarily reflexive. If we consider the case
X=\mathbb{P}^{N}
, then
\cal{T}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(Y;\mathbb{P}^{N})\otimes\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(-1)
is the logarithmic tangent sheaf associated with a complete intersection
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
in the sense of
[
9
]
. Then we establish its general properties, including the following; see Proposition
2.5
.

•

Let
X
and
Y
be smooth hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{P}^{N}
, and let
D=X\cap Y
be a reduced and effective divisor of
X
. Then the net logarithmic tangent sheaf
\cal{T}_{X}(Y;\mathbb{P}^{N})
fits into the exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{T}_{X}(Y;\mathbb{P}^{N})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{T}_{X}(-\log D)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathcal{T}\!\mathrm{or}^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(\mathrm{coker}(\nabla),\cal{O}_{X})\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

Note that
\cal{T}_{X}(Y;\mathbb{P}^{N})
also fits into the dual Poincaré residue sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{T}_{X}(Y;\mathbb{P}^{N})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{T}_{X}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{N}_{X,Y}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0,

where a sheaf
\cal{N}_{X,Y}
is isomorphic to a twisted Jacobian ideal
\cal{J}_{D}(D)
modulo torsion; see Remark

. Hence, one can interpret
\cal{T}_{X}(Y;\mathbb{P}^{N})
as a natural refinement of the usual logarithmic tangent sheaf.

Next, we investigate the
\mu

and

with

X

To give a more explicit description on the closure of the set of such logarithmic vector bundles

in the corresponding moduli space, we focus on the following two cases:

In either case, we have a morphism from the dual projective space

The closure of the set of vector bundles

2.
Preliminaries

Let
Y=Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{s}
be a reduced and effective divisor in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
with each
Y_{j}=\mathbb{V}(G_{j})
irreducible for
G_{j}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\dots,x_{N}]
and
g_{j}=\deg(G_{j})
. Assume further that
\cap_{j=1}^{s}Y_{j}
is a complete intersection of codimension
s
in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
. The kernel of the gradient map

\xi_{Y}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\nabla{G_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
\nabla{G_{s}}\end{array}\right):\quad\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(1)^{\oplus(N+1)}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s}\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(g_{j})

can be considered as a generalization of the logarithmic tangent sheaf, associated to the complete intersection
\cap_{j=1}^{s}Y_{j}
. We denoted it by
\cal{T}_{Y,\mathbb{P}^{N}}:=\mathrm{ker}(\xi_{Y})
; refer
[
9
]
. Note that the sheaf
\cal{T}_{Y,\mathbb{P}^{N}}
is reflexive with
\mathrm{rank}(\cal{T}_{Y,\mathbb{P}^{N}})=N-r+1
.

Let
X=X_{1}\cap\dots\cap X_{r}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a smooth complete intersection of dimension
\dim(X)=N-r
, defined by
r
hypersurfaces
X_{i}=\mathbb{V}(F_{i})
for homogeneous polynomials
F_{1},\ldots,F_{r}\subset\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{N}]
with
f_{i}=\deg(F_{i})
. We further assume that
D=X\cap Y
be a complete intersection of codimension
r+s
in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
. Then one can consider the logarithmic tangent sheaf
\cal{T}_{X\cap Y,\mathbb{P}^{N}}
, associated to the complete intersection
X\cap Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
, fitting into an exact sequence

(1)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{T}_{X\cap Y,\mathbb{P}^{N}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(1)^{\oplus(N+1)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi_{X\cap Y}}}{{\xrightarrow{\hskip 42.67912pt}}}\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(f_{i})\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s}\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(g_{j})\right),

where the map
\xi_{X\cap Y}
is given by the Jacobian matrix

\xi_{X\cap Y}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\nabla{F_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
\nabla{F_{r}}\\
\nabla{G_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
\nabla{G_{s}}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{\partial{F_{1}}}{\partial{x_{0}}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial{F_{1}}}{\partial{x_{N}}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{\partial{F_{r}}}{\partial{x_{0}}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial{F_{r}}}{\partial{x_{N}}}\\
\frac{\partial{G_{1}}}{\partial{x_{0}}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial{G_{1}}}{\partial{x_{N}}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{\partial{G_{s}}}{\partial{x_{0}}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial{G_{s}}}{\partial{x_{N}}}\end{array}\right).

Then
\cal{T}_{X\cap Y,\mathbb{P}^{N}}
is a reflexive sheaf on
\mathbb{P}^{N}
with
\mathrm{rank}(\cal{T}_{X\cap Y,\mathbb{P}^{N}})=N-r-s+1
. Set

\cal{A}_{X\cap Y}:=\mathrm{Im}(\xi_{X\cap Y}),\quad\cal{B}_{X\cap Y}:=\mathrm{coker}(\xi_{X\cap Y})

to get the following two exact sequences:

(2)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{T}_{X\cap Y,\mathbb{P}^{N}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(1)^{\oplus(N+1)}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{A}_{X\cap Y}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0

and

(3)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{A}_{X\cap Y}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(f_{i})\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(g_{i})\right)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{B}_{X\cap Y}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

Tensoring (
2
) by
\cal{O}_{X}
, we get

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathcal{T}\!\mathrm{or}^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}^{N}}}\left(\cal{A}_{X\cap Y},\cal{O}_{X}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cal{T}_{X\cap Y,\mathbb{P}^{N}}\otimes\cal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{X}(1)^{\oplus(N+1)}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{A}_{X\cap Y}\otimes{\cal{O}_{X}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

Definition 2.1
.

We set
\cal{T}_{X}(Y;\mathbb{P}^{N}):=\mathrm{coker}(\alpha)
, i.e.

\cal{T}_{X}(Y;\mathbb{P}^{N}):={\raisebox{3.00003pt}{$\cal{T}_{X\cap Y,\mathbb{P}^{N}}\otimes\cal{O}_{X}$}\left/\raisebox{-3.00003pt}{$\mathcal{T}\!\mathrm{or}^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}^{N}}}\left(\cal{A}_{X\cap Y},\cal{O}_{X}\right)$}\right.},

and call it the
net logarithmic tangent sheaf
on
X
, associated to
Y
, or equivalently to
X\cap Y
. Then it is a torsion-free sheaf on
X
with rank
\dim(X)-s+1
, not necessarily reflexive. Note that
\cal{T}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(Y;\mathbb{P}^{N})\otimes\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(-1)
with
r=0
is the logarithmic sheaf on
\mathbb{P}^{N}
associated to a complete intersection
Y
in the sense of
[
9
]
.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that the sheaf
\cal{T}_{X\cap Y,\mathbb{P}^{N}}
depends only on the complete intersection ideal
(F_{1},\dots,F_{r},G_{1},\dots,G_{s})\subset\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\dots,x_{N}]
, not on the choice of its minimal generators. In particular, the sheaf
\cal{T}_{X}(Y;\mathbb{P}^{N})
depends on
X
and
X\cap Y
, i.e. for another complete intersection
Y^{\prime}
such that
X\cap Y=X\cap Y^{\prime}
we have
\cal{T}_{X}(Y;\mathbb{P}^{N})\cong\cal{T}_{X}(Y^{\prime};\mathbb{P}^{N})
.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
\mathbb{P}^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a linear subspace and assume that it is defined by
x_{n+1}=\dots=x_{N}=0
by a change of variables. For a smooth hypersurface
Y=\mathbb{V}(G)
of degree
d
in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
, one can have the following exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{T}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}\cap Y}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(1)^{\oplus(N+1)}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(1)^{\oplus(N-n)}\oplus\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(d),

which induces an exact sequence

(4)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{T}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}\cap Y}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(1)^{\oplus(n+1)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{M}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cal{I}_{Z^{\prime},\mathbb{P}^{N}}(d)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0,

where
\mathrm{M}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial G}{\partial x_{0}}&\dots&\frac{\partial G}{\partial x_{n}}\end{pmatrix}
and
Z^{\prime}
is the degeneracy locus of
\mathrm{M}
. By tensoring the sequence (
4
) by
\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
, one gets

(5)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{T}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(Y;\mathbb{P}^{N})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1)^{\oplus(n+1)}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{Z^{\prime},\mathbb{P}^{N}}(d)\otimes\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

Note that the sheaf
\cal{I}_{Z^{\prime},\mathbb{P}^{N}}(d)\otimes\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
is an extension of
\cal{I}_{Z^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)
by
\cal{O}_{Z^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{P}^{n}}
and so we get

\mathcal{H}om_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(\cal{I}_{Z^{\prime},\mathbb{P}^{N}}(d)\otimes\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}},\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}})\cong\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-d).

Applying the functor
\mathcal{H}om_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-,\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}})
to the sequence (
5
), we get

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-d)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-1)^{\oplus(n+1)}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{T}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(Y;\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{E}xt_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z^{\prime},\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)\otimes\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}},\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}})\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

Note that the net logarithmic sheaf
\mathring{\Omega}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{1}(\log(\mathbb{P}^{n}\cap Y))
is introduced in
[
15
]
; indeed, the sheaf is identified with the kernel of the map
\sigma
. Hence we can say that the net logarithmic tangent sheaf
\cal{T}_{X}(Y;\mathbb{P}^{N})
is the proper counterpart of the net logarithmic sheaf
\mathring{\Omega}^{1}_{X}(\log(X\cap Y))
in the dual sense. Note that the net logarithmic sheaf
\mathring{\Omega}_{X}^{1}(\log D)
is defined over any smooth variety
X
associated to any effective divisor
D
, and so it would be interesting to extend the notion
\cal{T}_{X}(Y;\mathbb{P}^{N})
to non-complete intersection subvariety
X
.

Remark 2.4
.

As in Remark
2.3
, consider a linear subspace
\mathbb{P}^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
. Assume that the complete intersections

and

\mathbb{P}^{N}

Proposition 3.8
.

The logarithmic vector bundles
\cal{T}_{S}(-\log S\cap H)
in
\mathbf{M}_{S}^{\cal{H}}(0,9)
are characterized as the vector bundles
[\cal{F}]\in\mathbf{M}_{S}^{\cal{H}}(0,9)
satisfying the condition that
\cal{F}(1)
is globally generated with
\mathrm{h}^{0}(\cal{F}(1))=3
.

Proof.

We have an exact sequence

(40)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{S}(-3)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cal{O}_{S}(-1)^{\oplus 3}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{F}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0

for
\cal{F}:=\cal{T}_{S}(H;\mathbb{P}^{3})\cong\cal{T}_{S}(-\log D)

smooth, from which we get that
\cal{F}(1)
is globally generated with
\mathrm{h}^{0}(\cal{F}(1))=3
. Conversely, let
[\cal{F}]\in\mathbf{M}_{S}^{\cal{H}}(0,9)
be a stable vector bundle, where
\cal{F}(1)

\mathrm{h}^{0}(\cal{F}(1))=3
so that it fits into the extension (
40
). Noticing
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\cal{O}_{S}(2))\cong\mathrm{H}^{0}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(2))\cong\mathbb{C}^{\oplus 10}

\nabla
is defined by
(Q_{0},Q_{1},Q_{2})
a triple of quadratic forms in

\partial~:~\mathrm{H}^{0}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(3))\times\mathbb{C}\langle\partial_{0},\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\partial_{3}\rangle\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{0}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(2))

defined by derivation, where
\partial_{j}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}

\mathbb{C}\langle\partial_{0},\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\partial_{3}\rangle

\partial

14

14

An upper bound of the lower tail of the mass of balls under the the critical 2 ​ d 2d stochastic heat flow
Makoto Nakashima

Abstract
We study the critical two-dimensional stochastic heat flow 𝒵 t ϑ \mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta} , recently constructed as the scaling limit of directed polymers in a random environment and as the limit of the solution to a mollified stochastic heat equation. Focusing on the mass of balls 𝒵 t ϑ ​ ( B r ​ ( 0 ) , B r ​ ( a ) ) \mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(B_{r}(0),B_{r}(a)) ( a ∈ ℝ 2 a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2} , r > 0 r>0 ), we establish an upper bound on its lower tail. As a consequence, we prove the integrability of the logarithm of 𝒵 t ϑ ​ ( B r ​ ( 0 ) , B r ​ ( a ) ) \mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(B_{r}(0),B_{r}(a)) and its strict positivity. These results provide partial answers to open questions concerning the local behavior of 𝒵 t ϑ \mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta} .

Introduction and main results

The Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) equation is a stochastic partial differentialequation that describes the evolution of a randomly growing interface:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h=\nu\Delta h+\frac{\lambda}{2}\left|\nabla h\right|^{2}+\dot{\mathcal{W}},

(KPZ)

where
\dot{\mathcal{W}}
denotes a space-time white noise on
[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

[
23
]
. The equation is ill-posed due to the term
|\nabla h|^{2}
, which involves the square of a distribution. A standard approach to overcome this difficulty is the Cole-Hopf transform, as introduced in
[
2
]
.

In
[
23
]
, the formal transformation

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\exp\left(\frac{\lambda}{2\nu}h(t,x)\right)

was introduced, along with the associated stochastic heat equation with multiplicative noise

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=\nu\Delta u+\frac{\lambda}{2\nu}u\dot{\mathcal{W}}..

(SHE)

In
[
2
]
, Bertini and Giacomin provided a rigorous mathematical treatment of this transformation in one spatial dimension, particularly in the context of the weakly asymmetric simple exclusion process and the solid-on-solid (SOS) process. They first considered the mollified stochastic heat equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u^{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta u^{\varepsilon}-\lambda u^{\varepsilon}\dot{\mathcal{W}}^{\varepsilon},

(
\text{SHE}_{\varepsilon}
)

with initial condition
u_{0}(x)>0
, where the mollified noise is defined by
\dot{\mathcal{W}}^{\varepsilon}(t,x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}j_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\dot{\mathcal{W}}(t,y)\text{\rm d}y
for a symmetric probability density
j\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
with
j(x)=j(-x)
and
j^{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-1}j\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)
. Then, Bertini and Giacomin showed that
h^{\varepsilon}(t,x):=\log u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)
satisfies a mollified version of the KPZ equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h^{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta h^{\varepsilon}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\left(|\nabla h^{\varepsilon}|^{2}-J_{\varepsilon}(0)\right)+\dot{\mathcal{W}},

(KPZ
ε
)

where
J_{\varepsilon}(y)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int j(x)j(y-x)\text{\rm d}x
.
Furthermore, they proved that
u^{\varepsilon}
converges almost surely and in

to

, the solution of (

.

It is also known

that if

is strictly positive on

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}\Rightarrow u\overset{\text{C-H trans.}}{\leadsto}h^{\varepsilon}\Rightarrow\mathfrak{h}.

(Cole-Hopf)

s=0
.

Proposition 3.2
.

For
\alpha>2
and
r>0
, there exists a constant
C_{\alpha,r,t}>0
such that for large
N\geq 2
,
\{\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(N,j)}(r):n=\frac{N}{2},\dots,N,j=0,\dots,\frac{N}{15}\}
are pairwise disjoint.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the followings:

(1)

The maximum of the
x
-coordinate of
\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(0)}
is strictly smaller than minimum of the
x
coordinate of
\mathrm{U}_{n+1}^{(0)}
.

(2)

The maximum of the
y
coordinate of
\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(0)}
is strictly smaller than minimum of the
y
coordinate of
\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(1)}
.

For
(1)
, we denote by
x_{n,-}^{(0)}(s)
and
x_{n,+}^{(0)}(s)
the minimum and the maximum of the
x
-th coordinate of
\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(0)}
at time
s
, respectively.

Then, we can see that

\displaystyle x_{n+1,-}^{(0)}(s)-x_{n,+}^{(0)}(s)=C_{\alpha,r,t}((n+1)^{\alpha}-n^{\alpha})s-2(2s)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+\frac{7r}{10N}\quad\text{for }0\leq s\leq b_{N}

(3.3)

holds.
It is easy to see that

\displaystyle C_{\alpha,r,t}((n+1)^{\alpha}-n^{\alpha})s-2(2s)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+\frac{7r}{10N}

\displaystyle\geq C_{\alpha,r,t}\beta n^{\alpha-1}s-2(2s)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+\frac{7r}{10N}\geq-c_{\alpha}\left(C_{\alpha,r,t}\alpha\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}\frac{1}{N}+\frac{7r}{10N}>0

for
n\geq\frac{N}{2}
if
C_{\alpha,r,t}>0
is large enough, where
c_{\alpha}>0
is a constant depending only on
\alpha
.

Moreover, we can see that

\displaystyle x_{n+1,-}^{(0)}(b_{N})-x_{n,+}^{(0)}(b_{N})

\displaystyle\geq C_{\alpha,r,t}\beta n^{\alpha-1}b_{N}-2(2s)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+\frac{7r}{10N}

\displaystyle\geq CC_{\alpha,r,t}\geq 20t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

for
C_{\alpha,r,t}
large enough.

Then, it follows that
x_{n+1,-}^{(0)}(s)>x_{n,+}^{(0)}(s)
for
s\in[b_{N},\frac{t}{2}]
since

\displaystyle x_{n+1,-}^{(0)}(s)-x_{n,+}^{(0)}(s)

\displaystyle\geq x_{n+1,-}^{(0)}(b_{N})-x_{n,+}^{(0)}(b_{N})-2(2s)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}-2(b_{N})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}>0

for
\frac{t}{2}\leq s\leq t
.

Next, we will prove
(2)
. We denote by
y_{n,-}^{(1)}(s)
the minimum of the
y
-th coordinate of
\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(1)}
at time
s
, and also
denote by
y_{n,+}^{(0)}(s)
the maximum of the
y
-th coordinate of
\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(0)}
at time
s
.

Then,
y_{n,-}^{(1)}(s)
is given by

\displaystyle\left(C_{\beta,r,t}n^{\beta}s+\frac{4r}{5N}\right)\sin\frac{20\pi}{N}-\frac{r}{20N}-(2s)^{\alpha}

Thus, it holds that

\displaystyle y_{n,-}^{(1)}(s)-y_{n,+}^{(0)}(s)\geq\widetilde{C}_{\alpha,r,t}\frac{n^{\alpha}}{N}s+\frac{15r}{N}-2(2s)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},\quad s\in[0,b_{N}]

for
n\geq\frac{N}{2}
for
N\geq 2
large enough, where
\widetilde{C}_{\alpha,r,t}
is a constant depending on
C_{\alpha,r,t}
, and it is increasing in
C_{\alpha,r,t}>0
.
In the same argument as
(1)
, we find that
y_{n,-}^{(1)}(s)-y_{n,+}^{(0)}(s)>0
for
0\leq s\leq b_{N}
and
y_{n,-}^{(1)}(b_{N})-y_{n,+}^{(0)}(b_{N})\geq 10t^{\alpha}
when we take
C_{\alpha,r,t}>0
large enough.

By construction, we can see that

\displaystyle(y_{n,-}^{(1)}(s)-y_{n,+}^{(0)}(s))-(y_{n,-}^{(1)}(b_{N})-y_{n,+}^{(0)}(b_{N}))\geq-4(2s)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\quad\text{for }b_{N}\leq s\leq\frac{t}{2}.

Hence
y_{n,-}^{(1)}(s)-y_{n,+}^{(0)}(s)\geq 0
for
0\leq s\leq t
.
∎

For a compact set
K\in[0,t]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, we define random variables

\displaystyle u_{t}^{\vartheta,\varepsilon}(K):=\int_{B_{r}(0)}\text{\rm d}x\mathbf{E}_{x}\left[\exp\left(\beta_{\vartheta,\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\dot{\mathcal{W}}^{\varepsilon}(s,\omega_{s})\text{\rm d}s-\frac{\beta_{\vartheta,\varepsilon}^{2}}{2}J_{\varepsilon}(0)t\right):(s,\omega_{s})\in K\right],

where
{\mathbb{P}}_{x}
is the law of the Brownian motion
\omega
starting at
x
,
\mathbf{E}_{x}
denotes the expectation with respect to
\mathbf{P}_{x}
, and
J_{\varepsilon}(0)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int j(x)j(-x)\text{\rm d}x
.

We remark that
u_{t}^{\vartheta,\varepsilon}([0,t]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
corresponds to the Feynman-Kac formula associated with (
SHEϑ,ε
).

Then, the family of random variables
\left\{u_{t}^{\vartheta,\varepsilon}(\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(N,j)}):n=\frac{N}{2},\dots,N,j=0,\dots,\frac{N}{15}\right\}
satisfies that

\displaystyle E\left[\left(\sum_{n=\frac{N}{2}}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{\frac{N}{15}}u_{t}^{\vartheta,\varepsilon}(\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(N,j)})^{2}\right)^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq E\left[\left(\sum_{n=\frac{N}{2}}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{\frac{N}{15}}u_{t}^{\vartheta,\varepsilon}(\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(N,j)})\right)^{2p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq E\left[u_{t}^{\vartheta,\varepsilon}(B_{r}(0),B_{r}(0))^{2p}\right]

for
p>1
since
\{u_{t}^{\vartheta,\varepsilon}(\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(N,j)})\}_{j=0,\dots,\frac{N}{15},n=\frac{N}{2},\dots,N}
are positive.

Therefore, we find a subsequence
\{\varepsilon_{m}\}_{m\geq 1}
such that a weak limit

\displaystyle\left(\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(B_{r}(0),B_{r}(0)),\left\{\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(N,j)}):n=\frac{N}{2},\dots,N,j=0,\dots,\frac{N}{15}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle:=\text{w-}\lim_{m\to\infty}\left(u_{t}^{\vartheta,\varepsilon_{m}}([0,t]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}),\left\{u_{t}^{\vartheta,\varepsilon_{m}}(\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(N,j)}):n=\frac{N}{2},\dots,N,j=0,\dots,\frac{N}{15}\right\}\right),

exists and satisfies

\displaystyle\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(B_{r}(0),B_{r}(0))\geq\sum_{n=\frac{N}{2}}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{\frac{N}{15}}\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(N,j)}).

We remark that

\left\{\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(N,j)}):n=\frac{N}{2},\dots,N,j=0,\dots,\frac{N}{15}\right\}
are independent random variables,
\left\{\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(N,j)}):j=0,\dots,\frac{N}{15}\right\}
are identically distributed. Here, we remark that
U_{n}^{(N,j)}
and
U_{m}^{(N,k)}
for
n\not=m
are not identically distributed since the shapes of the regions are different.

Hence, we find that

\displaystyle P\left(\log\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(B_{r}(0),B_{r}(0))<-3C_{\alpha,r,t}^{2}N^{\alpha+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq P\left(\bigcap_{n=\frac{N}{2}}^{N}\bigcap_{j=0}^{\frac{N}{15}}\left\{\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(N,j)})<e^{-3C_{\alpha,r,t}^{2}N^{\alpha+1}}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{n=\frac{N}{2}}^{N}\prod_{j=0}^{\frac{N}{15}}P\left(\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(N,j)})<e^{-3C_{\alpha,r,t}^{2}N^{\alpha+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{N}{15}\sum_{n=\frac{N}{2}}^{N}P\left(\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(N,0)})\geq e^{-3C_{\alpha,r,t}^{2}N^{\alpha+1}}\right)\right).

Thus, we will complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
once we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

For each
t>0
,
\alpha>2
, and
r>0
, there exists a constant
c_{\alpha,r,t}>0
such that for
N\geq 2
large enough, we have

\displaystyle P\left(\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(N,0)})\geq e^{-3C_{\alpha,r,t}N^{\beta+1}}\right)\geq\frac{c_{\alpha,r,t}}{(\log N)^{2}}

for
n\geq\frac{N}{2}
.

Proof.

Let
h_{n}:[0,t]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be the path transformation defined by

\displaystyle h_{n}(s,x,y)=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{4nr}{5N}+x+C_{\alpha,r,t}n^{\alpha}s,y\right)\quad&\text{for }s\in[0,b_{N}]\\
\left(h_{n}(b_{N},s,y)+(s-b_{N}),y\right)\quad&\text{for }s\in[b_{N},\frac{t}{2}]\\
h_{n}(t-s,x,y)\quad&\text{for }s\in[\frac{t}{2},t].\end{cases}

We consider the curved cones defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}_{n}=\left\{(s,x)\in[0,t]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:|x|\in r_{n,r}(s)\right\},

where
r_{n,r}(s)
is the radius of
\mathrm{U}_{n}
at time
s
given at (
3.2
). Thus
\mathrm{C}_{n}
is the congruent with the region
\mathrm{U}_{n}
obtained by removing the drift of the center.

Applying the Girsanov transformation, we have

\displaystyle u_{t}^{\vartheta,\varepsilon}(\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(N,0)})

\displaystyle=\int_{D_{n,r}(0)}\text{\rm d}x\mathbf{E}_{x}\left[\mathcal{E}_{n}(\omega)\exp\left(\beta_{\vartheta,\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\dot{\mathcal{W}}^{\varepsilon}(s,h_{n}(s,\omega_{s}))\text{\rm d}s-\frac{\beta_{\vartheta,\varepsilon}^{2}}{2}J_{\varepsilon}(0)t\right):(s,\omega_{s})\in\mathrm{C}_{n}\right],

(3.4)

where the Radon-Nikodym deriavtive
\mathcal{E}_{n}(\omega)
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(C_{\alpha,r,t}n^{\alpha}(\omega_{b_{N}}-\omega_{0})-\frac{C_{\alpha,r,t}^{2}n^{2\alpha}b_{N}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left((\omega_{\frac{t}{2}}-\omega_{b_{N}})-\frac{\frac{t}{2}-b_{N}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(-(\omega_{t-b_{N}}-\omega_{\frac{t}{2}})-\frac{\frac{t}{2}-b_{N}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(-C_{\alpha,r,t}n^{\alpha}(\omega_{t}-\omega_{t-b_{N}})-\frac{C_{\alpha,r,t}^{2}n^{2\alpha}b_{N}}{2}\right).

We remark that for
\omega\in\mathrm{C}_{n}
,
\max_{0\leq s\leq t}|\omega_{s}|\leq 3t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+1
. Recalling
b_{N}=\frac{1}{N^{\alpha-1}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n}(\omega)\geq\exp\left(-\frac{3}{2}C_{\alpha,r,t}^{2}N^{\alpha+1}\right)\quad\text{for }\omega\in\mathrm{C}_{n},

for
n=\frac{N}{2},\dots,N
if
N\geq 2
large enough.

Thus, we can see that

\displaystyle u_{t}^{\vartheta,\varepsilon}(\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(N,0)})

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left(-\frac{3}{2}C_{\alpha,r,t}N^{\alpha+1}\right)\int_{D_{n,r}(0)}\text{\rm d}x\mathbf{E}_{x}\left[\exp\left(\beta_{\vartheta,\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\dot{\mathcal{W}}^{\varepsilon}(s,h_{n}(s,\omega_{s}))\text{\rm d}s-\frac{\beta_{\vartheta,\varepsilon}^{2}}{2}J_{\varepsilon}(0)t\right):(s,\omega_{s})\in\mathrm{C}_{n}\right]

Here, we note that

\displaystyle\int_{D_{n,r}(0)}\text{\rm d}x\mathbf{E}_{x}\left[\exp\left(\beta_{\vartheta,\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\dot{\mathcal{W}}^{\varepsilon}(s,h_{n}(s,\omega_{s}))\text{\rm d}s-\frac{\beta_{\vartheta,\varepsilon}^{2}}{2}J_{\varepsilon}(0)t\right):(s,\omega_{s})\in\mathrm{C}_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{d}{=}u_{t}^{\vartheta,\varepsilon}(\mathrm{C}_{n}).

Then, we have

\displaystyle P\left(\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(N,0)})\geq e^{-3C_{\alpha,r,t}^{2}N^{\beta+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\varlimsup_{m\to\infty}P\left(u_{t}^{\vartheta,\varepsilon_{m}}(\mathrm{U}_{n}^{(N,0)})\geq e^{-3C_{\alpha,r,t}^{2}N^{\alpha+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\varliminf_{m\to\infty}P\left(u_{t}^{\vartheta,\varepsilon_{m}}(\mathrm{C}_{n})>e^{-2C_{\alpha,r,t}^{2}N^{\alpha+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq P\left(\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(\mathrm{C}_{n})>e^{-2C_{\alpha,r,t}^{2}N^{\alpha+1}}\right),

where
\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(\mathrm{C}_{n})
is a weak limit of a subsequence of
\{u_{t}^{\vartheta,\varepsilon_{m}}(\mathrm{C}_{n})\}_{m\geq 1}
, which is
L^{p}
-tight for any
p>1
.

Furthermore, one can see that

\displaystyle E\left[\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(\mathrm{C}_{n})\right]=\int_{B_{\frac{r}{20N}}(0)}\mathbf{P}_{x}\left((s,\omega_{s})\in\mathrm{C}_{n}\right)\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle E\left[\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(\mathrm{C}_{n})^{2}\right]\leq E\left[\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}\left(B_{\frac{r}{20N}}(0),B_{\frac{r}{20N}}(0)\right)^{2}\right].

The law of iterated logarithms implies that there exists a constant
c_{t,\alpha}>0
such that for any
n\geq 1
and
x\in B_{\frac{r}{20N}}(0)
,

\displaystyle\frac{\text{\rm d}\mathbf{P}_{x}\left(|\omega_{s}|\leq r_{n,r}(s)\text{ for }0\leq s\leq\frac{t}{2},\omega_{\frac{t}{2}}\in\text{\rm d}y\right)}{\text{\rm d}y}\geq c_{t,\alpha}>0

uniformly
for
|y|\leq\frac{1}{2}t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
. Then, considering the reverse of Brownian motion, we obtain that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{x}\left((s,\omega_{s})\in\mathrm{C}_{n}\right)\geq c_{t,\alpha}^{2}\frac{r^{2}}{(20N)^{2}}

uniformly for
x\in B_{\frac{r}{20N}}(0)
.

We have from (
3.4
) and the Paley-Zygmund inequality

\displaystyle P\left(\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(\mathrm{C}_{n})>e^{-2C_{\alpha,r,t}^{2}N^{\alpha+1}}\right)\geq P\left(\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(\mathrm{C}_{n})>\frac{1}{2}E\left[\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(\mathrm{C}_{n})\right]\right)\geq\frac{1}{4}\frac{E\left[\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(\mathrm{C}_{n})\right]^{2}}{E\left[\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(\mathrm{C}_{n})^{2}\right]}.

Therefore, we obtain from Proposition
2.3
that there exists a constant
\widetilde{C}_{\vartheta}>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{E\left[\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(\mathrm{C}_{n})\right]^{2}}{E\left[\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(\mathrm{C}_{n})^{2}\right]}\geq\frac{\widetilde{C}_{\vartheta,t,\alpha}}{\left(\log\frac{10N}{r}\right)^{2}}.

∎

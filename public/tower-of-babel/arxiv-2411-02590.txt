Rate of convergence of a semi-implicit time Euler scheme for a 2D Bénard-Boussinesq model
Hakima Bessaih, Annie Millet

Abstract
. We prove that a semi-implicit time Euler scheme for the two-dimensional Bénard-Boussinesq model on the torus D D converges. The rate of convergence in probability is almost 1/2 for a multiplicative noise; this relies on moment estimates in various norms for the processes and the scheme. In case of an additive noise, due to the coupling of the equations, provided that the difference on temperature between the top and bottom parts of the torus is not too big compared to the viscosity and thermal diffusivity, a strong polynomial rate of convergence (almost 1/2) is proven in ( L 2 ​ ( D ) ) 2 (L^{2}(D))^{2} for the velocity and in L 2 ​ ( D ) L^{2}(D) for the temperature. It depends on exponential moments of the scheme; due to linear terms involving the other quantity in both evolution equations, the proof has to be done simultaneaously for both the velocity and the temperature. These rates in both cases are similar to that obtained for the Navier-Stokes equation.

1.
Introduction

The Boussinesq equations have been used as a model in many geophysical applications. They have been widely studied in a both
the deterministic and stochastic settings.
We take random forcings into account and formulate the Bénard convection problem as a system of stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs).
The need to take stochastic effects into account for modeling complex systems has now become widely recognized. Stochastic
partial differential equations (SPDEs) arise naturally as mathematical models for nonlinear macroscopic dynamics under random influences.
It is a coupled system of the stochastic Navier–Stokes equations and the stochastic transport equation for temperature.
For us this system will be subject to
an additive random perturbation which will be defined later.

Numerical schemes and algorithms have been introduced to best approximate the solution to non-linear PDEs. The time approximation
is either an implicit Euler or a time splitting scheme coupled with Galerkin approximation or finite elements to approximate the space variable.
The literature on numerical analysis for SPDEs is now very extensive.
In many papers the models are either linear, have global Lipschitz properties,
or more generally some monotonicity property. In this case the convergence is proven to be in mean square.
When nonlinearities are involved that are not of Lipschitz or monotone type, then a rate of convergence
in mean square is more difficult to obtain.
Indeed, because of the stochastic perturbation, one may not use the Gronwall lemma after taking the expectation of the
error bound
since it involves a nonlinear term which is often quadratic; such a nonlinearity requires some localization.

In a random setting, the discretization of the Navier-Stokes equations has been intensively investigated.
Various space-time numerical schemes have been studied for the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations with a multiplicative or an additive noise, that is

As stated previously, the main tool to get the convergence in probability is the localization of the nonlinear term over a space of large probability.
We studied the strong (that is

) rate of convergence of the time implicit Euler scheme
(resp. space-time implicit Euler scheme coupled with
finite element space discretization) in our previous papers
[

The method is based on the fact that the solution (and the scheme) have finite moments (bounded uniformly on the time mesh).
For a general multiplicative noise, the strong rate is logarithmic. When the diffusion coefficient is bounded (which is a slight extension of an additive noise),
the solution has exponential moments; we used this property in

The convergence of a fully implicit time Euler scheme for the two-dimensional Boussinesq equation on the torus has been studied in

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|\tilde{A}\theta(s)\|_{H^{0}}\|u(s)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{4}}\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s)\|_{L^{4}}\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s)\|_{H^{0}}^{2(p-1)}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|\tilde{A}\theta(s)\|_{H^{0}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|u(s)\|_{V^{1}}\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s)\|_{H^{0}}^{2p-\frac{3}{2}}ds.

Then, using the Hölder and Young’s inequalities, we deduce

\displaystyle 2p\int_{0}^{t}

\displaystyle\big|\langle(u(s).\nabla)\theta(s),{\tilde{A}}\theta(s)\rangle\big|\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s)\|_{H^{0}}^{2(p-1)}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;(2p-1)\,\kappa\int_{0}^{t}\|{\tilde{A}}(\theta(s))\|_{H^{0}}^{2}\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s)\|_{H^{0}}^{2(p-1)}ds

\displaystyle\;+C(\kappa,p)\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|u(s)\|_{V^{1}}^{4}\int_{0}^{t}\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s)\|_{H^{0}}^{2p}ds.

(7.11)

The growth condition (
2.14
) and Hölder’s and Young inequalities imply that

\int_{0}^{t}\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\tilde{G}}(\theta(s))\|_{H^{0}}^{2}\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s)\|_{H^{0}}^{2(p-1)}ds\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\big[1+\|\theta(s)\|_{H^{0}}^{2p}+\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s)\|_{H^{0}}^{2p}\big]ds,

(7.12)

and a similar computation yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\big\|\big({\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\,{\tilde{G}}\big)^{*}(\theta(s))\big({\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s)\big)\|_{K}^{2}\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s)\|_{H^{0}}^{2(p-2)}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\big[1+\|\theta(s)\|_{H^{0}}^{2p}+\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s)\|_{H^{0}}^{2p}\big]ds.

(7.13)

Let
\tilde{\tau}_{N}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(t)\|_{H^{0}}\geq N\}
. The upper estimates (
7.10
)–(
7.13
)
written for
t\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{N}
instead of
t
imply

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}

\displaystyle\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(t\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{N})\|_{H^{0}}^{2p}+\kappa\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{N}}\|{\tilde{A}}\theta(s)\|_{H^{0}}^{2}\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s)\|_{H^{0}}^{2(p-1)}ds\leq\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta_{0}\|_{H^{0}}^{2p}

\displaystyle+C\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\!\|u(s)\|_{V^{1}}^{4}\!\!\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tilde{\tau_{N}}}\!\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s)\|_{H_{0}}^{2p}ds+C\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{N}}\!\!\big(1+\|\theta(s)\|_{H^{0}}^{2p}+\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s)\|_{H^{0}}^{2p}\big)ds

\displaystyle+2p\,\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{N}}\big({\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\tilde{G}}(\theta(s))d{\tilde{W}}(s),{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s)\big)\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s)\|_{H^{0}}^{2(p-1)}.

Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, Fubini’s theorem, (
2.18
) and (
7.5
), we deduce

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\sup_{s\in[0,T]}

\displaystyle\|u(s)\|_{V^{1}}^{4}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{N}}\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s)\|_{H^{0}}^{2p}ds\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\Big\{{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|u(s)\|_{V^{1}}^{8}\Big)\Big\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\{\int_{0}^{T}{\mathbb{E}}\big(\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{N})\|_{H^{0}}^{4p}\big)ds\Big\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C.

(7.14)

The Davis inequality, the growth condition (
2.14
) and the Cauchy–Schwarz, Young and Hölder inequalities imply that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big(

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{N}}\big({\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\tilde{G}}(\theta(s))d{\tilde{W}}(s),{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s)\big)\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s)\|_{H^{0}}^{2(p-1)}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\,{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\Big\{\int_{0}^{T}{\rm Tr}({\tilde{Q}})\big[\tilde{K}_{2}+\tilde{K}_{3}\|\theta(s\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{N})\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\big]\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{N})\|_{H^{0}}^{4p-2}ds\Big\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\,{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\big(\sup_{s\leq T}\big(\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{N})\|_{H^{0}}^{p}\big)({\rm Tr}({\tilde{Q}}))^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big\{\int_{0}^{T}\big[\tilde{K}_{2}+\tilde{K}_{3}\|\theta(s\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{N})\|_{H^{0}}^{2}+\tilde{K}_{3}\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{N})\|_{H^{0}}^{2}\big]\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{N})\|_{H^{0}}^{2(p-1)}ds\Big\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4p}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\sup_{s\leq T}\big(\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{N})\|_{H^{0}}^{2p}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+C{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\big[1+\|\theta(s\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{N})\|_{H^{0}}^{2p}+\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{N})\|_{H^{0}}^{2p}\big]ds\Big).

Therefore, the upper estimates (
2.17
), (
7.5
) and (
7.14
) imply that

\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\sup_{s\leq T}\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{N})\|_{H^{0}}^{2p}\Big)+\kappa\;{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{N}}\|{\tilde{A}}\theta(s\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{N})\|_{H^{0}}^{2}\|{\tilde{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta(s)\|_{H^{0}}^{2(p-1)}ds\Big)\leq C

for some constant
C
independent of
N
.
As
N\to+\infty
, we deduce (
2.19
);
this completes the proof of Proposition
2.4
.

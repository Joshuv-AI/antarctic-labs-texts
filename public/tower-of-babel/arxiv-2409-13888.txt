Causal Feature Selection Method for Contextual Multi-Armed Bandits in Recommender System Conference: October, 2024; Bari, Italy;
Zhenyu Zhao, Yexi Jiang

Abstract
. Features (a.k.a. context) are critical for contextual multi-armed bandits (MAB) performance. In practice of large scale online system, it is important to select and implement important features for the model: missing important features can led to sub-optimal reward outcome, and including irrelevant features can cause overfitting, poor model interpretability, and implementation cost. However, feature selection methods for conventional machine learning models fail short for contextual MAB use cases, as conventional methods select features correlated with the outcome variable, but not necessarily causing heterogeneuous treatment effect among arms which are truely important for contextual MAB. In this paper, we introduce model-free feature selection methods designed for contexutal MAB problem, based on heterogeneous causal effect contributed by the feature to the reward distribution. Empirical evaluation is conducted based on synthetic data as well as real data from an online experiment for optimizing content cover image in a recommender system. The results show this feature selection method effectively selects the important features that lead to higher contextual MAB reward than unimportant features. Compared with model embedded method, this model-free method has advantage of fast computation speed, ease of implementation, and prune of model mis-specification issues.



1.
Introduction

Contextual multi-armed bandits (CMAB)
(
Tang et al. 2014b
;
Tang et al. 2015
)
have emerged as a pivotal framework for optimizing decision-making in dynamic environments, particularly in personalization for recommender systems.
Selecting the most relevant features is crucial to ensuring optimal model performance, improving interpretability and lower implementation costs.

Traditional feature selection methods
(
Bolón-Canedo et al. 2013
;
Chandrashekar and Sahin 2014
;
Tang et al. 2014a
)
focus on identifying features that are strongly correlated with the outcome variable. However, these methods may not be directly applicable to contextual MAB, where the goal is to identify features that contribute to heterogeneous treatment effects among different arms. In this context, the features that drive differential best actions given the context are more important than those that merely predict the average outcome.

Li et al.
(
Li et al. 2010
)
introduced a model-based approach for feature selection in contextual MABs, where feature importance is inferred by the reward from model using such feature. Zhao et al.
(
Zhao et al. 2022
)
proposed feature selection methods for HTE for uplift models
(
Olaya et al. 2020
;
Rzepakowski and Jaroszewicz 2012
)
with one treatment and one control, that has limitation for multiple arms.

In this paper, we propose novel, model-free feature selection methods for contextual MABs. The advantage of the proposed method includes:

•

It calculates the feature importance based on the incremental value and distribution divergence brought by the feature to the reward, instead of conventional correlation to outcome, therefore leading to better outcome in contextual MAB.

•

It considers nonlinear relationship between the feature and the heterogeneous causal effect among the arms.

•

It adapts to both continuous feature and categorical feature.

•

It is model free that is prune of model mis-specification.

•

It has a fast computation speed, especially compared with model-based methods.

We demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method through empirical evaluations using both simulated data and real-world data from online experiments. The results indicate that this approach not only enhances the performance of contextual MAB models but also offers advantages in terms of computational efficiency and robustness against model mis-specification.

2.
Feature Selection Method

In the context of contextual Multi-Armed Bandit (MAB) problems, a feature is deemed important if it leads to heterogeneous treatment effects (HTE) across different arms. This means that the distribution of rewards from the arms varies depending on the values of the feature. Specifically, a feature is considered crucial if it causes the optimal arm to change across different ranges of the feature values, allowing the contextual MAB to optimize rewards based on the feature’s context.

Figure 1.
Feature Pattern Illustration: The Ineffectiveness of Correlational Features Compared to HTE Features in CMAB

Figure
1
illustrates the distinction between correlational features and HTE features. Correlational features, while predictive of the outcome variable, do not affect the relative rewards of the arms across different feature values. In contrast, HTE features are valuable to the CMAB problem because they influence reward distributions and determine the best arm based on varying feature values.

We introduce two methods of calculating feature important scores: one based on the contribution to heterogeneous winning arms given the feature and the other based on the distribution divergence heterogeneity due to the feature. These two scores can be used separately or in combination.

For a contextual MAB problem, assuming there are
k
arms trying to optimize reward
Y
(assuming binary for simplicity). For a feature
x
containing contextual information, to study the nonlinear feature importance and be adaptive to both continuous and categorical features, we cut
x
into
m_{x}
bins each with sample size
N_{b}
(for
b=1,...,m_{x}
).

2.1.
Heterogeneous Incremental Effect (HIE) Score

It is desirable to have different winning arms in different context that provides incremental gain compared with choosing the global winner (without context) for contextual MAB optimization problem.
The winning arm in bin
b
is denoted as
w_{b}=argmax_{i\in{1,...,k}}P_{b_{i}}(Y=1)
and the global winning arm is denoted as
w^{*}
.
The HWTE feature importance score is defined as

\displaystyle FI_{HIE}(x)=\sum_{b=1}^{m_{x}}\frac{N_{b}}{N}(P_{w_{b}}(Y=1)-P_{w^{*}}(Y=1))

2.2.
Heterogeneous Distribution Divergence (HDD) Score

The HDD Score is calculated as the sample weighted sum of contextual KL divergence offset the non-contextual KL divergence:

\displaystyle FI_{HDD}(x)=\sum_{b=1}^{m_{x}}\frac{N_{b}}{N}D_{b}(P_{b_{1}},...,P_{b_{k}})-D(P_{1},...,P_{k})

where
D_{b}(P_{b_{1}},...,P_{b_{k}})
calculates the KL divergence for bin
b
, and
D(P_{1},...,P_{k})
calculates the KL divergence based on all data, with details in Appendix
A.1
.

2.3.
Combined Feature Importance Score

While HIE and HDD scores can be used independently to score features, we discuss an approach to combine these two scores to balance two objectives: optimizing heterogeneous incremental effect and maximizing distribution divergence.
The combined score is a weighted sum of two normalized scores:

(1)

\displaystyle FI(x)=\alpha_{1}\cdot\hat{FI}_{HIE}(x)+\alpha_{2}\cdot\hat{FI}_{HDD}(x)

where
\hat{FI}_{HIE}(x)
and
\hat{FI}_{HDD}(x)
are normalized scores (such as the Min-Max Normalization) to recale these two scores to a comparable levels.
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
are hyperparameters that adjusts the weight for prioritizing winning arm heterogeneity over general distribution heterogeneity.

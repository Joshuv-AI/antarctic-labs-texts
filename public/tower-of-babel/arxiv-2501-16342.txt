Non-Linear Interactions in Neural Network Operators: New Theorems on Symmetry-Preserving Transformations
Rômulo Damasclin Chaves dos Santos, Jorge Henrique de Oliveira Sales

Abstract
This paper advances the study of multivariate function approximation using neural network operators activated by symmetrized and perturbed hyperbolic tangent functions. We propose new non-linear operators that preserve dynamic symmetries within Euclidean spaces, extending current results on Voronovskaya-type asymptotic expansions. The developed operators utilize parameterized deformations to model higher-order interactions in multivariate settings, achieving improved accuracy and robustness. Fundamental theorems demonstrate convergence properties and quantify error bounds, highlighting the operators’ ability to approximate functions and derivatives in Sobolev spaces. These results provide a rigorous foundation for theoretical studies and further applications in symmetry-driven modeling and regularization strategies in machine learning and data analysis.



Keywords:
Neural Network Operators. Symmetry-Preserving Transformations. Convergence Analysis. Hölder Spaces.

Mathematical Foundations

Let
\mathbb{R}^{N}
denote a complete Euclidean space, where
N\in\mathbb{N}
. We define the symmetrized and parameterized activation function
g_{q,\lambda}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
as:

g_{q,\lambda}(x)=\frac{e^{\lambda x}-qe^{-\lambda x}}{e^{\lambda x}+qe^{-\lambda x}},\quad q,\lambda>0,x\in\mathbb{R}.

(1)

The function
g_{q,\lambda}(x)
is differentiable and odd, satisfying
g_{q,\lambda}(-x)=-g_{q,\lambda}(x)
. Its derivative is given by:

g^{\prime}_{q,\lambda}(x)=\frac{4q\lambda e^{\lambda x}e^{-\lambda x}}{(e^{\lambda x}+qe^{-\lambda x})^{2}},

(2)

indicating that
g^{\prime}_{q,\lambda}(x)
is non-negative and symmetric about
x=0
.

The density function
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is defined as:

\Phi(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left(M_{q,\lambda}(x)+M_{1/q,\lambda}(x)\right),

(3)

where
M_{q,\lambda}(x)
is expressed as:

M_{q,\lambda}(x)=\frac{1}{4}\left(g_{q,\lambda}(x+1)-g_{q,\lambda}(x-1)\right).

(4)

The function
\Phi(x)
satisfies the following properties:

1.

Symmetry:
\Phi(-x)=\Phi(x)
,
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}
.

2.

Normalization:
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(x)\,dx=1
.

3.

Positivity:
\Phi(x)>0
,
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Using
\Phi
, we construct multivariate density functions on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
:

Z(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\Phi(x_{i}),\quad x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(5)

This construction ensures that
Z(x)
inherits symmetry and normalization properties from
\Phi
. Specifically:

\displaystyle Z(-x)

\displaystyle=Z(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

(6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Z(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=1.

(7)

The neural network operators are defined as follows:

1.

Basic Operator:

A_{n}(f,x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)Z(nx-k),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(8)

The operator
A_{n}
approximates
f
by weighted sums of its values at discrete points, with weights determined by
Z
.

2.

Kantorovich Operator:

K_{n}(f,x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left(n^{N}\int_{\frac{k}{n}}^{\frac{k+1}{n}}f(t)\,dt\right)Z(nx-k).

(9)

This operator generalizes
A_{n}
by averaging
f
over subintervals, introducing robustness to variations in
f
.

3.

Quadrature Operator:

Q_{n}(f,x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\delta_{n,k}(f)Z(nx-k),

(10)

where
\delta_{n,k}(f)
represents a weighted evaluation of
f
over subintervals, given by:

\delta_{n,k}(f)=\sum_{r=1}^{\theta}w_{r}f\left(\frac{k}{n}+\frac{r}{n\theta}\right),

(11)

with weights
w_{r}\geq 0
satisfying
\sum_{r=1}^{\theta}w_{r}=1
.

These operators are designed to approximate functions
f\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where
C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
denotes the space of
m
-times continuously differentiable functions. Each operator leverages the symmetry and smoothness of
\Phi
to ensure accurate and stable approximations.

3
Main Theorems

Theorem 1 (Symmetry-Preserving Convergence).
Let
f\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The operator
A_{n}
satisfies:

A_{n}(f,x)-f(x)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\frac{f^{(\alpha)}(x)}{\alpha!}A_{n}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\cdot-x_{i})^{\alpha_{i}}\right)+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{m-\epsilon}}\right),

(12)

where
\alpha
is a multi-index, and
o(\cdot)
denotes the asymptotic remainder.

Proof.
Let
f\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and consider its multivariate Taylor expansion around
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
:

f(y)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\frac{f^{(\alpha)}(x)}{\alpha!}(y-x)^{\alpha}+R_{m}(y,x),

(13)

where
R_{m}(y,x)
is the remainder term, given by:

R_{m}(y,x)=\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{m-1}f^{(\alpha)}(x+t(y-x))(y-x)^{\alpha}\,dt.

(14)

Substituting this expansion into
A_{n}(f,x)
, we obtain:

\displaystyle A_{n}(f,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)Z(nx-k)

(15)

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\frac{f^{(\alpha)}(x)}{\alpha!}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)^{\alpha}Z(nx-k)+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}R_{m}\left(\frac{k}{n},x\right)Z(nx-k).

(16)

The first term represents the main approximation, while the second term captures the remainder. By symmetry of
Z
, only even powers of
\alpha
contribute to the sum, ensuring cancellation of odd-order terms. For the remainder term, we use the boundedness of
f^{(\alpha)}
and the localization of
Z
to show:

\left|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}R_{m}\left(\frac{k}{n},x\right)Z(nx-k)\right|\leq\frac{C}{n^{m-\epsilon}},

(17)

where
C
depends on
\|f^{(\alpha)}\|_{\infty}
and the decay of
Z
.

Thus, the theorem is proved. ∎

Theorem 2 (Error Bounds in Sobolev Spaces).
For
f\in W^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the Sobolev space of order
m
, the operators
K_{n}
and
Q_{n}
satisfy:

\displaystyle\|K_{n}(f)-f\|_{W^{m,p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{n^{m}},

(18)

\displaystyle\|Q_{n}(f)-f\|_{W^{m,p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{n^{m-\epsilon}},

(19)

where
C
is a constant depending on
f
and
\Phi
.

Proof.
To prove the bounds, let us first recall the definition of the Sobolev norm:

\|f\|_{W^{m,p}}=\left(\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D^{\alpha}f(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p},

(20)

where
D^{\alpha}f
represents the weak derivative of
f
associated with the multi-index
\alpha
.

For the Kantorovich operator
K_{n}
, we decompose the error:

\|K_{n}(f)-f\|_{W^{m,p}}\leq\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D^{\alpha}(K_{n}(f)-f)(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}.

(21)

By properties of the operator and density
\Phi
, we express the derivative term as:

D^{\alpha}K_{n}(f)(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left(n^{N}\int_{\frac{k}{n}}^{\frac{k+1}{n}}D^{\alpha}f(t)\,dt\right)D^{\alpha}Z(nx-k).

(22)

Using the smoothness of
\Phi
and bounding higher-order terms with
\|f\|_{W^{m,p}}
, we establish:

\|K_{n}(f)-f\|_{W^{m,p}}\leq C\frac{1}{n^{m}}.

(23)

For the quadrature operator
Q_{n}
, a similar argument applies, with additional remainder terms arising from weighted evaluations. These terms contribute an additional error factor, leading to:

\|Q_{n}(f)-f\|_{W^{m,p}}\leq C\frac{1}{n^{m-\epsilon}}.

(24)

Thus, the theorem is proved. ∎

Theorem 3 (Convergence in Hölder Spaces).
Let
f\in C^{m,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where
C^{m,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
denotes the space of
m
-times continuously differentiable functions with
m
-th derivatives that are
\alpha
-Hölder continuous. Then, the operators
A_{n}
,
K_{n}
, and
Q_{n}
satisfy the following error estimates:

\displaystyle\|A_{n}(f)-f\|_{C^{m,\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{n^{m+\alpha}},

(25)

\displaystyle\|K_{n}(f)-f\|_{C^{m,\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{n^{m+\alpha}},

(26)

\displaystyle\|Q_{n}(f)-f\|_{C^{m,\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{n^{m+\alpha-\epsilon}},

(27)

where
C
is a constant depending on
f
and
\Phi
, and
\epsilon>0
is an arbitrarily small parameter.

Proof.
To prove the theorem, we need to revisit the properties of Hölder spaces and how the operators
A_{n}
,
K_{n}
, and
Q_{n}
behave in these spaces.

The Hölder space
C^{m,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined as the set of functions
f
that are
m
-times continuously differentiable and whose
m
-th derivatives satisfy the Hölder condition:

|D^{\beta}f(x)-D^{\beta}f(y)|\leq C|x-y|^{\alpha},

(28)

for
|\beta|=m
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The norm in the Hölder space is given by:

\|f\|_{C^{m,\alpha}}=\sum_{|\beta|\leq m}\|D^{\beta}f\|_{\infty}+\sum_{|\beta|=m}\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|D^{\beta}f(x)-D^{\beta}f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}.

(29)

Analysis of the Operators:

1.
Operator
A_{n}
:

Consider the Taylor expansion of
f
around
x
:

f(y)=\sum_{|\beta|\leq m}\frac{D^{\beta}f(x)}{\beta!}(y-x)^{\beta}+R_{m}(y,x),

(30)

where
R_{m}(y,x)
is the remainder term. Substituting this expansion into
A_{n}(f,x)
, we obtain:

A_{n}(f,x)=\sum_{|\beta|\leq m}\frac{D^{\beta}f(x)}{\beta!}A_{n}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\cdot-x_{i})^{\beta_{i}}\right)+A_{n}(R_{m}(\cdot,x)).

(31)

Using the symmetry of
Z
and the smoothness of
f
, we can show that:

\|A_{n}(f)-f\|_{C^{m,\alpha}}\leq C\frac{1}{n^{m+\alpha}}.

(32)

2.
Operator
K_{n}
:

For the operator
K_{n}
, we use the definition:

K_{n}(f,x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left(n^{N}\int_{\frac{k}{n}}^{\frac{k+1}{n}}f(t)\,dt\right)Z(nx-k).

(33)

Decomposing the error and using the smoothness of
f
and the symmetry of
Z
, we obtain:

\|K_{n}(f)-f\|_{C^{m,\alpha}}\leq C\frac{1}{n^{m+\alpha}}.

(34)

3.
Operator
Q_{n}
:

For the operator
Q_{n}
, we have:

Q_{n}(f,x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\delta_{n,k}(f)Z(nx-k),

(35)

where
\delta_{n,k}(f)=\sum_{r=1}^{\theta}w_{r}f\left(\frac{k}{n}+\frac{r}{n\theta}\right)
. Using the smoothness of
f
and the symmetry of
Z
, we obtain:

\|Q_{n}(f)-f\|_{C^{m,\alpha}}\leq C\frac{1}{n^{m+\alpha-\epsilon}}.

(36)

Therefore, the theorem is proved, showing that the operators
A_{n}
,
K_{n}
, and
Q_{n}
converge in Hölder spaces with specific rates, ensuring the robustness and accuracy of the approximations for functions with Hölder regularity. ∎

The symmetry-preserving properties of the operators ensure that approximations maintain essential structural characteristics of the target function, as demonstrated in Theorem 1. The framework provided for constructing multivariate density functions enables the formulation of neural network operators that are both flexible and theoretically grounded. This approach opens avenues for applications where symmetry and smooth approximations are critical.

Analytical and numerical study on radius of investigation in composite reservoirs
Authors: Haijun Fan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractRadius of investigation (ROI) is of great significance in well testing and reserve estimation. The studies on ROI over the past decades were mainly focusing on homogeneous reservoirs. In heterogeneous formations, such as composite reservoirs and fractured reservoirs, the problem remains unsolved. Few ROI evaluation methods for composite reservoirs have been validated and commonly accepted. This paper conducts extensive analytical and numerical analysis on the pressure propagation of composite reservoirs, analyzing the limitations and misunderstandings of previous methods on the evaluation of ROI. The pressure solution of a two-zone composite reservoir is presented in the Laplace domain and is used to analyze the impact of heterogeneity parameters on pressure transient propagation and the evaluation of radius of investigation. A new ROI formula for composite reservoirs is proposed, which is in great agreement with the analytical solution. The study reveals that the inner zone of a composite reservoir cannot be treated as homogeneous when the pressure front approaches the interface of the two zones with high heterogeneity. A large mobility ratio will slow pressure propagation in composite reservoirs, while a low storativity ratio will increase the value of ROI. Reservoir simulation and real case studies are used to demonstrate the findings of the analytical study. The new study sheds insights into the pressure propagation and eases the calculation of ROI in composite reservoirs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-81494-9.Keywords: Composite reservoir, Radius of investigation, Distance of influence, Mobility ratio, Reservoir heterogeneitySubject terms: Fossil fuels, Hydrology

Introduction

Pressure front propagation is crucial in many reservoir performance analyses. Radius of investigation (ROI), as a measure of pressure propagation, is a fundamental concept in reservoir engineering and is essentially required in pressure transient tests, well-spacing designs, and reserve estimations. ROI is defined as the distance of pressure wave traveled with the elapse of time after it was initiated from the well-bottom. It depends on many reservoir parameters, such as fluid mobility and rock compressibility. Different ROI equations for homogeneous reservoirs have been presented and applied for decades1–4. Meanwhile, the development of unconventional reservoirs necessitates the estimation of distance of investigation (DOI) for linear flow in fractured horizontal wells. Researchers have presented some new study results and equations on DOI5–11. For homogeneous reservoirs, the most commonly accepted equation in dimensionless form is Eq. (1).

The challenge in estimating ROI of heterogeneous reservoirs arises due to different formation heterogeneities12, and no commonly accepted equation is available. Yuan et al.13developed a new RTA and DOI evaluation method for heterogeneous unconventional reservoirs using the concept of continuous succession of steady states. Fan14studied linear flow formation with an exponential permeability profile and presented ROI euqation for a typical heterogeneous reservoir. Natural fractured reservoir is one type of heterogeneous formation. Sobbi15 and Aguilera16 presented different formulas for ROI in naturally fractured reservoirs, respectively. Fan17pointed out the limitations of these two equations and provided a new ROI equation based on the solution of Warren-Root dual-porosity model18.

Composite reservoirs are often encountered in situations such as geological discontinuity, well damage, polymer flooding, or thermal recovery of heavy oil. Few ROI equations for composite reservoirs have ever been presented. Wang19 studied the ROI for non-Newtonian fluids and presented an equation for ROI of composite reservoirs, as in Eq. (2).

Further, in Eq. (3), we cannot find the impact of mobility ratio and storativity ratio between the two zones. In deriving Eq. (3), Nie assumed that the flow rate at the interface of the I-zone and II-zone is equal to the constant well production rate at the sandface. Wang (1997) also assumed a constant formation flow rate, q (r, t) = qB (B is formation volume factor) when deriving Eq. (2). This assumption from the two authors is obviously invalid according to pressure transient flow theory. The formation flow rate q (r, t) varies with time and distance. It may be equal to the well bottom-hole production rate only when the time is very long at a certain distance.

Understanding the flow regime is essential to the study of radius of investigation in composite reservoirs. In this paper, we first derive the analytical solution for a two-zone composite reservoir and discuss the determination of ROI for the case of constant production rate. We analyze the influences of heterogeneity parameters of a composite reservoir on pressure propagation. Then, a numerical simulation of a composite reservoir model is conducted to study the performance of pressure transient propagation. Finally, a real case study is provided to compare the calculation of radius of investigation with different methods.

Definition of ROI in composite reservoirs under constant production rate

Various criteria for the definition of ROI have been studied4, among which is the flow rate ratio or pressure ratio, which is highly flexible, but worthfully applied. Define a front-bottomhole rate ratio (FBRR), which is the ratio of flow rate at pressure front to that at the well-bottom of a well under constant production rate9,10. (For constant-production-pressure well, it is FBPR, which is the ratio of dimensionless front pressure to dimensionless bottomhole pressure.) When FBRR reaches a certain predefined value, the pressure disturbance begins to be felt or observable in the formation. In a composite reservoir, relationship of FBRR and flow rate should be redefined according to different zones.

Figure 5 shows the relationship of FBRR with distance at different times. In the inner zone, as time increases, the FBRR gradually gets close to 1 (dimensionless wellbottom production rate is 1). In the I-zone far from the interface, the FBRR is almost not affected by the mobility ratio M. In this situation, the ROI equation for homogeneous reservoirs can be used. However, when the pressure front approaches or outdistances the interface, the ROI begins to be highly affected by the mobility ratio. It is obvious that the higher value of M, the longer it takes to reach a certain distance in the outer zone. The heterogeneity affects the behavior of the outer zone more than that of inter-zone, but the degree of this impact weakens with the increase of mobility ratio M.

By Eq. (9), we calculated the ROI in the outer zone at different times and compared the results with the analytical solution from this work. The results are shown in Fig. 10. It is noted that Wang’s model and analytical solution give the same ROI when M = 1. However, when M is great than 1, Wang’s model generates unreliable ROI values in outer zone, especially when the time is not very long, or the distance is near to the interface. Only when the propagating time becomes very long the ROI from Eq. (9) gets close to the analytical solution. Wang adopted the ROI equation for a homogeneous reservoir before the interface is reached. However, the main problem with Wang’s method is that the calculated ROI does not have continuity from the inter-zone to the outer zone. The abrupt change of ROI at the interface of a composite reservoir from Wang’s method is inexplainable.

Conclusions

This paper studies pressure traveling behavior and the estimation of radius of investigation in composite reservoirs based on solutions in the Laplace domain. The impact of heterogeneity on pressure propagation in a composite reservoir is analyzed using analytical and numerical methods. The conclusions include:

The inner zone of a composite reservoir is different from infinite acting homogeneous reservoir due to the existence of the interface and heterogeneities among different zones. ROI equations for homogeneous reservoirs cannot be simply applied to the pressure propagation in the inner zone, which will lead to discontinuity in ROI calculation.

The inner zone of a composite reservoir is different from infinite acting homogeneous reservoir due to the existence of the interface and heterogeneities among different zones. ROI equations for homogeneous reservoirs cannot be simply applied to the pressure propagation in the inner zone, which will lead to discontinuity in ROI calculation.

When pressure propagation approaches the interface of a composite reservoir, the pressure and flow rate profiles are highly affected by the degree of heterogeneities. In the case of an unfavorable mobility ratio (M > 1), the traveling time to reach a certain distance near or beyond the interface of a composite reservoir becomes longer. Equivalently, reduced ROI is obtained in the composite reservoir at an unfavorable mobility ratio.

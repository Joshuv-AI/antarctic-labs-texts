An equivariant Guillemin trace formula
Peter Hochs, Hemanth Saratchandran

Abstract
Guillemin’s trace formula is an expression for the distributional trace of an operator defined by pulling back functions along a flow on a compact manifold. We obtain an equivariant generalisation of this formula, for proper, cocompact group actions. This is motivated by the construction of an equivariant version of the Ruelle dynamical ζ \zeta -function in another paper by the same authors, which is based on the equivariant Guillemin trace formula. To obtain this formula, we first develop an equivariant version of the distributional trace that appears in Guillemin’s formula and other places.



1
Introduction

Guillemin’s trace formula

Consider a flow
\varphi
on a compact manifold
M
. For a flow curve
\gamma
of
\varphi
that is periodic with period
l
, let the linearised Poincaré map

P_{\gamma}\colon T_{\gamma(0)}M/\mathbb{R}\gamma^{\prime}(0)\to T_{\gamma(0)}M/\mathbb{R}\gamma^{\prime}(0)

be induced by the derivative of the flow map
\varphi_{l}
. We assume that
\varphi
is nondegenerate, in the sense that
1-P_{\gamma}
is invertible for all such
\gamma
.

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, consider the operator
\varphi_{t}^{*}
on
C^{\infty}(M)
given by pulling back functions along
\varphi_{t}
. If
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
, then we define the operator
\varphi^{*}_{\psi}
on
C^{\infty}(M)
by

\varphi^{*}_{\psi}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}}\psi(t)\varphi_{t}^{*}\,dt.

Nondegeneracy of
\varphi
implies that the wave front set of the Schwartz kernel
K_{\varphi^{*}_{\psi}}
of
\varphi^{*}_{\psi}
is disjoint from the conormal bundle to the diagonal
\Delta(M)\subset M\times M
. Hence
K_{\varphi^{*}_{\psi}}
has a well-defined restriction to
\Delta(M)
. Then the distributional
flat trace
of
\varphi_{\psi}^{*}
is

\Tr^{\flat}(\varphi^{*}_{\psi}):=\int_{\Delta(M)}K_{\varphi^{*}_{\psi}}|_{\Delta(M)}\,dm,

(1.1)

where this integral is the pairing of
K_{\varphi^{*}_{\psi}}|_{\Delta(M)}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Delta(M))
with the constant function
1
. This trace is used for example in Theorem 19.4.1 in
[
13
]
.

Guillemin’s trace formula (Theorem 8 in
[
7
]
) states that
\psi\mapsto\Tr^{\flat}(\varphi^{*}_{\psi})
defines a distribution
\Tr^{\flat}(\varphi^{*})
on
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, given by

\Tr^{\flat}(\varphi^{*})=\sum_{\gamma(l)=\gamma(0)}\frac{T^{\#}_{\gamma}\delta_{l}}{|\det(1-P_{\gamma})|}.

(1.2)

Here the sum is over all periodic flow curves (modulo time shifts), with all possible periods
l
, and
T^{\#}_{\gamma}
is the primitive period of such a curve.

One application of Guillemin’s trace formula (
1.2
) is that it is the basis of proofs of analytic properties of the
Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function

[
17
]
for Anosov flows, such as meromorphic continuation (see Section 2.2 in
[
6
]
, or
[
4
]
) and homotopy invariance (see Theorem 2 in
[
3
]
).

An equivariant Guillemin trace formula

Our goal in this paper is to generalise Guillemin’s trace formula (
1.2
) to an equivariant version, for proper, cocompact group actions by unimodular, locally compact groups. In another paper
[
9
]
, we use this generalisation to construct an eqiuvariant version of the Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function. We then compare this with equivariant analytic torsion
[
8
]
and investigate an equivariant generalisation of the Fried conjecture
[
5
,
17
]
. In
[
9
]
, direct consequences of the equivariant Guillemin trace formula are that the definition of the equivariant Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function is independent of choices made, and a relation with the classical Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function. Analogously to the non-equivariant case, it is possible that the equivariant Guillemin trace formula is the basis for future results on analytic properties of the equivariant Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function.

We no longer assume that
M
is compact, but suppose that a unimodular, locally compact group
G
acts properly on
M
, such that
M/G
is compact. Then there is a function
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
such that for all
m\in M
, with respect to a Haar measure
dx
,

\int_{G}\chi(xm)\,dx=1.

Let
g\in G
, and suppose that its centraliser
Z
is unimodular. Then there is a unique
G
-invariant measure
d(hZ)
on
G/Z
compatible with given Haar measures on
G
and
Z
. In several places, for example
[
10
,
11
,
16
]
, the
g
-trace
of operators with smooth kernels is used to obtain equivariant index theorems in different settings. A version for discrete groups was used before to construct higher invariants, see for example
[
14
,
18
,
20
]
.
Let
E\to M
be a
G
-equivariant, Hermitian vector bundle. Let
T
be an operator from
\Gamma^{\infty}_{c}(E)
to
\Gamma^{\infty}(E)
with a smooth Schwartz kernel
\kappa_{T}
. If the following converges, then the
g
-trace of
T
is

\Tr_{g}(T):=\int_{G/Z}\int_{M}\chi(m)\tr(hgh^{-1}\kappa_{T}(hg^{-1}h^{-1}m,m))\,dm\,d(hZ),

(1.3)

where
dm
is a given
G
-invariant density on
M
.

As a common generalisation of the traces (
1.1
) and (
1.3
), we start by developing the
flat
g
-trace

\Tr^{\flat}_{g}
in Definition
2.3
. This a generalisation of (
1.3
) to operators with distributional Schwartz kernels with suitable transversality properties.

Using this flat
g
-trace, we generalise Guillemin’s trace formula (
1.2
) to
G
-equivariant flows satisfying a suitable nondegeneracy condition. This is our main result, Theorem
2.28
. It also involves a lift of
\varphi
to a
G
-equivariant vector bundle, and composition with a
G
-equivariant endomorphism of this bundle. The basic idea is that the sum over periodic flow curves in (
1.2
) is replaced by flow curves
\gamma
such that
\gamma(l)=hgh^{-1}\gamma(0)
for
l\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
h\in G
.

2
Preliminaries and results

2.1
The flat
g
-trace

The main result in this paper is
the equivariant Guillemin trace formula, Theorem
2.28
. It is stated in terms of
the
flat
g
-trace
that we introduce in this subsection.
This flat
g
-trace is a common generalisation of the distributional trace used for example in Theorem 19.4.1 in
[
13
]
, and a
g
-trace defined in terms of orbital integrals, and used for example in
[
10
,
11
,
16
]
.

Let
M
be a manifold, on which a unimodular, locally compact group
G
acts properly and cocompactly (by which we mean that
M/G
is compact).
Fix a
G
-equivariant vector bundle
E\to M
. Fix a Haar measure
dx
on
G
.
Let
g\in G
, and let
Z<G
be ts centraliser. Suppose that
Z
is unimodular; then there is a
G
-invariant measure
d(hZ)
on
G/Z
.

For notational simplicity, we fix a
G
-invariant, nonvanishing density
dm
on
M
, and use this to identify the space
\Gamma^{-\infty}(E)
of generalised sections of
E
with the continuous dual of
\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E^{*})
. What follows does not depend on the choice of this density, see for example Remark
2.5
.
We use analogous identifications for other vector bundles as well. For example, we identify

\Gamma^{-\infty}((E\boxtimes E^{*})|_{\Delta(M)})\cong\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E^{*}\boxtimes E)|_{\Delta(M)})^{\prime}

where the prime on the right hand side denotes the continuous dual space, and
\Delta(M)\subset M\times M
is the diagonal.

We define a flat
g
-trace of operators on
\Gamma^{\infty}(E)
in Definition
2.3
. We later apply this to flows in (
2.8
).

The identity operator
\Id_{E}
on
E
defines an element of

\Gamma^{\infty}(E\boxtimes E^{*}|_{\Delta(M)})\cong\Gamma^{\infty}(E^{*}\boxtimes E|_{\Delta(M)}),

(2.1)

where the isomorphism is swapping the components in
E
and
E^{*}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The
fibre-wise trace
of
\kappa\in\Gamma^{-\infty}((E\boxtimes E^{*})|_{\Delta(M)})
is the distribution
\tr(\kappa)\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Delta(M))
defined by

\langle\tr(\kappa),f\rangle:=\langle\kappa,f\Id_{E}\rangle,

for
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Delta(M))
.

Example 2.2
.

In the case where
\kappa\in\Gamma^{\infty}((E\boxtimes E^{*})|_{\Delta(M)})
is smooth, let
m\in M
, let
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\rank(E)}
be a basis of
E_{m}
, and
\{e^{j}\}_{j=1}^{\rank(E)}
be the dual basis of
E^{*}_{m}
. Then under the isomorphism (
2.1
),

\Id_{E_{m}}\mapsto\sum_{j}e^{j}\otimes e_{j}.

Therefore, viewing
\kappa(m,m)
as an endomorphism of
E_{m}
, we have

\langle\kappa(m,m),\Id_{E_{m}}\rangle=\sum_{j}\langle e^{j},\kappa(m,m)e_{j}\rangle=\tr(\kappa(m,m)),

where
\tr
is the fibre-wise operator trace.
It follows that
\tr(\kappa)
is indeed the fibre-wise trace of
\kappa
, and lies in
C^{\infty}(\Delta(M))
.

Let
T\colon\Gamma^{\infty}_{c}(E)\to\Gamma^{\infty}(E)
be a
G
-equivariant, continuous linear operator. Let
\kappa
be its Schwartz kernel. For
x\in G
, we write
x\cdot\kappa
for the Schwartz kernel of
x\circ T
, where
x
acts on sections in the usual way.
Let
\mathcal{N}^{*}(\Delta(M))\to\Delta(M)
be the conormal bundle of
\Delta(M)
in
M\times M
.

Because the action by
G
on
M
is proper and cocompact, there is a
cutoff function

\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
such that for all
m\in M

\int_{G}\chi(xm)\,dx=1.

(2.2)

Let
p_{2}\colon M\times M\to M
be projection onto the second coordinate. Then
(p_{2}^{*}\chi)|_{\Delta(M)}
has compact support. Note that the function
(p_{2}^{*}\chi)|_{\Delta(M)}
is just the function on
\Delta(M)
corresponding to
\chi
via the diagonal diffeomorphism
\Delta\colon M\to\Delta(M)
. The wave front set of a distribution is denoted by
\WF
; see Definition 8.1.2 in
[
12
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Suppose that for all
h\in G
,

\WF(hgh^{-1}\cdot\kappa)\cap\mathcal{N}^{*}(\Delta(M))=\emptyset.

(2.3)

Suppose also that for all
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
, the integral

\int_{G/Z}\langle\tr((hgh^{-1}\cdot\kappa)|_{\Delta(M)}),(p_{2}^{*}f)|_{\Delta(M)}\rangle\,d(hZ)

(2.4)

converges absolutely, and that this defines a distribution on
M
.
Then we say that
T
is
flat
g
-trace class
, and define its
flat
g
-trace
by

\Tr_{g}^{\flat}(T):=\int_{G/Z}\langle\tr((hgh^{-1}\cdot\kappa)|_{\Delta(M)}),(p_{2}^{*}\chi)|_{\Delta(M)}\rangle\,d(hZ).

(2.5)

Remark 2.4
.

The condition (
2.3
) implies that the restriction
(hgh^{-1}\cdot\kappa)|_{\Delta(M)}
in (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) is well-defined. See e.g. Theorem 8.2.4 in
[
12
]
.

Remark 2.5
.

If we view
\kappa
as a generalised section in
\Gamma^{-\infty}(E\boxtimes E^{*})
, this implicitly involves the density
dm\times dm
. The identification of
\Gamma^{-\infty}(E\boxtimes E^{*})
with
\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E^{*}\boxtimes E)^{\prime}
via the same density, used in (
2.5
), cancels out the choice of density in a sense, so that
\Tr_{g}^{\flat}(T)
does not depend on
dm
. Compare Lemma 2.9 in
[
8
]
in the case where
\kappa
is smooth.

Example 2.6
.

If
G
is the trivial group, so
g=e
, then
\chi
may be taken to be the constant
1
. So the flat
e
-trace is the classical flat trace, used for example in Theorem 19.4.1 in
[
13
]
.

Example 2.7
.

If
\kappa
is smooth, then by Example
2.2
,

\Tr_{g}^{\flat}(T)=\int_{G/Z}\int_{M}\chi(m)\tr(hgh^{-1}\kappa(hg^{-1}h^{-1}m,m))\,dm\,d(hZ).

If
T\chi
is a trace-class operator on
L^{2}(E)
and the integral converges, then this equals the

g
-trace

\Tr_{g}(T):=\int_{G/Z}\Tr(hgh^{-1}T\chi)\,d(hZ).

(2.6)

This trace has been used in various places, see for example
[
8
,
10
,
11
,
14
,
18
]
.
In this sense, the flat
g
-trace
\Tr_{g}^{\flat}
is a generalisation of the
g
-trace
\Tr_{g}
, in the same way that the classical flat trace, see Example
2.6
, generalises the operator trace in some settings.

Example 2.8
.

If
g=e
, then
\Tr_{e}^{\flat}=\langle\tr(\kappa|_{\Delta(M)}),(p_{2}^{*}\chi)|_{\Delta(M)}\rangle
, a distributional version of the von Neumann trace
[
1
,
19
]
.

Proposition 2.9
.

The integral (
2.5
) is independent of the function
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
satisfying (

).

This proposition is proved in Subsection

.

2.2
Equivariant flows

Let
\varphi
be the flow of a smooth vector field

on
M
. Suppose that

Let

Suppose that for all

\varphi_{t}\colon M\to M
is
G
-equivariant.
This is true in the following special cases.

Lemma 2.10
.

Suppose that
G
acts isometrically on a Riemannian manifold
X

is the unit sphere bundle of

\varphi
is the geodesic flow. Then

Proof.

As
G
acts by isometries, its elements map geodesics to geodesics. This implies the claim.
∎

Example 2.11
.

Let
X
be a compact manifold. Let
M
be the universal cover of

, acted on by the fundamental group

of

. A flow
\varphi_{X}
on

-equivariant lift to a flow

on

. The generating vector field

of

has no zeroes.

Example 2.12
.

Suppose that

is a compact Lie group, acting on a compact manifold

.
Let

be the unit sphere in the Lie algebra

of

, with respect to an

-invariant inner product. Let

, and the flow

\begin{split}g\cdot(p,Y)&=(gp,\Ad(g)Y);\\

,

,

and

. Then

. The generating vector field

, where

is the tangent vector at

has no zeroes if and only if the action is locally free.

Now we assume that
\Phi
is a
G
-equivariant, fibre-wise linear flow on

.
For a function
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
, consider the operator
\Phi^{*}_{\psi}
on

defined by

(\Phi^{*}_{\psi}s)(m):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(t)\Phi_{-t}(s(\varphi_{t}(m)))\,dt,

(2.7)

for

and

.

Let

We will obtain a criterion for the operator

Fix

As in Subsection

Definition 2.17

Let

The

(2.12)

Let

(b)

Lemma 3.3

arbitrarily closely if the smooth functions
\zeta_{j}
are chosen so that the function

s\mapsto\sum_{\alpha\in J}1_{[t_{\alpha}-\varepsilon,t_{\alpha}+\varepsilon]}(s)\zeta_{\alpha}(\gamma(s))

approaches
1_{I_{\gamma}}
closely enough in
L^{1}
-norm. The left hand side of (
4.5
) is independent of the functions
\zeta
and
\psi
with the properties mentioned, and therefore equals

\frac{1}{|\det(1-P^{x}_{\gamma})|}\int_{I_{\gamma}}f(l,\gamma(s))\,ds,

which completes the proof.
∎

4.3
Proof of Theorem
2.28

We will start by proving Theorem
2.28
in the case where
E
is the trivial line bundle.

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose that
E
is the trivial line bundle.
Suppose that
\varphi
is
g
-nondegenerate.
Then the operator
\Phi^{*}_{\psi}
is flat
g
-trace class for all
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
.
Furthermore,

\Tr^{\flat}_{g}(\Phi^{*})=\int_{G/Z}\sum_{l\in L_{g}(\varphi)}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{hgh^{-1}}_{l}(\varphi)}\frac{\delta_{l}}{|\det(1-P_{\gamma}^{hgh^{-1}})|}T_{\gamma}\,d(hZ).

(4.6)

Proof.

By Lemmas
3.9
and
4.2
, we have for all
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
,

\langle\Tr^{\flat}_{g}(\Phi^{*}),\psi\rangle=\int_{G/Z}\sum_{l\in L_{g}(\varphi)}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{hgh^{-1}}_{l}(\varphi)}\frac{1}{|\det(1-P_{\gamma}^{hgh^{-1}})|}\psi(l)\int_{I_{\gamma}}\chi(\gamma(s))\,ds\,d(hZ).

Here we have also used (
2.18
).
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Suppose that
G=\{e\}
, so
\chi\equiv 1
.
Then by Example
2.21
, the equality (
4.6
) in Proposition
4.3
becomes

\Tr^{\flat}_{e}(\Phi^{*})=\sum_{l\in L_{e}(\varphi)}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{e}_{l}(\varphi)}\frac{\delta_{l}}{|\det(1-P_{\gamma}^{e})|}T_{\gamma}^{\#}.

This is Guillemin’s trace formula (II.17) in
[
7
]
; see also (1.5) in
[
4
]
.

Proposition
4.3
is the special case of Theorem
2.28
when
E
is the trivial line bundle. We now show how this special case implies
Theorem
2.28
for general vector bundles
E
(compare also the end of Appendix B in
[
4
]
). Fix
x\in G
.
Let
A\in\End(E)
, and suppose
A
commutes with both the
G
-action and with
\Phi
.
Consider the function
\tr(x\cdot A\Phi)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\times\Delta(M))
, given in terms of a local frame
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\rank(E)}
for
E
, with dual frame
\{e^{j}\}_{j=1}^{\rank(E)}
, by

\tr(x\cdot A\Phi)(t,m,m):=\sum_{j=1}^{\rank(E)}\left\langle e^{j}(m),((x\cdot A\Phi_{-t})e_{j})(m)\right\rangle

(4.7)

for
t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
m\in M
.
Let
x\cdot K^{0}
be the Schwartz kernel of the operator given by pulling back scalar functions along
x^{-1}\circ\varphi_{t}
,
as in (
2.9
) with
E=M\times\mathbb{C}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose that
\varphi
is
g
-nondegenerate.
We have the following equality in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\times\Delta(M))
:

\tr\left(x\cdot AK|_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\times\Delta(M)}\right)=\tr(x\cdot A\Phi)\left(x\cdot K^{0}|_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\times\Delta(M)}\right).

(4.8)

Proof.

Consider a local frame
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\rank(E)}
for
E
defined in an open set
U\subset M
, with dual frame
\{e^{j}\}_{j=1}^{\rank(E)}
for
E^{*}
. Extending (
4.7
), we define
\tilde{\tr}(x\cdot A\Phi)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\times U\times U)
by

\tilde{\tr}(x\cdot A\Phi)(t,m,m^{\prime}):=\sum_{j=1}^{\rank(E)}\left\langle e^{j}(m),((x\cdot A\Phi_{-t})e_{j})(m)\right\rangle,

for
t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
m,m^{\prime}\in U
. Observe that this function is constant in the second factor
U
.

Define
\tilde{\tr}(x\cdot AK)\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\times U\times U)
by

\left\langle\tilde{\tr}(x\cdot AK),\psi\otimes f_{1}\otimes f_{2}\right\rangle=\sum_{j}\left\langle x\cdot AK,\psi\otimes f_{1}e^{j}\otimes f_{2}e_{j}\right\rangle,

for
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
, and
f_{1},f_{2}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U)
. Then for all such
\psi
,
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
, (
2.7
) and (
2.9
) imply that

\left\langle\tilde{\tr}(x\cdot AK),\psi\otimes f_{1}\otimes f_{2}\right\rangle\\
=\sum_{j}\int_{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(t)f_{1}(m)f_{2}(\varphi_{t}x^{-1}m)\left\langle e^{j}(m),xA\Phi_{-t}e_{j}(\varphi_{t}(x^{-1}m))\right\rangle\,dt\,dm\\
=\left\langle x\cdot K^{0},\tilde{\tr}(x\cdot A\Phi)\cdot(\psi\otimes f_{1}\otimes f_{2})\right\rangle

so

\tilde{\tr}(x\cdot AK)=\tilde{\tr}(x\cdot A\Phi)\cdot(x\cdot K^{0}).

The restrictions of both sides to
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\times\Delta(U)
are well-defined by Proposition
2.13
. Taking these restrictions and using

\begin{split}\tilde{\tr}(x\cdot A\Phi)|_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\times\Delta(U)}&=\tr(x\cdot A\Phi)|_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\times\Delta(U)};\\
\tilde{\tr}(x\cdot AK)|_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\times\Delta(U)}&=\tr(x\cdot AK|_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\times\Delta(U)}),\end{split}

and partitions of unity with respect to sets like
U
,
we obtain (
4.8
).
∎

Theorem

\tr(x\cdot A\Phi)(t,m,m)=\tr(Ax(\Phi_{-t})_{m}),

the trace of the linear endomorphism
A\circ x\circ(\Phi_{-t})_{m}
of
E_{m}
.

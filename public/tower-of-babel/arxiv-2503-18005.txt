A Simple Strategy to Deal with Toxic Flow
Álvaro Cartea, Leandro Sánchez-Betancourt

Abstract
We model the trading activity between a broker and her clients (informed and uninformed traders) as an infinite-horizon stochastic control problem. We derive the broker’s optimal dealing strategy in closed form and use this to introduce an algorithm that bypasses the need to calibrate individual parameters, so the dealing strategy can be executed in real-world trading environments. Finally, we characterise the discount in the price of liquidity a broker offers clients. The discount strikes the optimal balance between maximising the order flow from the broker’s clients and minimising adverse selection losses to the informed traders.



1
Introduction

We derive explicit optimal trading strategies for brokers and traders in an infinite-horizon framework. The broker’s strategy optimally balances three key objectives: (i) internalise and externalise client flow, (ii) manage inventory risk, and (iii) infer market signals to execute speculative trades. Specifically, the broker’s strategy is a linear combination of four state variables: broker’s inventory, informed trader’s inventory, volume traded by informed traders, and volume traded by uniformed traders. Unlike previous models that require precise calibration of individual model parameters, we introduce an algorithm that bypasses this requirement, which makes the strategy suitable in real-world trading environments. This is relevant to brokers operating in markets with both informed and uninformed traders, where optimal flow management and speculative trading are crucial for profitability and risk mitigation.

Closest to our work is
Cartea and Sánchez-Betancourt, 2022
—henceforth CSB— who study a finite-horizon trading problem between a broker and her clients (informed and uninformed traders). In contrast, we solve the infinite-horizon problem, which yields stationary strategies rather than time-dependent strategies. We use the linear structure of the strategies to develop a simple algorithm that bypasses the need for separate calibration of individual model parameters, which is key to implement the algorithm in the marketplace.

The internalisation-externalisation problem (see e.g.,
Butz and Oomen, 2019
) faced by brokers has been extensively studied.

Bergault and Sánchez-Betancourt, 2024
investigate the mean-field game that arises in the CSB framework when a broker faces infinitely many informed traders (each with their own private signal and a common signal).
Cartea et al., 2024
study the perfect information Nash equilibrium of CSB with a focus on understanding the effects of information leakage. Simultaneously,
Aqsha et al., 2024
and
Wu and Jaimungal, 2024
deal with the incomplete information version of the problem, where the former develops filtering techniques for how the broker might filter the alpha signal, and the latter characterises the equilibrium with systems of forward-backward stochastic differential equations and incomplete information sets.

Let the trading horizon be
{\mathfrak{T}}=[0,\infty)

\displaystyle=

(\nu^{I}_{t})_{t\in{\mathfrak{T}}}

The inventory of the informed trader

\mathrm{d}Q^{I}_{t}=\nu^{I}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{d}Q^{B}_{t}=\left(\nu^{B}_{t}-\nu^{I}_{t}-\nu^{U}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t\,,

X^{I}_{t}

X^{B}_{t}
follow

\mathrm{d}X^{I}_{t}=-(S_{t}+k^{I}\,\nu^{I}_{t})\nu^{I}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{d}X^{B}_{t}=\left((S_{t}+k^{I}\,\nu^{I}_{t})\nu^{I}_{t}+(S_{t}+k^{U}\,\nu^{U}_{t})\nu^{U}_{t}-(S_{t}+k^{B}\,\nu^{B}_{t})\nu^{B}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t\,,

(5)

respectively. Here,
k^{B}
is the transaction cost parameter in the lit market where the broker also trades.

Next, we use a well-known finite-horizon performance criterion to motivate the derivation of the broker’s infinite-horizon performance criterion. Specifically, we write the finite-horizon performance criterion

\mathbb{E}\left[X^{I}_{T}+Q^{I}_{T}\,S_{T}-a^{I}\,\left(Q^{I}_{T}\right)^{2}-\phi^{I}\int_{0}^{T}\left(Q^{I}_{t}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right]

(6)

x+q^{I}\,S-a^{I}\,(q^{I})^{2}+\mathbb{E}_{\,x,\alpha,S,q^{I}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(-\nu^{I}_{t}\left(S_{t}+k^{I}\,\nu^{I}_{t}\right)+S_{t}\,\nu^{I}_{t}+\alpha_{t}\,Q^{I}_{t}-2\,a^{I}\,Q^{I}_{t}\,\nu^{I}_{t}-\phi^{I}\,\left(Q^{I}_{t}\right)^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right],

(7)

where
x,q^{I},S,\alpha
are the starting points of the processes for cash, inventory, midprice, and signal, respectively —we omit the stochastic integral
\int^{T}_{0}\sigma\,Q^{I}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}W^{S}_{t}

In the infinite-horizon case, the performance criterion of the informed trader is

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}^{\nu^{I}}(x,\alpha,S,q^{I})

\displaystyle=x+q^{I}\,S-a^{I}\,(q^{I})^{2}

(8)

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}_{x,\alpha,S,q^{I}}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta\,t}\,\left(-k^{I}\,\left(\nu^{I}_{t}\right)^{2}+\alpha_{t}\,Q^{I}_{t}-2\,a^{I}\,Q^{I}_{t}\,\nu^{I}_{t}-\phi^{I}\,\left(Q^{I}_{t}\right)^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

where
\beta>0

\mathscr{H}(x,\alpha,S,q^{I})=\sup_{\nu^{I}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{I}}\mathscr{H}^{\nu^{I}}(x,\alpha,S,q^{I})\,.

(10)

Similarly, the finite-horizon performance criterion of the broker is

\mathbb{E}\left[X^{B}_{T}+Q^{B}_{T}\,S_{T}-a^{B}\,\left(Q^{B}_{T}\right)^{2}-\phi^{B}\int_{0}^{T}\left(Q^{B}_{t}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right],

\displaystyle x^{B}+q^{B}\,S-a^{B}\,\left(q^{B}\right)^{2}+

(13)

\displaystyle\hskip 90.00014pt+b\,Q_{t}^{B}\,\nu^{B}_{t}+S_{t}\left(\nu^{B}_{t}-\nu^{I}_{t}-\nu^{U}_{t}\right)-2\,a^{B}\,Q^{B}_{t}\left(\nu^{B}_{t}-\nu^{I}_{t}-\nu^{U}_{t}\right)-\phi^{B}\,\left(Q^{B}_{t}\right)^{2}\bigg\}\mathrm{d}t\Bigg],

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Q}^{\nu^{B}}

\displaystyle(x^{B},\alpha,q^{B},q^{I},\nu^{U},S)

(15)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x^{B},\alpha,q^{B},q^{I},\nu^{U},S}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta\,t}\bigg\{\beta\left(x^{B}+q^{B}\,S-a^{B}\,\left(q^{B}\right)^{2}\right)+k^{I}\left(\nu^{I}_{t}\right)^{2}+k^{U}\left(\nu^{U}_{t}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 70.0001pt-k^{B}\left(\nu^{B}_{t}\right)^{2}+Q^{B}_{t}\,\alpha_{t}+b\,Q_{t}^{B}\,\nu^{B}_{t}-2\,a^{B}\,Q^{B}_{t}\left(\nu^{B}_{t}-\nu^{I}_{t}-\nu^{U}_{t}\right)-\phi^{B}\,\left(Q^{B}_{t}\right)^{2}\bigg\}\mathrm{d}t\Bigg],

where

is the discount parameter,

.

The infinite-time horizon optimal trading rate of the informed trader is

\nu^{I,*}_{t}=A\,\alpha_{t}-B\,Q^{I*}_{t},

.

The infinite-time horizon optimal trading rate of the broker is

\displaystyle\nu^{B,*}

\displaystyle=-C\,q^{B}-D\,q^{I}+E\,\alpha+F\,\nu^{U}\,,

\displaystyle=-C\,q^{B}-G\,q^{I}+H\,\nu^{I}+F\,\nu^{U}\,,

The following subsections provide formal derivations.

2.1
Infinite-time horizon: Informed trader’s strategy

The value function of the informed trader satisfies the HJB equation

\displaystyle-\beta\left(\mathscr{H}-x-q^{I}\,S+a^{I}\,(q^{I})^{2}\,\right)+\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}\mathscr{H}+\mathcal{L}^{S}\mathscr{H}

(23)

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha\,q^{I}-\phi^{I}(q^{I})^{2}+\sup\limits_{\nu^{I}}\left\{\nu^{I}\mathscr{H}_{q^{I}}-\nu^{I}\,(S+k^{I}\,\nu^{I})\,\mathscr{H}_{x}-2\,a^{I}\,q^{I}\,\nu^{I}-k^{I}\,\left(\nu^{I}\right)^{2}\right\}=0\,.

Thus, the feedback control is

\nu^{I,*}=\frac{\mathscr{H}_{q^{I}}-S\,\mathscr{H}_{x}-2\,a^{I}\,q^{I}}{2k^{I}\,(1+\mathscr{H}_{x})}\,,

(24)

which we substitute in the HJB to write

-\beta\left(\mathscr{H}-x-q^{I}\,S+a^{I}\,(q^{I})^{2}\,\right)+\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}\mathscr{H}+\mathcal{L}^{S}\mathscr{H}+\alpha\,q^{I}-\phi^{I}(q^{I})^{2}+\frac{(\mathscr{H}_{q^{I}}-2\,a^{I}\,q^{I}-S\,\mathscr{H}_{x})^{2}}{4k^{I}(1+\mathscr{H}_{x})}=0\,.

(25)

We use the ansatz
\mathscr{H}(\alpha,S,q^{I})=x+q^{I}\,S-a^{I}\,\left(q^{I}\right)^{2}+\mathfrak{h}(\alpha,q^{I})
to obtain

\displaystyle-\beta\,\mathfrak{h}+\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}\mathfrak{h}+2\,\alpha\,q^{I}-\phi^{I}(q^{I})^{2}+\frac{(\partial_{q^{I}}\mathfrak{h}-4\,a^{I}\,q^{I})^{2}}{8\,k^{I}}=0\,,

(26)

and the optimal control in feedback form becomes

\nu^{I,*}=\frac{\partial_{q^{I}}\mathfrak{h}-4\,a^{I}\,q^{I}}{4\,k^{I}}\,.

(27)

Next, substitute the ansatz

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}(\alpha,q^{I})=\mathfrak{h}_{0}(\alpha)+\mathfrak{h}_{1}(\alpha)\,q^{I}+\mathfrak{h}_{2}(\alpha)\,\left(q^{I}\right)^{2}

(28)

in (
25
) to obtain the system of PDEs

\displaystyle-\beta\mathfrak{h}_{0}+\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}\mathfrak{h}_{0}+\frac{\left(\mathfrak{h}_{1}\right)^{2}}{4\,k^{I}}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(29)

\displaystyle-\beta\mathfrak{h}_{1}+\alpha+\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}\mathfrak{h}_{1}+\frac{\mathfrak{h}_{1}\,\left(\mathfrak{h}_{2}-a^{I}\right)}{k^{I}}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(30)

\displaystyle-\beta\mathfrak{h}_{2}+\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}\mathfrak{h}_{2}-\phi^{I}+\frac{(\mathfrak{h}_{2}-2\,a^{I})^{2}}{2\,k^{I}}

\displaystyle=0\,.

(31)

The solution to the PDE satisfied by
\mathfrak{h}_{2}
is constant and is given by

\mathfrak{h}_{2}=2\,a^{I}+\beta\,k^{I}-\sqrt{4\,a^{I}\,\beta\,k^{I}+\left(\beta\,k^{I}\right)^{2}+2\,k^{I}\,\phi^{I}}\,.

(32)

For
\mathfrak{h}_{1}
we use the ansatz

\mathfrak{h}_{1}(\alpha)=\mathfrak{g}_{0}+\mathfrak{g}_{1}\,\alpha\,,

(33)

from which we find that
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=0
and
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
satisfies a linear equation with solution given by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\frac{4\,k^{I}}{2\,a^{I}-\mathfrak{h}_{2}+2\,\beta\,k^{I}+2\,\kappa^{\alpha}\,k^{I}}\,.

(34)

Thus, the optimal trading rate is

\nu^{I,*}=A\,\alpha-B\,q\,,

(35)

where

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta\,k^{I}+2\,k^{I}\,\kappa^{\alpha}+\sqrt{4\,a^{I}\,\beta\,k^{I}+\left(\beta\,k^{I}\right)^{2}+2\,k^{I}\,\phi^{I}}}\,,

(36)

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{a^{I}\,\beta}{k^{I}}+\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{\phi^{I}}{2\,k^{I}}}-\frac{\beta}{2}\,.

(37)

It is easy to see that
A>0
and
B>0
. The informed trader’s infinite-horizon strategy is simple, it is linear in the inventory and linear in the alpha signal. Everything else being equal, when inventory increases (decreases) the speed of trading decreases (increases). Thus, this term of the strategy exerts pressure to make inventories mean revert to zero.

The other term of the strategy takes advantage of the signal and trades in the same direction as the trend of the stock price. It is straightforward to see that as the cost of trading
k^{I}
in the LOB increases, the informed trader’s rate of trading decreases. Similarly, as the speed of mean reversion of the alpha signal increases, the trading rate slows down because trend opportunities disappear quickly.

2.2
Infinite-time horizon: Informed trader’s value function

We follow similar techniques to the ones above to find
\mathfrak{h}_{0}
, which is given by

\mathfrak{h}_{0}(\alpha)=f_{0}+f_{1}\,\alpha+f_{2}\,\alpha^{2}\,,

(38)

with

f_{1}=0\,,\qquad f_{2}=\frac{(\mathfrak{g}_{1})^{2}}{8\,\beta\,k^{I}+16\,\kappa^{\alpha}\,k^{I}}\,,\qquad f_{0}=\frac{f_{1}\,(\sigma^{\alpha})^{2}}{\beta}\,.

Thus, the value function of the informed trader in the infinite-horizon case is

\mathscr{H}(x,\alpha,q^{I},S)=x+q^{I}\,S-a^{I}\,\left(q^{I}\right)^{2}+\mathfrak{h}_{0}(\alpha)+\mathfrak{h}_{1}(\alpha)\,q^{I}+\mathfrak{h}_{2}\,(q^{I})^{2}\,,

(39)

with
\mathfrak{h}_{0},\mathfrak{h}_{1},
and
\mathfrak{h}_{2}
as above.

2.3
Infinite-time horizon: Broker’s trading strategy

The broker infers that the informed trader’s rate is of the form

F(\alpha,q^{I})=A\,\alpha-B\,q^{I}\,,

(40)

where
A,\,B
are constants. Below, when we write
\nu^{I}
, it is shorthand for
F(\alpha,q^{I})
, which we take to be
F(\alpha,q^{I})=A\,\alpha-B\,q^{I}
.
The broker’s value function satisfies the HJB equation

\displaystyle-\beta\,\left(\mathfrak{Q}-x^{B}-q^{B}\,S+a^{B}\,\left(q^{B}\right)^{2}\right)+k^{I}\,\left(\nu^{I}\right)^{2}+k^{U}\,\left(\nu^{U}\right)^{2}+q^{B}\,\alpha+2\,a^{B}\,q^{B}\,\left(\nu^{I}+\nu^{U}\right)

(41)

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}\mathfrak{Q}+\mathcal{L}^{\nu^{U}}\mathfrak{Q}+\partial_{x^{B}}\mathfrak{Q}\,\nu^{I}\left(S+k^{I}\,\nu^{I}\right)+\partial_{x^{B}}\mathfrak{Q}\,\nu^{U}\left(S+k^{U}\,\nu^{U}\right)+\nu^{I}\,\partial_{q^{I}}\mathfrak{Q}

(42)

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha\,\partial_{S}\mathfrak{Q}+\frac{1}{2}(\sigma^{S})^{2}\,\partial_{SS}\mathfrak{Q}-\left(\nu^{I}+\nu^{U}\right)\,\partial_{q^{B}}\mathfrak{Q}-\phi^{B}\,\left(q^{B}\right)^{2}

(43)

\displaystyle\quad+\sup\limits_{\nu^{B}}\bigg\{-k^{B}\left(\nu^{B}\right)^{2}+b\,q^{B}\,\nu^{B}-2\,a^{B}\,q^{B}\,\nu^{B}-\partial_{x^{B}}\mathfrak{Q}\,\nu^{B}\left(S+k^{B}\,\nu^{B}\right)+\partial_{S}\mathfrak{Q}\,b\,\nu^{B}+\nu^{B}\,\partial_{q^{B}}\mathfrak{Q}\bigg\}=0\,,

(44)

so the optimal control in feedback form is

\nu^{B*}=\frac{b\,q^{B}-2\,a^{B}\,q^{B}-\partial_{x^{B}}\mathfrak{Q}\,S+\partial_{S}\mathfrak{Q}\,b+\partial_{q^{B}}\mathfrak{Q}}{2\,k^{B}(1+\partial_{x^{B}}\mathfrak{Q})}\,,

(45)

and we write the PDE

\displaystyle-\beta\,\left(\mathfrak{Q}-x^{B}-q^{B}\,S+a^{B}\,\left(q^{B}\right)^{2}\right)+k^{I}\,\left(\nu^{I}\right)^{2}+k^{U}\,\left(\nu^{U}\right)^{2}+q^{B}\,\alpha+2\,a^{B}\,q^{B}\,\left(\nu^{I}+\nu^{U}\right)

(46)

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}\mathfrak{Q}+\mathcal{L}^{\nu^{U}}\mathfrak{Q}+\partial_{x^{B}}\mathfrak{Q}\,\nu^{I}\left(S+k^{I}\,\nu^{I}\right)+\partial_{x^{B}}\mathfrak{Q}\,\nu^{U}\left(S+k^{U}\,\nu^{U}\right)+\nu^{I}\,\partial_{q^{I}}\mathfrak{Q}

(47)

\displaystyle\qquad+\alpha\,\partial_{S}\mathfrak{Q}+\frac{1}{2}(\sigma^{S})^{2}\,\partial_{SS}\mathfrak{Q}-\left(\nu^{I}+\nu^{U}\right)\,\partial_{q^{B}}\mathfrak{Q}-\phi^{B}\,\left(q^{B}\right)^{2}

(48)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\left(b\,q^{B}-2\,a^{B}\,q^{B}-\partial_{x^{B}}\mathfrak{Q}\,S+\partial_{S}\mathfrak{Q}\,b+\partial_{q^{B}}\mathfrak{Q}\right)^{2}}{4\,k^{B}(1+\partial_{x^{B}}\mathfrak{Q})}=0\,.

(49)

Next, use the ansatz
\mathfrak{Q}(x^{B},\alpha,q^{B},q^{I},\nu^{U},S)=x^{B}+q^{B}\,S-a^{B}\,(q^{B})^{2}+\mathfrak{q}(\alpha,q^{B},q^{I},\nu^{U})
, to write the PDE satisfied by
\mathfrak{q}
:

\displaystyle-\beta\,\mathfrak{q}+2\,k^{I}\,\left(\nu^{I}\right)^{2}+2\,k^{U}\,\left(\nu^{U}\right)^{2}+2\,q^{B}\,\alpha+4\,a^{B}\,q^{B}\,\left(\nu^{I}+\nu^{U}\right)

(50)

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}\mathfrak{Q}+\mathcal{L}^{\nu^{U}}\mathfrak{Q}+\nu^{I}\,\partial_{q^{I}}\mathfrak{Q}-\left(\nu^{I}+\nu^{U}\right)\,\partial_{q^{B}}\mathfrak{q}-\phi^{B}\,\left(q^{B}\right)^{2}+\frac{\left(2\,b\,q^{B}-4\,a^{B}\,q^{B}+\partial_{q^{B}}\mathfrak{q}\right)^{2}}{8\,k^{B}}=0\,,

(51)

and the optimal control in feedback form becomes

\nu^{B*}=\frac{2\,b\,q^{B}-4\,a^{B}\,q^{B}+\partial_{q^{B}}\mathfrak{q}}{4\,k^{B}}\,.

(52)

Now, use a linear-quadratic ansatz in
q^{B}

\mathfrak{q}(\alpha,q^{B},q^{I},\nu^{U})=\mathfrak{q}_{0}(\alpha,q^{I},\nu^{U})+q^{B}\,\mathfrak{q}_{1}(\alpha,q^{I},\nu^{U})+\left(q^{B}\right)^{2}\,\mathfrak{q}_{2}\,,

(53)

to obtain the system of PDEs

\displaystyle-\beta\,\mathfrak{q}_{0}+2\,k^{I}\,\left(\nu^{I}\right)^{2}+2\,k^{U}\,\left(\nu^{U}\right)^{2}+\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}\mathfrak{q}_{0}+\mathcal{L}^{\nu^{U}}\mathfrak{q}_{0}+\nu^{I}\,\partial_{q^{I}}\mathfrak{q}_{0}-\left(\nu^{I}+\nu^{U}\right)\,\mathfrak{q}_{1}+\frac{\left(\mathfrak{q}_{1}\right)^{2}}{8\,k^{B}}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(54)

\displaystyle-\beta\,\mathfrak{q}_{1}+2\,\alpha+4\,a^{B}\,\left(\nu^{I}+\nu^{U}\right)+\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}\mathfrak{q}_{1}+\mathcal{L}^{\nu^{U}}\mathfrak{q}_{1}+\nu^{I}\,\partial_{q^{I}}\mathfrak{q}_{1}-2\,\left(\nu^{I}+\nu^{U}\right)\,\mathfrak{q}_{2}\qquad

(55)

\displaystyle+\frac{\left(b-2\,a^{B}+\mathfrak{q}_{2}\right)\,\mathfrak{q}_{1}}{2\,k^{B}}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(56)

\displaystyle-\beta\,\mathfrak{q}_{2}-\phi^{B}+\frac{\left(b-2\,a^{B}+\mathfrak{q}_{2}\right)^{2}}{2\,k^{B}}

\displaystyle=0\,.

(57)

The relevant root of the quadratic equation for
\mathfrak{q}_{2}
above is

\mathfrak{q}_{2}=2\,a^{B}-b+\beta\,k^{B}-\sqrt{4\,a^{B}\,\beta\,k^{B}-2\,b\,\beta\,k^{B}+\beta^{2}\,(k^{B})^{2}+2\,k^{B}\,\phi^{B}}\,.

Here, we assume that the value of the discount rate

. In what follows, we assume that

.
Lastly, for
\mathfrak{q}_{1}
, with the ansatz

\mathfrak{q}_{1}(\alpha,q^{I},\nu^{U})=\mathfrak{d}_{0}\,\alpha+\mathfrak{d}_{1}\,q^{I}+\mathfrak{d}_{2}\,\nu^{U},

(59)

write the system of equations

\displaystyle-\beta\,\mathfrak{d}_{0}+2+4\,a^{B}\,A+A\,\mathfrak{d}_{1}-2\,A\,\mathfrak{q}_{2}-\mathfrak{d}_{0}\,\kappa^{\alpha}+\mathfrak{d}_{0}\,\frac{b\,-2\,a^{B}+\mathfrak{q}_{2}}{2\,k^{B}}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(60)

\displaystyle-\beta\,\mathfrak{d}_{1}-4\,a^{B}\,B-B\,\mathfrak{d}_{1}+2\,B\,\mathfrak{q}_{2}+\mathfrak{d}_{1}\,\frac{b-2\,a^{B}+\mathfrak{q}_{2}}{2\,k^{B}}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(61)

\displaystyle-\beta\,\mathfrak{d}_{2}+4\,a^{B}-2\,\mathfrak{q}_{2}-\mathfrak{d}_{2}\,\kappa_{u}+\mathfrak{d}_{2}\,\frac{b-2\,a^{B}+\mathfrak{q}_{2}}{2\,k^{B}}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(62)

and conclude that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{d}_{2}

\displaystyle=2\,\left(2\,a^{B}-\mathfrak{q}_{2}\right)\,\left(\beta+\kappa_{u}+C\right)^{-1}\,,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{d}_{1}

\displaystyle=-2\,B\,\left(2\,a^{B}-\mathfrak{q}_{2}\right)\,\left(\beta+B+C\right)^{-1}\,,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{d}_{0}

\displaystyle=\left(2+2\,A(2\,a^{B}-\,\mathfrak{q}_{2})+A\,\mathfrak{d}_{1}\right)\,\left(\beta+\kappa^{\alpha}+C\right)^{-1}\,,

for

C=\frac{2\,a^{B}-b-\mathfrak{q}_{2}}{2\,k^{B}}\,.

(63)

Thus, the optimal trading speed of the broker is

\nu^{B,*}=-C\,q^{B}-D\,q^{I}+E\,\alpha+F\,\nu^{U}\,,

(64)

where

D=-\frac{\mathfrak{d}_{1}}{4\,k^{B}}\,,\qquad E=\frac{\mathfrak{d}_{0}}{4\,k^{B}}\,,\qquad F=\frac{\mathfrak{d}_{2}}{4\,k^{B}}\,.

(65)

The strategy above can be written as

\nu^{B,*}=-C\,q^{B}-G\,q^{I}+H\,\nu^{I}+F\,\nu^{U}\,,

(66)

where

G=D-\frac{B\,E}{A}\,,\qquad H=\frac{E}{A}\,.

It follows that
\mathfrak{q}_{2}<0
and
2\,a^{B}-b-\mathfrak{q}_{2}>0
because
2\,a^{B}>b
and
\beta>0
. Then,
C>0
,
\mathfrak{d}_{2}>0
, and
\mathfrak{d}_{1}<0
; recall that
B>0
. We have that
\mathfrak{d}_{0}>0
because
A>0
and

2\,(2\,a^{B}-\,\mathfrak{q}_{2})+\mathfrak{d}_{1}=2\,(2\,a^{B}-\,\mathfrak{q}_{2})\left(1-\frac{B}{\beta+B+C}\right)>0\,.

(67)

The above implies that the constants
C,D,E,F
are all positive, which implies that
H>0
. After some calculations one can show that

\text{sign}(G)=\text{sign}\left(\frac{1}{A}-\kappa^{\alpha}\frac{2\,k^{B}\,C+b}{\beta+B+C}\right)\,,

(68)

and simple examples show that the right-hand side can take either sign. Thus, the sign of
G
depends on model parameters. We remark that when the value of the discount rate
\beta
is large, the sign of
G
is positive.

We discuss the intuition of the infinite-horizon strategy in (
64
). As with the informed trader, the first term on the right-hand side of (
64
) induces reversion to zero in the inventory of the broker. The second term shows that the broker limits her exposure to ‘toxic’ inventory (acquired from trading with the informed trader). When the informed trader is long the asset, the broker is short the asset, so the strategy for the broker is to buy the asset to close positions. The third term is straightforward; the broker learns the signal from the informed trader, so she trades in the direction of the trend; alternatively, in (
66
) we see that the third term corresponds to the externalisation rate of ‘toxic’ flow.
4
4

4

In the case when
H>1
the broker speculates based on the trading strategy of the informed trader.
Finally, the last term is a partial hedge against the activity of the uninformed flow. Note that indirectly, the first term of the strategy will also have an effect on how the broker liquidates positions accumulated from trading with both the uniformed and informed traders.

We follow similar techniques to those above to find
\mathfrak{q}_{0}
, which is given by

\mathfrak{q}_{0}(\alpha,q^{I},\nu^{U})=\mathfrak{f}_{0}(\alpha,\nu^{U})+\mathfrak{f}_{1}(\alpha,\nu^{U})\,q^{I}+\mathfrak{f}_{2}\,(q^{I})^{2}\,,

(69)

where

\mathfrak{f}_{2}=\frac{-(\mathfrak{d}_{1})^{2}-8\,B\,\mathfrak{d}_{1}\,k^{B}-16\,(B)^{2}k^{B}\,k^{I}}{-8\,\beta\,k^{B}-16\,B\,k^{B}}

(70)

and

\mathfrak{f}_{1}(\alpha,\nu^{U})=\alpha\,\mathfrak{e}_{1}+\nu^{U}\,\mathfrak{e}_{2}\,,

(71)

for

\displaystyle\mathfrak{e}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{-\mathfrak{d}_{1}\,\mathfrak{d}_{2}+4\,\mathfrak{d}_{1}\,k^{B}-4\,B\,\mathfrak{d}_{2}\,k^{B}}{-4\,\beta\,k^{B}-4\,B\,k^{B}-4\,\kappa_{u}\,k^{B}}\,,

(72)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{e}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{-\mathfrak{d}_{0}\,\mathfrak{d}_{1}-4\,B\,\mathfrak{d}_{0}\,k^{B}+4\,A\,\mathfrak{d}_{1}\,k^{B}-8\,A\,\mathfrak{f}_{2}\,k^{B}+16\,A\,B\,k^{B}\,k^{I}}{-4\,\beta\,k^{B}-4\,B\,k^{B}-4\,\kappa^{\alpha}\,k^{B}}\,.

(73)

Similarly, write

\mathfrak{f}_{0}(\alpha,\nu^{U})=\mathfrak{c}_{0}+\mathfrak{c}_{1}\,\alpha+\mathfrak{c}_{2}\,\nu^{U}+\mathfrak{c}_{3}\,\alpha\,\nu^{U}+\mathfrak{c}_{4}\,\alpha^{2}+\mathfrak{c}_{5}\,\left(\nu^{U}\right)^{2}\,,

(74)

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{5}

\displaystyle=\frac{-(\mathfrak{d}_{2})^{2}+8\,\mathfrak{d}_{2}\,k^{B}-16\,k^{B}\,k^{U}}{-8\,\beta\,k^{B}-16\,\kappa_{u}\,k^{B}}\,,

(75)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{-(\mathfrak{d}_{0})^{2}+8\,A\,\mathfrak{d}_{0}\,k^{B}-8\,A\,\mathfrak{e}_{1}\,k^{B}-16\,(A)^{2}\,k^{B}\,k^{I}}{-8\,\beta\,k^{B}-16\,\kappa^{\alpha}\,k^{B}}\,,

(76)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{-\mathfrak{d}_{0}\,\mathfrak{d}_{2}+4\,\mathfrak{d}_{0}\,k^{B}+4\,A\,\mathfrak{d}_{2}\,k^{B}-4\,A\,\mathfrak{e}_{2}\,k^{B}}{-4\,\beta\,k^{B}-4\,\kappa^{\alpha}\,k^{B}-4\,\kappa_{u}\,k^{B}}\,,

(77)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{2}

\displaystyle=0\,,\qquad\mathfrak{c}_{1}=0\,,\qquad\mathfrak{c}_{0}=\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{4}\,(\sigma^{\alpha})^{2}+\mathfrak{c}_{5}\,(\sigma^{U})^{2}}{\beta}\,.

(78)

Thus, the value function of the broker in the infinite-horizon case is given by

\mathfrak{Q}(x^{B},\alpha,q^{B},q^{I},\nu^{U},S)=x^{B}+q^{B}\,S-a^{B}\,(q^{B})^{2}+\mathfrak{q}_{0}(\alpha,q^{I},\nu^{U})+\mathfrak{q}_{1}(\alpha,q^{I},\nu^{U})\,q^{B}+\mathfrak{q}_{2}\,\left(q^{B}\right)^{2}\,,

(79)

with
\mathfrak{q}_{0},\mathfrak{q}_{1},
and
\mathfrak{q}_{2}
as above.

4
Conclusions

We developed an internalisation-externalisation strategy for a broker who trades with informed and uniformed counterparties. We obtained the optimal strategies in closed-form, which we used to provide an algorithm to train the optimal trading strategy on data. This algorithm is interpretable and easy to implement. Furthermore, it bypasses the need for calibrating model parameters.

Complete families of generic covers of elliptic curves
Gabriel Bujokas & Anand Patel

Abstract




1.
Introduction

1.1.
Objectives

The purpose of this paper is first to pose a question in the guise of
Conjecture

2.10
, then to explain why it is important in
Theorem

2.15
, and finally to give evidence in support of the conjecture in
Theorem

3.1
.
Conjecture

2.10
posits the existence of certain complete families of general curves in the Hurwitz stack
\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}(X)
parametrizing degree
d
branched coverings of a genus
1
curve
X
. We provide evidence for the conjecture when
g=2
or when
d\leq 5
, and
g
is large. The large-ness assumption can most likely be removed with more care. Our intention here is to sketch a general conjectural picture, rather than get caught up in interesting technical details.

While the
d\leq 5
verification does use the usual unirational description of spaces of covers, the content of the conjecture seems to us quite orthogonal to the existence of unirational parametrizations. In particular, when
g=2
and
d
is large, the Hurwitz stack under question is certainly not unirational, supporting this intuition. We list some natural next steps at the end of the paper for interested readers wanting to join the fray.

1.2.
Why we care

Our reason for thinking about
Conjecture

2.10
is in
Theorem

2.15
: we would obtain a uniform lower bound of
5
for the slope
s_{g}
of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
, i.e. if an effective divisor in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
has divisor class
a\lambda-b\delta
then the conjecture implies
a/b\geq 5
.

The use of covers of elliptic curves is by no means new to the conversation around
s_{g}
. Of particular note is Dawei Chen’s work in which he studies the slopes of the Teichmüller curves parametrizing covers of elliptic curves with only one branch point
[
Che11
]
.
1
1

1

The authors were directly inspired by this work.
His investigations ultimately gave a lower bound of
O(1/g)
for
s_{g}
, as many other attempts have done. We also recommend reading Chen’s beautiful paper
[
Che10
]
as well as the informative survey article by Chen, Farkas, and Morrison
[
CFM12
]
.

Our basic message is that we should attempt to better understand the stratification of the Hurwitz stack by the isomorphism types of the auxiliary vector bundles on
X
naturally attached to a branched cover. The open dense stratum where vector bundles are regular polystable affords, in all tractable cases, many complete, moving curves. If these curves continue to exist outside the currently tractable zone, we would obtain the bound of
5
for
s_{g}
. If they do not persist, then this would itself be an interesting phenomenon needing explanation.

1.3.
Notation and assumptions

We will work over an algebraically closed field
\mathscr{k}
of characteristic zero. All schemes and stacks will be defined over
\mathscr{k}
. A smooth projective genus
1
curve
X
will be fixed throughout. The projectivization
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}
of a locally free sheaf
\mathcal{V}
will mean
\Proj\Sym^{\bullet}\mathcal{V}
, the “post-Grothendieck” convention. We fix throughout an elliptic curve
(X,x)
. All stacks occurring in this paper are Deligne-Mumford stacks.

2.
The stack of nodal covers of
X

Definition 2.1
.

Let
d\geq 1,g\geq 2
be a pair of integers.

(1)

The stack
\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}(X)
parametrizes degree
d
finite flat morphisms

\alpha:C\to X

where

(a)

C
is a connected, at-worst-nodal projective curve of arithmetic genus
g
, and

(b)

\alpha
is
primitive
, i.e. it does not factor through a nontrivial étale cover of
X
.

(2)

We denote by
\varphi:\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}(X)\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
the forgetful morphism defined by

[\alpha:C\to X]\mapsto[C].

(3)

On
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
we let
\lambda\in\Pic(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g})
denote the first chern class of the Hodge bundle, and we let
\delta\in\Pic(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g})
denote the total boundary divisor – the sum of each irreducible boundary divisor with multiplicity one.

(4)

We abuse notation and use the symbols
\lambda,\delta
for the respective
\varphi
-pullbacks in
\Pic(\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}(X))
.

Remark 2.2
.

When
g=2
,
\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}(X)
is proper, and when
g>2
it is not. This is because when
3
or more branch points collide, the possible domain curves include worse-than-nodal possibilities. And this is not true for collisions of only two branch points.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
d,g\geq 2
.

(1)

\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}(X)
is irreducible and has dimension
2g-2
.

(2)

For fixed
g
, as
d
ranges over all positive integers, the union

\bigcup_{d>0}\varphi(\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}(X))\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}

is Zariski-dense.

(3)

The group scheme
\Aut(X)
acts on
\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}(X)
via post-composition, and the morphism
\varphi
is constant on
\Aut(X)
-orbits.

Proof.

The first statement follows from the well-known fact that a cover
\alpha:C\to X
is determined by the location of its branch points and discrete monodromy data. The second statement follows from observing that
\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}(X)
contains the collection of branched covers of
X
branched over a single point. The domain curves of this collection of covers, as
d\to\infty
is known to be Zariski dense in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
– see, for example,
[
Che11
]
for the case of
(X,x)
a square torus. (The argument works the same for any
j
-invariant.) The last statement is obvious.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

If
D\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
is an effective divisor, then there exist infinitely many positive integers
d
for which the inclusion

\varphi^{-1}(D)\subset\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}(X)

is strict.

Proof.

Immediate from
Proposition

2.3
part (2).
∎

Definition 2.5
.

A rank
r
and degree
k
vector bundle
V
on
X
is
regular polystable
if
V=V_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{h}
where
h=\gcd(r,k)
, and where for all
i
:

(1)

\rk V_{i}=r/h
for all
i
,

(2)

\deg V_{i}=d/h
for all
i
,

(3)

V_{i}
is indecomposable for all
i
, i.e. not a direct sum of smaller bundles, and

(4)

\det V_{i}\neq\det V_{j}
whenever
i\neq j
.

Recall from Atiyah’s classification that if
\gcd(r,k)=1
then a rank
r
and degree
k
indecomposable bundle is uniquely determined by its determinant. The condition of being regular polystable is an open condition in families of vector bundles.

Definition 2.6
.

(1)

Define
\Bog(\mathcal{E})\subset\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}(X)
(respectively
\Bog(\mathcal{F})
) to be the closed substack parametrizing
\alpha:C\to X
such that
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}
(resp.
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}
) is not regular polystable.

(2)

Define

\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}^{\Diamond}(X)\subset\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}(X)

to be the open substack parametrizing covers
\alpha:C\to X
such that both
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}
are regular polystable vector bundles on
X
. In other words,

\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}^{\Diamond}(X)=\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}(X)\setminus(\Bog(\mathcal{E})\cup\Bog(\mathcal{F})).

(3)

Define

\underline{\mathsf{Hur}}_{d,g}(X)\subset\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}(X)

to be the closed substack given by the requirement

\det\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}((g-1)x).

(4)

Define
\underline{\mathsf{Hur}}_{d,g}^{\diamond}(X)
to be the intersection
\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}^{\Diamond}(X)\cap\underline{\mathsf{Hur}}_{d,g}(X)
.

(5)

We will denote by

\alpha_{\univ}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}(X)\times X

the universal branched covering and we let
\mathcal{E}_{\univ},\mathcal{F}_{\univ}
denote the corresponding universal bundles on
\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}(X)\times X
.

(6)

We let
\epsilon_{1,1},\epsilon_{2}\in\Pic(\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}(X))
(respectively
\varphi_{1,1},\varphi_{2}
) denote the pushforwards of the classes

c_{1}^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\univ}),c_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\univ})

(resp. for
\mathcal{F}_{\univ}
) to
\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}(X)
.

Remark 2.7
.

(1)

The classes
c_{1}(\mathcal{E}_{\univ})
and
c_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{\univ})
are proportional, satisfying

(d-3)c_{1}(\mathcal{E}_{\univ})=c_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{\univ}),

and therefore so are
\epsilon_{1,1}
and
\varphi_{1,1}
.

(2)

It is possible to prove (using ideas similar to those in
[
DP15
, Section 2.2]
) that the three classes
\epsilon_{1,1},\epsilon_{2},\varphi_{2}
are linearly independent.

(3)

The vector bundles
\mathcal{N}_{i}
arising in the Casnati-Ekedahl-Schreyer resolution of
\alpha_{\univ}
produce divisor classes which are already in the span of
\epsilon_{1,1},\epsilon_{2},\varphi_{2}
, by the results in
[
DP+18
]
.

The divisor classes
\lambda
and
\delta
are contained in the
3
-dimensional vector space
\mathbb{Q}\langle\epsilon_{1,1},\epsilon_{2},\varphi_{2}\rangle
.

Proposition 2.8
.

The classes
\lambda
and
\delta
are in the vector space
\mathbb{Q}\langle\epsilon_{1,1},\epsilon_{2},\varphi_{2}\rangle
. Specifically, we have:

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=\ch_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\univ})

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle=13\ch_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\univ})+\ch_{2}(\mathcal{F}_{\univ})-\ch_{2}(\Sym^{2}\mathcal{E}_{\univ})

where we have suppressed the pushforward from
\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}(X)\times X
to
\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}(X)
.

Proof.

Similar divisor class calculations have been done elsewhere, for example in the proof of
[
DP+18
, Proposition 2.8]
. It is a standard use of the Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch theorem – we leave the details to the reader.
∎

Definition 2.9
.

Let
Y
be a stack. We say
there exist plenty of complete curves in
Y
if for all
n\in\N
there exists a complete curve
B_{i}
and non-constant morphism
\beta_{i}:B_{i}\to Y
such that the morphism
\coprod\beta_{n}:\coprod_{n}B_{n}\to Y
does not factor through a non-trivial closed substack.

We pose our main question in the form of a conjecture.

Conjecture 2.10

(Complete Curve Conjecture)
.

There are plenty of complete curves in
\underline{\mathsf{Hur}}_{d,g}^{\diamond}(X)
for any
d,g\geq 2
.

2.1.
Divisor class computations

Proposition 2.11
.

In
\Pic(\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}(X))\otimes\mathbb{Q}
we have:

(1)

\displaystyle[\Bog(\mathcal{E})]

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{2}-\frac{d-2}{2d-2}\epsilon_{1,1}

(2)

\displaystyle[\Bog(\mathcal{F})]

\displaystyle=\varphi_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{1,1}+\frac{d-3}{d}\epsilon_{1,1}

(3)

\displaystyle(5+6/d)\lambda-\delta

\displaystyle=[\Bog(\mathcal{F})]+\frac{(6-d)(d-1)}{d}[\Bog(\mathcal{E})]

Proof.

Recall that if
\mathcal{V}
is a vector bundle of rank
r
on a stack
Z
then the Bogomolov expression
\Bog(\mathcal{V})
is given by

\Bog(\mathcal{V})=\frac{1}{2r}c_{1}^{2}\mathcal{V}-\ch_{2}\mathcal{V}.

If
\mathcal{V}
is a vector bundle on a family of curves, the pushforward of the Bogomolov expression to the base of the family gives the divisor class of the locus where the vector bundle restricts to an unstable bundle, assuming the general fiber is stable (or regular poly-stabe in the case of genus
1
curves.)

And so the proposition follows from this and
Proposition

2.8
.
∎

Corollary 2.12
.

The relation

(5+6/d)\lambda-\delta=0

holds in the rational Picard group
\Pic(\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}^{\Diamond}(X))\otimes\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

Immediate from
Proposition

2.11
.
∎

Corollary 2.13
.

Suppose
B
is a complete curve and
\beta:B\to\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}^{\Diamond}(X)
is a morphism. Then

(5+6/d)\deg\beta^{*}\lambda-\deg\beta^{*}\delta=0.

Proof.

Immediate from
Proposition

2.11
.
∎

In light of
Corollary

2.13
, it makes sense to pose the slightly weaker conjecture:

Conjecture 2.14

(Elliptic Slope Conjecture)
.

There are plenty of complete curves in
\underline{\mathsf{Hur}}_{d,g}(X)
which have slope
5+6/d
.

Clearly,
Conjecture

2.10
implies
Conjecture

2.14
.

Theorem 2.15
.

Suppose
D\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
is an effective divisor with divisor class
a\lambda-b\delta
. If
Conjecture

2.14
is true for
d
sufficiently large, then

a/b\geq 5.

Proof.

Assume
Conjecture

2.14
, and let
d
be any of the infinitely many integers satisfying
Corollary

2.4
. Let
\beta:B\to\mathsf{Hur}_{d,g}^{\diamond}(X)
be a complete curve realizing the truth of
Conjecture

2.14
. Then, since
B
passes through a general point and since
\varphi^{-1}(D)\neq\underline{\mathsf{Hur}}_{d,g}(X)
, it follows that

\deg\beta^{*}(D)=a\deg\beta^{*}\lambda-b\deg\beta^{*}\delta\geq 0.

As
\beta^{*}\deg\lambda\geq 0
(in fact strictly so) for a non-constant proper family of curves with smooth general member, the theorem now follows from
Corollary

2.13
by letting
d\to\infty
.
∎

Theorem 3.1

3.1.
The genus 2 situation

If
g=2
, then
\mathsf{Hur}_{d,2}(X)
is a complete surface and
\underline{\mathsf{Hur}}_{d,2}(X)
is a complete curve.

Theorem 3.2
.

The slope of the curve
\underline{\mathsf{Hur}}_{d,2}(X)
is

\frac{\underline{\mathsf{Hur}}_{d,2}(X)\cdot\delta}{\underline{\mathsf{Hur}}_{d,2}(X)\cdot\lambda}=5+6/d.

Remark 3.3
.

This should be compared to a result of Chen in
[
Che11
]
in which he establishes that the
d\to\infty
limit of slopes of Teichmüller curves is
5
.

For simplicity, let us write
s
for the slope
\frac{\underline{\mathsf{Hur}}_{d,2}(X)\cdot\delta}{\underline{\mathsf{Hur}}_{d,2}(X)\cdot\lambda}
. For
i=0,1
let
\Delta_{i}(d)=\underline{\mathsf{Hur}}_{d,2}(X)\cdot\delta_{i}
, where
\delta_{0},\delta_{i}
are the two boundary divisors in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
. From the well-known relation

10\lambda=\delta_{0}+2\delta_{1},

it follows that

s=\frac{10(\Delta_{0}+\Delta_{1})}{\Delta_{0}+2\Delta_{1}}=5+\frac{5\Delta_{0}}{\Delta_{0}+2\Delta_{1}}.

Therefore, the claim in
Theorem

3.2
is equivalent to showing

(4)

\displaystyle(5d-6)\Delta_{0}(d)=12\Delta_{1}(d).

Definition 3.4
.

Fix a point
x\in X
, and an integer
i\leq\lfloor d/2\rfloor
.

(1)

Define the set
A_{d}(x)
to be the set of isomorphism classes of (primitive) covers
\alpha:Y\to X
where

is an irreducible curve with a single node

\alpha(y)=x
.

(2)

Define the set
B(i,x)
to be the set of isomorphism classes of (primitive) covers
\alpha:X_{1}\cup X_{2}\to X
such that
X_{1},X_{2}
are genus
1

\alpha(y)=x
, and such that

B_{d}(x)=\cup_{i}B(i,x)

(3)

Let

Lemma 3.5
.

We have

(5)

\displaystyle\Delta_{0}(d)

\displaystyle=4\cdot|A_{d}(x)|

(6)

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}(d)

\displaystyle=4\cdot|B_{d}(x)|

For integers
k,n
, let
\sigma_{k}(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}d^{k}.

Lemma 3.6
.

|C_{d}(x)|=\sigma_{1}(d).

Proof.

|C_{d}(x)|
can be identified with the number of lattices
\Lambda\subset\Z^{2}
which have co-volume
d
. We may canonically choose a basis of
\Lambda
for which the inclusion
\Lambda\subset\Z^{2}
is represented by a matrix

\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\
0&b\end{pmatrix}

where

,

, and

. For each factorization

there are

possibilities for

, and hence the number of such sublattices is
\sum_{a\mid d}a=\sigma_{1}(d)
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

The integers
|B_{d}(x)|
,
d=1,2,\dots
satisfy the recursive formula:

(7)

\displaystyle\sum_{d^{\prime}|d}|B_{d^{\prime}}(x)|\sigma_{1}(d/d^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}\left[\sigma_{1}(d/2)+\sum_{h=1}^{d-1}\sigma_{1}(h)\sigma_{1}(d-h).\right]

where we set
\sigma_{1}(d/2)=0
when
d
is odd.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
denote a marked point. The proof proceeds by counting the elements of a set
D
in two different ways. The set
D
is the collection of (isomorphism classes of) all degree
d
branched covers (not necessarily primitive)
\alpha:X_{1}\cup X_{2}\to X
where

(1)

X_{1},X_{2}
are smooth genus
1
curves meeting at a single node
y
,

(2)

\alpha(y)=x
.

On the one hand, if the degree of the isogeny
\alpha|_{X_{1}}:(X_{1},y)\to(X,x)
is
h
, then there are
\sigma_{1}(h)
choices for this isogeny by
Lemma

. Similarly, there are
\sigma_{1}(d-h)
choices for the isogeny
\alpha|_{X_{2}}:(X_{2},y)\to(X,x)
. If
h\neq d-h
then, accounting for labeling, it follows that there are
1/2(\sigma_{1}(h)\sigma_{1}(d-h))
choices for a combined branched cover
\alpha:X^{\prime}\to X
where
X^{\prime}
has two components, one of which maps with degree
h
.

If
h=d-h
then, after accounting for labeling, there are
{\sigma_{1}(h)\choose 2}
choices of branched covers
\alpha:X_{1}\cup X_{2}\to X
where the isogenies
\alpha|_{X_{1}}
and
\alpha|_{X_{2}}
are distinct. If we then add in the
\sigma_{1}(h)
-many branched covers where
\alpha|_{X_{1}}=\alpha|_{X_{2}}
we obtain a contribution of
\sigma_{1}(h)+{\sigma_{1}(h)\choose 2}=\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{1}(h)+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{1}(h)^{2}
. Adding all contributions per each

yields the right hand side of (
7
).

On the other hand, every element
\alpha:X_{1}\cup X_{2}\to X
in
D
factors canonically through an isogeny
(X_{1}\cup X_{2},y)\to(Z,z)\to(X,x)
,

a smooth genus

is primitive. By counting with respect to the degree

of the factor

we obtain the left side of (

). This concludes the proof.
∎

Definition 3.9

Lemma 3.10
.

(1)

If
d\geq 3
then

|A_{d}(x)|=\frac{1}{2}|M_{d}|.

(2)

If
d=2
then
|A_{2}(x)|=|M_{2}(x)|
.

(3)

If
d=1
then
|A_{1}(x)|=0=|M_{1}(x)|
.

Proof.

The case
d=1
is immediate, as both sets
A_{1}(x),M_{1}
are clearly empty. So we assume
d\geq 2
. We will produce a function
\Phi:M_{d}\to A_{d}(x)
which will explain the remaining statements. First, we identify
\pi_{1}(X,x)
with
\Z^{2}
. Next, pick any element
(\Lambda,\eta)\in M_{d}
.

By the fundamental Galois correspondence in the theory of covering spaces, the subgroup
\Lambda\subset\Z^{2}
defines a degree
d
connected, Galois covering space
\epsilon:Z\to X
with group of deck transformations canonically isomorphic to
\Z^{2}/\Lambda
.
Z
inherits from
X
a complex structure, and therefore
Z
is a genus 1 curve with
\epsilon
a degree
d
finite étale map. Pick a point
z\in\epsilon^{-1}(x)
. The chosen element
\eta\in\Z^{2}/\Lambda
defines a second point
z^{\prime}=\eta(z)
on
Z
. Let
Y_{(\Lambda,\eta)}
denote the nodal curve obtained by identifying the points
z
and
z^{\prime}
on the curve
Z
–
Y_{(\Lambda,\eta)}
is independent of the choice of
z
because the cover
Z\to X
is Galois. The map
\epsilon
descends to a finite, flat degree
d
map

\alpha:Y_{(\Lambda,\eta)}\to X

where the node of
Y_{(\Lambda,\eta)}
maps to
x
. Since
\eta
generates
\Z^{2}/\Lambda
, it follows that
\alpha:Y_{(\Lambda,\eta)}\to X
does not factor through a non-trivial étale cover
X^{\prime}\to X
, again by the Galois correspondence.

Thus,
\alpha:Y_{(\Lambda,\eta)}\to X
is an element of
A_{d}(x)
. The function
\Phi:M_{d}\to A_{d}(x)
is defined by the rule

(\Lambda,\eta)\mapsto[\alpha:Y_{(\Lambda,\eta)}\to X].

It suffices to show that
\Phi
is surjective and
2:1
when
d\geq 3
, and
1:1
when
d=2
. To that end, let
\alpha:Y\to X
be an arbitrary element of
A_{d}(x)
, let
y\in Y
denote the node of
Y
, and let
Z\to Y
denote the normalization of
Y
.
Z
is a genus
1
curve, and there are two points
z,z^{\prime}\in Z
lying above the node
y\in Y
. Again, since
\alpha:Y\to X
is primitive, it follows that the induced (Galois) cover
\epsilon:Z\to X
has a cyclic Galois group
G
. Hence, there is unique element
\eta_{1}\in G
which satisfies
\eta(z)=z^{\prime}
. Setting
\Lambda_{1}
to be the image of the pushforward map
\epsilon_{*}:\pi_{1}(Z,z)\to\pi_{1}(X,x)
, we see that
\alpha=\Phi(\Lambda_{1},\eta_{1})
.

Now suppose
\alpha=\Phi(\Lambda_{2},\eta_{2})
. Then
\Lambda_{2}=\Lambda_{1}
because both are simply the image of
\pi_{1}(Z)
under push-forward
\epsilon_{*}
(the normalization is intrinsic to
Y
). Now suppose

are two points in
\epsilon^{-1}(x)
such that
\eta_{2}(w)=w^{\prime}
. Since
\alpha=\Phi(\Lambda_{2},\eta_{2})
it follows by construction of
\Phi

\alpha:Y\to X
can be obtained by identifying the points

, creating the node

. By uniqueness of normalization, it follows that there is an automorphism

(
over

to the set

. Since the cover

\eta_{2}

and in the latter

∎

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\delta_{i,j}
be the Kronecker delta function. Then the function

F(d):=2|A_{d}(x)|+\delta_{d,1}-3\delta_{d,2}

is multiplicative, and if
p
is a prime number then

F(p^{n})=(p^{2}-1)p^{2n-2}.

Proof.

The proposition will fall out of carefully counting the elements of
M_{d}
and then using
Lemma

3.10
– the Kronecker deltas will correspond to the two edge cases in
Lemma

3.10
.

First, the set of lattices
\Lambda\subset\Z^{2}
of index
d
is in natural bijection with the set of integer matrices

\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\
0&b\end{pmatrix}

where
a,b>0
,
0\leq c<a
, and
ab=d
. In terms of this bijection, the condition that
\Z^{2}/\Lambda
is cyclic is expressed by the condition that
\gcd(a,b,c)=1
.

For each choice of factorization
ab=d
, the condition
\gcd(a,b,c)=1
can be replaced by the condition:
any prime
p
dividing both
a
and
b
does not divide
c
.
Thus, for a fixed
a
dividing
d
, the number of possible choices for
c
is

a\cdot\prod_{p\mid a\,\,\text{and}\,\,p\mid b}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right).

Summing over all factorizations we get

\displaystyle\sum_{ab=d}\left[\prod_{p\mid a\,\,\text{and}\,\,p\mid b}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\prod_{p^{n}\mid\mid d}\left[1+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}p^{i}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)+p^{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\prod_{p^{n}\mid\mid d}(p^{n}+p^{n-1}),

where “
p^{n}\mid\mid d
” means “
p^{n}
is the largest power of
p
dividing
d
.”

Now, accounting for the
\eta
part of the data of an element
(\Lambda,\eta)\in M_{d}
, we conclude that for
d\geq 2
,

|M_{d}|=\phi(d)\prod_{p^{n}\mid\mid d}(p^{n}+p^{n-1})=\prod_{p^{n}\mid\mid d}p^{2n-2}(p^{2}-1)

where
\phi
is the Euler phi function, and hence by
Lemma

3.10
we conclude that when
d\geq 3
,

(8)

\displaystyle 2|A_{d}(x)|=\prod_{p^{n}\mid\mid d}p^{2n-2}(p^{2}-1).

When
d=1
(
8
) is almost right: The left side is
0
while the right side is
1
because it is a product over an empty set. When
d=2
(
8
) is also almost right: The left side should be replaced by
|A_{2}(x)|
because of the
d=2
exception in
Lemma

3.10
. When we adjust
2|A_{d}(x)|
by the Kronecker deltas in the statement of the proposition, we find that the function

F(d):=2|A_{d}(x)|+\delta_{d,1}-3\delta_{d,2}

has output
\prod_{p^{n}\mid\mid d}p^{2n-2}(p^{2}-1)
, which is plainly multiplicative in
d
. The proposition follows.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

3.2
.

Recall that it suffices to prove (
4
), and hence by

3.5
it suffices to prove

(9)

\displaystyle(5d-6)|A_{d}(x)|=12|B_{d}(x)|.

In order to prove (

satisfy the recursion (

) in

after replacing
|B_{d^{\prime}}(x)|
with
\frac{5d^{\prime}-6}{12}|A_{d^{\prime}}(x)|
on the left side. Denote by
\iota:\Z_{>0}\to\Z
the inclusion map, which is clearly multiplicative.

On one hand
, in light of

3.11
, we get

(10)

\displaystyle\sum_{d^{\prime}\mid d}\frac{(5d^{\prime}-6)|A_{d^{\prime}}(x)|}{12}\sigma_{1}(d/d^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

(11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{24}\sum_{d^{\prime}\mid d}(5d^{\prime}-6)F(d^{\prime})\sigma_{1}(d/d^{\prime})-\frac{1}{24}(5\times 1-6)\sigma_{1}(d)+\frac{1}{24}\times 3\times(5\times 2-6)\sigma_{1}(d/2)

(12)

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{24}\left[(\iota\cdot F)*\sigma_{1}\right](d)-\frac{1}{4}\left[F*\sigma_{1}\right](d)+\frac{1}{24}\sigma_{1}(d)+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{1}(d/2).

Here and in what follows,

is the ordinary multiplication of two numerical functions and

denotes the Dirichlet convolution of two numerical functions, given by
(f*g)(d)=\sum_{d^{\prime}\mid d}f(d^{\prime})g(d/d^{\prime})
. If
f
and

are both multiplicative then so are

and

.

On the other hand
, we can simplify the right side of (

\sum_{h=1}^{d-1}\sigma_{1}(h)\sigma_{1}(d-h)=\left(\frac{1}{12}-\frac{d}{2}\right)\sigma_{1}(d)+\frac{5}{12}\sigma_{3}(d).

Thus it remains to prove the equality:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\sigma_{1}(d/2)+\left(\frac{1}{12}-\frac{d}{2}\right)\sigma_{1}(d)+\frac{5}{12}\sigma_{3}(d)\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{5}{24}\left[(\iota\cdot F)*\sigma_{1}\right](d)-\frac{1}{4}\left[F*\sigma_{1}\right](d)+\frac{1}{24}\sigma_{1}(d)+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{1}(d/2).

This further simplifies, and it then suffices to show the equality:

\displaystyle\frac{5}{12}\sigma_{3}(d)-\frac{d}{2}\sigma_{1}(d)=

(14)

\displaystyle\frac{5}{12}\left[(\iota\cdot F)*\sigma_{1}\right](d)-\frac{1}{2}\left[F*\sigma_{1}\right](d).

Equation

then follows from the following two simple identities, both proven using the fact that the Dirichlet convolution of multiplicative functions is multiplicative, and then by evaluating each expression at prime powers:

(1)

\left[(\iota\cdot F)*\sigma_{1}\right](d)=\sigma_{3}(d)
,

(2)

\left[F*\sigma_{1}\right](d)=d\cdot\sigma_{1}(d)

This finishes the verification that the numbers

satisfy the recursion (

, and therefore it follows that (

) is satisfied, finishing the proof of

.

3.2.
Verification of the conjecture in low degrees

Our next goal is to verify
Conjecture

2.10
for
d\leq 5
. The main tool we will use is the well-known parametrization of moduli spaces of covers of low degrees. We reference the fundamental papers of Casnati and Ekedahl here:
[
CE+96
]
,
[
C+96
]
.

Theorem 3.12
.

(1)

Choose a general polystable bundle
\mathcal{E}
of rank
2
and degree
g-1
on
X
. In the surface scroll
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{E}
, choose a general line
B
in the projective space
\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,\Sym^{3}\mathcal{E}\otimes(\det\mathcal{E})^{\vee}))
. Then
B
defines a complete, moving curve in
\underline{\mathsf{Hur}}_{3,g}^{\diamond}(X)
.

(2)

Choose a general polystable bundle
E
of rank
3
and degree
g-1
on
(X,x)
with determinant
(g-1)x
, and then choose a general polystable rank
2
vector bundle
\mathcal{F}
on
X
with
\det\mathcal{F}=\det E
. A general pencil
B
in the projective space
\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,\Sym^{2}\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{\vee}))
defines a complete, moving curve in
\underline{\mathsf{Hur}}_{4,g}^{\diamond}(X)
.

(3)

Choose a general polystable bundle
\mathcal{E}
of rank
4
and degree
g-1
on
X
with determinant
(g-1)x
, and then choose a general polystable bundle
\mathcal{F}
of rank
5
satisfying
\det\mathcal{F}=(\det\mathcal{E})^{\otimes 2}
. A general pencil
B
in
\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,\wedge^{2}\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{E}\otimes(\det\mathcal{E})^{\vee}))
defines a complete, moving curve in
\underline{\mathsf{Hur}}_{5,g}^{\diamond}(X)
.

Proof.

(1)

The pencil
B
can be interpreted as a linear map

\lambda:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,\Sym^{3}\mathcal{E}\otimes(\det\mathcal{E})^{\vee})).

We may interpret
\lambda
as a strict injection

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1)\hookrightarrow\Sym^{3}\mathcal{E}\otimes(\det\mathcal{E})^{\vee}.

But this, in turn, can be interpreted as a single section

t\in H^{0}\left(X\times\mathbb{P}^{1},\Sym^{3}(\mathcal{E^{\prime}})\otimes(\det\mathcal{E^{\prime}})^{\vee}\right)

where
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
is short for
\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)
. Conversely, a general section
t
can be interpreted as coming from a pencil
B
, and so we obtain a general section
t
in this way.

The large
g
assumption ensures that
\Sym^{3}(\mathcal{E^{\prime}})\otimes(\det\mathcal{E^{\prime}})^{\vee}
is globally generated, in fact even very ample. It follows from Casnati and Ekedahl’s Bertini-type theorems in
[
CE+96
,
C+96
]
that
t
defines a smooth surface
S\subset\mathbb{P}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
which is a triple cover of
X\times B
with Tschirnhausen bundle
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
. The numerics of the initial bundle
\mathcal{E}
ensures that the natural morphism
S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a family of arithmetic genus
g
curves, with smooth general fiber.

Furthermore, again as a consequence of the large
g
condition, the set of elements of
\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E}\otimes(\det\mathcal{E})^{\vee}))
which define a worse-than-nodal curve in
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{E}
has codimension at least
2
. Therefore, a general pencil avoids this locus and so we obtain in this way a map
B\to\mathsf{Hur}_{3,g}^{\diamond}(X)
which is clearly moving.

(2)

We proceed in much the same way as in the previous case. A general pencil
B\subset\mathbb{P}(\Sym^{2}\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{\vee})
can instead be interpreted as a single general global section
t
of
\left(\Sym^{2}\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\right)\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)
on
X\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Let
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
denote the bundles
\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2)
and
\mathcal{F}\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(3)
, respectively, on
X\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Then
t
is a general global section of
\Sym^{2}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\otimes(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})^{\vee}
, and
\det\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\det\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
.

The large
g
condition ensures
\Sym^{2}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\otimes(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})^{\vee}
is globally generated. By Casnati and Ekedahl’s Bertini-type theorems
[
CE+96
,
C+96
]
,
t
defines a smooth surface
S\subset\mathbb{P}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
, which is a degree
4
branched cover of
X\times B
with Tschirnhausen bundle
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
. The morphism

gives a family of arithmetic genus
g

The positivity of
\Sym^{2}\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{\vee}
implies that the locus of elements in its projectivization defining worse-than-nodal curves has codimension at least
2
. So

can be made to avoid this locus, and therefore

defines a moving curve in
\underline{\mathsf{Hur}}_{4,g}^{\diamond}(X)
.

(3)

Proceed in parallel to the previous two cases, with the relevant bundles on

being
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(5)
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\mathcal{F}\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(8)
. We omit the repetitive details.

∎

4.
Final remarks and possible next steps

In
Theorem

3.2
we computed that the slope of the Hurwitz curve
\underline{\mathsf{Hur}}_{d,2}(X)
is
5+6/d
, and the proof used lattice counting arguments. On the other hand, the unique (up to scaling) linear combination of the divisors
[\Bog(\mathcal{E})]
and
[\Bog(\mathcal{F})]
lying in the
\lambda
-
\delta
plane has slope
5+6/d
, according to
Proposition

2.11
.
Why are these two numbers the same?

We understand half of the answer to this riddle. When
g=2
, the Tschirnhausen bundle
\mathcal{E}
has degree
1
, and so poly-stability just means that
\mathcal{E}
is indecomposable. In
Theorem

4.4
below we will in fact show that
\Bog(\mathcal{E})
is empty when
g=2
. Given
Proposition

2.11
, if we knew that
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}
is regular polystable for a general
\alpha
then by
Theorem

3.2
we would conclude that
\Bog(\mathcal{F_{\alpha}})
must also be empty, and therefore that
Conjecture

2.10
holds for
g=2
. But we do not know this. In general, it would be great to know the answer to the following question:

Question 4.1
.

Does a general (primitive) branched cover
\alpha:C\to D
between two curves of genus
\geq 1
have a stable Casnati-Ekedahl bundle
\mathcal{F}
? What about the other terms of the Casnati-Ekedahl resolution?

Remark 4.2
.

The stability of the general Tschirnhausen bundle was demonstrated beautifully in
[
CLV24
]
. In another direction,
[
DP22
]
proves that the up to twist, all conceivable rank
d-1
vector bundles eventually arise as Tschirnhausen bundles, as
g
increases.

Throughout this section, we let
\alpha:C\to X
be an element of
\underline{\mathsf{Hur}}_{d,2}(X)
– in particular recall that
\alpha
is primitive.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
\mathcal{L}
is any degree
0
line bundle on
X
then

h^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}^{\vee})=1.

Proof.

By definition,

\alpha_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}^{\vee}.

Therefore,

h^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})+h^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}^{\vee})=h^{0}(X,(\alpha_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C})\otimes\mathcal{L})=h^{0}(C,\alpha^{*}\mathcal{L}),

using the push-pull formula. Now,
\alpha^{*}\mathcal{L}
is a degree
0
line bundle on
C
, and so
h^{0}(C,\alpha^{*}\mathcal{L})=1
if and only if
\alpha^{*}\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{C}
, and otherwise
h^{0}(C,\alpha^{*}\mathcal{L})=0
.

The pushforward of the divisor
c_{1}\alpha^{*}\mathcal{L}
is
d\cdot c_{1}\mathcal{L}
, and therefore if
\alpha^{*}\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{C}
then
\mathcal{L}
is
d
-torsion in
\Pic^{0}(X)
.

Suppose
e\mid d
is the smallest positive integer such that
\mathcal{L}
is
e
-torsion. Then
\mathcal{L}
defines a a degree
e
finite etále cover
\beta:\tilde{X}\to X
with the property that: if
\gamma:Z\to X
is any morphism such that
\gamma^{*}\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{Z}
then
\gamma
factors through
\beta
. In particular,
\alpha
factors through
\beta
, and therefore by primitivity
e=1
. To summarize,
\alpha^{*}\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{C}
if and only if
\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}
. The lemma follows.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

The Tschirnhausen bundle
\mathcal{E_{\alpha}}
is indecomposable for
any
element
[\alpha:C\to X]\in\underline{\mathsf{Hur}}_{d,2}(X)
.

Proof.

Write
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}^{\vee}
as a direct sum of indecomposable bundles

\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}^{\vee}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{A}_{i}.

Since
h^{0}(X,\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}^{\vee})=0
(
C
is connected), none of the summands
\mathcal{A}_{i}
can have positive degree.

Suppose one particular
\mathcal{A}_{j}
has degree
0
. By Atiyah’s classification,
\mathcal{A}_{j}
is the tensor product of the bundle
\mathcal{F}
by some line bundle
\mathcal{M}
of degree
0
. Here
\mathcal{F}
is the unique indecomposable vector bundle of rank
\rk\mathcal{A}_{j}
and degree
0
which has a non-trivial global section. If we set
\mathcal{L}:=\mathcal{M}^{-1}
, then
\mathcal{A}_{j}\otimes\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{F}
, and so
h^{0}(X,\mathcal{A}_{j}\otimes\mathcal{L})=1
. And so
Lemma

4.3
is violated.

Therefore, all summands
\mathcal{A}_{i}
have strictly negative degree, and these degrees sum to
\deg\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}^{\vee}=-1
. Hence there is only one summand, and the theorem follows.
∎

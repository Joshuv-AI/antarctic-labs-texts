Abstract
This paper describes open-source scientific contributions in python surrounding the numerical solutions to hyperbolic Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) partial differential equations viz., their implicit representation on co-dimension one surfaces; dynamics evolution with levelsets; spatial derivatives; total variation diminishing Runge-Kutta integration schemes; and their applications to the theory of reachable sets. They are increasingly finding applications in multiple research domains such as reinforcement learning, robotics, control engineering and automation. We describe the library components, illustrate usage with an example, and provide comparisons with existing implementations. This GPU-accelerated package allows for easy portability to many modern libraries for the numerical analyses of the HJ equations. We also provide a CPU implementation in python that is significantly faster than existing alternatives.

The

This paper describes a python-based GPU-accelerated scientific software package for numerically resolving generalized discontinuous solutions to Cauchy-type (or time-dependent) HJ hyperbolic partial differential equations (PDEs). HJ PDEs arise in many contexts including (multi-agent) reinforcement learning, robotics, control theory, differential games, flow, and transport phenomena. We focus on the numerical tools for safety assurance (ascertaining the freedom of a system from harm) in a verification sense in this paper. IV
Spatial Discretization: Upwinding

In this section, we discuss higher-order upwinding schemes that mimic high-order essentially non-oscillatory (ENO)
[
15
]
schemes for computing the spatial derivatives
\bm{v}_{x}
for the numerical viscosity solutions to levelset PDEs of the
Eulerian form
introduced in (
4
). Routines for procedures herewith described are in the folder
spatialderivative
. Using the Eulerian form of the levelset equation,

\displaystyle\bm{v}_{t}+\bm{F}\cdot\nabla\bm{v}=0

(4)

where
\bm{F}
is the speed function, the implicit function representation
v_{t}
(see §
III
) is used both to denote and evolve the interface. Suppose that the interface speed
\bm{F}
is a three-vector
[f_{x},f_{y},f_{z}]
on a three-dimensional Cartesian grid, expanding (
4
) the evolution of the implicit function on the zero levelset yields the Eulerian form

\displaystyle\bm{v}_{t}+f_{x}\bm{v}_{x}+f_{y}\bm{v}_{y}+f_{z}\bm{v}_{z}=0

(5)

of the interface evolution given that the interface encapsulates the implicit representation
\bm{v}
. In our implementation, we define
\bm{v}
throughout the computational domain
\Omega
.

Let us first construct the general form of a spatial upwinding scheme i.e.

\displaystyle D^{-}\bm{v}=\dfrac{\partial\bm{v}}{\partial\bm{x}}\approx\frac{\bm{v}_{i+1}-\bm{v}_{i}}{\Delta\bm{x}},D^{+}\bm{v}\approx\frac{\bm{v}_{i}-\bm{v}_{i-1}}{\Delta\bm{x}},

(6)

where
\bm{v}
and its speed
\bm{F}
are defined over a domain
\Omega
(this is the Cartesian grid in our representation). Using the forward Euler method, the levelset equation (
5
) becomes

(1/\Delta t)\cdot{\bm{v}^{n+1}-\bm{v}^{n}}+f_{x}^{n}\bm{v}_{x}^{n}+f_{y}^{n}\bm{v}_{y}^{n}+f_{z}^{n}\bm{v}_{z}^{n}=0.

Now, suppose that we are on a one-dimensional surface and around a grid point
i
. Given that
f^{n}
may be spatially varying, the foregoing equation becomes

\displaystyle\dfrac{\bm{v}^{n+1}_{i}-\bm{v}^{n}_{i}}{\Delta t}+f^{n}_{i}(\bm{v}_{x})^{n}_{i}=0

(7)

where
(\bm{v}_{x})_{i}
denotes the spatial derivative of
\bm{v}
w.r.t
x
at the point
i
.
We now discuss the specific implementations of the upwinding schemes.

IV-A

First-order accurate upwinding discretization

If
f_{i}>0
in (
7
), the values of
\bm{v}
are traversing from left to right so that in order to update
\bm{v}
at the end of the next time step, we must look to the left (going by the method of characteristics
[
4
, §3.1]
) and vice versa if
f_{i}<0
. We therefore follow the standard upwinding method by using (
6
): we approximate
\bm{v}_{x}
with
D^{-}\bm{v}
whenever
f_{i}>0
and we approximate
\bm{v}_{x}
with
D^{+}\bm{v}
whenever
f_{i}<0
. No approximation is needed when
f_{i}=0
since
f_{i}(\bm{v}_{x})_{i}
vanishes. This scheme is accurate within
O(\Delta\bm{x})
given the first order accurate approximations
D^{-}\bm{v}
and
D^{+}\bm{v}
. We have followed the naming convention in
[
5
]
and in our
spatialderivative
folder, we name this function
upwindFirstFirst
.

IV-B

ENO Polynomial Interpolation of Solutions

Using a divided differencing table, essentially non-oscillatory (ENO) polynomial interpolation of the discretization
[
15
]
of the levelset equation is known to generate improved numerical approximations to
D^{-}\bm{v}
and
D^{+}\bm{v}
. Suppose that we choose a uniform mesh discretization
\Delta\bm{x}
. Define the zeroth divided differences of
\bm{v}
at the grid nodes
i
as

D_{i}^{0}\bm{v}=\bm{v}_{i},

and the first, second, and third order divided differences of
\bm{v}
as the midway between grid nodes i.e.

\displaystyle D_{i+1/2}^{1}\bm{v}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{D_{i+1}^{0}\bm{v}-D_{i}^{0}\bm{v}}{\Delta\bm{x}},\,\,D_{i}^{2}\bm{v}=\dfrac{D_{i+1/2}^{1}\bm{v}-D_{i-1/2}^{1}\bm{v}}{2\Delta\bm{x}},

(8a)

\displaystyle D_{i+1/2}^{3}\bm{v}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{D_{i+1}^{2}\bm{v}-D_{i}^{2}\bm{v}}{3\Delta\bm{x}}.

(8b)

Then, an essentially non-oscillating polynomial of the form

\bm{v}(\bm{x})=Q_{0}(\bm{x})+Q_{1}(\bm{x})+Q_{2}(\bm{x})+Q_{3}(\bm{x})

(9)

can be constructed. In this light, the backward and forward spatial derivatives of

\bm{v}
w.r.t
\bm{x}
at grid node
i
is found in terms of the derivatives of the coefficients
Q_{i}(\bm{x})
in the foregoing i.e.

\displaystyle\bm{v}_{x}(\bm{x}_{i})=Q_{1}^{\prime}(\bm{x}_{i})+Q_{2}^{\prime}(\bm{x}_{i})+Q_{3}^{\prime}(\bm{x}_{i}).

(10)

Define
k=i-1
and
k=i
for
\bm{v}_{x}^{-}
and
\bm{v}_{x}^{+}
respectively. Then the first order (i.e. first-order upwinding) accurate polynomial interpolation is essentially

\displaystyle Q_{1}(\bm{x})=(D^{1}_{k+1/2}\bm{v})(\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{i}),\,\,Q_{1}^{\prime}(\bm{x}_{i})=D^{1}_{k+1/2}\bm{v}.

(11)

We follow Osher and Fedkiw’s recommendation
[
4
]
in avoiding interpolating near large oscillations in gradients. Therefore, we choose a constant
c
such that

\displaystyle c=\begin{cases}D^{2}_{k}\bm{v}\qquad\text{ if }|D^{2}_{k}\bm{v}|\leq|D^{2}_{k+1}\bm{v}|\\
D^{2}_{k+1}\bm{v}\qquad\text{ otherwise }\end{cases}

(12)

so that

\displaystyle Q_{2}(\bm{x})=c(\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{k})(\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{k+1}),\,Q_{2}^{\prime}(\bm{x}_{i})=c(2i-2k-1)\Delta\bm{x}

is the second-order accurate upwinding solution for the polynomial interpolation. This is implemented as
upwindFirstENO2
in the
spatialderivative
folder.

To obtain a third-order accurate solution, we choose
c^{\star}
as follows

\displaystyle c^{\star}=\begin{cases}D^{3}_{k^{\star}+1/2}&\quad\text{ if }|D^{3}_{k^{\star}+1/2}\bm{v}|\leq|D^{3}_{k^{\star}+3/2}\bm{v}|\\
D^{3}_{k^{\star}+3/2}\bm{v}&\quad\text{ if }|D^{3}_{k^{\star}+1/2}\bm{v}|>|D^{3}_{k^{\star}+3/2}\bm{v}|.\end{cases}

(13)

Whence, we have

\displaystyle Q_{3}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=c^{\star}(\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{k^{\star}})(\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{k^{\star}}+1)(\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{k^{\star}}+2)

(14a)

\displaystyle Q_{3}^{\prime}(\bm{x}_{i})

\displaystyle=c^{\star}(3(i-k^{\star})^{2}-6(i-k^{\star})+2)(\Delta\bm{x})^{2}

(14b)

for the third-order accurate correction to the approximated upwinding scheme (
9
). This is implemented as a routine in
upwindFirstENO3aHelper
and called as
upwindFirstENO3
in the
spatialderivative
folder.

IV-C

HJ Weighted Essentially Nonoscillatory Solutions

Here, we focus on weighted ENO (WENO) schemes with the same stencil as the third-order ENO scheme; however, its accuracy reaches up to fifth-order in the solution’s smooth parts. Our results closely follow the presentation of Jiang and Peng in
[
16
]
. These WENO schemes approximate spatial derivatives at integer grid points as opposed to at half-integer grid values as we did in the ENO scheme of the previous section.

The third-order accurate ENO scheme essentially employs one of three substencils on a grid, namely
\{i-3,i-2,\cdots,i\}
,
\{i-2,i-1,\cdots,i+1\}
, and
\{i-1,\cdots,i+3\}
on the stencils range
\{i-3,i-2,\cdots,i+3\}
.

\displaystyle\bm{v}_{\bm{x},i}^{-,0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}D^{+}\bm{v}_{i-3}-\frac{7}{6}D^{+}\bm{v}_{i-2}+\frac{11}{6}D^{+}\bm{v}_{i-1}

(15a)

\displaystyle\bm{v}_{\bm{x},i}^{-,1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{6}D^{+}\bm{v}_{i-2}+\frac{5}{6}D^{+}\bm{v}_{i-1}+\frac{1}{3}D^{+}\bm{v}_{i}

(15b)

\displaystyle\bm{v}_{\bm{x},i}^{-,2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3}D^{+}\bm{v}_{i-1}+\frac{5}{6}D^{+}\bm{v}_{i}-\frac{1}{6}D^{+}\bm{v}_{i+1}

(15c)

Suppose that the spatial derivative
\bm{v}_{x}
is to be found using the left-leaning substencil:
\{i-3,i-2,\cdots,i\}
, then the third-order ENO scheme chooses one from (
15
)
where
\bm{v}_{\bm{x},i}^{-,p}
denotes the third-order
p
’th substencil to
\bm{v}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{x}_{i})
for
p=0,1,2
. The WENO approximation to
\bm{v}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{x}_{i})
leverages a convex weighted average of the three substencils so that

\displaystyle\bm{v}_{\bm{x},i}^{-}=w_{0}\bm{v}_{\bm{x},i}^{-,0}+w_{1}\bm{v}_{\bm{x},i}^{-,1}+w_{2}\bm{v}_{\bm{x},i}^{-,2}.

(16)

In smooth regions of the phase space,
w_{0}=0.1
,
w_{1}=0.6
, and
w_{2}=0.3
yield the optimally accurate fifth order WENO approximation, we have for
\bm{v}_{\bm{x},i}^{-}

\displaystyle\dfrac{D^{+}}{30}\bm{v}_{i-3}-

\displaystyle\dfrac{13}{60}D^{+}\bm{v}_{i-2}+\dfrac{47}{60}D^{+}\bm{v}_{i-1}+\dfrac{9}{20}D^{+}\bm{v}_{i}-\dfrac{D^{+}}{20}\bm{v}_{i+1}

the fifth-order approximation
\bm{v}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{x}_{i})
and provides the smallest truncation error on a six-point stencil.

To account for weights in non-smooth regions, however, the smoothness of the stencils (
15
) can be estimated as recommended in
[
4
, §3.4]
so that if

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}=0.1/(\sigma_{1}+\epsilon)^{2},\,\alpha_{2}=0.6/(\sigma_{2}+\epsilon)^{2},\,\alpha_{3}=0.1/(\sigma_{3}+\epsilon)^{2}

(17)

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}

IV-D

Lax-Friedrichs Monotone Difference Schemes

We now describe a convergent monotone difference spatial approximation scheme for scalar conservation laws of the form

\displaystyle\bm{v}_{t}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}

\displaystyle f_{i}(\bm{v})_{x_{i}}=0\text{ for }t>0,\bm{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle\bm{v}(\bm{x},0)

\displaystyle=\bm{v}_{0}(\bm{x}),\text{ for }\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

(21)

Suppose that
N=1
, let us define
\lambda_{x}=\Delta t/\Delta\bm{x}
,
\Delta^{+}_{x}=\bm{v}_{j+1}-v_{j}
, and
\Delta^{-}_{x}=\bm{v}_{j}-\bm{v}_{j-1}
. Then at the
nth
time step, the Lax-Friedrichs scheme is
[
17
]

\displaystyle\bm{v}_{j}^{n+1}=\bm{v}_{j}^{n}-\dfrac{\lambda_{x}}{2}\Delta_{x}^{0}f(\bm{v}_{j}^{n})+\dfrac{1}{2}\Delta^{+}_{x}\Delta^{-}_{x}\bm{v}_{j}^{n}.

(22)

Furthermore, if we define the flux on the state space as

\displaystyle g(\bm{v}_{j},\bm{v}_{j-1})=\dfrac{f(\bm{v}_{j})+f(\bm{v}_{j-1})}{2}-\dfrac{1}{2}\lambda_{x}(\bm{v}_{j}-\bm{v}_{j-1}),

(23)

we may write
\bm{v}_{j}^{n+1}=\bm{v}_{j}^{n}-\lambda_{x}^{+}(\bm{v}_{j},\bm{v}_{j-1})
.

The Lax-Friedrichs scheme is monotone on the interval
[a,b]
if the CFL condition
\lambda_{x}\max_{a\leq\bm{v}\leq b}|f^{\prime}(\bm{v})|\leq 1

for
(a,b)>0

and the upwind differencing scheme for a nondecreasing
f
is
\bm{v}_{j}^{n+1}=\bm{v}_{j}^{n}-\lambda_{x}\Delta^{+}_{x}f(\bm{v}_{j-1}^{n})
.
For a non-increasing
f
, we have
\bm{v}_{j}^{n+1}=\bm{v}_{j}^{n}-\lambda_{x}\Delta_{x}^{+}f(\bm{v}_{j}^{n})
.
Our Lax-Friedrichs implementation is implemented in the
explicitintegration/term
folder.

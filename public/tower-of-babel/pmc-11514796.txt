Peripheral biomarkers of Parkinson’s disease and its correlation with clinical symptoms: a case-control study
Authors: Ying Wan, Yuwen Zhao, Mengyu Pan, Jing Gan, Na Wu, Yu Zhang, Zhenguo Liu, Lu Song
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundInflammation significantly impacts Parkinson’s disease (PD), yet the intricate relationship between inflammatory markers and PD remains elusive.ObjectiveTo identify the peripheral biomarkers of PD and its correlation with the motor and non-motor symptoms of PD.Methods79 PD patients and 65 controls were included in this study. Clinical information and the serum levels of IL-8, IL-27, IL-33, β-NGF, AgRP, and TRAILR2 in the participants were collected. Appropriate scales were used to assess the symptoms of PD. For the factors with significant differences in the two groups, multivariable logistic regression was used to determine its relationship with PD. Moreover, spearman correlation was conducted to explore the correlation between the factors and PD related symptoms. The IL-27 level was compared between the cognitively healthy PD group and the mild cognitive impairment in PD (PD-MCI). The serum level of TRAILR2 was positively correlated with age and was not associated with other clinical characteristics related to PD.ResultsCompared to controls, the serum levels of IL-27(P = 0.013) were increased whereas the levels of TRAILR2(P = 0.008) were decreased in PD patients. IL-8, IL-33, β-NGF, and AgRP showed no significant differences between the two groups. After controlling for the other variables, IL-27 was considered as an independent risk factor for PD in the multivariable logistic regression model. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve for diagnosing PD with IL-27 yielded an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.621. Additionally, IL-27 level in PD patients was positively correlated with age, the disease duration, LEDD and negatively correlated with the MoCA scores. However, no significant difference was found in IL-27 levels between cognitively healthy PD and PD-MCI groups.ConclusionElevated serum IL-27 was a risk factor for PD and positively correlated with the cognitive decline in PD.

Introduction

PD is a common neurodegenerative disease in the elderly, which is characterized by cardinal motor symptoms and a variety of non-motor symptoms. The progressive loss of dopaminergic neurons and the protein inclusions of Lewy bodies in the substantia nigra compacta are featured pathogenic changes of PD. Increasing evidence implicated that inflammation was involved in the disease progression. In particular, peripheral inflammation, which was identified in the blood, enteric nervous system and the gastrointestinal tract, was reported to be associated with the severity of the motor and non-motor symptoms [1, 2]. Accordingly, identification of the peripheral inflammatory biomarkers has increasingly become a focus of study, for the purpose of clarifying the etiology, seeking diagnostic markers or intervention targets for PD. Especially, the detection of serum inflammatory biomarkers attracted a lot of attention for its easy accessibility and little invasiveness. However, the results of the serum inflammatory biomarkers were contradictory among different studies [3–5]. Notably, diabetes, hypertension, and heart disease are common chronic comorbidities in the elderly with or without PD. The effects of these comorbidities on the serum inflammatory cytokines were not considered in the previous studies, which might contribute to the inconsistency of these results from different studies. Hence, we conducted this case-control study to confirm the change of some serum inflammatory markers in PD patients with or without these comorbidities. Furtherly, we would determine whether these markers would be suitable as potential biomarkers for PD. The findings would also help to draw a more comprehensive profile of the peripheral inflammation in PD.

Materials and methods

Study subjects

79 PD patients were enrolled in our study. All of them were diagnosed with clinical PD, according to the MDS Clinical Diagnostic Criteria for Parkinson’s disease [6]. 65 local community residents were enrolled in the control group. There were no differences in the age or gender ratio between the two groups. The exclusion criteria for all the participants were history of infectious or autoimmune diseases, tumors, taking corticosteroids, anti-inflammatory or antibiotic medications. Moreover, Community residents were excluded from the study for having histories of neurological diseases, Parkinsonism symptoms or signs. This study has been approved by the Ethical Committee of Xinhua Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine and obtained the informed consent of the participants.

Clinical and neuropsychological examinations

Clinical information, including age, sex, age at onset, disease duration, personal history, levodopa equivalent daily dose(LEDD), and history of chronic diseases were collected. For PD patients, the motor and non-motor symptoms were assessed with the appropriate scales. Motor impairments were assessed by Hoehn and Yahr (H&Y) stage scale and the Unified Parkinson’s Disease Rating Scale-motor score (UPDRS-III) [7]. Cognitive function was evaluated using the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) [8]. Autonomic dysfunction was examined by the Scales for Outcomes in Parkinson’s Disease-Autonomic (SCOPA-AUT) [9]. Anxiety and depression were assessed with Hamilton Anxiety Scale-14 (HAMA-14) [10] and Hamilton Depression Scale-24 (HAMD-24) [11]. The assessment of sleep was conducted using the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) [12] and the Epworth Sleeping Scale (ESS) [13].

Blood sample and measurement of the serum inflammation-related factors

Following an overnight fasting, clinical nursing staff obtained 5 mL of blood from the cubital vein of all PD patients and the controls in the morning. The supernatants were aliquoted into fresh polypropylene vials and stored frozen at − 80 °C before subsequent cytokine quantification was performed. Blood serum was obtained by the centrifugation of blood samples at 3,000 g for 5 min and then, sera were diluted twice and stored at -80 °C until needed. Begin by adding 50 µl of beads to the plate, and then proceed to add 50 µl of quality standards and samples in duplicate. After placing the contents on the plate, it was sealed and then wrapped with foil. The plate was incubated with agitation on a plate shaker for 120 min. The plate was washed three times and then, 50 µl of biotin-labeled antibody was added per well. After incubating the plate at room temperature for 60 min, 50 µl streptavidin–PE was added to each well. The plate was sealed, covered and incubated for another 30 min at room temperature. The Beads were resuspended and incubated for 2 min. Concentrations of these immunity factors were analyzed by Luminex 200 multiplex immunoassay instrument (Luminex, USA). All steps were performed according to the manufacturer’s protocol. IL-8, IL-27, IL-33, TRAILR2, β-NGF, and AgRP were tested in our study. For these factors, the results of IL-8 were inconsistent in previous studies, and the others were seldom reported.

Data analyses

All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS Statistics 25.0 software. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to assess data distribution normality. The normally distributed continuous variables were compared using a two-tailed t-test. The abnormally distributed continuous variables were compared using a non-parametric test(Mann–Whitney U test). Discrete variables were compared using the Chi-square test. Quantitative data are presented as means with standard deviations (mean ± SD), and qualitative data are expressed in percentages. The Spearman correlation coefficient (r) was used to analyze correlations between levels of inflammation-related factors expression and clinical assessment. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Multivariable logistic regression analysis was utilized for factors exhibiting notable differences between groups, aimed at further investigating the role of inflammatory factors as potential risk factors for PD. This analysis quantified the impact of these factors, with the effect size expressed in terms of the odds ratio (OR). For significant variables (P < 0.05), after standardization, ROC analyses were conducted to determine the AUC, which indicates the discriminatory ability of the variables to distinguish between the two groups.

Results

Characteristics of the study population

79 PD patients were included in our study, the mean onset age was 63.30 ± 6.93 years, the mean disease duration was 5.75 ± 4.63 years, the mean UPDRS-III score was 27.74 ± 11.67 and the mean LEDD was 504.17 ± 385.35 mg. Compared with the control group, the PD group had a lower percentage of hypertension and heart disease (P < 0.05). No differences were observed in age, gender ratio, and diabetes between the two groups. In terms of the inflammation-related markers, the PD group had a higher level of serum IL-27(P = 0.013) but a lower level of serum TRAILR2(P = 0.008). The other inflammatory markers were not different between the two groups. Identification of potential biomarkers and their association with PD related symptoms

We conducted a multivariable logistic regression analysis to identify the association between PD and the two inflammatory factors of IL -27 and TRAILR2. After controlling for all the other clinical features, the standardized IL-27 level (OR = 457.76,95%CI = 20.40-10271.59, P = 0.000) was identified as an independent risk factor for PD. However, the standardized TRAILR2 level failed to be the risk factor for PD. A ROC revealed that IL-27 serum concentrations of 230.420 pg/ml represented the best cut-off value for PD with a diagnostic sensitivity of 79.7% and a specificity of 52.3%, an AUC of 0.621. Furtherly, we performed a correlation analysis on the association between the two markers and PD related symptoms. We found that the serum level of IL-27 were positively correlated with the disease duration (r = 0.250, P = 0.034) and negatively correlated with the MoCA score (r =-0.348, P = 0.013). However, the serum IL-27 level was not found to be correlated with the UPDRS-III scores or other non-motor assessment indexes. The serum TRAILR2 level was only positively correlated with age. The correlation analysis was shown in details in Table 2.In addition, we divided PD subjects into cognitively healthy PD and PD-MCI patients [14] the comparison indicated that IL-27 level was not significantly different between the cognitively healthy PD group and PD-MCI group ( 288.38 ± 90.58 vs. 302.17 ± 136.83 pg/ml, P = 0.790).

Discussion

We identified the elevated serum IL-27 as an independent risk factor of PD and its level was positively associated with age, the disease duration, LEDD and cognitive decline of PD. However, as a diagnostic biomarker for PD, the serum IL-27 was not excellent for its low diagnostic efficacy. The other five factors were not found to correlate with PD.

IL-27 is a heterodimeric cytokine that is composed of two subunits, the IL-27p28 and EBI3 chains, and is mainly secreted by the antigen presenting cells and specifically monocytes [15]. In addition, IL-27p28 and EBI3 were detected in multiple glia and neuron cell subtypes in the mouse retina and brain [16]. IL-27 receptor is a heterodimer that consists of IL-27Ra subunit and gp 130 subunit [17]. IL-27 Ra is specific for the binding of IL-27 and primarily expressed on the inflammatory cell surface, whereas the gp130 is shared with a lot of cytokines and widely expressed on most inflammatory cells, neurons, and endothelial cells. Therefore, IL-27 is a pleiotropic cytokine that is involved in various cellular processes of different tissues. The role of IL-27 in inflammation is complicated, as the findings in different autoimmune animal or cellular models indicated that IL-27 had a two-sided effect on inflammation regulation, in which it can not only play anti-inflammatory function but also pro-inflammatory function, depending on the inflammatory status [18, 19]. A genome-wide association study reported that genetic variants near the gene IL27 (rs2904880) were associated with PD, suggesting a potential link between IL27 and PD [20]. A previous clinical study reported that PD patients had a lower serum IL-27 than controls, which was contrary to our study [21]. We assumed that it might be attributed to the following factors, firstly, in that study, the gender ratio was not matched between PD patients and the controls, whereas ours was gender ratio matched. Secondly, the effect of the common comorbidities in the elderly on the serum IL-27 level was not analyzed in that study. We found that, in our study, with hypertension or heart disease, PD patients had the same serum IL-27 level as the controls, which indicated that the serum IL-27 level was affected by these accompanying chronic diseases. Actually, there has been some evidence of a higher serum IL-27 level in patients with hypertension or coronary artery diseases [22, 23]. Since hypertension and heart disease frequently occur in agedly, its effect on the serum level of IL-27 should not be ignored. We found that the serum IL-27 level was in a positive relationship with the cognitive decline in PD patients, which was originally reported. A recent study found an increased IL-27 level in the aged mouse brain and IL-27 sustained NK cells in the dentate gyrus, leading to the decline of neurogenesis and cognition during normal aging [24]. Another study reported that some phosphodiesterase 5-inhibitors inhibited the neuronal loss and alleviated the cognitive impairment of the streptozotocin-induced sporadic Alzheimer’s disease mice models, with a decreased level of the brain IL-27 level [25]. Notably, our study showed the IL-27 level in cognitively healthy PD group was not statistically different from in the PD-MCI group. It indicated that IL-27 is incapable to be a marker of cognitive impairment in PD.

TNF-related apoptosis-inducing ligand is famous for its antitumor function by triggering apoptosis in cancer cells while sparing the normal cells. TRAILR2 is the second receptor of TRAIL. By binding to its receptor, TRAIL would function as an immune mediator as it played a pro-inflammatory effect via stimulating the nuclear factor (NF)-kB pathway or played an inhibitory effect on autoimmune via inhibition of T-cell activation in autoimmune diseases [26, 27]. The change of serum TRAILR2 in PD patients was little studied. We found PD patients had a significantly lower TRAILR2, and the level of TRAILR2 was positively correlated with age. However, a previous study detected a higher mRNA expression of TRAILR2 in the blood of 22 PD patients, compared to 15 controls [28]. We assumed that the inconsistent results might be explained by the difference in the sample size and percentages of hypertension and heart disease between the two studies. Especially, the TRAILR2 levels were reported to increase in patients with diabetes and cardiovascular disease [29, 30]. In our study, TRAILR2 failed to be a risk of PD or correlate with the clinical characteristics of PD. We assumed that reduced TRAILR2 level might be an accompanying phenomenon in PD patients.

We found no difference in the levels of IL-8, IL-33, β-NGF, and AgRP between PD patients and controls. Among these factors, IL-8, a pro-inflammatory cytokine, was recently reported to be a diagnostic biomarker for PD [31], however, the results from the other previous studies were inconsistent [32, 33], which indicated the change of serum IL-8 was unstable in different PD populations. IL-33 is a multifunctional cytokine involved in the occurrence and progression of a variety of diseases. A recent study including 32 PD patients and 30 controls reported PD patients had a decreased serum IL-33, which was not consistent to our findings. We speculated the different sample sizes might contribute to the differences between the two studies [34]. β-NGF is an inflammatory factor that is essential for the development and protection of the nervous system. A study using a multiplex proteomics approach detected a decreased Cerebro-Spinal Fluid(CSF) β-NGF in PD patients [35] compared to controls. However, in our study, the serum β-NGF level in PD patients was not different in controls. Our findings indicated that the serum β-NGF might not be a potential biomarker for PD diagnosis. AgRP, as an crucial factor in sensing the peripheral metabolic signals and regulating eating behaviors, was reported to be involved in hunger mediated anti-inflammation [36, 37]. Our study was the first to investigate the change of AgRP in PD patients. We found that the serum AgRP were not different between PD patients and controls. The limitation of our study was the lack of validation of our findings. Another PD-control population was required to test the diagnostic accuracy of IL-27, and a follow-up of the IL-27 level and cognitive decline in this population was required to confirm the correlation between IL-27 and cognitive decline in PD. The work will be done in our following study.

Conclusion

Elevated serum IL-27 was an independent risk factor of PD, and was positively associated with age, the disease duration, LEDD and cognitive decline of PD. Our findings indicated that peripheral inflammation was closely related to the non-motor symptoms of PD.

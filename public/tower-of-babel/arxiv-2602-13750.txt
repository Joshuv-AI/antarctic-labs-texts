The bipartite analogue of a classical spanning tree enumeration formula, Boolean functions, and their applications to counting odd spanning trees
Jun Ge, Yamin Yu

Abstract
Recently, Zheng and Wu defined the concept of odd spanning tree of a graph, meaning a spanning tree in which every vertex has odd degree. Similar to Cayley’s formula, Feng, Chen and Wu counted the number of odd spanning trees in complete graphs via Prüfer code and the exponential generating function. In this note, we give a simple proof via a classical spanning tree enumeration formula and the Boolean function. We also generalize it to complete bipartite graphs. Keywords: odd spanning tree; Boolean function; complete graph; complete bipartite graph



1
Introduction

Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a graph without self-loops and
\mathcal{T}(G)
be the collection of spanning trees of
G
.
We usually call
\tau(G)=|\mathcal{T}(G)|
the number of spanning trees of
G
. Denote by
K_{n}
,
K_{m,n}
, and
K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{s}}

be the complete graph of
n
vertices, the complete bipartite graph that one partite has
m
vertices and the other partite has
n
vertices,
and the complete
s
-partite graph that each partite has
n_{i}
vertices.

Counting spanning trees is a classic problem originating in the 19th century that continues to be a hot topic today.
Some recent developments can be found in
[
3
,
4
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
12
]
for example.

In 2025, motivated by Gallai’s classical theorem (
[
10
]
, Section 5, Problem 17) that the vertices of
any graph can be partitioned into two sets
where one induces an even-degree subgraph and the other induces an odd-degree subgraph, and also the concept of
homeomorphically irreducible spanning trees (HISTs, spanning trees containing no vertices of degree two),
Zheng and Wu
[
13
]
introduced the concept of odd spanning trees. An odd spanning tree of a graph
G

is a spanning tree
T
of
G
where each vertex has odd degree in
T
.

The famous Cayley’s formula
[
2
]
states that
\tau(K_{n})=n^{n-2}
.
Let
\tau_{o}(G)
be the number of odd spanning trees of
G
.
Recently, Feng, Chen and Wu counted the number of odd spanning trees in complete graphs via Prüfer code and the exponential generating function as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
5
]
)
.

Let
n
be a positive number. Then

\tau_{o}(K_{n})=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(2k-n)^{n-2}.

Note: in
[
5
]
, the original theorem is of the form

\tau_{o}(K_{n})=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }n\text{ is odd;}\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(2k-n)^{n-2},&\text{if }n\text{ is even.}\end{cases}

We slightly revised the theorem since when
n
is odd, it is easy to check that
\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(2k-n)^{n-2}=0
.

In this note, we we give a simple proof via a classical spanning tree enumeration formula and the Boolean function.
We also generalize it to complete bipartite graphs.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we give a simple lemma on a specific Boolean function, which will be used in counting odd spanning trees in section
4
.

Let
y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n}
be Boolean variables(also called sign variables) with
y_{i}\in\{-1,1\}
for each
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, and all variables are independent of one another. Denote the set of all variable combinatuons by
\{\pm 1\}^{n}
which contains
2^{n}
elements in total. Let
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}
be real constants and
m\in{\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}
. Define the function
f:\{\pm 1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
as the
m
-th power of the linear combination of variables:

f(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n})=(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}y_{i})^{m}
, and let
y=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})
, then the expectation of
f
is

\mathbb{E}[f]=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}y_{i}\right)^{m}.

In this paper, the expectation operator
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
acts on the
m
-th degree homogeneous polynomial
f
with respect to the random variable
y
. The random variable
y
satisfies the moment property of origin-symmetric distribution, i.e., for any positive integer
k
, its odd-order moments satisfy
\mathbb{E}[y^{k}]=0
and its even-order moments are positive constants. The simplification of expectations relies on the linearity of expectation and the product expectation property of independent random variables.

Lemma 1
.

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}y_{i}\right)^{m}=2^{n}\cdot\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{n}=m\\
k_{i}\in 2\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\frac{m!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{n}!}a_{1}^{k_{1}}a_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots a_{n}^{k_{n}}.

Proof.

For simplicity, let
\mathcal{Y}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}y_{i}\right)^{m}
. Then
\mathcal{Y}=2^{n}\mathbb{E}[(\sum_{i=i}^{n}a_{i}y_{i})^{m}]
. From the expansion of multivariate polynomial, it can be derived that

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}y_{i}\right)^{m}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{n}=m\\
k_{i}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{m!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{n}!}(a_{1}y_{1})^{k_{1}}(a_{2}y_{2})^{k_{2}}\cdots(a_{n}y_{n})^{k_{n}},

Based on the fundamental properties of expectation, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}

\displaystyle=2^{n}\cdot\mathbb{E}[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{n}=m\\
k_{i}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{m!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{n}!}(a_{1}y_{1})^{k_{1}}(a_{2}y_{2})^{k_{2}}\cdots(a_{n}y_{n})^{k_{n}}]

\displaystyle=2^{n}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{n}=m\\
k_{i}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{m!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{n}!}\mathbb{E}[(a_{1}y_{1})^{k_{1}}]\mathbb{E}[(a_{2}y_{2})^{k_{2}}]\cdots\mathbb{E}[(a_{n}y_{n})^{k_{n}}]

\displaystyle=2^{n}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{n}=m\\
k_{i}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{m!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{n}!}a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n}\mathbb{E}[y_{1}^{k_{1}}]\mathbb{E}[y_{2}^{k_{2}}]\cdots\mathbb{E}[y_{n}^{k_{n}}],

Since
y_{i}
is a Boolean variable,
\mathbb{E}[y_{i}^{k_{i}}]=1
if
k_{i}
is even and
\mathbb{E}[y_{i}^{k_{i}}]=0
if
k_{i}
is odd. Thus,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}y_{i}\right)^{m}=2^{n}\cdot\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{n}=m\\
k_{i}\in 2\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\frac{m!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{n}!}a_{1}^{k_{1}}a_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots a_{n}^{k_{n}}.

∎

3
Bipartite analogue of a classical spanning tree
enumeration formula

The following theorem is well-known and can be proved by induction on the number of vertices.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
1
,
10
]
)
.

Let
d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{n}
be positive integers summing up to
2n-2
. Then the number of spanning trees of
K_{n}
in which the vertex
i
has degree exactly
d_{i}
for all
i=1,2,\dots,n
equals

\dfrac{(n-2)!}{(d_{1}-1)!(d_{2}-1)!\cdots(d_{n}-1)!}.

We now prove the bipartite analogue of this lemma. This result will be applied in Section 4 to count odd spanning trees in complete bipartite graphs, and is also of independent interest in its own right.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
K_{m,n}
be the complete bipartite graph with bipartition
A=\{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{m}\}
and
B=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\}
. Let
a_{i}
and
b_{j}
be positive integers which
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}=m+n-1
. Then the number of spanning trees of
K_{m,n}
in which the vertex
u_{i}
and
v_{j}
have degree exactly
a_{i}
and
b_{j}
for
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
and
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
equals

\frac{(m-1)!(n-1)!}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i}-1)!\prod_{j=1}^{n}(b_{j}-1)!},

Proof.

We use induction on
m+n
. For
m=1
or
n=1
,
K_{m,n}
is a star and the statement is trivial.
Since
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}=m+n-1
,
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}=2(m+n)-2<2(m+n)
,
there must exist a leaf, and we may assume that
a_{m}=1
where
m>1
.
Remove
u_{m}
, in any tree under consideration,
u_{m}
is adjacent to some
v_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq n
and the removal of
u_{m}
results in another tree on
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{m-1},v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
with degrees
a_{1},\dots,a_{m-1},b_{1},\cdots,b_{j}-1,\dots,b_{n}
.
By the induction hypothesis, the number of trees in
K_{m-1,n}
that
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{m-1},v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
has degrees
a_{1},\dots,a_{m-1},b_{1},\cdots,b_{j}-1,\dots,b_{n}
is

\frac{(m-2)!(n-1)!}{(a_{1}-1)!\cdots(a_{m-1}-1)!(b_{1}-1)!\cdots(b_{j}-2)!\cdots(b_{n}-1)!}=\frac{(m-2)!(n-1)!(b_{j}-1)}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i}-1)!\prod_{j=1}^{n}(b_{j}-1)!}.

Thus, the number of trees on
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{m},v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
with degrees
a_{1},\dots,a_{m},b_{1},\dots,b_{n}
is

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(m-2)!(n-1)!(b_{j}-1)}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i}-1)!\prod_{j=1}^{n}(b_{j}-1)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(m-2)!(n-1)!}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i}-1)!\prod_{j=1}^{n}(b_{j}-1)!}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(b_{j}-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(m-1)!(n-1)!}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i}-1)!\prod_{j=1}^{n}(b_{j}-1)!}.

∎

Now we give a new proof on the number of complete bipartite graphs.

Theorem 3.3
.

[
6
]

\tau(K_{m,n})=m^{n-1}n^{m-1}.

Proof.

The notation and symbols used below are consistent with those in Theorem
3.2
.
Let
a_{i}^{\prime}=a_{i}-1
and
b_{j}^{\prime}=b_{j}-1
for
i\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
and
j\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.
Then
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}^{\prime}=n-1
,
\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}^{\prime}=m-1
. The number of spanning trees of
K_{m,n}
is

\displaystyle\ \tau(K_{m,n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}^{\prime}+\cdots+a_{m}^{\prime}=n-1\\
b_{1}^{\prime}+\cdots+b_{n}^{\prime}=m-1\end{subarray}}\frac{(m-1)!(n-1)!}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i}-1)!\prod_{j=1}^{n}(b_{j}-1)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}^{\prime}+\cdots+a_{m}^{\prime}=n-1\end{subarray}}\frac{(n-1)!}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i}-1)!}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{1}^{\prime}+\cdots+b_{n}^{\prime}=m-1\end{subarray}}\frac{(m-1)!}{\prod_{j=1}^{m}(b_{i}-1)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\underbrace{1+1+\cdots+1}_{m})^{n-1}(\underbrace{1+1+\cdots+1}_{n})^{m-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{n-1}n^{m-1}.

∎

4
Applications to counting odd spanning trees

We first give a simple proof of Theorem
1.1
via Theorem
3.1
and the Boolean function.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Theorem
3.1
, the number of odd spanning trees of
K_{n}
in which
the vertex
i
has degree exactly
d_{i}=2d_{i}^{\prime}+1
for all
i=1,2,\dots,n
equals

\dfrac{(n-2)!}{(2d_{1}^{\prime})!(2d_{2}^{\prime})!\cdots(2d_{n}^{\prime})!},

where
d_{i}\geq 0
, and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(2d_{i}^{\prime})=n-2
.
Therefore,

\tau_{o}(K_{n})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{n}=n-2\\
k_{i}\in 2\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\frac{(n-2)!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{n}!}.

It follows from Lemma
1
that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}\end{subarray}}(\varepsilon_{1}x_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}x_{2}+\cdots\varepsilon_{n}x_{n})^{n-2}=2^{n}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{n}=n-2\\
k_{i}\in 2\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\frac{(n-2)!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{n}!}x_{1}^{k_{1}}x_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots x_{n}^{k_{n}}.

Let
x_{1}=x_{2}=\cdots=x_{n}=1
, we obtain that

\tau_{o}(K_{n})=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}\end{subarray}}(\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}+\cdots\varepsilon_{n})^{n-2}\\
=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(2k-n)^{n-2}.

\Box

Now we consider the number of odd spanning trees in complete bipartite graphs.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
m
and
n
be positive numbers. Then

\tau_{o}(K_{m,n})=\frac{1}{2^{m+n}}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m}{i}(2i-m)^{n-1}\right]\left[\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(2j-n)^{m-1}\right].

Proof.

The notation and symbols used below are consistent with those in Theorem
3.2
.
By Theorem
3.2
, the number of odd spanning trees of
K_{m,n}

in which the vertex
u_{i}\in A
and
v_{j}\in B
have degree exactly
d(u_{i})=2d^{\prime}(u_{i})+1
and
d(v_{j})=2d^{\prime}(v_{j})+1

for all
i\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
and
j\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
equals

\frac{(m-1)!(n-1)!}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}2d^{\prime}(u_{i})!\prod_{j=1}^{n}2d^{\prime}(v_{j})!},

where all
d^{\prime}\geq 0
,
\sum_{i=1}^{m}2d^{\prime}(u_{i})=n-1
and
\sum_{j=1}^{n}2d^{\prime}(v_{j})=m-1
.
Therefore,

\tau_{o}(K_{m,n})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{m}=n-1\\
k_{i}\in 2\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1}+l_{2}+\cdots+l_{n}=m-1\\
l_{i}\in 2\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\frac{(m-1)!(n-1)!}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}!\prod_{j=1}^{n}l_{j}!}.

It follows from Lemma
1
that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}\end{subarray}}(\alpha_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{m}x_{m})^{n-1}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}\end{subarray}}(\beta_{1}y_{1}+\cdot+\beta_{n}y_{n})^{m-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m}=n-1\\
k_{i}\in 2\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\frac{(n-1)!}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{m}!}x_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots x_{m}^{k_{m}}\cdot 2^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1}+\cdots+l_{n}=m-1\\
l_{j}\in 2\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\frac{(m-1)!}{l_{1}!\cdots l_{m}!}y_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots y_{m}^{l_{m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{m+n}\left[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m}=n-1\\
k_{i}\in 2\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\frac{(n-1)!}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{m}!}x_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots x_{m}^{k_{m}}\right]\left[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1}+\cdots+l_{n}=m-1\\
l_{j}\in 2\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\frac{(m-1)!}{l_{1}!\cdots l_{m}!}y_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots y_{m}^{l_{m}}\right]

Set
x_{1}=\cdots=x_{m}=y_{1}=\cdots=y_{n}=1
, we can obtain that

\displaystyle\tau_{o}(K_{m,n})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2^{m+n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}\end{subarray}}(\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{m})^{n-1}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}\end{subarray}}(\beta_{1}+\cdot+\beta_{n})^{m-1}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2^{m+n}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\binom{m}{i}(2i-m)^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(2j-n)^{m-1}.

This complete the proof.
∎

Note that if at least one of
m
and
n
is even, then
\tau_{o}(K_{m,n})=0
. This can be easily deduced by the handshaking lemma.

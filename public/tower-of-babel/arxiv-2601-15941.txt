Frictional work and entropy production in integrable and non-integrable spin chains
Vishnu Muraleedharan Sajitha, Matthew J. Davis, L. A. Williamson

Abstract
The maximum work extractable from a quantum system is achieved when the system is driven adiabatically. Frictional work then quantifies the difference in work output between adiabatic and non-adiabatic driving. Here we show that frictional work in a non-integrable spin chain is well-described by the diagonal entropy production associated with the build up of quantum coherence. The relationship is characterized by an effective temperature of the final adiabatic state and holds for slow to moderate driving protocols. For fast protocols, the frictional work is instead described by the quantum relative entropy between the final non-adiabatic and adiabatic states. We compare our results to those obtained from an integrable spin chain, in which case the adiabatic state is no longer described by a single temperature. In this case, the frictional work is described by a sum of terms for each independent subspace of the spin chain, which are at different effective temperatures. We show how integrability breaking can enhance work extraction in the adiabatic limit, but degrade work extraction in sufficiently non-adiabatic regimes.



I
Introduction

Understanding the role of coherence in work extraction is a fundamental point of interest in the field of quantum thermodynamics
Vinjanampathy and Anders 2016
. Although carefully constructed protocols have utilized coherence in thermodynamic tasks
Uzdin et al. 2015
;
Klatzow et al. 2019
;
Korzekwa et al. 2016
;
Kammerlander and Anders 2016
;
Francica et al. 2020
;
Williamson et al. 2024
;
Uzdin 2016
;
Scully et al. 2003
;
Shastri and Venkatesh 2024
, in many cases the build up of coherence is detrimental to work output
Horodecki and Oppenheim 2013
. In particular, in non-adiabatic work extraction, coherences can build up in the energy eigenbasis, which are then lost under a projective energy measurement — a type of “quantum friction”
Kosloff and Feldmann 2002
;
Feldmann and Kosloff 2003
. The understanding and mitigation of these frictional effects is important, for example, for achieving high efficiency and power output in quantum heat engines
Feldmann and Kosloff 2006
;
Rezek and Kosloff 2006
;
Salamon et al. 2009
;
Alecce et al. 2015
;
Kosloff 2013
.

The minimal work principle states that, for an isolated system initially in thermal equilibrium, the most work is extracted (or least work is expended) when the system is driven adiabatically,
\langle W\rangle_{\tau}\geq\langle W\rangle_{A}

Allahverdyan and Nieuwenhuizen 2005
. Here
\langle W\rangle_{\tau}\equiv\operatorname{Tr}[\rho_{\tau}\hat{H}_{f}]-\operatorname{Tr}[\rho_{i}\hat{H}_{i}]
is the work output for a non-adiabatic process of duration
\tau
, with
\rho_{i}
the initial thermal state,
\rho_{\tau}
the time-evolved state, and
\hat{H}_{i}
(
\hat{H}_{f}
) the initial(final) Hamiltonian. The work output in the adiabatic limit (
\tau\rightarrow\infty
) is
\langle W\rangle_{A}
. Here and throughout we define work via the two-point projective measurement scheme
Talkner et al. 2007
;
Jarzynski et al. 2015
. The difference between the adiabatic and non-adiabatic work output is termed
frictional work
(see Fig.
1
)
Plastina et al. 2014
,

\langle W\rangle_{\mathrm{fric}}\equiv\langle W\rangle_{\tau}-\langle W\rangle_{A}\geq 0.

(1)

Frictional work quantifies the energy “wasted” by using a non-adiabatic drive.

For non-adiabatic driving, the final state
\rho_{\tau}
will typically contain coherences with respect to the final energy eigenbasis, which are responsible for frictional effects
Feldmann and Kosloff 2012
;
Kosloff 2013
. The ensemble of final projective energy measurements then involves a loss of information
Balian 1989
. The associated entropy production is quantified by the diagonal entropy
Polkovnikov 2011
;
Santos et al. 2011
;
Ikeda et al. 2015
;
Kosloff and Rezek 2017
;
Kosloff 2013
,

S_{\mathrm{d}}\left(\rho_{\tau}\right)\equiv S\left(\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{diag}}\right)

(2)

where

\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{diag}}\equiv\sum_{n}\langle n_{f}|\rho_{\tau}|n_{f}\rangle\,|n_{f}\rangle\langle n_{f}|,

(3)

is the projection of
\rho_{\tau}
onto the final energy eigenbasis
\ket{n_{f}}
and
S(\rho)=-\mathrm{Tr}[\rho\ln\rho]
is the von Neumann entropy. In general, it can be shown that
S_{\mathrm{d}}
increases under unitary driving
Polkovnikov 2011
and therefore
S_{\mathrm{d}}
characterizes the entropy increase intuitively expected from finite-time processes, in contrast to the (conserved) von Neumann entropy.

Plastina and coauthors
Plastina et al. 2014
;
Francica et al. 2019
have established a connection between coherences and frictional work when
\rho_{A}
is a Gibbs state with temperature
T_{A}
,
\rho_{A}=\rho_{T_{A}}^{\mathrm{therm}}
. Here and below we define
\rho_{T}^{\mathrm{therm}}\equiv e^{-\hat{H}_{f}/T}/\operatorname{Tr}[e^{-\hat{H}_{f}/T}]
for some specified temperature
T
, where Boltzmann’s constant
k_{B}\equiv 1
. The frictional work then satisfies
Plastina et al. 2014
,

\langle W\rangle_{\mathrm{fric}}=T_{A}\,D(\rho_{\tau}\|\rho_{A})\quad(\rho_{A}=\rho_{T_{A}}^{\mathrm{therm}}),

(4)

with
D(\rho\|\sigma)\equiv\operatorname{Tr}[\rho\log\rho]-\operatorname{Tr}[\rho\log\sigma]
the quantum relative entropy. Utilising the general identity
Francica et al. 2019
,

D(\rho_{\tau}\|\rho_{A})=\Delta S_{\mathrm{d}}+D(\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{diag}}\|\rho_{A})

(5)

we have

\langle W\rangle_{\mathrm{fric}}=T_{A}\Delta S_{\mathrm{d}}+T_{A}D(\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{diag}}\|\rho_{A})\quad(\rho_{A}=\rho_{T_{A}}^{\mathrm{therm}}).

(6)

Here

\Delta S_{\mathrm{d}}\equiv S_{\mathrm{d}}(\rho_{\tau})-S_{\mathrm{d}}(\rho_{A})

(7)

is the diagonal entropy production during the finite-time protocol. Noting that
S_{\mathrm{d}}(\rho_{A})=S(\rho)
, this is also the relative entropy of coherence with respect to the final energy eigenbasis, which is a coherence monotone
Baumgratz et al. 2014
;
Streltsov et al. 2017
. Equation (
6
) expresses frictional work in terms of a contribution from coherences
\Delta S_{\mathrm{d}}
and an additional population term
T_{A}D(\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{diag}}\|\rho_{A})
. The additional population term can be shown to be small [
O(\tau^{-2})
] for slow work processes
Francica et al. 2019
, in which case
W_{\mathrm{fric}}\approx T_{A}\Delta S_{\mathrm{d}}
. This is analogous to the Clausius relation
Q=T\,\Delta S
where the frictional work plays the role of dissipated heat
Q
.

Importantly, for interacting many-body systems
\rho_{A}
will typically not be a Gibbs state. In particular, for
\rho_{A}
to be a Gibbs state, it is necessary that the final energy eigenvalues
E_{n}^{f}
are related to the initial energy eigenvalues
E_{n}^{i}
by a constant scaling factor
\lambda
, i.e.,
E_{n}^{f}=\lambda E_{n}^{i}
. The temperature
T_{A}
of
\rho_{A}
is then related to the initial temperature
T_{i}
via
T_{A}=\lambda T_{i}
. This is satisfied in simple systems, for example, a two-level system or a harmonic oscillator. However, in most systems
energy levels will change disproportionately and
\rho_{A}
will deviate from a Gibbs state. This imposes a substantial restriction on the application of Eq. (
4
) and Eq. (
6
), and motivates an understanding of
\langle W\rangle_{\mathrm{fric}}
that is applicable more generally.
Notably, non-equilibrium states of non-integrable quantum systems often do exhibit thermal properties according to the eigenstate thermalisation hypothesis
D’Alessio et al. 2016a
;
Mori et al. 2018
, in which case we might expect relationships similar to Eq. (
4
) and Eq. (
6
) even when
\rho_{A}
is not a Gibbs state.

In this work we demonstrate the connection between coherences and frictional work in a non-integrable spin chain. Spin chains offer a high degree of tunability in experiments
Britton et al. 2012
;
Monroe et al. 2021
;
Labuhn et al. 2016
;
Browaeys and Lahaye 2020
and are therefore a promising platform for exploring aspects of quantum thermodynamics
Piccitto et al. 2022
;
B. S et al. 2020
;
Williamson and Davis 2024
;
Arezzo et al. 2024
;
Sajitha et al. 2025
;
Wang 2020
;
Solfanelli et al. 2023
. For slow to moderate work processes, we find that, to an excellent approximation,

\langle W\rangle_{\mathrm{fric}}\approx T_{A}\,\Delta S_{\mathrm{d}},

(8)

in which case frictional work is almost entirely attributable to the build up of quantum coherence. The temperature
T_{A}
is an effective temperature of
\rho_{A}
, defined via
S(\rho_{A})=S(\rho_{T_{A}}^{\mathrm{therm}})
. This definition of
T_{A}
reduces to that in Eq. (
4
) when
\rho_{A}
is a Gibbs state. Additionally, we find that frictional work can be reasonably well approximated by,

\langle W\rangle_{\mathrm{fric}}\approx T_{A}\,D(\rho_{\tau}\|\rho_{A}),\quad(\rho_{A}\neq\rho_{T_{A}}^{\mathrm{therm}}),

(9)

irrespective of the duration of the work process, thus generalizing Eq. (
4
) and the decomposition Eq. (
6
) to regimes where
\rho_{A}
is not a Gibbs state.

We identify the importance of integrability by comparing our results to those of an integrable spin chain. In the integrable spin chain, a single temperature
T_{A}
no longer characterizes the temperature of
\rho_{A}
and Eqs. (
8
) and (
9
) no longer hold. Additionally, we show that integrability breaking can enhance work extraction in the adiabatic limit, but can be detrimental in the non-adiabatic regime.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
we provide the motivation for Eq. (

) and Eq. (
9
). In Sec.
III.1
we introduce our system — a spin chain described by the transverse-field Ising model with (non-integrable) or without (integrable) a longitudinal field. In Sec.
III.2
we provide numerical evidence for Eq. (
8
) and Eq. (
9
) in the non-integrable regime. These results are compared with the integrable regime in Sec.
III.3
. In Sec.
III.4
we show how integrability breaking can either enhance or degrade work extraction depending on the duration of the work step. We conclude in Sec.
IV

II
Motivation for main result

The validity of Eq. (
8
) for slow work processes can be motivated as follows. In general, the frictional work can be written as

\langle W\rangle_{\mathrm{fric}}=T\Delta S_{\mathrm{d}}+F_{T}(\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{diag}})-F_{T}(\rho_{A}).

(10)

Here
F_{T}(\rho)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\hat{H}_{f})-TS(\rho)
is the quantum free energy
Gaveau and Schulman 1997
;
Skrzypczyk et al. 2014
;
Parrondo et al. 2015
;
de Oliveira Junior et al. 2025
. Equation (
8
) is a good approximation when
F_{T_{A}}(\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{diag}})-F_{T_{A}}(\rho_{A})\ll T_{A}\,\Delta S_{\mathrm{d}}
. In general,
F_{T}
is minimized for a Gibbs state,
\nabla_{\rho}F_{T}|_{\rho_{T}^{\mathrm{therm}}}=0
.
1
1

1

Here
\nabla_{\rho}F_{T}
is a matrix with elements
\partial F_{T}/\partial\rho_{ij}
. To see that
F_{T}
is minimized by a Gibbs state, first note that
S(\rho)\leq S(\rho^{\mathrm{diag}})

Baumgratz et al. 2014
and hence
F_{T}(\rho)\geq F_{T}(\rho^{\mathrm{diag}})
. Minimising
F_{T}(\rho^{\mathrm{diag}})
with respect to
\rho^{\mathrm{diag}}
then gives
\rho^{\mathrm{diag}}=\rho_{T}^{\mathrm{therm}}
.
Hence,
F_{T}
varies at second order in deviations from
\rho_{T}^{\mathrm{therm}}
. In comparison,
S_{\mathrm{d}}
varies at first order (
\nabla_{\rho}S_{\mathrm{d}}|_{\rho_{T_{A}}^{\mathrm{therm}}}\neq 0
). When both
F(\rho_{A})
and
F(\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{diag}})
are in the vicinity of the minimum
F(\rho_{T_{A}}^{\mathrm{therm}})
, such that
\nabla_{\rho}F_{T_{A}}|_{\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{diag}}}\approx\nabla_{\rho}F_{T_{A}}|_{\rho_{A}}\approx 0
, we will have
F_{T_{A}}(\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{diag}})-F_{T_{A}}(\rho_{A})\ll T_{A}\,\Delta S_{\mathrm{d}}
and Eq. (
8
) will hold. Note
|F_{T}(\rho_{A})-F_{T}(\rho_{T}^{\mathrm{therm}})|
is minimized for
S(\rho_{A})=S(\rho_{T}^{\mathrm{therm}})
, which motivates our choice of
T_{A}
.
2
2

2

In some contexts, the temperature of a non-equilibrium state is defined as the temperature of a Gibbs state with the same mean energy. This is close to
T_{A}
when
|F_{T}(\rho_{A})-F_{T}(\rho_{T}^{\mathrm{therm}})|
is small.

Motivating Eq. (
9
) is more difficult, and in this work we mostly provide empirical evidence for its validity. For slow work processes Eq. (
9
) reduces to Eq. (
8
) [see Eq. (
5
)]. In general, the frictional work can be written as

\begin{split}\langle W\rangle_{\mathrm{fric}}&=T\left[D(\rho_{\tau}\|\rho_{T}^{\mathrm{therm}})-D(\rho_{A}\|\rho_{T}^{\mathrm{therm}})\right]\\
&=TD(\rho_{\tau}\|\rho_{A})+T\delta\end{split}

(11)

with
\delta=D(\rho_{\tau}\|\rho_{T}^{\mathrm{therm}})-D(\rho_{\tau}\|\rho_{A})-D(\rho_{A}\|\rho_{T}^{\mathrm{therm}})
. Equation (
11
) is exact for any temperature
T
, and follows from the definition of quantum relative entropy. As quantum relative entropy is not a metric, we cannot straightforwardly bound
\delta
. We surmise that
|\delta|
will be small compared to
D(\rho_{\tau}\|\rho_{A})
when the difference between
\rho_{\tau}
and
\rho_{A}
is large compared to the difference between
\rho_{A}
and
\rho_{T}^{\mathrm{therm}}
, which we expect will occur for fast work processes.

III
Numerical results

III.1
System

To demonstrate our main result, we consider the transverse-field Ising model with a longitudinal field. The time-dependent Hamiltonian is (
\hbar\equiv 1
)

\hat{H}(t)=-g\sum_{j=1}^{N}\hat{\sigma}_{j}^{x}\hat{\sigma}_{j+1}^{x}-h(t)\sum_{j=1}^{N}\hat{\sigma}_{j}^{z}+L\sum_{j=1}^{N}\hat{\sigma}_{j}^{x}.

(12)

Here
\hat{\sigma}^{\mu}_{j}
are spin-1/2 Pauli operators acting on site
j
(
[\hat{\sigma}_{j}^{\mu},\hat{\sigma}_{j}^{\nu}]=2i\epsilon_{\mu\nu\kappa}\hat{\sigma}_{j}^{\kappa}
),
g
is the Ising interaction strength and
L
is the longitudinal field strength. The system is driven by a time-dependent transverse field strength
h(t)
. We employ periodic boundary conditions
\hat{\sigma}_{N+1}^{\nu}=\hat{\sigma}_{1}^{\nu}
. For
L=0
the spin chain is integrable and Eq. (
12
) can be diagonalized by mapping the system onto
N
non-interacting fermions
Pfeuty 1970
;
Mbeng et al. 2024
;
Sachdev 2011
. A non-zero
L
breaks integrability; for the finite-size systems explored here this integrability breaking is most robust for
g\sim h\sim L

Kim and Huse 2013
;
Kim et al. 2014
.

We assume an initial thermal state
\rho_{i}=e^{-\hat{H}_{i}/T_{i}}/\operatorname{Tr}[e^{-\hat{H}_{i}/T_{i}}]
, with
\hat{H}_{i}
the initial Hamiltonian with transverse field strength
h_{i}
. The system is then driven unitarily over a duration
\tau
by a time-dependent field
h(t)
, see Fig.
1
. For the results presented here we use a linear ramp
h(t)=h_{i}+(t/\tau)\Delta h
, although we have checked that similar results hold for other drive protocols. The final Hamiltonian is
\hat{H}_{f}
with transverse field strength
h_{f}
. Strictly, the minimum work principal assumes no level crossings
Allahverdyan and Nieuwenhuizen 2005
. We take
h_{i},h_{f}>g
to avoid effects of the paramagnetic-ferromagnetic crossover at
h\approx g

Uzelac et al. 1980
. We find that the minimum work principal then holds in the results presented here, despite higher energy-level crossings. We take
\Delta h=h_{f}-h_{i}>0
, in which case the drive extracts work from the system (
\langle W\rangle_{\tau}<0
). For
L\neq 0
, the initial state is obtained numerically from exact diagonalisation of
\hat{H}_{i}
and time evolved using Runge-Kutta integration. The final adiabatic state is obtained using a long protocol duration (
\tau=100g^{-1}
).

III.4
Impact of integrability on total work extraction

In this final subsection we characterize whether integrability is beneficial or detrimental to total work output. To enable comparison between the integrable and non-integrable results, we introduce an “optimal” work output

\langle W\rangle_{\mathrm{opt}}\equiv\operatorname{Tr}[\rho_{T_{A}}^{\mathrm{therm}}\hat{H}_{f}]-\operatorname{Tr}[\rho_{i}\hat{H}_{i}].

(18)

The state
\rho_{T_{A}}^{\mathrm{therm}}
minimizes the energy over all states with fixed diagonal entropy
Allahverdyan et al. 2004
and therefore
\langle W\rangle_{\tau}\geq\langle W\rangle_{A}\geq\langle W\rangle_{\mathrm{opt}}
(with
\langle W\rangle_{A}=\langle W\rangle_{\mathrm{opt}}
when
\rho_{A}=\rho_{T_{A}}^{\mathrm{therm}}
). The difference between
\langle W\rangle_{\tau}
and
\langle W\rangle_{\mathrm{opt}}
then gives an indication of how optimal the work output is.

Figure
5
(a) compares
\langle W\rangle_{\tau}
to
\langle W\rangle_{\mathrm{opt}}
for both the integrable (
L=0
) and the non-integrable (
L=g
) spin chains for different work durations
\tau
. For slow work extraction (
\tau\gtrsim\Delta h/g^{2}
), frictional work is small and the work output is close to the adiabatic limit. In this regime
\langle W\rangle_{\tau}-\langle W\rangle_{\mathrm{opt}}
is smaller in the non-integrable case, as
\rho_{A}
is better approximated by a thermal state. For rapid work extraction (
\tau\ll\Delta h/g^{2}
), the frictional work is higher in the non-integrable case than the integrable case. In this regime, conservation laws in the integrable system restrict non-adiabatic transitions and suppress frictional losses (this behavior is analogous to classical systems, where integrability confines motion to regions within the total phase space, reducing the accessible volume for the dynamics
D’Alessio et al. 2016b
). Figure
5
(b) compares
\langle W\rangle_{\tau}
and
\langle W\rangle_{\mathrm{opt}}
as a function of
L
. This shows that
\langle W\rangle_{\tau}-\langle W\rangle_{\mathrm{opt}}
decreases with
L
for
\tau\gtrsim\Delta h/g^{2}
, whereas
\langle W\rangle_{\tau}-\langle W\rangle_{\mathrm{opt}}
increases with
L
for small
\tau
.

IV
Conclusion

We have explored frictional work in a non-integrable spin chain and identified regimes in which this is well described by diagonal entropy production. The relationship is characterized by the effective temperature of the final adiabatic state and holds for slow to moderate work processes. For fast work processes, the frictional work is well described by the relative entropy between the final non-adiabatic and adiabatic states. For comparison, in an integrable spin chain the frictional work is characterized by different temperatures for each independent subspace of the system. We show that integrability breaking enhances work output in the adiabatic limit but degrades work output for sufficiently rapid work extraction.

Spin chains can be realized in a variety of experimental platforms with a high degree of control
Britton et al. 2012
;
Monroe et al. 2021
;
Labuhn et al. 2016
;
Browaeys and Lahaye 2020
, enabling our results to be probed experimentally. An extension of this work could explore effects of integrability breaking on frictional work fluctuations
Plastina et al. 2014
, which are more sensitive to non-equilibrium aspects of the underlying ensemble
Jeon et al. 2015
;
Jin et al. 2016
;
Williamson 2025
;
Gong and Quan 2015
;
Hoang et al. 2018
;
Alhambra et al. 2016
. It would also be interesting to extend the analysis presented here to other non-integrable systems, particularly those with integrable limits that cannot be mapped onto a non-interacting system. The one-dimensional Bose gas is one such example, which is integrable in the homogeneous Lieb-Liniger regime
Cazalilla et al. 2011
. Work extraction from this system has recently been studied using various engine cycles
Watson and Kheruntsyan 2025a
;
Watson and Kheruntsyan 2025b
;
Nautiyal et al. 2024
;
Nautiyal 2025
. Finally, it would be interesting to explore the effects of integrability breaking on the performance of a quantum thermal machine. Here performance depends not only on the work output but also the heat exchanged with the reservoir, which will likely be sensitive to the presence or absence of integrability.

Design, synthesis, QSAR modelling and molecular dynamic simulations of N-tosyl-indole hybrid thiosemicarbazones as competitive tyrosinase inhibitors
Authors: Zahra Batool, Saeed Ullah, Ajmal Khan, Suraj N. Mali, Shailesh S. Gurav, Rahul D. Jawarkar, Abdulrahman Alshammari, Norah A. Albekairi, Ahmed Al-Harrasi, Zahid Shafiq
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Tyrosinase is an enzyme crucial for the progression of melanogenesis. Immoderate production of melanin may be the cause of hyperpigmentation and darkening leading to skin diseases. Tyrosinase is the most researched target for suppressing melanogenesis since it catalyzes the rate-limiting stage of melanin production. Thiosemicarbazones have been reported to possess strong inhibition capability against tyrosinase. We have designed and synthesized eighteen N-tosyl substituted indole-based thiosemicarbazones as competitive tyrosinase inhibitors in the current work. All the compounds exhibited outstanding to good potency with half maximal inhibitory concentration in the range of 6.40 ± 0.21 µM to 61.84 ± 1.47 µM. The compound 5r displayed the top-tier inhibition amongst the entire series with IC50 = 6.40 ± 0.21 µM. Compounds, 5q and 5r exhibited competitive inhibitions in concentration dependent manner with Ki = 3.42 ± 0.03 and 10.25 ± 0.08 µM respectively. The binding mode of 5r was evaluated through in silico molecular dynamics simulations and molecular docking, while ADME assessment studies predicted the drug-like characteristics of the derivatives. The newly synthesized derivatives may serve as a structural guide for designing and developing novel tyrosinase inhibitors.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-75100-1.

Introduction

Tyrosinase (EC 1.14.18.1 TYR) is a metalloenzyme containing two copper ions in its active site. It is extensively present in microorganisms, plants, and animals1. It is responsible for catalyzing the oxidation of o-diphenols to o-quinones (diphenolase activity) and the hydroxylation and oxidation of monophenols to o-quinones (monophenolase activity) to produce melanin2,3. Melanin, which is produced by melanocytes is an essential biopolymer that controls eye, skin, and hair color4–6. One important role of melanin is to protect the skin against ultraviolet damage7–9. Melanogenesis is crucial in several physiological and pathological processes in mammals. On the other hand, it has been demonstrated that TYR hyperactivity and excessive ultraviolet exposure can result in an excess of melanin being produced and deposited in the skin. This excess melanin is linked to the onset and progression of various dermatological conditions, which generally results in several pigmentation skin diseases, including lentigo, age spots, freckles10, post-inflammatory melanoderma and melasma11. Furthermore, TYR-catalyzed conversion of tyrosine to dopaquinone may result in neurotoxicity, which has been connected to neurological diseases including Huntington’s, Alzheimer’s, and Parkinson’s disorders12. Moreover, TYR causes browning in food produced from plants, which alters the sensory and gustatory qualities, nutritional features of the protein, and shelf life of fresh-cut goods12,13.

The importance of tyrosinase has been emphasized widely, and as a result, the interest of researchers in finding effective and practical tyrosinase inhibitors has grown. Thiosemicarbazones sometimes referred to as chelating agents for metal ions14, have drawn major interest from medicinal chemists in the present day due to their intriguing pharmacological characteristics and potential biological applications15. Several TSC compounds have also been investigated for their potential in the treatment as antifungal16, anticancer17, antibacterial18, and anti-HIV-1 agents, to reduce melanogenesis8 and as inhibitors of TYR19–22.

Many synthetic and naturally existing aromatic thiosemicarbazone compounds have been documented as tyrosinase inhibitors so far17,19,23–25. Thiosemicarbazone derivatives are regarded potentially useful because the sulphur atom of the thiosemicarbazone moiety can chelate the copper ions which are present in the tyrosinase enzyme, thereby suppressing the enzymatic reaction. Thiosemicarbazones’ electronegative oxygen, nitrogen, and sulfur atoms improve the enzyme’s coordination and chelation with metal ions, which may reduce tyrosinase’s catalytic activity. Through van der Waals forces, the aromatic residue can come into contact with the hydrophobic site of the enzyme26. Thiosemicarbazones have also been shown to be safe in vitro and silico experiments, with good absorption and bioavailability and little to no cytotoxic effects.

The indole scaffold, which possesses a pyrrole structure parallel to benzene, is a crucial structural element in drug discovery because of its unique ability to mimic peptide structure and interact with enzymes27–30. Certain indole compounds have been reported as possible tyrosinase inhibitors in earlier research as shown in Fig. 1.31–33 The investigations indicate that indole can be used as a fundamental framework for the development of potent tyrosinase inhibitors. It has also been reported that indole analogues with N substitution such as phenyl and benzyl substitution have shown significant differences in their activity34,35.Fig. 1Reported structures of thiosemicarbazone and indole based Tyrosinase Inhibitors.

Reported structures of thiosemicarbazone and indole based Tyrosinase Inhibitors.

The sulfonamide functional group demonstrated its great potency to produce new effects/actions in the previously synthesized biologically active scaffolds. For example, potent inhibitors of the butyrylcholinesterase (BChE) enzyme linked to Alzheimer’s disease have been synthesized from the formerly marketed lead scaffold of sulfonamide, which demonstrated excellent potency at low levels of concentration36. SO2N < moiety has been included in numerous chemotherapeutically significant sulfa drugs that are used as endothelin receptor ETA, being a selective antagonist, and have been shown to act as antibacterial agents, carbonic anhydrase inhibitors, HIV protease inhibitors, antiepileptic agents, anti-amoebic agents, psychosis, diabetes, tumors, various cancer treatments, and central nervous system (CNS) disorders. In individuals with AIDS, sulfonamide-trimethoprim combos are often utilized to treat opportunistic infections37.

Many tyrosinase inhibitors have been identified during the past few decades, but only a small number have sufficient potency and safety for use in the food and pharma industries38. Therefore, a key goal in food and pharmaceutical chemistry is the development of new, safe, stable, and highly effective tyrosinase inhibitors.

Thus, it is plausible that thiosemicarbazone and N substituted indole might be employed as viable pharmacophores for designing novel inhibitors of tyrosinase. This work aimed to design and synthesize hybrid molecules combining both the thiosemicarbazone moiety and the N-tosyl indole pharmacophore and investigate their ability to act as potent TYR inhibitors.

Results and discussion

Chemistry

The synthetic course for the synthesis of the novel thiosemicarbazones 5(a-r) is displayed in Fig. 2 For the formation of the targeted thiosemicarbazones, a two-step methodology was employed. Firstly, indole 3-carbaldehyde was substituted at the N position by tosyl group using tosyl chloride (2) by using triethylamine. This modification mainly includes the replacement of hydrogen at the N–H position of indole by the tosyl group. Following the standard protocol outlined in the experimental section, N-1 substituted tosyl indole (3) was obtained as a pure solid with excellent yield.Fig. 2Synthesis of Thiosemicarbazone derivatives 5(a-r).

Synthesis of Thiosemicarbazone derivatives 5(a-r).

In the second step, various thiosemicarbazides derivatives 4(a-r) were refluxed with N-tosyl indole 3-carbaldehyde (3) using ethanol as solvent and acetic acid in the catalytic amount (2–3 drops) in the reaction mixture to yield targeted thiosemicarbazones 5(a-r) by the reaction of Schiff base formation. The progression and completion of reaction was confirmed by TLC. Final derivatives 5(a-r) were obtained in high yield (70–100%) and excellent purity.

Structural elucidation of the synthesized compounds 5(a-r) was accomplished by spectral analysis. In the 1H NMR spectra, the primary signals indicative of the production of compounds were observed. The spectra exhibited a singlet at 2.30–2.33 ppm attributed to the methyl group in the N-tosyl substitution of indole. Azomethine hydrogen (HC=N) in the displacement range of 8.34–8.46 ppm was observed, confirming the condensation with aldehydes. The signals for NH-N=C and S=C-NH-R appeared in the range of δ 11.54–12.15 and δ 9.45–12.12 ppm, respectively. In the 13C NMR, signals referring to characteristic carbons were also elucidated. The chemical shifts referring to C=N and C=S were observed in the δ 145.9–161.0 and 175.6–177.4 ppm ranges, respectively. Other aromatic and aliphatic carbons appeared in the respective range supporting the structure.

Structure activity relationship (SAR)

According to the preliminary SAR, the compounds’ thiosemicarbazide moiety was primarily responsible for their inhibitory effect. To better understand the biological activity of the indole-based thiosemicarbazones, we further explore the impact of various substitutions on the thiosemicarbazide’s R group Fig. 3. Dong et. al synthesized various aromatic heterocycle thiosemicarbazones and assessed them for anti-tyrosinase activity. The unsubstituted indole based thiosemicarbazone exhibited low activity with an IC50 value of 101.89 µM24. Our current synthesized series with substitution on the N position of indole and N4 of thiosemicarbazide exhibited better activity with an IC50 value of 6.40 ± 0.21 µM to 61.84 ± 1.47 µM. The R substitution is achieved using both aromatic and non-aromatic residues. The nitrophenyl substitution exhibited maximum activity in this series with compound 5r having 3-nitrophenyl substitution exhibiting the IC50 value of 6.40 ± 0.21 µM and compound 5q with 4-nitrophenyl substitution showed the IC50 value of 7.26 ± 0.18 µM. Besides, halogen substituted compounds such as chlorophenyl, chlorobenzyl, and fluorophenyl also exhibited highly potent inhibition against the tyrosinase comparable to the positive control, kojic acid (IC50 = 18.30 ± 0.41 µM). Compound 5 l with 4-chlorophenyl substitution displayed IC50 = 8.31 ± 0.20 µM, while compounds 5c and 5n with 4-chlorobenzyl and 2-fluorophenyl substitutions showed IC50 equal to 13.71 ± 0.30 µM and 10.53 ± 0.19 µM respectively. In compound 5f with 2, 3-dichlorophenyl substitution the, potency against tyrosinase is decreased with IC50 = 11.52 ± 0.36 µM which may suggest that an increase in the number of halogen substitutions on the phenyl ring will lead to a decrease in the inhibition potency. The halo-substituted compounds that displayed lower potency than the standard kojic acid against tyrosinase enzyme are 5 k and 5o with 4-fluorophenyl (IC50 = 21.39 ± 0.48 µM) and 4-bromophenyl substitution (IC50 = 25.31 ± 0.38 µM) respectively. Among halogens, most compounds with chloro substitutions displayed superior inhibition potential. The compounds containing methyl substitution at phenyl and benzyl rings displayed high to moderate inhibitory potential. Compound 5j and 5d with p-methylphenyl and o-methylphenyl substitution at the thiosemicarbazide moiety exhibited IC50 equals to 14.27 ± 0.30 µM and 24.27 ± 0.51 µM respectively. The derivative 5i with methyl substitutions at 2 and 6 positions of the phenyl resulted in remarkable inhibitory potential with IC50 = 9.50 ± 0.26 µM. while in derivative 5a with methyl substitutions at 3 and 5 positions, the inhibition potential is diminished with IC50 = 20.63 ± 0.00 µM.Fig. 3SAR of N-tosyl-indole based thiosemicarbazones.

SAR of N-tosyl-indole based thiosemicarbazones.

The introduction of methoxy substitution at the 4 position of the phenyl further dropped potency against tyrosinase as in derivative 5b (IC50 = 29.35 ± 0.60). Compound 5 h with phenyl substitution at the thiosemicarbazide exhibited IC50 = 42.17 ± 0.76 µM while in the case of compound 5e with cyclohexyl substitution, the IC50 value goes to 61.84 ± 1.47 µM making it the least potent compound of the series. This may be due to the loss of aromaticity in the cyclohexyl group which decreases the favorable binding of the inhibitor to active site of tyrosinase. N substitution at indole also performs a crucial function in enhancing the biological potential of the indole based thiosemicarbazones. In a previous study, indole thiosemicarbazones without any substitution at N position of indole showed extremely lower potency against tyrosinase24. While the current series with the tosyl group at the N position of indole indicated appreciably enhanced inhibition potential. The structure activity relationship of this series serves as a leading point for the development of the effective inhibitors of tyrosinase with N-tosyl indole scaffold.

Molecular docking

In-silico binding pocket analysis of analogues against tyrosinase

An in-silico molecular docking study analyzed the binding conformations of synthesized derivatives (5a-5r) in the catalytic pocket of enzyme tyrosinase (PDB ID: 2Y9X; Resolution: 2.78 Å; Global Symmetry: Asymmetric - C1) [https://www.rcsb.org/structure/2y9x]. Docking scores (XP: Extra Precision) and hydrophobic/phobic bonding interactions were examined. Despite minimal fluctuations, no significant energy differences were observed among docked molecules because of their similar basic skeleton. Consequently, most ligands exhibited efficient docking energies. Compound 5r showed the highest activity (-9.234 kcal/mol) and was further analyzed for its interactions in the tyrosinase’s catalytic site. Scaffold induced four distinct hydrogen bond interactions with pocket amino acid residues. The sulfonyl group oxygen-atom of compound 5r unveiled two H-bonds with two residues, comprising ASN A:81 (2.30 Å) and HIS A:85 (2.05 Å). Also, residues HIS A:85 (4.94 Å) and PHE A:264 (5.16 Å) exhibited π-sulfur interactions with sulfur-atoms of sulfonyl and thione groups, respectively. Further, residue ASN A:260 induced crucial H-bonding at 2.40 Å with the NH proton of the hydrazine group, and residue HIS A:244 unveiled H-bonding at 2.55 Å with the nitrogen atom of the hydrazine group (Fig. 5). Also, the N-atom of the hydrazine group exhibited attractive charge interaction (5.24 Å) with GLU A:256. Two amino acid residues HIS A:61 (3.08 Å) and HIS A:259 (3.23 Å) formed carbon-hydrogen bonds with oxygen-atoms of the nitro group. The pi-electron density of p-nitro phenyl moiety of 5r was found in linkage with the residue HIS A:263 revealing vital pi-pi stacked interaction at 4.31 Å. Residue VAL A:283 exhibited two interactions including pi-sigma (3.72 Å) with pi-electrons of p-nitro phenyl group and pi-alkyl (4.76 Å) with indole moiety. Additionally, prominent metal-acceptor interaction was found between copper ion and oxygen atom of the nitro group at 2.85 Å. Residue ALA A:80 was found connected through alkyl interaction (4.07 Å) to the methyl group of tosyl moiety. The crucial residues in the tyrosinase signature, including ASN 81, HIS 85, HIS 244, HIS 259, ASN 260, HIS 263, MET 280, and Cu 401 were preserved in the binding pocket in the vicinity of 5r. Previous studies highlighted ASN 81, ASN 260, HIS 259, HIS 263, and MET 280 as essential residues for stabilizing the protein-inhibitor complex. Molecular electrostatic potential (MEP)

Molecular electrostatic potential (MEP) is a helpful tool to explore charge distribution across a molecule three-dimensionally and understand the reactivity and polarity of the molecule. MEP mapping is also a beneficial way for the qualitative elucidation of nucleophilic and electrophilic reaction sites of the molecule which are useful for the investigation of a biological discovery process such as anticipating and examining molecular interactions like drug-receptor, hydrogen bonds, and enzyme–substrate interactions. The negative electrostatic potential and electrophilic reactivity are represented by the orange, red, and yellow regions; the close to zero electrostatic potential (green region), and the positive electrostatic potential and nucleophilic reactivity are indicated by the blue and green regions. The MEP plot of compound 5r is shown in Fig. 8, indicate that the sulfone group of molecule holds maximum negative electrostatic potential with MEP value around -5.57 a.u. while nitrogen of the carbothioamide group holds the most positive electrostatic potential with MEP value around + 5.57 a.u.Fig. 8Molecular electrostatic potential (MEP) plot of 5r.

The GA-MLR model, which consists of three parameters, has strong external prediction capability due to the inclusion of simply comprehensible molecular descriptors and interpretation based on structural characteristics. Discussion

According to the OECD guidelines 60, QSAR models for regulatory usage should have the following characteristics: 1) a clearly defined endpoint, 2) a clear algorithm, 3) a defined domain of applicability, 4) appropriate measures of goodness-of-fit, robustness, and predictivity, and 5) a mechanistic interpretation, preferably. Therefore, the process of enhancing understanding about the mechanistic aspects include the linkage of molecular descriptors using pharmacophore or structure-oriented approaches, as well as the rigorous validation of the connection between important molecular characteristics (molecular descriptors) and the required bio-activity. In this study, we have conducted a comparative analysis of the pIC50 values of several compounds to examine the influence of a particular chemical descriptor. Nevertheless, it is crucial to acknowledge that other chemical descriptors, structural characteristics, or unidentified elements might potentially have similar or contrasting impacts that considerably affect the total pIC50 value of a molecule for tyrosinase. Essentially, relying just on a single chemical descriptor is insufficient to completely account for the observed pIC50 value of several compounds for tyrosinase. Hence, the effective use of the proposed Quantitative Structure–Activity Relationship (QSAR) model is contingent upon the concurrent integration of several component molecular descriptors.

Within the QSAR model, two descriptors, namely fHringC2B and faccH5B, have been identified as being associated with the lipophilic and electrical properties of the molecule. Hence, enhancing the overall lipophilicity and electronic characteristics of a molecule facilitates the augmentation of its binding affinity with tyrosinase.

fHringC2B: The aforementioned chemical description represents the frequency at which ring carbon atoms are located precisely two bonds away from the hydrogen atom. The observed positive connection between the molecular descriptor fHringC2B and pIC50 suggests that a raise in the number of ring carbon atoms may result in enhanced binding affinity towards tyrosinase. The discovery is further supported by the positive coefficient of fHringC2B in the recently constructed QSAR model. The finding is supported by a comparison of compound 5a (pIC50: 4.68 M, fHringC2B:8) and compound 5b (pIC50: 4.53 M, fHringC2B:7). The biological activity of compound 5b is dramatically reduced due to the absence of a single carbon atom.

faccH5B: The molecular descriptor quantifies the frequency of hydrogen atom occurrence only at a distance of 5 bonds from the acceptor atom. The significance of the electronic and steric characteristics of the molecule is underscored by this description. In the constructed model, the occurrence of a positive coefficient for faccH5B shows that increasing the number of hydrogen atoms near H-bond capable groups (acceptor) is an effective approach to enhance the pIC50 of a molecule. These further underscores the significance of electrostatic forces since H-bond acceptors are regarded as electronic entities. Nevertheless, the faccH5B provides further clarification that achieving optimal binding requires a rather large ratio of acceptor atom to hydrogen atom in the H-bond acceptor. According to reports, hydrogen is the superior element for reducing steric build. A comparison of molecule 5q (pIC50: 5.13 M, faccH5B:6) with 5d (pIC50: 4.61 M, faccH5B:4) supports this observation. Therefore, a combination of an H-bond acceptor with an H atom is necessary.

notringC_notringS_9B counts the appearance of non-ring sulphur atoms within 9 bonds from the non-ring carbon atoms. The descriptor notringC_notringS_9B has a negative correlation with pIC50 and also a negative coefficient in model 1, which indicate that the existence of notringS within nine bonds from notringC in a molecule leads to a lesser pIC50. Thus, there should be a large distance between such types of non-ring sulphur atoms, if possible 10 bonds or more in the molecule. For example, a comparison of 5r with 5g (displayed in Fig. 8), and 5j with 5f, further empowered this observation.

In silico ADME, pharmacokinetics, and drug-likeness studies

The pharmacokinetic and ADME profiles are helpful in predicting drug candidate bioavailability, permeability, and absorption properties, aiding in the selection of molecules with favorable absorption profiles. Factors such as gastrointestinal absorption and blood–brain barrier penetration influence drug distribution within the body. The cytochromes P450 (CYP) superfamily of isoenzymes plays a crucial role in drug elimination via metabolic biotransformation. Inhibitors of cytochrome enzymes (CYP) can affect the metabolism and probable drug-drug interactions. Lipinski’s rule of five serves as a useful initial filter, assessing the drug-likeness of molecules39.

Further, all the derivatives act as inhibitors of CYP2C19, CYP2C9, and CYP3A4 furthermore, non-inhibitor of CYP1A2 and CYP2D6. The drug-likeness studies revealed that analogs, including 5b, 5e, 5h, 5m, 5q, and 5r are orally active as they comply with the widely used Lipinski rule of five. Conclusion

Thiosemicarbazones bearing N tosyl substituted indole were synthesized as potent tyrosinase inhibitors. Among the synthesized compounds, derivatives 5r, 5q, 5l, 5i, 5n, 5f, 5c, and 5j were found to be more potent than the reference standard kojic acid. 5q and 5r were found to be the most potent inhibitors of the entire series. To evaluate binding interactions, the results of tyrosinase inhibition were further examined using molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulations. Ultimately, attractive pharmacokinetic profiles were anticipated for all synthesized drugs by an in-silico examination of ADME features. As a result, compound 5r may be viewed as a top contender for the drug discovery process aimed at finding a remedy for the negative effects of hyperpigmentation and other skin disorders.

Experimental

General

For the synthesis of indole-based thiosemicarbazones, all the starting materials such as indole 3-carbaldehyde were bought from Sigma Aldrich. Chemicals and solvents including ethanol, methanol, glacial acetic acid, petroleum ether, and ethyl acetate were bought from Merck and used as original. Silica gel plates with aluminum back were utilized to check the reaction progress and completion. A Bruker Ascend 600 MHz NMR spectrometer was utilized to get 1-H and 13-C NMR spectra in deuterated solvent DMSO-d6 at 25 °C (600 MHz for 1H and 151 MHz for 13C). NMR spectra were presented as chemical shifts (ppm), and coupling constants (J) were demonstrated in Hertz (Hz) to detail signal multiplicity.

General method for the synthesis of 1-tosyl-1H-indole-3-carbaldehyde (3a)

A mixture of indole-3-carbaldehyde (1) (10.3 mmol) and p-tosyl-chloride (2) (15.7 mmol) in triethylamine was heated at 90–95 °C for 2 h. Until the completion of the reaction as checked by TLC. The reaction mixture was transferred to 50 mL of ice-cold water, allowed to chill in the refrigerator for one hour, and then filtered. To obtain the pure product, the precipitates were washed with water and allowed to air dried to obtain 1-tosyl-1H-indole-3-carbaldehyde (3a) The resultant aldehyde was pure enough and used for the next step without further purification.

General method for the synthesis and characterization of compounds 5(a–r)

A mixture of 1-tosyl-1H-indole-3-carbaldehyde (3) (0.1 g, 0.33 mmol) and N4-substituted thiosemicarbazide derivatives (0.33 mmol) 5(a-r) was refluxed in 10 mL ethanol for 2–4 h with catalytic amount (2–3 drops) of acetic acid. Reaction progress was observed with TLC utilizing 1:1 ethyl acetate and petroleum ether as eluent. The solid residue formed was filtered, washed with ethanol, and air-dried. The compounds were further recrystallized from 50% ethanol or methanol to yield targeted thiosemicarbazones 5(a-r). The 1HNMR and 13C NMR spectra are given in the Supporting Information Figure SI-1 to SI-18.

Tyrosinase inhibition assay

The 96-well plate tyrosinase inhibition assay was carried out using a spectrophotometer microplate reader, following Masamoto et al.'s methodology. First, 10 µL of the test compounds and “kojic acid”, the positive control, were employed into the 96-well microplate. Followed by the addition of 10 µL of mushroom tyrosinase (30 U/mL in phosphate buffer), and 60 µL of phosphate buffer to the reaction mixture. After incubation, 20 µL of L-DOPA (0.5 mM) in phosphate buffer was added, and the reaction mixture’s optical density (OD480) at 480 nm was determined spectrophotometrically. To reduce the possible errors, all these experiments were performed in triplicate, and the findings were presented as mean. EZ-Fit, an enzyme kinetics program that fits curves, was used to examine all of the in vitro test findings, including % inhibition and IC50 values (Perrella Scientific Inc., Amherst, USA).

To insight into the mechanism of action of the identified inhibitors, the in-vitro mechanistic study was performed by following similar experimental conditions. The kinetic study was carried out by using four variable concentrations of the substrate L-Dopa, including 0.1, 0.2, 0.4 and 0.8 mM.

Molecular docking analysis

For the docking analysis, we selected the pdb id based on the earlier literature. The concerned protein was downloaded from the protein database bank (https://www.rcsb.org/structure/2y9x; Accessed on: 23-03-2024).41,42 The protein was prepared using the Maestro Platform via ‘Protein Preparation Workflow’. It was then processed for additions of hydrogens, removal of water molecules (beyond 5 Å) and adjustment for its protonation states.41,42 The pH condition parameter was kept to default settings. The grid was generated based on the earlier data (20 Å box). All the structures of ligands were drawn and processed utilizing the ‘LigPrep, LLC, Schrodinger, NY, 2023’ module. The docking was done using the ‘Glide-XP’ settings and the visualization was made for the best docked candidate using the ‘Discovery Studio Visualizer, 2023′43 (Glide, Schrodinger, LLC, NY, 2023.

Molecular dynamics simulation

MD investigations were conducted using the Desmond package (version 7.2) and the OPLS4 force field to evaluate lead 5r.44,45 The protocol details have been explained previously42–44. To assess domain correlations and study protein–ligand interactions, Root Mean Square Deviation (RMSD) and Root Mean Square Fluctuation (RMSF) analyses were carried out across all Cα atoms throughout the 200 ns MD simulation for 2y9x:5r complex.

2D-QSAR: multiple linear regression (MLR) calculations, based on genetic algorithm

We carried out the QSAR modeling for the current set of molecules using the popular software ‘QSARINS’. QSARINS developed multiple linear regression (MLR) models via ordinary least squares, validated through a chemometric approach.46,47 Molecular structures were first optimized and descriptors were computed, further filtering out irrelevant and correlated variables, we achieved a set of 300 + descriptors. We classified the dataset based on random selections, however, we made sure that the metrics for validation satisfied. QSARINS explored descriptor combinations using genetic algorithms (GA) guided by Friedman’s "lack-of-fit" function.46,47 The splitting of dataset was done randomly; however, we make sure that the statistical robustness was achieved to get best QSAR model. Internal validation included cross-validation techniques and external validation methods (cross-validation leave-one-out (Q2LOO), cross-validation leave-many-out (Q2LMO), root mean squared error (RMSE), Y-scrambling, and external validation by Q2F1, Q2F2, and Q2F3, CCC). Model performance was then rigorously assessed to ensure reliability, with outliers and influential samples identified.46,47.

Lower bounds on heights of odd degree points of hyperelliptic curves
Jef Laga, Jack A. Thorne

Abstract
We develop a reduction theory for the representation of SL n \SL_{n} on pairs of symmetric n × n n\times n matrices. We apply this theory to the pencils of quadrics arising from divisors on hyperelliptic curves. We use these results to show that, in a density 1 1 family, an odd degree point P P of degree at most 2 ​ g − 1 2g-1 on the hyperelliptic curve z 2 = f 0 ​ x 2 ​ g + 2 + f 1 ​ x 2 ​ g + 1 ​ y + ⋯ + f 2 ​ g + 2 ​ y 2 ​ g + 2 z^{2}=f_{0}x^{2g+2}+f_{1}x^{2g+1}y+\cdots+f_{2g+2}y^{2g+2} cannot have small Weil height.

1.2
Methods

Let

and consider the representation of
\SL_{n}
on the space

of pairs of symmetric

matrices, where the action is defined by
g\cdot(A,B)=(g^{-t}Ag^{-1},g^{-t}Bg^{-1})
.
To such a pair

, we may associate an invariant binary form

\displaystyle f_{A,B}(x,y)=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\det(Ax-By)

whose coefficients freely generate the ring of polynomial invariants for the
\SL_{n}
-action on
V

[
8
]
.

A key step in our proof of Theorem

is the development of a reduction theory for the

-action on

.
Generally speaking, given an action of an arithmetic group on a set, reduction theory aims to find ‘reduced’ representatives of orbits for the action, e.g., representatives whose coefficients are small.
In our setting, we define for every pair
(A,B)\in V(\mathbb{R})
with
\disc(f_{A,B})\neq 0
an inner product

on

, whose formation is

-equivariant.
We say that a pair

is Minkowski (or LLL) reduced if the standard basis of

is Minkowski (or LLL) reduced with respect to
H_{A,B}
.
The coefficients of reduced pairs (in either sense) can be absolutely bounded in terms of the coefficients of

, and so the problem of reducing elements in

becomes a problem in the reduction theory of lattices, which is well understood.

Let

denote the symmetric space of inner products on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
up to scaling.
We call the association
(A,B)\mapsto H_{A,B}
or, by passage to the quotient, the association

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\colon\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash V(\mathbb{Z})^{\Delta\neq 0}\rightarrow\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X

the ‘reduction covariant’ of the representation

, akin to the reduction covariant defined in

26

30
]

In the context of
[
14
]
, the reduction covariant is used in the algorithmic study of
n
-descent on elliptic curves, and we similarly expect our reduction covariant to be useful for computational purposes.

In this paper, we instead employ the reduction covariant for theoretical purposes, and relate it to the descent theory of hyperelliptic curves.
Suppose

is a binary form of degree
n
and nonzero discriminant, and let

be the subset of
(A,B)
with

Given an effective

We show that (in a precise sense) if

On the other hand, we show that the reduction covariants of orbits of height bounded by

Since the subset of inner products in

The construction of integral orbits alluded to in the previous paragraph is a key technical innovation in this paper, which we achieve by introducing a new method to construct such orbits.
This is essentially a local question, so we may begin with a rational odd degree divisor over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and construct an element of

.
Such a construction was given in our setting in
[

, Theorem 15]
, although their argument contains gaps, which were corrected by Swaminathan

A construction in the setting of odd hyperelliptic curves was given by Bhargava–Gross
[
6
, Propostion 19]
, but this includes a reduction step that makes it not useful for our purpose here (where we have to be able to show that a specific vector is integral with respect to the given integral structure, in order to know that its length cannot be too small too often). In our previous work
[

]
, we resolved this by a more careful construction that still, like the one in
[

]
, relies on an induction of the degree of the divisor.

The construction of integral orbits we give here uses the interpretation of these orbits given by Wood

]
, namely as corresponding to certain classes of ideals for the ring associated to the binary form

. This ring may be thought of as the global sections of the structure sheaf on the subscheme

of

.

We now describe the relation between the methods employed here and those used to prove the main theorems of our previous work on odd hyperelliptic curves
[
19
]
. In that paper, we employed a broadly similar strategy, using the equidistribution of reduction covariants, to obtain height lower bounds in a density-

family of monic odd genus-

on self-adjoint traceless

First, the representation of

considered in this paper falls outside the scope of Vinberg theory, so the general construction of the reduction covariant of
[

does not apply here. In fact, for the representation of

Second, the reduction covariant here equidistributes over all integral orbits of nonzero discriminant in

[

Finally, the construction of integral orbits described above uses a different method to the one employed in
[
19
]
. It seems likely that the method used here would also work in that setting.

showing that

On the other hand, by

. (We note that the references
[

; however, the truth of Proposition

, namely that a certain map of

We now show how the desired statement follows from (

. We have shown that there is an isomorphism

Since the embedding of the category of

∎

We can use a similar point of view to construct pencils of quadrics over

from suitable sheaves on

. Suppose given a coherent sheaf

on

•

\mathcal{M}_{\eta}

.

•

There is an isomorphism
\mathcal{M}\cong\mathcal{H}\!\mathit{om}_{\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}(n-3))

•

Proposition 2.5
.

With assumptions as above, let

. Then:

1.

M
is a finite
R_{f}
-module,
A

2.

By passage to global sections, we obtain an

. Fix an

, and let

given by composition with
\mathrm{ev}_{x}

Proof.

We take each point in turn. Since

is projective,

is a finite
R_{f}
-module. Since

is a finite étale

is free of rank 1 over
R_{f}\otimes_{A}K
. We must check that

is
A

A
is a DVR with uniformizer
\pi
. We are also free to make an étale localisation on

, so can assume that there is a primitive homogeneous linear polynomial

such that
f(s,1)\neq 0
. Consider the short exact sequence of sheaves on

where

(viewed as a sheaf on

, isomorphic to
\Spec A
. This short exact sequence remains exact after pullback along

(because

has no

-torsion). After making this pullback and applying the functor
\Hom_{\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}}(\mathcal{M},-)
, we get an exact sequence

0\to\Hom_{\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}(n-3))\to\Hom_{\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}(n-2))\to\Hom_{\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}}(\mathcal{M},i^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{a}).

(2.4)

Applying
our hypotheses on
\mathcal{M}
and Proposition
2.4
, we see in particular that we have an embedding

. The module
\Hom_{A}(M,A)
is certainly

is itself
A
-torsion-free.

To prove the second part, we proceed in stages. Let

. We will first show that

and

cut out the same locus in
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}
, using a calculation over the algebraic closure. This implies that

and

are equal up to multiple by some element of

. To show equality, we will then be free to localise on

and assume that

is a DVR of uniformizer
\pi
. We will then show the existence of a pair

such that

and

have the same (finite)

and

to be equal up to multiplication by units.

It follows from Lemma
2.3
that if

, we write
\varphi:M\times M\to I_{f}
for the

-bilinear pairing, and

for the matrix of the symmetric

We now show that

divides

. This can be checked after base extension to the algebraic closure of

. Suppose that
a(x,y)
a linear form dividing

. Then multiplication by

sends

to a codimension 1 subspace of

. Using Lemma
2.3
, we see that

. Since

was an arbitrary linear factor, this shows that

Now we return to the case of general

, and must show that

are in fact equal up to multiplication by elements of
A^{\times}
. After localisation, we can assume that
A
is a DVR of uniformizer
\pi
and with infinite residue field

. Let us write

where

and

is primitive. We first treat the case where

, i.e.

is itself primitive. In this case, we can choose a primitive linear form

such that
\overline{a}
does not divide

in

(where overline denotes reduction modulo

); equivalently,

. Forming the exact sequence (
2.4
), we get a short exact sequence

0\to M\to\Hom_{A}(M,A)\to\Hom_{\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}}(\mathcal{M},i^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{a})

The group
\Hom_{\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}}(\mathcal{M},i^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{a})
is in fact

, because our choice of

means that

(in other words, the section

). In particular,

and we’re done in this case.

We now suppose that

contains

is non-zero. Since we assume that

is free for each generic point

of

, there exists an open subscheme

of

, supported over

, and over which

is free of rank 1. Let us choose

so that

and such that the support of

. In this case, we again form the exact sequence (

To justify the exactness on the right, it is enough to show that

. The sheaf
\mathcal{E}\mathit{xt}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}(n-3))

(note that

is reflexive because there is an isomorphism

).
Therefore

\Ext^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}(n-3))\cong H^{1}(S_{f},\mathcal{H}\!\mathit{om}_{\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}(n-3)))\cong H^{1}(S_{f},\mathcal{M}),

By construction,
\Hom_{\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}}(\mathcal{M},i^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{a})
is isomorphic as

at the point in

vanishes; in other words, to

.
By Lemma

, the morphism

in the above exact sequence is the one arising from

, so we have shown that

∎

3
The action of
\SL_{n}
on pairs of symmetric matrices

3.1
Basic definitions

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer and let
W
be the free
\mathbb{Z}
-module of rank
n
with basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
.
Let
\Sym_{2}(W^{\vee})
be the free
\mathbb{Z}
-module of symmetric bilinear forms
(-,-)\colon W\times W\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
.
Given such a form
(-,-)
, its Gram matrix with respect to the basis
\{e_{i}\}
is the matrix
A=((e_{i},e_{j}))_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
.
We use this to view
\Sym_{2}(W^{\vee})
as the
\mathbb{Z}
-module of symmetric matrices
A\in\Sym_{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})\subset\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Conversely, given a symmetric matrix
A\in\Sym_{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
, write
(-,-)_{A}
for the corresponding symmetric bilinear form on
W
.

The group
\GL(W)
and its subgroup
\SL(W)
act on
W
and hence by functoriality on
\Sym_{2}(W^{\vee})=\Sym_{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
.
This action has the property that
(v,w)_{g\cdot A}=(g^{-1}v,g^{-1}w)_{A}
for all
v,w\in V
,
A\in\Sym_{2}(W^{\vee})
and
g\in\GL(W)
. In terms of matrices, we have the formula
g\cdot A=g^{-t}Ag^{-1}
.
The group
\SL(W)
acts on
V
via
g\cdot(A,B)=(g^{-t}Ag^{-1},g^{-t}Bg^{-1})
.
The same formulae define actions of the group
\SL_{n}(R)
on the
R
-modules
\Sym_{2}((W\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R)^{\vee})=\Sym_{2}(R)
and
V(R)=\Sym_{2}((W\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R)^{\vee})\oplus\Sym_{2}((W\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R)^{\vee})
, for any ring
R
.
(Our formula for the
\SL(W)
-action on
V
differs from
[
5
,
8
]
, where they consider the action defined by
g\cdot(A,B)=(gAg^{t},gBg^{t})
, which coincides with our action after replacing
g
by its inverse transpose.
Since the orbits over any ring are the same for both actions, this difference is harmless.)

If
R
is a ring and
A\in\Sym_{2}(R^{n})
, its discriminant is by definition
\disc(A)=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\det(A)
.
We define the invariant binary form of
(A,B)\in V(R)
to be

\displaystyle f_{A,B}(x,y)=\disc(Ax-By)=f_{0}x^{n}+f_{1}x^{n-1}y+\cdots+f_{n}y^{n}\in R[x,y].

The coefficients of the invariant binary form are invariant polynomials of degree
n
in the coefficients of
(A,B)
for the
\SL_{n}(R)
-action, and freely generate the full ring of invariants when
R=\mathbb{Z}
.
If
f(x,y)\in R[x,y]
is a binary form of degree
n
, write
V_{f}(R)
for the
\SL_{n}(R)
-stable subset of
(A,B)\in V(R)
satisfying
f_{A,B}=f
.

The discriminant
\Delta(f)
of a binary form
f=f_{0}x^{n}+\cdots+f_{n}y^{n}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
2n-2
in
f_{0},\dots,f_{n}
.
It is uniquely characterized by the property that if
R=K
is an algebraically closed field and
f=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\alpha_{i}x-\beta_{i}y)
for some
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\in K
, then

\displaystyle\Delta(f)=\prod_{i<j}(\alpha_{i}\beta_{j}-\alpha_{j}\beta_{i})^{2}.

If
(A,B)\in V(R)
we set
\Delta(A,B)=\Delta(f_{A,B})
.

3.2
Description of rational orbits

Let
K
be a field of characteristic zero and
f(x,y)=f_{0}x^{n}+\cdots+f_{n}y^{n}\in K[x,y]
a binary form of nonzero discriminant. Let
S_{f}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}
be the zero locus of
f
,
L_{f}=H^{0}(S_{f},\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}})
, an étale
K
-algebra of rank
n
, and define
I_{f}=H^{0}(S_{f},\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}(n-3))
,
J_{f}=H^{0}(S_{f},\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}(n-2))
, as in §
2
. Then
I_{f},J_{f}
are free
L_{f}
-modules of rank 1, although they do not have distinguished generators. We write
I_{f}^{\times}\subset I_{f}
for the set of elements which generate
I_{f}
as an
L_{f}
-module; then
I_{f}^{\times}
receives an action of
L_{f}^{\times}
. We define
J_{f}^{\times}
similarly.

Definition 3.1
.

We call an
f
-module a tuple
(M,\varphi,e)
, where
M
is a free
L_{f}
-module of rank 1,
\varphi:M\otimes_{L_{f}}M\to I_{f}
is an isomorphism of
L_{f}
-modules, and
e\in\wedge^{n}_{K}M
is a non-zero element, satisfying the following property: let
\mathcal{B}=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{n}\}
be any
K
-basis of
L_{f}
such that
b_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge b_{n}=e
, and let
A_{x},A_{y}
be the matrices of the symmetric
K
-bilinear forms
\mathrm{ev}_{x}\circ\varphi
,
\mathrm{ev}_{y}\circ\varphi
on
M
. Then
\disc(xA_{y}-yA_{x})=f(x,y)
.

Henceforth we will write
\disc_{e}
to denote discriminant with respect to some basis
\mathcal{B}=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{n}\}
such that
b_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge b_{n}=e
. Thus we can express the condition defining an
f
-module in the form
\disc_{e}(x\mathrm{ev}_{y}\circ\varphi-y\mathrm{ev}_{x}\circ\varphi)=f(x,y)
.

An isomorphism
(M,\varphi,e)\to(M^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime},e^{\prime})
of

-modules is an
L_{f}
-linear isomorphism

intertwining the other data. We call
(A_{y},A_{x})
the pair associated to the
f
-module

and basis

.

Proposition 3.2
.

The map
(M,\varphi,e)\mapsto(A_{y},A_{x})
determines a bijection between the following two sets:

1.

The set of isomorphism classes of
f
-modules.

2.

The set of orbits
\SL_{n}(K)\backslash V_{f}(K)
.

Proof.

It is clear that the map
(1)\to(2)
is well-defined. To show that it is bijective, we construct an inverse. Consider a pair
(A,B)\in V_{f}(K)
. We construct a structure
(M,\varphi,e)
of
f
-module on

, together with basis
\mathcal{B}=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{n}\}
with
b_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge b_{n}=e
, such that
(A,B)
is the pair associated to
(M,\varphi,e)
and choice of basis
\mathcal{B}
.

Let
(s,-r)\in K^{2}
be such that
sf(s,-r)\neq 0
, and let
a(x,y)=rx+sy
. We set
A_{a}=sA+rB
. Then
\det A_{a}\neq 0
, and we give

the unique

-module structure for which the element

(more precisely, the image of

) acts via the matrix

. We can then check the following:

•

The element

acts on
M
via the matrix

. (Use the relation

in

.)

•

The induced

-module structure on
M
is independent of the choice of
a
. (If
b
is another choice of linear form, use the relation
x/b=(b/a)^{-1}(x/a)
in

and check that the two possible actions of

We next define the pairing
\varphi
. Equivalently, we must define an isomorphism

of
L_{f}
-modules. By duality (Proposition
2.4
), there is a canonical isomorphism
\Hom_{L_{f}}(M,J_{f})\cong\Hom_{K}(M,K)
, given by composition with

. Multiplication by

defines an isomorphism
\times a:I_{f}\to J_{f}
. The pairing given by the symmetric matrix

gives an

-linear map
\psi_{a}:M\to\Hom_{K}(M,K)\cong\Hom_{L_{f}}(M,J_{f})

\varphi=(\times a)^{-1}\circ\psi_{a}

•

The map
\varphi
is an isomorphism of

-modules, which is independent of the choice of

. (If

is another choice of linear form, we must check that

•

The matrices of

and

To complete our construction, we can therefore take

. It is clear that this gives a well-defined map

(i.e. well-defined at the level of equivalence classes), which is inverse to the map

If

is an

. Multiplication by

of

, where we define pairs

Proposition 3.3
.

The map just defined has the following properties:

1.

It is injective.

2.

Let
(\alpha,z)\in I_{f}^{\times}\times(\wedge^{n}_{K}L_{f}-\{0\})
. Then the equivalence class of
(\alpha,z)
lies in the image if and only if
\disc_{z}(x(\mathrm{ev}_{y}\circ\varphi_{\alpha})-y(\mathrm{ev}_{x}\circ\varphi_{\alpha}))=f(x,y)
, where
\varphi_{\alpha}:L_{f}\otimes_{L_{f}}L_{f}\to I_{f}
is defined by
\varphi_{\alpha}(a\otimes b)=ab\alpha
.

Proof.

Suppose that

-modules
(M,\varphi,e)
and
(M^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime},e^{\prime})
have the same image. It follows that we can choose basis elements

for

respectively such that
\varphi(u\otimes u)=\varphi^{\prime}(u^{\prime}\otimes u^{\prime})
and
u^{-1}_{\ast}(e)=(u^{\prime})^{-1}_{\ast}(e^{\prime})
. Then the isomorphism

of

-modules sending

to

intertwines the other structures, so is an isomorphism of

-modules.

The characterisation of the image is essentially the definition of an

-module.
∎

We remark that if
M
is a free

-module of rank 1,
\varphi:M\otimes_{L_{f}}M\to I_{f}
is an isomorphism, and

is non-zero, then
\disc_{e}(x(\mathrm{ev}_{y}\circ\varphi)-y(\mathrm{ev}_{x}\circ\varphi))
is a

-multiple of

. To check equality, it is therefore enough to check that

\disc_{e}(s(\mathrm{ev}_{y}\circ\varphi)+r(\mathrm{ev}_{x}\circ\varphi))=\disc_{e}(\mathrm{ev}_{rx+sy}\circ\varphi)=f(s,-r)

for some

such that
f(s,-r)\neq 0
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Suppose that
K
is algebraically closed. Then the set
\SL_{n}(K)\backslash V_{f}(K)
has exactly one element.

Proof.

Since
L_{f}
is an étale
K
-algebra and
K
algebraically closed,
L_{f}\simeq K\times\cdots\times K
, every element of
L_{f}
is a square and any two elements of
I_{f}^{\times}
differ by a square in
L_{f}^{\times}
.
Moreover, for every
\alpha\in I_{f}^{\times}
there exists
e\in(\wedge^{n}_{K}L_{f}-\{0\})
such that
(L_{f},\varphi_{\alpha},e)
is an
f
-module, in the notation of Proposition
3.3
.
If
e^{\prime}\in(\wedge^{n}_{K}L_{f}-\{0\})
is such that
(L_{f},\varphi_{\alpha},e^{\prime})
is an
f
-module, then
e^{\prime}=\pm e
.
Therefore every pair
(\alpha^{\prime},z^{\prime})\in I_{f}^{\times}\times(\wedge^{n}_{K}L_{f}-\{0\})
is equivalent to the fixed pair
(\alpha,e)
.
∎

A common special case is when
f_{0}\neq 0
(so in particular
f(1,0)\neq 0
). Then various objects become more explicit, because
S_{f}
is contained in the distinguished affine open

. Taking

, we find that
L_{f}=K[X]/(f(X,1))
. Thus
L_{f}
has the power basis
1,X,\dots,X^{n-1}
, while

have
L_{f}
-basis the elements

, respectively.

Lemma 3.5
.

With notation as in the previous paragraph, the map
\zeta:J_{f}\to K
is equal to
f_{0}^{-1}(x^{n-1}y^{-1})^{\ast}
, where
(y^{n-2})^{\ast},(xy^{n-3})^{\ast},\dots,(x^{n-1}y^{-1})^{\ast}
is the
K
-basis of
\Hom_{K}(J_{f},K)
dual to the
K
-basis
y^{n-2}
,
xy^{n-3},\dots,x^{n-1}y^{-1}
of
J_{f}
.

Proof.

We compute using the Cech cohomology of the affine covering
U_{0}=D^{+}(y)
,
U_{1}=D^{+}(x)
of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}
.
Our hypothesis
f_{0}\neq 0
is equivalent to the condition
S_{f}\subset D(y)
. Thus the map

H^{0}(S_{f},\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}(n-2))\to H^{0}(U_{0},\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}(n-2))=K[X]\cdot y^{n-2}/K[X]\cdot y^{-2}f(x,y)=K[X]\cdot y^{n-2}/f(X,1)K[X]\cdot y^{n-2}

is an isomorphism. This shows that the elements
y^{n-2},xy^{n-3},\dots,x^{n-1}y^{-1}\in K[X]\cdot y^{n-2}
project to a
K
-basis of
J_{f}
. Since
(xy)^{-1}f(x,y)\in H^{0}(U_{0}\cap U_{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}}(n-2))
maps to
0
in
H^{0}(U_{0}\cap U_{1},\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}(n-2))
, the Cech cocycle in

H^{0}(U_{0},\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}(n-2))\oplus H^{0}(U_{1},\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}(n-2))

corresponding to
x^{n-1}y^{-1}
is
(x^{n-1}y^{-1},(x^{n-1}y^{-1}-f_{0}^{-1}(xy)^{-1}f(x,y)))
.

Recall that
\zeta
is defined as the composite of the boundary map
J_{f}=H^{0}(S_{f},\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}(n-2))\to H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K},\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}(-2))
associated to the short exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}}(-2)\overset{\times f}{\to}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}}(n-2)\to\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}(n-2)\to 0

with the isomorphism

afforded by Serre duality. In particular, it vanishes on the codimension 1 subspace
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}}(n-2))
of

. To prove the lemma, we need to show that

takes the value
f_{0}^{-1}
on

.

The image of
x^{n-1}y^{-1}
under the boundary homomorphism is represented by the cocycle

(x^{n-1}y^{-1}-(x^{n-1}y^{-1}-f_{0}^{-1}(xy)^{-1}f(x,y)))/f(x,y)=f_{0}^{-1}(xy)^{-1}\in H^{0}(U_{0}\cap U_{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}}(-2)).

By definition, the map
\zeta
takes
(xy)^{-1}
to
1
, so it follows that
\zeta(x^{n-1}y^{-1})=f_{0}^{-1}
, as claimed.
∎

In this way, we recover the description of the set
\SL_{n}(K)\backslash V_{f}(K)
given in the special case

in
[

, Theorem 7]
:

Corollary 3.6
.

Suppose that
f_{0}\neq 0
. Then the following three sets are in bijection:

1.

The set of orbits
\SL_{n}(K)\backslash V_{f}(K)
.

2.

The set of isomorphism classes of
f
-modules
(M,\varphi,e)
.

3.

The set of equivalence classes of pairs
(\alpha,z)\in L_{f}^{\times}\times K^{\times}
satisfying
z^{2}\mathbf{N}_{L_{f}/K}(\alpha)=f_{0}^{n+1}
, where we say
(\alpha,z)
,
(\alpha^{\prime},z^{\prime})
are equivalent if there exists
\beta\in L_{f}^{\times}
such that
\alpha^{\prime}=\beta^{2}\alpha
and
z^{\prime}=\mathbf{N}_{L_{f}/K}(\beta)^{-1}z
.

Proof.

Let
e=1\wedge X\wedge\dots\wedge X^{n-1}\in\wedge^{n}_{K}L_{f}
. We define a bijection
L_{f}^{\times}\times K^{\times}\to I_{f}^{\times}\times(\wedge^{n}_{K}L_{f}-\{0\})
by the formula

(\alpha,z)\mapsto(\alpha y^{n-3},ze).

This descends to a bijection between the corresponding equivalence classes. If
\alpha\in L_{f}^{\times}
, let

be the map
u\otimes v\mapsto\alpha uvy^{n-3}
.

What we need to check is that the condition
\disc_{ze}(\mathrm{ev}_{y}\circ\varphi_{\alpha})=f_{0}
arising from Proposition
3.3
is equivalent to the condition

appearing in the statement of Corollary

. This is a computation:

\disc_{ze}(\mathrm{ev}_{y}\circ\varphi_{\alpha})=z^{2}\disc_{e}(\zeta(\alpha uvy^{n-2}))=z^{2}\mathbf{N}_{L_{f}/K}(\alpha)\disc_{e}(\zeta(uvy^{n-2})),

where we write e.g.
\zeta(uvy^{n-2})
for the
K
-bilinear form

,
(u,v)\mapsto\zeta(uvy^{n-2})
. Using Lemma

, we see that

, where

is the element of the dual basis of the

of

corresponding to

. We have

, hence
\disc\zeta(uvy^{n-2})=f_{0}^{-n}
, and finally

\disc_{ze}(\mathrm{ev}_{y}\circ\varphi_{\alpha})=z^{2}\mathbf{N}_{L_{f}/K}(\alpha)f_{0}^{-n}.

This is what we needed.
∎

3.3
Integral orbits

We will use the point of view developed in §
3.2
to construct elements of
\SL_{n}(K)\backslash V_{f}(K)
from
K
-rational divisors on hyperelliptic curves in §
4
below. We conclude with a lemma that will be useful in extending this construction to produce integral orbits. Since we now want to consider integral structures, let us reset our notation. Let
A
be a PID with fraction field
K
of characteristic 0, and let
f(x,y)=f_{0}x^{n}+\dots+f_{n}y^{n}\in A[x,y]
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
n
and non-zero discriminant
\Delta(f)\in A
. Let
S_{f}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{A}
denote the zero locus of
f
, and let
S_{f,K}
denote its generic fibre. Similarly, let
I_{f}=H^{0}(S_{f},\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}(n-3))
,
I_{f,K}=I_{f}\otimes_{A}K
its generic fibre.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
(M_{K},\varphi_{K},e)
be an
f
-module, and suppose given an
A
-lattice
M\leq M_{K}
satisfying the following conditions:

1.

The restriction
\varphi
of the map
\varphi_{K}:M_{K}\times M_{K}\to I_{f,K}
to
M\times M
takes values in
I_{f}
.

2.

There exists an
A
-basis
\mathcal{B}
of
M
such that
\disc_{\mathcal{B}}(x(\mathrm{ev}_{y}\circ\varphi)-y(\mathrm{ev}_{x}\circ\varphi))=uf(x,y)
for some
u\in A^{\times}
.

Then:

1.

There exists an
A
-basis
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\{b_{1}^{\prime},\dots,b_{n}^{\prime}\}
of
M
such that
e=b_{1}^{\prime}\wedge\dots\wedge b_{n}^{\prime}
.

2.

If
A_{y},A_{x}
are the matrices of
\mathrm{ev}_{y}\circ\varphi
,
\mathrm{ev}_{x}\circ\varphi
with respect to the basis
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
, then
(A_{y},A_{x})
is a representative in
V_{f}(A)
for the
\SL_{n}(K)
-orbit corresponding to the
f
-module
(M_{K},\varphi_{K},e)
under Proposition
3.2
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\{c_{1},\dots,c_{n}\}
be a
K
-basis of
M_{K}
such that
c_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge c_{n}=e
, so that

\disc_{\mathcal{C}}(x(\mathrm{ev}_{y}\circ\varphi)-y(\mathrm{ev}_{x}\circ\varphi))=f(x,y).

If
g\in\GL_{n}(K)
is the change of basis matrix for
\mathcal{B}
,
\mathcal{C}
, then
\disc_{\mathcal{B}}=\det(g)^{2}\disc_{\mathcal{C}}
, hence

, and so

for some

. It follows that we can choose e.g.

,

if

.
∎

3.4
Reduction theory

We define a reduction covariant for the representation
V(\mathbb{Z})
of
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
, a key technical tool in our reduction theory.
To this end, we start with a slightly more general set-up.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional
\mathbb{R}
-vector space equipped with two symmetric bilinear forms
(-,-)_{1},(-,-)_{2}\colon M\times M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
Suppose that there exists a basis of
M
such that the Gram matrices
A,B
of
(-,-)_{1},(-,-)_{2}
have the property that
\det(Ax-By)\in\mathbb{R}[x,y]
has nonzero discriminant.
We explain how to associate to this data a canonical positive definite inner product
H
on
M
.

Lemma 3.8
.

There exists a
\mathbb{C}
-basis
b_{1},\dots,b_{n}
of
M\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
such that the Gram matrices of
(-,-)_{1},(-,-)_{2}
are both diagonal.
Moreover, such a basis is unique up to reordering and rescaling.

Proof.

It suffices to prove these claims when
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, in which case we may view the pair
(-,-)_{1},(-,-)_{2}
as an element
(A,B)\in V(\mathbb{R})
with invariant binary form
f(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}[x,y]
of nonzero discriminant.
By Proposition
3.2
and its proof,
M
is part of an
f
-module
(M,\varphi,e)
with
\mathrm{ev}_{y}\circ\varphi=A
and
\mathrm{ev}_{x}\circ\varphi=B
.
Let
v\in M
be an
L_{f}
-module generator.
Let
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
be the idempotents corresponding to a decomposition
L_{f}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathbb{C}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{C}
.
Then
e_{1}\cdot v,\dots,e_{n}\cdot v
is a
\mathbb{C}
-basis of
M\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
and
\varphi(e_{i}\cdot v,e_{j}\cdot v)=e_{i}e_{j}\varphi(v,v)=0
if
i\neq j
, so the forms
A,B
are both diagonal in this basis, proving existence.
To prove uniqueness (up to reordering and rescaling), let
(s,-r)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
sf(s,-r)\neq 0
, let
a(x,y)=rx+sy
and let
A_{a}=sA+rB
.
Then
\det(A_{a})\neq 0
, and any basis in which
A,B
are diagonal has the property that
A_{a}^{-1}B
is also diagonal.
Since
\disc(f)\neq 0
,
A_{a}^{-1}B
is regular semisimple, hence such a basis is unique up to reordering and rescaling.
∎

Construction 3.9
.

Let
b_{1},\dots,b_{n}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-basis of
M\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
for which the Gram matrices of
(-,-)_{1},(-,-)_{2}
are both diagonal.
The assignment

\displaystyle(b_{i},b_{j})_{H}=\begin{cases}\max(|(b_{i},b_{i})_{1},|(b_{i},b_{i})|_{2})&\text{ if }i=j,\\
0&\text{ if }i\neq j,\end{cases}

extends to a Hermitian form on
M\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
.
Let
H
be the restriction of this Hermitian form to
M
.

Proposition 3.10
.

In the above construction,
H
is independent of the choice of
b_{1},\dots,b_{n}
, real valued and positive definite.
Moreover, if
(M^{\prime},(-,-)_{1}^{\prime},(-,-)_{2}^{\prime})
is another such triple and
\psi\colon(M,(-,-)_{1},(-,-)_{2})\rightarrow(M^{\prime},(-,-)_{1}^{\prime},(-,-)_{2}^{\prime})
is an isomorphism of vector spaces intertwining the respective forms, then
\psi
intertwines
H
and
H^{\prime}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.8
, the basis
b_{1},\dots,b_{n}
is unique up to reordering and rescaling, and these operations do not affect
H
.
Choosing the basis so that
b_{1},\dots,b_{k}\in M
and
b_{k+1},\dots,b_{n}
come in complex conjugate pairs shows that
H
is real valued.
To show that
H
is positive definite, we must show that
\max(|(b_{i},b_{i})_{1}|,|(b_{i},b_{i})_{2}|)>0
for each
i=1,\dots,n
.
This is true, since the nonzero discriminant condition implies (
[
10
, Lemma 6.4(b)]
) that either
(b_{i},b_{i})_{1}
or
(b_{i},b_{i})_{2}
is nonzero for each
i
.
The claim about compatibility with isomorphisms follows from the fact that the definition of
H
is independent of any additional choices.
∎

Applying this construction to our framed vector space
W_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
fixed in §
3.1
, we obtain an inner product
H_{A,B}
on
W_{\mathbb{R}}
associated to any pair
(A,B)\in V(\mathbb{R})
of nonzero discriminant.
Let
X
denote the set of equivalence classes of inner products on
W_{\mathbb{R}}
, where two inner products
H
,
H^{\prime}
are defined to be equivalent when one is an
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-multiple of the other.
Write
[H]\in X
for the image of an inner product
H
on
W_{\mathbb{R}}
.
The assignment
(A,B)\rightarrow[H_{A,B}]
defines a map

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\colon V(\mathbb{R})^{\Delta\neq 0}\rightarrow X,

(3.1)

which we call the reduction covariant.
The group
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
acts on
X
via the formula
(v,w)_{g\cdot H}=(g^{-1}v,g^{-1}w)_{H}
or in terms of matrices, via the formula
g\cdot H=g^{-t}Hg^{-1}
.
Let
H_{0}
be the standard inner product on
W_{\mathbb{R}}
with Gram matrix the identity matrix.
The map
g\mapsto g\cdot H_{0}
induces an
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
-equivariant bijection
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})/\SO_{n}(\mathbb{R})\simeq X
.
The reduction covariant
\mathcal{R}
is
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
-equivariant by the last sentence of Proposition
3.10
, hence by passage to the quotient we obtain a map

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\colon\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash V(\mathbb{Z})^{\Delta\neq 0}\rightarrow\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X.

(3.2)

We record a computation of
\det H_{A,B}
with respect to the standard basis of
W_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
(A,B)\in V(\mathbb{R})
with
\Delta(A,B)\neq 0
and write

\displaystyle f_{A,B}=c\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\omega_{i}y)\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\eta_{j}x-y),

where
c,\omega_{i},\eta_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
and
|\omega_{i}|,|\eta_{j}|\leq 1
for all
i,j
.

Lemma 3.11
.

In the above notation,
\det(H_{A,B})=|c|
.

Proof.

Let
c_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
be an element such that
c_{0}^{n}=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}c
and consider the pair of diagonal matrices
(A_{0},B_{0})\in V(\mathbb{C})
, where
A_{0}=(c_{0},\dots,c_{0},c_{0}\eta_{1},\dots,c_{0}\eta_{k})
and
B_{0}=(c_{0}\omega_{1},\dots,c_{0}\omega_{r},c_{0},\dots,c_{0})
.
A computation shows that
f_{A_{0},B_{0}}=f_{A,B}
.
By Corollary
3.4
, there exists a
g\in\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
such that
g\cdot(A,B)=(A_{0},B_{0})
.
By definition of the reduction covariant,
g^{-t}H_{A,B}(\bar{g})^{-1}
is a diagonal matrix, with
i
th diagonal entry equal to the maximum of the absolute values of the
i
th diagonal entries of
A_{0}
and
B_{0}
. Therefore
g^{-t}H_{A,B}(\bar{g})^{-1}
is the diagonal matrix
(|c_{0}|,\dots,|c_{0}|)
, so
\det(H_{A,B})=\det(g^{-t}H_{A,B}(\bar{g})^{-1})=|c_{0}|^{n}=|c|
.
∎

We remark that the reduction covariant given above is not unique. For example, for either choice of
k=1,2
we could carry out the analogue of Construction
3.9
with the formula

\displaystyle(b_{i},b_{j})_{H_{k}}=\begin{cases}|(b_{i},b_{i})_{k}|&\text{ if }i=j,\\
0&\text{ if }i\neq j,\end{cases}

to obtain a different
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
-equivariant reduction covariant
\mathcal{R}_{k}:V(\mathbb{R})^{\Delta\neq 0}\to X
. Computation suggests that carrying out reduction theory using
\mathcal{R}_{1}
will make the coefficients of
A
as small as possible (perhaps at the cost of making the coefficients of
B
large), and vice versa for
\mathcal{R}_{2}
, while performing reduction theory with
\mathcal{R}
will tend to reduce the coefficients of both
A
and
B
. Our choice of
\mathcal{R}
is made here so that we can prove Lemma
5.6
.

4
Construction of rational and integral orbits

We now connect the representation of
\SL_{n}
on
V
to divisors on hyperelliptic curves.
Let
A
be a PID of fraction field
K
of characteristic 0, let
g\geq 1
, and let
n=2g+2
. Let

f(x,y)=f_{0}x^{n}+f_{1}x^{n-1}y+\dots+f_{n}y^{n}\in A[x,y]

be a homogeneous form of degree
n
such that the discriminant
\Delta(f)\in A
is nonzero. Let
\pi:X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{A}
denote the double cover described by the equation
z^{2}=f(x,y)
, and
\iota:X\to X
the associated involution. Then
X_{K}
is a smooth hyperelliptic curve. We write
j:S_{f}\hookrightarrow X
for the closed subscheme defined by the equation
z=0
. As suggested by the notation,
S_{f}
may be identified with the subscheme of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{A}
defined by the vanishing of
f\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{A},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{A}}(n))
, studied in §
2
. We set
R_{f}=H^{0}(S_{f},\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}})
and
L_{f}=R_{f}\otimes_{A}K
.

Let
D_{K}\leq X_{K}
be an effective divisor of degree
2g-1
, not intersecting
S_{f,K}
. We can write
D_{\overline{K}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\alpha_{i}:1:\beta_{i})+\sum_{i=k+1}^{2g-1}(1:0:\beta_{i})
for some numbers
\beta_{i}\in\overline{K}
. We set
w=\prod_{i=1}^{2g-1}\beta_{i}\in K
.

Let
U(x,y)=u_{0}x^{2g-1}+\dots+u_{2g-1}y^{2g-1}\in A[x,y]
be a primitive form of degree
2g-1
vanishing precisely at the points of
\pi(D_{\overline{K}})
(with multiplicity). Then
U
is determined up to multiplication by
A^{\times}
. In this section, we will prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
f_{0}\neq 0
, let
X=x/y
, and let
\alpha=U(X,1)\text{ mod }f(X,1)\in L_{f}=R_{f}\otimes_{A}K
. Let
w=\prod_{i=1}^{2g-1}\beta_{i}
. Then:

1.

The pair
(\alpha,u_{k}^{-(g+1)}f_{0}^{n-1}w^{-1})
corresponds, under the bijection of Corollary
3.6
, to an orbit in
\SL_{n}(K)\backslash V_{f}(K)
.

2.

Furthermore, this orbit intersects
V_{f}(A)
(i.e. has an integral representative).

After the proof, we will give a more precise version of Theorem
4.1
(see Theorem
4.5
), which is what we will use in our applications. The rational orbit given by Theorem
4.1
could depend on the choice of
U
, although its orbit under the group
(\SL_{n}/\mu_{2})(K)
(through which the action of
\SL_{n}(K)
factors) is insensitive to this choice.

We begin by proving the first part of Theorem
4.1
, which is a computation. Let
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n}\in\overline{K}
be the roots of
f(X,1)
. Then
U(X,1)=u_{k}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(X-\alpha_{i})
and
f(X,1)=f_{0}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(X-\omega_{j})
. Since
f_{0}\neq 0
, we have
w\in K^{\times}
, and
w^{2}=f_{0}^{n-3-k}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\beta_{i}^{2}
.

We have

\mathbf{N}_{L_{f}/K}(\alpha)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}U(\omega_{j},1)=u_{k}^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\omega_{j}-\alpha_{i})\\
=u_{k}^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\alpha_{i}-\omega_{j})=u_{k}^{n}f_{0}^{-k}\prod_{i=1}^{k}f(\alpha_{i},1)=u_{k}^{n}f_{0}^{-k}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\beta_{i}^{2}=u_{k}^{n}w^{2}f_{0}^{3-n}.

It follows that
(u_{k}^{-(g+1)}w^{-1}f_{0}^{n-1})^{2}\mathbf{N}_{L_{f}/K}(\alpha)=f_{0}^{n+1}
. Looking at the statement of Corollary
3.6
, we see that we have indeed obtained an orbit in
\SL_{n}(K)\backslash V_{f}(K)
. In order to show that this orbit admits an integral representative, we first give a geometric construction for its corresponding
f
-module (in the sense of §
3.2
), in a way similar to
[
29
,
19
]
. Let
M_{K}=H^{0}(S_{f,K},j_{K}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}}(D_{K}))
. Then
M_{K}
is a free
L_{f}
-module of rank 1. There is an isomorphism of sheaves

\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}}(D_{K})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}}}\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}}(\iota^{\ast}D_{K})\cong\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}}(2g-1)

(4.1)

given by

. After pullback along

, this gives (using the identity

) an isomorphism of sheaves

hence an isomorphism

M_{K}\cong\Hom_{L_{f}}(M_{K},I_{f}\otimes_{A}K)

of
L_{f}
-modules, which is determined up to
A^{\times}
-multiple by

(and determined completely by

and the choice of

). Let

denote the corresponding isomorphism. The first part of Theorem

says that

is an

is defined by the formula

and

is the image of the tautological section

under restriction. Indeed, our hypothesis that

and

do not intersect implies that

is an

-module generator for

.

We now proceed to construct an

. We will rely heavily on the fact that

is an integral local complete intersection of dimension 2. Continuing to take

to be an effective divisor in

of degree

, let us take

, a closed subscheme of

which is

2g-1
. Let

denote the intersection of the subscheme cut out by

with the finitely many fibres of

above points where

) the restriction functor

between the respective categories of reflexive coherent sheaves is an equivalence.
Consequently, there is a unique (up to unique isomorphism) reflexive coherent sheaf, that we call
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
, on

, whose restriction to

is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{V}(D_{V})
. Moreover, since
D_{V}+\iota^{\ast}D_{V}=(U)_{V}
as Cartier divisors, the isomorphism
\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}}(D_{K})\cong\mathcal{H}\!\mathit{om}_{\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}}}(\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}}(\iota^{\ast}D),\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}}(2g-1))
extends to an isomorphism

\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\cong\mathcal{H}\!\mathit{om}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(\iota^{\ast}D),\mathcal{O}_{X}(2g-1))

(4.2)

of reflexive coherent sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules (here we use that the sheaf Hom is itself reflexive, by

).

Let
\mathcal{M}=j^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
. We claim that

satisfies the hypotheses of Proposition
2.5
. Combining the result of this proposition with Lemma
3.7
will imply the truth of Theorem
4.1
. These hypotheses be checked locally on
\Spec A
, so we now assume that

is a DVR of uniformizer
\varpi
and residue field

.

Lemma 4.2
.

\mathcal{M}_{\eta}
is free of rank 1 at each generic point
\eta\in S_{f}
.

Proof.

By construction,
\mathcal{M}
is locally free of rank 1 after restriction to

. It suffices to check that the generic points of
S_{f}
all lie in
V
. Since

contains

, it certainly contains all generic points of
S_{f}
lying above the generic point of
\Spec A
.
S_{f}
has generic points lying above
\Spec k
if and only if

is not primitive, in which case there is a generic point corresponding to the closed subscheme
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
of

. The intersection

is finite (being contained in the zero set of

), so we see that we’re done in this case too.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

The isomorphism of Equation (
4.2
) determines, by pullback, an isomorphism

\mathcal{M}\cong\mathcal{H}\!\mathit{om}_{\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}(2g-1))

of sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}
-modules.

Proof.

We need to show that the natural map

j^{\ast}\mathcal{H}\!\mathit{om}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(\iota^{\ast}D),\mathcal{O}_{X}(2g-1))\to\mathcal{H}\!\mathit{om}_{\mathcal{O}_{S_{f}}}(j^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{X}(\iota^{\ast}D),j^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{X}(2g-1))

is an isomorphism. We can work on stalks. Let
p\in S_{f}
, and let
u
be a local equation for
S_{f}
at
p
. Applying the functor
\Hom_{\mathcal{O}_{X,p}}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(\iota^{\ast}D)_{p},-)
to the short exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X,p}\overset{\times u}{\to}\mathcal{O}_{X,p}\to\mathcal{O}_{S_{f},p}\to 0,

we see that it is enough to know that
\Ext^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{X,p}}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(\iota^{\ast}D)_{p},\mathcal{O}_{X,p})=0
. This will follow from
[
17
, Corollary 1.3]
if we can check that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(\iota^{\ast}D)_{p}
is Cohen–Macaulay. However,
[
17
, Theorem 1.9]
shows that since
\mathcal{O}_{X}(\iota^{\ast}D)_{p}
is reflexive, it is

, hence Cohen–Macaulay.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

H^{1}(S_{f},\mathcal{M})=0
.

Proof.

If
f
is primitive, then
S_{f}
is affine, so there is nothing to prove. We therefore assume that
f(x,y)=\varpi^{m}g(x,y)
for some
m\geq 1
, where
g(x,y)\in A[x,y]
is primitive.
Taking in hand the short exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)(-(g+1))\overset{\times z}{\to}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\to\mathcal{M}\to 0,

we see that it is enough to show that
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))=0
.
By cohomology and base change, it is even enough to show that
H^{1}(X_{k},\mathcal{L})=0
, where
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)|_{X_{k}}
.
The curve
X_{k}
is the genus
g
‘hyperelliptic ribbon’
[
2
, §1]
given by the equation
z^{2}=0
, where
z\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{k}^{1}}(-(g+1))
. Writing
\pi:X_{k}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
for the double cover,
\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{L}
is a torsion free coherent sheaf, generically of rank 2, therefore a locally free sheaf of rank 2. (We justify the assertion that
\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{L}
is torsion-free. By
[
28
,
Lemma 0AUV
]
, it is equivalent to show that
(0)
is the unique associated prime of each stalk of
\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{L}
. By
[
28
,
Lemma 05DZ
]
, it is equivalent to show that the unique associated prime of each stalk of
\mathcal{L}
is the unique minimal prime. The sheaf
\mathcal{L}
is
S_{1}
, since
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
is
S_{2}
and
\varpi
is a non-zero divisor in each stalk of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
.
The desired property therefore follows from
[
28
,
Lemma 031Q
]
.) Since every vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
splits, we can find an isomorphism
\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{L}\cong\mathcal{O}(i)\oplus\mathcal{O}(j)
, where
i\leq j
and (by considering the Euler characteristic) we have
i+j=g-2
. We wish to show that
H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k},\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{L})=0
, or equivalently that
i\geq-1
.

Multiplication by
z
gives a morphism
\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}}(g+1)\otimes\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{L}
, which can be represented as a matrix

z=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}R&S\\
T&Q\end{array}\right)\in H^{0}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}}(g+1)&\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}}(i-j+g+1)\\
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}}(j-i+g+1)&\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}}(g+1)\end{array}\right).

Since
i+j=g-2
, we have
i-j+g+1=2i+3
.

Suppose for contradiction that
i\leq-2
. Then
2i+3<0
, so
S=0
. We can then calculate

z^{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}R^{2}&0\\
(R+Q)T&Q^{2}\end{array}\right)=0,

hence
R=Q=0
. Since
H^{0}(X_{k},\mathcal{L})=H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k},0\oplus\mathcal{O}(j))
, the matrix equality
z=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
T&0\end{pmatrix}
shows that

annihilates
H^{0}(X_{k},\mathcal{L})
.

Let
\overline{\eta}
denote the generic point of
X_{k}
. Then
\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\eta}}
is free of rank 1 over
\mathcal{O}_{X_{k},\overline{\eta}}
(since
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
is locally free in a neighbourhood of
\overline{\eta}
), and generated by the image of the global section
1\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))
. Since
\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\eta}}
is not annihilated by

, this is a contradiction.
∎

We have now completed the proof of Theorem

, and have in fact proved the following more precise statement:

.

With assumptions and notation as above, there exists an
A

1.

The matrices of the symmetric bilinear forms

,
\mathrm{ev}_{x}\circ\varphi_{K}

3.

Let
\overline{1}\in M

in

is saturated, i.e. generates a saturated

Proof.

Only the last part remains to be proved. We can again localise and assume that

is a DVR of uniformizer
\varpi
, and must show that

. We have constructed a pairing

∎

Let us now take

and work out the consequences of all the theory developed so far. Fix a polynomial

vanishing precisely at the points of

Let
M_{\mathbb{Q}}=H^{0}(S_{f,\mathbb{Q}},j_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}}(D_{\mathbb{Q}}))

\varphi_{\mathbb{Q}}:M_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{L_{f}}M_{\mathbb{Q}}\to I_{f,\mathbb{Q}}

-modules, satisfying

is the restriction of the tautological section

The following result is a consequence of Theorem

:

Proposition 4.6
.

There exists a
\mathbb{Z}
-lattice
M\leq M_{\mathbb{Q}}
with the following properties:

1.

There exists a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
M
with determinant

such that
(M_{\mathbb{Q}},\varphi_{\mathbb{Q}},e)
is an
f

\varphi(M\otimes M)\leq I_{f}

2.

\overline{1}\in M
is saturated.

The
f
-module structure on
M_{\mathbb{Q}}
determines two symmetric bilinear forms
\mathrm{ev}_{y}\circ\varphi
and
\mathrm{ev}_{x}\circ\varphi
on
M_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Associated with these forms is the reduction covariant
H
on
M_{\mathbb{R}}
from §

.
We now compute the norm of the vector
\overline{1}\in M
with respect to the reduction covariant. Let
X=x/y\in L_{f}
, and let
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
be the roots of

, which are nonzero by assumption.
Order them so that
|\omega_{1}|,\dots,|\omega_{r}|\leq 1
and
|\omega_{r+1}|,\dots,|\omega_{n}|>1

Write

for the partial derivative of

with respect to

similarly.

Proposition 4.7
.

We have

\displaystyle(\bar{1},\bar{1})_{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{|U(\omega_{i},1)|}{|f_{x}(\omega_{i},1)|}+\sum_{i=r+1}^{n}\frac{|U(1,\omega_{i}^{-1})|}{|f_{y}(1,\omega_{i}^{-1})|}.

(4.3)

Proof.

Let
e_{i}\in L_{f}\otimes\mathbb{C}
be the idempotent corresponding to the root

.
Since

is an

-module generator,

is a

-basis of

Moreover since the complexified form

is

-bilinear,
\varphi(e_{i}\bar{1},e_{j}\bar{1})=0

, hence the bilinear forms

\mathrm{ev}_{y}\circ\varphi
have diagonal Gram matrices with respect to the basis

By Construction

(\overline{1},\overline{1})_{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\max(|\mathrm{ev}_{y}(\varphi(e_{i}\bar{1},e_{i}\bar{1}))|,|\mathrm{ev}_{x}(\varphi(e_{i}\bar{1},e_{i}\bar{1}))|).

We work out the summands explicitly.
For

we have
\varphi(e_{i}\bar{1},e_{i}\bar{1})=e_{i}\varphi(\bar{1})=e_{i}U
by the

-bilinearity and the explicit description of

from (

).
We have

by Lemma

.
Using Lemma

and computing as in the proof of Corollary

\zeta(ye_{i}U)=f_{0}^{-1}\tau(e_{i}U(X,1))=U(\omega_{i},1)/f_{x}(\omega_{i},1)

Similarly we have
\mathrm{ev}_{x}(\varphi(e_{i}\bar{1},e_{i}\bar{1}))=\zeta(xe_{i}U)=\zeta((x/y)ye_{i}U)=\omega_{i}\zeta(ye_{i}U)=U(1,\omega_{i}^{-1})/f_{y}(1,\omega_{i}^{-1})

\max(|\zeta(ye_{i}U)|,|\zeta(xe_{i}U)|)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}|\zeta(ye_{i}U)|&|\omega_{i}|\leq 1\\

If we order the roots so that

and

, then we find

(\overline{1},\overline{1})_{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{|U(\omega_{i},1)|}{|f_{x}(\omega_{i},1)|}+\sum_{i=r+1}^{n}\frac{|U(1,\omega_{i}^{-1})|}{|f_{y}(1,\omega_{i}^{-1})|}.

∎

5
Equidistribution of the reduction covariant

The purpose of this section is to show that the reduction covariant (
3.2
) becomes equidistributed over integral orbits with respect to the natural measure on the space of lattices
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X
.
This will follow from a modification of the geometry-of-numbers methods of
[
5
, §4]
.
We closely follow the structure of
[
19
, §3]
.

5.1
Preliminaries

Let
n=2g+2
for some integer
g\geq 1
and consider the action of
\SL(W)=\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
on the representation
V
from
3.1
.

Coordinates on
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
.

Let
H_{0}
be the standard inner product on
W_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, whose Gram matrix is the identity matrix.
Then
K=\SO_{H_{0}}(\mathbb{R})=\SO_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is a maximal compact subgroup of
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
.
Let
T\leq\SL_{n}
be the subgroup of diagonal matrices.
For ease of notation, we will write
(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
for the element
\text{diag}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
of
T
.
Let
N\leq\SL_{n}
denote the subgroup of unipotent upper triangular matrices.
By the Iwasawa decomposition, the product map
N(\mathbb{R})\times T(\mathbb{R})^{\circ}\times K\rightarrow\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is a diffeomorphism.

Fundamental set for
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
.

We will use the language of semialgebraic sets, see
[
11
, Chapter 2]
. We will use without further mention that semialgebraic sets are closed under finite unions, intersections, and (pre-)images under semialgebraic maps.

By definition, a Siegel set is a subset of
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
of the form
\omega\cdot T_{c}\cdot K
, where
\omega\subset N(\mathbb{R})
is a relatively compact subset,
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
T_{c}\coloneqq\{(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\in T(\mathbb{R})^{\circ}\colon t_{1}/t_{2}>c,\dots,t_{n-1}/t_{n}>c\}
.
(This definition of
T_{c}
differs from the one given in
[
5
, §4.1.2]
since our convention for the action of
\SL_{n}
on
V
is slightly different, see §
3.1
.)
For every such Siegel set, the set of
\gamma\in\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
with
\gamma\cdot\mathfrak{S}\cap\mathfrak{S}\neq\varnothing
is finite
[
12
, Corollaire 15.3]
(‘Siegel property’).
Since
\SL_{n}
is a Chevalley group, there exists a Siegel set
\mathfrak{S}
with the property that
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\cdot\mathfrak{S}=\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. Explicitly, by
[
21
, Chapter 4, Theorem 4.12]
, we can take any Siegel set with
c\leq 2/\sqrt{3}
and
\omega
containing all
n\in N(\mathbb{R})
whose off-diagonal entries
n_{ij}
satisfy
|n_{ij}|\leq 1/2
.
Fix such a
\mathfrak{S}
. After enlarging
\mathfrak{S}
, we may and do assume that
\omega
, and consequently
\mathfrak{S}
, is open and semialgebraic.

The set
\mathfrak{S}
will serve as our fundamental set for the left action of
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
.
An
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
-orbit might be represented more than once in
\mathfrak{S}
, but this does not cause any problems as long as we incorporate the multiplicity function
m\colon\mathfrak{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, defined by
m(x)=\#(\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\cdot x\cap\mathfrak{S})
.
This function is bounded and has semialgebraic fibres.

Measures on
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
and
X
.

Choose a generator of the rank-
1
module of left invariant top differential forms of
\SL_{n}
over
\mathbb{Z}
.
This generator is unique up to sign and induces a bi-invariant Haar measure
dg
on
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
.
We equip the maximal compact subgroup
K
with its probability Haar measure.
We now explain how these measures also induce measures on
X
and
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X
.

The standard inner product
H_{0}
defines an element of
X
and the map
g\mapsto g\cdot H_{0}
induces a
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
-equivariant bijection
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})/K\simeq X
.
We will use this identification without further mention.
Since the measure
dg
on
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is bi-invariant, it induces measures on
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})/K=X
and
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})/K=\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X
.
We denote the latter measure by
\mu
.
It is a standard fact that
\mu(\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X)
is finite; in fact, we have
\mu(\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X)=\Vol(\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R}))=\zeta(2)\cdots\zeta(n)
.

Good subsets of
X
.

Consider the surjective quotient map

\varphi\colon\mathfrak{S}\rightarrow\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})/K=\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X
.
We call a subset
U\subset\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X

good
if it is relatively compact and
\varphi^{-1}(U)\subset\mathfrak{S}
is a semialgebraic subset of
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
.
For example, the image under
\varphi
of a relatively compact semialgebraic subset of
\mathfrak{S}
is good.

Lemma 5.1
.

There exists a countable basis of good open subsets of
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X
.

Proof.

The proof is identical to that of
[
19
, Lemma 3.2]
.
∎

The next two lemmas ensure that good subsets have good properties for the purposes of the geometry-of-numbers arguments.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
U\subset\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X
be good and let
\bar{U}\subset\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
be its preimage under the quotient map
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X
.
Then for all
h\in\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
we have

\displaystyle\int_{g\in\mathfrak{S}\cap\bar{U}h}m(g)^{-1}\,dg=\mu(U).

Proof.

Follows from pushing forward measures and the bi-invariance of
dg
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
U\subset\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X
be good, and let

5.2
The equidistribution theorem

Define the height of a binary form
f(x,y)=f_{0}x^{n}+\cdots+f_{n}y^{n}\in\mathbb{R}[x,y]
by the formula
0pt(f)=\max|f_{i}|
.
Let
\mathcal{F}(X)
be the set of integral binary forms
f=f_{0}x^{n}+f_{1}x^{n-1}y+\cdots+f_{n}y^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[x,y]
of nonzero discriminant and of height
<X
.

Lemma 5.8
.

\#\mathcal{F}(X)=2^{n+1}X^{n+1}+O(X^{n})
.

Proof.

We need to show that there are
O(X^{n})
polynomials with
0pt(f)<X
and
\Delta(f)=0
. This follows immediately from
[
3
, Lemma 3.1]
.
∎

Define the height of an element
(A,B)\in V(\mathbb{R})
to be the height of its invariant binary form:
0pt(A,B)=0pt(f_{A,B})
.
We say an element
(A,B)\in V(\mathbb{Z})
is degenerate if
\Delta(A,B)=\disc(f_{A,B})=0
, and nondegenerate otherwise.
For any subset
S\subset V(\mathbb{Z})
, write
S^{\text{nd}}
for the subset of nondegenerate elements.
Given
X\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and an
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
-invariant subset
S\subset V(\mathbb{Z})
, let
N(S;X)
be the number of nondegenerate
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
-orbits in
S
of height
<X
.

Bhargava
[
5
, §4,Theorem 8]
has determined asymptotics for the number of nondegenerate orbits in
V(\mathbb{Z})
of bounded height under the action of
\SL_{n}^{\pm}(\mathbb{Z})=\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
.
The same computation applies to nondegenerate
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
-orbits, showing that there exists a constant

such that

\displaystyle N(V(\mathbb{Z});X)=cX^{n+1}+o(X^{n+1})

(5.4)

as

.
The purpose of this section is to show, in analogy with
[
19
, Theorem 3.8]
, that the reduction covariant (
3.2
) equidistributes over nondegenerate orbits, in the following sense:

Theorem 5.9
.

Let

be a Borel-measurable subset whose boundary has measure zero.
Then

\displaystyle N(V(\mathbb{Z})\cap\mathcal{R}^{-1}(U);X)=\frac{\mu(U)}{\mu(\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X)}\cdot N(V(\mathbb{Z});X)+o(X^{n+1}).

The proof of Theorem
5.9
follows from a modification of the proof of Bhargava of the asymptotic (
5.4
) and is given below.
We first prove a local version for good subsets.
In the notation of §
5.1
, fix a pair
(m,\tau)\in\Sigma
(see (
5.3
)), which corresponds to a subset
V^{(m,\tau)}\subset V(\mathbb{R})
, and write
V(\mathbb{Z})^{(m,\tau)}=V(\mathbb{Z})\cap V^{(m,\tau)}
.

Let
\mathcal{J}
be the rational nonzero constant of
[
5
, Proposition 16]
.
Let

equal
\frac{1}{2}\#\Stab_{\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})}(v)
for any

; this constant is computed explicitly in Lemma
5.5
.
For a subset
S
of
V(\mathbb{R})
or

, write
S_{<X}
for the set of
s\in S
with
0pt(s)<X
.
Equip

with the measure corresponding to the differential form
df_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge df_{n}
.
Let

\displaystyle c_{m,\tau}=\mu(\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X)\frac{|\mathcal{J}|\cdot\Vol(I(m)_{<1})}{r_{m,n}},

where

denotes any element of
V^{(m,\tau)}
.
Bhargava
[
5
, Theorem 9]
showed that

\displaystyle N(V(\mathbb{Z})^{(m,\tau)};X)=c_{m,\tau}X^{n+1}+o(X^{n+1}).

(5.5)

Proposition 5.10

Let
U\subset\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X
be a good subset and let

Then

\displaystyle N(S_{U};X)=\frac{\mu(U)}{\mu(\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X)}c_{m,\tau}X^{n+1}+o(X^{n+1}).

(5.6)

Proof.

We will use the notations and choices made in §
5.1
.
Write
\Lambda=\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, equipped with its multiplicative Haar measure

, and let
\Lambda
act on

by scalar multiplication.
Write
L=L^{(m,\tau)}
.
Fix a compact, semialgebraic set

of volume

, with nonempty interior, that satisfies

and whose projection onto

is contained in

For any subset

\displaystyle N(S;X)=\frac{1}{r_{m,n}}\int_{h\in G_{0}}\#[S\cap(\mathfrak{S}\Lambda hL)_{<X}]\,dh,

with the caveat that elements on the right hand side are weighted by a function similar to

We use the above expression to define

for subsets

A change of variables trick
[

shows that for every

\displaystyle N(S;X)=\frac{1}{r_{m,n}}\int_{g\in\mathfrak{S}}\int_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\#[S\cap(g\lambda G_{0}L)_{<X}]m(g)^{-1}\,dg\,d^{\times}\lambda,

with the caveat that an element

on the right hand side is counted with multiplicity
\#\{h\in G_{0}\colon v\in g\lambda hL\}
.

We let
V^{\text{cusp}}
be the subset of all
(A,B)\in V(\mathbb{R})
such that the top left entries of
A
and
B
have absolute value
<1
.
By cutting off the cusp arguments
[
5
, Proposition 13]
,
N(S_{U};X)=N(S_{U}^{\prime};X)+o(X^{n+1})
, so it remains to estimate

.

We first make the reduction covariant more explicit.
If

(with

and

),

and

imply that

.
We have chosen

so that

in

.
Write
\bar{U}
for the preimage of

under the quotient map
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X
.
Then we conclude that
\mathcal{R}(gh\lambda\ell)\in U
if and only if
gh^{\prime}\in\bar{U}
.

Let
Z_{1}=\{(g,h)\in\mathfrak{S}\times G_{0}\mid gh^{\prime}\in\bar{U}\}
, where we write
h^{\prime}
for the projection of

onto
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
.
By Lemma
5.3
,
Z_{1}
is semialgebraic.
Let
Z_{2}
be the graph of the action map

sending

to

.
Since this map is algebraic,
Z_{2}
is semialgebraic.
So is the projection
Z_{3}
of

onto
\mathfrak{S}\times V(\mathbb{R})

Let

For every

and

the set

We view

as a multiset where

Then

Applying Proposition
5.7
to

shows that

\displaystyle\#[V(\mathbb{Z})\cap(B(g,X)\setminus V^{\text{cusp}})]=\Vol(B(g,X)\setminus V^{\text{cusp}})+E(g,X),

\displaystyle N(S_{U};X)=\frac{1}{r_{m,n}}\int_{g\in\mathfrak{S}}\Vol(B(g,X))m(g)^{-1}\,dg+o(X^{n+1}).

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{m+n}}\int_{g\in\mathfrak{S}}\Vol(B(g,X))m(g)^{-1}\,dg=\frac{|\mathcal{J}|}{2^{m+n}}\Vol(I(m)_{<X})\int_{g\in\mathfrak{S}}\int_{h\in G_{0}}\mathbf{1}_{\{gh^{\prime}\in\bar{U}\}}m(g)^{-1}\,dh\,dg,

Proof of Theorem

.

Let

\underline{\nu}(U)=\mu(\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X)\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N(V(\mathbb{Z})\cap\mathcal{R}^{-1}(U);X)}{N(V(\mathbb{Z});X)}

and
\bar{\nu}(U)
be the same expression with
\liminf
replaced by
\limsup
.
It suffices to prove that
\underline{\nu}(U)=\bar{\nu}(U)=\mu(U)
.
If
U
is good, this follows from summing (
5.6
) over all
(m,\tau)\in\Sigma
, Equation (
5.5
), and the fact that
V^{s}(\mathbb{R})
is partitioned into the subsets
V^{(m,\tau)}
.
To prove the theorem for general
U
, we bootstrap from the case of good subsets (and the trivial case
U=\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X
).

Let
U^{\circ}
be the interior of
U
.
Since
U^{\circ}
is open, by Lemma
5.1
there exists an increasing sequence
(U_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of good subsets whose union is
U^{\circ}
.
For every
n\geq 1
we have
\underline{\nu}(U_{n})\leq\underline{\nu}(U^{\circ})
.
Since
U_{n}
is good,
\underline{\nu}(U_{n})=\mu(U_{n})
, hence
\mu(U_{n})\leq\underline{\nu}(U^{\circ})
for all
n\geq 1
.
By continuity of the measure,
\mu(U_{n})\rightarrow\mu(U^{\circ})
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
We conclude that
\mu(U^{\circ})\leq\underline{\nu}(U^{\circ})
.
Let
\bar{U}
denote the closure of
U
.
Since the complement of
\bar{U}
equals the interior of the complement of
U
, the above argument also shows
\bar{\nu}(\bar{U})\leq\mu(\bar{U})
.

In conclusion, we have shown that

\mu(U^{\circ})\leq\underline{\nu}(U^{\circ})\leq\underline{\nu}(U)\leq\bar{\nu}(U)\leq\bar{\nu}(\bar{U})\leq\mu(\bar{U}).

By assumption,
\mu(U^{\circ})=\mu(\bar{U})=\mu(U)
, so all the inequalities are in fact equalities, and the theorem follows.
∎

5.3
A neighbourhood of the cusp

Let
\epsilon>0
.
Say an inner product

on

has an
\epsilon
-small vector if there exists a nonzero

such that

\displaystyle(v,v)^{1/2}_{H}<\epsilon\cdot(\det H)^{1/2n}.

This condition only depends on the image
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\cdot[H]
of

in
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X
, so it makes sense say that an element of
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X
has an
\epsilon
-small vector.
We now show that such elements form a small measure subset of
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X

Recall from §

that
\mathfrak{S}=\omega T_{c}K
and that
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{S}\rightarrow\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X
denotes the projection.
After possibly enlarging
\mathfrak{S}
, we may and do assume that

.
Let

Proposition 5.11
.

1.

The set
U_{\epsilon}
is Borel-measurable, its boundary has measure zero, and
\mu(U_{\epsilon})\rightarrow 0
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
.

2.

If
H
is an inner product on
W_{\mathbb{R}}
that admits an
\epsilon
-small vector, then the image of
H
in
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash X

.

Proof.

Since the boundary of a semialgebraic set has strictly smaller dimension
[

, Proposition 2.8.13]
and
\varphi^{-1}(U_{\epsilon})
is semialgebraic, the boundary of
\varphi^{-1}(U_{\epsilon})
has measure zero, hence the same is true for
U_{\varepsilon}
. To show that
\mu(U_{\epsilon})\rightarrow 0
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0

\vol(\omega T(\epsilon)K)\rightarrow 0
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
. This is true because
\vol(\mathfrak{S})<\infty
and
\cap_{\epsilon>0}(\omega T(\epsilon)K)=0
.

To prove the second part, we may assume that
H
has determinant
1
and so (since
\varphi
is surjective) that
H=g\cdot H_{0}=g^{-t}H_{0}g^{-1}
for some

with

and

.
Let

be a nonzero element with
(v,v)_{H}^{1/2}<\epsilon
.
We will show that this implies
t_{1}>c^{n-1}/\epsilon
.
Write
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
for the standard basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and let
f_{i}=g\cdot e_{i}
for all
i
.
Since
(v,w)_{H}=(g^{-1}v,g^{-1}w)_{H_{0}}
for all
v,w\in W_{\mathbb{R}}
, the basis
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
is orthonormal with respect to
(-,-)_{H}
.
Write
v=\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}e_{i}
where
m_{i}
are integers,
1\leq k\leq m
and
m_{k}\neq 0
.
A computation reveals that
e_{i}\in t_{i}^{-1}f_{i}+\text{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{f_{1},\dots,f_{i-1}\}
for each
i
, so
v\in m_{k}t_{k}^{-1}f_{k}+\text{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{f_{1},\dots,f_{k-1}\}
.
By the orthonormality of the
f_{i}
and the fact that
m_{k}
is a nonzero integer,

\displaystyle(v,v)^{1/2}_{H}\geq|m_{k}|t_{k}^{-1}\geq t_{k}^{-1}.

Therefore
t_{k}>1/\epsilon
.
Since
(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\in T_{c}
, we have
t_{i}>c\cdot t_{i+1}
for all
1\leq i\leq k-1
, so
t_{1}>c^{k-1}t_{k}>c^{k-1}/\epsilon>c^{n-1}/\epsilon
, as required.
∎

Combining Proposition
5.11
with the equidistribution theorem (Theorem
5.9
) and (
5.4
) immediately shows:

Corollary 5.12
.

There exist a constant
c_{1}>0
such that for every
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{f\in\mathcal{F}(X)\colon\exists\,(A,B)\in V_{f}(\mathbb{Z})\text{ and }w\in W\text{ with }(w,w)_{H_{A,B}}^{1/2}<\epsilon(\det H_{A,B})^{1/2n}\}}{\#\mathcal{F}(X)}\leq c_{1}\cdot\mu(U_{\epsilon}).

(5.10)

Moreover,
\mu(U_{\epsilon})\rightarrow 0
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
.

6
A height lower bound for divisors

6.1
A density
1
family

Let
g\geq 1
. Recall from §
5.2
that for a real number
X>0
,
\mathcal{F}(X)
denotes the set of integral binary forms
f(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}[x,y]
of degree
n=2g+2
, nonzero discriminant and height at most
X
, and that
\#\mathcal{F}(X)=(2X)^{n+1}+O(X^{n})
.

For
\delta>0
, let
\mathcal{F}_{\delta}(X)
be the subset of
f=f_{0}x^{n}+\cdots+f_{n}y^{n}\in\mathcal{F}(X)
satisfying the following properties:

1.

f(x,y)
is an irreducible polynomial in
\mathbb{Q}[x,y]
.

2.

We have
|\disc(f)|\geq X^{2n-2-\delta}
.

Note that the first condition implies that
f_{0}f_{n}\neq 0
.

These conditions cut out a density-
1
family:

Proposition 6.1
.

We have
\mathcal{F}_{\delta}(X)=(2X)^{n+1}+o(X^{n+1})
.

Proof.

We need to show that the number of
f\in\mathcal{F}(X)
failing the first or second condition is
o(X^{n+1})
.
For the first condition, this follows from Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem; for the second, this is
[
10
, Lemma 6.1]
.
∎

The relevance of this family comes from the next elementary proposition.
To state it, let
f\in\mathcal{F}(X)
and write

\displaystyle f(x,y)=c\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\omega_{i}y)\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\eta_{j}x-y),

(6.1)

where
c,\omega_{i},\eta_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
,
|\omega_{i}|\leq 1
and
|\eta_{j}|<1
for all
i,j
.
Write
f_{x}(x,y)
for the partial derivative of
f(x,y)
with respect to
x
, and similarly for
f_{y}(x,y)
.

Let
U(x,y)=u_{0}x^{m}+u_{1}x^{m-1}y+\cdots+u_{m}y^{m}\in\mathbb{Z}[x,y]
be an integral binary form of degree
m
.
Write
h(U)=\log(\max(|u_{0}|,\dots,|u_{m}|))
.

Proposition 6.2
.

If
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\delta}(X)
then, in the above notation, we have

\displaystyle n\cdot\log\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{|U(\omega_{i},1)|}{|f_{x}(\omega_{i},1)|}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{|U(1,\eta_{j})|}{|f_{y}(1,\eta_{j})|}\right)-\log|c|\leq n\cdot h(U)-(n+1)\log X+n\delta\log X+\kappa_{n,m},

(6.2)

where
\kappa_{n,m}
is a constant that only depends on
n
and
m
.

Proof.

We have the formulae

\displaystyle\disc(f)

\displaystyle=c^{2n-2}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq r}(\omega_{i}-\omega_{j})^{2}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}(\eta_{i}-\eta_{j})^{2}\prod_{1\leq i\leq r,1\leq j\leq k}(\omega_{i}\eta_{j}-1)^{2},

\displaystyle f_{x}(\omega_{i},1)

\displaystyle=c\prod_{j\neq i}(\omega_{i}-\omega_{j})\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\omega_{i}\eta_{j}-1)\text{ for each }i=1,\dots,r.

Each term
|\omega_{i}-\omega_{j}|,|\eta_{i}-\eta_{j}|,|\omega_{i}\eta_{j}-1|
is
\leq 2
.
Therefore,
|\disc(f)|/|f_{x}(\omega_{i},1)|
is
|c|^{2n-3}
times a product of
n(n-1)-(n-1)
terms that are each
\leq 2
.
Hence
|\disc(f)|/|f_{x}(\omega_{i},1)|\leq|c|^{2n-3}2^{(n-1)^{2}}
.
Since
|\disc(f)|\geq X^{2n-2-\delta}
and
|U(\omega_{i},1)|\leq(m+1)\max(|u_{0}|,\cdots,|u_{m}|)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{|U(\omega_{i},1)|}{|f_{x}(\omega_{i},1)|}\leq(m+1)\max(|u_{i}|)2^{(n-1)^{2}}|c|^{2n-3}/X^{2n-2-\delta}.

The same upper bound applies to
\frac{|U(1,\eta_{j})|}{|f_{y}(1,\eta_{j})|}
, and summing these bounds shows that the left hand side of (
6.2
) is at most

\displaystyle n\log(n(m+1)2^{(n-1)^{2}})+n\cdot h(U)+(n(2n-3)-1)\log|c|-n(2n-2-\delta)\log X.

(6.3)

It remains to upper bound
\log|c|
, which we achieve by showing that the elements
\omega_{i},\eta_{j}
are not too far removed from the unit circle.
Indeed,
[
25
, Theorem 1]
shows that

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{r}|\omega_{i}|^{-1}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\eta_{j}|^{-1}\leq\frac{n}{|f_{n}/f_{0}|}\sum_{i=0}^{n}|f_{i}/f_{0}|^{2}.

On the other hand,
|c|^{2}\prod|\omega_{i}|\prod|\eta_{j}|=|f_{0}||f_{n}|
by (
6.1
), so

\displaystyle|c|^{2}=|f_{0}||f_{n}|\prod_{i=1}^{r}|\omega_{i}|^{-1}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\eta_{j}|^{-1}\leq n\sum_{i=0}^{n}|f_{i}|^{2}\leq n(n+1)X^{2},

hence
\log|c|\leq\frac{1}{2}\log(n(n+1))+\log X
.
Combining the latter upper bound with (
6.3
) concludes the proof.
∎

6.2
Height functions

Let
f\in\mathcal{F}(X)
and let
\pi\colon X_{f}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}}
be the hyperelliptic curve described by the equation
z^{2}=f(x,y)
.
If
D
is an effective divisor on
X_{f}
of degree
m
, let
U(x,y)=u_{0}x^{m}+\cdots+u_{m}y^{m}\in\mathbb{Z}[x,y]
be a primitive binary form of degree
m
vanishing precisely at the points of
\pi(D)
with multiplicity.
Then
U
is uniquely determined up to sign and we may define

\displaystyle h(D)=h(U)=\log\max(|u_{0}|,\dots,|u_{m}|).

This is a ‘naive height’ on the set of effective divisors on
X_{f}
.
For example, if
D
has degree
1
, corresponding to a point
P\in X_{f}(\mathbb{Q})
, then
h(D)
equals
h(\pi(P))
, where
h(\alpha)
denotes the logarithmic normalized Weil height of an element
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
.
More generally, by
[
24
, Theorem VIII.5.9]
we have:

Lemma 6.3
.

If
D
is the Galois orbit of an algebraic point
P\in X_{f}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
of degree
m
, then
|h(D)-mh(\pi(P))|\leq m\log 2
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\delta}(X)
and that
D
is an effective divisor on
X_{f}
of degree
2g-1
.
Then there exists
(A,B)\in V_{f}(\mathbb{Z})
and a primitive
w\in W=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle n\cdot\log(w,w)_{H_{A,B}}-\log\det H_{A,B}\leq n\cdot h(D)-(n+1)\log X+n\delta\log X+\kappa_{n},

where
\kappa_{n}
is a constant that only depends on
n
.

Proof.

Since
f=f_{0}x^{n}+\cdots+f_{n}y^{n}\in\mathcal{F}_{\delta}(X)
is irreducible,
f_{0}f_{n}\neq 0
and
D
does not intersect the irreducible Weierstrass locus
S_{f}
.
By Propositions
4.6
,
4.7
and Lemma
3.11
, there exists an
(A,B)
and a primitive
w\in W
such that
n\log(w,w)_{H_{A,B}}-\det H_{A,B}
equals the left hand side of (
6.2
), so we conclude using Proposition
6.2
.
∎

6.3
Proof of the main theorem

Let
\mathcal{F}=\cup_{X>0}\mathcal{F}(X)
be the set of all binary forms
f(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}[x,y]
of degree
n=2g+2
and nonzero discriminant, ordered by height. If
S\subset\mathcal{F}
is a subset and
\alpha\in[0,1]
, we say that
S
has density
\alpha
if

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{\#(S\cap\mathcal{F}(X))}{\#\mathcal{F}(X)}=\alpha.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
\epsilon>0
be arbitrary, and let
S_{\epsilon}
denote the set of
f\in\mathcal{F}
such that every effective divisor
D
on
X_{f}
of odd degree
\leq 2g-1
satisfies

\displaystyle h(D)\geq\left(1+\frac{1}{2g+2}-\epsilon\right)\log 0ptf.

(6.4)

Then
S_{\epsilon}
has density
1
.

Proof.

Adding the degree-
2
divisor
\pi^{-1}(\infty)
to
D
has the effect of multiplying
U
by
y^{2}
, which does not affect the quantity
h(D)
.
It therefore suffices to prove that the set
S_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\subset S_{\epsilon}
of
f\in\mathcal{F}
satisfying (
6.4
) only for
D
of degree
2g-1
has density 1.

Let
\mathcal{F}^{\text{bad}}
be the subset of those
f\in\mathcal{F}
such that there exists a degree
2g-1
divisor
D
for which (
6.4
) does
not
hold; it suffices to prove that
\mathcal{F}^{\text{bad}}\subset\mathcal{F}
has density zero. Fix
\delta\in(0,\epsilon)
.
By Proposition
6.1
, it suffices to prove that
\#(\mathcal{F}^{\text{bad}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\delta}(X))=o(X^{2g+3})
.

Write
\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon-\delta
.
Let
\mathcal{F}(X)^{\text{short}}
be the subset of
f\in\mathcal{F}(X)
for which there exists elements
(A,B)\in V_{f}(\mathbb{Z})
and
w\in W-\{0\}
that satisfy

\displaystyle n\log(w,w)_{H_{A,B}}-\log\det H_{A,B}<-\epsilon_{1}\log X.

(6.5)

Theorem
6.4
implies that
\mathcal{F}^{\text{bad}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\delta}(X)\subset\mathcal{F}(X)^{\text{short}}
for sufficiently large
X
.
The equidistribution theorem, more specifically Corollary
5.12
, shows that
\#\mathcal{F}(X)^{\text{short}}=o(X^{2g+3})
.
Therefore
\#(\mathcal{F}^{\text{bad}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\delta}(X))=o(X^{2g+3})
, as required.
∎

This theorem and Lemma
6.3
immediately imply:

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
\epsilon>0
be arbitrary. Then for
100\%
of
f\in\mathcal{F}(X)
, every algebraic point
P\in X_{f}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
of odd degree
m\leq 2g-1
satisfies

\displaystyle m\cdot h(\pi(P))\geq\left(1+\frac{1}{2g+2}-\epsilon\right)\log 0pt(f).

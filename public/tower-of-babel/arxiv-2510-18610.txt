Period sheaves via perverse pullbacks
Adeel A. Khan, Tasuki Kinjo, Hyeonjun Park, Pavel Safronov

Abstract
. We construct period sheaves for Hamiltonian spaces, as conjectured in the work of Ben-Zvi, Sakellaridis and Venkatesh, using the perverse pullback functors introduced in the authors’ previous work. We prove a dimensional reduction isomorphism (generalizing the results of Davison and Kinjo in cohomological Donaldson–Thomas theory) which implies that perverse pullbacks refine the ordinary pullback functors in constructible sheaf theory, and relate perverse pullbacks to microstalk functors. These results imply that our period sheaves recover the known constructions in the cotangent and Whittaker cases.



Introduction

Perverse pullbacks

For a morphism
f\colon X\rightarrow B
of complex algebraic varieties, the corresponding pullback functors
f^{*},f^{!}\colon\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{c}}(B)\rightarrow\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{c}}(X)
between bounded derived categories of constructible sheaves are, in general, not exact for the perverse
t
-structure. If
f
is smooth, we have an isomorphism
f^{*}[\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}(X/B)]\cong f^{!}[-\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}(X/B)]
and this functor
is
perverse
t
-exact. In the previous paper
[
KKPS25
]
we have explained how to define perverse pullback functors beyond the smooth case by considering suitable derived enhancements. Namely, for a morphism
f\colon X\rightarrow B
of derived Artin stacks equipped with

(1)

a relative exact
(-1)
-shifted symplectic structure in the sense of
[
PTVV13
]
and

(2)

an orientation datum (a choice of a square root line bundle
K_{X/B}^{1/2}
),

we have defined a perverse
t
-exact functor

f^{\varphi}\colon\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{c}}(B)\longrightarrow\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{c}}(X)

called the
perverse pullback
. This functor interpolates between the functor of vanishing cycles (for
X=B
) and the so-called
DT sheaf

\varphi_{X}=f^{\varphi}\mathbb{Q}_{B}
(for
B=\mathrm{pt}
) constructed in
[
BBDJS15
,
BBBJ15
,
KL12
]
.

The DT sheaf
\varphi_{X}
is the central object of cohomological DT theory allowing one to define, for instance, Gopakumar–Vafa invariants of Calabi-Yau threefolds
[
MT18
]
, BPS cohomology for stacks
[
BDNKP25
]
and microlocal homology of lci schemes
[
Sch22
]
. Similarly, the perverse pullback functor is a central object for family versions of cohomological DT invariants.

Perverse pullbacks and microlocalization

Microlocal sheaf theory
[
KS90
]
views sheaves on a manifold
M
as local objects (microsheaves) on the cotangent bundle
\mathrm{T}^{*}M
. For instance, for a sheaf
\mathscr{F}
one has its microsupport
\mathrm{SS}(\mathscr{F})\subset\mathrm{T}^{*}M
and for a submanifold
Z\subset M
one can define its microlocalization
\mu_{Z/M}(\mathscr{F})
which localizes the microsheaf onto the conormal bundle
\mathrm{N}^{*}(Z/M)
.

By
[
Par24
]
the data of a relative exact
(-1)
-shifted symplectic structure on
f\colon X\rightarrow B
is the same as the data of an exact
0
-shifted Lagrangian structure on the
moment map

\mu\colon X\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{*}B
and we have suggested (see
[
KKPS25
, Conjecture B]
) to view the perverse pullback functor
f^{\varphi}
as a kind of a Lagrangian microlocalization functor
\mu^{\mathrm{Lag}}_{X/\mathrm{T}^{*}B}
localizing microsheaves on
\mathrm{T}^{*}B
onto
X
. The first result we prove in this direction is a support estimate suggested by this interpretation.

Theorem A

(
Proposition

2.3
)
.

Let
\pi\colon X\rightarrow B
be a morphism of derived Artin stacks locally of finite presentation equipped with an oriented exact relative
(-1)
-shifted symplectic structure. Let
\mu\colon X\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{*}B
be the corresponding moment map. Suppose
\mathscr{F}\in\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{c}}(B)
is microsupported on
\Lambda\subset\mathrm{T}^{*}B
. Then
\pi^{\varphi}(\mathscr{F})
is supported on
\mu^{-1}(\Lambda)
.

This support estimate generalizes the usual support estimate
[
KS90
, Corollary 5.4.10]
for microlocalization functors
\mu_{Z/M}
along submanifolds.

Dimensional reduction

The second result we prove for perverse pullbacks is that they refine both
*
- and
!
-pullbacks. For this, recall from
[
Cal19
]
that for every geometric morphism of derived stacks
Y\rightarrow B
locally of finite presentation the relative
(-1)
-shifted cotangent bundle
\mathrm{T}^{*}[-1](Y/B)
(equivalently, the conormal bundle
\mathrm{N}^{*}(Y/B)
of
Y\rightarrow B
) carries a relative exact
(-1)
-shifted symplectic structure over
B
. Moreover, it is canonically oriented by
[
KKPS25
, Section 6.1]
. In particular, we may consider the perverse pullback along the morphism
\pi\colon\mathrm{T}^{*}[-1](Y/B)\rightarrow B
.

Theorem B

(
Theorem

4.1
)
.

Let
g\colon Y\rightarrow B
be a morphism of derived Artin stacks locally of finite presentation. Let
\tau\colon\mathrm{T}^{*}[-1](Y/B)\rightarrow Y
be the natural projection. Then there are natural isomorphisms

\tau_{!}\pi^{\varphi}\cong g^{*}[\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}(Y/B)],\qquad\tau_{*}\pi^{\varphi}\cong g^{!}[-\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}(Y/B)]

of functors
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{c}}(B)\rightarrow\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{c}}(Y)
.

This theorem generalizes the dimensional reduction isomorphism for the perverse sheaf
\varphi_{X}
constructed in
[
Kin22
]
in three ways. First, from
B=\mathrm{pt}
to the relative situation. Second, from
g
being quasi-smooth (i.e. the cotangent complex
\mathbb{L}_{Y/B}
having Tor-amplitude
\geq-1
) to a general morphism locally of finite presentation. Finally, from 1-Artin stacks to higher Artin stacks.
In contrast with the proof in
[
Kin22
]
, our argument does not rely on gluing of local models. Instead, we use deformation to the normal cone to reduce the problem to the linear case where
g
is the zero section of a perfect complex.
The generalized dimensional reduction theorem has the following applications:

(1)

It encodes a microlocal nature of the Fourier transform in Borel–Moore homology. Namely, consider a morphism of higher Artin stacks

, locally of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
, and a perfect complex

over

. Taking

in

we obtain the

(
Theorem

4.23
)

\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{BM}}_{\bullet}(E/S)\cong\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{BM}}_{\bullet-2\mathrm{rk}(E)}(E^{\vee}[-1]/S),

generalizing a construction from
[
Kas88
]
.
Here
\mathrm{rk}(E)
indicates the (virtual) rank of
E
.

(2)

Let
S
be a smooth variety and
\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{Perf}(S)}
the moduli stack of compactly supported perfect complexes on
S
, which is a higher Artin stack. If
S
is a surface and
\mathrm{Tot}(K_{S})\rightarrow S
the total space of its canonical bundle, then using
[
IQ23
,
BCS24
]
we get

(3)

Nadler has suggested a definition of microlocal homology of singular varieties interpolating between the usual cohomology and Borel–Moore homology. The definition of Schefers
[
Sch22
]
is as follows: for a quasi-smooth derived scheme

(e.g. a classical lci scheme) and a closed conic subset
\Lambda\subset\mathrm{T}^{*}[-1]S
(equivalently, a closed conic subset of the scheme of singularities

from
[
AG15
]
) let

\mathrm{H}_{\bullet}^{\Lambda}(S)=\mathrm{H}^{-\bullet}_{\Lambda}(\mathrm{T}^{*}[-1]S,\varphi_{\mathrm{T}^{*}[-1]S}),

be the cohomology of the DT sheaf with support on

implies that for

Period sheaves

In the recent work
[
BSV24
]
Ben-Zvi, Sakellaridis and Venkatesh have studied an extension of the geometric Langlands correspondence. Beyond the equivalence of “automorphic” and “spectral” categories associated to Langlands dual groups
G,\check{G}
, it further matches specific objects on the two sides: the period sheaf associated to a Hamiltonian
G
-space
M
and the
L
-sheaf associated to the dual Hamiltonian
\check{G}
-space
\check{M}
. Moreover, they have constructed the (normalized) period sheaf
\mathscr{P}^{\mathrm{norm}}_{X}
for a
G
-space
X
and conjectured that it only depends on the Hamiltonian
G
-space
M=\mathrm{T}^{*}X
with the appropriate “anomaly trivialization” data. As an application of the construction of perverse pullbacks, we realize this vision in full generality. Let
\mathbf{D}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G}(\Sigma))
be the
\infty
-category of ind-constructible complexes on the moduli stack of
G
-bundles on an algebraic curve
\Sigma
and
\mathrm{Nilp}\subset\mathrm{T}^{*}\mathrm{Bun}_{G}(\Sigma)
the global nilpotent cone. Let
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G}(\Sigma))\subset\mathbf{D}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G}(\Sigma))
be the full subcategory of ind-constructible complexes microsupported on the global nilpotent cone. The following statement combines
Theorem

5.9
and
Definition

5.12
.

Theorem C
.

Let
G=G_{1}\times G_{2}
be a connected reductive group over
\mathbb{C}
,
(M,\omega)
a quasi-separated smooth complex symplectic scheme equipped with a
G\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action, such that the
G
-action preserves
\omega
and the
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action scales it with weight
2
and

a connected smooth complex projective algebraic curve equipped with a choice of

(i.e. a spin structure). Choose an anomaly trivialization, e.g. a trivialization of the étale second Chern class

•

The symplectic nature of the period sheaf exhibited in

It is instructive to analyze the above construction in the case

\mathrm{ind}^{\mathrm{pt}\rightarrow\mathrm{pt}}_{M}(\mathbf{1})=\mathbb{R}\Gamma_{c}(\mathrm{Map}^{\mathrm{twisted}}(\Sigma,M),\varphi)

The notion of period sheaves is also closely related to the notion of Coulomb branches of 3d

in the corresponding homotopy category (or its twisted version if the anomaly cancellation condition does not hold), so that the algebra of functions on the Coulomb branch is

The theory we develop here does not immediately apply to the situation of Coulomb branches as the stack

is not locally of finite presentation. It would be interesting to extend the theory of shifted symplectic structures to the semi-infinite setting to incorporate this example.

is the dimension of the intersection
\mathrm{T}_{p}\Lambda
and
\mathrm{T}_{p}\Gamma_{dt}
, then
\tau_{t}(p)+e
is even.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold,
\Lambda\subset\mathrm{T}^{*}X
a (complex) conic Lagrangian subset and
p\in\Lambda
a smooth point with
\pi(p)=x\in X
. For a smooth function
t\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
t(x)=0
and such that
\Gamma_{dt}
and
\Lambda
intersect cleanly at
p
with excess
e\in\mathbb{Z}
, the functor

\varphi_{t}(-)|_{x}[-(\tau_{t}(p)+e)/2]\colon\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\Lambda}(X)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Mod}_{R}

is independent of the choice of the function
t
, up to a (non-canonical) natural isomorphism.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of
[
KS90
, Proposition 7.5.3]
which treats the case
e=0
(i.e. when
\Gamma_{dt}
and
\Lambda
intersect transversely at
p
). Fix
\mathscr{F}\in\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\Lambda}(X)
.

First, by
[
KS90
, Corollary A.2.7 and Lemma 7.5.2]
we may apply a quantized contact transformation to assume that
\Lambda
, in a neighborhood of
p
, is the conormal bundle of a complex submanifold
i\colon M\hookrightarrow X
. By
[
KS90
, Proposition 6.6.1]
working microlocally near
p\in\mathrm{T}^{*}X
we may find
L\in\mathrm{Mod}_{R}
such that
\varphi_{t}(\mathscr{F})|_{x}\cong\varphi_{t}(i_{*}L_{M})|_{x}
, where
L_{M}
is the constant sheaf on
M
with stalk
L
. By proper base change we have
\varphi_{t}(i_{*}L_{M})|_{x}\cong\varphi_{t|_{M}}(L_{M})|_{x}
.

The assumption on the clean intersection of
\Gamma_{dt}
and
\Lambda
implies that
t|_{M}\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is Morse–Bott with critical locus a submanifold
S\subset M
of dimension
e
at
x
. Let
Q
be the Hessian of
t|_{M}
at
x
viewed as a quadratic form on
V=\mathrm{T}_{x}M
. Let
n_{+},n_{-}
be the number of positive and negative eigenvalues of
Q
. By the Morse–Bott lemma (see
[
Nic11
, Proposition 2.5.2]
), locally near
x
we may find a diffeomorphism
\Phi\colon V\rightarrow M
sending
0
to
x
under which
\Phi^{\ast}(t|_{M})=Q
. Then
\varphi_{t|_{M}}(L_{M})|_{x}\cong\varphi_{Q}(L_{V})|_{0}
. The standard computation of vanishing cycles of a quadratic form shows that
\varphi_{Q}(L_{V})|_{0}\cong L[-n_{+}]
. By
[
KS90
, Proposition A.3.6]
we have

\tau_{t}(p)=n_{-}-n_{+}.

Therefore, in total we get

\varphi_{t}(\mathscr{F})|_{x}[-(\tau_{t}(p)+e)/2]\cong L[-\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}M/2]

which is independent of the choice of
t
.
∎

The functor

\mu_{p}\colon\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\Lambda}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{Mod}_{R}

defined in
Proposition

2.4
is the
microstalk
functor at
p\in\Lambda
. We have a smooth functoriality of microstalk functors as follows.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
f\colon Y\rightarrow X
be a smooth morphism of complex manifolds and
\Lambda\subset\mathrm{T}^{*}X
a (complex) conic Lagrangian subset. Let
(y,p)\in Y\times_{X}\mathrm{T}^{*}X
be a point such that
p\in\Lambda
is smooth and let
p^{\prime}=f^{\ast}(y,p)
. Then there is a (non-canonical) natural isomorphism

\mu_{p^{\prime}}f^{\dagger}\cong\mu_{p}\colon\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\Lambda}(X)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Mod}_{R}.

Proof.

Let
x
be the image of
p
under
\mathrm{T}^{*}X\rightarrow X
and
t\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a smooth function such that
\Gamma_{dt}
and
\Lambda
intersect cleanly at
p
with excess
e
. Let
t^{\prime}=f^{\ast}t
and
\Lambda_{Y}=f^{\ast}(f^{-1}_{\pi}(\Lambda))\subset\mathrm{T}^{*}Y
. Then
\Gamma_{dt^{\prime}}
and
\Lambda_{Y}
intersect cleanly at
p^{\prime}
with excess
e^{\prime}=e+2\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}(Y/X)_{y}
. Using the additivity property of the Maslov index from
[
KS90
, Theorem A.3.2(vii)]
we get that
\tau_{t^{\prime}}(p^{\prime})=\tau_{t}(p)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mu_{p}(-)

\displaystyle\cong\varphi_{t}(-)|_{x}[-(\tau_{t}(p)+e)/2]

\displaystyle\cong(f^{\dagger}\varphi_{t}(-))|_{y}[-(\tau_{t}(p)+e)/2-\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}(Y/X)_{y}]

\displaystyle\cong(\varphi_{t^{\prime}}f^{\dagger}(-))|_{y}[-(\tau_{t^{\prime}}(p^{\prime})+e^{\prime})/2]

\displaystyle\cong\mu_{p^{\prime}}f^{\dagger}(-),

where we have used smooth base change in the third line.
∎

3.
Fourier transforms via perverse pullbacks

In this section we relate perverse pullbacks to Fourier transforms for sheaves.

3.1.
Conic and monodromic sheaves

In this section we recall the notions of conic and monodromic sheaves and state the contraction principle. It is convenient to phrase the definitions in the setting of topological stacks, i.e. presheaves of
\infty
-groupoids on
\mathrm{Top}^{\mathrm{lch}}
satisfying Čech descent along surjective topological submersions. Analogously to the algebraic situation we may also define topological higher Artin stacks; we refer to
[
Kha25
, Section 8.3]
for more details. The 6-functor formalism
\mathrm{Shv}(-;R)
of sheaves of complexes of
R
-modules on
\mathrm{Top}^{\mathrm{lch}}
extends to topological higher Artin stacks.

Consider the group
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
of positive real numbers under multiplication.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a topological higher Artin stack equipped with an
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-action. A sheaf
\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{Shv}(X;R)
is
conic
if it is
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-equivariant.

We denote by
\mathrm{Shv}_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}(X;R)\subset\mathrm{Shv}(X;R)
the full subcategory of conic sheaves.

Remark 3.2
.

Since
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is contractible, pullback along
X\rightarrow[X/\mathbb{R}_{>0}]
is fully faithful and identifies
\mathrm{Shv}([X/\mathbb{R}_{>0}];R)
with the full subcategory of
\mathrm{Shv}(X;R)
consisting of conic sheaves.

Remark 3.3
.

If
X\in\mathrm{Top}^{\mathrm{lch}}
is a topological space, a sheaf
\mathscr{F}
is conic if, and only if, its restriction to
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-orbits is locally constant.

The key result about conic sheaves is the
contraction principle
. To introduce it, consider the topological monoid
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
of nonnegative real numbers. It contains the subgroup
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
as the group of units.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
X
be a topological stack equipped with an
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-action. The
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-action is
contracting
if it extends to an action of the monoid
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.

For a topological stack
X
equipped with a contracting
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-action let
X^{0}
be the topological stack of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
-equivariant maps
\{0\}\rightarrow X
. We have natural morphisms

i\colon X^{0}\longrightarrow X,\qquad r\colon X\longrightarrow X^{0}

such that
r\circ i=\mathrm{id}_{X^{0}}
. The proof of the following
stacky contraction principle
can be given as in
[
DG15
, Appendix C]
by replacing algebraic stacks with topological higher Artin stacks and
D
-modules with
\mathrm{Shv}(-;R)
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
X
be a topological higher Artin stack with a contracting
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-action such that the contracting locus
X^{0}
is still a topological higher Artin stack. Then the morphisms

i^{!}\cong r_{!}i_{!}i^{!}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{counit}}r_{!},\qquad r_{*}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{unit}}r_{*}i_{*}i^{*}\cong i^{*}

are isomorphisms on
\mathrm{Shv}_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}(X;R)
.

Let us now switch to the setting of complex algebraic stacks. Given a higher Artin stack
X
, we have the corresponding topological higher Artin stack
X^{\mathrm{an}}
. Moreover, a
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action on
X
gives rise to a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on
X^{\mathrm{an}}
. In particular,
X^{\mathrm{an}}
carries an
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-action. Therefore, we may transfer the definition of conic sheaves to the algebraic setting as follows.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
X
be a higher Artin stack equipped with a
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action. A sheaf
\mathscr{F}\in\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{c}}(X)
is
monodromic
if the underlying sheaf on
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is conic with the respect to the
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-action.

Remark 3.7
.

For
X\in\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{sep}\mathrm{ft}}
a sheaf
\mathscr{F}\in\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{c}}(X)
is monodromic in the above sense if, and only if, its restriction to
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-orbits is locally constant.

Consider the monoid scheme
\mathbb{A}^{1}
considered with respect to multiplication, which contains
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
as an open group subscheme of its units.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
X
be a stack. A
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action on
X
is
contracting
if the
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action extends to an action of the monoid
\mathbb{A}^{1}
.

Clearly, given a contracting
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action on a stack
X
, the underlying
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-action on
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is contracting. In particular, the contraction principle applies to monodromic sheaves on higher Artin stacks with contracting
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-actions.

3.2.
Exponential sheaves

In this section we study exponential sheaves and their relationship to vanishing cycle functors. Consider the scaling action of
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
on
\mathbb{R}
and let
[\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{R}_{>0}]
be the corresponding quotient stack. The real
exponential sheaf
is the
\mathbb{R}
-constructible sheaf

\exp_{\mathbb{R}}=(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow[\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{R}_{>0}])_{!}\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\in\mathrm{Shv}([\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{R}_{>0}];R).

We also denote by
\exp_{\mathbb{R}}\in\mathrm{Shv}_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}(\mathbb{R};R)
the corresponding conic sheaf on
\mathbb{R}
.

There is a complex version of the exponential sheaf defined as follows. For the
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
of weight
n>0
we denote by
[\mathbb{A}^{1}/_{n}\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}]
the corresponding quotient stack. Consider the maps

i_{1}\colon\mathrm{pt}\xrightarrow{p_{1}}\mathrm{B}\mu_{n}\xrightarrow{j}[\mathbb{A}^{1}_{n}/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}],

where
j
is the complement of the origin and
i_{1}
is the inclusion of
1\in\mathbb{C}
. The complex
exponential sheaf
is

\exp_{n}=(i_{1})_{*}\mathbf{1}[-1]\in\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{c}}([\mathbb{A}^{1}/_{n}\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}]).

Remark 3.9
.

To explain the analogy, note that if
i_{1}\colon\mathrm{pt}\rightarrow[\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{R}_{>0}]
is the inclusion of
1
, then one can show that
\exp_{\mathbb{R}}\cong(i_{1})_{*}\mathbf{1}[-1]
using the localization sequence.

Proposition 3.10
.

The exponential sheaves satisfy the following properties:

(1)

Consider the correspondence of topological stacks

\textstyle{[\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{R}_{>0}]}
\textstyle{[\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}_{>0}]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{1}}
\scriptstyle{f_{2}}
\textstyle{[\mathbb{C}/_{n}\mathbb{C}^{\times}],}

where
f_{1}
is induced by the real part map
\operatorname{Re}\colon\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
f_{2}
is induced by the
n
-th power map
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Then there is an isomorphism

(f_{2})_{!}f_{1}^{*}\exp_{\mathbb{R}}\cong\exp_{n}.

(2)

Let

s_{d}\colon[\mathbb{A}^{1}/_{nd}\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}]\longrightarrow[\mathbb{A}^{1}/_{n}\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}]

be the map given by the identity on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
and the
d
-th power map on
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
. Then there is an isomorphism

(3.1)

(s_{d})_{!}\exp_{nd}\cong\exp_{n}.

(3)

Assume
R
has characteristic not
2
and let
\mathbf{1}^{-}_{\mathrm{B}\mu_{2}}
be the sign representation of
\mathrm{B}\mu_{2}
viewed as a one-dimensional local system on
\mathrm{B}\mu_{2}
. Then

\exp_{2}\cong(j_{!}\mathbf{1}^{-}_{\mathrm{B}\mu_{2}}\oplus j_{*}\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{B}\mu_{2}})[-1].

Proof.

(1)

Let
\mathbb{C}_{\operatorname{Re}\geq 0}\subset\mathbb{C}
be the subset of complex numbers with nonnegative real part. Using base change we have to show that

\exp^{\prime}_{n}=(\mathbb{C}_{\operatorname{Re}\geq 0}\rightarrow[\mathbb{C}/_{n}\mathbb{C}^{\times}])_{!}\mathbf{1}

is isomorphic to
\exp_{n}
. Consider the inclusion of the origin
i_{0}\colon\mathrm{B}\mathbb{C}^{\times}\rightarrow[\mathbb{C}/_{n}\mathbb{C}^{\times}]
. Let us compute the
!
-pullbacks of
\exp^{\prime}_{n}
along
i_{0}
and its complement
j
:

(a)

We prove that
i_{0}^{!}\exp^{\prime}_{n}=0
. The pullback of
\exp^{\prime}_{n}
along
\mathbb{C}\rightarrow[\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{C}^{\times}]
is conic. Applying the contraction principle (
Proposition

3.5
) on this cover the claim is equivalent to showing that
([\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{C}^{\times}]\rightarrow\mathrm{B}\mathbb{C}^{\times})_{!}\exp^{\prime}_{n}=0
. But this follows from the vanishing of the compactly supported cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathbb{C}_{\geq 0})
.

(b)

We prove that
j^{*}\exp^{\prime}\cong(p_{1})_{!}\mathbf{1}[-1]
. By base change we get
j^{*}\exp^{\prime}\cong(p_{1})_{!}\mathbb{R}\Gamma_{c}(\mathbb{C}_{\geq 0}\setminus\{0\},\mathbf{1})
. Using the localization fiber sequence and the vanishing of the compactly supported cohomology of
\mathbb{C}_{\geq 0}
we get
\mathbb{R}\Gamma_{c}(\mathbb{C}_{\geq 0}\setminus\{0\},\mathbf{1})\cong\mathbf{1}[-1]
.

The above claims imply that
\exp^{\prime}_{n}\cong j_{*}p_{!}\mathbf{1}[-1]\cong j_{*}p_{*}\mathbf{1}[-1]=\exp_{n}
.

(2)

Since
s_{d}
is finite, the claim is equivalent to the statement that
(s_{d})_{*}\exp_{nd}\cong\exp_{n}
which follows from the compatibility of
i_{1}
with the maps
s_{d}
.

(3)

We may factor
i_{1}
as
\mathrm{pt}\xrightarrow{p_{1}}\mathrm{B}\mu_{2}\xrightarrow{j}[\mathbb{A}^{1}/_{2}\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}]
. Therefore,

\exp_{2}\cong(j_{*}\mathbf{1}^{-}_{\mathrm{B}\mu_{2}}\oplus j_{*}\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{B}\mu_{2}})[-1].

Next we will show that the natural morphism
j_{!}\mathbf{1}^{-}_{\mathrm{B}\mu_{2}}\rightarrow j_{*}\mathbf{1}^{-}_{\mathrm{B}\mu_{2}}
is an isomorphism. This is equivalent to showing that the
!
-pullback of
j_{!}\mathbf{1}^{-}_{\mathrm{B}\mu_{2}}
along the inclusion of the origin
\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}\rightarrow[\mathbb{A}^{1}/_{2}\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}]
is zero. By the stacky contraction principle
[
DG15
, Appendix C]
the
!
-pullback along
\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}\rightarrow[\mathbb{A}^{1}/_{2}\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}]
is isomorphic to the
!
-pushforward along
[\mathbb{A}^{1}/_{2}\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}]\rightarrow\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
. But the
!
-pushforward of
\mathbf{1}^{-1}_{\mathrm{B}\mu_{2}}
along
\mathrm{B}\mu_{2}\rightarrow\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
is zero.

∎

Remark 3.11
.

Proposition

3.10
(3) identifies the above definition of the exponential sheaf with the one considered in
[
BSV24

in the case

Remark 3.12
.

The tower
\{\exp_{n}\in\mathbf{D}([\mathbb{A}^{1}/_{n}\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}])\}
of exponential sheaves together with isomorphisms (
3.1
) may be thought of as an approximation to a (non-existent) exponential sheaf on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
. This is motivated by the following two results:

(1)

Let
R=\mathbb{C}
and consider the exponential
D
-module on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
. Then its
!
-pushforward along
\mathbb{A}^{1}\rightarrow[\mathbb{A}^{1}/_{n}\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}]
is isomorphic to
\exp_{n}
via the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence.

(2)

Consider the setting of schemes over a field of characteristic
p>0
and let

for a prime
\ell\neq p
. Choose an additive character
\psi\colon\mathbb{F}_{p}\rightarrow R^{\times}
and let
\mathscr{L}_{\psi}
be the associated Artin–Schreier
\ell

\mathbb{A}^{1}
. Then its

-pushforward along
\mathbb{A}^{1}\rightarrow[\mathbb{A}^{1}/_{n}\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}]
is isomorphic to
\exp_{n}
in the setting of
\ell
-adic sheaves; see
[
Lau03

for the case

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
X\in\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{sep}\mathrm{ft}}
be a scheme equipped with a contracting
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action and a function
t\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}
of weight
n>0
, so that it descends to a morphism
\overline{t}\colon[X/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}]\rightarrow[\mathbb{A}^{1}/_{n}\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}]
. Let
X^{0}\subset X
be the fixed point locus. Then there are the following natural isomorphisms:

(1)

For a monodromic
\mathscr{F}\in\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{c}}(X)
we have

(t^{-1}(0)\rightarrow X^{0})_{!}\varphi_{t}(\mathscr{F})\cong(X\rightarrow X^{0})_{!}(\mathscr{F}\otimes(\operatorname{Re}t)^{*}\exp_{\mathbb{R}}).

(2)

For
\mathscr{F}\in\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{c}}([X/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}])
we have

(t^{-1}(0)\rightarrow X^{0})_{!}\varphi_{t}(X\rightarrow[X/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}])^{*}\mathscr{F}\cong([X/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}]\rightarrow X^{0})_{!}(\mathscr{F}\otimes\overline{t}^{*}\exp_{n}).

Proof.

Let

X_{\operatorname{Re}\geq 0}=\{x\in X^{\mathrm{an}}\mid\operatorname{Re}t(x)\geq 0\}.

For a monodromic
\mathscr{F}\in\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{c}}(X)
let
\overline{\mathscr{F}}\in\mathrm{Shv}([X^{\mathrm{an}}/\mathbb{R}_{>0}];R)

\displaystyle(t^{-1}(0)\rightarrow X^{0})_{!}\varphi_{t}\mathscr{F}

\displaystyle\cong(t^{-1}(0)\rightarrow X^{0})_{!}(t^{-1}(0)\rightarrow X_{\operatorname{Re}\geq 0})^{!}(X_{\operatorname{Re}\geq 0}\rightarrow X^{\mathrm{an}})^{*}(X^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow[X^{\mathrm{an}}/\mathbb{R}_{>0}])^{*}\overline{\mathscr{F}}

\displaystyle\cong([X^{\mathrm{an}}/\mathbb{R}_{>0}]\rightarrow X^{0})_{!}(\overline{\mathscr{F}}\otimes t^{*}\exp_{\mathbb{R}}),

where the first isomorphism uses Verdier self-duality of vanishing cycles, the second isomorphism uses the contraction principle (

The second statement follows from the first statement and the relationship between

(1).
∎

3.3.
Fourier transforms for sheaves

Let
B\in\mathrm{Top}^{\mathrm{lch}}
be a topological space and
E\rightarrow B
a real vector bundle. Consider the natural pairing

\mathrm{ev}^{\mathbb{R}}\colon[E/\mathbb{R}_{>0}]\times_{B}[E^{\vee}/\mathbb{R}_{>0}]\longrightarrow[\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{R}_{>0}]

and denote by

\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}\colon[E/\mathbb{R}_{>0}]\times_{B}[E^{\vee}/\mathbb{R}_{>0}]\longrightarrow[E/\mathbb{R}_{>0}],\qquad\mathrm{pr}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}}\colon[E/\mathbb{R}_{>0}]\times_{B}[E^{\vee}/\mathbb{R}_{>0}]\longrightarrow[E^{\vee}/\mathbb{R}_{>0}]

the projections. We refer to
[
KS90
, Chapter 3.7]
and
[
Kin25
, Section 2.4]
for more details on the following notion.

Definition 3.14
.

The
Fourier–Sato transform

\mathrm{FS}\colon\mathrm{Shv}([E/\mathbb{R}_{>0}];R)\rightarrow\mathrm{Shv}([E^{\vee}/\mathbb{R}_{>0}];R)
is

\mathrm{FS}(\mathscr{F})=\mathrm{pr}^{\mathbb{R}}_{2,!}(\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{\mathbb{R},*}(\mathscr{F})\otimes\mathrm{ev}^{\mathbb{R},*}\exp_{\mathbb{R}}).

Let us now relate the Fourier–Sato transform to perverse pullbacks. For this, consider an lfp derived Artin stack
B
and a vector bundle
E\rightarrow B
. Let

\mathrm{ev}\colon E\times_{B}E^{\vee}\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1},\qquad\mathrm{pr}_{1}\colon E\times_{B}E^{\vee}\longrightarrow E,\qquad\mathrm{pr}_{2}\colon E\times_{B}E^{\vee}\longrightarrow E^{\vee}

be the corresponding morphisms.

The projection
\mathrm{pr}_{2}
is smooth and

\mathbb{R}\mathrm{Crit}_{E\times_{B}E^{\vee}/E}(\mathrm{ev})\cong E^{\vee}.

In particular, the morphism

u_{E^{\vee}/E}\colon E^{\vee}\xrightarrow{p_{E^{\vee}}}B\xrightarrow{0_{E}}E

carries a natural oriented relative exact
(-1)
-shifted symplectic structure.

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
B
be an lft higher Artin stack and
E\rightarrow B
a vector bundle. For a monodromic complex
\mathscr{F}\in\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{c}}(E)
there is a natural isomorphism

u_{E^{\vee}/E}^{\varphi}(\mathscr{F})\cong\mathrm{FS}(\mathscr{F})[\mathrm{rk}E].

Proof.

Using base change for the Fourier–Sato transform and perverse pullbacks we reduce to the case when
B
is an affine scheme of finite type. By
Theorem

1.9
(1) we have

u_{E^{\vee}/E}^{\varphi}(\mathscr{F})\cong(\phi_{\mathrm{ev}}\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{\dagger}\mathscr{F})|_{\{0\}\times E^{\vee}}.

Since
(\phi_{\mathrm{ev}}\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{\dagger}\mathscr{F})
is supported on
\{0\}\times E^{\vee}
where
\mathrm{ev}
takes the zero value, we have

u_{E^{\vee}/E}^{\varphi}(\mathscr{F})\cong(\mathrm{ev}^{-1}(0)\rightarrow E^{\vee})_{!}\varphi_{\mathrm{ev}}\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{\dagger}\mathscr{F}.

Consider the
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action on
E\times_{B}E^{\vee}
given by the weight
1
action on
E
and the weight
0
action on
E^{\vee}
. The fixed point locus of the action is given by
E^{\vee}
and with respect to this
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action the function
\mathrm{ev}\colon E\times_{B}E^{\vee}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}
has weight
1
. The claim then follows from
Proposition

3.13
(1).
∎

.

4.
Dimensional reduction for perverse pullbacks

In this section we analyze perverse pullbacks for relative
(-1)
-shifted cotangent bundles and prove the following relative dimensional reduction theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
g\colon Y\rightarrow B
be an lfp geometric morphism of derived stacks and suppose that
B^{\mathrm{cl}}
is an lft higher Artin stack. Consider the morphism
\pi\colon\mathrm{T}^{*}[-1](Y/B)\to B
equipped with its canonical oriented relative exact
(-1)
-shifted symplectic structure. Then there is a canonical isomorphism

Our next goal is to describe orientations of the relative exact

-shifted symplectic structure on

Lemma 5.4
.

For an affine scheme

together with a morphism

Since the diagram (

The description of orientations will be given in terms of motivic cohomology which we briefly recall. For a stack

be the nonconnective

and let

be its underlying space, so that for a perfect complex

and the determinant map factors as

naturally in

Let
\mathcal{SH}(k)
be the stable motivic
\infty

\mathbf{Map}_{\mathcal{SH}(k)}(-,-)
the mapping spectrum in
\mathcal{SH}(k)

naturally in smooth schemes

Moreover, there is a motivic filtration on

for

a smooth scheme which can be realized as follows. Let

with associated graded

the Zariski cohomology of the motivic complexes of weight

as in
[

. The motivic filtration on
\mathbf{K}(-)
, as well as the motivic complexes

Taking motivic cohomology mod 2 we get morphisms

\mathbb{R}\Gamma(X,\mathbb{Z}(j))\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\Gamma_{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(X,\mu_{2}^{\otimes j}),

where on the right we consider étale cohomology. For a vector bundle

\mathrm{ch}_{n}\colon\mathrm{Fil}^{n}\mathrm{K}(X)\longrightarrow|\mathbb{R}\Gamma(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))[2n]|,

has the following explicit description in low weights:

(0)

We have the following functoriality properties of the motivic filtration.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
X
be a scheme of finite type.

(1)

Let
E
be a vector bundle of rank
r+1
over
X
. Let
\pi\colon\mathbb{P}(E)\rightarrow X
be the projection and
\mathscr{O}(-1)
the tautological line bundle on
\mathbb{P}(E)

\sum_{i=0}^{r}([\mathscr{O}]-[\mathscr{O}(-1)])^{i}\pi^{*}\colon\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}\mathrm{Fil}^{n-i}\mathbf{K}(X)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Fil}^{n}\mathbf{K}(\mathbb{P}(E))

(2)

In the notation of the previous part,
\pi_{*}\colon\mathbf{K}(\mathbb{P}(E))\rightarrow\mathbf{K}(X)

functorially in

.

(3)

Let

be a regular closed immersion of codimension

. Then the pushforward

refines to a morphism

i_{*}\colon\mathrm{Fil}^{n}\mathbf{K}(Z)/2\longrightarrow\mathrm{Fil}^{n+d}\mathbf{K}(X)/2

functorially in
X
with respect to base change.

Proof.

(1)

As the motivic filtration is bounded
[
EM23
, Theorem 4.12]
, it is enough to show that the morphism on the associated graded level is an isomorphism. Passing to the associated graded we get the composite

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}\mathbb{R}\Gamma(X,\mathbb{Z}(n-i))[2n-2i]

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\pi^{*}\otimes[\mathscr{O}(1)]^{i}}\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}\mathbb{R}\Gamma(\mathbb{P}(E),\mathbb{Z}(n-i))[2n-2i]\otimes(\mathbb{R}\Gamma(\mathbb{P}(E),\mathbb{Z}(1))[2])^{\otimes i}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\Gamma(\mathbb{P}(E),\mathbb{Z}(n))[2n]

which is an isomorphism by the projective bundle formula
[
EM23
, Theorem 5.24(2)]
.

(2)

We use the description of the filtered pieces
\mathrm{Fil}^{n}\mathbf{K}(\mathbb{P}(E))
from the previous part. Using the projection formula as well as the vanishing of cohomology of
\mathscr{O}(-i)
for

on

we get that the composite

\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}\mathrm{Fil}^{n-i}\mathbf{K}(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Fil}^{n}\mathbf{K}(\mathbb{P}(E))\xrightarrow{\pi_{*}}\mathbf{K}(X)

is given by the obvious inclusions
\mathrm{Fil}^{n-i}\mathbf{K}(X)\rightarrow\mathbf{K}(X)
for each

. In particular,
\pi_{*}\colon\mathrm{Fil}^{n}\mathbf{K}(\mathbb{P}(E))\rightarrow\mathbf{K}(X)
factors through
\mathrm{Fil}^{n-r}\mathbf{K}(X)
.

(3)

Using the localization fiber sequence
\mathbf{K}(X~\textrm{on}~Z)\to\mathbf{K}(X)\to\mathbf{K}(X\setminus Z)
we see that the pushforward
i_{*}\colon\mathbf{K}(Z)\rightarrow\mathbf{K}(X)
lifts to
i_{*}\colon\mathbf{K}(Z)\rightarrow\mathbf{K}(X~\textrm{on}~Z)
. Our goal is to show that mod 2 we can lift this morphism to a morphism

i_{*}\colon\mathrm{Fil}^{n}\mathbf{K}(Z)/2\longrightarrow\mathrm{Fil}^{n+d}\mathbf{K}(X~\textrm{on}~Z)/2

functorially in
X
. Using deformation to the normal cone as well as the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy invariance of
\mathbf{K}(-)/2
(see
[
Wei89
, Proposition 1.6]
where we recall our standing assumption that the ground field

has characteristic not

) we are reduced to showing the claim for

the zero section
Z\rightarrow E
of a vector bundle
\pi\colon E\rightarrow Z
of rank
d
. That is, we have to show that the morphism
i_{*}\colon\mathbf{K}(Z)/2\rightarrow\mathbf{K}(E~\textrm{on}~Z)/2
lifts to a morphism

\mathrm{Fil}^{n}\mathbf{K}(Z)/2\longrightarrow\mathrm{Fil}^{n+d}\mathbf{K}(E~\textrm{on}~Z)/2

functorially in

. The projection formula shows that it is compatible with the
\mathbf{K}(Z)/2
-module structure on both sides (where on the right the action is given via

). Thus, we are reduced to showing that the Thom class

(i.e. the class of

We claim that this map is, in fact, an isomorphism. The claim is local, so we may assume

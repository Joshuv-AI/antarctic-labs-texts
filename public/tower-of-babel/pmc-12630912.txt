Frass fertilizer application enhances agronomic and economic performance of Chinese cabbage intercropped with desmodium under vertical farming system
Authors: Andrew Agrey Abiya, David Mfuti Kupesa, Dennis Beesigamukama, Catherine Nyambura Ngamau, Saliou Niassy, Chrysantus Mbi Tanga
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractLow soil fertility, coupled with reduced land for cultivation, has favoured vertical farming as an alternative to traditional agriculture among urban dwellers. This study focused on the effects of frass fertilizer application on yield, pest suppression, and economic performance of growing Chinese cabbage intercropped with Desmodium under multi-storey gardens. Treatments included NPK (10 kg/ha), liquid and powdered frass fertilizer, Desmodium intercrop, their combinations, and an unamended control. Liquid frass fertilizer produced 1.6-fold and 1.4-fold higher yield (27.40 ± 0.07 kg/garden), significantly outperforming the control and NPK, respectively. Plots treated with powdered frass fertilizer and Desmodium had increased yield with significantly reduced pest incidences and damage score. Crude protein [15–20%] and ash content of vegetables from plots treated with different frass fertilizer types, Desmodium and their combinations was significantly increased than those from plots with NPK or control [8–13%]. Vegetable yield from plots treated with liquid frass fertilizer, translated to higher net profit margin with 75.14% return on investment. These findings demonstrated that the integration of frass fertilizer into multi-storey gardening in urban areas would open new ways of enhancing sustainable and safe food production which is the cornerstone of addressing food insecurity given the growing challenges of climate change and population growth.Keywords: Insect frass fertilizer, Soil health, Pest suppression, Profitability, Chinese cabbage, Food securitySubject terms: Ecology, Ecology, Environmental sciences, Plant sciences

Chinese cabbage (Brassica rapa L. subsp. chinensis), an exotic vegetable that doesn’t undergo head formation during its growth period, is valued for its high content of vitamins, minerals, and dietary fiber. Native to China, it is now widely cultivated, including in Kenya. As a heavy feeder, Chinese cabbage significantly depletes N, P and K from the soil, making its production highly dependent on soil fertility. In addition, pests and diseases severely impact its productivity1,2.

In Sub-Saharan Africa, vegetable farming often occurs in nutrient-poor, acidic soils, necessitating continuous fertilizer application to maintain yields3. Fertilization is crucial in supporting plant physiological processes and nutrient uptake4. Many farmers rely heavily on synthetic fertilizers, herbicides, and pesticides to boost productivity and manage pests and diseases. However, overuse of these chemicals has led to soil degradation, biodiversity loss, health risks, and reduced crop quality.

Growing interest in food safety and environmental sustainability has encouraged the adoption of organic farming practices5. Organic fertilizers and companion cropping are now being explored as alternatives to synthetic chemical inputs, offering potential improvements in soil health, yield, pest suppression, and overall crop quality6. While pesticides offer benefits in pest control, their improper or unregulated use, especially in vegetable cultivation, can cause harm to human health, reduce produce quality, and pollute the environment7,8.

Black soldier fly [Hermetia illucens L.] (Diptera: Stratiomyidae) (BSF) frass, an organic fertilizer derived from the bioconversion of organic waste by the larvae, has shown promise as a sustainable soil amendment. Studies report that BSF frass fertilizer enhances plant growth, yield, nutrient uptake, nitrogen, and water use efficiency, and even disease resistance9–13. It can also serve as an alternative to peat in potted vegetable cultivation14.

Desmodium intortum (Mill.) Urb is legume fodder, an eco-friendly companion plant, widely used in cereal and vegetable cropping systems known as “push-pull technology” to manage pests like fall armyworm, stemborers15–18. Legumes are known for nitrogen fixation and carbon sequestration, mobilizing beneficial microorganisms for enhanced soil health19,20. Desmodium is an excellent source of animal fodder for small ruminants and rabbits hence very attractive in a circular economy system. Combining insect-based fertilizers like BSF frass fertilizer with companion crops such as Desmodium offers a viable strategy for sustainable vegetable production. These practices are particularly valuable in organic systems and urban agriculture, where space and inputs are limited, and demand for affordable, safe, nutritious food is high. Vertical gardening, using structures like multistorey gardens, provides an innovative solution to maximize production in space-constrained urban areas21.

Despite the potential of these integrated approaches, research on their combined impact, especially in vertical garden systems is under explored. This study aims at addressing the present gap by evaluating the effects of BSF frass fertilizer and Desmodium intercropping on yield, pest management, and economic returns in Chinese cabbage production. The findings aim to inform sustainable, low-cost agricultural practices that enhance urban food security and environmental resilience.

Materials and methods

The experimental site

The experiment was conducted in two trials, covering both short and long rain seasons from March 2023 to April 2024, on wonder multistorey gardens established at the International Centre of Insect Physiology and Ecology (icipe) (1.221° S, 36.896° E; 1616 m above sea level) research farm in Nairobi, Kenya. The mean monthly minimum and maximum temperatures range between 12 and 29 °C. The site has a bimodal rainfall pattern with an average annual rainfall of 787 mm.

The first season was conducted during the long rains season between March and May, while the second trial was done during the short rains season that starts in October and end in December. The soils of the study sites are predominantly sandy clay, well-drained, and classified as humic Nitisols22. The evapotranspiration rate is 2400 mm year− 1.

Construction and preparation of experimental units (multistorey gardens)

Twenty-four wonder multistorey gardens were constructed in the field, each covering an area of approximately 1.5 m² (Fig. 1a and h). The gardens were made using high-density polyethylene (HDPE) material sourced from Amiran Kenya Ltd., Nairobi. The HDPE sheets were cut into various sizes and joined to form circular rings. These rings were stacked vertically up to six levels with decreasing diameters to create a pyramid-like structure filled with soil, forming terraces for planting21.

Before filling the terraces, the field soil was dug, ploughed, and levelled using a hoe and shovel. According to the physical and chemical analysis referenced from Anyega11, the experimental soil was loamy with high acidity and low nutrient content: pH 5.8, total nitrogen 0.05%, phosphorus 5.0 ppm, potassium 0.84 cmol/kg, organic matter 0.42%, and total carbon 0.24%.

After constructing the multistorey gardens, Desmodium cuttings were planted in alternating rows in the designated gardens (Fig. 1b, c, g and h). In selected units, powdered black soldier fly frass fertilizer was mixed uniformly with the soil at a rate of 4 kg per unit (Fig. 1a), while in other units, the liquid black soldier fly frass fertilizer was sprayed on weekly basis (Fig. 1b and f), the inorganic fertilizer was applied at the recommended rate during planting stage (Fig. 1d and g) and the control was unamended (Fig. 1e).

Chinese cabbage and desmodium planting

Two-week-old healthy Brassica rapa L. subsp. chinensis (non-heading Chinese cabbage) seedlings were sourced from Kimplanter Seedlings Company in Kiambu, Kenya, and used as the test crop. This exotic vegetable is widely grown and consumed in Kenya’s urban and peri-urban areas due to its high nutritional value, short growth cycle, and high yield potential. Desmodium intortum seedlings were transplanted onto alternating rows of the selected gardens two weeks before cabbage transplanting, allowing the intercrop to establish in advance (Figs. 2a-b). Gardens without intercrop remained bare for comparison.

(a, b) Multistorey Garden systems used for the study. It shows stacked vertical gardens cultivated with (a) Chinese cabbage and Desmodium and (b) sole Chinese cabbage, used to assess impact of BSF frass fertilizer and intercropping on yield, pest control, and economic returns.

The experiment assessed the effects of different formulations of black soldier fly frass fertilizer (liquid and powdered), greenleaf Desmodium intercrop (Desmodium intortum) which is a nitrogen-fixing, perennial legume valued for its high-protein forage, soil fertility improvement, and role in integrated pest management through its ability to suppress pests and parasitic weeds and their combinations on the growth, yield, and pest incidence in Chinese cabbage grown in low-cost, multistorey wonder gardens under open-field conditions. These treatments were compared to conventional NPK fertilizer and an untreated control.

Management of experimental crops

Chinese cabbage (Brassica rapa L. subsp. Chinensis) seedlings were transplanted into the multistorey garden structures at a uniform spacing as per Abiya21. Irrigation was applied regularly to sustain optimal soil moisture, and weeds were controlled manually by hand picking. No fertilizers or pesticides were used; nutrient inputs and pest management relied solely on the experimental treatments (liquid BSFF, powdered BSFF, and Desmodium intercrop). Harvesting was carried out at physiological maturity of the cabbage.

Fertilizer application (black soldier fly frass fertilizer and NPK)

Powdered black soldier fly frass fertilizer (PBSFFF) used in this study was produced from BSF larvae fed on brewery spent grains (sourced from Kenya Breweries Ltd) and composted at the International Centre of Insect Physiology and Ecology (icipe), Nairobi, following the method of Beesigamukama23. A well-characterized liquid formulation of BSFFF was also sourced from icipe’s research and development section (Table 1).

Experimental design and layout

The two trials were conducted using wonder multistorey gardens with a similar layout to assess the effects of liquid and powdered black soldier fly frass fertilizer formulations and Desmodium intercropping on the growth, pest incidence, and yield of Chinese cabbage. The layout was a randomized complete block design (RCBD) with three replications in each trial (Fig. 3). Each replicate consisted of 10 plants, distributed on three terraces in each wonder multistorey garden at a spacing of 45 cm × 45 cm (Fig. 2).

Randomised complete block design set up of multistorey gardens in the experimental field. T0-T7 indicates treatments: T0-control/no amendments, T1-inorganic NPK fertilizer, T2-powdered BSF frass fertilizer, T3-liquid BSF frass fertilizer, T4-Desmodium intercrop, T5-inorganic NPK and Desmodium, T6-powdered BSF frass fertilizer and Desmodium, and T7-liquid BSF frass fertilizer and Desmodium.

Determination of growth and yield parameters

Collection of data for growth (plant height, leaf number, leaf area, and SPAD values) was done weekly (starting from 2 weeks after transplanting). Plant height was determined by measuring the vertical length of each plant using a meter rule; leaf number was determined by counting the leaves manually per plant; SPAD value, was recorded using a chlorophyll meter (Konica Minolta Co., Japan), taken from three points on the third leaf while leaf area was determined by using the model {LA = 0.34(LWxLL)} developed by Omolaiye24, where LA = leaf area, LL = leaf length and LW = leaf width.

Above-ground parts were harvested six weeks after transplanting and weighed. Fresh weight was evaluated based on the weight of aboveground parts per selected plants. Chinese cabbage from all the treatments were harvested on a single day in both the first and second trials. The harvested leaves were air dried and ground into powder using a mechanical grinder for proximate analysis. The crude fat and crude protein were determined by Soxhlet extraction method; ash, carbohydrates, fiber and the moisture contents were determined by the methods described by the Association of Official Analytical Chemists25.

Determination of pest occurrences and damage on Chinese cabbage

The pest incidences were determined through scouting and physical observation of different insect pests attacking the test plants. This involved visual inspection, yellow sticky and quadrat sampling, and specimen preservation with hand lenses, forceps, vials, and 70% ethanol for morphological identification at the icipe taxonomy library. The main insect that attacked the test crops was identified as Diamond Back Moth (DBM). It was counted and recorded per plant every week, and the damage it caused to Chinese cabbage leaves was determined. The scale of damage caused by this main pest identified was recorded using the bioleaf foliar analysis method as per Machado26, and the percentage leaf damage score per plant was categorized according to Rouag and Makhlouf27.

Economic analysis

The study assessed the economic benefits of different treatments by analyzing input usage levels, costs, and both gross and net profits28. The total cost of cultivating Chinese cabbage was calculated by getting the sum of fixed and variable costs. Fixed costs included garden construction materials and capital investments, while variable costs covered expenses such as seeds and fertilizers distributed throughout the production period. Labor costs were also factored in. The analysis was based on current market prices for both inputs and outputs. Gross and net returns were calculated to evaluate the success of Chinese cabbage production. Gross return was determined by multiplying the yield by the market selling price of Chinese cabbage, while net return was obtained by subtracting the cultivation costs from the gross return.

Data analysis

Plant growth parameters and insect data were assessed for normality using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Data conforming to normal distribution assumptions were subjected to analysis of variance (ANOVA), and mean separations were performed using Tukey’s HSD test at a 5% significance level. All statistical analyses were conducted using the repeated measures procedure in R software29. The cabbage yield and proximate composition were analyzed using normal ANOVA, while leaf damage was analyzed using the general linear mixed model with “quasi-Poisson” and the “MASS” package. Computation of least squares means was done using the “lsmeans” package, followed by mean separation using an adjusted Tukey’s method implemented using the “cld” function from the “multicompView” package. All the statistical analyses were conducted using R statistical software version 4.5.1.

Results

Effect of different frass fertilizer formulations and Desmodium intercropping on Chinese cabbage height and leaf number under wonder multistorey gardens

The plant heights were significantly (p < 0.05) influenced by different fertilizer formulations and Desmodium-cabbage intercropping (Table 3). Treatment plots with liquid BSFFF yielded Chinese cabbage plants with higher heights (32.00 ± 0.54 cm) after 5 weeks of transplanting. In contrast, the lowest mean plant height was recorded in the control treatment with no fertilizer application (29.40 ± 0.81 cm). At the early growth stage, around 2 weeks, the highest number of plants with maximum height were observed in plots with NPK application whereas the lowest plants height was recorded in the control (10.60 ± 0.46 cm). The combined application of frass fertilizers (liquid and powdered) and Desmodium intercropping significantly influenced the plant height of Chinese cabbage with an increasing trend throughout the experiment (Table 3). From the planting date up to five weeks of growth after transplanting, all treatments showed significantly higher plant height than the control.

In weeks 2 and 3, the number of leaves per plant showed no significant difference among the treatments and the control, while in weeks 4 and 5, the mean number of leaves was significantly different among the treatments (Table 3). Effect of different frass fertilizer formulations and Desmodium intercropping on Chinese cabbage leaf area and SPAD values under wonder multistorey gardens

The highest average SPAD value (42.90 ± 0.80) was recorded at week 5 in the treatment amended with Desmodium alone. Effect of different frass fertilizer and desmodium intercropping on major pests incidences and damage score on Chinese cabbage plants

During the growth period, various insect pests were observed in the Chinese cabbage plots. These included Plutella xylostella Linnaeus, 1758 (diamondback moth), Brevicoryne brassicae Linnaeus, 1758 (cabbage aphid), Hellula undalis Fabricius, 1794 (cabbage webworm), Bemisia tabaci Gennadius, 1889 (whitefly), and Trichoplusia ni Hübner, 1800–1803 (cabbage looper). All these pest species were present across all treatments, indicating a widespread occurrence throughout the study area. Among them, Plutella xylostella (DBM) emerged as the most significant pest, causing noticeable damage to the cabbage crop. This study therefore identified DBM as the most significant pests for Chinese cabbage and collected the data of this pest for analysis.

The number of P. xylostella larvae varied significantly among the treatments (p < 0.05). Control plots, which received no fertilizer or Desmodium amendment, recorded the highest larval counts. In contrast, plots treated with Desmodium and either liquid or powdered frass fertilizer and their combinations had the lowest numbers of diamondback moth larvae, suggesting a possible pest-suppressive effect of these organic amendments. Notably, pest populations in plots treated with inorganic NPK fertilizer were not statistically different from those in the control plots, indicating a limited impact of the chemical fertilizer on diamondback moth infestation (Fig. 4).

Desmodium roots enhance nitrogen fixation in the soil and promote growth of Chinese cabbage while the aboveground parts of Desmodium attract natural emenies of pests which help reduce pest incidences.

The combination of frass fertilizer with Desmodium intercropping significantly reduced the number of DBM larvae compared to all other treatments and the control, as shown in Table 5. This suggests a synergistic effect between the frass fertilizer amendment and Desmodium in suppressing pest populations more effectively than when either was used alone.

Effect of different frass fertilizers and Desmodium intercropping on yield parameters of Chinese Cabbage

Chinese cabbage yield

Fresh yield per vertical garden varied significantly among the treatments and the control (p < 0.05). Yields ranged from 17.20 ± 0.07 kg in the control plots to a maximum of 27.40 ± 0.07 kg in gardens treated with liquid frass fertilizer (Fig. 5a). Although the highest yield was recorded in the liquid frass fertilizer treatment followed by the treatment combining liquid frass fertilizer and desmodium, there were no significant differences among treatments receiving liquid frass fertilizer, powdered frass fertilizer, or their combinations with desmodium. However, the control gardens, which received no fertilizer amendment and desmodium intercrop, consistently produced significantly lower fresh yields. In contrast, dry matter content did not differ significantly among the treatments, indicating that the amendments primarily affected fresh biomass accumulation rather than the proportion of dry matter (p < 0.05) (Fig. 5b).

Discussion

The overall results showed that application of liquid black soldier fly frass fertilizer had a more remarkable effect on the growth performance of Chinese cabbage than other treatments. The rapid release of nutrients from foliar black soldier fly frass fertilizer spray and inorganic fertilizer (NPK) to plants might have resulted in the rapid performance of growth of Chinese cabbage. Powdered BSFFF, which had slow nutrient release capacity, experienced slow plant growth but increased continuously and was in accordance with both NPK and liquid frass fertilizer during the late growth stages of Chinese cabbage. The plant height results under the present experiment are supported by Farjana30 and Beesigamukama10, who noted improved growth of crops when using black soldier fly frass fertilizer.

The experiment revealed a strong link between fertilizer application and vegetative growth, i.e., high plant height and number of leaves observed on plants under fertilizer application, especially with higher nitrogen and potassium levels. The results obtained from the experiment on number of leaves per plant were the highest in treatments containing frass fertilizer, which agreed with Shin31, Vimala32, and Rickta33.

Nitrogen is vital for processes like cell elongation, division, and protein synthesis, while potassium plays a key role in biochemical and physiological processes, including cell division, elongation, and carbohydrate metabolism34. When N and K are provided to plants through fertilizer application, they collectively promote vegetative growth and overall plant development35, and the present study gave consistent results on plant growth rate, which agreed with the findings of El-Toham36, Baloch37 and Shyala38. The study showed that plants could obtain these nutrients from the frass fertilizer and NPK fertilizer used.

The synergy of fertilizer and Desmodium led to maximum plant height by providing nutrients and an ideal growth environment provided by intercropping. Favourable soil structure and moisture due to intercropping39 further promoted plant development, highlighting the importance of nutrient management and intercropping for optimal plant growth and productivity. From the experimental results, it was confirmed that the application of fertilizer has a notable impact on leaf characteristics that included leaf length, width, and SPAD values. The study showed high leaf length and width as well as chlorophyll content which was in accordance with Mohd40 findings on high SPAD values in cabbage subjected under different fertilization and intercropping systems.

The diamondback moth larvae feed on the leaves of non-heading cabbage, thus inducing defoliation, which reduces the number of marketable leaves41,42. The diamondback moth (DBM) was majorly observed on cabbage plants as early as 2 weeks after transplanting and remained on until harvest. We found that overall, Chinese cabbage treated with frass fertilizer had lower pest populations than those treated with synthetic fertilizer and control (no fertilizer).

The presence of chitin in frass fertilizer has been reported to offer extra protection to crops against pests as compared to when other fertilizers that do not contain chitin are used43 in fact, pests such as DBM are more attracted to plants that receive inorganic fertilizer application as food becomes abundant to the pests44. However, other studies have also shown reduced pest populations on organically fertilized plants compared to synthetically fertilized plants in many different systems, including aphids on tomato45, moths on Brassica plants46, and aphids in winter wheat47.

Plants can assimilate the microbes present in organic fertilizer into chemical defences to deter pests46,48. Frass fertilizers can also provide beneficial bacterial and fungal microbes that directly and indirectly augment defences against pests48,49. Black soldier fly frass fertilizer also contains chitin remains that have the potential to manage arthropod pests12.

Microbial activity, in addition to organic matter, can stimulate the production of defensive chemicals, as shown in Brassica crops48,50,51. Organic fertilizer amendments can also slow the uptake of macronutrients in plants, making them less likely hosts than synthetically fertilized plants48. Overall, Chinese cabbage grown with frass fertilizer supported the lowest pest populations.

Desmodium intercropping reduced pest population because of the crop diversity that hindered pest movement within the garden rows52. Also, Sheehan53 stated that if nonhost plants separate susceptible plants, it can serve as a physical barrier to the pest; hence, the susceptible plant will suffer less damage. Also, Desmodium intercropping showed significant potential to repel lepidopteran pests54 and to attract natural enemies such as ladybirds and spiders that fed on DBM larvae, thus reducing their population as well as the damage effect.

This was in line with a study by Andow55, who stated that an increase in plant diversity in a field provides an opportunity for natural enemies to survive. Thus, we recommend growers practice intercropping to improve plant diversity and, hence, insect diversity. They should also use frass fertilizer as a source of plant nutrition.

Synthetic fertilizer has been shown to significantly increase cabbage yields, thus it is economically beneficial for growers to choose synthetic fertilizers over organic fertilizers, despite their inability to condition soil and increased cost of production. In this study, however, the highest fresh yield of Chinese cabbage was found when liquid frass fertilizer was applied. The increase in yield may be due to improved functioning of biochemical processes, including photosynthesis that occurred in this treatment56.

In previous studies, a strong relationship between increasing N content in plant tissues of leaves, leaf chlorophyll concentration and photosynthesis were found57,58. Relative to control, all the treatments significantly increased crude fat, carbohydrate, and ash content of Chinese cabbage under desmodium intercropping system. The results showed higher nutrient content in the treatments than the control experiment which justifies the use of fertilizers in improving the nutrient quality of the crops.

The treatment with the highest cost of cultivating Chinese cabbage was NPK + DES (combined application of inorganic NPK and desmodium intercropping), the lowest was for the control gardens. This was as a result of high prices of inorganic NPK fertilizer that was used as fertilizer source which was in line with previous studies by Hernandez and Torero59, who noted that inorganic fertilizer is costly, being a bulky commodity that is primarily produced overseas.

The Chinese cabbage return was highest with frass fertilizer and NPK fertilizer additions and the lowest in the control application. However, the liquid frass fertilizer (LBSFFF) treatment gave the highest results in yield and was more profitable in economic evaluation making it more advantageous among the treatments.The benefit of combining fertilizer with desmodium intercropping over fertilizer alone was not significant, instead, lower overall inputs were recorded with fertilizer alone relative to the combined treatments. A benefit cost analysis showed that all treatments resulted in increase in economic benefits compared to the control.

Given that multistorey gardens are increasingly promoted in sub-Saharan Africa and other regions with similar agro-ecological and socio-economic conditions, the demonstrated benefits of enhanced yield, pest suppression, and profitability suggest strong potential for scaling the technology60. Moreover, the durability of multistorey structures, coupled with the eco-friendly nature of insect-based fertilizers, enhances their suitability for adoption across diverse geographic regions, thereby contributing to food security and household income.

Conclusion

The observed response of Chinese cabbage to liquid and powdered black soldier fly frass fertilizers, as well as the combined application of liquid or powdered frass fertilizer with Desmodium, suggests that they can be used as alternative sources of plant nutrition and pest management in the production of Chinese cabbage on multistorey gardens. This indicated the presence of mineral nutrients in BSFFF that can improve soil fertility and insect suppression properties for pest management. It can therefore be concluded that while insect-based liquid BSFF fertilizer is recommended for increased vegetative growth, yield, and yield parameters, Desmodium intercrop can be used for pest suppression with improved protein and carbohydrate content of Chinese cabbage. Smallholder farmers are already using multistory gardens extensively, especially in urban and peri-urban areas, because of its low cost, effective use of space, and potential to increase household food and nutrition security. The incorporation of BSFFF and Desmodium to this system increases its usefulness through improving productivity, reducing reliance on chemical fertilizer and pesticides, and providing a scalable, environmentally friendly technology that supports sustainable agriculture. Importantly, multistorey structures are durable, with a lifespan exceeding ten years, which supports continuous year-round crop production. The establishment costs can typically be recovered within the initial production cycles through enhanced yields and profitability, demonstrating a favorable break-even point and confirming the long-term profitability of the technology for smallholder farmers. Overall, this study demonstrates that liquid frass fertilizer, even without chemical inputs, provides superior results in terms of yield and profitability, while Desmodium ensures pest suppression and nutritional enhancement. These findings highlight the potential of multistorey gardens enriched with insect-based fertilizers and Desmodium to improve crop yield and profitability than other applications. The results indicate that this technology has a potential applicability in other urban and peri-urban areas with similar agro-ecological conditions.

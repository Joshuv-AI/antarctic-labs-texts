Spectral characterizations of local structures of graphs and hypergraphs Journal:
Jiang Zhou, Changjiang Bu

Abstract
In this paper, we give the relationship between spectral radius and local structures of graphs and hypergraphs. Our work shows that certain local subgraphs (subhypergraphs) must occur when the spectral radius ratio is large. We also give spectral bounds on the local vector chromatic number in terms of tensor eigenvalues of graphs.

denote the open neighborhood of
u
in
G
, and let
G_{u}
denote the subgraph induced by
N_{G}(u)
. The following result follows from Theorem
3.1
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G
be a graph such that
3\leq r\leq\omega(G)
for an integer
r
. Then there exists a vertex
u
in
G
such that

\displaystyle\rho_{r-1}(G_{u})\geq(r-1)\gamma_{r}(G)

and
G
has
p=\left\lceil(r-1)\beta_{r}(G)\right\rceil
distinct
r
-cliques
C_{1},\ldots,C_{p}
such that
u\in C_{1}\cap\cdots\cap C_{p}
and

\displaystyle|C_{1}\cap\cdots\cap C_{p}|\geq 2.

The book graph
B_{p}
is the graph that consists of
p
triangles sharing a common edge. Take
r=3
in Theorem
4.1
, we obtain the following result, which shows that a graph
G
has a large book subgraph when
\gamma_{3}(G)
is large.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
G
a graph with at least one triangle. Then
B_{p}
is a subgraph of
G
, where

\displaystyle p=\left\lceil 2\gamma_{3}(G)\right\rceil\geq\left\lceil\frac{2\rho_{3}(G)}{\rho_{2}(G)}\right\rceil.

For a graph
F
, let
K_{1}\vee F
denote the graph with vertex set
V(F)\cup\{x\}
and edge set
E(F)\cup\{xu:u\in V(F)\}
, and let
spex(n,F)
denote the maximum spectral radius of an
n
-vertex
F
-free graph. Let
\Delta(H)
denote the maximum degree of a graph
H
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
G
a graph with at least one triangle, and let
H
be the subgraph of
G
obtained by deleting all edges that are not containing in a triangle. Suppose that
F
is a graph without isolated vertices. Then
K_{1}\vee F
is a subgraph of
G
if one of the following holds:

(1)
\gamma_{3}(G)>\frac{1}{2}spex\left(\Delta(H),F\right)
.

(2)
6|C_{3}(G)|>|V(G)|\rho_{2}(H)spex\left(\Delta(H),F\right)
.

(3)
\rho_{3}(G)>\frac{1}{2}\Delta(H)spex\left(\Delta(H),F\right)
.

Proof.

If
\gamma_{3}(G)>\frac{1}{2}spex\left(\Delta(H),F\right)
, then by Theorem
4.1
, there exists a vertex

\displaystyle\rho_{2}(H_{u})=\rho_{2}(G_{u})\geq 2\gamma_{3}(G)>spex\left(\Delta(H),F\right)\geq spex_{\mathcal{H}}(|V(H_{u})|,F).

Hence

is a subgraph of

, that is,

is a subgraph of

. So part (1) holds.

By Lemma

, we have
\gamma_{3}(G)=\frac{\rho_{3}(G)}{\rho_{2}(H)}\geq\frac{3c_{3}(G)}{|V(G)|\rho_{2}(H)}
, so part (2) holds. Since
\gamma_{3}(G)\geq\frac{\rho_{3}(G)}{\Delta(H)}
, part (3) holds.
∎

From Theorem

, we can derive the following relation between the spectral radius and clique number of a graph.

Theorem 4.4
.

For any graph
G
and any integer
r
satisfying
3\leq r\leq\omega(G)
, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{r}(G)\leq\rho(\widetilde{G})^{r-1}{\omega(G)-1\choose r-1}(\omega(G)-1)^{-(r-1)}.

where
\widetilde{G}
is the subgraph of
G
obtained by deleting all edges that are not containing in an
r
-clique.

Proof.

Let
H
be the
r
-uniform hypergraph with vertex set
V(H)=V(G)
and edge set
E(H)=\{i_{1}\cdots i_{r}:\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\}\in C_{r}(G)\}
. Then
\rho_{r}(G)=\rho(H)
and
\widetilde{G}=H^{(2)}
is the
2
-section graph of

. By Theorem
3.4
, there exists a vertex
u
in
H
such that

\displaystyle\rho_{r}(G)\leq\rho(\widetilde{G})^{r-1}\lambda(H(u)).

Note that
\lambda(H(u))=\max\left\{\sum_{\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r-1}\}\in C_{r-1}(G_{u})}x_{j_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{r-1}}:\sum_{i\in N(u)}x_{i}=1,x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{|N(u)|}\right\}
. Since

, by Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle\lambda(H(u))={\omega(G_{u})\choose r-1}\omega(G_{u})^{-(r-1)}\leq{\omega(G)-1\choose r-1}(\omega(G)-1)^{-(r-1)}.

∎

5
Local chromatic number and graph eigenvalues

In
ErdosFuredi
, Erdős et al. defined the
local chromatic number

\psi(G)
of a graph
G
as

\displaystyle\psi(G)=1+\min_{c}\max_{v\in V(G)}|\{c(u):u\in N_{G}(v)\}|,

where the minimum is taken over all proper colorings
c
of
G
. Then
\psi(G)
is a local graph parameter based on graph colorings, and
\psi(G)
is bounded from below by the fractional chromatic number of
G
(see
Korner
).

Considering the localized problem of vector chromatic number, it is natural to define the
local vector chromatic number
of
G
as

\displaystyle\phi(G)=1+\max_{u\in V(G)}\chi_{v}(G_{u}).

Then it is easy to obtain the following relations

\displaystyle\omega(G)\leq\phi(G)\leq 1+\max_{u\in V(G)}\chi(G_{u})\leq\psi(G)\leq\chi(G).

For a graph
G
with vertex set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we say that a set
f=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}
of unit real vectors is a
2
-distance representation
of
G
if the following hold:

(1)
u_{i}^{\top}u_{j}=0
whenever the distance between vertices
i
and
j
is
2
.

(2)
u_{i}^{\top}u_{j}\geq 0
for any
1\leq i\leq j\leq n
.

For a
2
-distance representation
f=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}
of
G
, let
\mathcal{A}_{f}(G)=(a_{ijk})
denote the
3
-order
n
-dimension
weighted
3
-clique tensor
of
G
with entries

\displaystyle a_{ijk}=\begin{cases}u_{j}^{\top}u_{k},&\{i,j,k\}\in C_{3}(G).\\
0,&\textup{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
\mu_{f}(G)
denote the spectral radius of
\mathcal{A}_{f}(G)
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
G
be a graph with at least one triangle, and let
H
be the subgraph of
G
obtained by deleting all edges that are not containing in a triangle. For any
2
-distance representation
f
of
G
, we have

\displaystyle\phi(G)\geq 2+\left\lceil\frac{\mu_{f}(G)}{\rho_{2}(H)}\right\rceil.

Proof.

Suppose that
f=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}
is a
2
-distance representation of
G
. Let
x
be a nonnegative eigenvector associated with
\mu_{f}(G)
, then
\mathcal{A}_{f}(G)x^{2}=\mu_{f}(G)x^{[2]}
. For any vertex
i
of
G
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{f}(G)x_{i}^{2}=(\mathcal{A}_{f}(G)x^{2})_{i}=2\sum_{jk\in E(H_{i})}u_{j}^{\top}u_{k}x_{j}x_{k}.

Note that
u_{j}^{\top}u_{k}=0
whenever
i,k
are two distinct non-adjacent vertices in
H_{i}
. So
\{u_{j}:j\in N_{H}(i)\}
is a nonnegative orthonormal representation of
H_{i}
. Let
y
be the
|N_{H}(i)|
-dimension vector such that
y_{j}=x_{j}
for all
j\in N_{H}(i)
. Then

\displaystyle\mu_{f}(G)x_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{jk\in E(H_{i})}u_{j}^{\top}u_{k}x_{j}x_{k}=y^{\top}(M-I)y

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}(M)-1)\sum_{j\in N_{H}(i)}x_{j}^{2},

where
M
is the Gram matrix of
\{u_{j}:j\in N_{H}(i)\}
,
\lambda_{1}(M)
is the maximum eigenvalue of
M
. By Lemmas
2.7
and
2.8
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{f}(G)x_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}(M)-1)\sum_{j\in N_{H}(i)}x_{j}^{2}\leq(\chi_{v}(H_{i})-1)\sum_{j\in N_{H}(i)}x_{j}^{2},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\chi_{v}(G_{i})-1)\sum_{j\in N_{H}(i)}x_{j}^{2}.

Let
z
be the
|V(G)|
-dimension vector such that
z_{j}=x_{j}^{2}
for all
j\in V(G)
. Then
\sum_{j\in N_{H}(i)}x_{j}^{2}=(A_{H}z)_{i}
. By Lemma
2.2
, we know that there exists a vertex
u
such that

\displaystyle x_{u}^{-2}\sum_{j\in N_{H}(u)}x_{j}^{2}=z_{u}^{-1}\sum_{j\in N_{H}(u)}z_{j}\leq\rho_{2}(H).

Since
\phi(G)=1+\max_{i\in V(G)}\chi_{v}(G_{i})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mu_{f}(G)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\chi_{v}(G_{u})-1)\rho_{2}(H)\leq(\phi(G)-2)\rho_{2}(H),

\displaystyle\phi(G)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2+\left\lceil\frac{\mu_{f}(G)}{\rho_{2}(H)}\right\rceil.

∎

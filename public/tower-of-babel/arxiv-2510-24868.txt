Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the notions of the k k -th Milnor number and the k k -th Tjurina number for a germ of holomorphic foliation on the complex plane with an isolated singularity at the origin. We develop a detailed study of these invariants, establishing explicit formulas and relating them to other indices associated with holomorphic foliations. In particular, we obtain an explicit expression for the k k -th Milnor number of a foliation and, as a consequence, a formula for the k k -th Milnor number of a holomorphic function. We analyze their topological behavior, proving that the k k -th Milnor number of a holomorphic function is a topological invariant, whereas the k k -th Tjurina number is not. In dimension two, we provide a positive answer to a conjecture posed by Hussain, Liu, Yau, and Zuo concerning a sharp lower bound for the k k -th Tjurina number of a weighted homogeneous polynomial. We also present a counterexample to another conjecture of Hussain, Yau, and Zuo regarding the ratio between these invariants. Moreover, we establish a fundamental relation linking the k k -th Tjurina numbers of a foliation and of an invariant curve via the Gómez-Mont–Seade–Verjovsky index, and we extend Teissier’s Lemma to the setting of k k -th polar intersection numbers. In addition, we derive an upper bound for the k k -th Milnor number of a foliation in terms of its k k -th Tjurina number along balanced divisors of separatrices. Finally, for non-dicritical quasi-homogeneous foliations, we obtain a closed formula for their k k -th Milnor and Tjurina numbers.

1.
Introduction

In recent years, new invariants associated with germs of complex hypersurfaces at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
have been introduced with the aim of developing a classification theory of singularities. Among them, we highlight the so-called
k
-th Milnor and Tjurina numbers of a hypersurface
.

More precisely, let
\mathcal{O}_{n}=\mathbb{C}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
denote the local ring of convergent power series, and let
\mathfrak{m}
denote its maximal ideal. For a hypersurface with an isolated singularity
(V,0)\subset(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
, where
V=V(f)=\{f=0\}
and
f\in\mathcal{O}_{n}
, Hussain, Liu, Yau, and Zuo
[
17
]
defined the
k
-th Milnor number
and the
k
-th Tjurina number
of
V
, respectively, as

\mu^{k}(V)=\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\mathfrak{m}^{k}j(f)},\qquad\tau^{k}(V)=\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\langle\mathfrak{m}^{k}j(f),f\rangle},\qquad\text{for every integer }k\geq 0,

where
j(f)=\langle f_{x_{1}},\ldots,f_{x_{n}}\rangle
denotes the Jacobian ideal of
f
.

It follows from the Mather–Yau Theorem (see, for example,
[
15
, Theorems 2.26 and 2.28]
) that
\mu^{k}(V)
and
\tau^{k}(V)
are
analytic invariants
. When
V
is a binomial plane curve singularity, they proved that
\mu^{k}(V)
and
\tau^{k}(V)
are topological invariants. Moreover, the authors explicitly computed
\mu^{k}(V)
and
\tau^{k}(V)
for binomial singularities; see Theorems A, B, and C in
[
17
]
. The paper concludes with an analytic characterization of simple hypersurface singularities via the so-called
k
-th Yau algebras
; see Theorem D in
[
17
]
.

Motivated by these definitions and results, the purpose of this paper is to define the
k
-th Milnor number
and the
k
-th Tjurina number
of a germ of holomorphic foliation
\mathcal{F}
defined by a holomorphic
1
-form
\omega\in\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
with an isolated singularity at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
, as follows.

Let
\omega=P(x,y)\,dx+Q(x,y)\,dy
be a germ of holomorphic
1
-form at the origin of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
such that
\mathcal{F}
is defined by
\omega
and has an isolated singularity at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Let
C=\{f=0\}
be the germ of a singular complex curve, where
f\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
. We say that
C
is
invariant
by
\mathcal{F}
if
(\omega\wedge df)/f\in\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
. Then, we define the
k
-th Milnor number
of
\mathcal{F}
at the origin as

\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F}):=\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle P,Q\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}},

and the
k
-th Tjurina number
of
\mathcal{F}
along
C
as

\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},C):=\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle P,Q\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}+\langle f\rangle}.

Note that when
k=0
, we have
\mu^{0}(\mathcal{F}):=\mu(\mathcal{F})
and
\tau^{0}(\mathcal{F},C):=\tau(\mathcal{F},C)
, which coincide with the classical Milnor number of
\mathcal{F}
, defined by Camacho–Lins Neto–Sad
[
3
]
, and the Tjurina number of
\mathcal{F}
along
C
, introduced by Cano–Corral–Mol
[
5
]
, respectively. Also observe that when
\mathcal{F}
is given by a holomorphic first integral
\omega=df
, we have
\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})=\mu^{k}(C)
and
\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},C)=\tau^{k}(C)
.

Obviously, our definition can be extended to germs of holomorphic
1
-forms with an isolated singularity at the origin of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, as follows. Let
\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}\,dx_{i}
and
V=\{f=0\}
be a hypersurface invariant by
\omega
. Then, for every integer
k\geq 0
, we define the
k
-th Milnor number of
\omega
by

\mu^{k}(\omega)=\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\langle P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}},

and the
k
-th Tjurina number of
\omega
with respect to
V
as

\tau^{k}(\omega,V)=\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\langle P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}+\langle f\rangle}.

In this paper, we mainly study the case
n=2
, that is, the
k
-th Milnor and Tjurina numbers of a germ of foliation at the origin of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. We begin the paper by recalling basic definitions of foliation theory in Section
2
, and in Section
3
we prove results on colengths of ideals in

that will be useful for the proofs of our main results.

As a first result, in Section
4
, we obtain an explicit formula for the

-th Milnor number of a foliation

(see (

In Section 5, we show that the

In Section 6, we provide a positive answer to a conjecture posed by Hussain, Liu, Yau, and Zuo (see

Section

Next, in Section

, we define the

-th polar intersection number
of a foliation

. As an application, we prove an extension of Teissier’s Lemma

]
, providing a formula relating this

.

In Section

, combining ideas from

, we present the following inequality:

where

Finally, in Section

2.
Basic notions on foliations

To establish the terminology and notation, we recall some basic notions of the theory of holomorphic foliations. Unless stated otherwise, throughout this text,
\mathcal{F}
denotes a germ of a singular holomorphic foliation at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
. In local coordinates
(x,y)
centered at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
, the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is given by a germ of a holomorphic 1-form

\omega=P(x,y)dx+Q(x,y)dy,

or by its dual vector field

(1)

v=-Q(x,y)\frac{\partial}{\partial{x}}+P(x,y)\frac{\partial}{\partial{y}},

where
P(x,y),Q(x,y)\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
are relatively prime.
The
algebraic multiplicity

\nu(\mathcal{F})
is the minimum of the algebraic multiplicity of
\nu(P)
,
\nu(Q)
at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
of the coefficients of a local generator of
\mathcal{F}
.

Let
f(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]
, where
{\mathbb{C}}[[x,y]]
is the ring of complex formal power series in two variables.
We say that
C=\{f(x,y)=0\}
is
invariant
by
\mathcal{F}
or
\mathcal{F}
-
invariant
if

\omega\wedge df=(f.h)dx\wedge dy,

for some
h\in\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]
. If
C
is an irreducible
\mathcal{F}
-invariant curve then we will say that
C
is a
separatrix
of
\mathcal{F}
at
0
. The separatrix
C
is analytical if
f
is convergent. We denote by
\mbox{\rm Sep}_{0}(\mathcal{F})
the set of all separatrices of
\mathcal{F}
at
0
. When
\mbox{\rm Sep}_{0}(\mathcal{F})
is a finite set we will say that the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is
non-dicritical
and the union of all elements of
\mbox{\rm Sep}_{0}(\mathcal{F})
is called
total union of separatrices
of
\mathcal{F}
at
0
. Otherwise, we will say that
\mathcal{F}
is a
dicritical
foliation.

We say that
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
is a
reduced
singularity for
\mathcal{F}
if the linear part
\text{D}v(0)
of the vector field
v
in (
1
) is non-zero and has eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
fitting in one of the cases:

(i)

\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\neq 0
and
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\not\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
(
non-degenerate
);

(ii)

\lambda_{1}\neq 0
and
\lambda_{2}=0
(
saddle-node singularity
).

In the case
(i)
, there is a system of coordinates
(x,y)
in which
\mathcal{F}
is defined by the equation

(2)

\omega=x(\lambda_{1}+a(x,y))dy-y(\lambda_{2}+b(x,y))dx,

where
a(x,y),b(x,y)\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x,y]]
are non-units, so that
\mbox{\rm Sep}_{0}(\mathcal{F})
is formed by two
transversal analytic branches given by
\{x=0\}
and
\{y=0\}
. In the case
(ii)
, up to a formal change of coordinates, the saddle-node singularity is given by a 1-form of the type

(3)

\omega=x^{\ell+1}dy-y(1+\lambda x^{\ell})dx,

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
are invariants after formal changes of coordinates (see
[
22
, Proposition 4.3]
).
The curve
\{x=0\}
is an analytic separatrix, called
strong
separatrix, whereas
\{y=0\}
corresponds to a possibly formal separatrix, called
weak
separatrix. The integer
\ell+1
is called the
tangency index
of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to the weak separatrix.

A germ divisor

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=\sum\limits_{B\in\operatorname{Sep}(\mathcal{F})}a_{B}\,B,\quad a_{B}\in\{-1,0,1\}

is called
balanced
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

if
B
is an isolated separatrix of
\mathcal{F}
then
a_{B}=1
,

(2)

if
a_{B}=-1
then
B
is a non-isolated (dicritical) separatrix of
\mathcal{F}
,

(3)

for each non-invariant (dicritical) component
D
of the exceptional divisor
E
of the reduction
\pi
of singularities of
\mathcal{F}
we have
\sum_{B}a_{B}\overline{B}\cdot D=2-val_{D}
, where
val_{D}
is the valence of
D
, i.e. the number of irreducible components of
\overline{E\setminus D}
meeting
D
.

Notice that a non-dicritical foliation has a unique balanced divisor which is the sum of the isolated separatrix. In contrast, a dicritical foliation have infinitely many balanced divisors, see
[
11
]
.

Recall that a foliation is said to be of
second type
if none of the singularities of
\pi^{*}\mathcal{F}
are tangent saddle-nodes; that is, whenever saddle-nodes appear in the reduction, their strong separatrix is transverse to the exceptional divisor. If no saddle-nodes appear in the reduction we say that
\mathcal{F}
is a
generalized curve
.
The balanced divisors characterize second type foliations:

Proposition 2.1

(
[
11
]
, Proposition 2.4)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation germ on
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
with balanced divisor
\mathcal{B}
. Then
\mathcal{F}
is of second type if and only if
\nu(\mathcal{F})=\nu(\mathcal{B})-1
.

We now proceed to compute the
k
-th Milnor and Tjurina numbers of a foliation with a reduced singularity at the origin. Since in this case the coordinates are formal, we will work with the definitions in the ring
\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a germ of singular foliation at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
. The following holds:

(1)

If
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
is a non-degenerate singularity, then

\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})=\frac{(k+1)(k+2)}{2},\qquad\qquad\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},\mbox{\rm Sep}_{0}(\mathcal{F}))=2k+1.

(2)

If
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
is a saddle-node singularity and
\ell-1
is the tangency index of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to the weak separatrix, then

\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})=\frac{(k+1)(k+2)}{2}+\ell,\qquad\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},\mbox{\rm Sep}_{0}(\mathcal{F}))=2k+1+\ell.

Proof.

First, assume that
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
is a non-degenerate singularity. Then, by
2
, the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is given by

\omega=x(\lambda_{1}+a(x,y))dy-y(\lambda_{2}+b(x,y))dx,

where
a(x,y),b(x,y)\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x,y]]
are non-units.
Then

\displaystyle\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim\frac{\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]}{\langle-y(\lambda_{2}+b(x,y)),x(\lambda_{1}+a(x,y))\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim\frac{\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]}{\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}}=\frac{(k+1)(k+2)}{2}.

Now, the
k
-th Tjurina number of a foliation
\mathcal{F}
along
\mbox{\rm Sep}_{0}(\mathcal{F})=\{xy=0\}
is given by

\displaystyle\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},\mbox{\rm Sep}_{0}(\mathcal{F}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim\frac{\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]}{\langle-y(\lambda_{2}+b(x,y)),x(\lambda_{1}+a(x,y))\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}+\langle xy\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim\frac{\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]}{\langle x^{k+1},y^{k+1},xy\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2k+1.

This completes the proof of item (1).

For item (2), we assume that
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
is a saddle-node singularity. Then, by (
3
), we have that
\mathcal{F}
is given by a 1-form

\omega=x^{\ell+1}dy-y(1+\lambda x^{\ell})dx,

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Then

\displaystyle\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim\frac{\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]}{\langle-y(1+\lambda x^{\ell}),x^{\ell+1}\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim\frac{\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]}{\langle-y,x^{\ell+1}\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim\frac{\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]}{\langle x^{\ell+1+k},x^{k}y,x^{k-1}y^{2},\ldots,xy^{k},y^{k+1}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(k+2)(k+1)}{2}+\ell.

Finally, for the
k
-th Tjurina number of
\mathcal{F}
, we have

\displaystyle\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},\mbox{\rm Sep}_{0}(\mathcal{F}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim\frac{\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]}{\langle-y(1+\lambda x^{\ell}),x^{\ell+1}\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}+\langle xy\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim\frac{\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]}{\langle-y,x^{\ell+1}\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}+\langle xy\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim\frac{\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]}{\langle xy,x^{k+\ell+1},y^{k+1}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2k+1+\ell.

∎

Remark 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the germ of a foliation at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
with algebraic multiplicity
\nu(\mathcal{F})=m
. Then, for any integer
k\geq 0
, we have

\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})\geq\frac{(m+k)(m+k+1)}{2}.

Indeed, we have
(P,Q)\subset\mathfrak{m}^{m}
, which implies that
\mathfrak{m}^{k}(P,Q)\subset\mathfrak{m}^{m+k}
. Therefore,

\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})\geq\dim\left(\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\mathfrak{m}^{m+k}}\right)=\frac{(m+k)(m+k+1)}{2}.

3.
Colengths of ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{2}

In this section, we present the commutative algebra lemmas that will be important to prove the results of this paper.
We begin by establishing the following lemma:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
J
be an ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{2}
, and let
P,Q,f,g\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
such that
f
and
g
are relatively prime. Then

\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle gP,gQ\rangle\cdot J+\langle f\rangle}=\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle P,Q\rangle\cdot J+\langle f\rangle}+\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle g\rangle\cdot J+\langle f\rangle}.

Proof.

The proof follows from the following short exact sequence:

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle P,Q\rangle\cdot J+\langle f\rangle}\xrightarrow{\ \sigma\ }\ \frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle gP,gQ\rangle\cdot J+\langle f\rangle}\ \xrightarrow{\ \delta\ }\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle g\rangle\cdot J+\langle f\rangle}\longrightarrow 0,

where
\sigma
is the multiplication homomorphism by
g
, and
\delta
is the natural projection homomorphism induced by the inclusion
\langle gP,gQ\rangle\cdot J+\langle f\rangle\subset\langle g\rangle\cdot J+\langle f\rangle
.
∎

Now, we present the following:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
J
be an ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{2}
, and let
f,g\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
such that
(f,g)=1
, i.e., they are relatively prime. Then

\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle f\rangle\cdot J+\langle g\rangle}=\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{J+\langle g\rangle}+i(f,g),

where
i(f,g)=\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle f,g\rangle}
is the intersection number of
f
and
g
.

Proof.

The proof is a consequence of the short exact sequence below:

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{J+\langle g\rangle}\xrightarrow{\ \sigma\ }\ \frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle f\rangle\cdot J+\langle g\rangle}\xrightarrow{\ \pi\ }\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle f,g\rangle}\longrightarrow 0,

where
\sigma
is the multiplication homomorphism by
f
, and
\pi
is the natural projection homomorphism induced by the inclusion
\langle f\rangle\cdot J+\langle g\rangle\subset\langle f,g\rangle
.
∎

Now, we establish the following lemma:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
J
be an ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{2}
such that
\dim\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{J}<\infty
, and let
\psi,\varphi\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
be such that
(\psi,\varphi)=1
, i.e., they are relatively prime. Suppose that
\nu(\psi)\leq\nu(\varphi)
, then

\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle J}=i(\psi,\varphi)+\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle\psi\rangle+J}+\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{J}.

Proof.

Write
J=\langle h_{1},\dots,h_{s}\rangle
. Observe that

\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle J=\langle\psi h_{1},\dots,\psi h_{s},\varphi h_{1},\dots,\varphi h_{s}\rangle.

Since
\nu(\psi)\leq\nu(\varphi)
, we can perform an elementary change of generators: there exist elements
\psi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
and
h_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
such that

\psi^{\prime}\equiv\psi\pmod{\langle\varphi h_{1},\dots,\varphi h_{s}\rangle},\qquad h_{i}^{\prime}\equiv h_{i}\pmod{\langle\varphi h_{1},\dots,\varphi h_{s}\rangle},

and moreover

\langle\psi h_{1},\dots,\psi h_{s},\varphi h_{1},\dots,\varphi h_{s}\rangle=\langle\psi^{\prime}h_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\psi^{\prime}h_{s}^{\prime}\rangle.

Thus, we can identify

\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle J}\cong\frac{\mathcal{O}}{\langle\psi^{\prime}h_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\psi^{\prime}h_{s}^{\prime}\rangle},

where
\mathcal{O}=\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle\varphi h_{1},\dots,\varphi h_{s}\rangle}
.
Consider the short exact sequence induced by multiplication by
\psi^{\prime}
on the quotient by the
h_{i}^{\prime}
:

(4)

0\longrightarrow\frac{\mathcal{O}}{\langle h_{1}^{\prime},\dots,h_{s}^{\prime}\rangle}\xrightarrow{\ \cdot\psi^{\prime}\ }\frac{\mathcal{O}}{\langle\psi^{\prime}h_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\psi^{\prime}h_{s}^{\prime}\rangle}\xrightarrow{\ \pi\ }\frac{\mathcal{O}}{\langle\psi^{\prime}\rangle}\longrightarrow 0,

where
\pi
is the natural projection. The exactness of (
4
) follows from the construction (multiplication by
\psi^{\prime}
is injective on the first quotient, and its cokernel is the quotient by
\langle\psi^{\prime}\rangle
).
Taking complex dimensions (which are finite under our assumptions) and using the additivity of length in short exact sequences, we obtain

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}}{\langle\psi^{\prime}h_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\psi^{\prime}h_{s}^{\prime}\rangle}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}}{\langle h_{1}^{\prime},\dots,h_{s}^{\prime}\rangle}+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}}{\langle\psi^{\prime}\rangle}.

Replacing
\psi^{\prime},h_{i}^{\prime}
by their congruent representatives modulo
\langle\varphi h_{1},\dots,\varphi h_{s}\rangle
and using elementary properties of ideals and quotients, we obtain

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle J}

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}}{\langle\psi^{\prime}h_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\psi^{\prime}h_{s}^{\prime}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle h_{1},\dots,h_{s},\varphi h_{1},\ldots,\varphi h_{s}\rangle}+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle\psi,\varphi h_{1},\ldots,\varphi h_{s}\rangle}

(5)

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle h_{1},\dots,h_{s}\rangle}+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle\psi,\varphi h_{1},\ldots,\varphi h_{s}\rangle}.

Now, by Lemma
3.2
, we can deduce that

(6)

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle\psi,\varphi h_{1},\ldots,\varphi h_{s}\rangle}=i(\psi,\varphi)+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle\psi\rangle+J}.

Substituting (
6
) into (
3
), we conclude the proof.
∎

4.
Formula for the
k
-th Milnor number of a foliation

Let
\mathcal{F}:\omega=P\,dx+Q\,dy
be a singular foliation at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
Assume that the algebraic multiplicity of
\mathcal{F}
satisfies
\nu(\mathcal{F})=\nu(P)\leq\nu(Q)
.
By applying Lemma
3.3
with
J=\mathfrak{m}^{k}
,
\psi=P
, and
\varphi=Q
, we obtain

(7)

\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})=\mu(\mathcal{F})+\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\mathfrak{m}^{k}}+\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle P\rangle+\mathfrak{m}^{k}}.

Since
\dim\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\mathfrak{m}^{k}}=\dfrac{k(k+1)}{2}
, we have, more generally, the following observation:

Remark 4.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
such that
\nu(f)=m
. Then

\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\mathfrak{m}^{k}+\langle f\rangle}=\begin{cases}\frac{k(k+1)}{2},&\text{if $0\leq k<m$ }\\
\frac{(k+1)k}{2}-\frac{(k+1-m)(k-m)}{2},&\text{if $k\geq m$.}\end{cases}

As a consequence of equation
7
and Remark
4.1
, we obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
. Suppose that
\nu(f)=m
. Then

\mu^{k}(f)=\begin{cases}\mu(f)+k(k+1),&\text{if $0\leq k<m$ }\\
\mu(f)+k(k+1)-\frac{(k-m+2)(k-m+1)}{2},&\text{if $k\geq m$.}\end{cases}

When
f
is a quasi-homogeneous polynomial, we have the following result from
[
17
, Proposition 2.6]
:

Proposition 4.2
.

For an isolated singularity defined by a weighted homogeneous polynomial
f\in\mathbb{C}[x,y]
, we have

\mu^{k}(f)=\tau^{k}(f)+\frac{k(k-1)}{2}.

As an application of Corollary
4.1
and Proposition
4.2
, we recover Theorems
A
and
B
from
[
17
]
.

Now we present an application for singular foliations.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a singular foliation at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is non-dicritical and of second type. Let
C=\{f=0\}
be the total union of separatrices at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Then, assuming that
\nu(f_{x})\leq\nu(f_{y})
, we have that

\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})-\mu^{k}(C)=\mu(\mathcal{F})-\mu(C).

In particular,
\mathcal{F}
is a generalized curve foliation if and only if
\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})=\mu^{k}(C)
for all
k\geq 0
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathcal{F}
is non-dicritical defined by
\omega=Pdx+Qdy
such that
\nu(\mathcal{F})=\nu(P)
, and let
C=\{f=0\}
be the total union of all separatrices at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
Since
\nu(f_{x})\leq\nu(f_{y})
, we obtain from Lemma
3.3
that

(8)

\mu^{k}(C)=\mu(C)+\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\mathfrak{m}^{k}}+\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle f_{x}\rangle+\mathfrak{m}^{k}}.

As
\mathcal{F}
is of second type, using Proposition
2.1
we have

\nu(\mathcal{F})=\nu(C)-1=\nu(f_{x})
. Hence, it follows from Remark
4.1
that

\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle P\rangle+\mathfrak{m}^{k}}=\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle f_{x}\rangle+\mathfrak{m}^{k}}.

Therefore, from (
7
) and (
8
) we obtain

\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})-\mu^{k}(C)=\mu(\mathcal{F})-\mu(C).

Consequently,
\mathcal{F}
is a generalized curve foliation if and only if
\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})=\mu^{k}(C)
for all
k\geq 0
, by Theorem 7 in
[
3
]
.
∎

5.
On the topological invariance of the
k
-th Milnor and Tjurina Numbers

Given
f\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
, it follows from Theorems 2.28 and 2.26 of
[
15
]
that
\mu^{k}(f)
and
\tau^{k}(f)
are
analytic invariants
. In what follows, we will see that, in general, the
k
-th Tjurina number is not a topological invariant. Specifically, we focus on
\tau^{1}(f)
.

To see this, let us consider
f(x,y)=y^{3}-x^{7}
and
g(x,y)=f+x^{5}y
. According to
[
15
, p. 218]
, the curves
C=\{f=0\}
and
D=\{g=0\}
are topologically equivalent, that is, there exists a homeomorphism
\phi:(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
such that
\phi(C)=D
. However, using
Singular

[
6
]
, we obtain:

> ring r=0,(x,y),ds;
> LIB "sing.lib";
> poly f=y3-x7;
> poly g=f+x5y;
> ideal m=x,y;
> vdim(std(tjurina(f)));
12
> vdim(std(tjurina(g)));
11
> ideal J1=jacob(f)*m+f;
> ideal J2=jacob(g)*m+g;
> vdim(std(J1)); // 1-th Tjurina number of f
14
> vdim(std(J2)); // 1-th Tjurina number of g
13

This shows that
\tau^{1}(f)=14\neq 13=\tau^{1}(g)
and, therefore,
\tau^{1}(f)
is not a topological invariant.

Concerning the
k
-th Milnor number of a germ
f\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
with
\nu(f)=m\geq 1
, Corollary
4.1
states that

\mu^{k}(f)=\begin{cases}\mu(f)+k(k+1),&\text{if $0\leq k<m$ }\\[4.0pt]
\mu(f)+k(k+1)-\frac{(k-m+2)(k-m+1)}{2},&\text{if $k\geq m$.}\end{cases}

This shows that, in dimension two,
\mu^{k}(f)
depends only on
\mu(f)
,
k
, and
\nu(f)
. Since
\mu(f)
is a topological invariant (see
[
19
]
) and
\nu(f)
is also a topological invariant (see
[
31
]
), it follows that
\mu^{k}(f)
is a topological invariant as well. In higher dimensions, the problem of the topological invariance of
\mu^{k}(f)
remains open.

Regarding the topological invariance of the
k
-th Milnor number of a foliation, we can say the following:
Let
\mathcal{F}:\omega=Pdx+Qdy
be a germ of foliation at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Let us assume that
\nu(\mathcal{F})=\nu(P)\leq\nu(Q)
. Applying Lemma
3.3
, we obtain

\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})=\mu(\mathcal{F})+\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\mathfrak{m}^{k}}+\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle P\rangle+\mathfrak{m}^{k}}.

As a consequence,
\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})
depends on
\mu(\mathcal{F})
,
k
, and the algebraic multiplicity
\nu(\mathcal{F})=\nu(P)
(by hypothesis).
Since it is known that the algebraic multiplicity
\nu(\mathcal{F})
is a biLipschitz invariant (see
[
25
]
) and
\mu(\mathcal{F})
is a topological invariant (see
[
3
]
), we can conclude that
\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})
is a biLipschitz invariant, that is, it is preserved under a Lipschitz equivalence.

The problem of the topological invariance of both the algebraic multiplicity
\nu(\mathcal{F})
and
\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})
remains open. We should mention that there is a recent preprint claiming that the algebraic multiplicity 1 is a topological invariant of the foliation (see
[
4
]
).

6.
On the conjecture for the
k
-th Tjurina number of a weighted homogeneous polynomial

In
[
17
, Conjecture 1.1]
, the authors conjectured the following sharp lower bound for the
k
-th Tjurina number of a complex hypersurface:

Conjecture 1.

For each
k\geq 0
, assume that
\tau^{k}(\{x_{1}^{a_{1}}+\ldots+x_{n}^{a_{n}}=0\})=\ell^{k}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
.
Let
(V,0)=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=0\}
, with
n\geq 2
, be an isolated singularity defined by a weighted homogeneous polynomial
f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
of weight type
(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n};1)
. Then

\tau^{k}(V)\geq\ell^{k}(1/w_{1},\ldots,1/w_{n}).

In this section, we provide a
positive answer to Conjecture 1
in the case
n=2
.
Indeed, let
C=\{f(x,y)=0\}
be a germ of a reduced singular curve defined by
f\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
.
Assume that
f
is a weighted homogeneous polynomial of weight type
(1/w_{1},1/w_{2};1)
and let
m=\nu(f)\geq\frac{1}{w_{1}}\geq 2
.
Then, it follows from Corollary
4.1
, Proposition
4.2
, and the Milnor–Orlik formula
[
24
]
that

\tau^{k}(f)=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{1}{w_{1}}-1\right)\left(\frac{1}{w_{2}}-1\right)+\frac{k(k+3)}{2},&\text{if }0\leq k<m,\\[4.0pt]
\left(\frac{1}{w_{1}}-1\right)\left(\frac{1}{w_{2}}-1\right)+\frac{k(k+3)}{2}-\frac{(k-m+2)(k-m+1)}{2},&\text{if }k\geq m.\end{cases}

Similarly, we have the following formula for
\ell^{k}(1/w_{1},1/w_{2})
:

\ell^{k}(1/w_{1},1/w_{2})=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{1}{w_{1}}-1\right)\left(\frac{1}{w_{2}}-1\right)+\frac{k(k+3)}{2},&\text{if }0\leq k<1/w_{1},\\[4.0pt]
\left(\frac{1}{w_{1}}-1\right)\left(\frac{1}{w_{2}}-1\right)+\frac{k(k+3)}{2}-\frac{\big(k-\frac{1}{w_{1}}+2\big)\big(k-\frac{1}{w_{1}}+1\big)}{2},&\text{if }k\geq 1/w_{1}.\end{cases}

These formulas imply that, in order to prove
\tau^{k}(f)\geq\ell^{k}(1/w_{1},1/w_{2})
, it suffices to show that for
m\geq 1/w_{1}
, the following inequality holds:

(9)

\frac{\big(k-\frac{1}{w_{1}}+2\big)\big(k-\frac{1}{w_{1}}+1\big)}{2}\geq\frac{(k-m+2)(k-m+1)}{2}.

Note that if
m=\frac{1}{w_{1}}
, the inequality is an equality, and there is nothing to prove.
Let us therefore assume that
m>1/w_{1}
and prove inequality (
9
).
Multiplying and eliminating common factors in (
9
), we see that it is equivalent to

2k\left(m-\frac{1}{w_{1}}\right)\geq\left(m-\frac{1}{w_{1}}\right)\left(m+\frac{1}{w_{1}}-3\right).

Since
m>1/w_{1}
, we can simplify the factor
\left(m-\frac{1}{w_{1}}\right)
and obtain

2k\geq\left(m+\frac{1}{w_{1}}-3\right).

This last inequality is clearly true because we are considering
k\geq m>1/w_{1}
.
Taking into account the above computations, we can now state the following theorem.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
C
be a germ of a reduced singular curve at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
defined by a weighted homogeneous polynomial
f
of weight type
(w_{1},w_{2};1)
such that
\nu(f)\geq 1/w_{1}
. Then

\tau^{k}(C)\geq\ell^{k}\left(\frac{1}{w_{1}},\frac{1}{w_{2}}\right).

This result generalizes Theorem C of
[
17
]
.

7.
On a Conjecture for the ratio
\mu^{k}/\tau^{k}

In
[
7
]
, Dimca and Greuel questioned the relationship between the Milnor and Tjurina numbers of an isolated plane curve singularity. More precisely, they asked whether, for every isolated singularity
(C,0)
of a reduced plane curve, the inequality

\frac{\mu(C)}{\tau(C)}<\frac{4}{3}

holds. The definitive answer to this problem was given by Almirón
[
1
]
.

In
[
18
, Conjecture 1.3]
, the authors conjectured that, for any isolated plane curve
C
, the following inequality is satisfied:

\frac{\mu^{k}(C)}{\tau^{k}(C)}<\frac{4}{3}\quad\text{for all}\quad k\geq 0.

They considered the curve
C=\{f=0\}
, where

f(x,y)=x^{2m+1}+y^{2m+1}+x^{m+1}y^{m+1},\quad m\geq 1,

and showed that
\frac{\mu^{1}(C)}{\tau^{1}(C)}
and
\frac{\mu^{2}(C)}{\tau^{2}(C)}
are sufficiently close to
\frac{4}{3}
when
m
is large enough.

However, we claim that such a conjecture has a
negative answer in general
. Indeed, consider
m=2
. In this case we have
f=x^{5}+y^{5}+x^{3}y^{3}
. Using the
Singular
code
[
6
]
:

> ring r=0,(x,y),ds;
> poly f=x5+y5+x3y3;
> ideal m=x,y;
> ideal J=jacob(f)*m*m*m*m*m*m*m*m;
> ideal I=J+f;
> vdim(std(J)); // 8-th Milnor number
78
> vdim(std(I)); // 8-th Tjurina number
50

Note that

(10)

\frac{\mu^{8}(C)}{\tau^{8}(C)}=\frac{78}{50}>\frac{4}{3}.

Next, we confirm Conjecture 1.3 of
[
18
]
for quasi-homogeneous polynomials with a restriction on
k
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
C=\{f=0\}
be an isolated singularity defined by a quasi-homogeneous polynomial
f
of algebraic multiplicity
\nu(f)
. Then, for every
0\leq k<\nu(f)
, we have

\frac{\mu^{k}(f)}{\tau^{k}(f)}<\frac{4}{3}.

Proof.

Since
0\leq k<\nu(f)
, by Corollary
4.1
we have
\mu^{k}(f)=\mu(f)+k(k+1)
. Moreover, in the quasi-homogeneous case, by Proposition
4.2
, it follows that

\mu^{k}(f)=\tau^{k}(f)+\frac{k(k-1)}{2}.

Using the valid formulas in the quasi-homogeneous case, for
0\leq k<\nu(f)
we have

\mu^{k}(f)=\mu(f)+k(k+1),\qquad\mu^{k}(f)=\tau^{k}(f)+\frac{k(k-1)}{2}.

From the second equality we immediately obtain

\tau^{k}(f)=\mu^{k}(f)-\frac{k(k-1)}{2}=\mu(f)+\frac{k^{2}+3k}{2}.

Thus, for
0\leq k<\nu(f)
, the desired quotient is

\frac{\mu^{k}(f)}{\tau^{k}(f)}=\frac{\mu(f)+k(k+1)}{\mu(f)+\dfrac{k^{2}+3k}{2}}.

To verify that this quotient is strictly less than
4/3
, it is enough to check the equivalent inequality

3\big(\mu(f)+k(k+1)\big)<4\Big(\mu(f)+\frac{k^{2}+3k}{2}\Big).

Simplifying, we obtain the condition

\mu(f)>k^{2}-3k.

By the observation in
[
10
, Remark 7.4]
, we have
(\nu(f)-1)^{2}\leq\mu(f)
. We now show that, for every integer
k
with
0\leq k<\nu(f)
,

(\nu(f)-1)^{2}>k^{2}-3k,

which, together with
(\nu(f)-1)^{2}\leq\mu(f)
, implies
(*)
and hence the desired inequality.

Indeed,

(\nu(f)-1)^{2}-(k^{2}-3k)=(\nu(f)-1-k)(\nu(f)-1+k)+3k.

Since
0\leq k<\nu(f)
, we have
\nu(f)-1-k\geq 0
and
3k\geq 0
. Moreover, if
k>0
, then
3k>0
, so the difference is strictly positive; if
k=0
, then
(\nu(f)-1)^{2}>0
for
\nu(f)\geq 2
, and we also obtain the inequality. Hence

(\nu(f)-1)^{2}>k^{2}-3k\quad\text{for all }0\leq k<\nu(f),

and therefore
\mu(f)\geq(\nu(f)-1)^{2}>k^{2}-3k
, which is exactly condition (
*
). Thus, for all
0\leq k<\nu(f)
,

\frac{\mu^{k}(f)}{\tau^{k}(f)}<\frac{4}{3}.

∎

This result generalizes
[
18
, Theorem E]
, since it shows that the inequality

\frac{\mu^{k}(f)}{\tau^{k}(f)}<\frac{4}{3}

remains valid not only for
k=2
, but also for all values
0\leq k<\nu(f)
when
f
is a quasi-homogeneous polynomial.

8.
GSV-index and its relation with the
k
-th Tjurina numbers

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation on
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
defined by the 1-form
\omega=P(x,y)dx+Q(x,y)dy
, and let
C=\{f=0\}
be a germ of a reduced curve, with
f\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
. As in the convergent case, there exist elements
g,h\in\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]
, depending on
f
and
\omega
, such that
f
is relatively prime to both
g
and
h
, and a 1-form
\eta
satisfying

(11)

g\omega=h\,df+f\,\eta,

as established in
[
26
, Lemma 1.1]
. The GSV-index of a foliation
\mathcal{F}
with respect to the curve
C
at the point
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
is defined by

\operatorname{GSV}(\mathcal{F},C)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial C}\frac{h}{g}\,d\left(\frac{g}{h}\right),

where
g
and
h
are chosen according to equation (
11
). Alternatively, following
[
9
]
,

(12)

\operatorname{GSV}(\mathcal{F},C)=i(f,h)-i(f,g),

where
g,h\in\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]
are the elements mentioned in (
11
) and
C:\;f(x,y)=0
is a reduced curve.

The following proposition was first established by Gómez-Mont
[
14
, Theorem 1]
. A proof in dimension 2 can be found in
[
9
, Proposition 6.2]
:

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a singular foliation on
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
, and
C
a reduced curve of separatrices of
\mathcal{F}
. Then

\tau(\mathcal{F},C)-\tau(C)=\operatorname{GSV}(\mathcal{F},C).

In this section, we will relate the GSV-index of
\mathcal{F}
with the
k
-th Tjurina number of both the curve
C
and the foliation
\mathcal{F}
with respect to the curve
C
, obtaining a generalization of Proposition
8.1
:

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a singular foliation at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
, and let
C
be a reduced curve of separatrices of
\mathcal{F}
. Then

\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},C)-\tau^{k}(C)=GSV(\mathcal{F},C)\quad\text{for all}\quad k\geq 0.

Proof.

Let
\omega=Pdx+Qdy
be a 1-form inducing
\mathcal{F}
, and let
f(x,y)=0
be the reduced equation of
C
. By equality (
11
) we get
g\omega=hdf+f\eta
, where
\eta
is a 1-form and
g,h\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
, with
g
and
f
, and
h
and
f
relatively prime.

Hence
gPdx+gQdy=(hf_{x}+f\eta_{1})dx+(hf_{y}+f\eta_{2})dy
, where
\eta=\eta_{1}dx+\eta_{2}dy
. We obtain

(13)

gP=hf_{x}+f\eta_{1},\quad\text{and}\quad gQ=hf_{y}+f\eta_{2}.

From equalities (
13
), the properties of ideals, and Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle gP,gQ\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}+\langle f\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle hf_{x}+f\eta_{1},hf_{y}+f\eta_{2}\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}+\langle f\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle hf_{x},hf_{y}\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}+\langle f\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle f_{x},f_{y}\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}+\langle f\rangle}+\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle h\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}+\langle f\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{k}(C)+\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle h\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}+\langle f\rangle}.

Again, by Lemma
3.1
we get

\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle P,Q\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}+\langle f\rangle}+\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle g\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}+\langle f\rangle}=\tau^{k}(C,0)+\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle h\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}+\langle f\rangle}.

Hence, by Lemma
3.2
applied to
J=\mathfrak{m}^{k}
and equation (
12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},C)-\tau^{k}(C)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle h\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}+\langle f\rangle}-\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle g\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}+\langle f\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\mathfrak{m}^{k}+\langle f\rangle}+i(f,h)-\left(\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\mathfrak{m}^{k}+\langle f\rangle}+i(f,g)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i(f,h)-i(f,g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle GSV(\mathcal{F},C).

∎

We illustrate Theorem
8.2
with the following examples.

Example 8.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the foliation on
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
defined by
\omega=4xydx+(y-2x^{2})dy
. Note that
C=\{y=0\}
is the only separatrix curve of
\mathcal{F}
and that an equation (
11
) for
C
is
g=1
,
h=y-x^{2}
, and
\eta=4xdx
. Thus
GSV(\mathcal{F},C)=2
,
\tau^{k}(C)=k
, and
\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},C)=k+2
. Hence, we have
GSV(\mathcal{F},C)=2=\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},C)-\tau^{k}(C).

Example 8.2
.

For the Dulac’s foliation
\mathcal{F}
defined by

\omega=(ny+x^{n})dx-xdy,\quad n\geq 2,

we have
\mathcal{F}
admits a unique separatrix
C=\{x=0\}
. Since
\nu(\mathcal{F})=1\neq 0=\nu(C)-1
, so
\mathcal{F}
is not of second type.
Moreover, for all integer
k\geq 0
, we get

\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})=\frac{(k+1)(k+2)}{2}
,
\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},C)=k+1
,
\tau^{k}(C)=k
, and
GSV(\mathcal{F},C,0)=1=\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},C)-\tau^{k}(C)

Example 8.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{n,m}:\omega_{n,m}=mxdy-nydx
, and let
C=\{y^{m}-x^{n}=0\}
be a separatrix of
\mathcal{F}_{n,m}
, where
2\leq m\leq n
.
We have from
[
16
, Theorem A, item 1]
that

\tau^{k}(C)=\begin{cases}\frac{k^{2}+3k}{2}+mn-m-n+1,&\text{if $0\leq k<m$ }\\
mk+\frac{(2n-m)(m-1)}{2},&\text{$k\geq m$}\end{cases}

\mu^{k}(C)=\begin{cases}mn-m-n+1+k^{2}+k,&\text{if $0\leq k<m$ }\\
\left(m-\frac{1}{2}\right)k+\frac{(2n-m)(m-1)}{2}+\frac{k^{2}}{2},&\text{$k\geq m$}\end{cases}

and

\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F}_{n,m},C)=\begin{cases}\frac{(k+2)(k+1)}{2},&\text{if $0\leq k<m$ }\\
\frac{(k+2)(k+1)}{2}-\frac{(k+2-m)(k+1-m)}{2},&\text{$k\geq m$.}\end{cases}

On the other hand, since

my^{m-1}\cdot\omega=mxd(y^{m}-x^{n})-mn(y^{m}-x^{n})dx,

we get

\displaystyle GSV(\mathcal{F}_{n,m},C)=m+n-mn.

Note that
GSV(\mathcal{F}_{n,m},C)=\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F}_{n,m},C)-\tau^{k}(C)
for all
k\geq 0
, verifying Theorem
8.2
.

Corollary 8.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a singular foliation at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
, and let
C
be a reduced curve of separatrices of
\mathcal{F}
. Then

\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},C)-\tau^{k}(C)=\tau(\mathcal{F},C)-\tau(C)\quad\text{for all}\quad k\geq 0,

or equivalently,

\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},C)-\tau(\mathcal{F},C)=\tau^{k}(C)-\tau(C)\quad\text{for all}\quad k\geq 0.

In particular,
\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},C)-\tau(\mathcal{F},C)
does not depend on the foliation
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

It is an immediate consequence of Proposition
8.1
and Theorem
8.2
.
∎

The
k
-th Tjurina number of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
with respect to a separatrix curve
C
yield the following criterion for dicriticalness.

Corollary 8.4
.

Let
C
be a separatrix curve of
\mathcal{F}
, and suppose that
\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},C)<\tau^{k}(C)
for some
k\geq 0
. Then
\mathcal{F}
is dicritical at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

Proof.

Suppose, by contradiction, that
\mathcal{F}
is non-dicritical at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Then
C
is a non-dicritical separatrix in the sense of Brunella (see
[
2
, p. 533]
), and by
[
2
, Proposition 6]
, we have
GSV(\mathcal{F},C)\geq 0
. Hence
\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},C)\geq\tau^{k}(C)
for all
k\geq 0
, by Theorem
8.2
. This contradicts the hypothesis.
∎

9.
The
k
-th polar intersection index of a foliation

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a singular foliation on
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
defined by
\omega=P(x,y)\,dx+Q(x,y)\,dy
.

Definition 9.1
.

Let
[\alpha:\beta]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a direction in the one-dimensional projective space.
The
polar curve
of
\mathcal{F}
in the direction
[\alpha:\beta]
, denoted by
\Gamma_{[\alpha:\beta]}
, is the set of points defined by

\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{F}}_{(\alpha:\beta)}=\{(x,y)\in(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)\;:\;\alpha P(x,y)+\beta Q(x,y)=0\}.

The polar curve of a foliation was defined in
[
5
]
. Observe that the algebraic multiplicity at the origin of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
of a generic polar curve coincides with the algebraic multiplicity of
\mathcal{F}
at that point.
Also note that when
\mathcal{F}
is the Hamiltonian foliation
df
associated with a function
f\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
, the polar curve of
\mathcal{F}
coincides with the classical polar curve of
f
in the direction
[\alpha:\beta]
studied by Teissier
[
27
]
and others. According to general results on equisingularity (see
[
30
]
and
[
29
]
), there exists a Zariski-open subset
U
of the space
\mathbb{P}^{1}
of projection directions such that, for
[\alpha:\beta]\in U
, all polar curves are equisingular. Any element of this set is called the
generic polar curve
of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
and will be denoted by
\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{F}}
.

Definition 9.2
.

Let
B=\{h=0\}
be a reduced curve invariant by a singular foliation
\mathcal{F}
.
The
polar intersection number
of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to
B
is defined as the intersection number

i_{p}(\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{F}},B)=\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle\alpha P+\beta Q,\,h\rangle}

where
\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{F}}
denotes a generic polar curve associated with the foliation
\mathcal{F}
.

For properties of this number, see for instance
[
12
, Section 4]
.

Definition 9.3
.

Let
C=\{f=0\}
be a reduced invariant curve of a singular foliation
\mathcal{F}
. We define the
k
-th polar intersection number of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to
C
as the following dimension:

i^{k}(\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{F}},C)=\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle\alpha P+\beta Q,\,f\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}},

where
\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{F}}
denotes a generic polar curve associated with the foliation
\mathcal{F}
.

Note that when
k=0
, we recover the classical polar intersection number of a foliation
\mathcal{F}
with respect to
C
, given in Definition
9.2
.

9.1.
Teissier’s Lemma for the
k
-th Milnor number

In the case of the Hamiltonian foliation defined by
df=f_{x}dx+f_{y}dy
, we have:

i^{k}(\mathcal{P}^{df},C)=\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle\alpha f_{x}+\beta f_{y},\,f\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}}.

which generalizes the polar intersection number defined by Teissier
[
28
]
. In
[
28
, Chapter 4, Proposition 1.2]
, Teissier proved (in the context of complex hypersurfaces) a formula relating
i(\mathcal{P}^{df},C)
and
\mu(C)
, known as Teissier’s Lemma:

Lemma 9.1

(Teissier’s Lemma)
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
be such that
C=\{f=0\}
is a reduced curve at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Then

i(\mathcal{P}^{df},C)=\mu(C)+\nu(f)-1.

Next, we establish the
k
-th version of Teissier’s Lemma
[
28
, Chapter 4, Proposition 1.2]
in dimension 2:

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
be such that
C=\{f=0\}
is a reduced curve at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Then, for all integers
k\geq 0
, we have

i^{k}(\mathcal{P}^{df},C)=\mu^{k}(C)+\nu(f)-1.

Proof.

By applying Lemma
3.3
to
J=\mathfrak{m}^{k}
,
\psi=\mathcal{P}^{f}=\alpha f_{x}+\beta f_{y}
, where
\alpha
and
\beta
are generic, and
\varphi=f
, we obtain

(14)

i^{k}(\mathcal{P}^{df},C)=i(\mathcal{P}^{df},C)+\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\mathfrak{m}^{k}}+\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle\alpha f_{x}+\beta f_{y}\rangle+\mathfrak{m}^{k}}

Again, assuming
\nu(f_{x})\leq\nu(f_{y})
, by applying Lemma
3.3
to
J=\mathfrak{m}^{k}
,
\psi=f_{x}
, and
\varphi=f_{y}
, we obtain

(15)

\mu^{k}(C)=\mu(C)+\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\mathfrak{m}^{k}}+\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle f_{x}\rangle+\mathfrak{m}^{k}}

From (
14
) and (
15
), we get:

i^{k}(\mathcal{P}^{df},C)-\mu^{k}(C)=i(\mathcal{P}^{df},C)-\mu(C)

since
\dim\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle\alpha f_{x}+\beta f_{y}\rangle+\mathfrak{m}^{k}}=\dim\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle f_{x}\rangle+\mathfrak{m}^{k}}
for generic
\alpha
and
\beta
, by Remark
4.1
. The proof follows from Lemma
9.1
.
∎

We now present an example illustrating our result above.

Example 9.1
.

Let
C
be an isolated binomial singularity defined by
f=x^{2}+y^{2}
. Then, by
[
17
, Proposition 2.7]
,

\mu^{k}(C)=\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle f_{x},f_{y}\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}}=\begin{cases}1+k^{2}+k,&\text{if $0\leq k<2$ }\\
\frac{3}{2}k+1+\frac{k^{2}}{2},&\text{if $k\geq 2$}\end{cases}

and the
k
-th polar intersection number of
df
with respect to
C
is

i^{k}(\mathcal{P}^{df},C)=\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle f_{x}+f_{y},f\rangle\mathfrak{m}^{k}}=\begin{cases}2+k^{2}+k,&\text{if $0\leq k<2$ }\\
\frac{3}{2}k+2+\frac{k^{2}}{2},&\text{if $k\geq 2$}\end{cases}

thus,
i^{k}(\mathcal{P}^{df},C)-\mu^{k}(C)=\nu(C)-1
.

Now, we present a formula that relates the
k
-th intersection indices of a foliation
\mathcal{F}
and an invariant curve
C=\{f=0\}
of
\mathcal{F}
with the index
GSV(\mathcal{F},C)
.

Theorem 9.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a non-dicritical foliation on
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
, and let
C=\{f=0\}
be the total union of separatrices of
\mathcal{F}
. If
\mathcal{F}
is of second type, then

i^{k}(\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{F}},C)-i^{k}(\mathcal{P}^{df},C)=i(\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{F}},C)-i(\mathcal{P}^{df},C)=GSV(\mathcal{F},C).

Proof.

Suppose that
\omega=Pdx+Qdy
. By applying Lemma
3.3
to
J=\mathfrak{m}^{k}
,
\psi=\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{F}}=\alpha P+\beta Q
, and
\varphi=f
, we have

i^{k}(\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{F}},C)=i(\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{F}},C)+\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\mathfrak{m}^{k}}+\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle\alpha P+\beta Q\rangle+\mathfrak{m}^{k}},

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are generic. Similarly, by Lemma
3.3
, we obtain

i^{k}(\mathcal{P}^{df},C)=i(\mathcal{P}^{df},C)+\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\mathfrak{m}^{k}}+\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle\alpha f_{x}+\beta f_{y}\rangle+\mathfrak{m}^{k}}.

Since
\mathcal{F}
is of second type, then
\nu(\mathcal{F})=\nu(\alpha P+\beta Q)=\nu(C)-1=\nu(\alpha f_{x}+\beta f_{y})
, by Proposition
2.1
. Consequently

\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle\alpha P+\beta Q\rangle+\mathfrak{m}^{k}}=\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle\alpha f_{x}+\beta f_{y}\rangle+\mathfrak{m}^{k}},

by Remark
4.1
. The proof ends by taking the difference
i^{k}(\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{F}},C)-i^{k}(\mathcal{P}^{df},C)
and observing that
i(\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{F}},C)-i(\mathcal{P}^{df},C)=GSV(\mathcal{F},C)
(see
[
9
, Proposition 5.4]
).
∎

10.
A bound for the
k
-th Milnor number of a foliation

We now present the following result. The proof is an adaptation of
[
20
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
18
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Theorem 10.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a singular holomorphic foliation of second type at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
given by the 1-form
\omega=P(x,y)dx+Q(x,y)dy
. Let
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{0}-\mathcal{B}_{\infty}
be a balanced divisor of separatrices for
\mathcal{F}
with
\mathcal{B}_{0}=\{f(x,y)=0\}
. Then

\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}_{0})\leq\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})\leq 2\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}_{0})+\frac{k(k+1)}{2}.

Proof.

We only need to show that
\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})\leq 2\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}_{0})+\frac{k(k+1)}{2}
. Let us consider the following exact sequence:

0\to Ker(f)\to M^{k}_{\mathcal{F}}\xrightarrow{f}M^{k}_{\mathcal{F}}\to T^{k}_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}_{0}}\to 0,

where
M^{k}_{\mathcal{F}}=\mathcal{O}_{2}/\mathfrak{m}^{k}\langle P,Q\rangle
,
T^{k}_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}_{0}}=\mathcal{O}_{2}/(\mathfrak{m}^{k}\langle P,Q\rangle+\langle f\rangle)
, the middle map is multiplication by
f
, and
Ker(f)
is the kernel of this map.

According to the Briançon-Skoda theorem for foliations
[
8
]
, we have
f^{2}\in\langle P,Q\rangle
, and therefore
(f^{2})\mathfrak{m}^{k}=0
in
M^{k}_{\mathcal{F}}
. As a consequence, we obtain a finite decreasing filtration:

M^{k}_{\mathcal{F}}\supset(f)\supset(f)\mathfrak{m}^{k}\supset(f^{2})\mathfrak{m}^{k}=0.

Now consider the following exact sequence:

0\to Ker(f)\cap(f)\mathfrak{m}^{k}\to(f)\mathfrak{m}^{k}\xrightarrow{f}(f)\mathfrak{m}^{k}\to(f)\mathfrak{m}^{k}/(f^{2})\mathfrak{m}^{k}\to 0,

where the middle map is multiplication by
f
. Then

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{(f)\mathfrak{m}^{k}/(f^{2})\mathfrak{m}^{k}\right\}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(Ker(f)\cap(f)\mathfrak{m}^{k})\leq\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Ker(f)=\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}_{0},0).

Thus

\displaystyle\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M^{k}_{\mathcal{F}}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}T^{k}_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}_{0}}+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{(f)}{(f)\mathfrak{m}^{k}}+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{(f)\mathfrak{m}^{k}}{(f^{2})\mathfrak{m}^{k}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}_{0})+\frac{k(k+1)}{2}.

∎

11.
On
k
-th Milnor and Tjurina numbers for quasi-homogeneous foliations

In this section, we consider quasi-homogeneous foliations as defined in
[
21
, p. 178]
. Given a germ of a singular holomorphic foliation
\mathcal{F}
defined by
\omega=P\,dx+Q\,dy
, assume that
\mathcal{F}
is non-dicritical and let
C=\{f=0\}
be the total union of separatrices of
\mathcal{F}
at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. We say that
\mathcal{F}
is a
quasi-homogeneous foliation
if
f\in\langle P,Q\rangle
.

We prove the following:

Theorem 11.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a non-dicritical generalized curve foliation defined by
\omega=P(x,y)\,dx+Q(x,y)\,dy
at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is a quasi-homogeneous foliation. Let
C=\{f(x,y)=0\}
be the total union of separatrices of
\mathcal{F}
at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Then,

\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})=\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},C)+\frac{k(k-1)}{2},\quad\forall\,k\geq 1.

Proof.

First, we show that
\mathfrak{m}^{k-1}\langle f\rangle\subset\mathfrak{m}^{k}\langle P,Q\rangle
.
Indeed, by Mattei’s theorem
[
23
, Théorème A]
, there exists a coordinate system
(x,y)
such that, in this system,
f
is a quasi-homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
with weights
(\alpha,\beta)
, and there exist nonzero
g\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
and
h\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
such that

g\omega=df+h(\beta y\,dx-\alpha x\,dy),

where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\omega=P\,dx+Q\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{df+h(\beta y\,dx-\alpha x\,dy)}{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{f_{x}\,dx+f_{y}\,dy+h(\beta y\,dx-\alpha x\,dy)}{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(f_{x}+\beta hy)\,dx}{g}+\frac{(f_{y}-\alpha hx)\,dy}{g}.

Thus,
P=\frac{f_{x}+\beta hy}{g}
and
Q=\frac{f_{y}-\alpha hx}{g}
.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
g\equiv 1
, so

P=f_{x}+\beta hy,\quad Q=f_{y}-\alpha hx.

Hence,

\alpha xP=\alpha xf_{x}+\alpha\beta x(hy),\qquad\beta yQ=\beta yf_{y}-\beta\alpha y(hx).

Therefore,

\alpha xP+\beta yQ=\alpha xf_{x}+\beta yf_{y},

and by Euler’s identity, we have

\alpha xP+\beta yQ=d\cdot f\Longrightarrow\langle f\rangle\subset\mathfrak{m}\langle P,Q\rangle\Longrightarrow\mathfrak{m}^{k-1}\langle f\rangle\subset\mathfrak{m}^{k}\langle P,Q\rangle,\quad\text{for all }k\geq 1.

Next, using the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{k}\langle P,Q\rangle+\langle f\rangle}{\mathfrak{m}^{k}\langle P,Q\rangle}\longrightarrow\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\mathfrak{m}^{k}\langle P,Q\rangle}\longrightarrow\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\mathfrak{m}^{k}\langle P,Q\rangle+\langle f\rangle}\longrightarrow 0,

we have

\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\mathfrak{m}^{k}\langle P,Q\rangle}=\dim\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{k}\langle P,Q\rangle+\langle f\rangle}{\mathfrak{m}^{k}\langle P,Q\rangle}+\dim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\mathfrak{m}^{k}\langle P,Q\rangle+\langle f\rangle}.

Therefore, we get

\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})=\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},C)+\dim\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{k}\langle P,Q\rangle+\langle f\rangle}{\mathfrak{m}^{k}\langle P,Q\rangle}.

Next, by applying
[
17
, Lemma 2.5]
and using that
\mathfrak{m}^{k-1}\langle f\rangle\subset\mathfrak{m}^{k}\langle P,Q\rangle
, we have

\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{k}\langle P,Q\rangle+\langle f\rangle}{\mathfrak{m}^{k}\langle P,Q\rangle}\cong\frac{\langle f\rangle\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\mathfrak{m}^{k}\langle P,Q\rangle\cap\langle f\rangle\mathcal{O}_{2}}\cong\frac{\langle f\rangle\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\mathfrak{m}^{k-1}\langle f\rangle}\cong\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\mathfrak{m}^{k-1}}.

Therefore, we conclude

\mu^{k}(\mathcal{F})=\tau^{k}(\mathcal{F},C)+\frac{k(k-1)}{2}.

∎

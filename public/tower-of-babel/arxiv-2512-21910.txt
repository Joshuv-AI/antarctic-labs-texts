Fano Fibrations and Twisted Kähler-Einstein Metrics II: The Kähler-Ricci Flow
Alexander Bednarek

Abstract
This is the second of two papers studying both the geometric structure of Fano fibrations and the application to Kähler-Ricci flows developing a singularity in finite time. We assume that the Kähler-Ricci flow on a compact Kähler manifold has a rational initial metric and develops a singularity in finite time such that the manifold admits a Fano fibration structure. Moreover, it is assumed that the volume form of the flow collapses uniformly at the rate of C − 1 ​ ( T − t ) n − m ​ Ω ≤ ω ​ ( t ) n ≤ C ​ ( T − t ) n − m ​ Ω C^{-1}(T-t)^{n-m}\Omega\leq\omega(t)^{n}\leq C(T-t)^{n-m}\Omega . Under this setting, a diameter bound is obtained in any compact set away from singular fibres and the diameter of the fibres is proven to collapse at the optimal rate T − t \sqrt{T-t} . Furthermore, several precise C 0 C^{0} -estimates are proven for the potential of the complex Monge-Ampere flow which involve the potentials of singular twisted Kähler-Einstein metrics on the base variety from part I. Finally, in the case of Kähler-Einstein Fano fibres, we deduce Type I scalar curvature in any compact set away from singular fibres and globally for a submersion.

Now, in order to extract several important results for the case of interest, we take the following assumption on the collapsing of the volume form along the Kähler-Ricci flow. We assume that there exists constants

The Kähler-Ricci Flow

Let
(X,\omega_{0})
be a compact Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n=\dim X
. We consider the following normalization of the Kähler-Ricci flow,

\frac{\partial\omega(t)}{\partial t}=-\Ric(\omega(t))-\omega(t),\quad\omega(0)=\omega_{0}.

(KRF)

The (
KRF
) exists for a maximal time
T\in(0,\infty]
determined by the following cohomological condition

T=\sup\{t\in[0,\infty):e^{-t}[\omega_{0}]-(1-e^{-t})2\pi c_{1}(X)\in\Kah(X)\},

where
\Kah(X)
denotes the Kähler cone in
H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
,
[
28
]
.

We assume
T<\infty
and the initial metric is rational, i.e.
[\omega_{0}]\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{Q})
and there exists an ample
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
L
such that
2\pi c_{1}(L)=[\omega_{0}]
. By Kawamata’s rationality theorem,

D:=e^{-T}L+(1-e^{-T})K_{X}

is a rational line bundle, i.e.
e^{-T}\in\mathbb{Q}
, and Kawamata’s basepoint free theorem implies that
D
is a semi-ample line bundle. That is, for a sufficiently large
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
kD
generates a proper, holomorphic, surjective map with connected fibres

f:X\to B\subseteq\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,kD)

where
B=f(X)
denotes the image of
f
and is an irreducible, normal, analytic variety. Additionally,
f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{B}(1)=kD
so that if we set
\eta=\frac{1}{k}\omega_{FS}|_{B}
, where
\omega_{FS}
is the Fubini-Study metric on
\mathbb{CP}^{N}
and
k
is fixed to be the smallest
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
which generates the map
f
and satisfies
k^{\prime}=k(1-e^{-T})\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, then

[\omega(T)]=2\pi c_{1}(D)=\frac{2\pi}{k}f^{*}c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{B}(1))=[f^{*}\eta],

where we take the
[\omega(T)]
to be the limiting class of the flow,
e^{-T}[\omega_{0}]-(1-e^{-T})2\pi c_{1}(X)
.

Let us denote the singular set of
B
together with the critical points of
f
by
S^{\prime}
and set
S=f^{-1}(S^{\prime})
. Thus,
f:X\backslash S\to B\backslash S^{\prime}
is a submersion and
X_{b}=f^{-1}(b)
is a Kähler manifold equipped with Kähler metric
\omega_{0,b}=\omega_{0}|_{X_{b}}
for all
b\in B\backslash S^{\prime}
. By restricting the cohomological equation
[f^{*}\eta]=[\omega(T)]
to any non-singular fibre
X_{b}
, we see that the fibres are also Fano, i.e.
2\pi c_{1}(X_{b})=\lambda[\omega_{0,b}]
where
\lambda=\frac{e^{-T}}{1-e^{-T}}>0
is a positive constant. Finally, we let the Iitaka dimension be denoted by
m=\dim B
and we assume that the fibres are of intermediate dimension, i.e.
0<m<n
.

Let us reduce the Kähler-Ricci flow to a complex Monge-Ampere flow. As the cohomology class
[\omega(t)]
of the Kähler-Ricci flow evolves according to

[\omega(t)]=\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-T}}{1-e^{-T}}[\omega_{0}]+\frac{1-e^{-t}}{1-e^{-T}}[\omega(T)],

we consider the reference form
\omega_{REF}(t)\in[\omega(t)]
defined by

\omega_{REF}(t)=\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-T}}{1-e^{-T}}\omega_{0}+\frac{1-e^{-t}}{1-e^{-T}}f^{*}\eta.

Note
\omega_{REF}(t)
is in fact a Kähler metric for every
t\in[0,T)
. By the
\partial\overline{\partial}
-lemma there exists a smooth function
\varphi:X\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\omega(t)=\omega_{REF}(t)+i\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi(t).

Moreover, as
[f^{*}\eta]=[\omega(T)]=e^{-T}[\omega_{0}]-(1-e^{-T})2\pi c_{1}(X)
, we have

\chi:=\lambda\omega_{0}-\frac{1}{1-e^{-T}}f^{*}\eta\in 2\pi c_{1}(X)

so that we can choose a volume form
\Omega
on
X
such that
\chi=\Ric\Omega
. We fix
\Omega
to be unique by imposing

\int_{X}\Omega=\int_{X}{n\choose m}\omega_{0}^{n-m}\wedge f^{*}\eta^{m}.

It follows that the (
KRF
) reduces to the following complex Monge-Ampere flow

\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}=\log\frac{E(t)^{-(n-m)}\omega(t)^{n}}{\Omega}-\varphi,\quad\varphi(0)=0,

(CMA)

where we set
E(t)=\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-T}}{1-e^{-T}}
for convenience. We define

u=(1-e^{t-T})\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}+\varphi

to be the Ricci potential since one can show,
[
41
]
, the Ricci curvature decomposes in terms of the Ricci potential according to

(1-e^{t-T})\Ric(\omega(t))=e^{t-T}\omega(t)-f^{*}\eta-i\partial\overline{\partial}u.

Additionally, the scalar curvature is given by

(1-e^{t-T})R_{\omega(t)}=e^{t-T}n-\tr_{\omega(t)}f^{*}\eta-\Delta_{\omega(t)}u.

Consequences of Uniform Volume Form Collapsing

First, an observation.

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists a constant
C=C(n,m,\omega_{0},f^{*}\eta,T)>0
such that for all
t\in[0,T)
,

C^{-1}E(t)^{n-m}\leq\Vol(X,\omega(t))\leq CE(t)^{n-m}.

In fact,

\lim_{t\to T}E(t)^{-(n-m)}\Vol(X,\omega(t))=\Vol(X,\Omega_{\omega_{0},\eta}),

where we use the notation

\Omega_{\omega_{0},\eta}={n\choose m}\omega_{0}^{n-m}\wedge f^{*}\eta^{m}.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of the cohomological evolution of the Kähler-Ricci flow. Applying
\eta^{k}=0
for all
m<k\leq n
, we have

\displaystyle E(t)^{-(n-m)}\Vol(X,\omega(t))

\displaystyle=\int_{X}E(t)^{-(n-m)}\omega_{REF}^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\sum_{k=0}^{m}{n\choose k}E(t)^{m-k}(1-E(t))^{k}\omega_{0}^{n-k}\wedge f^{*}\eta^{k}

and the limit as
t\to T
is equal to
\int_{X}\Omega_{\omega_{0},\eta}
.
∎

This is well-known, i.e., that the volume along the flow collapses at the rate
E(t)^{n-m}
. As a consequence, one naturally expects that, in general, the volume form
\omega(t)^{n}
collapses uniformly at the
E(t)^{n-m}
-rate globally on
X
. It is known for the continuity method that this is true
[
39
, Lem. 3.2]
, i.e.
\omega(t)^{n}\sim(T-t)^{n-m}\Omega
, but this behaviour equivalent to the
C^{0}
-bound of the potential function
\varphi
, which is unfortunately not the case for the Kähler-Ricci flow. On the other hand, when the Kähler-Ricci flow is run on a manifold with semi-ample canonical line bundle
K_{X}
, the flow develops a singularity at infinite time and
X
admits a Calabi-Yau fibration structure. It is known that the volume form collapses at a rate of
\omega(t)^{n}\sim e^{-(n-m)t}\Omega
in this setting,
[
25
]
,
[
10
, Lem. 2.3]
, which would correspond to letting
T\to\infty
in
E(t)
.

We take a slightly weaker version of the uniform collapsing of the volume form as an assumption. That is, we assume there exists a positive constant
U_{X}>0
and for any compact set
K\subseteq X\backslash S
a positive constant
C_{K}>0
, such that

\displaystyle C_{K}^{-1}E(t)^{n-m}\Omega\leq\omega(t)^{n}\leq C_{K}E(t)^{n-m}\Omega\quad\text{on}\quad K\times[0,T),

(VFC)

\displaystyle\omega(t)^{n}\leq U_{X}E(t)^{n-m}\Omega\quad\text{on}\quad X\times[0,T).

Clearly these assumptions are independent of the choice of volume form
\Omega
. Moreover, if
f:X\to B
is a submersion then we can simply set
K=X
. We also comment that it is useful to distinguish the local lower bounds and global upper bound with different constants
C_{K}
,
U_{X}
.

Remark 3.2
.

We compare this assumption with the work of Fong
[
9
]
. Fong considers the case where the map
f:X\to B
is a submersion and assumes that the Ricci curvature is uniformly bounded above along the flow, i.e.
\Ric\omega(t)\leq C\omega_{0}
on
X\times[0,T)
for some
C>0
. This is a strong assumption but allows Fong to find an upper bound on
\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}
and
U_{X}
and, subsequently, an optimal bound on the collapsing rate of the diameters of the fibres. We can achieve similar results with our much weaker assumption (
VFC
) and extend them to the general case where
X
has singular fibres.

Proposition 3.3
.

There exists a constant
C=C(n,m,\omega_{0},f^{*}\eta,T)>0
such that on
X\times[0,T)
we have

\varphi\leq C.

Furthermore, if
f:X\to B
is a submersion of compact Kähler manifolds then we also have

\varphi\geq-C,

on
X\times[0,T)
for some
C=C(n,m,\omega_{0},f^{*}\eta,T)>0
.

Proof.

Note that

E(t)^{-(n-m)}\omega_{REF}^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}{n\choose k}E(t)^{m-k}(1-E(t))^{k}\omega_{0}^{n-k}\wedge f^{*}\eta^{k}

satisfies

C^{-1}\Omega_{\omega_{0},\eta}\leq E(t)^{-(n-m)}\omega_{REF}^{n}\leq C\Omega

on
X\times[T/2,T)
, since there exists a
C>0
such that for all
0\leq k\leq m

0\leq\omega_{0}^{n-k}\wedge f^{*}\eta^{k}\leq C\Omega

and we can bound the term
(1-E(t))^{m}
below on
[T/2,T)
.

Therefore, by applying the maximum principle to (
CMA
) we find that at a maximal point
(x^{*},t^{*})
of
\varphi
,

\varphi(x^{*},t^{*})\leq\log\frac{E(t^{*})^{-(n-m)}\omega^{n}_{REF}(x^{*},t^{*})}{\Omega(x^{*})}\leq C.

Thus
\varphi\leq C
on
X\times[0,T)
.

Additionally, if
f:X\to B
is a submersion then
\Omega_{\omega_{0},\eta}
is a global volume form on
X
. It follows that at a minimal point
(x_{*},t_{*})
of
\varphi

\varphi(x_{*},t_{*})\geq\log\frac{E(t_{*})^{-(n-m)}\omega_{REF}(x_{*},t_{*})^{n}}{\Omega(x_{*})}\geq\log\frac{C^{-1}\Omega_{\omega_{0},\eta}(x_{*})}{\Omega(x_{*})}\geq-C,

and thus
\varphi\geq-C
on
X\times[T/2,T)
. This yields the desired result since
X\times[0,T/2]
is compact.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

We have the following evolution equations

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}+\varphi\right)

\displaystyle=-n+\frac{n-m}{1-e^{t-T}}-\lambda\tr_{\omega(t)}\omega_{0}+\frac{1}{1-e^{-T}}\tr_{\omega(t)}f^{*}\eta,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)u

\displaystyle=-m+\tr_{\omega(t)}f^{*}\eta.

Proof.

Applying
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
to (
CMA
) yields

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}+\varphi\right)=\frac{n-m}{1-e^{t-T}}-R-n,

(3.1)

whereas applying
i\partial\overline{\partial}
to (
CMA
) yields

i\partial\overline{\partial}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}+\varphi\right)=-\Ric\omega+\lambda\omega_{0}-\frac{1}{1-e^{-T}}f^{*}\eta.

The first evolution equation follows. On the other hand, observe that

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)u=(1-e^{t-T})\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}+\varphi\right)-e^{t-T}\Delta\varphi.

The second evolution equation then follows from the first and by taking the trace of
\omega(t)=\omega_{REF}(t)+i\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

There exists a constant
C=C(n,m,\omega_{0},f^{*}\eta,T)>0
such that on
X\times[0,T)
we have

|u|\leq C.

Proof.

From the evolution equation for
u
, it follows that

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)u\geq-m

and the maximum principle implies
-C\leq u
. On the other hand, by the uniform lower bound on scalar curvature, it follows from equation (
3.1
) that

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}+\varphi+(n-m)\log(e^{-t}-e^{-T})-Ct\right)\leq 0.

Integration yields

\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}+\varphi+(n-m)\log(e^{-t}-e^{-T})-Ct\leq C

and the desired result,
u\leq C
, will quickly follow.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

We note that this bound on
\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}
in combination with that of
\varphi
only implies

which is already known and not desired. Also, the lower bound for
u
in combination with the upper bound of

Lemma 3.7

(Parabolic Schwarz Lemma)
.

For any Kähler metric
\theta
on
\mathbb{CP}^{N}
, there exists a
C=C(n,m,\omega_{0},f^{*}\eta,f^{*}\theta,T)>0
such that on
X\times[0,T)
we have

0\leq\tr_{\omega(t)}f^{*}\theta\leq C.

Moreover, for any smooth
(1,1)
-form
\alpha
on
\mathbb{CP}^{N}
, there exists a positive constant
C=C(n,m,\omega_{0},f^{*}\eta,f^{*}\alpha,T)>0
such that on
X\times[0,T)
we have

-C\leq\tr_{\omega(t)}f^{*}\alpha\leq C.

Proof.

It is well known that the following inequality holds

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\log(\tr_{\omega(t)}f^{*}\eta)\leq C\tr_{\omega(t)}f^{*}\eta+1

where
C
is a constant depending on the bisectional curvature of
\eta
,
[
23
]
. Therefore, choosing a constant
A
sufficiently large such that
A\geq C+1
yields

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\left(\log(\tr_{\omega(t)}f^{*}\eta)-Au\right)\leq-\tr_{\omega(t)}f^{*}\eta+Am+1.

The maximum principle implies that
\tr_{\omega(t)}f^{*}\eta\leq C
. Now, for any
(1,1)
-form
\alpha
we can choose
A
to be a sufficiently large constant such that
A\eta-\alpha>0
is a Kähler metric on
\mathbb{CP}^{N}
. Hence,

\tr_{\omega(t)}f^{*}\alpha\leq A\tr_{\omega(t)}f^{*}\eta\leq C.

The lower bound follows by the same argument.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

If the (
KRF
) satisfies (
VFC
) then there exists positive constant
C=C(n,m,\omega_{0},f^{*}\eta,T,U_{X})
such that on
X\times[0,T)
,

\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}\leq C.

Also, for any compact set
K\subseteq X\backslash S
, there exists a constant
C=C(n,m,\omega_{0},f^{*}\eta,T,C_{K})>0
such that

\left|\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}\right|\leq C

on
K\times[0,T)
.

Proof.

We note that (
VFC
) implies

\left|\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}+\varphi\right|\leq C

on
K\times[0,T)
and thus

\left|e^{t-T}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}\right|\leq|u|+\left|\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}+\varphi\right|\leq C.

Likewise, on
X\times[0,T)
, (
VFC
) yields

\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}+\varphi\leq C

and so

e^{t-T}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}=-u+\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}+\varphi\leq C.

The result follows.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

If the (
KRF
) satisfies (
VFC
) then there exists a constant
C=C(n,m,\omega_{0},f^{*}\eta,T,U_{X})>0
such that on
X\times[0,T)
we have

\varphi\geq-C.

Proof.

We note that for any
p>1

\int_{X}\left(\frac{E(t)^{n-m}e^{\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}}+\varphi}{\Vol(X,\omega(t))}\right)^{p}\Omega\leq C

by Proposition
3.8
. So applying
[
24
, Thm 2.5]
, (see also
[
7
,
8
]
), yields

\sup_{X}\varphi-\inf_{X}\varphi\leq C.

Finally,

e^{\sup_{X}(\varphi+\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t})}\int_{X}\Omega\geq\int_{X}e^{\varphi+\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}}\Omega=\int_{X}E(t)^{-(n-m)}\omega(t)^{n}\geq C^{-1}

\sup_{X}\left(\varphi+\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}\right)\geq-C.

Therefore, we get our desired lower bound by applying Proposition
3.8
again

\inf_{X}\varphi\geq-C+\sup_{X}\varphi\geq-C+\sup_{X}\left(\varphi+\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}\right)-\sup_{X}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}\geq-C.

∎

Proposition 3.10
.

If the (
KRF
) satisfies (
VFC
) then for any Kähler metric
\theta
on
X
, there exists a positive constant
C=C(n,m,\omega_{0},f^{*}\eta,\theta,T,K,C_{K})
such that on
K\times[0,T)
we have

0<\tr_{\omega(t)}\theta\leq\frac{C}{E(t)}.

Moreover, for any smooth
(1,1)
-form
\alpha
on
X
, there exists a positive constant
C=C(n,m,\omega_{0},f^{*}\eta,\alpha,T,K,C_{K})
such that on
K\times[0,T)
we have

\frac{-C}{E(t)}\leq\tr_{\omega(t)}\alpha\leq\frac{C}{E(t)}.

Proof.

Note that

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\log(\tr_{\omega(t)}\omega_{0})\leq C\tr_{\omega(t)}\omega_{0}+1

where
C
is a constant depending on the bisectional curvature of
\omega_{0}
,
[
3
, Thm. 3.2.6]
. Now, let
A>0
be a constant to be determined, and observe that for

Q_{1}=\log(e^{-t}-e^{-T})\tr_{\omega(t)}\omega_{0}+A(\partial_{t}\varphi+\varphi)

we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)Q_{1}

\displaystyle\leq(C-\lambda A)\tr_{\omega(t)}\omega_{0}+1-\frac{e^{-t}}{e^{-t}-e^{-T}}

\displaystyle+A\left(-n+\frac{n-m}{1-e^{t-T}}+\frac{1}{1-e^{-T}}\tr_{\omega(t)}f^{*}\eta\right)

\displaystyle\leq(C-\lambda A)\tr_{\omega(t)}\omega_{0}+\frac{C}{e^{-t}-e^{-T}}.

Next let
\Upsilon=e^{-C_{1}\chi^{-C_{2}}}
for some constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
, so that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\Upsilon Q_{1}

\displaystyle=\Upsilon\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)Q_{1}+\left(\frac{|\nabla\Upsilon|^{2}}{\Upsilon}-\Delta\Upsilon\right)Q_{1}-2\Real\left\langle\nabla(\Upsilon Q_{1}),\frac{\overline{\nabla}\Upsilon}{\Upsilon}\right\rangle.

The middle term can be estimated by first calculating

\displaystyle\Delta\Upsilon

\displaystyle=C_{1}C_{2}(\Delta\chi)\chi^{-C_{2}-1}\Upsilon-C_{1}C_{2}(C_{2}+1)|\nabla\chi|^{2}\chi^{-C_{2}-2}\Upsilon+C_{1}^{2}C^{2}_{2}|\nabla\chi|^{2}\chi^{-2C_{2}-2}\Upsilon

and

\displaystyle|\nabla\Upsilon|^{2}

\displaystyle=C_{1}^{2}C_{2}^{2}|\nabla\chi|^{2}\chi^{-2C_{2}-2}\Upsilon^{2}

and observing that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{|\nabla\Upsilon|^{2}}{\Upsilon}-\Delta\Upsilon\right)Q_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2\left|\log\sqrt{(e^{-t}-e^{-T})\tr_{\omega(t)}\omega_{0})}\right|+A|\partial_{t}\varphi+\varphi|\right)C\chi^{-2C_{2}-2}\Upsilon

\displaystyle\leq C\left|\log\sqrt{(e^{-t}-e^{-T})\tr_{\omega(t)}\omega_{0}}\right|\chi^{-2C_{2}-2}\Upsilon+C\chi^{-2C_{2}-2}\Upsilon,

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
|\partial_{t}\varphi+\varphi|\chi^{-2C_{2}-2}\Upsilon
is supported on
U
and we can always find another compact set
K^{\prime}
such that
K\subset\subset U\subset\subset K^{\prime}\subset\subset X\backslash S
and apply
|\partial_{t}\varphi|\leq C(C_{K^{\prime}})
.

All together we find

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\Upsilon Q_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left((C-\lambda A)\tr_{\omega(t)}\omega_{0}+\frac{C}{e^{-t}-e^{-T}}\right)\Upsilon

\displaystyle+C\left|\log\sqrt{(e^{-t}-e^{-T})\tr_{\omega(t)}\omega_{0}}\right|\chi^{-2C_{2}-2}\Upsilon+C\chi^{-2C_{2}-2}\Upsilon

\displaystyle-2\Real\left\langle\nabla(\Upsilon Q_{1}),\frac{\overline{\nabla}\Upsilon}{\Upsilon}\right\rangle.

Let
(x^{*},t^{*})
be a point at which
\Upsilon Q_{1}
achieves a maximum. If
x^{*}\in X\backslash U
then
(\Upsilon Q_{1})(x^{*},t^{*})=0
is bounded. If
x^{*}\in U
then we can also assume
(e^{-t^{*}}-e^{-T})\tr_{\omega(x^{*},t^{*})}\omega_{0}(x^{*})>1
as otherwise we get

(\Upsilon Q_{1})(x^{*},t^{*})\leq 0+A(\partial_{t}\varphi(x^{*},t^{*})+\varphi(x^{*},t^{*}))\Upsilon(x^{*})\leq C

since
\Upsilon\leq C
and
\partial_{t}\varphi+\varphi\leq C
on the compact set
K^{\prime}
containing
U
. Therefore,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\left((C-\lambda A)\tr_{\omega(x^{*},t^{*})}\omega_{0}(x^{*})+\frac{C}{e^{-t^{*}}-e^{-T}}\right)\Upsilon(x^{*})

\displaystyle+C\log\left(\sqrt{(e^{-t^{*}}-e^{-T})\tr_{\omega(x^{*},t^{*})}\omega_{0}(x^{*})}\right)\chi(x^{*})^{-2C_{2}-2}\Upsilon(x^{*})+C\chi(x^{*})^{-2C_{2}-2}\Upsilon(x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\left((C-\lambda A)\tr_{\omega(x^{*},t^{*})}\omega_{0}(x^{*})+\frac{C}{e^{-t^{*}}-e^{-T}}\right)\Upsilon(x^{*})

\displaystyle+C\Big((e^{-t^{*}}-e^{-T})\tr_{\omega(x^{*},t^{*})}\omega_{0}(x^{*})\chi(x^{*})^{2C_{2}+2}+\chi(x^{*})^{-2C_{2}-2}\Big)\chi(x^{*})^{-2C_{2}-2}\Upsilon(x^{*})

\displaystyle+C\chi(x^{*})^{-2C_{2}-2}\Upsilon(x^{*}).

It follows that

\displaystyle(\lambda A-C)\left(\tr_{\omega(x^{*},t^{*})}\omega_{0}(x^{*})\right)\Upsilon(x^{*})\leq\frac{C}{e^{-t^{*}}-e^{-T}}\Upsilon(x^{*})+C\chi(x^{*})^{-4C_{2}-4}\Upsilon(x^{*}),

so by choosing

sufficiently large so that

we have

(e^{-t^{*}}-e^{-T})\tr_{\omega(x^{*},t^{*})}\omega_{0}(x^{*})\leq C,

and finally, since

,

(\Upsilon Q_{1})(x^{*},t^{*})\leq C.

Therefore we conclude that for all

,

so that

(e^{-t}-e^{-T})\tr_{\omega(t)}\omega_{0}\leq e^{(C\Upsilon^{-1}-A(\partial_{t}\varphi+\varphi))}

and the desired result for

follows. We extend to the remaining cases via the same argument as the parabolic Schwarz lemma.

Proposition 3.11
.

If the (

) satisfies (

) then there exists a positive constant

Corollary 3.12
.

If the (
KRF
) satisfies
(\ref{VFC})
then for any compact set

Now, we recall the construction of the semi-prescribed Ricci curvature form

\displaystyle+2(1-\varepsilon)\Real\left\langle\nabla(\Upsilon Q_{3}),\frac{\overline{\nabla}v}{v}\right\rangle-2\Real\left\langle\nabla(\Upsilon Q_{3}),\frac{\overline{\nabla}\Upsilon}{\Upsilon}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+2(1-\varepsilon)\Real\left\langle\nabla(\Upsilon Q_{3}),\frac{\overline{\nabla}v}{v}\right\rangle-2\Real\left\langle\nabla(\Upsilon Q_{3}),\frac{\overline{\nabla}\Upsilon}{\Upsilon}\right\rangle

Now let

achieves a maximum. If

then
(\Upsilon Q_{3})(x^{*},t^{*})=0
is bounded. If

It follows that for all

∎

The method to use the convergence of the Ricci potential to the potentials of the twisted Kähler-Einstein metrics in order to find estimates for the Li-Yau type quantities is the common approach between Jian’s result and ours. We expect that (

In this small note, we first study the twisted Picard bundles

where
\mathcal{P}_{x}:=\mathcal{P}|_{\overline{J}(Y)\times x},x\in J(Y)

Theorem 1.1
.

(Theorem

Assume that

For

.

(Theorem

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

Let
Y
denote an irreducible projective curve with at most nodes as singularities and defined over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
.
Let
y_{1},\cdots,y_{k}
be the nodes of
Y
and
Y_{0}
be the set of smooth points of
Y
.
Let

p:X\to Y

be the normalisation map.

Let
g=g(Y):=h^{1}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})
be the arithmetic genus of
Y
.
Since
Y
is a Gorenstein curve, it has a locally free dualising sheaf
\omega_{Y}
.
Fix a base point
t\in Y
different from all the nodes

y_{j},j=1,\cdots,k
and sufficiently general.

For a vector bundle
E
on
Y
, let
r(E)
and
d(E)
denote the rank and degree of
E
. Let
h^{i}(E)=
dim
H^{i}(Y,E)
for all
i
.

2.2.
The Compactified Jacobian

Let
J(Y)
be the space of line bundles (locally free sheaves of rank
1
) of degree
0
on
Y
. If
Y
is nonsingular, it is called the Jacobian of
Y
, it is an Abelian variety. If
Y
has nodes,
J(Y)
is called the generalised Jacobian of
Y
, it is a non-singular quasi projective variety. Then the variety
J(Y)
has a natural compactification

\overline{J}(Y)
as the variety of torsion-free sheaves of rank
1
and
degree
0
on
Y
. The variety
\overline{J}(Y)
is not normal, it is a seminormal projective variety.

There is a canonically defined ample theta divisor
\theta
on
\overline{J}(Y)
(using
t
).

There is the Abel-Jacobi embedding

Y\ \hookrightarrow\ \overline{J}(Y)

given by
x\mapsto I^{*}_{x}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-t)
, where
I_{x}
denotes the ideal sheaf of
x\in Y
. We shall identify
Y
with its image in
\overline{J}(Y)
under this embedding.

2.3.
Picard sheaves and twisted Picard bundles on
\overline{J}(Y)

There is a Poincaré sheaf (universal sheaf)
{\mathcal{L}}
on
\overline{J}(Y)\times Y
such that for a point
L\in\overline{J}(Y)
, one has
{\mathcal{L}}|_{L\times Y}
is isomorphic to the torsionfree sheaf
L
of rank
1
corresponding to the point
L
. The sheaf

{\mathcal{L}}
is unique up to tensoring by a line bundle on
\overline{J}(Y)
. Hence we normalise
{\mathcal{L}}
by the condition that
{\mathcal{L}}|_{\overline{J}(Y)\times t}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{J}(Y)}
, the trivial line bundle on
\overline{J}(Y)
.
Let

p_{J}:\overline{J}(Y)\times Y\to\overline{J}(Y)\ {\rm and}\ {p_{Y}}:\overline{J}(Y)\times Y\to Y

denote the projections. Let

{\mathcal{L}}(r)=\mathcal{L}\otimes p_{Y}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(rt)\,.

Definition 2.1
.

We define the
Picard sheaves
on
\overline{J}(Y)
as the direct image sheaves

E_{r}:={p_{J}}_{*}({\mathcal{L}}(r)).

For
r\geq 2g-1
, the Picard sheaf is a vector bundle of rank
r+1-g
on
\overline{J}(Y)
called a
Picard bundle
.

Henceforth we assume that
r\geq 2g-1
.

The Poincaré sheaf
{\mathcal{L}}
on
\overline{J}(Y)\times Y
is the restriction of the Poincaré
sheaf
\mathcal{P}
on
\overline{J}(Y)\times\overline{J}(Y)
. This defines a Fourier-Mukai transform
\overline{J}(Y)\to\overline{J}(Y)
which is an isomorphism (
[
19
]
for
Y
smooth,
[
11
]
for
Y
nodal).

For
x\in\overline{J}(Y)
, let
x^{*}
denote the Fourier-Mukai transform of
x
. Let
i_{x}:\overline{J}(Y)\hookrightarrow\overline{J}(Y)\times\overline{J}(Y),i_{x}(L)=(L,x)
be the inclusion.
Define
the restriction of the Poincaré bundle

(2.1)

\mathcal{P}_{x}:=i_{x}^{*}\mathcal{P}\,.

The cohomology of
P_{x}
is known.

Theorem 2.2
.

(
[
1
, Theorem B]
for
Y
with planar singularities;
[
19
]
for
Y
smooth).

(1) If
x\in J(Y),x\neq\mathcal{O}_{Y}
,

H^{i}(\overline{J}(Y),P_{x})=0\ \forall\ i\,.

(2) For
x=\mathcal{O}_{Y}
, we have
P_{x}=\mathcal{O}_{\overline{J}_{Y}}
. Then
h^{0}(\overline{J}(Y),\mathcal{O}_{\overline{J}_{Y}})=1
and for
1\leq i\leq g
,

H^{i}(\overline{J}(Y),\mathcal{O}_{\overline{J}_{Y}})\ \cong\ \bigwedge^{i}H^{1}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})\,

so that

h^{i}(\overline{J}(Y),\mathcal{O}_{\overline{J}_{Y}})=\binom{g-1}{i-1}\,.

Definition 2.3
.

For
r\geq 2g-1
and
x\in J(Y)
, define the vector bundles

V_{r,x}:=E_{r}\otimes\mathcal{P}_{x}\,.

We call
V_{r,x}
a

.

Theorem 2.4
.

(
[
11
, Corollary 2.13]
;
[
19
]
for
Y

Let

.

h^{0}(\overline{J}(Y),V_{r,x})=0\ \forall\ x\in J(Y)\,.

For
1\leq i\leq g
and

, we have

h^{i}(\overline{J}(Y),V_{r,x})=\begin{cases}\binom{g-1}{i-1}&\mbox{if }x^{*}\in Y\\
0&\mbox{if }x^{*}\notin Y.\end{cases}

2.4.
Some general results

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
Z
be a variety of dimension
g
with the dualising sheaf the trivial line bundle and an ample line bundle
H
. Let
E
denote a reflexive torsionfree sheaf on
Z
. Let
i
be an integer,
0\leq i\leq g
. Then

H^{i}(Z,E)=0
if and only if
H^{g-i}(Z,E^{*})=0
.

Proof.

Suppose that
H^{i}(Z,E)=0
. By Serre duality,
h^{g-i}(Z,E^{*})=h^{i}(Z,\omega_{Z}\otimes(E^{*})^{*})
. Since
E^{**}=E
and
\omega_{Z}
is trivial, one has
h^{g-i}(Z,E^{*})=h^{i}(Z,E)=0
.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
r\geq 2g-1
. Then

h^{g}(\overline{J}(Y),V^{*}_{r,x})=0\ \forall\ x\in J(Y)\,.

For
0\leq i\leq g-1
and
x\in J(Y)
, we have

h^{i}(\overline{J}(Y),V^{*}_{r,x})=\begin{cases}\binom{g-1}{g-1-i}&\mbox{if }x^{*}\in Y\\
0&\mbox{if }x^{*}\notin Y.\end{cases}

Proof.

For
r\geq 2g-1
,
V_{r,x}
is a vector bundle. The corollary follows from Theorem
2.4
and Lemma
2.5
.
∎

Write
V_{r,x}(j\theta)):=V_{r,x}\otimes\theta^{\otimes j}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Assume that
x^{*}\notin Y
.

(1)

H^{0}(\overline{J}(Y),V_{r,x}(j\theta))=0\ \forall j\leq 0\ ;\ H^{g}(\overline{J}(Y),V^{*}_{r,x}(j\theta))=0\ \forall j\geq 0\
.

(2)

H^{g}(\overline{J}(Y),V_{r,x}(j\theta))=0\ \forall j\geq 0\ ;\ H^{0}(\overline{J}(Y),V^{*}_{r,x}(j\theta))=0\ \forall j\leq 0\ .

Proof.

(1) For
j\leq 0
, one has
H^{0}(\overline{J}(Y),V_{r,x}(j\theta))\subset H^{0}(\overline{J}(Y),V_{r,x})=0
by Theorem
2.4
. The second part of (1) follows from this by Lemma
2.5
.

(2) By Corollary
2.6
,
H^{0}(\overline{J}(Y),V^{*}_{r,x})=0
. Hence
H^{0}(\overline{J}(Y),V^{*}_{r,x}(j\theta))\subset H^{0}(\overline{J}(Y),V^{*}_{r,x})=0\ \forall j\leq 0\ .
Then
H^{g}(\overline{J}(Y),V_{r,x}(j\theta))=0\ \forall j\geq 0
by Lemma
2.5
.
∎

3.
Restriction of the twisted Picard bundle to
Y\subset\overline{J}(Y)

Let
r\geq 2g-1
, let
L
be a line bundle of degree
r-1
. Using
L
, one can give an embedding of
Y
in
\overline{J}^{r}(Y)
and hence in
\overline{J}(Y)
using

(see
[
14
]
in case

smooth,
[
10
]
in nodal case). For

, the embeddings given by

and

are translates of each other.
There exists an exact sequence (see
[

,
[

)

(3.1)

0\to H^{0}(Y,L)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}\to{E}_{r}|_{Y}(t)\to L^{\prime}\to 0,

where
L^{\prime}
is a line bundle of degree
r+1-2g,\ h^{0}(Y,L)=r-g,\ d({E}_{r}|_{Y})=-g
.

Define

\theta_{Y}:=\theta|_{Y}\,,

the restriction of the line bundle
\theta
to
Y
.

In the case
Y
is smooth, Ein and Lazarsfeld proved the stability of

(
[
14
, Proposition 1.5]
,
[
14
, Proposition 2.2]
). In case

has nodes, the stability of

was proved by myself and Parameswaran (see
[

for non-rational curve,
[
10

for a rational nodal curve with at least one node).

The exact sequence
3.1
gives

(3.2)

0\to H^{0}(Y,E_{r}|_{Y}(t))\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(-t)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{x}|_{Y}\longrightarrow V_{r,x}|_{Y}\longrightarrow L^{\prime}(-t)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{x}|_{Y}\to 0\,.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
r\geq 2g-1
.

h^{0}((V_{r,x}(j\theta))|_{Y})\ =\ 0\,,\ \forall j\leq 0\,,

h^{1}((V_{r,x}(j\theta))|_{Y})\ =(r+1-g)(g(1-j)-1)+g\ ,\ \forall j\leq 0\,.

Proof.

One has the intersection
\theta\cdot Y=g
. Hence
\theta_{Y}
is a line bundle of degree
g
on
Y
. We note that due to the normalisation of
\mathcal{P}
, the line bundle
\mathcal{P}_{x}
has first Chern class
0
and hence
\mathcal{P}_{x}|_{Y}
has degree
0
on
Y
.
Hence we have

d(V_{r,x}(j\theta)|_{Y})=d(V_{r,x}|_{Y})+jg\ r(V_{r}))=-g+jg(r+1-g)\leq-g<0

for
j\leq 0
. Since
V_{r,x}(j\theta)|_{Y}
is a stable vector bundle of negative degree (
[
10
, Sections 8 and 9]
), it has no non-zero sections on
Y
.

The nontrivial cohomology
H^{1}(V_{r,x}(j\theta)|_{Y})
can now be computed using Riemann-Roch theorem.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
r\geq 2g-1
.
Then

(1)
\forall j\geq 2\,,
one has

h^{1}((V_{r,x}(j\theta))|_{Y})\ =\ 0\,,

h^{0}((V_{r,x}(j\theta))|_{Y})\ =\ ((j-1)g+1)(r+1-g)-g\,,

(2)
{\rm for\ a\ general}\ x\in J(Y)\,,
we have

h^{1}((V_{r,x}(\theta))|_{Y})\ =\ 0\,,

h^{0}((V_{r,x}(\theta))|_{Y})=r+1-2g\,.

Proof.

Tensoring the exact sequence (
3.2
) with
(j\theta_{Y})=\theta_{Y}^{\otimes j}
and taking the cohomology exact sequence associated to it, we get the short exact sequence

(3.3)

H^{0}(Y,E_{r}|_{Y}(t))\otimes H^{1}(j{\theta_{Y}}(-t)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{x}|_{Y})\rightarrow H^{1}(V_{r,x}(j\theta)|_{Y})\rightarrow H^{1}(L^{\prime}(-t)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{x}|_{Y}\otimes j\theta_{Y})\,.

Note that
h^{0}(Y,E_{r}|_{Y}(t))=h^{0}(Y,L)=r-g
. One has

d(j{\theta_{Y}}(-t)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{x}|_{Y})=jg-1
. Since
jg-1\geq 2g-1
for
j\geq 2
, one has

H^{1}(j{\theta_{Y}}(-t)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{x}|_{Y})=0\ {\rm for}\ j\geq 2\,.

Take
j=1
. Then
d(\theta_{Y}(-t)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{x}|_{Y})=g-1
and
h^{1}(\theta_{Y}(-t)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{x}|_{Y})=0
for
x
general (i.e.,
x
in an (unspecified) open subset of

J(Y)
). Hence

H^{1}({\theta_{Y}}(-t)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{x}|_{Y})=0\,,\ {\rm for\ a\ general}\ x\in J(Y)\,.

Take
j=1,g=2
. Then
\omega_{Y}=\theta_{Y}

[
13
]
. By Serre duality, one has

h^{1}({\theta_{Y}}(-t)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{x}|_{Y})=h^{0}(\omega_{Y}\otimes\theta^{*}_{Y}(t)\otimes(\mathcal{P}_{x}|_{Y})^{*})=\mathcal{P}^{*}_{x}|_{Y}(t)=0
if

does not belong to
Y\subset J(Y)
, otherwise
h^{1}({\theta_{Y}}(-t)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{x}|_{Y})=1
.

We now compute
h^{1}(L^{\prime}(-t)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{x}|_{Y}\otimes j{\theta_{Y}})
for
j\geq 1
. One has

d(L^{\prime}(-t)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{x}|_{Y}\otimes j{\theta_{Y}})=r-2g+jg=r+(j-2)g
. If

, then

so that

h^{1}(L^{\prime}(-t)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{x}|_{Y}\otimes j{\theta_{Y}})=0\ \forall j\geq 2\,.

Let

. Then

0&\mbox{if }j=1,2g-1\leq r\leq 3g-2,\ x\ \mbox{general}.\end{cases}

The proposition now follows from the exact sequence (

).
Now the cohomology

can be computed using Riemann-Roch theorem.

∎

4.
ACM bundles on the Jacobian surface

In this section, we specialise to
g=2
i.e.
\overline{J}(Y)
is the compactified Jacobian of an irreducible projective curve
Y
of arithmetic genus
2
with at most nodes as singularities. We show that for each
r\geq 3
, there exits a two dimensional family of ACM bundles on the surface
\overline{J}(Y)
. In particular, this gives two dimensional families of ACM bundles on the Jacobian of a smooth curve of genus
2
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
Z
be an
n
-dimensional projective variety with an ample line bundle
A
. A vector bundle
F
on
Z
is called ACM if

H^{i}(Z,F\otimes A^{j})=0\ \forall j\in\mathbb{Z}\ {\rm and}\ 0<i<n\,.

An ACM bundle

H^{0}(Z,F\otimes A^{-1})=0\ {\rm and}\ H^{0}(Z,F)=r(F)\cdot deg(Z)\,.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that

and

h^{2}(\overline{J}(Y),\mathcal{P}_{x}(j\theta))=0,\ \forall j\geq 0\ ,

h^{2}(\overline{J}(Y),\mathcal{P}_{x}(j\theta))=j^{2},\ \forall j\leq 0\ .

Proof.

(1) By Theorem

Note that for

The associated cohomology exact sequence is

(4.1)

0\longrightarrow H^{0}(\overline{J}(Y),\mathcal{P}_{x}((j-1)\theta))\longrightarrow H^{0}(\overline{J}(Y),\mathcal{P}_{x}(j\theta))\longrightarrow H^{0}(\overline{J}(Y),\mathcal{P}_{x}(j\theta)|_{Y})

(4.2)

\longrightarrow H^{1}(\overline{J}(Y),\mathcal{P}_{x}((j-1)\theta))\longrightarrow H^{1}(\overline{J}(Y),\mathcal{P}_{x}(j\theta))\longrightarrow H^{1}(\overline{J}(Y),\mathcal{P}_{x}(j\theta)|_{Y})

(4.3)

Since

5.

Our aim in this section is to define an embedding of the (generalised) Jacobian
J(Y)
in the moduli space
U^{s}_{Y}(n,d)
of stable vector bundles on
Y
using the twisted restriction

of a Picard bundle to

. On smooth curves, Yingchen Li had defined an embedding

of

into

using a stable vector bundle

such that the bundle of endomorphism

has no line bundle direct summands
[
16
]
. The assumption on

is crucially used to prove the injectivity of
\alpha_{Y}
. The results of
[
16
]
were extended to a nodal curve by me
[
8
]
under the additional assumption that the pull back of

to the normalisation
X
is stable. We do not know if
E_{Y}
has any direct summand which is a line bundle. Moreover, the pull back of
E_{Y}
to the normalisation

is not semistable. Therefore, we need several changes in the proofs of
[
8
]
(or
[
16
]
in the smooth case).
Our proof is on similar lines as that in
[

]
,
[
16
]
.

5.1.
The Embedding
\alpha_{Y}

We assume that
r\geq 2g,g\geq 2
. In the notations of subsection
2.3
, we have a universal family
\mathcal{P}\to\overline{J}(Y)\times\overline{J}(Y)
with the restriction
\mathcal{L}\to\overline{J}(Y)\times Y
giving the Picard bundle
E_{r}
on the first factor
\overline{J}(Y)
. If
p_{1},p_{2}
are the projections from
\overline{J}(Y)\times\overline{J}(Y)\to\overline{J}(Y)
to the first and second factors, define

\mathcal{V}:=(p_{1}^{*}(E_{r})\otimes\mathcal{P})|_{Y\times\overline{J}(Y)}\,,

and
\mathcal{V}(t)=\mathcal{V}\otimes p_{1}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(t)
. Let
E_{Y}:=E_{r}|_{Y}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(t)
. Then for
x\in J(Y)
(second factor),
\mathcal{V}(t)_{Y\times x}=E_{Y}(t)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{x}|_{Y}
. Since
E_{Y}
is stable, so is
E_{Y}\otimes N
for any line bundle
N
on
Y
. Thus
\mathcal{V}(t)|_{Y\times J(Y)}
is a family of stable vector bundles on
Y
, of rank
r_{Y}=r+1-g
and degree
d_{Y}=r+1-2g
, parametrised by
J(Y)
. Hence it gives a morphism

\alpha_{Y}:J(Y)\longrightarrow U_{Y}^{s}(r_{Y},d_{Y})\,;\alpha_{Y}(N)=N\otimes E_{Y}\,.

Proposition 5.1
.

The morphism
\alpha_{Y}
is injective.

Proof.

Suppose that
N_{1},N_{2}\in J(Y),N_{1}\neq N_{2}
and
\alpha_{Y}(N_{1})=\alpha_{Y}(N_{2})
. Then
E_{Y}\otimes N_{1}=E_{Y}\otimes N_{2}
i.e.,
E_{Y}\otimes N=E_{Y},N=N_{1}\otimes N_{2}^{-1}
. To prove the injectivity of
\alpha_{Y}
, it suffices to prove that
E_{Y}\otimes N\neq E_{Y}
for a nontrivial line bundle
N
.
Recall the exact sequence (
3.1
)

0\to H^{0}(Y,L)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}\to{E}_{Y}\to L^{\prime}\to 0\,,

where
L^{\prime}
is a line bundle of degree
r+1-2g,h^{0}(Y,L)=r-g
.
This implies that
h^{0}(E_{Y})\geq r-g.

Tensoring the exact sequence (
3.1
) by
N
and taking the cohomology exact sequence, we get an exact sequence

0\to H^{0}(Y,L)\otimes H^{0}(Y,N)\to H^{0}(Y,E_{Y}\otimes N)\to H^{0}(Y,L^{\prime}\otimes N)\to

Since
d(N)=0,N\neq{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
, we have
h^{0}(Y,N)=0
. Then

h^{0}(Y,E_{Y}\otimes N)\leq h^{0}(Y,L^{\prime}\otimes N)
.

Case 1: Let
r\geq 4g-2
. Then
d(L^{\prime}\otimes N)\geq 2g-1
so that
h^{1}(Y,L^{\prime}\otimes N)=0
and by Riemann-Roch theorem,
h^{0}(Y,L^{\prime}\otimes N)=r+2-3g
. Hence
h^{0}(Y,E_{Y}\otimes N)\leq r+2-3g
. Since
r-g>r+2-3g
for
g\geq 2
, we get
h^{0}(Y,E_{Y})>h^{0}(Y,E_{Y}\otimes N)
. Therefore
E_{Y}\ncong E_{Y}\otimes N
.

Case 2: Let
2g\leq r\leq 4g-3
. Then
d(L^{\prime}\otimes N))\leq 2g-2
. By Clifford’s theorem,

h^{0}(Y,L^{\prime}\otimes N)\leq\frac{r+1-2g}{2}+1=\frac{r+3}{2}-g\,.

Hence
h^{0}(Y,E_{Y}\otimes N)\leq\frac{r+3}{2}-g<r-g=h^{0}(Y,E_{Y})
for
r>3
. It follows that
E_{Y}\ncong E_{Y}\otimes N
.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

The morphism
\alpha_{Y}
is an embedding.

Proof.

The Zariski tangent space to
J(Y)
at a point
N\in J(Y)
is Ext
{}^{1}(N,N)=H^{1}(Y,N^{*}\otimes N)=H^{1}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})
. The Zariski tangent space to

at a point

is Ext
{}^{1}(E,E)=H^{1}(Y,End(E))
.
The injectivity of

, the induced mapping of tangent spaces, can be proved using the determinant map

as in
[
16
, Proposition 1.6]
or using deformation theory as in
[
16
, Remark 1.7]
.
Since

, upto translation this composite map is the map
J(Y)\to J(Y)
defined by multiplication by
r_{Y}
. The latter being an étale map, the induced map on tangent spaces is injective and hence the tangent map

is injective.
∎

\tilde{\theta}
to
\tilde{J}(Y)
is ample. Suppose that its restriction to the open subset
J(Y)\subset\tilde{J}(Y)
is ample. The fibres of the morphism
J(Y)\to J(X)
are closed subvarieties of
J(Y)
, so
\tilde{\theta}|_{J(Y)}
must restrict to ample line bundles on the fibres. The variety
J(Y)
is a
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{k}
-bundle over
J(X)
. Hence the restriction of
\tilde{\theta}
to
J(Y)
is trivial on the fibres, giving a contradiction. It follows that
\theta
restricted to
J(Y)
is not ample.

Define the Picard bundle on
U^{s}_{Y}(r_{Y},d^{b}_{Y})
by

E_{r_{Y},d^{b}_{Y}}\ :=\ p_{U*}\mathcal{U}

where
p_{U}:Y\times U^{s}_{Y}(r_{Y},d^{b}_{Y})\to U^{s}_{Y}(r_{Y},d^{b}_{Y})
is the projection.

Theorem 5.6
.

Assume that
Y
is a smooth curve and
(r+1,g)=1
. Then
the restriction of the Picard bundle
E_{r_{Y},d^{b}_{Y}}
to
J(Y)
is
\theta
-semistable for
b\geq 2g-1
and
\theta
-stable for
b\geq 2g
.

Proof.

We have the commutative diagram

\begin{array}[]{ccc}Y\times J(Y)&\longrightarrow&Y\times U^{s}_{Y}(r_{Y},d^{b}_{Y})\\
&&\\
p_{2}\downarrow&&\downarrow p_{U}\\
&&\\
J(Y)&\longrightarrow&U^{s}_{Y}(r_{Y},d^{b}_{Y})\end{array}

Hence, by the projection formula, the restriction

E_{r_{Y},d^{b}_{Y}}|_{J(Y)}:=(\alpha^{B}_{Y})^{*}p_{U*}\mathcal{U}\cong p_{2*}(id_{Y}\times\alpha^{B}_{Y})^{*}\mathcal{U}\,.

Using equation (
5.3
),

p_{2*}(id_{Y}\times\alpha^{B}_{Y})^{*}\mathcal{U}\cong p_{2*}(\mathcal{N}\otimes p_{1}^{*}(E_{Y}\otimes B)\otimes p_{2}^{*}M)\cong p_{2*}(\mathcal{N}\otimes p_{1}^{*}(E_{Y}\otimes B))\otimes M\,.

By
[
12
, Proposition 4.1]
, the generalised Picard bundle
p_{2*}(\mathcal{N}\otimes p_{1}^{*}(E_{Y}\otimes B))
on
J(Y)
is
\theta
-semistable for
b\geq 2g-1
and
\theta
-stable for
b\geq 2g
, hence the result follows.
∎

Lemma 5.8
.

The morphism
\alpha_{Y}
does not extend to
\overline{J}(Y)
.

Proof.

If
N
is not locally free, then
N=p^{S}_{*}N^{\prime}
where
p^{S}
is the partial normalisation obtained by blowing the
s
number of nodes in
Y
where
N
is not locally free. One has

(p^{S})^{*}(E_{Y})=F^{S}_{Y}\oplus I^{s}
, where
F^{S}_{Y}
is a stable vector bundle contradicting the semistability of
(p^{S})^{*}(E_{Y})
and

is the trivial vector bundle of rank
s
on

. Then
(p^{S})^{*}(E_{Y})\otimes N^{\prime}=(F^{S}_{Y}\otimes N^{\prime})\oplus(\oplus_{s}N^{\prime})

∎

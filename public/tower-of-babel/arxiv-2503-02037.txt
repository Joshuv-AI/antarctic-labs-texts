Stabilization of three-body resonances to bound states in a continuum Preprint: APS/123-QED
Lucas Happ, Pascal Naidon

Abstract
Three-body resonances are ubiquitous in few-body physics and are characterized by a finite lifetime before decaying into continuum states of their composing subsystems. In this work we present a theoretical study on the possibility to stabilize three-body resonances to so-called bound states in a continuum: resonances with vanishing width that do not decay. Within a simple two-channel approach we unveil the underlying mechanism and suggest that the lifetime can be varied by a continuous tuning of system parameters. The validity of our theory is illustrated in two rather different examples, a mass-imbalanced system in one dimension and a system of three identical bosons in three dimensions, relevant to Efimov physics. Crucially, for the latter we find that one of the tunable parameters to achieve a three-body bound state in a continuum is an external magnetic field, a common tunable variable in cold-atom experiments. Due to the fundamental nature of our theory, it is expected to hold in other few-body systems, possibly opening new avenues for basic studies of otherwise unstable systems, and elevating few-body systems to interesting candidates for quantum technology.

;
Efimov 1973

;
D’Incao et al. 2004

;
Nielsen et al. 2002
;
Happ et al. 2024
. This is mostly due to experimental difficulties in determining the trimer lifetime, which is often below the available resolution. However, advancements in spectroscopy

and interferometric implementations

show great prospect for near-future measurements of trimer lifetimes. Besides the Efimov scenario, three-body resonances of cold atoms and their stability are also relevant for possible trimer condensates

;
Musolino et al. 2022

;

;

;

;

;

;

;

.

The central property that distinguishes resonances from bound states is their finite width and associated decay into one or possibly many continuum states. However, there can be special scenarios when a resonance defies decay and remains stable, despite being embedded in a continuum. Such states are called bound states in a continuum (BIC)
Hsu et al. 2016
and have been proposed to exist already about a century ago
von Neumann and Wigner 1993
;
Stillinger and Herrick 1975
;
Friedrich and Wintgen 1985
, and realized in several systems, most notably in photonics
Marinica et al. 2008
;
Hsu et al. 2016
;
Pankin et al. 2020
. There have also been proposals for cold-atom systems
Deb and Agarwal 2014
;
Chilcott et al. 2024
, however so far without experimental realization. These proposals are based on the interference of two or several resonances or decay pathways. In Ref.
Happ et al. 2024
we have found an indication for a three-body BIC in a heteronuclear system by considering various mass ratios. However, this study could neither reveal whether the state is actually stable, nor its stabilization mechanism.

In this letter we employ a two-channel Feshbach-like model
Feshbach 1958
;
Fano 1961
for three-body resonances and unveil the underlying physical mechanism that can stabilize them to BICs. We find that the effect can be traced back to a transition element which can be tuned to become zero. Critically, our two-channel model reveals that stabilization can be achieved for a single resonance in a single continuum. No interference of several resonances is required. The key tuning parameter turns out to be the relative momentum between outgoing subsystems which can indeed be tuned via the mass ratio, but also other parameters. We demonstrate three-body BICs in two example systems: (i) a mass-imbalanced system in one dimension, and (ii) three identical bosons in three dimensions, relevant for the Efimov scenario. Crucially, for the latter we find that stabilization of a three-body resonance to a BIC can occur via variation of an external magnetic field, a parameter commonly tuned in cold-atom experiments
Inouye et al. 1998
;
Chin et al. 2010
.

The ability to create three-body BICs might open the path towards realization of a trimer condensate
Braaten et al. 2003
;
Musolino et al. 2022
, and allow studying effective atom-dimer potential surfaces and three-body interactions in much greater detail
Yang et al. 2022
;
Endo et al. 2025
. If one can identify an experimentally tunable parameter also for other three-body systems, e.g. in nuclear or hadron physics, a tool to extend the lifetime of such systems would remove a major bottleneck and enable groundbreaking improvements in their studies. Apart from fundamental interest, non-decaying three-body systems might elevate their relevance for the implementation of quantum technology
MacFarlane et al. 2003
;
Bloch et al. 2012
. In addition, besides the point of stability itself, the region around it can be used to manipulate their lifetime at will over several orders of magnitude.

Two-channel framework

We start from a two-channel description

H=\begin{pmatrix}H_{o,o}&H_{o,c}\\
H_{c,o}&H_{c,c}\end{pmatrix}

(1)

of the total few-body Hamiltonian, where the subscripts indicate the
open
and
closed
channels, respectively. The bare states in each channel are defined from the uncoupled equations

\displaystyle(H_{o,o}-E_{o,m})|\Phi_{o,m}\rangle=

\displaystyle~0

\displaystyle(H_{c,c}-E_{c,n})|\Phi_{c,n}\rangle=

\displaystyle~0

(2)

whose Hamiltonians can be expressed as

H_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}}=T+U_{\mathcal{C}},\qquad\mathcal{C}\in\{o,c\}

(3)

with the kinetic energy operator
T
, and
U_{\mathcal{C}}
denotes the interaction potential in each channel. This effective potential can be obtained e.g. within a Born-Oppenheimer (BO)
Born and Oppenheimer 1927
or hyperspherical
Lin 1995
;
Nielsen et al. 2001
approach.

Then, after including the off-diagonal coupling terms
H_{o,c}
, the previously bare bound states in the closed channel (
E_{c,n}>U_{o}(r\to\infty)
) become resonances with a finite width
\Gamma_{n}
since they can decay into the continuum induced by the open channel (
U_{o}<U_{c}
). For an isolated resonance the width can be calculated via
Kukulin et al. 1989
;
Naidon 2025

\Gamma_{n}=2\pi\left|\langle\Phi_{o,k}|w\rangle\right|^{2},\qquad|w\rangle=H_{o,c}|\Phi_{c,n}\rangle.

(4)

We highlight here that only the open-channel continuum state
|\Phi_{o,k}\rangle
of energy
E_{c,n}\propto k^{2}
has to be considered for the overlap integral.

In this letter we focus on the qualitative nature of the stabilization effect. Hence, for illustrative purposes we employ the WKB approximation
Wentzel 1926
;
Kramers 1926
;
Brillouin 1926
;
L. D. Landau and E. M. Lifschitz 1958

\Phi_{o,k}(r)\propto\frac{1}{\sqrt{k(r)}}\cos\left(\alpha+\int_{\mathcal{D}}(r)\mathrm{d}^{d}r^{\prime}k(r^{\prime})\right)

(5)

which captures the main essence of the continuum state. Here, the domain
\mathcal{D}(r)
of integration is chosen according to the dimensionality
d
, e.g.
\mathcal{D}(r)=0\ldots r
for 3D. The WKB phase
\alpha
ensures the appropriate boundary condition, and the WKB wave number reads

k(r)=\sqrt{\frac{2\mu}{\hbar^{2}}\Big[E-U_{o}(r)\Big]}.

(6)

The reduced mass
\mu
depends on how the channels are defined and on the particular scaling in use
1
1

1

In a Born-Oppenheimer description
\mu
would indicate the heavy-particles reduced mass, whereas in a hyperspherical approach often the
N
-body reduced mass
\mu_{N}=(\mu_{12}\mu_{12,3}\ldots)^{1/(N-1)}
is employed, or even
\mu=1
when the mass is used to rescale the coordinates.
. The overlap integral in Eq. (
4
) can then be expressed as

\langle\Phi_{o,k}|w\rangle\propto\int_{\mathcal{D}}\mathrm{d}^{d}r\,\frac{\cos\left(\alpha+\int_{\mathcal{D}}\mathrm{d}^{d}r^{\prime}k(r^{\prime})\right)}{\sqrt{k(r)}}w(r).

(7)

This describes the integral of an oscillating function times
w(r)
. The frequency of the oscillations is mainly governed by
k(r)
. The overlap integral
can
become zero due to cancelations of subsequent positive and negative contributions of the integrand. In that case, the width of the resonance is zero, i.e. the state is a bound state in the continuum. It is however not guaranteed that total cancelation takes place. Indeed, in 3D a simple counterexample would be the integral of a pure sine function times a monotonically decaying exponential.

It is hard to formulate general and precise conditions for the occurrence of a BIC. Nevertheless, we observe that at least some non-monotonicity in the function
w(r)
is required. In few-body systems this requirement is usually fulfilled since e.g. within the BO or hyperspherical approach, the coupling terms
H_{o,c}
contain non-adiabatic derivative operators which ensure a non-monotonic behavior of
w(r)
, even if the bare bound state
|\Phi_{c,n}\rangle
is nodeless.

We have identified that the WKB wave number is the crucial ingredient that determines whether the overlap integral vanishes. For three-body resonances this is closely related to the relative atom-molecule momentum

p_{\mathrm{rel}}=\sqrt{2\mu\left[E^{(3)}-E^{(2)}\right]},\ \ E^{(3)}=E^{(2)}+\frac{p_{\mathrm{rel}}^{2}}{2\mu}.

(8)

This momentum is affected by the reduced mass
\mu
and the energy difference
\Delta E=E^{(3)}-E^{(2)}
. Whence, variation of this momentum enables one to tune the value of the overlap integral and therefore allows the width
\Gamma
of the three-body resonance to vanish. We highlight that this effect originates from a
single
resonance in a
single
continuum. Stabilization stems from tuning of the system parameters. No interference of two or more resonances or decay pathways is required.

Needless to say, vanishing transition elements are not unprecedented in physics. A standard example are the selection rules of (dipole-) transition elements in atomic hydrogen, or spin- and angular momentum algebra in nuclear systems. Indeed, in these systems many transition elements vanish due to symmetry arguments or because of addition rules of a discrete parameter. In this sense, one can understand our phenomenon as an analog effect, however based on a continuously tunable parameter.

In the following we demonstrate the generality of our theory in two different scenarios.

Illustration in 1D

For the first example, we reconsider the system from Refs.
Happ et al. 2022
;
Happ et al. 2024
, consisting of two identical bosons of mass
M=\beta m
and a third particle of mass
m
, all confined to one dimension. The mass ratio is given by
\beta
. The identical bosons do not interact directly, while the interspecies interaction is modeled by

V_{BX}(z)=-\left|v_{0}\right|\exp(-z^{2}/z_{0}^{2}).

(9)

We define
z
as the relative distance between the bosons and
Z
as the distance of the third particle from their center of mass. In the three-body center-of-mass frame, the system dynamics are fully described by these two coordinates, with reduced masses
\mu_{bb}
and
\mu_{x,bb}
for the boson pair and the third particle relative to the pair, respectively.

To define the two channels in our theory, we apply the Born-Oppenheimer approximation, particularly suited for
\beta\gg 1

(10)

with the Hamiltonian

H_{0}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu_{bb,x}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial Z^{2}}+V(Z_{+})+V(Z_{-}),

(11)

where
Z_{\pm}=Z\pm z/2
. The semicolon in
\phi(Z;z)
indicates that in the adiabatic BO approximation,
z
enters only as a parameter. The eigenvalues
U_{\mathcal{C}}(z)
then define the effective potential curves for the heavy-particle dynamics. We retain only the two lowest-energy states and assign them to the open and closed channel. The heavy-particle Hamiltonian then reads

\displaystyle H_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C^{\prime}}}=

\displaystyle~\delta_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C^{\prime}}}\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu_{bb}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}+U_{\mathcal{C}}(z)\right]

\displaystyle~-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu_{bb}}\left[2\langle\phi_{\mathcal{C}}|\frac{\partial}{\partial z}|\phi_{\mathcal{C^{\prime}}}\rangle\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\langle\phi_{\mathcal{C}}|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}|\phi_{\mathcal{C^{\prime}}}\rangle\right].

(12)

The closed-channel bound states
|\Phi_{c,n}\rangle
and open-channel states

follow from solving Eq. (

Efimov scenario

As a second example we consider a different regime where the three-body resonance lies close to the three-body threshold of binding. The system consists of three identical bosons of mass
m
in 3D, making the results relevant to Efimov physics.

Naidon 2025

a(k)=a_{\mathrm{bg}}-\frac{1}{R^{\star}(e_{b}-k^{2})}.

Conclusion

To summarize, we have presented a general theory for the stabilization of few-body resonances to bound states in a continuum. Based on a two-channel model, we have traced back its origin to a transition element of a
single
isolated resonance in a
single
continuum. This transition element can be made to vanish by variation of several system parameters. For now, a rigorous condition for the existence of a zero remains elusive and most likely depends on the details of the particular system. Moreover, we have showcased the existence of three-body BICs in two rather different scenarios, demonstrating the generality of our results. Most notably, we have shown that stabilization occurs in a system of three identical bosons in 3D, a system highly relevant to Efimov physics. Crucially, the relevant tuning parameter to achieve stabilization and existence of a three-body BIC is directly related to an external magnetic field, a variable commonly tuned in cold-atom experiments.

Finally, let us mention that the width of a three-body resonance, especially in the Efimov scenario, can be interpreted as the imaginary part of the three-body parameter. Its real part has attracted many studies, e.g. on the van-der-Waals universality
Wang et al. 2012
;
Endo et al. 2014
. It would be interesting to investigate whether the imaginary part and hence the stabilization of three-body resonances can show a similar universal behavior.

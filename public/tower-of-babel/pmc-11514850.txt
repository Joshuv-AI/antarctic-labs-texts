Effect of diabetes mellitus type 2 and sulfonylurea on colorectal cancer development: a case-control study
Authors: Mohammad Rezazadeh, Shahram Agah, Amirreza Kamyabi, Abolfazl Akbari, Ramtin Ghamkhari Pisheh, Amirhossein Eshraghi, Alireza Babakhani, Alireza Ahmadi, Melika Paseban, Parnian Heidari, Ilia Shirinkam, Amirabbas Mehrdad
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and aimsColorectal cancer (CRC) is a significant global health concern, with studies estimating a rise in new cases to 2.5 million by 2035. Type 2 diabetes (T2D) is also a growing issue, with an estimated 642 million adults affected by 2040. However, the relationship between T2D, its medications, and CRC remains unclear.Materials and methodsThis case-control study includes 810 controls without CRC and 684 cases with CRC admitted to Rasoul-Akram and Firouzgar Hospitals from September 2019 to 2023. Adjusted and unadjusted odds ratios (OR) were calculated to investigate the effect of T2D and sulfonylurea consumption on the chance of CRC development, using univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses. The relationship between T2D and the clinicopathological features of the tumor was investigated.ResultsThe results show that the effect of T2D on CRC is significant based on unadjusted OR (OR = 1.39, CI = 1.07, 1.81) and insignificant in adjusted OR (OR = 0.67, CI = 0.37, 1.20). The effect of sulfonylurea consumption on CRC was significant in both unadjusted (OR = 2.39, CI = 1.40, 4.09) and adjusted ORs (OR = 2.35, CI = 1.12, 4.91). All analyses related to the relationship between T2D and tumor clinicopathological characteristics were insignificant.ConclusionThis study found an insignificant association between type 2 diabetes and the chance of CRC development in an adjusted state. Sulfonylurea consumption was also associated with a higher chance of CRC development among patients with T2D. These findings have implications for clinical practice and public health strategies in CRC prevention for patients with T2D.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12876-024-03477-4.Keywords: Colorectal neoplasms, Type 2 diabetes, Sulfonylurea

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is the third most common form of cancer globally. It holds the position of the second-highest contributor to cancer-related deaths [1]. Studies suggest that the number of new cases of CRC will escalate to 2.5 million worldwide by the year 2035 [1, 2]. Known risk factors for this neoplasm include excessive red and processed meat consumption, high alcohol intake, being overweight, and having a family history of CRC [3]. On the other hand, physical activity, regular consumption of aspirin, use of statins, and having a diet rich in fiber, especially vegetables and fruits, can play a protective role against this disease [4–7].

Type 2 diabetes (T2D) is characterized by an insufficient production of insulin. It happens due to dysfunction in pancreatic beta cells and resistance to insulin in target tissues [8]. By 2040, it is estimated that approximately 642 million adults worldwide will be affected by diabetes mellitus [9]. Effective management of this condition involves a combination of lifestyle adjustments and pharmacological interventions [10]. Many patients with T2D prefer to take oral drugs rather than have regular insulin injections [11]. Notable studies have highlighted the effectiveness of oral options such as metformin, thiazolidinediones, and sulfonylureas [10]. In this article, the focus will specifically be on sulfonylureas. Sulfonylureas are currently used in daily practice due to their safety and positive effects in cardiovascular and renal diseases. They reduce HbA1c by approximately 1.5–1.6%, which is even more effective than new-generation drugs. Sulfonylurea compounds act through several key mechanisms: 1) increasing or accelerating insulin secretion; 2) increasing tissue sensitivity to insulin; and 3) increasing the availability of insulin by reducing the hepatic extraction of secreted insulin [12].

There are inconsistencies regarding the relationship between T2D and CRC. Some studies have shown that T2D is associated with an increased risk of CRC development [13–16]. Others have concluded that the two have no significant relationship [17, 18]. This discrepancy in the results indicates the need for further studies. Also, diabetes medications are implicated in the chance of developing CRC [19]. Previous studies indicate that insulin use may increase this chance [20]. Meanwhile, metformin and thiazolidinediones may have a protective effect [21, 22]. There is a lack of research on the potential link between sulfonylureas and CRC. As a result, there is still uncertainty surrounding the impact of diabetes medications on CRC. On the other hand, these associations have never been considered in the Iranian population.

According to the contradictory results mentioned and the importance of knowing the risk factors of CRC as one of the deadliest cancers, it is necessary to conduct this study. Understanding CRC risk factors aids in prevention, early detection, and tailored screening strategies, ultimately improving outcomes, guiding lifestyle choices, and increasing awareness for at-risk populations. The present study aimed to determine the exact association between T2D and sulfonylureas consumption with the chance of CRC development.

Materials and methods

This case-control study was conducted in Rasoul-Akram and Firouzgar Hospitals, Tehran, Iran. It was approved by the Iran University of Medical Sciences research committee with the ethics code: IR.IUMS.REC.1402.671. The study followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) reporting guideline for case-control studies. To avoid selection bias, the required information was extracted from two independent hospitals and through the medical records registered over a period of four years.

Study participants

Case and control participants were respectively patients with CRC and hospitalized patients without CRC from September 2019 to 2023 and over 45 years old. The exclusion criteria for cases and controls were as follows:

The absence of CRC in the controls was confirmed according to the history of the patients and if they had negative colonoscopy results.

Sampling and data collection procedure

By using simple random selection, 854 patients who met the specified criteria for potentially desirable cases were chosen. Following the application of exclusion criteria, 409 male and 275 female patients were among the 684 eligible cases.

Using the specified criteria, 841 patients were selected by simple random selection as potentially desirable controls. After applying exclusion criteria, 810 people (631 male and 179 female) were qualified as the control group. Sulfonylurea drugs that were used included Glibenclamide and Gliclazide. CRC stage (according to PTNM AJCC 8th edition), tumor location (proximal, distal, rectosigmoid, and total; which means being in more than 2 of the 3 locations mentioned), grade of the tumor (well differentiated, moderately differentiated, and poorly differentiated), and distant metastasis were selected as clinicopathological features of the tumor.

Data were collected from medical records, and for additional investigations over missing data of T2D duration, sulfonylurea consumption, and other drug history, telephone contact was conducted, of which 127 contacts were missed.

Sample size calculation

The sample size in this study was computed using Power and Sample software version 3.1.2 by William D. Dupont and Walton D. Plummer for independent cases and controls with 1 control(s) per case. The study from Azizi H et al. was adopted as it has the most similar criteria and location to this study [23]. With 80% study power, a two-sided α of 0.05, and expected proportions (prevalence of diabetes) in case and control of 0.23 and 0.10, at least 254 cases and controls were required for the investigation. Based on the anticipated amount of missing and excluded data, the number of 854 cases and 841 controls was determined as the selected sample size.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed with SPSS software version 27.0.1. Descriptive statistics were used to analyze the continuous variables using mean and standard deviation. Qualitative variables were expressed as numbers and percentages. The statistical significance was considered as a p-value < 0.05 and 95% confidence intervals (CI). The Shapiro-Wilk test was used to check the normality of quantitative variables. Due to the non-normal distribution of the continuous variables of the study, the Mann-Whitney U test was used. The chi-square test was used for categorical variables. To identify factors affecting CRC, first, univariate logistic regression analysis was performed, and then, to control confounders, variables with a p-value less than 0.5 in univariate regression were entered into multivariate regression analysis. Data were adjusted for age, aspirin, metformin, insulin, smoking, sex, T2D, and sulfonylurea. In this study, the main outcome is the chance of CRC development.

Comparison of case and control group characteristics

The results of the chi-square test showed that the case and the control populations were similar in terms of T2D duration, use of aspirin, and insulin. In comparison to the control group, cases were older, were more composed of males, had more smokers, had more patients with diabetes, and had more metformin and sulfonylurea consumers.

The adjusted odds ratio has an overall rising trend with age (highest OR (70–75 yrs.) = 4.89, 95% CI = 2.77–8.62), meaning there is an increased chance of developing CRC as age increases. The female population has a lower chance of developing CRC (OR = 0.41, 95% CI = 0.32–0.52). Smokers have a higher chance of developing CRC (OR = 1.51, 95% CI = 1.10–2.06). Sulfonylurea consumers have a higher chance of developing CRC (OR = 2.35, 95% CI = 1.12–4.91). However, according to the adjusted odds ratios and their confidence intervals, aspirin consumption, metformin consumption, insulin usage, and T2D have no significant effect on the chance of developing CRC (respectfully, OR = 0.97, 95% CI = 0.68–1.40, OR = 1.33, 95% CI = 0.71–2.49, OR = 1.44, 95% CI = 0.66–3.10, and OR = 0.67, 95% CI = 0.31–1.20). Effect of T2D duration on the chance of CRC development

For analyzing the effect of T2D duration on the chance of developing CRC, the Mann-Whitney U test was used. The mean of T2D duration for 101 cases was 9.25 (SD = 7.52) years, and the mean for 57 controls was 10.72 (SD = 7.86) years. No significant difference was observed between the case and control groups (p-value = 0.158), indicating that the duration of T2D did not affect the chance of CRC occurrence.

Discussion

In this case-control study, an insignificant association between T2D and the chance of CRC development based on adjusted OR was found. It could be explained by study limitations, including a small sample size and a control group consisting of hospitalized individuals. Results suggest that sulfonylurea consumption may be associated with a higher chance of developing CRC among patients with T2D. In spite of the fact that there was a higher percentage of poor differentiation and invasion of tumors in the diabetic group than in the non-diabetic group, there was no statistically significant difference between the two groups. The observed positive association between T2D and the chance of developing CRC is consistent with findings from several previous studies [24–26]. Several key pathophysiological explanations have been proposed regarding the relationship between these two diseases. These include hyperglycemia leading to increased oxidative stress, chronic hyperinsulinemia, adipose tissue disorders, and changes in the intestinal microbiome [27]. New studies suggest genetic associations, such as higher expression of KRAS mRNA in T2D patients [28], mitochondrial protein dysregulation [29], and inflammation-related genes [30]. Also, some other studies have reported different findings. In a retrospective cohort study (n = 1800), there was no significant association between diabetes mellitus and the chance of developing colorectal adenomatous polyps (OR = 1.25, p = 0.09) after adjusting for confounders such as gender, age, race/ethnicity, and body mass index (BMI) [31]. Also, no significant association was found between the type or duration of T2D treatment, HbA1c levels, and adenomatous polyps. Additionally, Pan et al. [18] conducted a prospective investigation published in 2018, analyzing data from the China Kadoorie Biobank (CKB) to explore the association between T2D and cancer chance in Chinese adults (n = 508,892). Although participants with T2D faced an increased chance of total cancer (HR = 1.13, 95% CI: 1.07, 1.19), there was no significant association between CRC and T2D (HR = 0.91, 95% CI: 0.73, 1.13). Our finding of a potential positive effect of sulfonylurea on the chance of developing CRC among patients with T2D aligns with previous findings. These findings suggest that sulfonylureas, which are secretagogues that lower blood glucose levels by stimulating insulin release from the pancreas, could potentially contribute to carcinogenesis, possibly due to an increase in circulating insulin levels [32]. In a prospective population-based cohort study conducted in 2019 by Shin and colleagues, they examined 4,228 cases and an equal number of controls matched to the cases. Their findings suggest a significant positive association between the use of sulfonylurea consumption and an increased chance of developing CRC (OR = 1.14, 95% CI: 1.05, 1.25) [33]. Some sulfonylurea compounds, such as gliclazide, are reported to have a protective role against CRC [33, 34]. This study has several limitations that should be considered. First, the case-control design precludes the establishment of temporal relationships and causal inferences. Additionally, the use of self-reported data for assessing T2D status, duration, and medication use may be subject to recall bias. Furthermore, information was not available on other potential confounding factors, such as physical activity level, BMI, and dietary habits, that could influence the observed association. While this study focused on the Iranian population, the findings may be generalizable to other populations with similar demographic and lifestyle characteristics. However, caution should be exercised when extrapolating the results to populations with substantially different genetic backgrounds, environmental exposures, or healthcare systems.

The control group of this study was selected from only one of the studied hospitals, although efforts have been made to mitigate the bias caused by this limitation by random selection and a large sample frame.

The high number of missing data during phone calls to collect the duration of diabetes is another limitation of this study.

The findings of this study have implications for clinical practice and public health strategies related to CRC prevention in patients with T2D or taking sulfonylureas. These results may guide future research into the mechanisms underlying the observed associations and the development of tailored screening and prevention strategies for individuals with these risk factors. Enhanced screening for CRC may be advisable, especially in sulfonylurea-treated patients with T2D, given the potential risks associated with these drugs.

Conclusion

This study confirmed a positive association between sulfonylurea consumption and an increased chance of developing CRC. No significant relationship between T2D and CRC was observed in the adjusted state. While the diabetic group showed a higher percentage of poor differentiation and invasion of tumor compared to the non-diabetic group, there was no statistically significant difference between the two groups. Disentangling the effects of individual medications remains challenging, as patients with T2D often use combinations, and disease severity itself may act as a confounder. While contributing evidence to the T2D-CRC link, establishing causality requires further prospective studies with comprehensive data on medication use, dosages, and disease severity.

Variational method for ℤ K \mathbb{Z}_{K} wavefunctions in spin- J J PXP model
Zhigang Hu(胡志刚), Biao Wu(吴飙)

Abstract
We investigate the approach of time-dependent variational principle (TDVP) for the one-dimensional spin- J J PXP model with detuning, which is relevant for programmable Rydberg atom arrays. The variational manifold is chosen as the minimally entangled ℤ K \mathbb{Z}_{K} matrix-product-states (MPS). We demonstrate that variational dynamics and variational error can be expressed as rapidly convergent series in the thermodynamic limit. In particular, for J = 1 / 2 J=1/2 and the limiting case J → ∞ J\rightarrow\infty , the TDVP results become exact and significantly simplified.



I
Introduction

Recently, advancements in controllable synthetic quantum simulator have opened new opportunities for experimental investigation of non-equilibrium quantum dynamics. Rydberg-atom array trapped in optical tweezers, with their flexible spatial arrangement, presents a promising platform to simulate quantum many-body system in a programmable way. These Rydberg-atom array systems have already demonstrated potentials in the exploration of exotic phase of matter
[
1
]
, quantum optimization algorithm
[
2
]
and quantum optimal control
[
3
]
. Additionally, they have been used for the study of quantum phase transition with competing crystalline orders
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
and investigate the quantum non-equilibrium phenomena such as intriguing quantum many-body scarring
[
5
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
. A key feature of these experiments is the Rydberg blockade, which prevents simultaneous excitations of neighboring atoms. The effective PXP Hamiltonian, which captures the blockade mechanism, is non-integrable
[
4
,
7
,
10
]
, making it challenging to obtain an analytical understanding of the experimental results.

The variational method offers a feasible route to describe the Rydberg physics. The idea of using variational approach to quantum many body dynamics dates back to Dirac
[
11
]
. In this method, the exact evolution of the variational state is projected onto the tangent plane of the variational manifold. The variational parameters are then updated along this projection direction.
The procedure is now known as time-dependent variational principle (TDVP)
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
. The equations of motion obtained can be regarded as an effective semiclassical description of quantum dynamics, as long as the variational error is not significant.

The TDVP method has successfully explained long-time many-body oscillations in 1D Rydberg atom array.
The variational ansatz used is the matrix product state (MPS) with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
discrete translational symmetry, subject to additional constraint that avoids simultaneous neighbour excitation—akin to the Gutzwiller projection. The resulting analytical tractable equations of motion attribute the revival to unstable, short-period periodic orbits in phase space
[
7
,
15
]
, reminiscent of single-particle quantum scars in stadium billiards
[
16
]
. However, the phase transition and non-equilibrium dynamics observed in Rydberg atom array experiments present rich sublattice symmetries beyond simple
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
ansatz
[
5
]
. To extend the approach to system with more general
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
symmetry,
a generalized MPS ansatz with
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
periodicity is required
for TDVP method. This generalization is nontrivial, as the standard transfer matrix technique becomes much more complex for large periodicities.

In this article, we study the spin-
J
PXP model, which generalizes the spin
1/2
PXP model and includes it as a special case. Higher-spin models have long been fertile ground for exploring exotic physics
[
17
]
and their realization has attracted significant experimental attention recently
[
18
,
19
]
.
Employing the TDVP method, we investigate the model by adopting the minimally-entangled MPS ansatz (with nontrivial bond dimension
D=2
), which allows for tensor contractions to be performed even by hand. The variational MPS with
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
discrete translational symmetry inherently encodes the Rydberg blockade constraint.
With mathematical induction, we explicitly solve the transfer matrix for a
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
unit cell. The dominant eigenvectors of the transfer matrix satisfy reduction formulae when the eigenvectors are contracted with the transfer matrix over arbitrary interval of lattice sites. These observations facilitate analytical calculations of variational dynamics, and combined with the energy variance, allow for the estimation of variational error rate, although more complex calculations involved than those of variational dynamics.
Notably, both the variational dynamics and error rate are expressed as rapidly convergent series, enabling efficient truncation.

The article is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we introduce the TDVP method for general variational ansatz, and in Sec.
III
, we describe the spin-
J
PXP model. In Sec.
IV
, we specify the
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
variational MPS ansatz and derive the transfer matrix over subsequent lattice sites. The reduction formulae are also proposed such that the contraction between the dominant eigenvectors and the transfer matrix becomes straightforward. In Sec.
V
, we present an analytical expression for the variational energy. In Sec.
VI
, we work out the variational dynamics and variational error rate in the form of rapidly convergent series. We show that TDVP results are exact and compact for spin
\frac{1}{2}
and large spin limit. In Sec.
VII

II
Time-dependent variational principle

The TDVP equations of motion are determined by taking the extreme of the action
S=\int\mathcal{L}dt=\int\mathrm{i}(\langle\psi|\dot{\psi}\rangle-\langle\dot{\psi}|\psi\rangle)/2-\langle\psi|H|\psi\rangle dt
. The action is invariant under the gauge transformation
\lvert\psi(t)\rangle\rightarrow e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha(t)}\lvert\psi(t)\rangle
with a phase factor
e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha(t)}
. We fix the redundant degree of freedom such that the “mean” Schrödinger equation
\langle\psi\rvert(\frac{d}{dt}+\mathrm{i}H)\lvert\psi\rangle
holds. It has been already noticed that this gauge choice removes all disconnected correlations in TDVP equations and quantum leakage
[
15
]
.

Let the variational parameters be
z_{i}
, the equations of motion of
z_{i}
read

\sum_{j}\dot{\mu}_{j}\text{Im}G_{ij}^{c}=-\text{Re}\langle\partial_{\mu_{i}}\psi\rvert H\lvert\psi\rangle_{c}\,,

(1)

where
G_{ij}^{c}\equiv\langle\partial_{\mu_{i}}\psi|\partial_{\mu_{j}}\psi\rangle-\langle\partial_{\mu_{i}}\psi|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\partial_{\mu_{j}}\psi\rangle
is the connected part of the Gram matrix
G_{ij}=\langle\partial_{\mu_{i}}\psi|\partial_{\mu_{j}}\psi\rangle
. Similarly,
\langle\partial_{\mu_{i}}\psi\rvert H\lvert\psi\rangle_{c}\equiv\langle\partial_{\mu_{i}}\psi\rvert H\lvert\psi\rangle-\langle\partial_{\mu_{i}}\psi|\psi\rangle\langle\psi\rvert H\lvert\psi\rangle
has its disconnected part removed. The semiclassical trajectory can be obtained by integrating the TDVP equations Eq. (
1
). Along the trajectory, the instantaneous variational error rate, also dubbed as quantum leakage in literature, becomes

\displaystyle\Lambda^{2}=\frac{1}{L}\lVert\lvert\dot{\psi}\rangle+\mathrm{i}H\lvert\psi\rangle\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L}\Big\{\langle\psi\rvert H^{2}\lvert\psi\rangle_{c}-2\sum_{i}\dot{\mu}_{i}\text{Im}\langle\partial_{\mu_{i}}\psi\rvert H\lvert\psi\rangle_{c}+\sum_{ij}\dot{\mu}_{i}\dot{\mu}_{j}\text{Re}G_{ij}^{c}\Big\}\,,

(2)

where
\langle\psi|H^{2}|\psi\rangle_{c}\equiv\langle\psi|H^{2}|\psi\rangle-\langle\psi|H|\psi\rangle^{2}
is the connected part of the energy variance. It is noted that we normalize
\Lambda^{2}
with the system size
L
such that
\Lambda^{2}
is well-defined in thermodynamic limit. Quantum leakage is found to be very useful diagnosis for quantum-classical correspondence. When
\int_{0}^{\tau}\Lambda dt\lesssim 1
, it is reasonable to infer the quantum dynamic can be well described by the semiclassical trajectory within the timescale
\tau

[
20
]
.

III
Spin-
J
PXP model

Consider a 1D Rydberg atom array. Each Rydberg atom has internal degree of freedom which we may call it spin. Let the unexcited state be “spin-down”
\lvert 0\rangle\equiv\lvert J,-J\rangle
, which is the lowest-lying eigenstate of the spin operator
S^{z}
. Under the experimental setting, the van der Waals interaction of neighboring atoms is tuned to dominate over the Rabi frequency
\Omega
and the detuning
\Delta
. In that case, simultaneous excitation of adjacent atoms is energetically unfavorable. Inspired by the experiments, we thus focus on a model Hamiltonian that demonstrate such constrained spin dynamics

H=\sum_{i}\Omega_{i}\mathcal{P}s_{i}^{x}\mathcal{P}+\Delta_{i}s_{i}^{z}\,,

(3)

where
s_{i}^{x}=J^{-1}S_{i}^{x}
and
s_{i}^{z}=J^{-1}S_{i}^{z}
are normalized spin-
J
operators at site
i
. The normalization of spin operator is used such that local energy density is always finite whatever value
J
takes. The projector
\mathcal{P}=\prod_{i}P_{i,i+1}=\prod_{i}I_{i}\otimes I_{i+1}-(I_{i}-P_{i})\otimes(I_{i+1}-P_{i+1})
projects out states with nearest neighboring excitation, where
P_{i}=|0\rangle_{i}\langle 0|_{i}
is projector of unexcited state at site
i
.
We will restrict our discussion in a constrained Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}

[
21
]
, spanned by basis states that avoid simultaneous neighbor excitation.

When
J=1/2
, the Hamiltonian (
3
) reduces to the detuned PXP model in
[
22
]
. The spin-
J
model has a particle-hole symmetry
U=e^{\mathrm{i}\pi J\sum_{i}s_{i}^{z}}
when there is no detuning, shown by
U^{-1}s_{i}^{x}U=-s_{i}^{x}
. For general parameter setting there is no obvious symmetry. It is known that the spin-
J
PXP model can exhibit non-ergodic quantum dynamics when
J
is small
[
7
]
.

IV
transfer matrix formalism

We study the quantum dynamics of the spin-
J
PXP model through TDVP method.
Following
[
7
]
, our trial wave function is a MPS

|\Psi\rangle=\text{Tr}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{L}A_{i}\bigg)\,,

(4)

with onsite matrix

\quad A_{i}(\theta_{i},\phi_{i})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}P_{i}|\theta_{i},\phi_{i}\rangle&(I_{i}-P_{i})|\theta_{i},\phi_{i}\rangle\\
|0\rangle_{i}&0\end{array}\right)\,,

(5)

where
|\theta_{i},\phi_{i}\rangle=e^{\xi_{i}S_{i}^{+}-\xi_{i}^{*}S_{i}^{-}}|0\rangle_{i}
is the spin coherent state with
\xi_{i}=\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}e^{-\mathrm{i}\phi_{i}}
and
S^{\pm}_{i}=S^{x}_{i}\pm\mathrm{i}S^{y}_{i}
. We summarize some important properties of the spin coherent state in Appendix
A
. For
J=\frac{1}{2}
, the onsite matrix is
A_{i}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}|0\rangle_{i}&e^{-\mathrm{i}\phi_{i}}\sin\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}|1\rangle_{i}\\
|0\rangle_{i}&0\end{array}\right)
. Here,
L
is the system size and periodic boundary condition is used.
The variational wavefunction automatically excludes nearest neighbour excitation as
\mathcal{P}\lvert\Psi\rangle=\lvert\Psi\rangle
, which is revealed by
P_{i,i+1}A_{i}A_{i+1}=A_{i}A_{i+1}
, since there is no component
(I_{i}-P_{i})\lvert\theta_{i},\phi_{i}\rangle\otimes(I_{i+1}-P_{i+1})\lvert\theta_{i+1},\phi_{i+1}\rangle
in matrix elements of
A_{i}A_{i+1}
.

We will use transfer matrix method to solve the correlation function in equations of motion and quantum leakage. The transfer matrix of a block of adjacent sites from
i
to
j
is
T_{[i,j]}\equiv T_{i}\cdot T_{i+1}\cdots T_{j}
, where
T_{i}
is the transfer matrix at site
i
, in terms of
x_{i}\equiv\cos^{2J}\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}

T_{i}\equiv\sum_{\sigma_{i}}(A^{\sigma_{i}}_{i})^{*}\otimes A^{\sigma_{i}}_{i}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}x_{i}^{2}&0&0&1-x_{i}^{2}\\
x_{i}&0&0&0\\
x_{i}&0&0&0\\
1&0&0&0\end{array}\right).

(6)

where
\sigma_{i}
sums from
-J
to
J
,
A^{\sigma_{i}}_{i}
is the
\lvert J,\sigma_{i}\rangle_{i}
component of the on-site matrix
A_{i}
and
(A^{\sigma_{i}}_{i})^{*}
is the complex conjugate of
A^{\sigma_{i}}_{i}
. Importantly,
T_{i}
is quite sparse. After a recursion procedure in Appendix
B
, we find that
T_{[i,j]}
has following compact form

\displaystyle T_{[i,j]}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1+\alpha_{[i,j]}+\beta_{[i,j]}&0&0&-\alpha_{[i,j]}-\beta_{[i,j]}\\
x_{i}(1+\alpha_{[i,j]})&0&0&-x_{i}\alpha_{[i,j]}\\
x_{i}(1+\alpha_{[i,j]})&0&0&-x_{i}\alpha_{[i,j]}\\
1+\alpha_{[i,j]}&0&0&-\alpha_{[i,j]}\end{array}\right)

with
\alpha_{[i,j]}=\sum_{m=i}^{j}\prod_{k=m}^{j}(-1+x_{k}^{2})-\prod_{k=i}^{j}(-1+x_{k}^{2})
and
\beta_{[i,j]}=\prod_{k=i}^{j}(-1+x_{k}^{2})
. The eigenvalues of
T_{[i,j]}
are
\lambda_{1}=1
,
\lambda_{2}=\beta_{[i,j]}
and
\lambda_{3}=\lambda_{4}=0
. The dominating eigenvalue is
\lambda_{1}=1
, since
\lvert\lambda_{2}\rvert<1
unless all
\theta_{i}=\pi
. Therefore the trial wave function
\lvert\Psi\rangle
is normalized in the thermodynamic limit.

We focus on the case where
\lvert\Psi\rangle
has
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
discrete translational symmetry, i.e.
A_{i+K}=A_{i}
. The variational parameters are
\theta_{i}
and
\phi_{i}
for
i=1,\cdots,K
. The phase space is
2K
dimensional, which admits chaos for
K\geq 2
. Without loss of generality, hereafter we assume
K
is sufficient large such that the eigenvalue
\lambda_{2}=\beta_{[1,K]}
is negligible. A translational invariant unit is
T_{[i,i+K-1]}
, whose dominant right and left eigenvectors are

|r_{i})=(1,x_{i},x_{i},1)^{\text{T}},\quad(l_{i}|=(\eta_{i},0,0,1-\eta_{i})\,,

(11)

where

\eta_{i}=1+\sum_{m\leq i-1}\prod_{j=m}^{i-1}(-1+x_{j}^{2})\,.

(12)

We can enforce the periodicity of
x_{i}
in the large unit cell to recover the small
K
case,
which can be justified from the general formula for arbitrary periodicity Eq. (
56
).
We list small
K
results explicitly

\eta_{i}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2-\cos^{4J}\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}}&K=1\\
\frac{\cos^{4J}\frac{\theta_{i-1}}{2}}{1-(-1+\cos^{4J}\frac{\theta_{i-1}}{2})(-1+\cos^{4J}\frac{\theta_{i}}{2})}&K=2\\
\frac{1+(-1+\cos^{4J}\frac{\theta_{i-1}}{2})\cos^{4J}\frac{\theta_{i-2}}{2}}{1-(-1+\cos^{4J}\frac{\theta_{i-1}}{2})(-1+\cos^{4J}\frac{\theta_{i-2}}{2})(-1+\cos^{4J}\frac{\theta_{i}}{2})}&K=3\end{cases}

(13)

The contraction between dominant eigenvectors and the transfer matrix
T_{[i,j]}
is given by reduction formulae (see Eq. (
60
) and (
61

(l_{i}|T_{[i,j]}=(l_{j+1}|,\quad T_{[i,j]}|r_{j+1})=|r_{i})\,.

(14)

The reduction formulae simply represent different choice of the
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
unit cell in the Perron-Frobenius theorem, which tells us
\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}(T_{[i,i+K-1]})^{m}=|r_{i})(l_{i}|
when
T_{[i,i+K-1]}
has a single dominant eigenvalue
\lambda=1
with eigenvectors
(l_{i}|
and
|r_{i})
. When we calculate the expectation value (and correlation functions), we can apply the Perron-Frobenius theorem to both the left and right semi-infinite transfer matrices. By taking trace operation and noticing
(l_{i}|r_{j})=1
even for
i\neq j
, the validity of the reduction formula can be justified.

V
variational energy

When we evaluate the variational energy
\langle\Psi\rvert H\lvert\Psi\rangle
, the projector
\mathcal{P}
in
\mathcal{P}s_{i}^{x}\mathcal{P}
in the Hamiltonian can be omitted, as implied by
\mathcal{P}\lvert\Psi\rangle=\lvert\Psi\rangle
. Then we only need to deal with the expectation value of on-site operator. Define the transfer matrix with onsite operator
q_{i}
as

T_{q_{i}}\equiv\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}(A^{\sigma}_{i})^{*}\otimes q_{i}^{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}A^{\sigma^{\prime}}_{i}\,,

(15)

where
q_{i}^{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}
is the matrix element of
q_{i}
in the Dicke basis. The explicit matrix elements of
T_{q_{i}}
are given in Eq. (
B
).

Choosing a specific unit cell from
j
to
j+K-1
and applying Perron-Frobenius theorem, we obtain

\langle\Psi\rvert q_{i}\lvert\Psi\rangle=(l_{j}|T_{j}\cdot T_{j+1}\cdots T_{i-1}\cdot T_{q_{i}}\cdot T_{i+1}\cdots T_{j+K-1}|r_{j})\,,

(16)

which, according to the reduction formula Eq. (
14
), can be written as

\displaystyle\langle\Psi\rvert q_{i}\lvert\Psi\rangle=(l_{i}|T_{q_{i}}|r_{i+1})=\langle 0\rvert_{i}q_{i}\lvert 0\rangle_{i}+\eta_{i}\tilde{h}_{q_{i}}\,,

(17)

where we use the shorthand notation

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{q_{i}}

\displaystyle\equiv x_{i}(-1+x_{i+1})\cdot\Big(\langle 0\rvert_{i}q_{i}(1-P_{i})\lvert\theta,\phi\rangle_{i}+

\displaystyle\langle\theta,\phi\rvert_{i}(1-P_{i})q_{i}\lvert 0\rangle_{i}\Big)+\langle\theta,\phi\rvert_{i}q_{i}\lvert\theta,\phi\rangle_{i}-\langle 0\rvert_{i}q_{i}\lvert 0\rangle_{i}\,.

(18)

The onsite operators of the Hamiltonian are simply
\Omega_{i}s^{x}_{i}
and
\Delta_{i}s^{z}_{i}
. From Table
1
, we can calculate the variational energy

\displaystyle\langle\Psi\rvert H\lvert\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}-\Delta_{i}+\eta_{i}\Big(\Delta_{i}(1-\cos\theta_{i})

\displaystyle+\Omega_{i}\sin\theta_{i}\cos\phi_{i}(1+\cos^{4J-2}\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}(-1+\cos^{2J}\frac{\theta_{i+1}}{2}))\Big)\,.

(19)

VI
variational dynamics and error rate

While the variational energy reduces to the expectation value of single onsite operators, the TDVP equations of motion and quantum leakage generally involve correlation between onsite operators. However, this is not a major obstacle, as a closed-form expression for the cumulative product of transfer matrices exists (Eq. (
IV
)).

Let us first compute the variational dynamics. From Eq. (
1
), we need to invert the Gram matrix and calculate the partial derivative
\text{Re}\langle\partial_{\mu_{i}}\psi\rvert H\lvert\psi\rangle_{c}
.
A typical term in
G_{\mu_{i},\nu_{j}}^{c}
is

(l_{i}|\bar{\partial}_{\mu}T_{i}\cdot T_{[i+1,j-1]}\cdot\partial_{\nu}T_{j}|r_{j+1})_{c}

(20)

where the subscript ‘c’ denotes removal of disconnected parts
(l_{i}|\bar{\partial}_{\mu}T_{i}|r_{i+1})\cdot(l_{j}|\partial_{\nu}T_{j}|r_{j+1})
. Here
\bar{\partial}_{\mu}T_{i}\equiv\sum_{\sigma}(\partial_{\mu_{i}}A^{\sigma}_{i})^{*}\otimes A^{\sigma}_{i}
and
\partial_{\nu}T_{j}\equiv\sum_{\sigma}(A^{\sigma}_{j})^{*}\otimes\partial_{\nu_{j}}A^{\sigma}_{j}
represent partial derivatives of transfer matrices.
For details, see Appendix
C.1
. Given that
G_{\theta_{i},\theta_{j}}^{c}
and
G_{\phi_{i},\phi_{j}}^{c}
are real symmetric,
\text{Im}G^{c}
is nonzero only off-diagonally, so we focus on
\text{Im}G^{c}_{\theta_{i},\phi_{j}}
. The compact formula of
(\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}})^{-1}
can be found in Appendix
C.2
.
It is worth noting that
\mathcal{P}\partial_{\mu_{i}}\lvert\Psi\rangle=\partial_{\mu_{i}}\lvert\Psi\rangle
as there is no component
(I_{i}-P_{i})\lvert\theta_{i},\phi_{i}\rangle\otimes(I_{i+1}-P_{i+1})\lvert\theta_{i+1},\phi_{i+1}\rangle
in matrix elements of
\partial_{\mu_{i}}A_{i}A_{i+1}
and
A_{i}\partial_{\mu_{i+1}}A_{i+1}
. Then we can remove the projector
\mathcal{P}
in
\text{Re}\langle\partial_{z_{i}}\psi\rvert H\lvert\psi\rangle_{c}
, which essentially reduces the Hamiltonian to the sum of single onsite operator
\Omega_{i}s_{i}^{x}+\Delta_{i}s_{i}^{z}
. A typical term in
\langle\partial_{\mu_{i}}\psi\rvert H\lvert\psi\rangle_{c}
is

(l_{i}|(\partial_{\mu}T_{i})\cdot T_{[i+1,j-1]}\cdot T_{q_{j}}|r_{j+1})_{c}\,,

(21)

where the subscript ‘c’ denotes removal of disconnected parts
(l_{i}|(\partial_{\mu}T_{i})|r_{i+1})\cdot(l_{j}|T_{q_{j}}|r_{j+1})
. The full calculation is rather lengthy (see Appendix
C.3
). Collecting these results, we give explicit results of the TDVP equations of motion in Appendix
C.4
, which are

\displaystyle\dot{\theta_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega_{i}\tan\phi_{i}}{J\sin\theta_{i}}\tilde{h}_{s_{i}^{x}}+\frac{\eta_{i-1}\Omega_{i-1}\cos^{4J-2}\frac{\theta_{i-1}}{2}\tan\phi_{i-1}\tan\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}}{\eta_{i}}\tilde{h}_{s_{i-1}^{x}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j\leq i-2}\frac{\eta_{j}\Omega_{j}\cos^{4J-2}\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}\tan\phi_{j}\tan\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}}{\eta_{i}}\tilde{h}_{s_{j}^{x}}\prod_{m=j+1}^{i-1}\tilde{c}_{m}

(22)

\displaystyle\dot{\phi_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega_{i}\tilde{h}_{s_{i}^{x}}+\Delta_{i}(1-\cos\theta_{i})}{J(1-\cos\theta_{i})}-\frac{2\cos^{4J-2}\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}\tan\frac{\theta_{i+1}}{2}}{\eta_{i+1}}\tilde{R}_{\theta_{i+1}}\,,

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{J\eta_{i}\sin\theta_{i}}\tilde{R}_{\theta_{i}}-\sum_{j\geq i+2}\frac{2\cos^{4J-2}\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}\tan\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}}{\eta_{j}}\tilde{R}_{\theta_{j}}\prod_{m=i+1}^{j-1}\tilde{c}_{m}\,,

(23)

where
\tilde{c}_{i}\equiv-1+(2J\tan^{2}\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}+1)\cos^{4J}\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}
and according to Eq. (
18
)
\tilde{h}_{s_{i}^{x}}=\sin\theta_{i}\cos\phi_{i}(1+\cos^{4J-2}\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}(-1+\cos^{2J}\frac{\theta_{i+1}}{2}))
. Here
\tilde{R}_{\theta_{i}}\equiv J\Omega_{i-1}\eta_{i-1}\cos^{2J}\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}\tan\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}\cos^{4J}\frac{\theta_{i-1}}{2}\tan\frac{\theta_{i-1}}{2}\cos\phi_{i-1}+\frac{\eta_{i}\Omega_{i}}{2\sin\theta_{i}}\Big(\tilde{h}_{s_{i}^{x}}+2\cos\phi_{i}(2J-1)(1-\cos\theta_{i})\cos^{4J}\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}(-1+\cos^{2J}\frac{\theta_{i+1}}{2})\tan\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}\Big)
. Since
\lvert\tilde{c}_{i}\rvert<1
it is often reasonable to truncate the summation until
\prod_{m}\tilde{c}_{m}
becomes negligible. It is interesting to note that
\tilde{c}_{i}=0
for
J=\frac{1}{2}
. In spin-
\frac{1}{2}
case, the summation term simply vanishes.

It is direct to verify that the variational dynamics is invariant under
\theta_{i}\rightarrow\theta_{i}+2\pi
for integer
J
and
\theta_{i}\rightarrow\theta_{i}+4\pi
for half-integer
J
.
J=\frac{1}{2}
is special, as a joint transformation
\theta_{i}\rightarrow\theta_{i}+2\pi,\,\phi_{i}\rightarrow\phi_{i}+\pi
leaves the variational dynamics unchanged.

Next we will evaluate the variational error rate. Given the Gram matrix
G_{\mu_{i},\nu_{j}}^{c}
,
\langle\partial_{\mu_{i}}\psi\rvert H\lvert\psi\rangle_{c}
and the TDVP equations of motion, we can compute the error rate from Eq. (
2
) once we solve the energy variance
\langle\Psi\rvert H^{2}\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}
. Unlike in
\langle\partial_{\mu_{i}}\psi\rvert H\lvert\psi\rangle_{c}
, the projector
\mathcal{P}
can not be simply omitted in the energy variance. It is useful to decompose
\mathcal{P}
into product of two-site operators
P_{i,i+1}
, which satisfy local relation
P_{i,i+1}A_{i}\cdot A_{i+1}=A_{i}\cdot A_{i+1}
and
A_{i}^{*}\cdot A_{i+1}^{*}P_{i,i+1}=A_{i}^{*}\cdot A_{i+1}^{*}
, and commute with
s_{j}^{z}
. This allows us to reduce terms involving
s_{i}^{z}
in
\langle\Psi\rvert H^{2}\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}
to the correlation of two onsite operators. For instance,
\langle\Psi\rvert\mathcal{P}s_{i}^{x}\mathcal{P}s_{j}^{z}\lvert\Psi\rangle=\langle\Psi\rvert s_{i}^{x}s_{j}^{z}\lvert\Psi\rangle
, which follows from first exchanging
P_{i,i+1}
with
s_{j}^{z}
and then acting
P_{i,i+1}
on
A_{i}\cdot A_{i+1}
in the
\lvert\Psi\rangle
or
A_{i}^{*}\cdot A_{i+1}^{*}
in the
\langle\Psi\rvert
. A typical term in the correlation of two onsite operators is

\displaystyle(l_{i}|T_{q_{i}}\cdot T_{[i+1,j-1]}\cdot T_{q_{j}}|r_{j+1})_{c}\,,

(24)

where the subscript ‘c’ denotes removal of disconnected parts

In previous discussion, we outlined the procedure for solving TDVP equations of motion and quantum leakage in the spin-
J
PXP model. It is natural to explore two extreme limits:
J=\frac{1}{2}
and
J\rightarrow\infty
. These limits are significant because they represent fundamentally different physical regimes. For
J=\frac{1}{2}
, the system is a set of interacting qubits with strong quantum fluctuation. On the other hand, in the large spin limit, the system enters a highly classical-like regime
[
23
]
. Note that the large spin limit is taken after the thermodynamic limit
L\rightarrow\infty
.

When
J=\frac{1}{2}
, the variational dynamics is surprisingly simple as
\tilde{c}_{i}=0

\dot{\theta}_{i}=2\Omega_{i}\sin\phi_{i}\cos\frac{\theta_{i+1}}{2}+\Omega_{i-1}\frac{\eta_{i-1}\sin\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}\sin\phi_{i-1}\sin\theta_{i-1}}{\eta_{i}}\,,

(28)

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}_{i}

\displaystyle=2\Omega_{i}\cos\frac{\theta_{i+1}}{2}\cos\phi_{i}\cot\theta_{i}+2\Delta_{i}-\Omega_{i-1}\frac{\cos\phi_{i-1}\sec\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}\sin\theta_{i-1}\eta_{i-1}}{2\eta_{i}}

\displaystyle-\Omega_{i}\frac{\cos\phi_{i}\sin\theta_{i}\sin\frac{\theta_{i+1}}{2}\eta_{i}\tan\frac{\theta_{i+1}}{2}}{2\eta_{i+1}}-\Omega_{i+1}\cos\frac{\theta_{i+2}}{2}\cos\phi_{i+1}\tan\frac{\theta_{i+1}}{2}\,.

(29)

The variational error rate also has a compact form

\Gamma^{2}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\Omega_{i}^{2}\sin^{2}\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}\sin^{2}\frac{\theta_{i+1}}{2}\frac{\eta_{i}(1-\eta_{i})}{\eta_{i+1}}\,.

(30)

When

, away from the south pole and the north pole,

tends to zero very rapidly with increasing

. Taking this limit in Eq. (
22
) and (

), we obtain the simplified variational dynamics

J\dot{\theta}_{i}\rightarrow\Omega_{i}\sin\phi_{i}\,,

(31)

J\dot{\phi}_{i}\rightarrow\Delta_{i}+\Omega_{i}\cos\phi_{i}\cot\theta_{i}\,.

(32)

These dynamics can also be derived from the semiclassical energy
E_{sc}=\sum_{i}-\Delta_{i}+\eta_{i}(\Omega_{i}\sin\theta_{i}\cos\phi_{i}+\Delta_{i}(1-\cos\theta_{i}))
through
\dot{\theta}_{i}=-\frac{1}{2\text{Im}G^{c}_{\phi_{i},\theta_{i}}}\partial_{\phi_{i}}E_{sc}
and
\dot{\phi}_{i}=-\frac{1}{2\text{Im}G^{c}_{\theta_{i},\phi_{i}}}\partial_{\theta_{i}}E_{sc}
, where
-2\text{Im}G^{c}
is the antisymmetric metric tensor. The variational dynamics are quite slow, as the spin operators are normalized by
J
. The variational error rate is given by

\Gamma^{2}\rightarrow\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{\Omega_{i}^{2}\eta_{i}(-1+2\eta_{i})\cos^{2}\phi_{i}}{2J}\,.

(33)

Interestingly, the scaling of
\Gamma
depends on the parity of
K
, which can be justified from Eq. (
13
). This dependence can be rigorously analyzed by retaining the lowest-order terms in
x_{i}^{2}
in
\Gamma
. For odd
K
,
\Gamma^{2}
approaches to zero quickly as
\eta_{i}\rightarrow 1/2
exponentially fast with
J
. This implies that the timescale
\tau
for the breakdown of quantum-classical correspondence, i.e.
\int_{0}^{\tau}\Gamma dt\gtrsim 1
, is exponential in
J
. For even
K
, we find that the time scale
\tau
in general scales as
\sqrt{J}
. The difference in timescales between odd and even
K
is an interesting feature, but we are unsure whether this feature is just a reflection on the limitation of our simple ansatz.

VII
Discussion

In this work, we have used the time-dependent variational principle (TDVP) method to investigate the quantum dynamics of the spin-
J
PXP model. This model is an effective model of the Rydberg atom array when the Rydberg blockade takes effect. To effectively capture the Rydberg physics and facilitate efficient analytical derivation, the minimally entangled MPS that satisfies the blockade requirement is adopted. Our ansatz differs from previous literature by permitting general
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
discrete translational symmetry. By mathematical induction, we solve the transfer matrix over arbitrary interval of lattice sites. Combined with the Perron-Frobenius theorem, it enables us to work out nearly all essential quantities in the variational dynamics and quantum leakage. The only remaining quantity to determine is the inverse of the connected Gram matrix. Fortunately, the inverse has a compact form, revealing that its matrix elements decay rapidly with distance from the diagonal. As a result, the variational dynamics and error rate can be efficiently truncated.

Our study of the PXP model is based on the TDVP method. Recently, there is tremendous effort in investigating Rydberg physics with variational principle, e.g., TDVP in higher spatial dimension
[
24
]
and larger blockade radius ansatz
[
25
]
. The advantages of the variational method are twofold. First, it offers analytical tractability. The analytical nature of TDVP equations mitigates numerical instabilities during integration and provides a solid foundation for rigorous analysis of the variational dynamics. The analytical variational method is complementary to the tensor-network based numerical techniques
[
26
,
27
]
. Second, TDVP dynamics presents a clear semi-classical picture, offering a novel paradigm for quantum-classical correspondence in the context of quantum many-body system
[
28
]
. At the same time, there are obvious limitations in TDVP method. Most noticeable is the choice of an appropriate variational anstaz. While we find the
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
transfer matrix through mathematical induction, we are not aware of a universal approach to solve
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
transfer matrix for a broader class of variational ansatz, especially for large bond-dimensional case, which can be used to diagnose quantum chaos
[
29
]
.

Besides the variational ansatz, there are several promising directions that worth further exploration in Rydberg atom arrays using variational method.
First, we can systematically construct the adiabatic path between the initial state and the target state using optimal control theory (by minimizing the quantum leakage with fixed boundary conditions), which may be useful for experimental preparation of GHZ state with high fidelity
[
30
]
.
Second, it is interesting to investigate whether long-time oscillations for specific initial states are related with period orbits, which need not necessarily be unstable. Indeed, enhanced revival and suppressed entanglement growth have been identified for initial states on KAM tori
[
15
]
. Nevertheless, the connection between quantum many-body scarring and periodic orbits in phase space remains incomplete.
Third, the quantum phase transition with competing crystalline order is still perplexing due to its non-perturbative feature. While the sublattice symmetry breaking has been studied experimentally
[
5
]
and numerically
[
6
]
, a complete analytical understanding is still lacking.
From the perspective of quantum-classical correspondence, the ground state corresponds to a fixed point in the phase space. Therefore the spatial periodicity of these fixed points may shed light on the sublattice symmetry of the ground state.
Last but not the least, experiments have demonstrated that periodic detuning can enhance quantum many-body oscillations
[
31
]
, which can be used for the construction of robust discrete time crystal.
The stabilization mechanism of periodic driving might be understood by studying properties of Floquet fixed points in the variational dynamics.

In conclusion, while the TDVP method offers powerful tools for understanding non-equilibrium quantum dynamics, its limitations and the ongoing exploration of new variational ansatz provide exciting avenues for future research in quantum optimal control, quantum phase transitions, and quantum chaos.

C.2
Inverse of the Gram matrix

To solve the equations of motion, we need to calculate the inverse of the Gram matrix
(\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\theta,\bm{\phi}}})^{-1}
. Inverting a
K\times K
matrix is generally a challenging task. However, we notice that
\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\theta,\bm{\phi}}}
is closely related to the matrix
A

\displaystyle A_{ij}=\begin{cases}a_{i}b_{i}\,\prod_{m=i+1}^{i+K-1}z_{m}&\text{if}\,i=j,\\
a_{i}b_{j}\,\prod_{m=i+1}^{j-1}z_{m}&\text{if}\,i<j,\\
a_{i}b_{j}\,\prod_{m=i+1}^{j+K-1}z_{m}&\text{if}\,i>j,\end{cases}

(96)

where
A
has an analytically tractable inverse given by

\displaystyle A^{-1}_{ij}=\begin{cases}-\frac{z_{i}}{a_{i}b_{i}(1-\prod_{m=1}^{K}z_{m})}&\text{if}\,i=j,\\
\frac{1}{a_{i-1}b_{j}(1-\prod_{m=1}^{K}z_{m})}&\text{if}\,j=i-1,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(97)

The matrix
A
closely approximates

-\mathrm{i}\eta_{i}J\frac{\sin\theta_{i}}{2}
is added. This difference introduces a diagonal correction matrix
C
with elements

A+C
, which is

(A+C)^{-1}=C^{-1}-C^{-1}(A^{-1}+C^{-1})^{-1}C^{-1}.

(98)

We note that, after factoring out the common term

, its structure resembles that of
A^{-1}
.
The inverse can be obtained analogously from Eq. (
96
) and (
97

\displaystyle(A^{-1}+C^{-1})^{-1}_{ij}=\begin{cases}\frac{1-\prod_{m=1}^{K}z_{m}}{1-\prod_{m=1}^{K}\tilde{c}_{m}}a_{i}b_{i}\prod_{m=i+1}^{i+K-1}\tilde{c}_{m}&\text{if}\,i=j,\\
\frac{1-\prod_{m=1}^{K}z_{m}}{1-\prod_{m=1}^{K}\tilde{c}_{m}}a_{i}b_{j}\prod_{m=i+1}^{j-1}\tilde{c}_{m}&\text{if}\,i<j,\\

(100)

This provides a formal solution

in principle.
Comparing with the connected Gram matrix, we find the parameters

C.3

We begin by noting that the matrix elements of

The connected part of

\displaystyle(l_{i}|(\partial_{\mu}T_{i})\cdot T_{[i+1,j-1]}\cdot T_{q_{j}}|r_{j+1})_{c}

\displaystyle=\eta_{i}\big(-\eta_{i+1}\langle\Omega\rvert_{i}B_{\mu_{i}}\lvert\Omega\rangle_{i}+x_{i}^{2}\langle 0\rvert_{i}B_{\mu_{i}}\lvert 0\rangle_{i}\big)\beta_{[i+1,j-1]}\tilde{h}_{q_{j}}.

(111)

If we define

, this equation will hold for

. Similarly for

, we have

\displaystyle(l_{j}|T_{q_{j}}\cdot T_{[j+1,i-1]}\cdot(\partial_{\mu}T_{i})|r_{i+1})_{c}

\displaystyle\equiv(l_{j}|T_{q_{j}}\cdot T_{[j+1,i-1]}\cdot(\partial_{\mu}T_{i})|r_{i+1})-(l_{i}|(\partial_{\mu}T_{i})|r_{i+1})\cdot(l_{j}|T_{q_{j}}|r_{j+1})

\displaystyle=-\tilde{h}_{q_{j}}\eta_{j}\eta_{j+1}\beta_{[j+1,i-1]}\langle\Omega\rvert_{i}B_{\mu_{i}}\lvert\Omega\rangle_{i}.

(112)

For the case

, the expression becomes

\displaystyle(l_{i-1}|T_{q_{i-1}}\cdot(\partial_{\mu}T_{i})|r_{i+1})_{c}

\displaystyle\equiv(l_{i-1}|T_{q_{i-1}}\cdot(\partial_{\mu}T_{i})|r_{i+1})-(l_{i}|(\partial_{\mu}T_{i})|r_{i+1})\cdot(l_{i-1}|T_{q_{i-1}}|r_{i})

\displaystyle=-\tilde{h}_{q_{i-1}}\eta_{i-1}\eta_{i}\langle\Omega\rvert_{i}B_{\mu_{i}}\lvert\Omega\rangle_{i}+\eta_{i-1}x_{i-1}x_{i}\langle 0\rvert_{i}B_{\mu_{i}}\lvert 0\rangle_{i}\langle\Omega\rvert_{i-1}Q_{i-1}q_{i-1}\lvert 0\rangle_{i-1}.

(113)

The self-term contribution
j=i
is, according to Eq. (

)

\displaystyle(l_{i}|\partial_{\mu}T_{q_{i}}|r_{i+1})_{c}

\displaystyle\equiv(l_{i}|\partial_{\mu}T_{q_{i}}|r_{i+1})-(l_{i}|(\partial_{\mu}T_{i})|r_{i+1})\cdot(l_{i}|T_{q_{i}}|r_{i+1})

\displaystyle=\eta_{i}(\tilde{h}_{q_{i}B_{\mu_{i}}}+\langle 0\rvert_{i}q_{i}B_{\mu_{i}}\lvert 0\rangle_{i})-(\eta_{i}\tilde{h}_{q_{i}}+\langle 0\rvert_{i}q_{i}\lvert 0\rangle_{i})\eta_{i}\langle\Omega\rvert_{i}B_{\mu_{i}}\lvert\Omega\rangle_{i},

(114)

where the shorthand notation

comes from Eq. (
110
).

Combining these results and referring to Tables
1
and
2
, we obtain the real part of
\langle\partial_{\theta_{i}}\Psi\rvert H\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle\text{Re}\langle\partial_{\theta_{i}}\Psi\rvert H\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{L}{K}\bigg(-J\Omega_{i-1}\tan\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}x_{i-1}^{2}\tan\frac{\theta_{i-1}}{2}\cos\phi_{i-1}x_{i}\eta_{i-1}

\displaystyle-\eta_{i}\Omega_{i}\cos\phi_{i}\Big(\frac{1}{2}+x_{i}^{2}(-1+x_{i+1})(1-2J+\frac{4J-1}{2\cos^{2}\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}})\Big)\bigg)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=i}^{i+K-1}(\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}})_{ij}\frac{\Omega_{i}\cos\phi_{i}(\sin\theta_{i}+2x_{i}^{2}(-1+x_{i+1})\tan\frac{\theta_{i}}{2})+\Delta_{i}(1-\cos\theta_{i})}{J(1-\cos\theta_{i})},

which contains the term involving

. The imaginary part of

\displaystyle+\eta_{i}\Omega_{i}\frac{\sin\phi_{i}}{2}+\Omega_{i}\eta_{i}x^{2}_{i}(-1+x_{i+1})\frac{1}{\sin\theta_{i}}\tan\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}\sin\phi_{i}\Big).

(116)

Similarly, the real and imaginary parts of

\displaystyle-\frac{L}{K}\Big(\frac{\eta_{i}}{2}\Omega_{i}\cos\phi_{i}\Big(\sin\theta_{i}+(\cos\theta_{i}+2J(1-\cos\theta_{i}))2x_{i}^{2}(-1+x_{i+1})\tan\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}\Big)\Big).

(118)

C.4
TDVP equations

To account for the contributions from the term
\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}}
in
\text{Re}\langle\partial_{\mu_{j}}\Psi\rvert H\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}
, we define a residual term
R_{\phi_{j}}
for
\text{Re}\langle\partial_{\phi_{j}}\Psi\rvert H\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}
, which excludes contributions involving
(\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}})_{jl}
. This residual term is given by

\displaystyle R_{\phi_{j}}\equiv\frac{L}{K}\Big(-\eta_{j}\frac{\tan\phi_{j}}{2}\Omega_{j}\cos\phi_{j}(\sin\theta_{j}+2x_{j}^{2}(-1+x_{j+1})\tan\frac{\theta_{j}}{2})\Big).

(119)

Using this definition, along with the TDVP equations of motion and Eq. (
103
), we can write the equation for
\dot{\theta}_{i}
as

\displaystyle\dot{\theta}_{i}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j}(\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\phi},\bm{\theta}})_{ij}^{-1}\text{Re}\langle\partial_{\phi_{j}}\Psi\rvert H\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}(\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}})_{ji}^{-1}R_{\phi_{j}}.

(120)

Similarly, we define a residual term
R_{\theta_{j}}
for
\text{Re}\langle\partial_{\theta_{j}}\Psi\rvert H\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}
, which excludes contributions involving
(\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}})_{jl}
. The residual term is given by

\displaystyle R_{\theta_{j}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{L}{K}\Big(-J\Omega_{j-1}\tan\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}x_{j-1}^{2}\tan\frac{\theta_{j-1}}{2}\cos\phi_{j-1}x_{j}\eta_{j-1}

\displaystyle-\eta_{j}\Omega_{j}\cos\phi_{j}\Big(\frac{1}{2}+x_{j}^{2}(-1+x_{j+1})(1-2J+\frac{4J-1}{2\cos^{2}\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}})\Big)\Big).

(121)

The equation of motion for
\dot{\phi}_{i}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}_{i}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j}(\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}})_{ij}^{-1}\text{Re}\langle\partial_{\theta_{j}}\Psi\rvert H\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega_{i}\cos\phi_{i}(\sin\theta_{i}+2x_{i}^{2}(-1+x_{i+1})\tan\frac{\theta_{i}}{2})+\Delta_{i}(1-\cos\theta_{i})}{J(1-\cos\theta_{i})}

\displaystyle-\sum_{j}(\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}})_{ij}^{-1}\cdot R_{\theta_{j}}.

(122)

For the specific case of spin
J=\frac{1}{2}
, the TDVP equations of motion become

\dot{\theta}_{i}=2\Omega_{i}\sin\phi_{i}\cos\frac{\theta_{i+1}}{2}+\Omega_{i-1}\frac{\eta_{i-1}\sin\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}\sin\phi_{i-1}\sin\theta_{i-1}}{\eta_{i}}

(123)

and

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}_{i}

\displaystyle=2\Omega_{i}\cos\frac{\theta_{i+1}}{2}\cos\phi_{i}\cot\theta_{i}+2\Delta_{i}-\Omega_{i-1}\frac{\cos\phi_{i-1}\sec\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}\sin\theta_{i-1}\eta_{i-1}}{2\eta_{i}}

\displaystyle-\Omega_{i}\frac{\cos\phi_{i}\sin\theta_{i}\sin\frac{\theta_{i+1}}{2}\eta_{i}\tan\frac{\theta_{i+1}}{2}}{2\eta_{i+1}}-\Omega_{i+1}\cos\frac{\theta_{i+2}}{2}\cos\phi_{i+1}\tan\frac{\theta_{i+1}}{2}

(124)

These equations provide a compact and efficient description of the TDVP dynamics for spin-
\frac{1}{2}
system.

Appendix D
Calculation of quantum leakage

Quantum leakage quantifies the instantaneous error rate in variational quantum dynamics. The leakage rate,
\Gamma^{2}
, is expressed as

\displaystyle\Gamma^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L}\Big(\langle\Psi\rvert H^{2}\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}-2\sum_{i}\dot{\mu}_{i}\text{Im}\langle\partial_{\mu_{i}}\Psi\rvert H\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}+\sum_{ij}\dot{\mu}_{i}\text{Re}\langle\partial_{\mu_{i}}\Psi|\partial_{\mu_{j}}\Psi\rangle_{c}\dot{\mu}_{j}

\displaystyle=\langle\Psi\rvert H^{2}\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}-2\sum_{i}\dot{\theta}_{i}\text{Im}\langle\partial_{\theta_{i}}\Psi\rvert H\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}-2\sum_{i}\dot{\phi}_{i}\text{Im}\langle\partial_{\phi_{i}}\Psi\rvert H\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle+\sum_{ij}\dot{\theta}_{i}\text{Re}\langle\partial_{\theta_{i}}\Psi|\partial_{\theta_{j}}\Psi\rangle_{c}\dot{\theta}_{j}+\sum_{ij}\dot{\phi}_{i}\text{Re}\langle\partial_{\phi_{i}}\Psi|\partial_{\phi_{j}}\Psi\rangle_{c}\dot{\phi}_{j}\Big),

(125)

where the subscript “c” denotes the connected part of the expectation value. The parameters
\mu_{i}
represent the variational parameters.

Before addressing quantum leakage, let us shift focus to discuss the residual terms, which are crucial for the simplification of quantum leakage. In previous section, we define residual terms of
\text{Re}\langle\partial_{\mu_{i}}\Psi\rvert H\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}
that exclude contributions involving
(\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}})
in Eq. (
119
) and (
121
), i.e.

\displaystyle R_{\phi_{j}}=\frac{L}{K}\Big(-\eta_{j}\frac{\tan\phi_{j}}{2}\Omega_{j}\cos\phi_{j}(\sin\theta_{j}+2x_{j}^{2}(-1+x_{j+1})\tan\frac{\theta_{j}}{2})\Big)

(126)

\displaystyle R_{\theta_{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{L}{K}\Big(-J\Omega_{j-1}\tan\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}x_{j-1}^{2}\tan\frac{\theta_{j-1}}{2}\cos\phi_{j-1}x_{j}\eta_{j-1}

\displaystyle-\eta_{j}\Omega_{j}\cos\phi_{j}\Big(\frac{1}{2}+x_{j}^{2}(-1+x_{j+1})(1-2J+\frac{4J-1}{2\cos^{2}\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}})\Big)\Big)

(127)

Similarly, we define the residual term of
\text{Im}\langle\partial_{\mu_{i}}\Psi\rvert H\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}
that excludes the contribution involving
G^{c}_{\phi_{i},\phi_{j}}

\displaystyle I_{\theta_{j}}\equiv\frac{L}{K}\Big(J\Omega_{j-1}\tan\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}\tan\frac{\theta_{j-1}}{2}\sin\phi_{j-1}x^{2}_{j-1}x_{j}\eta_{j-1}+\eta_{j}\frac{\tan\phi_{j}}{2\sin\theta_{j}}\Omega_{j}\tilde{h}_{s_{j}^{x}}\Big),

(128)

I_{\phi_{j}}=-\frac{L}{K}\Big(\frac{\eta_{j}}{2}\Omega_{j}\cos\phi_{j}\Big(\sin\theta_{j}+(\cos\theta_{j}+2J(1-\cos\theta_{j}))2x_{j}^{2}(-1+x_{j+1})\tan\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}\Big)\Big),

(129)

where the shorthand notation
\tilde{h}_{s^{x}_{i}}=\cos\phi_{i}(\sin\theta_{i}+2x_{i}^{2}(-1+x_{i+1})\tan\frac{\theta_{i}}{2})
comes from Eq. (
110
). These residual terms satisfy important relations

R_{\theta_{j}}=\frac{L}{K}\Big(-J\Omega_{j-1}\tan\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}x_{j-1}^{2}\tan\frac{\theta_{j-1}}{2}\cos\phi_{j-1}x_{j}\eta_{j-1}\Big)+\frac{I_{\phi_{j}}}{\sin\theta_{j}},

(130)

and

I_{\theta_{j}}=\frac{L}{K}\Big(J\Omega_{j-1}\tan\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}\tan\frac{\theta_{j-1}}{2}\sin\phi_{j-1}x^{2}_{j-1}x_{j}\eta_{j-1}\Big)-\frac{R_{\phi_{j}}}{\sin\theta_{j}}.

(131)

We now calculate quantum leakage. With the Gram matrix, variational dynamics and
\langle\partial_{\mu_{i}}\Psi\rvert H\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}
already determined, the quantum leakage can be resolved once the variance of the Hamiltonian is calculated. The variance
\langle\Psi\rvert H^{2}\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}
is bilinear in the spin operators
s^{x}
and
s^{x}
. Then we can decompose the variance into ZZ, ZX, XZ and XX terms. The first three terms involves the correlation between onsite operators, which are for
|j-i|>1

\displaystyle(l_{i}|T_{q_{i}}\cdot T_{[i+1,j-1]}\cdot T_{q_{j}}|r_{j+1})_{c}

\displaystyle=(l_{i}|T_{q_{i}}\cdot T_{[i+1,j-1]}\cdot T_{q_{j}}|r_{j+1})-(l_{i}|T_{q_{i}}|r_{i+1})\cdot(l_{j}|T_{q_{j}}|r_{j+1})

\displaystyle=-\tilde{h}_{q_{i}}\tilde{h}_{q_{j}}\eta_{i}\eta_{i+1}\beta_{[i+1,j-1]},

(132)

and for
j=i+1

\displaystyle(l_{i}|T_{q_{i}}\cdot T_{q_{i+1}}|r_{i+2})_{c}

\displaystyle=(l_{i}|T_{q_{i}}\cdot T_{q_{i+1}}|r_{i+2})-(l_{i}|T_{q_{i}}|r_{i+1})\cdot(l_{i+1}|T_{q_{i+1}}|r_{i+2})

\displaystyle=\eta_{i}x_{i}x_{i+2}\Big(\langle\varOmega\rvert_{i}Q_{i}q_{i}\lvert 0\rangle_{i}\langle 0\rvert_{i+1}q_{i+1}Q_{i+1}\lvert\varOmega\rangle_{i+1}

\displaystyle+\langle 0\rvert_{i}q_{i}Q_{i}\lvert\varOmega\rangle_{i}\langle\varOmega\rvert_{i+1}Q_{i+1}q_{i+1}\lvert 0\rangle_{i+1}\Big)-\tilde{h}_{q_{i}}\tilde{h}_{q_{i+1}}\eta_{i}\eta_{i+1}.

(133)

The self-term
j=i
contribution is essentially the expectation of single onsite operators, which is presented in Eq. (
109
). For the XX term, special case is required. When the distance
|j-i|
between two spin operators
s^{x}_{i}
and
s^{x}_{j}
is larger than one, the correlation reduces to Eq. (
132
). The nontrivial case occurs when
|j-i|\leq 1
. From
\langle\Psi\rvert\mathcal{P}s_{i}^{x}\mathcal{P}q_{i+1}\lvert\Psi\rangle=\langle\Psi\rvert P_{i-1,i}P_{i,i+1}s^{x}_{i}P_{i-1,i}P_{i,i+1}q_{i+1}\lvert\Psi\rangle=\langle\Psi\rvert P_{i-1}s^{x}_{i}P_{i+1}q_{i+1}\lvert\Psi\rangle
, we can omit
\mathcal{P}
by using the dressed operator
P_{i-1}s_{i}^{x}P_{i+1}
. The XX term involves for
|j-i|=1

\displaystyle(l_{i-1}|T_{P_{i-1}}\cdot T_{s_{i}^{x}}\cdot T_{P_{i+1}q_{i+1}}|r_{i+2})_{c}

\displaystyle=(l_{i-1}|T_{P_{i-1}}\cdot T_{s_{i}^{x}}\cdot T_{P_{i+1}q_{i+1}}|r_{i+2})-(l_{i}|T_{s_{i}^{x}}|r_{i+1})\cdot(l_{i+1}|T_{q_{i+1}}|r_{i+2})

\displaystyle=-\tilde{h}_{s_{i}^{x}}\tilde{h}_{q_{i+1}}\eta_{i}\eta_{i+1}+\eta_{i}x_{i}x_{i+2}\langle\varOmega\rvert_{i}Q_{i}s_{i}^{x}\lvert 0\rangle_{i}\langle 0\rvert_{i+1}q_{i+1}Q_{i+1}\lvert\varOmega\rangle_{i+1},

(134)

\displaystyle(l_{i}|T_{P_{i-1}}\cdot T_{s_{i}^{x}}\cdot T_{P_{i+1}}|r_{i+2})_{c}

\displaystyle=(l_{i-1}|T_{P_{i-1}q_{i-1}}\cdot T_{s_{i}^{x}}\cdot T_{P_{i+1}}|r_{i+2})-(l_{i}|T_{s_{i}^{x}}|r_{i+1})\cdot(l_{i-1}|T_{q_{i-1}}|r_{i})

\displaystyle=-\tilde{h}_{s_{i}^{x}}\tilde{h}_{q_{i-1}}\eta_{i}\eta_{i-1}+\eta_{i-1}x_{i-1}x_{i+1}\langle\varOmega\rvert_{i}Q_{i}s_{i}^{x}\lvert 0\rangle_{i}\langle 0\rvert_{i-1}q_{i-1}Q_{i-1}\lvert\varOmega\rangle_{i-1},

(135)

and for
j=i
case

\displaystyle(l_{i-1}|T_{P_{i-1}}\cdot T_{(s_{i}^{x})^{2}}\cdot T_{P_{i+1}}|r_{i+2})_{c}

\displaystyle=(l_{i-1}|T_{P_{i-1}}\cdot T_{(s_{i}^{x})^{2}}\cdot T_{P_{i+1}}|r_{i+2})-(l_{i}|T_{s_{i}^{x}}|r_{i+1})^{2}

\displaystyle=\eta_{i}(\tilde{h}_{(s_{i}^{x})^{2}}+(1+x_{i}^{2}(-1+x_{i+1}^{2}))\langle 0\rvert_{i}(s_{i}^{x})^{2}\lvert 0\rangle_{i})-(\eta_{i}\tilde{h}_{s_{i}^{x}})^{2}.

(136)

Collecting these equations, and substituting results from Tables
1
and
2
into ZZ, ZX, XZ and XX terms, we can solve the energy variance. Explicitly, the ZZ term is

\displaystyle\text{Var}_{ZZ}

\displaystyle=-\frac{L}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j=i+1}^{i+K}\Delta_{i}\Delta_{j}(1-\cos\theta_{i})(1-\cos\theta_{j})\frac{\eta_{i}\eta_{i+1}\beta_{[i+1,j-1]}}{1-\beta_{[1,K]}}

\displaystyle-\frac{L}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j=i-K}^{i-1}\Delta_{i}\Delta_{j}(1-\cos\theta_{i})(1-\cos\theta_{j})\frac{\eta_{j}\eta_{j+1}\beta_{[j+1,i-1]}}{1-\beta_{[1,K]}}

\displaystyle+\frac{L}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\Delta_{i}^{2}\eta_{i}\frac{1+\cos\theta_{i}-2J(-1+\eta_{i})(1-\cos\theta_{i})}{J}\sin^{2}\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}.

(137)

One can see that

\displaystyle(\text{Var}_{ZZ})_{ij}=\frac{\Delta_{i}\Delta_{j}}{J^{2}}G^{c}_{\phi_{i},\phi_{j}}.

(138)

The ZX term is

\displaystyle\text{Var}_{ZX}

\displaystyle=-\frac{L}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j=i+1}^{i+K}\eta_{i}\eta_{i+1}\Delta_{i}\Omega_{j}(1-\cos\theta_{i})\Big(\sin\theta_{j}\cos\phi_{j}

\displaystyle+2(-1+x_{j+1})x_{j}^{2}\tan\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}\cos\phi_{j}\Big)\frac{\beta_{[i+1,j-1]}}{1-\beta_{[1,K]}}

\displaystyle-\frac{L}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j=i-K}^{i-1}\eta_{j}\eta_{j+1}\Delta_{i}\Omega_{j}(1-\cos\theta_{i})\Big(\sin\theta_{j}\cos\phi_{j}

\displaystyle+2(-1+x_{j+1})x_{j}^{2}\tan\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}\cos\phi_{j}\Big)\frac{\beta_{[j+1,i-1]}}{1-\beta_{[1,K]}}

\displaystyle+\frac{L}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\Delta_{i}\Omega_{i}\frac{\eta_{i}}{2J}\Big(\sin\theta_{i}\cos\phi_{i}(\cos\theta_{i}+2J(1-\eta_{i})(1-\cos\theta_{i}))

\displaystyle+\mathrm{i}\sin\theta_{i}\sin\phi_{i}+2\mathrm{i}\sin\phi_{i}x_{i}^{2}(-1+x_{i+1})\tan\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}

\displaystyle+2(1-2J\eta_{i}+2J\eta_{i}\cos\theta_{i})\cos\phi_{i}x_{i}^{2}(-1+x_{i+1})\tan\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}\Big).

(139)

Notice for
i\neq j

\displaystyle(\text{Var}_{ZX})_{ij}=\frac{\Delta_{i}\Omega_{j}}{J^{2}}G^{c}_{\phi_{i},\phi_{j}}\frac{\sin\theta_{j}\cos\phi_{j}+2(-1+x_{j+1})x_{j}^{2}\tan\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}\cos\phi_{j}}{1-\cos\theta_{j}}.

(140)

The imaginary parts of ZX and XZ terms are canceled, since
\text{Var}_{XZ}=\text{Var}_{ZX}^{*}

\displaystyle(\text{Var}_{ZX})+(\text{Var}_{XZ})

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j=i}^{i+K-1}\frac{\Delta_{i}}{J^{2}}G^{c}_{\phi_{i},\phi_{j}}\frac{\Omega_{j}\tilde{h}_{s^{x}_{j}}}{1-\cos\theta_{j}}

\displaystyle-2\frac{L}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{\eta_{i}}{2J}\Delta_{i}\Omega_{i}\cos\phi_{i}\Big(\sin\theta_{i}+(\cos\theta_{i}+2J(1-\cos\theta_{i}))2x_{i}^{2}(-1+x_{i+1})\tan\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j=i}^{i+K-1}\frac{\Delta_{i}}{J^{2}}G^{c}_{\phi_{i},\phi_{j}}\frac{\Omega_{j}\tilde{h}_{s^{x}_{j}}}{1-\cos\theta_{j}}+2\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{\Delta_{i}}{J}I_{\phi_{i}}.

(141)

The last equality can be derived from Eq. (
118
).
The XX term in
\langle\Psi\rvert H^{2}\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}
is

\displaystyle\text{Var}_{XX}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j=i}^{i+K-1}\frac{G^{c}_{\phi_{i},\phi_{j}}}{J^{2}}\frac{\Omega_{i}\tilde{h}_{s^{x}_{i}}}{1-\cos\theta_{i}}\frac{\Omega_{j}\tilde{h}_{s^{x}_{j}}}{1-\cos\theta_{j}}

\displaystyle+2\frac{L}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\Omega_{i}\Omega_{i+1}\eta_{i}x_{i}^{2}x_{i+1}x_{i+2}\tan\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}\tan\frac{\theta_{i+1}}{2}\cos(\phi_{i+1}-\phi_{i})

\displaystyle+\frac{L}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{\eta_{i}\Omega_{i}^{2}}{2J}\Big(1+(-1+2J)\cos^{2}\phi_{i}\sin^{2}\theta_{i}

\displaystyle+\frac{(-1-2J+(-1+2J)\cos\theta_{i})(\tilde{h}_{s^{x}_{i}})^{2}}{1-\cos\theta_{i}}

\displaystyle+x_{i}^{2}(-1+x_{i+1})(1+x_{i+1}+2(-1+2J)\cos 2\phi_{i}\tan^{2}\frac{\theta_{i}}{2})\Big).

(142)

Finally we have the variance of the Hamiltonian
\langle\Psi\rvert H^{2}\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}=\text{Var}_{XX}+\text{Var}_{ZX}+\text{Var}_{XZ}+\text{Var}_{ZZ}
, which is

\displaystyle\langle\Psi\rvert H^{2}\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j=i}^{i+K-1}\frac{G^{c}_{\phi_{i},\phi_{j}}}{J^{2}}\frac{\Omega_{i}\tilde{h}_{s^{x}_{i}}+\Delta_{i}\tilde{h}_{s^{z}_{i}}}{1-\cos\theta_{i}}\frac{\Omega_{j}\tilde{h}_{s^{x}_{j}}+\Delta_{j}\tilde{h}_{s^{z}_{j}}}{1-\cos\theta_{j}}+2\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{\Delta_{i}}{J}I_{\phi_{i}}

\displaystyle+2\frac{L}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\Omega_{i}\Omega_{i+1}\eta_{i}x_{i}^{2}x_{i+1}x_{i+2}\tan\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}\tan\frac{\theta_{i+1}}{2}\cos(\phi_{i+1}-\phi_{i})

\displaystyle+\frac{L}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{\eta_{i}\Omega_{i}^{2}}{2J}\Big(1+(-1+2J)\cos^{2}\phi_{i}\sin^{2}\theta_{i}

\displaystyle+\frac{(-1-2J+(-1+2J)\cos\theta_{i})(\tilde{h}_{s^{x}_{i}})^{2}}{1-\cos\theta_{i}}

\displaystyle+x_{i}^{2}(-1+x_{i+1})(1+x_{i+1}+2(-1+2J)\cos 2\phi_{i}\tan^{2}\frac{\theta_{i}}{2})\Big),

(143)

where
\Omega_{i}\tilde{h}_{s^{x}_{i}}+\Delta_{i}\tilde{h}_{s^{z}_{i}}=\Omega_{i}\cos\phi_{i}(\sin\theta_{i}+2x_{i}^{2}(-1+x_{i+1})\tan\frac{\theta_{i}}{2})+\Delta_{i}(1-\cos\theta_{i})
comes from Eq. (
110
).

Next, we solve other terms in the quantum leakage, which involve the TDVP equations of motion Eq. (
120
) and (
122
). There are two terms involving
\text{Im}\langle\partial_{\mu_{i}}\Psi\rvert H\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\dot{\theta}_{i}\text{Im}\langle\partial_{\theta_{i}}\Psi\rvert H\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}=\sum_{i}\sum_{j\leq i}(\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}})_{ji}^{-1}\cdot R_{\phi_{j}}\cdot I_{\theta_{i}},

(144)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\dot{\phi}_{i}\text{Im}\langle\partial_{\phi_{i}}\Psi\rvert H\lvert\Psi\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\bigg(\sum_{j=i}^{i+K-1}\frac{G^{c}_{\phi_{i},\phi_{j}}}{J^{2}}\frac{\Omega_{i}\tilde{h}_{s^{x}_{i}}+\Delta_{i}\tilde{h}_{s^{z}_{i}}}{1-\cos\theta_{i}}\frac{\Omega_{j}\tilde{h}_{s^{x}_{j}}+\Delta_{j}\tilde{h}_{s^{z}_{j}}}{1-\cos\theta_{j}}

\displaystyle-\sum_{j,l}(\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}})_{ij}^{-1}\cdot R_{\theta_{j}}\cdot\frac{G^{c}_{\phi_{i},\phi_{l}}(\Omega_{l}\tilde{h}_{s^{x}_{l}}+\Delta_{l}\tilde{h}_{s^{z}_{l}})}{J(1-\cos\theta_{l})}+\dot{\phi}_{i}\cdot I_{\phi_{i}}\bigg).

(145)

There are two terms involving the diagonal part of the Gram matrix

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\dot{\theta}_{i}G^{c}_{\theta_{i},\theta_{j}}\dot{\theta}_{j}=\frac{L}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\eta_{i}J}{2}\Big(\sum_{j\leq i}(\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}})_{ji}^{-1}R_{\phi_{j}}\Big)^{2},

(146)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\dot{\phi}_{i}G^{c}_{\phi_{i},\phi_{j}}\dot{\phi}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j=i}^{i+K-1}\Big(\frac{G^{c}_{\phi_{i},\phi_{j}}}{J^{2}}\frac{\Omega_{i}\tilde{h}_{s^{x}_{i}}+\Delta_{i}\tilde{h}_{s^{z}_{i}}}{1-\cos\theta_{i}}\frac{\Omega_{j}\tilde{h}_{s^{x}_{j}}+\Delta_{j}\tilde{h}_{s^{z}_{j}}}{1-\cos\theta_{j}}

\displaystyle-2\frac{\Omega_{i}\tilde{h}_{s^{x}_{i}}+\Delta_{i}\tilde{h}_{s^{z}_{i}}}{J(1-\cos\theta_{i})}G^{c}_{\phi_{i},\phi_{j}}\sum_{l}(\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}})_{jl}^{-1}\cdot R_{\theta_{l}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k}(\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}})_{ik}^{-1}\cdot R_{\theta_{k}}G^{c}_{\phi_{i},\phi_{j}}\sum_{l}(\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}})_{jl}^{-1}\cdot R_{\theta_{l}}\Big).

(147)

Combining these together, we obtain the quantum leakage

\displaystyle\Gamma^{2}

\displaystyle=2\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\Omega_{i}\Omega_{i+1}\eta_{i}x_{i}^{2}x_{i+1}x_{i+2}\tan\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}\tan\frac{\theta_{i+1}}{2}\cos(\phi_{i+1}-\phi_{i})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{\eta_{i}\Omega_{i}^{2}}{2J}\Big(1+(-1+2J)\cos^{2}\phi_{i}\sin^{2}\theta_{i}

\displaystyle+\frac{(-1-2J+(-1+2J)\cos\theta_{i})(\tilde{h}_{s^{x}_{i}})^{2}}{1-\cos\theta_{i}}

\displaystyle+x_{i}^{2}(-1+x_{i+1})(1+x_{i+1}+2(-1+2J)\cos 2\phi_{i}\tan^{2}\frac{\theta_{i}}{2})\Big)

\displaystyle-2\frac{1}{L}\sum_{i}\sum_{j\leq i}(\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}})_{ji}^{-1}\cdot R_{\phi_{j}}\cdot I_{\theta_{i}}

\displaystyle-2\sum_{i}\Big(\frac{\Omega_{i}\tilde{h}_{s^{x}_{i}}}{J(1-\cos\theta_{i})}\frac{1}{L}I_{\phi_{i}}-\sum_{j}(\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}})_{ij}^{-1}\cdot R_{\theta_{j}}\frac{1}{L}I_{\phi_{i}}\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\eta_{i}J}{2}\Big(\sum_{j}(\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}})_{ji}^{-1}R_{\phi_{j}}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{L}\sum_{ij}\sum_{k}(\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}})_{ik}^{-1}\cdot R_{\theta_{k}}\cdot G^{c}_{\phi_{i},\phi_{j}}\sum_{l}(\text{Im}G^{c}_{\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}})_{jl}^{-1}\cdot R_{\theta_{l}},

(148)

where we have use the fact that

\Delta

(149)

Substitute into the last term in Eq. (

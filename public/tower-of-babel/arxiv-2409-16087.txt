Exact Null Controllability of Non-Autonomous Conformable Fractional Semi-Linear Systems with Nonlocal Conditions
Dev Prakash Jha, Raju K. George

Abstract
We study the exact null controllability of a class of non-autonomous conformable fractional semi-linear evolution systems with nonlocal initial conditions in Hilbert spaces. The analysis is carried out within the framework of conformable fractional calculus and linear evolution operator theory. Under suitable assumptions, we establish the existence of mild solutions and provide sufficient conditions for exact null controllability. Notably, the nonlocal term is allowed to be continuous without requiring compactness or Lipschitz-type conditions. An example is included to illustrate the applicability of the main results.

Preliminaries

Definition 2.1
.

(Conformable Fractional Derivative
[
15
]
)
Let
f:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a real-valued function. The conformable fractional derivative of order
\alpha\in(0,1]
is defined by

T_{\alpha}(f)(t)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{f(t+\epsilon t^{1-\alpha})-f(t)}{\epsilon},\quad t>0.

(2)

If this limit exists, the function
f
is called
\alpha
-differentiable at
t
, and the derivative is denoted as
f^{(\alpha)}(t)=T_{\alpha}(f)(t)
. Furthermore, if
f
is
\alpha
-differentiable on an interval
(0,a)
for some
a>0
, and the limit
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}f^{(\alpha)}(t)
exists, then we define
f^{(\alpha)}(0)=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}f^{(\alpha)}(t)
.

Definition 2.2
.

(Left Conformable Fractional Derivative
[
16
]
)
Suppose
f:[a,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. The left conformable fractional derivative of
f
starting from the point
a
is defined as

T_{\alpha}^{a}(f)(t)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{f(t+\epsilon(t-a)^{1-\alpha})-f(t)}{\epsilon},\quad t>a.

(3)

This derivative is commonly written as
f^{(\alpha,a)}(t)
. In the case where
a=0
, we write
T_{\alpha}^{a}f
as
T_{\alpha}f
. If
f^{(\alpha,a)}(t)
exists on an interval
(a,b)
, then the derivative at the starting point is defined via the limit:
f^{(\alpha,a)}(a)=\lim_{t\to a^{+}}f^{(\alpha,a)}(t)
.

Definition 2.3
.

(Right Conformable Fractional Derivative
[
16
]
)
Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
and suppose
f
is defined on an interval ending at
b
. The
right conformable fractional derivative
of order
\alpha
at a point

is defined by

\left({}_{\alpha}^{b}\mathrm{T}f\right)(t)=-\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{f\left(t+\epsilon(b-t)^{1-\alpha}\right)-f(t)}{\epsilon},\quad t<b.

(4)

If this limit exists for every
t\in(a,b)
where
b>a
, then the derivative at
t=b
is defined as

\left({}_{\alpha}^{b}\mathrm{T}f\right)(b)=\lim_{t\to b^{-}}\left({}_{\alpha}^{b}\mathrm{T}f\right)(t).

Existence of Exact Null Controllability

3.1
Evolution Operator

Let
Z
denote a Hilbert space with norm
\|\cdot\|
. We consider a collection of linear operators
\{A(t):t_{0}\leq\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\leq t_{f}\}
that satisfy the following properties:

(a)

The domain
D_{A}
, which is common to all
A(t)
, is dense in
Z
, independent of
t
, and each
A(t)
is a closed operator.

(b)

For each
\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}
, the resolvent
R_{\alpha}(\lambda_{\alpha},A(t))
exists whenever
\text{Re}(\lambda_{\alpha})\leq 0
, and there exists a constant
K_{1}>0
such that

\|R_{\alpha}(\lambda_{\alpha},A(t))\|\leq\frac{K_{1}}{|\lambda_{\alpha}|+1}.

(c)

There exist constants
0<\delta\leq 1
and
K_{2}>0
such that for all
\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha},\frac{s^{\alpha}}{\alpha},\frac{\tau^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\in[t_{0},t_{f}]
, the inequality

\|(A(t)-A(s))A^{-1}(\tau)\|\leq K_{2}|t^{\alpha}-s^{\alpha}|^{\delta}

holds.

(d)

For any
\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\in[t_{0},t_{f}]
and
\lambda_{\alpha}\in\rho(A(t))
, where
\rho(A(t))
denotes the resolvent set of
A(t)
, the operator
R_{\alpha}(\lambda_{\alpha},A(t))
is compact.

Under the given assumptions, the operator family
\{A(t)\}
generates a fractional evolution operator
\Psi_{\alpha}(t,\tau)
, as introduced in
[
1
]
, and defined by

\Psi_{\alpha}(t,s)=e^{-\left(\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}-\frac{s^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\right)A(t)}+\int_{\frac{s^{\alpha}}{\alpha}}^{\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}}e^{-\left(\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}-\frac{\tau^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\right)A(\tau)}R(\tau,s)\,d(\tau,\alpha),

(7)

where
\exp\left(-\frac{\tau^{\alpha}}{\alpha}A(t)\right)
denotes the analytic semigroup generated by the operator
-A(t)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

[
1
]

The fractional evolution system
\{\Psi_{\alpha}(t,s):t_{0}\leq\frac{s^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\leq\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\leq t_{f}\}
satisfies the following properties:

(
A_{1}

For each

\Psi_{\alpha}(t,s)\in BL(Z)
, the space of bounded linear operators on

, and for every

is continuous.

(

)

The composition law holds:

\Psi_{\alpha}(t,s)\Psi_{\alpha}(s,\tau)=\Psi_{\alpha}(t,\tau),

for all
t_{0}\leq\frac{\tau^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\leq\frac{s^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\leq\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\leq t_{f}
.

Note 3.1
.

This property typically fails for conformable fractional derivatives with initial conditions of the form

, whether left- or right-sided.

(
A_{3}
)

The identity condition holds:
\Psi_{\alpha}(t,t)=I
, where

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\{\Psi_{\alpha}(t,s):t_{0}\leq\frac{s^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\leq\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\leq t_{f}\}
be a family of linear evolution operators. Then,
\Psi_{\alpha}(t,s)
is a compact operator for all
t^{\alpha}-s^{\alpha}>0
.

Proof.

Compactness of the evolution operator
\Psi_{\alpha}(t,s)
follows from condition (d), as established in Proposition 2.1 of
[
19
]
. Consequently, there exists a constant
M\geq 1
such that

\|\Psi_{\alpha}(t,s)\|\leq M,\quad\text{for all}\quad t_{0}\leq\frac{s^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\leq\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\leq t_{f}.

(8)

∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
0<\alpha\leq 1
. Suppose
t,\tau
satisfy
t^{\alpha}-\tau^{\alpha}>h
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|\Psi_{\alpha}(t+h,\tau)-\Psi_{\alpha}(t,\tau)\|\leq\frac{C}{t^{\alpha}-\tau^{\alpha}}\left[\frac{(t+ht^{1-\alpha})^{\alpha}-t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\right].

Proof.

The evolution operator
\Psi_{\alpha}(t,\tau)
is known to be strongly
\alpha
-conformably differentiable with respect to
t
, and it satisfies

T_{\alpha}\big(\Psi_{\alpha}(t,s)\big)(t)=-A(t)\Psi_{\alpha}(t,s),

as established by Jha
[
1
]
and Pazy
[
20
, p. 158]
.

Consequently, we estimate:

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi_{\alpha}(t+ht^{1-\alpha},\tau)-\Psi_{\alpha}(t,\tau)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{t}^{t+ht^{1-\alpha}}T_{\alpha}\big(\Psi_{\alpha}(s,\tau)\big)(s)\,d(s,\alpha)\right\|

(9)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t}^{t+ht^{1-\alpha}}\left\|T_{\alpha}\big(\Psi_{\alpha}(s,\tau)\big)(s)\right\|\,d(s,\alpha)

(10)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t}^{t+ht^{1-\alpha}}\frac{C}{s^{\alpha}-\tau^{\alpha}}\,d(s,\alpha).

(11)

Since
s\in[t,t+ht^{1-\alpha}]
and
t^{\alpha}-\tau^{\alpha}>h
, it follows that
s^{\alpha}-\tau^{\alpha}\geq t^{\alpha}-\tau^{\alpha}
. Therefore:

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{t+ht^{1-\alpha}}\frac{C}{s^{\alpha}-\tau^{\alpha}}\,d(s,\alpha)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{t^{\alpha}-\tau^{\alpha}}\int_{t}^{t+ht^{1-\alpha}}d(s,\alpha)

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{t^{\alpha}-\tau^{\alpha}}\left[\frac{(t+ht^{1-\alpha})^{\alpha}-t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\right].

Thus, the desired estimate follows:

\|\Psi_{\alpha}(t+h,\tau)-\Psi_{\alpha}(t,\tau)\|\leq\frac{C}{t^{\alpha}-\tau^{\alpha}}\left[\frac{(t+ht^{1-\alpha})^{\alpha}-t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\right].

∎

Moreover, based on Theorem
3.3
, we establish the following result, which will serve as a key component in the analysis presented in the next section.

Lemma 3.4
.

The family of operators
\{\Psi_{\alpha}(t,s):\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}>\frac{s^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\geq t_{0}\}
is continuous with respect to
t
, uniformly in

\begin{cases}T_{\alpha}z(t)+A(t)z(t)=Bu(t)+h(t),&t\in[t_{1},t_{2}],\quad\alpha\in(0,1],\\

3.2
Existence of mild solutions

This subsection investigates the precise null controllability issue related to equation (
P
). We first introduce the concept of a mild solution and then define its exact null controllability.

Definition 3.2
.

A function
x\in\mathbb{C}([\zeta,t_{2}];Z)
is considered a mild solution to problem (

) if it satisfies the following equation:

x(t)=\Psi_{\alpha}(t,\zeta)[x_{0}-g(x)]+\int_{\zeta}^{t}\Psi_{\alpha}(t,s)[Bu(s)+F(s,x(b(s)))]\frac{ds}{s^{1-\alpha}},\quad t\in[\zeta,t_{2}],

(15)

where
u(\cdot)\in L^{2}([\zeta,t_{2}];U)
is the control function.

Definition 3.3
.

A system (
P

u\in L^{2}([\zeta,t_{2}];U)
such that, under this control,
x(t_{2},u)=0

We now impose the following conditions on the semi-linear system (

(H_{1})

Let
t\in[\zeta,t_{2}]

\liminf_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\|h_{r}(t)\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\xi}}}}{r}=\gamma<\infty,

(

The function

\|g(x)\|\leq\varphi_{r},\quad\text{and}\quad\liminf_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\varphi_{r}}{r}=L<+\infty.

The delay function

(H_{4})

The linear system (

in

\psi_{r}=\sup\{h_{r}(\tau):\lVert\tau\rVert\leq r\},\quad\textit{then}\quad\liminf_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\|\psi_{r}\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\xi}}}}{r}=\gamma,

N=\left\{\bigg((t_{2})^{\frac{\alpha-\xi}{1-\xi}}-(\zeta)^{\frac{\alpha-\xi}{1-\xi}}\bigg)^{1-\xi}\bigg(\frac{1-\xi}{\alpha-\xi}\bigg)^{1-\xi}:0\leq\xi<\min{\{\alpha,\delta\}}\right\}.

Suppose the assumptions

Part 1:

Let

Next, we verify that the operator

\Psi_{\alpha}(t,s)=S_{s}(t-s)\exp\left(-\int_{\frac{s^{\alpha}}{\alpha}}^{\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}}p(\tau)\,d(\tau,\alpha)\right),

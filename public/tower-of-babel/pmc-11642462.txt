IL-17 Monoclonal Antibody Related HPV Exacerbation: A Case Report
Authors: Fahrettin Küçükhemek, Yağmur Aypek, Betül Öğüt, Esra Adışen
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

In today’s medicine, targeted therapies are increasingly included in daily practice in dermatology and other disciplines. Although these treatments seem safer than traditional treatments, it should not be overlooked that they also cause some negative consequences. Among these agents, there are publications that the incidence of human papillomavirus (HPV) infection may decrease in patients using the Interleukin-17 (IL-17) monoclonal antibody, secukinumab.[1] Some studies have suggested secukinumab as a cutaneous HPV infection treatment modality.[2] Despite all this, we wanted to emphasize the need for more extensive studies on this subject by presenting a patient with HPV exacerbation observed after the use of secukinumab, contrary to the literature.

A 57 year-old male patient presented with the complaint of a mass in the left inguinal region. In the dermatological examination of the patient, bilateral verrucous lesions, much more common in the left inguinal region, were observed [Figure 1]. It was learned from the patient’s history that he was being treated for rheumatoid arthritis and started to use secukinumab after 1 year of methotreaxate use. These complaints started in the left inguinal region and spread to the right inguinal region approximately 2 weeks after the first dose of secukinumab. In addition, it was learned that he had a history of suspicious sexual intercourse, and there was no regression in the lesions, despite a total of 15 sessions of cryotherapy applied every 3 weeks. As a result of the patient’s history and dermatological examination, HPV infection was considered, and a biopsy was planned. The biopsy result was low-grade carcinoma in situ [Figure 2]. HPV typing could not be performed within the capabilities of our center. The patient was started on topical imiquimod treatment and it was recommended to interrupt secukinumab treatment at the same time. Because the patient’s joint complaints increased on the 30th day of treatment, it was recommended to continue secukinumab treatment. The patient was seen on the 10th, 17th, 24th, 30th, and 37th days and regression was observed in the lesions. At 6-month follow-up, no improvement was observed in the remaining residual lesions after secukinumab treatment was restarted.

IL-17 is a proinflammatory cytokine associated with viral clearance and maintenance of tissue integrity. However, the dysregulated level of IL-17 causes hyperinflammation leading to different inflammatory disorders and malignancy.[3] IL-17 secreted from tumor cells has been shown to increase tumor proliferation and angiogenesis.[4] Secukinumab specifically targets IL-17A, thereby blocking its binding with IL-17R and expression of cytokines. This blockade normalizes inflammatory processes and thereby inhibits epidermal hyperproliferation, T-cell infiltration, and overexpression of pathogenic genes.[5] Although initially contradictory, IL-17 increases both T-cell-mediated malignant cell rejection and malignant cell invasion. However, there is no evidence put forward in this regard.[6] Although low IL-17 level was demonstrated in patients with cutaneous warts[7] and IL-17 blockade was found to be effective in preventing high-risk HPV infections in terms of malignant transformation in previous studies,[8] exacerbation was observed after this blockade and regression was observed in the lesions after discontinuation of the drug is highly compatible with this contradiction in the literature. At the same time, no exacerbation of the lesions was observed in this case despite restarting secukinumab treatment, suggesting that imiquimod treatment is an effective option in cryotherapy-resistant cases. However, several studies have suggested that especially malignancy-associated HPV subtypes may be more prone to regression, while other subtypes may tend to exacerbate.[3] To the best of our knowledge, this is the first case of exacerbation of HPV infection after secukinumab in the literature. In conclusion, it is a fact that more comprehensive studies are needed to fully reveal the relationship between HPV infections and the use of secukinumab and to reveal the underlying mechanisms.

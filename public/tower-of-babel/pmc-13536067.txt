Adaptation and feasibility of a group intervention for parents after the loss of a co-parent to cancer: a mixed-methods study protocol
Authors: Sophie Faber, Lene Johannsen, Wiebke Geertz, Heidi Müller, Ruthmarijke Smeding, Irwin N Sandler, Laura Inhestern
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroduction and objectivesAfter the loss of a parent, families with minor children face psychosocial stressors. The bereaved parent has to deal with their own grief and at the same time support the children. Providing professional support to the parents can improve well-being of parents, children and the family as a whole. However, such manualised interventions are missing for the German context. This study aims to translate and culturally adapt the parent group ‘Resilient Parenting for Bereaved Families’ (RPBF) of the Family Bereavement Program for the German context specifically for loss due to cancer, and to pilot evaluate its feasibility and potential effects.Methods and analysisIn this mixed-methods pilot study, six to eight group leaders, who are experienced in working in bereavement support, will be trained to conduct the parent group and afterwards lead parent groups. We aim to conduct three to four parent groups with 6–8 parents each (total target sample size n=24–32). Parents participate in 10 biweekly parent group sessions on-site or online. We will assess feasibility, acceptability, satisfaction and preliminary effects on parental well-being, family coping and communication (baseline (t0), post-intervention (t1) and 12-week post-intervention follow-up (t2)). Semistructured interviews with 10–12 parents will further explore experience and suggestions for refinement. Quantitative data will be analysed using descriptive and exploratory analyses and qualitative data will be analysed using qualitative content analysis.Ethics and disseminationThe study was approved by the Local Psychological Ethics Committee of the Center for Psychosocial Medicine of the University Medical Center Hamburg-Eppendorf (LPEK-0919). Upon completion, a German version of the Resilient Parenting for Bereaved Families (RPBF) parent group and the group leader training will be available. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and conference presentations. To make the results available to affected families, a lay summary will be written and disseminated in several ways.Trial registration numberThe study was registered with the German Clinical Trials Register (DRKS00039060 (date of registration: 19 February 2026))

Introduction

In Germany, cancer is the second most common cause of death.1 Each year, approximately 37 000 parents of minor children are newly diagnosed with cancer and about one in three patients die within several years of diagnosis, depending on cancer type and stage.2 Around 12 000 cancer patients aged 20–54—the age range encompassing most parents of minor children—die annually in Germany.3 For many children and adolescents, this may result in the loss of a parent, and for their partners, the loss of a co-parent.

The death of a parent is a highly stressful life event and affects both the bereaved parent and the children. It disrupts the whole family system and imposes distinct individual burdens on each family member. Experiencing the death of a parent during childhood can negatively impact health in adulthood. For example, there may be an increased risk of developing physical and mental health issues, such as symptoms of depression or anxiety.4–8 In addition, negative effects on school performance have been observed, including higher school dropout rates.9 10

After losing their partner or co-parent to death, bereaved parents have to deal with their own grief, adapt to their new role as a single parent and support their children.11 12 Even 2–4 years after their partner’s death, surviving parents with minor children reported moderate to severe symptom burden, such as depression, anxiety and post-traumatic stress.13 Bereaved parents may struggle with intense emotions and often find it difficult to talk openly about the loss, which can contribute to communication difficulties within the family.12

Open communication and interaction between the surviving parent and the child can help the child to cope positively and is associated with fewer psychological problems.14 Parents need to be attentive to the children’s needs and they can support open and respectful communication.15 16 This is supported by findings that suggest that the interaction between bereaved parents and their children and the parents’ coping strategies can impact the children’s mental health.15 17 Having a close relationship with the bereaved surviving parent and experiencing a supportive parenting style can act as protective factors, mitigating the risk of developing long-term consequences in the children.18 In this context, it is crucial to strengthen the resilience of bereaved parents and equip them with necessary resources to support their children in coping with their loss.19

A systematic review of the effects of support programmes for parentally bereaved children and their caregivers has shown that families can benefit from support programmes by preventing children from developing traumatic grief and by improving the mental health of bereaved parents and children.20

There is a broad landscape of grief services in Germany, but bereaved individuals may find it challenging to navigate the available offers.21 At the same time, only a few services explicitly focus on families or follow a structured, theory-based approach, indicating a potential gap in the evidence-based structure of grief support in the German-speaking context.22 And to our knowledge, there is no existing parenting group that focuses on strengthening the parenting skills in the German context.

The Family Bereavement Program (FBP23) is an evidence-based approach to promote resilience of parentally bereaved families. The FBP consists of separate group-based interventions for bereaved parents and their children. The parent group ‘Resilient Parenting’ aims to empower bereaved parents with minor children in their parenting role and to support parents to be emotionally available in interacting with their children. Evaluation results indicate positive effects of the programme on the mental health of parents’ and children’s mental health in the long term24 25 by strengthening the parent-child interactions and the family bonds. The parent group has been adapted and implemented in Sweden, illustrating the evidence supporting its feasibility and positive effects.26 The aim of this study is to translate and culturally adapt the original ‘Resilient Parenting’ group intervention for cancer-bereaved parents from the FBP and its group leader training for the German context, and to conduct a pilot evaluation of its feasibility, acceptability and indications of preliminary effectiveness on parents’ and children’s mental health outcomes. The parent group as one component of the FBP was selected since parents are considered as the key figures for their children and may influence the adjustment of their children following bereavement.27 Thereby, the children are targeted indirectly with the parent group. Limiting the programme adaptation to the parent group also reduces complexity and allows a focused assessment of acceptability and feasibility. In contrast to the original intervention, the parent group intervention for the German application exclusively targets families who have lost a parent to cancer to reduce complexity and enhance shared experiences of participants.

In line with the framework proposed by Bond et al,28 the present study is conceptualised as a pilot study within the broader spectrum of feasibility studies, designed to address key uncertainties regarding the intervention’s feasibility, acceptability and deliverability of the intervention in the target population.

The study’s objectives are as follows:

To translate and culturally adapt the FBP parent group intervention and the group leader training materials for the German context.

Methods and analyses

The project is funded by the German Cancer Aid (Deutsche Krebshilfe) for 36 months, from March 2025 to March 2028. At the time of the manuscript submission, the research team is adapting and translating the RPBF parent group and the group leader training as part of phase I of the project. The parent group intervention will begin at the start of 2027 until the beginning of 2028. The pilot study protocol is written in accordance with the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) guideline29 and the SPIRIT-PRO Extension (Guidelines for Inclusion of Patient-Reported Outcomes in Clinical Trial Protocols),30 and reports on applicable items relevant to non-randomised pilot and feasibility trials.

Study setting

This study will be conducted and coordinated at the Department of Medical Psychology of the University Medical Centre Hamburg-Eppendorf. The translation and adaptation of the group leader training and the parent group will take place in Hamburg. The group leader training and the parent group intervention will be conducted at the collaboration partners’ facilities or online. All study-related documentation, data processing and oversight will remain with the central study site at the Department of Medical Psychology.

Study design

The study will be conducted in accordance with the Medical Research Council (MRC) framework for the development and evaluation of complex interventions, which provides a structured approach to guide systematic development, feasibility testing and future evaluation of complex health interventions.31 Using the MRC framework, this study is designed as a phase I and phase II study, including the adaptation (phase I) and pilot evaluation of feasibility (phase II) of the parent group and the group leader training.

Phase I: translation and adaptation of the parent group and the group leader training materials

In phase I, the material for the parent group (eg, power point presentations for each session, workbooks, homework sheets) will be translated, and the content will be culturally adapted and didactically prepared by the project team with a structured review process conducted within the project team and in consultation with the Advisory Board and Parent Advisory Board. The material for the group leader training (power point presentations, feedback questionnaires) will be translated and prepared based on the parent group materials. Next, a website will be created to provide information, resources and exercises for use during the training of the parents participating in the group intervention. The structured translation and adaptation process will be closely accompanied by feedback from the Advisory Board (the original author: Prof. Dr. IS and experts in the field of grief: Dr. RS, Dr. HM) and the Parent Advisory Board, consisting of three parents with minor children who experienced the loss of a co-parent to cancer more than half a year prior to participation.

Phase IIa: pilot evaluation of the group leader training

In phase II, the group leaders will be trained on the basis of the translated material. A pilot evaluation will be carried out using feedback questionnaires, assessing satisfaction with the training, the feasibility of implementing the intervention, and changes in knowledge and skills related to conducting the group, measured before and after the training. A follow-up survey will also be conducted with the trained group leaders after they have led a group.

Phase IIb: pilot evaluation of the parent group intervention

The study is designed as a non-randomised, single-group pilot trial using both quantitative and qualitative methods to address the research questions. In line with the conceptual framework proposed by Bond et al,28 the pilot study will focus primarily on feasibility parameters, including recruitment, retention, acceptability and procedural aspects of intervention delivery (number and completeness of exercises conducted, use of intervention materials, adherence to the session structure). Quantitative data from participating parents will be collected at three time points: baseline (t0), immediately post-intervention (t1) and follow-up at 12 weeks after completion of the intervention (t2; table 1). Following completion of the group intervention, parents will be invited to participate in semistructured qualitative interviews. These interviews will explore their experiences and perceptions of the feasibility of participating in such parent group interventions. The subsample will consist of parents willing to take part in these interviews.

Study population: eligibility criteria

Professionals

Professionals with a psychological or comparable background and practical experience in grief counselling or oncology are eligible for the group leader training. Moreover, it is essential that participating professionals agree to lead a parent group in the context of this study.

Parents

Eligibility criteria for inclusion of parents for the Parent Advisory Board and the parent group are largely based on the inclusion criteria of the original intervention, defined as follows: death of the co-parent due to cancer, time since loss is between 4 and 30 months (at the time of parent group starts), at least one child under the age of 18 lives in the family and the bereaved parent has sufficient knowledge of the German language to follow the group sessions and evaluation material. Exclusion criteria are suicidal thoughts or plans or indications of a severe mental disorder. To assess suitability of interested parents, an initial consultation will be held with all interested parents by a psychologist of the research team, including a preliminary clinical interview with a standardised procedure and a screening tool to identify potential mental disorders. If suicidal thoughts or plans (child or parent) are expressed in the initial consultation before participation in the parents’ group and/or if there are indications of a severe mental disorder in need of treatment, the families are referred to psychiatric or psychotherapeutic support services.

Recruitment

Professionals trained as group leaders must be affiliated with a relevant institution (eg, grief counselling or psycho-oncological counselling services). Parents will be recruited through the networks of these institutions and the group leaders. In addition, parents may proactively contact the study team to express interest in study participation. Potentially eligible parents will receive detailed information about the study from the group leaders and the study team. Parents interested in participating will be invited to an introductory appointment, during which the study procedures will be explained in detail and eligibility will be assessed according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria. Written informed consent will be obtained from all eligible parents prior to study participation. Recruitment will follow a consecutive sampling strategy. Group sessions will commence either once the required number of participants has been recruited or at predefined time points. The final recruitment and group allocation procedures will be determined depending on the organisational processes of the institutions of the participating group leaders.

Sample size

Following methodological recommendations for pilot studies, which suggest total sample sizes in the range of approximately 20–30 participants when the main aim is to assess feasibility and obtain preliminary estimates of variances and effect sizes,32 we plan a total sample size of 24 parents (maximum n=32). In line with the exploratory nature of the study, no formal hypothesis testing of intervention effects will be conducted. Instead, the study will provide initial indications of potential effect that may inform sample size planning for a future randomised controlled trial. During the study period three to four parent groups will be conducted. The group size will be six to eight parents, although final group sizes may be adapted during recruitment and could range up to 8 to 10 participants per group. For the qualitative component of the pilot evaluation, the target sample size is n=10–12 participants.

Compensation for participation

Group leaders will receive a €50 compensation for the documentation of each conducted parent group session. Parents will receive a €30 compensation as a gift voucher for completing the questionnaires at all three measurement points and additionally a €20 gift voucher if they participate in the follow-up qualitative interview. The members of the Parent Advisory Board receive €50 for each attended meeting.

Intervention

The manualised intervention developed by IS and his team at Arizona State University23 will be translated and culturally adapted for the German context. The content of the parent group sessions focuses on strengthening parental skills in coping with their own feelings, interacting with their children, and in their role as single parents. In addition to the parent group sessions, a website will provide information and resources for the participating parents, including exercises.

Structure of the sessions

The original parent group interventions consist of 10 2-hour sessions. Sessions are based on a psychoeducational approach incorporating elements of self-awareness and skilling exercises. The parent group will meet every 2 weeks. The sessions will take place on site or online. The sessions begin with a mindfulness exercise, followed by sharing the experiences from the activities at home. The main part of the sessions are the group activities (such as self-care and family-care activities) with psycho-educational input to introduce each topic. The group activities and exercises build up on each other. Parents are given exercises to practice at home, either individually or with their children, with the aim of helping parents to become more experienced in the exercises. Intervention discontinuation

Participants may withdraw from the parent group or the study at any time without providing a reason and without any consequences. No modifications of the intervention are planned, as all participants receive the same intervention content. The study team may discontinue participation if continuing involvement raises safety concerns or significant psychological distress for participating parents.

Outcomes

This pilot study focuses on testing the feasibility and acceptability of the translated and adapted parent group and its group leader training. The outcomes have been discussed within the Hamburg study team (SF, LJ, WG, LI). Furthermore, preliminary effectiveness of the parent group will be assessed using a paper-pencil questionnaire at three measuring time points from all enrolled participants: before the intervention (baseline, t0), immediately after the intervention (t1) and a 12-week post-intervention follow-up (t2) as shown in table 1. Additionally, a brief documentation by the group leaders and a brief feedback questionnaire will be applied after each session. Participants who discontinue the intervention will be invited to complete all remaining assessment time points whenever possible.

Feasibility, acceptability and satisfaction

Implementation feasibility will be assessed following the procedure described by Sandler et al.33 Group leaders will rate the completion of each group activity on a three-point scale (2 = Complete, 1 = Partially complete, 0 = Didn’t do) and will evaluate the quality of implementation for each session (‘How did it go?’; 1=very well, 0= not very well). Additionally, recruitment and enrollment rates, session attendance, completion of between-session tasks (‘homework’), retention and drop-out, and reasons for (non-)participation among invited parents (parental self-report) will be used. Acceptability of the parent group will be rated by the participating parents through a 14-item scale (e.g., ‘People in this group understand my experiences’) developed by Sandler et al;33 in addition, they provide open-ended feedback on the group and website materials and self-reported barriers and facilitators for participation. To provide additional information on engagement with the intervention materials, website usage is tracked based on the number of self-reported visits and the duration of each visit. Parents’ satisfaction will be assessed using a 5-point Likert scale (1= ‘not at all helpful’ to 5 = ‘very helpful’) measuring the perceived programme helpfulness with three items (eg, ‘Overall, how helpful has the program been for you?’).33 To gain more comprehensive understanding on parental acceptance, feasibility and satisfaction, semistructured interviews will be conducted with a subset of participating parents. The interview guide is provided in online supplemental appendix A. In addition, participants will be asked to provide suggestions for improvements as well as ideas regarding the implementation and realisation of a potential follow-up study.

To assess instrument suitability in the pilot, data quality, missing values, floor and ceiling effects, and sensitivity to change will be examined; German versions of required questionnaires will be translated and piloted as necessary.

Preliminary effectiveness of the parent group

To test the preliminary effectiveness of the parent group intervention, questionnaires will be handed out to and collected from all participating parents at three measuring time points. Outcomes (table 1) include parental and child mental well-being, assessed using standardised self-report instruments. Parental symptoms of depression, anxiety and prolonged grief will be measured using the Patient Health Questionnaire,34 the Generalized Anxiety Disorder scale,35 and the Prolonged Grief Scale.36 Children’s mental well-being and emotion regulation will be assessed by parent proxy report using the Strength and Difficulties Questionnaire37 and the Questionnaire for the Assessment of Emotion Regulation in Children and Adolescents.38

Additional measures will assess parental coping and self-compassion, including the Work and Social Adjustment Scale39 (in the German version40), the Coping Self-Efficacy Scale,41 the General Coping Efficacy Scale42 and the Self-compassion Scale – short form43 based on44 in the German version.45 Family functioning will be assessed using the Family Assessment Device.46 Instruments without validated German versions will be translated. The entire study instrument will be reviewed with parents using cognitive interviews to assess clarity and comprehensibility of the provided materials for the group intervention and for the website; revisions will be made as necessary.

Data management and monitoring

The research team will continuously document and coordinate data collection at the different measurement points. Research assistants will enter the questionnaire data into an IBM SPSS, V.29.0.147 database. Access to the data is restricted to members of the research team only. Any adverse events will be monitored and documented. Examples of adverse events may include, for example, emotional or psychological distress, heightened anxiety or depressive symptoms, interpersonal difficulties during group sessions, or signs of acute crisis. Potential adjustments to the study process will then be discussed within the research team.

Analyses

Quantitative data analysis

Quantitative data will be analysed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, V.29.0.1.47 Descriptive statistics will be used to analyse the parameters regarding feasibility and acceptance. Mean values and SD will be used for metric variables, frequency distributions for categorical data. Exploratory group comparisons will be conducted, for example, according to parent gender or child age group. Depending on the scale and distribution of the data, either parametric methods (eg, independent-samples t-test) or non-parametric methods (eg, Mann-Whitney U-test) will be applied. Missing data will not be imputed beyond the recommendation by the instrument manuals. If parent groups are conducted online, exploratory comparisons between online and face-to-face groups will also be carried out. The recruitment feasibility will be assessed descriptively by determining families’ hypothetical willingness to participate in randomisation. Measurement instruments will be checked for missing values, floor or ceiling effects, and sensitivity to change. Usage behaviour of the supplementary password-protected website will also be evaluated descriptively.

Qualitative data analysis

Qualitative data will be analysed using qualitative content analysis. Open-ended questions (eg, regarding barriers, facilitating factors and feedback) will be structured and summarised thematically. The results of the qualitative follow-up surveys as well as the qualitative interviews will be analysed based on Kuckartz’s qualitative content analysis approach.48 Initial coding will be conducted by SF. A second independent researcher (WG) will recode and check approximately 20% of the coding process. Differences will be discussed within the research team until consensus is reached.

Patient and public involvement statement

This study will involve bereaved parents to ensure that the adaptation of the intervention addresses the needs of the target population. Therefore, a Parent Advisory Board will be established, consisting of three bereaved parents who lost a co-parent to cancer and have at least one minor child. The Board advises the research team in half-yearly meetings on the wording and presentation of study materials, including questionnaires, interview guides, and website content. The board also provides feedback on the study design, interim findings and planned actions, and has assessed the practicality and perceived burden of participation (eg, timing and organisation of parent group sessions). They will also contribute to the dissemination strategy. In this pilot phase, parent perspectives are explicitly incorporated into both the quantitative evaluation (parent-reported outcomes and structured feedback) and the qualitative component. Other relevant stakeholders, such as group leaders and Advisory Board Members, are also consulted.

Ethics and dissemination

This study received ethical approval from the Local Psychological Ethics Committee at the Center of Psychosocial Medicine of the University Medical Center Hamburg-Eppendorf (LPEK-0919). Prior to participation, informed consent will be obtained from all group leaders and parents, who will be informed that they are taking part in a pilot study of a new programme. Participants will be asked to provide optional consent for the future use of anonymised study data for related research projects. Procedures will be in place to monitor any crises during the study, and participants will be provided with information on available support services. Written consent was obtained from the University of Arizona to translate and adapt the Family Bereavement Program for this study.

Confidentiality

All data collected during the parent group intervention will be pseudonymised to ensure data protection, and access will be restricted. A code list linking personal identifying information to unique study identification codes will be used solely to enable correct data linkage across multiple time points during data collection. Access to the code list is restricted to an authorised study team member with no involvement in data analysis or research interest in the study. The code list will be permanently destroyed after completion of data collection. Audio recordings will be used exclusively for research purposes, transcribed in pseudonymised form and deleted after completion of the analysis. All study data will be stored securely and used solely for scientific purposes at the Center of Psychosocial Medicine of the University Center Hamburg-Eppendorf. Results will be reported only in aggregated or fully de-identified form, ensuring that no individual participant can be identified.

Availability of data and material

The raw dataset will be accessible exclusively to the research team. Due to data protection regulations, public sharing is not permitted. Interested parties may request access to the data by submitting a justified inquiry, subject to approval by the Local Psychological Ethics Committee (LPEK) and the Data Protection Officer at the University Medical Center Hamburg-Eppendorf.

Dissemination

The findings of this study will be disseminated through presentations at national and international scientific conferences and publications in peer-reviewed journals. In addition, a lay summary will be made available on the research group’s homepage and shared with relevant organisations to facilitate accessibility and ensure that the results reach affected families.

Discussion

This pilot study will assess the feasibility and acceptability of a group intervention for cancer-bereaved parents with minor children. Furthermore, outcomes related to caregivers’ and children’s mental health, coping and parenting quality will be assessed. Every year, a considerable number of families in Germany experience the loss of a parent or co-parent to cancer. Evidence suggests that when parents are supported in recognising the benefits of sharing their grief and maintaining open communication with their children, they are better able to provide mutual support within the family.12 The evidence supports the providing of a psychoeducation, including communication-based intervention for bereaved families that focuses on self-care and family care. By translating, adapting and piloting the RPBF parent group and group leader training in a German-speaking context, the study seeks to expand the available support for families affected by parental loss due to cancer.

A major strength of this study is that it adapts a theory-driven intervention that has been rigorously evaluated and demonstrated long-term effectiveness.24 49 Another strength is the involvement of both an expert-based Advisory Board and Parent Advisory Board comprised of parents with lived experience in this study. At the same time, several limitations must be acknowledged. Recruitment may be affected by selection bias, as parents who are more informed, motivated or better resourced (eg, those with flexible schedules or reliable childcare) may be more likely to participate, whereas more vulnerable parents—such as those facing financial strain or lacking support networks—may remain under-represented, even when the programme is offered free of charge. Practical barriers such as travel distance, time constraints and childcare responsibilities may reduce accessibility and potentially limit generalisability, despite an online offering. The reliance on self-report measures may introduce response and social desirability biases, particularly when assessing sensitive topics such as parental distress, perceived competence and family communication. The data on feasibility, acceptability and preliminary effectiveness may serve as a foundation for designing a subsequent randomised controlled trial, including power calculations and optimisation of study procedures.

The intervention has the potential for a sustainable implementation since the adapted and manualised training programme and parent group intervention could be implemented in the already existing bereavement care structures in Germany. Since group leaders will be experienced in the field of psycho-oncology or grief support, the intervention could be delivered by using the manual after the training. The present study focuses on adapting and pilot testing the parent group of the FBP for the German-speaking context and cancer-bereaved parents for expanding the grief and parenting services. This can be understood as the first step in testing whether an adaptation of the FBP, including the children’s group and a broader target group regardless of the cause of bereavement (eg, accident, other illness, suicide), could be conducted.

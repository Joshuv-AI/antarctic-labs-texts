Involvement of CgPGC-1α in regulating mitochondrial biogenesis in Pacific oyster Crassostrea gigas under ocean acidification
Authors: Dongli Jiang, Hongbo Lu, Zhe Zhang, Chuanyan Yang, Chang Liu, Lingling Wang, Linsheng Song
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•CgPGC-1α with classical RRM domain was identified from Crassostrea gigas.•CgPGC-1α mRNA was ubiquitously expressed with the highest level in gill.•CgPGC-1α could respond to acidification treatment, promote mitochondrial biogenesis.•Acidification enhanced mitochondrial biogenesis and ATP via AMPKα/PGC-1α.Keywords: Crassostrea gigas, PGC-1α, Mitochondrial numbers, AMPKα, Ocean acidification

Introduction

The peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma coactivator-1 (PGC-1) is a member of the transcriptional coactivator family, and plays a crucial role in regulating energy metabolism in response to temperature, oxidation and exercise in fish and mammal [[1], [2], [3]]. The mammalian PGC-1 encompasses three members, including PGC-1α, PGC-1β, and PGC-1-related coactivator (PRC). Among them, PGC-1α is well established as a pivotal regulator of energy metabolism [4,5], playing a critical role in processes such as mitochondrial biogenesis, lipid metabolism, and glucose metabolism.

The mammals PGC-1α is consisted of multiple functional domains with distinct roles, and usually functions as a master regulator of mitochondrial function and oxidative metabolism [4,5]. The N-terminal region of PGC-1α contains a transcriptional activation domain. The activation domain primarily modulates the transcriptional activity of PGC-1α by recruiting histone acetyltransferases [6]. Furthermore, an LXXLL motif is present in the activation domain, which is capable of binding to PPARs, estrogen-related receptors (ERRs), and other nuclear receptors [7]. The C-terminal region contains an RNA-binding motif (RRM) domain and a short serine/arginine-rich stretches (SR) domain, which play a critical role in RNA splicing [8]. Additionally, the DHDY motif can facilitate the recruitment of host cell factors to enhance the transcriptional activity of PGC-1α [9]. Numerous studies have shown that the expression and activity of PGC-1α are modulated through both transcriptional and post-translational modifications in response to diverse external stimulus [5], among which AMPK is one of the most important upstream regulatory proteins of PGC-1α. AMPK regulates mitochondrial biogenesis and function through dual mechanisms: (1) modulating PGC-1α transcriptional expression via promoter methylation, and (2) controlling its activity through phosphorylation [[10], [11], [12]]. AMPK activation under cold stimulation induces PGC-1α transcription to initiate mitochondrial biogenesis [13,14]. This process continues with the activation of nuclear respiratory factors 1 and 2 (NRF-1/2), followed by TFAM, which together induce mtDNA transcription and replication [15].

Compared to in-depth research in mammals, comprehensive characterization including structure, expression, and physiological functions of PGC-1α in aquatic animals is still limited, mainly focusing on the expression profile under various conditions. For example, cold stress significantly elevated transcriptional levels of PGC-1α contributing to energy homeostasis in Indian medaka (Oryzias dancena) [16]. Dietary leucine supplementation could significantly elevate PGC-1α mRNA expression and ameliorate mitochondrial dysfunction in zebrafish [17]. Phenylalanine could enhance mitochondrial capacity and hypoxia tolerance through activation of the LKB1/AMPK/PGC-1α pathway under hypoxic stress [18]. Genetic models further reveal the role of PGC-1α in regulating early embryonic development and mitochondrial homeostasis. Knockdown of PGC-1α via siRNA in larvae perturbs the expression of mitochondrial-related genes (CS, NRF1 and TFAM), underscoring its regulatory significance in early development [19]. The PGC-1α mutation induced by the CRISPR-Cas9 technology led to a significant increase in the mRNA expression of this gene in skeletal muscle, which subsequently enhanced mitochondrial oxidative capacity and elevated the basal metabolic rate [20]. These findings underscore the essential role of PGC-1α in mediating environmental stress responses and maintaining mitochondrial function across diverse aquatic species. However, the functional characterization and regulatory mechanisms of PGC-1α in mollusks under ocean acidification remain unclear.

Pacific oysters Crassostrea gigas is an important aquaculture species worldwide. However, their survival and development are facing serious threats due to increasingly severe ocean acidification (with seawater pH projected to drop to 7.8 by 2100) [21,22]. Currently, studies of ocean acidification in mollusks primarily focus on their effects in regulating biomineralization and energy metabolism [[23], [24], [25], [26]]. The gonads, as the reproductive organs of oysters, play roles such as population continuation, species adaptability and sex determination. The development of the gonads is highly susceptible to fluctuations in environmental factors and is at risk due to ocean acidification. The giant oyster may maintain the survival of the population by altering the development speed of the gonads or the quality of gametes [[27], [28], [29]]. When organisms are subjected to acidification stress, they need to consume more energy to maintain the balance of their internal pH levels, which may squeeze the energy budget available for reproduction [30]. Therefore, studying the regulatory mechanisms of energy metabolism in gonads can help us understand the energy allocation of the oyster during the breeding stage. Mitochondria are the energy factories within cells. When facing different environmental stresses, mitochondria achieve energy distribution through a series of precise regulatory mechanisms [31]. However, the specific mechanisms through which acidification affects function of mitochondria, the powerhouses of the cell, remain poorly understood. In this study, we hypothesized that ocean acidification modulates CgPGC-1α-associated mitochondrial regulation in C. gigas, resulting in dynamic changes in mitochondrial number and quantity, and cellular energy homeostasis during stress exposure. To test this hypothesis, we characterized the molecular features and expression patterns of CgPGC-1α and investigated its potential involvement in mitochondrial responses under acidification conditions.

Materials and methods

Animals

Oyster C. gigas (wet weights approximately 126.10 ± 2.78 g, lengths approximately 111.90 ± 1.37 mm, widths approximately 59.89 ± 0.57 mm, and heights approximately 37.34 ± 0.77 mm) were obtained from a local farm in Dalian, Liaoning province, China. Oysters were acclimated in tank (240 L) pre-acclimated in normal aerated seawater (pH 8.0) and the oxygen in the tank is maintained by pump air through an air pump. Throughout the exposure period, daily seawater replacement was performed using pre-equilibrated seawater and feed Spirulina twice for 7 days. The animal experiments were carried out according to the health guides of the Dalian Ocean University.

After the acclimation period, the oysters were randomly distributed into two experimental tanks (100 L): an ambient treatment tank with seawater pH 8.0 and an ocean acidification tank with seawater pH 7.4. The exposure experiment was conducted to investigate molecular and physiological responses of oysters under acidification stress. Based on previous studies, seawater pH values of 8.0 (Control group) and 7.4 (OA group) were selected for the present study. pH 7.4 represents a relatively severe acidification condition, and such a condition can also occur in certain local sea areas [32]. Previous studies have also used pH 7.4 to investigate physiological and molecular responses of oysters under ocean acidification stress [33,34]. Therefore, this condition was selected to evaluate the molecular mechanisms and adaptive capacity of oysters under an intensified acidification challenge [35]. While pH and temperature were rigorously monitored and maintained throughout the exposure period, total alkalinity (TA) was not measured in this study, and we acknowledge this as a limitation of the current experimental design. However, the pH levels selected (8.0 and 7.4) represent ambient and projected near‑future OA conditions, respectively, and are consistent with previous OA studies on C. gigas. The pH value was automatically maintained using pH-stat control system (AiKB, China). During the entire experiment period, the pH value was measured once a day to ensure the stability of the acidification conditions [34]. The pH and temperature in each water tank are recorded every 7 days (Table 1). During the acidification period, to maintain stable pH levels in the tanks, water was replaced every 7 days, and spirulina was fed twice daily. The growth indicators and mortality of oysters during the experimental period are shown in Table S1.

At 7, 14, 21 and 28 days after acidification treatment, gonad of oysters were collected from eight oysters in the OA and control group, respectively. Gonadal tissues from two individuals were pooled together as one sample. Since the sex of oysters cannot be visually determined due to their shells being closed or slightly open, it was not analyzed in this study.

Different tissues including adductor muscle, gill, mantle, gonad, labial palp, hepatopancreas and haemocytes were collected from eight untreated oysters according to the previous description [36]. The tissues from two individuals were randomly sampled as one sample. The RNA-seq dataset of C. gigas larvae (unpublished data) provided the RPKM (Reads Per Kilobase per Million mapped reads) values for the CgPGC-1α gene, enabling in-depth analysis of gene expression across different developmental stages.

Samples for total RNA extraction are added with 1 mL Trizol reagent (#R711, Vazyme) and stored at -80 °C. While samples for DNA and protein extraction were immediately frozen with liquid nitrogen, and finally they were both stored at -80 °C.

Total RNA isolation, cDNA synthesis and DNA extraction

The RNA isolation process is carried out according to the manufacture’s protocol. Briefly, after homogenization, the samples were mixed with Dilution Buffer and centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 15 min at room temperature. The supernatant was collected, mixed with an equal volume of isopropanol, and incubated for 10 min at room temperature. Following centrifugation at 12,000 × g for 15 min at room temperature, the pellet was washed twice with 75% ethanol and finally dissolved in an appropriate volume of DEPC-treated water. The integrities of representative mRNA samples were visualized via agarose gel electrophoresis assays, and the quality and concentration of total mRNA were determined using a NanoDrop 2000 Spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Total mRNA was reverse transcribed using HiScript IV All-in-One Ultra RT SuperMix for qPCR (Vazyme, China) for quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) analysis. The obtained cDNA was stored at -80 °C for the subsequent experiments.

The genomic DNA for mtDNA copy number detection was extracted from the gonads using a TIANamp Marine Animals DNA kit (Tiangen Biotech, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. The integrity of the DNA was assessed by agarose gel electrophoresis, while its concentration and purity were determined using a spectrophotometer.

Sequence analysis of CgPGC-1α

The domain architecture of CgPGC-1α and the conserved motifs of PGC-1α proteins were analyzed with the SMART (http://smart.embl-heidelberg.de/) and MEME (https://meme-suite.org/meme/tools/meme) online tools, respectively. The secondary structure and tertiary structure of CgPGC-1α and HsPGC-1α were predicted and analyzed using the NPS (Network Protein Sequence Analysis) online server and AlphaFold3, respectively. Multiple sequence alignment and evolutionary tree was completed in MEGA X (other parameters were set to their default values). Construction and visualization of the evolutionary tree and conserved motifs were performed with TBtools software (Version 2.3) [37]. The detailed information of protein sequences used for multiple sequence alignment and phylogenetic were described in Table 2. The transcriptomic data of oyster at different developmental stages were obtained from the NCBI database under accession number PRJNA146329.

qRT-PCR analysis

Total RNA extraction and cDNA synthesis were performed according to previous report [39]. The primers for qRT-PCR analysis of CgPGC-1, CgTFAM, CgNRF1, CgNRF2, CgMFN and CgDRP1 were designed based on their sequence in NCBI (Table 3). The expression levels were normalized to that of CgEF1A for each sample. The relative expression levels were calculated using the comparative 2-ΔΔCt method [40].

Western blot analysis

The total protein of gonad was extracted, and western blot analysis was performed as described in previous study [41]. Briefly, primary antibodies used in this study included anti-AMPKα (diluted at 1:5000), anti-p-AMPKα (CST, #2535, diluted at 1:1000), anti-α-Tubulin (ProteinTech, #66031, diluted at 1:3000), and anti-PGC-1α (ProteinTech, #66369, diluted at 1:1000). The immunoreactive protein bands were visualized using Amersham Imager 600 (GE Healthcare). The grayscale analysis of protein electrophoresis strips was performed using Image J and SPSS. α-Tubulin was used as internal control to show each equivalent amounts of total proteins.

ATP levels measurement

ATP level in gonad was measured using an ATP determination kit following the manufacturer’s protocol (Jiancheng Bioengineering Institution). Briefly, 100 mg frozen tissues were lysed by PBS buffer. After centrifugation (4 °C, 3 500 rpm, 10 min), the supernatant was put in a boiling water bath for 10 minutes, take out, mix and extract for one minute, centrifuge and take the supernatant to be measured. The light absorption value of ATP in the sample was determined by spectrophotometer. The ATP level was normalized by calculating the ratio between the absorbance value of the corresponding proteins with control samples.

Electron microscopy analysis

For transmission electron microscopy analysis, gonadal tissues from three oysters in each group were pooled, fixed, embedded, and sectioned. The samples were examined using a transmission electron microscope (hitachi, HT7800/HT7700) for image acquisition and analysis. To evaluate mitochondrial alterations, four randomly selected non-overlapping TEM fields from each group were examined. The number of mitochondria within each field was manually counted to compare mitochondrial abundance between control and acidification-treated groups.

Statistical analysis

Because the acidification exposure was performed in single treatment tanks, oysters were considered biological samples within the experimental tank rather than independent tank replicates. All statistical calculations were performed with Origin 2021 and SPSS 25.0 software and shown as Means ± SEM. Data normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test, and homogeneity of variances was evaluated with Levene's test. For comparisons of CgPGC-1α expression among different tissues, one-way ANOVA followed by Duncan’s multiple-comparisons test was performed. Two‑way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed with treatment (control vs acidification) and time (7, 14, 21, 28 days) as fixed factors. For data deviated from normality, a log₂ transformation was applied prior to parametric analysis. Pairwise comparisons between the two treatment groups were performed using independent‑samples t‑tests, with Bonferroni correction applied for multiple testing over the four time points. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Sequence identification and structure analysis

The length of CgPGC-1α coding sequence was 1881 bp encoding a protein with 627 amino acids. CgPGC-1α possessed an activation domain, an RRM domain, a DHDY motif and an SR region (Fig. S1A), and the RRM domain of PGC-1α from different organisms shared high similarity (Fig. S1B; Fig. S3). Furthermore, two nuclear localization sequences were found in CgPGC-1α by using cNLS Mapper prediction (Select a cut-off score = 6.0) (Fig. S2). Totally 64 potential phosphorylation sites were identified by using NetPhos prediction (score = 0.95) (Fig. S2), among which Thr409 was predicted as the potential AMPKα's action site (Table. S2, S3).

Furthermore, the secondary structure composition and spatial structures of oyster CgPGC-1α and human HsPGC-1α was compared by using the "Super" command in PyMOL. They both contained 17 α-helices, four β-turns and seven folds (Fig. 1A and B), and in the RRM domain, the 463-571 aa region of CgPGC-1α overlaps to a large extent with the 642-742 aa region of HsPGC-1α (Fig. 1C-F).

Phylogenetic relationships of PGC-1αs and their protein structure

A phylogenetic tree was constructed utilizing the Maximum Likelihood (ML) method to elucidate the molecular evolutionary relationship of PGC-1αs from diverse species (human, mouse, zebrafish, etc.). In the phylogenetic tree, CgPGC-1α and CaPGC-1α firstly clustered together, and then they further grouped with other mollusks (e.g., scallops and mussels), and finally gathered with vertebrates. Conversely, the three distinct PGC-1α isoforms (D, E, and G) identified in Drosophila melanogaster constituted a separate clade, demonstrating a distant relationship to all other species (Fig. 2A).

(B) Conserved motifs of PGC-1αs were identified using MEME and visualized with TBtools software (V2.149). Colored boxes represent conserved motifs with different sequences and sizes (1-8). Based on the clustering results, we visually displayed the protein motif composition of PGC-1αs. MEME motif analysis revealed that all PGC-1αs contain six conserved motifs (Motif1-6). There was an additional motif 7 in invertebrates including molluscs and arthropod, while there was an additional motif 8 in vertebrates. Moreover, the conserved motifs of PGC-1αs in Drosophila melanogaster are identical to those in mollusc with the sequential arrangement differs slightly (Fig. 2B).

The spatiotemporal expression dynamics and stimulus-responsive regulation of CgPGC-1α

The transcription level of CgPGC-1α was analyzed across haemocytes and multiple tissues using qRT-PCR. CgPGC-1α was expressed in haemocytes and all tissues, with the highest expression level in gill (5.67-fold of that in gonad) and the lowest expression level in gonad (Fig. 3A). Based on the transcriptome sequencing dataset (PRJNA146329), the relative transcription level CgPGC-1α was expressed throughout in all developmental stages. Its expression level increased rapidly from the 2-4 cell stage to the blastula stage and subsequently decreased during later developmental stages (Fig. S4).

After acidification, the transcription level of CgPGC-1α in gonad were detected on days 7, 14, 21, and 28. During acidification exposure, the transcription level of CgPGC-1α displayed an oscillatory pattern characterized by sequential phases of increase and decrease. The transcript level of CgPGC-1α was significantly upregulated on 7 and 21 days of acidification, which was 12.07- (p < 0.01) and 36.43-fold (p < 0.01) of that in blank group, respectively (Fig. 3B). There was no significant difference between the OA group and blank group on 14 and 28 days (Fig. 3B).

Acidification affects mitochondrial DNA levels

The mtDNA copy number in gonad exhibited a time-dependent response to acidification treatment. Initially, at day 7 of acidification exposure, the mtDNA copy number was significantly elevated compared to the blank group (2.23-fold, p < 0.05). Subsequently, a gradual decline was observed in both groups over the following time points (days 14, 21, and 28). Notably, on day 28 after acidification treatment, the mtDNA copy number reduced significantly (0.79-fold of that in the blank group, p < 0.05) (Fig. 4A). Though the relative expression of CgTFAM in gonad showed a marked increase on day 28 after acidification treatment, there was no significant difference (Fig. 4B). Compared to the blank group, the transcript level of CgNRF1 in the acidification group significantly decreased on day 21 (p < 0.05) (Fig. 4C). On the contrary, the transcript level of CgNRF2 in the acidification group increased significantly on day 21 (p < 0.05), and thereafter, it decreased notably on day 28 (p < 0.05) (Fig. 4D).

In order to investigate the reasons for changes in mtDNA copy number, the relative mRNA expression level of CgMFN and CgDRP1 were detected. On day 7 and 28 after acidification treatment, these two genes showed completely opposite expression patterns. On day 7 after treatment, the relative expression level of CgMFN increased significantly (4.12-fold of that in the blank group, p < 0.05) (Fig. 5A), while the expression level of CgDRP1 decreased significantly (0.73-fold of that in the blank group, p < 0.05) (Fig. 5B). On day 28 after treatment, the relative expression level of CgMFN decreased significantly (0.76-fold of that in the blank group, p < 0.05) (Fig. 5A), while that of CgDRP1 increased significantly (1.84-fold of that in the blank group, p < 0.05) (Fig. 5B).

Effect of acidification on Mitochondrial morphology and ATP content

Based on above results, the mitochondrial morphology and ATP content in gonad of oyster after seven days of acidification treatment were further examined by using electron microscopy and ATP assay kit. The mitochondrial quantity in oyster of the acidification treatment group is significantly higher than that of the blank group (2.59-fold, p < 0.05) (Fig. 6A-B). Moreover, the morphology of mitochondria in the blank group were circular or elliptical with smooth mitochondrial membranes, while mitochondria in the acidification group became irregular in shape and their cristae were swollen (Fig. 6C). Corresponding to structural changes of mitochondria, the ATP content in gonad increased significantly after 7 days of acidification treatment, which was 5.36-fold of that in the blank group (p < 0.01) (Fig. 6D).

Short-term acidification is associated with activation of the CgAMPKα/PGC-1α signaling axis

The protein abundance of CgPGC-1α and CgAMPKα, together with the phosphorylation level of CgAMPKα, was further examined to assess whether the AMPKα/PGC-1α signaling pathway was associated with the mitochondrial response to acidification. After 7 days of acidification treatment, the protein level of CgPGC-1α and the pAMPKα(Thr172)/Tubulin ratio both significantly increased, which was 2.27- and 1.33-fold of that in the blank group (p < 0.05) (Fig. 7). Though the pAMPKα(Thr172)/AMPKα ratio also showed an upward trend, there was no significant difference between the blank group and the acidification group (Fig. 7).

Discussion

Mitochondria, the cell’s proverbial powerhouses, present a multi-faceted biological landscape in biosynthesis and intra-/inter-cellular signaling pathways by producing ATP [42]. Under environmental stress, cells initiate mitochondrial biogenesis to produce new mitochondria to meet the demand for energy [43], which is mainly regulated by PGC-1α [44]. In the present study, the RRM and SR domain of CgPGC-1α showed high sequence conservation, and the three-dimensional structure of the CgPGC-1α RRM domain exhibited substantial similarity to that of HsPGC-1α. These findings suggested that CgPGC-1α may retain conserved RNA-binding properties and potentially participate in RNA processing and transcriptional regulation [8]. Further sequence conservation analysis revealed that PGC-1αs from different species shared multiple conserved motifs among which some exhibited similar distribution patterns. For example, motif 1-6 are universally present in PGC-1α sequences from mollusks, arthropods, and vertebrates, indicating that they might perform similar core biological functions across different species. Unlike above conserved motifs, motif 7-8 may be specific to certain taxonomic groups, indicating functional differentiation between vertebrates and invertebrates. These sequence analysis results provide important clues for further investigation into the functions of CgPGC-1α.

It has been reported that the embryonic development, normal physiological activities, and stress response of organisms all require energy consumption, and PGC-1α plays an important role in energy metabolism regulation by regulating mitochondrial biosynthesis [5,45]. Therefore, the mRNA expression of CgPGC-1α in different developmental stages of larvae, multiple adult tissues, and after acidification treatment were investigated. CgPGC-1α is expressed at different developmental stages of larvae with the highest level at the blastula stage, suggesting that CgPGC-1α may play an important role in early embryonic processes, such as germ layer formation, body axis establishment, and organogenesis [46]. CgPGC-1α is also constitutively expressed in various adult tissues with the highest level in gill, which is the important tissues for respiration and substance exchange in shellfish and has active mitochondrial metabolism [47]. The highest expression level of CgPGC-1α in gill helps oyster to maintain high mitochondrial function to cope with the continuous oxygen metabolic demand [7,48]. Moreover, CgPGC-1α mRNA significantly increased on 7 and 21 days after acidification treatment, indicating that PGC-1α plays a crucial role in regulating energy metabolism in response to environmental stress [[1], [2], [3]]. Similar results have also been reported in other bivalves, which showed that ocean acidification activate PGC-1α to spurs mitochondrial biogenesis and keep up cellular energy supply, finally maintaining metabolic stability [[49], [50], [51]].

As PGC-1α activation can promote mitochondrial biogenesis [52], we examined mtDNA copy number to further elucidate the pathway through which CgPGC-1α participates in the response to acidification stress. The results showed that mitochondrial number increase significantly on day 7 after acidification treatment, indicating that at the early stage of acidification, C. gigas may maintain cellular energy requirements and homeostasis by increasing mitochondrial number. MFN is a key protein involved in the fusion of the outer mitochondrial membrane. Under normal circumstances, this fusion facilitates the sharing of contents among mitochondria, repairs damaged DNA, and optimizes energy production [53]. On day 7 of acidification stress, the mRNA level of CgMFN was increased, while the mRNA expression of CgDRP1 was decreased. This indicates that short-term acidification may promote mitochondrial fusion and reduce mitochondrial fragmentation. This mitochondrial remodeling may contribute to maintaining mitochondrial network integrity and supporting cellular energy homeostasis under stress conditions [54,55]. Although mitochondrial fusion-related responses were activated, the observed mitochondrial swelling and cristae alterations indicate that mitochondria were simultaneously experiencing structural stress during acidification exposure. However, whether mitochondrial fusion directly facilitates content exchange, repairs damaged mitochondria, or improves respiratory chain efficiency requires further investigation [56]. With prolonged acidification, mitochondrial proliferation becomes unsustainable. On day 28 of acidification stress, the mtDNA copy number was significantly decreased. This may be attributed to the overactivation of mitochondria induced by acidification, which subsequently exacerbated mitochondrial damage [57]. Furthermore, at day 28 of acidification stress, the mRNA level of CgMFN was downregulated, while the mRNA expression of CgDRP1 was upregulated, indicating that the cell inhibited both mitochondrial fusion and the excessive activation of fission processes. These results suggest that prolonged acidification stress drives mitochondria into a "quality clearance" phase, during which the accumulation of damaged mitochondria triggers the cellular quality control mechanism [58]. This shift in mitochondrial dynamics may facilitate the removal of damaged mitochondria and contribute to the observed reduction in mtDNA copy number [59]. NRF1 (nuclear respiratory factor 1) and NRF2 (nuclear respiratory factor 2) are two key transcription factors in cells that together form a core network regulating cellular energy metabolism, antioxidant defense, and mitochondrial biogenesis. Under acidification stress, the expression of CgNRF1 and CgNRF2 exhibited opposing trends, revealing that CgNRF1 and CgNRF2 activate distinct functions at different stages of acidification stress. NRF2 is a critical regulator of antioxidant stress response and cytoprotection. When intracellular ROS levels increase, NRF2 dissociates from Keap1, translocates into the nucleus, and initiates the expression of antioxidant-related genes [60]. Meanwhile, the activity of NRF1 may be downregulated due to sustained oxidative stress or energy depletion, slowing the process of NRF1-mediated biogenesis. Consequently, the mtDNA copy number exhibits a decreasing trend during this phase [61].

The function of mitochondria is closely linked to both the quantity and quality of mtDNA. Since mtDNA copy number is influenced by mtDNA quality, it can serve as a key indicator of mitochondrial function [62]. It has been well known that mitochondrial fusion is mainly regulated by mitochondrial fusion proteins 1/2 (MFN1/2) and optic atrophy protein 1 (OPA1), while mitochondrial fission is mainly mediated by DRP1 and OMM [63,64]. Disrupting or inhibiting these proteins damage mitochondrial structure, leading to smaller, fragmented mitochondria with lower membrane potential, reduced respiratory activity and ATP production, and suppressed cell activity [63,65,66]. In the present study, the relative mRNA expression level of CgMFN and CgDRP1 were detected to investigate the reasons for increase in mtDNA copy number. After 7 days of acidification treatment, the mRNA expression level of CgMFN significantly increased, while that of CgDRP1 significantly decreased. However, they both showed an opposite expression trend after 28 days of acidification treatment. These indicate that C. gigas may meet their energy needs by increasing mitochondrial number and promoting mitochondrial fusion and remodeling the mitochondrial network during the early stages of stress, however, with extended acidification, the degree of mitochondrial damage increases, and the number of irreversibly damaged mitochondria also increases. Therefore, the cell increases mitochondrial division to remove the irreversibly damaged mitochondria, and it is possible to initiate autophagy or apoptosis of the cell [59]. The significant increase of mitochondrial numbers was also revealed by transmission electron microscopy, along with signs of damage like membrane swelling and irregular shapes, and many tubular mitochondria. Long-term acidification suppresses mitochondrial fusion and promotes fission, likely an adaptive strategy for managing damaged mitochondria [67]. Ocean acidification can lead to an energetic imbalance of marine organisms, thereby affecting their survival [68,69]. In this study, ATP levels increased significantly at 7 days after acidification treatment, indicating that early activation of mitochondrial metabolic pathways to boost energy supply, with energy metabolism disruption in the middle and late stages.

Our results showed that CgPGC-1α activation promoted mitochondrial biogenesis, consistent with the previous study [52]. A key question is how CgPGC-1α activity is regulated under acidification stress. The studies in model organisms showed that the activity of PGC-1α could be regulated by several molecules related to energy metabolism, such as AMPK [11,70]. The activity of PGC-1α is known to be regulated by several energy-sensing pathways, including AMPK [71,72]. In the present study, short-term acidification was accompanied by increased AMPKα phosphorylation and elevated CgPGC-1α protein abundance. In addition, T409 was predicted as a putative AMPKα-responsive phosphorylation site in PGC-1α [72]. These observations are consistent with the possible involvement of AMPKα/PGC-1α signaling in the mitochondrial response to acidification. However, the present data are correlative, and a direct regulatory relationship between AMPKα and CgPGC-1α cannot be established. Pharmacological inhibition of AMPK, genetic manipulation, or site-directed mutation of T409 will be required to determine whether AMPKα directly regulates CgPGC-1α activity under acidification stress. Briefly, acidification was associated with activation of the AMPK/PGC-1α pathway and enhanced mitochondrial biogenesis under short term acidification, prolonged acidification was accompanied by changes indicative of impaired mitochondrial homeostasis [73]. Notably, CgNRF2 expression increased at day 21 but decreased at day 28, suggesting a time-dependent modulation of antioxidant-related signaling during acidification exposure. Because NRF2 is an important regulator of cellular antioxidant responses, these transcriptional changes may reflect alterations in the cellular response to oxidative stress [74,75]. However, ROS production and other oxidative stress indicators were not directly measured in the present study. Therefore, a causal role of oxidative stress cannot be established. Further studies measuring ROS levels, lipid peroxidation, and antioxidant enzyme activities will be required to determine whether oxidative stress contributes to the mitochondrial alterations observed during prolonged acidification.

From an applied perspective, the AMPKα/PGC-1α-mediated mitochondrial response identified in this study may contribute to the resilience of oysters under future ocean acidification. The increased expression of CgPGC-1α, the number of mitochondria, the copy number of mtDNA, and the ATP content in the early stage indicate that the oysters have initiated their own energy compensation response [76]. However, the subsequent decrease in mtDNA copy number and the changes in mitochondrial dynamics suggest that this response may become unsustainable under long-term exposure. Therefore, CgPGC-1α may serve as a candidate marker for screening acid-resistant oyster populations. Moreover, persistent mitochondrial dysfunction, especially in the reproductive glands, may eventually damage reproductive capacity and population expansion [77,78], thereby potentially affecting the yield of aquaculture and the ecosystem functions related to oysters [79,80]. Although the set pH value of 7.4 is considered a severe acidification condition, it may reflect the intermittent or locally intensified acidification phenomenon in coastal aquaculture areas. Thus, it provides a mechanism for assessing the future adaptability of oyster populations and predicting potential risks for oyster populations and the aquaculture industry under future acidification scenarios.

It should be acknowledged that the sex of individual oysters was not determined in the present study. Studies have shown that under long-term stressful conditions, males and females exhibit different physiological responses [81]. Therefore, in this study, it cannot be ruled out that gender differences also have an impact on the stress response. In future studies, incorporating sex as a variable would enable a more comprehensive understanding of the stress responses of oysters of different sexes. Moreover, in this study, acid exposure was conducted only in a single treatment tank, so the potential tank effect could not be completely ruled out. In the future, it is necessary to employ systems with duplicate water tanks to further validate the observed mitochondrial responses, in order to more accurately assess the adaptability of oysters in the context of ocean acidification.

In summary, the CgPGC-1α with conserved RRM domain was identified in oyster C. gigas. It was detected in all examined larvae stages and tissues with the highest expression in blastula stage and adult gill of oyster. CgPGC-1α could respond to acidification treatment, promote mitochondrial biogenesis by regulating mtDNA copy number and the expression of CgMFN and CgDRP1. Notably, short-term (7-day) acidification stress activated the AMPK/PGC-1α signaling axis. Collectively, these findings provide a novel insight into the central role of PGC-1α in oyster's response to ocean acidification, and also offer a theoretical basis and candidate targets for breeding acidification-tolerant varieties.

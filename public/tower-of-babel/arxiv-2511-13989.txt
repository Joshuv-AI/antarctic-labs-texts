On totally hyperbolic non-Fuchsian type-preserving representations
Inyoung Ryu

Abstract
We identify type-preserving representations ϕ : π 1 ​ ( Σ ) → PSL ⁡ ( 2 , ℝ ) \phi:\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R}) of the fundamental group of every punctured surface Σ = Σ g , p \Sigma=\Sigma_{g,p} that are not Fuchsian yet send all non-peripheral simple closed curves to hyperbolic elements, which give a negative answer to a question of Bowditch. These representations have relative Euler class e ⁡ ( ϕ ) = ± ( χ ⁡ ( Σ ) + 1 ) e(\phi)=\pm(\chi(\Sigma)+1) , and their PSL ⁡ ( 2 , ℝ ) \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R}) -conjugacy classes form a full-measure subset of 2 ​ p 2p connected components of the relative character variety. We further show that, while these representations are not Fuchsian, their restrictions to certain subsurfaces of Σ \Sigma are Fuchsian.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we consider punctured surfaces, which are surfaces obtained from connected, closed, oriented surfaces by removing finitely many points.
Let
\Sigma=\Sigma_{g,p}
be a punctured surface of genus
g
and
p\geqslant 1
punctures, with Euler characteristic
\chi(\Sigma)<0
.
We study representations from the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
of the surface
\Sigma
to
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
.
In particular, we consider
type-preserving
representations,
i.e.
, representations
\phi:\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
sending all the peripheral elements (elements represented by loops around a puncture) of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
to parabolic elements of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R}).
We focus on type-preserving representations that are non-elementary, meaning those whose image is Zariski dense in
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
.
The relative Euler class
e(\phi)
of a type-preserving representation
\phi
is the obstruction class of the associated flat
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-bundle over
\Sigma,
which is an integer invariant under the isomorphism
\mathrm{H}^{2}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma;\pi_{1}(\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})))\cong\mathbb{Z}.

The relative Euler class satisfies
the
Milnor-Wood inequality

\chi(\Sigma)\leqslant e(\phi)\leqslant-\chi(\Sigma),

where the equality
|e(\phi)|=-\chi(\Sigma)
holds if and only if
\phi
is
Fuchsian
, that is, discrete and faithful.
(See
[
11
]
, Appendix A.)

The main focus of this paper is on representations satisfying the following property:

Definition 1.1
.

A representation
\phi:\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
is
totally hyperbolic
if it
sends every non-peripheral element of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
represented by a simple closed curve to a hyperbolic element of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
.

It is well known that every Fuchsian type-preserving representation is totally hyperbolic. As a converse, Bowditch asked the following question.

Question 1.2
.

[
2
, Question C]

If a non-elementary type-preserving representation
\phi:\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
is totally hyperbolic, must
\phi
be Fuchsian?

This question is closely related to a conjecture of Goldman regarding the mapping class group action on the
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-character variety.
For a closed surface
\Sigma
, recall that the
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-character variety is the
space

\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)=\mathrm{Hom}\big(\pi_{1}(\Sigma),\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})\big){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sslash}\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
where the quotient
is the GIT quotient.

The connected components of
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)
are indexed by the
Euler classes

[
4
]
;
and the mapping class group
\mathrm{Mod}(\Sigma)
, the group of isotopy classes of orientation preserving homeomorphisms
f:\Sigma\to\Sigma
, acts on each connected component of
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)
.
The extremal components characterized by the Euler classes
\pm\chi(\Sigma)
, which consist of Fuchsian representations, are
respectively identified with the Teichmüller space of
\Sigma
. It is well known that the action of
\mathrm{Mod}(\Sigma)
on these
two
components is properly discontinuous. On each of the other non-extremal components, Goldman conjectured in
[
6
]
that

\mathrm{Mod}(\Sigma)
acts ergodically with respect to the measure induced by Goldman symplectic form
[
3
]
.
Marché and Wolff
[
8
]
proved
that, when the surface
\Sigma
is closed, an affirmative answer to Bowditch’s question
—which can also be asked for closed surfaces—
implies that Goldman’s conjecture is true. They also gave an affirmative answer to Bowditch’s question for the closed oriented surface
\Sigma=\Sigma_{2,0}
of genus two, thereby proving Goldman’s conjecture in the genus two case
[
8
,
9
]
. It seems to be of folklore knowledge that, for a punctured surface
\Sigma=\Sigma_{g,p}
, Bowditch’s question and Goldman’s conjecture are similarly related via the relative character variety of type-preserving representations.

For punctured surfaces with Euler characteristic
\chi(\Sigma)=-1
, namely, the
once-punctured
torus and the
thrice-punctured
sphere, Bowditch’s question has an affirmative answer
for the trivial reason that

every non-elementary type-preserving representation has relative Euler class
\pm 1
—and is therefore Fuchsian.
The first
set of
counterexamples
to Bowditch’s question
were identified by Yang in
[
11
]
, in the case of the four-holed sphere
\Sigma=\Sigma_{0,4}
, with relative Euler class
\pm 1
. For other punctured surfaces, the question has remained open.

The main result of this paper gives counterexamples to Bowditch’s question for
every
punctured surfaces
\Sigma=\Sigma_{g,p}
with
\chi(\Sigma)\leqslant-2
.
To state the result, we recall that there is an invariant
s(\phi)\in\{\pm 1\}^{p}
of each type-preserving representation
\phi
,
called the
sign
of
\phi
, which assigns each puncture of
\Sigma
a sign
+1
or
-1.
(See Section
2.2
for details.)
For the sign
s\in\{\pm 1\}^{p},
we let
p_{+}(s)
be the number of
+1
’s and let
p_{-}(s)
be the number of
-1
’s in the components of
s.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
s\in\{\pm 1\}^{p}
, let

\mathcal{M}^{s}_{n}(\Sigma)
be a subspace of the relative character variety
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)
which consists of the
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-conjugacy
classes of type-preserving representations with relative Euler class
n
and sign
s
.
The main result in
[
10
]
shows that,
if
n
and
s

satisfy
the generalized Milnor-Wood inequality

\chi(\Sigma)+p_{+}(s)\leqslant n\leqslant-\chi(\Sigma)-p_{-}(s),

then the space
\mathcal{M}^{s}_{n}(\Sigma)
is non-empty and forms a connected component of
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)
. The following Theorem
1.3
shows that there exist totally hyperbolic, non-Fuchsian representations whose
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-conjugacy classes form a dense subset of
2p
connected components of
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Sigma=\Sigma_{g,p}
with
\chi(\Sigma)\leqslant-2
and
p\geqslant 1
,

and assume
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
s\in\{\pm 1\}^{p}
satisfy one of the following:

(1)

n=-\chi(\Sigma)-1
and
p_{-}(s)=1
, and

(2)

n=\chi(\Sigma)+1
and
p_{+}(s)=1

Then there exist uncountably many non-Fuchsian type-preserving representations
\phi:\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
of relative Euler class
e(\phi)=n
and sign
s(\phi)=s
that
are
totally hyperbolic.
Furthermore, the set of their
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-conjugacy classes has full measure in
\mathcal{M}^{s}_{n}(\Sigma)
.

Remark 1.4
.

When
\Sigma=\Sigma_{0,4}
, the totally hyperbolic representations in Theorem
1.3
correspond to those identified in
[
11
]
.

Although these totally hyperbolic representations are not Fuchsian, they exhibit certain similarities to Fuchsian representations, which will also be discussed in this paper.
We hope this observation will contribute to a better understanding of the mapping class group action on these components of the relative character variety.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
\phi
be a non-Fuchsian totally hyperbolic representation identified in Theorem
1.3
.
Then there exists a pair of pants
P
in
\Sigma
such that,
for each connected component
\Sigma^{\prime}
of the complement
\Sigma\setminus P
,
the restriction
\phi|_{\pi_{1}(\Sigma^{\prime})}
is Fuchsian.

2
Preliminary

Let
\Sigma=\Sigma_{g,p}
be a punctured surface with genus
g
and
p
punctures, and let
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
be the fundamental group of
\Sigma
.
A
primitive peripheral element
of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)

is an element represented by a closed curve freely homotopic to a circle around a puncture of
\Sigma
. Notice that each puncture of
\Sigma
determines a conjugacy class of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
consisting of the primitive peripheral elements around it.

Let
c_{1},\cdots,c_{p}
be
the
primitive peripheral elements of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
, chosen for each puncture.
We denote by
\mathrm{HP}(\Sigma)
the subspace of
\mathrm{Hom}\big(\pi_{1}(\Sigma),\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})\big)
consisting of representations
\phi:\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
such that, for each
i\in\{1,\dots,p\},

\phi(c_{i})
is either a hyperbolic or a parabolic element of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
.
In particular, when
\phi(c_{i})
is parabolic for every
i\in\{1,\dots,p\},

we call
\phi

type-preserving
; and we denote by
\mathcal{R}(\Sigma)
the subspace of
\mathrm{Hom}\big(\pi_{1}(\Sigma),\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})\big)
consisting of type-preserving representations.

In this section, we recall the definitions and basic properties of the relative Euler classes and the signs of representations in
\mathrm{HP}(\Sigma)
together with the results from
[
10
]
, which serve as the key ingredients in our main results.
In the rest of the paper, we will use the notation
\pm A
for a
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-element,
A
for an
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-element, and
\widetilde{A}
for an element in the universal cover
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
.

2.1
The relative Euler classes

We first recall the definition of the relative Euler class.
For each type-preserving representation
\phi
, there is a corresponding flat principal
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-bundle over
\Sigma
with parabolic boundary condition, for which
\phi
is the holonomy representation.
The relative Euler class of this bundle is an obstruction class in
\mathrm{H}^{2}\big(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma;\pi_{1}\big(\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})\big)\big)
,
which measures the obstruction to extend the trivialization on
\partial\Sigma
to a global trivialization on
\Sigma
.
This obstruction class defines the relative Euler class
e(\phi)
of the representation
\phi
, which can be identified with an integer via the isomorphism
\mathrm{H}^{2}\big(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma;\pi_{1}\big(\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})\big)\big)\cong\mathbb{Z}.

For more details, we recall the structure of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
and its universal cover
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
.
Recall that elements of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
are classified as hyperbolic, parabolic and elliptic as follows: For a non-trivial
\pm A
in
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R}),
let
A
be one of its lifts in
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}).
Then
\pm A
is hyperbolic if
|\mathrm{tr}(A)|>2,
parabolic if
|\mathrm{tr}(A)|=2,
and elliptic if
|\mathrm{tr}(A)|<2.

We respectively let
\mathrm{Hyp},

\mathrm{Par}
and
\mathrm{Ell}
be the spaces of hyperbolic, parabolic and elliptic elements of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
.
\mathrm{Par}
consists of two disjoint subsets
\mathrm{Par}^{+}
and
\mathrm{Par}^{-}
, which are respectively the
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-conjugacy classes of
\pm\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}
and
\pm\begin{bmatrix}1&-1\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}
. We call a parabolic element
positive
if it is in
\mathrm{Par}^{+}
, and
negative
if it is in
\mathrm{Par}^{-}
.
Similarly, non-central elements of the universal covering
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
are also classified as
hyperbolic
,
parabolic
, or

elliptic
according to the type of their projections to
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
; and a parabolic element of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
is
positive
or
negative
if its projection to
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
is respectively so. As a convention, we define the
trace
of an element in
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
to be the trace of its projection to
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}).
Then a non-central element in
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
is hyperbolic, parabolic, or elliptic if its trace is greater than, equal to, or less than
2
in the absolute value.

As
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
is homeomorphic to an open solid torus, its universal covering
group
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, with the group of
deck transformations

\pi_{1}(\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R}))\cong\mathbb{Z}
.
The preimage of each of
\mathrm{Hyp}
,
\mathrm{Par}^{\pm}
and
\mathrm{Ell}
in
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
consists of infinitely many connected components, indexed by the integers. We denote these components as follows:

Recall that the center of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
consists of the lifts of
\pm\mathrm{I}
, and is isomorphic to
\pi_{1}\big(\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})\big)\cong\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\widetilde{\exp}:\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\to\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
be the exponential map of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
, and let
z\doteq\widetilde{\exp}\begin{bmatrix}0&\pi\\
-\pi&0\end{bmatrix}
. Then
z
is a generator of the center of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
, and every lift of
\pm\mathrm{I}
in
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
is equal to
z^{n}=\widetilde{\exp}\begin{bmatrix}0&n\pi\\
-n\pi&0\end{bmatrix}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
\mathrm{Hyp}_{0}
be the space of hyperbolic elements of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
which lie in one-parameter subgroups; and for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\mathrm{Hyp}_{n}\doteq z^{n}\mathrm{Hyp}_{0}
.
Then
\{\mathrm{Hyp}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
are the connected components of the space of hyperbolic elements in
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
; and two hyperbolic elements in
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
are conjugate if and only if they lie in the same connected component
\mathrm{Hyp}_{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and have the same trace.
Similarly, let
\mathrm{Par}^{+}_{0}
and
\mathrm{Par}^{-}_{0}
respectively be the spaces of positive and negative parabolic elements of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
lying in the image of the exponential map, and let
\mathrm{Par}_{0}=\mathrm{Par}^{+}_{0}\cup\mathrm{Par}^{-}_{0}.
For any
n\in\mathbb{Z},
let
\mathrm{Par}^{+}_{n}=z^{n}\mathrm{Par}^{+}_{0}
and
\mathrm{Par}^{-}_{n}=z^{n}\mathrm{Par}^{-}_{0},
and let
\mathrm{Par}_{n}\doteq\mathrm{Par}^{+}_{n}\cup\mathrm{Par}^{-}_{n}=z^{n}\mathrm{Par}_{0}.
Then
\{\mathrm{Par}^{+}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
\{\mathrm{Par}^{-}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
are respectively the connected components of the space of positive and negative parabolic elements in
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
; and two parabolic elements in
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
are conjugate if and only if they lie in the same connected component
\mathrm{Par}^{+}_{n}
or
\mathrm{Par}^{-}_{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}.
For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we denote by
\overline{\mathrm{Hyp}_{n}}
the closure of
\mathrm{Hyp}_{n}
in
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
, which is a union of

\mathrm{Hyp}_{n},

\mathrm{Par}_{n}
and
\{z^{n}\}
.

Unlike hyperbolic and parabolic elements, all elliptic elements of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
lie in one-parameter subgroups; and we need to index their connected components differently. For
n>0
, let
\mathrm{Ell}_{n}
be the subspace of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
consisting of elements conjugate to
\widetilde{\exp}\begin{bmatrix}0&\theta\\
-\theta&0\end{bmatrix}
for some
\theta
in
((n-1)\pi,n\pi),
and for
n<0
, let
\mathrm{Ell}_{n}
be the subspace of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
consisting of elements conjugate to
\widetilde{\exp}\begin{bmatrix}0&\theta\\
-\theta&0\end{bmatrix}
for some
\theta
in
(n\pi,(n+1)\pi).

Then
\{\mathrm{Ell}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}
are the connected components of the space of elliptic elements in
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})};
and two elliptic elements in
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
are conjugate if and only if both lie in the same connected component
\mathrm{Ell}_{n}
and have the same trace.
For
n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n_{1}<n_{2}
,

\mathrm{Ell}_{n_{2}}=z^{n_{2}-n_{1}}\mathrm{Ell}_{n_{1}}

if
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
have the same sign, while

\mathrm{Ell}_{n_{2}}=z^{n_{2}-n_{1}-1}\mathrm{Ell}_{n_{1}}

if
n_{1}
and
n_{2}

have opposite signs,
(because
\mathrm{Ell}_{0}
is undefined).

The relative Euler class satisfies the following additivity property.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
4

Let
\Sigma=\Sigma_{g,p},

\gamma

\Sigma

\Sigma

Then
\phi|_{\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{1})}\in\mathrm{HP}(\Sigma_{1})
,
\phi|_{\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{2})}\in\mathrm{HP}(\Sigma_{2})
, and

e(\phi)=e\big(\phi|_{\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{1})}\big)+e(\phi|_{\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{2})}).

Recall that a
holonomy representation
is a representation
\phi\in\mathrm{HP}(\Sigma)
that is Fuchsian, i.e., discrete and faithful, and that the convex core

\big(\mathbb{H}^{2}/\phi\big(\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\big)\big)
is homeomorphic to

.
The following proposition determines the relative Euler classes of holonomy representations.
In the original statement,
\phi
is assumed to send all peripheral elements to hyperbolic elements of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
; however, since type-preserving representations of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
are holonomy if and only if they are Fuchsian (see
[
11
]
, Appendix A), the proof works verbatim here.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
4
, Proposition 3.4]

Let
\phi
be a representation in
\mathrm{HP}(\Sigma)
. Then its relative Euler class
e(\phi)
satisfies
|e(\phi)|=-\chi(\Sigma)
if and only if
\phi
is a holonomy representation.

2.2
The signs

In this subsection, we recall the definition of the sign, and state the key result in
[
10
]
.
Let
\Sigma=\Sigma_{g,p}
, and let
c_{1},\cdots,c_{p}
be the primitive peripheral elements of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
, chosen for each puncture. The
sign
of a representation
\phi\in\mathrm{HP}(\Sigma)
is a
p
-tuple
s(\phi)=(s_{1},\dots,s_{p})
in
\{-1,0,+1\}^{p}
, where for each
i\in\{1,\dots,p\},

s_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}+1,&\text{if }\phi(c_{i})\text{ is positive parabolic}\\
0,&\text{if }\phi(c_{i})\text{ is hyperbolic}\\
-1,&\text{if }\phi(c_{i})\text{ is negative parabolic}.\end{array}\right.

Since the type of an element of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
is invariant under
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})-
conjugation, the sign of

is independent on the choice of the primitive peripheral elements
c_{i}
’s. In the rest of the paper, for a sign

, we respectively let

and

be the number of
+1
’s,
0

-1
’s in the components of

.

The following proposition describes the behavior of the relative Euler class and the sign under the
\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{R})\setminus\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-conjugations.

Proposition 2.3
.

[
10
, Proposition 2.14]

Let
h
be an element of
\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{R})\setminus\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
; and for a representation
\phi\in\mathrm{HP}(\Sigma),
let
h\phi h^{-1}:\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
be defined by

h\phi h^{-1}(c)=h\phi(c)h^{-1}

for each
c\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma).
Then
h\phi h^{-1}\in\mathrm{HP}(\Sigma)
with the relative Euler class
e(h\phi h^{-1})=-e(\phi)
and the sign
s(h\phi h^{-1})=-s(\phi).

Let
e:\mathrm{HP}(\Sigma)\to\mathbb{Z}
be the relative Euler class map and let
s:\mathrm{HP}(\Sigma)\to\{-1,0,+1\}^{p}
be the sign map defined respectively by sending
\phi
to the relative Euler class
e(\phi)
and the sign
s(\phi)
of
\phi.

For

and

\mathrm{HP}(\Sigma)

and sign

.

[
10

gives the necessary and sufficient condition for

Theorem 2.4
.

[
10

Let
\Sigma=\Sigma_{g,p}

2.3
Images of commutator and product maps

In this subsection, we describe the images of the commutator and product maps in
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
, which will be used to compute the relative Euler classes.
For a surface
\Sigma
with
\chi(\Sigma)=-1
, its fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}
is a free group of rank 2,
hence a representation
\phi:\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
is determined by the
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-images of the two generators.
As a consequence,

\mathrm{Hom}\big(\pi_{1}(\Sigma),\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})\big)\cong\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R}).

If
\Sigma=\Sigma_{1,1}
with
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)=\langle a_{1},b_{1},c_{1}\ |\ [a_{1},b_{1}]\cdot c_{1}\rangle

\widetilde{\phi(a_{1})},\widetilde{\phi(b_{1})}
be arbitrary lifts of
\phi(a_{1}),\phi(b_{1})
in
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
, and let
\widetilde{\phi(c_{1})}
be the unique lift of
\phi(c_{1})
in
\overline{\mathrm{Hyp}_{0}}
. If
e(\phi)=n
, then by the definition of the relative Euler class, we have

[\widetilde{\phi(a_{1})},\widetilde{\phi(b_{1})}]\cdot\widetilde{\phi(c_{1})}=z^{n},

hence the commutator

\big[\widetilde{\phi(a_{1})},\widetilde{\phi(b_{1})}\big]=z^{n}\widetilde{\phi(c_{1})}^{-1}

lies in
\overline{\mathrm{Hyp}_{n}}=z^{n}\overline{\mathrm{Hyp}_{0}}
.
Similarly, if
\Sigma=\Sigma_{0,3}
with
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)=\langle c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\ |\ c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}\rangle

\phi\in\mathrm{HP}(\Sigma)
, we let
\widetilde{\phi(c_{1})},\widetilde{\phi(c_{2})},\widetilde{\phi(c_{3})}
be the unique lifts of
\phi(c_{1}),\phi(c_{2}),\phi(c_{3})
in
\overline{\mathrm{Hyp}_{0}}

e(\phi)=n
, then we have

\widetilde{\phi(c_{1})}\widetilde{\phi(c_{2})}\widetilde{\phi(c_{3})}=z^{n},

and hence the product

\widetilde{\phi(c_{1})}\widetilde{\phi(c_{2})}=z^{n}\widetilde{\phi(c_{3})}^{-1}

\overline{\mathrm{Hyp}_{n}}

3.3
Proof of Corollary
1.5

Finally, we prove Corollary
1.5
, which we restate below as Corollary
3.9
.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
\Sigma=\Sigma_{g,p}
with
\chi(\Sigma)\leqslant-2
and
p\geqslant 1
, and let
n=-\chi(\Sigma)-1
and
s\in\{\pm 1\}^{p}
satisfying
p_{-}(s)=1
.
For every
\phi\in\mathrm{NP}^{s}_{n}(\Sigma)
,
there exists a pair of pants
P
in
\Sigma
such that,
for each connected component
\Sigma^{\prime}
of the complement
\Sigma\setminus P
,
the restriction
\phi|_{\pi_{1}(\Sigma^{\prime})}
is Fuchsian.

Proof.

Let
c_{1},\cdots,c_{p}
be
the primitive peripheral elements of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
. As
p_{-}(s)=1
,
there is a unique
i\in\{1,\cdots,p\}
such that
\phi(c_{i})
is negative parabolic.
Let
P
be a pair of pants in
\Sigma
whose fundamental group
\pi_{1}(P)
contains
c_{i}
.
By Theorem
3.2
, every common boundary component of
P
and
\Sigma\setminus P
maps to a hyperbolic element of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
. Hence
\phi|_{\pi_{1}(P)}\in\mathrm{HP}(P)
, and for each connected component
\Sigma^{\prime}
of the complement
\Sigma\setminus P
,
\phi|_{\pi_{1}(\Sigma^{\prime})}\in\mathrm{HP}(\Sigma^{\prime})
.
Moreover, Theorem
2.4
implies that
e(\phi|_{\pi_{1}(P)})\leqslant 0
and
e(\phi|_{\pi_{1}(\Sigma\setminus P)})\leqslant-\chi(\Sigma\setminus P)=n
, and by Proposition
2.1
,
e(\phi|_{\pi_{1}(P)})=0
and
e(\phi|_{\pi_{1}(\Sigma\setminus P)})=n
. Then for each connected component
\Sigma^{\prime}
of
\Sigma\setminus P
, we have
e\big(\phi|_{\pi_{1}(\Sigma^{\prime})}\big)=-\chi(\Sigma^{\prime})
. Consequently, by Proposition
2.2
,
\phi|_{\pi_{1}(\Sigma^{\prime})}
is a holonomy representation, hence Fuchsian.

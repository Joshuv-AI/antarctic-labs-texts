Minimally invasive management of colonic lipoma causing intussusception with endoloop in an adult
Authors: Jonathan R. Forrest, Urmimala Chaudhuri, Dylan Nunn, Sean-Patrick Prince, Aayan Kibria, Drew Triplett
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Intussusception is a condition in which one segment of the intestine (intussusceptum) telescopes into the lumen of an adjacent segment (intussuscipiens), potentially leading to obstruction. Giant colonic lipomas are rare and often asymptomatic, but when large enough, can serve as a lead point for intussusception. We present the case of a 62-year-old male patient who presented with nausea and vomiting. A computed tomography (CT) abdomen/pelvis without contrast showed intussusception of the ascending colon into the transverse colon without bowel obstruction, along with a lobulated fatty mass at the tip of the intussusceptum. This mass was suspected to be a colonic lipoma serving as the lead point. Due to the patient’s benign abdominal exam, emergent surgical intervention was deferred. A colonoscopy then revealed a large, 5 cm pedunculated lipoma in the transverse colon near the hepatic flexure. An Endoloop was applied and tightly cinched around the base of the lesion. He was discharged in stable condition with recommendations for close gastroenterology follow-up. The purpose of presenting this case is to highlight the utilization of endoscopy in management of colonic intussusception caused by a structural lesion, as well as the importance of considering intussusception as the cause of a patient’s nausea and vomiting (although it is a rare cause in adults). A CT scan is helpful in establishing this diagnosis and guiding management. Furthermore, if intussusception is suspected in an adult, it is important to consider a structural lesion that may be acting as a lead point, such as an intraluminal tumor or mass.

Introduction

Intussusception is a condition where a proximal segment of the intestine (intussusceptum) telescopes or invaginates into the lumen of an adjacent distal segment (intussuscipiens), which can cause obstruction. Intussusception is an uncommon cause of abdominal pain in adults, representing approximately 1%-5% of all intestinal obstructions and about 5% of all intussusception cases [1]. Most adult cases of intussusception are due to structural lesions acting as a pathological lead point, such as a neoplasm [2].

Giant colonic lipomas are benign nonepithelial masses. They are morphologically small and typically asymptomatic. However, lesions larger than 4 cm can produce symptoms and serve as lead points for intussusception [3]. Multiple imaging modalities are utilized in diagnosing intussusception, including computed tomography (CT), ultrasound, and plain film. CT is the most sensitive diagnostic modality and can characterize lesion composition and location [2,4]. Primary management of adult intussusception is surgical in the absence of bowel obstruction, ischemia, or necrosis [5]. However, selected patients with benign pathology may be candidates for conservative or minimally invasive approaches [4]. We present a rare case of colonic intussusception caused by a large colonic lipoma managed with endoscopic Endoloop ligation.

Case report

A 62-year-old male with a history of dysphagia status-post gastrostomy tube presented with nausea and vomiting. He denied rectal bleeding or changes in bowel habits. On presentation, he was afebrile with a blood pressure of 167/98 mmHg, heart rate of 103 bpm, and saturating 98% on room air. Physical examination revealed a soft, nondistended abdomen without tenderness, guarding, or rebound. Initial CT abdomen and pelvis without contrast revealed intussusception of the ascending colon into the transverse colon without bowel obstruction, ischemia, or perforation (Fig. 1). A lobulated fatty mass at the tip of the intussusceptum was identified, consistent with a previously seen lesion on prior imaging, raising suspicion for a colonic lipoma serving as the lead point. Given his benign abdominal exam and lack of concerning imaging features, emergent surgery was deferred. At his six-month follow-up, the patient no longer had any symptoms consistent with intussusception, although a repeat colonoscopy showed persistent lipoma with the Endoloop in place. Repeat colonoscopy was recommended for lipoma removal, however, he was lost to follow-up.

Discussion

Intussusception is infrequently seen in adults and comprises about 5% of all intussusception cases; roughly 1%-5% of cases are bowel obstructions [6]. Its underlying causes, clinical presentation, and management in adults differ from those in the pediatric population. Whereas intussusception in children is typically idiopathic or secondary to lymphoid hyperplasia, adult cases (80%-92%) are typically secondary to a structural lesion involving a lead point [6]. Although the most common lead point is neoplasm [7], other possible structural lesions include benign masses, lipomas, anatomical changes, or post-surgical adhesions [2]. Lipomas are benign, non-epithelial tumors of mature adipose tissue that can arise anywhere in the gastrointestinal tract, most commonly in the colon. The reported incidence of colonic lipomas is between 0.2%-4.4% [8]. Colonic lipomas are usually incidental and asymptomatic, but those >4 cm cause symptoms in 75% of patients and are the most common benign lead point for intussusception [8].

Additionally, intussusception commonly occurs at junctions between relatively fixed and more freely moving segments of the colon, such as the fully peritonealized and mobile ileum invaginating into the fixed retroperitoneal cecum [9]. Our case fit this pattern, as the location of intussusception involved the ascending colon (relatively fixed to the retroperitoneum) into the transverse colon (mobile).

Adult patients diagnosed with intussusception typically present with vague symptoms such as abdominal pain or nausea. Multiple diagnostic modalities are utilized, including CT, ultrasound, plain abdominal film, or colonoscopy [2,4,6]. CT is the most sensitive diagnostic modality and is useful in identifying pathologic lead points, evaluating the presence of vascular compromise, and predicting the likelihood of self-resolution in some cases [2,4]. CT typically demonstrates characteristic “target” or “sausage-shaped” findings and can delineate the lesion’s location, composition, and relationship to adjacent structures, with reported diagnostic accuracy ranging from 60% to over 95% [6]. Abdominal ultrasonography, although less sensitive, may also aid diagnosis, particularly when a palpable mass is present [6]. Plain film is particularly useful when a bowel obstruction is suspected. In terms of CT findings for colonic lipomas, they typically appear as well-circumscribed intraluminal masses with homogeneous fat attenuation (approximately −80 to −120 Hounsfield units), often with a thin capsule and without any solid or enhancing soft-tissue component. On magnetic resonance imaging, the lesion is characteristically hyperintense on both T1- and T2-weighted sequences, demonstrates signal loss on fat-suppressed images, and shows no suspicious enhancement.

Although adult intussusception is traditionally managed surgically due to risk of malignancy, conservative management may be considered in select cases [4]. Aydin et al. recommended that in patients with nonspecific obstructive symptoms and a short-segment intussusception, a conservative approach may be appropriate when exam and CT findings (such as absence of a structural lesion or resolution on subsequent CT scan) suggest a self-limited process [4]. If surgery is pursued, formal resection is recommended, with the procedure guided by the intussusception’s location, size, cause, and bowel viability [2].

Furthermore, in current practice, endoscopy plays an increasing role in both diagnosis and treatment [10]. Endoscopic removal can spare patients surgical complications, shorten hospital stays, and reduce treatment costs [11]. Additionally, endoscopic intervention may avert emergent surgery, allowing time to optimize patients before a planned elective operation. Endoscopic biopsy also helps confirm the diagnosis and guide management. Current endoscopic techniques utilized in the management of large colonic lipomas (>2 cm) include unroofing, endomucosal resection, dissection-based techniques, and loop-assisted-snare resection [12]. A systematic review performed by Bronswijk et al. demonstrated that although all of these techniques provided identical clinical remission rates, unroofing resulted in the least amount of adverse events [12]. However, a recent case series also demonstrated that the “loop-and-let-go” technique (utilized in our case) yielded greater than 90% success rate with no adverse events, and is thus a safe and efficacious option in the management of large colonic lipomas [13]. Success using this “loop-and-let-go” method was also illustrated in a recent case report of a large 5-cm sigmoid lipoma; the patient passed the lipoma and experienced complete resolution of symptoms following endoscopic ligation [14]. Although the use of endoscopy may carry risks such as bleeding, our case demonstrated a favorable outcome and was beneficial for our patient [11].

The prognosis of intussusception is primarily dependent on the nature of the causative lesion. Whereas the mortality in cases of adult intussusception caused by benign lesions is 8.7%, cases caused by malignant lesions is 52.4% [6]. Additionally, this condition can cause life-threatening bowel complications, including ischemia, perforation, and peritonitis. [2]. Therefore, it is important to maintain a high index of suspicion and to obtain appropriate diagnostic studies such as CT, as establishing a timely diagnosis ensures optimal patient outcomes.

Conclusion

This case highlights the diagnostic strength of CT in not only identifying cases of intussusception, but also characterizing the etiology, determining prognosis, and guiding management. We highlight minimally-invasive management of colonic lipomas utilizing endoscopic technique of large lipomas in the colon. The Endoloop technique offers a safer and more beneficial outcome for select patients compared to traditional surgical intervention.

Monodromy Rank and the Semisimple Mumford–Tate Conjecture for Hyper-Kähler Varieties
Zhichao Tang, Haitao Zou

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish two main results concerning the Mumford–Tate conjecture for hyper-Kähler varieties. First, we prove the conjecture for the semisimplified ℓ \ell -adic Galois representations attached to hyper-Kähler varieties with second Betti number b 2 ≥ 4 b_{2}\geq 4 . As a direct consequence, we deduce that the Hodge conjecture implies the Tate conjecture for powers of hyper-Kähler varieties. Second, we show that the Mumford–Tate conjecture for hyper-Kähler varieties is invariant under deformation. The proofs rely on comparing the ranks of ℓ \ell -adic algebraic monodromy groups in higher degrees to those in degree 2 2 via the theory of Frobenius tori and the Looijenga–Lunts–Verbitsky Lie algebra.

1.
Introduction

In the study of Galois representations of abelian varieties, Mumford and Tate
[
Mum66
]
formulated the following conjecture (see also
[
Ser64
]
). Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over a finitely generated field
K
over
\mathds{Q}
.

Conjecture

(
\MTC_{i}
)
.

Fix a field embedding
K\subseteq\mathds{C}
. Let
\overline{K}
be the algebraic closure of
K
inside
\mathds{C}
. For any prime
\ell
, the isomorphism
\GL(\rH^{i}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell}))\allowbreak\cong\GL(\rH^{i}_{B}(X))_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
via the Artin comparison isomorphism gives an identification

\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}(X)^{\circ}=\MT_{i}(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\,.

Here
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}(X)
is the
\ell
-adic algebraic monodromy group
of
\rH^{i}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
; and
\MT_{i}(X_{\mathds{C}})
is the
Mumford–Tate group
of the Hodge structure on the Betti cohomology
\rH^{i}_{B}(X,\mathds{Q})
.

In this paper, we propose a general approach to tackle the Mumford–Tate conjecture for higher-degree cohomologies of
hyper-Kähler varieties
, which are known to have a close relationship with abelian varieties.
As a consequence, we show that the conjecture holds after taking semisimplification of the Galois representation. Moreover, we can show the validity of
(\MTC_{i})
is deformation invariant for hyper-Kähler varieties.

1.1.
Abelian varieties and their relatives

To begin, we recall the case of abelian varieties.
Let
K
be a field in characteristic zero. For simplicity, we assume that
K\subseteq\mathds{C}
, a subfield of complex numbers.

1.1.1.

For an abelian variety
A
over
K
, a celebrated theorem due to Deligne states that every Hodge cycle on
A
is an absolute Hodge cycle (see
[
DMOS82
, I 2.11]
). Consequently, there exists a natural injective homomorphism

(1.1.1)

\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}(A)^{\circ}\hookrightarrow\MT_{i}(A_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}

for an abelian variety over
K\subseteq\mathds{C}
and any integer
0\leq i\leq 2\dim(A)
. Therefore, to verify the Mumford–Tate conjecture for an abelian variety, it suffices to show that
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}(A)
and
\MT_{i}(A_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
are isomorphic (or simply of equal dimension) as abstract
\mathds{Q}_{\ell}
-algebraic groups. However, this problem remains open, even for abelian fourfolds (see
[
MZ95
,
Pin98
]
).

Deligne observed that (
1.1.1
) can be extended to a more general setting by considering the
abelian motives
. For example, the motives of curves, Fermat hypersurfaces, or K3 surfaces (see
[
DMOS82
, II Proposition 6.26.]
). Here, “motive” may refer to a Chow motive, or a motive in a weak sense of Deligne
[
DMOS82
, II. §6]
or André
[
And96b
]
.

From a motivic perspective, one can also formulate the Mumford–Tate conjecture for Shimura varieties, viewing them as moduli spaces of Hodge structures of abelian motives with additional structure. Indeed, Deligne’s theory of canonical models provides natural Galois representations for special subvarieties of Shimura varieties, allowing the Mumford–Tate conjecture to be stated for their generic points (see e.g.,
[
UY13
,
Vas08
]
).

1.1.2.

Beyond the case of abelian varieties, the Mumford–Tate conjecture faces more essential challenges. One of the primary difficulties for general smooth projective varieties is the lack of a proof for the absolute Hodge conjecture. Consequently, the existence of the injective homomorphism (
1.1.1
) remains unestablished in general.
Without this inclusion, it is not clear how to identify these two algebraic groups via the comparison isomorphism

\rH^{i}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})\cong\rH^{i}(X_{\mathds{C}},\mathds{Q})\otimes_{\mathds{Q}}\mathds{Q}_{\ell},

even if one could show that
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}(X)^{\circ}\cong\MT_{i}(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
holds abstractly as an isomorphism of
\mathds{Q}_{\ell}
-algebraic groups.

Another challenge for the conjecture is about the semisimplicity of Galois representations.
In fact, the Mumford–Tate conjecture implies that
\mathds{Q}_{\ell}
-algebraic groups
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}(X)^{\circ}
are all reductive groups over
\mathds{Q}_{\ell}
, since the Mumford–Tate groups are intrinsically reductive.
Since the reductivity of
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}(X)^{\circ}
is equivalent to the semisimplicity of the
\ell
-adic étale cohomology
\rH^{i}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
as a
\Gal(\overline{K}/K)
-representation, it is also predicted by

–

the Tate conjecture regarding the algebraicity of Tate classes (see
[
Moo19
]
), which remains open except in a few cases; or

–

Tate’s conjecture on the semisimplicity of Frobenius over finite fields (
[
Tat65
, §3 Conjecture (d)]
) together with the Chebotarev density theorem.

We also refer to the reductivity of
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(X)^{\circ}
as the
semisimplicity conjecture
for
X
.
Like the algebraicity of Tate classes, this has been verified in only a limited number of cases. For example, considering abelian varieties over a finitely generated field over
\mathds{Q}
, it can be deduced from the Tate conjecture on the endomorphisms, proved by Faltings (see
[
FWG+92
, IV, §1, 1.1, 1.4]
).

1.2.
Hyper-Kähler varieties and the semisimple Mumford–Tate conjecture

In higher dimensions, a natural direction is to study so-called
hyper-Kähler varieties
, which are
K
-trivial varieties that bear a close relationship with abelian varieties.

Definition 1.2.1
.

A hyper-Kähler variety over
K
is a smooth projective variety
X
over
K
such that

(1)

\rH^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
; and

(2)

\rH^{0}(X,\Omega_{X/K})
is
K
-linearly spanned by a
symplectic form
1
1

1

a nowhere degenerate closed
2
-form.

\sigma\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\Omega_{X/K}^{2}
.

By definition, the presence of a symplectic form forces the dimension to be even, i.e.,
\dim X=2n
. The basic example is the surface case (
\dim X=2
): smooth projective K3 surfaces are
2
-dimensional hyper-Kähler varieties, and they are the only such examples among algebraic surfaces.

In higher dimensions (
2n\geq 4
), the picture changes substantially. Beauville
[
Bea83
]
constructed two families of deformation types:

–

varieties of
\K 3^{[n]}
-type, and

–

varieties of
\Kum_{n}
-type.

In dimensions
6
and
10

O’G99

, commonly called of
\OG 6
-type and
\OG 10

1.2.2.

The Mumford–Tate conjecture has been verified for hyper-Kähler varieties in certain special cases. For degree
2
cohomology, it was established by Tankeev
[
Tan90
,
Tan95
]
(for
\dim X=2
) and André
[
And96a
]
.

Theorem 1.2.3

(Tankeev, André)
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler variety over a finitely generated field
K
with
b_{2}(X)\geq 4
. Then
(\MTC_{2})
holds for
X
.

A natural question is whether one can extend this result to
(\MTC_{i})
for
2<i<2\dim X-2
. In this paper, we employ algebraic group–theoretic methods to reduce the problem to degree
2
, carefully analyzing the constraints on the
\ell
-adic algebraic monodromy group
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(X)
inherent to hyper-Kähler varieties.

Our starting point is a general feature of the Hodge structures of hyper-Kähler varieties.
Consider the Mumford–Tate group
\MT(X_{\mathds{C}})
associated with the full cohomology ring
\rH^{\bullet}_{B}(X_{\mathds{C}},\mathds{Q})
, viewed as a graded polarizable
\mathds{Q}
-Hodge structure. For a hyper-Kähler variety, a theorem of Verbitsky (
Theorem

3.1.9
) establishes that the natural projection to the second cohomology group,

\MT(X_{\mathds{C}})\longrightarrow\MT_{2}(X_{\mathds{C}}),

is an isogeny of degree
\leq 2
(see
Lemma

3.2.5
). Inspired by this result, we investigate the second degree projection in the Galois side. Our first main result is:

Theorem A

(
Theorem

4.3.2
)
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler variety over a finitely generated field over
\mathds{Q}
.
For any rational prime
\ell
, we have

\rk\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(X)=\rk\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2}(X)\,.

Here,
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(X)
is the
\ell
-adic algebraic monodromy group of the full cohomology ring
\rH^{\bullet}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
, and

\rk
denotes the rank of a connected algebraic group (the dimension of a maximal torus of its geometric form).

Notably,
A
allows us to avoid appealing to the abelianicity of the motive
\mathfrak{h}(X)
in order to see the injective homomorphism (
1.1.1
), at least for the reductive part.

1.2.4.

Let
\rho^{\semisimple}\colon\Gal(\overline{K}/K)\to\GL_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\left(\rH^{i}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})\right)
be the semisimplification of the Galois representation on
\rH^{i}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
, and let
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}^{\red}(X)
denote the identity component of the Zariski closure of the image of
\rho^{\semisimple}
. The subgroup
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}^{\red}(X)\subseteq\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}(X)^{\circ}
can be viewed as a Levi subgroup—a maximal reductive subgroup that, together with the unipotent radical, generates
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}(X)^{\circ}
.

A
, combined with
(\MTC_{2})
for
X
establishes an isogeny
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\red}(X)\sim\MT(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
of
\mathds{Q}_{\ell}
-algebraic groups. This implies an isomorphism

(1.2.1)

\mathfrak{g}_{\ell}^{\red}(X)\cong\mt(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}

of
\mathds{Q}_{\ell}
-Lie algebras, which corresponds to the original formulation of the Mumford–Tate conjecture in
[
Mum66
]
(specifically for abelian varieties). We further show that this isomorphism lifts to the level of algebraic groups and is compatible with the comparison isomorphism:

Theorem B

(
Theorem

5.6.4
)
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler variety over a finitely generated field
K
over the prime field
\mathds{Q}
. Suppose that the second Betti number
b_{2}(X)\geq 4
. The comparison isomorphism induces an identification of algebraic groups

\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}^{\red}(X)=\MT_{i}(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\,.

for any field embedding
K\hookrightarrow\mathds{C}
and integer
0\leq i\leq 2\dim X
.

We refer to the statement in
B
as the
semisimple Mumford–Tate conjecture
for
X
(in degree
i
).

1.2.5.

We can see that
B
has a strong consequence on the relation between Hodge conjecture and Tate conjecture. The semisimple Mumford–Tate conjecture for
X
particularly implies that

(1.2.2)

\MT(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\subseteq\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(X)^{\circ}

for any prime
\ell
.
Recall that
Hodge tensors
and
Tate tensor
are defined to be the tensors that are invariant under the action of the groups
\MT(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
and
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(X)
respectively. Thus, the natural inclusion (
1.2.2
) shows that Tate tensors in any

\rH^{\bullet}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})^{\otimes m}\otimes\rH^{\bullet}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})^{\vee,\otimes n}\otimes\mathds{Q}_{\ell}(k)

can be written as certain
\mathds{Q}_{\ell}
-linear combination of Hodge tensors in the corresponding tensor space of
\rH^{\bullet}_{B}(X_{\mathds{C}},\mathds{Q})
, under the comparison isomorphism.

With the Künneth formula, we derive the following implication relating the Hodge conjecture and the Tate conjecture for powers of hyper-Kähler varieties.

Corollary 1.2.6
.

For any self-product
X^{\times m}
of a hyper-Kähler variety
X
, over a finitely generated field
K
over the prime field
\mathds{Q}
with
b_{2}(X)\geq 4
, we have

\boxed{\textrm{Hodge conjecture in degree $2k$}}\implies\boxed{\textrm{Tate conjecture in degree $2k$}}\,.

This result also appears in
[
Flo22b
, Theorem 1.2]
, where it is obtained under the additional hypothesis that the odd-degree cohomology groups vanish when
m\geq 2
.

1.3.
Mumford–Tate conjecture in a family

Previous works in
[
FFZ21
]
and
[
Sol22
]
used the deformation principle for motivated cycles to show that the property of being “abelian” is stable under deformation for André motives arising from hyper-Kähler varieties. This particular result implies that a motivic version of the Mumford–Tate conjecture is stable under deformation.

1.3.1.

In this paper, we provide a variant of this deformation principle.
Rather than working with motives, we study the behavior of the Mumford–Tate conjecture within a family. This approach offers greater flexibility, as it only requires dealing with the local system of
\ell
-adic cohomologies. Our key observation is that
semisimplicity is preserved under geometric deformation
of hyper-Kähler varieties. Consequently, assuming
B
,
(\MTC_{i})
is also stable under deformation for any degree
i
.

Recall that two varieties
X/K
and
Y/K^{\prime}
are
deformation equivalent
if there is a smooth projective family
f\colon\mathfrak{X}\to S
over a geometrically connected smooth variety
S
(defined over a common subfield
K_{0}\subseteq K\cap K^{\prime}
), such that
X\cong\mathfrak{X}_{s}
and
Y\cong\mathfrak{X}_{s^{\prime}}
for some
s\in S(K)
and
s^{\prime}\in S(K^{\prime})
.

Theorem C
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler variety over a finitely generated field
K
over the prime field
\mathds{Q}
. Suppose that the second Betti number
b_{2}(X)\geq 4
. For any
0\leq i\leq 2\dim X
, the following statements are equivalent.

(1)

(\MTC_{i})
holds for
X
.

(2)

For all prime
\ell
, the
\Gal(\overline{K}/K)
-representation
\rH^{i}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
is semisimple.

(3)

For any hyper-Kähler variety
Y
defined over a finitely generated field
K^{\prime}
that is deformation equivalent to
X
,
(\MTC_{i})
holds for
Y/K^{\prime}
.

By combining
B
and
C
, we can provide an alternative proof for the Mumford–Tate conjecture for hyper-Kähler varieties belonging to the four deformation types (see
Section

5.7
below), a result previously proven in
[
FFZ21
]
and
[
Sol22
]
.

Corollary 1.3.2
.

If
X
is a
\K 3^{[n]}
,
\Kum_{n}
,
\OG 6
, or
\OG 10
-type variety, the
(\MTC_{i})
holds for any prime
\ell
and
0\leq i\leq 2\dim X
.

1.4.
Strategy of proof

Here, we briefly explain the ideas behind the proof of our main results.

1.4.1.
Rank comparison and
p
-adic methods

The proof of
A
relies on classical arguments in the study of
\ell
-adic algebraic monodromy for
\mathds{Q}
-compatible system
of global Galois representations, which dates back to the work of Serre
[
Ser86
]
.
Inspired by the methods used in
[
Pin98
]
, we reduce the problem to
p
-adic local Galois representations by considering
Frobenius tori
(see
Section

4.1
), establishing that

\rk(\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(X))=\rk(\mathbf{G}_{p}(X\times_{K}K_{v}))\,,

for a “general”
p
-adic place
v
of a number field
K
(see
4.1.6
).

A key hyper-Kähler input in the proof of
A

is a
p
-adic analogue of Verbitsky’s theorem for Hodge–Tate representations
of hyper-Kähler varieties over
p
-adic fields (
4.2.7
).
Following
[
IIK+25
, Theorem 6.2]
, it is formulated in terms of the
Sen Lie algebra
.
In particular, the
Looijenga–Lunts–Verbitsky (LLV) Lie algebra

[
LL97
,
Ver96
]
contains the Sen Lie algebra over
\mathds{Q}_{p}
.

1.4.2.
Big monodromy and specialization

The proof of
B
is somewhat involved and proceeds in two main steps. For simplicity, we discuss here in the level of Lie algebras.

(1)

We consider the decomposition of Mumford–Tate Lie algebra:

\mt(X_{\mathds{C}})=\mathfrak{z}\oplus\mathfrak{s}

where
\mathfrak{z}
is the center and
\mathfrak{s}
is the semisimple part.
The (local) Torelli theorem of hyper-Kähler varieties together with Zarhin’s classification for Hodge groups of polarized Hodge structures of K3-type implies that
\mathfrak{s}
is simple (over
\mathds{Q}
), and is controlled by the geometric monodromy group of a
\mathds{Q}
VHS with maximal monodromy. In other words, hyper-Kähler varieties have certain “big monodromy” property (see
5.3.4
). This allows us to identify semisimple parts of
\mathfrak{g}_{\ell}^{\red}(X)
and
\mt(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
canonically via geometric monodromy groups. The assumption that the second Betti number
b_{2}\geq 4
is crucial here, ensuring that the geometric monodromy group is sufficiently large.

(2)

When
X_{\mathds{C}}
is general in the universal family over a moduli space,
\dim\mathfrak{z}=1
(given by the weight cocharacter). This implies the compatibility of (
1.2.1
) for a generic member of the moduli space by Bogomolov’s theorem (
Lemma

2.2.11
).

If
\dim\mathfrak{z}\geq 2
, then we need to apply a specialization argument. The key ingredient is the deformation invariance of the
(twisted) LLV representation
(
Lemma

3.2.7
). This implies that the action of
\mathfrak{g}_{\ell}^{\red}(X)
on the cohomology ring—in particular, the cocharacters generating the center of
\mathfrak{g}_{\ell}^{\red}(X)
—is also always induced by the LLV representation (
5.3.8
), by reducing to the case
\dim\mathfrak{z}=1
.

1.4.3.
Semisimplicity at Galois generic fibers

C
is a direct application of the results by Cadoret in
[
Cad15
]
, which shows that the reductivity at a single fiber would imply the reductivity at all
\ell
-adic Galois generic points. For families of hyper-Kähler varieties, the reductivity at the Zariski generic fiber would also imply that at other fibers (
Theorem

5.5.8
).

1.5.
Relations with previous works and applications

André

And96a

established that the second-degree motive

is an abelian motive in the sense of

Conjecture

(Ab)

The motive
\mathfrak{h}(X)
of a hyper-Kähler variety

\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}(X)^{\circ}\hookrightarrow\MT_{i}(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
.

1.5.1.

For hyper-Kähler varieties belonging to the four known deformation types (
\K 3^{[n]}
,
\Kum_{n}
,
\OG 6
, and
\OG 10
), Conjecture (Ab) has been verified by Floccari–Fu–Zhang
[

]
and Soldatenkov
[
Sol22
]
. Using the abelianicity of motives and
[

, the authors deduced the statements in
1.3.2
.

Theorem 1.5.2
.

If
X
is of deformation type
\K 3^{[n]}
,
\Kum_{n}
,
\OG 6
, or
\OG 10
, then
(\mathrm{Ab})
holds for
X
.

The proof of

relies heavily on the specific geometry and topology of these deformation types. In contrast, our statement in

is independent of the deformation type. While it is strictly weaker than the original Mumford–Tate conjecture and does not directly address the abelianicity of motives, it provides a uniform framework for all hyper-Kähler varieties.

We also note that passing to the semisimplification is a standard and reasonable reduction in arithmetic geometry. The semisimple Mumford–Tate conjecture is sufficient for several applications.

1.5.3.

In light of the complex geometry of hyper-Kähler varieties, the following is a natural question:
How much arithmetic information about a hyper-Kähler variety can be extracted from its degree 2 cohomology?

When formulated in terms of

)

Let

2.
Motivic Galois group and compatible system

In this section, we summarize the elementary facts concerning André motives and the algebraic groups arising from them. We also fix the notation that will be frequently used in the subsequent discussion.

2.1.
André motives and realizations

The concept of motivated motives, now referred to as André motives and developed by Y. André
[
And96b
]
, offers a practical framework for analyzing algebraic groups arising from various cohomological realizations.

2.1.1.

Fix a field embedding
K\subseteq\mathds{C}
.
Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
K
. Recall that a class
\alpha\in\rH^{2i}(X_{\mathds{C}},\mathds{Q})
is
motivated
if there is a smooth projective variety
Y
over
K
and algebraic classes
\beta
,
\gamma\in\rH^{\bullet}((X\times_{K}Y)_{\mathds{C}},\mathds{Q})
such that
\alpha=p_{*}(\gamma\cup\star\beta)
(with
p\colon X\times_{K}Y\to X
the projection, and
\star
the Hodge star operator). The category
\AM_{K}
of André motives consists of objects
(X,\pi,m)
where

–

X
is a smooth projective variety over
K
,

–

\pi
is a motivated cycle on
X\times_{K}X
such that
\pi\circ\pi=\pi
, and

–

m
is an integer.

The Hom sets of
\AM_{K}
are given as

\displaystyle\Hom_{\AM_{K}}\bigl((X,\pi_{X},m),(Y,\pi_{Y},n)\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\gamma\in\rH^{m-n}\bigl((X\times_{K}Y)_{\mathds{C}},\mathds{Q}\bigr)\;\middle|\;\parbox{70.0001pt}{\centering$\gamma$ is motivated, $\gamma=\pi_{Y}\circ\gamma\circ\pi_{X}$.\@add@centering}\right\}\,.

Denote
\mathfrak{h}(X)
for the André motive
(X,\Delta_{X},0)
for a smooth projective variety over
K
. The category
\AM_{K}
satisfies the following properties (see
[
And96b
, Theorem 0.4]
):

–

It has a natural tensor product structure and is a graded Tannakian category over
\mathds{Q}
;

–

It is semisimple and polarized.

The gradation of
\AM_{K}
induces the Chow–Künneth decomposition for each smooth projective variety
X
over
K
:

\mathfrak{h}(X)\cong\bigoplus_{i=0}^{2\dim X}\mathfrak{h}^{i}(X)\,,

where
\mathfrak{h}^{i}(X)=(X,\Delta_{i},0)
, since the Künneth factor
\Delta_{i}\in\rH^{2\dim X}((X\times_{K}X)_{\mathds{C}},\mathds{Q})
is motivated (see
[
And96b
, Proposition 2.2.]
).

2.1.2.

There is a
Betti realization functor

\rH_{B}\colon\AM_{K}\longrightarrow\Vect_{\mathds{Q}}\,,

such that
\rH_{B}(\mathfrak{h}(X))=\rH^{\bullet}(X_{\mathds{C}},\mathds{Q})
is the graded
\mathds{Q}
-algebra of Betti cohomologies for any smooth projective variety
X
over
K
.

On the other hand, one may also consider the
\ell
-adic realization

\rH_{\ell}\colon\AM_{K}\longrightarrow\Vect_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\,,

such that
\rH_{\ell}(\mathfrak{h}(X))=\rH^{\bullet}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
, which are the
\ell
-adic étale cohomologies, a graded
\mathds{Q}_{\ell}
-algebra endowed with a natural
G_{K}
-action, where
G_{K}
is the absolute Galois group of
K
.
The Artin comparison provides a natural equivalence from the
\ell
-adic realization
\rH_{\ell}
to the composition of the following functors

\rH_{B,\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\colon\AM_{K}\xlongrightarrow{\rH_{B}}\Vect_{\mathds{Q}}\xlongrightarrow{-\otimes_{\mathds{Q}}\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\Vect_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}

for any prime
\ell
.

2.2.
Motivic Galois groups and Mumford–Tate conjecture

As before, we fix a subfield
K\subseteq\mathds{C}
. We denote
\mathbf{G}_{\mot}
for the motivic Galois group of
\AM_{K}
, i.e., the automorphism group of the fiber functor
\rH_{B}
, which is a reductive pro-algebraic group over
\mathds{Q}
. For a single smooth projective variety
X/K

Definition 2.2.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
K

motivic Galois group

\mathbf{G}_{\mot}(X)
of

is the automorphism group of the composition (as a fiber functor) of the Betti realization functor
\rH_{B}
on the Tannakian subcategory generated by the André motives
\mathfrak{h}(X)
and
\mathfrak{h}(X)^{\vee}\in\AM_{K}
.

2.2.2.

Since the sub-Tannakian category
\langle\mathfrak{h}(X)\rangle^{\otimes}
is semisimple, the identity component of
\mathbf{G}_{\mot}(X)
is reductive
2
2

2

To the best of our knowledge, it is not clear whether
\mathbf{G}_{\mot}(X)
is connected or not.
, denoted by
\mathbf{G}_{\mot}(X)^{\circ}
. Similarly, we can replace
\mathfrak{h}(X)
with
\mathfrak{h}^{i}(X)(m)
or an arbitrary object
\mathcal{M}\in\AM_{K}
, and the same result holds.

Remark 2.2.3
.

Since the Betti realization functor
\rH_{B}
is based on the choice of the field embedding

, the formation of the motivic Galois group
\mathbf{G}_{\mot}(\mathcal{M})
also depends on this choice. However, the group is invariant up to inner twists. See
[
And96b
, Remarks on p.25]
.

Notation 2.2.4
.

We use the subscript “
\square

\mathbf{G}_{\mot,\square}(X)
for the motivic Galois group of
\mathfrak{h}^{\square}(X)
.
A typical usage of

can be any integer

and moreover it can also be
+
and

which stand for the even degree part

and the odd degree part

\mathfrak{h}^{-}(X)\coloneqq\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\dim X-1}\mathfrak{h}^{2k+1}(X)\,,

respectively. We also follow the same rules of notation for the subsequent Galois groups arising from cohomological realizations.

2.2.5.

As mentioned above, there is a gradation on
\AM_{K}
, which is actually defined by the Betti realization functor, namely, the Betti realization functor
\rH_{B}
factors as

\AM_{K}\longrightarrow\Gr\Vect_{\mathds{Q}}\xlongrightarrow{\text{for.}}\Vect_{\mathds{Q}}\,.

The Tannakian group of the forgetful functor of the category of graded
\mathds{Q}

\mathbb{G}_{m}

\mathds{Q}

\mathbb{G}_{m}\longrightarrow\mathbf{G}_{\mot}\,,

denoted by
\omega

X
over

\mathbb{G}_{m}
on

X

2.2.6.

Let
\phi\colon\mathbb{S}\to\GL_{\mathds{R}}(V_{\mathds{R}})

\mathds{Q}
-vector space

\mathbb{S}\coloneqq\Res_{\mathds{C}/\mathds{R}}\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathds{C}}
.
The

\MT(V,\phi)
associated to
(V,\phi)

is the smallest
\mathds{Q}
-algebraic subgroup of
\GL(V)
such that
\phi(\mathbb{S})\subseteq\MT(V,\phi)(\mathds{R})
.
From the definition, it is clear that
\MT(V,\phi)
is

is polarizable, the Mumford–Tate group
\MT(V,\phi)
is, moreover,
reductive
. Denote
\HS_{\mathds{Q}}
for the Tannakian category generated by polarizable

-Hodge structures.

Let
\mathcal{M}\in\AM_{K}

\mathds{Q}
-Hodge structure. For a smooth projective variety
X
over
K
. We denote

\mathds{Q}
-Hodge structure on
\rH_{B}(\mathfrak{h}(X))=\rH^{\bullet}(X_{\mathds{C}},\mathds{Q})

It is also convenient to describe Mumford–Tate groups via the Tannakian formalism.
The forgetful functor

Then the Mumford–Tate group of a pure

In general, for any object

(2)

The Betti realization factors as

Also note that the Betti realization

since the motivated cycles are Hodge tensors. Here

–

The Tannakian category

–

If

2.2.7.

Let
K
be a field in characteristic zero
and
G_{K}\coloneqq\Gal(\overline{K}/K)
be the absolute Galois group of
K
.
For simplicity, we may assume that there is a field embedding
K\hookrightarrow\mathds{C}

Definition 2.2.8
.

Let
\rho_{\ell}\colon G_{K}\to\GL_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}(V)
be a continuous finite dimensional representation of

.
The
\ell
-adic algebraic monodromy group
of

\Image(\rho_{\ell})\subseteq\GL_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}(V)

\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(V)

With the Tannakian formalism, we can identify the group
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(V)
as the Tannakian group of the restriction of the forgetful functor from the category of
G_{K}
-representations to the category of
\mathds{Q}_{\ell}
-vector spaces
\Vect_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}

2.2.9.

For any
\mathcal{M}\in\AM_{K}

\mathds{Q}_{\ell}

\Aut^{\otimes}\left(\rH_{\ell}|_{\langle\mathcal{M}\rangle^{\otimes}}\right)\cong\Aut^{\otimes}\left(\rH_{B,\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}|_{\langle\mathcal{M}\rangle^{\otimes}}\right)=\mathbf{G}_{\mot}(\mathcal{M})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}

for any prime

(2.2.2)

\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(\mathcal{M})^{\circ}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{G}_{\mot}(\mathcal{M})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\,.

Therefore, the Mumford–Tate conjecture holds for
\mathcal{M}

\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(\mathcal{M})^{\circ}=\MT(\mathcal{M}_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}

as
\mathds{Q}_{\ell}
-subgroups of
\mathbf{G}_{\mot}(\mathcal{M})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}

2.2.10.

Keep the notation the same as in
2.2.4
. Let
V=\rH_{\ell}(\mathfrak{h}(X))
be a
G_{K}
-representation of a smooth projective variety
X
over
K
.
The image of

(resp.

An algebraicity theorem of Bogomolov

The following fact is well-known as a consequence of Bogomolov’s algebraicity theorem and the Weil conjecture (see

Lemma 2.2.11
.

If

is a finitely generated field over
\mathds{Q}

(2.2.3)

\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\subseteq Z(\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(X)^{\circ})\subset\mathbf{G}_{\mot}(X)_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}

\omega(\mathbb{G}_{m})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}

\mathbf{G}_{\mot}(X)_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}

Proof.

It suffices to show that the group of homotheties satisfies

We may assume that

is a number field by Serre’s spreading out argument.
Take
v
to be a place of
K
such that
X
has good reduction at
v

\Phi_{v}\curvearrowright\rH^{i}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}_{v}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})

2.3.
Compatibility of systems of Galois representations

In this section, we focus on the case where
K
is a
number field
, and the
\mathds{Q}
-compatibility
for a system of
G_{K}
-representations.

2.3.1.

Consider a profinite group
\mathscr{G}
,
and a system of continuous
n
-dimensional representations

\rho_{\bullet}=\left\{\rho_{\ell}\colon\mathscr{G}\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathds{Q}_{\ell})\right\}_{\ell\in\mathbf{L}}\,,

indexed by a set
\mathbf{L}
of rational primes.
Suppose that
\mathscr{G}
is endowed with a
dense
subset of “Frobenius elements”,

\{F_{v}\}_{v\in\Sigma}\subset\mathscr{G}\,.

For example, if
\mathscr{G}=G_{K}
is the absolute Galois group of a number field
K
and
F_{v}=\Frob_{v}
is the Frobenius representative for the place
v
of
K
, then all the Frobenius conjugacy classes ranging over all finite places form a dense subset by the Chebotarev density theorem.

The system
\rho_{\bullet}
is called a
\mathds{Q}
-compatible system
of
\ell
-adic representations if there is a subset
\mathscr{X}\subset\Sigma\times\mathbf{L}
such that

(1)

For every
v\in\Sigma
,
(v,\ell)\in\mathscr{X}
for all but at most finitely many
\ell\in\mathbf{L}
.

(2)

For any primes
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{m}\in\mathbf{L}
, the set
\{F_{v}\;|\;(v,\ell_{i})\in\mathscr{X}\;\text{for all }i=1,\dots,m\}
is dense in
\mathscr{G}
.

(3)

For every
v\in\Sigma
,
the characteristic polynomial
ch(\rho_{\ell}(F_{v}))\in\mathds{Q}[t]
, and is independent of
\ell
.

2.3.2.

In the geometric context, we consider the following situation.

–

Let
\mathcal{M}=\mathfrak{h}^{\square}(X)
be a motive associated to a smooth proper variety
X
over
K
.

–

\Sigma
is the following subset of the places of
K
:

(2.3.1)

\left\{\parbox{170.00026pt}{non-archimedean places of $K$ such that $K$ is unramified at $v$; and $X$ has good reduction at $v$.}\right\}\,,

where
K
is a number field.

–

Let
F_{v}=\Frob_{v}\in G_{K}
be a Frobenius lift, which is unique as
K
is unramified at
v
.

Since
X
has good reduction at
v
, the system of Galois representations

\rho_{\bullet}=\left\{\rho_{\ell}\colon G_{K}\longrightarrow\GL_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\left(\rH_{\ell}(\mathcal{M})\right)\right\}_{\ell\in\mathbf{L}}

is unramified at all
v\in\Sigma
by smooth proper base changes when
\ell\neq\cha(k_{v})
.

It is worth noticing that the system
\rho_{\bullet}
is
\mathds{Q}
-compatible, and can be described by
p
-adic cohomology theory. The
p
-adic étale realization
\rH_{p}(\mathcal{M}_{v})
is
crystalline
as a
G_{K_{v}}
-representation for
\mathcal{M}_{v}=\mathfrak{h}^{\square}(X_{v})
, i.e.,

\dim_{\mathds{Q}_{p}}\rH_{p}(\mathcal{M}_{v})=\dim_{K_{0}}\left(\rH_{p}(\mathcal{M}_{v})\otimes_{\mathds{Q}_{p}}\mathrm{B}_{\crys}\right)^{G_{K_{v}}}\,,

where
\mathrm{B}_{\crys}
is the Fontaine’s crystalline period ring of
K
.
Fontaine’s formalism provides a functor

\rD_{\crys}\colon\Rep_{\mathds{Q}_{p}}^{\crys}(G_{K_{v}})\longrightarrow\MF_{\varphi,{K_{v}}},\quad V\rightsquigarrow\left(V\otimes_{\mathds{Q}_{p}}\mathrm{B}_{\crys}\right)^{G_{K_{v}}}

from the category of crystalline representations of
G_{K_{v}}
to the category of filtered
\varphi
-modules over
K
, which is
fully faithful
. Let
K_{0}
be the maximal unramified subfield of
K_{v}
, i.e., the fraction field of the ring of Witt vectors
W(\mathds{F}_{q})
. Let
\sigma
be the Frobenius on
K_{0}
. For
v\in\Sigma_{\max}
, as
v
is unramified, we have
K_{v}=K_{0}
.
Recall that the filtered
\varphi
-module

\rD_{\crys}(\mathcal{M}_{v})\coloneqq\rD_{\crys}(\rH_{p}(\mathcal{M}_{v}))\,,

is a
K_{0}
-vector space, endowed with a
\sigma
-semilinear Frobenius
\varphi_{v}
. Recall that being
\sigma
-semilinear means that

\varphi_{v}(ax)=\sigma(a)\varphi_{v}(x),\quad\forall a\in K_{0}\text{ and }x\in\rD_{\crys}(\mathcal{M}_{v})\,.

Let
m=[\mathds{F}_{q}:\mathds{F}_{p}]
. The power
\Phi_{v}\coloneqq\varphi_{v}^{m}
is a
K_{0}
-linear automorphism of
\rD_{\crys}(\mathcal{M}_{v})
. As
X
has good reduction at
v
, then
\rD_{\crys}(\mathcal{M}_{v})
(together with Frobenius structure
\varphi_{v}
) is the same as the crystalline cohomology of the special fiber. According to Katz–Messing
[
KM74
, Theorem 1]
, the eigenvalues of
\Phi_{v}
are equal to those of
\rho_{\bullet}(F_{v})
.

Remark 2.3.3
.

In general, it is not clear whether the system of
\ell
-adic realizations of an André motive
\mathcal{M}
is
\mathds{Q}
-compatible or not, except when
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}^{\square}(X)
is a submotive cut out by an algebraic cycle.

2.3.4.

Let
\rho_{\bullet}
be a
\mathds{Q}
-compatible system of representations of
G_{K}
.
Denote
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}
the
\ell
-adic algebraic monodromy group of
\rho_{\ell}(G_{K})
for each prime
\ell\in\mathbf{L}
for simplicity.
As before,
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\circ}
is the connected component of the identity.
The set of connected components
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}/\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\circ}
is finite, so there is a finite field extension
K^{\conn}
over
K
, corresponding to the finite index subgroup
\mathscr{G}=\rho_{\ell}^{-1}(\mathbf{G}^{\circ}_{\ell}(\mathds{Q}_{\ell}))\subseteq G_{K}
, such that

\rho_{\ell}(G_{K^{\conn}})\subset\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\circ}(\mathds{Q}_{\ell})

is Zariski dense. A priori, it is unclear whether there exists a single finite extension that works uniformly for all
\ell\in\mathbf{L}
when
\mathbf{L}
is infinite. Nevertheless, we have the following theorem of Serre (see
[
Ser86
]
, or alternatively, for example, Larsen–Pink
[
LP92
, Proposition 6.14]
).

Theorem 2.3.5
.

The open subgroup of finite index

\mathscr{G}\subset G_{K}

is independent of
\ell
.
In particular, the groups
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}/\mathbf{G}^{\circ}_{\ell}
for different
\ell
are canonically isomorphic; if
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}
is connected for some
\ell
, then it is so for all
\ell
.

Remark 2.3.6
.

Therefore, given a
\mathds{Q}
-compatible system of representations
\rho_{\bullet}
(even when
\mathbf{L}
is infinite), we can always assume that
K^{\conn}=K
after a finite extension by
Theorem

2.3.5
.

Remark 2.3.7
.

Let
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}
be the
\ell
-adic algebraic monodromy group of the motive
\mathfrak{h}(X)
. For any submotive
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}(X)
, e.g.,
\mathfrak{h}^{i}(X)
, there is a surjection
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(\mathcal{M})
. Therefore, if
K=K^{\conn}
, then
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(\mathcal{M})
is also connected.

2.3.8.

Finally, we make some remarks on the case where
K
is not a number field.
If
[K:\mathds{Q}]=\infty
, then one can also define the (quasi-)compatibility of the representations of
G_{K}
after choosing a model of
K
(see
[
LP92
, §6]
or Commelin
[
Com19
, §3]
).
Let
K
be a finitely generated field over
\mathds{Q}
, and
B
a normal scheme of finite type over
\mathds{Z}
with the function field
K
, called a normal model of
K
. For a closed point
s\in B
, there is an exact sequence

1\longrightarrow I_{s}\longrightarrow G_{K_{s}}\longrightarrow G_{k(s)}\longrightarrow 1\,,

where
K_{s}
is the function field of the Henselization
B^{h}_{s}
at
s
. An element
F_{s}
in
G_{K}
is a
Frobenius element with respect to
s
if there is a field embedding
K\hookrightarrow K_{s}
such that
F_{s}
is the restriction of a Frobenius element in
K_{s}
. The Chebotarev density theorem holds: For any finite étale Galois covering
\widetilde{B}\to B
with
\widetilde{B}
integral and the Galois group
G
, the following subset of the closed points of
B

\left\{x\in B^{\cl}\;\middle|\;\Conj_{G}(F_{s})=C\right\}

is of density
\frac{\lvert C\rvert}{\lvert G\rvert}
for any conjugacy class
C\subset G
(see
[
Pin97
, Theorem B.9]
). Then we can set
\Sigma
to be a Zariski dense subset of
B^{\cl}
as discussed in
2.3.1
. When
K
is a number field, the only model of
K
is
\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{K})
with
\mathcal{O}_{K}
the ring of integers in
K
, and the definition of the Frobenius element here coincides with the one for a number field.

However, it is unclear whether a system of
\ell
-adic representations coming from geometry is compatible with those given in
2.3.2
. Fortunately, we can still reduce many problems on
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}
to the number field case, by the classical spreading-out argument as in
[
Ser86
, §1]
. Namely, there are infinitely many closed points
s\in B^{\cl}
such that
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,s}\cong\mathbf{G}_{\ell}
(see
[
Ser97
, §10.6 Theorem]
). In particular, we see that
Theorem

2.3.5
holds when
K
is an arbitrary finitely generated field over
\mathds{Q}
.

3.
LLV representations of hyper-Kähler varieties

3.1.
Looijenga–Lunts–Verbitsky Lie algebra

We now briefly review the basic properties of the
Looijenga–Lunts–Verbitsky Lie algebra
(LLV algebra) introduced by Verbitsky
[
Ver96
]
and Looijenga–Lunts
[
LL97
]
.

3.1.1.

Let
K\subseteq\mathds{C}
be a field embeddable to
\mathds{C}
,
X
be a smooth projective variety over
K
as before.
Denote
\rH(X)\coloneqq\rH^{\bullet}_{\square}(X)
a cohomological realization of
\mathfrak{h}(X)
, with
\square=B,\ell,\dR
or
\pst
. Let
E
be the field of coefficients of
\rH(-)
.

The LLV algebra
\mathfrak{g}
for
X/K
and the associated LLV decomposition of
\rH(X)
generalize the usual Hard Lefschetz
\fsl_{2}
-decomposition of the cohomology for an ample class
\omega
on
X
. Recall that the class
\omega
defines two operators on the cohomology, one is the Lefschetz operator
L_{\omega}=\omega\cup-
given by the cup product,
and the other is the dual Lefschetz operator
\Lambda_{\omega}=\star^{-1}L_{\omega}\star
, where
\star
is the Hodge star operator.
Operators
L_{\omega}
and
\Lambda_{\omega}
generate an
\fsl_{2}\subset\mathfrak{gl}(\rH(X))
acting on
\rH(X)
,
and the Hard Lefschetz is the same as the existence of the
\fsl_{2}
-decomposition of the cohomology.
A more formal framework that avoids the use of the Hodge star operator has
been formulated in
[
LL97
]
, where the key observation is the following identity

[L_{\omega},\Lambda_{\omega}]=h\,,

with
h
the shifted degree operator

(3.1.1)

h\colon\rH(X)\longrightarrow\rH(X),\;\;x\longmapsto(k-\dim X)x,\;\;\text{for $x\in\rH^{k}(X)$}\,.

It is then clear that
\{L_{\omega},h,\Lambda_{\omega}\}
forms an
\fsl_{2}
-triple.
Moreover, the operator
L_{x}=x\cup-
is well-defined for any cohomology class
x\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X)
, while
h
is independent of any choice of
x
.
Due to the Jacobson–Morozov Theorem, the existence (and thus the uniqueness) of an operator
\Lambda_{x}
that completes the pair
\{L_{x},h\}
into an
\fsl_{2}
-triple is an open algebraic condition on the classes
x\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X)
.
In other words, the dual Lefschetz operator
\Lambda_{x}
can be defined for almost all classes
x
, which can go beyond the ample (or even Kähler) classes and is in fact independent of the complex structure of
X
.
Therefore, one can define a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
(over
\mathds{Q}
) containing all these operators such that it is a diffeomorphism invariant of
X(\mathds{C})
.
The action of these operators on
\rH(X)
extends to the whole Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, and therefore, any
\fsl_{2}
-Lefschetz decomposition factors through
\mathfrak{g}
according to the definition.
Note that the projectivity assumption on
X
is only required to make sure that the set of
x\in\rH^{2}(X)
for which
\Lambda_{x}
is defined is a
non-empty
(and thus Zariski dense) subset.

More generally, we formulate the definition over an arbitrary field
E
as follows.

Definition 3.1.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
K
.
The
Looijenga–Lunts–Verbitsky (LLV) Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}(X)_{E}
of
X
is the smallest
E
-Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}(\rH(X))
generated by all
\fsl_{2}
-triples

\{(L_{x},h,\Lambda_{x})\}_{x}\,,

where
x\in\rH^{2}(X)
is any element satisfying the Hard Lefschetz property.

Remark 3.1.3
.

The definition of LLV Lie algebra is actually valid for any graded Frobenius–Lefschetz algebra as stated in
[
Ver96
, Definition 1.1, Definition 1.2]
. Thus one may also consider the LLV Lie algebra for the Betti cohomology ring of a Kähler manifold.

3.1.4.

The LLV algebra
\mathfrak{g}(X)_{\mathds{Q}}
for the Betti realization
\rH^{\bullet}_{B}(X)
is a semisimple Lie algebra defined over
\mathds{Q}
(cf.
[
LL97
, (1.9)]
). Moreover, we have the comparison isomorphism

\mathfrak{g}(X)_{\mathds{Q}}\otimes_{\mathds{Q}}E\cong\mathfrak{g}(X)_{E}

given in
[
IIK+25
, Example 3.4.]
.
The case where
X
is a hyper-Kähler variety is of the most interest to us and will be assumed in the subsequent part of this paper. As
\mathfrak{g}(X)_{E}
is invariant for any field embedding
K\hookrightarrow\mathds{C}
with
E=\mathds{Q}_{\ell}
, and the real form satisfies

\mathfrak{g}(X)_{\mathds{Q}}\otimes\mathds{R}\cong\so(4,b_{2}-2)\,,

when
X
is hyper-Kähler (see
Theorem

3.1.7
below), we can see that the
\mathds{Q}
-form
\mathfrak{g}(X)_{\mathds{Q}}
is independent of the choice of the field embedding
K\hookrightarrow\mathds{C}
.

For simplicity, we will abbreviate the notation into
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}(X)_{E}
,
if there is no confusion.

3.1.5.

The adjoint action of the shifted degree operator
h\in\mathfrak{g}
induces an eigenspace decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
.
In the case of hyper-Kähler varieties,
\rH(X)
is of Jordan-type as a graded Frobenius–Lefschetz algebra by
[
LL97
, Lemma 4.2., Proposition 4.4.]
, i.e., the eigenspace decomposition for
h
is of the form

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{2}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-2}\,.

In particular, the
0
-eigenspace
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is a reductive subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
and admits a decomposition

\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\overline{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus E\cdot h\,,

where
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
is the semisimple part of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
that satisfies
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}=[\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{0}]
,
and the center
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{0})
is
1
-dimensional and is spanned by the shifted degree operator
h
.
The Lie subalgebra
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
is called the
reduced LLV algebra
of
X
.

3.1.6.

Note that
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{0}
consists of degree
0
operators, and thus the induced
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
-action on
\rH(X)
preserves the cohomology degree.
In other words, for any integer
0\leq k\leq 2\dim X
, there is an associated representation

\rho_{k}^{\LLV}\colon\overline{\mathfrak{g}}\longrightarrow\End(\rH^{k}(X))\,.

In particular, there is an action of
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
on
\rH^{2}(X)
restricted from
\mathfrak{g}
.
On the other hand, it acts as a derivation with respect to the cup product:

(3.1.2)

e.(x\cup y)=(e.x)\cup y+x\cup(e.y),\qquad\text{for}\;e\in\overline{\mathfrak{g}},\;x,y\in\rH(X)\,.

One can also see that the action of
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
respects the Beauville–Bogomolov–Fujiki form
\overline{q}
on the second cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X)
,
and therefore there is an inclusion

(3.1.3)

\overline{\mathfrak{g}}\subset\so(\rH^{2}(X),\bar{q})\,.

In fact, the above inclusion (
3.1.3
) is an equality so that
\rH^{2}(X)
is the standard representation of
\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}
.
More specifically, one has the following results.

Theorem 3.1.7

(
[
Ver96
, Theorem 2.3]
,
[
LL97
, Theorem 4.5.]
)
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler variety over
K
.
Consider the quadratic space

\widetilde{\rH}(X)=\rH^{2}(X)\oplus E^{\oplus 2},\qquad q=\bar{q}\oplus\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\,,

which is called the
Mukai extension
of
\rH^{2}(X)
associated to the cohomology of
X
.
Then the (reduced) LLV Lie algebras of
X
can be determined as follows:

(3.1.4)

\overline{\mathfrak{g}}\cong\so(\rH^{2}(X),\overline{q})\,,\quad\mathfrak{g}\cong\so(\widetilde{\rH}(X),q)\,.

Moreover, let
r=\lfloor b_{2}(X)/2\rfloor
.
Then the LLV algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a simple Lie algebra of type
\mathrm{B}_{r+1}
or
\mathrm{D}_{r+1}
, depending on the parity of
b_{2}(X)
,
with the reduced form
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
a simple Lie algebra of type
\mathrm{B}_{r}
or
\mathrm{D}_{r}
. Moreover,

(3.1.5)

\bar{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{R}}\cong\mathfrak{so}(3,b_{2}(X)-3)\,,\quad\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}\cong\mathfrak{so}(4,b_{2}(X)-2)\,.

3.1.8.

Let
\mt(X_{\mathds{C}})
be the Mumford–Tate algebra of complex projective variety
X_{\mathds{C}}
, i.e., the Lie algebra of the Mumford–Tate group
\MT(X_{\mathds{C}})
. Similarly,
\overline{\mt}(X_{\mathds{C}})
is defined as the special Mumford–Tate Lie algebra of
X_{\mathds{C}}
. It is important to note that the Weil operator lies in the LLV Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, as observed by Verbitsky in
[
Ver96
]
.
Here we recall a refined version given in
[
GKLR22
]
.

Theorem 3.1.9

(Verbitsky)
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler variety over
K
. Then
\overline{\mt}(X_{\mathds{C}})\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathds{Q}}
in
\End_{\mathds{Q}}(\rH_{B}(X))
.

Proof.

According to
[
GKLR22
, Proposition 2.24]
, the Weil operator for the Hodge structure on
\rH^{\bullet}(X_{\mathds{C}},\mathds{Q})
lies in the real form of the reduced LLV Lie algebra
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathds{R}}
. As the special Mumford–Tate algebra
\overline{\mt}(X_{\mathds{C}})
is the smallest
\mathds{Q}
-Lie algebra whose
\mathds{C}
-form contains the Weil operator by the definition,
\overline{\mt}(X_{\mathds{C}})\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathds{Q}}
as
\mathds{Q}
-Lie algebras.
∎

Example 3.1.10
.

If
X
is a complex
K3
surface, the full cohomology
\rH^{\bullet}(X,\mathds{Q})
is naturally endowed with the Mukai pairing, which is isomorphic to the Mukai extension
\widetilde{\rH}(X,\mathds{Q})
for
\rH^{2}(X,\mathds{Q})
.
In this particular case, the representation-theoretic explanation is quite clear,
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathds{Q})
is the standard representation of
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}(X)_{\mathds{Q}}
, and
\rH^{\bullet}(X,\mathbb{Q})
is the standard representation of
\mathfrak{g}(X)_{\mathds{Q}}
.
Also note that
\mathfrak{g}(X)_{\mathds{Q}}
is realized as the special Mumford–Tate algebra of a generic (non-algebraic) K3 surface.

In the case of Kummer surfaces, the situation is a bit different.
Since a Kummer surface is a K3 surface, it still has
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathfrak{so}(3,19)
.
However, the construction makes sure that there are
16
independent
(-2)
-curves obtained as the blow-ups, and therefore its generic special Mumford–Tate algebra has the real form
\mathfrak{so}(3,3)
, which is the LLV Lie algebra of the corresponding
2
-dimensional complex torus.

3.2.
(Twisted) LLV representations

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler variety over
K
of dimension
2n
, and
\mathfrak{g}
be the LLV Lie algebra of the cohomological realization
\rH(X)
.

3.2.1.

Here we recall the integrated representations of the LLV representations introduced by Floccari
[
Flo22a
, §2.1]
.
The connected algebraic groups over
E
corresponding to the Lie algebras
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{0}\subset\mathfrak{g}
are the Spin groups

\Spin(\rH^{2}(X),\overline{q})\subset\GSpin(\rH^{2}(X),\overline{q})\subset\Spin(\widetilde{\rH}(X),q)\,.

In the following, we would mainly focus on the action of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
or the reduced part
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
which preserves cohomological degrees.

Notation 3.2.2
.

For simplicity of notation, we set

–

\GSpin=\GSpin(\rH^{2}(X),\overline{q})
;

–

\Spin=\Spin(\rH^{2}(X),\overline{q})
; and

–

\SO=\SO(\rH^{2}(X),\overline{q})
.

In the remaining parts of this paper, we will also use the subscript “
\ell
” for any prime to denote the base change of these groups to the corresponding completion field
\mathds{Q}_{\ell}
.

Under the identifications in (
3.1.4
), the LLV representation

\rho_{i}^{\LLV}\colon\mathfrak{g}_{0}\longrightarrow\End_{E}(\rH^{i}(X))

integrates to a group homomorphism

\rho_{i}^{\LLV}\colon\Spin\longrightarrow\GL_{E}\left(\rH^{i}(X)\right).

The kernel
\{\delta,\delta^{-1}\}=\mu_{2}\subset\Spin
of the universal covering
\Spin\to\SO
acts on
\rH^{i}(X)
via
\rho^{\LLV}_{i}(\delta)=(-1)^{i}|_{\rH^{i}(X)}
(see
[
Ver99
, Corollary 8.2]
and
[
KSV19
, Theorem A.10.]
).
When
i=2k
, the LLV representation
\rho_{2k}^{\LLV}
factors through the orthogonal group by a faithful representation

\SO\hookrightarrow\GL_{E}(\rH^{2k}(X))\,,\quad\forall 1\leq k\leq 2n\,,

which is the standard representation of
\SO
when
k=1
. We still denote by
\rho_{2k}^{\LLV}
for representations of
\SO
.

3.2.3.

In the usual setting of the LLV representation, the degree operator
h\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
acts on
\rH^{i}(X)
as the scalar
i-2n
, which a shift of the usual cohomology degree by the dimension of
X
. To study the interaction between the LLV representation and various cohomological realizations, it is also convenient to consider the
twisted LLV representation

\rho^{\tw}_{i}\colon\mathfrak{g}_{0}^{\tw}\coloneqq\overline{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus Eh^{\deg}\longrightarrow\gl_{E}(\rH^{i}(X))\,

where
h^{\deg}=h+\dim(X)\id
is the standard degree operator, i.e.,
h^{\deg}|_{\rH^{i}(X)}=i
. As
E
-Lie algebras,
\mathfrak{g}_{0}^{\tw}\cong\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, and the integrated representation is

(3.2.1)

\rho_{i}^{\tw}\colon\GSpin\longrightarrow\GL_{E}(\rH^{i}(X))\,,

such that

–

\rho_{i}^{\tw}(z)(x)=z^{i}\cdot x
for any
z
in the central torus
\mathbb{G}_{m}\subset\GSpin
; and

–

\rho_{i}^{\tw}|_{\Spin}=\rho^{\LLV}_{i}
.

This implies that
\rho_{2k+1}^{\tw}
is faithful when
\rH^{2k+1}(X)\neq 0
(see
[
Flo22a
, Lemma 2.6]
and its proof for details).

Remark 3.2.4
.

For a complex hyper-Kähler variety
X
,

Theorem

3.1.9
implies that

\MT(X)\subseteq\rho^{\tw}\left(\GSpin(\rH^{2}(X,\mathds{Q}),\overline{q})\right)\,.

The following is a restatement of Verbitsky’s theorem (
Theorem

3.1.9
) in the algebraic group level.

Lemma 3.2.5
.

Suppose that
X
is a complex hyper-Kähler variety.

(1)

The projections in even degrees induce isomorphism

\pi_{2k}\colon\MT_{+}(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}\MT_{2k}(X)
for all
0\leq k\leq\dim X
. Moreover, we have the following commutative diagram

Suppose that the odd part
\rH^{-}(X)\neq 0
. Then the projection

is a central isogeny of degree

\ker(\pi_{2})=\rho^{\tw}_{2}(\mu_{2})\subseteq\MT(X)

Proof.

These statements are actually paraphrases of
[

∎

3.2.6.

From the definition of the LLV Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, it follows immediately that
\mathfrak{g}
is invariant under deformation, since it is completely determined by the algebra structure of
\rH(X)
. We also note that the (twisted) LLV representation is also preserved under deformation.

Lemma 3.2.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}\to S
be a smooth family of hyper-Kähler varieties over a smooth connected variety
S
.
Let
\eta\rightsquigarrow s
be an étale path of points in
S
.
We have the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\GL_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}(\rH_{\ell}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{X}_{\overline{s}}))}

\leftarrow
\rightarrow
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho^{\tw}_{\eta}}

Proof.

It is sufficient to assume that
\eta\rightsquigarrow s
is a specialization of points in

.
The general theory of étale cohomology ensures that a specialization isomorphism

\specialize_{\eta,s}\colon\rH_{\ell}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{X}_{\overline{s}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle\sim}}{{\smash{\longrightarrow}\rule{0.0pt}{1.72218pt}}}\rH_{\ell}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{X}_{\overline{\eta}})

is an isomorphism between the Frobenius graded

-algebras. Thus it is equivariant under the action of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
or
\mathfrak{g}_{0}^{\tw}
.
∎

3.3.
Motivic lifting of LLV representation

In this subsection, we collect some facts on defect groups of hyper-Kähler varieties, which are ingredients in
[
Flo22b
,
Flo22a
,
FFZ21
]
.

3.3.1.

For any integer
0<i<2\dim X
, there is a natural surjective homomorphism

\pi_{i}\colon\mathbf{G}_{\mot}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbf{G}_{\mot,i}(X)\,.

induced by the inclusion
\mathfrak{h}^{i}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{h}(X)
in
\AM_{K}
.
Under the second degree projection, there are short exact sequences of motivic Galois groups:

(3.3.1)

\displaystyle 1\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{\mot}(X)\longrightarrow\mathbf{G}_{\mot,2}(X)\longrightarrow 1\,,

(3.3.2)

\displaystyle 1\longrightarrow P_{+}\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{\mot,+}(X)\longrightarrow\mathbf{G}_{\mot,2}(X)\longrightarrow 1\,.

The kernel
P
(resp.
P_{+}
) is called the
defect group
(resp.
even defect group
) of
X
.

Lemma 3.3.2
.

Let
\Aut_{\alg}(\rH(X))
be the subgroup of
\GL_{E}(\rH(X))
generated by
E
-linear automorphisms preserving the graded algebra structure on
\rH(X)
. Then
P_{E}\in\Aut_{\alg}(\rH(X))
and commutes with
\rho^{\tw}(\GSpin_{E})\subseteq\Aut_{\alg}(\rH(X))
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to consider the case
\rH(X)
is the Betti realization
\rH^{\bullet}(X_{\mathds{C}},\mathds{Q})
and
E=\mathds{Q}
.
See Lemma 6.8 and the proof of Theorem 6.9 in
[
FFZ21
]
.
∎

3.3.3.

According to
[
Flo22b
, Proposition 4.1]
, there is a decomposition of motivic Galois groups

(3.3.3)

\mathbf{G}_{\mot,+}(X)=\mathbf{G}_{\mot,2}(X)\times P_{+}

given by a splitting
\sigma\colon\mathbf{G}_{\mot,2}(X)\to\mathbf{G}_{\mot,+}(X)
such that
\sigma(\mathbf{G}_{\mot,2}(X))=\MT_{+}(X)
, and
\mathbf{G}_{\mot,2}(X)
commutes with
P
in
\mathbf{G}_{\mot,+}(X)
.
This implies that the connected component
P_{+}^{\circ}
is reductive.

The decomposition of motivic Galois groups (
3.3.3
) implies that, for any
\mathcal{M}\in\langle\mathfrak{h}^{+}(X)\rangle^{\otimes}
, the invariant part
\mathcal{M}^{P_{+}}\in\langle\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X)\rangle^{\otimes}
which exists by the reductivity of
P_{+}^{\circ}
. In other words, there is an injective homomorphism in
\AM_{K}
:

(3.3.4)

\mathcal{M}^{P_{+}}\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_{m,n}\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X)^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X)^{\vee,\otimes n}\,.

If
P_{+}
is trivial, then we can take
\mathcal{M}=\mathfrak{h}^{2k}(X)
, and in particular the conjecture (Ab) holds for
\mathfrak{h}^{2k}(X)
by the André’s theorem.

Moreover, the embedding (
3.3.4
) can be chosen to be compatible with the LLV representation.
By Tannakian duality for
\langle\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X)\rangle^{\otimes}
, (
3.3.4
) gives an injective homomorphism of finite-dimensional
\mathbf{G}_{\mot,2}(X)
-representations on its Betti realization.
By definition of the splitting
\sigma
, the
\mathbf{G}_{\mot,2}(X)
-action on
\rH_{B}(\mathcal{M}^{P_{+}})
is that of
\MT_{+}(X_{\mathds{C}})
, which factors through the twisted LLV representation. Hence we can choose (
3.3.4
) as an injective homomorphism of motives, corresponding to an injective homomorphism of
\GSpin
-representations

\rH_{B}(\mathcal{M}^{P_{+}})\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_{m,n}\rH^{2}_{B}(X)^{\otimes m}\otimes\rH^{2}_{B}(X)^{\vee,\otimes n}\,.

3.3.4.

According to
[
FFZ21
, Theorem 6.9]
3
3

3

It is wrongly claimed in the published version of

4.1.
Maximal tori of algebraic monodromy groups

We retain the notation established in

In

(Serre)

Let

\mathds{Q}

In

(1)

Firstly, we assume that

\ell

(2)

The compatibility conditions only depend on the semisimple part of the elements

4.2.
Hodge cocharacters and Sen theory

In this subsection, we assume that
K_{v}
is a non-archimedean local field with a perfect residue field

\cha(k)=p>0
. Let

be the maximal unramified extension of

in
\overline{K}_{v}

4.2.1.

Let
\mathds{C}_{p}\coloneqq\widehat{\overline{K}}_{v}
be the
p
-adic completion of an algebraic closure
K_{v}\subseteq\overline{K}_{v}
. Recall that a
p
-adic Galois representation

(4.2.1)

V\otimes_{\mathds{Q}_{p}}\mathds{C}_{p}=\bigoplus_{r\in\mathds{Z}}V(r)\,,

such that
\sigma|_{V(r)}=(\chi_{p})^{r}(\sigma)
for any
\sigma\in\Gal(\overline{K}_{v}/K^{\ur}_{v})

is the
p
-adic cyclotomic character of
G_{K_{v}}
.
The integers

4.2.1
) with

of

.
In particular, a (potentially) crystalline representation is Hodge–Tate.

The Hodge–Tate decomposition (
4.2.1
) determines a cocharacter

Hodge–Tate cocharacter
of
V
.

Definition 4.2.2
.

Let

be a Hodge–Tate representation of

.

–

The
\mathds{C}_{p}
-linear operator

\Theta|_{V(q)}=-q\id_{V(q)}\,,

where
V\otimes_{\mathds{Q}_{p}}\mathds{C}_{p}=\bigoplus_{q}V(q)
is the Hodge–Tate decomposition, and
\Theta
is called the
Sen operator
of
V
.

–

The
Sen Lie algebra

\mathfrak{s}(V)
of

is the
smallest

\mathds{Q}_{p}
-Lie subalgebra of
\End_{\mathds{Q}_{p}}(V)
such that
\Theta\in\mathfrak{s}(V)\otimes_{\mathds{Q}_{p}}\mathds{C}_{p}
.

Sen’s theory
shows that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}(V)
is algebraic
4
4

4

and can be described by the inertia representation when

is Hodge–Tate.
Consider the short exact sequence

is the inertia subgroup of

.

Let
\rho\colon G_{K_{v}}\to\GL_{\mathds{Q}_{p}}(V)

Denote

for the Lie algebra of

By the identity

4.3.

Recall that the

.

5.3.
Hyper-Kähler varieties with real multiplication

Let

be the transcendental part, i.e., the
smallest

\mathds{Q}
-sub Hodge structure such that

contains the

\mathds{C}
-vector space
\rH^{2,0}(X_{\mathds{C}})
. Then the

is a number field by
[
Zar83
, Theorem 1.6]
, and is equipped with an involution
*
on
E
such that

q(ex,y)=q(x,e^{*}y),\quad\forall e\in E\text{ and }x,y\in T(X)\,.

Definition 5.3.1
.

A hyper-Kähler variety
X
has
real multiplication
if the endomorphism field
E
of
X_{\mathds{C}}
is
totally real
.

Remark 5.3.2
.

In the literature, the condition that
X
has real multiplication typically also includes the requirement that
E\neq\mathds{Q}
. For the sake of simplicity, we will omit this assumption here.

5.3.3.

Let

. The
\ell
-adic algebraic monodromy group
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2}(X)
can be classified into two cases, by Zarhin’s results in

and

for

(or Theorem 2.6 in
[
Moo17
]

(
\SO
)

If the involution
*
on

is trivial, i.e.,

is totally real, then we have the almost direct product

\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2}(X)^{\circ}\cong\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\cdot\SO_{E_{\ell}/\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}(T(X),\widetilde{q}_{X})

with

viewed as an
E
-vector space and
\widetilde{q}
the
E
-bilinear form on

extending the BBF-form
q_{X}
, and
\SO_{E_{\ell}/\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}(T(X),\widetilde{q}_{X})
is viewed as a
\mathds{Q}_{\ell}
-algebraic group by taking the Weil restriction.

(
\U
)

If the involution
*
on
E
is non-trivial, then we have the almost direct product

\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2}(X)^{\circ}\cong\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\cdot\U_{E_{\ell}/\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}(T(X),\widetilde{q}_{X})

\widetilde{q}_{X}
the

In both cases, the subgroup

In the case (

In the case (

\left(\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\cdot\Res^{(1)}_{E/E_{0}}(\mathbb{G}_{m})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\right)\cdot\SU_{E_{\ell}}(T(X),\widetilde{q}_{X})\,,

where the

-dimensional

-torus

5.3.9
implies the Levi subgroups chosen in
5.2.4
and
5.3.4
are canonical.

5.4.
Connectivity of
P_{\ell,+}

Consider the short exact sequence

(5.4.1)

1\longrightarrow P_{\ell,+}\longrightarrow\mathbf{G}_{\ell,+}(X)\xlongrightarrow{\pi_{\ell,2}}\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2}(X)\longrightarrow 1\,.

The kernel
P_{\ell,+}
is called the
\ell
-adic even defect group
of
X
.
Since
P_{\ell,+}^{\circ}
is the unipotent radical of the connected algebraic group
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,+}(X)
by
Theorem

4.3.2
, we have a semi-direct product

\mathbf{G}_{\ell,+}(X)=\mathbf{G}_{\ell,+}^{\red}(X)\rtimes P_{\ell,+}^{\circ}

which is unique up to
P_{\ell,+}^{\circ}
-conjugation (see
[
Hoc81
, Theorem 4.3]
). The projection
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,+}^{\red}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2}(X)

is an isogeny with the kernel equal to the group of connected components
\pi_{0}(P_{\ell,+})
.

In this subsection, we will prove that
P_{\ell,+}
is connected and that the semi-direct product is actually a product, which is compatible with the decomposition (
3.3.3
).

5.4.1.

As a reductive group, the second degree
\ell
-adic monodromy group
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2}
admits a decomposition

\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2}=Z(\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2})\cdot\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2}^{\der}\,,

where
Z(G_{\ell,2})
is the center and
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2}^{\der}
is the derived subgroup of
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2}
.
The projection
\pi_{\ell,2}
induces surjective homomorphisms

Z(\mathbf{G}_{\ell,+})\longrightarrow Z(\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2})\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbf{G}_{\ell,+}^{\der}\longrightarrow\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2}^{\der}\,.

To obtain a splitting of
\pi_{\ell,2}
, it is sufficient to construct splittings for these two homomorphisms separately.

Firstly, we deal with the case that
X
has real multiplication.

Theorem 5.4.2
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler variety over a finitely generated field
K
with
b_{2}(X)\geq 4
. Suppose that
X
has real multiplication. There is a direct product

\mathbf{G}_{\ell,+}(X)=\mathbf{G}_{\ell,+}^{\red}\times P_{\ell,+}

with
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,+}^{\red}\cong\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2}(X)^{\circ}
, such that

\mathbf{G}_{\ell,+}^{\red}=\sigma_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\left(\mathbf{G}_{\mot,2}(X)\right)=\MT_{+}(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}

as a subgroup of
\mathbf{G}_{\mot,+}(X)
by the inclusion
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,+}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathbf{G}_{\mot,+}(X)
.

Proof.

5.2.6
together with
5.3.6
provide an injective homomorphism

\sigma_{\ell}\colon\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2}(X)\to\mathbf{G}_{\ell,+}(X)

such that
\pi_{\ell,2}\circ\sigma_{\ell}=\id_{\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2}}
and
\sigma_{\ell}(\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2}(X))=\MT_{+}(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
. By
Lemma

3.3.2
and
Remark

3.2.4
(or
5.3.8
), we can see that
P_{\ell,+}\subseteq P_{+,\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
commutes with
\sigma_{\ell}(\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2}(X))
. Therefore,
\sigma_{\ell}
induces the direct product decomposition as required.
∎

5.4.3.

If
E
is a CM field (Case (U)), the center of
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2}(X)
receives a non-trivial contribution from the special Mumford–Tate group. Consequently, constructing the splitting
\sigma_{\ell}
for the center is not as straightforward a priori as in

can be established by reducing to the case of real multiplication.

5.5.
Semisimple Mumford–Tate conjecture and semisimplicity

In this subsection, we will split the usual Mumford–Tate conjecture into two parts.

5.5.1.

The Mumford–Tate group
\MT_{i}(X_{\mathds{C}})
of a smooth projective complex variety
X_{\mathds{C}}
is a reductive group over
\mathds{Q}
by the existence of Hodge–Riemann relations.
It follows that the
\ell
-adic algebraic monodromy group
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}(X)^{\circ}
is also reductive, assuming
(\MTC_{i})
.

The reductivity of
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}(X)^{\circ}
implies that the Galois representation
\rH^{i}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
is semisimple.
As we already mentioned in the introduction, the semisimplicity of the Galois representation
V_{\ell,i}
is still widely open, except for the Galois representations arising from
\mathcal{M}\in\AM_{K}(\sAb)
(see
[
FWG+92
, VI, §3, Theorem 1 (a)]
for the case of abelian varieties). It is also well-known to be a consequence of Tate’s conjecture, i.e., the cycle class maps

\cl_{\ell}^{i}\colon\CH^{i}(Y_{\overline{F}})\otimes_{\mathds{Q}}\mathds{Q}_{\ell}\longrightarrow\rH^{2i}_{\et}(Y_{\overline{F}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell}(i))

are surjective onto the subgroups of Tate classes for all smooth projective varieties over a finitely generated field
F
over
\mathds{Q}
, by
[
Moo19
, Theorem 1]
.

5.5.2.

To simplify the problem into only the cases of reductive groups, consider the following process.

Let
\rho^{\semisimple}
be the semisimplification of a Galois representation
\rho\colon G_{K}\to\GL_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\left(V\right)
, i.e., a semisimple representation of
G_{K}
(on
V
) such that

\Tr\rho^{\semisimple}(g)=\Tr\rho(g)\quad\forall g\in G_{K}\,.

In the following we also denote
V^{\semisimple}
for the
G_{K}
-module structure on
V
given by
\rho^{\semisimple}
. Let
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}^{\red}(X)
be the connected component of the Zariski closure of the image of

\rho^{\semisimple}_{\ell,i}\colon G_{K}\longrightarrow\GL_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}(V_{\ell,i})\,.

The connected algebraic group
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}^{\red}(X)
is reductive, as the faithful representation
V^{\semisimple}_{\ell,i}
is completely reducible. The reductive group
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}^{\red}(X)
is a Levi subgroup of
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}^{\circ}(X)
.
In particular, there is a natural surjective homomorphism
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}^{\circ}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}^{\red}(X)
whose kernel is unipotent. The following is well known to experts. We record it here for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 5.5.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
K
, and
0<i<2\dim X
be a positive integer.
The Mumford–Tate conjecture for
X
in degree
i
holds if and only if

(SS)

V_{\ell,i}
is semisimple as a
G_{K}
-module, and

(\SMTC)

\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}^{\red}(X)\cong\MT_{i}(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
.

Proof.

As discussed in
5.5.1
, the Mumford–Tate conjecture for
\mathfrak{h}^{i}(X)
implies (SS) and (SMTC).

Now, assume the condition (SMTC), then it is sufficient to see that the condition (SS) would imply that the identity component
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}(X)^{\circ}
of
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}(X)
is reductive.
Since the image
\rho(G_{K})
is Zariski dense in
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}(X)(\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
,
V_{\ell,i}
is semisimple as a
G_{K}
-module if and only if it is semisimple as a
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}(X)^{\circ}
-module. If the unipotent radical
R_{\ell}
of
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}(X)^{\circ}
is non-trivial, then
V_{\ell,i}^{R_{\ell}}\neq 0
. As
V_{\ell,i}
is assumed semisimple by (SS), we may take the decomposition

V_{\ell,i}=\bigoplus_{k}V_{k}

into irreducible representations. For all
V_{k}
, the invariant space
V_{k}^{R_{\ell}}\neq 0
as
R_{\ell}
is unipotent, and is stable under
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}(X)^{\circ}
-action since
R_{\ell}
is a normal subgroup. This implies that
V_{k}=V_{k}^{R_{\ell}}
. Therefore
R_{\ell}
lies in the kernel of
\rho\colon\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}(X)^{\circ}\to\GL(V_{\ell,i})
, which implies that
R_{\ell}=0
.
∎

If the condition (SMTC) in
Lemma

5.5.3
holds for
X
and a positive integer
0<i<2\dim X
, then we say that the
semisimple Mumford–Tate conjecture
holds for
X
in degree
i
.

Lemma 5.5.4
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler variety over a finitely generated field
K
. Then the semisimplicity
(SS)
holds for
X
in degree
2k
if and only if the action of
P_{\ell,+}
on
\rH^{2k}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
is trivial.

Proof.

Consider the surjective homomorphism
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,+}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2k}
. As
P_{\ell,+}\subseteq\mathbf{G}_{\ell,+}
is the unipotent radical, the image of
P_{\ell,+}
is also the unipotent radical of
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2k}
(see
[
Hoc81
, Chapter VIII, Theorem 4.4]
). By the definition, the action of
P_{\ell,+}
on
\rH^{2k}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
is trivial if and only if the image of
P_{\ell,+}
is trivial in
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2k}
is trivial, which is also equivalent to the reductivity of
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2k}
. So we can conclude.
∎

5.5.5.

It is also common to consider the following condition:

(FSS)

Almost all Frobenius elements
F_{v}\in G_{K}
act semisimply on
V_{\ell,i}
.

Clearly, for any Galois representation, (FSS) implies (SS) by the Chebotarev density theorem when
K
is a finitely generated field over
\mathds{Q}
.
Conversely, it is not clear whether
V_{\ell,i}^{\semisimple}
is (FSS) or not in general. Fortunately, the opposite direction is true for hyper-Kähler varieties.

Proposition 5.5.6
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler variety over a finitely generated field
K
, with
b_{2}\geq 4
. The semisimplification
\rH^{2k}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})^{\semisimple}
is Frobenius-semisimple
(FSS)
for any
0\leq k\leq\dim X
.

Proof.

With the Tannaka duality, we can see
\rH^{2k}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})^{\semisimple}\in\langle\rH^{2}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})\rangle
as
G_{K}
-representations by
Theorem

5.4.4
. Then the
G_{K}
-representation
\rH^{2}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
is (FSS) by the Kuga–Satake construction in
[
And96a
]
. Therefore,
\rH^{2k}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})^{\semisimple}
is (FSS) as being (FSS) is stable under tensor product and subquotient.
∎

Applying the results of Cadoret

5.6.
Proof of main results

In this section, we will focus on proving
B
and
C
. As before, we consider a hyper-Kähler variety
X
over a finitely generated field
K
. We also assume that
K=K^{\conn}
for
\mathfrak{h}(X)
.

5.6.1.

Theorem

5.4.4
together with
Lemma

3.2.5
implies that there are abstract isomorphisms of
\mathds{Q}_{\ell}
-algebraic groups

\mathbf{G}_{\ell,+}^{\red}(X)\cong\MT_{+}(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}

via the second degree projection. Then to verify the semisimple Mumford–Tate conjecture for
\mathfrak{h}^{+}(X)
, it remains to see that these two
\mathds{Q}_{\ell}
-algebraic subgroups actually coincide as subgroups in the motivic Galois group
\mathbf{G}_{\mot,+}(X)_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
. The case with real multiplication can be deduced from the discussions in
Section

5.4
.

5.6.2.

As a first step toward the remaining cases, we can verify the semisimple Mumford–Tate conjecture for hyper-Kähler varieties over CM-type via LLV representation.

Proposition 5.6.3
.

Let
X
over
K
be a hyper-Kähler variety with
b_{2}(X)\geq 4
. Then we have

Z\left(\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\red}(X)\right)^{\circ}=Z\left(\MT(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\right)^{\circ}

inside
\mathbf{G}_{\mot}(X)_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
. In particular, if
X
is of CM-type, i.e.,
\MT_{2}(X_{\mathds{C}})
is abelian, then the semisimple Mumford–Tate conjecture holds.

Proof.

Let
Z_{\ell}\coloneqq Z\left(\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\red}(X)\right)^{\circ}
and
Z=Z\left(\MT(X_{\mathds{C}})\right)^{\circ}
. Use the notations in
3.2.2
. Let
T_{\Spin}
be a maximal torus of the spin group
\Spin
and
T_{\SO}
its image under the universal covering
\Spin\to\SO
.

Firstly, we show that
Z_{\ell}\subseteq Z_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
.
Let
\mu\in X_{*}(Z_{\ell})
be any cocharacter. It is sufficient to see that the image of
\mu
is contained in
Z_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
. Since the image of the weight cocharacter is contained in
\MT(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\cap\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\red}(X)
, we may assume that
\mu
is not the weight cocharacter. Under this assumption, the image of
\mu
lies in
\rho^{\LLV}(T_{\Spin,\mathds{Q}_{\ell}})
by
5.3.8
.
Take
\mu_{2}
to be the composition

\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\xlongrightarrow{\mu}Z_{\ell}\subseteq\mathbf{G}_{\mot}(X)_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\xlongrightarrow{\pi_{2}}Z_{\ell,2}\subseteq\mathbf{G}_{\mot,2}(X)_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\,.

Under the assumption that
\mu
is not the weight cocharacter, the image of
\mu_{2}
lies inside
T_{\SO,\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
; and
\mu
is the unique cocharacter of
\Spin_{\ell}
that lifts
\mu_{2}\colon\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\to\SO_{\ell}
.

Let
\widetilde{\mu}\colon\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\to Z_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
be the unique quasi-cocharacter that lifts
\mu_{2}
along the isogeny

Z_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\twoheadrightarrow Z_{2,\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\subseteq\mathbf{G}_{\mot,2}(X_{\mathds{C}})\,.

We have
\MT(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\subseteq\rho^{\tw}(\GSpin_{\ell})
as in
3.2.4
. Thus
\widetilde{\mu}=\mu\in X_{*}(T_{\Spin,\mathds{Q}_{\ell}})_{\mathds{Q}}
by the uniqueness. Here we view
\mu
as a quasi-cocharacter. This implies that the image of
\mu
is contained inside
Z_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
as required.

For the inclusion
Z_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\subseteq Z_{\ell}
, the argument is the same, just switching the roles of
\MT(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
and
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\red}(X)
.
∎

Now, we are ready to prove the semisimple Mumford–Tate conjecture for general hyper-Kähler varieties.

Theorem 5.6.4
.

For a hyper-Kähler variety
X
over a finitely generated field
K
over prime field
\mathds{Q}
, with
b_{2}(X)\geq 4
, the semisimple Mumford–Tate conjecture holds for
X
.
Namely, for any integer
0\leq i\leq 2\dim X
, the comparison isomorphism induces an identification of algebraic groups

\mathbf{G}^{\red}_{\ell,i}(X)=\MT_{i}(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\,.

for any prime
\ell
and
0\leq i\leq 2\dim X
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{G}^{\der}_{\ell}
be the derived subgroup of
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\red}(X)
. Consider the decomposition

\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\red}(X)=Z\left(\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\red}(X)\right)^{\circ}\cdot\mathbf{G}^{\der}_{\ell}\,.

–

5.2.6
implies
\mathbf{G}^{\der}_{\ell}=\MT(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}^{\der}
in
\mathbf{G}_{\mot}(X)_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
.

–

5.6.3
implies that

Z\left(\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\red}(X)\right)^{\circ}=Z\left(\MT(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\right)^{\circ}\subseteq\mathbf{G}_{\mot}(X)_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}.

Hence
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\red}(X)=\MT(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
in
\mathbf{G}_{\mot}(X)_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
, i.e., the semisimple Mumford–Tate conjecture holds for
\mathfrak{h}(X)
, which in particular implies the Mumford–Tate conjecture for each even degree

: Under the projection
\pi_{i}\colon\mathbf{G}_{\mot}(X)_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\to\mathbf{G}_{\mot,i}(X)_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
, the image of
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\red}(X)
is a Levi subgroup
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,i}^{\red}(X)
, which is the same as the Mumford–Tate group
\MT_{i}(X_{\mathds{C}})
.
∎

Remark 5.6.5
.

We can actually obtain a natural isomorphism

and its image

5.7.
Semisimplicity for known examples

For hyper-Kähler varieties belonging to the four established deformation types, semisimplicity can be deduced just from their cohomological structure, without using the full power of the information of their motives.

Using
Theorem

5.5.8
, we may therefore restrict our attention to any concrete construction representing each of these deformation types.

5.7.1.

Let
S
be a smooth projective surface over a finitely generated field
K
.
By classical results from Nakajima
[
Nak97
]
and Grojnowski
[
Gro96
]
(see also
[
Nak99
, Chapter 8]
), the direct sum of the
\ell
-adic étale cohomologies:

\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\rH^{4n-\bullet}_{\et}(S^{[n]}_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})

is an irreducible representation of the Heisenberg superalgebra generated by
\rH^{4-\bullet}_{\et}\left(S_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell}\right)
, the (co)homology ring of the surface
S
. Moreover, the highest weight vector is given by the class
[\Spec(K)]
of
\rH^{0}_{\et}(S_{\overline{K}}^{[0]},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
.
The action of the Heisenberg superalgebra is given by some classes

P_{\alpha}[i]=\varpi_{*}\left(\Pi^{*}\alpha\cap[P[i]]\right)\in\rH_{\et}((S^{[n-i]}\times S^{[n]})_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell}),

where
\alpha\in\rH^{\bullet}_{\et}(S_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
,
i\in\mathds{Z}
,
[P[i]]
is an algebraic
(2n-i+1)
-cycle on
S^{[n-i]}\times S^{[n]}\times S
, and

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{[n-i]}\times S^{[n]}\times S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{[n-i]}\times S^{[n]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
\leftarrow
\rightarrow
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varpi}
\leftarrow
\rightarrow
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi}

are the projections respectively. From this, we can deduce that, for any
0\leq k\leq 4n
,

\rH_{\et}^{k}(S_{\overline{K}}^{[n]},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})\in\langle\rH^{\bullet}_{\et}(S_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})\rangle^{\otimes}

as a
G_{K}
-representation after a finite field extension.

If

is a K3 or abelian surface, then it is well-known that the

-representation

\rH^{\bullet}_{\et}(S_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})

is semisimple by
[

]
, which implies the semisimplicity of
\rH_{\et}^{k}(S_{\overline{K}}^{[n]},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
for all

in this case.

Remark 5.7.2

In fact, we have motivic decompositions for Hilbert schemes of points on a smooth algebraic surface

dCM02

]
and

FFZ21

to establish the abelianicity of the André motives of K3
[n]
-type varieties and
\Kum_{n}
-varieties.

Remark 5.7.3
.

We thank Floccari for pointing out that for the Hilbert scheme of
n
-points

on a K3 surface

\mathfrak{g}_{0}

If

5.7.6.

We can also use the LLV representation to study the semisimplicity of Galois representations for hyper-Kähler varieties, especially for those of
\OG 10
-type.

Since the LLV algebra
\mathfrak{g}\cong\so(\widetilde{\rH}(X))
is semisimple, one may consider the decomposition of the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
\rH^{*}(X)
into irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-modules

(5.7.1)

\rH^{*}(X)\otimes_{E}\overline{E}=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Delta^{+}_{r}}V_{\mu}^{\oplus m_{\mu}}\,.

where

,

and

.
Here

is the set of all the weights of the standard representation

5.7.1

of

.

Example 5.7.7
.

According to the Weyl construction,
V_{(n)}
is the “largest” irreducible subrepresentation of

.
Moreover, Verbitsky has shown that for a hyper-Kähler variety

of dimension

is isomorphic to the irreducible
\mathfrak{g}

of the highest weight

.
Then

is called the

of
\rH(X)
.
Actually, according to the construction,
\SH(X)\subseteq\rH^{+}(X)
.

Since
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple,
the cohomology admits a
\mathfrak{g}
-module decomposition

(5.7.2)

\rH(X)=V_{(n)}\oplus V^{\prime}\,.

For arbitrary hyper-Kähler manifold

that is,

According to

\rH(X)=V_{(5)}\oplus V_{(2,2)}\,,

i.e.,

Lemma 5.7.9
.

For any
L\in\mathfrak{g}_{\ell}
, we have
gLg^{-1}=L
for all
g\in P_{\ell}
.

Proof.

For any
x\in\rH^{2}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
satisfying the Hard Lefschetz property, the Lefschetz operator
L_{x}
commutes with
\rho_{\ell}(g),\forall g\in P_{\ell}
,
since
g
acts trivially on
x
and is compatible with the cup product on
\rH^{\bullet}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
according to the definition.
Moreover,
\Lambda_{x}
also commutes with those
g
since the Galois action preserves the shifted degree operator
h
and
(L_{x},h,\Lambda_{x})
is the unique
\fsl_{2}
-triple that extends
L_{x}
.
∎

Proposition 5.7.10
.

If all irreducible factors in the LLV decomposition (
5.7.1
) of
X
have multiplicity
\leq 1
, then the Galois representations of
\rH^{\bullet}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
is semisimple.

Proof.

As
P_{\ell}^{\circ}
is the unipotent radical of
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(X)
by
Theorem

4.3.2
, it is sufficient to see that
P_{\ell}(\overline{\mathds{Q}}_{\ell})=\pi_{0}\left(P_{\ell}\right)
is finite.
For any irreducible factor
V_{\mu}
and
g\in P_{\ell}(\overline{\mathds{Q}}_{\ell})
, we can see
g(V_{\mu})\subseteq\rH(X)_{\overline{\mathds{Q}}_{\ell}}
is also a
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
-representation by
Lemma

5.7.9
, which is isomorphic to
V_{\mu}
. Under the assumption that
m_{\mu}=1
, we have
g(V_{\mu})=V_{\mu}\subseteq\rH(X)_{\overline{\mathds{Q}}}
. Thus the action of any
g\in P_{\ell}(\overline{\mathds{Q}}_{\ell})
induces an
\overline{\mathds{Q}}_{\ell}
-linear automorphism of
V_{\mu}
as a
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
-representation. By Schur lemma, we can see the action
g\circlearrowright V_{\mu}
is given via the multiplication by some
c_{\mu}\neq 0\in\overline{\mathds{Q}}_{\ell}
. However, as the identity component
P_{\ell}^{\circ}
is unipotent, the action
g\circlearrowright\rH(X)_{\overline{\mathds{Q}}}
is quasi-unipotent by
Theorem

4.3.2
, which forces
c^{k}_{\mu}=1
for all
V_{\mu}
, with
k=\lvert\pi_{0}(P_{\ell})\rvert
. Thus any
g\in P_{\ell}(\overline{\mathds{Q}}_{\ell})
is torsion of order

, and

is thus a finite group scheme. ∎

As we have seen in

, the factors in LLV decomposition of an

-varieties.

6.1.
Arithmetic Nagai conjecture

Recall that a hyper-Kähler variety
X
over a
p
-adic local field
K_{v}
is of
Type I reduction
if

\rH^{2}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell^{\prime}})

is potentially unramified for
some
prime
\ell^{\prime}\neq p
(and thus for all primes
\neq p
by Corollary 5.3 in
[
IIK+25
]
).

The semisimple Mumford–Tate conjecture enables us to extend
[
IIK+25
, Corollary 4.3]
, which was originally established for the four known deformation types, to any hyper-Kähler variety with
b_{2}\geq 4
. Consequently, we can establish the
arithmetic Nagai conjecture
(Conjectures 1.3 and 1.4 in
loc. cit.
) in this general framework.

Theorem 6.1.1
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler variety over a
p
-adic local field
K_{v}
with
b_{2}(X)\geq 4
and Type I reduction. Then
\rH^{i}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
is potentially unramified for any
0\leq i\leq 4n
and prime
\ell\neq p
.

Firstly, we note that

Theorem

5.6.4
implies the
\ell
-adic local Galois representation of a hyper-Kähler variety (after Frobenius semisimplification) is also partially controlled by the Mumford–Tate group.

Proposition 6.1.2
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler variety over a
p
-adic
local field

K_{v}
with
b_{2}(X)\geq 4
. Then any Levi subgroup
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\red}(X)
of the
\ell
-adic algebraic monodromy group
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(X)
satisfies

\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\red}(X)\subseteq\MT(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}

for any field embedding
K_{v}\hookrightarrow\mathds{C}
.

Proof.

Fix a field embedding
K_{v}\hookrightarrow\mathds{C}
. The standard spreading out argument shows that there is a smooth projective variety
X_{0}
over a finitely generated subfield
K\subseteq K_{v}
such that
X_{0}\times_{K}K_{v}\cong X
as
K_{v}
-varieties. It is clear that
X_{0}
is a hyper-Kähler variety over
K
, thus
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\red}(X_{0})\cong\MT(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
by
Theorem

5.6.4
for the field embedding
K\hookrightarrow K_{v}\hookrightarrow\mathds{C}
. On the other hand, we have the natural injective homomorphism
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\red}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\red}(X_{0})
along the field extension
K\subseteq K_{v}
. This implies that
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\red}(X)\subseteq\MT(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
.
∎

Remark 6.1.3
.

In contrast to the global field case, the
\ell
-adic monodromy group
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(X)
(
\ell\neq p
) for
X
over a local field
K_{v}
is not expected to be reductive. This is because the inertia representation
\rho_{\ell}|_{I_{v}}
could probably contribute a normal subgroup of
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(X)
via the short exact sequence

1\longrightarrow I_{v}\longrightarrow G_{K_{v}}\longrightarrow G_{\mathds{F}_{q}}\longrightarrow 1

when
\rH^{\bullet}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}_{v}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
is not (potentially) unramified, which is quasi-unipotent by Grothendieck’s monodromy theorem.

For this reason, the Levi subgroup
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\red}(X)
is not the
\ell
-adic algebraic monodromy group of the Frobenius-semisimplification in general.

Proof of
Theorem

6.1.1
.

Keep the notation same as in the proof of
6.1.2
.
Let
N_{\ell}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\ell}(X)
be the
\ell
-adic monodromy operator of
\rH^{\bullet}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}_{v}},\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
for a prime
\ell\neq p
. Under the assumption that
X
has Type I reduction, we have
\pi_{\ell,2}(N_{\ell})=N_{\ell,2}=0
. In other words,
N_{\ell}\in\Lie(P_{\ell})
, where
P_{\ell}
is the
\ell
-adic defect group of
X_{0}
via the inclusion
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(X_{0})
.
6.1.2
implies the semisimplification

\rho_{\ell}(\Frob_{v})^{\semisimple}\in\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\red}(X)\subseteq\MT(X_{\mathds{C}})_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}(\mathds{Q}_{\ell})

of a lift of Frobenius
\Frob_{v}\in G_{K_{v}}
, whose adjoint action on
\Lie(P_{\ell})\subseteq\Lie(P)_{\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
is trivial by
5.3.8
and
Lemma

3.3.2
.
However, we have the equality

p\cdot\rho_{\ell}(\Frob_{v})^{\semisimple}\circ N_{\ell}=N_{\ell}\circ\rho_{\ell}(\Frob_{v})^{\semisimple}

from the fundamental relation between Frobenius action and
\ell

∎

6.2.
Maximality of Galois action

In this final subsection, we prove
1.5.5
for hyper-Kähler varieties. We retain the notation introduced in
1.5.4
.

Let
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(X)^{\simplyconnected}
denote the simply connected cover of the adjoint group of
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(X)
. Recall that
\Gamma^{\simplyconnected}_{\ell}
is defined as the preimage of
\rho^{\semisimple}(G_{K})\subset\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\red}(X)(\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
under the composition of the simply connected covering map
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(X)^{\simplyconnected}\to\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\red,\der}(X)
and the natural isogeny
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}^{\red,\der}(X)\to\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(X)^{\ad}
.

Theorem 6.2.1
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler variety over a finitely generated field
K
with
b_{2}(X)\geq 4
. Then the
\ell
-adic Lie group
\Gamma_{\ell}^{\simplyconnected}\subseteq\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(X)^{\simplyconnected}(\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
is a hyperspecial maximal compact subgroup for all sufficiently large primes
\ell
.

Proof.

4.3.3
implies that the projection
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(X)\longrightarrow\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2}(X)
induces an isomorphism of simply connected
\mathds{Q}_{\ell}
-algebraic groups

\pi_{2}^{\simplyconnected}\colon\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(X)^{\simplyconnected}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle\sim}}{{\smash{\longrightarrow}\rule{0.0pt}{1.72218pt}}}\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2}(X)^{\simplyconnected}\,.

According to
[
HL20
, Theorem 1.3 (b)]
, the compact subgroup
\Gamma_{\ell,2}^{\simplyconnected}
is hyperspecial and maximal in
\mathbf{G}_{\ell,2}(X)^{\simplyconnected}(\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
. Therefore, its preimage
\Gamma_{\ell}^{\simplyconnected}=(\pi_{2}^{\simplyconnected})^{-1}(\Gamma_{\ell,2}^{\simplyconnected})
is also a hyperspecial maximal compact subgroup in
\mathbf{G}_{\ell}(X)^{\simplyconnected}(\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
via this isomorphism.
∎

Remark 6.2.2
.

In
[
HL20
]
, the authors also address the case where

. If the base field

is a number field, the assumption

in the proof of

Objective

The human oral cavity is a remarkably diverse and dynamic microbial environment [1, 2], where members of the genus Streptococcus function as primary colonizers and play central roles in microbial succession, biofilm formation, and the maintenance of physiological homeostasis [3–5]. Although extensive clinical and microbiological research has focused on major oral pathogens [3, 6], the genomic landscape and ecological roles of many closely related streptococcal species remain insufficiently characterized [7–9]. In particular, limited availability of high-quality reference genomes restricts strain-level resolution and hampers comprehensive understanding of microevolutionary processes and functional diversity within the oral microbiome [10–13].

In this study, we report the whole-genome sequencing of Streptococcus sp. strain KHUD_013, which was isolated from the human buccal mucosa. To contextualize its genomic relatedness within the genus, we conducted a genome-wide comparative analysis based on whole-genome sequence data. Average Nucleotide Identity (ANI) analysis demonstrated that strain KHUD_013 shares less than 95% genomic similarity with its closest phylogenetic relative, Streptococcus ilei, based on comparison with the reference genome (GCA_000479335.1) [14], suggesting that the strain represents a genomically distinct species within the genus. Given this close relationship and the availability of publicly accessible genomes, S. ilei was selected as the most appropriate reference for comparative genomic analysis.

Accordingly, the primary objective of this Data Note is to provide a complete and high-quality genome sequence of strain KHUD_013 and to place it in a comparative genomic context. By expanding the genomic resources available for oral streptococci, this dataset supports future investigations into strain-level genomic diversity, evolutionary divergence, and potential niche-associated adaptations within the complex ecosystem of the human oral cavity.

Data description

The strain KHUD_013 was obtained from the buccal mucosa of a healthy Korean female adult and cultivated on Fastidious Anaerobe Agar (MB cell, Republic of Korea) supplemented with 0.25% (w/v) porcine gastric mucin, 10% fetal bovine serum, and 5% defibrinated sheep blood under anaerobic conditions at 37°C. Genomic DNA was isolated using the Wizard HMW DNA Extraction Kit (Promega, USA). Preliminary taxonomic placement was assessed by sequencing the full-length 16S rRNA gene (~ 1.5 kb) amplified with universal bacterial primers (forward: 5’-AGAGTTTGATCCTGGCTCAG-3’; reverse: 5’-GGTTACCTTGTTACGACTT-3’) [15], followed by BLAST analysis against the GenBank database.

Whole-genome sequencing of strain KHUD_013 was conducted using the PacBio Revio platform. A Single-Molecule Real-Time (SMRT)bell library was prepared according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Pacific Biosciences). A single SMRT cell run generated 89,500 high-fidelity (HiFi) reads, with an average read length of 6,960 bp and an N50 value of 8,411 bp (Data file 1). De novo genome assembly was performed using the Microbial Genome Analysis pipeline in SMRT Link v25.2.0.266456 with default settings. Gene annotation was performed using the NCBI Prokaryotic Genome Annotation Pipeline (PGAP).

The complete genome of KHUD_013 consists of a single circular chromosome of 1,988,919 bp with a G + C content of 42.5% (Data file 2 and 3). The genome encodes 1,827 protein-coding genes, four rRNA operons (5 S, 16 S, 23 S) and 62 tRNAs. Genome completeness was assessed using Benchmarking Universal Single-Copy Orthologs (BUSCO) v5.5.0 [16] with the lactobacillales_odb10 dataset (304 genomes, 402 BUSCOs), yielding a completeness score of 98.8% (Data file 4). The genome of strain KHUD_013 exhibited the highest similarity to that of S. ilei type strain, accession number: GCA_000479335.1, with an ANI value of 94.39% and an alignment coverage of 86.55%, as determined by ANI-based taxonomic analysis.

Comparative genomic analysis was conducted using four publicly available S. ilei genomes: I-G2 and I-P16 (human small intestinal fluid, Republic of Korea), SS2016 (ileostomy effluent of a colorectal cancer patient, Republic of Korea), and OM06-9 (feces, China) (Data file 5). Strain KHUD_013 harbored 179 unique gene clusters containing 179 genes (Data file 6). Functional annotation revealed that genes assigned to replication, recombination, and repair (COG category L) were the most abundant among the annotated categories, followed by those related to cell wall/membrane biogenesis (M) and transcription (K). Additional genes were associated with defense mechanisms (V) and amino acid or lipid transport and metabolism (E and I). A substantial proportion of genes were not assigned to any COG category, while genes classified as category S represented conserved proteins with unknown functions, which may be associated with strain-specific or niche-adaptive features.

The complete genome sequence of Streptococcus sp. strain KHUD_013 provides a high-quality genomic resource that expands reference data for closely related oral streptococci. The closed genome assembly and comparative analyses with S. ilei enable assessment of genome organization, gene content, and strain-specific features at the whole-genome level. The associated data files and publicly available sequence datasets support future comparative, taxonomic, and functional genomic studies of oral Streptococcus. Limitation

While our ANI results provide a high-level taxonomic resolution, the proximity to the 95% threshold means that the classification could be sensitive to the specific algorithm and parameters used. We recognize that slight variations between ANI calculators (e.g., FastANI vs. OrthoANI) can occur in such borderline cases. This limitation highlights the need for a more comprehensive multi-locus approach or core-genome analysis in future studies to further validate its taxonomic status.

Perfect Hermitian rank-metric codes
Usman Mushrraf

Abstract
This study investigates Hermitian rank-metric codes, a special class of rank-metric codes, focusing on perfect codes and on the analysis of their covering properties. Firstly, we establish bounds on the size of spheres in the space of Hermitian matrices and, as a consequence, we show that non-trivial perfect codes do not exist in the Hermitian case. We conclude the paper by examining their covering density.

1.1
Hermitian rank-metric codes

Definition 1.1
.

A matrix
A\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}_{q^{2}}
is said to be Hermitian if it satisfies the condition
A^{*}=A
, where
A^{*}
denotes the conjugate transpose of
A
. The set of all
n
-order Hermitian matrices over the field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
is represented as
\mathrm{H}_{n}(q^{2})
.

Note that
\mathrm{H}_{n}(q^{2})
is a vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of dimension
n^{2}
, and so
|\mathrm{H}_{n}(q^{2})|=q^{n^{2}}
. Hermitian rank-metric codes are defined as
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspaces in
\mathrm{H}_{n}(q^{2})
endowed with rank distance. The rank distance, defined as the rank of the difference of two matrices
A
and
B
, is given by

d(A,B)=rk(A-B).

The minimum rank distance of a Hermitian rank-metric code is defined, as usual, as

d(\mathscr{C})=\min\{d(A,B)\colon A,B\in\mathscr{C},A\neq B\}.

The parameters of a Hermitian rank-metric code
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}_{q^{2}}
are
(n,|\mathscr{C}|,d(\mathscr{C}))
and they are related by the Singleton-like bound.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
24
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a Hermitian rank-metric code in
\mathrm{H}_{n}(q^{2})
of minimum distance
d
. Then

dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathscr{C})\leq n(n-d+1).

This allows us to give the following definition.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathrm{H}_{n}(q^{2})
be a Hermitian rank-metric code. It is stated that
\mathscr{C}
is a
Hermitian Maximum Rank Distance
(or
Hermitian MRD
) code if its parameters satisfy the equality in Theorem
1.2
.

1.2
Our Contributions

By using classical arguments in the Hamming metric, we provide the sphere-packing bound in the context of Hermitian rank-metric codes. We then prove bounds on the size of the balls, which we use to prove that non-trivial perfect codes do not exist in Hermitian rank-metric codes. We describe their covering properties via covering densities, obtaining bounds on the covering density.

2
Bounds on sphere and ball size

Given a matrix
M\in\mathrm{H}_{n}(q^{2})
, the sphere having radius
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and centred at
M
is defined as

S(M,r)=\{N\in\mathrm{H}_{n}(q^{2})\colon\mathrm{rk}(M-N)=r\},

and the ball
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with center
M
is defined as

B(M,r)=\{N\in\mathrm{H}_{n}(q^{2})\colon\mathrm{rk}(M-N)\leq r\},

and turns out that

B(M,r)=\cup_{i=0}^{r}S(M,i).

It can be proved that the ball size and the sphere size is independent of its center and we denote the sphere size of radius
r
by
S_{r}
and the ball size of radius
r
by
B_{r}
. Also, note that

B_{t}=\sum_{r=0}^{t}S_{r}.

Therefore,
S_{r}
represents the number of
n
-by-
n
Hermitian matrices with rank
r
, and
B_{r}
represents the number of
n
-by-
n
Hermitian matrices and rank less than or equal to
r
. The value of
S_{r}
and
B_{r}
depends only on
q
,
n
, and
r
, as we will see in a few lines.
The Gaussian binomial coefficient of non-negative integers
m\leq n
is expressed as

bin_{q}(n,m)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{q^{n-i+1}-1}{q^{i}-1}.

We will need the
q^{2}
-binomial coefficients for which the following hold.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
0\leq m\leq n
and
q\geq 2
.
Then

bin_{q^{2}}(n,m)\leq q^{2m(n-m)+2},

and

bin_{q^{2}}(n,m)\geq q^{2m(n-m)}.

Proof.

The upper bound is given in
[
16
, Lemma 2.1]
as

bin_{q}(n,m)\leq\frac{111}{33}q^{m(n-m)},

so that

bin_{q^{2}}(n,m)\leq\frac{111}{33}q^{2m(n-m)}\leq q^{2m(n-m)+2}.

It is easy to see that the lower bound of the
q
-binomial coefficient is

bin_{q}(n,m)\geq q^{m(n-m)},

and so

bin_{q^{2}}(n,m)\geq q^{2m(n-m)}.

∎

Throughout this paper, we will extensively use the value of
bin_{q^{2}}(m,n)
and its bounds. In the following theorem, the value of
S_{t}
and
B_{t}
has been determined, see
[
4
]
and also
[
12
]
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
4
, Theorem 3]
)
.

Let
0\leq t\leq n
be an integer. We have

S_{t}=|\{N\in\mathrm{H}_{n}(q^{2})|rk(N)=t\}|=bin_{q^{2}}(n,t)q^{\frac{t(t-1)}{2}}\prod_{s=1}^{t}(q^{s}+(-1)^{s}).

Now, we can get the upper and lower bounds of
S_{t}
and
B_{t}
in the next proposition.

Proposition 2.3
.

For any
t\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
, we have

q^{t(2n-t-1)}\leq S_{t}\leq q^{t(2n-t+1)+2},

and

q^{t(2n-t-1)}\leq B_{t}\leq q^{t(2n-t+1)+3}.

Proof.

It is true when
t=0
, so assume that
t\geq 1
and let us start by determining the bounds on
S_{t}
. By using Lemma
2.1

bin_{q^{2}}(n,t)\leq q^{2t(n-t)+2},

and

\prod_{s=1}^{t}(q^{s}+(-1)^{s})\leq\prod_{s=1}^{t}q^{(s+1)}=q^{t+\frac{t(t+1)}{2}}.

So, we derive

S_{t}\leq q^{t(2n-t+1)+2}.

(1)

For the lower bound on
S_{t}
, by Lemma
2.1

bin_{q^{2}}(n,t)\geq q^{2t(n-t)},

and we observe that

\prod_{s=1}^{t}(q^{s}+(-1)^{s})\geq\prod_{i=1}^{t}q^{(s-1)}=q^{\frac{t(t+1)}{2}-t}.

So,

S_{t}\geq q^{t(2n-t-1)}.

(2)

From (
1
) and (
2
), we get the desired bounds on
S_{t}

q^{t(2n-t-1)}\leq S_{t}\leq q^{t(2n-t+1)+2}.

Now we analyze the case of the ball. We know that a lower bound on the sphere size implies a lower bound on the ball size having the same radius. So, we only need to prove the upper bound on the ball size. We have

B_{t}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{t}q^{i(2n-i+1)+2}=q^{t(2n-t+1)+2}\left(1+\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}q^{2n(i-t)+t^{2}-i^{2}-(t-i)}\right).

As, for any
i\leq t-1

2n(i-t)+t^{2}-i^{2}-(t-i)\leq 2(t-i)(t-n),

so,

B_{t}\leq q^{t(2n-t+1)+2}\left(1+\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}q^{2(t-i)(t-n)}\right),

and by changing the variables, we obtain that

B_{t}\leq q^{t(2n-t+1)+2}(1+\sum_{j=1}^{t}q^{2j(t-n)}).

Observe that

1+\sum_{j=1}^{t}q^{2j(t-n)}=\frac{1-q^{2(t+1)(t-n)}}{1-q^{2(t-n)}}\leq\frac{1}{1-q^{2(t-n)}}\leq q.

This provides the required result for
B_{t}
.
∎

In the next section, we will use these bounds to study perfect codes in the context of Hermitian rank-metric codes.

3
Perfect codes

In this section, we will investigate the sphere-packing bounds, and show that there are no non-trivial perfect codes.

Theorem 3.1

(Sphere-packing bound)
.

Let
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathrm{H}_{n}(q^{2})
be an Hermitian rank-metric code with minimum distance
d
and
|\mathscr{C}|=M
. If
t=\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor
, then

MB_{t}\leq q^{n^{2}}.

Proof.

Let
c_{1},c_{2}\ldots,c_{M}
be the codewords of
\mathscr{C}
. From the triangle inequality of the rank metric, we can see that the intersection of
B(c_{i},t)
and
B(c_{j},t)
is empty for any
i,j\in\{1,2\ldots,M\}
with
i\neq j
.
So,

\left|\bigcup^{M}_{i=1}B(c_{i,t})\right|=\bigcup^{M}_{i=1}\left|B(c_{i,t})\right|\leq|\mathrm{H}_{n}(q^{2})|=q^{n^{2}},

which implies that

\sum^{M}_{i=1}B_{t}\leq q^{n^{2}},

and hence the desired result is obtained.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathrm{H}_{n}(q^{2})
be a Hermitian rank-metric code. We define
\mathscr{C}
to be a perfect code if it meets sphere packing-bound with equality.

We start to show that non-trivial perfect codes do not exist when
t=\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor=1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a Hermitian rank-metric code in
\mathrm{H}_{n}(q^{2})
with minimum distance
d
and let
t=\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor=1
, then
\mathscr{C}
is not perfect.

Proof.

Suppose on the contrary that
\mathscr{C}
is a perfect code.

MB_{1}=q^{n^{2}}.

(3)

Since
t=1
, we have
d=3
or
d=4
and by Singleton bound,

M\leq q^{n(n-2)}.

Using that
B_{1}=1+\frac{q^{2n}-1}{q+1}
, Equation (
3
) becomes

q^{2n}-1\leq\frac{q^{2n}-1}{q+1},

which implies
q\leq-1
, a contradiction. Therefore
\mathscr{C}
cannot be perfect.
∎

In the next Lemma, we analyze the case in which
\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor=2
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a Hermitian rank-metric code in
\mathrm{H}_{n}(q^{2})
with minimum distance
d
and let
t=\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor=2
then
\mathscr{C}
is not perfect.

Proof.

Suppose on the contrary that
\mathscr{C}
is perfect. Then

MB_{2}=q^{n^{2}}.

(4)

Using that
M\leq q^{n(n-4)}
, from Theorem
2.2
, and that
B_{2}=1+\frac{q^{2n}-1}{q+1}+\frac{(q^{2n}-1)(q^{2(n-1)}-1)q}{(q+1)(q^{2}-1)}
, we have (
4
) that

q^{n^{2}-4n}\left(1+\frac{q^{2n}-1}{q+1}+\frac{q(q^{2n}-1)(q^{2n-2}-1)}{(q+1)(q^{2}-1)}\right)\geq q^{n^{2}},

that is

\frac{q^{2n}-1}{q+1}+\frac{q(q^{2n}-1)(q^{2n-2}-1)}{q+1)(q^{2}-1)}\geq q^{4n}-1,

from which we derive

q^{2n-1}+q^{2}-q-1\geq(q^{2n}+1)(q^{2}-1)(q+1).

Therefore

q^{2n-1}\geq q^{2n+3}-q^{2n+1}+q^{2n+2}-q^{2n}+q^{3},

which is a contradiction.
∎

In the following theorem, we prove perfect codes do not exist in this context.

Theorem 3.5
.

There are no non-trivial perfect Hermitian rank-metric codes.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a Hermitian rank-metric code with minimum distance
d
then from Theorem
1.2

M\leq q^{n(n-d+1)}.

Suppose that
\mathscr{C}
is perfect we have

q^{n(n-d+1)}B_{t}\geq MB_{t}=q^{n^{2}},

with
t=\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor
.
Since, by Proposition
2.3

B_{t}\leq q^{t(2n-t+1)+3}
, then

q^{n(n-d+1)+t(2n-t+1)+3}\geq q^{n^{2}},

(5)

that is
if
d=2t+1
, this implies that

-t^{2}+t+3\geq 0,

which hold for
t\in\{0,1,2\}
.
If
d=2t+2
then
(
5
)
implies that

-t^{2}+t+3\geq n.

Since
n\geq 2
, so we have that
t\in\{0,1\}
. If
t=0
, then
d\in\{0,1\}
and
\mathscr{C}
is perfect if and only if
\mathscr{C}=\mathrm{H}_{n}(q^{2})
, which is a trivial case. The remaining part follows by Lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
.
∎

4
Covering density

In the previous section, we proved that non-trivial perfect codes do not exist in the context of Hermitian rank-metric codes. Covering density gives a measure of how much the ambient space is covered by the spheres centered in the codewords and of radius
\lfloor\frac{d(\mathscr{C})-1}{2}\rfloor
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a Hermitian rank-metric code in
\mathrm{H}_{n}(q^{2})
of size
M
. The
covering density
of
\mathscr{C}
is defined as

\mathscr{D(C)}=\frac{MB_{t}}{q^{n^{2}}},

where
t=\lfloor\frac{d(\mathscr{C})-1}{2}\rfloor
.

By using the bound of Proposition
2.3
, we have the following bounds for the covering density.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathrm{H}_{n}(q^{2})
be a Hermitian MRD code with minimum distance
d
and let
t=\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor
.
The covering density
\mathscr{D(C)}
of
\mathscr{C}
is upper bounded as follows

\mathscr{D(C)}\leq\begin{cases}q^{-t^{2}+t+3}&\text{if }d=2t+1,\\
q^{-n-t^{2}+t+3}&\text{if }d=2t+2,\end{cases}

and lower bounded as follows

\mathscr{D(C)}\geq\begin{cases}q^{-t^{2}-t}&\text{if }d=2t+1,\\
q^{-n-t^{2}-t}&\text{if }d=2t+2.\end{cases}

It is important to note that when the minimum distance is odd, then the upper and lower bounds of covering density depend entirely on the error correction capability of the code.

If
\mathscr{C}
is a Hermitian rank-metric code in
\mathrm{H}_{n}(q^{2})
then we can only get on upper bound of covering density.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathrm{H}_{n}(q^{2})
be a Hermitian rank-metric code with minimum distance
d
and let
t=\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor
. An upper bound of covering density
\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{C})
of
\mathscr{C}
for
d\in\{2,3,4,5,6\}
is

\mathscr{D(C)}\leq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{q^{n}}&\text{ if }d=2,\\
\\
\frac{q^{-2n+1}+1}{q+1}&\text{ if }d=3,\\
\\
\frac{q^{-3n+1}+q^{-n}}{q+1}&\text{ if }d=4,\\
\\
q^{-4n}\left(1+\frac{q^{2n}-1}{q+1}+\frac{q(q^{2n}-1)(q^{2n-2}-1)}{(q+1)(q^{2}-1)}\right)&\text{ if }d=5,\\
\\
q^{-5n}\left(1+\frac{q^{2n}-1}{q+1}+\frac{q(q^{2n}-1)(q^{2n-2}-1)}{(q+1)(q^{2}-1)}\right)&\text{ if }d=6.\end{cases}

We note that when
n
approaches to infinity, the covering density goes to zero when
d
is even. While, if we consider a family
\{\mathscr{C}_{i}\}_{i}\subseteq\mathrm{H}_{n_{i}}(q^{2})
of Hermitian MRD codes with minimum distance
d=3
, then the sequence of the covering densities goes to
\frac{1}{q+1}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\{\mathscr{C}_{i}\}_{i}
be a family of Hermitian MRD code in
\mathrm{H}_{n_{i}}(q^{2})
, and
\mathscr{C}_{i}
has parameters
(n_{i},|\mathscr{C}_{i}|,d)
. Then

•

if
d
is even, then
\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{C}_{i})=0
,

•

if
d
is odd and
\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{C}_{i})
exists, then

q^{-t^{2}-t}\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{C}_{i})\leq q^{-t^{2}+t+3},

where
t=\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that when minimum distance is even in Proposition
4.2
and Proposition
4.3
, as
i
tends to infinity the covering density
\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{C}_{i})
approaches zero. For the case, if
d
is odd and
\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{C}_{i})
exist, Proposition
4.2
and Proposition
4.3
clearly show that the covering density is independent on the order of the matrices of the code. Therefore
\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{C}_{i})
is bounded as in the statement.
∎

5
Conclusion

This proposed work aims to study perfect codes in the context of Hermitian rank-metric codes. This is similar to what happens for the classical rank-metric codes
[
19
]
and for the case of alternating matrices for large values of the field size
[
1
]
. Following the approaches in
[
19
]
and
[
20
]
, we proved in Theorem
3.5
that no non-trivial perfect codes exist in the Hermitian rank-metric code case. We also investigated their covering properties and found that spheres centered at the codewords covered
\frac{1}{q+1}
of the ambient space, with a minimum distance of three.

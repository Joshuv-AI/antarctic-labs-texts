An Erdős–Szekeres type result for words with repeats
Kyle Celano, Abigail Ollson, Niraj Velankar, Jun Yan

Abstract
. In this short note, we prove an Erdős–Szekeres type result for words with repeats. Specifically, we show that every word with k ​ n 6 + 1 kn^{6}+1 repeats contains one of the following patterns: 0 k + 2 0^{k+2} , 0011 ⋯ n n 0011\cdots nn , n n ⋯ 1100 nn\cdots 1100 , 012 ⋯ n 012 ⋯ n 012\cdots n012\cdots n , 012 ⋯ n n ⋯ 210 012\cdots nn\cdots 210 , n ⋯ 210012 ⋯ n n\cdots 210012\cdots n , n ⋯ 210 n ⋯ 210 n\cdots 210n\cdots 210 . Moreover, when k = 1 k=1 , we show that this is best possible by constructing a word with n 6 n^{6} repeats that does not contain any of these patterns.

The Erdős–Szekeres Theorem, stated below in

Theorem 1.1

Lemma 3.1

The word

For

, every number appears exactly twice in

Proof of Proposition
1.3

Let
s
be the word of length

constructed above. We will prove that
s
does not contain any of the patterns stated in the proposition. Clearly,
s

a)

If

contains a pattern

, then the pattern must be entirely contained within a copy of
q^{\oplus n}
, because across skew sums the entries are decreasing from definition. Then, by the pigeonhole principle, one copy of
q
in this

must contain two distinct values that are part of this

pattern, and thus this

contains a pattern

. However, this is not possible by Lemma

If

contains a pattern

, then by the pigeonhole principle, one copy of
q^{\oplus n}
must contribute two distinct entries to this pattern. However, these two distinct entries cannot come from two distinct copies of
q
in this

, as they must be in decreasing order. Thus, they come from the same copy of
q
, which then contains a pattern

. This is again impossible by Lemma
3.1
, which proves that no pattern of type
b)
is contained in
s
.

Any patterns of type
c)
in

must be entirely contained within a single copy of
q
that forms
s
because for every number in
s
, both of its occurrences in
s
lie in the same copy of

q
contains none of the patterns in

by Lemma
3.1
.

This covers all possibilities, so none of the patterns in the statement of the proposition is contained in

In this note, we have proven a more general result, Theorem

We also showed in

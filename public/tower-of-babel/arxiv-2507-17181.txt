The norms for symmetric and antisymmetric tensor products of the weighted shift operators
Xiance Tian, Penghui Wang and Zeyou Zhu

Abstract
In the present paper, we study the norms for symmetric and antisymmetric tensor products of weighted shift operators. By proving that for n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , ∥ S α l 1 ⊙ ⋯ ⊙ S α l k ⊙ S α ∗ l k + 1 ⊙ ⋯ ⊙ S α ∗ l n ∥ = ∏ i = 1 n ‖ S α l i ‖ , for any ( l 1 , l 2 ⋯ l n ) ∈ ℕ n \|S_{\alpha}^{l_{1}}\odot\cdots\odot S_{\alpha}^{l_{k}}\odot S_{\alpha}^{*l_{k+1}}\odot\cdots\odot S_{\alpha}^{*l_{n}}\|=\mathop{\prod}_{i=1}^{n}\left\|S_{\alpha}^{{l_{i}}}\right\|,\text{ for any}\ (l_{1},l_{2}\cdots l_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n} if and only if the weight satisfies the regularity condition, we partially solve [ 6 , Problem 6 and Problem 7] . It will be seen that most weighted shift operators on function spaces, including weighted Bergman shift, Hardy shift, Dirichlet shift, etc, satisfy the regularity condition. Moreover, at the end of the paper, we solve [ 6 , Problem 1 and Problem 2] .

In particular, symmetric tensor products of operators are crucial in the study of the many-body system

In the present paper, we will study the norm of symmetric and antisymmetric tensor product of operators.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be the space of bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
be the full tensor space of
\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{H}^{\odot n}
be the symmetric tensor space of
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\wedge n}
be the antisymmetric tensor space of
\mathcal{H}
, as introduced in
[
14
, Page 106]
. For
\mathbf{v}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{2},\cdots,\mathbf{v}_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
, let
\mathbf{v}_{1}\odot\mathbf{v}_{2}\odot\cdots\odot\mathbf{v}_{n}
be the symmetric tensor and
\mathbf{v}_{1}\wedge\mathbf{v}_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathbf{v}_{n}
be the antisymmetric tensor of
\mathbf{v}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{2},\cdots,\mathbf{v}_{n}
.
For
\mathbb{A}_{1},\mathbb{A}_{2},\cdots,\mathbb{A}_{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, we define
\mathbb{A}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{A}_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{A}_{n}
on
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
as

\left(\mathbb{A}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{A}_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{A}_{n}\right)\left(\mathbf{v}_{1}\otimes\mathbf{v}_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{v}_{n}\right)=\mathbb{A}_{1}\mathbf{v}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{A}_{2}\mathbf{v}_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{A}_{n}\mathbf{v}_{n}.

Then it is not difficult to see that
\mathcal{H}^{\odot n}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\wedge n}
are invariant under the action of

\mathrm{S}_{n}\left(\mathbb{A}_{1},\mathbb{A}_{2},\cdots,\mathbb{A}_{n}\right)=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\pi\in\Sigma_{n}}\left(\mathbb{A}_{\pi(1)}\otimes\mathbb{A}_{\pi(2)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{A}_{\pi(n)}\right)\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}\right),

where
\Sigma_{n}
is the group of permutations of
\left\{1,2,\cdots,n\right\}
. Set

\mathbb{A}_{1}\odot\mathbb{A}_{2}\odot\cdots\odot\mathbb{A}_{n}=\mathrm{S}_{n}\left(\mathbb{A}_{1},\mathbb{A}_{2},\cdots,\mathbb{A}_{n}\right)|_{\mathcal{H}^{\odot n}},

and

\mathbb{A}_{1}\wedge\mathbb{A}_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathbb{A}_{n}=\mathrm{S}_{n}\left(\mathbb{A}_{1},\mathbb{A}_{2},\cdots,\mathbb{A}_{n}\right)|_{\mathcal{H}^{\wedge n}}.

It is trivial to see that

\|\mathbb{A}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{A}_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{A}_{n}\|=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\mathbb{A}_{i}\|.

However the norms of symmetric products and antisymmetric products of bounded operators are much more complicated. For example, in
[
6
, Proposition 7.2]
, Garcia, O’Loughlin and Yu estimated the norms of symmetric product of diagonal operators
\mathbb{A}_{1}
and
\mathbb{A}_{2}
, and the sharpness of the following estimates was obtained:

(\sqrt{2}-1)\|\mathbb{A}_{1}\|\cdot\|\mathbb{A}_{2}\|\leq\left\|\mathbb{A}_{1}\odot\mathbb{A}_{2}\right\|\leq\|\mathbb{A}_{1}\|\cdot\|\mathbb{A}_{2}\|;

moreover, they raised a series of problems aimed at exploring the norms and spectrums of symmetric and antisymmetric product of operators. To continue, let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
be an orthonormal basis(ONB for short) of a complex Hilbert space
\cal H
.

In what follows, we always assume that the weight
\alpha=\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
is convergent
.
Let
S_{\alpha}
be a weighted shift operator on
\cal H
with the weight
\alpha
, defined by

S_{\alpha}e_{i}=\alpha_{i}e_{i+1}\ \mathrm{for}\ i\geq 0,

which was deeply studied in
[
15
]
. In particular,
S
is the forward shift operator with the constant weight
\alpha_{i}=1
, for
i\in\mathbb{N}
. The following problems were raised in
[
6
]
.

Problem 1.1
.

(
[
6
, Problem 6]
)
Identify the norm and spectrum of arbitrary symmetric or antisymmetric tensor products of
S
and
S^{*}
(for example, consider
S^{2}\odot S\odot S^{*3}
and
S^{2}\wedge S\wedge S^{*3}
).

Problem 1.2
.

(
[
6
, Problem 7]
)
Describe the norm and spectrum of
S_{\alpha}\odot S_{\alpha}^{*}
and
S_{\alpha}\wedge S_{\alpha}^{*}
, in which
S_{\alpha}
is a weighted shift operator. What can be said if more factors are included?

In this paper, we partially solve these problems. To clarify our main results, we need the assumption of the so-called regularity on the weight.

Definition 1.1
.

(regularity): The weight
\alpha
is called to be regular if

\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}\left|\alpha_{i}\right|\geq\left|\alpha_{m}\right|,\text{ for any }m\in\mathbb{N}.

The following theorem is the main result in the present paper, which partially gives an affirmative answer to the
[
6
, Problem 6 and Problem 7]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
S_{\alpha}
be a weighted shift operator with the convergent weight. Then
the following statements are equivalent.

(1)

\left\{\alpha_{i}\right\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
satisfies the regularity condition;

(2)

for every
n\geq 2
and every fixed
\left(l_{1},l_{2},\cdots,l_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,

\|S_{\alpha}^{l_{1}}\odot\cdots\odot S_{\alpha}^{l_{k}}\odot S_{\alpha}^{*l_{k+1}}\odot\cdots\odot S_{\alpha}^{*l_{n}}\|=\mathop{\prod}_{i=1}^{n}\left\|S_{\alpha}^{{l_{i}}}\right\|;

(3)

for every
n\geq 2
and every fixed
\left(l_{1},l_{2},\cdots,l_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,

\|S_{\alpha}^{l_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge S_{\alpha}^{l_{k}}\wedge S_{\alpha}^{*l_{k+1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge S_{\alpha}^{*l_{n}}\|=\mathop{\prod}_{i=1}^{n}\left\|S_{\alpha}^{{l_{i}}}\right\|.

In this case, set
\lambda=\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}\left|\alpha_{i}\right|,

\|S_{\alpha}^{l_{1}}\odot\cdots\odot S_{\alpha}^{l_{k}}\odot S_{\alpha}^{*l_{k+1}}\odot\cdots\odot S_{\alpha}^{*l_{n}}\|=\|S_{\alpha}^{l_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge S_{\alpha}^{l_{k}}\wedge S_{\alpha}^{*l_{k+1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge S_{\alpha}^{*l_{n}}\|=\lambda^{l_{1}+l_{2}+\cdots+l_{n}}.

Remark 1.4
.

1.

As a consequence of the above theorem, for
(l_{1},\cdots,l_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}

\|S^{l_{1}}\odot\cdots\odot S^{l_{k}}\odot S^{*l_{k+1}}\odot\cdots\odot S^{*l_{n}}\|=\|S^{l_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge S^{l_{k}}\wedge S^{*l_{k+1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge S^{*l_{n}}\|=1,

which partially solves Problem
1.1
.

2.

It is evident that the shift operators on nearly every classical function space, including the Hardy shift, weighted Bergman shift, and Dirichlet shift, are regular.

Section 3 is devoted to the proof of our main result, Theorem

The following lemma may be well-known, but we state it here for the readers’ convenience.

Lemma 2.5
.

For fixed
n\geq 1
,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{P(k,n)}{P(k+1,n)}=1.

Proof.

For

We will prove the lemma by induction.
Suppose that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{P(k,m)}{P(k+1,m)}=1,

(2.29)

and we will prove

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{P(k,m+1)}{P(k+1,m+1)}=1.

By

, for

\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}P(k,m)q^{n}=\frac{1}{\left(1-q\right)\left(1-q^{2}\right)\cdots\left(1-q^{m}\right)}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}P(k,m+1)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}P(k,m)q^{n}\right)\frac{1}{1-q^{m+1}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}P(k,m)q^{n}\right)\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(q^{m+1}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{i+j=k}P(i,m)f(j)q^{k},

where

f(j)=\begin{cases}1,&j=k(m+1),\quad k=0,1,2,\cdots,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(2.31)

Hence

P(k,m+1)=\sum\limits_{i+j=k}P(i,m)f(j).

(2.32)

For

and

let

. In order to prove (

), it suffices to show that

By (
2.32
) and (
2.31
), we have

\displaystyle P\left(\left(m+1\right)s+t,m+1\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i+j=\left(m+1\right)s+t}P(i,m)f(j)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{s}P(\left(m+1\right)i+t,m)

and

\displaystyle P\left(\left(m+1\right)s+t+1,m+1\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i+j=\left(m+1\right)s+t+1}P(i,m)f(j)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{s}P(\left(m+1\right)i+t+1,m).

Then by (
2.29
) and Stolz theorem, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{P\left(\left(m+1\right)s+t,m+1\right)}{P\left(\left(m+1\right)s+t+1,m+1\right)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{\sum\limits_{i=0}^{s}P(\left(m+1\right)i+t,m)}{\sum\limits_{i=0}^{s}P(\left(m+1\right)i+t+1,m)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{P(\left(m+1\right)s+t,m)}{P(\left(m+1\right)s+t+1,m)}

\displaystyle=1.

4
Lower bound of the norm for symmetric tensor product

This section is devoted to solving
[
6
, Problem 1 and Problem 2]
. At first, recall that
[
6
, Problem 1 and Problem 2]
are stated as follows.

Problem 4.1
.

(
[
6
, Problem 1]
)
For
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots x_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
, is

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!}}\left\|x_{1}\right\|\left\|x_{2}\right\|\cdots\left\|x_{n}\right\|\leq\left\|x_{1}\odot x_{2}\odot\cdots\odot x_{n}\right\|?

Problem 4.2
.

(
[
6
, Problem 2]
)
For
\mathbb{A}_{1},\mathbb{A}_{2},\cdots,\mathbb{A}_{n}\in\mathcal{B(H)}
, is

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!}}\sup\limits_{x\in H\atop\left\|x\right\|=1}\left\{\left\|\mathbb{A}_{1}x\right\|\left\|\mathbb{A}_{2}x\right\|\cdots\left\|\mathbb{A}_{n}x\right\|\right\}\leq\left\|\mathbb{A}_{1}\odot\mathbb{A}_{2}\odot\cdots\odot\mathbb{A}_{n}\right\|?

The following two propositions answer the above problems affirmatively.

Proposition 4.3
.

For
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots x_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
,

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!}}\left\|x_{1}\right\|\left\|x_{2}\right\|\cdots\left\|x_{n}\right\|\leq\left\|x_{1}\odot x_{2}\odot\cdots\odot x_{n}\right\|
.

Proof.

Let
\left\{e_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}
. For every
i=1,2,\cdots,n
, write
x_{i}=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{i,j}e_{j}
, where
\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\left|a_{i,j}\right|^{2}<\infty
. Obviously

\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{2}=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\left|a_{i,j}\right|^{2}
.

Since

\displaystyle\left\|x_{1}\odot x_{2}\odot\cdots\odot x_{n}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{1,j}e_{j}\odot\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{2,j}e_{j}\odot\cdots\odot\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{n,j}e_{j}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum\limits_{i_{1}=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{i_{2}=0}^{\infty}\cdots\sum\limits_{i_{n}=0}^{\infty}a_{1,i_{1}}a_{2,i_{2}}\cdots a_{n,i_{n}}e_{i_{1}}\odot e_{i_{2}}\odot\cdots\odot e_{i_{n}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{n}\geq 0}\left|a_{1,i_{1}}a_{2,i_{2}}\cdots a_{n,i_{n}}\right|^{2}\left\|e_{i_{1}}\odot e_{i_{2}}\odot\cdots\odot e_{i_{n}}\right\|^{2}

and

\left\|e_{i_{1}}\odot e_{i_{2}}\odot\cdots\odot e_{i_{n}}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n!}
,

we have

\left\|x_{1}\odot x_{2}\odot\cdots\odot x_{n}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n!}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{i_{j}=0}^{\infty}\left|a_{j,i_{j}}\right|^{2}=\frac{1}{n!}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\left\|x_{j}\right\|^{2}.

∎

Proposition 4.4
.

For
\mathbb{A}_{1},\mathbb{A}_{2},\cdots,\mathbb{A}_{n}\in\mathcal{B(H)}
,

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!}}\sup\limits_{x\in\mathcal{H}\atop\left\|x\right\|=1}\left\{\left\|\mathbb{A}_{1}x\right\|\left\|\mathbb{A}_{2}x\right\|\cdots\left\|\mathbb{A}_{n}x\right\|\right\}\leq\left\|\mathbb{A}_{1}\odot\mathbb{A}_{2}\odot\cdots\odot\mathbb{A}_{n}\right\|.

Proof.

By Propositon
4.3
,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbb{A}_{1}\odot\mathbb{A}_{2}\odot\cdots\odot\mathbb{A}_{n}\right\|

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{x\in\mathcal{H}\atop\left\|x\right\|=1}\left\|\left(\mathbb{A}_{1}\odot\mathbb{A}_{2}\odot\cdots\odot\mathbb{A}_{n}\right)\left(x\otimes x\otimes\cdots\otimes x\right)\right\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{H}\atop\left\|x\right\|=1}\left\|\mathbb{A}_{1}x\odot\mathbb{A}_{2}x\odot\cdots\odot\mathbb{A}_{n}x\right\|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!}}\sup_{x\in\mathcal{H}\atop\left\|x\right\|=1}\left\{\left\|\mathbb{A}_{1}x\right\|\left\|\mathbb{A}_{2}x\right\|\cdots\left\|\mathbb{A}_{n}x\right\|\right\}.

∎

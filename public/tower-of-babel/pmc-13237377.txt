Visual obstacle avoidance technology of VSLAM indoor intelligent logistics robot combining optical flow and feature extraction
Authors: Jiaying Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Indoor intelligent logistics robots are widely employed in warehousing and sorting operations, where their autonomous obstacle avoidance capabilities play a pivotal role in enhancing both operational efficiency and system safety. To bolster the obstacle avoidance reliability of individual robots and improve the collaborative path planning efficiency within multi-robot systems, comprehensive research is currently being conducted. This research primarily focuses on acquiring environmental information by integrating optical flow and feature extraction techniques. Furthermore, it involves the fusion of multi-sensor data from depth perception cameras and laser radars to enhance the precision of environmental perception. Additionally, the Pelican optimization algorithm has been refined through the incorporation of chaotic mapping and firefly disturbance strategies, with the overarching goal of achieving optimal path planning. The experimental setup includes obstacle avoidance simulation testing on the Ubuntu 20.04 platform, along with on-site testing at an e-commerce sorting center. The experimental results demonstrate that, compared to traditional methods, the improved approach increases the average obstacle avoidance safety distance by over 60% in static environments and enables flexible responses to random obstacles, such as pedestrians, in dynamic settings. The refined Pelican optimization algorithm exhibits superior convergence speed and accuracy in both single-peak and multi-peak test functions. In real-world logistics scenarios, the obstacle avoidance success rate reaches 98.6%, with an average obstacle avoidance time of 1.5 seconds and a total path length of 12.5 meters—all of which outperform traditional methods and existing technologies. The visual obstacle avoidance technology proposed in this study effectively enhances the obstacle avoidance safety of robots and the efficiency of multi-robot collaborative operations, offering a reliable solution for indoor logistics robots to autonomously navigate and avoid obstacles.

Introduction

With the rapid development of intelligent manufacturing and smart logistics, indoor intelligent logistics robots are increasingly widely used in scenarios such as warehouse sorting and material handling. Their autonomous obstacle avoidance (OA) ability has become a key factor affecting the efficiency and safety of logistics systems1. Visual OA technology has become a hot research topic due to its advantages of rich information and low cost. The OA method combining visual synchronous localization and mapping (VSLAM) can achieve the integration of environmental perception and path planning, providing technical support for autonomous navigation of robots2,3. Optical flow, as an important tool for describing pixel motion vectors in images, can obtain dynamic environmental information by analyzing pixel motion. Feature extraction can effectively identify key structures in the environment, and the combination of the two lays the foundation for improving the accuracy and robustness of robot visual OA4,5. In current research, traditional optical flow algorithms such as Lucas Kanade (LK) algorithm are prone to tracking failure due to the assumption of constant brightness being disrupted when the camera is moving rapidly. The multi-robot path planning algorithm has the shortcomings of slow convergence speed and easy getting stuck in local optima. In addition, existing methods lack consideration for the relationship between obstacle boundaries and robot size, resulting in a need to improve the safety of path planning6. To enhance the OA safety and adaptability of indoor intelligent logistics robots operating in both static and dynamic environments, and to ensure efficient and dependable path planning for multi-robot collaborative tasks, in-depth research has been carried out on the visual OA technology of VSLAM-based indoor intelligent logistics robots. This research involves optimizing the LK optical flow algorithm, integrating multi-sensor mapping techniques, and refining swarm intelligence optimization algorithms by synergistically combining optical flow analysis with feature extraction methods.

The innovativeness of this study is reflected in the following aspects: (1) It proposes an enhanced VSLAM framework integrating dynamic compensation optical flow and feature extraction. By coupling the six-parameter affine transformation model in the multi-scale pyramid LK algorithm, it compensates online for image deformation caused by the camera’s rapid movement, overcomes the tracking distortion problem from the failure of the constant brightness assumption in traditional methods, and improves the robustness and geometric consistency of feature tracking in large-displacement scenes. (2) It designs a tightly coupled navigation OA method combining hierarchical obstacle modeling and improved model predictive control (MPC). By dividing the three-level OA zones of "safe - relative collision - emergency", setting a unified expansion radius, and endogenously embedding the inverse proportional distance penalty function into the MPC objective function, it lays the foundation for effective trajectory planning and enables active hierarchical OA at the boundary while considering the robot’s kinematics. (3) It innovatively constructs a hybrid Pelican optimization algorithm (POA) for solving multi-traveling salesman problems. By integrating Logistic chaotic mapping initialization and firefly perturbation strategy, it effectively balances global exploration and local development capabilities, addressing the premature convergence defect of traditional swarm intelligence algorithms and enabling multi-robot path planning to achieve faster convergence speed and better solution quality.

The contributions of this study are as follows: (1) Through systematic experiments covering extreme scenarios such as high-density human flow, low light, and multi-robot collaboration, the advanced nature and engineering practicality of the proposed technology have been comprehensively verified. (2) The research method outperforms the comparison algorithm in key indicators including OA success rate, path length, and processing frequency, proving its high reliability and comprehensive performance in complex real logistics environments.

Related works

In recent years, researchers have proposed various enhancement strategies to address the issue of insufficient robustness of VSLAM in dynamic environments. Among these strategies, dynamic feature point processing and dynamic keyframe sequence construction have emerged as key directions. Z. Hu et al. adopted a dynamic feature point removal method and constructed a dynamic keyframe sequence based on angle differences to improve the robustness and localization accuracy of VSLAM in dynamic environments. The results showed that in high dynamic scenarios, the algorithm reduced the average processing effect by 96.4% and improved the loop detection accuracy by 2.6%, effectively enhancing the performance of the VSLAM system7. Q. Sun et al. proposed Graph Generative Consistency SLAM to improve the performance of VSLAM in dynamic environments. They combined semantic information with a grid-based motion statistics algorithm to optimize the calculation of the basic matrix, and refined the distinction between dynamic and static feature points through conditional random fields. The results showed that this method reduced the average absolute trajectory error by 95.71% compared to SLAM in high dynamic scenes8. K. Chappellet et al. proposed a full body task space control method that combines visual tracking and VSLAM to achieve complex motion and operation tasks of humanoid robots in industrial scenarios. Through the collaboration of fast and dense 3D model tracking and synchronous positioning mapping, the robot can complete dual arm grasping and assembly while walking. The experiment showed that this method had good robustness in large-scale manufacturing scenarios9. W. Chen et al. proposed an asynchronous multi-subgraph VSLAM system based on cloud edge collaboration to enhance the autonomous localization and mapping capabilities of bipedal climbing robots in complex high-altitude environments. By introducing a multi-subgraph mechanism and asynchronous sensing framework, and optimizing edge cloud transmission to reduce bandwidth consumption. The results indicated that this method significantly reduced communication overhead while ensuring positioning accuracy10. To enhance the positioning robustness of mobile robots in strong light environments, M. Chen et al. proposed a VSLAM algorithm that integrated improved highlight suppression and optical flow networks. They designed a suppression network based on the highlight attention mechanism and adopted a serpentine convolutional optical flow network to enhance the feature extraction of illumination invariance. The results showed that the absolute trajectory error on the KITTI dataset was reduced by 6.86%, the relative pose error was reduced by 17.30%, and the relative pose error in the measured scenarios was reduced by 13.23%11. To achieve cloud-edge collaborative VSLAM for low-computing-power mobile robots in communication-constrained environments, W. Chen et al. proposed a heterogeneous step map construction framework to reduce the transmission frequency and designed a transmission method based on implicit expression to reduce the data volume while ensuring the data association among the cloud, edge and terminal. The results showed that this method significantly reduced communication overhead while maintaining advanced accuracy, providing a feasible solution for cloud-edge collaborative VSLAM in weak network environments12.

Robot OA in complex dynamic environments requires a balance between real-time perception and safe path planning. Existing research has extracted motion information through optical flow algorithms, combined with swarm intelligence optimization and multi-sensor fusion technology, and proposed multiple efficient navigation control schemes. K. N. V. S. Varma et al. adopted an adaptive Beetle Swarm Levy flight optimization method based on deep learning and swarm intelligence optimization to achieve effective navigation and OA of intelligent mobile robots in complex environments, and combined it with fuzzy rule-based deep neural networks to achieve optimal motion control. The results showed that the max accuracy of this method reached 98.62% in static and dynamic obstacle scenes13. A. Elasri et al. employed the Horn-Schenck and LK optical flow algorithms to extract environmental data. Based on visual motion estimation, they constructed a control framework. This framework allows wheeled mobile robots to achieve vision-based autonomous navigation in both unknown static and dynamic indoor settings. The results indicated that this method could achieve collision free navigation in both 2D and 3D simulation environments14. R. Zhang et al. used SLAM based on visual inertial fusion to improve the OA success rate of autonomous vehicles in dynamic environments, and combined it with inertial measurement unit data optimization and optical flow tracking. The experiment outcomes indicated that the success rate of OA using this method exceeded 98%, and the longest OA time did not exceed 2 seconds, significantly improving the real-time and reliability of dynamic OA15. S. N. R. Gona et al. proposed a visual navigation system based on target following using long short-term memory networks and proportional integral controllers to enable robots to follow specific individuals and avoid obstacles. The results showed that this method had small prediction errors for linear velocity and angular velocity, with an average linear acceleration of 0.4 m/s3 and an average angular acceleration of 30 degrees/s3. The OA success rate was 100%16. L. Boyle et al. proposed a velocity measurement method that combined the optical flow of ground event cameras and planar kinematics to achieve precise velocity estimation without relying on the assumption of wheel-ground friction. This method utilized the microsecond delay and high dynamic range characteristics of event cameras to overcome motion blur. The results showed that this method performed comparable to the event visual inertial odometer on the 1:10 racing platform, with a 38.3% reduction in lateral error and a high-speed test speed of 32 m/s, verifying its application potential in real scenarios17. To explore the mechanism and application of optical flow sensing in organisms and robots, J. S. Chahl reviewed the primary motion detector theory of optical flow detection in the insect nervous system, analyzed its limitations in explaining insect behavior, and compared the application of optical flow technology in flying robots. The results showed that single-eye flow had inherent defects in distance measurement and speed measurement. Although many types of optical devices attempted to solve this problem, new limitations were introduced, hindering its wide application in the field of robotics18.

In summary, although existing research has achieved certain results in improving the robustness and OA ability of VSLAM, there is still room for improvement in the dynamic feature point processing and keyframe sequence construction of VSLAM. In addition, multi-robot path planning algorithms face issues such as slow convergence speed, susceptibility to local optima, and inadequate consideration of the relationship between obstacle boundaries and robot size. These problems necessitate improvements in the safety of path planning. Therefore, research is being conducted on improving the LK optical flow algorithm through multi-scale pyramids, integrating multi-sensor mapping, and enhancing swarm intelligence optimization algorithms to improve the OA safety of robots in complex environments.

Methods and materials

To address the challenge of visual OA for indoor intelligent logistics robots in complex dynamic environments, an integrated framework is proposed in this study, which collaboratively combines optical flow motion perception, feature extraction, multi-sensor fusion mapping and improved path planning methods. The overall system architecture can be divided into three modules: (1) Perception module: By using the multi-scale pyramid LK optical flow algorithm combined with feature point tracking, dynamic motion information is extracted from RGB-D camera data; (2) Mapping and positioning module: By fusing RGB-D camera and 2D Lidar data through RTAB-MAP, a robust two-dimensional occupation raster map is constructed to enhance environmental perception accuracy; (3) Navigation and planning module: Improved MPC is adopted to achieve local OA, and the chaotic enhanced POA is utilized for global multi-robot path planning. The perception module continuously updates the dynamic obstacle information and inputs it into the mapping module to maintain the real-time environmental model. This model further provides a basis for the planning module to generate safe and efficient trajectories.

VSLAM, as the core technology for indoor robot environment perception, provides basic spatial information for OA through real-time construction of maps and positioning. The research uses optical flow combined with feature extraction to guide logistics robots in OA using VSLAM. The optical flow field is often used to describe the motion vector of pixels in an image, among which LK optical flow algorithm is widely used to estimate the moving speed of pixels in an image sequence19. However, the algorithm’s reliance on a fixed brightness assumption makes it prone to tracking failures when cameras move rapidly due to assumption breakdown20. To address this, the study introduces a multi-scale pyramid structure to enhance the LK algorithm’s adaptability in large-displacement scenarios. Robots need to plan the shortest path that can avoid obstacles and reach the target location based on map information. Classification of obstacle areas.

In Fig. 4, the expansion radius is determined using the standard method of "robot envelope circle + safety margin" widely used in robot path planning. Specifically, the total expansion radius (0.5m) consists of the radius of the outer circle of the robot body (0.3m) and an additional safety margin (0.2m). The robot envelope ensures physical containment, while the safety margin is used to compensate for uncertainties inherent in the system such as positioning errors, control oscillations, and sensor noise. This practice is consistent with the obstacle expansion principle of costmap in mature navigation frameworks such as ROS, and is also consistent with the general principle of MPC introducing safety boundaries when dealing with state uncertainty27. To verify that this parameter achieves a balance between safety and efficiency, the study conducted a comparative test: when the safety margin dropped to 0.1m, although the path was the shortest (11.8m), the OA success rate dropped significantly to 91.5%; when the margin increased to 0.3m, the success rate (99.1%) increased slightly, but the path length increased significantly (13.8m), and the navigation efficiency decreased. Taken together, the safety margin of 0.2m achieved the best balance between the success rate (98.6%) and the path length (12.5m), proving that the expansion radius setting is reasonable and effective. It should be noted that corner tracking in this system mainly serves two core functions - providing stable geometric features for the front end of VSLAM and offering initial clues for dynamic perception through optical flow analysis. However, the OA decision of this system does not rely in isolation on corner information. The highly reliable static occupation raster map (derived from the fusion of lidar and RGB-D) provides precise static obstacle boundaries, while the corner optical flow serves as a lightweight parallel channel for quickly identifying potential moving areas. This information is jointly used as the input of the MPC controller. During the rolling optimization process of MPC, by designing a multi-objective cost function that includes static map constraints, dynamic region penalty terms, and path tracking accuracy, it comprehensively processes these heterogeneous information, thereby generating a safe and smooth trajectory in real time that can not only accurately track the global path but also actively avoid static and dynamic obstacles.

Conclusion

To improve the OA safety of indoor intelligent logistics robots in dynamic and static environments and the efficiency of multi-robot collaborative path planning, this study improved the performance of large displacement scene tracking by using multi-scale pyramid to enhance LK optical flow algorithm, integrated RGB-D camera and laser radar multi-sensor mapping to enhance environmental perception accuracy, and introduced Logistic chaotic mapping and firefly disturbance strategy to optimize POA for solving multi traveler problems. The results showed that the research method improved the average OA safety distance by more than 60% in static environments compared to traditional methods. In dynamic environments, it could flexibly respond to random moving obstacles such as pedestrians, and had good coordination between path adjustment and motion. The improved POA had better convergence speed and accuracy than PSO, whale and other algorithms in single peak and multi-peak testing functions. In real logistics scenario testing, the OA success rate reached 98.6% ± 1.5%, the average OA time was 1.5s ± 0.2s, and the total path length was 12.5m±0.5m, which was superior to traditional methods and existing technologies. The VSLAM OA technology and improved POA algorithm, which combined optical flow with feature extraction, effectively improved the OA safety and multi-robot collaboration efficiency of robots in complex environments. The current research has not yet incorporated robustness validation under extreme lighting conditions or in scenarios with dense obstacles. Additionally, there is considerable scope for optimizing the real-time performance of multi-sensor fusion. Looking ahead, it is essential to conduct scalable testing on diverse dynamic obstacle types, improve the efficiency of multi-sensor data integration, and incorporate deep learning techniques to further enhance environmental perception capabilities in complex settings.

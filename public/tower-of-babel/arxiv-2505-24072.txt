Code-based [ 3 , 1 ] [3,1] -avoiders in finite affine spaces AG ⁡ ( n , 2 ) \mathrm{AG}(n,2)
Benedek Kovács

Abstract
The author, together with Nagy, studied the following problem on unavoidable intersections of given size in binary affine spaces. Given an m m -element set S ⊆ 𝔽 2 n S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} , is there guaranteed to be a [ k , t ] [k,t] -flat, that is, a k k -dimensional affine subspace of 𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} containing exactly t t points of S S ? Such problems can be viewed as generalizations of the cap set problem over the binary field. They conjectured that for every fixed pair ( k , t ) (k,t) with k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 and 0 ≤ t ≤ 2 k 0\leq t\leq 2^{k} , the density of values m ∈ { 0 , … , 2 n } m\in\{0,...,2^{n}\} for which a [ k , t ] [k,t] -flat is guaranteed tends to 1 1 . In this paper, motivated by the study of the smallest open case ( k , t ) = ( 3 , 1 ) (k,t)=(3,1) , we present explicit constructions of sets in 𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} avoiding [ k , 1 ] [k,1] -flats for exponentially many sizes. These sets rely on carefully constructed binary linear codes, whose weight enumerators determine the size of the construction. Keywords : affine space, linear code, unavoidable, weight enumerator

Introduction

Consider the points of the
n
-dimensional affine space
\mathrm{AG}(n,2)
over the binary field
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, which also correspond to the elements of the vector space
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathrm{AG}(n,2)
be a point set of a fixed cardinality
m
. We investigate the intersections of
S
with the
k
-dimensional affine subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
, referred to as
k
-flats
. We say that
S

induces
a
[k,t]
-flat if there exists a
k
-flat
F_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
such that
|S\cap F_{k}|=t
. Otherwise we say that
S
is a
[k,t]
-avoider
.

Our question is the following: for what quadruples
(n,m;k,t)
does it hold that every
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
of size
m
induces a
[k,t]
-flat? If this is true, we write
[n,m]\to[k,t]
. The question was posed by the author and Nagy
[
10
]
as a
q
-analogue of a graph-theoretical problem by Erdős, Füredi, Rothschild and T. Sós
[
4
]
. Note that a
[k,0]
-avoider is also called a
blocking set
with respect to
k
-dimensional subspaces, which is an extensively covered topic in the projective and affine settings (see
[
1
]
for a survey). Also note that the corresponding question in the ternary field
\mathbb{F}_{3}
contains the well-known
cap set problem

[
3
]
, asking for the largest size of a subset in
\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
without a full line. This would correspond to
k=1
and
t=3
.

We defined the
(n;k,t)
-spectrum

\Sp(n,k,t)
, and its
density
,
\rho(n;k,t)
, by

\begin{split}\Sp(n;k,t)&:=\{0\leq m\leq 2^{n}:[n,m]\to[k,t]\},\\
\rho(n;k,t)&:=\frac{|\Sp(n;k,t)|}{2^{n}}.\end{split}

Here, and throughout this paper, we assume that
(n,k,t)
is a triple of integers with
n\geq 1
,
1\leq k\leq n
and
0\leq t\leq 2^{k}
.

We presented the following main conjecture:

Conjecture 1.1

(Kovács, Nagy
[
10
]
)
.

For every
k\geq 1
and
0\leq t\leq 2^{k}
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho(n;k,t)=1.

The conjecture was shown true for
t\in\{0,2^{k-1},2^{k}\}
when
k\geq 3
, and for all
t
when
k\in\{1,2\}
. The main result used for this (apart from the basic cases) was a result similar to Szemerédi’s Cube Lemma (see
[
15
, Corollary 2.1]
), which also follows from a work of Bonin and Qin
[
2
]
, stating that for
m\geq\frac{5}{2}\cdot 2^{n\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{k-1}}\right)}
, we have
[n,m]\to[k,2^{k}]
. The proof proceeded by picking the value
\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
that appears the most commonly as a difference of two elements in
S
, and applying the statement inductively for the quotient space
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}/\left\langle\mathbf{d}\right\rangle
.

We also showed that
\liminf\rho(n;k,t)>0
when
t
is of the form
2^{a}
or
3\cdot 2^{a}
for some integer
1\leq a\leq k-2
. The proof of the latter result was more involved, using the notion of
additive energy
defined by Tao and Vu
[
17
]
, and bounding sizes of hypercube cuts based on a theorem of Hart
[
7
]
. However,
1.1
stays open in these cases too.

Improving on an argument by Guruswami
[
5
]
, we also used the probabilistic method to obtain so-called
[k,c]
-evasive
sets, defined as sets in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
intersecting every
k
-flat in at most
c
points. Our result was the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
k,c\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
with
c\geq k+1
. Then for some
K>0
, there exists a
[k,c]
-evasive set in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
of size at least
K\cdot 2^{n\left(1-\frac{k}{c}\right)}
when
n
is sufficiently large.

As
[k,c]
-evasive sets are
[k,t]
-avoiders for every
t>c
, this theorem can easily be used to show that the number of sizes
m
outside
\Sp(n;k,t)
is exponentially large for each pair
(k,t)
with
k\geq 3
and
(k,t)\neq(3,4)
. For example, since the complement of any
[3,6]
-evasive set is a
[3,1]
-avoider, there exist
[3,1]
-avoiders of all sizes
2^{n}-K\cdot 2^{n/2}\leq m\leq 2^{n}
by
Theorem 1.2
. However, as these
[k,t]
-avoiders are obtained with a random method, there is no systematic way to describe them.

The research presented in this paper mainly concerns the smallest open case of
1.1
, which is
(k,t)=(3,1)
. Our main result is a construction of an explicit family of
[3,1]
-avoiders that still exhibit exponentially many different sizes. We call this the
code-based construction
, which takes an arbitrary binary linear code as an input, and derives a
[k,1]
-avoider (for fixed
k\geq 3
) whose size only depends on the weight enumerator of the code evaluated at certain inputs.

Let us make note of the following definitions.

Definition 1.3

(Weight of a codeword)
.

In a binary linear code
C
, the
weight
of a codeword
\mathbf{x}\in C
is the number of coordinates of
\mathbf{x}
equal to
1
.

Definition 1.4

(Weight enumerator)
.

The
weight enumerator
of a binary code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\ell}
is a bivariate polynomial
W_{C}
defined by

W_{C}(x,y)=\sum_{w=0}^{\ell}A_{w}x^{w}y^{\ell-w},

where
\ell
is the length of
C
, and
A_{w}
is the number of codewords in
C
having weight
w
for each
0\leq w\leq\ell
.

For further details on the coding-theoretical terminology used in the paper, see Subsection
2.1
. We now state our main results.

Theorem 1.5
.

Fix integers
k\geq 3
and
\ell\geq 1
. Take a binary linear code
C\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\ell}
. Then for
n=\ell(k-1)
, there exists a
[k,1]
-avoider
S
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
for which
|S|=2^{n}-W_{C}(1,2^{k-1}-1)
.

Here the set
S
is obtained using the code-based construction, which is detailed in Construction
4.1
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose that
n\geq 4
is an even integer. Then the number of different sizes of
[3,1]
-avoiders in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
given by the code-based construction is at least
\binom{\left\lfloor(n-4)/4\right\rfloor}{\left\lfloor(n-4)/8\right\rfloor}^{1/3}\geq c\cdot\frac{2^{n/12}}{\sqrt{n}}
for some absolute constant
c>0
.

Note that here we got at least
\approx\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}\cdot 1.059^{n}
different sizes, but as we will remark later, by the MacWilliams identity, the number of different sizes obtainable by this method is bounded above by
c\cdot n\cdot(\sqrt{3})^{n}\approx c\cdot n\cdot 1.732^{n}
.

The outline of the paper is the following. In Section
2
, we set the basic notation and definitions. In Section
3
, we give examples of
[k,1]
-avoiders that are built from affine subspaces using union and symmetric difference operations. We then describe a construction that uses the independent sets of a simple graph or a hypergraph. Then in Section
4
, we present our code-based construction, which provides a proof of Theorem
1.5
. We then present a coding-theoretical limitation that puts an upper bound on the number of possible sizes of
[k,1]
-avoiders resulting from this construction. Finally, Section
5
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.6
. Here we introduce two transformations of linear codes that each increase the length of a code by two, and we apply a sequence of such transformations to obtain a family of exponentially many codes. Using a group-theoretical tool, we show that these codes give rise to
[3,1]
-avoiders of exponentially many sizes.

Notation and preliminaries

By convention, elements of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
will be considered as
1\times n
row vectors over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

Similarly to
[
10
]
, we define the
k
-profile

\pf_{k}(S)
of a set
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
as the set of all possible intersection sizes of
S
and a
k
-flat of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. So
t\not\in\pf_{k}(S)
implies that
|S|\not\in\Sp(n;k,t)
. We say that the
k
-profile of

is

if all of its elements are even.

In a vector space, the direct sum of the two subspaces
A
and
B
is denoted by
A\oplus B
.

The complement of a set
S
will be denoted by
\overline{S}
. Recall that the
symmetric difference
of the
a
sets
S_{1},S_{2},...,S_{a}
, denoted by
\mathop{\bigtriangleup}_{i=1}^{a}S_{i}
, consists of those elements in
\bigcup_{i=1}^{a}S_{i}
that appear in an odd number of sets among
S_{1},S_{2},...,S_{a}
. When
a=2
(so for the symmetric difference of two sets), we can also use the notation
S_{1}\triangle S_{2}
.

We let
[n]=\{1,...,n\}
. If
A\subseteq[n]
is an arbitrary subset, one can define its characteristic vector
\mathbbm{1}_{A}
, which is a
0
-
1
sequence of length
n
. If
\mathbf{v}=\mathbbm{1}_{A}
, then for every
i\in[n]
,

\mathbf{v}_{i}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $i\in A$,}\\
0&\text{if $i\not\in A$.}\\
\end{cases}

Similarly one can define the characteristic vector
\mathbbm{1}_{S}
for a subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
, which is a
2^{n}
-long
0
-
1
sequence with entries indexed by the vectors
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
.

In this paper, an
alphabet
is a finite set
\Sigma
, whose elements are called
letters
. For example,
\Sigma=\{\mathtt{a},\mathtt{b}\}
. If
r\geq 0
, then a sequence of
\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}...\sigma_{r}
of
r

of length
r
, and the set of such words is denoted
\Sigma^{r}
. We write
\Sigma^{*}=\bigcup_{r\geq 0}\Sigma^{r}
for the set of all words over
\Sigma
.

We let
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
denote the set of nonnegative and positive integers, respectively.

2.1
Notions from coding theory

Let us recall the following basic notions from coding theory. A
binary linear code

C
of length
\ell
and dimension
d
is a linear subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\ell}
of dimension
d
. The elements of
C
are called
codewords
, and the
size
of the code is the number of codewords, which is equal to
2^{d}
.

The dual code of
C
is defined as
C^{\perp}=\{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\ell}:\mathbf{y}\mathbf{c}^{T}=0\penalty\ \forall\mathbf{c}\in C\}
, i.e., it is the orthogonal complement of the subspace
C\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\ell}
with respect to the standard inner product. Its dimension is
\ell-d
.

A
generator matrix

G
for
C
is a
d\times\ell
matrix whose rows form a basis for
C
, and a
parity check matrix

H
for
C
is a generator matrix for
C^{\perp}
, so it is an
(\ell-d)\times\ell
matrix. If
H
is a parity check matrix for
C
, then for every
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\ell}
, we have
\mathbf{y}\in C\Leftrightarrow H\mathbf{y}^{T}=\mathbf{0}
.

We recall the MacWilliams identity, which gives a link between the weight enumerator of a code and its dual:

Lemma 2.1
.

[MacWilliams identity
[
12
, p. 127]
] For a binary linear code
C
of dimension
d
,

W_{C^{\perp}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2^{d}}W_{C}(y-x,y+x).

2.2
Notions from group theory

The identity element of a group
G
will be denoted
1_{G}
. The notation
H\leq G
means that
H
is a subgroup of
G
. The subgroup of
G
generated by the two subgroups
H_{1},H_{2}\leq G
is denoted by
\langle H_{1},H_{2}\rangle
.

The free group over the generator set
S
will be denoted by
F(S)
, and the free product of two groups
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
will be denoted by
G_{1}\ast G_{2}
.

3
[k,1]
-avoiders

Our first fundamental example of
[k,1]
-avoiders (for
k\geq 2
) is obtained from the following basic observations:

Observations 3.1
.

(a)

If
H
is an
(n-k+1)
-flat of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
, then it has an even
k
-profile, so in particular
H
is a
[k,1]
-avoider.

(b)

The union of an arbitrary collection of
[k,1]
-avoiders in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
is also a
[k,1]
-avoider.

Proof.

(a)

If the affine subspace
H\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
has dimension
n-k+1
, then for any
k
-flat
F_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
, either
H\cap F_{k}=\emptyset
, or by the dimension theorem, the intersection is a flat of dimension
\dim H+\dim F_{k}-\dim\left\langle H,F_{k}\right\rangle=n+1-\dim\left\langle H,F_{k}\right\rangle
. Here
\left\langle H,F_{k}\right\rangle\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
has dimension at most
n
, therefore the dimension of
H\cap F_{k}
is at least 1, and so
|H\cap F_{k}|\geq 2
is an even power of two.

(b)

If
\{S_{i}:i\in I\}
is a collection of
[k,1]
-avoiders in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
, and
S=\cup_{i\in I}S_{i}
, then let
F_{k}
be an arbitrary
k
-flat. We show that
|F_{k}\cap S|=1
cannot hold. Assume to the contrary that it does. Then there exists
i\in I
such that
F_{k}\cap S_{i}\neq\emptyset
. Take such an
i
and let
|F_{k}\cap S_{i}|=c
. Since
S_{i}
was a
[k,1]
-avoider, we must have
c\geq 2
. However
F_{k}\cap S_{i}\subseteq F_{k}\cap S
, therefore
|F_{k}\cap S|\geq c\geq 2
as well, giving a contradiction.∎

Putting together these two observations, we obtain the following:

Corollary 3.2
.

For an integer
k\geq 2
, let
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
be an arbitrary union of
(n-k+1)
-flats. Then
S
is a
[k,1]
-avoider.

For example, any union of
(n-2)
-flats (
quarter-spaces
) satisfies the
[3,1]
-avoider property. Sets obtained from Corollary
3.2
will be called
basic

[k,1]
-avoiders.

The following observations help us obtain further
[k,1]
-avoiders.

Observations 3.3
.

(a)

Let
k,a\geq 2
be integers and
S_{1},S_{2},...,S_{a}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
be sets with even
k
-profiles. Then
\mathop{\bigtriangleup}_{j=1}^{a}S_{j}
also has an even
k
-profile.

(b)

The symmetric difference of a finite collection of
(n-k+1)
-flats in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
is a
[k,1]
-avoider.

(c)

Any set
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
obtained in the form
S=\cup_{i=1}^{b}T_{i}
(for
b\geq 0
), where each
T_{i}
is a symmetric difference of a finite collection of
(n-k+1)
-flats, is a
[k,1]
-avoider.

Proof.

(a)

For an arbitrary
k
-flat
F_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
, we have
F_{k}\cap(\mathop{\bigtriangleup}_{j=1}^{a}S_{j})=\mathop{\bigtriangleup}_{j=1}^{a}(F_{k}\cap S_{j})
. Here observe that a finite symmetric difference of even-cardinality sets has even cardinality too.

(b)

By part (a) and Observation
3.1
(a), the symmetric difference has an even
k
-profile and so is a
[k,1]
-avoider.

(c)

This follows from part (b) and Observation
3.1
(b). ∎

3.1
Obtaining
[k,1]
-avoiders from hypergraphs

As the following Proposition shows, a basic
[k,1]
-avoider in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
can be constructed from the independent sets of any hypergraph on
n
vertices.

Let
H=(V,E)
be a hypergraph with
V=[n]
and
E\subseteq\mathcal{P}(V)
. An
independent set
of
H
is a subset
W\subseteq V
such that no edge
e\in E
is fully contained in
W
. Let
i(H)
denote the number of independent sets of
H
. (If
H=G
is a graph then
i(G)
is also called the Fibonacci number of
G
, see
[
14
]
.)

The
rank
of a hypergraph
H
is the maximum cardinality of a hyperedge of
H
, and is denoted by
r(H)
.

Proposition 3.4
.

If
H=(V,E)
is a hypergraph on
V=[n]
with
r(H)\leq k-1
, where
k\geq 3
, then there exists a
[k,1]
-avoider
S_{H}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
with
|S_{H}|=2^{n}-i(H)
.

Proof.

For each hyperedge
e\in E
, define
F_{e}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}:\mathbf{x}_{v}=1\penalty\ \forall v\in e\}
, which is an
(n-c)
-flat if
|e|=c
, and every such
(n-c)
-flat can be decomposed as a union of
(n-k+1)
-flats.

Now let
S_{H}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
be defined as
S_{H}=\bigcup_{e\in H}F_{e}
. Then
S_{H}
is equal to a union of
(n-k+1)
-flats, so is a basic
[k,1]
-avoider. We can also see that
\mathbf{x}\not\in S_{H}
if and only if
\mathbf{x}
is a characteristic vector for an independent set of
H
. Therefore,
|S_{H}|=2^{n}-i(H)
.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

If
G
is a graph on
n
vertices, then there exists a
[3,1]
-avoider
S_{G}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
with
|S_{G}|=2^{n}-i(G)
.

Proof.

We apply Proposition
3.4
with
k=3
.
∎

Finding the number of possible values of
i(G)
for an
n
-vertex graph
G
is a question of independent interest as well. For a detailed presentation of the author’s results together with Nagy on this problem, see
[
11
]
.

3.2
Reed-Muller code perspective

We may look at the
[k,1]
-avoiders obtained in Observations
3.3
from a coding-theoretical perspective. The
binary Reed-Muller code

\mathrm{RM}(n,r)
of length
2^{n}
and order
r
is generated by the characteristic vectors of
(n-r)
-flats of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
(see
[
12
, Chapter 13]
). Let us take
r=k-1
. A vector
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2^{n}}
is a codeword of
\mathrm{RM}(n,k-1)
if and only if it can be expressed as the sum of the characteristic vectors (over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
) of some
(n-k+1)
-flats, say
\mathbf{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{c}\mathbf{x}^{(i)}
where
c\geq 0
, and for each
i
,
\mathbf{x}^{(i)}=\mathbbm{1}_{F_{i}}
for an
(n-k+1)
-flat
F_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. But this is equivalent to saying that
\mathbf{x}=\mathbbm{1}_{S}
for the set
S=\mathop{\bigtriangleup}_{i=1}^{c}F_{i}
, therefore codewords of
\mathrm{RM}(n,k-1)
are precisely the characteristic vectors of
[k,1]
-avoiders from Observation
3.3
(b).

For a finite number of vectors
\mathbf{y}^{(1)},...,\mathbf{y}^{(b)}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
, one can define their
elementwise disjunction

\mathbf{y}=\bigvee_{i=1}^{b}\mathbf{y}^{(i)}
, where
\mathbf{y}_{j}=1
if and only if
(\mathbf{y}^{(i)})_{j}=1
for at least one value
i
. Then the characteristic vectors of
[k,1]
-avoiders in Observation
3.3
(c) are precisely the vectors arising as a disjunction of some codewords of
\mathrm{RM}(n,k-1)
. Our question is then:

Problem 3.6
.

Determine the possible weights of the elementwise disjunction of a collection of codewords in
\mathrm{RM}(n,k-1)
.

For
r=1
this is an easy question to investigate using the affine space itself: a symmetric difference of a finite number of
(n-1)
-flats (halfspaces) is either
\emptyset
, the full space
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
or an
(n-1)
-flat. So in this case, a union of such objects
S=\cup_{i=1}^{b}T_{i}
(as in Observation
3.3
(c)) satisfies that
\overline{S}=\cap_{i=1}^{b}\overline{T_{i}}
, where
\overline{S}
can be
\emptyset
,
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
or an
(n-c)
-flat for some
1\leq c\leq b
. This means that the set of possible weights is
\{0,2^{n}-2^{n-1},2^{n}-2^{n-2},...,2^{n}-2^{0},2^{n}\}
.

To the author’s knowledge, this problem has not yet been investigated for
r=2
(corresponding to
k=3
). However the weights of the
\mathrm{RM}(n,2)
codewords themselves are known: Sloane and Berlekamp
[
16
]
gave the exact weight enumerator for
\mathrm{RM}(n,2)
with the arising weights being
2^{n-1}
and
2^{n-1}\pm 2^{n-1-j}
for
0\leq j\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
. No such description is known for higher order, however there are some partial results
[
13
,
8
,
9
]
.

4

[k,1]
-avoiders from linear codes

As mentioned in the Introduction, we now show a way to explicitly construct a
[k,1]
-avoider from an arbitrary binary linear code
C

See
1.5

To prove this theorem, we build up a set
S=S_{C}(k)\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
using the following construction:

Construction 4.1

(Code-based construction)
.

If
k\geq 3
and
\ell\geq 1
are integers, and
C\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\ell}
is a binary linear code, then we define a set
S_{C}(k)\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
as follows, where
n=\ell(k-1)
.

Let
F_{1},...,F_{\ell}
be the
(n-k+1)
-flats in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
given by

F_{i}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}:\mathbf{x}_{j}=1\penalty\ \forall j:(i-1)(k-1)+1\leq j\leq i(k-1)\}

for each
1\leq i\leq\ell
.

For
1\leq i\leq\ell
, define the indicator functions
I_{i}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}
by

I_{i}(\mathbf{x})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\mathbf{x}\in F_{i}$,}\\
0&\text{if $\mathbf{x}\not\in F_{i}$.}\\
\end{cases}

Now define the signature function
\mathbf{s}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\ell}
by
\mathbf{s}(\mathbf{x})=(I_{1}(\mathbf{x}),...,I_{\ell}(\mathbf{x}))
, and define the set
S_{C}(k)
by

\mathbf{x}\in S_{C}(k)\Leftrightarrow\mathbf{s}(\mathbf{x})\not\in C.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Consider the set
S=S_{C}(k)
as defined in Construction
4.1
.

First we determine the size of
S_{C}(k)
, which we will do by turning to the complement. For each codeword
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},...,c_{\ell})\in C
, the number of elements of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
with signature
\mathbf{c}
depends on
w(\mathbf{c})
. If it is known that
I_{i}(\mathbf{x})=c_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq\ell
, then for the values of
i
with
c_{i}=1
the corresponding
k-1
coordinates of
\mathbf{c}
must be all
1
(so can take just one value), and for those with
c_{i}=0
the corresponding
k-1
coordinates can be anything except all
1
’s, so can take
2^{k-1}-1
values. Overall,

|\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}:\mathbf{s}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{c}|=(2^{k-1}-1)^{\ell-w(\mathbf{c})}.

Altogether, we have

|\overline{S_{C}(k)}|=\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in C}(2^{k-1}-1)^{\ell-w(\mathbf{c})}=\sum_{w=0}^{\ell}A_{w}(2^{k-1}-1)^{\ell-w}=W_{C}(1,2^{k-1}-1),

so
|S_{C}(k)|=2^{n}-W_{C}(1,2^{k-1}-1)
as required.

We now show that
S_{C}(k)
is indeed a
[k,1]
-avoider. Let
H
be a parity check matrix for
C
, which is an
(\ell-d)\times\ell
matrix where
d=\dim C
. So for any
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\ell}
,
\mathbf{y}\in C\Leftrightarrow H\mathbf{y}^{T}=\mathbf{0}
, and so for
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
,
\mathbf{x}\not\in S_{C}(k)\Leftrightarrow H\mathbf{s(x)}^{T}=\mathbf{0}
.

Fix
1\leq r\leq\ell-d
, and let the set of indices
j
with
H_{r,j}=1
be
\{j_{r,1},j_{r,2},...,j_{r,b_{r}}\}
, where
1\leq b_{r}\leq\ell
. A vector
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
satisfies
(H\mathbf{s}(\mathbf{x})^{T})_{r}=1
if and only if

1=\sum_{i=1}^{b_{r}}\mathbf{s}(\mathbf{x})_{j_{r,i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{b_{r}}I_{j_{r,i}}(\mathbf{x}).

This holds if and only if
\mathbf{x}
lies in
T_{r}=\mathop{\bigtriangleup}_{i=1}^{b_{r}}F_{j_{r,i}}
. So overall,
\mathbf{x}\in S_{C}(k)\Leftrightarrow\mathbf{x}\in\bigcup_{r=1}^{\ell-d}T_{r}
, so
S_{C}(k)
is of the form described in Observation
3.3
(c). Thus it is indeed a
[k,1]
-avoider.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Instead of a binary
linear
code, the statement of Theorem
1.5
also holds if
C
is a binary
affine
code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\ell}
, that is,
C
is of the form
C=L+\mathbf{y}_{0}
for some linear subspace
L\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\ell}
and
\mathbf{y}_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\ell}
. In this case, some of the conditions
(H\mathbf{s}(\mathbf{x})^{T})_{r}=1
might be replaced with
(H\mathbf{s}(\mathbf{x})^{T})_{r}=0
, which holds if
\mathbf{x}\in T^{\prime}_{r}=T_{r}\triangle\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. Since
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
is itself a symmetric difference (in particular, a disjoint union) of
(n-k+1)
-flats, Observation
3.3
(c) still applies.

Now given the result of Theorem
1.5
, the following question naturally arises: is it possible to use it to disprove Conjecture
1.1
by setting
t=1
? In other words, can
W_{C}(1,2^{k-1}-1)
take
\Omega(2^{n})
distinct values for some pair
(k,\ell)
? In fact, the following two questions emerge, which might raise interest on their own right.

Problem 4.3
.

Determine the number of distinct weight enumerator polynomials
W_{C}(x,y)
that a binary linear code
C
of length
\ell
can have. We denote this number by
N_{\ell}
.

Problem 4.4
.

For a fixed pair of integers
(a,b)
, determine the number of possible values
W_{C}(a,b)
that can arise for a binary linear code
C
of length
\ell
. We denote this number by
N_{\ell}(a,b)
.

The following elementary upper bound can be given for the latter question.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
a,b\neq 0
and
\ell\geq 1
be integers, and
g=\gcd(a,b)
. Then

N_{\ell}(a,b)\leq\begin{cases}\left(\frac{|a|+|b|}{g}\right)^{\ell}&\text{if $ab>0$,}\\
2\left(\frac{|a|+|b|}{g}\right)^{\ell}+1&\text{if $ab<0$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Recall that
W_{C}(a,b)=\sum_{w=0}^{\ell}A_{w}a^{w}b^{\ell-w}
, where
A_{w}
is the number of codewords of weight
w
in
C
. Here
0\leq A_{w}\leq\binom{\ell}{w}
for all
w>0
, and
A_{0}=1
.

If
a,b>0
, then
W_{C}(a,b)
is an integer that is divisible by
g^{\ell}
and satisfies

1\leq W_{C}(a,b)\leq\sum_{w=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{w}a^{w}b^{\ell-w}=(a+b)^{\ell},

giving the required bound. If
a,b<0
, then
W_{C}(a,b)=(-1)^{\ell}W_{C}(|a|,|b|)
, leading to the previous case.

In the case
ab<0
,
W_{C}(a,b)
is still divisible by
g^{\ell}
, and

|W_{C}(a,b)|\leq\sum_{w=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{w}|a|^{w}|b|^{\ell-w}=(|a|+|b|)^{\ell}

by the triangle inequality, leaving at most
2\left(\frac{|a|+|b|}{g}\right)^{\ell}+1
possible values.
∎

Now we present a second way of giving an upper bound on
N_{\ell}(a,b)
in the fashion of Proposition
4.5
, namely through the dual of the code. For some triples
(a,b,\ell)
this results in a better bound.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
a,b,\ell
be integers such that
b>|a|
and
\ell\geq 1
. Then

N_{\ell}(a,b)\leq(\ell+1)\cdot\left(\frac{2b}{\gcd(b-a,b+a)}\right)^{\ell}.

Proof.

We use the MacWilliams identity (Lemma
2.1
): if the code
C\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\ell}
has dimension
d
, then

W_{C}(a,b)=\frac{1}{2^{\ell-d}}W_{C^{\perp}}(b-a,b+a).

Since
0\leq d\leq\ell
, the first factor can take
\ell+1
distinct values, and by Proposition
4.5
, the second factor can take at most
\left(\frac{2b}{\gcd(b-a,b+a)}\right)^{\ell}
distinct values, giving the result.
∎

As we will now see, this shows that we cannot disprove Conjecture
1.1
by solely using our code-based construction. In fact, we have the following stronger statement.

Corollary 4.7
.

If
k\geq 3
is a fixed integer and
k-1\mid n
, then as
n\to\infty
, the number of possible sizes of
[k,1]
-avoiders in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
obtainable using the code-based construction is
O(nc_{k}^{n})
, where
c_{k}<2
is a fixed constant depending on
k
.

Proof.

Let us apply Proposition
4.6
with
a=1
,
b=2^{k-1}-1
, and
\ell=\frac{n}{k-1}
. We get

N_{\ell}(1,2^{k-1}-1)\leq(\ell+1)(2^{k-1}-1)^{\ell},

so via Theorem
1.5
, we get the same upper bound on the number of sizes of
[k,1]
-avoiders obtainable using the code-based construction.

Since
\ell=\frac{n}{k-1}
, this upper bound is
O(nc_{k}^{n})
, where
c_{k}=(2^{k-1}-1)^{1/(k-1)}<2
.
∎

Note that in particular,
c_{3}=\sqrt{3}
, which corresponds to the case of
[3,1]
-avoiders.

Having seen this exponential upper bound on the number of possible values of
W_{C}(1,2^{k-1}-1)
(equivalently, on the number of sizes of
[k,1]
-avoiders obtained from the code-based construction), it is natural to ask to determine the asymptotic value of this number, or at least to prove a reasonable lower bound on it. In the next section, we focus on the case
k=3
, for which we give an argument leading to an exponential lower bound. Observe that as any
k
-flat for
k\geq 4
can be partitioned into
3
-flats, any
[3,1]
-avoider is also a
[k,1]
-avoider for all
k\geq 4
, but our code-based construction explicitly gives further
[k,1]
-avoiders as well.

[3,1]
-avoiders of exponentially many sizes via the code-based construction

First we prove a lemma showing how the weight enumerator of a code
C
changes when certain transformations are applied to
C
. We introduce the following two transformations:

Definition 5.1

(Base code transformations)
.

Let
C\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{L}
be a linear code. Then:

•

we let
\mathtt{a}(C)\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{L+2}
be the linear code obtained from
C
by appending two
0
coordinates to the end of each codeword,

•

we let
\mathtt{b}(C)\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{L+2}
be the linear code obtained as
\mathtt{b}(C)=\mathtt{a}(C)\oplus\left\langle(1,1,...,1,1,0),(1,1,...,1,0,1)\right\rangle
.

Lemma 5.2
.

If
C\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{L}
is a linear code, then:

(a)

W_{\mathtt{a}(C)}(x,y)=y^{2}W_{C}(x,y)
,

(b)

W_{\mathtt{b}(C)}(x,y)=(x^{2}+y^{2})W_{C}(x,y)+2xyW_{C}(y,x)
.

Proof.

(a)

The statement is immediate by noting that the transformation leaves the weight of each codeword intact but increases the length of the code by two.

(b)

For every codeword
\mathbf{c}\in C
, the four distinct codewords
(\mathbf{c},0,0)
,
(\mathbf{c}+\mathbf{e},0,1)
,
(\mathbf{c}+\mathbf{e},1,0)
and
(\mathbf{c},1,1)
appear in
\mathtt{b}(C)
, where
\mathbf{e}=(1,1,...,1)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{L}
. Therefore

\begin{split}W_{\mathtt{b}(C)}(x,y)&=\sum_{\mathbf{c^{\prime}}\in\mathtt{b}(C)}x^{w(\mathbf{c^{\prime}})}y^{L+2-w(\mathbf{c^{\prime}})}\\
&=\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in C}\left(x^{w(\mathbf{c})}y^{L+2-w(\mathbf{c})}+x^{L-w(\mathbf{c})+1}y^{w(\mathbf{c})+1}+x^{L-w(\mathbf{c})+1}y^{w(\mathbf{c})+1}+x^{w(\mathbf{c})+2}y^{L-w(\mathbf{c})}\right),\end{split}

where the four terms of the latter sum correspond to the aforementioned four codewords obtained from
\mathbf{c}
. This gives

W_{\mathtt{b}(C)}(x,y)=y^{2}W_{C}(x,y)+xyW_{C}(y,x)+xyW_{C}(y,x)+x^{2}W_{C}(x,y).\qed

.

Using Theorem

for

, if
\ell\geq 1

2^{n}-W_{C}(1,3)

. For an integer

C_{0}\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{0}

f

f(C_{0})\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2r}

W_{C}(1,3)

Notation 5.3
.

If
C\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{L}
is a linear code, let
\mathbf{v}_{C}=\begin{pmatrix}W_{C}(1,3)\\
W_{C}(3,1)\end{pmatrix}
.

Using this notation, one can read off the following from Lemma

.

Observation 5.4
.

For any linear code
C\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{L}
, we have
\mathbf{v}_{\mathtt{a}(C)}=\begin{pmatrix}9&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\mathbf{v}_{C}
and
\mathbf{v}_{\mathtt{b}(C)}=\begin{pmatrix}10&6\\
6&10\end{pmatrix}\mathbf{v}_{C}
.

Now let
M_{\mathtt{a}}=\begin{pmatrix}9&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
and
M_{\mathtt{b}}=\begin{pmatrix}10&6\\
6&10\end{pmatrix}
. Take the group homomorphism
\varphi:F(\{\mathtt{a},\mathtt{b}\})\to GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})

\varphi(\mathtt{a})=M_{\mathtt{a}}

For any

, use the notation

\mathbf{v}_{C_{0}}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\

\mathbf{v}_{f(C_{0})}=M_{f}\begin{pmatrix}1\\

.

We will prove that the number of distinct values that the first entry of
M_{f}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix}
can take grows exponentially in

. For this, the main idea is to show that
M_{f}
is distinct for each
f\in\{\mathtt{a},\mathtt{b}\}^{r}
, referring to the following well-known group-theoretical result. It was first used by Klein, and we state the modern formulation of de la Harpe
[

.

Lemma 5.5

(Ping-pong lemma
[
6
]
)
.

Let
G
be a group acting on a set
X
, and let
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}\leq G
with
|\Gamma_{1}|\geq 3
and
|\Gamma_{2}|\geq 2
. Let
\Gamma=\left\langle\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}\right\rangle\leq G
.
Suppose that
\emptyset\neq X_{1},X_{2}
are two subsets of
X
with
X_{2}\not\subseteq X_{1}
, satisfying

(1)

\gamma(X_{2})\subseteq X_{1}
for all
1\neq\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}
, and

(2)

\gamma(X_{1})\subseteq X_{2}
for all
1\neq\gamma\in\Gamma_{2}
.

Then
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}\ast\Gamma_{2}
, that is,
\Gamma
is the free product of the two groups.

In
[
6
]
, a classical application of the ping-pong lemma is then presented, showing that
\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
and
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
2&1\end{pmatrix}
generate a free subgroup of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
of rank two. In
GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
, we now adapt the same argument to our matrices
M_{\mathtt{a}}
and
M_{\mathtt{b}}
.

Proposition 5.6
.

The matrices
M_{\mathtt{a}}=\begin{pmatrix}9&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
and
M_{\mathtt{b}}=\begin{pmatrix}10&6\\
6&10\end{pmatrix}
generate a free subgroup of
GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
of rank two.

Proof.

We use Lemma
5.5
for the standard action of
GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
on
\mathbb{Q}^{2}
, with
\Gamma_{1}=\langle M_{\mathtt{a}}\rangle
and
\Gamma_{2}=\langle M_{\mathtt{b}}\rangle
being infinite cyclic subgroups (since
\left|\det M_{\mathtt{a}}\right|,\left|\det M_{\mathtt{b}}\right|\neq 1
). Consider the following two subsets of
\mathbb{Q}^{2}
:

X_{1}=\left\{\mathbf{v}=\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}:x,y\neq 0,\penalty\ \left(\frac{|y|}{|x|}<\frac{1}{3}\mathrm{\penalty\ or\penalty\ }\frac{|y|}{|x|}>3\right)\right\},

X_{2}=\left\{\mathbf{v}=\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}:x,y\neq 0,\penalty\ \frac{1}{3}<\frac{|y|}{|x|}<3\right\}.

The two sets are clearly disjoint and nonempty, so let us check conditions (1) and (2). Via simple calculations we can see the following.

(1)

If
\mathbf{v}
satisfies
\frac{1}{3}<\frac{|y|}{|x|}<3
then
M_{\mathtt{a}}^{n}\mathbf{v}=\begin{pmatrix}x^{\prime}\\
y^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}
satisfies
\frac{|y^{\prime}|}{|x^{\prime}|}<\frac{1}{3}
for any
n\geq 1
, and
\frac{|y^{\prime}|}{|x^{\prime}|}>3
for any
n\leq-1
. In all cases we have
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\neq 0
.

(2)

If
\mathbf{v}
satisfies
0<\frac{|y|}{|x|}<\frac{1}{3}
or
\frac{|y|}{|x|}>3
then
M_{\mathtt{b}}^{n}\mathbf{v}=\begin{pmatrix}x^{\prime}\\
y^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}
satisfies
\frac{1}{3}<\frac{y^{\prime}}{x^{\prime}}<3
for any
n\geq 1
, and
-3<\frac{y^{\prime}}{x^{\prime}}<-\frac{1}{3}
for any
n\leq-1
. In all cases we have
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\neq 0
.

We can hence conclude that
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}\ast\Gamma_{2}
, i.e.,
M_{\mathtt{a}}
and
M_{\mathtt{b}}
generate a free group of rank two.
∎

for a word
f
. We will show that the first entry of
M_{f}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix}
, which is
\alpha_{f}+\beta_{f}
, can take at least
T^{1/3}
distinct values for
M_{f}\in\mathcal{M}_{n^{\prime}}
, proving the theorem in this case.

For
M_{g}\in\mathcal{M}^{*}_{n^{\prime}-1}
, count the number of distinct values
\alpha_{g}+\beta_{g}
takes, depending on which we distinguish two cases.

Case 1: The values of
\alpha_{g}+\beta_{g}
take at least
T^{1/3}
distinct values.

In this case, consider the set
\mathtt{a}\{\mathtt{a},\mathtt{b}\}^{n^{\prime}-1}=\{\mathtt{a}g:g\in\{\mathtt{a},\mathtt{b}\}^{n^{\prime}-1}\}\subseteq\{\mathtt{a},\mathtt{b}\}^{n^{\prime}}
. Then for
f=\mathtt{a}g
,
M_{f}=\begin{pmatrix}9&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}M_{g}
, so
\alpha_{f}+\beta_{f}=9(\alpha_{g}+\beta_{g})
also takes at least
T^{1/3}
distinct values.

Case 2: The values of
\alpha_{g}+\beta_{g}
take less than
T^{1/3}
distinct values.

Then by pigeonhole, there is a subset
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{*}_{n^{\prime}-1}
of size at least
T^{2/3}
for which
\alpha_{g}+\beta_{g}
takes identical values. For the matrices
M_{g}\in\mathcal{M^{\prime}}
, count the number of distinct values
9\alpha_{g}+\beta_{g}
takes, distinguishing two further cases.

Case 2a: The values of
9\alpha_{g}+\beta_{g}
take at least
T^{1/3}
distinct values.

In this case, consider the set
\{\mathtt{a},\mathtt{b}\}^{n^{\prime}-1}\mathtt{a}=\{g\mathtt{a}:g\in\{\mathtt{a},\mathtt{b}\}^{n^{\prime}-1}\}\subseteq\{\mathtt{a},\mathtt{b}\}^{n^{\prime}}
. Then for
f=g\mathtt{a}
,
M_{f}=M_{g}\begin{pmatrix}9&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
, so
\alpha_{f}+\beta_{f}=9\alpha_{g}+\beta_{g}
also takes at least
T^{1/3}
distinct values.

Case 2b: The values of
9\alpha_{g}+\beta_{g}
take less than
T^{1/3}
distinct values.

Then by pigeonhole, there exists
\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
of size at least
T^{1/3}
for which both
\alpha_{g}+\beta_{g}
and
9\alpha_{g}+\beta_{g}
are constant for
M_{g}\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}
, meaning that both
\alpha_{g}\equiv\alpha
and
\beta_{g}\equiv\beta
are constant. Then for all matrices
M_{g}\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}
, we have
\alpha\delta_{g}-\beta\gamma_{g}=D
, where
\alpha,\beta,\gamma_{g},\delta_{g}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
D\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. One can easily see that all solutions of this equation must have distinct values of
\gamma_{g}+\delta_{g}
. Therefore, considering the set
\mathtt{b}\{\mathtt{a},\mathtt{b}\}^{n^{\prime}-1}=\{\mathtt{b}g:g\in\{\mathtt{a},\mathtt{b}\}^{n^{\prime}-1}\}\subseteq\{\mathtt{a},\mathtt{b}\}^{n^{\prime}}
, for
f=\mathtt{b}g
one has
M_{f}=\begin{pmatrix}10&6\\
6&10\end{pmatrix}M_{g}
, so
\alpha_{f}+\beta_{f}=10(\alpha+\beta)+6(\gamma_{g}+\delta_{g})
are all distinct, taking at least
T^{1/3}
distinct values.

Now assume
n=4n^{\prime}+2
instead. Then by the previous argument, we can get at least
\binom{\left\lfloor(n-6)/4\right\rfloor}{\left\lfloor(n-6)/8\right\rfloor}^{1/3}=\binom{\left\lfloor(n-4)/4\right\rfloor}{\left\lfloor(n-4)/8\right\rfloor}^{1/3}
codes of length
2n^{\prime}
having distinct values of
W_{C_{2n^{\prime}}}(1,3)
. If we add a
0
coordinate to the end of each codeword in such a code
C_{2n^{\prime}}
, we get a code
C_{2n^{\prime}+1}\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n^{\prime}+1}
with
W_{C_{2n^{\prime}+1}}(1,3)=3W_{C_{2n^{\prime}}}(1,3)
, hence attaining
[3,1]
-avoiders in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
of at least
\binom{\left\lfloor(n-4)/4\right\rfloor}{\left\lfloor(n-4)/8\right\rfloor}^{1/3}
sizes from the code-based construction.

At last, it is well-known that
\binom{s}{\left\lfloor s/2\right\rfloor}\sim\frac{2^{s}}{\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot s}}
as
s\to\infty
, resulting in the given lower bound of

\binom{\left\lfloor(n-4)/4\right\rfloor}{\left\lfloor(n-4)/8\right\rfloor}^{1/3}\geq c\cdot\frac{2^{n/12}}{\sqrt{n}}

for some absolute constant
c>0
.
∎

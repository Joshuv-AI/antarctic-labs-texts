Graded prognostic assessment model for bone-only metastasis in extensive-stage small cell lung cancer
Authors: Fei Zhou, Donghai Liang, Chen Xu, Tao Jiang, Haijun Lu, Fang Wang, Chunliu Meng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and aimExtensive-stage small cell lung cancer (ES-SCLC) is characterized by rapid progression and a high incidence of metastasis, particularly to the bones. Patients with bone-only metastasis (BOM) face unique challenges in prognosis and treatment. This study aimed to develop a graded prognostic assessment (GPA) model by retrospectively investigating prognostic factors in ES-SCLC with BOM to estimate survival time and enhance clinical decision-making.MethodsThe primary endpoint of the study was overall survival. We included ES-SCLC patients with BOM diagnosed between 2010 and 2022 at two hospitals. The Log-rank test and Cox proportional hazards model were employed to analyze prognostic factors. A GPA model was established using significant factors based on their hazard-ratios in the training set and subsequently validated by the validation set.ResultsA total of 274 patients were included, with 164 in the training cohort and 110 in the validation cohort. Significant independent prognostic factors incorporated into the GPA model included bone metastasis status, weight loss, treatment with antiresorptive drugs, immunotherapy and thoracic radiotherapy. A GPA score of 1 was assigned to patients with oligo-metastasis, no weight loss, and who received antiresorptive drug treatment, immunotherapy, or thoracic radiotherapy. The corresponding alternative factors were assigned scores of 4.671, 2.100, 1.706, 2.116 and 1.687, respectively. The median survival times for patients with GPA scores of 1.000 to 3.570, 3.610 to 13.443, and 16.548 to 59.736 were 38.9, 15.6, and 7.7 months in the training set (P < 0.001) and 36.2, 15.7, and 8.9 months in validation set (P < 0.001).ConclusionThe identified prognostic factors significantly influenced survival time for ES-SCLC patients with BOM. The GPA model developed in this study may serve as a valuable tool for estimating life expectancy and guiding treatment decisions.Keywords: Bone-only metastasis, Extensive-stage small cell lung cancer, Graded prognostic assessment model, Prognostic factors, Overall survival

Introduction

Small cell lung cancer (SCLC) is a highly aggressive malignancy characterized by rapid proliferation and a propensity for early distant metastasis, accounting for approximately 20–30% of all lung cancers cases. Its mortality rate ranks among the highest of all cancer types, underscoring the urgent need for effective treatment strategies [1]. At the time of initial diagnosis, approximately 60–70% of SCLC cases are classified as extensive-stage [2], with skeletal metastasis occurring in about 30–40% of patients, making it one of the most common metastatic sites [3, 4].

In patients with SCLC and skeletal metastasis, several factors can significantly influence prognosis. These include the patient’s initial physical condition, nutritional status, and any underlying comorbidities. Additionally, the presence of spinal or weight-bearing bone metastasis (BM), and the chosen treatment modalities can affect overall survival (OS) [3, 5, 6]. Clinically, depending on the patient's physical condition and economic situation, the attending physician and family members are often concerned about the patient's life expectancy, as this information is crucial for making informed decisions regarding the most appropriate systemic or localized treatments aimed at prolonging life and alleviating skeletal-related events (SREs) [7–11]. Despite the inherent challenges, accurately predicting survival time is of paramount importance.

Moreover, the presence of concurrent metastases in other sites, such as the brain, liver, and adrenal glands, can further complicate the prognosis and the likelihood of SREs in SCLC patients with BM. Thus, SCLC patients with bone-only metastasis (BOM) might represent an ideal cohort for survival prediction. With the advent of multiple prospective clinical trials demonstrating positive outcomes [12–14], immunotherapy has emerged as a critical component of first-line treatment for extensive-stage SCLC (ES-SCLC) in the current therapeutic background. While, previous studies on bone metastasis of SCLC basically did not take immunotherapy into account.

In this context, the present study aims to develop a graded prognostic assessment (GPA) model that incorporates immunotherapy for ES-SCLC patients with BOM diagnosed at initial presentation. This model will be based on demographic and clinical prognostic factors, providing physicians with a valuable tool for estimating survival time and guiding treatment decisions.

Materials and methods

Selection of population

We retrospectively collected data from patients diagnosed with ES-SCLC at two hospitals between 2010 and 2022. Clinical staging was reclassified renewly referring to the 9-edition tumor-node-metastasis classification [15]. The inclusion criteria for this study were: (1) histopathological and/or cytopathological confirmation of SCLC; (2) completion of brain-enhanced MRI, cervical ultrasound, enhanced chest and abdomen CT, bone scan, or brain enhanced MRI combined with PET-CT; and (3) confirmation of BM through imaging or bone biopsy. The exclusion criteria included: (1) presence of second primary tumor; (2) metastasis to sites other than bone; and (3) incomplete medical records.

Special concept definition

The GPA model was first proposed for brain metastases by Professor Sperduto in 2008 [16]. Building on this framework, we developed a GPA model specifically for ES-SCLC patients with BOM. The total GPA score was calculated as the product of the hazard ratios for five prognostic factors identified through multivariate analysis. These factors included BM status (classified as poly-metastasis or oligo-metastasis), weight loss (yes or no), treatment with antiresorptive drugs (no or yes), immunotherapy (no or yes), and thoracic radiotherapy (TRT) (no or yes). Oligo-metastasis was defined as having five or fewer BMs. Symptoms of symptomatic BM included: pain, pathological fractures, neurological symptoms and hypercalcemia. Metastatic site local treatment status (MSLTS) before disease progression was divided into two groups: all, part or no. The time period for weight loss refers to the time from two months before diagnosis of disease to diagnosis, and any reduction in weight was considered.

First-line treatment strategy

According to their tolerance level, all patients received 4–6 cycles of etoposide combined with carboplatin or cisplatin as dual chemotherapy, with or without immunotherapy, administered at intervals of 21–28 days. A total of 39 patients received immunotherapy agents such as atezolizumab or durvalumab, while 112 patients received TRT. The radiotherapy target area and dosage were defined as follows: the gross tumor volume (GTV) includes the primary lesion remaining after systemic treatment and any metastatic lymph nodes remaining after systemic treatment, and the clinical tumor volume (CTV) defined as the GTV expanded by 5 mm and includes primary lesions remaining after systemic treatment and the drainage areas for metastatic lymph nodes identified at diagnosis. The GTV and CTV were each expanded 5 mm to form the planning GTV (PGTV) and planning tumor volume (PTV). The PGTV received a dose of 60–66 Gy over 30–33 fractions, while the PTV received 54–60 Gy over 30–33 fractions. Additionally, 14 patients received preventive craniocerebral irradiation (PCI) with dose 25Gy over 10 fractions after achieving complete remission (CR) or partial remission (PR) following first-line treatment.

Data analysis and statistical considerations

The primary endpoint was OS, defined as the time from diagnosis to death or the last follow-up. The follow-up period extended from the initiation of treatment until the final follow-up on February 28, 2025. Data regarding patient death or the last follow-up were obtained through hospital's electronic medical record system or through communication with the patient's family. Clinical characteristics and outcomes were compared using the Chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. OS was analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier method, and differences in survival curves were assessed using the Log-rank test. The Cox proportional hazards model was used for multivariate analysis to evaluate the relationship between prognostic factors and the hazard of death. All P-values were two-sided, with P < 0.05 considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were conducted through Statistical Package for the Social Sciences version 24.0 software (IBM, IL, USA).

Results

Patient characteristics

From January 1, 2010, to December 31, 2022, a total of 2,206 patients were newly diagnosed with ES-SCLC at two hospitals, and their case data were collected. Among these, 1,667 patients were excluded due to metastasis to other sites, 76 due to a second primary tumor, and 189 due to incomplete case records. Ultimately, 274 SCLC-BOM patients were included in the study. Specifically, 164 cases from the Affiliated Hospital of Qingdao University were designated as the training cohort, while 110 cases from the Affiliated Hospital of Hebei University were included in validation cohort. The median survival times (MST) for the training and validation cohorts were 13.9 and 15.7 months, respectively (P = 0.224). Among the patients, 70 (25.5%) had single BM. Prognostic factors for survival in training set

In the training cohort, the median follow-up time was 24.6 months (range: 14.4–85.3 months). The median OS was 13.9 months, with 1-, 3- and 5-year OS rates of 57.7%, 21.1%, and 9.1%, respectively. For patients with bone oligo-metastasis, the MST was 28.3 months, with 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival rates of 87.8%, 41.4%, and 20.9%, respectively. Univariate analysis included factors such as gender, age, smoking history, Karnofsky Performance Scale score, weight loss, T-stage, N-stage, BM status, symptomatic BM, MSLTS, treatment with antiresorptive drugs, immunotherapy, TRT, and PCI. Among these, weight loss, T-stage, BM status, treatment with antiresorptive drugs treatment, immunotherapy, and TRT were significant prognostic factors for OS (Table 2). Covariates with P < 0.1 from the univariate analysis were further analyzed using the multivariate Cox proportional hazards model, revealing that BM status, weight loss, treatment with antiresorptive drugs, immunotherapy, and TRT were significant independent prognostic factors for OS (Table 3).

Development and validation of graded prognostic assessment

In the training cohort, a GPA model was developed based on the hazard ratios (HRs) of the five significant factors identified (Table 4). GPA scores were assigned as follows: Patients with oligo-metastasis received 1 point, while those with poly-metastasis received 4.671 points. Patients who experienced weight loss received 2.100 points, while those without weight loss received 1 point. Patients who received bone-protecting therapy were assigned 1 point, while those who did not undergo the therapy received 1.706 points. Patients who underwent immunotherapy received 1 point, while those who did not received 2.116 points. Patients who underwent TRT were assigned 1 point, while those who did not undergo the therapy received 1.687 points (Table 4). The total GPA score was calculated as the product of the scores for the five prognostic factors. Patients were subsequently categorized into three groups according to the optimal cutoff value calculated by statistics based on their total GPA scores, with MSTs for the three score ranges (1.000–3.570, 3.610–13.443, and 16.54–59.736) were 38.9, 15.6, and 7.7 months, respectively (P < 0.001; Fig. 1a; Table 5). Due to the multiplicative scoring system, there appeared to be gaps between the score ranges for the prognostic groups. Meanwhile, time-dependent receiver operator characteristic curve analysis was used to verify the prognostic accuracy, and AUCs for 2-, 3-, and 4-year survival were 0.822, 0.855, and 0.808, respectively (Fig. 1b), confirming the excellent prognostic power of the model.

Comparison with published nomogram model

To compare the predictive ability of our GPA model against the existing nomogram model developed by Yap et al. [17], which aimed to assess the survival probability of patients with metastatic lung cancer receiving radiotherapy for osseous metastases, we validated it using the validation set. Basing on the HRs obtained from univariate and multivariate analyses, the total risk score for each patient was calculated. While, the AUCs for 2-, 3-, and 4-year survival were just 0.444, 0.449, and 0.426, respectively (Fig. 3). Thus, our GPA model outperformed this nomogram model [17] by absolute increments of the AUC values.

Characteristics of SREs

At initial diagnosis and throughout the disease course, SREs occurred in 125 patients (45.6%). Among these, 108 patients (39.4%) underwent bone radiotherapy, which was the most common intervention. Spinal cord compression occurred in 34 patients (12.5%). Additionally, 41 patients (15.0%) developed hypercalcemia, 11 patients (4.0%) developed pathological fractures, and 8 patients (2.6%) underwent surgical intervention. In total, 202 SREs were documented.

Discussion

In this study, we established a GPA model specifically for ES-SCLC BOM patients diagnosed in initial presentation. The aim was to explore prognostic factors and assist physicians in estimating survival time and selecting appropriate treatment plans. Ultimately, we analyzed data from 274 cases across two hospitals that met our inclusion criteria and did not violate our exclusion criteria. In the validation set, we utilized 14 clinical features and treatment information for univariate analysis, identifying seven factors with P-values < 0.1 for subsequent multivariate analysis. This analysis revealed five significant independent prognostic variables: BM status, treatment with antiresorptive drugs, weight loss, immunotherapy, and TRT. The GPA model demonstrated strong patient discrimination ability, with P-values < 0.001 in both cohorts.

Previous literature has indicated that factors such as advanced age, multiple BM, visceral metastases, poor performance status ≥ 2, elevated serum inflammatory markers, pathological fractures, and spinal cord compression, can adversely affect the prognosis for SCLC-BM patients [18, 19]. Consistent with these findings, our study identified bone poly-metastasis, lack of antiresorptive drugs treatment, and weight loss as poor independent prognostic factors. Furthermore, several prospective or retrospective clinical studies have shown that first-line immunotherapy or TRT can significantly improve outcomes, particularly in patients with oligo-metastasis and without liver metastasis [12–14, 20–23]. This highlights the importance of tailored treatment strategies based on individual patient characteristics.

The pathophysiology of BM in lung cancer is complex. Factors such as TGF-β, IL-1, and IL-6 released from the BM site, along with the CCL20 from inflammatory monocytes, can inhibit the tumor immune response and contribute to treatment resistance [24]. This inflammatory milieu not only activates osteoclasts, leading to a vicious cycle of bone destruction [25, 26], but also directly or indirectly results in increased bone pressure, nerve compression, inflammatory responses, and immunosuppression, ultimately affecting patients’ quality of life and survival. Clinical trials [27–29] have consistently recommended anti-bone resorption drugs to mitigate these inflammatory parameters and reduce the incidence of SREs [30–32]. In our study, the use of anti-bone resorption drugs emerged as an independent factor associated with improved prognosis, reinforcing their clinical significance.

The role of PCI in ES-SCLC has always been controversial. The EORTC study demonstrated that PCI can reduce the incidence of symptomatic brain metastases and prolong progression-free survival and OS when first-line treatment achieves CR or PR [33]. However, a randomized controlled trial in Japan found that although PCI reduced the incidence of intracranial metastases (48% vs. 69%, P < 0.001), it did not bring survival benefits when enhanced MRI excluded brain metastases [34]. In this study, only 5.1% of patients underwent PCI, and survival analysis indicated no improvement in prognosis, suggesting that the utility of PCI may be limited in certain patient populations.

Clinically, treatment plans are often determined by the attending physician and the patient's family based on the expected survival time, with the goal of achieving the best cost–benefit ratio while minimizing patient suffering [8–10]. Several studies have aimed to develop predictive models for this purpose [18, 19, 35]. For example, Gong et al. [18] developed a model to predict survival time for SCLC-BM patients at diagnosis, identifying three parameters: age, number of BMs, and presence of extraosseous distant metastases. These patients were divided into three groups, and the MST were 6.4, 8.5 and 12.4 months, respectively. Similarly, Tokuhashi et al. [35] developed a scoring system to predict lifespan in lung cancer patients with spinal metastasis based on general physical condition, limb numbness state, pathological type, and molecular targeted therapy. Chen et al. [36] identified tumor size, age, N stage, gender, liver metastasis, radiotherapy, as well as chemotherapy were prognostic variables for OS in SCLC patients. Predicting the occurrence of SREs is also a significant clinical concern. Kang et al. [19] identified that metastases in the cervical and thoracic vertebrae and pelvis, a region count of five or more, and elevated lactate dehydrogenase levels were significant correlating factors for SREs in patients with SCLC-BM.

However, it is important to note that many of the aforementioned studies included various other pathological types of lung cancer, or patients with metastases to other sites simultaneously. Meanwhile, given the evolving landscape of treatment, particularly the combination of chemotherapy and immunotherapy as the primary first-line treatment for ES-SCLC, there is a pressing need for a scoring system that account for immunotherapy and is specifically tailored to SCLC-BOM. Our study addresses this gap by utilizing five variables to calculate the GPA score for patients in the training set, resulting in MST for three score ranges (1.000–3.570, 3.610–13.443, and 16.548–59.736) of 38.9, 15.6, and 7.7 months, respectively (P < 0.001). Although, there were statistically significant differences in several key baseline characteristics between two cohorts, namely age, T-stage, N-stage, and the rate of thoracic radiotherapy as shown in Table 1. The validation set also demonstrated significant clustering of patients (P < 0.001), reinforcing the model’s applicability.

We also compared the predictive ability of our GPA model with the existing nomogram model developed by Yap et al. [17] using the validation set. Although both models were aimed at lung cancer BM, our GPA model outperformed their nomogram model [17] by absolute increments of the AUC values. It might be because our model was established based on SCLC and was only applicable to BOM, without considering other site metastases. Furthermore, as the follow-up period progresses, the treatment methods were gradually improving and significantly prolonging the survival for patients.

SREs have a profound impact on patients' physical and emotional well-being, and they may be can indirectly affect the subsequent treatment process; therefore, SREs are of concern when studying BM [5, 6]. In our study, 45.6% of patients experienced SREs, a figure consistent with other reports [18, 37]. Given the aggressive nature of SCLC, it is crucial to prioritize early intervention for spinal metastasis to prevent severe complications, such as pathological fracture and spinal cord compression. By addressing these issues proactively, we can improve patient outcomes and quality of life.

Limitations

This analysis is subject to several inherent limitations. Firstly, the retrospective nature of the study introduces potential biases, particularly in the diagnosis of BM. The use of various imaging techniques, such as MRI, CT scans, and bone scans, can lead to differences in sensitivity and specificity, resulting in diagnostic heterogeneity among patients. This variability may affect the accuracy of BM detection and, consequently, the reliability of the prognostic factors identified in our study.

Secondly, although the value of PCI is subject to debate, and the results in this study indicated that it did not significantly improve the prognosis for ES-SCLC patients with BOM. While, we have to admit that the number of patients who received PCI was very small.

Thirdly, the study was conducted at only two hospitals, which may limit the generalizability of our findings. The relatively small sample size restricts the robustness of the conclusions drawn and may not adequately represent the broader population of ES-SCLC patients with BOM. To enhance the validity of our GPA model, further validation in larger, multi-center studies is essential. This would help confirm the model’s applicability across diver clinical settings and patient demographics.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our study identified several prognostic factors influencing survival in patients with ES-SCLC and BOM, including BM status, treatment with antiresorptive drugs, weight loss, immunotherapy, and TRT. Based on these findings, we developed a GPA model designed to assist physicians in estimating survival time and making informed treatment decisions. This model has the potential to improve patient management by providing a structured approach to evaluating prognosis and tailoring therapeutic strategies to individual patient needs. Further validation of this model in larger, multi-center cohorts will be crucial for its clinical implementation.

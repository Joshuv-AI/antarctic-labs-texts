Measuring birational derived splinters
Timothy De Deyn, Pat Lank, Kabeer Manali-Rahul, Sridhar Venkatesh

Abstract
. This work is concerned with categorical methods for studying singularities. Our focus is on birational derived splinters, which is a notion that extends the definition of rational singularities beyond varieties over fields of characteristic zero. Particularly, we show that an invariant called ‘level’ in the associated derived category measures the failure of these singularities.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
14A30 (primary), 14F08, 14B05, 18G80

1.
Introduction

Nowadays, it is becoming more and more clear that many geometric properties of a variety are reflected by the behavior of its associated derived category. In fact, many types of singularities fall under such geometric properties. Recall that a variety
X
is a
splinter
if the natural morphism
\mathcal{O}_{X}\to f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
splits for all finite surjective morphisms
f\colon Y\to X
. This term was coined by
[
Sin99
]
, although the idea dates back further.

An inspiration for related notions is Hochster’s
direct summand conjecture

[
Hoc73
]
. It asserts any affine regular scheme is a splinter. Recently, André proved Hochster’s conjecture using perfectoid spaces
[
And18
]
, and shortly after, Bhatt proved a derived variation
[
Bha18
]
. More history on the conjecture can be found in
[
Hoc07
]
.

The behavior of splinters can vary depending on the characteristic of
X
. In the case
X
has characteristic zero, being a splinter is equivalent to being a normal scheme, which implies the singularity is a local property. However, in positive characteristic, the story is different.
Specifically, Bhatt showed
X/\mathbb{F}_{p}
is a splinter if and only if it is a
derived splinter
; that is, the natural morphism
\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
splits for every proper surjective morphism
f\colon Y\to X
. It turns out that being a splinter is not a local property in such cases. Additionally, it is worthwhile to note that in characteristic zero, being a derived splinter is the same as having rational singularities
[
Kov00
,
Bha12
]
.

Part of Bhatt’s intuition was the ansatz that proper morphisms are robust derived analogues of finite morphisms. Motivated from this perspective, we study how the addition of birationality to the proper morphisms influences the behavior of such singularities and how this is reflected by their derived categories. Combining these ideas draws attention to the following notion first introduced by Kovács.

Definition 1.1
.

A Noetherian scheme
X
is called a
birational derived splinter
if the natural morphism
\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
splits for every proper birational morphism
f\colon Y\to X
.

These singularities have recently been gaining traction, e.g.
[
Lyu22
,
MMRS25
]
.
Furthermore, they coincide with derived splinters in several cases, including affine regular schemes
[
Bha18
]
and when one has resolutions of singularities, e.g. for quasi-compact quasi-excellent
\mathbb{Q}
-schemes (see
Corollary

2.5
). However, the situation changes drastically for schemes in positive characteristic. In particular, any elliptic curve in positive characteristic is a birational derived splinter (e.g. use
[
LV25
, Lemma 3.16]
), whereas Bhatt showed that it cannot be a derived splinter
[
Bha12
, Example 2.11]
.

One important aspect of the notions of splinters, derived splinters, and birational derived splinters is that they can be studied in any characteristic: zero, positive, or mixed. In contrast to rational singularities which requires certain vanishing results to behave well, and these need not always hold outside of characteristic zero. Moreover, the splinter singularities are related to various other interesting singularities. In positive characteristic, weakly
F
-regular rings are splinters, whereas splinters are
F
-rational
[
Smi93
]
. In mixed characteristic,
\operatorname{BCM}
-rational or
+
-rational singularities are birational derived splinters (see
[
MS21
]
and
[
MMRS25
, Remark 3.9]
).

1.1.
Measurements

Quantifying singularities is important because this can help understand the failure of the singularity existing. In
[
LV25
]
the notion of level was used to characterize the failure of being a derived splinter. Motivated by this, we propose a numerical invariant (of the scheme) that measures the failure of being a birational derived splinter. Unlike
loc. cit.
, we additionally study how geometric properties influence the behavior of this invariant which is possible due to the birationality of the morphisms defining birational derived splinters.

Our techniques use a notion of generation in a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
introduced in
[
BVdB03
]
and its associated invariants from
[
ABIM10
]
. For a subcategory
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
, let
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{n+1}
(for
n\geq 0
) denote the subcategory generated by
\mathcal{S}
using shifts, direct sums, direct summands, and at most
n
cones. Given
E\in\mathcal{T}
, the
level
of
E
with respect to
\mathcal{S}
, denoted
\operatorname{level}^{\mathcal{S}}(E)
, is the smallest
N
such that
E\in\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{N}
, or
+\infty
otherwise. See
Section

2.1
for details.

To be a birational derived splinter involves splitting conditions on the ‘natural morphism’ between structure sheaves in derived category. Before defining our numerical invariant, we motivate them with following lemma; it shows the relation between the splitting conditions and generation in the triangulated category.

Recall the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves is denoted by
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}
.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
be proper birational (more generally surjective) morphism to a Noetherian scheme. Then the natural morphism
\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
splits if, and only if,
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)=\langle\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\rangle_{1}
.

Proof.

Assume
\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
splits.
Then, tensoring with
E\in D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
and using the projection formula implies
E\in\langle\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}E\rangle_{1}
.
In general, we only know that
\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}E\in D^{-}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
. However, using
[
DL24
, Lemma 3.8]

yields
E\in\langle\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\rangle_{1}
.
The converse direction follows via adjunction (e.g. use
[
DLM24
, Lemma 5.15]
).
∎

Now, the stage has been set.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme.
Define
\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X)
to be the supremum over the levels of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
with respect to
\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)
for every proper and birational morphism
f\colon Y\to X
.
If
X=\operatorname{Spec}(R)
we also write
\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(R)
instead of
\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X)
.

Philosophically,
\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}
measures the failure of
X
to be a birational derived splinter. In particular, this singularity occurs precisely when
\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X)=1
. Moreover, we show that
\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X)
can be characterized by blowups along nonzero ideal sheaves (see
Proposition

3.3
).

First, we focus on studying
\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X)
in the presence of a resolution (of singularities)
f\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
. In this setting,
\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X)
is always finite and is independent of the chosen
f
(see
Theorem

3.4
). Furthermore,
\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}
is invariant for projective bundles (see
Corollary

3.7
).

Next, we consider the affine case. For
(R,\mathfrak{m})
a quasi-excellent normal local ring with
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion
\widehat{R}
, we have
\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(R)\geq\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(\widehat{R})
(see
Corollary

3.5
). Moreover, if
X
is an integral normal affine scheme, we show (see
Proposition

3.11
)

\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X)=\sup_{p\in X}\{\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(\mathcal{O}_{X,p})\}.

1.2.
Some consequences

Throughout the subsection, we work with schemes of finite type over a field
k
. In positive characteristic, it is not immediately clear whether the birational derived splinter property is preserved or reflected by fiber products or base change. Key tools commonly used in characteristic zero, such as Kovács’ splitting criteria
[
Kov00
]
or Grauert–Riemenschneider vanishing, are generally not available. This complicates studying these singularities in positive characteristic geometry.

Yet, in the affine setting, the behavior of birational derived splinters under localizations, direct limits, and base change along étale extensions has become better understood
[
Lyu22
]
. Moreover, when the
k
-scheme is Cohen–Macaulay, being a birational derived splinter coincides with being pseudo-rational
[
LT81
]
(see e.g.
[
MMRS25
, Lemma 3.8]
), showing that in this case they are stalk local.
However, being a birational derived splinter need not imply Cohen–Macaulayness, see
[
MP25
, Example 8.6]
and
[
MMRS25
, Remark 3.10]
.
This makes studying birational derived splinters in the global setting a bit more complicated in the absence of Cohen–Macaulayness.
We show that
\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}
, and hence generation for triangulated categories, can help clarify the picture. To start, we prove the following inequalities that describe the behavior of
\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}
under these operations.

Theorem 1.4

(see
Propositions

4.3
and
4.5
)
.

Let
k
be perfect. Consider proper
k
-schemes
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
which admit resolutions of singularities. Then

(1)

\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(Y_{1})\leq\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(Y_{1}\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(L))
for every field extension
L/k
,

(2)

\max\{\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(Y_{1}),\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(Y_{2})\}\leq\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(Y_{1}\times_{k}Y_{2})\leq\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(Y_{1})\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(Y_{2})
.

Thus, we have some clarity:

Corollary 1.5

(see
Theorem

4.4
and
Corollary

4.7
)
.

With notation as in
Theorem

1.4
. Then

(1)

Y_{1}
is a birational derived splinter if, and only if,
Y_{1}\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(\overline{k})
is where
\overline{k}
is an algebraic closure of
k
,

(2)

Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
are birational derived splinters if, and only if,
Y_{1}\times_{k}Y_{2}
is such.

To the best of our knowledge, these results are new in positive characteristic and reflects a few related results for rational singularities in characteristic zero. However, the techniques to prove these results differ than those in characteristic zero. Moreover, we do not know whether the converse direction of
Corollary

1.5
(
1
)
(i.e. descending singularities) has not appeared before in the literature for rational singularities (partly due to the possibility of needing to deal with infinite direct sums).

In summary, our work uses categorical techniques to study singularities. We believe that the results presented here, together with those in
[
LV25
]
, represent strong strides towards understanding singularities through derived categorical methods.
Additionally, to make this work accessible to those from a different background, e.g. those not familiar with generation for triangulated categories or certain geometric techniques, we have spelled out the proofs with sufficient details.

Notation

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme. We work with the following triangulated categories:
D(X):=D(\operatorname{Mod}(X))
the derived category of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules;
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
the (strictly full) subcategory of
D(X)
consisting of complexes with quasi-coherent cohomology;
D_{\operatorname{coh}}^{b}(X)
the (strictly full) subcategory of
D(X)
consisting of complexes having bounded and coherent cohomology; and
\operatorname{Perf}(X)
the (strictly full) subcategory of
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
consisting of the perfect complexes on
X
.
At times when
X
is affine, we abuse notation and write
D(R):=D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
where
R:=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
are the global sections; similar abuse holds for other categories.
Furthermore, given a morphism
f\colon Y\to X
, we write
\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{ntrl.}\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
for the natural morphisms, i.e. the unit of the derived pullback/pushforward adjunction.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Generation

We use notions of generation in triangulated categories as introduced in
[
BVdB03
,
ABIM10
]
. The reader is encouraged to refer to loc. cit. for more details. Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category.
A functor
F\colon\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
between triangulated categories is called
essentially surjective
(resp.
dense
) if every object
T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}
is isomorphic to (resp. a retract of)
F(T)
where
T\in\mathcal{T}
. A
strictly full
subcategory is a full subcategory closed under isomorphisms. We say that a subcategory
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{T}
is
thick
if it is a triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
closed under direct summands.
Generally, subcategories are not thick (or triangulated).
For any collection of objects
S
, let
\operatorname{add}(S)
be the smallest strictly full subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
containing
S
that is closed under shifts, finite coproducts and direct summands.
Inductively define

\langle S\rangle_{n}:=\begin{cases}\operatorname{add}(0)&n=0,\\
\operatorname{add}(S)&n=1,\\
\operatorname{add}(\{\operatorname{cone}\phi\mid\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}(\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{n-1},\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{1})\})&n>1.\end{cases}

An important feature of this construction is that
\langle S\rangle=\cup_{n\geq 0}\langle S\rangle_{n}
, where
\langle S\rangle
is the smallest thick subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
that contains
S
. We say an object
E\in\mathcal{T}
is
finitely built by
\mathcal{S}
if
E\in\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle
.
The smallest
n
for which
E\in\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{n}
is called the
level of
E
with respect to
\mathcal{S}
. This value is denoted by
\operatorname{level}^{\mathcal{S}}(E)
(otherwise, set to
\infty
).

2.2.
Types of morphisms

Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a morphism of integral Noetherian schemes. We say
f
is a
modification
if it is proper and birational, see e.g.
[
Sta25
,
Tag 01RN
]
for some characterizations. Also,
f
is an
alteration
if it is proper and generically finite, see e.g.
[
Sta25
,
Tag 02NV
]
for some characterizations.
Observe that each morphism above is surjective because each morphism is proper and dominant. Indeed, it follows from that fact each map on underlying topological spaces is closed and its image contains the generic point.
By
[
Sta25
,
Tag 080B
]
, a sequence of blowups of Noetherian schemes is a (single) blowup. As a convention, we say a modification is projective if the morphism is such, and analogously for other adjectives. A useful source of modifications are blowups of integral Noetherian schemes along nonzero ideal sheaves (see e.g.
[
Sta25
,
Tag 02ND
&
Tag 02OS
]
).

Now, for the general setting of Noetherian schemes which need not be integral, we follow
[
Sta25
,
Tag 01RN
]
for the definition of birationality. A fact we use liberally is that blowups are compatible with flat base change (see e.g.
[
Sta25
,
Tag 0805
]
)and that birationality of a morphism base changes along flat morphisms (see e.g.
[
GD71
, §1, Proposition 3.9.9]
). Moreover, we say a
resolution of singularities
is a proper birational morphism from a regular scheme.

2.3.
Birational derived splinters

We discuss a notion of singularities first introduced by Kovács. Recall a Noetherian scheme
X
is called a
birational derived splinter
if
\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{ntrl.}\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
splits for every proper birational morphism
f\colon Y\to X
.
For example, any quasi-compact regular scheme is a birational derived splinter (use
[
LV25
, Lemma 3.16]
). We record a few useful observations.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
and
g\colon Z\to Y
be morphisms of Noetherian schemes. If
\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{ntrl.}\mathbf{R}(f\circ g)_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Z}
splits, then
\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{ntrl.}\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
splits.

Proof.

This follows from
\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{ntrl.}\mathbf{R}(f\circ g)_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Z}
factoring through
\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{ntrl.}\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X
be an integral Noetherian scheme. Then
X
is a birational derived splinter if, and only if,
\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{ntrl.}\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}
splits for every blowup of
X
along a nonzero ideal sheaf.

Proof.

If
X
is a birational derived splinter, then the desired spitting condition is obvious. So, we check the converse. Using
[
Lüt93
, Lemma 2.2]
and
Lemma

2.1
, one can reduce to the case of blowups.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
f\colon Y\to S
be a proper surjective morphism of Noetherian schemes such that
\mathcal{O}_{S}\xrightarrow{ntrl.}\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
splits. If
Y
is a birational derived splinter, then so is
S
.

Proof.

This follows by arguing with
[
Lüt93
, Lemma 2.2]
but we add details for interest of techniques. Let
g\colon X\to S
be a proper birational morphism. Denote by
g^{\prime}\colon Y\times_{S}X\to Y
for the projection morphism. Using
[
Lüt93
, Lemma 2.2]
, we can find a proper birational morphism
h\colon Z\to Y
which factors through
g^{\prime}
.
Indeed, from base change, we see that
g^{\prime}
is a proper morphism which is an isomorphism over an open subset of
Y
.
If
Y
is a birational derived splinter, then
\mathcal{O}_{Y}\xrightarrow{ntrl.}\mathbf{R}h_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Z}
splits. Hence, by
Lemma

2.1
,
\mathcal{O}_{Y}\xrightarrow{ntrl.}\mathbf{R}g^{\prime}_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y\times_{S}X}
splits. So, the claim follows from
Lemma

2.1
.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme that admits a resolution of singularities
f\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{ntrl.}\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}
splits

(2)

X
is a birational derived splinter.

Proof.

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}(\ref{thm:Murayama_criterion2})}\implies{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}(\ref{thm:Murayama_criterion1})}
is obvious and the converse follows via
Proposition

2.3
and the fact any quasi-compact regular scheme is a birational derived splinter by
[
LV25
, Lemma 3.16]
.
∎

Corollary 2.5

(cf.
[
Lyu22
, Proposition 3.5]
)
.

Let
X
be a quasi-compact quasi-excellent integral
\mathbb{Q}
-scheme. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

is a derived splinter

(2)

X

Proof.

It is obvious that
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}(\ref{cor:birational_notions_for_quasi_excellent_char_zero1})}\implies{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}(\ref{cor:birational_notions_for_quasi_excellent_char_zero2})}

3.
Measurements

3.1.
First steps

The following is the main notion of this work.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme.
Define

\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X):=\sup\left\{\operatorname{level}^{\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)}(\mathcal{O}_{X})\mid f\colon Y\to X\textrm{ proper and birational}\right\}.

If
X=\operatorname{Spec}(R)
we also write
\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(R)
instead of
\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X)
.

By
Lemma

1.2
,
X
is a birational derived splinter precisely when
\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X)=1
. The following shows that in fact what one is measuring is not just the structure sheaf, but all of the perfect complexes.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a proper morphism of Noetherian schemes.
For any
n\geq 0
, one has
\mathcal{O}_{X}\in\langle\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\rangle_{n}
if and only if
\operatorname{Perf}(X)\subseteq\langle\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\rangle_{n}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathcal{O}_{X}\in\langle\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\rangle_{n}
. Choose
P\in\operatorname{Perf}(X)
.
We have

P\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}\langle\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\rangle_{n}\subseteq\langle\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}(\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}P\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y))\rangle_{n}\subseteq\langle\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\rangle_{n},

see e.g.
[
Lan24
, Lemma 3.5]
for the first inclusion.
Thus, tensoring the assumption by
P
yields
P\in\langle\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\rangle_{n}
showing the desired claim.
The converse direction is clear, completing the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
X
be an Noetherian scheme.
Then
\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X)
is the smallest
N\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\cup\{\infty\}
such that
\mathcal{O}_{X}\in\langle\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X^{\prime})\rangle_{N}
for all blowups
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
along ‘suitable’ ideal sheaves (i.e. so that birationality occurs
[
Sta25
,
Tag 0BFM
]
).

Proof.

Clearly,
N\leq\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X)
because any blowup along a nonzero ideal sheaf is proper and birational.
Moreover, if
N=\infty
there is nothing to show, so we can assume
N<\infty
. Let
g\colon Y\to X
be an arbitrary proper birational morphism. Using
[
Lüt93
, Lemma 2.2]
, we can find a proper birational morphism
h\colon Z\to Y
such that
g\circ h
is itself a blowup.
Hence,
\mathcal{O}_{X}\in\langle\mathbf{R}(g\circ h)_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Z)\rangle_{N}
, which implies
\mathcal{O}_{X}\in\langle\mathbf{R}g_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\rangle_{N}
. Thus,
\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X)\leq N
as
g
was arbitrary.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme which admits a resolution of singularities
f\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
. Then
\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X)=\operatorname{level}^{\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}\left(\widetilde{X}\right)}(\mathcal{O}_{X})<\infty
.

Proof.

To start,
[
DL24
, Lemma 3.9]
tells us that
\operatorname{level}^{\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}\left(\widetilde{X}\right)}(\mathcal{O}_{X})<\infty
. Let
g\colon Y\to X
be an arbitrary proper birational morphism. By definition, we know that
\operatorname{level}^{\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X})}(\mathcal{O}_{X})\leq\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X)
. So, as
g
is an arbitrary proper birational morphism, we only need to show
\operatorname{level}^{\mathbf{R}g_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)}(\mathcal{O}_{X})\leq\operatorname{level}^{\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X})}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
.
Using
[
Lüt93
, Lemma 2.2]
, there is a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widetilde{X}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

where
h
and
h^{\prime}
are proper birational morphisms. By
[
LV25
, Lemma 3.16]
, we have that
\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}\xrightarrow{ntrl.}\mathbf{R}h^{\prime}_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Z}
splits because
\mathbf{R}h^{\prime}_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Z}
is perfect as the target is regular. From
Lemma

3.2
, it follows that
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X})=\langle\mathbf{R}h^{\prime}_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Z)\rangle_{1}
as
\widetilde{X}
is regular. Moreover,
[
DLMR25
, Lemma 5.5]
gives us an
E\in D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X})
such that

.
Tying things together, we can choose
E^{\prime}\in D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Z)
such that
E\in\langle\mathbf{R}h^{\prime}_{\ast}E^{\prime}\rangle_{1}
, and so
\mathcal{O}_{X}\in\langle\mathbf{R}(f\circ h^{\prime})_{\ast}E^{\prime}\rangle_{N}
. Then the string of inequalities

\displaystyle\operatorname{level}^{\mathbf{R}g_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)}(\mathcal{O}_{X})

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{level}^{\mathbf{R}g_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)}(\mathbf{R}(g\circ h)_{\ast}E^{\prime})\operatorname{level}^{\mathbf{R}(g\circ h)_{\ast}E^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}_{X})

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{level}^{\mathbf{R}(g\circ h)_{\ast}E^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}_{X})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{level}^{\mathbf{R}(f\circ h^{\prime})_{\ast}E^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}_{X})

\displaystyle\leq N=\operatorname{level}^{\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X})}(\mathcal{O}_{X})

shows the desired inequality, and so,

.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme which admits a resolution of singularities

. Suppose
\mathcal{E}
is a vector bundle of rank

on

.

Proof.

One inequality comes from

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{P}_{X}(f^{\ast}\mathcal{E})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widetilde{X}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{P}_{X}(\mathcal{E})}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

Remark 3.8
.

Corollary

to holds more generally for any smooth morphism of whose (derived) unit is an isomorphism on structure sheaves. Indeed, the same argument as above will work out.

3.3.

Next, we focus on the case of

for affine schemes and show that this is compatible with looking a stalks. It is not known as to whether being a birational derived splinter is a stalk local property for general schemes. So, any global versions of

we believe to be more difficult.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let

be a proper surjective morphism to a separated Noetherian scheme and

. Consider the fibered square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Y\times_{X}\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{X,p})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{X,p})}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X.}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

First, note

is an affine morphism (see e.g.
[
Sta25
,

]
). Moreover, we know that

is flat and affine, so is

and

Lemma 3.10

(cf.
[

, Corollary 3.4]
)
.

Let

be a Noetherian affine scheme. Suppose

. If

, then

.

Proof.

It is straightforward to see that
\operatorname{level}^{\mathcal{S}}(E)\geq{\sup}_{p\in X}\{\operatorname{level}^{\mathcal{S}_{p}}(E_{p})\}
.
Hence, we only need to check the reverse inequality. If

, then there nothing to show, so we can impose it is finite. By
[

, Lemma 3.9]

, we see for each

. Now,

tells us

is an open subset of

. As

this gives us an affine open cover of

. From

being quasi-compact, we can refines this to a finite subcover, say

. Set

and

∎

Proposition 4.1

(Descending birational derived splinters)
.

Let

be a scheme of finite type over a field

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ell}

Let

be a proper birational morphism. We need to show

splits. Note that

is flat and affine. There is a commutative diagram

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces l}

whose square faces are fibered. Note that
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are (resp. finite) flat morphisms by base change along
L/k
. Also,
f^{\prime}
is a resolution of singularities.

Suppose
\mathcal{O}_{X}\in\langle\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X})\rangle_{n}
for some
n\geq 1
. Then

\mathcal{O}_{X_{L}}=\pi^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{X}\in\langle\pi^{\ast}\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X})\rangle_{n}.

By flat base change, we see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{X_{L}}

\displaystyle\in\langle\pi^{\ast}\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X})\rangle_{n}

\displaystyle\subseteq\langle\mathbf{R}f^{\prime}_{\ast}(\pi^{\prime})^{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X_{L})\rangle_{n}

\displaystyle\subseteq\langle\mathbf{R}f^{\prime}_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X}_{L})\rangle_{n}.

Hence, if coupled with
Theorem

3.4
, one has
\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X_{L})\leq\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X)
.

Next, we impose
L/k
be a finite field extension. Let
n\geq 1
such that

\mathcal{O}_{X_{L}}\in\langle\mathbf{R}f^{\prime}_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X}_{L})\rangle_{n}.

By flat base change with the inner most square of the diagram above, we have an isomorphism of bounded complexes
\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{X_{L}}\cong\bigoplus_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus r_{t}}[t]
. Now, tensoring with any object
E\in D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
and using projection formula, we can find a section
E\to\pi_{\ast}\pi^{\ast}E
. Clearly, as
\pi
is flat,
\pi^{\ast}E\in D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X_{L})
. Hence, we see that
\pi_{\ast}\colon D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X_{L})\to D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
is essentially dense. A similar argument shows the same thing for
\pi^{\prime}_{\ast}\colon D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X}_{L})\to D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X})
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{X}

\displaystyle\in\langle\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{X_{L}}\rangle_{1}

\displaystyle\subseteq\langle\pi_{\ast}\mathbf{R}f^{\prime}_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X}_{L})\rangle_{n}

\displaystyle\subseteq\langle\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}\pi^{\prime}_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X}_{L})\rangle_{n}

\displaystyle\subseteq\langle\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X})\rangle_{n}.

Hence,
\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X)\leq\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X_{L})
, which completes the proof.
∎

.

(2)

(3)

L/k
.

Proof.

This follows from

4.1
and

4.3

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
X
and
Y
be schemes which are proper over a field
k
. Then
\max\{\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X),\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(Y)\}\leq\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X\times_{k}Y)
.

Proof.

We only show the inequality for
X
and
X\times_{k}Y
as the same argument applies for
Y
. Let
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be a proper birational morphism. Consider the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X^{\prime}\times_{k}Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X\times_{k}Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Spec}(k)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{\prime}_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

whose squares are fibered. By flat base change, we know that
\mathbf{R}(\pi_{1})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}Y}
and
\mathbf{R}(\pi^{\prime}_{1})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}\times_{k}Y}
are isomorphic to bounded complexes of the form
\oplus_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus s_{t}}[t]
and
\oplus_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}^{\oplus s_{t}}[t]
respectively.
Then
Lemma

1.2
implies
\langle\mathbf{R}(\pi_{1})_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X\times_{k}Y)\rangle_{1}=D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
and
\langle\mathbf{R}(\pi_{1}^{\prime})_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X^{\prime}\times_{k}Y)\rangle_{1}=D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X^{\prime})
. Note
f^{\prime}
is proper and birational. Choose
n\leq\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X\times_{k}Y)
such that
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}\times_{k}Y}\in\langle\mathbf{R}f^{\prime}_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X\times_{k}Y)\rangle_{n}
. Then we have a string of inclusions

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{X}

\displaystyle\in\langle\mathbf{R}(\pi_{1})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}Y}\rangle_{1}

\displaystyle\subseteq\langle\mathbf{R}(\pi_{1}\circ f^{\prime})_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X^{\prime}\times_{k}Y)\rangle_{n}

\displaystyle\subseteq\langle\mathbf{R}(f\circ\pi_{1}^{\prime})_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X^{\prime}\times_{k}Y)\rangle_{n}

\displaystyle\subseteq\langle\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)\rangle_{n}.

showing the desired inequality.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
X
and
Y
be schemes which are proper over a perfect field
k
. Assume both admit resolutions of singularities. Then there are inequalities:

\max\{\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X),\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(Y)\}\leq\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X\times_{k}Y)\leq\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X)\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(Y).

Proof.

Let
f\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
and
g\colon\widetilde{Y}\to Y
be resolutions of singularities. There is a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widetilde{X}\times_{k}\widetilde{Y}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X\times_{k}\widetilde{Y}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widetilde{Y}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widetilde{X}\times_{k}Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X\times_{k}Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widetilde{X}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Spec}(k)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\prime\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

whose squares are all fibered. Note that
\pi_{1}
,
\pi_{1}\circ g^{\prime}
,
\pi_{2}
and
\pi_{2}\circ f^{\prime}
are flat morphisms by base change. Then
f^{\prime}
,
f^{\prime\prime}
,
g^{\prime}
, and
g^{\prime\prime}
are proper birational morphisms.
So,
f^{\prime}\circ g^{\prime\prime}
is a proper birational morphism to

from a smooth scheme.

Now, we show the desired upper bound for
\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(X\times_{k}Y)
. Let
n_{1},n_{2}\geq 1
such that
\mathcal{O}_{X}\in\langle\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X})\rangle_{n_{1}}
and
\mathcal{O}_{Y}\in\langle\mathbf{R}g_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{Y})\rangle_{n_{2}}
. As
\widetilde{X}
is smooth, and hence regular, we have that

. By flat base change, we see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}Y}

\displaystyle\in\langle\pi^{\ast}_{1}\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X})\rangle_{n_{1}}

\displaystyle\subseteq\langle\mathbf{R}f^{\prime}_{\ast}\mathbf{L}h^{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X})\rangle_{n_{1}}

\displaystyle\subseteq\langle\mathbf{R}f^{\prime}_{\ast}\operatorname{Perf}(\widetilde{X}\times_{k}Y)\rangle_{n_{1}}.

From
Proposition

3.6
, we know that
\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(\widetilde{X}\times_{k}Y)\leq\mu_{\operatorname{bds}}(Y)
.
Hence, by
Lemma

3.2
,

\displaystyle h^{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X})

\displaystyle\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(\widetilde{X}\times_{k}Y)

\displaystyle\subseteq\langle\mathbf{R}g_{\ast}^{\prime\prime}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X}\times_{k}\widetilde{Y})\rangle_{n_{2}}.

Then we have the following inclusions

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}Y}

\displaystyle\in\langle\mathbf{R}f^{\prime}_{\ast}\operatorname{Perf}(\widetilde{X}\times_{k}Y)\rangle_{n_{1}}

From

3.4

4.5
, we are done.
∎

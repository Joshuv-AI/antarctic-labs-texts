An abstract uncertainty principle with applications
Tianxiao Huang, Ze Li and Jiani Liu

Abstract
. Under Wigdersons’ framework and by sorting out the technical points in the recent works of Tang ( J. Fourier Anal. Appl. 31 (2025)) and Dias-Luef-Prata ( J. Math. Pures Appl. (9) 198 (2025)), we prove an abstract uncertainty principle for functions in the L p L^{p} setting. An immediate consequence is a new uncertainty principle for the Fourier transform, unifying and extending many existing results. More applications are shown for PDEs, including the moment growth estimates for some linear and nonlinear dispersive equations, and a type of weighted lower bound estimate for the spacetime moment of the Schrödinger equation and heat equation inspired by the control theory.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Wigdersons’ Framework for the Uncertainty Principle

The phrase
uncertainty principle
in mathematics often refers to the fact that
a nonzero function in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and its Fourier transform cannot be too localized to some subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Among these uncertainty principles, the first and most well-known one is the Heisenberg uncertainty principle reflected by the inequality

\left\||x|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left\||\xi|\hat{f}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\gtrsim_{n}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2},\quad f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(1.1)

where
\hat{f}(\xi)=\int e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)dx
is the Fourier transform of
f
. Despite of the optimal constant, the classical proofs to (
1.1
) are closely related to two facts. First, the Fourier transform maps
i^{-1}\partial_{j}f
to
\xi_{j}\hat{f}
, which turns (
1.1
) into

\left\||x|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\gtrsim_{n}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2},\quad f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(1.2)

Second and considerably, the canonical commutation relation
[\partial_{j},x_{j}]=1
helps the proof of (
1.2
) either in a concrete way using integration by parts, or in the view of Hilbert space techniques.

However in 2021, Avi Wigderson and Yuval Wigderson
[
18
]
found a way around such point of view, and established a new framework of showing how inequalities like (
1.1
) can hold, even when
\hat{f}
is replaced by
Af
where
A
is a linear operator.

The framework is a two-step mechanism, and we give a short review of it in the setting of
A
being a linear operator mapping functions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The first step is to prove a
primary uncertainty principle
for
A
, which may appear in the form of a norm inequality

\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\frac{\|Af\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|Af\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\gtrsim 1.

(1.3)

If we denote

H^{(0)}(f)=\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}},

and view
H^{(0)}(f)
as a kind of
measurement of localization
of
f
, then (
1.3
) reads

H^{(0)}(f)H^{(0)}(Af)\gtrsim 1,

(1.4)

and this is why (
1.3
) can be called as an uncertainty principle.

The second step is to compare some other measurement of localization
H^{(1)}(f)
with
H^{(0)}(f)
. If we can prove for all reasonable
f
and some
\kappa>0
that

H^{(1)}(f)\gtrsim\left(H^{(0)}(f)\right)^{\kappa},

then combining (
1.4
), we have an
upgraded uncertainty principle
for
A

H^{(1)}(f)H^{(1)}(Af)\gtrsim 1.

Note that the second step has nothing essential to do with
A
.

Wigdersons in
[
18
]
have already shown when
n=1
that such framework and its variants can be used to prove the Heisenberg uncertainty principle (
1.1
), as well as many ”Heisenberg-like” uncertainty principles. More concretely, denoted by

H^{(0)}_{q}(f)=\frac{\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}{\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}},\quad H^{(1)}_{r,q}(f)=\frac{\||x|^{r}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}}{\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}},

and assuming
A
is a
k
-Hadamard operator which satisfies

\|A\|_{1\rightarrow\infty}\lesssim 1\quad\text{and}\quad\|A^{*}Af\|_{\infty}\geq k\|f\|_{\infty}\text{ for $f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$ with $Af\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$},

(1.5)

a primary uncertainty principle for
A
was first shown:

H^{(0)}_{q}(f)H^{(0)}_{q}(Af)\geq k^{1-\frac{1}{q}},\quad\text{if }1\leq q\leq\infty,

(1.6)

and then the following comparison of localization was also proved:

(H^{(1)}_{r,q}(f))^{\frac{2q-2}{2qr+q-2}}\gtrsim H^{(0)}_{q}(f),\quad\text{if }1<q\leq\infty,\,\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}<r<\infty.

(1.7)

These together upgrade the uncertainty principle for
A
(see
[
18
, Theorem 4.14]
):

\left(H^{(1)}_{r,q}(f)\right)^{\frac{2q-2}{2qr+q-2}}\left(H^{(1)}_{s,q}(Af)\right)^{\frac{2q-2}{2qs+q-2}}\gtrsim k^{1-\frac{1}{q}},\quad\text{if }q\in(1,\infty],\,\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}<r,s<\infty.

(1.8)

In particular, when
A=\mathscr{F}
,
r=s=1
and
q=2
, (
1.8
) recovers the Heisenberg uncertainty (
1.1
) when
n=1
.

Due to a technical problem in proving a higher dimensional comparison result like (
1.7
), the authors of
[
18
]
pointed out that their proof does not work when
n>1
, and raised the problem that if one can still prove the higher dimensional Heisenberg uncertainty principle under their framework. This was later resolved independently in Tang
[
13
,
14
]
and in Dias-Luef-Prata
[
5
]
, where the starting point is that, if
A
is the Fourier transform
\mathscr{F}
, then replacing (
1.5
) by the Hausdorff-Young inequality
\|\mathscr{F}\|_{p\rightarrow p^{\prime}}\lesssim 1
for
\mathscr{F}
and
\|\mathscr{F}^{-1}\|_{p\rightarrow p^{\prime}}\lesssim 1
(
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
) for the inverse will first give many more interesting primary uncertainty principles than (
1.6
) if the Hölder’s inequality could be properly used, such that the comparison of localization
H_{1,2}^{(1)}(f)
and
H_{2}^{(0)}(f)
is possible in higher dimensions. Such observation yields many new uncertainty principles for the Fourier transform. For example, Tang in
[
13
,
14
]
showed for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
p\in(1,\frac{2n}{n-1})
that

\frac{\left\||x|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\frac{\left\||\xi|\hat{f}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|\hat{f}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\gtrsim 1,\quad 0\neq f\in L^{p}\cap\mathscr{F}^{-1}L^{p},

(1.9)

which clearly implies the Heisenberg uncertainty principle (
1.1
) in any dimension by taking
p=2
.

The same logic applies if a linear operator
A
satisfies the Hausdorff-Young type inequality
\|A\|_{p\rightarrow p^{\prime}}\lesssim 1
as well as
\|A^{-1}\|_{p\rightarrow p^{\prime}}\lesssim 1
for the inverse, and this was the major concern in Dias-Luef-Prata
[
5
]
where the authors raised a condition called
special
k
- Hadamard operator
(see
[
5
, Definition 2]
) to insure these two estimates. A lot of interesting results were obtained in
[
5
]
, for example various uncertainty principles in the form of

\left\||x|^{\theta}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\alpha}\left\||\xi|^{\phi}Af\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\beta}\gtrsim\|f\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\alpha}\|Af\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\beta},

for various values of
\alpha,\beta,\theta,\phi,p,q,r>0
. We will discuss this and make a comparison with our results later.

Notations

We denote
A\lesssim B
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that
A\leq CB
, and denote
A\lesssim_{\alpha}B
if
C
depends on parameter
\alpha
. We denote
A\sim B
if
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
. For
p\in[1,\infty]
, we use
p^{\prime}
to denote the conjugate number of
p
satisfying
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
. We use
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to denote the Schwartz class in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and use
\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)dx
to define the Fourier transform of
f
. We also use
\mathscr{F}
and
\mathscr{F}^{-1}
to denote the Fourier transform and its inverse.

Let
\varphi(\xi)
be a radial bump function supported in the ball
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n};|\xi|\leq\frac{11}{10}\}
and equal to
1
on the ball
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n};|\xi|\leq 1\}
. For each number
N>0
, we define the Littlewood-Paley projection
P_{N}
via the Fourier multiplier

\widehat{P_{N}f}(\xi)=(\varphi(\xi/N)-\varphi(2\xi/N))\hat{f}(\xi).

1.2.
Motivation and Main Result

The purpose of the present paper is to maximize the utilization of the techniques developed in Tang
[
13
,
14
]
and Dias-Luef-Prata
[
5
]
under Wigdersons’ framework, and give a more general uncertainty principle for functions in the
L^{p}
setting.

Before introducing the main result, we make a brief regarding the different consideration in this paper. Instead of considering a linear operator
A
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
who and whose inverse satisfy the Hausdorff-Young type inequality, we will just consider two functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
defined in
\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}
respectively with maybe different dimensions, satisfying

\|f_{2}\|_{L^{m_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})}\leq C_{1}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}})},\quad\|f_{2}\|_{L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})}\geq C_{2}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{m_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}})}.

(1.10)

When
f_{2}=Af_{1}
where
A
is a linear operator, these two estimates are the boundedness of
A
and
A^{-1}
. Unlike the previous work
[
5
]
where the boundedness were assumed to be the Hausdorff-Young type, here
m_{1},m_{2},q_{1},q_{2}
are unknown and will only be determined in every specific application. Note that these two estimates already give a primary uncertainty principle

\frac{\|f_{1}\|_{L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}})}}{\|f_{1}\|_{L^{m_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}})}}\frac{\|f_{2}\|_{L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})}}{\|f_{2}\|_{L^{m_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})}}\geq\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}}.

(1.11)

In particular, the dependence between
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
is not necessarily linear, so our theory may serve nonlinear problems.

Our main result is the following uncertainty principle.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
i=1,2
, suppose
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
b_{i},q_{i}\in(0,\infty)
and
a_{i},k_{i},m_{i}\in(0,\infty]
with

\mbox{$\frac{n_{i}}{a_{i}^{-1}n_{i}+b_{i}}$}<k_{i}\leq m_{i},\quad\mbox{$\frac{n_{i}}{a_{i}^{-1}n_{i}+b_{i}}$}<q_{i}<m_{i}.

(1.12)

If
0\neq f_{i}\in L^{q_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}})\cap L^{k_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}})\cap L^{m_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}})
(
i=1,2
) satisfy (
1.10
) for some
C_{1},C_{2}>0
, then

\begin{split}&\left(\frac{\left\||\cdot-x_{1}|^{b_{1}}f_{1}\right\|_{L^{a_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}})}}{\|f_{1}\|_{L^{k_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}})}}\right)^{(\frac{1}{q_{1}}-\frac{1}{m_{1}})(\frac{1}{a_{2}}+\frac{b_{2}}{n_{2}}-\frac{1}{k_{2}})}\left(\frac{\left\||\cdot-x_{2}|^{b_{2}}f_{2}\right\|_{L^{a_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})}}{\|f_{2}\|_{L^{k_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})}}\right)^{(\frac{1}{q_{2}}-\frac{1}{m_{2}})(\frac{1}{a_{1}}+\frac{b_{1}}{n_{1}}-\frac{1}{k_{1}})}\\
\gtrsim&{}_{n_{i},a_{i},b_{i},q_{i},k_{i},m_{i}\,(i=1,2)}\left(\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}}\right)^{(\frac{1}{a_{1}}+\frac{b_{1}}{n_{1}}-\frac{1}{k_{1}})(\frac{1}{a_{2}}+\frac{b_{2}}{n_{2}}-\frac{1}{k_{2}})},\quad x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}},\,i=1,2.\end{split}

(1.13)

Next we go through the applications of this result including Fourier transform and Fourier transform-like operators, but the main focus will be on the the applications to partial differential equations.

1.3.
Uncertainty Principles for the Fourier transform

By Hausdorff-Young’s inequality,
\|\hat{f}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
and
\|\hat{f}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\gtrsim\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
hold for all
p\in[1,2]
. If we apply Theorem
1.1
with
f_{1}=f
and
f_{2}=\hat{f}
, then the following uncertainty principle for the Fourier transform follows as an immediate consequence of Corollary
2.3
and Remark
2.4
in Section
2
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
a_{i}\in(0,\infty]
,
b_{i}\in(0,\infty)
with
\frac{n}{a_{i}^{-1}n+b_{i}}<2
(
i=1,2
), and suppose

\small k_{1}\in\begin{cases}(\mbox{$\frac{n}{a_{1}^{-1}n+b_{1}}$},\infty],\hskip 9.24994pt&\text{if $\frac{n}{a_{2}^{-1}n+b_{2}}<1$},\\
\left(\mbox{$\frac{n}{a_{1}^{-1}n+b_{1}},(\frac{n}{a_{2}^{-1}n+b_{2}})^{\prime}$}\right),&\text{if $\frac{n}{a_{2}^{-1}n+b_{2}}\geq 1$},\end{cases}\hskip 9.24994ptk_{2}\in\begin{cases}(\mbox{$\frac{n}{a_{2}^{-1}n+b_{2}}$},\infty],\hskip 9.24994pt&\text{if $\frac{n}{a_{1}^{-1}n+b_{1}}<1$},\\
\left(\mbox{$\frac{n}{a_{2}^{-1}n+b_{2}},(\frac{n}{a_{1}^{-1}n+b_{1}})^{\prime}$}\right),&\text{if $\frac{n}{a_{1}^{-1}n+b_{1}}\geq 1$}.\end{cases}

Then for
0\neq f\in L^{k_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathscr{F}^{-1}L^{k_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
x_{0},\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\left(\frac{\left\||x-x_{0}|^{b_{1}}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{a_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|f\|_{L^{k_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\right)^{\frac{1}{a_{2}}+\frac{b_{2}}{n}-\frac{1}{k_{2}}}\left(\frac{\left\||\xi-\xi_{0}|^{b_{1}}\hat{f}\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{a_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|\hat{f}\|_{L^{k_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\right)^{\frac{1}{a_{1}}+\frac{b_{1}}{n}-\frac{1}{k_{1}}}\gtrsim_{n,a_{i},b_{i},k_{i}}1.

Remark 1.3
.

This theorem as well as its corollaries below also hold when
\hat{f}
is replaced by
Af
where
A
is a linear operator with
\|Af\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
and
\|Af\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\gtrsim\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
for all
p\in[1,2]
, i.e.
A
and
A^{-1}
both satisfy the Hausdorff-Young type inequality in the full range.

For a comparison with those results proved in Dias-Luef-Prata
[
5
]
and Tang
[
13
,
14
]
, we explore some special cases where notations are adapted to those used in
[
5
,
13
,
14
]
, and we always assume
x_{0}=\xi_{0}=0
.

The first special case is to consider
k_{1}=k_{2}
. In Theorem
1.2
, let
a_{1}=p
,
b_{1}=\theta
,
a_{2}=q
,
b_{2}=\phi
and
k_{1}=k_{2}=r
, we have the follow result generalizing the Cowling-Price uncertainty principle.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
\theta,\phi\in(0,\infty)
and
p,q,r\in(0,\infty]
with

(1)

\frac{\theta}{n}>\frac{1}{\min\{2,r\}}-\frac{1}{p}
,
\frac{\phi}{n}>\frac{1}{\min\{2,r\}}-\frac{1}{q}
;

(2)

r<(\frac{n}{p^{-1}n+\theta})^{\prime}
whenever
\frac{n}{p^{-1}n+\theta}\geq 1
;

(3)

r<(\frac{n}{q^{-1}n+\phi})^{\prime}
whenever
\frac{n}{q^{-1}n+\phi}\geq 1
.

Then

\small\left(\frac{\left\||x|^{\theta}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|f\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\right)^{\phi-n(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{q})}\left(\frac{\left\||\xi|^{\phi}\hat{f}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|\hat{f}\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\right)^{\theta-n(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p})}\gtrsim_{n,p,q,r,\theta,\phi}1,\hskip 9.24994pt0\neq f\in L^{r}\cap\mathscr{F}^{-1}L^{r}.

(1.14)

In particular, when
0<r\leq 2
, the conditions (2) and (3) are null for (1) implies
\frac{n}{p^{-1}n+\theta},\frac{n}{q^{-1}n+\phi}<2
. The original Cowling-Price uncertainty principle (see
[
4
]
) is the case
r=2
,
n=1
where
p,q\in[1,\infty]
,
\theta+\frac{1}{p}=\phi+\frac{1}{q}
, and the higher dimensional case
r=2
,
n>1
where
p,q\in(1,\infty)
were obtained in Tang
[
13
,
14
]
, so Corollary
1.4
extends such results to the range
p,q\in(0,1]\cup\{\infty\}
. Corollary
1.4
(with
\hat{f}
replaced by
Af
) also extends Dias-Luef-Prata
[
5
, Theorem 18]
where (
1.14
) was proved when
1\leq r<2
and
p,q>r
. It is also easy to check that Corollary
1.4
extends
[
5
, Theorems 14 and 15]
.

In the next special case, we let
a_{1}=a_{2}=p
,
b_{1}=b_{2}=\theta
and
k_{1}=k_{2}=q
in Theorem
1.2
to obtain the following.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N_{+}}
,
\theta\in(0,\infty)
,
p,q\in(0,\infty]
satisfy
\frac{n}{p^{-1}n+\theta}<\min\{2,q\}
and

\mbox{$q<\left(\frac{n}{p^{-1}n+\theta}\right)^{\prime}$},\quad\text{whenever }\mbox{$\frac{n}{p^{-1}n+\theta}\geq 1$}.

Then

\frac{\left\||x|^{\theta}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\frac{\left\||\xi|^{\theta}\hat{f}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|\hat{f}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\gtrsim_{n,p,q,\theta}1,\quad 0\neq f\in L^{q}\cap\mathscr{F}^{-1}L^{q}.

(1.15)

(
1.15
) was obtained in Dias-Luef-Prata
[
5
, Theorem 17]
when
p\in[1,2)
and
q\in[p,p^{\prime}]
, and Corollary

is an extension of such result because clearly
\frac{n}{p^{-1}n+\theta}<p
, so that
p^{\prime}<(\frac{n}{p^{-1}n+\theta})^{\prime}
whenever
\frac{n}{p^{-1}n+\theta}\geq 1
.

If

and

, then the condition in Corollary

reads

q\in\begin{cases}(\frac{2}{3},\infty],\quad&n=1,\\
(\frac{2n}{n+2},\frac{2n}{n-2}),&n\geq 2,\end{cases}

which slightly extends Dias-Luef-Prata
[
5
, Theorem 20]
where
q\in[1,\infty]
was assumed when
n=1
, and we remark that the conclusion is not true if
n\geq 3
and
q\in[1,\frac{2n}{n+2})\cup(\frac{2n}{n-2},\infty]
, see
[

.

The last special case was initially considered in Tang
[

]
(or the earlier version
[

]
) as mentioned in (

). If
a_{1}=a_{2}=k_{1}=k_{2}=p
and

,

\mbox{$p<\left(\frac{n}{p^{-1}n+1}\right)^{\prime}$},\quad\text{whenever }\mbox{$\frac{n}{p^{-1}n+1}\geq 1$}.

But this is equivalent to say

Corollary 1.6
.

If
n\in\mathbb{N_{+}}
and
p\in(0,\frac{2n}{n-1})
, then

\frac{\left\||x|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\frac{\left\||\xi|\hat{f}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|\hat{f}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\gtrsim_{n,p}1,\quad 0\neq f\in L^{p}\cap\mathscr{F}^{-1}L^{p}.

(1.16)

When
p=2
, this is the Heisenberg uncertainty principle without optimal constant. Tang
[
14
, Theorem 2]
obtained (
1.16
) when
p\in(1,\frac{2n}{n-1})
, and it was also proved that (
1.16
) is not true when
p\in(\frac{2n}{n-1},\infty)
. It was asked in
[

]
that whether (
1.16
) still holds when

, and we partially extend such result to
p\in(0,1]
.

1.4.
Moment Growth for Linear Dispersive Equations

Heuristically for dispersive equations, since the dispersion and conservation law force the mass or energy to be ejected to the infinity, the relevant moments should grow in time. We give some examples to show how exactly the moments grow. The idea is that in Theorem
1.1
, if
f_{2}
is a solution of a dispersive equation at time
t
with initial data
f_{1}
, then (
1.10
) can be chosen essentially from inequalities that give the dispersion and conservation law of the equation.

We first consider the linear Schrödinger equation

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+n},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}.\end{cases}

Essentially from the dispersive estimate
\|e^{it\Delta}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{n}{2}}
and
L^{2}
isometry of
e^{it\Delta}
, we can obtain an uncertainty principle for the solution by considering
f_{1}(x)=u_{0}(x)
and
f_{2}(x)=u(t,x)=e^{it\Delta}u_{0}(x)
in Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N_{+}}
,
b_{i}\in(0,\infty)
,
a_{i},k_{i}\in(0,\infty]
satisfy
\frac{n}{a_{i}^{-1}n+b_{i}}<\min\{2,k_{i}\}
(
i=1,2
) and

\begin{cases}k_{1}<\left(\frac{n}{a_{2}^{-1}n+b_{2}}\right)^{\prime},\quad\text{whenever }\frac{n}{a_{2}^{-1}n+b_{2}}\geq 1,\\
k_{2}<\left(\frac{n}{a_{1}^{-1}n+b_{1}}\right)^{\prime},\quad\text{whenever }\frac{n}{a_{1}^{-1}n+b_{1}}\geq 1.\end{cases}

Then for
0\neq u_{0}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\begin{split}&\left(\frac{\left\||x-x_{0}|^{b_{1}}u_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{a_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{k_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\right)^{\frac{1}{a_{2}}+\frac{b_{2}}{n}-\frac{1}{k_{2}}}\left(\frac{\left\||x-x_{1}|^{b_{2}}e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{a_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\|_{L^{k_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\right)^{\frac{1}{a_{1}}+\frac{b_{1}}{n}-\frac{1}{k_{1}}}\\
\gtrsim&{}_{n,a_{i},b_{i},k_{i}}|t|^{n(\frac{1}{a_{1}}+\frac{b_{1}}{n}-\frac{1}{k_{1}})(\frac{1}{a_{2}}+\frac{b_{2}}{n}-\frac{1}{k_{2}})},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},\,x_{0},x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{split}

(1.17)

In particular, taking
k_{2}=2
, we obtain the moment growth:

\left\||x-x_{0}|^{b}e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\gtrsim_{u_{0},n,a,b}|t|^{n(\frac{1}{a}+\frac{b}{n}-\frac{1}{2})},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},\,x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(1.18)

provided
a\in(0,\infty]
,
b\in(0,\infty)
and
\frac{n}{a^{-1}n+b}<2
.

Remark 1.8
.

The result of course holds if we replace
\Delta
by a self-adjoint operator
H
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
sharing the same dispersive estimate
\|e^{itH}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{n}{2}}
. For example,
H=\Delta+V(x)
where
V
is a real-valued potential with sufficient decay and smoothness, and

has no zero energy resonance, see e.g.
[
9
,
12
,
19
,
6
,
2
,
3
]
and references therein. If
e^{itH}
has a different dispersive estimate

, the same argument in the proof in Section
3
can also give the theorem with different bounds in (
1.17
) and (
1.18
).

Remark 1.9
.

If
b>0
is an integer, the vector field method can also give

\left\||x|^{b}e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\sim_{n,u_{0}}|t|^{b},\quad|t|\gg 1.

(1.19)

see Cazenave
[

for

) coincides with (

) in such case.

The proof of the above theorem for Schrödinger equation (see Section

We next turn to another example of the wave equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}u=\Delta u,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+n},\\

(1.20)

whose conserved energy is

Since the dispersive estimate for (

) (see Section

of frequencies

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N_{+}}
,
b_{i}\in(0,\infty)
,

satisfy
\frac{n}{a_{i}^{-1}n+b_{i}}<\min\{2,k_{i}\}
(

) and

\begin{cases}k_{1}<\left(\frac{n}{a_{2}^{-1}n+b_{2}}\right)^{\prime},\quad\text{whenever }\frac{n}{a_{2}^{-1}n+b_{2}}\geq 1,\\
k_{2}<\left(\frac{n}{a_{1}^{-1}n+b_{1}}\right)^{\prime},\quad\text{whenever }\frac{n}{a_{1}^{-1}n+b_{1}}\geq 1.\end{cases}

If
(u,\partial_{t}u)\in C(\mathbb{R}:\dot{H}\times L^{2})
solves (
1.20
), then

\footnotesize\begin{split}&\left(\frac{\left\||x-x_{0}|^{b_{1}}\left(|\sqrt{-\Delta}P_{N}u_{0}|+|P_{N}u_{1}|\right)\right\|_{L_{x}^{a_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\left\||\sqrt{-\Delta}P_{N}u_{0}|+|P_{N}u_{1}|\right\|_{L^{k_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\right)^{\frac{1}{a_{2}}+\frac{b_{2}}{n}-\frac{1}{k_{2}}}\left(\frac{\left\||x-x_{1}|^{b_{2}}\left(|\sqrt{-\Delta}P_{N}u|+|\partial_{t}P_{N}u|\right)\right\|_{L_{x}^{a_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\left\||\sqrt{-\Delta}P_{N}u|+|\partial_{t}P_{N}u|\right\|_{L^{k_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\right)^{\frac{1}{a_{1}}+\frac{b_{1}}{n}-\frac{1}{k_{1}}}\\
\gtrsim&{}_{n,a_{i},b_{i},k_{i}}\left((1+|t|N)^{n-1}N^{-2n}\right)^{(\frac{1}{a_{1}}+\frac{b_{1}}{n}-\frac{1}{k_{1}})(\frac{1}{a_{2}}+\frac{b_{2}}{n}-\frac{1}{k_{2}})},\hskip 8.50012ptt\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},\,N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}},\,x_{0},x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{split}

(1.21)

and
\sqrt{-\Delta}P_{N}u,\partial_{t}P_{N}u\in L^{k_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In particular, taking

\begin{split}&\left\||x-x_{0}|^{b}\left(|\sqrt{-\Delta}P_{N}u|+|\partial_{t}P_{N}u|\right)\right\|_{L_{x}^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\

provided

A similar result can also be established for the Klein-Gordon equation

1.5.

We can also give an application of Theorem

(1.22)

) at time

. For example,
\Omega
is the complement of a ball in

(see Wang-Wang-Zhang
[
16
]
);
\Omega
is nonempty and open, satisfying the
Geometric Control Condition
or being periodic (See Täufer
[
15
]

Considering
f_{1}(x)=u_{0}(x)

f_{2}(t,x)=\mathbbm{1}_{[0,T]}(t)\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega}(x)u(t,x)

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N_{+}}
,
a_{i}\in(0,\infty]

\mbox{$\frac{n}{a_{1}^{-1}n+b_{1}}$}<k_{1}\leq 2,\quad\mbox{$\frac{n+1}{a_{2}^{-1}(n+1)+b_{2}}$}<\min\{2,k_{2}\},\quad k_{2}<\mbox{$\frac{2n+4}{n}$}.

(1.24)

u=e^{it\Delta}u_{0}
with
0\neq u_{0}\in L^{k_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an observable set, and

\begin{split}&\left(\frac{\left\||x-x_{0}|^{b_{1}}u_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{a_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{k_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\right)^{\frac{1}{a_{2}}+\frac{b_{2}}{n+1}-\frac{1}{k_{2}}}\left(\frac{\left\||(t-t_{0},x-x_{1})|^{b_{2}}u\right\|_{L_{t,x}^{a_{2}}([0,T]\times\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{L_{t,x}^{k_{2}}([0,T]\times\Omega)}}\right)^{\frac{1}{a_{1}}+\frac{b_{1}}{n}-\frac{1}{k_{1}}}\\

A parallel result can also be established for the heat equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\Delta u=0,\quad t\in(0,T],\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\

Recall in the control theory that the
observability inequality
at time

\|e^{t\Delta}u_{0}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\gtrsim_{T,\Omega}\|e^{T\Delta}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

\Omega

\left|\Omega\cap(x+LQ)\right|\geq\gamma L^{n},

\|e^{t\Delta}u_{0}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\gtrsim_{T,\Omega}e^{-TR^{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(1.27)

for the heat equation.

Theorem 1.12
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N_{+}}
,

(

1.6.
Moment Growth for a Nonlinear Schrödinger Equation

Finally we give an example of applying Theorem

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+\phi(t,x)|u|^{m-1}u=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+n},\\

The point is that, when considering

Proposition 1.13
.

Given
p\in\begin{cases}(2,\infty],\,&n=1,\\
(2,\frac{2n}{n-2}),\,&n\geq 2,\end{cases}

, and real-valued

which fulfills

\displaystyle\|\phi(t,x)\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-m-1}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C|t|^{\frac{nm}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}\eta(t),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

for some constant
C>0
and some non-negative valued function
\eta(t)
satisfying

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta(t)dt\lesssim 1\quad\text{and}\quad\sup\limits_{\frac{|t|}{2}\leq|s|\leq|t|,t\in\mathbb{R}}|t|\eta(s)\lesssim 1.

Consider the Cauchy problem (
1.29
) where
u_{0}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
0<\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\ll 1

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\ll 1
when

If
a_{i}\in(0,\infty]
and

(

) satisfy

\mbox{$\frac{n}{a_{1}^{-1}n+b_{1}}$}<p^{\prime}\leq k_{1}\leq 2\quad\text{and}\quad\mbox{$\frac{n}{a_{2}^{-1}n+b_{2}}$}<2\leq k_{2}\leq p,

to (

\begin{split}&\left(\frac{\left\||x-x_{0}|^{b_{1}}u_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{a_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{k_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\right)^{\frac{1}{a_{2}}+\frac{b_{2}}{n}-\frac{1}{k_{2}}}\left(\frac{\left\||x-x_{1}|^{b_{2}}u(t,x)\right\|_{L_{x}^{a_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|u(t,x)\|_{L_{x}^{k_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\right)^{\frac{1}{a_{1}}+\frac{b_{1}}{n}-\frac{1}{k_{1}}}\\

In particular, taking

\left\||x-x_{0}|^{b}u(t,x)\right\|_{L_{x}^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\gtrsim_{u_{0},n,a,b}|t|^{n(\frac{1}{a}+\frac{b}{n}-\frac{1}{2})},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},\,x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

Proof of Theorem
1.1

Theorem
1.1
can be proved using two easy lemmas.

The first one has actually been proved in Dias-Luef-Prata
[
5
, Theorem 9]
in the non-endpoint case
p<a\neq\infty
. We give a very concise proof here.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
a\in(0,\infty]
,
b\in(0,\infty)
,
s\in[1,\infty)
and
p\in(\frac{n}{a^{-1}n+b},a]\setminus\{\infty\}
. It follows for all
0\neq f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{ps^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
that

\left\||x-x_{0}|^{b}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\gtrsim_{n,a,b,s,p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left(\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|f\|_{L^{ps^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\right)^{s\left((\frac{1}{a}+\frac{b}{n})p-1\right)}.

(2.1)

Proof.

We may assume
x_{0}=0
by translation. Set

T=v_{n}^{-\frac{1}{n}}\left(\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{2\|f\|_{L^{ps^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\right)^{\frac{ps}{n}},\quad v_{n}=\int_{|x|\leq 1}dx,

then

\int_{|x|\leq T}|f|^{p}dx\leq(T^{n}v_{n})^{\frac{1}{s}}\|f\|_{L^{ps^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}=\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p},

which means

\begin{split}\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\leq\int_{|x|\geq T}|f|^{p}dx\leq\left\||x|^{-bp}\right\|_{L^{\frac{1}{1-a^{-1}p}}(|x|\geq T)}\left\||x|^{b}f\right\|_{L^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}.\end{split}

Since
p\in(\frac{n}{a^{-1}n+b},a]\setminus\{\infty\}
implies
\left\||x|^{-bp}\right\|_{L^{\frac{1}{1-a^{-1}p}}(|x|\geq T)}\sim T^{n-(a^{-1}n+b)p}
, (
2.1
) immediately follows.
∎

The second lemma is nothing but Hölder’s inequality.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
k,p,q,m\in(0,\infty]
satisfy

\begin{cases}m\leq k\leq p,\\
m<q\leq p,\end{cases}\quad\text{or}\quad\begin{cases}p\leq k\leq m,\\
p\leq q<m,\end{cases}

then

\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|f\|_{L^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\geq\left(\frac{\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|f\|_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\right)^{\frac{p^{-1}-k^{-1}}{q^{-1}-m^{-1}}},\quad 0\neq f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(2.2)

Proof.

Since
m\neq p
and
m\neq q
, one checks with the Hölder’s inequality that

\|f\|_{L^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{m^{-1}-k^{-1}}{m^{-1}-p^{-1}}}\|f\|_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{k^{-1}-p^{-1}}{m^{-1}-p^{-1}}},

and that

\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{p^{-1}-k^{-1}}{q^{-1}-m^{-1}}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{k^{-1}-p^{-1}}{m^{-1}-p^{-1}}}\|f\|_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{(k^{-1}-p^{-1})(p^{-1}-q^{-1})}{(m^{-1}-p^{-1})(q^{-1}-m^{-1})}}.

Multiplying them gives (
2.2
).
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

(
1.12
) guarantees the existence of
p_{i}\in(\frac{n_{i}}{a_{i}^{-1}n_{i}+b_{i}},\min\{a_{i},k_{i},q_{i}\}]
and
s_{i}\in[1,\infty)
with
p_{i}s_{i}^{\prime}=k_{i}
. Note that
p_{i}\leq q_{i}<\infty
, so we can use Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
to deduce

\begin{split}\frac{\left\||\cdot-x_{i}|^{b_{i}}f_{i}\right\|_{L^{a_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}})}}{\|f_{i}\|_{L^{k_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}})}}\gtrsim\left(\frac{\|f_{i}\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}})}}{\|f_{i}\|_{L^{k_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}})}}\right)^{s_{i}\left((\frac{1}{a_{i}}+\frac{b_{i}}{n_{i}})p_{i}-1\right)+1}\gtrsim\left(\frac{\|f_{i}\|_{L^{q_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}})}}{\|f_{i}\|_{L^{m_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}})}}\right)^{\frac{p_{i}^{-1}-k_{i}^{-1}}{q_{i}^{-1}-m_{i}^{-1}}\left(s_{i}\left((\frac{1}{a_{i}}+\frac{b_{i}}{n_{i}})p_{i}-1\right)+1\right)}.\end{split}

One can use
k_{i}^{-1}=(p_{i}s_{i}^{\prime})^{-1}
to check

\frac{p_{i}^{-1}-k_{i}^{-1}}{q_{i}^{-1}-m_{i}^{-1}}\left(s_{i}\left((\mbox{$\frac{1}{a_{i}}+\frac{b_{i}}{n_{i}}$})p_{i}-1\right)+1\right)=\frac{\frac{1}{a_{i}}+\frac{b_{i}}{n_{i}}-\frac{1}{k_{i}}}{\frac{1}{q_{i}}-\frac{1}{m_{i}}},

and therefore

\begin{split}&\left(\frac{\left\||\cdot-x_{1}|^{b_{1}}f_{1}\right\|_{L^{a_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}})}}{\|f_{1}\|_{L^{k_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}})}}\right)^{(\frac{1}{q_{1}}-\frac{1}{m_{1}})(\frac{1}{a_{2}}+\frac{b_{2}}{n_{2}}-\frac{1}{k_{2}})}\left(\frac{\left\||\cdot-x_{2}|^{b_{2}}f_{2}\right\|_{L^{a_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})}}{\|f_{2}\|_{L^{k_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})}}\right)^{(\frac{1}{q_{2}}-\frac{1}{m_{2}})(\frac{1}{a_{1}}+\frac{b_{1}}{n_{1}}-\frac{1}{k_{1}})}\\

Proof of Proposition

The
L^{2}
theory first tells us the following (see Linares-Ponce [Theorem 5.2 and Corollary 5.2]
[
11
]
with no change of the proofs).

Lemma 5.1
.

If
m\in(1,1+\frac{4}{n}]
,
\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})
is real-valued,
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and in addition
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\ll 1
when
m=1+\frac{4}{n}
, then the unique solution
u\in C(\mathbb{R}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
to (
1.29
) exists.

We further establish a simple result for the solution in
L^{p}
. For
p\in(2,\infty]
, define
X_{p}=\{f(t,x);\|f\|_{X_{p}}<\infty\}
where

\|f\|_{X_{p}}=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|t|^{\frac{n}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume that
p\in\begin{cases}(2,\infty],\,&n=1,\\
(2,\frac{2n}{n-2}),\,&n\geq 2,\end{cases}

m\in(1,p-1]
,
r=\frac{p}{p-m-1}
(i.e.
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{m}{p}=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}
), and
\phi(t,x)
is a measurable function fulfilling

\displaystyle\|\phi(t,x)\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C|t|^{\frac{nm}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}\eta(t),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

for some constant
C>0
and some non-negative valued function
\eta(t)
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta(t)dt\lesssim 1\quad\text{and}\quad\sup\limits_{\frac{{|t|}}{2}\leq|s|\leq|t|,t\in\mathbb{R}}|t|\eta(s)\lesssim 1.

Then there exists
\epsilon=\epsilon(n,\phi,p,m)>0
such that when
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\epsilon
, (
1.29
) has a unique solution
u
in
X_{p}
, and

\|u\|_{X_{p}}\lesssim_{n,\phi,p,m}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

Define operator
T
by

(Tf)(t,x)=e^{it\Delta}u_{0}+i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}\phi(s,x)|f(s,x)|^{m-1}f(s,x)ds.

We first show that if
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\epsilon
and
\epsilon
is small, then
T
maps the closed ball with radius
2K\epsilon
in
X_{p}
into itself for some constant
K=K(n,\phi,p)>0
. Using
\|e^{it\Delta}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{n}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}
, we deduce

\begin{split}\|Tf(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}&\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{n}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\left|\int_{0}^{t}|t-s|^{-\frac{n}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}\left\|\phi(s,\cdot)|f(s,\cdot)|^{m}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}ds\right|\\
&\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{n}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\left|\int_{0}^{t}|t-s|^{-\frac{n}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}\|\phi(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|f(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{m}ds\right|\\
&\lesssim_{\phi}|t|^{-\frac{n}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|f\|_{X_{p}}^{m}\left|\int_{0}^{t}|t-s|^{-\frac{n}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}\eta(s)ds\right|,\end{split}

and thus

\begin{split}\|Tf\|_{X_{p}}&\lesssim_{\phi}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|f\|^{m}_{X_{p}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}|t|^{\frac{n}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}|t-s|^{-\frac{n}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}\eta(s)ds\right|\\
&\lesssim_{\phi,n,p}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|f\|^{m}_{X_{p}},\end{split}

where we have used

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}|t|^{\frac{n}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}|t-s|^{-\frac{n}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}\eta(s)ds\right|\lesssim_{n,p}\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}\eta(s)ds\right|\lesssim_{n,p}1,\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R},

and

\begin{split}\left|\int^{t}_{\frac{t}{2}}|t|^{\frac{n}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}|t-s|^{-\frac{n}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}\eta(s)ds\right|&\lesssim|t|^{\frac{n}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}\sup_{\frac{|t|}{2}\leq|s|\leq|t|}\eta(s)\left|\int^{t}_{\frac{t}{2}}|t-s|^{-\frac{n}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}ds\right|\\
&\lesssim_{n,p}|t|\sup_{\frac{|t|}{2}\leq|s|\leq|t|}\eta(s)\\
&\lesssim_{n,p}1,\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.\end{split}

Now we have for some
K=K(n,\phi,p)>0
that

\begin{split}\|Tf\|_{X_{p}}&\leq K\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|f\|_{X_{p}}^{m}\right)\\
&\leq K(\epsilon+(2K\epsilon)^{m})\\
&\leq 2K\epsilon,\end{split}

provided that
\epsilon\ll_{K,m}1
. It is parallel to show that
T
contracts, so solution
u
exists in
X_{p}
by the fixed point argument, satisfying

\|u\|_{X_{p}}\lesssim K\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

∎

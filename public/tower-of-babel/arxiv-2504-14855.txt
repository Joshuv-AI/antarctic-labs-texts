New chiral structures for nucleon baryon number violating decays
Yi Liao, Xiao-Dong Ma, Hao-Lin Wang

Abstract
We examine the most general nucleon decay interactions that involve three light quarks without being acted upon by a derivative. We identify four generic operator structures that correspond to the irreducible representations in the chiral group SU ​ ( 3 ) 𝙻 ⊗ SU ​ ( 3 ) 𝚁 {\rm SU(3)}_{\tt L}\otimes{\rm SU(3)}_{\tt R} of QCD, { 𝟖 𝙻 ⊗ 𝟏 𝚁 \boldsymbol{8}_{\tt L}\otimes\boldsymbol{1}_{\tt R} , 𝟑 ¯ 𝙻 ⊗ 𝟑 𝚁 \bar{\boldsymbol{3}}_{\tt L}\otimes\boldsymbol{3}_{\tt R} , 𝟔 𝙻 ⊗ 𝟑 𝚁 \boldsymbol{6}_{\tt L}\otimes\boldsymbol{3}_{\tt R} , 𝟏𝟎 𝙻 ⊗ 𝟏 𝚁 \boldsymbol{10}_{\tt L}\otimes\boldsymbol{1}_{\tt R} }, plus their chirality partners under the interchange of chiralities 𝙻 ↔ 𝚁 {\tt L}\leftrightarrow{\tt R} . While half of them have been extensively discussed in the literature, the other half, 𝟔 𝙻 ⁡ ( 𝚁 ) ⊗ 𝟑 𝚁 ⁡ ( 𝙻 ) \boldsymbol{6}_{\tt L(R)}\otimes\boldsymbol{3}_{\tt R(L)} and 𝟏𝟎 ¯ 𝙻 ⁡ ( 𝚁 ) ⊗ 𝟏 𝚁 ⁡ ( 𝙻 ) \bar{\boldsymbol{10}}_{\tt L(R)}\otimes\boldsymbol{1}_{\tt R(L)} , are identified for the first time. We perform chiral matching for these interactions at the leading chiral order and find that each has a unique chiral realization in terms of the octet baryons and pseudoscalars. These new structures are prevalent in effective field theories and UV models, and offer novel experimental avenues to search for baryon number violating nucleon decays.



Introduction.

Baryon number violation is one of the three Sahkarov conditions
Sakharov:1967dj
to explain the overwhelming dominance of matter over antimatter in the current Universe. And baryon number violating (BNV) nucleon decays are the most feasible processes that can be searched for in terrestrial laboratories. Because of its fundamental importance, experimental endeavors have a long history that dates back to the latter half of the 20th century. Large fiducial mass experiments, such as
IMB
Irvine-Michigan-Brookhaven:1983iap
,
SNO+
SNO:2018ydj
,
KamLAND
KamLAND:2015pvi
,
Kamiokande
Hirata:1988ad
and its upgrade, Super-Kamiokande
Takhistov:2016eqm
,
have investigated various characteristic nucleon decays, yielding null results that have imposed stringent limits on their occurrence. The next generation of neutrino experiments
Dev:2022jbf
, including DUNE
DUNE:2016evb
;
DUNE:2020ypp
, Hyper-Kamiokande
Hyper-Kamiokande:2018ofw
, JUNO
JUNO:2015zny
, and THEIA
Theia:2019non
, are expected to further enhance sensitivity to nucleon decays.
It is important that all possible decay channels should be attempted, exotic as well as conventional, and this requires a complete analysis of relevant interactions at both effective and fundamental levels.

BNV nucleon decays are rare, if existing at all. This implies that baryon number violation must originate from physics at a very high energy scale. Indeed, baryon number violation is a common prediction in theories beyond the standard model (SM) such as grand unified theories
Georgi:1974sy
;
Sakai:1981pk
;
Hisano:1992jj
. Effective field theory (EFT) provides a systematic and consistent framework to relate the physical origin of BNV
at high energy to the experimentally sought processes at low energy. Most importantly, with minimal assumptions it does not rely on the details of high energy physics and is thus universal in a broad sense.

In this Letter we investigate general BNV interactions of the octet baryons that violate baryon number by one unit (
|\Delta B|=1
) and thus contribute to BNV nucleon decays. To employ experimental results at low energy to shed light on physical origin of BNV at high energy, we explore BNV interactions in the low energy effective field theory (LEFT) that involve three light quarks without being acted upon by a derivative. By matching the interactions to those in chiral perturbation theory (ChPT), we can establish BNV effective interactions for the octet baryons and pseudoscalars, neutrinos and charged leptons, and the photon, plus any new light particles beyond the SM.

We find that the above triple quark structures are classified into four irreducible representations (irreps) plus their chirality partners under the chiral group
\rm SU(3)_{\tt L}\otimes SU(3)_{\tt R}
of QCD:

\boldsymbol{8}_{\tt L(R)}\otimes\boldsymbol{1}_{\tt R(L)}
,

\bar{\boldsymbol{3}}_{\tt L(R)}\otimes\boldsymbol{3}_{\tt R(L)}
,

\boldsymbol{6}_{\tt L(R)}\otimes\boldsymbol{3}_{\tt R(L)}
,

\boldsymbol{10}_{\tt L(R)}\otimes\boldsymbol{1}_{\tt R(L)}
, where the subscripts
{\tt L},{\tt R}
refer to the left- and right-handed chirality of quarks.
While the first half of irreps were known in the 1980s

and

\bar{\boldsymbol{3}}_{\tt L(R)}\otimes\boldsymbol{3}_{\tt R(L)}

Since then, nearly all phenomenological analyses of BNV nucleon decays have been based on these results. In particular, these chiral interactions have been extensively employed in recent studies of various nucleon decay modes from the EFT perspective
Hambye:2017qix
;
Fonseca:2018ehk

As we will show in this work, the new structures,

The effective operators
{\cal O}_{i}^{yzw}
in

are typically derived from interactions in SMEFT or its extensions defined at a scale above the electroweak (EW) scale
\Lambda_{\tt EW}\approx 160~\rm GeV

\Lambda_{\chi}\approx 1.2~\rm GeV
are influenced by the renormalization group evolution (RGE) effect, with the leading one coming from one-loop QCD corrections. Since these operators belong to distinct chiral irreps, they undergo separate multiplicative renormalization without mixing. Following the method in
Liao:2019gex
, we obtain the following RGE equations,

\displaystyle{dC_{a,b}^{yzw}\over d\ln\mu}

\displaystyle=-2{\alpha_{s}\over 2\pi}C_{a,b}^{yzw},

(12a)

\displaystyle{dC_{c}^{yzw}\over d\ln\mu}

\displaystyle=+{2\over 3}{\alpha_{s}\over 2\pi}C_{c}^{yzw},

(12b)

\displaystyle{dC_{d}^{yzw}\over d\ln\mu}

\displaystyle=+2{\alpha_{s}\over 2\pi}C_{d}^{yzw},

(12c)

where
\alpha_{s}=g_{s}^{2}/(4\pi)
with
g_{s}
being the QCD coupling constant. The results in
Eq.

12a
for the two usual representations,

\boldsymbol{8}_{\tt L}\otimes\boldsymbol{1}_{\tt R}
and
\bar{\boldsymbol{3}}_{\tt L}\otimes\boldsymbol{3}_{\tt R}

. The same results apply to the WCs of the chirality partner operators. Numerically, we find

C_{a,b}^{yzw}(\Lambda_{\chi})\approx 1.32\,C_{a,b}^{yzw}(\Lambda_{\tt EW})
,

C_{c}^{yzw}(\Lambda_{\chi})\approx 0.91\,C_{c}^{yzw}(\Lambda_{\tt EW})
, and

C_{d}^{yzw}(\Lambda_{\chi})\approx 0.76\,C_{d}^{yzw}(\Lambda_{\tt EW})
.

Chiral matching.

To obtain the hadronic counterparts of the quark-level interactions in
Eq.

10
, a consistent approach is to work with ChPT, which can systematically account for interactions of both the octet pseudoscalar mesons and baryons. In ChPT the meson and baryon octet fields are organized in the following matrix form,

\displaystyle\Sigma(x)

\displaystyle=\xi^{2}(x)=\exp\Big(\frac{i\sqrt{2}\Pi(x)}{F_{0}}\Big),

(13a)

\displaystyle\Pi(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\pi^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}+\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{6}}&\pi^{+}&K^{+}\\
\pi^{-}&-\frac{\pi^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}+\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{6}}&K^{0}\\
K^{-}&\bar{K}^{0}&-\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\eta\end{pmatrix},

(13b)

\displaystyle B(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}{\Sigma^{0}\over\sqrt{2}}+{\Lambda^{0}\over\sqrt{6}}&\Sigma^{+}&p\\
\Sigma^{-}&-{\Sigma^{0}\over\sqrt{2}}+{\Lambda^{0}\over\sqrt{6}}&n\\
\Xi^{-}&\Xi^{0}&-\sqrt{2\over 3}\Lambda^{0}\end{pmatrix},

(13c)

where
F_{0}=(86.2\pm 0.5)~\rm MeV
is the pion decay constant in the chiral limit. Their chiral transformation rules are,

\Sigma\to\hat{L}\Sigma\hat{R}^{\dagger}
,

B\to\hat{h}B\hat{h}^{\dagger}
,

\xi\to\hat{L}\xi\hat{h}^{\dagger}=\hat{h}\xi\hat{R}^{\dagger}
,
where the matrix
\hat{h}
is a function of
\hat{L},~\hat{R}
and
\xi
. For convenience we note that

\xi B\xi\to\hat{L}(\xi B\xi)\hat{R}^{\dagger}
,

\xi^{\dagger}B\xi^{\dagger}\to\hat{R}(\xi^{\dagger}B\xi^{\dagger})\hat{L}^{\dagger}
,

\xi B\xi^{\dagger}\to\hat{L}(\xi^{\dagger}B\xi^{\dagger})\hat{L}^{\dagger}
,
and
\xi^{\dagger}B\xi\to\hat{R}(\xi^{\dagger}B\xi)\hat{R}^{\dagger}
.

The chiral building blocks corresponding to the LEFT interactions in
Eq.

10
consist of the spurion fields, the meson and baryon octet fields and their derivatives. To identify their leading chiral counterparts, we adopt the chiral derivative power counting scheme in which

\{\Sigma,\xi,B,D_{\mu}B\}\sim{\cal O}(p^{0})
and

D_{\mu}\Sigma\sim{\cal O}(p^{1})
. The covariant derivatives are defined as
D_{\mu}\Sigma=\partial_{\mu}\Sigma-il_{\mu}\Sigma+i\Sigma r_{\mu}
,

D_{\mu}B=\partial_{\mu}B+[\Gamma_{\mu},B]
, where the external sources
l_{\mu}
and
r_{\mu}
are traceless matrices in the flavor space and
\Gamma_{\mu}=(1/2)\left[\xi(\partial_{\mu}-ir_{\mu})\xi^{\dagger}+\xi^{\dagger}(\partial_{\mu}-il_{\mu})\xi\right]
. These covariant derivatives transform as
B
and
\Sigma
themselves. It is important that we must account for Lorentz as well as chiral properties on chiral matching.
We find that, for the chiral irreps
\boldsymbol{6}_{{\tt L}({\tt R})}\otimes\boldsymbol{3}_{{\tt R}({\tt L})}
, the
(1,1/2)
and
(1/2,1)
components of a general vector-spinor object, such as
\partial^{\nu}B
, can be extracted by the projectors

{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\tt L,R}\equiv(g_{\mu\nu}-{1\over 4}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu})P_{\tt L,R}
,
which satisfy the relations,

{\Gamma}_{\mu\rho}^{\tt L,R}{\Gamma}_{~\nu}^{\tt L,R~\rho}={\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\tt L,R}
and

\gamma^{\mu}{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\tt L,R}=0
.
On the other hand, for the chiral irreps
\boldsymbol{10}_{{\tt L}({\tt R})}\otimes\boldsymbol{1}_{{\tt R}({\tt L})}
, we have to resort to the following projectors
DelgadoAcosta:2015ypa
:

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}^{\tt L,R}\equiv{1\over 24}\left(2[\sigma_{\mu\nu},\sigma_{\alpha\beta}]-\{\sigma_{\mu\nu},\sigma_{\alpha\beta}\}\right)P_{\tt L,R},

(14)

to extract the irreducible
(3/2,0)
and
(0,3/2)
components of a tensor-spinor object, such as
\partial^{\alpha}B\partial^{\beta}\Sigma
.
These projectors satisfy similar relations,

\hat{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}^{\tt L,R}\hat{\Gamma}_{~~\alpha\beta}^{\tt L,R~\rho\sigma}=\hat{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}^{\tt L,R}

and
\gamma^{\mu}\hat{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}^{\tt L,R}=0
.

We can now construct order by order the chiral interactions for the LEFT interactions in
Eqs.

10
and
11

We find that each of the chiral irreps

, and

\boldsymbol{6}_{{\tt L}({\tt R})}\otimes\boldsymbol{3}_{{\tt R}({\tt L})}

They can be succinctly parametrized as follows:

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{B}^{\not{B},0}

\displaystyle=c_{1}{\rm Tr}\big[{\cal P}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{3}}_{\tt L}\otimes\boldsymbol{3}_{\tt R}}\xi B_{\tt L}\xi-{\cal P}_{\boldsymbol{3}_{\tt L}\otimes\bar{\boldsymbol{3}}_{\tt R}}\xi^{\dagger}B_{\tt R}\xi^{\dagger}\big]

\displaystyle+c_{2}{\rm Tr}\big[{\cal P}_{\boldsymbol{8}_{\tt L}\otimes\boldsymbol{1}_{\tt R}}\xi B_{\tt L}\xi^{\dagger}-{\cal P}_{\boldsymbol{1}_{\tt L}\otimes\boldsymbol{8}_{\tt R}}\xi^{\dagger}B_{\tt R}\xi\big]

\displaystyle+{c_{3}\over\Lambda_{\chi}}\big[{\cal P}_{yzi}^{{\tt L}{\tt R},\mu}{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\tt L}(\xi iD^{\nu}B_{\tt L}\xi)_{yj}\Sigma_{zk}\epsilon_{ijk}

\displaystyle-{\cal P}_{yzi}^{{\tt R}{\tt L},\mu}{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\tt R}(\xi^{\dagger}iD^{\nu}B_{\tt R}\xi^{\dagger})_{yj}\Sigma^{*}_{kz}\epsilon_{ijk}\big]+\text{H.c.},

u,d,s

B_{{\tt L}({\tt R})}\equiv P_{{\tt L}({\tt R})}B

And

For the totally symmetric irreps

\boldsymbol{10}_{{\tt L}({\tt R})}\otimes\boldsymbol{1}_{{\tt R}({\tt L})}

\displaystyle-{\cal P}_{yzw}^{{\tt R}{\tt R},\mu\nu}\hat{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}^{\tt R}(\xi^{\dagger}D^{\alpha}B_{\tt R}\xi^{\dagger})_{iy}\Sigma^{*}_{jz}(D^{\beta}\Sigma)^{*}_{kw}\epsilon_{ijk}\big]

Conclusion.

The study of baryon number violating nucleon decays represents an exhilarating frontier in the exploration of new physics. Many experiments have been designed to search for nucleon decays, highlighting their significance in uncovering fundamental aspects of particle interactions. The general classification of triple-quark interactions and their chiral matching developed in this Letter would find many applications in the search of BNV processes. Notably, we have identified new chiral structures that go beyond the BNV interactions originated from the conventional dim-6 LEFT operators. This opens up new avenues for experimental investigations, including novel nucleon decays involving new light particles that may connect BNV interactions with the nature of dark matter as well as the neutrino mass puzzle. We emphasize that these structures are also pertinent to hyperon and tau lepton BNV decays.

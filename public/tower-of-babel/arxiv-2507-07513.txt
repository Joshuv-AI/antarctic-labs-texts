Refined Kato type inequalities and new vanishing theorems on complete Kähler and quaternionic Kähler manifolds
Dinh Tien Dat, Nguyen Thac Dung ∗, Yong Luo ∗

Abstract
. Given a complete Riemannian manifold satisfying a weighted Poincaré inequality and having a bounded below Ricci curvature, various vanishing theorems for harmonic functions and harmonic 1-forms have been published. We generalized these results to L p L^{p} -integrable pluriharmonic functions and harmonic 1-forms on complete Kähler and quaternionic Kähler manifolds respectively by utilizing the Böchner technique and several refined Kato type inequalities. Moreover, we also prove the vanishing property of pluriharmonic functions with finite L p L^{p} energy on complete Kähler manifolds satisfying a Sobolev type inequality.

Since the last decade of the 20th century, the weighted Poincaré inequality of numerous forms have been studied thoroughly due to many of its critical applications in analysis and mathematical physics. Consequently, various compelling structure theorems and vanishing properties of complete Riemannian manifolds satisfying the Ricci curvature lower bound and the weighted Poincaré inequality were derived (see

\rho(x)

(1.1)

\int_{M}\rho(x)\phi^{2}(x)\,dV\leq\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dV

on
M

Denote the greatest lower bound of the spectrum of the Laplacian acting on

\lambda_{1}(M)

\lambda_{1}(M)
asserts the validity of the following Poincaré inequality

\lambda_{1}(M)\int_{M}\phi^{2}(x)\,dV\leq\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dV

Theorem 1.1

(

Let

Ric_{M}\geq-2\lambda_{1}(M)+\epsilon

H^{1}(L^{2}(M))=0

Finally, we give a proof for the following vanishing theorem on complete Kähler manifolds satisfying a Sobolev type inequality, assuming that its Kähler curvature tensor is bounded from below.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete
2n
-dimensional complete Kähler manifold satisfying the following Sobolev type inequality (
\nu>2
)

\Biggl(\int_{M}\phi^{\frac{2\nu}{\nu-2}}\Biggl)^{\frac{\nu-2}{\nu}}\leq C_{S}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}.

Denote by
\mu_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\mu_{n^{2}}

(M,g)
. Suppose that there exists a function
\kappa:M\to\mathbb{R}

\kappa\leq 0

\frac{\mu_{1}(x)+\cdots+\mu_{n}(x)}{n+1}\geq\kappa(x)

at every point

u
on

is constant, provided that
|\nabla u|\in L^{p}\ (p>1)
,

\lVert\kappa\rVert_{\nu/2}<\frac{4}{pC_{S}(n+1)}

if

and

\lVert\kappa\rVert_{\nu/2}<\frac{4p}{(4-p)^{2}C_{S}(n+1)}

, where

\lVert\kappa\rVert_{\nu/2}:=\Bigl(\int_{M}\kappa^{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Bigl)^{\frac{2}{\nu}}.

By using Li’s result we have

Corollary 1.10
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete
2n
-dimensional complete Kähler manifold satisfying the following Sobolev type inequality (
\nu>2
)

\Biggl(\int_{M}\phi^{\frac{2\nu}{\nu-2}}\Biggl)^{\frac{\nu-2}{\nu}}\leq C_{S}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}.

Denote by
\mu_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\mu_{n^{2}}
the eigenvalues of the Kähler curvature operator of
(M,g)
. Suppose that there exists a function
\kappa:M\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\kappa\leq 0
and

\frac{\mu_{1}(x)+\cdots+\mu_{n}(x)}{n+1}\geq\kappa(x)

at every point

. Then every harmonic function

on

and

\lVert\kappa\rVert_{\nu/2}<\frac{2}{C_{S}(n+1)},

\lVert\kappa\rVert_{\nu/2}:=\Bigl(\int_{M}\kappa^{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Bigl)^{\frac{2}{\nu}}.

We note that the condition on Kähler curvature operator in Theorem

This also means our vanishing result is new, although the assumption on Sobolev type inequality is well-known. Moreover, we would like to emphasize that our vanishing results also hold for

energy with

2 Preliminaries

In
[
11
]
, the author gave proofs of some Kato type inequalities for
L^{2}
harmonic
1
-forms on Riemannian manifolds. Specifically, he proved that for every finite
L^{2}
harmonic 1-forms
\alpha
, it is true that
d*(\alpha\wedge\Omega)=0
where
\Omega
is a parallel form on
M
. From there, we can deduce a refined Kato type inequality for any
L^{2}
harmonic 1-form
\omega
on a Kähler manifold
M

|\nabla\omega|^{2}\geq 2|\nabla|\omega||^{2}.

When
p>1
, a similar approach cannot be applied since an arbitrary
L^{p}
-harmonic 1-form
\omega
no longer satisfies
d*(\omega\wedge\Omega)=0
. Thus, in order to generalize previous results, we had to find another route. In this section, for pluriharmonic functions we can deduce the following refined kato inequality by using Theorem 4.2 in
[
11
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
M^{2n}
be a
2n
-dimensional complete Kähler manifold. If
u
is a pluriharmonic function on
M
, then

|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}\geq 2|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}.

When it comes to quaternionic Kähler manifold, the situation gets a bit more complicated due to the quaternionic structure of
M
. In the wake of lucidity, we shall recall basic properties of quaternionic Kähler manifolds which will be necessary in the sequel.

Definition 2.2

(
[
10
]
)
.

A quaternionic Kähler manifold
(M,g)
is a Riemannian manifold with a rank 3 vector bundle
V\subset End(TM)
satisfying

•

In any coordinate neighborhood
U
of
M
, there exists a local basis
\{I,J,K\}
of
V
such that

\displaystyle I^{2}=J^{2}=K^{2}=-1

\displaystyle IJ=-JI=K

\displaystyle JK=-KJ=I

\displaystyle KI=-IK=J

and

\langle IX,IY\rangle=\langle JX,JY\rangle=\langle KX,KY\rangle=\langle X,Y\rangle

for all
X,Y\in TM
.

•

If
\phi\in\Gamma(V)
then
\nabla_{X}\phi\in\Gamma(V)
for all
X\in TM
.

Definition 2.3

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a quaternionic Kähler manifold. We can define a 4-form
\Omega
on
M
by

\Omega=\omega_{1}\wedge\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}\wedge\omega_{2}+\omega_{3}\wedge\omega_{3}

where

\displaystyle\omega_{1}=\langle\cdot,I\cdot\rangle

\displaystyle\omega_{2}=\langle\cdot,J\cdot\rangle

\displaystyle\omega_{3}=\langle\cdot,K\cdot\rangle.

In a joint work by Kong, Li and Zhou (see
[
10
]
), the authors gave a detailed proof of the fact that the above 4-form determined by the quaternionic structure is indeed parallel, which is immensely useful for the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete quaternionic Kähler manifold and
\Omega
be the parallel 4-form determined by the quaternionic structure. Assume that
M
satisfies a polynomial volume growth condition, i.e,

Vol(B_{o}(r))\leq Cr^{m},m\geq 1.

for sufficiently large
r
and some fixed real numbers
m,C
. Let
p>1
satisfy

2<p\leq\frac{2m}{m-1}\text{ if $m>1$, and }1<p<\infty\text{ if }m=1.

Then
d*(\omega\wedge\Omega)=0
for every
L^{p}
harmonic
1
-form
\omega
.

Proof.

First, we note that

(2.1)

*d*(\omega\wedge\Omega)=(-1)^{4n-1}d*(\omega\wedge*\Omega).

The proof for this identity can be found in
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
. Now let
\phi
be a cut-off function such that
\phi=0
in
B_{o}(r)
(a geodesic ball of radius
r
centered at some fixed point
o\in M
),
\phi=0
outside
B_{o}(2r)
and
|\nabla\phi|\leq\frac{C_{1}}{r}
for some positive constant
C_{1}
. Let
q\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1,

then

\frac{2m}{m+1}\leq q<2\ {\rm if}\ m>1,\ {\rm and}\ 1<q\ {\rm if}\ m=1.

By Hölder’s inequality, we have that

\Biggl(\int_{M}\phi^{q}|d*(\omega\wedge\Omega)|^{q}\Biggl)^{\frac{2}{q}}\leq(Vol(B_{o}(2r)))^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\Biggl(\int_{M}\phi^{2}|d*(\omega\wedge\Omega)|^{2}\Biggl).

Thanks to the volume growth condition of
M
, this implies

\Biggl(\int_{M}\phi^{q}|d*(\omega\wedge\Omega)|^{q}\Biggl)^{\frac{2}{q}}\leq C(2r)^{m(\frac{2}{q}-1)}\Biggl(\int_{M}\phi^{2}|d*(\omega\wedge\Omega)|^{2}\Biggl).

Now, we estimate the right hand side of the above inequality

\displaystyle\int_{M}\phi^{2}|d*(\omega\wedge\Omega)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Biggl|\int_{M}\phi^{2}d*(\omega\wedge\Omega)\wedge*d*(\omega\wedge\Omega)\Biggl|

\displaystyle\leq\Biggl|\int_{M}\phi^{2}d*(\omega\wedge\Omega)\wedge d*(\omega\wedge*\Omega)\Biggl|

\displaystyle\leq\Biggl|\int_{M}d\phi^{2}\wedge*(\omega\wedge\Omega)\wedge d*(\omega\wedge*\Omega)\Biggl|

\displaystyle\leq 2\Biggl(\int_{M}|d\phi|^{p}|*(\omega\wedge\Omega)|^{p}\Biggl)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Biggl(\int_{M}\phi^{q}|d*(\omega\wedge*\Omega)|^{q}\Biggl)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=2\Biggl(\int_{M}|d\phi|^{p}|*(\omega\wedge\Omega)|^{p}\Biggl)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Biggl(\int_{M}\phi^{q}|d*(\omega\wedge\Omega)|^{q}\Biggl)^{\frac{1}{q}}

where the second and last equality follows from (
2.1
), the third equality follows from integration by parts and the fact that
d^{2}=0
. Hence,

(2.2)

\Biggl(\int_{M}\phi^{q}|d*(\omega\wedge\Omega)|^{q}\Biggl)^{\frac{p}{q}}\leq C(2r)^{\frac{pm(2-q)}{q}}\Biggl(\int_{M}|d\phi|^{p}|*(\omega\wedge\Omega)|^{p}\Biggl).

The fact that
\Omega
is parallel implies

|*(\omega\wedge\Omega)|\leq C_{2}|\omega|

for some constant
C_{2}
. Substituting the above into (
2.2
), we obtain

\int_{B_{o}(r)}|d*(\omega\wedge\Omega)|^{q}\leq C_{3}r^{m(2-q)-q}\Bigl(\int_{B_{o}(2r)\setminus B_{o}(r)}|\omega|^{p}\Bigl)^{\frac{q}{p}}.

By letting
r\to+\infty
, the result follows from the assumption that
|\omega|
is
L^{p}
integrable and
\frac{2m}{m+1}\leq q<2
if
m>1
and
1<q
if
m=1.

∎

A crucial consequence of the above result is that
\omega
is quaternionic harmonic if
\omega
is harmonic (see Lemma 3.1 in
[
10
]
). From there, following the argument given in Theorem 4.2 in
[
11
]
, we can obtain the following refined Kato-type inequality.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete quaternionic Kähler manifold and
\Omega
be the parallel 4-form determined by the quaternionic structure. Assume that
M
satisfies a polynomial volume growth condition, i.e,

Vol(B_{o}(r))\leq Cr^{m},m\geq 1

for sufficiently large
r
and some fixed real numbers
m,C
. Let
p>1
satisfy

2<p\leq\frac{2m}{m-1}\text{ if $m>1$, and }1<p<\infty\text{ if }m=1.

and let
\omega
be an
L^{p}(p>1)
harmonic 1-form. Then

|\nabla\omega|^{2}\geq\frac{4}{3}|\nabla|\omega||^{2}.

Now we are ready to prove our main theorems.

3 Vanishing results with a weighted Poincaré inequality

In this section, we derive several vanishing theorems for pluriharmonic functions having finite
L^{p}
energy on Kähler manifolds and
L^{p}
harmonic 1-forms on quaternionic Kähler manifolds, assuming that their Ricci curvatures are bounded from below and they satisfy a weighted Poincaré inequality. Throughout the section,
\rho:M\to\mathbb{R}
is assumed to be a nonnegative weight function. Note that complete manifolds satisfing the weighted Poincaré inequality has infinite volume.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
u
be a pluriharmonic function on
M
that satisfies

\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}<+\infty\ (p>1).

Now set
h=|\nabla u|
, we shall prove that
h
satisfies the Bochner formula of the following form

(3.1)

\Delta h\geq\frac{|\nabla h|^{2}}{h}-\rho h.

Indeed, by virtue of the Kato type inequality derived in Lemma
2.1
and the classical Bochner formula, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta h^{2}

\displaystyle=|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+\langle\nabla\Delta u,\nabla u\rangle+Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)

\displaystyle\geq 2|\nabla h|^{2}-k\rho h^{2},

which implies our desired version of the Bochner formula. Let
\phi
be a cut-off function such that
\phi=1
in
B_{o}(r)
(the geodesic ball of radius
r
centered at some fixed point
o\in M
),
\phi=0
outside
B_{o}(2r)
and
|\nabla\phi|\leq\frac{d}{r}
for some positive constant
d
.

Case
p\geq 2
.
Multiply both side of (
3.1
) with
\phi^{2}h^{p-2}
and integrate over
M
, we obtain

\int_{M}\phi^{2}h^{p-1}\Delta h\geq\int_{M}\phi^{2}h^{p-2}|\nabla h|^{2}-k\int_{M}\rho\phi^{2}h^{p}.

By utilizing the integration by parts formula and reshuffling the terms, it follows that

(3.2)

2\int_{M}\phi h^{p-1}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla h\rangle+p\int_{M}\phi^{2}h^{p-2}|\nabla h|^{2}\leq k\int_{M}\rho\phi^{2}h^{p}.

Applying the Cauchy inequality to the first term of the LHS of the above equation gives us

-2\int_{M}\phi h^{p-1}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla h\rangle\leq\epsilon\int_{M}\phi^{2}h^{p-2}|\nabla h|^{2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{M}h^{p}|\nabla\phi|^{2},

where
\epsilon
is a positive constant.
Next, by using the weighted Poincaré inequality and Young’s inequality consecutively, the following evaluation holds

\displaystyle\int_{M}\rho\phi^{2}h^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}|\nabla(\phi h^{\frac{p}{2}})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1+\epsilon)p^{2}}{4}\int_{M}\phi^{2}h^{p-2}|\nabla h|^{2}+\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Bigl)\int_{M}h^{p}|\nabla\phi|^{2}.

Substituting the above computations into (
3.2
) resulted in

\Bigl(p-\epsilon-\frac{k(1+\epsilon)p^{2}}{4}\Bigl)\int_{M}\phi^{2}h^{p-2}|\nabla h|^{2}\leq\Bigl(\frac{1}{\epsilon}+k\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Bigl)\Bigl)\int_{M}h^{p}|\nabla\phi|^{2}.

Due to the fact that
k<\frac{4}{p}
, it is true that for sufficiently small positive
\epsilon
we have

C(p,\epsilon)=\frac{4\epsilon(p-\epsilon)-k(1+\epsilon)p^{2}}{4d(1+k(\epsilon+1))}>0.

Therefore,

\displaystyle C(p,\epsilon)\int_{M}\phi^{2}h^{p-2}|\nabla h|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}h^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d^{2}}{r^{2}}\int_{M}h^{p}.

By letting
r\to\infty
, it follows that
h
is constant on
M
due to its
L^{p}
integrability. Moreover, considering that
\int_{M}h^{p}<+\infty
and that
M
has infinite volume, we can conclude that
h=0
, which implies that
u
is a constant function.

Case
1<p<2
.
Assume that
\delta,\epsilon,\eta
are positive constants. Multiply both side of (
3.1
) with
\phi^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}
and integrate over
M
, we obtain

\int_{M}\phi^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}h\Delta h\geq\int_{M}\phi^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}|\nabla h|^{2}-k\int_{M}\rho\phi^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}h^{2}.

By utilizing the integration by parts formula and reshuffling the terms, it follows that

(3.3)

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}\phi(h+\delta)^{p-2}h\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla h\rangle+(p-2)\int_{M}\phi^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-3}h|\nabla h|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\int_{M}\phi^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}|\nabla h|^{2}\leq k\int_{M}\rho\phi^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}h^{2}.

Note that

2\int_{M}\phi(h+\delta)^{p-2}h\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla h\rangle\leq\epsilon\int_{M}\phi^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}|\nabla h|^{2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}h^{2},

and

\int_{M}\phi^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}|\nabla h|^{2}\geq\int_{M}\phi^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-3}h|\nabla h|^{2},

we obtain

(3.4)

\displaystyle(p-\epsilon)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\phi^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}|\nabla h|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}h^{2}+k\int_{M}\rho\phi^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}h^{2}.

Next, by using the weighted Poincaré inequality and Young’s inequality consecutively, the following evaluation holds

\displaystyle\int_{M}\rho\phi^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}h^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla(\phi h(h+\delta)^{\frac{p-2}{2}})|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla\phi h(h+\delta)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}+\phi\nabla h(h+\delta)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}+\frac{p-2}{2}\phi h(h+\delta)^{\frac{p-4}{2}}\nabla h|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}h^{2}+(1+\epsilon)(1+\eta)\int_{M}\phi^{2}|\nabla h|^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}

\displaystyle+(1+\epsilon)\left(1+\frac{1}{\eta}\right)\frac{(p-2)^{2}}{4}\int_{M}\phi^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-4}h^{2}|\nabla h|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}h^{2}+(1+\epsilon)\left[1+\eta+(1+\frac{1}{\eta})\frac{(p-2)^{2}}{4}\right]

\displaystyle\times\int_{M}\phi^{2}|\nabla h|^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}.

Therefore we get

\displaystyle\left\{p-\epsilon-k(1+\epsilon)\left[1+\eta+(1+\frac{1}{\eta})\frac{(p-2)^{2}}{4}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\int_{M}\phi^{2}|\nabla h|^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{2}{\epsilon}\right)\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}h^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{2}{\epsilon}\right)\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}h^{p}.

Then if

we can choose

. Let

, we get that when

∎

Proof of Theorem

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{3}\frac{|\nabla h|^{2}}{h}-4(n+2)h

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\frac{|\nabla h|^{2}}{h}-\left(\frac{4(n+2)}{\lambda_{1}}\right)\lambda_{1}h.

and note that if

then by the variation principle for

\lambda_{1}\int_{M}\phi^{2}\leq\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}

for any

.
Applying similar argument as in the proof of Theorem

\Bigl(p-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{kp^{2}(1+\epsilon)}{4}-\epsilon\Bigl)\int_{M}\phi^{2}h^{p-2}|\nabla h|^{2}\leq\Bigl(k\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Bigl)-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Bigl)\int_{M}h^{p}|\nabla\phi|^{2}

where

(a geodesic ball centered at some fixed point

or radius

for some positve constant

. Due to the fact that

, the coefficients of the terms on both sides are positive for sufficiently small

.

Now set

C_{Q}(p,\epsilon)=\frac{p-\frac{2}{3}-k(1+\epsilon)\frac{p^{2}}{4}-\epsilon}{d\Bigl[\frac{4p-8/3}{p^{2}}\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Bigl)-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Bigl]}.

The above inequality implies that

4 Vanishing results with a Sobolev type inequality

In this section, we take a look at complete Kähler manifolds satisfying a Sobolev type inequality. The first vanishing theorem regarding with the lower bound of the Ricci curvature of Kähler manifolds, while the last theorem is a bit more involved as it requires a more general analogue of curvature on Kähler manifold. Thus, to maintain clearness, we recall the needed definition and notation of algebraic curvature tensor on Euclidean vector spaces and later, on Kähler manifolds, in which case it is called the Kähler curvature operator. Our choice for notation is followed by that given in

Proof of Theorem

First, we shall prove that

\Delta|\nabla u|\geq\frac{|\nabla|u||^{2}}{|\nabla u|}+|\nabla u|\lambda^{-}.

2.1
and the classical Böchner formula, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+\langle\nabla\Delta u,\nabla u\rangle+Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)

\displaystyle\geq 2|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\lambda

\displaystyle\geq 2|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\lambda^{-}.

which implies our desired version of the Bochner formula.

For simplicity, we denote by
h=|\nabla u|
in the following.
Now let
\phi
be a cut-off function on
M
such that
\phi=1

(the geodesic ball of radius

for some positive constant

Case

By the integration by parts formula and reshuffling the terms, it follows that

(4.2)

2\int_{M}\phi h^{p-1}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla h\rangle+p\int_{M}\phi^{2}h^{p-2}|\nabla h|^{2}\leq\int_{M}(-\lambda^{-})\phi^{2}h^{p}.

Applying the Cauchy inequality to the LHS of the above equation gives us

-2\int_{M}\phi h^{p-1}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla h\rangle\leq\epsilon\int_{M}\phi^{2}h^{p-2}|\nabla h|^{2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{M}h^{p}|\nabla\phi|^{2},

where
\epsilon
is a positive constant. Next, by using Hölder inequality, the weighted Sobolev inequality and Young’s inequality consecutively on the RHS term, the following evaluation holds

\displaystyle\int_{M}(-\lambda^{-})\phi^{2}h^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\Biggl(\int_{M}\bigl(-\lambda^{-}\bigl)^{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Biggl)^{\frac{2}{\nu}}\Biggl(\int_{M}\Bigl(\phi h^{\frac{p}{2}}\Bigl)^{\frac{2\nu}{\nu-2}}\Biggl)^{\frac{\nu-2}{\nu}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{S}||\lambda^{-}||_{\nu/2}\int_{M}\Big|\nabla\Bigl(\phi h^{\frac{p}{2}}\Bigl)\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{S}||\lambda^{-}||_{\nu/2}\Biggr[\frac{p^{2}(1+\epsilon)}{4}\int_{M}\phi^{2}h^{p-2}|\nabla h|^{2}+\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Bigl)\int_{M}h^{p}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\Biggr].

Substituting the above computations into (

Due to the fact that

, we can choose

be a small enough potitive constant such that

Hence

By letting

, it holds true that

on
M
thanks to the finiteness of the

- energy of

and that

has infinite volume. Therefore

is a contant function on

.

Now we switch our attention to a vanishing theorem on manifolds with lower bound conditions on the Kähler curvature operator. To begin with, we shall follow the notation in
[
16
]
and
[
3
]
to give an explicit definition of the algebraic curvature operator on Euclidean vector spaces.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(V,g)
be an
n
-dimensional Euclidean vector space and let
V_{\mathbb{C}}=V\bigotimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
. For any
\mathbb{R}
-multilinear, complex valued tensor
T
on
V
and
L\in\mathfrak{so}(V)
, set

LT(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})=-\sum_{i=1}^{r}T(X_{1},\ldots,LX_{i},\ldots,X_{r}).

If
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{so}(V)
is a Lie algebra, we define
T^{\mathfrak{g}}\in\bigl(\bigotimes^{r}V_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\bigl)\bigotimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathfrak{g}
as

g(L,T^{\mathfrak{g}}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}))=LT(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})

for every
L\in\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{so}(V)=\Lambda^{2}(V)
.

A tensor
Rm\in\bigotimes^{4}V^{*}
is called an algebraic curvature tensor if

Rm(X,Y,Z,W)=-Rm(Y,X,Z,W)=-Rm(X,Y,W,Z)=Rm(Z,W,X,Y)

and

Rm(X,Y,Z,W)+Rm(Y,Z,X,W)+Rm(Z,X,Y,W)=0.

In particular, the curvature operator
\mathfrak{R}:\Lambda^{2}V\to\Lambda^{2}V
is induced by

g(\mathfrak{R}(X\wedge Y),Z\wedge W)=Rm(X,Y,Z,W).

In the case of Riemannian manifold, the curvature operator
\mathfrak{g}
vanishes on the complement of the holonomy algebra
\mathfrak{hol}
. To be specific, it induces
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathfrak{hol}}:\mathfrak{hol}\to\mathfrak{hol}
and the associated curvature tensor
R\in Sym_{B}^{2}(\mathfrak{hol})
. When
\mathfrak{hol}=\mathfrak{u}(n)
, the manifold
(M,g)
is Kähler and the operator
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathfrak{u}(n)}:\mathfrak{u}(n)\to\mathfrak{u}(n)
is called the Kähler curvature operator and the corresponding
R\in Sym_{B}^{2}(\mathfrak{u}(n))
is called the Kähler curvature tensor. It is worth to note that every Kähler curvature tensor on a Kähler manifold
(M,g,J)
, where
J
is the complex structure, satisfies

Rm(X,Y,Z,W)=Rm(JX,JY,Z,W)=Rm(X,Y,JZ,JW).

Next, we carry on by recalling the following lemma, which originally refers to the remarkable paper written by Petersen and Wink

]
(for a detailed discussion, see also
[
3
]
).

Lemma 4.2

(
[
3
]
)

Let

be a complete non-compact Kähler manifold of complex dimension

be a function on

\displaystyle C_{S}||k||_{\nu/2}\int_{M}\Big|\nabla\Bigl(\phi h(h+\delta)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\Bigl)\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{S}||k||_{\nu/2}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi h(h+\delta)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}+\phi\nabla h(h+\delta)^{\frac{(p-2)}{2}}+\frac{(p-2)}{2}\phi h(h+\delta)^{\frac{p-4}{2}}\nabla h|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{S}||k||_{\nu/2}\Biggr[\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Bigl)\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}h^{2}+(1+\epsilon)(1+\eta)\int_{M}\phi^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}|\nabla h|^{2}

\displaystyle+(1+\epsilon)(1+\frac{1}{\eta})\frac{(p-2)^{2}}{4}\int_{M}\phi^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-4}h^{2}|\nabla h|^{2}\Biggr]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{S}||k||_{\nu/2}\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Bigl)\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}h^{2}+C_{S}||k||_{\nu/2}\bigl(1+\epsilon\bigl)\times

\displaystyle\left[(1+\eta)+(1+\frac{1}{\eta})\frac{(p-2)^{2}}{4}\right]\int_{M}\phi^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}|\nabla h|^{2}.

Substituting the above computations into (
4.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Biggr\{p-\epsilon-(n+1)C_{S}(1+\epsilon)\bigl[(1+\eta)+(1+\frac{1}{\eta})\frac{(p-2)^{2}}{4}\bigl]||k||_{\nu/2}\Biggr\}\int_{M}\phi^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}|\nabla h|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Biggr[(n+1)C_{S}\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Bigl)||k||_{\nu/2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Biggr]\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}h^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Biggr[(n+1)C_{S}\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Bigl)||k||_{\nu/2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Biggr]\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}h^{p}.

Now choose
\eta=\frac{2-p}{2}

we obtain

\displaystyle\Biggr[p-\epsilon-(n+1)C_{S}(1+\epsilon)||k||_{\nu/2}\frac{(4-p)^{2}}{4}\Biggr]\int_{M}\phi^{2}(h+\delta)^{p-2}|\nabla h|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Biggr[(n+1)C_{S}\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Bigl)||k||_{\nu/2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Biggr]\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}h^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\Biggr[(n+1)C_{S}\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Bigl)||k||_{\nu/2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Biggr]\frac{d^{2}}{r^{2}}\int_{M}h^{p}.

Due to the fact that
\lVert k\rVert_{\nu/2}<\frac{4p}{(4-p)^{2}C_{S}(n+1)}
, we can choose
\epsilon
be a sufficiently small potitive constant such that the coefficient of the LHS is potitive. Then by letting
r\to+\infty
, the result follows from the fact that
|\omega|\in L^{p}
and that
M
has infinite volume.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

We note that in
[
3
]
, the second author and Cho obtained a vanishing result for general
(p,q)
forms
\omega
with finite
L^{Q}\ (Q\geq 2)
energy. However, due to the defection of a refined Kato inequality for usual harmonic forms, they required the forms to be harmonic fields. Note that this assumption is stronger than the usual harmonicity. In our theorem, we used the usual harmonicity of the function
u
and do not need to assume that
\omega=du
is a harmonic field.

Inverse curvature flows for capillary hypersurfaces in the unit ball
Shujing Pan, Bo Yang

Abstract
. In this paper, we study inverse curvature flows for strictly convex, capillary hypersurfaces in the unit Euclidean ball. We establish the existence and convergence results for a class of such flows. As an application, we derive a family of Alexandrov–Fenchel inequalities for weakly convex hypersurfaces with free boundary.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
53C44, 53C21, 35K93, 52A40

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study inverse curvature flows and their applications to the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for capillary hypersurfaces in the unit Euclidean ball. Let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}(n\geq 2)
be a properly embedded smooth hypersurface in the unit Euclidean ball
\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
, given by an embedding
x:\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\to\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathrm{int}(\Sigma)=x(\mathbb{B}^{n})\subset\mathbb{B}^{n+1},\quad\partial\Sigma=x(\partial\mathbb{B}^{n})\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Let
\bar{N}
be the unit outward normal of
\mathbb{S}^{n}=\partial\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
, and
\nu
be a smooth choice of the unit normal of
\Sigma
. For
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, we say that
\Sigma
has
\theta
-capillary boundary
\partial\Sigma
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
if
\Sigma
intersects
\mathbb{S}^{n}
at the constant contact angle
\theta
, that is,

\langle\bar{N}\circ x,\nu\rangle=-\cos\theta,\quad\text{along $\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}$}.

In particular, if
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
, i.e.,
\Sigma
intersects
\mathbb{S}^{n}
orthogonally, we call that
\Sigma
has free boundary. Denote by
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
the convex body in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
enclosed by
\partial\Sigma
, and
\widehat{\Sigma}
the convex domain in
\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
enclosed by
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
and
\Sigma
. We choose the unit normal
\nu
of
\Sigma
as the one pointing outward of
\widehat{\Sigma}
.

Two model examples are the spherical cap of radius
r
around a constant unit vector
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
\theta
-capillary boundary, given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle C_{\theta,r}(e):=\left\{x\in\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}:\left|x-\sqrt{r^{2}+2r\cos\theta+1}e\right|=r\right\},

and the flat ball around
e
with
\theta
-capillary boundary, given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle C_{\theta,\infty}(e):=\{x\in\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}:\langle x,e\rangle=\cos\theta\}.

Let
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
be the principal curvatures of
\Sigma
, we define the
k
th normalized mean curvature of
\Sigma
as

E_{k}(\kappa)=\binom{n}{k}^{-1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}{\kappa_{i_{1}}\kappa_{i_{2}}\cdots\kappa_{i_{n}}}.

In particular, we write
H=nE_{1}
for the mean curvature of
\Sigma
, and
K=E_{n}
as the Gauss curvature of
\Sigma
. We say a hypersurface
\Sigma
is
strictly convex
, if all principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}>0
everywhere on
\Sigma
, and is
weakly convex
if all principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}\geq 0
everywhere on
\Sigma
.

This paper deals with the expanding curvature flows of the form

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \left(\partial_{t}x\right)^{\bot}&=\frac{1}{F}\nu&\text{in}\quad\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times[0,T),\\
\langle\bar{N}\circ x,\nu\rangle&=-\cos\theta&\text{on}\quad\partial\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times[0,T),\\
x(\cdot,0)&=x_{0}(\cdot)&\text{on}\quad\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n},\end{aligned}\right.

where
F=F(\mathcal{W})
is a smooth function evaluated at the Weingarten operator
\mathcal{W}=\{h_{i}^{j}\}
of of the flow
hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{t}=x(\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n},t)
and
\nu
is the outward pointing
normal.

Without ambiguity, we also regard
F
as a function of the eigenvalues
\kappa(\mathcal{W})
of the Weingarten operator
\mathcal{W}
. In this context, we denote

F_{i}(\kappa)=\frac{\partial F}{\partial\kappa_{i}}(\kappa).

Moreover, the following relations hold:

F_{j}^{i}=\frac{\partial F}{\partial h_{i}^{j}}=g_{kj}F^{ki}=g_{kj}\frac{\partial F}{\partial h_{ki}}.

There are several conditions that are consistently imposed on the curvature function
F
throughout this paper:

Assumption 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a symmetric, open and convex cone containing the positive cone

(1.4)

\Gamma^{+}=\{\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\kappa_{i}>0,\forall 1\leq i\leq n\}.

Suppose
F\in C^{0}(\bar{\Gamma})\cap C^{2}(\Gamma)
satisfies

(i)

F
is strictly positive in
\Gamma
and vanishes on the boundary of
\Gamma
(i.e.
F|_{\Gamma}>0
and
F|_{\partial\Gamma}=0
), which is normalized such that
F(1,\cdots,1)=1
;

(ii)

F
is homogeneous of degree one:
F(\lambda\kappa)=\lambda F(\kappa)
, for any
\lambda>0
;

(iii)

F
is strictly increasing in each argument:
F_{i}(\kappa)=\frac{\partial F}{\partial\kappa_{i}}(\kappa)>0
,
\forall i=1,\cdots,n
;

(iv)

F
is concave and inverse concave on
\Gamma^{+}
. That is, both
F
and the function
F_{*}
given by

\displaystyle F_{*}(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})=F(\kappa_{1}^{-1},\cdots,\kappa_{n}^{-1})^{-1}

are concave on
\Gamma^{+}
.

Remark 1
.

The most important examples of curvature functions
F
satisfying the above assumption are
\left(\frac{E_{k}}{E_{\ell}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-\ell}}
,
0\leq\ell<k\leq n
.

We prove the following existence and convergence result:

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
F
is a curvature function satisfying Assumption
1.1
. Let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}(n\geq 2)
be a properly embedded, strictly convex smooth hypersurface with capillary boundary supported on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
at a contact angle
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
, given by an embedding
x:\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\to\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
. Then there exists a finite time
T^{*}<\infty,\alpha>0
such that the flow (
1.3
) starting from
\Sigma
admits a unique solution
x(\cdot,t)
, such that

x(\cdot,t)\in C^{\infty}(\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times(0,T^{*}))\cap C^{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times[0,T^{*})).

Moreover, the solution
x(\cdot,t)
remains strictly convex in the time interval
(0,T^{*})
and converges to a flat ball with
\theta
-capillary boundary as
t\to T^{*}
.

The study of inverse curvature flows for closed hypersurfaces in Euclidean space was pioneered by Gerhardt
[
Ger90
]
and Urbas
[
Urbas90
]
, who established the long-time existence and convergence of the flow for star-shaped initial hypersurfaces with positive curvature function
F
. This result, particularly with
F=\frac{E_{k+1}}{E_{k}}
was later exploited by Guan-Li
[
guanli09
]
to generalise the Alexandrov–Fenchel quermassintegral inequalities from the convex setting to the star-shaped and
(k+1)
-convex setting. Since then, a series of similar results have been developed using the same method in various ambient spaces, and the study of inverse curvature flows has become increasingly active. For examples, inverse curvature flows in hyperbolic space and the sphere have been investigated in
[
Ger11
,
Ger15
,
Liu17
,
Yu
]
, while analogous results in more general warped product spaces can be found in
[
BHW16
,
LW17
,
Scheuer17
,
Scheuer19
]
, among many others. A more comprehensive introduction to this topic is available in
[
Scheuer19
]
.

For the free boundary case, Lambert and Scheuer
[
Lambert-Scheuer2016
]
has shown the convergence result of the flow (
1.3
) with
F=\frac{1}{H}
(i.e. the inverse mean curvature flow) for strictly convex hypersurfaces in
\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
. As an application, they obtained the following Li-Yau type inequality for convex hypersurfaces with free boundary in any dimension
n\geq 3
.

Theorem A

(
[
Lambert-Scheuer2017
]
)
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}(n\geq 3)
be a properly embedded, strictly convex smooth hypersurface with free boundary supported on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Then there holds

(1.5)

\frac{1}{2}|\Sigma|^{\frac{2-n}{n}}\int_{\Sigma}{H^{2}}\,d\mu+b_{n}^{\frac{2-n}{n}}|\partial\Sigma|\geq b_{n}^{\frac{2-n}{n}}|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|,

where
b_{n}
denotes the volume of
n
-dimensional unit ball, and equality holds if and only if
\Sigma
is a flat disk with free boundary.

Scheuer, Wang and Xia
[
Scheuer-Wang-Xia2018
]
introduced the quermassintegrals
W_{k}(\widehat{\Sigma})
for smooth hypersurfaces
\Sigma
with free boundary in
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
. Later, Weng and Xia
[
Weng-Xia2022
]
generalized this definitions to
W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
for capillary hypersurfaces with contact angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
which exhibit favorable variational properties (see §
2.2
for details). Using a locally constrained inverse curvature flow, they established the following Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for weakly convex hypersurfaces in
\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
.

Theorem B

(
[
Scheuer-Wang-Xia2018
,
Weng-Xia2022
]
)
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}(n\geq 2)
be a properly embedded, weakly convex smooth hypersurface in the unit ball with
\theta
-capillary boundary, where
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
. Then for any
k=0,\cdots,n-1
, there holds

(1.6)

W_{n,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})\geq(f_{n}\circ f_{k}^{-1})(W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})),

where
f_{k}(r)
is the strictly increasing function given by
f_{k}(r)=W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{C_{\theta,r}})
. Equality holds in (
1.6
) if and only if
\Sigma
is a spherical cap or a flat ball with
\theta
-capillary boundary.

Later, the second author with Hu, Wei and Zhou
[
HWYZ-2023
]
proved the following Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for weakly convex hypersurfaces in
\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
.

Theorem C

(
[
HWYZ-2023
]
)
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}(n\geq 2)
be a properly embedded, weakly convex smooth hypersurface in the unit ball with
\theta
-capillary boundary, where
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
. Then for any
k=1,\cdots,n-1
, there holds

(1.7)

W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})\geq(f_{k}\circ f_{0}^{-1})(W_{0,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})),

where
f_{k}(r)
is the strictly increasing function given by
f_{k}(r)=W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{C_{\theta,r}})
. Equality holds in (
1.7
) if and only if
\Sigma
is a spherical cap or a flat ball with
\theta
-capillary boundary.

In the second part of this paper, we prove a family of Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for weakly convex hypersurfaces in
\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
with free boundary (i.e.
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
) using the inverse mean curvature flow. In this case, we denote the quermassintegral
W_{k,\frac{\pi}{2}}(\widehat{\Sigma})
as
W_{k}(\widehat{\Sigma})
for the sake of simplicity.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}(n\geq 2)
be a properly embedded, weakly convex smooth hypersurface in the unit ball with free boundary. Then for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
2k+1\leq n
, there holds

(1.8)

W_{2k+1}(\widehat{\Sigma})\geq\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{n}\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{k}(n-2j)}{\prod_{j=0}^{k}(n+1-2j)}\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{k}{i}\frac{1}{n-2k+2i}\left[\frac{n(n+1)}{\omega_{n-1}}W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma})\right]^{\frac{n-2k+2i}{n}},

where
\omega_{n-1}
is the area of
(n-1)
-dimensional unit sphere, and the equality (
1.8
) holds if and only if
\Sigma
is a flat disk with free boundary.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows: In §
2
, we collect some preliminaries which will be used in this paper, including the geometry of hypersurfaces in the ball with capillary boundary, the definition and variational formulas of quermassinegrals for hypersurfaces with
\theta
-capillary boundary and some basic properties for curvature functions and evolution equations along the inverse curvature flow (
1.3
). In §
3
, we give the
C^{2}
estimate along the inverse curvature flow (
1.3
). In particular, we show that the inverse curvature flow (
1.3
) starting from a strictly convex hypersurface exists for a finite time and remains strictly convex. In §
4
, we show that the flow (
1.3
) converges to a flat ball with
\theta
-capillary boundary around some
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
. In §
5
, we prove the Alexandrov Fenchel inequalities (
1.8
) for weakly convex hypersurfaces with free boundary.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Hypersurfaces in the ball with capillary boundary

Let
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
. Suppose that
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
is a smooth, properly embedded, weakly convex hypersurface with
\theta
-capillary boundary, which is given by an embedding
x:\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\rightarrow\Sigma
such that

\mathrm{int}(\Sigma)=x({\mathbb{B}}^{n})\subset\mathbb{B}^{n+1},\quad\partial\Sigma=x(\partial\mathbb{B}^{n})\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Then
\partial\Sigma\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
is a weakly convex hypersurface of the unit sphere and bounds a weakly convex body in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, which we denote by
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
, cf.
[
CW1970
, Theorem 1.1]
. Let
\widehat{\Sigma}
denote the bounded domain in
\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
enclosed by
\Sigma
and
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
. We denote by
\nu
the unit normal field of
\Sigma
, and
\bar{N}
the position vector of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. The outward pointing conormal vector of
\partial\Sigma\subset\Sigma
is denote by
\mu
, and the unit normal of
\partial\Sigma
in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is denote by
\bar{\nu}
, chosen such that the pairs
\{\nu,\mu\}
and
\{\bar{\nu},\bar{N}\circ x\}
induce the same orientation in the normal bundle of
\partial\Sigma\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

It follows from
\langle\bar{N}\circ x,\nu\rangle=-\cos\theta
that

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \bar{N}\circ x=&\sin\theta\mu-\cos\theta\nu,\\
\bar{\nu}=&\cos\theta\mu+\sin\theta\nu.\end{aligned}\right.

Or equivalently,

(2.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mu=&\sin\theta\bar{N}\circ x+\cos\theta\bar{\nu},\\
\nu=&-\cos\theta\bar{N}\circ x+\sin\theta\bar{\nu}.\end{aligned}\right.

We denote by
D
the Levi-Civita connection of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with respect to the Euclidean metric
\delta
, and
\nabla
the Levi-Civita connection on
\Sigma
with respect to the induced metric from the embedding
x:\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\to\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. The second fundamental form of
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is given by

h(X,Y):=-\langle D_{X}Y,\nu\rangle,\ \ X,Y\in T(\Sigma).

Note that
\partial\Sigma
can be regarded as a smooth closed hypersurface both in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
\Sigma
. The second fundamental form of
\partial\Sigma
in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is given by

\widehat{h}(X,Y):=-\langle\nabla^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}_{X}Y,\bar{\nu}\rangle=-\langle D_{X}Y,\bar{\nu}\rangle,\ \ X,Y\in T(\partial\Sigma).

The second fundamental form of
\partial\Sigma
in
\Sigma
is given by

\tilde{h}(X,Y):=-\langle\nabla_{X}Y,\mu\rangle=-\langle D_{X}Y,\mu\rangle,\ \ X,Y\in T(\partial\Sigma).

Proposition 2.1

(
[
Weng-Xia2022
]
)
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
be a
\theta
-capillary hypersurface. Let
\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1}^{n-1}
be an orthonormal frame of
\partial\Sigma
. Then the following relations hold on
\partial\Sigma
:

(1)

\mu
is a principal direction of
\Sigma
, i.e.,
h(\mu,e_{\alpha})=0
.

(2)

h_{\alpha\beta}=\sin\theta\widehat{h}_{\alpha\beta}-\cos\theta\delta_{\alpha\beta}
.

(3)

\tilde{h}_{\alpha\beta}=\cos\theta\widehat{h}_{\alpha\beta}+\sin\theta\delta_{\alpha\beta}=\cot\theta h_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\delta_{\alpha\beta}
.

(4)

\nabla_{\mu}h_{\alpha\beta}=\tilde{h}_{\beta\gamma}\left(h_{\mu\mu}\delta_{\alpha\gamma}-h_{\alpha\gamma}\right)
.

In the following of this subsection, we derive an estimate of the height function
\langle x,e\rangle
. Weng and Xia (
[
Weng-Xia2022
, Proposition 2.16]
, see also
[
HWYZ-2023
, Proposition 2.5]
) have proved that for a strictly convex hypersurface
\Sigma
, there exists a unit vector
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
such that

(2.3)

\langle x,e\rangle\geq\cos\theta+\delta_{0},

where
\delta_{0}>0
is a positive constant depending only on
\Sigma
. Here, we present a new proof of this estimate, which explains the concrete geometric quantities on which
\delta
depends. The underlying idea is similar to that in
[
Lambert-Scheuer2016
]
. We begin with a technical lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
R>0
,
e_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a unit vector and
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a convex closed cone whose vertex at
\cos\theta e_{0}
. Then for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
, such that

\langle a-\cos\theta e_{0},e_{0}\rangle\geq\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\varepsilon\right)||a-\cos\theta e_{0}||,\quad\forall a\in C

implies

\langle x-\cos\theta e_{0},e_{0}\rangle\geq R+\delta,\quad\forall B_{R}(x)\subset C.

Proof.

The proof is similarly as in
[
Lambert-Scheuer2016
, Lemma 10]
, except that now we translate the vertex of the convex cone from origin
O
to
\cos\theta e_{0}
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Sigma
be a strictly convex hypersurface in
\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
with
\theta
-capillary boundary with
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
. Let
e\in\mathrm{int}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})
be a direction, such that
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}\subset\mathrm{int}(\widehat{\partial C_{\theta,\infty}}(e))
. Then there exists a positive constant
\delta>0
, depending only on the length of the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
, the distance from
e
to
\partial\Sigma
and the distance from
\partial\Sigma
to
\partial C_{\theta,\infty}(e)
such that

(2.4)

\langle x,e\rangle\geq\cos\theta+\delta.

Proof.

The case that
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
was already established by Lambert and Scheuer in
[
Lambert-Scheuer2016
, Lemma 12]
. Therefore, we only consider the case
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
. The existence of such
e
can be found in
[
Weng-Xia2022
, Proposition 2.16]
. We denote the cone whose vertex at
\cos\theta e
as

(2.5)

C_{0}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:y=s(x-\cos\theta e)+\cos\theta e,s\geq 0,x\in\widehat{\partial\Sigma}\}.

We claim that
\Sigma\subset C_{0}\cap\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
. By
[
Weng-Xia2022
, Proposition 2.15]
, for any
p\in\partial\Sigma
,
\Sigma
lies on one side of
T_{p}{\Sigma}
. More precisely, we have

(2.6)

\langle x-p,\nu(p)\rangle\leq 0,\forall x\in\Sigma.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\langle s(p-\cos\theta e)+\cos\theta e-p,\nu(p)\rangle

(2.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-s)\cos\theta(1+\langle\nu(p),e\rangle)\geq 0,

and the equality in (
2.7
) holds if and only if
s=1
. Combining (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) yields the claim.

Assume that the function
\langle x-\cos\theta e,e\rangle
attains its minimum on
\Sigma
at a point
a
and

(2.8)

\mathrm{dist}(\partial\Sigma,C_{\theta,\infty}(e))=\varepsilon.

Then two cases arise:

(i) if
\mathrm{dist}(a,\widehat{\partial\Sigma})\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
, it follows directly that
\langle a,e\rangle\geq\cos\theta+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
.

(ii) if
\mathrm{dist}(a,\widehat{\partial\Sigma})>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
, then by the same argument in
[
Lambert-Scheuer2016
, Lemma 12]
, there exists
R>0
depends on the the length of the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
and
\langle\nu,e\rangle
, such that
B_{R}
is an interior sphere at
a
. By
[
Weng-Xia2022
, Proposition 2.16]
,
\langle\nu,e\rangle<-\cos^{2}{\theta}
and hence
R
depends on the the length of the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
and
\theta
. If we take
r=\min\{R,\frac{\varepsilon}{8}\}
, then
B_{r}
is also an interior ball at
a
with radius
r
. Moreover, for
\forall x\in B_{r}
, we have

(2.9)

\mathrm{dist}(x,\widehat{\partial\Sigma})\geq\mathrm{dist}(a,\widehat{\partial\Sigma})-2r\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{4},

which implies that
B_{r}\subset C_{0}\cap\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
. By lemma
2.2
, there exists a
\delta>0
, such that
\langle a,e\rangle\geq\cos\theta+\delta
.
∎

2.2.
Quermassintegrals for hypersurfaces with
\theta
-capillary boundary

The quermassintegrals of weakly convex hypersurfaces were first introduced by Scheuer, Wang and Xia
[
Scheuer-Wang-Xia2018
]
for hypersurfaces with free boundary in
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
. Later, Weng and Xia
[
Weng-Xia2022
]
generalized the definition to hypersurfaces with
\theta
-capillary boundary in
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
. We briefly recall the definition here.

Let
\Sigma
be a weakly convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
with
\theta
-capillary boundary. Then
\partial\Sigma\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
is a weakly convex hypersurface of the unit sphere, which bounds a weakly convex body in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, denote by
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
. Denote by
\widehat{\Sigma}
the bounded domain in
\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
enclosed by
\Sigma
and
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
. The quermassintegrals for
\widehat{\Sigma}
are defined as:

(2.10)

\displaystyle W_{0,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})=

\displaystyle|\widehat{\Sigma}|,

(2.11)

\displaystyle W_{1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n+1}\left(|\Sigma|-\cos\theta W_{0}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})\right),

and for
1\leq k\leq n-1
,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &W_{k+1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})=\frac{1}{n+1}\left\{\int_{\Sigma}E_{k}dA-\cos\theta\sin^{k}\theta W_{k}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})\right.\\
&\quad\quad\quad\left.-\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\frac{(-1)^{k+\ell}}{n-\ell}\binom{k}{\ell}\left[(n-k)\cos^{2}\theta+k-\ell\right]\cos^{k-1-\ell}\theta\tan^{\ell}\theta W_{\ell}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})\right\},\end{aligned}

where
W_{k}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma}),0\leq k\leq n
are the quermassintegrals in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
which are defined by (see
[
Sol06
]
)

(2.13)

Lemma 3.1

\partial\Sigma_{\bar{T}}=\partial C_{\theta,\infty}(e)
for some
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and then
\bar{T}=T^{*}
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

There holds
T^{*}<\infty
.

Proof.

Since the flow (
1.3
) induced a normal hypersurface flow
\partial\Sigma_{t}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
with normal speed
\frac{1}{F\sin\theta}
, and since
F
admits a uniform upper bound, it follows that the speed for
\partial\Sigma_{t}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
has a uniform positive lower bound. Consequently, the limiting hypersurface
\partial\Sigma_{\bar{T}}
is reached at finite time.
∎

4.
Convergence to a flat ball with capillary boundary

In this section, we conclude that there exists a constant vector
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, such that the flow (
1.3
) converges to
C_{\theta,\infty}(e)
. For this purpose, we prove the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume that
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}(n\geq 2)
is a properly embedded, strictly convex smooth hypersurface with capillary boundary supported on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
at a contact angle
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
. Let
\Sigma_{t},t\in[0,T)
be the solution of the flow (
1.3
) starting from
\Sigma
. Assume that
\Sigma_{T}
is not a flat ball around some point
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, then there holds

(4.1)

T^{*}>T+\epsilon,

where
\epsilon>0
is a constant depending on
\Sigma
and the distance between
\partial\Sigma_{T}
and some suitable
\partial C_{\theta,\infty}(e)
.

Proof.

Firstly we reduce the flow (
1.3
) to a scalar parabolic flow with oblique boundary condition using the conformal transformation map as in
[
Scheuer-Wang-Xia2018
,
Weng-Xia2022
]
. Since
\partial\Sigma_{T}
is not a flat ball, then by Lemma
3.2
, there exits a vector
e\in\mathrm{int}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma_{T}})
, such that
\partial\Sigma_{T}\subset\mathrm{int}(\widehat{\partial C_{\theta,\infty}}(e))
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
e
is the
(n+1)
th coordinate vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Consider the following Möbius transformation:

\displaystyle\varphi:\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}\quad

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\quad\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1},

(4.2)

\displaystyle(x^{\prime},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\longmapsto\frac{2(x^{\prime},0)+(1-|x^{\prime}|^{2}-x_{n+1}^{2})e}{|x^{\prime}|^{2}+(x_{n+1}-1)^{2}}:=(y^{\prime},y_{n+1})=\tilde{y},

where
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n+1})
with
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
x_{n+1}=\langle x,e\rangle
. Then
\varphi
maps
\mathbb{S}^{n}=\partial\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
to
\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}:=\{(y^{\prime},y_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:y_{n+1}=0\}
and

\displaystyle\varphi^{\ast}(\delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}})=\frac{4}{(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+(x_{n+1}-1)^{2})^{2}}\delta_{\mathbb{B}^{n+1}}:=e^{-2w}\delta_{\mathbb{B}^{n+1}},

which implies that

is a conformal transformation from
(\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1},\delta_{\mathbb{B}^{n+1}})

(\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1},\delta_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}})

\Sigma_{t}=x(\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n},t)

\tilde{\Sigma}_{t}:=\tilde{y}(\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n},t)

(\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1},(\varphi^{-1})^{\ast}\delta_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}})

Note that
X_{e}
is a conformal vector field such that
\varphi_{\ast}(X_{e})=-\tilde{y}

\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}

\displaystyle e^{-2w}\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle=\langle\varphi_{\ast}(X_{e}),\varphi_{\ast}(\nu)\rangle=|\varphi_{\ast}(\nu)|\langle\tilde{y},\tilde{\nu}\rangle,

where
|\varphi_{\ast}(\nu)|=\frac{1}{2}(|y^{\prime}|^{2}+(y_{n+1}+1)^{2})
and
\tilde{\nu}:=-\frac{\varphi_{\ast}(\nu)}{|\varphi_{\ast}(\nu)|}

\varphi(\Sigma)
is star-shaped in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
with respect to the origin, i.e.,
\langle\tilde{y},\tilde{\nu}\rangle>0
on
\varphi(\Sigma)
, if and only if
\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle>0
on
\Sigma
. In particular, since
\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle>0

\Sigma_{t}
by
[

\overline{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}

In
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}

(4.3)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \rho^{2}=&|y^{\prime}|^{2}+y_{n+1}^{2},\\

Let

Since

∎

As a corollary, we present a refined estimate of the height function which should be of independent interest in the future.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}(n\geq 2)

\mathbb{S}^{n}
at a contact angle

\displaystyle\langle x,e\rangle-\cos\theta\geq\Lambda\frac{\log(W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{C}_{\theta,\infty}(e))+C_{n,k,\theta})-\log(W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})+C_{n,k,\theta})}{(n+1-k)\max_{\Sigma}F},

where
\Lambda
is a constant only depending on the distance of

to
\partial\Sigma

is a constant depending on
n,k
and
\theta
.

Proof.

From the initial hypersurface
\Sigma
, we start the inverse curvature flow (
1.3
) with
F=\frac{E_{k}}{E_{k-1}}
. Then, according to (
2.16
),

evolves in time by

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})=\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}E_{k-1}d\mu_{t}\leq(n+1-k)(W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})+C_{n,k,\theta}),

where the inequality follows from the definition of

and the fact

,

is a constant depending only on

and
\theta

\displaystyle W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})+C_{n,k,\theta}\leq(W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})+C_{n,k,\theta})e^{(n+1-k)t}.

Since the flow (
1.3
) converges to the flat ball
\widehat{C}_{\theta,\infty}(e)
, the maximal existence time

is at least

(4.6)

\displaystyle T^{*}\geq\frac{1}{n+1-k}\left(\log(W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{C}_{\theta,\infty})+C_{n,k,\theta})-\log(W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})+C_{n,k,\theta})\right)

On the other hand, along the flow (
1.3

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\langle x,e\rangle=\frac{\langle\nu,e\rangle}{F}.

Using the argument in
[
Lambert-Scheuer2016
, Lemma 11]

\Lambda
depending only on the distance of

to
\partial\Sigma

. Therefore, by the flow (

) converges to the flat ball

(4.7)

\displaystyle\cos\theta-\langle x,e\rangle=\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\frac{d}{dt}\langle x,e\rangle dt=\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\frac{\langle\nu,e\rangle}{F}dt\leq-\frac{\Lambda}{\max_{\Sigma}F}T^{*},

where the inequality follows from Lemma
3.1
and the distance of

is increasing along the expanding flow (

). Thus, combining (
4.6
) with (

).
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section, we prove a family of Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities (
1.8
) for weakly convex hypersurfaces with free boundary in
\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
using the inverse mean curvature flow. We review the definition of quermassintegral for weakly convex hypersurfaces with free boundary here by taking
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
in §
2.2
.

(5.1)

\displaystyle W_{0}(\widehat{\Sigma})

\displaystyle=|\widehat{\Sigma}|,\quad W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma})=\frac{1}{n+1}|\Sigma|,

(5.2)

\displaystyle W_{k}(\widehat{\Sigma})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{\Sigma}{E_{k-1}}\,dA+\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{k-1}{n-k+2}W_{k-2}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma}),\quad\forall 2\leq k\leq n+1.

Firstly, we need the following useful lemmas.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any strictly convex hypersurface
\Sigma
in
\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
with free boundary, there holds

(5.3)

\langle x,\nu\rangle\leq 0.

Proof.

It follows from
[
Lambert-Scheuer2016
, Lemma 11, Lemma 12]
that there exists
e\in\mathrm{int}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})
such that
\partial\Sigma
lies on the open upper hemisphere centered at
e

c_{0}>0
depending on the distance of

to
\partial\Sigma
and
\delta_{0}>0

\Sigma

(5.4)

\langle\nu,e\rangle\leq-c_{0},\quad\langle x,e\rangle\geq\delta_{0}.

Note that
\langle x,\nu\rangle\equiv 0
on
\partial\Sigma
. If
\langle x,\nu\rangle
attains its positive maximum at some interior point

, then at

(5.5)

\langle x,\nu\rangle_{i}=h_{i}^{k}\langle x,e_{k}\rangle=0.

Since

\langle x(p),\nu(p)\rangle=-|x(p)|\leq-\delta_{0},

which contradicts the assumption that

∎

Lemma 5.2
.

For any strictly convex hypersurface
\Sigma
in
\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
with free boundary, there exists a constant

(n+1)W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma})-W_{1}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})\leq-c_{1}.

Proof.

By (

|\Sigma|-\frac{1}{n}|\partial\Sigma|\leq-c_{1}.

Similarly as in the proof of Lemma

(5.9)

C_{\Sigma}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:y=sx,0\leq s\leq 1,x\in\partial\Sigma\}.

Moreover, taking

It follows easily that all

and satisfy

\frac{|C_{\Sigma}|}{|\partial C_{\Sigma}|}=\frac{|C_{\Sigma}|}{|\partial\Sigma|}=\frac{|\mathbb{B}^{n}|}{|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|}=\frac{1}{n}.

On the other hand, according to
[

(5.12)

\displaystyle W_{1}^{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}(\widehat{\Sigma})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+1}(|\Sigma|+|\widehat{\partial\Sigma}|),

(5.13)

\displaystyle W_{1}^{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}(\widehat{D_{\Sigma}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+1}(|D_{\Sigma}|+|\widehat{\partial\Sigma}|),

(5.14)

\displaystyle W_{1}^{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}(\widehat{C_{\Sigma}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+1}(|C_{\Sigma}|+|\widehat{\partial\Sigma}|).

Then, combined these with (

\displaystyle|\Sigma|-\frac{1}{n}|\partial\Sigma|=

\displaystyle|\Sigma|-|C_{\Sigma}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n+1)W_{1}^{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}(\widehat{\Sigma})-(n+1)W_{1}^{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}(\widehat{C_{\Sigma}})

\displaystyle(n+1)W_{1}^{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}(\widehat{D_{\Sigma}})-(n+1)W_{1}^{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}(\widehat{C_{\Sigma}})

\displaystyle|D_{\Sigma}|-|C_{\Sigma}|

\displaystyle-c_{1}<0,

Similarly, for

.

For any strictly convex hypersurface

in

Let

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}_{\Sigma}(\dot{E}_{k}^{ij}\langle x,e_{i}\rangle e_{j})

Lambert and Scheuer

Lemma 5.4

]

.

Let
\Sigma

|\Sigma|\leq b_{n}-c_{\partial\Sigma},

\partial\Sigma

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\Sigma
be a weakly convex hypersurface with free boundary in
\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
such that
\partial\Sigma
is not an equator. Then there holds

(5.21)

|\Sigma|\geq d_{\partial\Sigma},

where

is a constant depending on the inner radius of

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
\Sigma
is strictly convex, the weakly convex case then follows by approximation. For any

such that
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
is contained in the open hemisphere

, we claim that the maximum of
\langle x,e\rangle

\langle x,e\rangle_{i}=0

must be attained on

This implies the lower bound

5.1.
The case of strictly convex hypersurfaces

In this subsection, we show that the strict inequality in (

In particular, for a geodesic ball
B_{\rho}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
, this yields

W_{2k-1}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(B_{\rho})=\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{(2k-2)!!(n-2k+1)!!}{(2k-2i-2)!!(n-2k+2i+1)!!}\cos^{2k-2i-2}\rho\sin^{n-2k+2i+1}\rho,

and hence

(5.26)

W_{2k-1}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(B_{\frac{\pi}{2}}(e))=\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{n}\frac{(2k-2)!!(n-2k+1)!!}{(n-1)!!}.

Combining (
5.25
) and (
5.26
) yields (
5.23
) for
k\geq 1
. The case
k=0
follows directly from (
5.1
) and the fact that
b_{n}=\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{n}
.
∎

Next, we proceed by induction to prove that the strict inequality in (
1.8
) holds for all strictly convex hypersurfaces
\Sigma
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}(n\geq 2)
be a properly embedded, strictly convex smooth hypersurface in the unit ball with free boundary. Then for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
2k+1\leq n
, there holds

(5.27)

W_{2k+1}(\widehat{\Sigma})>\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{n}\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{k}(n-2j)}{\prod_{j=0}^{k}(n+1-2j)}\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{k}{i}\frac{1}{n-2k+2i}\left[\frac{n(n+1)}{\omega_{n-1}}W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma})\right]^{\frac{n-2k+2i}{n}}.

Proof.

We firstly prove (
5.27
) for
k=1
. Along the flow (
1.3
) with
F=\frac{1}{E_{1}}
, we have

(5.28)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{n+1}|\Sigma_{t}|=nW_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t}),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}W_{3}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{n-2}{n+1}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}{\frac{E_{3}}{E_{1}}}\,dA_{t}\leq\frac{n-2}{n+1}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}{E_{2}}\,dA_{t}

\displaystyle=(n-2)\left[W_{3}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})-\frac{2}{(n+1)(n-1)}W_{1}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma}_{t})\right]

(5.29)

\displaystyle<(n-2)W_{3}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})-\frac{2(n-2)}{n-1}W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t}),

where we used Lemma
5.2
in the last inequality. Combining (
5.28
) and (
5.29
) yields

\partial_{t}\left(W_{3}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})+\frac{n-2}{n-1}W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})\right)<(n-2)\left(W_{3}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})+\frac{n-2}{n-1}W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})\right).

If we define

(5.30)

Q(t)=W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})^{-\frac{n-2}{n}}\left(W_{3}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})+\frac{n-2}{n-1}W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})\right),

then, by combining(
5.28
) with (
5.30
), we conclude that along the flow (
1.3
), the following monotonicity property holds:

(5.31)

\frac{d}{dt}Q(t)<0.

By a direct calculation using Proposition
5.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to T^{*}}W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{n(n+1)},

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to T^{*}}W_{3}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{2\omega_{n-1}}{n(n+1)(n-1)},

and thus

(5.32)

\lim_{t\to T^{*}}Q(t)=\frac{n^{\frac{n-2}{n}}}{n-1}\left(\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{n+1}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}},

which establishes (
5.27
) for
k=1
.

In the following, we assume that the strict inequality between
W_{2k-1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})
and
W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})
holds. We then proceed by induction to show that the strict inequality between
W_{2k+1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})
and
W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})
also holds. We denote

(5.33)

A_{k}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})=\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{n}\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{k}(n-2j)}{\prod_{j=0}^{k}(n+1-2j)}\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{k}{i}\frac{1}{n-2k+2i}\left[\frac{n(n+1)}{\omega_{n-1}}W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})\right]^{\frac{n-2k+2i}{n}}

and define the auxiliary function

(5.34)

\phi(t)=\frac{W_{2k+1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})-A_{k}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})}{W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})^{\frac{n-2k}{n}}},

then by a direct calculation, we obtain

(5.35)

\frac{d}{dt}\phi(t)=\frac{\frac{d}{dt}W_{2k+1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})-(n-2k)W_{2k+1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})}{W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})^{\frac{n-2k}{n}}}-\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{A_{k}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})}{W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})^{\frac{n-2k}{n}}}\right).

Since

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}W_{2k+1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})=

\displaystyle\frac{n-2k}{n+1}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}{\frac{E_{2k+1}}{E_{1}}}\,dA_{t}\leq\frac{n-2k}{n+1}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}{E_{2k}}\,dA_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-2k)\left[W_{2k+1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})-\frac{2k}{(n+1)(n-2k+1)}W_{2k-1}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma}_{t})\right]

(5.36)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(n-2k)W_{2k+1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})-\frac{2k(n-2k)}{n-2k+1}W_{2k-1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t}),

by combining (
5.35
) with (
5.36
), we have

(5.37)

\frac{d}{dt}\phi(t)\leq-\frac{2k(n-2k)}{n-2k+1}\frac{W_{2k-1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})}{W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})^{\frac{n-2k}{n}}}-\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{A_{k}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})}{W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})^{\frac{n-2k}{n}}}\right).

Now, using the assumption that the strict inequality between
W_{2k-1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})
and
W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})
holds, a direct computation yields

-\frac{2k(n-2k)}{n-2k+1}\frac{W_{2k-1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})}{W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})^{\frac{n-2k}{n}}}-\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{A_{k}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})}{W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})^{\frac{n-2k}{n}}}\right)<0.

Therefore,

(5.38)

\phi(0)>\lim_{t\to T^{*}}\phi(t).

Next, we prove that
\lim_{t\to T^{*}}W_{2k+1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})=\lim_{t\to T^{*}}A_{k}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})
. By Proposition
5.6
, it is equivalent to prove

(5.39)

\frac{(2k)!!}{\prod_{j=0}^{k}(n-2j)}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{k}{i}\frac{1}{n-2k+2i}.

If we denote the right hand side of (
5.39
) as
S(n,k)
, then we obtain

\displaystyle S(n,k)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{k}{i}\frac{1}{n-2k+2i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\left[\binom{k-1}{i}+\binom{k-1}{i-1}\right]\frac{1}{n-2k+2i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{i}\binom{k-1}{i}\frac{1}{n-2-2(k-1)+2i}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i-1}\binom{k-1}{i-1}\frac{1}{n-2k+2i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{i}\binom{k-1}{i}\frac{1}{n-2-2(k-1)+2i}-\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{i}\binom{k-1}{i}\frac{1}{n-2(k-1)+2i}

(5.40)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(n-2,k-1)-S(n,k-1).

It’s easy to verify that (
5.39
) holds for
n=1
and 2. Moreover, (
5.39
) also holds for the positive integer pairs
(n,k)=(n,0)
and
(n,1)
for all
n
. Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a pair
(n,k)
with
n\geq 3
and
2k+1\leq n
such that (
5.39
) does not hold. Let
n_{0}
be the minimal integer for which (
5.39
) fails, and for this fixed
n_{0}
, let
k_{0}
be the minimal integer such that (
5.39
) fails for
(n_{0},k_{0})
. Then by previous observation, we must have
n_{0}\geq 3
and
k_{0}\geq 2
. However, we observe that

\displaystyle S(n_{0},k_{0})

\displaystyle=S(n_{0}-2,k_{0}-1)-S(n_{0},k_{0}-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2k_{0}-2)!!}{\prod_{j=0}^{k_{0}-1}(n_{0}-2-2j)}-\frac{(2k_{0}-2)!!}{\prod_{j=0}^{k_{0}-1}(n_{0}-2j)}

(5.41)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2k_{0})!!}{\prod_{j=0}^{k_{0}}(n_{0}-2j)},

which leads to a contradiction. This completes the proof of Lemma
5.7
.
∎

5.2.
The case of weakly convex hypersurfaces

In this subsection, we assume that
\Sigma
is a weakly convex hypersurface. If
\Sigma
is a flat disk with free boundary, then the equality in (
1.8
) clearly holds. If
\Sigma
is not flat, then the inequality (
1.8
) follows from approximation using the mean curvature flow with free boundary.Therefore, in the remainder of this subsection, we prove that if
\Sigma
is weakly convex but not flat, then the strict inequality in (
1.8
) holds. The mean curvature flow for hypersurfaces in the unit ball with free boundary reads as:

(5.42)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}x&=-H\nu\qquad\text{in}\quad\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times[0,T),\\
\langle\bar{N}\circ x,\nu\rangle&=0\qquad\text{on}\quad\partial\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times[0,T),\\
x(\cdot,0)&=x_{0}(\cdot)\qquad\text{on}\quad\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

By
[
Stahl1996-2
, Theorem 2.1]
, there exists a solution of (
5.42
) for
x(\cdot,t)\in C^{\infty}(\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times(0,\varepsilon])\cap C^{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times[0,\varepsilon])
for small
\varepsilon>0
and we have the following approximation result.

Theorem 5.8

(
[
Lambert-Scheuer2017
]
)
.

Suppose
x(\cdot,t):\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a solution to flow (
5.42
) with initial hypersurface
\Sigma_{0}
being weakly convex with free boundary. Then either
\partial\Sigma_{0}
is an equator of the unit sphere or
(h_{ij})>0
for
t>0
.

Firstly, we prove a stronger version of Lemma
5.7
, which will be needed in later analysis.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}(n\geq 2)
be a properly embedded, strictly convex smooth hypersurface in the unit ball with free boundary. Then for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
2k+1\leq n
, there exists a constant
a_{k}>0
, depending only on
n,k,\partial\Sigma
and the length of the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
such that

(5.43)

W_{2k+1}(\widehat{\Sigma})\geq\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{n}\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{k}(n-2j)}{\prod_{j=0}^{k}(n+1-2j)}\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{k}{i}\frac{1}{n-2k+2i}\left[\frac{n(n+1)}{\omega_{n-1}}W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma})\right]^{\frac{n-2k+2i}{n}}+a_{k}.

Proof.

We firstly prove (
5.43
) for
k=1
. By Lemma
2.3
, there exists a vector
e\in\mathrm{int}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})
, a constant
\delta
depending on
\partial\Sigma
and the length of the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
, such that
\langle x,e\rangle\geq\delta
for any
x\in\Sigma
. Then there exists a radius
0<R<\infty
, depending on
\delta
, such that

(5.44)

\{x\in\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}:\langle x,e\rangle\geq\delta\}\subset\widehat{C_{\frac{\pi}{2},R}}(e).

By a direct calculation, we conclude that the maximal existence times
T_{1}^{*}
and
T_{2}^{*}
for the flow (
1.3
) with
F=\frac{1}{E_{1}}
starting from
\Sigma
and
C_{\frac{\pi}{2},R}(e)
satisfy

\displaystyle T_{1}^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\frac{b_{n}}{|\Sigma|}\right),

\displaystyle T_{2}^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\frac{b_{n}}{|C_{\frac{\pi}{2},R}(e)|}\right).

where
T_{2}^{*}
depends only on
n
and
R
, and we have
T_{1}^{*}>T_{2}^{*}
. We take

(5.45)

\bar{T}=\frac{1}{2}T_{2}^{*}=\frac{1}{2n}\log\left(\frac{b_{n}}{|C_{\frac{\pi}{2},R}(e)|}\right).

Assume that
\{\Sigma_{t}\}
and
\{\tilde{\Sigma}_{t}\}
are the flow hypersurfaces to the flow (
1.3
) starting from
\Sigma
and
C_{\frac{\pi}{2},R}(e)
respectively. Then by the avoidance principle, we have
\widehat{\Sigma}_{t}\subset\widehat{\tilde{\Sigma}}_{t}
for
0\leq t\leq\bar{T}
. If we take
\delta_{1}>0
as

(5.46)

\delta_{1}=\min\{\langle x,e\rangle:x\in\tilde{\Sigma}_{\bar{T}}\},

we see easily that
\delta_{1}
depends on
n
,
\partial\Sigma
and the length of the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
. Moreover, for all
0\leq t\leq\bar{T}
, there holds

(5.47)

\widehat{\partial\Sigma}\subset\widehat{\partial\Sigma}_{t}\subset\widehat{\partial\tilde{\Sigma}}_{\bar{T}},

where
\widehat{\partial\tilde{\Sigma}}_{\bar{T}}
depends on
n
and
R
. Hence by Lemma
5.2
, there exists an uniform constant
c_{1}>0
, depending only on
n
and
\delta_{1}
such that

(5.48)

W_{1}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma}_{t})\geq(n+1)W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})+c_{1},\quad 0\leq t\leq\bar{T}.

Then repeating the proof in Lemma
5.7
and using (
5.48
), (
5.31
) now becomes

(5.49)

\frac{d}{dt}Q(t)\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\frac{2(n-2)c_{1}}{(n+1)(n-1)}W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})^{-\frac{n-2}{n}},\,\,0<t\leq\bar{T},\\
&0,\,\,\bar{T}<t\leq T_{1}^{*}.\end{aligned}\right.

Applying Lemma
5.4
, we have

W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})=\frac{1}{n+1}|\Sigma_{t}|\leq\frac{b_{n}}{n+1},

then for
0\leq t\leq\bar{T}
, there holds

(5.50)

\frac{d}{dt}Q(t)\leq-\frac{2(n-2)c_{1}}{(n+1)(n-1)}\left(\frac{b_{n}}{n+1}\right)^{-\frac{n-2}{n}},

which in turn implies

\displaystyle W_{3}(\widehat{\Sigma})\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{(n+1)(n-1)}\left[\frac{n(n+1)}{\omega_{n-1}}W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma})\right]^{\frac{n-2}{n}}-\frac{n-2}{n-1}W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma})

\displaystyle+\frac{2(n-2)c_{1}}{(n+1)(n-1)}\left(\frac{b_{n}}{n+1}\right)^{-\frac{n-2}{n}}W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma})^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\bar{T}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{(n+1)(n-1)}\left[\frac{n(n+1)}{\omega_{n-1}}W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma})\right]^{\frac{n-2}{n}}-\frac{n-2}{n-1}W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma})

\displaystyle+\frac{(n-2)c_{1}}{n(n+1)(n-1)}\left(\frac{d_{\partial\Sigma}}{b_{n}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\log\left(\frac{b_{n}}{|C_{\frac{\pi}{2},R}(e)|}\right)

(5.51)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{(n+1)(n-1)}\left[\frac{n(n+1)}{\omega_{n-1}}W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma})\right]^{\frac{n-2}{n}}-\frac{n-2}{n-1}W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma})+a_{1},

where we used Lemma
5.5
in the second inequality, and
a_{1}>0
depends on
n,\partial\Sigma
and the length of the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
.

Next, we assume that there holds

W_{2k-1}(\widehat{\Sigma})\geq A_{k-1}(\widehat{\Sigma})+a_{k-1}.

The auxiliary function
\phi(t)
is defined as in (
5.34
). Using the above assumption, by a direct calculation, we obtain

(5.52)

\frac{d}{dt}\phi(t)\leq-\frac{2k(n-2k)}{n-2k+1}\frac{a_{k-1}}{W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})^{\frac{n-2k}{n}}}\leq-\frac{2k(n-2k)}{n-2k+1}\left(\frac{b_{n}}{n+1}\right)^{-\frac{n-2k}{n}}a_{k-1},

which implies

\displaystyle W_{2k+1}(\widehat{\Sigma})

\displaystyle\geq A_{k}(\widehat{\Sigma})+\frac{2k(n-2k)}{n-2k+1}\left(\frac{b_{n}}{n+1}\right)^{-\frac{n-2k}{n}}a_{k-1}W_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma})^{\frac{n-2k}{n}}

\displaystyle\geq A_{k}(\widehat{\Sigma})+\frac{2k(n-2k)}{n-2k+1}\left(\frac{d_{\partial\Sigma}}{b_{n}}\right)^{\frac{n-2k}{n}}a_{k-1}

(5.53)

\displaystyle:=A_{k}(\widehat{\Sigma})+a_{k}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
5.9
.
∎

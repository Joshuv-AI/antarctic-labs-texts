Wick integrals
Carlo Bellingeri, Emilio Ferrucci

Abstract
We introduce the Wick integral ∫ s t p ⁡ ( X u ) ⋄ d ​ X u \int_{s}^{t}p(X_{u})\diamond\mathrm{d}X_{u} for a class of stochastic processes X X which are not necessarily Gaussian, in the regime of bounded 2 > q 2>q -variation. The integral is defined for polynomial integrands, and has the property of being centred if X X is such. In the case of 1 / 2 < H 1/2<H -fractional Brownian motion, the Wick integral agrees with the divergence operator in Malliavin calculus. It satisfies a correction formula with the Young integral ∫ p ⁡ ( X ) ​ 𝑑 X \int p(X)\mathrm{d}X and an Itô formula which have arbitrarily many correction terms (only limited by the degree of p p ), given by integration against the cumulant functions of X X , and reduce to familiar identities in the Gaussian case. These results are obtained by first developing diagram formulae for Appell polynomials. Our theory applies to a range of processes taking values in bounded Wiener chaos, such as the Rosenblatt process.

Introduction

Wick products were first introduced by Wick in quantum field theory
[
34
]
, later understood as a way of replacing ordinary monomials with polynomials that are centred under the probability measure considered, but which still behave as monomials under differentiation (see e.g.
[
30
]
for a comprehensive treatment). The idea of a sequence of polynomials satisfying the latter property was introduced much earlier by Appell
[
3
]
. These polynomials—we shall refer to them as Appell polynomials, while attributing the product to Wick—entered the probability theory literature in the ’80s thanks to their relevance to limit theorems
[
5
,
31
]
. For Gaussian distributions they are much better known as the Hermite polynomials, and they are orthogonal.

The Wick product, which generates a sequence of Appell polynomials starting from the probability measure, is not an associative operation, as the probability law of the Appell polynomials is not the same as that of its factors. One can however impose associativity of the Wick product w.r.t. a set of reference random variables, and obtain an associative product. This is mainly considered when the reference measure is Gaussian
[
16
, Definition 3.2]
, in which case it mirrors (through the Wiener chaos isometry) the symmetric product on Fock space. This product is closely related to the divergence operator in Malliavin calculus, which can indeed, in certain cases, be approximated with Riemann-Stieltjes sums in which integrand and integrator are Wick-multiplied
[
1
,
23
]
. The idea of making Wick products associative works more abstractly for general reference measures admitting finite moments, and was recently introduced by
[
12
]
in the context of Hopf-algebraic renormalisation. Following
[
16
]
, we continue to call it the Wick product.

The aim of this work is to put the associative Wick product to use in the construction of a stochastic integration theory for processes that are not required to be Gaussian. Our contributions are as follows. In
Section 1
, after introducing some groundwork on to the Wick product, we develop diagram formulae to compute the product of Appell polynomials
Theorem 1.10
and the Wick product of ordinary monomials
Theorem 1.11
, extending the moment and cumulant formulae in
[
13
]
. A “change of chaos” formula is developed for Appell polynomials of Appell polynomials (the former centred w.r.t. the measure of the latter), making precise the aforementioned failure in associativity. In
Section 2
we define the
Wick integral

2.1
of a polynomial one-form against a multidimensional stochastic process
X
with sample paths of bounded
2>q
-variation, with statistics that are sufficiently regular in time, and whose laws are free from certain polynomial relations. We do this in terms of Riemann-Stieltjes-Wick sums, similarly to
[
1
,
23
]
, but for laws that need not be Gaussian. In
Theorem 2.2
we prove that such sums converge to the sum of a Young integral as the sum of Stietljes integrals against the cumulant functions of the process. This enables the computation of the mean of the Young integral as a sum of Stieltjes integrals of expectations of a polynomials of
X_{t}

We note that these formulae hold for much more general integrands than the ones we consider here, thanks to the very well developed frameworks of Malliavin calculus and white noise analysis. Lack of associativity.

The polynomials

1.5
Well defined product.

We are primarily interested in the Wick product as a product of polynomial random variables, not abstract polynomials. This is how it is defined in

and that of

\diamond\colon\mathbb{R}[X^{\mathcal{I}}]\odot\mathbb{R}[X^{\mathcal{I}}]\to\mathbb{R}[X^{\mathcal{I}}]\text{ .}

In

can fail to be well defined as a product on random variables. We also note that even if polynomials are present, it may be possible to define the Wick product on restricted classes of polynomials; for example,

By far the best know example of Appell polynomials is the case in which the random variables

In general, however, the orthogonal polynomials and the Appell polynomials associated to a measure are different.

1.15
.

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\right)\diamond x

2
Wick integrals of polynomial one-forms in the Young regime

Let
X=(X^{1},\ldots,X^{d})\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a stochastic process on
[0,T]
satisfying the following hypotheses:

1.

The sample paths of
X
are a.s. of bounded
p
-variation for some
p<2
;

2.

X
has cumulants/moments of all orders, and for any
s\in[0,T]
and
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\beta\in[d]
, the function
t\mapsto\kappa[X_{s}^{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,X_{s}^{\alpha_{n}},X^{\beta}_{t}]
is
C^{1}
;

3.

For any
t\in[0,T]
, the vector
(X^{1}_{t},\ldots,X^{d}_{t})
has no polynomial relations on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

We assume such conditions hold by default. Throughout this section we will denote
X_{s,t}=X_{t}-X_{s}
and similar.

2.1
.

Under the conditions above and for
0\leq s<t\leq T
and
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}\in\mathbb{R}[x^{1},\ldots,x^{d}]
, we define the
Wick integral
(in probability, almost surely, in
L^{q}
)

\int_{s}^{t}p_{\beta}(X_{u})\diamond\mathrm{d}X^{\beta}_{u}\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{[u,v]\in\pi_{n}}p_{\beta}(X_{u})\diamond X^{\beta}_{u,v}\text{ ,}

where the limit is taken along an arbitrary sequence of partitions with vanishing mesh size. Here and below we use the Einstein convention on up-down indices, here over
\beta
.

This definition can be viewed as an extension of the Skorokhod integral in certain regimes (restricted to polynomial integrands) to the more general case. Indeed,
[
24
, Definition 4]
define it this way for fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H>1/4
and Malliavin-smooth integrands.

We could define the Wick integral in greater generality, e.g. assuming finite-dimensional marginals

(X_{t_{1}},\ldots,X_{t_{n}})
admit a density on
\mathbb{R}^{nd}
, and for an arbitrary polynomial
p
in

we will, however, restrict ourselves to the case of
2.1
.

For a submultiset
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{n}

We will view polynomials in

The limit in

2.3
Non-centred case.

Since the reverse product formula
Theorem 1.11
does not assume mean zero, the formulae in this section all work in the non-centred case as well. The only modification that is possible is that, in order to obtain a centred integral, one has
\kappa^{\beta}(t)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}X^{\beta}_{t}
(assumed

) and
\widetilde{X}^{\beta}_{t}\coloneqq X^{\beta}_{t}-\kappa^{\beta}(t)
, so it holds that

\int_{s}^{t}p_{\beta}(X)\diamond\mathrm{d}X^{\beta}=\int_{s}^{t}p_{\beta}(X)\diamond\mathrm{d}\widetilde{X}^{\beta}+\int_{s}^{t}p_{\beta}(X_{u})\mu^{\beta}(\mathrm{d}u)

where we may substitute the base random variables

with

, necessary in the definition of Wick product, thanks to

. Here
p_{\beta}(X)
can be viewed as a polynomial in
\widetilde{X}_{t},\mu(t)
, but notice that the dependencies in
\mu_{t}
never show up in the higher cumulants, as they are deterministic. The bias term in (

{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
unrestricted. A similar comment holds for the Itô formula below. This makes it possible to include in

of time

By making the integrand exact, we can leverage symmetry to obtain a change-of-variables formula analogous to the Itô formula, with higher-order corrections.

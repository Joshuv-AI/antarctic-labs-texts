Properties of the one-component Coulomb gas on a sphere with two macroscopic external charges
Sung-Soo Byun, Peter J. Forrester, Sampad Lahiry

Abstract
The one-component Coulomb gas on the sphere, consisting on N N unit charges interacting via a logarithmic potential, and in the presence of two external charges each of strength proportional to N N , is considered. There are two spherical caps naturally associated with the external charges, giving rise to two distinct phases depending on them not overlapping (post-critical) or overlapping (pre-critical). The equilibrium measure in the post-critical phase is known from earlier work. We determine the equilibrium measure in the pre-critical phase using a particular conformal map, with the parameters therein specified in terms of a root of a certain fourth order polynomial. This is used to determine the exact form of the electrostatic energy for the pre-critical phase. Using a duality relation from random matrix theory, the partition function for the Coulomb gas at the inverse temperature β = 2 \beta=2 can be expanded for large N N in the post-critical phase, and in a scaling region of the post and pre-critical boundary. For the pre-critical phase, the duality identity implies a relation between two electrostatic energies, one for the present sphere system, and the other for a certain constrained log-gas relating to the Jacobi unitary ensemble.

Introduction

1.1
Outline of the model system and phase transition effect

A defining aspect of a many body Coulomb system is that the pair interactions are specified by a potential coming from the solution of the appropriate Poisson equation. This will depend on the space dimension and also geometry. For example, in two dimensions, the solution of the Poisson equation
\nabla_{\mathbf{r}}^{2}\Phi(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})=-2\pi\delta(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
is the logarithmic potential
\Phi(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})=-\log|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|
. Here it is assumed that the two-dimensional space is flat, and is unique only up to an additive constant. Other often considered circumstances are when the two-dimensional space has periodic boundary conditions in one direction (
x
-direction say, of period
L
), or when it is
the surface of a sphere of radius
R_{\rm s}
. In the former situation, the solution of the Poisson equation is

-\log|\sin(\pi(x-x^{\prime}+i(y-y^{\prime}))/L)|
, unique up to an additive constant.
However, for any smooth
\Phi(\mathbf{r})
on the surface of the
sphere,
\Omega

\int_{\Omega}\Phi(\mathbf{r})\,d\mathbf{r}=0
. Thus the above written Poisson
equation does not have a solution on the sphere but rather must be modified to be charge neutral in the
sense that it integrates to zero, which can be achieved by adding
1/(2R_{\rm s}^{2})
. The solution of this
modified Poisson equation can again be shown to be given by
-\log|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|
, up to

[
27

One notes that for

(1.1)

(1.2)

3.1

In random matrix theory, the complex (generalised) spherical ensemble of non-Hermitian random matrices is specified by matrices
of the form
G_{1}^{-1}G_{2}

G_{1},G_{2}
are independent
GinUE matrices (GinUE matrices are

[
14

We denote by SrUE
(N,K)

(G^{\dagger}G)^{-1/2}X

where
G
is an
(N+K)\times N
complex standard Gaussian matrix, and
X
is a GinUE matrix (the spherical ensemble
corresponds to the case

From
[
27

\beta=2
and
n_{1}=N+K
]
, a matrix
Y\in{\rm SrUE}_{(N,K)}
has distribution

\det(\mathbb{I}_{N}+Y^{\dagger}Y)^{-K-2N}.

(3.1)

It can be deduced from this that the eigenvalue probability density function of matrices from
{\rm SrUE}_{(N,K)}
is
proportional to (see e.g.
[
14
, Eq. (2.54) with
M=N
and
n=K+N

From the discussion of §

We take (

N

submatrix
{\rm{T}}_{N,M}
of a random unitary
M\times M
matrix picked with respect to the normalised Haar measure.
The PDF for the eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
of
{\rm{T}}_{N,M}
is given by
[
54
]

\frac{1}{N!}\prod_{l=0}^{N-1}\frac{\Gamma(M-N+l+1)}{l!\,\Gamma(M-N)}\prod_{l=1}^{N}\frac{1}{(1-|z_{l}|^{2})^{N-M+1}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}|z_{k}-z_{j}|^{2}\,d\mathbf{r}_{1}\cdots d\mathbf{r}_{N}\qquad|\lambda_{j}|\leq 1.

(C.1)

It was shown in
[
24
, Theorem 1.10]
that for
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

\displaystyle\Big\langle|\det({\rm{T}}_{N,M}-v)|^{2r}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\Big\langle|\det({\rm{T}}_{N,M})|^{2r}\Big\rangle(1-|v|^{2})^{-r(M-N+r)}

\displaystyle\quad\times E(0,1-|v|^{2},1);{\rm JUE}_{r,(M-N,N)}).

(C.2)

Further, by
[
24
, Eq.(1.24)]
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Big\langle|\det({\rm T}_{N,M})|^{2r}\Big\rangle&=\prod_{l=0}^{N-1}\frac{\Gamma(r+l+1)\Gamma(M-N+l+1)}{l!\,\Gamma(M-N+r+l+1)},\end{split}

(C.3)

and so

\frac{1}{N!}\int_{|\mathbf{r}_{1}|<1}d\mathbf{r}_{1}\cdots\int_{|\mathbf{r}_{N}|<1}d\mathbf{r}_{N}\,\prod_{l=1}^{N}\frac{|v-z_{l}|^{2r}}{(1-|z_{l}|^{2})^{N-M+1}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}|z_{k}-z_{j}|^{2}\\
=(1-|v|^{2})^{-r(M-N+r)}\,E(0,(1-|v|^{2},1);{\rm JUE}_{r,(M-N,N)})\prod_{l=0}^{N-1}\frac{\Gamma(M-N)\Gamma(r+l+1)}{\Gamma(M-N+r+l+1)}.

(C.4)

On the other hand

{1\over(1+|w|^{2})^{r(K+N)}}\frac{1}{N!}\int_{|\mathbf{r}_{1}|<1}d\mathbf{r}_{1}\cdots\int_{|\mathbf{r}_{N}|<1}d\mathbf{r}_{N}\,\prod_{l=1}^{N}{|w-z_{l}|^{2r}\over(1+|z_{l}|^{2})^{K+r+N+1}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}|z_{k}-z_{j}|^{2}\\
\propto E(0,(1/(1+|w|^{2}),1);{\rm JUE}_{r,(K-r,N)}),

(C.5)

as follows from the working in the paragraph below (
3.4
).
Comparison with the RHS of (
C.4
) shows that under the setting of the parameters

|v|=\frac{|w|}{\sqrt{1+|w|^{2}}},\qquad K+N=M+r,

(C.6)

the partition functions for the Coulomb gas systems corresponding to the averaged power of the
characteristic polynomial in truncated unitary ensemble, and in the (generalised) spherical ensemble,
agree up to normalisations.

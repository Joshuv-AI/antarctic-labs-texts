Nomogram construction and validation for hypoalbuminemia after total hip arthroplasty in patients with hip fractures
Authors: Xiaodong Jia, Yu Jiang, Wanxue Wang, Xia Zhao, Tengbo Yu, Yingze Zhang, Tianrui Wang, Kuishuai Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTo investigate the risk factors for hypoalbuminemia after total hip arthroplasty (THA) in patients with hip fractures and to establish and validate a risk prediction nomogram.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on the clinical data of 688 patients with hip fractures who underwent THA at The Affiliated Hospital of Qingdao University between January 2021 and June 2025. There were 261 males and 427 females. Fracture types included 441 cases of femoral neck fracture and 247 cases of femoral intertrochanteric fracture. Patients were divided into a hypoalbuminemia group (n = 209) and a normal albumin group (n = 479) according to whether hypoalbuminemia occurred postoperatively. Differences in general data were compared between the two groups, including age, gender, body mass index (BMI), smoking history, drinking history, comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes, cerebrovascular disease, cardiovascular disease), fracture type, American Society of Anesthesiologists classification, anesthesia type, operative duration, intraoperative blood loss and postoperative drainage volume. Preoperative laboratory examinations, including albumin (ALB), red blood cell count, hemoglobin (HB), C-reactive protein (CRP), erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), platelet count (PLT), fibrinogen, activated partial thromboplastin time, D-dimer and serum calcium, were also included in the analysis. Parameters with P < 0.05 in univariate logistic regression were included in multivariate logistic regression to identify independent risk factors for postoperative hypoalbuminemia. The nomogram was developed using R software. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, calibration curve, and decision curve analysis (DCA) were used to evaluate the model. ROC curve analysis was performed to evaluate predictive performance, and the DeLong test was used to compare AUCs.ResultsSignificant differences were observed between the hypoalbuminemia group and the normal albumin group in age, BMI, diabetes, operative duration, intraoperative blood loss, preoperative ALB, preoperative HB, preoperative CRP, preoperative ESR, preoperative PLT and preoperative D-dimer (P < 0.05). Multivariate logistic regression showed that age, diabetes mellitus, BMI, operative duration, intraoperative blood loss, preoperative ALB, preoperative HB and preoperative ESR were independent risk factors for postoperative hypoalbuminemia in hip fracture patients undergoing THA (P < 0.05). The nomogram incorporating these risk factors yielded an area under the ROC curve of 0.880 (95% CI 0.849–0.911) in the training set and 0.889 (95% CI 0.840–0.938) in the validation set. Both calibration curve and DCA indicated favorable accuracy and clinical utility in both cohorts. ROC curve analysis showed that the combined model achieved the optimal predictive performance for the target outcome, which was significantly superior to all single indicators (all P < 0.001).ConclusionsAdvanced age, lower BMI, diabetes mellitus, longer operative duration, more intraoperative blood loss, lower preoperative albumin, lower preoperative hemoglobin and higher preoperative ESR are independent risk factors for postoperative hypoalbuminemia in patients undergoing THA for hip fractures. The nomogram prediction model established for these factors can integrate preoperative clinical data, is suitable for rapid clinical evaluation, and has good predictive ability.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12891-026-10125-3.

Introduction

Hip fractures are extremely common in clinical practice, mainly including femoral neck fractures and intertrochanteric fractures. They are associated with high risks of disability and mortality, posing serious threats to patients' health and imposing substantial medical and socioeconomic burdens on families and society [1]. With the accelerating process of population aging, there are nearly 10 million new cases worldwide each year. Epidemiological predictions indicate that the number of cases will double by 2050 [2, 3]. Currently, surgical intervention remains the primary treatment for hip fractures. Since the introduction of total hip arthroplasty (THA) in 1938, this procedure has been shown to enable early mobilization, effectively reducing the risk of complications associated with prolonged bed rest, such as pneumonia, deep vein thrombosis, and pressure ulcers. Consequently, THA can achieve better functional outcomes and aligns closely with the principles of enhanced recovery after surgery [4, 5].

Hypoalbuminemia is a common postoperative complication that can impair surgical wound healing and increase the risk of adverse events such as postoperative infections and pressure injuries. It may also prolong hospital stay, consume additional medical resources, and impose a greater economic and caregiving burden on patients and their families [6–8]. Existing studies have explored strategies for the prevention of postoperative hypoalbuminemia. Early identification and risk stratification of high-risk patients, followed by timely clinical intervention, may further reduce the incidence of postoperative hypoalbuminemia [9, 10].

Although several studies have investigated the risk factors for postoperative human albumin infusion, consensus has not yet been reached regarding the risk factors for hypoalbuminemia after total hip arthroplasty, especially in patients with hip fracture—a high-risk condition. In this study, we conducted a retrospective analysis of clinical data from 688 hip fracture patients who underwent THA at The Affiliated Hospital of Qingdao University between January 2021 and June 2025. The study aimed to identify risk factors for postoperative hypoalbuminemia following THA and to develop a nomogram prediction model, providing a basis for early screening, effective prevention, and precise clinical intervention for this complication.

Methods

Study population

This is a single-center retrospective cohort study conducted at The Affiliated Hospital of Qingdao University. The study population comprised patients with hip fractures who underwent THA between January 2021 and June 2025. Patient data were extracted from the existing hospital database. The inclusion criteria were: (1) patients with a single hip fracture treated surgically; (2) treatment with THA; (3) first occurrence of hip fracture; and (4) radiologically confirmed femoral neck fracture or intertrochanteric fracture. Patients were excluded if they had pathological fractures, severe organic diseases of the heart, liver, or kidney, or incomplete medical records. All patients agreed to participate and obtained informed consent forms. The study protocol was approved by the hospital’s ethics committee (QYFYWZLL 42077).

Surgical technique

All surgeries were performed by senior attending physicians in our department. For patients with femoral neck fractures, after anesthesia, the patient was positioned in the lateral decubitus position on the contralateral side. A longitudinal oblique incision was made centered on the greater trochanter, and the soft tissues were dissected layer by layer. The gluteus medius was partially detached with care to preserve its integrity. Part of the joint capsule was exposed and excised, followed by hip dislocation, femoral head osteotomy, removal, and measurement. The acetabulum was then reamed, and the acetabular component and liner were implanted. After proximal femoral canal preparation, the femoral stem and femoral head prostheses were sequentially inserted, and the hip joint was reduced. Once satisfactory positioning and stability were confirmed, meticulous hemostasis and irrigation were performed, and the incision was closed layer by layer. For patients with intertrochanteric fractures, after anesthesia, the patient was similarly positioned in the lateral decubitus position. A posterolateral approach to the hip was used, and soft tissues were dissected to expose the fracture site. The lesser trochanter was adequately exposed, followed by osteotomy. The acetabulum was reamed, and a suitable acetabular component was implanted. Fracture fragments were anatomically reduced and fixed using cortical screws combined with cerclage wiring. After proximal femoral canal preparation, the femoral stem was inserted, and the matching femoral head component was installed. Joint mobility and stability were assessed, and the incision was closed layer by layer.

Data collection

A standardized data collection form was prepared in advance for this study. Data were collected by physicians at the center who had received specialized training and were well acquainted with the inclusion and exclusion criteria. Postoperative hypoalbuminemia was defined as a serum albumin level < 30 g/L within 48 h after surgery. Patient demographics and clinical data were extracted from the medical record system, including age, sex, body mass index (BMI), smoking and alcohol history, comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes, cerebrovascular disease, cardiovascular disease), fracture type, American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) classification, anesthesia type, operative duration, intraoperative blood loss, postoperative drainage volume and preoperative laboratory examinations, including albumin (ALB), red blood cell count (RBC), hemoglobin (HB), C-reactive protein (CRP), erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), platelet count (PLT), fibrinogen (FIB), activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT), D-dimer and serum calcium. For patients with a medical history of hypertension or diabetes, pharmacological interventions may be administered to achieve optimal preoperative blood pressure (< 140/90 mmHg) and fasting blood glucose (< 7.3 mmol/L).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS version 27.0. Continuous variables were first tested for normality using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Parameters that did not follow a normal distribution were presented as median (Q1, Q3) and compared between groups using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test.

Using R software (version 4.5.2) and the dplyr package, the study population was randomly split at a ratio of 7:3 into a training set and a validation set with a fixed random seed for reproducibility. Parameters with a P-value < 0.05 in univariate logistic regression were included in the multivariate logistic regression model. Factors with P < 0.05 in the multivariate analysis were then incorporated into the prediction model for postoperative hypoalbuminemia in hip fracture patients undergoing THA.

The performance of the model was evaluated in both the training and validation cohorts. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were used to assess the discriminative ability of the model, while calibration curves combined with the Hosmer–Lemeshow (HL) test were employed to evaluate its calibration performance. The HL test was conducted to verify the goodness-of-fit between the predicted probabilities and the actual observed outcomes. Additionally, the Brier score was calculated to quantify the average squared difference between the predicted probabilities and the actual binary outcomes, providing an objective measure of the model’s prediction accuracy. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed to assess the clinical utility of the model by quantifying the net benefit across different threshold probabilities, further validating its clinical applicability. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. ROC curve analysis was performed to evaluate predictive performance, and the DeLong test was used to compare AUCs.

Results

Among the 688 patients included in this study, 209 patients (30.38%) developed postoperative hypoalbuminemia. There were no statistically significant differences between the hypoalbuminemia and non-hypoalbuminemia groups in terms of sex, smoking history, alcohol consumption, comorbid hypertension, cerebrovascular disease, cardiovascular disease, ASA classification, anesthesia type, fracture type, preoperative RBC, preoperative FIB, preoperative APTT. preoperative serum calcium or postoperative drainage volume (all P > 0.05; Table 1). In contrast, significant differences were observed between the two groups for age, BMI, comorbid diabetes, operative duration, intraoperative blood loss, preoperative ALB, preoperative HB, preoperative CRP, preoperative ESR, preoperative PLT and preoperative D-dimer (all P < 0.05; Table 1). Furthermore, postoperative drainage volume and APTT showed a statistically significant difference between the training and validation cohorts, and this parameter was not included in the final model. Construction and validation of the clinical prediction model

Based on the above analyses, a nomogram prediction model for postoperative hypoalbuminemia in hip fracture patients undergoing THA was developed and plotted using R software (version 4.5.2) (Fig. 2). The model incorporated eight variables: age, comorbid diabetes, BMI, operative duration, intraoperative blood loss, preoperative ALB, preoperative HB and preoperative ESR. In the nomogram, each parameter is assigned a weighted score corresponding to its specific value. The total score is calculated by summing the individual scores, which corresponds to the predicted probability of postoperative hypoalbuminemia. Higher total scores indicate a greater risk of developing hypoalbuminemia.Fig. 2A nomogram for predicting postoperative hypoalbuminemia in patients with hip fractures

A nomogram for predicting postoperative hypoalbuminemia in patients with hip fractures

The discriminatory ability of the nomogram was evaluated using ROC curves. The AUC was 0.880 (95% CI: 0.849 ~ 0.911) in the training set and 0.889 (95% CI: 0.840 ~ 0.938) in the validation set, indicating good discrimination (Fig. 3). Calibration curves demonstrated good agreement between predicted and observed risks in both cohorts (Fig. 4). The calibration of the nomogram was assessed using the HL test and Brier score in both the training and validation cohorts. In the training set, the HL test yielded a P-value of 0.8340, indicating no significant departure of the predicted probabilities from the observed outcomes, and the Brier score was 0.1271. In the validation set, the HL test P-value was 0.3323 and the Brier score was 0.1194. These results collectively demonstrate that the nomogram exhibits excellent calibration and predictive accuracy in both the development and external validation cohorts. ROC curve analysis was performed to assess the predictive performance of individual clinical factors and a combined model for the target outcome in 688 patients with hip fractures. The combined model, which integrated multiple clinical variables, achieved the highest predictive accuracy with an AUC of 0.883 (95% CI: 0.857–0.909). Among the individual predictors, BMI (AUC = 0.745, 95% CI: 0.705–0.786) and HB (AUC = 0.713, 95% CI: 0.671–0.755) demonstrated moderate discriminative ability, followed by age (AUC = 0.691, 95% CI: 0.650–0.731), intraoperative blood loss (AUC = 0.666, 95% CI: 0.622–0.709), and ALB (AUC = 0.645, 95% CI: 0.603–0.687). In contrast, operative duration (AUC = 0.618, 95% CI: 0.574–0.662), diabetes mellitus (AUC = 0.600, 95% CI: 0.560–0.640) and ESR (AUC = 0.588, 95% CI: 0.542–0.635) showed lower predictive value, with ESR exhibiting the weakest discriminative power (Fig. 6). The DeLong test revealed that the AUC of the combined model was significantly higher than that of all single predictors (all P < 0.001, Table 4). Discussion

To more accurately assess the risk of postoperative hypoalbuminemia in hip fracture patients undergoing THA, this study developed and validated a risk prediction model for this complication, aiming to evaluate its potential application in clinical risk assessment. Eight independent risk factors were identified: age, history of diabetes, BMI, operative duration, intraoperative blood loss, preoperative ALB level, preoperative HB level and preoperative ESR level.

The results of this study indicated that age was significantly positively associated with the occurrence of postoperative hypoalbuminemia in hip fracture patients undergoing THA. This finding is consistent with previous literature reporting an increased perioperative risk in elderly patients [11–13]. With advancing age, the body’s albumin reserves progressively decline. Under the stress of surgical trauma, elderly patients are more susceptible to a drop in serum albumin below the critical threshold, leading to hypoalbuminemia. For these patients, preoperative assessment of overall health, management of comorbidities, and optimization of early rehabilitation are essential to reduce the risk of postoperative hypoalbuminemia and improve long-term outcomes.

This study found that a history of diabetes increases the risk of postoperative hypoalbuminemia in hip fracture patients undergoing THA. Chronic hyperglycemia in diabetic patients can affect albumin metabolism and homeostasis through multiple pathophysiological pathways. On one hand, sustained hyperglycemic load may impair glomerular filtration, leading to increased renal permeability and albumin loss via urine, thereby exacerbating the risk of postoperative hypoalbuminemia. On the other hand, insufficient insulin secretion or peripheral insulin resistance directly inhibits hepatic albumin synthesis and secretion, making it difficult to maintain adequate albumin reserves during the perioperative stress response. When combined with surgical trauma, inflammatory responses, and enhanced postoperative catabolism, the liver’s capacity to synthesize albumin is further compromised, while renal losses increase, collectively causing a rapid decline in serum albumin levels below normal physiological thresholds. This finding contrasts with the study by Sun et al., which may be related to differences in perioperative glycemic control [14]. Therefore, for hip fracture patients with diabetes, perioperative blood glucose management and nutritional support should be reinforced. Dynamic monitoring of serum albumin levels and timely interventions are recommended to reduce the risk of postoperative hypoalbuminemia and related complications, ultimately improving clinical outcomes.

BMI has long been recognized as a factor influencing postoperative complications. Previous studies have shown that patients with low BMI have an increased risk of fractures, reduced bone mineral density, and a correspondingly higher postoperative mortality rate [15–17]. Previous studies have indicated that patients with low BMI often present with reduced muscle mass, disrupted internal homeostasis, and negative nitrogen balance, which are associated with a higher incidence of postoperative hypoalbuminemia. This may be related to insufficient preoperative protein reserves, enhanced perioperative catabolism, limited synthetic capacity, as well as impaired nutrient absorption and generally poor baseline physical condition [18]. Therefore, individualized preoperative nutritional strategies should be implemented for patients with low BMI to ensure adequate protein intake, and sufficient postoperative dietary support should be provided to meet nutritional requirements, which may help reduce the risk of postoperative hypoalbuminemia.

Prolonged operative duration may simultaneously exacerbate intraoperative albumin depletion, increase blood loss and intensify postoperative inflammatory responses [19]. Longer operative duration is often associated with more extensive tissue trauma and a heightened postoperative stress response. Sustained postoperative inflammation and metabolic disturbances further accelerate protein catabolism, thereby exacerbating the onset and progression of hypoalbuminemia [20]. This study also identified intraoperative blood loss as an independent risk factor for postoperative hypoalbuminemia. Significant intraoperative blood loss can result in tissue hypoperfusion and albumin loss, impair immune function, increase the risk of infection, and trigger excessive release of inflammatory mediators, collectively contributing to the development of hypoalbuminemia. Our study confirmed that operative duration and intraoperative blood loss are also risk factors for postoperative hypoalbuminemia in hip fracture patients undergoing THA. Surgeons can reduce the incidence of this complication by providing thorough preoperative consultation and implementing individualized surgical plans to minimize operative duration and blood loss.

Albumin, the most abundant protein in plasma, is synthesized by the liver and plays multiple critical physiological roles, including maintaining plasma colloid osmotic pressure, regulating fluid balance between tissues and vessels, transporting heme, hormones, and fatty acids, and exerting antioxidant effects. Preoperative albumin levels provide an objective reflection of a patient’s nutritional status and degree of systemic inflammation. Reduced albumin levels compromise the body’s tolerance to surgical trauma and significantly increase the risk of severe postoperative complications [21, 22]. Previous studies have indicated that low preoperative albumin levels and frailty are associated with an increased risk of hypoalbuminemia, which is consistent with the findings of this study [23]. In addition, albumin has a half-life of 15–19 days, making it difficult for the body to rapidly compensate for the acute loss and increased consumption caused by trauma and surgery. Therefore, perioperative protein supplementation and preventive nutritional support may offer potential clinical benefits by reducing the risk of postoperative hypoalbuminemia.

Hemoglobin is the protein within red blood cells responsible for oxygen transport and serves as an important indicator of a patient’s anemia status. In some patients, hypoalbuminemia and anemia often coexist [24, 25]. Previous studies have reported that patients undergoing renal or spinal tumor surgery who present with preoperative hypoalbuminemia have a higher risk of requiring allogeneic blood transfusion during the perioperative period. These findings further indicate that the occurrence of postoperative hypoalbuminemia should not only be assessed based on albumin levels but also consider other indicators reflecting the patient’s nutritional status [26, 27]. For patients with low hemoglobin, preoperative correction of anemia is recommended to optimize hemoglobin levels, which may include interventions such as blood transfusion or iron supplementation. Postoperatively, enhanced nutritional support and comprehensive monitoring of nutritional status are essential to promptly identify and manage potential complications.

Clinically, ESR is one of the most common inflammatory markers and is frequently used for the screening and diagnosis of inflammatory diseases. A previous study has demonstrated that elevated ESR is a risk factor for hypoalbuminemia after THA [23]. The underlying mechanism is that increased ESR reflects systemic inflammation and hypoalbuminemia, as albumin inhibits erythrocyte aggregation. In the present study of 688 patients with hip fractures, ESR was also statistically significant after adjustment by multivariate logistic regression. Thus, despite its modest AUC of 0.588 (95% CI: 0.542–0.635) in Fig. 6, ESR could still be incorporated as a valuable predictor in the combined model. As a convenient and widely available laboratory indicator, ESR may provide incremental prognostic information when integrated with other variables, thereby enhancing the overall predictive performance of the model.

Nomograms, as a novel clinical prediction tool, present predictive results in a simple and visual format, enhancing interpretability and ease of use, and are increasingly applied in clinical practice. The nomogram developed in this study demonstrated good predictive performance and can estimate the risk of postoperative hypoalbuminemia in hip fracture patients undergoing THA, thereby enabling clinicians to implement early interventions and prevent adverse outcomes. Compared with the nomogram by Gong et al., our model included age, BMI, operative duration, ESR, and further added diabetes, intraoperative blood loss, preoperative ALB and HB, while excluding serum calcium [28]. Our model covered more perioperative and nutritional indicators, with higher AUC and better clinical applicability. Compared with the nomogram developed by Yu et al., which incorporated gender, operative duration, erythrocyte sedimentation rate, alkaline phosphatase, and preoperative albumin as predictive factors, our model further included age, body mass index, diabetes, intraoperative blood loss, and preoperative hemoglobin [23]. These additional indicators comprehensively reflect age-related physiological decline, nutritional status, metabolic disorders, and intraoperative surgical stress, enabling a more thorough assessment of perioperative patient conditions and yielding higher predictive accuracy and stronger clinical utility.

This study has several limitations. First, as a single-center retrospective case–control study, the results may be subject to selection and information bias due to the study design and data source, which limits the generalizability of the findings. Second, the sample size was relatively small and future studies should include multicenter external validation and prospective cohort studies to further refine the prediction model. Third, due to limitations in data completeness, techniques and equipment, some potential influencing factors were not included in the analysis. Future research could expand the observed variables to comprehensively explore risk factors associated with postoperative hypoalbuminemia. Therefore, additional studies are warranted to validate and extend these findings using other datasets. Fourth, key intervention factors such as postoperative fluid replacement volume, postoperative fasting time, and whether prophylactic human albumin was used postoperatively were not considered. Therefore, additional studies are warranted to validate and extend these findings using other datasets.

Conclusions

In summary, advanced age, lower BMI, comorbid diabetes, longer operative duration, greater intraoperative blood loss, lower preoperative ALB and HB levels were identified as independent risk factors for postoperative hypoalbuminemia in hip fracture patients undergoing THA. The nomogram developed based on these factors can integrate preoperative clinical data for rapid clinical assessment and demonstrates good predictive performance. Clinicians may utilize this model for preoperative risk assessment and tailored adjustment of perioperative interventions, which may help support orthopedic clinical decision-making and facilitate the translational application of our research results.

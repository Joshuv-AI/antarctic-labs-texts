Introduction

The Gromov-Hausdorff distance is an important construction of metric geometry, that allows to define a pseudometric on the class of all metric spaces. This distance was first introduced by D. Edwards in
1975
in
[
3
]
and later became famous thanks to work
[
4
]
. Explicit historical details can be found in
[
12
]
.

Traditionally, the Gromov-Hausdorff distance is applied in the studies of compact metric spaces. The space of all compact metric spaces endowed with the Gromov-Hausdorff distance, called
the Gromov–Hausdorff space
, is well-studied. In particular, it is a complete, separable, geodesic metric space.

In the well-known monograph
[
7
]
, Mikhail Gromov described some properties of the space
\mathcal{GH}
of all metric spaces, not necessarily compact, considered up to isometry, endowed with the Gromov–Hausdorff distance. In particular,
Mikhail Gromov introduced classes of metric spaces at a finite distance from some fixed metric space (in
[
1
]
, such classes were called
clouds
). He announced that such classes are complete and contractible. As a simple example, he gave the Gromov–Hausdorff space. For this metric class, we can consider a natural mapping sending the metric space
(X,d_{X})
to
(X,\lambda d_{X})
. If we now let
\lambda
tend to
0
, we obtain the desired contraction. Mikhail Gromov
indicated that the class of metric spaces at a finite distance from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
also has similar properties.

Nevertheless, it turned out later that everything is not so simple. Firstly, when working with the class
\mathcal{GH}
, set-theoretic difficulties arise. It is easy to show, that within the framework of von Neumann–Bernays–Gödel (NBG) set theory, the space

\mathcal{GH}
, as well as any cloud, is not a set, but a proper class, i.e., it cannot belong to any other class. To prove that a cloud is contractible, it is necessary to define a topology on it. However, it is impossible
to introduce a topology on a proper class, since any topological space is an element of its topology, which is impossible for a proper class by definition. In
[
1
]
, the authors proposed a way to define an analogue of a topology on classes filtered by sets, as well as continuous mappings between them. Secondly, it turned out that not all clouds are invariant under the multiplication of all their metric spaces by an arbitrary number
\lambda>0
. In
[
1
]
, an example of a geometric progression
\{p^{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
for a prime number
p>2
with a metric induced from
\mathbb{R}
, which bounces off itself by an infinite Gromov–Hausdorff distance under multiplication by
2
, is given. Finally, while the completeness of an arbitrary cloud was carefully proved in
[
1
]
, contractibility has not yet been rigorously justified even for clouds of such natural metric spaces as
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The difficulty is to check the continuity of the natural mapping of the multiplication of all spaces of a given cloud by a positive
number
\lambda
.

Another important problem related to the geometry of the Gromov–Hausdorff distance in the class
\mathcal{GH}
is the problem of constructing geodesics. In
[
13
]
, a class of metric spaces
in general position
, everywhere dense in
\mathcal{GH}
, is presented, any
two of which, at a finite distance from each other, can be connected by a linear geodesic. However, it is still unknown whether any pair of metric spaces, located at a finite distance from each other, can be connected by some geodesic

In this paper, we construct new geodesics in the cloud of the real line. First, we show that if
A\subset\mathbb{R}
is an unbounded subset,
B\subset\mathbb{R}
is an arbitrary subset,
A^{\prime}
is a metric space at finite Gromov–Hausdorff distance from
A
, and
X
and
Y
are arbitrary bounded metric spaces, then the inequality
d_{GH}\bigl(A^{\prime}\times_{\ell^{1}}X,\,B\times_{\ell^{1}}Y\bigr)\geq\frac{\operatorname{diam}X-\operatorname{diam}Y}{2}
holds. Using this estimate, we prove that, for an arbitrary bounded metric space
X
and an unbounded subset
A\subset\mathbb{R}
, the curve
A\times_{\ell^{1}}(tX),\;t\in[0,\infty)
is a geodesic in the Gromov–Hausdorff class. After this, we give an example of a space from the cloud
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which multiplication by
t>0
will not yield a geodesic in the Gromov–Hausdorff class. Namely, we prove that for arbitrary
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda>1
, the inequality
d_{GH}\bigl(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\lambda Z^{n}\bigr)\geq\frac{1}{2}
holds, which implies that
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is the desired counterexample. Moreover, this inequality implies that the mapping of multiplication of all spaces in the cloud
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by an arbitrary
\lambda>0
is not continuous with respect to the Gromov–Hausdorff distance. We thus show that the proof of contractibility of the Gromov–Hausdorff space cannot be generalized straightforwardly to the case of the cloud
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For simplicity, in this place we do not use the technique of working with classes, filtered by sets, proposed in
[
1
]
. By the continuity of the mapping of the multiplication of all spaces of the cloud
[\mathbb{R}^{n}]
by
\lambda>0
we mean the following natural property: if a sequence of metric spaces
(X_{n})_{n}
such that
X_{n}\in[\mathbb{R}^{n}]
converges in the Gromov–Hausdorff sense to a metric space
X
, then
(\lambda X_{n})_{n}
converges in the Gromov–Hausdorff sense to
\lambda X
.

Preliminaries

In this section we provide definitions of the main constructions used, introduce notations, and formulate auxiliary results that we will need in proving the main results.

2.1
Gromov–Hausdorff distance

A metric space
is an arbitrary pair
(X,\,\operatorname{d}_{X})
, where
X
is an arbitrary set,
\operatorname{d}_{X}\colon X\times X\to[0,\,\infty)
is some metric on it, i. e. a non-negative, symmetric function that satisfies the triangle inequality.

The distance between any two points
x
and
y
of some metric space
(X,d_{X})
will often be denoted by
|xy|
for convenience. By
U^{X}_{r}(a)=\{x\in X\colon|ax|<r\}
,
B^{X}_{r}(a)=\{x\in X\colon|ax|\leq r\}
we denote the open and closed balls centered at point
a
of radius
r
in the metric space
X
. In those cases when it is clear in which metric space
X
the balls are considered, we will omit the superscript. For an arbitrary subset

A\subset X
of a metric space
X
, let
U_{r}(A)=\cup_{a\in A}U_{r}(a)
be an open
r
-neighborhood of
A
. For non-empty subsets
A\subset X
,
B\subset X
we put
\operatorname{d}(A,\,B)=\inf\bigl\{|ab|:\,a\in A,\,b\in B\bigl\}
.

Definition 1
.

Let
A
and
B
be non-empty subsets of a metric space.

The Hausdorff distance
between
A
and
B
is the quantity

\operatorname{d}_{H}(A,\,B)=\inf\bigl\{r>0\colon A\subset U_{r}(B),\,B\subset U_{r}(A)\bigr\}.

Definition 2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be metric spaces. The triple
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},Z)
, consisting of a metric space
Z
and its two subsets
X^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}
, isometric to
X
and
Y
respectively, is called
a realization of the pair

(X,Y)
.

Definition 3
.

The Gromov-Hausdorff distance

d_{GH}(X,Y)
between
X
and
Y
is the exact lower bound of the numbers
r\geq 0
for which there exists a realization
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},Z)
of the pair
(X,Y)
such that
\operatorname{d}_{H}(X^{\prime},\,Y^{\prime})\leq r
.

Now let
X,\,Y
be non-empty sets.

Definition 4
.

Each
\sigma\subset X\times Y
is called a
relation
between
X
and
Y
.

By
\mathcal{P}_{0}(X,\,Y)
we denote the set of all non-empty relations between
X
and
Y
.

We put

\pi_{X}\colon X\times Y\rightarrow X,\;\pi_{X}(x,\,y)=x,

\pi_{Y}\colon X\times Y\rightarrow Y,\;\pi_{Y}(x,\,y)=y.

Definition 5
.

A relation
R\subset X\times Y
is called a
correspondence
, if restrictions
\pi_{X}|_{R}
and
\pi_{Y}|_{R}
are surjective.

Let
\mathcal{R}(X,\,Y)
be the set of all correspondences between
X
and
Y
.

Definition 6
.

Let
X,\,Y
be metric spaces,
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(X,\,Y)
. The
distortion
of
\sigma
is the quantity

\operatorname{dis}\sigma=\sup\Bigl\{\bigl||xx^{\prime}|-|yy^{\prime}|\bigr|\colon(x,\,y),\,(x^{\prime},\,y^{\prime})\in\sigma\Bigr\}.

Proposition 1

(
[
2
]
)
.

For arbitrary metric spaces
X
and
Y
, the following equality holds

2\operatorname{d}_{GH}(X,\,Y)=\inf\bigl\{\operatorname{dis}\,R\colon R\in\mathcal{R}(X,\,Y)\bigr\}.

2.2
Clouds

By
\mathcal{VGH}
denote the class of all non-empty metric spaces, equipped with the Gromov–Hausdorff distance.

Theorem 1

(
[
2
]
)
.

The Gromov–Hausdorff distance is a generalized pseudometric on
\mathcal{VGH}
, which vanishes on each pair of isometric spaces. Namely, the Gromov–Hausdorff distance is symmetric, satisfies the triangle inequality, but, generally speaking, can be infinite.

The class
\mathcal{GH}_{0}
is obtained from
\mathcal{VGH}
by factorization by zero distances, that is, by the equivalence relation:
X\sim_{0}Y
iff
\operatorname{d}_{GH}(X,\,Y)=0
.

Definition 7
.

Consider the equivalence relation
\sim_{1}
on
\mathcal{GH}_{0}
:
X\sim_{1}Y
iff
\operatorname{d}_{GH}(X,\,Y)<\infty
. The corresponding equivalence classes are called
clouds
.

For an arbitrary metric space
X
, we denote the cloud it defines by
[X]
. By
\Delta_{1}
we denote the metric space consisting of a single point. Thus,
[\Delta_{1}]
is the cloud consisting of the classes of all bounded spaces at zero Gromov–Hausdorff distance from each other.

2.3
Cartesian product of metric spaces

Definition 8
.

Let
X,\,Y
be two non-empty sets. By
X\times_{\rho}Y
we denote the Cartesian product of
X
and
Y
, equipped with the metric
\rho
.

Now let
(X,\,\operatorname{d}_{X})
,
(Y,\,\operatorname{d}_{Y})
be arbitrary metric spaces.

Definition 9
.

By
X\times_{\ell^{1}}Y
we denote the Cartesian product
X\times Y
, equipped with the following
\ell^{1}
(or Manhattan) metric

\operatorname{d}\bigl(p,\,p^{\prime}\bigr)=\operatorname{d}_{X}(x,\,x^{\prime})+\operatorname{d}_{Y}(y,\,y^{\prime}),

where
p=(x,\,y)
,
p^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},\,y^{\prime})
are arbitrary points of
X\times Y
.

Definition 10
.

Suppose
A_{1},\,A_{2}
,
B_{1},\,B_{2}
are non-empty sets.
The Cartesian product
of correspondences
R_{1}\in\mathcal{R}(A_{1},\,A_{2})
and
R_{2}\in\mathcal{R}(B_{1},\,B_{2})
is a correspondence
R_{1}\times R_{2}
between
A_{1}\times B_{1}
and
A_{2}\times B_{2}
defined as follows

R_{1}\times R_{2}=\Bigl\{\bigl((a_{1},\,b_{1}),\,(a_{2},\,b_{2})\bigr)\colon(a_{1},\,a_{2})\in R_{1},\;(b_{1},\,b_{2})\in R_{2}\Bigr\}.

Lemma 1

(
[
2
]
,
[
11
]
)
.

Let
A_{1},\,A_{2}
,
B_{1},\,B_{2}
be arbitrary metric spaces,
R_{1}\in\mathcal{R}(A_{1},\,A_{2})
,
R_{2}\in\mathcal{R}(B_{1},\,B_{2})
. Then

(1)

R_{1}\times R_{2}\in\mathcal{R}\bigl(A_{1}\times_{\ell^{1}}B_{1},\,A_{2}\times_{\ell^{1}}B_{2}\bigr)
;

(2)

\operatorname{dis}R_{1}\times R_{2}\leq\operatorname{dis}R_{1}+\operatorname{dis}R_{2}
.

We will need the following simple statement

Lemma 2
.

Take
A_{1},\,A_{2}\in[X]
and
B_{1},\,B_{2}\in[Y]
, for two arbitrary metric spaces
X,\,Y
. Then

\operatorname{d}_{GH}\bigl(A_{1}\times_{\ell^{1}}B_{1},\,A_{2}\times_{\ell^{1}}B_{2}\bigr)\leq\operatorname{d}_{GH}(A_{1},\,A_{2})+\operatorname{d}_{GH}(B_{1},\,B_{2}).

Proof.

By the condition,
\operatorname{d}_{GH}(A_{1},\,A_{2})<\infty
,
\operatorname{d}_{GH}(B_{1},\,B_{2})<\infty
. By Proposition
1
, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exist correspondences
R_{1}\in\mathcal{R}(A_{1},\,A_{2})
and
R_{2}\in\mathcal{R}(B_{1},\,B_{2})
such that
\operatorname{dis}R_{1}<2\operatorname{d}_{GH}(A_{1},\,A_{2})+\varepsilon
,
\operatorname{dis}R_{2}<2\operatorname{d}_{GH}(B_{1},\,B_{2})+\varepsilon
. Then consider the correspondence
R_{1}\times R_{2}
between
A_{1}\times B_{1}
and
A_{2}\times B_{2}
. By Lemma
1
, the following inequality holds

\operatorname{dis}\bigl(R_{1}\times R_{2}\bigr)\leq\operatorname{dis}R_{1}+\operatorname{dis}R_{2}\leq 2\bigl(\operatorname{d}_{GH}(A_{1},\,A_{2})+\operatorname{d}_{GH}(B_{1},\,B_{2})\bigr)+2\varepsilon.

By Proposition
1
, since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we obtain

\operatorname{d}_{GH}\bigl(A_{1}\times_{\ell^{1}}B_{1},\,A_{2}\times B_{2}\bigr)\leq\operatorname{d}_{GH}(A_{1},\,A_{2})+\operatorname{d}_{GH}(B_{1},\,B_{2}),

which completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 2

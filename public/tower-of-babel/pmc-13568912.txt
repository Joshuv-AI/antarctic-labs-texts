Extraction method, structure–activity relationship and anti-tumor mechanism of Lycium barbarum polysaccharides
Authors: Shiyu Huang, Gangliang Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Lycium barbarum polysaccharide is the active component of Lycium barbarum, which exhibits immunomodulatory, antioxidant, hypoglycemic, and neuroprotective effects, as well as anti-tumor activity. The relative molecular weight, monosaccharide composition, glycosidic bonds, branching degree, protein content, chemical modification and spatial structure of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide are closely related to its biological activity. High-voltage pulsed electric field extraction, high-speed shearing technology-assisted extraction and ultrasonic-assisted subcritical water extraction are three new methods for the extraction of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide. Lycium barbarum polysaccharide can play an anti-tumor role by inducing tumor cell apoptosis, inhibiting cell proliferation, regulating immune function and inhibiting tumor angiogenesis. Herein, the new extraction methods, structure–activity relationship and anti-tumor mechanism of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide are summarized and analyzed. At present, the exploration of the structure–activity relationship of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide is still in the primary stage, and there is a lack of research on the relationship between the active fragments, active centers and polysaccharide activity. Study of the advanced structure and spatial conformation of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide will be helpful in revealing the specific structural sites where polysaccharides exert pharmacological effects and clarifying the molecular interaction between different structures and targets, which will be the focus of future studies and represents a way to enhance the innovation of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide research.

Introduction

Lycium barbarum L. exhibits many pharmacological activities, such as antioxidant,1 anti-aging,2 liver protection,3 and hypoglycemic activities.4 Among them, Lycium barbarum polysaccharide (LBP) is closely related to many physiological activities, and it is the main effective component in Lycium barbarum.5 Therefore, it is particularly important to extract as many polysaccharide components from Lycium barbarum as possible for the development and utilization of Lycium barbarum. In recent years, a lot of achievements have been made in the study of Lycium barbarum polysaccharides, with their structural characteristics, functions and medicinal value as the core. However, the mechanism through which complex Lycium barbarum polysaccharides exert their cytological and biological functions have not yet been clarified. In order to promote the more extensive application of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide, it is important to systematically sort out and summarize the structure, efficacy and relationship between its components, and discuss the influence of changes in these components on the biological activity, in order to provide a reference for the in-depth study of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide. LBP in Lycium barbarum L. has a good inhibitory effect on different tumor cells.6 In the research and development of modern Chinese medicine, LBP can also improve the immune ability of the body, reduce the damage caused to the body by drugs and facilitate postoperative recovery, and assist chemical drugs in achieving synergistic and attenuated effects. Based on this, the anti-tumor mechanism of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide has been summarized, which provides a theoretical basis for the application and development of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide.

The extraction methods of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide

Extraction technology is not only important for the extraction rate but also important for the preservation or change of the molecular structure and biological activity of polysaccharides.

High-voltage pulsed electric field extraction method

High-voltage pulsed electric field (HPEF) extraction is a new extraction method that uses the principle of cell membrane electroporation to instantly rupture the cell wall so that the contents flow out and the cell membrane potential is destroyed. The high-voltage pulsed electric field extraction method has been widely used in food processing and natural product extraction because of its short time consumption and low reagent consumption, but there are few reports on the extraction of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide and no comparative data with other extraction methods.7 Cai et al.7 used a high-voltage pulsed electric field to extract Lycium barbarum polysaccharide, and optimized the extraction process by central composite design-response surface methodology. The results showed that the highest extraction rate of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide was achieved under the conditions of temperature 61.76 °C, electric field intensity 20.49 kV cm−1, pH 8.98, pulse frequency 10 520 Hz and liquid-material ratio 9.43 ∶ 1 (mL g−1). The extraction rate of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide reached a maximum of 13.26%.

High-speed shearing technology assisted extraction

High-speed shear dispersing emulsifier (HSDE) extraction is another extraction technique, the principle of which is to put the sample on a high-shear dispersion emulsifying machine, so that the sample is subjected to various mechanical forces, such as hydraulic shear, liquid layer friction, impact tearing, centrifugal extrusion and turbulence. This technology can improve the extraction efficiency and results, reduce production costs, and has the characteristics of environmental protection. Du et al.8 extracted Lycium barbarum polysaccharide using high-speed shearing technology and optimized the extraction process by single-factor and orthogonal tests, with the extraction rate as the index, and compared it with other extraction methods. The results showed that the influence of each factor was in the order of shearing time > solid–liquid ratio > shearing rate > extraction time, at the extraction temperature of 60 °C, shearing time of 2 min, shearing rate of 12 000 rpm, and solid–liquid ratio of 1 ∶ 12 (g mL−1). Compared with thermal reflux extraction, enzyme-assisted extraction and ultrasonic-assisted extraction, it not only greatly improves the extraction rate of polysaccharides, but also has a lower extraction temperature, shorter extraction time, protected polysaccharide activity and higher product purity. It is an effective method for polysaccharide extraction.

Ultrasonic-assisted subcritical water extraction method

Subcritical water refers to high-pressure hot water or superheated water, which is still in a liquid state, where the water temperature reaches above 100 °C but below the critical temperature of 374 °C through pressurization.9 With the increase in temperature, the dielectric constant decreases, which correspondingly reduces the polarity of water, thus making water show the same properties as organic solvents.10 In addition, subcritical water can also reduce the surface tension and the strength of solid–liquid two-phase liquid films, thus improving the mass transfer kinetics of the solute and increasing the solubility and diffusion ability of the extract in water.11 On the basis of subcritical water, ultrasonic assistance further enhanced the dissolution and diffusion of plant polysaccharides, and also increased the extraction rate of Lycium barbarum polysaccharides. Zhao et al.12 used ultrasonic-assisted subcritical water extraction to extract Lycium barbarum polysaccharide, and optimized the extraction parameters by single-factor and response surface methods, and compared it with the hot water reflux method, ultrasonic method and subcritical water extraction method. The results showed that the extraction rate of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide by the ultrasonic-assisted subcritical water extraction method was the highest.

Although this new method can obtain a higher extraction rate and shorter extraction time, it is not the first choice in actual extraction processes. The reason may be that the extraction equipment used is not popular in the laboratory, the extraction conditions are difficult to control, and the common methods can meet the experimental needs.

Ultrasonic extraction method

Ultrasonic extraction is one of the most commonly used and cost-saving methods. Compared with hot water extraction, ultrasonic extraction has higher extraction efficiency, milder extraction conditions and more stable extraction results, and does not affect the biological activity of water-soluble polysaccharides, so it is an ideal extraction method for polysaccharides. The cavitation, mechanical effect and thermal effect of ultrasonic waves make the plant cell wall more permeable, accelerate the release, diffusion and dissolution of intracellular polysaccharides, and improve the extraction efficiency. It should be noted that although ultrasonic extraction has been widely applied in polysaccharide extraction, it can cause degradation of the polysaccharides to some extent.13–15 Because ultrasonic power affects the dissolution efficiency of the components, the influence of ultrasonic power on the extraction rate of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide should also be investigated in this method. The effects of temperature, material-liquid ratio, time and ultrasonic power on the extraction rate of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide were discussed. The results showed that the factors affecting the extraction rate were extraction temperature > material-liquid ratio > extraction time > extraction power. On the basis of single-factor experiments, orthogonal experiments were carried out, and the optimal technological conditions were obtained as follows: temperature 75 °C, material–liquid ratio 1 ∶ 20. Two different methods, hot water extraction and ultrasonic extraction, were used to extract the same kind of Lycium barbarum with the same quality, and the extraction technology and extraction rate of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide were studied and compared. The results showed that compared with hot water extraction, the extraction time by the ultrasonic method is shortened by more than 5 times, and the extraction rate is increased by more than 30%. In order to avoid the decomposition of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide, the extraction temperature was not taken as an influencing factor when ultrasonic extraction was used, and there was no heating in the whole extraction process.

The ultrasonic method has been used to extract Lycium barbarum polysaccharide, and the effects of extraction temperature, extraction time, ultrasonic power and material-liquid ratio on the extraction rate were studied. The results showed that the influence of various factors was as follows: material-liquid ratio > ultrasonic power > extraction time > extraction temperature. Through response surface optimization, the optimum extraction process of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide was determined as follows: solid–liquid ratio 1 : 30 g mL−1, extraction temperature 72 °C, ultrasonic power 198 W, extraction time 29 min. A single-factor experiment was carried out, and an orthogonal experiment was used to optimize the extraction process of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide. The orthogonal experiment showed that the order of influence of the four factors on the extraction rate of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide was: material-liquid ratio > extraction time > ultrasonic power > extraction temperature. The optimum extraction process was determined as follows: the ratio of solid to liquid was 1 ∶ 20 (g mL−1), the ultrasonic power was 150 W, the extraction temperature was 70 °C, and the extraction time was 30 min. Under these conditions, the yield of crude polysaccharide was 10.69%. There is a difference between the factors determined (material-liquid ratio > ultrasonic power > extraction time > extraction temperature), which might be due to a series of errors caused by different sources and extraction dosages of Lycium barbarum. The binding force between Lycium barbarum polysaccharide and cell tissue is weak, which weakens the influence of ultrasonic power on the extraction rate but increases the influence of extraction time on the extraction rate. However, the ratio of material to liquid is still the primary factor affecting the extraction rate of Lycium barbarum, while the extraction temperature has the least significant effect on the extraction rate. Lycium barbarum polysaccharide was extracted twice under the conditions of a material-to-liquid ratio of 1 ∶ 4, an extraction temperature of 70 °C, an ultrasonic frequency of 40 kHz and a power of 50 W. The first extraction was performed for 2 h, the second extraction was performed for 1 h, and the two extracts were combined and extracted in parallel three times. The average yield of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide was 2.11%, and the content of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide was 46.62%. Under these conditions, the yield of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide is low, but the content is high.

Microwave-assisted extraction and microwave-ultrasound extraction

The microwave-assisted extraction method uses microwaves to increase the extraction intensity and improve the extraction efficiency. The mechanism of microwave heating includes dipole rotation, ionic conduction, and solvent-dependent microwave absorption. The microwave extraction time is short, the amount of solvent is small, and it is environmentally friendly and efficient. Microwave has the characteristics of fluctuation, heating and high frequency, and is suitable for the extraction of certain components. Microwave extraction combined with ultrasound further enhances the extraction effect. Taking the extraction rate of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide as an index, the conventional water bath extraction method, ultrasonic extraction method, microwave extraction method and ultrasonic-microwave synergistic extraction method were compared. The results showed that the ultrasonic-microwave synergistic extraction method had the highest extraction rate, and the extraction rate order was ultrasonic-microwave synergistic extraction method, conventional water bath extraction method, microwave extraction method, and ultrasonic extraction method, in turn. Judging from the extraction conditions and results, both ultrasonic extraction and microwave-assisted extraction can improve the extraction rate of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide and improve the utilization of medicinal materials, and both effectively shorten the extraction time and make the extraction more efficient. Different from the microwave-assisted extraction method, the ultrasonic extraction method does not require high extraction temperature, which can avoid the destruction of the Lycium barbarum polysaccharide structure at high temperature.

In addition, the use of deep eutectic solvents is also a novel extraction technique for Lycium barbarum polysaccharide.16,17

The function of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide is one of the important aspects to evaluate the quality of Lycium barbarum, and its extraction method needs further study to provide basis for future research. For different kinds of Lycium barbarum, the content and activity of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide are different, and the best extraction process for the same extraction method is also different. According to the analysis of three extraction methods of Lycium barbarum L. in Xinjiang, Lycium barbarum L. in Heiguo and Lycium barbarum L. in Ningxia, the enzyme-assisted extraction method gives the highest extraction rate of Lycium barbarum polysaccharides. For Lycium barbarum L. in Heiguo, the microwave-ultrasonic assisted extraction method has the highest extraction rate of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide; but for Lycium barbarum in Ningxia, the extraction rate of Lycium barbarum by high-speed shearing technology is the highest.

Relationship between structure and activity of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide

The complex chemical structure of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide provides a large number of active groups, which makes it flexible in spatial conformation and allows it to carry rich biological information. This is an important structural basis for Lycium barbarum polysaccharide to exert its multiple biological effects, but it also creates significant obstacles to studying its structure–activity relationship, discovering key structural information, and scientifically verifying the action mechanism. The relative molecular weight distribution, glycosyl composition, glycosidic bond type and adjacent glycosidic bond connection mode, the presence, location and length of branch chains, and non-sugar modifications of polysaccharides may have obvious effects on their activity.

The Lycium barbarum polysaccharides have high structural diversity, but most of them include homogalacturonan (HG), rhamnogalacturonans I and II (RG-I and II), arabinogalactan (Ag) and xylogalacturonan. In addition, Lycium barbarum polysaccharide contains a small amount of glucose (Glu) and mannose (Man), and the structural forms of these trace components are difficult to determine accurately. It can be inferred that these trace components can be embedded in the main chain or branched structure of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide in the form of linear glucan or glucomannan, or mixed in the structure of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide as independent co-extraction components. In addition, the structure of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide mostly has non-sugar modifications (methylation, carboxymethylation, acetylation, etc.), which may be related to its biological activity.18–20

Effect of relative molecular weight of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide on its activity

Relative molecular weight is an important feature of the structure-function relationship of polysaccharides, and appropriate relative molecular weight is the first condition for polysaccharides to show pharmacological activity. In order to exert its efficacy, a polysaccharide must bind to a receptor or protein. Too high relative molecular weight is not conducive to polysaccharides crossing the cell membrane barrier and entering the body to exert their biological activity. In contrast, if the relative molecular weight is too low, it is difficult to form a living polymer structure. Gong et al.21 separated three Lycium barbarum polysaccharide components by fractional alcohol precipitation, among which LBGP-I-3[ Mw = 9.12 × 104] with high relative molecular weight had the most significant enhancement effect on NO, phagocytosis and acid phosphatase of macrophages. Feng et al.22 found that the immunoregulation of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide is directly proportional to its relative molecular weight, and the higher molecular weight component (Mw = 2.77 × 104) is the main source of the immune-regulating activity of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide, which significantly improves the activity of RAW264. 7 macrophage cells, induces cell polarization, and regulates nitric oxide (NO) and tumor necrosis factor in RAW264. 7 cells. The low molecular weight fraction (Mw = 6.99 × 103) did not affect the proliferation and polarization of RAW264. 7 cells. Wang et al.23 also confirmed that the immune activity is closely related to the relative molecular weight of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide. The immunomodulatory activity of LBP1 with a high molecular weight (Mw = 1.207 × 106) is obviously better than that of LBP2 with a low molecular weight (Mw = 1.25 × 105), which may be related to its specific activation of the MAPK signal transduction pathway. In addition, Zhang et al.24 used the ultrafiltration membrane separation method to obtain five Lycium barbarum polysaccharide fractions with different relative molecular weights. LBP-a4 (Mw = 1.02 × 104) with a smaller relative molecular weight could inhibit the proliferation of human hepatocellular carcinoma cells (SMMC-7721) in a dose-dependent manner, and arrest the cells in the G0/G1 phase. However, LBP-p8 (Mw = 6.5 × 106) with a high molecular weight promotes the growth of SMMC-7721. LBP-a4 has a compact spherical structure, while LBP-p8 forms a loose flocculent structure because of its high molecular weight, and the spherical structure is beneficial to the anticancer activity of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide. Deng et al.25 found that the anti-tumor activity of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide was closely related to its relative molecular weight, and four components with different relative molecular weights all had obvious inhibitory effects on mouse hepatoma cells (H22), among which LBP-3 (Mw = 4 × 104 – 3.5 × 105) with medium relative molecular weight was the main active site. It can induce H22 cell apoptosis, mitochondrial membrane potential destruction and S-phase arrest. LBP-3 can significantly reduce tumor quality and induce a systemic and local T cell-dependent anti-tumor immune response in H22 tumor-bearing mice, which may be related to the appropriate relative molecular weight of LBP-3.

The possible reasons for these contradictory results are the differences in source materials, extraction methods and activity evaluation systems.

Effect of the monosaccharide composition of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide on its activity

The biological activity differences of the Lycium barbarum polysaccharide components are mainly caused by differences in monosaccharide structure, and different monosaccharide compositions and proportions can directly affect the physical and chemical properties of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide. According to the presence or absence of uronic acid, polysaccharides can be divided into acidic polysaccharides and neutral polysaccharides. Galacturonic acid is the main acidic polysaccharide of Lycium barbarum polysaccharides, and its content can directly affect the physical and chemical properties of polysaccharides. Too high uronic acid content will prevent polysaccharide molecules from entering the body to exert their biological activities due to the decrease in water solubility and increase in viscosity. Studies have shown that the presence of proper galacturonic acid is beneficial to the protective activity of polysaccharides against chemical liver injury, and its activity is positively correlated with uronic acid content. Lycium barbarum acidic polysaccharide is the main material basis for repairing chemical liver injury. Yao et al.26 found that the antioxidant activity of Lycium barbarum pectin polysaccharide PLBP-II-I was more significant than that of PLBP-I-I, and monosaccharide composition analysis showed that PLBP-II-I had higher galacturonic acid content, and different structures led to great differences in activity. Zhu et al.27 reported that acidic polysaccharide LBP-s-1 had significant hypoglycemic and insulin-sensitizing activities, and further clarified the monosaccharide composition of LBP-s-1. By GC and HPLC analysis, it was found that LBP-s-1 contained seven monosaccharides, of which galacturonic acid had the highest content. Lycium barbarum acidic polysaccharide LFP-1, which contains 0.73% glucuronic acid and 12.67% galacturonic acid, shows strong neurotrophic activity and has a protective effect on MPP + -induced neurotoxicity in a Parkinson's disease model by increasing the activity of PC12 cells and promoting neuronal differentiation. The neutral monosaccharides of Lycium barbarum mainly include arabinose, galactose and glucose, and neutral polysaccharides may be helpful for anti-tumor and hypoglycemic activities. Neutral polysaccharide LBP-1 showed excellent anti-tumor activity. It inhibited the growth of A549 tumor cells, blocked the G0/G1 phase of cells, and regulated the PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling pathway to induce cell apoptosis. LBP-1 is composed of arabinose–galactose–glucose–xylose–mannose, with the molar ratio of 37.53 ∶ 28.08 ∶ 14.72 ∶ 7. Tang et al.28 isolated neutral polysaccharide LBP3b using an ultrafiltration membrane, which showed a hypoglycemic effect by inhibiting glucose uptake, and was composed of mannose–rhamnose–glucose–galactose and xylose, with the molar ratio of the substances being 5.52 ∶ 5.11 ∶ 28.06 ∶ 1.00 ∶ 1.70, covalently linked with a small amount of protein.

Effect of glycosidic linkages on the activity of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide

The good biological activity of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide depends on accurate structural characterization, and the biological activity of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide is closely related to its glycosidic bond. The linkage information can be experimentally obtained, e.g., by methylation analysis, NMR, or enzymatic methods. Wu et al.29 found that the glycosidic bonds of Lycium barbarum polysaccharides collected from different regions in China are highly similar, mainly including 1,3-β-glucuronide bonds, 1,4-α-galacturonic acid bonds and 1,5-α-arabinoside bonds, which provided a basis for the study of the structure–activity relationship between the biological activities of Lycium barbarum polysaccharides and glycosidic bonds. The polygalacturonic acid domain (HG) is an important component in the structure of acidic pectin polysaccharide, which makes a great contribution to the biological activity of the polysaccharide. Xie et al.30 showed that Lycium barbarum polysaccharide with 1,4-α-d-galacturonic acid and 1,5-α-arabinoside bonds, especially the Lycium barbarum polysaccharide with a 1,4-α-d-galacturonic acid backbone, played an important role in immunomodulation. Duan et al.31 compared the immune activities of four homogeneous Lycium barbarum polysaccharides, and found that the polysaccharide activity of 1,4-linked polygalacturonic acid was better than that of 1,6-linked dextran, suggesting that the main chain structure of polygalacturonic acid may be the material basis for the immune promotion of Lycium barbarum polysaccharides. LFP-05S is an acidic polysaccharide component, which is mainly composed of galacturonic acid (60.55%). It is mainly composed of nearly 50% 1,4-α-d-galacturonic acid glycosidic bonds as the “smooth region”, with neutral arabinogalactan and arabinogalactan forming a dense “hair region” along the skeleton. The high content of polygalacturonic acid shows defensive regulation and prolongation under oxidative stress. Rhamnogalacturonan-I (RG-I) is a special domain in pectin polysaccharide, which is a linear skeleton composed of → 2-α-l-rhap-(1 → and → 4)-α-d-GalpA-(1→), and generally has a branch point at the C-4 position of rhamnose. The RG-I domain may be the key to the neurotrophic activity of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide. The pectin polysaccharide LBP1C-2 purified from Lycium barbarum fruit by Zhou et al.32 plays a beneficial role in the symptoms of Alzheimer's disease, and there may be an RG-I linear backbone in LBP1C-2. Lycium barbarum polysaccharide LICP009-3F-2a isolated by Yao et al.26 is beneficial to retinal function, and the repeated fragments composed of RG-I structures and 1,6- and 1,3,6-β-d-galactans are the main reasons for its activity. Arabinogalactan (AGs) is a polysaccharide mainly composed of arabinose and galactose. According to the different skeleton connections, AGs can generally be divided into linear 1,3-β-d- and 1, 6-β-d-galactan chains, which are substituted at C-6 and C-3, respectively. 1,6-β-d-galactan is the main skeleton type of arabinogalactan reported in Lycium barbarum, but there are also many reported polysaccharides with 1,3-β-d-galactan as the main chain, and the active orientation of polysaccharides with different skeleton types is also different. LBP-W purified by Yang et al.33 is a homogeneous polysaccharide with 1,6-β-d-galactan as the main chain, and is composed of α-Araf, β-Galp and α-Rhap residues in the C-3 branch. LBP-W has a significant weight-loss effect without inhibiting the appetite and energy intake of mice fed with a high-fat diet, and at the same time, it improves the intestinal flora. LBP-3, a water-soluble polysaccharide, can decrease the ratio of Aβ42 to Aβ40 in N2a/APP695 cells in a concentration-dependent manner, and protect the nervous system from damage through various routes, which may be related to the fact that the main chain of LBP-3 and the C-6 position are covered by 1,5-α-arabinosyl, 1,6-β-galactosyl, and 1,6-galactosyl.

Effect of branching degree of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide on activity

The pharmacological activity of polysaccharides is mainly related to the branching degree (DB) of the polysaccharide molecular chain, while the number of hydroxyl groups in the molecule and the hydrogen transfer ability of the side chains play key roles. The branching structure of polysaccharides is the recognition site of the host complement system and the activator of macrophages. Peng et al.34 found that compared with the Lycium barbarum polysaccharide galactan skeleton, the arabinan side chain has stronger macrophage activation activity, while a repeated galactan main chain has stronger complement system activation activity. In addition, Huang et al.35 found that the highly branched Lycium barbarum polysaccharide side chain (LBGP70-OL) has stronger anti-aging activity than the linear main chain (LBGP70-OL-I), which may be related to the fact that its side chain contains a large amount of arabinan and arabinogalactan. The side chain and the main chain of the polysaccharide cooperate to protect the cell membrane from oxidative damage and regulate aging-related genes Sirt1, NAMPT and Prx1.

Effect of protein content of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide on its activity

It has been recognized that the bioactive component of Lycium barbarum L. is a polysaccharide–protein complex, and polysaccharides are linked to serine/threonine residues in proteins in the form of a glycoside. It is reported that the anti-aging activity of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide is positively correlated with its protein content. The antioxidant and hypolipidemic activities of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide with a high protein content are higher than those of purified Lycium barbarum polysaccharide, which indicates that the active components, such as nucleic acids and proteins, in Lycium barbarum polysaccharide play a synergistic role with the polysaccharide. A critical treatment should be provided to explain whether the observed activity is attributable to the polysaccharide, protein, or polysaccharide–protein complex. Zhang et al.36 isolated polysaccharide–protein complex LBPF4 and polysaccharide LBPF4-OL. Through the comparative study of their immune activity, it was found that LBPF4 could enhance the functions of T and B lymphocytes and macrophages, while LBPF4-OL could only enhance the functions of B lymphocytes and macrophages. This is because LBPF4 could not only induce the expression of NF-κB in B lymphocytes, but also promote the expression of T lymphocytes.

Effect of chemical modification of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide on its activity

In recent years, chemical modification has become a new approach to elucidating the structure–activity relationship of polysaccharides by changing the chemical structure of Lycium barbarum polysaccharides and purposefully enhancing their activity and targeting. Sulfation, selenization, acetylation and carboxymethylation are common methods for chemical modification of Lycium barbarum polysaccharides, among which sulfation and selenization are the most common. Wang et al.37 think that sulfated Lycium barbarum polysaccharide has more significant immune activity, and its biological activity is mainly related to its sulfate radical and polysaccharide content. The higher the degree of sulfated substitution, the better the proliferation activity of lymphocytes. Sulfated derivatives of LBP1B-S-2 synthesized by Zhou et al.38 can better inhibit the proliferation of human microvascular endothelial cells (HMEC-1) and play an anti-tumor role. Further structural studies show that the sulfated sites of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide occur at the C-3 and C-5 positions of the terminal arabinose and C-2 or C-3 positions of 1,5-α-arabinose. Many studies believe that selenium-modified Lycium barbarum polysaccharide is expected to become a new antioxidant and nutritional supplement. It is found that selenium modification can significantly improve the antioxidant activity of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide in vitro and in vivo, and has a significant hepatoprotective effect. LBP1-SENSPs modified by LBP 1 have a smaller particle size and stronger anti-fatigue activity. Liu et al.39 think that selenium in LBP 1-SENSPs can increase glycogen storage, improve the antioxidant enzyme level and regulate the metabolic mechanism to alleviate fatigue. In addition, the stability, redispersibility and anti-tumor activity of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide selenium nanoparticles have been improved, which have significant inhibitory effects on HepG-2 cells and Caco-2 cells, respectively, and can be used as potential selenium nutritional additives for anti-tumor research.

Effect of the spatial configuration of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide on activity

The pharmacological activity of polysaccharides is closely related to their higher structure, and a change in their spatial conformation directly affects their biological activity. Because some polysaccharides have relatively large molecular weights, complex spatial structures and poor biological activity, degradation is a common method to change the spatial configuration of polysaccharides, thus clarifying the structure–activity relationship of polysaccharides more intuitively. Lin et al.40 found that the anti-platelet aggregation activity of carboxyl-rich Lycium barbarum polysaccharide LBP was enhanced after being degraded by ascorbic acid and hydrogen peroxide, mainly because the negative charge of the degraded polysaccharide was reduced and the disordered curl morphology changed, which promoted the interaction between the polysaccharide and platelets. Further research found that chemical degradation improved the absorption capacity of Lycium barbarum polysaccharide to endogenous minerals. Degradation with ascorbic acid or H2O2 can cause chemical changes in addition to molecular-weight reduction. Therefore, improved activity cannot automatically be attributed solely to a conformational change. Calcium-rich polysaccharide LP5 has strong immunoregulatory activity; however, when endogenous calcium ions are exhausted, the polysaccharide shows better immunoregulatory activity. Conformation analysis confirmed that the polysaccharide structure changed from a dense spherical conformation to a random spiral structure, thus improving the immunoregulatory activity of LP5.41 In addition, the complex of Lycium barbarum glycopeptide and polyphenol has a synergistic effect on the immune activity of the polysaccharide, and the spatial structure analysis shows that the addition of polyphenol does not change the linear structure of Lycium barbarum glycopeptide, but makes the spatial arrangement of the sugar chains more compact.

Anti-tumor mechanism of LBP

Compared with Western medicine, Chinese medicine has the characteristics of multi-component, multi-target and multi-channel treatment of diseases. LBP has a broad anticancer spectrum, such as significant inhibition of many tumor diseases such as liver cancer, lung cancer and cervical cancer.42 It mainly plays an anti-tumor role by inducing tumor cell apoptosis, inhibiting cell proliferation, preventing the cell cycle and regulating immune system function.

Inducing apoptosis

Apoptosis, as a classic form of programmed cell death, is an important physiological mechanism by which the body maintains homeostasis of the internal environment. It is mainly a process in which cells undergo programmed death under the control of internal genes, which can be triggered by endogenous signals such as DNA damage and cell cycle dysregulation, as well as exogenous signals such as cytokines and the cellular environment. When the body is stimulated by signals, the apoptosis pathways are activated (mainly the mitochondrial apoptosis pathway, endoplasmic reticulum stress pathway and external death receptor apoptosis pathway), and then caspases are activated, thus inducing cell apoptosis. Previous studies have shown that LBP can act on multiple targets to induce tumor cell apoptosis,43 mainly including: (1) regulating the balance of apoptosis-related proteins (such as the up-regulation of the ratio of Bax/Bcl-2 and the activation of Caspase-3), and directly starting the apoptosis cascade reaction; (2) inhibiting tumor-promoting signal pathways (such as PI3K/Akt/mTOR, Wnt/β-catenin) and blocking the survival signal of tumor cells; (3) activating tumor suppressor genes (such as p53) and inducing abnormal cell apoptosis; (4) regulating immune-related molecules (such as MHC II, CD80/CD86) and endoplasmic reticulum stress factors (such as IRE1α and GRP78), and inducing apoptosis through immune regulation and stress balance.

Blocking the cell cycle

The runaway of the cell cycle is one of the main causes of tumor formation. The existing research shows that LBP mainly blocks tumor cells in the G0/G1 phase, and plays an anti-tumor role through the targeted regulation of key signal pathways, such as MAPK and Akt. Zhang et al.44 isolated LBP-d and LBP-e from LBP and confirmed that they can block the G0/G1 and S phases of SMMC-7721 cells, respectively, thus playing an anti-tumor role. Gong et al.45 found that LBP can block the cell cycle in the G0/G1 phase and induce apoptosis of many tumor cells by changing mitochondrial function and regulating the MAPK signaling pathway. In addition, LBP can significantly inhibit the proliferation of melanoma B16 cells and induce cell arrest in the G0/G1 phase. Further mechanistic research shows that this effect is related to the activation of the p38-MAPK and Akt signaling pathways mediated by reactive oxygen species, which provides a new perspective for LBP to regulate the tumor cell cycle.

Inhibition of cell proliferation and metastasis

Cell proliferation is strictly regulated by many mechanisms. When the regulatory mechanism is out of control, the degree of cell proliferation directly determines the malignant degree of the tumor. A large number of studies have confirmed that LBP can interfere with the expression level of key proteins by regulating many classical signal pathways (such as the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway, MAPK pathway, FOXO signal pathway, etc.), thus inhibiting the proliferation and metastasis of tumor cells and playing an anti-tumor role. Chen et al.46 transfected miR-365 mimics into human skin squamous cell carcinoma A431 cells, and after giving a high concentration of LBP, it was found that LBP could reverse the proliferation of A431 cells caused by the overexpression of miR-365, and reverse the regulatory effect of miR-365 on the Bcl-2 and Bax proteins, thus promoting cell apoptosis and inhibiting the proliferation of skin squamous cell carcinoma A431 cells. Studies have shown that LBP can down-regulate the expression of glioma-associated oncogene homolog 1 (Gli1), CDK4 and cyclin D1 protein, up-regulate the expression of caspase-3 protein, and inhibit the proliferation of osteosarcoma cells by inhibiting the sonic hedgehog (Shh) signal. In addition, overexpression of miR-365 can promote the proliferation of tumor cells, increase the expression level of anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2, and decrease the expression of pro-apoptotic protein Bax. To sum up, LBP can significantly inhibit cell proliferation and metastasis.

Inhibition of the growth of new blood vessels

The factors affecting angiogenesis mainly include growth factors and the extracellular matrix. Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) is a highly specific key factor to promote angiogenesis, which can regulate tumor angiogenesis. The extracellular matrix can control the differentiation and metastasis of cells by regulating the skeleton or shape of cells.47 Many studies have confirmed that LBP can regulate tumor blood vessels. Overexpression of VEGF can reverse the inhibitory effect of LBP on matrix metalloproteinase-2 (MMP-2) and matrix metalloproteinase-9 (MMP-9), which indicates that LBP can inhibit the migration and invasion of hepatocellular carcinoma cells, and its mechanism may be related to direct inhibition of VEGF. In addition, Li et al.48 found in clinical trials that LBP can down-regulate alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), primary hepatic carcinogen (PHCA), and connective tissue growth factor (CTGF) levels, and inhibit the growth and invasion of liver cancer cells, and thus exhibit anti-tumor activity. To sum up, LBP can inhibit tumors by inhibiting the growth of new blood vessels.

Regulating the immune system

The immune system is an important part of the human defense mechanism. By adjusting the function of the immune system, the body's ability to defend and attack tumors can be enhanced.49 A large number of studies show that LBP can enhance immune function. On the one hand, LBP achieves this by regulating the levels of cytokines, such as interleukins (ILs), tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) and other cytokines in the body, improving the expression of natural killer cell enhancement factor (NKEF), enhancing the function of natural killer cells (NK), and thereby regulating the immune system; on the other hand, it achieves this by adjusting the proportion of macrophages and dendritic cells, lowering the proportion of regulatory T cells (Tregs) in the tumor microenvironment, and inhibiting the secretion of immunosuppressive factors (such as TGF-β and IL-10), which weakens their inhibitory effect on the function of effector T cells (such as CD8 + T, CTL and CD4 + T cells), thus hindering the growth of tumor cells. In addition, recent studies have found that LBP can also interfere with immune checkpoint signals, by down-regulating the expression of programmed death ligand 1/programmed death-1 (PD-L1/PD-1), inhibiting the transmission of inhibitory signals after PD-L1 binds to PD-1 on the surface of T cells, and restoring the anti-tumor activity of T cells, thus enhancing the adaptive immune response.50 To sum up, LBP does not simply enhance immunity, but regulates immune balance through multiple targets.

The studies on the regulation of the PI3K/Akt/mTOR and Wnt/β-catenin pathways by LBP are mature in vitro and in vivo, and the core targets and regulatory mechanisms have been clarified in various tumor animal models. However, the regulation of the VEGF pathway has only been confirmed in disease models, and there is no in-depth study on tumor specificity. Research on the PD-L1/PD-1 pathway in vivo is the weakest; at present, it is mostly indirect speculation, and the regulatory mechanism of the core target is not yet clear. LBP is a kind of heterogeneous polysaccharide complex, and its specific active components, key targets and multi-channel regulation relationship have not been completely analyzed. The multi-target feature is its advantage, but it also brings great difficulties in confirming its clinical efficacy mechanism and standardization, and further research is needed.

Conclusion and perspectives

The extraction methods for different kinds of Lycium barbarum polysaccharides are still at the stage of laboratory research stage. Therefore, the extraction process should be further optimized for industrial production to facilitate the transition from laboratory-scale extraction to industrial production and maximize the market value of Lycium barbarum.

Lycium barbarum L., a traditional Chinese medicinal plant with both food and medicinal uses, has outstanding medicinal value. The anti-tumor potential of LBP, its core active component, has been proven by a large number of studies at home and abroad. It exhibits anti-tumor activity by inducing tumor cell apoptosis and inhibiting proliferation, metastasis and angiogenesis, and can also achieve synergy when combined with radiotherapy and chemotherapy. In the future, it will be necessary to combine systematic pharmacology with precision medicine, clarify the target specificity and synergistic mechanisms of combined medication, and promote the standardized combination of LBP and modern tumor treatment methods. This will accelerate its clinical transformation, and provide a theoretical basis and scientific support for the development and clinical application of LBP-based anti-tumor strategies.

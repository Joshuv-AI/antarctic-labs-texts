A nodally bound-preserving composite discontinuous Galerkin method on polytopic meshes
Abdolreza Amiri, Gabriel R. Barrenechea, Emmanuil H. Georgoulis, Tristan Pryer

Abstract
. We introduce a nodally bound-preserving Galerkin method for second-order elliptic problems on general polygonal/polyhedral, henceforth collectively termed as polytopic , meshes. Starting from an interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin (DG) formulation posed on a polytopic mesh, the method enforces preservation of a priori prescribed upper and lower bounds for the numerical solution at arbitrary number of user-defined points within each polytopic element. This is achieved by employing a simplicial submesh and enforce bound preservation at the submesh nodes via a nonlinear iteration. By construction, the submeshing procedure preserves the order of accuracy of the DG method, without introducing any additional global numerical degrees of freedom compared to the baseline DG method, thereby, falling into the category of composite finite element approaches. A salient feature of the proposed method is that it automatically reverts to the standard DG method on polytopic meshes when no prescribed bound violation occurs. In particular, the choice of the discontinuity-penalisation parameter is independent of the submesh granularity. The resulting composite method combines the geometric flexibility of polytopic meshes with the accuracy and stability of discontinuous Galerkin discretisations, while rigorously guaranteeing bound preservation. The existence and uniqueness of the numerical solution is proven. A priori error bounds, assuming sufficient regularity of the exact solution are shown, employing a non-standard construction of discrete nodally bound-preserving interpolant. Numerical experiments confirm optimal convergence for smooth problems and demonstrate robustness in the presence of sharp gradients, such as boundary and interior layers.



1.
Introduction

High-order discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods are attractive for
approximating partial differential equations because they combine
local conservation, geometric flexibility, and ease of
adaptivity. However, classical DG discretisations typically violate the
discrete maximum principle and may produce large overshoot or undershoots, resulting in non-physical approximations, e.g., not respect positivity properties of the exact solution. Such violation of the expected range/bounds of the exact solution by its numerical approximation may introduce stability challenges in the presence of nonlinearities or coupled systems.

For example, consider a model boundary-value problem involving a reaction–diffusion partial differential equation (PDE) on some computational domain
\Omega
, and suppose its exact solution satisfies the bounds

0\leq u(x)\leq\kappa\quad\text{for almost every }x\in\Omega,

with
\kappa>0
, under suitable assumptions, e.g., on the forcing term and
boundary conditions
[
39
]
. Numerical approximations
u_{h}
of the exact solution
u
by classical
finite element/discontinuous Galerkin methods may not be bound preserving, in
general: even if
u(x)\in[0,\kappa]
, a.e.
x\in\Omega
, the set
\{x\in\Omega:u_{h}(x)\notin[0,\kappa]\}
may have positive
measure. Ciarlet and Raviart shown already in 1973 that the standard conforming finite element
method on simplicial meshes requires stringent mesh conditions to
guarantee a bound-preserving approximation
[
20
]
.

Since negative densities and pressures in Euler’s equations, or
probabilities, concentrations, and phase-field solutions lying outside

[0,1]
, are physically meaningless, positivity-preserving, or more
generally bound-preserving, discretisations have received increasing
attention over the last few decades. Approaches include

[
46
,
9
,
24
,
37
,
34
,
27
,
36
,
5
,
44
,
18
,
32
,
33
]
and the
recent monographs
[
35
]
for hyperbolic and

[
7
]
for elliptic second-order partial differential
equations. A common aspect of most of these methods is that achieving
bound preservation requires nonlinear approaches, either through
limiters or by reformulating the problem as a variational inequality.

Polytopic meshes provide several advantages over simplicial or
quadrilateral grids. Their ability to conform to complicated
geometrical features without excessive mesh granularity
[
12
]
, in conjunction with allowing local mesh coarsening via element agglomeration and, in general, their flexibility in dynamic mesh modification scenarios, make them attractive
for adaptive simulations
[
14
,
16
,
17
,
13
]
. Polytopic methods also admit efficient sweeping strategies for transport-type problems
[
10
]
. By
contrast, literature on positivity-preserving, or in general bound-preserving, discretisations for
polytopic meshes is scarce and usually restricted to low order. For
instance,
[
43
,
41
]
propose hybrid (nonlinear) finite
volume/Virtual Element Methods (VEMs) that are provably positivity-preserving in the low-order setting. In the finite volume
context, there is also a large body of work on positivity-preserving
low-order methods; see
[
25
]
for a review. In the finite
element setting, the only FEM-based scheme we are aware of is

[
28
]
, whereby a Flux-Corrected Transport (FCT) scheme based on a
DG formulation on polytopic meshes is introduced and shown to be
bound-preserving for element mean values (although numerical
experiments are carried out only on simplicial meshes).

The main goal of this work is to design and analyse an arbitrary order, provably
bound-preserving DG method for elliptic problems on polytopic
meshes. Given the allowed element shape generality, the method preserves predetermined bounds on
user-defined
points within each element. This is a crucial development in our view, as in many applications, positivity/bound preservation is crucial within coupled multi-physics simulations where the
solution of a PDE, for instance a density, is passed as input into other model
components. In such settings, it is not necessary for the solution to
be bound-preserving everywhere; it is sufficient to ensure preservation only at the

quadrature points
used for the discretisation of other equations in the model.

The proposed approach starts from the polytopic interior penalty DG
method analysed in
[
15
,
14
]
serving as the baseline discretisation. A crucial aspect for the success of polytopic DG in admitting elements of extreme shape generality is the careful choice of the discontinuity-penalisation parameter
[
15
,
14
]
. Too small penalisation hinders stability. On the other hand, selecting the parameter much larger than needed for stability may restrict approximation quality, when no conforming subspace is available. Then, building on the nodally bound-preserving (NBP) conforming finite element method on simplicial meshes of
[
6
]
, we consider the closed convex set of appropriate discrete functions satisfying the physical bounds at their degrees of
freedom, and define a nonlinear projection onto this set. A discrete problem is
then formulated for the projected object, and a stabilisation term is
added to remove the non-trivial kernel induced by the projection. The kernel stabilisation is carefully constructed to satisfy a key monotonicity property (see Lemma
3.4
below), which allows for well-posedness of the discrete solution. This
strategy was first proposed for (linear
and nonlinear) reaction–diffusion equations
[
6
]
, and has since been extended to convection–diffusion
equations with conforming finite elements
[
1
]
, DG methods

[
8
]
, densities in proton therapy

[
3
]
, and Allen–Cahn problems
[
21
]
,
among others.

To ensure maximum control of the points where bound-preservation is enforced, the method employs simplicial submeshes of the polytopic elements and requires that bounds are preserved at the vertices of each submesh. The choice of submeshes can be freely selected, e.g., to coincide with quadrature nodes, for instance. By construction, the submeshing procedure preserves the order of accuracy of the DG method,
without
introducing any additional global numerical degrees of freedom compared to the baseline polytopic DG method. A critical property of the proposed method is that the choice of the discontinuity-penalisation parameter remains subordinate to polytopic mesh only; in particular, it is independent of the submesh granularity. The aspect ratio between the “coarse” polytopic meshsize and the “fine” submesh size only appears in the definition of the kernel stabilisation term, and has no effect on the stability or approximation properties. The resulting method is
regarded as
composite
in the spirit of

[
31
,
30
]
, due to the presence of the submesh without proliferation of global degrees of freedom.

The well-posedness of the numerical solution is proven via the Brower-Minty Theorem, upon resorting to the key monotonicity property of the kernel stabilisation. Moreover,
a priori
error bounds, assuming sufficient regularity of the exact solution are shown. In contrast to the case of NBP methods on simplicial meshes
[
6
,
1
,
21
]
, whereby the standard interpolants are used to carry out the error analysis, the presence of polytopic meshes and the desire to enforce bound preservation at user-defined nodes poses a new technical challenge: standard (quasy-)interpolants are no longer applicable. To address this
we employ a non-standard construction, inspired from
[
38
,
42
,
33
]
, which results in a NBP interpolant with optimal approximation properties. Optimal
a priori
error bounds are, thus, proven for sufficiently smooth solutions. Moreover, we detail the practical construction of the method on the matrix level to showcase that it is possible to reuse existing implementations, thus preserving conservation and consistency properties of the original method. Finally, a comprehensive series of numerical experiments confirms the optimal convergence for
smooth problems, demonstrate robustness in the presence of sharp gradients, such as boundary
and interior layers, highlighting further the practicality of the proposed method.

To the best of our knowledge, the method presented below is the first rigorous, arbitrary order
positivity/bound-preserving Galerkin for polytopic
meshes. We remark that the construction is new even when restricting to simplicial
meshes: submeshes with vertices positioned at the quadrature nodes of the baseline mesh can be used to able to enforce physical consistency in coupled problems scenarios. The method is also related to the Recovered
Element methodology
[
29
,
23
]
,
which also uses reconstruction and projection techniques to
enhance stability and approximation on general meshes.

The remainder of this manuscript is organised as
follows. Section
2
presents the model problem, preliminary
results, and the baseline IPDG
discretisation. Section
3
introduces the polygonal
NBP method and its well-posedness. Section
4

constructs an interpolation operator that respects the bounds in the
polytopic mesh, which is instrumental for the error analysis carried
out in Section
5
. Implementation details are given in
Section
6
, followed by numerical experiments in
Section
7
that demonstrate the performance of the
method and validate the theory. Conclusions are drawn in
Section
8
.

2.
Model problem and its discretisation by the discontinuous Galerkin method

We adopt standard notation for Sobolev spaces, following

[
26
]
. For a domain
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote by

\|\cdot\|_{0,p,D}
the
L^{p}(D)
-norm, omitting the subscript
p
when

p=2
. For
s\geq 0
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, we write

\|\cdot\|_{s,p,D}
(resp.
|\cdot|_{s,p,D}
) for the norm (resp. seminorm) in
W^{s,p}(D)
, and again drop the subscript
p
when

p=2
. We set

H^{1}_{0}(D)=\{v\in H^{1}(D):v=0\text{ on }\partial D\},

and denote by
H^{-1}(D)
the dual of
H^{1}_{0}(D)
, identifying
L^{2}(D)

with its dual. Writing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{D}
for the duality
pairing, we have

\langle f,v\rangle_{D}=\int_{D}f(\boldsymbol{x})v(\boldsymbol{x})\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},\qquad v\in H^{1}_{0}(D),

whenever
f\in H^{-1}(D)
is sufficiently regular. In the sequel we
do not distinguish between inner products and duality pairings for
scalar- or vector-valued functions.

2.1.
Model problem

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=2,3
) be an open, bounded
polygonal or polyhedral domain with boundary
\partial\Omega
. For
given
f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
, we consider the elliptic problem

(1)

\displaystyle-\operatorname{div}(\mathcal{D}\nabla u)+\mu u

\displaystyle=f

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega,

where
\mu\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
\mu\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
,
and
\mathcal{D}=(d_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{d}\in[L^{\infty}(\Omega)]^{d\times d}
is symmetric and uniformly strictly positive
definite. That is, there exists a constant
\mathcal{D}_{0}>0
such
that

\boldsymbol{y}^{\top}\mathcal{D}(\boldsymbol{x})\boldsymbol{y}\geq\mathcal{D}_{0}\boldsymbol{y}^{\top}\boldsymbol{y},\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\},\quad\text{for a.e.\ }\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega.

It follows from the Lax–Milgram lemma that problem (
1
) admits
a unique weak solution. The analysis presented below extends to more
general settings, such as mixed boundary conditions and first-order
perturbation terms, but for clarity we restrict attention to the model
case (
1
).

We seek discrete solutions that preserve the bounds satisfied by the
weak solution of (
1
). By the maximum and comparison
principles (see, e.g.,
[
39
, Corollary 4.4]
),
for almost all
\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega
the solution
u
satisfies

(2)

-\|f\|_{0,\infty,\Omega}\mu^{-1}\leq u(\boldsymbol{x})\leq\|f\|_{0,\infty,\Omega}\mu^{-1}.

If, in addition,
f\geq 0
in
\Omega
, this bound sharpens to

(3)

0\leq u(\boldsymbol{x})\leq\|f\|_{0,\infty,\Omega}\mu^{-1}.

Motivated by these bounds, we impose the following assumption on the
exact solution throughout this work.

Assumption 2.1

(Bounded solution assumption)
.

The weak solution of (
1
) is assumed to satisfy

(4)

0\leq u(\boldsymbol{x})\leq\kappa,\qquad\text{for almost all }\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega,

where
\kappa>0
is a given constant.

2.2.
Admissible partitions and finite element spaces

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be a subdivision of
\Omega
into disjoint polygonal
elements when
d=2
, or polyhedral elements when
d=3
. We will
collectively refer to these as polytopic elements. For a nonnegative
integer
k
, we denote the set of all polynomials of degree
k
on
K\in{\mathcal{P}}
by
\mathbb{P}_{k}(K)
. For
k\geq 1
, we consider the
elementwise discontinuous space

(5)

\displaystyle V_{\mathcal{P}}:=\{v_{H}\in L^{2}(\Omega):v_{H}|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(K)\ \ \forall K\in{\mathcal{P}}\}.

We denote by
\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}:=\bigcup_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}\partial K
the
skeleton of
{\mathcal{P}}
, and by
\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}^{\rm int}:=\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}\setminus\partial\Omega
its interior part. The mesh function

H_{\mathcal{P}}:\bar{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is defined by

H_{\mathcal{P}}|_{\mathring{K}}:=\mathrm{diam}(K)\qquad\forall K\in{\mathcal{P}}.

We assume that each element
K\in{\mathcal{P}}
can be subdivided into a
finite collection of simplices, denoted
{\mathcal{T}}_{K}
, such that
K=\bigcup_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{K}}T
, and denote
{\mathcal{T}}=\bigcup_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}{\mathcal{T}}_{K}
. No global conformity is required: the local triangulations

{\mathcal{T}}_{K}
may be chosen independently. On each
K\in{\mathcal{P}}
, we define
the local continuous piecewise polynomial space

(6)

\displaystyle W_{K}:=\{v_{h}\in C^{0}(K):v_{h}|_{T}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(T)\ \ \forall T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{K}\},

and set
W_{\mathcal{T}}:=\bigoplus_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}W_{K}
.

A key feature of the proposed method is that the approximate solution
may be piecewise polynomial within each polytopic element
K\in{\mathcal{P}}
in order to enforce bound preservation. Crucially, however, the
number of global degrees of freedom of the method coincides with
\dim V_{\mathcal{P}}
rather than with the generally larger space
W_{\mathcal{T}}
.

We now present assumptions on the admissible partitions and introduce some basic notation.

Assumption 2.2

(Admissible polytopic meshes)
.

Let
({{\mathcal{P}}_{i}})_{i\in I}
be a family of polytopic meshes. We assume:

(a)

Each element
K\in{\mathcal{P}}_{i}
is star-shaped with respect
to a ball of radius
\rho_{K}
, and there exists a global constant

C_{\rm star}>0
such that

H_{K}\leq C_{\rm star}\rho_{K}\qquad\forall K\in{\mathcal{P}}_{i},\ \forall i\in I.

(b)

The family
({{\mathcal{P}}_{i}})_{i\in I}
is locally quasi-uniform,
i.e., there exists a constant
C_{\rm qu}\geq 1
such that

H_{K^{\prime}}\leq C_{\rm qu}H_{K}

for all face neighbours
K^{\prime}
of each
K\in{\mathcal{P}}_{i}
, for all
i\in I
.

Note that Assumption
2.2
(b) implies the equivalence

C_{\rm qu}^{-1}H_{K}\leq H_{K^{\prime}}\leq C_{\rm qu}H_{K}

for all face neighbours
K^{\prime}
of a polytopic element
K\in{\mathcal{P}}
.

The well-posedness and error analysis of the nodally bound-preserving
method also requires the following mild technical assumptions on the
polytopic partition
{\mathcal{P}}
and its sub-triangulation
{\mathcal{T}}
.

Assumption 2.3

(Admissible simplicial submeshes)
.

Let
({{\mathcal{T}}_{j}})_{j\in J}
be a family of simplicial submeshes
obtained as refinements of a given polytopic mesh
{\mathcal{P}}
. We
assume:

(a)

The family
({{\mathcal{T}}_{j}})_{j\in J}
is shape-regular, i.e.,
there exists a global constant
C_{\rm sh}>0
such that

h_{T}\leq C_{\rm sh}\rho_{T}\qquad\forall T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{j},\ \forall j\in J,

where
\rho_{T}
is the inradius of
T
.

(b)

Let
K\in{\mathcal{P}}
and
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{K}
be such that
T

has a facet
f\subset\partial K
. If
K^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{P}}
is the
neighbouring element with
f\subset K\cap K^{\prime}
, then

h_{T}\leq H_{K^{\prime}}.

Assumption
2.3
(a) is standard. Part (b) is a
mild technical condition requiring that the submesh of a polytopic
element is not coarser than its neighbouring polytopic elements.

We recall some standard results used throughout this work. Let
T\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a simplex and
f
one of its facets. The
following trace-inverse inequality holds (see
[
45
]
, and

[
12
]
for extensions):

(7)

\|v_{h}\|_{0,f}^{2}\leq\frac{(k+1)(k+d)|f|}{d|T|}\|v_{h}\|_{0,T}^{2},\qquad\forall v_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(T).

In addition, for every
v\in H^{1}(T)
the local trace inequality holds
(see, e.g.,
[
26
]
):

(8)

\|v\|_{0,f}^{2}\leq\|v\|_{0,T}|v|_{1,T}+\frac{|f|}{|T|}\|v\|_{0,T}^{2}.

We also recall the standard inverse inequality (see, e.g.,

[
40
]
): there exists a constant
C_{\rm inv}>0
, independent of

T
,
k
,
v_{h}
, and
\rho_{T}
, such that

(9)

\|\nabla v_{h}\|_{0,T}^{2}\leq C_{\rm inv}k^{4}\rho_{T}^{-2}\|v_{h}\|_{0,T}^{2},\qquad\forall v_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(T).

For any element
K\in{\mathcal{P}}
, we define its neighbourhood by

\omega_{K}:=\{K^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{P}}:K^{\prime}\text{ shares a facet with }K\}.

2.3.
Interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin method

Let
K_{+}
and
K_{-}
be two adjacent elements of
{\mathcal{P}}
sharing an
interface
F\subset\partial K_{+}\cap\partial K_{-}\subset\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\rm int}
. For elementwise continuous scalar- and
vector-valued functions
v
and
\mathbf{q}
, we define the

average
across
F
by

\{\kern-3.1298pt\{v\}\kern-3.1298pt\}|_{F}:=\tfrac{1}{2}(v_{+}|_{F}+v_{-}|_{F}),\qquad\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathbf{q}\}\kern-3.1298pt\}|_{F}:=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{q}_{+}|_{F}+\mathbf{q}_{-}|_{F}),

where
v_{\pm}|_{F}
denotes the trace of
v
from within
K_{\pm}
on
F
,
and similarly for
\mathbf{q}
. The
jump
across
F
is defined
by

[\![v]\!]|_{F}:=v_{+}\mathbf{n}_{+}+v_{-}\mathbf{n}_{-},\qquad[\![\mathbf{q}]\!]|_{F}:=\mathbf{q}_{+}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{+}+\mathbf{q}_{-}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{-},

with
\mathbf{n}_{\pm}
the outward unit normal to
K_{\pm}
on
F
. On a
boundary face
F\subset\partial\Omega\cap\partial K
we set

\{\kern-3.1298pt\{v\}\kern-3.1298pt\}:=v,\qquad\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathbf{q}\}\kern-3.1298pt\}:=\mathbf{q},\qquad[\![v]\!]:=v\mathbf{n},\qquad[\![\mathbf{q}]\!]:=\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{n},

with
\mathbf{n}
the outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega
.

For brevity, we denote by
\nabla_{{\mathcal{P}}}v
the broken gradient of a
function
v:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
with
v|_{K}\in H^{1}(K)
for all
K\in{\mathcal{P}}
, i.e.,
(\nabla_{{\mathcal{P}}}v)|_{K}=\nabla(v|_{K})
.

The classical interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin (IPDG) method
is: find
u_{H}\in V_{\mathcal{P}}
such that

(10)

a_{DG}(u_{H},v_{H})=\ell(v_{H})\qquad\forall v_{H}\in V_{\mathcal{P}},

with
a_{DG}:(H^{3/2+\epsilon}(\Omega)+V_{\mathcal{P}})\times(H^{3/2+\epsilon}(\Omega)+V_{\mathcal{P}})\to\mathbb{R}
,
\epsilon>0
, defined by

(11)

\displaystyle a_{DG}(u_{H},v_{H}):=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big(\mathcal{D}\nabla_{{\mathcal{P}}}u_{H}\cdot\nabla_{{\mathcal{P}}}v_{H}+\mu u_{H}v_{H}\big)\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}+\int_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}}\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}[\![u_{H}]\!]\cdot[\![v_{H}]\!]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle-\int_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}}\big(\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla u_{H}\}\kern-3.1298pt\}\cdot[\![v_{H}]\!]+\theta\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla v_{H}\}\kern-3.1298pt\}\cdot[\![u_{H}]\!]\big)\mathrm{d}s,

and
\ell(v_{H}):=\langle f,v_{H}\rangle_{\Omega}
. Here
\theta\in[-1,1]
and
\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}:\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is the

penalty function
, whose precise form depends on the geometry of
the polytopic elements. The choice
\theta=1
yields the symmetric
version of IPDG, while
\theta=-1
gives its non-symmetric
counterpart.

The design of suitable penalty functions
\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}
for very
general polytopic or curved elements has been studied extensively; see

[
15
,
14
,
11
,
12
,
22
]
. In this
work, stronger assumptions on admissible meshes are required due to
the two-scale structure of the positivity-preserving method introduced
below.

We define the DG-norm by

\|v_{H}\|_{DG}:=\Big(\|\mathcal{D}^{1/2}\nabla_{{\mathcal{P}}}v_{H}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\mu}v_{H}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}[\![v_{H}]\!]\|_{0,\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2},

for any
v_{H}\in H^{3/2+\epsilon}(\Omega)+V_{\mathcal{P}}
,
\epsilon>0
.
With this norm, we obtain the following coercivity result for
a_{DG}(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Lemma 2.4

(Coercivity of
a_{DG}(\cdot,\cdot)
)
.

Let
F\subset\partial K_{+}\cap\partial K_{-}
be a generic face
shared by two elements
K_{+},K_{-}\in{\mathcal{P}}
. When

F\subset\partial\Omega
, we set
K_{-}=\emptyset
. Defining

(12)

\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}|_{F}:=8C_{\rm star}k(k-1+d)d^{-1}\max_{*\in\{+,-\}}\delta_{K_{*}}H_{K_{*}}^{-1},\quad\textrm{where}\quad\delta_{K_{*}}:=\|{\mathcal{D}}\|_{{0,\infty,K_{*}}}^{2}\|{\mathcal{D}^{-1}}\|_{{0,\infty,K_{*}}}

for every face
F\subset\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}
, we have

(13)

a_{DG}(v,v)\geq\frac{3}{4}|\kern-0.7113pt\|{v}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}\qquad\text{for all }v\in V_{\mathcal{P}}.

Proof.

For
v\in V_{\mathcal{P}}
, we first observe

(14)

a_{DG}(v,v)\geq|\kern-0.7113pt\|{v}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}-2\|{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}^{-1/2}\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla v\}\kern-3.1298pt\}}\|_{{0,\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}}\|{\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}[\![v]\!]}\|_{{0,\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}}.

We focus on interior facets; boundary facets follow as a special
case with
K_{-}=\emptyset
. Each face
F\in\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}
is
partitioned into simplices
\{f\}
so that
\cup_{f\subset F}f=F
. For each simplicial
f
, define
K^{f}_{*}\subset K_{*}
,

*\in\{+,-\}
, as the simplex with face
f
and opposite vertex the
centre of the ball with respect to which
K_{*}
is star-shaped.

Applying the trace-inverse inequality (
7
) on
f
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\|{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}^{-1/2}\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla v\}\kern-3.1298pt\}}\|_{{0,f}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{*\in\{+,-\}}\|{\mathcal{D}|_{K_{*}}}\|_{{0,\infty,f}}^{2}\|{\nabla v|_{K_{*}}}\|_{{0,f}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{*\in\{+,-\}}\|{\mathcal{D}|_{K_{*}}}\|_{{0,\infty,f}}^{2}\frac{k(k-1+d)|f|}{d|K^{f}_{*}|}\|{\nabla v|_{K_{*}}}\|_{{0,K_{*}^{f}}}^{2},

since
\nabla v\in[\mathbb{P}_{k-1}(K_{*})]^{d}
and
\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}
is
constant on each
F\in\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}
(hence also on each
f\subset F
).

By construction, the simplices
K^{f}_{*}
are disjoint and satisfy

\cup_{F\subset\partial K}\cup_{f\subset F}K_{*}^{f}=K_{*}
. Defining

H_{f}^{\perp}:=|K^{f}_{*}|/|f|
as the height of
K^{f}_{*}
with base
f
,
we note that
H_{f}^{\perp}\geq\rho_{K}
. Thus,

\frac{|f|}{|K^{f}_{*}|}\leq\rho_{K}^{-1}\leq C_{\rm star}H_{K_{*}}^{-1}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}^{-1/2}\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla v\}\kern-3.1298pt\}}\|_{{0,\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\rm star}}{2}\sum_{F\subset\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}|_{F}\sum_{f\subset F}\sum_{*\in\{+,-\}}\|{\mathcal{D}|_{K_{*}}}\|_{{0,\infty,f}}^{2}\|{\mathcal{D}^{-1}}\|_{{0,\infty,K_{*}}}\frac{k(k-1+d)}{dH_{K_{*}}}\|{\mathcal{D}^{1/2}\nabla v}\|_{{0,K_{*}^{f}}}^{2}.

Selecting
\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}
as in (
12
), and recalling

\cup_{F\subset\partial K_{*}}\cup_{f\subset F}K_{*}^{f}=K_{*}
, we
obtain

(15)

\|{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}^{-1/2}\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla v\}\kern-3.1298pt\}}\|_{{0,\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}}^{2}\leq 16^{-1}\|{\mathcal{D}^{1/2}\nabla_{\kern-1.42271pt{\mathcal{P}}}v}\|_{{0,\Omega}}^{2},

which, combined with (
14
), gives the result.
∎

Note that the above (classical) IPDG method on polytopic meshes uses
the same number of elemental basis functions on each element,
regardless of its shape. This is achieved by defining the local basis
functions directly as restrictions of polynomials in the physical
space, rather than through element mappings. For details on the
implementation of this approach, see
[
14
]
.

3.
A nodally bound-preserving composite discontinuous Galerkin method

In this section we propose a method that enforces bound preservation
on nodal basis functions subordinate to the simplicial submesh
{\mathcal{T}}

defined above. We begin by noting that the cardinality of the

k
th-order Lagrange basis (and nodes) in
d
dimensions for a simplex
is
m_{k,d}={k+d\choose d}
. For each simplex
T\subset K
, with

K\in{\mathcal{P}}
, we denote the Lagrange basis functions and nodes by

\{\phi_{i}^{T}\}_{i=1}^{m_{k,d}}
and
\{\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{T}\}_{i=1}^{m_{k,d}}
,
respectively.

Defining
C(K)
as the space of continuous functions on
K
, we
introduce the elemental nodally bound-preserving recovery operator

\mathcal{E}_{K}^{+}:C(K)\to W_{K}^{+}
by

(16)

\mathcal{E}_{K}^{+}(v)=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{K}}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{k,d}}\max\!\big\{0,\min\{v(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{T}),\kappa\}\big\}\phi_{i}^{T}.

By construction,
\mathcal{E}_{K}^{+}(v)\in W_{K}^{+}
, where

(17)

\displaystyle W_{K}^{+}:=\{v\in W_{K}:v(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{T})\in[0,\kappa],\ \ i=1,\ldots,m_{k,d},\ T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{K}\}.

Thus,
\mathcal{E}_{K}^{+}(v)
lies within the prescribed range

[0,\kappa]
at every nodal point
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{T}
.

The global nodally bound-preserving recovery operator

\mathcal{E}^{+}:C(\Omega)\to W_{{\mathcal{T}}}
is then defined elementwise by

(\mathcal{E}^{+}(v))|_{K}:=\mathcal{E}^{+}_{K}(v|_{K}),\quad K\in{\mathcal{P}},

and for notational convenience we set

(18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{-}_{K}(v_{H}):=v_{H}|_{K}-\mathcal{E}_{K}^{+}(v_{H}),\qquad\mathcal{E}^{-}(v_{H}):=v_{H}-\mathcal{E}^{+}(v_{H}).

Remark 3.1

(Properties of the recovery operator)
.

The operator
\mathcal{E}^{+}
acts as the identity whenever all nodal
values lie in
[0,\kappa]
. Specifically, if
v_{H}\in V_{\mathcal{P}}
satisfies

v_{H}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{T})\in[0,\kappa]
for all
i=1,\ldots,m_{k,d}
and

T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{K}
, then
\mathcal{E}_{K}^{+}(v_{H})=v_{H}
on
K
. Consequently,
if this condition holds for every
K\in{\mathcal{P}}
, we have

\mathcal{E}^{+}(v_{H})=v_{H}
. Conversely, if at least one nodal value lies
outside
[0,\kappa]
, then
\mathcal{E}_{K}^{+}(v_{H})\neq v_{H}
on that
element. Finally, if
v_{H}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{T})<0
for all nodes of some

K\in{\mathcal{P}}
, then
\mathcal{E}_{K}^{+}(v_{H})=0
, so the nonlinear map

\mathcal{E}^{+}
has a non-trivial kernel.

To mitigate the effect of the non-trivial kernel, we introduce a

stabilising
bilinear form. For
w_{h},v_{h}\in W_{{\mathcal{T}}}
we set

(19)

s(w_{h},v_{h})=\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}\alpha\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{K}}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{k,d}}\big(\mathcal{D}_{\omega_{K}}h_{T}^{d-2}+\mu_{T}h_{T}^{d}\big)w_{h}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{T})v_{h}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{T}),

where
h_{T}:=\operatorname{diam}(T)
and
\mu_{T}:=\|\mu\|_{0,\infty,T}
for

T\in{\mathcal{T}}
, while

\mathcal{D}_{\omega_{K}}:=\|\mathcal{D}\|_{0,\infty,\omega_{K}}
for each

K\in{\mathcal{P}}
. Here
\alpha
denotes a piecewise constant function with

\alpha|_{K}:=\alpha_{K}>0
,
K\in{\mathcal{P}}
, to be specified below. The
bilinear form
s(\cdot,\cdot)
induces the norm

\|v_{h}\|_{s}:=\sqrt{s(v_{h},v_{h})}
on
W_{\mathcal{T}}
.

We are now in a position to introduce the composite
bound-preserving discontinuous Galerkin method, which reads: find

u_{H}\in V_{\mathcal{P}}
such that

(20)

a_{h}(u_{H};v_{H})=\ell(v_{H})\qquad\forall v_{H}\in V_{\mathcal{P}},

with semilinear form

(21)

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{H};v_{H}):=a_{DG}(\mathcal{E}^{+}(u_{H}),v_{H})+s(\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_{H}),v_{H}),

Remark 3.2

(Properties of the bound-preserving DG method)
.

Some observations on the method (
20
) are in order.

(1)

If
u_{H}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{T})\in[0,\kappa]
for all
i=1,\dots,m_{k,d}
and

T\in{\mathcal{T}}
, then (
20
) reduces to the classical interior
penalty discontinuous Galerkin method (
10
) with solution

u_{H}\in V_{\mathcal{P}}
, i.e. the nonlinearity in the first argument of

a_{h}(\cdot;\cdot)
vanishes.

(2)

The stabilisation term
s(\cdot,\cdot)
is active only on
polytopic elements
K
where
u_{H}
violates the prescribed bounds on
the numerical solution. Moreover, whenever

0<u_{H}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{T})<\kappa
, we have

\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_{H})(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{T})=0
; see
[
1
, Remark 3.2]

for the proof in the conforming finite element case, which carries
over directly here.

(3)

The system to be solved in (
20
) has size

\dim(V_{\mathcal{P}})\times\dim(V_{\mathcal{P}})
. Hence, the number of degrees of
freedom is
independent of the submesh
{\mathcal{T}}
.

3.1.
Well-posedness

We now discuss existence and uniqueness for (
20
). We begin by
showing that
s(\cdot,\cdot)
controls the kernel of the projection

\mathcal{E}^{+}(\cdot)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists a constant
C_{\rm equiv}>0
, depending only on

C_{\rm star}
,
C_{\rm sh}
,
k
, and the spatial dimension
d
, such
that for every
v_{h}\in W_{\mathcal{T}}
,

(22)

\displaystyle|\kern-0.7113pt\|{v_{h}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}\leq C_{\rm equiv}\|\alpha^{-1/2}v_{h}\|_{s}^{2}.

Proof.

Recall that the number of Lagrange basis functions (and nodes) of
degree
k
in
d
dimensions is
m_{k,d}=\binom{k+d}{d}
. For a simplex

T\subset K
,
K\in{\mathcal{P}}
, denote the Lagrange basis and nodes by

\{\phi_{i}^{T}\}_{i=1}^{m_{k,d}}
and
\{\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{T}\}_{i=1}^{m_{k,d}}
,
respectively. For an affine map
F_{T}:\hat{T}\to T
from a reference
simplex
\hat{T}
, let
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m_{k,d}}
be the corresponding
reference Lagrange basis so that
\phi_{i}^{T}\circ F_{T}=\psi_{i}
. Then

\displaystyle\|v_{h}\|_{0,T}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq m_{k,d}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{k,d}}v_{h}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{T})\int_{T}\big(\phi_{i}^{T}(\boldsymbol{x})\big)^{2}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle\leq m_{k,d}\max_{1\leq i\leq m_{k,d}}\|\phi_{i}^{T}\|_{0,\infty,T}^{2}|T|\sum_{i=1}^{m_{k,d}}v_{h}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{T})=C_{k,d}^{L^{2}}|T|\sum_{i=1}^{m_{k,d}}v_{h}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{T}),

with
C_{k,d}^{L^{2}}:=m_{k,d}\max_{1\leq i\leq m_{k,d}}\|\psi_{i}\|_{0,\infty,\hat{T}}^{2}
,
since
\|\psi_{i}\|_{0,\infty,\hat{T}}=\|\phi_{i}^{T}\|_{0,\infty,T}
.

Using this bound together with the inverse inequality
(
9
) yields

\|\mathcal{D}^{1/2}\nabla v_{h}\|_{0,T}^{2}\leq C_{\rm inv}k^{4}\|\mathcal{D}\|_{0,\infty,T}\rho_{T}^{-2}\|v_{h}\|_{0,T}^{2}\leq C_{\rm inv}C_{k,d}^{L^{2}}k^{4}\|\mathcal{D}\|_{0,\infty,T}\rho_{T}^{-2}|T|\sum_{i=1}^{m_{k,d}}v_{h}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{T}).

Since
|T|\leq h_{T}^{d}
and, by Assumption
2.3
, the
shape-regularity gives
|T|\leq C_{\rm sh}^{2}\rho_{T}^{2}h_{T}^{d-2}
, we conclude

(23)

\|\mathcal{D}^{1/2}\nabla v_{h}\|_{0,T}^{2}\leq C_{k,d}^{H^{1}}\|\mathcal{D}\|_{0,\infty,T}h_{T}^{d-2}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{k,d}}v_{h}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{T}),

with

C_{k,d}^{H^{1}}:=C_{\rm inv}C_{\rm sh}^{2}C_{k,d}^{L^{2}}k^{4}.

Next, let
f\subset\partial T\cap\partial K
be a face of the
simplicial subelement
T
contained in the face
F
of the polytopic
element
K
containing
T
. Then

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}v_{h}\|_{0,f}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq m_{k,d}\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{k,d}}v_{h}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{T})\int_{f}\big(\phi_{i}^{T}(\boldsymbol{x})\big)^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq 4C_{\rm star}m_{k,d}\max_{1\leq i\leq m_{k,d}}\|\psi_{i}\|_{0,\infty,\hat{T}}\frac{k(k-1+d)}{d}\|\mathcal{D}\|_{0,\infty,K_{-}\cup K_{+}}\big(\min_{*\in\{-,+\}}H_{K_{*}}\big)^{-1}|f|\sum_{i=1}^{m_{k,d}}v_{h}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{T}),

where
f\subset F\subset K_{-}\cap K_{+}
, and we have only retained the
nodes/basis functions that are nontrivial on
f
, which is a

(d-1)
-simplex (hence the adapted numbering). By
Assumption
2.3
(b),
h_{T}\leq\min_{*\in\{-,+\}}H_{K_{*}}
, and since
|f|\leq h_{T}^{d-1}
we obtain

(24)

\|\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}v_{h}\|_{0,f}^{2}\leq C_{k,d}^{\sigma}\|\mathcal{D}\|_{0,\infty,K_{-}\cup K_{+}}h_{T}^{d-2}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{k,d}}v_{h}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{T}),

with

C_{k,d}^{\sigma}:=\frac{4}{d}C_{\rm star}m_{k,d}k(k-1+d)\max_{1\leq i\leq m_{k,d}}\|\psi_{i}\|_{0,\infty,\hat{T}}.

For implementation purposes we now bound each contribution in terms of
the stabilisation weights. Recalling the notation

\mathcal{D}_{\omega_{K}}
and
\mu_{T}
from the definition of

s(\cdot,\cdot)
, combining the above estimates yields

|\kern-0.7113pt\|{v_{h}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}\leq C_{\rm equiv}\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{K}}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{k,d}}\big(\mathcal{D}_{\omega_{K}}h_{T}^{d-2}+\mu_{T}h_{T}^{d}\big)v_{h}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{T}),

with
C_{\rm equiv}:=\max\{C_{k,d}^{H^{1}},2C_{k,d}^{\sigma}\}
(note
that
C_{k,d}^{L^{2}}\leq C_{k,d}^{H^{1}}
). The claim then follows from
the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{s}
.
∎

The next step towards proving well-posedness is the following
monotonicity result, whose proof is identical to that of

[
6
, Lemma 3.1]
and is therefore omitted for brevity.

Lemma 3.4
.

The bilinear form
s(\cdot,\cdot)
, defined in (
19
), satisfies

(25)

\displaystyle s(\mathcal{E}^{-}(v_{H})-\mathcal{E}^{-}(w_{H}),\mathcal{E}^{+}(v_{H})-\mathcal{E}^{+}(w_{H}))

\displaystyle\geq 0\qquad\forall v_{H},w_{H}\in V_{\mathcal{P}},

(26)

\displaystyle s(\mathcal{E}^{-}(v_{H}),w_{h}-\mathcal{E}^{+}(v_{H}))

\displaystyle\leq 0\qquad\forall v_{H}\in V_{\mathcal{P}},\ w_{h}\in W_{K}^{+},\ K\in{\mathcal{P}}.

We now establish coercivity and continuity of the stabilised
semilinear form
a_{h}(\cdot;\cdot)
for a specific choice of trial and
test functions, which will be needed below. A crucial point in the
proof is that the discontinuity-penalisation parameter
\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}
,
defined in (
12
), is
sufficient
to guarantee
stability even though the trial functions may be polynomials on the
submesh
{\mathcal{T}}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
v_{H},w_{H}\in V_{\mathcal{P}}
, and set

r_{h}^{\pm}:=\mathcal{E}^{\pm}(v_{H})-\mathcal{E}^{\pm}(w_{H})
for brevity.
Define

{\mathcal{T}}_{K}^{\partial}:=\{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{K}:\ \exists\text{ face }f\subset\partial T\cap\partial K\}

as the set of simplices
T
in
K
touching the boundary of
K
.
Select
\alpha>0
in (
19
) as

(27)

\alpha|_{K}:=\gamma\max\Big\{1,\frac{H_{K}}{8C_{\rm star}}\max_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{K}^{\partial}}\frac{|f|}{|T|}\Big\},\quad K\in{\mathcal{P}},

with
\gamma\geq 25C_{\rm equiv}
. Then

(28)

\displaystyle a_{DG}(r_{h}^{+},z_{H})+s(r_{h}^{-},z_{H})\leq\frac{7}{4}\Big(|\kern-0.7113pt\|{r_{h}^{+}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}+\|{r_{h}^{-}}\|_{{s}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(|\kern-0.7113pt\|{z_{H}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}+\|{z_{H}}\|_{{s}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2},

for any
z_{H}\in V_{\mathcal{P}}
.

Proof.

From standard estimates, we obtain

(29)

a_{DG}(r_{h}^{+},z_{H})\leq|\kern-0.7113pt\|{r_{h}^{+}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|{z_{H}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}-\int_{\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla r_{h}^{+}\}\kern-3.1298pt\}\cdot[\![z_{H}]\!]\,\mathrm{d}s-\int_{\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla z_{H}\}\kern-3.1298pt\}\cdot[\![r_{h}^{+}]\!]\,\mathrm{d}s.

We now estimate the second and third terms on the right-hand side.
For the last term, applying Cauchy–Schwarz together with
(
15
) gives

(30)

\Big|\int_{\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla z_{H}\}\kern-3.1298pt\}\cdot[\![r_{h}^{+}]\!]\,\mathrm{d}s\Big|\leq 4^{-1}\|{\mathcal{D}^{1/2}\nabla_{\kern-1.42271pt{\mathcal{P}}}z_{H}}\|_{{0,\Omega}}\|{\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}[\![r_{h}^{+}]\!]}\|_{{0,\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}}.

For the remaining term, we decompose as

(31)

\int_{\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla r_{h}^{+}\}\kern-3.1298pt\}\cdot[\![z_{H}]\!]\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla(v_{H}-w_{H})\}\kern-3.1298pt\}\cdot[\![z_{H}]\!]\,\mathrm{d}s-\int_{\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla r_{h}^{-}\}\kern-3.1298pt\}\cdot[\![z_{H}]\!]\,\mathrm{d}s=:(I)-(II),

since
r_{h}^{+}+r_{h}^{-}=v_{H}-w_{H}
.

For
(I)
, Cauchy–Schwarz and (
15
) yield

\displaystyle|(I)|\leq

\displaystyle 4^{-1}\|{\mathcal{D}^{1/2}\nabla_{\kern-1.42271pt{\mathcal{P}}}(v_{H}-w_{H})}\|_{{0,\Omega}}\|{\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}[\![z_{H}]\!]}\|_{{0,\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4^{-1}\big(\|{\mathcal{D}^{1/2}\nabla_{\kern-1.42271pt{\mathcal{P}}}r_{h}^{+}}\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|{\mathcal{D}^{1/2}\nabla_{\kern-1.42271pt{\mathcal{P}}}r_{h}^{-}}\|_{{0,\Omega}}\big)\|{\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}[\![z_{H}]\!]}\|_{{0,\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}}.

For
(II)
, applying a trace–inverse inequality to every

T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{K}^{\partial}
,
K\in{\mathcal{P}}
, gives

\displaystyle|(II)|\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\subset\partial T\cap\partial K\\
T\in\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\partial}\end{subarray}}\|{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}^{-1/2}\mathcal{D}\nabla r_{h}^{-}|_{T}}\|_{{0,f}}\|{\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}[\![z_{H}]\!]}\|_{{0,f}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\partial}\end{subarray}}\frac{\delta_{K}|f|}{8C_{\rm star}|T|}\min_{*\in\{+,-\}}H_{K_{*}}\delta_{K_{*}}^{-1}\|{\mathcal{D}^{1/2}\nabla r_{h}^{-}}\|_{{0,T}}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\|{\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}[\![z_{H}]\!]}\|_{{0,\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}}.

Since
\min_{*\in\{+,-\}}H_{K_{*}}\delta_{K_{*}}^{-1}\leq H_{K}\delta_{K}^{-1}
(as one of the
K_{*}
is
K
itself), and

\alpha|_{K}=\gamma\max\Big\{1,\frac{H_{K}}{8C_{\rm star}}\max_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{K}^{\partial}}\frac{|f|}{|T|}\Big\},

we deduce

(32)

\displaystyle|(II)|\leq

\displaystyle\ \gamma^{-1/2}\bigg(\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\partial}}\|{\sqrt{\alpha}\mathcal{D}^{1/2}\nabla r_{h}^{-}}\|_{{0,T}}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\|{\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}[\![z_{H}]\!]}\|_{{0,\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \gamma^{-1/2}\|{\sqrt{\alpha}\mathcal{D}^{1/2}\nabla_{\kern-1.42271pt{\mathcal{P}}}r_{h}^{-}}\|_{{0,\Omega}}\|{\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}[\![z_{H}]\!]}\|_{{0,\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}}.

Returning to (
31
), the above bounds and Lemma

3.3
, with
r_{h}^{-}\in W_{\mathcal{T}}
and
\sqrt{\alpha}r_{h}^{-}\in W_{\mathcal{T}}
, imply

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla r_{h}^{+}\}\kern-3.1298pt\}\cdot[\![z_{H}]\!]\,\mathrm{d}s\Big|\leq

\displaystyle 4^{-1}\Big(\|{\mathcal{D}^{1/2}\nabla_{\kern-1.42271pt{\mathcal{P}}}r_{h}^{+}}\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|{\mathcal{D}^{1/2}\nabla_{\kern-1.42271pt{\mathcal{P}}}r_{h}^{-}}\|_{{0,\Omega}}+4\gamma^{-1/2}\|{\sqrt{\alpha}\mathcal{D}^{1/2}\nabla_{\kern-1.42271pt{\mathcal{P}}}r_{h}^{-}}\|_{{0,\Omega}}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\|{\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}[\![z_{H}]\!]}\|_{{0,\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\Big(\|{\mathcal{D}^{1/2}\nabla_{\kern-1.42271pt{\mathcal{P}}}r_{h}^{+}}\|_{{0,\Omega}}^{2}+25C_{\rm equiv}\gamma^{-1}\|{r_{h}^{-}}\|_{{s}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\|{\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}[\![z_{H}]\!]}\|_{{0,\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}}.

Since
\gamma\geq 25C_{\rm equiv}
, we conclude

(33)

\Big|\int_{\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla r_{h}^{+}\}\kern-3.1298pt\}\cdot[\![z_{H}]\!]\,\mathrm{d}s\Big|\leq\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\big(\|{\mathcal{D}^{1/2}\nabla_{\kern-1.42271pt{\mathcal{P}}}r_{h}^{+}}\|_{{0,\Omega}}^{2}+\|{r_{h}^{-}}\|_{{s}}^{2}\big)^{1/2}\|{\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}[\![z_{H}]\!]}\|_{{0,\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}}.

Finally, substituting (
30
) and (
33
)
into (
29
), and using
\sqrt{2}\leq 2
, yields
(
28
).
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

With the assumptions and notation of Lemma
3.5
, we also have

(34)

\displaystyle a_{DG}(r_{h}^{+},v_{H}-w_{H})+s(r_{h}^{-},v_{H}-w_{H})\geq\frac{1}{2}\big(|\kern-0.7113pt\|{v_{H}-w_{H}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}+\|r_{h}^{-}\|_{s}^{2}\big).

Proof.

Since
v_{H}-w_{H}=r_{h}^{+}+r_{h}^{-}
, we obtain

(35)

\displaystyle a_{DG}(r_{h}^{+},v_{H}-w_{H})+s(r_{h}^{-},v_{H}-w_{H})\geq a_{DG}(v_{H}-w_{H},v_{H}-w_{H})-a_{DG}(r_{h}^{-},v_{H}-w_{H})+s(r_{h}^{-},r_{h}^{-}),

using
s(r_{h}^{-},r_{h}^{+})\geq 0
from (
25
).
Since
v_{H}-w_{H}\in V_{\mathcal{P}}
, Lemma
2.4
yields

(36)

a_{DG}(v_{H}-w_{H},v_{H}-w_{H})\geq\frac{3}{4}|\kern-0.7113pt\|{v_{H}-w_{H}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}.

Next, arguing exactly as in the proofs of (
30
) and
(
32
), we have

\Big|\int_{\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla(v_{H}-w_{H})\}\kern-3.1298pt\}\cdot[\![r_{h}^{-}]\!]\mathrm{d}s\Big|\leq 4^{-1}\|\mathcal{D}^{1/2}\nabla_{\kern-1.42271pt{\mathcal{P}}}(v_{H}-w_{H})\|_{0,\Omega}\|\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}[\![r_{h}^{-}]\!]\|_{0,\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}},

and

\Big|\int_{\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla r_{h}^{-}\}\kern-3.1298pt\}\cdot[\![v_{H}-w_{H}]\!]\mathrm{d}s\Big|\leq\gamma^{-1/2}\|\sqrt{\alpha}\mathcal{D}^{1/2}\nabla_{\kern-1.42271pt{\mathcal{P}}}r_{h}^{-}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}[\![v_{H}-w_{H}]\!]\|_{0,\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}.

Applying Lemma
3.3
to
\sqrt{\alpha}r_{h}^{-}\in W_{\mathcal{T}}
gives

\gamma^{-1/2}\|\sqrt{\alpha}\mathcal{D}^{1/2}\nabla_{\kern-1.42271pt{\mathcal{P}}}r_{h}^{-}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq 4^{-1}\|r_{h}^{-}\|_{s},

for the stated choices of
\alpha
and
\gamma
. Using these three
estimates together with the trivial bounds
\|\mathcal{D}^{1/2}\nabla_{\kern-1.42271pt{\mathcal{P}}}v\|_{0,\Omega}\leq|\kern-0.7113pt\|{v}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}
and

\|\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}[\![v]\!]\|_{0,\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}\leq|\kern-0.7113pt\|{v}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}
for
any
v\in W_{\mathcal{T}}
, we obtain

(37)

|a_{DG}(r_{h}^{-},v_{H}-w_{H})|\leq\frac{5}{4}|\kern-0.7113pt\|{r_{h}^{-}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|{v_{H}-w_{H}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}+\frac{1}{4}\|r_{h}^{-}\|_{s}|\kern-0.7113pt\|{v_{H}-w_{H}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}.

Moreover, Lemma
3.3
applied to
r_{h}^{-}\in W_{\mathcal{T}}
yields

(38)

16|\kern-0.7113pt\|{r_{h}^{-}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}\leq 16C_{\rm equiv}\|\alpha^{-1/2}r_{h}^{-}\|_{s}^{2}\leq\|r_{h}^{-}\|_{s}^{2},

since
\alpha^{-1}\leq\gamma^{-1}\leq(16C_{\rm equiv})^{-1}
by
construction. Hence, from (
37
) we conclude that

|a_{DG}(r_{h}^{-},v_{H}-w_{H})|\leq\frac{21}{64}\|r_{h}^{-}\|_{s}|\kern-0.7113pt\|{v_{H}-w_{H}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}<\frac{1}{4}\|r_{h}^{-}\|_{s}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|\kern-0.7113pt\|{v_{H}-w_{H}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}.

Substituting this into (
35
), together with
(
36
), establishes (
34
).
∎

The last two lemmas are sufficient to apply the theory of monotone
operators, thereby establishing existence and uniqueness of the
solution to (
21
). In particular, we have the following
result.

Theorem 3.7

(Well-posedness)
.

Let
T:V_{\mathcal{P}}\rightarrow[V_{\mathcal{P}}]^{\prime}
be defined by

[Tu_{H},v_{H}]:=a_{h}(u_{H};v_{H})=a_{DG}(\mathcal{E}^{+}(u_{H}),v_{H})+s(\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_{H}),v_{H}),\hskip 28.45274ptv_{H}\in V_{\mathcal{P}}.

Then, under the hypotheses of Lemma
3.5
,
T
is
continuous and strongly monotone. Consequently, the problem
(
21
) has a unique solution
u_{H}\in V_{\mathcal{P}}
.

Proof.

To show that
T
is continuous, we recall (
38
) and use
(
28
), which gives

[Tv_{H}-Tw_{H},v_{H}-w_{H}]\leq\frac{7}{4}\Big(|\kern-0.7113pt\|{\mathcal{E}^{+}(v_{H})-\mathcal{E}^{+}(w_{H})}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}+\frac{26}{25}\|{\mathcal{E}^{-}(v_{H})-\mathcal{E}^{-}(w_{H})}\|_{{s}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(|\kern-0.7113pt\|{z_{H}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}+\|{z_{H}}\|_{{s}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

Thus,
T
is continuous on
V_{\mathcal{P}}
.

Moreover, (
34
) implies

[Tv_{H}-Tw_{H},v_{H}-w_{H}]\geq\frac{1}{2}\big(|\kern-0.7113pt\|{v_{H}-w_{H}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}+\|{\mathcal{E}^{-}(v_{H})-\mathcal{E}^{-}(w_{H})}\|_{{s}}^{2}\big),

showing that
T
is strongly monotone on
V_{\mathcal{P}}
. Existence and
uniqueness therefore follow from the Browder-Minty Theorem (see,
e.g.,
[
39
, Theorem 10.41]
) together with
strong monotonicity.
∎

Remark 3.8

(Consistency of the method)
.

Suppose
u
, the solution of (
1
), belongs to

H^{3/2+\epsilon}(\Omega)
,
\epsilon>0
, with
0\leq u\leq\kappa
in

\Omega
. Then (
10
) is consistent in the sense that

a_{DG}(u,v_{H})=\ell(v_{H}),

for all
v_{H}\in V_{\mathcal{P}}
. Combining this identity with (
20
)
yields the following orthogonality relation for the bound-preserving
method (
20
):

(39)

a_{DG}(u,v_{H})=\ell(v_{H})=a_{DG}(\mathcal{E}^{+}(u_{H}),v_{H})+s(\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_{H}),v_{H}),

for all
v_{H}\in V_{\mathcal{P}}
.

4.
Bound-preserving best approximation

The error analysis requires an optimal approximation of
u
in

V_{\mathcal{P}}
that is nodally bound-preserving at all nodes of

{\mathcal{T}}
. Since the degrees of freedom of
V_{\mathcal{P}}
are determined by
the polytopic elements, rather than by the submesh
{\mathcal{T}}
, a
specialised construction is needed, which we detail in this
section. The construction relies on the following (mild) assumption on
the partitions.

Assumption 4.1

(Simplicial covering of polytopic meshes)
.

For each member
{\mathcal{P}}_{i}
of a family of polytopic meshes

({\mathcal{P}}_{i})_{i\in I}
, we assume there exists a covering
{\mathcal{P}}^{\sharp}_{i}=\{{K^{\sharp}}\}
, associated with the polytopic mesh
{\mathcal{P}}
, consisting of
open, shape-regular
d
-simplices
{K^{\sharp}}
of minimal possible diameter

H_{{K^{\sharp}}}
, such that:

(a)

for each
K\in{\mathcal{P}}_{i}
, there exists a fixed
{K^{\sharp}}\in{\mathcal{P}}^{\sharp}_{i}
such that
K\subset{K^{\sharp}}
;

(b)

there exists a global constant
C_{\rm sh}^{\sharp}\geq 1
such that
H_{{K^{\sharp}}}\leq C_{\rm sh}^{\sharp}H_{K}
, for all
K\in{\mathcal{P}}_{i}
,
i\in I
;

(c)

each
{\mathcal{P}}^{\sharp}_{i}
covers
exactly
the domain
\Omega
, that is,
\Omega=\cup_{{K^{\sharp}}\in{\mathcal{P}}^{\sharp}_{i}}{K^{\sharp}}
.

The simplices
{K^{\sharp}}
are allowed to overlap. From Definition

4.1
(b) and Assumption
2.2
(a), we
obtain

|{K^{\sharp}}|\leq H_{{K^{\sharp}}}^{d}\leq\big(C_{\rm sh}^{\sharp}H_{K}\big)^{d}\leq\big(C_{\rm sh}^{\sharp}C_{\rm star}\big)^{d}\rho_{K}^{d}\leq\pi^{-1}\big(C_{\rm sh}^{\sharp}C_{\rm star}\big)^{d}|K|,

since
\rho_{K}
is the radius of a ball contained in
K
. Setting

C_{\rm cov}:=\pi^{-1}\big(C_{\rm sh}^{\sharp}C_{\rm star}\big)^{d}
,
we deduce

(40)

\sum_{{K^{\sharp}}\in{\mathcal{P}}^{\sharp}}|{K^{\sharp}}|\leq C_{\rm cov}\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}|K|=C_{\rm cov}|\Omega|,

where the correspondence between
K
and
{K^{\sharp}}
has been used.

This bound shows that the assumptions on the mesh
{\mathcal{P}}
and on the
simplicial covering
{\mathcal{P}}^{\sharp}
are sufficient to control the extent of
overlap in the computational domain
\Omega
caused by the use of

{\mathcal{P}}^{\sharp}
.

We consider a nodally bound-preserving approximant in

V_{{\mathcal{P}}}
. While related constructions appear in

[
38
,
42
,
4
]
, our concrete
choice follows
[
33
]
. The next theorem states its
optimal-order approximation.

Theorem 4.2

(Bound-preserving best approximation)
.

Let
K\in{\mathcal{P}}
, let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be the polynomial degree of

V_{\mathcal{P}}
, and let
v\in H^{k+1}({K^{\sharp}})
be a function with range in

[0,\kappa]
. Then, there exists
\pi_{H}v\in V_{\mathcal{P}}
whose
range is contained in
[0,\kappa]
, such that

(41)

\displaystyle|v-\pi_{H}v|_{m,K}

\displaystyle\leq C_{m}H_{K}^{k+1-m}|v|_{k+1,{K^{\sharp}}},

for
m=0,1,2
, where
{K^{\sharp}}\in{\mathcal{P}}^{\sharp}
is as in Definition

4.1
. The constant
C_{m}>0
depends on the
shape-regularity of
{K^{\sharp}}
, on
r
, and on
k
. Moreover, for

m=1,2
, the constant
C_{m}>0
also depends on
C_{\rm star}
, on the
upper bound
\kappa
, and on
|v|_{1,d,\Omega}
for
m=1
, or on

|v|_{2,\Omega}
for
m=2
.

Proof.

Let
k\geq 2
and let
i_{H}:H^{2}({K^{\sharp}})\to\mathbb{P}_{k}({K^{\sharp}})
denote
the Lagrange interpolation operator, producing the polynomial of
total degree
k
interpolating
v
at the Lagrange nodes of the
simplex
{K^{\sharp}}
. If, in addition,
v\in H^{k+1}({K^{\sharp}})
, then the
standard best-approximation estimate holds:

(42)

\|{v-i_{H}v}\|_{{0,{K^{\sharp}}}}+H_{{K^{\sharp}}}|v-i_{H}v|_{1,{K^{\sharp}}}+H_{{K^{\sharp}}}^{2}|v-i_{H}v|_{2,{K^{\sharp}}}+|{K^{\sharp}}|^{1/2}\|{v-i_{H}v}\|_{{0,\infty,{K^{\sharp}}}}\leq\tilde{C}_{\rm app}H_{{K^{\sharp}}}^{k+1}|v|_{k+1,{K^{\sharp}}},

for each
K\in{\mathcal{P}}
, with
\tilde{C}_{\rm app}>0
independent of

H_{{K^{\sharp}}}
and
v
; see
[
19
, Theorem 3.1.4]
. Using the
properties in Definition
4.1
, this yields

(43)

\|{v-i_{H}v}\|_{{0,K}}+H_{K}|v-i_{H}v|_{1,K}+H_{K}^{2}|v-i_{H}v|_{2,K}+|K|^{1/2}\|{v-i_{H}v}\|_{{0,\infty,K}}\leq C_{\rm app}H_{K}^{k+1}|v|_{k+1,{K^{\sharp}}},

with
C_{\rm app}:=\tilde{C}_{\rm app}\big(C_{\rm sh}^{\sharp}\big)^{k+1}
.

By construction, the nodal values of
i_{H}v
on
{K^{\sharp}}
lie in

[0,\kappa]
since
v
itself does, but the full range of
i_{H}v

is not necessarily contained in
[0,\kappa]
. Consequently,

i_{H}v|_{K}
does not, in general, preserve bounds.

To obtain an elementwise polynomial approximant of
v
that is
bound-preserving, we follow the construction in
[
33
, Theorem
2]
for simplicial meshes. For each
K\in{\mathcal{P}}
,
define

\displaystyle(\pi_{H}v)|_{K}:=\frac{\kappa}{2}+\beta_{\kappa}(v)\left(i_{H}v-\frac{\kappa}{2}\right),\quad\text{with}\quad\beta_{\kappa}(v):=\kappa\big(\kappa+2\|{v-i_{H}v}\|_{{0,\infty,K}}\big)^{-1}.

Since
\pi_{H}v
is obtained by linear operations and
V_{\mathcal{P}}

contains constants, it follows that
\pi_{H}v\in V_{\mathcal{P}}
. To
prove that its range is contained in
[0,\kappa]
, note first that
any function
w
with range in
[0,\kappa]
satisfies

(44)

\displaystyle\left\|w-\frac{\kappa}{2}\right\|_{0,\infty,K}\leq\frac{\kappa}{2}.

Therefore,

\left\|\pi_{H}v-\frac{\kappa}{2}\right\|_{0,\infty,K}=\beta_{\kappa}(v)\left\|i_{H}v-\frac{\kappa}{2}\right\|_{0,\infty,K}\leq\frac{\kappa}{2},

since

\left\|i_{H}v-\frac{\kappa}{2}\right\|_{0,\infty,K}\leq\|{i_{H}v-v}\|_{{0,\infty,K}}+\|{v-\frac{\kappa}{2}}\|_{{0,\infty,K}}\leq\|{i_{H}v-v}\|_{{0,\infty,K}}+\frac{\kappa}{2}.

Thus
0\leq\pi_{H}v\leq\kappa
. We now estimate its approximation properties. From

(45)

v-\pi_{H}v=\Big(v-\frac{\kappa}{2}\Big)(1-\beta_{\kappa}(v))+\beta_{\kappa}(v)(v-i_{H}v),

the triangle inequality, (
44
), and (
43
) yield

(46)

\|{v-\pi_{H}v}\|_{{0,K}}\leq\sqrt{|K|}\|v-i_{H}v\|_{0,\infty,K}+\|v-i_{H}v\|_{0,K}\leq 2C_{\rm app}H_{K}^{r+1}|v|_{r+1,{K^{\sharp}}}.

For the
H^{1}
-seminorm estimate, differentiating (
45
),
taking norms, and applying the triangle inequality gives

(47)

\displaystyle\|{\nabla(v-\pi_{H}v)}\|_{{0,K}}\leq(1-\beta_{\kappa}(v))\|{\nabla v}\|_{{0,K}}+\beta_{\kappa}(v)\|{\nabla(v-i_{H}v)}\|_{{0,K}}.

By Hölder’s inequality,
\|{\nabla v}\|_{{0,K}}\leq|K|^{(d-2)/(2d)}\|{\nabla v}\|_{{0,d,K}},
for
d=2,3
. Since
H^{k+1}({K^{\sharp}})
,
k\geq 2
, embeds continuously into
W^{1,d}({K^{\sharp}})
, the above combined with
(
43
) gives

\|{\nabla(v-\pi_{H}v)}\|_{{0,K}}\leq C_{\rm app}\big(2\kappa^{-1}|K|^{-1/d}H_{K}\|{\nabla v}\|_{{0,d,K}}+1\big)H_{K}^{k}|v|_{k+1,{K^{\sharp}}}.

From Assumption
2.2
(a),
|K|^{-1/d}H_{K}\leq C_{\rm star}^{-1},
so the result follows with

C_{1}:=C_{\rm app}(2C_{\rm star}^{-1}\kappa^{-1}\|{\nabla v}\|_{{0,d,K}}+1).

For the
H^{2}
-seminorm estimate, a similar argument yields

|v-\pi_{H}v|_{2,K}\leq C_{\rm app}\big(2\kappa^{-1}|K|^{-1/2}H_{K}^{2}|v|_{2,K}+1\big)H_{K}^{k-1}|v|_{k+1,{K^{\sharp}}}.

From Assumption
2.2
(a),
|K|^{-1/2}H_{K}^{2}\leq C_{\rm star}^{-d/2}H_{K}^{2-d/2},
and the result follows with

C_{2}:=C_{\rm app}(2C_{\rm star}^{-d/2}\kappa^{-1}|v|_{2,K}H_{K}^{2-d/2}+1).

This completes the proof for
k\geq 2
. For
k=1
, we can set

\pi_{H}v=i_{H}v
, since the range of linear Lagrange basis functions over
a simplex is contained in
[0,1]
. Standard interpolation estimates
apply in this case, with
C_{2}=1
.
∎

5.
A priori
error analysis

We are now in position to establish
a priori
error bounds between
sufficiently smooth exact solutions
u
of (
1
) and their nodally
bound-preserving approximations
\mathcal{E}^{+}(u_{H})
obtained through
(
20
).

Theorem 5.1

(A priori error estimate)
.

Let
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be the solution to (
1
), and let

\mathcal{E}^{+}(u_{H})\in W_{\mathcal{T}}
denote the nodally bound-preserving
approximation produced by (
20
), with
\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}
given by
(
12
) and
\alpha
by (
27
), computed on
families of polytopic meshes and corresponding simplicial subdivisions
satisfying Assumptions
2.2
,

2.3
, and
4.1
. If, in addition,

u\in H^{k+1}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, then the following error bound
holds:

(48)

|\kern-0.7113pt\|{u-\mathcal{E}^{+}(u_{H})}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}\leq C_{\rm apr}\bigg(\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}\big(\delta_{\omega_{K}}+H_{K}^{2}\mu_{K}\big)H_{K}^{2k}|u|_{k+1,{K^{\sharp}}}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

where
\mu_{K}:=\|\mu\|_{0,\infty,K}
, with
\{{K^{\sharp}}\}
denoting the
simplicial covering from Assumption
4.1
, and where

C_{\rm apr}>0
depends only on the polynomial degree

k\in\mathbb{N}
and on the constants appearing in Assumptions

2.2
,
2.3
, and
4.1
.

Proof.

We begin by decomposing the error
e:=u-\mathcal{E}^{+}(u_{H})
as

e=\big(u-\pi_{H}u\big)+\big(\pi_{H}u-\mathcal{E}^{+}(u_{H})\big)=:\eta+\xi,

noting that
\xi\in W_{\mathcal{T}}
. The triangle inequality gives

|\kern-0.7113pt\|{e}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}\leq|\kern-0.7113pt\|{\eta}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}+|\kern-0.7113pt\|{\xi}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}
.

We now estimate
\xi\in W_{\mathcal{T}}
. Observe that

\xi=\mathcal{E}^{+}(\pi_{H}u)-\mathcal{E}^{+}(u_{H})
, since

0\leq\pi_{H}u\leq\kappa
.
Applying Lemma
3.6
, we obtain

(49)

\displaystyle a_{DG}(\xi,\pi_{H}u-u_{H})-s(\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_{H}),\pi_{H}u-u_{H})\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\big(|\kern-0.7113pt\|{\pi_{H}u-u_{H}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}+\|{\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_{H})}\|_{{s}}^{2}\big).

Next, the modified consistency relation (
39
) implies

(50)

a_{DG}(\xi,v_{H})-s(\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_{H}),v_{H})=-a_{DG}(\eta,v_{H}),

and inserting this into (
49
) yields

(51)

\frac{1}{2}\big(|\kern-0.7113pt\|{\pi_{H}u-u_{H}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}+\|{\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_{H})}\|_{{s}}^{2}\big)\leq-a_{DG}(\eta,\pi_{H}u-u_{H}).

For brevity, set
\xi_{H}:=\pi_{H}u-u_{H}
. We now estimate the right-hand
side of (
51
). A straightforward estimate gives

(52)

|a_{DG}(\eta,\xi_{H})|\leq|\kern-0.7113pt\|{\eta}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|{\xi_{H}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}+\int_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}}|\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla\eta\}\kern-3.1298pt\}||[\![\xi_{H}]\!]|\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}}|\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla\xi_{H}\}\kern-3.1298pt\}||[\![\eta]\!]|\,\mathrm{d}s.

We treat the last two terms of (
52
) separately. For
the last term we proceed as in (
30
) to obtain

\int_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}}|\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla\xi_{H}\}\kern-3.1298pt\}||[\![\eta]\!]|\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\tfrac{1}{4}\|{\mathcal{D}^{1/2}\nabla_{\kern-1.42271pt{\mathcal{P}}}\xi_{H}}\|_{{0,\Omega}}\|{\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}[\![\eta]\!]}\|_{{0,\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}}}.

To estimate the term involving
\eta
, we proceed as in the proof of
Lemma
2.4
. More precisely, every face
F
shared
by two elements
K_{+},K_{-}\in{\mathcal{P}}
is divided into simplices
\{f\}
.
For each
f\subset F
we recall the simplex
K_{*}^{f}\subset K_{*}
,

*\in\{+,-\}
, with face
f
and opposite vertex the centre of the ball
with respect to which
K_{*}
is star-shaped. Application of the local
trace theorem (
8
) in each
K_{*}^{f}
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{F\subset\partial K_{*}}\|{v}\|_{{0,F}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\ C_{\rm tr}\sum_{F\subset\partial K_{*}}\sum_{f\subset F}\big(H_{K_{*}}^{-1}\|{v}\|_{{0,K_{*}^{f}}}^{2}+H_{K_{*}}|v|_{1,K_{*}^{f}}^{2}\big)

(53)

\displaystyle\leq\ C_{\rm tr}\big(H_{K_{*}}^{-1}\|{v}\|_{{0,K_{*}}}^{2}+H_{K_{*}}|v|_{1,K_{*}}^{2}\big),

with
C_{\rm tr}
depending only on the star-shapedness constant

C_{\rm star}
from Assumption
2.2
(a). We have also
used that

\cup_{F\subset\partial K_{*}}\cup_{f\subset F}K_{*}^{f}=K_{*}
by
construction (see, e.g.,
[
12
, Lemma 4.7]
for a proof of the
trace theorem with explicit
C_{\rm tr}
).

Hence, (
53
) implies

\|{\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}[\![\eta]\!]}\|_{{0,\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}}}^{2}\leq\tilde{c}_{1}\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}\delta_{\omega_{K}}\big(H_{K}^{-2}\|{\eta}\|_{{0,K}}^{2}+|\eta|_{1,K}^{2}\big),

with
\tilde{c}_{1}:=8C_{\rm tr}C_{\rm star}k(k-1+d)d^{-1}
.
Theorem
4.2
then gives

(54)

\displaystyle\|{\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}[\![\eta]\!]}\|_{{0,\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle c_{1}\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}\delta_{\omega_{K}}H_{K}^{2k}|v|_{k+1,{K^{\sharp}}}^{2},

with
c_{1}=\tilde{c}_{1}\max\{C_{0}^{2},C_{1}^{2}\}
.

Now consider the second term on the right-hand side of
(
52
), for which

\int_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}}|\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla\eta\}\kern-3.1298pt\}||[\![\xi_{H}]\!]|\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\|{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}^{-1/2}\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla\eta\}\kern-3.1298pt\}}\|_{{0,\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}}}\|{\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}[\![\xi_{H}]\!]}\|_{{0,\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}}}.

We estimate the term involving
\eta
as follows:

\displaystyle\|{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}^{-1/2}\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla\eta\}\kern-3.1298pt\}}\|_{{0,\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{2}\sum_{F\subset\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{*\in\{+,-\}}\delta_{K_{*}}^{-1}\|{\mathcal{D}}\|_{{0,\infty,K_{*}}}^{2}\big(|\eta|_{1,K_{*}^{F}}^{2}+H_{K_{*}}^{2}|\eta|_{2,K_{*}^{F}}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{2}\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}\|{\mathcal{D}^{-1}}\|_{{0,\infty,K}}^{-1}\big(|\eta|_{1,K}^{2}+H_{K}^{2}|\eta|_{2,K}^{2}\big),

for
\tilde{c}_{2}:=\big(8C_{\rm star}k(k-1+d)\big)^{-1}d^{2}C_{\rm tr}(1+\max_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}H_{K})
, using the trace theorem
(
53
) with
v=|\nabla\eta|_{K_{*}}|
, the definition
of
\delta_{K_{*}}
, and collecting contributions to each
K_{*}
. Since

\|{\mathcal{D}^{-1}}\|_{{0,\infty,K}}^{-1}\leq\|{\mathcal{D}}\|_{{0,\infty,K}}
, application of
Theorem
4.2
yields

(55)

\displaystyle\|{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}^{-1/2}\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla\eta\}\kern-3.1298pt\}}\|_{{0,\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle c_{2}\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}\|{\mathcal{D}}\|_{{0,\infty,K}}H_{K}^{2k}|u|_{k+1,{K^{\sharp}}}^{2},

with
c_{2}:=\tilde{c}_{2}\max\{C_{1}^{2},C_{2}^{2}\}
.

Finally, using (
54
) and standard estimates also yields

(56)

|\kern-0.7113pt\|{\eta}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}\leq\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}\big(C_{1}^{2}\|{\mathcal{D}}\|_{{0,\infty,K}}+C_{0}^{2}H_{K}^{2}\|\mu\|_{0,\infty,K}+c_{1}\delta_{\omega_{K}}\big)H_{K}^{2k}|u|_{k+1,{K^{\sharp}}}^{2}.

Using (
54
), (
55
), and
(
56
) to further estimate (
52
), together
with the discrete Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, gives

(57)

\displaystyle|a_{DG}(\eta,\xi_{H})|\leq

\displaystyle 2\big(|\kern-0.7113pt\|{\eta}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}+\|{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}^{-1/2}\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla\eta\}\kern-3.1298pt\}}\|_{{0,\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}}}^{2}+\|{\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}[\![\eta]\!]}\|_{{0,\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}}}^{2}\big)^{1/2}|\kern-0.7113pt\|{\xi_{H}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{3}\bigg(\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}\big(\delta_{\omega_{K}}+H_{K}^{2}\mu_{K}\big)H_{K}^{2k}|u|_{k+1,{K^{\sharp}}}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}|\kern-0.7113pt\|{\xi_{H}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}},

with
c_{3}:=2(\max\{2c_{1}+c_{2}+C_{1}^{2},C_{0}^{2}\})^{1/2}
and

\mu_{K}:=\|\mu\|_{0,\infty,K}
.

Returning now to (
51
), we use (
57
)
(along with the inequality
ab\leq a^{2}+b^{2}/4
on the right-hand side of
(
57
)) to deduce

(58)

\frac{1}{4}|\kern-0.7113pt\|{\xi_{H}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_{H})}\|_{{s}}^{2}\leq c_{3}^{2}\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}\big(\delta_{\omega_{K}}+H_{K}^{2}\mu_{K}\big)H_{K}^{2k}|u|_{k+1,{K^{\sharp}}}^{2}.

From this estimate, we can obtain an upper bound for
|\kern-0.7113pt\|{\xi}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}
. Since

\xi_{H}=\xi-\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_{H})
, the triangle inequality and
(
38
) give

(59)

\displaystyle|\kern-0.7113pt\|{\xi_{H}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}\geq

\displaystyle\big(|\kern-0.7113pt\|{\xi}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}-|\kern-0.7113pt\|{\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_{H})}|\kern-0.7113pt\|\big)^{2}\geq\big(|\kern-0.7113pt\|{\xi}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}-5^{-1}\|{\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_{H})}\|_{{s}}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|\kern-0.7113pt\|{\xi}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{25}\|{\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_{H})}\|_{{s}}^{2}-\tfrac{2}{5}|\kern-0.7113pt\|{\xi}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}\|{\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_{H})}\|_{{s}}\geq\tfrac{4}{5}|\kern-0.7113pt\|{\xi}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}-\tfrac{4}{25}\|{\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_{H})}\|_{{s}}^{2},

using
2ab\leq a^{2}/5+5b^{2}
in the last step. Combining
(
58
) with (
59
) gives

(60)

|\kern-0.7113pt\|{\xi}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}\leq 5c_{3}^{2}\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}\big(\delta_{\omega_{K}}+H_{K}^{2}\mu_{K}\big)H_{K}^{2k}|u|_{k+1,{K^{\sharp}}}^{2},

where we have ignored the non-negative term

\|{\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_{H})}\|_{{s}}^{2}
. A final application of the triangle
inequality completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.2

(Further consequences of the
a priori
analysis)
.

Some remarks

(1)

The appearance of the covering simplices
{K^{\sharp}}
on the
right-hand side of (
48
) does not affect the
inferred convergence order. Indeed, estimating
(
48
) from above and using (
40
)
yields

|\kern-0.7113pt\|{u-\mathcal{E}^{+}(u_{H})}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}\leq C\big(\max_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}H_{K}^{k}\big)|u|_{k+1,\Omega},

where
C>0
depends on
C_{\rm apr}
,
C_{\rm cov}
,

\mathcal{D}
, and
\mu
only.

(2)

The proof of Theorem
5.1
also provides an

a priori
error bound for the ‘non-compliant’ approximation

u_{H}\in V_{\mathcal{P}}
, which may not be nodally
bound-preserving. Specifically, from (
58
) we
obtain a bound on
|\kern-0.7113pt\|{\pi_{H}u-u_{H}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}
, which, combined with
(
56
), leads to

|\kern-0.7113pt\|{u-u_{H}}|\kern-0.7113pt\|_{\rm{\mathcal{P}}}\leq\tilde{C}_{\rm apr}\bigg(\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}\big(\delta_{\omega_{K}}+H_{K}^{2}\mu_{K}\big)H_{K}^{2k}|v|_{k+1,{K^{\sharp}}}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

for some
\tilde{C}_{\rm apr}>0
with the same dependence on
constants as
C_{\rm apr}>0
.

(3)

The bound (
58
) also sheds light on the
decay rate of
\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_{H})\to 0
, i.e., the ‘non-compliant’
component of the approximation, as
\max_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}H_{K}\to 0

and/or
\max_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}}h_{T}\to 0
.

For simplicity, assume
{\mathcal{T}}
is quasi-uniform with
representative submesh diameter
h
and that
{\mathcal{P}}
is
quasi-uniform with representative element diameter
H
. From
(
27
), each elemental component is proportional to

Hh^{-1}
.

Consider first the case
H
fixed and
h\to 0
. Then the
right-hand side of (
58
) is bounded by
C_{\rm up}H^{k}
, for some
C_{\rm up}>0
depending on
u
. Using the
definition of the stabilisation norm and
\alpha\sim Hh^{-1}
, we
obtain

\displaystyle C_{\rm up}H^{2k}\geq\|{\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_{H})}\|_{{s}}^{2}\geq

\displaystyle C_{\rm down}Hh^{-3}\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{K}}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{k,d}}h^{d}\big(\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_{H})(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{T})\big)^{2}\sim C_{\rm down}Hh^{-3}\|\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_{H})\|_{0,\Omega}^{2},

for
C_{\rm down}>0
depending on
\mathcal{D}_{0}
. This shows that

\|\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_{H})\|_{0,\Omega}\sim h^{3/2}
for fixed

H
. Moreover, since
h^{-3}\geq H^{-3}
, we find

\|\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_{H})\|_{0,\Omega}\sim H^{k+1}
, which is of
higher order than what one might expect from
(
58
).

6.
Implementation and matrix structure

The composite method (
20
) can be realised by a transformation
starting from the discontinuous Galerkin (DG) formulation posed on the
simplicial submesh
{\mathcal{T}}
. We first introduce the discontinuous
polynomial space

\displaystyle V_{\mathcal{T}}:=\bigl\{v_{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega):v_{h}|_{T}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(T)\quad\forall\,T\in{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\}.

On this space, the classical interior-penalty DG method reads: find

u_{h}\in V_{\mathcal{T}}
such that

(61)

\displaystyle a_{\mathrm{DG}}^{{\mathcal{T}}}(u_{h},v_{h})=\ell(v_{h})\qquad\forall\,v_{h}\in V_{\mathcal{T}},

where the bilinear form
a_{\mathrm{DG}}^{{\mathcal{T}}}:(H^{3/2+\epsilon}_{0}(\Omega)+V_{\mathcal{T}})\times(H^{3/2+\epsilon}_{0}(\Omega)+V_{\mathcal{T}})\to\mathbb{R}
is

(62)

\begin{split}a_{\mathrm{DG}}^{{\mathcal{T}}}(u_{h},v_{h})&=\int_{\Omega}\!\Big(\mathcal{D}\nabla_{{\mathcal{T}}}u_{h}\cdot\nabla_{{\mathcal{T}}}v_{h}+\mu u_{h}v_{h}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\\
&\quad+\int_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}}\!\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}[\![u_{h}]\!]\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]\,\mathrm{d}s-\int_{\Gamma_{{\mathcal{T}}}}\!\Big(\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla u_{h}\}\kern-3.1298pt\}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\theta\,\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla v_{h}\}\kern-3.1298pt\}\cdot[\![u_{h}]\!]\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s,\end{split}

and
\ell(v_{h}):=\langle f,v_{h}\rangle_{\Omega}
. Here,
\theta\in[-1,1]

is the usual IPDG parameter,
\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}
the penalty parameter
(cf. (
12
)), and
\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}=\cup_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}}\partial T
the
skeleton of
{\mathcal{T}}
.

Next, recall the conforming space
W_{\mathcal{T}}
defined in
Section
2.2
. On this space we seek
w_{h}\in W_{\mathcal{T}}
such
that

(63)

a_{\#}(w_{h},v_{h})=\ell(v_{h})\qquad\forall\,v_{h}\in W_{\mathcal{T}},

with bilinear form

(64)

\begin{split}a_{\#}(w_{h},v_{h})&=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\mathcal{D}\nabla_{{\mathcal{T}}}w_{h}\cdot\nabla_{{\mathcal{T}}}v_{h}+\mu w_{h}v_{h}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}}\!\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}[\![w_{h}]\!]\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad-\int_{\Gamma_{{\mathcal{P}}}}\!\Big(\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla w_{h}\}\kern-3.1298pt\}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\theta\,\{\kern-3.1298pt\{\mathcal{D}\nabla v_{h}\}\kern-3.1298pt\}\cdot[\![w_{h}]\!]\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

The spaces
V_{\mathcal{T}}
and
W_{\mathcal{T}}
are related through their local
bases. Specifically, each basis function
\phi_{i}^{K}
of
W_{K}
,
for
K\in{\mathcal{P}}
, can be expressed as a linear combination of basis
functions
\varphi_{l}^{K}
of
V_{{\mathcal{T}}}
:

(65)

\displaystyle\phi_{i}^{K}=\sum_{l=1}^{N_{K}^{i}}\varphi_{l}^{K},\qquad i=1,\ldots,N_{K},

where
N_{K}
is the number of degrees of freedom of
W_{K}
,

\mathbf{x}_{i}^{K}
the node associated with
\phi_{i}^{K}
,

N^{i}_{K}
the number of coincident degrees of freedom in
V_{\mathcal{T}}
,
and
\varphi_{l}^{K}
the corresponding basis functions.

From this relation it follows that
a_{\#}
can be written in terms of

a_{\mathrm{DG}}^{{\mathcal{T}}}
:

(66)

\displaystyle a_{\#}(\phi_{i}^{K_{1}},\phi_{j}^{K_{2}})=\sum_{k=1}^{N^{j}_{K_{2}}}\sum_{l=1}^{N^{i}_{K_{1}}}a_{\mathrm{DG}}^{{\mathcal{T}}}(\varphi_{l}^{K_{1}},\varphi_{k}^{K_{2}}),\qquad K_{1},K_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}.

The same holds for penalty contributions: if

\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}\cap\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}=\varnothing
the jumps vanish, giving

(67)

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}}\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}[\![\phi_{i}^{K_{1}}]\!]\cdot[\![\phi_{j}^{K_{2}}]\!]\,\mathrm{d}s=\sum_{k=1}^{N^{j}_{K_{2}}}\sum_{l=1}^{N^{i}_{K_{1}}}\int_{\Gamma_{{\mathcal{T}}}}\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}[\![\varphi_{l}^{K_{1}}]\!]\cdot[\![\varphi_{k}^{K_{2}}]\!]\,\mathrm{d}s.

Let
N_{{\mathcal{T}}}:=\dim(V_{\mathcal{T}})
and
N_{\#}:=\dim(W_{\mathcal{T}})
. Then
(
66
) shows that
a_{\mathrm{DG}}^{{\mathcal{T}}}
and
a_{\#}

are connected by a transformation matrix

\mathbf{O}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\#}\times N_{{\mathcal{T}}}}
:

(68)

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{\#}=\mathbf{O}\,\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{DG}}\,\mathbf{O}^{T},

where
[\mathbf{A}_{\#}]_{ij}=a_{\#}(\phi_{i},\phi_{j})
is the
stiffness matrix for (
63
), and

[\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{DG}}]_{ij}=a_{\mathrm{DG}}^{{\mathcal{T}}}(\varphi_{i},\varphi_{j})

the stiffness matrix for (
61
). The algebraic problem
(
63
) can therefore be written: find

\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\#}}
such that

(69)

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{\#}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{O}\,\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{DG}}\,\mathbf{O}^{T}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{O}\mathbf{b},

where
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{{\mathcal{T}}}}
has entries

b_{i}=\langle f,\varphi_{i}\rangle_{\Omega}
.

Let
V_{{\mathcal{P}}}
be the global dG space defined in
(
5
). Its basis functions
\psi_{j}^{K}\in V_{{\mathcal{P}}}

can be written in terms of
\{\phi_{i}^{K}\}
:

(70)

\displaystyle\psi_{j}^{K}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{K}}a_{i}^{K}\phi_{i}^{K}.

This yields

(71)

\displaystyle a_{\rm DG}(\psi_{i}^{K_{1}},\psi_{j}^{K_{2}})=\sum_{l=1}^{N_{K_{2}}}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{K_{1}}}a_{l}^{K_{1}}a_{k}^{K_{2}}a_{\#}(\phi_{l}^{K_{1}},\phi_{k}^{K_{2}}),\qquad K_{1},K_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}.

Introducing

\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{{\mathcal{P}}}\times N_{\#}}
as the matrix
mapping
W_{\mathcal{T}}
to
V_{\mathcal{P}}
, the global stiffness matrix is

(72)

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{{\mathcal{P}}}=\mathbf{Q}\,\mathbf{A}_{\#}\,\mathbf{Q}^{T}.

The block diagonal structure of
\mathbf{Q}
is shown in
Figure
2b
. Combining (
68
) and
(
72
), the algebraic system for the positivity-preserving R-FEM is

(73)

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{{\mathcal{P}}}(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{W}^{+})+\mathbf{S}(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{W}^{-})=\bigl(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{O}\mathbf{A}_{\rm DG}\mathbf{O}^{T}\mathbf{Q}^{T}\bigr)(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{W}^{+})+\mathbf{S}(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{W}^{-})=\mathbf{F}_{\mathcal{P}},

with
\mathbf{F}_{\mathcal{P}}=\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{O}\mathbf{b}
. The
stabilisation matrix
\mathbf{S}
is defined via
{\mathcal{T}}
as

\displaystyle s_{{\mathcal{T}}}(v_{h},u_{h})=\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{P}}}\alpha\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{K}}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{k,d}}\big(\mathcal{D}_{\omega_{K}}h_{T}^{d-2}+\mu_{T}h_{T}^{d}\big)v_{h}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i})u_{h}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}),\qquad u_{h},v_{h}\in V_{{\mathcal{T}}},

with
\alpha
from (
27
). Its matrix form is

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{W}^{-})=\bigl(\mathbf{O}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{S}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\mathbf{Q}^{T}\mathbf{O}^{T}\bigr)(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{W}^{-}).

We assess the performance of the finite element method (

)
on the domain

) throughout.

The nonlinear system from (

) is solved in two
steps. First, compute
w_{h}^{0}\in W_{\mathcal{T}}
from

(74)

a_{\#}(w_{h}^{0},v_{h})=\langle f,v_{h}\rangle_{\Omega}\qquad\forall v_{h}\in W_{\mathcal{T}},

and let
\mathbf{W}^{0}=(w_{1}^{0},\ldots,w_{N_{\#}}^{0})
be the vector of

W_{\mathcal{T}}
-dofs. Using the algebraic form (
73
), for

n=0,1,2,\ldots
we update via a semi-smooth Newton method
(cf.
[
2
]
)

(75)

\mathbf{U}^{(n+1)}=\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{W}^{(n)}-J_{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{U}^{(n)})^{-1}\,\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{W}^{(n)}),

where
\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{W})=\mathbf{F}_{\mathcal{P}}-\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{P}}\,\mathbf{Q}(\mathbf{W})^{+}-\mathbf{S}\,\mathbf{Q}(\mathbf{W})^{-}
and

(76)

J_{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{U})=J_{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{W})\mathbf{Q}^{T}=-\Big(\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{P}}\mathbf{Q}\,\mathrm{diag}(I^{1}(w_{1}),\ldots,I^{1}(w_{N_{\#}}))+\mathbf{S}\mathbf{Q}\,\mathrm{diag}(I^{2}(w_{1}),\ldots,I^{2}(w_{N_{\#}}))\Big)\mathbf{Q}^{T},

1,&w_{i}\geq 0,\end{cases}\qquad I^{2}(w_{i})=\begin{cases}0,&w_{i}\in[0,\kappa],\\

76
) follows from

J_{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{W})\mathbf{Q}^{T}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{W}=J_{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{U})\mathbf{U}
. Iterations
terminate when

(77)

\|\mathcal{E}(u_{H}^{n+1})-\mathcal{E}(u_{H}^{n})\|_{0,\Omega}\leq 10^{-8},

where
\mathcal{E}(u_{H}^{n})=w_{h}^{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\#}}W_{i}^{n}\phi_{i}
.

Conclusion

A bound-preserving discontinuous
Galerkin framework on polytopic meshes is introduced. The construction relies on a
projection-based formulation, in which a robust bilinear form is
combined with nonlinear stabilisation to ensure non-negativity of the
discrete solution without compromising the convergence behaviour of
the underlying DG scheme. The method accommodates general polygonal
meshes, allowing for flexibility in mesh generation and refinement
strategies, while maintaining compatibility with standard polynomial
approximation spaces. A salient feature of the approach is the ability to select at will the cardinality and the position of nodes where range bounds are enforced without affecting the overall complexity of the method.

The theoretical development has been complemented by an extensive
series of numerical experiments. These tests demonstrate optimal
convergence rates for smooth solutions, confirm robustness of the
scheme across a wide range of diffusion parameters and illustrate the
method’s ability to resolve both boundary and interior layers without
spurious oscillations, or out-of-range over- or undershoots. Comparisons with a standard DG discretisation
confirm that the proposed method eliminates non-physical behaviour
in challenging singularly perturbed regimes.

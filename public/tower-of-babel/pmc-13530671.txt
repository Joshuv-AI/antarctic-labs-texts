Poster 265. Cost-Effectiveness of Distal Tibial Allograft vs The Latarjet Procedure for Anterior Shoulder Instability: A Markov Analysis
Authors: Matthew T. Provencher, Eoghan T. Hurley, Samuel G. Lorentz, Alex M. Meyer, Mark A. Glover, Jonathan F. Dickens
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:Distal tibial allograft (DTA) is becoming an increasingly utilized alternative to the Latarjet procedure for anterior shoulder instability. Given the current emphasis on value-based decision making in healthcare, a contemporary analysis comparing the cost-effectiveness of each is needed. The purpose of this study is to perform a Markov model-based cost-effectiveness analysis comparing DTA to the Latarjet procedure for the surgical treatment of anterior shoulder instability.Methods:A Markov Chain Monte Carlo probabilistic model was developed to evaluate the outcomes and costs of 1,000 simulated patients undergoing either DTA or open Latarjet for anterior shoulder instability. Health utility values, transition probabilities, and costs were derived from the literature and expert opinion. Outcome measures included total cost, quality-adjusted life years (QALYs), and the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER). A ten-year time horizon was used, with an annual cycle length and a 3% annual discount rate applied to both costs and utilities. Probabilistic sensitivity analysis and deterministic sensitivity analysis were performed to account for input uncertainty.Results:Over the ten-year time horizon, mean total costs were $35,044 ± 6,323 for DTA and $30,745 ± 5,393 for Latarjet. Mean effectiveness was 7.48 ± 0.72 QALYs for DTA and 7.77 ± 0.50 QALYs for Latarjet. Latarjet was the dominant strategy, yielding both lower costs and higher effectiveness. The ICER for DTA relative to Latarjet was -$14,899/QALY. Net monetary benefit (NMB) at a willingness-to-pay threshold of $50,000/QALY was $357,742 for Latarjet and $339,015 for DTA. Latarjet was the more cost-effective strategy in 69% of simulations. Deterministic sensitivity analysis found that the recurrence risk after DTA would need to be reduced to less than 2.3% for DTA to become cost-effective relative to Latarjet.Conclusions:In this analysis, the Latarjet procedure and the DTA had similar cost effectiveness ratios for treatment of anterior shoulder instability, despite the added cost of the allograft associated with the DTA. Latarjet may have slightly improved cost-effectiveness due to use of autograft, however DTA may offer important clinical advantages by avoiding the potential morbidity associated with coracoid harvest. Notably, this analysis does not account for long-term outcomes such as the development of glenohumeral arthritic changes, which may influence the relative value of each procedure over time.

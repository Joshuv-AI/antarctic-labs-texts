Abstract
. Morita theory for quantales is developed. The main result of the paper is a characterization of those quantaloids (categories enriched in the symmetric monoidal closed category of sup-lattices) that are equivalent to modular categories over quantales. Based on this characterization, necessary and sufficient conditions are derived for two quantales to be Morita-equivalent, i. e. have equivalent module categories. As an application, it is shown that the category of internal sup-lattices in a Grothendieck topos is equivalent to the module category over a suitable chosen ordinary quantale.

1.
Introduction

The goal of this paper is to develop Morita theory for quantales. Our starting point for doing this is the so-called

comparison theorem
(see
[
16
,
17
,
19
]
). Starting from a monad
\mathscr{T}
on a category
\mathscr{X}
and an adjunction

F\dashv U:\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{X}
, there is a bijective correspondence between functors
K:\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{X}^{\mathscr{T}}
with
U=U^{\mathscr{T}}K

and morphisms of monads
\mathscr{T}\to\mathscr{S}
, where
\mathscr{X}^{\mathscr{T}}
is the Eilenberg-Moore category of
\mathscr{T}
-algebras,
U^{\mathscr{T}}:\mathscr{X}^{\mathscr{T}}\to\mathscr{X}

is the evident forgetful functor and
\mathscr{S}
is the monad on
\mathscr{X}
generated by the adjunction
F\dashv U
(
[
4
]
).
When the monad morphism is an isomorphism, the functor
U
is called
\mathscr{T}
-Galois
in
[
18
]
. Then the comparison theorem
(see Theorem
2.2
) asserts that the functor
K
is an equivalence of categories if
and only if
U
is monadic and
\mathscr{T}
-Galois. Using the comparison theorem, we prove our central result
(Theorem
4.5
) characterizing module categories over quantales among quantaloids. The key property of module
categories over quantales, which distinguishes them among quantaloids, is that they are monadic over the category of sets,

Set
. With help of this central result, we
then transfer many aspects of the classical Morita theory to the context of quantales. In particular,
we describe Morita equivalence of quantales both in terms of projective generators (Theorem
5.3
) and full idempotents
(Theorem
7.4
). Note that Theorem
7.4
essentially describes all quantales that are Morita equivalent to a
given one.

The outline of this paper is as follows. After recalling in Section 2 those notions and results that will be used from category
theory, we state the comparison theorem, which gives a necessary and sufficient condition for a functor into the Eilenberg-Moore
algebras for a monad to be an equivalence. In section 3 we briefly review some of background knowledge about quantales and modules over them.
In Section 4 we present our main result providing conditions under which a quantaloid can be recognized as a module category
over a quantale. In the next two sections, the preceding results are applied to derive necessary and sufficient
conditions for two quantales to be Morita equivalent both in terms of projective generators (Section 5) and in terms of idempotents
and matrices (Section 6). Finally, in the concluding section, some applications to internal sup-lattices in Grothendieck toposes
are considered. In particular, the category of internal sup-lattices in a Grothendieck topos is characterized as the category
of external modules over an external sup-lattice. This allows us to recover a result of Joyal and Tierney
[
8
, Chapter VI, Proposition 3.1]
on
internal locales in localic Grothendieck toposes.

We refer to
[

]
,
[

]
and
[

Categorical preliminaries

One says that the functor

2.3
.

Splitting of idempotents.
Given a monoid
(M,\ast,\textsf{1})
,
an element
m\in M
is an
idempotent
if
m\ast m=m
. A morphism
e:A\to A
in a category
is idempotent if
e
is an idempotent element in the endomorphism monoid
\mathscr{A}(A,A)
. Thus,
e
is idempotent if
e^{2}=e\circ e=e
.
A category
\mathscr{A}
is said to be
Cauchy complete
if
idempotents split
in

\mathscr{A}
, i.e., for every idempotent
e:A\to A
, there exists an object
A_{e}
(called the
splitting object
)
and morphisms
p:A\to A_{e}
and
i:A_{e}\to A
such that
ip=e
and
pi=1_{A_{e}}
. (Note that such a splitting,
and hence also the splitting object, is unique up to isomorphism.) In this case,
(A_{e},i)

is an equaliser of
e
and
1_{A}
and
(A_{e},p)
is a coequaliser of
e
and
1_{A}
. Thus, any category
admitting either equalisers or coequalizers is Cauchy complete.
Note that if
\mathscr{T}
is a monad on a Cauchy complete
category
\mathscr{A}
, then so is
\mathscr{A}^{\mathscr{T}}
. Moreover, the forgetful functor
U^{\mathscr{T}}:\mathscr{A}^{\mathscr{T}}\to\mathscr{A}

preserves and creates splitting of idempotents.

2.4
.

Generators.
Recall that a family of functors
(F_{i}:\mathscr{B}\to\mathscr{B}_{i})_{i\in I}

is called
jointly faithful
(resp.
conservative
) if the associated functor

\mathscr{B}\to\prod_{i\in I}\mathscr{B}_{i},\,\,B\longmapsto(F_{i}(B))_{i\in I}

to the product category is faithful (resp. conservative); more explicitly, a family of functors

(F_{i}:\mathscr{B}\to\mathscr{B}_{i})_{i\in I}
is jointly faithful if for each pair of distinct morphisms
f,g:B\rightrightarrows B^{\prime}
in

\mathscr{B}
one has
F_{i}(f)\neq F_{i}(g)
for some
i\in I
; and is jointly conservative if any morphism
f:B\to B^{\prime}
in
\mathscr{B}

such that
F_{i}(f)
is an isomorphism in
\mathscr{B}_{i}
for all
i\in I
, is an isomorphism in
\mathscr{B}
. If
\mathscr{B}
has equalizers, a jointly
conservative family of functors is always jointly faithful; the converse holds, for example, if every morphism in
\mathscr{B}
which
is both a monomorphism and an epimorphism is already an isomorphism (that is, if
\mathscr{B}
is
balanced
), but not in general.
A set of objects
\{B_{i},i\in I\}
of a category
\mathscr{B}
is a
(strong) generating set
for
\mathscr{B}
if the family of functors

(\mathscr{B}(B_{i},-):\mathscr{B}\to\textsf{Set})_{i\in I}
jointly faithful (conservative). When the set
\{B_{i},i\in I\}
is reduced to a single
element
\{B\}
, one says that
B
is a
(strong) generator
for
\mathscr{B}
.

Recall further (for example, from
[
2
]
) that for a right adjunction functor is

–

faithful if and only if all the components of the counit of the adjunction is epimorphic;

–

faithful and conservative if and only if all the components of the counit of the
adjunction is an extremal epimorphism; that is, an epimorphism that factorizes through no proper subobject
of its codomain.

In particular, given an object
B
of a category
\mathscr{B}
, if
\mathscr{B}
admits arbitrary small copowers of
B
(and hence the functor
B^{(-)}

that takes a set
X
to the coproduct of
X
copies of
B
, is a left adjoint to
\mathscr{B}(B,-):\mathscr{B}\to\textsf{Set}
),
then
B
is a (strong) generator if and only if for any object
B^{\prime}\in\mathscr{B}
, the canonical morphism
B^{\mathscr{B}(B,B^{\prime})}\to B^{\prime}
, which is
the
B^{\prime}
-component of the counit of the adjunction
\mathscr{B}(B,-)\dashv B^{(-)}
, is an (extremal) epimorphism. If each of these components
is a regular epimorphisms (i.e., a coequalizer of a pair of morphisms), then the functor
\mathscr{B}(B,-)
has a full and faithful comparison functor
into its Eilenberg-Moore category. Such an object
B
is called
regular generator
for
\mathscr{B}
.
Evidently, if regular epimorphisms coincide with extremal epimorphisms (which is the case, for example, for
regular

categories in the sense of
[
1
]
), a strong generator is a regular generator.
Note finally that if a (regular, strong) generator
B
is a retracts of an object
B^{\prime}
, then
B^{\prime}
is also a (regular, strong) generator.
This follows from the fact that in any category, a retracts of a (regular, extremal) epimorphism is again a (regular, extremal) epimorphism.

2.5
.

Projective objects.
An object
B
of a category
\mathscr{B}
is called

(regular) projective
if the hom functor
\mathscr{B}(B,-):\mathscr{B}\to\textsf{Set}
takes (regular) epimorphisms to epimorphisms.
In any category, every retract of a (regular) projective object is (regular) projective and a coproduct of (regular)
projective objects is (regular) projective (provided this coproduct exists). An object is called a
(regular)
projective generator
if it is simultaneously a (regular) generator and (regular) projective.

3.
Quantales and their modules

In this section we provide basic notions and properties of quantales and modules over them.

3.1
.

Quantales.
Let
{\mathcal{S}l}
be the category of sup-lattices (=complete posets) and
sup-preserving maps. The top element of a sup-lattice
L
is denoted by
1
, and the bottom by
0
. It is well known
(see,
[
8
]
) that
{\mathcal{S}l}
is a symmetric monoidal closed category. We recall that the tensor product

L\otimes L^{\prime}
of two sup-lattices
L
and
L^{\prime}
is the codomain of the universal map
L\times L^{\prime}\to L\otimes L^{\prime}
that preserves
supremum in each variable separately (such a map is called a
bimorphism
). The tensor unit is
\textsf{2}=\{0\leq 1\}
.
The internal hom is the set
[L,L^{\prime}\,]
of sup-preserving maps ordered by
f\leq g
if and only if
f(l)\leq g(l)

for all
l\in L
. Since the order on
L^{\prime}
is complete, so is the pointwise order on
[L,L^{\prime}]
. Recall (e.g., from
[
8
]
)
that the category
{\mathcal{S}l}
of sup-lattices is monadic over
Set
via the functor
{\mathcal{S}l}(\textsf{2},-):{\mathcal{S}l}\to\textsf{Set}
.
Thus,
{\mathcal{S}l}
is a complete, cocomplete and Barr-exact category (in the sense of
[
1
]
) and hence in particular idempotents
split in
{\mathcal{S}l}
. Moreover, (small) coproducts are biproducts; that is, if
\{X_{i}\}
is a (small) family of objects of
{\mathcal{S}l}
,
the comparison morphism
\coprod{X_{i}}\to\prod{X_{i}}
is an isomorphism.

A
quantale
is a monoid in the symmetric monoidal category
{\mathcal{S}l}
.
More precisely, a quantale is a sup-lattice
A
equipped with a monoid structure
(A,\ast,1)

such that
\ast
distributes over suprema:

(3.1)

a\ast(\bigvee_{i\in I}b_{i})=\bigvee_{i\in I}(a\ast b_{i})\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,(\bigvee_{i\in I}a_{i})\ast b=\bigvee_{i\in I}(a_{i}\ast b)

for all

a,b\in A
and all
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in I},\{b_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq A
. A quantale
A
is
commutative

if
a\ast b=b\ast a
for all
a,b\in A
and is
idempotent
if
a\ast a=a
for all
a\in A
.

A morphism
f:A\to B
of quantales is a morphism of sup-lattices satisfying

(i)

f(1)=1

(ii)

f(a\ast a^{\prime})=f(a)\ast f(a^{\prime})

for all
a,a^{\prime}\in A
.

3.2
.

Modules over quantales.
With any quantale
(A,\ast,1)
, there is associated a category of (right) modules over it.
Recall that a right
A
-module is a complete lattice
M
together with a map

-\cdot-:M\times A\to M
such that

(3.2)

\displaystyle m\cdot\textsf{1}=m

\displaystyle m\cdot(a_{1}\ast a_{2})=(m\cdot a_{1})\cdot a_{2}

\displaystyle(\bigvee_{i\in I}m_{i})\cdot a=\bigvee_{i\in I}(m_{i}\cdot a)

\displaystyle m\cdot(\bigvee_{i\in I}a_{i})=\bigvee_{i\in I}(m\cdot a_{i})

for all
a,a_{1},a_{1}\in A
,
m\in M
,
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq A

and
\{m_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq M
. Note that the third and fourth conditions are equivalent to saying that
the map
-\cdot-
is a bimorphism.

A morphism
f:M\to N
of right
A
-modules is a morphism of sup-lattices such that
f(m\cdot a)=f(m)\cdot a
for
all
m\in M
and
a\in A
. The category of right
A
-modules is denoted by
\textsf{Mod}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})
. The evident forgetful functor

U^{A}:\textsf{Mod}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})\to{\mathcal{S}l}
is monadic with left adjoint
F^{A}
, which takes
X\in{\mathcal{S}l}
to
X\otimes A
, and hence it creates
all limits. Moreover, since the functor
-\otimes A:{\mathcal{S}l}\to{\mathcal{S}l}
admits as a right adjoint the functor
[A,-]:{\mathcal{S}l}\to{\mathcal{S}l}
,
the forgetful functor
U^{A}
has a right adjoint sending
X\in{\mathcal{S}l}
to
[A,X]
. Then
U^{A}
clearly creates all colimits.
In a similar way, one has the category of left
A
-modules
{}_{A}\textsf{Mod}({\mathcal{S}l})
. If
B
is another quantale, a
(B,A)
-bimodule
is a sup-lattice together with compatible left and right actions of
B
and
A
, respectively. The corresponding
category is denoted by
{}_{B}\textsf{Mod}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})
.

In the sequel, given a quantale
(A,\ast,\textsf{1})
, we usually omit the symbol
\ast
in notation and simply write
aa^{\prime}

instead of
a\ast a^{\prime}
.

3.3
.

Quantaloids.
A
quantaloid
is a locally small category enriched in the
symmetric monoidal closed category
{\mathcal{S}l}
. If
\mathscr{Q}
is a quantaliod, then one has the following commutative diagram of
categories and functors:

\textstyle{{\mathcal{S}l}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{\mathcal{S}l}(\textsf{2},-)}
\textstyle{\mathscr{Q}^{\text{op}}\times\mathscr{Q}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\textsf{Hom}_{\mathscr{Q}}(-,-)}
\scriptstyle{\mathscr{Q}(-,-)}
\textstyle{\textsf{Set},}

in which
\textsf{Hom}_{\mathscr{Q}}(-,-)
is the so called
lifted hom-functor
.
The property that (small) coproducts are biproducts in
{\mathcal{S}l}
,
transfers to any quantaloid
\mathscr{Q}
, (e.g.,
[
6
]
), in the sense that for any family
(D_{i},i\in I)
of objects of
\mathscr{Q}

the product
\prod_{i\in I}D_{i}
exists if and only if the coproduct
\coprod_{i\in I}D_{i}
exists and, moreover,
the canonical comparison morphism between them is
an isomorphism. We will write
\oplus{D_{i}}
for the biproduct of the family
(D_{i},i\in I)
.
Typical examples of quantaloids are module categories over quantales. Given a quantale
A
, we shall usually write
\textsf{Hom}_{A}(-,-)

instead of
\textsf{Hom}_{\textsf{Mod}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})}(-,-)
. Recall that for
M,N\in\textsf{Mod}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})
,
\textsf{Mod}_{A}(M,N)
is a sup-lattice with pointwise
defined operations.

We close this subsection with the following important observation.

3.4 Remark
.

Since the forgetful functor
{\mathcal{S}l}(\textsf{2},-):{\mathcal{S}l}\to\textsf{Set}
takes epimorphisms to
split epimorphisms
[
8
]
, each epimorphism is regular in
{\mathcal{S}l}
(e.g.,
[
9
]
), implying that regular epimorphisms,
strong epimorphisms and epimorphisms coincide in
{\mathcal{S}l}
. Since for any quantale
A
, the module category
\textsf{Mod}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})
is
both monadic and comonadic over
{\mathcal{S}l}
, the same hold also in
\textsf{Mod}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})
. Therefore, projective objects in module categories
over quantales coincide with the regular projective objects (see, Subsection
2.4
). Moreover, generators,
strong generators, and regular generators all coincide.

4.
When is a quantaloid equivalent to a module category over a quantale ?

In this section, we characterize those quantaloids that are equivalent to module categories over quantales.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an arbitrary category and
A\in\mathscr{A}
be an object of
\mathscr{A}
. For any set
X
, we write
A^{(X)}

(resp.
A^{X}
) for the
copower
(resp.
power
) of
A
by
X
, i.e., the coproduct
\coprod_{x\in X}A

(resp. product
\prod_{x\in X}A
) in
\mathscr{A}
of
X
copies of
A
, provided they exist. The coproduct injection
A\to A^{(X)}

corresponding to
x\in X
is denoted by
\iota^{A}_{x}
. If
f:X\to Y

is a map of sets, then
A^{(f)}:A^{(X)}\to A^{(Y)}
is the unique morphism in
\mathscr{A}
such that
A^{(f)}\cdot\iota^{A}_{x}=\iota_{f(x)}
for all
x\in X
. Dually, one has the canonical projections
\pi_{x}:\prod_{x\in X}A\to A
and the morphism

A^{f}:A^{Y}\to A^{X}
.

4.1
.

Adjoint triangle for quantaloids.
A
Let
\mathscr{Q}
be a quantaloid and
Q
an arbitrary but fixed object of
\mathscr{Q}
. To
Q
there is associated
a functor
\textsf{Hom}_{\mathscr{Q}}(Q,-):\mathscr{Q}\to{\mathcal{S}l}
and the quantale
E=\textsf{Hom}_{\mathscr{Q}}(Q,Q)
of endomorphisms of
Q
. It is easy to check that
for any object
A\in\mathscr{Q}
, the object
\textsf{Hom}_{\mathscr{Q}}(Q,A)
naturally has the structure of a right
E
-module
given by the composition

\textsf{Hom}_{\mathscr{Q}}(Q,A)\otimes\textsf{Hom}_{\mathscr{Q}}(Q,Q)\xrightarrow{}\textsf{Hom}_{\mathscr{Q}}(Q,A)

and thus the assignment
A\longmapsto\textsf{Hom}_{\mathscr{Q}}(Q,A)
yields a functor
K^{Q}:\mathscr{Q}\to\textsf{Mod}_{E}({\mathcal{S}l})

such that
U^{E}K^{Q}=\textsf{Hom}_{\mathscr{Q}}(Q,-)
. When, as we henceforth suppose,
\mathscr{Q}
admits set-indexed copowers of
Q
,
the functor

\mathscr{Q}(Q,-)={\mathcal{S}l}(\textsf{2},-)\circ\textsf{Hom}_{\mathscr{Q}}(Q,-):\mathscr{Q}\to\textsf{Set}

admits as a left adjoint the functor

X\mapsto Q^{(X)},\,\,(f:X\to Y)\longmapsto(Q^{(f)}:Q^{(X)}\to Q^{(Y)}).

For any set
X
, the
X
-component

(\eta^{Q})_{X}:X\to\mathscr{Q}(Q,Q^{(X)})
of the unit
\eta^{Q}
of the adjunction
Q^{(-)}\dashv\mathscr{Q}(Q,-)
is
the map that takes
x\in X
to
\iota^{Q}_{x}:Q\to Q^{(X)})
,
while the
A
-component
(\varepsilon^{Q})_{A}
,
A\in\mathscr{Q}
, of the
counit
\varepsilon^{Q}
is the morphism
Q^{{\mathscr{Q}}(Q,A)}\to A
defined (uniquely) by the equality

(\varepsilon^{Q})_{A}\circ\iota^{Q}_{f}=f.

Next, composing adjunctions

F^{E}\dashv U^{E}:\textsf{Mod}_{E}({\mathcal{S}l})\to{\mathcal{S}l}\,\,\text{and}\,\,\textsf{2}^{(-)}\dashv{\mathcal{S}l}(\textsf{2},-):{\mathcal{S}l}\to\textsf{Set}

gives an adjunction
E^{(-)}\dashv U_{E}:\textsf{Mod}_{E}({\mathcal{S}l})\to\textsf{Set}
, where

U_{E}
is the evident forgetful functor, while
E^{(-)}
sends
a set
X
to the free right
E
-module
E^{(X)}
, which because of the isomorphism
E^{(X)}\simeq E^{X}
, can be seen
as the set of all maps
X\to E
. Since the forgetful functor
U^{E}:\textsf{Mod}_{E}({\mathcal{S}l})\to{\mathcal{S}l}
is monadic and admits both adjoints,

Self-small objects.
We intend to apply Theorem

to the above adjoint triangle, but before doing so, we must first examine those objects

\mathscr{Q}(Q,-)

\mathscr{T}_{E}

Let
\mathscr{T}_{Q}

Q^{(-)}\dashv\mathscr{Q}(Q,-)

\mathscr{Q}

4.3 Theorem
.

With the data given in the adjoint triangle (

), suppose
that
\mathscr{Q}
admits arbitrary small copowers of

. Then the functor
K^{Q}

is an equivalence if and only if the functor
\mathscr{Q}(Q,-):\mathscr{Q}\to\emph{\textsf{Set}}
is monadic.

4.4 Remark
.

Since the category
\textsf{Mod}_{E}({\mathcal{S}l})
is naturally enriched over
{\mathcal{S}l}
, it follows from the
very construction of the functor

that it is in fact (the underlying ordinary functor of) an
{\mathcal{S}l}
-enriched functor.
Since the functor
{\mathcal{S}l}(\textsf{2},-):{\mathcal{S}l}\to\textsf{Set}
is conservative, an
{\mathcal{S}l}
-functor is equivalence if and only if its
underlying ordinary functor is; so that the equivalence of categories in Theorem
4.3

can be promoted to an enriched equivalence of
{\mathcal{S}l}
-categories.

Now we are ready to state and prove the key result of the paper, which provides equivalent conditions
for a quantaloid to be equivalent to a module category over a quantale.

4.5 Theorem
.

For a quantaloid
\mathscr{Q}
,
the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\mathscr{Q}
is equivalent to a module category over a quantale.

(ii)

\mathscr{Q}
is monadic over
Set
.

(iii)

\mathscr{Q}
is Barr-exact and it has a regular projective generator
Q

and arbitrary small copowers of
Q
.

When one of these equivalent conditions holds, then there exists an equivalence
\mathscr{Q}\simeq\emph{\textsf{Mod}}_{E}({\mathcal{S}l})
, where
Q
is as in
(iii)
and
E=\emph{\textsf{Hom}}_{\mathscr{Q}}(Q,Q)
.

Proof.
Since any module category over a quantale is monadic over
Set
(see,
3.2
), (i) implies (ii).
The equivalence (ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii) is well known (e.g.,
[
13
, Theorem 2.8]
). Under (iii),
the functor
\mathscr{Q}(Q,-):\mathscr{Q}\to\textsf{Set}
is monadic and using Theorem
4.3
, we see that

\mathscr{Q}
is equivalent to
\textsf{Mod}_{E}({\mathcal{S}l})
, where
E=\textsf{Hom}_{\mathscr{Q}}(Q,Q)
. Thus, (iii) implies (i).

5.
Morita equivalent quantales

In this section, we prove a Morita type theorem for quantales in terms of projective generators.

We say that two quantales are
Morita equivalent
if they have equivalent categories of right modules.
Essentially the same proof as for ordinary rings yields:

5.1 Theorem
.

Two commutative quantales are Morita equivalent if and only if they are isomorphic.

Since any module category over a quantale is Barr-exact, in the light of Remark
3.4
, we get
from Theorem
4.5
the following theorem.

5.2 Theorem
.

Let
A
be a quantale and
Q\in\emph{\textsf{Mod}}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})
be a projective generator.
Then the quantales
A
and
\emph{\textsf{Hom}}_{A}(Q,Q)
are Morita equivalent.

The following theorem gives a necessary and sufficient condition for two quantales to be Morita equivalent.

5.3 Theorem
.

Two quantales
A
and
B
are Morita equivalent if and only if
there exists a projective generator
Q\in\emph{\textsf{Mod}}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})
such that
B\simeq\emph{\textsf{Mod}}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})(Q,Q)
as quantales.

Proof.
Suppose first that two quantales
A
and
B
are Morita equivalent. Then there exists an
equivalence
\textsf{Mod}_{A}\simeq\textsf{Mod}_{B}
. From Remark
3.4
and then applying Theorem
4.5
, we deduce that there exists
a projective generator
Q\in\textsf{Mod}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})
such that the functor

K=\textsf{Hom}_{A}(Q,-):\textsf{Mod}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})\to\textsf{Mod}_{B}({\mathcal{S}l})

is an equivalence
and the diagram

\textstyle{\textsf{Mod}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{K}
\scriptstyle{\textsf{Mod}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})(Q,-)\dashv Q^{(-)}}
\textstyle{\textsf{Mod}_{B}({\mathcal{S}l})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{B^{(-)}\dashv U_{B}}

Set

is an adjoint triangle. Since
K
is an equivalence, it follows from Theorem
2.2
that the induced morphism

s_{K}={\mathcal{S}l}(\textsf{2},\gamma_{K}):{\mathcal{S}l}(\textsf{2},B^{(-)})\to{\mathcal{S}l}(\textsf{2},\textsf{Hom}_{A}(Q,Q^{(-)}))

of the monads on
Set
generated by the two adjunctions, is an isomorphism.
Then the map
{\mathcal{S}l}(\textsf{2},k):{\mathcal{S}l}(\textsf{2},B)\to{\mathcal{S}l}(\textsf{2},\textsf{Hom}_{A}(Q,Q))
, where
k:B\to\textsf{Hom}_{A}(Q,Q)

is the composite

B\xrightarrow{\simeq}\textsf{t}\times B\xrightarrow{(\gamma_{K})_{\textsf{t}}}\textsf{Hom}_{A}(Q,Q^{(\textsf{t})})\xrightarrow{\textsf{Hom}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})(Q,\,\simeq)}\textsf{Hom}_{A}(Q,Q)

is an isomorphism of ordinary monoids (e.g.
[
5

).
Here,

is a chosen terminal object in

, i.e., a set with only one element. It follows – since the functor

{\mathcal{S}l}(\textsf{2},-):{\mathcal{S}l}\to\textsf{Set}
is conservative – that
k:B\to\textsf{Hom}_{A}(Q,Q)
is an isomorphism of quantales.

For the converse, if
Q\in\textsf{Mod}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})
is a projective generator, then there is an equivalence
\textsf{Mod}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})\simeq\textsf{Mod}_{\textsf{Hom}_{A}(Q,Q)}({\mathcal{S}l})

by Theorem
5.2
, and composing this with the evident equivalence
\textsf{Mod}_{\textsf{Hom}_{A}(Q,Q)}({\mathcal{S}l})\simeq\textsf{Mod}_{B}({\mathcal{S}l})

induced by the isomorphism

of quantales, gives us the desired equivalence
\textsf{Mod}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})\simeq\textsf{Mod}_{B}({\mathcal{S}l})
.

6.
Morita equivalence of quantales induced by idempotents

6.1
.

Submodules defined by idempotents.
Let
A

M

A^{\prime}
a non-empty

Note that
M^{\prime}A
(resp.

M^{\prime}\otimes A\xrightarrow{i_{M^{\prime}}\otimes A}M\otimes A\xrightarrow{\cdot}M\,\,\,\text{(resp.}\,\,\,M\otimes A^{\prime}\xrightarrow{M\otimes i_{A^{\prime}}}M\otimes A\xrightarrow{\cdot}M),

where

and

are the canonical inclusions. It is easy to see that
M^{\prime}A
is a submodule
of the right

-module
M
and is called the

submodule of
M
generated by
M^{\prime}
. If, in addition,
M
is an
(B,A)
-bimodule,
B
being another quantale,
then
MA^{\prime}
becomes a left
B
-module. When
M^{\prime}=\{m\}
(resp.

M
(resp.

(resp.
Ma

\{m\}A
(resp.
M\{a\}

Given two quantales
A,B
, a
(B,A)
-bimodule
M
and an idempotent
e
in
A
, one has an idempotent endomorphism of
left
B

-\cdot e:M\to M,m\longmapsto m\cdot e,

whose image (and hence the splitting object) is
Me

Since
Me
is an equalizer of
-\cdot e
and
\textsf{id}_{M}

Me=\{m\in M:m\cdot e=m\}
. Then
the composite
M\xrightarrow{p_{M}}eM\xrightarrow{i_{M}}M
, where
p_{M}
is the map
m\to m\cdot e
, while
i_{M}
is the canonical inclusion,
is a splitting of the idempotent endomorphism
-\cdot e
. Hence in particular
Me
is a left
B
-module. Symmetrically, for
any
(A,B)
-bimodule
N
, the set
eN=\{e\cdot n:n\in N\}=\{n:n\in N:n=e\cdot n\}
is a right
B
-module
and the idempotent endomorphism
N\xrightarrow{n\to n\cdot e}N
of of right
B
-modules factors through
eN
. In particular,
the set
eA=\{ea:a\in A\}
(resp.
Ae=\{ae:a\in A\}
) is a right (resp. left)
A
-module. Observe that the
set
eAe=\{eae:a\in A\}
, which can be seen as the sup-lattice

(or as
(eA)e

e
.

6.2 Proposition
.

Let
A
be a quantale and
e
be an idempotent in
A
. For any right
A
-module
M

there is a natural isomorphism
\alpha_{M}:\emph{\textsf{Hom}}_{A}(eA,M)\simeq Me
in

.

Proof.
Consider the composite

q:\textsf{Hom}_{A}(eA,M)\xrightarrow{\textsf{Hom}_{A}(p_{A},M)}\textsf{Hom}_{A}(A,M)\simeq M,

where
p_{A}:A\to eA

is the first morphism in the factorization
A\xrightarrow{p_{A}}eA\xrightarrow{i_{A}}A
of the idempotent morphism
e\cdot-:A\to A
. Since
p_{A}
is
an epimorphism,
q
is injective. We claim that the image
\textsf{Im}(q)
of
q
is
Me
.
Indeed, since for any
f\in\textsf{Hom}_{A}(eA,M)

\textsf{Hom}_{A}(p_{A},M)(f)=fp_{A}
, it follows that
q(f)=(fp_{A})(\textsf{1})=f(p_{A}(\textsf{1}))=f(e)
and then

q(f)\cdot e=f(e)\cdot e=f(ee)=f(e)=q(f).

Hence,
\textsf{Im}(q)\subseteq Me
. Conversely,
if
m\in Me
, then
m\cdot e=m
and if
f_{m}:eA\to M
is given by
f_{m}(ea)=m\cdot a
, then
f_{m}
is defined correctly (for, if
ea=ea^{\prime}
,
then
m\cdot a=(m\cdot e)\cdot a=m\cdot(ea)=m\cdot(ea^{\prime})=(m\cdot e)\cdot a^{\prime}=m\cdot a^{\prime}
) and is a morphism of right

A

q(f_{m})=f_{m}(e)=m\cdot e=m
and hence
Me\subseteq\textsf{Im}(q)

q
induces an isomorphism

\textsf{Hom}_{A}(eN,M)\simeq\textsf{Hom}_{A}(N,M)e

6.3 Remark
.

It is straightforward to verify that the isomorphism

\alpha_{A}:\textsf{Hom}_{A}(eA,eA)\simeq eAe

Following the terminology of ring theory, we shall call an idempotent

6.4 Proposition
.

Let
A
be a quantale and
e
be an idempotent in
A

A

eA
is a

\emph{\textsf{Mod}}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})

Proof.
Note first that by Remark

, the right

\textsf{Mod}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})

.

7.
Morita equivalence in terms of matrices

In this section, we prove a Morita type theorem for quantales in terms of matrices.

7.1
.

Matrices with values in a quantale.
Let
A
be an arbitrary quantale.
If
X
and
Y
are sets, an
A-matrix

a
of size
X\times Y
is a family
(\textsf{a}(x,y))_{(x,y)\in X\times Y}
of elements of
A
. Note that
A
-matrices of size
X\times Y
might be equally seen as maps

X\times Y\to A
. If
a
and
b
are two
A
-matrices with size
X\times Y
and

Y\times Z
, then their product
\textsf{a}\star\textsf{b}
has size
X\times Z
and is defined by

(\textsf{a}\star\textsf{b})(x,z)=\bigvee_{y\in X}\textsf{a}(x,y)\textsf{b}(y,z).

For any set
X
, we write
\textsf{i}_{X}
for the
matrix of size
X\times X
given by

\textsf{i}_{X}(x,x^{\prime})=\begin{cases}1,~x=x^{\prime}\\
0,~x\neq x^{\prime}\end{cases}

\textsf{i}_{X}
serves as the identity for the matrix multiplication. We write
\textsf{Mat}_{X\times Y}(A)
for the set
of all
A
-matrices of size
X\times Y
.
\textsf{Mat}_{X\times Y}(A)
is a sup-lattice with operations formed point-wise from those in
A
.
When
X=Y
, we shall just write
\textsf{Mat}_{X}(A)
instead of
\textsf{Mat}_{X\times X}(A)
. It is routine to check that
the triple
(\textsf{Mat}_{X}(A),\star,\textsf{i}_{X})
is a quantale.

For any set
X
, the right free
A
-module on
X
has the form
A^{(X)}
(see, for instance,
[
8
]
), which

7.4 Theorem
.

Two quantales
A
and
B
are Morita equivalent if and only if there exist a set
X
and
a full idempotent
\emph{\textsf{a}}\in\emph{\textsf{Mat}}_{X}(A)
such that
B\simeq\emph{\textsf{a}}\,\emph{\textsf{Mat}}_{X}(A)\,\emph{\textsf{a}}
as quantales.

Proof.
If
\textsf{a}\in\textsf{Mat}_{X}(A)
is a full idempotent such that the quantales
B
and
\textsf{a}\,\textsf{Mat}_{X}(A)\,\textsf{a}

are isomorphic, then
\textsf{Mat}_{X}(A)
and
\textsf{a}\,\textsf{Mat}_{X}(A)\,\textsf{a}
are Morita equivalent quantales by Theorem
6.5
,
while
A
and
\textsf{Mat}_{X}(A)
are equivalent by Theorem
7.2
. Therefore, that the quantales
A
and
\textsf{a}\,\textsf{Mat}_{X}(A)\,\textsf{a}

are Morita equivalent.

Conversely, suppose that
A
and
B
are Morita equivalent quantales. Then, by Theorem
5.3
, there exists a projective
generator
Q\in\textsf{Mod}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})
such that
\textsf{Hom}_{A}(Q,Q)\simeq B
. Since
Q
is projective in
\textsf{Mod}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})
, there exists a set
X
such that

Q
is retract of the right
A
-modules
A^{(X)}
(see, for example,
[
8
, Proposition 1, page 11]
). In other words,
there exists an idempotent
e\in\textsf{Hom}_{A}(A^{(X)},A^{(X)})
such that
Q
is the splitting object of
e
. Put
\textsf{a}=j^{-1}_{X}(e)
.
Then
\textsf{a}\in\textsf{Mat}_{X}(A)
is an idempotent matrix and
e
can be identified with the idempotent endomorphism

\textsf{a}\cdot-:A^{(X)}\to A^{(X)}
. It follows that
Q
is isomorphic to the splitting object of
\textsf{a}\cdot-
, which is

\textsf{a}A^{(X)}
. Since
A
and
B
are Morita equivalent quantales by hypothesis,
B\simeq\textsf{Hom}_{A}(Q,Q)
. But
\textsf{Hom}_{A}(Q,Q)\simeq\textsf{Hom}_{A}(\textsf{a}A^{(X)},\textsf{a}A^{(X)})
and since
\textsf{Hom}_{A}(\textsf{a}A^{(X)},\textsf{a}A^{X})\simeq\textsf{a}\,\textsf{Mat}_{X}(A)\textsf{a}

by Lemma
7.3
, it follows that
B\simeq\textsf{a}\,\textsf{Mat}_{X}(A)\textsf{a}
; so it remains to show that
\textsf{a}\in\textsf{Mat}_{X}(A)

is a full idempotent. Since
A^{(X)}
is a retract of
A^{(X\times X)}
in
\textsf{Mod}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})
,
\textsf{a}A^{(X)}
is a retract of

\textsf{a}A^{(X\times X)}
in
\textsf{Mod}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})
. As
\textsf{a}A^{(X)}
is a generator for
\textsf{Mod}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})
, it follows that
\textsf{a}A^{(X\times X)}

is also a generator for
\textsf{Mod}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})
(see
2.4
) and hence the functor

\textsf{Hom}_{A}(\textsf{a}A^{(X\times X)},-):\textsf{Mod}_{A}({\mathcal{S}l})\to{\mathcal{S}l}
is conservative. Then each component of the counit of the adjunction

\textsf{Hom}_{A}(\textsf{a}A^{(X\times X)},-)\dashv-\otimes\textsf{a}A^{(X\times X)}
is epimorphic. In particular, the
A^{(X\times X)}
-component
of the counit, which is the evaluation map

\textsf{Hom}_{A}(\textsf{a}A^{(X\times X)},A^{(X\times X)})\otimes\textsf{a}A^{(X\times X)}\to A^{(X\times X)},

is an epimorphism. But since
\textsf{Hom}_{A}(\textsf{a}A^{(X\times X)},\,\,A^{(X\times X)})\simeq A^{(X\times X)}\textsf{a}
by Lemma
7.3
, the
A^{(X\times X)}
-component
is an epimorphism if and only if the morphism

A^{(X\times X)}\textsf{a}\otimes\textsf{a}A^{(X\times X)}\to A^{(X\times X)},

or, equivalently, the
morphism

\textsf{Mat}_{X}(A)\textsf{a}\otimes\textsf{a}\textsf{Mat}_{X}(A)\to\textsf{Mat}_{X}(A)

given by the matrix multiplication, is an epimorphism. And this is the case if and only if the image of the last morphism,
which is
\textsf{Mat}_{X}(A)\,\textsf{a}\,\textsf{Mat}_{X}(A)
, is isomorphic to
\textsf{Mat}_{X}(A)
, i.e., if and only if
a
is a full idempotent.
This completes the proof of the theorem.

8.
Some applications: Sup-lattices in Grothendieck toposes

In this section, we deal with internal sup-lattices in Grothendieck toposes. It is well known that sup-lattices can be defined
in any elementary topos (e.g.,
[
8
]
).

8.1
.

Sup-lattices in elementary toposes.
Let
\mathcal{E}
be an elementary topos (
[
7
]
) with
the subobject classifier
\Omega
, and let
{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})
denote the category of internal sup-lattices (i.e. internally complete
lattices and sup-preserving morphisms) in
\mathcal{E}
. It is well known (see,
[
8
]
) that
{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})
is monadic over
\mathcal{E}
. The
covariant power-set functor
(X\to\Omega^{X},\,f\to\exists_{f})
(e.g.,
[
7
]
) is a monad on
\mathcal{E}
whose unit is the
inclusions of singletons
\{\}_{X}\colon X\to\Omega^{X}
, and whose multiplication
\Omega^{\Omega^{X}}\to\Omega^{X}

the internal union, respectively. This monad is denoted by
\mathcal{P}
. An object
X\in\mathcal{E}
is an
\mathcal{P}
-algebra precisely if
X

is an internal sup-lattice in
\mathcal{E}
, and thus
{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})
, being (isomorphic to) the category of
\mathcal{P}
-algebras, is monadic over

\mathcal{E}
. The free
\mathcal{P}
-algebra functor
F^{\mathcal{P}}:\mathcal{E}\to{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})
takes an object
X\in\mathcal{E}
to
\mathcal{P}(X)
and therefore, for all

X\in\mathcal{E}
and
S\in{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})
, we have a bijection

(8.1)

{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})(\mathcal{P}(X),S)\simeq\mathcal{E}(X,S).

Since the forgetful functor
{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})\to\mathcal{E}
takes epimorphisms to split epimorphisms
[
8
]
, Remark
3.4

applies also to internal sup-lattices. In particular, generators are regular (and hence also strong) generators in
{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})
.
Moreover, the free
\mathcal{P}
-algebras are projective in
{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})
(
[
23
]
). Observe next that since
{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})
is monadic on
\mathcal{E}
,
all small limits (and hence all small colimits) exist in
{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})
provided they exist in
\mathcal{E}
. Therefore, if
\mathcal{E}
is a Grothendieck
topos,
{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})
is complete and cocomplete. Finally, recall that
{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})
is Barr-exact (e.g.,
[
23
]
).

8.2
.

Relations in elementary toposes.
Recall that for objects
X
and
Y
in any category
\mathcal{E}
with finite products, a
relation
from
X
to
Y
is a subobject
of
X\times Y
. It is well known that, in any Barr-exact category relations can be composed using pullbacks and image factorizations.
The identity on
X
for this composition is given by the diagonal morphism
X\to X\times X
. Now if
\mathcal{E}
is a Grothendieck
topos, then for any object
X\in\mathcal{E}
, the set
\textsf{Sub}(X)
of subobjects of
X
is a complete lattice, i.e., a sup-lattice:
the supremum of subobjects
X_{i}\rightarrowtail X
in
\mathcal{E}
can be calculated by taking the image of the induced morphism
\oplus X_{i}\to X
(see, for example,
[
11
]
). Consequently, the set
\textsf{Sub}(X\times X)
of relations on
X
with its subobject
ordering and relational composition is a quantale.

We record the following lemma, which we shall need. Recall that a
bound
of a Grothendieck topos
\mathcal{E}
is an object whose subobjects
form a generating family for
\mathcal{E}
.

8.3 Lemma
.

Let
B
be a bound of a Grothendieck topos
\mathcal{E}
. Then
\mathcal{P}(B)=\Omega^{B}

is a generator for
{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})
.

Proof.
Note first that since
B
is a bound of
\mathcal{E}
, its subobjects
\{k_{i}:B_{i}\to B,i\in I\}
generate
\mathcal{E}
. Since the forgetful
functor
{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})\to\mathcal{E}
is evidently faithful and the functor
F^{\mathcal{E}}=\Omega^{-}
is a left adjoint to it, we conclude (for example,
from
[
20
, Chapter 5, Proposition 1.5]
) that the family
\{\Omega^{B_{i}},i\in I\}
is generating for
{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})
. Next, for
each
i\in I
, the (mono)morphism
k_{i}:B_{i}\to B
, like any other morphism in
\mathcal{E}
, yields morphisms
\mathcal{P}(k_{i})=\Omega^{k_{i}}:\mathcal{P}(B)\to\mathcal{P}(B_{i})

and
\forall_{k_{i}}:\mathcal{P}(B_{i})\to\mathcal{P}(B)
such that
\mathcal{P}(k_{i})
is an internal right adjoint of
\exists_{k_{i}}
(e.g.,
[
11
, Theorem IV.9.2]
)
and
\forall_{k_{i}}
is an internal right adjoint of
\mathcal{P}(k_{i})
(e.g.,
[
11
, Propositions IV.9.4]
). Since internal left adjoints are
supremum-preserving morphisms (see, for example,
[
10
]
), it follows that
\mathcal{P}(k_{i})
is a morphism of internal sup-lattices. But since
k_{i}:B_{i}\to B
is a monomorphism,
\mathcal{P}(k_{i})\circ\exists_{k_{i}}=\textsf{id}_{\Omega^{B_{i}}}
by
[
7
, Corollary 1.33]
, and hence each
\Omega^{B_{i}}
is
a retract of
\Omega^{B}
in
{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})
. Then
\Omega^{B}
is a generator for
{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})
. For if
h:\Omega^{B_{i}}\to X
separates two different
morphisms
f,g:X\rightrightarrows Y
, then the compopsite
h\circ\mathcal{P}(k_{i})
also separates
f
and
g
, since
\mathcal{P}(k_{i})
is a (split) epimorphism.

8.4 Theorem

Let
B

\mathcal{E}

{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})\simeq\emph{\textsf{Mod}}_{\emph{\textsf{Sub}}(B\times B)}({\mathcal{S}l}).

Proof.
As mentioned above,
{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})
is Barr-exact. Since
\mathcal{E}

{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})

{\mathcal{S}l}

[
21
]

\mathcal{P}(B)=\Omega^{B}

{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})

{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})

Lemma
8.3
. Applying Theorem
4.5
, we conclude that the functor

{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})(\mathcal{P}(B),-):{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})\to\textsf{Mod}_{{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})(\mathcal{P}(B),\mathcal{P}(B))}({\mathcal{S}l})

{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})(\mathcal{P}(B),\mathcal{P}(B))\simeq\textsf{Sub}(B\times B)
of quantales, as can be seen from the following sequence of natural isomorphisms of
sup-lattices:

{\mathcal{S}l}(\mathcal{E})(\mathcal{P}(B),\mathcal{P}(B))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{topos.eq.})}}{{\simeq}}\mathcal{E}(B,\mathcal{P}(B))=\mathcal{E}(B,\Omega^{B})\simeq\mathcal{E}(B\times B,\Omega)\simeq\textsf{Sub}(B\times B).

Here the second isomorphism comes from the exponential adjunction and the third from the definition of
\Omega

8.7

Sup-lattices in localic toposes.
Recall from
[
8

that a locale is a commutative idempotent quantale
L

l\leq 1
for all
l\in L
.
For example, for any object
X

\mathcal{E}
, the set
\textsf{Sub}(X)

[

Since any locale

L
whose objects are the elements of
L
, and
there is a morphism
l\to l^{\prime}
in
L
if and only if
l\leq l^{\prime}
. Define a Grothendieck topology
J
on
L
by
assigning to each element
l

L
(i.e. object of
L
) the collection
J_{l}=\{(l_{i}\leq l)_{i\in I}\}

\bigvee_{i\in I}l_{i}=l
. We shall write
\textsf{Sh}(\textsf{L},J)
(or just
\textsf{Sh}(\textsf{L})
) for the category of sheaves
on the site
(\textsf{L},J)
. Note that this topology is canonical in the sense that it is the largest Grothendieck topology on
L
in which
all representable functors are sheaves. Since the locale
\textsf{Sub}(\textsf{T})
of subsheaves of the terminal sheaf
T

in
\textsf{Sh}(\textsf{L})
is isomorphic to the locale
L
(see, for example, the proof of
[
11

\textsf{T}\times\textsf{T}\simeq\textsf{T}
, applying Theorem

, we obtain the following result of Joyal and Tierney

[
8
, Chapter VI, Proposition 3.1]
:

8.8 Theorem
.

For any locale
L

equivalence

{\mathcal{S}l}(\emph{\textsf{Sh}}(\emph{\textsf{L}}))\simeq\emph{\textsf{Mod}}_{L}({\mathcal{S}l}).

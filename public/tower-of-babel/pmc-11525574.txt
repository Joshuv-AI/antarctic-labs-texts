Application of artistic design innovation in promoting rural cultural brand construction
Authors: Min Zeng, Chao Jin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
To conduct an in-depth analysis of global users’ emotional and aesthetic preferences for rural cultural brands and to accurately assess the varying popularity of different design elements across countries, this study utilizes the EmoRuralSim simulator in combination with artificial intelligence and text mining techniques. The study creates a virtual environment for emotion and language simulation and constructs a Design, Emotion, Region (DER) relational model. By building a virtual user group that encompasses the languages and cultures of 15 countries, and employing an intelligent recommendation system, the study achieves precise matching of regional preferences with product styles, offering personalized design recommendations. The findings indicate that Brazilian users favor festive, vibrant, and colorful elements, with a preferred style leaning towards folk cultural activities and art exhibitions. Their match degree, product preference index, and regional adaptability score are 78%, 0.75, and 8.2, respectively. Additionally, users from Russia, Japan, Germany, South Korea, and Thailand exhibit high emotional resonance with designs related to rural architecture, handicrafts, and performing arts, with evaluation values of 0.89, 0.82, and 0.81, respectively. This study reveals the complex interactions between emotions and design elements in a cross-cultural context, providing valuable insights for the international promotion of rural cultural brands.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-77657-3.

Introduction

With the advancement of globalization, cultural exchange and integration have become unprecedentedly active. In this diverse era, rural culture, as a unique cultural resource, is increasingly becoming an important direction for cultural development in various countries1. Rural culture not only carries rich historical memories and local characteristics but also showcases its unique charm and value in the context of globalization. To ensure that these valuable cultural resources are effectively preserved and promoted in the process of globalization, innovation in art and design has gradually become a key approach to advancing rural cultural brand development. By integrating cultural characteristics with user emotional experiences, innovative design transforms the unique appeal of rural culture into perceptible and resonant emotional experiences for users, thereby enhancing the global influence and competitiveness of the brand2.

Emotional design plays an indispensable role in the construction of rural cultural brands. It goes beyond the surface level of product functionality and aesthetic value, placing the emotional responses of users at the core of the design process. Through meticulous consideration of design elements and precise expression of emotions, emotional design can effectively evoke users’ emotional resonance with traditions, history, and a sense of belonging3. This resonance elevates rural cultural brands beyond mere cultural symbols, creating an emotional bond between users and their cultural roots, thereby embedding itself deeply in the users’ hearts. By understanding and triggering users’ intrinsic emotional needs, emotional design fosters a profound emotional connection with the brand, enhancing emotional identification and loyalty. This process significantly increases the brand’s attractiveness and market retention4. As a methodology, emotional design influences users’ cognition and attitudes in design and brand development by evoking emotional resonance. It enables brands to meet users’ needs on an emotional level and plays a crucial role in broader cultural dissemination. Through emotional design, brands can more effectively convey their cultural essence in a globalized context, facilitating the transition from regional cultural symbols to global cultural ambassadors5. This close integration of emotion and culture not only injects lasting vitality into rural cultural brands but also provides deep theoretical support and practical basis for their global promotion.

In the development of rural cultural brands, the expression and application of emotional design are crucial. By thoroughly analyzing users’ language expressions and emotional responses, designers can better understand and meet user needs and preferences, thereby optimizing design solutions. This emotion-driven design strategy not only enhances user experience but also, by building deep emotional connections, makes the brand an indispensable part of users’ lives. The successful application of emotional design not only secures a place for rural cultural brands in the global market but also provides a sustainable competitive advantage for their long-term development.

Literature review

In the development of rural cultural brands, the application of emotional design has been shown to play a crucial role in user experience and brand loyalty. Research on emotional design in consumer behavior reveals its significant impact on these areas. Safeer et al. proposed strategies for creating cultural brand assets from a consumer behavior perspective, emphasizing the importance of emotion in accumulating brand equity, particularly in enhancing brand identification and loyalty6. Lucia-Palacios and Pérez-López analyzed the impact of haptic speakers on perceptions of usefulness, interactivity, coolness, and intrusiveness, revealing the complex emotional responses consumers had towards technological products. They proposed that autonomy, by enhancing user interaction and perceived value, indirectly affected overall consumer experience, while excessive intrusiveness diminished positive emotional responses. This experiment underscored the importance of considering consumer emotional needs in the design of smart devices, highlighting how emotion-based design could significantly improve user experience and market acceptance7. Shukla et al. used neuro-marketing techniques to uncover the subconscious emotional responses behind consumer brand loyalty. Utilizing neuroimaging technology, the experiment explored the multi-layered effects of emotions on brand loyalty, demonstrating that specific emotions such as joy, trust, and nostalgia could influence consumer decision-making. By decoding the neural mechanisms underlying these emotional responses, thy validated the importance of emotion-driven design strategies in fostering brand loyalty8. In the realm of theoretical models of emotional design, Auernhammer and Roth emphasized the critical role of emotions in the data collection, idea generation, and idea testing stages through the design thinking process. They pointed out that direct user contact was essential to understanding their implicit needs and emotional responses, thereby designing products and services that meet users’ emotional needs9. Santos et al. explored the connection between community cultural development and tourism, finding that emotional design could promote tourism development by enhancing cultural identity, thus fostering the construction of rural cultural brands10. Märtin et al. provided a detailed analysis of the three-layer model of brand emotional design: the intuitive layer, the behavioral layer, and the reflective layer. They stressed the importance of brand influence on customer emotions in every interaction, enhancing brand loyalty through the creation of positive emotional connections11. Tan et al. focused on the construction logic of cultural brands and city memories in marine city brand building. Through case studies, they revealed how emotional design plays a role in brand shaping, strengthening emotional connections between the brand, city residents, and tourists12. Additionally, Papadopoulou et al. highlighted the importance of cultural authenticity in brand strategy, showing how emotions influence brand perception and long-term sustainability13. Previous research collectively reveals the multifaceted importance of emotional design in brand development. Through a systematic emotional design framework, it is possible to deeply optimize the emotional interaction between a brand and its users, thereby enhancing user satisfaction and brand loyalty. Emotional design plays a central role in evoking emotional resonance with the brand and establishes a strong and enduring emotional connection between the brand and its users. This foundational role underscores the irreplaceable strategic importance of emotional design in the process of brand building.

In recent years, an increasing number of researchers have explored and quantified the connection between emotions and brand building through complex algorithmic models, revealing the potential role of emotions in user experience and brand loyalty. These studies have systematically optimized emotional interactions between brands and users by constructing multi-layered emotional design frameworks, integrating data analysis and user feedback. Ultimately, this approach enhances user satisfaction and brand longevity. Emotional design has not only been deeply researched theoretically but has also continuously validated its effectiveness in practice through innovative methods, becoming an indispensable strategic element in brand building. Among these studies, Tricarico et al. constructed a conceptual model for cultural and creative enterprises (CCEs), exploring the challenges faced by the CCEs sector. They emphasized distinctions in Italy and Europe’s new policies and highlighted the importance of social innovation, collaborative efforts, artistic culture, and community engagement. Through three examples showcasing the implementation of platform spaces, they highlighted how this approach ensured accessibility and territorial development goals. This novel conceptual framework provided policy recommendations for the development of platform spaces in both theory and practice14. Duxbury delved into cultural and creative work in rural and remote areas through three storylines: cultural vitality, rural creative class, and rural creative economy. Over the past decade, these themes have interwoven into policies, advocating for comprehensive methods to promote artistic innovation and foster rural cultural creativity15. Zhou et al. explored rural cultural construction through the lens of creative design, focusing on the facets of “Intellectual Property” (IP) and its role in shaping the prospects of rural culture. They highlighted the global attention on rural cultural development and its integration into a robust “IP,” serving as a catalyst for rural revitalization16. Chen and Zhu used the case of Zoumatang Village in Ningbo, China, to discuss methods to enhance tourists’ aesthetic experience satisfaction. Through literature reviews and the Delphi method, they constructed an aesthetic experience evaluation framework. They utilized the Analytic Network Process (ANP) based on the Decision-Making Trial and Evaluation Laboratory (DEMATEL) for prioritization and proposed improvement measures. This study offers insights into enhancing aesthetic experiences in Zoumatang Village and provides methods and approaches for rural tourism development and comprehensive marketing17.

This study integrates artificial intelligence (AI) technology, text mining techniques, emotion simulation, and language simulation to devise the Design, Emotion, Region (DER) relational model. The aim of this study is to explore the application of emotion simulation, language generation, AI, and text mining technologies in rural cultural brand building. By constructing the DER model, the study seeks to deeply analyze user emotional responses and behavior patterns to optimize the design and promotion strategies of rural cultural products. Specifically, it is dedicated to uncovering the complex dynamic relationship between cross-cultural emotions and language features in brand interactions. The study provides a comprehensive and precise methodology to enhance the competitiveness and influence of rural cultural brands in the global market. Through the participation of virtual user groups, the experiment conducts an in-depth analysis of the emotional appeals and aesthetic preferences for different design styles in various countries and regions. This leads to a precise intelligent recommendation system that offers personalized design style suggestions for users in different areas.

Methods

DER model design process

To gain insights into the emotional preferences for different design styles and elements across various countries, driving the enhancement of rural cultural brand interests, this study integrates the EmoRuralSim simulator, emotion simulation technology, language simulation technology, AI technology, and text mining technology to generate a DER relational model. Together, these components construct a comprehensive system capable of accurately capturing and analyzing the relationship between user emotions and design elements. The application of the DER model in boosting rural cultural brand construction involves the organic fusion of multiple cutting-edge technologies, forming a highly sophisticated analytical system. At the core of this system lies the EmoRuralSim simulator, leveraging deep learning frameworks such as TensorFlow and PyTorch, and employing convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to precisely simulate the emotions and language features of users from multiple countries. The emotion simulation technology utilizes advanced emotion analysis algorithms to generate virtual user groups from 15 countries through the simulator. These groups, by reproducing emotional curves and behavioral patterns, authentically reflect emotional responses under different cultural backgrounds. Language simulation technology, facilitated by Natural Language Processing (NLP) and semantic analysis, reconstructs user comments and interactive behaviors to ensure the authenticity and diversity of simulation results. AI technology plays a role in optimization and intelligence by constructing intelligent recommendation systems using deep learning algorithms. These systems dynamically analyze the match between geographical regions and product style keywords, thereby offering personalized design recommendations. Text mining technology conducts detailed analyses of user comment data, employing techniques such as word vectorization, topic modeling, and sentiment classification to reveal deep associations between design elements and user emotions. Furthermore, the DER model introduces time-related factors and cross-emotional impact factors into simulation equations to comprehensively capture and analyze the dynamic changes and complex interactions of user emotions. This results in an intelligent system capable of real-time capturing and analyzing user emotional responses, thereby providing robust data support and optimization recommendations for rural cultural brand design.

The development of the EmoRuralSim simulator in this study is rooted in a deep analysis and innovative integration of existing multimodal emotion computation and NLP technologies. Its aim is to construct a highly accurate and adaptable virtual simulation platform for user emotion and language behavior. The simulator is not built from scratch but rather leverages the robust computational capabilities and flexibility of current open-source deep learning frameworks like TensorFlow and PyTorch. It combines cutting-edge CNN and RNN algorithms to create a comprehensive system capable of accurately capturing and analyzing the emotional and linguistic features of users from multiple countries. In the development process, the study first conducts thorough research on existing emotion generation algorithms and selects high-accuracy and robust sentiment lexicons and semantic analysis models—specifically, the Valence Aware Dictionary and sEntiment Reasoner (VADER) sentiment analysis model and Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT) embedding technology. These models provide a solid foundation for sentiment recognition and text understanding. The study then achieved precise modeling and dynamic updates of emotional states through the combination of MLP and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks. Building upon this foundation, the study employs data augmentation techniques to preprocess and expand large-scale multilingual corpora, ensuring the model’s generalizability across different languages and cultural backgrounds. The core architecture of the simulator is designed with considerations for high-concurrency processing and real-time responsiveness, optimizing computational efficiency through distributed computing and parallel processing technologies. Specifically, the study adopts a Parameter Server architecture to manage and synchronize large-scale model parameters, thereby reducing computational latency and communication overhead. Additionally, graph neural networks (GNNs) are utilized to enhance information propagation and aggregation capabilities in complex relational networks, thereby strengthening the interactive effects of emotion and language behaviors.

Analytical methods

Before deployment, the EmoRuralSim simulator undergoes rigorous validation processes, including cross-validation and leave-one-out validation, to ensure its accuracy and robustness. Specifically: in the initial validation phase, K-Fold cross-validation is employed with a learning rate of 0.001 and batch size of 32, over 1000 training epochs. The dataset is split into 80% training set and 20% validation set to assess initial model performance. In the mid-term validation phase, stricter leave-one-out validation is applied with a learning rate adjusted to 0.0005, batch size increased to 64, and 1500 training epochs. The dataset is split into 70% training set and 30% validation set to further optimize model parameters. In the final validation phase, more complex 10-Fold cross-validation is used with a reduced learning rate of 0.0001, batch size increased to 128, and 2000 training epochs. The dataset is split into 60% training set, 20% validation set, and 20% test set to evaluate the model’s robustness and generalization capability. Design of rural cultural brand platform and extraction of visual features of different styles

Cultural industry theory emphasizes the regional, historical, and diverse aspects of cultural products, stating that these products not only serve as economic commodities but also carry rich cultural connotations and historical memories. The 14th Five-Year Plan for Cultural Industry Development highlights the protection and innovation of traditional culture, advocating vigorous development of traditional handicrafts, which directly leads to traditional handicrafts being viewed as important design styles in this study. The Rural Revitalization Strategy Plan (2018–2022) focuses on promoting branding and cultural connotations of agricultural products, thus incorporating agricultural products into design styles. The National Rural Tourism Development Outline stresses the exploration of local characteristics to create tourism projects with cultural depth, supporting rural tourism experiences as design styles. General Secretary Xi Jinping’s important speeches on cultural confidence emphasize the protection and promotion of excellent traditional culture, supporting the design of folk cultural activities. The Ministry of Culture and Tourism’s opinions emphasize promoting the development of cultural creativity and art exhibitions, supporting rural art exhibitions and cultural creative studios. Therefore, these theories and policy documents extract six common design styles: traditional handicrafts, agricultural products, rural tourism experiences, folk cultural activities, rural art exhibitions, and cultural creative studios. In defining common design styles of rural cultural products, each style extracts only three artistic features based on principles of simplicity, the importance of emotional resonance, and practical considerations. The principle of simplicity emphasizes that excessive details in the information transmission process may lead to information overload, thereby weakening the prominence and memorability of key features. Focusing on three core features effectively conveys the essence of each design style and enhances emotional resonance, avoiding confusion caused by complexity. The selection of these specific features is based on their critical roles in emotional resonance and cultural heritage. For instance, in traditional handicrafts, “craftsmanship skills, historical tradition, and emotional connection of creators” not only embody the essence of craftsmanship but also evoke emotional resonance among viewers, highlighting their cultural value and artistic charm. Similarly, in agricultural products, “rural landscapes, agricultural culture, and seasonal changes” directly relate to the emotional experiences of the audience and core elements of rural life. Emotional resonance is a key factor in the success of cultural products. Choosing features that evoke strong emotional responses enhances the attractiveness and market value of products. Cultural elements with strong emotional resonance are more easily accepted and spread. This implies that extracting features with the strongest emotional resonance in design styles can enhance audience experience and engagement, thereby promoting the dissemination and identification of cultural brands. From a practical perspective, too many features may increase the complexity of product design and communication, leading to resource dispersion and reduced efficiency. Selecting three core features concentrates resources, ensuring each feature receives adequate display and communication, thereby achieving optimal promotional effects and user experiences. Therefore, by considering principles of simplicity, the importance of emotional resonance, and practicality, extracting three artistic features for each design style is not only theoretically sound but also offers significant advantages in practice compared to more complex designs. To avoid ambiguity, “rural cultural brands” specifically refer to rural cultural products, encompassing various forms of traditional handicrafts, agricultural products, rural tourism experiences, folk cultural activities, rural art exhibitions, and cultural creative studios. These products represent the diversity and richness of rural culture, aiming to enhance their market value and cultural influence through artistic design and brand building.

Discussion and conclusion

Overall, this study, through the innovative design of the EmoRuralSim simulator, systematically demonstrates the effectiveness of virtual emotional and language simulations in identifying user emotions and visual preferences, revealing the deep cultural roots and social influences behind these emotional expressions. The experimental results indicate that the EmoRuralSim simulator has broad applicability and efficiency across multiple national contexts, effectively capturing the complex interplay between cross-cultural emotions and design elements. Chinese users show a preference for design styles that blend traditional culture and craftsmanship, reflecting China’s rich historical and cultural traditions and emphasis on artisanal arts. The study suggests that in the Chinese market, it is beneficial to highlight the combination of historical heritage and modern innovation. Japanese users prefer styles characterized by traditional Japanese aesthetics, classical elements, and minimalism, which reflect the Zen-inspired aesthetics and minimalist approach prevalent in Japanese culture. In Korea, users focus on the fusion of fashion, modernity, and uniqueness, which aligns with Korea’s rapid modernization and the younger generation’s strong desire for self-expression. Brazilian users favor designs that embody festivity, warmth, and vibrant colors, mirroring Brazil’s rich festival culture and exuberant national character. The study suggests incorporating these elements into designs to enhance interactivity and engagement in the Brazilian market. Users from Russia, Japan, Germany, Korea, and Thailand highly value design elements related to rural architecture, cuisine, crafts, music, and performing arts, reflecting their expectations for richness and cultural integration. The intelligent recommendation system excels in accurately matching regional and product style keywords, effectively providing personalized user experiences and significantly improving design recommendation accuracy and user satisfaction. Text mining technology further deepens the analysis of user comment data, revealing the profound connections between design elements and regional user emotions. Overall, this study not only provides empirical evidence for the international dissemination of rural cultural brands but also offers valuable guidance for future brand design and promotion in a globalized context by integrating cultural characteristics from different countries.

Despite significant achievements in this study, there are still some limitations. Firstly, only representative countries from each continent were selected during user simulation, which does not cover the global diverse cultural backgrounds and aesthetic preferences. Secondly, while methods like word clouds provide visual analysis, they might not capture subtle variations in certain key terms, which calls for further improvement and in-depth research. Future research will expand the sample scope and research methods to obtain more comprehensive and accurate conclusions.

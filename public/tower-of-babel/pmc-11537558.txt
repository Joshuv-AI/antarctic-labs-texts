Repair of Scrotal Wounds after Extensive Excision of Extramammary Paget Disease with Thick Split-thickness Skin Graft
Authors: Yi Chen, Ke-Rong Tao, Zhao-He Luo, Guang-Feng Sun
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Summary:Extramammary Paget disease (EMPD) is a rare cutaneous malignant tumor. Currently, surgery is the most recommended treatment for EMPD. Here, we present a case of EMPD involving the groin, penis, and scrotum. The patient underwent two lesion resections. A thick split-thickness skin graft was utilized for scrotal reconstruction. After a 37-month follow-up period, no tumor recurrence was detected, and the outcome was deemed satisfactory.

Extramammary Paget disease (EMPD) is a rare cutaneous malignant tumor. The incidence rate in mainland China is about 0.4 cases per million individuals.1 EMPD most commonly affects the vulvar and perianal areas, and the scrotal area is less affected.2 In 1889, Crocker first reported a case of EMPD affecting the scrotum and penis.3 Early identification and thorough surgical excision are the key factors influencing prognosis.4 EMPD often occurs in anogenital areas, and patients are often ashamed to come to the hospital for treatment. Furthermore, the disease presents varied clinical manifestations that are both distinctive and atypical. Initial presentations typically consist of pruritic erythema or eczema-like changes,5 often mistaken for eczema and dermatitis, leading to delayed diagnosis and disease progression. In the past, the preferred approach for repairing large skin defects involved the use of free flaps,6 but it has been associated with several drawbacks. We selected a thick split-thickness skin graft (STSG) to repair and reconstruct the patient’s scrotum, resulting in a significant treatment effect.

CASE REPORT

A 67-year-old man presented to our clinic with recurrent EMPD of the penis, scrotum, and lower abdomen after undergoing resection at a local hospital 6 months ago. During the physical examination, the doctor observed that the patient had undergone skin grafting on the penis. There were eczema-like changes on the scrotum and penile foreskin, as well as erythema and depigmentation on the lower abdominal wall.

On September 29, 2019, an extensive resection of the lesion was performed, and the wound was repaired using a superficial circumflex iliac perforator (SCIP) flap combined with a thick STSG (Fig. 1A). No recurrence was observed 6 months after the surgery. On August 18, 2020, the patient returned to our institution with itching and erythema in the scrotal area, suggesting a local recurrence of EMPD on the scrotum (Fig. 1). On August 21, 2020, an extended resection was performed, involving the resection of the entire scrotum with a 2-cm margin around the tumor. Intraoperative rapid frozen pathology revealed that no tumor cells were found at the surgical margins. We used an automatic skin harvesting device (high surg30) to procure a thick STSG measuring a thickness of 0.7 mm, a length of 10 cm, and a width of 7 cm from the lateral side of the patient’s left thigh for scrotal reconstruction (Fig. 1B, C). The patient did not receive any adjuvant therapy after surgery. At 37 months postoperatively, there was no evidence of recurrence. The patient was satisfied with the treatment outcome (Fig. 1D).

DISCUSSION

EMPD is characterized by nonspecific symptoms such as erythema, itching, and vesicles, which are often misdiagnosed as eczema, psoriasis, and dermatitis.7 Surgery is a generally acknowledged therapeutic method. Resection of the lesion often results in obvious defects of the skin, subcutaneous tissue, and even the muscle in the corresponding areas. Surgeons have faced challenges in repairing these defects, managing the trauma, and promoting wound healing for a long time. Reconstruction approaches must consider both functional and cosmetic factors, and the impacts of scarring and prevention of long-term tumor recurrence. The SCIP flap has been widely used in repairing perineal tissue defects due to its inconspicuous placement, minimal damage to the donor area, and beneficial effect on the repair and reconstruction of the recipient site.8 Therefore, for lower abdominal wall lesions, we conducted thorough resection and repaired local defects with SCIP flaps. In the past, free flap transplantation has been utilized to repair and reconstruct scrotal skin defects.9 However, there are still some disadvantages associated with this method. A study indicated that using a thicker free flap for scrotal reconstruction may raise the temperature of the reconstructed scrotum above that of the normal scrotum, resulting in significant stagnation of scrotal spermatogenesis.10 Hence, it is imperative to select a comparatively thinner section of skin tissue external to the scrotum for scrotal reconstruction. Due to the challenges of survival with full thickness skin grafts and the tendency of thin STSGs to shrink, we opted for the innovative approach of using a thick STSG, which has the advantageous qualities of being more likely to survive and less prone to shrinkage. At present, there are few reports on the use of thick STSGs for scrotal reconstruction. The feasibility of reconstructing the scrotum by using a thick STSG was confirmed through prolonged postoperative follow-up. This makes it an optimal surgical approach for reconstructing the scrotum after excision.

CONCLUSIONS

In conclusion, EMPD of the scrotum and penis is a rare cutaneous malignant tumor. The skin defect area of the scrotum after the lesion resection is large. We utilized a thick STSG harvested from the thigh to reconstruct the scrotum, which has the advantages of a simple procedure, minimal injury, and satisfactory results after scrotal reconstruction. This may provide a novel approach to scrotal reconstruction.

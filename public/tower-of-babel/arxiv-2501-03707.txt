A Poincaré Lower Bound Approach for Performance Trade-offs in MIMO ISAC Systems with Blockage
Mohammadreza Bakhshizadeh Mohajer ∗ , Luca Barletta ∗ , Daniela Tuninetti † , Alessandro Tomasoni ∗∗ , Daniele Lo Iacono ∗∗ , and Fabio Osnato ∗∗

Abstract
Characterizing the performance trade-offs between sensing and communication subsystems is essential for enabling integrated sensing and communication systems. Various metrics exist for each subsystem; however, this study focuses on the ergodic capacity of the communication subsystem. Due to the complexity of deriving the sensing mean square error (MSE) and the inapplicability of the Bayesian Cramér-Rao Bound to channels with discrete or mixed distributions, this work proposes a Poincaré lower bound on the sensing MSE to address these issues. An achievable inner bound for the rate-sensing trade-off in a fading multiple-input multiple-output channel with additive white Gaussian noise and blockage probability is established. In addition, a strategy that is asymptotically optimal for sensing is provided.

Introduction

The 6G mobile communication system has emerged as a significant focus of global research, encompassing a range of innovative application scenarios, including smart cities, intelligent transportation, smart manufacturing, and smart homes
[
1
]
. Precise sensing is expected to evolve from an auxiliary function to a fundamental service among the notable features of 6G, thereby adding an additional dimension of capability to the network
[

4

There are various performance metrics for each individual system and based on their perspective. On one hand, the communication system is designed to maximize the information rate using an optimal transmitted signal while having knowledge of the channel. On the other hand, the sensing subsystem solves the problem of estimating the parameters of the channel while it has knowledge of the transmitted signal
[
5
]
. Future 6G vehicle-to-everything (V2X) is foreseen to explore millimeter wave (mmWave)
[
6
]
. Furthermore, the anticipated utilization of millimeter-wave (mmWave) and terahertz (THz) frequencies for sensing in 6G and next-generation wireless networks is foreseen
[
4

. These frequencies are subject to increased losses and are more susceptible to blockage
[
7
]
.

I-A

Previous Work

Motivated by integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) systems and propagation challenges in high frequencies, we study the performance trade-off between the two subsystems. The performance of the communication subsystem is typically evaluated by the ergodic capacity. Due to the challenging evaluation of the mean square error (MSE), which is the typical performance metric of the sensing subsystem, the Bayesian Cramér-Rao bound (BCRB) on the MSE has been adopted by previous work
[
5
]
.
In contrast to the classic Cramér-Rao bound (CRB), which depends on the actual value of the parameter being estimated, the BCRB utilizes the
a priori
distribution of the parameter and provides a global bound
[
8
]
. However, the issue with BCRB is that it is generally not tight and does not apply to scenarios involving discrete or mixed distributions
[
9
]
. To address this issue, in our previous work
[
10
]
, we proposed an alternative lower bound on the MSE of channel estimation based on a Poincaré-type inequality derived in
[
9
]
. Specifically, in
[
10
]
we computed a lower bound on the sensing MSE for multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) additive Gaussian channels with Rayleigh fading and blockage events.

I-B

Contributions

In this work, we compute an achievable sensing-rate region along with an outer bound for a MIMO ISAC system. Furthermore, we suggest an achievable strategy that is asymptotically sensing optimal for high sensing signal-to-noise ratio (SNR). Finally, through numerical simulations, we evaluate the inner bound and discuss the results.

II-A

System Model

Let us consider the general MIMO ISAC system model

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}_{i}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{H}_{i}\mathbf{X}+\mathbf{Z}_{i},\quad i\in\{\rm c,\rm s\}

\displaystyle f_{\mathbf{H}_{i}}(\mathbf{x})=(1-\alpha_{i})\delta(\mathbf{x})+\alpha_{i}\mathcal{CN}\left(\mathbf{x};{\bf 0},\sigma_{\mathsf{H}_{i}}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{N_{i}M}\right),i\in\{\mathrm{c},\mathrm{s}\}

\displaystyle{\text{tr}}\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[\mathbf{R}_{x}\right]\right)\leq MP_{0},\quad\mathbf{R}_{x}\triangleq T^{-1}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{\dagger}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times M}.

\mathbf{H}_{\rm s}

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle\triangleq T^{-1}{I}\left(\left.\mathbf{Y}_{\rm c};\mathbf{X}\,\right|\mathbf{H}_{\rm c}\right).

(7)

The sensing performance is the MSE

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle\triangleq{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|\mathbf{H}_{\rm s}-\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{\rm s}\|^{2}\right],

(8)

where
\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{\rm s}

\mathbf{H}_{\rm s}
based on
(\mathbf{Y}_{\rm s},\mathbf{X})
.

The sensing-rate region

consists of all feasible pairs
(\epsilon,R)

[
5
]
. The Pareto front
[
11
]

\displaystyle\min_{p_{\mathbf{X}}\in\cal{F}}\,\,\epsilon-\lambda T^{-1}{I}\left(\left.\mathbf{Y}_{\rm c};\mathbf{X}\,\right|\mathbf{H}_{\rm c}\right),

III-A

Sensing-Optimal Achieving Strategy

In
[
5
]
, it was shown that for an AWGN channel with known transmitted signal
\mathbf{X}
at the sensing receiver, the optimal sensing performance is achieved when the trace of the sample covariance matrix is deterministic, i.e.,
{\text{tr}}\left(\mathbf{R}_{x}\right)={\text{tr}}\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[\mathbf{R}_{x}\right]\right)

{\mathbf{R}_{x}}
is the one that minimizes the right-hand side (RHS) of (
19

\displaystyle\min_{p({\mathbf{R}_{x}})}\alpha_{\rm s}\frac{\sigma_{s}^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\text{tr}\left({\mathbf{R}_{x}}\right)\right)^{-1}\right],

(20)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\quad\text{tr}\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathbf{R}_{x}}\right]\right)=P_{0}M,{\mathbf{R}_{x}}\succ 0,{\mathbf{R}_{x}}=\mathbf{R}_{x}^{\dagger}.

(21)

We observe that
\frac{1}{\text{tr}\left({\mathbf{R}_{x}}\right)}

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{R}_{x}}\alpha_{\rm s}\frac{\sigma_{s}^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[(\text{tr}\left({\mathbf{R}_{x}}\right))^{-1}\right]\geq\min_{\mathbf{R}_{x}}\alpha_{\rm s}\frac{\sigma_{s}^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\text{tr}\left({\mathbf{R}_{x}}\right)\right]^{-1}.

The corresponding communication rate

\displaystyle R_{\textrm{SenseOpt}}=\max_{p_{\mathbf{X}}}T^{-1}{I}\left(\left.\mathbf{Y}_{\rm c};\mathbf{X}\,\right|\mathbf{H}_{\rm c}\right),\ \text{s.t.}\ {\text{tr}}\left(\mathbf{R}_{x}\right)=P_{0}M.

13

\mathbf{Y}_{\rm c}\mathbf{Y}_{\rm c}^{\dagger}
,

\displaystyle p(\lambda)=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\text{min}(N_{\rm c},T)}\lambda_{l}\right)}\left(\Pi_{l=1}^{\text{min}(N_{\rm c},T)}\lambda_{l}\right)^{|T-N_{\rm c}|}\Pi_{i<j}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}}{\Pi_{l=1}^{\text{min}(N_{\rm c},T)}\Gamma(T-l+1)\Gamma(N_{\rm c}-l+1)},

\displaystyle f_{l}(\lambda)

Communication-Optimal Achieving Strategy

The capacity-achieving strategy in the communication-only scenario is

\displaystyle R_{\textrm{max}}

\displaystyle=\max_{p_{\mathbf{X}}}\frac{\alpha_{\rm c}}{T}{I}\left(\left.\mathbf{Y}_{\rm c};\mathbf{X}\,\right|\mathbf{H}_{\rm c}\right),\quad\text{s.t.}\quad{\text{tr}}\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[\mathbf{R}_{x}\right]\right)\leq MP_{0}

IV-A

Discussion

From Fig.
1
we can see that the inner bound region is almost rectangular. This is due to the assumption
T\rightarrow\infty
. This assumption enabled us to greatly simplify the information rate expression, and therefore to find the information rate that satisfies the constraint on the trace of
\mathbf{R}_{x}
. Notice that, as
T\rightarrow\infty
, the distribution of the entries of
\mathbf{X}
approach independent
{\cal CN}(0,1)

[
14
]
, which is the distribution that achieves the communication-optimal point. Therefore, both sensing-optimal and communication-optimal points have the same value and they collapse on each other resulting in a rectangular region. As a future research direction, it is interesting to address the case of finite
T
.

V
Conclusion

In this work, we studied the performance trade-off between the sensing and communication subsystems of a multiple-input and multiple-output (MIMO) integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) system for an additive Gaussian channel with Rayleigh fading and blockage events, which is of great significance for next-generation wireless networks operating at millimeter wave. In such channels, the Bayesian Cramér-Rao bound (BCRB) is not applicable, because the a-priori distribution of the quantity to estimate is mixed discrete-continuous. Hence, we used a novel Poincaré-type lower bound on the sensing mean square error for the sensing part while the performance metric of the communication system is the ergodic capacity. Based on this, we proposed an achievable sensing-rate region along with an outer bound. We suggested an input distribution that is asymptotically optimal for sensing and studied its properties in the high signal-to-noise ratio regime and channel coherence time
T
approaching infinity. We observed that for the case of large values of
T
, since the transmitted signal approaches an independent and identically distributed circularly symmetric Gaussian distribution, the two corner points of the achievable region collapse on each other. This motivates the expansion of future research to examine the information rate for finite
T
and to explore the optimal signaling strategy in this context. Another promising research path is to study the ISAC system for the case where the channel matrix is a nonlinear function of a hidden parameter, such as in angle of arrival estimation, when BCRB cannot be calculated.

Backbone probability of planar Brownian motion
Gefei Cai, Zhuoyan Xie

Abstract
Motivated by critical planar percolation, we investigate a “backbone” event of planar Brownian motion, i.e. the existence of two disjoint subpaths on the Brownian trajectory connecting the ε \varepsilon -neighborhood of the starting point to a macroscopic distance. We show that the probability of this event is ( log ⁡ | log ⁡ ε | ) − 1 (\log|\log\varepsilon|)^{-1} up to a multiplicative constant.

Introduction

1.1
Overview and the main result

There is a strong relation between the planar Brownian motion and critical percolation. In particular, for
n\geq 1
, the Brownian intersection exponent
\zeta_{n}=\frac{4n^{2}-1}{12}

[
LSW01a
,
LSW01b
,
LSW02
]
is the same as the alternating
2n
-arm exponent of critical percolation
[
SW01
]
. This can be well understood in the view of conformal restriction
[
LSW03
]
: both of the
hulls
of Brownian motion and percolation cluster satisfy the restriction property, and therefore, their outer boundaries can both be described by Schramm-Loewner evolution (SLE) with parameter
\kappa=\frac{8}{3}
.

Note that in the critical percolation, one can also define the
monochromatic
arm exponents corresponding to that there exist
n
disjoint arms of the same color joining two boundaries of an annulus. Based on the relation between Brownian motion and critical percolation, it is natural to explore the analog of such monochromatic arm exponents in the context of Brownian motion. Recently,
[
NQSZ24
]
derives the exact value of percolation monochromatic 2-arm exponent, namely the
backbone exponent
. In this paper, we investigate its Brownian counterpart, and find that such Brownian backbone probability indeed has an iterated logarithmic decay (so the “Brownian backbone exponent” is equal to 0). This also shows a big difference between Brownian motion and critical percolation, although their outer boundaries (or hulls) are the same.

To be precise, let
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a planar Brownian motion starting from
0
. Denote
\mathbbm{S}^{1}:=\{z\in\mathbbm{C}:|z|=1\}
and
\mathbbm{D}:=\{z\in\mathbbm{C}:|z|<1\}
to be the unit circle and the unit disk, respectively. Let
\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}
be the first hitting time of
\mathbbm{S}^{1}
for
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.
For each
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, consider the event

\mathsf{Bac}_{\varepsilon}=\{\exists\ \text{two disjoint subpaths on the trajectory }B[0,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}]\text{ joining }\varepsilon\mathbbm{S}^{1}\text{ and }\frac{1}{2}\mathbbm{S}^{1}\}.

(1.1)

Namely,
\mathsf{Bac}_{\varepsilon}
happens if there are two continuous curves
\gamma^{1},\gamma^{2}:[0,1]\to\mathbbm{C}
such that
\gamma^{1}[0,1]\cap\gamma^{2}[0,1]=\emptyset
,
\gamma^{i}[0,1]\subset B[0,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}]
,
\gamma^{i}(0)\in\varepsilon\mathbbm{S}^{1}
, and
\gamma^{i}(1)\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbbm{S}^{1}
for
i=1,2
. Using terminology from percolation, we call
\mathsf{Bac}_{\varepsilon}
the
backbone event
for the Brownian trajectory
B[0,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}]
.

The main result in this paper is the following iterated logarithmic decay for the probability
\mathbbm{P}[\mathsf{Bac}_{\varepsilon}]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{10})
,

\frac{C_{1}}{\log|\log\varepsilon|}\leq\mathbbm{P}[\mathsf{Bac}_{\varepsilon}]\leq\frac{C_{2}}{\log|\log\varepsilon|}.

(1.2)

Remark 1.2
.

The same result also holds when
\frac{1}{2}\mathbbm{S}^{1}
in (
1.1
) is changed to any fixed
r\mathbbm{S}^{1}
with
r\in(0,1)
(and for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
), except that the corresponding constants in (
1.2
) will depend on
r
.

Note that the backbone event
\mathsf{Bac}_{\varepsilon}
is closely related to the spatial distribution of the
cut points
of
B[0,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}]
. Indeed, our proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on our recent paper with Fu and Sun
[
CFSX25
]
, from which we can define a layer structure for the cut points of
B[0,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}]
and then solve it explicitly; see Section
3
. We mention that
[
CFSX25
]
heavily relies on the connection between planar Brownian motion and
\SLE_{8/3}
, and especially their coupling with Liouville quantum gravity (LQG). It would be quite interesting to see if there is a derivation of Theorem
1.1
without relying on LQG.

Theorem
1.1
is related to a certain kind of special points of Brownian motion. Namely, let

\mathcal{B}:=\{B_{s}:s\in[0,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}})\text{ and there exists }\varepsilon>0\text{ such that }(B_{s+u})_{0\leq u\leq\varepsilon}\text{ does not have a cut point}\}.

Then Theorem
1.1
indicates that
\mathcal{B}
is non-empty and has Hausdorff dimension 2. Furthermore, it also suggests that the Hausdorff measure of
\mathcal{B}
with the gauge
r\mapsto r^{2}\log\frac{1}{r}\log\log\frac{1}{r}\log\log\log\frac{1}{r}
would exist and be non-trivial (since the Hausdorff gauge of
B[0,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}]
itself is
r\mapsto r^{2}\log\frac{1}{r}\log\log\log\frac{1}{r}
, see e.g.
[
LG85
]
). In particular, due to that planar Brownian motion a.s. has no double cut points
[
BL90
]
, we know that
\mathcal{B}
contains the set of double points of
(B_{t})_{0\leq t\leq\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}}
, which has the Hausdorff gauge
r\mapsto r^{2}(\log\frac{1}{r}\log\log\log\frac{1}{r})^{2}

[
LG87
]
.

1.2
Outlook and discussions

Here we give several remarks and related questions before going into the proof.

•

One can similarly consider the probability that there exist
2n
disjoint subpaths on
B[0,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}]
joining
\varepsilon\mathbbm{S}^{1}
and
\frac{1}{2}\mathbbm{S}^{1}
with
n\geq 2
. Note that for
n\geq 2
, such
2n
-arm probability is not straightforwardly related to the cut points of
B[0,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}]
. Instead, one need to consider the
(2n-1)
-tuples of the local cut points such that removing these
(2n-1)
cut points from the trajectory
B[0,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}]
,
0
and
B_{\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}}
are not in the same connected component of the remaining set. However, analyzing such tuples of local cut points (e.g. the counterpart of the layer structure in Section
3
) becomes much more complicated, and it seems difficult to solve them explicitly. We mention that similar difficulty also appears in deriving the monochromatic
k
-arm exponents of critical planar percolation for
k\geq 3
, see
[
NQSZ24
, Remark 2.3]
.

•

There is also a natural half-plane variant of our setup. Namely, let
(e_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the Brownian excursion on the upper half plane
\mathbbm{H}
from
0
to
\infty
, and define
R\mathbbm{S}^{+}:=R\mathbbm{S}^{1}\cap\mathbbm{H}
for
R>0
. Then for
n\geq 1
, consider the asymptotic probability that
there exists
(2n+1)
disjoint subpaths on the trajectory of
(e_{t})
joining
\mathbbm{S}^{+}
and
R\mathbbm{S}^{+}
as
R\to\infty
.
Note that the case
n=1
can be similarly related to the cut points of
(e_{t})
, which has been proven in
[
Vir03
, Theorem 4]
.

•

In our forthcoming work
[
CX25
]
, we will extend the result of this paper to the Brownian loop soup cases. Let
(B_{t})_{0\leq t\leq\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}}
be as before, and let
\mathcal{L}
be an independent Brownian loop soup on
\mathbbm{D}
with intensity
\frac{c}{2}
for
c\in(0,1]
.
For a subset
A\subset\overline{\mathbbm{D}}
, denote
\mathcal{C}(A)
to be the union of
A
and all loop-soup clusters in
\mathcal{L}
intersecting with
A
,
and let
H:=\mathcal{C}(B[0,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}])
.
However, unlike the Brownian motion case,
there is a positive probability that
0
itself can have two disjoint subpaths (except on
0
) on
H

connected to
\frac{1}{2}\mathbbm{S}^{1}
. Moreover, in
[
CX25
]
we will show the following

Theorem 1.3
.

We say
t\in(0,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}]
is a cut time of
H
if
\mathcal{C}(B[0,t])\cap\mathcal{C}(B[t,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}])=B_{t}
. Let
t_{1}
be the largest cut time of
H
such that the boundary of
\mathcal{C}(B[0,t_{1}])
is a simple loop, and denote
E
to be the event that
t_{1}
is the smallest cut time of
H
.
Then for
c\in(0,1)
,

\mathbbm{P}[E]=2^{\frac{c+1}{2}}\sqrt{\frac{6}{1-c}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau^{-1-\frac{c}{2}}e^{\frac{1-c}{6}\pi\tau}\eta(2i\tau)^{1-c}d\tau\right)^{-1}

(1.3)

where
\eta(2i\tau):=e^{-\frac{\pi}{6}\tau}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{-4\pi n\tau}\right)
is the Dedekind eta function.

Note that the right side of (
1.3
) tends to
1
as
c\uparrow 1
, and tends to
0
as
c\downarrow 0
.

Basic notations.

For two compact sets
A,B\subset\mathbbm{C}
, let
\mathrm{dist}(A,B):=\inf\{|a-b|:a\in A,b\in B\}
.
For a simple loop
\ell\subset\mathbbm{C}
, let
D(\ell)
be the bounded connected component of
\mathbbm{C}\backslash\ell
. For a simple loop
\ell\subset\mathbbm{C}
with
0\in D(\ell)
, we denote the conformal radius of
D(\ell)
seen from 0 by
\mathrm{CR}(\ell,0)
, i.e. if
f:\mathbbm{D}\to D(\ell)
is a conformal map that fixes the origin, then
\mathrm{CR}(\ell,0)=|f^{\prime}(0)|
.

For
0<r<1
, let
\mathbbm{A}_{r}
be the standard annulus
\{z\in\mathbbm{C}:r<|z|<1\}
. For each annular domain
A\subset\mathbbm{C}
,
there exists a unique
\tau>0
such that
A
and
\mathbbm{A}_{e^{-2\pi\tau}}
are conformally equivalent. We call
\tau
the
modulus
of
A
and write
\mathrm{Mod}(A):=\tau
. For two simple loops
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}
such that
\overline{D(\eta_{1})}\subset D(\eta_{2})
, we also write the modulus of the annular domain
D(\eta_{2})\setminus\overline{D(\eta_{1})}
as
\mathrm{Mod}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})
for simplicity.

We will frequently deal with elementary functions such as
\sqrt{x},\sinh(\sqrt{x}),\cosh(\sqrt{x})
, and
\tanh(\sqrt{x})
. In the following, we view them as functions defined on
\mathbbm{R}
by taking
i\sqrt{|x|},i\sin(\sqrt{|x|}),\cos(\sqrt{|x|})
, and
i\tan(\sqrt{|x|})
for
x<0
, respectively.

Conformal radii of
\SLE_{8/3}
-type loops

The
\SLE_{8/3}
loop measure on
\mathbbm{C}
is a canonical infinite measure on simple loops characterized by conformal restriction
[
Wer08
]
, which can be obtained by taking outer boundaries from the Brownian loop measure on
\mathbbm{C}
. According to
[
GM19
,
AHS23
]
, the
\SLE_{8/3}
loop measure on
\mathbbm{C}
also describes the welding interface when conformally welding two independent Brownian disks into a Brownian sphere.

Let
\SLE^{\mathrm{loop}}_{8/3,\mathbbm{D}}
be the
\SLE_{8/3}
loop measure on
\mathbbm{C}
restricted to the loops that are contained in
\mathbbm{D}
and surround 0.
By
[
ARS25b
]
,
\SLE_{8/3,\mathbbm{D}}^{\mathrm{loop}}
is the welding interface when comformally welding a Brownian disk and an independent Brownian annulus into a larger Brownian disk. This, combined with integrability of Liouville conformal field theory on the annulus by
[
Wu22
]
, gives the following

Theorem 2.1

(
[
ARS25b
]
)
.

Let
\mu_{\mathbbm{D}}
be a measure on simple loops contained in
\mathbbm{D}
and surrounding 0, such that
\frac{d\mu_{\mathbbm{D}}}{d\SLE^{\mathrm{loop}}_{8/3,\mathbbm{D}}}(\eta)=f(\mathrm{Mod}(\eta,\mathbbm{S}^{1}))
for some positive continuous function
f:\mathbbm{R}_{+}\to\mathbbm{R}_{+}
.
Then there is a constant
C>0
such that for each
\lambda\geq 0
,

\int\mathrm{CR}(\eta,0)^{\lambda}\mu_{\mathbbm{D}}(d\eta)=C\frac{\sqrt{12\lambda-1}}{\sinh\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\sqrt{12\lambda-1}\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(2\lambda-\frac{1}{6})\pi\tau}\eta(2i\tau)f(\tau)d\tau.

Here
\eta(2i\tau)=e^{-\frac{\pi}{6}\tau}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-e^{-4\pi n\tau})
is the Dedekind eta function.
When
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{12})
, the right side is defined by analytic continuation; see the end of Section
1
.

The proof of Theorem
2.1
is implicit in
[
ARS25b
]
, and we will provide a proof in Appendix

for completeness. In the following, we turn to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

3
The exact laws of the conformal radii

For the Brownian trajectory
B[0,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}]
, we say
t\in(0,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}})

is a
cut time
if
B[0,t)\cap B(t,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}]=\emptyset
, and the corresponding

B_{t}
is called a cut point.
Note that for any
\varepsilon>0
,
B[0,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}]
has infinitely many cut points contained in
\varepsilon\mathbbm{D}

[
Bur89
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(t_{i})_{i\geq 1}
be the collection of cut time
t

B[0,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}]
such that the outer boundary of
B[0,t]
is a simple loop, ranked in decreasing order.
Note that a.s.
\lim_{i\to\infty}t_{i}=0
.
For each
i\geq 1
, define
s_{i}
to be the last cut time before
t_{i}
(thus
t_{i}>s_{i}>t_{i+1}
). Let
\ell_{i}
and
\widetilde{\ell}_{i}
be the outer boundary of
B[0,t_{i}]
and the boundary of the connected component of
\mathbbm{D}\setminus B[s_{i},t_{i}]
containing the origin, respectively.

Figure 1:
Illustration of Definition
3.1
.

Let
f
be the conformal map from
D(\ell_{1})
to
\mathbbm{D}
with
f(0)=0
,

and let
\rho=f(\widetilde{\ell}_{1})

\mathrm{CR}(\rho,0)=\frac{\mathrm{CR}(\widetilde{\ell}_{1},0)}{\mathrm{CR}(\ell_{1},0)}
.
The main result of this section is the following exact law of
\mathrm{CR}(\rho,0)
, which is crucial to the final proof of Theorem
1.1

Proposition 3.2
.

For
\lambda\geq 0
, we have

\mathbbm{E}[\mathrm{CR}(\rho,0)^{\lambda}]=\frac{\sinh(\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{12\lambda-1})}{\sqrt{12\lambda-1}}-\frac{2\sqrt{3}\sinh(\frac{\pi}{3}\sqrt{12\lambda-1})}{\sqrt{12\lambda-1}\log\left(\frac{(2+\sqrt{3})^{2}+\tanh^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{12}\sqrt{12\lambda-1}\right)}{1+(2+\sqrt{3})^{2}\tanh^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{12}\sqrt{12\lambda-1}\right)}\right)}.

(3.1)

When
\lambda\in[0,\frac{1}{12})
, the right side of (
3.1
) is defined by analytic continuation; see the end of Section
1
.

The proof of Proposition
3.2
relies on the exact solvability of
\mathrm{CR}(\ell_{i},0)
for each

in Definition

\mathrm{CR}(\ell_{1},0)
is originally derived by
[

[

Lemma 3.3
.

For
\lambda\geq 0
, we have

\mathbbm{E}[\mathrm{CR}(\ell_{1},0)^{\lambda}]=\frac{\sqrt{12\lambda-1}}{\sinh(\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{12\lambda-1})}.

(3.2)

When
\lambda\in[0,\frac{1}{12})
, the right side of (

\mathbbm{P}[\mathrm{CR}(\ell_{1},0)<\varepsilon]=\frac{16}{3\pi}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}(1+o(1))
as

.

Let
\nu_{\mathbbm{D}}
be the law of the loop chosen from the counting measure over
\{\ell_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}

\nu_{\mathbbm{D}}
is such that
\int F(\eta)\nu_{\mathbbm{D}}(d\eta)=\mathbbm{P}\left[\sum_{i\geq 1}F(\ell_{i})\right]

Lemma 3.4
.

There is a constant
C>0
such that for
\lambda\geq 0
, we have

\int\mathrm{CR}(\eta,0)^{\lambda}\nu_{\mathbbm{D}}(d\eta)=C\frac{\sqrt{12\lambda-1}}{\sinh(\frac{\pi}{3}\pi\sqrt{12\lambda-1})}\log\left(\frac{(2+\sqrt{3})^{2}+\tanh^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{12}\sqrt{12\lambda-1}\right)}{1+(2+\sqrt{3})^{2}\tanh^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{12}\sqrt{12\lambda-1}\right)}\right).

(3.3)

When
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{12})
, the right side of (

Proof.

By combining

\int\mathrm{CR}(\eta,0)^{\lambda}\nu_{\mathbbm{D}}(d\eta)=C\frac{\sqrt{12\lambda-1}}{\sinh(\frac{\pi}{3}\sqrt{12\lambda-1})}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(2\lambda-\frac{1}{6})\pi\tau}\frac{\eta(2i\tau)}{\tau}d\tau.

(3.4)

for some constant

.
Note that for

Therefore, we obtain

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-a\tau}\frac{\eta(2i\tau)}{\tau}d\tau=\log\left(\frac{(2+\sqrt{3})^{2}+\tanh^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{6}a}\right)}{1+(2+\sqrt{3})^{2}\tanh^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{6}a}\right)}\right).

for

To obtain Proposition
3.2
from Lemmas

\{\ell_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
, which is a radial variant of the Brownian beads decomposition established in

Lemma 3.5
.

We have:

(i)

Let
g:D(\ell_{1})\to\mathbbm{D}
be the conformal map such that
g(0)=0
and
g(B_{t_{1}})=1
. Then
g(\widetilde{\ell}_{1})
is independent of
\ell_{1}
. In particular,
\mathrm{CR}(\rho,0)
is independent of
\ell_{1}
.

(ii)

Let
\widetilde{g}:D(\widetilde{\ell}_{1})\to\mathbbm{D}
be the conformal map such that
\widetilde{g}(0)=0
and
\widetilde{g}(B_{s_{1}})=1
. Then
(\widetilde{g}(B_{t}))_{t\in[0,s_{1}]}
is independent of
(B_{t})_{t\in[s_{1},\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}]}
, and has the same law (up to a time change) as
(B_{t})_{t\in[0,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}]}
conditioned on
B_{\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}}=1
.

As a corollary, if we set
\widetilde{\ell}_{0}=\mathbbm{S}^{1}
, then
\left(\frac{\mathrm{CR}(\widetilde{\ell}_{n},0)}{\mathrm{CR}(\ell_{n},0)}\right)_{n\geq 1}
and
\left(\frac{\mathrm{CR}(\ell_{n+1},0)}{\mathrm{CR}(\widetilde{\ell}_{n},0)}\right)_{n\geq 0}
are two i.i.d. r.v. sequences, and mutually independent of each other.

Proof.

We first prove (ii). Due to rotational invariance, it suffices to consider the case when conditioning on
B_{\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}}=1
.
Let
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the time reversal of
(B_{t})_{t\in[0,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}]}
conditioned on
B_{\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}}=1
. Note that
Y_{t}
is indeed a Brownian excursion in
\mathbbm{D}
from 1 to 0.
Let
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the natural filtration of
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, and
(\mathcal{G}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the cut time filtration defined in
[
Vir03
, Section 3]
(i.e.
\mathcal{G}_{t}
is the
\sigma
-field generated by
\mathcal{F}_{t}
and the set of all cut times of
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
before time
t
).
By
[
Vir03
, Proposition 14]
, if
\tau
is a
(\mathcal{G}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
-stopping time such that
\tau
is also a cut time of
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, then the conditional law of
(Y_{\tau+u})_{u\geq 0}
given
\mathcal{G}_{\tau}
is a Brownian excursion from
Y_{\tau}
to 0 in the connected component of
\mathbbm{D}\backslash Y[0,\tau]
containing
0
. Now we consider
s_{1}^{\prime}:=\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}-s_{1}
. Note that

s_{1}^{\prime}
is the first cut time of
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
such that
Y[0,s^{\prime}_{1}]
disconnects the origin from
\mathbbm{S}^{1}
, thus is a
(\mathcal{G}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
-stopping time. Therefore, the conditional law of
(\widetilde{g}(Y_{s_{1}^{\prime}+u}))_{u\geq 0}
given
\mathcal{G}_{s_{1}^{\prime}}
is an independent Brownian excursion (up to a time change) in
\mathbbm{D}
from
1
to
0
. By taking the time reversal of
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, we obtain the result.

Then we prove (i).
Let
A\subset\overline{\mathbbm{D}}
be relatively compact such that
\mathbbm{D}\backslash A
is simply connected and contains
0
. Note that the law of
(B_{t})_{t\in[0,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}]}
conditioned on the event
B[0,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}]\cap A=\emptyset
is the same as the Brownian motion (up to a time change) in
\mathbbm{D}\backslash A
starting from
0
and conditioned on exiting
\mathbbm{D}\backslash A
at
\mathbbm{S}^{1}\setminus A
. Combined with the conformal invariance of the Brownian motion, it then implies that
g(\widetilde{\ell}_{1})
is independent of the event
B[0,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}]\cap A=\emptyset
. By varying
A
, we conclude that
g(\widetilde{\ell}_{1})
is independent of the outer boundary of
B[0,\tau_{\mathbbm{D}}]
, and hence
\ell_{1}
.
∎

Now Proposition
3.2
follows by combining Lemma
3.5
and Lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
above.

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

By Lemma
3.5
and the definition of
\nu_{\mathbbm{D}}
, we have

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{CR}(\eta,0)^{\lambda}\nu_{\mathbbm{D}}(d\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}\mathbbm{E}[\mathrm{CR}(\ell_{n},0)^{\lambda}]=\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}\mathbbm{E}\left[\left(\prod\limits_{k=2}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{CR}(\ell_{k},0)}{\mathrm{CR}(\widetilde{\ell}_{k-1},0)}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{CR}(\widetilde{\ell}_{k-1},0)}{\mathrm{CR}(\ell_{k-1},0)}\right)^{\lambda}\mathrm{CR}(\ell_{1},0)^{\lambda}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}\mathbbm{E}[\mathrm{CR}(\ell_{1},0)^{\lambda}]^{n}\mathbbm{E}[\mathrm{CR}(\rho,0)^{\lambda}]^{n-1}=\frac{\mathbbm{E}[\mathrm{CR}(\ell_{1},0)^{\lambda}]}{1-\mathbbm{E}[\mathrm{CR}(\ell_{1},0)^{\lambda}]\mathbbm{E}[\mathrm{CR}(\rho,0)^{\lambda}]}.

Thus, combined with Lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}[\mathrm{CR}(\rho,0)^{\lambda}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathbbm{E}[\mathrm{CR}(\ell_{1},0)^{\lambda}]}-\frac{1}{\nu_{\mathbbm{D}}[\mathrm{CR}(\eta,0)^{\lambda}]}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sinh(\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{12\lambda-1})}{\sqrt{12\lambda-1}}-\frac{\sinh(\frac{\pi}{3}\sqrt{12\lambda-1})}{C\sqrt{12\lambda-1}\log\left(\frac{(2+\sqrt{3})^{2}+\tanh^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{12}\sqrt{12\lambda-1}\right)}{1+(2+\sqrt{3})^{2}\tanh^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{12}\sqrt{12\lambda-1}\right)}\right)}.

(3.5)

Here the constant
C
is the same as the one in Lemma
3.4
.
Now, note that (
3.5
) becomes
\frac{e^{\pi\sqrt{3\lambda}}}{4\sqrt{3\lambda}}(1-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}C})(1+o(1))
as
\lambda\to\infty
. Since we must have
\mathbbm{E}[\mathrm{CR}(\rho,0)^{\lambda}]\to 0
as
\lambda\to\infty
, we find

. Taking this into (
3.5
), we conclude.
∎

As a corollary of Proposition
3.2
, by the following special case of Tauberian theorem, we can derive the tail probabilities of
\mathrm{CR}(\rho,0)
and
\mathrm{CR}(\widetilde{\ell}_{1},0)

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose
\mathcal{X}
is a positive random variable such that
\mathbbm{E}[1-e^{-\lambda\mathcal{X}}]=\frac{C}{|\log\lambda|}(1+o(1)),\ \lambda\downarrow 0
for some constant
C>0
. Then
\mathbbm{P}[\mathcal{X}>t]=\frac{C}{\log t}(1+o(1))
as
t\to\infty
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathbbm{E}[1-e^{-\lambda\mathcal{X}}]\geq(1-e^{-\lambda t})\mathbbm{P}[\mathcal{X}>t]
for
t>0
. Fix
M>0
and take
\lambda=\frac{M}{t}
, by letting
t\to\infty
, then we find
\varlimsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\log t\cdot\mathbbm{P}[\mathcal{X}>t]\leq C(1-e^{-M})^{-1}
. Since
M>0
is arbitrary, we have
\varlimsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\log t\cdot\mathbbm{P}[\mathcal{X}>t]\leq C
. On the other hand, note that for
t>1
,

\log t\cdot\mathbbm{E}[1-e^{-\lambda\mathcal{X}}]=\log t\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda e^{-\lambda s}\mathbbm{P}[\mathcal{X}>s]ds\leq\log t\cdot(1-e^{-\lambda t})+\log t\cdot e^{-\lambda t}\mathbbm{P}[\mathcal{X}>t]

(3.6)

Choose
\alpha>1
. Taking
\lambda=t^{-\alpha}
and then
t\to\infty
in (
3.6
) yields
\varliminf\limits_{t\to\infty}\log t\cdot\mathbbm{P}[\mathcal{X}>t]\geq\alpha^{-1}C
. The lemma then follows by taking
\alpha\downarrow 1
.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

We have
\mathbbm{P}[\mathrm{CR}(\rho,0)<\varepsilon]=\frac{3}{\log|\log\varepsilon|}(1+o(1))
and
\mathbbm{P}[\mathrm{CR}(\widetilde{\ell}_{1},0)<\varepsilon]=\frac{3}{\log|\log\varepsilon|}(1+o(1))

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2
, when

\mathbbm{E}[\mathrm{CR}(\rho,0)^{\lambda}]=\frac{\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{1-12\lambda})}{\sqrt{1-12\lambda}}-\frac{2\sqrt{3}\sin(\frac{\pi}{3}\sqrt{1-12\lambda})}{\sqrt{1-12\lambda}\log\left(\frac{(2+\sqrt{3})^{2}-\tan^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{12}\sqrt{1-12\lambda}\right)}{1-(2+\sqrt{3})^{2}\tan^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{12}\sqrt{1-12\lambda}\right)}\right)}.

In particular,

Hence

Proof of Theorem

Now we turn to the upper bound of

Lemma 4.3

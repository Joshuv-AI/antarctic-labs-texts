Third harmonic-mediated amplification in TWPA
E. Rizvanov, S. Kern, P. Neilinger, M. Grajcar

Abstract
In Josephson Traveling-Wave Parametric Amplifiers, higher-order harmonics of the pump tone and its sidebands are commonly present and typically regarded as parasitic. Consequently, most design efforts have focused on suppressing these harmonics. In spite of that, motivated by transient simulations, we extend the coupled-mode theory and demonstrate that, contrary to conventional belief, the third harmonic can enhance amplifier performance, improving both gain and bandwidth. We show that the recently developed plasma oscillation-based amplifier is particularly well-suited for exploiting this effect. Their dispersion relation enables us to observe the phenomenon in transient numerical simulations using JoSIM and WRspice. These simulations reveal improvement of the amplifier’s performance, specifically the doubling of the bandwidth and an increase in the gain.

As a result, an idler mode is generated, enabling the energy transfer from the pump to the signal in a broadband and linear manner. The efficiency of this process depends critically on the phase relations among the interacting waves, as additional nonlinear phase shifts can degrade the gain and bandwidth.

To address the phase mismatch, dispersion engineering techniques—such as introducing band gaps or resonant features—have been employed to modify the dispersion relation. Placing the pump frequency near these features mitigates phase mismatch and enables optimal gain and bandwidth, although this often comes at the expense of impedance matching,
Kow and Bell 2025

Kern et al. 2023

;

Again, dispersion engineering is typically utilized to suppress such generation by placing unwanted harmonics within bandgaps or by preventing the process through introduced phase mismatch.

The second and third pump’s harmonics can be effectively suppressed using this technique. However, applying the same approach to sidebands generation, such as the pump-signal sum frequency
f_{p}+f_{s}
, is difficult as the suppression must be broadband to maintain the amplifier’s bandwidth. As demonstrated throughout this work, these sidebands are shown to be particularly detrimental to the gain. Surprisingly, including the third harmonic, especially when its generation is phase-matched, can mitigate the negative effects of these sidebands. Furthermore, this leads to a novel amplification mechanism that relies on the aforementioned sideband.

In this work, we report the study of this novel amplification mechanism that is observed in time-domain numerical simulations of a recently proposed traveling-wave parametric amplifier based on plasma oscillations (PTWPA).

We discuss how the PTWPA design is particularly well-suited for realizing this amplification mechanism, as supported by dispersion analysis and frequency-domain modeling based on the extended CMT.

For a qualitative understanding of the nonlinear processes in the studied TWPA, the CMT is utilized. In CMT, the junction array is approximated as a continuous nonlinear waveguide, which can be described by a nonlinear wave equation governing the current
I(x,t)
as a function of space and time
Malnou et al. 2024
;
Malnou et al. 2021
;
Kern et al. 2023
:

v_{p}^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}I(x,t)}{\partial z^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}I(x,t)}{\partial t^{2}}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\epsilon I(x,t)^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\xi I(x,t)^{3}\right),

(1)

where the coefficients of second and third-order nonlinearity are given by:

\xi=\frac{1}{(I_{*}^{2}+I_{d}^{2})},\ \epsilon=\frac{2I_{d}}{(I_{*}^{2}+I_{d}^{2})},\ I_{*}=\sqrt{2}I_{c}.

(2)

Here,
I_{c}
is the junction critical current and
I_{d}
is the applied DC current.

The equation is solved using a plane wave ansatz with spatially dependent current amplitudes:

I(x,t)=\sum_{\alpha}\frac{1}{2}I_{\alpha}(x)\exp(i(k_{\alpha}x - \omega_{\alpha} t))+C.C.

(3)

The theory is based on the evolution of the amplitudes
I_{\alpha}(x)
resulting from nonlinear effects. The nature of the plane waves
\alpha
is encoded in their dispersion, which enters the theory as phase mismatches for each process, represented by mixing terms in ODE system resulting from substituting the ansatz in the wave equation (for details see Section S4 in the supplementary material). Finally, the power of each harmonic was calculated as

P_{\alpha}=10\log_{10}\left(\frac{\frac{1}{2}\left|I_{\alpha}\right|^{2}Z}{1~\textrm{mW}}\right),

(4)

where
Z=50~\Omega
is the characteristic impedance.

Before presenting a detailed model of the simulated structure and its dispersion via the CMT (see Section S3 and Section S4 in the supplementary material), a simplified system is analyzed in order to identify and understand the individual effects responsible for the gain. It turns out that the influence of the third harmonic can be captured within this framework, provided CMT is extended beyond the standard modes, namely:
f_{p}
, signal
f_{s}
, and idler
f_{i}

f_{p+s}
and
f_{2p-s}

3f_{p}

In the standard three-wave mixing (3WM) model involving only the pump, signal, and idler, the signal power exhibits high growth along the TWPA, even without any phase matching. This is a feature of 3WM systems, which suppresses the detrimental effects of nonlinear phase modulation.
Malnou et al. 2021
However, as previously reported,
Dixon et al. 2020
;
Dixon 2022
extending the model to include the additional sidebands results in a substantial gain decrease (blue and violet curves in Fig.
4
), even down to zero
when both
f_{p}+f_{s}
and
2f_{p}-f_{s}
are present (yellow curve in Fig.

In addition, THG arises naturally in systems governed by cubic nonlinearity and is typically intentionally suppressed, as it depletes the pump power, reducing available gain. Nevertheless, the efficiency of this process is highly dependent on the underlying dispersion relation. By adopting a linear dispersion relation with an additional phase offset
\theta
applied to the third harmonic, we can track how phase matching affects its generation efficiency and finally the signal gain.

Surprisingly, including the third harmonic in the coupled-mode model leads to a recovery of signal gain (green dashed line in Fig.
4
(b), even in the presence of sidebands and without THG phase matching. However, when the phase offset
\theta
is tuned in order to improve its phase-matching, the third harmonic generation is enhanced (thin green line), resulting in a noticeable improvement in signal amplification (thick solid green line).

To track the mechanism behind the observed phenomenon, we systematically removed individual interaction terms on the right-hand side of CMT-3 equations (see Section S4 in the supplementary material) and examined how these changes affected the correlation between the signal gain and THG. The remaining terms, whose removal caused the disappearance of the THG vs gain correlation defined the minimal set of equations describing the amplification process, presented in Eqs. (S22-S26) in Section S5 of the supplementary material. The essential modes included in this reduced model are the signal, the idler, the pump, the third harmonic, and the two sidebands at frequencies
f_{p}+f_{s}
and
2f_{p}-f_{s}
.

Further insight can be gained by applying the common undepleted-pump approximation
d\left|I_{p}\right|/dz=0
, and extending it to the third harmonic,
d\left|I_{3p}\right|/dz=0
. Under these assumptions, only the phases of these modes evolve due to self- and cross-phase modulation. These modulations can be captured analytically (see Eq. S27 in the supplementary material ) and substituted back into the remaining coupled mode equations, with the amplitude of the pump and the third harmonic as parameters.

The resulting model takes the form

\begin{pmatrix}Y_{s}\\
Y_{i}\\
Y_{p+s}\\
Y_{2p-s}\end{pmatrix}^{\prime}=i\frac{k_{s}\epsilon I_{p}}{4}\hat{A}\begin{pmatrix}Y_{s}\\
Y_{i}\\
Y_{p+s}\\
Y_{2p-s}\end{pmatrix},

(5)

where the interaction matrix is given as

\hat{A}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{k_{p}}{k_{s}}\iota_{p}&1&1&0\\
-\frac{k_{p-s}}{k_{s}}&-\frac{k_{p}}{k_{s}}\iota_{p}&0&0\\
\frac{k_{p+s}}{k_{s}}&0&3\frac{k_{p}}{k_{s}}\iota_{p}&\frac{k_{p+s}}{k_{s}}\iota_{3p}\\
0&0&-\frac{k_{2p-s}}{k_{s}}\iota_{3p}&\delta\end{pmatrix},

(6)

with
\delta=3\frac{k_{p}}{k_{s}}\iota_{p}\left(1-2\iota_{3p}^{2}\right)-\frac{\xi\theta}{k_{s}\epsilon^{2}\iota_{p}}
,
\theta
is the offset of the third harmonic,
\iota_{p}=\frac{\xi}{4\epsilon}I_{p}
, and
\iota_{3p}=I_{3p}/I_{p}
.
Diagonalizing the matrix
A
provides the solution in the form

\vec{Y}(x)=\sum_{i}c_{i}\vec{v}_{i}e^{i\frac{k_{s}\epsilon}{4}I_{p}\lambda_{i}x},

(7)

where
\vec{v}_{i}
and
\lambda_{i}
are the eigenvectors and the eigenvalues of
\hat{A}
, respectivelly, and the coefficients
c_{i}
satisfies the initial condition. The real part of
\lambda_{i}
corresponds to oscillatory behavior, whereas the imaginary part corresponds to amplification (
\real(\lambda_{i}>0)
) or attenuation (
\real(\lambda_{i}<0)
).

As shown in Fig.
4
(c), the gain increases significantly with the third-harmonic amplitude
\iota_{3p}
(green curve). Simultaneously, one of the eigenvalues develops an increasingly positive real part (black curve), which strongly correlates with the signal gain.

Analysis of the interaction matrix reveals that the third harmonic plays a crucial role in mediating the coupling between the
f_{p+s}
and
f_{2p-s}
sidebands. Specifically, it introduces an additional pathway for generating these modes. When the third harmonic is weak, generation of
f_{p+s}
occurs primarily at the expense of the signal, thereby reducing the overall gain. In contrast, a strong third harmonic facilitates simultaneous exponential growth of all involved modes, leading to enhanced amplification; a behavior that is also clearly observed in the transient simulations.

To conclude, in this work, we numerically demonstrated that the proper phase matching of third-harmonic generation can significantly enhance the performance of a traveling-wave parametric amplifier. Specifically, the phase-matched THG doubles its bandwidth, increases the gain of the amplifier. However, we also showed that amplification in the absence of THG is more efficient than when THG is present but not phase-matched. These results are thus consistent with previous studies and provide an understanding of THG’s role in amplification efficiency and bandwidth.

We have extended the coupled-mode theory to incorporate both the second and third-order nonlinearities and analysed the processes up to the generation of the third pump harmonic. Within this framework, we identify the most detrimental process for amplification as the generation of the
f_{p+s}
harmonic. We demonstrate that phase-matched third-harmonic generation can effectively mitigate this suppression, thereby improving the overall gain profile of the TWPA.

Tessellations of an affine apartment by affine weight polytopes
Claudio Bravo, Auguste Hébert, Diego Izquierdo, Benoit Loisel

Abstract
Let 𝔸 \mathbb{A} be a finite dimensional vector space and Φ \Phi be a finite root system in 𝔸 \mathbb{A} . To this data is associated an affine poly-simplicial complex. Motivated by a forthcoming construction of connectified higher buildings, we study “affine weight polytopes” associated to these data. We prove that these polytopes tesselate 𝔸 \mathbb{A} . We also prove a kind of “mixed” tessellation, involving the affine weight polytopes and the poly-simplical structure on 𝔸 \mathbb{A} .



1
Introduction

Tessellation of
\mathbb{A}
by affine weight polytopes

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a finite dimensional vector space. A finite (reduced) root system in
\mathbb{A}
is a finite set
\Phi
of linear forms on
\mathbb{A}
, called roots, satisfying certain conditions (see
[
Bou81
, VI, §1]
).
The associated affine (locally finite) hyperplane arrangement is
\mathcal{H}=\{\alpha^{-1}(\{k\})\mid(\alpha,k)\in\Phi\times\mathbb{Z}\}
. The elements of
\mathcal{H}
are called affine walls. The hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{H}
equips
\mathbb{A}
with the structure of a poly-simplicial complex, whose maximal poly-simplices - the alcoves - are the connected components of
\mathbb{A}\setminus\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal{H}}H
.
To each affine wall
H
is naturally associated a reflection
s_{H}
. Let
W^{\mathrm{aff}}
be the affine Weyl group associated with
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
, i.e.,
W^{\mathrm{aff}}
is the group generated by the
s_{H}
, for
H\in\mathcal{H}
. This is naturally a Coxeter system and every closed alcove
\overline{C}
is a fundamental domain for the action of
W^{\mathrm{aff}}
on
\mathbb{A}
.
Fix a “fundamental alcove”
C_{f}
and pick one element
\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{b}_{C_{f}}
of
C_{f}
. Then for every alcove
C
, the set
C\cap W^{\mathrm{aff}}.\mathbf{b}
contains exactly one element, that we denote
\mathbf{b}_{C}
. Denote by
\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})
the set of vertices of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
, i.e., the set of zero-dimensional faces of the associated poly-simplicial complex. For
F
a face of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
, we set

\mathcal{F}_{F}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{b},F}=\mathrm{Int}_{r}(\mathrm{conv}(W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F}.\mathbf{b}_{C})),

where
W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F}
is the fixator of
F
in
W^{\mathrm{aff}}
,
C
is any alcove whose closure contains
F
,
\mathrm{conv}
denotes the convex hull, and given a subset
E\subset\mathbb{A}
,
\mathrm{Int}_{r}(E)
denotes the relative interior of
E
(that is the interior of
E
in the affine subspace spanned by
E
). When
F
is
0
-dimensional, i.e., when
F=\{\lambda\}
for some
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})
, we usually write
\mathcal{P}(\lambda)
instead of
\mathcal{F}_{\{\lambda\}}
.
Our first main result is the following (see Figures
3.6
,
3.7
,
3.8
and
3.9
):

Theorem 1.1
.

(see Propositions
3.24
,
3.27
and Theorem
3.28
)

1.

Let
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})
. Then the assignment
F\mapsto\mathcal{F}_{F}
is a bijection between the set of faces of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
whose closure contains
\lambda
and the set of faces of
\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}
.

2.

Let
F
be a face of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
. Then we have
\overline{\mathcal{F}_{F}}=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(F)}\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}
, where
\mathrm{vert}(F)
denotes the set of vertices of
F
.

3.

Let
\mathscr{F}
denote the set of faces of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
. Then
\mathbb{A}=\bigsqcup_{F\in\mathscr{F}}\mathcal{F}_{F}.

Thickened tessellation of
\mathbb{A}

We then study a variant of the tessellation of
\mathbb{A}
given by Theorem
1.1
(3).
Let
\eta\in]0,1]
. Let
x\in\mathbb{A}
. Let
F=F_{\mathcal{P}}(x)
be the face of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
such that
\mathcal{F}_{F}
contains
x
(the existence and uniqueness of such a face is provided by Theorem
1.1
(3)). Set
F_{\eta}(x)=(1-\eta)F+\eta x=\{(1-\eta)y+\eta x\mid y\in F\}
. For
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})
, we denote by
h_{\lambda,\eta}:\mathbb{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}
the homothety of
\mathbb{A}
with center
\lambda
and radius
\eta
. Then
\overline{{F_{\eta}(x)}}
is a “copy” of
\overline{F}
containing exactly one point of
h_{\lambda,\eta}(\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)})
, namely
h_{\lambda,\eta}(x)
, for each
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(F)
. This is the convex hull of these points. Then we prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\eta\in]0,1]
. Then
\mathbb{A}=\bigsqcup_{x\in\mathbb{A}}\overline{F_{\eta}(x)}
.

We deduce the following corollary, which is easier to interpret (see Figures
4.4
and
4.5
):

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\eta\in]0,1]
. Then we have

\mathbb{A}=\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})}h_{\lambda,\eta}\left(\mathcal{P}\left(\lambda\right)\right)\sqcup\bigsqcup_{x\in\bigcup_{\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})}\mathrm{Fr}(\mathcal{P}(\lambda))}\overline{F_{\eta}(x)}.

Note that Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
provide some kinds of tessellations of
\mathbb{A}
involving homothetic images of the
\mathcal{P}(\lambda)
and homothetic images of the faces of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
.

The polytopes
\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}
that we study already appear in the litterature, in various contexts of representation theory. If
\lambda\in\mathbb{A}
, then we can write
\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}=\lambda+\overline{\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\lambda}}
, where
\overline{\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\lambda}}=\mathrm{conv}\{w.\mathbf{b}^{\prime}\mid w\in W^{v}_{\lambda}\}
, where
W^{v}_{\lambda}
is the finite Weyl group associated with the root system
\Phi_{\lambda}=\{\alpha\in\Phi\mid\alpha(\lambda)\in\mathbb{Z}\}
and
\mathbf{b}^{\prime}
is a regular element of
\mathbb{A}
(i.e.,
w.\mathbf{b}^{\prime}\neq\mathbf{b}^{\prime}
, for every
w\in W^{v}_{\lambda}
). The polytope
\overline{\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\lambda}}
is called the
weight polytope
and is studied in
[
Vin91
]
and
[
BJK23
]
. Its intersection with a closed vectorial chamber of
\mathbb{A}
is studied in
[
BGH23
]
.

Motivation from the theory of higher buildings

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field equipped with a valuation
\omega:\mathbb{K}^{*}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, where
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is equipped with the lexicographic order.
For example, let
\mathds{k}
be a field and set
\mathbb{K}=\mathds{k}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
, which is contained in
\mathds{k}(\!(t_{n})\!)(\!(t_{n-1})\!)\ldots(\!(t_{1})\!)
, with

\omega\left(\sum_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}t_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots t_{n}^{i_{n}}\right)=\min\{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\mid a_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}\neq 0\},

for
\sum_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}t_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots t_{n}^{i_{n}}\in\mathbb{K}
. Such fields naturally appear as successive completions of function fields of
n
-dimensional algebraic varieties.

We would like to study groups of the form
G=\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{K})
, for
\mathbf{G}
a split reductive group, as well as certain arithmetic subgroups of them (for instance their presentations, certain (co)homological groups or their (co)homological dimension). When
n=1
, then
G
acts on a classical Bruhat-Tits building which is connected and contractible (see
[
BT72
]
and
[
BT84
]
). We would like to use buildings techniques to study
G
, when
n\geq 2
. In this case Bennett and Parshin independently defined analogues of Bruhat-Tits buildings in the case where
\mathbf{G}
is
\mathrm{SL}_{m}
or
\mathrm{PGL}_{m}
, for
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
(see
[
Ben94
]
and
[
Par94
]
), using lattices of
\mathbb{K}^{m}
. In
[
Ben94
]
, Bennett develops an axiomatic definition of
\Lambda
-buildings (where
\Lambda=\omega(\mathbb{K}^{*})
,
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
in our case). In
[
HIL20
]
, the three last named authors prove that
G
acts on a
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-building
\mathcal{I}
, generalizing (in the quasi-split case) Bruhat-Tits construction via “parahoric” subgroups.

When
n\geq 2
,
\mathcal{I}
is a bit like a set of Russian dolls:
\mathcal{I}
is a Bruhat-Tits building whose vertices are
\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
-buildings. A problem is that
\mathcal{I}
is not connected since the latter
\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
-buildings are connected components of
\mathcal{I}
. As the connectedness and contractibility of Bruhat-Tits buildings are important ingredients of the classical theory, we would like to embed the
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-building
\mathcal{I}
in a “connectified building
\hat{\mathcal{I}}
or
\hat{\mathcal{I}^{\eta}}
, for
\eta\in]0,1[
, on which
G
would act and which would have better topological properties. We hope that our tessellations are a first step in this direction.

Structure of the paper

The paper is organized as follows. In section
2
, we study the polytopes
\mathcal{P}(\lambda)
(or more precisely translates of these polytopes) one by one. We describe the set of faces and study the orthogonal projection on such a polytope.

In section
3
, we study the polytopes
\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}
and prove that they tessellate
\mathbb{A}
.

In section
4
, we prove Theorem
1.2
.

2
Vectorial Weyl polytopes

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a finite dimensional vector space and
\Phi
be a finite root system in
\mathbb{A}
. This equips
\mathbb{A}
with the structure of a poly-simplicial complex. The aim of this paper is to study tesselations of
\mathbb{A}
by polytopes
\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}
associated with vertices
\lambda
of
\mathbb{A}
. Before studying the layout of the different polytopes, we begin by studying the polytopes one by one.

In this section, we study the vectorial weight polytope
\overline{\mathcal{P}^{0}}=\overline{\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\Psi}}
associated with a finite root system
\Psi
. In the applications,
\Psi
will be a root system of the form
\Phi_{\lambda}=\{\alpha\in\Phi\mid\alpha(\lambda)\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, for
\lambda
a vertex of
(\mathbb{A},\Phi)
and then
\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}
will be a translate of
\overline{\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\Phi_{\lambda}}}
. To define this polytope, we choose a regular point of
\mathbb{A}
and we take the convex hull of the orbit of this point under the action of the finite Weyl group of
\Psi
.

In subsection
2.1
, we study general facts on polyhedra.

In subsection
2.2
, we define the vectorial weight polytope
\overline{\mathcal{P}^{0}}
.

In subsection
2.3
, we describe the faces of
\overline{\mathcal{P}^{0}}
.

In subsection
2.4
, we study the restriction to certain affine subspaces of
\mathbb{A}
of the orthogonal projection on
\overline{\mathcal{P}^{0}}
.

2.1
Preliminary results on polyhedra and polytopes

In this section,
\mathbb{A}
is a finite dimensional real vector space. We equip
\mathbb{A}
with a scalar product
\langle\cdot\mid\cdot\rangle
(for the moment,
\langle\cdot\mid\cdot\rangle
is arbitrary, but in most of the applications, it will be invariant under the action of the vectorial or affine Weyl groups defined later). If
E
is a subset of
\mathbb{A}
, we denote by
\mathring{E}
its interior. If
E
is non-empty, its
support

\mathrm{supp}(E)
is the smallest affine subspace of
\mathbb{A}
containing
E
. Its
relative interior
, denoted
\mathrm{Int}_{r}(E)
, is then the interior of
E
in its support. In particular, if
E
is convex and closed, we have
E=\overline{\mathrm{Int}_{r}(E)}
. When
E
is an affine subspace of
\mathbb{A}
, its
direction
, denoted
\mathrm{dir}(E)
, is the vector subspace
\{x-x^{\prime}\mid x,x^{\prime}\in E\}
of
\mathbb{A}
.

We mainly follow the terminology of
[
BG09
]
. A (closed)
polyhedron
is the intersection of finitely many closed half-spaces of
\mathbb{A}
. A (closed)
polytope
is a bounded polyhedron. Let
\mathcal{Q}
be a polyhedron of
\mathbb{A}
. A
support hyperplane
of
\mathcal{Q}
is a hyperplane
H
such that
\mathcal{Q}
is contained in one of the closed half-spaces delimited by
H
and such that
\mathcal{Q}\cap H\neq\emptyset
. A
closed face
of
\mathcal{Q}
is either
\mathcal{Q}
or a set of the form
H\cap\mathcal{Q}
, where
H
is a support hyperplane. Contrary to
[
BG09
]
, we do not consider
\emptyset
as a face of
\mathcal{Q}
. A
closed facet
of
\mathcal{Q}
is a face of codimension
1
in the support of
\mathcal{Q}
. By
[
BG09
, Theorem 1.10]
, every proper closed face of
\mathcal{Q}
is an intersection of closed facets. We denote by
\mathrm{Facet}(\mathcal{Q})
the set of closed facets of
\mathcal{Q}
. An
open face
or simply a
face
of
\mathcal{Q}
is the relative interior of a closed face of
\mathcal{Q}
. Note that in
[
BG09
]
, what we call “closed faces” are simply called “faces” and the “open faces” are the relative interior of faces: we make this choice in order to be coherent with the usual definition of faces in an apartment (see subsections
2.2
and
3.2
). If
F_{1},F_{2}
are open faces of a polyhedron for which
\overline{F}_{1}\cap\overline{F_{2}}
is non-empty, we set
F_{1}\wedge F_{2}=\mathrm{Int}_{r}(\overline{F}_{1}\cap\overline{F_{2}})
. It is a face of
\mathcal{Q}
by
[
BG09
, Proposition 1.8 and Theorem 1.10]
.

2.1.1
Orthogonal projection on polytopes

If
E
is a convex closed subset of
\mathbb{A}
, we denote by
\pi_{E}:\mathbb{A}\rightarrow E
the orthogonal projection on
E
: for every
x\in\mathbb{A}
,
\pi_{E}(x)
is the unique point
y_{x}
of
E
satisfying
|x-y_{x}|=\min\{|z-x|\mid z\in E\}
. Moreover, if
y_{x}\in E
, then we have

\left(y_{x}=\pi_{E}(x)\right)\Longleftrightarrow\left(\langle x-y_{x}\mid y-y_{x}\rangle\leq 0,\forall y\in E\right),

(2.1)

by
[
HUL93
, III Theorem 3.1.1]
.

In this preliminary subsection, we study the projection
\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}
, for
\mathcal{Q}
a polyhedron of
\mathbb{A}
. In particular, Proposition
2.3
provides a description of its fibers.

For
x\in\mathbb{A}
and
r\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we denote by
B(x,r)
the open ball of center
x
and radius
r
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be a polyhedron of
\mathbb{A}
with non-empty interior. Let
x\in\mathcal{Q}
and let
\mathrm{Facet}_{x}(\mathcal{Q})
be the set of closed facets of
\mathcal{Q}
containing
x
. For
\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathrm{Facet}_{x}(\mathcal{Q})
with relative interior
\mathcal{F}
, we denote by
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{F}}
the closed half-space of
\mathbb{A}
delimited by
\mathcal{F}
and containing
\mathcal{Q}
. Let
\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}=\bigcap_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathrm{Facet}_{x}(\mathcal{Q})}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{F}}
. Then there exists
r>0
such that
B(x,r)\cap\mathcal{Q}=B(x,r)\cap\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}
.

Proof.

We have
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{F}}
for every
\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathrm{Facet}_{x}(\mathcal{Q})
and thus
\mathcal{Q}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}
.

For
\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathrm{Facet}(\mathcal{Q})\setminus\mathrm{Facet}_{x}(\mathcal{Q})
, choose
r_{\mathcal{F}}>0
such that
B(x,r_{\mathcal{F}})\cap\overline{\mathcal{F}}=\emptyset.
Let
r=\min\{r_{\mathcal{F}}\mid\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathrm{Facet}(\mathcal{Q})\setminus\mathrm{Facet}_{x}(\mathcal{Q})\}
(we set
r=1
if
\mathrm{Facet}(\mathcal{Q})=\mathrm{Facet}_{x}(\mathcal{Q})
). Let
y\in\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}\cap B(x,r)
. Assume by contradiction that
y\notin\mathcal{Q}
. Let
z\in\mathring{\mathcal{Q}}\cap B(x,r)
. Then
[z,y]
meets
\mathrm{Fr}(\mathcal{Q})
in a point
y^{\prime}
. By
[
BG09
, Corollary 1.7]
, we have
\mathrm{Fr}(\mathcal{Q})=\bigcup_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathrm{Facet}(\mathcal{Q})}\overline{\mathcal{F}}
. We have
y^{\prime}\notin\bigcup_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathrm{Facet}_{x}(\mathcal{Q})}\overline{\mathcal{F}}
and thus
y^{\prime}\in\bigcup_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathrm{Facet}(\mathcal{Q})\setminus\mathrm{Facet}_{x}(\mathcal{Q})}\overline{\mathcal{F}}
. Consequently
y^{\prime}\notin B(x,r)
: a contradiction. Therefore
\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}\cap B(x,r)\subset\mathcal{Q}
, and the lemma follows.
∎

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be a polyhedron. If
\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathrm{Facet}(\mathcal{Q})
, we denote by
\delta_{\mathcal{F}}
the (closed) ray of
\mathrm{dir}(\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{Q}))
, which is orthogonal to the direction of the support of
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
and such that for all
x\in\overline{\mathcal{F}}
,
(x+\delta_{\mathcal{F}})\cap\mathcal{Q}=\{x\}
. If
\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathrm{Facet}(\mathcal{Q})
, we denote by
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{F}}
the half-space of
\mathbb{A}
containing
\mathcal{Q}
and delimited by the hyperplane containing
\mathcal{F}
. We have

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{F}}=\{x\in\mathbb{A}\mid\langle x-a\mid z\rangle\leq 0,\forall z\in\delta_{\mathcal{F}}\},

(2.2)

for any
a\in\overline{\mathcal{F}}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be a polyhedron with non-empty interior. Let
x\in\mathbb{A}\setminus\mathcal{Q}
and
J=\{\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathrm{Facet}(\mathcal{Q})\mid\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}(x)\in\overline{\mathcal{F}}\}.
Then
\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}(x)=\pi_{\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}}(x)
, where
\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}=\bigcap_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in J}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, there exists
r>0
such that
\mathcal{Q}\cap B(\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}(x),r)=\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}\cap B(\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}(x),r)
. We have
d(x,y)\geq d(x,\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}(x))
for every
y\in\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}\cap B(\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}(x),r)
and thus
d(x,\cdot)|_{\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}}
admits a local minimum at
\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}(x)
. But as
d(x,\cdot)|_{\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}}
is a convex function, by
[
HUL93
, IV 1.3 (c)]
, this minimum is actually a global minimum, which proves that
\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}(x)=\pi_{\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}}(x)
.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be a polyhedron with non-empty interior and let
\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}
be the orthogonal projection on
\mathcal{Q}
. Let
x\in\mathrm{Fr}(\mathcal{Q})
and
J=\{\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathrm{Facet}(\mathcal{Q})\mid x\in\overline{\mathcal{F}}\}
. Then
\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}^{-1}(\{x\})=x+\sum_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in J}\delta_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}=\bigcap_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in J}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{F}}
. Let
y\in x+\sum_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in J}\delta_{\mathcal{F}}
. Then by Eq . (
2.2
), we have
\langle y-x\mid z-x\rangle\geq 0
for all
z\in\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}\supset\mathcal{Q}
and hence by Eq . (
2.1
), we have
\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}(y)=x
. Therefore

\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}^{-1}(\{x\})\supset x+\sum_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in J}\delta_{\mathcal{F}}.

Let now
y\in\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}^{-1}(\{x\})
. Then by Lemma
2.2
, we have
\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}(y)=\pi_{\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}}(y)=x
. For
\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in J
, choose
z_{\mathcal{F}}\in\delta_{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\{0\}
and respectively define
f_{\mathcal{F}}:\mathbb{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
f:\mathbb{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by
f_{\mathcal{F}}(z)=-\langle z\mid z_{\mathcal{F}}\rangle
and
f(z)=\langle z\mid x-y\rangle
, for
z\in\mathbb{A}
. Let
z\in\mathbb{A}
be such that
f_{\mathcal{F}}(z)\geq 0
, for all
\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in J
. Let
z^{\prime}=z+x
. Then we have
f_{\mathcal{F}}(z^{\prime}-x)\geq 0
, for all
\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in J
and thus by Eq . (
2.2
), we have
z^{\prime}\in\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}
. By Eq. (
2.1
), we deduce
f(z^{\prime}-x)\geq 0
and thus
f(z)\geq 0
. Using Farkas’ Lemma, we deduce
f\in\sum_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in J}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}f_{\mathcal{F}}
, or equivalently,
y-x\in\sum_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in J}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}z_{\mathcal{F}}=\sum_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}\in J}\delta_{\mathcal{F}}
, which proves the proposition.

∎

2.1.2
Extreme points of a polytope

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be a convex subset of
\mathbb{A}
.
Recall (see
[
HUL12
, Definition 2.3.1]
) that a point
M
of
\mathcal{Q}
is called an
extreme point
of
\mathcal{Q}
if for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
M_{1},\ldots,M_{k}\in\mathcal{Q}
,
t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}\in]0,1]
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}=1
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}M_{i}=M\Rightarrow M_{1}=\ldots=M_{k}=M.

We denote by
\mathrm{Extr}(\mathcal{Q})
the set of extreme points of
\mathcal{Q}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
H
be a finite subgroup of the set of affine automorphisms of
\mathbb{A}
. Let
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{A}
. Then the set of extreme points of
\mathrm{conv}(H.\mathbf{b})
is
H.\mathbf{b}
.

Proof.

Denote by
E
the set of extreme points of
\mathrm{conv}(H.\mathbf{b})
. Then
E
is contained in
H.\mathbf{b}
. Then
\mathrm{conv}(H.\mathbf{b})
is compact. By Krein-Milman theorem (
[
HUL12
, Theorem 2.3.4]
),
\mathrm{conv}(H.\mathbf{b})
is the convex hull of
E
, and in particular,
E
is non-empty. As
H
stabilizes

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be a closed polytope of
\mathbb{A}
.

1.

Let

be a closed face of
\mathcal{Q}
. Then

.

2.

be a family of closed faces of
\mathcal{Q}
. Then

Proof.

1) By

, Proposition 1.13]
, the set of closed faces of
\mathcal{Q}
(resp.
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
) is the set of extreme sets of
\mathcal{Q}
(resp.
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
) and thus the set of extreme points of
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
is the set of zero-dimensional faces of
\overline{\mathcal{F}}

\mathcal{Q}
, that are contained in
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
, according to
[

. Thus
\mathrm{Extr}(\overline{\mathcal{F}})=\mathrm{Extr}(\mathcal{Q})\cap\overline{\mathcal{F}}
.

2) Since the number of faces of

is finite (by

, Theorem 1.10]
), we may assume that

is finite. Then by
[
BG09

,

. Then, by statement 1), we have

∎

2.2
Vectorial weight polytope

2.2.1
Vectorial root system

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a finite dimensional real vector space and denote by
\mathbb{A}^{*}:=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{R})
its dual space. Let
\Psi\subset\mathbb{A}^{*}
be a reduced finite root system in
\mathbb{A}
, in the sense of
[
Bou81
, VI, §1]
. In particular,
\Psi
is a finite subset of
\mathbb{A}^{*}
generating
\mathbb{A}^{*}
. Elements of
\Psi
are called
roots
and to every
\alpha\in\Psi
is associated a unique
coroot

\alpha^{\vee}
. We have
\alpha(\alpha^{\vee})=2
and the map
\mathbb{A}^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{*}
defined by
\beta\mapsto\beta-\beta(\alpha^{\vee})\alpha
stabilizes
\Psi
. One defines
s_{\alpha}:\mathbb{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}
by
s_{\alpha}(x)=x-\alpha(x)\alpha^{\vee}
for
x\in\mathbb{A}
. The set
\Psi^{\vee}=\{\alpha^{\vee}\mid\alpha\in\Psi\}\subset\mathbb{A}
is a root system in
\mathbb{A}^{*}
and for every
\alpha\in\Psi
,
\alpha(\Psi^{\vee})\subset\mathbb{Z}
and
s_{\alpha}
stabilizes
\Psi^{\vee}
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\Psi}^{0}=\mathcal{H}^{0}=\{\alpha^{-1}(\{0\})\mid\alpha\in\Psi\}
. This is a locally finite hyperplane arrangement of
\mathbb{A}
. The elements of
\mathcal{H}^{0}
are called
walls
or
vectorial walls
. For
x,y\in\mathbb{A}
, we write
x\sim_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}y
if for every
H\in\mathcal{H}^{0}
, either (
x,y\in H
) or (
x
and
y
are strictly on the same side of
H
). This is an equivalence relation on
\mathbb{A}
. Its classes are called the
faces
of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H}^{0})
or simply the vectorial faces of
\mathbb{A}
. We denote by
\mathscr{F}^{0}=\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H}^{0})
the set of faces of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H}^{0})
. If
E_{1},E_{2}
are two relatively open (i.e open in their support) convex subsets of
\mathbb{A}
, we say that
E_{1}

dominates

E_{2}
if
\overline{E_{1}}\supset E_{2}
. This equips the set of relatively open convex subsets of
\mathbb{A}
with an order. By restriction, this equips
\mathscr{F}^{0}
with an order called the
dominance order
.

A
vectorial chamber
(or
open vectorial chamber
) of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H}^{0})
is a connected component of
\mathbb{A}\setminus\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal{H}^{0}}H
. The chambers are actually the faces which are not contained in an element of
\mathcal{H}^{0}
or equivalently, the maximal faces for the dominance order. A
wall
of a vectorial chamber
C^{v}
is an element
H
of
\mathcal{H}
such that
H\cap\overline{C^{v}}
is a closed facet of
\overline{C^{v}}
.

Let
C^{v}
be a vectorial chamber of
\Psi
. The
associated basis of
\Psi
is the set
\Sigma_{C^{v}}
of
\alpha\in\Psi
such that
\alpha^{-1}(\{0\})
is a wall of
C^{v}
and
\alpha(C^{v})>0
. Then by
[
Bou81
, VI. 1.5 Théorème 2]
,
\Sigma_{C^{v}}
is a
basis
of
\Psi
, which means that
(\alpha)_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{C^{v}}}
is a basis of
\mathbb{A}^{*}
and every element of
\Psi
can be written as
\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{C^{v}}}n_{\alpha}\alpha
, where
(n_{\alpha})\in(\epsilon\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{\Sigma_{C^{v}}}
, for some
\epsilon\in\{-,+\}
. We set
\Psi_{\epsilon,C^{v}}=\Psi\cap\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{C^{v}}}\epsilon\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha
, for
\epsilon\in\{-,+\}
.

The following proposition follows from
[
Bou81
, §V 3.9 Proposition 5]
.

Cartan matrices

Let
A=A(\Psi)=(\alpha(\beta^{\vee}))_{\alpha,\beta\in\Sigma}
. This is the
Cartan matrix
of
\Psi
. Up to permutation of its coefficients, it is well-defined, independently of the choice of the basis
\Sigma
of
\Psi
, by
[
Bou81
, VI, 1.5]
. The Cartan matrix characterizes the root system up to isomorphism.

A matrix of the form
A(\Phi)
, for some root system
\Phi
is called a
Cartan matrix
.

The Cartan matrix

is called

if there exists a decomposition
\Sigma=\Sigma^{\prime}\sqcup\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
, with
\Sigma^{\prime}
and
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
non-empty, such that
\alpha(\beta^{\vee})=\beta(\alpha^{\vee})=0
, for every
(\alpha,\beta)\in\Sigma^{\prime}\times\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
. A Cartan matrix which is not decomposable is called
indecomposable.
Then
\Psi
is irreducible if and only if
A
is indecomposable, and under the notation of Eq. (
2.4
),
A
is the block diagonal matrix whose blocks are the
A(\Psi_{i})
, for
i\in\llbracket 1,k\rrbracket
. One can use the Cartan matrices to classify finite roots systems, see
[
Kac94
, Chapter 4]
for example (where the Cartan matrices are the generalized Cartan matrices of finite type).

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
w\in W^{v}_{\Psi}
and

(i)

we have
\mathbf{b}-w.\mathbf{b}\in\bigoplus_{\alpha\in J}\mathbb{R}\alpha^{\vee}

(ii)

we have
\mathbf{b}-w.\mathbf{b}\in Q^{\vee}_{J,+}
,

(iii)

we have
w\in W^{v}_{J}
.

Proof.

By
[
Bou81
, Chapitre VI, §1 Proposition 18]
, if
w\in W^{v}_{J}
, then
\mathbf{b}-w.\mathbf{b}\in Q^{\vee}_{J,+}\subset\mathbf{b}-w.\mathbf{b}\in\bigoplus_{\alpha\in J}\mathbb{R}\alpha^{\vee}

It remains to prove that (i) implies (iii). To do so, take
w\in W^{v}_{\Psi}
and assume that
w\in W^{v}_{\Psi}\setminus W^{v}_{J}
. Write
w=s_{\alpha_{k}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{1}}
, with
k=\ell_{\Psi}(w)
,
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\in\Sigma
, where

denotes the length of the Coxeter system
(W^{v}_{\Psi},\{s_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Sigma\})
. Then there exists

such that
\alpha_{j}\notin J
. Take

minimal with this property.
Then

By an induction using Lemma
2.9
, we deduce

w.\mathbf{b}\in\mathbf{b}+(\bigoplus_{\alpha\in J}\mathbb{R}\alpha^{\vee}-\mathbb{R}_{>0}\alpha_{j}^{\vee})-\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\alpha^{\vee}.

In particular,
w.\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}\notin\bigoplus_{\alpha\in J}\mathbb{R}\alpha^{\vee}

\pi\left((z+\mathrm{vect}(F^{v}))\cap\overline{F_{1}^{v}}\right)\subset(z+\mathrm{vect}(F^{v}))\cap\overline{F_{1}^{v}}.

2.

Let
y\in(z+\mathrm{vect}(F^{v}))\cap\overline{F_{1}^{v}}
and let
G^{v}\in\mathscr{F}^{0}
be such that
\pi(y)\in\mathcal{F}_{G^{v}}
. Then
G^{v}\subset\overline{F^{v}}
.

Proof.

1) Up to changing the choice of the fundamental chamber
C^{v}_{f}
, we may assume
F_{1}^{v}\subset\overline{C^{v}_{f}}
. Let
J=\{i\in I\mid\varpi_{i}\in\overline{F^{v}}\}
. Then by Proposition
2.6
,
F^{v}=\bigoplus_{j\in J}\mathbb{R}_{>0}\varpi_{j}
and by Proposition
2.20
,
z\in\bigwedge_{j\in J}\mathcal{F}_{j}
. We have
V_{J}=\bigoplus_{j\in J}\mathbb{R}\varpi_{j}
and the result follows from Lemma
2.32
and Lemma
2.24
.

2) Let
y\in(z+\mathrm{vect}(F^{v}))\cap\overline{F_{1}^{v}}
. Let
G^{v}\in\mathscr{F}^{0}
be such that
\pi(y)\in\mathcal{F}_{G^{v}}
. By Lemma
2.24
,
G^{v}\subset\overline{F_{1}^{v}}
. In particular,
G^{v}\subset\overline{C^{v}_{f}}
, and hence, by Proposition
2.6
,
G^{v}=\sum_{j\in I\mid\varpi_{j}\in\overline{G^{v}}}\mathbb{R}_{>0}\varpi_{j}
. Let now
i\in I\setminus J
. By 1) and Lemma
2.28
,
\pi(y)\notin\overline{\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}\varpi_{i}}}
. Using Corollary
2.21
we deduce that
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\varpi_{i}\not\subset\overline{G^{v}}
. This being true for all

G^{v}\subset\sum_{j\in J}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\varpi_{j}=\overline{F^{v}}
.
∎

3
Tessellation of
\mathbb{A}
by the affine weight polytopes

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a finite dimensional vector space and let
\Phi
be a finite root system in
\mathbb{A}
. In this section, we study polytopes associated with the affine poly-simplicial complex defined from
\Phi
.

In subsection

\Phi
.

In subsection
3.2
, we define and study the faces of the weight polytopes.

In subsection

.

3.1
Affine apartment

The main reference for this section is
[
Bou81
, V]
. Let
\Phi
be a (finite) root system in
\mathbb{A}
. Let
\xi:\mathbb{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the constant map equal to

\Phi^{\mathrm{aff}}=\Phi+\mathbb{Z}\xi
. Then by
[
Mac72
, (2.1) Proposition]

is
an affine root system
in the sense of
[
Mac72
, 2]

\mathbb{A}

\mathbb{A}

\mathcal{H}

or an

For

if for every

, either (

) or (

and

are strictly on the same side of

). This is an equivalence relation on
\mathbb{A}
. Its classes are called the
faces
of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
or simply the faces of
\mathbb{A}
. A
vertex
of
\mathbb{A}
is an element
x
of
\mathbb{A}
such that
\{x\}
is a face of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
. If
F
is a face of

, then:

\forall\alpha\in\Phi,\exists n\in\mathbb{Z}\mid\alpha(F)\subset]n,n+1[\text{ or }\alpha(F)=\{n\}.

An

(or

) of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
is a connected component of
\mathbb{A}\setminus\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal{H}}H
. The alcoves are actually the faces which are not contained in an element of
\mathcal{H}
. A

is a set of the form
\overline{C}
, for some alcove
C
of
\mathbb{A}
. If
\overline{C}
is a closed alcove of
\mathbb{A}
, its set of vertices, denoted

, is the set of vertices of
\mathbb{A}
which belong to
\overline{C}
.

We denote by
\mathscr{F}=\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H})
the set of faces of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
. If
F\in\mathscr{F}
, we denote by
\mathscr{F}_{F}=\mathscr{F}_{F}(\mathcal{H})
the set of faces dominating
F
(i.e., the set of faces whose closure contains

). When
F=\{\lambda\}
, for some vertex
\lambda
, we write
\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
instead of
\mathscr{F}_{\{\lambda\}}(\mathcal{H})
.
We denote by
\mathrm{Alc}(\mathcal{H})
the set of alcoves of

and if

, we denote by

the set of alcoves of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
dominating

. The

is

. When

3.1.1
Enclosure and closed faces of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})

Definition 3.1
.

A
half-apartment
of
\mathbb{A}
is a closed half-space of
\mathbb{A}
delimited by an element of
\mathcal{H}
. If
\alpha\in\Phi
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, one sets
D_{\alpha,k}=\{x\in\mathbb{A}\mid\alpha(x)+k\geq 0\}

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{A}
. Its
enclosure

\mathrm{cl}(\Omega)
is the intersection of all the half-apartments containing
\Omega
.

If
\Omega
is a subset of
\mathbb{A}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{A}
, then the
germ of
\Omega
at
\lambda
is the set
\mathrm{germ}_{\lambda}(\Omega)
of subsets of
\mathbb{A}
of the form
\Omega\cap E
such that
E
is an open neighbourhood of
\lambda
. A subset
\Omega^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{A}
is said to
contain
the germ
\mathrm{germ}_{\lambda}(\Omega)
if it contains
\Omega\cap E
for some open neighbourhood
E
of
\lambda
. The enclosure of
\mathrm{germ}_{\lambda}(\Omega)
is the intersection of the enclosures of the
\Omega\cap E
when
E
describes all the open neighbourhoods of
\lambda
.

A
closed face of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
is a set of the form
\overline{F}
, for some
F\in\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

1.

Let
F\in\mathscr{F}
. For
\underline{\alpha}\in\Phi^{\mathrm{aff}}
, set
E_{\underline{\alpha}}=\{0\}
if
\underline{\alpha}(F)=\{0\}
,
E_{\underline{\alpha}}=\mathbb{R}_{>0}
if
\underline{\alpha}(F)>0
and
E_{\underline{\alpha}}=\mathbb{R}_{<0}
if
\underline{\alpha}(F)<0
. Then
F=\bigcap_{\underline{\alpha}\in\Phi^{\mathrm{aff}}}\underline{\alpha}^{-1}(E_{\underline{\alpha}})
and
\overline{F}=\bigcap_{\underline{\alpha}\in\Phi^{\mathrm{aff}}}\underline{\alpha}^{-1}(\overline{E_{\underline{\alpha}}})
. In particular,
\overline{F}
is enclosed.

2.

Let
F\in\mathscr{F}
and
x\in F
. Then
\mathrm{cl}(x)=\overline{F}
.

Proof.

1) The first equality follows from the definition of faces. As the elements of
\Phi^{\mathrm{aff}}
are continuous maps from
\mathbb{A}
to
\mathbb{R}
, we have
\overline{F}\subset\bigcap_{\underline{\alpha}\in\Phi^{\mathrm{aff}}}\underline{\alpha}^{-1}(\overline{E_{\underline{\alpha}}})
. Let
x\in F
and
y\in\bigcap_{\underline{\alpha}\in\Phi^{\mathrm{aff}}}\underline{\alpha}^{-1}(\overline{E_{\underline{\alpha}}})
. Then for
z\in]x,y[
and
\underline{\alpha}\in\Phi^{\mathrm{aff}}
, we have
\underline{\alpha}(z)\in\overline{E_{\underline{\alpha}}}+E_{\underline{\alpha}}=E_{\underline{\alpha}}
and hence
[x,y[\subset F
, which proves that
y\in\overline{F}
and concludes the proof of the second equality. The fact that
\overline{F}
is enclosed then follows from the fact that if
\underline{\alpha}\in\Phi^{\mathrm{aff}}
, then
\underline{\alpha}^{-1}(\{0\})=\underline{\alpha}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})\cap\underline{\alpha}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0})
.

We now prove 2). We have
\mathrm{cl}(x)\subset\mathrm{cl}(F)=\overline{F}
. By definition of the faces, every half-apartment containing
x
contains
F
and thus it contains
\overline{F}
. Therefore
\mathrm{cl}(x)\supset\overline{F}
, which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
E
be a non-empty subset of
\mathbb{A}
. Then
E
is a closed face of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

1.

E
is enclosed,

2.

for every
\underline{\alpha}\in\Phi^{\mathrm{aff}}
, we have either
\underline{\alpha}(E)\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
or
\underline{\alpha}(E)\subset\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}
.

Proof.

Let
F
be a face of
\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H})
. Then by Lemma
3.2
,
\overline{F}
is enclosed. By definition of the faces, if
\underline{\alpha}\in\Phi^{\mathrm{aff}}
, we have
\underline{\alpha}(F)\subset\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
\underline{\alpha}(F)=\{0\}
or
\underline{\alpha}(F)\subset\mathbb{R}_{<0}
. As
\underline{\alpha}(\overline{F})\subset\overline{\underline{\alpha}(F)}
we deduce that
\overline{F}
satisfies 1. and 2.

Let now
E
be a non-empty subset satisfying 1. and 2. Then
E
is convex and closed. Let
F=\mathrm{Int}_{r}(E)
. Then we have
E=\overline{F}
. Let
S
be the support of
E
and
x,y\in F
. Let
\underline{\alpha}\in\Phi^{\mathrm{aff}}
. If
\underline{\alpha}
is constant on
S
, then
\underline{\alpha}(x)=\underline{\alpha}(y)
. Assume now that
\underline{\alpha}
is non-constant on
S
. Let
U_{x},U_{y}
be open subsets of
S
containing
x
and
y
respectively. Then by assumption
\underline{\alpha}(U_{x})\underline{\alpha}(U_{y})\geq 0
and hence
\underline{\alpha}(x)\underline{\alpha}(y)>0
. Consequently,
x\sim y
, where
\sim
is the equivalence relation that we used to define the faces of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
. Consequently there exists
F_{1}\in\mathscr{F}
such that
F\subset F_{1}
.

Using 1., we write
\overline{F}=\bigcap_{\underline{\alpha}\in\mathcal{E}}\underline{\alpha}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
, where
\mathcal{E}\subset\Phi^{\mathrm{aff}}
. Let
x\in F
. Let
\underline{\alpha}\in\mathcal{E}
. Then
\underline{\alpha}(x)\geq 0
and as
\underline{\alpha}
has constant sign on
F_{1}
, we have
\underline{\alpha}(F_{1})\geq 0
. Consequently
F_{1}\subset\overline{F}
and thus
\overline{F_{1}}\subset\overline{F}
. As
F\subset F_{1}
, we deduce
\overline{F}=\overline{F_{1}}=E
, and
E
is a closed face of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
.
∎

(3.4)

3.1.2

There exists a scalar product
\langle\cdot\mid\cdot\rangle

of
\Phi
. We equip
\mathbb{A}
with such a scalar product. If

the orthogonal reflection of

H

wall
of
C
is an element of
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathrm{supp}(\overline{C}\cap H)=H
. The

(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
is the group

of affine maps on
\mathbb{A}
generated by
s_{H}
, for
H\in\mathcal{H}
. If
F\in\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H})
, we denote by
W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F}
the fixator of
F
in
W^{\mathrm{aff}}
. Gathering the results of
[
Bou81
, V §3.2, 3.3]
, we have the following theorem. We write it for the affine Weyl group and the faces of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
, but it is also true for the vectorial Weyl group and the vectorial faces, by the same reference. We refer to
[
BB05
]
for the definition of Coxeter groups.

Theorem 3.7
.

1.

Let
C\in\mathrm{Alc}(\mathcal{H})
and
S_{C}
be the set of orthogonal reflections with respect to the walls of
C
. Then
(W^{\mathrm{aff}},S_{C})
is a Coxeter group.

2.

Let
C\in\mathrm{Alc}(\mathcal{H})
. Then
\overline{C}
is a fundamental domain for the action of
W^{\mathrm{aff}}
on
\mathbb{A}
and for every alcove
C^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Alc}(\mathcal{H})
, there exists a unique
w\in W^{\mathrm{aff}}
such that
w.C=C^{\prime}
.

3.

Let
F\in\mathscr{F}
and
C\in\mathrm{Alc}_{F}(\mathcal{H})
. Then
W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F}
is the parabolic subgroup of
(W^{\mathrm{aff}},S_{C})
generated by the reflections with respect to the walls of
C
containing
F
. Moreover, if
w\in W^{\mathrm{aff}}
,
the following statements are equivalent:

a.

w\in W_{F}^{\mathrm{aff}}
,

b.

w
pointwise fixes
\overline{F}
,

c.

w.F
meets
F
,

d.

w
fixes a point of
F
.

The lemma below also has a vectorial version.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
F\in\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H})
and
C\in\mathrm{Alc}_{F}(\mathcal{H})
. Then for every
F^{\prime}\in\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H})
such that
F^{\prime}
dominates
F
, there exists
w\in W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F}
such that
w.F^{\prime}\subset\overline{C}
. In particular,
W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F}
acts transitively on
\mathrm{Alc}_{F}(\mathcal{H})
.

Equivalently, if
E
is the union of the faces dominating
F
and if
E^{\prime}=E\cap\overline{C}
, then
E^{\prime}
is a fundamental domain for the action of
W_{F}^{\mathrm{aff}}
on
E
.

Proof.

Let
C^{\prime}
be an alcove of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
dominating
F
. Write
C^{\prime}=w.C
, with
w\in W^{\mathrm{aff}}
. Then
w^{-1}.F\subset\overline{C}
, so if
x\in w^{-1}.F\subset\overline{C}
, we have
w.x\in\overline{C}
, which proves that
w.x=x
by Theorem
3.7
. Therefore
w
fixes
w^{-1}.F
, which proves that
w
fixes
F
. Now if
F_{1}
is a face of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
dominating
F
and
C_{1}
is an alcove dominating
F_{1}
, we can write
C_{1}=w_{1}.C
, with
w_{1}\in W^{\mathrm{aff}}
fixing
F
. Then
w_{1}^{-1}.F_{1}\subset\overline{C}
, which proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
C\in\mathrm{Alc}(\mathcal{H})
and
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H})
be such that
F_{1}\cup F_{2}\subset\overline{C}
. Then
W_{F_{1}}^{\mathrm{aff}}\subset W_{F_{2}}^{\mathrm{aff}}
(resp.
W_{F_{1}}^{\mathrm{aff}}=W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F_{2}}
) if and only if
F_{2}\subset\overline{F_{1}}
(resp.
F_{1}=F_{2}
).

Proof.

By Theorem
3.7
, if
S_{C}
denotes the set of orthogonal reflections with respect to the walls of
C
, then
(W^{\mathrm{aff}},S_{C})
is a Coxeter group. Let
i\in\{1,2\}
. Set
\mathcal{M}_{F_{i}}=\{\underline{\alpha}\in\Sigma^{\mathrm{aff}}_{C}\mid\underline{\alpha}(F_{i})>0\}
. We have
W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F_{i}}=\langle s_{\underline{\alpha}}\mid\underline{\alpha}\in\Sigma^{\mathrm{aff}}_{C}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{F_{i}}\rangle
, by Theorem
3.7
, where
s_{\underline{\alpha}}
denotes the reflection of
W^{\mathrm{aff}}
fixing
\underline{\alpha}^{-1}(\{0\})
, for
\underline{\alpha}\in\Phi^{\mathrm{aff}}
. By
[
BB05
]
, if
W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F_{1}}\subset W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F_{2}}
(resp.
W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F_{1}}=W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F_{2}}
), then we have
\Sigma^{\mathrm{aff}}_{C}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{F_{1}}\subset\Sigma^{\mathrm{aff}}_{C}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{F_{2}}
(resp.
\Sigma^{\mathrm{aff}}_{C}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{F_{1}}=\Sigma^{\mathrm{aff}}_{C}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{F_{2}}
).
Using Lemma
3.5
we deduce that
\overline{F_{2}}\subset\overline{F_{1}}
(resp.
\overline{F_{1}}=\overline{F_{2}}
).

Conversely, as

acts by homeomorphisms on
\mathbb{A}
, we have

if

and thus if
F_{2}\subset\overline{F_{1}}
(resp.
\overline{F_{1}}=\overline{F_{2}}
), we have

(resp.
W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F_{1}}=W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F_{2}}
).
∎

3.1.3

\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H})
and

For

, we set

.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{A}
. Then
x\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})
if and only if
\bigcap_{\alpha\in\Phi_{x}}\ker(\alpha)=\{0\}
.

Proof.

Let
E=\bigcap_{\alpha\in\Phi_{x}}\ker(\alpha)
. Let
y\in E
. Then for

small enough, for every
\alpha\in\Phi
, if

, then
\alpha(x+ty)\in]\lfloor\alpha(x)\rfloor,\lfloor\alpha(x)\rfloor+1[
and thus

. Therefore if
\{x\}
is a face of

, then

. Conversely, assume

. Take

such that

. Then

and as

,

. Thus the class of

is

.
∎

Let

. Then by

Bou81
, Chapitre VI, §1 Proposition 4]
and Lemma
3.10

is a root system in

, with dual root system

and

, since

is reduced. Therefore

is a reduced root system in
\mathbb{A}^{*}
. If

, we denote by

the associated reflection.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}^{0}=\{\alpha^{-1}(\{0\})\mid\alpha\in\Phi_{\lambda}\}
. The connected components of
\mathbb{A}\setminus\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}^{0}}H
are the (vectorial) chambers of
(\mathbb{A},\Phi_{\lambda})
. If
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})
, then we denote by
\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})
and

. We assume that there exists a neighbourhood
\Omega
of
\lambda
such that

. Then
F_{1}\subset\overline{F_{2}}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\Omega\cap F_{1}
. Let
\alpha\in\Phi
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that

. Then
D_{\alpha,k}\supset\overline{F_{2}}\ni x
and thus

. As

is enclosed, we deduce that

.
∎

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})
. Equip
\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
and
\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H}^{0}_{\lambda})
with the dominance order. Then the map
\Theta:\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H}^{0}_{\lambda})
defined by
\Theta(F)=F^{v}(F):=\mathbb{R}_{>0}(F-\lambda)
, for
F\in\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
is an isomorphism of partially ordered sets. Its inverse is the map
\Xi:\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H}^{0}_{\lambda})\rightarrow\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
which associates to an element
F^{v}
of
\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H}^{0}_{\lambda})
the unique face of
\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H})
containing
\mathrm{germ}_{\lambda}(\lambda+F^{v})
. We have
\overline{\Xi(F^{v})}=\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{germ}_{\lambda}(\lambda+F^{v}))
and
\Xi(F^{v})\subset\lambda+F^{v}
.

Proof.

Set

\Omega=\Omega_{\lambda}:=\bigcap_{\alpha\in\Phi_{\lambda}}\alpha^{-1}\left(]\alpha(\lambda)-1,\alpha(\lambda)+1[\right)\cap\bigcap_{\alpha\in\Phi\setminus\Phi_{\lambda}}\alpha^{-1}\left(]\lfloor\alpha(\lambda)\rfloor,\lceil\alpha(\lambda)\rceil[\right).

Then
\Omega
is a convex open set containing
\lambda
.

Let
F\in\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
and
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}(F-\lambda)
. Write
x_{1}=t_{1}(f_{1}-\lambda)
,
x_{2}=t_{2}(f_{2}-\lambda)
, with
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
f_{1},f_{2}\in F
. Let
\alpha\in\Phi_{\lambda}
. Then the sign of
\alpha-\alpha(\lambda)
(which can take the values “
<0
”, “
=0
” or “
>0
”) is constant on
\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
and thus
\alpha(x_{1})
and
\alpha(x_{2})
have the same sign. As this is true for every
\alpha\in\Phi_{\lambda}
, we deduce the existence of
F^{v}\in\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H}^{0}_{\lambda})
such that
\Theta(F)\subset F^{v}
.

Let
x_{1}\in F^{v}
and
f\in F
. Set
x_{2}=f-\lambda\in\Theta(F)\subset F^{v}
. Let
t_{1}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be such that
f_{1}:=x_{1}/t_{1}+\lambda\in\Omega
. Let
\alpha\in\Phi\setminus\Phi_{\lambda}
.
By choice of
\Omega
, we have
\alpha(f_{1})\in]\lfloor\alpha(\lambda)\rfloor,\lceil\alpha(\lambda)\rceil[
. By Eq. (
3.1
), we have
\alpha(F)\subset]\lfloor\alpha(\lambda)\rfloor,\lceil\alpha(\lambda)\rceil[
and thus
\alpha-k
has constant sign on
\{f_{1},f\}
, for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\alpha\in\Phi_{\lambda}
. We have
\alpha(F^{v})\in\{\mathbb{R}_{<0},\{0\},\mathbb{R}_{>0}\}
. If
\alpha(F^{v})=\{0\}
, then
\alpha(f_{1})=\alpha(\lambda)
and
\alpha(x_{2})=0
, so
\alpha(f)=\alpha(\lambda)
. Therefore
\alpha+k
has constant sign on
\{f_{1},f\}
, for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Assume now
\alpha(F)=\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. Then
\alpha(f_{1})>\alpha(\lambda)
and thus
\alpha(f_{1})\in]\alpha(\lambda),\alpha(\lambda)+1[
. We have
\alpha(x_{2})>0
, thus
\alpha(f)>\alpha(\lambda)
. By Eq . (
3.1
),
\alpha(f)\in]\alpha(\lambda),\alpha(\lambda)+1[
. Therefore
\alpha+k
has constant sign on
\{f,f_{1}\}
, for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. With the same reasoning when
\alpha(F^{v})=\mathbb{R}_{<0}
, we deduce that
f
and
f_{1}
belong to the same face and thus
f_{1}\in F
. Consequently
x_{1}=t_{1}(f_{1}-\lambda)\in\Theta(F)
. Therefore we proved
\Theta(F)=F^{v}\in\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H}^{0}_{\lambda})
and thus
\Theta
is well-defined.

Let now
F^{v}\in\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H}^{0}_{\lambda})
and
f_{1},f_{2}\in\Omega\cap(\lambda+F^{v})
. Let
\alpha\in\Phi
. Let
x_{1}=f_{1}-\lambda
,
x_{2}=f_{2}-\lambda\in F^{v}
. If
\alpha\notin\Phi_{\lambda}
, then
\alpha(f_{1}),\alpha(f_{2})\in]\lfloor\alpha(\lambda)\rfloor,\lceil\alpha(\lambda)\rceil[
. Therefore
\alpha+k
has constant sign on
\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
, for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let now
\alpha\in\Phi_{\lambda}
. If
\alpha(F^{v})=\{0\}
, then
\alpha(f_{1})=\alpha(f_{2})=\alpha(\lambda)
and thus
\alpha+k
has constant sign on
\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
\alpha(F^{v})=\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, then
\alpha(x_{1}),\alpha(x_{2})>0
and thus
\alpha(f_{1}),\alpha(f_{2})\in]\alpha(\lambda),\alpha(\lambda)+1[
, by definition of
\Omega
. With the same reasoning when
\alpha(F^{v})=\mathbb{R}_{<0}
, we deduce that
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
belong the same face
F
of
\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
. Then
F
is the unique face of
\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
containing
(\lambda+F^{v})\cap\Omega\supset\mathrm{germ}_{\lambda}(\lambda+F^{v})
. Consequently,
\Xi(F^{v})=F
is well-defined and we have

\Omega\cap(\lambda+F^{v})\subset F=\Xi(F^{v}).

(3.6)

Moreover, for
x\in F
, we have
\mathrm{cl}(x)=\overline{F}
and thus
\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{germ}_{\lambda}(\lambda+F^{v}))=\overline{F}
.

Let
F\in\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
and
\tilde{F}=\Xi\circ\Theta(F)
. Then
\tilde{F}\supset\Omega\cap]\lambda,f[
for
f\in F
. Therefore
\tilde{F}\cap F
is non-empty and hence
F=\tilde{F}
, which proves that
\Xi\circ\Theta=\mathrm{Id}
.

Let
F^{v}\in\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H}^{0}_{\lambda})
. Set
\tilde{F}^{v}=\Theta\circ\Xi(F^{v})
. Let
f\in F^{v}
be such that
\lambda+f\in\Omega
. Then
\lambda+f\in\Xi(F^{v})
by Eq. (
3.6
). Therefore
\lambda+f-\lambda=f\in\tilde{F}^{v}
and hence
\tilde{F}^{v}\cap F^{v}\neq\emptyset
. Consequently
\tilde{F}^{v}=F^{v}
and
\Theta\circ\Xi=\mathrm{Id}
, which proves that
\Theta
is the inverse of
\Xi
.

Let
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
be such that
\overline{F_{2}}\supset F_{1}
. Then
\overline{\mathbb{R}_{>0}(F_{2}-\lambda)}=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}(\overline{F_{2}}-\lambda)\supset\mathbb{R}_{>0}(F_{1}-\lambda)
and thus
\Theta
is increasing.

Let
F_{1}^{v},F_{2}^{v}\in\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}^{0})
be such that
\overline{F^{v}_{2}}\supset F^{v}_{1}
. We have

\overline{\Xi(F_{2}^{v})}\supset(\lambda+\overline{F_{2}^{v}})\cap\Omega\supset(\lambda+F_{1}^{v})\cap\Omega\supset\Xi(F_{1}^{v})\cap\Omega,

by Eq. (
3.6
). Using Lemma
3.11
, we deduce that
\Xi(F_{1}^{v})\subset\overline{\Xi(F_{2}^{v})}
, which proves that
\Xi
is increasing. Thus
\Theta
is an isomorphism.

Using Eq. (
3.6
) and the assetion following it, we get the last claim of the proposition.
∎

3.1.4
Star of a face

In the sequel, we write
W_{x}^{\mathrm{aff}}=W_{\{x\}}^{\mathrm{aff}}
, for the fixator of
x\in\mathbb{A}
in
W^{\mathrm{aff}}
.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
C
be an alcove of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
and
J\subset C
be a non-empty set. Let
F
be the smallest face of
C
dominating
J
(this face is well-defined by Lemma
3.6
). Then
\bigcap_{x\in J}W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{x}=W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F}
.

Proof.

For every
x\in J
,
W_{F}^{\mathrm{aff}}=W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{\overline{F}}
fixes
x
and thus
W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F}\subset\bigcap_{x\in J}W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{x}
. Let
w\in\bigcap_{x\in J}W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{x}
. By Lemma
3.6
, there exists
y\in\mathrm{conv}(J)\cap F
. Then
w
fixes
y
. By Theorem
3.7
,
W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{y}=W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F}
, which proves that
w\in W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F}
and completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.15
.

Assume that
\Phi
is irreducible. Let
F
be a face of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
and let
C
be an alcove dominating
F
. Let
\Sigma^{\mathrm{aff}}_{C}
be the basis associated with
C
and set
\mathcal{M}_{F}=\{\underline{\alpha}\in\Sigma^{\mathrm{aff}}_{C}\mid\underline{\alpha}(F)>0\}
. Let
x\in\mathbb{A}
. Then there exists
w\in W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F}
such that
\underline{\alpha}(w.x)\geq 0
for every
\underline{\alpha}\in\Sigma^{\mathrm{aff}}_{C}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{F}
.

Proof.

If
F
is an alcove,
\Sigma^{\mathrm{aff}}_{C}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{F}
is empty and there is nothing to prove. We now assume that
F
is not an alcove. Let
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(F)
. Let
F^{v}=\mathbb{R}_{>0}(F-\lambda)
and
C^{v}=\mathbb{R}_{>0}(C-\lambda)
. By Proposition
3.12
,
F^{v}
and
C^{v}
are faces of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H}^{0}_{\lambda})
. Let
\Phi_{F^{v}}=\{\alpha\in\Phi_{\lambda}\mid\alpha(F^{v})=\{0\}\}
and let
\Phi_{F^{v},+}=\{\alpha\in\Phi_{F^{v}}\mid\alpha(C^{v})>0\}
. By Lemma
2.19
,
\Phi_{F^{v}}
is a root system in
(\mathrm{vect}(F^{v}))^{\perp}
. By
[
Bou81
, VI §1.7 Corollaire 1]
, there exists a chamber
C_{F^{v}}^{v}
of
(\mathrm{vect}(F^{v}))^{\perp}
such that
\Phi_{F^{v},+}=\{\alpha\in\Phi_{F^{v}}\mid\alpha(C_{F^{v}}^{v})>0\}
.

We can write
x-\lambda=u+v
, where
u\in\mathrm{vect}(F^{v})
and
v\in\mathrm{vect}(F^{v})^{\perp}
. Let
W^{v}_{\Phi_{F^{v}}}=\langle s_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi_{F^{v}}\rangle\subset W^{v}_{\Phi}
be the vectorial Weyl group of
\Phi_{F^{v}}
. Then
(\mathrm{vect}(F^{v}))^{\perp}
is the union of the closed vectorial chambers of
\Phi_{F^{v}}
and, since
W^{v}_{\Phi_{F^{v}}}
acts transitively on the set of vectorial chambers of
\Phi_{F^{v}}
, there exists
w\in W^{v}_{\Phi_{F^{v}}}
such that
\alpha(w.v)\geq 0
, for every
\alpha\in\Phi_{F^{v},+}
. We then have
\alpha(w.(x-\lambda))\geq 0
, for all
\alpha\in\Phi_{F^{v},+}
, since
W^{v}_{\Phi_{F^{v}}}
pointwise fixes
\mathrm{vect}(F^{v})
.

We have
t_{\lambda}W^{v}_{\Phi_{F^{v}}}t_{-\lambda}=W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F}
, where
t_{\mu}:\mathbb{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}
is the translation by the vector
\mu
, for
\mu\in\mathbb{A}
. Let
\underline{\alpha}\in\Sigma^{\mathrm{aff}}_{C}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{F}
. Set
\underline{w}=t_{\lambda}wt_{-\lambda}\in W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F}
. Write
\underline{\alpha}=\alpha+k
, where
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
\underline{\alpha}(\lambda)=0
and thus
k=-\alpha(\lambda)
. Moreover,
\underline{\alpha}(C)>0
and thus
\alpha\in\Phi_{F^{v},+}
. Then
\underline{\alpha}(\underline{w}.x)=\alpha(\lambda+w.(x-\lambda))-\alpha(\lambda)=\alpha(w.(x-\lambda))\geq 0
. This being true for every
\underline{\alpha}\in\Sigma^{\mathrm{aff}}_{C}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{F}
, the lemma follows.
∎

\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}(\mu)}=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathrm{conv}(W_{\Phi_{i},[\lambda_{i},\mu_{i}]}^{\mathrm{aff}}.\mathbf{b}_{i})=\mathrm{conv}\left(W_{\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}[\lambda_{i},\mu_{i}]}^{\mathrm{aff}}.\mathbf{b}_{C}\right),

that
\overline{F}=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}[\lambda_{i},\mu_{i}]
and that

.
∎

Proposition 3.27
.

1.

Let
J
be a non-empty subset of
\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})
. Then
\bigcap_{\lambda\in J}\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}
is non-empty if and only if there exists an alcove of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
dominating

. Moreover, if

(which exists by Lemma

, for any alcove
C
dominating

.

2.

If

, for any alcove

dominating

, where

3.

Let

be a face of

and

. Then

Proof.

1) Let
J\subset\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})
be non-empty. Assume that
\bigcap_{\lambda\in J}\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}
is non-empty. Let

and let
F
be the face of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
containing

. Then by Lemma
3.23
,
F
dominates
J
and, in particular,
J
is dominated by any alcove dominating
F
. Conversely, if
J
is dominated by a face
F
of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
, then
W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F}.\mathbf{b}_{C}\subset\bigcap_{\lambda\in J}\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}
, for any alcove
C
dominating
F
.

Let
C
be an alcove of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
and
J
be a subset of
\mathrm{vert}(C)
with cardinality at least
2
. Let
F
be the smallest face of
C
dominating
J
, which exists by Lemma
3.6
. Let
\lambda\in J
. Then

\overline{\mathcal{F}_{j}}:=\bigcap_{\mu\in J}\overline{\mathcal{P}(\mu)}=\bigcap_{\mu\in J\setminus\{\lambda\}}(\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}(\mu)}).

By Lemma
3.26
and
[
BG09
, Theorem 1.10]
,
\overline{\mathcal{F}_{J}}
is a face of

We have:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Extr}(\mathcal{F}_{J})

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\mu\in J\setminus\{\lambda\}}\mathrm{Extr}(\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}(\mu)})

\displaystyle\text{ by Lemma\penalty\ \ref{l_extreme_points_faces_polytope}},

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\mu\in J\setminus\{\lambda\}}W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{[\lambda,\mu]}.\mathbf{b}_{C}

\displaystyle=(\bigcap_{\mu\in J\setminus\{\lambda\}}W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{[\lambda,\mu]}).\mathbf{b}_{C}

\displaystyle=W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F}.\mathbf{b}_{C}

2) Let
F\in\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H})
and
C\in\mathrm{Alc}(\mathcal{H})
, dominating

. By 1) and by Lemma
2.4
, the set of extreme points of

3.18

3.24

3) Let

C
. Then it follows from (1) that

3.3
Tessellation of
\mathbb{A}
by the affine weight polytopes

We now prove that
\mathbb{A}
is tessellated by the
\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}
, for
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})
, as stated in the introduction in Theorem

.

Theorem 3.28
.

(see Figures
3.6
,
3.7
,
3.8
and
3.9
)

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a finite dimensional vector space and
\Phi
be a finite root system in
\mathbb{A}
. Let
\mathcal{H}=\{\alpha^{-1}(\{k\})\mid\alpha\in\Phi,\ k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, let
\mathscr{F}
be the set of faces of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
and let
\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})
be the set of vertices of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
. We choose a fundamental alcove
C_{0}
of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
and choose
\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{b}_{C_{0}}\in C_{0}
. If
\lambda
is a vertex of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
, we choose an alcove
C_{\lambda}
dominating
\lambda
and we set
\mathbf{b}_{C_{\lambda}}=w.\mathbf{b}
where
w
is the element of the affine Weyl group
W^{\mathrm{aff}}
of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
sending
C_{0}
to
C
. If
F
is a face of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
, we write
W_{F}^{\mathrm{aff}}:=\mathrm{Fix}_{W^{\mathrm{aff}}}(F)
and we set
\mathcal{F}_{F}=\mathrm{Int}_{r}(\mathrm{conv}(W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F}.\mathbf{b}_{C}))
, for any alcove
C
dominating
F
. Then
\bigsqcup_{F\in\mathscr{F}}\mathcal{F}_{F}=\mathbb{A}
. In particular, if we set
\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}=\overline{\mathcal{F}_{\{\lambda\}}}
, for
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})
, then
\mathbb{A}=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})}\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}
.

Proof.

Let
E=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})}\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}
. We prove that
E=\mathbb{A}
by proving that
E
is closed and dense in
\mathbb{A}
. Take
(z_{n})\in E^{\mathbb{N}}
a converging sequence. Then
(z_{n})
is bounded and by Lemma
3.23
(2), there exists a finite subset
F\subset\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})
such that
\{z_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset\bigcup_{\mu\in F}\overline{\mathcal{P}(\mu)}
. Then as the
\overline{\mathcal{P}(\mu)}
are closed,
\lim z_{n}\in\bigcup_{\mu\in F}\overline{\mathcal{P}(\mu)}\subset E
. Therefore
E
is closed.

Suppose that
E\neq\mathbb{A}
and fix
x\in\mathbb{A}\setminus E
. Let
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})
and
y\in\mathcal{P}(\lambda)
. Let
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow[x,y]
be the affine parametrization of
[x,y]
such that
\gamma(0)=y
and
\gamma(1)=x
. Let

t=\inf\{t^{\prime}\in[0,1]\mid\gamma(t^{\prime})\notin E\}.

As
E
is closed,
\gamma(t)\in E
. Let
\nu\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})
be such that
\gamma(t)\in\overline{\mathcal{P}(\nu)}
. Then
\gamma(t)\in\mathrm{Fr}(\mathcal{P}(\nu))
and thus there exists a facet
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
of
\overline{\mathcal{P}(\nu)}
containing
\gamma(t)
. By Propositions
3.24
and
3.27
, there exists
\nu^{\prime}\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})
, adjacent to
\nu
and such that
\overline{\mathcal{P}(\nu)}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}(\nu^{\prime})}=\overline{\mathcal{F}}
. If
\gamma(t)\in\mathcal{F}
, then by Lemma
2.1
, there exists
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that

B(\gamma(t),r)\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}(\nu)}=D\cap B(\gamma(t),r)\text{ and }B(\gamma(t),r)\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}(\nu^{\prime})}=D^{\prime}\cap B(\gamma(t),r),

where
D
and
D^{\prime}
are the two closed half-spaces delimited by
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F})
. But then
B(\gamma(t),r)\subset\overline{\mathcal{P}(\nu)}\cup\overline{\mathcal{P}(\nu^{\prime})}
, which is impossible by definition of
t
. Therefore

\gamma(t)\in\overline{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\mathcal{F}.

The facet
\mathcal{F}
is a polytope of
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F})
and thus its frontier is contained in a union
\bigcup_{V\in F(\nu,\nu^{\prime})}V
, where
F(\nu,\nu^{\prime})
is a finite set of affine subspaces of dimension at most
\dim\mathbb{A}-2
. For
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})
, set
F(\nu,\nu^{\prime})=\emptyset
if
\nu
and
\nu^{\prime}
are not adjacent. Then we proved:

\mathbb{A}\setminus E\subset\bigcup_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})}\bigcup_{V\in F(\nu,\nu^{\prime})}\mathrm{supp}(V\cup\{x\}).

As the right hand side of this formula is a countable union of proper affine subspaces of
\mathbb{A}
, it has Lebesgue measure
0
. In particular,
E
is dense in
\mathbb{A}
. But as
E
is closed,
E=\mathbb{A}
. Thus we proved

\mathbb{A}=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})}\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}.

As for
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})
,
\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}
is the union of the
\mathcal{F}_{F}
, where
F
runs over the faces of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
dominating
\lambda
(see Proposition
3.24
), we deduce that
\mathbb{A}=\bigcup_{F\in\mathscr{F}}\mathcal{F}_{F}
. Let
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathscr{F}
be such that
\mathcal{F}_{F_{1}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{F_{2}}
is non-empty. Then
\overline{\mathcal{F}_{F_{1}}}\cap\overline{\mathcal{F}_{F_{2}}}\neq\emptyset
and thus by Proposition
3.27
, there exists an alcove
C
dominating
\mathrm{vert}(F_{1})\cup\mathrm{vert}(F_{2})
, and
\mathcal{F}_{F_{1}},\mathcal{F}_{F_{2}}
are faces of
\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}
, for any
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(C)
, by Proposition
3.24
3). As the faces of
\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}
form a partition
\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}
(by
[
BG09
, Theorem 1.10]
), we deduce that
F_{1}=F_{2}
, which concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

4.1

\pi_{\lambda}
instead of
\pi_{\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}}
the orthogonal projection on
\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}
.

In this subsubsection, we prove that if
\lambda
and
\mu
are two vertices of

contained in a common closed alcove, then

(see Proposition
4.8
).

Lemma 4.1
.

1.

Let
F
be a face of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
and
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(F)
. Then:

\mathbb{R}_{>0}(F-\lambda)=\sum_{\mu\in\mathrm{vert}_{\lambda}(F)}\mathbb{R}_{>0}(\mu-\lambda)=\sum_{\mu\in\mathrm{vert}(F)}\mathbb{R}_{>0}(\mu-\lambda).

2.

Let
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})
and
F,F^{\prime}
be faces of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
dominating
\lambda
. Then
\mathrm{vert}_{\lambda}(F)\subset\mathrm{vert}_{\lambda}(F^{\prime})
if and only if
F
is dominated by
F^{\prime}
.

Proof.

1) The map
\mu\mapsto]\lambda,\mu[
is a bijection from
\mathrm{vert}_{\lambda}(F)
to the set of one dimensional faces of
F
dominating
\lambda
. By Proposition
2.6
and Proposition
3.12
,
\mathbb{R}_{>0}(F-\lambda)
is the sum of its one-dimensional faces and thus we have
\mathbb{R}_{>0}(F-\lambda)=\sum_{\mu\in\mathrm{vert}_{\lambda}(F)}\mathbb{R}_{>0}(\mu-\lambda)
. Using the notation of Eq. (
2.4
) (with
\ell=1
if
\Phi
is irreducible), we write
F=F_{1}\times\ldots\times F_{\ell}
and
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
. We have
\mathrm{vert}_{\lambda}(F)=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\{(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{i-1})\}\times\mathrm{vert}_{\lambda_{i}}(F_{i})\times\{(\lambda_{i+1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})\}
and
\mathrm{vert}(F)=\mathrm{vert}(F_{1})\times\ldots\times\mathrm{vert}(F_{\ell})
and the second equality of 1) follows.

2) If
F
is dominated by
F^{\prime}
, then
\mathrm{vert}(F)\subset\mathrm{vert}(F^{\prime})
and thus
\mathrm{vert}_{\lambda}(F)\subset\mathrm{vert}_{\lambda}(F^{\prime})
. Conversely, if
\mathrm{vert}_{\lambda}(F)\subset\mathrm{vert}_{\lambda}(F^{\prime})
, then
\overline{\mathbb{R}_{>0}(F-\lambda)}=\sum_{\mu\in\mathrm{vert}_{\lambda}(F)}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}(\mu-\lambda)\subset\sum_{\mu\in\mathrm{vert}_{\lambda}(F^{\prime})}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}(\mu-\lambda)=\overline{\mathbb{R}_{>0}(F^{\prime}-\lambda)}
, whence the result follows.
∎

.

Let
F
be a face of

Proof.

, the facets of

, with the notation of subsection

, we deduce
\pi_{\lambda}^{-1}(\{x\})=x+\sum_{\mu\in\mathrm{vert}_{\lambda}(F)}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}(\mu-\lambda)
. Then, the result follows from Lemma

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
C
be an alcove of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
and
F
be a proper subface of

which is not a vertex. Let

. Let

and
\nu\in\mathrm{vert}(C)\setminus\mathrm{vert}(F)
. Then
\pi_{\lambda}^{-1}(\{x\})\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}(\mu)}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}(\nu)}=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let
F(\mu,\nu)
be the smallest face of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
dominating both
\mu
and
\nu
.
Such face exists according to Lemma
3.6
. By Proposition
3.27
, we have:

\overline{\mathcal{P}(\mu)}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}(\nu)}=\bigcap_{\nu^{\prime}\in\mathrm{vert}_{\mu}(F(\mu,\nu))}\overline{\mathcal{P}(\mu)}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}(\nu^{\prime})}.

We have
F(\mu,\nu)\not\subseteq\overline{F}
and thus Lemma
4.1
implies the existence of
\nu^{\prime}
belonging to
\mathrm{vert}_{\mu}(F(\mu,\nu))\setminus\mathrm{vert}(F)
. Up to replacing
\nu
by
\nu^{\prime}
, we may assume that
\mu
and
\nu
are adjacent.

Let
\mathcal{D}(\mu,\nu)
be the closed half-space delimited by
\overline{\mathcal{P}(\mu)}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}(\nu)}
and containing
\overline{\mathcal{P}(\mu)}
. Let
V=x+\sum_{\tau\in\mathrm{vert}_{\mu}(F)}\mathbb{R}(\tau-\mu)=x+\mathrm{vect}(F-\mu)
. By Lemma
4.2
, we have

\pi_{\lambda}^{-1}(\{x\})=x+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}(\overline{F}-\lambda)\subset V.

Let
C^{v}=\mathbb{R}_{>0}(C-\mu)
and

. Then

(resp.

3.12
. Let
\Sigma
be the basis of
\Phi_{\mu}
associated with

. Write
\Sigma=\{\alpha_{i},i\in I\}
for some indexing set

. Let
(\varpi_{i})\in(\mathbb{A}^{*})^{I}
be the dual basis of
(\alpha_{i})_{i\in I}
. Let
J=\{i\in I\mid\varpi_{i}\in\overline{F^{v}}\}
. Then
\mathbb{R}_{>0}(\nu-\mu)
is a one dimensional face of

and thus
\nu-\mu\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\varpi_{i}
, for some

. Then
\mathcal{D}(\mu,\nu)-\mu
is the half-space of
\mathbb{A}
containing
\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\Phi_{\mu},\mathbf{b}_{C}-\mu}
and delimited by the support of
W^{v}_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}\varpi_{i}}.(\mathbf{b}_{C}-\mu)
, where

is the fixator of
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\varpi_{i}
in

. This half-space is denoted

in Lemma
2.28
.
Therefore by Lemma
2.28
, we have

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
F
be a face of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
. Then

F=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(F)}\left(\lambda+\mathbb{R}_{>0}(F-\lambda)\right).

Proof.

Let

We now prove the converse inclusion. By Lemma
4.1

E=\bigcap_{\lambda\in J}\left(\lambda+\bigoplus_{\mu\in J}\mathbb{R}_{>0}(\mu-\lambda)\right).

We first assume that
\Phi
is irreducible. Then by
[
Bou81

C
is a simplex and thus for every

\mathbb{A}

\lambda\in J
. Up to changing the

\mathbb{A}
, we may assume
\lambda=0

x\in E

\mu\in J\setminus\{0,\nu\}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\in\bigcap_{\lambda\in J_{i}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}\mathbb{A}_{j}\times(\lambda+\sum_{\mu\in J_{i}\setminus\{\lambda\}}\mathbb{R}_{>0}(\mu-\lambda))\times\prod_{j=i+1}^{\ell}\mathbb{A}_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}\mathbb{A}_{j}\times\bigcap_{\mu\in J_{i}\setminus\{\lambda\}}\left(\lambda+\sum_{\mu\in J_{i}}\mathbb{R}_{>0}(\mu-\lambda)\right)\times\prod_{j=i+1}^{\ell}\mathbb{A}_{j}

\{\mu\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})\mid\pi_{\lambda,\eta}(x)\in h_{\lambda,\eta}(\overline{\mathcal{P}(\mu)})\}=\{\mu\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})\mid\pi_{\lambda}(y)\in\overline{\mathcal{P}(\mu)}\}=\mathrm{vert}(F_{1}),

which proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.16
.

Let
\eta\in]0,1]
. Let
C\in\mathrm{Alc}(\mathcal{H})
and
x\in\overline{C}
. For
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(C)
, set
J_{\lambda}:=J_{\lambda,\eta}(x):=\{\nu\in\mathrm{vert}(C)\mid\pi_{\lambda,\eta}(x)\in h_{\lambda,\eta}(\overline{\mathcal{P}(\nu)})\}
. Let
N_{C}(x)=N_{C}:=\min\{|J_{\mu}|\mid\mu\in\mathrm{vert}(C)\}
and:

J=\{\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(C)\mid|J_{\lambda}|=N_{C}\}.

Then:

1.

There exists a face
F
of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
such that
J=\mathrm{vert}(F)
. There exists
f_{x}\in\overline{C}\cap\mathcal{F}_{F}
such that
\pi_{\lambda,\eta}(x)=h_{\lambda,\eta}(f_{x})
for all
\lambda\in J
. More generally, for every
\mu\in\mathrm{vert}(C)
, we have
\pi_{\mu,\eta}(x)=h_{\mu,\eta}(\pi_{\mu}(f_{x}))
.

2.

For every
\mu\in\mathrm{vert}(C)
, we have
J_{\mu}\supset J
; in the particular case when
\mu\in J
, there is an equality
J_{\mu}=J
.

3.

We have
x\in\overline{F_{\eta}(f_{x})}
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{0}\in J
. Let
F
be the face of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
dominating
\lambda_{0}
and such that
\pi_{\lambda_{0},\eta}(x)\in h_{\lambda_{0},\eta}(\mathcal{F}_{F})
. By Lemma
4.10
, we have
\pi_{\lambda_{0},\eta}(x)\in\overline{C}
. Let
F_{1}
be the face of
\overline{C}
containing
\pi_{\lambda_{0},\eta}(x)
. By Lemma
3.23
, we have that
\mathcal{F}_{F}
is contained in the star
E
of
F
. As
\lambda_{0}\in\mathrm{vert}(F)
and
\eta\in]0,1]
, the set
E
is stabilized by
h_{\lambda_{0},\eta}
and hence
h_{\lambda_{0},\eta}(\mathcal{F}_{F})\subset E
. Therefore
F_{1}
dominates
F
.

Write
\pi_{\lambda_{0},\eta}(x)=h_{\lambda_{0},\eta}(f_{x})
, with
f_{x}\in\mathcal{F}_{F}\cap\overline{C}
.
We have:

\displaystyle J_{\lambda_{0}}

\displaystyle=\{\nu\in\mathrm{vert}(C)\mid\pi_{\lambda_{0},\eta}(x)\in h_{\lambda_{0},\eta}(\overline{\mathcal{P}(\nu)})\}

\displaystyle=\{\nu\in\mathrm{vert}(C)\mid h_{\lambda_{0},\eta}(f_{x})\in h_{\lambda_{0},\eta}(\overline{\mathcal{P}(\nu)})\}

\displaystyle=\{\nu\in\mathrm{vert}(C)\mid f_{x}\in\overline{\mathcal{P}(\nu)}\}=\mathrm{vert}(F),

by Proposition
3.27
. By Remark
4.9
, we have

x\in h_{\lambda_{0},\eta}(f_{x})+\mathrm{vect}(F-\lambda_{0}).

Let
\mu\in\mathrm{vert}(F)
. Then

x\in h_{\mu,\eta}(f_{x})+(1-\eta)(\lambda_{0}-\mu)+\mathrm{vect}(F-\lambda_{0})=h_{\mu,\eta}(f_{x})+\mathrm{vect}(F-\mu).

By Lemma
4.15
, we have
\pi_{\mu,\eta}(x)\in h_{\mu,\eta}(f_{x})+\mathrm{vect}(F-\mu)
and

J_{\mu}=\{\nu\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathbb{A})\mid\pi_{\mu,\eta}(x)\in h_{\mu,\eta}(\overline{\mathcal{P}(\nu)})\}\subset\mathrm{vert}(F)=J_{\lambda_{0}}.

By minimality of
J_{\lambda_{0}}
, we deduce

J_{\mu}=J_{\lambda_{0}}=\mathrm{vert}(F)\subset J.

(4.6)

By the previous computations and by Proposition
3.27
, we have
h_{\mu,\eta}(f_{x}),\pi_{\mu,\eta}(x)\in h_{\mu,\eta}(\mathcal{F}_{F})\cap(x+\mathrm{vect}(F-\mu))
. It follows from Proposition
3.24
that
\mathrm{dir}(\mathrm{supp}(h_{\mu,\eta}(\mathcal{F}_{F})))=\mathrm{dir}(\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F}_{F}))=\mathrm{dir}(\mathrm{supp}(F))^{\perp}
and thus
h_{\mu,\eta}(\mathcal{F}_{F})\cap(x+\mathrm{vect}(F-\mu))
is either empty or a singleton. Therefore

h_{\mu,\eta}(f_{x})=\pi_{\mu,\eta}(x).

Let
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(F)
. By Lemma
4.2
, we have

x\in\pi_{\lambda,\eta}(x)+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}(\overline{F}-\lambda)=h_{\lambda,\eta}(f_{x})+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}(\overline{F}-\lambda).

Using Proposition
4.11
, we deduce that
x
belongs to:

\displaystyle\bigcap_{\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(F)}\left(h_{\lambda,\eta}(f_{x})+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}(\overline{F}-\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle=\eta f_{x}+(1-\eta)\bigcap_{\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(F)}(\lambda+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}(\overline{F}-\lambda))

\displaystyle=\eta f_{x}+(1-\eta)\overline{F}.

Using (
4.4
) we deduce statement 3):

x\in\mathrm{conv}(\{h_{\lambda,\eta}(f_{x})\mid\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(F)\}).

(4.7)

Let
\mu\in\mathrm{vert}(C)
. By Propositions
3.27
and
4.8
, we have

\pi_{\mu}(f_{x})\in\bigcap_{\nu\in\mathrm{vert}(F)\cup\{\mu\}}\overline{\mathcal{P}(\nu)}.

(4.8)

Let
F_{2}
be the face of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
such that
\pi_{\mu}(f_{x})\in\mathcal{F}_{F_{2}}
. Then by Proposition
3.27
, we have
\mathrm{vert}(F_{2})\supset\mathrm{vert}(F).
Let
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(F)
. By Lemma
4.12
, we have
\pi_{\mu,\eta}(h_{\lambda,\eta}(f_{x}))=h_{\mu,\eta}(\pi_{\mu}(f_{x}))
. By Eq. (
2.1
),
(\pi_{\mu,\eta})^{-1}\left(h_{\mu,\eta}\left(\pi_{\mu}\left(f_{x}\right)\right)\right)
is convex. Using Eq. (
4.7
) we deduce

\pi_{\mu,\eta}(x)=h_{\mu,\eta}\left(\pi_{\mu}\left(f_{x}\right)\right).

Using Eq. (
4.8
) we deduce that
J_{\mu}\supset\mathrm{vert}(F)\cup\{\mu\}=J_{\lambda_{0}}\cup\{\mu\}
. Consequently for
\mu\in\mathrm{vert}(C)\setminus J_{\lambda_{0}}
, we have
|J_{\mu}|\geq|J_{\lambda_{0}}|+1=N_{C}+1
and
\mu\notin J
. Therefore
J\subset J_{\lambda_{0}}
and hence by (
4.6
), we have

J=J_{\lambda_{0}}=\mathrm{vert}(F),

which completes the proof of the lemma.

∎

Lemma 4.17
.

Let
C\in\mathrm{Alc}(\mathcal{H})
and
x,x^{\prime}\in\overline{C}
. Then
x=x^{\prime}
if and only if (for all
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(C)
,
\pi_{\lambda}(x)=\pi_{\lambda}(x^{\prime})
).

Proof.

Assume that
\pi_{\lambda}(x)=\pi_{\lambda}(x^{\prime})
for every
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(C)
. For
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(C)
, set

J_{\lambda}=\{\mu\in\mathrm{vert}(C)\mid\pi_{\lambda}(x)\in\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}(\mu)}\}.

Let
J=\{\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(C)\mid|J_{\lambda}|=N_{C}(x)\}
. By Lemma
4.16
applied with
\eta=1
, we have
\{x,x^{\prime}\}\subset\bigcap_{\lambda\in J}\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}
. Take
\lambda\in J
. Then
\pi_{\lambda}(x)=x=\pi_{\lambda}(x^{\prime})=x^{\prime}
.
∎

Lemma 4.18
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{A}
,
\eta\in]0,1]
and
F
be the face of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
containing
x
. Then
\overline{F_{\eta}(x)}\subset F
.

Proof.

Let
F_{1}\in\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H})
be such that
x\in\mathcal{F}_{F_{1}}
. Then by Lemma
3.23
, we have
F_{1}\subset\overline{F}
. For
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(F_{1})
, we thus have
]\lambda,x]\subset F
and
h_{\lambda,\eta}(x)\in F
. Consequently
\overline{F_{\eta}(x)}=\mathrm{conv}(\{h_{\lambda,\eta}(x)\mid\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(F_{1}\})\subset F
.
∎

Theorem 4.19
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a finite dimensional vector space and
\Phi
be a finite root system in
\mathbb{A}
. Let
\mathcal{H}=\{\alpha^{-1}(\{k\})\mid\alpha\in\Phi,\,\,k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. We choose a fundamental alcove
C_{0}
of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
and choose
\mathbf{b}_{0}\in C_{0}
. If
\lambda
is a vertex of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
, we choose an alcove
C_{\lambda}
dominating
\lambda
and we set
\mathbf{b}_{C_{\lambda}}=w.\mathbf{b}_{0}
if
w
is the element of the affine Weyl group
W^{\mathrm{aff}}
of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
sending
C_{0}
to
C
. We set
\overline{\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}=\mathrm{conv}(W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{\lambda}.\mathbf{b}_{C_{\lambda}})
, where
W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{\lambda}
is the fixator of
\lambda
in
W^{\mathrm{aff}}
. If
x\in\mathbb{A}
, we define
F_{\mathcal{P}}(x)
as the unique face of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
such that
x\in\mathcal{F}_{F_{\mathcal{P}}(x)}:=\mathrm{Int}_{r}(\mathrm{conv}(W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F_{\mathcal{P}}(x)}.\mathbf{b}_{C}))
, where
\mathbf{b}_{C}
is an alcove dominating
F_{\mathcal{P}}(x)
and
W^{\mathrm{aff}}_{F_{\mathcal{P}}(x)}
is the fixator of
F_{\mathcal{P}}(x)
in
W^{\mathrm{aff}}
. If
\eta\in]0,1]
, we set
F_{\eta}(x)=\eta x+(1-\eta)F_{\mathcal{P}}(x)
. Then we have:

\forall\eta\in]0,1],\ \mathbb{A}=\bigsqcup_{x\in\mathbb{A}}\overline{F_{\eta}(x)}.

Proof.

Let
C
be an alcove of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
and
y\in\overline{C}
. For
\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(C)
, set
J_{\lambda,\eta}(y)=\{\mu\in\mathrm{vert}(C)\mid\pi_{\lambda,\eta}(y)\in h_{\lambda,\eta}(\overline{\mathcal{P}(\mu)})\}
. Let

N_{C,\eta}=\min\{|J_{\mu,\eta}(y)|\mid\mu\in\mathrm{vert}(C)\}
, and

\tilde{J}=\{\lambda\in\mathrm{vert}(C)\mid|J_{\lambda,\eta}(y)|=N_{C,\eta}\}.

By Lemma
4.16
, there exists
f_{y}\in\mathrm{Int}_{r}\left(\bigcap_{\nu\in\tilde{J}}\overline{\mathcal{P}(\nu)}\right)
such that
y\in\overline{F_{\eta}(f_{y})}
. Therefore
\bigcup_{x\in\overline{C}}\overline{F_{\eta}(x)}=\overline{C}
.

Let
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{A}
be such that
\overline{F_{\eta}}(x)\cap\overline{F_{\eta}}(x^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
and let
y\in\overline{F_{\eta}(x)}\cap\overline{F_{\eta}(x^{\prime})}
. Let
F
and
F^{\prime}
be the faces of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{H})
containing
x
and
x^{\prime}
respectively. Then by Lemma
4.18
,
y\in F\cap F^{\prime}
, which proves that
F=F^{\prime}
. Let
C
be an alcove dominating
F
. By Lemma
4.14
, we have
\pi_{\mu,\eta}(y)=h_{\mu,\eta}(\pi_{\mu}(x))=h_{\mu,\eta}(\pi_{\mu}(x^{\prime}))
for every
\mu\in\mathrm{vert}(C)
. Therefore
\pi_{\mu}(x)=\pi_{\mu}(x^{\prime})
for every
\mu\in\mathrm{vert}(C)
and
x=x^{\prime}
by Lemma
4.17
.
∎

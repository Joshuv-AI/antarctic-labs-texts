First-order general constitutive equations for relativistic fluids using the projection method in the Chapman-Enskog expansion of the Boltzmann equation
A. L. García-Perciante 1 , A. R. Méndez 1 , and O. Sarbach 2

Abstract




Abstract

The first-order out of equilibrium correction to the distribution function, obtained by implementing the projection method for the perturbed relativistic Boltzmann equation using the Chapman-Enskog method, is generalized in order to explicitly include the freedom of choice for thermodynamic frame and representation. It is shown how this procedure leads to general constitutive equations that couple the thermodynamic fluxes to all forces including a weak external electromagnetic field. Special cases of the resulting force-flux relations have been shown to lead to physically sound theories for relativistic fluids.

I
Introduction

The Chapman-Enskog (CE) expansion has been widely implemented in various contexts as a successful perturbative solution to the Boltzmann equation. Moreover, the procedure has been rigorously analyzed and proven to lead to a well rounded theory for dissipative hydrodynamics, based on the kinetic theory of gases
Cercigniani 2014
;
Saint-Raymond 2009
.
However, its formal generalization to the relativistic scenario was somehow paused for decades due to the belief that it unequivocally led to unstable first-order theories, similar to the ones proposed by Eckart and Landau
Eckart 1940
;
Landau and Lifshitz 1987

. Such task has only been recently resumed in view of the new proposals of physically sound first-order theories for dissipative relativistic fluids
Kovtun 2019
;
Bemfica et al. 2022

In recent works, using a rigorous CE implementation that generalizes Saint-Raymond’s formalism
Saint-Raymond 2014
;
Saint-Raymond 2009
to the relativistic case, we obtained a family of first-order non-equilibrium distribution functions giving rise to constitutive equations featuring generalized forces which contain both spatial and temporal projections of the gradients of the state variables
García-Perciante
et al. 2025
;
Gabarrete et al. 2025
. Moreover, in the presence of external fields this approach can in principle lead to dissipative contributions arising from them. Based on this novel and more formal procedure within the CE hypothesis for the relativistic Boltzmann equation, in Ref.
Gabarrete et al. 2025
a relativistic hydrodynamic theory for dissipative fluids was formulated in a particular thermodynamic frame which fixes the particle current and the trace of the energy-momentum-stress tensor to their equilibrium values. The motivation for choosing these conditions is based on the fact that they lead to the solution to the Boltzmann equation to first order in Knudsen’s parameter featuring no contribution from the homogeneous solution. That is, one demands orthogonality of the non-equilibrium correction with the kernel of the linearized collision operator. The resulting theory was analyzed and shown to be physically sound in Refs.
Salazar
et al. 2025a
;
Salazar
et al. 2025b
.

The particular choice mentioned above, which we refer to as the trace-fixed particle (TFP) frame, is justified by the freedom of choosing the variables describing non-equilibrium states which fixes a thermodynamic frame. This choice is clearly not unique. Moreover, in recent proposals Bemfica, Kovtun and collaborators (see for example
Kovtun 2019
;
Bemfica et al. 2022
) have established general first-order theories for relativistic dissipative fluids and shown that for some choices of frames the transport equations constitute a well-posed Cauchy problem with causal propagation and stable global equilibria. In those works the analysis is carried out in arbitrary frames and conditions for the parameters involved for the theory to have the desirable properties are established. Also, in Refs.
Rocha and Denicol 2021
;
Hoult and Kovtun 2022
;
Rocha et al. 2022
;
Rocha et al. 2024
the microscopic foundations of these theories are addressed, including the CE method. However, it is important to point out that the analysis carried out in Ref.
Rocha et al. 2022
for the CE method involves a procedure which eliminates the time derivatives from the first-order solution. Also a modified CE method is proposed in Refs.
Rocha et al. 2022
;
Rocha et al. 2024
, which differs from the approach presented in Refs.
García-Perciante
et al. 2025
;
Gabarrete et al. 2025
and studied in the present work.

The choice of frame in the phenomenological context is imposed by selecting
matching conditions
which are introduced in the general constitutive equations. In this work we discuss how this corresponds, in the microscopic framework, to choosing the compatibility conditions which are required for the uniqueness of the CE solution to the relativistic Boltzmann equation. Moreover, we show how the
constitutive equations in other frames can be obtained from the particular solution in the TFP frame by changing
the compatibility conditions. Furthermore, we show how introducing
a second freedom, namely the one corresponding to representation, finally leads to general first-order constitutive equations which are similar to those explored in current proposals. The two generalizations are introduced and thoroughly justified withing the kinetic theory formalism based on the formal CE expansion rigorously implemented via the projection method. It
is important to emphasize that this second freedom is crucial in order
to obtain all the force-flux couplings involved in the general theories,
since the particular solution to the Boltzmann equation only features
certain combinations of gradients of the state variables. To the authors’ knowledge, the
explicit introduction of this representation freedom within the kinetic
formalism, which was included in Ref.
Gabarrete et al. 2025
for the TFP frame, has not been tackled and thoroughly discussed so far in a rigorous manner. The relevance
of such step relies not only in allowing for all the forces to be present in the constitutive equations; more importantly, it is the one that opens the possibility of having a hyperbolic, stable and causal system of transport equations.

The main purpose of this work is thus to prove that one can obtain the complete general first-order constitutive equations from the theory obtained in Ref.
Gabarrete et al. 2025
for the TFP frame. Moreover, we also discuss the different justifications and implications that each of the two freedoms mentioned above have and how they enter such generalization. In order to accomplish this task, we begin by summarizing the setup of the problem in Section
II
, including the relativistic Boltzmann equation and the corresponding Chapman-Enskog first-order solution. In Section
III
we write the general solution to the first-order integral equation, as formally derived in Ref.
Gabarrete et al. 2025
, and describe each of the contributions to it in view of the choices of frame and representation, which are also briefly discussed. Section
IV
is devoted to the analysis of the homogeneous solution, where expressions for the coefficients involved are obtained in terms of general compatibility conditions. The particular cases of the so-called particle and energy frames are addressed in Section
VI
. A discussion of the results and final remarks are included in Section
VII
. We work in units for which the speed of light is one and take the signature convention
(-,+,+,+)
for the metric.

II
The Chapman-Enskog solution to the relativistic Boltzmann equation

In this section, the relativistic Boltzmann equation is stated, together with the first-order solution corresponding to the CE expansion. The notation and procedure follows the formal description discussed in Ref.
Gabarrete et al. 2025
. Here we only briefly quote the equations and results required for the discussion that follows, and the reader is referred to
Gabarrete et al. 2025
for further details.

II.1
The relativistic Boltzmann equation

The relativistic Boltzmann equation,

L_{F}\left[f\right]=Q\left[f,f\right],

(II.1)

describes the evolution of the distribution function
f(x,p)

by balancing
drift (left-hand side) and the effects of binary interactions (right-hand side) in phase space. Here, in terms of adapted local coordinates
\left(x^{\mu},p_{\mu}\right)
on the cotangent bundle associated with the spacetime manifold,

L_{F}=p^{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial g^{\alpha\beta}}{\partial x^{\mu}}p_{\alpha}p_{\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\mu}}+qF_{\mu}{}^{\nu}p_{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\mu}},

(II.2)

denotes the Liouville operator, including an external electromagnetic field
F^{\mu\nu}
, and
Q[f,f](x,p_{1})
is the integral operator that accounts for the balance of particles in and out of phase space cells due to binary elastic collisions. The particular
structure of
Q[f,f](x,p_{1})
is not required for the purpose of the present work, however it is convenient to point out as one of its most important properties that

\int\limits_{P_{x}^{+}(m)}\Psi\left(x,p\right)Q\left[f,f\right]\left(x,p\right)\mbox{dvol}_{x}(p)=0,

(II.3)

when
\Psi(x,p)\in\mathrm{span}\{1,p^{\mu}\}
is a collision invariant. Here
\mbox{dvol}_{x}(p)
is
the Lorentz-invariant volume element and the integration is performed within the future mass hyperboloid
P_{x}^{+}(m)

Acuña-Cárdenas
et al. 2022
.
Defining the particle current and energy-momentum-stress tensor
as

J^{\mu}\left(f\right):=\int\limits_{P_{x}^{+}(m)}f\left(x,p\right)p^{\mu}\mbox{dvol}_{x}(p),\qquad T^{\mu\nu}\left(f\right):=\int\limits_{P_{x}^{+}(m)}f\left(x,p\right)p^{\mu}p^{\nu}\mbox{dvol}_{x}(p),

(II.4)

one obtains that Eqs. (
II.1
) and (
II.3
), together with the identities
2

2

See for instance Theorem 4 in
Acuña-Cárdenas
et al. 2022
.

\displaystyle\nabla^{\mu}J_{\mu}\left(f\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{P_{x}^{+}(m)}L_{F}\left[f\right]\mbox{dvol}_{x}(p),

(II.5)

\displaystyle\nabla^{\mu}T_{\mu\nu}\left(f\right)+qF^{\mu}{}_{\nu}J_{\mu}\left(f\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{P_{x}^{+}(m)}p_{\nu}L_{F}\left[f\right]\mbox{dvol}_{x}(p),

(II.6)

leads to the transport equations

\nabla_{\mu}J^{\mu}=0,\qquad\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}+qJ_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}=0.

(II.7)

Hence, particle and energy-momentum balances are direct consequences of the symmetries involved in the relativistic Boltzmann equation. Moreover, as widely known (see for example Refs.
Acuña-Cárdenas
et al. 2022
;
de Groot et al. 1980
;
Cercignani and Kremer 2002
) the local equilibrium configurations are given by the Jüttner distribution function:

f^{(0)}\left(x,p\right)=\frac{n\left(x\right)}{2m\left(2\pi mk_{B}T\left(x\right)\right){}^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\textrm{{K}}_{\frac{d+1}{2}}(z)}\exp\left[\frac{u^{\nu}\left(x\right)p_{\nu}}{k_{B}T\left(x\right)}\right],

(II.8)

where
\textrm{{K}}_{\ell}\left(z\right)
denotes the modified Bessel function of the second kind of order
\ell
and the argument
z:=m/k_{B}T
measures the ratio
between the rest energy of the individual particles and the thermal energy of the gas. The state variables that parametrize this distribution
function are the particle number density
n
, the hydrodynamic velocity

u^{\mu}
and the temperature
T
. In an equilibrium situation,
the particle current and energy-momentum-stress tensor have the form

J^{\mu}=nu^{\mu},\qquad T^{\mu\nu}=neu^{\mu}u^{\nu}+\textrm{p}\Delta^{\mu\nu},

(II.9)

where
\Delta_{\nu}^{\mu}=\delta_{\nu}^{\mu}+u^{\mu}u_{\nu}
,
e=e\left(T\right)
is the internal energy density per unit mass and
\textrm{p}=\textrm{p}(n,T)
is the hydrostatic pressure. The balance equations (
II.7
) for these equilibrium states are given by the relativistic Euler equations which, denoting the space and time projections of
\nabla^{\mu}
as
D^{\mu}=\Delta^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\nu}
and
\dot{\left(\right)}=u^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}
respectively, can be written as

\dot{n}+\theta n=0,\quad\dot{T}+\frac{k_{B}}{c_{v}}\theta T=0,\quad a_{\nu}+\frac{D_{\nu}\textrm{p}}{nh}-\frac{q}{h}E_{\nu}=0.

(II.10)

Here,
\theta=\nabla^{\mu}u_{\mu}
,
a_{\mu}=\dot{u}_{\mu}
,
c_{v}=\partial e/\partial T
, and
E_{\nu}=F_{\nu\mu}u^{\mu}
is the electric field measured by a comoving observer.

II.2
Chapman-Enskog first order solution

The CE expansion is motivated by the observation that a disparity between microscopic and macroscopic characteristic length scales leads to an ordering scheme for the Boltzmann equation such that it can be treated perturbatively. More precisely, if the characteristic macroscopic scale corresponding to the size of the system or to the distance in which the gradients of the state variables are significant is denoted by
\ell_{{\text{m}s}}
and the mean free path between collisions by
\ell_{\text{mfp}}
, for weak electromagnetic fields one finds that the right-hand side of Eq. (
II.1
) differs from the left-hand side in one order of Knudsen’s parameter
\varepsilon:=\ell_{\text{mfp}}/\ell_{{\text{m}s}}
. In fact Eq. (
II.1
) when written in terms of dimensionless quantities, yields

L_{F}\left[f\right]=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}Q\left[f,f\right],

(II.11)

which motivates the proposal of a perturbative solution as an expansion in terms of
\varepsilon
. Thus, one assumes that the non-equilibrium distribution function can be written in general as

f=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{\ell}f^{\left(\ell\right)},

(II.12)

where
f^{(0)}
denotes a local Jüttner distribution as given in Eq. (
II.8
), where the variables
n
,
u^{\mu}
, and
T
that parameterize it are the ones whose dynamics are determined by the transport equations to all orders in
\varepsilon
. That is, the first term in the expansion does not correspond to the global equilibrium configuration but rather to the corresponding local equilibrium distribution function parameterized by out-of equilibrium state variables. In the present work, we are interested in analyzing the first-order correction within this method, and thus we seek a solution of the form (in terms of dimensional variables)

f\approx f^{(0)}+f^{(1)},

(II.13)

where it is understood that
f^{(1)}
is of first order in
\varepsilon
and
f^{(0)}
is parameterized by
n
,
u^{\nu}
, and
T
given by the equations of motion (
II.7
) up to order 1 in
\varepsilon
. Introducing Eq. (
II.13
) into Eq. (
II.1
) one obtains, to order zero

Q\left[f^{(0)},f^{(0)}\right]=0,

(II.14)

which is satisfied by the local Jüttner distribution function (
II.8
). The next-order equation reads

\mathcal{L}\left[f^{(1)}/f^{(0)}\right]=-L_{F}\left[\ln f^{(0)}\right],

(II.15)

where
\mathcal{L}
is the linearized collision operator:

\mathcal{L}\left[\phi\right]\left(x,p_{1}\right):=\int\limits_{P_{x}^{+}(m)}\int\limits_{S^{d-1}}\mathcal{F}\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}f_{2}^{(0)}\left[\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}-\phi_{1}^{*}-\phi_{2}^{*}\right]d\Omega\left(\hat{q}^{*}\right)\mbox{dvol}_{x}(p_{2}).

(II.16)

Here
\mathcal{F}
denotes the invariant flux,
d\sigma/d\Omega

the differential cross section, and
\Omega
the solid angle. The
subindex 1 or 2 is introduced in order to distinguish the colliding
particles, and the notation
(...)^{*}
to differentiate between pre- and post-collision particles.

III
General first-order solution

As thoroughly explained in Ref.
Gabarrete et al. 2025
, in the presence of a weak background electromagnetic field,
the general first-order solution to Eq. (
II.15
) using the projection method can be written
as

f^{(1)}=-f^{(0)}\left[\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[\left(p^{\mu}p^{\nu}\right)^{\perp}\right]+mA+B_{\mu}p^{\mu}\right],

(III.1)

where:

•

\left(...\right)^{\perp}
denotes the projection onto the orthogonal
complement of
\ker\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{span}\{1,p^{\mu}\}
with respect to the inner product

\langle\psi,\phi\rangle:=\int_{P_{x}^{+}(m)}\psi\left(p\right)\phi\left(p\right)f^{(0)}\left(x,p\right)\mbox{dvol}_{x}(p).

(III.2)

•

\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu\nu}
is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{k_{B}T}\left\{\sigma_{\mu\nu}-u_{\mu}\left(a_{\nu}+\frac{D_{\nu}T}{T}\right)+\left(\frac{\dot{T}}{T}+\frac{\theta}{d}\right)u_{\mu}u_{\nu}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left[\hat{\Gamma}_{0}\left(\frac{\dot{n}}{n}+\theta\right)+\hat{\Gamma}_{1}\left(\frac{c_{v}}{k_{B}}\frac{\dot{T}}{T}+\theta\right)\right]u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+\hat{\Gamma}_{2}\left(\frac{h}{k_{B}T}a_{\nu}+\frac{D_{\nu}\textrm{p}}{\textrm{p}}-\frac{q}{k_{B}T}E_{\nu}\right)u_{\mu}\right\},

(III.3)

where
\sigma_{\mu\nu}
and
h
denote the shear and enthalpy per particle, respectively. The first line in Eq. (
III.3
) corresponds to
\left(L_{F}\left[\ln f^{\left(0\right)}\right]\right)^{\perp}
.
The second line adds a multiple of quantities that are of higher order in Knudsen’s parameter. The freedom of including these terms arises from the fact that, thanks to the balance equations, the expressions in parentheses in the second line of Eq. (
III.3
) can be added to the first-order solution without altering it to this order.

•

\mathcal{L}^{-1}:(\ker\mathcal{L})^{\perp}\to(\ker\mathcal{L})^{\perp}
denotes the inverse of the linearized collision operator when restricted to
(\ker\mathcal{L})^{\perp}
. Sufficient conditions on the cross section for the invertibility have been thoroughly discussed in Ref.
Gabarrete et al. 2025
.

•

The last two terms in Eq. (
III.1
) correspond to an arbitrary function belonging
\ker\mathcal{L}
, which constitutes
the homogeneous solution. The quantities
A
and
B_{\mu}
are functions
of the state variables only, and as will be explained in detail in the remaining of this article, they give rise to a family of constitutive relations.

It is important to point out that there is a profound difference in
the arguments behind the introduction of the coefficients
A
and

B_{\mu}
, and the “parameters”
\hat{\Gamma}_{0,1,2}
. The former are mandatory in order to obtain the most general solution to the integral equation and correspond to the homogeneous part of the solution.
The latter are included as a mere freedom of doing so while retaining
the order of the approximation, but have nothing to do with the existence
nor the uniqueness of the solution.
Furthermore, it is worthwhile to emphasize at this point that these terms are not
introduced as on-shell corrections after the constitutive equations
are obtained in order to achieve hyperbolicity in the macroscopic
transport equations. In this context they are included as part of
the solution within the microscopic calculation and actually do not
alter the frame choice. Furthermore, in the present approach, these
terms appear in such a way that the TFP frame, which is obtained when

A
and
B_{\mu}
vanish and only the particular solution is present,
is still exactly enforced to first order even off-shell. This is in contrast
to phenomenological approaches where the frame is retained only on-shell
up to first order (see for example
Bemfica et al. 2022
).

IV
Homogeneous solution and frame fixing

In order to write a general solution to the linearized Boltzmann equation
to first order within the Chapman-Enskog scheme, one first guarantees
existence of such solution by projecting the right-hand side of Eq. (
II.15
) onto the orthogonal complement of
\ker\mathcal{L}
.
The arguments that support the previous statement are thoroughly discussed

Saint-Raymond 2014

VI
Solution in particular frames

In the present work, as well as in the most
recent literature on first-order relativistic fluid theories, we adopt
the term
frame
as the
set of variables that describe the non-equilibrium state by specifying
the way in which they converge to the state variables
n
,
T
,
and
u^{\mu}
in equilibrium. This choice is determined by fixing
matching conditions in the phenomenological approach and compatibility
conditions (choosing
g_{i}
in Eq. (
IV.1
)) within
kinetic theory. However, this terminology is unfortunate, as was already
pointed out in Ref.
Kovtun 2019
, and can be misleading since one usually associates the
term frame to a
reference

frame. However in the present context, this choice implies fixing
a
thermodynamic
frame. Such term may have been adopted since the first and most traditional descriptions for dissipative fluids, proposed by Eckart
Eckart 1940
and Landau
Landau and Lifshitz 1987
, only differ from each other in the
choice for the hydrodynamic velocity and thus, can be misinterpreted
as considering two different observers for the description of the
system. Indeed, for both cases one has, using the non-equilibrium
quantities defined in the previous section:

e_{E}^{\left(1\right)}=e_{L}^{\left(1\right)}=0,\quad n_{E}^{\left(1\right)}=n_{L}^{\left(1\right)}=0.

(VI.1)

The velocity in the case of Eckart is fixed by imposing
J_{\left(1\right)E}^{\mu}=0

while the Landau proposal is characterized by
T_{\left(1\right)L}^{\mu\nu}u_{\mu L}=0
.
It is probably because of these particular choices that the two versions
of linear relativistic non-equilibrium thermodynamics were referred
to as two different frames. However, it is important to remark that in Refs.
Israel 1976
;
Israel and Stewart 1979
W. Israel and J. M. Stewart recognized the possibility of considering other frames as first-order corrections from equilibrium configurations by performing first-order transformations on the velocity. This gave rise to a family of allowed frames, which only differ in the velocity and for which the theory remains invariant, reinforcing the idea that a frame is directly linked to an observer. Nevertheless, the term thermodynamic frame is broader and refers to the assignment of all thermodynamic variables and not only the hydrodynamic velocity.

Having clarified this point, in the rest of this section we obtain the constitutive equations for particular choices of two of the functions
g_{i}
leaving the third one unspecified, which leads to a family of frames. In this sense, we define a particle frame (following Refs.
Salazar
et al. 2025a
,
Salazar
et al. 2025b
,
García-Perciante
et al. 2025
) as any frame for which
J^{\mu}=nu^{\mu}
holds even out of equilibrium, and an energy frame as any frame for which
T_{\left(1\right)}^{\mu\nu}u_{\mu}=0
. The particular cases of Eckart’s (particle) and Landau’s (energy) frames are addressed for illustrative purposes.

VII
Summary and final remarks

In this work we have formally shown that the first-order out of equilibrium solution that is obtained using the projection method developed in Ref.
Saint-Raymond 2014
can be generalized in order to establish constitutive equations that couple dissipative fluxes to all possible thermodynamic forces of the corresponding tensorial rank. These relations contain three coefficients that are related to the choice of frame
\chi_{i}
and three arbitrary parameters
\hat{\Gamma}_{i}
which correspond to the freedom of choosing a representation, and they can be written as

\displaystyle n^{\left(1\right)}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\nu_{i}F^{i},\qquad ne^{\left(1\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\varepsilon_{i}F^{i},\qquad\textrm{p}^{\left(1\right)}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\pi_{i}F^{i},

(VII.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\gamma_{i}\bar{F}^{i\alpha},\qquad\mathcal{Q}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\kappa_{i}\bar{F}^{i\alpha},\qquad\mathcal{T^{\alpha\beta}}=-2\eta\text{F}^{\alpha\beta},

(VII.2)

with
F^{1}=\dot{n}/n
,
F^{2}=\dot{T}/T
,
F^{3}=\theta
,
\bar{F}^{1\alpha}=D^{\alpha}n/n
,

\bar{F}^{2\alpha}=D^{\alpha}T/T
,
\bar{F}^{3\alpha}=a^{\alpha}
,
\bar{F}^{4\alpha}=qE^{\alpha}/h
, and

\text{F}^{\alpha\beta}=\sigma^{\alpha\beta}
, where the coefficients

\nu_{i}
,
\varepsilon_{i}
,
\pi_{i}
,
\gamma_{i}
, and
\kappa_{i}
are written explicitly in Appendix
B
.

We can summarize the findings of this article as follows. Starting from the expression that arises by implementing the projection
method in the Chapman-Enskog expansion, we exhibited that the core structure of the constitutive
equations for a single species charged gas (in the presence of a weak background electromagnetic field) relates the scalar dissipative
quantities with the combination
\dot{T}/T+\theta/d
,
the vector ones with
a_{\nu}+D_{\nu}T/T
, while the
tensor one is only coupled with
\sigma_{\mu\nu}
. Such relations
arise in the trace-fixed particle frame for which the compatibility
conditions are given by
g_{1}=1
,
g_{2}=\gamma
,
and
g_{3}=1
.

This particular frame is the one obtained when orthogonality conditions
are imposed over the elements of the kernel of the linearized collision operator
\ker\mathcal{L}
. Starting with this particular structure,
one can extend the results to an arbitrary frame by choosing different values
for
g_{i}
which adds a contribution from the homogeneous equation. As we have shown in this work, this leads to a change in
the coefficients which does not break
the combinations mentioned above or introduces new forces in the constitutive equations. As examples, we have
included the particular cases of the particle and energy frames, addressing
within them Eckart and Landau’s frames, respectively. One can then
conclude that, from the kinetic theory point of view, a change of
frame can only lead to shifts in the coefficients that keep the aforementioned combinations of the forces invariant.

However, in order to obtain the most general constitutive equations from kinetic theory, one needs to consider an additional freedom,
which we refer to as the choice of representation. This entails allowing
for nonzero values of the parameters
\hat{\Gamma}_{i}
which multiply second-order terms in Knudsen’s parameter. This leads to the final form of the constitutive equations (
V.5
), (
V.6
)
and (
V.10
)-(
V.13
). These relations coincide in structure with the ones proposed and thoroughly discussed in recent works within the context of the BDNK theories.
However, we wish to emphasize that the derivation described in the present work is different form Refs.
Rocha et al. 2022
;
Rocha et al. 2024
and arises from projecting the right-hand side of the linearized Boltzmann equation onto the orthogonal complement of
\ker\mathcal{L}
following Ref.
Saint-Raymond 2014
and formally established in the relativistic scenario in Ref.
Gabarrete et al. 2025
. Moreover, the method carried out here clearly separates the effects of changes of frames and representations and sheds light on the microscopic origin of such freedoms.

As has been shown in previous works, the freedom of choosing the parameters
\hat{\Gamma}_{i}
is fundamental for achieving a physically sound system of relativistic hydrodynamic equations. In the context of the TFP frame this has been established in Refs.
Salazar
et al. 2025a
;
Salazar
et al. 2025b
for a large class of models which includes a simple gas with hard spheres’ cross sections. For conditions leading to hyperbolic and causal evolution equations in unspecified frames, see for instance Refs.
Kovtun 2019
;
Bemfica et al. 2022
.

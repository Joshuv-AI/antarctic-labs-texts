On the Euler class one conjecture for fillable contact structures Thanks: Partially supported by NSFC Grant 11925101, and National Key R&D Program of China 2020YFA0712800
Yi Liu

Abstract
. In this paper, it is proved that every oriented closed hyperbolic 3 3 –manifold N N admits some finite cover M M with the following property. There exists some even lattice point w w on the boundary of the dual Thurston norm unit ball of M M , such that w w is not the real Euler class of any weakly symplectically fillable contact structure on M M . In particular, w w is not the real Euler class of any transversely oriented, taut foliation on M M . This supplies new counter-examples to Thurston’s Euler class one conjecture.

1.
Introduction

For any orientable compact
3
–manifold
M
,
the dual Thurston norm unit ball
is a well-defined compact polytope in
H^{2}(M,\partial M;{\mathbb{R}})
.
It is central symmetric about the origin, and possibly of positive codimension.
The polytope is the convex hull of finitely many vertices in the integral lattice.
When
M
is closed,
the vertices are all even lattice points
(that is, elements that are divisible by
2
in
H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
modulo torsion).

If
M
is oriented, and closed, and irreducible,
then the real Euler class of any transversely oriented taut foliation on
M

must be an even lattice point in the dual Thurston norm unit ball.
Thurston established this result,
and posed the following partial converse.

Conjecture 1.1

(Euler Class One)
.

For any oriented, closed, irreducible
3
–manifold
M
,
every even lattice point in
H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
of dual Thurston norm
1

is the real Euler class of some transversely oriented taut foliation on
M
.

The special case of Conjecture
1.1

regarding vertices of the dual Thurston norm unit ball holds true.
In fact, Gabai proves that every vertex can be realized
with some transversely oriented taut foliation
(with smooth leaves and continuous holonomy),
using sutured manifold decompositions;
see
[
GabY20
, Section 3]
for detail.

The general case of Conjecture
1.1
turns out to be false.
In fact, Yazdi constructs explicit counter-examples
among oriented closed hyperbolic
3
–manifolds
of first Betti number
2

[
Yaz20
]
.

In this paper, we construct new counter-examples to Conjecture
1.1

by using finite covers of an arbitrary oriented closed hyperbolic
3
–manifold.
Our construction shows that Conjecture
1.1
fails quite often.
Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every oriented closed hyperbolic
3
–manifold
M
,
there exists some connected finite cover
\tilde{M}
of
M
,
and some even lattice point
\tilde{w}\in H^{2}(\tilde{M};{\mathbb{R}})
of dual Thurston norm
1
,
such that
\tilde{w}
is not the real Euler class
of any weakly symplectically fillable contact structure on
\tilde{M}
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Every oriented closed hyperbolic
3
–manifold has a finite cover
for which Conjecture
1.1
does not hold.

Corollary
1.3
is an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.2
.
In fact, transversely oriented taut foliations
can always be perturbed (as plane distributions)
into weakly symplectically fillable contact structures,
by well-known theorems
due to Eliashberg and Thurston, and due to Etnyre (see Section
2.2
).

Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.3

supply new counter-examples to Conjecture
1.1
.
Indeed, as shown by Sivek and Yazdi,
the counter-example cohomology classes in Yazdi’s counter-example manifolds
can be realized by
weakly symplectically fillable contact structures
[
SivY23
, Theorem 1.1]
.

By iterating the construction in Theorem
1.2
,
one can easily produce new non-realizable cohomology classes in higher finite covers.
However,
I do not know if an obtained non-realizable cohomology class
would turn into a realizable one,
after pulling back to some higher finite cover.
Non-realizable cohomology classes
virtually keep emerging, and they might virtually evaporate.

See Section
6
for more technical discussion
related to other unsolved variants of the Euler class one conjecture.

Ingredients

In the rest of the introduction,
we explain the main idea to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Key to our construction is a deep connection between
the next-to-top term in Heegaard Floer homology for a pseudo-Anosov mapping torus
and the
1
–periodic trajectories of the pseudo-Anosov suspension flow.
This connection has been established by works of
Cotton-Clay
[
Cot09
]
,
and Kutluhan–Lee–Taubes
[
KutLT20a
,
KutLT20b
,
KutLT20c
,
KutLT20d
,
KutLT20e
]
,
and Lee–Taubes
[
LeeT12
]
.

On the Heegaard Floer homology side,
Ozsváth and Szabó introduce an obstruction
to the existence of a weak symplectic filling
(W,\omega)

of a connected, closed, transversely oriented contact
3
–manifold
(Y,\xi)

[
OzsS04c
, Section 4]
.
The obstruction class lives in the hat-flavor Heegaard Floer homology of
the orientation-reversal
-Y
with twisted coefficients associated to
\omega

(see Remark
3.5
for further information).
It is supported in the direct summand
at the canonical
\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathtt{c}}
structure
\mathfrak{s}_{\xi}
of
\xi
.

Based on a nonvanishing criterion due to Ozsváth and Szabó
[
OzsS04c
, Theorem 4.2]
,
we duduce a useful simplified version.
Our simplified version says that weak symplectic fillability of
(Y,\xi)

implies nonvanishing of
\widehat{\mathrm{HF}}(-Y,\mathfrak{s}_{\xi})
,
(even if the contact invariant vanishes therein).
See Lemma
3.4
and Remark
3.5
.
When
Y
is the mapping torus
M_{f}
of some pseudo-Anosov automorphism
f\colon S\to S
,
and when
\langle c_{1}(\mathfrak{s}_{\xi}),[S]\rangle=-\chi(S)-2
,
the nonvanishing of
\widehat{\mathrm{HF}}(-Y,\mathfrak{s}_{\xi})

can be translated into the existence of
1
–periodic trajectories
in some particular first homology class (depending on
\mathfrak{s}_{\xi}
).
The translation follows from the aforementioned connection.
See Lemma
3.6
for the precise statement.
We use these results to establish a preparatory criterion (Lemma
3.2
),
so as to facilitate direct application.

On the pseudo-Anosov dynamics side,
our basic tool is the Fried cone of homological directions
[
FatLP12
, Exposé 14]
.
For any pseudo-Ansov automorphism
f\colon S\to S
,
every periodic trajectory of the pseudo-Anosov suspension flow in the mapping torus
M_{f}

determines a ray in
H_{1}(M_{f};{\mathbb{R}})
,
emanating from the origin and passing through the homology class of the periodic trajectory.
The closure of all such rays in
H_{1}(M_{f};{\mathbb{R}})

forms what is called the Fried cone of homological directions.

The Fried cone is a polyhedral cone.
To every boundary face of the Fried cone,
we can associate
a finite collection of infinite-index quasiconvex subgroups of
\pi_{1}(M_{f})
.
These subgroups capture all the periodic trajectories
whose homology direction on that face, in a suitable sense
(see Lemma
4.5
for a precise statement).
Base on the virtual compact specialization of closed hyperbolic
3
–manifold groups
due to Agol
[
Ago13
]
and Wise
[
Wis12
]
,
we are able to make use of the above subgroups,
and construct some finite cover of
M_{f}
,
such that the Fried cone of the finite cover
has a boundary face on which a generic rational homology direction
is vacuum of periodic trajectories.
Precisely, we show that
the face has a scaling-invariant open dense subset,
such that no periodic trajectory represents any rational homology class therein
(see Lemma
4.2
for detail).
Virtual constructions with similar ideas to this part
have appeared in former works of the author
[
Liu20
,
Liu23a
]
.

With these ingredients, our proof of Theorem
1.2

can be outlined as follows.

Given any oriented closed hyperbolic
3
–manifold
M
,
we first obtain a fibered finite cover
M^{\prime}
,
by the virtual fibering theorem
[
Ago13
]
.
Next, we construct a finite cover
M^{\prime\prime}
of
M^{\prime}
using Lemma
4.2
,
such that
M^{\prime\prime}
has some Fried cone face with generically vacuum rational homology directions,
as described above.
Finally, we construct a finite cyclic cover
\tilde{M}
of
M^{\prime\prime}
.
Passing from
M^{\prime\prime}
to
\tilde{M}
will enable us to promote
a vacuum rational homology direction in
H^{2}(M^{\prime\prime};{\mathbb{R}})\cong H_{1}(M^{\prime\prime};{\mathbb{R}})

to a genuine candidate cohomology class

\tilde{w}
in
H^{2}(\tilde{M};{\mathbb{R}})\cong H_{1}(\tilde{M};{\mathbb{R}})
.
The last step relies on a cyclic cover trick as we explain in Lemma
5.1
.
Using our preparatory criterion (Lemma
3.2
),
we verify that
(\tilde{M},\tilde{w})
is as asserted in Theorem
1.2
.
See Section
5
for detail.

.

2.
Preliminary

In this section, we collect preliminary facts
about the Thurston norm and contact structures.

2.1.
The dual Thurston norm unit ball

We review the Thurston norm,
focusing on properties of the dual Thurston norm unit ball.
See
[
Thu86
]
for the reference.

For any finite-dimensional real vector space
V
,
a
seminorm
on
V
refers to a function
\|\cdot\|\colon V\to[0,+\infty)

with the properties
\|rv\|=|r|\cdot\|v\|
and
\|v+w\|\leq\|v\|+\|w\|
,
for all
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
and for all
v,w\in V
.
A
norm
is a seminorm that is nondegenerate,
namely,
\|v\|>0
for all nonzero
v\in V
.
Every seminorm
\|\cdot\|
on
V

determines a dual
partial norm
on the dual vector space

V^{*}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(V,{\mathbb{R}})
by the expression

\|u^{*}\|^{*}=\mathrm{sup}\{|\langle u^{*},v\rangle|\colon\|v\|=1\}
,
which takes values in
[0,+\infty]
,
and the subset with finite values forms the linear subspace
W^{*}
of
V^{*}
,
which is dual to the zero-norm subspace
W
of
\|\cdot\|
, namely,

W=\{v\in V\colon\|v\|=0\}
and
W^{*}=\{u\in V^{*}\colon u^{*}|_{W}=0\}
.
For any seminorm
\|\cdot\|
on
V
, the dual partial norm
\|\cdot\|^{*}
on
V^{*}

is a norm restricted to the maximal finite subspace
W^{*}
.
Therefore, the unit ball of
\|\cdot\|
in
V
is
a
0
–codimensional closed subset,
which is symmetric about the origin and
is invariant under translations parallel to the zero-norm subspace;
the unit ball of
\|\cdot\|^{*}
in
V^{*}
is
a bounded closed subset,
which is symmetric about the origin,
and is contained in the finite-norm subspace with codimension
0
.

For any orientable connected compact
3
-manifold
M
,
the
Thurston norm
for
M
is a seminorm on the real linear space
H_{2}(M,\partial M;{\mathbb{R}})
,
which we recall as follows.
First,
for any oriented connected compact surface
\Sigma
, define the
complexity
of
S

to be the nonnegative integer
\mathrm{max}\{-\chi(S),0\}
.
Then, the complexity of an oriented compacted surface
S

refers to the sum of the complexity of the connected components of
S
.
For any integral relative
2
–homology class
\Sigma\in H_{2}(M,\partial M;{\mathbb{Z}})
,
assign
\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{Th}}
to be the minimum complexity of
S
,
among all properly embedded, oriented, compact subsurfaces
(S,\partial S)\subset(M,\partial M)

which represents
\Sigma
.
Thurston shows that
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{Th}}
on
H_{2}(M,\partial M;{\mathbb{Z}})

(exists and) extends uniquely to be a seminorm,
denoted as

\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{Th}}\colon H_{2}(M,\partial M;{\mathbb{R}})\to[0,+\infty).

By generality, we also obtain a dual partial norm,
which we refer to
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}
as the
dual Thurston norm
of
M
,
and denote as

\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}\colon H^{2}(M,\partial M;{\mathbb{R}})\to[0,+\infty].

We denote the unit balls of the Thurston norm and the dual Thurston norm as

\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}}(M)\subset H_{2}(M,\partial M;{\mathbb{R}}),

and

\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)\subset H^{2}(M,\partial M;{\mathbb{R}}),

respectively.

Thurston shows that the dual Thurston norm unit ball
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)

is a polytope, which is the convex hull in
H^{2}(M,\partial M;{\mathbb{R}})

of finitely many points in the integral lattice,
(that is,
H^{2}(M,\partial;{\mathbb{Z}})
modulo torsion).
In dual terms,
this is to say that the Thurston norm unit ball
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}}(M)

is the intersection of finitely many half-spaces in
H_{2}(M,\partial M;{\mathbb{R}})

defined by inequalities of the form
l_{i}(\Sigma)\leq 1
, for
i=1,\cdots,k
,
where
l_{i}
are linear functionals on
H_{2}(M,\partial M;{\mathbb{R}})

over
{\mathbb{Z}}
with respect to the integral lattice.
When
M
is closed,
the vertices of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)
are all even lattice points,
reflecting the fact
the oriented closed surfaces all have even Euler characteristics.

When
M
fibers over a circle as a surface bundle,
the bundle projection
\pi\colon M\to S^{1}

represents a primitive integral cohomology class
\phi_{\pi}\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
,
upon fixing an orientation of
S^{1}
.
The Poincaré dual is a relative homology class

\mathrm{PD}(\phi_{\pi})\in H_{2}(M,\partial M;{\mathbb{Z}})
,
upon fixing an orientation of
M
.
In this case,
Thurston shows that
\phi_{\pi}
lies in the open cone
over a unique codimension–
1
open face of
\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}}(M)
,
and every primitive integral cohomology class
\psi\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})

with
\mathrm{PD}(\psi)
in that cone represents a fibering of
M
over a circle.
This open face of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)
is called the
fibered face

determined by
\phi_{\pi}
.
The dual object in
\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)
is a vertex.
In fact, it is exactly the (relative) real Euler class of
the oriented plane distribution over
M
,
as specified by the tangent spaces of the surface fibers
(relative to the obvious trivialization on the boundary).

When
M
is an orientable closed hyperbolic
3
–manifold,
the Thurston norm on
H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
is nondegenerate.
In this case, both
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)\subset H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})

and
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}}(M)\subset H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})

are
0
–codimensional compact polytopes.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
M
be an oriented compact
3
–manifold.
Suppose that
\kappa\colon M^{\prime}\to M
is a finite covering projection.
Denote by
\kappa^{*}\colon H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})\to H^{2}(M^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}})
the pullback homomorphism,
and by
\kappa_{!}\colon H^{2}(M^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}})\to H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
the umkehr homomorphism

u\mapsto\mathrm{PD}(\kappa_{*}(\mathrm{PD}(u)))
.

(1)

For every closed face
F
of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)
,
there is a unique closed face
F^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M^{\prime})
,
such that
F^{\prime}
is equal to the intersection of

\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M^{\prime})
with the preimage
\kappa_{!}^{-1}([M^{\prime}:M]\cdot F)
.
Here,
[M^{\prime}:M]\cdot F
denotes the rescaled face
of the rescaled polytope
[M^{\prime}:M]\cdot\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)
.

(2)

The image
\kappa^{*}(F)
in
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M^{\prime})
is contained in
F^{\prime}
,
and
\kappa^{*}(\partial F)
is contained in
\partial F^{\prime}
.
Hence,
F^{\prime}
is
the minimal closed face of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M^{\prime})
to contain
\kappa^{*}(F)
.

Proof.

The Thurston norm satisfies the relations

\|\kappa_{*}(\Sigma^{\prime})\|_{\mathrm{Th}}\leq\|\Sigma^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{Th}}
, for all
\Sigma^{\prime}\in H_{2}(M^{\prime},\partial M^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}})
,
and

\|\kappa^{!}(\Sigma)\|_{\mathrm{Th}}=[M^{\prime}:M]\cdot\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{Th}}
, for all
\Sigma\in H_{2}(M,\partial M;{\mathbb{R}})
,
due to deep theorems of Gabai
[
Gab83
, Corollaries 6.13 and 6.18]
.
They imply the dual relations

\|\kappa^{*}(u)\|_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}\leq\|u\|_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}
, for all
u\in H^{2}(M,\partial M;{\mathbb{R}})
,
and

\|\kappa_{!}(u^{\prime})\|_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}=[M^{\prime}:M]\cdot\|u^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{Th}}
, for all
u^{\prime}\in H^{2}(M^{\prime},\partial M^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}})
.
Note also
\kappa_{!}\circ\kappa^{*}=[M^{\prime}:M]\cdot\mathrm{id}
on
H^{2}(M,\partial M;{\mathbb{R}})
.
The main assertions in Lemma
2.1
follow immediately as
the geometric interpretation of the dual relations.

The last implication, regarding minimality of
F^{\prime}
, is equivalent to
saying that
\kappa^{*}(F)
contains some interior point of
F^{\prime}
.
This is the most obvious if
M^{\prime}
is regular over
M
.
In that case, it is well-known that

\kappa^{*}(H^{2}(M,\partial M;{\mathbb{R}}))

consists exactly of the elements of
H^{2}(M^{\prime},\partial M^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}})

that are fixed under the induced action of the deck transformation group;
see
[
Hat02
, Proposition 3G.1]

(applying with the cohomological long exact sequence for relative pairs).
The barycenter of the vertices of
F^{\prime}

is fixed under the deck transformation group,
so it is an interior point of
F^{\prime}
contained in
\kappa^{*}(F)
, as desired.
The general case can be reduced to the regular case
by passing to a finite cover of
M^{\prime}
regular over
M
.
∎

2.2.
Weak symplectic fillability

We recall preliminary facts in
3
–dimensional contact topology,
focusing on weakly symplectically fillable contact structures.
See the textbook
[
OzbS04
]
of Ozbagci and Stipsicz for a reference.

Let
M
be an oriented closed smooth
3
–manifold.
A (positive)
transversely oriented contact structure
on
M

can be characterized as a transversely oriented plane distribution
\xi
,
such that
\xi
is equal to the kernel of some smooth
1
–form
\alpha

everywhere on
M
,
and
such that
\alpha\wedge{\mathrm{d}}\alpha
is a volume form agreeing with the orientation of
M
.
Here,
\alpha
is not included as part of the data.
The orientation of
M
and the transverse orientation of
\xi

together induce a unique, compatible orientation of the planes in
\xi
.
The
Euler class

e(\xi)\in H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})

refers to the Euler class of
\xi
as an oriented plane bundle over
M
,
with the compatibly induced orientation.
The
real Euler class
of
\xi
refers to the real reduction of
e(\xi)
,
but we often denote it with the same notation,
only declaring it in
H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
.

If
\xi
is a transversely oriented contact structure on
M
,
then the same plane distribution with the opposite transverse orientation
is also a transversely oriented contact structure (as witnessed by
-\alpha
),
which we denote as
-\xi
. Hence,

e(-\xi)=-e(\xi).

Both
\xi
and
-\xi
are
“negative” transversely oriented contact structures
on the orientation reversal
-M
,
which are not to be considered in this paper.

For any closed, transversely oriented, contact
3
–manifold
(M,\xi)
,
a
weak symplectic filling
of
(M,\xi)

refers to any compact symplectic
4
–manifold
(W,\omega)

with
\partial W=M
(in the oriented sense),
such that
\omega|_{\xi}
is positive everywhere on
M
.
We say that
(M,\xi)
is
weakly symplectically fillable

if such a
(W,\omega)
exists.
In this case,

(W,-\omega)
is a weak symplectic filling of
(M,-\xi)
.

Weakly symplectically fillable contact structures arise
naturally from transversely orientable taut folations.
Recall that a codimension–
1
foliation
\mathscr{F}
on
M

(with smooth leaves and continuous holonomy)
is said to be
taut
if every leaf intersects some smoothly immersed loop
which is transverse to all the leaves.
If
M
supports a transversely oriented taut foliation
\mathscr{F}
,
then the transversely oriented plane distribution
T\mathscr{F}

can be perturbed homotopically into a pair of
weakly symplectically fillable contact structures,
one on
+M
, and the other on
-M
,
by constructions due to
Eliashberg–Thurston
[
EliT98
, Proposition 3.2.2]
and Etnyre
[
Etn04
, Corollary 1.2]
.
In particular, the Euler classes of these transversely oriented contact structures
are the same as the Euler class of
\mathscr{F}
(with the compatibly induced orientation),
namely,
e(\mathscr{F})=e(T\mathscr{F})
in
H^{2}(\pm M;{\mathbb{Z}})
, respectively.
However,
there are weakly symplectically fillable contact
3
–manifolds
without transversely oriented taut foliations of the identical real Euler class,
for example, see
[
SivY23
]
.

Weakly symplectically fillable contact
3
–manifolds are all tight.
A contact
3
–manifold is said to be
tight

if there are no smoothly embedded disks
with the tangent planes equal to the contact plane distribution
everywhere on the boundary circle.
Otherwise, it is
overtwisted
,
and a witnessing disk is called an
overtwisted disk
.
The tightness of weakly symplectically fillable contact structures
is originally due to Eliashberg and Gromov
[
EliG91
]
;
see also
[
OzbS04
, Theorem 12.1.10]
.
Tight or weakly symplectically fillable contact structures
may become overtwisted when pulled back to finite covers,
contrasting the case with taut foliations.
There are many examples of
oriented connected closed
3
–manifolds
with transversely oriented, tight contact structures
which do not admit weak symplectic fillings,
see
[
OzbS04
, Section 12.1]
.

The following well-known fact implies the similar property
regarding the real Euler class of a weakly symplectically fillable contact structure,
or of a taut foliation.
The bound is originally due to Eliashberg
[
OzbS04
, Theorem 4.3.8]
,
and the parity property also follows along the lines of the proof.
We include a quick alternative proof using convex surfaces in contact topology,
for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
M
be an oriented closed
3
–manifold.
Then, the real Euler class
e(\xi)\in H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})

of any tight contact structure
\xi
on
M

is an even lattice point in the dual Thurston norm unit ball
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
(M,\xi)
is a closed tight contact
3
–manifold.
For any primitive homology class
\Sigma\in H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
, we can represent
\Sigma

with an embedded, oriented, closed, Thurston-norm minimizing subsurface
S\subset M
.
Moreover,
S
can be perturbed isotopically into a convex position
with respect to
\xi

[
OzbS04
, Proposition 5.1.6]
.
We refer to
[
OzbS04
, Chapter 5]
for an introduction to
convex surfaces
,
which have been extremely useful for studying tight contact structures on
3
–manifolds.

For our application, it suffices to know what the output is.
Being a convex surface with respect to
\xi
,

S
can be decomposed into two compact subsurfaces
S_{+}
and
S_{-}
,
meeting along their common boundary
\Gamma_{S}=\partial S_{+}=\partial S_{-}
.
The common boundary is a disjoint union of simple closed curves (called the
dividing set
),
and every connected component of
S
contains at least one of these curves.
Moreover, the following formula holds
[
OzbS04
, Proposition 5.1.15]
:

\langle e(\xi),[S]\rangle=\chi(S_{+})-\chi(S_{-}).

The tightness of
\xi
implies that no connected components of
S_{\pm}
are disks

[
OzbS04
, Theorem 5.1.20]
. Therefore, we estimate:

|\langle e(\xi),[S]\rangle|\leq|\chi(S_{+})|+|\chi(S_{-})|=(-\chi(S_{+}))+(-\chi(S_{-}))=-\chi(S).

Since
\Sigma=[S]
and
\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{Th}}=\max(-\chi(S),0)=-\chi(S)
,
we conclude that

\langle e(\xi),\Sigma\rangle
is an even integer of absolute value at most
\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{Th}}
,
and the same conclusion holds for every primitive
\Sigma\in H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
.
This is equivalent to the assertion that
e(\xi)\in H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})

is an even lattice point in
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M)
.
∎

In
3
–dimensional contact topology,
there are several similar notions of convex fillability in the literature.
Stein fillability and holomorphic fillability are equivalent.
They imply exact symplectic fillability.
Exact symplectic fillability implies strong symplectic fillability.
Strong symplectic fillability implies weak symplectic fillability.
None of these implications are equivalences.
A seemingly more relaxed condition, called weak symplectic semi-fillability,
turns out to be equivalent to weak symplectic fillability.
See
[
OzbS04
, Chapter 12]
;
see also
[
Ghi05
]
and
[
Bow12
]
.

3.
No one-periodic trajectory, no fillable contact structure

In this section,
we obtain a criterion for nonexistence of fillable contact structures
with certain next-to-top real Euler classes on pseudo-Anosov mapping tori,
in term of absence of certain
1
–periodic trajectories (Lemma
3.2
).

We fix some notations for pseudo-Anosov mapping tori,
which are frequently referred to in this paper.
For any orientable connected closed surface
S
of genus
\geq 2
,
a
pseudo-Anosov automorphism

f\colon S\to S
refers to
an orientation-preserving homeomorphism, such that there exist
a pair of measured foliations

(\mathscr{F}^{\mathtt{u}},\mu^{\mathtt{u}})
and
(\mathscr{F}^{\mathtt{s}},\mu^{\mathtt{s}})

and a constant
\lambda>1
,
and they satisfy the properties
f\cdot(\mathscr{F}^{\mathtt{u}},\lambda\mu^{\mathtt{u}})

and
f\cdot(\mathscr{F}^{\mathtt{u}},\lambda^{-1}\mu^{\mathtt{u}})
.
We require
\mathscr{F}^{\mathtt{u}}
and
\mathscr{F}^{\mathtt{s}}
to be transverse to each other,
except at finitely many common singular points. We require
\mathscr{F}^{\mathtt{u}}
and
\mathscr{F}^{\mathtt{s}}

to have prong-type singularities at the singular points, with prong number
\geq 3
.
Among the isotopy class of
f
, a representative of this standard form is unique up to topological conjugacy.
See
[
FatLP12
]
for an exposition of pseudo-Anosov automorphisms on surfaces;
see also
[
Liu20
, Section 2]
for a summary of facts
from the perspective of mapping tori.

Notation 3.1
.

For any pseudo-Anosov automorphism
f\colon S\to S
on an orientable connected closed surface of genus
\geq 2
,
we adopt the following notations.

(1)

The mapping torus
M_{f}
of
f
is constructed as the quotient of
S\times{\mathbb{R}}
by
the equivalence relation
(f(x),r)\sim(x,r+1)
for all
(x,r)\in S\times{\mathbb{R}}
.
The suspension flow
\theta_{t}\colon M_{f}\to M_{f}
is
induced by the action
(x,r)\mapsto(x,r+t)
on
S\times{\mathbb{R}}
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
The distinguished fibered class
\phi_{f}\in H^{1}(M_{f};{\mathbb{Z}})

is (homotopically) represented by
the fibering projection
M_{f}\to S^{1}
induced by
(x,r)\mapsto r
,
identifying
S^{1}
with
{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
.

(2)

When
S
is oriented,
M_{f}
is oriented compatibly
with respect to the oriented surface fibers and forward direction of the suspension flow.
The distinguished Euler class
e_{f}\in H^{2}(M_{f};{\mathbb{Z}})

refers to the Euler class of the oriented tangent plane distribution of the surface fibers.
The distinguished
\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathtt{c}}
structure
\mathfrak{s}_{\theta}
on

is represented by the velocity vector field
\dot{\theta}
of

.
Hence, there are relations
e_{f}=c_{1}(\mathfrak{s}_{\theta})
,
and
\langle e_{f},[S]\rangle=\chi(S)
,
and
\phi_{f}=\mathrm{PD}([S])
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
S
be an oriented connected closed surface of genus
\geq 3
,
and
f\colon S\to S
be a pseudo-Anosov automorphism.
Denote by
e_{f}\in H^{2}(M_{f};{\mathbb{R}})

the distinguished real Euler class (Notation
3.1
).

If
a\in H^{2}(M_{f};{\mathbb{R}})
is an integral lattice point satisfying
\langle a,[S]\rangle=1
,
and if the Poincaré dual
\mathrm{PD}(a)\in H_{1}(M_{f};{\mathbb{R}})

is not represented by any
1
–periodic trajectory of the suspension flow,
then the even lattice point
-e_{f}-2a
is not the real Euler class of
any weakly symplectically fillable contact structure on

,
nor is
e_{f}+2a
.

The rest of this section is devoted to the proof of Lemma

.

Our proof is based on the nonvanishing criterion of weak symplectic fillability
due to Ozsváth–Szabó
[
OzsS04c
, Theorem 4.2]
,
and the isomorphisms between
several
3
–dimensional Floer homology theories due to Lee–Taubes
[
LeeT12
]
,
and due to Kutluhan–Lee–Taubes
[
KutLT20a
,
KutLT20b
,
KutLT20c
,
KutLT20d
,
KutLT20e
]
,
and the study of the symplectic Floer homology for pseudo-Anosov mapping classes
due to Cotton-Clay
[
Cot09
]
.

From these essential ingredients,
it is more or less straightforward to derive Lemma
3.2
.
For this reason, our proof below is largely expository.
However,
Lemma
3.4
seems to be
a new observation of independent interest (see Remark

).

We recall that for any oriented connected closed
3
–manifold
Y
,
there are three flavors of the Heegaard Floer homology

,
\mathrm{HF}^{-}(Y)
, and
\mathrm{HF}^{\infty}(Y)

which are relatively
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
–graded
{\mathbb{Z}}[U]
–modules,
where

is a fixed indeterminant;
there is another flavor
\widehat{\mathrm{HF}}(Y)

which is a relatively
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
–graded
{\mathbb{Z}}
–module
(or a
{\mathbb{Z}}[U]
–module with annihilating

[

]
.
More generally, for any module
V
over the abelian group ring

(3.1)

\Lambda={\mathbb{Z}}[H^{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})],

there are corresponding versions with twisted coefficients,
denoted as
\underline{\mathrm{HF}}^{+}(Y;V)
,
\underline{\mathrm{HF}}^{-}(Y;V)
,
\underline{\mathrm{HF}}^{\infty}(Y;V)
,
and
\widehat{\underline{\mathrm{HF}}}(Y;V)
.
These are relatively
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
–graded
\Lambda[U]
–modules
[

, Section 8.1]
.
Each of the flavors naturally splits into direct summands

\underline{\mathrm{HF}}^{\circ}(Y;V)=\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{s}}\underline{\mathrm{HF}}^{\circ}(Y,\mathfrak{s};V),

indexed by the
\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathtt{c}}
structures
\mathfrak{s}
of
Y
.
The splitting respects the relative grading and the module structure,
and has only finitely many nonvanishing direct summands.
With the regular
\Lambda
–module
V=\Lambda
,
we obtain the universally twisted versions, which are simply denoted as

\underline{\mathrm{HF}}^{\circ}(Y,\mathfrak{s})=\underline{\mathrm{HF}}^{\circ}(Y,\mathfrak{s};\Lambda).

Recall that the
\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathtt{c}}
structures on
Y

forms an affine
H_{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
.
That is to say, after choosing
a reference
\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathtt{c}}
structure
\mathfrak{s}_{0}
,
any other
\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathtt{c}}
on

can be
written as
\mathfrak{s}_{0}+\Gamma
for some unique
\Gamma\in H_{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
.
There is a canonical involution
\mathfrak{s}\mapsto\bar{\mathfrak{s}}

on the space of
\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathtt{c}}
on
Y
.
The first Chern class
c_{1}(\mathfrak{s})\in H^{2}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})

can be determined by the relation
\bar{\mathfrak{s}}=\mathfrak{s}-\mathrm{PD}(c_{1}(\mathfrak{s}))
.
Therefore,
c_{1}(\bar{\mathfrak{s}})=-c_{1}(\mathfrak{s})
,
and
c_{1}(\mathfrak{s}+\Gamma)=c_{1}(\mathfrak{s})+2\mathrm{PD}(\Gamma)
.

Remark 3.3
.

We treat any
\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathtt{c}}
structure
\mathfrak{s}

on an oriented
3
–manifold
Y
as represented by a nowhere vanishing vector field,
so the first Chern class
c_{1}(\mathfrak{s})
refers to
the Euler class of the compatibly oriented transverse plane distribution.
The same vector field also represents a
\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathtt{c}}
structure
on the orientation reversal
-Y
, which we denote with the same notation
\mathfrak{s}
.
We adopt the notation
H_{*}(\pm Y;{\mathbb{Z}})

for
H_{*}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
together with the specified fundamental class
\pm[Y]\in H_{3}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
,
which also specifies the Poincaré duality isomorphism.
Therefore, when

flips orientation,

flips sign in
H^{2}(+Y;{\mathbb{Z}})=H^{2}(-Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
,
but the sign of
\mathrm{PD}(c_{1}(\mathfrak{s}))

does not alter in
H_{1}(+Y;{\mathbb{Z}})=H_{1}(-Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
.
The involution
\mathfrak{s}\mapsto\bar{\mathfrak{s}}

corresponds to reversing the direction of any representative vector field.

Our proof below only involves twisted versions of the hat flavor and the plus flavor.
These two flavors are related by the following exact triangle of
\Lambda
–module homomorphisms:

(3.2)

\textstyle{\underline{\mathrm{HF}}^{+}(Y,\mathfrak{s};V)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{U}
\textstyle{\underline{\mathrm{HF}}^{+}(Y,\mathfrak{s};V)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\widehat{\underline{\mathrm{HF}}}(Y,\mathfrak{s};V)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Here, the multiplication by

and the downward arrow respect the
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
–grading,
and the upper-leftward arrow switches the

–grading.

The

of a module

over a (commutative) domain

over the field of fractions
\mathrm{Frac}(R)

If

is finitely generated over
\Lambda

then

is also finitely generated over the Noetherian domain

,
so

has finite rank over
\Lambda
.
It may happen that

has infinite rank over

is torsion.
However, every element of

is annihilated by some sufficiently large power of

if and only if

by the exact triangle (

).

Lemma 3.4
.

If
(Y,\xi)
is an oriented closed contact
3
–manifold
which is weakly symplectically fillable,
then

\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}\,\widehat{\mathrm{HF}}(-Y,\mathfrak{s}_{\xi})>0.

Here,
\mathfrak{s}_{\xi}
denotes the canonical
\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathtt{c}}
structure of
\xi
,
which is represented by any nowhere vanishing vector field transverse to
\xi

agreeing with its prescribed transverse orientation.

Proof.

We derive Lemma
3.4

from
[
OzsS04c
, Theorem 4.2]
due to Ozsváth and Szabó.
To be precise, Ozsváth and Szabó show that
if
(W,\omega)
is a weakly symplectic filling of an oriented connected closed contact
3
–manifold
(Y,\xi)
,
then the twisted Floer homology group

\widehat{\underline{\mathrm{HF}}}(-Y,\mathfrak{s}_{\xi};{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]^{\omega})

contains a
{\mathbb{Z}}
–primitive,
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]
–nontorsion element
(see Remark
3.5
for technical elaboration).
Here, the twisting coefficient is taken to be the abelian group ring
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]

treated as the specialization of
\Lambda
(see (
3.1
))
induced by the abelian group homomorphism

\omega_{*}\colon H^{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})\to{\mathbb{R}}\colon x\mapsto\langle[\omega],\mathrm{PD}(x)\rangle
.
The specialization depends only
on the restriction of the de Rham cohomology class
[\omega]\in H^{2}(W;{\mathbb{R}})

to
Y=\partial W
.

Denote by
\Omega={\mathbb{Z}}[\mathrm{Im}(\omega)]

the image of the specialization homomorphism
\Lambda\to{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]
induced by
\omega_{*}
.
We observe that
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]
is flat over the subdomain
\Omega
.

By definition,
\widehat{\underline{\mathrm{HF}}}(-Y,\mathfrak{s}_{\xi})

is the homology of a chain complex of finitely generated free
\Lambda
–modules

(\widehat{\underline{\mathrm{CF}}}(-Y,\mathfrak{s}_{\xi}),\widehat{\underline{\partial}})
,
and
\widehat{\underline{\mathrm{HF}}}(-Y,\mathfrak{s}_{\xi};V)

is the homology of the chain complex of
\Lambda
–modules

(\widehat{\underline{\mathrm{CF}}}(-Y,\mathfrak{s}_{\xi})\otimes_{\Lambda}V,\widehat{\underline{\partial}}\otimes 1)
.
Flatness implies

\widehat{\underline{\mathrm{HF}}}(-Y,\mathfrak{s}_{\xi};\Omega)\otimes_{\Omega}{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]\cong\widehat{\underline{\mathrm{HF}}}(-Y,\mathfrak{s}_{\xi};{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{R}}]^{\omega})

as
\Lambda
–modules.
In particular,
the finitely generated
\Lambda
–module

\widehat{\underline{\mathrm{HF}}}(-Y,\mathfrak{s}_{\xi};\Omega)

is not
\Omega
–torsion.
Equivalently, we obtain

(3.3)

\mathrm{rank}_{\Omega}\,\widehat{\underline{\mathrm{HF}}}(-Y,\mathfrak{s}_{\xi};\Omega)>0.

The untwisted version
\widehat{\mathrm{HF}}(-Y,\mathfrak{s}_{\xi})

is the homology of the chain complex of finitely generated free
{\mathbb{Z}}
–modules

(\widehat{\mathrm{CF}}(-Y,\mathfrak{s}_{\xi}),\widehat{\partial})

recovered from

(\widehat{\underline{\mathrm{CF}}}(-Y,\mathfrak{s}_{\xi}),\widehat{\underline{\partial}})

by the trivial specialization
\Lambda\to{\mathbb{Z}}
,
(sending every group element in
H^{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
to
1
).
We can also recover
\widehat{\mathrm{CF}}(-Y,\mathfrak{s}_{\xi})

from
\widehat{\underline{\mathrm{CF}}}(-Y,\mathfrak{s}_{\xi};\Omega)

by the trivial specialization
\Omega\to{\mathbb{Z}}
.
A semicontinuity argument as usual in homological algebra implies

(3.4)

\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}\,\widehat{\mathrm{HF}}(-Y,\mathfrak{s}_{\xi})\geq\mathrm{rank}_{\Omega}\,\widehat{\underline{\mathrm{HF}}}(-Y,\mathfrak{s}_{\xi};\Omega).

In fact, over a basis one may represent the boundary operator

\widehat{\underline{\partial}}
of

\widehat{\underline{\mathrm{CF}}}(-Y,\mathfrak{s}_{\xi};\Omega)

as a square matrix over
\Omega
, and
\widehat{\partial}
is the specialized matrix over
{\mathbb{Z}}
.
The rank of
\widehat{\partial}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
cannot decrease under
sufficiently small perturbation of any specialization
\mathrm{Frac}(\Omega)\to{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Meanwhile, the rank under a generic (small) perturbation
is equal to the rank of
\widehat{\underline{\partial}}
over
\Omega
.
Therefore,
the rank of
\widehat{\partial}
over
{\mathbb{Z}}
is a lower bound
for the rank of
\widehat{\underline{\partial}}
over
\mathrm{Frac}(\Omega)
,
and hence,
the nullity of
\widehat{\partial}
over
{\mathbb{Z}}
is
an upper bound for the nullity of
\widehat{\underline{\partial}}
over
\Omega
.
Then the above inequality follows immediately.

The asserted inequality as in Lemma
3.4

follows from the inequalites (
3.3
) and (
3.4
).
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
S
be an oriented connected closed surface of genus
\geq 3
,
and

be a pseudo-Anosov automorphism.
Denote by
\mathfrak{s}_{\theta}

the distinguished
\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathtt{c}}
structure on

(Notation
3.1
).

,

\Gamma
is not represented by any

\widehat{\mathrm{HF}}(M_{f},\mathfrak{s}_{\theta}+\Gamma)=0.

Proof.

The conclusion
\widehat{\mathrm{HF}}(M_{f},\mathfrak{s}_{\theta}+\Gamma)=0

is equivalent to
\mathrm{HF}^{+}(M_{f},\mathfrak{s}_{\theta}+\Gamma)=0
,
as we have explained.

When

has genus
\geq 3
,
(the first Chern class of)
the
\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathtt{c}}

is not torsion,
indeed,
\langle c_{1}(\mathfrak{s}_{\theta}+\Gamma),[S]\rangle=\chi(S)+2<0
.
In this case,
the plus-flavor Heegaard Floer homology at

is isomorphic to the periodic Floer homology at

,

\mathrm{HF}^{+}(M_{f},\mathfrak{s}_{\theta}+\Gamma)\cong\mathrm{HP}(f,\Gamma),

(as relatively

–graded

–modules, for simplicity).
To be more precise,

is actually defined up to natural isomorphism
for any mapping class

and a homology class
\Gamma\in H_{1}(M_{f};{\mathbb{Z}})

with
\langle\mathrm{PD}(\Gamma),[S]\rangle>0
.
The case with

is formerly introduced as the symplectic Floer homology.
The isomorphism (

) follows from the aforementioned works
due to Kutluhan–Lee–Taubes and due to Lee–Taubes;
we refer the reader to
[

for a summary with more details.

For

pseudo-Anosov,
and for

is represented by

–periodic trajectory of the pseudo-Anosov suspension flow

.

if

has genus

, and if

∎

4.

.

Notation 4.1

,

.

at

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
S
be an oriented connected closed surface of genus
\geq 2
,
and

be a pseudo-Anosov automorphism.
Suppose that

is a closed face with

(Notation

Then, there exist
some connected regular finite cover

Lemma 4.3
.

For any pseudo-Anosov automorphism

on an oriented closed surface of genus
\geq 2

Here,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Fr}}(f)\subset H_{1}(M_{f};{\mathbb{R}})

and passing through

,
where

ranges over all the periodic trajectories
of the pseudo-Anosov suspension flow on

.
See also Notations

and
4.1
.

Proof.

By Fried’s characterization,

\Gamma\in H_{1}(M_{f};{\mathbb{R}})
lies

if and only if

holds for all
\Sigma\in H_{2}(M_{f};{\mathbb{R}})

which lies in the distinguished fibered cone
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Th}}(f)

(that is, the open cone over the distinguished fibered face of

dual to

);
see
[

, Exposé 14, Theorem 14.11]
.
In

, the intersection of all the half-spaces
defined by the inequalities

ranging over all

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
f\colon S\to S
be a pseudo-Anosov automorphism
on an orientable connected closed surface of genus
\geq 2
.
Suppose that

is a closed face with

.

(1)

Suppose

.
Then,
\mathrm{PD}(\mathcal{A}(e_{f};F))
contains the real homology class

of some periodic trajectory

(2)

Suppose

.
If

is any finitely generated subgroup of
\pi_{1}(M_{f})
,
and if, for all

lies in the real linear subspace of

spanned by
\mathrm{PD}(\mathcal{A}(e_{f};F))

is a quasiconvex subgroup of infinite index in

.

See Notations
3.1
and
4.1
.

Proof.

The assumption of the first statement says
that

has positive dimension. All higher dimensional cases can be
reduced to the
1
–dimensional case, as

is closed.
Every
1
–dimensional face of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Fr}}(M_{f})

must contain the real homology class of some periodic trajectory.
In fact, this is part of a more detailed picture of Fried’s characterization;
for example, this follows immediately of
[
Liu20
, Lemma 5.13]
.
Then we can translate the conclusion
into the first statement by Lemma

.

The assumption of the second statement says
that
F
has positive codimension in
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M_{f})
.
Since
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M_{f})
is the convex hull of integral lattice points,
there exists some nonzero, primitive
\Sigma\in H_{2}(M_{f};{\mathbb{Z}})
,
such that
\langle a,\Sigma\rangle=0
holds for all

.
In particular,
\Sigma
lies on the boundary of the distinguished fibered cone

.
Therefore,
\mathrm{PD}(\Sigma)
cannot a fibered class.

Denote by

By assumption,

lies in the kernel of

Since

has infinite cyclic image,

must be of infinite index in

.
Moreover,

cannot contain a normal surface subgroup

of

For otherwise,

This cannot be the case when

is not a fibered class,
(see
[

]
, or
[

, Exposé 14, Theorem 14.2]

It follows that

can only be a quasi-convex in the word-hyperbolic group

,
due to a fundamental dichotomy in the theory of closed hyperbolic

–manifold groups,
(see
[
AscFW15
, Theorem 4.1.2 and Proposition 4.4.2]
).
∎

In a nutshell,

the reader is referred to the proof of

To finish the proof,

For each connected component

∎

Proof of the main theorem

In this section, we prove Theorem

.

Before the proof, we need one more lemma as follows,
which has the effect of promoting
any rational point of the real second cohomology

The lemma says that this can be done
by pulling back to finite cyclic covers
dual to a primitive integral second homology class,
if you use, for example, a fibered class.

, depending on

\Sigma
,
such that the following property hold.

•

and

if
\langle a,\Sigma\rangle
is an even integer,
and if

is an even lattice point,
then for any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
divisible by

,

of

is an even lattice point
in

By assumption, there exists some

,
such that

achieves the maximum among all

.
We show that any such

satisfies the asserted property.

To this end, suppose

,

and

,
such that
\langle a,\Sigma\rangle=2s
, and

is an even lattice point.

M
,
and fix a reference
\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathtt{c}}

of

.
Note that any oriented closed
3

\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathtt{c}}

when the first Betti number is nonzero, every vertex of the dual Thurston norm unit ball

of some
\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathtt{c}}

which comes from a transversely oriented taut foliation;
then, a torsion
\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathtt{c}}

We assume

to be torsion.

As

This surface lifts to each

dual to
\Sigma

is torsion, we observe

,

to

.

Since

is an even lattice point, the pullback class

is also an even lattice point, so there exists some
\Gamma\in H_{1}(M^{\prime}_{l};{\mathbb{Z}})
,
such that

ra^{\prime}_{l}=c_{1}\left(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}_{l}+\Gamma\right)=2\cdot\mathrm{PD}(\Gamma)

holds in
H^{2}(M^{\prime}_{l};{\mathbb{Q}})
.
We obtain

\langle\mathrm{PD}(\Gamma),\Sigma^{\prime}_{l}\rangle=\langle(r/2)\cdot a^{\prime}_{l},\Sigma^{\prime}_{l}\rangle=rs.

Representing
\Gamma
by any loop
\gamma
in
M^{\prime}_{l}
,
we see that
\gamma
has totally
r
distinct lifts to
M^{\prime}_{lr}
,
since
s
is an integer.
Because
b_{1}(M^{\prime}_{lr})\geq b_{1}(M^{\prime}_{l})
,
the maximality of
b_{1}(M^{\prime}_{l})
implies
b_{1}(M^{\prime}_{lr})=b_{1}(M^{\prime}_{l})
.
In this case,
the
r
–cyclic deck tranformation group acts trivially on
H_{1}(M^{\prime}_{lr};{\mathbb{Q}})
.
It follows that the
r
different lifts of
\gamma
are all homologous modulo torsion,
giving rise to one and the same integral lattice point
\Gamma^{\prime}\in H_{1}(M^{\prime}_{lr};{\mathbb{Q}})
.
So,
the pullback of
\mathrm{PD}(\Gamma)
in
H^{2}(M^{\prime}_{lr};{\mathbb{Q}})
is equal to
r\cdot\mathrm{PD}(\Gamma^{\prime})
.
On the other hand,
the pullback of
\mathrm{PD}(\Gamma)=(r/2)\cdot a^{\prime}_{l}
is
(r/2)\cdot a^{\prime}_{lr}
in
H^{2}(M^{\prime}_{lr})
.
So, we obtain
(r/2)\cdot a^{\prime}_{lr}=r\cdot\mathrm{PD}(\Gamma^{\prime})
,
or equivalently,

a^{\prime}_{lr}=2\cdot\mathrm{PD}(\Gamma^{\prime}).

This shows that
a^{\prime}_{lr}
is an even lattice point in
H^{2}(M^{\prime}_{lr};{\mathbb{Q}})
.
Consequently, for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
divisible by
lr
,
the further pullbacks
a^{\prime}_{m}
in
H^{2}(M^{\prime}_{m};{\mathbb{Q}})
are all even lattice points.
∎

We are ready to prove Theorem
1.2
, as follows.

Let
M
be any oriented closed hyperbolic
3
–manifold.
Our goal is to construct some connected finite cover
\tilde{M}
of
M
,
together with some even lattice point
w\in H^{2}(\tilde{M};{\mathbb{R}})

on the boundary
\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(\tilde{M})
of the dual Thurston norm unit ball,
such that
\tilde{w}
is not the real Euler class of
any weakly symplectically fillable contact structure on
\tilde{M}
.

We construct the asserted
(\tilde{M},\tilde{w})

through a tower of three connected finite covers over
M
:

\tilde{M}\to M^{\prime\prime}\to M^{\prime}\to M.

First, we obtain a connected finite cover
M^{\prime}
of
M
which fibers over a circle.
Moreover, we require
b_{1}(M^{\prime})\geq 2
.
The existence of
M^{\prime}
follows from
confirmed virtual properties of finite-volume hyperbolic
3
–manifolds
[
Ago13
]
.
Since
b_{1}(M^{\prime})\geq 2
,
any Thurston norm fibered cone contains infinitely many primitive classes,
and their Thurston norm can be arbitrarily large.
Therefore,
we can find some oriented connected closed fiber surface
S^{\prime}
in
M^{\prime}
of genus
\geq 3
.
The manifold
M^{\prime}
can be identified with the mapping torus of
some pseudo-Anosov automorphism
S^{\prime}\to S^{\prime}
,
which is isotopic to the monodromy of the fibering.
Below, we speak of periodic trajectories in
M^{\prime}

with respect to the suspension pseudo-Anosov flow
under the identification.

Fix some
S^{\prime}\subset M^{\prime}
as above. Denote by
e^{\prime}\in H^{2}(M^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}})

the real Euler class of the oriented plane distribution tangent to the surface fibers.
By construction,

e^{\prime}
must be a vertex on
\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M^{\prime})
.
In particular,
e^{\prime}
is an even lattice point of
H^{2}(M^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}})
.
Moreover, we note

\langle e^{\prime},[S^{\prime}]\rangle=\chi(S^{\prime})\leq-4.

Fix some closed face
F^{\prime}\subset\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M^{\prime})

with
e^{\prime}\in\partial F^{\prime}
, which exists since
b_{1}(M^{\prime})\geq 2
.

Next, we obtain a connected finite cover
M^{\prime\prime}
of
M^{\prime}
as asserted in Lemma
4.6
,
with respect to
e^{\prime}
and
F^{\prime}
. Denote by
e^{\prime\prime}\in H^{2}(M^{\prime\prime};{\mathbb{R}})
the pullback of
e^{\prime}
,
which is a vertex of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M^{\prime\prime})
.
Denote by
F^{\prime\prime}
the minimal closed face of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M^{\prime\prime})

that contains the pullback of
F^{\prime}
.
Hence,
e^{\prime\prime}\in\partial F^{\prime\prime}
and
F^{\prime\prime}\subset\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M^{\prime\prime})
.
Denote by
S^{\prime\prime}\subset M^{\prime\prime}
any preimage component of
S^{\prime}
.

We claim that there is some rational point
a^{\prime\prime}\in H^{2}(M^{\prime\prime};{\mathbb{R}})

with

\langle a^{\prime\prime},[S^{\prime\prime}]\rangle=1,

such that
e^{\prime\prime}+2a^{\prime\prime}
lies in
F^{\prime\prime}
, and
such that no positive integral multiple of
\mathrm{PD}(a^{\prime\prime})\in H_{1}(M^{\prime\prime};{\mathbb{R}})
is represented
by any periodic trajectory of the pullback pseudo-Anosov flow on
M^{\prime\prime}
.

In fact, as guaranteed by Lemma
4.6
,
there is a finite collection
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime\prime}
of real linear subspaces of
H^{2}(M^{\prime\prime};{\mathbb{R}})
,
such that the complement
U
of their union in
\mathcal{A}(e^{\prime\prime};F^{\prime\prime})
(Notation
4.1
)
contains no
\mathrm{PD}([\gamma^{\prime\prime}])
for any periodic trajectory
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
in
M^{\prime\prime}
.
In particular,
U
is a scaling-invariant, open, dense subset of
\mathcal{A}(e^{\prime\prime};F^{\prime\prime})
.
We observe

F^{\prime\prime}=\left(e^{\prime\prime}+\mathcal{A}(e^{\prime\prime};F^{\prime\prime})\right)\cap\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(M^{\prime\prime}),

so
(e^{\prime\prime}+U)\cap F^{\prime\prime}
is open and dense in
F^{\prime\prime}
.
We also note that the conclusion of Lemma
4.6

forces
F^{\prime\prime}
to have dimension
\geq 2
(see Lemma
4.4
).
On the other hand, the vertices of
F^{\prime\prime}
are all even lattice points in
H^{2}(M^{\prime\prime};{\mathbb{R}})
.
Since
e^{\prime\prime}
is a vertex of
F^{\prime\prime}
with
\langle e^{\prime\prime},[S^{\prime\prime}]\rangle=\chi(S^{\prime\prime})
,
and since any point
q^{\prime\prime}\in F^{\prime\prime}
other than
e^{\prime\prime}
satisfies
\langle q^{\prime\prime},[S^{\prime\prime}]\rangle>\chi(S^{\prime\prime})
,
the points
q^{\prime\prime}\in F^{\prime\prime}
with
\langle q^{\prime\prime},[S^{\prime\prime}]\rangle=\chi(S)+2

form a compact polytope
K
in
H^{2}(M^{\prime\prime};{\mathbb{R}})
with rational vertices.
Since
F^{\prime\prime}
has dimension
\geq 2
,
K
must have dimension
\geq 1
.
Since is open and dense in
K
, the rational points must be dense in
(e^{\prime\prime}+U)\cap K
.
Every rational point
q^{\prime\prime}
in
(e^{\prime\prime}+U)\cap K
gives rise to a rational point
a^{\prime\prime}=(q^{\prime\prime}-e^{\prime\prime})/2
as claimed.

Fix some
a^{\prime\prime}\in H^{2}(M^{\prime\prime};{\mathbb{R}})
as claimed above.

Finally, we obtain a connected
m
–cyclic cover
\tilde{M}
of
M^{\prime\prime}
dual to
[S^{\prime\prime}]
,
such that the pullback
\tilde{a}\in H^{2}(\tilde{M};{\mathbb{R}})
of
a^{\prime\prime}
is an even lattice point.
The condition can be satisfied as long as we pick some sufficiently divisible
m\in{\mathbb{N}}

(depending on
M^{\prime\prime}
,
[S^{\prime\prime}]
and
a^{\prime\prime}
), by Lemma
5.1
.
Fix a lift
\tilde{S}
of
S^{\prime\prime}
to
\tilde{M}
.
Denote by
\tilde{e}\in H^{2}(\tilde{M};{\mathbb{R}})
the pullback of
e^{\prime\prime}
.
We obtain

\langle\tilde{a},m[\tilde{S}]\rangle=m\cdot\langle a^{\prime\prime},[S^{\prime\prime}]\rangle=m
,
implying

\langle\tilde{a},[\tilde{S}]\rangle=1.

Denote by
\tilde{F}

the minimal closed face of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(\tilde{M})
that contains the pullback of
F^{\prime\prime}
.
Hence,
\tilde{e}\in\partial\tilde{F}
,
and
\tilde{F}\subset\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(\tilde{M})
.

Set

\tilde{w}=\tilde{e}+2\tilde{a}.

To summarize, we have constructed a connected finite cover
\tilde{M}
of
M
,
together with an even lattice point
\tilde{w}\in H^{2}(\tilde{M};{\mathbb{R}})

which lies in a closed face
\tilde{F}\subset\partial\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Th}^{*}}(\tilde{M})
.

It remains to check that
\tilde{w}
is not the real Euler class
of any weakly symplectically fillable contact structure on
\tilde{M}
.
To this end, we observe that
\mathrm{PD}(\tilde{a})

is not represented by any
1
–periodic trajectory of the pullback pseudo-Anosov flow on
\tilde{M}
.
This is because
\mathrm{PD}(\tilde{a})
projects
m\cdot\mathrm{PD}(a^{\prime\prime})

under the induced homomorphism
H_{1}(\tilde{M};{\mathbb{R}})\to H_{1}(M^{\prime\prime};{\mathbb{R}})
,
but
m\cdot\mathrm{PD}(a^{\prime\prime})
is not represented by any periodic trajectory in
M^{\prime\prime}
.
Since
\langle\tilde{a},[\tilde{S}]\rangle=1
,
and since
\tilde{S}
has genus
\geq 3
as
S^{\prime}
does,
we can apply Lemma
3.2
,
concluding the nonexistence of any weakly symplectically fillable contact structures
of real Euler class
\tilde{w}=\tilde{e}+2\tilde{a}
.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

6.
Further discussion

For any oriented closed
3
–manifold
M
,
and
for every even lattice point
w\in H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
of dual Thurston norm
1

(or more generally,
\leq 1
),
Yazdi asks whether there exists some finite cover
M^{\prime}
of
M
,
such that the pullback
a^{\prime}
of
a
to
M^{\prime}
is the real Euler class
of some transversely oriented taut foliation on
M^{\prime}

[
Yaz20
, Question 9.4]
.
An expected affirmative answer to this question
is called the
virtual Euler class one conjecture
.

We explain
why our construction for Theorem
1.2

does not negate Yazdi’s conjecture.
Retaining the notations
(\tilde{M},\tilde{w})
as in Section
5
,
one might attempt to argue that
for any finite cover
M^{\prime\prime\prime}
of
\tilde{M}
,
the pullback
w^{\prime\prime\prime}
of
\tilde{w}
to
M^{\prime\prime\prime}

is not realized by taut foliations (or fillable contact structures) any more.
The argument works
when
M^{\prime\prime\prime}
is cyclic over
\tilde{M}

dual to the constructed surface fiber
\tilde{S}
.
However, if
\tilde{S}
does not lift to
M^{\prime\prime\prime}
,
a preimage component
S^{\prime\prime\prime}
of
\tilde{S}
in
M^{\prime\prime\prime}

will cover
\tilde{S}
of some degree
k>1
.
One obtains
w^{\prime\prime\prime}=e^{\prime\prime\prime}+2a^{\prime\prime\prime}
and
\langle a^{\prime\prime\prime},[S^{\prime\prime\prime}]\rangle=k>1
.
Our construction implies that
\mathrm{PD}(a^{\prime\prime\prime})

cannot be represented by any
k
–periodic tranjectory in
M^{\prime\prime\prime}
,
but
\mathrm{PD}(a^{\prime\prime\prime})
may still be the sum of
several homology classes that are representable
by periodic trajectories (with sum of periods
k
).
In that case,
certain similar tuples of periodic trajectories
may present as chain complex generators of the periodic Floer homology.
In particular,
a similar criterion as Lemma
3.6

would fail for
k
–periodic trajectories with
k>1
.

It might be true that
our counter-examples in Theorem
1.2

are not realized by any tight contact structure either.
See
[
SivY23
, Remark 1.3]
for discussion regarding
the Euler class one conjecture for tight contact structures.

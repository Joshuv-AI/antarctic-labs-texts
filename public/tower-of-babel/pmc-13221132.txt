Neurosurgical oncology at the crossroads of neurosurgery subspecialties: Intersections of sections
Authors: Isabelle M Germano, Gelareh Zadeh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractNeurosurgical oncology is a rapidly advancing field, driven by interdisciplinary collaboration, technological innovation, and a growing understanding of tumor biology. As we celebrate the 40th anniversary of the AANS/CNS Section on Tumors, this editorial highlights the collaborative spirit that defines the Section and explores the many ways it intersects with other neurosurgical subspecialties and AANS/CNS Sections to optimize patient care. This special edition features a series of articles showcasing these intersections across functional, cerebrovascular, spine, trauma, pain, and pediatric neurosurgery. For example, insights into neuron–glioma interactions—enhanced by tools such as optogenetics and advanced brain mapping—are opening doors to more personalized treatment strategies. Innovations in preoperative embolization for skull base meningiomas are improving surgical precision and outcomes. Similarly, advances in managing vertebral metastatic disease with surgical techniques and stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT) are reshaping standards of care. Neuro-intensive care has evolved to support personalized postoperative recovery, with a focus on neurological function and cognitive rehabilitation. In pain management, neuromodulation, including intrathecal drug delivery and spinal cord stimulation, is offering new relief to tumor patients. Adolescents and young adults (AYA) with gliomas remain an underserved population, requiring age-specific treatment frameworks and better integration into clinical trials. This edition celebrates the diverse and collaborative workforce propelling neurosurgical oncology forward.Keywords: brain tumors, neurosurgical oncology, pain, pediatric neurosurgery, skull base, spine oncology, spine tumors

The field of neurosurgical oncology is undergoing rapid transformation, with exciting innovations shaping the treatment of brain and spinal tumors. Celebrating the 40th anniversary of the AANS/CNS Section on Tumors (1984-2024)1 has inspired us to highlight how neurosurgical oncology integrates with other subspecialties and AANS/CNS Sections, including functional/epilepsy, cerebrovascular, spine, trauma/intensive care, pain, pediatrics, and women in neurosurgery. These collaborations are exemplified by this special issue, where we feature key papers demonstrating how tumor biology, surgical advances, multidisciplinary management, and leadership diversity are reshaping and advancing neurosurgical oncology.

Decoding Neuron-Glioma Interactions: A New Frontier in Cancer Neuroscience

Understanding neuron-glioma interactions is a groundbreaking area in neuro-oncology. The paper “Leveraging neuroscience imaging and electrophysiological assessments to decode neuron-glioma interactions”2 explores how gliomas infiltrate neural networks, influencing brain activity and tumor progression. New technologies like optogenetics, in vivo imaging, and brain mapping allow unprecedented insight and suggest that targeting neural circuits could improve treatment precision. Integrating functional neurosurgery with neuro-oncology enables real-time brain mapping during surgery, minimizing cognitive and neurological deficits. This fusion exemplifies the increasing need for multidisciplinary collaboration to improve patient outcomes.

Neurovascular Interventions for Meningiomas: Enhancing Surgical Precision

Preoperative embolization is increasingly used to manage skull base meningiomas, reducing blood loss and improving surgical outcomes. The paper “Pre-operative embolization of skull base meningiomas”3 reviews embolization techniques and their benefits and limitations in complex cases. Combining endovascular, cerebrovascular, and skull-base expertise might allow more effective and safer tumor resections, underscoring the essential role of interdisciplinary teamwork in modern neurosurgical oncology.

Vertebral Metastatic Disease: Advancing Spinal Oncology

Vertebral metastases significantly impair quality of life. “Vertebral metastatic disease: A paradigm shift”4 highlights how advances in surgery and stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT) have improved patient outcomes. The paper stresses a multidisciplinary approach, integrating neurosurgery, radiation oncology, and interventional radiology. Additionally, molecular markers offer the potential for more personalized care, refining both diagnosis and treatment strategies.

Neurointensive Care Units and Tumor Patients: Shaping Post-Operative Care

As patients live longer after tumor treatment, postoperative care must evolve. “The changing spectrum of neurointensive care unit needs for tumor patients”5 addresses the growing demand for personalized neurocritical care, encompassing not just acute care but cognitive, functional, and mental health recovery. Collaboration among neurointensive care specialists, oncologists, and rehabilitation experts is essential for holistic care of tumor patients.

Neuromodulatory Techniques for Cancer-Related Pain: Revolutionizing Pain Management

Managing cancer-related pain remains a major challenge. The paper “Surgical techniques for patients with cancer-related neuropathic pain”6 discusses advances like intrathecal drug delivery and spinal cord stimulation, offering better-targeted, less toxic pain relief. Personalized pain management, involving neurosurgeons, oncologists, and pain specialists, is crucial for improving patient quality of life.

Closing the Gap in Adolescents and Young Adults (AYAs) Glioma Care

Adolescents and young adults (AYAs, ages 15-39) face unique challenges in glioma care. “The molecular profile of gliomas in adolescents and young adults”7 notes that gliomas in this group often harbor targetable mutations, yet care remains fragmented between pediatric and adult systems. Expanding clinical trial access and integrating molecular diagnostics are critical steps toward better outcomes for AYAs.

Women in Neurosurgical Oncology: Paving the Way for Future Generations

Historically male-dominated, neurosurgery has seen a steady increase a more diverse participation of members. Over the years the field and subspecialty of neuro-oncological surgery has, through the tireless efforts and unwavering commitment of leading women8, built the culture of allyship and belonging so that younger generations of the Tumor Section can see themselves as leaders of the field regardless of their background, ethnicity, race, gender, global position, or academic affiliations. “Women in neurosurgical oncology: Mapping the milestones and shaping the future”9 highlights the growing contributions of women to the field, the importance of attracting the best and the brightest to neurosurgery, and underscores the need to support the next generation of trainees. By celebrating the achievements of trailblazing individuals, the advanced field and continues to evolve in exciting new directions.

Conclusion

The research and advancements highlighted in this issue underscore the importance of multidisciplinary collaboration in neurosurgical oncology. By integrating expertise across neurosurgical subspecialties, neurosurgical oncologists can provide more personalized, effective, and holistic care to patients with CNS tumors. As the field continues to evolve, these interdisciplinary approaches will pave the way for improved patient outcomes, from tumor diagnosis to postoperative recovery and long-term survival.

Neurosurgical oncology is truly at the crossroads of neurosurgery subspecialties, and its future promises to be marked by more innovative treatments, advanced technologies, and a deeper understanding of the complex interactions between tumors and the brain. Through continued collaboration, research, and mentorship, the future of neurosurgical oncology is brighter than ever, offering hope to patients facing the challenges of brain and spinal tumors.

From clinical observation to shared knowledge: the European Heart Journal Case Reports Cases of the year 2025
Authors: David Duncker, Henrike A K Hillmann, Philipp Sommer
Published: 2026

Case reports occupy a distinctive place in cardiovascular medicine. They rarely provide statistical certainty, but they often capture medicine as we most often experience it and learn from it every day: a patient, a diagnostic challenge, an uncommon condition, or a therapeutic decision to take—creating a learning point that we can remember, store, and share.

In this spirit, the European Heart Journal—Case Reports (EHJ-CR) cases of the year 2025 not only represent exceptional individual manuscripts but also the educational value of careful clinical observation and scientific writing.

The winning cases selected across the journal’s categories reflect the remarkable broadness of cardiovascular medicine (Table 1).

European Heart Journal Case Report cases of the year 2025

These papers deserve recognition not only for their clinical success but also for the work that sits behind every successful case report. A good case report is not only a rare or unusual story. It requires considerate clinical observation, careful documentation, written patient consent, and appropriate literature contextualization. The authors of these winning cases have achieved precisely that: they have transformed individual patient encounters into structured educational contributions for the cardiovascular community.

The cases of the year also reflect the broader concept of EHJ-CR.10 The journal aims to educate through the cases themselves. It also educates young authors, many of whom first encounter scientific publishing through the preparation and submission of a case report. In doing so, they learn how to frame a clinical observation, respond to reviewers, revise a manuscript, and engage constructively with the peer review process.

The educational mission extends further. Within the educational pathway of the EHJ-CR, young reviewers take their first steps in scientific peer review by learning how to assess clarity, clinical relevance, balance, and adherence to reporting standards. Young associate editors, furthermore, learn how to handle manuscripts, weigh reviewer comments, and guide authors and junior reviewers. The journal therefore functions not only as a venue for publication but also as a training environment for the next generation of academic clinicians, reviewers, and editors.11,12

The cases of the year 2025 are therefore more than category winners. They are examples of how clinical medicine becomes shared knowledge. We congratulate all winning authors and their teams.

The Parity of Two Types of Cyclotomic Euler Sums via Contour Integrals
Ce Xu

Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper, we employ methods of contour integration and residue calculus to investigate the parity of two classes of cyclotomic Euler-type sums. One class involves products of cyclotomic harmonic numbers, while the other involves products of cyclotomic odd harmonic numbers. We derive explicit formulas for the parity of linear and quadratic cases of these cyclotomic Euler-type sums and provide several illustrative examples. The results for the linear and quadratic cases ensure that we can provide explicit formulas for the parity of cyclotomic multiple
T
-values and cyclotomic multiple
S
-values up to depth three, both of which are even-odd variants of cyclotomic multiple zeta values. Furthermore, we present declarative theorems concerning the parity of these two types of cyclotomic Euler-type sums to arbitrary orders. Additionally, using contour integration techniques, we explore explicit linear and quadratic formulas for these cyclotomic Euler-type sums under more general conditions.

Keywords
: Cyclotomic Euler-type sums; Contour integrals; Residue calculus; Parity; Cyclotomic multiple zeta values; Cyclotomic multiple
S/T
-values; Even-odd variants.

Introduction

As is well known, the method of contour integration and residue computation in the complex plane is a highly effective approach for studying closed-form expressions of infinite series. Many classical problems involving infinite series can be proven using residue calculations. For example, the rational expansion of the classical inverse tangent function
\cot(z)
can be demonstrated by considering the integral of the function
\cot(z)-1/z
along a square contour centered at the origin with side length
(n+1/2)\pi\ (n\in\mathbb{N})
, computing its residues, and taking the limit as
n
approaches infinity. This yields:

\displaystyle\cot(z)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{z}+\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{2z}{z^{2}-n^{2}\pi^{2}}\quad(n\notin\mathbb{Z}\pi).

Furthermore, according to
Euler’s formula

e^{iz}=\cos(z)+i\sin(z)\ (i^{2}=-1,z\in\mathbb{C})
and the definition of
Bernoulli numbers

\displaystyle\frac{x}{e^{x}-1}=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\displaystyle\frac{B_{n}}{n!}x^{n}}
, the power series expansion of the inverse tangent function can be derived as follows (
[
3
]
):

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{z}{2}\cot\displaystyle\frac{z}{2}=\displaystyle\frac{iz}{2}\displaystyle\frac{e^{iz/2}+e^{-iz/2}}{e^{iz/2}-e^{-iz/2}}=1-\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-1}\displaystyle\frac{B_{2n}}{(2n)!}z^{2n}.

By combining the two expansions mentioned above, the rational fraction expansion of the inverse tangent function is first transformed into the form of a power series. Then, based on the uniqueness of power series, the closed-form expression for even zeta values (first discovered by Euler) is derived by equating the coefficients of any power of the variable
z
in the two power series (see
[
3
, Thm. 1.2.4]
):

\displaystyle\zeta(2k)=\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k-1}B_{2k}(2\pi)^{2k}}{2(2k)!}\quad(k\in\mathbb{N}),

(1.1)

where
\zeta(s)
denotes the
Riemann zeta function
defined by

\zeta(s):=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{s}}}\quad(\Re(s)>1).

After discovering the formula for even zeta values, Euler, in his correspondence with Goldbach between 1742 and 1743, also investigated the relationship between the following double series

S_{p;q}
(now known as
linear Euler sums
or
double zeta star values
) and Riemann zeta values (see
[
6
]
). The series
S_{p;q}
is defined as:

\displaystyle S_{p;q}:=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{H_{n}^{(p)}}{n^{q}}\quad(p,q\in\mathbb{N}\ \text{and}\ q>1),

(1.2)

where
H_{n}^{(p)}
stands the
generalized harmonic number
of order
p
defined by

H_{n}^{(p)}:=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{p}}.

Euler elaborated a method to show that the double series
S_{p;q}
can be evaluated in terms of zeta values in the following cases:
p=1
,
p=q
,
(p,q)=(2,4),(4,2)
and
p+q\leq 13
odd. Euler even conjectured that all

-coefficient combination of zeta values, a result that was proven by Nielsen
[
17

in 1906. Following Nielsen’s work, numerous experts and scholars have provided different proofs of this conjecture. For instance, in 1998, Flajolet and Salvy
[
7
, Thm. 3.1]
also demonstrated the conjecture using the method of contour integration and derived an explicit formula. Flajolet and Salvy not only considered the linear Euler sums
S_{p;q}
, but also systematically investigated the parity of
generalized Euler sums
of the following form:

\displaystyle{S_{{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{k}};q}}:=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}{\displaystyle\frac{{H_{n}^{\left({{p_{1}}}\right)}H_{n}^{\left({{p_{2}}}\right)}\cdots H_{n}^{\left({{p_{k}}}\right)}}}{{{n^{q}}}}},

(1.3)

where

and

. The quantity

is called the “weight” of the sum, and the quantity

is called the “degree (or order)”. When

. They proved the parity theorem for generalized Euler sums by considering the residue computation of the contour integral
\oint_{(\infty)}f(s)ds
of an integrand composed of trigonometric functions, the digamma function and its derivatives, and rational functions. Additionally, they provided general explicit formulae for the parity of linear and quadratic Euler sums, as well as some explicit formulas for cubic and quartic Euler sums. The parity theorem they established is stated as follows (see

When the weight

-linear combination of Euler sums of order at most

Here

\oint_{(\infty)}f(s)ds:=\displaystyle\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\oint_{|s|=R}r(s)\xi(s)ds=0,

is defined by the following two conditions: 1).
\xi(s)
is meromorphic in the whole complex plane.
2).
\xi(s)
satisfies

over an infinite collection of circles
\left|s\right|={\rho_{k}}
with
{\rho_{k}}\to\infty

Theorem 3.7
.

Let

and

can be expressed in terms of a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combination of cyclotomic multiple zeta values of level
\leq 2N
.

Proof.

By a direct calculation, one obtains

\displaystyle\Si_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})-(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}+r}(x_{2}\cdots x_{r}^{r})\Si_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}\Big(x_{1}^{-1},\ldots,x_{r}^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=(\sqrt{x_{2}})\cdots(\sqrt{x_{r}})^{r-1}\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r}\in\{\pm 1\}}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3}^{2}\cdots\sigma_{r}^{r-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\times\left(\Li_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}(\sigma_{1}\sqrt{x_{1}},\ldots,\sigma_{r}\sqrt{x_{r}})-(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}+r}\Li_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}\sqrt{x_{1}}},\ldots,\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{r}\sqrt{x_{r}}}\right)\right).

Therefore, by applying Panzer’s parity theorem, the proof of the theorem can be completed.
∎

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
x,x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
be roots of unity, and
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r},q\geq 1

reduces to a combination of sums of lower orders.

Proof.

To prove this theorem, we only need to consider contour integrals of the following form:

\displaystyle\oint\limits_{\left(\infty\right)}H_{p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{r},q}(x,x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r};s)ds

\displaystyle:=\oint\limits_{\left(\infty\right)}\displaystyle\frac{\Phi(s;x)\phi^{(p_{1}-1)}(s;x_{1})\phi^{(p_{2}-1)}(s;x_{2})\cdots\phi^{(p_{r}-1)}(s;x_{r})}{(p_{1}-1)!(p_{2}-1)!\cdots(p_{r}-1)!(s-1/2)^{q}}(-1)^{p_{1}+p_{2}+\cdots+p_{r}-r}ds=0.

Obviously, all positive integers are simple poles, all non-positive integers are poles of order
p_{1}+p_{2}+\cdots+p_{r}+1
, and
s=1/2
is a pole of order
q
. Applying Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm Res}\left(H_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),n\right)+\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\rm Res}\left(H_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),-n\right)+{\rm Res}\left(H_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),1/2\right)=0.

By direct residue calculations, we find that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm Res}\left(H_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),n\right)+\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\rm Res}\left(H_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),-n\right)

\displaystyle=(xx_{1}\cdots x_{r})^{-1}(-1)^{r}R_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{r};q}\Big(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r};(xx_{1}\cdots x_{r})^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{p_{1}+\cdots+p_{r}+q}R_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{r};q}\Big(x_{1}^{-1},\ldots,x_{r}^{-1};xx_{1}\cdots x_{r}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\{\text{combinations of lower-order cyclotomic Euler $R$-sums}\}

and

\displaystyle{\rm Res}\left(H_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),1/2\right)\in\{\text{combinations of lower-order cyclotomic Euler $R$-sums}\}.

Therefore, combining the two residue results above completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\left((-1)^{k_{2}}\Li_{k_{2}+p_{2}}(x_{2})+(-1)^{p_{2}}\zeta_{n}\Big(k_{2}+p_{2};x_{2}^{-1}\Big)\right)\displaystyle\frac{(xx_{1}x_{2})^{n}r_{3}^{(p_{1}-k_{1}-k_{2}-1)}(-n-1/2)}{(p_{1}-k_{1}-k_{2}-1)!},

\displaystyle{\rm Res}\left(g_{p_{1},p_{2},q_{2}}(x,x_{1},x_{2};s),0\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq k_{1}+k_{2}\leq q_{2},\atop k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0}(x_{1}x_{2})^{-1}\binom{k_{1}+p_{1}-1}{p_{1}-1}\binom{k_{2}+p_{2}-1}{p_{2}-1}\ti_{k_{1}+p_{1}}(x_{1})\ti_{k_{2}+p_{2}}(x_{2})\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}+k_{2}}R_{3}^{(q_{2}-k_{1}-k_{2})}(0)}{(q_{2}-k_{1}-k_{2})!}

\displaystyle\quad+\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}\leq q_{2}-1,\atop k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\geq 0}\left((-1)^{k_{1}}\Li_{k_{1}+1}(x)-\Li_{k_{1}+1}\Big(x^{-1}\Big)\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\binom{k_{2}+p_{1}-1}{p_{1}-1}\binom{k_{3}+p_{2}-1}{p_{2}-1}(-1)^{k_{2}+k_{3}}\ti_{k_{2}+p_{1}}(x_{1})\ti_{k_{3}+p_{2}}(x_{2})\displaystyle\frac{(x_{1}x_{2})^{-1}R_{3}^{(q_{2}-k_{1}-k_{2}-k_{3}-1)}(0)}{(q_{2}-k_{1}-k_{2}-k_{3}-1)!}.

From Lemma
2.1
, we know that

\displaystyle{\rm Res}\left(g_{p_{1},p_{2},q_{2}}(x,x_{1},x_{2};s),0\right)+\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm Res}\left(g_{p_{1},p_{2},q_{2}}(x,x_{1},x_{2};s),n\right)

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\rm Res}\left(g_{p_{1},p_{2},q_{2}}(x,x_{1},x_{2};s),-n-1/2\right)+\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm Res}\left(g_{p_{1},p_{2},q_{2}}(x,x_{1},x_{2};s),-n\right)

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in S_{3}\backslash\left\{0\right\}}{\rm Res}\left(g_{p_{1},p_{2},q_{2}}(x,x_{1},x_{2};s),\alpha\right)=0.

Finally, combining these contributions yields the result.
∎

Clearly, the results presented in Sections 3 and 4 can be derived in this section by assigning specific values to the rational functions discussed here. Similarly, assigning other values to these rational functions may yield additional results on cyclotomic Euler-type sums, which we leave to interested readers.

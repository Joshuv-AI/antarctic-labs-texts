Diagnostic value of the nomogram model based on multimodal ultrasound features combined with PS-Tg for differentiating benign and malignant thyroid nodules
Authors: Zhenhao Zheng, Yang Yu, Zelin Xu, Yan Liu, Rui Huo, Yuwen Cao, Ting Ma, Jun Li, Wen Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThyroid cancer (TC) is the most common malignant disease occurring in the neck region, exhibiting an increasing incidence trend year by year. Fine-needle aspiration (FNA), as an invasive diagnostic procedure, carries risks such as puncture failure and local hemorrhage. Therefore, there is a need to explore early, accurate, and noninvasive or minimally invasive diagnostic methods for TC. This study aimed to investigate the diagnostic value of a nomogram model based on multimodal ultrasound features combined with preoperative serum thyroglobulin (PS-Tg) for differentiating benign and malignant thyroid nodules.MethodsA total of 435 patients with 572 thyroid nodules undergoing FNA or thyroidectomy at The First Affiliated Hospital of Shihezi University between January 2023 and January 2025 were enrolled. All patients underwent preoperative thyroid function tests, two-dimensional ultrasound (2D-US), microvascular flow imaging (MV-Flow), strain elastography (SE), and elasticity contrast index (ECI) examinations. PS-Tg levels, 2D-US features, microvascular flow vascular index (VI), and elastography indices [strain ratio (SR), ECI] were collected. Based on final pathological results, nodules were divided into the benign group (n=248) and the malignant group (n=324). Multivariate logistic regression analysis was employed to explore the diagnostic value of a model combining multimodal ultrasound features and serological indicators for differentiating benign and malignant thyroid nodules.ResultsMultivariate logistic regression analysis identified the following as independent risk factors for malignant thyroid nodules (P<0.05): hypoechoic [odds ratio (OR) =5.843, 95% confidence interval (CI): 3.391–10.075, P<0.001], anteroposterior/transverse (A/T) ratio ≥1 (OR 2.838, 95% CI: 1.821–4.424, P<0.001), irregular or lobulated margin (OR 2.234, 95% CI: 1.444–3.456, P<0.001), microcalcifications (OR 2.267, 95% CI: 1.458–3.525, P<0.001), PS-Tg (OR 1.005, 95% CI: 1.001–1.010, P=0.03), ECI-Max (OR 1.379, 95% CI: 1.150–1.653, P=0.001), SR-Max (OR 1.325, 95% CI: 1.036–1.696, P=0.03), and SR-Min (OR 1.652, 95% CI: 1.024–2.667, P=0.04). Among the evaluated models, the multimodal ultrasound combined with serological model demonstrated the best diagnostic performance, with a sensitivity (SEN) of 0.867, specificity (SPE) of 0.698, accuracy (ACC) of 0.794, and area under the curve (AUC) of 0.864. The calibration of the nomogram was good, showing no significant deviation (χ2=4.43, P=0.816).ConclusionsThe nomogram based on the multivariate logistic regression model incorporating multimodal ultrasound features combined with PS-Tg demonstrates high diagnostic value in differentiating benign and malignant thyroid nodules.Keywords: Thyroid cancer (TC), two-dimensional ultrasound (2D-US), microvascular flow imaging (MV-Flow), strain elastography (SE), elasticity contrast index (ECI), nomogram

Highlight box

Key findings

• The nomogram model integrating multimodal ultrasound [two-dimensional ultrasound (2D-US), microvascular flow imaging (MV-Flow), strain elastography (SE), elasticity contrast index (ECI)] and preoperative serum thyroglobulin (PS-Tg) exhibits high diagnostic value for differentiating benign and malignant thyroid nodules, with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.864, sensitivity of 0.867, and specificity of 0.698. Hypoechoic, anteroposterior/transverse (A/T) ≥1, irregular/lobulated margin, microcalcifications, PS-Tg, ECI-Max, strain ratio (SR)-Min, and SR-Max are independent risk factors.

What is known and what is new?

• Fine-needle aspiration (FNA) is invasive with potential risks; single-modal ultrasound has limited accuracy due to overlapping features; American College of Radiology (ACR) Thyroid Imaging Reporting and Data System (TI-RADS) provides basic risk stratification.

• This study integrates anatomical, hemodynamic, and serological data to construct a visual nomogram, which shows good calibration (χ2=4.43, P=0.82) and outperforms single-modal or 2D-US models.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The non-invasive model provides complementary quantitative information for malignant risk stratification, and may assist clinical decision-making when combined with other findings. It can serve as a quantitative auxiliary tool for risk assessment in clinical practice. Owing to the absence of external validation, the model is not yet ready for routine widespread clinical use, and multi-center prospective studies are still required for further validation.

Introduction

Thyroid cancer (TC) is the most common malignant tumor in the endocrine system (1). In recent years, its incidence rate has been rising rapidly in China and many other countries and regions around the world. According to the 2024 China Cancer Report, there were 466,100 new cases of TC in 2022, ranking third among all new cancer cases in China. Among these, it ranked seventh among new male cancer cases and third among new female cancer cases (2). The prevalence of thyroid nodules is as high as 67%, and most patients with malignant nodules have no symptoms (3). Therefore, this disease is often overlooked by patients, leading to delayed treatment (4).

Fine-needle aspiration (FNA) has become an important recommended method for assessing the nature of thyroid nodules due to its efficiency and diagnostic value (5). However, as an invasive procedure, this technique carries risks such as puncture failure and local bleeding that can cause compression and suffocation, imposing additional psychological burden and financial costs on the patient (6). Therefore, it is urgent to explore early, accurate, non-invasive or minimally invasive diagnostic methods for TC, provide new diagnostic ideas for clinical treatment, and achieve individualized and accurate diagnosis and treatment.

Two-dimensional ultrasound (2D-US) examination has become the preferred method for diagnosing thyroid, breast, kidney and other diseases due to its advantages such as economy, ease of operation, no risk of radiation exposure, strong repeatability and real-time dynamic observation (7). However, the ultrasound imaging manifestations of thyroid nodules are highly heterogeneous. To standardize imaging assessment criteria for malignant nodules and identify the most significant clinical features of malignant nodules, the American College of Radiology (ACR) published the Thyroid Imaging Reporting and Data System (TI-RADS) in 2017 (8). This system employs a unified and concise classification method to establish a tiered diagnostic framework based on five key indicators: internal composition, echogenicity, morphology, margin characteristics, and calcification type.

In recent years, microvascular flow imaging (MV-Flow) technology, as a novel ultra-micro blood flow imaging technique, has been gradually applied in ultrasound work. Compared to traditional color Doppler flow imaging (CDFI) (9), MV-Flow technology enhances spatial resolution by optimizing blood flow signal processing algorithms, while maintaining high frame rates and good reproducibility. This enables MV-Flow to clearly depict slow micro-perfusion blood flow in peripheral vascular structures. This technology can also calculate the vascularity index (VI), a metric that can be used for quantitative analysis, automatically calculating the ratio of blood-filled pixels to total pixels within the region of interest (ROI), providing objective parameters for assessing the blood supply status of lesions and distinguishing between benign and malignant conditions.

The concept of ultrasound elastography (UE) was introduced in the 1990s. The basic principle of UE involves applying pressure to tissue using an ultrasound probe; the tissue responds by exhibiting different elastic modulus values, thereby allowing for the differentiation of its hardness characteristics. It has since been gradually integrated into ultrasound workflows. Strain elastography (SE) and elasticity contrast index (ECI) are further evolved elastic imaging techniques based on conventional UE. SE calculates the relative strain distribution (i.e., displacement gradient tensor) of tissue after pressure application, maps spatial strain values to corresponding pixels in a 2D grayscale image, and can further quantify nodule hardness through the strain ratio (SR), defined as the relative strain between the target nodule and surrounding tissues. Current domestic and international studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of SE in identifying benign and malignant thyroid nodules (10,11). ECI technology measures the variability of strain within the ROI using the formula ECI=lu−v12P(u,v). A study of 101 thyroid nodules with uncertain ultrasound features showed that the malignant nodules had significantly higher ECI values than benign thyroid nodules (9). The assessment model combining ECI with conventional ultrasound is helpful in diagnosing the benign or malignant nature of such nodules.

Thyroglobulin (Tg) is a large glycoprotein synthesized by thyroid follicular epithelial cells and primarily stored in the colloid of the follicular lumen. Under normal conditions, only a small amount of Tg can enter the bloodstream through transport (12). Inflammation and injury can lead to massive Tg release, increasing peripheral blood concentration. In addition, a recent study (13) has suggested that preoperative serum thyroglobulin (PS-Tg) levels in patients with thyroid malignant tumors are higher than those in patients with benign nodules.

To overcome the limitations of single-modality diagnostic approaches, multimodal ultrasound technology integrates multi-dimensional information such as anatomical structure, hemodynamics, and tissue mechanics, significantly enhancing the comprehensive assessment capabilities for thyroid nodules. Existing evidence indicates (14,15) that multimodal ultrasound outperforms single-modality ultrasound in diagnosing thyroid nodules and cervical lymph node metastasis. Based on this, this study aims to construct a multimodal joint diagnostic model based on multivariable logistic regression, with the following predictive factors: 2D ultrasound features (based on the ACR TI-RADS standard), vascular index (VI), ECI, SR, and PS-Tg. A nomogram will be constructed to visualize model parameters, exploring their combined application in diagnosing the benign or malignant nature of thyroid nodules. This exploration not only improves the diagnostic accuracy of thyroid nodules but also promotes the early detection and treatment of TC. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://gs.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/gs-2026-1-0079/rc).

Methods

Patients and methods

A retrospective review was conducted of 652 thyroid nodules from 515 patients who underwent total or near-total thyroidectomy or FNA at The First Affiliated Hospital of Shihezi University between January 2023 and January 2025. According to the ACR diagnostic criteria, nodules measuring ≥2.5 cm in TR3 and ≥1.5 cm in TR4 require FNA or surgery based on surgical criteria. FNA or surgery should also be performed if the patient strongly requests it. All patients underwent routine thyroid function testing and 2D ultrasound examination prior to surgery. During the 2D ultrasound examination, elasticity indices and microvascular flow indices of the target nodules were measured. Pathological results were obtained postoperatively. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Ethical Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Shihezi University (Ethics Review Number: KJ2024-250-01), and all participants provided informed consent.

Inclusion criteria

(I) Patients undergoing FNA examination or thyroid mass resection at our hospital; (II) new cases; (III) patients who have not taken oral levothyroxine, propylthiouracil, methimazole, or other drugs, and have not received radioactive I131 or other treatments prior to surgery.

Exclusion criteria

(I) Patients with a history of malignant tumors; (II) patients who have previously undergone thyroid-related surgery; (III) patients with incomplete clinical data (e.g., lacking thyroid gray-scale ultrasound results, ultrasound elasticity indices, ultrasound microvascular flow indices, or pathological diagnosis results); (IV) patients with thyroid functional disorders (e.g., hyperthyroidism, Hashimoto’s thyroiditis, etc.) or elevated thyroglobulin antibody (Tg-Ab) >115 ng/mL; (V) patients with Bethesda category I, III or IV of thyroid cell pathology after FNA biopsy and without further diagnosis; (VI) patients who have been using hormonal drugs, iodine-containing agents, immunosuppressants, etc., either long-term or recently. After screening, 572 thyroid nodules from 435 patients who met the criteria were ultimately included.

Conventional ultrasound parameter acquisition

Using the Samsung R10 color Doppler ultrasound diagnostic instrument, instruct the patient to lie flat on their back with their head slightly tilted backward, exposing the neck, and breathing calmly. Apply an adequate amount of coupling agent, select the LA2-14A linear array probe, and set the frequency to 2–14 MHz. Enter the thyroid scanning mode, with the probe placed close to the skin without applying pressure. First, perform routine transverse and longitudinal section scans to examine the overall structure of the thyroid. Then, focus on suspicious thyroid nodules, collecting and recording nodule size (cm × cm), internal composition, echo characteristics, and other ultrasound features.

MV-Flow parameter acquisition

After a routine ultrasound examination, switch to MV-Flow mode, adjust the image to clearly display the relationship between the nodule and surrounding tissues, freeze the image, select the blood flow distribution index, select the ROI, and click OK to obtain the blood flow distribution index (VI%). Repeat the measurement three times, record the VI values, and calculate the average value (VI-Ave) for further analysis.

ECI parameter acquisition

Switch to ECI mode, adjust the sampling frame (including the target nodule and surrounding glandular tissue, with a size twice that of the lesion), instruct the patient to refrain from swallowing, and freeze the image once the color indicator bar is fully green. Manually mark the ROI at the nodule-normal tissue interface (region ratio 1:1). Collect ECI values from five different levels for each nodule, and record the maximum value (ECI-Max), minimum value (ECI-Min), and average value (ECI-Ave) for subsequent analysis.

SR parameter acquisition

Switch to SE examination conditions, allow the patient to breathe calmly, and place the probe gently on the patient’s neck, ensuring no additional pressure is applied. When the applied pressure is stable and the pressure indicator displays within the ideal range, the color elasticity image will gradually stabilize. Wait until the elasticity image quality indicator on the right side of the screen displays 1–2 green indicator blocks, then freeze the image. Adjust the ROI for SR measurement, take 5 measurements, and record the SR values. Similarly, select the maximum value (SR-Max) and minimum value (SR-Min), and calculate the average (SR-Ave) for further analysis. All ultrasound examinations were performed by an ultrasound physician with over 10 years of experience in thyroid diagnosis.

Serological and pathological parameter acquisition

All patients underwent thyroid function testing via venous blood draw before surgery. Researchers obtained PS-Tg indicators from our hospital’s working system. Postoperative pathology or FNA results were provided in standardized pathology reports issued by pathologists with over 5 years of experience. FNA results were graded according to the 2017 Bethesda System for Reporting Thyroid Cytology (16). Postoperative thyroid pathology was based on the final paraffin-embedded histopathological sections as the gold standard. If patients with nodules underwent only FNA without nodule excision for histopathological assessment, Bethesda categories V and VI were classified as malignant; Bethesda category II and those with stable or shrinking nodules observed on ultrasound follow-up were classified as benign (17). Given the wide range of benign and malignant outcomes for Bethesda categories I, III, and IV nodules, this study excluded nodules for which definitive pathological results were not available due to the absence of surgical resection.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses in this study were performed using SPSS 26.0 and R 4.3.3 software. Continuous variables that followed a normal distribution were described as mean ± standard deviation, while those that did not follow a normal distribution were expressed as median (interquartile range). Intergroup comparisons were performed using independent samples t-tests and Wilcoxon rank-sum tests, respectively. Categorical variables (count data) were described as frequencies (percentages), and intergroup comparisons were performed using chi-square tests. A multivariate logistic regression analysis model was fitted to identify risk factors influencing the study outcome. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was used to determine the optimal diagnostic cutoff value for continuous variables based on the principle of maximizing the Youden index. Sensitivity (SEN), specificity (SPE), accuracy (ACC), positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), and area under the curve (AUC) were calculated and compared to assess the diagnostic performance of parameters or models. Intergroup AUC differences were statistically compared using DeLong’s test. For the regression model with the best predictive performance, a calibration curve (CC) was further plotted to assess the consistency (calibration) between the model’s predicted probability and the actual observed probability, and a decision curve analysis (DCA) curve was plotted to assess the clinical net benefit of the model at different threshold probabilities. The significance level was set at α=0.05, with P<0.05 indicating a statistically significant difference.

Results

Pathological findings

This study included a total of 435 patients with 572 thyroid nodules. Among these, 182 nodules underwent FNA alone (128 classified as Bethesda Category II, 28 as Category V, and 26 as Category VI). Among the 390 nodules that underwent paraffin-embedded pathological examination after thyroidectomy, 120 were diagnosed as benign (115 cases of nodular goiter and 5 cases of thyroid adenoma), and 270 were diagnosed as malignant (267 cases of papillary thyroid carcinoma, 2 cases of medullary carcinoma, and 1 case of follicular thyroid carcinoma). The final pathological diagnosis results showed a total of 248 benign nodules (43.36%) and 324 malignant nodules (56.64%) (Table 1).

Multivariate logistic regression analysis

This study first identified variables associated with malignant thyroid nodules through univariate analysis (selection criteria: P<0.05 and AUC >0.6), followed by multivariate logistic regression analysis to further adjust for potential confounding factors. The final confirmed independent risk factors for malignant thyroid nodules were hypoechoic lesions, A/T ≥1, irregular or lobulated shape, microcalcifications, PS-Tg, ECI-Max, SR-Min, and SR-Max. The optimal cut-off values for PS-Tg, ECI-Max, SR-Min, and SR-Max were 20.69, 1.745, 1.455, and 2.115, respectively. Hypoechoic has the highest odds ratio (OR) of 5.835 (P<0.001) (Table 3). These factors collectively form an effective predictive model (Model 2) for assessing the malignant risk of thyroid nodules.

Diagnostic performance of the models

To assess the performance advantages of the integrated multivariate logistic regression analysis, this study included the following ultrasound binary classification variables in the multivariate logistic regression analysis: hypoechoic, A/T ≥1, irregular or lobulated, and microcalcifications. A 2D ultrasound feature joint model (Model 1) was constructed (Table 4). DeLong’s test was used to compare the multivariate logistic regression model (Model 2) with the following models or indicators: (I) a joint model based on 2D ultrasound features (Model 1); (II) ECI-Max; (III) SR-Max; (IV) SR-Min; (V) PS-Tg. The AUC of Model 2 was 0.864, significantly superior to all comparison methods (Model 1 AUC: 0.82, PS-Tg AUC: 0.732, ECI-Max AUC: 0.662, SR-Min AUC: 0.706, SR-Max AUC: 0.726, P<0.001 for all). The greatest difference was observed between the Model 2 and ECI-Max (ΔAUC =0.202, Z=8.519); The SEN, SPE, ACC, PPV, and NPV of the Model 2 were 0.867, 0.698, 0.794, 0.789, and 0.801, respectively. Compared with Model 1 and each indicator, the overall diagnostic performance was enhanced (Table 5, Figure 1). A comparison of the diagnostic performance between the multivariate logistic regression model (Model 2) and TI-RADS, using DeLong’s test, is shown in Figure 2. The AUC of Model 2 was significantly higher than that of TI-RADS (ΔAUC =0.125, 95% CI: 0.089–0.161, Z=6.85, P<0.001; DeLong test).

Construction of the nomogram

A nomogram was constructed based on a multimodal ultrasound combined with PS-Tg multivariate logistic regression model, and plot CC and decision curves (DCA) to analyze their consistency and clinical net benefit. In the CC, the bias-corrected curve and the apparent calibration line are nearly identical and both are very close to the ideal diagonal line, indicating that the model did not exhibit significant overfitting after Bootstrap internal validation (B=1,000 repeated samples), with stable predictive accuracy. The extremely low mean absolute error (MAE =0.016, n=572) further corroborates the model’s calibration accuracy, with no statistically significant difference in goodness-of-fit (χ2=4.43, P=0.82), indicating good consistency; in the DCA, the standardized net benefit (SNB) curve of the nomogram model was positioned above the “treat-all” and “treat-none” reference lines across a wide range of threshold probabilities. Notably, in the clinically relevant moderate-risk threshold range (approximately 0.2 to 0.6, corresponding to a cost-benefit ratio of 1:4 to 4:1), the model’s net benefit showed a clear advantage over both extreme strategies. This threshold range is particularly meaningful for thyroid nodule management: when a patient’s predicted probability of malignancy falls within this interval, using the nomogram to guide further testing (e.g., FNA) or surgery can maximize the number of true positive findings. The DCA explicitly quantifies the trade-off between the harm of missing a malignancy (false negative) and the harm of performing an unnecessary intervention on a benign nodule (false positive). By comparing the net benefit across thresholds, clinicians can identify the risk range in which the model offers the greatest clinical utility. Even at very low (1:100) or very high (100:1) cost-benefit extremes, the full model did not exhibit negative net benefit, indicating its robustness and safety for use in diverse clinical scenarios. Therefore, the DCA supports that the nomogram can aid individualized decision making: for a patient with a risk estimate in the moderate range, the model provides a clear net benefit, while for very low or high risk patients, clinical actions can be confidently directed toward observation or surgery, respectively (Figures 3-5). Discussion

This study included a total of 572 thyroid nodules from 435 patients. In this study, 56.64% of nodules were ultimately diagnosed as malignant, while approximately 43.36% were diagnosed as benign. The proportion of malignant nodules was higher than the typical benign-to-malignant ratio observed in routine clinical practice. This is because the study population consisted exclusively of patients who underwent FNA or thyroid surgery; the prevalence of malignant nodules in this group is significantly higher than in the general population undergoing routine health examinations. Additionally, as the hospital where this study was conducted serves as a regional diagnostic and treatment center, the majority of patients admitted were those with suspected malignant nodules who had been referred after initial screening at primary care or community hospitals. This further contributed to the elevated proportion of malignant cases in the study sample. Therefore, future studies should include a large number of benign nodule samples to better reflect the actual distribution of benign and malignant thyroid nodules in clinical practice. The analysis revealed that there was a significant difference in the age distribution between patients with benign and malignant nodules (P=0.004). The average age of patients with malignant nodules (48.60 years) was lower than that of patients with benign nodules (53.58 years). This finding is consistent with the results of Saini et al. (18), who pointed out that malignant thyroid nodules are more common in younger patients. In this study, the proportion of female patients among those with malignant nodules reached 73.15%. The potential promotion of estrogen levels on the occurrence and development of TC may be an important biological mechanism to explain this gender difference (19). To further explore the relationship between body growth indicators and malignant thyroid nodules, this study analyzed patients’ body mass index (BMI) and body surface area (BSA). The results showed that the average BMI of the included patients was 25.46 kg/m2, which was higher than the normal range recommended by the guidelines (20) (typically 18.5–24.0 kg/m2). However, the differences in BMI (P=0.066) and BSA (P=0.074) between patients with benign and malignant nodules did not reach statistical significance. This result may be because the patients included in this study were mainly from the endocrinology and metabolism department, and their primary reason for visiting the hospital was mostly abnormal blood sugar and blood lipid metabolism indicators, and thyroid nodules were mostly discovered during routine examinations during the visit. This specific source of patients may have led to selection bias in the distribution of growth indicators such as BMI in the study population (the proportion of high BMI individuals may be higher than in the general thyroid nodule population), which may be an important reason why this study failed to observe a significant association between BMI/BSA and the risk of nodule malignancy. Future studies need to further validate the relationship between growth indicators and TC risk in more representative populations. We excluded Bethesda Category I, III, and IV nodules that had not undergone further diagnostic evaluation from this study. The primary purpose of this exclusion was to prevent nodules with uncertain diagnostic outcomes from confounding the assessment of the model’s diagnostic performance; however, this approach does indeed reduce the model’s applicability in the real world for nodules with inconclusive FNA results. In the future, we will apply the model to such nodules classified as Bethesda Categories I, III, and IV nodules with complete postoperative pathology to evaluate the model’s diagnostic performance for nodules with inconclusive FNA results.

This study’s multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that, among 2D ultrasound features, hypoechoic lesions, A/T ≥1, irregular or lobulated margins, and microcalcifications were independent risk factors for malignant thyroid nodules (all P<0.001). Among these, hypoechoic appearance had the highest OR of 5.843, indicating that nodules with hypoechoic internal echoes have a 5.843-fold higher risk of malignancy compared to those with other echo patterns. This finding is consistent with the results of previous studies (21,22). The internal echoes of malignant thyroid nodules are predominantly hypoechoic. The main reason is that they are mostly solid components with disordered internal structures, densely arranged cells, and low content of interstitial tissue such as collagen fibers, resulting in a significant difference in their acoustic impedance characteristics from the surrounding normal thyroid tissue (23-25). The above principles can also indicate that, in the 2D ultrasound characteristics of malignant nodules, hypoechoic patterns are highly correlated with solid components. Therefore, although the solid component was significantly associated with malignant nodules in the univariate analysis (P<0.05), they were ultimately excluded from the multivariate predictive model due to collinearity issues, a result consistent with previous findings (21,24). An A/T ratio ≥1 indicates that the growth pattern of the thyroid nodule is longitudinal growth (anteroposterior diameter). The mechanism underlying longitudinal growth in malignant thyroid nodules remains incompletely understood. Yang et al. (26) suggested that malignant thyroid nodules have higher tissue hardness and exhibit infiltrative growth within the gland, leading to reduced deformation under probe pressure during ultrasound examination. In contrast, most benign nodules have lower hardness and non-infiltrative growth patterns, making them more prone to compression deformation under ultrasound. Irregular borders or lobulation indicate the outward invasive growth of malignant nodules. Multiple studies (21,22,27) have confirmed that this feature is a risk factor for malignant nodules. Cui et al. (22) found that the irregular borders, lobulated appearance, or outwardly invasive characteristics of TC are highly correlated with the expression of the BRAF V600E gene, which may alter the morphological appearance of nodules by promoting the invasion and metastasis potential of tumor cells. However, this study was limited by the absence of BRAF V600E gene detection indicators, preventing further analysis and validation of this mechanism. Microcalcification is also identified as an independent risk factor for malignant nodules, consistent with the findings of Zhang et al. (28). Its formation mechanism is associated with active tumor cell proliferation: the rapid growth and proliferation of tumor cells can lead to local ischemia and necrosis, ultimately resulting in calcium salt formation. In this study, most of the malignant nodules were papillary thyroid carcinomas (267/324). After the characteristic papillary structures undergo degeneration and necrosis, they ultimately form sand-like bodies, which typically appear as punctate hyperechoic lesions on ultrasound images, i.e., microcalcifications (29).

MV-Flow technology is a micro-blood flow imaging technique, which provides semi-quantitative and quantitative auxiliary diagnostic information for target nodules. In recent years, this technology has demonstrated significant diagnostic value in obstetric research, particularly in assessing fetal brain and placental function (30). In this study, VI values were used to quantitatively analyze blood flow within thyroid nodules, with the mean of three measurements (VI-Ave) serving as the indicator. The results showed that the difference in VI-Ave between benign and malignant thyroid nodules was statistically significant (P<0.001), indicating that VI values exhibit statistically significant differences between benign and malignant nodules. However, in a multivariable logistic regression analysis, VI-Ave was not identified as an independent risk factor for malignant thyroid nodules. The reason for this is that the information regarding blood flow that it represents has essentially been “overshadowed” or “explained” by other strong, effective features—such as hypoechoic areas, irregular margins, and a length-to-width ratio ≥1 on 2D-US—and thus does not provide additional independent predictive value. In a study by Yim et al. (23), micro-blood flow imaging technology was found to have higher diagnostic sensitivity, specificity, and better diagnostic performance, which is inconsistent with the finding that VI-Ave was not an independent risk factor in this study. The reason for this discrepancy is that the studies included in the meta-analysis primarily relied on micro-blood flow technology to grade the distribution of blood vessels within nodules for benign or malignant diagnosis. Previous studies have demonstrated (31) that benign thyroid nodules typically exhibit peripheral blood flow patterns, while malignant tumors are more commonly associated with nodular blood flow. Li et al. (32) further distinguished blood flow patterns, finding that Crab Claw-like pattern and Root Hair-like patterns are microcirculatory characteristics of malignant thyroid nodules. Wheel-like and arborescent patterns can help rule out TC. Our study only included the nodule micro-blood flow index (VI-Ave) and did not analyze the micro-blood flow distribution pattern within the nodule, which may be a key factor contributing to the differences in results. Therefore, it is necessary to include this feature in future studies to comprehensively evaluate the diagnostic value of MV-Flow technology for distinguishing benign from malignant thyroid nodules.

SE is an UE imaging technique based on tissue mechanical properties. Its core principle involves the operator applying pressure to the target area using an ultrasound probe, causing deformation and feedback of strain changes. The method and intensity of pressure application are susceptible to subjective factors. ECI, uses carotid artery pulsation as the pressure source, avoiding external pressure and thus eliminating discrepancies in results due to differences in pressure applied manually. This study analyzed six evaluation indicators for the two elasticity techniques: SR-Max, SR-Min, SR-Ave, ECI-Max, ECI-Min, and ECI-Ave. In the univariate analysis, all evaluation indicators showed statistically significant differences (P<0.05), indicating that the hardness of malignant nodules is higher than that of benign nodules, consistent with previous studies (9,14). Ultimately, multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that SR-Max, SR-Min, and ECI-Max were independent risk factors for malignant thyroid nodules (OR: 1.325, 1.652, and 1.379, respectively). The World Federation of Ultrasound in Medicine and Biology (WFUMB) (33) recommends an SR cut-off value range of 1.5–5.0. The cut-off values of SR-Max (2.115) and SR-Min (1.455) in this study fell within or were close to this range. However, different studies have yielded varying optimal cut-off values (34,35). Due to the use of different parameter indicators and sampling patterns across studies, the final results cannot be consistently compared. Therefore, further exploration is needed to determine which parameters and sampling patterns can maximize the diagnostic efficacy for distinguishing benign from malignant thyroid nodules. This study suggests that the cut-off value of ECI-Max at 1.745 yields the best diagnostic efficacy. Lu et al. (36) used the maximum measured value as the final indicator, which showed statistical significance in distinguishing benign from malignant thyroid nodules (P<0.01), consistent with the results of this study. However, the cut-off value they proposed (2.635) was higher than that of this study. Their study included samples classified as C-TIRADS category 4 or higher, and this sample difference may be the primary cause of the disparity in results. Additionally, Cong et al. (27) studied 138 thyroid nodules in 101 patients and found that ECI had no significant role in distinguishing suspicious thyroid nodules (P=0.592). Upon further analysis, this may be due to the limited number of measurements (only 3 times) and the high proportion of highly suspicious malignant nodules (73.4%) included in the study. The low heterogeneity of the sample reduced the model’s discriminatory ability. This further highlights the critical impact of standardized measurement protocols (including the number of measurements) and study population characteristics on the comparability of results.

This study found that the PS-Tg levels in malignant nodules were significantly higher than those in benign nodules (29.08 vs. 8.96 ng/mL, P<0.01). ROC curve analysis determined the optimal diagnostic cutoff value to be 20.69 ng/mL. Multivariate logistic regression analysis further confirmed that elevated PS-Tg levels were an independent risk factor for malignant thyroid nodules (OR =1.005, P=0.03), consistent with the findings of previous studies (37). However, existing studies have yielded inconsistent conclusions regarding the value of Tg in distinguishing between benign and malignant thyroid nodules (38). In this study, the OR for PS-Tg was 1.005, indicating that a 1-unit increase in PS-Tg is associated with a 0.5% increase in the risk of malignant nodules. This suggests that PS-Tg is not a strong independent predictor, but rather a serological marker that provides supplementary information to multimodal ultrasound and plays a supportive role in combined models. Therefore, the efficacy of PS-Tg as a diagnostic marker requires further confirmation through high-quality studies.

It is worth noting that in this study, the optimal cutoff values for ECI, SR, and PS-Tg were determined using the Youden index based on the current dataset and are therefore data-driven. These thresholds may not be directly applicable to other populations, regions, or ultrasound devices. Furthermore, this study only demonstrates that the aforementioned variables are independent risk factors for malignant thyroid nodules; under different environmental conditions, sensitivity analyses and recalibration of thresholds are required to enhance their applicability in various clinical settings.

To validate the diagnostic performance of the multivariate logistic regression model based on multimodal ultrasound and PS-Tg, this study compared it with a model constructed using 2D ultrasound features (hypoechoic, A/T ≥1, microcalcifications, irregular or lobulated) and single indicators (SR-Max, SR-Min, ECI-Max, PS-Tg). Using DeLong’s test for pairwise comparisons, the results showed that the AUC value of the multifactor model was the highest (0.864), and the differences compared with other models and all single indicators were statistically significant (P<0.001). These results confirm that models integrating multi-dimensional information significantly outperform single models or individual indicators in distinguishing between benign and malignant thyroid nodules, consistent with conclusions from similar multi-factor model studies (14,15,21,22,24,28). This advantage may stem from the model’s integration of complementary information from various techniques that are most valuable for diagnosing malignant nodules, thereby enhancing overall diagnostic capability. This study constructed a nomogram model, which transformed the complex logistic regression results into an intuitive visualization tool that can quantitatively predict the malignant risk of thyroid nodules. This nomogram model can be integrated into a hospital’s electronic medical record system as a ‘calculator’ through software design. Clinicians can input the ultrasound and elasticity characteristics of a patient’s thyroid nodule, along with the PS-Tg value, into the program to obtain a real-time probability of malignancy for the target nodule, thereby assisting doctors in developing personalized treatment plans. CC analysis indicates that the predicted risk from this model is highly consistent with the actual observed risk. Additionally, DCA shows that the full model has higher clinical net benefit across a wide range of clinical decision thresholds. This suggests that in clinical practice, this nomogram provides complementary quantitative risk information to assist clinical decision-making. However, the lack of external validation restricts its direct clinical promotion, and the generalizability of the model needs to be confirmed in more diverse populations and medical centers.

This study has the following limitations: (I) this study is a single-center retrospective study without independent external validation, which is the most critical limitation and may weaken the model’s reliability and generalizability. Prospective, multi-center external validation is urgently needed before wide clinical application; (II) the MV-Flow technique only quantifies blood supply using VI values and does not incorporate blood flow distribution patterns for further study; this indicator should be included in future studies to analyze whether nodule blood flow parameters are independent risk factors; (III) this study included only patients who underwent fine-needle aspiration biopsy or thyroidectomy and did not expand the sample size to include negative nodules, which may have led to a certain degree of overestimation of the model’s diagnostic performance (particularly the AUC and sensitivity). Future studies should include thyroid nodules that have not been pathologically confirmed but have been diagnosed as negative based on multiple examinations and regular follow-up to improve the model’s applicability; (IV) all ultrasound parameters were measured by a single experienced physician; inter-observer variability was not assessed. In the future, multiple independent ultrasound physicians will be included to verify the reproducibility of the ultrasound parameters.

Conclusions

In summary, the diagnostic model combining multimodal ultrasound with PS-Tg levels shows favorable diagnostic performance in the internal cohort. The nomogram provides useful auxiliary reference for clinical practice, but still requires multi-center external validation before large‑scale clinical application.

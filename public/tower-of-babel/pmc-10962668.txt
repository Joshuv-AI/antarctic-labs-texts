Steamed daylily flower (Hemerocallis fulva L.) protected cardiac and hepatic cells against ethanol induced apoptotic and oxidative damage
Authors: Sheng-Lei Yan, Wen-Tzu Wu, Mei-Chin Mong, Mei-Chin Yin
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractOur previous study examined the phytochemical composition and bio-activities of raw daylily flower (Hemerocallis fulva L.). However, this plant food is usually served via heat process such as cooking in a soup. This study aimed to investigate the phytochemical profile and biofunctions of steamed daylily flower (SDF). The content of total phenolic acids, total flavonoids, total carotenoids, total anthocyanins and total triterpenoids in SDF aqueous extract was assessed. Normal cardiac and hepatic cells, H9c2 and L-02 cells, were used to evaluate the protective effects of SDF against ethanol. SDF concentrations of 0.25%, 0.5%, and 1% were applied to treat H9c2 or L-02 cells for 48 h at 37 °C initially, followed by exposure to ethanol at 150 mM for 24 h at 37 °C. Results showed that the content of assessed phytochemicals was in the range of 1019–2045 mg/100 g dry weight. Flavonoids and triterpenoids were two major detected phytochemicals in SDF. SDF treatments at 0.5% and 1% increased the viability of H9c2 cells, but at three concentrations enhanced the survival of L-02 cells. SDF at 0.5% and 1% up-regulated Bcl-2 messenger RNA (mRNA) expression and down-regulated Bax mRNA expression. Ethanol increased reactive oxygen species production, decreased glutathione content, as well as lowered glutathione peroxidase and catalase activities. SDF treatments reversed these changes. SDF at 0.5% and 1% reduced the activity of cytochrome P450 2E1 and nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate oxidase, limited p47phox mRNA expression, as well as enhanced factor E2-related factor 2 and heme oxygenase-1 mRNA expression. SDF at three concentrations decreased gp91phox mRNA expression. In conclusion, these novel findings indicated that SDF aqueous extract was rich in phytochemicals and provided anti-apoptotic and anti-oxidative actions to protect cardiac and hepatic cells against ethanol. Thus, SDF might be considered as a functional food with multiple bio-activities.Keywords: Apoptosis, Ethanol, Oxidative stress, Phytochemical profile, Steamed daylily flower

1. Introduction

Alcohol abuse is a global public health concern because it is responsible for the high prevalence of alcoholic cardiac and hepatic diseases [1,2]. It is reported that heavy alcohol consumption caused disorders in organs through impairing mitochondrial functions [3]. Activation of mitochondrial apoptotic pathway including the increased expression of pro-apoptotic protein, Bax, and diminished expression of anti-apoptotic protein, Bcl-2, contributes to cell apoptosis [4]. Cardiac or hepatic cell death due to the plasma membrane disturbance or disruption leads to the loss of mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) and the release of lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), creatine phosphokinase (CPK), alanine transaminase (ALT) and aspartate transaminase (AST) [5,6].

Ethanol and its metabolite, acetaldehyde, are toxic substances toward heart and liver because these compounds stimulate cytochrome P450 2E1 (CYP2E1), activate nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) oxidase system and impair anti-oxidative defensive elements such as factor E2-related factor 2 (Nrf2) and heme oxygenase (HO) [7–9]. These events increase oxidative stress in heart and liver, which in turn promote the massive production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and 8-hydroxydeoxyguanosine (8-OHdG), and consequently facilitate mitochondrial apoptotic progression. Activated NADPH oxidase is a major source of ROS in many mammalian cells including cardiac and hepatic cells [10]. NADPH oxidase derived ROS played an important role in the deterioration of alcoholic liver disorders [11]. NADPH oxidase consists of many subunits, in which gp91phox is a crucial membrane-associated catalytic subunit and p47phox is a key cytosolic subunit [12]. Chronic alcohol intake up-regulated cardiac and hepatic expression of p47phox [13,14]. Thus, any compound(s) with the effects to limit mitochondrial apoptotic pathway, maintain biomembrane integrity and attenuate oxidative stress might be able to protect cardiac and hepatic cells against ethanol.

Daylily flower (Hemerocallis fulva L.) is consumed as a vegetable in many Asia countries. It is reported that ethanol extract of this plant food could scavenge free radicals in HepG2 cells [15], and attenuate neurotoxicity in corticosterone or glutamate treated PC12 cells [16]. Our previous study indicated that aqueous extract prepared from raw daylily flower contained phenolic acids and flavonoids, and protected human umbilical vein endothelial (HUVE) cells against high glucose induced apoptotic, oxidative and inflammatory injury through mediating the messenger RNA (mRNA) expression of several crucial factors [17]. Those previous studies suggested that this plant food possessed multiple bio-activities, and might benefit chronic diseases prevention. However, daylily flower is usually served via cooking in a soup or stir-frying in a meal, especially in Taiwan. It is unknown whether heat treatment alters the phytochemical composition and bio-activities of this plant food. Thus, the purpose of this study was to assess the phytochemical profile of aqueous extract prepared from steamed daylily flower (SDF).

H9c2 cells are normal rat cardiac cells and L-02 cells are normal human hepatic cells. Both cell lines are often used as cell models for medical researches including pathological progression and drug development [18,19]. The other purpose of our present study was to evaluate the protective effects of SDF aqueous extract for H9c2 and L-02 cells against ethanol. The impact of this aqueous extract at various concentrations upon cell survival, apoptotic and oxidative biomarkers were examined. The results of this study might enhance the medical and nutritional applications of SDF to prevent or protect alcoholic cardiac and hepatic disorders.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. SDF preparation

Fresh daylily flowers were purchased from farms in Hwa-Lian County, Taiwan, in 2018 summer. Daylily flowers were evenly put on a steamer basket. Then, this basket was placed over 2 L boiling water for 25 min. After cooled down at room temperature, 100 g sample was chopped and mixed with 300 mL distilled water. After homogenized by a Waring blender for 3 min at high speed, samples were stilled for 12 h at 25 °C. Aqueous extract of SDF was collected by filtrating the homogenate through a filter paper. SDF was dried to powder via a freeze-drying process. Fresh daylily flower and SDF are presented in Fig. 1.

Fresh daylily flower (left) and steamed daylily flower (right).

2.2. Phytochemical profile measurements

Total phenolic acids content was assessed via a Folin-Ciocalteu method and absorbance at 755 nm was read [20]. Data were expressed as mg gallic acid equivalent/100 g dry weight (DW). Total flavonoids content was quantified by a colorimetrical method and absorbance at 420 nm was determined [21]. Data were presented as mg quercetin equivalent/ 100 g DW. Total carotenoids content was evaluated via a spectrophotometrical method and absorbance at 461 and 664 nm was monitored [22]. Data were shown as mg lutein equivalent/100 g DW. Total anthocyanins content was analyzed via a pH differential method and absorbance at 510 and 700 nm was measured [23]. Data were reported as mg cyanidin-3-glucoside equivalent/100 g DW. Total triterpenoids content was assayed via a vanillin/glacial acetic acid method and absorbance was read at 548 nm [24]. Data were expressed as mg ursolic acid equivalent/100 g DW. The total content of these five groups of phytochemicals was applied to standardize the used SDF samples for cell lines experiments.

2.3. Cell culture

H9c2 cell line and L-02 cell line were purchased from American Type Culture Collection (Manassas, VA, USA). Both cells were cultured in Roswell Park Memorial Institute (RPMI)-1640 medium (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) under an air/CO2 (95:5) condition at 37 °C. This medium contained 5.5 mM glucose, 10% fetal calf serum, 1.5 g/L NaHCO3, 100 U/mL streptomycin and 100 U/mL penicillin. RPMI medium was routinely refreshed every 12 h. The chemicals used for cell culture were purchased from Sigma–Aldrich Chem. Co. (St. Louis, MO, USA). Cells at 105/mL, about 75–80% confluence, were plated onto culture dishes. Both H9c2 and L-02 cells at the 20th passage were used for experiments. The effects of SDF at 0.25–20% and ethanol at 25–300 mM upon the viability of H9c2 and L-02 cells were examined, and data are presented in Fig. 2. When SDF concentrations were >2%, cell loss was observed. Thus, SDF at 0.25, 0.5 and 1% was used for present study. SDF was added into medium to treat H9c2 or L-02 cells first and followed by incubating for 48 h at 37 °C. Then, ethanol (Etoh) at 150 mM was added and samples were further incubated for 24 h at 37 °C. Ethanol at 150 mM was used because it caused 50–55% survival in both cell lines (Fig. 2). Untreated groups were cells without SDF and ethanol. SDF groups were cells treated with 1% SDF and without ethanol. Control groups were cells treated with 150 mM ethanol only.

Effects of SDF (a) and ethanol (b) at various concentrations upon cell viability. H9c2 cells or L-02 cells were treated with SDF at 0, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10 and 20% for 48 h at 37 °C, or treated with ethanol (EtOH) at 0, 25, 50, 75, 100, 150, 200 and 300 mM for 24 h at 37 °C. Data were expressed as mean ± SD (n = 9).

2.4. Cell survival and damage evaluation

3-(4,5-Dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide (MTT, Sigma–Aldrich Chem. Co., St. Louis, MO, USA) at 0.25 mg/mL was mixed with cells at 105/mL, and followed by incubating for 3 h at 37 °C. The formazan product was quantified by recording the change in the absorbance at 570 nm via a microplate reader (Model 550, Bio-Rad Co., Hercules, CA, USA). The supernatants of H9c2 cells and L-02 cells were collected after centrifugation. The activity of LDH, CPK, ALT and AST in supernatants was measured by using assay kits (Randox Lab. Ltd., Crumlin, UK).

2.5. Measurement of mitochondrial injury

Cell sample was centrifugated at 5000×g for 10 min at 4 °C to obtain pellet. Then, cell pellet was fractionated. Mitochondrial fraction was obtained and collected via a mitochondrial isolation kit (Pierce, Rockford, IL, USA). Na+-K+ ATPase activity was determined to indicate mitochondrial damage [25]. In brief, mitochondria were put in a Tris–HCl buffer (30 mM, pH 7.4), and ATP was added to initiate the reaction. After incubation at 37 °C for 15 min, this reaction was terminated by adding 30 μL trichloroacetic acid (20%). The absorbance at 640 nm was read by a fluorophotometer (Molecular Devices, Sunnyvale, CA, USA). Mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) was assessed by analyzing mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) value. Cell suspension, 100 μL, was mixed with 200 μL Rhodamine 123 (Wako Pure Chem. Indus. Ltd., Tokyo, Japan). This mixture was incubated at 37 °C for 30 min. After washed twice with cold phosphate buffered saline, the MFI value was determined by a flow cytometry (Model FC500, Beckman Coulter, Fullerton, CA, USA).

2.6. Measurement of oxidative factors

ROS level was analyzed by an oxidation sensitive dye, Dichloro-dihydro-fluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA, Sigma–Aldrich Chem. Co., St. Louis, MO, USA). Cell suspension was mixed with 10 μmol/L DCFH-DA and incubated at 37 °C for 60 min. Fluorescence value was recorded via a fluorescence microplate reader (Molecular Devices, Sunnyvale, CA, USA). The excitation and emission wavelengths were respectively set at 485 and 530 nm. Protein concentration of the collected supernatants was measured with a BCA protein assay kit (Pierce, Rockford, IL, USA). Relative fluorescence unit (RFU)/mg protein was reported. 8-OHdG is a marker of DNA oxidative damage. Cell DNA was extracted and purified by using a DNA Extract WB kit (Wako Pure Chem. Indus. Ltd., Tokyo, Japan). The concentration of 8-OHdG was quantified via an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) method and the absorbance at 450 nm was read. 8-OHdG standards used for this assay were in the range of 0–20 ng/mL. Glutathione (GSH) level was analyzed by a colorimetric kit (OxisResearch Co., Portland, OR, USA). The activity of catalase and glutathione peroxidase (GPX) was measured by ELISA kits (EMD Biosciences Co., San Diego, CA, USA).

2.7. Determination of CYP2E1 and NADPH oxidase activities

CYP2E1 activity was evaluated by detecting the generation rate of p-nitrocatechol from p-nitrophenol (PNP) [26]. Cell homogenate at 200 μL was mixed with 100 mM potassium-phosphate buffer and 0.2 mM PNP. Reaction was initiated by adding 1 mM NADPH and followed by incubation at 37 °C for 1 h. This reaction was stopped by adding 30 μL 20% trichloroacetic acid. Cell supernatant was used to react with sodium hydroxide and the absorbance at 546 nm was read. NADPH oxidase activity was assayed by monitoring the variation of lucigenin-derived chemiluminescence via using a luminometer (Tunner 20/20, Sunnyvale, CA, USA). In brief, cells were seeded onto 96-well culture plates at 105 cells/well, and followed by overnight culture. Cells were lysed with Hank's balanced salt solution (HBSS, Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Tokyo, Japan) consisted of 0.1 N NaOH and 0.1% Triton X-100. Then, cell lysates were further incubated with HBSS containing 200 μM NADPH and 100 μM lucigenin at 37 °C for 30 min in the dark. Chemiluminescence was measured at 5 min intervals for 60 min.

2.8. Analyses of mRNA expression

Real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) was processed to analyze mRNA expression of Bcl-2, Bax, gp91phox, p47phox, Nrf2 and HO-1. Total RNA was extracted from cells by TRIzol® reagents (Life Technologies, Grand Island, NY, USA). The absorbance at 260 nm was read to measure RNA concentration. Then, RNA at 2 μg was used to synthesize complementary DNA (cDNA) via a commercial synthesis kit (Legene Biosciences, San Diego, CA, USA). cDNA was further used for RT-PCR analyses. PCR amplification was processed by a SYBR Premix Ex Taq™ II kit and an AB7500 real-time PCR system (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA). PCR amplification program was: 40 cycles of 94 °C for 15 s, 60 °C for 15 s and 72 °C for 60 s. The used PCR primers in this study are presented in Table 1. Glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) was used to normalize the target mRNA expression. All PCR primers were purchased from Bio-Rad Co. (Hercules CA, USA).

2.9. Statistical analyses

For phytochemical analyses, samples purchased from 7 different farms were performed (n = 7). For cell line study, nine replicates were performed in each experiment (n = 9). Data were reported as means ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical analyses were processed by using one-way analysis of variance and SAS (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA). The difference among groups was treated by Least Significance Difference Test, in which P value < 0.05 was considered as significant.

3.2. Anti-apoptotic effects of SDF

Ethanol treatment caused cell death in both cell lines (Fig. 3, P < 0.05). SDF treatments concentration-dependently enhanced the survival of L-02 cells, but only at 0.5 and 1% increased the viability of H9c2 cells (P < 0.05). As shown in Fig. 4, ethanol decreased Bcl-2 mRNA expression and increased Bax mRNA expression (P < 0.05). SDF at 0.5 and 1% up-regulated Bcl-2 mRNA expression (P < 0.05). SDF at three test concentrations down-regulated Bax mRNA expression in both cell lines (P < 0.05), in which concentration-dependent manner was found in L-02 cells (P < 0.05).

3.3. Protective effects of SDF for plasma and mitochondrial membranes

Ethanol increased CPK and LDH activities in H9c2 cells, and raised ALT and AST activities in L-02 cells (Table 3, P < 0.05). SDF at 0.5 and 1% reduced CPK activity in H9c2 cells (P < 0.05). SDF concentration-dependently lowered LDH activity in H9c2 cells (P < 0.05). SDF also concentration-dependently decreased ALT and AST activities in L-02 cells (P < 0.05). Ethanol lowered Na+-K+ ATPase activity and MMP in both cell lines (Table 4, P < 0.05). SDF at 0.5 and 1% increased Na+-K+ ATPase activity and MMP in H9c2 cells (P < 0.05). SDF concentration-dependently reversed these changes in L-02 cells (P < 0.05).

3.4. Anti-oxidative effects of SDF

Ethanol increased ROS and 8-OHdG levels, decreased GSH content, as well as diminished GPX and catalase activities in both cell lines (Table 5, P < 0.05). Besides 8-OHdG level in H9c2 cells, SDF at three test concentrations reversed these changes in both cell lines (P < 0.05). As shown in Fig. 5, ethanol increased CYP2E1 and NADPH oxidase activities in both cell lines (P < 0.05). SDF at 0.5 and 1% reduced CYP2E1 and NADPH oxidase activities in H9c2 cells, but concentration-dependently decreased CYP2E1 and NADPH oxidase activities in L-02 cells (P < 0.05). Ethanol enhanced mRNA expression of p47phox and gp91phox in both cell lines (Fig. 6, P < 0.05). SDF at 0.5 and 1% suppressed p47phox mRNA expression in H9c2 cells, and concentration-dependently lowered p47phox mRNA expression in L-02 cells (P < 0.05). SDF at three test concentrations down-regulated gp91phox mRNA expression in both cell lines, and concentration-dependent manner was found in H9c2 cells (P < 0.05). Ethanol decreased Nrf2 and HO-1 mRNA expression in both cell lines (Fig. 7, P < 0.05). SDF at 0.5 and 1% up-regulated Nrf2 and HO-1 mRNA expression in H9c2 cells (P < 0.05). SDF at three test concentrations increased Nrf2 and HO-1 mRNA expression in L-02 cells (P < 0.05).

4. Discussion

As shown in Fig. 1, SDF displayed similar color as fresh daylily flower. Obviously, steam treatment did not affect the appearance of this plant food. Our previous study reported that the content of total phenolic acids and total flavonoids in aqueous extract of fresh daylily flower was 1109 ± 159 and 1306 ± 211 mg/100 g DW, respectively, and that aqueous extract exhibited protective effects for HUVE cells against high glucose induced apoptotic, oxidative and inflammatory stress [17]. In our present work, higher amounts of total phenolic acids (1408 ± 127 mg/100 g DW) and total flavonoids (1830 ± 109 mg/100 g DW) were detected in SDF. Furthermore, other three kinds of phytochemicals, carotenoids, anthocyanins and triterpenoids, were found in SDF, in the range of 1528 ± 54, 1019 ± 82 and 2045 ± 163 mg/100 g DW, respectively. Apparently, SDF contained more phytochemicals than raw daylily flower, and might offer greater nutritional values. It is possible that steaming process (100 °C) disrupted cellular membranes and/or activated certain enzymes responsible for phytochemical conversion, which in turn favored the release and/or production of phytochemicals in SDF. The study of Tadesse et al. [27] revealed that blanching temperatures from raw to 90 °C led to different total phenolic content in dawrach (Raphanus raphanistrum L.), in which the highest amount was detected in samples with 70 °C treatment. Hazra et al. [28] reported that the interaction of temperature, solvent and extraction time affected the available phenolic content in Causonis trifolia (L.) Mabb. & J. Wen, an herb. Thus, temperature and other associated factors used for plant food processing should be carefully managed for optimizing the available phytochemicals. In addition, our data revealed that SDF markedly alleviated apoptotic and oxidative damage induced by ethanol in normal cardiac and hepatic cells. It is highly possible that certain SDF phytochemicals penetrated cells within 48 h incubation, and subsequently performed both anti-apoptotic and anti-oxidative actions against ethanol in SDF treated cells.

Ethanol has toxic effects toward heart and liver [7,29]. In our present study, ethanol decreased mRNA expression of Bcl-2, an anti-apoptotic protein; and stimulated mRNA expression of Bax, a pro-apoptotic protein, in both H9c2 and L-02 cells. Consequently, it was reasonable to observe cell loss. Our data agreed that ethanol impaired cardiac and hepatic cells. However, we found that SDF pre-treatments markedly attenuated ethanol induced apoptosis in both cell lines through reducing Bax mRNA expression and increasing Bcl-2 mRNA expression. Both Bcl-2 and Bax are important regulators involved in mitochondrial intrinsic cell death pathway [30]. Since SDF substantially mediated mRNA expression of these two key proteins, it is likely that certain phytochemicals in SDF entered test cells and restricted mitochondrial death pathway. It is interesting to find that SDF at 0.25% did not increase Bcl-2 mRNA expression, but significantly diminished Bax mRNA expression in both cell lines. Thus, the greater viability in SDF treated cells might be mainly due to SDF's suppression upon pro-apoptotic factors. These novel findings suggest that SDF could protect these cells at molecular level via mediating mRNA expression. Increased activities of CPK, LDH, ALT or AST due to ethanol are biomarkers of destroyed plasma and mitochondrial membranes [31,32]. SDF treatments effectively reduced the activity of those enzymes in both cell lines. These data implied that SDF benefited the integrity and stability of plasma and mitochondrial membranes. In addition, as reported by others [33,34] and observed by our present study, the lower Na+-K+ ATPase activity and MMP caused by ethanol indicated that biomembranes of H9c2 and L-02 cells were disrupted and sub-organelles were leaked. However, SDF treatments substantially raised both Na+-K+ ATPase activity and MMP in these two cell lines. These results once again suggested that some phytochemicals of SDF could penetrate to those cells, which contributed to maintain the integrity and stability of cell biomembranes.

Ethanol, acetaldehyde and other metabolites increased CYP2E1 activity, which in turn aggravated oxidative stress due to GSH depletion and mass production of oxidants such as ROS and 8-OHdG [35,36]. In our present study, SDF treatments markedly reduced CYP2E1 activity in ethanol treated cells, which led to decrease ROS and 8-OHdG production. Consequently, it was reasonable to observe the diminished oxidative stress in SDF treated cells. Furthermore, we found that ethanol raised NADPH oxidase activity and up-regulated mRNA expression of p47phox and gp91phox, two subunits of NADPH oxidase, in H9c2 and L-02 cells. Apparently, NADPH oxidase derived ROS generation was involved in ethanol induced oxidative stress in both cell lines. p47phox is a regulatory subunit located in cytosol and gp91phox is a constitutive subunit located on cell membrane [37]. The over-expression of both subunits due to the stimulus like ethanol promoted excessive production of ROS [38]. Our data revealed that SDF declined NADPH oxidase activity and suppressed mRNA expression of these two subunits in ethanol treated cells, which indicated that SDF was able to improve ethanol induced oxidative stress through mitigating NADPH oxidase system. Thus, the observed lower ROS and 8-OHdG levels in SDF treated cells could be partially ascribed to SDF mediating NADPH oxidase system. It seems likely that certain phytochemicals of SDF spared GSH, inhibited CYP2E1 activity and regulated NADPH oxidase system in ethanol treated cells.

Nrf2, a transcription factor, is involved in modulating the expression of antioxidant genes through the actions of antioxidant-response elements (AREs) [39]. The activation of Nrf2/ARE signaling pathway could limit oxidative reactions via trigging the expression of enzymatic antioxidants such as GPX and catalase [40]. HO-1, a target gene of Nrf2, is a critical ARE [41]. Ethanol and acetaldehyde impair antioxidant defensive capability via suppressing mRNA expression of Nrf2 and HO-1, which consequently decreased the activity of antioxidant enzymes [42]. We found that SDF benefited mRNA expression of Nrf2 and HO-1 in ethanol treated H9c2 and L-02 cells, which in turn enhanced the activity of downstream antioxidant enzymes. Thus, the observed greater GPX and catalase activities could be partially ascribed to the already increased mRNA expression of Nrf2 and HO-1. These findings implied that SDF could attenuate ethanol induced oxidative injury through mediating Nrf2/ARE/HO-1 pathway. It is also possible that certain phytochemicals of SDF entered cells and participated this mediation of Nrf2/ARE/HO-1 pathway.

SDF treatment at 0.25% failed to affect several examined factors in H9c2 cells, but exhibited protective actions for L-02 cells. Apparently, H9c2 cells responded to SDF less efficiently than L-02 cells. The regulatory elements involved in the cellular response to SDF may be different in these two kinds of cells, and this issue needs further investigation. Daylily flower is an edible plant food. Thus, SDF aqueous extract should be considered as a safe mixture of phytochemicals because the source is natural and chemical is not used for preparation. Our data revealed that SDF consisted of five kinds of phytochemicals, at least. Therefore, the observed protective effects from SDF could be ascribed to the actions of certain phytochemicals, or the interactions among phytochemicals. Since SDF could provide multiple bio-activities to protect various cells against ethanol, SDF could be considered as a functional supplement to prevent chronic diseases related to alcoholic abuse.

5. Conclusion

Aqueous extract prepared from SDF was rich in phytochemicals including phenolic acids, flavonoids, carotenoids, anthocyanins and triterpenoids, in the range of 1019–2045 mg/100 g dry weight. Furthermore, this aqueous extract protected H9c2 and L-02 cells, normal cardiac and hepatic cells, against ethanol induced apoptotic and oxidative injury. SDF regulated mRNA expression of some crucial factors such as Bax, NADPH oxidase and Nrf2. SDF mediated the activity of associated enzymes such as LDH, ALT and GPX. SDF stabilized the key molecules of mitochondrial biomembranes such as Na+-K+ ATPase activity and MMP. Consequently, SDF treatments counteracted the adverse impact of ethanol and increased the viability of cardiac and hepatic cells. These novel findings suggest that steamed daylily flower has more nutritional values than raw daylily flower. Thus, the aqueous extract of SDF might be a potent functional dietary supplement to prevent or improve alcoholic diseases.

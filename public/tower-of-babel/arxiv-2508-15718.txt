On hollowness in multiplicative lattices
Amartya Goswami, Joseph Zelezniak

Abstract
. The aim of this article is to extend the notions of strongly hollow and completely strongly hollow ideals of commutative rings to multiplicative lattices. We investigate their basic structural properties and prove several characterizations in terms of localizations at maximal elements and the behaviour of residuals. In particular, we study properties of strongly hollow elements in various types of C C -lattices: Gelfand, semi-simple, and Prüfer. We also provide characterizations of quasi-local weak r r -lattices by completely strongly hollow elements. Furthermore, we give characterization of strongly hollow elements in Noether lattices, and obtain explicit descriptions in Prüfer and r r -lattices. Using strongly hollow and completely strongly hollow elements, we obtain representabilities of multiplicative lattices.

Introduction

A

(Q,\leqslant,0,1)
endowed with an associative,
commutative multiplication (denoted by

7

, and

is said to be

if every element of

. A multiplicative lattice

is called a

is multiplicatively closed, contains

-lattice

We consider the standard Zariski topology on

As a generalization of strongly hollow ideals, in

Let us now summarize the main results in this paper. After introducing the notions of strongly hollow and completely strongly hollow elements in Section

We provide a characterization of completely strongly hollow elements in terms of their associated completely irreducible elements (Theorems

In Section

In Section

Finally, Section

2.
Basic Properties

Definition 2.1
.

An element
a\in Q
is called
strongly hollow
if for
j
,
k\in Q
and
a\leqslant j\vee k
implies that
a\leqslant j
or
a\leqslant k
. An element
a\in Q
is called
completely strongly hollow
if for an arbitrary indexed family
\{i_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subseteq Q
and
a\leqslant\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Lambda}i_{\lambda}
implies that
a\leqslant i_{\lambda^{\prime}}
, for some

\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda
.

Remark 2.1
.

It is worth mentioning that strongly hollow elements are studied in other works, such as
[
3
]
, where they are called ‘join-prime’ elements of a lattice.

Recall that in the category
\mathds{ML}\text{at}
of multiplicative lattices, an adjunction
(f,u):X\rightarrow Y
is a pair of order preserving maps:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u}

with
f(x)\leqslant y\Leftrightarrow x\leqslant u(y)
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(f,u):X\rightarrow Y
be a morphism in
\mathds{ML}\text{at}
and
u
preserves binary joins (e.g. is a left adjoint itself ). If
x
is a strongly hollow element in
X
, then
f(x)
is strongly hollow in
Y
.

Proof.

Suppose that
x
is strongly hollow in
X
and
f(x)\leqslant r\vee s
. Then we have that
x\leqslant u(r\vee s)=u(r)\vee u(s)
. Therefore,
x\leqslant u(r)
or
x\leqslant u(s)
giving
f(x)\leqslant r
or
f(x)\leqslant s
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

If
u
is completely join-preserving, that is, it is a left adjoint, then the same argument above shows that
f
preserves completely strongly hollow elements.

In the case of finitely generated elements, the two types of hollowness coincide.

Proposition 2.4
.

A finitely generated element
a\in Q
is completely strongly hollow if and only if it is strongly hollow.

Proof.

If
a
is completely strongly hollow, then by definition, it is strongly hollow when restricted to finite joins.
For the converse, suppose
a
is a finitely generated element of
Q
and
a
is strongly hollow. Then
a
is compact (see
[
6
, Proposition 2.1]
). Assume that
a\leqslant\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Omega}i_{\lambda}
, then there exists a finite subset
S\subset\Omega
such that
a\leqslant\bigvee_{\lambda\in S}i_{\lambda}
. Since
a
is strongly hollow, there exists
\lambda\in S
such that
m\leqslant i_{\lambda}
. Thus,
a
is completely strongly hollow.
∎

A particularly important characteristic of completely strongly hollow elements is that if a multiplicative lattice
Q
is generated by a set of elements
S\subset Q
, then any completely hollow element
a\in Q
is a member of the generating set, that is,
a\in S
. This means that in any multiplicative lattice which is generated by principal (weak principal), compact, or prime elements, any completely strongly hollow element has the property in question. Moreover, if the generating set is finite, the same statement holds for strongly hollow elements. Consequently, in any multiplicative lattice generated by principal, compact, or prime elements, any subsequent result that assumes a completely strongly hollow element is a member of the generating set holds for all completely strongly hollow elements.

Proposition 2.5
.

If an element
0\neq a\in Q
is completely strongly hollow, then the set
\{j\mid j<a\}
has exactly one maximal element.

Proof.

Let
A:=\{j\mid j<a\}
. Since
0<a
, we have
A\neq\emptyset
. Now
\bigvee A\leqslant a
, and suppose for contradiction that
\bigvee A=a
. Then
a\leqslant\bigvee A
, which implies
a\leqslant j
, for some
j\in A
, a contradiction. Thus,
\bigvee A
is the unique maximal element.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

If
i
,
j
are strongly hollow elements in
Q
, then
i\vee j
is strongly hollow if and only if either
i\leqslant j
or
j\leqslant i
.

Proof.

If either
i\leqslant j
or
j\leqslant i
, the result is trivial.
Suppose
i\vee j
is strongly hollow, and apply the definition to
i\vee j\leqslant i\vee j
, which gives
i\vee j\leqslant i
or
i\vee j\leqslant j
. Then
i\vee j=i
or
i\vee j=j
, so
i\leqslant j
or
j\leqslant i
.
∎

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
a(\neq 0)
be a strongly hollow element of
Q
. Then the following hold:

(1)

If
a=r\vee s
, then either
r\leqslant s
or
s\leqslant r
.

(2)

For
r\in Q
, either
a\leqslant r
or
a\leqslant s^{+}
, where
s^{+}:=\bigwedge\{s\mid s\vee r\geqslant a\}
.

Proof.

(1) Suppose
a
is strongly hollow and
a=r\vee s
. Then
a\leqslant r
or
a\leqslant s
. Thus, if
a\leqslant r
, then
a=r\vee s\leqslant r
implies that
s\leqslant r
, and the other implication follows in the same way when supposing
a\leqslant s
.

(2) For ease of notation take
S:=\{s\mid s\vee r\geq a\}
. We have that
a\leqslant r\vee s
, for all
s\in S
. If
a\leqslant r
, then we are done; otherwise
a\leqslant s
for all
s\in S
, and thus
a\leqslant\bigwedge S=s^{+}
.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
0\neq a\in Q
. If
a
is strongly hollow, then either
a\leqslant J(Q)
or there exists a unique maximal element of
Q
which is not greater than
a
.

Proof.

Suppose
a\nleq J(Q)
, then there exists a maximal element
\mathfrak{m}\ngeq a
. Now if
\mathfrak{n}
is another maximal element, then
a\leqslant\mathfrak{m}\vee\mathfrak{n}=1
. Hence
a\leqslant\mathfrak{n}
.
∎

Proposition 2.9
.

If
Q
has a strongly hollow element which is also a cancellation element, then
Q
has at most one maximal element.

Proof.

Let
a\in Q
be strongly hollow as well as a cancellation element. Suppose there exist two maximal elements
\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathfrak{n}
. Then
a\leqslant\mathfrak{m}\vee\mathfrak{n}=1
, and thus,

a\cdot\mathfrak{m}\vee a\cdot\mathfrak{n}=a\cdot(\mathfrak{m}\vee\mathfrak{n})=a.

Without loss of generality, suppose by the strongly hollow property of
a
that
a\leqslant a\cdot\mathfrak{m}
. Then, since
a\cdot\mathfrak{m}\leqslant a\wedge\mathfrak{m}\leqslant a
, we have that
a=a\cdot\mathfrak{m}
. Thus, since
a
is a cancellation element,
\mathfrak{m}=1
, a contradiction. Hence,
Q
has at most one maximal element.
∎

The next two results are on the behaviour of hollow elements in quasi-local lattices.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let (Q,m) be a quasi-local lattice. Then m is a strongly hollow element if it is weak principal.

Proof.

Suppose that
m
is weak principal and
m\leqslant r\vee s
. If either
r
or
s
equals
1
, then we are done. Thus, assume that
m=r\vee s
. Then, since
s,r\leqslant m
and since
m
is weak meet principal, there exist
c,d\in Q
with
r=cm
and
s=dm
. Hence,

m=cm\vee dm=m(c\vee d),

and now, since
m
is weak join principal, we have that
1=(c\vee d)\vee(0:m)
. If
(0:m)=1
, then we have that
m=0
, which implies
Q
is a trivial finite lattice and
m
is completely strongly hollow. If
c\vee d=1
, then either
c=1
or
d=1
giving
m=r
or
m=s
, leads to the desired conclusion.
∎

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
1\in Q
be compact. Then
1
is completely strongly hollow if and only if
Q
is a quasi-local lattice.

Proof.

The ‘if’ direction is trivial since
1
is compact and strongly hollow (since every element is below the unique maximal element). Notice that if
1
is strongly hollow and
m,n\in Q
are two distinct maximal elements, then
1\leqslant m\vee n
, implying that
1=m\text{ or }1=n
, which is a contradiction. Therefore,
Q
is quasi-local.
∎

We next aim to show that every completely strongly hollow element is associated with a completely strongly irreducible element. We also show that the property of being strongly hollow is preserved by passage to a quotient lattice and under certain localizations.
Let us first introduce a few new notations.

Definition 2.2
.

For an element
a\in Q
we set
T(a):=\{k\mid a\nleq k\}
and
\kappa(a):=\bigvee T(a)
, and we denote
L_{a}=(\kappa(a):a)
.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
a
be a non-zero compact element of
Q
. Then
a
is strongly hollow (and thus, completely strongly hollow) if and only if there exists the greatest element of
Q
with respect to not being greater than
a
. In the case where
a
is completely strongly hollow, this element is
\kappa(a)
.

Proof.

Suppose
a
is a non-zero strongly hollow compact element of
Q
. Then if
k\in T(a)
, we have
k\leqslant\kappa(a)
. If
a\leqslant\kappa(a)
then, since
a
is completely strongly hollow, we have
a\leqslant k
for some
k\in T(a)
, a contradiction. Thus
a\nleq\kappa(a)
and
\kappa(a)
is the greatest element not greater than
a
.

Now suppose there exists a greatest element
j
with respect to not being greater than
a
. Consider an arbitrary family
\{i_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Omega}\subseteq Q
such that
a\leqslant\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Omega}i_{\lambda}
. Assume, for contradiction, that
a\nleq i_{\lambda}
for all
\lambda
. For fixed
\lambda
, define

U_{\lambda}:=\{k\mid i_{\lambda}\leqslant k\text{ and }a\nleq k\}.

We show
U_{\lambda}
has a maximal element. Let
C\subseteq U_{\lambda}
be a chain. Then
\bigvee C\in U_{\lambda}
because if
a\leqslant\bigvee C
, then by compactness of
a
,
a\leqslant\bigvee F
for some finite
F\subseteq C
. Since
F
is a finite chain,
\bigvee F\in F
, so
a\leqslant k
for some
k\in C
, a contradiction. Hence
\bigvee C\in U_{\lambda}
, so by Zorn’s Lemma,
U_{\lambda}
has a maximal element
u_{\lambda}
.
Now, each
u_{\lambda}
is not greater than
a
and maximal with this property, so
u_{\lambda}=j
. Thus
i_{\lambda}\leqslant j
for all
\lambda
, and hence,
a\leqslant\bigvee i_{\lambda}\leqslant j
, contradicting
j\nleq a
. So, some
i_{\lambda}
satisfies
a\leqslant i_{\lambda}
. Hence,
a
is completely strongly hollow.
∎

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
a
be a non-zero compact element of
Q
. Then
a
is completely strongly hollow if and only if
a\nleq\kappa(a)
.

Proof.

If
a
is strongly hollow, then by Proposition
2.12
,
a\nleq\kappa(a)
.
Conversely, suppose
a\nleq\kappa(a)
and
a\leqslant r\vee s
for some
r,s\in Q
. If
a\nleq r
and
a\nleq s
, then
r,s\leqslant\kappa(a)
, and thus,
a\leqslant r\vee s\leqslant\kappa(a)
, contradiction. Therefore,
a
is strongly hollow, and thus, completely strongly hollow (since
a
is compact).
∎

Corollary 2.14
.

If
a
and
b
are completely strongly hollow elements in
Q
then,

a\leqslant b\Rightarrow\kappa(a)\leqslant\kappa(b).

Proof.

Suppose
a\leqslant b
and
b\leqslant\kappa(a)
, then
a\leqslant b\leqslant\kappa(a)
, which is a contradiction. Then by the definition of
\kappa(b)
and the fact that
b\nleq\kappa(a)
, we have
\kappa(a)\leqslant\kappa(b)
. Now suppose
\kappa(a)\leqslant\kappa(b)
. Notice that if
a\nleq b
, then
b\leqslant\kappa(a)
, and thus,
b\leqslant\kappa(b)
, which is a contradiction, thus
a\leqslant b
.
∎

Our next goal is to study strongly hollow elements in quotient lattices. Let
Q
be a multiplicative lattice and
i\in Q
. According to
[
7
]
, a
quotient lattice
,
Q/i
, is a complete sublattice of Q with a top
1
and a bottom
i
, with elements corresponding to
Q/i:=\{x\in Q\mid x\geqslant i\}=:[i,1]
. It can be endowed with a multiplication (denoted by
\circ
) in order to make it a multiplicative lattice,

a\circ b:=(a\cdot b)\vee i\quad\forall a,b\in Q.

Q/i
is closed under this multiplication, and residuals are equivalent in both
Q
and
Q/i
. Furthermore, any element
b\in Q
has a corresponding element
b\vee i\in Q/i
, we shall often refer to
b\vee i
as
b/i
.
For the rest of this paper, we shall denote by
Q/i
the multiplicative lattice given by
(Q/i,\wedge,\vee,\circ,1,i)
for any element
i\in Q
.

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
a
be a strongly hollow element of
Q
and
i\in Q
. Then
a\vee i
is strongly hollow in
Q/i
.

Proof.

If
a\leqslant i
, then
a/i=a\vee i=i
, which is the bottom element of
Q/i
, and thus, is strongly hollow, and we are done. Therefore, suppose
a\nleq i
and
a\vee i\leqslant r\vee s
for some
r,s\in Q/i
. Then
a\leqslant r\vee s\Rightarrow a\leqslant r\;\text{or}\;a\leqslant s
. Without loss of generality, assume
a\leqslant r
. Since
r\in Q/i
, we have that
r\geqslant i
. Hence
a\vee i\leqslant r
.
∎

Now one can show directly that
a\vee i
is compact in
Q/i
and obtain an equivalence of the above proof for completely strongly hollow elements. We include the proof below to demonstrate the use of
\kappa(a)
.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
a
be a completely strongly hollow element of
Q
and
i\in Q
. Then
a\vee i
is completely strongly hollow in
Q/i
.

Proof.

If
a\leqslant i
, then
a/i=a\vee i=i
, which is the bottom element of
Q/i
, and we are done. Thus, assume that
a\nleq i
. If
a\vee i\leqslant a
, then
a\vee i=a
, and thus
a\vee i
is strongly hollow in the
Q/i
since join and meet are the same in the sublattice as in the normal lattice. Thus, we can assume
a\vee i\nleq a
, and additionally notice that
a\vee i\nleq i
because
a\nleq i
. Thus, suppose, for a contradiction, that

a\vee i\leqslant\kappa_{Q/i}(a\vee i)=\bigvee T_{Q/i}(a\vee i)=\bigvee\{r\mid r\geqslant i\;\&\;a\vee i\nleq r\},

and hence,
a\leqslant\kappa_{Q/i}(a\vee i)
. If
x\in T_{Q/i}(a\vee i)
, then
x\geqslant i
and
x\ngeq a\vee i
, which implies that
x\ngeq a
, hence
x\in T(a)
. Then
T_{Q/i}(a\vee i)\subseteq T(a)
, and thus,
\kappa_{Q/i}(a\vee i)\leqslant\kappa(a)
. This gives that
a\leqslant\kappa(a)
, which is a contradiction. Therefore,
a\vee i\nleq\kappa_{Q/i}(a\vee i)
. Consider
a\vee i\leqslant\bigvee_{\alpha\in\Omega}r_{\alpha}
for
r_{\alpha}\geqslant i
. Since
a
is compact, there is a finite subset
F\subseteq\Omega
such that
a\leqslant\bigvee_{\alpha\in F}r_{\alpha}
,which gives that
a\vee i\leqslant\bigvee_{\alpha\in F}r_{\alpha}
, and so
a\vee i
is compact in
Q/i
. Therefore,
a\vee i
is completely strongly hollow in
Q/i
.
∎

We next study the nature of elements of the form
L_{a}
, whenever

is a strongly hollow element.

Theorem 2.17
.

Let

4.
Decomposition of multiplicative lattices

In this section, we outline how we can decompose both elements and multiplicative lattices through products involving completely strongly hollow and strongly hollow elements. In order to proceed, we will need a few more definitions.
We show how completely strongly hollow and strongly hollow elements occur in the direct product of lattices and give requirements for lattices to have a finite number of maximal and minimal elements based on the existence of hollow elements.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
Q=Q_{1}\times\cdots\times Q_{n}
be a direct product of multiplicative lattices and

. Then a is strongly hollow if and only if there exists

with
a=\langle 0,...,a_{i},...,0\rangle
and
a_{i}
in the i-th index such that

is strongly hollow. In this case, we have
\kappa(a)=\langle 0,...,\kappa_{Q_{i}}(a_{i}),...,0\rangle
and
(\kappa(a):a)=\langle 1,...,(\kappa_{Q_{i}}(a_{i}):a_{i}),...,1\rangle
.

Proof.

Let
a
be strongly hollow in
Q
. Then
a=\langle a_{1},..,a_{n}\rangle
. Let
\bar{a_{i}}
denote the element with
a_{i}
in the
i
-th index and
0
everywhere else. Then, we have

a=\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}\bar{a_{i}}
. Since
a
is strongly hollow, we must have
a\leqslant\bar{a_{i}}
, for some
i\in\{1,...,n\}
and therefore,
a=\langle 0,...,a_{i},...\rangle
. Now, let
x,y\in Q_{i}
and consider
\bar{x},\bar{y}\in Q
. We have
a_{i}\leqslant x\vee y
implies

, and hence,
a_{i}\leqslant x
or
a_{i}\leqslant y
(since
a
is strongly hollow). Therefore,
a_{i}
is strongly hollow in
Q_{i}
.

Conversely, suppose
a_{i}
is strongly hollow in
Q_{i}
and consider
\bar{a_{i}}
. Suppose
\bar{a_{i}}\leqslant x\vee y
, for
x,y\in Q
. Therefore,
x
and
y
are zero in every index but the
i
-th index. Since
a_{i}
is strongly hollow, we have
a\leqslant x_{i}
or
a_{i}\leqslant y_{i}
, and hence,
a\leqslant x
or
a\leqslant y
. Notice that, if

, then

, and for all

. Therefore, we have
\kappa(a)=\langle 1,...,\kappa_{Q_{i}}(a_{i}),...,1\rangle

(\kappa(a):a)=\langle 1,...,(\kappa_{Q_{i}}(a_{i}):a_{i}),...,1\rangle.\qed

The same proof follows for completely strongly hollow elements by just considering domination of
a
by arbitrary joins. We now describe the representation of elements of a lattice in terms of completely strongly hollow elements.

Definition 4.1

An element

is called

and completely strongly hollow representable whenever

is called

, whenever
a_{j}\nleq\bigvee_{j\neq i\in\Omega}a_{i}
for each

.

Any completely strongly hollow element is completely strongly hollow representable, and any completely strongly hollow representable element is semi-pseudo strongly hollow representable.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let

. Then every element of Q is semi-pseudo strongly hollow representable if and only if for every
i\in\{1,...,n\}
, every element of

is semi-pseudo strongly hollow representable.

Proof.

We will only show it is true for

. Let
Q=R\times S
, and suppose that every element of

is semi-pseudo strongly hollow representable. Let

be non-zero. Then
\langle i,0\rangle=\bigvee_{j\in\Omega}\langle x_{j},0\rangle
with

completely strongly hollow, for every

must be completely strongly hollow in

is a semi-pseudo strongly hollow representation of

.

For the converse, assume that every element

and every element

is semi-pseudo strongly hollow representable. Let

and

and

Theorem 4.3

Let
\bigvee_{\omega\in\Omega}a_{\omega}

, and hence,
a_{\omega^{\prime}}\geqslant a_{\omega}
. Since the representation is minimal, we must have
\omega=\omega^{\prime}
and therefore,
a_{\omega}=b_{\lambda}
. The same argument by minimality as above gives the uniqueness.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

We obtain from the uniqueness of the representation that every completely strongly hollow representable element has a unique minimal completely strongly hollow representation.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
Q
be a multiplicative lattice with
1
compact and semi-pseudo strongly hollow representable. Then
Q
is semi-local.

Proof.

Since
1
is compact and semi-pseudo hollow representable, we find
1
is completely strongly hollow representable. Let
1=\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}
be a completely strongly hollow representation. Then, for each
i\in\{1,...,n\}
, we have either
a_{i}\leqslant J(Q)
or there is a unique maximal element
m\ngeq a_{i}
. If
a_{i}\nleq J(Q)
, for all
i\in\{1,...,n\}
, then at most every
a_{i}
has a unique
m_{i}
such that
a_{i}\nleq m_{i}
. Then, if there were an additional
m_{m+1}
, we would have all
a_{i}\leqslant m_{n+1}
giving that
m_{n+1}=1
. Therefore, there are at most
n
maximal elements in
Q
.
∎

We denote by
\mathrm{Soc}(Q)
the
socle
of
Q
, which is the join of all minimal elements of
Q
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
Q
be a multiplicative lattice such that every non-zero element is completely strongly hollow representable by non-zero completely strongly hollow elements. Then every minimal element of Q is completely strongly hollow, and the set of all minimal elements of Q is finite.

Proof.

Let
i
be a minimal element of
Q
and
i=\bigvee_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}
a completely strongly hollow representation of
i
. Since
i
is minimal and the representatives are non-zero, we have
a_{j}=i
, and so
i
is completely strongly hollow. If
\mathrm{Soc}(Q)=0
, then we are done. Otherwise, consider a minimal representation of
\mathrm{Soc}(Q)=\bigvee_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}
. Notice that, since each
i
is completely strongly hollow, they are in one-to-one correspondence with the minimal representation by uniqueness, and therefore, there is a finite number of minimal elements in
Q
.
∎

5.
Completely Strongly Hollow elements in Weak Noether lattices

In this section, we provide sufficient conditions for a lattice to be a weak Noether lattice.
A weak
r
-lattice which satisfies the ascending chain condition is known as a
weak Noether lattice
. A
principal element lattice
is a multiplicative lattice in which every element is a principal element. For basic information and facts about weak
r
-lattices and weak Noether lattices, we refer the reader to
[
5
]
. Notice that in a weak
r
-lattice every completely strongly hollow element is principal and compact.

We first introduce some lemmas that will be used often in the sequel.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
Q
be a weak
r
-lattice. If
(Q,m)
is a quasi-local principal element lattice, then
Q
is a weak Noether chain lattice.

Proof.

By
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
, we have that
Q
is a weak Noether lattice. Since every element is principal, every compact element is principal, and thus
Q
is a prüfer lattice. Therefore,
Q_{m}
is totally ordered. Since
Q
is a quasi-local lattice with unique maximal element
m
, we have
Q_{m}=Q
, and therefore,
Q
is a chain lattice.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
Q
be a weak Noether chain lattice. Then every element of
Q
is completely strongly hollow.

Proof.

Let
a\in Q
and consider
a\leqslant\bigvee_{i\in\Omega}r_{i}
. Since
Q
is a Noether lattice, it satisfies the ascending chain condition, which implies that every chain has a maximal element and thus every maximal element is the maximum element (since
Q
is a chain). Therefore
\bigvee_{i\in\Omega}r_{i}=r_{J}
, for some
j\in\Omega
. Hence
a
is completely strongly hollow.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
Q
be a weak
r
-lattice. Then every element of
Q
is a finite product of completely strongly hollow elements if and only if
Q
is a weak Noether chain lattice.

Proof.

The backwards implication follows from Lemma
5.2
. Conversely, since every element is a finite product of completely strongly hollow elements, we have that
1
is as well, and therefore, since
1
can only be decomposed into a product with itself, we have that
1
is completely strongly hollow. Therefore,
Q
is quasi-local. Since the finite product of principal elements is still principal, we have that
Q
is a principal element lattice. Therefore,
Q
is a weak Noether chain lattice by Lemma
5.1
.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
Q
be a weak
r
-lattice. Then every non-zero element of
Q
is strongly hollow if and only if
Q
is a chain lattice. In particular, every non-zero element of
Q
is completely strongly hollow if and only if
Q
is a weak Noether chain lattice.

Proof.

If
Q
is a chain, then it is clear that every element is strongly hollow. Conversely, suppose every element is strongly hollow and let
a,b\in Q
such that
a\nleq b
. Since
a\vee b\leqslant a\vee b
, we have
a\vee b\leqslant a
or
a\vee b\leqslant b
, and hence
b\leqslant a
and hence
Q
is a chain lattice. The complementary result for completely strongly hollow elements follows clearly from Lemma
5.2
and Proposition
5.3
.
∎

A
\pi
-lattice
is a lattice which is generated by a set of elements which are all the products of prime elements. A
ZPI-lattice
is a lattice in which every element is a product of prime elements. A
UFD lattice
is a principally generated multiplicative lattice domain in which every principal element is a product of principal primes. A
special principal element lattice
is a principal element lattice with a unique maximal element whose powers generate the entire lattice.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
Q
be a weak
r
-lattice. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

Every non-zero prime element is completely strongly hollow.

(2)

Q
is either the finite boolean algebra
B_{4}
or a weak Noether chain lattice which is either a UFD or a special principal element lattice.

(3)

Every non-zero element of
Q
is completely strongly hollow.

(4)

Every non-zero element of
Q
is the finite product of completely strongly hollow elements.

Proof.

We have
(4)\Leftrightarrow(3)\Rightarrow(1)
by Lemma
5.2
and Proposition . Notice that if
Q
is a finite boolean algebra
B_{4}
, every element is trivially completely strongly hollow, and if
Q
is a Noether chain lattice, then by Lemma
5.2
, every element is completely strongly hollow, and hence,
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
. We will now show that
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
. Suppose every non-zero prime element is completely strongly hollow. Then every prime element is a principal element and thus
Q
is a principal element lattice (see
[
12
, p. 586]
). Therefore, by
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
,
Q
is a weak Noether lattice. Now, suppose there exist
3
distinct maximal elements
m,n,l
in
Q
. Then, we have
m\leqslant n\vee l
, which gives
m\leqslant n
or
m\leqslant l
, contradicting maximality. Therefore, there are at most
2
maximal elements in
Q
. Suppose there exist exactly
2
distinct maximal elements
m,n\in Q
. Consider
x\in Q
such that
x<m
and
x\nleq n
. Then
m\leqslant x\vee n
, giving a contradiction. Therefore, by applying the same argument to
n
, we have that for all
x\in Q
,
x<m\Leftrightarrow x<n
. Notice that
m^{2}\nleq n
gives
m^{2}=m
, and the same for
n
. Therefore, by
[
3
, Theorem 2.3]
,
Q
is a finite Boolean algebra. Let
x\notin\{m,n,1\}
, and hence
x<m
and
x<n
. Since
Q
is a Boolean algebra, there exists an element
c\in Q
such that
c\vee x=1
and
c\wedge x=0
. If
c\neq 1
, then
c
is less than
m
or
n
and
c\vee x\neq 1
. Therefore,
c=1
, and as a result,
x=0
. Therefore,
Q=\{0,1,m,n\}=B_{4}
. Now suppose
Q
has a unique maximal element
m
such that
m^{2}\neq m
. Then by Lemma
5.1
, we find
Q
is a weak Noether chain lattice. Since
Q
is a principal element lattice, we have that
Q
is a ZPI-lattice, and hence, a
\pi
-lattice (see
[
12
, Theorem 8]
). Therefore,
Q
is either a special principal element lattice or a domain (see
[
5
, Corollary 2.1]
). If
Q
is a domain, then
Q
is a UFD (see
[
5
, Corollary 2.3]
).
∎

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
Q
be a weak
r
-lattice. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

Every non-zero maximal element of
Q
is completely strongly hollow.

(2)

Q
is either the finite boolean algebra
B_{4}
or
Q
is a quasi-local lattice whose unique maximal element is principal.

Proof.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
: Follows from Theorem
5.5
, Proposition
2.10
, and the fact that principal elements are compact (see
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
). Now, suppose every non-zero maximal element of
Q
is completely strongly hollow. Then, with the intent of obtaining a contradiction, suppose that
Q
is not local. Then every maximal element of
Q
is idempotent by Lemma
3.7
. Therefore,
Q
is a finite Boolean algebra by
[
3
, Theorem 2.3]
, and by the same argument as in Theorem
5.5
,
Q=\{0,1,n,m\}=B_{4}
. Hence, suppose
Q
is quasi-local. Then the unique maximal element is completely strongly hollow, and thus, principal.
∎

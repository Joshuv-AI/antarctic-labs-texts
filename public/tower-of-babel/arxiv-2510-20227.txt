Optimization of Bregman–Variational Learning Dynamics
Jinho Cha, Youngchul Kim, Jungmin Shin, Jaeyoung Cho, Seon Jin Kim, Junyeol Ryu, [6pt] Department of Computer Science, Gwinnett Technical College, Lawrenceville, GA, USA, [4pt] Corresponding author: jinho.cha@gwinnetttech.edu

Abstract
We develop a general optimization-theoretic framework for Bregman–Variational Learning Dynamics (BVLD), a new class of operator-based updates that unify Bayesian inference, mirror descent, and proximal learning under time-varying environments. Each update is formulated as a variational optimization problem combining a smooth convex loss f t f_{t} with a Bregman divergence D ψ D_{\psi} . We prove that the induced operator is averaged, contractive, and exponentially stable in the Bregman geometry. Further, we establish Fejér monotonicity, drift-aware convergence, and continuous-time equivalence via an evolution variational inequality (EVI). Together, these results provide a rigorous analytical foundation for well-posed and stability-guaranteed operator dynamics in nonstationary optimization.



Keywords:
Bregman divergence contraction mapping
variational operator exponential stability
optimization dynamics

1
Introduction

Adaptive decision systems frequently operate in nonstationary environments
where model parameters evolve over time.
Learning such systems often requires iterative updates that reconcile
simulation-based predictions and empirical observations.
While classical Bayesian and proximal point methods
provide convenient heuristic mechanisms for updating beliefs or decisions,
their stability and convergence properties under time-varying conditions
remain poorly understood

[
18
,
62
,
26
,
22
,
15
]
.

From an optimization-theoretic perspective,
the dynamics of learning can be expressed through
operator mappings that integrate a convex loss with a divergence penalty.
This viewpoint connects Bayesian inference,
mirror descent, and proximal algorithms
under a unified fixed-point framework

[
9
,
5
,
23
,
19
]
.
Classical results in convex and variational analysis
have emphasized the role of monotone and nonexpansive operators
in ensuring convergence of iterative regularization methods,
including hierarchical or alternating proximal schemes

[
22
,
15
]
.
However, most existing analyses address
Euclidean or mirror-proximal mappings under static assumptions,
providing asymptotic convergence but offering little insight into
how the operator itself evolves
when both the loss function and the divergence generator vary with time.
This absence of a dynamic operator characterization
has limited the theoretical understanding of adaptive and Bayesian updates
in nonstationary optimization systems

[
36
,
11
,
13
,
64
]
.

Recent work in online and dynamic optimization
has begun to investigate the stability of time-varying operators
through the lens of regret minimization and adaptive regularization

[
40
,
53
,
63
,
51
,
27
,
47
,
58
]
.
These studies extend mirror-descent and proximal-point methods
to nonstationary settings, establishing upper bounds on dynamic regret
and continuity properties under nonstationary losses.
Nevertheless, they typically rely on Euclidean metrics
or gradient-smoothness arguments, which do not capture
the intrinsic geometry induced by general Bregman divergences.
In parallel, research on distributionally robust and inverse optimization
has emphasized resilience to uncertainty and data drift

[
32
,
45
]
.
While these approaches provide valuable sensitivity results,
they interpret learning as a sequence of static equilibria
rather than as an evolving operator process with its own contraction geometry.
Dynamic regularization studies in variational inequalities

[
42
,
20
]

and geometric analyses of learning in information manifolds

[
1
]

have further revealed deep connections between
Bregman geometry, dissipativity, and exponential stability.
Yet, to date, a unified theory that rigorously characterizes
when a family of Bregman-proximal operators
remains contractive and exponentially stable under time variation
has not been established.

This paper aims to bridge this theoretical gap
by developing a unified operator-theoretic framework for adaptive learning,
termed
Bregman–Variational Learning Dynamics (BVLD)
.
The proposed formulation generalizes Bayesian inference,
mirror descent, and proximal point iterations
under a single variational structure,
allowing rigorous analysis of stability, contraction, and exponential convergence.
Specifically, we define a time-varying operator sequence

\{T_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
of the form

T_{t}(p)=\arg\min_{q}\{f_{t}(q)+D_{\psi}(q\|p)\},

where each mapping combines a convex,
L
-smooth loss
f_{t}

with a
\mu
-strongly convex Bregman generator
\psi
.
This operator generalizes the classical Euclidean proximal mapping and mirror-descent dynamics, and also encompasses Bayesian posterior updates as geometric special cases.

Under what conditions does a Bregman-proximal update
behave as a contraction operator and yield
well-posed, exponentially stable learning dynamics?

The main results of this study are summarized as follows:

(i)

Formalization of a time-varying variational operator

T_{t}(p)=\arg\min_{q}\{f_{t}(q)+D_{\psi}(q\|p)\}

that subsumes Bayesian and proximal updates as special cases;

(ii)

Proof of averaged contraction, Fejér monotonicity,
and exponential stability using Bregman geometry and operator theory;

(iii)

Extension to distributionally robust optimization (DRO) and
multi-objective (Pareto) formulations,
establishing well-posedness under convexity and smoothness.

The proposed BVLD framework introduces a

Bregman–variational interpretation of adaptive learning dynamics
.
It unifies Bayesian inference, mirror descent, and proximal mappings
within a single variational optimization structure.
Our analysis provides new sufficient conditions for
operator contraction and exponential convergence,
thereby establishing the first rigorous operator-level foundation for
stability-guaranteed adaptive learning under drift and noise.

2
Geometric and Analytical Preliminaries

We begin by establishing the geometric foundation on which all subsequent
variational operators are defined.

Consider a real Hilbert space
(\mathcal{H},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)

endowed with the norm
\|x\|=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle}
.
A function
\psi:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
is called
Legendre-type

if it satisfies the standard conditions of strict convexity,
essential smoothness, and steepness

[
5
,
61
]
.

A function
\psi:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
is called
Legendre-type
if it is
(i) strictly convex,
(ii) continuously differentiable on
\operatorname{int}(\mathrm{dom}\,\psi)
, and
(iii)
steep
, i.e.,
\|\nabla\psi(x_{k})\|\!\to\!\infty
whenever

x_{k}\!\to\!\partial(\mathrm{dom}\,\psi)
.
Throughout,
\psi
is assumed
\mu
-strongly convex for some
\mu>0
, so that

\psi(y)\geq\psi(x)+\langle\nabla\psi(x),y-x\rangle+\tfrac{\mu}{2}\|y-x\|^{2},\qquad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

(1)

The function
\psi
thus defines a non-Euclidean reference geometry
that induces a mirror map
\nabla\psi:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}^{*}

used in all Bregman–variational updates.

For any
x,y\in\operatorname{int}(\mathrm{dom}\psi)
,
the
Bregman divergence
generated by
\psi
is defined as

D_{\psi}(x\|y)=\psi(x)-\psi(y)-\langle\nabla\psi(y),x-y\rangle,

(2)

which was first introduced by Bregman
[
18
]
.
This divergence generalizes many classical distances:
for
\psi(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}
, one recovers the squared Euclidean distance,
and for
\psi(x)=\sum_{i}x_{i}\log x_{i}
, the Kullback–Leibler divergence.
Although asymmetric, it satisfies
D_{\psi}(x\|y)\geq 0
with equality iff
x=y
,
and it induces a non-Euclidean geometry characterized by the convex potential

\psi

[
61
,
5
]
.

Strong convexity immediately implies the standard quadratic bounds:

D_{\psi}(x\|y)\geq\tfrac{\mu}{2}\|x-y\|^{2},\qquad\|\nabla\psi(x)-\nabla\psi(y)\|\leq\tfrac{1}{\mu}\|x-y\|.

(3)

Hence the mirror map
\nabla\psi
is one-to-one,
(1/\mu)
-Lipschitz,
and
\nabla\psi^{-1}
is
\mu
-strongly monotone.

For each iteration
t\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
f_{t}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
be a convex, twice continuously differentiable

task function
whose gradient is
L
-Lipschitz:

\|\nabla f_{t}(x)-\nabla f_{t}(y)\|\leq L\|x-y\|,\qquad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

(4)

Equivalently,
f_{t}
satisfies the
Descent Lemma

|f_{t}(x)-f_{t}(y)-\langle\nabla f_{t}(y),x-y\rangle|\leq\tfrac{L}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}.

(5)

The constants
(\mu,L)
quantify the curvature contrast between
the reference geometry induced by
\psi
and
the instantaneous landscape of
f_{t}
.
Their ratio
\mu/L
will later govern the contraction factor
of the Bregman–variational operator.

Lemma 2.1

(Co-coercivity (Baillon–Haddad)
[
4
,
5
]
)
.

If
f_{t}
is convex and
L
-smooth on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, then for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
,

\langle\nabla f_{t}(x)-\nabla f_{t}(y),\,x-y\rangle\;\geq\;\tfrac{1}{L}\,\|\nabla f_{t}(x)-\nabla f_{t}(y)\|^{2}.

(6)

Proof.

By convex
L
–smoothness, the Fenchel conjugate
f_{t}^{*}
is
(1/L)
–strongly convex:

f_{t}^{*}(u)\geq f_{t}^{*}(v)+\langle\nabla f_{t}^{*}(v),\,u-v\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2L}\|u-v\|^{2},\quad\forall u,v\in\mathcal{H}.

(7)

Let
u=\nabla f_{t}(x)
,
v=\nabla f_{t}(y)
.
Because
\nabla f_{t}^{*}
and
\nabla f_{t}
are inverses,

\nabla f_{t}^{*}(\nabla f_{t}(x))=x
and
\nabla f_{t}^{*}(\nabla f_{t}(y))=y
.
Applying (
7
) with these substitutions and again with
(u,v)
interchanged,
then summing the two inequalities, cancels the
f_{t}^{*}
terms and gives

\langle x-y,\,\nabla f_{t}(x)-\nabla f_{t}(y)\rangle\geq\tfrac{1}{L}\,\|\nabla f_{t}(x)-\nabla f_{t}(y)\|^{2},

which is exactly (
6
).
∎

Inequality (
6
), originally due to
Baillon and Haddad
[
4
]
and formalized in
monotone operator theory
[
61
,
5
]
,
entails several key geometric and analytical consequences.

Since
\nabla f_{t}
is
(1/L)
–co–coercive, it is monotone and
(1/L)
–strongly monotone in the dual sense:

\langle\nabla f_{t}(x)-\nabla f_{t}(y),\,x-y\rangle\geq 0,\qquad\|\nabla f_{t}(x)-\nabla f_{t}(y)\|\leq L\,\|x-y\|.

(8)

Thus
\nabla f_{t}
is firmly nonexpansive after scaling by
1/\sqrt{L}
.

The Fenchel conjugate
f_{t}^{*}
inherits
(1/L)
–strong convexity from the
L
–smoothness of
f_{t}
:

f_{t}^{*}(v)\geq f_{t}^{*}(u)+\langle\nabla f_{t}^{*}(u),\,v-u\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2L}\|v-u\|^{2}.

(9)

Consequently,
\nabla f_{t}^{*}
is
(1/L)
–Lipschitz and the pair
(f_{t},f_{t}^{*})
satisfies
the standard smooth–strong duality relation.

Combining (
1
) for
\psi
and (
6
) for
f_{t}
yields

D_{\psi}(x\|y)\;\geq\;\tfrac{\mu}{2L^{2}}\,\|\nabla f_{t}(x)-\nabla f_{t}(y)\|^{2}.

(10)

This inequality quantifies the curvature coupling between the reference geometry (through
\mu
)
and the task smoothness (through
L
). It provides the analytical foundation for
the Bregman–variational contraction results in Section
4
.

When
\psi(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}
(so
\mu=1
), the divergence reduces to

D_{\psi}(x\|y)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}
, and Lemma
2.1
becomes
the classical co–coercivity inequality for
L
–smooth convex functions.
In general mirror geometries, (
6
) characterizes the
alignment between the displacement
x-y
and the gradient difference

\nabla f_{t}(x)-\nabla f_{t}(y)
, guaranteeing that the induced
Bregman–proximal flow is contractive with respect to
D_{\psi}
.

The geometric and analytical properties established above serve as the
structural assumptions for the operator construction and
contraction analysis developed in Section
4
.

3
Optimization–Theoretic Foundations

Building upon the geometric preliminaries in Section
2
,
we now establish the optimization–theoretic backbone of the proposed
BVLD).
This section connects the variational update

T_{t}(p)=\argmin_{q\in\Theta}\{f_{t}(q)+D_{\psi}(q\|p)\}

to classical notions in convex optimization, monotone operator theory, and
gradient–flow analysis, providing the analytical core for the contraction
and stability results developed later.

The equivalence between the optimality condition of a convex program
and a monotone inclusion
[
61
,
62
,
5
]

is a cornerstone of convex analysis and operator theory.
For the Bregman–variational map
T_{t}
, this correspondence
yields the following result.

Proposition 3.1

(KKT
\Longleftrightarrow
Fixed Point of
T_{t}
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{H},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a Hilbert space,

f_{t}:\mathcal{H}\!\to\!\mathbb{R}
be convex and differentiable, and

\psi:\mathcal{H}\!\to\!\mathbb{R}
be
\mu
–strongly convex and continuously differentiable.
For a nonempty closed convex set
\Theta\subseteq\mathcal{H}
, define

\Phi_{t}(q;p):=f_{t}(q)+D_{\psi}(q\|p)=f_{t}(q)+\psi(q)-\psi(p)-\langle\nabla\psi(p),q-p\rangle.

(11)

Then
q^{\star}=T_{t}(p):=\argmin_{q\in\Theta}\Phi_{t}(q;p)
if and only if

0\in\nabla f_{t}(q^{\star})+\nabla\psi(q^{\star})-\nabla\psi(p)+N_{\Theta}(q^{\star}),

(12)

and this is equivalent to the variational inequality

\langle\nabla f_{t}(q^{\star})+\nabla\psi(q^{\star})-\nabla\psi(p),\,q-q^{\star}\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall q\in\Theta.

(13)

Moreover, if
\Theta=\mathcal{H}
, any fixed point
p^{\star}=T_{t}(p^{\star})
satisfies

\nabla f_{t}(p^{\star})=0
.

Proof.

By differentiability of
f_{t}
and
\psi
, for each
q\in\operatorname{int}(\mathrm{dom}\,\psi)
,

\displaystyle\partial_{q}\Phi_{t}(q;p)

\displaystyle=\nabla f_{t}(q)+\nabla\psi(q)-\nabla\psi(p)+N_{\Theta}(q).

(14)

Strong convexity of
\psi
ensures
\Phi_{t}(\cdot;p)
is
\mu
–strongly convex,
so minimizers are unique.

(1)
\Longrightarrow
(2):

If
q^{\star}=T_{t}(p)
, by Fermat’s rule,

0\in\partial_{q}\Phi_{t}(q^{\star};p)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:subdiff}}}{{=}}\nabla f_{t}(q^{\star})+\nabla\psi(q^{\star})-\nabla\psi(p)+N_{\Theta}(q^{\star}),

(15)

which is (
12
).

(2)
\Longrightarrow
(3):

From the definition of the normal cone,

v\in N_{\Theta}(q^{\star})\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\langle v,\,q-q^{\star}\rangle\leq 0,\quad\forall q\in\Theta.

(16)

Substituting
v=-\nabla f_{t}(q^{\star})-\nabla\psi(q^{\star})+\nabla\psi(p)
from (
12
)
into (
16
) yields (
13
).

(3)
\Longrightarrow
(1):

Suppose (
13
) holds.
For any
q\in\Theta
and
\tau\in(0,1)
, define
q_{\tau}=q^{\star}+\tau(q-q^{\star})\in\Theta
.
Then

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}(q_{\tau};p)-\Phi_{t}(q^{\star};p)

\displaystyle=f_{t}(q_{\tau})-f_{t}(q^{\star})+\psi(q_{\tau})-\psi(q^{\star})-\langle\nabla\psi(p),q_{\tau}-q^{\star}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\tau\langle\nabla f_{t}(q^{\star})+\nabla\psi(q^{\star})-\nabla\psi(p),\,q-q^{\star}\rangle+\tfrac{\mu}{2}\tau^{2}\|q-q^{\star}\|^{2},

(17)

where the first inequality uses convexity of
f_{t}
and the
\mu
–strong convexity of
\psi
.
Under (
13
), the first inner product is nonnegative, hence

\Phi_{t}(q_{\tau};p)\geq\Phi_{t}(q^{\star};p)
for all
q_{\tau}\in\Theta
.
Letting
\tau\to 1
gives
\Phi_{t}(q;p)\geq\Phi_{t}(q^{\star};p)
, proving

q^{\star}
minimizes
\Phi_{t}(\cdot;p)
and thus
q^{\star}=T_{t}(p)
.

If
\Theta=\mathcal{H}
, then
N_{\Theta}(q)\equiv\{0\}
, and setting
p=q^{\star}
in
(
12
) gives
\nabla f_{t}(p^{\star})=0
whenever
p^{\star}=T_{t}(p^{\star})
.
∎

Define the composite monotone operator

\mathcal{A}_{t}(x):=\nabla f_{t}(x)+N_{\Theta}(x),\qquad\mathcal{B}(x):=\nabla\psi(x).

(18)

Then
\mathcal{A}_{t}
is
L
–Lipschitz monotone and
\mathcal{B}
is

\mu
–strongly monotone.
The BVLD update corresponds to the
resolvent
of
\mathcal{A}_{t}

with respect to
\mathcal{B}
:

T_{t}=(I+\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{A}_{t})^{-1},

(19)

which is
(1/(1+\mu/L))
–averaged and hence firmly nonexpansive
in the
D_{\psi}
–metric.

Throughout this section, we assume that each loss function
f_{t}
is
\mu
–strongly convex
and has
L
–Lipschitz continuous gradients under the Bregman geometry induced by
\psi
.

Let
\kappa:=\frac{\mu}{\mu+L}\in(0,1)
denote the constant contraction factor,
which will repeatedly appear in the averagedness and stability results below.

Theorem 3.2

(Averagedness and Firm Nonexpansiveness)
.

If
f_{t}
is convex and
L
–smooth and
\psi
is
\mu
–strongly convex,
then the Bregman–variational operator

T_{t}(p):=\arg\min_{q\in\Theta}\{f_{t}(q)+D_{\psi}(q\|p)\}

is
\kappa
–averaged in the
D_{\psi}
–metric with

\kappa=\mu/(\mu+L)\in(0,1)
, i.e.,

D_{\psi}(T_{t}(p)\|T_{t}(q))\;\leq\;(1-\kappa)\,D_{\psi}(p\|q),

(20)

and hence
T_{t}
is firmly nonexpansive.

Proof.

Let
\kappa:=\frac{\mu}{\mu+L}\in(0,1)
denote the contraction factor associated with the

L
–Lipschitz monotone operator
\nabla f_{t}
and the
\mu
–strongly monotone operator
\nabla\psi
.

Fix
p,q\in\Theta
and write
p^{+}=T_{t}(p)
,
q^{+}=T_{t}(q)
. By Proposition
3.1

(first-order optimality of the Bregman–variational subproblem), for all
r\in\Theta
,

\langle\nabla f_{t}(p^{+})+\nabla\psi(p^{+})-\nabla\psi(p),\,r-p^{+}\rangle\geq 0,

\langle\nabla f_{t}(q^{+})+\nabla\psi(q^{+})-\nabla\psi(q),\,r-q^{+}\rangle\geq 0.

Choosing
r=q^{+}
in the first and
r=p^{+}
in the second, summing and rearranging give

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\psi(p)-\nabla\psi(q),\,p^{+}-q^{+}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\langle\nabla f_{t}(p^{+})-\nabla f_{t}(q^{+}),\,p^{+}-q^{+}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\nabla\psi(p^{+})-\nabla\psi(q^{+}),\,p^{+}-q^{+}\rangle.

By Lemma
2.1
(Baillon–Haddad),

\langle\nabla f_{t}(p^{+})-\nabla f_{t}(q^{+}),\,p^{+}-q^{+}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{L}\,\|\nabla f_{t}(p^{+})-\nabla f_{t}(q^{+})\|^{2}\geq 0,

and by
\mu
–strong convexity of
\psi
,

\langle\nabla\psi(p^{+})-\nabla\psi(q^{+}),\,p^{+}-q^{+}\rangle\geq 2\mu\,D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|q^{+}).

Combining the two inequalities yields

\langle\nabla\psi(p)-\nabla\psi(q),\,p^{+}-q^{+}\rangle\geq 2\mu\,D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|q^{+}).

Invoking the Bregman three-point identity twice and subtracting gives

\langle\nabla\psi(p)-\nabla\psi(q),\,p^{+}-q^{+}\rangle=D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|q)+D_{\psi}(q^{+}\|p)-D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|p)-D_{\psi}(q^{+}\|q).

Hence the basic Fejér-type inequality

D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|q)+D_{\psi}(q^{+}\|p)\geq D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|p)+D_{\psi}(q^{+}\|q)+2\mu\,D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|q^{+}).

(21)

Now use the three-point identity again to link cross terms:

\displaystyle D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|q)

\displaystyle=D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|p)+D_{\psi}(p\|q)+\langle\nabla\psi(q)-\nabla\psi(p),\,p^{+}-p\rangle,

\displaystyle D_{\psi}(q^{+}\|p)

\displaystyle=D_{\psi}(q^{+}\|q)+D_{\psi}(q\|p)+\langle\nabla\psi(p)-\nabla\psi(q),\,q^{+}-q\rangle.

(22)

Adding and recalling
D_{\psi}(q\|p)+D_{\psi}(p\|q)\geq 0
gives

\displaystyle D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|q)+D_{\psi}(q^{+}\|p)

\displaystyle\leq D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|p)+D_{\psi}(q^{+}\|q)+D_{\psi}(p\|q)

(23)

\displaystyle+\;\langle\nabla\psi(q)-\nabla\psi(p),\,p^{+}-p\rangle+\langle\nabla\psi(p)-\nabla\psi(q),\,q^{+}-q\rangle.

Using the optimality conditions with
r=p
and
r=q
,

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\psi(q)-\nabla\psi(p),\,p^{+}-p\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-\langle\nabla f_{t}(p^{+}),\,p^{+}-p\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\psi(p)-\nabla\psi(q),\,q^{+}-q\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-\langle\nabla f_{t}(q^{+}),\,q^{+}-q\rangle.

(24)

Substituting into (
23
) and completing squares gives

\displaystyle D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|q)+D_{\psi}(q^{+}\|p)

\displaystyle\leq D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|p)+D_{\psi}(q^{+}\|q)+D_{\psi}(p\|q)

\displaystyle-\;\langle\nabla f_{t}(p^{+})-\nabla f_{t}(q^{+}),\,p^{+}-q^{+}\rangle

\displaystyle-\;\langle\nabla f_{t}(p^{+}),\,q^{+}-p\rangle-\;\langle\nabla f_{t}(q^{+}),\,p^{+}-q\rangle.

By convexity of
f_{t}
,

f_{t}(q)-f_{t}(p^{+})\geq\langle\nabla f_{t}(p^{+}),\,q-p^{+}\rangle,\qquad f_{t}(p)-f_{t}(q^{+})\geq\langle\nabla f_{t}(q^{+}),\,p-q^{+}\rangle.

Adding and rearranging, then applying Baillon–Haddad, yields

\displaystyle D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|q)+D_{\psi}(q^{+}\|p)

\displaystyle\leq D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|p)+D_{\psi}(q^{+}\|q)+D_{\psi}(p\|q)

\displaystyle-\;\frac{1}{L}\|\nabla f_{t}(p^{+})-\nabla f_{t}(q^{+})\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\;\big[f_{t}(p)+f_{t}(q)-f_{t}(p^{+})-f_{t}(q^{+})\big].

Combining with (
21
) and cancelling common terms gives

\displaystyle 2\mu\,D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|q^{+})

\displaystyle\leq D_{\psi}(p\|q)-\frac{1}{L}\|\nabla f_{t}(p^{+})-\nabla f_{t}(q^{+})\|^{2}

(25)

\displaystyle-\,[\,f_{t}(p)+f_{t}(q)-f_{t}(p^{+})-f_{t}(q^{+})\,].

Using the descent lemma for an
L
–smooth convex
f_{t}
,

f_{t}(p)+f_{t}(q)-f_{t}(p^{+})-f_{t}(q^{+})\geq-\tfrac{1}{2L}\|\nabla f_{t}(p^{+})-\nabla f_{t}(q^{+})\|^{2},

substitution in (
25
) yields

2\mu\,D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|q^{+})\leq D_{\psi}(p\|q)-\tfrac{1}{2L}\|\nabla f_{t}(p^{+})-\nabla f_{t}(q^{+})\|^{2}\leq D_{\psi}(p\|q).

Therefore,

D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|q^{+})\leq\tfrac{1}{2\mu}\,D_{\psi}(p\|q).

Finally, interpolating the co–coercivity and strong monotonicity bounds gives

\frac{1}{L}\|\nabla f_{t}(p^{+})-\nabla f_{t}(q^{+})\|^{2}\geq\frac{2\mu^{2}}{\mu+L}\,D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|q^{+}),

which implies

2\mu\,D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|q^{+})\leq D_{\psi}(p\|q)-\frac{2\mu^{2}}{\mu+L}\,D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|q^{+}),

and hence

D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|q^{+})\leq\Big(1-\frac{\mu}{\mu+L}\Big)D_{\psi}(p\|q).

Hence the mapping
T_{t}
is
(1-\kappa)
–contractive in the
D_{\psi}
–metric,
with
\kappa=\mu/(\mu+L)
, completing the proof.
∎

Inequality (
20
) implies that the mapping

T_{t}
contracts Bregman distances by the factor
(1-\kappa)
,
consistent with classical results on firmly nonexpansive and
averaged operators
[
62
,
5
]
.
For Euclidean
\psi(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}
, this reduces to the
proximal-point contraction geometry of Rockafellar
[
62
]
..
In general mirror geometries,
\kappa=\mu/(\mu+L)
quantifies the
curvature–dependent trade-off between responsiveness and stability,
a relationship that parallels adaptive regularization analyses in
dynamic online learning
[
40
,
51
,
27
]
.

The
Bregman–Moreau envelope
generalizes the classical
Moreau–Yosida regularization
[
61
]

to non-Euclidean geometries and has been recently extended to
relatively smooth and time-varying settings
[
50
,
48
,
42
]
.
It provides a smoothed surrogate of
f_{t}
whose mirror-space gradient,
derived via generalized Danskin-type differentiation
[
69
,
7
]
,
is given by

\mathcal{E}_{t}(p):=\min_{q\in\Theta}\{f_{t}(q)+D_{\psi}(q\|p)\}.

(27)

\nabla_{\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}(p)=\nabla\psi(p)-\nabla\psi(T_{t}(p)).

(28)

Proposition 3.3

(Smoothness and contraction of the Bregman–Moreau envelope)
.

Suppose
f_{t}
is convex and
L
–smooth, and
\psi
is
\mu
–strongly convex on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
Then the envelope
\mathcal{E}_{t}
is convex and
(L/(\mu+L))
–smooth with respect to the metric induced by
\psi
.
Moreover, its mirror gradient vanishes if and only if
p=T_{t}(p)
, and for all
p,q\in\Theta
,

D_{\psi}(T_{t}(p)\|T_{t}(q))\leq\tfrac{L}{\mu+L}\,D_{\psi}(p\|q).

(29)

Proof.

Let
T_{t}(p)=\arg\min_{q\in\Theta}\{f_{t}(q)+D_{\psi}(q\|p)\}
.
Existence and uniqueness follow from
\mu
–strong convexity of
D_{\psi}(\cdot\|p)
.
The optimality condition is

0\in\nabla f_{t}(T_{t}(p))+\nabla\psi(T_{t}(p))-\nabla\psi(p)+N_{\Theta}(T_{t}(p)).

(30)

Differentiating
\mathcal{E}_{t}(p)
with respect to
p
via Danskin’s theorem gives

\nabla_{\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}(p)=\nabla\psi(p)-\nabla\psi(T_{t}(p))
, proving (
28
).
By the Legendre property of
\psi
,
\nabla_{\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}(p)=0
if and only if
p=T_{t}(p)
.

Let
p,q\in\Theta
, and denote
p^{+}=T_{t}(p)
and
q^{+}=T_{t}(q)
.
From (
30
) applied at both points and the monotonicity of
N_{\Theta}
, we have

\langle\nabla f_{t}(p^{+})-\nabla f_{t}(q^{+})+\nabla\psi(p^{+})-\nabla\psi(q^{+}),\,p^{+}-q^{+}\rangle\leq\langle\nabla\psi(p)-\nabla\psi(q),\,p^{+}-q^{+}\rangle.

(31)

By the Baillon–Haddad (co–coercivity) inequality and strong convexity of
\psi
,

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{L}\|\nabla f_{t}(p^{+})-\nabla f_{t}(q^{+})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\langle\nabla f_{t}(p^{+})-\nabla f_{t}(q^{+}),\,p^{+}-q^{+}\rangle,

\displaystyle 2\mu\,D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|q^{+})

\displaystyle\leq\langle\nabla\psi(p^{+})-\nabla\psi(q^{+}),\,p^{+}-q^{+}\rangle.

(32)

Substituting into (
31
) and using the Bregman three–point identity yields

2\mu D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|q^{+})+\tfrac{1}{L}\|\nabla f_{t}(p^{+})-\nabla f_{t}(q^{+})\|^{2}\leq D_{\psi}(p\|q),

(33)

which implies the contraction bound (
29
).

Finally, since

\nabla_{\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}(p)-\nabla_{\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}(q)=\big[\nabla\psi(p)-\nabla\psi(q)\big]-\big[\nabla\psi(T_{t}(p))-\nabla\psi(T_{t}(q))\big],

combining the strong convexity of
\psi
with (
29
) yields

\mathcal{E}_{t}(p)\leq\mathcal{E}_{t}(q)+\langle\nabla_{\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}(q),\,p-q\rangle+\tfrac{L}{2(\mu+L)}\,\|p-q\|_{\psi}^{2},

so
\mathcal{E}_{t}
is
(L/(\mu+L))
–smooth in the
\psi
–metric.
∎

The Polyak–Łojasiewicz (PL) inequality
[
60
]

and its extensions to nonsmooth and analytic settings
[
49
,
14
,
46
]

form the basis of many modern convergence analyses.
In Bregman geometries, these relationships have recently been revisited
to establish generalized PL and Lyapunov stability conditions

[
3
,
15
,
69
,
48
,
41
]
.

Definition 3.4

(PL and Quadratic Growth (QG))
.

A differentiable function
F
satisfies the PL condition with constant

\lambda>0
if

\tfrac{1}{2}\|\nabla F(x)\|^{2}\geq\lambda\,(F(x)-F^{\star}),\qquad F^{\star}=\inf_{x}F(x).

(34)

It satisfies the QG condition with parameter
\nu>0
if

F(x)-F^{\star}\geq\tfrac{\nu}{2}\|x-x^{\star}\|^{2},\quad x^{\star}\in\argmin F.

(35)

Proposition 3.5

(PL
\Rightarrow
QG and Linear Rate)
.

If
\mathcal{E}_{t}
satisfies PL condition with constant
\lambda>0
,
then it also satisfies the QG condition with constant
\nu=\lambda/\mu
.
Furthermore, the BVLD iteration
p_{t+1}=T_{t}(p_{t})
obeys

D_{\psi}(p_{t+1}\|p^{\star})\leq\Bigl(1-\tfrac{\lambda\mu}{\mu+L}\Bigr)\,D_{\psi}(p_{t}\|p^{\star}),

(36)

which implies linear convergence in the Bregman distance.

Proof.

For all
p\in\Theta
, the PL condition can be written as

\tfrac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}(p)\|^{2}\;\geq\;\lambda\big(\mathcal{E}_{t}(p)-\mathcal{E}_{t}(p^{\star})\big),\qquad\nabla_{\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}(p)=\nabla\psi(p)-\nabla\psi(T_{t}(p)),

(37)

where
p^{\star}
is a fixed point of
T_{t}
, i.e.,
p^{\star}=T_{t}(p^{\star})
.

(a) From PL to QG.

Since
\psi
is
\mu
–strongly convex, we have for all
x,y
:

\langle\nabla\psi(x)-\nabla\psi(y),\,x-y\rangle\;\geq\;2\mu\,D_{\psi}(x\|y).

(38)

Substituting
x=p
and
y=T_{t}(p)
gives

\|\nabla_{\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}(p)\|\,\|p-T_{t}(p)\|\;\geq\;\langle\nabla_{\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}(p),\,p-T_{t}(p)\rangle\;\geq\;2\mu\,D_{\psi}(p\|T_{t}(p)).

Because
D_{\psi}(p\|T_{t}(p))\geq\tfrac{\mu}{2}\|p-T_{t}(p)\|^{2}
, it follows that

\|\nabla_{\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}(p)\|\;\geq\;\mu\,\|p-T_{t}(p)\|.

(39)

Furthermore, since
p^{\star}
is a fixed point of
T_{t}
, the averagedness of
T_{t}
(see Proposition
3.3
) implies a local error bound:

\mathrm{dist}(p,\mathrm{Fix}\,T_{t})\;\leq\;\frac{1}{1-\sqrt{L/(\mu+L)}}\,\|p-T_{t}(p)\|\;\leq\;\frac{\mu+L}{\mu}\,\|p-T_{t}(p)\|.

(40)

Combining (
39
)–(
40
) yields

\|\nabla_{\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}(p)\|\;\geq\;\frac{\mu^{2}}{\mu+L}\,\mathrm{dist}(p,\mathrm{Fix}\,T_{t})\;\geq\;\frac{\mu^{2}}{\mu+L}\,\|p-p^{\star}\|.

Together with the PL inequality (
37
) and the lower equivalence

D_{\psi}(p\|p^{\star})\geq\tfrac{\mu}{2}\|p-p^{\star}\|^{2}
, we obtain

\mathcal{E}_{t}(p)-\mathcal{E}_{t}(p^{\star})\;\geq\;\tfrac{\lambda}{\mu}\,D_{\psi}(p\|p^{\star}),

which establishes the QG condition with
\nu=\lambda/\mu
.

(b) Linear convergence.

From Proposition
3.3
, the operator
T_{t}
is contractive in
D_{\psi}
:

D_{\psi}(T_{t}(p)\|T_{t}(q))\leq\tfrac{L}{\mu+L}\,D_{\psi}(p\|q).

(41)

Setting
q=p^{\star}=T_{t}(p^{\star})
gives

D_{\psi}(p_{t+1}\|p^{\star})\;\leq\;\tfrac{L}{\mu+L}\,D_{\psi}(p_{t}\|p^{\star}).

Combining this contraction with the QG property yields

\mathcal{E}_{t}(p_{t+1})-\mathcal{E}_{t}(p^{\star})\;\leq\;\tfrac{L}{\mu+L}\big(\mathcal{E}_{t}(p_{t})-\mathcal{E}_{t}(p^{\star})\big)\;\leq\;\Bigl(1-\tfrac{\lambda\mu}{\mu+L}\Bigr)\big(\mathcal{E}_{t}(p_{t})-\mathcal{E}_{t}(p^{\star})\big),

which, by the QG–PL equivalence, implies

D_{\psi}(p_{t+1}\|p^{\star})\;\leq\;\Bigl(1-\tfrac{\lambda\mu}{\mu+L}\Bigr)D_{\psi}(p_{t}\|p^{\star}).

Hence, the sequence
\{p_{t}\}
converges linearly to
p^{\star}
in Bregman distance.
∎

Theorem 3.6

(Kurdyka–Łojasiewicz (KL) Rates)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{t}
be the Bregman–Moreau envelope defined in (
27
).
Assume (for a fixed
t
; if
t
varies, assume uniform KL constants in a neighborhood of the limit set) that:

1.

\mathcal{E}_{t}
satisfies the KL property at every critical point
p^{\star}
with desingularizing function
\varphi(s)=c\,s^{1-\theta}
for some
\theta\in[0,1)
and
c>0
;

2.

the BVLD iterates
p_{k+1}=T_{t}(p_{k})
are bounded and satisfy the
sufficient decrease

\mathcal{E}_{t}(p_{k+1})\;\leq\;\mathcal{E}_{t}(p_{k})\;-\;\alpha\,D_{\psi}(p_{k+1}\,\|\,p_{k}),\qquad\alpha>0,

(42)

where
\psi
is
\mu
-strongly convex so that

\nabla_{\!\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}(p)=\nabla\psi(p)-\nabla\psi(T_{t}(p))
and

\|\nabla_{\!\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}(p_{k})\|^{2}\geq 2\mu\,D_{\psi}(p_{k+1}\,\|\,p_{k})
.
Then the following regimes hold:

(i)

Finite termination (
\theta=0
): there exists
K
such that
p_{k}=p^{\star}
for all
k\geq K
.

(ii)

Linear (geometric) convergence (
\theta\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
): there exist
\rho\in(0,1)
and
C,C^{\prime}>0
with

\mathcal{E}_{t}(p_{k})-\mathcal{E}_{t}(p^{\star})\;\leq\;C(1-\rho)^{k},\qquad D_{\psi}(p_{k}\,\|\,p^{\star})\;\leq\;C^{\prime}(1-\rho)^{k}.

(iii)

Sublinear (polynomial) convergence (
\theta\in(\tfrac{1}{2},1)
): there exist
C,C^{\prime}>0
such that

\mathcal{E}_{t}(p_{k})-\mathcal{E}_{t}(p^{\star})\;\leq\;C\,k^{-\frac{1-2\theta}{1-\theta}},\qquad D_{\psi}(p_{k}\,\|\,p^{\star})\;\leq\;C^{\prime}\,k^{-\frac{1-2\theta}{1-\theta}}.

Proof.

Let
F_{k}:=\mathcal{E}_{t}(p_{k})-\mathcal{E}_{t}(p^{\star})\geq 0
.
By the KL property, there exist
\eta>0
and
c>0
such that for all
p
with
0<F:=\mathcal{E}_{t}(p)-\mathcal{E}_{t}(p^{\star})<\eta
,

\varphi^{\prime}(F)\;\|\partial\mathcal{E}_{t}(p)\|\;\geq\;1.

(43)

Since
\mathcal{E}_{t}
is smooth in the mirror metric induced by
\psi
, we may take
\partial\mathcal{E}_{t}=\nabla_{\!\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}
and,
by the envelope gradient formula and strong convexity of
\psi
,

\|\nabla_{\!\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}(p_{k})\|^{2}=\|\nabla\psi(p_{k})-\nabla\psi(T_{t}(p_{k}))\|^{2}\;\geq\;2\mu\,D_{\psi}(p_{k+1}\,\|\,p_{k}).

(44)

From the sufficient decrease (
42
),
\{F_{k}\}
is nonincreasing and

F_{k}-F_{k+1}\;\geq\;\alpha\,D_{\psi}(p_{k+1}\,\|\,p_{k}).

(45)

For all large
k
,
p_{k}
remains in the KL neighborhood, so combining (
43
) at
p=p_{k}

with (
44
) and (
45
) yields

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(F_{k})\big(F_{k}-F_{k+1}\big)

\displaystyle\;\geq\;\alpha\,\varphi^{\prime}(F_{k})\,D_{\psi}(p_{k+1}\,\|\,p_{k})

\displaystyle\;\geq\;\frac{\alpha}{2\mu}\,\|\nabla_{\!\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}(p_{k})\|^{2}\,\varphi^{\prime}(F_{k})\,\frac{1}{\|\nabla_{\!\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}(p_{k})\|^{2}}\;\geq\;\frac{\alpha}{2\mu c^{2}}.

(46)

where the last step uses
\varphi^{\prime}(F_{k})\,\|\nabla_{\!\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}(p_{k})\|\geq 1
(i.e., (
43
)) and the fact that
1/\|\nabla_{\!\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}(p_{k})\|\leq 1
after rescaling
c
if necessary.
Equivalently,

\varphi^{\prime}(F_{k})\,\big(F_{k}-F_{k+1}\big)\;\geq\;\gamma,\qquad\gamma:=\frac{\alpha}{2\mu c^{2}}>0.

(47)

By the choice
\varphi(s)=c\,s^{1-\theta}
, we have

\varphi^{\prime}(s)=c(1-\theta)\,s^{-\theta}
for
s>0
.
Plugging this into (
47
) gives

c(1-\theta)\,F_{k}^{-\theta}\,(F_{k}-F_{k+1})\;\geq\;\gamma.

We now distinguish cases:

(i)
\theta=0
.

Then
\varphi^{\prime}(s)=c
and (
47
) implies
F_{k}-F_{k+1}\geq\gamma/c>0
whenever
F_{k}>0
.
Hence
\{F_{k}\}
decreases by at least a fixed amount until it hits
0
in finitely many steps; finite termination follows.

(ii)
\theta\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
.

Set
G_{k}:=F_{k}^{1-\theta}
(concave power). Since
F_{k}
is nonincreasing, the mean value inequality yields

G_{k}-G_{k+1}\;=\;F_{k}^{1-\theta}-F_{k+1}^{1-\theta}\;\geq\;(1-\theta)\,F_{k}^{-\theta}\,(F_{k}-F_{k+1})\;\geq\;\frac{\gamma}{c}.

Thus
G_{k}
decreases by at least a constant each step; standard arguments imply
G_{k}
decays geometrically once sufficiently close to
0
, yielding

F_{k}\leq C(1-\rho)^{k}
for some
\rho\in(0,1)
.
Strong convexity of
\psi
and the envelope smoothness then give the same geometric rate for
D_{\psi}(p_{k}\,\|\,p^{\star})
.

(iii)
\theta\in(\tfrac{1}{2},1)
.

Rewriting the recursion as

F_{k+1}^{1-\theta}-F_{k}^{1-\theta}\leq-\frac{\gamma}{c}
and summing, one obtains

F_{k}^{1-\theta}\leq F_{k_{0}}^{1-\theta}-\frac{\gamma}{c}(k-k_{0})
for
k\geq k_{0}
,
which implies the polynomial decay

F_{k}=O\!\big(k^{-\frac{1-2\theta}{1-\theta}}\big)
.
Again, strong convexity of
\psi
converts the rate to
D_{\psi}(p_{k}\,\|\,p^{\star})
.

Finally, boundedness of
\{p_{k}\}
(and thus staying within the KL neighborhood for large
k
),
together with (
45
), also yields
\sum_{k}D_{\psi}(p_{k+1}\,\|\,p_{k})<\infty
.
This completes the proof.
∎

provides an explicit conjugate representation of the

.
The optimal dual variable

coincides with the mirror gradient

\nabla_{\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}(p)

Furthermore, the subregularity framework
of

Proposition 3.7

(Fenchel Dual of the BV Subproblem)
.

For the Bregman–Moreau envelope

\mathcal{E}_{t}(p)=\min_{q\in\Theta}\bigl\{\,f_{t}(q)+D_{\psi}(q\|p)\,\bigr\},

the associated Fenchel dual problem is

\max_{y\in\mathcal{H}^{*}}\Bigl\{-\,f_{t}^{*}(-y)-\,\psi^{*}\bigl(\nabla\psi(p)-y\bigr)+\,\psi^{*}\bigl(\nabla\psi(p)\bigr)\Bigr\},

(48)

where
f_{t}^{*}
and
\psi^{*}
denote the Fenchel conjugates of
f_{t}
and
\psi
, respectively.
The optimal dual variable

y^{\star}=\nabla\psi(p)-\nabla\psi(T_{t}(p))

coincides with the mirror gradient

\nabla_{\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}(p)
defined in (
28
).

Proof.

The primal objective in (
27
) can be rewritten as

\min_{q\in\Theta}\bigl\{f_{t}(q)+\psi(q)-\psi(p)-\langle\nabla\psi(p),q-p\rangle\bigr\}.

Introducing the auxiliary variable
y\in\mathcal{H}^{*}
dual to
q
and applying the Fenchel–Rockafellar duality formula,

\min_{q}\{f_{t}(q)+\psi(q)-\langle y,q\rangle\}\;=\;\max_{y}\bigl\{-f_{t}^{*}(-y)-\psi^{*}(\nabla\psi(p)-y)\bigr\},

up to the constant
\psi^{*}(\nabla\psi(p))
.
This yields the dual expression (
48
).
The first-order optimality condition for
y^{\star}
satisfies

\nabla\psi(T_{t}(p))=\nabla\psi(p)-y^{\star}
,
implying

y^{\star}=\nabla\psi(p)-\nabla\psi(T_{t}(p))
.
Hence,
y^{\star}
exactly represents the mirror gradient
\nabla_{\psi}\mathcal{E}_{t}(p)
, completing the proof.
∎

4
Construction of the Bregman–Variational Operator

We now formalize the central operator underlying BVLD and
develop its analytical and computational foundations.
The objective is to construct a single unifying map that
encompasses the Euclidean proximal, mirror–descent, and
Bayesian update mechanisms within a common Bregman–variational framework.

Let
(\mathcal{H},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a real Hilbert space and

\Theta\subseteq\mathcal{H}
a nonempty, closed, and convex feasible set.
Consider a convex and
L
–smooth loss function
f_{t}:\Theta\to\mathbb{R}

and a
\mu
–strongly convex, differentiable Legendre potential

\psi:\Theta\to\mathbb{R}
.
We define the
Bregman–variational operator
at time
t
as

T_{t}(p):=\argmin_{q\in\Theta}\bigl\{\,f_{t}(q)+D_{\psi}(q\|p)\,\bigr\},

(49)

where

D_{\psi}(q\|p)=\psi(q)-\psi(p)-\langle\nabla\psi(p),q-p\rangle

denotes the Bregman divergence generated by
\psi

[
18
,
6
]
.

The operator
T_{t}
generalizes several well-known update rules across different geometries.
In the
Euclidean case
, when
\psi(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}
,
the operator reduces to
T_{t}(p)=(I+\nabla f_{t})^{-1}(p)
,
which corresponds to the classical proximal mapping
[
62
,
59
]
.
In the
entropic or Bayesian case
, where
\psi(x)=\sum_{i}x_{i}\log x_{i}
,
the mapping
T_{t}(p)
coincides with a mirror-descent update or, equivalently,
a Bayesian posterior update under exponential-family geometry
[
9
,
44
,
50
]
.
More generally, for an arbitrary Legendre potential
\psi
,
the operator
T_{t}
acts as the
resolvent
of a monotone operator
in the
\psi
-induced geometry, thereby providing curvature-adaptive
regularization
[
7
,
24
]
.

Hence, the BVLD framework establishes a unified variational operator
that continuously interpolates between proximal, mirror, and
Bayesian dynamics
[
16
,
50
,
42
]
.
This operator forms the foundation for the subsequent analysis of
duality (Proposition
3.7
),
smoothness of the Bregman–Moreau envelope (Proposition
3.3
),
and convergence behavior (Theorem
3.6
).

8
]
.

Therefore,

is the unique stationary (and hence fixed) point of
T_{t}
.

The following fundamental identity, often attributed to
Bregman
[
18
]
and later formalized in
modern convex analysis texts
[
6
,
24
]
,
plays a central role in establishing geometric relationships
between primal and mirror coordinates.

Lemma 4.2

(Bregman Three-Point Identity)
.

For any
x,y,z\in\operatorname{int}(\mathrm{dom}\psi)
,

D_{\psi}(x\|y)+D_{\psi}(y\|z)-D_{\psi}(x\|z)=\langle\nabla\psi(z)-\nabla\psi(y),\,x-y\rangle.

(51)

Proof.

Expanding the definition (
2
) for each term gives

\displaystyle D_{\psi}(x\|y)+D_{\psi}(y\|z)-D_{\psi}(x\|z)

\displaystyle=\big[\psi(x)-\psi(y)-\langle\nabla\psi(y),x-y\rangle\big]+\big[\psi(y)-\psi(z)-\langle\nabla\psi(z),y-z\rangle\big]

\displaystyle-\big[\psi(x)-\psi(z)-\langle\nabla\psi(z),x-z\rangle\big],

and all
\psi(\cdot)
terms cancel, leaving (
51
).
∎

Lemma
4.2
reveals that the Bregman divergence
forms a generalized non-Euclidean triangle,
where the residual term

\langle\nabla\psi(z)-\nabla\psi(y),x-y\rangle

captures the curvature-induced asymmetry of the mirror space.
This asymmetry serves as the analytical link between
convex geometry and the dynamical stability properties
established later for the BVLD operator.

Identity (
51
) constitutes the geometric hinge of all
operator-level analyses that follow.
Its algebraic simplicity conceals a deep analytical role that links geometry,
monotonicity, and stability.

The first implication arises from the coupling of successive iterates.
For the BVLD recursion
p_{t+1}=T_{t}(p_{t})
, substituting

(x,y,z)=(p^{\star},p_{t+1},p_{t})
into (
51
) gives

D_{\psi}(p^{\star}\|p_{t+1})-D_{\psi}(p^{\star}\|p_{t})=\langle\nabla\psi(p_{t})-\nabla\psi(p_{t+1}),\,p^{\star}-p_{t+1}\rangle-D_{\psi}(p_{t+1}\|p_{t}),

which forms the telescoping structure central to Fejér monotonicity proofs.
It allows
D_{\psi}(p_{t+1}\|p^{\star})
to be bounded by

D_{\psi}(p_{t}\|p^{\star})
plus a residual term,
directly leading to the contraction result of
Theorem
3.2
and the Lyapunov inequality
in Section
5
.

The second implication concerns the Lyapunov interpretation.
The Bregman energy
V_{t}=D_{\psi}(p_{t}\|p^{\star})
satisfies

V_{t+1}-V_{t}\leq\langle\nabla\psi(p_{t})-\nabla\psi(p_{t+1}),\,p_{t+1}-p^{\star}\rangle,

so the right-hand side acts as a discrete-time Lyapunov decrement,
providing an analytical bridge between convexity-based optimality
conditions and dynamical stability arguments.

Finally, a continuous-time correspondence emerges by taking

\Delta t\!\to\!0
and dividing (
51
) by
\Delta t
, yielding

\frac{d}{dt}D_{\psi}(p(t)\|p^{\star})=\langle\dot{p}(t),\,\nabla\psi(p(t))-\nabla\psi(p^{\star})\rangle.

This relation underpins the evolution-variational-inequality (EVI)
form in the continuous limit and connects discrete BVLD dynamics
to continuous-time mirror flows.

In summary, Lemma
4.2
and its corollaries provide the
algebraic mechanism that connects local convexity (via
\psi
)
to global stability of the BVLD operator.
Together with Proposition
4.1
,
it furnishes the analytical foundation for
the contraction, Fejér monotonicity, and exponential convergence
results established in Section
5
.

Theorem 5.1

(
\kappa
–Contraction / Averaged Nonexpansiveness)
.

Suppose
f_{t}
is convex and
L
–smooth, and
\psi
is
\mu
–strongly convex.
Then the Bregman–variational operator
T_{t}
defined in (
49
)
is
(1-\kappa)
–averaged with respect to
D_{\psi}
, i.e.,
for all
p,q\in\Theta
,

D_{\psi}(T_{t}(p)\|T_{t}(q))\leq(1-\kappa)\,D_{\psi}(p\|q),\qquad\kappa=\frac{\mu}{\mu+L}\in(0,1).

(52)

Proof.

Let
p^{+}=T_{t}(p)
and
q^{+}=T_{t}(q)
.
By the optimality conditions of (
49
), we have

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f_{t}(p^{+})+\nabla\psi(p^{+})-\nabla\psi(p),\,q^{+}-p^{+}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq 0,

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f_{t}(q^{+})+\nabla\psi(q^{+})-\nabla\psi(q),\,p^{+}-q^{+}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq 0.

(53)

Adding the two inequalities yields

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\psi(p)-\nabla\psi(q),\,p^{+}-q^{+}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\langle\nabla f_{t}(p^{+})-\nabla f_{t}(q^{+}),\,p^{+}-q^{+}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\langle\nabla\psi(p^{+})-\nabla\psi(q^{+}),\,p^{+}-q^{+}\rangle.

(54)

By
L
–smoothness of
f_{t}
, the co–coercivity inequality holds:

\langle\nabla f_{t}(p^{+})-\nabla f_{t}(q^{+}),\,p^{+}-q^{+}\rangle\geq\tfrac{1}{L}\|\nabla f_{t}(p^{+})-\nabla f_{t}(q^{+})\|^{2},

and by
\mu
–strong convexity of
\psi
,

\langle\nabla\psi(p^{+})-\nabla\psi(q^{+}),\,p^{+}-q^{+}\rangle\geq 2\mu\,D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|q^{+}).

Combining these with (
54
) and applying Young’s inequality gives

2\mu\,D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|q^{+})\leq\langle\nabla\psi(p)-\nabla\psi(q),\,p^{+}-q^{+}\rangle-\tfrac{1}{L}\|\nabla f_{t}(p^{+})-\nabla f_{t}(q^{+})\|^{2}.

The right-hand side is bounded above by

D_{\psi}(p\|q)-(f_{t}(p)+f_{t}(q)-f_{t}(p^{+})-f_{t}(q^{+}))
.
By the descent lemma for
L
–smooth convex functions,

f_{t}(p)+f_{t}(q)-f_{t}(p^{+})-f_{t}(q^{+})\geq-\tfrac{1}{2L}\|\nabla f_{t}(p^{+})-\nabla f_{t}(q^{+})\|^{2}.

Substituting and simplifying yields

\frac{1}{L}\|\nabla f_{t}(p^{+})-\nabla f_{t}(q^{+})\|^{2}\geq\frac{2\mu^{2}}{\mu+L}\,D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|q^{+}),

which, together with (
54
), implies

D_{\psi}(p^{+}\|q^{+})\leq\Big(1-\frac{\mu}{\mu+L}\Big)D_{\psi}(p\|q)=(1-\kappa)\,D_{\psi}(p\|q).

Hence,
T_{t}
is
(1-\kappa)
–averaged, completing the proof.
∎

Remark 5.2

(Interpretation of
\kappa
)
.

The parameter
\kappa=\mu/(\mu+L)
measures the
intrinsic adaptation speed of the BVLD operator.
A higher curvature ratio
\mu/L
implies faster convergence,
whereas a smaller
\mu/L
yields slower adaptation and weaker contraction.

Theorem 5.3

(Fejér Monotonicity and Stability)
.

Let
\{p_{t}\}
be the BVLD sequence defined by
p_{t+1}=T_{t}(p_{t})
, and
let
p^{\star}\in\mathrm{Fix}(T_{t})
denote a fixed point.
If the contraction coefficients satisfy
\kappa_{t}\geq\kappa>0
for all
t
, then

D_{\psi}(p_{t+1}\|p^{\star})\leq(1-\kappa)\,D_{\psi}(p_{t}\|p^{\star}),

(55)

and consequently
p_{t}\to p^{\star}
exponentially in
D_{\psi}
.

Here
\varepsilon_{t}
represents the residual term induced by stochastic observation noise,
typically bounded as
\varepsilon_{t}:=\tfrac{1}{2}\eta_{t}^{2}\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}
,
where
\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}
denotes the variance of the perturbation
in gradient evaluation.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.1
, each operator
T_{t}
satisfies

D_{\psi}(T_{t}(p)\|T_{t}(q))\leq(1-\kappa_{t})D_{\psi}(p\|q)
for all
p,q\in\Theta
.
Letting
q=p^{\star}
and noting that
T_{t}(p^{\star})=p^{\star}
, we obtain

D_{\psi}(p_{t+1}\|p^{\star})=D_{\psi}(T_{t}(p_{t})\|T_{t}(p^{\star}))\leq(1-\kappa_{t})\,D_{\psi}(p_{t}\|p^{\star}).

Since
\kappa_{t}\geq\kappa>0
, applying the inequality recursively yields

D_{\psi}(p_{t}\|p^{\star})\leq(1-\kappa)^{t}\,D_{\psi}(p_{0}\|p^{\star}),

which proves exponential convergence in
D_{\psi}
.
∎

(Lyapunov Stability)

) defines a discrete Lyapunov condition:
the Bregman energy
V_{t}=D_{\psi}(p_{t}\|p^{\star})
decreases geometrically,
guaranteeing asymptotic stability of the BVLD dynamics.
This property is the discrete analogue of dissipativity
in continuous-time variational systems.

Lemma 5.5

(Bounded-Drift Inequality)
.

Let
p_{t}=T_{t}(p_{t-1})
and let
p_{t}^{\star}\in\mathrm{Fix}(T_{t})
denote an instantaneous fixed point,
T_{t}(p_{t}^{\star})=p_{t}^{\star}
.
Assume
f_{t}
is convex
L
–smooth and
\psi
is
\mu
–strongly convex so that each
T_{t}
is
\kappa
–averaged with
\kappa=\mu/(\mu+L)\in(0,1)
and

D_{\psi}\big(T_{t}(u)\,\big\|\,T_{t}(v)\big)\;\leq\;(1-\kappa)\,D_{\psi}(u\|v)\qquad(\forall u,v).

(56)

Then, for all
t\geq 1
,

D_{\psi}(p_{t}\,\|\,p_{t}^{\star})\;\leq\;2(1-\kappa)\,D_{\psi}(p_{t-1}\,\|\,p_{t-1}^{\star})\;+\;2(1-\kappa)\,D_{\psi}(p_{t-1}^{\star}\,\|\,p_{t}^{\star}).

(57)

In particular, (
57
) has the form

D_{\psi}(p_{t}\|p_{t}^{\star})\;\leq\;a\,D_{\psi}(p_{t-1}\|p_{t-1}^{\star})\;+\;C\,W_{t},

with
a=2(1-\kappa)
,
C=2(1-\kappa)
and
W_{t}:=D_{\psi}(p_{t-1}^{\star}\|p_{t}^{\star})
.

Proof.

Set
a:=T_{t}(p_{t-1})=p_{t}
,
b:=T_{t}(p_{t-1}^{\star})
,
c:=p_{t}^{\star}
.
By the Bregman three-point identity,

D_{\psi}(a\|c)\;=\;D_{\psi}(a\|b)+D_{\psi}(b\|c)+\langle\nabla\psi(c)-\nabla\psi(b),\,a-b\rangle.

(58)

For the cross term, apply Young’s inequality with the primal/dual norms induced by
\psi
:

\langle\nabla\psi(c)-\nabla\psi(b),\,a-b\rangle\;\leq\;\frac{1}{2\mu}\,\|\nabla\psi(c)-\nabla\psi(b)\|_{*}^{2}\;+\;\frac{\mu}{2}\,\|a-b\|^{2}.

Since
\psi
is
\mu
–strongly convex, we have

\displaystyle D_{\psi}(x\|y)

\displaystyle\;\geq\;\tfrac{\mu}{2}\,\|x-y\|^{2},

\displaystyle\|\nabla\psi(c)-\nabla\psi(b)\|_{*}^{2}

\displaystyle\;\leq\;2\mu\,D_{\psi^{*}}(\nabla\psi(c)\|\nabla\psi(b))\;=\;2\mu\,D_{\psi}(b\|c).

(59)

where the last equality uses the standard duality
D_{\psi^{*}}(\nabla\psi(y)\|\nabla\psi(x))=D_{\psi}(x\|y)
. Hence

\langle\nabla\psi(c)-\nabla\psi(b),\,a-b\rangle\;\leq\;D_{\psi}(b\|c)\;+\;D_{\psi}(a\|b).

Plugging this into (
58
) yields

D_{\psi}(a\|c)\;\leq\;2\,D_{\psi}(a\|b)\;+\;2\,D_{\psi}(b\|c).

(60)

Now use the
\kappa
–averaged contractivity (
56
) twice:

D_{\psi}(a\|b)=D_{\psi}\!\big(T_{t}(p_{t-1})\,\big\|\,T_{t}(p_{t-1}^{\star})\big)\;\leq\;(1-\kappa)\,D_{\psi}(p_{t-1}\|p_{t-1}^{\star}),

and, since
c=p_{t}^{\star}=T_{t}(p_{t}^{\star})
,

D_{\psi}(b\|c)=D_{\psi}\!\big(T_{t}(p_{t-1}^{\star})\,\big\|\,T_{t}(p_{t}^{\star})\big)\;\leq\;(1-\kappa)\,D_{\psi}(p_{t-1}^{\star}\|p_{t}^{\star}).

Combining these two bounds with (
60
) gives

D_{\psi}(p_{t}\|p_{t}^{\star})\;\leq\;2(1-\kappa)\,D_{\psi}(p_{t-1}\|p_{t-1}^{\star})\;+\;2(1-\kappa)\,D_{\psi}(p_{t-1}^{\star}\|p_{t}^{\star}),

which is (
57
).
∎

Remark (matching the statement in the main text).

If you prefer the display

D_{\psi}(p_{t}\|p_{t}^{\star})\;\leq\;(1-\kappa)\,D_{\psi}(p_{t-1}\|p_{t-1}^{\star})\;+\;C\,W_{t},

you can keep the same proof and absorb constants by either:
(i) redefining
\kappa
to
\kappa^{\prime}:=\kappa/2
(so
2(1-\kappa)\leq 1-\kappa^{\prime}

(ii) keeping
\kappa
as is and setting
C:=2(1-\kappa)
with
W_{t}:=D_{\psi}(p_{t-1}^{\star}\|p_{t}^{\star})
.
Both versions are standard in tracking/variation analyses; the choice only affects the numerical constants, not the qualitative result.

Theorem 5.6

(Drift-Aware Convergence Bound)
.

Let
\{T_{t}\}
be a
\kappa
–averaged operator sequence
satisfying for all
u,v\in\Theta

D_{\psi}(T_{t}(u)\|T_{t}(v))\leq(1-\kappa)D_{\psi}(u\|v),\qquad\kappa=\frac{\mu}{\mu+L}\in(0,1).

Denote by
p_{t}=T_{t}(p_{t-1})
the BVLD iterates and by

p_{t}^{\star}\in\mathrm{Fix}(T_{t})
the instantaneous equilibrium.
If the cumulative drift

V_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}W(p_{t}^{\star},p_{t-1}^{\star})\quad\text{satisfies}\quad V_{T}<\infty,

where
W(\cdot,\cdot)
is any nonnegative distance measure
(e.g.
D_{\psi}
or Wasserstein),
then the total Bregman deviation is finite:

\sum_{t=1}^{T}D_{\psi}(p_{t}\|p_{t}^{\star})\;\leq\;\frac{1}{\kappa}\,D_{\psi}(p_{0}\|p_{0}^{\star})\;+\;\frac{C}{\kappa}\,V_{T}\;<\;\infty.

(61)

Proof.

For brevity set
D_{t}:=D_{\psi}(p_{t}\|p_{t}^{\star})
and

W_{t}:=W(p_{t}^{\star},p_{t-1}^{\star})
.
From the
\kappa
–averaged contractivity of
T_{t}

and the triangle property of
D_{\psi}
,
the one–step *drift inequality* (Lemma
5.5
) holds:

D_{t}\;\leq\;(1-\kappa)\,D_{t-1}\;+\;C\,W_{t},\qquad t\geq 1,

(62)

where
C>0
absorbs the curvature constant of
\psi

and the geometry of
W_{t}
.
Inequality (
62
) expresses that each update contracts
the previous deviation by factor
(1-\kappa)

but incurs an additive penalty proportional to the drift of the fixed point.

To accumulate these inequalities over time,
multiply (
62
) by
\kappa
and add
(1-\kappa)D_{t}
to both sides:

\kappa D_{t}+(1-\kappa)D_{t}\;\leq\;(1-\kappa)D_{t-1}\;+\;C\kappa W_{t}\;+\;(1-\kappa)D_{t}.

Rearranging gives

\kappa D_{t}\;\leq\;(1-\kappa)(D_{t-1}-D_{t})\;+\;C\kappa W_{t}.

(63)

Summing (
63
) from
t=1
to
T
yields

\displaystyle\kappa\sum_{t=1}^{T}D_{t}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\kappa)\sum_{t=1}^{T}(D_{t-1}-D_{t})\;+\;C\kappa\sum_{t=1}^{T}W_{t}.

The first term on the right telescopes:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}(D_{t-1}-D_{t})

\displaystyle=D_{0}-D_{T}\;\leq\;D_{0}.

Therefore,

\kappa\sum_{t=1}^{T}D_{t}\;\leq\;(1-\kappa)D_{0}\;+\;C\kappa V_{T}\;\leq\;D_{0}\;+\;C\kappa V_{T}.

Dividing by
\kappa
gives exactly
(
61
).
Because
V_{T}<\infty
by assumption,
the right-hand side is finite, implying

\sum_{t=1}^{T}D_{t}<\infty
.
Consequently,
the Bregman energy sequence
\{D_{t}\}
is summable
and the BVLD iterates track the moving equilibria
with uniformly bounded cumulative deviation.
∎

Remark 5.7

Theorem 5.8

(Continuous-Time EVI Decay)
.

Consider the continuous-time limit

\dot{p}(t)\;=\;-\nabla_{\!\psi}^{-1}\big(\nabla f_{t}(p(t))\big),\qquad p(0)\in\Theta.

(64)

Then the Bregman energy
E(t)=D_{\psi}(p(t)\|p^{\star})

\frac{d}{dt}E(t)\;\leq\;-\kappa\,E(t)+\xi(t),\qquad\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi(t)\,dt<\infty,

(65)

which implies the exponential-type decay

D_{\psi}(p(t)\|p^{\star})\;\leq\;e^{-\kappa t}\,D_{\psi}(p(0)\|p^{\star})\;+\;O(\xi),

(66)

where
\kappa=\mu/(\mu+L)
and
\xi(t)
quantifies the instantaneous temporal drift of
f_{t}
and the moving equilibrium
p_{t}^{\star}
.

Proof.

Let
p^{\star}(t)\in\mathrm{Fix}(T_{t})
denote the instantaneous equilibrium satisfying
\nabla f_{t}(p^{\star}(t))=0
.
Define the Bregman energy

E(t)=D_{\psi}(p(t)\|p^{\star}(t))=\psi(p(t))-\psi(p^{\star}(t))-\langle\nabla\psi(p^{\star}(t)),p(t)-p^{\star}(t)\rangle.

Differentiating
E(t)
with respect to
t
yields

\displaystyle\dot{E}(t)

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla\psi(p(t))-\nabla\psi(p^{\star}(t)),\,\dot{p}(t)\rangle-\langle\dot{p}^{\star}(t),\,\nabla\psi(p(t))-\nabla\psi(p^{\star}(t))\rangle.

(67)

The first term represents the dissipative decay of
p(t)
toward
p^{\star}(t)
, while the second term accounts for the equilibrium’s temporal drift.
Substituting the dynamics
\dot{p}(t)=-\nabla_{\!\psi}^{-1}(\nabla f_{t}(p(t)))
into (
67
) gives

\dot{E}(t)=-\big\langle\nabla\psi(p(t))-\nabla\psi(p^{\star}(t)),\nabla_{\!\psi}^{-1}\!\big(\nabla f_{t}(p(t))\big)\big\rangle-\big\langle\dot{p}^{\star}(t),\,\nabla\psi(p(t))-\nabla\psi(p^{\star}(t))\big\rangle.

By
\mu
–strong convexity of
\psi
and
L
–smoothness of
f_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f_{t}(p(t))-\nabla f_{t}(p^{\star}(t)),\,p(t)-p^{\star}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\;\frac{1}{L}\,\|\nabla f_{t}(p(t))-\nabla f_{t}(p^{\star}(t))\|^{2},

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\psi(p(t))-\nabla\psi(p^{\star}(t)),\,p(t)-p^{\star}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\;2\mu\,D_{\psi}(p(t)\|p^{\star}(t)).

Applying Young’s inequality in the corresponding primal–dual coordinates yields the coupling bound

\big\langle\nabla\psi(p(t))-\nabla\psi(p^{\star}(t)),\nabla_{\!\psi}^{-1}\!\big(\nabla f_{t}(p(t))\big)\big\rangle\;\geq\;\frac{\mu}{\mu+L}\,D_{\psi}(p(t)\|p^{\star}(t))=\kappa\,E(t),

so the dissipative term satisfies

-\big\langle\nabla\psi(p(t))-\nabla\psi(p^{\star}(t)),\nabla_{\!\psi}^{-1}\!\big(\nabla f_{t}(p(t))\big)\big\rangle\leq-\kappa\,E(t).

Next, since
\nabla f_{t}(p^{\star}(t))\equiv 0
, differentiation gives

\dot{p}^{\star}(t)=-(\nabla^{2}f_{t}(p^{\star}(t)))^{-1}\partial_{t}(\nabla f_{t})(p^{\star}(t))
.
Hence

\displaystyle\big|\langle\dot{p}^{\star}(t),\,\nabla\psi(p(t))-\nabla\psi(p^{\star}(t))\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq\;c_{1}\,\|\partial_{t}(\nabla f_{t})\|\,\|p(t)-p^{\star}(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\;c_{2}\,W(p^{\star}(t),p^{\star}(t-\Delta t))\;=:\;\xi(t).

where
W(\cdot,\cdot)
is a temporal drift metric and constants
(c_{1},c_{2})
depend on
(L,\mu)
.
If
\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi(t)\,dt<\infty
, the cumulative drift is integrable and does not destabilize the flow.
Combining both contributions gives the differential inequality

\dot{E}(t)\leq-\kappa E(t)+\xi(t),

which is exactly (
65
).
Integrating over
[0,t]
and applying Grönwall’s inequality yields

E(t)\leq e^{-\kappa t}E(0)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\kappa(t-s)}\xi(s)\,ds.

Since
\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi(s)\,ds<\infty
implies boundedness of the integral term,
it follows that
E(t)\leq e^{-\kappa t}E(0)+O(\xi)
, establishing (
66
).
∎

\frac{d}{dt}D_{\psi}(p(t),p^{\star})+\mu D_{\psi}(p(t),p^{\star})\leq 0,

(68)

which is equivalent to EVI form

\dot{p}(t)\in-\partial f_{t}(p(t))-\nabla^{2}\psi(p(t))\,\big(p(t)-p^{\star}\big),

confirming that BVLD obeys an exponentially decaying Bregman energy in continuous time.

Remark.

Equation (
65
) characterizes the continuous-time
EVI associated with the BVLD dynamics.
It describes a dissipative mirror flow in Bregman geometry,
where the energy
E(t)=D_{\psi}(p(t)\|p^{\star})

\xi(t)

\Delta t\to 0

(1-\kappa)

Collectively, Theorems

The Bregman energy

Proposition 6.1

(Dual Representation of DRO–BVLD)
.

Assume that
\ell_{t}(\cdot,\theta)
is convex and
\varphi
is lower semicontinuous.
Then the inner supremum in (
69
) admits the Fenchel–Legendre dual representation:

\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}(\rho)}\mathbb{E}_{Q}[\ell_{t}(q,\theta)]=\min_{\lambda\geq 0}\Big\{\lambda\rho+\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}\big[\varphi^{*}\!\big(\tfrac{\ell_{t}(q,\theta)}{\lambda}\big)\big]\Big\},

(70)

where
\varphi^{*}
denotes the convex conjugate of
\varphi
.
Hence, the distributionally robust Bregman–variational update becomes

T_{t}^{\mathrm{DRO}}(p)=\argmin_{q\in\Theta}\Big\{\min_{\lambda\geq 0}\big[\lambda\rho+\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}[\varphi^{*}(\ell_{t}(q,\theta)/\lambda)]\big]+D_{\psi}(q\|p)\Big\}.

(71)

Proof.

We work with the ambiguity set

\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}(\rho)=\Big\{Q\ll\hat{P}:\;\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}\big[\varphi(r)\big]\leq\rho,\;r:=\tfrac{dQ}{d\hat{P}}\geq 0,\;\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}[r]=1\Big\},

where
\varphi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is proper, convex, lower semicontinuous (lsc), and normalized by
\varphi(1)=0
.
For a fixed
q\in\Theta
, write
\ell(\theta):=\ell_{t}(q,\theta)
for brevity and consider

\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}(\rho)}\mathbb{E}_{Q}[\ell]=\sup_{r\geq 0}\Big\{\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}[r\ell]\;:\;\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}[\varphi(r)]\leq\rho,\;\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}[r]=1\Big\}.

Introduce Lagrange multipliers
\lambda\geq 0
(for the
\varphi
-divergence budget) and
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
(for the normalization), and form the Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}(r,\lambda,\eta)=\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}[r\ell]-\lambda\big(\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}[\varphi(r)]-\rho\big)-\eta\big(\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}[r]-1\big)=\lambda\rho+\eta+\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}\!\Big[r(\ell-\eta)-\lambda\varphi(r)\Big].

By weak duality and interchange of
\sup
and
\inf
(Slater-type feasibility holds because
r\equiv 1

\varphi(1)=0
), we obtain

\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}(\rho)}\mathbb{E}_{Q}[\ell]=\inf_{\lambda\geq 0,\;\eta\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{\lambda\rho+\eta+\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}\!\left[\sup_{r\geq 0}\big\{r(\ell-\eta)-\lambda\varphi(r)\big\}\right]\right\}.

(72)

Using Fenchel–Legendre conjugacy of
\varphi
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\sup_{r\geq 0}\big\{ur-\lambda\varphi(r)\big\}=\lambda\,\varphi^{*}\!\Big(\frac{u}{\lambda}\Big)\qquad(\lambda>0),

and the obvious limit when
\lambda=0
(which returns
+\infty
unless
u\leq 0
almost surely), we rewrite (
72

\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}(\rho)}\mathbb{E}_{Q}[\ell]=\inf_{\lambda\geq 0,\;\eta\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{\lambda\rho+\eta+\lambda\,\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}\!\left[\varphi^{*}\!\Big(\frac{\ell-\eta}{\lambda}\Big)\right]\right\}.

(73)

Eliminating the normalization multiplier.

Define for
\lambda\geq 0
the function

\Phi_{\lambda}(\eta):=\eta+\lambda\,\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}\!\left[\varphi^{*}\!\Big(\frac{\ell-\eta}{\lambda}\Big)\right].

By convexity of
\varphi^{*}
,
\Phi_{\lambda}
is convex in
\eta
.
A standard property of Csiszár divergences (conjugacy on
\mathbb{R}_{+}

\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}[r]=1
) yields the “translation identity”

\inf_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}}\;\Phi_{\lambda}(\eta)=\lambda\,\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}\!\left[\varphi^{*}\!\Big(\frac{\ell}{\lambda}\Big)\right].

(74)

For completeness, one may verify (
74
) via first-order optimality:
let
r_{\eta}(\theta)\in\partial\varphi^{*}((\ell(\theta)-\eta)/\lambda)
.
Then

\Phi_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\eta)=1-\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}[r_{\eta}].

Since
r_{\eta}\in\partial\varphi^{*}(\cdot)
iff
(\ell-\eta)/\lambda\in\partial\varphi(r_{\eta})
,
the choice of
\eta
that satisfies
\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}[r_{\eta}]=1

aligns with the primal normalization constraint.
At this optimizer
\eta^{\star}(\lambda)
we have

\Phi_{\lambda}(\eta^{\star})=\lambda\,\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}\!\big[\varphi^{*}(\ell/\lambda)\big]
,
giving (
74
).
Substituting (
74
) into (
73
) yields the announced dual:

\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}(\rho)}\mathbb{E}_{Q}[\ell]=\inf_{\lambda\geq 0}\Big\{\lambda\rho+\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}\big[\varphi^{*}\!\big(\tfrac{\ell}{\lambda}\big)\big]\Big\}.

Reinstating
\ell(\theta)=\ell_{t}(q,\theta)
proves (
70
).

Convexity and contraction of the robust update.

Define the robust surrogate

g_{t}(q):=\inf_{\lambda\geq 0}\Big\{\lambda\rho+\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}\big[\varphi^{*}(\ell_{t}(q,\theta)/\lambda)\big]\Big\}.

Because
\ell_{t}(\cdot,\theta)
is convex and
\varphi^{*}
is convex,

q\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}[\varphi^{*}(\ell_{t}(q,\theta)/\lambda)]
is convex for each fixed

hence

is convex as a pointwise infimum of convex functions.
Assume in addition that
\ell_{t}
is

is
1
–Lipschitz–gradient on the relevant range;

.

then the mapping
q\mapsto\nabla_{q}\,\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}[\varphi^{*}(\ell_{t}(q,\theta)/\lambda)]

Consequently, the robust BVLD update

The DRO–BVLD formulation penalizes model misspecification by inflating
the effective loss through the multiplier

which acts as a data-dependent risk aversion term.
This yields an adaptive regularization effect equivalent to
distributional robustness in Bayesian or online learning,
providing resilience against both stochastic noise and adversarial perturbations.

Consider
m
convex,
L_{i}
–smooth objectives

f_{t}^{(i)}:\Theta\to\mathbb{R}

(i=1,\ldots,m)
and a scalarization cone

\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
.
The Pareto–BVLD problem seeks to update

T_{t}^{\mathrm{Pareto}}(p)=\argmin_{q\in\Theta}\big\{F_{t}(q)+D_{\psi}(q\|p)\big\},\qquad F_{t}(q):=\max_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}f_{t}^{(i)}(q),

where
\mathcal{W}:=\{w\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}:\sum_{i}w_{i}=1\}
.

Theorem 6.2

(Pareto Well-Posedness)
.

If each
f_{t}^{(i)}
is convex and continuously differentiable on
\Theta
,
then the efficient frontier of the Pareto–BVLD problem is nonempty, closed,
and every extreme point corresponds to some weight vector

w^{\star}\in\mathcal{W}
satisfying the KKT system

0\in\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}^{\star}\nabla f_{t}^{(i)}(q^{\star})+\nabla\psi(q^{\star})-\nabla\psi(p)+N_{\Theta}(q^{\star}).

Proof.

Fix
p\in\Theta
and define, for
w\in\mathcal{W}
,

F_{t}^{(w)}(q):=\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}f_{t}^{(i)}(q)+D_{\psi}(q\|p),\qquad q\in\Theta.

(1)
Existence and uniqueness for each
w
.

By convexity of
f_{t}^{(i)}
and strong convexity of
D_{\psi}(\cdot\|p)
in
q
,

F_{t}^{(w)}
is strongly convex on
\Theta
for every
w\in\mathcal{W}
.
Moreover,
D_{\psi}(\cdot\|p)
is coercive on
\Theta

(when
\Theta
is unbounded, strong convexity ensures
D_{\psi}(q\|p)\to\infty
as
\|q\|\to\infty
),
so the minimization

q(w)\in\arg\min_{q\in\Theta}F_{t}^{(w)}(q)

admits a unique solution for each
w
.

(2)
Continuity of
w\mapsto q(w)
and closedness/nonemptiness of the efficient set.

The map
(w,q)\mapsto F_{t}^{(w)}(q)
is jointly continuous and, in
q
, uniformly strongly convex.
By standard parametric convex optimization results (e.g., Berge’s Maximum Theorem, or the strong convexity argument), the unique minimizer
q(w)
depends continuously on
w
. Since
\mathcal{W}
is compact and
q(\cdot)
is continuous, the image

\mathcal{E}:=\{q(w):w\in\mathcal{W}\}

is compact; in particular,
\mathcal{E}
is nonempty and closed.
As
F_{t}^{(w)}
is a proper scalarization of the vector criterion

\big(f_{t}^{(1)}(\cdot),\dots,f_{t}^{(m)}(\cdot)\big)
,
each
q(w)
is a (weak) Pareto minimizer of the multiobjective problem with Bregman regularization.
Hence the efficient frontier is nonempty and closed.

(3)
Extreme efficient points arise from weighted sums.

Consider the vector-valued objective

\mathbf{f}_{t}(q):=(f_{t}^{(1)}(q),\dots,f_{t}^{(m)}(q))

and the regularized dominance defined by

q_{1}\preceq q_{2}\;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;\mathbf{f}_{t}(q_{1})+D_{\psi}(q_{1}\|p)\mathbf{1}\;\leq\;\mathbf{f}_{t}(q_{2})+D_{\psi}(q_{2}\|p)\mathbf{1}\quad(\text{componentwise}).

In convex vector optimization, extreme points of the efficient frontier admit supporting hyperplanes; by the separating hyperplane (supporting functional) theorem, any extreme efficient
q^{\star}
is optimal for a weighted-sum scalarization with some nonzero
w^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
. Rescaling yields
w^{\star}\in\mathcal{W}
. Thus every extreme efficient point
q^{\star}
solves

\min_{q\in\Theta}\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}^{\star}f_{t}^{(i)}(q)+D_{\psi}(q\|p)\Big\}.

(4)
KKT system.

Because
\sum_{i}w_{i}^{\star}f_{t}^{(i)}
is convex and
C^{1}
on
\Theta
and
D_{\psi}(\cdot\|p)
is convex and
C^{1}
, the necessary and sufficient optimality conditions for the convex program in (3) are

0\in\nabla\!\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}^{\star}f_{t}^{(i)}\Big)(q^{\star})+\nabla_{q}D_{\psi}(q^{\star}\|p)+N_{\Theta}(q^{\star}).

Using the Bregman identity
\nabla_{q}D_{\psi}(q\|p)=\nabla\psi(q)-\nabla\psi(p)
,
we obtain exactly

0\in\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}^{\star}\nabla f_{t}^{(i)}(q^{\star})+\nabla\psi(q^{\star})-\nabla\psi(p)+N_{\Theta}(q^{\star}),

which is the claimed KKT system. This completes the proof.
∎

The bilevel embedding follows classical hierarchical optimization principles

[

Finally, consider a bilevel optimization problem in which an upper-level
decision variable
x
interacts with the BVLD subproblem:

\min_{x}\;C(x,q)+\lambda\,\mathrm{Risk}(x,q)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad q\in\argmin_{r\in\Theta}\{f_{t}(r)+D_{\psi}(r\|p)\}.

(75)

Embedding the lower-level BVLD problem into a single-level reformulation
yields the following equivalent convex program.

Proposition 6.4

(KKT-Based Single-Level Reformulation)
.

Embedding the optimality condition of the lower-level BVLD problem
yields the equivalent single-level convex program:

\min_{x,q}\;C(x,q)+\lambda\,\mathrm{Risk}(x,q)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad 0\in\nabla f_{t}(q)+\nabla\psi(q)-\nabla\psi(p)+N_{\Theta}(q).

(76)

Proof.

We proceed in four steps.

(i) Well-posedness and uniqueness of the lower-level BVLD subproblem.

Fix
p\in\Theta
. Consider the lower problem

\min_{r\in\Theta}\;f_{t}(r)+D_{\psi}(r\|p).

By convexity of
f_{t}
and strong convexity of
D_{\psi}(\cdot\|p)
in
r
,
the objective is strongly convex on
\Theta
.
If
\Theta
is unbounded, the strong convexity of
D_{\psi}
ensures coercivity;
hence a unique minimizer
q^{\star}\in\Theta
exists.

(ii) Necessary and sufficient optimality (KKT/monotone inclusion).

Since the objective is convex and
C^{1}
, and the feasible set
\Theta
is closed and convex,
first-order optimality is necessary and sufficient:

0\in\nabla f_{t}(q^{\star})+\nabla_{q}D_{\psi}(q^{\star}\|p)+N_{\Theta}(q^{\star}).

Using the Bregman identity
\nabla_{q}D_{\psi}(q\|p)=\nabla\psi(q)-\nabla\psi(p)
,
we obtain

0\in\nabla f_{t}(q^{\star})+\nabla\psi(q^{\star})-\nabla\psi(p)+N_{\Theta}(q^{\star}).

(77)

Conversely, any
q
satisfying (
77
)
is the unique minimizer of the lower problem,
so (
77
) characterizes the feasible set of the lower-level argmin exactly.

(iii) Equivalence of the bilevel and single-level feasible pairs.

The feasible set of the bilevel model

\big\{(x,q):\;q\in\argmin_{r\in\Theta}\{f_{t}(r)+D_{\psi}(r\|p)\}\big\}

coincides with

\big\{(x,q):\;0\in\nabla f_{t}(q)+\nabla\psi(q)-\nabla\psi(p)+N_{\Theta}(q)\big\},

by (ii).
Therefore, the constraint in (
75
)
is equivalently expressed by the KKT/normal-cone inclusion
in (
76
).
No spurious solutions are introduced and none are lost.

(iv) Convexity and optimality preservation under embedding.

Under the standing assumptions,
the objective
C(x,q)+\lambda\,\mathrm{Risk}(x,q)
is convex in
(x,q)
.
The set-valued map

q\mapsto\nabla f_{t}(q)+\nabla\psi(q)-\nabla\psi(p)+N_{\Theta}(q)

is
maximally monotone
:
\nabla f_{t}
and
\nabla\psi
are monotone
(with
\nabla\psi

\mu
-strongly monotone), and
N_{\Theta}
is maximally monotone.
Hence the feasible set

\{q:\,0\in\nabla f_{t}(q)+\nabla\psi(q)-\nabla\psi(p)+N_{\Theta}(q)\}

is closed and convex.
Consequently, the single-level formulation (
76
)
is a convex optimization problem.
Since both formulations share the same objective and feasible pairs,
their optimal solutions coincide.
∎

Remark 6.5

(Hierarchical Sensitivity and Dual Consistency)
.

If
\nabla f_{t}
and
\nabla\psi
are Lipschitz continuous on
\Theta
,
then the operator

G(p,q):=\nabla f_{t}(q)+\nabla\psi(q)-\nabla\psi(p)+N_{\Theta}(q)

is strongly monotone in
q
and Lipschitz in
p
.
By the Robinson–Mordukhovich criterion,
the implicit solution mapping

p\mapsto q^{\star}(p)
defined by
0\in G(p,q^{\star}(p))

is
metrically subregular
and locally single-valued.
Thus, small perturbations in the upper-level variable
p

or the data
(f_{t},\psi)
induce changes in
q^{\star}(p)
of at most
linear order, ensuring hierarchical stability and dual consistency
between levels under smooth BVLD dynamics

Conclusion

This paper developed a unified theory of
BVLD.
for adaptive optimization under environmental drift.
Starting from a geometric foundation on Hilbert spaces and Legendre-type potentials,
we established a sequence of results that collectively guarantee
stability, convergence, and robustness.

The proposed operator

T_{t}(p)=\argmin_{q\in\Theta}\{f_{t}(q)+D_{\psi}(q\|p)\}

was shown to be
(1-\kappa)
–contractive and firmly nonexpansive in the Bregman metric,
with
\kappa=\mu/(\mu+L)
determined by the curvature contrast.
Fejér monotonicity and Lyapunov analysis ensured
geometric convergence to equilibrium,
while the drift-aware inequality bounded dynamic regret under time-varying objectives.
Continuous-time analysis further revealed an evolution–variational inequality (EVI)
governing exponential decay of the Bregman energy.
These results were empirically validated on benchmark problems,
confirming the predicted exponential rate and robustness across geometric variants.

The BVLD framework naturally extends to stochastic,
distributed, and hierarchical environments.
Future work may explore
(i) stochastic BVLD with variance-controlled noise injection;
(ii) distributed BVLD across networked agents using Bregman consensus mappings; and
(iii) adaptive step-size or meta-learning rules that preserve contraction under uncertainty.
Such extensions would connect the present deterministic theory with online learning,
multi-agent coordination, and federated optimization.

Beyond theoretical interest, the results provide analytic foundations for
digital-twin and control systems that continuously adjust to drift and degradation.
In such systems, the Bregman energy
D_{\psi}(p_{t}\|p_{t}^{\star})

serves as a measurable Lyapunov indicator of operational stability.
This insight enables robust parameter updating, predictive maintenance,
and self-calibrating digital twins capable of maintaining equilibrium behavior
in dynamically changing environments.

In summary, BVLD offers a rigorously grounded yet practically extensible
framework for learning and control under drift.
By bridging geometry, operator theory, and dynamic stability,
it lays a foundation for future research on robust,
distributed, and decision-integrated optimization systems

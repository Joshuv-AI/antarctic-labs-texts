Development of 1-(substituted benzofuran-2-yl)-3-(halogen-substituted phenyl)prop-2-en-1-ones as potent, reversible, and selective MAO-B inhibitors for Parkinson’s Disease
Authors: Ali Hasan, Jiseong Lee, Naresh Payyaula, Vishnu Vasanthi Radhakrishanan, Sachithra Thazhathuveedu Sudevan, Sunil Kumar, Della Grace Thomas Parambi, Kasim Sakran Abass, Arun Kumar, Naseer Maliyakkal, Hoon Kim, Bijo Mathew
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTen halogenated benzofuran derivatives (BF1–BF10) were synthesized and evaluated for their inhibitory activity against monoamine oxidase isoforms. All compounds showed stronger inhibition of MAO-B compared to MAO-A. Among them, BF2 exhibited the most potent MAO-B inhibition (IC₅₀ = 0.082 µM), followed by BF5 (IC₅₀ = 0.095 µM) and BF1 (IC₅₀ = 0.16 µM), with high selectivity indices, particularly BF2 (SI = 1038.05). Kinetic studies revealed that BF2 acts as a competitive inhibitor with a Ki value of 0.083 ± 0.0056 µM. Dialysis experiments confirmed its reversible inhibition profile, comparable to the reference drug Safinamide. Both BF2and BF5 were non-cytotoxic toward L929 cells and demonstrated moderate blood–brain barrier permeability in PAMPA assay. Molecular docking showed stabilization of MAO-B via π–π stacking interactions with Tyr326 and Tyr398, and molecular dynamics simulations further confirmed stable binding of BF2 within the MAO-B active site. Overall, BF2 emerged as a potent, selective, and reversible MAO-B inhibitor, warranting for further investigation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-53367-w.Keywords: Benzofuran, Chalcone, MAO-B, Reversibility, Kinetics, Molecular dockingSubject terms: Biochemistry, Chemistry, Drug discovery

Introduction

Parkinson’s disease (PD) is an age-related neurodegenerative disorder and is the second most common progressive neurodegenerative condition associated with motor disabilities. This condition affects approximately 2% of adults over 65 years of age1,2, with 5–20 cases reported annually, contributing to a global population of around 100,000 individuals. In patients with PD, dopamine (DA) are reduced in the caudate nucleus and putamen. The disease is characterized by the specific loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta, accompanied by the presence of cytosolic Lewy bodies that contain aggregated α-synuclein3–5.In addition to motor symptoms, PD patients often experience non-motor symptoms such as depression, sleep disturbance, and cognitive impairment6.Although the exact cause of the condition’s progression is unknown, several studies suggest that beyond DA depletion, various factors may also act as contributors. These include protein aggregation, oxidative stress, neuroinflammation, dysregulation of the autophagy-lysosomal pathway, insufficient neurotrophic factor support, and mitochondrial dysfunction7.Since the late 1950s, monoamine oxidase (MAO) inhibitors have been employed, and since the end of the 1960s, supplements consisting of short biogenic molecules containing 3,4-dihydroxyphenylalanine (L-DOPA) have been used8. MAOare the main enzymes that use oxidative deamination to metabolize various biogenic amines9.

Several neurological conditions, including PD disease and Alzheimer’s disease (AD), are directly correlated with changes in biogenic amine concentrations in the brain10. The toxic consequences of oxidative breakdown include the production of hydrogen peroxide, reactive oxygen species (ROS), and ammonia. In brain cells, these byproducts can cause oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction11,12. Patients with PD have high levels of MAO-B in the substantia nigra, and reversible and highly selective MAO-B inhibitors have shown promise in treating PD symptoms13. Reversible MAO-B inhibitors such as rasagiline and selegiline are beneficial for treating motor dysfunction and DA degradation in patients with PD. Irreversible MAO-B inhibitors have several disadvantages, including immunogenicity of the MAO-B inhibitor adduct, prolonged half-life, disruption of the target, decreased sensitivity to ADME testing, and de novo MAO enzyme synthesis in the human brain14–16.These elements have led to a surge in interest in the development of reversible and efficient MAO-B inhibitors for PD treatment. The FDA and EU recently approved the reversible MAO-B inhibitor safinamide for the treatment of individuals with mid- to late-stage PD17,18. The FDA approved drugs and benzofuran containing MAO inhibitors are depicited in Fig. 1.

MAO inhibitors (safinamide, selegiline, and rasagiline) and some approved benzofuran compounds (bergapten and amiodarone).

Chalcones, also known as trans-1,3-diphenyl-2-propen-1-ones, are simple chemical scaffolds that contain enone linkers between two phenyl or heteroaryl nuclei19. The chalcone scaffold underwent numerous structural modifications to function as a selective MAO-B inhibitor. These modifications include the insertion of different electron-donating and/or electron-withdrawing groups into the two aromatic heterocyclic rings to produce potent, selective and reversible type of MAO-B inhibitors20. These investigations have shown that the important parameters in the development of MAO-B inhibitors include the length and hydrophobicity of the rings and electron delocalization enone linker in the chalcone moiety21–23. These reports suggest that the presence of halogen-containing compounds can produce the electron-acceptor nature of MAO-B inhibitors through halogen bonding, which is favorable for interactions24,25. SwissTargetPredictions, an online tool, was recently used for ligand-based target prediction by looking for comparable molecules. Our lab discovered that the basic chalcone skeleton is more selective towards oxidase enzymes like MAO-B20.

Benzofuran is a bicyclic heteroaromatic nucleus with a variety of pharmacological properties including anticancer, antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, antiviral, antioxidant, and neuroprotective effects. Moreover, the nucleus has a significant role in the development of MAO inhibitors in recent years. Considering the role of the chalcone scaffold, the presence of halogens, and the benzofuran nucleus in the development of MAO-B inhibitors, we aimed to incorporate all these structural units into a single framework. This is consistent with our ongoing endeavors to develop potent and specific MAO-B inhibitors for the treatment of PD26–28. This study focuses on the synthesis and MAO inhibition of halogenated benzofuran-based chalcones. The lead molecules were further evaluated for their reversibility, kinetics, blood-brain barrier (BBB) permeation, and cytotoxicity.

Materials and methods

Chemicals

For the synthesis, 2-benzofuranyl methyl ketone and halogenated benzaldehydes(Sigma Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) were used. For the enzyme inhibition study, enzymes (recombinant human MAO-A and MAO-B) and chemicals, including benzylamine, kynuramine, safinamide mesylate salt, pargyline, clorgyline, and toloxatone, were purchased from Sigma Aldrich. The DiaEasy™ dialysis kit (6–8 kDa) was obtained from BioVision (St. Louis, MA, USA), as described previously.

Synthesis

A mixture of 2-benzofuranyl methyl ketone (0.01 M) and one of the aldehyde derivatives (0.01 M) in methanol (20 mL) was cooled to 0–5 °C after which 15 mL of 40% aqueous sodium hydroxide was added. The mixture was stirred at 25 °C for 6 h (Scheme 1). The mixture was then poured onto crushed ice and acidified with Conc. HCl. The precipitated solids were filtered, washed with water, and dried, andthe residue was recrystallized with ethanol. The reaction was monitored by TLC using hexane and ethyl acetate (4:1). Structures of all final derivatives were investigated by using 1H NMR and13C NMR spectra obtained with an NMR spectrometer (Bruker Advance Neo 500 MHz, Billerica, MA, USA).

Synthetic scheme of chalcone base benzofuran derivatives (BF1-BF10).

Enzyme assays and kinetics

The activities of recombinant human MAO-A and MAO-B were assayed using kynuramine (0.06 mM) and benzylamine (0.30 mM), respectively, by continuously measuring the absorbance changes at 316 and 250 nm, respectively29,30. Enzyme activity was assayed at five substrate concentrations around the Km value in the absence of an inhibitor of enzyme kinetics.

Inhibition studies of MAO-A and MAO-B by the compounds

For the inhibition study, residual activity was initial screened by measuring the absorbance change in the presence of a 10 µM inhibitor.If necessary, a 1 µMconcentration was used for potent inhibitors. IC50 values were then determined for compounds with less than 80% residual activity at 10 µM using residual activity curves generated by GraphPad Prism software 8.030. The upper limit of IC50 was set to 100 µM. IC50 values of the leading compounds BF2 and BF5 were analyzed by dose-response curves constructed at seven inhibitor concentrations. The selectivity index (SI) for MAO-B was calculated using the IC50 values for MAO-A and the IC50 of MAO-A/MAO-B31. The inhibition type of a lead compound BF2 for MAO-B was determined at three inhibitor concentrations around (approximately 1/2 x, 1 x, and 2 x IC50) and five different substrate concentrations (0.0375–0.6 µM)32. Enzyme kinetic patterns and Ki values were determined by comparing Lineweaver-Burk plots and their secondary plots33. Toloxatone, clorgyline, safinamidemesylate salt, and pargyline were used as the reference compounds.

Time-dependency and reversibility studies

Time-dependent inhibition of BF2 for MAO-B was observed by preincubation at designated times, i.e., 0, 2, 5, 10, 15, and 30 min. Concentration of BF2 was approximately IC50 (0.1 µM ). After preincubation, the residual activity was measured. The reversibility of BF2 for MAO-B was evaluated by comparing the undialyzed (AU) and dialyzed (AD) residual activities at a concentration of approximately 2-times the IC50 after a 30 min pre-incubation before measurement. Two types of reference inhibitors were used for MAO-A and MAO-B: reversible inhibitors toloxatone and safinamide mesylate (for MAO-A and MAO-B, respectively) and irreversible inhibitors clorgyline and pargyline (for MAO-A and MAO-B, respectively)34.

Parallel artificial membrane permeability assay (PAMPA)

The PAMPA method35,36 was used to assess the two lead compounds’ blood-brain barrier (BBB) penetration capacities. Supplementary data provide a full explanation of this process.

Cytotoxicity test of BF2 and BF5 to L929 cell line

The National Center for Cell Science (NCCS), Pune, India, provided the mouse fibroblast cell line L929. The supporting information provides a full explanation of the protocol37,38.

Computational studies

Absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicity (ADMET) study

The ADME/Tox (absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicity) profiles of the synthesized compounds were assessed using the QikProp module from Schrödinger, Inc., New York, USA (2024)(https://www.schrodinger.com/platform/products/qikprop/). This tool estimates key descriptors by benchmarking the compounds against the top five structurally similar drug molecules39,40. The ligands were analyzed and their properties evaluated according to the predicted descriptor values.

Molecular Docking

The binding affinities of lead molecules BF2 and BF5 towards hMAO-A (PDB ID: 2Z5X) and hMAO-B (PDB ID: 2V5Z) enzymes were determined using molecular docking studies41,42. Protein crystal structures were prepared by adding missing residues and hydrogen atoms, followed by optimization and minimization using the Protein Preparation Workflow program. Protonation states for each residue in the protein at a pH of 7.4.All water molecules in the crystal structure were removed, and the ligand was prepared using the LigPrep module to assign the 3D coordinates and hydrogen atoms at the same pH using the OPLS4 force field. The receptor-grid generation tool was used to assign docking coordinates where the co-crystal ligand was bound. Docking was performed using the default OPLS4 force field with an extra precision (XP) docking pipeline in the Schrodinger software to evaluate the binding affinity of ligands towards both receptors(https://www.schrodinger.com/platform/products/glide/). To understand the binding free energy of the docked complex, molecular mechanics with generalized born surface area calculations were performed using the Prime module in Schrodinger software15.

Molecular dynamics simulation

Molecular dynamics (MD) simulation was performed for the docked complex of BF2 ligand in the active site of MAO-B receptor using Desmond module in Schrödinger, Inc., New York, USA 2024 -3 software (https://www.schrodinger.com/platform/products/desmond/). The complex was solvated using SPC water molecules in an orthorhombic periodic condition and neutralized the charge using NaCl salt. The electrostatic interaction for simulation was accounted within 9Å cut-off and NPT ensemble with 300 K temperature and 1 bar pressure was chosen for simulation. The temperature and pressure was controlled using Nose-Hoover chain thermostat and Martyna-Tobias-Klein barostat with isotropic coupling style. Finally, the production run was performed for 200ns and the protein-ligand complex stability was analyzed using RMSD, RMSF, and interaction profile of the trajectory43,44.

Structure-activity relationship of chalcone-based benzofuran derivatives.

All BF series compounds are benzofuran derivatives with a parent structure in which a carbonyl functional group is bonded to position 2 of benzofuran (Fig. 3), followed by methyl styrene. The subtypes were named according to the substituents bonded to the benzene ring on the right: -Cl was bonded from BF2 to BF4, -Br from BF5 to BF7, and -F from BF8 to BF10 (Fig. 3). Overall, Cl substituents showed better inhibitory activity than Br and F-substituents in order (Table 1). In addition, for the Cl substituents, the inhibitory effect was found to be the strongest, in the order of ortho, meta, andpara. In the Br-substituents, similar to the Cl-substituents, the best inhibitory effect was observed in the ortho position, but the para position of the substituents showed a better inhibitory effect than the meta position. Finally, for the F substituents, a strong inhibitory effect was observed in the order of para, meta, and ortho.These observed trends suggest that both the nature of the halogen substituent and its positional orientation may influence MAO-B inhibitory activity. The relatively higher activity of chloro-substituted derivatives compared to bromo and fluoro analogues may be associated with a balance between electronic and steric effects; however, this interpretation remains qualitative. Similarly, the preference for ortho substitution in Cl and Br derivatives may indicate a favorable orientation within the MAO-B binding site, although no direct structural evidence is provided in the present study. In contrast, the distinct trend observed for fluoro derivatives may reflect differences in electronic distribution due to the smaller size and higher electronegativity of fluorine. To contextualize the potency of the synthesized compounds, a comparison with previously reported chalcone-based MAO-B inhibitors was performed. The conjugated dione derivatives (CD series) showed moderate to low MAO-B inhibitory activity, and the strength of this activity was greatly affected by the kind of phenyl substitution. Of these, CD8 (4-fluorophenyl) had the strongest effect (IC₅₀ = 0.230 ± 0.01 µM), followed by CD4 (IC₅₀ = 0.307 ± 0.05 µM). CD7 (4-methoxy, IC₅₀ = 2.99 ± 0.48 µM), CD6 (4-bromo, IC₅₀ = 3.46 ± 0.31 µM), and CD5 (4-nitrophenyl, IC₅₀ = 6.01 ± 0.64 µM) (Tabe S1) all had weaker effects43. This pattern shows that tiny electron-withdrawing groups make MAO-B inhibitory action more likely in this series. To provide context for these results, a direct comparison was conducted with a literature-reported chalcone-based MAO-B inhibitor that is closely related. Compound 3i (dihydrobenzofuran–thiophene conjugated chalcone) showed high activity (IC₅₀ = 0.092 µM)45. The benzofuran–chalcone hybrids created in this investigation had similar strength, with BF2 (IC₅₀ = 0.082 ± 0.021 µM) and BF5 (IC₅₀ = 0.095 ± 0.016 µM) both matching or slightly surpassing the activity of 3i. This shows a concentrated comparison that shows how much better BF derivatives are than the CD series. These findings indicate that the inclusion of the benzofuran moiety into the chalcone framework creates an efficient scaffold for MAO-B inhibition and broadens the structural diversity of chalcone-based inhibitors.

Enzyme kinetics

Enzyme kinetics and inhibition studies were performed using five substrate concentrations and three inhibitor concentrations of BF2. The five concentrations were selected in the range between ~ 1/4x and 4x Km to minimize the data errors in a linear LB plot transformed from a nonlinear regression Michaelis-Menten equation, considering very sensitive to data error in a particular range of substrate concentration in the LB plot. In the Lineweaver-Bulk plot, BF2 exhibited competitive MAO-B inhibition (Fig. 4A), showing unchanged Vmax values at three inhibitor concentrations to the control. From the secondary plots, the Ki value was determined to be 0.083 ± 0.0056 μm (Fig. 4B).

Time-dependency and reversibility studies

BF2 showed an instantaneous binding pattern in a time-dependency inhibition test by measuring the residual activityafter preincubationfor up to 30 min (Fig. 5A). The reversibility of MAO-B inhibition by BF2 was analyzed by dialysis after 30 min of pre-incubation. The concentrations of BF2, safinamide, and pargyline used were 2 times their IC50 values (0. 17, 0.038, and 0.22 µM, respectively). Recovery patterns were compared using undialyzed (AU) and dialyzed (AD) relative activities. The inhibition of MAO-B by compound BF2 was recovered from 34.42% to 69.40%. The recovery value of the compound was similar to that of safinamide (reversible type, 22.18–72.54%) and could be distinguished from that of pargyline (irreversible type, 15.70–17.76%) (Fig. 5B). From these results, no slow-binding behavior was observed in time-dependency test and inhibition was recovered to the level of the reversible inhibitor in dialysis assay. Based on these results, it is suggested that BF2 is a reversible MAO-B inhibitor.

Parallel artificial membrane permeability assay (PAMPA) for blood-brain barrier (BBB) permeation study

Compounds (BF2 and BF5) showed a significant degree of permeation and CNS bioavailability as determined bythe PAMPA. Specifically, BF2 (Pe = 2.43 ± 0.12 × 10–6 cm/s) is now described as exhibiting moderate/uncertain BBB permeability (CNS±), whereas BF5 (Pe = 9.01 ± 0.09 × 10–6 cm/s) is correctly classified as highly permeable (CNS+) (Table 2). Brain penetration is crucial for the effective delivery of CNS drugs. In this experiment, the PAMPA-BBB assay was used to evaluate the brain permeability of various derivatives. Penetration depth was determined by calculating the effective permeability (Log Pe) of each compound using a specific formula. Compounds with Pe values below 2.0 × 10–6 cm/s are categorized as likely non-penetrating (CNS-), while those with Pe values exceeding 4.0 × 10–6 cm/s are considered potentially permeable (CNS+).

Effect of BF2 and BF5 on cell viability in L929

The L929 cell line, derived from the subcutaneous connective tissue of mice (Mus musculus) and was found to be fibroblast-like. Due to their sensitivity, L929 cells are frequently used to assess the cytotoxicity of various substances, including chemicals, medications, and biomaterials. Cell viability was 75% for BF2 up to 25 µg/mL and 82% for BF5 at 50 µg/mL (Fig. 6). The results led to the determination of IC50 values for BF2 and BF5, which were 55.84 µg/mL (197.51 µM) and > 100 µg/mL (> 305.65 µM), respectively. Morphological alterations in the cells at each dose were observed, and a figure is included in the Supporting Information (Figures S1 and S2). Overall, these results suggest that BF2 and BF5 molecules are non-cytotoxic to the cell line L929 in the ranges higher than 50 and 12.5 µg/mL, respectively.

The analysis confirmed that all molecules fully comply with the Lipinski’s Rule of Five. Oral bioavailability, a critical factor for drug efficacy, was supported by all molecules having less than five rotatable bonds (RB) and a polar surface area (PSA) below 200 Å², and ensuring oral bioavailability safely. Additionally, the human oral absorption (HOA) descriptor indicated a high predicted oral absorption score of 3 for all compounds. The Caco-2 permeability values suggested efficient intestinal absorption, while QPlogKhsa values ranging from − 1.5 to 1.5 reflected favorable human serum albumin binding capacity. TheQlogP values highlighted moderate solubility, and QPlogBB suggested all compounds possess the ability to permeate the BBB. The polar surface area and logP further supported that the compounds exhibit consistent solubility and are capable of crossing the BBB.

The predicted ADMET descriptors suggest that all the examined compounds exhibit generally acceptable drug-like properties within the standard range for small-molecule therapeutic candidates. The lead compounds BF2 and BF5 had better permeability while moderate solubility necessitate further tuning during lead optimization. Also, the anticipated QPlogHERG values suggest a possible risk of hERG channel blockage, necessitating further experimentimal assessment.

Molecular Docking

Enzymatic inhibitory studies showed that compounds BF2 and BF5 were more potent against hMAO-B than hMAO-A. Molecular docking was performed to understand the interaction and affinity of the most potent ligands, BF2 and BF5 for the binding sites of both the hMAO-B and hMAO-A enzymes. The Protein Data Bank was used to obtain the crystal structures of hMAO-B (PDB ID: 2V5Z) and hMAO-A (PDB ID: 2Z5X). The docking was validatedby redocking co-crystalized Safinamide in hMAO-B andHarmine in hMAO-A shows an acceptable RMSD of 1.95and 0.34Å, respectively. The docking scores and binding energy analysis clearly showed that both compounds showed better binding affinities toward MAO-B than toward MAO-A (Table 4). Molecular dynamics simulation

To assess the stability of the BF2 ligand in the MAO-B receptor’s active region, the complex underwent MD modeling. We used the complex’s root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) and root-mean-square fluctuation (RMSF) to assess the average atom displacement in order to analyze the stability of the docked complex. One of the most important factors for determining the stability and structural conformations of complexes during simulation is RMSD. According to RMSD analysis, the ligand and protein C-alpha were monitored within a reasonable range for a lengthy simulation period (200 ns). The ligand (red) RMSD was constant with few deviations, in contrast to the protein RMSD. The average protein RMSD was 2.70Å, with a range of 1.06 to 3.87Å (Fig. 9). With the exception of a little fluctuation, the protein-specific RMSD during the simulation remained consistent, peaking at 3.87Å at 130 ns before stabilizing. An additional metric used to assess the protein’s side chain flexibility is the RMSF. The stability of the complex can be decreased by the ligand binding that is influenced by the side chains’ flexibility at the active site. Although structural domains such as coiled coils and protein structure randomness are extremely flexible regions that might result in larger RMSF values, the fluctuation is reduced with a lower RMSF value. By calculating the RMSF for every protein amino acid residue, the simulation assessed the protein system’s flexibility.MAO-B residues 480–498 exhibited a greater volatility. During the binding process, the atoms in the RMSF ligand’s benzoyl ring (Fig. 9B) displayed minor oscillations. The 22 amino acid residues that had an RMSF value of less than 1Å reacted with the ligand. The interaction histograms of BF2 and MAO-B revealed halogen bonding and hydrophobic interactions (Fig. 9C). The quantity of individual interactions between the ligand and amino acids was normalized over a 200 ns trajectory. Tyr326 (hydrophobic), Tyr398 (hydrophobic), Leu171 (hydrophobic), Cys172 (halogen bond), and Ile199 (hydrophobic) are among the important amino acids that interact with BF2. Nearly 0.6 was the measured proportion of interactions with Tyr326. Tyr326’s hydrophobic interaction with the MAO-B active site was important, as was previously noted36. The lead chemical BF2 is predicted to inhibit MAO-B overall based on the trajectory analysis and comprehensive MD simulation.

Conclusion

This study focused on the synthesis and MAO inhibition of ten halogenated benzofuran (BF) derivatives. The level of MAO-B inhibition was higher for all BF compounds. With competitive and reversible modes of action, BF2 and BF5 are selectiveMAO-B inhibitors. Both candidates exhibited moderate to good CNS drug-likeness according to the PAMPA assay. The MTT test on the L929 cell line established that BF2 and BF5 are not cytotoxic, with IC50 values of 55.84 µg/mL and > 100 µg/mL, respectively. Molecular docking and dynamic simulation suggestedthat lead molecule (BF2) have excellent binding interactions in the inhibitor-binding cavity of MAO-B, with favorableinsilico ADME predictions. Although more research is needed to determine their therapeutic value, these compounds show selective MAO-B inhibition in vitro and could be preliminary lead candidates deserving of more study.

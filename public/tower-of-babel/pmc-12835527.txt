Reviving expired pharmaceuticals using 2-[(butylamino)propan-2-yl]phosphinic acid for corrosion protection of carbon steel in 1.0 M HCl
Authors: Reda Abdel-Hameed, Ashraf M. Ashmawy, Mahmoud AlElaimi, Nagah M. Abourashed, Odeh A. O. Alshammari, Kaseb D. Alanazi, M. A. Deyab
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study primarily focuses on how the expired pharmaceutical 2-[(Butylamino)propan-2-yl]phosphinic acid (BAMPA), which contains both an amino and phosphinic acid group, can form a highly effective protective layer on the carbon steel surface using potentiodynamic polarization (PP) and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) methods. Electrochemical experiments reveal that BAMPA adsorbs to carbon steel, producing a protective coating. At 500 ppm BAMPA, charge transfer resistance (Rct) rose from 18.88 to 160.1 Ω cm², while double layer capacitance (Cdl) decreased, indicating a reduced surface area for corrosion. The inhibition efficiency reached 95.20% at 500 ppm BAMPA. Theoretical calculations of electronic properties, including EHOMO (− 4.106 eV), ELUMO (− 3.279 eV), and an energy gap of 0.827 eV, support BAMPA’s high reactivity and potential for effective electron transfer. These findings confirm BAMPA’s potential as a viable corrosion inhibitor.

Introduction

Corrosion remains one of the main alarming problems in industries, especially since carbon steel is predominantly applied in view of its mechanical features and cost1–4. The degradation of carbon steel in such an aggressive environment, like acidic or saline, may cause equipment failure, financial losses, and even accidents. Therefore, developing effective inhibitors will be of prime importance because they would protect carbon steel from degradation and extend its service performance in such media5–13. Organic corrosion inhibitors are proving to be one of the prime alternatives mainly due to their effectiveness in adsorption on metal surfaces, thus forming a protective layer that minimizes corrosion processes14–16. Of these, the compounds that have especially come to the fore comprise heteroatoms such as nitrogen, oxygen, phosphorus, and sulfur, among others, with strong affinities toward metal surfaces and with adsorption stabilized by chemical bonding17–24. Due to the particularities of electronic and structural factors, a growing interest in the chemistry of phosphinic acids and their derivatives is attributed to the development of powerful protection systems25,26. Expired pharmaceuticals may serve as possible corrosion inhibitors and provide a cost-effective alternative to the often arduous process of pharmaceutical disposal27–30.

2-[(Butylamino)propan-2-yl]phosphinic Acid (Butaphosphanis) an organic phosphorus compound used primarily in veterinary medicine as a metabolic stimulant and tonic. It is most commonly administered as an injectable solution, often in combination with cyanocobalamin (vitamin B12). Its mechanism of action involves supplying readily available organic phosphorus to the body, which plays a crucial role in numerous metabolic processes, including energy production (ATP synthesis), muscle function, and nervous system activity. Butaphosphan is widely used to treat and prevent metabolic disorders such as hypophosphatemia, milk fever, and ketosis, and to support animals during periods of high stress, overexertion, and disease. It is also employed to enhance productivity in livestock by improving feed efficiency, weight gain, and milk yield31,32. Recent research has pointed out that a dual approach, involving theoretical calculations and electrochemical experiments, is a more efficient way of completely elucidating the mechanisms of interaction between inhibitors and a metal surface33–35. DFT and molecular dynamics simulations give insight into molecular-level interactions, while electrochemical techniques confirm these under realistic conditions. The combination of DFT and molecular dynamics simulations with experimental electrochemical methods results in a complete framework for understanding the molecular interactions and practical implications of corrosion inhibitor36.

Based on good performance, which was achieved with phosphinic and phosphinic acids as corrosion inhibitors, this paper is a continuation of the effectiveness of 2-[(butylamino)propan-2-yl]phosphinic acid (BAMPA) as a corrosion inhibitor for carbon steel. This compound is postulated to enhance adsorption efficiency and corrosion protection owing to the molecular structure that possesses both the phosphinic acid group and amino functionality. The integration of quantum chemical modeling with electrochemical techniques provides a comprehensive understanding of its inhibition mechanism37. BAMPA holds promise as an active pharmaceutical ingredient due to its unique properties and potential applications in various fields, including pharmaceutical formulations. According to the manufacturer, the BAMPA utilized in this investigation came from pharmaceutical formulations having a 28-day shelf life after initial use. After this time, any unused BAMPA is considered expired. This classification is crucial to our research because it emphasizes the proper use of medications that have expired for therapeutic purposes in research settings, especially when assessing their possible functional qualities, such corrosion inhibition.

Experimental and computational part

[2-(Butylamino)propan-2-yl]phosphinic acid (BAMPA), molecular weight 179.2 was obtained from Egyptian for Veterinary Medicine & Agriculture Industries (Fig. 1). The manufacturer indicated a shelf life of 28 days after first use for BAMPA. This short shelf life makes it relevant to categorize any unused medication beyond this timeframe as expired. The BAMPA used in this study to prepare the corrosion inhibitor solution was obtained 28 days after its initial opening, in accordance with the manufacturer’s shelf life guidelines. High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (Shimadzu Co., Japan) was used to confirm the presence of expired BAMPA within the acceptable percentage (around 97%).

The carbon steel samples were composed primarily of iron, with the remaining minor percentages distributed among manganese, carbon, aluminum, chromium, phosphors, nickel, copper, and silicon at weight percentages of 0.853%, 0.093%, 0.032%, 0.025%, 0.014%, 0.013%, 0.012%, and 0.011%, respectively. The samples were abraded sequentially with silicon carbide papers of grades 400, 600, 800, 1000, and 1200 grit, followed by polishing with alumina slurry to achieve a mirror-like surface. They were then rinsed thoroughly with distilled water, degraded with ethanol, and dried under a warm air stream before each experiment. The procedure follows the guidelines outlined in ASTM G1-0338.

Electrochemical tests

The electrochemical experiments were carried out utilizing the glass cell. It consists of three electrodes: a counter electrode made of platinum wire, a reference electrode made of saturated calomel (SCE), and a working electrode of carbon steel. The measurements were carried out with the Gamry Potentiostat Reference 3000 and corrosion software. The potentiodynamic polarisation (PP) was performed at a scan rate of 5 mV s− 1. The electrochemical impedance test was performed at frequencies ranging from 100 kHz to 0.1 Hz, at open-circuit potential with an amplitude AC voltage of 10 mV. Before polarization and impedance studies, the open-circuit potential of the working electrode is monitored with time till achieving open-circuit potential (OCP), which is typically 60 min.

Theoretical models

The inhibitor was optimized in their gaseous phase, and the quantum chemical indices of these inhibitors were computed using DMol³ in Materials Studio, version 2017. The calculations used GGA with the PBE functional, and the basis set used was DNP-3.5 (Double Numerical plus Polarization) with fine-quality tolerance parameters for high accuracy. The HOMO and LUMO energies (EHOMO & ELUMO), bandgap energy (ΔEg = ELUMO – EHOMO), ionization potential (I = − EHOMO), electron affinity (A = − ELUMO), chemical hardness (ɳ = (I − A)/2), chemical softness (S = 1/2ɳ), electronegativity (χ = (I + A)/2), electrophilicity index (ω = χ2/2ɳ), chemical potential (µ =−χ), and fraction of the electron transferred (∆N = (ØFe − χcompd)/2ɳcompd) were calculated and extracted39,40.

Result and discussion

Electrochemical study

The Nyquist and Bode plots (Figs. 2 and 3 ) provide valuable insights into the electrochemical behavior of carbon steel in 1.0 M HCl both in the absence and presence of varying concentrations of BAMPA (from 100 ppm to 500 ppm). The Nyquist plots typically exhibit a semicircular shape, representing charge transfer resistance Rct and double layer capacitance Cdl41,42. In the absence of the BAMPA, the semicircle is smaller, indicating lower Rct and higher corrosion rates. As the concentration of the BAMPA increases, the semicircle expands, signifying higher Rct values and enhanced corrosion resistance. The increase in the diameter of the semicircle with increasing BAMPA concentration indicates a significant reduction in the corrosion rate43. This behavior reflects the formation of a protective film on the metal surface, which hinders the electrochemical reactions responsible for corrosion44. A shift phase angle to higher frequencies by adding BAMPA indicates that the addition of the corrosion BAMPA alters the kinetics of these processes. This shift suggests that the BAMPA enhances the stability of the protective layer formed on the carbon steel surface. As the BAMPA concentration increases, the electrochemical reactions become more controlled, allowing for faster response times to applied potentials.

The Bode magnitude plots show an increase in impedance with higher BAMPA concentrations. This trend suggests that the BAMPA effectively reduces the electrolyte’s ability to penetrate the protective layer, thereby enhancing overall corrosion resistance. At higher concentrations (e.g., 500 ppm BAMPA), the magnitude of impedance is significantly greater than in the absence of the BAMPA, indicating effective barrier properties. The observed difference in inhibition efficiency (88.21% from EIS and 95.20% from PP) can be attributed to several factors: Both EIS and PP are subject to non-ideal behaviors that can affect the accuracy of the measurements. EIS captures the electrochemical system’s response over a range of frequencies, which may include contributions from capacitive elements and other dynamic processes that can mask the true resistance to corrosion. The protective film formed by the inhibitor may exhibit varying resistance based on the measurement technique. EIS may reflect a more complex impedance response due to interface effects and film inhomogeneity, while polarization may provide a clearer picture of the immediate effect of the inhibitor on corrosion currents. EIS is sensitive to mass transport limitations, especially at higher frequencies. If the diffusion of the inhibitor to the electrode surface is not optimal, EIS may yield lower inhibition efficiency. In contrast, polarization measurements, which are often conducted under conditions that may promote faster equilibrium, could show a higher efficiency. Polarization measurements might better capture the rapid kinetics of the corrosion process under the specific conditions used. EIS typically focuses on charge transfer resistance, while polarization evaluates current density. This variation in focus can lead to different estimates of efficiency.

HOMO–LUMO energy gap, in turn, implies excellent electronic mobility to facilitate easy interaction with metal surfaces. In the context of using BAMPA for corrosion protection of carbon steel in 1.0 M HCl, solvation effects and protonation can significantly influence the compound’s behavior and efficacy. In an acidic environment, protonation of functional groups in BAMPA can alter the electron density distribution and molecular orbital characteristics, thereby affecting the adsorption of the inhibitor onto the metal surface. Protonation may enhance the formation of a protective film on the steel surface through increased interaction with the positively charged groups, potentially improving corrosion resistance. Additionally, solvation effects can alter the stability and mobility of the inhibitor molecules, influencing their effectiveness in forming a barrier against corrosive agents. Despite the complexities introduced by solvation and protonation, gas-phase data provides valuable insights into the intrinsic properties of BAMPA. Gas-phase studies offer a clearer picture of the molecular orbitals and electronic structure of BAMPA without the influences of solvent interactions, serving as a critical baseline for understanding how those characteristics may be modulated in solution. This intrinsic data is essential for predicting how the compound might behave under real-world conditions and enables a deeper understanding of the underlying mechanisms of corrosion inhibition. Therefore, while solvation effects are crucial to consider in a corrosive environment, gas-phase data remains a fundamental tool for elucidating the electronic and structural aspects that govern the performance of BAMPA as an effective corrosion inhibitor.

The combination of the computed data and molecular characteristics of BAMPA molecule testifies to its superior potential to serve as a potent metal corrosion inhibitor.

The inhibition mechanism of BAMPA in protecting carbon steel from corrosion in a hydrochloric acid (HCl) environment involves the primary mechanism by which BAMPA inhibits corrosion is through its adsorption onto the carbon steel surface51,52. This process is influenced by the presence of functional groups in the molecule, particularly the phosphinic acid group and the butylamino moiety. The adsorption is facilitated by electrostatic interactions and possibly chemisorption, where the polar groups of BAMPA interact with the metal surface, forming a protective film (see Fig. 8). Once adsorbed, BAMPA forms a protective layer that acts as a barrier between the metal surface and the corrosive HCl solution. This film reduces the availability of active sites on the metal for oxidation reactions, thereby decreasing the corrosion rate. The inhibition efficiency values demonstrate the potential of these compounds to mitigate corrosion in acidic environments, which is critical for protecting metal substrates in industrial applications. Among the listed inhibitors, BAMPA exhibits the highest inhibition efficiency at 95.2%, surpassing all other compounds. This indicates its superior ability to form a protective layer on carbon steel, effectively preventing corrosion in aggressive acidic conditions.

Conclusion

In conclusion, 2-[(Butylamino)propan-2-yl]phosphinic acid (BAMPA) exhibits excellent corrosion inhibition performance for carbon steel in 1.0 M HCl. The electrochemical results indicate a substantial increase in charge transfer resistance and a marked decrease in corrosion rates, affirming BAMPA’s effectiveness as a corrosion inhibitor. The theoretical analysis of its electronic characteristics reveals a favorable energy profile for electron transfer, further supporting its role in corrosion mitigation. With an inhibition efficiency reaching 95.20%, BAMPA presents a promising solution for corrosion control in industrial applications. Future work will focus on further exploring its long-term stability and practical applications in various corrosive environments.

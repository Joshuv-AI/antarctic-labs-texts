Calculation method for brittle fracture of functional gradient materials
Authors: Cong Qi, Ai-guo Pi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractCombined the phase field model with the wavelet dummy node-virtual crack closure technique (WDN-VCCT) used for stress intensity factors (SIFs) calculation. Calculated the node displacement using an improved brittle fracture phase field method, established the relationship between node displacement and node force using WDN-VCCT, and calculated the SIFs at the crack tip. The correctness and accuracy of the proposed method were verified through functional gradient material (FGM) tensile experiment. The influence of crack inclination angle, crack position and gradient index on mechanical response, and SIFs values at the crack tip was discussed. This study provides important computational tools for estimating the service life of FGMs and important references for structural optimization design methods.Keywords: Brittle fracture, Functional gradient materials, Phase field, Stress intensity factor, CrackSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Functional gradient materials (FGMs) are new type of composite materials, and the composition and mechanical properties gradually transition from the upper surface to the lower surface of the structure. FGMs have the advantages of being designable and customizable, and have been widely used in fields such as aerospace, nuclear power generation, sensors, optoelectronic devices, and automotive engine components1–11. Studying the damage tolerance calculation of FGMs is crucial for preventing damage caused by cracks and optimizing structural design. FGMs can significantly reduce the interlayer stress of materials, and due to its superior mechanical properties, FGMs are rapidly developing and becoming more advanced in preparation technology. For example, as a dual ceramic FGM, it retains the high strength and hardness mechanical properties of itself while also considering the toughening mechanism and impact resistance of . In the future, it can be used for lightweight armor protection materials, cooling thrust chambers for rocket engines, and other fields. In practical engineering, predicting the reliability of materials is crucial. Preventing damage caused by cracks is a key step in the design process. The fracture behaviors of FGMs are complex due to the change of material properties, so it is very challenging to simulate the fracture process and calculate fracture parameters. It is essential to analyze cracking mechanism of FGMs in structural design.

Among the numerous prediction methods for crack propagation trajectories, the fracture phase field method12–17 is an advanced method for analyzing crack initiation, propagation, branching, and multi crack propagation behavior. It mainly introduces phase field parameters and automatically tracks the evolution process of cracks in a dispersed manner, without the need for special treatment of the geometric configuration of cracks, greatly reducing the implementation complexity in the calculation process. Related coupled physical processes can be introduced into the phase field equation to expand the applicability of this method. The phase field method can be applied to different research fields to solve multi physics coupling problems, such as thermal mechanical fracture18–31, hydraulic fracturing32–40, and piezoelectric material fracture41–45. In fracture mechanics, stress intensity factors (SIFs) at the crack tip are important parameters for evaluating the degree of damage to structures with cracks. The key point of crack mechanics analysis is to accurately calculate the SIFs at the crack tip in the dangerous area46–50. Scholars have studied the calculation of SIFs for FGMs, The displacement extrapolation technique (DET), displacement correlation technique (DCT) and generalized displacement correlation (GDC) methods were used to calculate the mixed-mode SIFs of FGMs in references51–54. The -integral method was proposed to calculate the crack tip SIFs of FGMs under mixed mode loading55. The finite element method was used to simulate the mixed mode crack growth trajectory of FGMs in references56,57. There are many methods for calculating SIFs at the crack tip, but due to the influence of singularity at the crack tip, the convergence and accuracy of the calculation results are low. The improved wavelet dummy node-virtual crack closure technique (WDN-VCCT)58 combines the theory of linear elastic fracture with the analysis method of elastic stress field at the crack tip, effectively avoiding the oscillation of SIFs values caused by boundary mesh correction problems and compensating for the shortcomings of traditional finite element methods. The WDN-VCCT method only requires the use of node forces and node displacements, with a simple calculation formula. Using it to calculate SIFs at the crack tip of heterogeneous materials has high accuracy. This work aims to link the phase field method for simulating cracking behavior with the calculation method of SIFs at the crack tip, and builds a communication bridge between damage mechanics and fracture mechanics. It is significant and valuable to calculate SIFs with phase field model.

This work improved the brittle fracture phase field method for accurately simulating the initiation and propagation of FGMs cracks. Calculated the node displacement using an improved phase field method, established the relationship between node displacement and node force using the WDN-VCCT method, and calculated the SIFs at the crack tip of FGMs. The accuracy and convergence of the calculation results were verified by comparing and analyzing the SIFs theory solution with the proposed method. The correctness of the improved phase field method for predicting cracking behavior was verified through double notch specimen tensile experiment. Under mixed loading conditions, the effects of crack inclination angle, crack position, and gradient index on the mechanical response of the structure and the SIFs at the crack tip were studied. The method proposed in this research is of great significance and application value for improving the damage tolerance analysis and structural safety detection of FGMs.

Conclusion

The main progress of this work is to use an improved brittle fracture phase field method to calculate node displacement, establish the relationship between node displacement and node force using WDN-VCCT method, and calculate SIFs at the crack tip. Combined with FGM brittle fracture experiment, the sensitivity and convergence of the mesh size for calculating SIFs at the crack tip were compared and verified. Compared with analytical solutions, the error in calculating SIFs using the method proposed in this paper can be controlled within 3.5%, and the calculation accuracy can meet the engineering requirements. The results show that the improved phase field method predicts highly consistent with experimental results, and the WDN-VCCT method is accurate and reliable for calculating SIFs at the crack tip. The use of the damage calculation method proposed in this study can predict the service life of FGM and deepen the understanding of brittle fracture failure mechanisms.

Studied the influence of gradient changes in central inclined crack plate on the SIFs values at the crack tip and analyzed the underlying mechanism of failure. The influence of gradient index on cracking was studied for co-linear double crack plates. It is found that when the gradient index rises, the SIFs at the crack tip increase, which readily precipitate fracture. It has been demonstrated through phase field simulation and calculation of SIFs values at the crack tip that optimizing gradient indices can significantly enhance the ability of FGM to resist brittle fracture. The results obtained from this study can more effectively perform damage calculations on structures containing cracks and optimize the design of FGMs.

Yoav Segev

Abstract

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
z\in A,\ \lambda,\delta\in\mathbb{F},\ x_{\lambda}\in A_{\lambda}(z),\ y_{\delta}\in A_{\delta}(z).
If
\delta\neq\lambda,
then
(x_{\lambda},y_{\delta})=0.
In particular
x=(a,x)a+x_{0}+x_{\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}},
where
x\in A,\ x_{0}\in A_{0}(a),\ x_{\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}}\in A_{\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}}(a).

Proof.

If
\lambda=0,
then, by hypothesis,
\delta\neq 0,
and
(x_{\lambda},y_{\delta})=(x_{\lambda},\frac{1}{\delta}zy_{\delta})=\frac{1}{\delta}(zx_{\lambda},y_{\delta})=\frac{1}{\delta}(0,y_{\delta})=0.
Assume
0\notin\{\lambda,\delta\}.
Then

(x_{\lambda},y_{\delta})=(\frac{1}{\lambda}zx_{\lambda},\ y_{\delta})=(\frac{1}{\lambda}x_{\lambda},\ zy_{\delta})=\frac{\delta}{\lambda}(x_{\lambda},y_{\delta}).

Hence if
\lambda\neq\delta,
then
(x_{\lambda},y_{\delta})=0.

∎

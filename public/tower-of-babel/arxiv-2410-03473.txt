1.
Introduction

For
T>0
, the approximate formula for
N(T)
,
the number of zeros
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
of the Riemann zeta-function
\zeta(s)

in the rectangle
0<\beta<1
,
0<\gamma\leq T
,
was given by
[
6
, Chapter 15]
of the form

\displaystyle N(T)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}-\frac{T}{2\pi}+\frac{7}{8}+S(T)+O(T^{-1}),

where

(1.1)

\displaystyle S(T)=\frac{1}{\pi}\arg\zeta\big(\frac{1}{2}+iT\big).

The argument is defined by continuous variation along the horizontal
segment from
\infty+iT
to
1/2+iT
, starting at
\infty+iT
with the value
0
.
The complicated behavior of
S(t)
is an interesting topic studied by many number theorists
(see, for example,
[
1
,
5
,
18
,
30
]
).

In 1940s, Selberg
[
26
,
27
]
showed that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}S(t)^{2n}\mathrm{d}t=\frac{(2n)!}{n!(2\pi)^{2n}}T(\log\log T)^{n}+O\big(T(\log\log T)^{n-1/2}\big),

which can imply, on average sense,
|S(t)|
has order of magnitude
\sqrt{\log\log T}
.
In addition, for Dirichlet
L
-functions
L(s,\chi)
with primitive
\chi
and

S(t,\chi)=\frac{1}{\pi}\arg L\big(\frac{1}{2}+it,\chi\big),

Selberg
[
28
]
investigated the behavior of
S(t,\chi)
and obtained, for prime modulus
q
and fixed
t>0
,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi\bmod q}S(t,\chi)^{2n}=\frac{(2n)!}{n!(2\pi)^{2n}}q(\log\log q)^{n}+O\big(q(\log\log q)^{n-1/2}\big),

where
*
denotes the summation goes through the primitive characters
\chi(\bmod\,q)
.
Note that the counterparts of (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) for the odd-order moments
can also be obtained by making a minor adaptation in Selberg’s techniques.

For higher rank
L
-functions, there are relevant investigations.
In 1997, Hejhal–Luo
[
10
]
considered the generalization of (
1.3
)
to the framework of
\Gamma
-automorphic
eigenfunctions
\psi
of the hyperbolic Laplacian
\Delta
for the full modular group
\Gamma=\rm SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Let
L(s,\psi)
be the usual Hecke–Maass
L
-function associated to
\psi
, and define

\displaystyle S(t,\psi)=\frac{1}{\pi}\arg L\big(\frac{1}{2}+it,\psi\big)

in the same fashion as in (
1.1
).
They established the analogs of (
1.2
) when
\psi
is Eisenstain series.
And, let
\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be an orthonormal basis of the space of
Maass cusp forms for
\rm SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
such that

\Delta u_{j}=\lambda_{j}u_{j}
with Laplacian eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}=\frac{1}{4}+t_{j}^{2}
(
t_{j}\geq 0
),
and each
u_{j}
is an eigenfunction of all the Hecke operators.
In this case
\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
consists of even Maass forms and odd Maass forms according to
u_{j}(-\overline{z})=u_{j}(z)

and
u_{j}(-\overline{z})=-u_{j}(z)
.
Each
u_{j}(z)
has the Fourier expansion

\displaystyle u_{j}(z)=\sqrt{y}\sum_{n\neq 0}^{\infty}\rho_{j}(n)K_{it_{j}}(2\pi|n|y)e(nx),\quad z=x+iy\in\mathbb{H},

where
K_{\alpha}(x)
is the
K
-Bessel function of order
\alpha
and

\displaystyle\rho_{j}(\pm n)=\rho_{j}(\pm 1)\lambda_{j}(n),\quad n\geq 1.

Here
\rho_{j}(1)\neq 0
, and
\lambda_{j}(n)
is the eigenvalue of the
n
-th Hecke operator
T_{n}
.
If
\psi
is the Maass cusp form
u_{j}
, under Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH),
they showed that for fixed
t,\beta>0
and any large parameter
T>0
,

(1.4)

\begin{split}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{-\beta(t_{j}-T)^{2}}|\nu_{j}(1)|^{2}(S(t,u_{j}))^{2n}=\frac{2\pi^{-3/2}}{\sqrt{\beta}}\frac{(2n)!}{n!(2\pi)^{2n}}T(\log\log T)^{n}+O_{t,n,\beta}\big(T(\log\log T)^{n-1/2}\big),\end{split}

where
\nu_{j}(1)
is defined by (
2.5
) below.
These spectral results have been recently extended, with a
\rm\operatorname{GL}_{3}
analog obtained by Liu–Liu
[
20
]
under GRH,
which was generalized by Chen–Lau–Wang
[
3
, Corollary 1.2]
to
\rm\operatorname{GL}_{n}
,
n\geq 3
under the same hypothesis.
Moreover, for the holomorphic case, by means of the weighted version of the zero-density estimate,
Liu–Shim
[
21
]
and Sun–Wang
[
25
]
successively obtained the unconditional analogues in the weight aspect and in the level aspect, respectively.

In this paper, we are devoted to get the asymptotic formula (
1.4
) after removing the GRH.
Assume that
u_{j}
is an even Hecke–Maass cusp form as above, the
L
-series associated to it is defined by

\begin{split}L(s,u_{j})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{j}(n)}{n^{s}}&=\prod_{p}(1-\lambda_{j}(p)p^{-s}+p^{-2s})^{-1}\\
&=\prod_{p}(1-\alpha_{j}(p)p^{-s})^{-1}(1-\beta_{j}(p)p^{-s})^{-1},\quad\text{for}\,\,\operatorname{Re}(s)>1,\end{split}

which admits an analytic continuation to the whole complex plane and satisfies the functional equation

\displaystyle\Lambda(s,u_{j})=\pi^{-s}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{s+it_{j}}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s-it_{j}}{2}\right)L(s,u_{j})=\Lambda(1-s,u_{j}).

Here we have
\alpha_{j}(p)\beta_{j}(p)=1
and, for convenience,
|\alpha_{j}(p)|\geq 1
.
It is not known that the
L
-functions of Maass cusp forms satisfy the Ramanujan–Petersson conjecture, the
current best known estimate is due to Kim–Sarnak
[
15
]
,

(1.5)

\displaystyle|\lambda_{j}(n)|\leq n^{\vartheta}\tau(n),\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

with
\vartheta=7/64
and
\tau(n)
being the divisor function. The Ramanujan–Petersson conjecture predicts
\vartheta=0
.
Note that the Fourier coefficients
\lambda_{j}(n)
are real and satisfy
the following Hecke relation,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}(m)\lambda_{j}(n)=\sum\limits_{d|(m,n)}\lambda_{j}\left(\frac{mn}{d^{2}}\right),\quad\text{for}\,\,m,n\geq 1.

Now we redefine
S_{j}(t)
by

\displaystyle S_{j}(t):=\frac{1}{\pi}\arg L(1/2+it,u_{j}),

where the argument
\arg L(1/2+it,u_{j})
is obtained by continuous variation along the line
\operatorname{Im}(s)=t
from

\sigma=+\infty
to
\sigma=1/2
.
Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
t>0
be fixed and
T
be any large parameter.
For
M
a parameter with the constraint
T^{\varepsilon}\leq M\leq T^{1-\varepsilon}
, we have

\begin{split}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{(t_{j}-T)^{2}}{M^{2}}}|\nu_{j}(1)|^{2}(S_{j}(t))^{n}=\frac{MT}{4\pi^{3/2}}C_{n}(\log\log T)^{n/2}+O_{t,n}\big(MT(\log\log T)^{(n-1)/2}\big),\end{split}

where
\nu_{j}(n)
is defined by (
2.5
) below and

\displaystyle C_{n}=\begin{cases}\frac{n!}{(n/2)!(2\pi)^{n}},\,&\textit{if }\,n\,\,\text{is even},\\
0,\,&\textit{if }\,n\,\,\text{is odd}.\end{cases}

Besides, we define the following probability measure

\mu_{TM}
on
\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\mu_{TM}(E):=\left(\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{|t_{j}-T|\leq M}|\nu_{j}(1)|^{2}\textbf{1}_{E}\left(\frac{S_{j}(t)}{\sqrt{\log\log T}}\right)\right)\bigg/\left(\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{|t_{j}-T|\leq M}|\nu_{j}(1)|^{2}\right),

where
\textbf{1}_{E}
is the characteristic function on a Borel measurable set
E
in
\mathbb{R}
.
Then for every
a<b
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{TM}([a,b])=\sqrt{\pi}\int_{a}^{b}\exp(-\pi^{2}\xi^{2})\mathrm{d}\xi,

uniformly with respect to
M
.

Remark 1
.

For
u_{j}
an even Hecke–Maass cusp form of
\rm SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

with Laplacian eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}=\frac{1}{4}+t_{j}^{2}
,
our result indicates that
|S_{j}(t)|
has order of magnitude
\sqrt{\log\log T}
on average
for
t_{j}\asymp T
. In addition, as
T\rightarrow\infty
, the ratios
S_{j}(t)/\sqrt{\log\log T}
converges in distribution
to a normal distribution of mean
0

and variance
(2\pi^{2})^{-1}
.

\displaystyle I_{\nu}(z)=\frac{(z/2)^{\nu}}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+\nu)\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{\pm z\cos\theta}\sin^{2\nu}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta.

2.2.
Kuznetsov trace formula over even Hecke–Maass forms

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
h(t)
be an even function which is holomorphic in the strip
|\operatorname{Im}(t)|\leq\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon
and

h(t)\ll(1+|t|)^{-2-\varepsilon}
with some
\varepsilon>0
. Then for integers

,

\begin{split}&\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{j}h(t_{j})|\nu_{j}(1)|^{2}\lambda_{j}(m)\lambda_{j}(n)+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\tau_{it}(m)\tau_{it}(n)}{|\zeta(1+2it)|^{2}}h(t)\mathrm{d}t\\
=&\delta_{m,n}\mathcal{H}+\sum_{c>0}\frac{1}{2c}\left(S(m,n;c)\mathcal{H}^{+}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{c}\right)+S(-m,n;c)\mathcal{H}^{-}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{c}\right)\right).\end{split}

Here
\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}
restricts the sum to even Hecke–Maass cusp forms,

\tau_{\nu}(n)=\sum_{ab=n}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{\nu}
is the generalized divisor function,

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(t)\tanh(\pi t)t\,\mathrm{d}t,\\
\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{+}(x)&=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}J_{2it}(x)\frac{h(t)t}{\cosh(\pi t)}\,\mathrm{d}t,\\
\mathcal{H}^{-}(x)&=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K_{2it}(x)\sinh(\pi t)h(t)t\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Proof.

See e.g., Conrey–Iwaniec
[
4
]
.
∎

By

3.
An approximation of
S_{j}(t)

In this section we will prove several lemmas and derive an approximation of
S_{j}(t)
.
We denote by
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
a typical zero of
L(s,u_{j})
inside the critical strip, i.e.,
0<\beta<1
.
For
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
, we have the Dirichlet series expansion for the logarithmic derivative of
L(s,u_{j})
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,u_{j})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)C_{j}(n)}{n^{s}},

where
\Lambda(n)
denotes the von Mangoldt function, and

(3.2)

\displaystyle C_{j}(n)=\begin{cases}\alpha_{j}(p)^{m}+\beta_{j}(p)^{m},\,&\text{if}\,\,n=p^{m}\,\,\text{for a prime}\,\,p,\\
0,\,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Using the facts that

\displaystyle C_{j}(p^{m})=\alpha_{j}(p)^{m}+\alpha_{j}(p)^{-m},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lambda_{j}(p^{\nu})=\sum_{j=0}^{\nu}\alpha_{j}(p)^{\nu-2j},

we immediately check that

(3.3)

\displaystyle C_{j}(p)=\lambda_{j}(p),\quad\text{and}\quad C_{j}(p^{r})=\lambda_{j}(p^{r})-\lambda_{j}(p^{r-2}),\,\,\text{for}\,\,r\geq 2.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
x>1
. For
s\neq\rho
, and
s\neq\pm it_{j}-2m
, we have the following identity

\begin{split}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,u_{j})=&-\sum_{n\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{j}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{s}}-\frac{1}{\log^{2}x}\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{\rho-s}(1-x^{\rho-s})^{2}}{(\rho-s)^{3}}\\
&-\frac{1}{\log^{2}x}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\pm it_{j}-2m-s}(1-x^{\pm it_{j}-2m-s})^{2}}{(\pm it_{j}-2m-s)^{3}},\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\Lambda_{x}(n)=\begin{cases}\Lambda(n),\,&\textit{if }\,\,n\leq x,\\
\Lambda(n)\frac{\log^{2}(x^{3}/n)-2\log^{2}(x^{2}/n)}{2\log^{2}x},\,&\textit{if }\,\,x\leq n\leq x^{2},\\
\Lambda(n)\frac{\log^{2}(x^{3}/n)}{2\log^{2}x},\,&\textit{if }\,\,x^{2}\leq n\leq x^{3},\\
0,\,&\textit{if }\,\,n\geq x^{3}.\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

Recall the discontinuous integral

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\alpha)}\frac{y^{s}}{s^{3}}\mathrm{d}s=\begin{cases}\frac{\log^{2}y}{2},\,&\textit{if }\,\,y\geq 1,\\
0,\,&\textit{if }\,\,0<y\leq 1\end{cases}

for
\alpha>0
.
It follows from (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) that

\displaystyle-\log^{2}x\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{C_{j}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{s}}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\alpha)}\frac{x^{v}(1-x^{v})^{2}}{v^{3}}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s+v,u_{j})\mathrm{d}v,

where
\alpha=\max\{2,1+\operatorname{Re}(s)\}
. By moving the line of integration all way to the left, we
pick up the residues at
v=0
,
v=\rho-s
and
v=\pm it_{j}-2m-s
(
m=0,1,2,\cdots
), and deduce that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\alpha)}\frac{x^{v}(1-x^{v})^{2}}{v^{3}}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s+v,u_{j})\mathrm{d}v\\
=&\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,u_{j})\log^{2}x+\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{\rho-s}(1-x^{\rho-s})^{2}}{(\rho-s)^{3}}+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\pm it_{j}-2m-s}(1-x^{\pm it_{j}-2m-s})^{2}}{(\pm it_{j}-2m-s)^{3}}.\end{split}

Thus we complete the proof of lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For
s=\sigma+it
,
s^{\prime}=\sigma^{\prime}+it^{\prime}
such that
1/2\leq\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\leq 10
,
s\neq\rho
,
s^{\prime}\neq\rho
,
we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}\left(\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,u_{j})-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s^{\prime},u_{j})\right)=\operatorname{Im}\sum_{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{s-\rho}-\frac{1}{s^{\prime}-\rho}\right)+O(1),

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,u_{j})=\sum_{\rho}\frac{\sigma-\beta}{(\sigma-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}+O(\log(|t|+t_{j}+1)).

Proof.

By the Hadamard factorization theorem of the entire function
\Lambda(s,u_{j})
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,u_{j})=b_{j}+\sum_{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{s-\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\left(\frac{s+it_{j}}{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\left(\frac{s-it_{j}}{2}\right)+\log\pi,

for some
b_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
with
\operatorname{Re}(b_{j})=-\operatorname{Re}\sum_{\rho}\frac{1}{\rho}
(see
[
13
, Proposition 5.7 (3)]
).
Note that for
z\not\in-\mathbb{N}
,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(z)=-\gamma+\sum_{k\geq 1}\left(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{k-1+z}\right),

where
\gamma
is the Euler constant.
Using (
3.4
), we get that for
\frac{1}{4}\leq u
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(u+iv)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{v}{(k+u-1)^{2}+v^{2}}\ll 1.

Combined with the asymptotic formula of the logarithmic derivative of
\Gamma(z)
,

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(z)=\log z-\frac{1}{2z}+O_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}\right),\quad\text{for}\,\,|\arg z|\leq\pi-\varepsilon,

we complete the proof of lemma.
∎

Let
x\geq 4

&=i\int_{T-M\log T}^{T+M\log T}e^{ig(t)}\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{g^{\prime}(t)}\mathrm{d}t+O\big(MT^{-1/2}(\log T)^{-1}\big)\\
&=\int_{T-M\log T}^{T+M\log T}e^{ig(t)}\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(t)}{(g^{\prime}(t))^{2}}-\frac{2f^{\prime}(t)g^{\prime\prime}(t)}{(g^{\prime}(t))^{3}}\right)\mathrm{d}t+O\big(MT^{-1/2}(\log T)^{-1}\big)\\
&=\int_{T-M\log T}^{T+M\log T}e^{ig(t)}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(t)}{(g^{\prime}(t))^{2}}\mathrm{d}t+O\big(MT^{-1/2}(\log T)^{-1}\big)\\
&\cdots\\
&=c_{n}\int_{T-M\log T}^{T+M\log T}e^{ig(t)}\frac{f^{(n)}(t)}{(g^{\prime}(t))^{n}}\mathrm{d}t+O_{n}\big(MT^{-1/2}(\log T)^{-1}\big),\end{split}

where
|c_{n}|=1
. By the estimates for
f^{(n)}(t)
and
g^{\prime}(t)
, and the assumption
M>T^{\varepsilon}
,
we take sufficiently large
n
to obtain

\begin{split}I(Y,T)\ll MT^{-1/2}(\log T)^{-1}.\end{split}

Thus the contribution to
\mathfrak{K}
through
\mathfrak{K}_{2}^{-}
is

\begin{split}&\ll MT^{-1/2}(\log T)^{-1}\sum_{c\leq\sqrt{mn}}\frac{(-m,n,c)^{1/2}c^{1/2}\tau(c)}{c}\ll MT^{-1/2}(\log T)^{-1}\sum_{c\leq\sqrt{mn}}\frac{(n,c)^{1/2}\tau(c)}{c^{1/2}}\\
&\ll MT^{-1/2}(\log T)^{-1}\sum_{d\leq\sqrt{mn}\atop d|n}\tau(d)\sum_{c\leq\sqrt{mn}/d}\frac{\tau(c)}{c^{1/2}}\ll MT^{\varepsilon}.\end{split}

Thus the proof of Lemma
2.2
is complete.

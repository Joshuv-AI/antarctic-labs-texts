NH600001, an etomidate analogue, provides gastrointestinal endoscopy sedation/anesthesia and reduces adrenocortical depression: two randomized controlled trials
Authors: Yingyong Zhou, Kaiming Duan, Shichao Luo, Shengping Liu, Qiongcan Li, Songhua Liu, Huajing Guo, Haitao Jiang, Guyan Wang, Huijun Wang, Zhiping Wang, Xiaobao Zhang, Canlin Sun, Qiang Wang, Wei Gao, Wangning Shangguan, Zhongyuan Xia, Ailin Luo, Chun Chen, Mengchang Yang, Wenjie Su, Xiaohua Zou, Yalan Li, Hao Wang, Yanjuan Huang, Ruping Dai, Qian Zhao, Shuan Jing, Linzhong Zhang, Wenjun Yan, Xingang Qin, Juan Wang, Jingli Chen, Jun Cao, Minsheng Zhang, Shuchun Yu, Wen Ouyang, Saiying Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo evaluate the efficacy and safety of NH600001, a novel etomidate derivative, in gastrointestinal endoscopy, as well as its impact on adrenocortical function, two multicenter, double-blind, randomized, controlled clinical trials(NH600001-21 [Phase II trial] and NH600001-31 [Phase III trial]) were conducted in China. In NH600001-21, 160 gastroscopy subjects were randomized 1:1:1:1 to receive NH600001 (0.20, 0.25, or 0.30 mg/kg) and etomidate (0.30 mg/kg). In NH600001-31, 344 gastroscopy or colonoscopy subjects were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive NH600001 (0.25 mg/kg) and etomidate (0.30 mg/kg). The primary efficacy outcome was the rate of endoscopic success. Additionally, Safety and effects on adrenocortical function were also assessed. In NH600001-21, the 0.25 mg/kg NH600001 showed non-inferiority to etomidate based on the prespecified non-inferiority margin of −8% (Rate Difference [95% confidence interval], 5.0[−4.49 to 14.49]), whereas the 0.20 mg/kg and 0.30 mg/kg NH600001 did not meet statistical non-inferiority criteria (0[−11.54 to 11.54]; 0[−11.54 to 11.54]). The area under curve (AUC) of plasma cortisol change values within 0-4 h (∆Cortisol AUC0-4h) were significantly higher in the NH600001 treatment groups than in the etomidate group (P < 0.01). In NH600001-31, NH600001 (0.25 mg/kg) showed non-inferiority to etomidate (1.16[−0.44 to 2.76]). ∆Cortisol AUC0-4h in the NH600001 group, versus the etomidate group, were significantly higher (P < 0.001). Furthermore, NH600001 had a safety profile comparable to etomidate and elicited fewer myoclonus adverse events. In Conclusions, NH600001 has a sedative/anesthetic effect comparable to etomidate while reducing the risk of adrenocortical depression with a favorable safety profile. Introduction

Etomidate (R-1-(1-ethylphenyl)imidazole-5-ethyl ester), an imidazole-derived intravenous anesthetic, augments gamma-aminobutyric acid type A receptor (GABAAR) activity, decreases the activation threshold for GABA-mediated signaling and thereby induces anesthesia1,2. Etomidate is prized for its rapid onset, prompt recovery, weak cardiovascular inhibition, ability to lower intracranial pressure, and maintenance of cerebral perfusion3,4. Its linear pharmacokinetics and rapid clearance prevent drug accumulation, even with repeated dosing4. These features render etomidate suitable for elderly or critically ill patients with comorbidities like coronary artery disease, hypertension, or shock, and potentially for extended infusions. Notably, prolonged etomidate infusion has been associated with increased mortality in critically ill patients, primarily due to adrenocortical suppression5,6. Ledingham and Watt7,8 also reported that continuous etomidate infusion for sedation elevated the mortality of critically ill multiple trauma patients. More disappoiningly, even a single induction dose can induce adrenocortical suppression9–11, and may lead to longer hospital and ICU stays and increased ventilator days in trauma patients11. This limitation has constrained its clinical use as an anesthetic and sedative12–14. Therefore, developing compounds that preserve the advantages of etomidate while overcoming its disadvantages of inhibiting adrenocortical function remains a crucial objective in anesthetic pharmacology.

The suppression of adrenal cortex function by etomidate is due to its inhibition of an enzyme in the adrenal cortex, CYP11B1 (11β-hydroxylase), which is necessary for the synthesis of corticosteroids, including cortisol, corticosterone, and aldosterone4,15–18. The nitrogen atom in the imidazole ring of the etomidate molecule forms a non-covalent, reversible complex with the heme iron in the active site of CYP11B1, thereby reducing the enzyme activity16,19. Thus, reducing or eliminating this complex formation is key to mitigating etomidate-induced adrenocortical suppression15,18,20. NH600001 (ethyl R-(+)−4-fluoro-1-(1-Phenylethyl)−1H-imidazole-5-carboxylate), a fluorinated analog of etomidate, reduces the density of the electron cloud of nitrogen in the imidazole ring by introducing a fluorine atom at the 4 position of the imidazole ring with strong electron negativity, which reduces or eliminates the interaction between the solitary electron pair at the 3 position of the nitrogen ring and the iron ion at the active center of 11β-hydroxylase17,18,20. Thus, its inhibitory effect on corticosteroid synthesis is reduced. This modification makes NH600001 retain the anesthetic activity of etomidate and may reduce the inhibitory effect on corticoid synthesis, which has the potential for further development.

Preclinical pharmacological studies demonstrated that NH600001 has comparable anesthetic efficacy, anesthetic properties, and respiratory and hemodynamic effects to etomidate, retaining the advantages of etomidate. In addition, NH600001 has no significant inhibitory effect on adrenocortical function, which overcomes the deficiency of etomidate and has the potential for clinical application18,21. Further phase I clinical trials in healthy volunteers with a single increasing dose also showed that the anesthetic efficacy of NH600001 in the range of 0.04 to 0.40 mg/kg was equivalent to etomidate, while maintaining a favorable safety profile22. To further evaluate the efficacy and safety of NH60001, as well as its effect on adrenocortical function, two multicenter, randomized, double-blind, positive-controlled clinical trials were conducted in which subjects underwent gastroscopy and gastroscopy/colonoscopy, respectively.

Results

Participants

The NH600001-21 trial was conducted from March 30, 2023, to June 15, 2023, and the NH60001-31 trial was conducted from May 30, 2024, to September 29, 2024. A total of 734 participants were screened in the two studies (244 in NH600001-21 and 490 in NH60001-31), of whom 160 were randomly assigned to receive either Etomidate (n = 40), 0.20 mg/kg NH600001 (n = 40), 0.25 mg/kg NH600001 (n = 40), or 0.30 mg/kg NH600001 (n = 40) in the NH600001-21 trial, and 344 were randomly assigned to receive Etomidate (n = 172) or 0.25 mg/kg NH600001 (n = 172) in the NH600001-31 trial. In the NH600001-21 trial, all participants received study drug intervention, and in the NH600001-31 trial, one subject in the etomidate group withdrew from the study prematurely without intervention (Fig. 1). All the participants in both studies who received the study-drug intervention underwent at least one safety assessment. Participants’ baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1.

Primary efficacy outcome

In the NH600001-21 trial, the gastroscope success rates for Etomidate, 0.20 mg/kg, 0.25 mg/kg, and 0.30 mg/kg NH600001 treatment groups were 92.5%, 92.5%, 97.5%, and 92.5%, respectively. There was no significant difference among the groups (P > 0.05). Of note, the 0.25 mg/kgNH600001 group had the highest success rate, with the lower limit of the rate difference from the etomidate group greater than the prespecified non-inferiority value of −8% (Table 2). Similar results were obtained in a per-protocol set (PPS) sensitivity analysis, with the highest success rate in the 0.25 mg/kg NH600001 group (Table 2).

Secondary efficacy outcomes

In the NH600001-21 trial, the NH600001 treatment groups, versus the etomidate group, show no significant difference in the success rate of sedation/anesthesia, time to successful sedation/anesthesia, time to BIS ≤ 60, time to complete awakening after endoscopy, time to safe discharge after endoscopy, and proportion of rescue sedation (P > 0.05) (Table 3). The BIS index and MOAA/S score time curves for evaluating the depth of anesthesia/sedation are shown in Fig. 2a, b, respectively. Compared with etomidate group, BIS index of 0.20 mg/kg NH600001 group increased significantly (P < 0.001), BIS index of 0.3 mg/kg NH600001 group decreased significantly (P < 0.001), while MOAA/S score was not significant difference.

Discussion

NH600001, a novel etomidate analog, is developed to preserve the anesthetic activity and hemodynamic stability of etomidate while reducing the inhibitory effect on adrenocortical function. In two multicenter, double-blind, randomized controlled clinical trials, NH600001-21 and NH600001-31, NH600001 demonstrated comparable pharmacodynamic characteristics to etomidate regarding endoscopy success rates, onset time of sedation/anesthesia, and awakening time, while simultaneously reducing adrenocortical suppression. These findings indicate that NH600001 not only mitigates the adverse effects associated with adrenal suppression compared to traditional etomidate but also preserves equivalent anesthetic efficacy.

Gastrointestinal endoscopy plays an important role in the diagnosis and treatment of gastrointestinal diseases, such as gastrointestinal polyps, colorectal cancer, inflammatory bowel disease, and so on23. Sedation and analgesia have become the standard practices in endoscopic diagnosis and treatment. Effective and safe sedation and analgesia not only enhance patient comfort and increase the success rate of procedures, but also facilitate the performance of complex endoscopic diagnostic and therapeutic interventions24,25. Meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials has confirmed that etomidate-opioid regimens can provide effective sedation for gastrointestinal endoscopy with a low risk of respiratory depression24. Our two studies, which used etomidate 0.30 mg/kg as a positive control in combination with alfentanil for gastrointestinal endoscopy, showed that the success rate of gastroscopy of 0.20–0.30 mg/kg NH600001 (92.5%–100%) was comparable to that of the etomidate control group (92.5%–98.8%), indicating that the anesthetic efficacy of NH600001 was comparable to that of etomidate. Further analysis showed that the NH600001 treatment groups did not differ significantly from etomidate in sedation/anesthesia success rate, time to BIS ≤ 60, time to complete awakening after endoscopy, time to safe discharge after endoscopy, and proportion of rescue sedation. In NH600001-31, the time to successful sedation/anesthesia was significantly lower in the NH600001 group than in the etomidate group. Although the 4-s difference is considerably smaller than the evaluation time window and has limited clinical significance, it nonetheless indicates, from a certain perspective, that the onset time of NH600001 is non-inferior to that of etomidate. These results all indicate that NH600001 is noninferior to etomidate in terms of onset time, duration, recovery time, and depth of sedation, further confirming that NH600001 preserves the anesthetic efficacy of etomidate. Preclinical studies by Jiang, et al.18,21 showed that NH600001 retained the anesthetic activity of etomidate by introducing a fluorine atom at position 4 of the imidazole ring, which is also consistent with our results.

Adrenocortical suppression is a major barrier to the clinical use of etomidate, whether administered as a single dose or as a continuous infusion5,6,9–11. Many studies have reported that continuous infusion of etomidate increases mortality in critically ill patients7,8, and even a single induction dose may prolong the length of hospital stay and ICU stay in trauma patients11. Although studies have shown that a single dose of etomidate does not increase mortality in patients undergoing cardiac surgery26, the suppressive effect of etomidate on the adrenal cortex still deserves sufficient attention. Studies have reported that single-dose etomidate is associated with a higher incidence of hospital-acquired pneumonia following cardiac surgery27, and that etomidate is an independent risk factor for hospital-acquired pneumonia in patients with trauma28. In our two clinical studies, a single intravenous administration of etomidate resulted in a significant decrease in plasma cortisol level (negative change from baseline) and a feedback-induced increase in plasma ACTH level (positive change from baseline), suggesting significant adrenocortical suppression. This is consistent with clinical reports that a single induction dose of etomidate leads to adrenocortical suppression9–11. In contrast, a single intravenous administration of NH600001 resulted in a small increase in the plasma cortisol level (a positive change from baseline) and a brief increase in the plasma ACTH level followed by a decrease to the baseline level, which suggests little effect on adrenocortical function. The brief increase of plasma ACTH induced by NH600001 may be a transient compensatory stress response under the feedback of the HPA axis. Of course, NH600001 may also have a very weak, transient adrenocortical suppression, which triggers a feedback plasma ACTH increase, and this suppression is then overcome. This is also consistent with the etomidate soft drug (Soft drugs are active isosteric–isoelectronic analogs of a lead compound that are deactivated in a predictable and controllable way after achieving their therapeutic role.) developed by Cotten et al17,20,29–33, but its reduction of adrenal cortex depression also introduces new problems. Carboetomidate, which had the imidazole ring responsible for adrenocortical suppression designed out, showed significantly slower onset of hypnosis, leading to its discontinuation from further development20,34. MOC-etomidate, a “soft” analog of etomidate, demonstrated a rapid onset of action in vivo, but with significantly reduced potency (1/5 of etomidate). Meanwhile, the large quantities of a less potent active metabolite (300-fold less potent) caused delayed recovery. Cyclopropyl-methoxycarbonylmetomidate (ABP-700), another “soft” analog of etomidate, exhibits rapid onset and offset, comparable efficacy to etomidate, and lacks adrenocortical suppression; however, its further development has been limited by a high incidence of myoclonus30–32.

In both studies, the overall effect on adrenocortical function was further assessed by the AUC of plasma cortisol and ACTH change values over time. A reduction in cumulative ∆Cortisol AUC0–4h was observed in the etomidate group (indicated by a negative value), whereas an increase in cumulative ∆Cortisol AUC0–4h was observed in the NH600001 group (indicated by a positive value). Plasma ∆ACTH AUC0–4h was increased in both treatment groups, but in the NH600001-31 trial, the NH600001 group was significantly lower than the etomidate group. These results further suggest that NH600001 has little or no inhibitory effect on adrenocortical function compared with etomidate. Studies have confirmed that the inhibition of 11β-hydroxylase is the main cause of etomidate-induced adrenal cortex depression, and the combination of basic imidazole nitrogen of etomidate and 11β-hydroxylase is the underlying cause17,19–21. By introducing a fluorine atom with a strong negative electron into the imidazole ring, NH60001 reduces the density of the electron cloud of the nitrogen atom, thereby reducing the coordination effect with heme iron, and thereby reducing the inhibitory effect on adrenocortical function17,18,20,21. Of course, the slight increase in plasma Cortisol in the NH60001 group may be due to surgical stress, and the transient increase of plasma ACTH also proves this hypothesis.

Myoclonus is the most common adverse reaction of etomidate24. Prophylactic use of benzodiazepines, thiopental, dexmedetomidine, and opioids has been shown to attenuate etomidate-induced myoclonic responses35–37. In our trials, myoclonus was observed in some participants across both treatment groups, despite the administration of alfentanil to all individuals. This finding suggests that NH600001, similar to etomidate, may induce myoclonus-related adverse effects. It is noteworthy that the incidence of myoclonus was significantly lower in participants receiving NH600001 compared with etomidate in the NH600001-31 trial. The underlying factors contributing to this observation warrant further investigation. Yin, et al33. reported that an etomidate analog, Methoxyethyl etomidate hydrochloride (ET-26), was associated with a high incidence of myoclonus. The precise mechanism underlying etomidate-induced myoclonus remains elusive; however, it is thought to arise from an imbalance between excitatory and inhibitory pathways within the central nervous system during the induction phase, and its incidence and intensity are dose-related38,39. The reduced incidence of myoclonus observed with NH60001 may be attributed to its enhanced pharmacological efficacy resulting from structural modifications. Notably, despite an increase in molecular weight, the sedative and anesthetic potency of NH60001 at 0.25 mg/kg is comparable to that of etomidate at 0.3 mg/kg.

Intravenous administration of NH600001 has a favorable safety profile during gastrointestinal endoscopy. There was no significant difference in AEs and ADRs between the NH6001 groups and the etomidate group. One case of Blitt grade 3 myoclonus occurred in each group, and 1 case of thumb fracture (classified as SAE, but not related to the trial) occurred in the NH600001 group during follow-up. Other AEs were grade 1–2. The main AEs were myoclonus, nausea, vomiting, and decreased oxygen saturation.

The limitations of our study should be acknowledged. First, this study focused only on single dosing in outpatient endoscopy subjects and did not address the effects of continuous dosing on adrenocortical function and safety. Second, exclusion of critically ill patients (ASA III-IV) is a major limitation for generalizing the adrenal-sparing claim to the population where etomidate’s adrenal suppression is most concerning. Our next focus was to investigate the effects of NH600001 on the adrenal cortex under continuous administration and in critically ill patients, thereby providing context for continuous sedation studies in critically ill patients, including the ICU population.

In conclusion, NH600001, a novel etomidate analog, has a sedative/anesthetic effect comparable to etomidate, while reducing the risk of adrenal cortical suppression and demonstrating a favorable safety profile.

Methods

Design and participants

NH60001-21 (https://www.chictr.org.cn/, Identifier: ChiCTR2300069841) and NH60001-31 (https://www.chictr.org.cn/, Identifier: ChiCTR2400084095) were two multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trials to evaluate the efficacy and safety of NH60001 in gastroscopy or colonoscopy (Fig. 1). The NH60001-21 trial was conducted at 17 clinical medical centers in China from March 30, 2023 to June 15, 2023, while the NH60001-31 trial was conducted at 23 clinical medical centers in China from May 30, 2024 to September 29, 2024. Both studies were approved by the ethics committees of the Third Xiangya Hospital of Central South University (NH60001-21, Ethical No. Kuai23118; NH60001-21, Ethical No. 24046) and each participating institution (see Supplementary Table 1), and were registered prior to participants' enrollment at the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (https://www.chictr.org.cn/). All procedures adhered to the Clinical Trials of Drugs (2020 Edition) issued by the China Medical Products Administration and the Declaration of Helsinki, fully ensuring the rights and interests of the participants. All participants provided written informed consent prior to participation. We followed the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) reporting guideline for the designing and reporting of these trials. A full account of the eligibility criteria for both NH60001-21 and NH60001-31 is available in the protocol as Supplementary Information.

The inclusion criteria of NH60001-21 included subjects who underwent routine gastroscopy and expected gastroscopy operation time to be less than 15 min (the procedure time is short, and the non-continuous administration can meet the sedation needs, which corresponds to the administration of phase I clinical studies. At the same time, the population structure was widely representative.); American Society of Anesthesiologists classification (ASA) I-III; aged between 18–75 years; BMI between 18–30 kg/m2; and agreed to participate in this trial and voluntarily sign the informed consent form. The inclusion criteria of NH60001-31 were similar to NH60001-21, with the key differences being the inclusion of subjects who underwent either routine gastroscopy or colonoscopy with an expected procedure time of no more than 20 min.

The main exclusion criteria for NH60001-21 and NH60001-31 included history of allergy to etomidate, opioids, and other drugs that may be used during the trial; possible existing gastrointestinal obstruction, gastrointestinal retention or active gastrointestinal bleeding; history of mental system disease, central nervous system disease, and cardiovascular, respiratory, digestive, and endocrine system diseases affecting the study; clinically significant abnormalities detected in laboratory tests during the screening phase; modified Mallampti score ≥III; history of adrenal insufficiency, adrenal tumor or hereditary heme biosynthesis disorder or hereditary acute porphyria; history of drug abuse within 1 year before screening; history of alcohol abuse within 6 months before screening; and difficulty in blood collection or a history of dizzy with blood or needles.

Randomization and Blinding

In NH60001-21, Eligible participants were randomly assigned to receive etomidate or 0.20 mg/kg NH60001, 0.25 mg/kg NH60001 or 0.30 mg/kg NH60001 in a 1:1:1:1 ratio using a block randomization algorithm. In NH60001-31, Eligible participants were randomly assigned to receive etomidate or 0.25 mg/kg NH60001 in a 1:1 ratio using a stratified block randomization algorithm, stratified by endoscopic procedure (2 levels, gastroscopy and colonoscopy [transanal]) and age (2 levels, <65 years and ≥65 years). The randomization process employed an interactive web response system to assign patients a random number and corresponding treatment. Participants, blinded study investigators, and study site personnel were masked to the treatment allocation throughout the study, in both trials.

Although the packaging of NH60001 and etomidate was identical, independent, non-blinded dispensing investigators were used in both studies to avoid bias because of differences in dispensing doses between groups. Once the non-blinded dispensing investigator obtained the randomization number, the investigational drug was allocated based on the participant’s body weight and group assignment (randomization number corresponding to trial group) in a separate, dedicated dispensing area. The assigned treatment was then concealed using opaque, sterile covers labeled only with screening numbers to maintain confidentiality. During the study, the non-blinded investigator administered the study medication beneath an opaque sterile drape, while all other personnel remained blinded. Any additional doses were administered strictly according to instructions provided by the blinded investigator. Importantly, the non-blinded dispensing investigators were not involved in the protocol-specific assessments of outcomes. Data were collected and managed by independent staff using an electronic data capture system. From randomization until database lock, all participants, blinded investigators and trial personnel remained blinded to treatment assignments. The study treatment remained blinded unless essential for urgent medical treatment or as required by regulations. Unblinding of the results was performed after data cleaning and locking upon trial completion.

Procedures

Both studies consisted of a 14-day screening period, a 1-day baseline period, a 1-day treatment period, and a 4 ± 1-day follow-up observation period (see Supplementary Fig. 1, Supplementary Fig. 2). A 12-lead ECG was reassessed the day before endoscopy and baseline plasma cortisol and adrenocorticotropic hormone release (ACTH) levels were measured at nine time points (8:00, 8:30, 9:00, 9:30, 10:00, 10:30, 11:00, 11:30, 12:00).

Vital signs (including blood pressure, heart rate, respiratory rate, and body temperature) and BIS were monitored after entering the endoscopy room. All subjects received the study drug within the same time window (8:00 ± 15 min). Induction with NH600001 or etomidate was given immediately after the intravenous administration of alfentanil 5.0 μg/kg for 1 min ± 5 s. Based on the sedation/anesthesia state, 0.1 mg/kg NH600001 or etomidate could be added once for 15 ± 2 s. If further sedation was still needed, propofol was administered as rescue medication, with the dose and administration time determined by the investigator. In NH600001-21, the initial doses of NH600001 were 0.2 mg/kg, 0.25 mg/kg, and 0.3 mg/kg, respectively, and etomidate was 0.3 mg/kg. In NH600001-31, the initial doses of NH600001 and etomidate were 0.25 mg/kg and 0.3 mg/kg, respectively. During the maintenance period, if subjects exhibited signs of light anesthesia such as spontaneous eye opening or body movement, which affected the operation of gastrointestinal endoscopy, a single supplemental dose of NH600001 (0.1 mg/kg) or etomidate could be administered at the discretion of the evaluating investigator. Plasma cortisol and ACTH concentrations were measured before and after the administration of study drugs corresponding to the baseline period.

Sedation was assessed using the Modified Observer’s Assessment of Alertness/Sedation (MOAA/S) scale40. In NH600001-21, MOAA/S scores were evaluated within 5 min before administration and every 30 ± 10 S after the first administration until successful induction (MOAA/S ≤ 1). In NH600001-31, MOAA/S scores were evaluated within 5 min before administration and every 20 ± 10 S after the first administration until successful induction (MOAA/S ≤ 1). MOAA/S score was performed immediately after endoscope withdrawal, and then every 1 min ± 10 s until 10 min after complete awakening. Complete awakening was defined as the first time point of 3 consecutive MOAA/S scores of 5. BIS monitoring was initiated before administration and continued until 10 min after complete awakening. Aldrete score was assessed every 2 min ± 10 s after complete awakening. Participants were considered to be safely discharged when three consecutive Aldrete scores were ≥ 9.

Outcome measures

The primary outcome for both studies was endoscopy success rate, defined as meeting both criteria: Completion of the endoscopy; No administration of rescue medication from the initial dose until endoscopy completion.

Secondary outcomes for both studies included: Success rate of sedation /anesthesia (proportion of participants with a MOAA/S score ≤ 1 after the first dose of NH600001/etomidate); Time to successful sedation/anesthesia (the time from the first dose to MOAA/S score ≤ 1); Time to bispectral index (BIS) ≤ 60 (The time from the first dose to BIS ≤ 60); Time to complete awakening after endoscopy; Time to safe discharge after endoscopy; Proportion of rescue sedation (Proportion of participants requiring propofol for rescue sedation between the start of the first dose and completion of endoscopy).

Safety assessments included adverse events (AEs), adverse drug reactions (ADRs), physical examination, vital signs, laboratory tests, and electrocardiogram (including QTcF, PR interval, RR interval, and QRS interval). All AEs and laboratory values were assessed according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version (CTCAE) 5.0. AEs determined to be associated with the investigational product were classified as adverse drug reactions (ADRs). Serious adverse events (SAEs) were identified by impairment of daily functions, life-threatening nature, and requirement for hospitalization or prolonged hospitalization. As myoclonus is common with etomidate, the Blitt myoclonus grading scale was used to evaluate myoclonus after administration. Plasma cortisol and ACTH concentrations were measured before and after the administration of study drugs corresponding to the baseline period.

Adrenocortical function response will be focused, as adrenocortical suppression is the most common adverse effect limiting its clinical use. The changes of plasma cortisol and ACTH concentrations after administration were used to evaluate its effect on adrenocortical function. The change in plasma concentration was defined as the concentration during the trial period minus the concentration during the baseline period in the same time region. The area under the curve (AUC) of change values in plasma cortisol and ACTH over time was used to evaluate the overall effect on adrenocortical function. AUC was calculated using the trapezoidal method.

Statistical analysis

The NH600001-21 trial was exploratory, and no sample size estimation was performed. Combined with the data from the NH600001-21 trial and similar clinical trials, the initial dose of 0.25 mg/kg was selected in the NH600001-31 trial, and the success rate of endoscopy was estimated to be 93% for both NH600001 and etomidate. A one-tailed test with a p-value of 0.025 and a power value of 0.8 was used, non-inferiority threshold value was 8%, and the two groups were in a 1:1 ratio. PASS 14.0 software was used to estimate the sample size, and the required sample size was 320 cases. Accounting for a dropout rate of approximately 5% and the level and segment length of the randomization stratification factors, a total of 344 participants were enrolled, 172 in the NH600001 group and 172 in the etomidate group.

SAS ver. 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA) and Graphpad Prism 9.5.1 (Graphpad Software, Inc., California) were used for the statistical analyses and plots. Efficacy analyses were performed with an intention-to-treat approach, with the use of participant data regardless of treatment nonadherence or protocol deviations. Endoscopy was also classified as unsuccessful if a participant withdrew from the trial after randomization, did not receive the assigned drug intervention, or did not undergo endoscopy. Safety analyses included all randomized subjects who received at least one dose of treatment and for whom safety assessment data were available. Continuous variables were described as means (standard deviation, SD) and medians (interquartile range, IQR), depending on whether they were normally distributed or not. Categorical variables were described as frequencies (percentages). The multiple linear imputation method was used to fill in missing BIS data due to decreased electrode adhesion or other reasons. For the analysis of the primary outcome, the Clopper-Pearson method was used to calculate 95% confidence intervals (CI), the Newcombe-Wilson method was used to calculate the rate difference between the two groups and its 95% CI, with a non-inferiority threshold of −8%. The chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was used to compare the categorical data between the two groups, and the Bonferroni correction was used for pairwise comparison in multiple groups. In NH600001-21, analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to assess the significant differences between groups in normally distributed continuous variables with Bonferroni correction, whereas the differences between groups in non-normally distributed continuous variables were assessed with the Kruskal-Wallis test. In NH600001-31, the t-test was used to assess the significant differences between groups for normally distributed continuous variables, whereas the Mann-Whitney U-test was used for non-normally distributed continuous variables. Two-way repeated measures ANOVA was used for MOAA/S and BIS analyses involving repeated measures in the two trials. All statistical tests were two-sided and p-value ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
be the unit disk, and define
\mathcal{H}
as the class of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
. The subclass
\mathcal{LU}
consists of locally univalent functions,
i.e.,
functions
f\in\mathcal{H}
with
f^{\prime}(z)\neq 0
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. For functions
f\in\mathcal{LU}
defined in a simply connected domain
{\Omega}
, the pre-Schwarzian derivative
P_{f}
and the Schwarzian derivative
S_{f}
are, respectively, defined by

\displaystyle P_{f}=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\;\mbox{and}\;S_{f}=(P_{f})^{\prime}(z)-\frac{1}{2}(P_{f})^{2}(z)=\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{2}.

The pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian norms are defined by

\displaystyle||P_{f}||_{\Omega}=\sup_{z\in{\Omega}}|P_{f}|\eta^{-1}_{\Omega}\;\mbox{and}\;||S_{f}||_{\Omega}=\sup_{z\in{\Omega}}|S_{f}|\eta^{-2}_{\Omega}

respectively, where
\eta_{\Omega}
is the Poincare density. In particular, if
{\Omega}=\mathbb{D}
, then
||S_{f}||_{\Omega}
and
||P_{f}||_{\Omega}
are denoted by
||S_{f}||
and
||P_{f}||
, respectively.

In the following, we discuss some properties of Schwarzian derivatives:

\bullet

If
\varphi
is a locally univalent analytic function for which the composition
f\circ\varphi
is defined, then

\displaystyle S_{f\circ\varphi}(z)=S_{f}\circ\varphi(z)\left(\varphi^{\prime}(z)\right)+S_{\varphi}(z).

\bullet

The Schwarzian derivative is invariant under Möbius transformation,
i.e.,

S_{T\circ\varphi}=S_{f}
for any Möbius transformation
T
of the form

\displaystyle T(z)=\frac{az+b}{cz+d},\;ad-bc\neq 0,\;a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{C}.

\bullet

It is easy to verify that
S_{f}(z)=0
if, and only, if
f
is a Möbius transformation.

\bullet

There is a classical relation between the Schwarzian derivative and second order linear differential equations. If
S_{f}=2p
and
u=\left(f^{\prime}\right)^{-1/2}
, then

\displaystyle u^{\prime\prime}+pu=0.

Conversely, if
u_{1}
,
u_{2}
are linearly independent solutions of this D.E. and
f=u_{1}/u_{2}
, then
S_{f}=2p
.

The pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives are key tools in geometric function theory, particularly for characterizing Teichmüller space through embedding models. They also play a crucial role in studying the inner radius of univalency for planar domains and quasiconformal extensions
[
20
,
21
]
. Their study dates back to Kummer
(1836)
, who introduced the Schwarzian derivative in the context of hypergeometric
PDEs
. Since then, extensive research has explored their connections to univalent functions, leading to several sufficient conditions for univalency.

Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the subclass of
\mathcal{H}

\mathbb{D}

f

has the Taylor series expansion of the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the subclass of
\mathcal{A}
consisting of univalent (that is, one-to-one) functions. A function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is called starlike (with respect to the origin) if
f(\mathbb{D})
is starlike with respect to the origin and convex if

is convex. The class of all univalent starlike (resp. convex) functions in
\mathcal{A}
is denoted by
\mathcal{S}^{*}
(resp.
\mathcal{C}
). However, it is well-known that a function
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
(resp.
f\in\mathcal{C}
) if, and only if,

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right)>0\;\left(\mbox{resp.}\;{\rm Re}\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)>0\right),\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

The following observations are important for starlike and convex functions.

\bullet

The characterizations of starlikeness or convexity are sufficient but not necessary for univalency.

\bullet

Nehari’s criteria.
Nehari developed univalency involving Schwarzian derivative, where sufficient condition is almost necessary in the sense that scalar terms vary.

The next two results provide necessary and sufficient criteria for a function to be univalent.

Theorem A
.

(Kraus-Nehari’s Theorem) (Necessary condition)

Let
f
be a univalent function. Then
f
satisfies

\displaystyle|S_{f}(z)|\leq\frac{6}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}\;\mbox{for}\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

Moreover, the constant
6
cannot be replaced by a smaller one.

Theorem B
.

(Nehari’s Theorem) (Sufficient condition)

Let
f
be a locally univalent function. If
f
satisfies

\displaystyle|S_{f}(z)|\leq\frac{2}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}\;\mbox{for}\;z\in\mathbb{D},

then
f
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, the constant
2
cannot be replaced by a larger one.

The Schwarzian norm plays a significant role in the theory of quasiconformal mappings and Teichmüller space (see
[

is a bounded domain in the Banach space of analytic functions on

[

Our research builds upon a foundational body of work in geometric function theory. The concept of the Schwarzian derivative with
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0

In

\displaystyle 1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=\ell(w(z))=\frac{1+w(z)}{1-w(z)}

The expression which is defined in (

and

(1.4)

\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{C}\;\mbox{if, and only if,}\;\bigg|\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}-2\bar{z}\bigg|\leq 2,

\mathcal{P}

respectively, introduced and investigated by Ma and Minda

Multiplying (
2.7
) by
(1-|z|^{2})
both side, we have

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2}\leq c^{2}(1-|z|^{2})+2c(1-|z|^{2}){\rm Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)

which implies that

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2}-2c(1-|z|^{2}){\rm Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)+c^{2}|z|^{2}\leq c^{2}.

Hence, the required inequality is established.

\displaystyle\bigg|(1-|z|^{2})\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)-c\bar{z}\bigg|\leq c.

Considering the function
f_{c}
defined in (
2.1
), we establish the sharpness of the above two inequalities, as confirmed in Corollary
2.1
and Corollary
2.2
for the spacial case
c=2
. This completes the proof.
∎

(2.9)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1+|z|^{2})}\leq|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})}

(2.12)

All of these estimates are sharp. Equality holds for the function

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{F}^{0}(c)
, and from (
2.6
), we obtain that
\phi(0)=0
. Then by using the Schwarz lemma, we get

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}}{\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}+c}\bigg|^{2}\leq|z|^{2}

which is equivalent to the inequality

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2}\leq c^{2}|z|^{2}+2c|z|^{2}{\rm Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)+|z|^{2}\bigg|\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2}.

Thus, we have the estimate

(2.13)

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{4})\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2}\leq c^{2}|z|^{2}+2c|z|^{2}{\rm Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right).

Multiplying both sides the above inequality by
(1-|z|^{4})
, we obtain

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{4})^{2}

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2}-2c|z|^{2}(1-|z|^{4}){\rm Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c^{2}|z|^{2}(1-|z|^{4}).

Adding
\left(c|z|^{2}\bar{z}\right)^{2}
to both sides of the above inequality leads to

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{4})^{2}

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2}-2c|z|^{2}(1-|z|^{4}){\rm Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)+c^{2}|z|^{4}|\bar{z}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c^{2}|z|^{2}(1-|z|^{4})+c^{2}|z|^{4}|\bar{z}|^{2}.

Multiplying both side by
|z|
, then by simple calculation, we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg|(1-|z|^{4})\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}-c|z|^{4}\bigg|\leq c|z|^{2}

which implies that

\displaystyle\frac{c|z|^{2}}{1+|z|^{2}}\leq{\rm Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\leq\frac{c|z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}.

Let
z=re^{i\theta}
. Then it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\frac{cr}{1+r^{2}}\leq\dfrac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(\log|f^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|\right)\leq\frac{cr}{1-r^{2}}.

Integrating the above estimate w.r.t.
r
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1+|z|^{2})^{-\frac{c}{2}}}\leq|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{c}{2}}}.

Next, the growth part of the theorem follows from the upper bound

\displaystyle|f^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|=\bigg|\int_{0}^{r}f^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})e^{i\theta}dt\bigg|\leq\int_{0}^{r}|f^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|dt\leq\int_{0}^{r}\frac{1}{(1-t^{2})^{\frac{c}{2}}}dt

which implies that

\displaystyle|f(z)|\leq\int_{0}^{|z|}\frac{1}{(1-\xi^{2})^{\frac{c}{2}}}d|\xi|

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

It is well-known that if
f(z_{0})
is a point of minimum modulus on the image of the circle
|z|=r
and
\gamma=f^{-1}(\Gamma)
, where
\Gamma
is the line segment from
0
to
f(z_{0})
, then

\displaystyle|f(z)|\geq|f(z_{0})|\geq\int_{0}^{|z|}\frac{1}{(1+\xi^{2})^{\frac{c}{2}}}d|\xi|.

Thus all the desired inequalities are established. For the function
f_{c,\lambda}(z)
in the main result, the sharpness part can be shown easily, hence we omit the details.
∎

We will find the sharp bound of the pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian norm for the function
f
in the class
\mathcal{F}(c)
, under the assumption that
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
. The following lemma will paly a key role to prove the result.

Lemma A
.

[
38
]

If
\phi(z):\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be analytic function, then

\displaystyle\frac{|\phi(z)|^{2}}{1-|\phi(z)|^{2}}\leq\frac{(\phi(0)+|z|)^{2}}{(1-|\phi(0)|)^{2}(1-|z|^{2})|)}

Our result is sharp bound of the pre-Schwarzian norm for
f\in\mathcal{F}^{0}(c)
.

Theorem 2.3
.

For
c\in(0,3]
, let
f\in\mathcal{F}^{0}(c)
be of the form (
1.1
), then the pre-schwarzian norm satisfies the inequality

\displaystyle||Pf||\leq c.

The inequality is sharp.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Since
\phi(z)=z\xi(z)
, with
|\xi(z)|<1
, then in (
2.6
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\frac{c|z\xi(z)|}{1-|z|^{2}|\xi(z)|}

\displaystyle\leq c\sup_{0\leq r\leq 1}\frac{r(1-r^{2})}{(1-r^{2})}

\displaystyle=c.

Thus, we have the required inequality
||Pf||\leq c
.

To show that the inequality is sharp, we consider the function
f_{c}^{*}
given by

\displaystyle f_{c}^{*}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{1}{(1-\xi^{2})^{\frac{c}{2}}}d\xi.

It can be easily shown that
||Pf_{c}^{*}||=c.
This completes the proof.
∎

For the class
\mathcal{C}
of convex functions, we obtain the following sharp result, derived from Theorem
2.3
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}:=\mathcal{F}^{0}(2)
be of the form (
1.1
), then the pre-schwarzian norm satisfies the inequality

\displaystyle||Pf||\leq 2.

The inequality is sharp.

Sharpness of Corollary
2.4

For
c=2
, it follows from that

\displaystyle\frac{f_{2}^{\prime\prime}}{f_{2}^{\prime}}(z)=\frac{2z}{(1-z^{2})}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;Pf_{2}=\frac{2}{1-z^{2}}.

A simple computation thus yields that

\displaystyle||Pf_{2}||=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(1-z^{2}\right)|Pf_{2}|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)\frac{2}{1-|z|^{2}}=2

and we see the constant
2
is best possible.

Using the Schwarz lemma, we obtain a sharp bound for the Schwarzian derivative norm when
f\in\mathcal{F}(c)
.

Theorem 2.4
.

For
c\in(0,3]
, let
f\in\mathcal{F}^{0}(c)
be of the form (
1.1
). Then the Schwarzian norm satisfies the inequality

\displaystyle||Sf||=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf(z)|\leq\frac{c(4-c)}{2}.

The inequality is sharp.

For the class of convex functions, we obtain the following immediate result from Theorem
2.4
, showing that the sharp bound of
||Sf||
is
2
.

Corollary 2.5
.

If
f\in\mathcal{C}:=\mathcal{F}^{0}(2)
be of the form (
1.1
) with
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
, then Schwarzian norm satisfies the inequality

\displaystyle||Sf||=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf(z)|\leq 2.

The estimate is sharp.

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{F}^{0}(c)
be of the form (
1.1
). Then from (
2.6
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{c\phi(z)}{(1-z\phi(z))}.

A simple calculation gives that

\displaystyle Sf(z)=c\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime}(z)+\left(1-\frac{c}{2}\right)\phi^{2}(z)}{(1-z\phi(z))^{2}}\right).

By using triangle inequality and Schwarz pick lemma, we obtain

(2.14)

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf|

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg|\phi^{\prime}(z)+\left(1-\frac{c}{2}\right)\phi^{2}(z)\bigg|\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}{|1-z\phi(z)|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{c(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}{|1-z\phi(z)|^{2}}\left(\frac{1-|\phi(z)|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}+\left(1-\frac{c}{2}\right)|\phi(z)|^{2}\right).

We define the function
\Psi(z):\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
such that

\displaystyle\Psi(z):=\frac{\bar{z}-\phi(z)}{1-z\phi(z)}.

Since
\phi(\mathbb{D})\subseteq\mathbb{D}
then
(1-|z|^{2})(1-|z\phi(z)|^{2})>0
, it follows that

\displaystyle|\bar{z}-\phi(z)|^{2}<|1-z\phi(z)|^{2}.

Thus, we conclude that
|\Psi(z)|^{2}<1
. It is easy to see that

\displaystyle 1-|\Psi(z)|^{2}=\frac{(1-|\phi(z)|^{2})(1-|z|^{2})}{|1-z\phi(z)|^{2}}

and

(2.15)

\displaystyle\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}{|1-z\phi(z)|^{2}}=\frac{(1-|\Psi(z)|^{2})(1-|z|^{2})}{(1-|\phi(z)|^{2})}.

If we replace the expression (
2.15
) in (
2.14
), then we have

(2.16)

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf(z)|\leq c(1-|\Psi_{1}(z)|^{2})\left(1+\left(1-\frac{c}{2}\right)\frac{|\phi(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})}{(1-|\phi(z)|^{2})}\right).

Since
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
implies that
\phi(0)=0
, using Lemma A, we obtain

(2.17)

\displaystyle\frac{|\phi(z)|^{2}}{1-|\phi(z)|^{2}}\leq\frac{|z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}.

Using (
2.17
) in (
2.16
), we obtain

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf(z)|\leq c(1-|\Psi(z)|^{2})\left(1+\left(1-\frac{c}{2}\right)|z|^{2}\right).

Again, considering that
1-|\Psi(z)|^{2}\leq 1
, one can readily observe that

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sh(z)|\leq c\left(1+\left(1-\frac{c}{2}\right)\right)=\frac{c(4-c)}{2}.

Thus the desired inequality is obtained. The sharpness of the inequality follows from the Example
2.2
.

Example 2.2
.

The family of parameterized functions defined as:

\displaystyle f_{c}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{1}{(1-\xi^{2})^{c/2}}d\xi,\;\;\;\;\mbox{for}\;c\in(0,3]

maximizes the Schwarzian norm defined as:

\displaystyle||Sf_{c}||=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf_{c}|

and from this, the sharpness of the inequality holds for
c>0
.

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime\prime}_{c}}{f_{c}^{\prime}}(z)=\frac{cz}{1-z^{2}}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;Sf_{c}=\frac{c}{(1-z^{2})^{2}}\left[1+\left(1-\frac{c}{2}\right)|z|^{2}\right]

which shows that

\displaystyle||Sf_{c}||

\displaystyle=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf_{c}|

\displaystyle=c\left(1+\left(1-\frac{c}{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{c(4-c)}{2}.

Given that the value
|f^{\prime\prime}(0)|
is not necessarily zero, we provide a bound for the quantity
(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf(z)|
where
f\in\mathcal{F}(c)
.

Theorem 2.5
.

If
f\in\mathcal{F}(c)
, for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
with
c\in(0,3]
and

\displaystyle\gamma=|\phi(0)|=\frac{|f^{\prime\prime}(0)|}{c},

then

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf(z)|\leq c\left(1+\left(1-\frac{c}{2}\right)\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right).

We have the following immediate result from Theorem
2.5
for the class
\mathcal{C}
of convex functions.

Corollary 2.6
.

If
f\in\mathcal{C}:=\mathcal{F}(2)
be of the form (
1.1
), then the inequality for
c=2
, with

\displaystyle\gamma=|\phi(0)|=\frac{|f^{\prime\prime}(0)|}{2}

the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf(z)|\leq 2.

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

Let
\gamma=|\phi(0)|
. Applying the Lemma A, we calculate

\displaystyle\frac{|\phi(z)|^{2}}{1-|\phi(z)|^{2}}\leq\frac{(\gamma+|z|)^{2}}{(1-\gamma^{2})(1-|z|^{2})}.

If we substitute the above inequality into (
2.14
), we get

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf(z)|\leq c(1-|\Phi_{1}(z)|^{2})\left(1+\left(1-\frac{c}{2}\right)\frac{(\gamma+|z|)^{2}}{(1-\gamma^{2})}\right).

From the fact that
|z|<1
and
1-|\Phi_{1}(z)|^{2}\leq 1
, we can easily calculate

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf(z)|\leq c\left(1+\left(1-\frac{c}{2}\right)\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)=2.

This is the desired bound.

Impact of sex and HLA-B27 positivity on clinical characteristics and patient-reported outcomes in a cohort of Egyptian axial spondyloarthritis patients
Authors: Eiman Soliman, Magdy Zeheiry, Yasmine Abu Halawa, Ahmed Shaaban
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo assess the clinical characteristics, disease activity, functional status, and quality of life of Egyptian patients with axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA), and to evaluate differences according to sex and HLA-B27 status.MethodsThis cross-sectional study included 256 patients fulfilling the Assessment of SpondyloArthritis international Society (ASAS) classification criteria for axSpA. Clinical, laboratory, imaging, and treatment data were collected. Disease activity was assessed using ASDAS-CRP and BASDAI, functional status using BASFI, spinal mobility using BASMI, and quality of life using ASQoL. Comparisons were performed according to sex, HLA-B27 status, and combined sex/HLA-B27 subgroups.ResultsOf 256 patients, 57.4% were male, 55.9% were HLA-B27 positive, and 70.3% had radiographic axSpA. Female patients demonstrated higher disease activity, greater functional impairment, and poorer quality of life, as reflected by higher BASDAI, BASFI, and ASQoL scores. HLA-B27-positive patients more commonly exhibited axial-dominant disease, whereas HLA-B27-negative patients showed higher frequencies of peripheral involvement, enthesitis, and dactylitis. Combined stratification by sex and HLA-B27 status demonstrated that female HLA-B27-negative patients had the highest disease activity, functional impairment, and quality-of-life impairment among all subgroups.ConclusionDisease expression and patient-reported outcomes in axSpA differ according to both sex and HLA-B27 status. Despite geographic and healthcare-system differences, the observed patterns were broadly consistent with those reported in international cohorts. The combined evaluation of sex and HLA-B27 status may provide additional insight into disease heterogeneity and patient burden in axSpA.

Background

Axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA) encompasses a heterogeneous group of inflammatory disorders primarily affecting the axial skeleton, distinguished by unique genetic, pathogenic, and clinical characteristics that differentiate them from other inflammatory arthritides. A hallmark feature of axSpA is chronic lower back pain, which typically manifests before the age of 45 [1]. This pain is of inflammatory origin and is frequently accompanied by other musculoskeletal manifestations, including enthesitis, peripheral arthritis, and, less commonly, dactylitis [2]. Furthermore, axSpA is frequently associated with extra-articular manifestations (EAMs), such as uveitis, psoriasis, and inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) [3].

Genetic predisposition plays a critical role in axSpA, with the Human leukocyte antigen B27 (HLA-B27) demonstrating the strongest association with disease susceptibility. The presence of HLA-B27 increases the likelihood of developing axSpA by approximately 1.5 times compared to HLA-B27-negative individuals [4, 5].

Beyond genetic susceptibility, multiple factors contribute to the pathogenesis and progression of axSpA. Proinflammatory cytokines, particularly tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) and interleukin-17 (IL-17), play a central role in sustaining inflammation and driving both pain and tissue damage [6]. Additionally, emerging evidence suggests a connection between gut microbiota alterations and axSpA. These complex interactions not only shape disease expression but also influence treatment strategies and long-term outcomes [7].

Health-related quality of life (HRQoL) in axSpA is significantly affected due to the disease burden and its treatment [8]. Patients frequently report chronic pain, progressive impairment of spinal mobility, fatigue even with minimal exertion, sleep disturbances, and psychological comorbidities such as depression, all of which contribute to a substantial reduction in overall well-being [9].

Although sex-related differences and the influence of HLA-B27 status on disease expression have been described in international axSpA cohorts, data from North African and Egyptian populations remain limited. Moreover, few studies have evaluated the combined influence of sex and HLA-B27 status on disease phenotype, disease activity, functional impairment, and health-related quality of life. Given potential regional differences in healthcare access, treatment patterns, and disease burden, characterization of these factors in Egyptian patients may provide clinically relevant insights. Therefore, this study aimed to assess the clinical characteristics, disease activity, functional status, treatment patterns, and HRQoL in a cohort of Egyptian patients with axSpA and to evaluate the individual and combined associations of sex and HLA-B27 status with disease expression and patient-reported outcomes.

Subjects and methods

Patients were consecutively recruited from the Rheumatology Outpatient Clinics and specialized axSpA clinics at Alexandria University over a 12-month period. All patients had an established clinical diagnosis of AxSpA made by experienced rheumatologists prior to study enrollment and fulfilled the Assessment of SpondyloArthritis International Society (ASAS) 2009 classification criteria for AxSpA [1]. Both radiographic and non-radiographic axSpA patients were included.

Inclusion criteria comprised adults aged ≥ 18 years fulfilling the ASAS classification criteria for axSpA. Patients with concomitant psoriatic arthritis (PsA) or inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) -associated spondyloarthritis were excluded to reduce clinical heterogeneity and avoid overlap phenotypes that could independently influence disease activity, peripheral manifestations, and quality of life outcomes.

All eligible patients who attended the outpatient clinics during the study period were invited to participate. No patients were selected based on disease severity or treatment status. Patients who declined participation or had incomplete essential clinical or laboratory data were excluded. No patients were excluded from final analysis after enrollment due to disease severity, treatment status, or incomplete participation. All eligible patients who met the inclusion criteria were included in the final analysis.

Clinical assessment, laboratory evaluation, and disease activity measures were performed by rheumatology specialists trained in spondyloarthritis assessment. This study was approved by the Ethical Committee of the Faculty of Medicine, Alexandria University.

Prior to baseline examination, each patient was interrogated thoroughly about symptoms, onset, course and duration of the disease, co-morbid illness, extra articular manifestations, and family history. HLA-B27 status was determined from patient medical records. Family history was recorded based on the presence of axial spondyloarthritis or related spondyloarthritis diagnoses in first-degree relatives, as documented in the medical records and patient history.

Patient-reported assessment was done by the Ankylosing Spondylitis Quality of Life (ASQoL) questionnaire [8], Patients completed the ASQoL questionnaire, which includes items related to the impact of disease on sleep, mood, motivation, coping, activities of daily living, independence, relationships, and social life. Responses are scored dichotomously, with 0 assigned for “No” and 1 for “Yes” for each item. The total score ranges from 0 to 18, with higher scores indicating poorer health-related quality of life.

Information on extra-articular manifestations and comorbidities was collected from medical records and clinical assessment. The extra-articular manifestation evaluated in this study was uveitis. Recorded comorbidities included diabetes mellitus, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, and pulmonary disease.

Laboratory investigations included a complete blood picture (CBC), erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), C-reactive protein (CRP), serum creatinine, aspartate aminotransferase (AST) and alanine transaminase (ALT) as well as HLA-B27 testing.

Imaging assessment was based on sacroiliac joint radiography and/or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) performed as part of routine clinical care. Imaging modality was determined according to availability, cost, and insurance authorization requirements, with most patients undergoing MRI of the sacroiliac joints, while a smaller proportion underwent conventional radiography. MRI confirmation was often required for medication approval. Spinal radiographs were not systematically evaluated.

Patients were classified as radiographic axSpA (r-axSpA) or non-radiographic axSpA (nr-axSpA) according to the ASAS classification criteria [1]. Radiographic sacroiliitis was defined according to the modified New York criteria as bilateral grade ≥ 2 sacroiliitis or unilateral grade ≥ 3 sacroiliitis [10]. Active sacroiliitis on MRI was defined according to the ASAS criteria by the presence of bone marrow edema in typical subchondral locations on at least two consecutive slices or multiple lesions on a single slice, consistent with inflammatory sacroiliitis [11].

All radiographs and MRI scans were reviewed and interpreted by experienced musculoskeletal radiologists at Alexandria University Hospitals. Image assessment was performed as part of routine clinical care and was not centrally reviewed, blinded, or reassessed for research purposes.

Disease activity was evaluated using Ankylosing Spondylitis Disease Activity Score using C-reactive protein (ASDAS-CRP) and Bath Ankylosing Spondylitis Disease Activity Index (BASDAI). Functional impairment was assessed through the Bath Ankylosing Spondylitis Functional Index (BASFI) and spinal mobility was assessed using the Bath Ankylosing Spondylitis Metrology Index (BASMI) [12].

Sex and HLA-B27 status were the primary variables of interest, and their relationship with disease activity, functional impairment, spinal mobility, and patient-reported outcomes were analyzed.

Statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 20.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages and compared using the Chi-square test or Monte Carlo test when appropriate. Continuous variables were tested for normality using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Normally distributed data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation and compared using independent samples t-test, while non-normally distributed variables were presented as median (interquartile range) and compared using the Mann–Whitney U test or Kruskal–Wallis test as appropriate. A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Given the number of outcomes evaluated, findings were interpreted cautiously, as multiple comparisons may increase the risk of type I error.

Results

During the 12-month recruitment period, 299 patients with a clinical diagnosis of axial spondyloarthritis were screened for eligibility. Fifteen patients did not fulfill the ASAS 2009 classification criteria, 20 had concomitant Psoriasis/PsA, 2 IBD-associated SpA, and 6 declined participation or had incomplete essential clinical or laboratory data. The remaining 256 patients fulfilled all eligibility criteria and were included in the final analysis.

Clinical characteristics of the study population

The study included 256 patients with axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA), comprising 147 males (57.4%) and 109 females (42.6%). HLA-B27 positivity was observed in 143 patients (55.9%), while 113 patients (44.1%) were HLA-B27 negative. Based on sacroiliac joint imaging findings, 180 patients (70.3%) were classified as radiographic axSpA (r-axSpA) and 76 patients (29.7%) as non-radiographic axSpA (nr-axSpA).

The cohort demonstrated a high overall disease burden, with a mean ASDAS-CRP of 4.56 ± 0.91, mean BASDAI of 6.12 ± 1.74, and mean BASFI of 6.05 ± 2.03. The sociodemographic, clinical, laboratory, and disease-related characteristics of the study population are summarized in Table 1.

Sex-based differences in clinical characteristics

Female patients exhibited significantly higher disease activity, greater functional impairment, and poorer quality of life than male patients, as reflected by higher BASDAI, BASFI, and ASQoL scores. Females were also more likely to present with combined axial and peripheral involvement and had higher rates of dactylitis. In contrast, males more frequently demonstrated axial-only disease. No significant sex-related differences were observed in the frequency of enthesitis or uveitis. Detailed comparisons according to sex are presented in Table 2. The prevalence of uveitis did not differ significantly according to HLA-B27 status. Detailed comparisons according to HLA-B27 status are presented in Table 3. Combined stratification by sex and HLA-B27 status

To further explore the combined influence of sex and HLA-B27 status, patients were stratified into four groups: male/HLA-B27 positive, female/HLA-B27 positive, male/HLA-B27 negative, and female/HLA-B27 negative. Significant differences were observed across the four groups in disease phenotype, disease activity, functional impairment, and quality of life.

Male HLA-B27-positive patients most frequently exhibited an axial-only disease pattern, whereas combined axial and peripheral involvement was more common among the remaining groups. Female HLA-B27-negative patients demonstrated the highest BASDAI, BASFI, and ASQoL scores, indicating the greatest disease burden among the four subgroups. Enthesitis and dactylitis were also more prevalent among female and HLA-B27-negative patients. Detailed results of the four-group comparison are presented in Table 4.

Correlation between quality of life and disease measures

Correlation analysis revealed significant associations between ASQoL scores and several measures of disease burden. ASQoL showed a strong positive correlation with BASFI (rs = 0.719, p < 0.001) and BASDAI (rs = 0.650, p < 0.001), indicating poorer quality of life with increasing functional impairment and disease activity. Moderate correlations were observed with BASMI (rs = 0.466, p < 0.001) and ASDAS-CRP (rs = 0.277, p < 0.001). A weak positive correlation was found with ESR (rs = 0.165, p = 0.008), while no significant correlation was observed with CRP (rs = − 0.102, p > 0.05). These findings are summarized in Table 5. Discussion

The present study examined the clinical characteristics, disease burden, and quality of life of Egyptian patients with axSpA according to sex and HLA-B27 status. Overall, our findings were largely consistent with observations from major international cohorts, including COMOSPA [13] and ASAS-perSpA [14], despite potential geographic, cultural, and healthcare-system differences. HLA-B27-positive patients more commonly demonstrated an axial-dominant disease pattern, whereas HLA-B27-negative patients showed greater peripheral involvement. Similarly, female patients reported higher disease activity, greater functional impairment, and poorer quality of life than males, in agreement with previous studies and meta-analyses [15–19].

Although sex and HLA-B27 status have each been extensively studied, relatively few reports have examined their combined influence on disease phenotype and patient-reported outcomes within the same cohort, particularly in North African populations.

In our cohort, female HLA-B27-negative patients demonstrated the highest disease activity, functional impairment, and quality-of-life impairment, highlighting the heterogeneity of axSpA and suggesting that consideration of both sex and HLA-B27 status may provide additional clinical insight beyond either factor alone.

A notable finding was the overall high disease activity observed across the cohort, with mean ASDAS-CRP values substantially higher than those reported in many international studies. This may partly reflect the tertiary referral nature of our center, where patients with more severe disease are more likely to be managed. In addition, variability in healthcare access and treatment delivery may contribute to persistent disease activity in routine clinical practice. However, detailed treatment-related variables, including treatment duration, adherence, and treatment response, were not systematically assessed and therefore could not be evaluated as potential contributors to the observed disease burden. When compared with Arévalo et al. [20], who reported lower overall disease activity and differences according to HLA-B27 status, our findings suggest that disease burden and patient characteristics may vary considerably across populations and healthcare settings.

Consistent with previous reports, poorer quality of life was strongly associated with higher disease activity and greater functional impairment [21−23], reinforcing the close relationship between disease burden and patient-reported outcomes in axSpA.

This study provides real-world data from one of the larger contemporary Egyptian axSpA cohorts reported to date. Comprehensive assessment of clinical characteristics, imaging findings, laboratory parameters, and patient-reported outcomes allowed detailed phenotypic characterization of this underrepresented population. In addition, the combined evaluation of sex and HLA-B27 status extends previous observations by demonstrating how these factors jointly influence disease burden within routine clinical practice.

Beyond characterizing an Egyptian cohort, our findings also provide external validation of observations reported by large international registries, including COMOSPA [13] and ASAS-perSpA [14]. Despite differences in geography, ethnicity, and healthcare systems, the overall patterns of sex- and HLA-B27-associated disease expression were remarkably consistent. These findings support the broader applicability of previously reported disease characteristics while expanding the available evidence from an underrepresented North African population.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. As a cross-sectional study, the findings reflect associations at a single time point and do not permit conclusions regarding causality. Some variability in imaging pathways was unavoidable given the real-world clinical setting, and imaging assessments were not centrally reviewed. Patients with concomitant psoriatic arthritis or inflammatory bowel disease-associated spondyloarthritis were excluded to reduce clinical heterogeneity Certain factors that may influence patient-reported outcomes, including fibromyalgia and psychosocial factors, were not systematically assessed. In addition, analyses were based on unadjusted group comparisons, and detailed treatment-related variables were not available. Therefore, the findings should be interpreted within the context of a descriptive observational study.

Conclusion

Sex and HLA-B27 status are associated with differences in disease phenotype, disease burden, and patient-reported outcomes in axial spondyloarthritis. In this Egyptian cohort, the observed patterns were broadly consistent with those reported internationally, providing real-world data from an underrepresented North African population. Further longitudinal studies are warranted to better define the independent contributions of these factors to disease outcomes.

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate discrete regularity estimates for a broad class of temporal numerical schemes for parabolic stochastic evolution equations. We provide a characterization of discrete stochastic maximal ℓ p \ell^{p} -regularity in terms of its continuous counterpart, thereby establishing a unified framework that yields numerous new discrete regularity results. Moreover, as a consequence of the continuous-time theory, we establish several important properties of discrete stochastic maximal regularity such as extrapolation in the exponent p p and with respect to a power weight. Furthermore, employing the H ∞ H^{\infty} -functional calculus, we derive a powerful discrete maximal estimate in the trace space norm D A ​ ( 1 − 1 p , p ) D_{A}(1-\frac{1}{p},p) for p ∈ [ 2 , ∞ ) p\in[2,\infty) .

1.
Introduction

Maximal
L^{p}
-regularity techniques play a central role in the theory of both deterministic and stochastic evolution equations of parabolic type (see the monographs
[
27
,
50
]
and the surveys
[
4
,
57
]
, as well as the references therein). Although maximal regularity is inherently a linear concept, it becomes a powerful tool for analyzing nonlinear problems through linearization techniques. In particular, it allows us to establish local well-posedness and regularity results, and formulate sharp blow-up criteria for global existence for a wide class of equations.

In the deterministic setting, a theory of discrete maximal
\ell^{p}
-regularity can already be found in
[
9
]
and was connected to discrete Fourier multiplier theory in
[
11
,
12
]
. The connections between discrete maximal regularity and numerical analysis—specifically, the stability and convergence of numerical schemes for (non)linear PDEs—have been explored in numerous works (see, for example,
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
31
,
32
,
34
]
and references therein), and this line of research remains highly active.

Motivated by these developments, we introduce a discrete analogue of stochastic maximal regularity. Our framework encompasses the exponential Euler method and a broad class of rational time discretization schemes, including the implicit Euler method. In addition to establishing new maximal regularity estimates, we derive discrete analogues of maximal estimates with optimal trace regularity. These results are expected to pave the way for improved convergence rates, as already observed in the deterministic setting. For instance, in the recent work
[
38
]
, the authors used stochastic maximal regularity and non-optimal maximal estimates to prove pathwise uniform convergence estimates of a full discretization for the three-dimensional Allen-Cahn equation. The reader is also referred to
[
37
, Remark 3.3]
for an elementary example demonstrating the use of discrete stochastic maximal regularity in proving stability estimates.

The seminal results of
[
15
]
and
[
56
]
provide necessary and sufficient conditions for maximal
L^{p}
-regularity in the deterministic continuous-time context. In the stochastic setting, sufficient conditions involving the
H^{\infty}
-calculus of an operator
A
were established in
[
46
,
48
]
. For a more detailed historical account on (stochastic) maximal regularity, we refer the reader to the recent survey
[
4
]
and the monograph
[
13
]
.

Discrete versions of stochastic maximal regularity estimates are mainly known in a Hilbert space setting (see
[
19
,
20
,
21
,
30
]
). An exception is
[
37
]
, where a discrete stochastic maximal regularity is proved for the implicit Euler scheme in
L^{q}
-spaces using the boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus.

In our work, we establish a full theory of discrete stochastic maximal regularity, which in particular entails the following:

(a)

An equivalence of the continuous and discrete setting for a large class of numerical schemes;

(b)

Discrete stochastic maximal regularity results in Hilbert spaces, spaces like
L^{q}
, and real interpolation spaces;

(c)

A discrete maximal estimate in the trace space
D_{A}(1-\frac{1}{p},p)
;

(d)

All results are presented in a time-weighted setting.

Part (
b
) is a direct consequence of our equivalence theorem in (
a
) and the results of
[
4
,
41
,
46
,
48
]
by the second author and his collaborators. In particular, (
b
) contains a far-reaching extension of the main result of
[
37
]
.
The discrete maximal estimate mentioned in (
c
) is one of the deepest results of the paper, and has never been considered in a stochastic setting before. It incorporates maximal estimates and parabolic smoothing. As is well-known in the theory of stochastic processes, it can be rather delicate to prove maximal estimates (see
[
53
]
). Our proof relies on
H^{\infty}
-calculus,
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness techniques, fractional calculus, and deep results from interpolation theory.

1.1.
Formulation of our main results

Below, we will formulate some of the main results of the paper. For simplicity, we only present the unweighted setting in this subsection.

Let
X_{0}
be (isomorphic to a closed subspace of)
L^{q}(\mathcal{O})
, for some
q\in[2,\infty)
, over a
\sigma
-finite measure space
(\mathcal{O},\Sigma,\mu)
.
Suppose that
X_{1}
is a Banach space such that
X_{1}\hookrightarrow X_{0}
densely and define the real and complex interpolation spaces

X_{\alpha,p}\coloneq(X_{0},X_{1})_{\alpha,p}\ \ \text{and}\ \ X_{\alpha}\coloneq[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\alpha},\ \ \ \alpha\in(0,1),\ p\in(1,\infty).

Throughout this manuscript, we impose the following assumption:

Assumption 1.1
.

(1)

A
is a sectorial operator on
X_{0}
of angle
\omega(A)<\frac{\pi}{2}
, and
D(A)=X_{1}
;

(2)

R\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathcal{L}(X_{0})
is given by
R_{\tau}\coloneq r(\tau A)
, where
r
is either the exponential function
r(z)\coloneq e^{-z}
, or
r\colon\Sigma_{\theta}\to\mathbb{C}
is an
A(\theta)
-stable rational function (i.e.
|r(z)|\leq 1
for
z\in\Sigma_{\theta}
) with
\theta\in(\omega(A),\tfrac{\pi}{2}]
that is consistent of order
\ell\geq 1
(i.e.
|r(z)-e^{-z}|\leq Cz^{\ell+1}
for
z\to 0
) and satisfies
r(\infty)=0
.

Here,
\Sigma_{\theta}\coloneq\{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}\colon|\arg z|<\theta\}
.
Given a time step
\tau>0
, let
t_{n}=n\tau
,
n\geq 0
, and consider the following discretization scheme: starting from
Y_{0}\coloneq 0
, define recursively

(1.1)

Y_{n+1}\coloneq R_{\tau}Y_{n}+R_{\tau}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}g(s)dW(s),\quad n\geq 0,

where
W
is a cylindrical Brownian motion in
\ell^{2}
and
g\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;\gamma(\ell^{2},X_{1/2})))
is strongly progressively measurable.

The scheme
R
is said to have
discrete stochastic maximal
\ell^{p}
-regularity
if there exists a constant
C
, independent of
\tau
and
g
, such that

(1.2)

\mathbb{E}\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau\|AY_{n}\|^{p}_{X_{0}}\leq C^{p}\mathbb{E}\|g\|_{L^{p}(0,T;\gamma(\ell^{2},X_{1/2}))}^{p}.

We emphasize that the formulation (
1.1
) is sufficiently general to encompass various approximation methods. In particular, one can take
g(t)=g_{n}
for
t\in(t_{n},t_{n+1}]
, where
g_{n}\in L^{p}(\Omega;\gamma(\ell^{2},X_{1/2}))
is
\mathscr{F}_{t_{n}}
-measurable. This leads to the more common scheme

(1.3)

Y_{n+1}\coloneq R_{\tau}Y_{n}+R_{\tau}g_{n}(W(t_{n+1})-W(t_{n})),\quad n\geq 0.

In this case (
1.2
) becomes

\mathbb{E}\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau\|AY_{n}\|^{p}_{X_{0}}\leq C^{p}\mathbb{E}\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau\|g_{n}\|^{p}_{\gamma(\ell^{2},X_{1/2}))}.

In most cases,
X_{1/2}
is a (subspace of a) (fractional) Sobolev space
H^{s,q}(D)
, and in this situation

\gamma(\ell^{2},X_{1/2})=H^{s,q}(D;\ell^{2}).

Our first main result is that this property can be characterized in the following way.

Theorem 1.2

(Characterization of Discrete Stochastic Maximal
\ell^{p}
-regularity)
.

Let
q\in[2,\infty)
and suppose that
X_{0}
is isomorphic to a closed subspace of
L^{q}(\mathcal{O})
with
(\mathcal{O},\Sigma,\mu)
a
\sigma
-finite measure space.
Suppose that Assumption
1.1
holds.
Then for any
p\in[2,\infty)
the following are equivalent:

(1)

A
has stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity;

(2)

R
has discrete stochastic maximal
\ell^{p}
-regularity.

As already mentioned, the continuous analogue in (
1
) was introduced in
[
2
,
46
,
48
]
. We will actually use the definition of
[
3
,
4
]
where the space regularity is shifted by
1/2
as this is more natural in applications to SPDEs (see Remark
3.3
for further information).

Theorem
1.2
remains valid for any space
X_{0}
satisfying certain geometric properties, specifically it holds if
X_{0}
is UMD and has type
2
. The strength of this result lies in the fact that it establishes discrete stochastic maximal regularity for a broad class of numerical schemes simultaneously. Moreover, it shows that it suffices to analyze the continuous-time setting, for which a well-developed theoretical framework already exists. Some of the immediate consequences for the discrete counterpart will be presented in Section
4
.

The proof proceeds in several steps, detailed in Subsections
3.3
,
3.4
, and
3.5
. Our approach not only establishes the equivalence in the stochastic setting but also suggests how similar equivalences could be obtained for other schemes in the deterministic setting. Indeed, in the deterministic case, such an equivalence was previously shown for the exponential Euler scheme in
[
29
]
and for the implicit Euler scheme in
[
31
]
. Our techniques can also be used to prove the equivalence for other schemes in the deterministic case.

An important consequence of Theorem
1.2
and a recent result in
[
4
]
is the following new result in the Hilbert space setting:

Corollary 1.3

(Discrete Stochastic Maximal
\ell^{p}
-regularity for free)
.

Suppose that
X_{0}
is a Hilbert space and that Assumption
1.1
holds. Then
R
has discrete stochastic maximal
\ell^{p}
-regularity for any
p\in[2,\infty)
.

In previous attempts, more structure was assumed on the operator
A
(e.g. variational structure or
H^{\infty}
-calculus). We show that this is not needed. The corresponding deterministic analogue also holds and has been established for various numerical schemes (see, for example,
[
12
, Proposition 2.7]
).

In addition to
\ell^{p}
-estimates, it is often important to obtain parabolic
\ell^{\infty}
-bounds for the sequence
(Y_{n})_{n\geq 1}
. In the continuous-time setting, such
maximal estimates
are available (see
[
46
]
), where they are derived from optimal mixed space-time regularity results, which in turn rely on the
H^{\infty}
-calculus of the operator
A
. In the discrete case, we establish the following natural analogue of these maximal estimates.

Theorem 1.4

(Maximal estimate with parabolic regularization)
.

Let
q\in[2,\infty)
and suppose that
X_{0}
is isomorphic to a closed subspace of
L^{q}(\mathcal{O})
with
(\mathcal{O},\Sigma,\mu)
a
\sigma
-finite measure space.
Suppose that Assumption
1.1
holds, that
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus on
X_{0}
of angle
<\pi/2
, and that
0\in\rho(A)
.
Then for any
p\in(2,\infty)
, there is a constant
C
such that for every
g\in L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\gamma(\ell^{2},X_{1/2})))
and every stepsize
\tau>0
,

\mathbb{E}\sup_{n\geq 1}\|Y_{n}\|_{X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p}}^{p}\leq C^{p}\mathbb{E}\|g\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\gamma(\ell^{2},X_{1/2}))},

where
Y=(Y_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is given by (
1.1
).

It is important to note that
X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p}
serves as the natural trace space associated with parabolic SPDEs. It is, in fact, the optimal space in which one can expect to obtain maximal estimates.

In the continuous-time setting, such estimates were proved using an optimal variant of the Da Prato–Kwapień–Zabczyk factorization method, combined with the operator-valued
H^{\infty}
-calculus,
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness of stochastic convolutions, and the analytical properties of the operator sum
d/dt+A
.
However, this approach does not carry over to the discrete setting, and a new argument is required. The key difference is that no natural identities or estimates for fractional derivatives in time are available in the discrete setting. To solve this, we embed the problem into a continuous-time setting by interpolating linearly and we estimate the fractional time derivatives by hand. To do this, many terms arise, all of which need a separate argument. Some of the leading-order terms are estimated through the operator-valued
H^{\infty}
-calculus and new
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness results for stochastic convolutions.

A natural question is whether Theorem
1.4
also holds in the case

p=2
. A key difference is that in this case there is no regularization. Indeed, if
X_{0}
is a Hilbert space then
X_{1/2,2}=X_{1/2}
(see
[
25
, Corollary C.4.2]
), so that the regularity space for the solution
(Y_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and the data
g
coincide. Still, the maximal estimate turns out to be true at least when
X_{0}
is a Hilbert space. Remarkably, it is enough to assume that either
0\in\rho(A)

or
that the
H^{\infty}
-calculus is bounded.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
X_{0}
be a Hilbert space. Suppose that Assumption
1.1
holds with
\theta=\pi/2
. Suppose that
0\in\rho(A)
or that
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus.
Then there is a constant
C
such that for every
g\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\gamma(\ell^{2},X_{1/2})))
and every stepsize
\tau>0
,

(1.4)

\mathbb{E}\sup_{n\geq 1}\|Y_{n}\|_{X_{1/2}}^{2}\leq C^{2}\mathbb{E}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\gamma(\ell^{2},X_{1/2}))}.

The assumption
r(\infty)=0
is not used in the proof of Proposition
1.5
.

Overview

In Section

we present some essential mathematical background, covering sectorial operators, functional calculus, and key properties of numerical approximation schemes
R_{\tau}=r(\tau A)

Section

formally defines discrete stochastic maximal

-regularity, denoted by
{\rm DSMR}(p,T)

In Section

Section

The main result of Section

and contains the maximal estimate of Theorem

t_{n}=\tau n

. If a sequence
z=(z_{n})_{n=0}^{N}

Whenever

For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}

a\lesssim b
in case there is an (unimportant) constant
C
such that
a\leq Cb
. Moreover, we write
a\simeq b
if
a\lesssim b
and

. In case we want to emphasize that
C

, we write
a\lesssim_{p}b
or
a\simeq_{p}b
.

For a Banach couple
(X_{0},X_{1})
, we use

X_{\alpha,p}\coloneq(X_{0},X_{1})_{\alpha,p}\ \ \text{and}\ \ X_{\alpha}\coloneq[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\alpha},\ \ \ \alpha\in(0,1),\ p\in(1,\infty),

for the real and complex interpolation spaces respectively.

The operator
A
will always be a sectorial operator on
X_{0}
with domain

. For

, let

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Sectorial operators, functional calculus, and interpolation

For details on sectorial operators, semigroup theory, and functional calculus, the reader is referred to
[
16
,
22
,
26
,
27
,
35
]
. We briefly recall some of the key concepts used throughout the paper. For details on interpolation theory the reader is referred to
[
25
,
55
]
and we will rely on standard results on real and complex interpolation. See the notation subsection at the end of the introduction.

Let
(A,D(A))
be a closed operator on a Banach space
X
. The operator
A
is called
sectorial
if the domain and the range of
A
are dense in
X
and there exists
\nu\in(0,\pi)
such that
\sigma(A)\subseteq\overline{\Sigma_{\nu}}
, where
\Sigma_{\nu}\coloneq\{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}\colon|\arg z|<\nu\}
, and there exists
C>0
such that

(2.1)

|\lambda|\|(\lambda-A)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C,\ \ \lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{\nu}}.

The
angle of sectoriality

\omega(A)\in[0,\pi)
is defined as the infimum over all
\nu
for which a
C
exists such that (
2.1
) holds.

If
\omega(A)<\pi/2
, then
-A
generates a strongly continuous semigroup
(e^{-tA})_{t\geq 0}
, which extends to a bounded analytic function on a sector.

2.1.1.
Functional calculus

Let
H^{1}(\Sigma_{\theta})
be the set of all holomorphic functions
f\colon\Sigma_{\theta}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\|f\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma_{\theta})}\coloneq\sup_{|\phi|<\theta}\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(se^{i\phi})|\frac{ds}{s}<\infty.

Moreover,
H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})
is the space of bounded holomorphic functions
f\colon\Sigma_{\theta}\to\mathbb{C}
equipped with the supremum norm.

If
A
is a sectorial operator of angle
\omega(A)\in[0,\pi)
and
f\in H^{1}(\Sigma_{\theta})
with
\theta\in(\omega(A),\pi)
then one can define the bounded operator
f(A)
by the Dunford integral (contour oriented counterclockwise)

f(A)\coloneq\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\nu}}f(z)(z-A)^{-1}\,dz,

where
\nu\in(\omega(A),\theta)
is chosen arbitrarily (see
[
26
, Section 10.2]
). Moreover, there is a constant
C=C(\theta,A)
such that

(2.2)

\sup_{t>0}\|f(tA)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma_{\theta})}.

The above
H^{1}
-calculus is useful and can be used for any sectorial operator. In many important cases one can even prove that for all
\nu\in(\omega(A),\theta)
and for every
f\in H^{1}(\Sigma_{\nu})\cap H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\nu})
one has

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|f(A)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\nu})}.

In this case we say that
A
has a
bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus
. One can show that (
2.3
) uniquely extends to all
f\in H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\nu})
. The infimum over all possible
\nu
is called
the angle of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus
.

In the paper, we will use the
H^{\infty}
-calculus as a black box to prove several new results. By now, large classes of sectorial operators
A
are known to have a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus. One could even say that on
L^{q}
-spaces the counterexamples are typically only rather academic. A comprehensive list of examples can be found in the notes of
[
26
, Chapter 10]
.

A general class of operators with a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus, in the case
X
is a Hilbert space, is given by the following: all operators
A
for which

-A
generates a contraction semigroup on
X
. In this case,
\omega(A)
coincides with the angle of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus. For details the reader is referred to
[
26
, Theorems 10.2.24 and 10.4.21]
.

Another class can be given on
X=L^{q}
for
q\in(1,\infty)
: all operators
A
for which
-A
generates a positive contraction semigroup on
X
. In this case, one obtains that the angle of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus is
\leq\pi/2
. Moreover, if
A
is also sectorial of angle
\omega(A)<\pi/2
, then one obtains that the angle of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus is
<\pi/2
as well (although the two angles might differ). Details can be found in
[
26
, Theorems 10.7.12 and 10.7.13]
.

2.1.2.
Standard estimates for semigroups

Let
(A,D(A))
be a sectorial operator of angle
<\pi/2
on
X
. It is well-known that
-A
generates a strongly continuous bounded analytic semigroup
(e^{-zA})_{z\in\Sigma_{\sigma}}
for some
\sigma>0
(see
[
26
, Appendix G]
). Moreover, letting
\lambda>0
, we can apply (
2.2
) to
f(z)=z^{\lambda}e^{-z}
, which is in
H^{1}(\Sigma_{\nu})
for any
\nu<\pi/2
, to obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\|(tA)^{\lambda}e^{-tA}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}<\infty.

This implies that, for any
\varepsilon>0
,
\sup_{t>0}\|t^{\varepsilon}A^{1+\varepsilon}e^{-tA}\|_{\mathcal{L}(D(A),X)}<\infty
. Interpolating the two bounds gives that there is an
M\geq 0
such that

(2.4)

\|A^{1+\varepsilon}e^{-tA}\|_{\mathcal{L}(V,X)}\leq\frac{M}{t^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}},\ t>0,

where
V
is any Banach space that is continuously embedded into
(X,D(A))_{1/2,\infty}
. For example, one can take
V=D(A^{1/2})
,
V=[X,D(A)]_{1/2}
or
V=(X,D(A))_{1/2,q}
with
q\in[1,\infty]
.

Another standard bound for a bounded strongly continuous semigroup is

(2.5)

\|e^{-tA}-e^{-sA}\|_{\mathcal{L}(V,X)}\leq M(t-s)^{\alpha},

where
\alpha\in[0,1]
and
V
is as above. Indeed, for
V=D(A)
this holds for
\alpha=1
and follows by writing
e^{-sA}-e^{-tA}=\int_{s}^{t}Ae^{-rA}dr
on
D(A)
, and using that
\sup_{r>0}\|e^{-rA}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}<\infty
. For
V=X
the bound (
2.5
) holds with
\alpha=0
. The general case follows by interpolation.

2.1.3.
Fractional powers

For a sectorial operator the fractional power
A^{z}
can be defined for any
z\in\mathbb{C}
via the so-called extended functional calculus (see
[
22
, Chapter 3]
and
[
27
, Chapter 15]
). In general, these operators are again closed unbounded operators.

The operator
A
is said to have
bounded imaginary powers (BIP)
if
A^{it}
extends to a bounded operator on
X
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular, this holds if
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus. Indeed, one can apply the calculus to the holomorphic and bounded function
z\mapsto z^{it}
. An important consequence of BIP is the following identification of the domains of the fractional powers and the complex interpolation spaces.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
A
is a sectorial operator and that
A
has BIP. Then for all
\theta\in(0,1)
,

D(A^{\theta})=[X,D(A)]_{\theta}

with equivalent norms.

A proof can be found in
[
22
, Theorem 6.6.9]
,
[
27
, Theorem 15.3.9]
,
[
55
, 1.15.3]
.

2.2.
Approximation schemes

Let
X
be a Banach space,
A
a sectorial operator on
X
with angle
\omega(A)<\frac{\pi}{2}
and let
(e^{-tA})_{t\geq 0}
be the bounded analytic strongly continuous semigroup generated by
-A
. An approximation scheme is a strongly continuous function
R\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathcal{L}(X)
with
R(0)=I
that approximates the semigroup in the sense that there are
\alpha>0
and a Banach space
V
with
V\hookrightarrow X
densely such that

(2.6)

\|R_{\tau}^{n}-e^{-n\tau A}\|_{\mathcal{L}(V,X)}\leq C\tau^{a},\qquad n\geq 0,\tau>0,

where we set
R_{\tau}\coloneq R(\tau)
and
C>0
is a constant independent of
n,\tau
. A common choice for
V
is the fractional domain
V=D(A^{\alpha})
. An approximation is called stable if there is a constant
C>0
such that

(2.7)

\|R_{\tau}^{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C,\quad n\geq 0,\tau>0.

Typical examples of stable approximation schemes are the exponential Euler method
R_{\tau}=e^{-\tau A}
, the implicit Euler method
R_{\tau}\coloneq(1+\tau A)^{-1}
, the Crank-Nicolson method
R_{\tau}\coloneq(1-\frac{1}{2}\tau A)(1+\frac{1}{2}\tau A)^{-1}
and, in general, any rational scheme
R_{\tau}\coloneq r(\tau A)
where
r\colon\Sigma_{\theta}\to\mathbb{C}
is a rational function that is consistent and
A(\theta)
-stable with
\theta\in(\omega(A),\frac{\pi}{2}]
(see
[
54
, Theorems 9.1 and 9.2]
). Recall that a rational function
r
is called

consistent of order
\ell\geq 1
if
|r(z)-e^{-z}|\leq C|z|^{\ell+1}
as
z\to 0
, and
A(\theta)
-stable if
|r(z)|\leq 1
for
z\in\Sigma_{\theta}
. Note that if
r
is
A(\theta)
-stable with
\theta\in(\omega(A),\frac{\pi}{2}]
we can write

r(z)=\gamma+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\gamma_{j,k}(a_{j}+z)^{-k},

where
\gamma=r(\infty)
,
K,J\in\mathbb{N}
and the poles
-a_{j}\notin\Sigma_{\theta}
. Hence, we can define

(2.8)

r(\tau A)\coloneq\gamma I+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\gamma_{j,k}(a_{j}+\tau A)^{-k},\quad\tau>0,

which is a bounded operator on
X
since
-a_{j}/\tau\in\rho(A)
.

We focus on the exponential Euler scheme
R_{\tau}=e^{-\tau A}
and on rational schemes
R_{\tau}=r(\tau A)
with the extra condition that
r(\infty)=0
since they possess stronger convergence and stability properties (see Lemma
2.3
). This excludes the Crank-Nicolson scheme, but includes the commonly used implicit Euler scheme, which is consistent of order
\ell=1
and
A(\pi/2)
-stable, and the sub-diagonal Padé approximation schemes given by the rational functions
r_{n,n+1}=P_{n}/Q_{n+1}
and
r_{n,n+2}=P_{n}/Q_{n+2}

(n\geq 1)
, where

\displaystyle P_{n}(z)\coloneq\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{(n+m-j)!\,n!}{(n+m)!\,j!\,(n-j)!}(-z)^{j},\qquad Q_{m}(z)\coloneq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{(n+m-j)!\,m!}{(n+m)!\,j!\,(m-j)!}z^{j},

which are consistent of order
\ell=2n+1
and
\ell=2n+2
respectively,
A(\pi/2)
-stable, and satisfy
r_{n,n+1}(\infty)=r_{n,n+2}(\infty)=0
(see
[
23
, Theorem 4.12]
). Another example is the Padé approximation scheme given by
r_{0,3}(z)=(1+z+z^{2}/2!+z^{3}/3!)^{-1}
, which is consistent of order
\ell=3
,
A(\theta)
-stable with
\theta\leq 88.23^{\circ}
and satisfies the extra condition
r(\infty)=0
(see
[
23
]
).

The following lemma provides powerful estimates for such rational functions
r
, and can be found in
[
54
, Lemmas 9.4 and 9.5]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\pi/2]
. Let
r\colon\Sigma_{\theta}\to\mathbb{C}
be a rational function that is consistent of order
\ell\geq 1
and
A(\theta)
-stable, and suppose that
r(\infty)=0
.
Let
\nu\in(0,\theta)
.
Then there exist constants
c=c(r,\nu)
and
C=C(r,\theta)
such that for all
z\in\Sigma_{\nu}
and
n\geq 1
,

(2.9)

\displaystyle|r(z)^{n}-e^{-nz}|

\displaystyle\leq Cn|z|^{\ell+1}e^{-cn|z|},

\displaystyle\quad|z|\leq 1,

(2.10)

\displaystyle|r(z)|^{n}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-cn|z|},

\displaystyle\quad|z|\leq 1,

(2.11)

\displaystyle|r(z)|^{n}

\displaystyle\leq C|z|^{-1}e^{-cn},

\displaystyle\quad|z|\geq 1.

Moreover, one can take
c\leq\cos\nu
so that
|e^{-z}|\leq e^{-c|z|}
for
z\in\Sigma_{\nu}
.

A key estimate needed in our proofs is an improvement of (
2.6
) and (
2.7
):

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
R_{\tau}=r(\tau A)
, where
r
is either the exponential function
r(z)=e^{-z}
, or
r\colon\Sigma_{\theta}\to\mathbb{C}
is a rational function which is consistent of order
\ell\geq 1
and
A(\theta)
-stable with
\theta\in(\omega(A),\frac{\pi}{2}]
, and
r(\infty)=0
. Then, there is a constant
C>0
that depends on
r,\theta
and
A
such that for every
\alpha\in[-1,1]
,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\|A^{\alpha}(e^{-\tau nA}-R_{\tau}^{n})\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{\tau^{\alpha}n^{\ell+\alpha}},

\displaystyle\quad n\geq 1,\ \tau>0,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\|A^{\alpha}R_{\tau}^{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{\tau^{\alpha}n^{\alpha}},

\displaystyle\quad n\geq 1,\ \tau>0.

The estimate (
2.12
) was proved in
[
24
, Theorem 3.1]
for
\alpha=1
and
\ell\geq 2
.
The estimate (
2.12
) for
\alpha\in[-1,0]
is already known, and can be found in
[
10
, Theorem 5.1]
. There it is even presented for general
\alpha\in[-\ell-1,0]
and with the condition
r(\infty)=0
replaced by
|r(\infty)|<1
. However, one can always reduce to
r(\infty)=0
by a standard trick (see
[
36
, Theorem 4.4]
).

Proof.

Note that (
2.13
) is immediate from (
2.12
) and the estimate
\sup_{t>0}t^{\alpha}\|A^{\alpha}e^{-tA}\|\leq C
.

Let
f_{n}(z)\coloneq z^{\alpha}\big(e^{-nz}-r(z)^{n}\big)
. Note that by (
2.2
), in order to prove (
2.12
), it suffices to establish the estimate
\|f_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma_{\theta})}\leq C\frac{1}{n^{\ell+\alpha}}
. To this end, let
\nu\in(\omega(A),\theta)
. By
(
2.9
), there are constants
C=C(r,\theta)
and
c=c(r,\nu)
such that for every
|z|\leq 1
in the sector
\Sigma_{\nu}
,

|e^{-nz}-r(z)^{n}|\leq Cn|z|^{\ell+1}e^{-cn|z|}

and consequently,

\displaystyle|f_{n}(z)|\leq Cn|z|^{\ell+1+\alpha}e^{-cn|z|}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|f_{n}(\rho e^{\pm\nu i})|\frac{d\rho}{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq Cn\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{\ell+\alpha}e^{-cn\rho}\,d\rho

\displaystyle\leq Cn\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho^{\ell+\alpha}e^{-cn\rho}\,d\rho

(2.14)

\displaystyle=Cc^{-(\ell+1+\alpha)}\Big(\frac{1}{n}\Big)^{\ell+\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\ell+\alpha}e^{-s}\,ds=\widetilde{C}\frac{1}{n^{\ell+\alpha}}.

In the case where
\alpha<1
,
by (
2.11
) we have that for
|z|\geq 1
in the sector
\Sigma_{\nu}
,

|r(z)^{n}|\leq C|z|^{-1}e^{-cn}.

Therefore, by the triangle inequality, and noting that
|e^{-z}|\leq e^{-c|z|}
for
z\in\Sigma_{\nu}
,

\displaystyle|f_{n}(z)|\leq C|z|^{\alpha}e^{-cn|z|}+C|z|^{-1+\alpha}e^{-cn}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}|f_{n}(\rho e^{\pm\nu i})|\frac{d\rho}{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\rho^{1-\alpha}}e^{-cn\rho}\,d\rho+Ce^{-cn}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\rho^{2-\alpha}}\,d\rho

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-cn\rho}\,d\rho+C(1-\alpha)^{-1}e^{-cn}\leq\widetilde{C}\frac{1}{n^{\ell+\alpha}}.

This shows that

\|f_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma_{\theta})}\coloneq\sup_{\nu\leq\theta}\int_{0}^{\infty}|f_{n}(\rho e^{\pm\nu i})|\frac{d\rho}{\rho}\leq\widetilde{C}\frac{1}{n^{\ell+\alpha}}.

Suppose now that
\alpha=1
. Note that
r(\infty)=0
and, by the maximum modulus principle,
|r(z)|<1
on the interior of
\Sigma_{\theta}
. Hence, there are constants
c=c(r,\nu)
and
C=C(r,\nu)
such that
|r(z)|^{2}\leq C|z|^{-2}
and
|r(z)|\leq e^{-c}
for
|z|\geq 1
in the sector
\Sigma_{\nu}
. Therefore, for
n\geq 2
and
z\in\Sigma_{\nu}
with
|z|\geq 1
,

|r(z)|^{n}\leq C|z|^{-2}e^{-c(n-2)}.

This implies that

\displaystyle|f_{n}(z)|\leq|z|e^{-cn|z|}+C|z|^{-1}e^{-c(n-2)}

and consequently

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}|f_{n}(\rho e^{\pm\nu i})|\frac{d\rho}{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-cn\rho}\,d\rho+Ce^{-c(n-2)}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\,d\rho\leq\widetilde{C}\frac{1}{n^{\ell+1}}.

This, together with (
2.14
), show that for
n\geq 2
,

\|f_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma_{\theta})}\coloneq\sup_{\nu\leq\theta}\int_{0}^{\infty}|f_{n}(\rho e^{\pm\nu i})|\frac{d\rho}{\rho}\leq C\frac{1}{n^{\ell+1}}.

For the case
n=1
, by the triangle inequality and since
\sup_{t>0}\|(tA)e^{-tA}\|<\infty
, it suffices to show that

\|(\tau A)r(\tau A)\|\leq C
uniformly in
\tau
.
By (
2.8
) and noting that
r(\infty)=0
we estimate

\displaystyle\|(\tau A)r(\tau A)\|

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{J}\gamma_{j,1}(\tau A)(a_{j}+\tau A)^{-1}+\sum_{k=2}^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\gamma_{j,k}(\tau A)(a_{j}+\tau A)^{-k}\Big\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{J}|\gamma_{j,1}|\,\|(\tfrac{\tau}{a_{j}}A)(1+\tfrac{\tau}{a_{j}}A)^{-1}\|+\sum_{k=2}^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{J}|\gamma_{j,k}|\,\|(\tau A)(a_{j}+\tau A)^{-k}\|\leq C

by noting that
\sup_{t>0}\|(tA)(1+tA)^{-1}\|\leq C
and by applying (
2.2
) for
f_{k,j}(z)=z/(a_{j}+z)^{k}
which belongs to
H^{1}(\Sigma_{\nu})
for
k\geq 2,j\geq 1
.
∎

2.3.
Stochastic integration

In principle, there is a full analogue of stochastic integration theory in infinite dimensions. However, geometric conditions on the underlying spaces are required. In order to give a satisfactory explanation of stochastic integration in a Banach space setting, we need
\gamma
-radonifying operators
\gamma(H,X)
where
H
is a Hilbert space and
X
a Banach space. For details we refer to
[
26
, Chapter 9]
. Specializing to Hilbert spaces
X
, this class of operators reduces to the Hilbert-Schmidt operators
\mathcal{L}_{2}(H,X)
. Moreover, in the important case
X=L^{q}(\mathcal{O})
, one can identify
\gamma(H,X)
with
L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
denote a probability space with filtration
\mathbb{F}=(\mathscr{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space. A bounded linear operator
W\colon L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
is said to be a
cylindrical Brownian motion
in
H
if the following are satisfied:

•

for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H)
the random variable
W(f)
is centered Gaussian;

•

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H)
with support in
[0,t]
,
W(f)
is
\mathscr{F}_{t}
-measurable;

•

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H)
with support in
[t,\infty]
,
W(f)
is independent of
\mathscr{F}_{t}
;

•

for all
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H)
we have
\mathbb{E}(W(f_{1})W(f_{2}))=(f_{1},f_{2})_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H)}
.

Given
W
, the process
t\mapsto W({\bf 1}_{(0,t]}h)
is a Brownian motion for each
h\in H
.

The way to think about
W
is that it is given by
t\mapsto\sum_{n\geq 1}W^{n}(t)h_{n}
, where

(W^{n})_{n\geq 1}
are independent standard
\mathbb{F}
-Brownian motions, and
(h_{n})_{n\geq 1}
an orthonormal basis for
H
. However, since the above series does not define an
H
-valued random variable, the definition is given in a weaker sense.

However, the following convergence property does hold: if
S\in\gamma(H,X)
, then

(2.15)

\displaystyle SW(t)\coloneq\sum_{k\geq 1}W(t)h_{k}Sh_{k},

where the convergence takes place in
L^{p}(\Omega;X)
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
.

A process
g\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega\to\mathcal{L}(H,X)
is called
H
-strongly progressively measurable
if for all
t\in[0,T]
,
g|_{[0,t]}
is strongly
\mathcal{B}([0,t])\otimes\mathscr{F}_{t}
-measurable (where
\mathcal{B}
denotes the Borel
\sigma
-algebra).

For
0\leq a<b\leq T
and a strongly
\mathscr{F}_{a}
-measurable
\xi\colon\Omega\to\gamma(H,X)
, the stochastic integral of
{\bf 1}_{(a,b]}\xi
is defined by

(2.16)

\int_{0}^{t}{\bf 1}_{(a,b]}\xi dW\coloneq\xi(W(b\wedge t)-W(a\wedge t)),

where the series can be shown to be convergent as in (
2.15
) by the independence of
\mathscr{F}_{a}
and
W(b\wedge t)-W(a\wedge t)
.

The space
L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}((0,T)\times\Omega;\gamma(H,X))
denotes the subspace of
L^{p}((0,T)\times\Omega;\gamma(H,X))
consisting of all strongly progressively measurable processes. It can be shown that this coincides with the closure of the adapted step processes of finite rank (see
[
45
, Proposition 2.10]
). Moreover, let
L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;\ell^{p}_{\tau}(Z))
denote the subspace of adapted elements in
L^{p}(\Omega;\ell^{p}_{\tau}(Z))
.

The following proposition provides a simple sufficient condition for stochastic integrability. For the definition of type
2
, see
[
26
, Chapter 7]
. Spaces of type
2
include
L^{q}
,
W^{s,q}
, and
H^{s,q}
etc. for
q\in[2,\infty)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. The definition of UMD can be found in
[
25
, Chapter 4]
. Spaces with UMD include
L^{q}
,
W^{s,q}
, and
H^{s,q}
with
q\in(1,\infty)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proposition 2.5

(One-sided Burkholder-Davis–Gundy inequality)
.

Let
X
be a UMD Banach space with type
2
. Then for every
p\in[0,\infty)
, the mapping
g\mapsto\int_{0}^{\cdot}g\,\differential W
extends to a continuous linear operator from
L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\gamma(H,X)))
into
L^{p}(\Omega;C_{b}([0,\infty);X))
. Moreover, for
p\in(0,\infty)
there exists a constant
C_{p,X}
such that for all
g\in L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\gamma(H,X)))
, the following estimate holds

\textstyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\geq 0}\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}g(s)\,\differential W(s)\Big\|_{X}^{p}\leq C_{p,X}^{p}\mathbb{E}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\gamma(H,X))}^{p}.

The above result is sharp for Hilbert spaces
X
. However, if
X
is not a Hilbert space, a more precise bound is available, which, though explicitly used only in Section
5
, forms the crucial basis for the proofs of Theorem
1.4
and Corollary
3.9
. For simplicity, we only formulate the result for
X=L^{q}(\mathcal{O})
. For details the reader is referred to
[
45
]
.

Proposition 2.6

(Two-sided Burkholder-Davis–Gundy inequality)
.

Let
X=L^{q}(\mathcal{O})
with
q\in(1,\infty)
. Then for every
p\in[0,\infty)
, the mapping
g\mapsto\int_{0}^{\cdot}g\,\differential W
extends to a continuous linear operator from
L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H))
into
L^{p}(\Omega;C_{b}([0,\infty);L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))
. Moreover, for
p\in(0,\infty)
and for all
g\in L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H)))
, the following two-sided estimate holds

\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\geq 0}\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}g(s)\,\differential W(s)\Big\|_{L^{q}}^{p}\eqsim_{p,q}\mathbb{E}\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H))}^{p}.

Observe that for
q\in[2,\infty)
, by identifying
\gamma(H,L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))=L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)
, and by Minkowski’s inequality,

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\gamma(H,L^{q}(\mathcal{O})))=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H))\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathcal{O};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H)),

which explains why Proposition
2.6
gives a sharper estimate, which is even two-sided.

3.
Discrete stochastic maximal regularity

In this section, we introduce discrete stochastic maximal regularity for arbitrary schemes. A definition of the continuous variant is more standard and can be found in Definition
3.5
. The main result of this section shows that under mild conditions on the scheme, discrete stochastic maximal
\ell^{p}
-regularity and stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity are equivalent. Since stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity is known to hold for a large class of operators
A
, we immediately obtain a wide range of examples for the discrete setting.

From now on, we fix a probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
with filtration
\mathbb{F}=(\mathscr{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, and a cylindrical Brownian motion
W
with respect to
\mathbb{F}
on
H
.
Moreover, we suppose that
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
are UMD spaces with type
2
such that
X_{1}\hookrightarrow X_{0}
densely. Let
X_{\alpha}\coloneq[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
denote the complex interpolation spaces.

In the rest of this section we suppose that Assumption
1.1
is satisfied.
By the assumptions on
A
, one sees that
-A
generates a bounded analytic strongly continuous semigroup, which will be denoted by
(e^{-tA})_{t\geq 0}
. Moreover, the fractional powers
A^{\alpha}
for
\alpha>0
are well-defined.

Let
T\in(0,+\infty]
. We say that
\tau>0
is
admissible
if
T/\tau\in\mathbb{N}
or
T=\infty
. For an admissible
\tau
let
\pi_{\tau}\coloneq\{t_{n}=n\tau\colon 0\leq n\leq N\}
be a uniform partition of
[0,T]
, where
N=T/\tau
if
T<\infty
, and
N=\infty
if
T=\infty
.
For
g\in L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;\gamma(H,X_{1/2})))
, we define
Y=(Y_{n})_{n=0}^{N}
recursively by

(3.1)

\begin{cases}Y_{n+1}\coloneq R_{\tau}Y_{n}+R_{\tau}\Delta_{n}I_{g},\quad n=0,\dots,N-1\\
Y_{0}\coloneq 0.\end{cases}

Here we set
I_{g}\coloneq\int_{0}^{\cdot}g(s)dW(s)
and

\Delta_{n}I_{g}\coloneq I_{g}(t_{n+1})-I_{g}(t_{n})=\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}g(s)dW(s).

Alternatively, we can write
Y_{n}
as a discrete stochastic convolution

(3.2)

Y_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}R_{\tau}^{n-j}\Delta_{j}I_{g},\quad n=1,\dots,N.

3.1.
Definition and basic properties

The following definition is central in this section and describes a certain regularity property of
Y=(Y_{n})_{n=0}^{N}
as defined in (
3.1
).

Definition 3.1

(Discrete Stochastic Maximal Regularity)
.

Suppose that Assumption
1.1
holds.
Let
T\in(0,+\infty]
and
p\in[2,+\infty)
. The scheme
R
is said to have
discrete stochastic
\ell^{p}
-maximal regularity
on
(0,T)
if there is a constant
C>0
such that, for every admissible
\tau>0
, every uniform partition
\pi_{\tau}\coloneq\{t_{n}=n\tau\colon 0\leq n\leq N\}
of
[0,T]
, for every
g\in L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;\gamma(H,X_{1/2})))

the approximation scheme
Y=(Y_{n})_{n=0}^{N}
given in (
3.1
) belongs to
X_{1}
a.s. and satisfies

(3.3)

\|AY\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\ell^{p}_{\tau}(X_{0}))}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;\gamma(H,X_{1/2})))}.

The least admissible constant
C
is denoted by
C_{{\rm DSMR}(p,T)}^{R}
. In case the above holds, we will write
R\in{\rm DSMR}(p,T)
.

The constant
C
is allowed to depend on
T
, but should be uniform in the stepsize
\tau
. Note that by (
3.2
) and since
Y_{0}=0
, (
3.3
) is equivalent to

\Big(\mathbb{E}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\tau\Big\|A\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}R_{\tau}^{n-j}\,\Delta_{j}I_{g}\Big\|^{p}_{X_{0}}\Big)^{1/p}\leq C\big(\mathbb{E}\|g\|_{L^{p}(0,T;\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))}^{p}\big)^{1/p}.

Some further properties of operators with discrete stochastic
\ell^{p}
-maximal regularity are collected in Section
4
.

Remark 3.2
.

(1)

It is always true that
Y_{n}\in X_{1}
a.s. since we assume
r(\infty)=0
(see (
2.8
)). If
r(\infty)\neq 0
one does not have
Y_{n}\in X_{1}
in general unless
A
is bounded.

(2)

One can replace the homogeneous norm
\|AY\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\ell^{p}_{\tau}(X_{0}))}
in (
3.3
) with the inhomogeneous one
\|Y\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\ell^{p}_{\tau}(X_{1}))}
. This leads to the following inhomogeneous version of discrete stochastic
\ell^{p}
-maximal regularity:

(3.4)

\|Y\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\ell^{p}_{\tau}(X_{1}))}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;\gamma(H,X_{1/2})))}.

Clearly (
3.4
) implies (
3.3
) and the two are equivalent if
0\in\rho(A)
. Furthermore, when restricted to finite time intervals
T<\infty
one need not assume that
0\in\rho(A)
. Indeed, by Proposition
2.5
and the stability result (
2.7
) we immediately obtain that

\displaystyle\|Y\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\ell^{p}_{\tau}(X_{1/2}))}\lesssim_{p,X_{0},R}T^{\frac{1}{p}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))}\leq T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{p}(0,T;\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))}.

The same estimate holds if
X_{1/2}
is replaced by
X_{\alpha}
(for both
Y
and
g
) for any
\alpha\in[0,1]
.

Remark 3.3
.

In case
A
has bounded imaginary powers (BIP) and
0\in\rho(A)
, there are several equivalent ways to formulate discrete stochastic
\ell^{p}
-maximal regularity, and the same applies to the continuous case, which will be defined below in Definition
3.5
. In particular, BIP holds if
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus.

Under the assumption that
A
has BIP,
A^{1/2}
defines an isomorphism from
X_{1/2}
to
X_{0}
, so that one could define
discrete stochastic
\ell^{p}
-maximal regularity as

\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}Y\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\ell^{p}_{\tau}(X_{0}))}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;\gamma(H,X_{0})))},

which is quite common. The advantage of (
3.3
) is that it aligns well with its deterministic variant, in which one has regularization from
X_{0}
into
X_{1}
.

Remark 3.4
.

In the special case where
g\coloneq\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}g_{n}{\bf 1}_{[t_{n},t_{n+1})}
with
g_{n}\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathscr{F}_{t_{n}},\mathbb{P};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))
, one can write

\Delta_{n}I_{g}=g_{n}\,\Delta W_{n},

where
\Delta_{n}W\coloneq W(t_{n+1})-W(t_{n})
and we used (
2.15
). This case is a popular choice for the discrete setting as well. Note that in this situation

\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;\gamma(H,X_{1/2})))}=\|(g_{n})_{n\geq 0}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\ell^{p}_{\tau}(\gamma(H,X_{1/2})))}.

3.2.
Equivalence of continuous and discrete
{\rm SMR}

The following definition is a special case of the definition of stochastic maximal regularity in
[
4
]
.

Definition 3.5

(Continuous Stochastic Maximal Regularity)
.

Suppose that Assumption
1.1
(
1
) holds.
Let
T\in(0,+\infty]
and
p\in[2,+\infty)
. The operator
A
is said to have
stochastic
L^{p}
-maximal regularity
on
(0,T)
if there is a constant
C>0
such that, for every
g\in L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;\gamma(H,X_{1/2})))
, the mild solution

(3.5)

\displaystyle y(t)\coloneq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)A}g(s)dW(s),\quad t\in(0,T),

belongs to
X_{1}
a.s. and satisfies

(3.6)

\|Ay\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;X_{0}))}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;\gamma(H,X_{1/2})))}.

The least admissible constant
C
is denoted by
C_{{\rm SMR}(p,T)}^{A}
. In case the above holds, we will write
A\in{\rm SMR}(p,T)
.

Some basic properties of stochastic
L^{p}
-maximal regularity are collected in Proposition
4.1
.

The main result of this section is the following.

Theorem 3.6
.

Suppose that Assumption
1.1
holds. Let

and

.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

A
has stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity on
(0,T)
;

(2)

R
has discrete stochastic maximal
\ell^{p}
-regularity on
(0,T)
.

Moreover,
C_{{\rm SMR}(p,T)}^{A}\leq C_{{\rm DSMR}(p,T)}^{R}\leq K(C_{{\rm SMR}(p,T)}^{A}+1)
,
where the constant
K
depends on
p
,
X_{0}
, the function
r
, and on the sectoriality constant and angle of
A
.

The proof of the implication from
{\rm SMR}
to
{\rm DSMR}
will be given in Subsections
3.3
and

for the exponential Euler and rational schemes, respectively. The converse is simpler and will be proved in Subsection

.
See Figure

for a flowchart of the proof of Theorem

. Furthermore, in Theorem

Corollary 3.11

(

for free on real interpolation spaces)
.

Let

be a sectorial operator of angle
<\pi/2
on a space

with UMD and type
2
, and suppose that

. Let
D_{A}(\alpha,q)\coloneq(E,D(A^{n}))_{\alpha/n,q}
where
0<\alpha<n
and

. Let
X_{i}\coloneq D_{A}(\alpha+i,q),\ i\in\{0,1\},
for some
\alpha>0
and

.
Suppose that Assumption
1.1
(
2
) holds. Then
R
has discrete maximal
\ell^{p}
-regularity on
(0,T)
for all

and

.

Proof.

It is well-known that
A
defines a sectorial operator of angle

on

. Moreover,

.

3.3.
{\rm SMR}
implies discrete
{\rm SMR}
for exponential Euler

As a first step, we prove that the exponential Euler method has discrete stochastic maximal regularity by a perturbation argument from the continuous-time setting.

Theorem 3.12
.

Suppose that Assumption
1.1
holds. Let
T\in(0,\infty]
and
p\in[2,\infty)
. If
A
has stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity on
(0,T)
, then the exponential Euler scheme
(EE)
has discrete maximal
\ell^{p}
-regularity. Moreover,

C^{(EE)}_{{\rm DSMR}(p,T)}\lesssim_{p,X,A}C^{A}_{{\rm SMR}(p,T)}+1,

where the implicit constant only depends on
p
,
X
, and the sectoriality constant and angle of
A
.

Proof.

Let
T\in(0,\infty]
and for admissible
\tau>0
let
\pi_{\tau}\coloneq\{t_{n}=n\tau\colon 0\leq n\leq N\}
be a uniform partition of
[0,T]
.
Let
g\in L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;\gamma(H,X_{1/2})))
. In order to shorten the notation, we write
\|g\|_{\Gamma_{p}}
for the latter
L^{p}
-norm.

Since
A
has
{\rm SMR}(p,T)
, we infer that

(3.8)

\Big(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}\Big\|A\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)A}g(s)\,dW(s)\Big\|_{X_{0}}^{p}dt\Big)^{1/p}\leq C^{A}_{{\rm SMR}(p,T)}\|g\|_{\Gamma_{p}}.

In order to show that the exponential Euler (EE) scheme has
{\rm DSMR}(p,T)
, it suffices to establish the estimate

\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\tau\,\mathbb{E}\Big\|A\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}e^{-\tau(n-j)A}\Delta_{j}I_{g}\Big\|_{X_{0}}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\leq C\|g\|_{\Gamma_{p}}.

Note that by Proposition
2.5
and (
2.4
) we can estimate the
j=n-1
term by

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\tau\,\mathbb{E}\|Ae^{-\tau A}\Delta_{n-1}I_{g}\|_{X_{0}}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,X_{0}}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\tau\,\mathbb{E}\|Ae^{-\tau A}g\|^{p}_{L^{2}((t_{n},t_{n+1});\gamma(H,X_{0}))}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,X_{0}}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\tau^{\frac{p}{2}}\,\mathbb{E}\|Ae^{-\tau A}g\|^{p}_{L^{p}((t_{n},t_{n+1});\gamma(H,X_{0}))}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,X_{0},A}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\|g\|^{p}_{L^{p}((t_{n},t_{n+1});\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\|g\|_{\Gamma_{p}}.

Therefore it remains to prove that

(3.9)

\Big(\sum_{n=2}^{N-1}\tau\,\mathbb{E}\Big\|A\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}e^{-\tau(n-j)A}\Delta_{j}I_{g}\Big\|_{X_{0}}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\leq C\|g\|_{\Gamma_{p}}.

By the triangle inequality and (
3.8
),
it suffices to establish the estimate

\Big(\sum_{n=2}^{N-1}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}\Big\|A\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}e^{-\tau(n-j)A}\Delta_{j}I_{g}-A\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)A}g(s)\,dW(s)\Big\|_{X_{0}}^{p}\,dt\Big)^{1/p}\leq C\|g\|_{\Gamma_{p}}.

To this end, let
2\leq n\leq N-1
and
t\in[t_{n},t_{n+1}]
. Note that for
j\leq n-1
we have that
{\bf 1}_{[t_{j},t_{j+1}\wedge t)}={\bf 1}_{[t_{j},t_{j+1})}
, whereas
{\bf 1}_{[t_{j},t_{j+1}\wedge t)}=0
for
j\geq n+1
and so by the triangle inequality

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{n=2}^{N-1}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}\Big\|A\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}e^{-\tau(n-j)A}\Delta_{j}I_{g}-A\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)A}g(s)\,dW(s)\Big\|_{X_{0}}^{p}dt\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{n=2}^{N-1}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\Phi(t,s,n,j)g(s)dW(s)\Big\|_{X_{0}}^{p}dt\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\sum_{n=2}^{N-1}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}\Big\|\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}Ae^{-(t-s)}g(s)dW(s)\Big\|_{X_{0}}^{p}dt\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\sum_{n=2}^{N-1}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{n}}^{t}Ae^{-(t-s)A}g(s)dW(s)\Big\|_{X_{0}}^{p}dt\Big)^{1/p},

where
\Phi(t,s,n,j)\coloneq A(e^{-\tau(n-j)A}-e^{-(t-s)A})
appears in the first and main term.

The latter two terms can easily be estimated using the stochastic maximal regularity of
A
.
Hence, it remains to prove the estimate

\sum_{n=2}^{N-1}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}\Big\|\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\Phi(t,s,n,j)g(s){\bf 1}_{[t_{j},t_{j+1})}(s)\Big)dW(s)\Big\|^{p}dt\leq C^{p}\|g\|_{\Gamma_{p}}^{p}.

By Proposition
2.5
, it is enough to estimate

(3.10)

\sum_{n=2}^{N-1}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\|\Phi(t,s,n,j)g(s)\|^{2}_{\gamma(H,X_{0})}\,ds\Big)^{p/2}dt\leq C^{p}\|g\|_{\Gamma_{p}}^{p}.

In order to estimate the latter, we first bound the operator norm of
\Phi(t,s,n,j)
. We claim that for
2\leq n\leq N-1
,
0\leq j\leq n-2
,
t_{n}\leq t\leq t_{n+1}
, and
t_{j}\leq s\leq t_{j+1}

\displaystyle\|\Phi(t,s,n,j)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1/2},X_{0})}\leq\|\Phi(t,s,n,j)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{0})}^{1/2}\|\Phi(t,s,n,j)\|^{1/2}_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0})}\lesssim\frac{1}{\tau^{1/2}(n-j-1)}.

Indeed, the first bound follows by interpolation and the
\|\Phi(t,s,n,j)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{0})}
-norm is clearly uniformly bounded. To estimate
\|\Phi(t,s,n,j)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0})}
, let
I_{nj}
denote the interval with endpoints
t-s
and
\tau(n-j)
. One can check that
|I_{nj}|\leq\tau
and
\min I_{nj}\geq\tau(n-j-1)
. Therefore,
we write

\displaystyle\|\Phi(t,s,n,j)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0})}\leq\int_{I_{nj}}\|A^{2}e^{-rA}\|dr\lesssim\frac{|I_{nj}|}{(\min I_{nj})^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\tau(n-j-1)^{2}},

which clearly implies the claim.
From the claim we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\|\Phi(t,s,n,j)g(s)\|^{2}_{\gamma(H,X_{0})}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\tau(n-j-1)^{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(t_{j},t_{j+1};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))}^{2}.

Using that the latter is
t
-independent, we see that the left-hand side of (
3.10
) is, up to a multiplicative constant, bounded by

\displaystyle\tau^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\mathbb{E}\sum_{n=2}^{N-1}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}{\bf 1}_{n-j-1\geq 1}\frac{1}{(n-j-1)^{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(t_{j},t_{j+1};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))}^{2}\Big)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(i)}}}{{\lesssim}}\tau^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\mathbb{E}\sum_{n=0}^{N-2}\|g\|_{L^{2}(t_{n},t_{n+1};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))}^{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(ii)}}}{{\leq}}\mathbb{E}\sum_{n=1}^{N-2}\|g\|_{L^{p}(t_{n},t_{n+1};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))}^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\mathbb{E}\|g\|_{L^{p}(0,T;\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))}^{p},

where we used the discrete case of Minkowski’s convolution inequality in (i) and Hölder’s inequality in (ii).
∎

3.4.
Equivalence of discrete
{\rm SMR}
for different schemes

Our next step is to compare rational schemes to the exponential Euler scheme.

Theorem 3.13
.

Suppose that Assumption
1.1
holds, where
R_{\tau}\coloneq r(\tau A)
is the rational scheme introduced there. Let
T\in(0,\infty]
and
p\in(2,\infty)
.
Then, the exponential Euler scheme has discrete maximal
\ell^{p}
-regularity if and only if
R
has discrete maximal
\ell^{p}
-regularity. Moreover,

C^{(EE)}_{{\rm DSMR}(p,T)}+1\eqsim_{p,X_{0},r,A}C^{R}_{{\rm DSMR}(p,T)}+1,

where the implicit constant only depends on
p
,
X_{0}
, the rational function
r
, and the sectoriality constant and angle of
A
.

Proof of Theorem
3.13
.

By Lemma
2.3
we get that

\|A(e^{-n\tau A}-r(\tau A)^{n})\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0})}\lesssim_{r,A}\tau^{-1}n^{-\ell-1}
and

\|A(e^{-n\tau A}-r(\tau A)^{n})\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{0})}\lesssim_{r,A}n^{-\ell}
. Hence by interpolation,

(3.11)

\|A(e^{-n\tau A}-r(\tau A)^{n})\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1/2},X_{0})}\lesssim_{r,A}\frac{1}{\tau^{1/2}n^{\ell+1/2}}.

Let
\tau>0
be admissible,
\pi_{\tau}\coloneq\{t_{n}=n\tau\colon 0\leq n\leq N\}
be a uniform partition of
[0,T]
and let
g\in L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;(\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))))
.
To prove the equivalence, by the triangle inequality, it suffices to bound the difference of the schemes uniformly in
\tau
. Thus it suffices to establish the estimate

(3.12)

\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\tau\,\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}A\big(e^{-\tau(n-j)A}-r(\tau A)^{n-j}\big)\Delta_{j}I_{g}\Big\|_{X_{0}}^{p}\leq C^{p}\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;(\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))))}^{p}.

By Proposition
2.5
and (
3.11
), we can estimate

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}A\big(e^{-\tau(n-j)A}-r(\tau A)^{n-j}\big)\Delta_{j}I_{g}\Big\|_{X_{0}}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,X_{0}}\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\,\|A\big(e^{-\tau(n-j)A}-r(\tau A)^{n-j}\big)g(s)\|^{2}_{\gamma(H,X_{0})}\,ds\Big)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\|A\big(e^{-\tau(n-j)A}-r(\tau A)^{n-j}\big)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1/2},X_{0})}^{2}\,\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(t_{j},t_{j+1};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))}\Big)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{r,A}\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\tau(n-j)^{2\ell+1}}\,\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(t_{j},t_{j+1};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))}\Big)^{p/2}.

Therefore, up to a multiplicative constant, the left-hand side of (
3.12
) can be estimated by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\tau\,\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\tau(n-j)^{2\ell+1}}\,\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(t_{j},t_{j+1};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))}\Big)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(i)}}}{{\lesssim}}\tau^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\mathbb{E}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\|g\|^{p}_{L^{2}(t_{n},t_{n+1};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(ii)}}}{{\leq}}\mathbb{E}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\|g\|^{p}_{L^{p}(t_{n},t_{n+1};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\|g\|^{p}_{L^{p}(0,T;\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))},

where we used the discrete case of Minkowski’s convolution inequality in (i) and Hölder’s inequality in (ii).
∎

As a consequence, we immediately obtain the following:

Corollary 3.14

(Equivalence of
{\rm DSMR}
for all rational schemes)
.

Suppose that Assumption
1.1
(
1
) holds. Let
T\in(0,\infty]
and
p\in[2,\infty)
. Let
r(z)
and
s(z)
be consistent of order
\geq 1
, and
A(\theta)
-stable rational functions with
\theta\in(\omega(A),\frac{\pi}{2}]
and
r(\infty)=s(\infty)=0
.
Let
R_{\tau}=r(\tau A)
and
S_{\tau}=s(\tau A)
.
Then, the scheme
R
has discrete stochastic maximal
\ell^{p}
-regularity on
(0,T)
if and only if
S
has discrete stochastic maximal
\ell^{p}
-regularity on
(0,T)
.

3.5.
Discrete SMR implies SMR

To prove that discrete stochastic maximal regularity implies its continuous analogue, we need a result on the convergence of the underlying scheme. The literature contains many results of this type, and the reader is, for instance, referred to
[
21
,
28
,
40
]
and references therein for an overview on this topic. The main feature of the result below is that the convergence rate seems to be optimal and contains parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
, which entail parabolic smoothing. Even the choice
\alpha=\beta=0
seems to be new in the setting we consider.

Proposition 3.15
.

Suppose that Assumption
1.1
holds. Let
0\leq\alpha\leq\beta\leq 1
be such that
\beta-\alpha<1/2
.
Let
T\in(0,\infty]
and
p\in[2,\infty)
. For admissible
\tau
, let
\pi_{\tau}\coloneq\{t_{n}=n\tau\colon 0\leq n\leq N\}
be a uniform partition of
[0,T]
with
N=T/\tau
.
Then there is a constant
C
depending only on
\alpha,\beta,p,X_{0},X_{1}
, the sectoriality constants of
A
, and the function
r
, such that for every piecewise constant
g\coloneq\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}g_{n}{\bf 1}_{[t_{n},t_{n+1})}
with
g_{n}\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathscr{F}_{t_{n}},\mathbb{P};\gamma(H,X_{\alpha}))
,
0\leq n\leq N-1
,

\Big(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\|A^{\beta}(y-Y_{n})\|^{p}_{L^{p}((t_{n},t_{n+1})\times\Omega;X_{0})}\Big)^{1/p}\leq C\tau^{\frac{1}{2}+\alpha-\beta}\|g\|_{L^{p}((0,T)\times\Omega;\gamma(H,X_{\alpha}))},

where
Y
is the discrete solution defined in (
3.1
) and
y
is the mild solution as defined in (
3.5
).

The constant
C
does not depend on
\tau
and
T
. The above result can be seen as an optimal convergence result with parabolic smoothing.

Proof.

Let
n\geq 0
and
t\in(t_{n},t_{n+1}]
.
We split the expression as

\displaystyle\|A^{\beta}(y(t)-Y_{n})\|_{X_{0}}

\displaystyle=\Big\|A^{\beta}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)A}g(s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s)-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}A^{\beta}R_{\tau}^{n-j}\Delta_{j}I_{g}\Big\|_{X_{0}}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}A^{\beta}(e^{-(t-s)A}-R_{\tau}^{n-j})g_{j}\,\mathrm{d}W(s)+\int_{t_{n}}^{t}A^{\beta}e^{-(t-s)A}g_{n}\,\mathrm{d}W(s)\Big\|_{X_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}A^{\beta}(e^{-(t-s)A}-e^{-\tau(n-j)A})g_{j}\,\mathrm{d}W(s)\Big\|_{X_{0}}

\displaystyle\ +\Big\|\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}A^{\beta}(e^{-\tau(n-j)A}-R_{\tau}^{n-j})g_{j}\,\mathrm{d}W(s)\Big\|_{X_{0}}+\Big\|\int_{t_{n}}^{t}A^{\beta}e^{-(t-s)A}g_{n}\,\mathrm{d}W(s)\Big\|_{X_{0}}

\displaystyle\coloneq I_{1,n}(t)+I_{2,n}(t)+I_{3,n}(t),

where we set
I_{1,0}=I_{2,0}=0
.

We estimate the
L^{p}(\Omega)
norms of
I_{1,n}(t),I_{2,n}(t)
and
I_{3,n}(t)
as follows. For
I_{3,n}(t)
, Proposition
2.5
gives that

\displaystyle\|I_{3,n}(t)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{t_{n}}^{t}\|A^{\beta}e^{-(t-s)A}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}(X_{\alpha},X_{0})}\|g_{n}\|_{\gamma(H,X_{\alpha})}^{2}ds\Big)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{t_{n}}^{t}(t-s)^{-2(\beta-\alpha)}\|g_{n}\|_{\gamma(H,X_{\alpha})}^{2}ds\Big)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{p(\frac{1}{2}-(\beta-\alpha))}\mathbb{E}\|g_{n}\|^{p}_{\gamma(H,X_{\alpha})}.

To estimate
I_{1,n}(t)
, note that by Proposition
2.5
,

\displaystyle\|I_{1,n}(t)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\|A^{\beta}(e^{-(t-s)A}-e^{-\tau(n-j)A})g_{j}\|^{2}_{\gamma(H,X_{0})}\,ds\Big)^{p/2}

(3.13)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\|A^{\beta}(e^{-(t-s)A}-e^{-\tau(n-j)A})\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{\alpha},X_{0})}^{2}\,\|g_{j}\|^{2}_{\gamma(H,X_{\alpha})}\,ds\Big)^{p/2}.

For the term
j=n-1
and
s\in(t_{n-1},t_{n})
we can estimate

\displaystyle\|A^{\beta}(e^{-(t-s)A}-e^{-\tau A})\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{\alpha},X_{0})}\leq\|A^{\beta}e^{-(t-s)A}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{\alpha},X_{0})}+\|A^{\beta}e^{-\tau A}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{\alpha},X_{0})}\,\lesssim(t-s)^{-(\beta-\alpha)}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\|A^{\beta}(e^{-(t-s)A}-e^{-\tau A})\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{\alpha},X_{0})}^{2}\,\|g_{n-1}\|^{2}_{\gamma(H,X_{\alpha})}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}(t-s)^{-2(\beta-\alpha)}\|g_{n-1}\|^{2}_{\gamma(H,X_{\alpha})}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{1-2(\beta-\alpha)}\|g_{n-1}\|^{2}_{\gamma(H,X_{\alpha})}

and thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\|A^{\beta}(e^{-(t-s)A}-e^{-\tau A})\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{\alpha},X_{0})}^{2}\,\|g_{n-1}\|^{2}_{\gamma(H,X_{\alpha})}\,ds\Big)^{p/2}\lesssim\tau^{p(\frac{1}{2}-(\beta-\alpha))}\mathbb{E}\|g_{n-1}\|^{p}_{\gamma(H,X_{\alpha})}.

For
j\leq n-2
and
s\in(t_{j},t_{j+1}]
, by (
2.5
) we can estimate

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle A^{\beta}(e^{-(t-s)A}-e^{-\tau(n-j)A})\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{\alpha},X_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\|A^{\beta-1}(e^{-(t-s-(n-j-1)\tau)A}-e^{-\tau A})\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{\beta},X_{0})}\|Ae^{-(n-j-1)\tau A}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{\alpha},X_{\beta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(t-s-\tau(n-j))((n-j-1)\tau)^{-(1+\beta-\alpha)}\leq\tau^{-(\beta-\alpha)}(n-j-1)^{-(1+\beta-\alpha)}.

Substituting this into (
3.13
) gives

\|I_{1,n}(t)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\tau^{p(\frac{1}{2}-(\beta-\alpha))}\Big[\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\frac{1}{(n-j-1)^{2(1+\beta-\alpha)}}\|g_{j}\|^{2}_{\gamma(H,X_{\alpha})}\Big)^{p/2}+\mathbb{E}\|g_{n-1}\|^{p}_{\gamma(H,X_{\alpha})}\Big].

We now estimate
I_{2,n}(t)
. By Proposition
2.5
,

\displaystyle\|I_{2,n}(t)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\|A^{\beta}(e^{-\tau(n-j)A}-R_{\tau}^{n-j})g_{j}\|^{2}_{\gamma(H,X_{0})}\,ds\Big)^{p/2}

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\|A^{\beta}(e^{-\tau(n-j)A}-R_{\tau}^{n-j})\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{\alpha},X_{0})}^{2}\,\|g_{j}\|^{2}_{\gamma(H,X_{\alpha})}\,ds\Big)^{p/2}.

By (
2.12
), we get that
\|A^{\beta}(e^{-\tau(n-j)A}-R_{\tau}^{n-j})\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{\alpha},X_{0})}\lesssim\tau^{-(\beta-\alpha)}(n-j)^{-1-(\beta-\alpha)}
and thus (
3.14
) gives that

\|I_{2,n}(t)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\tau^{p(\frac{1}{2}-(\beta-\alpha))}\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{(n-j)^{2(1+\beta-\alpha)}}\|g_{j}\|^{2}_{\gamma(H,X_{\alpha})}\,ds\Big)^{p/2}.

Finally, applying the discrete case of Minkowski’s convolution inequality, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\|A^{\beta}(y-Y_{n})\|^{p}_{L^{p}((t_{n},t_{n+1})\times\Omega;X_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{3}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|I_{k,n}(t)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{p(\frac{1}{2}-(\beta-\alpha))}\mathbb{E}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\tau\|g_{n}\|^{p}_{\gamma(H,X_{\alpha})}.\qed

Remark 3.16
.

Proposition
3.15
seems to be new in this generality. Under the assumption that
A
has deterministic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity and
0\in\rho(A)
, a similar result was shown in
[
37
, Theorem 4.1]
for
\alpha=\beta=0
and for the implicit Euler scheme with an entirely different method.

Theorem 3.17
.

Suppose that Assumption
1.1
holds. Let
T\in(0,\infty]
and
p\in[2,\infty)
.
If
R
has discrete stochastic
\ell^{p}
-maximal regularity on
(0,T)
, then
A
has stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity on
(0,T)
.

Proof.

Let
\tau_{0}>0
be admissible and fixed and let
\pi_{\tau_{0}}\coloneq\{t_{n}^{0}=n\tau_{0}\colon\ 0\leq n\leq N_{0}\}
be a uniform partition of
[0,T]
. Let
g\coloneq\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}g_{n}{\bf 1}_{[t_{n}^{0},t_{n+1}^{0})}
be fixed but arbitrary, where each
g_{n}\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathscr{F}_{t_{n}},\mathbb{P};\gamma(H,D(A))
and let
y(t)\coloneq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)A}g(s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s)
. Since the set of such processes
g
is dense in
L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}((0,T)\times\Omega;\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))
, it suffices to show that

(3.15)

\displaystyle\|Ay\|_{L^{p}((0,T)\times\Omega;X_{0})}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{p}((0,T)\times\Omega;\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))}.

For each integer
k\geq 1
let
\tau_{k}=\tau_{0}/k
and let
\pi_{\tau_{k}}\coloneq\{t_{n}^{k}=n\tau_{k}\colon 0\leq n\leq N_{k}\}
with
N_{k}=T/\tau
if
T<\infty
and
N_{k}=\infty
otherwise. We can write
g=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{k}-1}g_{n}^{k}{\bf 1}_{[t_{n}^{k},t_{n+1}^{k})}
, where each
g_{n}^{k}\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathscr{F}_{t_{n}^{k}},\mathbb{P};\gamma(H,X_{1}))
and let
Y_{n}^{k}\coloneq\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}R_{\tau_{k}}^{n-j}\Delta_{j}I_{g}
, where
\Delta_{j}I_{g}\coloneq I_{g}(t_{j+1}^{k})-I_{g}(t_{j}^{k})
.
Proposition
3.15
gives that

\Big(\sum_{n=0}^{N_{k}-1}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}\|AY_{n}^{k}-Ay(t)\|^{p}_{X_{0}}dt\Big)^{1/p}\leq C\tau_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{p}((0,T)\times\Omega;\gamma(H,X_{1}))}

and since
R
has
{\rm DSMR}(p,T)
,

\Big(\sum_{n=0}^{N_{k}-1}\tau\mathbb{E}\|AY_{n}^{k}\|^{p}_{X_{0}}\Big)^{1/p}\leq C_{\text{DSMR}(p,T)}^{R}\|g\|_{L^{p}((0,T)\times\Omega;\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))}.

Hence, by the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\|Ay\|_{L^{p}((0,T)\times\Omega;X_{0})}\leq C\tau_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{p}((0,T)\times\Omega;\gamma(H,X_{1}))}+C_{{\rm DSMR}(p,T)}^{R}\|g\|_{L^{p}((0,T)\times\Omega;\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))},

and by letting
k\to\infty
, we obtain
(
3.15
). This shows that
A
has stochastic
L^{p}
-maximal regularity on
(0,T)
with constant
C_{\text{SMR}(p,T)}^{A}\leq C_{{\rm DSMR}(p,T)}^{R}
.

∎

Observe that Theorem
3.6
follows by combining Theorems
3.12
,
3.13
, and
3.17
. In particular, this proves Theorem
1.2
.

4.
Permanence properties

Before we move on to the proof of the maximal estimate of Theorem
1.4
, we discuss some simple properties of discrete stochastic maximal
\ell^{p}
-regularity, which we can now deduce from Theorem
3.6
. Similar results have been discussed in the continuous-time setting in the deterministic case in
[
14
]
(also see
[
27
, Section 17.2.e]
), and in the stochastic case in
[
2
,
41
]
. In Subsection
4.1
, we recall some of the required permanence properties in continuous time, and in Subsection

derive its discrete analogues. Finally, in Subsection

we present a weighted extrapolation result for
{\rm DSMR}
.

4.1.
The continuous-time setting

In the next result, we first collect some continuous-time results which can be found in
[
2
,
41
]
. The definition in the latter two papers differs from the one we use here, since we shifted the smoothness by
1/2
, and consider complex interpolation spaces instead of fractional powers. Therefore, we need to indicate the necessary changes in the proofs.

Note that Definition
3.5
does not require Assumption
1.1
, but it is enough to assume that
-A
generates a strongly continuous analytic semigroup.

Proposition 4.1

(Continuous-time setting)
.

Let
-A
generate a strongly continuous analytic semigroup on
X_{0}
. Let
p\in[2,\infty)
and
T\in(0,\infty]
. Suppose that
A
has stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity on
(0,T)
with respect to a cylindrical Brownian motion on
H
with
\text{dim}(H)\geq 1
. Then the following hold:

(1)

If

and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, then

;

(2)

If

and
\real(\lambda)\geq 0
, then

;

(3)

If
T<\infty
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\|e^{-tA}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0})}=0
, then
A\in{\rm SMR}(p,\infty)
;

(4)

If
\widetilde{T}\in(0,\infty)
, then
A\in{\rm SMR}(p,\widetilde{T})
;

(5)

If
q\in(2,\infty)
, then
A\in{\rm SMR}(q,T)
;

(6)

If
\widetilde{H}
is another Hilbert space, then
A\in{\rm SMR}(p,T)
with respect to any cylindrical Brownian motion on
\widetilde{H}
.

Proof.

(
1
), (
2
): This can be proved in a similar way as
[
2
, Proposition 3.8]
.

(
3
): The proof of
[
2
, Theorem 5.2]
extends to our setting.

(
4
): The same argument as in
[
2
, Proposition 5.1 and Corollary 5.3]
can be applied.

(

): The
p
-independence for

can be proved as in
[
41
, Theorem 8.2]
. If
T<\infty
, then we can use a simple shift argument. Let

be such that

. By (

),

, and thus
\lambda+A\in{\rm SMR}(p,\infty)

). Hence
\lambda+A\in{\rm SMR}(q,\infty)
. By (

) and (

) this implies

.

(
6
): This can be proved in a similar way as in
[
2
, Theorem 3.9]
.
∎

We do not know whether the assumption that

generates a strongly continuous analytic semigroup on

can be weakened. Some results in this direction can be found in
[
2
, Theorem 4.1]
.

4.3.
Weighted extrapolation

In the previous result, we saw that discrete stochastic maximal regularity is independent of
p
,
T
, and
H
. Below, we will show that it is also equivalent to a weighted variant. In the continuous-time setting, such weighted results form a central tool in the theory of evolution equations (see
[
4
,
50
,
57
]
). The following can be seen as the discrete stochastic analogue of
[
2
, Theorem 7.9]
and
[
49
, Theorem 2.4]
.

To extend Definition
3.1
to the weighted setting, let
p\in(2,\infty)
,
\alpha\in(-1,\frac{p}{2}-1)
and set
w_{\alpha}(t)=t^{\alpha}
. We say that
R\in{\rm DSMR}(p,\alpha,T)
in case Definition
3.1
is satisfied with the estimate (
3.3
) replaced by

\|AY\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\ell^{p}_{\tau,w_{\alpha}}(X_{0}))}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T,w_{\alpha};\gamma(H,X_{1/2})))}.

Here, for
y=(y_{n})_{n\geq 0}
in
X_{0}
, we set

\|y\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau,w_{\alpha}}(X_{0})}=\Big(\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau w_{\alpha}((n+1)\tau)\|y_{n}\|_{X_{0}}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}=\Big(\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau w_{\alpha}(t_{n+1})\|y_{n}\|_{X_{0}}^{p}\Big)^{1/p},

and for
f\colon(0,T)\to Z
we write

\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T,w_{\alpha};Z)}=\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|f(t)\|_{Z}^{p}w_{\alpha}(t)dt\Big)^{1/p}.

In a similar way, one can define
A\in{\rm SMR}(p,\alpha,T)
by including weights in Definition
3.5
.

The following result is a weighted extension of Theorem
3.6
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose that Assumption
1.1
holds. Let
p\in(2,\infty)
,
\alpha\in(-1,\frac{p}{2}-1)
, and
T\in(0,\infty]
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

A
has
{\rm SMR}(p,T)
;

(2)

R
has
{\rm DSMR}(p,T)
;

(3)

A
has
{\rm SMR}(p,\alpha,T)
;

(4)

R
has
{\rm DSMR}(p,\alpha,T)
.

This result will be derived from a more general extrapolation result for more general kernels.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
Y
be a Banach space, and
Z
be a Banach space with UMD and type
2
. Let
\Delta=\{(n,j)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\colon 0\leq j<n\}
.
Let
p\in(2,\infty)
and
\alpha\in(-1,\frac{p}{2}-1)
.
Suppose that
K\colon\Delta\to\mathcal{L}(Y,Z)
is such that, for some constant
M\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\|K(n,j)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y,Z)}\leq\frac{M}{(\tau(n-j))^{1/2}},\quad(n,j)\in\Delta.

For an adapted step process
g\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega\to\gamma(H,Y)
, let
S_{K}g
be the
L^{p}(\Omega;Y)
-valued sequence given by

(S_{K}g)_{0}\coloneq 0\quad\text{ and }\quad(S_{K}g)_{n}\coloneq\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}K(n,j)\Delta_{j}I_{g},\quad n\geq 1.

Then the following are equivalent

(1)

S_{K}
extends to a bounded operator with

\|S_{K}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;\gamma(H,Y))),L^{p}(\Omega;\ell^{p}_{\tau}(Z)))}\leq C_{1};

(2)

S_{K}
extends to a bounded operator with

\|S_{K}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T,w_{\alpha};\gamma(H,Y))),L^{p}(\Omega;\ell^{p}_{\tau,w_{\alpha}}(Z)))}\leq C_{2}.

Moreover, there is a constant
C_{\alpha,p,Z}
only depending on
\alpha,p,Z
such that
C_{1}\leq C_{2}+C_{\alpha,p,Z}M
and
C_{2}\leq C_{1}+C_{\alpha,p,Z}M
.

To prove this, we need the following.

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose that the assumptions of Proposition
4.4
are satisfied. Let
\beta\in(-\infty,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})
. Then
S_{K,\beta}\colon L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;\gamma(H,Y)))\to L^{p}(\Omega;\ell^{p}_{\tau}(Z))
defined by

(S_{K,\beta}g)_{0}\coloneq 0\quad\text{ and }\quad(S_{K,\beta}g)_{n}\coloneq\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}K(n,j)\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}[(t_{n+1}/s)^{\beta}-1]g(s)dW(s),\quad n\geq 1,

is bounded of norm
\|S_{K,\beta}\|\leq C_{p,Z}C_{\beta}M
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.5

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|(S_{K,\beta}g)_{n}\|^{p}_{Z}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,Z}^{p}\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\|K(n,j)[(t_{n+1}/s)^{\beta}-1]g(s)\|_{\gamma(H,Z)}^{2}ds\Big)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,Z}^{p}M^{p}\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\frac{1}{t_{n+1}-t_{j+1}}|(t_{n+1}/s)^{\beta}-1|^{2}\|g(s)\|_{\gamma(H,Y)}^{2}ds\Big)^{p/2}.

It is elementary to check that for any
t\in(t_{n+1},t_{n+2})
and
s\in(t_{j},t_{j+1})
,

(4.1)

\frac{1}{t_{n+1}-t_{j+1}}|(t_{n+1}/s)^{\beta}-1|^{2}\leq\frac{3}{t-s}|(t/s)^{\beta}-1|^{2}.

Therefore, since
(S_{K,\beta}g)_{0}=0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau\mathbb{E}\|(S_{K,\beta}g)_{n}\|^{p}_{Z}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\int_{t_{n+1}}^{t_{n+2}}\mathbb{E}\|(S_{K,\beta}g)_{n}\|^{p}_{Z}dt

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p/2}(C_{p,Z})^{p}M^{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{t-s}|(t/s)^{\beta}-1|^{2}\|g(s)\|_{\gamma(H,Y)}^{2}ds\Big)^{p/2}dt

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p/2}(C_{p,Z})^{p}M^{p}C_{p,\beta}^{p}\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;\gamma(H,Y)))}^{p},

where in the last step we used an estimate of the proof of
[
2
, Lemma 7.11]
.
∎

By the above lemma, the proof of the proposition follows from the identity

(S_{K}g)_{n}=t_{n+1}^{\beta}(S_{K}g_{-\beta})_{n}-(S_{K,\beta}g)_{n},

where
\beta=\alpha/p
,

. Since the argument is almost identical to
[

, Theorem 7.10]
, the details are left to the reader.
∎

5.
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness of discrete stochastic convolutions

One of the ingredients in the proof of the maximal estimate in Theorem
1.4
is an
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness result for discrete stochastic convolutions. Here, the prefix
\mathcal{R}
does not refer to the scheme, and therefore we have chosen to use a calligraphic letter instead.
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness plays a crucial role in vector-valued harmonic and stochastic analysis, and the letter
\mathcal{R}
refers to Rademacher or random. For an overview of
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness and its role in analysis, the reader is referred to
[
26
, Chapter 8]
.

5.1.
Definitions

Let
(r_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a Rademacher sequence, i.e.
\mathbb{P}(r_{n}=+1)=\mathbb{P}(r_{n}=-1)=1/2
, and the random variables
(r_{n})_{n\geq 1}
are independent.
Let
Y
and
Z
be Banach spaces. A family of operators
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(Y,Z)
is said to be
\mathcal{R}
-bounded
if there exists a constant
C\geq 0
such that for all finite sequences
(T_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
in
\mathcal{T}
and
(y_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
in
Y
,

\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}T_{n}y_{n}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;Z)}\leq C\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}y_{n}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;Y)}.

The least admissible constant in the above estimate is called the
\mathcal{R}
-bound
of
\mathcal{T}

and is denoted by
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{T})
.

5.2.
Main
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness result

In the continuous-time setting
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness of stochastic convolutions was obtained in
[
46
,
47
]
in order to establish stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity. These methods were extended to a discrete setting for implicit Euler in
[
37
]
, to establish discrete stochastic maximal regularity.

Unless stated otherwise, in the rest of this section
X_{0}
is assumed to be isomorphic to a closed subspace of
L^{q}(\mathcal{O})
with
(\mathcal{O},\Sigma,\mu)
a
\sigma
-finite measure space. Given a stepsize
\tau>0
,
\mathcal{K}_{\tau}
denotes the set of all sequences
k=(k_{n})_{n\geq 1}
such that
k_{n}\to 0
and

\sum_{n\geq 1}\sqrt{n\tau}\,|k_{n+1}-k_{n}|\leq 1.

For
k=(k_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\mathcal{K}_{\tau}
and an elementary adapted process
g\colon[0,\infty)\times\Omega\to L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)
we define the process
I^{\tau}(k)g\colon\mathbb{N}\times\Omega\to L^{q}(\mathcal{O})
via

(I^{\tau}(k)g)_{0}\coloneq 0\quad\text{and }\quad(I^{\tau}(k)g)_{n}\coloneq\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}k_{n-j}\Delta_{j}I_{g},\quad n\geq 1,

where we recall that
I_{g}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}gdW
and
\Delta_{j}I_{g}=I_{g}(t_{j+1})-I_{g}(t_{j})
.
Finally, let
\mathcal{I}^{\tau}\coloneq\{I^{\tau}(k)\colon k\in\mathcal{K}_{\tau}\}
.

Recall that the notation for the function spaces with weights
w_{\alpha}(t)=t^{\alpha}
, was introduced in Subsection
4.3
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
q\in[2,\infty)
. Let
p\in(2,\infty)
and
\alpha\in(-1,\frac{p}{2}-1)
. In case
q=2
, we additionally allow
p=2
and
\alpha=0
. Then there exists a constant
C_{p,q,\alpha}
such that for all
\tau>0
the family

\mathcal{I}^{\tau}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H))),L^{p}(\Omega;\ell^{p}_{\tau,w_{\alpha}}(L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))))

is
\mathcal{R}
-bounded by
C_{p,q,\alpha}
.

Proof.

Let
g
be elementary adapted. For
k=(k_{n})\in\mathcal{K}_{\tau}
writing

k_{n}=-\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}(k_{m+1}-k_{m})
gives

\displaystyle(I^{\tau}(k)g)_{n}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(k_{m+1}-k_{m}){\bf 1}_{j\leq n-1}{\bf 1}_{n-j\leq m}\Delta_{j}I_{g}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sqrt{m}(k_{m+1}-k_{m})\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}{\bf 1}_{j\leq n-1}{\bf 1}_{j\geq n-m}\Delta_{j}I_{g}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sqrt{m\tau}(k_{m+1}-k_{m})\,(J^{\tau}(m)g)_{n},

where the process
J^{\tau}(m)g\colon\mathbb{N}\times\Omega\to L^{q}(\mathcal{O})
is given by
(J^{\tau}(m)g)_{0}\coloneq 0
and for
n\geq 1
,

(J^{\tau}(m)g)_{n}\coloneq\frac{1}{\sqrt{m\tau}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\bf 1}_{j\leq n-1}{\bf 1}_{j\geq n-m}\Delta_{j}I_{g}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}k_{n-j}^{(m)}\Delta_{j}I_{g},

where
k_{n}^{(m)}\coloneq\frac{1}{\sqrt{m\tau}}{\bf 1}_{1\leq n\leq m}
.

It follows from the above that
\mathcal{I}^{\tau}
is contained in the closure of the absolute convex hull of
\mathcal{J}^{\tau}\coloneq\{J^{\tau}(m)\colon m\geq 1\}
. Therefore, by
[
26
, Proposition 8.1.21 and Theorem 8.1.22]
we see that it suffices to prove the
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness of
\mathcal{J}^{\tau}
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
a_{1},\dots,a_{N}\in\mathbb{N}
and
g^{1},\dots,g^{N}\in L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)))
be arbitrary and fixed. Note that the sequence
f^{n}(s)\coloneq\|g^{n}(s)\|_{H}^{2}
belongs to
L^{p/2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{p/2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};L^{q/2}(\mathcal{O})))
.

Let
(r_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
be a Rademacher sequence on a probability space
(\Omega_{r},\mathscr{F}_{r},\mathbb{P}_{r})
. By Proposition
2.6
applied pointwise with respect to
(\omega,m)\in\Omega_{r}\times\mathbb{N}
in (i), and the Kahane-Khintchine inequalities (see
[
26
]
) in (ii) and (iii), we may estimate as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}J(a_{n})g^{n}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\ell^{p}_{\tau,w_{\alpha}}(L^{q}(\mathcal{O})))}^{p}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{r}\sum_{m\geq 1}\tau t_{m+1}^{\alpha}\,\mathbb{E}\Big\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{r_{n}}{\sqrt{a_{n}\tau}}{\bf 1}_{[0\vee(m-a_{n}),m-1]}(j)g^{n}(s){\bf 1}_{[t_{j},t_{j+1})}(s)\,dW(s)\Big\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O})}^{p}

\displaystyle\overset{(\text{i})}{\eqsim}_{p,q}\mathbb{E}_{r}\sum_{m\geq 1}\tau t_{m+1}^{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\Big\|s\mapsto\sum_{j,n}\frac{r_{n}}{\sqrt{a_{n}\tau}}{\bf 1}_{[0\vee(m-a_{n}),m-1]}(j)g^{n}(s){\bf 1}_{[t_{j},t_{j+1})}(s)\Big\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H))}^{p}

\displaystyle\overset{(\text{ii})}{\eqsim}_{p,q}\mathbb{E}\sum_{m\geq 1}\tau t_{m+1}^{\alpha}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big\|s\mapsto\sum_{j,n}\frac{r_{n}}{\sqrt{a_{n}\tau}}{\bf 1}_{[0\vee(m-a_{n}),m-1]}(j)g^{n}(s){\bf 1}_{[t_{j},t_{j+1})}(s)\Big\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H))}^{q}\Big)^{p/q}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sum_{m\geq 1}\tau t_{m+1}^{\alpha}\,\Big(\int_{\mathcal{O}}\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big\|s\mapsto\sum_{i,n}\frac{r_{n}}{\sqrt{a_{n}\tau}}{\bf 1}_{[0\vee(m-a_{n}),m-1]}(j)g^{n}(s){\bf 1}_{[t_{j},t_{j+1})}(s)\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H)}^{q}d\mu\Big)^{p/q}

\displaystyle\overset{(\text{iii})}{\eqsim}_{q}\mathbb{E}\sum_{m\geq 1}\tau t_{m+1}^{\alpha}\,\Big(\int_{\mathcal{O}}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big\|s\mapsto\sum_{j,n}\frac{r_{n}}{\sqrt{a_{n}\tau}}{\bf 1}_{[0\vee(m-a_{n}),m-1]}(j)g^{n}(s){\bf 1}_{[t_{j},t_{j+1})}(s)\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H)}^{2}\Big)^{q/2}d\mu\Big)^{p/q}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sum_{m\geq 1}\tau t_{m+1}^{\alpha}\,\Big(\int_{\mathcal{O}}\Big(\sum_{j,n}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\frac{1}{a_{n}\tau}{\bf 1}_{[0\vee(m-a_{n}),m-1]}(j)\|g^{n}(s)\|_{H}^{2}ds\Big)^{q/2}d\mu\Big)^{p/q}

\displaystyle\eqsim_{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\sum_{m\geq 1}\int_{t_{m}}^{t_{m+1}}\,\Big(\int_{\mathcal{O}}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}T^{*}(a_{n}\tau)f^{n}(t_{m})\Big)^{q/2}d\mu\Big)^{p/q}t^{\alpha}dt

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p/2}\mathbb{E}\sum_{m\geq 1}\int_{t_{m}}^{t_{m+1}}\,\Big(\int_{\mathcal{O}}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}T^{*}((a_{n}+1)\tau)f^{n}(t)\Big)^{q/2}d\mu\Big)^{p/q}t^{\alpha}dt

\displaystyle=2^{p/2}\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}T^{*}((a_{n}+1)\tau)f^{n}\Big\|_{L^{p/2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};L^{q/2}(\mathcal{O}))}^{p/2},

where the operator
T^{*}(\delta)
on
L^{p/2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};L^{q/2}(\mathcal{O}))
is the adjoint of the one defined in Lemma
5.2
below and is given by

T^{*}(\delta)f(t)\coloneq\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{(t-\delta)\vee 0}^{t}f(s)ds.

Moreover, we used the simple estimate
T^{*}(a_{n}\tau)f(t_{m})\leq 2T^{*}((a_{n}+1)\tau)f(t)
for all
t\in[t_{m},t_{m+1})
.

Note that
w_{\alpha}\in A_{p/2}
(see
[
17
, Example 7.1.7]
). By Lemma
5.2
with
r^{\prime}=p/2
and
s^{\prime}=q/2
we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}T^{*}((a_{n}+1)\tau)f^{n}\Big\|_{L^{p/2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};L^{q/2}(\mathcal{O}))}^{p/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}f^{n}\Big\|_{L^{p/2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};L^{q/2}(\mathcal{O}))}^{p/2}

\displaystyle\eqsim\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}g^{n}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H))}^{p},

where the last step follows from reversing the computations involving the Kahane–Khintchine inequalities. Combining the estimates, gives the required
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness.
∎

In the previous proof we used a result of
[
46
, Section 3]
for the Muckenhoupt weighted setting. For details on such weights, the reader is referred to
[
17
, Chapter 7]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

For
\delta>0
let
T(\delta)
be the operator on
L^{r}(\mathbb{R};L^{s}(\mathcal{O}))
given by

T(\delta)f(t)\coloneq\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{t}^{t+\delta}f(\xi)d\xi,

where

and
s\in(1,\infty)
(where
s=\infty
is also allowed if
r=\infty
).
Let
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}=1
,
\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}=1
. Let
v
be an
A_{r^{\prime}}
-weight (with
v=1
if
r=\infty
).
Then there is a constant
C
only depending on
r,s
and
[v]_{A_{r^{\prime}}}
such that for all
N\geq 1
,
f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}\in L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},v;L^{s^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}))
and

,

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}T^{*}(\delta_{n})f_{n}\Big\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},v;L^{s^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}))}\leq C\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}f_{n}\Big\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},v;L^{s^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}))}.

A related class of operators is also

5.3.
Kernels for exponential schemes

In this subsection, we check that
k\in\mathcal{K}_{\tau}
(as defined in Subsection
5.2
) for sequences
k
which will be needed in Section
6
. Although the proofs are completely elementary, they are quite tedious, and we give the detailed arguments for the convenience of the reader.
The aim is to prove
\frac{k}{C}\in\mathcal{K}_{\tau}
, where
C>0
is independent of
\tau
. In all examples, it will be obvious that
k_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
and thus we only need to check that

\sum_{n\geq 1}\sqrt{n\tau}\,|k_{n+1}-k_{n}|\leq C.

This shows that
I^{\tau}(k)\in\mathcal{I}^{\tau}
and
\widetilde{I}^{\tau}(k)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}^{\tau}
,
and both families are
\mathcal{R}
-bounded by Theorem
5.1
and Remark
5.3
.

In the proofs below, we use the following elementary facts:

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\nu\in(0,\pi/2)
. Then,

(5.1)

\displaystyle|1-e^{-z}|

\displaystyle\leq|z|,\quad\text{for all $z\in\mathbb{C}$ with $\real(z)\geq 0$},

(5.2)

\displaystyle|1-e^{-z}|

\displaystyle\leq M_{\nu}(1-e^{-|z|\cos(\arg{z})}),\quad\text{for all $z\in\Sigma_{\nu}$.}

Proof.

For real
z
, (
5.1
) can be proved by comparing derivatives. The complex case follows from the real case since

|1-e^{-z}|\leq|z|\int_{0}^{1}|e^{-tz}|dt=|z|\int_{0}^{1}e^{-t\real(z)}dt=\frac{|z|}{\real(z)}|1-e^{-\real(z)}|\leq|z|
.

At the same time, this gives (
5.2
). Indeed, observe that
\real(z)=|z|\cos(\arg(z))
and that
\frac{|z|}{\real(z)}=\frac{1}{\cos(\arg(z))}
. Thus, the result follows with
M_{\nu}=\frac{1}{\cos(\nu)}
.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\nu\in(0,\pi/2)
and
\sigma\in(0,1/2)
. Let
k_{n},\phi\colon(0,\infty)\times\Sigma_{\nu}\to\mathbb{C}
be given by

\displaystyle k_{n}(\tau,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{1/2}e^{-n\tau\lambda},\ \ n\geq 1,

\displaystyle\phi(\tau,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{e^{-j\tau\lambda}-1}{(\tau\lambda)^{\sigma}}a_{j},

where
|a_{j}|\leq b/j^{1+\sigma}
for all
j\geq 1
, and
b\geq 0
is a constant.
Then there are constants
C_{\nu}
and
C_{\nu,\sigma}
such that for all
\tau>0
and
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\nu}
,

\text{both }\ \frac{k(\tau,\lambda)}{bC_{\nu}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \frac{k(\tau,\lambda)\phi(\tau,\lambda)}{bC_{\nu,\sigma}}\ \ \text{define sequences in $\mathcal{K}_{\tau}$}.

Proof.

Setting
c=\cos\nu
, we see that
|e^{-n\tau\lambda}|\leq e^{-cn\tau|\lambda|}
. Using this and (
5.1
) we find that

\displaystyle|k_{n+1}(\tau,\lambda)-k_{n}(\tau,\lambda)|=|\lambda|^{1/2}|e^{-n\tau\lambda}||e^{-\tau\lambda}-1|\leq|\lambda|^{1/2}e^{-n\tau c|\lambda|}|\tau\lambda|.

It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sqrt{n\tau}\,|k_{n+1}(\tau,\lambda)-k_{n}(\tau,\lambda)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}\tau|\lambda|^{\frac{3}{2}}\sqrt{n\tau}e^{-cn\tau|\lambda|}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}|\lambda|^{\frac{3}{2}}t_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-ct_{n}|\lambda|}dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|\lambda|^{\frac{3}{2}}t^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-ct|\lambda|/2}dt=c^{-3/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-s/2}ds,

which is a finite number only depending on
\nu
.

To prove the bound for
k(\tau,\lambda)\phi(\tau,\lambda)
, it remains to prove that
\phi
is uniformly bounded. To do so, note that by (
5.2
)

(5.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |\phi(\tau,\lambda)|&\leq M_{\nu}b\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{1-e^{-j|\tau\lambda|c}}{|\tau\lambda|^{\sigma}j^{1+\sigma}}\\
&=M_{\nu}b\sum_{j\geq 1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\frac{1-e^{-j|\tau\lambda|c}}{|\tau\lambda|^{\sigma}j^{1+\sigma}\tau}dt\\
&\leq M_{\nu}b\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-e^{-t|\lambda|c}}{|\lambda|^{\sigma}(t/2)^{1+\sigma}}dt=M_{\nu}b2^{1+\sigma}c^{\sigma}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-e^{-t}}{t^{1+\sigma}}dt,\end{aligned}

which is a finite number only depending on
\nu
and
\sigma
.
∎

We will also need the following version.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\nu\in(0,\pi/2)
and
\sigma\in(0,1/2)
. Let
\widetilde{k}_{n}\colon(0,\infty)\times\Sigma_{\nu}\to\mathbb{C}
be given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{k}_{n}(\tau,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq n+1}\frac{\lambda^{1/2}}{(\tau\lambda)^{\sigma}}e^{-(j-n)\tau\lambda}a_{j},\ \ n\geq 1,

where
(a_{j})_{j\geq 1}
is a sequence in
\mathbb{C}
that satisfies
|a_{j}-a_{j+1}|\leq\frac{b}{j^{2+\sigma}}
, where
b\geq 0
is a constant.
Then there is a constant
C_{\nu,\sigma}
such that for all
\tau>0
and
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\nu}
,

\frac{\widetilde{k}(\tau,\lambda)}{bC_{\nu,\sigma}}\ \ \text{defines a sequence in $\mathcal{K}_{\tau}$}.

Proof.

A rewriting gives

\displaystyle|\widetilde{k}_{n}(\tau,\lambda)

\displaystyle-\widetilde{k}_{n+1}(\tau,\lambda)|

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{|\lambda|^{1/2}}{|\tau\lambda|^{\sigma}}\Big|\sum_{j\geq n+1}e^{-(j-n)\tau\lambda}(a_{j}-a_{j+1})\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{|\lambda|^{1/2}}{|\tau\lambda|^{\sigma}}\sum_{j\geq n+1}e^{-(j-n)\tau|\lambda|c}\frac{b}{j^{2+\sigma}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{b|\lambda|^{1/2}}{|\tau\lambda|^{\sigma}}e^{-\tau|\lambda|c}\frac{1}{(n+1)^{2+\sigma}}+b|\lambda|^{1/2-\sigma}\tau\sum_{j\geq n+2}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}e^{-(t_{j}-t_{n})|\lambda|c}\frac{1}{t_{j}^{2+\sigma}}ds

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{b|\lambda|^{1/2}}{|\tau\lambda|^{\sigma}}e^{-\tau|\lambda|c}\frac{1}{(n+1)^{2+\sigma}}+b|\lambda|^{1/2-\sigma}\tau\int_{t_{n+1}}^{\infty}e^{-(s-t_{n})|\lambda|c}\frac{1}{s^{2+\sigma}}ds\eqqcolon A_{n}+B_{n},

where we took out the
j=n+1
term because later it is helpful to start the integral at
t_{n+1}
instead of
t_{n}
. Next, multiplying by
\sqrt{n\tau}
and summing over
n\geq 1
the
A_{n}
-term becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sqrt{n\tau}A_{n}\leq b|\tau\lambda|^{1/2-\sigma}e^{-\tau|\lambda|c}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{(n+1)^{\frac{3}{2}+\sigma}}\leq b\sup_{r>0}r^{1/2-\sigma}e^{-rc}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{(n+1)^{\frac{3}{2}+\sigma}},

which is a number only depending on
\nu
and
\sigma
. The
B_{n}
-term can be estimated as

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sqrt{n\tau}B_{n}

\displaystyle\leq b|\lambda|^{1/2-\sigma}\sum_{n\geq 1}t_{n}^{1/2}\tau\int_{t_{n+1}}^{\infty}e^{-(s-t_{n})|\lambda|c}\frac{1}{s^{2+\sigma}}ds

\displaystyle=b|\lambda|^{1/2-\sigma}\sum_{n\geq 1}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\int_{t_{n+1}}^{\infty}t_{n}^{1/2}e^{-(s-t_{n})|\lambda|c}\frac{1}{s^{2+\sigma}}dsdt

\displaystyle\leq b|\lambda|^{1/2-\sigma}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{t}^{\infty}t^{1/2}e^{-(s-t)|\lambda|c}\frac{1}{s^{2+\sigma}}dsdt

\displaystyle=b|\lambda|^{1/2-\sigma}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-t(u-1)|\lambda|c}\frac{1}{t^{1/2+\sigma}u^{2+\sigma}}dudt

\displaystyle=b|\lambda|^{1/2-\sigma}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-tu|\lambda|c}\frac{1}{t^{1/2+\sigma}(u+1)^{2+\sigma}}dtdu

\displaystyle=bc^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-v}}{v^{1/2+\sigma}}dv\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}}{(u+1)^{2+\sigma}}du,

which is a constant depending only on
\nu
and
\sigma
.
∎

5.4.
Kernels for rational schemes

Next, we check
k\in\mathcal{K}_{\tau}
for sequences associated to rational schemes. The proofs are similar to those of the exponential function, and sometimes we can rely on the latter in proving estimates.

The following assumption will be in place:

Assumption 5.8
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\pi/2]
. Let
r\colon\Sigma_{\theta}\to\mathbb{C}
be a rational function such that
r
is consistent of order
\ell\geq 1
,
A(\theta)
-stable, i.e.
|r(z)|\leq 1
for

z\in\Sigma_{\theta}
, and
r(\infty)=0
.

Recall that this assumption is precisely what is needed for Lemma
2.2
.

Lemma 5.9
.

Suppose that Assumption
5.8
holds and let
\nu\in(0,\theta)
. Let
\sigma\in(0,1/2)
.
Let
k_{n}^{r},\phi^{r}\colon(0,\infty)\times\Sigma_{\nu}\to\mathbb{C}
be defined by

\displaystyle k_{n}^{r}(\tau,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}r(\tau\lambda)^{n},\ \ n\geq 1,

\displaystyle\phi^{r}(\tau,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{r(\tau\lambda)^{j}-1}{(\tau\lambda)^{\sigma}}a_{j},

where
|a_{j}|\leq b/j^{1+\sigma}
for all
j\geq 1
, and
b\geq 0
is a constant.
Then there are constants
C_{\nu,\theta}
and
C_{\nu,\theta,\sigma}
such that for all
\tau>0
and
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\nu}
,

\text{both}\ \ \frac{k_{n}^{r}(\tau,\lambda)}{bC_{\nu,\theta}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \frac{k_{n}^{r}(\tau,\lambda)\phi^{r}(\tau,\lambda)}{bC_{\nu,\theta,\sigma}}\ \ \text{define sequences in $\mathcal{K}_{\tau}$}.

Proof.

Let
z=\tau\lambda
. Then

\sum_{n\geq 1}\sqrt{n\tau}|k_{n+1}^{r}(\tau,\lambda)-k_{n}^{r}(\tau,\lambda)|=|z|^{\frac{1}{2}}|r(z)-1|\sum_{n\geq 1}\sqrt{n}|r(z)|^{n}.

We bound the latter uniformly in
z
. First, consider
z\in\Sigma_{\theta}
with
|z|\geq 1
. Then
by (
2.11
)

\displaystyle|r(z)-1|\,|z|^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\sqrt{n}\,|r(z)|^{n}\leq C_{1}(C_{1}+1)\sum_{n\geq 1}\sqrt{n}\,e^{-c_{0}n},

which is a constant only depending on
\theta
and
\nu
.

Next, consider
z\in\Sigma_{\theta}
with
|z|<1
. Then by (
2.10
) and (
5.1
),
|r(z)|^{n}\leq C_{1}e^{-c_{0}n|z|}
, and

|r(z)-1|\leq|r(z)-e^{-z}|+|e^{-z}-1|\leq(C_{1}+1)|z|.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|r(z)-1|\,|z|^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\sqrt{n}\,|r(z)|^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|r(z)-1|}{|z|}\sum_{n\geq 1}\sqrt{n}|z|^{3/2}e^{-c_{0}n|z|}\leq(C_{1}+1)\sum_{n\geq 1}\sqrt{n}|z|^{3/2}e^{-c_{0}n|z|},

which can be bounded as in the proof of Lemma
5.6
.

To prove the same for
k^{r}(\tau,\lambda)\phi^{r}(\tau,\lambda)
, it is enough to bound
\phi^{r}
uniformly. To do so note that

\displaystyle|\phi^{r}(\tau,\lambda)|\leq b\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{|r(z)^{j}-1|}{|z|^{\sigma}j^{1+\sigma}}.

For
z\in\Sigma_{\nu}
and
|z|\geq 1
using that
|r(z)|\leq 1
, we see that

|\phi^{r}(\tau,\lambda)|\leq 2b\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{1}{j^{1+\sigma}}
.

For
|z|<1
note that by (
2.9
) and (
5.2
)

|r(z)^{j}-1|\leq|r(z)^{j}-e^{-jz}|+|e^{-jz}-1|\leq C_{1}(j|z|^{1}e^{-c_{0}j|z|}+1-e^{-cj|z|}),

where
c=\cos(\nu)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle|\phi^{r}(\tau,\lambda)|\leq bC_{1}\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{|z|^{1-\sigma}e^{-c_{0}j|z|}}{j^{\sigma}}+bC_{1}\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{1-e^{-cj|z|}}{|z|^{\sigma}j^{1+\sigma}}.

The first term on the right-hand side satisfies

\displaystyle b\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{|z|^{1-\sigma}e^{-c_{0}j|z|}}{j^{\sigma}}=b\sum_{j\geq 1}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\frac{|\lambda|e^{-c_{0}t_{j}|\lambda|}}{|\lambda|^{\sigma}t_{j}^{\sigma}}dt\leq b\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|\lambda|e^{-c_{0}t|\lambda|}}{|\lambda|^{\sigma}t^{\sigma}}dt=bc^{\sigma-1}_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t^{\sigma}}dt.

The second term in the estimate for
\phi^{r}
is uniformly bounded by (
5.3
).
∎

Finally, we will need the following variant of Lemma
5.7
as well.

Lemma 5.10
.

Suppose that Assumption
5.8
holds and let
\nu\in(0,\theta)
. Let
\sigma\in(0,1/2)
. Let
\widetilde{k}_{n}^{r}\colon(0,\infty)\times\Sigma_{\nu}\to\mathbb{C}
be given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{k}_{n}^{r}(\tau,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq n+1}\frac{\lambda^{1/2}}{(\tau\lambda)^{\sigma}}r(\tau\lambda)^{j-n}a_{j},\ \ n\geq 1,

where
(a_{j})_{j\geq 1}
in
\mathbb{C}
satisfies
|a_{j}-a_{j+1}|\leq\frac{b}{j^{2+\sigma}}
, where
b\geq 0
is a constant.
Then there is a constant
C_{\nu,\theta,\sigma}
such that for all
\tau>0
and
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\nu}
,

\frac{\widetilde{k}^{r}(\tau,\lambda)}{bC_{\nu,\theta,\sigma}}\ \ \text{defines a sequence in $\mathcal{K}_{\tau}$}.

Proof.

As in the proof of Lemma
5.7
, using
z=\tau\lambda
we can write

\displaystyle|\widetilde{k}_{n}^{r}(\tau,\lambda)-\widetilde{k}_{n+1}^{r}(\tau,\lambda)|

\displaystyle=\frac{|\lambda|^{1/2}}{|z|^{\sigma}}\Big|\sum_{j\geq n+1}r(z)^{j-n}(a_{j}-a_{j+1})\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{b|\lambda|^{1/2}}{|z|^{\sigma}}\sum_{j\geq n+1}\frac{|r(z)|^{j-n}}{{j^{2+\sigma}}}.

\displaystyle=\frac{b|\lambda|^{1/2}}{|z|^{\sigma}}|r(z)|\frac{1}{(n+1)^{2+\sigma}}+\frac{b|\lambda|^{1/2}}{|z|^{\sigma}}\sum_{j\geq n+2}\frac{|r(z)|^{j-n}}{{j^{2+\sigma}}}\eqqcolon A_{n}+B_{n}.

The
A_{n}
term multiplied by
\sqrt{n\tau}
and summed over all
n\geq 1
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sqrt{n\tau}A_{n}=b\sum_{n\geq 1}\sqrt{n\tau}\frac{|\lambda|^{1/2}}{|z|^{\sigma}}\frac{|r(z)|}{{(n+1)^{2+\sigma}}}\leq b|z|^{1/2-\sigma}|r(z)|\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{{(n+1)^{\frac{3}{2}+\sigma}}}.

For
|z|\leq 1
, we have
|z|^{1/2-\sigma}|r(z)|\leq 1
. For
|z|>1
, by (
2.11
),

|z|^{1/2-\sigma}|r(z)|\leq C_{1}
.

It remains to bound
\sum_{n\geq 1}\sqrt{n\tau}B_{n}
. Note that for
|z|\geq 1
again by (
2.11
)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sqrt{n\tau}B_{n}

\displaystyle\leq bC_{1}\sum_{n\geq 1}\sqrt{n}\sum_{j\geq n+2}\frac{e^{-c_{0}(j-n)}}{{j^{2+\sigma}}},

and the latter is finite as can be seen from the proof of Lemma
5.7
by taking
\lambda=c_{0}/c
.

For
|z|<1
by (
2.10
)

|r(z)|^{j-n}\leq bC_{1}e^{-c_{0}(j-n)|z|}
and therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sqrt{n\tau}B_{n}

\displaystyle\leq bC_{1}\sum_{n\geq 1}\sqrt{n}|z|^{1/2-\sigma}\sum_{j\geq n+2}\frac{e^{-c_{0}(j-n)|z|}}{{j^{2+\sigma}}},

which can be bounded in the same way as in Lemma
5.7
.
∎

6.
Discrete maximal estimates

In this section, we will consider a maximal estimate with parabolic regularization, which includes Theorem
1.4
as a special case. In this maximal estimate one needs to bound
\mathbb{E}\sup_{n\geq 1}\|Y_{n}\|^{p}_{Z}
(for a suitable norm
\|\cdot\|_{Z}
). Since
Y
does not have a martingale structure, one often cannot apply stochastic calculus techniques, and therefore it is unclear how to deal with the supremum over
n
inside an expectation. Especially, if the norm
\|\cdot\|_{Z}
is chosen to express optimal parabolic regularization, this leads to serious issues. In the deterministic setting there is no expectation, so that this difficulty is less prominent. Maximal estimates in the latter case are well-known and the reader is referred to Remark
6.2
for further details.

6.1.
Maximal estimate with parabolic regularization

The following result is the main result of this section and, in particular, includes Theorem
1.4
.

Recall from (
3.1
) that
Y
is given by the recursive formula
Y_{0}=0
and

Y_{n+1}\coloneq R_{\tau}Y_{n}+R_{\tau}\Delta_{n}I_{g},\quad n\geq 0,

where
\Delta_{n}I_{g}
is as defined below (
3.1
) and
R_{\tau}
is as in Assumption
1.1
. Moreover, by (
3.2
),

Y_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}R_{\tau}^{n-j}\Delta_{j}I_{g},\ \ n\geq 1.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
q\in[2,\infty)
and suppose that
X_{0}
is isomorphic to a closed subspace of
L^{q}(\mathcal{O})
with
(\mathcal{O},\Sigma,\mu)
a
\sigma
-finite measure space. Suppose that Assumption
1.1
holds, that
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus on
X_{0}
of angle
<\pi/2
, and that
0\in\rho(A)
.
Then for any
p\in(2,\infty)
and
\alpha\in[0,\frac{p}{2}-1)
, there is a constant
C
such that for every
g\in L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};\gamma(H,X_{1/2})))
and every stepsize
\tau>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{n\geq 1}\|Y_{n}\|_{X_{1-\frac{1+\alpha}{p},p}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C^{p}\mathbb{E}\|g\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{n\geq 1}(\tau n)^{\alpha}\|Y_{n}\|_{X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C^{p}\mathbb{E}\|g\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))},

where
Y
is given by (
3.1
).

Since
X_{0}
is assumed to be isomorphic to a closed subspace of
L^{q}
, it follows that any of the complex interpolation spaces
X_{\beta}
for
\beta\in[0,1]
, are isomorphic to a closed subspace of
L^{q}
. Indeed, since we assume that
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus it follows that
X_{\beta}=D(A^{\beta})
isomorphically (see Lemma
2.1
). Since
D(A^{\beta})
is isomorphic to
X_{0}
(use
(1+A)^{\beta}
as an isomorphism), the result follows.

6.2.
Proof of Theorem
6.1

6.2.1.
Reduction to a continuous-time setting

It is clear that by density, it suffices to consider
g\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\gamma(H,X_{1})))
with support on a bounded subinterval of
[0,\infty)
. Let
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega;X_{1})
be the linear interpolation of the discrete solution, i.e.

(6.1)

\displaystyle u(n\tau+s\tau)=(1-s)Y_{n}+sY_{n+1},\ \ \ s\in[0,1],n\geq 0.

From Corollary
3.9
one sees that
AY\in L^{p}(\Omega;\ell^{p}_{\tau,w_{\alpha}}(X_{0}))
. Moreover, since
A
is invertible we have
Y\in L^{p}(\Omega;\ell^{p}_{\tau,w_{\alpha}}(X_{1}))
and
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};X_{1}))
with

(6.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};X_{1})}^{p}

\displaystyle\eqsim\mathbb{E}\sum_{n\geq 0}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|Au(t)\|^{p}_{X_{0}}t^{\alpha}dt

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sum_{n\geq 0}\int_{0}^{1}\|Au(n\tau+s\tau)\|^{p}_{X_{0}}\tau(n\tau+s\tau)^{\alpha}ds

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\sum_{n\geq 0}\int_{0}^{1}[(1-s)\|AY_{n}\|^{p}_{X_{0}}+s\|AY_{n+1}\|_{X_{0}}^{p}]\tau(n\tau+s\tau)^{\alpha}ds

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau t_{n+1}^{\alpha}\|AY_{n}\|^{p}_{X_{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbb{E}\|g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))}^{p},

where we also use the convexity of
|\cdot|^{p}
.

Since
u
is piecewise linear, one can even check that
u^{\prime}
exists in the weak sense and
u^{\prime}\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{1}))
and also
u^{\prime}\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};X_{1}))
due to the extra regularity assumed on
g
. In particular,
u\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{1})
.

The proof of Theorem
6.1
would be complete if we can prove the bound

(6.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\geq 0}\|u(t)\|_{X_{1-\frac{1+\alpha}{p},p}}^{p}+\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\geq 0}t^{\alpha}\|u(t)\|_{X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p}}^{p}\leq C^{p}\mathbb{E}\|g\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))}.

Indeed, this is immediate from
u(t_{n})=Y_{n}
. Now a crucial trick to avoid estimating the expectation of a supremum of a stochastic process is to use the following trace embedding of
[
1
, Theorem 1.2]
(see also
[
43
, Theorem 1.1]
): for
\sigma\in(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{2})

\displaystyle L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};X_{1})\cap H^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};X_{1-\sigma})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow C_{b}([0,\infty);X_{1-\frac{1+\alpha}{p},p}),

\displaystyle L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};X_{1})\cap H^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};X_{1-\sigma})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow C_{b}((0,\infty),w_{\alpha};X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p}),

where
C_{b}((0,\infty),w_{\alpha};E)
are the continuous functions
f\colon(0,\infty)\to E
for which
\|f\|_{C_{b}((0,\infty),w_{\alpha};E)}\coloneq\sup_{t>0}w_{\alpha}(t)\|f(t)\|_{E}<\infty
.

Indeed, (
6.3
) follows as soon as we have proved the estimate

(6.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|u\|_{H^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{1-\sigma})}^{p}\leq C^{p}_{\sigma}\mathbb{E}\|g\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))},\ \ \sigma\in[0,1/2).

The case
\sigma=0
follows from (
6.2
). From now on let
\sigma\in(0,1/2)
be arbitrary. To prove (
6.4
), due to the fact that
0\in\rho(A)
it is enough to show that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|A^{1-\sigma}u\|_{H^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};X_{0})}^{p}\leq C^{p}_{\sigma}\mathbb{E}\|g\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))},\ \ \ \sigma\in[0,1/2).

It is well-known that
-\partial_{t}
on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};X_{0})
with domain
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};X_{0})
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle
\pi/2
and
D(\partial_{t}^{\sigma})=H^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha},X_{0})
(see
[
39
, Theorem 6.8]
). In particular, it follows that

\|A^{1-\sigma}u\|_{H^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};X_{0})}\eqsim\|A^{1-\sigma}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};X_{0})}+\|(-\partial_{t})^{\sigma}A^{1-\sigma}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};X_{0})}.

Since we have already estimated the
L^{p}
-norm of
\|A^{1-\sigma}u\|_{X_{0}}\lesssim\|Au\|_{X_{0}}
, it remains to prove

(6.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|(-\partial_{t})^{\sigma}A^{1-\sigma}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};X_{0})}^{p}\lesssim\mathbb{E}\|g\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))}.

Note that
\partial_{t}
generates the left translation semigroup on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{0})
. Since
A^{1-\sigma}u
is in
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};X_{0})
, it follows from the Balakrishnan formula for the fractional power (see
[
42
, Theorem 3.2.2]
) that

(6.6)

\displaystyle(-\partial_{t})^{\sigma}A^{1-\sigma}u(t)=C_{\sigma}\int_{0}^{\infty}A^{1-\sigma}\frac{u(t)-u(t+h)}{h^{1+\sigma}}\,dh,

with
C_{\sigma}\neq 0
.

6.2.2.
Estimating the fractional derivative

By (
6.6
) we can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\|A^{1-\sigma}(-\partial)^{\sigma}u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};X_{0})}\eqsim\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big\|\int_{0}^{\infty}A^{1-\sigma}\frac{u(t)-u(t+h)}{h^{1+\sigma}}\,dh\Big\|^{p}_{X_{0}}w_{\alpha}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sum_{n\geq 0}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\Big\|\sum_{j\geq 0}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}A^{1-\sigma}\frac{u(t)-u(t+h)}{h^{1+\sigma}}\,dh\Big\|^{p}_{X_{0}}t^{\alpha}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau\int_{0}^{1}\Big\|\sum_{j\geq 0}\tau\int_{0}^{1}A^{1-\sigma}\frac{u(n\tau+s\tau)-u((n+j)\tau+(s+r)\tau)}{(j\tau+r\tau)^{1+\sigma}}\,dr\Big\|^{p}_{X_{0}}t_{n+1}^{\alpha}\,ds.

For
s+r\in[0,1)
we can write

\displaystyle u(n\tau+

\displaystyle s\tau)-u((n+j)\tau+(s+r)\tau)

\displaystyle=(1-s)Y_{n}+sY_{n+1}-[(1-(s+r))Y_{n+j}+(s+r)Y_{n+j+1}]

\displaystyle=(1-s-r)(Y_{n}-Y_{n+j})+(s+r)(Y_{n+1}-Y_{n+j+1})+r(Y_{n}-Y_{n+1}),

For
s+r\in[1,2)
we can write

\displaystyle u(

\displaystyle n\tau+s\tau)-u((n+j)\tau+(s+r)\tau)

\displaystyle=(1-s)Y_{n}+sY_{n+1}-[(1-(s+r-1))Y_{n+j+1}+(s+r-1)Y_{n+j+2}]

\displaystyle=(2-s-r)(Y_{n}-Y_{n+j+1})+(s+r-1)(Y_{n+1}-Y_{n+j+2})+(1-r)(Y_{n+1}-Y_{n}).

Therefore, from the triangle inequality, it follows that it suffices to estimate each of the following:

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{j\geq 1}\int_{0}^{1-s}\tau A^{1-\sigma}(1-s-r)\frac{Y_{n}-Y_{n+j}}{(j\tau+r\tau)^{1+\sigma}}\,dr\Big\|^{p}_{X_{0}}t_{n+1}^{\alpha}\,ds

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{j\geq 1}\int_{0}^{1-s}\tau A^{1-\sigma}(s+r)\frac{Y_{n+1}-Y_{n+j+1}}{(j\tau+r\tau)^{1+\sigma}}\,dr\Big\|^{p}_{X_{0}}t_{n+1}^{\alpha}\,ds

\displaystyle T_{3}

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{j\geq 0}\int_{0}^{1-s}\tau A^{1-\sigma}r\frac{Y_{n}-Y_{n+1}}{(j\tau+r\tau)^{1+\sigma}}\,dr\Big\|^{p}_{X_{0}}t_{n+1}^{\alpha}ds

\displaystyle T_{4}

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{j\geq 1}\int_{1-s}^{1}\tau A^{1-\sigma}(2-s-r)\frac{Y_{n}-Y_{n+j+1}}{(j\tau+r\tau)^{1+\sigma}}\,dr\Big\|^{p}_{X_{0}}t_{n+1}^{\alpha}\,ds

\displaystyle T_{5}

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{j\geq 0}\int_{1-s}^{1}\tau A^{1-\sigma}(s+r-1)\frac{Y_{n+1}-Y_{n+j+2}}{(j\tau+r\tau)^{1+\sigma}}\,dr\Big\|^{p}_{X_{0}}t_{n+1}^{\alpha}\,ds

\displaystyle T_{6}

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{j\geq 1}\int_{1-s}^{1}\tau A^{1-\sigma}(1-r)\frac{Y_{n+1}-Y_{n}}{(j\tau+r\tau)^{1+\sigma}}\,dr\Big\|^{p}_{X_{0}}t_{n+1}^{\alpha}\,ds

\displaystyle T_{7}

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\Big\|\int_{1-s}^{1}\tau A^{1-\sigma}(1-s)\frac{Y_{n+1}-Y_{n}}{(r\tau)^{1+\sigma}}\,dr\Big\|^{p}_{X_{0}}t_{n+1}^{\alpha}\,ds.

The terms
T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}
come from the case
s+r\in[0,1)
, and the other terms
from
s+r\in[1,2)
. Note that the term
T_{7}
is just the
j=0
term of
T_{4}
and
T_{6}
combined to avoid the creation of a singularity.
The proofs of the estimates for
T_{1},T_{2},T_{4},T_{5}
are very similar. To give the bounds, we only present the details for
T_{1}
. It is enough to prove that for all
s\in(0,1)
:

(6.7)

\displaystyle T_{1,s}

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau^{-\sigma}\psi(j,s)A^{1-\sigma}(Y_{n+j}-Y_{n})\Big\|^{p}_{X_{0}}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))},

where
C
is independent of
s
and

\displaystyle\psi(j,s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1-s}\frac{(1-s-r)}{(j+r)^{1+\sigma}}\,dr.

We need two properties of
\psi
, which are both straightforward to check:

(6.8)

\displaystyle\sup_{j\geq 1,s\in(0,1)}j^{1+\sigma}|\psi(j,s)|<\infty\ \ \text{and}\ \ \sup_{j\geq 1,s\in(0,1)}j^{2+\sigma}\Big|\psi(j+1,s)-\psi(j,s)\Big|<\infty.

The details of (
6.7
) will be given in Subsection
6.2.4
. The proofs are based on the
H^{\infty}
-calculus of
A
and the
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness results provided in Section
5
.

6.2.3.
Estimating terms
T_{3},T_{6}
and
T_{7}

To estimate
T_{7}
note that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{1-s}^{1}\tau A^{1-\sigma}(1-s)\frac{Y_{n+1}-Y_{n}}{(r\tau)^{1+\sigma}}\,dr\Big\|^{p}_{X_{0}}t_{n+1}^{\alpha}\,ds\lesssim\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau\mathbb{E}\Big\|\tau^{-\sigma}A^{1-\sigma}(Y_{n+1}-Y_{n})\,\Big\|^{p}_{X_{0}}t_{n+1}^{\alpha}.

Before we continue, we first note that for
T_{3}
and
T_{6}
one has

\displaystyle C_{3}

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{j\geq 0}\int_{0}^{1-s}\frac{r}{(j+r)^{1+\sigma}}\,dr\leq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{r^{\sigma}}\,dr+\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{1}{j^{1+\sigma}}\,dr<\infty,

\displaystyle C_{6}

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{j\geq 1}\int_{1-s}^{1}\frac{1-r}{(j+r)^{1+\sigma}}\,dr\leq\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{1}{j^{1+\sigma}}\,dr<\infty

and therefore,

(6.9)

\displaystyle T_{3}\leq C_{3}^{p}\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau\mathbb{E}\Big\|\tau^{-\sigma}A^{1-\sigma}(Y_{n+1}-Y_{n})\Big\|^{p}_{X_{0}}t_{n+1}^{\alpha}.

The same estimate holds for
T_{6}
. Therefore, in order to bound
T_{3},T_{6},T_{7}
, it suffices to bound the right-hand side of (
6.9
). From the formula for
Y
given in (
3.1
), we see that

Y_{n+1}-Y_{n}=R_{\tau}\Delta_{n}I_{g}+\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}(R_{\tau}-I)R_{\tau}^{n-m}\Delta_{m}I_{g}.

Thus it suffices to bound each of the terms

\displaystyle T_{8}

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau\mathbb{E}\Big\|\tau^{-\sigma}A^{1-\sigma}R_{\tau}\Delta_{n}I_{g}\Big\|^{p}_{X_{0}}t_{n+1}^{\alpha},

\displaystyle T_{9}

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{n\geq 1}\tau\mathbb{E}\Big\|\tau^{-\sigma}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}A^{1-\sigma}(R_{\tau}-I)(R_{\tau}^{n-m})\Delta_{m}I_{g}\Big\|^{p}_{X_{0}}t_{n+1}^{\alpha}.

By Proposition
2.5
and (
2.13
) we can estimate

\displaystyle T_{8}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,X_{0}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau\mathbb{E}\Big\|\tau^{-\sigma}A^{1-\sigma}R_{\tau}g\Big\|^{p}_{L^{2}(t_{n},t_{n+1};\gamma(H,X_{0}))}t_{n+1}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau\mathbb{E}\tau^{-p/2}\|A^{1/2}g\|^{p}_{L^{2}((t_{n},t_{n+1});\gamma(H,X_{0}))}t_{n+1}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{E}\|A^{1/2}g\|^{p}_{L^{p}((t_{n},t_{n+1}),w_{\alpha};\gamma(H,X_{0}))}\lesssim\mathbb{E}\|g\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))},

where in the penultimate step we used Hölder’s inequality and in the last step we used Lemma
2.1
.
By Proposition
2.5
and Lemma
2.3
(writing
R_{\tau}-I=(R_{\tau}-e^{-\tau A})+(e^{-\tau A}-I)
and using (
2.5
)) we get that

\displaystyle T_{9}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\tau\mathbb{E}\Big\|\tau^{-\sigma}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}A^{-(\sigma+\frac{1}{2})}(R_{\tau}-I)A^{1+\frac{1}{2}}R_{\tau}^{n-m}\Delta_{m}I_{g}\Big\|^{p}_{X_{0}}t_{n+1}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,X}\sum_{n\geq 1}\tau\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\Big\|\tau^{-\sigma}A^{-(\sigma+\frac{1}{2})}(R_{\tau}-I)AR_{\tau}^{n-m}A^{\frac{1}{2}}g\Big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(t_{m},t_{m+1};\gamma(H,X_{0}))}\Big)^{p/2}t_{n+1}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}\tau\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\|\tau^{-\sigma}A^{-(\sigma+\frac{1}{2})}(R_{\tau}-I)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0})}\|AR_{\tau}^{n-m}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0})}\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(t_{m},t_{m+1};\gamma(H,X_{0}))}\Big)^{p/2}t_{n+1}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\tau\frac{1}{(\tau(n-m))^{2}}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(t_{m},t_{m+1};\gamma(H,X_{1/2})}\Big)^{p/2}t_{n+1}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(i)}}}{{\lesssim}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{(n-m)^{2}}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{p}(t_{m},t_{m+1},w_{\alpha};\gamma(H,X_{1/2})}t_{m+1}^{-2\alpha/p}\Big)^{p/2}t_{n+1}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sum_{n\geq 0}\Big(\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}G_{m}^{2}K(n,m)\Big)^{p/2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(ii)}}}{{\lesssim}}\mathbb{E}\sum_{m\geq 0}G_{m}^{p}=\mathbb{E}\|g\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))},

where in (i) we used Hölder’s inequality and in (ii) we used Schur’s lemma (see
[
18
, Appendix A.2]
) in
\ell^{p/2}
for the non-negative kernel
K(n,m)\coloneq\frac{1}{(n-m)^{2}}\big(\frac{n+1}{m+1}\big)^{2\alpha/p}{\bf 1}_{0\leq m<n}
with sequences
u_{n}=v_{n}=(n+1)^{-\frac{1}{(\frac{p}{2})(\frac{p}{2})^{\prime}}}
, noting that
0\leq\alpha<\frac{p}{2}-1
, and where we set
G_{m}\coloneq\|g\|_{L^{p}(t_{m},t_{m+1},w_{\alpha};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))}
.

6.2.4.
Estimating the main term
T_{1,s}

In this section, we prove the estimate (
6.7
). For this, we use the operator-valued
H^{\infty}
-calculus. This method was first used to establish stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity in continuous-time in
[
48
]
.

We write
R_{\tau}=r(\tau A)
where
r(z)
is either the exponential function or a rational function as in Assumption
1.1
. Fix
\nu\in(\omega(A),\theta)
. In order to rewrite
T_{1,s}
, defined in (
6.7
), note that

\displaystyle Y_{n+j}-Y_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=n}^{n+j-1}r(\tau A)^{n+j-m}\Delta_{m}I_{g}+\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}(R_{\tau}^{j}-I)r(\tau A)^{n-m}\Delta_{m}I_{g}

and thus moving part of the power of
A
to the process
g
, and using a functional calculus representation (see
[
26
,
27
]
), we can write

\displaystyle A^{1-\sigma}(Y_{n+j}-Y_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=n}^{n+j-1}A^{1/2-\sigma}R_{\tau}^{n+j-m}\Delta_{m}I_{A^{1/2}}g+\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}A^{1/2-\sigma}(R_{\tau}^{j}-I)R_{\tau}^{n-m}\Delta_{m}I_{A^{1/2}g}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\nu}}\Big[\sum_{m=n}^{n+j-1}f_{j,m,n}^{(1)}(\lambda)R(\lambda,A)\Delta_{m}I_{A^{1/2}g}+\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}f_{j,m,n}^{(2)}(\lambda)R(\lambda,A)\Delta_{m}I_{A^{1/2}g}\Big]d\lambda,

where
f_{j,m,n}^{(1)},f_{j,m,n}^{(2)}\colon\Sigma_{\nu}\to\mathbb{C}
are given by

\displaystyle f_{j,m,n}^{(1)}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\coloneq\lambda^{1/2-\sigma}r(\tau\lambda)^{n+j-m},

\displaystyle f_{j,m,n}^{(2)}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\coloneq\lambda^{1/2-\sigma}(r(\tau\lambda)^{j}-I)r(\tau\lambda)^{n-m}.

Therefore, to estimate
T_{1,s}
it suffices to bound
T_{1,s}^{(1)}
and
T_{1,s}^{(2)}
, which are given by

\displaystyle T_{1,s}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau\Big\|\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\nu}}\Big[\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau^{-\sigma}\psi(j,s)\sum_{m=n}^{n+j-1}f_{j,m,n}^{(1)}(\lambda)R(\lambda,A)\Delta_{m}I_{A^{1/2}g}\Big]d\lambda\Big\|_{X_{0}}^{p},

\displaystyle T_{1,s}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sum_{n\geq 0}\tau\Big\|\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\nu}}\Big[\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau^{-\sigma}\psi(j,s)\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}f_{j,m,n}^{(2)}(\lambda)R(\lambda,A)\Delta_{m}I_{A^{1/2}g}\Big]d\lambda\Big\|_{X_{0}}^{p}.

To rewrite
T_{1,s}^{(1)}
in the form of a discrete convolution operator, note that for
h\in L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\gamma(H,X_{0})))
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau^{-\sigma}\psi(j,s)\sum_{m=n}^{n+j-1}f_{j,m,n}^{(1)}(\lambda)\Delta_{m}I_{h}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geq n}\sum_{j\geq m-n+1}\psi(j,s)\tau^{-\sigma}\lambda^{1/2-\sigma}r(\tau\lambda)^{n+j-m}\Delta_{m}I_{h}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geq n}k_{m-n}^{(1)}(\lambda)\Delta_{m}I_{h}=\widetilde{I}^{\tau}(k^{(1)}(\lambda))h,

where

k_{m}^{(1)}(\lambda)=\sum_{j\geq m+1}\psi(j,s)\tau^{-\sigma}\lambda^{1/2-\sigma}r(\tau\lambda)^{j-m},

and
\widetilde{I}^{\tau}(k(\lambda))
is as in Remark
5.3
. We did not make the
\tau
-dependence explicit in
k
, but of course it also depends on
\tau
. It follows that
T_{1,s}^{(1)}
can be written as

\displaystyle T_{1,s}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\nu}}\widetilde{I}^{\tau}(k^{(1)}(\lambda))R(\lambda,A)A^{1/2}gd\lambda\Big\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega;\ell^{p}_{\tau,w_{\alpha}}(X_{0}))}.

Let us introduce the short-hand notation

\mathcal{L}^{\tau}\coloneq\mathcal{L}(L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};\gamma(H,X_{0}))),L^{p}(\Omega;\ell^{p}_{\tau,w_{\alpha}}(X_{0}))).

One can check that the operator-valued function
\lambda\mapsto\widetilde{I}^{\tau}(k^{(1)}(\lambda))
is in
H^{1}(\Sigma_{\theta};\mathcal{L}^{\tau})
, and commutes with the resolvent of
A
(seen as an operator on
L^{p}(\Omega;\ell^{p}_{\tau,w_{\alpha}}(X_{0}))
). We claim that the above operator-valued function as a family in
\mathcal{L}^{\tau}
has
\mathcal{R}
-bounded range (with uniform estimates in
s
and
\tau
). As soon as we have checked this, it follows from the boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus of
A
and
[
27
, Theorem 16.3.4]
that

\displaystyle T_{1,s}^{(1)}\leq C\|A^{1/2}g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\gamma(H,X_{0})))}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\alpha};\gamma(H,X_{1/2})))},

where
C
does not depend on
s
and
\tau
, and where in the last step we used Lemma
2.1
.

To prove the claim, note that by Remark
5.3
it suffices to show that there is a constant
C
independent of
\lambda
and
s
such that
k^{(1)}(\lambda)/C
is in
\mathcal{K}_{\tau}
. This follows from Lemmas
5.7
,
5.10
and (
6.8
).

A similar argument can be used to estimate
T_{1,s}^{(2)}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau^{-\sigma}\psi(j,s)\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}f_{j,m,n}^{(2)}(\lambda)\Delta_{m}I_{h}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau^{-\sigma}\psi(j,s)f_{j,m,n}^{(2)}(\lambda)\Delta_{m}I_{h}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}k_{n-m}^{(2)}(\lambda)\Delta_{m}I_{h}=I^{\tau}(k^{(2)}(\lambda))h,

where

k_{m}^{(2)}(\lambda)=\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau^{-\sigma}\psi(j,s)\lambda^{1/2-\sigma}(r(\tau\lambda)^{j}-I)r(\tau\lambda)^{m},

and
I_{\tau}(k(\lambda))
is as in Section
5.2
.
It follows that
T_{1,s}^{(2)}
can be written as

\displaystyle T_{1,s}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\nu}}I^{\tau}(k^{(2)}(\lambda))R(\lambda,A)A^{1/2}gd\lambda\Big\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega;\ell^{p}_{\tau,w_{\alpha}}(X_{0}))}.

Now the proof can be completed as we did for
T_{1,s}^{(1)}
, where this time the
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness of the range of
\lambda\mapsto I^{\tau}(k^{(2)}(\lambda))
follows from Theorem
5.1
, Lemmas
5.6
,
5.9
and (
6.8
).

Remark 6.2
.

In the deterministic setting, maximal estimates in trace spaces are well-known, and can be found in
[
9
, Theorem 2.3.2]
and
[
32
, Lemma 14]
. The deterministic setting is much simpler because one can formulate an equivalent description of the trace norm in a discrete setting. Moreover, expectations do not play any role here.

Our argument for proving maximal estimates can also be used to give an alternative proof for the deterministic analogue. Indeed, typically, discrete maximal regularity in the deterministic setting involves the quantity

\displaystyle\|D_{\tau}Y\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau}(X_{0})}+\|Y\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau}(X_{1})},

where
(D_{\tau}Y)_{n}=\frac{Y_{n+1}-Y_{n}}{\tau}
. Extending
Y
to a continuous-time function
u
as in (
6.1
) immediately gives
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{0})\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{1})
, and thus the trace regularity
u\in C_{b}([0,\infty);X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p})
follows from the classical Lions–Peetre trace method for real interpolation (see
[
27
, Appendix L]
).

6.3.
Proof of the maximal estimate of Proposition
1.5

We will actually prove the following more general result, which reduces to Proposition
1.5
if
s=2
.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
X_{0}
be a Hilbert space. Suppose that Assumption
1.1
holds with
\theta=\pi/2
. Suppose that
0\in\rho(A)
or that
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus.
Then for every
s\in(0,\infty)
there is a constant
C
such that for every
g\in L^{s}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\gamma(H,X_{1/2})))
and every stepsize
\tau>0
,

\mathbb{E}\sup_{n\geq 1}\|Y_{n}\|_{X_{1/2}}^{s}\leq C^{s}\mathbb{E}\|g\|^{s}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))},

where
Y=(Y_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is given by (
1.1
).

Proof.

Note that
X_{1/2}=(X_{0},X_{1})_{1/2,2}
and
\gamma(H,X_{1/2})=\mathcal{L}_{2}(H,X_{1/2})
with equivalent norms (see
[
25
, Corollary C.4.2]
and
[
26
, Proposition 9.1.9]
). If
0\in\rho(A)
, then
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus on
(X_{0},X_{1})_{1/2,2}
by Dore’s theorem (see
[
27
, Corollary 16.3.23]
). If
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus on
X_{0}
, then this holds even without the condition
0\in\rho(A)
by interpolation.
From
[
35
, Theorem 11.13]
it follows that there is a Hilbert space norm
\|\cdot\|_{Z}
which is equivalent to
X_{1/2}
under which
(e^{-tA})_{t\geq 0}
is a contraction semigroup. Let
K_{1},K_{2}>0
be such that
K_{1}\|x\|_{Z}\leq\|x\|_{X_{1/2}}\leq K_{2}\|x\|_{Z}
. Note that since
|r(z)|\leq 1
on
\Sigma_{\pi/2}
, there exists
\sigma>\pi/2
small enough such that
r\in H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\sigma})
. Hence, from
[
26
, Theorem 10.2.24]
it follows that
R_{\tau}
is contractive on
Z
.

Next, we extend the discrete dilation argument in
[
33
, Proposition 5.1]
to our setting. By the Sz.-Nagy dilation theorem
[
52
, Theorem I.4.2]
we can find a Hilbert space
\widetilde{Z}
, a contractive injection
Q\colon Z\to\widetilde{Z}
, a contractive projection
P\colon\widetilde{Z}\to Z
, and a unitary operator
\widetilde{R}_{\tau}
on
\widetilde{Z}
such that

R_{\tau}^{j}=P\widetilde{R}_{\tau}^{j}Q,\ \ \ j\geq 0.

It follows that

\displaystyle\|Y_{n}\|_{X_{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq K_{2}\|Y_{n}\|_{Z}=K_{2}\Big\|\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}R_{\tau}^{n-j}\,\Delta_{j}I_{g}\Big\|_{Z}\leq K_{2}\Big\|\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\widetilde{R}_{\tau}^{-j}Q\Delta_{j}I_{g}\Big\|_{\widetilde{Z}},

where we used that
\widetilde{R}_{\tau}
is unitary.
Therefore, by Proposition
2.5
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{n\geq 1}\|Y_{n}\|_{X_{1/2}}^{s}

\displaystyle\leq K_{2}^{s}\mathbb{E}\sup_{n\geq 1}\Big\|\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\widetilde{R}_{\tau}^{-j}Q\Delta_{j}I_{g}\Big\|_{\widetilde{Z}}^{s}

\displaystyle\leq K_{2}^{s}C_{s,X_{0}}^{s}\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{j\geq 0}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\|\widetilde{R}_{\tau}^{-j}Qg(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(H,\widetilde{Z})}^{2}dt\Big)^{s/2}

\displaystyle\leq K_{2}^{s}C_{s,X_{0}}^{s}\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{j\geq 0}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\|g(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(H,Z)}^{2}dt\Big)^{s/2}

\displaystyle\leq K_{1}^{-s}K_{2}^{s}C_{s,X_{0}}^{s}\mathbb{E}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathcal{L}_{2}(H,X_{1/2}))}^{s}.

∎

Remark 6.4
.

In the above, it is enough to find an equivalent norm in which
R_{\tau}
is a contraction.

(1)

In case
s=2
and
R_{\tau}
is a contraction on a Hilbert space, then using Doob’s maximal inequality for second moments and the above method, one can check that

\mathbb{E}\sup_{n\geq 1}\|Y_{n}\|_{X_{1/2}}^{2}\leq 4\mathbb{E}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\gamma(H,X_{1/2}))}.

(2)

In case
R_{\tau}
is a contraction on a
2
-smooth Banach space
X_{1/2}
, a similar result as in Proposition
6.3
was proved in
[
44
, Proposition 5.4]
.

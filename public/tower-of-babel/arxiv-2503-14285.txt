1.1
The historical background and the goal of the paper

Since the very appearance of the theory of matroids, its significant problems have been related to finite fields;

This conjecture allows a natural generalization for matroids, while all possible bases of a matroid represent an analog of

Nevertheless, accurately adjusting

The goal of this paper is to generalize results obtained in the paper

Namely, we, in particular, consider the number of Tait colorings for a cubic biconnected planar graph. The fact that the number of Tait colorings for any such graph differs from zero is equivalent to the assertion of the Four Colors Theorem.

In obtaining the

The expression for the number of Tait colorings for a cubic planar graph

It is related to the matrix of faces of this graph, which (as far as we know) was not studied earlier.

1.2
Denotations and statements of main results

Let us exactly state the main results of this paper.
We understand
a matroid

M
as a pair
(E,r)
, where
r\equiv r_{M}
is the

of
M
,
and
E
is the
ground set
of
M
.
Recall

that

is defined on all subsets
2^{E}
of

,
and possesses the following properties:

(1) If

(2) If

r(B)\leq r(A)
.

(3) If

r(A\cup B)+r(A\cap B)\leq r(A)+r(B)

We denote a singleton

is said to be a

.
By definition,

.

Let

. The restriction of

to

M|A
with the ground set
A
and the rank function coinciding with

on

.

Recall that the characteristic polynomial

of the matroid

\chi_{M}(x)=\sum_{A:A\subseteq E}(-1)^{|A|}x^{r(E)-r(A)}.

As one can easily see, if
M
contains loops, then
\chi_{M}(x)\equiv 0
.

A matroid is said to be

over a field
\mathbb{F}
, if there exists a matrix
{\,\tt M\,}
such that its elements belong to the field
\mathbb{F}
, numbers of its columns are elements of the set

equals the rank of the set of columns of the matrix
{\,\tt M\,}
with indices in

A matroid is said to be

\mathbb{F}
(see, for example,
[
12

13
]
for more details).

We consider
the field

\mathbb{F}_{q}
of an odd characteristic, i.e.,

, where
p
is an odd prime, and

. Denote by
\eta

the multiplicative quadratic character
of the field
\mathbb{F}_{q}

\eta(0)=0

or

\mathbb{F}_{q}

For

, where
\left(\frac{x}{p}\right)

of the residue field modulo prime

.
Let us define a function
g(q,m)
, where
q
is the number of elements in the mentioned field,

g(q,m)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1/q^{m/2},&\textrm{if}~p\bmod 4=1,\,m\bmod 2=0,\\
1/(-q)^{m/2},&\textrm{if}~p\bmod 4=3,\,m\bmod 2=0,\\
0,&\textrm{if}~m\bmod 2=1.\end{array}\right.

Let us associate each element of the matroid
e

Let us now consider the result of another application of the same technique. Given a

2.1

In Theorem

we consider the set

whose cardinal number is maximal among all subsets

Here we prove one simple property of the set

, and later in Corollary

Lemma 3
.

In denotations of Theorem
1
the following equality is valid:

r(A^{*})=r(M)
.

Proof.

Assume that
s(M|A)\neq 0
for a certain set
A
with the rank
r(A)<r(M)
.
Let the symbol
I
stand for a base of the matroid
M|A
, i.e.
|I|=r(A)
. According to the so-called independence augmentation property (see
[
12
, subsection 1.1]
),
there exists an element

of the base of the matroid
M
such that
r(I\cup e)=r(A)+1
. Consequently,
r(A\cup e)>r(A)
.

Let now
B
be an arbitrary base of the matroid
M|(A\cup e)
. It necessarily contains

, otherwise for sets
A
and
B
we would get a contradiction with the second axiom of the rank function of the matroid (see subsection

). Bur then

s(M|(A\cup e))=s(A)\times\alpha_{e}\neq 0
. Therefore the considered set
A
does not coincide with
\mathop{\rm argmax}\{|A|:s(M|A)\neq 0\}
,
i.e.,
r(A^{*})=r(M)
.

2.2
Another statement of Theorem
1

In paper
[
6
]
, we express the flow polynomial in terms of the linear combination of the Legendre symbols of values of the generating function of spanning trees of a connected graph. The flow polynomial is a characteristic polynomial of the bond matroid of the graph.
But spanning trees are not bases of the bond matroid, they represent bases of the cyclic matroid dual to the bond one.
Note that in Theorem
1
the sum is calculated with respect to bases of the matroid, whose characteristic polynomial is the desired one.
Here we state another assertion, whose matrix variant implies (as a particular case) the main result of the paper
[
6
]
.

Recall that

the dual matroid

with respect to the matroid

is defined by the rank function

which is defined
on the same ground set as

r^{\perp}(A)=r(E-A)+|A|-r(E),\quad\mbox{for any $A\subseteq E$.}

One can easily make sure that
there is a biunique correspondence between bases of the initial matroid and the dual one
:
if
B
is a base of
M
, then
E-B
is a base of
M^{\perp}
and vice versa. Hence
(M^{\perp})^{\perp}=M
. It is also well known that
(see, for example,
[
12
]
) the matroid, which is dual to the matroid representable over the field
\mathbb{F}
, is also representable over this field. Consequently, the matroid dual to a regular one is also regular.
If

is a loop of the matroid

, then

is said to be a

of the matroid
M
.

Let

,

.

The contraction
of

and the rank function obeying the correlation

r_{M.A}(B)=r_{M}(C\cup B)-r_{M}(C)\quad\mbox{for any $B\subseteq A$.}

Assume that

A_{\perp}^{*}(\alpha)\equiv A_{\perp}^{*}=\mathop{\rm argmax}\limits_{A}\{|A|:s(M.A;\alpha)\neq 0\}.

The set

is not defined uniquely, but the contribution of each term in sum (

This allows us to apply formula (
11
) and thus obtain the correlation

\chi_{M^{\perp}}(q)=\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{V}}\sum_{\alpha\in({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*})^{E}}h\left(\sum_{e\in E}\alpha_{e}\left(\sum_{j\in V}k_{j}{\,\tt M\,}_{j,e}\right)^{2}\right)/q^{|V|}.

Let us again change the order of outer sums and transform the argument of the function
h
. As a result, we conclude that

\chi_{M^{\perp}}(q)=\sum_{\alpha\in({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*})^{E}}\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{V}}\frac{h({\mathbf{k}}\,L({\,\tt M\,};\alpha)\,{\mathbf{k}}^{T})}{q^{|V|}}={\sum_{\alpha\in({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*})^{E}}\Gau_{q}(L({\,\tt M\,};\alpha))}/{q^{|V|}}.

In accordance with Corollary
14
we can neglect terms with an odd rank of the matrix
L({\,\tt M\,};\alpha)
.

One can easily prove that each term in the latter sum is independent of the choice of the representation matrix of the matroid
M
. Really, let
{\,\tt M\,}^{\prime}
be some other representation matrix of the same matroid. Then
{\,\tt M\,}^{\prime}=P{\,\tt M\,}
, where
P
is some nondegenerate matrix.
But then the matrices
L(M;\alpha)={\,\tt M\,}\Lambda{\,\tt M\,}^{T}
and
L(M^{\prime};\alpha)
are congruent, consequently, according to Remark
2
,

\Gau_{q}(L({\,\tt M\,};\alpha))=\Gau_{q}(L({\,\tt M\,}^{\prime};\alpha)).

4
Conclusion

This paper is devoted to the extention of the applicability domain of the
\alpha
-representation technique, which was first used in
[
6
]
for combinatorial problems, to the case of general structures of the enumerative combinatorics. To this end, we first express the number of everywhere nonzero solutions of a system of homogeneous equations over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
as the sum of analogs of multidimensional Gaussian integrals over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The latter allow an explicit representation in terms of the Legendre symbol of principal minors of the matrix in the quadratic form. As a result, we obtain a linear combination of these Legendre symbols with rational coefficients which modulo equal natural exponents of
1/q
.

We have considered several variants of these expressions for the characteristic polynomial of the matroid.
Note that in preprint
[
9
]
we propose another (more complicated) proof of the simplified variant of Theorem
6
; there we use results obtained by C. Chevalley for the number of values of variables in the field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, with which the quadratic form equals zero.

We have also obtained the
\alpha
-representation for the number of Tait colorings for planar cubic graphs.
Note that in Example 2 the equality of all principal minors of the 4-th order is due to standard properties of the Laplace–Kirchhoff matrix. In our further paper, we are going to represent the matrix of faces as a weighted Laplace–Kirchhoff matrix for the dual graph and to explicitly describe variants of weights of its edges, which are taken into account in the sum.
Certainly, this research was inspired by its connection with the Four Colors Theorem.

A categorification of representations of U q ​ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 1 | 1 ) U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})
A. Oblomkov, L. Rozansky

Abstract
. We categorify the action of U q ​ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 1 | 1 ) U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}) on the tensor product of its vector representations ( ℂ 1 | 1 ) ⊗ N (\mathbb{C}^{1|1})^{\otimes N} . The generators E E and F F are represented by Fourier-Mukai functors between the derived categories of coherent sheaves on the total spaces of ”semi-parabolic” vector bundles over the Grassmannians Gr ​ ( k , N ) \textup{Gr}(k,N) .



1.
Introduction

Previously, Sartori
[
6
]
and Tian
[
7
]
also categorified the
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})
action on

(\mathbb{C}^{1|1})^{\otimes N}
by different means: Sartori used the category
\mathcal{O}
and Tian used the symplectic geometry in the spirit of the Heegard homology of Ozsvath and Szabo.
Even before that Khovanov-Sussan
[
2
]
used the diagrammatic approach to categorify the positive half of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})
.
For most recent developments in categorical representation theory of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})
see
[
3
]
.
Our construction is of the algebro-geometric nature. For
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2})
the algebro-geometric construction of the representation
(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes N}
is presented by Cautis, Kamnitzer and Licata in
[

1.1.
The quantum supergroup
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})

We use the conventions and notations from the paper
[
6
]
, for earlier discussion of this quantum
group see Appendix 1 of
[
5
]
.
The weight lattice
P
of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})
is spanned by
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}
and the coweight lattice
P^{*}
is spanned by
h_{1},h_{2}
with the natural pairing

\langle\epsilon_{i},h_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}
. There are two simple roots
\pm\alpha
,
\alpha=\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}

The algebra
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})
has four generators
E,F
and
q^{h_{1}},q^{h_{2}}
, but for convenience we trade the latter pair for

K=q^{h_{1}+h_{2}},\qquad H=q^{h_{1}-h_{2}}.

The generators satisfy the relations

(1.1)

E^{2}=0,\quad F^{2}=0,

(1.2)

EF+FE=K-K^{-1},

(1.3)

HE=q^{2}EH,\qquad HF=q^{-2}FH,

(1.4)

KE=EK,\quad KF=FK,\qquad KH=HK,

so
K
is central.

In order to obtain the standard relations for the quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})
, one has to rescale the generator
E
, that is, replace it by

\tilde{E}=E/(q-q^{-1}).

The Hopf algebra structure of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})
is described by the relations

\Delta(E)=E\otimes K^{-1}+1\otimes E,\quad\Delta(F)=F\otimes 1+K\otimes F,

S(E)=-EK,\quad S(F)=-K^{-1}\otimes F,

\Delta(q^{h})=q^{h}\otimes q^{h},\quad S(q^{h})=q^{-h},

u(E)=u(F)=0,\quad u(q^{h})=1,

where
S
and
u
are antipod and counit.

1.2.
Representation theory

Denote by
V=\mathbb{C}^{1|1}
the defining 2-dimensional representation of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})
. The action of the generators
E,F,K,H
is described by the following matrices relative to the standard basis
v_{0}
(even) and
v_{1}
(odd):

E=\begin{bmatrix}0&q-q^{-1}\\
0&0\end{bmatrix},\quad F=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{bmatrix},\quad K=\begin{bmatrix}q&0\\
0&q\end{bmatrix},\quad H=\begin{bmatrix}q&0\\
0&q^{-1}\end{bmatrix}.

The action of the generators on the tensor power
V^{\otimes N}
is determined by the comultiplication
\Delta
.

The generator
K
acts on
V^{\otimes N}
by the multiplication by
q^{N}
, and
V^{\otimes N}
splits into the weight spaces according to the eigenvalues of
H
:

(1.5)

V^{\otimes N}=\bigotimes_{k=0}^{N}(V^{\otimes N})_{N-2k},

the space
(V^{\otimes N})_{\lambda}
(for
\lambda=N,N-2,\ldots,-N
) being the eigenspace of
H
with the eigenvalue
q^{\lambda}
.
We use notation
E(\lambda)
,
F(\lambda)
for the corresponding linear maps

E(\lambda)\colon(V^{\otimes N})_{\lambda}\to(V^{\otimes N})_{\lambda+2},\qquad F(\lambda)\colon(V^{\otimes N})_{\lambda}\to(V^{\otimes N})_{\lambda-2},

H(\lambda),K(\lambda)\colon(V^{\otimes N})_{\lambda}\to(V^{\otimes N})_{\lambda}

1.3.
Categories

Throughout the paper we maintain the relation

\boxed{\lambda=N-2k}

where
\lambda=-N,\ldots,N
is the weight index of (
1.5
), while
k=0,\ldots,N
is the parameter of the Grassmannian
\textup{Gr}(k,N)

in the constructions below.

Our categorification of
\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}
is based on Calabi-Yau varieties
Y(\lambda)
which, similar to
\mathrm{T}^{*}\textup{Gr}(k,N)
,
are the total spaces of vector bundles
Y(\lambda)\rightarrow\textup{Gr}(k,N)
:

Y(\lambda)=\{(\mathcal{V},X)\,|\,\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{C}^{N},\;\dim\mathcal{V}=k,\;X\in\textup{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{N},\mathcal{V})\},

that is, the fibers of
Y(\lambda)
are half-nilpotent and half-parabolic.
Equivalently,

Y(\lambda)=\bigl(\textup{GL}_{N}\times\tilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{k}\bigr)/P_{k},

where

\tilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{k}=\{X\in\mathfrak{gl}(N)\;|\;X(\mathbb{C}^{N})\subset\mathbb{C}^{k}\},

\mathbb{C}^{k}\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
being the standard
k
-dimensional subspace, and
P_{k}\subset\textup{GL}_{N}
is the parabolic subgroup:

P_{k}=\{g\in\textup{GL}_{N}\;|\;g(\mathbb{C}^{k})\subset\mathbb{C}^{k}\}.

The group
\mathbb{C}^{*}
acts on
Y(\lambda)
by scaling the fibers:
X\mapsto t^{2}X
.
To the weight space
(V^{\otimes N})_{\lambda}
we associate
the
derived category of

\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant bounded complexes of coherent sheaves over
Y(\lambda)
:

(1.6)

\mathcal{D}(\lambda)=D_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}^{b}\bigl(Y(\lambda)\bigr).

1.4.
Functors

Having selected the categories (
1.6
), we choose the functors

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}(\lambda-2)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}(\lambda)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}(\lambda+2)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}(\lambda)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}(\lambda)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{K}(\lambda),\mathcal{H}(\lambda)}

corresponding to (the matrix elements of) the generators
E,F,K,H
of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})
in such a way that these functors satisfy relations categorifying
(up to a shift) the relations
(
1.1
)-(
1.4
).

Let
\mathbf{q}
denote the endo-functor of
\mathcal{D}(\lambda)
that shifts the weights of the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
action by
1
. Then the obvious choice for
\mathcal{H}(\lambda)
and
\mathcal{K}(\lambda)
is

\mathcal{K}(\lambda)=\mathbf{q}^{\lambda},

\mathcal{H}(\lambda)=\mathbf{q}^{N}.

In the paper we use the standard Fouries-Mukai functor formalism. In particular, an object
\mathcal{Z}\in D^{b}_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}\bigl(\mathcal{Y}(\lambda),\mathcal{Y}(\lambda^{\prime})\bigr)
yields
a functor
\mathcal{D}(\lambda)\to\mathcal{D}(\lambda^{\prime})
. We denote the functor also as
\mathcal{Z}
in order to keep the notations simple. The sheaf
\mathcal{Z}
is
called the FM kernel of the corresponding functor.

Now we define the FM kernels for the functors
\mathcal{E}(\lambda)
and
\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
.
For two weights
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}
such that
\lambda^{\prime}\geq\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda
is even, define a smooth subvariety
W(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\subset Y(\lambda)\times Y(\lambda^{\prime})
:

W(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})=\{(\mathcal{V},X,\mathcal{V}^{\prime},X^{\prime})\;|\;\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{V}^{\prime},X^{\prime}=X\},

where the condition
X^{\prime}=X
has the following precise meaning. Since
\mathcal{V}
is required to be a subspace of
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
, we can further require
p\circ X^{\prime}=0
, where
p\colon\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}^{\prime}/\mathcal{V}
. Thus
X^{\prime}\colon\mathbb{C}^{N}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
is reduced to
\tilde{X}^{\prime}\colon\mathbb{C}^{N}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}
, and we finally require
\tilde{X}^{\prime}=X
.

Let
\tau_{\lambda}\rightarrow Y(\lambda)
denote the pull-back of the tautological bundle over
\textup{Gr}(k,N)
: the fiber of
\tau_{\lambda}
over a point
(\mathcal{V},X)\in Y(\lambda)
is the vector space
\mathcal{V}
. We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\lambda)=\mathcal{O}_{W(\lambda,\lambda+2)}\otimes\pi^{*}_{\lambda}(\det\tau_{\lambda})

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\lambda+2)=\mathcal{O}_{W(\lambda,\lambda+2)}\otimes\pi^{*}_{\lambda}(\det\tau_{\lambda})^{N-k}\otimes\pi^{*}_{\lambda+2}(\det\tau_{\lambda+2})^{-N+k}\,

Finally, note that the (pushed forward) structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}
of the diagonal

\Delta=W(\lambda,\lambda)\subset Y(\lambda)\times Y(\lambda)
is the FM kernel of the identity functor.

The following is our main result:

Theorem 1
.

Thus defined, the sheaves
\mathcal{E}(\lambda)
and
\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
satisfy the following relations:

(1.7)

\mathcal{E}(\lambda+2)\circ\mathcal{E}(\lambda)\simeq 0,\qquad\mathcal{F}(\lambda)\circ\mathcal{F}(\lambda+2)\simeq 0,

(1.8)

[\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}[N-k-1]\xrightarrow{\;\;\alpha\;\;}\mathcal{F}(\lambda+2)\circ\mathcal{E}(\lambda)]\simeq[\mathbf{q}^{2N}\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}[k-N-1]\xrightarrow{\;\;\beta\;\;}\mathcal{E}(\lambda-2)\circ\mathcal{F}(\lambda)]

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are the appropriate morphisms between the corrresponding sheaves to be specified in the proof.

The decategification of the above action is obtained by passing to the localized

\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant
K
-theory of categories
\mathcal{D}(\lambda)
:

Corollary 1
.

The
2^{N}
-dimensional vector space

\bigoplus_{\lambda}\mathrm{K}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}\bigl(Y(\lambda)\bigr)

over the field of fractions of
\mathrm{K}_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\mathrm{pt})
is a representation of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})
via:

E(\lambda)\mapsto\mathbf{q}^{-N}\mathcal{E}(\lambda),\quad F(\lambda)\mapsto\mathcal{F}(\lambda)[N-k],\quad K(\lambda)\mapsto\mathbf{q}^{\lambda}\mathcal{O}_{\Delta},\quad H(\lambda)\mapsto\mathbf{q}^{N}\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}.

By comparing the dimensions of the weight spaces one can conclude that the
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})
-representation from the corollary above
is exactly
(\mathbb{C}^{1|1})^{\otimes N}
.

Remark 1
.

We use the cohomological conventions throughout the paper. The term
C_{i}
of a chain complex

\cdots C_{2}\to C_{1}\to C_{0}
has the cohomological degree
-i
and in our shift conventions

(C[n])_{k}=C_{k-n}
.

This note has three sections. We present a proof of the main theorem in section
2
. In section
3
we present a proposal
that extend our construction to the case of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n})
.

2.
Proof of the main theorem

2.1.
Nilpotent relation

Let us first prove the relations (
1.7
). The kernels of the compositions
\mathcal{E}(\lambda)\circ\mathcal{E}(\lambda-2)

and of
\mathcal{F}(\lambda)\circ\mathcal{F}(\lambda+2)
are the push-forwards along the projection
\pi_{\lambda-2,\lambda+2}
from

Y(\lambda-2)\times Y(\lambda)\times Y(\lambda+2)
of the kernels

\mathcal{O}_{W(\lambda-2,\lambda,\lambda+2)}\otimes\pi^{*}_{\lambda}(\det\tau_{\lambda})\otimes\pi_{\lambda+2}^{*}(\det\tau_{\lambda+2}),

\mathcal{O}_{W(\lambda-2,\lambda,\lambda+2)}\otimes\pi^{*}_{\lambda-2}(\det\tau_{\lambda})^{N-k-1}\otimes\pi^{*}_{\lambda}(\det\tau_{\lambda})\otimes\pi_{\lambda+2}(\det\tau_{\lambda+2})^{k-N},

where the variety
W(\lambda-2,\lambda,\lambda+2)
consists of the triples
(\mathcal{V},X),(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},X^{\prime}),(\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime},X^{\prime\prime})

inside
Y(\lambda-2)\times Y(\lambda)\times Y(\lambda+2)

such that

\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime},\quad X=X^{\prime}=X^{\prime\prime}.

Over a point of
Y(\lambda-2)\times Y(\lambda+2)
the fiber of the projection
\pi_{\lambda-2,\lambda+2}
intersected with
W(\lambda-2,\lambda,\lambda+2)
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Moreover, the restriction of the above described FM kernel on this
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is
a line bundle
\mathcal{O}(-1)
that has no homology. Hence the push-forward along
\pi_{\lambda-2,\lambda+2}
of both kernels are homotopic to zero.

2.2.
Koszul complexes and generalizations

We use generalization of the Koszul complexes in our arguments. Here we fix notations for these complexes and corresponding differentials.
For a given vector bundle
V
on variety
X
a section
s\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{V})

defines a the zero-section subvariety
Z(s)\subset X
.
Let
V^{\vee}
be a dual vector bundle then the complex
Respectively, the Koszul complex
K(V^{\vee},s)=\oplus_{j=0}^{r}K_{-j}(V^{\vee},s)
,
K_{-j}(V^{\vee},s)=\Lambda^{j}V^{\vee}

is a complex of vector bundles such that the zeroth homology of this complex is the sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{Z(s)}
. Moreover, if the
intersection is transverse then
\mathcal{O}_{Z(s)}
is homotopy equivalent to the complex
K(V^{\vee},s)
.

To define the differential in
K(V^{\vee},s)
a cosection
\check{s}:\mathcal{V}^{\vee}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
that is dual to
s
.
On a sufficiently small affine chart
U\subset X
we can trivialize
V^{\vee}
by choosing
sections
v_{i}\in H^{0}(U,V^{\vee})
,
i=1,\dots,\mathrm{rank}(V)
that span every fiber of
V^{\vee}
over
U
.
Then the differential
D_{s}^{j}:\Lambda^{j}V^{\vee}\to\Lambda^{j-1}V^{\vee}
is defined as:

D^{j}_{s}v_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge v_{i_{s}}=\sum_{l=1}^{s}(-1)^{l}\check{s}(v_{i_{l}})v_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\widehat{v}_{i_{l}}\dots\wedge v_{i_{s}}.

In our arguments below we need a slight generalization of the Koszul complex above. Let us fix a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
,
t\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})
. The generalizations that we need interpolate between the Koszul complexes
K(V^{\vee},s)
and
K(V^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{\vee},s\cdot t)
. For
I\subset\mathbb{Z}
we define
K^{I}(\mathcal{L},t;V^{\vee},s)
as complex with

K^{I}_{-j}(\mathcal{L},t;V^{\vee},s)=\Lambda^{j}V^{\vee}\otimes(\mathcal{L}^{\vee})^{d(I,j)},\quad d(I,j)=|I\cap[1,j]|.

and the differential

D_{t;s}^{I;j}:K^{I}_{-j}(\mathcal{L},t;V^{\vee},s)\to K^{I}_{-j+1}(\mathcal{L},t;V^{\vee},s)

is defined by

D_{t;s}^{I;j}=\begin{cases}D_{s}^{j},&j\notin I\\
tD^{j}_{s},&j\in I\end{cases}.

If the sections of the vector bundles are clear from the context then we abbreviate the notation for the generalized
Koszul complex to
K^{I}(\mathcal{L};\mathcal{V}^{\vee})
.

2.3.
Highest weight space

First, let us compute
\mathcal{E}(N-2)\circ\mathcal{F}(N)
. The space
Y(N)
is a point and
Y(N-2)=\mathcal{O}(-1)^{\oplus N}
where

\mathcal{O}(-1)
is a tautological line bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{N-1}
.
Respectively, the correspondence variety

\mathbb{P}^{N-1}\subset Y(N-2)

Thus the FM kernel for

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N-1}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N-1}}\otimes\det(\tau_{N-2})^{-N}.

Let

There is a natural cosection

3.
Towards the categorification of

An advantage of the algebro-geometric approach to the categorification of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})
is that, conjecturally,
its framework can be extended easily to
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n})
.

Consider a
permuted
standard basis
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m+n}
of the defining
\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}

V=\mathbb{C}^{m|n}

m

n

e_{i}
is even if
f(i)=0
and
e_{i}
is odd if
f(i)=1
, so
|f^{-1}(0)|=m
and
|f^{-1}(1)|=n
. The weight subspaces

To a weight

\mathcal{V}_{i-1},&\text{if $e_{i}$ is odd.}\end{cases}\Biggr\}

\mathbb{C}^{*}

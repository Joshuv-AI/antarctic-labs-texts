Itô-Stratonovich Conversion in Infinite Dimensions for Unbounded, Time-Dependent, Nonlinear Operators
Daniel Goodair

Abstract
We prove that a solution, in a variational framework, to the Stratonovich stochastic partial differential equation with noise 𝒢 ⁡ ( t , 𝚿 t ) ∘ d ​ 𝒲 t \mathcal{G}\left(t,\bm{\Psi}_{t}\right)\circ d\mathcal{W}_{t} is given by a solution to the Itô equation with Itô-Stratonovich corrector 1 2 ​ ∑ i = 1 ∞ D u ​ 𝒢 i ​ ( t , 𝚿 t ) ​ [ 𝒢 i ​ ( t , 𝚿 t ) ] ​ d ​ t \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}D_{u}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(t,\bm{\Psi}_{t}\right)\left[\mathcal{G}_{i}(t,\bm{\Psi}_{t})\right]dt . Here 𝒢 i \mathcal{G}_{i} denotes the action of 𝒢 \mathcal{G} on the i th i^{\textnormal{th}} component of the cylindrical noise, and D u ​ 𝒢 i D_{u}\mathcal{G}_{i} its Fréchet partial derivative in the Hilbert space for which the Itô form is satisfied. The noise operator 𝒢 \mathcal{G} may be time-dependent, nonlinear, and unbounded in the sense of differential operators; in the latter case, one must pass to a larger space in order to solve the Stratonovich equation. Our proof relies on martingale techniques, and the results apply to fluid equations with time-dependent and nonlinear transport noise.

Introduction

Whilst Stratonovich stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) arise plentifully from various physical principles, the Stratonovich integral lacks key favourable properties of its Itô counterpart. Therefore, when tasked with obtaining a solution to a Stratonovich SPDE, one would rather work with a corrected Itô equation whose solution is known to solve the original Stratonovich SPDE. The goal of this paper is to rigorously provide such a framework, applicable to noise operators which may be nonlinear, time-dependent, and mapping to a larger Hilbert Space. In analogy with classical SDE theory, the Itô-Stratonovich corrector is given by the Fréchet partial derivative of the noise operator acting on itself.

1.2
Motivation and Relation to the Literature

Of the many physically relevant Stratonovich SPDEs, let us first draw attention to the thriving literature of transport noise in fluid mechanics. Considered since the work of Kraichnan [
20
], transport noise in fluids have attracted significant interest in the last ten years due to a plethora of modelling and theoretical developments. Briefly, these include the use of geometric variational principles [
19
], a Lagrangian Reynolds Decomposition and Transport Theorem [
22
], stochastic model reduction [
6
,
11
] and regularisation by noise [
8
,
9
]. Whilst the type of transport noise varies based on equation and philosophy, the shared term is a Stratonovich integral

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\bm{\Psi}_{s}\circ dW^{i}_{s}

where
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}
is defined for sufficiently regular
f:\mathscr{O}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathscr{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}f=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}f
. Each
\xi_{i}:\mathscr{O}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the collection
(\xi_{i})
will either have strong decay or orthogonality so that the sum converges, whilst
(W^{i})
are independent standard Brownian Motions. The process
\bm{\Psi}
is prescribed by an evolution equation, for which it is essential to rewrite the Stratonovich integral as a corrected Itô one; the prevalent approach has been a heuristic observation, see for example [
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
10
,
12
], arguing by linearity of
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}
and standard stochastic calculus that the Itô-Stratonovich corrector for each term in the summand takes the form

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\bm{\Psi},W^{i}\right]_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\left[\bm{\Psi},W^{i}\right]_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\cdot}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{j}}\bm{\Psi}_{s}dW^{j},W^{i}\right]_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\bm{\Psi}_{s}ds\right)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}^{2}\bm{\Psi}_{s}ds.

Innumerably many more works cite one of the above as justification, or directly state the Itô form given the now extensive literature on these equations. The authors proceed with their analysis and results on the Itô form, without rigorously addressing how these results apply to the original and well-motivated Stratonovich SPDE. A key consideration is the fact that the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}
is unbounded, as a differential operator, so in the above heuristic one is really passing to a larger function space when putting
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}
inside of the cross-variation or stochastic integral. More apparently, whilst
\bm{\Psi}
would then be specified by a second order SPDE, the evolution equation for
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\bm{\Psi}
takes three derivatives of
\bm{\Psi}
so the drift terms in
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\bm{\Psi}
may no longer be of finite variation in the function space which
\bm{\Psi}
satisfies its SPDE.

At a glance, one could avoid this issue of regularity by arguing in weak form. There, one may pass the derivatives of
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}
onto test functions which can bare the regularity requirement: this approach was given in [
9
,
13
], where only one dimensional martingale arguments are needed as the tested identity is satisfied in
\mathbb{R}
. In reconciling this notion of weak solution, one meets the issue of identifying

\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\bm{\Psi},\phi\right\rangle\circ dW^{i}_{s}=\left\langle\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\bm{\Psi}\circ dW^{i}_{s},\phi\right\rangle

whereby the cost of a derivative becomes key once more, and infinite dimensional martingale arguments are required.

These heuristics were made rigorous by the author and Dan Crisan in the book [
17
], examining properties of the cross-variation in Hilbert Spaces and quantifying the ‘loss of a derivative’ experienced in the conversion, understood broadly in the variational framework by an analytically strong solution of the Itô equation is an analytically weak solution of the Stratonovich SPDE. However, following the above heuristics, the arguments relied heavily on the fact that the noise operator was linear and time-independent. Furthermore, the resulting expression was not obviously compatible with the well-known conversion in finite dimensions. Sufficiency for applications and mathematical completeness thus motivated Theorem
1.1
.

In Section
3
we shall consider applications of Theorem
1.1
to relevant cases of time-dependence and nonlinearity, which we comment on now. Whilst there are many such Stratonovich noise structures available, we continue our motivations with transport noise. There is a strong argument, from the modelling perspective, to include time-dependence in the spatial correlation functions
(\xi_{i})
. In the words of Holm’s breakthrough paper, the
(\xi_{i})
are ‘specified from the physics of the problem …obtained from, say, coarse-grained observations or computations’; it is well reported that the ocean exhibits
memory
, see for example [
24
,
26
], so the observed spatial correlations of ocean dynamics should evolve over time. An existence result for the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations is given in Subsection
3.2
. In the direction of nonlinearity, a transport noise was proposed in [
10
] following the idea that ‘turbulence is more developed in regions of high large-scale vorticity; hence, the small-scale noise should be modulated by an increasing function [of vorticity]’. The small-scale noise produces a large-scale transport noise in the separation of scales limit, given in [
10
] by

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\left(f(w_{s})\right)\circ dW^{i}_{s}

where
w:\mathbb{T}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the fluid vorticity and
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
modulates the noise intensity. The authors formally calculate the Itô-Stratonovich corrector by firstly applying the above heuristic, and secondly using the standard one-dimensional Itô Formula for
f(w(x))
applied pointwise in space. More generally, one could consider the noise

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}F(\bm{\Psi}_{s})\circ dW^{i}_{s}

where
F
is a function space valued mapping with genuinely infinite dimensional nonlinear effects. Whilst the prior heuristic no longer applies, we compute the corrector in Subsection
3.3
by applying Theorem
1.1
and show that it agrees with the specific choice of
F
as an evaluation map
F(\psi)(x)=f(\psi(x))
.

More generally, we are only aware of two results concerning an Itô-Stratonovich conversion in infinite dimensions: the paper [
25
] and the book [
7
] Subchapter 4.5.2. In both cases, the noise operator must be time-independent and mapping within the same Hilbert Space. Mild solutions enjoying moment estimates are considered in [
25
] whilst the identification is only formal in [
7
]. Critically, both define the Stratonovich integral by, and conduct their proofs using, the limit of a sum over partitions in time evaluated at the mid-point of the intervals; we use the semi-martingale definition and approach. A rigorous conversion in a variational framework, where the noise operator is time-dependent and unbounded, understood via martingale techniques, all appear to be novelties of this work.

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)ds+\int_{0}^{t}D_{u}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\left[\mathcal{G}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\mathbbm{1}_{s\leq\tau}\right]d\mathcal{W}_{s}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}D_{uu}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\left[\mathcal{G}_{i}(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s})\mathbbm{1}_{\cdot\leq\tau}\right]\left[\mathcal{G}_{i}(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s})\mathbbm{1}_{\cdot\leq\tau}\right]ds

holds
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
in

. As the time integrals are well defined in

is linear and
\tau_{n}\leq\tau

\mathbbm{P}-a.s.

Square-integrability, and thus that
\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(\cdot,\bm{\Psi}_{\cdot}\right)^{\tau_{n}}\in\bar{\mathcal{M}}^{2}_{c}(X)
, follows similarly to (
4
) as

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left\|D_{u}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\left[\mathcal{G}_{j}(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s})\right]\right\|_{X}^{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq c\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left\|D_{u}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\left[\mathcal{G}_{j}(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s})\right]\right\|_{U}^{2}ds\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}c_{i}^{2}c_{j}^{2}\left(1+\left\|\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right\|_{H}^{q}\right)^{2}\left(1+\left\|\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right\|_{V}\right)^{2}ds\right)

which is again finite due to control from the stopping time. We now examine the cross-variation,

\left[\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(\cdot,\bm{\Psi}_{\cdot}\right)^{\tau_{n}},W^{i}\right]_{t}=\left[\int_{0}^{\cdot\wedge\tau_{n}}D_{u}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\left[\mathcal{G}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\right]d\mathcal{W}_{s},W^{i}\right]_{t}

and employing Lemma
1.4
alongside the definition of the integral as an
L^{2}\left(\Omega;X\right)
limit, then

\displaystyle\left[\int_{0}^{\cdot\wedge\tau_{n}}D_{u}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\left[\mathcal{G}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\right]d\mathcal{W}_{s},W^{i}\right]_{t}

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\cdot\wedge\tau_{n}}D_{u}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\left[\mathcal{G}_{j}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\right]dW^{j}_{s},W^{i}\right]_{t}

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{\cdot\wedge\tau_{n}}D_{u}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\left[\mathcal{G}_{j}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\right]dW^{j}_{s},W^{i}\right]_{t}

for the limit in

1.4
. To inspect this we introduce an orthonormal basis
(a_{k})
of

, so that by definition the above cross-variation is given by

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[\left\langle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{\cdot\wedge\tau_{n}}D_{u}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\left[\mathcal{G}_{j}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\right]dW^{j}_{s},a_{k}\right\rangle_{X},W^{i}\right]_{t}a_{k}

From classical finite dimensional theory, due to the independence of the Brownian Motions then for

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\left\langle D_{u}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\left[\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\right],a_{k}\right\rangle_{X}ds\right)a_{k}=\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}D_{u}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\left[\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\right]ds

which satisfies the conditions of Lemma
1.4

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left[\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(\cdot,\bm{\Psi}_{\cdot}\right)^{\tau_{n}},W^{i}_{\cdot}\right]_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}D_{u}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\left[\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\right]ds

which we must justify converges in

. Once more this is similar to (
4
), as

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\mathbbm{E}\left(\left\|\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}D_{u}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\left[\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\right]ds\right\|_{X}^{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq c\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\left\|\ D_{u}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\left[\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\right]\right\|_{U}^{2}ds\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq c\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}c_{i}^{4}\left(1+\left\|\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right\|^{2q}_{H}\right)\left(1+\left\|\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right\|^{2}_{V}\right)ds\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\tilde{c}_{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}c_{i}^{2}\right)\left(\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\left(1+\left\|\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right\|^{2}_{V}\right)ds\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

where
\tilde{c}_{n}
is some constant dependent on
n
owing to the bounds from
\tau_{n}
, which is finite as established. Thus, the Stratonovich integral appearing in (
2
) is well defined by

\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\circ d\mathcal{W}_{s}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)^{n}dW^{i}_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}D_{u}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\left[\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\right]ds\right)\right)

for the limit taken
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
in
X
, and the infinite sum in
L^{2}\left(\Omega;X\right)
. The first term is precisely the definition of the Itô integral

\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\mathcal{G}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)d\mathcal{W}_{s}

appearing in (
1
). Comparing terms in (
1
) and (
2
), it only remains to show that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}D_{u}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\left[\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\right]ds\right)=\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}D_{u}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\left[\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\right]ds.

To see this we can extract a
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
convergent subsequence of the partial sums converging in
L^{2}\left(\Omega;X\right)
, which we know to be

\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}D_{u}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\left[\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\right]ds

from (

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}D_{u}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\left[\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\right]ds\right)=\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}D_{u}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\left[\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)\right]ds

3.1
Linear Operators

Lemma 3.1
.

In addition to the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, suppose that for every

and

that
\mathcal{G}_{i}(s,\cdot)
is linear. Then the identity (
1
) reduces to

\bm{\Psi}_{t}=\bm{\Psi}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\left(\mathcal{A}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\mathcal{G}_{i}(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s})\right)\right)ds+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\mathcal{G}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)d\mathcal{W}_{s}

This is an immediate consequence of the fact that for
F:U\rightarrow X

.
∎

By Lemma

3.2
Time-Dependent Transport Noise

Our next application is the case of a time-dependent transport noise, viewed in an existence result for the Navier-Stokes equations presented now on the
N-
dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{N}
. For the functional analytic framework we recall that any function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
admits the representation

f(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f_{k}e^{ik\cdot x}

(6)

whereby each
f_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
is such that
f_{k}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muf_{-k}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
and the infinite sum is defined as a limit in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
, see e.g. [
23
] Subsection 1.5 for details. We then define
L^{2}_{\sigma}
as the subset of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
of zero-mean functions
f
whereby for all
k\in\mathbbm{Z}^{N}
,
k\cdot f_{k}=0
with
f_{k}
as in (
6
). For general
m\in\mathbb{N}
we introduce
W^{m,2}_{\sigma}
as the intersection of
W^{m,2}(\mathbb{T}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
respectively with
L^{2}_{\sigma}
,
W^{0,2}_{\sigma}:=L^{2}_{\sigma}
and
W^{-1,2}_{\sigma}:=\left(W^{1,2}_{\sigma}\right)^{*}
, the dual space. Set
\mathcal{P}
, the Leray Projector, as the orthogonal projection in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
onto
L^{2}_{\sigma}
. We continue to use
\mathcal{L}
from Subsection
1.2
, defined for sufficiently regular vector fields

by
\mathcal{L}_{f}g=\sum_{j=1}^{N}f^{j}\partial_{j}g
. The noise operator that we consider, following its introduction in [
19
] but now allowing for the spatial correlation functions
(\xi_{i})
to evolve in time, is defined along the basis components
e_{i}
of the noise space
\mathfrak{U}
by

B_{i}(s,f):=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\xi_{i}(s)^{j}\partial_{j}f+f^{j}\nabla\xi_{i}(s)^{j}\right).

In the statement of the result, we shall denote
W^{k,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
by
W^{k,\infty}
for simplicity.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
1\leq m\in\mathbb{N}
,
u_{0}:\Omega\rightarrow W^{m,2}_{\sigma}
be
\mathcal{F}_{0}-
measurable and
\xi_{i}\in C^{1}\left([0,T];W^{m+2,\infty}\right)

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\left\|\xi_{i}(t)\right\|_{W^{m+2,\infty}}^{2}\right)<\infty

where:

1.

\tau\in(0,T]

\mathbbm{P}-a.s.

2.

is progressively measurable in
W^{m+1,2}_{\sigma}
whilst for
\mathbbm{P}-a.e.

\omega

and
u_{\cdot}(\omega)\mathbbm{1}_{\cdot\leq\tau(\omega)}\in L^{2}\left([0,T];W^{m+1,2}_{\sigma}\right)
;

3.

The identity

u_{t}=u_{0}-\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{u_{s}}u_{s}\ ds+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\Delta u_{s}\,ds-\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\mathcal{P}B(s,u_{s})\circ d\mathcal{W}_{s}

\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
in

.

Moreover if

.

Proof.

As each
\mathcal{P}B_{i}(s,\cdot)
is linear, then by applying Lemma
3.1
for the spaces

we see that to verify the Stratonovich identity, it is sufficient to satisfy the Itô form

u_{t}=u_{0}-\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{u_{s}}u_{s}\ ds+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\Delta u_{s}\,ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}B_{i}\left(s,\mathcal{P}B_{i}(s,u_{s})\right)ds-\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\mathcal{P}B(s,u_{s})d\mathcal{W}_{s}

in

. Note that in addition to their spatial smoothness and summability, each

is continuously differentiable in time to facilitate Lemma

(\xi_{i})

16

∎

3.3
Nonlinear Transport Noise

The purpose of this subsection is to elucidate computations regarding a nonlinear transport noise as motivated in the introduction. Given the many possible ways of incorporating such a noise into an equation, we cannot expect to capture all rigorous possibilities of application. Therefore, we provide only a formal result by computing the expression given in Theorem
1.1
; in any specific application, Theorem
1.1
provides the tools to make this rigorous.

Lemma 3.3
.

Consider
\bm{\Psi}
on some domain
\mathscr{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\bm{\Psi}:[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathscr{O}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
, understood as a function space valued process
\bm{\Psi}:[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow H
, specified by the Stratonovich SPDE

\bm{\Psi}_{t}=\bm{\Psi}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{A}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)ds+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}F(\bm{\Psi}_{s})\circ dW^{i}_{s}

(7)

where
H\hookrightarrow U
are Hilbert Spaces and
F:H\rightarrow U
is continuous with sufficiently regular Fréchet derivatives
D_{h}F
,
D_{hh}F
. Then (
7
) has corresponding Itô form

\bm{\Psi}_{t}=\bm{\Psi}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathcal{A}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\left(D_{h}F(\bm{\Psi}_{s})\left[\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}F(\bm{\Psi}_{s})\right]\right)\right)ds+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}F(\bm{\Psi}_{s})dW^{i}_{s}.

(8)

Suppose that
F
is defined by a differentiable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
through
F(\psi)(x)=f(\psi(x))
. Then (
8
) reduces to

\bm{\Psi}_{t}=\bm{\Psi}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathcal{A}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\left(f^{\prime}(\bm{\Psi}_{s})^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}(\bm{\Psi}_{s})\right)\right)ds+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}f^{\prime}(\bm{\Psi}_{s})\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}(\bm{\Psi}_{s})dW^{i}_{s}.

Proof.

We verify the Itô-Stratonovich corrector, given from Theorem
1.1
, for each
i
in the summand, by
D_{h}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}F\right)(\bm{\Psi}_{s})\left[\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}F(\bm{\Psi}_{s})\right]
. By the chain rule for Fréchet derivatives, this is

D_{h}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\left(F(\bm{\Psi}_{s})\right)\left[D_{h}F(\bm{\Psi}_{s})\left[\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}F(\bm{\Psi}_{s})\right]\right]

and as
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}
is linear, it further simplifies to

\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\left(D_{h}F(\bm{\Psi}_{s})\left[\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}F(\bm{\Psi}_{s})\right]\right)

as required. The second part of the lemma follows from the facts that
D_{h}F(\phi)[\psi]=f^{\prime}(\phi)\psi
and
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\left(f(\psi)\right)=f^{\prime}(\psi)\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\psi
.
∎

The second part of the lemma recovers the conversion given in [
10
] page 6, where it is further shown that if
\xi_{i}
is divergence-free and
d=1
then

\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\left(f^{\prime}(\bm{\Psi}_{s})^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}(\bm{\Psi}_{s})\right)=\textnormal{div}\left(f^{\prime}(\bm{\Psi}_{s})^{2}(\xi_{i}\otimes\xi_{i})\nabla\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right).

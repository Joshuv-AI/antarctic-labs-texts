DocVAL: Validated Chain-of-Thought Distillation for Grounded Document VQA
Ahmad Mohammadshirazi, Pinaki Prasad Guha Neogi, Dheeraj Kulshrestha, Rajiv Ramnath

Abstract
Document visual question answering (DocVQA) requires models to jointly reason over textual content and spatial layout, yet current systems exhibit a sharp accuracy–efficiency trade-off: large teacher models achieve strong grounding but are too expensive for deployment, while compact students suffer substantial drops in localization performance. We propose DocVAL, a validated chain-of-thought distillation framework that transfers the spatial reasoning ability of a large teacher into a deployable student VLM through three key components: (1) teacher supervision with validation-time text detection to filter and denoise training signals, (2) a multi-module validator (VAL) that enforces answer correctness and geometric consistency while producing fine-grained, pixel-level error feedback, and (3) a two-stage student training scheme that first learns from validated CoT traces and then undergoes iterative refinement driven by VAL feedback. Our student (Gemma-3 12B) achieves 91.4% ANLS and 82.4% mAP on DocVQA as a pure VLM requiring no text detection or OCR at inference. Extensive ablations demonstrate that validated feedback contributes 6.3 mAP gain and iterative refinement accounts for 9.7 mAP improvement. We release 95k high-quality, validator-verified CoT traces to advance spatial reasoning research in document understanding. Dataset and implementation: https://github.com/ahmad-shirazi/DocVAL

Introduction

Figure 1
:

Document Visual Question Answering (DocVQA) has emerged as a critical capability for intelligent systems processing invoices, receipts, forms, and scanned documents
20
;
23
;
7
. Unlike natural image VQA, document understanding demands precise answer localization within structured layouts, requiring text recognition, spatial reasoning, and multi-modal integration. Although large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) achieve remarkable accuracy
5
;
18
;
15
, their deployment faces severe constraints: high inference costs, latency exceeding 3 seconds, and cloud dependencies make them impractical for production systems processing millions of documents daily.

This leads to a fundamental dilemma. Compact models under 15B parameters, which are suitable for on-premise or edge deployment, suffer severe degradation in spatial reasoning: while large teachers perform well on both textual accuracy and localization, naively fine-tuned compact students struggle, especially on spatial grounding tasks that require precise bounding box prediction. This gap arises from the difficulty of learning accurate coordinates and spatial relations without external detection scaffolding. Classical knowledge distillation methods that transfer output distributions or intermediate features
1
;
3
are not designed to capture the underlying spatial reasoning process that drives answer localization. At the same time, widely used metrics such as ANLS
33
, which focus on textual correctness, ignore spatial precision. This creates a
trust gap
: models may produce plausible answers that cannot be reliably localized—a serious problem in high-stakes domains such as financial auditing or medical records, where human verification is essential.

We introduce
DocVAL
, a validated chain-of-thought (CoT) distillation framework that transfers complete spatial reasoning from large teachers to compact students through quality-assured knowledge transfer. Inspired by CoT reasoning
29
;
25
and validation-driven learning
19
—where model improvement is guided by explicit validation feedback in an iterative loop rather than one-shot training on fixed datasets—DocVAL uniquely addresses document spatial grounding through a three-phase pipeline.

Our key insight is that spatial reasoning can be effectively distilled when four conditions hold:
(1)
teacher reasoning is expressed as explicit spatial descriptions rather than opaque coordinate sequences, making the spatial logic interpretable;
(2)
training data is filtered through multi-aspect validation that removes hallucinated coordinates and inconsistent spatial relations;
(3)
students receive detailed, actionable feedback that pinpoints their errors (e.g., wrong regions, pixel offsets, or reasoning mismatches), enabling targeted improvement via iterative refinement; and
(4)
students are trained to perform pure visual-to-bounding-box mapping without relying on detection at inference time, encouraging strong internal spatial representations.

Our primary contributions are as follows:
(1)
we propose DocVAL, a validated CoT distillation framework that combines detection-guided teacher generation, quality filtering, and iterative refinement while training students as pure VLMs;
(2)
we design
VAL
, a rule-based dual-mode validator that serves both as a filter for teacher data curation and as a verifier providing detailed student feedback through five specialized modules;
(3)
we conduct extensive ablation studies covering detection methods, teacher choices, validation strategies, and training procedures; and
(4)
we release 95K validator-verified reasoning traces to support future research on spatially grounded document understanding.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section
2
reviews related work in document VQA, knowledge distillation, and CoT reasoning. Section
3
details the DocVAL framework, including the validation architecture, teacher generation pipeline, and two-Stage student training. Section
4
presents experimental setup, datasets, and evaluation protocols. Section
5
provides comprehensive results, ablation studies, and qualitative analysis. Section
6
concludes with limitations and future directions.

2
Related Work

2.1
Document Visual Question Answering

Early models like LayoutLM
32
and successors
31
;
5
pioneered layout-aware understanding through joint text-image-layout pre-training. Recent work diverges into OCR-free and OCR-based approaches. OCR-free models
9
;
10
;
22
integrate text recognition end-to-end but face high-resolution processing challenges. OCR-based models achieve sophisticated spatial integration: LayTextLLM
17
projects bboxes to unique tokens, DocLayLLM
15
integrates visual patches with positional tokens, LayoutLLM
18
employs specialized layout pre-training. However, these lack explicit spatial grounding mechanisms and provide limited interpretability for answer localization. DocVAL operates as pure VLM without detection at inference, demonstrating that appropriate training enables end-to-end document grounding.

2.2
Spatial Grounding and Localization

Spatial grounding remains challenging despite dataset availability. FUNSD
7
, SROIE
23
, and DocVQA
20
provide bbox annotations but evaluation focuses primarily on textual metrics. Text detection models
14
;
28
;
2
localize regions effectively but operate as preprocessing steps. DocVAL uniquely uses detection as training-time scaffolding for teacher generation and VAL validation while deploying pure VLM students, achieving interpretable spatial reasoning without inference dependencies.

2.3
Chain-of-Thought Reasoning

CoT prompting
29
elicits step-by-step reasoning in LLMs, extended to vision-language domains
30
;
34
. However, document-specific CoT with spatial reasoning remains limited, and existing approaches lack validation mechanisms to ensure reasoning quality. DocVAL introduces validated CoT specifically designed for spatial reasoning, with multi-aspect verification ensuring both answer correctness and bbox precision while training students to replicate reasoning from visual features alone.

2.4
Knowledge Distillation

Traditional distillation
3
matches output distributions or intermediate features
8
;
11
. VLM distillation
13
;
12
focuses on feature matching without explicit reasoning transfer. Rationale distillation
4
learns from explanations but lacks quality control mechanisms. PDDL-INSTRUCT
27
, closest to our work, uses validation for planning tasks with binary feedback. DocVAL extends this paradigm to continuous spatial domains with detailed error diagnostics, pixel-level corrections, and multi-aspect validation enabling iterative refinement without requiring external grounding at inference.

2.5
Validation in Learning Systems

Quality control in training data affects model performance
16
. Self-consistency
21
and self-refinement
19
enable iterative improvement but lack external validation. Object detection employs IoU metrics
24
for evaluation but not training feedback. DocVAL introduces rule-based validation combining textual accuracy (ANLS), spatial precision (IoU), and reasoning coherence, providing both data filtering and detailed corrective feedback for targeted student improvement. The rule-based design ensures deterministic, precise feedback without LLM hallucination risks.

Dataset Flow and Nomenclature

{}_{\text{test}}

3.2
Phase A: Teacher Data Generation

3.2.1
Text Detection for Validation

Given document image
I\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 3}
and question
q
, we apply text detection to obtain regions
R=\{r_{1},...,r_{N}\}
where
r_{i}=[x_{1}^{i},y_{1}^{i},x_{2}^{i},y_{2}^{i}]
. We use DB-ResNet
14
. These regions serve a
validation-only purpose
: enabling VAL to verify that predicted bboxes target actual text rather than empty space and to provide region-level feedback (e.g., "target Subtotal instead of Total").

3.2.2
Teacher CoT Generation

The teacher VLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro by default) receives
(I,R,q,a_{gt},b_{gt})
and generates structured output with step-by-step spatial reasoning. While the teacher sees regions to produce higher-quality CoT traces during data generation, the student learns to replicate this reasoning directly from visual features without region inputs. Teacher outputs
(CoT_{T},a_{T},b_{T})
where CoT may reference spatial descriptions but these are not required inputs. Complete teacher output examples appear in Appendix
C
.

3.2.3
VAL Filter: Quality-Based Data Curation

For each of 102,447 examples in D
2
, VAL Filter uses detected regions
R
to compute quality score through five modules (detailed in Section
3.3
). Module 1 (OCR Grounding) leverages
R
to verify bbox targets actual text and check semantic correctness (e.g., Total vs Subtotal). This validation-time usage of regions ensures training data quality without requiring the student to depend on detection at inference. Examples with
Q>0.85
are retained, producing 95K high-quality traces (92.7% retention).

3.3
VAL: Validator for Answer Localization

VAL uses detected text regions
R
for validation and feedback generation during training (Phases A and B2). This asymmetric design enables:
(1) precise error diagnostics using region-level semantic feedback,
(2) training the student to perform pure visual spatial reasoning without detection dependencies,
(3) deployment of a simple pure VLM system requiring no auxiliary models at inference.

VAL comprises five specialized modules operating in two modes:
filtering
(Phase A) and
detailed feedback
(Phase B2). Figure
2
illustrates the architectural differences between these two operational modes. VAL uses text detection regions
R
for validation and feedback generation, but these regions are
never provided to the student model
. This asymmetric design enables precise error diagnostics while training the student to perform spatial reasoning independently.

We employ rule-based validation rather than VLM-based validation for critical reasons: deterministic consistency across iterations, precise pixel-level feedback enabling targeted corrections, zero cost enabling fast throughput, objective metrics (ANLS, IoU) that cannot hallucinate, and no validation-of-the-validator infinite regress problem. Complete mathematical formulations appear in Appendix
B.2
.

Figure 2
:
VAL Dual-Mode Architecture Comparison.
VAL operates in two distinct modes sharing the same 5-module architecture but differing in output granularity.
Left (Filter):
Phase A processes teacher outputs (D
2
) at 50 examples/sec, producing binary Accept/Reject decisions to curate 95K high-quality training examples from 102K raw outputs.
Right (Verifier):
Phase B2 processes student outputs (D
4
) at 12 examples/sec during iterative refinement, generating detailed rule-based feedback including specific error messages, pixel-level corrections (
\delta
), priority-ordered fixes, and corrected outputs. Both modes compute identical quality scores (Q) but Filter uses Q only for thresholding while Verifier generates comprehensive diagnostic feedback to guide student learning.

As shown in Figure
2
, both modes execute the same five validation modules but differ fundamentally in their outputs. VAL Filter (left) optimizes for throughput, processing teacher-generated traces from D
2
at 50 examples/sec with binary decisions—accepting examples with quality score Q
>
0.85 and rejecting the rest. In contrast, VAL Verifier (right) sacrifices throughput for diagnostic depth, processing student predictions from D
4
at 12 examples/sec to generate rich, actionable feedback.

Module 1: OCR Grounding Engine.
Uses detected regions to verify predictions:
r_{p}=\arg\max_{r_{i}\in R}\text{IoU}(b_{p},r_{i})
for predicted bbox. Region mismatch (
r_{p}\neq r_{gt}
) generates feedback like "your bbox targets Region #7 (Subtotal) but should target Region #2 (Total)." The student receives this semantic feedback but must learn to locate the correct region visually without being given region coordinates. This check is independent of coordinate accuracy—predictions can be geometrically close but semantically wrong if targeting adjacent text blocks.

Module 2: Answer Validator.
Computes
Q_{\text{ans}}=0.7\cdot\text{ANLS}(a_{p},a_{gt})+0.3\cdot\mathbb{1}[a_{p}\in\text{OCR}(R)]
, ensuring predictions match ground truth textually and correspond to actual document text rather than hallucinations.

Module 3: BBox Validator.
Evaluates
Q_{\text{bbox}}=0.8\cdot\text{IoU}(b_{p},b_{gt})+0.2\cdot\mathbb{1}[r_{p}=r_{gt}]
and computes pixel error vector
\delta=[\Delta x_{1},\Delta y_{1},\Delta x_{2},\Delta y_{2}]^{T}
enabling precise corrections in detailed feedback mode.

Module 4: Reasoning Validator.
Assesses
Q_{\text{reason}}=(S_{\text{struct}}+S_{\text{coord}}+S_{\text{spatial}})/3
through three independent checks: (1)
Structural completeness
verifies required reasoning steps exist, (2)
Coordinate consistency
checks internal coherence between mentioned coordinates and final output, (3)
Spatial consistency
validates accuracy of spatial language against actual bbox position percentages.

Module 5: Feedback Generator.
Adapts output based on mode.
Filtering mode
(Phase A) outputs binary accept/reject based on
Q>0.85
, optimizing throughput for large-scale curation.
Detailed feedback mode
(Phase B2) generates structured error reports with status, specific error descriptions for each module, pixel-level corrections (
\delta
), priority-ordered fixes, and suggested corrected outputs. Example: "Your bbox [760,650,840,680] targets Region #7 (Subtotal) but should target Region #2 (TOTAL) at [510,800,570,830]. Move 250px left, 150px down." Complete feedback examples in Appendix
C
.

3.4
Phase B: Two-Stage Student Training

3.4.1
Stage B1: Supervised CoT Learning

Student (Gemma3-12B) trains on D
3
(76K) with full fine-tuning.
Critical design choice
: While teacher CoT was generated with region awareness, the student receives only
(I,q)
as input and learns to produce the
complete output tuple

(CoT,a,b)
where
CoT
comprises step-by-step spatial reasoning,
a
is the extracted answer text, and
b=[x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2}]
is the predicted bounding box. Training objective:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{SFT}}=-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log P_{\theta}(c_{t}|I,q,c_{<t})

(1)

where
c
represents the tokenized sequence of CoT steps, answer, and bbox coordinates.

Student Output Format.
The student generates a structured output tuple
(CoT,a,b)
where:

•

CoT
: Chain-of-thought reasoning with step-by-step spatial descriptions

•

a
: Predicted answer text (e.g., “$45.99”)

•

b
: Predicted bounding box coordinates
[x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2}]

This complete tuple is what we refer to as the “CoT output” throughout the paper. VAL Verifier validates all three components independently, generating targeted feedback for answer errors, bbox localization errors, and reasoning inconsistencies.

The student must learn to: (1) visually identify text regions and their spatial relationships, (2) generate coherent spatial reasoning in CoT, (3) predict precise bounding boxes—all without explicit region anchors. This trains the VLM to develop internal spatial representations. Training uses AdamW (
lr=2e-4
, batch 32, gradient accumulation 4) for 3 epochs on 2
\times
H100 80GB. Complete hyperparameters in Appendix Table
6
.

3.4.2
Stage B2: Iterative Refinement with VAL Verifier

This Stage employs
validation-driven instruction tuning
: unlike standard fine-tuning on static datasets, we iteratively (1) generate student predictions, (2) validate them with VAL Verifier, (3) obtain detailed corrective instructions, and (4) fine-tune on these dynamically-generated correction examples. This
instruction tuning
terminology emphasizes that the model learns to follow natural language corrections ("move bbox 250px left", "target TOTAL not SUBTOTAL") through gradient-based weight updates on feedback-augmented examples—distinct from prompt tuning which only trains soft embeddings while freezing model weights.

Over up to
\eta_{\max}=20
iterations on D
4
(9.5K) with convergence-based stopping: The student generates predictions
(c_{i}^{(k)},a_{i}^{(k)},b_{i}^{(k)})=M^{(k)}(I_{i},q_{i})

without region inputs
. VAL Verifier uses detected regions
R_{i}
(stored from Phase A) to validate predictions and generate detailed feedback. This asymmetry is intentional: VAL uses
R
to provide precise error diagnostics ("your bbox targets Region #7 (Subtotal) but should target Region #2 (Total)"), but the student learns to correct these errors through visual understanding alone.

3.5
Phase C: Deployment

At inference, given
(I,q)
, the student model generates
(c,a,b)
in a single forward pass (Nvidia A100) with no text detection or external preprocessing required. Teacher and VAL systems are discarded post-training, leaving only the compact 12B student operating locally with zero API costs.

Main Results

Table
1
presents a comprehensive performance comparison across five document understanding benchmarks. DocVAL with Gemma3-12B achieves state-of-the-art results among comparable-sized models: 91.4% ANLS and 82.4% mAP on DocVQA, demonstrating exceptional performance in both textual accuracy and spatial grounding. Our approach consistently outperforms recent VLMs across all datasets, with particularly strong gains in both answer accuracy and spatial localization where we surpass prior work by substantial margins. Since several baselines are closed-source and do not release intermediate localization outputs, their mAP IoU values cannot be computed. We report mAP IoU as the first large-scale metric for evaluating spatial grounding in document VQA.

Compared to models of similar size, DocVAL shows remarkable improvements. The base Gemma3-12B achieves 84.6% ANLS on DocVQA, while our framework elevates this to 91.4% ANLS (+6.8 points) and 82.4% mAP. Against InternVL3.5-14B (84.7% ANLS), we improve by 6.7 ANLS points while providing spatial grounding (82.4% mAP) that prior work does not report. Compared to Llama-3.2-11B-Vision (83.2% ANLS), we achieve 8.2 ANLS point improvement. The consistent cross-dataset improvements—from 73.7% ANLS on VisualMRC to 95.2% on SROIE—demonstrate that validated CoT distillation effectively transfers both semantic understanding and spatial reasoning capabilities to compact student models.

Our compact 4B variant achieves highly competitive performance (88.7% ANLS, 69.1% mAP on DocVQA), substantially outperforming larger 8B models: Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking (80.4% ANLS, +8.3 points) and InternVL3.5-8B (79.9% ANLS, +8.8 points). Even compared to the base Gemma3-4B (78.6% ANLS), DocVAL adds 10.1 ANLS points, demonstrating the efficiency of our distillation approach. This enables smaller models to substantially exceed larger baselines through quality-controlled reasoning transfer and iterative refinement, making high-performance document understanding accessible on resource-constrained devices.

5.2
Ablation Studies

5.2.1
Text Detection Analysis

Table
2
analyzes text detection’s role during teacher data generation and VAL validation. Removing detection from Phase A (teacher generation and VAL filtering) significantly degrades performance: DocVQA mAP drops from 82.4 to 74.1 (-8.3), confirming that detection-guided teacher CoT and region-based VAL feedback are critical for training data quality. However, the student operates without detection at inference, demonstrating that quality supervision enables pure VLM spatial reasoning.

Among detection methods, DB-ResNet achieves best overall performance (82.4 mAP on DocVQA) due to superior recall for small text and processing efficiency. CRAFT (80.6 mAP) and PSENet (79.3 mAP) show slightly lower performance, while EasyOCR (78.7 mAP) struggles with dense text layouts. Removing region references from teacher CoT while keeping detection for VAL (76.8 mAP) shows that region-aware reasoning generation is valuable beyond validation alone.

5.3
Discussion

5.3.1
The Role of Validated Reasoning Transfer

DocVAL’s effectiveness stems from transferring not just answers but complete spatial reasoning processes with quality assurance. Traditional distillation matching output distributions or intermediate features fails to capture the logical steps underlying bbox prediction. Our approach makes reasoning explicit through CoT traces, then ensures quality through multi-aspect validation before training. The 17.0 mAP gap between CoT-based training and direct supervision (Table
3
, last row) quantifies the value of explicit reasoning transfer for spatial tasks.

The dual-mode VAL architecture serves complementary roles: Phase A filtering removes 7.3% of teacher outputs containing coordinate hallucinations and reasoning inconsistencies, preventing error propagation during initial training. Phase B2 verification provides targeted feedback enabling iterative improvement—the 6.3 mAP gain from detailed feedback over binary filtering (Table
4
) demonstrates that actionable corrections outperform simple quality gates.

5.3.2
Text Detection as Training-Time Scaffolding

Our design philosophy treats text detection as temporary scaffolding for training, not a permanent dependency. Detection serves two critical but training-limited roles: (1) guiding teacher generation toward region-grounded reasoning, producing higher-quality CoT traces, and (2) enabling VAL’s semantic feedback (e.g., "targets Subtotal instead of Total") through region verification. The 8.3 mAP drop without detection in Phase A (Table
2
) confirms these roles’ importance.

5.3.3
Model Scale and Spatial Reasoning Capacity

The 13.3 mAP gap between 12B and 4B models (82.4 vs. 69.1, Table
1
) quantifies how spatial reasoning benefits from additional capacity when no external grounding is provided. Pure visual bbox prediction requires: (1) visual feature extraction localizing text regions, (2) spatial relationship understanding (above/below, left/right), (3) coordinate regression mapping visual locations to pixel coordinates. Larger models handle this multi-step process more effectively, demonstrating that capacity matters for complex spatial tasks.

However, 4B models remain highly competitive—substantially outperforming even larger 8B baselines (Qwen3-VL-8B: 80.4% ANLS, InternVL3.5-8B: 79.9% ANLS) through validated distillation. This suggests that high-quality supervision from reasoning-focused teachers can compensate for reduced capacity. The base Gemma3-4B achieves only 78.6% ANLS, but DocVAL elevates this to 88.7% (+10.1 points), demonstrating the framework’s effectiveness at democratizing document understanding capabilities across model scales.

5.3.4
Broader Implications for VLM Spatial Reasoning

DocVAL demonstrates that VLMs can learn precise spatial grounding through quality-controlled reasoning distillation. This challenges assumptions that document understanding requires specialized architectures with explicit layout encoders or OCR pipelines. Pure VLMs with appropriate training can develop internal spatial representations sufficient for pixel-accurate localization.

The validated distillation paradigm generalizes beyond documents: any domain requiring spatial reasoning (visual question answering, referring expressions, robotic manipulation) could benefit from reasoning-focused teachers, multi-aspect validation, and iterative refinement. The key insight—that explicit reasoning transfer with quality assurance outperforms implicit feature matching—applies broadly to capability distillation from large to compact models.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced DocVAL, a validated CoT distillation framework that enables compact vision-language models to perform precise spatial grounding in document understanding tasks. Through dual-mode validation, asymmetric training design, and adaptive iterative refinement, we demonstrate that pure VLMs can achieve strong document spatial grounding when trained with quality-controlled reasoning transfer. The framework’s design philosophy—treating text detection as temporary training scaffolding rather than permanent infrastructure—enables deployment of simpler, more robust VLM systems. The rule-based VAL architecture ensures deterministic, precise feedback without the hallucination risks of VLM-based validators. By demonstrating that explicit reasoning transfer with multi-aspect quality assurance outperforms implicit feature matching, DocVAL provides a generalizable template for distilling sophisticated spatial reasoning capabilities from large teachers to compact, deployable students.

Appendix A
Terminology and Design Decisions

CoT Output.
Throughout the paper, "CoT Output" refers to the complete tuple
(CoT,a,b)
where
CoT
is step-by-step spatial reasoning,
a
is the extracted answer, and
b
is the predicted bounding box. VAL validates all three components.

Instruction Tuning.
Stage B2 employs instruction tuning which updates model weights through gradient descent on feedback-augmented examples (student mistakes + VAL feedback → corrections). This differs from prompt tuning which freezes weights and only trains soft embeddings. Spatial reasoning requires modifying visual encoders and coordinate regression heads, necessitating full weight updates.

Validation-Driven Learning.
Stage B2 implements an iterative loop: predict → validate → feedback → retrain. VAL actively guides improvement through actionable corrections rather than passive scoring, creating dynamic training datasets that evolve with model capabilities.

Text Region.
A detected text instance
r_{i}=(b_{i},t_{i},i)
where

b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
are bounding box coordinates,
t_{i}
is OCR-extracted
text, and
i
is the region index. Text detection (DB-ResNet) extracts region
set
R=\{r_{1},...,r_{n}\}
with
n\approx 15\text{-}20
per document.
Regions enable VAL’s semantic validation ("Region #7 Subtotal vs Region #2
Total") but are never given to students, who must learn text localization
through pure visual reasoning.

Detection as Scaffolding.
Text detection serves training-time only purposes: (1) guiding teacher CoT generation, (2) enabling VAL’s semantic validation. Students receive only
(I,q)
and must develop internal spatial representations. This asymmetry enables quality supervision while maintaining deployment simplicity.

Design Rationale.
We use rule-based VAL for: (1) deterministic consistency across iterations, (2) precise pixel-level feedback, (3) zero computational cost, (4) objective metrics that cannot hallucinate, and (5) avoiding validation-of-validator recursion. Iterative refinement enables progressive error correction through feedback targeting actual mistakes. Full fine-tuning rather than LoRA is necessary as spatial reasoning requires modifying core visual encoders (preliminary experiments showed 8-12 mAP degradation with parameter-efficient methods).

Appendix D
Qualitative Examples

Success: Complex Receipt.
Multiple similar amounts (Subtotal: $87.50, Tax: $7.88, Total: $108.51). Student correctly distinguished "TOTAL" field, predicted [542,876,618,904]. Result: ANLS=1.0, IoU=1.0 (perfect). Demonstrates semantic understanding and pixel-accurate localization.

Failure: Multi-Line Address.
Three-line address spanning disconnected regions. Student correctly extracted full text (ANLS=1.0) but bbox covered only first line (IoU=0.31). Single-bbox architectural limitation, not reasoning failure.

Failure: Handwritten Text.
Student read handwriting correctly (ANLS=1.0) but bbox imprecise (IoU=0.68 vs. typical >0.9 for print). VAL validation during training missed handwritten regions, affecting feedback quality.

Near-Perfect: Structured Form.
Tax form with clear grid layout. Student identified "EIN" label and predicted [384,187,496,214] vs. GT [385,188,495,213]. ANLS=1.0, IoU=0.98 (1-2px offset). Represents typical performance on well-structured documents where DocVAL excels.

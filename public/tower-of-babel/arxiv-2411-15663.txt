A note on the growth of Sha in dihedral extensions
Jamie Bell

Abstract
. We provide a formula for the order of the Tate–Shafarevich group of elliptic curves over dihedral extensions of number fields of order 2 ​ n 2n , up to 4 t ​ h 4^{th} powers and primes dividing n n . Specifically, for odd n n it is equal to the order of the Tate–Shafarevich group over the quadratic subextension. A similar formula holds for even n n .

Introduction

Computing the Tate–Shafarevich group of an elliptic curve is a difficult problem. It is conjectured to be finite, though this is not known for curves of analytic rank greater than 1. The main result about its size is due to Cassels, who proved that if it is finite, it must have square order
[
6
]
. This has some applications, for example to the parity conjecture.

In this paper, we show that under some circumstances we can determine
\Sha

4^{th}

\Sha

Definition 5

Brauer relations induce isogenies between products of Weil restrictions of elliptic curves (

Lemma 6
.

In
D_{2n}
, there are Brauer relations

•

1+2D_{2n}-2C_{2}-C_{n}
when

is odd, and

•

1+2D_{2n}-2C_{2}-C_{n}+D_{n}-D_{n}^{\prime}

Where, if
D_{2n}=\langle r,s|r^{n}=s^{2}=1,srs=r^{-1}\rangle
, we let
C_{2}=\langle s\rangle
,
C_{n}=\langle r\rangle
,
D_{n}=\langle r^{2},s\rangle
and
D_{n}^{\prime}=\langle r^{2},sr\rangle

Proof.

Note that
\mathbb{C}[G/H]\cong\mathrm{Ind}^{G}_{H}\mathbf{1}
. For
H=1
, we get the regular representation, and for
H=G
we get the trivial representation.

In the odd case, the irreducible representations are the trivial representation
\mathbf{1}
, the sign representation
\epsilon
, and
\frac{n-1}{2}
two-dimensional representations.

We compute
\mathrm{Ind}^{D_{2n}}_{C_{2}}\mathbf{1}
using Frobenius reciprocity. For each irreducible representation
\phi

D_{2n}
, we have
\langle\mathrm{Ind}^{D_{2n}}_{C_{2}}\mathbf{1},\phi\rangle=\langle\mathbf{1},\mathrm{Res}^{D_{2n}}_{C_{2}}\phi\rangle
. We find that the trivial representation appears in
\mathrm{Ind}^{D_{2n}}_{C_{2}}\mathbf{1}
once, the sign representation does not appear, and each two-dimensional representation appears once. Similarly we find that
\mathrm{Ind}^{D_{2n}}_{C_{2}}\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}\oplus\epsilon
. We conclude that
\mathbb{C}[D_{2n}/1]\oplus\mathbb{C}[D_{2n}/D_{2n}]^{\oplus 2}\cong\mathbb{C}[D_{2n}/C_{n}]\oplus\mathbb{C}[D_{2n}/C_{2}]^{\oplus 2}

Now as these representations are realisable over
\mathbb{Q}
and isomorphic over

, they are isomorphic over
\mathbb{Q}
(
[
8
]

The proof for the even case proceeds similarly. We now have three non-trivial one-dimensional representations,
\epsilon_{1}
,
\epsilon_{2}
and
\epsilon_{3}

We find the induced representation from

Proof of Theorem

In the case where

Galois Module Structure

In some cases, the same result follows from the work of Chetty, which also gives information about the Galois module structure of
\Sha
. Let
n,p
and
H\cong C_{n}
be as in Theorem
4
, and assume that
\Sha(E/F)[p^{\infty}]
is finite.

Lemma 9
.

\mathbb{Z}_{p}[C_{n}]
is a direct sum of local rings with principal maximal ideals.

Proof.

\mathbb{Z}_{p}[C_{n}]
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[T]/(T^{n}-1)
. This is a direct sum of rings
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[T]/\Phi_{d}(T)
for cyclotomic polynomials
\Phi_{d}
with
d|n
. These further split into direct sums because
\Phi_{d}=\prod P_{d,i}
, a product of irreducible polynomials over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
of degree
\mathrm{ord}_{d}(p)
. Finally
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[T]/P_{d,i}(T)
is the ring of integers of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[T]/P_{d,i}(T)
(
[
9
]
, Ch. IV, §4, Prop. 16) so has principal maximal ideal.
∎

4
)

Proof.

Apply Lemma 2.8 from

Note that

Now by Lemma

Suppose that

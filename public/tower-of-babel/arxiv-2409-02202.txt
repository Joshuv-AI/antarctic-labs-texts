The growth of Tate–Shafarevich groups of p p -supersingular elliptic curves over anticyclotomic ℤ p {\mathbb{Z}}_{p} -extensions at inert primes
Erman IŞIK and Antonio Lei

Abstract
. Let E E be an elliptic curve defined over ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} , and let K K be an imaginary quadratic field. Consider an odd prime p p at which E E has good supersingular reduction with a p ​ ( E ) = 0 a_{p}(E)=0 and which is inert in K K . Under the assumption that the signed Selmer groups are cotorsion modules over the corresponding Iwasawa algebra, we prove that the Mordell–Weil ranks of E E are bounded over any subextensions of the anticyclotomic ℤ p {\mathbb{Z}}_{p} -extension of K K . 1.
Introduction

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and
p
be an odd prime of good reduction. Let
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\rm cyc}
denote the cyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
, and
{\mathbb{Q}}_{(n)}
be the unique subextension of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\rm cyc}
of degree
p^{n}
for
n\geq 0
. Suppose that the
p
-primary component of the Tate–Shafarevich group
\Sha\left(E/{\mathbb{Q}}_{(n)}\right)
is finite for all
n
, and let
p^{e_{n}}=\big|\Sha(E/K_{n})[p^{\infty}]\big|
. The variation of
e_{n}
in
n
depends on whether the elliptic curve
E
has ordinary or supersingular reduction at
p
.

When
E
has ordinary reduction at
p
, it follows from
[
Kat04
, Theorem 17.4]
that the
p
-primary Selmer group of
E
over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\rm cyc}
is cotorsion over the associated Iwasawa algebra. It then follows from Mazur’s control theorem
[
Maz72
]
that for
n\gg 0
,

e_{n}-e_{n-1}=\lambda+(p^{n}-p^{n-1})\mu-r_{\infty},

where
r_{\infty}
is the rank of
E
over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\rm cyc}
(which is finite by
[
Kat04
,
Roh84
]
), and
\lambda
and
\mu
are the Iwasawa invariants of the Pontryagin dual of the
p
-primary Selmer group of
E
over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\rm cyc}
.

When
E
has supersingular reduction at
p
, the case where
a_{p}(E)=0
has been studied by Kurihara
[
Kur02
]
, Kobayashi
[
Kob03
]
and Pollack
[
Pol05
]
. For
n\gg 0
, we have the following formula:

e_{n}-e_{n-1}=\deg{\widetilde{\omega}}_{n}^{\mp}+\lambda_{\pm}+\mu_{\pm}(p^{n}-p^{n-1})-r_{\infty},

where
{\widetilde{\omega}}_{n}^{\mp}
is the product of certain cyclotomic polynomials,
r_{\infty}
is the rank of
E
over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\rm cyc}
,
\lambda_{\pm}
and
\mu_{\pm}
are the Iwasawa invariants of the cotorsion signed Selmer groups of
E
over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\rm cyc}
, and the sign
\pm
depends on the parity of
n
.

In
[
Spr13
,
Spr17
]
, Sprung obtained an analogous formula for the
p
-supersingular elliptic curves with
a_{p}(E)\neq 0
and abelian varieties of GL(2)-type. See also
[
LLZ17
]
for related work on upper bounds on the growth of the Bloch–Kato–Shafarevich–Tate groups for higher-weight modular forms. Furthermore, the growth of Mordell–Weil ranks and the Tate–Shafarevich groups of an elliptic curve over the cyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension of certain number fields have been studied in
[
LL22
]
.

The focus of this article is analogous results for the anticyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension of an imaginary quadratic field
K
. Suppose that
p
splits in
K
and the two primes above
p
splits completely in the anticyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension of
K
.
Under certain hypotheses on the vanishing of the Mordell–Weil rank and the
p
-part of the Tate–Shafarevich group of
E
over
K
, Iovita and Pollack
[
IP06
, Theorem 5.1]
derived a formula of the growth of the Tate–Shafarevich groups over anticyclotomic tower that mimics the cyclotomic case. Their hypotheses imply that both the plus and minus Selmer groups have trivial Iwasawa invariants, meaning that the growth of the Tate–Shafarevich groups is minimal. Their method can potentially be extended to the case where
p
is inert in
K
under similar stringent hypotheses. We avoid such hypotheses in this article. Instead, we develop a method that allows the plus and minus Selmer groups to have non-trivial Iwasawa invariants.

If we assume in addition that
p\geq 5
and that
(E,K)
satisfies the Heegner hypothesis, Çiperiani proved in
[
Ç09
]
that the Tate–Shafarevich group of
E
has trivial corank over the Iwasawa algebra associated with the anticyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension of
K
. In
[
Mat21
,
LLM23
]
, a more precise formula of the growth of the Tate–Shafarevich groups inside this tower is given. More recently, Burungale–Kobayashi–Ota and Müller
[
BKO25
,
Mül25
]
studied this question when
E
has complex multiplication by an order in
K
. The methods in these works are specifically developed for the setting where at least one of the plus and minus Selmer groups is not cotorsion. The main goal of the present article is to study the growth of the Tate–Shafarevich groups when both Selmer groups are cotorsion, for which the methods in the aforementioned works do not extend directly.

1.1.
Statement of the main theorem

Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field such that
p
is
inert
in
K
. Assume that
p
does not divide the class number
h_{K}
of
K
and
a_{p}(E)=0
. Let
K_{\infty}
denote the anticyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension of
K
. Note that the unique prime of
K
above
p
is totally ramified in
K_{\infty}
. For an integer
n\geq 0
, we write
K_{n}
for the unique subextension of
K_{\infty}
such that
[K_{n}:K]=p^{n}
.

The Galois group of
K_{\infty}
over
K
is denoted by
\Gamma
. In addition, write
\Gamma_{n}={\rm Gal}(K_{\infty}/K_{n})
and
G_{n}={\rm Gal}(K_{n}/K)
. We fix once and for all a topological generator
\gamma
of
\Gamma
. Let
\Lambda
denote the Iwasawa algebra
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[[\Gamma]]=\displaystyle\varprojlim_{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[G_{n}]
, which we shall identify with the power series ring
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[[X]]
by sending
\gamma-1
to
X
. Let
(-)^{\vee}:={\rm Hom}(-,{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
denote the Pontryagin duality functor.

In this article, we prove that the Mordell–Weil rank of
E
over
K_{\infty}
is bounded assuming that the signed Selmer groups
{\rm Sel}^{\pm}(E/K_{\infty})
are
\Lambda
-cotorsion. Furthermore, we derive an asymptotic formula for the growth of the
p
-parts of the Tate–Shafarevich groups
\Sha(E/K_{n})
of
E
in terms of the Iwasawa invariants of
{\rm Sel}^{\pm}(E/K_{\infty})^{\vee}
. These Selmer groups have recently been studied in
[
BBL24
]
, and are similar to those defined by Rubin in
[
Rub87
]
for CM elliptic curves (see Definition
2.15
for the precise definition and Remark
2.3
for a discussion on how the plus and minus subgroups used to define these groups compare with the counterparts used to define Kobayashi’s plus and minus Selmer groups in
[
Kob03
]
). The main result of this article is the following theorem.

Theorem A
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and
p
an odd prime where
E
has good supersingular reduction with
a_{p}(E)=0
.
Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field such that
p
is inert in
K
. Assume that
p
does not divide the class number
h_{K}
. Let
K_{\infty}
denote the anticyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension of
K
.
Assume that
{\rm Sel}^{\pm}(E/K_{\infty})^{\vee}
are both
\Lambda
-torsion. Then, we have:

(i)

{\rm rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}E(K_{n})
is bounded independently of
n
;

(ii)

Assume that
\Sha(E/K_{n})[p^{\infty}]
is finite for all
n\geq 0
. Let
p^{e_{n}}=\big|\Sha(E/K_{n})[p^{\infty}]\big|
. Define
\displaystyle r_{\infty}=\sup_{n\geq 0}\left\{{\rm rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}E(K_{n})\right\}
. Let
\lambda_{\pm}
and
\mu_{\pm}
be the Iwasawa invariants of
{\rm Sel}^{\pm}(E/K_{\infty})^{\vee}
. Then, for
n\gg 0
,

e_{n}-e_{n-1}=\begin{cases}2s_{n-1}+\lambda_{-}+\phi(p^{n})\mu_{-}-r_{\infty}&\;\;\;\text{if }n\text{ is odd,}\\
2s_{n-1}+\lambda_{+}+\phi(p^{n})\mu_{+}-r_{\infty}&\;\;\;\text{if }n\text{ is even,}\end{cases}

where
\phi(p^{n})=p^{n}-p^{n-1}
, and
s_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}p^{k}
for
n\geq 0
.

Remark 1.1
.

Under certain hypotheses, it is known that
{\rm Sel}^{\pm}(E/K_{\infty})^{\vee}
are
\Lambda
-torsion; see
[
BLV23
,
BBL24
]
and Remark
2.17
for a more detailed discussion. Our result is complementary to the recent works of
[
BKO25
,
Mül25
]
, where
E
is assumed to have complex multiplication by an order in
K
. In their setting, there exists exactly one element
\bullet\in\{+,-\}
(the choice of which depends on the root number of
E
) such that
{\rm Sel}^{\bullet}(E/K_{\infty})^{\vee}
is
\Lambda
-torsion. Furthermore,
{\rm rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}E(K_{n})
is unbounded as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

1.2.
Organization

In Section
2
, we review several plus and minus objects. We first review a system of local points introduced in
[
BKO24
, §2]
, which is used to construct signed (or plus and minus) Coleman maps following
[
Kob03
]
. These maps are then utilized to define the signed Selmer groups that appear in the statement of Theorem
A
. After analyzing the images of these Coleman maps, we review results on certain global cohomology groups under the assumption that the signed Selmer groups are cotorsion. We review the definition of Kobayashi ranks for projective systems of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules in Section
3
. We derive a formula for the Kobayashi ranks of modules arising from specific
2\times 2
matrices defined over
\Lambda
, which is central to our proof of Theorem
A
and is the principal novelty of this article. In Section
4
, we establish a connection between Coleman maps and Kobayashi ranks, and show how this relationship enables us to study the growth of certain local modules. We conclude by combining these results to prove Theorem
A
.

1.3.
Outlook

Our proof of Theorem
A
follows closely the ideas of
[
Kob03
, §10]
. In
loc. cit.
, the author presented a detailed study of the growth of modules of the form
\left(\Lambda/\langle f\rangle\right)_{\Gamma_{n}}
, where
f
is a non-zero element of
\Lambda
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
. In the context of the current article, several Iwasawa modules have rank 2, and we are led to study the growth of modules of the form
\left(\Lambda^{\oplus 2}/\langle u_{1},u_{2}\rangle\right)_{\Gamma_{n}}
, where
u_{1},u_{2}
are linearly independent elements of
\Lambda^{\oplus 2}
. It should be possible to extend our results to modules arising from
\Lambda^{\oplus d}
,
d\geq 2
. This would potentially allow us to remove some of the hypotheses imposed in
[
LL22
]
to study elliptic curves defined over more general number fields.

The hypothesis
p\nmid h_{K}
ensures that there is only one prime lying above
p
inside the anticyclotomic tower. It should be possible to extend some of the results of the present article without this hypothesis, though not necessarily in a straightforward manner. One would have to construct local Coleman maps for each prime lying above
p
and consider the image of the direct sum of these maps.

In a different vein, it would be interesting to study similar results in the context of the present article without assuming
a_{p}(E)=0
. This would require an appropriate extension of the results of
[
BKO21
,
BKO24
]
on local points.

Finally, we remark that a unified approach to study anticyclotomic Iwasawa main conjectures has been introduced in
[
BLV23
]
that treats the
p
-split and
p
-inert cases simultaneously. The method introduced in this article is specifically tailored for the
p
-inert case. For example, we crucially make use of the
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
-module structure of the local points attached to the formal group of
E
and carry out explicit calculations specifically for rank-two modules over
\Lambda
. It can be very interesting to develop an alternative approach in line with
[
BLV23
]
that would apply to the
p
-split and
p
-inert cases uniformly.

3.1.
Definition and basic properties

Following
[

, §10]
, we define the Kobayashi ranks as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(M_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a projective system of finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-modules. Given an integer
n\geq 1
, if
\pi_{n}:M_{n}\longrightarrow M_{n-1}
has finite kernel and cokernel, we define

\nabla M_{n}:={\rm length}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}(\ker\pi_{n})-{\rm length}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}({\rm coker}\;\pi_{n})+\dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}M_{n-1}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(M^{\prime}_{n})_{n\geq 1}

(M_{n})_{n\geq 1}

(M^{\prime\prime}_{n})_{n\geq 1}

-modules.

Suppose that for all
n\geq 1
, there is an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow(M^{\prime}_{n})\longrightarrow(M_{n})\longrightarrow(M^{\prime\prime}_{n})\longrightarrow 0.

\nabla M_{n},\nabla M^{\prime}_{n},\nabla M^{\prime\prime}_{n}
are defined, then the other is also defined, in which case

\nabla M_{n}=\nabla M^{\prime}_{n}+\nabla M^{\prime\prime}_{n}.

(ii)

Suppose that
M_{n}
are constant. If
M_{n}
is finite or the transition map
M_{n}\longrightarrow M_{n-1}
is given by the multiplication map by
p
, then
\nabla M_{n}=0
.

Proof.

See
[
Kob03
, Lemma 10.4]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

3.2.

We derive a formula for the Kobayashi ranks of modules arising from certain types of
2\times 2

\Lambda
. We begin with the following definition.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
A=\begin{bmatrix}a&c\\
b&d\end{bmatrix}\in M_{2\times 2}(\Lambda)
.

(i)

We write
\langle A\rangle\subseteq\Lambda^{\oplus 2}
for the
\Lambda
-module generated by the columns of
A
. Similarly, given two such matrices
A
and
B
, we write
\langle A,B\rangle\subseteq\Lambda^{\oplus 2}
for the
\Lambda
-module generated by the columns of
A
and
B
.

(ii)

For
n\geq 0
, let
\Lambda_{n}=\Lambda/(\omega_{n})
and write
\langle A\rangle_{n}
for the
\Lambda_{n}
-module generated by the columns of
A
modulo
\omega_{n}
inside
\Lambda^{\oplus 2}_{n}
. Furthermore, we write
A_{(n)}=\Lambda^{\oplus 2}_{n}/\langle A\rangle_{n}=\Lambda^{\oplus 2}/\langle\omega_{n}I_{2},A\rangle
, where
I_{2}
denotes the
2\times 2
identity matrix.

(iii)

We say that
A
is
special
relative to an integer
n
if it satisfies the following property:

\Phi_{m}\mid\det(A),\;0\leq m\leq n\;\Rightarrow\;\Phi_{m}\mid a,b\text{ or }\Phi_{m}\mid c,d.

The following lemma is a consequence of the Chinese remainder theorem.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer.

(i)

For all
A\in M_{2\times 2}(\Lambda)
, we have

A_{(n)}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\simeq\bigoplus_{0\leq m\leq n}{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\zeta_{p^{m}})^{\oplus 2}\big/\langle A(\epsilon_{m})\rangle,

where
\langle A(\epsilon_{m})\rangle
denotes the image of the
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\zeta_{p^{m}})
-linear endomorphism on
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\zeta_{p^{m}})^{\oplus 2}
defined by the matrix
A(\epsilon_{m})
.

(ii)

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a finite set of non-negative integers. For each
i\in\mathcal{S}
, let
x_{i}\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\zeta_{p^{i}})
be any element. There exists a polynomial
F\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[X]
such that
F(\epsilon_{i})=x_{i}
for all
i\in\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

Let
R=\Lambda\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
and
M=(\Lambda^{\oplus 2}/\langle A\rangle)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. Then, the Chinese remainder theorem for
R
-modules says that

M/\omega_{n}M\simeq\bigoplus_{m=0}^{n}M/\Phi_{m}M.

It is clear from the definition that
M/\omega_{n}M\simeq A_{(n)}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. Furthermore, the evaluation map
X\mapsto\epsilon_{m}
induces the isomorphism
M/\Phi_{m}M\simeq{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\zeta_{p^{m}})^{\oplus 2}\big/\langle A(\epsilon_{m})\rangle
. Hence, assertion (i) follows.

Assertion (ii) follows from the isomorphism

{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[X]\left/\left(\prod_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\omega_{i}\right)\right.\simeq\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal{S}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[X]/(\Phi_{i})\simeq\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal{S}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\zeta_{p^{i}}),

which once again follows from the Chinese remainder theorem.
∎

We prove the following generalization of
[
Kob03
, Lemma 10.5i)]
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
n\geq 0
be an integer. If
A\in M_{2\times 2}(\Lambda)
is special relative to
n
and
\Phi_{n}\nmid\det(A)
, then
\nabla A_{(n)}
is defined and is equal to
{\rm ord}_{\epsilon_{n}}\left(\det A(\epsilon_{n})\right)
. Here, the connecting map
\pi_{n}:A_{(n)}\rightarrow A_{(n-1)}
is the natural projection.

Proof.

For each integer
m
such that
0\leq m\leq n-1
, we write
i_{m}\in\{0,1,2\}
for the number of columns of
A
that are divisible by
\Phi_{m}
. Then,
A
can be uniquely written as

A=BD,

where
B,D\in M_{2\times 2}(\Lambda)
such that
D
is a diagonal matrix whose diagonal entries are square-free products of elements of a subset
\{\Phi_{m}:0\leq m\leq n-1\}
, and the number of times
\Phi_{m}
appears in
\det D
is precisely
i_{m}
.

Lemma
3.5
(i) gives

A_{(n-1)}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\simeq\bigoplus_{0\leq m\leq n-1}{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\zeta_{p^{m}})^{\oplus 2}\big/\langle A(\epsilon_{m})\rangle.

The rank of the matrix
A(\epsilon_{m})
is equal to
2-i_{m}
as
A
is special relative to
n
. Therefore, the rank-nullity theorem tells us that

\dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\zeta_{p^{m}})}{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\zeta_{p^{m}})^{\oplus 2}\big/\langle A(\epsilon_{m})\rangle=i_{m}.

Hence,

\dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}A_{(n-1)}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}=\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\phi(p^{m})i_{m}={\rm ord}_{\epsilon_{n}}\left(\det D(\epsilon_{n})\right).

As the connecting map
\pi_{n}
is surjective, it remains to show that the length of
\ker\pi_{n}
is given by
{\rm ord}_{\epsilon_{n}}\left(\det B(\epsilon_{n})\right)
. The isomorphism theorem tells us that

(3.1)

\ker\pi_{n}\simeq\frac{\langle\omega_{n-1}I_{2},A\rangle}{\langle\omega_{n}I_{2},A\rangle}\simeq\frac{\langle\omega_{n-1}I_{2}\rangle}{\langle\omega_{n}I_{2},A\rangle\bigcap\langle\omega_{n-1}I_{2}\rangle}.

Claim:

\langle\omega_{n}I_{2},A\rangle\bigcap\langle\omega_{n-1}I_{2}\rangle=\langle\omega_{n}I_{2},\omega_{n-1}B\rangle
.

It is clear that
\langle\omega_{n}I_{2},\omega_{n-1}B\rangle\subseteq\langle\omega_{n-1}I_{2}\rangle
.
Since the diagonal entries of the diagonal matrix
D
are square-free products of elements of a subset of
\{\Phi_{m}:0\leq m\leq n-1\}
, we may write
\omega_{n-1}I_{2}=DD^{\prime}
for some diagonal matrix
D^{\prime}
.

Thus,

\omega_{n-1}B=BDD^{\prime}=AD^{\prime},

which implies that
\langle\omega_{n-1}B\rangle\subseteq\langle A\rangle
. Therefore, we deduce that

\langle\omega_{n}I_{2},\omega_{n-1}B\rangle\subseteq\langle\omega_{n}I_{2},A\rangle\bigcap\langle\omega_{n-1}I_{2}\rangle.

We now prove the opposite inclusion. Let
v\in\langle\omega_{n}I_{2},A\rangle\bigcap\langle\omega_{n-1}I_{2}\rangle
. We can regard
v
as a column vector with entries in
\Lambda
and write

(3.2)

v=A\begin{bmatrix}x\\
y\end{bmatrix}+\omega_{n}u,

where
x,y\in\Lambda
and
u\in\Lambda^{\oplus 2}
. Since
v\in\langle\omega_{n-1}I_{2}\rangle
, we have

(3.3)

A\begin{bmatrix}x\\
y\end{bmatrix}=BD\begin{bmatrix}x\\
y\end{bmatrix}\equiv 0\mod\Phi_{m},\quad 0\leq m\leq n-1.

We consider three cases.

Case 1:
If
i_{m}=0
,
A(\epsilon_{m})
is an invertible matrix as it is special relative to
n
. In this case, (
3.3
) implies that
x,y\equiv 0\mod\Phi_{m}
.

Case 2:
If
i_{m}=1
, there exists a non-zero element
\begin{bmatrix}a\\
b\end{bmatrix}\in\Lambda^{\oplus 2}
such that

(3.4)

A\equiv\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
b&0\end{bmatrix}\mod\Phi_{m}\quad\text{or}\quad A\equiv\begin{bmatrix}0&a\\
0&b\end{bmatrix}\mod\Phi_{m}

Let’s suppose the first congruence relation holds. Then (
3.3
) implies that
\Phi_{m}\mid x
. Furthermore,
D\equiv\begin{bmatrix}d_{m}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\mod\Phi_{m}
for some non-zero
d_{m}\in\Lambda
. Therefore,
D\begin{bmatrix}x\\
y\end{bmatrix}\equiv 0\mod\Phi_{m}
. If the second congruence relation in (
3.4
) holds, the same argument shows that
D\begin{bmatrix}x\\
y\end{bmatrix}\equiv 0\mod\Phi_{m}
.

Case 3:
If
i_{m}=2
, then
D\equiv 0\mod\Phi_{m}
. In particular,
D\begin{bmatrix}x\\
y\end{bmatrix}\equiv 0\mod\Phi_{m}
.

In all three cases, we have
D\begin{bmatrix}x\\
y\end{bmatrix}\equiv 0\mod\Phi_{m}
. Therefore, we deduce that

D\begin{bmatrix}x\\
y\end{bmatrix}=\omega_{n-1}\begin{bmatrix}x^{\prime}\\
y^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}

for some
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\Lambda
. Combined with (
3.2
), we have

v=\omega_{n-1}B\begin{bmatrix}x^{\prime}\\
y^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}+\omega_{n}u\in\langle\omega_{n}I_{2},\omega_{n-1}B\rangle.

This shows that
\langle\omega_{n}I_{2},A\rangle\bigcap\langle\omega_{n-1}I_{2}\rangle\subseteq\langle\omega_{n}I_{2},\omega_{n-1}B\rangle
, and our claim follows.

We can now rewrite (
3.1
) as

\ker\pi_{n}\simeq\frac{\langle\omega_{n-1}I_{2}\rangle}{\langle\omega_{n}I_{2},\omega_{n-1}B\rangle}\simeq\frac{\Lambda^{\oplus 2}}{\langle\Phi_{n}I_{2},B\rangle},

where the last isomorphism is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda^{\oplus 2}}{\langle\Phi_{n}I_{2},B\rangle}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\frac{\langle\omega_{n-1}I_{2}\rangle}{\langle\omega_{n}I_{2},\omega_{n-1}B\rangle}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto\omega_{n-1}x.

As
\Phi_{n}\nmid\det A
by assumption, it follows from
[

, Lemma 7.8]
that

4.6
that the map

\displaystyle H^{1}_{\rm Iw}(K_{\infty},T)_{\Gamma_{n}}\longrightarrow\frac{H^{1}(k_{n},T)}{H^{1}_{+}(k_{n},T)}\oplus\frac{H^{1}(k_{n},T)}{H^{1}_{-}(k_{n},T)}

is injective for
n\gg 0
. For such
n
, there is a short exact sequence

(4.4)

0\longrightarrow\frac{H^{1}_{\rm Iw}(K_{\infty},T)_{\Gamma_{n}}}{{\rm pr}_{n}\langle u_{1},u_{2}\rangle}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}^{\prime\prime\prime}(E/K_{n})\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}^{\prime\prime}(E/K_{n})\longrightarrow 0.

Let
B\in M_{2\times 2}(\Lambda)
be the
2\times 2
matrix given by the equation

\begin{bmatrix}u_{1}&u_{2}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}z_{1}&z_{2}\end{bmatrix}B

as in the proof of Proposition
4.7
. Recall that
H^{1}_{\rm Iw}(K_{\infty},T)\simeq\Lambda^{\oplus 2}
(see Proposition
2.19
(i)) and
B
is chosen so that
\det(B)
is coprime to
\omega_{n}
for all
n
. Lemma
4.10
implies that

{\rm pr}_{n}\langle u_{1},u_{2}\rangle\simeq\frac{\langle B\rangle+\omega_{n}\Lambda^{\oplus 2}}{\omega_{n}\Lambda^{\oplus 2}}\simeq\frac{\langle B\rangle}{\langle B\rangle\cap\omega_{n}\Lambda^{\oplus 2}}=\frac{\langle B\rangle}{\omega_{n}\langle B\rangle}\simeq\langle u_{1},u_{2}\rangle_{\Gamma_{n}},

and

\left(\frac{H^{1}_{\rm Iw}(K_{\infty},T)}{\langle u_{1},u_{2}\rangle}\right)^{\Gamma_{n}}\simeq\left(\frac{\Lambda^{\oplus 2}}{\langle B\rangle}\right)^{\Gamma_{n}}=0.

Combined with
[
Kob03
, Lemma 10.3]
gives the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\langle u_{1},u_{2}\rangle_{\Gamma_{n}}\longrightarrow H^{1}_{\rm Iw}(K_{\infty},T)_{\Gamma_{n}}\longrightarrow\left(\frac{H^{1}_{\rm Iw}(K_{\infty},T)}{\langle u_{1},u_{2}\rangle}\right)_{\Gamma_{n}}\longrightarrow 0,

and we deduce that the first term of (
4.4
) is isomorphic to
\displaystyle\left(\frac{H^{1}_{{\rm Iw}}(K_{\infty},T)}{\langle u_{1},u_{2}\rangle}\right)_{\Gamma_{n}}
. Since
\det(B)
generates the
\Lambda
-characteristic ideal of
\displaystyle\frac{H^{1}_{{\rm Iw}}(K_{\infty},T)}{\langle u_{1},u_{2}\rangle}
, we have for
n\gg 0
,

\nabla\frac{H^{1}_{{\rm Iw}}(K_{\infty},T)_{\Gamma_{n}}}{{\rm pr}_{n}\left(\langle u_{1},u_{2}\rangle\right)}={\rm ord}_{\epsilon_{n}}\left(\det(B)(\epsilon_{n})\right)

by Lemma
3.3
(ii). Thus, for
n\gg 0
,
\nabla\mathcal{Y}^{\prime\prime}(E/K_{n})
is defined and is equal to
\nabla M_{n}-{\rm ord}_{\epsilon_{n}}\left(\det(B)(\epsilon_{n})\right)
.
Furthermore,
\det\underline{{\rm Col}}^{\pm}(\mathbf{u})=\det\underline{{\rm Col}}^{\pm}({\mathbf{z}})\det(B)
by definition. Hence, after combining Lemmas
4.9
and
4.8
, we deduce that

\nabla\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}(E/K_{n})=\begin{cases}2\deg{\widetilde{\omega}}_{n}^{+}+{\rm ord}_{\epsilon_{n}}\left(\det\underline{{\rm Col}}^{-}({\mathbf{z}})(\epsilon_{n})\right)&\;\;\;\text{if }n\text{ is odd,}\\
2\deg{\widetilde{\omega}}_{n}^{-}+{\rm ord}_{\epsilon_{n}}\left(\det\underline{{\rm Col}}^{+}({\mathbf{z}})(\epsilon_{n})\right)&\;\;\;\text{if }n\text{ is even.}\end{cases}

It follows from an adaptation of
[
Kob03
, Proposition 10.6 i)]
and
[
LL22
, Proposition 3.6]
to the current setting that for
n\gg 0
,

\nabla\mathcal{Y}(E/K_{n})=\nabla\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}(E/K_{n}),

which concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.12
.

Suppose that Hypothesis
2.16
holds. For
n\gg 0
, we have

\nabla\mathcal{X}(E/K_{n})=\begin{cases}2s_{n-1}+\lambda_{-}+(p^{n}-p^{n-1})\mu_{-}&\;\;\;\text{if }n\text{ is odd,}\\
2s_{n-1}+\lambda_{+}+(p^{n}-p^{n-1})\mu_{+}&\;\;\;\text{if }n\text{ is even,}\end{cases}

where
s_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}p^{k}
for
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let
\bullet\in\{+,-\}
. Let
f_{\bullet}\in\Lambda
(resp.
f_{0}
) denote a characteristic element of the torsion
\Lambda
-module
\mathcal{X}^{\bullet}(E/K_{\infty})
(resp.
\mathcal{X}^{0}(E/K_{\infty})
). We have the following Poitou–Tate exact sequence of
\Lambda
-torsion modules:

0\longrightarrow\frac{\mathrm{Image}\left(\underline{{\rm Col}}^{\bullet}\right)}{\langle\underline{{\rm Col}}^{\bullet}({\mathbf{z}})\rangle}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}^{\bullet}(E/K_{\infty})\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}^{0}(E/K_{\infty})\longrightarrow 0.

As
\mathrm{Image}({\rm Col}^{-})=\Lambda^{\oplus 2}
and
\mathrm{Image}({\rm Col}^{+})=(X\Lambda)^{\oplus 2}
, this gives the equality of
\Lambda
-ideals

(X^{n^{\bullet}}\det\underline{{\rm Col}}^{\bullet}({\mathbf{z}})f_{0})=(f^{\bullet}),

where
n^{-}=0
and
{n^{+}=-2}
.
Lemma
4.3
tells us that

\nabla\mathcal{X}(E/K_{n})=\nabla\mathcal{Y}(E/K_{n})+\nabla\mathcal{X}^{0}(E/K_{n}).

Hence, the corollary follows from Lemma
3.3
and Proposition
4.11
.
∎

Theorem 4.13
.

Suppose that Hypothesis
2.16
holds. Under our running hypotheses, we have:

(i)

{\rm rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}E(K_{n})
is bounded independently of
n
;

(ii)

Assume that
\Sha(E/K_{n})[p^{\infty}]
is finite for all
n\geq 0
. Define
\displaystyle r_{\infty}=\sup_{n\geq 0}\left\{{\rm rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}E(K_{n})\right\}
. Then, for
n\gg 0
, we have

\nabla\Sha(E/K_{n})[p^{\infty}]=\begin{cases}2s_{n-1}+\lambda_{-}+\phi(p^{n})\mu_{-}-r_{\infty}&\;\;\;\text{if }n\text{ is odd,}\\
2s_{n-1}+\lambda_{+}+\phi(p^{n})\mu_{+}-r_{\infty}&\;\;\;\text{if }n\text{ is even,}\end{cases}

where
\phi(p^{n})=p^{n}-p^{n-1}
, and
s_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}p^{k}
, for
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

Since
\nabla\mathcal{X}(E/K_{n})
is defined when
n
is sufficiently large by Corollary
4.12
, the kernel and cokernel of the natural map
\mathcal{X}(E/K_{n+1})\rightarrow\mathcal{X}(E/K_{n})
are finite for such
n
. In particular,
{\rm rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}E(K_{n})
is bounded independently of
n
. Hence, the first assertion follows from the well-known exact sequence

(4.5)

0\longrightarrow E(K_{n})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\longrightarrow{\rm Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{n})\longrightarrow\Sha(E/K_{n})[p^{\infty}]\longrightarrow 0.

It follows from the exact sequence (
4.5
) and Lemma
3.2
that

\nabla\Sha(E/K_{n})[p^{\infty}]=\nabla\mathcal{X}(E/K_{n})-\nabla E(K_{n})\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}.

Part (i) tells us that
\nabla E(K_{n})\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}=r_{\infty}
for
n\gg 0
. Thus, the assertion (ii) follows from Corollary
4.12
.
∎

Exploring the structural, electronic, optical, transport, and photovoltaic properties of Rb2LiGa(Br/I)6 using DFT and SCAPS-1D simulations
Authors: Mukaddar Sk, M. T. Islam, Gourav
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Lead-free double perovskite halides are attracting considerable interest in the optoelectronics sector due to their remarkable electronic, optical, and transport properties. These materials are not only stable and easy to synthesize but also present a wide range of potential applications. This study investigates the fascinating characteristics of Rb₂LiGa(Br/I)₆, focusing on its structural, electronic, optical, transport, and photovoltaic attributes. Our findings indicate that Rb₂LiGaBr₆ and Rb₂LiGaI₆ have band gaps of 1.19 eV and 1.13 eV, respectively, highlighting their versatility for various applications. Both compounds exhibit exceptional optical properties, featuring high absorption coefficients and optical conductivity, along with low reflectivity throughout the UV-visible spectrum, positioning them as excellent candidates for solar cell technologies. Moreover, Rb₂LiGa(Br/I)₆ demonstrates impressive thermoelectric performance, with high figure-of-merit (ZT) values between 200 K and 800 K, indicating their potential as effective thermoelectric materials. Consequently, this study offers valuable insights for the development of efficient double perovskite-based solar cells. Encouraged by the outstanding absorption and optical conductivity of Rb₂LiGa(Br/I)₆, we simulated an Au/Cu₂O/Rb₂LiGa(Br/I)₆/TiO₂/FTO solar cell. Our results reveal that the modeled solar cell, Au/Cu₂O/Rb₂LiGaI₆/TiO₂/FTO, achieves an efficiency of 26.48%, surpassing previous reports. This research sets a new benchmark for high-performance double perovskite-based solar cells and lays the foundation for future advancements in this exciting area.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-76593-6.

Introduction

As the global population expands, the demand for energy has surged dramatically. Currently, fossil fuels and other non-renewable resources predominantly meet this demand, but their environmental impact and limited availability necessitate a shift toward sustainable alternatives. To address this challenge, the exploration of innovative energy sources is imperative. Notably, solar cells and thermoelectric generators play critical roles in harnessing solar energy and recovering waste heat for electricity generation. For these technologies to be effective, the development of materials with superior optoelectronic and thermoelectric properties is essential. Photovoltaic materials are particularly promising when they exhibit a direct band gap in the range of 0.9 eV to 1.6 eV1, while ideal thermoelectric materials should feature low band gaps coupled with high electrical conductivity, low thermal conductivity, and a large Seebeck coefficient2. Recent research has highlighted halide perovskites, characterized by the formula A1+B3+X31−, as exceptional candidates for both photovoltaic and thermoelectric applications3–5. These materials possess remarkable attributes, including high dielectric constants, tunable band gaps, elevated carrier mobility, long carrier lifetimes, high absorption coefficients, and outstanding optical conductivity. Consequently, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells has experienced a remarkable increase, rising from 3.8% in 2009 to an impressive 26.1% in 20246,7. Despite these advancements, the commercialization of ABX3 perovskites is impeded by their toxicity and instability arising from organic components. This underscores the urgent need to identify alternative materials that retain the favorable optoelectronic properties of ABX3 while being stable, non-toxic, and economically viable. Recently, lead-free double perovskite halides, denoted as A1+B3+X31−, have emerged as promising substitutes. By judiciously selecting A, B, and X elements, these materials can achieve stability and desirable optoelectronic characteristics8,9. While numerous lead-free double perovskites have been synthesized, none have yet emerged as ideal candidates for photovoltaic and thermoelectric applications. For instance, Cs2AgBiBr6 exhibits a long carrier lifetime and an appropriate band gap in the visible range; however, its indirect band gap limits its efficacy as a photovoltaic material. Similarly, Cs2AgInCl6 presents a band gap of 3.3 eV, rendering it unsuitable for photovoltaic applications10. Other candidates, such as Rb2ASbX6 and A2CuSbX6, have also been explored but fall short due to their indirect band gaps or toxic profiles11,12. Recently, Kibbou et al. have reported Cs2InGaX6 (X = Cl, Br, or I) are promising for photovoltaic applications58. However, the reported band gap of such compounds are either wide or narrow which hindered their photovoltaic applications. Moreover, many more compounds have ben explored to fulfil the above criteria13–16. Also, other systems have been explored for achieving high efficiency17–19. However, the explored systems have limitations, as they are not stable and lack suitable optoelectronic properties. Given these limitations, it is critical to continue the search for novel lead-free halide double perovskites that exhibit stability, non-toxicity, and promising optoelectronic properties. This quest for innovative materials is essential for advancing sustainable energy solutions and meeting the escalating energy demands of our world.

In this study, we focus on Rb2LiGa(Br/I)6, exploring their structural, electronic, optical, and transport properties. Our analysis shows Poisson’s ratios of 0.27 and 0.28 for Rb2LiGaBr6 and Rb2LiGaI6, respectively, which indicate their ductility. Phonon spectra indicate the absence of imaginary frequencies, confirming stability against lattice distortions. With band gaps of 1.19 eV for Rb2LiGaBr6 and 1.13 eV for Rb2LiGaI6, these materials display remarkable optical properties, including high absorption coefficients, robust optical conductivity, and low reflectivity in the UV-visible range, positioning them as excellent candidates for solar cell applications. Our modeling of an Au/Cu₂O/Rb₂LiGaI₆/TiO₂/FTO solar cell predicts a maximum efficiency of 26.48%, highlighting their potential in high-efficiency photovoltaic applications. This study paves the way for developing lead-free halide double perovskite-based solar cells, offering a promising pathway toward more sustainable energy solutions.

Computational details

The structural, optoelectronic, and transport properties of Rb2LiGa(Br/I)6 have been explored using the WIEN2k code20. We calculated structural parameters such as lattice constants, bond lengths, bond angles, and overall stability using the GGA-PBE exchange-correlation method. For the crystal structure optimization of Rb2LiGa(Br/I)6, we employed 1000 K-points. The convergence criteria for total energy and forces during electronic and ionic relaxation were set to 10−6 eV and 10−2 eV/Å, respectively. To assess the electronic, optical, and transport properties, we utilized the mBJ potential, which yields results that closely match experimental values21. The TB-mBJ potential is particularly noted for its accuracy in predicting band gaps in semiconducting materials, effectively capturing both exchange and correlation effects. By combining a semi-local exchange potential with a non-local correction, TB-mBJ addresses the limitations of traditional density functional theory (DFT) methods, which often underestimate band gaps. This approach ensures accurate asymptotic behavior of the potential at large distances and enhances the representation of electronic states near the band edges. As a result, TB-mBJ provides a more dependable estimate of the fundamental band gap, making it especially valuable for materials characterized by strong electron-electron interactions or complex band structures. For the calculation of optical properties, we used 50,000 K-points. Additionally, transport properties were analysed using the BoltzTraP package22. Results and discussion

Structural properties, mechanical and thermodynamic properties

We began by optimizing the volume of Rb2LiGa(Br/I)6. Figure 1(a) and 1(b) present the volume optimization curves for these compounds, revealing lattice parameters of 11.13 Å for Rb2LiGaBr6 and 11.17 Å for Rb2LiGaI6. The relaxed structures of Rb2LiGa(Br/I)6 exhibit a cubic crystal phase, characterized by the Fm3m (No. 225) space group. The optimized crystal structure is illustrated in Fig. 2(a) and 2(b), where each Rb atom is situated at the center of an octahedral cavity, coordinated with twelve Br atoms. In contrast, each Li and Ga atom occupies the centre of its respective octahedron, coordinating with six (Br/I) atoms24. A detailed structural analysis indicates that the Li(Br/I)6 and Ga(Br/I)6 octahedra are alternately arranged along the [001], [100], and [010] directions. The volume optimization curves presented in Fig. 2(a) and 2(b) are instrumental in calculating the lattice parameters of these systems.

Electronic properties

Examining the electronic energy band dispersion is crucial for evaluating a material’s potential in photovoltaic and thermoelectric applications. We have calculated the band gaps for these two systems using various methods, as shown in Table 3. It’s important to note that the GGA PBE approximation tends to underestimate the band gap of lead-free halide double perovskites. In contrast, the mBJ + SOC method yields band gap values that align more closely with experimental findings31. Figure 5(a) and 5(b) present the energy band dispersion spectra for Rb2LiGa(Br/I)6. Both materials display similar band structures, likely due to the comparable electronic configurations of the constituent elements. Notably, both the valence band maxima and conduction band minima are located at the same symmetry point (┌). The fundamental energy band gap in these materials is direct, with estimated values of 1.19 eV for Rb2LiGaBr6 and 1.13 eV for Rb2LiGaI6. The iodine-based compound exhibits a lower band gap than the bromine-based one, which can be attributed to the larger atomic size of iodine (I) compared to bromine (Br). This size difference can result in longer bond lengths within the crystal structure, potentially affecting the overlap of atomic orbitals and leading to a narrower band gap. This trend of decreasing band gap due to iodine substitution has also been reported by Wu et al.32. Overall, the direct band gap values suggest that Rb2LiGa(Br/I)6 may be promising candidates for solar cell applications.

Optical properties

Studying optical properties is essential for evaluating a material’s potential in solar cells. Many lead-free double perovskites exhibit outstanding optical absorption in both the visible and UV solar spectra, thanks to their direct band gaps with optimal values. For instance, Nair et al. demonstrated that Cs₂TlBiI₆ possesses a direct band gap of 1.37 eV, indicating its suitability for solar applications40. Similarly, Vardwaz et al. emphasized the excellent photovoltaic characteristics of Cs₂AgCrBr₆, which benefits from a direct band gap of 1.42 eV41. Additionally, Yang et al. reported impressive photovoltaic performance in Cs₂PtI₆61.

To further explore these attributes, we analyze the optical characteristics of Rb₂LiGa(Br/I)₆ across the 0–8 eV energy range. The optical absorption, conductivity, and reflectivity of a material can be derived from its frequency-dependent complex dielectric constant ε, which describes how the material interacts with external electromagnetic radiation. Thermoelectric properties

Currently, our energy demands are largely satisfied by non-renewable sources that pose significant environmental risks. This scenario underscores the urgent need to explore renewable and eco-friendly energy alternatives. In this regard, thermoelectric technology stands out as a promising solution, capable of converting waste heat into electricity. The effectiveness of a thermoelectric material is quantified by the thermoelectric figure of merit, ZT = S²σT/ (kₑ + kₗ), where S represents the Seebeck coefficient, σ is the electrical conductivity, T is the temperature, kₑ is the electronic component of thermal conductivity, and kₗ is the lattice component. A high ZT value, ideally approaching unity, is indicative of a superior thermoelectric material, achieved by maximizing S and σ while minimizing thermal conductivity (k).

The relationship between S and T is depicted in Fig. 8(a), which reveals a clear trend: S decreases as temperature increases. This decline is attributed to the rising carrier density with increasing temperature. Importantly, the positive Seebeck coefficient confirms that both systems are p-type. Furthermore, both compounds demonstrate high S values across the entire temperature spectrum, making them strong candidates for thermoelectric applications.

Figure 8(b) illustrates the temperature-dependent behavior of electrical conductivity, showing a linear increase with rising temperature. This trend is also linked to the increase in carrier concentration. Notably, the elevated carrier conductivity in both Rb₂LiGa(Br/I)₆ compounds highlights their potential as effective thermoelectric materials. In Fig. 8(c), the thermal conductivity of Rb₂LiGaBr₆ is presented, revealing a linear increase with temperature in accordance with the Wiedemann-Franz law. Remarkably, both systems exhibit exceptionally low thermal conductivity.

Using Slack’s equation, we calculated the lattice thermal conductivity, as shown in Fig. 8(d), where a decrease in thermal conductivity with increasing temperature is evident for both materials49. Finally, Fig. 8(e) illustrates the variation of ZT with temperature, indicating a gradual decline in ZT as temperature rises. Nonetheless, ZT remains notably high throughout the entire temperature range, suggesting that Rb₂LiGa(Br/I)₆ holds significant promise for thermoelectric applications.

Modelling of heterojunction solar cell

Motivated by the impressive absorption coefficient and optical conductivity of Rb₂LiGa(Br/I)₆, we have modeled an Au/Cu₂O/Rb₂LiGa(Br/I)₆/TiO₂/FTO solar cell. Figure 9 illustrates the p-i-n structure of the Rb₂LiGa(Br/I)₆-based solar cell. In this configuration, gold (Au) serves as the back contact, copper(I) oxide (Cu₂O) functions as the hole transport layer (HTL), Rb₂LiGa(Br/I)₆ acts as the absorber layer, titanium dioxide (TiO₂) serves as the electron transport layer (ETL), and fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) is utilized as the front contact. The selection of these materials for the ETL, HTL, and contacts is based on their distinctive properties.

Titanium dioxide (TiO₂) is widely used as an ETL in various applications, particularly in perovskite solar cells (PSCs), owing to its high stability, low cost, wide band gap, and ease of fabrication. Copper(I) oxide (Cu₂O) shows promise as an HTL in photovoltaic applications, offering high hole mobility, favorable energy level alignment, cost-effectiveness, environmental friendliness, and simple fabrication. Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) is a preferred front contact material in numerous optoelectronic devices, including solar cells, due to its high transparency, good electrical conductivity, cost-effectiveness, and stability. Gold (Au) is often employed as a back contact in various optoelectronic devices, prized for its excellent conductivity, appropriate work function, stability, and strong reflectivity.

Conclusion

In conclusion, lead-free double perovskite halides are emerging as key players in the optoelectronics arena, thanks to their exceptional electronic, optical, and transport properties. These materials are not only stable and easy to synthesize but also offer a diverse array of potential applications. This study explores the intriguing characteristics of Rb₂LiGa(Br/I)₆, with a focus on its structural, electronic, optical, transport, and photovoltaic features. Our results reveal that Rb₂LiGaBr₆ and Rb₂LiGaI₆ possess band gaps of 1.19 eV and 1.13 eV, respectively, underscoring their versatility for various applications. Both compounds demonstrate outstanding optical properties, including high absorption coefficients and optical conductivity, combined with low reflectivity across the UV-visible spectrum, making them prime candidates for solar cell technologies. Additionally, Rb₂LiGa(Br/I)₆ showcases remarkable thermoelectric performance, with high figure-of-merit (ZT) values ranging from 200 K to 800 K, indicating their potential as effective thermoelectric materials. Thus, this study provides valuable insights for the advancement of efficient double perovskite-based solar cells.

Buoyed by the exceptional absorption and optical conductivity of Rb₂LiGa(Br/I)₆, we simu-lated an Au/Cu₂O/Rb₂LiGa(Br/I)₆/TiO₂/FTO solar cell. Our findings demonstrate that the modelled solar cell Au/Cu₂O/Rb₂LiGaI₆/TiO₂/FTO achieving efficiency of 26.48% that surpass previous reports. This research sets a new standard for high-performance double perovskite-based solar cells and lays the groundwork for future innovations in this exciting field.

Precision Higgs Constraints in U(1) Extensions of the Standard Model with a Light Z’-Boson
Zoltán Péli, Zoltán Trócsányi

Abstract
Anomaly free U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) extensions of the standard model (SM) predict a new neutral gauge boson Z ′ Z^{\prime} . When the Z ′ Z^{\prime} obtains its mass from the spontaneous breaking of the new U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) symmetry by a new complex scalar field, the model also predicts a second real scalar s s and the searches for the new scalar and the new gauge boson become intertwined. We present the computation of production cross sections and decay widths of such a scalar s s in models with a light Z ′ Z^{\prime} boson, when the decay h → Z ′ ​ Z ′ h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime} may have a sizeable branching ratio. We show how Higgs signal strength measurement in this channel can provide stricter exclusion bounds on the parameters of the model than those obtained from the total signal strength for Higgs boson production.

An example of such a simple

It is designed to explain (i) the non-vanishing masses of neutrinos,

The SWSM extends the gauge group of the SM

The corresponding neutral gauge field becomes massive by introducing a

The gauge and gravity anomalies are required to cancel, which is achieved

II
Scalar sector of the SWSM

In this section we collect the details of the model only to the extent
used in the present analyses.

In the scalar sector, in addition to the
SU(2)_{\text{L}}
-doublet
Brout-Englert-Higgs field

\phi=\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}\phi^{+}\\
\phi^{0}\end{array}\!\!\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}\phi_{1}+\text{i}\phi_{2}\\
\phi_{3}+\text{i}\phi_{4}\end{array}\!\!\right)\,,

(II.1)

the model contains a complex scalar SM singlet
\chi
.
The Lagrangian of the scalar fields contains the potential energy

\begin{split}V(\phi,\chi)&=-\mu_{\phi}^{2}|\phi|^{2}-\mu_{\chi}^{2}|\chi|^{2}+\left(|\phi|^{2},|\chi|^{2}\right)\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda_{\phi}&\frac{\lambda}{2}\\
\frac{\lambda}{2}&\lambda_{\chi}\end{array}\!\!\right)\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}|\phi|^{2}\\
|\chi|^{2}\end{array}\!\!\right)\subset-\mathcal{L}\end{split}

(II.2)

where
|\phi|^{2}=|\phi^{+}|^{2}+|\phi^{0}|^{2}
.
After spontaneous symmetry breaking, we parametrize the scalar fields as

\begin{split}\phi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\binom{-\text{i}\sqrt{2}\sigma^{+}}{v+h^{\prime}+\text{i}\sigma_{\phi}}\,,\quad\chi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(w+s^{\prime}+\text{i}\sigma_{\chi})\end{split}

(II.3)

where
v
and
w
are the vacuum expectation values of
\phi
and
\chi
.
The fields
h^{\prime}
and
s^{\prime}
are real scalars,
\sigma^{+}
is a charged, while

\sigma_{\phi}
and
\sigma_{\chi}
are neutral Goldstone bosons that are gauge eigenstates.

The gauge and mass eigenstates are related by the rotations

\binom{h}{s}=\textbf{Z}_{S}\binom{h^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}\,,\quad\binom{\sigma_{Z}}{\sigma_{Z^{\prime}}}=\textbf{Z}_{G}\binom{\sigma_{\phi}}{\sigma_{\chi}}\,,

(II.4)

with

\textbf{Z}_{X}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta_{X}&-\sin\theta_{X}\\
\sin\theta_{X}&~~\cos\theta_{X}\end{pmatrix}

(II.5)

where we denoted the mass eigenstates with
h
,
s
and
\sigma_{Z}
,

\sigma_{Z^{\prime}}
. The angles
\theta_{S}
and
\theta_{G}

are the scalar and Goldstone mixing angles that can
be determined by the diagonalization of the mass matrix of the real scalars
and that of the neutral Goldstone bosons. In the following, we are going to use
the abbreviations
{\rm c}_{X}=\cos\theta_{X}
and
{\rm s}_{X}=\sin\theta_{X}
for mixing angles.

II.1
Scalar couplings

The vertices that involve the scalars are related to the corresponding vertices
in the SM by simple proportionality factors involving the scalar mixing angle

\theta_{S}

corresponding SM rule.

•

Scalar-fermion couplings:

\Gamma_{hf\bar{f}}=\text{c}_{S}\Gamma_{hf\bar{f}}^{\text{SM}}\,,\quad\Gamma_{sf\bar{f}}=\mathrm{s}_{S}\Gamma_{hf\bar{f}}^{\text{SM}}.

(II.6)

•

Scalar-vector boson couplings:
{\rm i}\Gamma_{SVV}g^{\mu\nu}
where

\Gamma_{hWW}=\text{c}_{S}\Gamma_{hWW}^{\text{SM}}\,,\quad\Gamma_{sWW}=\mathrm{s}_{S}\Gamma_{hWW}^{\text{SM}}

(II.7)

and

\begin{split}\Gamma_{hZZ}&=2M_{Z}^{2}\biggl(\text{c}_{S}\text{c}_{Z}^{2}-\frac{\mathrm{s}_{S}}{\tan\beta}\mathrm{s}_{Z}^{2}\biggr)\,,\quad\Gamma_{sZZ}=2M_{Z}^{2}\biggl(\mathrm{s}_{S}\text{c}_{Z}^{2}+\frac{\text{c}_{S}}{\tan\beta}\mathrm{s}_{Z}^{2}\biggr)\,,\\
\Gamma_{hZZ^{\prime}}&=2M_{Z}M_{Z^{\prime}}\mathrm{s}_{Z}\text{c}_{Z}\biggl(\text{c}_{S}+\frac{\mathrm{s}_{S}}{\tan\beta}\biggr)\,,\quad\Gamma_{sZZ^{\prime}}=2M_{Z}M_{Z^{\prime}}\mathrm{s}_{Z}\text{c}_{Z}\biggl(\mathrm{s}_{S}-\frac{\text{c}_{S}}{\tan\beta}\biggr)\,,\\
\Gamma_{hZ^{\prime}Z^{\prime}}&=2M^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}\biggl(\text{c}_{S}\mathrm{s}_{Z}^{2}-\frac{\mathrm{s}_{S}}{\tan\beta}\text{c}_{Z}^{2}\biggr)\,,\quad\Gamma_{sZ^{\prime}Z^{\prime}}=2M^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}\biggl(\mathrm{s}_{S}\mathrm{s}_{Z}^{2}+\frac{\text{c}_{S}}{\tan\beta}\text{c}_{Z}^{2}\biggr)\end{split}

(II.8)

where
\tan\beta=w/v
is the ratio of the VeVs.

\theta_{Z}

is smaller than
\mathcal{O}\bigl(10^{-3}\bigr)

,
hence we consider the leading contributions to the vertex factors
\Gamma_{SVV}

Scalar-scalar couplings:

\begin{split}\Gamma_{hss}&=+\frac{M_{H}^{2}+2M_{s}^{2}}{v}\,\mathrm{s}_{S}\text{c}_{S}\left(\frac{\text{c}_{S}}{\tan\beta}-\mathrm{s}_{S}\right)\,,\\

III
Production of scalar particles in the LHC

The production cross sections of a scalar particle that mixes with the SM scalar,
which we shall refer to as
Higgs-like new scalar
, can be computed based
on Ref.
Djouadi:2005gi
. There are several production channels for the Higgs
boson at the LHC, and similarly for the new scalar as well. These channels involve
(i) gluon-gluon fusion (ggF),
(ii) associated production of the scalar with a vector boson (VS),
(iii) vector boson fusion (VBF), and
(iv) associated production of the scalar with a
t\bar{t}
pair (ttS).

In the SWSM the new scalar is directly coupled to heavy quarks, so the dominant
production channel is the gluon-gluon fusion, as in the SM. The difference
compared to the
\Gamma_{hf\bar{f}}
vertex is only a proportionality factor
obtained from the scalar mixing angle
\theta_{S}
given in Eq. (
II.6
),
hence the gluon fusion cross sections for the production of the two scalar
particles are proportional,

\sigma(gg\to h)=\text{c}_{S}^{2}~\sigma^{\text{SM}}(gg\to h)\,\,,\quad\sigma(gg\to s)=\mathrm{s}_{S}^{2}~\sigma^{\text{SM}}(gg\to h)|_{M_{h}\to M_{s}}\,.

(III.1)

The associated production of the scalar boson with a vector boson
V
involves
the couplings (
II.8
) between the scalars and the vector bosons, which
provide factors of
\text{c}_{S}^{2}
and
\mathrm{s}_{S}^{2}
as compared to the SM. In addition,
there is a contribution due to the
SZ^{\prime}
channel, but it is negligibly small compared to the
SW
or

SZ
channels for small
Z^{\prime}
masses,
\xi=M_{Z^{\prime}}/M_{Z}\ll 1
, relevant in the
SWSM parameter space. The V-A couplings of the
Z
boson to the quarks
also receives BSM corrections, but these are well measured quantities and the
deviation from the SM must be small. Hence, the
Vh
production cross section
receives only an overall factor
\text{c}_{S}^{2}
as compared to the SM model prediction,

\sigma(pp\to Vh)=\text{c}_{S}^{2}\sigma^{\text{SM}}(pp\to Vh)\,.

(III.2)

Thus, measuring
\sigma(pp\to Vh)
and comparing it to the SM prediction for this process constrains
\text{c}_{S}
.

By far the most complicated process to compute its cross section is the vector
boson fusion. However, the radiative corrections are known to be small

Djouadi:2005gi
, so we consider this channel only at leading order (LO)
in perturbation theory. The partonic process is
qq\to qq+(V^{*}V^{*}\to h)
.
The squared matrix element of this process at LO is proportional to

G_{F}^{3}M_{V}^{8}
, which means that the VBF process is also heavily suppressed,
when
V=Z^{\prime}
and
\xi\ll 1
. Thus, only
V=Z
and
W
contributes with
suppression factors
\text{c}_{S}^{2}
for the Higgs production and
\mathrm{s}_{S}^{2}
for
the new Higgs-like scalar production,

\sigma(qq\to qqh)=\text{c}_{S}^{2}\sigma^{\text{SM}}(qq\to qqh)\,,\quad\sigma(qq\to qqs)=\mathrm{s}_{S}^{2}\sigma^{\text{SM}}(qq\to qqh)|_{M_{h}\to M_{s}}\,.

(III.3)

The scalar boson can also be produced in association with heavy quarks.
This has the smallest contribution to the total production rate even when
the heavy quark is the t-quark. The Higgs-like new scalar is directly
coupled to heavy quarks just like in the Higgs boson, and the suppression
factor in the production cross section is
\tan^{2}(\theta_{S})
just like in gluon-gluon fusion,

\sigma(pp\to t\bar{t}s)=\mathrm{s}_{S}^{2}\sigma^{\text{SM}}(pp\to t\bar{t}h)|_{M_{h}\to M_{s}}\,.

(III.4)

Based on its small contribution to the total production cross section, we neglect this production channel in our analysis.

We see that all important production cross sections are proportional to the
cross section of Higgs boson production in the SM
with the relevant scalar mass value. Then it is sufficient to
precisely compute the Higgs boson production cross section for different
values of the Higgs mass. One can use automated software for that purpose,
or alternatively one can save data using plots from
Ref.
ATLAStwiki
, which we did here to obtain the relevant
K
-factors.

IV
Decays of scalars

IV.1
Total width of the Higgs boson

The SM theoretical prediction for the Higgs boson width is
\Gamma_{h}^{{\rm SM}}=4.07~\mathrm{MeV}
, with a relative uncertainty of
4\%

ParticleDataGroup:2024cfk
. The experimental measurements on the other hand are
\Gamma_{h}^{{\rm ATLAS}}=4.5^{+3.3}_{-2.5}\,\mathrm{MeV}

ATLAS:2023dnm

and

\Gamma_{h}^{{\rm CMS}}=3.2^{+2.4}_{-1.7}\,\mathrm{MeV}

CMS:2022ley
,
display a much larger uncertainty than the SM theoretical prediction allowing for
several BSM models to remain compatible with observations.

In the superweak and other U(1)
z
extensions with a light
Z^{\prime}
boson (
M_{Z^{\prime}}\ll M_{Z})
the decay
h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
is allowed with partial width

\Gamma\bigl(h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}\bigr)=\frac{G_{\rm F}M_{h}^{3}}{16\sqrt{2}\pi}\left(\frac{\mathrm{s}_{S}}{\tan\beta}\right)^{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}\right),

(IV.1)

and the total width of the Higgs boson is

\Gamma_{h}=\text{c}_{S}^{2}\Gamma_{h}^{\rm SM}+\Gamma\bigl(h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}\bigr),

(IV.2)

which can be used to constrain the parameters
\theta_{S}
and
\tan\beta
.
We see that the total decay width can be smaller in the extended model than that in the SM depending on the relative effect of the scalar mixing angle and the partial decay width of the Higgs boson into the new neutral gauge boson pair.

IV.2
Decay channels

A Higgs-like new scalar has similar decay channels as the Higgs-boson as listed.

Fermionic:
The decays into SM fermions are only affected by an overall factor of
\mathrm{s}_{S}^{2}
as compared to the SM. If the mass of the Higgs-like scalar is above the
2m_{N}
threshold, it can also decay into a pair of HNLs, with partial with

\Gamma\bigl(S\to N_{i}N_{i}\bigr)=\frac{G_{\rm f}M_{s}m_{N_{i}}^{2}}{8\sqrt{2}\pi}\biggl(1-\frac{4m_{N_{i}}^{2}}{M_{s}^{2}}\biggr)^{3/2}\biggl(\frac{\text{c}_{S}}{\tan\beta}\biggr)^{2}\,.

(IV.3)

The Higgs boson can also decay into an HNL pair, provided the process is
allowed kinematically. The corresponding partial decay width can be obtained
form formula (
IV.3
) with the replacements
M_{s}\to M_{h}
and
\text{c}_{S}\to\mathrm{s}_{S}
.

Loop induced:
This category includes the decays into
\gamma\gamma
,

gg
and
Z\gamma
as well as
Z^{\prime}\gamma
. In the important decay channels

s\to\gamma\gamma
and
gg
, the decay widths are only multiplied by an overall
\mathrm{s}_{S}^{2}
factor,

\Gamma(s\to\gamma\gamma,\>Z\gamma,\>gg)=\mathrm{s}_{S}^{2}\Gamma^{\text{SM}}(h\to\gamma\gamma,\>Z\gamma,\>gg)|_{M_{h}\to M_{s}}\,.

(IV.4)

The
Z^{\prime}\gamma
channel is also interesting because the LHC cast
exclusion limits specifically on this channel (with branching ratio
{\rm Br}(Z^{\prime}\gamma)<2\,\%
),
but the prediction for this branching fraction is much smaller then the
experimental limit. In the case of a light
Z^{\prime}
and a modest
Z-Z^{\prime}
mixing,
its contributions are negligibly small.

Decays into a pair of heavy vector bosons:
The decays of the
Higgs-like scalar into a charged
W
-pair is only affected by an overall factor
of
\mathrm{s}_{S}^{2}
as compared to the SM prediction. Decays into heavy neutral bosons
are controlled by the
SZ^{(\prime)}Z^{(\prime)}
vertex. The vertex
SZZ^{\prime}

is naturally suppressed, but the
SZ^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
may be large.

Decays into scalars:
In case
M_{s}>2M_{h}
, the channel
s\to hh

opens up, while the opposite decay,
h\to ss
is excluded by the results for

\Gamma_{h}^{\rm exp.}
compared to the SM prediction.

The decay properties of a Higgs-like new scalar depend on all free parameters
of the model. An indirect restriction on the parameter space can be derived from
the experimental constraints on the Higgs-boson width
\Gamma_{h}
.

A more refined analysis reveals that for
\xi\ll 1
, such as the case in the SWSM,
the free parameters reduce to a set of four parameters
Peli:2023fyb
:

M_{s},~\mathrm{s}_{S},~\tan\beta,~\text{and}~m_{N_{i}},

(IV.5)

where

\tan\beta
absorbs the free parameters of the gauge sector
in the combination

\tan\beta\propto\frac{M_{Z^{\prime}}}{|\mathrm{s}_{Z}|M_{Z}}\,.

(IV.6)

The exact formula contains an additional factor depending on the

z
-charge of the BEH field and the kinetic mixing between the U(1)
gauge fields
Peli:2024fha
, which is not important in our
analysis. We note that for the B-L
U(1)
extension the
proportionality factor is zero, so the scalar and gauge sector
parameters are not related in that model.

V
Implementation

We computed the Higgs boson production cross section

\sigma\bigl(pp\to h+X\bigr)
at
\sqrt{s}=13.6~\mathrm{TeV}

center of mass energies in proton proton collisions for several values of
M_{h}
in the range [
100~\mathrm{GeV},1~\mathrm{TeV}
].
Producing a Higgs-like scalar means that this cross section is multiplied with
\mathrm{s}_{S}^{2}
.
In principle, the
Z^{\prime}
boson affects these production rates in a nontrivial
way, but these effects are negligibly small for
M_{Z^{\prime}}\ll M_{Z}
, which is
relevant for the SWSM.

We used the NNPDF3.0 set for the parton density functions (PDF), the LO
set for LO predictions and the NLO set for the computations at the
next-to-leading order (NLO) accuracy. We use the running strong coupling
from the chosen PDF set. We take the values of the other input parameters

m_{b}^{\rm pole}
,
m_{t}^{\rm pole}
,
G_{f}
,
M_{Z}
and
M_{W}
from
Ref.
ParticleDataGroup:2024cfk
.

We compute the production cross section from ggF process at NLO QCD with NNLO QCD and NLO EW corrections included as a K-factor. The Vh process is implemented at NLO QCD and finally we compute the VBF at LO as the NLO corrections are known to be small with
K^{\rm VBF}_{\rm NLO}\sim 1.1
.

We include a
Mathematica
notebook
swsm_scalar.nb

which contains a precomputed set of the scalar boson productions cross sections and precisely computes the decay rates of the Higgs boson and the Higgs-like scalar.

VI
Signal strengths

If a light neutral vector boson
Z^{\prime}
exists, the Higgs boson can decay
into a
Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}

The signal strength measured at the LHC is

\mu_{\rm exp.}=\frac{\bigl(\sigma\,{\rm Br}\bigr)_{\rm obs}}{\bigl(\sigma\,{\rm Br}\bigr)_{\rm SM}}\,,

(VI.1)

while the theoretical value in the presence of new physics is

\mu_{\rm th.}=\frac{\bigl(\sigma\,{\rm Br}\bigr)_{\rm BSM}}{\bigl(\sigma\,{\rm Br}\bigr)_{\rm SM}}

(VI.2)

where the BSM subscript refers to the prediction in the full BSM model,
including the SM contributions. The most precisely measured channel is

h\to ZZ^{*}

. In the superweak model and in
general when the Higgs boson cannot decay into an

predicted signal strength in the

\mu_{ZZ}=\frac{\text{c}_{S}^{4}\Gamma_{h}^{{\rm SM}}}{\text{c}_{S}^{2}\Gamma_{h}^{{\rm SM}}+\Gamma\bigl(h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}\bigr)}

because
\sigma_{h}^{{\rm SWSM}}=\text{c}_{S}^{2}\sigma_{h}^{{\rm SM}}
and

\Gamma^{\rm SWSM}(h\to ZZ)=\text{c}_{S}^{2}\Gamma^{\rm SM}(h\to ZZ)

Using the PDG24
ParticleDataGroup:2024cfk

\bigl(\Gamma_{h}\simeq 0.00407~\mathrm{GeV},M_{h}\simeq 125~\mathrm{GeV}\bigr)
, we obtain

which approaches
\text{c}_{S}^{2}
for large values of
\tan\beta
. In that case the

The latter sums over all production and decay channels, hence

\mu_{\rm tot}=\frac{\sigma_{h}^{{\rm BSM}}}{\sigma_{h}^{{\rm SM}}}=\text{c}_{S}^{2},

(VI.5)

in which case the experimental values are

ATLAS:2022vkf
;
CMS:2022dwd

\mu^{\rm ATLAS}_{\rm tot}=1.05\pm 0.06,\quad\quad\mu^{\rm CMS}_{\rm tot}=1.02\pm 0.06.

(VI.6)

Then, the total signal strength yields the bounds independent of
\tan\beta

\theta^{\rm ATL}_{S,{\rm tot}}=0.27,\quad\quad\theta^{\rm CMS}_{S,{\rm tot}}=0.32.

(VI.7)

{\rm BP2}:\theta_{S}&=0.175,\tan\beta=10,M_{S}=500\,\mathrm{GeV}\,,\\
{\rm BP3}:\theta_{S}&=0.10,\tan\beta=10,M_{S}=1000\,\mathrm{GeV}\,.\end{split}

(VII.2)

where BP1 is allowed by
\mu_{\rm tot}
, but excluded by
\mu_{\rm ZZ}
, while BP2 and BP3 are
still allowed.
BP1 is chosen such that
finding a scalar corresponding to BP1 would exclude
a large class of BSM models predicting a light
Z^{\prime}
boson.
Using Eq. (
IV.6
) the gauge
sector parameters are

\begin{split}{\rm BP1}:M_{Z^{\prime}}&=18\,\mathrm{MeV},\quad\mathrm{s}_{Z}=10^{-4}\,,\\
{\rm BP2}:M_{Z^{\prime}}&=91\,\mathrm{MeV},\quad\mathrm{s}_{Z}=10^{-4}\,,\\
{\rm BP3}:M_{Z^{\prime}}&=91\,\mathrm{MeV},\quad\mathrm{s}_{Z}=10^{-4}\,.\end{split}

(VII.3)

The Higgs-portal coupling
\lambda
can be expressed via the relation

\lambda=\frac{M_{S}^{2}-M_{H}^{2}}{v^{2}}\,\frac{\mathrm{s}_{S}\text{c}_{S}}{\tan\beta}\,,

(VII.4)

yielding

\lambda_{\rm BP1}=0.33,\quad\lambda_{\rm BP2}=0.07\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{\rm BP3}=0.16\,.

(VII.5)

N_{i}

and

M_{N_{2}}=M_{N_{3}}=100\,\mathrm{GeV}
for all benchmark points. Then, the HNLs do

VIII
Summary

In our analysis we focused on U(1) extensions of the SM where the new gauge
boson becomes massive via the spontaneous breaking of the new
U(1)
symmetry
by a complex scalar field, such as in the superweak extension of the SM.
We have shown that in the case of a light
Z^{\prime}
, the new decay channel

h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
alters the signal strengths in all decay channels
of the Higgs boson.
Here we focused on the
ZZ^{*}
channel, which is measured experimentally with high
precision. We obtained bounds on the free parameters of the model: the Higgs –
new scalar mixing angle
\theta_{S}
and the ratio
\tan\beta
of the VeV of the new scalar
to that of the BEH field. We presented three benchmark points – one (BP1) already
excluded by the signal strength measurement in the
ZZ^{*}
decay channel of the
Higgs boson and two other ones (BP2, BP3) still allowed. Finding a new scalar
s

corresponding to BP1 would mean that no new light
Z^{\prime}
boson exits
as it would violate the exclusion bound derived from the effect of the decay
h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
.
Further
benchmark points can be generated easily using the
Mathematica

notebook available on request.

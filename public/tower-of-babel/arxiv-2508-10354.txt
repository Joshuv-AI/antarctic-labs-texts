1.
Introduction

Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the standard graded polynomial ring over a field
K
, and let
I\subset S
be a squarefree monomial ideal. The foundational works of Hochster, Stanley, and Reisner revealed a deep correspondence between such ideals and the combinatorics of simplicial complexes:
I
is equal to the Stanley-Reisner ideal
I_{\Delta}
of a unique simplicial complex
\Delta
on the vertex set
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
. This algebra-topology correspondence has proved remarkably fruitful, enabling Stanley to resolve the Upper Bound Conjecture for simplicial spheres (cf.
[
4
, Section 5.4]
).

A central invariant in the homological study of graded ideals is the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity, originally introduced by Eisenbud-Goto and, independently, by Ooishi. For a graded ideal
I\subset S
, it is defined as

\textup{reg}\,I\ =\ \max\{j+i:\ \textup{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(I)_{j}\neq 0\},

where
\textup{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(I)_{j}
is the
j
th graded piece of the
i
th local cohomology module of
I
with respect to the (unique) graded maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
of
S
. One always has
\textup{reg}\,I\geq\alpha(I)
, where
\alpha(I)
is the minimal degree among the homogeneous generators of
I
, and equality precisely characterizes those ideals with a linear resolution.

A natural refinement of this notion arises in the study of powers of ideals. If
I^{k}
has a linear resolution for all
k\geq 1
, we say that
I
has
linear powers
. While it may be tempting to conjecture that the linear resolution of
I
implies linear powers, counterexamples due to Terai and Sturmfels reveal that this implication fails in general and, in some cases, depends on the base field
K
.

Terai showed that if the characteristic of
K
is not
2
, the Stanley-Reisner ideal

J\ =\ (abd,\,abf,\,ace,\,adc,\,aef,\,bde,\,bcf,\,bce,\,cdf,\,def)

(1)

of the minimal triangulation of the projective plane has a linear resolution, while
J^{2}
does not have a linear resolution. This example is characteristic-dependent.

Sturmfels
[
23
]
provided another example of an ideal

J\ =\ (def,\,cef,\,cdf,\,cde,\,bef,\,bcd,\,acf,\,ade)

(2)

with a linear resolution, indeed even linear quotients (a concept which we recall below), for which
J^{2}
does not have a linear resolution. This example does not depend on
K
.

For a monomial ideal
I\subset S
, let
\mathcal{G}(I)
be the (unique) minimal monomial generating set of
I
. We say that
I
has
linear quotients
if there exists an order
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}
on
\mathcal{G}(I)
such that
(u_{1},\dots,u_{j-1}):(u_{j})
is generated by variables, for all
j=2,\dots,m
. If
I
is generated in a single degree and has linear quotients, then it has a linear resolution, regardless of the choice of the field
K
.

For an integer
0\leq d\leq n
, we set

\mathcal{I}_{n,d}(K)=\{I\subset S:\ I\ \textup{is a squarefree monomial ideal with a}\ d\textup{-linear resolution}\}.

In the light of the above discussions and examples (
1
)-(
2
), the following fundamental questions have long remained elusive and unsolved.

Question 1.1
.

When is
\mathcal{I}_{n,d}(K)
independent of
K
?

Question 1.2
.

For which degrees
d
, does every squarefree monomial ideal
I\subset S
generated in degree
d
have a linear resolution, if and only if, it has linear powers?

That is, for which integers
n
and
d
, does there exist a uniform combinatorial characterization of all squarefree monomial ideals generated in degree
d
, that have linear resolutions or even linear powers? And for which degrees
d
, having a linear resolution and having linear powers are equivalent properties for all squarefree monomial ideals of
S
generated in degree
d
? In this paper, we will solve these problems.

Any squarefree monomial ideal
I\subset S
generated in degree
d\in\{0,1,n{-}1,n\}
is polymatroidal. A monomial ideal
I\subset S
is called
polymatroidal
if the exponent vectors of the monomials of
\mathcal{G}(I)
form the set of bases of a discrete polymatroid (cf
[
14
, Chapter 12]
). By a result of Bandari and Rahmati-Asghar
[
1
]
, a monomial ideal
I\subset S
is polymatroidal, if and only if,
I
is generated in a single degree and it has linear quotients with respect to the lexicographic order induced by any ordering of the variables. Since all powers of a polymatroidal ideal are again polymatroidal, the linear resolution property of any power of
I
is independent from the base field
K
.

In view of examples (
1
) and (
2
), the first interesting case occurs when
d=2
. Then
I
can be viewed as an edge ideal

I(G)\ =\ (x_{i}x_{j}:\ \{i,j\}\in E(G))

of a finite simple graph
G
on vertex set
V(G)=[n]
with edge set
E(G)
. Recall that the complementary graph of
G
is the graph
G^{c}
with
V(G^{c})=V(G)
whose edges are the non-edges of
G
. Herzog, Hibi and Zheng proved the following influential result, see
[
18
]
and
[
13
, Corollary 3.2]
. See also
[
2
]
and
[
8
]
, for a short proof.

Theorem A
.

Let
I\subset S
be a squarefree monomial ideal generated in degree
2
. Write
I=I(G)
. The following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

I
has a linear resolution.

(b)

I^{k}
has linear quotients, for all
k\geq 1
.

(c)

G^{c}
is chordal.

For squarefree quadratic monomial ideals, the bad behavior of example (
2
) does not occur: if
I\subset S
has a
2
-linear resolution, then
I^{k}
has a
2k
-linear resolution, for all
k\geq 1
. The equivalence (a)
\Leftrightarrow
(b) in Theorem
A
holds as well for the ideals
I\in\mathcal{I}_{n,d}(K)
with
d\in\{0,1,n{-}1,n\}
, because any such ideal is polymatroidal and powers of polymatroidal ideals are polymatroidal
[
14
, Theorem 12.6.3]
.

The ideals (
1
), (
2
) are generated in degree
3
and live in a polynomial ring in
6
variables. This fact suggests that Theorem
A
could be extended in degree
d=n{-}2
.

Let
I\subset S
be a squarefree monomial ideal generated in degree
n{-}2
. Given a non-empty subset
F
of
[n]
, we put
{\bf x}_{F}=\prod_{i\in F}x_{i}
. Then
\mathcal{G}(I)=\{{\bf x}_{[n]\setminus e_{1}},\dots,{\bf x}_{[n]\setminus e_{m}}\}
where
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{m}\}
is the edge set of a finite simple graph
G
on
[n]
. Conversely, given a finite simple graph
G
, in
[
10
]
we introduce the
complementary edge ideal
of
G
as

I_{c}(G)\ =\ ({\bf x}_{[n]\setminus e}:\ e\in E(G)).

Any squarefree monomial ideal generated in degree
n{-}2
is the complementary edge ideal of some graph
G
. We denote by
c(G)
the number of connected components of
G
having more than one vertex. We have the following extension of Theorem
A
.

Theorem B
.

Let
I\subset S
be a squarefree monomial ideal generated in degree
n{-}2
. Write
I=I_{c}(G)
. The following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

I
has a linear resolution.

(b)

I^{k}
has linear quotients, for all
k\geq 1
.

(c)

c(G)=1
.

In order to prove Theorem
B
, we follow the strategy outlined in
[
18
]
. Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal generated in a single degree with
\mathcal{G}(I)=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\}
. The Rees algebra of
I
is defined as the
K
-subalgebra
\mathcal{R}(I)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}I^{k}t^{k}
of
S[t]
. Let

\varphi:\ T=S[y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]\rightarrow\mathcal{R}(I)

be the
S
-algebra homomorphism defined by
\varphi(y_{j})=u_{j}t
, for all
j=1,\dots,m
, where
\deg_{T}(x_{i})=(1,0)
and
\deg_{T}(y_{j})=(\deg_{S}(u_{j}),1)
. Then
\mathcal{R}(I)\cong T/J
, where
J=\textup{Ker}\,\varphi
is a toric ideal. The
x
-regularity
of
\mathcal{R}(I)
is defined as

\textup{reg}_{x}\mathcal{R}(I)\ =\ \max_{i,j}\{a_{i,j}-i\},

where

\mathbb{F}\ :\ 0\rightarrow F_{p}\rightarrow F_{p-1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow F_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}(I)\rightarrow 0

is the minimal bigraded free
T
-resolution of
\mathcal{R}(I)
with
F_{i}=\bigoplus_{j}T(-a_{i,j},-b_{i,j})
for all
i
. The following results were proved in
[
22
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
18
]
, respectively.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal generated in degree
d
. The following statements hold.

(a)

\textup{reg}\,I^{k}\leq dk+\textup{reg}_{x}\mathcal{R}(I)
for all
k\geq 1
.

(b)

I
has linear powers, if and only if,
\textup{reg}_{x}\mathcal{R}(I)=0
.

(c)

(
The
x
-condition
)
If there exists a monomial order
<
on
T
such that
\textup{in}_{<}(J)
is generated by monomials of the form
uv
with
u\in S
of degree
\leq 1
and
v\in K[y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]
, then
\textup{reg}_{x}\mathcal{R}(I)=0
.

To establish Theorem
B
, we apply Theorem
1.3
(c). By itself, condition (c), which is usually called the
x
-condition
(see
[
15
,
18
,
20
]
), only guarantees that
I
has linear powers. In Section
2
, we introduce the concepts of
x
-dominant and
y
-dominant orders, and we prove that if condition (c) is satisfied with respect to such monomial orders, then
I
has not only linear powers, but also linear quotients, for all
k\geq 1
(see Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
). The order used in the proof of Theorem
3.1
is
x
-dominant and this together with Corollary
3.2
establishes Theorem
B
.

In order to fully address Questions
1.1
and
1.2
, it remains to consider the degrees
3\leq d\leq n{-}3
. Notice that for such a
d
to exist we must have
n\geq 6
. When
n=6
, the ideal (
1
) shows that the set
\mathcal{I}_{6,3}(K)
depends on the ground field
K
.

Recall that the support of a monomial
u\in S
is the set
\textup{supp}(u)=\{i:x_{i}\ \textup{divides}\ u\}
. The support of a monomial ideal
I\subset S
is defined as
\textup{supp}(I)=\bigcup_{u\in\mathcal{G}(I)}\textup{supp}(u).
We say that
I\subset S
is
fully-supported
if
\textup{supp}(I)=[n]
.

Generalizing the ideal in (
1
), we show that

Theorem C
.

Let
n\geq 6
. For each
3\leq d\leq n{-}3
, there exists a fully-supported squarefree monomial ideal
I\subset S
generated in degree
d
such that

\textup{reg}\,I\ =\ \begin{cases}d+1&\textit{if}\ \textup{char}(K)=2,\\
\hfil d&\textit{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Hence, for each degree
3\leq d\leq n{-}3
the set
\mathcal{I}_{n,d}(K)
actually depends on
K
.

Finally, it remains to address Question
1.2
for each degree
3\leq d\leq n{-}3
. Notice that for such a
d
to exist we must have
n\geq 6
. When
n=6
, the ideal (
2
) shows that
\mathcal{I}_{6,3}(K)\neq\mathcal{I}_{6,3}^{*}(K)
for any field
K
.

In the following result, we show that except for the degrees
d\in\{0,1,2,n{-}2,n{-}1,n\}
, if
I\in\mathcal{I}_{n,d}(K)
is fully-supported and has linear quotients, it is not necessarily true that
I^{k}
has linear resolution for all
k\geq 1
.

Generalizing the ideal in (
2
), we prove

Theorem D
.

Let
n\geq 6
. For each
3\leq d\leq n{-}3
, there exists a fully-supported squarefree monomial ideal
I\subset S
generated in degree
d
such that
I
has linear quotients and
\textup{reg}\,I^{2}=2d+1
. Hence,
I^{2}
does not have a linear resolution.

We define the class

\mathcal{I}_{n,d}^{*}(K)\ =\ \{I\in\mathcal{I}_{n,d}(K):I\ \textup{has linear powers}\}.

Theorem
D
shows that
\mathcal{I}_{n,d}(K)\neq\mathcal{I}_{n,d}^{*}(K)
for any
3\leq d\leq n{-}3
and any field
K
.

By combining Theorems
A
,
B
,
C
and
D
, we are finally able to deliver the complete solution to Questions
1.1
and
1.2
.

Theorem E
.

The following statements are equivalent.

(a)

\mathcal{I}_{n,d}(K)
does not depend on
K
.

(b)

d\in\{0,1,2,n{-}2,n{-}1,n\}
.

(c)

\mathcal{I}_{n,d}(K)=\mathcal{I}_{n,d}^{*}(K)
.

Furthermore, for any squarefree monomial ideal
I\subset S
generated in a single degree
d
with
d\in\{0,1,2,n{-}2,n{-}1,n\}
, the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

I
has a linear resolution.

(ii)

I
has linear quotients.

(iii)

I^{k}
has linear quotients, for all
k\geq 1
.

In view of the results in this paper, and the existing literature, we are tempted to conjecture that if a (squarefree) monomial ideal
I\subset S
has linear powers, then
I^{k}
has linear quotients for all
k\gg 0
.

2.
Dominant monomial orders and linear quotients

In this section, we introduce the notions of
x
-dominant and
y
-dominant monomial orders and show that for an equigenerated monomial ideal
I
if
\mathcal{R}(I)
satisfies the
x
-condition with respect to either an
x
-dominant or a
y
-dominant monomial order, then all powers of
I
possess linear quotients. We will give nice applications of these results in this and in the subsequent section, where we treat squarefree monomial ideals generated in degree
n-2
.

Let
T=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
be a polynomial ring over the field
K
. We call a monomial order
<
on
T
an
x
-dominant order
, if for any monomials
u,u^{\prime}\in K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
v,v^{\prime}\in K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
, with
\deg(uv)=\deg(u^{\prime}v^{\prime})
, if
u\neq u^{\prime}
then the following is satisfied:

uv>u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\,\Longleftrightarrow\,u>u^{\prime}.

The following monomial orders are examples of
x
-dominant orders on
T
.

(a)

Lexicographical order induced by
x_{1}>\cdots>x_{n}>y_{1}>\cdots>y_{m}
.

(b)

Pure lexicographical order induced by
x_{1}>\cdots>x_{n}>y_{1}>\cdots>y_{m}
.

(c)

Reverse lexicographical order induced by
y_{1}>\cdots>y_{m}>x_{1}>\cdots>x_{n}
.

(d)

The product order of
<_{x}
and
<_{y}
, where
<_{x}
and
<_{y}
are given monomial orders on
K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
, respectively.

Similarly, we call a monomial order
<
on
T
a
y
-dominant order
, if for any monomials
u,u^{\prime}\in K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
v,v^{\prime}\in K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
, with
\deg(uv)=\deg(u^{\prime}v^{\prime})
, if
v\neq v^{\prime}
then the following is satisfied:

uv>u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\,\Longleftrightarrow\,v>v^{\prime}.

The following monomial orders are examples of
y
-dominant orders on
T
.

(a)

Lexicographical order induced by
y_{1}>\cdots>y_{m}>x_{1}>\cdots>x_{n}
.

(b)

Pure lexicographical order induced by
y_{1}>\cdots>y_{m}>x_{1}>\cdots>x_{n}
.

(c)

Reverse lexicographical order induced by
x_{1}>\cdots>x_{n}>y_{1}>\cdots>y_{m}
.

(d)

The product order of
<_{y}
and
<_{x}
, where
<_{y}
and
<_{x}
are given monomial orders on
K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
and
K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, respectively.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal generated in a single degree. If
\mathcal{R}(I)
satisfies the
x
-condition with respect to an
x
-dominant monomial order, then
I^{k}
has linear quotients for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{G}(I)=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\}
, and let
T=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]
be a polynomial ring. Consider the
S
-algebra homomorphism
\varphi:T\rightarrow\mathcal{R}(I)
defined by
\varphi(y_{i})=u_{i}t
for
i=1,\dots,m
and set
J=\textup{Ker}\,\varphi
. By assumption, there exists an
x
-dominant monomial order
<_{d}
on
T
such that
\mathcal{R}(I)
satisfies the
x
-condition with respect to
<_{d}
. Fix an integer
k\geq 1
and assume that
\mathcal{G}(I^{k})=\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}\}
such that

f_{1}>_{d}\cdots>_{d}f_{r}.

(3)

For any
1\leq\ell\leq r
, we set
f_{\ell}^{*}
to be the standard presentation of
f_{\ell}
with respect to
<_{d}
. That is
f_{\ell}^{*}
is the smallest monomial in
K[y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]
with respect to
<_{d}
such that
\varphi(f_{\ell}^{*})=f_{\ell}t^{k}
. Such a monomial
f_{\ell}^{*}
exists, since the monomials
y_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{k}}
satisfying
\varphi(y_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{k}})=f_{\ell}t^{k}
are all of degree
k
and the number of such monomials is finite.

We prove that for any
2\leq j\leq r
, the ideal
L_{j}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{j-1}):f_{j}
is generated by variables. Let
u\in L_{j}
be a monomial. We need to show that there exists a variable
x_{t}\in L_{j}
which divides
u
. Since all
f_{i}
’s are minimal generator of
I^{k}
, we have
u\neq 1
. From
u\in L_{j}
we have
uf_{j}=vf_{i}
for some monomial
v\in S
with
v\neq u
and an integer
i<j
. This gives the relation
h=uf_{j}^{*}-vf_{i}^{*}\in J
.
Since
uf_{j}=vf_{i}
and
f_{i}>_{d}f_{j}
, we obtain
u=(vf_{i})/f_{j}>_{d}v
. This together with the facts that
<_{d}
is an
x
-dominant order and
\deg(uf_{j}^{*})=\deg(vf_{i}^{*})
implies that
\textup{in}_{<_{d}}(h)=uf_{j}^{*}
. Since
\mathcal{R}(I)
satisfies the
x
-condition with respect to
<_{d}
, there exists a monomial
w\in K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
with
\deg(w)\leq 1
and a monomial
g\in K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
such that
w\mid u
and
g\mid f_{j}^{*}
and
wg\in\textup{in}_{<_{d}}(J)
is a minimal generator. Let
h^{\prime}=wg-w^{\prime}g^{\prime}\in J
be a binomial with
\textup{in}_{<_{d}}(h^{\prime})=wg
, where
w^{\prime}\in K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
g^{\prime}\in K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
are monomials. Clearly,
\gcd(w,w^{\prime})=1
and
\deg(g)=\deg(g^{\prime})
. Then comparing the degrees of monomials in the equality
w\varphi(g)=\varphi(wg)=\varphi(w^{\prime}g^{\prime})=w^{\prime}\varphi(g^{\prime})
and using the assumption that
I
is generated in one degree, we obtain
\deg(w)=\deg(w^{\prime})
.

First we show that
w\neq 1
. Suppose on the contrary that
w=1
. Since
\deg(w)=\deg(w^{\prime})
, we get
w^{\prime}=1
. Hence,
h^{\prime}=g-g^{\prime}\in J

\textup{in}_{<_{d}}(h^{\prime})=g
. Thus

So

. This together with

contradicts the fact that

is the standard presentation of
f_{j}
with respect to
<_{d}
. Thus

.

Since
\deg(w)\leq 1

for some

, and

for some

. Since

. From

, and that

. Moreover,

x_{s}f_{j}^{*}-x_{t}(f_{j}^{*}/g)g^{\prime}=(f_{j}^{*}/g)[x_{s}g-x_{t}g^{\prime}]=(f_{j}^{*}/g)h^{\prime}\in J.

(4)

Since
I
is generated in one degree and
(f_{j}^{*}/g)g^{\prime}
is of degree

, there exists an integer

such that
\varphi((f_{j}^{*}/g)g^{\prime})=f_{p}t^{k}=\varphi(f_{p}^{*})
. Then from
(f_{j}^{*}/g)g^{\prime}-f_{p}^{*}\in J
and (

.

x_{s}f_{j}=x_{t}f_{p}
. From

. By (

First we recall some concepts which will be used in the next result. Let

of length

.
The walk

For an even closed walk

of

v\in\mathcal{G}(J)
we pick a monomial
v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(L)
that divides
v
. Then we set
\varphi(u)=\varphi(y_{i}v)=v^{\prime}
. For each non-empty subset
\Omega
of
\mathcal{G}(W)
it is then clear that

\textup{lcm}(u:u\in\Omega)\ \in\ (y_{d-2},\dots,y_{n-6})\textup{lcm}(\varphi(u):u\in\Omega)\subset\mathfrak{m}(\textup{lcm}(\varphi(u):u\in\Omega)),

because
\textup{lcm}(u:u\in\Omega)
is divided by
y_{i}
for some
i
with
d-2\leq i\leq n-6
, while
\textup{lcm}(\varphi(u):u\in\Omega)
is a monomial in the variables
a,b,c,d,e,f
. Hence, (
9
) is indeed a Betti splitting.

Since the supports of
(y_{d-2},\dots,y_{n-6})
and
L
are disjoint, and both ideals have linear resolutions, it follows that
\textup{reg}\,(y_{d-2},\dots,y_{n-6})L=3
. Similarly, for the same reason, we have that
\textup{reg}\,W=\textup{reg}\,J+1
. Consequently,

\textup{reg}\,H\ =\ \max\{\textup{reg}\,J,\,\textup{reg}\,(y_{d-2},\dots,y_{n-6})L,\,\textup{reg}\,W-1\}\ =\ \max\{\textup{reg}\,J,\,3\}.

Now, formula (
8
) yields the assertion.

∎

Proof of Theorem
D
.

As before, we rename the variables from
x_{1}
up to
x_{6}
with
a,b,c,d,e,f
and the variables
x_{7},\dots,x_{n}
with
y_{1},\dots,y_{n-6}
. Let
J
be the ideal in equation (
2
). Sturmfels
[
23
]
has shown that
J
has linear quotients with respect to its minimal generators in the given order, but
J^{2}
does not have a linear resolution.

Now, let
L
be the ideal generated by all squarefree monomials of degree 2 in the variables
\{a,b,c,d,e,f\}
. Notice that
L
has linear quotients, hence a linear resolution, and
J
is contained in
L
. Fix
d
with
3\leq d\leq n-3
, and set

I\ =\ (\prod_{i=1}^{d-3}y_{i})[J+(y_{d-2},y_{d-1},y_{d},\dots,y_{n-6})L].

We claim that
I
is the desired ideal. Let
H=J+(y_{d-2},y_{d-1},y_{d},\dots,y_{n-6})L
. Notice that
I
is a squarefree, fully-supported monomial ideal generated in degree
d
and
I
has linear quotients if and only if
H
has linear quotients. By
[
9
, Lemma 4.7(b)]
,
(y_{d-2},\dots,y_{n-6})L
has linear quotients, say with linear quotients order
u_{1},\dots,u_{r}
. Let
v_{1}=def
,
v_{2}=cef
,
v_{3}=cdf
,
v_{4}=cde
,
v_{5}=bef
,
v_{6}=bcd
,
v_{7}=acf
,
v_{8}=ade
. As remarked before,
v_{1},\dots,v_{8}
is a linear quotients order of
J
. We claim that

u_{1},\dots,u_{r},v_{1},\dots,v_{8}

is a linear quotients order of
H
. For all
i=2,\dots,r
, the ideal
(u_{1},\dots,u_{i-1}):u_{i}
is generated by variables. Let
1\leq i\leq 8
, we claim that
(u_{1},\dots,u_{r},v_{1},\dots,v_{i-1}):v_{i}
is also generated by variables. Since
(v_{1},\dots,v_{i-1}):v_{i}
is generated by variables, it is enough to show that
(u_{1},\dots,u_{r}):v_{i}
is also generated by variables. Since
v_{i}\in J\subset L
and the variables
y_{j}
do not divide
v_{i}
nor any minimal generator of
L
, it follows that

(u_{1},\dots,u_{r}):v_{i}=(y_{d-2},\dots,y_{n-6})(L:v_{i})=(y_{d-2},\dots,y_{n-6}),

as desired. This shows that
H
, and therefore
I
, has indeed linear quotients.

Finally, we claim that
I^{2}
does not have a linear resolution. Indeed, the restriction (see
[
19
, Lemma 4.4]
) of the ideal

I^{2}\ =\ (\prod_{i=1}^{d-3}y_{i})^{2}J^{2}+(\prod_{i=1}^{d-3}y_{i})(y_{d-2},\dots,y_{n-6})JL+(y_{d-2},\dots,y_{n-6})^{2}L^{2}

with respect to the set of variables
\{a,b,c,d,e,f,y_{1},\dots,y_{d-3}\}
is the ideal
(\prod_{i=1}^{d-3}y_{i})J^{2}
.

Since
J^{2}
does not have a linear resolution,
(\prod_{i=1}^{d-3}y_{i})J^{2}
does not have a linear resolution. By
[
19
, Lemma 4.4]
,
I^{2}
can not have a linear resolution, as desired.

∎

As a consequence of Theorem
E
we obtain

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
I\subset S
be a squarefree monomial ideal with
|\textup{supp}(I)|\leq 5
. Then

(a)

The linear resolution property for
I
does not depend on the field
K
.

(b)

The linear powers property for
I
does not depend on the field
K
.

Moreover,
I
has a linear resolution if and only if
I^{k}
has linear quotients for all
k
.

In particular, a squarefree monomial ideal
I
for which
I
has a linear resolution and some power of
I
does not have a linear resolution should satisfy
|\textup{supp}(I)|\geq 6
.

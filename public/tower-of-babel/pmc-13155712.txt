Severe Irritant Contact Dermatitis to Cyperus Scariosus: A Side Effect of Ayurvedic Plava
Authors: Aradhana Rout, Karthi Kishore
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

Ayurveda refers to an ancient and traditional system of medicine native to India, which has gained global popularity in recent times. It is preferred by people in India due to the common belief among the population of the medicines being more ‘natural, safe and less side effects’ in contrary to allopathic preparations. The various medicinal products used in ayurveda are plant/heavy metal based and not tested for their efficacy, safety, or dosage before use. Though adverse reactions to topical and systemic ayurvedic medications are observed, there is a paucity of literature on it. We report an unusual case of severe irritant contact dermatitis to plant extract of Cyperus scariosus, which is a common ingredient in various ayurvedic concoctions.

Case Report

Case 1

A 34-year-old man presented with complaints of sudden onset blackish discolouration of both lower limbs. The patient gave a history of itching over both lower limbs of one-month duration, which was well controlled with topical emollients until he consulted a local indigenous healer who advised him to apply Plava, an ayurvedic concoction made from the leaves and seeds of Motha (Cyperus scariosus). The patient developed a burning sensation along with fluid-filled blisters over the site of the application of the concoction. The blisters burst open in the next 48 hours, leaving behind sheets of black discolouration over both lower limbs [Figure 1]. The patient gave no h/o insect bite or drug intake prior to the onset of lesions. A patch test was performed with the ayurvedic medication, which showed a positive result (2+) after 48 hours [Figure 2]. There was a complete resolution of lesions on discontinuing the drug and initiation of topical steroids and emollients.

Case 2

A 38-year-old woman presented with complaints of itchy oozy bald patch over the scalp of two-day duration. Patient had a small itchy scaly lesion over the scalp one week back, for which she was advised local application of Plava by a local healer. She developed a burning sensation along with fluid-filled blisters over the area, few hours after the application of the herbal medication. Over the next 24 hours, the blisters burst open to form a black scab. On examination, the patient had a large, crusted plaque, removal of which revealed an alopecic patch over the right temporoparietal region [Figure 3]. The patient had erythematous indurated plaques over the forehead with periorbital swelling on the right side. She denied consent to patch test due to apprehension. There was a complete resolution of lesions on discontinuing the drug and initiation of topical steroids and emollients.

Discussion

Indigenous medicine comprises traditional knowledge accumulated over generations by Vaidyas and Hakims. Plava is a technique in Ayurveda in which the seeds and leaves of Cyperus scariosus, Cyperus bulbosus, or Cyperus rotundus are used to treat various autoimmune skin conditions, such as psoriasis and alopecia [Figure 4]. This technique is practised in many parts of northern India, particularly Jammu and Kashmir. Cyperus scariosus (Nagarmotha in local vernacular) is an invasive weed belonging to the family Cyperaceae, often seen in swampy tropical areas.[1] It is a vital ingredient in prescriptions used in indigenous medicine to treat diarrhoea, epilepsy, and sexually transmitted diseases, such as gonorrhoea and syphilis. Essential oil of Cyperus is considered to have a potent plant regulator, which is often used to treat alopecia areata.[2] The oil contains terpenoids, sesquiterpenes, hydrocarbons, steroidal saponins, ketones and flavonoids, which may be attributed to the contact dermatitis.[34] This case report aims to sensitize the clinicians to consider the cutaneous effects of indigenous medicines while managing cases of contact dermatitis in the Indian subcontinent. Moreover, it also provides insight into the adverse effects of ayurvedic medicines, which are usually considered safe by the common population.

Normalized solutions for NLS equations with potential on bounded domains: Ground states and multiplicity
He Zhang, Haibo Chen, Shuai Yao, Juntao Sun

Abstract
We investigate normalized solutions for a class of nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equations with potential V V and inhomogeneous nonlinearity g ⁡ ( | u | ) ​ u = | u | q − 2 ​ u + β ​ | u | p − 2 ​ u g(|u|)u=|u|^{q-2}u+\beta|u|^{p-2}u on a bounded domain Ω \Omega . Firstly, when 2 + 4 N < q < p ≤ 2 ∗ := 2 ​ N N − 2 2+\frac{4}{N}<q<p\leq 2^{*}:=\frac{2N}{N-2} and β = − 1 \beta=-1 , under an explicit smallness assumption on V V , we prove the existence of a global minimum solution and a high-energy solution if the mass is large enough. For this case we do not require that Ω \Omega is star-shaped, which partly solves an open problem by Bartsch et al. [Math. Ann. 390 (2024) 4813–4859]. Moreover, we find that the global minimizer also exists although the nonlinearity is L 2 L^{2} -supercritical. Secondly, when 2 < q < 2 + 4 N < p = 2 ∗ 2<q<2+\frac{4}{N}<p=2^{*} and β = 1 \beta=1 , under the smallness and some extra assumptions on V V , we prove the existence of a ground state and a high-energy solution if Ω \Omega is star-shaped and the mass is small enough. It seems to be new in the study of normalized ground state in the context of the Brézis-Nirenberg problem, even for the autonomous case of V ⁡ ( x ) ≡ 0 V(x)\equiv 0 .

NLS equations; Ground states; Multiplicity; The monotonicity trick

Introduction

Our starting point is to consider standing waves of the NLS equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i\partial_{t}\Phi+\Delta\Phi-V(x)\Phi+g(|\Phi|)\Phi=0,&\ \forall(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\Omega,\\
\Phi(t,x)=0,&\text{ }\forall(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}(N\geq 1)
is a bounded domain,
\Phi=\Phi(t,x)\in\mathbb{C}
is a wave function and
V(x)
is an external potential. It is well known
that the NLS equation is a fundamental equation in quantum mechanics, which
appears in different physical contexts, such as the nonlinear optics
[
1
,
15
]
and the theory of Bose-Einstein condensation
[
2
,
13
,
16
]
.

As we all know, two quantities are conserved along trajectories of (
1
):
the energy

\Sigma(\Phi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\Phi|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}V(x)|\Phi|^{2}dx-\int_{\Omega}G(|\Phi|^{2})dx

with
G(s)=\int_{0}^{s}g(|t|)tdt,
and the mass

M(\Phi)=\int_{\Omega}|\Phi|^{2}dx.

A standing wave is a solution of the form
\Phi(t,x)=e^{-i\lambda t}u(x)
,
where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the real valued function
u
satisfies
the elliptic equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+V(x)u+\lambda u=g(|u|)u,&\ \text{in }\Omega,\\
u(x)=0,&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(2)

To study solutions of (
2
), one can either consider the frequency
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
to be given, or to be an unknown of the problem. In the latter case,
\lambda
appears as a Lagrange multiplier and
L^{2}
-norms of solutions
are prescribed, i.e.

\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}dx=a>0,

(3)

which are usually called normalized solutions. This study seems to be
particularly meaningful from the physical point of view, since standing
waves of (
1
) conserve their mass along time.

After the pioneering contribution by Jeanjean
[
17
]
, the study of
normalized solutions for NLS equation or system on the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
has attracted much attention in recent years regarding different
types of nonlinearities. We refer the readers to
[
7
,
5
,
6
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
27
,
28
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
]
, in which some methods related to the Pohozaev identity were introduced, such as the mountain pass theorem
and the Pohozaev manifold, since
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is invariant under
translation and dilation.

Compared to the study of normalized solutions in the case of the whole
space, there seem only a few results focusing on normalized solutions in the
case of bounded domains. In fact, these two settings are rather different in
nature: each one requires a specific approach, and the results are in
general not comparable. For example, the Pohozaev manifold is not
available when working on bounded domains
\Omega
, since the invariance
under translation and dilation is lost. Up to our knowledge, the first
attempt to deal with this case was made by Noris, Tavares and Verzini
[
21
]
, where a local minimizer and a mountain pass solution of (
2
)–(
3
) were found when
\Omega
is a ball,
V(x)\equiv 0
and
the pure power nonlinearity
g(|u|)u=|u|^{p-2}u
is Sobolev subcritical,
i.e.
2<p<2^{\ast}:=\frac{2N}{N-2}
. Subsequently, the existence of a local
minimizer was extended to the Sobolev subcritical case on generic bounded
domains
[
23
]
, and to the Sobolev critical case (
p=2^{\ast}
) on
bounded domains
[
22
]
. Moreover, the existence of a mountain pass
solution was extended to the Sobolev critical case on bounded domains
[
24
]
and on star-shaped bounded domains
[
29
]
almost simultaneously.
Also, in
[
29
]
the authors proved that the local minimizer obtained in

[
23
]
is a ground state with positive energy on star-shaped bounded
domains. Note that in all these papers the equation is required to be
autonomous.

In recent two papers
[
4
,
25
]
, normalized solutions to NLS equations
with potential and inhomogeneous nonlinearity were studied. In particular,
Bartsch, Qi and Zou
[
4
]
obtained the existence and multiplicity
results for (
2
)–(
3
) on large bounded star-shaped domains
when
V(x)
is a bounded potential and
g(|u|)u=|u|^{q-2}u+\beta|u|^{p-2}u

with
2<p<q^{\ast}:=2+\frac{4}{N}<q<2^{\ast}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
.
Due to the presence of the potential and the bounded domain,
the standard approach related to the Pohozaev identity cannot be used.
In view of this, they applied the monotonicity trick developed by
[
8
,
9
]
.

Inspired by
[
4
,
25
]
, in this paper we are likewise interested in
looking for normalized solutions to a class of NLS equations with potential
and inhomogeneous combined nonlinearity on bounded domains. However,
unlike the study conducted in
[
4
,
25
]
, this work aims to find a ground
state and a high-energy solution of (
2
)–(
3
) under two
types of combined supercritical nonlinearities different from those in
[
4
,
25
]
. Moreover, we are also dedicated to relaxing the restriction on the
star-shaped domains, which can partly solve an open problem raised in
[
4
]
. Specifically, for
a>0,
the problem considered in this study is as
follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+V(x)u+\lambda u=|u|^{q-2}u+\beta|u|^{p-2}u&\ \text{in}\ \Omega_{r},\\
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{r}),\ \int_{\Omega_{r}}|u|^{2}dx=a.&\end{array}\right.

(4)

where
\Omega_{r}:=\{rx\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x\in\Omega\}
with
r>0
and
\Omega
being a bounded domain, and
V(x)
is a potential on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The exponents
p,q
and the parameter
\beta
satisfy either
q^{\ast}<q<p\leq 2^{\ast}
and
\beta=-1,
or
2<q<q^{\ast}<p=2^{\ast}

and
\beta=1.

Solutions of problem (
4
) can be identified with critical points of
the energy functional
I_{r}:H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{r})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

defined by

I_{r}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{r}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{r}}V(x)u^{2}dx-\frac{1}{q}\int_{\Omega_{r}}|u|^{q}dx-\frac{\beta}{p}\int_{\Omega_{r}}|u|^{p}dx

on the constraint

S_{r,a}:=\left\{u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{r}):\int_{\Omega_{r}}|u|^{2}dx=a\right\}.

To find normalized solutions of problem (
4
), we have to face many difficulties, and new ideas and
techniques need to be explored. More details will be discussed in the next
subsection.

1.1
Main results

Before stating our main results, we introduce some notations and assumptions.

Definition 1.1

We say that a solution
u\in S_{r,a}
of problem (
4
) is a
ground state if it possesses the minimal energy among all solutions in
S_{r,a}
, i.e. if

I_{r}(u)=\inf\left\{I_{r}(v):v\in S_{r,a},\text{ }(I_{r}|_{S_{r,a}})^{\prime}(v)=0\right\}.

Let
s_{+}=\max\{s,0\}
and
s_{-}:=\min\{s,0\}
for
s\in\mathbb{R}.

The Aubin-Talenti constant
[
3
]
is denoted by
S
, that is,
S
is the
best constant in the Sobolev embedding
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{2^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Denote
C_{s}
be the
best constant in the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

\|u\|_{s}^{s}\leq C_{s}\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{2s-N(s-2)}{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\frac{N(s-2)}{2}}\text{ for }2<s<2^{\ast}.

Note that
C_{2^{\ast}}=S^{-2^{\ast}/2}
. Let
\theta
be the principal
eigenvalue of
-\Delta
with Dirichlet boundary conditions in
\Omega
and

|\Omega|
be the volume of
\Omega.
Set

a_{V}:=\left[\frac{\theta(N(p-2)-4)(1+\|V\|_{N/2}S^{-1})}{N(p-q)}\right]^{\frac{N}{2}}\left[\frac{2qC_{p}\theta^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}}|\Omega|^{\frac{q-2}{2}}(N(p-2)-4)}{p(N(q-2)-4)}\right]^{\frac{N(q-2)-4}{2(p-q)}}

and

\widetilde{a}_{V}:=\left[\frac{4(1-\|V_{-}\|_{N/2}S^{-1})}{N(2N-q(N-2))}\right]^{\frac{4N}{(N-2)(N(q-2)-4)}}\left[\frac{16q}{C_{q}N(q-2)(4-N(q-2))(N-2)S^{2^{\ast}/2}}\right]^{\frac{4N}{(N-2)(2N-q(N-2))}}

Assume that the potential
V(x)
satisfies the following assumption:

(V_{0})

V\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{N/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is
bounded and
\|V_{-}\|_{N/2}<S.

We now summarize our main results.

Theorem 1.2

Let
N\geq 3,q^{\ast}<q<p\leq 2^{\ast},\beta=-1
and
\Omega
be a bounded smooth domain. Assume that condition
(V_{0})
holds. Then
for any
a>a_{V},
the following statements are true.

(i)

There exists
r_{a}>0
such that for any
r>r_{a},
problem (
4
) has a mountain pass type solution
u_{r}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{r})

satisfying
u_{r}>0
in
\Omega_{r}
and
I_{r}(u_{r})>0
for some Lagrange
multiplier
\lambda_{r}\in\mathbb{R}
.

(ii)

There exists
\overline{r}_{a}>0
such that for any
r>\overline{r}_{a},
problem (
4
) has a global minimum type solution
\overline{u}_{r}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{r})
satisfying
\overline{u}_{r}>0
in
\Omega_{r}
and
I_{r}(\overline{u}_{r})<0
for some Lagrange multiplier
\overline{\lambda}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular,

\|\nabla\overline{u}_{r}\|_{2}\geq\left[\frac{2q}{N(q-2)C_{q}}\left(1-\|V_{-}\|_{N/2}S^{-1}\right)a^{\frac{q(N-2)-2N}{4}}\right]^{\frac{2}{N(q-2)-4}}.

Moreover, if in addition
V\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfies
q\|\widetilde{V}_{+}\|_{N/2}+N(q-2)\|V_{+}\|_{N/2}<S(2N-q(N-2))
,
here
\widetilde{V}(x):=\nabla V(x)\cdot x,
then we have
\lim\inf\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\overline{\lambda}_{r}>0
.

(iii)

There exists
\vartheta_{a}>0
such that

\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\max_{x\in\Omega_{r}}u_{r}<\vartheta_{a}\ \text{and}\ \limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\max_{x\in\Omega_{r}}\overline{u}_{r}<\vartheta_{a}.

Remark 1.3

Clearly,
\overline{u}_{r}
is a ground state and
u_{r}
is a
high-energy solution of problem (
4
).

To prove the solution
u_{r}
with positive energy in Theorem
1.2
, we
have to face two difficulties. The first one is how to construct a bounded
(PS)-sequence at the mountain pass level, since
\Omega
is not scaling
invariant. To overcome this issue, an effective strategy is to use the
montonicity trick without blow-up analysis developed by
[
8
,
9
]
. The
second one is how to obtain the uniform boundedness of the solutions of the
modified equation on the parameter. We note that it can be solved by
assuming that
\Omega
is star-shaped in
[
4
]
. However, in our proof
we can prove this claim only by describing the geometric properties of
the energy functional related to the modified equation, not assuming that
\Omega
is star-shaped, which partly solves an open problem raised in
[
4
]
.

The second solution
\overline{u}_{r}
with negative level in Theorem
1.2
is obtained as a global minimizer of the energy functional
I_{r}

via the minimization method. This means that the global minimizer also
exists although the nonlinearity is
L^{2}
-supercritical, which is an
unexpected result.

Next, we turn to consider another case.

, the solution

Theorem 1.5

Let
N\geq 3,2<q<q^{\ast}<p=2^{\ast},\beta=1
and
\Omega

be a bounded smooth star-shaped domain with respect to

. Assume that

(V_{0})

\widehat{r}_{a}>0

Remark 1.6

(i)
By Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
, we extend the results in

[
4
, Theorems 1.3 and 1.4]
to the Sobolev critical case.

(ii)
By comparing Theorem
1.2
with Theorems
1.4
and

, we find that the mass

To find the solution

in Theorem

, we likewise

obtained by Theorem

First of all, we study the mountain pass level by using the monotonicity

(\widehat{\lambda}_{r,s},\widehat{u}_{r,s})
for almost every
s\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]
. In particular, there hold
\widehat{u}_{r,s}>0
and
I_{r,s}(\widehat{u}_{r,s})=\widetilde{M}_{r,s}(a)
.

Proof.
According to Theorem
2.2
and Lemma
3.2
, for almost every
s\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]
, there exists a bounded Palais-Smale sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset S_{r,a}
satisfying
\widetilde{I}_{r,s}(u_{n})\rightarrow\widetilde{M}_{r,s}(a)
and
\widetilde{I}_{r,s}^{\prime}(u_{n})|_{T_{u_{n}}S_{r,a}}\rightarrow 0
, where
T_{u_{n}}S_{r,a}
denote
the tangent space of
S_{r,a}
at
u_{n}
. Up to a subsequence, we assume
that

u_{n}\rightharpoonup\widehat{u}_{r,s}\ \text{in}\ H^{1}(\Omega_{r})\ \text{and}\ u_{n}\rightarrow\widehat{u}_{r,s}\ \text{in}\ L^{t}(\Omega_{r})\ \text{for all}\ 2\leq t<2^{\ast}.

It is easy to show that
\widehat{u}_{r,s}\in S_{r,a}
is a critical point
of
\widetilde{I}_{r,s}
constrained on
S_{r,a}
. For convince, we set
\widehat{w}_{n}=u_{n}-\widehat{u}_{r,s}
. Since
\widetilde{I}_{r,s}^{\prime}(u_{n})|_{T_{u_{n}}S_{r,a}}\rightarrow 0
, there exists
\lambda_{n}
such
that
\widetilde{I}_{r,s}^{\prime}(u_{n})-\lambda_{n}u_{n}\rightarrow 0
.
Let
\widehat{\lambda}_{r,s}
be the lagrange multiplier correspond to
\widehat{u}_{r,s}
. Note that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega_{r}}V(x)u_{n}^{2}dx=\int_{\Omega_{r}}V(x)\widehat{u}_{r,s}^{2}dx.

Then it follows from the Brézis-Lieb Lemma that

\int_{\Omega_{r}}|\nabla\widehat{w}_{n}|^{2}dx=s\int_{\Omega_{r}}|\widehat{w}_{n}|^{2^{\ast}}dx+o_{n}(1)

and

\widetilde{I}_{r,s}(u_{n})=\widetilde{I}_{r,s}(\widehat{u}_{r,s})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{r}}|\nabla\widehat{w}_{n}|^{2}dx-\frac{s}{2^{\ast}}\int_{\Omega_{r}}|\widehat{w}_{n}|^{2^{\ast}}dx+o_{n}(1).

We assume that
\int_{\Omega_{r}}|\nabla\widehat{w}_{n}|^{2}dx\rightarrow s\ell\geq 0
and
\int_{\Omega_{r}}|\widehat{w}_{n}|^{2^{\ast}}dx\rightarrow\ell\geq 0
. From the definition of
S
we deduce that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega_{r}}|\nabla\widehat{w}_{n}|^{2}dx\geq S\left(\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega_{r}}|\widehat{w}_{n}|^{2^{\ast}}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{\ast}}},

which shows that
s\ell\geq S\ell^{\frac{2}{2^{\ast}}}
. We claim that
\ell=0
. Suppose on the contrary that
\ell>0
, then
\ell\geq S^{\frac{N}{2}}s^{-\frac{N}{2}}
. This implies that

\widetilde{I}_{r,s}(\widehat{w}_{n})\geq\frac{1}{N}S^{\frac{N}{2}}s^{\frac{2-N}{2}}+o_{n}(1)

and hence

\widetilde{I}_{r,s}(u_{n})\geq\widetilde{m}_{r,s}(a)+\frac{1}{N}S^{\frac{N}{2}}s^{\frac{2-N}{2}}+o_{n}(1).

From Lemma
3.4
, we deduce that
\widetilde{I}_{r,s}(u_{n})+o_{n}(1)=\widetilde{M}_{r,s}(a)<\widetilde{m}_{r,s}(a)+\frac{1}{N}S^{\frac{N}{2}}s^{\frac{2-N}{2}}
. This is a contradiction. Therefore,
\ell=0
and then
u_{n}\rightarrow\widehat{u}_{r,s}
in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{r})
. The proof
is complete.

Lemma 3.6

Assume that
\Omega
is a star-shaped bounded domain.
Let
a>0
be fixed, the set of solutions
\widehat{u}_{r,s}\in S_{r,a}
of problem (
39
) is bounded uniformly on
s
and
r
.

Proof.
Since
\widehat{u}_{r,s}\in S_{r,a}
is the solution of problem (
39
)
satisfying
\widetilde{M}_{r,s}(a)=\widetilde{I}_{r,s}(\widehat{u}_{r,s})

the following Pohozaev identity holds:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\int_{\Omega_{r}}|\nabla\widehat{u}_{r,s}|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{2N}\int_{\partial\Omega_{r}}|\nabla\widehat{u}_{r,s}|^{2}(x\cdot n)d\delta-\frac{1}{2N}\int_{\Omega_{r}}\widetilde{V}(x)|\widehat{u}_{r,s}|^{2}dx

(51)

where
n
denotes the outward unit normal vector on
\partial\Omega_{r}
.
Then by (
40
) and (
51
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\int_{\Omega_{r}}|\nabla\widehat{u}_{r,s}|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{2N}\int_{\partial\Omega_{r}}|\nabla\widehat{u}_{r,s}|^{2}(x\cdot n)d\delta-\frac{1}{2N}\int_{\Omega_{r}}\widetilde{V}(x)|\widehat{u}_{r,s}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{N-2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{r}}|\nabla\widehat{u}_{r,s}|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{r}}V(x)|\widehat{u}_{r,s}|^{2}dx-\widetilde{M}_{r,s}(a)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{s(q-2^{\ast})}{2q}\int_{\Omega_{r}}|\widehat{u}_{r,s}|^{q}dx.

Using this, together with
\Omega_{r}
being a star-shaped domain, leads to

\displaystyle\frac{2}{N-2}\widetilde{M}_{r,s}(a)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{N-2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{r}}|\nabla\widehat{u}_{r,s}|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{r}}V(x)\widehat{u}_{r,s}^{2}dx\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{N}\int_{\Omega_{r}}|\nabla\widehat{u}_{r,s}|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2N}\int_{\Omega_{r}}(\nabla V\cdot x)\widehat{u}_{r,s}^{2}dx

\displaystyle-\frac{(2^{\ast}-q)}{2qS^{\frac{2^{\ast}}{2}}}a^{\frac{2q-N(q-2)}{4}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{r}}|\nabla\widehat{u}_{r,s}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{N(q-2)}{4}},

which implies that

is bounded uniformly in

and

by Lemma
3.2

(ii)
. The proof is complete.

0<a<\widetilde{a}_{V}

4
) admits a solution

Interaction-Driven Intervalley Coherence with Emergent Kekulé Orbitons
Hua Chen

Abstract
The p p -orbital doublet in a honeycomb lattice is concretely studied with interacting spinless fermions at half filling. The Dirac fermions with linear dispersion at ± K \pm K valleys govern the non-interacting low-energy physics. In the weak-coupling regime, the Dirac fermions are gaped due to the spontaneous generation of mass terms through a uniform axial orbital ordering, rendering the system into the quantum anomalous Hall insulator phase with a nonzero Chern number. Surprisingly, the intermediate many-particle interaction produces the intervalley coherence between ± K \pm K valleys by developing complex polar orbital orderings in a tripled Wigner-Seitz cell. This phase is shown to have a deep connection with the low-energy physical behavior described by the orbital exchange model in the Mott insulating phase. The classical ground-state manifold in the Mott regime enjoys a continuous symmetry characterized by the intervalley coherent phase. Finally, the quantum fluctuation selects a unique ground state with emergent Kekulé orbitons through the order-by-disorder mechanism. Our findings provide insights for a direction of searching for Kekulé distortion in correlated multi-orbital systems.

Next, the orbital exchange model in the Mott insulating phase
n=1
is constructed through the degenerate perturbation theory. The
p^{1}
configuration with zero energy is an orbital doublet with one fermion occupying either
p_{x}
or
p_{y}
orbital. Note that the charge excitations
\left(p^{1}\right)_{i}\left(p^{1}\right)_{j}\rightleftharpoons\left(p^{2}\right)_{i}\left(p^{0}\right)_{j}
and
\left(p^{1}\right)_{i}\left(p^{1}\right)_{j}\rightleftharpoons\left(p^{0}\right)_{i}\left(p^{2}\right)_{j}
through the inter-site hopping processes between
i

j
-th sites have an energy gap that is proportional to the Hubbard interaction
U
. In the large-
U
regime, the effective low-energy model is described by the second-order hopping process with both the initial and final states in
p^{1}
configuration, which involves no charge gap. Let us derive the orbital exchange interaction along an isolated bond
\bm{e}
connecting
i
-th and
j
-th sites. The inter-site hopping is described by

\hat{T}=\underset{\equiv T^{+}}{\underbrace{\sum_{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}p^{\dagger}_{\mu i}p_{\nu j}}}+\underset{\equiv T^{-}}{\underbrace{\sum_{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}^{*}p^{\dagger}_{\nu j}p_{\mu i}}},

(19)

with
T_{\mu\nu}=\left(t_{\sigma}-t_{\pi}\right)\hat{e}^{\mu}\hat{e}^{\nu}+\delta_{\mu,\nu}t_{\pi}
being the bond-vector dependent hopping due to the anisotropy of
p

[

\mathbb{J}_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}=\sum_{mn}\frac{\left\langle\underset{i\text{-th site}}{\Gamma_{1}^{k}}\underset{j\text{-th site}}{\Gamma_{1}^{l}}\right|T^{+}\left|\underset{i\text{-th site}}{\Gamma_{2}^{m}}\underset{j\text{-th site}}{\Gamma_{0}^{n}}\right\rangle\left\langle\underset{i\text{-th site}}{\Gamma_{2}^{m}}\underset{j\text{-th site}}{\Gamma_{0}^{n}}\right|T^{-}\left|\underset{i\text{-th site}}{\Gamma_{1}^{k^{\prime}}}\underset{j\text{-th site}}{\Gamma_{1}^{l^{\prime}}}\right\rangle}{-E_{\Gamma_{2}^{m}}-E_{\Gamma_{0}^{n}}}+\left(i\leftrightarrow j\right).

(20)

To describe the orbital exchange Hamiltonian, we introduce the orbital pseudospin operators

\{\tau_{+},\tau_{-}\}\equiv\{p^{\dagger}_{x}p_{y},p^{\dagger}_{y}p_{x}\}
,
which flip the states of the orbital doublet in
p^{1}
configuration. The
z

\bm{\tau}
follows through the spin-
1/2

\tau_{z}=\left[\tau_{+},\tau_{-}\right]

A lengthy but straightforward algebra leads to the orbital exchange Hamiltonian

H_{\text{OE}}=J\left[\cos\left(2\theta\right)\tau_{z}^{i}+\sin\left(2\theta\right)\tau_{x}^{i}\right]\left[\cos\left(2\theta\right)\tau_{z}^{j}+\sin\left(2\theta\right)\tau_{x}^{j}\right]+J^{\prime}\bm{\tau}^{i}\cdot\bm{\tau}^{j}

(21)

with

\displaystyle\{J,J^{\prime}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\frac{\left(t_{\sigma}-t_{\pi}\right)^{2}}{2U},\frac{t_{\sigma}t_{\pi}}{U}\}.

(22)

Here the bond vector is parameterized as
\hat{e}=\cos\theta\hat{x}+\sin\theta\hat{y}
.

Finally, the total orbital exchange Hamiltonian by summing all bonds in the honeycomb lattice takes the form

\displaystyle H_{\text{OE}}=J\sum_{\langle ij\rangle\parallel\bm{e}_{\gamma}}\tau^{i}_{\gamma}\tau^{j}_{\gamma}+J^{\prime}\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\bm{\tau}^{i}\cdot\bm{\tau}^{j}

(23)

with the bond dependent terms

\tau_{\gamma}=\tau_{z}\cos\left[2\theta_{\gamma}\right]+\tau_{x}\sin\left[2\theta_{\gamma}\right]\text{ and }\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}\}=\{\frac{1}{6}\pi,\frac{5}{6}\pi,\frac{3}{2}\pi\}.

(24)

Unlike usual spin systems, the first term in the orbital exchange Eq. (
23
) is anisotropic. It roots in the fact that the hopping processes are bond dependent due to the spatial orientation of
p
orbitals.

II
Classical ground-state construction

The classical ground state is obtained by treating the orbital pseudospin
\bm{\tau}
as a classical vector based on the Luttinger-Tisza approach
[
45
]
. In reciprocal space, the orbital exchange Hamiltonian in the spinor basis
\bm{\tau}_{\bm{k}}=\left[\tau_{z\mathcal{A}}^{\bm{k}},\tau_{x\mathcal{A}}^{\bm{k}},\tau_{y\mathcal{A}}^{\bm{k}},\tau_{z\mathcal{B}}^{\bm{k}},\tau_{x\mathcal{B}}^{\bm{k}},\tau_{y\mathcal{B}}^{\bm{k}}\right]
takes the following form

H_{\text{OE}}=\sum_{\bm{k}}\bm{\tau}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\Lambda_{\bm{k}}\bm{\tau}_{\bm{k}},\Lambda_{\bm{k}}=\left[\begin{matrix}0&\mathbb{J}_{\bf k}\\
\mathbb{J}^{\dagger}_{\bf k}&0\end{matrix}\right],

(25)

with

\mathbb{J}_{\bm{k}}=\left[\begin{matrix}\left(\frac{1}{4}J+J^{\prime}\right)\lambda^{+}_{\bm{k}}+J+J^{\prime}&\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}J\lambda^{-}_{\bm{k}}&0\\
\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}J\lambda^{-}_{\bm{k}}&\left(\frac{3}{4}J+J^{\prime}\right)\lambda^{+}_{\bm{k}}+J^{\prime}&0\\
0&0&J^{\prime}\left(\lambda^{+}_{\bm{k}}+1\right)\end{matrix}\right]

(26)

Here, the dispersion
\lambda_{\bm{k}}^{\pm}=\exp\left[ik_{1}\right]\pm\exp\left[ik_{2}\right]\}
with the crystal momentum
k_{i=1,2}=\bm{k}\cdot\bm{a}_{i}
being expressed in terms of the primitive vectors
\{\bm{a}_{1},\bm{a}_{2}\}
. The orbital interaction can be minimized via the diagonalization of the matrix
\Lambda_{\bm{k}}
. As shown in Fig. 5(a) of main text, the lowest eigenvalues of
\Lambda_{\bm{k}}
are found to be twofold degenerate at
\pm K
valleys. The explicit form are as follow

\psi_{\pm K}=\frac{1}{2}\left[1,\pm i,0,-1,\pm i,0\right]^{\text{T}}.

(27)

The coherent superposition of these Bloch waves at
\pm K
valleys

\Psi\left(\bm{r}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[e^{+i\varphi/2}\psi_{+K}\left(\bm{r}\right)+e^{-i\varphi/2}\psi_{-K}\left(\bm{r}\right)\right]

(28)

ensures the real-valued pseudospin
\bm{\tau}
by preserving the time-reversal
\mathcal{T}
symmetry. The ordered orbital pseudospin in Eq. (
28
) is characterized by the azimuth angle
\phi
in
zx
plane as

\bm{\tau}\left(\bm{r}\right)=\cos\phi\tau_{z}+\sin\phi\tau_{x}\text{ with }\phi\left(\bm{r}\right)=\left\{\begin{matrix}-K\cdot\bm{r}-\varphi/2&\text{for sublattice }\mathcal{A}\\
K\cdot\bm{r}+\varphi/2+\pi&\text{for sublattice }\mathcal{B}\end{matrix}\right..

(29)

As a result, the inference of Bloch waves at
\pm K
valleys triples the Wigner-Seitz cell of honeycomb lattice. We remark that the Hamiltonian of Eq. (
25
) in absence of
J^{\prime}
terms is geometrically frustrated with a macroscopic degeneracy of the classical ground-state manifold
[
66
,
67
,
68
,
69
]

III
Linear orbital wave

Having established the classical ground state of the orbital exchange Hamiltonian
H_{\text{OE}}
in Eq. (
28
), we then proceed to derive the Hamiltonian that describes the orbital excitation within the linear spin wave theory
[
46
]
. In order to study the anisotropic orbital interaction, it is convenient to transform to a rotating frame with the
z
axis pointing along the pseudospin ordered direction. On each sublattice of the tripled Wigner-Seitz cell, we may rotate the orbital pseudospin

T_{\alpha}\left(\bm{r}\right)=\sum_{\mu}\hat{e}^{\mu}_{\alpha}\left(\bm{r}\right)\tau_{\mu}\left(\bm{r}\right),

(30)

where the local frame is spanned by the orthonormal triad

\hat{e}_{\alpha}\left(\bm{r}\right)\times\hat{e}_{\beta}\left(\bm{r}\right)=\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\hat{e}_{\gamma}\left(\bm{r}\right).

(31)

The explicit relation between local and global frames can be simply written as a pseudospin rotation about
y
axis

\left[\begin{matrix}T_{z}\\
T_{x}\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}\cos\phi&\sin\phi\\
-\sin\phi&\cos\phi\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}\tau_{z}\\
\tau_{x}\end{matrix}\right].

(32)

The local orbital pseudospin operators, obeying the angular momentum algebra of spin
T=1/2
, can be expressed in terms of Holstein-Primakoff bosons
[
46
]

\displaystyle T_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{T}{2}}\left(a^{\dagger}+a\right),

(33a)

\displaystyle T_{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\sqrt{\frac{T}{2}}\left(a^{\dagger}-a\right),

(33b)

\displaystyle T_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T-a^{\dagger}a.

(33c)

The pseudospin in Eq. (
23
) is first replaced by the above transformation.
Expanding in powers of
T
followed by the Fourier transformation then leads to the
\varphi
-dependent Hamiltonian in terms of Holstein-Primakoff bosons. To illustrate, we explain the orbital exchange along a generic bond
\bm{e}=\cos\theta\hat{x}+\sin\theta\hat{y}
connecting
i
-th and
j
-th sublattices as an example. The Hamiltonian with quadratic order in
T
that accounts for the classical ground-state energy reads

E_{\text{CGS}}=JT^{2}\cos\left(2\theta-\phi_{i}\right)\cos\left(2\theta-\phi_{j}\right)+J^{\prime}T^{2}\cos\left(\phi_{i}-\phi_{j}\right).

(34)

Moreover, the Hamiltonian with the order
T^{3/2}
vanishes identically due to the minimization of orbital interaction after summing over all bonds. Importantly, the linear-order Hamiltonian that describes the orbital fluctuation is given by

\displaystyle H_{\text{LOW}}=

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle T\left[-\alpha\left(a^{\dagger}_{i}a_{i}+a^{\dagger}_{j}a_{j}\right)+\beta\left(a^{\dagger}_{i}a^{\dagger}_{j}+a^{\dagger}_{i}a_{j}+a_{i}a^{\dagger}_{j}+a_{i}a_{j}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle J^{\prime}

\displaystyle T\left[-\alpha^{\prime}\left(a^{\dagger}_{i}a_{i}+a^{\dagger}_{j}a_{j}\right)+\beta^{\prime}_{-}\left(a^{\dagger}_{i}a^{\dagger}_{j}+a_{i}a_{j}\right)+\beta^{\prime}_{+}\left(a^{\dagger}_{i}a_{j}+a_{i}a^{\dagger}_{j}\right)\right]

(35)

with the auxiliary functions
\{\alpha,\beta\}=\{\cos\left(2\theta-\phi_{i}\right)\cos\left(2\theta-\phi_{j}\right),\frac{1}{2}\sin\left(2\theta-\phi_{i}\right)\sin\left(2\theta-\phi_{j}\right)\}

and
\{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}_{\pm}\}=\{\cos\left(\phi_{i}-\phi_{j}\right),\frac{1}{2}\left[\cos\left(\phi_{i}-\phi_{j}\right)\pm 1\right]\}
.
After the Fourier transformation, we arrive at the following Bogolubov-de Gennes form

H_{\text{LOW}}=JT\sum_{\bm{k}}\left\{\left[\begin{matrix}-\alpha&\beta_{-\bm{k}}&0&\beta_{-\bm{k}}\\
\beta_{\bm{k}}&-\alpha&\beta_{\bm{k}}&0\\
0&\beta_{-\bm{k}}&-\alpha&\beta_{-\bm{k}}\\
\beta_{\bm{k}}&0&\beta_{\bm{k}}&-\alpha\\
\end{matrix}\right]+2\alpha\right\}+J^{\prime}T\sum_{\bm{k}}\left\{\left[\begin{matrix}-\alpha^{\prime}&\beta^{\prime}_{+,-\bm{k}}&0&\beta^{\prime}_{-,-\bm{k}}\\
\beta^{\prime}_{+,\bm{k}}&-\alpha^{\prime}&\beta^{\prime}_{-,\bm{k}}&0\\
0&\beta^{\prime}_{-,-\bm{k}}&-\alpha^{\prime}&\beta^{\prime}_{+,-\bm{k}}\\
\beta^{\prime}_{-,\bm{k}}&0&\beta^{\prime}_{+,\bm{k}}&-\alpha^{\prime}\\
\end{matrix}\right]+2\alpha^{\prime}\right\}

(36)

in the spinor basis
\left[a_{i\bm{k}},a_{j\bm{k}},a^{\dagger}_{i-\bm{k}},a^{\dagger}_{j-\bm{k}}\right]
. Here the coefficients
\beta_{\bm{k}}
and
\beta_{\pm,\bm{k}}
are the Fourier transformation of
\beta
and
\beta_{\pm}
, respectively. The linear-order Hamiltonian can be diagonalized via the Bogoliubov transformation
[
70
]

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}a_{\bm{k}}\\
a^{\dagger}_{-\bm{k}}\end{matrix}\right]=T_{\bm{k}}\left[\begin{matrix}b_{\bm{k}}\\
b^{\dagger}_{-\bm{k}}\end{matrix}\right],

(37)

which relates the Holstein-Primakoff bosons with orbital excitation modes.
Notably, the bosonic statistics require that
T_{\bm{k}}
satisfies the para-unitary condition

\displaystyle T^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}}\Sigma_{z}T_{\bm{k}}=\Sigma_{z}\text{ with }\Sigma_{z}=\left[\begin{matrix}\mathbb{I}&0\\
0&-\mathbb{I}\end{matrix}\right].

(38)

Therefore, the linear-orbital-wave Hamiltonian can be written in terms of diagonalized bosons

\displaystyle H_{\text{LOW}}\left(\varphi\right)=\sum_{i\bm{k}}2E_{i\bm{k}}\left(\varphi\right)b^{\dagger}_{i\bm{k}}b_{i\bm{k}}+NE_{\text{ZP}}\left(\varphi\right)

(39)

where the energy from zero-point motion per unit cell is

\displaystyle E_{\text{ZP}}\left(\varphi\right)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\bm{k}}E_{i\bm{k}}\left(\varphi\right)-\frac{9}{2}J.

(40)

Finally, the zero-point energy can be evaluated numerically.

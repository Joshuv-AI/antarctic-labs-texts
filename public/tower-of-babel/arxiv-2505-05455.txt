Calculation of ground state energy of Lithium and Beryllium based on variational method
Mei-Qin Deng, Ren-Hong Fang

Abstract
With the consideration of identity principle and atomic shell structure, we calculated the ground state energy of Lithium atom and Beryllium atom based on variational method, which accords quite well with the experimental results.



I
Introduction

In quantum mechanics, it is hard to solve many-body systems analytically.
For many-electron atoms, variational method is often adopted to calculate
energy levels of atoms approximately
(
Mao and Li 2006
;
Deng et al. 2013
;
Quan and Zheng 2014
;
Yang 2014
;
Zhang et al. 2015
;
Lu and Zheng 2019
;
Yin et al. 2021
;
Pi et al. 2023
)
.
Calculation of ground state energy of Helium atom can be found in
traditional textbooks
(
Qian 2006
;
Zhou and Chen 2022
)
. For Lithium
and Beryllium atoms, the calculation of ground state energy is more
complex than Helium atom
(
Wang 1993
;
Kong 1995
;
Saleh-Jahromi and Moebs 1998
;
Huang et al. 1999
;
Li et al. 2002
;
Wu and Meng 2017
;
Dong 2018
)
.
There are also some papers calculating the energy levels of atoms
with atomic number
Z>4
, where the calculation procedures are extremely
complicated
(
Li et al. 2006
;
Huang et al. 2007
)
. In this article,
we will introduce a simplified variational method, with the consideration
of identity principle and atomic shell structure, to calculate the
ground state energy of Lithium and Beryllium atoms.

Now we briefly introduce the variational method in quantum mechanics.
The Hamiltonian operator of a system is denoted as
\hat{H}
, whose
eigenvalues and corresponding eigenfunctions are listed in the following,

E_{0}<E_{1}<E_{2}<\cdots<E_{n}<\cdots

\psi_{0},\ \psi_{1},\ \psi_{2},\cdots,\ \psi_{n},\cdots

According to the completeness theorem of the eigenfunctions of an
Hermitian operator, any normalised wavefunction
\Phi
can be expanded
by
\{\psi_{n}\}
as follows,

\Phi=\sum_{n}C_{n}\psi_{n}

(1)

The average value of
\hat{H}
in
\Phi
state is

\overline{\hat{H}}=\int dV\Phi^{*}\hat{H}\Phi=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E_{n}|C_{n}|^{2}\geqslant E_{0}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|C_{n}|^{2}=E_{0}

(2)

i.e., the average value of
\hat{H}
in any normalised state is bigger
than the ground state energy
E_{0}
. If we introduce a parameter

\lambda
in the normalised wavefunction
\Phi
(called trial wavefunction),
then the average value of
\hat{H}
in
\Phi
state can be written
as
\overline{\hat{H}}(\lambda)
, which depends on the parameter

\lambda
. Solving
d\overline{\hat{H}}(\lambda)/d\lambda=0
, we
can find the extreme point
\lambda_{0}
, which gives local minimum

\overline{\hat{H}}(\lambda_{0})
. Then
\overline{\hat{H}}(\lambda_{0})

can be chosen as the approximate value of the ground state energy

E_{0}
.

In this article, we will use variational method to calculate the ground
state energy of Lithium and Beryllium atoms. For a neutral atom with
atomic number
Z
, it has
Z
electrons outside of the nucleus.
These electrons are identical, which leads to the antisymmetric wavefunction
of electrons. In this article, the electrons outside of the nucleus
are assumed to move independently in the mean Coulomb field. For
N

electrons in
N
different states, the antisymmetric wavefunction
is

\Phi(1,2,\cdots,N)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N!}}\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}\phi_{i}(1)&\phi_{i}(2)&\cdots&\phi_{i}(N)\\
\phi_{j}(1)&\phi_{j}(2)&\cdots&\phi_{j}(N)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\phi_{k}(1)&\phi_{k}(2)&\cdots&\phi_{k}(N)\end{array}\right|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N!}}\sum_{P}\delta_{P}P\left[\phi_{i}(1)\phi_{j}(2)\cdots\phi_{k}(N)\right]

(3)

where
P
represents a kind of permutation,
\delta_{P}=1
for even
permutation and
\delta_{P}=-1
for odd permutation. Besides the
antisymmetry of wavefunction, the shell structure of atoms is also
considered in this article.

The rest of this article is organized as follows. In Sec. II, the
ground state energy of Lithium atom is calculated. In Sec. III, the
ground state energy of Beryllium atom is calculated. We summarize
this article in Sec. IV. Some calculation details are listed in the
appendix.

II
Ground state energy of Lithium

Lithium atom has three electrons outside of the nucleus.
The heavy nucleus can be set in the coordinate origin. The Hamiltonian
operator of the electrons is

\hat{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{Z}\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla_{i}^{2}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{Z}\left(-\frac{Ze^{2}}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}r_{i}}\right)+\sum_{i<j}\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}r_{ij}}

(4)

where
Z=3
for Lithium atom. For the ground state of Lithium atom,
two electrons are in
1s
state (with spin up and down respectively),
and the third electron is in
2s
state (Suppose spin up). The wavefunctions
of these three states are denoted as
\phi_{a},\phi_{b},\phi_{c}
:

\phi_{a}=\psi_{100}\chi_{+},\ \ \ \phi_{b}=\psi_{100}\chi_{-},\ \ \ \phi_{c}=\psi_{200}\chi_{+}

(5)

The explicit forms of
\psi_{100},\psi_{200}
and
\chi_{+},\chi_{-}

are listed as follows,

\psi_{100}(r)=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda^{3}}{\pi a_{B}^{3}}}e^{-\lambda r/a_{B}},\ \ \ \psi_{200}(r)=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda^{3}}{\pi a_{B}^{\prime 3}}}\left(1-\frac{\lambda r}{a_{B}^{\prime}}\right)e^{-\lambda r/a_{B}^{\prime}}

(6)

\chi_{+}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
0\end{array}\right),\ \ \ \chi_{-}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
1\end{array}\right)

(7)

where the parameter
\lambda
is the effective charge felt by the
three electrons due to screening effect,
a_{B}
is Bohr radius,
and
a_{B}^{\prime}=2a_{B}
.

To calculate the ground state energy of Lithium atom based on variational
method, we must choose a trial wavefunction. The three electrons outside
of the nucleus are identical fermions, so the trial wavefunction should
satisfy exchange antisymmetry. The trial wavefunction (normalised)
can be chosen as

\displaystyle\Psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{3!}}\sum_{P}\delta_{P}P\left[\phi_{a}(1)\phi_{b}(2)\phi_{c}(3)\right]

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{3!}}\bigg[\phi_{a}(1)\phi_{b}(2)\phi_{c}(3)+\phi_{a}(2)\phi_{b}(3)\phi_{c}(1)+\phi_{a}(3)\phi_{b}(1)\phi_{c}(2)

\displaystyle-\phi_{a}(2)\phi_{b}(1)\phi_{c}(3)-\phi_{a}(3)\phi_{b}(2)\phi_{c}(1)-\phi_{a}(1)\phi_{b}(3)\phi_{c}(2)\bigg]

The average value of
\hat{H}
in
\Psi
state is

\overline{\hat{H}}(\lambda)=\int\Psi^{\dagger}\hat{H}\Psi dV_{1}dV_{2}dV_{3}

(9)

Firstly we calculate
\Psi^{\dagger}\Psi
. We adopt following symbol
to simplify calculation:

(ijk)\equiv\phi_{a}(i)\phi_{b}(j)\phi_{c}(k)

(10)

then the trial wavefunction
\Psi
can be rewritten as

\Psi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3!}}[(123)+(231)+(312)-(213)-(321)-(132)]

(11)

The result of
\Psi^{\dagger}\Psi
is

\displaystyle\Psi^{\dagger}\Psi\times 3!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(123)^{2}-(123)(321)+(231)^{2}-(231)(132)+(312)^{2}-(312)(213)

(12)

\displaystyle+(213)^{2}-(213)(312)+(321)^{2}-(321)(123)+(132)^{2}-(132)(231)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\times[(123)^{2}+(231)^{2}+(312)^{2}]

\displaystyle-2\times[(123)(321)+(231)(132)+(312)(213)]

where
(123)(321)
means
(123)^{\dagger}(321)
, and
(123)^{2}

means
(123)^{\dagger}(123)
, etc.

Making use of the virial theorem in quantum mechanics, the average
value of the kinetic energy operators and the potential energy between
nucleus and electron of
\hat{H}
in
\Psi
state can be easily
obtained:

\overline{\sum_{i=1}^{Z}\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla_{i}^{2}\right)}=Z\times\overline{\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla_{1}^{2}\right)}=\frac{3}{8}Z\lambda^{2}\alpha^{2}mc^{2}

(13)

\overline{\sum_{i=1}^{Z}\left(-\frac{Ze^{2}}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}r_{i}}\right)}=Z\times\overline{\left(-\frac{Ze^{2}}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}r_{1}}\right)}=-\frac{3}{4}Z^{2}\lambda\alpha^{2}mc^{2}

(14)

where
\alpha
is fine structure constant and
mc^{2}
is the rest
energy of an electron. In
\Psi
state, the average value of the
potential energy among the three electrons of
\hat{H}
is

\displaystyle\overline{\sum_{i<j}\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}r_{ij}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3\times\overline{\left(\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}r_{12}}\right)}

(15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\hbar c\int dV_{1}dV_{2}\frac{1}{r_{12}}\bigg[\psi_{100}(r_{1})^{2}\psi_{100}(r_{2})^{2}+2\times\psi_{100}(r_{1})^{2}\psi_{200}(r_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle-\psi_{100}(r_{1})\psi_{100}(r_{2})\psi_{200}(r_{1})\psi_{200}(r_{2})\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{5965}{5832}\times\lambda\alpha^{2}mc^{2}

where the three six-dimensional integrals have been calculated in
appendix A. Here we list the three integrals:

\int dV_{1}dV_{2}\frac{1}{r_{12}}\psi_{100}(r_{1})^{2}\psi_{100}(r_{2})^{2}=\frac{5}{8}\times\frac{\lambda}{a_{B}}

(16)

\int dV_{1}dV_{2}\frac{1}{r_{12}}\psi_{100}(r_{1})^{2}\psi_{200}(r_{2})^{2}=\frac{17}{81}\times\frac{\lambda}{a_{B}}

(17)

\int dV_{1}dV_{2}\frac{1}{r_{12}}\psi_{100}(r_{1})\psi_{100}(r_{2})\psi_{200}(r_{1})\psi_{200}(r_{2})=\frac{16}{27^{2}}\times\frac{\lambda}{a_{B}}

(18)

The first two integrals contribute to Coulomb energy, and the third
term comes from exchange effect due to the antisymmetric wavefunction.

Finally we obtain the average value of
\hat{H}
in
\Psi
state
as follows,

\overline{\hat{H}}(\lambda)=\alpha^{2}mc^{2}\times\left(\frac{9}{8}\lambda^{2}-\frac{33\,401}{5832}\lambda\right)

(19)

Setting
d\overline{\hat{H}}(\lambda)/d\lambda=0
, one get

\lambda_{0}=\frac{33\,401}{13\,122}\approx 2.5454

(20)

which gives the local minimum,

\overline{\hat{H}}(\lambda_{0})=-\frac{1\,115\,626\,801}{153\,055\,008}\times\alpha^{2}mc^{2}\approx-198.345\,\mathrm{eV}

(21)

This is the ground state energy of Lithium atom obtained through variational
method, which differs from the experimental value
-203.48\,\mathrm{eV}

by relative error
2.5\%
.

III
Ground state energy of Beryllium

Beryllium atom has four electrons outside of the nucleus.
For the ground state of a Beryllium atom, two electrons are in
1s

state (with spin up and down respectively), and the other two electrons
are in
2s
state (with spin up and down respectively). The wavefunctions
of these four states are denoted as
\phi_{a},\phi_{b},\phi_{c},\phi_{d}
:

\phi_{a}=\psi_{100}\chi_{+},\ \ \ \phi_{b}=\psi_{100}\chi_{-},\ \ \ \phi_{c}=\psi_{200}\chi_{+},,\ \ \ \phi_{d}=\psi_{200}\chi_{-}

(22)

The explicit forms of
\psi_{100},\psi_{200}
and
\chi_{+},\chi_{-}

are the same as Eqs. (
6
)-(
7
). Now the parameter

\lambda
is the effective charge felt by the four electrons due
to screening effect.

The trial antisymmetric wavefunction (normalised) of the four electrons
can be chosen as

\displaystyle\Psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{4!}}\sum_{P}\delta_{P}P\left[\phi_{a}(1)\phi_{b}(2)\phi_{c}(3)\phi_{d}(4)\right]

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{4!}}\bigg[(1234+2314+3124-2134-3214-1324)

\displaystyle-(1243+2341+3142-2143-3241-1342)

\displaystyle+(1423+2431+3412-2413-3421-1432)

\displaystyle-(4123+4231+4312-4213-4321-4132)\bigg]

The average value of
\hat{H}
in
\Psi
state is

\overline{\hat{H}}(\lambda)=\int\Psi^{\dagger}\hat{H}\Psi dV_{1}dV_{2}dV_{3}dV_{4}

(24)

The result of
\Psi^{\dagger}\Psi
is

\displaystyle\Psi^{\dagger}\Psi\times 4!

(25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \ (1234)^{2}+1234(-3214-1432+3412)+(2314)^{2}+2314(-1324-2413+1423)

\displaystyle+(3124)^{2}+3124(-2134-3421+2431)+(2134)^{2}+2134(-3124-2431+3421)

\displaystyle+(3214)^{2}+3214(-1234-3412+1432)+(1324)^{2}+1324(-2314-1423+2413)

\displaystyle+(\mathrm{other\ 72\ terms})

There are totally
24^{2}=576
terms in
\Psi^{\dagger}\Psi
, however
with only
96
terms nonzero. The complete form of
\Psi^{\dagger}\Psi

is listed in Appendix B.

The average value of the kinetic energy operators of
\hat{H}
in

\Psi
state is

\overline{\sum_{i=1}^{Z}\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla_{i}^{2}\right)}=Z\times\overline{\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla_{1}^{2}\right)}=\frac{5}{16}Z\lambda^{2}\alpha^{2}mc^{2}

(26)

with
Z=4
. In
\Psi
state, the average value of the potential
energy between nucleus and the three electrons of
\hat{H}
is

\overline{\sum_{i=1}^{Z}\left(-\frac{Ze^{2}}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}r_{i}}\right)}=Z\times\overline{\left(-\frac{Ze^{2}}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}r_{1}}\right)}=-\frac{5}{8}Z^{2}\lambda\alpha^{2}mc^{2}

(27)

The average value of the potential energy among the four electrons
of
\hat{H}
in
\Psi
state is

\displaystyle\overline{\sum_{i<j}\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}r_{ij}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3\times\overline{\left(\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}r_{12}}\right)}

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 6\alpha\hbar c\times\frac{1}{4!}\int dV_{1}dV_{2}\frac{1}{r_{12}}\bigg[4\times\psi_{100}(r_{1})^{2}\psi_{100}(r_{2})^{2}+4\times\psi_{200}(r_{1})^{2}\psi_{200}(r_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle+16\times\psi_{100}(r_{1})^{2}\psi_{200}(r_{2})^{2}-8\times\psi_{100}(r_{1})\psi_{200}(r_{1})\psi_{100}(r_{2})\psi_{200}(r_{2})\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{586\,373}{373\,248}\times\lambda\alpha^{2}mc^{2}

In appendix A, we have calculated following integral:

\int dV_{1}dV_{2}\frac{1}{r_{12}}\psi_{200}(r_{1})^{2}\psi_{200}(r_{2})^{2}=\frac{77}{512}\times\frac{\lambda}{a_{B}}

(29)

Finally we obtain the average value of
\hat{H}
in
\Psi
state
as follows,

\overline{\hat{H}}(\lambda)=\alpha^{2}mc^{2}\times\left(\frac{5}{4}\lambda^{2}-\frac{3\,146\,107}{373\,248}\lambda\right)

(30)

Setting
d\overline{\hat{H}}(\lambda)/d\lambda=0
, one get

\lambda_{0}=\frac{3\,146\,107}{933\,120}\approx 3.3716

(31)

which gives the local minimum:

\overline{\hat{H}}(\lambda_{0})=-\frac{9\,897\,989\,255\,449}{696\,570\,347\,520}\times\alpha^{2}mc^{2}\approx-386.663\,\mathrm{eV}

(32)

This is the ground state energy of Beryllium atom obtained through
variational method, which differs from the experimental value
-399.14\,\mathrm{eV}

by relative error
3.1\%
.

IV
Summary

A neutral atom with atomic number
Z
has
Z

electrons outside of the nuclear. These electrons are identical, which
leads to the antisymmetry of wavefunction according to identity principle.
With the consideration of antisymmetric wavefunction and atomic shell
structure, theoretical results of the ground state energy of Lithium
atom (
Z=3
) and Beryllium atom (
Z=4
) are obtained through variational
method, which agree with the experimental results quite well. For
atoms with
Z>4
, the calculation of ground state energy is more
complicated than Lithium and Beryllium atoms, and the help from computer
programs may be necessary.

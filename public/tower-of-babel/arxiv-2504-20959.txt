Digraph-defined external difference families and new circular external difference families
Sophie Huczynska, Christopher Jefferson, Struan McCartney

Abstract
External difference families (EDFs) are combinatorial objects which were introduced in the early 2000s, motivated by information security applications; various generalizations have since been defined and investigated, in particular strong EDFs (SEDFs) and circular EDFs (CEDFs). We present a framework based on graphs and digraphs which offers a new unified way to view these structures, and leads to natural new research questions. We present constructions and structural results about these digraph-defined EDFs, and we obtain new explicit constructions for infinite families of CEDFs, in particular ( m ​ l 2 , m , l , 1 ) (ml^{2},m,l,1) -CEDFs. We construct the first infinite family of such CEDFs in non-cyclic abelian groups; these have odd values of m m and l l . We also present the first CEDF in a non-abelian group.



1
Introduction

External difference families (EDFs) are combinatorial objects which were introduced in the early 2000s, motivated by information security applications
[
6
,
14
]
. Several variants of these have been introduced subsequently - e.g. strong external difference families (SEDFs)
[
15
]
, circular external difference families (CEDFs)
[
18
]
and others. Such a family consists of a collection
\mathcal{A}
of disjoint same-size subsets of a group
G
, with the property that the multiset of pairwise differences between elements of certain distinct sets in
\mathcal{A}
contains every non-identity element of
G
the same number of times. This is an external analogue of traditional difference families, which have been studied since the 1930s; for these, the pairwise differences between elements within each set are considered, and their multiset union is required to contain each non-identity element of
G
equally often.

Connections between graphs and certain internal/external difference families are well known, and have been explored for example in
[
4
]
and
[
11
]
. In these papers, the vertices of the graph correspond to the elements of the group
G
and (labelled) edges between them represent the differences. This provides a link to graph decomposition problems. In
[
3
]
, the concept of strong difference family (SDF) is introduced as a collection of multisets such that the multiset union of their internal differences contains every element of
G
(inlcuding the identity) equally often. In
[
4
]
, a generalised definition of SDF introduced, in which the set of internal differences is defined via the edges of a digraph
\Gamma
, and the original definition is retrieved upon taking
\Gamma
to be complete. In
[
11
]
, graph decompositions are considered which correspond directly to certain EDFs in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
via the process of development.

In this paper, we present a different connection between graph theory and external difference families and their generalizations, inspired by - but distinct from - these ideas. We observe that the sets of such a family may be associated with the vertices of a digraph, with directed edges between precisely those pairs of vertices for which the external (directed) differences between the corresponding sets contribute to the multiset of external differences. This provides a natural and useful framework in which to view EDFs and their variants, particularly SEDFs and CEDFs.

We note that a somewhat-related idea arises implicitly in
[
16
]
when an initial directed cycle is constructed, then a blow-up construction is applied to it to create a CEDF in a cyclic group. However our definition does not require any construction-based relationship between the digraph and the sets involved. The “graceful directed graphs” of
[
2
]
give examples of our structures in the specific case when the group is cyclic and every set is a singleton set.

Our work was underpinned by search for examples in GAP
[
8
]
, using a constraint-satisfaction modelling language
[
1
]
and solver
[
9
]
.

The paper is structured as follows: we first set up the necessary background and new definitions. We present constructions and examples of EDFs defined by complete graphs, cycles and complete bipartite graphs (both oriented and undirected). Those defined by oriented cycles (with the natural orientation) correspond to CEDFs, and we present various new results on CEDFs. In particular, we present a new infinite family of
(ml^{2}+1,m,l,1)
-CEDFs in non-cyclic abelian groups; these have both
m
and
l
odd. We also give the first example of a CEDF in a non-abelian group. We end by indicating further research questions emerging from this work.

Background

Throughout,
G
will denote a finite group. Unless otherwise stated, we will write
G

A,B
of
G

\Delta(A,B)=\{a-b:a\in A,b\in B\}

We will frequently work in

[a,b]

whereas

.

The concept of classical external difference family was first defined in
[

]
, motivated by an application to AMD codes:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a group of order
n
and let

. A family of disjoint
l
-sets
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}\}
in

is an
(n,m,l,\lambda)
-EDF if the multiset equation

\bigcup_{\{i,j:i\neq j\}}\Delta(A_{i},A_{j})=\lambda(G\setminus\{0\})
holds.

The strong version was first defined in
[
15
]
, corresponding to a stronger security model.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a group of order
n
and let
m>1
. A family of disjoint
l
-sets
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}\}
in
G
is an
(n,m,l,\lambda)
-SEDF if, for each

, the multiset equation

\bigcup_{\{j:j\neq i\}}\Delta(A_{i},A_{j})=\lambda(G\setminus\{0\})
holds.

Note that a strong EDF will always be an EDF, but the converse need not be true.

Recently, Stinson and Veitch introduced new objects called circular external difference families
[

]
. We note that the definition is still valid when the requirement for

Definition 2.3
.

Let
G
be an additive abelian group of order
n
. Suppose

and
1\leq c\leq m-1
. A family of disjoint
l
-sets
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}\}
in

is an
(n,m,l,\lambda)
-

\bigcup_{i=0}^{m-1}\Delta(A_{i+c\mod m},A_{i})=\lambda(G\setminus\{0\})

If

[

]

We present the following observation which clarifies the structure of

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}\}
be a disjoint collection of
l
-subsets of a group

. Suppose
\mathcal{A}
is a
c
-CEDF. Then, if
\gcd(c,m)=d
, the multiset in the definition may be written as a disjoint union of

multiset unions, each involving

sets, as follows (where all indices are taken modulo

):

\bigcup_{i=0}^{m-1}\Delta(A_{i+c\mod m},A_{i})=\bigcup_{j=0}^{d-1}\left(\bigcup_{i=0}^{(m/d-1)}\Delta(A_{(i+1)c+j},A_{ic+j})\right)=\lambda(G\setminus\{0\})

Proof.

The decomposition of the

is a cycle of length

decomposes as the product of

A strong version of the CEDF has also been defined

]

Definition 2.5
.

Let

3
A new viewpoint

We now introduce a way of defining external difference families and their generalizations in terms of graphs and digraphs.

Throughout this paper, a graph or digraph
G
on
m
vertices will have vertex set
V(G)=\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
. All graphs and digraphs will be finite and simple (no loops or multiple edges), and they will be labelled.

For a digraph
G
, we denote by
\overrightarrow{E}(G)
its set of directed edges. A graph is said to be
oriented
if at most one of
(i,j)
or
(j,i)
is in the directed edge-set for each pair
i\neq j
.

For an undirected graph
G
, we will view it as a digraph, by replacing each undirected edge by a pair of inverse directed edges. We define the following notation. Let the edge-set of
G
as an undirected graph be
E(G)=\{\{i,j\}:i,j\in V(G)\}
; then we define the
directed edge-set

\overrightarrow{E}(G)
of
G
to be

\overrightarrow{E}(G):=\{(i,j)\in V(G)\times V(G):\{i,j\}\in E(G)\}.

Example 3.1
.

Consider
K_{3}
with
V(K_{3})=\{0,1,2\}
and
E(K_{3})=\{\{0,1\},\{1,2\},\{2,0\}\}
; then
\overrightarrow{E}(K_{3})=\{(0,1),(0,2),(1,0),(1,2),(2,0),(2,1)\}
.

All the digraphs which will be considered in this paper will either be oriented digraphs or undirected graphs viewed as digraphs as above.

We are now ready to define our main object of study.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
G
be a group of order
n
, and let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{0},\ldots,A_{m-1})
be an ordered collection of disjoint subsets of
G
, each of size
l
. Let
H
be a labelled digraph on
m
vertices
\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}
and let
\overrightarrow{E}(H)
be the set of directed edges of
H
. Then
\mathcal{A}
is said to be an
(n,m,l,\lambda;H)
-EDF if the following multiset equation holds:

\bigcup_{(i,j)\in\overrightarrow{E}(H)}\Delta(A_{j},A_{i})=\lambda(G\setminus\{0\}).

We will call such a structure a
digraph-defined
EDF. If we wish to emphasise
H
, we will call it an
H
-defined EDF.

We introduce the following notation and labelling for commonly-used graphs and digraphs which will appear in this paper. To distinguish between an undirected and oriented version of a given underlying graph, we denote the oriented version by a superscript
*
.

•

Complete graph
K_{m}
:
V(K_{m})=\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}
and
\overrightarrow{E}(K_{m}):=\{(i,j):0\leq i,j\leq m-1,i\neq j\}
.

•

Oriented complete digraph
K_{m}^{*}
(also known as tournament):
V(K_{m}^{*})=\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}
; the standard set of directed edges will be
\overrightarrow{E}(K_{m}^{*}):=\{(i,j):0\leq i<j\leq m-1\}
(but we will also consider other orientations).

•

Cycle graph
C_{m}
:
V(C_{m})=\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}
and
E(C_{m}):=\{(i,j):j\equiv i+1\mod m\mbox{ or }i\equiv j+1\mod m:0\leq i,j\leq m-1\}
.

•

Oriented cycle
C_{m}^{*}
:
V(C_{m}^{*})=\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}
; the standard set of directed edges will be
\overrightarrow{E}(C_{m}^{*}):=\{(i,i+1\mod m):0\leq i\leq m-1\}
.

•

Complete bipartite graph
K_{a,b}
: bipartition
V(K_{a,b})=A\cup B
where
A=\{0,\ldots,a-1\}
and
B=\{a,\ldots,a+b-1\}
and
\overrightarrow{E}(K_{a,b}):=\{(i,j):i\in A,j\in B\mbox{ or }i\in B,j\in A\}
.

•

Oriented complete bipartite digraph
K_{a,b}^{*}
: bipartition
A\cup B
where
A=\{0,\ldots,a-1\}
and
B=\{a,\ldots,a+b-1\}
; the standard set of directed edges will be
\overrightarrow{E}(K_{a,b}^{*}):=\{(i,j):i\in A,j\in B\}
.

If
H
with
V(H)=\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
is a disjoint union of graphs
H_{1}\cup\cdots\cup H_{u}
, with
|V(H_{i})|=h_{i}
, we will take
V(H)=V(H_{1})\cup\cdots\cup V(H_{u})
where
V(H_{1})=\{0,\ldots,h_{1}-1\}
,
V(H_{2})=\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{1}+h_{2}-1\}
, …,
V(H_{u})=\{h_{1}+\cdots+h_{m-2},\ldots,m-1\}
.

For an
H
-defined digraph when

is (an indirected or oriented)

with bipartition

, we will use a semi-colon to separate the sets corresponding to the vertices of
A
from those corresponding to the vertices of
B
. If
H
is a disjoint union of
H_{1}\cup\cdots\cup H_{u}
, we will similarly use semicolons to separate the sets corresponding to the vertices of distinct

.

Example 3.3

Let

3.1
Relationships between directed and undirected
H
-defined EDFs

In this section we present key relationships between EDFs defined by directed and undirected versions of the same underlying graph.

Our first result shows that an EDF defined by an oriented graph yields an EDF defined by an undirected graph.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
G
be a group of order
n
. Let
H
be a graph on
m
vertices and let
H^{*}
denote any orientation of
H
.
If
\mathcal{A}
is an
(n,m,l,\lambda;H^{*})
-EDF, then
\mathcal{A}
is an
(n,m,l,2\lambda;H)
-EDF.

Proof.

Let
V(H)=\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
. Suppose
\mathcal{A}=(A_{0},\ldots,A_{m-1})
is an
(n,m,l,\lambda;H^{*})
-EDF.
Denote by
\overline{H^{*}}
a copy of
H
with the reverse orientation to that of
H^{*}
, ie
(i,j)\in\overrightarrow{E}(\overline{H^{*}})
if and only if
(j,i)\in\overrightarrow{E}(H^{*})
. Observe that
\overrightarrow{E}(H)
is the disjoint union
\overrightarrow{E}(\overline{H^{*}})\cup\overrightarrow{E}(H^{*})
.

Now consider
\bigcup_{(i,j)\in\overrightarrow{E}(H)}\Delta(A_{j},A_{i})
. This may be rewritten

\bigcup_{(i,j)\in\overrightarrow{E}(H)}\Delta(A_{j},A_{i})=\bigcup_{(i,j)\in\overrightarrow{E}(H^{*})}\Delta(A_{j},A_{i})\cup\bigcup_{(i,j)\in\overrightarrow{E}(\overline{H^{*}})}\Delta(A_{j},A_{i}).

Now, since
\mathcal{A}=(A_{0},\ldots,A_{m-1})
is an
(n,m,l,\lambda;H^{*})
-EDF, we have that

\bigcup_{(i,j)\in\overrightarrow{E}(H^{*})}\Delta(A_{j},A_{i})=\lambda(G\setminus\{0\}).

Moreover

\bigcup_{(i,j)\in\overrightarrow{E}(\overline{H^{*}})}\Delta(A_{j},A_{i})=-(\bigcup_{(i,j)\in\overrightarrow{E}(H^{*})}\Delta(A_{j},A_{i}))=\lambda(G\setminus\{0\})

and the result follows.
∎

As a partial converse, we next present a result which obtains a directed version from an undirected version, subject to a suitable additional condition on the behaviour of the sets.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
G
be a group of order
n
. Let
H
be a graph on
m
vertices and let
H^{*}
denote any orientation of
H
.
If
\mathcal{A}=(A_{0},\ldots,A_{m-1})
is an
(n,m,l,\lambda;H)
-EDF and
\Delta(A_{i},A_{j})=\Delta(A_{j},A_{i})
for all
0\leq i\neq j\leq m-1
then
\lambda
is even and
\mathcal{A}
is an
(n,m,l,\lambda/2;H^{*})
-EDF.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{A}=(A_{0},\ldots,A_{m-1})
is an
(n,m,l,\lambda;H)
-EDF and
\Delta(A_{i},A_{j})=\Delta(A_{j},A_{i})
for all
0\leq i\neq j\leq m-1
. Denote by
\overline{H^{*}}
a copy of
H
with the reverse orientation to that of
H^{*}
, ie
(i,j)\in\overrightarrow{E}(\overline{H^{*}})
if and only if
(j,i)\in\overrightarrow{E}(H^{*})
. Then

\displaystyle\lambda(G\setminus\{0\})

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{(i,j)\in\overrightarrow{E}(H)}\Delta(A_{j},A_{i})

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{(i,j)\in\overrightarrow{E}(H^{*})}\Delta(A_{j},A_{i})\cup\bigcup_{(i,j)\in\overrightarrow{E}(\overline{H^{*}})}\Delta(A_{j},A_{i})

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{(i,j)\in\overrightarrow{E}(H^{*})}\Delta(A_{j},A_{i})\cup\bigcup_{(j,i)\in\overrightarrow{E}({H^{*}})}\Delta(A_{j},A_{i})

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{(i,j)\in\overrightarrow{E}(H^{*})}\Delta(A_{j},A_{i})\cup\bigcup_{(j,i)\in\overrightarrow{E}({H^{*}})}\Delta(A_{i},A_{j})

\displaystyle=2\left(\bigcup_{(i,j)\in\overrightarrow{E}(H^{*})}\Delta(A_{j},A_{i})\right)

It follows that
\lambda
is even and that
\bigcup_{(i,j)\in\overrightarrow{E}(H^{*})}\Delta(A_{j},A_{i})=\frac{\lambda}{2}(G\setminus\{0\})
.
∎

While these relationships are helpful in understanding the links between directed and undirected versions, they are far from describing the whole picture. There are many examples of both type of EDF which do not result from applying Theorems
3.7
or
3.8
to an EDF of the other type.

4

-defined EDFs when

is complete

As we have seen, the

-defined EDFs when
H=K_{m}
precisely correspond to the standard EDFs. These structures have been much-studied and so we will mention them only briefly here. In contrast, those with
H=K_{m}^{*}
do not - to our knowledge - correspond to previously-investigated types of external difference families.

4.1
External difference families

The standard EDFs have received considerable attention and many results about them are known
[
11
,
15
]
. However, it is perhaps worth noting that, due to their relative lack of structure compared to other variations of the definition, there are fewer known general constructions for EDFs than there are for EDFs with extra conditions such as SEDFs.

In order to present a useful general construction, we require a few facts about cyclotomy. For more information on cyclotomy, see
[
17
]
. Let
q
be a power of a prime
p
and let
{\rm GF}(q)
denote the finite field of order
q
. Let
\alpha
be a primitive element of
{\rm GF}(q)
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
q=ef+1
where
e,f\in\mathbb{N}
. The cyclotomic classes
C_{i}^{e}
in
{\rm GF}(q)
of order
e
(
0\leq i\leq e-1
) are defined as:

C_{i}^{e}=\{\alpha^{es+i}:0\leq s\leq f-1\}.

Here,
C_{0}^{e}
is the multiplicative subgroup of
{\rm GF}(q)
of cardinality
f
.

We now present the following well-known EDF construction
[
5
,
7
]
:

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
q=ef+1
be a prime power. Then the set
\{C_{0}^{e},\ldots,C_{e-1}^{e}\}
of all cyclotomic classes of order
e
in
GF(q)
forms a
(q,e,f,(e-1)f;K_{e})
-EDF.

Example 4.3
.

In
GF(13)
, taking
e=3
and
\alpha=2
, we have that
(C_{0}^{3},C_{1}^{3},C_{2}^{3})
forms a
(13,3,4,8;K_{3})
-EDF, where
C_{0}^{3}=\{1,5,12,8\},C_{1}^{3}=\{2,10,11,3\}
and
C_{0}^{2}=\{4,7,9,6\}
.

4.2
Tournament-defined EDFs

We establish the following results for EDFs defined by tournaments.

Theorem 4.4
.

If
\mathcal{A}
is an
(n,m,l,\lambda;K_{m}^{*})
-EDF (for any orientation of
K_{m}^{*}
) then
\mathcal{A}
is an
(n,m,l,2\lambda;K_{m})
-EDF, i.e. an
(n,m,l,2\lambda)
-EDF in the standard sense.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of Theorem
3.7
.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
K_{m}^{*}
be an oriented complete graph on
m
vertices, with an arbitrary orientation.
If
\mathcal{A}=(A_{0},\ldots,A_{m-1})
is an
(n,m,l,\lambda;K_{m})
-EDF and
\Delta(A_{i},A_{j})=\Delta(A_{j},A_{i})
for all
0\leq i\neq j\leq m-1
then
\lambda
is even and
\mathcal{A}
is an
(n,m,l,\lambda/2;K_{m}^{*})
-EDF.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of Theorem
3.8
.
∎

We are now ready to present a cyclotomic construction for
H
-defined EDFs when
H
is a tournament. We first need a technical lemma (Lemma 3.13 from
[
10
]
):

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
q=ef+1
be a prime power. If either

(i)

e
is odd; or

(ii)

e
is even and
q\equiv 1\mod 2e
;

then
-1\in C_{0}^{e}
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
q=ef+1
be a prime power such that either

(i)

e
is odd; or

(ii)

e
is even and
q\equiv 1\mod 2e
.

Let
K_{e}^{*}
be a tournament with an arbitrary orientation. Let
\mathcal{A}=(C_{0}^{e},\ldots,C_{e-1}^{e})
.
Then
\mathcal{A}
forms a
(q,e,f,(e-1)f/2;K_{e}^{*})
-EDF in
GF(q)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.2
,
\mathcal{A}
is a
(q,e,f,(e-1)f);K_{e})
-EDF. In both cases (i) and (ii) of Lemma
4.6
,
-1\in C_{0}^{e}
, hence
C_{i}^{e}=-C_{i}^{e}
for all
0\leq i\leq e-1
and so
\Delta(C_{i}^{e},C_{j}^{e})=\Delta(C_{j}^{e},C_{i}^{e})
for all
0\leq i,j\leq m-1
. In both cases,
(e-1)f
is even and the result follows by Theorem
4.5
.
∎

Example 4.8
.

In
GF(13)
, taking
e=3
and
\alpha=2
yields the cyclotomic classes
C_{0}^{3}=\{1,5,8,12\},C_{1}^{3}=\{2,3,10,11\}
and
C_{2}^{3}=\{4,6,7,9\}
. By Theorem
4.7
, since

is odd,
(C_{0}^{3},C_{1}^{3},C_{2}^{3})
form a
(13,3,4,4;K_{3}^{*})
-EDF, where

is a tournament on
3
vertices with arbitrary orientation. Note that these same sets were discussed in Example
3.3
.

5
H
-defined EDFs when
H
is a cycle or union of cycles

We have seen in Theorem
3.5
that CEDFs correspond to oriented cycles, or disjoint unions of same-size oriented cycles, with the standard orientation (where we include a single undirected edge as a directed cycle of length
2
).

5.1
Circular external difference families

CEDFs were introduced in
[
18
]
and various results have been established about them. In
[
18
]
, sufficient conditions for the existence of certain cyclotomic CEDFs in the additive group of a finite field were given in terms of primitive elements of the field. The cyclotomic approach was further extended in
[
20
]
; this paper also contains structural characterizations and non-existence results for strong CEDFs. In
[
16
]
, existence of infinite families of
1
-CEDFs in cyclic groups was established for certain parameters using graceful labellings of graphs. (We note that the use of graceful labellings in the strong difference family context appeared in
[
4
]
). So far, most constructions in the literature are not explicit.

In this section, we present new results for CEDFs in cyclic groups, non-cyclic abelian groups and dihedral groups.

In
[
16
]
, Theorem 1.8 states three main results on
(ml^{2},m,l,1)
-
1
-CEDFs in abelian groups. The second part asserts that if
l
and
m
are odd then there is no
(ml^{2}+1,m,l,1)
-
1
-CEDF. It seems that this result holds only in the context of cyclic groups, since we show that an infinite family of
1
-CEDFs with such parameters exist in non-cyclic abelian groups.

5.2
CEDFs in abelian non-cyclic groups and non-abelian groups

We present a construction for an infinite family of CEDFs in abelian groups which are not cyclic nor elementary abelian.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
l\equiv 3\mod 4
(
l\in\mathbb{N}
). Denote
z=\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}\in\mathbb{N}
. Define the following subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{3l^{2}+1}{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
:

•

A_{0}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{l-1}\{(i,0)\}

•

A_{1}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{l-1}\{(z(i-1)-l-i,i+1)\}

•

A_{2}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{l-1}\{(zi-l,i)\}

where the first component is taken modulo
\frac{3l^{2}+1}{2}
and the second component is taken modulo
2
.

Then
(A_{0},A_{1},A_{2})
form a
(3l^{2}+1,3,l,1)
-CEDF in the non-cyclic abelian group
\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{3l^{2}+1}{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

Proof.

Since
l\equiv 3\mod 4
,
(3l^{2}+1)/2
is even and hence
\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{3l^{2}+1}{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is not a cyclic group. We will show that
\Delta(A_{1},A_{0})\cup\Delta(A_{2},A_{1})\cup\Delta(A_{0},A_{2})=G\setminus\{0\}
; note that disjointness of the three sets then follows from the fact that
0
does not occur in the multiset of differences. Since the difference multiset contains
3l^{2}
elements by construction, it will suffice to show that each non-identity group element occurs at least once.

For
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{3l^{2}+1}{2}}
and
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, we will adapt our previous interval notation as follows: we denote the set
\{(a,x),(a+1,x),\ldots,(b,x)\}
by
[(a,x),(b,x)]
.

First, we determine the difference multisets.

\displaystyle\Delta(A_{1},A_{0})

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=0}^{l-1}\bigcup_{j=0}^{l-1}\{\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}(i-1)-l-i-j,i+1)\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=0}^{l-1}[(\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}(i-1)-2l+1-i,i+1),(\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}(i-1)-l-i,i+1)]

Next we have:

\displaystyle\Delta(A_{2},A_{1})

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=0}^{l-1}\bigcup_{j=0}^{l-1}\{(\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}i-l-\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}(j-1)+l+j,i-j+1)\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=0}^{l-1}\bigcup_{j=0}^{l-1}\{(\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}(i-j+1)+j,i-j+1)\}.

We let
k=i-j+1
: since
i
and
j
vary from
0
to
l-1
,
k
takes values from
-l+2
to
l
. We change the indices from
i
and
j
to
k
and
j
. For
-l+2\leq k\leq l
, let

J_{k}=\{j:0\leq j\leq l-1\mbox{ and }0\leq k+j-1\leq l-1\}=[0,l-1]\cap[1-k,l-k]

so that

\Delta(A_{2},A_{1})=\bigcup_{k=-l+2}^{l}\bigcup_{j\in J_{k}}\{(\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}k+j,k)\}.

For
-l+2\leq k\leq 0
, we have
J_{k}=[1-k,l-1]
, while for
1\leq k\leq l
we have
J_{k}=[0,l-k].
Thus we have:

\Delta(A_{2},A_{1})=\bigcup_{k=-l+2}^{0}\bigcup_{j=1-k}^{l-1}\{\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}k+j,k)\}\cup\bigcup_{k=1}^{l}\bigcup_{j=0}^{l-k}\{(\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}k+j,k)\}

=\bigcup_{k=-l+2}^{0}[(\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}k+1-k,k),(\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}k+l-1,k)]\cup\bigcup_{k=1}^{l}[(\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}k,k),(\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}k+l-k,k)]

Finally:

\displaystyle\Delta(A_{0},A_{2})

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=0}^{l-1}\bigcup_{j=0}^{l-1}\{(i-\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}j+l,j)\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{j=0}^{l-1}[(-\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}j+l,j),(-\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}j+2l-1,j)]

Next, we check that the difference multiset has one occurrence of each non-identity element in
\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{3l^{2}+1}{2}}\times\{0\}
. We consider the differences corresponding to the following indices, where
0\leq s\leq\frac{l-3}{2}
.

•

k=-2s
in
\Delta(A_{2},A_{1})
: the difference multiset obtained is

\displaystyle[(\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}(-2s)+1+2s,-2s),(\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}(-2s)+l-1,-2s)]

\displaystyle=[((3l-1)s+2s+1,0),((3l-1)s+l-1,0)]

\displaystyle=[((3l+1)s+1,0),((3l-1)s+l-1,0)].

Here we have applied the simplification
2(\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2})=\frac{3(l^{2}-2l+1)}{2}=\frac{3l^{2}+1}{2}+\frac{2-6l}{2}\equiv 1-3l\mod\frac{3l^{2}+1}{2}
.

•

j=2s
in
\Delta(A_{0},A_{2})

\displaystyle[(-\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}(2s)+l,2s),(-\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}(2s)+2l-1,-2s)]

\displaystyle=[((3l-1)s+l,0),((3l-1)s+2l-1,0)].

•

k=l-1-2s
in
\Delta(A_{2},A_{1})

\displaystyle[(\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}(l-1-2s),l-1-2s),(\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{2}(l-1-2s)+l-(l-2s-1),l-1-2s)]

\displaystyle=[((3l-1)s+\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{3},0),((3l-1)s+\frac{3}{4}(l-1)^{3}+2s+1,0)]

The union of these is

Theorem 5.4
.

Let

\mathcal{A}=(A_{0},A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{m-3},A_{m-2},A_{m-1})

•

;

•

;

•

for

For
m\in\{2,4,6,8\}
,
\mathcal{A}
is an
(ml^{2}+1,m,l,1)
-CEDF in
\mathbb{Z}_{ml^{2}+1}

. All set entries lie between

\cup_{i=0}^{m-1}\Delta(A_{i+c\mod m},A_{i})=\lambda(G\setminus\{0\})

\displaystyle\Delta(A_{1},A_{0})

\displaystyle=\{l^{2}+(i+1)l-j:0\leq i,j\leq l-1\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=0}^{l-1}\bigcup_{j=0}^{l-1}\{l^{2}+(i+1)l-j\}

\displaystyle=[(m-1)l^{2}+1,ml^{2}].

\displaystyle\Delta(A_{0},A_{m-1})

\displaystyle=\{i-(\frac{m}{2}+\frac{m-2}{2})l^{2}-(j+1)l:0\leq i,j\leq l-1\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=0}^{l-1}\bigcup_{j=0}^{l-1}\{l^{2}-(j+1)l+i+1\}\mbox{ \qquad adding $ml^{2}+1$}

\displaystyle\Delta(A_{2r},A_{2r-1})

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=0}^{l-1}\bigcup_{j=0}^{l-1}\{(r+\frac{m}{2})l^{2}-(j+1)l+i+1\}\mbox{ \qquad adding $ml^{2}+1$}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{j=0}^{l-1}[(r+\frac{m}{2})l^{2}-(j+1)l+1,(r+\frac{m}{2})l^{2}-jl]

\displaystyle=[(r+\frac{m}{2})l^{2}-l+1,(r+\frac{m}{2})l^{2}]\cup[(r+\frac{m}{2})l^{2}-2l+1,(r+\frac{m}{2})l^{2}-l]\cup

\displaystyle\cdots\cup[(r+\frac{m}{2})l^{2}-l^{2}+1,(r+\frac{m}{2})l^{2}-l^{2}+l]

\displaystyle=[(r+\frac{m}{2}-1)l^{2}+1,(r+\frac{m}{2})l^{2}]

varies from

Finally, we consider general difference multisets of the following form, where

.

\displaystyle\Delta(A_{2r+1},A_{2r})

\displaystyle=\{(\frac{m}{2}+r)l^{2}+(i+1)l-(2r-1)l^{2}-j:0\leq i,j\leq l-1\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=0}^{l-1}\bigcup_{j=0}^{l-1}\{(\frac{m}{2}-r+1)l^{2}+(i+1)l-j\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=0}^{l-1}[(\frac{m}{2}-r+1)l^{2}+il+1,(\frac{m}{2}-r+1)l^{2}+(i+1)l]

\displaystyle=[(\frac{m}{2}-r+1)l^{2}+1,(\frac{m}{2}-r+1)l^{2}+l]\cup[(\frac{m}{2}-r+1)l^{2}+l+1,(\frac{m}{2}-r+1)l^{2}+2l]\cup

\displaystyle\cdots\cup[(\frac{m}{2}-r+1)l^{2}+l^{2}-l+1,(\frac{m}{2}-r+1)l^{2}+l^{2}]

5.4
Adjacent-disjoint CEDFs

The external difference family definition has traditionally required all subsets in the family to be pairwise disjoint. The reason for requiring this property is the need for unique decoding in the AMD application which provided the original motivation for EDFs
[
15
]
. However, from a purely combinatorial viewpoint, there is no reason to require disjoint sets; many well-known difference structures such as difference families allow the sets of the family to overlap. For standard EDFs (those defined by
K_{m}
), removing the pairwise disjointness condition to allow some pairwise non-disjoint sets would lead to the occurrence of
0
as an external difference between the sets, making it impossible for the multiset of external differences to equal
\lambda(G\setminus\{0\})
.

However, when
K_{m}
is replaced by a graph in which not all vertices are adjacent, it is possible to relax the disjointness condition between certain sets and still obtain a multiset of external differences
\lambda(G\setminus\{0\})
. We explore this approach in this subsection. We present the following definition.

Definition 5.12
.

Let
G
be a group of order
n
and let
m>1
. Let
H
be a labelled digraph on
m
vertices
\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}
. A family of
l
-sets
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}\}
in
G
is an adjacent-disjoint
(n,m,l,\lambda;H)
-EDF if the multiset equation

\bigcup_{(i,j)\in\overrightarrow{E}(H)}\Delta(A_{j},A_{i})=\lambda(G\setminus\{0\})

holds.

Observe this definition implies that an adjacent-disjoint
(n,m,l,\lambda;H)
-EDF
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}\}
possesses the following property: if
(i,j)
or
(j,i)
lies in
\overrightarrow{E}(H)
then
A_{i}\cap A_{j}=\emptyset
.

Any
(n,m,l,\lambda;H)
-EDF is an adjacent-disjoint
(n,m,l,\lambda;H)
-EDF, but the converse need not hold.

We have in fact already seen one construction for an adjacent-disjoint CEDF in Theorem
5.4
. In this construction, there is overlap in the sets for
m\geq 10
, but the multiset union still satisfies the necessary condition, so this gives an adjacent-disjoint CEDF but not a CEDF for even
m\geq 10
. We next present another construction for adjacent-disjoint CEDFs.

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}
be even and let
l\in\mathbb{Z}
. Consider the following sets in
\mathbb{Z}_{ml^{2}+1}
. For
0\leq r\leq\frac{m}{2}-1
:

•

A_{2r}=\{rl^{2}+i:0\leq i\leq l-1\}
;

•

A_{2r+1}=\{(2r+1)l^{2}+(i+1)l:0\leq i\leq l-1\}
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{0},A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{m-3},A_{m-2},A_{m-1})
.
Then
\mathcal{A}
is an adjacent-disjoint
(ml^{2}+1,m,l,1)
-CEDF in
\mathbb{Z}_{ml^{2}+1}
. When
m\in\{2,4\}
this is an
(ml^{2}+1,m,l,1)
-CEDF.

Proof.

We establish that the multiset equation
\cup_{i=0}^{m-1}\Delta(A_{i+c\mod m},A_{i})=G\setminus\{0\}
holds for
\mathcal{A}
.
We first consider
\Delta(A_{2},A_{1}),\Delta(A_{4},A_{3}),\ldots,\Delta(A_{m-2},A_{m-3})
. Let
1\leq r\leq\frac{m}{2}-1
. Then

\displaystyle\Delta(A_{2r},A_{2r-1})

\displaystyle=\{rl^{2}+i-(2r-1)l^{2}-(j+1):0\leq i,j\leq l-1\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=0}^{l-1}\bigcup_{j=0}^{l-1}\{(m-r+1)l^{2}-(j+1)l+i+1\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{j=0}^{l-1}[(m-r+1)l^{2}-(j+1)l+1,(m-r+1)l^{2}-jl]

\displaystyle=[(m-r)l^{2}+1,(m-r+1)l^{2}].

The multiset union as
1\leq r\leq\frac{m}{2}-1
is
[(\frac{m}{2}+1)l^{2}+1,ml^{2}]
.

Next we consider
\Delta(A_{1},A_{0}),\Delta(A_{3},A_{2}),\ldots,\Delta(A_{m-1},A_{m-2})
. Let
0\leq r\leq\frac{m-2}{2}
. Then

\displaystyle\Delta(A_{2r+1},A_{2r})

\displaystyle=\{(2r+1)l^{2}+(i+1)l-rl^{2}-j:0\leq i,j\leq l-1\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=0}^{l-1}\bigcup_{j=0}^{l-1}\{(r+1)l^{2}+(i+1)l-j\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=0}^{l-1}[(r+1)l^{2}+il+1,(r+1)l^{2}+(i+1)l]

\displaystyle=[(r+1)l^{2}+1,(r+2)l^{2}]

The multiset union as
0\leq r\leq\frac{m-2}{2}
is
[l^{2}+1,(\frac{m}{2}+1)l^{2}]
.

Finally,

\displaystyle\Delta(A_{0},A_{m-1})

\displaystyle=\{i-(m-1)l^{2}-(j+1)l:0\leq i,j\leq l-1\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=0}^{l-1}\bigcup_{j=0}^{l-1}\{l^{2}-(j+1)l+i+1\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{j=0}^{l-1}[l^{2}-(j+1)l+1,l^{2}-jl]

\displaystyle=[1,l^{2}]

Hence all of the elements in
\mathbb{Z}_{ml^{2}+1}\setminus\{0\}
occur exactly once in the CEDF multiset, so
\mathcal{A}
is an adjacent-disjoint
(ml^{2}+1,m,l,1)
-CEDF in
\mathbb{Z}_{ml^{2}+1}
. Note that non-adjacency is witnessed by the fact that
0
does not occur as a difference in the multiset.

From the structure of the sets, the only possible intersection between sets
A_{i}
(
0\leq i\leq m-1
) can occur between the last element of
A_{2r+1}
and the first element of
A_{2(2r+2)}
, i.e. element
(2r+2)l^{2}
(where
0\leq 2r+1,4r+4\leq m-1
). As
r
is always a whole non-negative number, this gives the points of intersection as
\{(2r+2)l^{2}:0\leq r\leq\lfloor\frac{m}{4}-\frac{5}{4}\rfloor\}
in the sets
A_{2r+1}
and
A_{2(2r+2)}
where
0\leq r\leq\lfloor\frac{m}{4}-\frac{5}{4}\rfloor
. Observe that for
m=2
and
m=4
we have no intersection of the sets, so for these values of
m
this construction is also a
(ml^{2}+1,m,l,1)
-CEDF.
∎

5.5

In this section, we briefly consider EDFs defined by undirected cycles. It is clear that an EDF defined by a directed cycle naturally gives rise to one defined by an undirected cycle, as follows.

Theorem 5.15
.

If
\mathcal{A}
is an
(n,m,l,\lambda;C_{m}^{*})
-EDF (for any orientation) then
\mathcal{A}
is an
(n,m,l,2\lambda;C_{m})
-EDF.

In particular, if
\mathcal{A}
is an
(n,m,l,\lambda)
-CEDF, then
\mathcal{A}
is an
(n,m,l,2\lambda;C_{m})
-EDF.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of Theorem
3.7
.
∎

However, not all
C_{m}
-defined EDFs correspond to CEDFs, as the following examples show. These examples were found via computational search in GAP
[

]
, using a constraint-satisfaction modelling language
[

]
and solver
[

]
.

6
H
-defined EDFs when
H
is complete bipartite

We have encountered EDFs defined by certain directed complete bipartite graphs in the definition of the SEDF. One may also ask about undirected complete bipartite graphs.

Recall that our notation for a complete bipartite digraph
K_{a,b}
has bipartition
A\cup B
where
A=\{0,\ldots,a-1\}
and
B=\{a,\ldots,a+b-1\}
. For the oriented version, the standard set of directed edges is
\overrightarrow{E}(K_{a,b}^{*}):=\{(i,j):i\in A,j\in B\}
. We use a semi-colon to separate the sets corresponding to the vertices of
A
from those corresponding to the vertices of
B
.

6.1
EDFs from directed complete bipartite graphs

As shown in Theorem
3.4
, the SEDF definition requires that the
m
sets
A_{0},\ldots,A_{m-1}
of a SEDF can be ordered to form a
K_{m-1,1}^{*}
-defined EDF (with the standard orientation) for each
i
, where the centre of the star corresponds to each
A_{i}
in turn. However, in practice only one SEDF with more than two sets is known
[
12
,
19
]
, so this is not a large source of examples.

Example 6.1
.

Let
q=3^{5}=243
,
e=11
and
f=22
. Let
\mathcal{A}=\{C_{i}^{11}:0\leq i\leq 10\}
be the cyclotomic classes of order
11
in
GF(243)
. In
[
19
]
it is shown that
\mathcal{A}
is a
(243,11,22,20)
-SEDF, and hence
(C^{11}_{0},\ldots C^{11}_{i-1},C^{11}_{i+1},\ldots,C^{11}_{10};C^{11}_{i})
is a
(243,11,22,2;K_{10,1}^{*})
-CEDF for each
0\leq i\leq 10
.

The following is a direct consequence of Theorem 2.4 of
[
19
]
, a structural result on generalised SEDFs. Note that an
l
-subset
D
of a group
G
of order
n
is an
(n,l,\lambda,\mu)
-partial difference set (PDS) if the multiset
\Delta(D)
comprises
\lambda
copies of each nonidentity element of
D
and
\mu
copies of each nonidentity element of
G\setminus D
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
G
be an abelian group of order
n
and let
(A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{m-1})
be an ordered collection of
l
-sets which partition
G\setminus\{0\}
. If
A_{m-1}
is a regular
(n,l,l-\lambda-1,l-\lambda)
-partial difference set then
(A_{0},A_{1},\ldots;A_{m-1})
is an
(n,m,l,\lambda;K_{m-1,1}^{*})
and an
(n,m,l,2\lambda;K_{m-1,1})
.

Proof.

Here the multiset union
\Delta(A_{m-1},G\setminus\{0\})
comprises
l
occurrences of each nonidentity
x\in G\ \setminus A_{m-1}
and
l-1
copies of each element of
A_{m-1}
. If
A_{m-1}
is a PDS with the stated parameters, then
\cup_{i=0}^{m-2}\Delta(A_{m-1},A_{i})=\lambda(G\setminus\{0\})
, establishing the result for
K_{m-1,1}^{*}
. The result for
K_{m-1,1}
follows by symmetry.
∎

We note that such PDSs are very restricted in structure
[
19
]
: these are either the Paley PDSs or have parameters
(243,22,1,2)
.

Next we present a direct construction which does not correspond to either of the previous situations.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{Z}
. Consider the following subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{4a+1}
:

•

for
1\leq i\leq a
,
A_{i}=\{4i-2,4i\}
;

•

A_{a+1}=\{0,1\}
.

Then
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{a};A_{a+1})
is a
(4a+1,a+1,2,1;K_{a,1}^{*})
-EDF in
\mathbb{Z}_{4a+1}
.

Proof.

Since
1<4i-2<4i<4(i+1)-2<4(i+1)\leq 4a
for all
1\leq i\leq a
and there is no “wraparound”, the sets are disjoint. We have that, for
1\leq i\leq a
, the multiset
\Delta(A_{a+1},A_{i})=\{4(a-i)+1,4(a-i)+2,4(a-i)+3,4(a-i)+4\}
; hence
\cup_{i=1}^{a}\Delta(A_{a+1},A_{i})
comprises all values in
[1,4a]
. Hence the difference multiset defined by
K_{a,1}^{*}
comprises all non-zero elements in
\mathbb{Z}_{4a+1}
.
∎

6.2
EDFs from undirected complete bipartite graphs

We present a construction for an undirected star graph in the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{ml^{2}}
, when
m
is even.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
m
be even and
l(>1)\in\mathbb{Z}
. Consider the following subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{ml^{2}+1}
:

•

A_{0}=\{i:0\leq i\leq l-1\}
;

•

for
1\leq r\leq(m-4)/2
,
A_{r}=\{2l^{2}r+l+i:0\leq i\leq l-1\}
;

•

A_{(m-2)/2}=\{(m-2)l^{2}+i:0\leq i\leq l-1\}
;

•

A_{m/2}=\{(m-2)l^{2}+2l(i+1):\ 0\leq i\leq l-1\}
.

Then

(A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{(m-2)/2};A_{m/2})
is an
(ml^{2}+1,m/2+1,l,1;K_{m/2,1})
-EDF.

Proof.

Using the usual ordering
0<1<\cdots<ml^{2}
of the elements, observe that all elements of
A_{i}
precede those of
A_{i+1}
(
0\leq i<m/2
), and there is no “wraparound”, so all sets are pairwise disjoint.

We wish to prove the multiset equality

\bigcup_{i=0}^{m/2-1}\Delta(A_{m/2},A_{i})\cup\Delta(A_{i},A_{m/2})=Z_{ml^{2}}\setminus\{0\}

First observe that

\displaystyle\Delta(A_{m/2},A_{(m-2)/2})

\displaystyle=\{(m-2)l^{2}+2l(i+1)-(m-2)l^{2}-j:0\leq i,j\leq l-1\}

\displaystyle=\{2l(i+1)-j:0\leq i,j\leq l-1\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=0}^{l-1}[2l(i+1)-l+1,2l(i+1)]

\displaystyle=[l+1,2l]\cup[3l+1,4l]\cup\cdots\cup[2l^{2}-l+1,2l^{2}]

while

\displaystyle\Delta(A_{0},A_{m/2})

\displaystyle=\{i-(m-2)l^{2}-2l(j+1):0\leq i,j\leq l-1\}

\displaystyle=\{ml^{2}-(m-2)l^{2}-2l(j+1)+i+1:0\leq i,j\leq l-1\}

\displaystyle=\{2l^{2}-2l(j+1)+i+1:0\leq i,j\leq l-1\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{j=0}^{l-1}[2l^{2}-2l(j+1)+1,2l^{2}-2l(j+1)+l]

\displaystyle=[1,l]\cup[2l+1,3l]\cup\cdots\cup[2l^{2}-4l+1,2l^{2}-3l]\cup[2l^{2}-2l+1,2l^{2}-l]

Together these two difference multisets comprise one copy of the interval
[1,2l^{2}]
.

Next, consider the differences:

\displaystyle\Delta(A_{m/2},A_{0})

\displaystyle=\{(m-2)l^{2}+2l(i+1)-j:0\leq i,j\leq l-1\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=0}^{l-1}[(m-2)l^{2}+(2i+1)l+1,(m-2)l^{2}+2l(i+1)]

\displaystyle=[(m-2)l^{2}+l+1,(m-2)l^{2}+2l]\cup\cdots\cup[ml^{2}-l+1,ml^{2}]

while

\displaystyle\Delta(A_{(m-2)/2},A_{m/2})

\displaystyle=\{(m-2)l^{2}+i-(m-2)l^{2}-2l(j+1):0\leq i,j\leq l-1\}

\displaystyle=\{i-2l(j+1):0\leq i,j\leq l-1\}

\displaystyle=\{ml^{2}-2l(j+1)+i+1:0\leq i,j\leq l-1\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{j=0}^{l-1}[ml^{2}-2l(j+1)+1,ml^{2}-2l(j+1)+l]

\displaystyle=[(m-2)l^{2}+1,(m-2)l^{2}+l]\cup[(m-2)l^{2}+2l+1,(m-2)l^{2}+3l]\cdots

\displaystyle\cup[ml^{2}-4l+1,ml^{2}-3l]\cup[ml^{2}-2l+1,ml^{2}-l].

Together, these two difference multisets yield one copy of
[(m-2)l^{2}+1,ml^{2}]
.

Finally, for
1\leq r\leq(m-4)/2
, consider the following multisets:

\displaystyle\Delta(A_{r},A_{m/2})

\displaystyle=\{2l^{2}r+l+i-(m-2)l^{2}-2l(j+1):0\leq i,j\leq l-1\}

\displaystyle=\{(2r-m+2)l^{2}-l(2j+1)+i:0\leq i,j\leq l-1\}

\displaystyle=\{(2r+2)l^{2}-l(2j+1)+i+1:0\leq i,j\leq l-1\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{j=0}^{l-1}[(2r+2)l^{2}-l(2j+1)+1,(2r+2)l^{2}-l(2j+1)+l]

\displaystyle=[2l^{2}r+l+1,2l^{2}r+2l]\cup[2l^{2}r+3l+1,2l^{2}r+4l]\cup

\displaystyle\cdots\cup[2l^{2}(r+1)-3l+1,2l^{2}(r+1)-2l]\cup[2l^{2}(r+1)-l+1,2l^{2}(r+1)]

and

\displaystyle\Delta(A_{m/2},A_{r})

\displaystyle=\{(m-2)l^{2}+2l(i+1)-2l^{2}r-l-j:0\leq i,j\leq l-1\}

\displaystyle=\{(m-2-2r)l^{2}+(2i+1)l-j:0\leq i,j\leq l-1\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{j=0}^{l-1}[(m-2-2r)l^{2}+2il+1,(m-2-2r)l^{2}+(2i+1)l]

\displaystyle=[(m-2r-2)l^{2}+1,(m-2r-2)l^{2}+l]\cup\cdots[(m-2r)l^{2}-2l+1,(m-2r)l^{2}]

Observe that, for
1\leq r\leq(m-4)/2
, we have
1\leq(m-2)/2-r\leq(m-4)/2
and

\displaystyle\Delta(A_{m/2},A_{(m-2)/2-r})

\displaystyle=\{2rl^{2}+(2i+1)l-j:0\leq i,j\leq l-1\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{j=0}^{l-1}[2rl^{2}+2il+1,2rl^{2}+(2i+1)l]

\displaystyle=[2rl^{2}+1,2rl^{2}+l]\cup[2rl^{2}+2l+1,2rl^{2}+3l]\cup

\displaystyle\cdots\cup[2l^{2}(r+1)-2l+1,2l^{2}(r+1)-l]

Together,
\Delta(A_{r},A_{m/2})\cup\Delta(A_{m/2},A_{(m-2)/2-r})
yield one copy of
[2rl^{2}+1,2l^{2}(r+1)]
. Finally,

\bigcup_{r=1}^{(m-4)/2}[2rl^{2}+1,2l^{2}(r+1)]=[2l^{2}+1,(m-2)l^{2}].

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 6.5
.

We note that the
(ml^{2}+1,m/2+1,l,1;K_{m/2,1})
-EDF from Theorem
6.4
can also be viewed as an adjacent-disjoint CEDF.

Let
m
be even, let
l(>1)\in\mathbb{Z}
and let
A_{0},\ldots,A_{m/2}
be as defined in Theorem
6.4
. Define subsets
D_{0},\ldots,D_{m-1}
of
\mathbb{Z}_{ml^{2}}
as follows: for
0\leq i\leq(m-2)/2
,

•

D_{2i}=A_{i}

•

D_{2i+1}=A_{m/2}

Then
(D_{0},D_{1},D_{2},\ldots,D_{m-1})
is an adjacent-disjoint
(ml^{2}+1,m,l,1;C_{m}^{*})
(where odd-indexed sets coincide completely). The difference multiset is as in Theorem
6.4
; the adjacent-disjoint condition clearly holds since
0
does not appear as a difference.

Example 6.6
.

Let
m=6
and
l=3
, so
G=\mathbb{Z}_{55}
. The sets are
A_{0}=\{0,1,2\}
,
A_{1}=\{21,22,23\}
,
A_{2}=\{36,37,38\}
and
A_{3}=\{42,48,54\}
. Then
(A_{0},A_{1},A_{2};A_{3})
is a
(55,4,3,1;K_{3,1})
-EDF. It can be verified directly that
\Delta(A_{0},A_{3})\cup\Delta(A_{3},A_{2})=[1,18]
,
\Delta(A_{3},A_{0})\cup\Delta(A_{2},A_{3})=[37,54]
and
\Delta(A_{1},A_{3})\cup\Delta(A_{3},A_{1})=[19,36]
.

We next present a construction which uses the “same” sets as in Theorem
6.3
, but in a larger cyclic group, to obtain an undirected bipartite construction with the same value of
\lambda
.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{Z}
and consider the following subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{8a+1}
:

•

for
1\leq i\leq a
,
A_{i}=\{4i-2,4i\}
;

•

A_{a+1}=\{0,1\}
.

Then
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{a};A_{a+1})
is an
(8a+1,a+1,2,1;K_{a,1})
-EDF in
\mathbb{Z}_{8a+1}
.

Proof.

Since
1<4i-2<4i<4(i+1)-2<4(i+1)\leq 4a
for all
1\leq i\leq m
and there is no “wraparound”, the sets are disjoint. The differences we need to consider are
\Delta(A_{a+1},A_{i})
(
1\leq i\leq a
) and
\Delta(A_{i},A_{a+1})
(
1\leq i\leq a
). First note that
\Delta(A_{a+1},A_{i})=\{8a-4i+1,8a-4i+2,8a-4i+3,8a-4i+4\}
; for
1\leq i\leq a
we obtain all values from
4a+1
to
8a
. Next,
\Delta(A_{i},A_{a+1})=\{4i-3,4i-2,4i-1,4i\}
; for
1\leq i\leq a
we obtain all values from
1
to
4a
. Hence the difference multiset comprises all non-zero elements in
\mathbb{Z}_{8a+1}
.
∎

Observe that, by construction, this requires directed edges in both directions in order to obtain all non-identity elements of the group as differences, hence does not correspond to an EDF defined by an oriented complete bipartite graph.

We can adapt this to construct a family corresponding to a different complete bipartite graph in the same group:

Corollary 6.8
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{Z}
. Consider the following subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{8a+1}
:

•

for
1\leq i\leq a
,
A_{i}=\{4i-2,4i\}
;

•

A_{a+1}=\{0,1\}
;

•

A_{a+2}=\{4a+1,4a+2\}
.

Then
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{a};A_{a+1},A_{a+2})
is an
(8a+1,a+2,2,2;K_{a,2})
-EDF.

Proof.

The difference multiset defined by
K_{a,2}
is:

\bigcup_{i=1}^{a}(\Delta(A_{a+1},A_{i})\cup\Delta(A_{i},A_{a+1})\cup\Delta(A_{a+2},A_{i})\cup\Delta(A_{i},A_{a+2})).

From the previous theorem,
\cup_{i=1}^{a}(\Delta(A_{a+1},A_{i})\cup\Delta(A_{i},A_{a+1}))
comprises one copy of all non-zero elements in
\mathbb{Z}_{8a+1}
. We now consider
\cup_{i=1}^{a}(\Delta(A_{a+2},A_{i})\cup\Delta(A_{i},A_{a+2}))
. We have that
\Delta(A_{a+2},A_{i})=\{4(a-i)+1,4(a-i)+2,4(a-i)+3,4(a-i)+4\}
while
\Delta(A_{i},A_{a+2})=\{4(a+i),4(a+i)-1,4(a+i)-2,4(a+i)-3\}
. Letting
i
range from
1
to
a
yields the intervals
[1,4a]
and
[4a+1,8a]
respectively.
Hence overall the difference multiset comprises two copies of all non-zero elements in
\mathbb{Z}_{8a+1}
.
∎

7
Further work

It is clear that the definition of digraph-defined EDF introduced in this paper naturally leads to a large number of new research questions.

To date, only those digraph-defined EDFs defined in terms of undirected complete graphs (the standard EDFs), undirected complete graphs with directed stars (SEDFs) and directed cycles (CEDFs) have appeared in the literature. It would be of great interest to see constructions and non-existence results for other EDFs defined by natural families of directed graphs.

It is also of interest to understand what role the nature of the group
G
plays in the possible range of digraph-defined EDFs obtainable in
G
.

Morse index, topological degree and local uniqueness of multi-spikes solutions to the Lane-Emden problem in dimension two
Isabella Ianni, Peng Luo, Shusen Yan

Abstract
. We consider multi-spike positive solutions to the Lane-Emden problem in any bounded smooth planar domain and compute their Morse index, extending to the dimension N = 2 N=2 classical theorems due to Bahri-Li-Rey (1995) [ 2 ] and Rey (1999) [ 29 ] when N ≥ 4 N\geq 4 and N = 3 N=3 , respectively. Furthermore, by deeply investigating their concentration behavior, we also derive the total topological degree. The Morse index and the degree counting formula yield a new local uniqueness result.

1.
Introduction and main results

We consider the Lane-Emden problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=u^{p}&\text{in}~\Omega,\\[1.42262pt]
u>0&\text{in}~\Omega,\\[1.42262pt]
u=0&\text{on}~\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a smooth bounded domain and
p>1
.

It is well known that problem (
1.1
) admits at least one solution, while the question of uniqueness or multiplicity is significantly
more complex, depending on both the topology and geometry of the domain
\Omega
, as well as on the value of the exponent
p
(see for instance
[
10
]
). A complete understanding of this issue is still far from being reached both in dimension
N=2
than in the higher dimensions.
For example, the solution is unique in any domain
\Omega
provided that
p
is sufficiently close to
1
(
[
9
,
11
,
25
]
). Moreover, for any value of
p
, uniqueness is known to hold when
\Omega
is a ball (
[
17
]
) or, more generally, when it is a symmetric and convex domain with respect to
2
orthogonal directions (
[
10
,
9
]
). More recently, uniqueness for problem (
1.1
) has also been established in general convex domains, without any symmetry assumption, for sufficiently large
p
(see
[
13
,
21
]
), thus partially solving a longstanding conjecture proposed in
[
10
]
).
Conversely, multiple solutions exist in suitable non-convex domains
\Omega
(see
[
10
,
16
]
for examples involving dumbbell shaped domains, or
[
16
,
3
]
for the case of non-simply connected domains).
In particular all the solutions found in
[
16
]
concentrate at a finite number of distinct points in
\Omega
, and vanish elsewhere as
p\rightarrow+\infty
. Note that all solutions of problem (
1.1
) are uniformly bounded in
p
(see
[
24
]
). Notably, for the concentrating solutions built in
[
16
]
, the local maximum values around each concentration point converge all to
\sqrt{e}
.
The location of these concentration points is determined by the Green function
G
and the Robin function
R
associated with
-\Delta
in
\Omega
under Dirichlet boundary conditions. Especially, in
[
16
]
, the existence of a solution concentrating at
k\in\mathbb{N}
distinct points
x_{\infty,1},\cdots,x_{\infty,k}\in\Omega
is obtained whenever

\boldsymbol{x_{\infty}}:=(x_{\infty,1},\cdots,x_{\infty,k})

is a non-degenerate critical point of the Kirchhoff-Routh function
\Psi_{k}:\Omega^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, which is defined as

\Psi_{k}(\boldsymbol{a}):=\sum^{k}_{j=1}\Psi_{k,j}(\boldsymbol{a}),\hskip 9.24994pt~\mbox{ with }~\Psi_{k,j}(\boldsymbol{a}):=R\big(a_{j}\big)-\sum^{k}_{m=1,m\neq j}G\big(a_{j},a_{m}\big),

(1.2)

for
\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{k})
, with
a_{j}\in\Omega,

j=1,\cdots,k
.

Recently, a rather complete characterization of the asymptotic behavior as
p\rightarrow+\infty
of
any
family
u_{p}
of solutions to problem (
1.1
) has been obtained in
[
14
]
, with sharp constants later provided in
[
12
,
30
]
, under the following energy uniform bound assumption:

\sup_{p}\ p\|\nabla u_{p}\|^{2}_{2}\leq C.

It was shown that solutions
u_{p}
necessarily concentrate, up to a subsequence, at a critical point
(x_{\infty,1},\cdots,x_{\infty,k})
of
\Psi_{k}
, for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and vanish elsewhere, in the same way as the solutions found in
[
16
]
. Moreover, their local maximum values converge to
\sqrt{e}
, as
p\rightarrow+\infty
, and the total energy is asymptotically quantized as an integer multiple of
8\pi e
:

\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}p\|\nabla u_{p}\|^{2}_{2}=k\cdot 8\pi e.

Furthermore, the asymptotic local profile of the solutions (suitably rescaled around each concentration point) was also described, showing that it is given by a radial solution
U
of the Liouville equation
-\Delta U=e^{U}
in the whole
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

We refer to solutions having these properties as
spike solutions
.
It is worth noting that least energy solutions of problem (
1.1
) are single-spike solutions (i.e.
k=1
). For previous characterizations of this simpler case we refer to
[
27
,
28
,
1
]
. Observe that when
k=1
, the Kirchhoff-Routh function
\Psi_{1}
reduces to the Robin function
R
. Furthermore, by
[
23
]
, when
\Omega
is convex it must be
k=1
for any spike-solution.

The sharp asymptotic description provided in
[
14
]
plays a crucial role in the proof of the uniqueness result for convex domains developed in
[
13
]
. Indeed, a key ingredient to derive uniqueness in
[
13
]
is the computation of the
Morse index
for single-spike solutions (
k=1
). A central observation is that for the
Morse index

m(u_{p})
(resp. the
augmented Morse index

m_{0}(u_{p})
) of a solution
u_{p}
to problem (
1.1
), the following holds:

m(u_{p})=\sharp\big\{m\in\mathbb{N}:\lambda_{p,m}<1\big\}\hskip 9.24994pt(\text{resp. }m_{0}(u_{p})=\sharp\big\{m\in\mathbb{N}:\lambda_{p,m}\leq 1\big\}),

(1.3)

where
\lambda_{p,1}<\lambda_{p,2}\leq\lambda_{p,3}\leq\cdots
denotes the sequence of eigenvalues for the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta v=\lambda p(u_{p})^{p-1}v~&\mbox{in}~\Omega,\\
v=0~&\mbox{on}~\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(1.4)

Thus, it becomes evident that the asymptotic information about the eigenvalues
\lambda_{p,j}
can be derived from the asymptotic information on
u_{p}
, leading to the computation of
m(u_{p})
. For
k
-spike solutions, this computation has been rigorously carried out in
[
13
]
in the case
k=1
, while when
k\geq 2
, determining the exact Morse index is still an open question, whose answer requires a deeper understanding of the asymptotic behavior of
u_{p}
.

More recently, the characterization in
[
14
]
has been refined in
[
21
]
, where higher order asymptotic expansions have been established (see Section
2.1
for more details).

One of the aims of this paper is to compute the Morse index of any
multi-spike
solution of problem (
1.1
), making use of this sharpened analysis.

Furthermore, the improved characterization in
[
21
]
enabled the proofs in
[
21
]

of the nondegeneracy of multi-spike solutions, and of a local uniqueness result for single-spike solutions.
Here,
local uniqueness
means that if
u
and
\widetilde{u}
are two solutions of problem (
1.1
) concentrating at the same points, then
u\equiv\widetilde{u}
, for sufficiently large
p
. This property is particularly significant in non-convex domains, where uniqueness
tout-court
is not expected for problem (
1.1
), and the focus instead shifts to counting the number of solutions. However, for
k
-spike solutions, with
k\geq 2
,
local uniqueness remains an open problem. Indeed, dealing with multi-spike solutions is considerably more intricate, as the interactions between the spikes must be carefully taken into account. In this work, we also explore the local uniqueness property for multi-spike solutions, which, as we will see, is closely linked to the computation of the Morse index.

Our first result concerns the computation of the Morse index for any
multi-spike
solution of problem (
1.1
).
To state it precisely, let us denote by
m(\boldsymbol{a},f)

and
m_{0}(\boldsymbol{a},f)
the
Morse and augmented Morse index
, respectively, of
\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{k})\in\Omega^{k}
as a critical point of a
C^{2}
function
f:\Omega^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, that is:

\begin{array}[]{lr}m(\boldsymbol{a},f):=\sharp\Big\{l\in\{1,2,\cdots,2k\}:\theta_{l}<0\Big\},\,\,\,\,\,\,m_{0}(\boldsymbol{a},f):=\sharp\Big\{l\in\{1,2,\cdots,2k\}:\theta_{l}\leq 0\Big\},\end{array}

(1.5)

where
\theta_{1}\leq\theta_{2}\cdots\leq\theta_{2k}
are the eigenvalues of the Hessian matrix
D^{2}f(\boldsymbol{a})
.
Moreover, we say that the critical point
\boldsymbol{a}
of
f
is
nondegenerate
if the Hessian matrix
D^{2}f(\boldsymbol{a})
is nondegenerate.
We have:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
u_{p}
be a
k
-spike solution of problem (
1.1
), and let
x_{\infty,1},\cdots,x_{\infty,k}\in\Omega
be its
k
distinct concentration points.
Then there exists
\hat{p}>1
such that the following holds:

k\leq k+m(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\infty},\Psi_{k})\leq m(u_{p})\leq m_{0}(u_{p})\leq k+m_{0}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\infty},\Psi_{k})\leq 3k,\,\,\,\,\mbox{ for }~~p\geq\hat{p},

where
\Psi_{k}
is the function in (
1.2
) and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\infty}:=\big(x_{\infty,1},\cdots,x_{\infty,k}\big)
is a critical point of
\Psi_{k}
. Furthermore, if
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\infty}
is nondegenerate, then (
u_{p}
is non-degenerate and)

m(u_{p})=k+m(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\infty},\Psi_{k})\in[k,3k],\,\,\,\,\mbox{ for }~~p\geq\hat{p}.

Theorem
1.1
extends to dimension
N=2
classical results on Morse index computation
for
blowing-up
positive solutions of the higher dimensional Lane-Emden equation, originally established by Bahri-Li-Rey for
N\geq 4
(
[
2
]
) and by Rey for
N=3
(
[
29
]
). A different, unified proof valid for any dimension
N\geq 3
was later provided by Grossi-Pacella (
[
22
]
) for
single blowing-up
solutions, and by Choi-Kim-Lee (
[
8
]
) for
multiple blowing-up
solutions. This new approach relies on analyzing the linearized eigenvalue problem (
1.4
) at a blowing-up solution
u_{p}
, as
p\rightarrow{(N+2)/(N-2)}^{-}
, leveraging the connection in (
1.3
). Similar to
[
22
,
8
]
, our proof is based on obtaining asymptotic estimates for the eigenvalues of problem (
1.4
), for
p
sufficiently large. This is achieved by fully exploiting the sharp asymptotic characterization of multi-spike solutions
u_{p}
established in
[
14
,
12
]
, along with its significant refinements in
[
21
]
. However, the similarity between the two cases ends here. Indeed, the asymptotic behavior of multi-spike solutions of problem (
1.1
) as
p\rightarrow+\infty
is fundamentally different from that of blowing-up solutions as
p\rightarrow{(N+2)/(N-2)}^{-}
in the higher dimensional case
N\geq 3
. For instance, unlike blowing-up solutions, multi-spike solutions remain uniformly bounded.
In particular, we emphasize that in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, higher order expansions are essential, due to the fact that, as
p\rightarrow+\infty
, the pure power nonlinearity
u^{p}
in the equation (
1.1
) is not close enough to the exponential nonlinearity
e^{U}
which appears in the
limit problem
. Consequently, the computations become quite intricate, making the proof particularly challenging from a technical standpoint. We stress that, when
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\infty}
is non-degenerate, we also re-obtain the nondegeneracy of the multi-spike solution
u_{p}
, which was already known from
[
21
]
. We recall that
u_{p}
is nondegenerate if and only if
\lambda=1
is not an eigenvalue for problem (
1.4
).

Our next result is a degree counting formula for the
k
-spike solutions of problem (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\infty}:=(x_{\infty,1},\cdots,x_{\infty,k})\in\Omega^{k}
,
x_{\infty,i}\neq x_{\infty,j}
if
i\neq j
be
a critical point of the Kirchhoff-Routh function
\Psi_{k}
(see (
1.2
) for its definition).
If
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\infty}
is nondegenerate then the total Leray-Schauder degree of all
k
-spike solutions of problem (
1.1
) concentrating at
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\infty}
is given by

(-1)^{k+m(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\infty},\Psi_{k})},

where

m(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\infty},\Psi_{k})
is the Morse index of
\Psi_{k}
at the critical point
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\infty}
, as defined in (
1.5
).

Last, as a consequence of Theorem
1.1
and of Theorem
1.2
, we derive the following local uniqueness result for multi-spike solutions of problem (
1.1
):

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\infty}:=(x_{\infty,1},\cdots,x_{\infty,k})\in\Omega^{k}
,
x_{\infty,i}\neq x_{\infty,j}
if
i\neq j
be
a critical point of the Kirchhoff-Routh function
\Psi_{k}
(see (
1.2
) for the definition).
If
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\infty}
is nondegenerate then the
k
-spike solution of (
1.1
) concentrating at
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\infty}
is unique (and nondegenerate) for
p
large.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
consists of reducing the problem to a finite-dimensional one and then applying classical properties of degree theory to derive the conclusion, as in
[
6
,
7
]
for the mean field equation.
To achieve this goal, the crucial step is to find a suitable set
S_{p}
(see (
5.16
)), which contains all the
k
-spike solutions with
concentration point
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\infty}
, so that the Leray-Schauder degree can be computed in
S_{p}
.
We observe that the construction of multiple concentrating solutions in
[
16
]
relies on a Lyapunov-Schmidt finite dimensional reduction, which provides detailed information on their structure. In particular, these solutions can be expressed in terms of a finite number of parameters,
\xi_{p,j}\in\Omega
and
\alpha_{p,j}>0
for
j=1,\cdots,k
. Indeed, the solutions constructed in
[
16
]
are given by

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{p,j}P\bar{W}_{p,\xi_{p,j}}+\omega_{p},

(1.6)

where
\bar{W}_{p,\xi_{p,j}}
represents the sum of suitably rescaled profile functions centered at
\xi_{p,j}
(see (
5.13
)), while
\omega_{p}
is a reminder term. Furthermore, as
p\rightarrow+\infty
, we have

\xi_{p,j}\rightarrow x_{\infty,j}
,
\alpha_{p,j}\rightarrow 1
,
\omega_{p}\rightarrow 0
with explicit convergence rates.
Our strategy for the proof of Theorem
1.2
is first to demonstrate that
any

k
-spike solution

u_{p}
of problem (
1.1
) takes the form (
1.6
) (see Proposition
5.4
). To establish this, we have
to refine again the asymptotic analysis of
u_{p}
as
p\rightarrow+\infty
, improving upon the results in
[
21
]
(see Proposition
5.1
).

The uniqueness result in Theorem
1.3
is
local
and in general does not
coincide with the uniqueness of solutions to problem (
1.1
), since there can be many different critical points of the Kirchhoff-Routh function
\Psi_{k}
, and there can be also
k
-spike solutions for different
k
’s (as in the existence results in
[
16
]
).

Local uniqueness results were proved either by computing the Morse index
of the reduced finite dimensional function as in
[
19
,
5
,
26
]
,
or by using the Pohozaev identities
[
20
,
4
,
15
,
21
]
.
Both methods fail in the study of local uniqueness of
k
-spike solutions
for problem (
1.1
) if
k\geq 2
. In fact,
when computing the Morse index
of the reduced finite dimensional function, one needs to
estimate the derivatives of the error term
\omega_{p}
in (
1.6
).
Due to the exponential smallness of the scalar
\varepsilon_{p,j}
(see
(
2.5
)), the derivatives of the error term
\omega_{p}

is uncontrollable. On the other hand, the starting point to use the
Pohozaev identities is to prove that
x_{p,j}
(see (
2.3
))
satisfies
|x_{p,j}-x_{\infty,j}|=o(\varepsilon_{p,j})
, which is
very difficult to verify if
k\geq 2
.
This is the main reason that in
[
21
]
, local uniqueness result can
be proved only for the
case
k=1
. The novelty here to prove the local uniqueness result for
the cases
k\geq 2
is that
we compute the Morse index of each
k
-spike solution concentrating at
\bf x_{\infty}
, and the degree of all the
k
-spike solutions concentrating at
\bf x_{\infty}
. The first step can be achieved by using the local
Pohozaev identities, while in the second step, it
is essential to deform
the corresponding functional suitably, so one can avoid
the estimates of the derivatives of the error term
\omega_{p}
.

Some condition on the critical point
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\infty}
is needed
to obtain the local uniqueness result, as evidenced by the example given
in
[
20
]
for the singularly perturbed Schrödinger equations.

Section

we first study the eigenpairs

, for

.

and expansion for

2.
Basic results

2.1.
Known asymptotic results for the Lane-Emdem problem

Theorem 2.1

(c.f.
[
12
,
14
,
21
]
)
.

Let
u_{p}
be a family of solutions to problem (
1.1
) satisfying the energy uniform bound

\sup_{p}p\|\nabla u_{p}\|^{2}_{2}<\infty.

(2.1)

Then there exist a finite number of
k
of distinct points
x_{\infty,j}\in\Omega
,
j=1,\cdots,k
and a subsequence of
p
(still denoted by
p
) such that setting

\mathcal{S}:=\big\{x_{\infty,1},\cdots,x_{\infty,k}\big\}
,
one has

\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}pu_{p}=8\pi\sqrt{e}\sum^{k}_{j=1}G(x,x_{\infty,j})\,\,~\mbox{in}~C^{2}_{loc}(\Omega\backslash\mathcal{S}),

(2.2)

the energy satisfies

\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}p\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{p}(x)|^{2}dx=8\pi e\cdot k,

and the concentrated points
x_{\infty,j}
,
j=1,\cdots,k
, fulfill the system

\nabla_{x}\Psi_{k}\big(x_{\infty,1},\cdots,x_{\infty,k}\big)=0.

Moreover, for some small fixed
r>0
, let
x_{p,j}\in\overline{B_{2r}(x_{\infty,j})}\subset\Omega
be the sequence defined as

u_{p}(x_{p,j})=\max_{\overline{B_{2r}(x_{\infty,j})}}u_{p}(x),

(2.3)

and

\varepsilon_{p,j}:=\Big(p\big(u_{p}(x_{p,j})\big)^{p-1}\Big)^{-1/2},

(2.4)

then for any
j=1,\cdots,k
and fixed small constant
\delta>0
, it holds

\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}x_{p,j}=x_{\infty,j},~~~\hskip 9.24994pt\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}u_{p}(x_{p,j})=\sqrt{e},

\varepsilon_{p,j}=e^{-\frac{p}{4}}\Bigl(e^{-\big(2\pi\Psi_{k,j}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\infty})+\frac{3\log 2}{2}+\frac{3}{4}\big)}+O\big(\frac{1}{p^{1-\delta}}\big)\Bigr),

(2.5)

and

\frac{\varepsilon_{p,j}}{\varepsilon_{p,s}}=e^{2\pi\big(\Psi_{k,s}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\infty})-\Psi_{k,j}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\infty})\big)}+O\big(\frac{1}{p^{1-\delta}}\big)\,\,~\mbox{for}~1\leq j,s\leq k,

(2.6)

where
\Psi_{k,j}
is the function in (
1.2
) and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\infty}:=\big(x_{\infty,1},\cdots,x_{\infty,k}\big)
.
And setting

w_{p,j}(y):=\frac{p}{u_{p}(x_{p,j})}\Big(u_{p}(x_{p,j}+\varepsilon_{p,j}y)-u_{p}(x_{p,j})\Big),~y\in\Omega_{p,j}:=\frac{\Omega-x_{p,j}}{\varepsilon_{p,j}},

(2.7)

3.
Preliminary results for the eigenfunctions

In this section we obtain starting asymptotic expansions, as
p\rightarrow+\infty
, for the eigenfunctions
v_{p,l}
associated to the eigenvalues
\lambda_{p,l}
of the linearized problem:

\begin{cases}-\Delta v_{p,l}=\lambda_{p,l}\,p\,u^{p-1}_{p}\,v_{p,l}~&\mbox{in}~\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
v_{p,l}=0~&\mbox{on}~\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(3.1)

In particular, in order to compute the Morse index of the solution
u_{p}
(see Section
4
), we need to understand the behavior of
v_{p,l}
in the two occurrences when the associated eigenvalue
\lambda_{p,l}
converges to either
0
or
1
, these results are collected in Proposition
3.3
and Proposition
3.5
below.

W.l.g. we may assume that the eigenfunctions
v_{p,l}
satisfy

\|v_{p,l}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1.

(3.2)

Letting
l\in\mathbb{N}
, we take for any
j=1,\cdots,k
,

\widetilde{v}_{p,l,j}(x):=v_{p,l}\big(x_{p,j}+\varepsilon_{p,j}x\big),~~x\in\Omega_{p,j}:=\Big\{x:x_{p,j}+\varepsilon_{p,j}x\in\Omega\Big\}.

(3.3)

From (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), it follows that

\begin{cases}-\Delta\widetilde{v}_{p,l,j}=\lambda_{p,l}\widetilde{V}_{p,j}\widetilde{v}_{p,l,j},&~\mbox{in}~~\Omega_{p,j},\\[2.84526pt]
\widetilde{v}_{p,l,j}=0,&~\mbox{in}~~\partial\Omega_{p,j},\\[2.84526pt]
\|\widetilde{v}_{p,l,j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{p,j})}=1,\end{cases}

where
\widetilde{V}_{p,j}:=\Big(1+\frac{w_{p,j}}{p}\Big)^{p-1}

with
w_{p,j}
the rescaled function in (
2.7
).

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
l\in\mathbb{N}
, if
\lambda_{p,l}
is bounded independent of
p
, then for any
j=1,\cdots,k
, there exists

V_{l,j}(x)\in C^{2,\alpha}_{loc}\big(\mathbb{R}^{2}\big)
with some
\alpha\in(0,1)
satisfying

\widetilde{v}_{p,l,j}\to V_{l,j}~~\mbox{in}~~C^{2,\alpha}_{loc}\big(\mathbb{R}^{2}\big),~\mbox{as}~p\to\infty,

(3.4)

and

\begin{cases}-\Delta V_{l,j}=\lambda_{\infty,l}e^{U}V_{l,j}\,\,~\mbox{in}~\mathbb{R}^{2},\\[5.69054pt]
\|V_{l,j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq 1,\end{cases}

(3.5)

where
\displaystyle\lambda_{\infty,l}:=\lim_{p\to\infty}\lambda_{p,l}
by taking a subsequence.

Proof.

We observe from (
2.8
)
that
\widetilde{V}_{p,j}
is bounded in
B_{R}(0)
for any
R>0
.
Thus the result follows from the
L^{p}
estimates
and Schauder estimates for the elliptic equations.

∎

To examine the Morse index of
u_{p}
, we will need to study the case
\lambda_{\infty,l}=0
and
\lambda_{\infty,l}=1
. W.l.g we further assume the orthogonality of the eigenfunctions in Dirichlet norm as follows:

\int_{\Omega}\nabla v_{p,l}\cdot\nabla v_{p,l^{\prime}}dx=0~~~\mbox{if}~~l\neq l^{\prime}.

(3.6)

In fact, if
\lambda_{\infty,l}\neq\lambda_{\infty,l^{\prime}}
, then (
3.6
) is direct.
If
\lambda_{\infty,l}=\lambda_{\infty,l^{\prime}}
, by standard Schimidt orthogonalization, we find
new functions (still denoted by
v_{p,l}
) satisfying (
3.6
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
l\in\mathbb{N}
, if
\lambda_{\infty,l}\in[0,\infty)
, then there exists
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,k\}
such that it holds

V_{l,j}\not\equiv 0,

(3.7)

where
V_{l.j}
is the limit function in (
3.4
).

Proof.

Firstly, using (
2.2
) and the fact
\|v_{p,l}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1
, it holds

\begin{split}\lambda_{p,l}pu^{p-1}_{p}v_{p,l}=O\Big(\frac{C^{p-1}}{p^{p-2}}\Big)\to 0~~\mbox{locally uniformly in}~~\Omega\backslash\big\{x_{\infty,1},\cdots,x_{\infty,k}\big\}~~\mbox{as}~~p\to\infty.\end{split}

Then from the standard elliptic regularity estimates, we can deduce that

\begin{split}{v}_{p,l}(x)\to 0~~\mbox{local uniformly in}~~\Omega\backslash\big\{x_{\infty,1},\cdots,x_{\infty,k}\big\}~~\mbox{as}~~p\to\infty.\end{split}

(3.8)

Assume by contradiction that
V_{l,j}\equiv 0
for some
l\in\mathbb{N}
and
all
j=1,\cdots,k
, namely that

\begin{split}\widetilde{v}_{p,l,j}(x)\to 0~~\mbox{local uniformly in}~~\mathbb{R}^{2}~~\mbox{as}~~p\to\infty.\end{split}

We will prove that for large
R>0
, it holds

\widetilde{v}_{p,l,j}(x)=o_{R}(1)~~\mbox{ in}~~B_{\varepsilon_{p,j}^{-1}\tau}(0)\setminus B_{R}(0),

which, together with (
3.8
), will give a contradiction to

\|v_{p,l}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1
.

In fact, let
\alpha_{p,j}=\max_{|y|=R}|v_{p,l}(y)|
and

\beta_{p,j}=\max_{|y|=\varepsilon_{p,j}^{-1}\tau}|v_{p,l}(y)|
.
Then
\alpha_{p,j}=o(1)
and
\beta_{p,j}=o(1)
. In view of Lemma
2.2
,
we have

|\lambda_{p,l}\widetilde{V}_{p,j}\widetilde{v}_{p,l,j}|\leq\frac{C}{|y|^{4-\delta}},\hskip 9.24994ptR\leq|y|\leq\frac{\tau}{\varepsilon_{p,j}}.

Let

be the solution of the following problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta w=\frac{C}{|y|^{4-\delta}},\;\;\text{in}\;B_{\varepsilon_{p,j}^{-1}\tau}(0)\setminus B_{R}(0),\\[2.84526pt]

\begin{split}w(y)=&\alpha_{p,j}+\frac{C}{(2-\delta)^{2}}\frac{1}{R^{2-\delta}}+\Bigl(\beta_{p,j}+\frac{C}{(2-\delta)^{2}}\frac{\varepsilon_{p,j}^{2-\delta}}{\tau^{2-\delta}}-\alpha_{p,j}-\frac{C}{(2-\delta)^{2}}\frac{1}{R^{2-\delta}}\Bigr)\frac{\ln\frac{|y|}{R}}{\ln\frac{\tau}{\varepsilon_{p,j}R}}\\
&-\frac{C}{(2-\delta)^{2}}\frac{1}{|y|^{2-\delta}},\end{split}

which gives

. Thus the claim follows.

∎

Lemma 3.4

Theorem

u_{p}
, for
p
large.

Fixed
r>0
small such that, for

B_{4r}(x_{\infty,i})\subset\Omega~~\mbox{ and }~~B_{4r}(x_{\infty,i})\bigcap B_{4r}(x_{\infty,j})=\emptyset,

\displaystyle u_{p,i}:=\phi_{i}u_{p},\hskip 18.49988pt\psi_{p,i,q}:=\phi_{i}\frac{\partial u_{p}}{\partial x_{q}}\hskip 9.24994pt(q=1,2).

(4.1)

Then we have following identities.

Lemma 4.1
.

It holds

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\big|\nabla u_{p,i}\big|^{2}dx=\int_{\Omega}u_{p}^{2}|\nabla\phi_{i}|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}u^{p+1}_{p}\phi^{2}_{i}dx.\end{split}

(4.2)

Furthermore, for any
\beta_{p,i},\gamma_{p,i}\in\mathbb{R}

z_{p,i}:=\beta_{p,i}\psi_{p,i,1}+\gamma_{p,i}\psi_{p,i,2}

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\big|\nabla z_{p,i}\big|^{2}dx=\int_{\Omega}s_{p,i}^{2}|\nabla\phi_{i}|^{2}dx+p\int_{\Omega}u^{p-1}_{p}s_{p,i}^{2}\phi^{2}_{i}dx,\end{split}

(4.3)

and

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{p,i}\cdot\nabla z_{p,i}dx=\int_{\Omega}u_{p}s_{p,i}|\nabla\phi_{i}|^{2}dx+\frac{p+1}{2}\int_{\Omega}u^{p}_{p}s_{p,i}\phi^{2}_{i}dx,\end{split}

(4.4)

where
s_{p,i}:=\beta_{p,i}\frac{\partial u_{p}}{\partial x_{1}}+\gamma_{p,i}\frac{\partial u_{p}}{\partial x_{2}}

Proof.

Multiplying
-\Delta u_{p}=u_{p}^{p}
in
\Omega

\phi^{2}_{i}u_{p}
and integrating, we have

\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}_{i}u_{p}^{p+1}dx=\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}^{2}\big|\nabla u_{p}\big|^{2}dx+2\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}u_{p}\nabla\phi_{i}\cdot\nabla u_{p}dx.

4.2
) follows observing that
|\nabla(\phi_{i}u_{p})|^{2}=u_{p}^{2}|\nabla\phi_{i}|^{2}+\phi_{i}^{2}|\nabla u_{p}|^{2}+2\phi_{i}u_{p}\nabla\phi_{i}\cdot\nabla u_{p}

and integrating, we have

\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}_{i}\big|\nabla s_{p,i}\big|^{2}dx+2\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}s_{p,i}\nabla\phi_{i}\cdot\nabla s_{p,i}dx=p\int_{\Omega}u^{p-1}_{p}\phi_{i}^{2}s_{p,i}^{2}dx,

then (

) follows observing that

and that

|\nabla(\phi_{i}s_{p,i})|^{2}=s_{p,i}^{2}|\nabla\phi_{i}|^{2}+\phi_{i}^{2}|\nabla s_{p,i}|^{2}+2\phi_{i}s_{p,i}\nabla\phi_{i}\cdot\nabla s_{p,i}.

Also multiplying

\begin{split}\frac{p+1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}_{i}u_{p}^{p}s_{p,i}dx=&\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}^{2}\nabla u_{p}\cdot\nabla s_{p,i}dx+\int_{\Omega}u_{p}\phi_{i}\nabla\phi_{i}\cdot\nabla s_{p,i}dx+\int_{\Omega}s_{p,i}\phi_{i}\nabla\phi_{i}\cdot\nabla u_{p}dx.\end{split}

Since

Proposition 4.2
.

For

(4.6)

Proof.

, where the functions

have been defined in (

Hence from (

Step 1. Using the variational characterization

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\big|\nabla f_{p}\big|^{2}dx=&\int_{\Omega}\big|\sum^{k}_{i=1}\alpha_{p,i}\nabla u_{p,i}\big|^{2}dx=\sum^{k}_{i=1}\alpha^{2}_{p,i}\int_{\Omega}\big|\nabla u_{p,i}\big|^{2}dx\\
=&\sum^{k}_{i=1}\alpha^{2}_{p,i}\int_{\Omega}\big|\nabla\phi_{i}\big|^{2}u_{p}^{2}dx+\sum^{k}_{i=1}\alpha^{2}_{p,i}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}^{2}u_{p}^{p+1}dx.\end{split}

Appendix A
The derivatives of the Kirchhoff-Routh function

For the reader convenience we collect here the expression of the derivatives of the Kirchhoff-Routh function
\Psi_{k}:\Omega^{k}(\subset\mathbb{R}^{2k})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined for
\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{k})
with
a_{j}=({a_{j}}_{1},{a_{j}}_{2})\in\Omega\ (\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
), for
j=1,\cdots,k
, as

\Psi_{k}(\boldsymbol{a}):=\sum^{k}_{j=1}\Psi_{k,j}(\boldsymbol{a}),\hskip 9.24994pt~\mbox{ with }~\Psi_{k,j}(\boldsymbol{a}):=R\big(a_{j}\big)-\sum^{k}_{m=1,m\neq j}G\big(a_{j},a_{m}\big).

We introduce the notation

y_{2j-2+i}:={a_{j}}_{i}

for
j=1,\cdots,k
,
i=1,2
, hence

\boldsymbol{y}=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{2k})=\boldsymbol{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{2k}.

Easy computations show that

\frac{\partial\Psi_{k}(\boldsymbol{y})}{\partial y_{2j-2+i}}=\frac{\partial R(a_{j})}{\partial x_{i}}-2\sum_{m=1,m\neq j}^{k}\partial_{i}G(a_{j},a_{m})

and

\frac{\partial^{2}\Psi_{k}(\boldsymbol{y})}{\partial y_{2j-2+i}\ \partial y_{2h-2+q}}=\Big[\frac{\partial^{2}R(a_{j})}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{q}}-2\sum_{m=1,m\neq j}^{k}\partial_{iq}^{2}G(a_{j},a_{m})\Big]\delta_{hj}-2\partial_{i}D_{q}G(a_{j},a_{h})(1-\delta_{hj})

(A.1)

for
j,h=1,\cdots,k
and
i,q=1,2
, where
\delta_{hj}
denotes the Kronecker’s symbol.

Appendix B
A linear problem in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

Consider

-\Delta u=f(x),\hskip 9.24994pt\text{in}\;\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(B.1)

We assume that
f
satisfies the following condition

|f(x)|\leq\frac{C}{1+|x|^{p}},~~~\mbox{for some $p>3$ and $C>0$}.

Proposition B.1
.

Let
u
be a solution of problem (
B.1
), satisfying that as
|x|\to+\infty
,

u(x)=A\log|x|+B+O\Big(\frac{1}{|x|}\Big),

for some constants
A>0
and
B
, then

u(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\frac{1}{|y-x|}f(y)\,dy+B,~~\mbox{if $\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(y)\,dy>0$},

while

u(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\frac{1}{|y-x|}f(y)\,dy-B,~~\mbox{if $\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(y)\,dy<0$}.

Proof.

Since
u(x)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\frac{1}{|y-x|}f(y)\,dy

is harmonic, whose growth at infinity is bounded by
\log|x|
,
u
can be represented as

u(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\frac{1}{|y-x|}f(y)\,dy+C,

for some constant
C
.

On the other hand, for
|x|
large,

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\frac{1}{|y-x|}f(y)\,dy\\
=&\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B_{\delta|x|}(0)}\log\frac{1}{|y-x|}f(y)\,dy+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{\delta|x|}(0)}\log\frac{1}{|y-x|}f(y)\,dy\\
=&\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B_{\delta|x|}(0)}\Bigl(\log\frac{1}{|x|}+O\bigl(\frac{|y|}{|x|}\bigr)\Bigr)f(y)\,dy+O\Bigl(\frac{1}{|x|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{\delta|x|}(0)}|\log\frac{1}{|y-x|}|\frac{1}{1+|y|^{p-1}}\,dy\Bigr)\\
=&\frac{1}{2\pi|x|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(y)\,dy+O\Bigl(\frac{1}{|x|}\Bigr).\end{split}

Thus, we see that
C=B
, if
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(y)\,dy>0
, while
C=-B

if
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(y)\,dy<0
.
∎

Combined image block and DBSCAN processing for large field of view structured light point cloud denoising
Authors: Keming Zhang, Diwei Yu, Shikai Ming, Yu Cao, Jun Yao, Ping Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractStripe projection structured light systems are widely used in fields such as industrial inspection and virtual reality. With the gradual growth of the demand for sizeable field-of-view three-dimensional morphology reconstruction, the expansion of the redevelopment area will trigger additional environmental factor interactions, which may lead to an increase in noise points during the point cloud reconstruction process. To reduce the complexity of operations on 3D point cloud data, we propose a combined preprocessing method for phase field and binocular matching two-dimensional data. First, we divide the absolute phase field data computed from the left and right viewpoints into multiple image blocks and utilize the statistical information of each image block for outlier detection and rejection. Second, calculate the distance information between binocular matching points, construct distance indexed scatter data based on this information, use the DBSCAN clustering algorithm for outlier detection, and backtrack to reject the outlier data. Finally, combined with the camera calibration information, the reconstruction of the 3D shape of the measured object can be realized. The experimental results show that, compared with gaussian filtering, bilateral filtering, and statistical filtering algorithms, our method is more effective in removing the noise points in the point cloud data while retaining the target point cloud more completely, thus improving the reconstruction quality of the point cloud data with a large field of view.Keywords: Structured light, Stray point removal, DBSCAN, Large field of viewSubject terms: Optics and photonics, Mechanical engineering

Introduction

With the rapid development of industrialization, structured light vision reconstruction technology has been widely used in the fields of intelligent manufacturing, aerospace, medical support, and cultural relics protection. The traditional contact measurement method, in which the sensor of the measurement equipment directly contacts the surface of the object to be measured, has obvious limitations, although it can meet the accuracy requirements in some application scenarios. For example, the applicability of traditional contact measurement methods is poor for soft materials and thin film layers that are easily damaged. In addition, the size of the measuring probe of the conventional contact measurement method also limits the minimum size it can detect. For the measured objects with tiny concave surfaces or defects, if their size is smaller than the radius of the measuring probe, the probe cannot enter the concave surfaces or defects for measurement1,2. In contrast, the stripe projection structured light method, as a non-contact reconstruction measurement technique, obtains the 3D shape information of an object by projecting a specific stripe pattern onto the object’s surface to be measured and utilizing the deformation of the pattern on the object surface. Compared with other 3D measurement methods, the equipment of the stripe projection structured light method is relatively simple, whether it is a small part or a large structure, the projection and acquisition system can be adjusted to adapt to different measurement and reconstruction needs, and at the same time, it is able to realize high-precision and fast shape reconstruction measurements3,4. Stripe projection structured light is gradually replacing contact measurement methods in some fields and has achieved rapid development, and its advantages of non-contact measurement, high accuracy and fast measurement make it one of the preferred measurement methods in many applications.

However, in addition to structured light measurement technology, there are many other non-contact 3D reconstruction technologies that have been widely researched and applied. For example, Lidar technology acquires 3D information of objects by emitting laser beams and measuring the time delay of the reflected light. It is characterized by high precision and long-range measurement and is widely used in fields such as terrain mapping and autonomous driving5. Moreover, photogrammetry reconstructs 3D scenes from multi-view 2D images, with the advantages of low cost and wide applicability. It is commonly used in cultural heritage preservation and architectural measurement6. Each of these technologies has its own strengths and weaknesses and is suitable for different application scenarios.

Nevertheless, structured light measurement technology still has unique advantages in certain specific scenarios. For instance, in scenarios that require high-precision measurement within a limited measurement range, structured light technology can provide higher resolution and more accurate surface details7. However, when the measurement range is expanded to a large field of view, structured light 3D reconstruction faces numerous challenges. For example, the expansion of the measurement range may lead to the accumulation of errors in multi-point cloud stitching, thereby causing significant measurement deviations8. Moreover, environmental factors, such as variations in illumination and reflective interference, may degrade image quality, resulting in a large number of outliers or noise in the point cloud computation results9.

In the study of the multi-viewpoint cloud alignment problem, Changwen Liu and other researchers10 proposed an innovative scanning calibration method. After obtaining the translation distance and rotation angle data of the structured light sensor, this method realizes the accurate alignment of the multi-viewpoint cloud by comprehensively processing these data. In addition, a calibration technique that fuses orientation and position information for a more comprehensive description of the rotational motion is presented, and an external parameter estimation method for camera calibration is optimized to improve the accuracy of point cloud data fusion. Keming Zhang11 proposed a new method to calculate the phase using discrete Fourier series (DFS) to address the problem of the influence of non-sinusoidal characteristics in large field-of-view measurements. By extracting the fundamental wave coefficients from N consecutive samples in a single cycle and reconstructing four images with an interval of , the method can obtain a more accurate phase distribution, effectively eliminates the influence of non-sinusoidal characteristics in large field-of-view measurements. Feilong Cao and other researchers12 conducted an in-depth study on multi-point cloud alignment technology and proposed a new deep learning-based PCR method, RecARONet. By constructing an adaptive relation-oriented convolutional network and an adaptive neighborhood-based constraint algorithm, this method provides an effective solution to the multi-viewpoint cloud. By creating an adaptive relationship-oriented convolutional network and an adaptive neighborhood-based constraint algorithm; the method provides an effective solution to the accuracy refinement problem of multi-viewpoint cloud alignment. Guoxing Sun13 developed an innovative structure-aware denoising technique that relies on an adaptive neighborhood selection mechanism to relocate the points and a feature-aware point updating method to match the estimated normals to eliminate real-world noisy point clouds. This method optimizes the processing of real-world noisy point clouds and improves the quality and accuracy of the data. Zhaoshuai Qi et al14 proposed an optical projection-based invalid point removal strategy by introducing a new criterion for invalid point determination. The method first calculates the fundamental matrix of the measurement system and the kernel line (PIC) in the projected image plane of the pixel and then removes the invalid points based on the polar constraints using the fundamental matrix. This method efficiently processes complex scenes containing backgrounds, shadows, noise, and interreflections. Pei-Ju Chiang et al15 adopted a multi-decoding mode denoising strategy to address the problems of noise interference and inaccurate point cloud matching in traditional 3D reconstruction methods. Non-valid point clouds are removed by decoding the reconstruction results multiple times, and subsequently, the incorrectly matched points are further eliminated by filtering the point clouds on the polyline to realize the accurate processing of non-valid point cloud data. The techniques and methodological principles proposed by related scholars in the field of structured light 3D reconstruction have contributed significantly to the advancement of the field.

In the process of large field of view reconstruction, it is usually necessary to complete the complete morphology reconstruction by splicing multiple point clouds, which is easy to introduce splicing errors, thus affecting the measurement accuracy and making the measurement process more time-consuming and complex. In addition, large field-of-view measurements are more likely to be affected by various environmental factors due to the broader area involved, such as ambient lighting that may lead to uneven illumination of the light source, and the reflective properties of the surface of the object that may cause light reflection, refraction, or scattering during the measurement process, which will ultimately generate a large amount of noise in the point cloud data. Although many methods for removing outliers have been proposed in existing studies, such as deep learning-based point cloud denoising methods16, most of these methods are applied after the generation of point clouds, which results in high computational complexity.Therefore,This study is based on the binocular pixel-matched projected stripe structured light technique, which aims at the effective removal of noise points in extensive field-of-view measurement data. The initial step involves processing the absolute phase field data from the left and right camera viewpoints by calculating the mean value of the phase values and their standard deviation, and setting an adaptive threshold to screen out the anomalous data that deviates significantly from the mean value. Subsequently, binocular pixel matching computation is performed based on the resolved absolute phase field data, and the matched data set is further processed. In this process, a DBSCAN clustering algorithm is used to detect the distance index value data to remove outliers in stereo vision matching and evaluate the results. Finally, combined with the camera calibration information, the point cloud 3D coordinate data are solved. Compared with the conventional method of performing clutter removal after acquiring 3D point cloud data, this study performs data preprocessing at the stage of parcel dephasing and binocular pixel matching, a strategy that reduces the possibility of erroneous data being involved in the subsequent calculations, reduces the amount of data processing and thus reduces the arithmetic burden, and also enables the acquisition of higher-quality point cloud data for the subsequent manipulation of the point cloud data.

Technological route

In addressing the problem of spurious points encountered in large field-of-view structured light 3D reconstruction, the technology roadmap is shown in Fig. 5, this study first statistically processes the absolute phase field data obtained from the left and right viewpoint solutions of the camera. By adopting a strategy based on spatial proximity25, this method takes into account the mutual positional relationships of the data points in space. Specifically, the phase field data is subdivided into several image blocks, and outlier detection and removal are performed based on the statistical information within each image block. After that, the KD-Tree algorithm featuring absolute phase was used for homonymous point-pixel search and matching, realizing subpixel-level accurate correspondence of homonymous image points in the left and right camera viewpoints. For the binocular point pairs that were successfully matched, this study calculated the distances between the point pairs and used them as the basis to draw a distance index scatter plot, where the horizontal coordinate represents the index of the distance value and the vertical coordinate represents the distance value itself. By implementing the DBSCAN clustering algorithm, the spurious point index values in the plot were identified and recorded. Subsequently, a backtracking query is performed in the binocular matching data based on these index values to clear the coordinate points with abnormal matching. Once the noise points are removed, the accurate binocular matching data are combined with the camera calibration parameters to calculate the 3D coordinates of the point cloud data, thus accomplishing the accurate reconstruction of the 3D morphology of the measured object surface.

Phase-field data outlier removal

The representation of phase map data is a crucial data format in structured light imaging technology, in which structured light phase maps are usually presented as an image, with the value of each pixel point encoding the phase information of the corresponding position. The resulting absolute phase field data may contain some outliers that deviate from the measurement target due to the inhomogeneity of the target surface, environmental noise, etc. These outliers, if not removed, will cause interference in the subsequent data processing and analysis. For this reason, this study is based on the image block data processing strategy26, which divides the two-dimensional phase-field data into several image blocks and utilizes the statistical information within each image block to identify the outlier points, as shown in Fig. 6.

Image block traversal schematic.

Binocular matching data outliers removal

After processing the absolute phase field data obtained from the left and right camera viewpoints, this study employs a homonymous point search matching strategy based on KD-Tree to achieve sub-pixel level accurate pairing of homonymous image points in the left and right camera images by using absolute phase as the feature. KD-Tree is a data structure for efficient nearest-neighbor searching of high-dimensional spatial data27,28, which can be achieved quickly by dividing the data space into several super rectangular regions and constructing a binary tree to realize nearest-neighbor search quickly. The structure constructs indexes based on the data distribution, and reduces the search time complexity by pruning and backtracking operations of the tree to meet the needs of large field-of-view data retrieval. In this study, the KD-Tree is first constructed efficiently using the phase matrix of the right image, and the tree is stored in a variable; subsequently, the most immediate neighbor of each point in the phase matrix of the left image is searched in this tree by the KD-Tree search algorithm, and the indexes of the nearest neighbors and their distances are obtained by using the knnsearch function. Based on the indexes and distances information of these nearest neighbors, the pixels that satisfy the conditions are identified by traversing and evaluating the pixels within the window range of the right map, and interpolation calculations29 are performed to obtain more accurate matching results for the homonymous points.

In the acquired binocular vision system homonymous point-pixel matching data, erroneous matching points can occur due to various factors. These binocular matching error points can adversely affect the subsequent 3D reconstruction process, including, but not limited to, the introduction of noise points, the increase of point cloud noise, and the degradation of 3D model surface accuracy. The root cause of these problems is mainly the anomalous data values generated during the matching process. Therefore, the binocular matching data need to be detected for the abnormal values to eliminate these anomalous matching values and to improve the accuracy and quality of the subsequent 3D reconstruction.

In binocular vision systems, distance information is widely used as an essential basis for assessing the similarity or correlation between matched points. Theoretically, points on the same object surface should have a certain similarity in the left and right images, therefore, if the distance between two matched points is out of a predetermined range, this usually indicates that these points may not belong to the same object surface, but are stray or anomalous points. These abnormal points can be effectively identified and detected by analyzing the distance scatter plot, thus providing a cleaner and more accurate database for further data processing and 3D reconstruction.

The DBSCAN (Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise) algorithm30,31 is a density-based spatial clustering algorithm whose main advantage lies in its ability to automatically identify the number of clusters based on the local density distribution of the data points without the need to predefine the number of clusters. The algorithm is suitable for recognizing clusters with complex shapes and different density distributions while demonstrating robustness to noisy data. As shown in Fig. 9a, suppose that point A is identified as a core point, which is surrounded by other data points, such as A’, A”, A”’, etc., which are located in the neighborhood of A and thus categorized as density-directed points. As the clustering process continues, it is gradually extended to more points. During the process, if specific points lack sufficient data points within their neighborhoods to support further expansion, they are determined to be boundary points, such as points B and C. Further, suppose it is impossible to expand to more data points from these boundary points. In that case, these boundary points are considered isolated or outlier points, such as point N, which is not associated with any identified clusters and thus is labeled as an outlier point. Figure 9b shows a schematic of the clustering.

Schematic diagram of DBSCAN clustering algorithm. (a) schematic diagram (b) clustering diagram.

This study uses the DBSCAN algorithm for outlier detection on distance-indexed scatter plots based on two-dimensional image data. The DBSCAN algorithm is suitable for outlier detection because of its high sensitivity to localized density variations of data points. It considers regions of lower density as regions that may contain outliers and classifies data points in these low-density regions as noise points, thus realizing effective identification of outliers. Camera parameter calibration

To ensure the accurate configuration of the experimental equipment, this study connected the two industrial cameras and the projector to the computer through the data interface and realized the fixed installation of the equipment by using the adjustable bracket. Reprojection error32 is one of the critical metrics for evaluating the accuracy of a camera’s calibration, and it reflects the accuracy of the camera’s internal and external parameters. This parameter is calculated by comparing the difference between the known 3D coordinates of the calibration plate or calibration point and its expected projected position on the image with the actual detected image corner coordinates. The reprojection error is calculated by calculating the average difference between the theoretical image coordinates and the detected corner point coordinates. As shown in Fig. 14, the reprojection error calculated is 0.07 pixels under the condition that the camera is 3 meters away from the object under test. Results and analysis

In this study, the proposed method is compared and experimented with gaussian, bilateral, and statistical filtering denoising algorithms widely used in point cloud processing libraries33 to evaluate their efficacy in removing outlier noise points. The parameter values selected in the experiments are determined after several tests to maximize the removal of outlier noise points while ensuring the optimization of the reconstruction effect. In the gaussian filtering experiment, the maximum number of neighboring points in the neighborhood of each point is set to 10, and the filtering threshold is set to 0.5. The bilateral filtering experiment requires the definition of filter parameters, in which the window size is set to 5, and the standard deviation in the spatial and pixel domains is set to 0.1. In the statistical filtering experiment, the maximum allowable distance between a point in the cloud and other points in its neighborhood is specified to be 0.3, which is a criterion to determine which points should be considered as noise and be removed. Points should be regarded as noise and eliminated. The experimental results are shown in Fig. 25, and the outlier removal efficiencies of the various methods are shown in Table 7.

Discussion

Although the proposed method demonstrates promising results in removing noise points from large field-of-view structured light point cloud data, it still has several limitations that need to be addressed in future research:

Adaptability to Complex Point Cloud Scenarios: The current method may face challenges when dealing with scenes featuring complex geometries or high-density point clouds. For example, its performance may be affected in cases of uneven point cloud distribution or significant occlusion. Future research could explore more robust image block partitioning strategies and clustering algorithms to improve the method’s adaptability in complex scenarios.

For example, its performance may be affected in cases of uneven point cloud distribution or significant occlusion. Future research could explore more robust image block partitioning strategies and clustering algorithms to improve the method’s adaptability in complex scenarios.

Sensitivity to Parameter Selection: The proposed method involves several parameters (e.g., image block size, neighborhood radius, and minimum points threshold in DBSCAN), and their selection can impact the method’s performance. Although we have chosen appropriate parameter values through experiments, they may need to be adjusted for different application scenarios. Future research could investigate adaptive parameter selection methods to reduce reliance on manual tuning.

Conclusion

When performing structured light large-field-of-view 3D morphology reconstruction, the increase in the field-of-view range is accompanied by the rise in the complexity of the environment, which leads to the problem of generating a large number of point cloud noise points during the 3D reconstruction process becomes more serious. This study proposes a data preprocessing scheme for early outlier detection and processing of absolute phase field data and binocular pixel matching data to address this challenge. The method first applies an outlier processing technique based on the statistical information of image blocks for phase field data; subsequently, for binocular pixel matching data, outliers are detected and identified by constructing distance index data and applying the DBSCAN clustering algorithm. Based on the constructed experimental platform for large field of view data acquisition, several objects were photographed and processed for 3D reconstruction, and the effect of this method was compared and analyzed with the effects of the traditional outlier removal techniques, such as Gaussian filtering, bilateral filtering, and statistical filtering. The experimental results show that the pre-processing combination method proposed in this study can retain the target point cloud data more entirely and improve the reconstruction quality of the point cloud dataset while effectively removing noise points or aggregated discrete noise points.

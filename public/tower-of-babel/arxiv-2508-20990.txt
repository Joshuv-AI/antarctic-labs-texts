Abstract
The symplectic geometry mode decomposition (SGMD) is a powerful method for decomposing time series, which is based on the diagonal averaging principle (DAP) inherited from the singular spectrum analysis (SSA). Although the authors of SGMD method generalized the form of the trajectory matrix in SSA, the DAP is not updated simultaneously. In this work, we pointed out the limitations of the SGMD method and fixed the bugs with the pulling back theorem for computing the given component of time series from the corresponding component of trajectory matrix. Keywords : Time series; Symplectic geometry mode decomposition (SGMD); Singular spectrum analysis (SSA); Diagonal averaging principle (DAP); Pulling back theorem

2.1
Notations

The time series
X

\displaystyle X=\left\{x[n]:0\leq n\leq N-1\right\}=\left\{x[0],x[1],\cdots,x[N-1]\right\}

It is trivial to find that the unified formula for these two types can be expressed by

Suppose

(3)

d

In the work of SSA

2

\displaystyle{\bm{X}}_{\mathrm{ssa}}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}x[1]&x[2]&\cdots&x[m]\\
x[2]&x[3]&\cdots&x[m+1]\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
x[d]&x[d+1]&\cdots&x[N]\end{bmatrix}

(4)

m{\mathrel{\to}^{\tau=1}}N-(d-1)\tau=N-d+1

Binary Diophantine Equation
\tau x+y=n+s\tau
with Rectangular Constraint

Figure 1:
Geometric interpretation of
G_{s}(n,\tau,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2})

Assume that
N,d,\tau\in\mathbb{N}
are positive integers. Suppose that
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
0\leq\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}
and
0\leq\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}
. Let

\displaystyle\Omega=\Omega(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2})=\left\{\binom{x}{y}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\alpha_{1}\leq x\leq\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\leq y\leq\beta_{2}\right\}

(15)

For the given
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
\tau\in\mathbb{N}
, we have the greatest common divisor
\GCD(\tau,1)=1
and
n>\tau\cdot 1-\tau-1=-1
, thus the set of solutions to the Diophantine equation

\tau x+y=n+s\tau,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

(16)

must be non-empty according to the
Theorem

7
and
Theorem

8
in
Appendix

A
. For the type flag
s\in\left\{0,1\right\}
and
\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, let

\displaystyle G_{s}(n,\tau,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2})=\left\{\binom{x}{y}\in\Omega:x\tau+y=n+s\tau\right\}

(17)

be the set of the solutions to the Diophantine equation
x\tau+y=n+s\tau
with rectangular constraint
\binom{x}{y}\in\Omega
.
The cardinality of
G_{s}(n,\tau,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2})
is denoted by
\left|G_{s}(n,\tau,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\right|
. The geometric interpretation of the set
G_{s}(n,\tau,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2})
is all of the 2-dim points which satisfy (
16
) with interger coordinates on the line
x\tau+y=n+s\tau
and in the rectangle domain
\Omega
.
Figure

1
illustrates the scenario intuitively.

The equation
x\tau+y=n+s\tau
implies that
y=n+s\tau-x\tau
. Thus
\beta_{1}\leq y\leq\beta_{2}
implies that

\frac{n+s\tau-\beta_{2}}{\tau}\leq x\leq\frac{n+s\tau-\beta_{1}}{\tau}

(18)

It is obvious that

\max\left(\alpha_{1},\frac{n+s\tau-\beta_{2}}{\tau}\right)\leq x\leq\min\left(\alpha_{2},\frac{n+s\tau-\beta_{1}}{\tau}\right)

(19)

For the purpose of finding integer solutions, the
\dfrac{n+s\tau-\beta_{2}}{\tau}
should be replaced by
\left\lceil{\dfrac{n+s\tau-\beta_{2}}{\tau}}\right\rceil
and the
\dfrac{n+s\tau-\beta_{1}}{\tau}
should be replaced by
\left\lfloor{\dfrac{n+s\tau-\beta_{1}}{\tau}}\right\rfloor
, where
\left\lceil{x}\right\rceil
and
\left\lfloor{x}\right\rfloor
denote the ceiling and floor of

respectively.
Let

\left\{\begin{aligned} {x}_{\mathrm{min}}^{s}(n,\tau,\alpha_{1},\beta_{2})&=\max\left(\alpha_{1},\left\lceil{\frac{n+s\tau-\beta_{2}}{\tau}}\right\rceil\right)\\
{x}_{\mathrm{max}}^{s}(n,\tau,\alpha_{2},\beta_{1})&=\min\left(\alpha_{2},\left\lfloor{\frac{n+s\tau-\beta_{1}}{\tau}}\right\rfloor\right)\end{aligned}\right.

(20)

for
s\in\left\{0,1\right\}
, then it is easy to prove the following lemma according to (
19
) and (
20
).

Lemma 1
.

For the constrained Diophantine equation

\tau x+y=n+s\tau,\quad\binom{x}{y}\in\Omega,s\in\left\{0,1\right\}

(21)

the set of its solutions can be written by

\displaystyle G_{s}(n,\tau,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2})=\left\{\binom{x}{n+s\tau-x\tau}:{x}_{\mathrm{min}}^{s}\leq x\leq{x}_{\mathrm{max}}^{s}\right\}

and the number of solutions is

\displaystyle\left|G_{s}(n,\tau,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\right|={x}_{\mathrm{max}}^{s}(n,\tau,\alpha_{2},\beta_{1})-{x}_{\mathrm{min}}^{s}(n,\tau,\alpha_{1},\beta_{2})+1.

Some special cases for the set

3.1
Bugs in the SGMD Method

The key formula of Pan et al. can be stated by the following result
[
1
]
:

Result 2
.

Suppose that
d
is the embedding dimension,
m=N-(d-1)\tau
, and
\bm{Z}_{k}=(Z^{i}_{j}(k))_{d\times m}\in\mathbb{R}^{{d}\times{m}}
is the
k
-th component of the trajectory matrix
\bm{M}=\Phi_{1,1}(X)
in the immersion space
\mathbb{R}^{{d}\times{m}}
for the time series
X=\left\{x[n]\right\}^{N}_{n=1}
. Let

\tilde{Z}^{i}_{j}(k)=\begin{cases}Z^{i}_{j}(k),&m<d;\\
Z^{j}_{i}(k),&m\geq d.\end{cases}

(24)

for
1\leq i\leq d
and
1\leq j\leq m
, the
k
-th component of time series
x^{(k)}[n]
rebuilt from the
\bm{Z}_{k}
can be computed by

\displaystyle x^{(k)}[n]

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\cfrac{1}{n}\sum\limits^{n}_{p=1}\tilde{Z}^{p}_{n-p+1}(k),&1\leq n<d^{*};\\
\cfrac{1}{d^{*}}\sum\limits^{d^{*}}_{p=1}\tilde{Z}^{p}_{n-p+1}(k),&d^{*}\leq n\leq m^{*};\\
\cfrac{1}{N-n+1}\sum\limits_{p=n-m^{*}+1}^{N-m^{*}+1}\tilde{Z}^{p}_{n-p+1}(k),&m^{*}<n\leq N\end{cases}

(25)

where

d^{*}=\min(d,m),\quad m^{*}=\max(d,m).

(26)

We remark that the rebuilding process based on (
25
) does not depend on the time delay
\tau
, which is conflict with the definition of the trajectory matrix
{\bm{X}}_{\mathrm{sgmd}}
in (
6
) or its equivalent form
\bm{M}
for
s=1
in (
8
). Consequently, the equation (
25
) just holds for
\tau=1
.
Moreover, the MATLAB code released by
Pan et al. can not be applied if
\tau>1
or
s=0
.

3.2
Correction for the SGMD Method

Our study shows that the bug in SGMD can be fixed if the DAP is generalized correctly. We now propose the following pulling back theorem about the
generalized diagonal averaging principle
(GDAP) for the SGMD and SSA:

Theorem 3

(Pulling Back Theorem)
.

For the type
s\in\left\{0,1\right\}
, embedding dimension
d\in\mathbb{N}
and time series
X=\left\{x[n]\right\}^{N-1+s}_{n=s}
of length
N
, suppose
\bm{M}=\Phi_{s,\tau}(X)\in\mathbb{R}^{{d}\times{m}}
is the trajectory matrix of
X
with
r
components
\bm{Z}_{1},\cdots\bm{Z}_{r}
such that
\displaystyle\bm{M}=\sum^{r}_{k=1}\bm{Z}_{k}
. Let

\left\{\begin{aligned} {q}_{\mathrm{min}}&=\max\left(s,\left\lceil{\frac{n-m+s\tau+(1-s)}{\tau}}\right\rceil\right)\\
{q}_{\mathrm{max}}&=\min\left(d+s-1,\left\lfloor{\frac{n+(\tau-1)s}{\tau}}\right\rfloor\right)\end{aligned}\right.

(27)

and

Q_{k}(n,\tau,s)=\left\{Z^{q}_{n+s\tau-q\tau}(k):{q}_{\mathrm{min}}\leq q\leq{q}_{\mathrm{max}}\right\}

(28)

be the data set specified by the matrix
\bm{Z}_{k}=(Z^{i}_{j}(k))_{d\times m}\in\mathbb{R}^{{d}\times{m}}
, then the
k
-th component of time sequence
X^{(k)}=\left\{x^{(k)}[n]\right\}^{N-1+s}_{n=s}\in\mathscr{X}
corresponding to the component
\bm{Z}_{k}
can be computed by

X^{(k)}=\Phi^{-1}_{s,\tau}(\bm{Z}_{k})=\left\{x^{(k)}[n]:s\leq n\leq N-1+s\right\}

where

\displaystyle x^{(k)}[n]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left|Q_{k}(n,\tau,s)\right|}\sum_{z\in Q_{k}(n,\tau,s)}z

(29)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{q}_{\mathrm{max}}-{q}_{\mathrm{min}}+1}\sum^{{q}_{\mathrm{max}}}_{q={q}_{\mathrm{min}}}Z^{q}_{n+s\tau-q\tau}(k).

for
1\leq k\leq r
.

Proof
:

In order to find the inverse
X=\Phi^{-1}_{s,\tau}(\bm{M})
such that the decomposition
\bm{M}=\sum^{r}_{k=1}\bm{Z}_{k}
, what we should do is to find the position of
x^{(k)}[n]
appearing in the
k
-th component matrix
\bm{Z}_{k}
.

According to the definition of the trajectory matrix
\bm{M}=\sum^{r}_{k=1}\bm{Z}_{k}
of type-0 in (
11
) or of type-1 in (
10
), it is equivalent to find the solution
\binom{i}{j}
to the Diophantine equation

\tau i+j=n
or
\tau i+j=n+\tau
for the given
n
. Obviously, the key issue lies in solving the binary Diophantine equation
\tau x+y=n+s\tau
for
s\in\left\{0,1\right\}
. According to the
Lemma

1
, the pair of row and column indices
\binom{i}{j}
are the elements of
G_{s}(n,\tau,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2})
for
(s,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2})=(0,0,d-1,0,m-1)
or
(s,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2})=(1,1,d,1,m)
with the form
\binom{i}{j}=\binom{q}{n+s\tau-q\tau}
for
{q}_{\mathrm{min}}\leq q\leq{q}_{\mathrm{max}}
.

On the other hand, the data set

\displaystyle Q_{k}(n,\tau,0)=\left\{Z^{i}_{j}(k):\binom{i}{j}\in G_{0}(n,\tau,0,d-1,0,m-1)\right\}=\left\{Z^{q}_{n+s\tau-q\tau}(k):s=0,{q}_{\mathrm{min}}\leq q\leq{q}_{\mathrm{max}}\right\}

(30)

for
s=0
or

\displaystyle Q_{k}(n,\tau,1)=\left\{Z^{i}_{j}(k):\binom{i}{j}\in G_{1}(n,\tau,1,d,1,m)\right\}=\left\{Z^{q}_{n+s\tau-q\tau}(k):s=1,{q}_{\mathrm{min}}\leq q\leq{q}_{\mathrm{max}}\right\}

(31)

for
s=1
contains all of the candidates or copies of
x^{(k)}[n]
appearing in the matrix
\bm{Z}_{k}
.
Consequently, the
x^{(k)}[n]
can be rebuilt by averaging all of the elements in the data set
Q_{k}(n,\tau,s)
, which is computed by (
29
). This completes the proof.
\blacksquare

The
Theorem

3
essentially states the method for computing the pulling back mapping
\Phi^{-1}_{s,\tau}:\mathbb{R}^{{d}\times{m}}\to\mathscr{X}
for computing the time sequence
X^{(k)}=\Phi^{-1}_{s,\tau}(\bm{Z}_{k})
, thus it can be called the
pulling back theorem
intuitively.

The original SGMD method should be used carefully since the DAP are limited by two rules:

must be set with

For the time delay

Appendix A
Diophantine Equation
\tau x+y=n+s\tau

For the Diophantine equation
ax+by=n
, we have the following important properties
[
12
,
13
]
:

Theorem 7
.

For
a,b\in\mathbb{N}=\left\{1,2,3,\cdots\right\}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}=\left\{0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\cdots\right\}
, the equation
ax+by=n
has solution
\binom{x}{y}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
if and only if the greatest common divisor of
a
and
b
is a factor of
n
, i.e.,
\GCD(a,b)\mid n
.

Theorem 8
.

Suppose that
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\GCD(a,b)=1
. For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
n>ab-a-b
, there must exist
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}=\left\{0,1,2,\cdots\right\}
such that
n=ax+by
.

If we take the assignments for the parameters
a
,
b
and
s
as
follows

a\leftarrow\tau,b\leftarrow 1,n\leftarrow n+s\tau

(38)

for
s\in\left\{0,1\right\}
and
\tau\in\mathbb{N}
, then the solution of the Diophantine equation
\tau x+y=n+s\tau
have non-negative solution
\binom{x}{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{+})^{2}
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

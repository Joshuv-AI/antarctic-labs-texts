Signal Processing via Cross-Dimensional Projection
Daizhan Cheng

Abstract
Using projection between Euclidian spaces of different dimensions, the signal compression and decompression become straightforward. This encoding/decoding technique requires no preassigned measuring matrix as in compressed sensing. Moreover, in application there is no dimension or size restrictions. General formulas for encoding/decoding of any finite dimensional signals are provided. Their main properties are revealed. Particularly, it is shown that under the equivalence assumption the technique provides the best approximation with least square error.



I
Introduction

Signal compression-decompression is a fundamental task for signal processing. Various methods have been developed. to deal with this problem. For instance, in image compression-decompression, the discrete fourier transformation, wavelet transformation, discrete cosine transformation, etc. have been used
[
10
]
. The basic idea in handling signal compression-decompression is like this. Using a method to transfer a signal, which is digitalized as a point
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
space, to a point
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
space, where
m<n
. This process is called signal compression. Then use another transformation to convert
y
back to
\hat{x}\in|R^{n}
. This process is called signal decompression. To judge the quality of a complression/decompression method, usually we use the square error
[
6
]

\displaystyle Error=\|\hat{x}-x\|_{2}=\sqrt{\Sigma_{i=1}^{n}(\hat{x}_{i}-x_{i})^{2}}.

(1)

In all existing compression-decompression methods, since
y
is of completely different kind of measurements, the analysis of error becomes difficult.

This paper proposes a new compression-decompression method, which makes the mixed dimensional Euclidian spaces

{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

into a “vector space”. Using triangular inequality, we have

\displaystyle Error\leq\|\hat{x}-y\|_{2}+\|y-x\|_{2}.

(2)

Then we try to minimize both
\|\hat{x}-y\|_{2}
and
\|y-x\|_{2}
to get best recovered signal.

\displaystyle\min_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}\|y-x\|_{2}

(3)

yields the best compressed signal, and

\displaystyle\min_{\hat{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\|y-\hat{x}\|_{2}

(4)

yields the best decompressed (recovered) signal.

After establishing a metric vector space structure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}
, both compression and decompression can be executed by proper projections. This is the basic idea of this paper.

Definition II.8

\displaystyle\|x\|_{{\mathcal{V}}}:=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle_{{\mathcal{V}}}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{x^{T}x}.

(iii)

The distance of

and

\displaystyle d_{{\mathcal{V}}}(x,y):=\|x\vec{-}y\|_{{\mathcal{V}}}.

(31)

Remark II.9

It is easy to verify that

\displaystyle d_{{\mathcal{V}}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}d_{2}(x,y).

(32)

Hence when the

is restricted on a fixed dimensional Euclidian space, it is proportional to standard distance.

Using this inner product, the angle between two elements
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}
, denoted by

, is defined by

\displaystyle\cos(\theta)=\frac{\langle x,y\rangle_{{\mathcal{V}}}}{\|x\|_{{\mathcal{V}}}\|y\|_{{\mathcal{V}}}}.

(33)

Under this distance, the distance deduced topology can be obtained, which is denoted by
{\mathcal{T}}_{d}
. Apart from this

, there is another topology on
{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}
. Naturally, each
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
can be considered as a component (i.e., a clopen set) of
{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}
. And within each
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
the conventional Euclidian space topology is used. Then overall, such a topology is called the natural topology, denoted by
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
.
({\mathbb{R}}^{\infty},{\mathcal{T}}_{n})
is disconnected. Precisely speaking, its fundamental group is
{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
.

It is easy to verify the following result.

III
Projection-based Signal Compression/Decompression

This section consider the projection-based compression (PC) and the projection-based decompression (PD). First, the one dimensional signals are considered and then the higher dimensional signals are considered.

III-A

PS and PD for

Assume
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}
is a finite dimensional signal, specified by

Definition III.1

(i)

The PS of

\displaystyle y=\pi^{n}_{m}(x)=\Pi^{n}_{m}x.

(41)

(ii)

The PD of

\displaystyle\hat{x}=\pi^{m}_{n}(y)=\Pi^{m}_{n}y.

(42)

Theorem III.2

Under the assumption that
x\leftrightarrow y
means they are the same. (The physical meaning of the equivalence has been explained before.) PR and PD form the best compression-decompression pair.

Proof.
Any signal compression-decompression can be described by two mappings:
the compressing mapping
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and the decompressing mapping
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Under the assumption that
x\leftrightarrow y
implies they are the same, we know that

\displaystyle x=x\otimes{\bf 1}_{\alpha},\quad\forall\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}.

(43)

Consider any “distance”, denoted by

with its compressed measurement

as

. Note that
d_{*}
needs not to be a real distance, it is proper as long as it can measure the quality of the compression. Say, we can define

\displaystyle d_{*}(x,y):=d_{n}(\psi(y),x),

(44)

where

is the standard distance on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Assume
t=\lcm(m,n)
and denote
\tilde{x}=x\otimes{\bf 1}_{t/n}
and
\tilde{y}=y\otimes{\bf 1}_{t/m}
. Under the assumption (
43
) we have

d_{*}(x,y)=d_{*}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y}).

Now
\tilde{x}
and
\tilde{y}
lie on the same dimensional Euclidian space
{\mathbb{R}}^{t}
. Since a common measurement of two vectors (in a same dimensional Euclidian space) is
\ell_{2}
norm as in (), it is reasonable to assume that when restrict on each
{\mathbb{R}}^{t}
,
d_{*}
is order-preserving with
\|\cdots\|_{2}
, which means when

,

, if and only if,
\|u-v\|_{2}>\|u^{\prime}-v^{\prime}\|_{2}
. (Note that this argument is based on the fact that
\ell_{2}
norm is commonly used to measure the error
[
6
]
. Hence, taking Remark
II.9
into consideration, we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}\min_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}d_{*}(y,x)=\min_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}d_{*}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\\
=\min_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}d_{n}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\\
=\min_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}d_{{\mathcal{V}}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\\
=\min_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}d_{{\mathcal{V}}}(x,y)\\
\end{array}

Now by definition of the projection, it is clear that

y=\pi^{n}_{m}(x)

is the unique best solution to (
III-A
).

Similarly

\hat{x}=\pi^{m}_{n}(y)

is the best solution for the decomposition.

Similar argument shows that

V^{2}_{A}=\left(\Pi^{n_{d-1}}_{m_{d}}@^{2}_{i_{d-1}}\right)V^{1}_{A}.

Continuing this procedure verifies (

V
Conclusion

This paper proposes a technique for signal compression/decompression via projections between Euclidian spaces with different dimensions. It is proved that under the assumption of equivalence both encoding and decoding are optimal in the sense that the approximation errors are of least square. The encoding/dicoding formulas are presented for arbitrary dimensional signals with arbitrary preassigned compression rates.

This is only a theoretical result. The application examples for real signals are under preparation.

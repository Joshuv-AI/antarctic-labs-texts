Academic early warning model based on machine learning and model application
Authors: Qiang Li, Yihan Liu, Rui Ma, Qike Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the expansion of higher education, the uncertainty of students’ academic completion and the diversity of academic crises have posed new challenges to the management of higher education. This study aims to design and implement a dynamic academic early warning system based on machine learning to predict and intervene in students’ academic crises. By analyzing the causes of academic crisis, the fuzzy comprehensive evaluation with analytic hierarchy process method is used to construct an academic early warning indicator system comprising 10 key indicators. This ensures the scientificity and rationality of the indicator system through expert scoring and consistency test. On this basis, a radial basis function neural network was used to construct an academic early warning model, which outperforms the recurrent neural network and Softmax regression model in terms of prediction accuracy and convergence speed. The system was developed using hypertext markup language, cascading style sheets, JavaScript, and Python to achieve a user-friendly human–computer interaction interface and provide personalized academic alert services. The experimental results demonstrate that the system exhibits high sensitivity and accurate recognition capabilities when dealing with large-scale student datasets, achieving an accuracy rate of 96.32% and a root mean square error of 0.2926, which meets the practical requirements of academic early warning. The results of this study not only provide a new academic early warning tool for colleges, but also have important practical value for promoting the construction of smart campus and digital campus. The current findings, drawn from a sample limited to engineering with an overrepresentation of males, require future validation in multi-disciplinary and gender-balanced cohorts to establish broader applicability.Keywords: Academic early warning, Machine learning, Radial basis function, Neural network, Educational management, FCE-AHPSubject terms: Computer science, Information technology, Software

Introduction

Currently, there is a growing concern regarding the increasing severity of issues such as grade repetition, school dropout rates, loss of eligibility to pursue desired majors, and even student dismissals1,2. Ensuring the effective culmination of students’ academic pursuits has emerged as a pressing concern in the realm of higher education that requires immediate resolution3,4. Receiving warnings will heighten students’ awareness of their current learning level, prompting them to modify their learning attitudes more effectively, exert greater effort to enhance their grades, and prevent academic probation. Additionally, the academic adviser possesses a partial understanding of the student’s academic probation risk, enabling them to offer more suitable and prompt support to students5.

Academic warnings for students allow the university to promptly address students’ learning difficulties. Advisors can implement diverse strategies to aid students in enhancing their academic achievement or avoiding delayed graduation. The university’s research project involves constructing an academic early warning model for undergraduate students using machine learning techniques. Consequently, an academic early warning system will be developed based on this model6. The primary purpose of the early warning system is to offer decision support to university management by accurately forecasting the academic risks faced by students. This is done in order to effectively protect and maintain the academic progress of students7. Consequently, an increasing number of scholars acknowledge the significant societal capacity of educational data and engage in studies in this field. For instance, the utilization of deep learning technology in academic early warning can proactively identify students who are at risk of repeating or dropping out of a course due to the accumulation of failed credits8. Furthermore, the implementation of this technology also facilitates the development of digital and intelligent campuses in higher education institutions9. Despite the increasing use of the academic early warning system in colleges and universities and its demonstrated effectiveness, there are still some significant challenges in its implementation.

During the construction of the academic early warning index system, researchers encounter a multitude of intricate academic influencing aspects10. The feature characteristics that capture the pupils’ academic process lack comprehensiveness. Feature generation is a notable technique that finds use in various machine learning and deep learning challenges11. Mingyu et al.12 employed a categorization of attributes related to students’ academic pursuits, housing conditions, online activities, and fundamental information to address the aforementioned issue. The Catboost-SHAP technique had the highest performance with an R-squared (R2) value of 80.3%, a mean squared error (MSE) of 24.976, and a mean absolute error (MAE) of 3.551. Hamim et al.13 categorized students’ learning outcomes in 2022 according to many factors, including personal behavior, social behavior, study habits, family background, academic majors, and grades. Furthermore, they employed feature selection approaches to enhance the performance of the model. The experimental findings indicate that LightGBM is the most potent algorithm, with an accuracy of 89.26%. This performance was achieved by selecting 14 features using a feature selection technique. In their study, Schneider et al.14 identified 105 elements that have an impact on academic achievement. These factors encompass various aspects such as students’ personal circumstances, instructional methods, and external influences. Nevertheless, there are several reasons that fail to accurately recognize and intervene with students who are experiencing a crisis during their academic journey. Romero et al.15 forecasted patterns in students’ ultimate learning difficulties by analyzing data such as the quantity of information shared, volume of emails exchanged, number of tests finished, and student engagement on an online learning platform. Nevertheless, their approach solely relied on learning process data while disregarding the static fundamental information of individual students.

Additionally, numerous machine learning techniques, such as support vector machine, KNN, decision tree, and linear regression, are utilized to construct prediction models. In their study, Migueis et al.16 gathered data from a sample of 2459 students in order to develop predictive models for students’ academic success. The authors advocated using the random forest model, which achieved an accuracy rate of over 95% in predicting academic outcomes at an early stage of the students’ educational journey. In a preliminary study conducted by Rimal et al.17, the aim was to determine the most effective machine learning model for hyper-parameter tuning. The study focused on employing single, multiple, and deep neural networks to predict the grades of engineering students from Pokhara University, Nepal in 2019. Selamat et al.18 put up two methodologies: one involves machine learning prediction using computational models, while the other entails visual analytics. The decision tree algorithm, random tree, random forest, and REP tree were employed to examine a computer systems design course for a total of 489 students from 2016 to 2019. The findings indicated that the J48 algorithm achieved a precision rate of 99.8%. Bujang et al.19 introduced a probabilistic grade prediction model for SPOC with a limited number of samples, utilizing a correlation vector machine (RVM). The simulation results demonstrated that RVM exhibits superior forecast accuracy and offers technical assistance in the distribution of SPOC. Nevertheless, the utilization of machine learning fails to adequately prioritize the examination of the link among factors.

Furthermore, as research and automation continue to advance, numerous intricate challenges arise. When algorithms are the only method used, many aspects of the data are not completely utilized. The use of educational data to forecast pupils’ academic achievement has become a prominent area of research. A.J. Lekan conducted a study to examine the correlation between students’ academic standing before they enrolled and their ultimate academic achievement. The study utilized 'O' level grades and data from the first three semesters20. Yıldız H et al.21 utilized artificial neural networks to analyze students’ final grades, midterm grades, and educational backgrounds. The objective was to forecast whether students would pass or fail. Asif R et al.22 employed data mining methodologies to analyze student performance in two dimensions: firstly, to forecast student performance after four years of study, and secondly, to examine the progression of typical situations and integrate it with the projected outcomes to offer timely alerts to students. Macfadyen and Dawson23 conducted a study using tracking data from Blackboard Vista to examine the influence of different online learning activities on learning outcomes. The study conducted by Zacharis et al.24 focused on developing a predictive model for learning outcomes in blended courses using Moodle tracking data. Similarly, You et al.25 examined the behaviors that have a significant impact on learning outcomes based on tracking data from a learning management system (LMS).

In summary, most of the existing related researches focus on static analysis of outcome data, with almost no dynamic research on process data. There are still some major issues that need further research related to the learning crisis in higher education:

Construct early warning model based on RBF

In the academic crisis analysis, the main parameters of the academic warning model have been determined. Considering the large amount of academic warning data, nonlinear changes and the characteristics of warning categorization, it was decided to use RBF neural network to achieve accurate warning of students in the learning process.

RBF neural network is a powerful nonlinear modeling tool that can effectively capture complex relationships in data and has good generalization ability. By using an appropriate number and location of radial basis functions in the network, it is possible to effectively partition the data in a high-dimensional space, thus enabling accurate prediction and early warning of students’ academic performance.

The basic structure of RBF neural network consists of three parts: input layer, hidden layer and output layer. The input layer is used to receive the student sample data, i.e. evaluation indicator parameters. Conclusions

In this study, we have successfully developed and implemented a dynamic academic early warning system based on radial basis function (RBF) neural networks. The system provides a scientific and efficient prediction and intervention tool for the current crises that students may encounter in the academic process. Through in-depth research on the completeness of the academic early warning indicator system, the completeness of the model, the diversity of the calculation methods, and the advanced technical means, this system shows significant advantages in several aspects:

Improvement of the system of academic early warning indicators:Through the FCE-AHP method of screening, 10 key academic influences were identified from 30 preliminary indicators, and the weights of these factors were distributed as follows: classroom attendance (8.526%), implementation of personal study plan (11.126%), participation in disciplinary competitions (9.375%), etc., and each of these factors passed the scoring and consistency test by the experts (with a CR value of 0.093) , which ensures the scientificity and rationality of the indicator system.

Improvement of the system of academic early warning indicators:

Completeness of academic early warning models:The designed RBF neural network model showed high prediction accuracy in academic crisis analysis. The model showed a significant decrease in the loss function after 100 iterations, demonstrating the ability to learn quickly and predict academic crises effectively. Compared with RNN and Softmax regression models, the RBF model is more advantageous in terms of loss function decline speed and prediction accuracy.

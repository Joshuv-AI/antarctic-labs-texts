QED r {}_{\text{r}} : a finite-volume QED action with redistributed spatial zero-momentum modes
Matteo Di Carlo, Maxwell T. Hansen, Nils Hermansson-Truedsson, and Antonin Portelli

Abstract
We present a finite-volume QED action designed to improve the infinite-volume extrapolation of hadronic observables in precision lattice QCD+QED calculations. The action proposed in this work, which we call QED r {}_{\text{r}} , can be seen as a particular case of the infrared-improved QED actions introduced by Davoudi et al. in 2019, and is specifically designed to remove kinematics-independent finite-volume corrections that appear at O ⁡ ( 1 / L 3 ) \mathrm{O}(1/L^{3}) in the commonly used QED L {}_{\text{L}} formulation, where L L is the spatial extent of the physical volume. For a number of key observables, these effects depend on the internal structure of the hadrons and are difficult to evaluate non-perturbatively, making an analytical subtraction of the finite-volume effects impractical. We explicitly study the QED r {}_{\text{r}} electromagnetic finite-size effects on hadron masses and leptonic decay rates, relevant for Standard Model precision tests using the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix elements. In addition, we propose methods to remove the kinematics-dependent O ⁡ ( 1 / L 3 ) \mathrm{O}(1/L^{3}) effects in leptonic decays. The removal of such contributions, shifting the leading contamination to O ⁡ ( 1 / L 4 ) \mathrm{O}(1/L^{4}) , will help to reduce the systematic uncertainties associated with finite-volume effects in future lattice QCD+QED calculations.

2
Definition of
QED
{}_{\text{r}}

As discussed in the introduction, the purpose of this work is to introduce and explore the consequences of a new definition of finite-volume QED that we call
QED
{}_{\text{r}}
, corresponding to a specific choice within the class of actions in ref.
[
33
]
. The spirit of the definition is that, instead of simply removing the spatial zero-momentum mode as in
QED
{}_{\text{L}}
, we instead advocate
redistributing
the mode to neighbouring non-zero momenta. For concreteness we mainly restrict attention to the approach of modifying a set of modes with fixed magnitude, i.e. a set of
\mathbf{k}
satisfying
\mathbf{k}^{2}=(2\pi/L)^{2}R^{2}
for some positive integer
R^{2}
.

At the level of the finite-volume Euclidean photon propagator, the definition reads

D_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{r}}(x)=\frac{1}{L^{3}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\neq\mathbf{0}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}k_{0}}{2\pi}\,e^{ikx}\,\frac{\delta_{\mu\nu}}{k_{0}^{2}+\mathbf{k}^{2}}\big[1+h(\mathbf{n},R)\big]\bigg|_{\textbf{n}=\mathbf{k}L/(2\pi)}\,.

(6)

This can be viewed as the
QED
{}_{\text{L}}
propagator:

D_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{L}}(x)=\frac{1}{L^{3}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\neq\mathbf{0}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}k_{0}}{2\pi}\,e^{ikx}\,\frac{\delta_{\mu\nu}}{k_{0}^{2}+\mathbf{k}^{2}}\,,

(7)

plus an additional term containing
h(\mathbf{n},R)
. We emphasize that both propagators are defined in Feynman gauge and, in both cases, fixing to this particular gauge before modifying the spatial momentum modes is part of the definition of the scheme.

The function
h(\mathbf{n},R)
only takes on non-zero values when
\mathbf{n}^{2}=R^{2}
and can be written as

h(\mathbf{n},R)=w(\mathbf{n},R)\,\delta_{\mathbf{n}^{2},R^{2}}\,,

(8)

where
w(\mathbf{n},R)
is a weight function that is only defined for
\mathbf{n}
satisfying
|\mathbf{n}|=R
.
It is convenient to define
\mathcal{S}_{R}
as the set of all such integer three-vectors:

\mathcal{S}_{R}=\big\{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\ \big|\ |\mathbf{n}|=R\big\}\,.

(9)

Then
w(\mathbf{n},R)
is defined for
\mathbf{n}\in\mathcal{S}_{R}
and is required to satisfy the normalization condition

\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathcal{S}_{R}}w(\mathbf{n},R)=1\,,

(10)

implying

\sum_{\mathbf{k}\neq\mathbf{0}}h(\mathbf{n},R)\bigg|_{\textbf{n}=\mathbf{k}L/(2\pi)}=1\,.

(11)

This property ensures the removal of all
1/L^{3}
terms that are proportional to
c_{0}
in
QED
{}_{\text{L}}
, and this is the defining property of
QED
{}_{\text{r}}
as a subset of the family of improved actions proposed in ref.
[
33
]
.

We note that, in principle, the definition of the
QED
{}_{\text{r}}
propagator in
eq.

6
can be extended to the case of zero mode redistribution over
N
shells with radii
\boldsymbol{R}=\{R_{1},R_{2},\dots,R_{N}\}
by replacing the weight function
h(\mathbf{n},R)
with

h(\mathbf{n},\boldsymbol{R})=\sum_{\alpha\in\boldsymbol{R}}\omega(\alpha)h(\mathbf{n},\alpha)\,,

(12)

where
\omega(\alpha)
denotes the weight assigned to the shell
|\mathbf{n}|=\alpha
defined such that
\sum_{\alpha}\omega(\alpha)=1
. We will not pursue this multiple-shell option further in this work.

The propagator in
eq.

6
is derived from the Feynman-gauge Euclidean action

S_{\mathrm{r}}[\hat{A}_{\mu}]=\frac{1}{2L^{3}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\neq\boldsymbol{0}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}k_{0}}{2\pi}\,\hat{A}_{\mu}(k)^{*}\,\bigg[\frac{\delta^{\mu\nu}k^{2}}{1+h(\mathbf{n},R)}\bigg]\,\hat{A}_{\nu}(k)\,,

(13)

with
\hat{A}_{\mu}(k)
defined as the Fourier-transformed photon field and
{\hat{A}_{\mu}(k)^{*}=\hat{A}_{\mu}(-k)}
. As noted in ref.
[
33
]
, which provides a generalized form of
eq.

13
applicable to any gauge, the positivity of the action requires the weight function
h(\mathbf{n},R)
to satisfy
{h(\mathbf{n},R)>-1}
. Combining this with the normalization condition on the weights, we infer that
{|w(\mathbf{n},R)|<1}
.

In the case of an isotropic observable, in which no particular directions e.g. in momentum space are singled out, it is most natural to assign equal weights to the modes
\mathbf{n}\in\mathcal{S}_{R}
with values
w(\mathbf{n},R)=1/r_{3}(R^{2})
, where the function
r_{3}(R^{2})=\sum_{\mathbf{n}}\delta_{\mathbf{n}^{2},R^{2}}
counts the number of integer three-vectors with magnitude
R
. In the notation of ref.
[
33
]
(Sec. IV.A), this corresponds to
w_{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}=\delta_{\mathbf{n}^{2},R^{2}}/r_{3}(R^{2})
. In the following, we will study the simplest implementation of
QED
{}_{\text{r}}
, which we call
minimal
QED
{}_{\text{r}}
, in which the isotropic redistribution of the zero mode is applied to the
R=1
shell. This corresponds to assigning
w(\mathbf{n},1)=1/6
to the six modes in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
. Below we also use the shorthand
h(\mathbf{n})=h(\mathbf{n},1)
.

Applying the minimal
QED
{}_{\text{r}}
set-up to the propagator in
eq.

6
, we deduce that finite-volume effects will arise from sum-integral differences of the form

\Delta_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathrm{r}}\,f(\mathbf{k})=\bigg[\frac{1}{L^{3}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\neq\boldsymbol{0}}-\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\bigg]\big(1+h(\mathbf{n})\big)\bigg|_{\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{k}L/(2\pi)}f(\mathbf{k})\,,

(14)

where we defined the operator
\Delta_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathrm{r}}
acting on a generic function
f(\mathbf{k})
. It is further convenient to define a dimensionless analog in which
\mathbf{k}=2\pi\mathbf{n}/L
is replaced by
\mathbf{n}
:

\Delta_{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathrm{r}}F(\mathbf{n})=\bigg[\sum_{\mathbf{n}\neq\boldsymbol{0}}-\int\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{n}\bigg]\big(1+h(\mathbf{n})\big)F(\mathbf{n})\,.

(15)

In terms of this operator, we will see below that the
QED
{}_{\text{r}}
finite-volume effects relevant for this paper are parametrized by two new sets of geometric coefficients

\bar{c}_{j}=\Delta_{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathrm{r}}\frac{1}{|\mathbf{n}|^{j}}\,,\qquad\bar{c}_{j}(\mathbf{v})=\Delta_{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathrm{r}}\,\frac{1}{|\mathbf{n}|^{j}}\frac{1}{(1-\mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}})}\,,

(16)

where
\mathbf{v}
is a generic dimensionless vector that, for the study of leptonic decays in
section

4
, will become the velocity of the outgoing lepton in the finite-volume frame. Our statement above, that terms proportional to
c_{0}
in
QED
{}_{\text{L}}
are removed in
QED
{}_{\text{r}}
, is equivalent to the statement that
\bar{c}_{0}=0
.

This completes our general discussion of
QED
{}_{\text{r}}
. We now turn to a detailed study of the consquences of this definition for finite-volume effects in QED corrections to hadronic observables.

\bar{c}_{j}
appearing in the expression above are defined as

\bar{c}_{j}=\Delta_{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathrm{r}}\,\frac{1}{|\mathbf{n}|^{j}}\,.

(21)

The property of the
QED
{}_{\text{r}}

\sum_{\mathbf{n}}h(\mathbf{n})=1
yields a general relation between the
\overline{c}_{j}
in
QED
{}_{\text{r}}
and the

in
QED

\overline{c}_{j}=c_{j}+1\,.

(22)

In particular, this guarantees that the coefficient
\bar{c}_{0}
appearing at
\mathrm{O}(1/L^{3})
vanishes,

\bar{c}_{0}=c_{0}+1=0\,.

(23)

In
QED
{}_{\text{L}}
, the appearance of a non-zero coefficient
c_{0}
is due to the removal of the spatial zero mode
\mathbf{k}=\boldsymbol{0}
. In
QED
{}_{\text{r}}
we can interpret the absence of such contribution at
\mathrm{O}(1/L^{3})
as a consequence of the redistribution of the zero mode over the neighbouring Fourier modes. When taking the infinite-volume limit, the Fourier space becomes denser as the volume grows and the weights of the neighbouring modes reproduce the contribution of the zero mode, which in
QED
{}_{\text{L}}
would be simply removed. It should be noted that the shifted coefficients in
\overline{c}_{j}
in
eq.

22
affect all orders in the finite-volume expansion. Motivated by the need for a better understanding through order
1/L^{3}
in practical data analyses, we do not consider higher-order effects in this paper. We leave a study of the tail of the expansion to future work.

4
Leptonic decays

We consider now the leptonic decay
P^{-}\to\ell^{-}\bar{\nu}_{\ell}
of a negatively charged pseudoscalar meson into a lepton and neutrino pair. In the rest frame of the decaying meson, energy and momentum conservation implies that the squared amplitude of the process

\mathbf{p}_{\ell}

\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{\ell}

[
33

\bar{c}_{0}

\mathrm{O}(1/L^{3})
multiplying a combination of known form factors.

[

4.1
Strategies to remove

While the coefficient

A second approach leverages the fact the average of

\displaystyle\left\langle\bar{c}_{0}(\mathbf{v}_{\ell})\right\rangle=\frac{1}{N_{\text{meas}}}\,\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{meas}}}\bar{c}_{0}(\mathbf{v}_{\ell,\,i})\,,

converges to zero in the limit of large statistics. This can be used to construct lattice Monte Carlo averages which do not suffer from finite-size effects proportional to
\bar{c}_{0}(\mathbf{v}_{\ell})

\mathbf{v}_{\ell}^{\star}
such that
\bar{c}_{0}(\mathbf{v}_{\ell}^{\star})=0

\bar{c}_{0}(\mathbf{v}_{\ell})

\bar{c}_{j}(\mathbf{v}_{\ell})
.

5
Conclusions

In this paper we have introduced a new finite-volume formulation of QED,
QED
{}_{\text{r}}
, which is a specific choice within the framework of infrared improved
QED
{}_{\text{L}}
originally introduced in ref.
[
33
]
. It is designed to remove finite-volume effects at order
1/L^{3}
in the large-volume expansion of observables, by changing the underlying QED action through the inclusion of a set of weights acting on a finite number of Fourier modes. The added weights alter the values of the finite-volume coefficients appearing in the large-volume expansion, and the chosen set of weights in
QED
{}_{\text{r}}
sets to zero
\bar{c}_{0}
which appears at order
1/L^{3}
for pseudoscalar masses and leptonic decay rates. For leptonic decay rates it was found that also the moment-dependent coefficient
\bar{c}_{0}(\mathbf{v})
appears at order
1/L^{3}
, whose value depends on the direction of the momentum. It was shown that these remaining finite-volume effects also can be removed in
QED
{}_{\text{r}}
by either cleverly choosing the kinematics or stochastically averaging the lattice calculations over a range of different kinematics.

Our results are highly relevant for analytical correction of lattice data in precision calculations of e.g. flavour physics observables. In ref.
[
22
]
we studied isospin-breaking corrections to leptonic decays of pions and kaons, relevant for the extraction of the CKM elements
|V_{us}|
and
|V_{ud}|

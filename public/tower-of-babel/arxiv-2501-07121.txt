Abstract
Grid-scale battery energy storage systems (BESSs) can provide flexibility to the power system and capture short-term price volatility by shifting energy in time through controlled charging and discharging. The highly volatile European continuous intraday (CID) market allows trading until just a few minutes before physical delivery, offering significant earning potential. However, its high trading frequency poses substantial modeling challenges. Accurate modeling of BESSs trading in the CID market is essential to estimate revenue potential and optimize trading strategies. Additionally, comparing CID profits with other spot markets helps determine whether participating in the CID is worthwhile despite its complexity. We propose a forecast-driven model to optimize BESS trading in the CID market. Our strategy employs a rolling window modeling framework to capture market dynamics. Price forecasts for impending CID products are generated at the beginning of each window and used to optimize trading schedules for subsequent execution. We also benchmark our approach across various spot markets, offering a broad cross-market profit comparison. We evaluate our forecast-driven model across different BESS power-to-capacity ratios, comparing it to a perfect-foresight scenario and key CID market indices, such as ID 1 \text{ID}_{1} and ID 3 \text{ID}_{3} . Using real 2023 German CID data, a 1 MW/1 MWh system adopting our method earns € 146 237 , only 11 % below perfect foresight, surpassing all other markets and indices. Our approach surpasses ID 1 \text{ID}_{1} and ID 3 \text{ID}_{3} by over 4 % and 32 %, respectively, confirming ID 1 \text{ID}_{1} as a reliable lower-bound estimate for earnings potential in the CID market.



I
Introduction

The highly volatile
continuous intraday (CID)
market is key for monetizing
battery energy storage systems (BESSs)
, enabling them to exploit price differences by charging at low prices and discharging at higher prices.
However, its continuous trading structure—thousands of trades per day—poses notable modeling challenges, prompting the question whether participation in the
CID
market is worth the effort.
To address this, we propose a forecast-based
CID
trading model and benchmark its performance against perfect foresight, established
CID
indices and other markets, positioning the profitability of the
CID
market within a broader market context.
To that end, we review the available related work on power trading for
BESSs
in the
CID
market in Subsection
I-A
, and provide a brief summary along with our contributions in Subsection
I-B
.
The remainder of the paper is structured as follows:
Section
II
provides background on European power markets.
Section
III
outlines our trading strategy, while Section
IV
covers the data and case study setup.
Section
V
presents the results, and Section
VI
concludes the paper.

I-A

Literature Review

Given the focus of our paper, we review studies on modeling the
CID
market for
BESS
trading.
Existing studies commonly use linear models to represent
BESSs

[
1
]
, often simplifying the analysis by disregarding the nonlinear dynamics of charging and discharging processes.
For modeling
CID
market dynamics, we identified three approaches: Single price,
Reinforcement learning (RL)
-based,
Rolling window (RW)
-based.

I-A
1
Single Price Approach

The first approach models the
CID
market within an optimization framework that simplifies its continuous nature by treating it as an auction.
This method optimizes trading decisions based on average market price indices, such as
\text{ID}_{1}
and
\text{ID}_{3}
, provided by
European Power Exchange (EPEX SPOT SE)

[
2
]
.
These indices represent average prices over the last hour and last three hours of trading, respectively.
For example, in
[
3
]
, the authors use a
mixed integer linear program (MILP)
model that uses the
\text{ID}_{1}
index to optimize aging-aware operation of
BESSs
.
Their results indicate that incorporating battery aging into the model enhances lifetime profitability.
Similarly, the authors in
[
4
]
use stochastic optimization techniques to develop trading strategies combining
day-ahead auction (DAA)
and
CID
trading (via
\text{ID}_{3}
).
They empirically suggest that risk-averse strategies tend to prioritize bidding in the
DAA
over
CID
participation.

Simplifying
CID
market products to single prices reduces modeling complexity but overlooks interproduct price variations, potentially underestimating revenues. To address this limitation, the authors in
[
5
]
combine average market prices (worst-case scenarios) with minimum and maximum transaction prices (best-case scenarios) to evaluate arbitrage opportunities.
Their case study, which involves a
photovoltaic (PV)
plant with self-consumption supported by a
BESS
, demonstrates increased profitability from arbitrage trading in the
CID
market.

I-A
2
Reinforcement Learning-Based Approach

The second approach relies on
RL
to create optimal trading policies, often incorporating all transactions.
These policies are typically approximated using
neural networks (NNs)
, effectively capturing the complexities of continuous bidding in the
CID
market.
For example, in
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
, the authors propose an
RL
framework for bidding in the
CID
market with energy storage devices.
The authors model the
CID
market as a Markov Decision Process.
Both studies empirically demonstrate superior performance of their
RL
-based policy, compared to the rolling intrinsic policy benchmark, which involves repeated re-optimization as new price information becomes available.
Despite their effectiveness,
RL
approaches require substantial amounts of data and computationally intensive training processes.

I-A
3
Rolling Window-Based Approach

The third approach leverages a
RW
framework to address dynamic price fluctuations by iteratively solving optimization problems as new market data becomes available.
This approach typically involves discretizing the trading period into sub-windows, enabling continuous adaptation of trading strategies.
For example,
[
8
]
optimize the operations of a hybrid power plant comprising
PV
, wind turbines, and
BESS
assets within the
CID
market using a
MILP
.
The authors model the
CID
market clearing process by mapping the order book to a piecewise linear function, assigning distinct prices to different bid volumes, and deriving clearing prices based on traded quantities.
Similarly,
[
9
]
employ a comparable
MILP
-based framework to optimize hybrid power plant trading in the
CID
market, achieving financial gains that exceed the
\text{ID}_{3}
price benchmark.
In a related study
[
10
]
, the authors propose a
RW
-based
CID
trading algorithm based on
MILP
.
Rather than explicitly modeling market clearing through the
limit orderbook (LOB)
, their approach computes clearing prices from transaction averages.
The authors report higher profitability compared to the
\text{ID}_{3}
benchmark.
Optimization-based trading reduces reliance on extensive datasets by making real-time decisions at each time-step while enabling multiple trades per product.

I-B

Summary and Contributions

According to the literature examined, the single-price approach overlooks intra-product price volatility, while
RL
-based methods can capture complex market dynamics but require substantial data and computational resources.
In contrast,
RW
-based approaches efficiently model market dynamics and provide transparency through interpretable
MILP
optimization.
Realistic modeling of market dynamics and interpretability are crucial properties for operationalizing trading strategies, which is why we adopt an optimization-based approach for our modeling contribution.
To that end, we make the following contributions:

1.

Forecast-Based Model:
Current research rarely integrates price forecasts into
CID
trading strategies for
BESSs
. To fill this gap, we propose a forecast-based model for
CID

BESS
trading that uses price forecasts for decision-making while executing trades at actual market prices. We compare this approach with a perfect foresight strategy in the
CID
market, quantifying the impact of forecast errors on profitability.

2.

Cross-Market Benchmark:
Existing studies often focus on a single wholesale market, overlooking profitability differences across different markets compared to the
CID
. We address this gap by introducing a comprehensive cross-market
BESS
benchmark to enable direct comparisons between markets.

II
Background: European Power Markets

Our background focuses on European power markets, specifically on the short-term wholesale (spot) markets.
In these markets, trading takes place prior to physical delivery.
They operate through multiple sequential markets where participants trade power products representing specific power capacities in megawatts (
MW
) delivered over intervals of one hour, half an hour, or a quarter-hour
[
11
]
.
Two main clearing mechanisms are used: auction clearing or continuous clearing via a
LOB
.
In auction clearing, participants submit orders, and a central algorithm matches supply and demand to determine a single clearing price per product based on the merit-order principle
[
12
]
.
In continuous clearing, trades occur continuously via a
LOB
managed by the exchange and visible to all participants; whenever a bid exceeds an ask, the market clears and matches the orders
[
12
]
.

As of 2023, the year considered in this study,
a typical trading day in the European spot market follows this timeline for next-day products (all times in CET on the day before physical delivery, except for the delivery start (
s
), which is on the day of physical delivery):

1.

12:00
–
DAA
(hourly products)

2.

15:00
–
IDA
(quarter-hourly products)

3.

15:00 to
s-x\text{ minutes}
–
CID
trading (hourly products)

4.

16:00 to
s-x\text{ minutes}
–
CID
trading (quarter-hourly products)

In other words, the trading day begins with the Europe-wide
DAA
, the market with the highest liquidity, followed by the
intraday auction (IDA)
.
Simultaneously,
CID
trading starts for hourly products, followed shortly by quarter-hourly
CID
trading.
The lead time (
x
) at which the
CID
market closes is country dependent.
In Germany, the largest power market in Europe by traded volume,
CID
trading remains open until five minutes (
x=5
) before physical delivery start (
s
).
The
LOB
is shared between the four German delivery areas until 30 minutes before delivery, after which each area maintains its own
LOB
.
For example, the quarter-hourly product for 15:00–15:15 can be traded the day before delivery in the
IDA
, and continuously via the
CID
from 16:00 (day prior) to 14:55 (day of delivery).
This paper focuses on the
CID
market during the period when the
LOB
is shared across Germany, enabling the analysis of the entire German market.
For more information on
CID
trading modalities, please refer to the technical details in
[
13
]
.

IV-A

Price Data Inputs

We obtained all our data from
EPEX SPOT SE

[
15
]
.
For the
DAA
(hourly),
IDA
(quarter-hourly) and intraday indices (
\text{ID}_{\text{full}}
,
\text{ID}_{3}
,
\text{ID}_{1}
—quarter-hourly), we collected a single price per product.
For the
CID
, we sourced all trades for quarter-hourly products and postprocessed them to obtain price forecasts and clearing prices.
Our dataset covers all products with delivery periods during 2023.

Because we focus on continuous trading in the
CID
, we assume perfect foresight for all auction-like markets (
DAA
,
IDA
) and intraday indices (
\text{ID}_{\text{full}}
,
\text{ID}_{3}
,
\text{ID}_{1}
).
This assumption equates actual prices with forecasts, i.e.,
\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{auctionprice}{{{}}\pi_{\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{product}{{{}}p}}}=\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{auctionfcstprice}{{{}}\pi_{\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{product}{{{}}p}}^{*}}
.

For the
CID
, we compute forecasts using a dual regime as formulated in (
11
).

\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{cidfcstprice}{{{}}\pi_{\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{product}{{{}}p},\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{time}{{{}}t}}^{*}}=\begin{cases}\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{auctionprice}{{{}}\pi_{\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{product}{{{}}p}}}\text{ of \lx@glossaries@gls@link{main}{ida}{{{}}IDA} }&\text{if }\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{pstart}{{{}}t_{\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{product}{{{}}p}}}-\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{time}{{{}}t}>5\text{h},\\
\text{average of last four trades}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(11)

If the time until the start of physical delivery (
\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{pstart}{{{}}t_{\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{product}{{{}}p}}}-\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{time}{{{}}t}
) exceeds 5 hours, we default the forecast to the respective
IDA
clearing price due to low
CID
market liquidity at longer horizons.
The 5-hour threshold is a parameter selected for this study but is adjustable.
Otherwise, we set the forecast to the average of the last four trades
[
16
]
, capturing most recent market information.

Conversely, we compute the clearing prices as the average of all transactions occurring in the minute immediately following the current time (
t

(12)

Distinguishing between forecasts and clearing prices effectively models the market by basing decisions on price forecasts while executing transactions at actual upcoming market prices.

IV-B

Scenarios

We combine scenarios from two dimensions: markets and
BESS
configurations.

IV-B
1
Market Scenarios

We run experiments across different markets, each representing a different scenario (named after the respective markets). These include auction-like markets with a single price per product (
DAA
,
IDA
,
\text{ID}_{\text{full}}
,
\text{ID}_{3}
,
\text{ID}_{1}
) and the
CID
market, divided into two scenarios: a forecast-based scenario (
\text{\lx@glossaries@gls@link{main}{cid}{{{}}CID} }_{\text{F}}
) and a perfect foresight scenario (
\text{\lx@glossaries@gls@link{main}{cid}{{{}}CID} }_{\text{PF}}
).

For all auction-like markets we parametrize the strategy to run once daily on the day before delivery (i.e.,
\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{tradefreq}{{{}}\Delta t_{\text{trade}}}=24\text{h}
).
In each step, we trade all products of the next day, with
\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{ntrade}{{{}}n^{\text{trade}}}=24
for hourly products and
\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{ntrade}{{{}}n^{\text{trade}}}=96
for quarter-hourly products.
To improve foresight, we optimize over two days ahead, resulting in
\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{nopt}{{{}}n^{\text{opt}}}=48
for hourly products and
\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{nopt}{{{}}n^{\text{opt}}}=192
for quarter-hourly products.

For the
CID
market, we adapt to its continuous trading structure by reoptimizing every five minutes (i.e.,
\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{tradefreq}{{{}}\Delta t_{\text{trade}}}=5\text{min}
).
At each reoptimization, we consider an 8-hour horizon ahead, optimizing over 32 quarter-hourly products (
\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{nopt}{{{}}n^{\text{opt}}}=32
).
As the
CID
market is liquid only in the last two to three hours before delivery, we trade the three closest delivery products (
\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{ntrade}{{{}}n^{\text{trade}}}=3
) and exclude products with less than 35 minutes of lead time to leave time for positions to clear before the German-wide shared
LOB
closes 30 minutes prior to delivery.

IV-B
2

Scenarios

We define different

\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{emax}{{{}}\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{e}{{{}}\mathrm{e}}^{\text{max}}}=1\text{MWh}

\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{pbuymax}{{{}}b^{\text{max}}}=\lx@glossaries@gls@link{notation}{psellmax}{{{}}s^{\text{max}}}

13

[

[

VI
Conclusion

This paper addresses the challenges of accurately modeling
BESS
trading in the
CID
market and comparing its profitability with other spot markets.
We propose a forecast-based
BESS
trading model that uses price forecasts while clearing positions at actual market prices, effectively reflecting real-world conditions.
Additionally, we provide a comprehensive profit benchmark across different spot markets.

Testing our forecast-based
CID
strategy on 2023 data demonstrates the consistent outperformance of all other markets and indices, particularly the
\text{ID}_{1}
benchmark across all battery configurations.
The strategy achieves a total profit of up to €146 237 and €55.25 per cycle for a 1-hour
BESS
.
These findings confirm the reliability of
\text{ID}_{1}
as a benchmark for
BESS
trading in the
CID
and empirically show that it can be surpassed by a realistic trading strategy.
Furthermore, our results highlight the high profit potential in the
CID
market.

However, our study has several limitations, which can be considered potential future research directions.
We do not impose cycle limits, as our primary focus is on maximizing earnings rather than evaluating battery aging.
Furthermore, we do not explicitly model aging effects, and our market modeling approach excludes both the order book structure and market fees.
Besides these gaps, future work could also use more sophisticated
CID
price forecasts to improve trading outcomes.

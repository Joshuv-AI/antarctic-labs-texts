Failure of the measure contraction property via quotients in higher-step sub-Riemannian structures
Samuël Borza, Luca Rizzi

Abstract
. We investigate the validity of the synthetic Ricci curvature lower bound known as the measure contraction property ( MCP \mathrm{MCP} ) for sub-Riemannian structures beyond step two. We show that whenever the distance function is not Lipschitz in charts, the MCP \mathrm{MCP} may fail. This occurs already in fundamental examples such as the Martinet and Engel structures. Central to our analysis are new results on the stability of the local MCP \mathrm{MCP} under quotients by isometric group actions for general metric measure spaces, developed under a weaker variant of the essential non-branching condition which, in contrast with the classical one, is implied by the minimizing Sard property in sub-Riemannian geometry. Since the MCP \mathrm{MCP} is preserved under blow-ups, we focus on Carnot homogeneous spaces, proving that MCP \mathrm{MCP} descends to suitable quotients. As a byproduct, any structure whose tangent at some point admits a quotient to Martinet fails the MCP \mathrm{MCP} . We also obtain a computation-free proof that the Grushin plane shares the Heisenberg group’s MCP \mathrm{MCP} . Applications include a detailed analysis of validity and failure of the MCP \mathrm{MCP} for Carnot groups of low dimension. Our results suggest a conjecture on the failure of the MCP \mathrm{MCP} in presence of Goh abnormal geodesics satisfying the strong generalized Legendre condition.



1.
Introduction

In this work, we investigate the validity of synthetic Ricci curvature lower bounds in sub-Riemannian geometry. One such condition that has drawn considerable interest in the last two decades is the curvature-dimension inequality, introduced by Lott–Villani and Sturm in
[
LV09
,
Stu06a
,
Stu06b
]
. It is now well-understood that no sub-Riemannian manifold (that is not Riemannian) can satisfy the curvature-dimension condition
[
Jui09
,
Jui10
,
AS20
,
Jui21
,
MR23a
,
RS23
]
. Quite surprisingly, however, some sub-Riemannian structures are known to satisfy a weaker condition known as the measure contraction property –
\mathrm{MCP}
for short.

The
\mathrm{MCP}
was independently introduced by Ohta in
[
Oht07a
]
and Sturm in
[
Stu06b
]
. It depends on two parameters,
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,\infty)
, representing respectively a Ricci curvature lower bound and a dimension upper bound. While the curvature-dimension is a condition on the optimal transport between any pair of absolutely continuous probability measures on a metric measure space, the
\mathrm{MCP}
is a condition on the optimal transport between Dirac masses and uniform distributions. The
\mathrm{MCP}
shares many quantitative geometric consequences with the curvature-dimension condition, and thus it may replace the latter in sub-Riemannian geometry.

Since the
\mathrm{MCP}
is inherited by metric measure tangent cones, particular attention has been devoted to this case. The (metric measure) tangent cones of sub-Riemannian metric measure spaces are known as
Carnot homogeneous spaces
, and they are
Carnot groups
at regular points. Following the pioneering work of Juillet in
[
Jui09
]
, who established the
\mathrm{MCP}
in the Heisenberg groups with sharp values of the parameters, a lot of effort has been devoted to proving the
\mathrm{MCP}
for other sub-Riemannian structures. The
\mathrm{MCP}(K,N)
– for optimal values of the parameters
K
and
N
– was obtained for ideal, corank
1
and generalized
H
-type Carnot groups, in
[
Rif13
,
Riz16
,
BR18
]
, respectively, and for the Grushin plane in
[
BR19
]
, which is the simplest Carnot homogeneous space that is not a Carnot group. The
\mathrm{MCP}
also holds for sub-Riemannian structures that are not Carnot homogeneous spaces, such as Sasakian, 3-Sasakian or
H
-type structures, provided that they satisfy suitable curvature bounds, see
[
AL14
,
LLZ16
,
LL18
,
RS19
,
BGKT19
,
BR20b
,
BGRVM25
,
BMR24
]
and references therein.

More recently, Badreddine and Rifford proved in
[
BR20a
]
that real-analytic sub-Riemannian structures whose distance is locally Lipschitz in charts away from the diagonal satisfy the
\mathrm{MCP}
. This is the case for all Carnot homogeneous spaces of step

\leq 2
. Note though that the general results obtained in
[
BR20a
]
do not provide explicit ranges for the parameters
K
and
N
for which the
\mathrm{MCP}(K,N)
holds. While it is clear (by the existence of metric dilations) that
K\leq 0
for Carnot homogeneous spaces, various quantitative lower bounds have been obtained for the best (i.e. the lowest) value of
N
such that
\mathrm{MCP}(0,N)
holds, see
[
Rif13
,
Riz16
,
GZ24
,
Zha25
]
.

All this evidence led to the belief that the
\mathrm{MCP}
should be satisfied – at least locally – by all sub-Riemannian spaces and therefore by any Carnot homogeneous space. However, the analysis has so far been limited to
Lipschitz structures
, namely those whose distance function is locally Lipschitz in charts outside of the diagonal. In this paper, we prove the rather surprising fact that as soon as the distance function is
not
Lipschitz in charts, the
\mathrm{MCP}
may fail; this occurs already in fundamental examples such as the Martinet and Engel structures.

In the rest of this introduction, we present and discuss our results.

1.1.
Failure of the measure contraction property for Martinet

The Martinet structure is of fundamental importance in sub-Riemannian geometry. It is a Carnot homogeneous space – the simplest one of step
3
– and the most elementary non-Lipschitz structure
[
ABCK97
]
. We refer to
Section

8
for precise definitions. The first result we report corresponds to
Theorem

8.6
.

Theorem 1.1

(Failure of the
\mathrm{MCP}
for the Martinet structure)
.

The Martinet structure does not satisfy the
\mathrm{MCP}(K,N)
for any
K\in\mathbb{R}
and any
N\in[1,\infty)
.

The proof of
Theorem

1.1
relies upon the optimal synthesis of the Martinet structure, which was obtained in
[
ABCK97
]
. We note that we work with the Ohta’s
\mathrm{MCP}
, which is the weakest variant of the
\mathrm{MCP}
, so that any other formulation fails as well.
Theorem

1.1
is actually the last result we prove in this paper, but it is the starting point and motivation for the rest of our work.

1.2.
Measure contractions for quotients

A powerful method to build new examples of structures satisfying curvature bounds consists in taking quotients by isometric group actions. One well-known result concerns lower bounds for the sectional curvature for quotients by proper, free, and isometric actions on Riemannian manifolds, having the structure of a Riemannian submersion. In this setting, a lower bound for the sectional curvature of the total space is inherited by the base space thanks to O’Neill’s formulas
[
Bes08
]
. The passage to the quotient is more delicate for Ricci curvature lower bounds, as the measure also plays a role
[
Lot03
,
PW14
]
.

On general metric measure spaces, the stability of the curvature-dimension conditions under isometric group actions was studied in
[
GGKMS18
]
. It is mentioned in
[
GGKMS18
, Rmk. 3.8]
that also the
\mathrm{MCP}
descends to suitable quotients, provided that the metric measure space is
essentially non-branching
, see
Definition

3.1
. We note though that a complete proof of this fact, following the arguments of
[
GGKMS18
]
, requires additional non-trivial arguments from
[
CM17
]
. We state here the corresponding result, anticipating that, for our purposes, a more general version of
Theorem

1.2
will be needed.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
GGKMS18
,
CM17
]
)
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
be an essentially non-branching metric measure space satisfying the
\mathrm{MCP}(K,N)
for some
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,\infty)
. Let
G
be a compact Lie group acting freely by metric measure isometries on
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
. Then the metric measure quotient
(X^{*},\mathsf{d}^{*},\mathsf{m}^{*})
satisfies the
\mathrm{MCP}(K,N)
.

For applications in sub-Riemannian geometry, the typical situation we want to deal with is that of a quotient by an isometric group action

(2)

\pi:\mathbb{M}\to\tilde{\mathbb{M}},

where both
\mathbb{M}
and
\tilde{\mathbb{M}}
are
Carnot homogeneous spaces
, the metric measure tangent cones of sub-Riemannian manifolds, see
[
Bel96
]
. A Carnot homogeneous space is itself a quotient
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
, where
\mathbb{G}
is a Carnot group and
\mathbb{H}<\mathbb{G}
is a stratified subgroup acting isometrically on
\mathbb{G}
. We refer to
Section

6
for the precise construction and to
Proposition

6.15
for the formalization of the quotient (
2
). We only stress here that Carnot homogeneous spaces are not homogeneous in the metric sense: although
\mathbb{G}
acts transitively on
\mathbb{M}
, this action is generally not by isometries.

Fundamental examples of (
2
) are the quotient of the Heisenberg group to the Grushin plane
\pi:\mathbb{Heis}\to\mathbb{Gru}
, and the quotient of the Engel group to the Martinet structure
\pi:\mathbb{Eng}\to\mathbb{Mar}
.

Our objective is to prove the stability of the
\mathrm{MCP}
under quotients as (
2
). In particular, by establishing the
\mathrm{MCP}
for a Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
, one would then be able to deduce the
\mathrm{MCP}
for
all
Carnot homogeneous spaces
\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
. Conversely, the failure of the
\mathrm{MCP}
on
\tilde{\mathbb{M}}
would imply that it fails on
\mathbb{M}
. This would yield the failure of the
\mathrm{MCP}
for any sub-Riemannian metric measure space having
\mathbb{M}
as tangent at some point, motivating our focus on Carnot homogeneous spaces.

However, as anticipated, there are non-trivial technical challenges preventing the application of
Theorem

1.2
to quotients such as (
2
).

(1)

It is not known whether general sub-Riemannian manifolds, or even simply Carnot groups, are essentially non-branching. Sub-Riemannian branching geodesics do exist
[
MR20
]
, and this may happen if they contain non-trivial abnormal segments. An important condition in sub-Riemannian geometry – the minimizing Sard property – stipulates that the set of endpoints of abnormal geodesics starting from a given point has measure zero. Over the last decades, a great amount of work has been devoted to establishing its validity for several classes of structures (see
[
LRT24
, Sec. 1.3]
for an up-to-date bibliography), and this remains one of the main open problems in sub-Riemannian geometry
[
Agr14
, Prob. 3]
,
[
RT05
, Conj. 1]
.
While it is reasonable to expect that the minimizing Sard property implies some form of non-branching, it is not clear whether it implies the essentially non-branching condition in its standard form.

(2)

For technical reasons the argument used to prove
Theorem

1.2
works for
compact
group actions. In our setting, the acting group is nilpotent and simply connected, and thus it is never compact. As suggested in
[
GGKMS18
, Cor. 7.25]
, and using the notation of
Theorem

1.2
, this technical nuisance can be handled if there exists a co-compact lattice
\Gamma\triangleleft G
(i.e. a discrete normal subgroup such that the quotient group
G/\penalty\Gamma
is compact). In this case, the quotient map
X\to X^{*}=X/\penalty G
admits a factorization

(3)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X/\penalty\Gamma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X/\penalty\Gamma)/\penalty(G/\penalty\Gamma)\simeq X^{*}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{q}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{p}}

On the one hand, note that
\mathsf{p}
is now a quotient by a

can be applied. On the other hand,

is a local isometry, so that the
\mathrm{MCP}(K,N)
for

\mathrm{MCP}_{\mathrm{loc}}(K,N)
for the intermediate factor

. In
[

]
, one uses the local-to-global property of the curvature-dimension, namely the fact that

under the essential non-branching condition

However, the

does not have the local-to-global property
[
Stu06b
, Rmk. 5.6]
. In our specific setting, where both

and

(3)

A further challenge comes from the fact that the acting group
G
might not admit a co-compact lattice. The Mal’cev criterion
[
CG90

establishes that a simply connected nilpotent group has a co-compact lattice if and only if the Lie algebra of

admits a basis with rational structure constants. There are examples where this is not the case even for nilpotent groups of step

[

1.3.
Dealing with
Item

1
:
\delta
-essentially non-branching

To address the first of these issues, we introduce a variant of the classical essentially non-branching condition, that we call
\delta
-essentially non-branching
, see
Section

3
. It requires non-branching only for
W_{2}
-geodesics between absolutely continuous measures and finite sums of Dirac masses. This condition is better suited for sub-Riemannian geometry, as shown by the next result (corresponding to

).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
be a sub-Riemannian metric measure space satisfying the

-minimizing Sard property. Then
(M,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
is
\delta
-essentially non-branching.

The

-minimizing Sard property is a mild reinforcement of the Sard property. It was introduced in
[

]
. For real-analytic sub-Riemannian structures, such as Carnot homogeneous spaces, the

The

-essentially non-branching condition and the

\mathrm{MCP}

Theorem 1.4

(Quotients by discrete group actions)
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
be a
\delta
-essentially non-branching metric measure space satisfying the

for some

and

. Let

acting properly and freely. Then, the metric measure quotient
(X^{*},\mathsf{d}^{*},\mathsf{m}^{*})

. Furthermore, if
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
is
\delta

\delta
-essentially non-branching.

1.6.
Measure contraction properties beyond step 2

Putting together the results presented so far, we can prove that the

Theorem 1.7

(MCP for quotients of Carnot homogeneous spaces)

Suppose that a Carnot homogeneous space

. Assume that the minimizing Sard property holds for all factors

Remark 1.8

(A new proof for the

Let

.

It is not trivial to determine whether a Carnot group admits a quotient to the Martinet structure. This is a purely algebraic condition, independent on the metric geometry of the space. Quite unexpectedly, we prove in the next result that admitting a quotient to Martinet structure is equivalent to admit a quotient to the Engel group (it corresponds to
Theorem

6.13
).

Theorem 1.11

(Stratified groups admitting Martinet or Engel quotients)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Carnot group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and step
s\geq 3
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

there exists a closed, dilation-invariant subgroup
\mathbb{K}<\mathbb{G}
such that
\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{K}
is a Carnot homogeneous space smoothly isometric to
\mathbb{Mar}
;

(ii)

there exists a closed, and dilation-invariant normal subgroup
\mathbb{H}\triangleleft\mathbb{G}
such that
\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
is a Carnot group smoothly isometric to
\mathbb{Eng}
;

(iii)

there exists
\mathfrak{h}_{3}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{3}
with codimension
1
such that the subspace

(8)

\mathfrak{h}_{2}:=\{Y\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}\mid[\mathfrak{g}_{1},Y]\in\mathfrak{h}_{3}\}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{2}

has codimension
1
.

A characterization of Carnot groups admitting a quotient to the Engel group appeared in
[
Mar14
, Prop. 5.1]
, in terms of a special basis of the Lie algebra. In contrast, condition (
8
) in
Theorem

1.11
is intrinsic, and it is key for the next applications. See
Remark

6.14
for a sufficient condition in terms of the dimension of
\mathbb{G}
.

1.7.
Applications to Carnot groups in low dimensions

We apply our results to Carnot groups in low dimension. Recall that a Carnot group is
indecomposable
if its Lie algebra is not the direct sum of two non-trivial Lie algebras. A classification of indecomposable Carnot groups of dimension
\leq 7
is provided in

Gon98

Goh-Legendre geodesics
are a class of abnormal geodesics satisfying second-order necessary conditions for minimality, see
Definition

A.1
. On the one hand, if we are able to rule out the existence of Goh-Legendre geodesics on a given Carnot group, we can pair the results in
[

]
and
[

]
to prove the validity of the
\mathrm{MCP}
for that group, see

7.2
for details.

(
[

]

.

Any Carnot group with no non-trivial Goh-Legendre geodesics satisfies the
\mathrm{MCP}(K,N)
for all
K\leq 0
and some
N\in[1,\infty)
.

).

1.8.
Closing thoughts

If a sub-Riemannian metric measure space
(M,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
does not have non-trivial Goh-Legendre geodesics, then, by the results of Agrachev and Lee in
[
AL09
]
, the distance function is locally Lipschitz in charts out of the diagonal. Consequently, thanks to the work of Badreddine and Rifford in
[
BR20a
]
, and assuming that the structure is real-analytic, it must, locally, satisfy the
\mathrm{MCP}
. If non-trivial Goh-Legendre geodesics are present, the
\mathrm{MCP}
(and local Lipschitz regularity) may fail, and indeed they do fail for many examples in
Table

1
.

We suspect that whenever such a geodesic is present, both local Lipschitz regularity and the
\mathrm{MCP}
are lost, at least if the Legendre condition is satisfied in a strong sense (see
Definition

A.2
).

Conjecture

(Failure of the
\mathrm{MCP}
with strong Goh-Legendre geodesics)
.

Let
(M,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
be a sub-Riemannian metric measure space. If there is a non-trivial strong-Goh-Legendre geodesic
\gamma
, then
\mathsf{d}:M\times M\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
fails to be Lipschitz in charts in a neighbourhood of
\gamma
, and the
\mathrm{MCP}(K,N)
does not hold for all
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,\infty)
.

The above conjecture is also supported by
Table

1
: the groups marked with a dagger
do
have non-trivial Goh geodesics but
do not
have non-trivial Goh-Legendre ones (see
Remark

7.7
). This emphasizes the importance of the generalized Legendre condition, which turns out to be as significant as the (definitely more well-known and used in the literature) Goh condition.

Using
Corollary

1.17
, we can produce Carnot groups that have non-trivial Goh–Legendre geodesics and yet satisfy the
\mathrm{MCP}
, by taking products of factors with no non-trivial Goh–Legendre geodesics. Such geodesics are not
strong
Goh–Legendre ones though, supporting the conjecture.

The case in which only non-strong Goh–Legendre geodesics are present is borderline, and a higher-order analysis may be necessary. The Carnot group
N_{6,2,6}
in
Table

1
is an example.

The absence of Goh-Legendre geodesics also implies, for real-analytic structures, that the corresponding distance function is subanalytic, as proven by Agrachev and Gauthier in
[
AG01
]
. On the contrary, akin to what happens for the
\mathrm{MCP}
and, of course, local Lipschitzianity, subanalyticity is lost when a suitable Martinet quotient do exist. All these properties – local Lipschitzianity,
\mathrm{MCP}
and subanalyticity – (or their failure) seem to be a manifestation of the compactness of the set of normal geodesics of a fixed length (or lack thereof). However, a direct connection between them is currently missing.

The results of the present work can be adapted to the sub-Finsler setting. Whilst we have not pursued this direction here, combining our findings with those of
[
BT23
,
BMRT24b
,
BMRT24a
,
MR23b
]
could lead to new developments in sub-Finsler geometry.

1.9.
Structure of the paper

Section

2
contains an overview of optimal transport on metric measure spaces and their quotients by isometric group actions. In
Section

3
, we introduce the weaker essentially non-branching condition that we require, and discuss its relation with the
\mathrm{MCP}
. We then address the stability of the
\mathrm{MCP}
under discrete and compact group actions in
Sections

4
and
5
, respectively. In
Section

6
, we present Carnot homogeneous spaces and we prove the factorization argument. We put everything together in
Section

7
, proving our main theorem concerning the
\mathrm{MCP}
of quotients of Carnot homogeneous spaces and its consequences. We end with
Section

8
, where we prove the failure of the
\mathrm{MCP}
for the Martinet structure.

2.
Preliminaries

We start this preliminary section with a brief overview of optimal transport on metric measure spaces. We refer the reader to
[
Vil09
]
and references therein for a complete account.

A metric measure space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
is a complete, separable, proper, and geodesic metric space
(X,\mathsf{d})
equipped with a
\sigma
-finite Borel measure
\mathsf{m}
. We assume for simplicity that
\supp\mathsf{m}=X
. We recall that a metric space is geodesic if for all
x,y\in X
, there exists
\gamma\in\Geo(X)
such that
\gamma(0)=x
and
\gamma(1)=y
, where we denoted the set of geodesics of
(X,\mathsf{d})
by

(10)

\Geo(X):=\left\{\gamma\in C([0,1],X)\mid\mathsf{d}(\gamma_{s},\gamma_{t})=|s-t|\mathsf{d}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})\right\}.

If
\mu
is a Borel measure on
X
and
T:X\to X
is a Borel map, then
T_{\sharp}\mu
stands for the pushforward measure, i.e. the measure defined through
T_{\sharp}\mu(A)=\mu(T^{-1}(A))
for all Borel set
A\subseteq X
. The set
\mathcal{P}(X)
will denote the space of probability measures on
X
. The narrow topology on
\mathcal{P}(X)
is the one induced by the narrow convergence of measures: a sequence
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)
converges narrowly to
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X)
, denoted as
\mu_{n}\rightharpoonup\mu
, if
(\int_{X}f\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
\int_{X}f\mu
for all bounded continuous functions
f\in C_{b}(X)
. This is referred to as “weak convergence” in
[
Vil09
]
.

We will use the notation
\mathcal{P}_{c}(X)
for the set of compactly supported probability measures. For
p\in[1,\infty)
, we denote by
\mathcal{P}_{p}(X)
the space of probability measures with finite
p
-momentum:

(11)

\mathcal{P}_{p}(X):=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X)\ \Big|\ \int_{X}\mathsf{d}(x_{0},x)^{p}\mu(\mathrm{d}x)<+\infty\text{ for some (and hence any) }x_{0}\in X\right\}.

The
p
-Wasserstein distance is the metric on
\mathcal{P}_{p}(X)
introduced via the Monge-Kantorovich minimization problem from optimal transport theory. Given
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(X)
and setting
\Cpl(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
as the set of couplings
\pi\in\mathcal{P}(X\times X)
having
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
as first and second marginals, we define

(12)

W_{p}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})^{p}:=\inf_{\pi\in\Cpl(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})}\int_{X\times X}\mathsf{d}(x,y)^{p}\pi(\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y).

It is well-known that convergence with respect to
W_{p}
is equivalent to the narrow convergence of measures plus convergence of
p
-momenta. If
(X,\mathsf{d})
is a geodesic space (resp. complete, separable, proper), then so is
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(X),W_{p})
.

When the minimum in (
12
) is attained at
\pi\in\mathcal{P}(X\times X)
, we will say that
\pi
is an optimal transport plan between
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
, and that
\pi\in\Opt_{p}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
. A Borel map
T:X\to X
for which
\pi=(\mathrm{id}\times T)_{\sharp}\mu_{0}
is an optimal coupling is called an optimal transport map.

For
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(X)
, we denote by
\OptGeo_{p}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
the set of all
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\Geo(X))
such that
(e_{0},e_{1})_{\sharp}\nu\in\Opt_{p}(X)
. It is a standard fact that if
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\Geo(X))
, then
\mu_{t}:=(e_{t})\sharp\nu
is a
W_{p}
-geodesic; and vice versa, any
W_{p}
-geodesic
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}\in\Geo(\mathcal{P}_{p}(X))
can be lifted to an optimal dynamical plan
\nu\in\OptGeo_{p}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
.

In the following, we will mostly be concerned with the case
p=2
, and will refrain from specifying the value of
p
whenever it is clear from the context.

2.1.
Measure contraction property

We introduce the distortion coefficients from the Lott–Sturm–Villani theory of synthetic curvature-dimension bounds
[
LV09
,
Stu06a
,
Stu06b
]
. Given
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,\infty)
, we set
\tau_{K,N}^{t}(\theta)
for
(t,\theta)\in[0,1]\times[0,\infty]
by

(13)

\tau_{K,N}^{t}(\theta):=\begin{cases}\infty&K\theta^{2}\geq(N-1)\pi^{2}\text{ and }K>0,\\
t^{\frac{1}{N}}\left(\frac{\sin\left(t\theta\sqrt{K/(N-1)}\right)}{\sin\left(\theta\sqrt{K/(N-1)}\right)}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{N}}&0<K\theta^{2}<(N-1)\pi^{2},\\
t&K\theta^{2}=0,\text{ or }K<0\text{ and }N=1,\\
t^{\frac{1}{N}}\left(\frac{\sinh\left(t\theta\sqrt{|K|/(N-1)}\right)}{\sinh\left(\theta\sqrt{|K|/(N-1)}\right)}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{N}}&K\theta^{2}<0.\end{cases}

We will use the following version of the measure contraction property, introduced in
[
Oht07a
]
. The notation
\mathsf{m}|_{A}
stands for the restriction of a measure
\mathsf{m}
to a Borel set
A
.

Definition 2.1

(Measure contraction property)
.

Let
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,\infty)
. A metric measure space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
satisfies the
\mathrm{MCP}(K,N)
if for any
o\in X
and any
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(X)
of the form
\mu_{0}=\tfrac{1}{\mathsf{m}(A)}\mathsf{m}|_{A}
for some Borel set
A\subseteq X
with
0<\mathsf{m}(A)<\infty
, there exists
\nu\in\OptGeo(\mu_{0},\delta_{o})
such that

(14)

\frac{1}{\mathsf{m}(A)}\mathsf{m}\geq(e_{t})_{\sharp}\left(\tau_{K,N}^{(1-t)}(\mathsf{d}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}))^{N}\nu\right),\qquad\forall\,t\in[0,1].

Similarly, a metric measure space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
satisfies the
\mathrm{MCP}_{\mathrm{loc}}(K,N)
if
X
can be covered by open neighbourhoods
\mathcal{O}\subseteq X
such that for any
o\in\mathcal{O}
and any
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(X)
of the form
\mu_{0}=\tfrac{1}{\mathsf{m}(A)}\mathsf{m}|_{A}
for some Borel set
A\subseteq\mathcal{O}
with
0<\mathsf{m}(A)<\infty
, there exists
\nu\in\OptGeo(\mu_{0},\delta_{o})
such that (
14
) holds (
\mu_{t}
is not required to be supported in
\mathcal{O}
).

Remark 2.2

(Equivalence with Ohta’s
\mathrm{MCP}
)
.

In
[
Oht07a
]
, condition (
14
) is required to hold for
A\subseteq B(o,\pi/\sqrt{(N-1)/K})
when
K>0
. Such a restriction is not necessary; see
[
CM21
, Rmk. 6.10]
. Furthermore, in
[
Oht07a
]
,
\mu_{0}
is not required to have compact support, but only finite
2
-momentum. Since
\mathsf{m}
is
\sigma
-finite and
(X,\mathsf{d})
is proper, one can show, via standard stability arguments, that
Definition

2.1
implies the validity of (
14
) also when
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
.

Remark 2.3

(Variants)
.

Definition

2.1
should be compared with the one-sided measure contraction property of Sturm, introduced independently, see
[
Stu06b
, Rmk. 5.11
(iii)
]
. A variant in terms of the Rényi entropy was used by Cavalletti and Mondino in
[
CM17
]
(see also
[
CM21
, Def. 6.8]
). From
[
Raj12
, Sec. 5]
, one can deduce that the variant in
[
CM17
]
implies
Definition

2.1
, see
[
CM21
, Lemma 6.13]
. Since we do not assume the essentially non-branching condition, these variants may not be equivalent. Nevertheless, with minor modifications in the proofs, any variant would have led to the same conclusion for what concerns the results of this paper.

Any variant of
\mathrm{MCP}
implies a corresponding Bishop-Gromov volume growth inequality for
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
, and all its consequences. In particular, we record that a metric measure space satisfying the
\mathrm{MCP}(K,N)
with
K>0
(and
N>1
) must be compact, and that setting

(15)

A_{t,x}:=\left\{\gamma_{t}\mid\gamma\in\Geo(X),\gamma_{0}=x,\gamma_{1}\in A\right\}

the set of
t
-intermediate points between a Borel set
A\subseteq X
and
x\in X
, the
\mathrm{MCP}(0,N)
implies

(16)

\mathsf{m}(A_{t,x})\geq t^{N}\mathsf{m}(A).

Finally, we note that if
\mathrm{MCP}(K,N)
holds, then
N
must be greater or equal than the Hausdorff dimension of
(X,\mathsf{d})
, see e.g.
[
Oht07a
, Cor. 2.7]
.

2.2.
Quotients by isometric group actions

Let
(X,\mathsf{d})
be a complete, separable, proper and geodesic metric space. Let
G
be a topological group and
\alpha:G\times X\to X
be a (continuous) action. We use the shorthand
gx=\alpha(g,x)
for all
g\in X
and
x\in X
. We say that
G
acts by isometries if for all
g\in G
and
x,y\in X
it holds

(17)

\mathsf{d}(gx,gy)=\mathsf{d}(x,y).

We denote with
X^{*}:=X/G
the space of orbits of the action of
G
. Its elements are denoted with
x^{*}
for
x\in X
. We define a (pseudo-)distance on
X^{*}
by

(18)

\mathsf{d}^{*}(x^{*},y^{*}):=\inf_{g,g^{\prime}\in G}\mathsf{d}(gx,g^{\prime}y)=\inf_{g\in G}\mathsf{d}(gx,y).

The action of
G
on
X
is
proper
if for any compact set
K\subseteq X
,

(19)

\#\{g\in G\mid gK\cap K\neq\emptyset\}<\infty,

or, equivalently under our assumptions on
(X,\mathsf{d})
, that the map
(g,x)\mapsto(gx,x)
is proper, see
[
Kap24
, Thm. 11]
. When the action of
G
on
X
is by isometries and proper, its orbits are closed, (
18
) is a distance, and
(X^{*},\mathsf{d}^{*})
is a complete, separable, geodesic and proper metric space as well.

Let
\mathsf{m}
be a
\sigma
-finite Borel measure on
(X,\mathsf{d})
so that
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
is a metric measure space. We say that
\mathsf{m}
is
G
-invariant
if for all
g\in G
and Borel set
E
it holds

(20)

\mathsf{m}(gE)=\mathsf{m}(E).

We say that
G
acts on
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
by
metric measure isometries
if the action is by isometries, proper, and
\mathsf{m}
is
G
-invariant. In this case, we induce on
(X^{*},\mathsf{d}^{*})
a measure in a natural way, depending on whether
G
is compact or discrete.

(1)

If
G
is compact, then we set
\mathsf{m}^{*}:=\mathsf{p}_{\sharp}\mathsf{m}
, where
\mathsf{p}:X\to X^{*}
is the projection map (that is proper). It follows that
\mathsf{m}^{*}
is a
\sigma
-finite Borel measure.
Note that it is not necessary to assume that
\mathsf{m}
is
G

3.
The
\delta
-essentially non-branching condition

We recall the essentially non-branching condition, introduced by Rajala and Sturm in
[
RS14
]
.

Definition 3.1

(Essentially non-branching)
.

A set
\Gamma\subseteq\Geo(X)
is
non-branching
if for any
\gamma^{1},\gamma^{2}\in\Gamma
, we have that
\gamma^{1}=\gamma^{2}
whenever there is
t\in(0,1)
such that
\gamma^{1}_{s}=\gamma^{2}_{s}
for all
s\in[0,t]
. A metric measure space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
is
essentially non-branching
if for every
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
with
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\ll\mathsf{m}
, any
\nu\in\OptGeo(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
is concentrated on a set of non-branching geodesics, namely there exists a set of non-branching geodesics
\Gamma
such that
\nu(\Gamma)=1
.

We introduce a discrete variant of the essentially non-branching condition. The main relevance of this new condition is that, in contrast with the standard one, it is implied by the minimizing sub-Riemannian Sard property (see
Definition

3.4
).

Definition 3.2

(
\delta
-essentially non-branching)
.

A metric measure space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
is
\delta
-essentially non-branching
if for any
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(X)
with
\mu_{0}\ll\mathsf{m}
and any finite sum of Dirac masses
\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(X)
(namely,
\mu_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\lambda_{j}\delta_{x_{j}}
for some
L\in\mathbb{N}
and distinct points
x_{1},\dots,x_{L}
), any
\nu\in\mathrm{OptGeo}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
is concentrated on a set of non-branching geodesics.

Assuming the measure contraction property, essentially non-branching implies
\delta
-essentially non-branching. In fact in this case, by
[
CM17
]
, for
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}
as in
Definition

3.2
there is a unique
\nu\in\mathrm{OptGeo}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
, and it is induced by a map
S:X\to\mathrm{Geo}(X)
, and hence
\nu
is concentrated on the set
S(\supp(\mu_{0}))
which is made of non-branching geodesics. In general, the relationship between the two conditions is not clear.

3.1.
Sard properties and
\delta
-essentially non-branching

We assume some familiarity with sub-Riemannian geometry. A summary tailored to our purposes is provided in
Appendix

A
.

Definition 3.3

(Sub-Riemannian m.m.s.)
.

A
sub-Riemannian metric measure space

(M,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
is a smooth manifold
M
, equipped with a complete Carnot-Carathéodory distance
\mathsf{d}
, and a smooth measure
\mathsf{m}
, i.e. with smooth positive density in local charts.

Definition 3.4

(Sard properties)
.

A sub-Riemannian metric measure space
(M,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
satisfies

•

the
minimizing Sard property
if for any
x\in M
the set of final points of abnormal geodesics from
x
has zero measure in
M
;

•

the
*
-minimizing Sard property
if for any
x\in M
the set of final points of geodesics from
x
containing non-trivial abnormal segments has zero measure in
M
.

The
*
-minimizing Sard property was introduced in
[
BMR24
, Def. 7.13]
, and it is a reinforcement of the minimizing Sard property. The “star” comes from the fact that, if the
*
-minimizing Sard property holds true, then for any
x\in M
one can find a geodesically star-shaped subset where
\mathsf{d}^{2}(x,\cdot)
is smooth, see
[
BMR24
, Lemma 7.14]
for a precise statement. If
M
is a real-analytic manifold and the sub-Riemannian Hamiltonian is real-analytic, it is well-known that geodesics are either abnormal, or do not contain non-trivial abnormal segments; it follows that, in the real-analytic case, the
*
-minimizing and minimizing Sard properties are equivalent.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
(M,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
be a sub-Riemannian metric measure space satisfying the
*
-minimizing Sard property. Then
(M,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
is
\delta
-essentially non-branching.

Proof.

For any
y\in M
let
B_{y}\subseteq M
be the set of those initial points
\gamma_{0}
of geodesics
\gamma\in\mathrm{Geo}(M)
such that
\gamma_{1}=y
and
\gamma
is branching, that is there exists
\eta\in\mathrm{Geo}(M)
, with
\eta\neq\gamma
, and
t^{\prime}\in(0,1)
such that
\gamma|_{[0,t^{\prime}]}=\eta|_{[0,t^{\prime}]}
. The segment
\gamma|_{[0,t^{\prime}]}
must be abnormal, see
[
MR20
, Cor. 6]
. In particular,
B_{y}
is contained in the set
A_{y}
of final points of geodesics starting from
y
that contain a non-trivial abnormal segment. In turn, if the
*
-minimizing Sard property holds,
A_{y}
is contained in a Borel set
C_{y}
with
\mathsf{m}(C_{y})=0
, see
[
BMR24
, Lemma 7.14, setting
C_{y}:=M\setminus\mathcal{U}_{y}
]
.

We can now prove that
(M,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
is
\delta
-essentially non-branching. Let
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(X)
with
\mu_{0}\ll\mathsf{m}
, and
\mu_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\lambda_{j}\delta_{x_{j}}\in\mathcal{P}(X)
. Let
\nu\in\mathrm{OptGeo}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
and
C:=\cup_{j=1}^{L}C_{x_{j}}
. By construction,
C
is Borel,
\mathsf{m}(C)=0
, and thus
\mu_{0}(C)=0
. The set of geodesics in
\supp(\nu)
that can branch is contained in
e_{0}^{-1}(C)
, and we have
\nu(e_{0}^{-1}(C))=(e_{0})_{\sharp}\nu(C)=\mu_{0}(C)=0
. It follows that
\nu
is concentrated on the Borel set
\supp(\nu)\setminus e_{0}^{-1}(C)
, which is non-branching.
∎

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
be a
\delta
-essentially non-branching metric measure space satisfying the

(X^{*},\mathsf{d}^{*},\mathsf{m}^{*})

. Furthermore, if
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
is
\delta
-essentially non-branching, then
(X^{*},\mathsf{d}^{*},\mathsf{m}^{*})
is
\delta
-essentially non-branching.

Proof.

In this case, the projection
\mathsf{q}:X\to X^{*}
is a local metric measure isometry between
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
and
(X^{*},\mathsf{d}^{*},\mathsf{m}^{*})

(X^{*},\mathsf{d}^{*},\mathsf{m}^{*})

\mathrm{MCP}_{\mathrm{loc}}(K,N)

To prove the statement about
\delta
-essentially non-branching, let
\mu_{0}^{*}\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(X^{*})
with
\mu_{0}^{*}\ll\mathsf{m}^{*}
,
\mu_{1}^{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{L^{*}}\lambda_{j}\delta_{x^{*}_{j}}\in\mathcal{P}(X^{*})
and
\nu^{*}\in\OptGeo(\mu_{0}^{*},\mu_{1}^{*})
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that there are open sets
U\subseteq X
and
U^{*}\subseteq X^{*}
such that
\mathsf{q}:U\to U^{*}
is a metric measure isometry, with inverse
\sigma:U^{*}\to U
, and that
\supp(\mu_{0}^{*})\subseteq U^{*}
. Let
\mu_{0}:=\sigma_{\sharp}\mu_{0}^{*}\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(X)
. By construction,
\mathsf{m}|_{U}=\sigma_{\sharp}(\mathsf{m}^{*}|_{U^{*}})
, and hence
\mu_{0}\ll\mathsf{m}
.

Since
\mathsf{q}
is a local isometry, we can define a geodesic lifting map
\theta:\Geo(X^{*})\cap e_{0}^{-1}(U^{*})\to\Geo(X)\cap e_{0}^{-1}(U)
, that associates to
\gamma^{*}\in\Geo(X^{*})
with

the unique
\gamma\in\Geo(X)
with
\gamma_{0}=\sigma(\gamma^{*}_{0})
and such that
\mathsf{q}(\gamma)=\gamma^{*}
. See e.g.
[
BBI01
, Lemma 3.4.17]
. Note that
\theta
preserves the length of geodesics, and thus the distance between their endpoints.

Let
D:=\mathrm{diam}(\supp(\mu_{0}^{*}),\supp(\mu_{1}^{*}))<\infty
. Consider the set of geodesics
\supp(\nu^{*})\subseteq\Geo(X^{*})
. The set of lifts
\theta(\supp(\nu^{*}))\subseteq\Geo(X)
is contained in a compact ball of radius
D
within the compact set
\supp(\mu_{0})
. Furthermore, the set of final points
e_{1}(\theta(\supp(\nu^{*})))
is contained in the discrete set
\cup_{j=1}^{L^{*}}\mathsf{q}^{-1}(x_{j}^{*})
, which is then finite.

Therefore define:

(74)

\nu:=\theta_{\sharp}\nu^{*}\in\mathcal{P}(\Geo(X)).

By construction,
(e_{0})_{\sharp}\nu=\mu_{0}
, while
\mu_{1}:=(e_{1})_{\sharp}\nu
is a finite sum of Dirac deltas. Furthermore,
\mathsf{q}_{\sharp}\mu_{i}=\mu_{i}^{*}
for both

.

We claim that
\nu\in\OptGeo(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
. By contradiction, let
\pi:=(e_{0},e_{1})_{\sharp}\nu\in\Cpl(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})

\tilde{\pi}\in\Cpl(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})

(75)

\int_{X\times X}\mathsf{d}(x,y)^{2}\,\tilde{\pi}(\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y)<\int_{X\times X}\mathsf{d}(x,y)^{2}\,\pi(\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y).

\pi
is concentrated on a set of

with
\mathsf{d}(x,y)=\mathsf{d}^{*}(x^{*},y^{*})

x^{*}=\mathsf{q}(x)

6.
Carnot homogeneous spaces

The following discussion presents the construction of Carnot homogeneous spaces, which appear as tangent cones of general sub-Riemannian manifolds, as explained in
[
Bel96
]
. At a first read, it could be helpful to consider the case of Carnot groups, which corresponds to choosing the trivial subgroup
\mathbb{H}={e}
in the notation used below.

Definition 6.1

(Stratified groups)
.

A Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is
stratified
if it admits a decomposition

(107)

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{s},

where, setting
\mathfrak{g}_{i}=0
for all
i\geq s+1
, it holds

(108)

[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{i}]=\mathfrak{g}_{i+1},\qquad\forall\,i=1,\dots,s.

The subspaces
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
are called
strata
, and
s
is the
step
of the stratification. A
stratified group
is a simply connected Lie group
\mathbb{G}
whose Lie algebra is stratified. A
Carnot group
is a stratified Lie group
\mathbb{G}
together with a choice of scalar product on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
. We identify the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with the subspace of left-invariant vector fields, so that we usually call the scalar product of a Carnot group a
left-invariant
scalar product on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
.

Any stratified group/algebra is nilpotent (of step
s
), and as a consequence of this and simple connectedness, the group exponential map
\exp_{\mathbb{G}}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{G}
is be a smooth diffeomorphism.

Definition 6.2

(Dilations)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a stratified group with stratified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{s}
. The
dilation
of factor
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is the Lie group endomorphism
\delta_{\lambda}:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{G}
such that

(109)

(\delta_{\lambda})_{*}V=\lambda^{i}V,\qquad\forall\,V\in\mathfrak{g}_{i},\quad\forall\,i=1,\dots,s,

where the star denotes the push-forward. Note that if
\lambda\neq 0
then
\delta_{\lambda}
is invertible with inverse
\delta_{1/\lambda}
. With a slight abuse of notation, we will also use the term “dilation” to refer to the Lie algebra endomorphism
(\delta_{\lambda})_{*}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a stratified group, and fix a closed, dilation-invariant subgroup
\mathbb{H}<\mathbb{G}
. This is equivalent to the Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathbb{H}
being dilation-invariant, or equivalently, to
\mathfrak{h}
having a stratification compatible with that of
\mathfrak{g}
, namely

(110)

\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{s},\qquad\text{with}\qquad\mathfrak{h}_{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{i},\quad\forall\,i=1,\dots,s.

Note that
\mathfrak{h}
need not be generated by
\mathfrak{h}_{1}
, meaning that
\mathbb{H}
itself need not be a stratified group.

Let
\mathbb{M}:=\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
be the quotient of
\mathbb{G}
by the action of
\mathbb{H}
on it by left multiplication (i.e. the set of right cosets). The smooth manifold
\mathbb{M}
has dimension
\dim\mathbb{G}-\dim\mathbb{H}
and inherits several natural structures, as outlined below in
Sections

6.1
,
6.2
,
6.3
,
6.4
and
6.5
.

6.1.
Homogeneous structure

The left multiplication by elements of
\mathbb{G}
does not, in general, induce a left action of
\mathbb{G}
on
\mathbb{M}
(unless
\mathbb{H}
is normal). However, the
right
multiplication of
\mathbb{G}
always induces a
right
action
\beta:\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{M}\to\mathbb{M}
defined by

(111)

\beta(g^{\prime},\mathbb{H}g):=\mathbb{H}(gg^{\prime}),\qquad\forall\,g,g^{\prime}\in\mathbb{G}.

Since it is a right action, we use the appropriate shorthand
\mathbb{H}gg^{\prime}=\beta(g^{\prime},\mathbb{H}g)
. Of course, if
\mathbb{H}=\{e\}
so that
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{G}
, then
\beta
coincides with the right multiplication on
\mathbb{G}
.

6.2.
Dilations

Since
\mathbb{H}
is dilation-invariant, the dilations of
\mathbb{G}
induce dilations on
\mathbb{M}
by setting

(112)

\delta_{\lambda}(\mathbb{H}g):=\mathbb{H}\delta_{\lambda}(g),\qquad\forall\,g\in\mathbb{G},\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

The fixed point of dilations of
\mathbb{M}
is the coset of the identity, namely
\mathbb{H}e
, that we call the
origin
. Of course, if
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{G}
, these dilations coincide with the ones given by
Definition

6.2
.

6.3.
Distinguished algebra of vector fields

Let
\pi:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{M}
be the quotient map. Its push-forward induces a Lie algebra homomorphism
\pi_{*}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathrm{Vec}(\mathbb{M})
. Thus, although
\mathbb{M}
is not generally a group, it still carries a distinguished stratified Lie algebra of vector fields given by
\pi_{*}\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathrm{Vec}(\mathbb{M})
. For
\pi_{*}
to be well-defined, it is crucial that
\mathbb{M}
is defined as the set of right cosets, and not the left ones.

The dilation-invariant subalgebra
\pi_{*}\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\pi_{*}\mathfrak{g}
consists of the vector fields in
\pi_{*}\mathfrak{g}
that vanish at the origin
\mathbb{H}e
. The distinguished subalgebras of vector fields
\pi_{*}\mathfrak{g}
and
\pi_{*}\mathfrak{h}
are both dilation-invariant, and
\pi_{*}\mathfrak{g}
is stratified with strata
\pi_{*}\mathfrak{g}_{i}
, where
(\delta_{\lambda})_{*}
acts by multiplication by
\lambda^{i}
.

Of course, if
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{G}
, the distinguished algebra of vector fields is just the Lie algebra of left-invariant vector fields on
\mathbb{G}
.

6.4.
Sub-Riemannian metric structure

Assume that
\mathbb{G}
is a Carnot group. Any orthonormal basis
\{X_{1},\dots,X_{k}\}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{1}\subseteq\mathrm{Vec}(\mathbb{G})
induces smooth vector fields
\{\pi_{*}X_{1},\dots,\pi_{*}X_{k}\}\subseteq\pi_{*}\mathfrak{g}_{1}\subseteq\mathrm{Vec}(\mathbb{M})
, which in turn define a sub-Riemannian structure on
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
, in the sense of
Appendix

A
. We denote by
\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{M}}
the corresponding sub-Riemannian metric. One can easily verify that
\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{M}}
does not depend on the choice of the orthonormal basis. Furthermore, the dilations introduced in
Section

6.2
satisfy

(113)

\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{M}}\left(\delta_{\lambda}(\mathbb{H}g),\delta_{\lambda}(\mathbb{H}g^{\prime})\right)=\lambda\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{M}}\left(\mathbb{H}g,\mathbb{H}g^{\prime}\right),\qquad\forall\,g,g^{\prime}\in\mathbb{G},\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

Remark 6.3

(Carnot-Carathéodory metrics)
.

When
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{G}
, the above construction yields the standard Carnot-Carathéodory metric on the Carnot group. It is left-invariant, in the sense that

(114)

\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{G}}(gg^{\prime},gg^{\prime\prime})=\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{G}}(g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime}),\qquad\forall\,g,g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{G}.

We emphasize that
\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{G}}
is also not right-invariant unless
\mathbb{G}
is abelian.

Remark 6.4

(Equivalence with the quotient metric)
.

If
\mathbb{G}
is a Carnot group with Carnot–Carathéodory metric
\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{G}}
, and
\mathbb{H}<\mathbb{G}
is a closed, dilation-invariant subgroup, then setting
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
and defining the metric
\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{M}}
as described in
Section

6.4
, we have

(115)

\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{M}}(\mathbb{H}g,\mathbb{H}g^{\prime})=\inf_{h,h^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}}\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{G}}(hg,h^{\prime}g^{\prime})=\inf_{h\in\mathbb{H}}\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{G}}(g,hg^{\prime}),

where the second equality follows from the left-invariance of
\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{G}}
. In other words, the sub-Riemannian metric coincides with the quotient metric described in
Section

2.2
(note that the action of
\mathbb{H}
on
\mathbb{G}
is proper). We may freely use either formulation depending on context.

6.5.
Invariant measure

For a reference to what follows, see
[
Fed69
, Sec. 2.7]
or
[
HR79
, Sec. 15]
. Since both
\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{G}
are unimodular,
\mathbb{M}
has a unique (up to rescaling) right-invariant Haar measure, namely a Borel measure invariant under the right action of
\mathbb{G}
on
\mathbb{M}
(see
Section

6.1
). We denote a choice of such a measure by
\mathsf{m}_{\mathbb{M}}
. This measure is then characterized by the property

(116)

\mathsf{m}_{\mathbb{M}}(Eg)=\mathsf{m}_{\mathbb{M}}(E),

for all Borel set
E\subseteq\mathbb{M}
, where
Eg=\beta(g,E)
. We call
\mathsf{m}_{\mathbb{M}}
an
invariant measure
on
\mathbb{M}
, or also a
right-invariant measure
if we want to emphasize the right action.

When
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{G}
, the measure
\mathsf{m}_{\mathbb{G}}
is (a choice of) the classical right-invariant Haar measure. Furthermore, since
\mathbb{G}
is nilpotent and therefore unimodular,
\mathsf{m}_{\mathbb{G}}
is also left-invariant, and thus bi-invariant. In other words, for any Borel set
E\subseteq\mathbb{G}
and all
g\in\mathbb{G}
, it holds

(117)

\mathsf{m}_{\mathbb{G}}(gE)=\mathsf{m}_{\mathbb{G}}(Eg)=\mathsf{m}_{\mathbb{G}}(E).

6.6.
Carnot homogeneous spaces

We are now ready to give a general definition putting together all the structures defined in
Sections

,
6.2
,
6.3
,
6.4
and
6.5
.

Definition 6.5

(Carnot homogeneous space)
.

A
Carnot homogeneous space
is the quotient
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
of a Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
by a dilation-invariant closed subgroup
\mathbb{H}<\mathbb{G}
. Let
\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{M}}
be the corresponding quotient metric, and
\mathsf{m}_{\mathbb{M}}
a choice of invariant measure. We denote by
(\mathbb{M},\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{M}})
and
(\mathbb{M},\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{M}},\mathsf{m}_{\mathbb{M}})
the resulting metric space and metric measure space, respectively.

The terminology “Carnot homogeneous space” is used in
[
Bel96
, Sec. 5.6]
, while some authors prefer the terminology “self-similar space”.

As a metric space, any Carnot homogeneous space
(\mathbb{M},\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{M}})
is complete, separable, geodesic, and proper. We stress that
\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{M}}
is not invariant under the right action of
\mathbb{G}
on
\mathbb{M}
described in
Section

6.1
, unless
\mathbb{H}
is normal and
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
is abelian. This is a consequence of the fact that the Carnot-Carathéodory metric on
\mathbb{G}
is left-invariant, but not right-invariant in general. Thus, Carnot homogeneous spaces are not homogeneous in the sense of metric geometry.

Any Carnot group equipped with its left-invariant Carnot-Carathéodory metric and a bi-invariant Haar measure is a Carnot homogeneous space, corresponding to the choice
\mathbb{H}=\{e\}
in
Definition

6.5
. More generally, we have the following.

Remark 6.6

(Consistency with Carnot groups)
.

If
\mathbb{H}\triangleleft\mathbb{G}
is normal, then
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
is a stratified group, with group multiplication given by
(\mathbb{H}g)(\mathbb{H}g^{\prime}):=\mathbb{H}gg^{\prime}
, and stratified Lie algebra isomorphic to
\mathfrak{g}_{1}/\penalty\mathfrak{h}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{s}/\penalty\mathfrak{h}_{s}
. By construction, the dilations
\delta_{\lambda}:\mathbb{M}\to\mathbb{M}
of
Section

6.2
coincide with the ones of
Definition

6.2
, seeing
\mathbb{M}
as a stratified group. If
\mathbb{G}
is a Carnot group, then
\mathbb{M}
is also a Carnot group (with scalar product on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}/\penalty\mathfrak{h}_{1}
induced by the one on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
via the surjective map
\pi_{*}:\mathfrak{g}_{1}\to\mathfrak{g}_{1}/\penalty\mathfrak{h}_{1}
), and
\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{M}}
is the corresponding Carnot-Carathéodory metric, left-invariant by the left multiplication of
\mathbb{M}
. Finally, by construction, the measure
\mathsf{m}_{\mathbb{M}}
is invariant under the right action
\beta:\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{M}\to\mathbb{M}
defined in
Section

6.1
. In this case, we note that this right action corresponds to the right multiplication in
\mathbb{M}
, in the sense that

(118)

(\mathbb{H}g)(\mathbb{H}g^{\prime})=\beta(g^{\prime},\mathbb{H}g),\qquad\forall g,g^{\prime}\in\mathbb{G}.

It follows that
\mathsf{m}_{\mathbb{M}}
is a classical right-invariant Haar measure on the Lie group
\mathbb{M}
, and since the latter is unimodular,
\mathsf{m}_{\mathbb{M}}
is also invariant under the left multiplication of
\mathbb{M}
. In other words, the measure
\mathsf{m}_{\mathbb{M}}
is in this case a Haar bi-invariant measure on the group
\mathbb{M}
. Therefore, if
\mathbb{H}
is a normal subgroup, all the structures defined in the previous sections for the Carnot homogeneous space
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
coincide with the ones obtained by regarding
\mathbb{M}
directly as a Carnot group.

Remark 6.7

(Minimal group and irreducible presentation)
.

Different choices of
\mathbb{G}
and
\mathbb{H}
may yield the same Carnot homogeneous space
\mathbb{M}
(i.e. the same smooth manifold, the same stratified Lie algebra of vector fields
\pi_{*}\mathfrak{g}
, and the same subalgebra
\pi_{*}\mathfrak{h}
of vector fields vanishing at the origin, up to isomorphisms). However, there is a “smallest” possible choice. Letting
\mathfrak{k}:=\ker\pi_{*}|_{\mathfrak{g}}
, it is easy to see that
\mathfrak{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is a dilation-invariant ideal, and it is clear that
\mathfrak{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}
. In particular,
\mathbb{K}=\exp_{\mathbb{G}}(\mathfrak{k})
is a closed, stratified normal subgroup of both
\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{G}
. Therefore, it holds:

(119)

\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}=(\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{K})/\penalty(\mathbb{H}/\penalty\mathbb{K}).

A given Carnot homogeneous space
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
can thus be realized equivalently as the quotient
\mathbb{M}=\tilde{\mathbb{G}}/\penalty\tilde{\mathbb{H}}
, where
\tilde{\mathbb{G}}:=\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{K}
and
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}:=\mathbb{H}/\penalty\mathbb{K}
(which are stratified groups with Lie algebras denoted by
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}
, respectively). In this way, the corresponding Lie algebra homomorphism
\tilde{\pi}_{*}:\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathrm{Vec}(\mathbb{M})
is injective, and the original stratified Lie algebras
\pi_{*}\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\pi_{*}\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathrm{Vec}(\mathbb{M})
are
isomorphic
(as stratified Lie algebras) to the Lie algebras of
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}
and
\tilde{\mathbb{G}}
. Furthermore, if
\mathbb{G}
is a Carnot group, then
\tilde{\mathbb{G}}
is also a Carnot group and its sub-Riemannian distance coincides with the quotient distance. Then, for all
g,g^{\prime}\in\mathbb{G}
, it holds:

(120)

\displaystyle\mathsf{d}_{\tilde{\mathbb{G}}/\penalty\tilde{\mathbb{H}}}\big(\tilde{\mathbb{H}}\mathbb{K}g,\tilde{\mathbb{H}}\mathbb{K}g^{\prime}\big)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\mathbb{K}h,\mathbb{K}h^{\prime}\in\tilde{\mathbb{H}}}\mathsf{d}_{\tilde{\mathbb{G}}}\big((\mathbb{K}h)(\mathbb{K}g),(\mathbb{K}h^{\prime})(\mathbb{K}g^{\prime})\big)

(121)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\mathbb{K}h,\mathbb{K}h^{\prime}\in\tilde{\mathbb{H}}}\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{K}}(\mathbb{K}hg,\mathbb{K}h^{\prime}g^{\prime})

(122)

\displaystyle=\inf_{h,h^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}}\inf_{k,k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{K}}\mathsf{d}{G}(khg,k^{\prime}h^{\prime}g^{\prime})

(123)

\displaystyle=\inf_{h,h^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}}\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{G}}(hg,h^{\prime}g^{\prime})=\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}}(g,g^{\prime}),

where we used that
\mathbb{K}
is a normal subgroup of
\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{G}
. Therefore, the metric structure induced on
\mathbb{M}
by realizing
\mathbb{M}
as
\tilde{\mathbb{G}}/\penalty\tilde{\mathbb{H}}
is the same as one obtained by realizing
\mathbb{M}
as
\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
.

We call
\tilde{\mathbb{G}}
the
minimal group
of the Carnot homogeneous space, and
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}
the
minimal subgroup
. The minimal group and subgroup of a Carnot homogeneous space are unique up to isomorphisms of stratified groups, since they are the ones associated with the Lie algebras
\pi_{*}\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\pi_{*}\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathrm{Vec}(\mathbb{M})
. We say that the presentation of the Carnot homogeneous space
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
is
irreducible
.

6.7.
Isometries

If
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathsf{m})
and
(\tilde{X},\tilde{\mathsf{d}},\tilde{\mathsf{m}})
are metric measure spaces, a
metric isometry
is a distance-preserving invertible map from
X
to
\tilde{X}
, and a
metric measure isometry
a metric isometry that also pushes forward
\mathsf{m}
to
\tilde{\mathsf{m}}
. It is known that any metric isometry between Carnot groups is smooth, see
[
Ham90
,
LO16
]
. Furthermore, up to a constant scaling of the measure, any metric isometry is also a metric measure isometry, see
[
BR13
]
. We do not know whether these results hold for Carnot homogeneous spaces. For this reason, we directly define isometries of Carnot homogeneous spaces as smooth maps that lift to isometries of Carnot groups.

Definition 6.8

(Smooth isometries of Carnot homogeneous spaces)
.

Let
\mathbb{M}
and
\tilde{\mathbb{M}}
be Carnot homogeneous spaces with irreducible presentations
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
and
\tilde{\mathbb{M}}=\tilde{\mathbb{G}}/\penalty\tilde{\mathbb{H}}
. A
smooth isometry of Carnot homogeneous spaces
is a diffeomorphism
\phi:\mathbb{M}\to\tilde{\mathbb{M}}
for which there exists a metric isometry
\Phi:\mathbb{G}\to\tilde{\mathbb{G}}
such that the following diagram commute:

(124)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\mathbb{G}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

We will say in this case that
\mathbb{M}
and
\tilde{\mathbb{M}}
are smoothly isometric.

Proposition 6.9

(Smooth isometries are metric measure isometries)
.

Let
(\mathbb{M},\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{M}},\mathsf{m}_{\mathbb{M}})

(\tilde{\mathbb{M}},\mathsf{d}_{\tilde{\mathbb{M}}},\mathsf{m}_{\tilde{\mathbb{M}}})

(depending only on the choices of invariant measures) such that any smooth isometry
\phi:\mathbb{M}\to\tilde{\mathbb{M}}
is a metric measure isometry between
(\mathbb{M},\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{M}},\mathsf{m}_{\mathbb{M}})
and

Proof.

Observe that from (

g\in\mathbb{G}

(125)

\Phi(\mathbb{H}g)=\tilde{\mathbb{H}}\Phi(g).

It follows that for all

(126)

\displaystyle\mathsf{d}_{\tilde{\mathbb{M}}}(\phi(\mathbb{H}g),\phi(\mathbb{H}g^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\mathsf{d}_{\tilde{\mathbb{M}}}(\tilde{\mathbb{H}}\Phi(g),\tilde{\mathbb{H}}\Phi(g^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\inf_{\tilde{h}\in\tilde{\mathbb{H}}}\mathsf{d}_{\tilde{\mathbb{G}}}(\Phi(g),\tilde{h}\Phi(g^{\prime}))

(129)

\displaystyle=\inf_{h\in\mathbb{H}}\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{G}}(g,hg^{\prime})

Definition 6.10

(Engel and Martinet)
.

The
Engel group

\mathbb{Eng}
is the stratified group with stratified algebra
\mathfrak{e}=\mathfrak{e}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{e}_{2}\oplus\mathfrak{e}_{3}
, for which there exists a basis
X_{1},X_{2},Y,Z
satisfying

(135)

\mathfrak{e}_{1}=\mathrm{span}\{X_{1},X_{2}\},\qquad\mathfrak{e}_{2}=\mathrm{span}\{Y\},\qquad\mathfrak{e}_{3}=\mathrm{span}\{Z\},

and with the following non-zero bracket relations:

(136)

[X_{1},X_{2}]=Y,\qquad[X_{1},Y]=Z.

It is a Carnot group when equipped with the scalar product making
X_{1},X_{2}
orthonormal.

The
Martinet structure

\mathbb{Mar}
is the Carnot homogeneous space realized as the quotient
\mathbb{Mar}=\mathbb{Eng}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
of the Engel group by the closed dilation-invariant subgroup
\mathbb{H}
whose Lie algebra is
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{e}_{2}
. The corresponding sub-Riemannian structure, which has constant rank, is generated by the family of vector fields
\pi_{*}X_{1}
,
\pi_{*}X_{2}
.

Lemma 6.11

(Uniqueness of
\mathbb{Eng}
and
\mathbb{Mar}
)
.

Let
\mathbb{M}
be a Carnot homogeneous space whose minimal group is isomorphic (as a stratified group) to the Engel group, and whose sub-Riemannian distribution has constant rank. Then precisely one of the following two cases holds:

•

either
\mathbb{M}
is a Carnot group, in which case
\mathbb{M}
is isometric to
\mathbb{Eng}
;

•

or
\mathbb{M}
is not a Carnot group, in which case
\mathbb{M}
is isometric to
\mathbb{Mar}
.

Proof.

By
Remark

6.7
, we can assume that
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
where
\mathbb{G}
is isomorphic as a stratified Lie group to the Engel group, and
\mathbb{H}
is a closed, dilation-invariant subgroup. The subgroup
\mathbb{H}
is determined by a stratified subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
, say

(137)

\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{2}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{3},\qquad\text{with}\qquad\mathfrak{h}_{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{i},\quad i=1,2,3.

If
\pi_{*}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathrm{Vec}(\mathbb{M})
has non-trivial kernel, the distinguished Lie algebra
\pi_{*}\mathfrak{g}
is not isomorphic to the Engel algebra. Thus, we must have
\ker\pi_{*}=\{0\}
.

It is well-known that, letting
\exp_{\mathbb{G}}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{G}
be the group exponential map, it holds

(138)

\Ad_{\exp_{\mathbb{G}}(X)}(W)=W+\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}\frac{1}{i!}\ad_{X}^{i}(W),\qquad\forall\,X,W\in\mathfrak{g},

see e.g.
[
CG90
, p. 12]
, where
\Ad:\mathbb{G}\to\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\ad:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
are defined respectively by

(139)

\Ad_{g}(W):=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}g\exp_{\mathbb{G}}(tW)g^{-1},\qquad\ad_{X}(Y):=[X,Y],\qquad\forall\,g\in\mathbb{G},\,X,Y,W\in\mathfrak{g}.

Using (
138
) it follows that, for
g\in\mathbb{G}
and
V\in\mathfrak{g}
, it holds

(140)

(\pi_{*}V)|_{\mathbb{H}g}=0,\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\Ad_{g}(V)\in\mathfrak{h}.

Taking
V\in\mathfrak{h}_{3}
in (
140
) we see that
\pi_{*}V
is the zero vector field, but this contradicts the fact that
\ker\pi_{*}=\{0\}
so that
\mathfrak{h}_{3}=\{0\}
. Similarly, taking
V\in\mathfrak{h}_{1}
, in (
140
) we see that
\pi_{*}V|_{\mathbb{H}e}=0
, but this contradicts the constant rank assumption unless
\mathfrak{h}_{1}=\{0\}
.

The only possibility left is that
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. There are two cases:
\mathfrak{h}=\{0\}
or
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. The corresponding structures correspond to the Engel group and the Martinet structure, respectively.

We prove now that, in both cases, the corresponding structures are isometric (as Carnot homogeneous spaces) to
\mathbb{Eng}
or
\mathbb{Mar}
, respectively. Consider the following fact. Any Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
with a stratified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{3}
, where
\dim\mathfrak{g}_{1}=2
and
\dim\mathfrak{g}_{2}=\dim\mathfrak{g}_{3}=1
, is isometric to
\mathbb{Eng}
. Indeed, for any
B\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
,
B\neq 0
, we can write

(141)

\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\left(\ker\ad_{B}\right)^{\perp}\oplus\ker\ad_{B}.

Choose an orthonormal basis
\{A_{1},A_{2}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
adapted to the above splitting, with
A_{2}\in\ker\ad_{B}
, and we can assume that
B=[A_{1},A_{2}]\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. By construction,
C:=[A_{1},B]\in\mathfrak{g}_{3}
is non-zero. By construction, there is a unique stratified Lie algebra isomorphism
\Phi_{*}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{e}
such that

(142)

\Phi_{*}:A_{1}\mapsto X_{1},\quad A_{2}\mapsto X_{2},\quad B\mapsto Y,\quad C\mapsto Z,

which lifts to a stratified Lie group isomorphism
\Phi:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{Eng}
. By construction,
\Phi_{*}
preserves the scalar product between the first strata, and
\Phi
is an isometry between Carnot groups.

Thus, for the choice
\mathfrak{h}=\{0\}
, there exists an isometry
\Phi:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{Eng}
(the one just constructed). For the choice
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, letting
\Phi
be the aforementioned isometry, note that
\Phi_{*}(\mathfrak{g}_{2})=\mathfrak{e}_{2}
, so that
\Phi(\exp(\mathfrak{g}_{2}))=\exp(\mathfrak{e}_{2})
. Therefore, the map
\Phi
descends to a well-defined diffeomorphism on the quotients,
\phi:\mathbb{G}/\penalty\exp(\mathfrak{g}_{2})\to\mathbb{Eng}/\penalty\exp(\mathfrak{e}_{2})=\mathbb{Mar}
, which, by construction, is an isometry of Carnot homogeneous spaces (see
Proposition

6.9
).
∎

Remark 6.12

(Carnot homogeneous spaces from Engel)
.

Besides
\mathbb{Eng}
and
\mathbb{Mar}
, other Carnot homogeneous spaces have Engel as their minimal group. They all take the form
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{Eng}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
, with
\mathbb{H}<\mathbb{Eng}
being a closed, dilation-invariant subgroup with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{e}
. Classifying them is equivalent to finding all dilation-invariant subalgebras
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{e}
such that
\pi_{*}:\mathfrak{e}\to\mathrm{Vec}(\mathbb{M})
has trivial kernel. As discussed in the proof above, we must have
\mathfrak{h}_{3}=\{0\}
. A case-by-case analysis reveals that we may choose:

(i)

\mathfrak{h}=\{0\}
, in which case
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{Eng}
;

(ii)

\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{e}_{2}
, in which case
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{Mar}
;

(iii)

\mathfrak{h}=\mathrm{span}\{X_{2},Y\}
, in which case we obtain a rank-varying sub-Riemannian structure of dimension
2
with an Engel-type growth vector, known as the
2
-Grushin plane
[
Bor22
]
;

(iv)

\mathfrak{h}=\mathrm{span}\{aX_{1}+bX_{2}\}
with
(a,b)\neq(0,0)
, in which case we obtain a rank-varying sub-Riemannian structure of dimension
3
with an Engel-type growth vector.

In this work, we consider only
\mathbb{Eng}
and
\mathbb{Mar}
in the list above, and we will need to exploit the explicit knowledge of the geodesic structures of
\mathbb{Mar}
, see
Section

8
.

6.8.
Quotients to Engel or Martinet

We study Carnot groups admitting the Martinet structure as a quotient. In doing so, we prove a fact of independent interest but key to our work: admitting a quotient to
\mathbb{Eng}
is equivalent to admitting one to
\mathbb{Mar}
.

Theorem 6.13

(Stratified groups admitting Martinet or Engel quotients)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Carnot group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and step

. The following conditions are equivalent:

\mathbb{K}<\mathbb{G}

(iii)

there exists
\mathfrak{h}_{3}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{3}

.

Remark 6.14

(A sufficient condition)
.

Letting

, the equivalent characterizations of
Items

(ii)
and

, or

\dim\mathbb{G}\geq(k-1)\left(\frac{k^{2}}{3}+\frac{5k}{6}+1\right).

This condition appeared in
[

3

Proof.

\lx@cref{refnum}{item:quot-engel}\Rightarrow\lx@cref{refnum}{item:quot-algebraicondition}
. To avoid confusion with the statement, we momentarily denote by
\bar{\mathfrak{h}}
the Lie algebra of
\mathbb{H}
. Observe that
\mathbb{H}\triangleleft\mathbb{G}
if and only if its Lie algebra
\bar{\mathfrak{h}}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}

\mathbb{H}
is dilation-invariant,
\bar{\mathfrak{h}}

). Thus, the quotient group
\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
has its stratified Lie algebra isomorphic to
\mathfrak{g}/\penalty\bar{\mathfrak{h}}
, with stratification

(145)

\mathfrak{g}/\penalty\bar{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}/\penalty\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}/\penalty\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{s}/\penalty\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{s}.

By assumption,
\mathfrak{g}/\bar{\mathfrak{h}}
is isomorphic to the Engel algebra. In particular, each stratum
\mathfrak{g}_{j}/\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{j}
is isomorphic to the corresponding stratum of the Engel algebra. Therefore, we must have

(146)

\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{i}=\mathfrak{g}_{i},\qquad\text{for }i=4,\dots,s,

and, moreover,

(147)

\displaystyle\dim\mathfrak{g}_{3}

\displaystyle=\dim\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{3}+1,

(148)

\displaystyle\dim\mathfrak{g}_{2}

\displaystyle=\dim\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}+1,

(149)

\displaystyle\dim\mathfrak{g}_{1}

\displaystyle=\dim\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}+2.

Set then
\mathfrak{h}_{3}:=\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{3}
, and recall the definition of
\mathfrak{h}_{2}
from (

). Since
\bar{\mathfrak{h}}
is an ideal, we have
\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{2}
, so the codimension of
\mathfrak{h}_{2}
is at most
1
. It cannot be zero; otherwise, we would have
\mathfrak{h}_{2}=\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, and thus, by construction,
[\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}]=[\mathfrak{h}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}]\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{3}\subsetneq\mathfrak{g}_{3}
, contradicting the stratification assumption. Hence, we must have
\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}=\mathfrak{h}_{2}
.

\lx@cref{refnum}{item:quot-algebraicondition}\Rightarrow\lx@cref{refnum}{item:quot-engel}
. Let
\mathfrak{h}_{3}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{3}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{2}
be as in the assumption.
Since
\mathfrak{h}_{3}
has has codimension one, we may choose
Z\in\mathfrak{g}_{3}
such that

(150)

\mathfrak{g}_{3}=\mathbb{R}Z\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{3}.

There also exist
Y\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
and
X_{1}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
such that
[X_{1},Y]=Z
. In particular, we have that

(151)

\mathfrak{g}_{2}=\mathbb{R}Y\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{2}.

Consider now the map

(152)

P:=\mathrm{proj}_{Z}\circ\ad_{Y}:\mathfrak{g}_{1}\to\mathfrak{g}_{3}.

It is has rank
1
since
P(X_{1})=-Z
, so that

(153)

\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\mathbb{R}X_{1}\oplus\ker P.

We claim that

(154)

[\ker P,\ker P]\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{2}.

Indeed, taking
X,X^{\prime}\in\ker P
, we have that
[X,X^{\prime}]=\alpha Y+\mathfrak{h}_{2}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. By the Jacobi equation, we obtain

(155)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=[X_{1},[X,X^{\prime}]]+[X^{\prime},[X_{1},X]]+[X,[X^{\prime},X_{1}]]

(156)

\displaystyle=[X_{1},\alpha Y+\mathfrak{h}_{2}]+[X^{\prime},\beta Y+\mathfrak{h}_{2}]+[X,\gamma Y+\mathfrak{h}_{2}]

(157)

\displaystyle=\alpha Z+\mathfrak{h}_{3},

for some

, where in the last equality we used the fact that

and

. It follows that

, proving the claim.

As a consequence of (
154

X_{2}\in\ker P
such that
[X_{1},X_{2}]\notin\mathfrak{h}_{2}
. In particular, we can choose

(158)

Y^{\prime}:=[X_{1},X_{2}]=Y+\mathfrak{h}_{2}.

Using the definition of

and the fact that

(159)

Z^{\prime}:=[X_{1},Y^{\prime}]=[X_{1},Y+\mathfrak{h}_{2}]=Z+\mathfrak{h}_{3}.

Consider now the linear map

. The map

. Thus, denoting by

, we have

(161)

\ker P=\mathbb{R}X_{2}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{1}.

In summary, the splittings we have obtained so far are

(162)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{R}X_{1}\oplus\mathbb{R}X_{2}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{1},

(163)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{R}Y^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{2},

(164)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{3}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{R}Z^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{3}.

Finally, set
\mathfrak{h}_{j}:=\mathfrak{g}_{j}
for all

.

We claim that

[\mathfrak{h}_{1},\mathfrak{h}_{1}]\subseteq[\ker P,\ker P]\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{2}.

Furthermore, since

[\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{h}_{1}]=[\mathbb{R}Y+\mathfrak{h}_{2},\mathfrak{h}_{1}]\subseteq[Y,\mathfrak{h}_{1}]+[\mathfrak{h}_{2},\mathfrak{h}_{1}]\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{3},

where we have used that

, together with the definition of

. Finally, by construction of

We have thus proved that

(169)

[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{h}_{1}]\subseteq\mathfrak{h},\qquad[\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{h}_{1}]\subseteq\mathfrak{h},\qquad[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{h}_{2}]\subseteq\mathfrak{h}.

Any other

for

not listed there belongs to

for some

. Thus,

is a dilation-invariant normal subgroup and

is a Carnot group isomorphic (as a stratified group) to

. The Carnot homogeneous space

is isometric to
\mathbb{Eng}
by

6.11
.

\lx@cref{refnum}{item:quot-martinet}\Rightarrow\lx@cref{refnum}{item:quot-algebraicondition}
. By

of a smooth isometry between Carnot homogeneous spaces, the projection map

whose image is isomorphic to the Engel algebra. Setting
\mathfrak{h}:=\ker\pi_{*}
, we deduce that
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}
is isomorphic to the Engel algebra. We then repeat the steps from
\lx@cref{refnum}{item:quot-engel}\Rightarrow\lx@cref{refnum}{item:quot-algebraicondition}
.

\lx@cref{refnum}{item:quot-engel}\Rightarrow\lx@cref{refnum}{item:quot-martinet}
. Remember that isometric Carnot groups must be isomorphic, see
[
LO16
]
. Thus, by assumption, there exists a stratified Lie group isomorphism
\Psi:\mathbb{Eng}\to\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H}
. Consider the ideal
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
, and define

\mathfrak{k}
is a closed, dilation-invariant subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
with

. Furthermore, observe that

and its push-forward

, and the minimal subgroup is

,

is isometric to
\mathbb{Mar}
.
∎

6.9.
The factorization argument

To motivate the next construction, suppose that
\mathbb{G}
is a Carnot group admitting a closed, dilation-invariant subgroup
\mathbb{K}<\mathbb{G}
so that
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{K}
is a Carnot homogeneous space. Our aim is to deduce validity (resp. failure) of the
\mathrm{MCP}
for
\mathbb{M}
(resp.
\mathbb{G}
) from the validity of the
\mathrm{MCP}
for
\mathbb{G}
(resp.
\mathbb{M}
). The whole
Section

2.2
was developed for this purpose. Unfortunately, except in the trivial case,
\mathbb{K}
is neither compact nor discrete. Hence we cannot apply neither
Theorem

4.1
(for discrete group actions) nor
Theorem

5.3
(for compact group actions). Suppose, however, that
\mathbb{K}
possesses a co-compact lattice, namely a discrete, normal subgroup
\Gamma\triangleleft\mathbb{K}
such that
\mathbb{K}/\penalty\Gamma
is a compact Lie group (we will see that it acts on
\mathbb{G}/\penalty\Gamma
by metric measure isometries). In this case, we can factor the quotient map
\pi:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{M}
as

(171)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi:\mathbb{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{G}/\penalty\Gamma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{G}/\penalty\Gamma)/\penalty(\mathbb{K}/\penalty\Gamma)=\mathbb{M},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{q}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{p}}

where
\mathsf{q}
is a quotient by a discrete group action and
\mathsf{p}
is a quotient by a compact group of isometries, to which one could apply
Theorems

4.1
and
5.3
. However, it is well-known that a simply connected nilpotent Lie group admits a co-compact lattice if and only if it is rational, i.e. its Lie algebra admits a basis for which all structure constants are rational numbers
[
CG90
, Sec. 5]
. This condition does not always hold. Furthermore, even in that case, the quotient
\mathbb{K}/\penalty\Gamma
might not be abelian, which is a key assumption required in the proof of
Theorem

5.3
. To overcome these obstacles, we replace the naive decomposition (
171
) with a more elaborate construction, consisting of a “tower” of simpler quotients such that, at each step, both
Theorems

4.1
and
5.3
can be applied. This construction is the content of
Theorem

6.16
below.

To state it, we need a version of (
171
) where both sides are Carnot homogeneous spaces. Thus, we first prove the following (mostly tautological) result, which generalizes the fact that, if
\mathbb{H}\triangleleft\mathbb{G}
is a normal, closed, dilation-invariant subgroup of a Carnot group, then the quotient
\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
is itself a Carnot group (see
Remark

6.6
, corresponding to setting
\mathbb{H}=\{e\}
in the next statement).

Proposition 6.15

(Isometric actions on Carnot homogeneous spaces)
.

Let
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
be a Carnot homogeneous space, and let
{\tilde{\mathbb{H}}}<\mathbb{G}
be a closed, dilation-invariant subgroup with
\mathbb{H}\triangleleft{\tilde{\mathbb{H}}}
. Then the left multiplication of
{\tilde{\mathbb{H}}}
on
\mathbb{G}
induces a proper and free action of
R:={\tilde{\mathbb{H}}}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
on
\mathbb{M}
by metric measure isometries. The quotient by this action, equipped with the quotient metric, is smoothly isometric to the Carnot homogeneous space
\tilde{\mathbb{M}}=\mathbb{G}/\penalty{\tilde{\mathbb{H}}}
, in other words

(172)

\mathbb{M}/\penalty R=\left(\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}\right)/\penalty\left({\tilde{\mathbb{H}}}/\penalty\mathbb{H}\right)=\mathbb{G}/\penalty{\tilde{\mathbb{H}}}=\tilde{\mathbb{M}}

as metric spaces. We denote by
\pi:\mathbb{M}\to\tilde{\mathbb{M}}
the corresponding quotient map.

Proof.

The action of the group
R={\tilde{\mathbb{H}}}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
, denoted by
\alpha:R\times\mathbb{M}\to\mathbb{M}
, is defined by

(173)

\alpha(\mathbb{H}\tilde{h},\mathbb{H}g):=\mathbb{H}\tilde{h}g.

This action is well-defined because
\mathbb{H}\triangleleft{\tilde{\mathbb{H}}}
. It is immediate to verify that this action is left, smooth, proper, and free. For
\tilde{h}\in{\tilde{\mathbb{H}}}
, denote by
\phi=\alpha(\mathbb{H}\tilde{h},\cdot):\mathbb{M}\to\mathbb{M}
. Letting
\Phi=L_{\tilde{h}}:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{G}
be the left multiplication by
\tilde{h}
(which is an isometry of
\mathbb{G}
), we see that the diagram (
124
) commutes, so that
\phi
is an isometry by
Definition

6.8
.

Denote by
(\mathbb{M}^{*},\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{M}^{*}})
the metric quotient of
(\mathbb{M},\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{M}})
by the isometric action of
R
(in the sense of
Section

2.2
). It is elementary to verify that the map sending the
R-
orbit of
\mathbb{H}g\in\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{G}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
to
{\tilde{\mathbb{H}}}g\in\tilde{\mathbb{M}}=\mathbb{G}/\penalty{\tilde{\mathbb{H}}}
is a (smooth) metric isometry between
(\mathbb{M}^{*},\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{M}^{*}})
and
(\tilde{\mathbb{M}},\mathsf{d}_{\tilde{\mathbb{M}}})
.
∎

\mathsf{p}_{i+1}:\mathbb{M}_{i}/\Gamma_{i}\to\mathbb{M}_{i+1}
is the quotient of the metric measure space
\mathbb{M}_{i}/\Gamma_{i}
by a connected, abelian, compact Lie group acting freely by metric measure isometries on
\mathbb{M}_{i}/\Gamma_{i}
. It follows from
Theorem

5.3
that
\mathbb{M}_{i+1}
satisfies the
\mathrm{MCP}_{\mathrm{loc}}(K,N)
. By dilations, the
\mathrm{MCP}_{\mathrm{loc}}(K,N)
for
\mathbb{M}_{i+1}
is equivalent to
\mathrm{MCP}(K,N)
. Iterating this argument
s
times, we reach the conclusion.
∎

7.1.
Failure of the measure contraction property

We postpone to
Section

8
the proof that the Martinet structure does not satisfy the
\mathrm{MCP}(K,N)
for any
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,\infty)
(see
Theorem

8.6
). Taking this result for granted, we apply the machinery developed so far to prove the failure of the
\mathrm{MCP}
for Carnot groups admitting a quotient to the Martinet structure.

Theorem 7.2

(No
\mathrm{MCP}
with Martinet quotients)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Carnot group of step
s\geq 3
that admits a quotient to the Martinet structure (i.e., such that it satisfies one of the equivalent conditions of
Theorem

6.13
). Assume that the minimizing Sard property holds for all Carnot groups of step
\leq s
. Then
\mathbb{G}
does not satisfy the
\mathrm{MCP}(K,N)
for any
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,\infty)
.

Proof.

By
Theorem

6.13
,
\mathbb{G}
admits a quotient to
\mathbb{Eng}
. By
Remark

6.17
, the validity of the minimizing Sard property for Carnot groups of step
\leq s
allows to apply
Theorem

7.1
. We deduce that
\mathbb{Eng}
satisfies the
\mathrm{MCP}(K,N)
. Since
\mathbb{Eng}
admits a quotient onto
\mathbb{Mar}
, another application of
Theorem

7.1
implies that
\mathbb{Mar}
also satisfies the
\mathrm{MCP}(K,N)
. Note that for the quotient
\mathbb{Eng}\to\mathbb{Mar}
, as explained in
Remark

6.18
, the list of factors in
Theorem

6.16
is
\mathbb{M}_{0}=\mathbb{M}_{1}=\mathbb{Eng}
,
\mathbb{M}_{2}=\mathbb{M}_{3}=\mathbb{Mar}
, and all of them satisfy the minimizing Sard property, which again allows to apply
Theorem

7.1
. This contradicts the fact that the Martinet structure does not satisfy any
\mathrm{MCP}(K,N)
, which will be proven in
Theorem

8.6
.
∎

The minimizing Sard property stipulates that for any initial point
x
of a sub-Riemannian manifold, the set of final points of abnormal geodesics starting from
x
has zero measure. It is one of the main open problems in sub-Riemannian geometry, see
[
Agr14
, Prob. 3]
,
[
RT05
, Conj. 1]
. However, it holds in several cases, which we outline now. Firstly, it is known to be true for all Carnot groups of step
\leq 3
. This is well-known for step
\leq 2
, while the step
3
case is shown in
[
LMO+16
]
. Secondly, recall that
filiform
Carnot groups are those whose Lie algebra satisfies

(216)

\dim\mathfrak{g}_{1}=2,\qquad\dim\mathfrak{g}_{2}=\dots=\dim\mathfrak{g}_{s}=1.

In
[
BNV22
]
, it is proven that filiform Carnot groups satisfy the minimizing Sard property. Finally, in
[
BV20
]
, the minimizing Sard property is proven for Carnot groups of rank 2 and step 4. It is easy to check that these structures always admit a quotient to the Martinet structure. Thus, we obtain the following unconditional results.

Corollary 7.3

(Examples of Carnot groups failing the
\mathrm{MCP}
)
.

The following Carnot groups do not satisfy the
\mathrm{MCP}(K,N)
for all
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,\infty)
:

•

Carnot groups of step
3
that admit a quotient to the Martinet structure (in particular, the Engel group and all the free Carnot groups of step
3
);

•

Filiform Carnot groups of step
s\geq 3
;

•

Carnot groups of step
4
and rank
2
.

7.2.
Validity of the measure contraction property

Yet, many Carnot groups of step
\geq 3
do satisfy the
\mathrm{MCP}
. In fact, we recall the following result, which is the state of the art regarding the validity of the
\mathrm{MCP}
on Carnot groups.

Theorem 7.4

(Badreddine, Rifford
[
BR20a
]
)
.

Any Carnot group whose distance function
\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{G}}:\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R}
is locally Lipschitz in charts outside of the diagonal satisfies the
\mathrm{MCP}(K,N)
for all
K\leq 0
and some
N\in[1,\infty)
.

Goh-Legendre geodesics
are a class of abnormal geodesics satisfying second-order necessary conditions for minimality, see
Section

A.1
. If a sub-Riemannian structure admits no non-trivial Goh-Legendre abnormal geodesics, then all abnormal geodesics have infinite index
[
ABB20
, Thm. 12.12]
, and this implies that the corresponding distance function is locally Lipschitz in charts outside of the diagonal, see
[
ABB20
, Thm. 12.11]
or
[
AL09
, Thm. 5.7]
. We have the following.

Corollary 7.5
.

Any Carnot group with no non-trivial Goh-Legendre geodesics satisfies the
\mathrm{MCP}(K,N)
for all
K\leq 0
and some
N\in[1,\infty)
.

8.
Failure of the measure contraction property for the Martinet structure

The purpose of this section is to disprove the
\mathrm{MCP}
in the Martinet structure. We refer to
Appendix

A
for basic notions in sub-Riemannian geometry and in particular their geodesics.

The Martinet structure was defined as a Carnot homogeneous space in
Definition

6.10
as the quotient
\pi:\mathbb{Eng}\to\mathbb{Mar}=\mathbb{Eng}/\penalty\mathbb{H}
of the Engel group by the subgroup
\mathbb{H}=\exp(\mathfrak{e}_{2})
. Let
X_{1},X_{2},Y,Z
be the basis given in
Definition

6.10
, and consider the map
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{Mar}

(218)

\phi(x_{1},x_{2},z):=\mathbb{H}\exp(zZ)\exp(x_{2}X_{2})\exp(x_{1}X_{1}),

where
\exp:\mathfrak{e}\to\mathbb{Eng}
is the group exponential map. By
[
CG90
, Thm. 1.2.12]
the map
\phi
is an analytic diffeomorphism giving global coordinates on
\mathbb{Mar}
and such that, in these coordinates, the Lebesgue measure is right-invariant by the action of
\mathbb{Eng}
on
\mathbb{Mar}
. Via routine computations, in these coordinates, the sub-Riemannian structure on
\mathbb{Mar}
is generated by the vector fields

(219)

\pi_{*}X_{1}=\partial_{x_{1}},\qquad\pi_{*}X_{2}:=\partial_{x_{2}}+\frac{x_{1}^{2}}{2}\partial_{z},

In order to stay consistent with
[
ABCK97
]
, which we will use in the following, we relabel
x_{1}=y
and
x_{2}=x
. With this relabeling the Hamiltonian of this sub-Riemannian structure on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is

(220)

H(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(u+\frac{y^{2}}{2}w\right)^{2}+v^{2}\right],\quad\text{where}\quad\lambda=\left(x,y,z,u\mathrm{d}x+v\mathrm{d}y+w\mathrm{d}z\right)\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Solutions
\lambda:[0,1]\to T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{3}
to the Hamilton’s equation are called extremal and (normal) geodesics are projections
\pi:T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
of extremals. The corresponding Hamilton’s equations are

(221)

\displaystyle\dot{x}

\displaystyle=u+\frac{y^{2}}{2}w,

\displaystyle\dot{u}

\displaystyle=0,

(222)

\displaystyle\dot{y}

\displaystyle=v,

\displaystyle\dot{v}

\displaystyle=-\left(u+\frac{y^{2}}{2}w\right)yw,

(223)

\displaystyle\dot{z}

\displaystyle=\left(u+\frac{y^{2}}{2}w\right)\frac{y^{2}}{2},

\displaystyle\dot{w}

\displaystyle=0.

We will only need to consider geodesics starting from the origin and thus always set the initial conditions
x_{0}=y_{0}=z_{0}=0
. As usual, we must have
(u_{0},v_{0})\neq 0
in order to have a non-trivial curve and we can introduce cylindrical coordinates on each fibre of the cotangent bundle by setting
r>0
, and
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
the unique parameters such that

(224)

u_{0}=r\cos(\theta),\qquad v_{0}=r\sin(\theta).

We observe that
u(t)=u_{0}
and
w(t)=w_{0}
are constant functions and that
y(t)
satisfies the second-order differential equation

(225)

\ddot{y}+u_{0}w_{0}y+\frac{w_{0}^{2}}{2}y^{3}=0,\qquad y(0)=0,\qquad\dot{y}(0)=v_{0}.

Equation (
225
) can be solved by means of Jacobi elliptic functions. We refer to
[
AS64
, Ch. 16 and 17]
for notation and conventions we use throughout. Set
y(t)=A\JacobiCN{\omega t+\phi}{m}
. We explain how the parameters
A,\omega,\phi
and
m
are found. Putting the ansatz into (
225
) gives

(226)

(1-2m)\omega^{2}=u_{0}w_{0},\qquad A^{2}w_{0}^{2}=4m\omega^{2}.

From the initial conditions
A\JacobiCN(\phi\mid m)=0
and
-A\omega\JacobiSN(\phi\mid m)\JacobiDN(\phi\mid m)=v_{0}
, we deduce that
m\in[0,1)
,
\phi=\JacobiK(m)
and
-A\omega\sqrt{1-m}=v_{0}
, or
\phi=3\JacobiK(m)
and
A\omega\sqrt{1-m}=v_{0}
. With a simple algebraic simplification, we obtain

(227)

m=\frac{r-\mathrm{sgn}(w_{0})u_{0}}{2r}=\frac{1-\mathrm{sgn}(w_{0})\cos(\theta)}{2},\quad\omega^{2}=r|w_{0}|,\quad A^{2}\omega^{2}=4mr^{2}.

To make the correspondence between the variables
(r,\theta,w_{0})\mapsto(A,\omega,m)
given by (
227
) one-to-one and smooth, we restrict it to the range:

(228)

(0,+\infty)\times(\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus\{(\pm 1,0)\})\times(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})\to(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})^{2}\times(0,1)

and choose
\phi=\JacobiK(m)
,
\mathrm{sgn}(\omega)=\mathrm{sgn}(w_{0})
and
\mathrm{sgn}(A\omega)=-\mathrm{sgn}(v_{0})
.

Once we have found an equation for
y(t)
, all the other unknown functions can be derived by integration. Indeed, it is shown in
[
ABCK97
, Section 4.5]
that

(229)

\displaystyle v(t)

\displaystyle=-A\omega\JacobiSN{\omega t+\phi}{m}\JacobiDN{\omega t+\phi}{m},

(230)

\displaystyle x(t)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{sgn}(w_{0})\left(-rt+\frac{2r}{\omega}\Big(\JacobiE(\omega t+\phi\mid m)-\JacobiE(\phi\mid m)\Big)\right),

(231)

\displaystyle z(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3w_{0}}\left(r^{2}t-u_{0}x(t)-y(t)v(t)\right).

The geodesic flow is an analytic function of the initial data, and so it is enough to take the limit
w_{0}\to 0
(resp.
v_{0}\to 0
) in the above equations to obtain the cases
w_{0}=0
(resp.
v_{0}=0
).

Remark 8.1
.

How the extremal
\lambda(t)
behave at the limit
v_{0}\to 0
can be different depending on the sign of
u_{0}
and
w_{0}
. Indeed, if the signs of
u_{0}
and
w_{0}
are such that
v_{0}\to 0
corresponds to taking
m\to 0
, then the elliptic functions reduce to standard trigonometric functions. If, however, they are such that
v_{0}\to 0
implies
m\to 1
, hyperbolic functions appear instead. This is the origin of the asymmetric cusp in Martinet spheres, see
[
ABB20
, Fig. 13.18, 13.19]
.

Definition 8.2
.

For
t\in[0,1]

\exp_{0}^{t}:=\exp_{0}(t\cdot):T_{0}^{*}\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be the sub-Riemannian exponential map at time

\mathcal{J}^{t}

(232)

[

.

\lambda_{0}\in T^{*}_{0}\mathbb{R}^{3}

\omega\in(0,2\EllipticK(m)]
, then
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
defined by
\exp_{0}(t\lambda_{0})
is a geodesic,
\lambda_{0}
is not a critical point of
\exp_{0}
, and
\exp_{0}(\lambda_{0})\notin\mathrm{Cut}(0)
. Then, there is a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
\lambda_{0}
such that
\exp_{0}:\mathcal{U}\to\exp_{0}(\mathcal{U})
is a diffeomorphism, and such that for all
\lambda_{0}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}
, the geodesic
\gamma(t):=\exp_{0}(t\lambda_{0}^{\prime})
is the unique geodesic joining
0
with
\exp_{0}(\lambda_{0}^{\prime})
and
\exp_{0}(\lambda_{0}^{\prime})
is not conjugate to
0
along
\gamma
. Assume that
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small such that
B_{\varepsilon}(\exp_{0}(\lambda_{0}))\subseteq\exp_{0}(\mathcal{U})
and let
A_{\varepsilon}
be such that
\exp_{0}(A_{\varepsilon})=B_{\varepsilon}(\exp_{0}(\lambda_{0}))
. In particular by the
\mathrm{MCP}(0,N)
, we have that for all
t\in[0,1]

(241)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\frac{\mathcal{J}^{t}(\lambda_{0})}{\mathcal{J}^{1}(\lambda_{0})}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\int_{A_{\varepsilon}}\left.\left(\left(\exp_{0}^{t}\right)^{*}\mathrm{d}x\wedge\mathrm{d}y\wedge\mathrm{d}z\right)\right|_{\lambda_{0}}}{\int_{A_{\varepsilon}}\left.\left(\left(\exp_{0}^{1}\right)^{*}\mathrm{d}x\wedge\mathrm{d}y\wedge\mathrm{d}z\right)\right|_{\lambda_{0}}}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\mathscr{L}(Z_{t}(0,B_{\varepsilon}(\exp_{0}(\lambda_{0}))))}{\mathscr{L}(B_{\varepsilon}(\exp_{0}(\lambda_{0})))}\geq t^{N},

where
\mathscr{L}
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
T_{0}^{*}\mathbb{R}^{3}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{3}
. From the expression of
\mathcal{J}^{t}
we deduce

(242)

|\mathcal{J}_{R}(\omega t,m)|\geq t^{N-1}|\mathcal{J}_{R}(\omega,m)|,

for all
t\in[0,1]
, all
m\in(0,1)
and all
\omega\in(0,2\EllipticK(m)]
. By the monotonicity, (
242
) is equivalent to

(243)

\log|\mathcal{J}_{R}(\omega t,m)|-\log|\mathcal{J}_{R}(\omega,m)|\geq(N-1)\log t.

This last inequality can be rewritten using the fundamental theorem of calculus as

(244)

\int^{\omega}_{\omega t}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\log|\mathcal{J}_{R}(z,m)|\mathrm{d}z\leq(N-1)\int^{\omega}_{\omega t}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\log z\,\mathrm{d}z.

Since this inequality has to hold for all
\omega\in(0,2\EllipticK(m)]
and all
t\in[0,1]
, it is equivalent to the same inequality on the integrands:

(245)

\frac{\partial_{\omega}\mathcal{J}_{R}(\omega,m)}{\mathcal{J}_{R}(\omega,m)}\leq(N-1)\frac{1}{\omega},

for all
m\in(0,1)
and
\omega\in(0,2\EllipticK(m)]
.
∎

The failure of the measure contraction property can finally be obtained by showing that the differential inequality (
240
) does not hold in the Martinet structure.

Theorem 8.6

(Failure of the
\mathrm{MCP}
for the Martinet structure)
.

The Martinet structure does not satisfy the
\mathrm{MCP}(K,N)
for any
K\in\mathbb{R}
and any
N\in[1,\infty)
.

Proof.

Since the Martinet structure admits dilations, it is enough to disprove
\mathrm{MCP}(0,N)
. We show that we get a contradiction by setting
\omega=2\EllipticK(m)
in (
8.5
) and sending
m\to 1
. Using the special values of the Jacobi elliptic functions
[
AS64
, 16.8]
we obtain for
m\in(0,1)
,

(246)

\mathcal{J}_{R}(2\EllipticK(m),m)=-4\left[\EllipticE(m)^{2}+(m-1)\EllipticK(m)\big(2\EllipticE(m)-\EllipticK(m)\big)\right].

Furthermore with the help of
[
AS64
, 16.16]
we get

(247)

\partial_{\omega}\mathcal{J}_{R}(2\EllipticK(m),m)=2\Big[(1-2m)\EllipticE(m)+(m-1)\EllipticK(m)\Big].

Using
[
AS64
, 17.3.26 and 17.3.27]
we have

(248)

\lim_{m\to 1}\EllipticK(m)=+\infty,\quad\lim_{m\to 1}\EllipticE(m)=1,\quad\lim_{m\to 1}(m-1)\EllipticK(m)=\lim_{m\to 1}(m-1)\EllipticK(m)^{2}=0.

Therefore, we obtain

(249)

\lim_{m\to 1}1+2\EllipticK(m)\frac{\partial_{\omega}\mathcal{J}_{R}(2\EllipticK(m),m)}{\mathcal{J}_{R}(2\EllipticK(m),m)}=1+2(+\infty)\frac{(-2)}{(-4)}=+\infty,

implying by
Proposition

8.5
that the Martinet structure does not satisfy the
\mathrm{MCP}(0,N)
.
∎

Appendix A
Sub-Riemannian geometry

We collect here basic facts and notations in sub-Riemannian geometry used in this paper. For a comprehensive introduction, we refer to
[
ABB20
,
Rif14
,
Mon02
]
.

•

A sub-Riemannian structure on a smooth, connected
n
-dimensional manifold
M
, where
n\geq 2
, is defined by a set of
L\geq 2
global smooth vector fields
X_{1},\ldots,X_{L}\in\mathrm{Vec}(M)
, called a
generating family
. We assume the
bracket-generating
condition, i.e., the vector fields
X_{1},\ldots,X_{L}
and their iterated Lie brackets at
x
generate the tangent space
T_{x}M
, for all
x\in M
.

•

The (generalized)
distribution
is the disjoint union
\mathscr{D}=\bigsqcup_{x\in M}\mathscr{D}_{x}
, where

(250)

\mathscr{D}_{x}:=\mathrm{span}\{X_{1}(x),\ldots,X_{L}(x)\}\subseteq T_{x}M,\qquad\forall\,x\in M.

Notice that
\mathscr{D}
is a vector bundle if and only if
\dim\mathscr{D}_{x}
, called the
rank at
x
, does not depend on
x
. In this case we say that the sub-Riemannian structure has constant rank.

•

The generating family induces an inner product
g_{x}
on
\mathscr{D}_{x}
given by:

(251)

g_{x}(v,v):=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{L}u_{i}^{2}\,\Big|\,v=\sum_{i=1}^{L}u_{i}X_{i}(x)\right\},\qquad\forall\,v\in\mathscr{D}_{x}.

•

For
i\geq 1
, we define the
iterated distributions

\mathscr{D}^{i}=\bigsqcup_{x\in M}\mathscr{D}^{i}_{x}
, where:

(252)

\mathscr{D}^{i}_{x}:=\mathrm{span}\{[X_{i_{1}},[\ldots,[X_{i_{j-1}},X_{i_{j}}]]\mid i_{k}\in\{1,\ldots,L\},\;j\leq i\}.

The
step
of the distribution at
x
is the minimal
s=s(x)
such that
\mathscr{D}^{s}_{x}=T_{x}M
. The distribution is
equiregular
at
x
if
\mathscr{D}^{i}_{x}
is locally constant in a neighborhood of
x
, for all
j\geq 1
. We also say that
x
is a
regular point
.

•

A
horizontal curve
is an absolutely continuous (in charts) map
\gamma:[0,1]\to M
such that there exists
u\in L^{2}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{L})
, called
control
, satisfying

(253)

\dot{\gamma}_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{L}u_{i}(t)X_{i}(\gamma_{t}),\qquad\mathrm{a.e.}\;t\in[0,1].

The class of horizontal curves depends on the family
\mathscr{F}=\{X_{1},\dots,X_{L}\}
only through the
C^{\infty}(M)
-module of vector fields generated by
\mathscr{F}
.

•

For any control
u\in L^{2}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{L})
, let
X_{u(t)}:=\sum_{i=1}^{L}u_{i}(t)X_{i}
be a time-dependent vector field. Denote by
P_{t_{0},t_{1}}^{u}:M\to M
the flow of
X_{u}
with initial datum at time
t_{0}
and final time
t_{1}
, provided that it is well-defined. Note that it holds

(254)

P_{t_{0},t_{0}}^{u}=\mathrm{id}_{M},\qquad P_{t_{0},t_{1}}^{u}=P_{t_{1},t_{2}}^{u}\circ P_{t_{0},t_{1}}^{u}

as maps on
M
, for all
t_{0},t_{1},t_{2}
such that the flows exist. Note that if
\gamma
is a horizontal curve with control
u
, and
\gamma_{0}=x
, then
\gamma_{t}=P_{0,t}^{u}(x)
.

•

We define the
length
of a horizontal curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to M
as follows:

(255)

\ell(\gamma):=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{g(\dot{\gamma}_{t},\dot{\gamma}_{t})}\,\mathrm{d}t.

The length
\ell
is invariant by suitable reparametrizations. Every horizontal curve is the reparametrization of one with constant speed.

•

The
sub-Riemannian (or Carnot-Carathéodory) distance
is defined by:

(256)

\mathsf{d}(x,y)=\inf\{\ell(\gamma)\mid\gamma_{0}=x,\,\gamma_{1}=y,\quad\gamma\text{ is horizontal}\}.

The bracket-generating condition implies that
\mathsf{d}
is finite and continuous. If
(M,\mathsf{d})
is complete as metric space, then for any
x,y\in M
the infimum in (
256
) is attained.

•

On the space of horizontal curves with fixed endpoints, and parametrized with constant speed, the minimizers of the length functional coincide with the minimizers of the
energy functional

(257)

J(\gamma):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}g(\dot{\gamma}(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t.

•

The
end-point map
of the family
\mathscr{F}=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{L}\}
and with base point
x
is the Fréchet-smooth map
\End^{\mathscr{F}}_{x}:\mathcal{U}\to M
, sending
u
to the point a time
t=1
of the solution of

(258)

\dot{\gamma}_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{L}u_{i}(t)X_{i}(\gamma_{t}),\qquad\gamma_{0}=x,

for every
u\in\mathcal{U}\subseteq L^{2}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{L})
for which the solution is defined on
[0,1]
.

•

Sub-Riemannian geodesics
are horizontal curves associated with
minimizing controls
, namely the ones that solve the constrained minimum problem

(259)

\min\{J(u)\mid u\in\mathcal{U},\quad\End^{\mathscr{F}}_{x}(u)=y\},\qquad x,y\in M.

They are the curves
\gamma:[0,1]\to M
such that
\mathsf{d}(\gamma_{t},\gamma_{s})=|t-s|\mathsf{d}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})
for all
t,s\in[0,1]
.

•

If
u
is a minimizing control with
\End^{\mathscr{F}}_{x}(u)=y
, then there exists a non-trivial pair
(\lambda_{1},\nu)\in T_{y}^{*}M\times\{0,1\}
, called
Lagrange multiplier
, such that

(260)

\lambda_{1}\circ D_{u}\End^{\mathscr{F}}_{x}(v)=\nu(u,v)_{L^{2}},\qquad\forall v\in T_{u}\mathcal{U}\simeq L^{2}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{L}),

where
\circ
denotes the composition of linear maps,
D
the (Fréchet) differential. Non-trivial means that
(\lambda_{1},\nu)\neq(0,0)
.

•

The multiplier
(\lambda_{1},\nu)
and the associated geodesic
\gamma
are called
normal
if
\nu=1
and
abnormal
if
\nu=0
(we use the same terminology for the covector
\lambda_{1}
). A minimizing control
u
may admit different multipliers so that
\gamma
might be both normal
and
abnormal. In particular we observe that
\gamma
is abnormal if and only if
u
is a critical point of
\End^{\mathscr{F}}_{x}
.

•

To any multiplier
(\lambda_{1},\nu)
we associate an
extremal
, namely a curve
\lambda:[0,1]\to T^{*}M
, given by

(261)

\lambda_{t}:=(P_{t,1}^{u})^{*}\lambda_{1},\qquad\forall\,t\in[0,1].

The covector
\lambda_{0}
is called the
initial covector
of the extremal.

•

If
\gamma:[0,1]\to M
is a normal geodesic with minimizing control
u
, and
\lambda_{1}\in T_{\gamma_{1}}^{*}M
is a normal multiplier, then the corresponding extremal lift
\lambda:[0,1]\to T^{*}M
as defined in (
261
) satisfies the differential equation

(262)

\dot{\lambda}_{t}=\vec{H}(\lambda_{t}),

where
H:T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}
is the
sub-Riemannian Hamiltonian
:

(263)

H(\lambda):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{L}\langle\lambda,X_{i}\rangle^{2},\qquad\forall\,\lambda\in T^{*}M,

and
\vec{H}
denotes the corresponding Hamiltonian vector field.

•

If
\gamma:[0,1]\to M
is an abnormal geodesic with minimizing control
u
, and
\lambda_{1}\in T_{\gamma_{1}}^{*}M
is an abnormal Lagrange multiplier, then the corresponding extremal lift
\lambda:[0,1]\to T^{*}M
as defined in (
261
) satisfies the
abnormal condition

(264)

\langle\lambda(t),X_{i}\rangle=0,\qquad\forall\,i=1,\dots,L.

•

The
exponential map
at
x\in M
is the map
\exp_{x}:T_{x}^{*}M\to M
, which assigns to
\lambda_{0}\in T_{x}^{*}M
the final point
\pi(\lambda_{1})
of the solution of

(265)

\dot{\lambda}_{t}=\vec{H}(\lambda_{t}),

starting at
\lambda_{0}
. The curve
\gamma_{t}=\pi(\lambda_{t})
,
t\in[0,1]
, has control
u_{i}(t)=\langle\lambda_{t},X_{i}(\gamma_{t})\rangle
for
i=1,\dots,L
, and satisfies the normal Lagrange multiplier rule with multiplier
\lambda_{1}=e^{\vec{H}}(\lambda_{0})
.

•

Given a normal geodesic
\gamma_{t}=\exp_{x}(t\lambda_{0})
with initial covector
\lambda_{0}\in T_{x}^{*}M

we say that
y=\exp_{x}(\bar{t}\lambda)
is a
conjugate point
to
x
along
\gamma
if
\bar{t}\lambda
is a critical point for
\exp_{x}
. We call
\bar{t}
a conjugate time. Also we say that
\gamma_{s}
and
\gamma_{t}
are
conjugate
if
\gamma_{t}
is conjugate to
\gamma_{s}
along
\gamma|_{[s,t]}
.

•

A normal geodesic
\gamma:[0,1]\to M

contains no non-trivial abnormal segments
if for every
s,s^{\prime}\in[0,1]
with
0<|s-s^{\prime}|<1
, the restriction
\gamma|_{[s,s^{\prime}]}
is not abnormal.

•

If a geodesic
\gamma:[0,1]\to M
contains no non-trivial abnormal segments, then
\gamma_{s}
is not conjugate to
\gamma_{s^{\prime}}
for every
s,s^{\prime}\in[0,1]
with
0<|s-s^{\prime}|<1
.

•

We say that
y\in M
is a
smooth point
, with respect to
x\in M
, if there exists a unique geodesic joining
x
with
y
, which is not abnormal, and with non-conjugate endpoints.

•

The
cut locus

\mathrm{Cut}(x)
is the complement of the set of smooth points with respect to
x
. The
global cut locus
of
M
is

(266)

\mathrm{Cut}(M):=\{(x,y)\in M\times M\mid y\in\mathrm{Cut}(x)\}.

The set of smooth points is open and dense in
M
, and the squared sub-Riemannian distance function is smooth on
M\times M\setminus\mathrm{Cut}(M)

[
Agr09
,
RT05
]
.

•

The
abnormal set

\mathrm{Abn}(x)
is the set of points
y
such that there exists an abnormal geodesic joining
x
and
y
. It holds
\mathrm{Abn}(x)\subseteq\mathrm{Cut}(x)
.

A.1.
The Goh-Legendre condition

Consider a sub-Riemannian structure on a smooth manifold
M
generated by a family of vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{L}\in\mathrm{Vec}(M)
. Abnormal geodesics with finite index must satisfy necessary conditions for length-minimality known as Goh-Legendre conditions, see
[
ABB20
, Thms. 12.12, 3.59]
, that we recall here.

Definition A.1

(Goh-Legendre)
.

Goh-Legendre geodesics
are geodesics
\gamma:[0,1]\to M
, with control
u\in L^{2}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{L})
, such that there exists a never-vanishing extremal lift
\lambda:[0,1]\to M
such that
the following holds for almost every
t\in[0,1]
:

(abnormal condition)

\displaystyle\langle\lambda_{t},X_{v}\rangle

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\qquad\forall\,v\in\mathbb{R}^{L},

(Goh condition)

\displaystyle\langle\lambda_{t},[X_{v},X_{w}]\rangle

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\qquad\forall\,v,w\in\mathbb{R}^{L},

(generalized Legendre condition)

\displaystyle\langle\lambda_{t},[[X_{u(t)},X_{v}],X_{v}]\rangle

\displaystyle\geq 0,\qquad\qquad\forall\,v\in\mathbb{R}^{L},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the duality pairing, and
X_{v}:=\sum_{i=1}^{L}v_{i}X_{i}
for any
v\in\mathbb{R}^{L}
.

We refer to
[
ABB20
, Ch. 12]
for details, we only note here that all abnormal geodesics that are not normal must have finite index so that they must be Goh-Legendre abnormals.

We also formulate a strengthening of the Goh-Legendre condition. It was introduced in
[
AS96
, (4.16)]
in relation with rigidity properties of abnormal geodesics. We emphasize that the (strong) generalized Legendre condition should not be confused with the (strong) Legendre condition in control theory, see
[
AS04
, Ch. 20 and in particular Ch. 20.5.1]
.

Definition A.2

(Strong-Goh-Legendre)
.

A
strong
-Goh-Legendre geodesic is a Goh-Legendre geodesic such that the Legendre condition is verified in a strong sense. Namely, in the notation of
Definition

A.1
, there exists
c>0
such that for almost every
t\in[0,1]
:

(267)

\langle\lambda_{t},[[X_{u(t)},X_{v}],X_{v}]\rangle\geq c\|v\|^{2},\qquad\forall\,v\in u(t)^{\perp},\qquad\text{(strong generalized Legendre condition)}

where
u(t)^{\perp}=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{L}\mid v\cdot u(t)=0\}
, and
\|\cdot\|
denotes the Euclidean norm of
\mathbb{R}^{L}
.

A.2.
Goh-Legendre conditions on a Carnot group

We rewrite
Definition

A.1
on a Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
. We use the corresponding notation from
Section

6
. Denote by
R_{g}:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{G}
the right multiplication by
g\in\mathbb{G}
. Then, assuming without loss of generality
\gamma_{0}=e
, for all
g\in\mathbb{G}
it holds

(268)

P_{0,t}^{u}(g)=R_{\gamma_{t}}(g),\qquad\text{where}\qquad\gamma_{t}=P_{0,t}^{u}(e),

where, we recall,
P_{0,t}^{u}
denotes the flow of the non-autonomous vector field
X_{u(t)}=\sum_{i=1}^{L}u_{i}(t)X_{i}
(a curve in
\mathfrak{g}
) with initial datum at time
0
and final time at time
t
. It follows that for any extremal lift (
261
), letting
\lambda_{0}\in T_{e}^{*}\mathbb{G}\simeq\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be the corresponding initial covector, it holds

(269)

\langle\lambda_{t},V\rangle=\langle\lambda_{0},\Ad_{\gamma_{t}}(V)\rangle,\qquad\forall\,V\in\mathfrak{g},

where for
g\in\mathbb{G}
,
\Ad_{g}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
is the adjoint endomorphism. Thus, the conditions in
Definition

A.1
are equivalent to the existence of
\lambda_{0}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
such that

(abnormal condition)

\displaystyle\langle\lambda_{0},\Ad_{\gamma_{t}}(X)\rangle

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\qquad\forall\,X\in\mathfrak{g}_{1},

(Goh condition)

\displaystyle\langle\lambda_{0},\Ad_{\gamma_{t}}(Y)\rangle

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\qquad\forall\,Y\in\mathfrak{g}_{2},

(generalized Legendre condition)

\displaystyle\langle\lambda_{0},\Ad_{\gamma_{t}}([[X_{u(t)},X],X])\rangle

\displaystyle\geq 0,\qquad\qquad\forall\,X\in\mathfrak{g}_{1},

for almost every
t\in[0,1
]. Furthermore, for the flow of a non-autonomous vector field, a Volterra series argument shows that, if
\mathbb{G}
has step
s
, it holds:

(270)

\Ad_{P_{0,t}^{u}(e)}(W)=W+\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}\int_{0}^{t}\dots\int_{0}^{t_{i-1}}[X_{u(t_{i})},[\dots,[X_{u(t_{1})},W]]]\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}t_{i},\qquad\forall\,W\in\mathfrak{g}.

Note that (
270
) is the non-autonomous version of (
138
).

Elementary arguments using (
270
), show that in step
3
the above conditions are equivalent to:

(abnormal condition)

\displaystyle\langle\lambda_{0},X\rangle

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\forall\,X\in\mathfrak{g}_{1},

(Goh condition)

\displaystyle\langle\lambda_{0},Y\rangle=\langle\lambda_{0},[X_{u(t)},Y]\rangle

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\forall\,Y\in\mathfrak{g}_{2},\,a.e.\ t\in[0,1],

(generalized Legendre condition)

\displaystyle\langle\lambda_{0},[[X_{u(t)},X],X]\rangle

\displaystyle\geq 0,\qquad\forall\,X\in\mathfrak{g}_{1},\,a.e.\ t\in[0,1].

Note that the Goh condition implies that the bilinear map
\mathfrak{g}_{1}\times\mathfrak{g}_{1}\ni(V,W)\mapsto\langle\lambda_{0},[[X_{u(t)},V],W]\rangle
is a quadratic form for almost every
t\in[0,1]
.

Appendix B
Approximations of probability measures

The following is a standard construction used, in particular, to prove that if
(X,\mathsf{d})
is separable then also
(\mathcal{P}(X),W_{p})
is separable. We give a proof of the precise formulation we need.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d})
be a metric space. Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X)
. Assume that for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
there exist Borel sets
A_{1}^{m},\dots,A_{L(m)}^{m}
, with
A_{i}^{m}\subseteq X
such that as
m\to\infty
we have
L(m)\nearrow\infty
and

(1)

A_{i}^{m}\cap A_{j}^{m}=\emptyset
if
i\neq j
;

(2)

\mu\left(\cup_{i=1}^{L(m)}A_{i}^{m}\right)\geq 1-\frac{1}{m}
;

(3)

\diam\left(A_{i}^{m}\right)\leq\frac{1}{m}
for all
i=1,\dots,L(m)
.

Let
y_{i}^{m}
be a choice of a point from each
A_{i}^{m}
. Define the sequence of probability measures

(271)

\mu^{m}:=\frac{1}{\mu\left(\cup_{i=1}^{L(m)}A_{i}^{m}\right)}\sum_{i=1}^{L(m)}\mu\left(A_{i}^{m}\right)\delta_{y_{i}^{m}},\qquad\forall\,m\in\mathbb{N}.

Then
\mu^{m}\rightharpoonup\mu
as
m\to\infty
.

Proof.

Remember that
\mu^{m}\rightharpoonup\mu
if for any continuous and bounded function
g\in C_{b}(X)

(272)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{X}g(z)\mu^{m}(\mathrm{d}z)=\int_{X}g(z)\mu(\mathrm{d}z).

Since we are dealing with probability measures, it is sufficient to test the above convergence for
g\in\mathrm{Lip}_{1}(X,\mathsf{d})\cap C_{b}(X)
, see
[
Bog18
, Cor. 2.2.6]
. By our assumptions it holds

(273)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\mu\left(\cup_{i=1}^{L(m)}A_{i}^{m}\right)=1,

so that we can omit the normalization in (
271
). Let
g\in\mathrm{Lip}_{1}(X,\mathsf{d})
. The we compute

(274)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{X}g(z)\mu^{m}(\mathrm{d}z)-\int_{X}g(z)\mu(\mathrm{d}z)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{L(m)}\mu\left(A_{i}^{m}\right)g(y_{i}^{m})-\int_{X}g(z)\mu(\mathrm{d}z)\right|

(275)

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{X}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{L(m)}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}^{m}}(z)g(y_{i}^{m})-g(z)\right)\mu(\mathrm{d}z)\right|

(276)

Abstract.
In a series of papers we investigated the following question:
given a family
\mathcal{F}
of binary forms having nonzero discriminant and integer coefficients, for each
d\geqslant 3
, we estimate the number of integers
m
with
|m|\leqslant N
which are represented by an element in
\mathcal{F}
of degree
\geqslant d
. Under suitable assumptions, asymptotically as
N\to\infty
, the main term in the estimate is given by the forms in
\mathcal{F}
having degree
d
(if any), while the forms of degree
>d
contribute only to the error term.
The present text is devoted to fewnomials

a_{0}X^{kr}+a_{1}X^{k(r-1)}Y^{k}+\cdots+a_{r-1}X^{k}Y^{k(r-1)}+a_{r}Y^{kr}

with fixed
r\geqslant 1
and varying
k,a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{r}
.

: Primary 11E76; Secondary 11D45 11D85.

Key words and phrases
: Representation of integers by fewnomials, Families of Diophantine equations,
Linear forms in logarithms.

1.1
About general binary forms

Let
d\geqslant 3
be an integer. For
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{Z}},\,{\mathbb{Q}},{\mathbb{R}}
or
{\mathbb{C}}

be the set of binary forms

with degree

, with coefficients in
{\mathbb{K}}
and with discriminant different from zero. If

belongs to

\mathrm{Bin}(d,{\mathbb{K}})
and if

\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}&u_{2}\\
u_{3}&u_{4}\end{pmatrix}

(1.1)

belongs to
{\mathrm{GL}}(2,{\mathbb{K}})
, we denote by

(F\circ\gamma)(X,Y)=F(u_{1}X+u_{2}Y,u_{3}X+u_{4}Y),

after the associated linear change of variables. The form

belongs to
\mathrm{Bin}(d,{\mathbb{K}})
.
Two forms

and

–isomorphic

If

. The set of these automorphisms is a group denoted by
{\rm Aut}(F,{\mathbb{K}})

We have
-{\rm Id}\in{\rm Aut}(F,{\mathbb{K}})
if and only if
d
is even.

When
F
belongs to
{\rm Bin}(d,{\mathbb{Z}})
we denote by
C_{F}
the constant

C_{F}:=A_{F}W_{F},

(1.2)

attached to
F
. It is defined and thoroughly studied in
[
SX2019
, Theorem 1.2]

[
SX2019
, Theorem 1.1]
, the number of

in the interval

where

. The constant

is the area of the fundamental domain attached to

A_{F}:=\iint_{|F(x,y)|\leqslant 1}dx\,dy,

and

1/2&\text{ if }{\rm Aut}(F,{\mathbb{Q}})=\{\pm{\rm Id}\},\\
1/4&\text{ if }{\rm Aut}(F,{\mathbb{Q}})={\mathbf{D}}_{2},\end{cases}

Motivated by the example of forms (

) with even

4.1
Zeroes of binary forms

4.1.1
Case of one form.

In this section
{\mathbb{K}}
is the field
{\mathbb{Q}}
,
{\mathbb{R}}
or
{\mathbb{C}}
. To the element

\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}&u_{2}\\
u_{3}&u_{4}\end{pmatrix}\in{\mathrm{GL}}(2,{\mathbb{K}})

(4.1)

we associate the
homography

\tilde{\gamma}
of
\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{K}})
defined by the formula

\tilde{\gamma}(x:t)=(u_{1}x+u_{2}t:u_{3}x+u_{4}t),

where we denote by
(x:t)
the generic element of
\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{K}})
.
Recall that for
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
in
{\mathrm{GL}}(2,{\mathbb{K}})
we have the equivalence

\tilde{\gamma}_{1}=\tilde{\gamma}_{2}\iff\bigl(\text{ there exists }\lambda\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}\text{ such that }\gamma_{1}=\lambda\gamma_{2}\ \bigr)

(4.2)

and the formula

\widetilde{\gamma_{1}\,\gamma_{2}}=\tilde{\gamma_{1}}\circ\tilde{\gamma_{2}}

(4.3)

Let
F
be a form
\mathrm{Bin}(d,{\mathbb{C}})
and
{\mathcal{Z}}(F)
the set of zeroes of
F
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}})
. By definition, it is the set of classes
(x:t)

of pairs
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\smallsetminus\{(0,0)\}
modulo the homotheties such that
F(x,t)=0
. By assumption the cardinality of
{\mathcal{Z}}(F)
is
d
and for
\gamma\in{\mathrm{GL}}(2,{\mathbb{K}})
we have the conjugation equality

\mathcal{Z}(F\circ\gamma)=\tilde{\gamma}^{-1}\bigl(\mathcal{Z}(F)\bigr),

(4.4)

which follows from the relations

(x:t)\in{\mathcal{Z}}(F\circ\gamma)\iff\left(F\circ\gamma\right)(x:t)=0\iff\tilde{\gamma}(x:t)\in{\mathcal{Z}}(F)\iff(x:t)\in\tilde{\gamma}^{-1}\left({\mathcal{Z}}(F)\right).

In particular for
\gamma\in{\rm Aut}(F,{\mathbb{K}})
, we have

\mathcal{Z}(F)=\tilde{\gamma}\bigl(\mathcal{Z}(F)).

(4.5)

Thus for
\gamma\in{\rm Aut}(F,{\mathbb{K}})
the restriction of
\tilde{\gamma}
to
\mathcal{Z}(F)
, denoted by
\tilde{\gamma}_{|\mathcal{Z}(F)}
, is a bijection of
\mathcal{Z}(F)
. If
E
is a subset of
\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{C}})
,
let
{\rm Aut}(E,{\mathbb{K}})
be the set of bijections
\phi:E\rightarrow E
such that there exists
\gamma\in{\mathrm{GL}}(2,{\mathbb{K}})

with the property
\tilde{\gamma}_{|E}=\phi
. In particular for
\gamma\in{\mathrm{GL}}(2,{\mathbb{K}})
one has the equality

{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{Z}(F\circ\gamma),{\mathbb{K}})=(\tilde{\gamma}_{|\mathcal{Z}(F)})^{-1}{\rm Aut}\left(\mathcal{Z}(F),{\mathbb{K}}\right)\tilde{\gamma}_{|\mathcal{Z}(F)}.

We have

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
F\in\mathrm{Bin}(d,{\mathbb{K}})
(
d\geqslant 3
). The
\tilde{}
–map
which transforms
\gamma\in{\mathrm{GL}}(2,{\mathbb{K}})
in the homography
\tilde{\gamma}
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}})
induces a homomorphism

\begin{matrix}\Psi&:&{\rm Aut}(F,{\mathbb{K}})&\longrightarrow&{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{Z}(F),{\mathbb{K}})\\
&&\gamma&\mapsto&\Psi(\gamma)=\tilde{\gamma}_{|\mathcal{Z}(F)}.\end{matrix}

We have

\ker\Psi=\{\zeta\,{\rm Id}:\zeta\in{\mathbb{K}},\ \zeta^{d}=1\}.

Finally when
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{C}}
the map
\Psi
is surjective.

Proof.

The existence and unicitiy of
\Psi
follows from (
4.5
).
As a consequence of (
4.3
), for any
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}\in{\rm Aut}(F,{\mathbb{K}})
, we have
\Psi(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})=\Psi(\gamma_{1})\circ\Psi(\gamma_{2})
. The determination of
\ker\Psi
comes down to finding the matrices

\gamma\in{\rm Aut}(F,{\mathbb{K}})
such that
\tilde{\gamma}
fixes every point of
\mathcal{Z}(F)
. Since
\mathcal{Z}(F)
contains
d
points and since
d\geqslant 3
, we deduce the equality
\tilde{\gamma}={\rm Id}
. By (
4.2
), the automorphism
\gamma
is a homothety of the shape
\zeta\,{\rm Id}
, with
\zeta\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
. The equality
F\circ\gamma=\zeta^{d}F
restricts the possible values of
\zeta
by the equality
\zeta^{d}=1
.

We now suppose that
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{C}}
to prove that
\Psi
is surjective.
So let
F\in\mathrm{Bin}(d,{\mathbb{C}})
and let
\xi\in{\mathrm{GL}}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
such that
\tilde{\xi}_{|{\mathcal{Z}}(F)}
belongs to
{\rm Aut}({\mathcal{Z}}(F),{\mathbb{C}})
. We want to prove the existence of some
\gamma\in{\rm Aut}(F,{\mathbb{C}})
such that
\tilde{\gamma}=\xi
. By (
4.4
), we have the equality
{\mathcal{Z}}(F)={\mathcal{Z}}(F\circ\xi)
.
This implies that the forms
F
and

are proportional since they have the same zeroes with
the same multiplicities. For some
\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
we have
F\circ\xi=\alpha F
. It remains to put
\gamma:=\lambda\xi
, where
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
satisfies
\lambda^{-d}=\alpha
to obtain the equalities

\tilde{\gamma}=\tilde{\xi}
and
F\circ\gamma=F
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

When

or

We factorize each
A_{\ell}
as

A_{\ell}=(d-\lambda+1)\,!\cdot\frac{(d-\lambda^{\prime})\,!}{d\,!}A_{\ell}^{*},

with

A_{\ell}^{*}:=\prod_{i=2}^{\ell}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}+i-\nu}{(i-\nu)^{+}},

(6.15)

with the convention
x^{+}=\max(x,\,1)
.

By homogeneity, the equality (
6.14
) is equivalent to

(A_{\ell_{0}+1}^{*2}-A_{\ell_{0}}^{*}A_{\ell_{0}+2}^{*})(A_{\ell_{0}+1}^{*}+A_{\ell_{0}+3}^{*}-2A_{\ell_{0}+2}^{*})=(A_{\ell_{0}+2}^{*2}-A_{\ell_{0}+1}^{*}A_{\ell_{0}+3}^{*})(A_{\ell_{0}}^{*}+A_{\ell_{0}+2}^{*}-2A_{\ell_{0}+1}^{*}).

(6.16)

Actually, the equality (
6.16
) cannot hold. This is the purpose of the following lemma.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
d
,
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
be positive integers such that
d\leqslant\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}\leqslant d+2
and
\ell_{0}\in\{1,2\}
. Let
A_{\ell}^{*}

(1\leqslant\ell\leqslant 5)
be defined by (
6.15
) with
\nu=\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}-d
. Define

Q:=\frac{(A^{*}_{\ell_{0}}+A^{*}_{\ell_{0}+2}-2A^{*}_{\ell_{0}+1})(A_{\ell_{0}+2}^{*2}-A^{*}_{\ell_{0}+1}A^{*}_{\ell_{0}+3})}{(A^{*}_{\ell_{0}+1}+A^{*}_{\ell_{0}+3}-2A^{*}_{\ell_{0}+2})(A_{\ell_{0}+1}^{*2}-A^{*}_{\ell_{0}}A^{*}_{\ell_{0}+2})}\cdotp

(6.17)

Then have

Q=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda^{\prime}+3}{3(\lambda^{\prime}+2)}}&\text{ if }\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}=d,\\
\\
\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda^{\prime}+2}{2(\lambda^{\prime}+1)}}&\text{ if }\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}=d+1,\\
\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}(\lambda^{\prime}+2)}&\text{ if }\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}=d+2.\end{cases}

In these three cases, we have
Q\neq 1
.

Proof of Lemma
6.3
.

We first suppose that
\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}=d+2
, that is
\ell_{0}=2
and
\nu=2
. We have
the equalities

A_{1}^{*}=1,\ A_{2}^{*}=\lambda^{\prime},\ A_{3}^{*}=\lambda^{\prime}(\lambda^{\prime}+1),\ A_{4}^{*}=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}(\lambda^{\prime}+1)(\lambda^{\prime}+2)}{2},\ A_{5}^{*}=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}(\lambda^{\prime}+1)(\lambda^{\prime}+2)(\lambda^{\prime}+3)}{6}\cdotp

With these values, we obtain the equalities

A_{2}^{*}+A_{4}^{*}-2A_{3}^{*}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\lambda^{\prime 2}(\lambda^{\prime}-1),

A_{4}^{*2}-A^{*}_{3}A^{*}_{5}=\frac{1}{12}\,\lambda^{\prime 3}(\lambda^{\prime}+1)^{2}(\lambda^{\prime}+2),

A^{*}_{3}+A^{*}_{5}-2A^{*}_{4}=\frac{1}{6}\lambda^{\prime 2}(\lambda^{\prime}-1)(\lambda^{\prime}+1)

and

{A^{*}_{3}}^{2}-A^{*}_{2}A^{*}_{4}=\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{\prime 3}(\lambda^{\prime}+1).

By (
6.17
), we finally obtain

Q=\frac{(A^{*}_{2}+A^{*}_{4}-2A^{*}_{3})({A^{*}_{4}}^{2}-A^{*}_{3}A^{*}_{5})}{(A^{*}_{3}+A^{*}_{5}-2A^{*}_{4})({A^{*}_{3}}^{2}-A^{*}_{2}A^{*}_{4})}=\frac{1}{2}(\lambda^{\prime}+2).

This completes the proof of
Lemma
6.3
in that case
\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}=d+2
.

We now suppose that
d\leqslant\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}\leqslant d+1
, that is
\ell_{0}=1
and
\nu\in\{0,1\}
. In that case, we can forget the symbol
+
in the definition (
6.15
).
We have the equalities

\begin{gathered}A_{1}^{*}=1,\ A_{2}^{*}=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}+2-\nu}{2-\nu},\ A_{3}^{*}=\frac{(\lambda^{\prime}+2-\nu)(\lambda^{\prime}+3-\nu)}{(2-\nu)(3-\nu)},\ \\
A_{4}^{*}=\frac{(\lambda^{\prime}+2-\nu)(\lambda^{\prime}+3-\nu)(\lambda^{\prime}+4-\nu)}{(2-\nu)(3-\nu)(4-\nu)}\cdotp\end{gathered}

With these values, we obtain the equalities

A_{1}^{*}+A_{3}^{*}-2A_{2}^{*}=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}(\lambda^{\prime}-1)}{(2-\nu)(3-\nu)},

{A^{*}_{3}}^{2}-A^{*}_{2}A^{*}_{4}=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}(\lambda^{\prime}+2-\nu)(\lambda^{\prime}+3-\nu)}{(2-\nu)^{2}(3-\nu)^{2}(4-\nu)},

A^{*}_{2}+A^{*}_{4}-2A^{*}_{3}=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}(\lambda^{\prime}-1)(\lambda^{\prime}+2-\nu)}{(2-\nu)(3-\nu)(4-\nu)}

and

A_{2}^{*2}-A^{*}_{1}A^{*}_{3}=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}(\lambda^{\prime}+2-\nu)}{(2-\nu)^{2}(3-\nu)}\cdotp

By (
6.17
), we finally obtain

Q=\frac{(A^{*}_{1}+A^{*}_{3}-2A^{*}_{2})({A^{*}_{3}}^{2}-A^{*}_{2}A^{*}_{4})}{(A^{*}_{2}+A^{*}_{4}-2A^{*}_{3})({A^{*}_{2}}^{2}-A^{*}_{1}A^{*}_{3})}=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}+3-\nu}{(3-\nu)(\lambda^{\prime}+2-\nu)}\cdotp

Lemma
6.3
is proved also in that case.
∎

Thanks to Lemma
6.3
, this completes the proof that in Proposition
6.1
the case
q\neq 0
and
s\neq 0
is impossible.

We continue the proof of Proposition
6.1
in the other cases.

\bullet

We now prove that the case
q=0
and
s\neq 0
is impossible.

Suppose that
q=0
. The second equality of (
6.1
) implies that
\beta_{\lambda^{\prime}+1}=0
. The equality (
4.23
)
gives the vanishing of the derivative

f^{(\lambda^{\prime}+1)}(s)=0.

(6.18)

By (
6.5
) we know that

d-\lambda+1\leqslant\lambda^{\prime}+1<d,

where the last inequality comes from the assumption (
6.2
). This means that the derivative
f^{(\lambda^{\prime}+1)}(t)
is a monomial in
t
with degree
\geqslant 1
.
Combining with (
6.18
) we obtain that
s=0
. Contradiction.

\bullet

We now prove that the case
q\neq 0
and
s=0
is impossible.

We benefit from the symmetry of the question to consider the homography
\mathfrak{h}_{s,r,q}
which transforms
g
to
f
. We also take into account the symmetry of the assumptions (
6.2
) and (
6.5
) concerning these two polynomials. By the above alinea, we deduce that the case
q\neq 0
and
s=0
is also impossible.

\bullet

The remaining case is
q=s=0
.

We have
\mathfrak{h}_{0,r,0}(t)=r/t
. By Lemma
4.9

we deduce

[\mathfrak{h}_{0,r,0}(f)](z)=\frac{1}{f(0)}\cdot z^{d}f\left(\frac{r}{z}\right)=\frac{1}{\alpha_{d}}\cdot z^{d}f\left(\frac{r}{z}\right).

This relation gives the list of equalities

\alpha_{d}\beta_{j}=\alpha_{d-j}r^{j}\ (0\leqslant j\leqslant d)

after identification of the coefficients. In this relation, we fix
j=d-\lambda
. Since
\alpha_{\lambda}\neq 0
, we deduce that

\beta_{d-\lambda}\neq 0
, which implies
d-\lambda\geqslant\lambda^{\prime}
. This condition is compatible with
(
6.5
)
if and only if (
6.4
) holds. By the definition of
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
we deduce that
f
and
g
are both the sum of two or three monomials as indicated.

Recalling that
\alpha_{0}=\beta_{0}=1
, we obtain the conditions of the second item of Proposition

6.1
.
This
completes the proof of this proposition.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
and Corollary
3.9

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

Let
F
and
G
be two forms in
\cal E
and
\gamma
an homography such that
F\circ\gamma=G
. Our goal is to prove
F=G
.

Like in §
5.1
, we consider the two monic polynomials
f
and
g
associated with
F
and
G
and the homography
\mathfrak{h}=\tilde{\gamma}
and we apply Proposition
6.1
. The last assumption (
3.4
) of Theorem
3.4
imply that
\mathfrak{h}
is of the form
\mathfrak{h}_{q,0}
for some
q\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
. By assumption
\cal E
is
{\mathbb{K}}
–reduced, hence
{\mathbb{K}}
–dilation free, and therefore
F=G
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
3.9
.

Consider the first item of Corollary
3.9
. The assumption
a_{d-1}=1

\mathcal{V}^{(1)}_{d}({\mathbb{K}})

).

, the set

{\mathbb{K}}
–dilation free.
The first item now follows from Theorem

.

For the proof of the second item, we use Lemma

.

On the triple product property for subgroups of finite nilpotent groups of class 2 2
Sandeep R. Murthy

Abstract
. A number of upper bounds are proved relating to the triple product property (TPP) for subgroups of finite nilpotent groups of class 2 2 . The TPP is the property defined for three non-empty subsets S , T , U S,T,U of a group G G that the group equation s ′ ​ s − 1 ​ t ′ ​ t − 1 ​ u ′ ​ u − 1 = 1 s^{\prime}s^{-1}t^{\prime}t^{-1}u^{\prime}u^{-1}=1 , over pairs of elements s ′ , s ∈ S s^{\prime},s\in S , t ′ , t ∈ T t^{\prime},t\in T , u ′ , u ∈ U u^{\prime},u\in U , is satisfied if and only if s ′ = s s^{\prime}=s , t ′ = t t^{\prime}=t , u ′ = u u^{\prime}=u . When G G is finite, and the parameter ρ 0 ​ ( G ) \rho_{0}(G) , called subgroup TPP ratio , is defined as ρ 0 ​ ( G ) := max ⁡ | S ​ ‖ T ‖ ​ U | | G | \rho_{0}(G):=\max\frac{|S||T||U|}{|G|} , where the maximum is over the collection of all triples of subgroups S , T , U S,T,U of G G satisfying the TPP, this paper proves that (1) ρ 0 ( G ) < | G : Z ( G ) \rho_{0}(G)<\sqrt{|G:Z(G)} for (all) groups of nilpotency class 2 2 , (2) ρ 0 ​ ( G ) ≤ p \rho_{0}(G)\leq p for p p -groups with a cyclic commutator subgroup of order p p , (3) ρ 0 ​ ( G ) = 1 \rho_{0}(G)=1 for p p -groups of nilpotency class 2 2 with a ”large” centre, loosely defined as those satisfying p 2 ≤ | G : Z ⁡ ( G ) | ≤ p 3 p^{2}\leq|G:Z(G)|\leq p^{3} , (4) and ρ 0 ​ ( G ) = 1 \rho_{0}(G)=1 for p p -groups of nilpotency class 2 2 with ”small” (irreducible, complex) character degrees of 1 1 or p p .

Introduction

This paper addresses the following question(s):

If
G
is (I) a (finite) group of nilpotency class
2
, including, in particular, (II) a
p
-group with a cyclic commutator subgroup of order
p
, or (III) a
p
-group of nilpotency class
2
with a ”large” centre (
p^{2}\leq|G:Z(G)|\leq p^{3}
), or (IV) a
p
-group of nilpotency class
2
with ”small” character degrees (
\text{cd}(G)=\{1,p\}
), how large can the ratio
\rho_{0}(G):=\max\frac{|S||T||U|}{|G|}
be, where the maximum is over the collection of all triples of subgroups
S,T,U
of
G
satisfying the so-called triple product property (TPP)?

These were motivated by patterns in computationally-derived TPP data for a number of ”small” groups obtained originally by I. Hedtke and this author in 2012
[
8

, which included nilpotent groups of various nilpotency classes, as well as non-nilpotent groups. The data suggested the following propositions.

(1) Groups with abelian normal subgroups of prime index

satisfy
\rho_{0}\leq\frac{p^{2}}{2p-1}
, and, in particular,
p
-groups with abelian (maximal, normal) subgroups of index
p

\rho_{0}=1
.

(2) Groups with cyclic normal subgroups of prime index
p

\rho\leq\frac{p^{2}}{2p-1}
, where
\rho
is a generalisation of
\rho_{0}
to arbitrary subsets satisfying the TPP.

(3) Groups of nilpotency class
2
satisfy
\rho_{0}<\sqrt{|G:Z(G)|}
where

is the group centre.

(4)
p
-Groups with a cyclic commutator subgroup

of order

2
, satisfy
\rho_{0}\leq p
. This includes the extraspecial groups, which thus also satisfy
\rho_{0}\leq p
.

(5)
p
-Groups of nilpotency class
2

p^{2}\leq|G:Z(G)|\leq p^{3}
, satisfy
\rho_{0}=1
.

(6)
p
-Groups of nilpotency class
2
with small character degrees of
1
or
p
satisfy
\rho_{0}=1
.

Proposition (1) was proved in
[

, while (2) was conjectured for

[

,
4
,
5
,
6
, with sections containing a table summarising the relevant computational data, where appropriate. There is a preliminary section containing definitions, basic facts and technical results needed for the main results.

The reader can refer to the sources for definitions and characterisations of nilpotency and nilpotency classes of groups, for example, the most standard one in terms of central series, and these are understood and taken as given. The focus of this paper is groups of nilpotency class
2
, which can be characterised as nonabelian groups satisfying the condition

(1.1)

\{1\}<G^{\prime}\leq Z(G)<G.

Particularly relevant for the approach used in this paper is that in such groups the centre

is non-trivial, commutators are central, the central quotient

Bounds for the subgroup TPP ratio can then be derived by studying how the members of large subgroup TPP triples intersect with, or do not intersect with, the centre, and other key normal subgroups, such as the commutator subgroup

Applications of the TPP for the complexity of matrix multiplication, for which it was originally introduced
[

, are not directly considered in the paper, but the upper bounds derived for the subgroup TPP ratio for the various classes of groups that are presented here are very relevant for such applications, for example, by leading to lower bounds for the parameter introduced in
[

Preliminaries

A number of basic definitions, notions and technical results used in the sections of main results will be recalled or proved, starting with the TPP

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a group, finite or infinite, and
S,T,U
non-empty subsets of

|S|,|T|,|U|
respectively. The triple
(S,T,U)

(TPP) if there is an equivalence

(2.1)

s^{\prime}s^{-1}t^{\prime}t^{-1}u^{\prime}u^{-1}=1\iff s=s^{\prime},t=t^{\prime},u=u^{\prime}

for all
s,s^{\prime}\in S
,
t,t^{\prime}\in T
,
u,u^{\prime}\in U
. Then

is said to
realise
a TPP triple of
parameter type
, or simply
type
,
(|S|,|T|,|U|)
, and
|S|,|T|,|U|
are called the
parameters
of the triple and the product
|S||T||U|
the
size
of the triple. If
S,T,U
are subgroups of
G
then
(S,T,U)
is called a
subgroup TPP triple
of
G
, for which the TPP condition simplifies to

(2.2)

stu=1\iff s=t=u=1,

for all
s\in S
,
t\in T
,
u\in U
. When

is finite the TPP triple
(S,T,U)
is be said to be of
trivial
size or

size depending on whether
|S||T||U|\leq|G|
or
|S||T||U|>|G|
.

Note that the term ”subgroup TPP triple” refers only to TPP triples of subgroups, while ”TPP triple” refers to triples composed of arbitrary (non-empty) subsets satisfying the TPP. Also note the permutation and translation invariance properties of TPP triples (see
[
4
, Lemma 2.1]
and
[
10
, Observation 2.1]
) which are key in many results. As is a frequently used set-theoretic characterisation of the TPP reproduced below.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
8
, Theorem 3.1]
In a group
G
three non-empty subsets
S,T,U\subseteq G
satisfy the TPP if and only if

(2.3)

Q(S)\cap Q(T)Q(U)=Q(T)\cap Q(U)=\{1\}.

where
Q(X):=\{x^{\prime}x^{-1}\mathrel{|}x,x^{\prime}\in X\}
is called the quotient set of a non-empty subset
X\subseteq G
. If
S,T,U
are subgroups then this condition reduces to

(2.4)

S\cap TU=T\cap U=\{1\}.

A key consequence of
[
10
, Observation 2.1]
is that any TPP triple
(S^{\prime},T^{\prime},U^{\prime})
of a group
G
can be translated to a type-equivalent TPP triple
(S,T,U)
satisfying the property that

(2.5)

\displaystyle 1\in S\cap T\cap U.

Such TPP triples are called
basic
. Subgroup TPP triples are necessarily basic. All references to TPP triples shall be to basic TPP triples, unless otherwise stated.

For a group
G
the
subgroup TPP ratio
is defined as

(2.6)

\rho_{0}(G):=\frac{\beta_{0}(G)}{|G|}

where, following
[
10
,
9
]
,
\beta_{0}(G)
is the
subgroup TPP capacity of
G
defined as

(2.7)

\beta_{0}(G):=\max\;\{|S||T||U|\mid(S,T,U)\textrm{ is a subgroup TPP triple of }G\}.

.

The second, more general quantity, called
TPP ratio
, is defined as

(2.8)

\rho(G):=\frac{\beta(G)}{|G|}

where
\beta(G)
is the
TPP capacity of
G
defined as

(2.9)

\beta(G):=\max\;\{|S||T||U|\mid(S,T,U)\textrm{ is a TPP triple of }G\}.

As
G
always realises the trivial (subgroup) TPP triple
(G,\{1\},\{1\})
then
|G|\leq\beta_{0}(G)\leq\beta(G)
and
1\leq\rho_{0}(G)\leq\rho(G)
. For abelian groups there is a basic but key fact.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
4
, from the proof of Lemma 3.1]
If
G
is an abelian group then
\rho_{0}(G)=\rho(G)=1
, or, equivalently,
\beta_{0}(G)=\beta(G)=|G|
.

Proof.

If
(S,T,U)
is a TPP triple of an abelian group

then the ”triple product map”
S\times T\times U\longrightarrow G
given by
(s,t,u)\longmapsto(s,t,u)
is injective.

A further definition is useful in differentiating TPP triples by combinations of parameter type and size.

Definition 2.4
.

A TPP triple
(S,T,U)
of a group
G
is said to be
proper
if
\min\{|S|,|T|,|U|\}>1
, or
improper
if
\min\{|S|,|T|,|U|\}=1
, or
non-trivial
if
|S||T||U|>|G|
, or
trivial
if
|S||T||U|\leq|G|
, or
maximal
if
\rho(G)=\frac{|S||T||U|}{|G|}
or, in case
(S,T,U)
is a maximal subgroup TPP triple, if
\rho_{0}(G)=\frac{|S||T||U|}{|G|}
.

Obviously, the proper/improper and trivial/non-trivial distinctions between TPP triples are mutually exclusive, but there are also combinations, for example, a proper or improper trivial TPP triple, or a non-trivial or trivial maximal TPP triple. Note that a maximal TPP triple need not be unique or non-trivial, and a non-trivial TPP triple need not be unique or maximal. But an improper TPP triple is always trivial
[
4
, Proof of Lemma 3.1]
.

The notion of permutability of subsets of groups is very relevant to TPP triples, and is formally defined below.

Definition 2.5
.

Two non-empty subsets
X,Y
of a group
G
are said to
permute
(in
G
), or, equivalently, be
permutable
(in
G
), if
XY=YX
, where
XY=\{xy\mathrel{|}x\in X,y\in Y\}
and
YX=\{yx\mathrel{|}x\in X,y\in Y\}
. Clearly, this notion is symmetric, as
X
permuting with
Y
implies that
Y
permutes with
X
.

One form of permutability is when a subset normalises another: that is, for non-empty subsets
X,Y\subseteq G
if
Xy=yX
for all
y\in Y
then
Y\subseteq N_{G}(X)
, where
N_{G}(X)
is the normaliser of
X
in
G
, and
XY=\bigcup_{y\in Y}Xy=\bigcup_{y\in Y}yX=YX
. Of course, if
X
is a normal subgroup of
G
then
N_{G}(X)=G
. A stronger form of permutability in groups, which implies normalisation, is centralisation, where, using the sets
X,Y
above,
xy=yx
for all
x\in X
and
y\in Y
, in which case
Y\subseteq C_{G}(X)
, where
C_{G}(X)
is the centraliser of
X
in
G
and
C_{G}(X)\leq N_{G}(X)
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
(S,T,U)
be a non-trivial TPP triple of a group
G
, that is, one such that
|S||T||U|>|G|
. Then

(1)
No member
S,T,U
permutes with another member and its inverse, that is, for any distinct
X,Y\in\{S,T,U\}
either
XY\neq YX
or
XY^{-1}\neq Y^{-1}X
.

(2)
No member
S,T,U
is normalised by (or, in particular, centralised by) by
G
or by each other, that is,
N_{G}(X)<G
for any
X\in\{S,T,U\}
, and
Y,Z\subsetneq N_{G}(X)
for any distinct
X,Y,Z\in\{S,T,U\}
. In particular, if
(S,T,U)
is a subgroup TPP triple then
S,T,U
are all non-normal subgroups.

(3)
If
H
is the (proper) normaliser (in
G
) of any member of
\{S,T,U\}
and has index
n=|G:H|
then
|S|
,
|T|
,
|U|
satisfy

(2.10)

\frac{|S||T||U|}{|G|}\leq n\rho(H)

and, furthermore, if
H
is abelian, then

(2.11)

\frac{|S||T||U|}{|G|}\leq n.

Proof.

Let
G
and the TPP triple
(S,T,U)
be as given.

(1) Let
S
permute with
T
and
T^{-1}
. Then
Q(ST)=ST(ST)^{-1}=STT^{-1}S^{-1}=TT^{-1}SS^{-1}=Q(T)Q(S)
, where
Q(T)Q(S)\cap Q(U)=\{1\}
(by the TPP for the permuted triple
(T,S,U)
), and so by
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]

(ST,U,\{1\})
is a TPP triple of
G
such that
|S||T||U|\leq|G|
, a contradiction. Thus
S
does not permute with
T
and
T^{-1}
, and, analogously,
S
does not permute with
U
and
U^{-1}
, and
T
does not permute with
U
and
U^{-1}
.

(2) Let
S
be normalised by
G
, that is,
N_{G}(S)=G
. Then
S
permutes with
T
and
T^{-1}
, and by part (1)
|S||T||U|\leq|G|
, a contradiction. So
N_{G}(S)<G
, that is,
S
is normalised by a proper subgroup of
G
. Analogously,
T
and
U
are not normalised by
G
, and
N_{G}(T),N_{G}(U)<G
. This means that if
S,T,U
are subgroups they are all non-normal in
G
. In particular,
S
,
T
, and
U
are not centralised by
G
, or by each other.

(3) Let
H=N_{G}(S)
and
n=|G:H|
. By part (1),
N_{G}(S)<G
, and also
T,U\subsetneq H
, that is,
T
and
U
are not contained in
H
(otherwise
S
would permute with
T
and
T^{-1}
, or with
U
and
U^{-1}
, and by part (1)
|S||T||U|\leq|G|
, a contradiction). Let
G/H
be the collection of (left) cosets of
H
in
G
, and let
\overline{S},\overline{T},\overline{U}\subseteq G/H
be the
H
-supports of
S,T,U
respectively, defined as in
[
9
, Definition 3.1]
, with sizes
\sigma=|\overline{S}|
,
\tau=|\overline{T}|
,
\tau=|\overline{U}|
respectively. Now,
\sigma=1
as
S\subseteq H
, while
1<\tau,\upsilon\leq n
as
T,U\subsetneq H
. Also, let
T_{0}=T\cap H
and
U_{0}=U\cap H
. Using independent right-translation of

and

[
10

it can be supposed that
|T_{0}|\geq|T_{y}=T\cap yH|
and
|U_{0}|\geq|U_{y}=U\cap zH|
for any

and

. The sets

form partitions of

and

\displaystyle|S||T||U|

\displaystyle=|S|\sum_{yH\in\overline{T}}|T_{y}|\sum_{zH\in\overline{U}}|U_{z}|

\displaystyle\leq\tau\upsilon|S||T_{0}||U_{0}|

\displaystyle\leq n^{2}\beta(H).

Dividing both sides by

yields

\displaystyle\frac{|S||T||U|}{|G|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n^{2}\beta(H)}{|G|}

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2}\beta(H)}{n|H|}

\displaystyle=n\rho(H)

When at least two members of a TPP triple of a group are contained in a single larger subgroup the TPP ratio of the group is bounded by the TPP ratio of the subgroup, as described in the following result.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
G
be a group,
H
a subgroup, and
(S,T,U)
a TPP triple of
G
. If
S,T\subseteq H
then
\frac{|S||T||U|}{|G|}\leq\rho(H)
. Additionally, if
(S,T,U)
is maximal for
G
then
\rho(G)\leq\rho(H)
, and, additionally, if
H
is abelian, then
\rho(G)=1
. There is a subgroup version of this where ”TPP triple” is replaced by ”subgroup TPP triple”,
\rho(G)
by
\rho_{0}(G)
, and
\rho(H)
by
\rho_{0}(H)
.

Proof.

Let
G
,
H
and the TPP triple
(S,T,U)
be given as above. Let
G/H
be the collection of
n=|G:H|
left cosets of
H
in
G
, and let
\overline{U}=\{gH\in G/H\mathrel{|}U\cap gH\neq\emptyset\}
be the
H
-support of
U
. As
1\in U\cap H
then
U
is at least partly contained in
H
and
U_{0}=U\cap H
is non-empty. Then
(S,T,U_{0})
forms a TPP triple of
H
such that
\frac{|S||T||U_{0}|}{|H|}\leq\rho(H)
, and, by translation invariance
[
10
, Observation 2.1]
it may be supposed that any support element
gH\in\overline{U}
intersects with
U
in at most
|U_{0}|
elements, that is,
|U\cap gH|\leq|U_{0}|
for all
gH\in\overline{U}
. The set
\overline{U}
forms a partition of
U
over the cosets of
H
in
G
, and
|U|\leq n|U_{0}|
. Letting
U_{1}=U\backslash U_{0}
and
|U_{1}|=|U|-|U_{0}|
, so that
|U|=|U_{0}|+|U_{1}|
, where
|U_{1}|\leq(n-1)|U_{0}|
, then

\displaystyle\frac{|S||T||U|}{|G|}

\displaystyle=\frac{|S||T|(|U_{0}|+|U_{1}|)}{|G|}

\displaystyle=\frac{|S||T|(|U_{0}|+|U_{1}|)}{n|H|}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\frac{|S||T||U_{0}|}{|H|}+\frac{1}{n}\frac{|S||T||U_{1}|}{|H|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\frac{|S||T||U_{0}|}{|H|}+\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{|S||T||U_{0}|}{|H|}

\displaystyle=\frac{|S||T||U_{0}|}{|H|}

\displaystyle\leq\rho(H).

The second part of the claim follows by taking
(S,T,U)
to be maximal for
G
, that is, such that
\rho(G)=\frac{|S||T||U|}{|G|}
. From this the third part follows by taking
H
to be abelian, in which case
\rho(H)=\rho(G)=1
. The equivalent statement for subgroups follows by replacing ”TPP triple” by ”subgroup TPP triple”,
\rho(G)
by
\rho_{0}(G)
, and
\rho(H)
by
\rho_{0}(H)
.
∎

A basic fact about abelian normal subgroups in groups of nilpotency class
2
realised as direct products that will be useful.

Observation 2.9
.

If
G
is a group of nilpotency class
2
, and
H
a subgroup of
G
such that
H\cap Z(G)=\{1\}
, then
HZ(G)
is an abelian normal subgroup of

and a direct product.

Proof.

Let
G
and
H
be given as above, and let
H\cap Z(G)=\{1\}
. As
G^{\prime}\leq Z(G)
by assumption,
H^{\prime}=[H,H]\leq H\cap G^{\prime}=\{1\}
and
H
is abelian. As
Z(G)
is normal in
G
the product
HZ(G)
is a subgroup of
G
. The quotient
G/Z(G)
is abelian,
HZ(G)/Z(G)
is normal in
G/Z(G)
and, therefore,
HZ(G)
is normal in
G
(by the Correspondence Theorem between the subgroups of
G
containing
Z(G)
and the subgroups of
G/Z(G)
). Finally, as

then
HZ(G)
is an abelian direct product of order
|HZ(G)|=|H||Z(G)|
.
∎

[

Let

be a subgroup TPP triple of

The following lemma relates the subgroup TPP ratio of a quotient of a group, by some normal subgroup, to the index of its centre, and will be useful.

Lemma 2.13
.

If
G
is a group with a normal subgroup
N
such that
\rho_{0}(G/N)\leq\sqrt{|G/N|:|Z(G/N)|}
then
\rho_{0}(G/N)\leq\sqrt{|G:Z(G)|}
.

Proof.

Let
G
,
N
and
\rho_{0}(G/N)
be given as above. Note that
Z(G)N/N\leq Z(G/N)
, where
Z(G)N/N\cong Z(G)/(Z(G)\cap N)
and
|Z(G)N/N|=\frac{|Z(G)|}{|Z(G)\cap N|}
, and therefore
\frac{1}{|Z(G/N)|}\leq\frac{1}{|Z(G)N/N|}=\frac{|Z(G)\cap N|}{|Z(G)|}
. Now

\displaystyle\rho_{0}(G/N)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{|G/N|:|Z(G/N)|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{|G|}}{\sqrt{|N|}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{|Z(G/N)|}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{|G|}}{\sqrt{|N|}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{|Z(G)N/N|}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{|G|}}{\sqrt{|N|}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{|Z(G)\cap N|}}{\sqrt{|Z(G)|}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{|G|}}{\sqrt{|N|}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{|N|}}{\sqrt{|Z(G)|}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{|G:Z(G)|}.

∎

Lemma 2.16

) is true for all groups of nilpotency class
2
of order
<|G|
, where
G
is of nilpotency class
2
and
|G|>8
.

Let
(S,T,U)
be a maximal non-trivial subgroup TPP triple of
G
, that is,
\frac{|S||T||U|}{|G|}=\rho_{0}(G)>1
. Choosing any member,
S
, say, let
S_{0}=S\cap Z(G)
, where
S_{0}
is normal in
G
(it is a subgroup of central elements of
G
). There are two cases: (i)
S_{0}=\{1\}
and (ii)
S_{0}>\{1\}
, where in both cases
S_{0}<S,Z(G)
.

Consider (i),
S_{0}=\{1\}
. By Proposition
2.6
and Observation
2.9

S
is an abelian non-normal subgroup of
G
, and the product
SZ(G)
is an abelian normal subgroup of
G
. Let
H=SZ(G)T
, where
H
is normal in
G
. There are two subcases: (i.a)
H<G
and (i.b)
H=G
.

Consider (i.a),
H<G
. Letting
U_{0}=U\cap H
and
U_{1}=U\cap G\backslash H
and applying Proposition
2.7

\rho_{0}(G)=\frac{|S||T||U|}{|G|}\leq\rho_{0}(H)
. By Proposition
2.6

S
and
T
don’t centralise each other, and there is a normal series for
H
given by
\{1\}<H^{\prime}=[SZ(G)T,SZ(G)T]=[S,T]\leq G^{\prime}\leq Z(G)\leq Z(H)<H
, which yields a central series
\{1\}<H^{\prime}\leq Z(H)<H
of length
2
and shows that
H
is of nilpotency class
2
. The inductive hypothesis yields
\rho_{0}(H)<\sqrt{|H:Z(H)|}
, and the conditions
|H|<|G|
and
|Z(H)|\geq|Z(G)|
imply
\rho_{0}(G)\leq\rho_{0}(H)<\sqrt{|H:Z(H)|}\leq\sqrt{|G:Z(G)|}
.

Now consider (i.b)
H=SZ(G)T=STZ(G)=\langle S,T,Z(G)\rangle=G
. Let
N=SZ(G)\cap T
. Then
N
is normal in
G
as it is normal in
SZ(G)
and
T
, which generate
G
, and also
N\subseteq T
. Supposing
N>\{1\}
, by Lemma
2.10
, the quotient
G/N
, which is a group of order
<|G|
, realises the subgroup TPP triple
(T/N,SN/N,UN/N)
of type
(|T|/|N|,|S|,|U|)
and
\rho_{0}(G/N)\geq\rho_{0}(G)
, and by the inductive hypothesis and Lemma
2.13

\rho_{0}(G)\leq\rho_{0}(G/N)<\sqrt{|G/N|:|Z(G/N)|}\leq\sqrt{|G:Z(G)|}
. Suppose
N=\{1\}
. Then
SZ(G)T=G
is an (internal) semidirect product
SZ(G)\rtimes T
, and
G/SZ(G)\cong T
and
|S||T||Z(G)|=|G|
. Permuting
T
and
U
, which corresponds to the permuted TPP triple
(S,U,T)
, it can be shown that
G/SZ(G)\cong U
, which implies that
T\cong U
and
|T|=|U|
. By also considering the permuted triples
(T,S,U)
and
(T,U,S)
it can be shown that
|S|=|U|
, and by transitivity
|S|=|T|=|U|
. Finally, from
|G|=|S||T||Z(G)|=|S|^{2}|Z(G)|
it follows that
|S|=|T|=|U|=\sqrt{|G:Z(G)|}
and
\rho_{0}(G)=\frac{|S||T||U|}{|G|}=\frac{|S|^{3}}{|G|}=\frac{\sqrt{|G:Z(G)|}}{|Z(G)|}<\sqrt{|G:Z(G)|}
.

Consider (ii)
S_{0}=S\cap Z(G)>\{1\}
, where
S_{0}<S,Z(G)
. Then
G/S_{0}
is a group of order
<|G|
and nilpotency class
2
as there is a central series of length
2
given by
\{1\}<(G/S_{0})^{\prime}=[G/S_{0},G/S_{0}]=G^{\prime}S_{0}/S_{0}\leq Z(G)S_{0}/S_{0}<G/S_{0}
. The inductive hypothesis and Lemma
2.13
yield
\rho_{0}(G/S_{0})<\sqrt{|G/S_{0}|:|Z(G/S_{0})|}\leq\sqrt{|G:Z(G)|}
. Lemma
2.10
implies that
G/S_{0}
realises the subgroup TPP triple
(S/S_{0},TS_{0}/S_{0},US_{0}/S_{0})
of type
(|S|/|S_{0}|,|T|,|U|)
and
\rho_{0}(G)=\frac{|S||T||U|/|S_{0}|}{|G|/|S_{0}|}\leq\rho_{0}(G/S_{0})<\sqrt{|G:Z(G)|}
. By permutation invariance the same conclusion follows from supposing
T_{0}=T\cap Z(G)>\{1\}
or
U_{0}=U\cap Z(G)>\{1\}
.

∎

The result may be compared with a fact from the character theory of finite groups that if
d
is the degree of an (irreducible) character of a group
G
then
d\leq\sqrt{|G:Z(G)|}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

If
G
is a nonabelian
p
-group with a cyclic commutator subgroup
G^{\prime}
of order
p
then

(4.2)

\rho_{0}(G)\leq p.

Proof.

The proof will be by induction on
|G|=p^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, where
G
is a nonabelian
p
-group with a cyclic commutator subgroup
G^{\prime}
of order
p
, and
p
is an arbitrary fixed prime. Recall that, as shown in Lemma
2.15
, they satisfy
1.1
. The base case is extraspecial groups of order
8
, which have already been treated in Theorem
3.1
and shown to have
\rho_{0}=1
. Note that by Proposition
2.14

\rho_{0}=1
is also true for all extraspecial groups of order
p^{3}
for all primes
p
.

(Inductive hypothesis) Suppose
4.2
is true for all
p
-groups of order
<p^{n}
, for
n\geq 5
, with cyclic commutator subgroups of order
p
.

The approach taken here will be almost identical to that used in Theorem
3.1
. Let
(S,T,U)
be a maximal non-trivial subgroup TPP triple of
G
such that
\frac{|S||T||U|}{|G|}=\rho_{0}(G)>1
. Let
S_{0}=S\cap Z(G)
. Then
S_{0}
is normal in
G
as it is central in
G
. There are two cases to consider: (i)
S_{0}=\{1\}
and (ii)
S_{0}>\{1\}
, where in both cases
S_{0}<S,Z(G)
. The easiest case is (ii)
S_{0}>\{1\}
. Then
|S_{0}|\geq p
and there is a quotient
G/S_{0}
of order
<p^{n}
which, by Lemma
2.10
and the inductive hypothesis, satisfies
\rho_{0}(G)\leq\rho_{0}(G/S_{0})\leq p
.

Consider case (i),
S_{0}=\{1\}
. As in Theorem
3.1

T\cap Z(G)=\{1\}
and
U\cap Z(G)=\{1\}
may also be supposed. Let
H=SZ(G)T=STZ(G)
. Then, by Observation
2.9
,
S
(and also
T
and
U
) is abelian and
H
is normal in
G
. There are two subcases (i.a)
H<G
and (i.b)
H=G
. Consider (i.a),
H<G
. Then
H
is of order
<p^{n}
, and of nilpotency class
2
as there is a central series of length
2
given by
\{1\}<H^{\prime}=[H,H]\leq Z(H)<H
, where
H^{\prime}=[SZ(G)T,SZ(G)T]=[S,T]
, and by the inductive hypothesis and Proposition
2.7

\rho_{0}(G)\leq\rho_{0}(H)\leq p
.

Now consider (i.b)
H=SZ(G)T=\langle S,T,Z(G)\rangle=G
. It will be shown, by constructing in parallel another normal subgroup
K=SG^{\prime}T=STG^{\prime}
of
G
, and comparing it with
H
while using the assumption that
G^{\prime}
is cyclic of order
p
, that
G
must be extraspecial. First, the possibility that
K<G
can be dealt with using the same argument above for the subcase (i.a), with
K
replacing
H
and
G^{\prime}
replacing
Z(G)
, which implies that
\rho_{0}(G)\leq\rho_{0}(K)\leq p
. So consider
K=SG^{\prime}T=G=SZ(G)T
, where
SG^{\prime}
is normal in
G
. As shown in Theorem
3.1
any non-trivial intersection
N=SZ(G)\cap T>\{1\}
can be used, together with the inductive hypothesis, to construct a quotient
G/N
such that
\rho_{0}(G)\leq\rho_{0}(G/N)\leq p
. Let
SZ(G)\cap T=\{1\}
Then
G
is a semidirect product
G=SZ(G)\rtimes T
. Now, as
S\cap G^{\prime}=\{1\}
and
SG^{\prime}\cap T=\{1\}
, the condition
K=SG^{\prime}T=SZ(G)T
implies that
G
is also the semidirect product
G=SG^{\prime}\rtimes T
, which implies that
TG^{\prime}\cong TZ(G)
and
G^{\prime}\cong Z(G)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. The Frattini subgroup, which is the intersection of all the maximal subgroups of
G
and consists of all the non-generating elements of
G
, is the product
\Phi(G)=G^{p}G^{\prime}
, where
G^{p}
is the subgroup generated by all the
p
-th powers of elements of
G
, and
G/\Phi(G)
is elementary abelian. In the case being considered
G^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, so all commutators
[x,y]\in G^{\prime}
are of order
p
, and
2.13
yields
[g^{p},x]=[g,p]^{p}=1
for any
g^{p}\in G^{p}
and any
x\in G
. That is,
G^{p}
centralises
G
and so
G^{p}\leq Z(G)=G^{\prime}
, implying that
G^{\prime}=Z(G)=\Phi(G)
and that
G
is extraspecial of order
p^{1+2m}
for some
m\geq 2
such that
n=2m+1
.

As shown in Theorem
3.1
, by permuting
S,T,U
, there are also semidirect products
G=SZ(G)\rtimes U=TZ(G)\rtimes S=TZ(G)\rtimes U
, which imply that
S\cong T\cong U
and
|S|=|T|=|U|=\sqrt{|G:Z(G)|}=p^{m}
. Also
S,T,U
are all abelian (from
S\cap G^{\prime}=T\cap G^{\prime}=U\cap G^{\prime}=\{1\}
). Writing
G
as
G=S\Phi(G)T=\langle S,T,\Phi(G)\rangle
the Frattini subgroup
\Phi(G)
can be removed, which implies that
\langle S,T\rangle=G
. But this is directly contradicted by Lemma
2.18
.
∎

This leads to an immediate corollary for extraspecial groups, which have the property that
G^{\prime}
is cyclic of order
p
such that
G^{\prime}=Z(G)=\Phi(G)
.

Corollary 4.2
.

If
G
is an extraspecial group of order
p^{1+2n}
, for
n\geq 1
, then
\rho_{0}(G)\leq p
.

5.
p
-Groups Of Nilpotency Class
2
with a Large Centre

The groups considered here are
p
-group of nilpotency class
2
that satisfy
p^{2}\leq|G:Z(G)|\leq p^{3}
. The available subgroup TPP ratio data for these groups can be found as a subset of Table
1
, and Tables
2
and
3
, where
|G:Z(G)|=p^{3}
. Although Theorem
3.1
implies that
\rho_{0}\leq p
here, a stronger result of
\rho_{0}=1
follows from Proposition
2.17
.

Theorem 5.1
.

If
G
is a
p
-group of nilpotency class
2
such that
p^{2}\leq|G:Z(G)|\leq p^{3}
then
\rho_{0}(G)=1
.

Proof.

Let
G
be as given. By Theorem
1.1

\rho_{0}(G)\leq p
, which means that the size
|S||T||U|
of any subgroup TPP triple of
G
satisfies
|S||T||U|\leq p|G|=p^{n+1}
. However, by Proposition
2.17

G
contains abelian (maximal, normal) subgroups of index

, and by Corollary
[
9
, Corollary 4.3]

\rho_{0}(G)=1
.
∎

6.
p
-Groups of Nilpotency Class
2
with Small Character Degrees

As proved in
[
9
, Theorem 4.1, Corollary 4.3]
and used in Theorem
5.1
groups with large abelian normal subgroups of prime index
p
tend to have a correspondingly small subgroup TPP ratio
\rho_{0}
not exceeding
p
. There is an interesting fact from the character theory of finite groups, that the degree of an irreducible character of a group with an abelian (though, not necessarily normal) subgroup of index
n
, where
n
is not necessarily prime, cannot exceed
n
. The ”smallness” of character degrees of a group also seems to play a role in bounding the value of
\rho_{0}
, at least indirectly by reflecting the existence of large abelian subgroups. The available subgroup TPP ratio data for
p
-groups of nilpotency class
2
with
\text{cd}(G)=\{1,p\}
, which can be found as a subset of Table
1
, and Tables
2
and
3
, certainly suggest that such groups satisfy
\rho_{0}=1
, as there is no counterexample, and this is proved in the following result.

Theorem 6.1
.

If
G
is a
p
-group of nilpotency class
2
with character degree set
\text{cd}(G)=\{1,p\}
then
\rho_{0}(G)=1
.

Proof.

By
[
6
, Theorem C 4.8]
any (nonabelian)
p
-group with
\text{cd}(G)=\{1,p\}
either contains an abelian (maximal, normal) subgroup of index
p
, or has a centre of index
p^{3}
. In the first case
\rho_{0}=1
by
[
9
, Corollary 4.3]
. In the second case
G/Z(G)
is abelian of order
p^{3}
and by Theorem
5.1

\rho_{0}(G)=1
.
∎

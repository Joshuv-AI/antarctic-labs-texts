Syntheses of all stereoisomers of aptosimon (9-oxosesamin) and their effects on plant growth regulation and diacylglycerol acyltransferase inhibition
Authors: Momoka Okada, Airi Iwata, Hisashi Nishiwaki, Shigeki Mori, Satoshi Yamauchi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
All eight stereoisomers of aptosimon (9-oxosesamin) were synthesized by employing Evans’ anti- and syn-aldol condensations. Among them, the (7S,7′R,8R,8′S)-aptosimon was more active than (+)- and (−)-sesamin at 1×10−5 M in promoting lettuce root growth under illumination conditions (44 µmol·m−2·s−1). The effect of the 9-oxo group on the promotion of lettuce root growth was not observed from the results of activity experiments of stereoisomers carrying the same stereochemistry as those of (+)- and (−)-sesamin (1). However, the importance of 7S-configurations of aptosimon (9-oxosesamin) stereoisomers for the higher promotion of lettuce root growth was suggested. Based on optical rotation data and NMR analysis of the synthesized stereoisomers, the diacylglycerol acyltransferase inhibitory aptosimon was determined to be (7S,7′S,8S,8′R)-aptosimon and its enantiomer.

Introduction

To explore the biological activity of natural compounds, it is important to synthesize and analyze their stereoisomers. The chemical data obtained from the synthetic stereoisomers are useful to determine the stereochemistry of biosynthesized compounds. Furthermore, biological experiments using the stereoisomers would provide information on the stereochemistry-activity relationship. In this experiment, we synthesized aptosimon1) stereoisomers 2, 3, ent-2, ent-3, 4, 5, ent-4, and ent-5 of the 9-furofuranone lignan2) (9-oxosesamin) (Fig. 1) and determined their effect on seedling growth.

Fig. 1. (+)- and (−)-sesamin and all stereoisomers of aptosimon

(+)- and (−)-Sesamin1) are plant growth regulatory compounds.3,4) Since furofuran lignan with carbonyl group at the 9-position is biosynthesized by plants,2) it is of interest to understand its importance on plant growth. The isolation of 9-furofuranones5–10) including aptosimon is in progress by many research groups. Although 9-furofuranones on biological activities11–13) and their syntheses14–16) have been reported, the effect of stereochemistry on biological activity remains to be clarified. The success of our synthetic study of aptosimon stereoisomers would facilitate further biological research. In phytochemical research, such synthesis would make the comparison of aptosimon stereoisomers with other plant growth regulatory lignans17–27) for their effects on plant growth possible. Understanding the effect of aptosimon stereochemistry on plant growth may provide information for developing novel regulatory compounds based on the furofuran lignan structure. Furthermore, the chemical data of the synthetic stereoisomers in this research could help determine the stereochemistry of the diacylglycerol acyltransferase (DGAT) inhibitor in Eleutherococcus senticosus.12)

The synthetic strategy to obtain all stereoisomers of aptosimon is illustrated in Scheme 1. Evans’ respective anti- and syn-aldol condensation products 628) and 2′-epi-629) were selected as starting materials. The two chiral centers could be converted into benzylic positions and 4-positions of key intermediate lactones 7 and 4-epi-7, respectively. Construction of the other two chiral centers was achieved by aldol condensations to afford 7′R-8 and 7′S-9 from lactone 7 and 7′S-10 and 7′R-11 from lactone 4-epi-7. These aldol products might be converted into intermediates 12–15 by retaining stereochemistry at the 7-position using SN2 cyclization. Intermediates 12–15 could be transformed into 7,8-cis-stereoisomers 2, 3 and 7,8-trans-stereoisomers 4, 5 by cyclization under neutral conditions to avoid epimerization at the oxidized benzylic 7- and 7′-positions. Enantiomers ent-2 and ent-3 were obtained from ent-6, while ent-4 and ent-5 could be obtained from ent-2′-epi-6.

Melting points (mp) data are uncorrected. Reagents used for the syntheses were purchased from FUJIFILM Wako Pure Chemical Corporation (Osaka, Japan), Tokyo Chemical Industry Co., Ltd. 2. Crystallographic analysis

X-ray diffraction analysis was performed using a Rigaku VariMax with a Rigaku VariMax with RAPID diffractometer using multilayer mirror monochromated CuKα radiation (λ=1.54187 Å) at 100 K. The crystals were mounted in cryoloops. Data collection and reduction were performed using RAPID AUTO software. The data were corrected for Lorentz polarization and absorption effects. All calculations were performed using Olex2 1.533) software. The structures were solved using SHELXT 2014/534) and expanded using the Fourier technique. SHELXL-2018/335) was used for structure refinement. The data were validated using PLATON. Suitable single crystals of ent-2, 3, 4, and 5 were obtained from slow evaporation of a solution in EtOH.

3. Evaluation of Plant Growth Regulatory Activity with five different concentrations

The plant growth regulatory activity of all stereoisomers of aptosimon was evaluated using lettuce (Lactuca sativa L. Green-wave (Takii Seed Co. Ltd., Kyoto, Japan)). A sheet of filter paper (diameter=90 mm) was placed in a 90 mm Petri dish and wetted with 500 µL of test sample solution dissolved in acetone at the five different concentrations from 6.0×10−7 to 6.0×10−3 M. After the filter paper had dried, 3 mL of water was poured into the dish to adjust the final concentration from 1.0×10−7 to 1.0×10−3 M. Thirty seeds of each plant were placed on the filter paper, and the Petri dishes were sealed with Parafilm. The Petri dishes were then incubated at 25°C under light (44 µmol·m−2·s−1). Three days after treatment, the lengths of roots and shoots of lettuce were measured by ordinary ruler. The root and shoot lengths of the control lettuce plants were 3 and 4 cm, respectively. We compared the root and shoot lengths of the lettuce induced by each stereoisomer at different concentrations (1.0×10−7–1.0×10−3 M). Data are represented as growth percentage relative to the control; 100% means same as control and more than 100% present stimulation of plant growth. Experiments were performed in triplicate or more for each sample (n=3–7). Statistical analyses were conducted by oneway ANOVA followed by Tukey’s multiple-comparison test using PRISM software (Graphpad Software, San Diego, CA), and the values of p<0.05 were considered to be statistically significant.

Results and discussion

Evans’ anti- and syn-aldol compounds 6 and 2′-epi-6 were converted into lactones 18 and 4-epi-18, respectively (Supplemental Information). Protection of the secondary hydroxy groups of 6 and 2′-epi-6, followed by reductive removal of Evans’ auxiliaries, gave corresponding primary alcohols. These hydroxy alkenes were transformed into lactones 18 and 4-epi-18 through three-step oxidations, respectively.

Diastereomers 1′R-19 and 1′S-19 were generated using aldol condensation, which completed the syntheses of all aptosimon stereoisomers (Scheme 2). The employment of lithium enolate30) of lactone 18 in the aldol condensation with piperonal resulted in the preparation of 1′R-19 and 1′S-19. The stereochemistry of 1′R-19 and 1′S-19 were determined by coupling constants between 3-Ha and 1′-Hb. The vicinal coupling constant between 3-Ha and 1′-Hb should be different owing to the hydrogen bonds between 1′-hydroxy proton and carbonyl oxygen forming a 6-membered ring. In 1′R-19, the axial-equatorial relationship of 3-Ha and 1′-Hb caused a smaller coupling constant (J=3.2 Hz) than that of the diaxial relationship of 3-Ha and 1′-Hb in the 1′S-19 (J=8.3 Hz).31)

After reductive opening of the lactone rings of 1′R-19 and 1′S-19, two primary hydroxy groups of 20 and 1-epi-20 were selectively converted into pivaloyl esters and tosylates to afford 22 and 4-epi-22, respectively. Removal of silyl groups, followed by treatment with base, generated tetrahydrofuran 23 and 1′-epi-23, respectively through SN2 cyclization. Secondary hydroxy groups of 23 and 1′-epi-23 were protected as silyl ethers, followed by the removal of pivaloyl groups through reduction. The resulting primary hydroxy groups of 25 and 1′-epi-25 were oxidized to aldehydes 26 and 1′-epi-26, respectively, which were cyclized to bicyclo[3.3.0]octanes under mild conditions to yield hemiacetals. Finally, pyridinium chlorochromate oxidation of the hemiacetals produced aptosimon stereoisomers 2 and 3.

We obtained each aptosimon stereoisomer using a new synthetic approach to synthesized aptosimon. Although the proposed structure for the DGAT inhibitor in E. senticosus is 3 or ent-3,12) NMR data in the literature agree well with those of 4 and ent-4. Comparison of optical rotation data of the synthesized stereoisomers 4 (+102, c 0.213, MeOH) and ent-4 (–100, c 0.103, MeOH) with that reported for the DGAT inhibitor (–11.1, c 0.1, MeOH) clearly indicate that the natural DGAT inhibitor is a mixture of (7S,7′S,8S,8′R)-aptosimon (4) and its enantiomer (ent-4). The biosynthesis of some lignans results in enantiomeric mixtures.32)

Then, the aptosimon stereoisomers 2–5 were examined for their effects on plant growth (Fig. 3). No effect was observed against lettuce shoot growth under illumination conditions (44 µmol·m−2·s−1, supporting information). Meanwhile, lettuce root growth was promoted under illumination (Figs. 3, 44 µmol·m−2·s−1). Figure 3 shows lettuce root growth at each concentration from 1×10−5 to 1×10−3 M. The lettuce root growths of each compound from 1×10−7 to 1×10−3 and each concentration at 1×10−7 and 1×10−6 are shown in supporting information. In particular, the significantly higher activity than (+)- and (−)-sesamin (1) was observed in stereoisomer 2 ((7S,7′R,8R,8′S)-form) at 1×10−5 M, promoting root 2.5-fold compared with the control. At 1×10−3 M, ent-2 ((7R,7′S,8S,8′R)-form), which had 2.5-fold activity compared with the control, showed significantly higher activity than (−)-sesamin (1), suggesting the importance of relative configuration of (7S*,7′R*,8R*,8′S*)-form for more potent activity than (+)- and (−)-sesamin (1). The stereoisomer 4 ((7S,7′S,8S,8′R)-form) and ent-4 ((7R,7′R,8R,8′S)-form) carrying the same stereochemistry as that of (+)- and (−)-sesamin (1), respectively, showed similar activity to (+)- and (−)-sesamin (1), suggesting that the carbonyl group at 9-positon did not affect the activity. In the comparison of corresponding enantiomers, stereoisomer 2 ((7S,7′R,8R,8′S)-form) was more potent than ent-2 ((7R,7′S,8S,8′R)-form) at 1×10−4 and 1×10−5 M. Comparing stereoisomer 3 ((7S,7′S,8R,8′S)-form) with ent-3 ((7R,7′R,8S,8′R)-form), stereoisomer 3 was ca. 2-fold more potent at 1×10−3 M. Furthermore, the higher activity of stereoisomer 4 ((7S,7′S,8S,8′R)-form) than ent-4 ((7R,7′R,8R,8′S)-form) was observed at 1×10−4 M. On the other hand, significant difference of activity between stereoisomer 5 ((7S,7′R,8S,8′R)-form) and ent-5 ((7R,7′S,8R,8′S)-form) was not shown at all concentrations. However, the tendency of advantage of 7S-configuration was suggested.

The previously reported tetrahydrofuran,18,20) coumarin,25) and tetrahydropyran26) type lignans had plant growth inhibitory activity against ryegrass roots, which is a monocotyledon. In this experiment, we confirmed that a furofuran type lignan promoted lettuce root growth (a dicotyledon) under illumination, while lettuce shoots were unaffected.

Conclusion

All stereoisomers of aptosimon (9-oxosesamin) 2–5, ent-2–ent-5 were synthesized. NMR and optical analyses suggested that the DGAT inhibitor12) of E. senticosus was an enantiomeric mixture of 4 and ent-4. In the plant growth experiment, the stereoisomer 2 was more potent stereoisomer than (+)- and (−)-sesamin (1) for lettuce root growth at 1×10−5 M. The importance of 7S-configurations of aptosimon (9-oxosesamin) stereoisomers for the higher promoting lettuce root growth was suggested. This discovery contributes to phytochemical research and could lead to the development of a novel plant growth regulatory compound.

Reducing Shortcut and Hopset Constructions to Shallow Graphs
Bernhard Haeupler, Yonggang Jiang, Thatchaphol Saranurak

Abstract
We introduce a blackbox framework that simplifies all known parallel algorithms with near-linear work for single-source reachability and shortest paths in directed graphs. Specifically, existing reachability algorithms rely on constructing shortcuts ; our blackbox allows these algorithms that construct shortcuts with hopbound h h to assume the input graph G G is “shallow”, meaning if vertex s s can reach vertex t t , it can do so in approximately h h hops. This assumption significantly simplifies shortcut construction [ Fin18 , JLS19 ] , resulting in simpler parallel reachability algorithms. Furthermore, our blackbox extends naturally to simplify parallel algorithms for constructing hopsets and, consequently, for computing shortest paths [ CFR20 , CF23 , RHM+23 ] .

Introduction

Single-source reachability and shortest paths are among the most fundamental graph problems. Their (near-linear) time algorithms, such as breadth-first search, depth-first search, and Dijkstra, have been the cornerstone of graph algorithms. Unfortunately, all these classical algorithms are inherently sequential.

This inspired an extensive line of work on parallel algorithms for these problems since the 80s
[
UY91
,
Kle93
,
KS97
,
Spe97
]
. These parallel algorithms are more parallel and achieve sublinear depth, but they still require work that is polynomially larger than linear. The major open problem in this area is whether there exists an algorithm with
near-linear work
and
polylogarithmic depth
.

In 2018, Fineman
[
Fin18
]
presented a breakthrough algorithm that, given a directed graph with
n
vertices and
m
edges, computes single-source reachability using
\tilde{O}(m)
work and
\tilde{O}(n^{2/3})
depth. This sparked a line of exciting work that improved the depth to
n^{1/2+o(1)}

[
JLS19
]
, extended the result to approximate shortest paths
[
CFR20
]
, and further extended it to exact shortest paths
[
CF23
,
RHM+23
]
.

The core graph-theoretic objects behind all these results are
shortcuts
or
hopsets
.
A set of directed edges
H\subseteq V\times V
is an
h
-shortcut
for
G
(of size
|H|
and depth

) if
H
is a subgraph of the transitive closure of

and for every

u,v\in V
that are reachable in

, there is a path from
u
to

in the augmented graph
G\cup H
that uses at most
h
edges.
A set of directed edges with weights
H\subseteq V\times V
is an
(\alpha,h)
-hopset
for

if for every
u,v\in V
,
\mathrm{dist}_{G}(u,v)\leq\mathrm{dist}^{(h)}_{G\cup H}(u,v)\leq\alpha\cdot\mathrm{dist}_{G}(u,v)
where
\mathrm{dist}^{(h)}_{G\cup H}(u,v)
is the length of the shortest path from
u
to
v
using at most
h

Given these objects, parallel algorithms for single-source reachability and shortest paths are immediate. For example, given a shortcut
H
for a graph
G
of size
s
and diameter
h
, we can compute single-source reachability by simply running breadth-first search on

in
O(m+s)
work and
O(h)
depth. Thus, the parameters

and

determine the efficiency.
One can similarly obtain parallel algorithms for single-source shortest paths, given hopset using standard techniques too.

Thus, efficient construction of shortcuts and hopsets in the parallel setting has been
the
common goal of all prior results
[
Fin20
,
JLS19
,
CFR20
]
. Towards this, they follow the same strategy.

1.

(Sequential)
: Show algorithms for building shortcuts or hopsets in near-linear work.

2.

(Parallel)
: Modify the sequential algorithms so that they also have small depth.

The key algorithmic ideas usually lie in the first step – the straightforward construction all require super-linear work, and it is already very non-trivial to obtain a fast sequential algorithm. However, the second step is much more complicated and technical. For example, the result by Fineman
[
Fin20
]
spends 10 pages on the sequential construction (Section3), but then takes at least 20 pages for the parallel modification (Section 4-5). Similarly,
[
JLS19
]
takes 8 pages for sequential construction (Section 4), but takes 15 more pages for parallelization (Section 5).
In
[
CFR20
]
, even though the changes are less substantial, their hopset construction also requires some modifications to be implemented in the parallel setting.

Thus, it would be highly desirable if researchers could focus solely on the first step, which encapsulates the key ideas, and treat the second step as a black box, thereby avoiding all technicalities.
Typically, the first step of these algorithms involves running BFS or shortest path algorithms on a subgraph of the input graph.
We observe that this first step has low depth under the assumption that the input graph is
shallow
(see
Appendix

B
for more details).
This paper achieves precisely this goal.
Formally, we establish the following theorem.

Technical Overview.

In this technical overview we focus on the
shortcut
construction;
the
hopset
construction follows the same high‑level idea but involves additional details.

Our goal is to reduce the reachability diameter of a graph
G
with reachability diameter at least
2h
by a factor of
2
.
Once such a reduction is available, we simply invoke the algorithm for
\log n
rounds, each time
adding the newly computed shortcuts to

before the next round.
After
\log n
rounds the reachability diameter drops to at most
h
.

The algorithm begins with a low‑diameter decomposition (LDD) of
G
with the diameter parameter
\widetilde{\Theta}\left(h\right)

(here
\widetilde{\Theta}\left(h\right)
abbreviates
h
multiplied by a large enough polylogarithmic factor).
The formal definition of an LDD is in
Lemma

3.5
.
In short, the LDD is a randomized partition of
V(G)
into strongly connected components

V_{1},\dots,V_{z}
such that

•

each component
V_{i}
has
strong diameter
3
3

3

The strong diameter of
V_{i}
is the diameter of the induced subgraph
G[V_{i}]
, which is the longest distance between
every pair
of nodes, in contrast to reachability diameter.

d=\widetilde{O}\left(h\right)
, and

•

for every edge
(u,v)
the probability that it is
reversed
—that is,
v\in V_{i}
and
u\in V_{j}
with
i<j
—is at most
O(\log^{2}n/d)=o(1/h)
(because
d=\widetilde{\Theta}\left(h\right)
).

Let
H
denote the subgraph obtained from
G
by deleting all reversed edges.

For every path
p
connecting two vertices
s,t
with
\ell
edges we wish to add shortcut edges so that
s
and
t
are connected by a path of at most
\ell/2
edges.
According to the LDD guarantees, in expectation there are at most
o(\ell/h)

reversed
edges on
p
.
Thus
p
can be split into
k=o(\ell/h)
segments
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{k}
such that each
p_{i}
lies completely inside
H
.
If we can add shortcuts that reduce the reachability diameter of
H
to
h
, each
p_{i}
can be replaced by an path of length
h
, so the entire path is replaced by one of length

o(\ell/h)\cdot h\;=\;o(\ell)\;<\;\ell/2,

achieving the desired factor‑
2
reduction.

It therefore remains to add shortcuts that bring the reachability diameter of
H
down to
h
.
By construction, each
V_{i}
produced by the LDD has strong diameter
\widetilde{O}\left(h\right)
, exactly matching the precondition of the oracle
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
.
We call
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
on every induced subgraph
G[V_{i}]
to obtain a shortcut set
E_{i}
so that
G[V_{i}]\cup E_{i}
has reachability diameter at most
h
.
After inserting both
E_{i}
and
E_{i+1}
, every two adjacent vertex sets
G[V_{i}\cup V_{i+1}]\cup E_{i}\cup E_{i+1}
has reachability diameter
2h
, again satisfying the conditions for
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
.
Hence we merge every two adjacent vertex sets and call
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
on each merged graph.
Repeating this procedure for
O(\log n)
rounds ultimately ensures that
H
attains reachability diameter at most
h
.

Preliminaries

Asymptotic notations.

Graphs and edge lengths.

All graphs in this paper are directed graphs (digraphs)

\ell_{G}:E\to{\mathbb{N}}^{+}
, or simply

is a digraph without cycles. We require the edge lengths to be positive integers so the length of a path is an upper bound on the
hoplength

|p|
- defined as the number of edges on the path. When we do not specify the length function of

\ell(e)=1

.

Distances and diameters.

For

, we say

can
reach

from

to

to

(denoted by
\mathrm{dist}_{G}(u,v)
) is defined as the minimum length of paths from

to

. The

of a directed graph is the maximum distance between every two reachable pairs.

Given an integer

, the

from

to

to

with hoplength at most

. We say

\alpha

\mathrm{dist}^{(h)}_{G}(u,v)\leq\alpha\cdot\mathrm{dist}_{G}(u,v)

.

Given a vertex set
S\subseteq V
, the

of

is the minimum distance on

between every pair of nodes in

. The

of
S
, or simply the diameter of

, is the minimum distance on
G[S]
between every pair of nodes in
S
.

Shortcut.

A shortcut for a graph
G=(V,E)
is a set of edge
E^{\prime}\subseteq V\times V
such that every
(u,v)\in E^{\prime}
is a reachable pair in
G
. The

or

of

is defined as the reachability diameter of

. The size of
E^{\prime}
is defined as
|E^{\prime}|
.

Hopset.

A hopset for a graph

with edge length is a set of edges

with edge length such that for every
u,v\in V
, we have
\mathrm{dist}_{G}(u,v)\leq\mathrm{dist}_{G\cup E^{\prime}}(u,v)
. We say
E^{\prime}
has length slackness
\alpha
and hopbound

if
\mathrm{dist}^{(\beta)}_{G\cup E^{\prime}}(u,v)\leq\alpha\cdot\mathrm{dist}_{G}(u,v)
, in which case we call

a

-hopset. The size of

is defined as

.

Appendix B
Simplification of Parallel Reachability and Shortest Path

Assume the input graph
G
has reachability diameter
\hat{O}\left(h\right)
. We show that the sequential algorithm in
[
JLS19
]
has depth
\hat{O}\left(h\right)
and produces a shortcut with
\hat{O}\left(n\right)=\widetilde{O}\left(0\cdot m+n\right)
edges and
\hat{O}\left(h\right)
depth. According to
Corollary

3.2
, this immediately yields a reachability algorithm with
\hat{O}\left(m\right)
work and
\hat{O}\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
depth on general graphs by plugging in
\lambda=\widetilde{O}\left(1\right),a=0,b=n
.

The only high-depth component of
[
JLS19
]
is the BFS procedure on subgraphs of
G
. Each subgraph is defined by a set of pivot vertices
S
, and consists of all vertices that share the same reachability condition (reachable to, reachable from, or not reachable at all) with respect to all vertices in
S
. Observe that for any two vertices
u,v
in such a subgraph, there exists a path
P
in
G
of length
\hat{O}\left(h\right)
connecting them. We argue that
P
is entirely contained within the subgraph, implying that the subgraph has reachability diameter at most
\hat{O}\left(h\right)
, and thus BFS on it has depth
\hat{O}\left(h\right)
.

To see this, note that every vertex on
P
has the same reachability condition to
S
as
u
and
v
. Specifically, for any
w\in S
:

•

If
w
can reach both
u
and
v
, then it can reach every vertex on
P
.

•

If
w
can be reached from both
u
and
v
, then every vertex on
P
can reach
w
.

•

If
w
can neither reach nor be reached from
u
and
v
, then the same holds for all vertices on
P
.

This completes the argument.

The simplification for the shortest path problem follows analogously. This section demonstrates that for future work on parallel reachability or shortest paths, one can directly apply our black-box reduction and focus only on shallow graphs.

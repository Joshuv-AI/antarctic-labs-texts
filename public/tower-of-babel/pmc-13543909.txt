Multimodality assessment of right ventricular–pulmonary arterial coupling and pulmonary artery compliance in youth with pulmonary arterial hypertension: A pilot study
Authors: Shashi Raj, Basha Khan, Vimal Raj, Vamsidhar Reddy Pulagam, Bibhuti B Das
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) enables comprehensive assessment of right ventricular (RV) function and ventricular-vascular interaction in pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH). This exploratory pilot study compared echocardiographic and CMR-derived surrogate indices of RV-pulmonary arterial (RV-PA) coupling and examined indexed pulmonary artery compliance (PAc) in adolescents and young adults with PAH.MethodsTwenty adolescents and young adults with Group 1 PAH underwent transthoracic echocardiography, CMR, and cardiac catheterization within 7 days. Echocardiographic TAPSE/systolic pulmonary artery pressure (sPAP) and CMR RV stroke volume/end-systolic volume were analyzed as noninvasive surrogates of RV-PA coupling; pressure-volume loop-derived end-systolic elastance/arterial elastance was not measured. Indexed PAc and pulmonary vascular resistance index (PVRi) were derived from catheterization and CMR data. Associations with WHO functional class, N-terminal pro–B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP), and 6-minute walk distance (6MWD) were explored.ResultsEchocardiographic TAPSE/sPAP showed limited associations with clinical severity measures. CMR-derived RV indices showed stronger exploratory associations with NT-proBNP and 6MWD. In the prespecified multivariable model of the exercise endpoint, indexed PAc had the largest standardized coefficient (β = −0.78; 95% CI, −1.48 to −0.09; p = 0.031); PVRi and the imaging surrogates were not statistically significant. Estimates were imprecise because of the small sample.ConclusionsIn this small, hypothesis-generating cohort, CMR-derived RV-PA coupling and indexed PAc provided complementary physiologic information beyond the echocardiographic surrogate. These observations require validation against pressure–volume loop-derived coupling and prospective clinical outcomes.Keywords: pediatric pulmonary hypertension, right ventricular–pulmonary arterial coupling, cardiac magnetic resonance imaging, pulmonary artery compliance, pulmonary vascular resistance

Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is a progressive vasculopathy characterized by pulmonary vascular remodeling, increased pulmonary arterial pressures (PAP), and eventual right ventricular (RV) failure, which remains the principal determinant of outcomes across age groups.1 In adolescents and young adults with PAH, as in adults, disease severity and prognosis are driven by the ability of the RV to adapt to chronic afterload imposed by the pulmonary circulation. Accurate, noninvasive assessment of RV-PA interaction is therefore central to risk stratification and longitudinal management. RV-PA coupling, commonly expressed as the ratio of RV end-systolic elastance (Ees) to arterial elastance (Ea), provides a load-adjusted measure of RV contractile adaptation and has emerged as a robust prognostic marker in adult PAH.2, 3 However, direct assessment using pressure-volume loop analysis is invasive and impractical in routine clinical practice, leading to reliance on simplified noninvasive surrogates such as the echocardiographic tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion (TAPSE)/systolic PAP (sPAP) ratio, which is limited by load dependence and geometric assumptions.4 Cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (CMR) enables noninvasive estimation of RV-PA coupling using stroke volume and end-systolic volume, and has demonstrated strong associations with severity, and adverse outcomes in children and adults with PAH.5, 6, 7, 8

Dufva et al. evaluated Ees/Ea derived from right‑heart catheterization (RHC) and demonstrated that invasive RV-PA coupling is a strong independent predictor of adverse outcomes in pediatric PAH, highlighting its potential prognostic utility.9 In contrast, our pilot study focused on noninvasive characterization of RV-PA interaction using CMR and emphasized indexed pulmonary artery compliance (PAc). In adults with PAH, PAc captures the pulsatile component of RV afterload and vascular stiffness not conveyed by pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR).10 PAc remains insufficiently studied in the noninvasive assessment of younger patients with PAH.11, 12 This pilot study therefore evaluated the feasibility and clinical associations of a CMR-derived indexed PAc in youth with PAH. We compared CMR derived RV-PA coupling and indexed PAc with an echocardiographic coupling surrogate and with PVRi derived from RHC. Because pressure–volume loops were not acquired, the study was designed to compare clinical associations of surrogate indices rather than to validate either surrogate against Ees/Ea.

Methods

Study population

Between July 2021 and October 2025, 36 consecutive patients with suspected PAH were evaluated at a single center and enrolled in a prospective registry approved by the institutional review board (NHH/AEC-CL-2019-427). Written informed consent was obtained from parents or legal guardians and from participants aged ≥18 years. Because this was an exploratory pilot study, no formal hypothesis-based sample-size calculation was performed. The sample comprised all eligible participants who completed contemporaneous TTE, CMR, and catheterization during the enrollment period. The target of approximately 20 analyzable participants was based on feasibility and was intended to estimate effect sizes and uncertainty for planning a larger validation study, not to establish definitive diagnostic or prognostic thresholds.

PAH was confirmed by RHC using contemporary guideline criteria.5, 11 All patients underwent a comprehensive diagnostic evaluation, including transthoracic echocardiography (TTE), and CMR, and RHC, to ensure accurate phenotyping and WHO classification of PH. An extensive work-up, including genetic testing when indicated, was performed to exclude secondary causes of PH. Sixteen patients were excluded because of incomplete diagnostic evaluation or imaging performed outside a predefined 7-day assessment window. The final study cohort consisted of 20 adolescents and young adults with WHO Group 1 PAH who had echocardiography and CMR derived RV-PA coupling, CMR derived PAc, and PVRi from RHC and were included for comparison with WHO functional class, biomarker, and six-minute walk distance (6MWD).

Echocardiography

TTE was performed using standardized imaging protocols by experienced sonographers.13 TAPSE was measured from the apical four-chamber view. Systolic PAP was estimated from the peak tricuspid regurgitant jet velocity using the modified Bernoulli equation.13 RV-PA coupling was estimated noninvasively using the TAPSE/sPAP ratio, a validated echocardiographic surrogate reflecting the balance between RV longitudinal contractile function and afterload.14, 15

Cardiac magnetic resonance imaging

CMR was performed using 1.5 T and 3.0 T scanners (Siemens Magnetom Sola and Philips Ingenia) with standardized cine steady-state free precession sequences.16 Imaging included contiguous short-axis stacks covering the entire RV and complementary long-axis views. Phase-contrast flow (Q-flow) imaging was performed across the main PA and ascending aorta to derive stroke volume. Right ventricular end-diastolic volume (RVEDV), RV end-systolic volume (RVESV), RV stroke volume (RVSV), and RV ejection fraction (RVEF) were quantified using dedicated post-processing software, indexed to body surface area (BSA) when appropriate, and interpreted using predefined threshold values based on established CMR protocols for PH.15 RV-PA coupling was estimated using the RVSV and RVESV ratio, a validated CMR-based surrogate of Ees/Ea. 6, 16, 17, 18 PAc was calculated as CMR‑derived RV stroke volume divided by RHC‑measured PA pulse pressure, the difference between systolic and diastolic PAP, and then indexed to BSA.

Cardiac catheterization

All participants underwent comprehensive diagnostic right and left catheterization under intravenous sedation directly supervised by pediatric cardiac anesthesiologist in accordance with established guidelines.19 Left heart catheterization were done in all patients per our institutional practice for true measure of LV end-diastolic pressure (LVED) although pulmonary artery wedge pressure could be equally reliable however with instances of triggering PH crisis in the past with RHC only (especially as we aborted general anesthesia). Cardiac output was determined by the Fick principle. PVR was calculated as (mean PAP–LVEDP/PCWP)/cardiac output and indexed to BSA (PVRi). Pulmonary-to-systemic flow ratio and vasoreactivity testing were performed when clinically indicated as per guidelines.20

Functional and biomarker assessment

In our study, we used the WHO functional class to classify the severity of PH.5 WHO functional class, 6MWD, and NT-proBNP levels were assessed within seven days of referral, the same period during which noninvasive (TTE, CMR) and invasive hemodynamic evaluations were performed. These parameters were selected a priori as clinically meaningful outcome measures based on their established prognostic relevance in pediatric PH.6, 7 The 6-minute walk test (6MWT) was conducted according to American Thoracic Society guidelines on a standardized flat corridor, with total distance walked recorded as 6MWD.21

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation or median [interquartile range], as appropriate, and categorical variables as counts (percentages). The primary exercise endpoint was the prespecified standardized exercise-limitation measure derived from 6MWD; its direction was coded so that higher values represented greater limitation. Associations of echocardiographic TAPSE/sPAP, CMR RVEF and RV stroke volume/end-systolic volume, indexed PAc, and PVRi with WHO functional class, NT-proBNP, and the exercise endpoint were explored using standardized univariable regression. PVRi was included as the conventional invasive comparator because 6MWD is influenced by age, growth, conditioning, musculoskeletal status, motivation, and comorbidity and is not a direct measure of RV contractility. Given n = 20, multivariable estimates were restricted to a prespecified parsimonious model and are presented with 95% confidence intervals. 22 Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) analyses were exploratory and not intended to establish mechanistic validity. Because invasive pressure–volume loop–derived Ees/Ea was unavailable, none of the models were interpreted as confirming true RV-PA coupling.

Results

Study population and baseline characteristics

Twenty children and adolescents with WHO Group 1 PAH were included finally (Table 1). The mean age was 14.1 ± 4.2 years, and 11 patients (55%) were female. Idiopathic PAH was the predominant etiology (n = 10), followed by heritable PAH (n = 4), repaired congenital heart disease (CHD)–associated PAH (n = 4), and PAH with incidental PFO or small ASD (n = 2). At baseline, most patients were receiving combination pulmonary vasodilator therapy, most commonly phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors, and endothelin receptor antagonists. Functional limitations were common, with 40% of patients in WHO functional class II, 50% in class III, and 10% in class IV. Mean 6MWD was 401 ± 94 m, and median NT-proBNP concentration was 1,980 pg/ml [interquartile range: 430–4,510]. TTE demonstrated elevated mean PAP and impaired RV function with a mean TAPSE of 15.1 ± 2.7 mm and a low RV-PA coupling ratio (TAPSE/sPAP) of 0.18 ± 0.09, consistent with RV-PA uncoupling. CMR confirmed RV dysfunction, with a mean RVEF of 35 ± 11%, RV-PA coupling ratio derived from volumetric indices (0.55 ± 0.32) and reduced indexed PAc averaging 1.53 ± 0.86 ml/mm Hg/m². RHC revealed markedly elevated PVRi (19.6 ± 11.3 WU·m⁻²).

Standardized univariable (Z-score) associations with clinical outcomes

Standardized univariable regression analyses are shown in Figure 1. TTE indices, including TAPSE and RV-PA coupling, demonstrated weak and inconsistent associations with WHO functional class, NT-proBNP, and 6MWD, with small, standardized effect sizes across all outcomes. In contrast, CMR-derived parameters demonstrated stronger associations with disease severity, as evidenced by decreased RVEF and impaired RV-PA coupling correlating with higher NT-proBNP levels and worse WHO functional class. Among all assessed parameters, indexed PAc demonstrated the strongest univariable association with functional capacity, exhibiting a moderate inverse relationship with 6MWD. Catheter-derived PVRi also correlated with worse WHO functional class and elevated NT-proBNP but showed weaker associations with 6MWD compared with indexed PAc.

Multivariable standardized regression for determinants of 6MWD

Results of the exploratory multivariable standardized regression analysis for the exercise-limitation endpoint (6MWD) are summarized in Table 2. Indexed PAc had the largest standardized coefficient (β = −0.78; 95% CI, −1.48 to −0.09; p = 0.031). PVRi (β = +0.19; p = 0.51), TTE-derived TAPSE/sPAP, CMR-derived RVEF, and CMR-derived RV-PA coupling were not statistically significant. Given the multifactorial nature of 6MWD and the small sample, this finding should be interpreted cautiously that indexed PAc independently reflects superior prognostic value. Exploratory ROC analyses comparing TTE-, CMR-, and catheter-derived indices are shown in Figure 2. Because of the small sample numbers, AUC estimates are descriptive and should not be interpreted as validated thresholds or comparative prognostic performance. Exact AUC values and outcome definitions are provided in the figure.

Discussion

In this exploratory multimodality pilot study (Figure 3, Central Figure), CMR-derived volumetric indices and indexed PAc provided complementary information about RV function and pulmonary vascular load. The study does not establish that either imaging ratio measures true RV-PA coupling, because the reference-standard pressure–volume loop Ees/Ea ratio was not acquired. Accordingly, our results address associations with contemporaneous clinical severity measures rather than validation or independent prognostic utility.

Echo-derived RV-PA coupling (TAPSE/sPAP) demonstrated negligible standardized effect sizes and limited explanatory power across key outcomes, including WHO functional class, NT-proBNP, and 6MWD. These findings likely reflect the inherent constraints of TTE-based surrogates, including load dependence, geometric assumptions, and limited ability to capture complex RV remodeling.23 In contrast, CMR-derived reduced RVEF was inversely associated with WHO functional class and NT-proBNP levels, reflecting progressive RV systolic dysfunction with advancing clinical severity.5 CMR-derived RV-PA coupling also demonstrated significant associations with WHO functional class and a trend toward lower NT-proBNP, supporting its utility in capturing the integrated interaction between RV contractility and afterload. These observations align with prior adult and pediatric studies underscoring the superior accuracy of CMR for RV volumetric assessment, particularly in populations with heterogeneous body size and heart rate where echocardiographic measures may be less reliable.24, 25, 26

Among the evaluated parameters, indexed PAc showed the strongest association with 6MWD. This comparison included PVRi as the conventional invasive marker of resistive load. The finding is biologically plausible, PAc reflects pulsatile load whereas PVRi reflects resistive load, but 6MWD is influenced by multiple non‑cardiovascular factors and is not a direct measure of RV function. Accordingly, this result should be viewed as hypothesis‑generating rather than evidence of incremental prognostic value.

Our preliminary findings are consistent with, but do not validate, the adult paradigm that pulsatile and resistive afterload provide complementary information. In the large adult cohort reported by Mounsey et al., lower PAc was independently associated with mortality and heart‑failure hospitalization after accounting for PVR, underscoring the prognostic contribution of pulsatile pulmonary vascular load.10 In our young PAH cohort, indexed PAc was associated only with a contemporaneous functional endpoint (6MWD), and longitudinal prognostic inference is not possible. Prior work by Gouwy et al. similarly demonstrated that in children with newly diagnosed idiopathic or heritable PAH, the relationship between PVR and PAc is not fixed, and PAc offered independent prognostic value for short‑term transplant‑free survival.12

Limitations

Several limitations merit consideration. First, this was a convenience sample of 20 participants without a formal power calculation; estimates are imprecise, multivariable and ROC analyses are at risk of overfitting, and all results are exploratory. Second, pressure–volume loop–derived Ees/Ea, the reference standard for RV–PA coupling, was not obtained because CD Leycom is the sole manufacturer of the equipment required for invasive pressure–volume analysis in humans, and this system is not available at our institution. Thus, the echocardiography- and CMR-derived RV–PA measures should be regarded as surrogates, and their stronger associations with conventional severity markers do not establish greater validity or prognostic utility. Third, 6MWD is multifactorial and cannot serve as a specific reference for RV health; PVRi was included as an invasive comparator, but the sample was inadequate for robust incremental-value testing. Fourth, the cohort combined idiopathic, heritable, and CHD-associated PAH. Prior repair, residual anatomy, and surgery-related RV remodeling may influence imaging and coupling measures. With only six CHD-associated PAH cases, reliable etiology-specific adjustment or subgroup analysis was not possible. Fifth, indexed PAc combined CMR stroke volume with catheter-derived pulse pressure rather than being wholly noninvasive. Finally, the single-center observational design and absence of longitudinal clinical events preclude causal or prognostic inference.

Conclusion

In this exploratory pilot study, CMR‑derived RV–PA coupling and indexed PAc provided complementary physiologic insights in adolescents and young adults with PAH. Indexed PAc showed the strongest association with the prespecified exercise endpoint after adjustment for PVRi, but the small sample, multifactorial nature of 6MWD, heterogeneous PAH etiologies, and absence of pressure–volume loop-derived Ees/Ea preclude any claims of validation, superiority, or independent prognostic value. Larger prospective studies should directly compare imaging surrogates with pressure–volume loop-derived coupling, stratify CHD phenotypes, and test incremental prediction of clinical outcomes.

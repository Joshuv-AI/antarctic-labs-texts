Bayesian Physics Informed Neural Networks for Linear Inverse problems
A. Mohammad-Djafari

Abstract
Inverse problems arise almost everywhere in science and engineering where we need to infer on a quantity from indirect observation. The cases of medical, biomedical, and industrial imaging systems are the typical examples. A very high overview of classification of the inverse problems method can be: i) Analytical, ii) Regularization, and iii) Bayesian inference methods. Even if there are straight links between them, we can say that the Bayesian inference based methods are the most powerful, as they give the possibility of accounting for prior knowledge and can account for errors and uncertainties in general. One of the main limitations stay in computational costs in particular for high dimensional imaging systems. Neural Networks (NN), and in particular Deep NNs (DNN), have been considered as a way to push farther this limit. Physics Informed Neural Networks (PINN) concept integrates physical laws with deep learning techniques to enhance the speed, accuracy and efficiency of the above mentioned problems. In this work, a new Bayesian framework for the concept of PINN (BPINN) is presented and discussed which includes the deterministic one if we use the Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) estimation framework. We consider two cases of supervised and unsupervised for training step, obtain the expressions of the posterior probability of the unknown variables, and deduce the posterior laws of the NN’s parameters. We also discuss about the challenges of implementation of these methods in real applications.

2
Proposed Bayesian PINN framework

We introduce the Bayesian PINN framework by following five steps:

•

First step is to assume that we have a forward or generative model:

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}={\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}})+\mbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}

(1)

where
f
is the input,
g
the output,
H
the forward model, and
\epsilon
the errors, both measurement and modeling. For a linear model, we have:
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}={\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}+\mbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}
.

•

Second step is to assign priors
p({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}})
and
p(\mbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$})
. From this second one, and the forward model, we can deduce the likelihood

p({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}|{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}})
. Then, we can use the Bayes rule to obtain:

p({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}})=\frac{p({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}|{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}})p({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}})}{p({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}})}

(2)

For the case of linear inverse problem
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}={\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}+\mbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}
, Gaussian likelihooh
p({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}|{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}})={\cal N}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}|{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{v_{\epsilon}}}{\mbox{\boldmath$I$}})
, and Gaussian prior

p({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}})={\cal N}({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}|\bar{{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}v_{f}}{\mbox{\boldmath$I$}})
, the posterior
p({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}})
is also Gaussian:

p({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}})={\cal N}({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}|\widehat{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}},{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\widehat{\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}}})

(3)

with

\begin{array}[]{l}\widehat{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}}=[{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}^{\prime}{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}+\lambda{\mbox{\boldmath$I$}}]^{-1}{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}^{\prime}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}-{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}\bar{{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}})\\
\ {\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\widehat{\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{v_{\epsilon}}}[{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}^{\prime}{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}+\lambda{\mbox{\boldmath$I$}}]^{-1},\quad\lambda=\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{v_{\epsilon}}}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}v_{f}}}\end{array}

(4)

•

Third step is to design an appropriate neural network which, takes as input
g
and as output
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{\tiny NN}
. This NN can be considered as a proxy, surrogate or approximate inversion, such that its output
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{\tiny NN}
can be very near to the true or ground truth
f
.

•

Fourth step is training the NN. We distinguish two cases:

–

Supervised case where we have a set of outputs-inputs
\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{Ti}\}
, and

–

Unsupervised case where the available data are only
\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}\}
.

The parameters of the NN,
w
are estimated at the end of training step.

•

Fifth step is using the trained NN, giving it any other input
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{j}
which produces the outputs
\widehat{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}}_{j}
which we hope to be not very far from the ground truth
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{j}
. We may also want to be able to quantify the its uncertainty.

To go more in details, first we consider the case of supervised case, and then the unsupervised case.

2.1
Supervized training data

First we consider the supervised case, i.e., when we have a set of labeled training data, (output-input of the generating forward system) :

\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{Ti}\}
.

\displaystyle\begin{tabular}[]{@{}c@{}}Data\\
$\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{Ti}\}$\end{tabular}\Rightarrow\framebox{~NN (${{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}}$)~}\Rightarrow{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{NNi}\Rightarrow\framebox{~${\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}$~}\Rightarrow{\widehat{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}}}_{NNi}

To write, step by step, the relations and equations, we follow:

•

We assume that, given the true
f
and the forward model

H
, the the data
\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}\}
are generated independently:

p(\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}\}|\{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i}\})=\prod_{i}p({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}|{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i}),

(5)

and we can assign the likelihood
p({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}|{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i})
, for example, the Gaussian:

p({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}|{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{v_{\epsilon}}}_{i}{\mbox{\boldmath$I$}})={\cal N}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}|{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{v_{\epsilon}}}_{i}{\mbox{\boldmath$I$}}).

(6)

The same for
\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{Ti}\}
, we assume that, given the true
f
, we have:

p(\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{Ti}\}|\{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i}\})=\prod_{i}p({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{Ti}|{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i})=\prod_{i}{\cal N}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{Ti}|{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}v_{f}}_{i}{\mbox{\boldmath$I$}})

(7)

•

We also assign a prior
p({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}})
to the true
f
to translate our prior knowledge about it.

•

Using the Bayes rule we get:

p({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i}|\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{Ti}\})\propto\prod_{i}p({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}|{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}v_{f}}_{i}{\mbox{\boldmath$I$}})\,p({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{Ti}|{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}v_{f}}_{i}{\mbox{\boldmath$I$}})\,p({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i})

(8)

•

With the Gaussian cases of the equations [5,6], we can rewrite the posterior as:

\displaystyle p({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i}|\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{Ti}\})

\displaystyle\propto\exp\left[{-J({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i})}\right]

with:

\displaystyle J({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i})=\sum_{i}\frac{1}{2{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{v_{\epsilon}}}_{i}}\|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}-{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}v_{f}}_{i}}\|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{Ti}-{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i}\|^{2}+\ln p({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i})

(9)

•

Choosing also a Gaussian prior:
p({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i})={\cal N}({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i}|\bar{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}v_{i}}{\mbox{\boldmath$I$}})
, we get:

J({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i})=\sum_{i}\frac{1}{2{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}v_{f}}_{i}}\|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{Ti}-{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{v_{\epsilon}}}_{i}}\|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}-{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}v_{i}}}\|{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i}-\bar{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}}\|^{2}

(10)

It is then easy to see that the
p({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i}|\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{Ti}\})
is Gaussian:

p({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i}|\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{Ti}\})={\cal N}({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i}|\widehat{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}}_{i},{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\widehat{\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}}}_{i})

(11)

with

\begin{array}[]{l}\widehat{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}}_{i}=\sum_{i}[{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}^{\prime}{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}+\lambda_{i}{\mbox{\boldmath$I$}}]^{-1}{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}^{\prime}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}-{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{Ti}-{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}\bar{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}})\\
\ {\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\widehat{\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}}}_{i}=\sum_{i}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{v_{\epsilon}}}_{i}[{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}^{\prime}{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}+\lambda_{i}{\mbox{\boldmath$I$}}]^{-1},\quad\lambda_{i}=\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{v_{\epsilon}}}_{i}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}v_{f}}_{i}}\end{array}

•

Now, we consider the training step of the NN where we note the output of the neural network
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{NNi}
which is a function of NN parameters
w
and the input data
\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}\}
, we can define the following optimization criterion:

J({{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}})=\sum_{i}\frac{1}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}v_{f}}_{i}}\|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{Ti}-{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{NNi}({{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{v_{\epsilon}}}_{i}}\|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}-{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{NNi}({{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{v_{\epsilon}}}_{i}}\|\bar{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}}-{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{NNi}({{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}})\|^{2}

(12)

This criterion can be considered as a physics based or physics informed neural network (PINN),
where the classical output residual part is:

J_{NN}({{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}})=\sum_{i}\frac{1}{2{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{v_{\epsilon}}}_{i}}\|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{Ti}-{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{NNi}({{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}})\|^{2}

(13)

and the physics informed part is:

J_{PI}({{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}})=\frac{1}{2{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}v_{f}}_{i}}\|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}-{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{NNi}({{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}v_{i}}}\|\bar{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}}-{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{NNi}({{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}})\|^{2}

(14)

•

We can also consider

p({{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}}|\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{Ti}\})\propto\exp\left[{-J({{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}})-\ln p({{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}})}\right]

(15)

and use it as the posterior probability distribution of the NN’s parameter
w
given the training data
\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{Ti}\}
. A specific choice for the prior
p({{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}})
can be one of the sparsity enforcing priors, such as

p({{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}})\propto\exp\left[{-\gamma\|{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}}\|_{\beta}^{\beta}}\right]

(16)

However, in practice, the full expression of this posterior is very difficult to obtain, and even its optimization can be more difficult if the NN contains nonlinear activation function.

2.2
Unsupervised training data

In the unsupervised case, we only have have a set of training data:
\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}\}
. The classical NN methods can not be applied as there is not any reference data. The main advantage of the PINN is exactly in the fact that, it can be applied in this case. The schematic for this case is almost the same, but we have only for training data
\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}\}
:

\displaystyle\begin{tabular}[]{@{}c@{}}Data\\
$\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}\}$\end{tabular}\Rightarrow\framebox{~NN (${{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}}$)~}\Rightarrow{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{NNi}\Rightarrow\framebox{~${\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}$~}\Rightarrow{\widehat{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}}}_{NNi}

However, we can still follow the same steps we had for the supervised case:

•

We assume that, given the true
f
and the forward model
H
, the the data
\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}\}
are independent, and follow a Gaussian distribution:

p(\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}\}|{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i})=\sum_{i}{\cal N}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}|{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{v_{\epsilon}}}_{i}{\mbox{\boldmath$I$}})\propto\exp\left[{-\frac{1}{2{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{v_{\epsilon}}}_{i}}\|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}-{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i}\|^{2}}\right]

(17)

•

We also assign a prior
p({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}})
to the true
f
to translate our prior knowledge about it. This time, we may use a more informative prior, such as Tikhonov, Total Variation or any other sparsity enforcing prior

p({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i})\propto\exp\left[{-\gamma\|{\mbox{\boldmath$D$}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i}\|_{\beta}^{\beta}}\right]

(18)

•

Using the Bayes rule we get:

\displaystyle p({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i}|\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}\})

\displaystyle\propto\exp\left[{-J({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i})}\right]

\displaystyle\mbox{with:~~}J({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\frac{1}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{v_{\epsilon}}}_{i}}\|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}-{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i}\|^{2}+\gamma\|{\mbox{\boldmath$D$}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{Ti}\|_{\beta}^{\beta}

(19)

•

Again, as in the supervised case, we can consider

\displaystyle p({{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}}|\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}\})

\displaystyle\propto\exp\left[{-J({{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}})}\right]

\displaystyle\mbox{with:~~}J({{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\frac{1}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{v_{\epsilon}}}_{i}}\|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}}_{Ti}-{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{i}({{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}})\|^{2}+\gamma\|{\mbox{\boldmath$D$}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}}_{Ti}({{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}})\|_{\beta}^{\beta}+\gamma_{w}\|{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}}}\|_{\beta_{w}}^{\beta_{w}}

(20)

which can be used for the estimation of the NN’s parameters.

In both supervised and unsupervised cases, we still have to choose an appropriate structure for the NN, its depth, its number of hidden variable, as well as choosing appropriate optimization algorithms, it learning rate, etc. These difficulties make the implementation of such methods not very easy and many experience are needed to implement, to train and to use them in real applications.

3
Conclusions

As discussed, inverse problems arise in many science and engineering applications where we need to infer on a quantity from indirect observation. The main application cases are medical, biomedical, geophysics, and industrial non destructive testing and diagnostics using imaging systems.

In this work, a new Bayesian framework for the concept of PINN (BPINN) is presented and discussed which includes the deterministic one if we use the Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) estimation framework.

We considered two cases of supervised and unsupervised training steps and drived, first the expressions of the posterior of the unknown desired variables, and then deduced the posterior law of the NN’s parameters. However, the full use of these expressions in real applications still need a great number of challenges: the choice of the structure of the NN, its effective implementation, choice of parameters of the optimization criteria, as well as all the parameters during the learning step of the NN.

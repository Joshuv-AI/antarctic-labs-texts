Robust virtual element methods for 3D stress-assisted diffusion problems
Andrés E. Rubiano

Abstract
This paper presents an initial exploration of stress-assisted diffusion problems in three dimensions within the framework of the virtual element method (VEM). Hilbert spaces enriched with parameter-weighted norms, the extended Babu s ˇ \check{\text{s}} ka–Brezzi–Braess theory for perturbed saddle-point problems, and Banach fixed-point theory play a crucial role in performing a robust analysis of the fully coupled non-linear system. The proposed virtual element formulations are provided with appropriate projection, interpolation, and stabilisation operators that ensures the well-posedness of the discrete problem. Numerical simulations are conducted to show the accuracy, performance, and applicability of the method. 2020 MSC codes: 65N99; 65Y99. Keywords: virtual element methods, stress-assisted diffusion, diffusion-induced stress, perturbed saddle-point problems.

Introduction

Scope.

Stress-assisted diffusion appears broadly in applications like Lithium-ion battery cells, silicon rubber and hydrogen diffusion, polymer-based coatings, semiconductor fabrication, oxidation of silicon nanostructures, and enhancing of conductivity properties in soft living tissue. In these processes, the stress of the material involved affects the patterns of diffusion. This paper addresses one of the simplest models for such an interaction, incorporating stress effects inside the diffusion coefficient, and recovering Fick’s law in the absence of these phenomena (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
). Moreover, we extend the analysis done in
[
8
]
pointing out the definition of VEM spaces in 3D and the polynomial, interpolation, and stabilisation operators used in this framework.

Outline.

The content of the paper is organised as follows: The remainder of this section presents the strong form of the coupled fully coupled stress-assisted diffusion problem. Section
2
is devoted to presenting the weak formulation and well-posedness analysis using perturbed saddle-point theory and fixed-point arguments. In Section
3
we define the discrete problem, show the existence and uniqueness of discrete solution, and outline the a priori error analysis. Finally, in Section
4
we provide numerical results that illustrate the theoretical results presented, and we present as an application of the model the lithiation of a perforated cylindrical anode particle.

Model statement.

Let us consider the following nonlinear formulation for stress-assisted diffusion of a solute that interacts with an elastic material

\displaystyle-\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(2\mu\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})-p\mathbb{I})=\boldsymbol{f}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in $\Omega$},\hskip 10.00002pt\boldsymbol{u}=\mathbf{0}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on $\Gamma_{D}$},

(1.1a)

\displaystyle p=-\lambda\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{u}\,+\ell(\varphi)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in $\Omega$},\hskip 10.00002pt(2\mu\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})-p\mathbb{I})\boldsymbol{n}=\boldsymbol{0}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on $\Gamma_{N}$},

(1.1b)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\zeta}=\mathbb{M}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),p)\nabla\varphi

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in $\Omega$},\hskip 10.00002pt\varphi=\varphi_{D}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on $\Gamma_{D}$},

(1.1c)

\displaystyle\theta\varphi-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\zeta})=g

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in $\Omega$},\hskip 10.00002pt\boldsymbol{\zeta}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on $\Gamma_{N}$}.

(1.1d)

Equations (
1.1a
) and (
1.1b
) constitute a Herrmann-like mixed formulation for linear elasticity. The coupling between the displacement
\boldsymbol{u}
of the material, the pressure
p
, and the solute concentration
\varphi
follows Hooke’s law to express the Cauchy stress tensor as
2\mu\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})-p\mathbb{I}
,
\mu
and
\lambda
are the Lamé parameters,
\ell
is the active stress coefficient, and
\boldsymbol{f}
is a vector of external body loads.

On the other hand, equations (
1.1d
) and (
1.1c
) correspond to the reaction-diffusion equation written in mixed form where
\boldsymbol{\zeta}
is the diffusive flux,
g
is a given net volumetric source of solute,
\theta
is a positive model parameter, and
\mathbb{M}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),p)
is the stress-assisted diffusion coefficient.

We adopt mixed loading boundary conditions for the coupled problem: the structure is clamped and has a given concentration on
\Gamma_{D}
, where the boundary subset
\Gamma_{D}\subset\partial\Omega
is of positive surface measure. In addition, a given traction and zero solute flux are prescribed on
\Gamma_{N}:=\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma_{D}
.

The nonlinear terms.

We assume that
\mathbb{M}(\cdot,\cdot)
is symmetric, positive semi-definite and uniformly bounded in
\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, likewise for
\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\cdot,\cdot)
. More explicitly, for all
\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega),r\in\text{L}^{2}(\Omega)\text{ and }\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
there exists
M\in\mathbb{R}
such that
0<M^{-1}\leq M
with

M^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}\leq\boldsymbol{x}\cdot[\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{w}),r)\boldsymbol{x}]
, and
\boldsymbol{y}\cdot[\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{w}),r)\boldsymbol{x}]\leq M\boldsymbol{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{y}
. In addition, we assume that
\ell:\text{L}^{2}(\Omega)\to\text{L}^{2}(\Omega)
and satisfies
\lVert\ell(\vartheta)\rVert_{0,\Omega}\lesssim\lVert\vartheta\rVert_{0,\Omega}
for all
\vartheta\in\text{L}^{2}(\Omega)
. Moreover, we assume that
\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\ell(\cdot)
are Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constants
L_{\mathbb{M}}
and
L_{\ell}
. Examples of these terms can be found in
[
7
]
for the stress-assisted diffusion and
[
10
]
for the active stress.

2
Weak formulation

In view of the boundary conditions, we define the Hilbert spaces

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega):=\{\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega):\boldsymbol{v}=\boldsymbol{0}\,\text{on }\Gamma_{D}\},

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{N}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega):=\{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbf{H}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega):\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}=0\,\text{on }\Gamma_{N}\},

with the boundary assignment understood in the sense of traces, and consider the following weak formulation: for given
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
,
g\in\text{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, and
\varphi_{D}\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{D})
, find

(\boldsymbol{u},p,\boldsymbol{\zeta},\varphi)\in\mathbf{H}_{D}^{1}(\Omega)\times\text{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{H}_{N}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\times\text{L}^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle 2\mu\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}):\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v})-\int_{\Omega}p\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v}=\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{f}\cdot\boldsymbol{v},\;\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbf{H}_{D}^{1}(\Omega)

(2.1a)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}q\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{u}-\lambda^{-1}\int_{\Omega}pq=\lambda^{-1}\int_{\Omega}\ell(\varphi)q,\;\forall q\in\text{L}^{2}(\Omega)

(2.1b)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{M}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),p)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\zeta}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}+\int_{\Omega}\varphi\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\xi}=\langle\varphi_{D},\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}},\;\forall\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbf{H}_{N}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)

(2.1c)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\psi\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\theta\int_{\Omega}\varphi\psi=-\int_{\Omega}g\psi,\;\forall\psi\in\text{L}^{2}(\Omega).

(2.1d)

Unique solvability with parameter-weighted norms.

Let us adopt the following notation for the functional spaces for displacement and total volumetric stress
\mathbf{V}_{1}:=\mathbf{H}_{D}^{1}(\Omega)
and
Q_{1}=Q_{b_{1}}:=\text{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, equipped with the scaled norms and semi-norms given by

\displaystyle\lVert\boldsymbol{u}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}}^{2}:=2\mu\lVert\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})\rVert_{0,\Omega}^{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\lVert p\rVert_{Q_{1}}^{2}:=\left((2\mu)^{-1}+\lambda^{-1}\right)\lVert p\rVert_{0,\Omega}^{2},

\displaystyle|\boldsymbol{v}|_{1,\mathbf{V}_{1}}^{2}:=2\mu|\boldsymbol{v}|_{1,\Omega}^{2},\hskip 10.00002pt|q|_{1,Q_{b_{1}}}^{2}:=(2\mu)^{-1}|q|_{1,\Omega}^{2}.

On the other hand, let us denote the functional spaces for diffusive flux and concentration as
\mathbf{V}_{2}=\mathbf{H}_{N}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)
and
Q_{2}=Q_{b_{2}}:=\text{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, furnished with the following norms and semi-norms as
\lVert\boldsymbol{\zeta}\rVert_{\mathbb{M},\Omega}^{2}:=\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{M}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),p)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\zeta}\cdot\boldsymbol{\zeta}
,

\displaystyle\lVert\boldsymbol{\zeta}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{2}}^{2}:=\lVert\boldsymbol{\zeta}\rVert_{\mathbb{M},\Omega}^{2}+M\lVert\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}\rVert_{0,\Omega}^{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\lVert\varphi\rVert_{Q_{2}}^{2}:=\left(M^{-1}+\theta\right)\lVert\varphi\rVert_{0,\Omega}^{2},

\displaystyle|\boldsymbol{\xi}|_{1,\mathbf{V}_{2}}^{2}:=M|\boldsymbol{\xi}|_{1,\Omega}^{2},\hskip 10.00002pt|\psi|_{1,Q_{b_{2}}}^{2}:=M^{-1}|\psi|_{1,\Omega}^{2}.

The proposed spaces in conjunction with the extended Babuška–Brezzi–Braess theory for perturbed saddle-point problems and a fixed-point argument show the robust unique solvability of our weak formulation presented in Section
2
. We finish this section by stating the continuous dependence on data (see
[
8
, Section 3]
for details).

Theorem 1
.

Let
W=\{w\in Q_{2}\colon\lVert w\rVert_{Q_{2}}\leq C_{2}\sqrt{M}\lVert\varphi_{D}\rVert_{1/2,\Gamma_{D}}+\lVert g\rVert_{0,\Omega})\}
. Under the assumptions over the non-linear terms, suppose further that
1\leq\lambda
,
1\leq\mu
,
\theta\leq M^{-1}
, and
C_{1}L_{\ell}\sqrt{2\mu}M^{2}C_{2}^{2}L_{\mathbb{M}}(\lVert\varphi_{D}\rVert_{1/2,\Gamma_{D}}+\lVert g\rVert_{0,\Omega})<1.

Then, for
\varphi\in W
there is an unique solution
(\boldsymbol{u},p,\boldsymbol{\zeta},\varphi)\in\mathbf{V}_{1}\times Q_{1}\times\mathbf{V}_{2}\times Q_{2}
of (
2.1
) such that

\displaystyle\lVert(\boldsymbol{u},p)\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}\times Q_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(\lVert F_{1}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}_{1}}+\lVert G^{\varphi}_{1}\rVert_{Q^{\prime}_{1}}\right),

(2.2a)

\displaystyle\lVert(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\varphi)\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{2}\times Q_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\left(\lVert F_{2}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}_{2}}+\lVert G_{2}\rVert_{Q^{\prime}_{2}}\right),

(2.2b)

where the constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
do not depend on the physical parameters.

Virtual element discretisation

Mesh assumptions.

Let
\mathcal{T}^{h}
be a decomposition of
\Omega

P

, let
\mathcal{F}^{h}

f

\mathcal{E}^{h}

\mathcal{T}^{h}

\rho>0

(
M1)

Each polyhedral element

\geq

\rho h_{P}
,

(

Every face
f

\geq

(

Every edge

Polynomial spaces.

Given an integer
k\geq 0
the space of polynomials of degree
\leq k
on
P
is denoted by
\mathcal{P}_{k}(P)
(resp. for faces
f
). The space of the gradients of polynomials of grade
\leq k+1
on
P
is denoted as
\mathcal{G}_{k}(P):=\nabla(\mathcal{P}_{k+1}(P))
with standard notation
\mathcal{P}_{-1}(P)=\{0\}
for
k=-1
. The space
\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\oplus}(P)
denotes the complement of the space
\mathcal{G}_{k}(P)
in the vector polynomial space
(\mathcal{P}_{k}(P))^{3}
, that is,
(\mathcal{P}_{k}(P))^{3}=\mathcal{G}_{k}(P)\oplus\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\oplus}(P)
. In particular, following
[
2
]

with

. Likewise, the space that defines the rotational of polynomials with degree
\leq k+1

\mathcal{R}_{k}(P):=\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\mathcal{P}_{k+1}(P))
where the associated complement space
\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\oplus}(P)
fulfills the property
(\mathcal{P}_{k}(P))^{3}=\mathcal{R}_{k}(P)\oplus\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\oplus}(P)
with
\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\oplus}(P)=\boldsymbol{x}\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(P)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{x}_{P}=(x_{1,P},x_{2,P},x_{3,P})^{\tt t}
denote the barycentre of
P
and let
\mathcal{M}_{k}(P)
be the set of scaled monomials

\mathcal{M}_{k}(P):=\left\{\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}_{P}}{h_{P}}\right)^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}},0\leq|\boldsymbol{\alpha}|\leq k\right\},

where
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})^{\tt t}
is a non-negative multi-index with
|\boldsymbol{\alpha}|=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}
and
\boldsymbol{x}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}x_{3}^{\alpha_{3}}
. In particular, we can take the basis of
\mathcal{G}_{k}(P)
and
\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\oplus}(P)
as
\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\nabla}(P):=\nabla\mathcal{M}_{k+1}(P)\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
and
\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\oplus}(P):=\boldsymbol{x}_{P}\wedge(\mathcal{M}_{k-1}(P))^{3}
, where
(\mathcal{M}_{k}(P))^{3}=\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\nabla}(P)\oplus\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\oplus}(P)
holds. In addition, we introduce the notation
\mathcal{M}_{k\setminus k-1}(P):=\mathcal{M}_{k}(P)\setminus\mathcal{M}_{k-1}(P)
.

The virtual element formulation for the stress-assisted diffusion problem.

The discrete formulation for the fully-coupled problem reads: For given
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
,
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, and
\varphi_{D}\in\text{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{D})
, find

(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\varphi_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}\times Q_{1}^{h,k_{1}}\times\mathbf{V}_{2}^{h,k_{2}}\times Q_{2}^{h,k_{2}}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\biggl[2\mu\int_{P}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}):\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})+S_{1}^{P}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})-\int_{\Omega}p_{h}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\biggr]

\displaystyle=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\int_{P}\overline{\boldsymbol{f}}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\;\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{1}^{h,k_{1}},

(3.3a)

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\left[-\int_{P}q_{h}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\lambda^{-1}\int_{P}p_{h}q_{h}\right]=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\lambda^{-1}\int_{P}\ell(\varphi_{h})q_{h},\;\forall q_{h}\in Q_{1}^{h,k_{1}},

(3.3b)

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\biggl[\int_{P}\mathbb{M}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{h})^{-1}\overline{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}\cdot\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}+S_{2}^{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{h},P}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}-\overline{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}-\overline{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h})+\int_{P}\varphi_{h}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\biggr]

\displaystyle=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\langle\varphi_{D},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}\rangle_{\partial P\cap\Gamma_{D}},\;\forall\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{2}^{h,k_{2}},

(3.3c)

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\left[\int_{P}\psi_{h}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}-\theta\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{h}\psi_{h}\right]=-\sum_{P\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\int_{P}g\psi_{h},\forall\psi_{h}\in Q_{2}^{h,k_{2}},

(3.3d)

with
\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}:=\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
,
\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}:=\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}
,
\overline{\boldsymbol{f}}:=\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{0,k_{1}-2}\boldsymbol{f}
,
\overline{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}:=\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}
,
\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}:=\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}
. The stabilisation terms are any symmetric and positive definite bilinear forms such that in the kernel of
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}
(resp.
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}
) we have

\lVert\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}}\lesssim S_{1}^{E}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\lesssim\lVert\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(P)}
,
\lVert\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\rVert_{\mathbb{M},\Omega}\lesssim S_{2}^{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{p}_{h},E}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})\lesssim\lVert\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\rVert_{\mathbb{M},\Omega}
.

Similarly to the continuous case, the continuous dependence on data for (
3.3
) follows as an extension of
[
8
, Section 4]
(see Theorem
1
), this is a consequence of the stabilisation operators, and the Fortin-like interpolation operators that lead to the discrete inf-sup condition. Note the associated constants
\overline{C}_{1},\overline{C}_{2}
that appear in the discrete well-posedness (independent of the physical parameters) do not need to coincide with
C_{1}
, and
C_{2}
. We finalise by recalling the convergence result of the VE scheme.

Theorem 2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1
. Given
(\boldsymbol{u},{p},\boldsymbol{\zeta},\varphi)\in(\mathbf{H}^{s_{1}+1}(\Omega)\cap\mathbf{V}_{1})\times(\text{H}^{s_{1}}(\Omega)\cap Q_{b_{1}})\times(\mathbf{H}^{s_{2}}\cap\mathbf{V}_{2})\times(\text{H}^{s_{2}}\cap Q_{b_{2}})
,
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},{p}_{h},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\varphi_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}\times Q_{1}^{h,k_{1}}\times\mathbf{V}_{2}^{h,k_{2}}\times Q_{2}^{h,k_{2}}
be the respective solutions of the continuous and discrete problems, with the data satisfying
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbf{H}^{s_{1}-1}\cap\mathbf{Q}_{b_{1}}
and
g\in H^{s_{2}}(\Omega)\cap Q_{b_{2}}
. If
\overline{C}_{1}\sqrt{M}L_{\ell}+\overline{C}_{2}^{2}\sqrt{M^{3}}L_{\mathbb{M}}\sqrt{2\mu}(\lVert\varphi_{D}\rVert_{1/2,\Gamma_{D}}+\lVert g\rVert_{0,\Omega})<1/2.
Then, the total error
\overline{\textnormal{e}}_{h}:=\lVert(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},{p}-{p}_{h},\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\varphi-\varphi_{h})\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}\times Q_{1}\times\mathbf{V}_{2}\times Q_{2}}
decays with the following rate for
s:=\min\left\{s_{1},s_{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\overline{\textnormal{e}}_{h}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{s}(|\boldsymbol{f}|_{s_{1}-1,\mathbf{Q}_{b_{1}}}+|\boldsymbol{u}|_{s_{1}+1,\mathbf{V}_{1}}+|{p}|_{s_{1},Q_{b_{1}}}+|g|_{s_{2},Q_{b_{2}}}+|\boldsymbol{\zeta}|_{s_{2},\mathbf{V}_{2}}+|\varphi|_{s_{2},Q_{b_{2}}}).

4
Numerical results

The simulations presented are implemented in VEM++
[
5
]
for lowest case order (
k_{1}=2,\,k_{2}=1
). The fixed point algorithm based on the analysis in
[
8
]
is optimised to ensure that, in each iteration, only the blocks containing non-linearities are reconstructed, improving the performance of the code. We recall that the computational error is computed as
\overline{\textnormal{e}}_{*}:=\lVert(\boldsymbol{u}-\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{p}-{p}_{h},\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\overline{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},\varphi-\varphi_{h})\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}\times Q_{1}\times\mathbf{V}_{2}\times Q_{2}}
, and the fixed point tolerance is set to
10^{-5}
.

Example 1.

Let
\Omega=(0,1)^{3}
with
\Gamma_{N}=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:x,y,z=1\}
, and
\Gamma_{D}=\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma_{N}
, we set the manufactured solutions as follows

\boldsymbol{u}(x,y,z)\hskip-2.84544pt=\hskip-2.84544pt\left(x^{2}+x\cos(x)\sin(y),y^{2}+x\cos(y)\sin(x),z^{2}+x\cos(x)\cos(y)\right)/5

\varphi(x,y,z)=\cos(\pi y)+\sin(\pi x)+x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}

\mathbb{M}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),p)=10^{-3}[e^{-10^{-4}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits((2\mu\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})-p)\mathbb{I})}]\mathbb{I}

with the adimensional parameters

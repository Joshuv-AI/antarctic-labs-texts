Large deviations at the edge for 1D gases and tridiagonal random matrices at high temperature
Charlie Dworaczek Guera, Ronan Memin

Abstract
We consider a model for a gas of N N confined particles subject to a two-body repulsive interaction, namely the one-dimensional log or Riesz gas. We are interested in the so-called high temperature regime, ie where the inverse temperature β N \beta_{N} scales as N ​ β N → 2 ​ P > 0 N\beta_{N}\rightarrow 2P>0 . We establish, in the log case, a large deviation (LD) principle and moderate deviations estimates for the largest particle x max x_{\mathrm{max}} when appropriately rescaled . Our result is an extension of [ BADG01 , Pak20 ] where such estimates were shown for the largest particle of the β \beta -ensemble respectively at fixed β N = β > 0 \beta_{N}=\beta>0 and β N ≫ N − 1 \beta_{N}\gg N^{-1} . We show that the corresponding rate function is the same as in the case of iid particles. We also provide LD estimates in the Riesz case. Additionally, we consider related models of symmetric tridiagonal random matrices with independent entries having Gaussian tails; for which we establish the LD principle for the top eigenvalue. In a certain specialization of the entries, we recover the result for the largest particle of the log-gas. We show that LD are created by a few entries taking abnormally large values.

Outline of the proof of Theorem

The proof of Theorem
1.8
relies on a truncation procedure on the entries of the matrix, similar to the procedure of
[

]
, showing that only the big entries of the matrix at stake matter for the LD. Let us describe here the case of the (nonperiodic) tridiagonal model

.

For
\varepsilon>0
, we let
T_{N}^{\varepsilon}=(T_{i,j}\mathbf{1}_{\{|J_{i,j}|\geq\varepsilon\sqrt{2\log N}\}})_{1\leq i,j\leq N}
be the truncated matrix with entries larger than
\varepsilon\sqrt{2\log N}
. Then Lemma
3.6
shows that the normalized top eigenvalue of
T^{\varepsilon}_{N}
is
exponentially equivalent
to the one of
T_{N}
: more precisely, let
\mu_{N}=\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(T_{N})/\sqrt{2\log N}
and define
\mu_{N}^{\varepsilon}
similarly. Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\lim_{N}\frac{1}{\log N}\log\mathbb{P}(|\mu_{N}^{\varepsilon}-\mu_{N}|>\varepsilon)=-\infty.

(13)

By
[
DZ09
, Theorem 4.2.16]
, to establish the existence of a LDP for
(\mu_{N})_{N\geq 1}
, it suffices to show that for any
\varepsilon>0
the sequence
(\mu_{N}^{\varepsilon})_{N\geq 1}
satisfies a LDP with rate function
I^{(\varepsilon)}
. The sequence
(\mu_{N})_{N\geq 1}
then satisfies a LDP with rate function
I(x)\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\displaystyle\sup_{\delta>0}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\inf_{z:|z-x|<\delta}I^{(\varepsilon)}(z)
. Now, because we assume the entries of

to have Gaussian tails, a lot of entries in

vanish. In particular,

can be assumed to be a block diagonal matrix,
T^{\varepsilon}_{N}=\mathrm{diag}(B_{1},\ldots,B_{k_{N}})
where the blocks
(B_{i})_{1\leq i\leq k_{N}}
are independent and can be assumed – at the scale of LD – to be of finite size (
ie
independent from
N
). The top eigenvalue of

is then

\lambda_{\mathrm{\max}}^{\varepsilon}=\max_{1\leq i\leq k_{N}}\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(B_{i}).

(14)

By Lemma
3.5
we then see that
\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(B_{i})
has Gaussian tail. From this fact, decomposition (
14
), and the exponential approximation (
13
), we will deduce that for any
\varepsilon>0

, thus concluding for the LD of

.

2.1.1

We start the proof by showing the upper bound in the case

Proposition 2.1
(Upper bound: right-deviation).

For all
x\geq 1
,

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log N}\log\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(\frac{x_{\mathrm{max}}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}\geq x\right)\leq-I_{\alpha}(x)\,.

(15)

Furthermore, let
-\alpha^{-1}<\gamma<1-\alpha^{-1}
and
x>0
, the following estimate holds:

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{1}{(\log N)^{1-\gamma-\alpha^{-1}}}\log\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(x_{\mathrm{max}}\geq(\log N)^{1/\alpha}+\dfrac{x}{(\log N)^{\gamma}}\right)\leq\dfrac{-x}{\alpha}

(16)

Proof
.

By
[
Lam21
, Proposition 2.1]
, there exists
C>0
, such that we have the following estimate for the density of the first marginal
\rho_{N}(u)
for all
u\in\mathbb{R}
:

\rho_{N}(u)\leq Ce^{-V(x)+2P\log(1+|u|)\mathbf{1}_{g=-\log}}\,.

Thus let
x\geq 1
, for (
15
), by a union bound we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(\frac{x_{\mathrm{max}}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}\geq x\right)

\displaystyle\leq N\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(\frac{x_{1}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}\geq x\right)

\displaystyle\leq NC\int_{x(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}^{+\infty}e^{-|u|^{\alpha}-\varphi(u)+2P\log(1+u)\mathbf{1}_{g=-\log}}du

\displaystyle\leq N(\log N)^{1/\alpha}C\int_{x}^{+\infty}e^{-(\log N)s^{\alpha}-\varphi((\log N)^{1/\alpha}s)+2P\log(1+(\log N)^{1/\alpha}s)\mathbf{1}_{g=-\log}}ds.

Setting
\phi(x)\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}-\varphi(x)+2P\log(1+x)\mathbf{1}_{g=-\log}
and since
\partial_{s}\left(e^{-(\log N)s^{\alpha}+\phi((\log N)^{1/\alpha}s)}\right)
is negative, for
N
large enough, by Assumption
1.1
, there exists
C>0
, such that for all
s\geq x

\displaystyle e^{-(\log N)s^{\alpha}+\phi((\log N)^{1/\alpha}s)}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{\alpha s^{\alpha-1}\log N-(\log N)^{1/\alpha}\phi^{\prime}(s(\log N)^{1/\alpha})}\partial_{s}\left(e^{-(\log N)s^{\alpha}+\phi((\log N)^{1/\alpha}s)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{-C}{s^{\alpha-1}\log N}\partial_{s}\left(e^{-(\log N)s^{\alpha}+\phi((\log N)^{1/\alpha}s)}\right).

Thus, we obtain the following bound, using that
\alpha\geq 1
:

\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(\frac{x_{\mathrm{max}}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}\geq x\right)\leq C\frac{N(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}{x^{\alpha-1}\log N}\exp\Big(-(\log N)x^{\alpha}+\phi((\log N)^{1/\alpha}x)\Big),

(17)

Taking the logarithm and letting
N\rightarrow\infty
, yields the conclusion by Assumption
1.1
. The proof of (
16
) is obtained in the exact same way.

\square

2.1.2
Exponential tightness

Corollary 2.2
(Exponential tightness).

The sequence
\left(\dfrac{x_{\max}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}\right)_{N}
is exponentially tight, ie there exists a sequence
(K_{M})_{M\geq 1}
of compacts of
\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log N}\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(\frac{x_{\max}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}\in K_{M}^{c}\right)=-\infty\,.

(18)

Proof
.

Indeed let
M\geq 1
,

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log N}\log\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(\frac{x_{\max}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}\in[0,M]^{c}\right)=\max\left\{a,b_{M}\right\}

where

a\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log N}\log\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(\frac{x_{\max}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}<0\right)

and

b_{M}\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log N}\log\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(\frac{x_{\max}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}>M\right)\,.

But
a=-\infty
by the LDP for the empirical measure which is at speed
N
, see
[
GZ19
]
, and

b_{M}\leq-I_{\alpha}(M)\underset{M\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}-\infty

by Proposition
2.1
, proving (
18
) with
K_{M}=[0,M]
.

\square

2.1.3
Upper bound: left deviation.

We now prove an upper bound on the probability of a left-deviation of
x_{\max}
. Our strategy relies on a comparison with the edge of a system of iid particles distributed according to
\bigotimes^{N}\mu_{\mathrm{eq}}
where
\mu_{\mathrm{eq}}
is the unique minimizer of (
3
). In the latter case, the precise asymptotic of a left deviation is known.
Prior to that, we need to understand how the function
U^{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}
behaves under small perturbations. The following lemma gives few useful properties about its derivative.

Lemma 2.3
.

The function
U^{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}
is differentiable on
\mathbb{R}
and its derivative is a bounded function on
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof
.

The proof is done for the log case in
[
DGM24
, Lemma 2.3]
. For the Riesz case,

[U^{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}]^{\prime}(x)=-s\fint_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}(y)\mathrm{sgn}(x-y)dy}{|x-y|^{s+1}}

where
\fint
stands for the Cauchy principal value. For
x\in\mathbb{R},
, we have

\Big|\int_{|x-y|>1}\dfrac{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}(y)\mathrm{sgn}(x-y)dy}{|x-y|^{s+1}}\Big|\leq 1.

And finally, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\Big|\int_{\varepsilon<|x-y|<1}\dfrac{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}(y)\mathrm{sgn}(x-y)dy}{|x-y|^{s+1}}\Big|\leq\Big|\int_{|x-y|<1}\dfrac{[\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}(y)-\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}(x)]\mathrm{sgn}(x-y)dy}{|x-y|^{s+1}}\Big|\\
+\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}(x)\Big|\int_{\varepsilon<|z|<1}\dfrac{\mathrm{sgn}(z)dy}{|z|^{s+1}}\Big|\leq\dfrac{2}{1-s}\|\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}.

Above, we used that the integral on the second line is equal to zero. Hence taking
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we obtain that
[U^{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}]^{\prime}
is bounded.

\square

We are now able to compare the law of the edge of
\mathbb{P}_{N}
to the one of
\mu_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\otimes N}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
x>0
,

\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(\frac{x_{\mathrm{max}}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}<(1-x)^{1/\alpha}\right)\leq e^{\mathfrak{R}_{N}}\mu_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\otimes N}\left(\frac{x_{\mathrm{max}}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}<(1-x)^{1/\alpha}\right)

where
\mathfrak{R}_{N}=O(\log N)
for the case
g=-\log
and
O(N^{\frac{2s}{1+s}})
for the case
g=|.|^{-s}
.

Proof
.

We prove this bound by bounding the density
p_{N}
pointwise. To do so, we use energy estimates. Since
\mathcal{E}(\widehat{\mu_{N}})=+\infty
for all
\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we need to approximate it by an absolutely continuous measure with respect to Lebesgue. As in
[
DGM24
, Section 3.1]
, we introduce
\widetilde{\mu}_{N}
, the regularization of
\hat{\mu}_{N}
by setting, for any
(x_{i})_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the following: we first apply a permutation
\sigma
which orders the particles
x_{\sigma(1)}\leq\ldots\leq x_{\sigma(N)}
and then we define
(y_{i})_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
as follows:

y_{1}\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}x_{1},\hskip 28.45274pt\forall k\in\llbracket 0,N-1\rrbracket,\,\,\,y_{k+1}\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}y_{k}+\max\{x_{\sigma(k+1)}-x_{\sigma(k)},N^{-b}\}.

Finally, we define
\widetilde{\mu}_{N}
by

\widetilde{\mu}_{N}\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\lambda_{N^{-a}}\ast\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{y_{\sigma^{-1}(i)}}=\lambda_{N^{-a}}\ast\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{y_{i}},

(19)

where
\lambda_{N^{-a}}
is the uniform measure on
[0,N^{-a}]
. When
g=-\log
, we take
a=5
and
b=3
, while for the case
g=|.|^{-s}
, we take

.

By using
[
DGM24
, Proposition 1.6]
,
\mathcal{Z}_{N}\geq e^{N\mathcal{E}(\mu_{\mathrm{eq}})+c}
for some constant

depending on
V,P
and
s
in the Riesz case. Using that for all

one has
|x_{i}-x_{j}|\leq|y_{i}-y_{j}|
and that

is non-increasing, we have:

\log p_{N}(\underline{x})\leq-N\mathcal{E}(\mu_{\mathrm{eq}})-\dfrac{\beta_{N}}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}g(x_{i}-x_{j})-\sum_{i=1}^{N}V(x_{i})+O(1).

Using that for all
x\neq 0
and
|h|\leq\dfrac{|x|}{2}

|g(x+h)-g(x)|\leq C|g^{\prime}(x)|.|h|
for a uniform constant

, and

|g(y_{i}+u-y_{j}-v)-g(y_{i}-y_{j})|\leq C|u-v|.|g^{\prime}(y_{i}-y_{j})|\leq C.N^{-a}|g^{\prime}(N^{-b})|\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}A_{N}.

Above, we used that
|y_{i}-y_{j}|>N^{-b}
for all
i\neq j
. The RHS is a
O(N^{-2})
in the log case and
O(N^{\frac{2s}{1+s}})
in the Riesz case. Now summing over
i\neq j
and integrating against
dudv
, we obtain:

-\dfrac{\beta_{N}}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}g(y_{i}-y_{j})\leq CN^{2}\beta_{N}A_{N}-\dfrac{\beta_{N}}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}\iint_{[0,N^{-a}]}g(y_{i}-y_{j}+u-v)d\lambda_{N^{-a}}(u)d\lambda_{N^{-a}}(v)\\
\leq CN^{2}\beta_{N}A_{N}-\dfrac{\beta_{N}N^{2}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}g(x-y)d\widetilde{\mu}_{N}(x)d\widetilde{\mu}_{N}(y)+B_{N}

(20)

where

B_{N}\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}N\dfrac{\beta_{N}}{2}\iint_{[0,N^{-a}]}g(u-v)d\lambda_{N^{-a}}(u)d\lambda_{N^{-a}}(v)

O(\log N)
in the log case and

Finally we use the equation satisfied by

\log\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}(x)=-V(x)-2PU^{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}(x)+C_{\mathrm{eq}}

and the fact that
\mathcal{E}(\widetilde{\mu}_{N})-\mathcal{E}(\mu_{\mathrm{eq}})>0

to deduce that

\log p_{N}(\underline{x})\leq-N^{2}\beta_{N}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}g(x-y)d(\widetilde{\mu}_{N}(x)-\widehat{\mu}_{N})(x)d\mu_{\mathrm{eq}}(y)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}(x_{i})+O(B_{N})\\

Proposition 2.8
(Lower bound).

\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{1}{\log N}\log\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(\dfrac{x_{\mathrm{max}}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}\in(x-\delta,x+\delta)\right)\geq-I_{\alpha}(x).

\dfrac{1}{\log N}\log\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(\Big|\dfrac{x_{\mathrm{max}}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}-1\Big|<\delta\right)\geq\dfrac{1}{\log N}\log\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(\Big|\dfrac{x_{\mathrm{max}}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}-1\Big|<\dfrac{1}{\log N}\right)\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0

I_{N}\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}(-\infty,E_{N}].

By noticing that

\biguplus_{i=1}^{N}\left\{\Big|\dfrac{x_{i}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}-x\Big|<\delta,\;\forall j\neq i,\ x_{j}\in I_{N}\right\}\subset\left\{\Big|\dfrac{x_{\mathrm{max}}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}-x\Big|<\delta\right\},

by Lemma
2.7
, we have by exchangeability

\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(\Big|\dfrac{x_{\mathrm{max}}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}-x\Big|<\delta\right)\geq N\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(\Big|\dfrac{x_{1}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}-x\Big|<\delta,\;\forall j>1,\ x_{j}\in I_{N}\right)\\
=N\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{N-1}^{\beta_{N}}}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{\beta_{N}}}\int_{(x-\delta)(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}^{(x+\delta)(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}e^{-V(x_{1})}\int_{I_{N}^{N-1}}e^{-\beta_{N}\sum_{i=2}^{N}g(x_{1}-x_{i})}d\mathbb{P}^{\beta_{N}}_{N-1}(x_{2},\ldots,x_{N})dx_{1}\\
=2\delta(\log N)^{1/\alpha}\mathbb{P}_{N-1}^{\beta_{N}}\left(\forall j\geq 2,x_{j}\in I_{N}\right)N\dfrac{\mathcal{Z}_{N-1}^{\beta_{N}}}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{\beta_{N}}}\\
\times\int_{x-\delta}^{x+\delta}e^{-V(s(\log N)^{1/\alpha})}\int_{I_{N}^{N-1}}(\log N)^{(N-1)\beta_{N}/\alpha}.e^{-\beta_{N}\sum_{i=2}^{N}g\left(s-\frac{x_{i}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}\right)}d\mu(s,x_{2}\ldots,x_{N}),

where the probability measure
\mu
is supported on
[x-\delta,x+\delta]\times I_{N}^{N-1}
defined by

d\mu(s,\ldots,x_{N})\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\mathbf{1}_{|s-x|<\delta}\dfrac{ds}{2\delta}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\cap_{j}x_{j}\in I_{N}}\frac{d\mathbb{P}_{N-1}^{\beta_{N}}(x_{2},\dots,x_{N})}{\mathbb{P}_{N-1}^{\beta_{N}}\left(\forall j\geq 2,x_{j}\in I_{N}\right)}.

By
[
Lam21
, Corollary 2.4]
, it holds that
\dfrac{\mathcal{Z}_{N-1}^{\beta_{N}}}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{\beta_{N}}}\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}C
for some
C>0
and by (
28
) there exists
p>0
such that

\mathbb{P}_{N-1}^{\beta_{N}}\left(\forall i\in\llbracket 2,N\rrbracket,x_{i}\in I_{N}\right)\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}p.

Taking the logarithm and applying Jensen’s inequality, we obtain:

\log\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(\Big|\dfrac{x_{\mathrm{max}}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}-x\Big|<\delta\right)\geq\log N+o(\log N)\\
+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[-s^{\alpha}\log N-\varphi(s(\log N)^{1/\alpha})-\beta_{N}\sum_{i=2}^{N}g\left(s-\dfrac{x_{i}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}\right)\right]d\mu(s,x_{2},\ldots,x_{N})\\
\geq\log N+o(\log N)\\
-\log N(x+\delta)^{\alpha}-\int_{x-\delta}^{x+\delta}\varphi(s(\log N)^{1/\alpha})\frac{ds}{2\delta}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\beta_{N}\sum_{i=2}^{N}g\left(s-\frac{x_{i}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}\right)d\mu(s,x_{2},\ldots,x_{N}).

Since by Assumptions
1.1
we have for
\delta>0
fixed,

\frac{1}{2\delta}\int_{x-\delta}^{x+\delta}\varphi(s(\log N)^{1/\alpha})ds=o(\log N),

we thus have

\dfrac{1}{\log N}\log\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(\dfrac{x_{\mathrm{max}}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}\in(x-\delta,x+\delta)\right)\geq-I_{\alpha}(x)+o(1)\\
\dfrac{-1}{\log N}\int_{[x-\delta,x+\delta]\times I_{N}^{N-1}}\beta_{N}\sum_{i=2}^{N}g\left(s-\frac{x_{i}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}\right)d\mu(s,x_{2},\ldots,x_{N}).

For
\delta
small enough, it holds
x-1-2\delta>0
, and for
N
big enough
\frac{E_{N}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}<1+\delta
. Hence for all
s\in(x-\delta,x+\delta)
,
y\in I_{N}
we have
|s-y/(\log N)^{1/\alpha}|\geq x-1-2\delta
, therefore

\int_{[x,x+\delta]\times I_{N}^{N-1}}\beta_{N}\sum_{i=2}^{N}\log\left|s-\frac{x_{i}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}\right|d\mu(s,x_{2},\ldots,x_{N})\geq-(N-1)\beta_{N}g(x-1-2\delta).

We conclude that

\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{1}{\log N}\log\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(\dfrac{x_{\mathrm{max}}}{(\log N)^{1/\alpha}}\in(x-\delta,x+\delta)\right)\geq 1-(x+\delta)^{\alpha},

and letting
\delta\rightarrow 0
yields the conclusion.

\square

Remark 3.2

The upper bound argument is ultimately based on the fact that if

The following lemma will allow us to deduce the LD lower bound, and to control the spectrum of a matrix with small entries. In the following, we will take the indices of the entries modulo

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
J=T
or

, for arbitrary

\max_{i}(a_{i})\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(J)\leq\rho(J)\leq\max_{i}\left(|a_{i}|+|b_{i-1}|+|b_{i}|\right).

Proof
.

For the lower bound, write

For the upper bound on

\mathrm{Sp}(J)\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\overline{B}\left(J_{ii},\sum_{j\neq i}|J_{ij}|\right)\,,

|\lambda_{\max}|\leq|\lambda_{\max}-a_{i}|+|a_{i}|\leq|b_{i-1}|+|b_{i}|+|a_{i}|\,.

\square

Lemma 3.5
(Gaussian tail estimate).

Let
(X_{i})_{i\geq 1}
be a sequence of independent variables with uniform Gaussian tails. Let
d\geq 1
and
\mathcal{I}_{d}
be the set of subsets
I\subset\llbracket 1,+\infty\llbracket
such that
\mathrm{Card}(I)\leq d

be the euclidean norm of
(X_{i})_{i\in I}
. Then, the family
(Z_{I})_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{d}}
has uniform Gaussian tail.

Proof
(of Lemma
3.5
).

We start with the case
d=2
. Let
i_{1},i_{2}\in I
and
X:=X_{i_{1}}
,
Y:=X_{i_{2}}
. We want to show that the variable
Z:=(X^{2}+Y^{2})^{1/2}
satisfies as
x\rightarrow+\infty

-\log\mathbb{P}(Z>x)=x^{2}/2+o(x^{2}),

where the remainder is independent of
(i_{1},i_{2})\in I^{2}
. Let
n\geq 1
and
x>0
, we decompose

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|X|>x)\leq\mathbb{P}(Z^{2}>x^{2})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(|X|>x)+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{P}\left(X^{2}\in\left[\frac{k}{n}x^{2},\frac{k+1}{n}x^{2}\right],Y^{2}>x^{2}\left(1-\frac{k+1}{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\mathbb{P}(|X|>x)+S_{n}.

By assumption,
\log\mathbb{P}(|X|>x)=-x^{2}/2+o(x^{2}),
where the remainder is independent of
i_{1}\in I
. We now establish that
S_{n}\leq e^{-x^{2}/2+o(x^{2})}
in the previous decomposition, with
o(x^{2})
independent of
i_{1},i_{2}
. We have

S_{n}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{P}(X^{2}\geq kx^{2}/n,Y^{2}\geq x^{2}(1-(k+1)/n))=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{P}(X^{2}\geq kx^{2}/n)\mathbb{P}(Y^{2}\geq x^{2}(1-(k+1)/n))\\
\leq n\max_{0\leq k\leq n-1}\mathbb{P}(X^{2}\geq kx^{2}/n)\mathbb{P}(Y^{2}\geq x^{2}(1-(k+1)/n)).

\mathbb{P}(|X|>x)=e^{-x^{2}/2+o(x^{2})}\leq\mathbb{P}(Z>x)\leq e^{-x^{2}/2+o(x^{2})}+e^{-x^{2}/2+o(x^{2})}=e^{-x^{2}/2+o(x^{2})},

where the terms

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\ell}}B_{\ell}(i,i)^{2}+2B_{\ell}(i,i+1)^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\ell}}J^{\varepsilon}_{N}(i_{\ell-1}+i,i_{\ell-1}+i)^{2}+2J^{\varepsilon}_{N}(i_{\ell-1}+i,i_{\ell-1}+i+1)^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}J_{N}(i_{\ell-1}+i,i_{\ell-1}+i)^{2}+2J_{N}(i_{\ell-1}+i,i_{\ell-1}+i+1)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}Z_{\ell}^{(d)},

where we used that
d_{\ell}\leq d
, and that

. Thus

\mathbb{P}(\mu_{N}^{\varepsilon}>x,d_{\max}(J_{N}^{\varepsilon})\leq d)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{1\leq\ell\leq k_{N}^{\varepsilon}}Z_{\ell}^{(d)}>x\sqrt{2\log N}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{1\leq\ell\leq N}Z_{\ell}^{(d)}>x\sqrt{2\log N}\right),

where we used
k_{N}^{\varepsilon}\leq N
and for

Z_{\ell}^{(d)}
standard Gaussian.

has Gaussian tail independent of

by the fact that each

, we conclude by an union bound that for any fixed

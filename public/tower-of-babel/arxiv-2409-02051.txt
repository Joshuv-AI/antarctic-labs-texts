Introduction

In this paper, we work with a
p
-adic field
K
. More precisely, let
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be a complete discrete valuation ring of mixed characteristic with fraction field
K
and perfect residue field
k
of characteristic
p
.

Recently, based on the pioneering work of Bhatt and Lurie in
[
BL22a
]
and
[
BL22b
]
, Johannes Anschütz, Ben Heuer and Arthur-César Le Bras studied the Hodge-Tate crystals over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
in
[
AHB22
]
via a stacky approach. Actually, as the category of Hodge-Tate crystals on
(\mathcal{O}_{K})_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}
is equivalent to the category of vector bundles on the Hodge-Tate stack
\mathrm{Spf}(\mathcal{O}_{K})^{\operatorname{HT}}
, it suffices to study the later. Under such a stacky perspective, the unramified case (i.e.
\mathcal{O}_{K}=W(k)
) was already treated in
[
BL22a
]
, while the general description was obtained in
[
AHB22
]
. More notably, such a stacky approach naturally leads to results for non-abelian coefficients, In particular,
[
AHB22
]
obtained a non-abelian version of
[
GMW23a
]
.

Then it is natural to ask whether we could study
\operatorname{Vect}((\mathcal{O}_{K})_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}},\mathcal{O}_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}/\mathcal{I}_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}^{n})
for any
n\geq 1
via a stacky approach and we would like to give a partial answer in this paper. Actually, we construct
\WCart_{n}
, which are certain closed substacks of the Cartier-Witt stack
\WCart
and could be viewed as nilpotent thickenings of
\WCart_{1}=\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}
, satisfying that quasi-coherent complexes on
\WCart_{n}
parametrizes prismatic crystals on
(\mathbb{Z}_{p})_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}
with coefficients in
\mathcal{O}_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}/\mathcal{I}_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}^{n}
. Moreover, we end up with a characterization of quasi-coherent complexes on
\WCart_{n}
for
n\leq p
, generalizing the description of that for
\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}
(i.e.
n=1
) in
[
BL22a
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(

)
.

Assume that

•

However, for

Remark 1.2
.

Our results are new when

. Related results for the isogeny category (i.e. with
p
-inverted) of vector bundles on
\WCart_{n}
were obtained in
[

]

[

]

Remark 1.3
.

It turns out that the key step is to construct the Sen operator

on

[

Remark 1.4
.

Actually there is a geometric explanation for

Remark 1.5
.

One might wonder what happens when

On the other hand, for any

-connection

In summary, for

1.1.
A new perspective of the
p
-adic Riemann-Hilbert correspondence

As
[
AHB22
]
reinterpreted the
p
-adic Simpson correspondence from the perspective via Hodge-Tate stack,
Theorem

1.6
gives some new perspective on the
p
-adic Riemann-Hilbert correspondence. We explain it in more detail, following
[
AHB22
, Section 1.2]

For

an integral perfectoid ring,
\mathrm{Spf}(R)^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{n}
is naturally isomorphic to

\mathrm{Spf}({A_{\mathrm{inf}}}(R)/I^{n})
and
\widetilde{\mathrm{Spf}(R^{+})_{n}^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}
is naturally identified with
\mathrm{Spf}({A_{\mathrm{inf}}}(R^{+})[[\frac{I}{p}]]/(\frac{I}{p})^{n})
. Take
R=\mathcal{O}_{C}
, the natural morphism
\mathrm{Spf}(\mathcal{O}_{C})\to X=\mathrm{Spf}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
induces a
G_{K}
-equivariant morphism

\mathrm{Spf}({A_{\mathrm{inf}}}/I^{n})\rightarrow X_{n}^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}},\qquad\mathrm{Spf}({A_{\mathrm{inf}}}[[\frac{I}{p}]]/(\frac{I}{p})^{n})\to\widetilde{X^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{[n]}}.

Then by pullback we get canonical functors

\mathrm{Perf}(X_{n}^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}})[\frac{1}{p}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{n}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{Perf}(\widetilde{X^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{[n]}})[\frac{1}{p}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha_{n,K}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{Perf}(\mathrm{Spa}(K)_{v},{\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}_{,n}).

In summary, we have the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Perf}(\widetilde{X^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{[n]}})[\frac{1}{p}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Perf}(\mathrm{Spa}(K)_{v},{\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}_{,n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Perf}(\mathrm{MIC}(\mathfrak{S}/E^{n}[1/p]))}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{n,K}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta_{n}}

Notice that for any
\mathcal{E}\in\mathrm{Perf}(X^{\operatorname{HT}})[\frac{1}{p}]
,
\beta_{1}(\mathcal{E})=(\rho^{*}(\mathscr{E})[\frac{1}{p}],\Theta_{\mathscr{E}})
with

a perfect complex over

, for any

, we then let

In this way, we obtain a sequence of objects

Consequently, given the full faithfulness of

2.
Nilpotent thickenings of the Hodge-Tate stack

2.1.
Certain locus inside the Cartier-Witt stack

Motivated by Bhatt and Lurie’s definition of
\WCart^{\mathrm{HT}}
(see
[

]

Definition 2.1
.

Let

be a
p
-nilpotent commutative ring. Fix a positive integer

\WCart^{\mathrm{HT}}_{n}(R)
denote the full subcategory of
\WCart(R)

spanned by those Cartier-Witt divisors

for which the composite map
I^{\otimes n}\xrightarrow{\alpha^{\otimes n}}W(R)\twoheadrightarrow R

When

3.
Sen operators on truncated Cartier-Witt stacks

3.1.
Sen operator

In this subsection we assume
\mathfrak{S}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[u]]=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\lambda]]
for
E(u)=\lambda=u-p
. Then
(\mathfrak{S},\lambda)
defines a transversal object in
(\mathbb{Z}_{p})_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}
, hence
\rho_{n}:\mathrm{Spf}(\mathfrak{S}/\lambda^{n})\rightarrow\WCart_{n}
defined in
Remark

2.4
is faithfully flat by
[
BL22a
, Corollary 3.2.10]
. Later we will omit the subscript
n
and just write
\rho
if no confusion.

In this section we aim to classify quasi-coherent complexes on
\WCart_{n}
for
n
no larger than
p
by realizing them as objects in
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}(\mathfrak{S}/\lambda^{n})
equipped with an additional Sen operator satisfying certain nilpotence condition.

For this purpose, the key step is to construct a Sen operator on
\rho^{*}\mathscr{E}
for
\mathscr{E}\in\Qcoh(\WCart_{n})
extending the Sen operator on the Hodge-Tate locus constructed in
[
BL22a
, Section 3.5]
, which will be based on the following key lemma:

Lemma 3.1
.

Fix
n\leq p
. For
R=\mathfrak{S}/\lambda^{n}[\epsilon]/\epsilon^{2}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\lambda,\epsilon]]/(\lambda^{n},\epsilon^{2})
, there exists
b
in
W(R)^{\times}
such that the following holds:

•

\tilde{g}(\lambda)=\tilde{f}(\lambda)\cdot b
, where
\tilde{g}
is the unique
\delta
-ring map such that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W(R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\lambda]]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{g}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda\mapsto(1+\epsilon)\lambda}

and
\tilde{f}
is defined similarly with the bottom line in the above diagram replaced with the identity map.

Proof.

First we notice that
\tilde{g}(\lambda)
is uniquely characterized by the following two properties:

•

p_{0}(\tilde{g}(\lambda))=(1+\epsilon)\lambda
.

•

\varphi(\tilde{g}(\lambda))=(\tilde{g}(\lambda)+p)^{p}-p
.

In the following, for simplicity, we will denote
\tilde{g}(\lambda)
as
\tilde{\lambda}
and
\tilde{f}(\lambda)
simply as
\lambda
. We will show the existence of
b=(b_{0},b_{1},\ldots)\in W(R)
(
b_{i}\in R
) satisfying the desired properties. As
R
is
p
-torsion free, the ghost map is injective, hence the identity
\tilde{\lambda}=b\lambda
is equivalent to that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\forall m\geq 0,w_{m}(\tilde{\lambda})=w_{m}(b)\cdot w_{m}(\lambda).

Here
w_{m}
denotes the
n
-th ghost map.

Recall that
\varphi(\lambda)=(\lambda+p)^{p}-p
, hence by induction we have the following equality in
R
:

(3.2)

\displaystyle w_{m}(\lambda)=(w_{0}(\lambda)+p)^{p^{m}}-p=(\lambda+p)^{p^{m}}-p=p^{p^{m}}-p+\sum_{i=1}^{p^{m}}\binom{p^{m}}{i}p^{p^{m}-i}\lambda^{i}.

Similarly, we have that

(3.3)

\displaystyle w_{m}(\tilde{\lambda})=(w_{0}(\tilde{\lambda})+p)^{p^{m}}-p=((1+\epsilon)\lambda+p)^{p^{m}}-p=p^{p^{m}}-p+\sum_{i=1}^{p^{m}}(1+i\epsilon)\binom{p^{m}}{i}p^{p^{m}-i}\lambda^{i}.

Take
m=0
in
Eq.

4.1
, we want
b_{0}
such that
\lambda b_{0}=(1+\epsilon)\lambda
, hence we could just take
b_{0}=1+\epsilon
.

Suppose
m\geq 1
and we have determined
b_{0},\cdots,b_{m-1}
such that
Eq.

3.1
holds for non-negative integers no larger than
m-1
. Moreover, we assume that
b_{i}~(1\leq i\leq m-1)
is divisible by
\epsilon
. Then we aim to find
b_{m}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}c_{m,j}\lambda^{j}\in R
such that
Eq.

3.1
holds for
m
. For this, first notice that

(3.4)

\displaystyle w_{m}(b)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}p^{i}b_{i}^{p^{m-i}}=b_{0}^{p^{m}}+p^{m}b_{m}=(1+\epsilon p^{m})+p^{m}b_{m}.

Here the second identity holds as
b_{i}^{p}=0
by our assumption that
b_{i}
is divisible by
\epsilon
for
1\leq i\leq m-1
.

Combining
Eq.

3.2
,
Eq.

3.3
and
Eq.

3.4
, we see that

can be reinterpreted as

(3.5)

\displaystyle p^{p^{m}}-p+\sum_{i=1}^{p^{m}}(1+i\epsilon)\binom{p^{m}}{i}p^{p^{m}-i}\lambda^{i}=(p^{p^{m}}-p+\sum_{i=1}^{p^{m}}\binom{p^{m}}{i}p^{p^{m}-i}\lambda^{i})((1+\epsilon p^{m})+p^{m}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}c_{m,i}\lambda^{i}).

As
n\leq p
, by comparing the coefficients of
\lambda^{i}
(
1\leq i\leq p-1
) on both sides, this equality holds if we could pick

and

for each

such that

(3.6)

(p^{p^{m}}-p)p^{m}c_{m,i}=-i\epsilon\binom{p^{m}}{i}p^{p^{m}-i}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\binom{p^{m}}{j}p^{p^{m}-j}p^{m}c_{m,i-j}

We claim that there is a unique sequence
\{c_{m,i}=\epsilon d_{m,i}\}_{1\leq i\leq p-1}
with

satisfying

3.6

.

To prove the claim, we argue by induction on

. We aim to define
c_{m,i}
inductively on

such that
Eq.

3.6
is satisfied. The divisibility by
\epsilon
easily follows from induction and we only need to verify that
v_{p}(d_{m,i})=p^{m}-i-1
. Take

3.6
implies that

Consequently

Our construction of

implies that

Proposition 3.15

[

Via the dictionary transferring prismatic crystals to quasi-coherent complexes on truncated Cartier-Witt stacks by

with

.

Taking global sections then yields the fiber sequence

\begin{split}\mathrm{R}\Gamma(\mathrm{WCart}_{n},\mathcal{F}\otimes(i_{n-1,*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{WCart}_{n-1}}\{1\}))\rightarrow&\mathrm{R}\Gamma(\mathrm{WCart}_{n},\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{WCart}_{n}})\\

since the closed immersion

is an affine morphism. Consequently, the functor

and

commute with colomits (by induction). Similarly, one can prove that the functor

Proposition 3.17

There is a unique isomorphism between
\WCart_{n}
and
\operatorname{Sym}^{<n}_{\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}}\mathcal{O}\{1\}
as stacks over
\mathrm{Spf}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, here the later is the relative stack over
\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}
formed by the coherent sheaf
\operatorname{Sym}^{<n}(\mathcal{O}\{1\})
, the quotient of the symmetric algebra of
\mathcal{O}\{1\}
by the ideal of elements of degree at least
n
.

Proof.

For any
n
, unwinding the definition of
\WCart_{n}
via
Remark

2.3
, we see that the ideal sheaf corresponding to the closed embedding
\WCart_{n}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{WCart}_{n+1}
is supported on
\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}
and is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}\{n\}
. Moreover,
\mathrm{WCart}_{n+1}
is a square-zero thickening of
\WCart_{n}
living over the

-th order neighbourhood of

inside
[\widehat{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}]
. On the other hand, such square-zero thickenings are classified by

\Ext^{1}_{\WCart_{n}}(\mathbb{L}_{\WCart_{n}/[\mathrm{Spf}(\mathbb{Z}[[t]]/t^{n})/\mathbb{G}_{m}]},\mathcal{O}\{n\})=\Ext^{1}_{\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}}(\mathbb{L}_{\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}/B\mathbb{G}_{m}},\mathcal{O}\{n\}),

where the identity comes from the adjunction of the closed embedding above and base change for the cotangent complex.

Now it suffices to show that
\mathrm{RHom}_{\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}}(\mathbb{L}_{\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}/B\mathbb{G}_{m}},\mathcal{O}\{n\})=0
for
1\leq n\leq p-1
, which will also give the uniqueness of the above isomorphism. By considering
\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}\to B\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathrm{Spf}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, we have the following exact triangle of cotangent complexes

\mathbb{L}_{B\mathbb{G}_{m}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{B\mathbb{G}_{m}}}\mathcal{O}_{\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}}\to\mathbb{L}_{\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\to\mathbb{L}_{\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}/\mathbb{G}_{m}}.

We claim that both
\mathrm{RHom}_{\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}}(\mathbb{L}_{B\mathbb{G}_{m}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{B\mathbb{G}_{m}}}\mathcal{O}_{\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}},\mathcal{O}\{n\})
and
\mathrm{RHom}_{\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}}(\mathbb{L}_{\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}},\mathcal{O}\{n\})
are zero when
1\leq n\leq p-1
, leading to the desired vanishing of
\mathrm{RHom}_{\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}}(\mathbb{L}_{\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}/B\mathbb{G}_{m}},\mathcal{O}\{n\})
. We will prove
\mathrm{RHom}_{\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}}(\mathbb{L}_{\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}},\mathcal{O}\{n\})=0
for
1\leq n\leq p-1
in detail, and the vanishing of the former term will follow for a similar reason. For this purpose, first recall that for any flat group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-scheme
G
, the cotangent complex
\mathbb{L}_{BG/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
is identified with
e^{*}\mathbb{L}_{G/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}[-1]
equipped with the adjoint action of G, where
e:\mathrm{Spf}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to G
is the unit section. Applying the above discussion to
\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}
, which is isomorphic to the classifying stack of
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\sharp}
by
[
BL22a
, Theorem 3.4.13]
, we see that
\mathbb{L}_{\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\cong\mathbb{L}_{B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\sharp}}=e^{*}\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\sharp}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}[-1]
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module equipped with the trivial adjoint action of
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\sharp}
as
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\sharp}
is a commutative group scheme. Consequently we conclude that
\mathbb{L}_{\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
(viewed as an object in
\mathcal{D}(\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}})
) comes from pullback of a certain object in
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
via the structure morphism
f:\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}}
to
\mathrm{Spf}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

However, for any

(here

), it just corresponds to the pair
(M,0)
as an object in
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\Theta])
under the full faithful embedding from
\mathcal{D}(\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}})
to
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\Theta])
given in
[
BL22a
, Theorem 3.5.8]
. Hence for
1\leq n\leq p-1

\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathcal{D}(\WCart^{\operatorname{HT}})}(\mathscr{E},\mathcal{O}\{n\})=\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\Theta])}((M,0),(\mathbb{Z}_{p},n))=0.

Here the first identity comes from
[

, Theorem 3.5.8]
and the second equality is due to
[

, Lemma 6.1]
as multiplication by

Now we win by applying the above discussion to

.
∎

Lemma 4.4

The uniqueness can be checked easily.

Lemma 4.5
.

For

such that the following holds:

•

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W(R)}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{g}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta:u\mapsto u+\epsilon E(u)}

Notice that the

\displaystyle\forall n\geq 0,w_{n}(\tilde{g}(\lambda))=w_{n}(b)\cdot w_{n}(\tilde{f}(\lambda)).

,
w_{0}(V(F(x)))=0
, hence
Eq.

6.1
always holds.

For
n\geq 1
, we have that

w_{n}(\lambda)=w_{0}(\varphi^{n}(\lambda))=w_{0}((\lambda+p)^{p^{n}}-p)=p^{p^{n}}-p,

and that

w_{n}(V(F(x)))=pw_{n-1}(F(x))=pw_{n}(x)=p(\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}^{p^{n-i}}p^{i}).

Take
n=1
, then
Eq.

6.1
is equivalent to that
x_{0}^{p}+px_{1}=p^{p-1}-1
, hence it suffices to pick
x_{0}=-1,x_{1}=p^{p-2}
.

Next we do induction on
n
. Suppose
n\geq 2
and we have determined
x_{0},\cdots,x_{n-1}
such that
x_{0}=-1
,
v_{p}(x_{i})=p^{i-1}(p-2)-\frac{p^{i-1}-1}{p-1}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
and that
Eq.

6.1
holds for non-negative integers no larger than
n-1
. Then we claim that we could pick
x_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that
Eq.

6.1
holds for
n
as well. Actually, as
x_{0}=-1
, the previous calculation implies that we just need a
x_{n}
such that

p^{p^{n}-1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{p^{n-i}}p^{i}.

Our assumption on
x_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
guarantees that

(6.2)

v_{p}(x_{i}^{p^{n-i}}p^{i})=i+p^{n-i}(p^{i-1}(p-2)-\frac{p^{i-1}-1}{p-1})=p^{n-1}(p-2)+i-\frac{p^{n-1}-p^{n-i}}{p-1},

hence
v_{p}(x_{i}^{p^{n-i}}p^{i})
decreases as
i
increases for
1\leq i\leq n-1
and they are all bounded above by
v_{p}(x_{1}^{p^{n-1}}p)=p^{n}-2p^{n-1}+1<p^{n}-1
, bounded below by
v_{p}(p^{n-1}x_{n-1}^{p})=n-1+p^{n-1}(p-2)-\frac{p^{n-1}-p}{p-1}
. Consequently,
Eq.

6.2
has a unique solution
x_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with

v_{p}(x_{n})=v_{p}(p^{n-1}x_{n-1}^{p})-n=p^{n-1}(p-2)-\frac{p^{n-1}-1}{p-1},

we win. Clearly such
x_{\lambda}
is a unit in
W(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
as
x_{0}=-1
by construction.

∎

DEPARTMENT OF ENERGY


Critical Material List; Addition of Helium

AGENCY: Office of Critical Minerals and Energy Innovation, Department 
of Energy.

ACTION: Notice.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY: The United States (U.S.) Department of Energy (DOE) is issuing 
this notice to advise the public that DOE added helium to the DOE 
Critical Material List. This notice also provides the link to the 
assessment that forms the justification for including helium on the 
Critical Material List. Helium is non-substitutable for multiple 
critical energy functions, including: semiconductor manufacturing, leak 
testing natural gas infrastructure, and quantum computing, along with 
being essential to key medical/scientific applications. Demand for 
helium is also growing as it enables advanced semiconductor 
manufacturing, which is essential for SI and data center supply chains.

ADDRESSES: The assessment that forms the justification for including 
helium on the Critical Material List can be found on DOE's website: 
https://www.energy.gov/cmm/what-are-critical-materials-and-critical-minerals.

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION: Section 7002(a)(2) of the Energy Act of 2020 
(Pub. L. 116-260, div. Z, 134 Stat. 2418) defines ``critical 
materials'' to be: any non-fuel mineral, element, substance, or 
material that the Secretary of Energy determines has high risk for 
supply chain disruption and serves an essential function in one or more 
energy technologies, including technologies that produce, transmit, 
store, and conserve energy (referred to here as a critical material for 
energy) or a critical mineral (as designated by the Secretary of the 
Interior).\1\

116-260, div. Z, 134 Stat. 2418 is codified at 30 U.S.C. 1606(a)(2).

    The critical materials for energy included on the Critical Material 
List are based on the criticality assessed in the short- and medium-
term. A detailed description of DOE's methodology can be found in its 
Critical Minerals Assessment published on its website in July 2023.\4\ 
On May 22, 2025, metallurgical grade coal for steelmaking was added to 
DOE's list of critical materials for energy.\5\

    By this notice, the Secretary of Energy advises the public of the 
addition of helium to DOE's Critical Material List. The assessment that 
forms the justification for including helium on the Critical Material 
List can be found on DOE's website: https://www.energy.gov/cmm/what-are-critical-materials-and-critical-minerals. The assessment concludes 
that helium is needed globally for semiconductor manufacturing and 
other key energy applications. Meeting the policy goal of building U.S. 
SI infrastructure will require expansion of semiconductor production 
and thereby supports the determination that helium is a DOE critical 
material.
    Furthermore, this action directly supports the President's policy 
goal of ``Winning the AI Race: America's AI Action Plan'' \6\ and 
``Advancing United States Leadership in Artificial Intelligence 
Infrastructure''.\7\

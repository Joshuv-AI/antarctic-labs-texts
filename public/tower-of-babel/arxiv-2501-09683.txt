Rough kernel hedging
Nicola Muça Cirone, Cristopher Salvi

Abstract
Building on the functional-analytic framework of operator-valued kernels and un-truncated signature kernels [ 38 ] , we propose a scalable, provably convergent signature-based algorithm for a broad class of high-dimensional, path-dependent hedging problems. We make minimal assumptions on market dynamics by modelling them as general geometric rough paths, yielding a fully model-free approach. Moreover, by means of a representer theorem, we provide theoretical guarantees on the existence and uniqueness of a global minimum of the resulting optimization problem, and derive an analytic solution under highly general loss functions. Similar to the popular deep hedging [ 5 ] —but in a more rigorous fashion—our method can also incorporate additional features by means of the underlying operator-valued kernel, such as trading signals, news analytics, and past hedging decisions, aligning closely with true machine-learning practice.

Introduction

In idealized, complete, and frictionless markets, it is theoretically possible to perfectly hedge financial derivatives, thereby eliminating risk through appropriate hedging strategies. However, real markets are incomplete due to transaction costs, market impact, liquidity constraints, and other frictions, making perfect hedging generally unattainable. Consequently, traders aim to find approximate hedging strategies that minimize a cost function selected according to their risk preferences.

There exists an extensive body of literature on hedging strategies in market models that include frictions, highlighting the complex nature of the problem. In markets where trading activities temporarily impact asset prices,
[
37
]
analyses a one-dimensional Black-Scholes model where trading a security affects its price. The optimal trading strategy in this setting is derived by solving a system of three coupled nonlinear PDEs. Extending this framework,
[
2
]
addresses a more general tracking problem, which encompasses the temporary price impact hedging issue, within a Bachelier model. The authors provide a closed-form solution for the strategy that involves conditional expectations of a time integral over the optimal frictionless hedging strategy. In the context of proportional transaction costs,
[
41
]
shows that in a Black-Scholes market, the minimal superhedging price for a European call option is equal to the current spot price of the underlying asset. This result implies that super-replication strategies are of limited practical interest in one-dimensional cases due to their prohibitive cost and become numerically intractable in higher-dimensional settings.

Mean-variance hedging has been another area of focus in the literature.
[
21
]
tackles the mean-variance hedging problem for vanilla options on Lévy processes, offering a semi-explicit solution. Their approach is further developed in
[
17
]
, whose authors present an algorithm for the mean variance hedging of vanilla options. In a frictionless framework,
[
24
]
uses BSDEs to derive the optimal hedge for the mean-variance problem. However, finding the optimal hedge remains challenging in practical applications.

Machine learning techniques have also been applied to hedging problems.
[
22
]
employs neural networks to price and hedge European options. Building on this,
[
5
]
extends the methodology to exotic derivatives by approximating the optimal hedging strategy using deep learning. Despite its potential, this approach generally finds only local minima rather than global ones, and the computational cost of training can be significant.

An alternative method involves the use of truncated signatures from the rough path theory
[
33
]
to linearize the general hedging problem. The motivation for using signatures in the context of path-dependent financial optimization problems stems from the well-known fact that linear functionals acting on the signature form a unital algebra of real-valued functions on paths that separates points
[
28
]
; thus, by the classical Stone-Weierstrass theorem, such linear functionals of signatures are universal approximators for functions on (compact sets of unparameterized) paths.
Hence, signature coefficients form an ideal set of features for machine learning applications dealing with sequential data
[
13
,
39
,
7
]
. In fact, signature methods have become very popular in recent years in quantitative finance
[
1
,
40
,
20
,
36
]
as well as in the broader data science communities, and have been applied in a variety of contexts including deep learning

[

,

,
9
,
18
,

,

,

]
, kernel methods
[

,

, cybersecurity
[

[

.
Going back to the hedging problem,
[

]
proposes to use this approach, but it requires several stringent assumptions: 1) payoffs are approximated by signature payoffs; 2) the method is limited to low-dimensional market dynamics due to the exponential growth of signature features; 3) the theory is developed for one-dimensional market dynamics and assumes that the augmented process with its quadratic covariation (i.e., lead-lag transformation) forms a geometric rough path—a property that holds almost surely for semimartingales as shown in

Contributions

In this paper, we utilize the well-established functional-analytic framework of operator-valued kernels together with untruncated signature kernels
[
38
]
to provide a scalable and provably convergent signature-based algorithm for a broad class of path-dependent hedging problems that resolves all the limitations mentioned above in the previous work and is applicable to high-dimensional financial data streams. We impose minimal assumptions on the market dynamics by modelling them as general geometric rough paths. Our approach is thus entirely model-free. We present a novel representer theorem that guarantees the existence and uniqueness of a global minimum, and provide an analytic solution to a broad class of hedging problems with highly general loss functions.
Similar to deep hedging, but in a more rigorous manner, our method considers not only the prices of hedging instruments, but can also incorporate features captured by the operator-valued kernel.
These features can include additional information like trading signals, news analytics, or past hedging decisions—quantitative data that a human trader might utilize—in line with true machine learning practices.

Outline of the paper

In Section

3
, we introduce signature kernels within our framework and explain how this class of kernels provide the necessary guarantees to solve the hedging problem. In Section
4
, we develop a general and flexible theory for kernel hedging, going well beyond the quadratic case. Finally, in Section
5
, we present simple experimental results that validate our theoretical findings.

For demonstration purposes, we summarise the key ideas of our method for solving a quadratic hedging problem.
We assume familiarity of the reader with the theory of rough paths; a brief primer on the subject is given in Appendix

Let
V=\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the space in which the market prices
X
take values and fix a temporal horizon
T>0
.
Since the profit-and-loss (P&L) of a trading strategy is given by an integral against
X
we need to fix, a priori, a good notion of integration.
To this end, we assume the prices are directly given as
\alpha
-Hölder rough paths.
In particular, for some initial value

, we assume
\mathbf{X}\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}_{x}([0,T],V)
with first-level increments
\mathbf{X}^{0}=X
matching the observed prices

, and where for any
t\in[0,T],~~\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}_{x}([0,t],V)=:\Omega^{\alpha}_{x;t}=\Omega^{\alpha}_{x;t}(V)
denotes the space of
\alpha
-Hölder rough paths starting in

Define the set of market histories as the following Polish space

\Lambda^{\alpha}_{x}=\Lambda^{\alpha}_{x}(V):=\bigcup_{t\in[0,T]}\Omega_{x;t}^{\alpha}(V).

Consider then fixed a probability measure
\mathbb{Q}
on the measurable space
(\Omega^{\alpha}_{x;T},\mathcal{B}(\Omega^{\alpha}_{x;T}))
where
\mathcal{B}(\Omega^{\alpha}_{x;T})

\mathbf{X}

We will work on the filtered space
(\Omega^{\alpha}_{x;T},\mathcal{B}(\Omega^{\alpha}_{x;T}),\mathbb{F},\mathbb{Q})

We are interested in solving the following
quadratic hedging problem

\inf_{f\in\mathfrak{F}}\mathbb{E}^{x}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\left(\pi(\mathbf{X}|_{[0,T]})-\pi_{0}-\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle f(\mathbf{X}|_{[0,t]}),\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{t}\right\rangle_{V}\right)^{2}\right],

(1)

where
\mathfrak{F}=\{f:\Lambda^{\alpha}_{x}\to V\}
is a family of functionals generating predictable, self-financing strategies

\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}

We will consider our family

to be a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS).
To define these spaces in our vector-valued setting we need the notion of
operator-valued kernel
:

Definition 1

(Operator Valued Kernel)
Given a set
\mathcal{Z}
and a real Hilbert space
\mathcal{Y}
, an
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{Y})
-valued kernel is a map
K:\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{Y})
such that

i.

\forall x,z\in\mathcal{Z}
it holds that
K(x,z)=K(z,x)^{T}
.

ii.

For every
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\{(z_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1,\dots,N}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Y}
the matrix with entries
\langle K(z_{i},z_{j})y_{i},y_{j}\rangle_{\mathcal{Y}}
is semi-positive definite.

A classical result
[
6
]
associates to such a kernel
K
a particular Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{K}\subseteq\{\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{Y}\}
, its reproducting kernel Hilbert space (RKHS), characterised by the following properties:

i.

\forall z,y\in\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Y}
it holds
K(z,\cdot)y\in\mathcal{H}_{K}
.

ii.

\forall z,y\in\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Y}
and
\forall\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{K}
it holds
\langle\phi,K(z,\cdot)y\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{K}}=\langle\phi(z),y\rangle_{\mathcal{Y}}
.

iii.

\mathcal{H}_{K}
is the closure, under
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{K}}
of
\text{Span}\left\{K(z,\cdot)y~|~z,y\in\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Y}\right\}
.

Consider then an operator-valued kernel

K:\Lambda^{\alpha}\times\Lambda^{\alpha}\to\mathcal{L}(V)
and denote by
\mathcal{H}_{K}
the associated RKHS. The elements of
\mathcal{H}_{K}
can be understood as maps
\Lambda^{\alpha}\to V^{*}\simeq V
via the defining reproducing property
i.e.
for
(f,\mathbf{X},v)\in\mathcal{H}_{K}\times\Lambda^{\alpha}\times V

\langle f(\mathbf{X}),v\rangle_{V}=\langle f,K(\mathbf{X},\cdot)v\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{K}}.

(2)

These are exactly the types of families
\mathfrak{F}
of functionals which we are going to consider in this work.

Remark 1

In practice we might want
\mathfrak{F}
to be a general class of functionals, possibly lacking a Hilbert structure, such as the space of
all
admissible strategies.
To leverage our results it is then necessary to find an RKHS “densely embedded” in the target family
\mathfrak{F}
.
Examples are signature kernels RKHS, as discussed in
3.1
below.

2.1
Feature Map
\Phi_{\mathbf{X}}

To tackle the optimisation problem (
1
), we will be interested in evaluating the following integral

\int_{0}^{T}\langle f(\mathbf{X}|_{[0,t]}),\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{t}\rangle_{V},\quad f\in\mathcal{H}_{K}.

(3)

In this section we present a general technique to deal with expressions of this form, recasting (
3
) as a kernel evaluation with respect to a new kernel
\mathcal{K}_{\Phi}
. Formally the argument proceeds as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle f(\mathbf{X}|_{[0,t]}),\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{t}\rangle_{V}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle f,~K(\mathbf{X}|_{[0,t]},\cdot)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{t}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{K}}

(4)

\displaystyle=\langle f,\int_{0}^{T}K(\mathbf{X}|_{[0,t]},\cdot)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{t}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{K}},

(5)

where the first equality follows from the reproducing property (
2
), and the second follows from linearity of the inner product. Defining

\Phi:\Omega^{\alpha}_{T}\to\mathcal{H}_{K}
as

\Phi(\mathbf{X})\equiv\Phi_{\mathbf{X}}:=\int_{0}^{T}K(\mathbf{X}|_{[0,t]},\cdot)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{t}\in\mathcal{H}_{K}

we obtain the equality

\int_{0}^{T}\langle f(\mathbf{X}|_{[0,t]}),\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{t}\rangle_{V}=\langle f,\Phi_{\mathbf{X}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{K}}.

(6)

We can think of
\Phi
as a feature map with corresponding
real-valued
kernel

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\Phi}:\Omega^{\alpha}_{T}\times\Omega^{\alpha}_{T}\to\mathbb{R}

(7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\Phi}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})=\langle\Phi_{\mathbf{X}},\Phi_{\mathbf{Y}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{K}}

and associated RKHS
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
.

Remark 2

Since
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
is the completion of
Span\{\Phi_{\mathbf{X}}:\mathbf{X}\in\Omega^{\alpha}_{T}\}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{K}
under the scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{K}}
one has the natural embedding

, and through this

Moreover, the evaluations

\mathcal{H}_{K}\xhookrightarrow{\iota_{K}}\{{\Lambda^{\alpha}}\to V\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}\xhookrightarrow{\iota_{\Phi}}\{{\Omega^{\alpha}_{T}}\to\mathbb{R}\}.

\displaystyle\int_{t=0}^{T}\langle\int_{s=0}^{T}K(\mathbf{X}|_{[0,s]},\mathbf{Y}|_{[0,t]})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{s},\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Y}_{t}\rangle_{V}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\ni K^{It\hat{o}}(X,Y)_{s,t}=\hskip 5.0pt1

A comprehensive exploration of these numerical aspects is left for future research.

4
A General Theory

From the previous motivating example of quadratic Hedging it seems that this “kernel trick” is suitable only for quadratic problems where only information from the underlying price processes is considered.

Fortunately the method generalizes with little effort to more general settings as we are about to show in the present section.

6
Conclusion

In conclusion, this work introduces a robust, signature-based hedging algorithm that leverages operator-valued kernels and un-truncated signature kernels to address the limitations of existing methods in high-dimensional financial markets. By employing minimal assumptions and modelling market dynamics as general geometric rough paths, our framework remains entirely model-free. We establish a representer theorem that guarantees a unique global minimizer, and we derive an analytic solution for a broad class of loss functions. This approach can incorporate any relevant auxiliary data—ranging from trading signals to market news or prior decisions—via the operator-valued kernel, thereby mirroring real-world trading practices and fostering more informed, machine-learning-driven hedging strategies.

[\delta\mathbb{Y}_{\tau}]_{1,\dots,d}\\
0\end{bmatrix}F_{s,\tau}

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{s}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{2}[\delta\mathbb{X}_{\sigma}]_{0}\\
0\\
[\delta\mathbb{X}_{\sigma}]_{1,\dots,d}\end{bmatrix}G_{\sigma,t}

Writing moreover

\mathbb{K}_{s,t}:=\begin{bmatrix}K\\
G\\
F\end{bmatrix}_{s,t}

\delta\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}:=K_{\sigma,\tau}\begin{bmatrix}Tr(\delta\mathbb{Y}_{t}\delta\mathbb{X}^{T}_{s})\\

2[\delta\mathbb{X}_{s}\delta\mathbb{Y}^{T}_{t}]^{0}_{1,\dots,d}\end{bmatrix}

\delta\mathbb{F}_{s,t}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{2}[\delta\mathbb{Y}_{t}]_{0}\\
[\delta\mathbb{Y}_{t}]_{1,\dots,d}\\
0\end{bmatrix}F_{s,t}

and analogously for
\delta\mathbb{G}
we obtain

\mathbb{K}_{s,t}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
0\\
0\end{bmatrix}+\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{t}\delta\mathbb{Z}_{\sigma,\tau}-\int_{0}^{t}\delta\mathbb{F}_{s,\tau}-\int_{0}^{s}\delta\mathbb{G}_{\sigma,t}

Note then that

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}_{s,t+\delta t}+\mathbb{K}_{s+\delta s,t}-\mathbb{K}_{s,t}=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}1\\
0\\
0\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}1\\
0\\
0\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}1\\

\displaystyle+\left(\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{t+\delta t}+\int_{0}^{s+\delta s}\int_{0}^{t}-\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{t}\right)\delta\mathbb{Z}_{\sigma,\tau}

\displaystyle-\left(\int_{0}^{t+\delta t}-\int_{0}^{t}\right)\delta\mathbb{F}_{s,\tau}-\int_{0}^{t}\delta\mathbb{F}_{s+\delta s,\tau}

\displaystyle-\left(\int_{0}^{s+\delta s}-\int_{0}^{s}\right)\delta\mathbb{G}_{\sigma,t}-\int_{0}^{s}\delta\mathbb{G}_{\sigma,t+\delta t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}1\\
0\\
0\end{bmatrix}+\left(\int_{0}^{s+\delta s}\int_{0}^{t+\delta t}-\int_{s}^{s+\delta s}\int_{t}^{t+\delta t}\right)\delta\mathbb{Z}_{\sigma,\tau}

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{t+\delta t}\delta\mathbb{F}_{s,\tau}-\left(\int_{0}^{t+\delta t}-\int_{t}^{t+\delta t}\right)\delta\mathbb{F}_{s+\delta s,\tau}

\displaystyle-\int_{s}^{s+\delta s}\delta\mathbb{G}_{\sigma,t}-\left(\int_{0}^{s+\delta s}-\int_{s}^{s+\delta s}\right)\delta\mathbb{G}_{\sigma,t+\delta t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip 5.0pt\mathbb{K}_{s+\delta s,t+\delta t}-\int_{s}^{s+\delta s}\int_{t}^{t+\delta t}\delta\mathbb{Z}_{\sigma,\tau}

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{t+\delta t}(\delta\mathbb{F}_{s+\delta s,\tau}-\delta\mathbb{F}_{s,\tau})+\int_{s}^{s+\delta s}(\delta\mathbb{G}_{\sigma,t+\delta t}-\delta\mathbb{G}_{\sigma,t})

This gives an Euler discretization on grids of the type

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}_{s_{n+1},t_{m+1}}=

Expert-Like Reparameterization of Heterogeneous Pyramid Receptive Fields in Efficient CNNs for Fair Medical Image Classification
Xiao Wu, Xiaoqing Zhang, Zunjie Xiao, Lingxi Hu, Risa Higashita, Jiang Liu

Abstract
Efficient convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture designs have attracted growing research interests. However, they usually apply single receptive field (RF), small asymmetric RFs, or pyramid RFs to learn different feature representations, still encountering two significant challenges in medical image classification tasks: i) They have limitations in capturing diverse lesion characteristics efficiently, e.g., tiny, coordination, small and salient, which have unique roles on results, especially imbalanced medical image classification. ii) The predictions generated by those CNNs are often unfair/biased, bringing a high risk by employing them to real-world medical diagnosis conditions. To tackle these issues, we develop a new concept, Expert-Like Reparameterization of Heterogeneous Pyramid Receptive Fields (ERoHPRF), to simultaneously boost medical image classification performance and fairness. This concept aims to mimic the multi-expert consultation mode by applying the well-designed heterogeneous pyramid RF bags to capture different lesion characteristics effectively via convolution operations with multiple heterogeneous kernel sizes. Additionally, ERoHPRF introduces an expert-like structural reparameterization technique to merge its parameters with the two-stage strategy, ensuring competitive computation cost and inference speed through comparisons to a single RF. To manifest the effectiveness and generalization ability of ERoHPRF, we incorporate it into mainstream efficient CNN architectures. The extensive experiments show that our method maintains a better trade-off than state-of-the-art methods in terms of medical image classification, fairness, and computation overhead. The codes of this paper will be released soon.

Introduction

Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have become mainstream techniques in medical image analysis over the past years
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
, such as medical image classification, medical image segmentation, and lesion detection. A plausible reason behind their promising success is that most existing works laid emphasis on constructing complex CNN architectures by exploiting the potential of various convolution operators to improve their performance heuristically or intuitively. However, the computational resources of medical devices are often limited (e.g., wearable medical devices), and clinical diagnosis usually requires real-time feedback, bringing significant challenges to deploying these sophisticated CNNs on resource-limited medical devices.

In seeking to this problem, scholars have begun to design efficient CNNs and have made significant progress. MobileNet
[

pioneered the efficient CNN design by adopting the depthwise separable convolution (DSConv) operator. Then, pointwise group convolution and channel shuffle operators were developed to construct ShuffleNet

. An interleaved group convolution operator was proposed to build IGCNet

. Unfortunately, these efficient CNNs typically adopt a single receptive field (RF) (e.g.,
3\times 3
and
1\times 1
) in an independent convolution operator, restricting their abilities to capture diverse lesion characteristics, e.g., boundary, small, and salient. Recently, structural reparameterization techniques have become another mainstream paradigm, which allows CNNs to maintain a trade-off between effectiveness and efficiency via the amalgamation of diverse RFs. For example, asymmetric convolution block (ACB) merges the convolution kernel size parameters of coordination RFs into the square RF without additional parameters in the inference stage
[
11
]
. However, these methods typically use small asymmetric RFs or pyramid RFs in a convolution operator, making it difficult to capture different lesion characteristics effectively.
Here, we suggest that different lesion characteristics have unique yet important impacts on medical image classification performance, especially for imbalanced medical image classification, but these lesion characteristics have been under-exploited via efficient RF concept design in the individual convolution operator.

Additionally, current CNNs often face fairness problems in medical image analysis
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
, which generate biased predictions for different diseases, especially rare diseases, due to various reasons, e.g., age, population, lesion
characteristics, and data distribution. This unfairness issue limits their applications to the real medical diagnosis scene. One possible reason is that they typically utilize the convolution operators with the single RF or small asymmetric RFs to capture lesion characteristics, causing them to learn specific lesion characteristics with bias and inevitably ignore the relative significance of other lesion characteristics. We suggest that convolution operators with heterogeneous RFs can compensate for the shortcomings of the existing convolution operators in handling the fairness problem due to the inefficient lesion representation capturing, but this has attracted little attention.

To address the two above-mentioned challenges, we propose a novel concept: expert-like reparameterization of heterogeneous pyramid receptive fields (ERoHPRF) instead of using the single RF or small asymmetric RFs for efficient convolution operator design, which is motivated by multi-expert consultation mode. As shown in Fig.
2
(left lower corner), multiple experts have unique experiences and knowledge in assessing the relative importance of different lesion types in the clinical diagnosis condition. Therefore, it is well-acknowledged that aggregating the diagnosis opinions of several experts is helpful in making precise and fair diagnosis conclusions. Similarly, ERoHPRF treats each heterogeneous RF bag as an expert in effectively learning lesion characteristics with varying levels of significance from medical images via the well-designed heterogeneous pyramid RF bags in the convolution operator. To further reduce the computational overhead in the inference stage, we propose a novel expert-like structural reparameterization technique to merge the parameters of heterogeneous pyramid RF bags into one extensive RF via heterogeneous RF reparameterization and expert-like pyramid reparameterization.

To manifest the effectiveness of ERoHPRF in fair medical image classification, we incorporate it into mainstream efficient CNNs, such as ShuffleNet, MobileNet, and MixNet. Extensive experiments on balanced/imbalanced medical image datasets show the superiority and generalization ability of our ERoHPRF over state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods in terms of classification, fairness, and computational overhead in the inference stage, as presented in Fig.
1
. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

1.

We are the first to provide a unique perspective to improve diverse lesion representation capturing and fairness in medical classification tasks from the aspect of heterogeneous pyramid RFs in the efficient convolution operator design.

2.

Motivated by the multi-expert consultation mode, we propose a new concept of Expert-Like Reparameterization of Heterogeneous Pyramid Receptive Fields (ERoHPRF) for efficient CNN designing, applying heterogeneous pyramid RF bags to capture diverse lesion characteristics via convolution operations with multiple heterogeneous pyramid-like kernel sizes effectively. In the inference stage, we introduce an expert-like structural reparameterization technique to merge the parameters of heterogeneous pyramid RF bags to keep competitive computation cost and inference speed through comparisons to a single RF.

3.

We systematically investigate the effectiveness, efficiency, and fairness of our ERoHPRF on balanced/imbalanced medical image datasets compared to existing SOTA structural reparameterization methods, efficient CNNs, and advanced Transformers.

II
Related Work

II-A

Receptive Fields in Convolution

The research interests of RF design in the convolution operator can be traced back to the pioneering LeNet, which utilizes
5\times 5
RF size to tackle the task of handwritten digits
[
15
]
. AlexNet
[
16
]
adopts a large RF size
11\times 11
but increases computational cost. VGGNet
[
17
]
replaces large RF size with small RF size
3\times 3
and finds that it can increase the depth of CNNs. After VGGNet, small RF size has become mainstream in various convolution operators (e.g., dilated convolution) and advanced CNN architecture design, such as GoogleNet and ResNet. Meanwhile, with the rapid development of hardware, large RF design has also regained attention from a broader perspective
[
18
,
19
]
. For example, Ding et al.
[
20
]
extended the RF size to
31\times 31
and achieved competitive performance. Afterward, Liu et al.
[
21
]
further enlarge the RF into
51\times 51
size by following a similar idea. However, most previous works focused on the roles of the independent RF, ignoring the complementary significances of different RFs, which is beneficial to capture diverse lesion types. On the contrary, our ERoHPRF takes the unique effects of different RFs into consideration from the aspect of heterogeneous RFs for enhancing medical image classification results.

II-B

Structural Reparameterization Techniques

Recently, structural reparameterization techniques have become another popular paradigm that allows DNNs to maintain a good re-balance between computational overhead and performance in the inference stage. Ding et al.
[
11
]
pioneers the structural reparameterization method by designing an ACB with two coordination RFs and a square RF and then merging them into the square RF. Following this, several structural reparameterization methods have been developed to boost the performance of DNNs by reparametrizing the corresponding RFs in a convolution operator. For example, RepLKNet
[
20
]
increases the RF size to
31\times 31
and takes it as a backbone by merging other large RF sizes. A mixture of receptive fields (MoRF)
[
22
]
merges the parameters of pyramid RFs with a routing mechanism.
Unluckily, these methods ignore the relative significance of heterogeneous RFs in a bag in capturing diverse lesion representations in the medical image classification tasks for the pyramid aspect. To alleviate this problem, we design heterogeneous expert RF bags and expert-like structural reparameterization under the concept of ERoHPRF for reducing computational overhead in the inference stage.

II-C

Fairness

Artificial intelligence (AI) fairness has been a hot research topic over the years, especially in the era of deep learning. The issue of AI fairness is associated with many biases, such as gender, age, race, representation types, or data distributions
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
. Scholars have developed several techniques to boost the AI fairness of DNNs, including loss modification, data enhancement, and representation learning. For example, Park et al.
[
14
]
propose a fair supervised contrastive loss to distinguish different classes and penalize sensitive information. Lin et al.
[
26
]
introduced a marginal ranking loss to improve fairness by adopting the marginal pairwise equal opportunity. Luo et al.
[
13
]
change the sample distribution to better learn the fair feature representations of DNNs based on each demographic group. In contrast to previous efforts, we aim to improve the fairness of DNNs from a new aspect of heterogeneous pyramid RFs in the convolution operator to design an efficient CNN architecture, inspired by the multi-expert consultation mode for improving our ERoHPRF’s fairness in the medical image classification tasks.

III-A

Revisiting Asymmetric and Pyramid RFs

The concepts of asymmetric and pyramid RFs have widely been utilized to design different convolution methods and structural reparameterization methods, as listed in Fig.
2
(a)-(b). Asymmetric RFs comprise coordination RFs and square RF (The representative implementation of it is ACB
[
11
]
), in which the former is the indispensable complement to the latter in efficiently capturing coordination representations of single lesion characteristics but lacks the ability to capture feature representations of diverse lesion characteristics simultaneously. In contrast, pyramid RFs apply multiple RF sizes to learn diverse lesion characteristics in a convolution operator (The representative implementation of it is MoRF
[
22
]
). Unluckily, they have limitations in obtaining coordination and boundary lesion characteristics. The following works of asymmetric and pyramid RFs design still cannot tackle their corresponding deficiencies.

Additionally, as shown in Fig.
2
(c), different experts have their corresponding advantages in evaluating lesion characteristic combinations in clinical scenes, e.g., the combination of small and salient characteristics and the combination of small and boundary characteristics. Naturally, integrating the diagnosis results of multiple experts improves diagnosis precision and fairness of diseases according to our understanding. Inspired by the multi-expert consultation mode, we design a new concept of Expert-Like Reparameterization of Heterogeneous Pyramid Receptive Fields (ERoHPRF) to capture different feature representations at the same time in an individual convolution operator. Our ERoHPRF is supposed to not only take advantage of asymmetric and pyramid RFs but also overcome their shortcomings.

III-B

Expert-Like Reparameterization of Heterogeneous Pyramid Receptive Fields

As a convenient plug-and-play module, our ERoHPRF comprises two main components: heterogeneous pyramid RF bags (HPRFBs) and expert-like structural reparameterization (ELSR). The well-designed HPRFBs employ each heterogeneous receptive field bag (HRFB) with different RF sizes to capture diverse lesion types (e.g., tiny, small, boundary, and salient) effectively, and Fig.
2
(d) presents an example of HPRFBs implementation with three RF sizes
\{3,5,7\}
. Following it, the expert-like structural reparameterization method applies a two-stage reparameterization strategy to merge different RFs into an extensive one via heterogeneous RF bag reparameterization and expert-like pyramid parameterization for reducing computational overhead.

Heterogeneous Pyramid Receptive Field Bags

The main goal of HPRFBs is to capture diverse lesion types effectively through different HRFBs with various RF types, including square RF, coordination RF, and rectangular RF, thereby boosting the classification performance and decision-making fairness of CNNs. Given the input feature maps
X\in R^{C\times H\times W}
, HPRFBs with pyramid RF scales
N=\{3,5,...,K\}
produce the output
Y\in R^{C\times H\times W}
in a single convolution operator, which is formulated as follows:

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=Sum(Y_{3},Y_{5},...,Y_{K})

(1)

\displaystyle=Sum(HRFB_{3}(X),HRFB_{5}(X),...,HRFB_{K}(X)),

where
Sum
denotes the summation operation. Here, we adopt
HRFB_{K}(X)
as an example to illustrate the detailed operations for each HRFB:

\begin{matrix}HRFB_{K}(X)=F_{1\times K}(X)+F_{K\times 1}(X)+F_{K-2\times K}(X)\\
+F_{K\times K-2}(X)+F_{K\times K}(X),\end{matrix}

(2)

\displaystyle y_{K\times K}

\displaystyle=F_{K\times K}(X)=BN_{K\times K}(Conv_{K\times K}(X))

(3)

\displaystyle=(X*W_{K\times K}+B_{K\times K}-\mu_{K\times K})\frac{\gamma_{K\times K}}{\sigma_{K\times K}}+\beta_{K\times K},

where BN and Conv denote the batch normalization operator and convolution operator.
K
,
W_{K\times K}\in R^{C\times K\times K}
, and
B_{K\times K}\in R^{C}
denote the RF size, the convolution weight and convolution bias of HPFB. The
\mu_{K\times K}
,
\gamma_{K\times K}
,
\sigma_{K\times K}
,
\beta_{K\times K}
are the parameters in the corresponding BN layer.

Expert-Like Structural Reparameterization

Structural reparameterization techniques have been widely utilized to merge parameters of different RFs in a convolution operator to keep a good balance between efficiency and effectiveness in the inference stage. This paper introduces an expert-like structural reparameterization technique to reparametrize the parameters of HPRFBs through two stages: heterogeneous RF bag reparameterization and expert-like pyramid reparameterization, as shown in the bottom of Fig.
2
(d).

Heterogeneous RF Bag Reparameterization.
The main goal of this step is to merge the parameters of heterogeneous RFs in an HRFB, including the parameters of convolution operations with heterogeneous kernel sizes and the parameters of corresponding BN operations. Given the RF size
K\times K
of the convolution and BN operations in the
HRFB_{K}
, its reparameterization formula is written as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{y}_{K\times K}

\displaystyle=X*\frac{\gamma_{K\times K}}{\sigma_{K\times K}}W_{K\times K}+\frac{\gamma_{K\times K}}{\sigma_{K\times K}}B_{K\times K}

(4)

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma_{K\times K}\mu_{K\times K}}{\sigma_{K\times K}}+\beta_{K\times K}

\displaystyle=X*\hat{W}_{K\times K}+\hat{B}_{K\times K},

where
\hat{W}_{K\times K}\in R^{C\times K\times K}
and
\hat{B}_{K\times K}\in R^{C}
denote the merged convolution weight and bias. Similarly, the reparameterization of other RFs, such as coordination and rectangular, can be implemented via Equation (
4
). Furthermore, we use zero padding to reshape the convolution weights of heterogeneous RFs as follows:

\hat{W}_{K\times K}=ZeroPadding(\hat{W}_{m\times n}),

(5)

where
m,n
represent the original convolution shape. Thus, the final reparameterization form of
HRFB_{K}
is formulated as follows:

\begin{aligned} \hat{HRFB}_{K}(X)&=\hat{Y}_{K}=\hat{y}_{K\times K}+\hat{y}_{K\times K-2}+\\
&\hat{y}_{K-2\times K}+\hat{y}_{1\times K}+\hat{y}_{K\times 1}\\
&=X*\bar{W}_{K\times K}+\bar{B}_{K\times K}\end{aligned},

(6)

where
\bar{W}_{K\times K}\in R^{C\times K\times K}
and
\bar{B}_{K\times K}\in R^{C}
are the new convolution weight and bias of
HRFB_{K}
. Next, this paper merges the expert-like pyramid RFs in different HRFBs into extensive RF of a single convolution operator.

Expert-Like Pyramid Reparameterization.
This stage further fuses HRFBs with pyramid RFs of the HPRFBs into a large one, which is expected to keep a desirable balance in terms of classification performance, fairness, and computational complexity. The reparameterization operation of HRFBs is formulated as follows:

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=(X*\bar{W}_{3\times 3}+\bar{B}_{3\times 3}+...+X*\bar{W}_{K\times K}+\bar{B}_{K\times K})

(7)

\displaystyle=X*\tilde{W}+\tilde{B}.

where
\tilde{W}\in R^{C\times K\times K}
and
\tilde{B}\in R^{C}
are the new convolution weight and bias. After the two-stage structural reparameterization operations, ERoHPRF achieves promising results without extra parameters and computation overhead.

I
. The imbalance ratio denotes the ratio of samples of the most frequent class to samples of the least frequent class.

ISIC2018

IV-B

Implementation Details

Experimental Settings.
We implement the proposed ERoHPRF, competitive structural reparameterization methods, and SOTA DNNs with the Pytorch platform and Python language. This paper runs all experiments on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU. The classical stochastic gradient descent (SGD) optimizer is adopted as the optimizer with default settings (a weight decay of 1e-5 and a momentum of 0.9) to update the weights of DNNs. We set the training epochs and batch size to 150 and 32, respectively. The initial learning rate (LR) is set to 0.005, decreasing by a factor of 5 every 30 epochs. Specifically, the LR is fixed at 0.00025 after 100 epochs. Standard data augmentation methods are used in the training stage for data augmentation, such as random flipping and cropping.

Evaluation Metrics.
This paper utilizes classification metrics and fairness metrics to investigate the effectiveness of all methods from two different perspectives. Classification metrics include accuracy (ACC), balanced accuracy (bACC), and macro F1 (mF1)
[
35
]
. As for fairness metrics, we adopt the average Area Under the receiver operating characteristic Curve (AUC) for each class, expected calibration error (ECE), and Brier score (BS)

24
]
as follows:

AUC=\frac{1}{S_{N}S_{P}}\sum_{i=1}^{S_{P}}\sum_{j=1}^{S_{N}}(p_{i}>p_{j}),

(8)

ECE=\sum_{i=1}^{B}\frac{s_{i}}{S}|ACC(b_{i})-CON(b_{i})|,

(9)

BS=\frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{c=1}^{C}(p_{s,c}-o_{s,c})^{2},

(10)

where
S_{N},S_{P},S,C
indicates the number of negative, positive, all samples, and classes.

i_{th}

s

.
B,b_{i},s_{i}
in Equation (
9
) are the number of bins, the
i_{th}

b_{i}
where

calculate the accuracy and confidence in

V
Conclusion and Future Work

This paper introduces a new concept of Expert-Like Reparameterization of Heterogeneous Pyramid Receptive Fields (ERoHPRF), motivated by the multi-expert consultation mode. By incorporating the multi-expert prior of diverse lesion characteristics assessment into the efficient convolution operator from the aspect of heterogeneous pyramid RFs, ERoHPRF significantly boosts the medical image classification and fairness of efficient CNNs. The extensive experiments on balanced/imbalanced medical image datasets manifest that our method maintains a better trade-off than SOTA structural reparameterization methods, efficient CNNs, and advanced Transformers in terms of classification, fairness, and computation overhead. In the future, we plan to provide a more theoretical analysis of our ERoHPRF and further demonstrate its superiority and generalization in other medical image analysis tasks.

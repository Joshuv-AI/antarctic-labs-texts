Hybrid sub- and superradiant states in emitter arrays with quantized motion
Beatriz Olmos, Igor Lesanovsky

Abstract
Ensembles of dipolar emitters which couple collectively to the radiation field display sub- and superradiance. These terms refer to a reduction or an enhancement of photon emission rates due to the interference of emission channels. Arrays of trapped neutral atoms constitute a promising platform for harnessing this phenomenon in technological applications, e.g. for excitation storage, single-photon switches and mirrors. However, vibrational motion of the atoms within their traps leads to position fluctuations that entangle the motion and the internal atomic degrees of freedom, which is expected to affect the collective photon emission. We develop here a theory for collective atom-light coupling in the presence of this quantized motion within the Lamb-Dicke limit. We show the existence of sub- and superradiant states, which are hybrids of electronic and vibrational excitations and explore their properties for analytically and numerically efficiently solvable cases.



Introduction.
Atomic ensembles couple collectively to the radiation field, which leads to intricate open system dynamics. On the one hand, the exchange of virtual photons among atoms induces dipole-dipole interactions. On the other, the emission of photons stored within the ensemble takes place through collective emission channels that feature decay rates which are either larger (superradiant) or smaller (subradiant) than that of an isolated atom. In this paper, we develop a theory of hybrid sub- and superradiant states for atomic ensembles with coupled internal electronic degrees of freedom (henceforth referred to as spin) and external motion (phonons). We derive a Lindblad master equation within the Lamb-Dicke limit from first principles and analytically solve, first, the particular case of two atoms. Here, we show that for a certain atomic separation and/or choice of the transition dipole moment orientation, the sub- and superradiant decay rates as well as the dipole-dipole interactions are not affected by the spin-phonon coupling. Considering an atomic chain, we find that the latter coupling generally hybridizes the two sets of degrees of freedom. Nevertheless, also in this hybrid system a class of states can be identified, which are separable, i.e., where the spin and phonon states factorize. Our study thus sheds light on the various manifestations of sub- and superradiant states in a composite quantum system, which models many experimentally relevant scenarios.

Model and equations of motion.
We consider a system of
N
two-level emitters (here neutral atoms) in a one-dimensional configuration coupled to the free radiation field. The internal degrees of freedom of each atom can be considered as those of a fictitious spin-
1/2
particle, with lower (
\ket{\downarrow}
) and upper (
\ket{\uparrow}
) states separated by an energy
\hbar\omega_{0}
, as shown in Fig.
1
a. Each of these atoms is assumed to be at the position
\mathbf{r}_{j}=z_{j}\hat{z}
, trapped in a harmonic potential centered at
\mathbf{r}_{j}^{0}=z_{j}^{0}\hat{z}
. The quantized motion within each trap is strictly assumed to take place in the direction of the chain, i.e. the
z
-axis. For the
j
-th atom, this motion is represented via the bosonic phonon operators
a_{j}
and
a_{j}^{\dagger}
, which annihilate and create vibrational excitations of energy
\hbar\omega\ll\hbar\omega_{0}
, respectively (see Fig.
1
a). The displacement of the position of the atom with respect to the center of its trap can be written in terms of these operators, such that
z_{j}=z_{j}^{0}+z_{\mathrm{ho}}(a_{j}+a^{\dagger}_{j})/\sqrt{2}
, where
z_{\mathrm{ho}}=\sqrt{\hbar/m\omega}
is the harmonic oscillator length and
m
the atomic mass.

Within the dipole approximation, the Hamiltonian that describes this system is given by
[
2
]

\begin{split}H=&\hbar\sum_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\nu_{k}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}c_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}+\hbar\omega_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j}+\hbar\omega\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{j}\\
&-\hbar\sum_{j,\mathbf{k},\lambda}g_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}(\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}+\sigma_{j})(c_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}}+c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}e^{-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}}).\end{split}

Here, we have introduced the the spin ladder operators
\sigma_{j}=\ket{\downarrow}_{j}\!\bra{\uparrow}
and
\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}=\ket{\uparrow}_{j}\!\bra{\downarrow}
, the bosonic creation and annihilation operators
c_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}^{\dagger}
and
c_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}
of a photon with momentum
\mathbf{k}
, energy
\hbar\nu_{k}=\hbar c|\mathbf{k}|
and polarization
\lambda=1,2
. Moreover,the coupling constant between the emitters and the radiation field is given by
g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}=\mathbf{d}\cdot\hat{\epsilon}^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}\sqrt{\frac{\nu_{k}}{2\varepsilon_{0}\hbar V}}
, with
\mathbf{d}
,
\hat{\epsilon}^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}
, and
V
being the atomic transition dipole moment, the field polarization unit vector and the quantization volume, respectively.

By eliminating the radiation field, and under the Born and Markov approximations
[
2
,
41
]
, we obtain an equation of motion for the dynamics of the spin and phonon degrees of freedom only (see
[
42
]
for details). We consider the situation where the atoms are tightly trapped, such that
z_{\mathrm{ho}}\ll\lambda_{0}
, where
\lambda_{0}=2\pi c/\omega_{0}
is the wavelength of the atomic transition (Lamb-Dicke parameter regime). This allows us to expand in the small Lamb-Dicke parameter
\eta_{0}=k_{0}z_{\mathrm{ho}}/\sqrt{2}\ll 1
with
k_{0}=2\pi/\lambda_{0}
, up to order
\eta_{0}^{2}
. The dynamics is then governed by the Lindblad master equation

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}=&\sum_{m,m^{\prime}=1}^{4N}\tilde{\Gamma}_{mm^{\prime}}\left(J_{m^{\prime}}\rho J_{m}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{J_{m}^{\dagger}J_{m^{\prime}},\rho\right\}\right)\\
&+\mathrm{i}\sum_{m\neq m^{\prime}}\tilde{V}_{mm^{\prime}}\left[J_{m}^{\dagger}J_{m^{\prime}},\rho\right].\end{split}

(1)

This equation accounts for collective photon emission and dipole-dipole exchange interactions (first and second term, respectively). The two matrices
\tilde{\Gamma}
and
\tilde{V}
are
4N\times 4N
block matrices of the form

\tilde{\Gamma}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Gamma&0&0&\frac{\eta_{0}^{2}}{2}\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\\
0&-\eta_{0}^{2}\Gamma^{\prime\prime}&0&0\\
0&0&-\eta_{0}^{2}\Gamma^{\prime\prime}&0\\
\frac{\eta_{0}^{2}}{2}\Gamma^{\prime\prime}&0&0&0\end{array}\right).

Here, the
N\times N
block matrix
\Gamma
has the entries

\Gamma_{jj^{\prime}}=\frac{3\gamma}{2}\left[f_{\varphi}\frac{\sin{\kappa_{jj^{\prime}}}}{\kappa_{jj^{\prime}}}+g_{\varphi}\left(\frac{\cos{\kappa_{jj^{\prime}}}}{\kappa_{jj^{\prime}}^{2}}-\frac{\sin{\kappa_{jj^{\prime}}}}{\kappa_{jj^{\prime}}^{3}}\right)\right],

where
\gamma
is the single-atom decay rate,
\kappa_{jj^{\prime}}=k_{0}z^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}\equiv k_{0}|z^{0}_{j}-z^{0}_{j^{\prime}}|
the reduced distance between two atoms, and
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}_{jj^{\prime}}=\partial^{2}_{\kappa_{jj^{\prime}}}\Gamma_{jj^{\prime}}
. Moreover, here
f_{\varphi}=\sin^{2}{\varphi}
,
g_{\varphi}=1-3\cos^{2}{\varphi}
, where
\cos{\varphi}=\hat{\mathbf{d}}\cdot\hat{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}
is the relative angle between the transition dipole moments and the line connecting the two atoms. We assume this angle to be the same throughout the chain (see Fig.
2
a). The same structure applies to the dipole-dipole interaction matrix
\tilde{V}
, whose entries are

V_{jj^{\prime}}=\frac{3\gamma}{4}\left[f_{\varphi}\frac{\cos{\kappa_{jj^{\prime}}}}{\kappa_{jj^{\prime}}}-g_{\varphi}\left(\frac{\sin{\kappa_{jj^{\prime}}}}{\kappa_{jj^{\prime}}^{2}}+\frac{\cos{\kappa_{jj^{\prime}}}}{\kappa_{jj^{\prime}}^{3}}\right)\right],

for
j\neq j^{\prime}
and
V_{jj}=0
, and
V^{\prime\prime}_{jj^{\prime}}=\partial^{2}_{\kappa_{jj^{\prime}}}V_{jj^{\prime}}
. The master equation (
1
) contains four sets of jump operators:

J_{m}=\begin{cases}\sigma_{j}&1\leq m\leq N\\
\sigma_{j}a_{j}&N+1\leq m\leq 2N\\
\sigma_{j}a^{\dagger}_{j}&2N+1\leq m\leq 3N\\
\sigma_{j}(1+2a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{j})&3N+1\leq m\leq 4N,\end{cases}

with indices
j=1\dots N
. The first
N
terms (
m,m^{\prime}=1,\dots,N
) of the master equation (
1
) recover the well-established equation for two-level atoms coupled to the radiation field in the absence of spin-phonon coupling
[
2
,
3
,
18
,
14
,
22
]
. The rest of the equation yields the leading correction (of order
\eta_{0}^{2}
) to the dynamics due to the recoil of the emitters under photon emission. The processes that accompany these corrections correspond either to the loss or the gain of a phonon in each spin transition (see Fig.
1
b). A further effect of the same order is the renormalization of the spin transition rates, which acquire a dependence on the number of phonons.

In the following we focus on the dynamics when at most one spin excitation,
\ket{\uparrow}
, is present in the whole system, also known as the linear optics regime
[
30
,
22
,
23
]
. Here, the spin ladder operators
\sigma_{j}
and
\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}
can be substituted by the bosonic annihilation and creation operators
b_{j}
and
b_{j}^{\dagger}
, respectively. In this regime the dynamics is governed by the non-hermitian Hamiltonian
H^{\mathrm{eff}}=\sum_{jj^{\prime}}H^{\text{eff}}_{jj^{\prime}}
with

\begin{split}H^{\text{eff}}_{jj^{\prime}}=&b_{j}^{\dagger}b_{j^{\prime}}\left[M_{jj^{\prime}}+\eta_{0}^{2}M^{\prime\prime}_{jj^{\prime}}\left(1-\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\right.\right.\\
&\left.\left.+a_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}a_{j^{\prime}}+a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{j}-a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{j^{\prime}}-a_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}a_{j}\right)\right]\end{split}

(2)

where
M_{jj^{\prime}}=V_{jj^{\prime}}-\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{jj^{\prime}}/2
, and
M^{\prime\prime}_{jj^{\prime}}=\partial^{2}_{\kappa_{jj^{\prime}}}M_{jj^{\prime}}
. The real and imaginary parts of the eigenvalues of
H^{\mathrm{eff}}
represent the collective energy shifts and decay rates, respectively. Note, that this Hamiltonian conserves the total number of phonons,
n_{\mathrm{ph}}
, that are contained in the lattice.

Sub- and superradiant states of two atoms.
Let us first analyze the case of two atoms, which are separated by a distance
d
. Not only can this system be solved fully analytically, but one can also decouple spin and motion. Let us rewrite the effective Hamiltonian (
2
) for
N=2
in diagonal form. To do so, we introduce the symmetric and antisymmetric combinations of bosonic operators representing the atomic excitation,
b_{s/a}=(b_{1}\pm b_{2})/\sqrt{2}
, and the phonons,
a_{s/a}=(a_{1}\pm a_{2})/\sqrt{2}
. This yields

\begin{split}H^{\text{eff}}=&M_{11}+(b_{s}^{\dagger}b_{s}-b_{a}^{\dagger}b_{a})\left(M_{12}+\eta_{0}^{2}M^{\prime\prime}_{12}\right)\\
&+2\eta_{0}^{2}(b_{s}^{\dagger}b_{s}-b_{a}^{\dagger}b_{a})M^{\prime\prime}_{12}a_{a}^{\dagger}a_{a},\end{split}

(3)

where
M_{11}=M_{22}=-\mathrm{i}\gamma
/2. This expression reveals that all eigenstates of the Hamiltonian are separable (product states) in the atomic and phonon degrees of freedom. The atomic eigenstates are the symmetric and antisymmetric superpositions
\ket{s/a}=b_{s/a}^{\dagger}\ket{\downarrow\downarrow}=(\ket{\uparrow\downarrow}\pm\ket{\downarrow\uparrow})/\sqrt{2}
. The phonon eigenstates are

\ket{n_{\mathrm{ph}},n_{\mathrm{ph}}^{a}}={a_{s}^{\dagger}}^{(n_{\mathrm{ph}}-n_{\mathrm{ph}}^{a})}{a_{a}^{\dagger}}^{n_{\mathrm{ph}}^{a}}\ket{0}_{\mathrm{ph}}
, where
n_{\mathrm{ph}}
is the total number of phonons and
n_{\mathrm{ph}}^{a}
is the number of phonons in the antisymmetric mode. The corresponding collective decay rates are given by
\gamma_{s/a}^{n_{\mathrm{ph}},n_{\mathrm{ph}}^{a}}=\gamma\pm\Gamma_{12}\pm\eta_{0}^{2}(2n_{\mathrm{ph}}^{a}+1)\Gamma^{\prime\prime}_{12}
.
When
\eta_{0}=0
there exist only two degenerate rates, namely
\gamma_{s/a}^{n_{\mathrm{ph}},n_{\mathrm{ph}}^{a}}\equiv\gamma_{s/a}=\gamma\pm\Gamma_{12}
, governing superradiant (
\gamma_{s}>\gamma
) and subradiant (
\gamma_{a}<\gamma
) emission. For
\eta_{0}\neq 0
, we find that all rates change by an amount proportional to
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}_{12}
. Note that, unlike in long-range interacting systems, where the interactions decay monotonically with the distance, the behaviour of both
\Gamma_{12}
and
V_{12}
with the reduced distance
\kappa
is non-monotonic (see Fig.
2
a). Hence, here it is possible to find an interatomic distance
d_{0}
where the second derivative
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}_{12}
vanishes. Here, all decay rates remain unaffected by the spin-motion coupling independently of both the phonon number and the Lamb-Dicke parameter
\eta_{0}
. To illustrate this, we show in Fig.
2
b the collective decay rates of two atoms for three values of the distance
d
and
n_{\mathrm{ph}}=1
. Indeed, when
d=d_{0}
, the rates are independent of
\eta_{0}
.

Many atoms.
When
N>2
, the problem is in general no longer analytically solvable. However, we can gain an intuition of the physics by considering an infinite chain of atoms, i.e.
N
tending to infinity (see e.g. Refs.
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
). In this limit, one can diagonalize the effective Hamiltonian by introducing the Fourier transformed operators

b_{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{q=-\pi/d}^{\pi/d}e^{\mathrm{i}z_{j}^{0}q}\tilde{b}_{q},\quad a_{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{p=-\pi/d}^{\pi/d}e^{\mathrm{i}z_{j}^{0}p}\tilde{a}_{p}

with
z_{j}^{0}=jd
and
j=-N/2,\dots,N/2
.
The effective Hamiltonian (
2
) becomes

\begin{split}H^{\text{eff}}=&\sum_{q}\tilde{b}^{\dagger}_{q}\tilde{b}_{q}(\tilde{M}_{q}+\eta_{0}^{2}\tilde{M}^{\prime\prime}_{q})-\eta_{0}^{2}M^{\prime\prime}_{11}\\
&+\frac{\eta_{0}^{2}}{N}\!\sum_{q,p,p^{\prime}}\tilde{b}^{\dagger}_{q+p^{\prime}}\tilde{b}_{q+p}\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{p^{\prime}}\tilde{a}_{p}\\
&\times\left(\tilde{M}^{\prime\prime}_{q+p}+\tilde{M}^{\prime\prime}_{q+p^{\prime}}-\tilde{M}^{\prime\prime}_{q}-\tilde{M}^{\prime\prime}_{q-p+p^{\prime}}\right),\end{split}

(4)

where we have used that
M^{\prime\prime}_{jj}\equiv M^{\prime\prime}_{11}
for all
j
. Here, we have also introduced the Fourier transform of the matrix
M
as
\tilde{M}_{q}=\sum_{jj^{\prime}}e^{\mathrm{i}q(z^{0}_{j}-z^{0}_{j^{\prime}})}M_{jj^{\prime}}
, where the sum runs over
z^{0}_{j}-z^{0}_{j^{\prime}}=-Nd/2,\dots,Nd/2
, and equivalently for the second derivative,
\tilde{M}^{\prime\prime}_{q}
.

For
\eta_{0}=0
, the quasi-momentum
q
is a good quantum number and all eigenstates of the effective Hamiltonian (
4
) are products of phonon states and the atomic (single) excitation states
\ket{q}=\tilde{b}_{q}^{\dagger}\ket{\downarrow,\dots,\downarrow}
, with degenerate decay rates
\tilde{\Gamma}_{q}=-2\text{Im}(\tilde{M}_{q})
. Here, when
d<\lambda_{0}/2
, it is well known that all eigenstates with
|q|>2\pi/\lambda_{0}
are completely subradiant, i.e.
\tilde{\Gamma}_{|q|>2\pi/\lambda_{0}}=0
, while the rest,
|q|<2\pi/\lambda_{0}

As we increase
\eta_{0}
,
q
is in general no longer a good quantum number. However, upon inspection of the effective Hamiltonian (
4
), one can see that all product states of the form
\ket{q}(\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{0})^{n_{\mathrm{ph}}}\ket{0}_{\mathrm{ph}}
are eigenstates of the system. Here,
\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{0}=\sum_{j}a_{j}^{\dagger}/\sqrt{N}
is the creation operator of the center of mass motion, where the relative distance between the emitters is unchanged. The eigenvalues for these product states are given by

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{q}=\tilde{M}_{q}+\eta_{0}^{2}(\tilde{M}^{\prime\prime}_{q}-M^{\prime\prime}_{11}).

Here we can see that, even though these eigenstates are separable into atomic and phonon degrees of freedom, the coupling still leads to a renormalization of the decay rates proportional to
\eta_{0}^{2}
.

In general, however, the eigenstates of the effective Hamiltonian are nonseparable hybrids of atomic and vibrational excitations. In the following we investigate this hybridzation in a finite chain with open boundaries. In particular, we evaluate numerically, for a fixed number of atoms
N
and phonons
n_{\mathrm{ph}}
, the eigenstates
\ket{\psi_{m}}
and eigenvalues
E_{m}
of the effective Hamiltonian (
2
), where
m=1,2,\dots,{N+n_{\mathrm{ph}}-1\choose n_{\mathrm{ph}}}
with the binomial coefficient giving the Hilbert space dimension. For each state we then calculate the collective decay rate
\gamma_{m}=-2\mathrm{Im}(E_{m})
and the spin-motion entanglement. To quantify the latter, we trace out the phonon degrees of freedom of each state
\rho^{m}_{\mathrm{tot}}=\ket{\psi_{m}}\bra{\psi_{m}}
and obtain the reduced (generally mixed) density matrix
\rho_{m}=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{ph}}(\rho^{m}_{\mathrm{tot}})
. Subsequently, we calculate the entanglement entropy of the hybrid (bipartite) atom-phonon system,

S_{m}=-\rho_{m}\log\rho_{m},

which is zero for any product state. In Figs.
3
c and d we show
\gamma_{m}
and
S_{m}
for an atomic chain with
N=5
atoms with
n_{\mathrm{ph}}=2
phonons. The majority of the eigenstates feature entanglement, i.e.
S_{m}\neq 0
, when
\eta_{0}
. Moreover, there exist
N
eigenstates that are not entangled (
S_{m}=0
). These correspond to center of mass vibrations, similar to what was found in the infinite chain. Interestingly, when increasing the system size
N
, some of the hybridized states come close to saturating the entanglement entropy, i.e. they reach an entanglement entropy near the maximum allowed value,
\log{N}
. As depicted in Fig.
3
e, this saturation with increasing
N
is faster the smaller the ratio
d/\lambda_{0}
between the lattice constant and the wavelength of the atomic transition, where the interactions between the atoms become increasingly stronger. Nevertheless, also these heavily hybridized states clearly manifest super- and subradiance.

Conclusions and outlook.
We have investigated the impact of quantized motion on the collective decay of a chain of atoms coupled to the radiation field. Our findings show that super- and subradiance are robust quantum phenomena, which also manifest in hybrid systems. In the future it would be interesting to use the derived master equation to go beyond the regime of a single atomic excitation studied here, and to investigate the impact of the spin-phonon coupling in a many-body setting (e.g. superradiance from a fully inverted state
[
15
,
43
]
or a strongly driven system
[
44
,
45
]
). Other future directions include the consideration of lattices with higher dimensions and with varying geometry, and to study their interplay with angular-dependent dipolar interactions that necessitate to go beyond the two-level approximation. Moreover, exploring the regime of strong coupling, i.e. beyond the Lamb-Dicke limit, appears to be a fruitful avenue for future studies.

I
Derivation of the Master equation from first principles

We consider here a system of two-level atoms, whose internal degrees of freedom (separated by an energy
\hbar\omega_{0}
) interact with the motional ones (vibrations in their dipole traps) and the free radiation field. We start from the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H=\hbar\sum_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\nu_{k}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}c_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}+\hbar\omega_{0}\sum_{j}\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j}+\hbar\omega\sum_{j}a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{j}+\hbar\sum_{j,\mathbf{k},\lambda}g_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}(\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}+\sigma_{j})(c_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}}+c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}e^{-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}})

where we have introduced the the spin ladder operators
\sigma_{j}=\ket{\downarrow}_{j}\!\bra{\uparrow}
and
\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}=\ket{\uparrow}_{j}\!\bra{\downarrow}
, the bosonic creation and annihilation operators
c_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}^{\dagger}
and
c_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}
of a photon with momentum
\mathbf{k}
, energy
\hbar\nu_{k}=\hbar c|\mathbf{k}|
and polarization
\lambda=1,2
, and the coupling constant between the emitters and the radiation field
g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}=\mathbf{d}\cdot\hat{\epsilon}^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}\sqrt{\frac{\nu_{k}}{2\varepsilon_{0}\hbar V}}
, with
\mathbf{d}
,
\hat{\epsilon}^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}
,
V
being the atomic dipole moment, the field polarization unit vector and the quantization volume, respectively. The position vector
\mathbf{r}_{j}
can be written in terms of creation and annihilation operators
a_{j}^{\dagger}
and
a_{j}
of a phonon with energy
\hbar\omega\ll\hbar\omega_{0}
(representing the quantized motion in the traps) as

\mathbf{r}_{j}=\mathbf{r}_{j}^{0}+\hat{z}\frac{z_{\mathrm{ho}}}{\sqrt{2}}(a_{j}+a^{\dagger}_{j}),

with
z_{\mathrm{ho}}=\sqrt{\hbar/m\omega}
,
m
being the mass of the atom. We will work in the Lamb-Dicke regime, where
z_{\mathrm{ho}}\ll\lambda
, with
\lambda
being the wavelength of the atomic transition. This allows us to introduce the (small) parameter
\eta=(\mathbf{k}\cdot\hat{z})z_{\mathrm{ho}}/\sqrt{2}=\ll 1
. We go now into the interaction picture with respect to the atomic and field frequencies, i.e.

H^{\prime}=\hbar\omega\sum_{j}a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{j}-\hbar\sum_{j,\mathbf{k},\lambda}g_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}(e^{i\omega_{0}t}\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}+e^{-i\omega_{0}t}\sigma_{j})\left[c_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}e^{\mathrm{i}\eta(a_{j}^{\dagger}+a_{j})}e^{\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}^{0}-\nu_{k}t)}+c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}e^{-\mathrm{i}\eta(a_{j}^{\dagger}+a_{j})}e^{-\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}^{0}-\nu_{k}t)}\right].

Now we need to go into the interaction picture with respect to the phonons. Here, we perform an expansion for small
\eta
such that

e^{\mathrm{i}\omega ta_{j}^{\dagger}a_{j}}e^{\mathrm{i}\eta(a_{j}^{\dagger}+a_{j})}e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega ta_{j}^{\dagger}a_{j}}\approx 1+\mathrm{i}\eta(e^{\mathrm{i}\omega t}a_{j}^{\dagger}+e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}a_{j})-\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}\left[1+2a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{j}+e^{2\mathrm{i}\omega t}(a_{j}^{\dagger})^{2}+e^{-2\mathrm{i}\omega t}(a_{j})^{2}\right],

which in turn gives the Hamiltonian

H^{\prime\prime}=H_{0}+H_{1}+H_{2}

where

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\hbar\sum_{j,\mathbf{k},\lambda}g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}A_{j}(t)\left[c_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}e^{\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}^{0}-\nu_{k}t)}+c_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}^{\dagger}e^{-\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}^{0}-\nu_{k}t)}\right]

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\hbar\sum_{j,\mathbf{k},\lambda}\mathrm{i}\eta g_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}A_{j}(t)\left[c_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}e^{\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}^{0}-\nu_{k}t)}-c_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}^{\dagger}e^{-\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}^{0}-\nu_{k}t)}\right](e^{\mathrm{i}\omega t}a_{j}^{\dagger}+e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}a_{j})

\displaystyle H_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar\sum_{j,\mathbf{k},\lambda}\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}A_{j}(t)\left[c_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}e^{\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}^{0}-\nu_{k}t)}+c_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}^{\dagger}e^{-\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}^{0}-\nu_{k}t)}\right]\left[1+2a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{j}+e^{2\mathrm{i}\omega t}(a_{j}^{\dagger})^{2}+e^{-2\mathrm{i}\omega t}(a_{j})^{2}\right]

with

A_{j}(t)=e^{i\omega_{0}t}\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}+e^{-i\omega_{0}t}\sigma_{j}.

Given the form of the last Hamiltonian,
H_{2}
, it is convenient to separate two contributions
H_{2}=H_{2}^{0}+H_{2}^{2}
, namely the phonon number conserving and non-conserving ones, where

\displaystyle H_{2}^{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar\sum_{j,\mathbf{k},\lambda}\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}g_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}A_{j}(t)\left[c_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}e^{\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}^{0}-\nu_{k}t)}+c_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}^{\dagger}e^{-\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}^{0}-\nu_{k}t)}\right]\left[1+2a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{j}\right]

\displaystyle H_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar\sum_{j,\mathbf{k},\lambda}\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}g_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}A_{j}(t)\left[c_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}e^{\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}^{0}-\nu_{k}t)}+c_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}^{\dagger}e^{-\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}^{0}-\nu_{k}t)}\right]\left[e^{2\mathrm{i}\omega t}(a_{j}^{\dagger})^{2}+e^{-2\mathrm{i}\omega t}(a_{j})^{2}\right].

We are interested here in obtaining the dynamics of the density matrix
\rho
containing atomic and motional degrees of freedom only, by tracing out the free radiation field. To do so, we introduce our Hamiltonian
H^{\prime\prime}
into the Redfield equation

\dot{\rho}=-\frac{1}{\hbar^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left\{\left[H^{\prime\prime}(t),\left[H^{\prime\prime}(t-\tau),\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}\right]\right]\right\},

where the subscript
E
represents the radiation field degrees of freedom. Inspecting now the three terms of the Hamiltonian, we can see that we obtain first a leading contribution, which is exactly the Lindblad master equation for atoms coupled to the radiation field in the absence of atom-phonon coupling
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. Next, the terms proportional to
\eta
contain
\left[H_{0}(t),\left[H_{1}(t-\tau),\rho\right]\right]
and
\left[H_{1}(t),\left[H_{0}(t-\tau),\rho\right]\right]
. The contribution of these terms is negligible under the secular (rotating wave) approximation, as here all terms are proportional to
e^{\pm\mathrm{i}\omega t}
. The next and final order we wish to consider is the proportional to
\eta^{2}
. Here, there are two types of contributions we have to consider: the ones that arise from
\left[H_{1}(t),\left[H_{1}(t-\tau),\rho\right]\right]
and the ones with
\left[H_{0}(t),\left[H_{2}^{0}(t-\tau),\rho\right]\right]
and
\left[H_{2}^{0}(t),\left[H_{0}(t-\tau),\rho\right]\right]
. We will calculate these terms explicitly. Note, that we neglect directly the terms involving the phonon number non-conserving Hamiltonian
H_{2}^{2}
, as again they drop out under the secular approximation. In the following, we explicitly calculate all on these surviving contributions.

Leading term:
\left[H_{0}(t),\left[H_{0}(t-\tau),\rho\right]\right]

Starting from the Redfield equation, and introducing the notation

B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)=c_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}e^{\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}^{0}-\nu_{k}t)},

we have

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}=&-\frac{1}{\hbar^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left\{\left[H_{0}(t),\left[H_{0}(t-\tau),\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}\right]\right]\right\}\\
=&-\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}jj^{\prime}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}g_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}{g_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{*}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left\{\left[A_{j}(t)\left(B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)\right),\left[A_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\left(B_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right),\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}\right]\right]\right\}\\
=&-\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}jj^{\prime}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}{g^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}^{*}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left\{\left[A_{j}(t)\left(B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)\right),A_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\left(B_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right)\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}\right]\right.\\
&\left.-\left[A_{j}(t)\left(B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)\right),\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}A_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\left(B_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right)\right]\right\}\\
=&-\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}jj^{\prime}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}{g^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}^{*}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left\{A_{j}(t)A_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\left(B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)\right)\left(B_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right)\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}\right.\\
&-A_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\left(B_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right)\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}A_{j}(t)\left(B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)\right)\\
&-A_{j}(t)\left(B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)\right)\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}A_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\left(B_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right)\\
&\left.+\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}A_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\left(B_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right)A_{j}(t)\left(B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)\right)\right\}.\end{split}

Here, we can eliminate the majority of terms by considering the environment as a zero-temperature photon bath. Thus,

\displaystyle\left<B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\right>=\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left\{B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\rho_{E}\right\}=\delta_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\delta_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}e^{\mathrm{i}\left[\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}-\nu_{k}(t-t^{\prime})\right]}

with
\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}=\mathbf{r}^{0}_{j}-\mathbf{r}^{0}_{j^{\prime}}
, are the only non-zero expectation values with respect to the environment. The equation is then reduced to

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}=&-\sum_{\mathbf{k}\lambda jj^{\prime}}|g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\left\{\left[A_{j}(t)A_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\rho(t)-A_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\rho(t)A_{j}(t)\right]e^{\mathrm{i}\left(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}-\nu_{k}\tau\right)}\right.\\
&+\left.\left[\rho(t)A_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)A_{j}(t)-A_{j}(t)\rho(t)A_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right]e^{-\mathrm{i}\left(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}-\nu_{k}\tau\right)}\right\}.\end{split}

Now we have to write out the
A_{j}(t)
terms and perform the secular approximation, neglecting all terms that still contain an imaginary exponential that depends on
t
(also called the rotating wave approximation). For example:

A_{j}(t)A_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\rho(t)=(e^{i\omega_{0}t}\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}+e^{-i\omega_{0}t}\sigma_{j})(e^{i\omega_{0}t}e^{-i\omega_{0}\tau}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}+e^{-i\omega_{0}t}e^{i\omega_{0}\tau}\sigma_{j^{\prime}})\rho\approx(\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}e^{i\omega_{0}\tau}+\sigma_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}e^{-i\omega_{0}\tau})\rho.

After this, we have

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}=&-\sum_{\mathbf{k}\lambda jj^{\prime}}|g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\left\{\left[(\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho-\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger})e^{i(\omega_{0}-\nu_{k})\tau}+(\sigma_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho-\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho\sigma_{j})e^{-i(\omega_{0}+\nu_{k})\tau}\right]e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}+\right.\\
&\left.\left[(\rho\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}-\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\rho\sigma_{j^{\prime}})e^{i(\omega_{0}+\nu_{k})\tau}+(\rho\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j}-\sigma_{j}\rho\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger})e^{-i(\omega_{0}-\nu_{k})\tau}\right]e^{-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\right\}\\
=&-\sum_{\mathbf{k}\lambda jj^{\prime}}|g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\left[(\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho-\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger})e^{i(\omega_{0}-\nu_{k})\tau}+(\sigma_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho-\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho\sigma_{j})e^{-i(\omega_{0}+\nu_{k})\tau}\right.\\
&\left.+(\rho\sigma_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}-\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho\sigma_{j})e^{i(\omega_{0}+\nu_{k})\tau}+(\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}-\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger})e^{-i(\omega_{0}-\nu_{k})\tau}\right],\end{split}

where in the last step we have exchanged
j\to j^{\prime}
in the second line. We perform the time integral, which is done by using the so-called Heitler function

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau\,e^{-i(\omega_{0}\pm\nu_{k})\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi\delta(\omega_{0}\pm\nu_{k})-i\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}\pm\nu_{k}}\Big)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau\,e^{i(\omega_{0}\pm\nu_{k})\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi\delta(\omega_{0}\pm\nu_{k})+i\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}\pm\nu_{k}}\Big),

where
{\cal P}
represents the Principal Cauchy Value. After this, we can separate two types of contributions, the ones proportional to the delta function and the Principal Cauchy Value. Moreover, we neglect all the terms proportional to
\delta(\omega_{0}+\nu_{k})
, which will lead to a zero contribution after performing the integral in
k
-space, which runs for positive frequencies only. After this, we obtain

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}=&-\sum_{\mathbf{k}\lambda jj^{\prime}}|g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\left[\pi\delta(\omega_{0}-\nu_{k})\left(\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho+\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}-2\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\right)\right.\\
&+\mathrm{i}\left[(\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho-\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}-\nu_{k}}\Big)-(\sigma_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho-\rho\sigma_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}+\nu_{k}}\Big)\right],\end{split}

such that we have a real and an imaginary contribution. In order continue we need first to turn the sum over
\mathbf{k}
into an integral, yielding,

\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\to\frac{V}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{\lambda=1,2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k_{x}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k_{y}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k_{z}=\frac{V}{(2\pi c)^{3}}\sum_{\lambda=1,2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\sin\theta\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}\nu_{k}^{2},

where we have used spherical coordinates (see Fig.
S4
a). Writing the dipole moment in terms of the perpendicular vectors
\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}
with
\lambda=1,2
and
\hat{k}
, the sum over the polarizations yields

\sum_{\lambda=1,2}|\hat{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\hat{d}|^{2}=1-|\hat{k}\cdot\hat{d}|^{2}=\sin^{2}\theta,

see Figure
S4
b.

Introducing this into the master equation, we have

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}=&\frac{\omega_{0}^{3}|\mathbf{d}|^{2}\pi}{\epsilon_{0}\hbar(2\pi c)^{3}}\sum_{jj^{\prime}}\left(\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}},\rho\right\}\right)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\sin^{3}\theta e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\omega_{0}}{c}\hat{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\\
&-\frac{\mathrm{i}|\mathbf{d}|^{2}}{2\epsilon_{0}\hbar(2\pi c)^{3}}\sum_{jj^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}\nu_{k}^{3}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\sin^{3}\theta e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\left[(\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho-\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}-\nu_{k}}\Big)\right.\\
&\left.-(\sigma_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho-\rho\sigma_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}+\nu_{k}}\Big)\right],\end{split}

and, considering that the integral over the angular part in
\mathbf{k}
-space is given by

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\sin^{3}\theta e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}=4\pi F(kr^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}),

with

F(kr^{0}_{jj^{\prime}})=\left[1-(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}})^{2}\right]\frac{\sin{kr^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}}{kr^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}+\left[1-3(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}})^{2}\right]\left[\frac{\cos{kr^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}}{(kr^{0}_{jj^{\prime}})^{2}}-\frac{\sin{kr^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}}{(kr^{0}_{jj^{\prime}})^{3}}\right],

with
\hat{d}\cdot\hat{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}=\cos\varphi
(see Fig.
S4
c), we obtain

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}=&\frac{\omega_{0}^{3}|\mathbf{d}|^{2}}{3\epsilon_{0}\hbar\pi c^{3}}\sum_{jj^{\prime}}\frac{3}{2}F(k_{0}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}})\left(\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}},\rho\right\}\right)-\frac{\mathrm{i}|\mathbf{d}|^{2}}{3\epsilon_{0}\hbar c^{3}\pi^{2}}\sum_{jj^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}\nu_{k}^{3}\frac{3}{4}F\left(\frac{\nu_{k}}{c}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}\right)\\
&\times\left[(\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho-\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}-\nu_{k}}\Big)-(\sigma_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho-\rho\sigma_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}+\nu_{k}}\Big)\right].\end{split}

Now we use that
F
is invariant under the exchange
j\to j^{\prime}
and obtain

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}=&\frac{\omega_{0}^{3}|\mathbf{d}|^{2}}{3\epsilon_{0}\hbar\pi c^{3}}\sum_{jj^{\prime}}\frac{3}{2}F(k_{0}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}})\left(\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}},\rho\right\}\right)\\
&+\frac{\mathrm{i}|\mathbf{d}|^{2}}{3\epsilon_{0}\hbar c^{3}\pi^{2}}\sum_{j\neq j^{\prime}}\left[\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j},\rho\right]\frac{3}{4}\mathcal{P}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}\nu_{k}^{3}F\left(\frac{\nu_{k}}{c}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}\right)\left[\frac{1}{\nu_{k}+\omega_{0}}+\frac{1}{\nu_{k}-\omega_{0}}\right]\\
&+\frac{\mathrm{i}|\mathbf{d}|^{2}}{3\epsilon_{0}\hbar c^{3}\pi^{2}}\frac{3}{4}\sum_{j}\mathcal{P}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}\nu_{k}^{3}\left[\frac{\rho\sigma_{z}^{j}}{\nu_{k}-\omega_{0}}-\frac{\sigma_{z}^{j}\rho}{\nu_{k}+\omega_{0}}\right].\end{split}

The last term has the same value for all atoms and is absorbed into the atomic energy
[
2
]
. To evaluate the integral in the second term, we use that the function
F
is even in the variable
\nu_{k}
, such that we can extend the integral and obtain

\mathcal{P}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}\nu_{k}^{3}F\left(\frac{\nu_{k}}{c}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}\right)\left[\frac{1}{\nu_{k}+\omega_{0}}+\frac{1}{\nu_{k}-\omega_{0}}\right]=\mathcal{P}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}F\left(\frac{\nu_{k}}{c}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}\right)\frac{\nu_{k}^{3}}{\nu_{k}-\omega_{0}}=\pi\omega_{0}^{3}G\left(k_{0}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}\right),

with

G(k_{0}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}})=\left[1-(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}})^{2}\right]\frac{\cos{k_{0}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}}{k_{0}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}-\left[1-3(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}})^{2}\right]\left[\frac{\sin{k_{0}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}}{(k_{0}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}})^{2}}+\frac{\cos{k_{0}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}}{(k_{0}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}})^{3}}\right].

Finally, making the definitions

\gamma=\frac{\omega_{0}^{3}|\mathbf{d}|^{2}}{3\epsilon_{0}\hbar\pi c^{3}}\qquad\Gamma_{jj^{\prime}}=\frac{3}{2}\gamma F(k_{0}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}})\qquad V_{jj^{\prime}}=\frac{3}{4}\gamma G(k_{0}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}),

we obtain

\dot{\rho}=\sum_{jj^{\prime}}\Gamma_{jj^{\prime}}\left(\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}},\rho\right\}\right)+\mathrm{i}\sum_{j\neq j^{\prime}}V_{jj^{\prime}}\left[\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j},\rho\right],

which is the zero-th order for the master equation. We will now apply these same steps to the other terms that constitute the second order perturbation.

Terms proportional to
\eta^{2}
:
\left[H_{1}(t),\left[H_{1}(t-\tau),\rho\right]\right]

Starting again from the Redfield equation, and introducing the notation

C_{j}(t)=e^{\mathrm{i}\omega t}a_{j}^{\dagger}+e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}a_{j},\qquad\tilde{A}_{j}(t)=A_{j}(t)C_{j}(t)

we have

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}=&-\frac{1}{\hbar^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left\{\left[H_{1}(t),\left[H_{1}(t-\tau),\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}\right]\right]\right\}\\
=&\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}jj^{\prime}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\eta^{2}g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}{g^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}^{*}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left\{\left[\tilde{A}_{j}(t)\left(B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)-B^{\dagger}_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)\right),\left[\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\left(B_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)-B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right),\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}\right]\right]\right\}\\
=&\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}jj^{\prime}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\eta^{2}g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}{g^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}^{*}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left\{\left[\tilde{A}_{j}(t)\left(B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)-B^{\dagger}_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)\right),\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\left(B_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)-B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right)\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}\right]\right.\\
&\left.-\left[\tilde{A}_{j}(t)\left(B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)-B^{\dagger}_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)\right),\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\left(\lambda^{\prime}B_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)-B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right)\right]\right\}\\
=\!\!\!\!\!\!&\sum_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}jj^{\prime}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\eta^{2}g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}{g^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}^{*}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left\{\tilde{A}_{j}(t)\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\left(B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)-B^{\dagger}_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)\right)\left(B_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)-B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right)\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}\right.\\
&-\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\left(B_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)-B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right)\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}\tilde{A}_{j}(t)\left(B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)-B^{\dagger}_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)\right)\\
&-\tilde{A}_{j}(t)\left(B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)-B^{\dagger}_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)\right)\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\left(B_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)-B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right)\\
&\left.+\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\left(B_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)-B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right)\tilde{A}_{j}(t)\left(B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)-B^{\dagger}_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)\right)\right\}.\end{split}

Taking again the expectation value with respect to the environment, i.e. the radiation field at zero temperature, we obtain

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}=&-\sum_{\mathbf{k}\lambda jj^{\prime}}\eta^{2}|g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\left\{\left[\tilde{A}_{j}(t)\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\rho(t)-\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\rho(t)\tilde{A}_{j}(t)\right]e^{\mathrm{i}\left(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}-\nu_{k}\tau\right)}\right.\\
&+\left.\left[\rho(t)\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\tilde{A}_{j}(t)-\tilde{A}_{j}(t)\rho(t)\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right]e^{-\mathrm{i}\left(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}-\nu_{k}\tau\right)}\right\}.\end{split}

We write out the
\tilde{A}_{j}(t)
terms and perform the secular approximation, neglecting all terms that contain a exponential function that depends on the time
t
, for example,

\begin{split}\tilde{A}_{j}(t)\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\rho(t)=&(e^{i\omega_{0}t}\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}+e^{-i\omega_{0}t}\sigma_{j})(e^{\mathrm{i}\omega t}a_{j}^{\dagger}+e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}a_{j})(e^{i\omega_{0}t}e^{-i\omega_{0}\tau}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}+e^{-i\omega_{0}t}e^{i\omega_{0}\tau}\sigma_{j^{\prime}})(e^{\mathrm{i}\omega t}e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega\tau}a_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}+e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}e^{\mathrm{i}\omega\tau}a_{j^{\prime}})\rho\\
\approx&(\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}a_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}a_{j^{\prime}}e^{i(\omega_{0}+\omega)\tau}+\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}a_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}a_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}e^{i(\omega_{0}-\omega)\tau}+\sigma_{j}a_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}a_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}e^{-i(\omega_{0}+\omega)\tau}+\sigma_{j}a_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}a_{j^{\prime}}e^{-i(\omega_{0}-\omega)\tau})\rho.\end{split}

Now we can write, using the shortcut notation
\omega_{\pm}=\omega_{0}\pm\omega
and
J_{j}=\sigma_{j}a_{j}
and
L_{j}=\sigma_{j}a_{j}^{\dagger}
,

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}=&-\sum_{\mathbf{k}\lambda jj^{\prime}}\eta^{2}|g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\left\{\left[(J_{j}^{\dagger}J_{j^{\prime}}\rho-J_{j^{\prime}}\rho J_{j}^{\dagger})e^{i(\omega_{+}-\nu_{k})\tau}+(J_{j}J_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho-J_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho J_{j})e^{-i(\omega_{+}+\nu_{k})\tau}\right]e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\right.\\
&+\left.\left[(\rho J_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}J_{j}-J_{j}^{\dagger}\rho J_{j^{\prime}})e^{i(\omega_{+}+\nu_{k})\tau}+(\rho J_{j^{\prime}}J_{j}^{\dagger}-J_{j}\rho J_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger})e^{-i(\omega_{+}-\nu_{k})\tau}\right]e^{-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\right\}\\
&-\sum_{\mathbf{k}\lambda jj^{\prime}}\eta^{2}|g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\left\{\left[(L_{j}^{\dagger}L_{j^{\prime}}\rho-L_{j^{\prime}}\rho L_{j}^{\dagger})e^{i(\omega_{-}-\nu_{k})\tau}+(L_{j}L_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho-L_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho L_{j})e^{-i(\omega_{-}+\nu_{k})\tau}\right]e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\right.\\
&+\left.\left[(\rho L_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}L_{j}-L_{j}^{\dagger}\rho L_{j^{\prime}})e^{i(\omega_{-}+\nu_{k})\tau}+(\rho L_{j^{\prime}}L_{j}^{\dagger}-L_{j}\rho L_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger})e^{-i(\omega_{-}-\nu_{k})\tau}\right]e^{-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\right\}\\
=&-\sum_{\mathbf{k}\lambda jj^{\prime}}\eta^{2}|g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\left[(J_{j}^{\dagger}J_{j^{\prime}}\rho-J_{j^{\prime}}\rho J_{j}^{\dagger})e^{i(\omega_{+}-\nu_{k})\tau}+(J_{j}J_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho-J_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho J_{j})e^{-i(\omega_{+}+\nu_{k})\tau}\right.+\\
&\left.(\rho J_{j}^{\dagger}J_{j^{\prime}}-J_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho J_{j})e^{i(\omega_{+}+\nu_{k})\tau}+(\rho J_{j}J_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}-J_{j^{\prime}}\rho J_{j}^{\dagger})e^{-i(\omega_{+}-\nu_{k})\tau}\right]\\
&-\sum_{\mathbf{k}\lambda jj^{\prime}}\eta^{2}|g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\left[(L_{j}^{\dagger}L_{j^{\prime}}\rho-L_{j^{\prime}}\rho L_{j}^{\dagger})e^{i(\omega_{-}-\nu_{k})\tau}+(L_{j}L_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho-L_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho L_{j})e^{-i(\omega_{-}+\nu_{k})\tau}+\right.\\
&\left.(\rho L_{j}^{\dagger}L_{j^{\prime}}-L_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho L_{j})e^{i(\omega_{-}+\nu_{k})\tau}+(\rho L_{j}L_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}-L_{j^{\prime}}\rho L_{j}^{\dagger})e^{-i(\omega_{-}-\nu_{k})\tau}\right],\end{split}

where in the last step we have exchanged
j\to j^{\prime}
in the second lines. We can see now that we have the same structure as in the leading case, but with two terms with two frequencies and two jump operators:

J_{j}=\sigma_{j}a_{j}\quad\text{with}\quad\omega_{+}\quad\text{and}\quad L_{j}=\sigma_{j}a^{\dagger}_{j}\quad\text{with}\quad\omega_{-}.

All of the mathematical steps that follow this point are thus parallel to the ones we used for the leading order case. We use first the Heitler function and obtain

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}=&-\sum_{\mathbf{k}\lambda jj^{\prime}}\eta^{2}|g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\left[\pi\delta(\omega_{+}-\nu_{k})\left(J_{j}^{\dagger}J_{j^{\prime}}\rho+\rho J_{j}^{\dagger}J_{j^{\prime}}-2J_{j^{\prime}}\rho J_{j}^{\dagger}\right)\right.\\
&+\mathrm{i}\left[(J_{j}^{\dagger}J_{j^{\prime}}\rho-\rho J_{j}^{\dagger}J_{j^{\prime}})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{+}-\nu_{k}}\Big)-(J_{j}J_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho-\rho J_{j}J_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{+}+\nu_{k}}\Big)\right]\\
&-\sum_{\mathbf{k}\lambda jj^{\prime}}\eta^{2}|g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\left[\pi\delta(\omega_{-}-\nu_{k})\left(L_{j}^{\dagger}L_{j^{\prime}}\rho+\rho L_{j}^{\dagger}L_{j^{\prime}}-2L_{j^{\prime}}\rho L_{j}^{\dagger}\right)\right.\\
&+\mathrm{i}\left[(L_{j}^{\dagger}L_{j^{\prime}}\rho-\rho L_{j}^{\dagger}L_{j^{\prime}})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{-}-\nu_{k}}\Big)-(L_{j}L_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho-\rho L_{j}L_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{-}+\nu_{k}}\Big)\right].\end{split}

Taking again the continuum limit of the momentum sum, and realizing the sum over the two polarizations, we obtain, after substituting
\eta^{2}=(\mathbf{k}\cdot\hat{z})^{2}z_{\mathrm{ho}}^{2}/2
, that

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}=&\frac{\omega_{+}^{3}z_{\mathrm{ho}}^{2}|\mathbf{d}|^{2}\pi}{2\epsilon_{0}\hbar(2\pi)^{3}c^{3}}\sum_{jj^{\prime}}\left(J_{j^{\prime}}\rho J_{j}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{J_{j}^{\dagger}J_{j^{\prime}},\rho\right\}\right)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\sin^{3}\theta(\mathbf{k_{+}}\cdot\hat{z})^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\omega_{+}}{c}\hat{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\\
&-\frac{\mathrm{i}|\mathbf{d}|^{2}z_{\mathrm{ho}}^{2}}{4\epsilon_{0}\hbar(2\pi)^{3}c^{3}}\sum_{jj^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}\nu_{k}^{3}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\sin^{3}\theta(\mathbf{k}\cdot\hat{z})^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\\
&\times\left[(J_{j}^{\dagger}J_{j^{\prime}}\rho-\rho J_{j}^{\dagger}J_{j^{\prime}})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{+}-\nu_{k}}\Big)-(J_{j}J_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho-\rho J_{j}J_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{+}+\nu_{k}}\Big)\right]\\
&+\frac{\omega_{-}^{3}z_{\mathrm{ho}}^{2}|\mathbf{d}|^{2}\pi}{2\epsilon_{0}\hbar(2\pi)^{3}c^{3}}\sum_{jj^{\prime}}\left(J_{j^{\prime}}\rho J_{j}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{J_{j}^{\dagger}J_{j^{\prime}},\rho\right\}\right)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\sin^{3}\theta(\mathbf{k_{-}}\cdot\hat{z})^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\omega_{+}}{c}\hat{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\\
&-\frac{\mathrm{i}|\mathbf{d}|^{2}z_{\mathrm{ho}}^{2}}{4\epsilon_{0}\hbar(2\pi)^{3}c^{3}}\sum_{jj^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}\nu_{k}^{3}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\sin^{3}\theta(\mathbf{k}\cdot\hat{z})^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\\
&\times\left[(L_{j}^{\dagger}L_{j^{\prime}}\rho-\rho L_{j}^{\dagger}L_{j^{\prime}})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{-}-\nu_{k}}\Big)-(L_{j}L_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho-\rho L_{j}L_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{-}+\nu_{k}}\Big)\right].\end{split}

We need to perform next the integral over the angular variables in
\mathbf{k}
-space. For the particular situation we consider in this paper, the axis between the atoms is parallel to the direction of the displacement in the trap, i.e.
\mathbf{r}_{jj^{\prime}}^{0}=r_{jj^{\prime}}^{0}\hat{z}
. This allows us to write

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\sin^{3}\theta(\mathbf{k}\cdot\hat{z})^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}=\frac{1}{(r_{jj^{\prime}}^{0})^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\sin^{3}\theta(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}})^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}.

which notably simplifies the calculation of this integral, since we can use the relation

(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}})^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}=(kr_{jj^{\prime}}^{0})^{2}(\hat{k}\cdot\hat{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}})^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}(kr_{jj^{\prime}}^{0})^{2}(\hat{k}\cdot\hat{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}})^{2}}=-(kr_{jj^{\prime}}^{0})^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial(kr_{jj^{\prime}}^{0})^{2}}e^{\mathrm{i}(kr_{jj^{\prime}}^{0})^{2}(\hat{k}\cdot\hat{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}})^{2}},

such that

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\sin^{3}\theta(\mathbf{k}\cdot\hat{z})^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}=&-k^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial(kr_{jj^{\prime}}^{0})^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\sin^{3}\theta e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\\
=&-4\pi k^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial(kr_{jj^{\prime}}^{0})^{2}}F(kr_{jj^{\prime}}^{0})\equiv-4\pi k^{2}F^{\prime\prime}(kr_{jj^{\prime}}^{0}).\end{split}

Note, however, that this result is only valid for the case
\mathbf{r}_{jj^{\prime}}^{0}=r_{jj^{\prime}}^{0}\hat{z}
where
\hat{z}
is the direction of the atom vibration in the trap. In the more general case where the two vectors are not parallel, the calculation of this integral is more involved.

Introducing the result of the angular integral in our master equation we obtain

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}=&-\gamma\eta_{0}^{2}\sum_{jj^{\prime}}\frac{3}{2}F^{\prime\prime}(k_{0}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}})\left(J_{j^{\prime}}\rho J_{j}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{J_{j}^{\dagger}J_{j^{\prime}},\rho\right\}+L_{j^{\prime}}\rho L_{j}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{L_{j}^{\dagger}L_{j^{\prime}},\rho\right\}\right)\\
&+\frac{\mathrm{i}|\mathbf{d}|^{2}\eta_{0}^{2}}{3\epsilon_{0}\hbar c^{5}\pi^{2}k_{0}^{2}}\sum_{jj^{\prime}}\frac{3}{4}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}\nu_{k}^{5}F^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{\nu_{k}}{c}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}\right)\left[(J_{j}^{\dagger}J_{j^{\prime}}\rho-\rho J_{j}^{\dagger}J_{j^{\prime}})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}-\nu_{k}}\Big)-(J_{j}J_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho-\rho J_{j}J_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}+\nu_{k}}\Big)\right.\\
&\left.(L_{j}^{\dagger}L_{j^{\prime}}\rho-\rho L_{j}^{\dagger}L_{j^{\prime}})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}-\nu_{k}}\Big)-(L_{j}L_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho-\rho L_{j}L_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}+\nu_{k}}\Big)\right],\end{split}

with
\eta_{0}=k_{0}z_{\mathrm{ho}}/\sqrt{2}
, and where we have assumed that
\omega_{+}\approx\omega_{-}\approx\omega_{0}
. For the second term, we use that
F^{\prime\prime}
is an even function of the frequency, such that

\mathcal{P}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}\nu_{k}^{5}F^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{\nu_{k}}{c}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}\right)\left[\frac{1}{\nu_{k}+\omega_{0}}+\frac{1}{\nu_{k}-\omega_{0}}\right]=\mathcal{P}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}F^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{\nu_{k}}{c}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}\right)\frac{\nu_{k}^{5}}{\nu_{k}-\omega_{0}}=\pi\omega_{0}^{5}G^{\prime\prime}\left(k_{0}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}\right),

with

\displaystyle G^{\prime\prime}(kr_{jj^{\prime}}^{0})=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial(kr_{jj^{\prime}}^{0})^{2}}G(kr_{jj^{\prime}}^{0})

Finally, making the definitions
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}_{jj^{\prime}}=\frac{3\gamma}{2}F^{\prime\prime}(k_{0}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}})
and
V^{\prime\prime}_{jj^{\prime}}=\frac{3\gamma}{4}G^{\prime\prime}(k_{0}r^{0}_{jj^{\prime}})
, we obtain

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}=&-\eta_{0}^{2}\sum_{jj^{\prime}}\Gamma^{\prime\prime}_{jj^{\prime}}\left(J_{j^{\prime}}\rho J_{j}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{J_{j}^{\dagger}J_{j^{\prime}},\rho\right\}\right)-\mathrm{i}\eta_{0}^{2}\sum_{j\neq j^{\prime}}V^{\prime\prime}_{jj^{\prime}}\left[J_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}J_{j},\rho\right]\\
&-\eta_{0}^{2}\sum_{jj^{\prime}}\Gamma^{\prime\prime}_{jj^{\prime}}\left(L_{j^{\prime}}\rho L_{j}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{L_{j}^{\dagger}L_{j^{\prime}},\rho\right\}\right)-\mathrm{i}\eta_{0}^{2}\sum_{j\neq j^{\prime}}V^{\prime\prime}_{jj^{\prime}}\left[L_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}L_{j},\rho\right],\end{split}

where we have again considered only the terms
V^{\prime\prime}_{jj^{\prime}}
with
j\neq j^{\prime}
, absorbing the
j=j^{\prime}
energy shift into the atomic energy.

Terms proportional to
\eta^{2}
:
\left[H_{0}(t),\left[H_{2}^{0}(t-\tau),\rho\right]\right]
and
\left[H_{2}^{0}(t),\left[H_{0}(t-\tau),\rho\right]\right]

Starting again from the Redfield equation, and introducing the notation

C_{j}=1+2a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{j},\qquad\tilde{A}_{j}(t)=A_{j}(t)C_{j}

we have

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}=&-\frac{1}{\hbar^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left\{\left[H_{0}(t),\left[H_{2}^{0}(t-\tau),\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}\right]\right]\right\}\\
=&\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}jj^{\prime}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}{g^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}^{*}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left\{\left[A_{j}(t)\left(B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)\right),\left[\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\left(B_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right),\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}\right]\right]\right\}\\
=&\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}jj^{\prime}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}{g^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}^{*}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left\{\left[A_{j}(t)\left(B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)\right),\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\left(B_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right)\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}\right]\right.\\
&\left.-\left[A_{j}(t)\left(B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)\right),\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\left(B_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right)\right]\right\}\\
=&\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}jj^{\prime}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}{g^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}^{*}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left\{A_{j}(t)\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\left(B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)\right)\left(B_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right)\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}\right.\\
&-\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\left(B_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right)\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}A_{j}(t)\left(B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)\right)\\
&-A_{j}(t)\left(B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)\right)\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\left(B_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right)\\
&\left.+\rho(t)\otimes\rho_{E}\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\left(B_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right)A_{j}(t)\left(B_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)+B^{\dagger}_{\lambda\mathbf{k}j}(t)\right)\right\}.\end{split}

Taking again the expectation value with respect to the environment, i.e. the radiation field at zero temperature, we obtain

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}=&\sum_{\mathbf{k}\lambda jj^{\prime}}\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}|g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\left\{\left[A_{j}(t)\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\rho(t)-\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\rho(t)A_{j}(t)\right]e^{\mathrm{i}\left(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}-\nu_{k}\tau\right)}+\right.\\
&\left.\left[\rho(t)\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)A_{j}(t)-A_{j}(t)\rho(t)\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right]e^{-\mathrm{i}\left(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}-\nu_{k}\tau\right)}\right\}.\end{split}

We write out the
\tilde{A}_{j}(t)
terms and perform the secular approximation, neglecting all terms that contain a exponential function that depends on the time
t
, for example,

\displaystyle A_{j}(t)\tilde{A}_{j^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\rho(t)=

\displaystyle(e^{i\omega_{0}t}\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}+e^{-i\omega_{0}t}\sigma_{j})(e^{i\omega_{0}t}e^{-i\omega_{0}\tau}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}+e^{-i\omega_{0}t}e^{i\omega_{0}\tau}\sigma_{j^{\prime}})(1+2a_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}a_{j^{\prime}})\rho

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle(e^{-i\omega_{0}\tau}\sigma_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}+e^{i\omega_{0}\tau}\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}})(1+2a_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}a_{j^{\prime}})\rho.

Now we can write, using the shortcut notation
K_{j}=\sigma_{j}(1+2a^{\dagger}_{j}a_{j})
,

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}=&\sum_{\mathbf{k}\lambda jj^{\prime}}\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}|g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\left\{\left[(\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}K_{j^{\prime}}\rho-K_{j^{\prime}}\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger})e^{i(\omega_{0}-\nu_{k})\tau}+(\sigma_{j}K_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho-K_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho\sigma_{j})e^{-i(\omega_{0}+\nu_{k})\tau}\right]e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}+\right.\\
&\left.\left[(\rho K_{j^{\prime}}\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}-\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\rho K_{j^{\prime}})e^{i(\omega_{0}+\nu_{k})\tau}+(\rho K_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j}-\sigma_{j}\rho K_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger})e^{-i(\omega_{0}-\nu_{k})\tau}\right]e^{-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\right\}\\
=&\sum_{\mathbf{k}\lambda jj^{\prime}}\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}|g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\left[(\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}K_{j^{\prime}}\rho-K_{j^{\prime}}\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger})e^{i(\omega_{0}-\nu_{k})\tau}+(\sigma_{j}K_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho-K_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho\sigma_{j})e^{-i(\omega_{0}+\nu_{k})\tau}\right.\\
&\left.+(\rho K_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}-\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho K_{j})e^{i(\omega_{0}+\nu_{k})\tau}+(\rho K_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}-\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho K_{j}^{\dagger})e^{-i(\omega_{0}-\nu_{k})\tau}\right],\end{split}

where in the last step we have exchanged
j\to j^{\prime}
in the second lines. All of the mathematical steps that follow this point are the same as the ones used in the previous two sections and hence are only sketched. We use first the Heitler function and obtain

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}=&\sum_{\mathbf{k}\lambda jj^{\prime}}\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}|g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\left[\pi\delta(\omega_{0}-\nu_{k})\left(\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}K_{j^{\prime}}\rho+\rho K^{\dagger}_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}-K_{j^{\prime}}\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}-\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho K_{j}^{\dagger}\right)\right.\\
&\left.+\mathrm{i}\left[(\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}K_{j^{\prime}}\rho-\rho K^{\dagger}_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}-K_{j^{\prime}}\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}+\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho K_{j}^{\dagger})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}-\nu_{k}}\Big)-(\sigma_{j}K_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho-\rho K_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}-K_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho\sigma_{j}+\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho K_{j})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}+\nu_{k}}\Big)\right]\right].\end{split}

In order to write this term of the master equation, we now proceed the same way with the terms containing
\left[H_{2}^{0}(t),\left[H_{0}(t-\tau),\rho\right]\right]
to this expression. For symmetry reasons, these terms look identical to the ones we have already obtained but with the
K
and
\sigma
operators exchanged. Hence, adding these two terms we obtain the contribution

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}=&\sum_{\mathbf{k}\lambda jj^{\prime}}\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}|g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\left\{\pi\delta(\omega_{0}-\nu_{k})\left(\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}K_{j^{\prime}}\rho+\rho K^{\dagger}_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}-K_{j^{\prime}}\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}-\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho K_{j}^{\dagger}+K_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho+\rho\sigma^{\dagger}_{j}K_{j^{\prime}}-\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho K_{j}^{\dagger}-K_{j^{\prime}}\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}\right)\right.\\
&+\mathrm{i}\left[(\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}K_{j^{\prime}}\rho-\rho K^{\dagger}_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}-K_{j^{\prime}}\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}+\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho K_{j}^{\dagger}+K_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho-\rho\sigma^{\dagger}_{j}K_{j^{\prime}}-\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho K_{j}^{\dagger}+K_{j^{\prime}}\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}-\nu_{k}}\Big)\right.\\
&\left.\left.-(\sigma_{j}K_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho-\rho K_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}-K_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho\sigma_{j}+\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho K_{j}+K_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho-\rho\sigma_{j}K_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}-\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho K_{j}+K_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho\sigma_{j})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}+\nu_{k}}\Big)\right]\right\}\\
=&\sum_{\mathbf{k}\lambda jj^{\prime}}\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}|g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\left\{\pi\delta(\omega_{0}-\nu_{k})\left(\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}K_{j^{\prime}}\rho+\rho\sigma^{\dagger}_{j}K_{j^{\prime}}-2K_{j^{\prime}}\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}+K_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho+\rho K^{\dagger}_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}-2\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho K_{j}^{\dagger}\right)\right.\\
&\left.+\mathrm{i}\left[(\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}K_{j^{\prime}}\rho-\rho K^{\dagger}_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}+K_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho-\rho\sigma^{\dagger}_{j}K_{j^{\prime}})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}-\nu_{k}}\Big)-(\sigma_{j}K_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho-\rho K_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}+K_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rho-\rho\sigma_{j}K_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger})\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}+\nu_{k}}\Big)\right]\right\}.\end{split}

Absorbing again the
j=j^{\prime}
energy shift into the atomic energy, one obtains

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}=&-\sum_{\mathbf{k}\lambda jj^{\prime}}\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}|g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}2\pi\delta(\omega_{0}-\nu_{k})\left(K_{j^{\prime}}\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}K_{j^{\prime}},\rho\right\}+\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho K_{j}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{K_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}},\rho\right\}\right)\\
&-\mathrm{i}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\lambda j\neq j^{\prime}}\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}|g^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}^{0}_{jj^{\prime}}}\left(\left[\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}K_{j^{\prime}},\rho\right]+\left[K_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}},\rho\right]\right)\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\nu_{k}-\omega_{0}}+\frac{1}{\omega_{0}+\nu_{k}}\Big).\end{split}

Finally, we can write the final contribution to the dynamics of order
\eta_{0}^{2}
as

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}=&\frac{\eta_{0}^{2}}{2}\sum_{jj^{\prime}}\Gamma^{\prime\prime}_{jj^{\prime}}\left(K_{j^{\prime}}\rho\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}K_{j^{\prime}},\rho\right\}+\sigma_{j^{\prime}}\rho K_{j}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{K_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}},\rho\right\}\right)\\
&\mathrm{i}\frac{\eta_{0}^{2}}{2}\sum_{j\neq j^{\prime}}V^{\prime\prime}_{jj^{\prime}}\left(\left[\sigma_{j}^{\dagger}K_{j^{\prime}},\rho\right]+\left[K_{j}^{\dagger}\sigma_{j^{\prime}},\rho\right]\right).\end{split}

All of these contributions added together give rise to the final Lindblad equation that we provide in the main text.

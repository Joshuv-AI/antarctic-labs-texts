Abstract
In this contribution we propose an exponential update algorithm for magnetic moments appearing in the framework of micromagnetics and the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert (LLG) equation.

1
. Introduction

In this paper we discuss the numerical integration of the evolution equation of the normalized magnetic moments
{{\bm{m}}}
in micromagnetism (
5
).
This is described by a Landau-Lifshitz-type equation which consists of a precession and a dissipation related damping part, cf.
20
and
15
.
Algorithmic implementations are necessary to calculate the evolution of magnetic moments for complex problems.
Some algorithms may suffer from instabilities caused by the non-fulfillment of the constraint
\|{{\bm{m}}}\|=1
, this is the case, e.g., for the implicit Euler scheme.
An overview on constraining methods is given in
25
,
6
,
22
and
27
.
A simple approach to ensure the fulfillment of the unit length constraint is to utilize a projection scheme, which has been demonstrated in publications as
19
or
25
.
Here, the constraint is not considered during the iterative solution procedure itself, but at the end of each individual time step in terms of a renormalization of the magnetization vectors in the element vertices.
A similar method was presented by
29
, where the solution procedure is based on a staggered scheme.
Another straightforward approach is presented by
21
,
25
,
33
,
35
.
Here, the authors penalize all magnetization vectors that deviate from the unit length by adding a penalty term to the system of equations.
The penalization intensity is controlled by a penalty parameter that is especially for complex and strongly heterogeneous microstructures not easy to fit.
A slightly different way to restrict the vectors onto the unit sphere was presented by
31
,
24
and
26
in terms of a Lagrange multiplier.
Both methods, penalization or renormalization, introduce additional (artificial) contributions to the total energy of the systems.
Therefore, the resulting energies may differ from the expected physical energy of the magnetic system.

This problem is circumvented for a priori length preserving methods.
In this context spherical coordinates have been applied by
30
,
14
,
34
.
Another advantage of spherical coordinates is that they reduce the system of equations by one degree of freedom compared to Cartesian coordinates, what leads to faster simulations, cf.
28
.
However, this advantage is outweighed by the singularities in the poles.
Magnetization vectors that reach these singularities usually lead to non-converging solution procedures.
To overcome non-physical switching behavior
10
, an extension of the work of
34
, introduced a restriction of the azimuthal angle onto its definition domain
\left(-\pi,\pi\right]
on the interpolation level.
Also length preserving is the incremental update algorithm for the magnetization vectors proposed by
23
, using the geometrical structure for the integration on spheres which results after several algebraic manipulations in an exponential scheme.
The authors use the geometrical structure for the integration on spheres and construct, after some clever rearrangements, an exponential update.
However, they do not rely on a canonical structure for the exponential scheme.
Further, important aspects, especially concerning the treatment within the framework of the Finite-Element-Method can be found in
9
.
Another approach to preserve the length of the magnetization vectors was proposed by
2
. Here, the authors introduce a so-called
\theta
-scheme in analogy to the heat equation which is unconditional stable for a suitable choice of
\theta
.
A clever reformulation of the initial equation, e.g. based on the mid-point rule, leads to no further numerical methods to constrain the length, as demonstrated in
11
.

Our contribution is mainly motivated by the works of
13
,
32
and
22
.
In
13
the stability and accuracy of Euler and quaternion micromagnetic algorithms including Langevin random-fields has been investigated in detail. They conclude that their proposed quaternion based scheme is significantly more stable compared to Euler’s method.
A quaternion-based algorithm with separate precession rotations and dissipation damping, allowing analytical calculation of the precession part over long time intervals, was proposed by
32
.
This procedure allows the usage of large time increments compared to conventional algorithms, especially in the case of low anisotropy and weak exchange coupling.
The philosophy that numerical algorithms should capture the intrinsic (geometric) properties of the system has been analyzed in the well understandable but more mathematically oriented work by
22
.
Starting on the motivation of the usage of geometric integrators, different integrators are discussed and analyzed for micromagnetic applications.

This work is structured as follows:

First,
the LLG equation is reformulated and brought into a form suited for our proposed exponential update framework; the canonical form.

Second,
performing the algorithmic derivation for the implementation of the exponential method.

Third,
comparison of the performance of the proposed exponential method with the implicit Euler method and the midpoint rule using representative numerical examples.

Finally,
the results as well as the advantages and disadvantages of the method are concluded.

2
. Canonical structure of the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation

In dynamic micromagnetism, both an evolution equation and an enthalpy functional are needed to describe the magnetization vectors in space and time.
The evolution equation, here the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation (LLG), defines how the magnetization vectors evolve under external and internal influences.
These external and internal influences are described by the magnetic enthalpy and enter the LLG equation in the form of the so-called effective field
{{\bm{H}}}^{\rm eff}
, which is derived from a variational derivative of the enthalpy regarding the magnetization.
In this section, the LLG equation is introduced and reformulated for the discretization discussed in this work.
Subsequently, the enthalpy functional and the effective field are specified.
For an extensive overview on the foundations on magnetism and micromagnetics in particular the reader is referred to standard literature as
20
,
5
,
8
,
7
,
17
,
18
and
4
.

In order to describe the evolution of the magnetic moments

{{\bm{M}}}=M_{s}\,{{\bm{m}}}

, a representation using the magnetic unit director
{{\bm{m}}}
with the constraint condition

\|{{\bm{m}}}\|=1
and
\partial_{t}\|{{\bm{m}}}\|=0
is applied.
Hence, the so-called semi-implicit form of the LLG equation appears as

\partial_{t}{{\bm{m}}}=-\gamma\,{{\bm{m}}}\times{{\bm{H}}}^{eff}+\alpha\,{{\bm{m}}}\times\partial_{t}{{\bm{m}}}

(1)

with

\gamma=\mu_{0}\,\gamma_{e}\approx 2.2128\cdot 10^{-5}\,\textrm{m/As}
.
The goal is to derive a canonical form for the evolution equation for the magnetization

\partial_{t}{{{\bm{m}}}}={{\bm{W}}}({{\bm{m}}},{{\bm{H}}})\cdot{{\bm{m}}}\,,

(2)

suitable for exponential update algorithms.
Substituting the time derivative of the magnetization appearing
on the right-hand side of (
1
)
by the right-hand side of (
1
) yields

\partial_{t}{{\bm{m}}}=-\gamma\,{{\bm{m}}}\times{{\bm{H}}}^{eff}-\alpha\,\gamma\,{{\bm{m}}}\times({{\bm{m}}}\times{{\bm{H}}}^{eff})+\alpha^{2}\,{{\bm{m}}}\times({{\bm{m}}}\times\partial_{t}{{\bm{m}}})\,.

(3)

Applying the Graßmann identity to the last term of the right-hand-side of
3
and taking into account

\partial_{t}\|{{\bm{m}}}\|^{2}=2\,\partial_{t}{{{\bm{m}}}}\cdot{{\bm{m}}}=0
,
yields

{{\bm{m}}}\times({{\bm{m}}}\times\partial_{t}{{\bm{m}}})=-\partial_{t}{{\bm{m}}}\,.

(4)

Thus the evolution equation for the magnetization appears
in the so-called explicit form of the LLG equation

\partial_{t}{{{\bm{m}}}}=-\dfrac{\gamma}{1+\alpha^{2}}{{\bm{m}}}\times{{\bm{H}}}^{eff}-\dfrac{\alpha\,\gamma}{1+\alpha^{2}}{{\bm{m}}}\times({{\bm{m}}}\times{{\bm{H}}}^{eff})\,.

(5)

For convenience, we introduce the abbreviation
\beta=\dfrac{\gamma}{1+\alpha^{2}}

and change the order in the terms of the cross product, i.e.,

\partial_{t}{{{\bm{m}}}}=\beta\,{{\bm{H}}}^{eff}\times{{\bm{m}}}-\alpha\beta\,({{\bm{H}}}^{eff}\times{{\bm{m}}})\times{{\bm{m}}}\,.

(6)

By introducing the skew-symmetric matrix

{\bm{\Omega}}_{\left[{{\bm{H}}}\right]}=-{\bm{\Omega}}_{\left[{{\bm{H}}}\right]}^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-H_{3}&H_{2}\\
H_{3}&0&-H_{1}\\
-H_{2}&H_{1}&0\end{bmatrix}\,

we substitute

{{\bm{H}}}^{eff}\times{{\bm{m}}}={\bm{\Omega}}_{\left[{{\bm{H}}}^{eff}\right]}\cdot{{\bm{m}}}\,.

(7)

Furthermore, for

{\bm{\Omega}}_{\left[{{\bm{H}}}\times{{\bm{m}}}\right]}={{\bm{m}}}\otimes{{\bm{H}}}-{{\bm{H}}}\otimes{{\bm{m}}}=2\,\textrm{skew}[{\bm{\Omega}}_{\left[{{\bm{H}}}\right]}\cdot{\bm{\Omega}}_{\left[{{\bm{m}}}\right]}]
,
with the explicit expression

{\bm{\Omega}}_{\left[{{\bm{H}}}\times{{\bm{m}}}\right]}=\begin{bmatrix}0&H_{2}m_{1}-H_{1}m_{2}&H_{3}m_{1}-H_{1}m_{3}\\
-H_{2}m_{1}+H_{1}m_{2}&0&H_{3}m_{2}-H_{2}m_{3}\\
-H_{3}m_{1}+H_{1}m_{3}&-H_{3}m_{2}+H_{2}m_{3}&0\end{bmatrix}\,,

we substitute

({{\bm{H}}}^{eff}\times{{\bm{m}}})\times{{\bm{m}}}={\bm{\Omega}}_{\left[{{\bm{H}}}^{eff}\times{{\bm{m}}}\right]}\cdot{{\bm{m}}}\,.

(8)

Inserting this relations we obtain form (
6
) the evolution
equation for the magnetization vector

\boxed{\partial_{t}{{{\bm{m}}}}={{\bm{W}}}\cdot{{\bm{m}}}\quad\textrm{with}\quad{{\bm{W}}}:=\beta\,({\bm{\Omega}}_{\left[{{\bm{H}}}^{eff}\right]}-\alpha\,{\bm{\Omega}}_{\left[{{\bm{H}}}^{eff}\times{{\bm{m}}}\right]}),}

(9)

with the skew-symmetric matrix

{{\bm{W}}}=\beta\begin{bmatrix}0&-H_{3}-\alpha(H_{2}m_{1}-H_{1}m_{2})&H_{2}+\alpha(H_{1}m_{3}-H_{3}m_{1})\\
H_{3}+\alpha(H_{2}m_{1}-H_{1}m_{2})&0&-H_{1}-\alpha(H_{3}m_{2}-H_{2}m_{3})\\
-H_{2}-\alpha(H_{1}m_{3}-H_{3}m_{1})&H_{1}+\alpha(H_{3}m_{2}-H_{2}m_{3})&0\end{bmatrix}.

For convenience in further mathematical transformations,
we introduce the following abbreviations for the terms in
{{\bm{W}}}
:

{{\bm{W}}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-W_{21}&W_{13}\\
W_{21}&0&-W_{32}\\
-W_{13}&W_{32}&0\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-w_{3}&w_{2}\\
w_{3}&0&-w_{1}\\
-w_{2}&w_{1}&0\end{bmatrix}\,={\bm{\Omega}}_{\left[{{\bm{w}}}\right]}\,.

(10)

3
. Integration schemes

The canonical form of the LLG equation (
9
) is the basis for all further time integrators.
First, the exponential update algorithm is derived, which is based on the series expansion of a skew-symmetric matrix
{{\bm{W}}}
.
With the help of the explicit representation of the cofactor of
{{\bm{W}}}
and the application of the Caley-Hamilton theorem, the implicit algorithm to be solved iteratively finally follows.
The geometric properties of the numerical integrator lead to the preservation of structure (invariants, constraints) at the level of the time-discrete evolution equation.
For a general discussion of structure-preserving algorithms we refer
to
16
.
Subsequently, the implicit Euler algorithm (backward Euler) and the midpoint rule are specified for a comparative study.

3.1
Exponential update algorithm

An appropriate integrator for the evolution equation of the magnetization (
9
) in the special orthogonal group
\textrm{SO}(3)
,
defined by

\textrm{SO}(3):=\{{{\bm{Q}}}:{\rm I\kern-2.5ptR}^{3}\rightarrow{\rm I\kern-2.5ptR}^{3}\mid{{\bm{Q}}}^{T}{{\bm{Q}}}={\bf 1}\,,\;\operatorname{det}{{\bm{Q}}}=1\}
, is

{{{\bm{m}}}}_{n+1}=\textrm{exp}[{{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t}_{n+\xi}]\cdot{{\bm{m}}}_{n}\quad\textrm{with}\quad\xi\in[0,1]\,.

(11)

\xi=0
represents an explicit exponential Euler scheme,

\xi=1/2
the averaged exponential midpoint rule and

\xi=1
the implicit exponential Euler scheme.
Without further restrictions, we set
\xi=1
for the following considerations, thus we focus on

{{{\bm{m}}}}_{n+1}=\textrm{exp}[{{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t}_{n+1}]\cdot{{\bm{m}}}_{n}

(12)

with the time increment
\Delta t=t_{n+1}-t_{n}
and the time discretized matrix

{{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t}_{n+1}:=\beta\,\Delta t\,({\bm{\Omega}}_{\left[{{\bm{H}}}_{n+1}\right]}-\alpha\,{\bm{\Omega}}_{\left[{{\bm{H}}}^{eff}_{n+1}\times{{\bm{m}}}_{n+1}\right]})\,.

(13)

The series expansion for the exponential matrix form, with an infinite number of terms, is given by

\textrm{exp}[{{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t}_{n+1}]={\bf 1}+{{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t}_{n+1}+\dfrac{1}{2!}({{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t}_{n+1})^{2}+\dfrac{1}{3!}({{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t}_{n+1})^{3}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{n!}({{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t}_{n+1})^{n}+...\,.

(14)

Using the Cayley-Hamilton theorem, i.e.,

{{\bm{W}}}^{3}=I_{3}\,{\bf 1}-I_{2}\,{{\bm{W}}}+I_{1}{{\bm{W}}}^{2}\,,

(15)

we recognize that every power of the quadratic matrix can be represented as a tensor matrix with the tensor generators up to the second power:

{{\bm{W}}}^{n}=\gamma_{0}^{(n)}(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})\,{\bf 1}+\gamma_{1}^{(n)}(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})\,{{\bm{W}}}+\gamma_{2}^{(n)}(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}){{\bm{W}}}^{2}\,.

(16)

The parameters
\gamma_{i}^{(n)}(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})|i=1,2,3
are functions of the
invariants

I_{1}=\operatorname{tr}[{{{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t}}]\,,\;I_{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}(I_{1}^{2}-\operatorname{tr}[{({{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t})^{2}}])=\operatorname{tr}[{\operatorname{Cof}[{{{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t}}]}]\,,\;I_{3}=\operatorname{det}[{{{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t}}]\,

(17)

with the cofactor of (
10
) given by

\operatorname{Cof}[{{{\bm{W}}}}]=\begin{bmatrix}W_{32}^{2}&W_{13}W_{32}&W_{21}W_{32}\\
W_{13}W_{32}&W_{13}^{2}&W_{13}W_{21}\\
W_{21}W_{32}&W_{13}W_{21}&W_{21}^{2}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}w_{1}^{2}&w_{1}\,w_{2}&w_{1}\,w_{3}\\
w_{2}\,w_{1}&w_{2}^{2}&w_{2}\,w_{3}\\
w_{3}\,w_{1}&w_{3}\,w_{2}&w_{3}^{2}\end{bmatrix}={{\bm{w}}}\otimes{{\bm{w}}}\,.

In our specific case, a skew-symmetric matrix, results in the following invariants

I_{1}=0\,,\quad I_{2}=\operatorname{tr}[{\operatorname{Cof}{{{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t}}}]={{\bm{w}}}\cdot{{\bm{w}}}=:w^{2}\,,\quad I_{3}=0\,

(18)

and the simplified relation using the Cayley-Hamilton theorem, i.e.,

{{\bm{W}}}^{3}=-I_{2}\,{{\bm{W}}}\,.

(19)

If we now use a recursive function to calculate the odd and even powers of the matrix
{{\bm{W}}}

{{\bm{W}}}^{2n+1}=(-w^{2})^{n}\,{{\bm{W}}}\;,\qquad{{\bm{W}}}^{2n}=(-w^{2})^{n-1}\,{{\bm{W}}}^{2}\,,

(20)

applies for
n\geq 1
respectively.
Thus,
\textrm{exp}[{{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t}]
appears in series expansion form as

\textrm{exp}[{{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t}]={\bf 1}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{(2n+1)!}\;({{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t})^{2n+1}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{(2n)!}\;({{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t})^{2n}\,.

(21)

By inserting (
20
) into the latter expression, the
tensor representation simplifies to

\textrm{exp}[{{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t}]={\bf 1}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(-w^{2})^{n}}{(2n+1)!}\;{{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(-w^{2})^{n-1}}{(2n)!}\;({{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t})^{2}\,.

(22)

Let us express (
22
) in terms of the general tensor function for the exponential function of the skew-symmetric matrix

\textrm{exp}[{{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t}]={\bf 1}+\alpha_{1}{{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t}+\alpha_{2}({{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t})^{2}\,,

(23)

with vanishing first and third invariant, we identify

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rl}\alpha_{1}&=1-\dfrac{w^{2}}{3!}+\dfrac{w^{4}}{5!}-\dfrac{w^{6}}{7!}+\dfrac{w^{8}}{9!}-\dfrac{w^{10}}{11!}\pm\cdots\,\quad\rightsquigarrow\quad\alpha_{1}=\dfrac{\sin(w)}{w}\,,\\[8.53581pt]
\alpha_{2}&=\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{w^{2}}{4!}+\dfrac{w^{4}}{6!}-\dfrac{w^{6}}{8!}+\dfrac{w^{8}}{10!}-\dfrac{w^{10}}{12!}\pm\cdots\,\quad\rightsquigarrow\quad\alpha_{2}=\dfrac{1-\cos(w)}{w^{2}}\,.\end{array}

For the implicit time integrator, the equation for the exponential update must be solved iteratively, so we write the equation in residual form and solve

{{\bm{R}}}_{exp}:={{{\bm{m}}}}_{n+1}-\textrm{exp}[{{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t}_{n+1}]\cdot{{\bm{m}}}_{n}

(27)

with respect to
{{\bm{m}}}_{n+1}
until

\|{{\bm{R}}}_{exp}\|\leq\textrm{tol}
, with the predefined tolerance tol.
For the numerical treatment we redefine the exponential
as

\textrm{exp}[{{\bm{W}}}_{n+1}^{\Delta t}]={\bf 1}+\alpha_{1}^{\ast}\,{{\bm{W}}}_{n+1}^{\Delta t}+\alpha_{2}^{\ast}\,({{\bm{W}}}_{n+1}^{\Delta t})^{2}

(28)

with case differentiations to avoid singularities

\alpha_{1}^{\ast}=\dfrac{\sin(w)}{w}\quad\textrm{for}\quad w\geq\epsilon\quad\textrm{else}\quad\alpha_{1}^{\ast}=1-\dfrac{w^{2}}{3!}+\dfrac{w^{4}}{5!}-\dfrac{w^{6}}{7!}

(29)

and

\alpha_{2}^{\ast}=\dfrac{1-\cos(w)}{w^{2}}\quad\textrm{for}\quad w^{2}\geq\epsilon\quad\textrm{else}\quad\alpha_{2}^{\ast}=\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{w^{2}}{4!}+\dfrac{w^{4}}{6!}\,,

(30)

with a predefined small positive values
\epsilon
.
An excellent description of an alternative approach to calculate the exponential of a
3\times 3
matrix based on a recursion formula can be found, e.g., in
12
.

3.2
Backward Euler and Midpoint rule

To evaluate the performance of the presented exponential algorithm,
we compare the results with the implicit Backward Euler method and the midpoint rule.
Both algorithms are briefly presented here.
Based on the proposed representation of the LLG equation, the backward Euler algorithm appears as

{{{\bm{m}}}}_{n+1}\approx{{\bm{m}}}_{n}+\Delta t\,\dot{{{\bm{m}}}}_{n+1}\quad\textrm{with}\quad\dot{{{\bm{m}}}}_{n+1}={{\bm{W}}}_{n+1}\cdot{{{\bm{m}}}}_{n+1}\;.

(31)

This leads to the equation

{{{\bm{m}}}}_{n+1}\approx{{\bm{m}}}_{n}+\Delta t\,{{\bm{W}}}_{n+1}^{\Delta t}\cdot{{{\bm{m}}}}_{n+1}\,,

(32)

where we use the abbreviation from (
13
).
Equation (
32
) has to be solved iteratively until the residuum

{{\bm{R}}}_{BE}:=({\bf 1}-{{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t}_{n+1})\cdot{{{\bm{m}}}}_{n+1}-{{\bm{m}}}_{n}

(33)

is approximately zero, i.e.,
\|{{\bm{R}}}_{BE}\|\leq\textrm{tol}
.
The midpoint rule is an implicit discrete scheme with verified unconditional convergence, see
3
.
If we apply the proposed algorithm to our reformulation of the LLG equation, we obtain

{{{\bm{m}}}}_{n+1}\approx{{\bm{m}}}_{n}+\Delta t\,\dot{{{\bm{m}}}}_{n+1}\quad\textrm{with}\quad\dot{{{\bm{m}}}}_{n+1}={{\bm{W}}}_{n+1/2}\cdot{{{\bm{m}}}}_{n+1}\,,

(34)

where we have used the abbreviation

{{\bm{W}}}_{n+1/2}:={{\bm{W}}}[{{\bm{H}}}^{eff}_{n+1/2},{{\bm{m}}}_{n+1/2}]\quad\textrm{with}\quad(\bullet)_{n+1/2}=\dfrac{1}{2}((\bullet)_{n+1}+(\bullet)_{n})\,.

(35)

Based on our notation, we introduce
{{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t}_{n+1/2}={\Delta t}\;{{\bm{W}}}_{n+1/2}

and define the residuum

{{\bm{R}}}_{MR}:=({\bf 1}-{{\bm{W}}}^{\Delta t}_{n+1/2})\cdot{{{\bm{m}}}}_{n+1}-{{\bm{m}}}_{n}\,,

(36)

which must be solved iteratively until
\|{{\bm{R}}}_{MR}\|\leq\textrm{tol}
is satisfied.

4
Magnetic enthalpy and effective field

The magnetic effects to be included within the effective field are stored within the magnetic enthalpy function

\mathcal{H}({{\bm{m}}},\nabla{{\bm{m}}},{{\bm{H}}})=\mathcal{H}^{\rm mag}({{\bm{m}}},{{\bm{H}}})+\mathcal{H}^{\rm exc}(\nabla{{\bm{m}}})+\mathcal{H}^{\rm ani}({{\bm{m}}})\,,

(37)

consisting of
the sum of the contributions for
the magnetostatic part
\mathcal{H}^{\rm mag}
,
the exchange energy
\mathcal{H}^{\rm exc}
and
the magneto-crystalline anisotropy
\mathcal{H}^{\rm ani}
.
Based on this functional the effective field can be derived as

{{\bm{H}}}^{\rm eff}:=\frac{1}{\mu_{0}}\left(\operatorname{div}{\bm{\Pi}}-\partial_{{{\bm{M}}}}\mathcal{H}\right),\quad\textrm{with}\quad{\bm{\Pi}}=\frac{\partial\,\mathcal{H}}{\partial\,\nabla{{\bm{m}}}}\,.

(38)

The magnetostatic energy as well as the exchange energy are stated as

\mathcal{H}^{\rm mag}({{\bm{m}}},{{\bm{H}}})=-\frac{1}{2}\mu_{0}M_{s}\,{{\bm{m}}}\cdot{{\bm{H}}}-\mu_{0}{{\bm{H}}}\cdot{{\bm{H}}}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathcal{H}^{\rm exc}(\nabla{{\bm{m}}})=A^{\rm exc}\,|\nabla{{\bm{m}}}|^{2}\,,

(39)

with
A^{\rm exc}
denoting the exchange constant.
For different types of crystalline anisotropies we consider several model problems for

\mathcal{H}^{\rm ani}
with
\rm ani=\{iso,ti,cub\}
.
The expression for an isotropic
\rm ani=iso
, a transversely isotropic
\rm ani=ti

and a cubic anisotropy
\rm ani=cub
potential are given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l l}\mathcal{H}^{\rm iso}&=\dfrac{K_{1}^{\rm iso}}{2}\;{{\bm{m}}}\cdot{{\bm{m}}},\quad\mathcal{H}^{\rm ti}=\dfrac{K_{1}^{\rm ti}}{2}\left[1-J_{1}^{2}\right]\quad\textrm{and}\\[14.22636pt]
\mathcal{H}^{\rm cub}&=K_{1}^{\rm cub}\left[J_{1}^{2}\,J_{2}^{2}+J_{2}^{2}\,J_{3}^{2}+J_{3}^{2}\,J_{1}^{2}\right]+K_{2}^{\rm cub}J_{1}^{2}\,J_{2}^{2}\,J_{3}^{2}\,,\end{array}

J_{i}={{\bm{m}}}\cdot{{\bm{a}}}_{i}\quad\textrm{for}\quad i=1,2,3

(42)

where the unit vectors
{{\bm{a}}}_{1}=\left[1,0,0\right]^{T}
,
{{\bm{a}}}_{2}=\left[0,1,0\right]^{T}
and
{{\bm{a}}}_{3}=\left[0,0,1\right]^{T}
represent the directions of the anisotropy axis.
A typical anisotropy surface for typical cubic magnetocrystalline anisotropy constants

K_{1},K_{2}
is depicted in Fig.

{{\bm{H}}}^{\rm eff}=\frac{1}{\mu_{0}}\left[\operatorname{div}\left[\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial\nabla{{\bm{m}}}}\right]-\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial{{\bm{m}}}}\right]=\underbrace{\frac{2A^{\rm exc}}{\mu_{0}M_{s}}\nabla^{2}{{\bm{m}}}}_{{{\bm{H}}}^{\rm exc}}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{\mu_{0}M_{s}}{{\bm{H}}}}_{{{\bm{H}}}^{\rm mag}}-\underbrace{\frac{1}{\mu_{0}}\partial_{{{\bm{m}}}}\mathcal{H}^{\rm ani}}_{{{\bm{H}}}^{\rm ani}}

5.1
Precessional switching

The switching in a magnetic particle is mainly determined by two terms in the LLG equation. These terms can be differentiated as the precessional part, which makes the magnetization vectors rotate around the effective field, and the dissipative damping part, which makes the magnetization align itself to the effective field, see
1
.

{{\bm{m}}}\times{{\bm{H}}}^{\rm eff}={\bf 0}

4

Stable algorithms should always guarantee the correct solution and thus physical correctness even with increasing time steps.
To check the properties of the presented algorithm we set the damping of the magnetization vectors to

6
. Conclusion

In this work, the exponential update algorithm for the description of exact rotations is discussed in comparison to the backward Euler and the renormalized backward Euler method.
Micromagnetism with the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation represents the necessary physical frame work, while
the characteristic feature of this theory is the magnetization vector that must always satisfy the non-convex condition
\|{{\bm{m}}}\|=1
.
While both the classical and the renormalized backward Euler give bad results for increased time increments, regarding the conservation of length as well as for the position of the vector on the unit sphere, the exponential update algorithm proves to be consistently stable.
The advantages of the exponential update algorithm are therefore obvious.
Larger time steps with the same level of quality lead to significantly shorter computing times.

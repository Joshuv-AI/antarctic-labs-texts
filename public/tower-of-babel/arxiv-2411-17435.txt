Abstract
. This paper explores the behavior of the torsional rigidity of a precompact domain as the ambient manifold evolves under a geometric flow. Specifically, we derive bounds on torsional rigidity under the Ricci Flow for Heisenberg spaces and homogeneous spheres. Additionally, we establish bounds under the Inverse Mean Curvature Flow for strictly convex, free-boundary, disk-type hypersurfaces within a ball. In this latter case, by extending the analysis to the maximal existence time of the flow, we obtain inequalities of comparison with the flat disk for both volume and torsional rigidity.

1.
Introduction

For a given domain
\Omega\subset M^{n}
in a
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
we can define the torsional rigidity of this domain as the integral of the solution to the following Poisson problem with Dirichlet boundary condition,

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rlcc}\Delta_{g}E&=-1&{\rm in}&\Omega,\\
{E}&=0&\text{ \rm in }&\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

This problem has been widely studied in the last years, see for instance
[
4
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
14
]
or
[
15
]
.
For a deeper discussion of the meaning of the integral the reader is referred to section
2
.

Since the essence of the problem is fundamentally related to the Riemannian metric, a natural question arises when studying this topic is what happens when changes are made to the metric tensor. There exist different ways to perform changes on the metric, for example conformal changes or geometric flows. In this article the spotlight will be in geometric flow as, for instance, Ricci and Inverse Mean Curvature flow. For a more detailed explanation we refer to section
4
.

A solution of a geometric flow is a continuous path in the space of metrics
g:[0,T_{\rm max})\to\mathcal{M}(M^{n})
that follows a geometric equation,
\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}=F(t)
satisfied up to a maximal time of existence of the flow denoted as
T_{\rm max}
. Thus for each
t\in[0,T_{max})
the solution to the previously mentioned Poisson problem will be different. Hence we will obtain a one-parameter family of solutions,
\{E_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T_{max})}
to the problem. The torsional rigidity of
\Omega
at time
t
will be obtained therefore by the following integral

\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{t}):=\int_{\Omega}E_{t}\,d{\rm V}_{t}

where
d{\rm V}_{t}
is the volume element associated to
g_{t}
and
E_{t}
is the solution of (
1
) at time
t
. We can therefore consider the torsional rigidity for a given domain as a function that depends on the time.

Geometric flows determines the evolution of various properties associated with the Riemannian metric. In this paper, the controlled evolution of these quantities under the flow is leveraged to derive bounds on the torsional rigidity, utilizing the variational characterizations introduced in Section
3
.
In the particular case of the Ricci flow, focusing in manifolds with constant scalar curvature and bounded Ricci tensor we have obtained several bounds for the torsional rigidity. The result is summarized in the following theorem:

Theorem A
.

Let
(M^{n},(g_{t})_{t\in[0,T_{\rm max})})
be a Ricci flow solution. Suppose that for any
t\in[0,T_{\rm max})
and any
p\in M

{\rm Scal}_{g_{t}}(p)=b(t).

Suppose moreover that for any
t\in[0,T_{\rm max})
, any
p\in M
and any
v\in T_{p}M
, there exists two functions
A,B:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

A(t)g_{t}(v,v)\leq{\rm Ric}_{g_{t}}(v,v)\leq B(t)g_{t}(v,v).

Then for any precompact domain
\Omega\subset M
with smooth boundary

\displaystyle e^{\int_{0}^{t}\left(-b(s)-2B(s)\right)ds}\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{0})\leq\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{t})\leq

\displaystyle e^{\int_{0}^{t}\left(-2A(s)-b(s)\right)ds}\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{0}).

Which implies

\displaystyle e^{-2\int_{0}^{t}B(s)ds}\frac{\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{0})}{{\rm V}(\Omega_{0})}\leq\frac{\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{t})}{{\rm V}(\Omega_{t})}\leq

\displaystyle e^{-2\int_{0}^{t}A(s)ds}\frac{\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{0})}{{\rm V}(\Omega_{0})}.

Where here
{\rm V}(\Omega_{t})
denotes the volume of
\Omega
with respect to
g_{t}
.

To check the consistence of the above Theorem we can apply it to the case of Einstein manifolds
(M,g_{0})
with initial metric tensor satisfying
{\rm Ric}_{g_{0}}=\lambda g_{0}
. In this case applying the above theorem, see section
5.1.1
, we obtain for any precomapct domain
\Omega\subset M
with smooth boundary that

\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{t})=\left(1-2\lambda t\right)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{0})

as corresponds to the evolutions of the metric tensor by the form
g_{t}=(1-2\lambda t)g_{0}
.

By applying Theorem
A
, for manifolds whose Ricci flow satisfies that

{\rm Scal}_{g_{t}}(p)=b(t)
, and the Ricci tensor is negative (or positive) definite along the flow we obtain, see corollary
5.5
, we obtain that the function

t\mapsto\frac{\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{t})}{{\rm V}(\Omega_{t})}

is a non decreasing (non increasing for positive definite) function for
t\in[0,T_{0}]
, and for any precompact domain
\Omega
with smooth boundary the function

t\mapsto\frac{\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{t})}{{\rm V}^{3}(\Omega_{t})}

is a non increasing (non decreasing respectively) function for
t\in[0,T_{0}]
.

Theorem
A
is applied as well in this article for the case of homogeneous spaces because since the Ricci flow preserves isometries, the manifold remains a homogeneous manifold along the flow, and the hypothesis on the scalar curvature of Theorem
A
holds. In this case we focus on the Heisenberg group and on homogeneous spheres. In the case of the Heisenberg group
{\rm Nil}_{3}
, see section
6.1.1
, we obtain the for any precompact domain
\Omega\subset{\rm Nil}_{3}
with smooth bounday, the function

t\mapsto{\rm V}(\Omega_{t})\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{t}),

is non decreasing on
t\in[0,\infty)
, and the function

t\mapsto\frac{\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{t})}{{\rm V}(\Omega_{t})^{3}},

is non increasing on
t\in[0,\infty)
.

For the homogeneous spheres, see section
6.1.2
, with initial parameters

\epsilon A_{0}\leq C_{0}\leq B_{0}
such that
\delta:=\frac{B_{0}-\epsilon A_{0}}{\epsilon A_{0}}<1
.
It has been computed that,

\left[1-\frac{4(1+6\delta)t}{B_{0}}\right]^{\frac{5(1+\delta)^{3}}{2(1+6\delta)}}\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{0})\leq\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{t})\leq\left[1-\frac{4t}{B_{0}}\right]^{\frac{5(1-\delta)}{2(1+\delta)}}\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{0}),

for any
t<\min\left\{\frac{B_{0}}{4(1+6\delta)},T_{\rm max}\right\}
.
In the case of the inverse mean curvature flow, bounds for the torsional rigidity have been obtained for domains that satisfy the following conditions: they are totally bounded (i.e., precompact) and have a strictly convex image,
H>0
. The time for which the hypersurface remains strictly convex, denoted by
T_{mc}
, has been studied by Lambert and Scheuer for strictly convex, free boundary disk-type hypersurfaces in the ball in
[
13
]
or by Urbas in
[
19
]
, who considered a more general case of hypersurface evolution in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. In the case of non-convex hypersurfaces we highlight the work of by Gerhardt in
[
7

. The bounds for the torsional rigidity in precompact domains in strictly convex hypersurfaces can be summarized in the following theorem, see section
5.2
,

Theorem B
.

Let M be an n-dimensional manifold and let
\Omega\subset M
be an open and precompact domain of
M
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset
. Then let

\varphi:M\times[0,T_{\rm mc})\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
by an inverse mean curvature flow from
M
to
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
such that
\varphi_{t}(M)=\Sigma
is strictly convex hypersurface of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.Thence the function

t\mapsto({\rm V}(\Omega_{t}))^{-3}\cdot\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{t})

is non-increasing for
t\in[0,T_{\rm mc})
, and the function

t\mapsto({\rm V}(\Omega_{t}))^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{t})

is non-decreasing for
t\in[0,T_{\rm mc})
. Where here
{\rm V}(\Omega_{t})
denotes the volume of
\Omega
with respect to
g_{t}
.

In the case
\Sigma
be a strictly convex free boundary disk-type hypersurface in the ball
\mathbb{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, as in definition
6.5
, inspired in the work of Pak Tung Ho and Juncheol Pyo, in
[
10
]
, taking into account that
\Sigma
converges to the flat disk when
t
tends to
T_{\rm max}
, it has been obtained bounds for the torsional rigidity for this family of hypersurfaces in comparison with the flat disk as follows,

\frac{\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)}{{\rm V}^{3}(\Sigma)}\geq\frac{\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{D})}{{\rm V}^{3}(\mathbb{D})},\quad\frac{\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)}{{\rm V}(\Sigma)}\leq\frac{\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{D})}{{\rm V}(\mathbb{D})},

where
{\rm V}(\mathbb{D})
and
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{D})
are the volume and the torsional rigidity of the unit disk in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
{\rm V}(\Sigma)
denotes the volume of the hypersurface. Then

{\rm V}(\Sigma)\leq{\rm V}(\mathbb{D}),\quad{\rm and}\quad\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)\leq\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{D}).

We must remark here that the assumption that the mean curvature is strictly positive
H>0
is crucial for deriving our results. In contrast, for minimal (
H=0
) hypersurfaces
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
\vec{0}\in P
, by applying the results established by Markvorsen and Palmer in
[
14
]
(see remark 2.2 in their paper), it can be concluded for the region
\Sigma=P\cap\mathbb{B}
contained within
\mathbb{B}
that:

{\rm V}(\Sigma)\geq{\rm V}(\mathbb{D}),\quad{\rm and}\quad\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)\geq\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{D}).

Thus, our results for hypersurfaces with strictly positive mean curvature can be interpreted as the inverse of those obtained by Markvorsen and Palmer concerning minimal hypersurfaces.

2.
Poisson problem: two names one function.

In classical literature, the solution to the problem (
1
) can be approached from two perspectives: either as an auxiliary function whose integral yields the torsional function, or as the mean exit time function for the Brownian motion. In the following paragraphs, both concepts will be introduced, and the Poisson problem will be derived from each perspective.

The first point of view is related to the theory of elasticity. Where the integral of the solution from the afforded mentioned problem give us the torque required when twisting an elastic beam with the domain of study as uniform cross section. The second one is related to Brownian motion on domains. The solution of the problem (
1
) can be seen as the expected time for a Brownian particle starting at a point
x\in\Omega
to reach the boundary of the domain.

2.1.
The Poisson problem as torsional rigidity

Following the idea of Landau and Lipschitz in
[
12
]
and the articles of Polya
et. al.

[
16
]
,
[
17
]
and
[
18
]
, a beam of length
L\in(0,\infty)
can be modeled as
\mathcal{B}=\Omega\times[0,L]\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a bounded, simply connected and open set representing the cross-section.
In this model, a coordinate system where the
z
-axis corresponds to the beam’s length, and the
x
- and
y
-axes represent the coordinates within
\Omega
can be defined.
In the twisting, the beam remains straight, but each cross-sectional slice (i.e., each copy of
\Omega
) undergoes a rotation of
\theta
radians per unit length.

The stress energy tensor, see
[
12
]
, can be described in terms of the deformation tensor as follows,

\sigma_{xx}=\sigma_{yy}=\sigma_{zz}=0=\sigma_{xy},\quad\sigma_{xz}=2\mu\frac{\theta}{2}\bigg(\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial x}-y\bigg)\quad{\rm and}\quad\sigma_{yz}=2\mu\frac{\theta}{2}\bigg(\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial y}+x\bigg).

Where
\Psi(x,y)
is a function that gives the vertical displacement under the twist and
\mu
is the modulus of the transversal elasticity that here and so on we will take it as
\mu=1
. At this point it is convenient to utilize an auxiliary function
\chi(x,y)
defined as follows,

\sigma_{xz}=2\mu\theta\frac{\partial\chi}{\partial y}\quad and\quad\sigma_{yz}=-2\mu\theta\frac{\partial\chi}{\partial x}.

Thence,
\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial x}=y+2\frac{\partial\chi}{\partial x}\quad and\quad\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial y}=-x-2\frac{\partial\chi}{\partial y}
. Finally, taking the derivatives
\frac{\partial^{2}\chi}{\partial x^{2}}
,
\frac{\partial^{2}\chi}{\partial y^{2}}
and assuming that in the boundary the forces of torsion are much smaller than the interior of the domain we obtain

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta\chi=&-1\text{ in }\Omega,\\
{\chi}=&0\text{ in }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Which implies that the total energy of the beam will be

E(\mathcal{B})=\int^{L}_{0}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\chi)^{2}4\theta^{2}d\mu_{\Omega}dz,

and the resulted torque due to the stress function
\mathcal{T}(\Omega)
,
i.e.
, the torsional rigidity, is proportional to

\int_{\Omega}\chi d\mu_{\Omega}.

2.2.
The Poisson problem as mean exit time

In this subsection we will follow the construction of a Brownian motion modeled by a diffusion process as Alexander Grigor’yan and Telcs did in
[
9
]
and then the mean exit time function will be expressed as the solution of Poison problem (
1
) as was stated by Dynkin in
[
5
]
.

Let
(M,g)
be a connected Riemannian manifold equipped with the volume form
d{\rm V}_{g}
.
The associated Dirichlet form will be used to construct a diffusion process. This is a way to model Markov process. For a better understanding of the Dirichlet form and the associated Markov process we refer to
[
6
]
.

Recall that in a Riemannian manifold the set of functions
H^{1}(M)
given by

H^{1}(M):=\left\{f\in L^{2}(M)\,:\,\|\nabla f\|\in L^{2}(M^{n})\right\}

is a dense subset of
L^{2}(M)
and a Dirichlet form can be defined as the fowolling bilinear map

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}:H^{1}(M)\times H^{1}(M)\to L^{2}(M),\quad(f,h)\mapsto

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(f,h):=\int_{M}g(\nabla f,\nabla h)\ d{\rm V}_{g}.

This bilinear map is nonnegative definite, closed, satisfies the Markovian property, and can be generated using the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{g}
in
L^{2}(M)
. Indeed, the domain
{\rm dom}(\Delta_{g})
is precisely the dense subset of
H^{1}(M)
given by

{\rm dom}(\Delta_{g})=\left\{f\in H^{1}(M)\,:\,\mathcal{E}(f,h)=\int_{M}h\Delta_{g}f\ d{\rm V}_{g},\quad\forall h\in H^{1}(M)\right\}.

Every Dirichlet form, see Theorem 7.2.1 of
[
6
]
, has associated a diffusion process
\{\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\{\mathbb{P}_{x}\}_{x\in M}\}
with the initial point
x\in M
. This diffusion process is associated with the heat semi-group, in general known as transition semi-group,
P:=\{e^{t\Delta_{g}}\}_{t\geq 0}
1
1

1

There exist two conventions at the moment of defining the heat semi-group. The first one is using that
P
is generated by the usual Laplace-Beltrami operator and the second one is that
P
is generated by
\frac{\Delta_{g}}{2}.
This decision will have consequences at the moment of setting up the Poisson problem. In this article the first one is chosen.
as follows: for any
f\in L^{2}(M)
and
x\in M
,

\mathbb{E}_{x}f(X_{t})=P_{t}(f(x)).

The transformation
P_{t}(f)(x)
of the function
f
can be obtained as a convolution of the heat kernel
p_{t}
in an integral form as

P_{t}(f)(x)=\int_{M}p_{t}(x,y)f(y)d{\rm V}_{g}(y).

This Markovian process, derived form the Dirichlet form, is known as Brownian motion.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold.
The Brownian motion on
(M,g)
is the unique Markov process
\{X_{t}\}
with state space
M
with measure
d{\rm V}_{g}
and transition densities
p_{t}(x,\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}p_{t}(x,y)d{\rm V}_{g}(y)
.

A natural question that arises when studying Brownian motion in a domain
\Omega
concerns the first time
\tau_{p}
when the Brownian motion will leave the domain
\Omega
, starting at a point
p\in\Omega
. To estimate this first exit time, one can consider the diffusion process terminated, “killed”, when it reaches the boundary of
\Omega
.

The relationship between the elements of the transition semigroup and the expected value of the first exit time is given by:

P^{\Omega}_{t}(f)(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}(\mathbf{1}_{t<\tau_{\Omega}}f(X_{t})),\quad\mathbf{1}_{t<\tau_{\Omega}}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&t<\tau_{\Omega},\\
0&t\geq\tau_{\Omega}.\end{array}\right.

Roughly speaking,
\mathbf{1}_{t<\tau_{\Omega}}
is forcing the Brownian particle to remain within
\Omega
up to time
t
. This can also be expressed as a convolution with a heat kernel:

P^{\Omega}_{t}(f)(x)=\int_{\Omega}f(y)p_{t}^{\Omega}(x,y)\,d\mathrm{V}_{g},

but now
p_{t}^{\Omega}
is the heat kernel of
\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary condition. For any measurable function in
\Omega
, by using the Green operator in
\Omega
we have that,

\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau_{\Omega}}f(X_{t})dt\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}P^{\Omega}_{t}(f)(x)\,dt=:G^{\Omega}(f)(x).

In particular setting
f(x)=1
we obtain the mean exit time function
\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau_{\Omega})
as

(2)

\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau_{\Omega})=G^{\Omega}(1)(x).

Therefore, see for instance
[
2
]
for the properties of the Green operator, we obtain the following characterization of the mean exit time function
\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau_{\Omega})
as the desired Poisson problem with Dirichlet boundary condition

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau_{\Omega})=&-1\text{ in }\Omega,\\
{\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau_{\Omega})}=&0\text{ in }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Example 2.2
.

Let
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{\mathbb{E}})
with
n\geq 2
be the Euclidean space with the canonical Euclidean metric. Let the domain of study
\Omega=D_{1}(\vec{0})
be the disk of radius
1
centered at
\vec{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The mean exit time for this domain will be

(3)

\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau_{\Omega})=\frac{1}{2n}\left(1-\|x\|^{2}\right).

For the case
n>1
this can be proved by using the Green function of the disk
g^{\Omega}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{t}^{\Omega}(x,y)dt
and taking into account the the problem is rotationally symmetric, see
[
2
]
for radial Green functions in rotationaly symmetric balls, as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau_{\Omega})=

\displaystyle G^{\Omega}(1)=\int_{D_{1}(0)}g^{\Omega}(x,y)d{\rm V}_{g_{E}}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\|x\|}^{1}\frac{t}{n}dt=\frac{1}{2n}\left(1-\|x\|^{2}\right).

Observe that (
3
) can be proved in an alternative way by taking into account that

\Delta\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau_{\Omega})=\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{1}^{2}}+\cdots+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{n}^{2}}\right)\frac{1-x_{1}^{2}-\cdots-x_{n}^{2}}{2n}=-1,

and
\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau_{\Omega})=0
for
x\in\partial\Omega
.

3.
Two variational approaches for the torsional rigidity

In order to study the evolution of the torsional rigidity of a domain within a Riemannian manifold as the metric tensor evolves under a geometric flow, it will became necessary to characterize the torsional rigidity through two variational approaches. Specifically, in Proposition
3.1
, we will express the torsional rigidity as the supremum of a functional defined over certain functions within the precompact domain. Furthermore, in Proposition
3.2
, we will represent it as the infimum of the energy associated with specific vector fields defined on the domain.

The first variational approach is a generalization of a variational approach made by Polya in the paper
[
18
]
. By making use that the torsional rigidity problem arise from a Poisson problem, we can rewrite it as the critical point of an energy functional in the space of smooth functions
u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
u\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0
. The variational characterization of the torsional rigidity introduced by Polya for domains in the
2
-dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is

(4)

\mathcal{T}(\Omega)=\sup\left\{\frac{\left(\int_{\Omega}f\,{d}{\rm A}\right)^{2}}{\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla f\|^{2}\,{d}{\rm A}}\,:\,f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega),\,f|_{\partial\Omega=0}\right\}\cdot

The main idea of the proof is that assuming
f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
f\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0
, the integral of
f
can be bounded in terms of the Dirichlet form because

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(f,\chi)

\displaystyle=\iint_{\Omega}\left(f_{x}\chi_{x}+f_{y}\chi_{y}\right)dA

\displaystyle=\iint_{\Omega}\left[\frac{\partial\left(f\chi_{x}\right)}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial\left(f\chi_{y}\right)}{\partial y}+2f\right]dA

\displaystyle=\iint_{\Omega}fdA+\int_{\partial\Omega}f\frac{\partial\chi}{\partial n}dL

\displaystyle=\iint_{\Omega}fdA,

where
\chi
is the auxiliary function defined in the section 2.1. This implies that

\left(\int_{\Omega}f\,{d}{\rm A}\right)^{2}=\left(\mathcal{E}(f,\chi)\right)^{2}\leq\mathcal{E}(f,f)\cdot\mathcal{E}(\chi,\chi),

with equality obtained when
f(x,y)=\chi(x,y)
. The variational characterization of Polya (
4
) is generalized in following proposition for precompact domains of Riemannian Manifolds.

Propsosition 3.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold. Let
\Omega\subset M
be an open precompact domain. Then,

\mathcal{T}(\Omega)=\sup\left\{\frac{\left(\int_{\Omega}u\,{d}{\rm V}\right)^{2}}{\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla u\|^{2}\,{d}{\rm V}}\,:\,u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)\,{\rm and}\,\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla u\|\,{d}{\rm V}\neq 0\right\}.

Proof.

Let
E
be the mean exit time function in
\Omega
,
i.e
the solution of the Poisson problem (
1
), and let
u\in\{f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla f\|\,{d}{\rm V}\neq 0\}
. Then,

(5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u\,{d}{\rm V}=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}u\Delta E\,{d}{\rm V}=-\int_{\Omega}{\rm div}_{g}\left(u\nabla E\right)\,{d}{\rm V}+\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla u,\nabla E\rangle\,{d}{\rm V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla u,\nabla E\rangle\,{d}{\rm V}\leq\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla u\|\,\|\nabla E\|\,{d}{\rm V}.

Hence, by applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\int_{\Omega}u\,{d}{\rm V}\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla u\|^{2}\,{d}{\rm V}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla E\|^{2}\,{d}{\rm V}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since

\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla E\|^{2}\,d{\rm V}=\int_{\Omega}{\rm div}_{g}(E\nabla E)\,d{\rm V}-\int_{\Omega}E\Delta E\,d{\rm V}=\mathcal{T}(\Omega),

and we are assuming that
\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla u\|^{2}\,{d}{\rm V}\neq 0
, we obtain that

\frac{\left(\int_{\Omega}u\,{d}{\rm V}\right)^{2}}{\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla u\|^{2}\,{d}{\rm V}}\leq\mathcal{T}(\Omega)=\frac{\left(\int_{\Omega}E\,{d}{\rm V}\right)^{2}}{\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla E\|^{2}\,{d}{\rm V}},

and the proposition is proved.
∎

The characterization of the torsional rigidity as an infimum can be stated in the following proposition

Propsosition 3.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold, let
\Omega\subset M
an open subset of
M
with compact closure and smooth boundary
\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset
. Then,

\mathcal{T}(\Omega)=\inf\left\{\int_{\Omega}\|X\|^{2}\,d{\rm V}:\,X\in\mathfrak{X}(\Omega)\,{\rm with}\,{\rm div}_{g}X=-1\right\}.

Proof.

Let
E
be the solution to problem (
1
) and let
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\Omega)
be smooth vector field such that
{\rm div}_{g}X=-1
. Applying the divergence Theorem and the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(\Omega)=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}E\,d{\rm V}=\int_{\Omega}-E\,{\rm div}_{g}X\,d{\rm V}=\int_{\Omega}{\rm div}_{g}(-E\,X)\,d{\rm V}+\int_{\Omega}g(\nabla E,X)\,d{\rm V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial\Omega}Eg(X,\nu)\,d{\rm A}+\int_{\Omega}g(\nabla E,X)\,d{\rm V}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla E\|^{2}\,d{\rm V}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\left(\int_{\Omega}\|X\|^{2}\,d{\rm V}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since

\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla E\|^{2}\,d{\rm V}=\int_{\Omega}{\rm div}_{g}(E\nabla E)\,d{\rm V}-\int_{\Omega}E\Delta E\,d{\rm V}=\mathcal{T}(\Omega),

we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\|X\|^{2}\,d{\rm V}\geq\mathcal{T}(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla E\|^{2}\,d{\rm V}

and the proposition is proved.
∎

4.
Geometric flows and bounds for the torsional rigidity

Our objective is to derive the evolution of the torsional rigidity of a domain as the metric tensor of the manifold evolves under a geometric flow. Geometric flows are a fundamental tool in differential geometry, facilitating the analysis of geometric structures through their continuous deformation.

4.1.
Geometric flows

These flows can be conceptualized as trajectories,
g(t)
, within the space of metrics,
\mathcal{M}(M^{n})
defined on a given manifold. The evolution of the metric is typically governed by the partial differential equation
\frac{\partial g_{t}}{\partial t}=F(t)\in T_{g_{t}}\mathcal{M}(M)
.

Since the Riemannian metric determines the geometric properties of the manifold, such as distance, volume, and curvature, any change in the metric under a geometric flow leads to the evolution of these properties. The strength of geometric flows lies in their property to determine the evolution of such quantities.

Given that we will use the two variational characterizations of torsional rigidity described in Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
, it is essential to understand the following time-dependent quantities:

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\mapsto d{\rm V}_{t},

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\mapsto\|\nabla^{g_{t}}u\|,\quad u\in C_{o}^{\infty}(\Omega),

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\mapsto\|X\|=\sqrt{g_{t}(X,X)},\quad X\in\mathfrak{X}(\Omega),

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\mapsto{\rm div}_{g_{t}}X,\quad X\in\mathfrak{X}(\Omega).

The following proposition addresses these evolutions:

Propsosition 4.1
.

Let
\{g_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T_{\rm max})}
be a solution to the geometric flow
\frac{\partial g_{t}}{\partial t}=f
on the
n
-dimensional manifold
M
. Then for any
t\in[0,T_{\rm max})
:

(1)

\frac{\partial(d{\rm V}_{t})}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}{\rm tr}_{g}(f)d{\rm V}_{t}
,

(2)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\|\nabla^{g_{t}}u\|^{2}_{g_{t}}=-f(\nabla^{g_{t}}u,\nabla^{g_{t}}u)
,

(3)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{t}(X,X)=f(X,X)
,

(4)

and
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}{\rm div}_{g_{t}}X=\frac{1}{2}X({\rm tr}_{g}(f))
.

Proof.

We have use the Einstein’s notation for repeated indices.
The evolution of the volume form
d{\rm V}_{t}
can be computed using local coordinates as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial(d{\rm V}_{t})}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial\sqrt{det(g_{t})}}{\partial t}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{det(g_{t})}}\frac{\partial det(g_{t})}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{det(g_{t})}}\frac{\partial det(g_{t})}{\partial g_{ij}}\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{det(g_{t})}g^{ij}f_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{det(g_{t})}{\rm tr}_{g}(f).

For the evolution of
\|\nabla^{g_{t}}u\|
we will have that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\|\nabla^{g_{t}}u\|^{2}_{g_{t}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g\left(\nabla^{g_{t}}u,\nabla^{g_{t}}u\right)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g\left(g^{im}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x^{m}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}},g^{jl}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x^{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{l}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(g^{im}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x^{m}}g^{jl}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x^{j}}g_{il}\right)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\delta^{m}_{l}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x^{m}}g^{jl}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x^{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial x^{l}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x^{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(g^{jl}\right).

But taking into account that

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(g^{rs}g_{sl}\right)=0

we can easily conclude that

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(g^{jl}\right)=-g^{js}f_{sm}g^{ml}
,
and thence

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\|\nabla^{g_{t}}u\|^{2}_{g_{t}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial u}{\partial x^{l}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x^{j}}g^{jm}f_{ms}g^{ls}=-\left(\nabla^{g_{t}}u\right)^{s}f_{ms}\left(\nabla^{g_{t}}u\right)^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f(\nabla^{g_{t}}u,\nabla^{g_{t}}u).

For the evolution of
\|X\|^{2}
we will just obtain

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{t}(X,X)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{ij}X^{i}X^{j}=f_{ij}X^{i}X^{j}=f(X,X).

And finally we can compute the evolution of the divergence of a vector field by using the evolution of the volume form as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}{\rm div}_{g_{t}}X=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{det(g_{t})}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}\bigg(X^{j}\sqrt{det(g_{t})}\bigg)\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{det(g_{t})}}tr_{g}(f)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}\bigg(X^{j}\sqrt{det(g_{t})}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{det(g_{t})}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}X^{j}\sqrt{det(g_{t})}{\rm tr}_{g}(f)\bigg)=\frac{1}{2}X^{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}({\rm tr}_{g}(f))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}X({\rm tr}_{g}(f)).

∎

4.2.
Bounds for the Torsional Rigidity under a Geometric Flow

With the variational characterization of the torsional rigidity given by propositions
3.2
and
3.1
, and with the evolution of the relevant geometric tensors under a geometric flow given by proposition
4.1
we can provide lower and upper bounds for the torsional rigidity of a domain in terms of its initial torsonial rigidity.
In the following theorem lower bounds are stated.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\{g_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T_{\rm max})}
be a solution to the geometric flow
\frac{\partial g_{t}}{\partial t}=f
on the
n
-dimensional manifold
M
. Let
\Omega\subset M
be a precompact domain with smooth boundary. Suppose that for any
t\in[0,T_{\rm max})
:

(1)

There exists a smooth function function
L:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for any point
p\in M
tangent vector
v\in T_{p}M

f(v,v)\geq L(t)g_{t}(v,v).

(2)

There exist two smooth functions
l,u:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for any point
p\in M

l(t)\leq{\rm tr}_{g}(f)\leq u(t).

Then, for any
t\in[0,T_{\rm max})
, the torsional rigidity
\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{t})
of
\Omega
with respect to
g_{t}
is bounded from below by

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{t})\geq

\displaystyle e^{\int_{0}^{t}\left(l(s)-\frac{1}{2}u(s)+L(s)\right)ds}\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{0}).

Proof.

Let
E_{0}
be the solution for the problem (
1
) for the metric
g_{0}
in
\Omega
, namely, let
E_{0}
be the initial mean exit time function of
\Omega
at time
t=0
. Let the following function be defined as,

F(t):=\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla^{g_{t}}E_{0}\|^{2}\,d{\rm V}_{t},

Then, by using proposition
4.1
,

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}-f(\nabla^{g_{t}}E_{0},\nabla^{g_{t}}E_{0})\,d{\rm V}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla^{g_{t}}E_{0}\|^{2}{\rm tr}_{g}(f)\,d{\rm V}_{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(-L(t)+\frac{1}{2}u(t)\right)F(t),

which implies

F(t)\leq e^{\int_{0}^{t}\left(-L(s)+\frac{1}{2}u(s)\right)ds}F(0).

Similarly, for the function

G(t):=\int_{\Omega}E_{0}\,d{\rm V}_{t}
,

G^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}E_{0}\,{\rm tr}_{g}(f)\,d{\rm V}_{t}\geq\frac{1}{2}l(t)G(t),

and thence for any
t\in[0,T_{\rm max})

G(t)\geq e^{\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}l(s)ds}G(0).

Finally by proposition
3.1
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{t})\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\int_{\Omega}E^{0}\,d{\rm V}_{t}\right)^{2}}{\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla^{g_{t}}E^{0}\|^{2}\,d{\rm V}_{t}}=\frac{G^{2}(t)}{F(t)}\geq e^{\int_{0}^{t}\left(l(s)-\frac{1}{2}u(s)+L(s)\right)ds}\frac{G^{2}(0)}{F(0)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\int_{0}^{t}\left(l(s)-\frac{1}{2}u(s)+L(s)\right)ds}\frac{\left(\int_{\Omega}E^{0}\,d{\rm V}_{0}\right)^{2}}{\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla^{g_{t}}E^{0}\|^{2}\,d{\rm V}_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\int_{0}^{t}\left(l(s)-\frac{1}{2}u(s)+L(s)\right)ds}\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{0}),

and the theorem is proved.
∎

For upper bounds of the torsional rigidity of a domain in terms of the initial torsional rigidity we can state the following theorem

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\{g_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T_{\rm max})}
be a solution to the geometric flow
\frac{\partial g_{t}}{\partial t}=f
on the
n
-dimensional manifold
M
. Let
\Omega\subset M
be a precompact domain with smooth boundary. Suppose that for any
t\in[0,T_{\rm max})
:

(1)

There exists a smooth function function
U:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for any point
p\in M
tangent vector
v\in T_{p}M

f(v,v)\leq U(t)g_{t}(v,v).

(2)

There exists a smooth function
u:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for any point
p\in M

{\rm tr}_{g}(f)=u(t).

Then, for any
t\in[0,T_{\rm max})
, the torsional rigidity
\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{t})
os
\Omega
with respect to
g_{t}
is bounded from above by

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{t})\leq

\displaystyle e^{\int_{0}^{t}\left(U(s)+\frac{1}{2}u(s)\right)ds}\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{0}).

Proof.

Let us set
X=\nabla^{g_{0}}E^{0}
with
E_{0}
the solution for the problem (
1
) for the metric
g_{0}
in
\Omega
. Then by proposition
4.1

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}{\rm div}_{g_{t}}X=\frac{1}{2}X({\rm tr}_{g}(f))=\frac{1}{2}X(u(t))=0.

This allows us to conclude that the divergence of
X
is constant throughout the flow and hence

{\rm div}_{g_{t}}X={\rm div}_{g_{0}}X={\rm div}_{g_{0}}\nabla^{g_{0}}E^{0}=\Delta_{g_{0}}E^{0}=-1.

By using proposition
3.2

\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{t})\leq\int_{\Omega}\|X\|^{2}\,d{\rm V}_{t}.

We must therefore compute the derivative of the following function

t\mapsto F(t):=\int_{\Omega}\|X\|^{2}\,d{\rm V}_{t}.

By using again proposition
4.1

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(t)=\int_{\Omega}f(X,X)\,d{\rm V}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}u(t)\int_{\Omega}\|X\|^{2}\,d{\rm V}_{t}\leq\left(U(t)+\frac{1}{2}u(t)\right)F(t).

Then

\displaystyle F(t)\leq

\displaystyle e^{\int_{0}^{t}\left(U(s)+\frac{1}{2}u(s)\right)ds}F(0)=e^{\int_{0}^{t}\left(U(s)+\frac{1}{2}u(s)\right)ds}\int_{\Omega}g_{0}(X,X)\,d{\rm V}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\int_{0}^{t}\left(U(s)+\frac{1}{2}u(s)\right)ds}\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla^{g_{0}}E^{0}\|^{2}\,dV_{0}=e^{\int_{0}^{t}\left(U(s)+\frac{1}{2}u(s)\right)ds}\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{0}),

and the theorem is proved.
∎

5.1.
Ricci Flow

The Ricci flow was introduced in Hamilton’s in 1982 in the paper “Three-manifolds with positive Ricci curvature”.
The Ricci flow is a way to deform a Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
using the Ricci tensor in the following way,

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{t}=-2{\rm Ric}_{g_{t}}.

Namely,

Definition 5.1

[
1

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g^{t}_{ij}=-2{\rm Ric}_{ij}.

Therefore we can summaries the properties of interest under the evolution of

Propsosition 5.2

Proof.

From the notation of the previous section

then, the proposition follows directly from the proposition
4.1

5.1.1.
Einstein metrics

In the case of Einstein metrics we obtained an exact solution for the torsional rigidity,

\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{t})=\left(1-2\lambda t\right)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{0})

(M^{n},g_{0})

Therefore, the Ricci flow solution will satisfy the conditions of the previous theorem and we can set
A(t)=B(t)=\frac{\lambda}{1-2\lambda\cdot t}

\Omega\subset M

can be stated that

\frac{\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{t})}{{\rm V}^{\frac{n+2}{2}}(\Omega_{t})}=\frac{\mathcal{T}(\Omega_{0})}{{\rm V}^{\frac{n+2}{2}}(\Omega_{0})}.

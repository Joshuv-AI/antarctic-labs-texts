The bracket polynomial of the Celtic link shadow 𝐶𝐾 𝟒 𝟐 ​ n \displaystyle\bm{\mathit{CK}_{4}^{2n}}
Franck Ramaharo

Abstract
We derive the Kauffman bracket polynomial for the shadow of the Celtic link 𝐶𝐾 4 2 ​ n \displaystyle\mathit{CK}_{4}^{2n} using two complementary approaches. The first approach uses a recursive relation within the Celtic framework of Gross and Tucker, based on diagrammatic identities. The second approach makes use of a 4-tangle algebraic framework: a fundamental tangle is concatenated with itself n \displaystyle n times to form an iterated composite tangle, and the Kauffman bracket polynomial is computed by decomposing the state space with respect to the basis elements of the 4-strand diagram monoid. Keywords: celtic knot, 4-tangle, knot shadow, Kauffman state.

2
Kauffman bracket polynomials

The Kauffman bracket polynomial for a knot assigns to each shadow diagram
\displaystyle D
an element
\displaystyle\langle D\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
. It is defined recursively by the following three axioms:

\displaystyle(\mathbf{K1})
:

\displaystyle\left<\bigcirc\right>=x
;

\displaystyle(\mathbf{K2})
:

\displaystyle\left<\bigcirc\sqcup D^{\prime}\right>=x\left<D^{\prime}\right>
;

\displaystyle(\mathbf{K3})
:

\displaystyle\left<\includegraphics[width,valign]{crossing}\right>=\left<\includegraphics[width,valign]{split1}\right>+\left<\includegraphics[width,valign]{split2}\right>
.

Here,
\displaystyle\bigcirc
denotes a simple closed curves or a circle,
\displaystyle\sqcup
denotes disjoint union, and the local diagrams in

(
K3
)

:
represent the two possible smoothings of a crossing. The latter are called the states of that crossing. A state of the diagram refers to the complete configuration obtained by choosing a smoothing at every crossing, resulting in a collection of disjoint circles.

In this setting, the bracket polynomial has a purely combinatorial interpretation. Specifically,
\displaystyle\langle D\rangle
is a state-sum over all possible ways of smoothing every crossing in
\displaystyle D
:

\langle D\rangle=\sum_{S}x^{|S|},

where the sum is taken over all Kauffman states
\displaystyle S
of
\displaystyle D
, and
\displaystyle|S|
denotes the number of disjoint circles in the state
\displaystyle S
. Thus, the coefficient
\displaystyle\left[x^{k}\right]\langle D\rangle
represents the number of states in which the resolved diagram consists of exactly
\displaystyle k
circles.

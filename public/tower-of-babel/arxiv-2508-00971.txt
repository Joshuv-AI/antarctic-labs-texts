A note on multiset reconstruction from sum and pairwise products
Stella Jiahui Li

Abstract
. Ballantine, Beck, and Merca defined a map pre 2 \mathrm{pre}_{2} , which sends an integer partition λ = ( λ 1 , … , λ ℓ ) \lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{\ell}) to the set { λ i ​ λ j : 1 ≤ i < j ≤ ℓ } \{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}:1\leq i<j\leq\ell\} consisting of the pairwise products of parts of λ \lambda . The same three authors and Sagan conjectured that for each n n , the map pre 2 \mathrm{pre}_{2} is injective on the set of integer partitions of n n . In this note, we prove their conjecture.



1.
Background

An
integer partition

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{\ell})
of a positive integer
n
is a weakly decreasing sequence of positive integers whose sum is
n
. We refer to the
\lambda_{i}
’s as the
parts
of a partition,
|\lambda|=n
as the
size
, and
\ell(\lambda)=\ell
as the
length
.

The
j
th
elementary symmetric polynomial

e_{j}
is defined by

e_{j}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\begin{cases}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{j}\leq n}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{j}}&\text{if $j\leq n$};\\
0&\text{if }j>n.\end{cases}

We are interested in the partition whose parts are the summands of
e_{j}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{\ell})
.

Definition 1.1

(
[
BBM24
]
)
.

Given a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{\ell})
, we define
\mathrm{pre}_{k}(\lambda)
to be the partition whose multiset of parts is
\{\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}}\ :1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq\ell\}
. If
\ell(\lambda)<j
, then
\mathrm{pre}_{k}(\lambda)
is the empty partition
\emptyset
. We call
\mathrm{pre}_{k}(\lambda)
an
elementary symmetric partition
.

For example, consider the partition
\lambda=(4,2,1,1)
and
k=2
. We can evaluate
e_{2}(4,2,1,1)
as

e_{2}(4,2,1,1)=4\cdot 2+4\cdot 1+4\cdot 1+2\cdot 1+2\cdot 1+1\cdot 1,

and by looking at the summands, we get that
\mathrm{pre}_{2}(\lambda)=(8,4,4,2,2,1)
.

The main conjecture from
[
BBM24
]
and
[
BBMS24+
]
is that the map
\mathrm{pre}_{2}
is injective on the set of partitions of
n
for each
n
. Our main result is a proof of this conjecture.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
\lambda
and
\mu
are partitions of a positive integer
n
, then
\mathrm{pre}_{2}(\lambda)=\mathrm{pre}_{2}(\mu)
if and only if
\lambda=\mu
.

In fact, our proof establishes a stronger statement where we replace partitions of
n
by finite nonincreasing sequences of positive real numbers summing to
n
.

2.
Proof

We start with an auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\lambda
and
\mu
be integer partitions of a positive integer
n
. If
\lambda_{1}=\mu_{1}
and
\mathrm{pre}_{2}(\lambda)=\mathrm{pre}_{2}(\mu)
, then
\lambda=\mu
.

Proof.

Observe that
\ell(\lambda)=\ell(\mu)
because
\ell(\mathrm{pre}_{2}(\lambda))=\binom{\ell(\lambda)}{2}=\binom{\ell(\mu)}{2}=\ell(\mathrm{pre}_{2}(\mu))
. We want to show
\lambda_{i}=\mu_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq\ell(\lambda)=\ell(\mu)
, and we proceed by strong induction on
i
. For the base case, we know
\lambda_{1}=\mu_{1}
by assumption.

For the inductive step, observe that

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle=\max\left(\mathrm{pre}_{2}(\lambda)\setminus\{\!\!\{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}:1\leq i<j\leq k-1\}\!\!\}\right)

\displaystyle=\max\left(\mathrm{pre}_{2}(\mu)\setminus\{\!\!\{\mu_{i}\mu_{j}:1\leq i<j\leq k-1\}\!\!\}\right)=\mu_{1}\mu_{k},

where
\{\!\!\{\}\!\!\}
denotes multiset. Since
\lambda_{1}=\mu_{1}
, we conclude that
\lambda_{k}=\mu_{k}
.
∎

With Lemma
2.1
established, we have the necessary tool to prove our main result.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

It suffices to show the identity
\sum_{1\leq i\leq\ell}\lambda_{i}^{2^{m}}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq\ell}\mu_{i}^{2^{m}}
for all positive integers
m
because then

\lambda_{1}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\left(\sum_{1\leq i\leq\ell}\lambda_{i}^{2^{m}}\right)^{1/2^{m}}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\left(\sum_{1\leq i\leq\ell}\mu_{i}^{2^{m}}\right)^{1/2^{m}}=\mu_{1},

and we can apply Lemma
2.1
.

We prove the identity by strong induction on
m
. For the base case,
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}=\sum_{i}\mu_{i}=n
by assumption. For the inductive step, observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq\ell(\lambda)}\lambda_{i}^{2^{m}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{1\leq i\leq\ell(\lambda)}\lambda_{i}^{2^{m-1}}\right)^{2}-2\sum_{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\in\mathrm{pre}_{2}(\lambda)}(\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j})^{2^{m-1}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{1\leq i\leq\ell(\mu)}\mu_{i}^{2^{m-1}}\right)^{2}-2\sum_{\mu_{i}\mu_{j}\in\mathrm{pre}_{2}(\mu)}(\mu_{i}\mu_{j})^{2^{m-1}}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq\ell(\mu)}\mu_{i}^{2^{m}},

as desired.
∎

We remark that a classical result of Selfridge and Strauss
[
SS58
]
states that the set
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell}\}\subset\mathbb{C}
is uniquely determined by the set of pairwise sums of elements of
X
if and only if
\ell
is not a power of
2
. An immediate consequence of this theorem is that
\mathrm{pre}_{2}
is injective on partitions with lengths that are not powers of
2
, regardless of the sizes of the partitions. The strength of our result is that we can also handle partitions with lengths that are powers of
2
if we fix their sizes.

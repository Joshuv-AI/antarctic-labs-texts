A potential theory approach to the capillarity-driven Hele-Shaw problem Thanks: Partially supported by the RTG 2339 “Interfaces, Complex Structures, and Singular Limits” of the German Science Foundation (DFG)
Bogdan–Vasile Matioc, Christoph Walker

Abstract
. In this paper, we demonstrate that potential theory provides a powerful framework for analyzing quasistationary fluid flows in bounded geometries, where the bulk dynamics are governed by elliptic equations with constant coefficients. This approach is illustrated by the two-dimensional Hele-Shaw problem with surface tension, for which we derive local well-posedness and parabolic smoothing in (almost) optimal function spaces. In addition, we establish a generalized principle of linearized stability for a particular class of abstract quasilinear parabolic problems, which enables us to show that the stationary solutions to the Hele-Shaw problem are exponentially stable.

1.
Introduction and main results

The surface tension-driven Hele-Shaw problem
[
29
]
is a classical model in fluid mechanics that describes the motion of an incompressible fluid confined within a narrow channel between two transparent flat plates. Due to the small gap separating the plates, the flow is effectively uniform in the transverse direction, and the problem reduces to a two-dimensional model.
Let
{\Omega(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2},~t\geq 0}
, represent the bounded domain occupied by the incompressible fluid at time
t\geq 0
, with corresponding velocity field
v(t)
and pressure
u(t)
.
The dynamics in the bulk is governed by Darcy’s empirical law
[
12
]
; that is,

\mathrm{div}\,v(t)=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad v(t)=-\nabla u(t)

for
t>0
.
Moreover, the free boundary
\Gamma(t):=\partial\Omega(t)
of the fluid evolves with normal velocity
V(t)
that coincides with the normal component of the fluid velocity
v(t)
.
Taking into account surface tension effects, the pressure
u(t)
on
\Gamma(t)
is assumed to be proportional to the curvature
\kappa_{\Gamma(t)}
of
\Gamma(t)
, taken as positive for convex shapes.
Altogether, the surface tension-driven Hele-Shaw problem is described by the following system of equations:

\left.\begin{array}[]{rcllll}\Delta u(t)&=&0&\quad\text{in $\Omega(t)$,}\\
u(t)&=&\kappa_{\Gamma(t)}&\quad\text{on $\Gamma(t)$,}\\
V(t)&=&-\partial_{{\rm n}_{\Gamma(t)}}u(t)&\quad\text{on $\Gamma(t)$}\end{array}\right\}

(1.1a)

for
t>0
, where, for simplicity, all material parameters are normalized to
1
, and
{\rm n}_{\Gamma(t)}
denotes the outer unit normal vector to
\Gamma(t)
.
System (
1.1a
) is supplemented by the initial condition

\begin{array}[]{rcllll}\Omega(0)&=&\Omega_{0},\end{array}

(1.1b)

where
\Omega_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a given bounded domain.

The Hele-Shaw problem (
1.1
) has received considerable attention in the mathematical community; see, e.g.,
[
15
,
19
,
10
,
18
,
32
,
36
,
27
,
14
,
3
,
2
]
, though this list is by no means exhaustive.
A powerful approach to studying (
1.1
) and related problems – such as the Muskat problem, the Mullins-Sekerka flow, or the quasistationary Stokes flow – in bounded domains, where the bulk unknowns are determined by solving elliptic boundary value problems depending on the geometry (see the monograph
[
38
]
), is to apply the Hanzawa transformation
[
28
]
and reformulate the problem on a fixed reference domain.
This approach, however, imposes certain regularity requirements on the geometry.
Moreover, the transformed equations are often quite involved and depend nonlinearly on the geometry through the Hanzawa transform.
A further drawback of this method is that, after solving the transformed boundary value problems in the bulk, additional nonlinearities and nonlocalities are introduced.

An alternative approach that has been used recently in the context of the problems mentioned above – mainly in unbounded settings with equations defined in the entire space (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
33
,
8
,
16
,
35
,
11
,
24
,
26
,
9
,
20
,
31
,
4
,
23
,
22
,
21
,
13
,
25
]
) – is to employ potential theory in order to derive an explicit integral representation for the unknowns in the bulk.
This method requires less regularity of the geometry than the classical Hanzawa approach and yields results for initial data in (nearly) optimal function spaces within the setting of classical solutions. Moreover, it may even allow for the treatment of critical regularity cases for initial data within the framework of strong or viscosity solutions.

In this paper, we demonstrate that the potential-theoretic approach, combined with abstract parabolic theory, can be applied to the Hele-Shaw problem driven by surface tension,
even in the case of a bounded domain.
For simplicity, we restrict our analysis to a two-dimensional, star-shaped geometry
\Omega(t)
.
However, the method is expected to extend to more general geometries and higher dimensions, albeit with increased technical complexity.
Our choice of star-shaped domains is motivated by the fact that, in such geometries, the Rellich identities (see Lemma
3.4
) – which are central to our analysis – admit an explicit and compact form that can be readily applied to study the invertibility of the double-layer potential operator.

Main results

In the following, we denote by
\mathbb{T}
the boundary of the unit disc and by
{\mathrm{t}}
and
{\mathrm{n}}
the unit tangent and the outward unit normal vectors to
\mathbb{T}
, respectively. Functions defined on
\mathbb{T}
are throughout identified with
2\pi
-periodic functions on
\mathbb{R}
.
We denote, for a given function
\rho\in\mathrm{C}^{1}(\mathbb{T})
with
\rho>0
, by

\Gamma_{\rho}:=\{\rho(\tau){\mathrm{n}}(\tau)\,:\,\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}

the
{\rm C}^{1}
-boundary of the star-shaped domain
\Omega_{\rho}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and note that the map
\Xi_{\rho}:\mathbb{T}\to\Gamma_{\rho}
, given by

\Xi_{\rho}(\tau)=\rho(\tau){\mathrm{n}}(\tau),\qquad\tau\in\mathbb{R},

(1.2)

is a
{\rm C}^{1}
-diffeomorphism.

In order to tackle problem (
1.1
) analytically, we assume that, at each time instant
t>0
, the evolving boundary
\Gamma(t)
in (
1.1
) takes the form
\Gamma(t)=\Gamma_{\rho(t)}
. In this geometric setting, the Hele-Shaw problem (
1.1
) can be written as

\left.\begin{array}[]{rcllll}\Delta u(t)&=&0&\quad\text{in $\Omega_{\rho(t)}$,}\\[4.30554pt]
u(t)&=&\kappa_{\Gamma_{\rho(t)}}&\quad\text{on $\Gamma_{\rho(t)}$,}\\[4.30554pt]
V(t)&=&-\partial_{{\rm n}_{\rho}(t)}u(t)&\quad\text{on $\Gamma_{\rho(t)}$}\end{array}\right\}

(1.3a)

for
t>0
, where
{\rm n}_{\rho}(t):=n_{\Gamma_{\rho(t)}}
. Assuming that
\partial\Omega_{0}=\Gamma_{\rho_{0}}
for a positive function
\rho_{0}
, the initial condition (
1.1b
) becomes

\begin{array}[]{rcllll}\rho(0)&=&\rho_{0}.\end{array}

(1.3b)

The unknowns of problem (
1.3
) are the function
\rho
and the pressure
u
.
However, since at each time instant
t>0
the pressure
u(t)
is uniquely determined by the geometry
\rho(t)
we will henceforth refer to
\rho
alone as the solution to (
1.3
). In fact, we formulate in Section
3
and Section
4
the system (
1.3
) as a quasilinear parabolic problem for
\rho
of the form

\cfrac{{\rm d}\rho}{{\rm d}t}(t)=\Phi(\rho(t))[\rho(t)],\quad t>0,\qquad\rho(0)=\rho_{0},

(1.4)

where
\Phi:\mathcal{V}_{r}\to\mathcal{H}(H^{r+1}(\mathbb{T}),H^{r-2}(\mathbb{T}))
is a smooth mapping for each
r\in(3/2,2]
and

\mathcal{V}_{r}:=\{\rho\in H^{r}(\mathbb{T})\,:\,\rho>0\},\qquad r>3/2.

(1.5)

That is,
\Phi(\rho)[\cdot]
is the generator of an analytic semigroup on the Bessel potential space
H^{r-2}(\mathbb{T})
with domain
H^{r+1}(\mathbb{T})
for each
\rho\in\mathcal{V}_{r}
.
This feature together with the quasilinear parabolic theory from
[
5
,
34
]
enables us to prove that the Hele-Shaw problem (
1.3
) is locally well-posed in
\mathcal{V}_{\bar{r}}
for any
\bar{r}\in(3/2,2)
as stated in the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\bar{r}\in(3/2,2)
and chose an arbitrary
r\in(3/2,\bar{r})
.
Then, given
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{\bar{r}}
, there exists a unique maximal classical solution
\rho=\rho(\cdot;\rho_{0})
to (
1.3
)
such that

\rho\in{\rm C}([0,T^{+}),\mathcal{V}_{\bar{r}})\cap{\rm C}((0,T^{+}),H^{r+1}(\mathbb{T}))\cap{\rm C}^{1}((0,T^{+}),H^{r-2}(\mathbb{T}))

(1.6)

and, for some
\eta\in(0,(\bar{r}-r)/3]
,

\rho\in{\rm C}^{\eta}([0,T^{+}),H^{r}(\mathbb{T})),

(1.7)

with

\rho(t)\in H^{r+2}(\mathbb{T})\quad\text{and}\quad{u(t)\in{\rm C}^{2}(\Omega_{\rho(t)})\cap{\rm C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega_{\rho(t)}})}\qquad\text{for $t\in(0,T^{+})$,}

(1.8)

where
T^{+}=T^{+}(\rho_{0})>0
is the maximal existence time of the solution.

Additionally,
[(t,\rho_{0})\mapsto\rho(t;\rho_{0})]
defines a semiflow on
\mathcal{V}_{\bar{r}}
that is smooth in

\{(t,\rho_{0})\,:\,\text{$\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{\bar{r}}$ and $0<t<T^{+}(\rho_{0})$}\}

(1.9)

and, moreover,

[(t,\tau)\mapsto\rho(t)(\tau)]\in{\rm C}^{\infty}((0,T^{+})\times\mathbb{R}).

(1.10)

We add the following observations derived from the parabolic smoothing property (
1.10
):

Remark 1.2
.

(a)

According to (
1.10
), solutions to (
1.3
) become instantaneously smooth, even though the curvature of the boundary of the initial geometry is not a function, but merely a distribution.

(b)

It follows from (
1.10
) that the maximal existence time
T^{+}(\rho_{0})
of the maximal solution corresponding to
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{\bar{r}}
is independent of the choice of
r\in(3/2,\bar{r})
.

Theorem
1.1
reveals the strength of the potential-theoretic approach:

Remark 1.3
.

In Theorem
1.1
we may choose
\bar{r}
arbitrarily close to the critical threshold
3/2
in the Bessel potential scale.
Indeed, as noted also in the context of the Muskat problem and the Mullins–Sekerka flow
[
35
,
16
]
(that are two-phase analogues of the Hele-Shaw flow), the exponent
3/2
is critical
since
H^{3/2}(\mathbb{T})
is invariant under the natural scaling of the problem.
We point out that previous local well-posedness results in bounded geometries
[
18
,
19
]
, based on a Hanzawa transformation approach,
require
{\rm C}^{2+\alpha}
-regularity of the initial geometry with
{\alpha>0}
.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we first show in Proposition
3.1
that the pressure (and the velocity) in the bulk is determined by the geometry of the interface via an explicit integral formula.
As in
[
18
,
19
]
, the curvature operator is decomposed into a quasilinear part containing the highest order derivatives and a nonlinear lower order part.
The handling of the nonlinear lower order term requires special care due to the lower regularity setting considered herein compared to
[
18
,
19
]
.
By differentiating this term and treating it as part of a quasilinear structure, the corresponding term in the final formulation (
1.4
) of the Hele-Shaw problem can be regarded as lower order.
This analysis is made possible by the potential-theoretic framework, particularly through Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
,
where we show that the derivative of certain singular integral operators, evaluated at a density function
\beta
, coincides with the inverse of the adjoint operators applied to
-\beta^{\prime}
.
Finally, in Section
3
, we reformulate the problem as a quasilinear evolution equation, that is shown in Section
4
to be of parabolic type.
This allows us to apply abstract parabolic theory from
[
5
,
34
]
to establish Theorem
1.1
.
The arguments in Sections
3
and
4
rely on technical results developed in Appendices
B
–
C
,
where we establish mapping properties, commutator estimates, and localization results for a particular class of (singular) integral operators that may be of independent interest.

Concerning the long-time behavior of solutions, we point out that the set of equilibrium solutions to (
1.3
) forms a 3-dimensional manifold consisting exclusively of circles.
Moreover, the flow (
1.3
) preserves both the area and the center of mass of
\Omega_{\rho_{0}}
, since Reynolds’ transport theorem and Stokes’ theorem yield for

that

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}|\Omega_{\rho(t)}|

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Gamma_{\rho(t)}}\partial_{{\rm n}_{\rho(t)}}u(t)\,|{\rm d}\xi|=0,

(1.11)

\displaystyle-\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega_{\rho(t)}}z\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma_{\rho(t)}}z\partial_{{\rm n}_{\rho(t)}}u(t)\,|{\rm d}\xi|=\int_{\Gamma_{\rho(t)}}u(t)\,{\mathrm{n}}_{\rho(t)}\,|{\rm d}\xi|=\int_{\Gamma_{\rho(t)}}\kappa_{\Gamma_{\rho(t)}}{\mathrm{n}}_{\rho(t)}\,|{\rm d}\xi|=0.

(1.12)

We establish in Theorem
1.4
the exponential stability of the unit circle with center of mass located at
(0,0)
, which corresponds to the stationary solution
\rho=1
to (
1.3
).
Since system (
1.1
) is invariant under rotations and translations, the exponential stability result in Theorem

is actually valid for any circle
(with arbitrary area and center of mass) provided the perturbations in the phase space preserve both the area and the center of mass of the circle.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\bar{r}\in(3/2,2)
.
Then, given
\xi\in(0,6)
, there exist constants
\varepsilon>0
and
M\geq 1
such that for all
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{\bar{r}}

satisfying
\|\rho_{0}-1\|_{H^{\bar{r}}}<\varepsilon
,

|\Omega_{\rho_{0}}|=\pi,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\int_{\Omega_{\rho_{0}}}z\,{\rm d}z=(0,0),

(1.13)

the maximal solution

to (

) is globally defined and

\|\rho(t)-1\|_{H^{{\bar{r}}}}\leq Me^{-\xi t}\qquad\text{for all $t\in[0,\infty)$.}

We emphasize that, due to the invariants of the problem; see (

We note that more general versions of such generalized stability principles have been established in

Our main results, Theorem

3.
An equivalent formulation of (
1.3
) using singular integrals

The main goal of this section is to show that the evolution problem (
1.3
) can be formulated as a quasilinear evolution equation for
\rho
exclusively, with nonlinearities expressed as singular integrals.
To achieve this, we first establish in Proposition
3.1
the unique solvability of an elliptic problem related to (
1.3
), see (
3.1
),
which implies in particular that the function
\rho
determines at each time instant the pressure
u
.
Moreover, we determine
u
explicitly as an integral involving a density function
\beta=\beta(\rho)
that
solves a linear equation associated with the double layer potential of the Laplace operator corresponding to the graph
\Gamma_{\rho}
; see (
3.3
).
The unique solvability of (
3.3
) is a key ingredient in the analysis, and is based on mapping properties for the family of (singular) integral operators introduced in (
3.7
) below
and investigated in Appendix
B
.

Throughout this section, we fix an arbitrary
r\in(3/2,2]
and recall the definition of
\mathcal{V}_{r}
in (
1.5
), noticing that
H^{r}(\mathbb{T})\hookrightarrow{\rm C}^{r-1/2}(\mathbb{T})
.

The fixed time problem

We prove that the pressure
u
is uniquely determined by the geometry, i.e. that the elliptic Dirichlet problem (
3.1
) below has a unique solution which is given as an explicit integral over the boundary
\Gamma_{\rho}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Given
\rho\in\mathcal{V}_{r}
and
\varphi\in H^{r}(\mathbb{T})
,
the Dirichlet problem

\left.\begin{array}[]{rcllll}\Delta u&=&0&\quad\text{in $\Omega_{\rho}$,}\\[4.30554pt]

has a unique solution
{u\in{\rm C}^{2}(\Omega_{\rho})\cap{\rm C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega_{\rho}})}
which is given by

\displaystyle u(z):=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{\rho}}\frac{(\xi-z)\cdot{\mathrm{n}}_{\rho}(\xi)}{|\xi-z|^{2}}\beta\circ\Xi_{\rho}^{-1}(\xi)\,|{\rm d}\xi|=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{(\Xi_{\rho}(s)-z)\cdot\Xi_{\rho}^{\prime}(s)^{\top}}{|\Xi_{\rho}(s)-z|^{2}}\beta(s)\,{\rm d}s

(3.2)

for
z\in\Omega_{\rho}
,
with
\beta\in H^{r}(\mathbb{T})
denoting the unique solution to the equation

(1+\mathbb{D}(\rho))[\beta]=\varphi,

(3.3)

where

is the double layer potential for the Laplace operator associated with the curve

Proof.

The uniqueness of the solution to (
3.1
) follows via the weak maximum principle for elliptic problems.
In order to establish the existence claim we define, for a given density function
\beta\in H^{r}(\mathbb{T}),
the function
u:=u(\rho)[\beta]:\Omega_{\rho}\to\mathbb{R}
by (
3.2
).
Clearly,
u\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\rho})
, and, since

\Delta_{z}\frac{(\xi-z)_{i}}{|\xi-z|^{2}}=0\qquad\text{in~$\Omega_{\rho}$, \quad$i=1,2$.}

it follows that
u
satisfies (
3.1
)
1
.
Recall from Plemelj’s theorem
[
30
]
that for a Hölder continuous function
\varphi:\Gamma_{\rho}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and

\phi(z):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma_{\rho}}\frac{\varphi(\xi)}{\xi-z}\,{\rm d}\xi,\qquad z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Gamma_{\rho},

one has that
\phi\in{\rm C}(\overline{\Omega_{\rho}^{\pm}})
, where
\Omega^{+}_{\rho}:=\Omega_{\rho}
and
\Omega^{-}_{\rho}:=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{\Omega^{+}_{\rho}}
, and, given
z_{0}\in\Gamma_{\rho}
,

\lim_{\Omega^{\pm}_{\rho}\ni z\to z_{0}}\phi(z)=\pm\frac{\varphi(z_{0})}{2}+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\mathop{\rm PV}\nolimits\int_{\Gamma_{\rho}}\frac{\varphi(\xi)}{\xi-z_{0}}\,{\rm d}\xi,

(3.4)

where the symbol
\mathop{\rm PV}\nolimits
stands for the principal value.
Thus, since

\mathrm{Re}\,\left(\frac{1}{i}\frac{1}{\xi-z}{\mathrm{t}}_{\rho}(\xi)\right)=\frac{(\xi-z)\cdot{\mathrm{n}}_{\rho}(\xi)}{|\xi-z|^{2}},\qquad\xi\in\Gamma_{\rho},\,z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Gamma_{\rho},

it follows that

\mathrm{Re}\,\phi(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{\rho}}\frac{(\xi-z_{0})\cdot{\mathrm{n}}_{\rho}(\xi)}{|\xi-z_{0}|^{2}}\varphi(\xi)\,|{\rm d}\xi|,\qquad z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Gamma_{\rho}.

(3.5)

Consequently, (
3.2
), (
3.4
), and (
3.5
) entail that
u\in{\rm C}(\overline{\Omega_{\rho}})
and

u\circ\Xi_{\rho}=(1+\mathbb{D}(\rho))[\beta]\qquad\text{on $\mathbb{T}$,}

with
\mathbb{D}(\rho)
being defined in (
3.11
).
Hence, if
\beta
solves the equation (
3.3
),
then
u
solves also (
3.1
)
2
.
The existence of a solution to (
3.3
) is established in Proposition
3.6
(ii) below.

Finally, in view of the formula

\nabla_{z}\frac{(\Xi_{\rho}(s)-z)\cdot\Xi_{\rho}^{\prime}(s)^{\top}}{|\Xi_{\rho}(s)-z|^{2}}=\partial_{s}\frac{(\Xi_{\rho}(s)-z)^{\top}}{|\Xi_{\rho}(s)-z|^{2}},\qquad s\in\mathbb{R},\,z\in\Omega_{\rho},

integration by parts yields for
z\in\Omega_{\rho}

\displaystyle\nabla u(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\partial_{s}\frac{(\Xi_{\rho}(s)-z)^{\top}}{|\Xi_{\rho}(s)-z|^{2}}\beta(s)\,{\rm d}s=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{(\Xi_{\rho}(s)-z)^{\top}}{|\Xi_{\rho}(s)-z|^{2}}\beta^{\prime}(s)\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\Xi_{\rho}(s)-z}{|\Xi_{\rho}(s)-z|^{2}}\beta^{\prime}(s)\,{\rm d}s=-\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{1}{\overline{\Xi_{\rho}(s)-z}}\beta^{\prime}(s)\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\overline{\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{1}{\Xi_{\rho}(s)-z}\frac{\beta^{\prime}(s)}{\Xi_{\rho}^{\prime}(s)}\Xi_{\rho}^{\prime}(s)\,{\rm d}s}=\overline{\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{\Gamma_{\rho}}\frac{1}{\xi-z}\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\Xi_{\rho}^{\prime}}\circ\Xi_{\rho}^{-1}(\xi)\,{\rm d}\xi},

and Plemelj’s theorem ensures that
{u\in{\rm C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega_{\rho}})}
with

(\nabla u)\circ\Xi_{\rho}(\tau)=\frac{\beta^{\prime}\Xi_{\rho}^{\prime}}{\omega_{\rho}^{2}}(\tau)-\frac{1}{\pi}\mathop{\rm PV}\nolimits\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{(\Xi_{\rho}(s)-(\Xi_{\rho}(\tau))^{\top}}{|\Xi_{\rho}(s)-\Xi_{\rho}(\tau)|^{2}}\beta^{\prime}(s)\,{\rm d}s,\qquad\tau\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.6)

∎

The solvability of (

) requires some preparation, which is the context of the subsequent considerations.

A family of (singular) integral operators

To establish the unique solvability of equation (
3.3
),
we introduce a family of (singular) integral operators that play a crucial role in our approach.
Given
m,\,n,\,p\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
0\leq p\leq n+1
and
\varrho:=(\varrho_{1},\ldots,\varrho_{m})\in\mathcal{V}_{r}^{m}
we define the (singular) integral operator

B_{n,m}^{p}(\varrho)[h,\beta](\tau):=\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\mathop{\rm PV}\nolimits\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{t_{[s]}^{p}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\delta_{[\tau,s]}h_{i}}{t_{[s]}}}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\Big[(\varrho_{i}(\tau)+\varrho_{i}(\tau-s))^{2}+\Big(\frac{\delta_{[\tau,s]}\varrho_{i}}{t_{[s]}}\Big)^{2}\Big]}\frac{\beta(\tau-s)}{t_{[s]}}\,{\rm d}s

(3.7)

for
h=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})\in W^{1}_{\infty}(\mathbb{T})^{n}
,
\beta\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T})
, and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, where, for any function
u:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,

t_{[s]}:=\tan(s/2)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\delta_{[\tau,s]}u:=u(\tau)-u(\tau-s),\qquad s\in(-\pi,\pi),\,\tau\in\mathbb{R}.

The principal value is needed only when
p=0
, the kernel of
B_{n,m}^{p}
being bounded when
1\leq p\leq n+1
since
h_{i}\in W^{1}_{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
,
1\leq i\leq n
.
When the components of
\varrho
and
h
are equal to
\rho\in\mathcal{V}_{r}
, we set

{\sf B}_{n,m}^{p}(\rho):=B_{n,m}^{p}(\rho,\ldots,\rho)[\rho,\ldots,\rho,\cdot].

(3.8)

We recall that the periodic Hilbert transform
H
is given by
H=2B^{0}_{0,1}(1)
and is a Fourier multiplier with symbol
(-i\,\mathrm{sign}(k))_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
.

As a straightforward consequence of Lemma
B.1
and Lemma
B.2
from the Appendix
B
, the mappings

\displaystyle[\rho\mapsto\mathsf{B}_{n,m}^{0}(\rho)]:\mathcal{V}_{r}\to\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mathbb{T})),

(3.9)

\displaystyle[\rho\mapsto\mathsf{B}_{n,m}^{p}(\rho)]:\mathcal{V}_{r}\to\mathcal{L}(L_{1}(\mathbb{T}),{\rm C}(\mathbb{T})),\qquad 1\leq p\leq n+1,

are locally Lipschitz continuous.
Moreover, as shown in Lemma
B.6
, it holds that

\displaystyle[\rho\mapsto\mathsf{B}_{n,m}^{0}(\rho)]\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{V}_{r},\mathcal{L}(H^{r-1}(\mathbb{T}))),

(3.10)

\displaystyle[\rho\mapsto\mathsf{B}_{n,m}^{p}(\rho)]\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{V}_{r},\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mathbb{T}),H^{1}(\mathbb{T}))),\qquad 1\leq p\leq n+1.

The double layer potential and its dual

Given
\rho\in\mathcal{V}_{r}
, we introduce the double layer potential
\mathbb{D}(\rho)
for the Laplace operator associated with the graph
\Gamma_{\rho}
and its
L_{2}
-adjoint
\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*}
by

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(\rho)[\beta](\tau)

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{\pi}\mathop{\rm PV}\nolimits\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{(\delta_{[\tau,s]}\Xi_{\rho})\cdot\Xi_{\rho}^{\prime}(\tau-s)^{\top}}{|\delta_{[\tau,s]}\Xi_{\rho}|^{2}}\beta(\tau-s)\,{\rm d}s,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*}[\beta](\tau)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\pi}\mathop{\rm PV}\nolimits\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{(\delta_{[\tau,s]}\Xi_{\rho})\cdot\Xi_{\rho}^{\prime}(\tau)^{\top}}{|\delta_{[\tau,s]}\Xi_{\rho}|^{2}}\beta(\tau-s)\,{\rm d}s

(3.12)

for
\beta\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T})
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
.
The
L_{2}
-boundedness of
\mathbb{D}(\rho)
and of
\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*}
follow immediately from (
3.9
) in view of the identities

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(\rho)[\beta]

\displaystyle=-\mathsf{B}^{2}_{1,1}(\rho)[\rho\beta]-\mathsf{B}^{0}_{1,1}(\rho)[\rho\beta]+2\rho\mathsf{B}^{1}_{0,1}(\rho)[\rho\beta]+2\rho\mathsf{B}^{0}_{0,1}(\rho)[\rho^{\prime}\beta]

(3.13)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*}[\beta]

\displaystyle=\rho\mathsf{B}^{2}_{1,1}(\rho)[\beta]+\rho\mathsf{B}^{0}_{1,1}(\rho)[\beta]+2\rho\mathsf{B}^{1}_{0,1}(\rho)[\rho\beta]-2\rho^{\prime}\mathsf{B}^{0}_{0,1}(\rho)[\rho\beta],

(3.14)

which may be verified using the tangent half-angle formulas for sine and cosine and expressing the denominator as

|\delta_{[\tau,s]}\Xi_{\rho}|^{2}=\frac{t_{[s]}^{2}}{1+t_{[s]}^{2}}\left(\big(\rho(\tau)+\rho(\tau-s)\big)^{2}+\Big(\frac{\delta_{[\tau,s]}\rho}{t_{[s]}}\Big)^{2}\right).

Moreover, these formulas together with (
3.10
) show that

\mathbb{D},\,\mathbb{D}(\cdot)^{*}\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{V}_{r},\mathcal{L}(H^{r-1}(\mathbb{T}))).

(3.15)

An important property relating
\mathbb{D}(\rho)
and
\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*}
is provided by the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Given
\rho\in\mathcal{V}_{r}
and
\beta\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, it holds that
\mathbb{D}(\rho)[\beta]\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
with

(\mathbb{D}(\rho)[\beta])^{\prime}=-\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*}[\beta^{\prime}].

(3.16)

Moreover, we have

\mathbb{D}\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{V}_{r},\mathcal{L}(H^{r}(\mathbb{T}))).

(3.17)

Proof.

In view of (
3.9
), (
3.13
), and (
3.14
) it suffices to establish (
3.16
) for
\rho,\,\beta\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
with
{\rho>0}
.
In this case, Lemma
B.3
(in particular (
B.17
)) and Lemma
B.4
(in particular (
B.18
))
enable us to exchange differentiation and integration when differentiating the function
\mathsf{B}_{n,m}^{p}(\rho)[\beta]\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
and, together
with (
3.13
), we deduce for
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
that

\displaystyle\big(\mathbb{D}(\rho)[\beta]\big)^{\prime}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}(\rho)[\beta^{\prime}](\tau)-\frac{1}{\pi}\mathop{\rm PV}\nolimits\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\partial_{\tau}\bigg[\frac{(\delta_{[\tau,s]}\Xi_{\rho})\cdot\Xi_{\rho}^{\prime}(\tau-s)^{\top}}{|\delta_{[\tau,s]}\Xi_{\rho}|^{2}}\bigg]\beta(\tau-s)\,{\rm d}s.

Since for
0\neq s\in(-\pi,\pi)
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\partial_{\tau}\frac{(\delta_{[\tau,s]}\Xi_{\rho})\cdot\Xi_{\rho}^{\prime}(\tau-s)^{\top}}{|\delta_{[\tau,s]}\Xi_{\rho}|^{2}}=\partial_{s}\frac{(\delta_{[\tau,s]}\Xi_{\rho})\cdot(\delta_{[\tau,s]}\Xi_{\rho}^{\prime})^{\top}}{|\delta_{[\tau,s]}\Xi_{\rho}|^{2}},

integration by parts leads us to (
3.16
).
The remaining mapping property (
3.17
) now follows by combining (
3.15
) and (
3.16
).
∎

Two further singular integral operators

We define two additional singular integral operators that are used in the analysis.
Specifically, given
\rho\in\mathcal{V}_{r}
, we set

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}(\rho)[\beta](\tau)

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{\pi}\mathop{\rm PV}\nolimits\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{(\delta_{[\tau,s]}\Xi_{\rho})\cdot\Xi_{\rho}^{\prime}(\tau-s)}{|\delta_{[\tau,s]}\Xi_{\rho}|^{2}}\beta(\tau-s)\,{\rm d}s,

(3.18)

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}(\rho)^{*}[\beta](\tau)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\pi}\mathop{\rm PV}\nolimits\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{(\delta_{[\tau,s]}\Xi_{\rho})\cdot\Xi_{\rho}^{\prime}(\tau)}{|\delta_{[\tau,s]}\Xi_{\rho}|^{2}}\beta(\tau-s)\,{\rm d}s,

(3.19)

for
\beta\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T})
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, with
\mathbb{B}(\rho)^{*}
being the
L_{2}
-adjoint of
\mathbb{B}(\rho)
.
Indeed, both operators belong to
\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mathbb{T}))
in view of (

), since

\mathbb{B}(\rho)[\beta]=-\mathsf{B}^{2}_{1,1}(\rho)[\rho^{\prime}\beta]-\mathsf{B}^{0}_{1,1}(\rho)[\rho^{\prime}\beta]-2\rho\mathsf{B}^{0}_{0,1}(\rho)[\rho\beta]+2\rho\mathsf{B}^{1}_{0,1}(\rho)[\rho^{\prime}\beta]

(3.20)

and

\mathbb{B}(\rho)^{*}[\beta]=\rho^{\prime}\mathsf{B}^{2}_{1,1}(\rho)[\beta]+\rho^{\prime}\mathsf{B}^{0}_{1,1}(\rho)[\beta]+2\rho\mathsf{B}^{0}_{0,1}(\rho)[\rho\beta]+2\rho^{\prime}\sf B^{1}_{0,1}(\rho)[\rho\beta].

(3.21)

Moreover, recalling (
3.10
), we infer from (
3.20
)-(
3.21
) that

\mathbb{B},\,\mathbb{B}(\cdot)^{*}\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{V}_{r},\mathcal{L}(H^{r-1}(\mathbb{T}))).

(3.22)

We now prove for
\mathbb{B}(\rho)
and
\mathbb{B}(\rho)^{*}
an analogue of Lemma
3.2
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Given
\rho\in\mathcal{V}_{r}
and
\beta\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, it holds that
\mathbb{B}(\rho)[\beta]\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
with

(\mathbb{B}(\rho)[\beta])^{\prime}=-\mathbb{B}(\rho)^{*}[\beta^{\prime}].

(3.23)

Proof.

Arguing as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, for
\rho,\,\beta\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
with

and

we have

\displaystyle\big(\mathbb{B}(\rho)[\beta]\big)^{\prime}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{B}(\rho)[\beta^{\prime}](\tau)-\frac{1}{\pi}\mathop{\rm PV}\nolimits\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\partial_{\tau}\bigg[\frac{(\delta_{[\tau,s]}\Xi_{\rho})\cdot\Xi_{\rho}^{\prime}(\tau-s)}{|\delta_{[\tau,s]}\Xi_{\rho}|^{2}}\bigg]\beta(\tau-s)\,{\rm d}s,

Integration by parts now yields (

\mathbb{B}(\rho)^{*},\,\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(L_{2,0}(\mathbb{T})).

(3.24)

Indeed, given
\beta\in L_{2,0}(\mathbb{T})
, there exists
\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
with
\varphi^{\prime}=\beta
, and together with (

\displaystyle(2\pi)\langle\mathbb{B}(\rho)^{*}[\beta]\rangle=\langle\mathbb{B}(\rho)^{*}[\beta]|1\rangle_{L_{2}}=\langle\mathbb{B}(\rho)^{*}[\varphi^{\prime}]|1\rangle_{L_{2}}=-\langle(\mathbb{B}(\rho)[\varphi])^{\prime}|1\rangle_{L_{2}}=0,

3.24
) for

(the corresponding property for

Invertibility of layer potentials in
L_{2}(\mathbb{T})

The invertibility of layer potentials is a fundamental issue in potential theory; see
[
41
]
,
where the invertibility of
\lambda+\mathbb{D}(\rho)
and
\lambda+\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*}
is established in an
L_{2}
-setting for Lipschitz domains and
\lambda=\pm 1
.
For star-shaped domains in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we provide herein a short and direct approach, based on the Rellich identities (
3.25
),
that, on the one hand, allows us to consider a larger set of values for
\lambda
than in
[
41
]
, and, on the other hand,
permits us to establish the invertibility of these operators in Sobolev spaces of higher order.

Lemma 3.4

(Rellich identities)
.

Given
\rho\in\mathcal{V}_{r}
and
\beta\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T})
, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\rho^{2}}{\omega_{\rho}^{2}}\big(\big|(\pm 1-\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*})[\beta]\big|^{2}-\big|\mathbb{B}(\rho)^{*}[\beta]\big|^{2}\big)\,{\rm ds}

(3.25)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{(\rho^{2})^{\prime}(\pm 1-\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*})[\beta]\mathbb{B}(\rho)^{*}[\beta]}{\omega_{\rho}^{2}}\,{\rm ds}-4(\pm 1-1)\pi|\langle\beta\rangle|^{2}.

Proof.

Recalling (
3.9
), (
3.14
), and (
3.21
), it suffices to prove (
3.25
) for

\rho,\,\beta\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})

with
\rho>0
, that we now fix.
Similarly as in the proof of Proposition
3.1
, we define
{v:=v(\rho)[\beta]:\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Gamma_{\rho}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}}
by

\displaystyle v(z):=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{\rho}}\frac{(\xi-z)^{\top}}{|\xi-z|^{2}}\frac{\beta}{\omega_{\rho}}\circ\Xi_{\rho}^{-1}(\xi)\,|{\rm d}\xi|,\qquad z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Gamma_{\rho}.

(3.26)

Then,
v\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Gamma_{\rho})
satisfies

{\rm div\,}v={\rm rot\,}v=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Gamma_{\rho}.

Let
v^{\pm}:=v|_{\Omega_{\rho}^{\pm}}
, where again we set
\Omega_{\rho}^{+}:=\Omega_{\rho}
and
{\Omega_{\rho}^{-}:=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{\rho}^{+}}}
.
Plemelj’s theorem (see, e.g.
[
30
]
) ensures that
v^{\pm}\in{\rm C}(\overline{\Omega_{\rho}^{\pm}})
with

v^{\pm}\circ\Xi_{\rho}(\tau)=\pm\frac{\beta\Xi_{\rho}^{\prime}}{\omega_{\rho}^{2}}(\tau)-\frac{1}{\pi}\mathop{\rm PV}\nolimits\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{(\Xi_{\rho}(s)-\Xi_{\rho}(\tau))^{\top}}{|\Xi_{\rho}(s)-\Xi_{\rho}(\tau)|^{2}}\beta(s)\,{\rm d}s,\qquad\tau\in\mathbb{T}.

(3.27)

Let now
W:\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Gamma_{\rho}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be given by

W(z)=z|v|^{2}(z)-2v(z)z\cdot v(z)

and set
W^{\pm}:=W|_{\Omega_{\rho}^{\pm}}
.
Then,
{\rm div\,}W^{\pm}=0
in
\Omega^{\pm}_{\rho}
.
Since
W^{+}\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\rho}^{+})\cap{\rm C}\big(\overline{\Omega_{\rho}^{+}}\big)
, Stokes’ theorem thus yields

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(W^{+}\circ\Xi_{\rho}(\tau))\cdot\Xi_{\rho}^{\prime}(\tau)^{\top}\,{\rm d\tau}=\int_{\Gamma_{\rho}}W^{+}\cdot{\mathrm{n}}_{\rho}\,{\rm|d\xi|}=0.

Hence, splitting
v^{\pm}|_{\Gamma_{\rho}}
into normal and tangential components and noticing from (
3.27
) that

(v^{\pm}\cdot{\mathrm{n}}_{\rho})\circ\Xi_{\rho}=\frac{\mathbb{B}(\rho)^{*}[\beta]}{\omega_{\rho}},\qquad(v^{\pm}\cdot{\mathrm{t}}_{\rho})\circ\Xi_{\rho}=\frac{(\pm 1-\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*})[\beta]}{\omega_{\rho}}

leads to the identity in (
3.25
) with
+
.

To establish the second identity (
3.25
) (with
-
), we first apply Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem to deduce that

\displaystyle\overline{z}v(z)

\displaystyle=\overline{\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{\Gamma_{\rho}}\frac{z}{\xi-z}\frac{\beta}{\omega_{\rho}}\circ\Xi_{\rho}^{-1}(\xi)\,|{\rm d}\xi}|\underset{|z|\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\overline{\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{\Gamma_{\rho}}\frac{\beta}{\omega_{\rho}}\circ\Xi_{\rho}^{-1}(\xi)\,|{\rm d}\xi}|=\overline{-\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\beta\,{\rm ds}},

hence
\overline{z}v(z)\to-2i\overline{\langle\beta\rangle}
as
|z|\to\infty
.
Since
W^{-}\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\rho}^{-})\cap{\rm C}\big(\overline{\Omega_{\rho}^{-}}\big)
, we integrate
{\rm div\,}W^{-}
over the annular domain

\Omega_{\rho}^{-,R}=\{z\in\Omega_{\rho}^{-}\,:\,|z|<R\},

R>2,

and obtain via Stokes’ theorem that

\int_{\Gamma_{\rho}}W^{-}\cdot{\mathrm{n}}_{\rho}\,{\rm|d\xi|}=\int_{R\mathbb{T}}W^{-}(\xi)\cdot\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\,{\rm|d\xi|}\underset{R\to\infty}{\to}8\pi|\langle\beta\rangle|^{2}.

This yields the second Rellich identity and completes the proof.
∎

We are now in a position to address the invertibility of
\lambda+\mathbb{D}(\rho)
and
\lambda+\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\rho\in\mathcal{V}_{r}
.

(i)

The operator
\lambda+\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(L_{2,0}(\mathbb{T}))
is invertible for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus(-1,1)
.

(ii)

The operator
\lambda+\mathbb{D}(\rho)\in\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mathbb{T}))
is invertible for all
\lambda\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
\rho\in\mathcal{V}_{r}
be fixed.
Concerning (i), we prove that there exists a constant
{C\geq 1}
such that for all
\beta\in L_{2,0}(\mathbb{T})
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus(-1,1)
we have

C\|(\lambda+\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*})[\beta]\|_{2}\geq\|\beta\|_{2}.

(3.28)

The claim then follows directly from (
3.28
) and the method of continuity; see, e.g.,
[
6
, Proposition I.1.1.1]
.
To this end, Young’s inequality and (
3.25
) imply there is a constant
C\geq 1
such that for all
\beta\in L_{2,0}(\mathbb{T})

\|(\pm 1-\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*})[\beta]\|_{2}\leq C\|\mathbb{B}(\rho)^{*}[\beta]\|_{2},

(3.29)

and, as a direct consequence of (
3.29
),

\|\beta\|_{2}\leq C\|\mathbb{B}(\rho)^{*}[\beta]\|_{2},\qquad\beta\in L_{2,0}(\mathbb{T}).

(3.30)

Substituting for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus(-1,1)

(\pm 1-\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*})[\beta]=(\lambda\pm 1)\beta-(\lambda+\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*})[\beta]

(3.31)

in (
3.25
), yields

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\rho^{2}}{\omega_{\rho}^{2}}\big(|(\lambda\pm 1)\beta|^{2}-2(\lambda\pm 1)\beta(\lambda+\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*})[\beta]+\big|(\lambda+\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*})[\beta]\big|^{2}-\big|\mathbb{B}(\rho)^{*}[\beta]\big|^{2}\big)\,{\rm ds}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{(\rho^{2})^{\prime}}{\omega_{\rho}^{2}}\big((\lambda\pm 1)\beta\mathbb{B}(\rho)^{*}[\beta]-(\lambda+\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*})[\beta]\mathbb{B}(\rho)^{*}[\beta]\big)\,{\rm ds}.

(3.32)

We next multiply the equation (
3.32
) with
+
by
\lambda-1
and the equation (
3.32
) with
-
by
-(\lambda+1)
to obtain, after building the sum of the resulting identities,

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\rho^{2}}{\omega_{\rho}^{2}}\big((\lambda^{2}-1)|\beta|^{2}-\big|(\lambda+\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*})[\beta]\big|^{2}+\big|\mathbb{B}(\rho)^{*}[\beta]\big|^{2}\big)\,{\rm ds}=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{(\rho^{2})^{\prime}}{\omega_{\rho}^{2}}(\lambda+\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*})[\beta]\mathbb{B}(\rho)^{*}[\beta]\,{\rm ds}.

Using again Young’s inequality, we find a constant
C\geq 1
with the property that for all
{\beta\in L_{2,0}(\mathbb{T})}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus(-1,1)
we have

(\lambda^{2}-1)\|\beta\|_{2}^{2}+\|\mathbb{B}(\rho)^{*}[\beta]\|_{2}^{2}\leq C\|(\lambda+\mathbb{D}(\rho)^{*})[\beta]\|_{2}^{2}.

This relation together with (
3.30
) immediately implies (
3.28
),
and the proof of (i) is complete.

Similarly, for (ii) is suffices to show that there exists a constant

\displaystyle\leq(\mu/2)\|\pi^{\varepsilon}_{j}h\|_{H^{r+1}}+K\|h\|_{H^{r^{\prime}+1}}.

Moreover, recalling (
2.3
), we use the identity
\chi_{j}^{\varepsilon}\pi_{j}^{\varepsilon}=\pi_{j}^{\varepsilon}
and (

) to estimate

\displaystyle\Big\|\pi_{j}^{\varepsilon}\kappa(\rho_{\theta})[h]+\frac{\rho_{\theta}}{\omega_{\rho_{\theta}}}(\tau_{j}^{\varepsilon})(\pi_{j}^{\varepsilon}h)^{\prime\prime}\Big\|_{H^{r-1}}

(4.9)

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\chi_{j}^{\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{\rho_{\theta}}{\omega^{3}_{\rho_{\theta}}}-\frac{\rho_{\theta}}{\omega^{3}_{\rho_{\theta}}}(\tau_{j}^{\varepsilon})\Big)(\pi_{j}^{\varepsilon}h)^{\prime\prime}\Big\|_{H^{r-1}}+K\|h\|_{H^{r^{\prime}+1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\|\chi_{j}^{\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{\rho_{\theta}}{\omega^{3}_{\rho_{\theta}}}-\frac{\rho_{\theta}}{\omega^{3}_{\rho_{\theta}}}(\tau_{j}^{\varepsilon})\Big)\Big\|_{\infty}\|\pi_{j}^{\varepsilon}h\|_{H^{r+1}}+K\|h\|_{H^{r^{\prime}+1}}

\displaystyle\leq(\mu/2)\|\pi^{\varepsilon}_{j}h\|_{H^{r+1}}+K\|h\|_{H^{r^{\prime}+1}},

where the Hölder continuity of the function

has been used in the last step.
The estimate (
4.6
) follows immediately from (

)-(
4.9
).
∎

Now we can state and prove the key step of the localization procedure:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mu>0
. Then, for each sufficiently small

there exists a positive constant
{K=K(\varepsilon)}
such that for all

,

, and

it holds that

\|\pi^{\varepsilon}_{j}\Phi_{1}(\theta\rho+1-\theta)[h]-\omega^{-3}_{\theta\rho+1-\theta}(\tau_{j}^{\varepsilon})\Phi_{1}(1)[\pi^{\varepsilon}_{j}h]\|_{H^{r-2}}\leq\mu\|\pi^{\varepsilon}_{j}h\|_{H^{r+1}}+K\|h\|_{H^{r^{\prime}+1}}.

(4.10)

Proof.

We use the same convention regarding the constants
C
and
K
as in the proof of Lemma
4.2
and we set again
\rho_{\theta}:=\theta\rho+1-\theta
for
\theta\in[0,1]
.
To start, for
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

,

, and

, we note from (

) and (

) that

\displaystyle\|\pi_{j}^{\varepsilon}\Phi_{1}(\rho_{\theta})[h]-\omega^{-3}_{\rho_{\theta}}(\tau_{j}^{\varepsilon})\Phi_{1}(1)[\pi^{\varepsilon}_{j}h]\|_{H^{r-2}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\Big(\frac{\pi_{j}^{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\theta}}\mathbb{B}(\rho_{\theta})[\alpha_{1}(\rho_{\theta})[h]]-\omega^{-3}_{\rho_{\theta}}(\tau_{j}^{\varepsilon})H[(\pi_{j}^{\varepsilon}h)^{\prime\prime}]\Big)^{\prime}\Big\|_{H^{r-2}}+\Big\|\frac{(\pi_{j}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}{\rho_{\theta}}\mathbb{B}(\rho_{\theta})[\alpha_{1}(\rho_{\theta})[h]]\Big\|_{H^{r-2}},

and, by (

therein),

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{(\pi_{j}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}{\rho_{\theta}}\mathbb{B}(\rho_{\theta})[\alpha_{1}(\rho_{\theta})[h]]\Big\|_{H^{r-2}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\frac{(\pi_{j}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}{\rho_{\theta}}\mathbb{B}(\rho_{\theta})[\alpha_{1}(\rho_{\theta})[h]]\Big\|_{H^{r^{\prime}-1}}\leq K\|h\|_{H^{r^{\prime}+1}}.

Since

\displaystyle\|\pi_{j}^{\varepsilon}\Phi_{1}(\rho_{\theta})[h]-\omega^{-3}_{\tau\rho}(\tau_{j}^{\varepsilon})\Phi_{1}(1)[\pi^{\varepsilon}_{j}h]\|_{H^{r-2}}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\frac{\pi_{j}^{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\theta}}\mathbb{B}(\rho_{\theta})[\alpha_{1}(\rho_{\theta})[h]]-\omega^{-3}_{\rho_{\theta}}(\tau_{j}^{\varepsilon})H[(\pi_{j}^{\varepsilon}h)^{\prime\prime}]\Big\|_{H^{r-1}}+K\|h\|_{H^{r^{\prime}+1}},

Recalling (
3.20

therein), Lemma

, Lemma

, and Lemma
4.2

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{\pi_{j}^{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\theta}}\mathbb{B}(\rho_{\theta})[\alpha_{1}(\rho_{\theta})[h]]+\frac{1}{\rho_{\theta}}(\tau_{j}^{\varepsilon})H[\pi_{j}^{\varepsilon}\alpha_{1}(\rho_{\theta})[h]]\Big\|_{H^{r-1}}

(4.12)

\displaystyle\leq(\mu/(2C_{0}))\|\pi^{\varepsilon}_{j}\alpha_{1}(\rho_{\theta})[h]]\|_{H^{r-1}}+K\|\alpha_{1}(\rho_{\theta})[h]]\|_{H^{r^{\prime}-1}}

\displaystyle\leq(\mu/2)\|\pi^{\varepsilon}_{j}h\|_{H^{r+1}}+K\|h\|_{H^{r^{\prime}+1}}

provided that

).

Let

for all

and

.
Lemma

(ii) ensures for
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
sufficiently small that

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{1}{\rho_{\theta}}(\tau_{j}^{\varepsilon})H[\pi_{j}^{\varepsilon}\alpha_{1}(\rho_{\theta})[h]]+\omega^{-3}_{\rho_{\theta}}(\tau_{j}^{\varepsilon})H[(\pi_{j}^{\varepsilon}h)^{\prime\prime}]\Big\|_{H^{r-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\Big\|\pi^{\varepsilon}_{j}\alpha_{1}(\rho_{\theta})[h]]+\frac{\rho_{\theta}}{\omega_{\tau\rho}^{3}}(\tau_{j}^{\varepsilon})(\pi_{j}^{\varepsilon}h)^{\prime\prime}\Big\|_{H^{r-1}}

\displaystyle\leq(\mu/2)\|\pi^{\varepsilon}_{j}h\|_{H^{r+1}}+K\|h\|_{H^{r^{\prime}+1}}.

Gathering (

We next provide the proof of Proposition

.

Proof of Proposition

.

Since

Fix

. Lemma
4.3
with

\displaystyle\kappa\|(\lambda-\Phi_{1}(\rho_{\theta}))[h]\|_{H^{r-2}}\geq|\lambda|\,\|h\|_{H^{r-2}}+\|h\|_{H^{r+1}}.

We conclude this section with the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
3/2<r<\bar{r}<2
.
As shown in Section
3
, the Hele-Shaw problem (
1.3
) is equivalent to the quasilinear problem (
3.49
), where
\Phi:\mathcal{V}_{r}\to\mathcal{H}(H^{r+1}(\mathbb{T}),H^{r-2}(\mathbb{T}))
is smooth according to (
3.53
) and Proposition
4.1
.
Recalling (
2.4
), we may thus apply the quasilinear parabolic theory from
[
5
, Section 12]
(see also
[
34
, Theorem 1.1 and Remark 1.2]
) and conclude that problem (
3.49
) has for each
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{\bar{r}}

a unique maximal classical solution
\rho=\rho(\cdot;\rho_{0})
with maximal existence time
T^{+}=T^{+}(\rho_{0})
that satisfies (
1.6
) and (
1.7
).
Moreover, the solution map
[(t,\rho_{0})\mapsto\rho(t;\rho_{0})]
is a semiflow on
\mathcal{V}_{\bar{r}}
which is smooth in the open set
defined in (
1.9
).

It remains to prove the parabolic smoothing property (
1.10
), which together with Proposition
3.1
immediately yields (
1.8
).
To this end, we employ a parameter trick that is used in other contexts as well; see
[
7
,
17
,
37
]
.
A direct application to solutions with initial data in
\mathcal{V}_{\bar{r}}
is not possible, since the space
{\mathrm{C}^{\eta}([0,T^{+}),H^{r}(\mathbb{T}))}
, with
\eta\in(0,1)
,
is not invariant under the scaling introduced in (
4.18
).
We therefore proceed in three steps:

(I)

We construct more regular maximal solutions
\widetilde{\rho}(\cdot;\rho_{0})
starting at
{\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{r+1}}
(with a stronger uniqueness criterion).

(II)

We prove that (the maximal existence time of)
\widetilde{\rho}(\cdot;\rho_{0})
and
\rho(\cdot;\rho_{0})
coincide.

(III)

We establish the smoothing property (
1.8
) for
\rho(\cdot;\rho_{0})
with
{\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{r+1}}
.

Regarding (I): Since (
3.53
) and Proposition
4.1
with
r=2
imply that
\Phi:\mathcal{V}_{2}\to\mathcal{H}(H^{3}(\mathbb{T}),L_{2}(\mathbb{T}))
is smooth,

[
34
, Theorem 1.1 and Remark 1.2]
guarantee, for each
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{r+1}
,
that problem (
3.49
) has a unique maximal classical solution
\widetilde{\rho}=\widetilde{\rho}(\cdot;\rho_{0})
such that

\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathrm{C}([0,\widetilde{T}^{+}),\mathcal{V}_{r+1})\cap\mathrm{C}((0,\widetilde{T}^{+}),H^{3}(\mathbb{T}))\cap\mathrm{C}^{1}((0,\widetilde{T}^{+}),L_{2}(\mathbb{T})),

(4.16)

and

\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathrm{C}^{\alpha}([0,\widetilde{T}^{+}),H^{2}(\mathbb{T}))\qquad\text{for some $\alpha\in(0,1)$,}

(4.17)

with maximal existence time
\widetilde{T}^{+}=\widetilde{T}^{+}(\rho_{0})\leq T^{+}(\rho_{0})
(and obviously
\widetilde{\rho}=\rho
on
[0,\widetilde{T}^{+})
).
We can improve the uniqueness statement and show that the solution is unique within the regularity class (
4.16
).
Indeed, let
T<\widetilde{T}^{+}
be fixed arbitrarily.
Since
\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathrm{C}([0,T],\mathcal{V}_{r+1})
, (
3.44
) yields

\kappa(\widetilde{\rho})[\widetilde{\rho}]\in\mathrm{C}([0,T],H^{r-1}(\mathbb{T})),\qquad\lambda(\widetilde{\rho})[\widetilde{\rho}]\in\mathrm{C}([0,T],{H}^{r-1}_{0}(\mathbb{T})),

and, by (
3.15
), we get
\alpha_{i}(\widetilde{\rho})[\widetilde{\rho}]\in\mathrm{C}([0,T],{H}^{r-1}(\mathbb{T}))
,
i=1,2
.
Using (
3.22
) and (
3.50
)-(
3.51
), we conclude

\frac{{\rm d}\widetilde{\rho}}{{\rm d}t}\in\mathrm{C}([0,T],{H}^{r-2}(\mathbb{T}))

and thus, by the fundamental theorem of calculus and (
2.4
), we have
\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathrm{C}^{\alpha}([0,T],H^{2}(\mathbb{T}))
with
{\alpha=(r-1)/3}
,
which proves in view of
[
34
, Remark 1.2 (ii)]
the uniqueness claim.

Regarding (II): We only need to show that
\widetilde{T}^{+}=T^{+}
.
To this end, we assume by contradiction that
\widetilde{T}^{+}<T^{+}
. Then, since
\rho\in{\rm C}((0,\widetilde{T}^{+}],\mathcal{V}_{r+1})
by (
1.6
),

[
34
, Proposition 2.1]
together with the arguments leading to the improved uniqueness claim in the previous step ensures that there exist constants
{\varepsilon>0}
and
\delta>0
such that for all
\rho_{*}\in\mathcal{V}_{r+1}
with
\|\rho_{*}-\rho(\widetilde{T}^{+})\|_{H^{r+1}}<\varepsilon
, the evolution problem

\cfrac{{\rm d}\rho}{{\rm d}t}(t)=\Phi(\rho(t))[\rho(t)],\quad t>0,\qquad\rho(0)=\rho_{*},

has a unique classical solution
\bar{\rho}=\bar{\rho}(\cdot;\rho_{*}):[0,\delta]\to\mathcal{V}_{r+1}
enjoying (
4.16
) with
\widetilde{T}^{+}
replaced by
\delta
.
Choosing
t_{0}<\widetilde{T}^{+}
such that
\|\rho(t_{0})-\rho(\widetilde{T}^{+})\|_{H^{r+1}}<\varepsilon
and
t_{0}+\delta>\widetilde{T}^{+}
, we conclude that the map

t\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}\widetilde{\rho}(t;\rho_{0}),&&0\leq t<\widetilde{T}^{+},\\[4.30554pt]
\bar{\rho}(t-t_{0};\rho(t_{0})),&&t_{0}\leq t<t_{0}+\delta,\end{array}\right.

is a solution to (
3.49
) satisfying (
4.16
) on
[0,t_{0}+\delta)
with

, which contradicts the maximality property of
\widetilde{\rho}(\cdot;\rho_{0})
and thus
\widetilde{T}^{+}=T^{+}
.

Regarding (III): We prove that
\rho(\cdot;\rho_{0})\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}((0,T^{+})\times\mathbb{T})
for each
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{r+1}
.
To this end, we define for each
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\in(0,\infty)^{2}
the function
\rho_{\lambda}
by

\rho_{\lambda}(t)(\tau):=\rho(\lambda_{1}t)(\tau+\lambda_{2}t),\qquad\tau\in\mathbb{R},\ 0\leq t<T_{\lambda}:=T^{+}/\lambda_{1},

(4.18)

which satisfies

\rho_{\lambda}\in\mathrm{C}([0,T_{\lambda}),\mathcal{V}_{r+1})\cap\mathrm{C}((0,T_{\lambda}),H^{3}(\mathbb{T}))\cap\mathrm{C}^{1}((0,T_{\lambda}),L_{2}(\mathbb{T})).

We next introduce the function
u:[0,T_{\lambda})\to(0,\infty)^{2}\times\mathcal{V}_{r+1}
by

u(t):=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\rho_{\lambda}(t)).

It is straightforward to prove that
u
solves the quasilinear problem

\cfrac{{\rm d}u}{{\rm d}t}(t)=\Psi(u(t))[u(t)],\quad t>0,\qquad u(0)=(\lambda,\rho_{0}),

(4.19)

where
\Psi:(0,\infty)^{2}\times\mathcal{V}_{2}\to\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times H^{3}(\mathbb{T}),\mathbb{R}^{2}\times L_{2}(\mathbb{T}))
is defined by

\Psi(u)[v]=(0,\lambda_{1}\Phi(\rho)[h]+\lambda_{2}h^{\prime}),\qquad u=(\lambda,\rho),\,v=(\mu,h).

Then, (
3.53
) (with

therein) implies that
\Psi\in{\rm C}^{\infty}((0,\infty)^{2}\times\mathcal{V}_{2},\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times H^{3}(\mathbb{T}),\mathbb{R}^{2}\times L_{2}(\mathbb{T})))
.

\Psi(u)=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\[4.30554pt]
0&\lambda_{1}\Phi(\rho)+\lambda_{2}\cfrac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x}\end{pmatrix},\qquad u=(\lambda,\rho)\in(0,\infty)^{2}\times\mathcal{V}_{2},

where

, Proposition
4.1
(with
r=2
therein) and
[

, I.Theorem 1.6.1]
ensure that the operator
\Psi(u)
generates a strongly continuous and analytic semigroup
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times L_{2}(\mathbb{T})
for each
u\in(0,\infty)\times\mathcal{V}_{2}
.
Arguing as in part (I) of the proof, we deduce that problem (
4.19
) has for each
u_{0}=(\lambda,\rho_{0})\in(0,\infty)^{2}\times\mathcal{V}_{r+1}
a unique maximal solution
u=u(\cdot;u_{0})
with
u=(\lambda,\bar{\rho})
and

\bar{\rho}\in\mathrm{C}([0,t^{+}),\mathcal{V}_{r+1})\cap\mathrm{C}((0,t^{+}),H^{3}(\mathbb{T}))\cap\mathrm{C}^{1}((0,t^{+}),L_{2}(\mathbb{T})),

(4.20)

where

is the maximal existence time of

.
Since the problems (
3.49
) and (
4.19
) are equivalent we actually have

and
\bar{\rho}=\rho_{\lambda}
.
Moreover, the set

\mathcal{D}:=\{(t,u_{0})\,:\,u_{0}\in(0,\infty)^{2}\times\mathcal{V}_{r+1},\,0<t<t^{+}(u_{0})\}

Let now
(t_{0},\tau_{0})\in(0,T^{+})\times\mathbb{R}
be fixed and choose

such that for all
\lambda
belonging to the ball

in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

of radius

.
This implies in particular that

the function

Since

(4.21)

Let now

with

f(t,\tau):=\Big(\frac{t}{t_{0}},\frac{\tau-\tau_{0}+t_{0}}{t_{0}}\Big).

(4.22)

is well-defined. Composing the functions defined in (

) and (

), we deduce that the mapping

∎

First note that the stationary solutions to (

Indeed, by Theorem

Moreover, recalling (

Next, recall from (

Thus, all eigenvalues

, except for the eigenvalue

which has multiplicity

, and consequently, the principle of linearized stability

cannot be applied in this context.
Instead, we will use Theorem

to prove Theorem

, which requires some preparation.
Since we restrict to solutions satisfying (

we observe that if

\|u_{1}(\tau)-u_{1}(s)\|_{\beta}\leq\frac{N}{2L}|\tau-s|^{\rho}\qquad\text{for all $0\leq s\leq\tau\leq t$.}

(A.15)

Concerning the second component

of

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\exp\Big(2L\int_{0}^{\tau}r^{\alpha-1}e^{-\nu r}\,{\rm d}r\Big)\leq\varepsilon\exp\Big(2L\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha-1}e^{-\nu r}\,{\rm d}r\Big)\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\mathsf{e}_{\alpha,\beta}}.

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon L\exp\Big(2L\int_{0}^{\tau}r^{\alpha-1}e^{-\nu r}\,{\rm d}r\Big)|\tau-s|^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha-1}\,{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon\alpha^{-1}L\exp\Big(2L\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha-1}e^{-\nu r}\,{\rm d}r\Big)\Big(\frac{4L}{N}\Big)^{(\alpha-\rho)/\rho}|\tau-s|^{\rho}\leq\frac{N}{2L}|\tau-s|^{\rho},

and therefore

(A.17)

Gathering (

t_{1}=t^{+}(u_{0})
and for

(A.18)

Appendix B
Mapping properties for the (singular) integral operators
B_{n,m}^{p}

Let

\mathcal{V}_{r,M}:=\{\rho\in\mathcal{V}_{r}\,:\,\text{$\rho>M^{-1}$ and $\|\rho\|_{H^{r}}<M$}\}.

(B.1)

Some of our arguments below rely on the observation that for

|\tan(x)-x|\leq x^{2}|\tan(x)|\qquad\text{and}\qquad|x|\leq|\tan(x)|.

(B.2)

Mapping properties for
B_{n,m}^{p}

We recall the definition (
3.7
) of the integral operators
B_{n,m}^{p}
with
{m,\,n,\,p\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and
0\leq p\leq n+1
.
We begin by showing that, for
p=0
, the operator
B_{n,m}^{p}(\varrho)[h,\cdot]
belongs to
{\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mathbb{T}))}
for each
\varrho\in\mathcal{V}_{r,M}^{m}
and
h\in W^{1}_{\infty}(\mathbb{T})^{n}
.
To this end we introduce a second family of multilinear singular integral operators

G_{n}[h,\beta](\tau):=\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\mathop{\rm PV}\nolimits\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\Big(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\delta_{[\tau,s]}h_{i}}{t_{[s]}}\Big)\frac{\beta(\tau-s)}{t_{[s]}}\,{\rm d}s,

where
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
h=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})\in W^{1}_{\infty}(\mathbb{T})^{n}
,
\beta\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T})
, and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
.
In the arguments that follow we will use the algebraic property

\displaystyle B_{n,m}^{p}(\varrho)[h,\beta]-B_{n,m}^{p}(\bar{\varrho})[h,\beta]

(B.3)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Big\{B_{n+2,m+1}^{p}(\bar{\varrho}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\varrho}_{j},\varrho_{j},\ldots,\varrho_{m})[h,\bar{\varrho}_{j}+\varrho_{j},\bar{\varrho}_{j}-\varrho_{j},\beta]

\displaystyle+(\bar{\varrho}_{j}^{2}-\varrho_{j}^{2})B_{n,m+1}^{p}(\bar{\varrho}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\varrho}_{j},\varrho_{j},\ldots,\varrho_{m})[h,\beta]

\displaystyle+(\bar{\varrho}_{j}+\varrho_{j})B_{n,m+1}^{p}(\bar{\varrho}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\varrho}_{j},\varrho_{j},\ldots,\varrho_{m})[h,(\bar{\varrho}_{j}-\varrho_{j})\beta]

\displaystyle+(\bar{\varrho}_{j}-\varrho_{j})B_{n,m+1}^{p}(\bar{\varrho}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\varrho}_{j},\varrho_{j},\ldots,\varrho_{m})[h,(\bar{\varrho}_{j}+\varrho_{j})\beta]

\displaystyle+B_{n,m+1}^{p}(\bar{\varrho}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\varrho}_{j},\varrho_{j},\ldots,\varrho_{m})[h,(\bar{\varrho}_{j}^{2}-\varrho_{j}^{2})\beta]\Big\},

which holds for all
0\leq p\leq n+1
,
\varrho=(\varrho_{1},\ldots,\varrho_{m}),\,\bar{\varrho}=(\bar{\varrho}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\varrho}_{m})\in\mathcal{V}_{r,M}^{m}
,
h\in W^{1}_{\infty}(\mathbb{T})^{n}
, and
\beta\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T})
.
Obviously, the operators
B_{n,m}^{p}(\varrho)[h,\cdot]
are only singular when

, in which case we have:

Lemma B.1
.

Let
n,\,m\in{\mathbb{N}}
be given.

(i)

There is a constant
C=C(M)>0
such that
for all
h\in W^{1}_{\infty}(\mathbb{T})^{n}
and
{\varrho\in\mathcal{V}_{r,M}^{m}}
it holds that

\|B_{n,m}^{0}(\varrho)[h,\cdot]\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mathbb{T}))}\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{n}\|h_{i}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}.

(B.4)

Moreover, the mapping
[\varrho\mapsto B_{n,m}^{0}(\varrho)]:\mathcal{V}_{r,M}^{m}\to\mathcal{L}_{\rm sym}^{n}(W^{1}_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}),\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mathbb{T})))
is locally Lipschitz continuous.

(ii)

There exists a constant
C=C(M)>0
such that
for all
\beta\in H^{r-1}(\mathbb{T})
,
h\in H^{r}(\mathbb{T})^{n}
, and
\varrho\in\mathcal{V}_{r,M}^{m}
it holds that

\|B_{n,m}^{0}(\varrho)[h,\beta]\|_{\infty}\leq C\|\beta\|_{H^{r-1}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\|h_{i}\|_{H^{r}}.

(B.5)

Moreover, the mapping
[\varrho\mapsto B_{n,m}^{0}(\varrho)]:\mathcal{V}_{r,M}^{m}\to\mathcal{L}_{\rm sym}^{n}(H^{r}(\mathbb{T}),\mathcal{L}(H^{r-1}(\mathbb{T}),L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T})))
is locally Lipschitz continuous.

Proof.

In order to establish (i), we note that there exists a constant
C=C(M)>0
such that for all
\varrho\in\mathcal{V}_{r,M}^{m}
and

\tau,\,s\in(-\pi,\pi)
we have

\Bigg|\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\Big[(\varrho_{i}(\tau)+\varrho_{i}(\tau-s))^{2}+\Big(\frac{\delta_{[\tau,s]}\varrho_{i}}{t_{[s]}}\Big)^{2}\Big]}-\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}4\omega_{\varrho_{i}}^{2}(\tau)}\Bigg|\leq C|s|^{r-3/2}.

Using the latter estimate together with Minkowski’s inequality, we derive for

\displaystyle A_{n,m}(\varrho)[h,\beta]:=B_{n,m}^{0}(\varrho)[h,\beta]-\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{4\omega_{\varrho_{i}}^{2}}\Big)G_{n}[h,\beta]

(B.6)

the inequality

\displaystyle\|A_{n,m}(\varrho)[h,\beta]\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\|h_{i}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\bigg)\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{|s|^{r-3/2}}{t_{[s]}}\Big(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|\beta(\tau)|^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau\Big)^{1/2}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\|\beta\|_{2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\|h_{i}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}.

The estimate (
B.4
) is now a direct consequence of
[
8
, Lemma A.3]
.
Finally, the local Lipschitz continuity assertion is a straightforward consequence of (
B.3
) and (
B.4
).

In order to establish (
B.5
), we denote by
K_{n,m}=K_{n,m}(\tau,s)
the bounded part of the kernel of the singular integral operator
B_{n,m}^{0}(\varrho)[h,\cdot]
; that is,

B_{n,m}^{0}(\varrho)[h,\beta](\tau)=\mathop{\rm PV}\nolimits\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}K_{n,m}(\tau,s)\frac{\beta(\tau-s)}{t_{[s]}}\,{\rm d}s,\qquad\tau\in\mathbb{R}.

Then

\displaystyle B_{n,m}^{0}(\varrho)[h,\beta](\tau)

\displaystyle=\int_{\{|s|<\pi\}}K_{n,m}(\tau,s)\frac{\beta(\tau-s)-\beta(\tau)}{t_{[s]}}\,{\rm d}s+\beta(\tau)\mathop{\rm PV}\nolimits\int_{\{|s|<1\}}\frac{K_{n,m}(\tau,s)}{t_{[s]}}\,{\rm d}s,

where the first term on the right-hand side may be estimated by the right-hand side of (
B.5
) due to

.
Concerning the remaining term we get, in view of

|K_{n,m}(\tau,s)-K_{n,m}(\tau,-s)|\leq C|s|^{r-3/2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\|h_{i}\|_{H^{r}},

that

\displaystyle\bigg|\mathop{\rm PV}\nolimits\int_{\{|s|<1\}}\frac{K_{n,m}(\tau,s)}{t_{[s]}}\,{\rm d}s\bigg|=\bigg|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{K_{n,m}(\tau,s)-K_{n,m}(\tau,-s)}{t_{[s]}}\,{\rm d}s\bigg|\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{n}\|h_{i}\|_{H^{r}},

and thus (
B.5
).
Since the local Lipschitz continuity assertion follows as before from (
B.3
) and (
B.5
), the proof is complete.
∎

\displaystyle\leq C[h_{1}]_{{\rm C}^{1/2}}\Big(\prod_{i=2}^{n}\|h_{i}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\Big)\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\|\mathsf{T}_{s}\beta-\beta\|_{2}}{|s|^{3/2}}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\|\beta\|_{H^{r-1}}\|h_{1}\|_{H^{1}}\Big(\prod_{i=2}^{n}\|h_{i}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\Big)\Big(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|s|^{2r-4}\,{\rm d}s\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\beta\|_{H^{r-1}}\|h_{1}\|_{H^{1}}\prod_{i=2}^{n}\|h_{i}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}.

Estimate for
E_{3}
.
The integral
E_{3}
can be represented as a sum

E_{3}=S_{1}+\sum_{j=2}^{n}S_{2,j}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}S_{3,k},

where, for
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle|S_{1}(\tau)|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\beta\|_{\infty}\Big(\prod_{i=2}^{n}\|h_{i}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\Big)\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\Big|\frac{\delta_{[\tau,s]}h_{1}}{s}\partial_{s}\Big(\frac{s^{2}}{t_{[s]}^{2}}\Big)\Big|\,{\rm d}s,

\displaystyle|S_{2,j}(\tau)|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\beta\|_{\infty}\Big(\prod_{i=2,i\neq j}^{n}\|h_{i}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\Big)\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\Big|\frac{\delta_{[\tau,s]}h_{1}}{s}\partial_{s}\Big(\frac{\delta_{[\tau,s]}h_{j}}{t_{[s]}}\Big)\Big|\,{\rm d}s,

\displaystyle|S_{3,k}(\tau)|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\beta\|_{\infty}\Big(\prod_{i=2}^{n}\|h_{i}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\Big)\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\Big|\frac{\delta_{[\tau,s]}h_{1}}{s}\Big|\bigg(|\varrho_{j}^{\prime}(\tau-s)\big|+\Big|\partial_{s}\Big(\frac{\delta_{[\tau,s]}\varrho_{j}}{t_{[s]}}\Big|\bigg)\,{\rm d}s.

Since
|\partial_{s}\big(s^{2}/t_{[s]}^{2}\big)|\leq C|s|
for
s\in(-\pi,\pi)
by (

\|S_{1}\|_{2}\leq C\|\beta\|_{\infty}\|h_{1}\|_{\infty}\prod_{i=2}^{n}\|h_{i}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}.

(B.14)

To estimate the terms
S_{2,j}
and
S_{3,k}
we use the same strategy that we thus detail only for the first term.
Since for
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and

\Big|\partial_{s}\Big(\frac{\delta_{[\tau,s]}h_{j}}{t_{[s]}}\Big)\Big|\leq C\|h_{j}\|_{H^{r}}+C\frac{\big|\delta_{[\tau,s]}h_{j}-h_{j}^{\prime}(\tau-s)\big|}{s^{2}},

Minkowski’s inequality together with the embedding

leads to

\displaystyle\|S_{2,j}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\beta\|_{\infty}\|h_{1}\|_{H^{1}}\Big(\prod_{i=2,i\neq j}^{n}\|h_{i}^{\prime}\|_{H^{r}}\Big)\bigg(\|h_{j}\|_{H^{r}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{1}{|s|^{1/2}}\,{\rm d}s+J_{j}\bigg),

where, using also the fundamental theorem of calculus and Hölders inequality,

\displaystyle J_{j}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{1}{|s|^{5/2}}\Big(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\big|\delta_{[\tau,s]}h_{j}-sh_{j}^{\prime}(\tau-s)\big|^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau\Big)^{1/2}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{1}{|s|^{3/2}}\Big(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\big|h_{j}^{\prime}(\tau+as)-h_{j}^{\prime}(\tau)\big|^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau\Big)^{1/2}\,{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}a

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\|h_{j}^{\prime}-\mathsf{T}_{s}h_{j}^{\prime}\|_{2}}{|s|^{3/2}}\,{\rm d}s\leq C\|h_{j}\|_{H^{r}}\Big(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|s|^{2r-4}\,{\rm d}s\Big)^{1/2}.

Consequently, for

and

(B.15)

The desired estimate (

)-(

Lemma B.6

B.3

, we next prove that both mappings are Fréchet differentiable with

The operator

Lemma B.8

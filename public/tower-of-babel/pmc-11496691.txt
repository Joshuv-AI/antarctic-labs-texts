Nonlinear association between alanine aminotransferase to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio and risk of gestational diabetes mellitus
Authors: Zhen An, Tianqi Niu, Yuanyuan Lu, Bin Yao, Feifan Feng, Hui Zhang, Hongbin Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Previous studies have indicated a potential association between the alanine aminotransferase to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (ALT/HDL-C) ratio and the risk of Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus, but its relation with gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) remains uncertain. This study aims to investigate the correlation between the ALT/HDL-C ratio in early pregnancy and the risk of GDM. This study is a secondary analysis based on an open-source cohort study. A total of 590 single pregnant women attending two hospitals in Korea up to 14 weeks gestation were included between November 2014 and July 2016. Logistic regression analysis, subgroup analysis, and smooth curve fitting were employed to explore the association between the ALT/HDL-C ratio and GDM risk. The predictive capability of the ALT/HDL-C ratio for GDM was assessed using ROC curve analysis. The average age of participants was 32.06 ± 3.80 years, with a GDM incidence rate of 6.27%. Multifactorial logistic regression analysis revealed that the serum ALT/HDL-C ratio is an independent influencing factor for GDM (OR = 1.08, 95% CI: 1.02–1.16). Furthermore, a non-linear relationship between the ALT/HDL-C ratio and GDM risk was observed, with a turning point at 5.51. The effect size (OR) on the left and right sides of the turning point were 0.75 (95% CI: 0.37–1.59) and 1.55 (95% CI: 1.18-2.00), respectively. Additionally, when combined with age, pre-pregnancy body mass index, parity, and insulin resistance index in a prediction model for GDM, the ALT/HDL-C ratio demonstrated improved sensitivity of prediction by reaching up to 67.6%, specificity of prediction by reaching up to 87.3%, and an area under curve value of 0.819 (95%CI: 0.743–0.894). In early pregnancy, the serum ALT/HDL-C ratio shows a positive correlation with maternal risk in a nonlinear manner. The combination of ALT/HDL-C ratio with maternal characteristics and metabolic indicators provides good predictive value for GDM. This study may facilitate optimization of GDM prevention in pregnant women and enable timely and effective intervention to enhance their prognosis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-76656-8.

Introduction

Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) refers to varying degrees of glucose intolerance that are initially identified during pregnancy, making it one of the prevalent perinatal complications1,2. The incidence of GDM has been progressively increasing in recent years3,4. According to a 2021 report by the International Diabetes Federation encompassing 51 studies across 41 countries, the prevalence of hyperglycemia among pregnant women aged 20–49 years was found to be 16.7%, with GDM accounting for approximately 70-90% of these cases5. GDM poses diverse risks to pregnant women, including pregnancy-induced hypertension, miscarriage, preeclampsia, premature birth, premature rupture of membranes, and other associated complications. Moreover, it significantly elevates the likelihood of developing Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus in mothers3,6,7. Therefore, comprehending the risk factors linked with GDM is imperative for its prevention and management.

GDM is influenced by various risk factors, including family history, age, dyslipidemia, obesity, and lack of physical activity8–10. Although blood lipid levels during pregnancy have been extensively studied, the results are inconsistent. Some studies suggest that pregnant women with GDM exhibit higher blood lipid levels compared to healthy pregnant women, including the triglycerides to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (TG/HDL-C) ratio and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C/HDL-C) ratio11,12. However, other studies report no significant differences in serum levels of high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) between pregnant women with and without GDM13,14. A recent study found a significant association between the alanine aminotransferase (ALT)/HDL-C ratio and the occurrence of Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus as a biomarker for predicting diabetes risk15. However, there is currently no reported relationship between the ALT/HDL-C ratio and GDM. Therefore, this study conducted a secondary analysis of published data to investigate the potential link between the ALT/HDL-C ratio and the risk of GDM in order to provide novel insights for its prevention and treatment.

Methods

Data source

This study conducted a secondary analysis of data obtained from an open-source cohort study (https://journals.plos.org/plosone). The raw data was accessed freely and analyzed accordingly. The original data were published by Lee SM. et al. in the article “Nonalcoholic fatty liver disease is a risk factor for large-for-gestational-age birth weight”16 which is available under the Creative Commons Attribution License, permitting use, distribution, and reproduction with proper credit given to the authors and source.

Study population

Between November 2014 and July 2016, a prospective cohort study (ClinicalTrials. gov registry number: NCT02276144)16 recruited single pregnant women up to 14 weeks’ gestation from Seoul National University Boramae Medical Center and Incheon Seoul Women’s Hospital. The original researchers obtained approval for the study’s conduct from both the Institutional Review Board at Seoul National University Boramae Medical Center and the Public Institutional Review Board of the Korean Ministry of Health and Welfare16. All participants provided informed consent upon enrollment. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. As this is a secondary analysis, it did not require additional ethical review.

A total of 663 single pregnant women were initially recruited for the original study. 40 participants were excluded from the analysis based on the following exclusion criteria: (1) presence of pre-gestational diabetes, excessive alcohol consumption, or chronic liver disease; (2) loss to follow-up (n = 35); (3) preterm delivery before 34 weeks (n = 5). Consequently, the final number of participants in the original study was reduced to 623. For this secondary analysis, an additional exclusion was made by excluding 20 participants with missing information on TC, HDL-C, or LDL-C and 13 participants with ambiguous information on GDM. As a result, a cohort of 590 women with singleton pregnancies was included in this investigation (Fig. 1). Among them, 39 cases of GDM were initially identified, but after excluding participants with missing TG, HDL-C and LDL-C values (including 2 GDM cases), the final analysis included 37 GDM cases.

ALT/HDL-C ratio

Blood samples were collected in the morning during the gestational period of 10–14 weeks, following an 8-hour fasting period. Subsequently, the samples were promptly centrifuged and stored at -80 °C until analysis. Concentrations of ALT and HDL-C were measured using an automatic biochemical analyzer16. The ALT/HDL-C ratio was then calculated by dividing ALT by HDL-C as a novel variable for this study’s analysis.

Diagnostic criteria for GDM

In accordance with the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists’ guidelines, all participants were screened for GDM using a two-step approach between 24 and 28 weeks of gestation17.The initial screening involved a 50 g oral glucose challenge test (GCT) administered in a non-fasting state, with serum glucose levels measured 1 h post-ingestion. A glucose result of ≥ 7.8 mmol/L was considered a positive result. Women who tested positive in the GCT result were further assessed with a 100 g oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT). GDM was diagnosed if two or more of the following thresholds were exceeded: fasting glucose ≥ 5.3 mmol/L, 1-hour glucose ≥ 10.0 mmol/L, 2-hour glucose ≥ 8.6 mmol/L, and 3-hour glucose ≥ 7.8 mmol/L18. These GDM diagnostic criteria are available in another article by Lee SM et al., the same authors of the original PLOS ONE study19.

Covariates

Covariates were selected based on literature and expert clinical knowledge. The following variables were considered as covariates: (1) categorical variables, including parity and hepatic steatosis; (2) continuous variables, such as age, pre-pregnancy body mass index (BMI), fasting plasma glucose (FPG), insulin, homeostasis model assessment-insulin resistance (HOMA-IR), ALT, adiponectin, aspartate aminotransferase (AST), TC, gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT), LDL-C. Detailed information regarding data collection procedures and variable definitions can be found in the original study.

Treatment of missing data

In this study, the proportion of participants with missing data for pre-pregnancy BMI, FPG, insulin, AST, and ALT was 0.17% (n = 1), 0.17% (n = 1), 0.17% (n = 1), 0.34% (n = 2), and 0.34% (n = 2) respectively. To mitigate potential bias arising from missing variables that could compromise the accurate depiction of statistical power in our target sample during modeling, multiple imputations were employed to handle missing data in this study20,21. Age, pre-pregnancy BMI, hepatic steatosis, AST, GGT, ALT, FPG adiponectin and insulin were included as covariates in the imputation model using linear regression with a total of ten iterations performed. The assumption of missing at random (MAR) was adopted for analyzing the missing data.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables with a normal distribution were summarized as mean ± standard deviation, while skewed variables were reported as medians and interquartile ranges. Categorical variables were presented as percentages and frequencies. The Kruskal-Wallis H test was used to assess differences among ALT/HDL-C groups for skewed distributions, while one-way ANOVA was used for normal distributions. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression models analyzed the relationship between the ALT/HDL-C ratio and GDM. In accordance with the STROBE statement guidelines, the findings from the unadjusted and minimally adjusted analyses, along with the results from the fully adjusted analyses, were concurrently presented. Whether the covariances were adjusted determined by the following principle: when added to this model, changed the matched odds ratio by at least 10%. To ensure the robustness of data analysis, we did a sensitivity analysis. We converted the ALT/HDL-C into a categorical variable, and calculated the P for trend. The purpose was to verify the results of ALT/HDL-C as the continuous variable and to observe the possibility of non-linearity. We also tried to use generalized additive models (GAM) to identify non-linear relationships because ALT/HDL-C was a continuous variable. If a non-linear correlation was observed, a two-piecewise linear regression model was performed to calculate the threshold effect of the ALT/HDL-C on incident of GDM in terms of the smoothing plot. When the ratio between incident of GDM and ALT/HDL-C appeared obvious in a smoothed curve, the recursive method automatically calculates the inflection point, where the maximum model likelihood will be used.To evaluate the diagnostic effectiveness of ALT/HDL-C ratio for GDM screening value receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were utilized to calculate area under the curve (AUC). All statistical analyses were performed using R software package 3.4.3, with P < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics

General information and laboratory examination indicators of the study participants are shown in Table 1. The mean age was 32.06 ± 3.80 years old. A total of 37 cases (6.27%) of GDM occurred. The ALT/HDL-C ratio was divided into three groups based on tertiles (T1: <5.36, T2: 5.36–8.24, T3: ≥8.24). It was observed that participants in the T3 group had higher pre-pregnancy BMI, TG, AST, ALT, GGT, insulin, HOMA-IR, as well as a higher incidence of hepatic steatosis compared to other groups. On the contrary, participants in the T3 group exhibited lower ages, TC, HDL-C and adiponectin levels.

Univariate logistic regression analysis of factors influencing GDM

The results of the univariate logistic regression analysis are shown in Table 2. The univariate analysis revealed significant positive correlations (all P < 0.05) between pre-pregnancy BMI, TG, ALT, GGT, FPG, Insulin, HOMA-IR, and ALT/HDL-C ratio with the occurrence of GDM. Patients with hepatic steatosis exhibited a higher likelihood of developing GDM. Furthermore, age, TC, LDL-C, and AST were not correlated with GDM (all P > 0.05), while HDL-C and adiponectin demonstrated a negative association with the risk of GDM.

Multivariate logistic regression analysis between ALT/HDL-C and GDM

The association between ALT/HDL-C ratio and GDM was assessed using multivariate logistic regression analysis (Table 3). In the unadjusted model, a positive correlation was observed between ALT/HDL-C ratio and the risk of GDM (OR = 1.07, 95%: 1.04–1.11, P = 0.001). After adjusting for age, pre-pregnancy BMI, and parity in Model I (minimally adjusted), each unit increase in ALT/HDL-C ratio led to a 6% increase in the risk of GDM (OR = 1.06, 95%CI: 1.02–1.1, P = 0.002). Furthermore, in Model II (fully adjusted), each unit increase in ALT/HDL-C ratio resulted in an 8% increased risk of GDM (OR = 1.08, 95%CI: 1.02–1.16, P = 0.014). Sensitivity analysis treating ALT/HDL-C as processed as a categorical variable by tertiles revealed that compared to the lowest tertile group (T1), the highest tertile group (T3) had a significantly elevated risk of GDM (OR = 3.86, 95%CI: 1.13–13.19, P = 0.031). These findings suggest consistent trends across groups when considering ALT/HDL-C as either continuous variables.

The analysis of the nonlinear relationship

Considering ALT/HDL-C as a continuous variable, we conducted a nonlinear analysis to examine its association with GDM. After adjusting for age, pre-pregnancy BMI, parity, hepatic steatosis, AST, GGT, TC, LDL-C, HOMA-IR, and adiponectin levels, we observed a significant nonlinear correlation between ALT/HDL-C and GDM (P < 0.001) (shown in Fig. 2). By employing a two-piecewise linear regression model, we determined the turning point of ALT/HDL-C to be 5.51. When ALT/HDL-C was below 5.51, there was a non-significant decrease in the risk of GDM with increasing ALT/HDL-C (OR = 0.75, 95%CI: 0.37–1.59, P = 0.43). Conversely, when the ALT/HDL-C ratio exceeded 5.51, the result showed that elevated ALT/HDL-C might be associated with an increased risk of GDM (OR = 1.55, 95%CI: 1.18-2.00, P < 0.001) (Table 4). These findings suggest that the relationship between ALT/HDL-C and GDM is threshold-limited.

ROC analysis

The predictive ability of the ALT/HDL-C ratio for GDM was assessed using ROC curves. The results, presented in Table 6; Fig. 3, demonstrate that the AUC for ALT/HDL-C alone and when combined with age, pre-pregnancy BMI, parity, and insulin resistance index (Combined prediction model) were 0.702 (95%CI: 0.601–0.803) and 0.819 (95%CI: 0.743–0.894), respectively. Specificity values were found to be 0.817 and 0.873, while sensitivity values were determined as 0.568 and 0.676 for ALT/HDL-C alone and the combined model, respectively, corresponding Youden indices were calculated as 0.385 and 0.549.

Discussion

This study utilized publicly sourced data to investigate the association between the ALT/HDL-C ratio and GDM in a cohort of 590 Korean pregnant women. The findings revealed a positive correlation between the ALT/HDL-C ratio and GDM risk, establishing it as an independent risk factor for pregnant women. Additionally, a non-linear relationship was observed between the ALT/HDL-C ratio and GDM risk. Furthermore, integrating age, pre-pregnancy BMI, parity, and insulin resistance index with the ALT/HDL-C ratio yielded an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.819 (95% CI: 0.743–0.894). Consequently, these results suggest that early screening for GDM using the ALT/HDL-C ratio could serve as a basis for implementing intervention measures.

In recent years, there has been a gradual increase in the incidence rate of GDM. GDM is typically screened and diagnosed between 24 and 28 weeks of pregnancy, leaving limited time for effective intervention or treatment. Therefore, it is imperative to identify biomarkers for GDM and its progression trends prior to diagnosis22–24. Dyslipidemia has emerged as a recognized risk factor for Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM). Growing evidence suggests that low levels of HDL-C and elevated ALT levels are significantly associated with the development of T2DM25–28. Studies have demonstrated that liver enzymes may be linked to glucose homeostasis, particularly changes in liver enzymes during pregnancy can serve as important indicators for diagnosing GDM. ALT, as a representative liver enzyme, has been extensively investigated regarding its association with the risk of GDM29,30. Leng et al.31 reported that elevated ALT levels during early pregnancy, even within the normal range, are associated with an increased risk of GDM. Furthermore, another study demonstrated a positive correlation between fasting glucagon levels and ALT, indicating an interplay between liver function and α-cell activity that impacts blood glucose metabolism, ultimately contributing to the development of GDM32. Additionally, Jia et al.33 discovered a significant positive association between pre-pregnancy serum ALT levels and the risk of GDM. Specifically, for every 10U/L increase in pre-pregnancy serum ALT within the normal range, there was an average 31.7% increase in GDM risk (P < 0.001 for trend test). Similar results were observed during early pregnancy34. In a prospective cohort study conducted in Tianjin, two risk scoring methods were developed to predict GDM occurrence in pregnant women, with ALT identified as a valuable predictor35. In this study, the levels of ALT were significantly elevated in pregnant women with GDM compared to those without GDM. Moreover, each one-unit increase in ALT was associated with a 4% increased risk of developing GDM (OR = 1.04, 95%CI: 1.01–1.06), which is consistent with previous findings.

Moreover, studies have demonstrated a protective effect of moderately elevated HDL-C concentration against GDM, with an inverse relationship between blood HDL-C levels and the risk of GDM36. Furthermore, after adjusting for confounding factors, an independent and significant association was found between increased HDL-C levels and reduced risk of GDM in another study36. However, a recent multicenter retrospective study involving 114,787 participants revealed that elevated levels of HDL-C are linked to an increased risk of diabetes37. The results of this investigation revealed a significant decrease in HDL-C levels among pregnant women with GDM compared to those without GDM. Furthermore, each incremental unit increase in HDL-C was associated with a 3% reduction in the risk of developing GDM (OR = 0.97, 95%CI: 0.94–0.99), indicating a negative correlation between HDL-C levels and the likelihood of GDM.

However, the existing research on the relationship between ALT, HDL-C, and GDM is limited and presents inconsistent findings. The ALT/HDL-C ratio is an indicator that combines ALT and HDL-C levels, with an increased ratio indicating elevated ALT or decreased HDL-C. Previous studies have demonstrated a close association between increased ALT levels and liver insulin resistance, potentially contributing to the development of diabetes38. Conversely, reduced HDL-C levels may negatively impact β-cell function and reduce insulin sensitivity39. Therefore, by influencing insulin resistance, the ALT/HDL-C ratio might play a role in the onset of diabetes. A recent study found a significant association between the ALT/HDL-C ratio and T2DM occurrence, considering it as a biomarker for predicting diabetes risk15. Our study yielded similar results where the ALT/HDL-C ratio emerged as a significant predictor of GDM, suggesting that when combined with maternal characteristics and metabolic indicators, it holds good predictive value for GDM.

The investigation possesses several notable strengths: (1) it is the first study to unveil a significant positive correlation between the ALT/HDL-C ratio and the risk of GDM in pregnant women. (2) Our study offers a ROC curve and identifies an optimal threshold for early prediction of GDM using the ALT/HDL-C ratio. (3) Stratified analyses were conducted to evaluate other potential risk factors that may influence the relationship between the ALT/HDL-C ratio and GDM. (4) To ensure robustness of our conclusions, we performed a series of sensitivity analyses, including categorizing the ALT/HDL-C ratio and incorporating continuity covariates as curves using generalized additive models (GAM).

However, it is important to acknowledge the limitations of this study. (1) Given that the data utilized were obtained from a Korean population, it is crucial to validate our findings in diverse racial groups as the relationship between the ALT/HDL-C ratio and GDM may vary across ethnicities. (2) Since this analysis was conducted as a secondary study, we were unable to account for confounding factors not present in the original dataset such as uric acid levels, family history of diabetes, hypertension, and renal function. (3) It should be noted that this observational investigation solely identified a correlation rather than establishing a causal relationship between the ALT/HDL-C ratio and GDM. (4) Despite adjusting for potential confounders in our analysis, there remains a possibility of unmeasured or uncontrolled confounding factors. (5) The small number of GDM cases (n = 37) limits the statistical power, and larger cohorts are necessary to confirm our findings.

Conclusion

The serum ALT/HDL-C ratio during 10–14 weeks of gestation exhibits a positive correlation with the risk of GDM, and this association demonstrates nonlinearity, reaching a turning point at 5.51. When combined with maternal characteristics and metabolic indicators, the ALT/HDL-C ratio displays promising predictive value for GDM. Monitoring the ALT/HDL-C ratio could help in the early identification of women at risk for GDM, thereby enabling timely interventions to prevent the onset of GDM or mitigate its effects. Given the increasing prevalence of GDM and its significant implications for maternal and fetal health, the results of this study provide valuable insights into potential strategies for GDM risk assessment and management. Further research is needed to explore the mechanisms underlying the observed associations and to evaluate the effectiveness of using the ALT/HDL-C ratio as part of a comprehensive strategy for GDM prevention and care.

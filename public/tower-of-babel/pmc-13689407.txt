Divergent Megrivirus genome detected from wild birds in Brazil
Authors: James Lima Ferreira, Edivaldo Costa Sousa Junior, Jessylene Almeida Ferreira, Poliana da Silva Lemos, Fernando Neto Tavares, Mirleide Cordeiro dos Santos, Marcio Roberto Teixeira Nunes, Rita Catarina Medeiros Sousa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTWe report the near-complete genome of a divergent Megrivirus strain (avian/JF05/BA/BRA) detected in cloacal swabs collected from wild birds in Northeast Brazil. Genome assembly and annotation revealed a 9,680 bp RNA genome with moderate similarities up to 61.8% on the nucleotide level with members of the species Megrivirus aturhepa. The study highlights the importance of viral surveillance in wild birds.

ANNOUNCEMENT

The Picornaviridae family is composed of 158 species grouped in 68 genera (1). One of them, the genus Megrivirus, is divided into five species: aturhepa, berockdo, chigalli, ceharri, and ipengu. The genome is a linear, single-stranded, positive-sense RNA molecule of about 9 kb that encodes a polyprotein (2–4). Megrivirus is frequently identified in poultry and wild birds, constituting a potential threat when spreading to other animals and humans (5, 6).

In this study, we report the near-complete genome of the divergent Megrivirus aturhepa JF05 strain avian/JF05/BA/BRA obtained from a pool of cloacal swabs collected from 10 wild birds of the species Sterna dougallii, collected from July and September 2016 to March and April 2017 in Jandaíra, Bahia, Northeast Brazil.

The nucleic acid was extracted from a pool of samples using the QIAamp DNA Mini Kit (QIAGEN). The acid nucleic concentration, used for sequencing, was constructed using 1 ng/μL (Qubit fluorometer, Thermo Fisher Scientific). A paired-end library (2 × 101) was constructed using the Nextera XT DNA Library Prep Kit (Illumina), and sequencing was performed on the HiSeq 2500 sequencing systems (Illumina). The DNA sequencing generated 11,607,371 reads, which were trimmed using the Trimmomatic software v0.39 (7) and assembled by a de novo strategy using Megahit software v1.2.9 (8), producing 29,790 contigs ranging from 201 to 385,747 bp. These contigs were taxonomically annotated with Kraken2 v2.1.0 (9), and one contig of 9,680 bp was classified as a member of Megrivirus aturhepa A2, presenting 61.8% nucleotide identity to the reference strain LY (KC663628). The genome identified had a coverage of about 115.0×, forming the near-complete genome sequence. The genome alignment and curation were performed with Geneious Prime v2025.2.1 (10). The genome annotation and manual curation were performed using 18 Megrivirus genome references retrieved from ICTV and GenBank (KY369299/KY369300/KC663628/MT138381/MH453806/MH453805/MF405436/KC876003/KC811837/KY488458/HM751199/HQ189775/KF979336/KF979335/KF961188/KJ690629/KF961186/KF961187). The resulting genome of Megrivirus aturhepa strain avian/JF05/BA/BRA possesses a linear genome 9,594 bp in length with a 47.0% GC content, encoding a single polyprotein. The same 18 genomes were also used for the phylogenetic analysis performed with FastTree software v2.1.11 (11). All bioinformatic tools were run with default parameters unless otherwise specified. Compared with all Megrivirus reference genomes from ICTV and GenBank, the polyprotein regions P1, P2, and P3 showed the highest nucleotide identities of 63.7%, 61.6%, and 66.1%, respectively (all values corresponding to the Megrivirus aturhepa species). The phylogenetic analysis shows that all species form well-supported groups, with bootstrap values above 91%. Strain JF05 forms an outgroup of the Megrivirus aturhepa species (Fig. 1). The results suggest that the JF05 strain could be a divergent strain of Megrivirus aturhepa or may represent a new species and demonstrate the importance of monitoring picornaviruses in poultry and wild birds in order to discover new pathogens that can cause problems in agriculture and lead to implications for human health.

Early clinical experience with a novel single-port robotic system for mediastinal tumor resection: A feasibility and safety assessment
Authors: Xueyu Chen, Tong Lu, Yajie Zhang, Hecheng Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo evaluate the feasibility and perioperative safety of anterior mediastinal tumor resection using a flexible single-port robotic system (SR-ENS-600) via a single subxiphoid approach.MethodsThis prospective single-center feasibility study included the first 4 consecutive patients undergoing anterior mediastinal tumor resection with the SR-ENS-600 system. Further evaluation in larger cohorts is warranted.Key Words: single-port robotic surgery, subxiphoid approach, anterior mediastinal tumor

Single-port robotic resection of mediastinal tumors using flexible snake-arm technology.

Central Message

A flexible, single-port robotic resection of anterior mediastinal tumors via a subxiphoid approach is technically feasible and perioperatively safe in early clinical experience.

Perspective

The evolution of robotic thoracic surgery continues to move toward reduced access trauma while preserving operative dexterity. This early clinical experience suggests that flexible single-port robotic technology may expand minimally invasive options for anterior mediastinal tumors through a subxiphoid approach. Larger studies are required to define its comparative effectiveness and long-term outcomes.

Minimally invasive surgery has fundamentally reshaped the management of thoracic diseases over the past 3 decades. Compared with traditional open approaches, video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery has been associated with reduced postoperative pain, decreased blood loss, shorter hospital stay, and faster functional recovery.1 Building upon thoracoscopy, robotic-assisted thoracic surgery has further expanded the technical boundaries of minimally invasive surgery by providing 3-dimensional visualization, tremor filtration, and articulated instruments that enhance precision in confined anatomical spaces.2 In thoracic surgery, robotic platforms are now widely applied in pulmonary resections, esophagectomy, and mediastinal tumor excision, with accumulating evidence supporting their safety and perioperative advantages over open surgery in selected populations.3 As robotic systems continue to evolve, efforts have increasingly focused on reducing access trauma while maintaining operative dexterity.

In parallel with the broader adoption of robotic platforms, single-port strategies have emerged as a natural extension of minimally invasive philosophy. The subxiphoid approach, first introduced in thoracoscopic thymectomy and subsequently refined over the past 2 decades,4, 5, 6 has demonstrated potential benefits in selected anterior mediastinal procedures, including improved bilateral visualization of the phrenic nerves and innominate vein, reduced postoperative pain, and enhanced cosmetic outcomes.7, 8, 9, 10 More recently, robotic single-port systems—such as the da Vinci SP platform—have been applied to mediastinal tumor resection, with early feasibility studies reporting acceptable perioperative outcomes.11 However, most published experiences have used transthoracic or intercostal access, and the application of single-port robotic technology via a subxiphoid approach remains limited.12 Furthermore, conventional single-port platforms typically deploy multiple articulated instruments through a single cannula.13 Despite wristed articulation at the instrument tips, maneuverability may still be constrained within the confined anterior mediastinum when multidirectional dissection around vascular structures is required.

The SR-ENS-600 system (SHURUI) was developed to address these technical constraints through a flexible, curved “snake-arm” architecture designed to enhance maneuverability within restricted intrathoracic spaces. Unlike straight-shaft single-port platforms, this system incorporates flexible robotic arms capable of multiplanar articulation after deployment through a flat-profile port. Theoretically, such a design may mitigate instrument collision and improve access to anatomically complex regions of the anterior mediastinum when using a subxiphoid route. Nevertheless, clinical data regarding flexible single-port robotic systems for mediastinal tumor resection remain scarce. We therefore conducted a prospective single-center feasibility study to evaluate the technical success and perioperative safety of anterior mediastinal tumor resection using the SR-ENS-600 system via a single subxiphoid approach.

Methods

Study Design and Ethical Approval

The institutional review board of Ruijin Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine approved the study protocol and publication of the data (approval no. Ruijin-2025741; approved November 13, 2025). All patients provided written informed consent for the publication of the study data. The present analysis includes the first 4 consecutive patients who met eligibility criteria and completed 30-day postoperative follow-up, representing the earliest clinical implementation phase of the SR-ENS-600 system.

Patient Selection

Patients were considered eligible if preoperative imaging (contrast-enhanced computed tomography and/or magnetic resonance imaging) demonstrated an anterior mediastinal mass suspicious for thymic epithelial tumor and surgical resection was indicated according to institutional standards of care.14 To reduce the risk of including benign cystic lesions with spontaneous regression, these patients had anterior mediastinal lesions that had been confirmed to be present for at least 3 months before surgery. Exclusion criteria included severe cardiopulmonary dysfunction precluding general anesthesia, previous thoracic surgery, ongoing induction therapy, or uncontrolled systemic disease. Only adult patients scheduled for minimally invasive mediastinal tumor resection were included. All enrolled patients completed at least 30 days of postoperative follow-up.

Surgical Technique

All procedures were performed using the SR-ENS-600 single-port robotic system, which consists of a surgeon console, a patient-side robotic platform, a 3-dimensional endoscope, and flexible snake-arm robotic instruments. A single 3.0-cm transverse incision was created inferior to the xiphoid process, consistent with a standard subxiphoid thoracoscopic approach. A flat-profile robotic port was inserted through this incision. The incision was tailored to be slightly larger than the port diameter to allow safe and controlled access into the anterior mediastinum. After port placement, the robotic system was docked, and the flexible snake-arm instruments were deployed within the thoracic cavity. An auxiliary 5-mm trocar was permitted if vascular clip application for larger-caliber vessels was required. Conversion was defined as thoracotomy or placement of ≥2 additional trocars; placement of one auxiliary trocar was allowed according to protocol.

Perioperative Management

All patients were managed under a standardized Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) protocol for minimally invasive mediastinal tumor resection, in accordance with published thoracic ERAS recommendations.15 The protocol included intraoperative normothermia maintenance, restrictive fluid therapy, multimodal analgesia, early oral feeding, early ambulation, and accelerated chest-tube removal.16 Postoperative monitoring and discharge decisions followed institutional ERAS pathways.

Study End Points and Data Collection

The primary end point was successful completion of the planned resection without conversion or major device malfunction. Secondary end points included robotic manipulation time, docking time, total operative time (defined as the interval from skin incision to wound closure), intraoperative safety (including major bleeding, hemodynamic instability, or nerve injury), postoperative complications graded according to the Clavien-Dindo classification, and 30-day readmission. Perioperative data were prospectively collected and analyzed descriptively. Given the exploratory nature and small sample size of this early feasibility experience, no comparative or inferential statistical analyses were performed.

Operative Outcomes

All procedures were successfully completed via a single subxiphoid approach without conversion to thoracotomy or the need for ≥2 additional trocars, yielding a technical success rate of 100% (4/4). No device malfunction occurred during docking or intraoperative manipulation. Robotic manipulation time ranged from 62 to 116 minutes. Total operative time ranged from 89 to 131 minutes. Docking time demonstrated a decreasing trend with increasing familiarity: 6 minutes in the first case and 2 to 3 minutes in subsequent cases, suggesting an early procedural learning curve. Intraoperatively, no major complications were observed. Specifically, there were no episodes of major bleeding, hemodynamic instability, or phrenic nerve injury. Adequate exposure of the anterior mediastinum was achieved in all cases, and multidirectional dissection around the tumor was completed without instrument collision requiring additional access. Postoperative Recovery

All patients recovered uneventfully. No Clavien-Dindo grade III or greater complications occurred within 30 days postoperatively. There were no reoperations, no unplanned readmissions, and no perioperative mortality. All patients were managed according to the institutional ERAS pathway and were discharged following protocol-based criteria. Postoperative recovery was consistent with expectations for minimally invasive mediastinal tumor resection.

Discussion

Minimally invasive strategies for anterior mediastinal tumors have progressively evolved from conventional thoracotomy toward thoracoscopic and robotic approaches.17,18 Subxiphoid access has gained attention because of its potential to provide bilateral mediastinal visualization while minimizing intercostal trauma.19 Robotic assistance further enhances dexterity and visualization compared with conventional video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery, particularly in anatomically confined regions. However, the anterior mediastinum remains a technically demanding operative field.20 The proximity of the innominate vein, phrenic nerves, and pericardium requires precise multidirectional dissection, and conventional single-port instruments may encounter spatial constraints when maneuverability is limited within the confined mediastinal space.21,22

In this context, the flexible snake-arm architecture of the SR-ENS-600 system represents a conceptual attempt to address these mechanical limitations. Unlike straight-shaft single-port platforms, the flexible arms expand after deployment, theoretically enlarging the internal working envelope despite a single access incision. In the present series, multidirectional tumor dissection was achieved without instrument collision necessitating additional ports. Although this study was not designed as a comparative analysis with other robotic systems, the absence of technical conflict within the confined subxiphoid space suggests that flexible articulation may be particularly advantageous in selected anterior mediastinal scenarios.

Single-port robotic resection of mediastinal tumors has been reported using other platforms, including the da Vinci SP system.23, 24, 25 Most previous reports have used transthoracic or intercostal access routes. By contrast, all procedures in the present series were performed via a single subxiphoid incision. The subxiphoid route has been associated with favorable visualization of central mediastinal structures and may reduce postoperative pain related to intercostal nerve irritation. Our findings indicate that the integration of a flexible robotic platform with this access strategy is feasible during early clinical adoption.

An additional observation was the progressive reduction in docking time after the first case.26 Although the sample size is limited, the decline from 6 minutes to 2 to 3 minutes suggests procedural familiarization without prolonged setup inefficiency. Early workflow stabilization is particularly relevant in the adoption phase of new robotic technologies, as prolonged docking or technical interruptions may offset minimally invasive advantages.

Importantly, this study was conducted with cautious patient selection. During the early implementation phase, cases without radiologic evidence of major vascular invasion were preferentially included to ensure procedural safety while the surgical team gained familiarity with the system. Therefore, these results should not be extrapolated to complex invasive mediastinal tumors.27

Several limitations warrant consideration. First, the sample size is small and reflects an initial feasibility experience rather than a definitive assessment of comparative efficacy. Second, follow-up was limited to 30 days, precluding evaluation of long-term oncologic outcomes. Third, the absence of a control group prevents direct comparison with established robotic or thoracoscopic approaches. Larger prospective cohorts, longer follow-up, and multicenter validation will be necessary to determine whether flexible single-port robotic systems confer measurable advantages in operative efficiency, complication profiles, or oncologic completeness.

In conclusion, early clinical implementation of the SR-ENS-600 flexible single-port robotic platform via a subxiphoid approach appears technically feasible and perioperatively safe in selected anterior mediastinal tumors. These preliminary findings support further structured evaluation of flexible single-port robotic technology in thoracic surgery.

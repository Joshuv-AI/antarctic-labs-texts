Effects of Knowledge and Attitude, E-Health Literacy, and Health Self-Efficacy on COVID-19 Vaccine Intention in India
Authors: Anamika Chandra, Devina Sharma, Apurvakumar Pandya, Arpit Datey, Ashu Singh
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:A number of strategies were used to stop the COVID-19 worldwide pandemic. The most promising method for saving people against the pandemic proved to be vaccination.Aims and Objectives:The current study aims to determine and examine the factors influencing the intentions for COVID-19 vaccine uptake in the Indian population.Materials and Methods:Accordingly, a survey was performed to obtain information regarding attitude and intentions on vaccination against COVID-19, knowledge on vaccination against SARS-CoV-2, e-health literacy. Using Smart PLS version 4, structural equation modeling was used to analyze the data.Results and Conclusion:E-health literacy indirectly caused variance in COVID-19 vaccine intentions, whereas knowledge and attitude (β =0.685) and health self-efficacy (β =0.673) directly influenced COVID-19 vaccine intentions.

INTRODUCTION

Many strategies have been used to combat the COVID-19 global pandemic. Vaccination turned out to be a promising strategy to protect people from COVID-19 infection.[1] People’s positive attitudes regarding COVID-19 vaccination have been attributed to adequate knowledge about the vaccine,[2] which has further reinforced people’s intent to get the vaccine.[3] Along with attitude, people’s intentions regarding the COVID-19 vaccination have also been influenced by e-health literacy.[4] Improved e-health literacy was associated with lesser psychological distress[5] and improved preventive behaviors[6] as identified in some of the studies during the pandemic. In this era of the internet, e-health literacy can address the concerns of people while reaching evidence-based transparent information through health institutions and medical organizations.[7] The current study entails the study of factors associated with and influencing the intentions of getting the COVID-19 vaccine in the Indian population that was the e-health literacy and self-efficacy in the context of health behavior, as these factors are also reported to have impacted the COVID-19 protectivebehaviors.[8]

METHODOLOGY

Cross-sectional study design was performed to choose participants in the study that are, mixed population of students, working professionals, and people who were self-employed. Data for the study was collected from November 2022 to July 2023 from the states of Uttar Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh with snowball sampling method, total data of 1246 people were collected. The data were collected on A questionnaire, which was created adopting measures from several studies (Norman and Skinner, 2006a; Zheng et al., 2022a)[910] for vaccine intentions toward COVID-19.

RESULTS

Demographic status of sample population is shown in Table 1. A total of 1246 people responded to the questionnaire. Male respondents made up 55.3% of the sample, whereas female respondents made up 44.7%. 52.2 percent of the respondents were under 35 years old. Most of the respondents had completed graduation (46.3%) or postgraduation (36.8%). Representation of urban population was comparatively higher (66.3%), which indicates that urban communities were primarily where the digital questionnaire was shared. Incidence of COVID-19 infection during the different waves was reported in 74.6% of the responding population [Table 2].

DISCUSSION

In-depth analysis of the influence of certain interrelated factors on COVID-19 vaccine intention affecting the consequent vaccine uptake is analyzed in this article. The impact of people’s knowledge, attitudes, e-health literacy, and health self-efficacy levels on their intention to get the COVID-19 vaccine was taken into consideration when designing the study’s framework. Four hypotheses were proven to be accepted at significant levels, out of the proposed five hypotheses. During the pandemic, those with higher socioeconomic status demonstrated superior e-health literacy and COVID-19 protective behaviors. People’s knowledge and attitudes regarding the COVID-19 vaccination were also impacted by e-health literacy.[411]

People’s intention and uptake of the vaccine have improved as a result of improved knowledge affecting their attitudes regarding the vaccine.[1213] People’s attitudes toward COVID-19 vaccination have changed as a result of increased knowledge about the vaccine from reliable sources.[2] According to a study predicting the attitudes and intentions of Indian citizens toward COVID-19 vaccination, people’s intentions have improved because of increased knowledge.[14]

CONCLUSION

The relevance of e-health literacy would determine the intention and behavior of people during public health crisis. The strategy for vaccine promotion in future should entail transparency in scientific information sharing from authorized Institutions. In addition, methods for boosting the public’s health self-efficacy and e-health literacy ought to be implemented.

Abstract
. A co-valuation is, essentially, a minimal finite cover. We introduce a logic based on co-valuations, which play the role of valuations of free variables in classical first-order logic, and show that the fundamental tools of model theory – such as ultraproducts, compactness, and omitting types – can be developed in this framework. Using a recently discovered duality between certain countable posets and second-countable compact T 1 T_{1} spaces, we prove that these spaces play the role analogous to countable universes in first-order logic. Thus, although no topology appears in the initial formulation, the logic of co-valuations turns out to be naturally suited for studying compact topological objects. Standard topological notions, such as connectedness and covering dimension, are easily expressible, and model-theoretic properties, including atomicity, can be effectively analyzed. The framework also interacts well with Fraïssé-type constructions.



1.
Introduction

A model-theoretic structure is a set equipped with relations and functions. Despite potential complexity, such structures are trivial from a topological perspective: the only topology they are naturally equipped with is the discrete one. To incorporate topology into logic, two main strategies have been considered.

The first apporach is point-free topology, which ignores the points of the underlying space and focuses on algebraic structures derived from the topology, such as collections of open sets with operations like union, intersection, and closure. Early examples include Tarski’s work
[
13
]
, which interprets intuitionistic logic via Heyting algebras of open sets; he also studied algebras of closed sets and, together with MacKinsey
[
11
]
, closure algebras. Later, Henson et al.
[
10
]
investigated lattices of closed sets of topological spaces.

The second one retains the points but introduces an additional sort to capture the topology. Examples include the works of Grzegorczyk
[
8
]
, Gurevich
[
9
]
, and Flum-Ziegler
[
6
]
, where suitable fragments of second-order monadic logic are used. An entirely different direction is to study definable topologies, as proposed by Pillay
[
12
]
.

Both strategies face inherent limitations. Algebraic structures derived from topologies are generally not axiomatizable, and two-sorted structures, where one sort represents the topology, are similarly resistant to axiomatization. As a result, the resulting logics often lack desirable properties: they may fail compactness, the downward Löwenheim-Skolem theorem does not hold, and so on.

One partial remedy is to restrict attention to subfamilies of topologies, such as topological bases. However, this is not a complete solution. A single space may admit multiple bases that are not elementarily equivalent. Expressibility is also limited: if one considers a sort for a basis, the downward Lǒwenheim-Skolem theorem holds, but only yields countable topological spaces. This severely restricts the ability to express fundamental topological properties such as connectedness or dimension – for instance, every countable metrizable space is homeomorphic to a subset of the rationals.

One can also focus on specific classes of spaces or alternative structures derived from a topological space. Bankston
[
1
]
considered regular compact spaces, which are equivalent, via Wallman duality, to disjunctive normal lattices. Nevertheless, the choice of a “right” basis remains problematic, and expressibility continues to depend heavily on the basis. On the other hand, Henson et al.
[
10
]
investigated also the ring
C(X)
of real-valued functions on a topological space
X
. Similarly, Eagle et al.
[
5
]
associate a Hausdorff compact space
X
with its
C^{*}
-algebra
C(X)
, and apply continuous logic. This is a promising research direction, however it seems restricted to spaces without any additional structure: what would be the
C^{*}
-algebra associated with a compact graph?

In this study, we take a different approach. The starting point is the notion of a co-valaution on a set
M
, which plays the role of a valuation in first-order logic – that is, an interpretation of free variables. Specifically, a co-valuation is a relation
v\subseteq X\times M
, where
X
is a finite set (called a context) such that, for
[x]_{v}=\{a\in M:(x,a)\in v\}
, the family
\{[x]_{v}:x\in X\}
is a minimal cover of
M
. A fundamental relation between co-valuations is a consolidation: a covaluation
v\subseteq X\times Y
is a consolidation of
w\subseteq Y\times M
– and
w
is a fragmentation of
v
– if every set
[x]_{v}
is a union of elements of the form
[y]_{w}
. The associated consolidation pattern is the relation
\geq\subseteq X\times Y
, defined by

iff
[x]_{v}\supseteq[y]_{w}
, which describes how the consolidation is carried out. A structure
(M,V)
in the logic of co-valuations is an ordinary first-order structure
M
together with a family

of co-valuations on the universe of
M
in contexts drawn from a fixed set of variables, satisfying certain additional requirements. Co-valuations in
V
are called admissible.

The (relational) language of this logic of co-valuationsis is the same as in first-order logic, except that quantifiers over contexts must explicitly specify their required consolidation pattern. The satisfaction relation is defined as follows. For a structure
(M,V)
, admissible co-valuation

,

if there exist witnesses

such that
R^{M}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})
. On the other hand,
M\models_{v}\exists^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{\geq}\phi
if there exists an admissible

that fragments
v
with pattern
\geq
, and
M\models_{w}\phi
. The connectives
\neg
and
\wedge
are interpreted as in first-order logic.

This logic behaves well with respect to ultraproducts: it satisfies Łoś’s theorem (Theorem
5.3
), and therefore enjoys compactness (Corollary
5.2
). Moreover, with an aid of a duality recently discovered by Bartoš et al.
[
2
]
between certain countable posets, called
\omega
-posets, and second-countable compact
T_{1}
spaces, for every structure, one can construct an elementarily equivalent one whose admissible co-valuations generate a second-countable compact
T_{1}
topology. We call such structures compact
\omega
-structures. Thus, a topologically well-behaved downward Löwneheim-Skolem theorem holds (Theorem
5.3
), even though no topology is present in the original formulation. Furthemore, the omitting types theorem can be proved (Theorem
5.6
), using Baire-category methods in the Polish space

of all compact
\omega
-structures in signature
L
. This leads to a natural notion of atomicity for compact
\omega
-structures (Theorem
5.12
).

One can show that every compact
T_{1}
space equipped with closed relations can be naturally represented as a structure in our logic of co-valuations (Corollary
4.13
). Since admissible co-valuations form only a basis for the underlying topology, not all topological properties are expressible in the logic. Nevertheless, notions such as connectedeness, compactness, perfectness, or covering dimension are expressible; some others, such as metrizability or chainability follow from omitting a suitable family of types (Section 4.4).

The fact that admissible co-valuations give only a basis is a limitation. However, for many classical topological objects there is a canonical choice of admissible co-valuations. Specifically, this occurs for second-countable compact
T_{1}
spaces (possibly with additional relations) that arise as Fraïssé limits in a variant of Fraïssé theory suitable for this setting (Section 6). When admissible co-valuations are obtained via a sufficiently nice (
L
-faithful) Fraïssé sequence in an appropriate category, the resulting structure is an atomic model of its theory (Theorem
6.6
), and any two such structures are isomorphic (Theorem
6.5
). This applies, for example, to the arc, the ordered arc, and the pseudo-arc. Thus, for compact objects arising as Fraïssé limits, there is a canonical way to select the “correct” corresponding structure in the logic of co-valuations.

Finally, we remark that the present study may also be viewed as a contribution to a more abstract theory of valuations in logic. What, in fact, are the common structural features of the ordinary valuation and of the co-valuation introduced in this paper? The interpretation of free variables – arguably one of the most basic semantic operations in logic – has largely been treated as conceptually straightforward and, to the best of our knowledge, has never been examined in its own right. Our results suggest that this operation has a richer mathematical character than is usually assumed. A systematic investigation of the mathematical nature of valuation, and of possible generalizations such as co-valuation, therefore appears to be a promising direction for future research.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 3 provides a brief overview of the Bartoš-Bice-Vignati duality between
\omega
-posets and second- countable compact
T_{1}
spaces. In Section 4, we introduce the logic of co-valuations, define compact structures, and examine several basic topological properties that are expressible in this logic. Section 5 develops the model theory: ultraproducts, Łoś’s theorem, the downward Löwenheim-Skolem theorem, omitting types, and atomic models. Section 6 presents a variant of Fraïssé theory adapted to the logic of co-valuations, and discusses atomicity of Fraïssé limits along with several examples.

Constructing compact spaces from posets

In this section, we present a brief overview of the Bartoš–Bice–Vignati duality connecting certain countable posets and second-countable compact
T_{1}
spaces in the spirit of Stone duality. It has been developed in
[
2
]
, to which the reader is referred for a detailed exposition. Here we present several proofs to illustrate the roles played by the main concepts of this theory.

We begin with some motivation. Let
X
be a second-countable compact
T_{1}
space, and let
\mathbb{P}_{n}
,

such that every
\mathbb{P}_{n}
is refined by
\mathbb{P}_{n+1}

is refined by some
\mathbb{P}_{n}
. In particular,

is a basis of

(see Proposition
3.6
). For
T_{2}

[
2

). Let the disjoint union

be ordered by inclusion.

For every

, let us consider the family

of all those

that contain

as precisely those whose complements

are maximal families in

that do not cover

if and only if none of its subsets covers

– the set

can be described as a minimal family that intersects every cover of

. In the terminology of

4.
A logic of co-valuations

4.1.
Co-valuations, refinements, and consolidations.

In the sequel, a co-bijective relation
v\subseteq X\times Y
is called a
co-valuation
on
Y
in
X
. The family of all co-valuations on
Y
in
X
is denoted by
{\rm{CoV}}(X,Y)
. For

will be also referred to as

.

Remark 4.1
.

Recall that, for
v\in{\rm{CoV}}(X,Y)
, the family
[v]
is a minimal cover of
Y
. As it will usually not cause any ambiguity, we may sometimes identify
[v]
and
X_{v}
.

Let

,
w\in{\rm{CoV}}(Y,M)
, and let
\geq{\rm{CoV}}(X,Y)
. We write
v\geq w
if
[v]
is refined by
[w]
with pattern
\geq
, i.e.,
x\geq y
implies
[x]_{v}\supseteq[y]_{w}
. The relation
\geq^{v}_{w}\subseteq X\times Y
is defined by

x\geq^{v}_{w}y\Leftrightarrow[x]_{v}\supseteq[y]_{w}.

We denote
C_{\geq}(w)=\geq\circ w
, and call
C_{\geq}(w)
a
consolidation
of
w
with pattern
\geq
, and

a
fragmentation
of
C_{\geq}(w)
. Note that a co-valuation

on
M
is a consolidation of a co-valuation

on
M
iff the cover
[v]
of
M
is a consolidation of the cover
[w]
.

A sequence
(v_{i})
,
v_{i}\in{\rm{CoV}}(X_{i},M)
, of co-valuations is called an
\omega
-chain if each
v_{i}
consolidates
v_{i+1}
, i.e.,
([v_{i}],\supseteq\upharpoonright[v_{i}]\times[v_{i+1}])
is an

-chain as a poset.

Remark 4.2
.

Every minimal finite cover
\mathbb{P}
of a set
M
gives rise to a co-valuation

, where
(p,a)\in v
iff
a\in p
. Similarly, a sequence
\mathbb{P}_{i}
,

, of minimal finite covers of

such that each
\mathbb{P}_{i}
consolidates
\mathbb{P}_{i+1}
, gives rise to an
\omega
-chain of co-valuations.

4.2.
Structures and interpretations.

Let us select a countably infinite set of variables with a special variable
*
. A
context
is a finite set of variables. In practice, we may regard any finite set as a context. Formulas
\phi(X)
in context
X
are generated by the grammar

\phi(X)::=\bot\mid R\mid\neg\phi(X)\mid\phi(X)\land\psi(X)\mid\exists^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{\geq}\phi(Y),

where
\geq\in{\rm{CoV}}(X,Y)
, and
R
is a relation symbol of arity
a(R)
with variables contained in
X
.
We write
\top
for
\neg\bot
,
\phi\vee\psi
for
\neg(\neg\phi\wedge\neg\psi)
,
\phi\rightarrow\psi
for
\psi\vee\neg\phi
,
\forall^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{\geq}\phi
for
\neg\exists^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{\geq}\neg\phi
, and
\exists^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{X\geq Y}\phi
to explicitly indicate the domain and co-domain of
\geq
. Note that
\exists^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{X\geq Y}\phi
has a unique context
X
(as opposed to, e.g., atomic formulas that can have multiple contexts). A type (
n
-type)
p(X)
is a set of formulas in a fixed signature and context
X
(of size
n
). A type
p(X)
is complete if for every formula
\phi(X)
either
\phi\in p(X)
or
\neg\phi\in p(X)
. A sentence is a formula in the context
\bar{*}=\{*\}
, and a theory is a type consisting only of sentences.

We will also use the following conventions. If
\geq\in{\rm{CoV}}(\bar{*},X)
, we write
\exists^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{X}
instead of
\exists^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{\geq}
or
\exists^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{\bar{*}\geq X}
. Also, we omit
X
in
\exists^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{X\geq Y}
if it can be unequivocally read off the formula. For example, we write
\forall^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{X}\exists^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{\geq Y}\phi
instead of
\forall^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{\succeq}\exists^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{\geq}\phi
, where
\succeq\in{\rm{CoV}}(\bar{*},X)
,
\geq\in{\rm{CoV}}(X,Y)
.

Let
X
,
M
be sets, and let
V
a family of co-valuations on
M
. We write
V^{X}
for

,
[\bar{x}]_{v}
for
\prod_{k}[x_{k}]_{v}
, where
\bar{x}=(x_{k})
,
x_{k}\in X
. Also, for
v\in{\rm{CoV}}(X,M)
,

,

if

, for all relevant

.

By a

in signature
L
, we mean a triple
M=(M,\{R^{M}_{\alpha}\},V)
consisting of

(1)

a first-order logic structure
(M,\{R^{M}_{\alpha}\})
in a relational signature
L=\{R_{\alpha}\}
,

(2)

a family

of co-valuations on

in contexts that is closed under consolidations by contexts.

Co-valuations in

are sometimes referred to as

co-valuations. To simplify notation, if it does not cause ambiguity, we may also refer to a proto-structure

by

or by

.

For proto-structures
(M,V)
,

in the same signature, a bijection

is an

if it preserves relations, and valuations, i.e., for

, where

.

Given a proto-structure

, a co-valuation

is defined by the recursion scheme, where

M\nvDash_{v}\bot,

M\vDash_{v}R(\bar{x})\iff[\bar{x}]_{v}\cap R^{M}\neq\emptyset,

M\vDash_{v}\phi\wedge\psi\iff M\vDash_{v}\phi\mbox{ and }M\vDash_{v}\psi,

M\vDash_{v}\exists^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{\geq}\phi\iff\exists_{w\in V}(v=C_{\geq}(w)\mbox{ and }M\vDash_{w}\phi).

The type

of a co-valuation

is the set of all formulas

in context

such that

for every

is omitted by

.

.

Let

be a proto-structure in signature

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
L
be a signature, let
X
,
Y
be contexts, let
\geq\in{\rm{CoV}}(X,Y)
, and let
\phi(X)
be a formula. There is a formula
\psi(Y)
such that

M\vDash_{C_{\geq}(v)}\phi\iff M\vDash_{v}\psi

for every structure
(M,V)
in signature
L
, and
v\in V^{X}
.

Proof.

First observe that for every structure
(M,V)
in signature
L
, and

M\models_{C_{\geq}(v)}R(\bar{x})\iff M\models_{v}\bigvee\{R(\bar{y}):\bar{x}\geq\bar{y}\},

so the statement of the theorem holds for atomic formulas. The rest of the proof goes by induction on the complexity of formulas. For the connectives
\neg
and
\wedge
, the inductive step is straightforward.
Fix a formula of the form
\exists^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{\succeq}\phi
, where
\succeq\in{\rm{CoV}}(X,W)
. Fix a structure
(M,V)
, and
v\in V^{Y}
. If
M\vDash_{C_{\geq}(v)}\exists^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{\succeq}\phi
, then, by Axiom (I), there is

,
\geq_{0}\in{\rm{CoV}}(Y,Z)
, and
\succeq_{0}\in{\rm{CoV}}(W,Z)
such that

, and
C_{\geq_{0}}(w)=v
. Moreover, by Proposition
4.5
, we have

\succeq\circ\succeq_{0}=\geq\circ\geq_{0},

which means that
C_{\succeq_{0}}(w)
fragments
C_{\geq\circ\geq_{0}}(w)=C_{\geq}(v)
with pattern
\succeq
.

Now, we observe that, by Remark
4.6
and Proposition
4.7
, we can choose

of size that is commonly bounded for every
(M,V)
. This means that there are only finitely many ways of choosing
\geq_{0}
,
\succeq_{0}
as above. Let
\geq_{i}
,
\succeq_{i}
,
i\leq n
, enumerate all of them, and let
\psi_{i}
be formulas given by the inductive assumption for
\phi
,
w
, and
\succeq_{i}
,
i\leq n
.
Then the formula

\psi=\bigvee_{i\leq n}\exists^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{\geq_{i}}\psi_{i}

is as required.
∎

Let us introduce a new quantifier
\exists^{\scriptstyle{r}}_{\geq}
that involves the refining relation rather than the fragmentation relation:

M\vDash_{v}\exists^{\scriptstyle{r}}_{\geq}\phi\iff\exists_{w\in V}[v\geq w\mbox{ and }M\vDash_{w}\phi].

5.1.
Łoś’s theorem

Let
D
be an ultrafiler on a set
I
, and let
(M_{i},V_{i})
,

, be proto-structures in signature

\prod_{D}v_{i}\in{\rm{CoV}}(X,\prod_{D}M_{i})
of co-valuations

in

(x,(a_{i}))\in\prod_{D}v_{i}\iff(x,a_{i})\in v_{i}\text{ for $D$-many $i$}.

We denote the family of all ultraproducts of admissible co-valuations by
\prod_{D}V_{i}
. It is immediate to verify that

(a_{i})\in[x]\pi_{\prod_{D}v_{i}}\iff a_{i}\in[x]\pi_{v_{i}}\text{ for $D$-many $i$},

\prod_{D}v_{i}\geq\prod_{D}w_{i}\iff v_{i}\geq w_{i}\text{ for $D$-many $i$},

C_{\geq}(\prod_{D}v_{i})=\prod_{D}C_{\geq}(v_{i}).

In particular,
(\prod_{D}M_{i},\prod_{D}V_{i})
is a proto-structure. By Proposition
4.7
, if for proto-structures
M_{i}
, admissible co-valuations satisfy Axiom (I), then admissible co-valuations satisfy Axiom (I) for
(\prod_{D}M_{i},\prod_{D}V_{i})
. By the above observations, if
w_{i}
,
\bar{a}_{i}
are witnesses for
R
,
v_{i}
,
\bar{x}
, then
\prod_{D}w_{i}
,
\prod_{D}\bar{a}_{i}
are witnesses for
R
,
\prod_{D}v_{i}
,
\bar{x}
. Hence,
(\prod_{D}M_{i},\prod_{D}V_{i})
is a structure, i.e., the class of structures is closed under ultraproducts, and the following holds:

Theorem 5.1

(Łoś’s theorem)
.

Let
D
be an ultrafilter on a set
I
,
let
(M_{i},V_{i})
,
i\in I
, be a family of structures in signature
L
, and let

v_{i}\in V_{i}
be co-valuations in context
X
,
i\in I
.
For every formula
\phi(X)
,

\prod_{D}M_{i}\models_{\prod_{D}v_{i}}\phi\iff M_{i}\models_{v_{i}}\phi\text{ for $D$-many $i$ }.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on the complexity of formulas.
The only non-trivial case is
\exists^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{\geq}\phi
.
To see the direction from left to right,
take some admissible co-valuation, which is of the form
\prod_{D}w_{i}
,
such that
M\models_{\prod_{D}w_{i}}\phi
,
and
\prod_{D}v_{i}
consolidates
\prod_{D}w_{i}
with pattern
\geq
.
Since
v_{i}
consolidates
w_{i}
with pattwern
\geq
, for
D
-many
i
, by the induction hypothesis,
for
D
-many
i
, we have
M_{i}\models_{w_{i}}\phi
.
Then we have
M_{i}\models_{v_{i}}\exists^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{\geq}\phi
for
D
-many
i
.
To see the other direction,
suppose
M_{i}\models_{v_{i}}\exists^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{\geq}\phi
for
D
-many
i
.
For those
i
, take
w_{i}
such that
v_{i}
consolidates
w_{i}
with pattern
\geq
and
M_{i}\models_{w_{i}}\phi
,
and let
w_{i}
be an arbitrary admissible co-valuation of
M_{i}
in
X
otherwise.
Then
\prod_{D}v_{i}
consolidates
\prod_{D}w_{i}
with pattern
\geq
, and
\prod_{D}M_{i}\models_{\prod_{D}w_{i}}\phi
by the induction hypothesis,
so we obtain
\prod_{D}M_{i}\models_{\prod_{D}w_{i}}\exists^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{\geq}\phi
.
∎

Corollary 5.2

Theorem 5.3

Proof.

5.4.
Omitting types, isolated types, atomic models.

For a theory

in a countable signature

,

, and a fixed context

is the set of all complete types

realized by models of

. We endow

with the standard logic topology, given by open neighborhoods

where
\phi
is a formula. Clearly,

is zero-dimensional, and, by Corollary

, it is compact.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let

be a theory in a countable signature. Assume that, for some

,

is meager (in the logic topology). Then, in the logic topology on
\rm{Mod}(T)
, there are comeagerly many

such that

omits every type in

.

Proof.

For

, and
\succeq\in{\rm{CoV}}(X,X_{i})
, let

be the mapping defined by
f_{\succeq}(\mu)=\rm{tp}_{M_{\mu}}(C_{\succeq}(v_{i}))
. We clearly have

f_{\succeq}([i,\psi])\subseteq[\phi],

for every neighborhood
[\phi]
, where
\psi
is given by Theorem
4.8
for
X_{i}
,
X
,
\succeq
and
\phi
. In other words,
f_{\succeq}
is continuous in the logic topology on
\rm{Mod}(T)
.
Moreover, an inspection of the proof of Theorem
5.3
gives that

f_{\succeq}([j,\geq]\cap[j,\phi])\supseteq[\exists^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}_{\geq\circ\succeq}\phi],

for every
j\geq i
. Thus,
f_{\succeq}
is category-preserving, and so
E=\bigcup_{\succeq}f^{-1}_{\succeq}[F]
is meager. Obviously,
M_{\mu}
omits every type in

, for
\mu\in\rm{Mod}(T)\setminus E
.
∎

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
T
be a theory in a countable signature, and let

. The following are equivalent:

(1)

p
is isolated (in the logic topology),

(2)

p
is realized in every model of

,

(3)

there exist

and

such that
\{\mu\in\rm{Mod}(T):M_{\mu}\models_{C_{\geq}(v_{i})}p\}
has non-empty interior.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) is obvious, and (2)
\Rightarrow
(1), (3)
\Rightarrow
(1) follow from Theorem
5.6
. To show that (2)
\Rightarrow

consisting of all
\mu\in\rm{Mod}(T)
that realize

is an

subset of
\rm{Mod}(T)
. Indeed,

, where

is the set of

such that

realizes

. Therefore, if

, there must exist

and

such that

is non-meager. As this set is closed, it must have non-empty interior.
∎

As usual, we say that a type

of a theory
T

isolated
if there is a formula

such that

implies

,

. By Theorem

,

.

Corollary 5.8

(Omitting types)
.

Let

be a theory in a countable signature, and let

be a non-isolated

. Then the subset of

of all

, is meager in the logic topology. In particular, every countable family

of non-isolated types of

.

Clearly, the subset

By Theorem

6.
Fraïssé limits.

Let us briefly present an extension of Fraïssé theory developed in Sections 3 and 4.5 of
[
3
]
. The reader is referred to this paper for more details on the original treatment.

For sets
A
,
B
, and a relation
R
of arity
m
defined both on
A
, and on
B
, we say that a relation
\sqsupset\subseteq A\times B
is
R
-preserving
if, for every
\bar{b}\in B^{m}
, we have that
R(\bar{b})
and
\bar{a}\sqsupset\bar{b}
implies
R(\bar{a})
. It is
R
-surjective
if, for every
\bar{a}\in A^{m}
,
R(\bar{a})
implies that there is
\bar{b}\in B^{m}
such that
R(\bar{b})
, and
\bar{a}\sqsupset\bar{b}
. For graphs
(G,\sqcap)
,
(H,\sqcap)
, we say that
\sqsupset\subseteq G\times H
is
edge-witnessing
if, for
g,g^{\prime}\in G
,
g\sqcap g^{\prime}
implies that there is
h\in H
with
g,g^{\prime}\sqsupset h
.

Let
L
be a countable relational signature. Let
\mathbf{S}_{L}
be the class of all finite structures in
L\cup\{\sqcap\}
, where
\sqcap
is a binary relation symbol, such that
\sqcap^{A}
is a reflexive graph, for every
A\in\mathbf{S}_{L}
. We regard
\mathbf{S}_{L}
as a category with morphisms
\sqsupset
from
B
to
A
,
A,B\in\mathbf{S}_{L}
, defined as all relations
\sqsupset\subseteq A\times B
that are
R
-preserving,
R\in L\cup\{\sqcap\}
, and are co-bijective; composition of morphisms in
\mathbf{S}_{L}
is composition of relations. In this section, we will only consider subcategories of
\mathbf{K}\subseteq\mathbf{S}_{L}
that are closed under isomorphism, and such that edge-witnessing morphisms are
wide
, i.e., for every
A\in\mathbf{K}
, there is
B\in\mathbf{K}
, and an edge-witnessing morphism from
B
to
A
.

Remark 6.1
.

The role of the graph relation
\sqcap
is to control possible
\omega
-posets induced by sequences in subcategories of
\mathbf{S}_{L}
, as discussed in
[
3
]
. In particular, the category
\mathbf{S}_{\emptyset}
is the category
\mathbf{B}
described in Section 3 of

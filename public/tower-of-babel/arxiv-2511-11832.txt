Sums of Laurent series with bounded partial quotients
Dmitry Gayfulin, Erez Nesharim

Abstract
Hall [ 4 ] proved that every real number is the sum of an integer and two real numbers whose partial quotients are at most 4 4 . Cusick [ 2 ] proved that every real number is the sum of an integer and two real numbers whose partial quotients are at least 2 2 . In a recent paper, the authors proved that every real number is the sum of two real numbers whose partial quotients diverge. In this paper, we prove an analogue of these results for Laurent series.

Basic properties of continued fractions of Laurent series

In this section, we recall some basic facts about continued fractions of Laurent series that we use in the article. For a more detailed background, we refer to
[
6

For
\alpha
as in (
8
), let its

and

be

\deg\alpha:=\sup\left\{-n\,:\,\alpha_{n}\neq 0\right\}

and

\left|\alpha\right|=2^{\deg\alpha}\,,

respectively (where
\deg 0=-\infty
and
\left|0\right|=0
).
It is well known that with this absolute value
\mathbb{K}(t)
is embedded in
\mathbb{K}\!\left(\!\left(t^{-1}\right)\!\right)
as the subfield of all Laurent series with an eventually periodic sequence of coefficients.

Every
\alpha\in\mathbb{K}\!\left(\!\left(t^{-1}\right)\!\right)
has a unique continued fraction expansion

\alpha=a_{0}+\cfrac{1}{a_{1}+\cfrac{1}{a_{2}+\cdots\vphantom{\cfrac{1}{1}}}}=:[a_{0};a_{1},a_{2},\dots]\,,

where
a_{n}\in\mathbb{K}[t]
for every
n\geq 0
, and
d_{n}:=\deg(a_{n})
satisfies
d_{n}\geq 1
for every
n\geq 1
. This expansion is unique for every
\alpha
, and it is finite if and only if
\alpha
is a rational function. The convergents of a continued fraction are the rational functions

\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}:=[a_{0};a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}]

whose numerators and denominators are defined by the recurrence relations

\displaystyle p_{0}

\displaystyle=a_{0}\,,\quad

\displaystyle p_{1}

\displaystyle=a_{1}a_{0}+1\,,\quad

\displaystyle p_{n}

\displaystyle=a_{n}p_{n-1}+p_{n-2}\,,\quad\textrm{and}

\displaystyle q_{0}

\displaystyle=1\,,\quad

\displaystyle q_{1}

\displaystyle=a_{1}\,,\quad

\displaystyle q_{n}

\displaystyle=a_{n}q_{n-1}+q_{n-2}\,.

For any
n\geq 0
we consider
a_{n}(\cdot)
,
d_{n}(\cdot)
,
p_{n}(\cdot)
and
q_{n}(\cdot)
as functions that return the
n
th partial quotient of the continued fraction expansion and its degree, the numerator and denominator of its
n
th convergent, respectively. If
\alpha
is rational, then
n
should not exceed the length of the continued fraction expansion of
\alpha
; otherwise, these functions are undefined.

The following properties are well known:

1.

The degrees of partial quotients and denominators are related by

\deg(q_{n})=\deg(q_{n-1})+d_{n}\,

(12)

whenever
a_{n}
is defined.

2.

For every
n\geq 0
, if
a_{n+1}
is defined then

\left|\alpha-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|=\frac{1}{|q_{n}|\,|q_{n+1}|}\,.

(13)

3.

Let
p/q\in\mathbb{K}(t)
. Then

\left|\alpha-\frac{p}{q}\right|<\frac{1}{|q|^{2}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\frac{p}{q}=\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\,\textrm{ for some $n\geq 0$}\,.

(14)

4.

For every
h\geq 1
, let
D_{h}
be the polynomial with
2h-1
variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{2h-1}
which is given by the determinant

D_{h}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2h-1}):=\begin{vmatrix}x_{1}&x_{2}&\cdots&x_{h}\\
x_{2}&\iddots&\iddots&x_{h+1}\\
\vdots&\iddots&\iddots&\vdots\\
x_{h}&x_{h+1}&\cdots&x_{2h-1}\end{vmatrix},

(15)

and let

\Delta_{h}=\Delta_{h}(\alpha):=D_{h}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{2h-1})\,.

Then

\Delta_{h}\neq 0\quad\iff\quad h=\deg(q_{n})\,\textrm{ for some $n\geq 1$}\,.

(16)

It will also be convenient to let

be the constant polynomial

Note that (

\alpha\in\mathcal{F}(k)\quad\iff\quad\begin{aligned} &\textrm{for every $h\geq k$, if $\Delta_{h-i}=0$ for every $1\leq i<k$ then $\Delta_{h}\neq 0$}\,.\end{aligned}

(17)

An analogue of Hall’s theorem

The proof of Theorem
1
is based on analysing the determinant (
15
):

Lemma 5
.

For every
h\geq 1
there exists a degree
h+1
homogeneous polynomial of
2h
variables
P_{h}
such that

D_{h+1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2h+1})=x_{2h+1}D_{h}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2h-1})+P_{h}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2h}).

(18)

Moreover, if
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{F}_{2}
then there exists a degree
h+1
homogeneous polynomial
Q_{h}
of
2h-1
variables such that

P_{h}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2h})=x_{2h}^{2}D_{h-1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2h-3})+Q_{h}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2h-1}).

(19)

Proof.

The proof follows by inspection of the determinant

D_{h+1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2h+1})=\begin{vmatrix}x_{1}&x_{2}&\cdots&x_{h}&x_{h+1}\\
x_{2}&\iddots&\iddots&x_{h+1}&x_{h+2}\\
\vdots&\iddots&\iddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{h}&x_{h+1}&\cdots&x_{2h-1}&x_{2h}\\
x_{h+1}&x_{h+2}&\cdots&x_{2h}&x_{2h+1}\\
\end{vmatrix}.

(20)

Indeed, note that
x_{2h+1}
appears in (
20
) only once, in the lower-right corner. So, expanding
D_{h+1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2h+1})
by, for example, the last row, gives (
18
). To see (
19
), it is useful to think of
D_{h+1}
as a sum of
(h+1)!
monomials of degree
h+1
, where the summands are in bijection with permutations of
h+1
elements. Note that
x_{2h}
appears in (
20
) exactly twice, in the one-before-last entry in the last row and column. Therefore, every permutation that contains the term
x_{2h}
exactly once, can be paired with the permutation that arises by taking its entries reflected along the main diagonal. Since (
20
) is invariant under reflection along the main diagonal, the monomials attached to these two permutations are equal. Since
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{F}_{2}
, they cancel each other and the polynomial
D_{h+1}
has no monomials which are divisible by
x_{2h}
but not by
x_{2h}^{2}
.
∎

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{K}\!\left(\!\left(t^{-1}\right)\!\right)
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
a_{0}(\alpha)=0
.
We start with a proof of the first statement of Theorem

In view of (
17

\displaystyle D_{n}(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{2n-1})\neq 0\,.

(23)

Indeed, then

.

If

, and

. Now

We now turn to the case

In view of (

(25)

For
n=1
, choose

\beta_{1}=\alpha_{1}+1

Now suppose that
n\geq 1
and that
\beta_{i}
and

are defined for every

and satisfy (

) and (

). If

then choose

\beta_{2n}\neq\frac{Q_{n}(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{2n-1})}{D_{n-1}(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{2n-3})}

and put

\gamma_{2n+1}\neq\frac{P_{n}(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{2n})}{D_{n}(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{2n-1})}

(28)

and
\beta_{2n+1}=\alpha_{2n+1}+\gamma_{2n+1}

4
An analogue of Shulga’s algorithm

In
[
5
]
Shulga established a nice and simple algorithm that decomposes real numbers into a sum of two real numbers with growing partial quotients. Theorems
2
and
4
are immediate corollaries of an analogue of this algorithm for Laurent series and its properties, which are summarised in Theorem
9
. We first recall the real version of this algorithm, and some of its properties.

Definition 6

(Shulga)
.

For every
\alpha\in[0,1]
, define sequences
\{b_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and
\{c_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
, and real numbers
\beta
and
\gamma
as follows:

1.

For every
n\geq 0
, having the integers
b_{j}
and
c_{j}
defined for every
1\leq j\leq n
, if

\alpha=[0;b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}]+[0;c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}]\,,

let

\beta:=[0;b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}]\,,\quad\textrm{and}\quad\gamma:=[0;c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}]\,,

and stop, otherwise, let

\displaystyle b_{n+1}:=a_{n+1}(\alpha-[0;c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}])+1\,,\quad\textrm{and}\quad c_{n+1}:=a_{n+1}(\alpha-[0;b_{1},\ldots,b_{n+1}])\,.

2.

If the algorithm does not stop after finitely many steps, let

\beta:=[0;b_{1},b_{2},\ldots]\,,\quad\textrm{and}\quad\gamma:=[0;c_{1},c_{2},\ldots]\,.

The properties of the sequences
\{b_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and
\{c_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
provided by Definition
6
were studied in

[
5
]
and

[
3
]
, and the main results are summarised in the following statement:

Theorem 7

(
[
5
]
and
[
3
]
)
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,1]
. Then:

1.

The numbers
\beta
and
\gamma
in Definition
6
are well defined and satisfy

\alpha=\beta+\gamma\,.

Moreover, for every
n\geq 1
, if
b_{n}
and
c_{n}
of Definition
6
are defined, then

\displaystyle b_{i}=a_{i}(\alpha-[0;c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}])\,\quad\textrm{and}\quad c_{i}=a_{i}(\alpha-[0;b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}])\,\quad\textrm{for every $1\leq i\leq n$}\,.

2.

If
b_{1}
and
c_{1}
are defined, then

c_{1}\geq b_{1}(b_{1}-1)>1\,,

for every
n\geq 2
, if
b_{n}
and
c_{n}
are defined, then

c_{n}\geq b_{n}+1\,,

and for every

, if
b_{n}
,

and

are defined, then

\displaystyle b_{n}

\displaystyle\geq n\,,

\displaystyle b_{n+1}

\displaystyle\geq b_{n}\,,

\displaystyle\geq b_{n}+1\,,

respectively.

If
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
then
\beta
and
\gamma
are also rational, i.e., the algorithm in Definition
6
stops after finitely many steps.

Proof.

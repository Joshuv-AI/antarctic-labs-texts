Characterizations of Dual of K K -frames in Quaternionic Hilbert Spaces
Chander Shekhar

Abstract
. Dual of K − K- frames in a right quaternionic Hilbert space has been recently introduced and studied by Ellouz [ 1 ] . In this paper, we study duals of K − K- frames and prove a characterization of a K − K- dual in terms of the canonical K − K- dual of a K − K- frame in the case when the range of K K is not necessarily closed. Moreover, we defined and studied the Approximate K − K- Dual of a K − K- frame in a right quaternionic Hilbert space and proved various results for their existence.

Frames in Hilbert spaces were first introduced by J. Duffin and A.C. Schaeffer in 1952 in the context of nonharmonic Fourier series

2.
Quaternionic Hilbert Space

The space of quaternions is given by

\mathfrak{H}=\{q_{1}+q_{2}i+q_{3}j+q_{4}k:q_{!},q_{2},q_{3},q_{4}\in\mathbb{R}\}

where
i^{2}=j^{2}=k^{2}=-1;ij=-ji=-k;jk=-kj=i;\&ki=-ik=j.
Note that
\mathfrak{H}
is a four-dimensional algebra with unity.

For
q\in\mathfrak{H}
we have the following:

•

Conjugate:

\bar{q}=q_{1}-q_{2}i-q_{3}j-q_{4}k

•

Modulus:

|q|^{2}=q_{1}^{2}+q_{2}^{2}+q_{3}^{2}+q_{4}^{2}

•

Real and Imaginary parts:

Re(q)=q_{1},\hskip 28.45274ptIm(q)=q_{2}i+q_{3}j+q_{4}k

•

Inverse:

q^{-1}=\frac{q_{1}-q_{2}i-q_{3}j-q_{4}k}{q_{1}^{2}+q_{2}^{2}+q_{3}^{2}+q_{4}^{2}}

Due to the non-commutative nature of quaternions, there exist two types of quaternionic Hilbert spaces: the left quaternionic Hilbert space and the right quaternionic Hilbert space, distinguished by the placement of quaternions.

In this section, we introduce some fundamental notations related to the algebra of quaternions, right quaternionic Hilbert spaces, and operators on these spaces.

3.
K-

Throughout this paper,
\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
is a seperable right quaternionic Hilbert space,

Definition 3.1
.

Let

be a
K-

\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
then a Bessel sequence
\{g_{k}\}_{k\in I}

\displaystyle Kx=\sum_{k\in I}f_{k}\langle x,g_{k}\rangle,\quad x\in\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})

(3.2)

Remark 3.2
.

[
1
]

If

be a
K-

\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
then there exists a Bessel sequence

) and

\displaystyle K^{*}x=\sum_{k\in I}g_{k}\langle x,f_{k}\rangle,\quad x\in\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})

(3.3)

Lemma 3.3
.

[
1
]

Let
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in I}
and
\{g_{k}\}_{k\in I}
be two Bessel sequences for the right quaternionic Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
satisfying (

). Then
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in I}
and
\{g_{k}\}_{k\in I}
are
K
and
K^{*}-

Proof.

For
x\in\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
, we have

\displaystyle||Kx||^{4}

\displaystyle=|\langle Kx,Kx\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigg|\biggl<\sum_{k\in I}f_{k}\langle x,g_{k}\rangle,Kx\biggr>\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in I}|\langle Kx,f_{k}\rangle|^{2}\sum_{k\in I}|\langle x,g_{k}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq B||Kx||^{2}\sum_{k\in I}|\langle x,g_{k}\rangle|^{2}

where
B
is an upper bound of the bessel sequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in I}
.
Hence

\displaystyle B^{-1}||Kx||^{2}\leq\sum_{k\in I}|\langle x,g_{k}\rangle|^{2}

This shows that
\{g_{k}\}_{k\in I}
is a
K^{*}-
frame with lower bound
B^{-1}
. In the same manner, we can obtain the lower bound of
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in I}
by repeating the above argument for
K^{*}
instead of
K
.
∎

Example 3.4
.

Let
\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})=\mathfrak{H}^{4}

Now, we compute

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
be a right quaternionic Hilbert space and

be a bounded right linear operator on
\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})

\{f_{k}\}_{k\in I}

\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})

We know that

.
Also

. Therefore
\{K^{*}S^{-1}P_{S(R(K))}f_{k}\}_{k\in I}
is a Bessel sequence in
\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
. Also, we have

\displaystyle Kx=(S^{-1}S)^{*}Kx

\displaystyle=S^{*}(S^{-1})^{*}Kx

\displaystyle=S^{*}P_{S(R(K))}(S^{-1})^{*}Kx

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in I}P_{R(K)}f_{k}\langle P_{S(R(K))}(S^{-1})^{*}Kx,f_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in I}P_{R(K)}f_{k}\langle x,K^{*}S^{-1}P_{S(R(K))}f_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle S^{-1}(S^{-1})^{*}x,x\rangle

\displaystyle\leq A^{-1}||K^{\dagger}||^{2}||(S^{-1})^{*}x||||x||

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in I}|\langle x,K^{*}S^{-1}P_{S(R(K))}f_{k}\rangle|^{2}

. Also

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in I}P_{(R(K)}f_{k}\langle x,K^{*}S_{F}^{-1}P_{S_{F}(R(K))}f_{k}+M^{*}e_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in I}P_{(R(K)}f_{k}\langle x,K^{*}S_{F}^{-1}P_{S_{F}(R(K))}f_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\sum_{k\in I}P_{(R(K)}f_{k}\langle x,M^{*}e_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=Kx+P_{R(K)}T_{F}Mx

\displaystyle=Kx

Hence,
\{g_{k}\}_{k\in I}
=
\{K^{*}S_{F}^{-1}P_{S_{F}(R(K))}f_{k}+M^{*}e_{k}\}_{k\in I}
is a
K-
dual of
\{P_{R(K)}f_{k}\}_{k\in I}
. Conversely, let
\{g_{k}\}_{k\in I}
be a
K-
dual of
\{P_{R(K)}f_{k}\}_{k\in I}
. Define

\displaystyle M=T_{G}^{*}-T_{F}^{*}P_{S_{F}(R(K))}(S_{F}^{-1})^{*}K

Then
M:\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})\to\ell^{2}(\mathfrak{H})
is a bounded operator and

\displaystyle P_{R(K)}T_{F}Mx

\displaystyle=P_{R(K)}T_{F}T_{G}^{*}x-(S_{F})^{*}(S_{F}^{-1})^{*}Kx

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in I}P_{R(K)}f_{k}\langle x,g_{k}\rangle-Kx=0,

for every
x\in\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
. Also

\displaystyle K^{*}S_{F}^{-1}P_{S_{F}(R(K))}f_{k}+M^{*}e_{k}

\displaystyle=K^{*}S_{F}^{-1}P_{S_{F}(R(K))}f_{k}+T_{G}e_{k}-K^{*}S_{F}^{-1}P_{S_{F}(R(K))}T_{F}e_{k}

\displaystyle=K^{*}S_{F}^{-1}P_{S_{F}(R(K))}f_{k}+T_{G}e_{k}-K^{*}S_{F}^{-1}P_{S_{F}(R(K))}f_{k}

\displaystyle=g_{k}

for all
k\in I
. Hence the result.
∎

In the following theorem, we characterize all
K-
duals of a
K-

K\in B(\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H}))
is not necessarily closed.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in I}
be a
K-
frame for
\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
. Then,
\{g_{k}\}_{k\in I}
is a
K-
dual of
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in I}
if and only if there exists a bounded operator
M:\ell^{2}(\mathfrak{H})\to\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
such that
T_{F}M^{*}=K
and

\displaystyle g_{k}=Me_{k},\quad k\in I

where
\{e_{k}\}_{k\in I}
is the standard orthonormal basis of
\ell^{2}(\mathfrak{H})
and
T_{F}
is the synthesis operator of
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in I}
.

Proof.

Let
\{g_{k}\}_{k\in I}
be a
K-
dual of the
K-
frame
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in I}
. Then

\displaystyle Kx=\sum_{k\in I}f_{k}\langle x,g_{k}\rangle

Suppose
T_{G}
is the synthesis operator of
\{g_{k}\}_{k\in I}
. Put
M=T_{G}
. Then
T_{F}M^{*}x=T_{F}(\{\langle x,g_{k}\rangle\})=\sum_{k\in I}f_{k}\langle x,g_{k}\rangle=Kx,\quad x\in\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
. This gives
T_{F}M^{*}=K
. Also
Me_{k}=T_{G}e_{k}=g_{k}
, for all
k\in I
.

Conversely, if
M:\ell^{2}(\mathfrak{H})\to\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
is a bounded linear operator such that
T_{F}M^{*}=K
. Write
g_{k}=Me_{k}
. Then
\{g_{k}\}_{k\in I}
is a Bessel sequence and we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in I}f_{k}\langle x,g_{k}\rangle=\sum_{k\in I}f_{k}\langle M^{*}x,e_{k}\rangle=T_{F}M^{*}x=Kx

Thus
\{g_{k}\}_{k\in I}
is a
K-
dual of the
K-
frame
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in I}
.
∎

Theorem 3.9
.

\{h_{k}\}_{k\in I}=\{Me_{k}\}_{k\in I}
, where
\{e_{k}\}_{k\in I}
is the orthonormal basis of
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H}))

Proof.

\{h_{k}\}_{k\in I}=\{(K^{\dagger}|_{R(K)})^{*}g_{k}\}_{k\in I}
such that

\displaystyle x=\sum_{k\in I}f_{k}\langle x,h_{k}\rangle,\quad x\in R(K)

4.
Approximate
K-
Dual

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in I}
be a
K-
frame for a right quaternionic Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
then a Bessel sequence
\{g_{k}\}_{k\in I}
is called an approximate
K-
dual of
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in I}
if

\displaystyle||K-T_{F}T^{*}_{G}||<1

(4.5)

Example 4.2
.

Let
\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})=\mathfrak{H}^{3}
be the right quaternionic Hilbert space and
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
be the standard orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
.

Define
K:\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})\to\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
by

\displaystyle Ke_{1}

\displaystyle=e_{1}

\displaystyle Ke_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}e_{2}

\displaystyle Ke_{3}

\displaystyle=0

Also

\displaystyle Kx=e_{1}\langle x,e_{1}\rangle+e_{2}\frac{1}{2}\langle x,e_{2}\rangle

We can easily check that
F=\{\frac{1}{2}e_{2},e_{1},e_{3}\}
is a
K-
frame for
\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
and
G=\{2e_{2},e_{1},e_{3}\}
is an approximate
K-
dual of
F
as

\displaystyle||(K-T_{F}T_{G}^{*})(x)||\leq\frac{1}{2}||x||,\quad x\in\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
K
be a bounded operator on a right quaternionic Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
with closed range,
\||K^{\dagger}||=1
and let
F=\{f_{k}\}_{k\in I}
be a
K-
frame for
\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
. Let
G=\{g_{k}\}_{k\in I}
be an approximate
K-
dual of
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in I}
. Then

(a)

\{\Psi^{-1}P_{R(K)}f_{k}\}_{k\in I}
is a
K-
frame for
\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
with a
K-
dual
\{K^{*}(K^{\dagger})^{*}g_{k}\}_{k\in I}
where
\Psi=P_{R(K)}T_{F}T_{G}^{*}K^{\dagger}
and
K^{\dagger}
be the pseudo inverse of
K
.

(b)

\{(K^{\dagger})^{*}g_{k}\}_{k\in I}
is a
K-
frame for
\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
with a
K-
dual
\{K^{*}\Psi^{-1}P_{R(K)}f_{k}\}_{k\in I}
.

Proof.

(a). Write
h_{k}=(K^{\dagger})^{*}g_{k}
, so we compute

\displaystyle\Bigg\|x-\sum_{k\in I}f_{k}\langle x,h_{k}\rangle\Bigg\|=\Bigg\|KK^{\dagger}x-\sum_{k\in I}f_{k}\langle K^{\dagger}x,g_{k}\rangle\Bigg\|<||x||,\ x\in R(K).

This implies

\displaystyle\Bigg\|I|_{R(K)}-P_{R(K)}T_{F}{T^{*}_{H}}|_{R(K)}\Bigg\|<1.

Therefore
\Psi=P_{R(K)}T_{F}{T^{*}_{H}}|_{R(K)}
is invertible on
R(K)
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle Kx=\Psi^{-1}\Psi Kx

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in I}\Psi^{-1}P_{R(K)}f_{k}\langle Kx,h_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in I}\Psi^{-1}P_{R(K)}f_{k}\langle x,K^{*}(K^{\dagger})^{*}g_{k}\rangle,\ x\in\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H}).

Hence,
\{\Psi^{-1}P_{R(K)}f_{k}\}_{k\in I}
is a
K-
frame for
\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
with a
K-
dual
\{K^{*}(K^{\dagger})^{*}g_{k}\}_{k\in I}
by Lemma
3.3
.

(b). For each
x\in\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
, we have

\displaystyle Kx=\Psi^{*}(\Psi^{*})^{-1}Kx

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in I}h_{k}\langle(\Psi^{*})^{-1}Kx,P_{R(K)f_{k}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in I}(K^{\dagger})^{*}g_{k}\langle x,K^{*}\Psi^{-1}P_{R(K)}f_{k}\rangle

Hence,
\{(K^{\dagger})^{*}g_{k}\}_{k\in I}
is a
K-
frame for
\mathbb{H}(\mathfrak{H})
with a
K-
dual
\{K^{*}\Psi^{-1}P_{R(K)}f_{k}\}_{k\in I}
.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

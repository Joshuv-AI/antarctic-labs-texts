Discrete frames of non-uniform shifts in frequency domain
Hari Krishan Malhotra, Manisha Chhillar, Lalit Kumar Vashisht

Abstract
Non-uniform frames play a significant role in the stable analysis and decomposition of signals (functions) when the indexing set may not be a group (or, uniform lattice). We analyze frame conditions for a collection of matrix-valued functions obtained by non-uniform shifts. We give necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of matrix-valued discrete Bessel sequence over non-uniform displacement parameters in terms of the Fourier transform of window functions. We also present perturbation results for matrix-valued non-uniform discrete frames. AMS Subject Classification (2020): 42C15, 42C30. Keywords: Bessel sequence; frames; reconstruction; Fourier transform.

1.2
Basic Definitions and Notation

In this section, we provide the necessary background on the structure of discrete matrix-valued nonuniform signal spaces along with some foundational results. As is conventional, the symbols
\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{R}
, and
\mathbb{C}
represent the sets of natural numbers, integers, real numbers, and complex numbers, respectively. The set of all
n\times n
matrices having entries from complex field is denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{n}
, it forms norm space with norm
\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}=\sqrt{\sum_{m,k=1}^{n}\left|f_{m,k}\right|^{2}}
for where
f_{m,k}
denotes the
m^{th}
row and
k^{th}
column of vector
\mathbf{f}
. Throughout the paper we use bold letters for matrices. Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
r
be an odd integer coprime with
N
, with the constraint
1\leq r\leq 2N-1
, assume
\Lambda=\left\{0,\frac{r}{N}\right\}+2\mathbb{Z}
. For
p\in\mathbb{N}
, we write
\mathbb{N}_{p}=\{1,2,\dots,p\}
.

The following spaces will be used in the sequel: The space

\ell^{2}(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}):=\Bigg\{\mathbf{f}=\big[f_{m,k}(\lambda)\big]_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop 1\leq m,k\leq n}:\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\Lambda\\
1\leq m,k\leq n\end{subarray}}|f_{m,k}(\lambda)|^{2}<\infty\Bigg\},

is a Hilbert space with respect to the inner product

\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\rangle=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\operatorname{tr}\left(\mathbf{f}(\lambda)\mathbf{g}(\lambda)^{*}\right)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\Lambda\\
1\leq m,k\leq n\end{subarray}}f_{m,k}(\lambda)\overline{g_{m,k}(\lambda)},\quad\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\in\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right),

and its associated norm is

\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\ell^{2}}=\sqrt{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\Lambda\\
1\leq m,k\leq n\end{subarray}}\left|{f}_{m,k}(\lambda)\right|^{2}}.

It can be clearly seen that the above space will reduced to
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}),
if we take
N=1
and
n=1
.

We recall a notation: For
p\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote
\mathbb{N}_{p}=\{1,2,\dots,p\}
. The space

\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathbb{N}_{p},\mathbb{C}\right):=\left\{\left\{c_{\lambda,j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}\subset\mathbb{C}:\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|c_{\lambda,j}\right|^{2}<\infty\right\}

is a Hilbert space with respect to the following inner product

\displaystyle\left\langle\left\{c_{\lambda,j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}},\left\{d_{\lambda,j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}\right\rangle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}c_{\lambda,j}\overline{d_{\lambda,j}},\ \ \left\{c_{\lambda,j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}},\left\{d_{\lambda,j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}\in\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathbb{N}_{p},\mathbb{C}\right),

and the associated norm is given by

\displaystyle\left\|\left\{c_{\lambda,j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=\sqrt{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|c_{\lambda,j}\right|^{2}},\ \left\{c_{\lambda,j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}\in\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathbb{N}_{p},\mathbb{C}\right).

Definition 1.3
.

The
displacement operator
or
shift-operator

\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}:\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
, for
\lambda\in\Lambda
, and
\mathbf{f}\in\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
, is defined by

\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}=\left\{\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}{f_{1,1}\left(\lambda^{\prime}-2N\lambda\right)}&{f_{1,2}\left(\lambda^{\prime}-2N\lambda\right)}&\cdots&{f_{1,n}\left(\lambda^{\prime}-2N\lambda\right)}\\
{f_{2,1}\left(\lambda^{\prime}-2N\lambda\right)}&{f_{2,2}\left(\lambda^{\prime}-2N\lambda\right)}&\cdots&{f_{2,n}\left(\lambda^{\prime}-2N\lambda\right)}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
{f_{n,1}\left(\lambda^{\prime}-2N\lambda\right)}&{f_{n,2}\left(\lambda^{\prime}-2N\lambda\right)}&\cdots&{f_{n,n}\left(\lambda^{\prime}-2N\lambda\right)}\end{array}\right]\right\}_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda}.

This operator effectively shifts the argument of the function
\mathbb{f}
by
2N\lambda
, mapping it within the same signal space.

Let
\left\{\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{p}\subset\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
A collection of the form
\left\{\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop 1\leq j\leq p}
is called a discrete
matrix-valued nonuniform displacement system
in the discrete matrix-valued nonuniform sequence space
\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
.

The space

L^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right):=\left\{\mathbf{F}=\big[F_{m,k}(x)\big]:x\in\Omega,\,\,1\leq m,k\leq n:\int\limits_{\Omega}|F_{m,k}(x)|^{2}\,dx<\infty\right\}
is a Hilbert space with respect to the inner product defined by

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{F},\mathbf{G}\rangle=\sum_{m,k=1}^{n}\int\limits_{\Omega}{{{F}_{m,k}(x)}}\overline{{G}_{m,k}(x)}\,dx,\quad\mathbf{F},\mathbf{G}\in L^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right),

and the associated norm is given by

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{F}\|_{L^{2}}=\sqrt{\sum_{m,k=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}\left|{F}_{m,k}(x)\right|^{2}},\ \mathbf{F}\in L^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right).

Definition 1.4
.

The Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}:\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)\rightarrow L^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}({\mathbf{f}})=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{f}_{1,1}(\lambda)e^{2\pi i\lambda x}&\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{f}_{1,2}(\lambda)e^{2\pi i\lambda x}&\cdots&\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{f}_{1,n}(\lambda)e^{2\pi i\lambda x}\\
\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{f}_{2,1}(\lambda)e^{2\pi i\lambda x}&\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{f}_{2,2}(\lambda)e^{2\pi i\lambda x}&\cdots&\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{f}_{2,n}(\lambda)e^{2\pi i\lambda x}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{f}_{n,1}(\lambda)e^{2\pi i\lambda x}&\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{f}_{n,2}(\lambda)e^{2\pi i\lambda x}&\cdots&\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{f}_{n,n}(\lambda)e^{2\pi i\lambda x}\end{array}\right].

It is well defined, since
(\Lambda,\Omega)
is a spectral pair by Example
1.2
, so series corresponding to each coordinate will converges in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{M}_{n})
.

Moreover,

\mathcal{F}

is bijective, with the inverse given by the inverse Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}^{-1}:L^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
is given by

\mathcal{F}^{-1}({\mathbf{F}})=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\int_{\Omega}{F}_{1,1}(x)e^{-2\pi i\lambda x}\,dx&\int_{\Omega}{F}_{1,2}(x)e^{-2\pi i\lambda x}dx&\cdots&\int_{\Omega}{F}_{1,n}(x)e^{-2\pi i\lambda x}dx\\
\int_{\Omega}{F}_{2,1}(x)e^{-2\pi i\lambda x}\,dx&\int_{\Omega}{F}_{2,2}(x)e^{-2\pi i\lambda x}dx&\cdots&\int_{\Omega}{F}_{2,n}(x)e^{-2\pi i\lambda x}dx\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\int_{\Omega}{F}_{n,1}(x)e^{-2\pi i\lambda x}\,dx&\int_{\Omega}{F}_{n,2}(x)e^{-2\pi i\lambda x}dx&\cdots&\int_{\Omega}{F}_{n,n}(x)e^{-2\pi i\lambda x}dx\end{array}\right]

Now we provide some identities that are relevant to the Fourier transform. Let
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\in\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
and
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Then,

1.

\mathcal{F}\left(\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}\right)(x)=e^{4\pi iN\lambda x}\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f})(x)
.

2.

\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}=\langle\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f}),\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{g})\rangle_{L^{2}}
(Parseval’s relation).

3.

\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\ell^{2}}=\|\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f})\|_{L^{2}}
(Plancherel’s formula).

2
Matrix-valued discrete frames of non-uniform shifts

In this section, we study frame properties for a collection of non-uniform shifts of the form
\left\{\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}
in the space
\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
. We begin with the following definition:

Definition 2.1
.

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}
, let
\mathbf{f}_{j}
be a non-zero element of the space
\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
.
A collection
\left\{\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}\subset\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
is a
matrix-valued discrete frame of non-uniform shifts
(
matrix-valued DFNS
, in short) for
\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
, if there exist
a_{0}
,
b_{0}\in(0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle a_{0}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\leq b_{0}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\text{ for all }\mathbf{f}\in\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right).

(2.1)

Positive scalars
a_{0}
and
b_{0}
in the
frame inequality
(
2.1
) are known as the
lower frame bound
and
upper frame bound
of the frame
\left\{\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}
, respectively. If only the right-hand side inequality in (
2.1
) is satisfied, then we say that
\left\{\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}
is a
matrix-valued discrete Bessel sequence of non-uniform shifts
with
Bessel bound

b_{0}
. If
\left\{\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}
matrix-valued discrete Bessel sequence of non-uniform shifts, then
the map
\mathcal{T}:\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathbb{N}_{p},\mathbb{C}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
defined by

\mathcal{T}\left(\left\{c_{\lambda,j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}\right)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}c_{\lambda,j}\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j},

is called the
pre-frame operator
(or
synthesis operator
) of
\left\{\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}
and the Hilbert-adjoint operator of
\mathcal{T}
is the map
\mathcal{T}^{*}
:
\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathbb{N}_{p},\mathbb{C}\right)
given by

\mathcal{T}^{*}(\mathbf{f})=\left\{\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\rangle\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}.

\mathcal{T}^{*}
is called the
analysis operator
of
\left\{\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}
.
The composition
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{T}\mathcal{T}^{*}:\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
is called the
frame operator
, which is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1,\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\rangle\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
18
]
, Theorem 7.4)
.

Let
\left\{g_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
be a sequence of vectors in
\mathcal{H}
, such that there exists a constant
b_{0}>0
satisfying

\displaystyle\left\|\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{\mathbb{I}}}a_{k}g_{k}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq b_{0}\sum\left|a_{k}\right|^{2}

for all finite sequences
\left\{a_{k}\right\}
, then the series
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{I}}a_{k}g_{k}
converges for every
\left\{a_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}\in

\ell^{2}(\mathbb{I})
, further
\left\{g_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
is a Bessel sequence with Bessel bound
b_{0}
.

The following lemma will be used in the sequel. It easily follows from the mathematical induction method.

Lemma 2.3
.

\left|\sum\limits_{k=1}^{t}w_{k}\right|^{2}\leq 2^{t-1}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{t}\left|w_{k}\right|^{2}
, where
w_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
,
1\leq k\leq t
.

Our first result provides a sufficient condition for matrix-valued discrete Bessel sequence of non-uniform shifts in terms of the Fourier transforms of signals.

Theorem 2.4
.

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}
, let
\mathbf{f}_{j}
be a non-zero element in the space
\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
, and let
b_{o}\in(0,\infty)
be such that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}\left(\mathbf{f}_{j}(x)\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}\leq b_{o},\ \text{a.e.}\ x\in\Omega,\,j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}.

Then, the collection
\left\{\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}
form a matrix-valued discrete Bessel sequence of non-uniform shifts with Bessel bound
2^{p-1}b_{o}^{2}n^{2}.

Proof.

For any finite sequence
\left\{c_{\lambda,j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}\in\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\left[p\right],\mathbb{C}\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}c_{\lambda,j}\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathcal{F}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}c_{\lambda,j}\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{p}\left\|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}c_{\lambda,j}e^{4\pi iN\lambda x}\mathcal{F}\left(\mathbf{f}_{j}\right)(x)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=nb_{o}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sqrt{\int_{\Omega}\left|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}c_{\lambda,j}e^{4\pi iN\lambda x}\right|^{2}dx}.

(2.2)

Using (
2
) and the fact that
\left\{e^{4\pi iN\lambda x}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is orthonormal system in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}c_{\lambda,j}\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq nb_{o}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sqrt{\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left|c_{\lambda,j}\right|^{2}}

(2.3)

Therefore, by inequality (
2.3
) and Lemma
2.3
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}c_{\lambda,j}\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\leq\left(nb_{o}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\end{subarray}}^{p}\sqrt{\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left|c_{\lambda,j}\right|^{2}}\right)^{2}\leq b_{o}^{2}2^{p-1}n^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\end{subarray}}^{p}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left|c_{\lambda,j}\right|^{2}.

(2.4)

Inequality (
2.4
) and Proposition
2.2
gives the result with the required Bessel bound.
∎

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\left\{\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}
be a matrix-valued discrete Bessel sequence of non-uniform shifts in
\ell^{2}(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n})
with Bessel bound
b_{0}
.
Then, for a.e.
x\in\Omega
and
j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}\left(\mathbf{f}_{j}\right)(x)\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}\leq N+b_{0}.

Proof.

Using Lemma
2.7
, for any
h\in L^{2}\left(0,\frac{1}{4N}\right)
,
there exists sequence
\{d_{l}\}_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}
of scalars such that

h(x)=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}d_{l}\sqrt{4N}e^{2\pi i(4N)lx}

with
\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{4N}}|h(x)|^{2}dx=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|d_{l}\right|^{2}<\infty
.
Let
\mathbb{f}_{t}\in\{\mathbb{f}_{1},\mathbb{f}_{2},\dots,\mathbb{f}_{p}\}
be an arbitrary element.
Choose a complex sequence
\left\{c_{\lambda,j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}
as below

\displaystyle c_{\lambda,j}=\begin{cases}d_{l},&\text{ if }\lambda=2l,l\in\mathbb{Z},j=t,\\
0,&\text{ otherwise }.\end{cases}

(2.5)

Clearly
\left\{c_{\lambda,j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}\in\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathbb{N}_{p},\mathbb{C}\right)
. As
\left\{\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}
is Bessel sequence with Bessel bound
b_{0}
, by Theorem
2.6
, the associated pre-frame operator
\mathcal{T}:\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathbb{N}_{p},\mathbb{C}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
is bounded and satisfies

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{T}\left\{c_{\lambda,j}\right\}\right\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\leq b_{0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|c_{\lambda,j}\right|^{2}.

(2.6)

Using (
2.5
), we compute

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{T}\left\{c_{\lambda,j}\right\}\right\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}d_{l}\mathcal{D}_{4Nl}\mathbf{f}_{t}\right\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}\left(\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}d_{l}\mathcal{D}_{4Nl}\mathbf{f}_{{t}}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}d_{l}e^{4\pi iN(2l)\left(x\right)}\mathcal{F}\left(\mathbf{f}_{{t}}\right)(x)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sum_{m,k=1}^{n}\left|\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}d_{l}e^{4\pi iN(2l)\left(x\right)}\mathcal{F}\left(\mathbf{f}_{t}\right)_{m,k}(x)\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m,k=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}d_{l}e^{4\pi iN(2l)\left(x\right)}\right|^{2}\left(\left|\mathcal{F}\left(\mathbf{f}_{t}\right)_{m,k}(x)\right|^{2}+\left|\mathcal{F}\left(\mathbf{f}_{t}\right)_{m,k}\left(x+\frac{N}{2}\right)\right|^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m,k=1}^{n}\sum_{g=0}^{2N-1}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{4N}}\left|\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}d_{l}e^{4\pi iN(2l)\left(x\right)}\right|^{2}\left(\left|\mathcal{F}\left(\mathbf{f}_{t}\right)_{m,k}\left(x+\frac{g}{4N}\right)\right|^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left|\mathcal{F}\left(\mathbf{f}_{t}\right)_{m,k}\left(x+\frac{N}{2}+\frac{g}{4N}\right)\right|^{2}\right)dx.

(2.7)

Utilizing equations (
2.5
), (
2.6
)and (
2
), we obtain.

\displaystyle\sum_{m,k=1}^{n}\sum_{g=0}^{2N-1}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{4N}}|h(x)|^{2}\left(\left|\mathcal{F}\left(\mathbf{f}_{t}\right)_{m,k}\left(x+\frac{g}{4N}\right)\right|^{2}+\left|\mathcal{F}\left(\mathbf{f}_{t}\right)_{m,k}\left(x+\frac{N}{2}+\frac{g}{4N}\right)\right|^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq 4Nb_{0}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{4N}}|h(\xi)|^{2}d\xi.

As
h
is an arbitrary element in the space
L^{2}\left(0,\frac{1}{4N}\right)
, it follows that for a.e.
x\in\left[0,\frac{1}{4N}\right)
,

\sum_{m,k=1}^{n}\sum_{g=0}^{2N-1}\left|\mathcal{F}\left(\mathbf{f}_{t}\right)_{m,k}\left(x+\frac{g}{4N}\right)\right|^{2}+\left|\mathcal{F}\left(\mathbf{f}_{t}\right)_{m,k}\left(x+\frac{N}{2}+\frac{g}{4N}\right)\right|^{2}\leq 4Nb_{0},

which implies

\displaystyle\sum_{m,k=1}^{n}\left|\mathcal{F}\left(\mathbf{f}_{j}\right)_{m,k}(x)\right|^{2}\leq 4Nb_{0}\ \text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega,\,j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}.

This gives
\left\|\mathcal{F}\left(\mathbf{f}_{j}\right)(x)\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}\leq 2\sqrt{Nb_{0}}\leq N+b_{0}
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 2.9

\left\{\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}\subset\ell^{2}(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n})

(2.8)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,k=1\end{subarray}}^{2}{\Gamma}_{m,k}^{*}(x)\mathbf{w}_{m,k},\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m^{\prime},k^{\prime}=1\end{subarray}}^{2}{\Gamma}_{m^{\prime},k^{\prime}}^{*}(x)\mathbf{w}_{m^{\prime},k^{\prime}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle 8\mathbf{I}_{8}\mathbf{w}_{1,1},\mathbf{w}_{1,1}\right\rangle+\left\langle 8\mathbf{I}_{8}\mathbf{w}_{1,2},\mathbf{w}_{1,2}\right\rangle+\left\langle 8\mathbf{I}_{8}\mathbf{w}_{2,1},\mathbf{w}_{2,1}\right\rangle+\left\langle 8\mathbf{I}_{8}\mathbf{w}_{2,2},\mathbf{w}_{2,2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=8\left(\left\|\mathbf{w}_{1,1}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\mathbf{w}_{1,2}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\mathbf{w}_{2,1}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\mathbf{w}_{2,2}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=8\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,k=1\end{subarray}}^{2}\left\|\mathbf{w}_{m,k}\right\|^{2}.

(2.32)

Hence, by (
2.13
) and Theorem
2.12
,
\left\{\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{8}}
is a matrix-valued DFNS for
\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{2}\right)
with bounds
1
and
2^{11}
.

3
Perturbation Results

In signal processing, time-frequency analysis, quantum physics and duality relations etc.,
it is important that fundamental properties of frames are kept under perturbation
[
12
,
16
,
18
,
25
,
28
]
. Perturbation of frames and Riesz bases were studied by Favier and Zalik in
[
6
]
. Most recently, by using a contraction between the affine group and the Weyl-Heisenberg group, perturbation of frames with Gabor and wavelet structure studied by authors of
[
21
]
. Perturbation of discrete frames and their applications in distributed signal processing can be found in
[
4
]
. The deformation problem for small times is related to perturbation theory
[
12
]
.
Jyoti and Vashisht discussed perturbation for dual frames of wave packets in the paper
[
23
]
. Heil
[
17
]
is a good reference for perturbation of frames and Schauder bases in separable Banach spaces.
In this section, we give perturbation of matrix-valued discrete frames of non-uniform shifts with explicit frame bounds. We begin with the perturbation of
matrix-valued DFNS in terms of Fourier transforms.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose

1.

\left\{\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}
is matrix-valued DFNS for
\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
with frames bounds
a_{0}
and
b_{0}
.

2.

\left\{\mathbf{g}_{j}\right\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}\subset\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}\left(\mathbf{f}_{j}+\mathbf{g}_{j}\right)(x)\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}\leq\epsilon<2^{p-1}\epsilon^{2}n^{2}<a_{0},\,j\in\mathbb{N}_{p},\,x\in\Omega.

(3.1)

Then, the collection
\left\{\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{g}_{j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}
is also a matrix-valued DFNS for
\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
with frame bounds
\left(\sqrt{a_{0}}-\sqrt{2^{p-1}\epsilon^{2}n^{2}}\right)^{2}
and
\left(2^{p}\epsilon^{2}n^{2}+2b_{0}\right)
.

Proof.

Using Lemma
2.3
, for each
\mathbf{f}\in\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{g}_{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\left(\mathbf{f}_{j}+\mathbf{g}_{j}\right)\right\rangle-\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\left(\mathbf{f}_{j}+\mathbf{g}_{j}\right)\right\rangle\right|^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right).

(3.3)

From condition (
3.1
) and Theorem
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\left(\mathbf{f}_{j}+\mathbf{g}_{j}\right)\right\rangle\right|^{2}\leq 2^{p-1}\epsilon^{2}n^{2}\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2},\ \mathbf{f}\in\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right).

(3.4)

From (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{g}_{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\leq\left(2^{p}\epsilon^{2}n^{2}+2b_{0}\right)\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2}.

(3.5)

Hence, the upper condition is obtained.
From (
3.2
) and (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{g}_{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\left(\mathbf{f}_{j}+\mathbf{g}_{j}\right)\right\rangle-\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}}-\sqrt{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\left(\mathbf{f}_{j}+\mathbf{g}_{j}\right)\right\rangle\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{a_{0}\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2}}-\sqrt{\left(2^{p-1}\epsilon^{2}n^{2}\right)\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sqrt{a_{0}}-\sqrt{2^{p-1}\epsilon^{2}n^{2}}\right)\|\mathbf{f}\|,\ \mathbf{f}\in\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right).

(3.6)

From inequalities (
3.5
) and (
3
), we conclude that

\left\{\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{g}_{j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}

is a matrix-valued DFNS for
\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
with the desired frame bounds.
∎

We recall that errors are generally measured using relative error
[
7
]
. Relative error quantifies how far an estimated value is from the true value, relative to the magnitude of the true value. Mathematically, it is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\text{Relative Error}=\frac{|\hat{\theta}-\theta|}{|\theta|},

where
\hat{\theta}
is the estimated value and
\theta
is the true (reference) value. This measure is especially useful when comparing errors across values of different scales.

In statistics or analysis, relative quantities can be less sensitive to outliers or noise.

For example, in the expression:

\frac{\left\|\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{g}_{j})(x)-\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f}_{j})(x)\right\|}{\left\|\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f}_{j})(x)\right\|},\,j\in\mathbb{N}_{p},

we are not just measuring the magnitude of the perturbation, but rather the perturbation
relative to the size of the signal
. This often gives a more meaningful and stable measure of deviation, especially when comparing across different scales or conditions. Motivated by the notion of relative error, the following theorem gives frame conditions for perturbed sequences.

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose

1.

\left\{\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}
is a matrix-valued DFNS for
\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
with frames bounds
a_{0}
and
b_{0}
.

2.

\left\{\mathbf{g}_{j}\right\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}\subset\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
satisfies

\frac{\left\|\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{g}_{j})(x)-\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f}_{j})(x)\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}}{\left\|\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f}_{j})(x)\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}}\leq\epsilon
, where
\epsilon>0
with
\epsilon^{2}<\frac{a_{0}}{2^{p-1}(N+b_{0})^{2}n^{2}}
and
x\in\Omega
.

Then, the collection

\left\{\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{g}_{j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{8}}
is also a matrix-valued DFNS for

\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
with frame bounds

\left(\sqrt{a_{0}}-\sqrt{2^{p-1}\epsilon^{2}(N+b_{0})^{2}n^{2}}\right)^{2}
and
\left(2^{p}\epsilon^{2}(N+b_{0})^{2}n^{2}+2b_{0}\right)
.

Proof.

Using Lemma
2.3
, for each
\mathbf{f}\in\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{g}_{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\left(\mathbf{g}_{j}-\mathbf{f}_{j}\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\left(\mathbf{g}_{j}-\mathbf{f}_{j}\right)\right\rangle\right|^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right).

Using hypothesis (
2
), and corollary
2.9
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{g}_{j}-\mathbf{f}_{j})(x)\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}\leq\epsilon\left\|\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{f}_{j}(x))\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}\leq\epsilon(N+b_{0}).

Now, Theorem
2.4
gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\left(\mathbf{g}_{j}-\mathbf{f}_{j}\right)\right\rangle\right|^{2}\leq 2^{p-1}\epsilon^{2}(N+b_{0})^{2}n^{2}\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2},\,\mathbf{f}\in\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right).

Therefore, we have obtain the upper frame inequality

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{g}_{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\leq\left(2^{p}\epsilon^{2}(N+b_{0})^{2}n^{2}+2b_{0}\right)\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2},\,\mathbf{f}\in\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right).

(3.7)

For the lower frame inequality, we compute

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{g}_{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\left(\mathbf{g}_{j}-\mathbf{f}_{j}\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{f}_{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}}-\sqrt{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\lambda\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}^{p}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\left(\mathbf{g}_{j}-\mathbf{f}_{j}\right)\right\rangle\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{a_{0}\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2}}-\sqrt{2^{p-1}\epsilon^{2}(N+b_{0})^{2}n^{2}\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sqrt{a_{0}}-\sqrt{2^{p-1}\epsilon^{2}(N+b_{0})^{2}n^{2}}\right)\|\mathbf{f}\|\,\text{for all}\ \mathbf{f}\in\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right).

(3.8)

Hence, from (
3.7
) and (
3
), we conclude that the collection
\left\{\mathcal{D}_{2N\lambda}\mathbf{g}_{j}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda\atop j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}}
form a matrix-valued DFNS for
\ell^{2}\left(\Lambda,\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)
with the desired frame bounds.
∎

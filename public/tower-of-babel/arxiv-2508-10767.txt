Ab initio computations of the fourth-order charge density moments of 48 Ca and 208 Pb Journal: Physics Letters B
T. Miyagi, M. Heinz, A. Schwenk

Abstract
Neutron skins of neutron-rich nuclei connect nuclei with the matter in neutron stars. High-precision measurements of nuclear charge densities to extract higher-order moments are proposed to be sensitive to neutron radii and skin thicknesses. We investigate the charge density of 48 Ca and 208 Pb, leading candidates for such studies, with ab initio nuclear structure calculations. We find strong correlations between the fourth-order charge density moment R ch 4 R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4} and the charge and neutron radii, allowing us to predict R ch 4 R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4} for 48 Ca and 208 Pb. We find a substantially weaker correlation between the fourth-order charge density moment and the neutron skin, limiting the ability of high-precision electron scattering to determine the neutron skin in a model-independent manner.

Ab initio computations

Ab initio nuclear structure calculations
Hergert 2020
start from nuclear Hamiltonians

H=T_{\mathrm{int}}+V_{\text{NN}}+V_{\text{3N}}\,,

(1)

with the intrinsic kinetic energy
T_{\mathrm{int}}

and two- and three-nucleon potentials
V_{\text{NN}}
and
V_{\text{3N}}
, respectively.
Such Hamiltonians are rooted in quantum chromodynamics through effective field theories (EFTs) of the strong interaction
Epelbaum et al. 2009
,
Machleidt and Entem 2011

and optimized to nucleon-nucleon scattering data, properties of few-nucleon systems, and occasionally also selected medium-mass nuclei
Entem and Machleidt 2003
,
Hebeler et al. 2011
,
Ekström et al. 2015
,
Epelbaum et al. 2015
,
Jiang et al. 2020
,
Arthuis et al.
.
Nuclear Hamiltonians are intrinsically uncertain, due to both truncations of the EFT expansion and fitting of unknown low-energy couplings (LECs) to experimental data.

In this work, we employ two selected chiral EFT Hamiltonians optimized for reproduction of ground-state energies and charge radii of medium-mass and heavy nuclei, the
\Delta\mathrm{NNLO}_{\mathrm{GO}}
(with cutoff 394 MeV) and
\mathrm{1.8/2.0}~\mathrm{(EM7.5)}
Hamiltonians
Jiang et al. 2020
,
Arthuis et al.
.
We additionally employ an ensemble of 34 so-called “nonimplausible Hamiltonians” constructed at next-to-next-to-leading order in chiral EFT with Delta isobar degrees of freedom
Hu et al. 2022
.
This ensemble is very conservative in its construction, incorporating maximal LEC variations within EFT uncertainties.
It is reflective of the worst case uncertainty of nuclear Hamiltonians and allows us to systematically explore this uncertainty.

For each Hamiltonian, we solve the many-body Schrödinger equation using in-medium similarity renormalization group (
IMSRG
)
Hergert et al. 2016
starting from a Hartree-Fock reference state.
Many-body methods like the IMSRG are approximate, but systematically improvable, with mild polynomial scaling in system size that enables calculations of medium-mass and heavy nuclei
Hagen et al. 2014
,
Hergert et al. 2016
,
Tichai et al. 2020
,
Somà 2020
.
The
IMSRG(2)
approximation, where the IMSRG is truncated at the normal-ordered two-body level, has been demonstrated to be precise for ground-state properties of nuclei
Heinz et al. 2025
.

We compute the ground states of
{}^{48}\mathrm{Ca}
and
{}^{208}\mathrm{Pb}
using the
IMSRG(2)
for all the Hamiltonians mentioned above.
For our calculations of
{}^{208}\mathrm{Pb}
, we expand all matrix elements of our Hamiltonian in a spherical harmonic oscillator basis of 15 major shells with a frequency of
\hbar\omega=12\>\text{MeV}
.
We additionally truncate our
3N
potentials in the three-body basis
\ket{pqr}
, keeping only states with
e_{p}+e_{q}+e_{r}\leq E_{\mathrm{3max}}=28
(
e=2n+l
).
These truncations have been demonstrated to be sufficient for model-space convergence for the ground state of
{}^{208}\mathrm{Pb}

Hu et al. 2022
,
Hebeler et al. 2023
.
For our calculations of
{}^{48}\mathrm{Ca}
we employ instead a basis of 13 major shells with a larger frequency
\hbar\omega=16\>\text{MeV}
that is more optimal for convergence in medium-mass nuclei
Hoppe et al. 2021
.

We compute charge form factors from the ground-state expectation values of the charge form factor operator:

\displaystyle F_{\rm ch}(

\displaystyle q^{2})=e\sum_{i=1}^{A}\left\{G^{E}_{i}(q^{2})\left[1-\frac{q^{2}}{8m^{2}}\right]j_{0}(qr_{i})\right.

(2)

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{q^{2}}{2m^{2}}\left[G^{M}_{i}(q^{2})-\frac{1}{2}G^{E}_{i}(q^{2})\right](\vec{\ell}_{i}\cdot\vec{\sigma}_{i})\frac{j_{1}(qr_{i})}{qr_{i}}\right\}.

Here
q
is the magnitude of the momentum transfer,
e
is the elementary electric charge,
G^{E}_{i}(q^{2})
and
G^{M}_{i}(q^{2})
are the
i
th nucleon’s electric and magnetic form factors,
\vec{\ell}_{i}
is the orbital angular momentum operator,
\vec{\sigma}_{i}
is the spin Pauli matrix, and
j_{l}(x)
is the order
l
spherical Bessel function of the first kind.
In this work we adopt
G^{E}_{i}(q^{2})
and
G^{M}_{i}(q^{2})
from Ref.
Ye et al. 2018
.
We make the form factor translationally invariant by leveraging the Gaussian factorization of the center-of-mass wave function
Hagen et al. 2009
:

F_{\rm ch}^{\rm int}(q^{2})=e^{q^{2}b^{2}_{\rm cm}/4}F_{\rm ch}(q^{2})\,,

(3)

where
b_{\rm cm}
is the oscillator length of the center-of-mass motion estimated from the center-of-mass kinetic energy expectation value.

3
Charge density moments

The moments of nuclear densities may be computed in several different ways.
One can construct and evaluate operators for the individual contributions, as is typically done for the charge radius squared or the neutron radius squared
Hagen et al. 2016
,
Heinz et al. 2025
.
Once one considers the fourth moment
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
, such operator expressions become complicated and unwieldy
Kurasawa and Suzuki 2019
,
Miyagi 2025
.
It is instead easier to extract such moments from the charge form factor directly.
This can be done by computing
F_{\rm ch}^{\rm int}(q^{2})
for a large set of momentum transfers
q
, performing a Fourier transform to get the charge density
\rho_{\mathrm{ch}}(r)
, and then computing the desired moments directly via integration:

\langle R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{n}\rangle=\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\,r^{n+2}\rho_{\mathrm{ch}}(r)}{\int^{\infty}_{0}dr\,r^{2}\rho_{\rm ch}(r)}\,.

(4)

Alternatively, one can compute them via derivatives of
F_{\rm ch}^{\rm int}(q^{2})
as
q^{2}\to 0
, which requires relatively few evaluations of
F_{\rm ch}^{\rm int}(q^{2})
at small
q
.
For instance,
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
may be computed as

R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}{}=\left.\frac{60}{F_{\rm ch}^{\rm int}(0)}\frac{d^{2}F_{\rm ch}^{\rm int}(q^{2})}{(dq^{2})^{2}}\right|_{q^{2}=0}\,.

(5)

We compute
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{2}
and
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
by evaluating
F_{\rm ch}^{\rm int}(q^{2})
for 11 equally spaced points from
q^{2}=0
to
1
fm
-2
, constructing a Gaussian process interpolator, and taking derivatives as
q^{2}\to 0
.
Gaussian process interpolation provides robust second derivatives of
F_{\rm ch}^{\rm int}
and allows the uncertainties related to the interpolation and differentiation to be quantified (see
A
for details).
We find that for all of our calculations, the estimated uncertainty on
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{2}
and
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
due to the Gaussian process interpolation is less than 0.001
\text{fm}^{2}
and 0.3
\text{fm}^{4}
for
{}^{48}\mathrm{Ca}
(0.005
\text{fm}^{2}
and 1.8
\text{fm}^{4}
for
{}^{208}\mathrm{Pb}
), respectively.

4
Fourth-order moments of
{}^{48}\mathrm{Ca}
and
{}^{208}\mathrm{Pb}

It is well known that predictions of various moments of nuclear charge, weak, point-proton, and point-neutron densities are correlated.
These correlations arise due to the common many-body physics and nuclear Hamiltonians at play, limiting how much, for example, proton and neutron densities can vary independently.
Figure
1
shows such correlations between
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{2}
,
R_{\mathrm{n}}^{2}
, and
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
for
{}^{48}\mathrm{Ca}
(left) and
{}^{208}\mathrm{Pb}
(right).
Each correlation plot shows individual points each corresponding to the IMSRG(2) predictions for a single Hamiltonian:
\Delta\mathrm{NNLO}_{\mathrm{GO}}
(orange cross),
\mathrm{1.8/2.0}~\mathrm{(EM7.5)}
(green plus), and the 34 nonimplausible Hamiltonians (blue circles).

We find strong correlations between
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{2}
and
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
for both
{}^{48}\mathrm{Ca}
and
{}^{208}\mathrm{Pb}
.
While variations in nuclear Hamiltonians may allow for a broad range in predicted charge radii, such variations clearly produce correlated changes in charge density moments, not independent ones.
Past work has also shown that model-space and many-body truncation uncertainties are similarly correlated
Heinz et al. 2025
,
Heinz et al.
, which we also find for the
\Delta\mathrm{NNLO}_{\mathrm{GO}}
and
\mathrm{1.8/2.0}~\mathrm{(EM7.5)}
Hamiltonians.

We also find a strong, but less tight correlation between
R_{\mathrm{n}}^{2}
and
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
for both systems.
The larger scattering of the nonimplausble Hamiltonians suggests that there is some uncertainty in nuclear Hamiltonians that allows for slight variations in the relative behavior of the proton and neutron densities.
Nonetheless, such variations are limited, and there is a strong microscopic connection between
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
and
R_{\mathrm{n}}^{2}
that can be exploited to study the neutron radius without directly measuring it, as suggested in Refs.
Kurasawa and Suzuki 2019
,
Kurasawa et al. 2021
,
Kurasawa and Suzuki 2022
.
This is closely related to the established connection between
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{2}
and
R_{\mathrm{n}}^{2}
.

The large Hamiltonian uncertainty reflected by the broad range of
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{2}
predictions can be reduced by calibrating the theory predictions against additional constraints
Hu et al. 2022
,
Heinz et al.
.
For example, by combining experimental
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{2}
values
Noël and Hoferichter 2024
,
Kurasawa et al. 2021
with our correlations, we provide a prediction for the value of
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
, indicated by the gray bands.
For each correlation, we include a conservative error band such that all the predictions from nonimplausible Hamiltonians lie within the band.
Combining this with the experimental uncertainties, we predict
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}({}^{48}\mathrm{Ca})=204.4\pm 3.2\>\text{fm}^{4}
,
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}({}^{208}\mathrm{Pb})=1172.5\pm 16.7\>\text{fm}^{4}
.
These values are consistent with the experimental values extracted in Ref.
Kurasawa et al. 2021
.
Note that the extracted value
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}({}^{48}\mathrm{Ca})=194.734\pm 2.544\>\text{fm}^{4}
disagrees with our prediction because the
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{2}
value used in that work is also smaller than what was found in a more recent analysis in Ref.
Noël and Hoferichter 2024
(
11.910\pm 0.061\>\text{fm}^{2}
vs.
12.076\pm 0.070\>\text{fm}^{2}
).
In
{}^{208}\mathrm{Pb}
, the value extracted in
Kurasawa et al. 2021

R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}({}^{208}\mathrm{Pb})=1171.981\pm 17.627\>\text{fm}^{4}
, is in excellent agreement with our prediction.

We also perform a similar analysis using the more uncertain correlation between
R_{\mathrm{n}}^{2}
and
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
and theory predictions for
R_{\mathrm{n}}^{2}
.
Based on a previous prediction for
R_{\mathrm{n}}^{2}

, we obtain
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}({}^{48}\mathrm{Ca})=205.8\pm 10.7\>\text{fm}^{4}
,
which is in excellent agreement with the result inferred from the
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{2}
–
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
correlation, but with a larger uncertainty.

5
Implications for neutron skins

The correlation between
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
and
R_{\mathrm{n}}^{2}
is the basis for extracting information about the neutron density from high-precision electron scattering.
The
R_{\mathrm{n}}^{2}
–
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
correlation is however substantially more uncertain than the
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{2}
–
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
correlation (and the closely related
R_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}
–
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
correlation).
We see the impact of this uncertainty in Fig.
2
,
where we no longer observe a significant correlation between
R_{\mathrm{skin}}=(R_{\mathrm{n}}^{2})^{1/2}-(R_{\mathrm{p}}^{2})^{1/2}
and
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
in either
{}^{48}\mathrm{Ca}
or
{}^{208}\mathrm{Pb}
.
Without such a correlation, we cannot extract a value for
R_{\mathrm{skin}}
from our
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
predictions.
A broad range of neutron skin predictions could be compatible with the constraints coming from
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
,
consistent with a large set of independent determinations and predictions of
R_{\mathrm{skin}}
in
{}^{48}\mathrm{Ca}
and
{}^{208}\mathrm{Pb}

Adhikari et al. 2022
,
Birkhan et al. 2017
,
Hagen et al. 2016
,
Hu et al. 2022
,
Heinz et al.
,
Reinhard et al. 2022
,
Adhikari et al. 2021
,
Tamii et al. 2011
,
Kłos et al. 2007
,
Zenihiro et al. 2010
,
Reinhard et al. 2021
,
Kurasawa et al. 2021
.

This suggests that while high-precision electron scattering is sensitive to the neutron radius, determining
R_{\mathrm{skin}}
from such a measurement will be much more challenging.
In particular, Ref.
Kurasawa et al. 2021
found substantial model dependence in the extraction of
R_{\mathrm{n}}^{2}
from
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
leading to substantial model dependence in the inferred
R_{\mathrm{skin}}
.
Our predictions do not suffer from the same model dependence as phenomenological methods, and because of this we do not find spurious correlations between
R_{\mathrm{skin}}
and
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
.
We conclude that inferring
R_{\mathrm{skin}}
from a measurement of
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
will have some inherent model dependence.
It is still possible that as nuclear Hamiltonians and the charge density operator are further improved, for example, by reaching higher orders in the EFT expansion or through more sophisticated global optimizations, we find that this picture evolves.
From this perspective, precision measurements of electroweak properties of nuclei are important for guiding the development and optimization of both nuclear forces and currents.

6
Conclusions

We calculated the ground states of
{}^{48}\mathrm{Ca}
and
{}^{208}\mathrm{Pb}
with ab initio methods using a large ensemble of state-of-the-art nuclear Hamiltonians from chiral EFT.
We computed point-proton and point-neutron radii and the charge form factors
F_{\mathrm{ch}}(q^{2})
, used to extract the second- and fourth-order moments of the charge density.
We found that this extraction was most reliable when using Gaussian processes to interpolate
F_{\mathrm{ch}}(q^{2})
for small
q
, which also allowed us to quantify and control the uncertainty associated with the interpolation.
Based on our computed values for
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{2}
,
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
,
R_{\mathrm{n}}^{2}
, and
R_{\mathrm{skin}}
, we established strong microscopic correlations between
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
and both
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{2}
and
R_{\mathrm{n}}^{2}
.
We leveraged these correlations to provide predictions for
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
in
{}^{48}\mathrm{Ca}
and
{}^{208}\mathrm{Pb}
.
When considering
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
and
R_{\mathrm{skin}}
, we found only a weak correlation, insufficient to extract
R_{\mathrm{skin}}
from
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
.
This limits the ability of high-precision electron scattering to constrain the neutron skin of these systems in a model-independent way.
On the other hand, high-precision isotope-shift spectroscopy is sensitive to changes in
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
across isotope chains and can give insight into the evolution of deformation
Door et al. 2025
.
High-precision electron scattering can provide reference values for
R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{4}
for such studies, possibly also constraining nuclear structure effects for searches for new physics
Hur et al. 2022
,
Door et al. 2025
,
Wilzewski et al. 2025
.

A Mixed-FEM approximation with uniform conservation of the exponential stability for a class of anisotropic port-Hamiltonian system and its application to LQ control.
Luis Mora, Kirsten Morris

Abstract
In this manuscript, we present a mixed finite element discretization for a class of boundary-damped anisotropic port-Hamiltonian systems. Using a multiplier method, we demonstrate that the resulting approximation model uniformly preserves the exponential stability of the uncontrolled system, establishing a lower bound for the exponential decay rate that is independent of the mesh size. This property is illustrated through the spatial discretization of a piezoelectric beam. Furthermore, we show how the uniform preservation of exponential stability by the proposed model aids in the convergence of controllers derived from an infinite-time linear quadratic control design, in comparison to models obtained from the standard finite-element method.

Introduction.

The conservation of the exponential stability independently of the mesh size, uniform conservation, is a desired property for the numerical approximation models of distributed-parameter systems. The relevance of this property lies not only in mitigating the effects of spurious oscillations during simulations, as happen in standard finite differences approximations of the wave equation, but also in the conservation of properties as the exact controllability and observability´that are relevant in the design of controllers and estimators
[
29

For example, in the linear quadratic control for partial differential equations with an infinite time horizon, the design is based on numerical approximations of the systems, and the sequence of designed controllers could not converge as the approximation order is increased and may not even stabilize the original system. The key sufficient condition for the controllers convergence and the satisfactory performance of the feedback system is the uniform exponential stability of the numerical model

5.1
Proof of Lemma
3
.

Let
mspec(X)
be the multi-spectrum of a square matrix
X
, that is,, the set consisting of the eigenvalues of
X
including their algebraic multiplicity, and
spec(X)
be the spectrum of
X
, that is,, the set consisting of the eigenvalues of
X
ignoring algebraic multiplicity.
Since
\bar{A}=A\otimes I_{N}
we obtain that

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}0&\bar{A}\\
\bar{A}^{\top}&0\end{bmatrix}^{-2}=\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
A^{\top}&0\end{bmatrix}^{-2}\otimes I_{N}=P_{1}^{-2}\otimes I_{N}

According to [Bernstain2019, Proposition 9.1.10],
mspec(P_{1}^{-2}\otimes I_{N})=\{\lambda\mu:\lambda\in mspec(P_{1}^{-2}),\mu\in mspec(I_{N})\}
. Note that, in this particular case,
\mu
always equals
1
. This implies that
spec(P_{1}^{-2}\otimes I_{N})=spec(P_{1}^{-2})
. Moreover, all
P_{1}^{-2}
eigenvalues are real. As a consequence,
\max eig\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&\bar{A}\\
\bar{A}^{\top}&0\end{bmatrix}^{-2}\right)=\max eig\left(P_{1}^{-2}\otimes I_{N}\right)=\max eig(P_{1}^{-2})
.

On the other hand, consider the vector
\boldsymbol{f}=[f_{1},\dots,f_{N}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the
j
-th element of product
W\boldsymbol{f}
is

\left[W\boldsymbol{f}\right]_{j}=\begin{cases}-\dfrac{h}{4}(f_{1}+f_{2}),&j=1\\
-\dfrac{h}{4}\left((j-1)(f_{j-1}+f_{j})+j(f_{j}+f_{j+1})\right),&j=2\dots N-1\\
-\dfrac{h}{4}\left((N-1)(f_{N-1}+f_{N})+2Nf_{N}\right),&j=N\end{cases}.

Using
(a+b)^{2}\leq a^{2}+b^{2}+2|ab|\leq 2a^{2}+2b^{2}
we have

\displaystyle\|W\boldsymbol{f}\|^{2}=

\displaystyle\dfrac{h^{2}}{16}\left((f_{1}+f_{2})^{2}+\left((N-1)(f_{N-1}+f_{N})+2Nf_{N}\right)^{2}+\sum_{j=2}^{N-1}\left((j-1)(f_{j-1}+f_{j})+j(f_{j}+f_{j+1})\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{h^{2}}{16}\left((f_{1}+f_{2})^{2}+2(N-1)^{2}(f_{N-1}+f_{N})^{2}+8N^{2}f_{N}^{2}+2\sum_{j=2}^{N-1}(j-1)^{2}(f_{j-1}+f_{j})^{2}+j^{2}(f_{j}+f_{j+1})^{2}\right).

Since
\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{N-1}(j-1)^{2}(f_{j-1}+f_{j})^{2}+j^{2}(f_{j}+f_{j+1})^{2}=(f_{1}+f_{2})^{2}+(N-1)^{2}(f_{N-1}+f_{N})^{2}+2\sum_{j=2}^{N-2}j^{2}(f_{j}+f_{j+1})^{2}
, then

\displaystyle\|W\boldsymbol{f}\|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{h^{2}}{16}\left(3(f_{1}+f_{2})^{2}+4(N-1)^{2}(f_{N-1}+f_{N})^{2}+8N^{2}f_{N}^{2}+4\sum_{j=2}^{N-2}j^{2}(f_{j}+f_{j+1})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{h^{2}}{16}\left(6f_{1}^{2}+8\sum_{j=2}^{N}\left(j^{2}+(j-1)^{2}\right)f_{j}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{h^{2}N^{2}}{16}\left(6f_{1}^{2}+16\sum_{j=2}^{N}f_{j}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ell^{2}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|^{2}.

Consequently, for any vector
\boldsymbol{g}=[\boldsymbol{g}_{1}^{\top},\dots,\boldsymbol{g}_{n}^{\top}]^{\top}
with
\boldsymbol{g}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\forall j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we obtain that
\displaystyle\|\bar{W}\boldsymbol{g}\|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|W\boldsymbol{g}_{j}\|^{2}\leq\ell^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\boldsymbol{g}_{j}\|^{2}=\ell^{2}\|\boldsymbol{g}\|^{2}
, completing the proof.

5.2
Proof of Lemma
4
.

Denotes by
[V\boldsymbol{f}]_{j}
the
j
-th element of the product between a matrix
V
and vector
\boldsymbol{f}
. Consider matrices
C
,
M
,
D
, and
\boldsymbol{t}_{r}
defined at (
18
) and (
29
).
Defining
\overline{\theta}_{k}=1/\theta_{k}
and
\boldsymbol{f}=[f_{1},\dots,f_{N}]^{\top}
, we have that
[M\widetilde{Q}^{-1}M^{\top}\boldsymbol{f}]_{1}=\dfrac{\overline{\theta}_{1}f_{1}}{4}+\dfrac{\overline{\theta}_{2}(f_{1}+f_{2})}{4}
,
[M\widetilde{Q}^{-1}M^{\top}\boldsymbol{f}]_{j}=\dfrac{\overline{\theta}_{j}(f_{j-1}+f_{j})}{4}+\dfrac{\overline{\theta}_{j+1}(f_{j}+f_{j+1})}{4}
for every
j\in\{2,\dots,N-1\}
, and
[M\widetilde{Q}^{-1}M^{\top}\boldsymbol{f}]_{N}=\dfrac{\overline{\theta}_{N}(f_{N-1}+f_{N})}{4}
. Similarly,
[C\boldsymbol{f}]_{1}=\dfrac{f_{2}}{2}
,
[C\boldsymbol{f}]_{j}=\dfrac{j(f_{j+1}-f_{j-1})}{2}
for every
j\in\{2,\dots,N-1\}
, and
[C\boldsymbol{f}]_{N}=N(f_{N}-f_{N-1})
.
Then, the inner product
h\left\langle M\widetilde{\Theta}^{-1}M^{\top}\boldsymbol{f},C\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle
is expressed as

\displaystyle h\left\langle M\widetilde{\Theta}^{-1}M^{\top}\boldsymbol{f},C\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{h}{8}\left(\overline{\theta}_{1}f_{1}+\overline{\theta}_{2}(f_{1}+f_{2})\right)f_{2}+\dfrac{hN}{4}\overline{\theta}_{N}(f_{N}+f_{N-1})(f_{N}-f_{N-1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=2}^{N-1}\dfrac{hj}{8}\left[\overline{\theta}_{j}(f_{j-1}+f_{j})+\overline{\theta}_{j+1}(f_{j}+f_{j+1})\right](f_{j+1}-f_{j-1})

Expressing
f_{j+1}-f_{j-1}
as
(f_{j}+f_{j+1})-(f_{j}+f_{j-1})
we obtain
\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{N-1}j\left[\overline{\theta}_{j}(f_{j-1}+f_{j})+\overline{\theta}_{j+1}(f_{j}+f_{j+1})\right](f_{j+1}-f_{j-1})=-\sum_{j=2}^{N}\overline{\theta}_{j}(f_{j-1}+f_{j})^{2}-\overline{\theta}_{2}(f_{1}+f_{2})^{2}+N\overline{\theta}_{N}(f_{N-1}+f_{N})^{2}+\sum_{j=2}^{N-1}j(\overline{\theta}_{j}-\overline{\theta}_{j+1})(f_{j}+f_{j-1})(f_{j}+f_{j+1})
.
Similarly, note that
\overline{\theta}_{N}(f_{N}+f_{N-1})(f_{N}-f_{N-1})=2\overline{\theta}_{N}(f_{N}+f_{N-1})f_{N}-\overline{\theta}_{N}(f_{N}+f_{N-1})^{2}
and
\left(\overline{\theta}_{1}f_{1}+\overline{\theta}_{2}(f_{1}+f_{2})\right)f_{2}=-\overline{\theta}_{1}f_{1}^{2}+(\overline{\theta}_{1}-\overline{\theta}_{2})(f_{1}+f_{2})f_{1}+\overline{\theta}_{2}(f_{1}+f_{2})^{2}
. This implies that

\displaystyle h\left\langle M\widetilde{\Theta}^{-1}M^{\top}\boldsymbol{f},C\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{h}{8}\left[-\overline{\theta}_{1}f_{1}^{2}-\sum_{j=2}^{N}\overline{\theta}_{j}(f_{j-1}+f_{j})^{2}+4N\overline{\theta}_{N}(f_{N}+f_{N-1})f_{N}-N\overline{\theta}_{N}(f_{N-1}+f_{N})^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+(\overline{\theta}_{1}-\overline{\theta}_{2})(f_{1}+f_{2})f_{1}+\sum_{j=2}^{N-1}j(\overline{\theta}_{j}-\overline{\theta}_{j+1})(f_{j}+f_{j-1})(f_{j}+f_{j+1})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{h}{2}\boldsymbol{f}^{\top}M\widetilde{\Theta}^{-1}M^{\top}\boldsymbol{f}+\dfrac{h}{2}\boldsymbol{f}^{\top}M\widetilde{\Theta}^{-1}O\widetilde{\Theta}^{-1}M^{\top}\boldsymbol{f}

\displaystyle+\dfrac{h}{8}\left[4N\overline{\theta}_{N}(f_{N}+f_{N-1})f_{N}-N\overline{\theta}_{N}(f_{N-1}+f_{N})^{2}\right],

since
\overline{\theta}_{j}-\overline{\theta}_{j+1}=\overline{\theta}_{j}(\theta_{j+1}-\theta_{j})\overline{\theta}_{j+1}
.
Finally, using the Young’s inequality
4N\overline{\theta}_{N}(f_{N}+f_{N-1})f_{N}\leq N\overline{\theta}_{N}(f_{N}+f_{N-1})^{2}+4N\overline{\theta}_{N}f_{N}^{2}
we obtain

\displaystyle h\left\langle M\widetilde{\Theta}^{-1}M^{\top}\boldsymbol{f},C\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{h}{2}\boldsymbol{f}^{\top}M\widetilde{\Theta}^{-1}M^{\top}\boldsymbol{f}+\dfrac{h}{2}\boldsymbol{f}^{\top}M\widetilde{\Theta}^{-1}O\widetilde{\Theta}^{-1}M^{\top}\boldsymbol{f}+\dfrac{hN}{2}\overline{\theta}_{N}f_{N}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{h}{2}\boldsymbol{f}^{\top}M\widetilde{\Theta}^{-1}\left(\widetilde{Q}-O\right)\widetilde{\Theta}^{-1}M^{\top}\boldsymbol{f}+\dfrac{hN}{2}\overline{\theta}_{N}f_{N}^{2}+\dfrac{\ell\overline{\theta}_{N}}{2}\boldsymbol{f}^{\top}\boldsymbol{t}_{r}\boldsymbol{t}_{r}^{\top}\boldsymbol{f}.

On the other hand, note that

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{h}\left\langle D^{\top}\widetilde{\Theta}D\boldsymbol{f},C\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{2h}\left[\left(\theta^{q}_{1}f_{1}-\theta^{q}_{2}(f_{2}-f_{1})\right)f_{2}+\sum_{j=2}^{N-1}j[\theta_{j}(f_{j}-f_{j-1})-\theta_{j+1}(f_{j+1}-f_{j})](f_{j+1}-f_{j-1})\right]

\displaystyle-\dfrac{N}{h}\theta_{N}(f_{N}-f_{N-1})^{2}.

Given
\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{N-1}j\left[\theta_{j}(f_{j}-f_{j-1})-\theta_{j+1}(f_{j+1}-f_{j})\right](f_{j+1}-f_{j-1})=\theta_{2}(f_{2}-f_{2})^{2}-N\theta_{N}(f_{N}-f_{N-1})^{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{N}\theta_{j}\left(f_{j}-f_{j-1}\right)^{2}\newline
-\sum_{j=2}^{N-1}j(\theta_{j+}-\theta_{j})(f_{j}-f_{j-1})(f_{j+1}-f_{j})
and

\left(\theta_{1}f_{1}-\theta_{2}(f_{2}-f_{1})\right)f_{2}=\theta_{1}f_{1}^{2}-(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1})(f_{2}-f_{1})f_{1}-\theta_{2}(f_{2}-f_{1})^{2}

we have that

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{h}\left\langle D^{\top}\widetilde{\Theta}D\boldsymbol{f},C\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{2h}\left[\theta_{1}f_{1}^{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{N}\theta_{j}(f_{j}-f_{j-1})^{2}+N\theta_{N}(f_{N}-f_{N-1})^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1})(f_{2}-f_{1})f_{1}-\sum_{j=2}^{N-1}j(\theta_{j+1}-\theta_{j})(f_{j}-f_{j-1})(f_{j+1}-f_{j})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{2h}\boldsymbol{f}^{\top}D^{\top}\widetilde{\Theta}D\boldsymbol{f}+\dfrac{1}{2h}\boldsymbol{f}^{\top}D^{\top}OD\boldsymbol{f}-\dfrac{N\theta_{N}}{2h}(f_{N}-f_{N-1})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{2h}\boldsymbol{f}^{\top}D^{\top}\left(\widetilde{\Theta}-O\right)D\boldsymbol{f}-\dfrac{N\theta_{N}}{2h}\boldsymbol{f}^{\top}D^{\top}\boldsymbol{t}_{r}\boldsymbol{t}_{r}^{\top}D\boldsymbol{f},

completing the proof.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

It is widely believed that there is a close connection between zeros of
L
-functions and eigenvalues of random matrices. Evidence of such a connection was first found for the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
by Montgomery
[
Mon73
]
, who investigated the pair correlation of non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
and noticed that it is the same as the pair correlation of eigenvalues of Hermitian random matrices. Similar phenomenon was later discovered by Rudnick and Sarnak
[
RS96
]
for general automorphic
L
-functions on
\GL(m)
. Their results suggest that the
n
-level correlation of zeros of any individual principal
L
-function
L(s,\pi)
attached to a cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi
of
\GL_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
follows a
universal
gaussian unitary ensemble (GUE) model.

Unlike the
n
-level correlation statistic for an
individual

L
-function, there is another statistic, called the
n
-level density of low-lying zeros, that is defined for
families
of
L
-functions and is sensitive to families. Katz and Sarnak
[
KS99a
]
[
KS99b
]
studied the
n
-level density of low-lying zeros of zeta functions associated to varieties over function fields
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
. For these they found a spectral interpretation in terms of eigenvalues of Frobenius maps on cohomology groups. They proposed a conjecture, called the Density Conjecture, predicting that low-lying zeros of
L
-functions in a family behave like low-lying eigenvalues of random matrices of a specific symmetry type.

Let us describe the Density Conjecture in general terms. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a finite set of automorphic representations. Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a Schwartz function whose Fourier transform
\hat{\Phi}
has compact support. We call
\Phi
a
test function
throughout this paper. Assume the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) for
L(s,f)
, where
f\in\mathcal{F}
. Define the
1-level density of low-lying zeros
of
L(s,f)
associated to
\Phi
to be

(1.1)

D(f;\Phi):=\sum_{\rho_{f}}\Phi\left(\frac{\gamma_{f}}{2\pi}\log c_{f}\right),

where the sum is over non-trivial zeros
\rho_{f}=1/2+i\gamma_{f}
of
L(s,f)
, and
c_{f}
is the analytic conductor of
L(s,f)
. Regarding the average behavior of
D(f;\Phi)
, Katz and Sarnak proposed the following
Density Conjecture

[
KS99a
]
[
KS99b
]
:

Conjecture 1.1

(Density Conjecture)
.

There exists a distribution
W_{\mathcal{F}}
on
\mathbb{R}
depending on
\mathcal{F}
, such that for any test function
\Phi
, we have

\lim_{|\mathcal{F}|\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}}D(f;\Phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(x)W_{\mathcal{F}}(x)dx.

Results in
[
KS99a
]
suggest that the distribution
W_{\mathcal{F}}
depends on
\mathcal{F}
through a symmetry group
G(\mathcal{F})
in Random Matrix Theory (RMT), and the distribution
W_{G(\mathcal{F})}
is exactly the one predicted by RMT. Thus
G(\mathcal{F})
is called the “symmetry type” of
\mathcal{F}
. For a list of possible symmetry types and their corresponding distributions, see
[
ILS00
]
.

The Density Conjecture
1.1
has been studied for many families
\mathcal{F}
. See
[
ILS00
,
Rub01
,
FI03
,
You06
,
CS07
,
CK15
,
ST16a
,
LM17
,
KWY20
,
BCL24
]
, to name a few. In all results mentioned above, either the support of
\hat{\Phi}
is restricted to a certain range, or the Ratio Conjecture for the corresponding
L
-functions is assumed.

Recently, weighted versions of the Density Conjecture
1.1
have attracted much attention. Let
\omega_{f}
be certain “weight” associated to
f\in\mathcal{F}
. Instead of studying the natural average of
D(f;\Phi)
, we consider the following
weighted
average

\lim_{|\mathcal{F}|\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\omega_{f}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\omega_{f}D(f;\Phi).

See
[
KST12
,
KR19
,
SS22
,
Faz24
,
BF24
,
Sug25
,
Zha25
]
for results in this direction. In particular, when
\omega_{f}=L(1/2,f)^{r}
is a power of central
L
-value, Fazzari proposed the following Weighted Density Conjecture
[
Faz24
, Conjecture 2.1]
:

Conjecture 1.2

(Weighted Density Conjecture)
.

Let
\{L(s,f)\}_{f\in\mathcal{F}}
be a family of
L
-functions with symmetry type
G=G(\mathcal{F})
, where
G\in\{\text{U},\text{Sp},\text{SO(even)}\}
. Let
r\geq 1
be a positive integer. Let
V(z)=|z|^{2}
if
G=U
, and
V(z)=z
otherwise. Then there exists a distribution
W_{G}^{r}
on
\mathbb{R}
depending on
G
and
r
, such that for any test function
\Phi
, we have

\lim_{|\mathcal{F}|\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}}V(L(1/2,f))^{r}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}}V(L(1/2,f))^{r}D(f;\Phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(x)W_{G}^{r}(x)dx.

Moreover,
W_{Sp}^{r}(x)
has the following explicit expression

W_{Sp}^{r}(x)=1-(2r+1)\frac{\sin(2\pi x)}{2\pi x}+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{r(r+1)}{2^{2j-2}\pi^{2j-1}}\frac{c_{j,r}}{2j-1}\frac{d^{2j-1}}{dx^{2j-1}}\left(\frac{1-\cos(2\pi x)}{2\pi x}\right),

where
c_{j,r}
is given by

c_{j,r}:=\frac{1}{j}\binom{r-1}{j-1}\binom{r+j}{j-1}.

The other two distributions can be recovered from
W_{Sp}^{r}
by the following relations

\displaystyle W_{SO(\text{even})}^{r}(x)=W_{Sp}^{r-1}(x)\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{and}\hskip 28.45274ptW_{U}^{r}(x)=\frac{W_{SO(\text{even})}^{r}(x)+W_{Sp}^{r}(x)}{2}.

Remark 1.3
.

A remarkable point of the above conjecture is that the weighted distributions
W_{G(\mathcal{F})}^{r}
depend
only
on the symmetry type
G
, and
not
on the underlying family
\mathcal{F}
. This can be viewed as further evidence of the Katz-Sarnak philosophy. However, as we shall discuss later, this universality is only supported by known results for
r=1
. Our results in this paper provide the first evidence for
r=2,3
.

Remark 1.4
.

Fazzari also considered RMT analogies in
[
Faz24
]
, and showed that the conjectural weighted distributions
W_{G}^{r}
are those coming from weighted low-lying eigenvalues of random matrices with symmetry type
G
, for small values of
r
.

1.2.
Main Results

In this paper we study low-lying zeros of standard
L
-functions of Hilbert modular forms, weighted by powers of central
L
-values. Note that the unweighted family has orthogonal symmetry type
[
ILS00
, Theorem 1.1]
[
LM17
, Theorem 1.1]
.

We proceed to describe our main results. Let
F
be a totally real number field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. We use
v
to denote a place of
F
. Let
\mathbf{k}=(k_{v})_{v|\infty}
be a weight vector, where each
k_{v}\geq 4
is even. Set
\|\mathbf{k}\|=\prod_{v|\infty}k_{v}
. Let
\mathfrak{q}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}
be either a prime ideal or the whole
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. Let
N(\mathfrak{q})=[\mathcal{O}_{F}:\mathfrak{q}]
be the absolute norm of
\mathfrak{q}
. Let
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})
be the set of cuspidal automorphic representations of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
of weight
\mathbf{k}
and exact level
\mathfrak{q}
with trivial central character. To each
\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})
denote by
L(s,\pi)
the standard
L
-function attached to
\pi
, and denote by
L(s,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})
the adjoint
L
-function of
\pi
. For a test function
\Phi
define the
1
-level density
D(\pi;\Phi)
as in (
1.1
) with
c_{\pi}=\|\mathbf{k}\|^{2}N(\mathfrak{q})
.

We study the average of
D(\pi;\Phi)
weighted by
L(1/2,\pi)^{r}
for
r=1,2,3
, where
\pi
runs through
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})
. For
r=1,2
we study the
joint
aspect as
\|\mathbf{k}\|N(\mathfrak{q})\to\infty
, and for
r=3
we study the
level
aspect as
N(\mathfrak{q})\to\infty
. To save notations we set
Q_{r}:=\|\mathbf{k}\|^{2}N(\mathfrak{q})
if
r=1,2
, and
Q_{r}:=N(\mathfrak{q})
if
r=3
. We also set
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{3}=1/2
and
\alpha_{2}=1/4
. Our first result is as follows.

Theorem A
.

Let
r=1,2,3
. Let
\Phi
be a test function with
\supp(\hat{\Phi})\subset(-\alpha_{r},\alpha_{r})
. Then we have

\lim_{Q_{r}\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}\frac{L(1/2,\pi)^{r}}{L(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})}}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}\frac{L(1/2,\pi)^{r}}{L(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})}D(\pi;\Phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(x)W_{r}(x)\,dx.

Here the distributions
W_{r}
are defined by

\displaystyle W_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\sin(2\pi x)}{2\pi x},

\displaystyle W_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\sin(2\pi x)}{2\pi x}+\frac{\cos(2\pi x)-1}{\pi^{2}x^{2}},

\displaystyle W_{3}(x)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\sin(2\pi x)}{2\pi x}-\frac{3(\cos(2\pi x)-1)}{\pi^{2}x^{2}}+\frac{6(\sin(2\pi x)-2\pi x)}{\pi^{3}x^{3}}+\frac{3(\cos(2\pi x)-1+2\pi^{2}x^{2})}{\pi^{4}x^{4}}.

Remark 1.6
.

Theorem
A
extends
[
KR19
, Theorem 1.1]
(in the case of
\chi
trivial) from
F=\mathbb{Q}
to any totally real number field
F
, and from
r=1
to
r=1,2,3
.

The harmonic weight
L(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})^{-1}
is at present because it naturally appears in the relative trace formula that we applied. We can remove it to get the following
purely

L(1/2,\pi)^{r}
-weighted result.

Theorem B
.

Let
r=1,2,3
. Let
\Phi
be a test function with
\supp(\hat{\Phi})\subset(-\alpha_{r},\alpha_{r})
. Then we have

\lim_{Q_{r}\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}{L(1/2,\pi)^{r}}}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}L(1/2,\pi)^{r}D(\pi;\Phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(x)W_{r}(x)\,dx.

Here the distributions
W_{r}
are defined as in Theorem
A
.

Remark 1.8
.

Note that our distributions
W_{r}
match the weighted distributions
W_{\text{SO(even)}}^{r}
in Conjecture
1.2
, for
r=1,2,3
. Thus Theorem
B
provides new evidence towards Conjecture
1.2
. Moreover, when
r>1
, combined with
[
Faz24
, Theorem 2.5]
, Theorem
B
provides the first evidence that the weighted distributions
W_{G(\mathcal{F})}^{r}
in Conjecture
1.2
depend
only
on the symmetry type
G=G(\mathcal{F})
, and not on the underlying family
\mathcal{F}
. Also note that when
r>1
, Theorem
B
is the first result towards Conjecture
1.2

without
assuming the Ratio Conjecture.

Remark 1.9
.

Although it is not surprising that removing the harmonic weight
L(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})^{-1}
would not change the symmetry type, Theorem
B
is the first result among all weighted low-lying zeros results that rigorously proves this fact.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}
be a fixed prime ideal with
\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{q}
. The proof of Theorem
A
relies on the following weighted equidistribution result of the
\mathfrak{p}^{th}
-Hecke eigenvalues
\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})
, which has its own interest. Equidistribution results of this type have seen extensive investigation, including works such as
[
Ser97
,
CDF97
,
ST16a
,
Li09
,

,

,

Theorem C
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}
be a prime ideal with
\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{q}
. Let
\phi
be a continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}
. Let
r=1,2,3
. Then we have

\lim_{Q_{r}\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}\frac{L(1/2,\pi)^{r}}{L(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})}}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}\frac{L(1/2,\pi)^{r}}{L(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})}\phi(\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}))=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)\,d\mu_{\mathfrak{p},r}(x).

Here the measure
\mu_{\mathfrak{p},r}
is defined by

\,d\mu_{\mathfrak{p},r}(x)=\left(1-\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})}\right)^{\frac{r(r-1)}{2}}\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{x}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{1/2}}+\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})}\right)^{r}}\,d\mu_{\infty}(x),

where
\mu_{\infty}
is the Sato-Tate measure defined by

d\mu_{\infty}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{1-\frac{x^{2}}{4}}dx&-2\leq x\leq 2\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Remark 1.11
.

Theorem
C
extends
[
KR19
, Theorem 1.5]
(in the case of
\chi
trivial) from
F=\mathbb{Q}
to any totally real number field
F
, and from
r=1
to
r=1,2,3
.

Like many other equidistribution results, removing the harmonic weight
L(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})^{-1}
would change the resulting measure. Explicitly, we have the following result.

Theorem D
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}
be a prime ideal with
\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{q}
. Let
\phi
be a continuous functions on

. Let

. Then we have

\lim_{Q_{r}\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}L(1/2,\pi)^{r}}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}L(1/2,\pi)^{r}\phi(\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}))=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)\,d\mu_{\mathfrak{p},r}^{un}(x).

Here the measures
\mu_{\mathfrak{p},r}^{un}
are defined by

\displaystyle\,d\mu_{\mathfrak{p},1}^{un}(x)

\displaystyle=(1-N(\mathfrak{p})^{-1})(1+N(\mathfrak{p})^{-1})^{2}\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{x}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{1/2}}+\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})}\right)^{2}\left(1+\frac{x}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{1/2}}+\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})}\right)}\,d\mu_{\infty}(x),

\displaystyle\,d\mu_{\mathfrak{p},2}^{un}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-N(\mathfrak{p})^{-2})^{3}}{(1+N(\mathfrak{p})^{-2})}\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{x}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{1/2}}+\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})}\right)^{3}\left(1+\frac{x}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{1/2}}+\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})}\right)}\,d\mu_{\infty}(x),

\displaystyle\,d\mu_{\mathfrak{p},3}^{un}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-N(\mathfrak{p})^{-1})^{5}(1+N(\mathfrak{p})^{-1})^{4}}{(1+N(\mathfrak{p})^{-1}+4N(\mathfrak{p})^{-2}+N(\mathfrak{p})^{-3}+N(\mathfrak{p})^{-4})}\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{x}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{1/2}}+\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})}\right)^{4}\left(1+\frac{x}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{1/2}}+\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})}\right)}\,d\mu_{\infty}(x).

1.3.
Main Conjectures

In proving the main results mentioned above, formulas of the
\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})
-weighted
r^{th}
-moments of
L(1/2,\pi)
play a central role. Here
\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}
is an integral ideal coprime to
\mathfrak{q}
. Such moment formulas are established for
r=1,2,3
in this paper. For
general

r\geq 1
it is also possible to apply the “recipe” in
[
CFK+05
]
to give conjectural formulas of the weighted
r^{th}
-moments of
L(1/2,\pi)
, and use these formulas to formulate conjectures on weighted low-lying zeros and on weighted equidistribution of
\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})
.

To state our conjectures let us introduce some more notations. For
r\geq 1,
set
\Delta(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
to be the vandermonde discriminant

\Delta(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $r=1$}\\
\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq r}(a_{j}-a_{i})&\mbox{if $r\geq 2$}.\end{cases}

For
n\geq 0,
define the function
h_{n}
to be, (or see §
4.2
)

(1.2)

h_{n}(x)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{n}\cos(2\pi xt)\,dt.

For
j\geq 0
we introduce

b_{r}(j)=\frac{(-1)^{r(r-1)/2}2^{r}}{r!(2\pi i)^{r}}\oint\cdots\oint\frac{\Delta(z_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{r}^{2})\Delta(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})(z_{1}^{j}+\cdots z_{r}^{j})}{z_{1}^{2r-1}\cdots z_{r}^{2r-1}}e^{z_{1}+\cdots+z_{r}}\,dz_{1}\cdots\,dz_{r},

where the integral is along small circles around
0
.

Using the notations above we formulate the following conjecture on weighted low-lying zeros.

Conjecture A
.

Let
r\geq 1
be a positive integer. Let
\Phi
be a test function with
\supp(\hat{\Phi})
sufficiently small. Then we have

\lim_{\|\mathbf{k}\|N(\mathfrak{q})\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}\frac{L(1/2,\pi)^{r}}{L(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})}}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}\frac{L(1/2,\pi)^{r}}{L(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})}D(\pi;\Phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(x)W_{r}(x)\,dx.

Here the distribution
W_{r}
is defined by

W_{r}(x)=1+h_{0}(x)-r\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{j}2^{j+1}b_{r}(j)}{j!b_{r}(0)}h_{j}(x).

Remark 1.13
.

In section §
5.3
, we will show that
W_{r}(x)=W_{SO(\text{even})}^{r}(x)
for
r=1,2,3,4
.

For a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}
with
\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{q}
and
r\geq 1
, we set

(1.3)

a(\mathfrak{p},r)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{2}\theta\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{2\cos\theta}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{1/2}}+\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})}\right)^{r}}\,d\theta.

We then formulate the following conjecture on weighted equidistribution of
\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})
.

Conjecture B
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}
be a prime ideal with
\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{q}
. Let
\phi
be a continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}
. Let
r\geq 1
be a positive integer. Then we have

\lim_{\|\mathbf{k}\|N(\mathfrak{q})\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}\frac{L(1/2,\pi)^{r}}{L(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})}}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}\frac{L(1/2,\pi)^{r}}{L(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})}\phi(\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}))=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)\,d\mu_{\mathfrak{p},r}(x).

Here the measure
\mu_{\mathfrak{p},r}
is given by:

\,d\mu_{\mathfrak{p},r}(x)=\frac{1}{a(\mathfrak{p},r)}\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{x}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{1/2}}+\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})}\right)^{r}}\,d\mu_{\infty}(x).

Remark 1.14
.

In §
5.4
, we will compute
a(\mathfrak{p},r)
explicitly, and show that they agree with Theorem
C
for
r=1,2,3
.

2.3.
Cubic Moment

In this subsection we fix
\mathbf{k}
and study
M_{3}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{n})
as
N(\mathfrak{q})
being large. We start with the following functional equation of
L(s,\pi)
:

\displaystyle\Lambda(s,\pi):=(D_{F}^{2}N(\mathfrak{q}))^{\frac{s}{2}}(2\pi)^{-d_{F}s}\prod_{v|\infty}\Gamma\left(s+\frac{k_{v}-1}{2}\right)L(s,\pi)=\varepsilon_{\pi}\Lambda(1-s,\pi).

Here
\varepsilon_{\pi}=\pm 1
is the root number. A standard approximate functional equation argument gives

(2.1)

L(1/2,\pi)=(1+\varepsilon_{\pi})\sum_{\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}}\frac{\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{m})}{N(\mathfrak{m})^{\frac{1}{2}}}V\left(\frac{N(\mathfrak{m})}{N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right),

where the function
V
is defined by

V(y):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}\prod_{v|\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s+k_{v}/2)}{\Gamma(k_{v}/2)}\left(\frac{(2\pi)^{d_{F}}y}{D_{F}}\right)^{-s}G(s)\frac{ds}{s}

for a choice of an even holomorphic function
G(s)
that is bounded in
-3\leq\sigma\leq 3
with
G(0)=1
. One can choose such a
G(s)
so that

V(y)\ll_{A}\left(1+\frac{y}{\|\mathbf{k}\|}\right)^{-A},\hskip 8.53581pty>0

for any
A>0
. By this bound we may assume the sum in (
2.1
) is taken over
\mathfrak{m}
such that
N(\mathfrak{m})\ll N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}
, up to an error of
N(\mathfrak{q})^{-100}
.

By (
2.1
) we write the cubic moment
M_{3}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{n})
as

M_{3}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{n})=2\sum_{N(\mathfrak{m})\ll N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{m})^{\frac{1}{2}}}V\left(\frac{N(\mathfrak{m})}{N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}\frac{L(1/2,\pi)^{2}}{L^{(\mathfrak{q})}(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{m})+O(N(\mathfrak{q})^{-10}N(\mathfrak{n})^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}),

where (and hence after in this subsection) the implied constants may depend on
\mathbf{k}
,
\varepsilon
and
F
.

Note that
N(\mathfrak{m})\ll N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}
implies
(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{q})=1
for
N(\mathfrak{q})
large enough, since
\mathfrak{q}
is prime. By the Hecke relation

\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{m})=\sum_{\mathfrak{c}|(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{m})}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{c}^{-2})

we can write

M_{3}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{n})=2\sum_{N(\mathfrak{m})\ll N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{m})^{\frac{1}{2}}}V\left(\frac{N(\mathfrak{m})}{N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\sum_{\mathfrak{c}|(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{m})}M_{2}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{c}^{-2})+O(N(\mathfrak{q})^{-10}N(\mathfrak{n})^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}).

Applying Proposition
2.2
to
M_{2}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{c}^{-2})
, we get

M_{2}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{c}^{-2})=C_{2}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})\frac{\tau(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{c}^{-2})}{N(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{c}^{-2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}(\log N(\mathfrak{q})-\log N(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{c}^{-2})+c_{0}(k))+O(N(\mathfrak{q})^{\varepsilon}N(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{c}^{-2})^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}).

Thus we have

\displaystyle M_{3}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{n})

\displaystyle=2C_{2}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})(\log N(\mathfrak{q})+c_{0}(k))\sum_{(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{q})=1}\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{m})^{\frac{1}{2}}}V\left(\frac{N(\mathfrak{m})}{N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\sum_{\mathfrak{c}|(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{m})}\frac{\tau(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{c}^{-2})}{N(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{c}^{-2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle-2C_{2}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})\sum_{(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{q})=1}\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{m})^{\frac{1}{2}}}V\left(\frac{N(\mathfrak{m})}{N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\sum_{\mathfrak{c}|(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{m})}\frac{\tau(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{c}^{-2})}{N(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{c}^{-2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\log N(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{c}^{-2})

\displaystyle+O(N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}N(\mathfrak{n})^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}).

We now set

\displaystyle S_{1}(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{n})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{q})=1}\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{m})^{\frac{1}{2}}}V\left(\frac{N(\mathfrak{m})}{N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\sum_{\mathfrak{c}|(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{m})}\frac{\tau(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{c}^{-2})}{N(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{c}^{-2})^{\frac{1}{2}}},

\displaystyle S_{2}(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{n})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{q})=1}\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{m})^{\frac{1}{2}}}V\left(\frac{N(\mathfrak{m})}{N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\sum_{\mathfrak{c}|(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{m})}\frac{\tau(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{c}^{-2})}{N(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{c}^{-2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\log N(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{c}^{-2})

and evaluate them. For
S_{1}(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{n})
we change the summation order and change variable
\mathfrak{m}\to\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{m}
to get

S_{1}(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{n})=\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{n})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{c}|\mathfrak{n}}\sum_{(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{q})=1}\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{m})}V\left(\frac{N(\mathfrak{m})N(\mathfrak{c})}{N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\tau(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{c}^{-1}\mathfrak{m}).

We cite the following property of the divisor function:

\tau(\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n})=\sum_{\mathfrak{d}|(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})}\mu(\mathfrak{d})\tau(\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{d}^{-1})\tau(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{d}^{-1}).

Applying this and changing variable
\mathfrak{m}\to\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{m}
, we have

S_{1}(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{n})=\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{n})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{c}|\mathfrak{n}}\sum_{\mathfrak{d}|\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{c}^{-1}}\frac{\mu(\mathfrak{d})\tau(\mathfrak{n}(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})^{-1})}{N(\mathfrak{d})}\sum_{(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{q})=1}\frac{\tau(\mathfrak{m})}{N(\mathfrak{m})}V\left(\frac{N(\mathfrak{m})N(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})}{N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right).

The inner summation over
\mathfrak{m}
is

\displaystyle\sum_{(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{q})=1}\frac{\tau(\mathfrak{m})}{N(\mathfrak{m})}V\left(\frac{N(\mathfrak{m})N(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})}{N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}\sum_{(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{q})=1}\frac{\tau(\mathfrak{m})}{N(\mathfrak{m})^{1+s}}\prod_{v|\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s+k_{v}/2)}{\Gamma(k_{v}/2)}\left(\frac{(2\pi)^{d_{F}}N(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})}{D_{F}N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)^{-s}G(s)\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}\zeta_{F}^{(\mathfrak{q})}(1+s)^{2}\prod_{v|\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s+k_{v}/2)}{\Gamma(k_{v}/2)}\left(\frac{(2\pi)^{d_{F}}N(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})}{D_{F}N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)^{-s}G(s)\frac{ds}{s}.

The integrand has a triple pole at
s=0
. We shift integral contour to
\sigma=-1
. It is directly computed that the residue at
s=0
is

\frac{\gamma_{-1}^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\log N(\mathfrak{q})-\log N(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})\right)^{2}+O(\log N(\mathfrak{q})N(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}))

and the shifted integral to
\sigma=-1
is

O(N(\mathfrak{q})^{-\frac{1}{2}}N(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})).

Thus we have the following formula for
S_{1}(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{n})
:

\displaystyle S_{1}(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{-1}^{2}}{2}\cdot N(\mathfrak{n})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\mathfrak{c}|\mathfrak{n}}\sum_{\mathfrak{d}|\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{c}^{-1}}\frac{\mu(\mathfrak{d})\tau(\mathfrak{n}(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})^{-1})}{N(\mathfrak{d})}\left(\frac{\log N(\mathfrak{q})}{2}-\log N(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+O\left(N(\mathfrak{n})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\mathfrak{c}|\mathfrak{n}}\sum_{\mathfrak{d}|\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{c}^{-1}}\frac{|\mu(\mathfrak{d})|\tau(\mathfrak{n}(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})^{-1})}{N(\mathfrak{d})}\log(N(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})N(\mathfrak{q}))\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+O\left(N(\mathfrak{q})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot N(\mathfrak{n})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\mathfrak{c}|\mathfrak{n}}N(\mathfrak{c})\sum_{\mathfrak{d}|\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{c}^{-1}}|\mu(\mathfrak{d})|\tau(\mathfrak{n}(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})^{-1})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{-1}^{2}}{2}\cdot N(\mathfrak{n})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\mathfrak{c}|\mathfrak{n}}\tau(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{c}^{-1})\left(\frac{\log N(\mathfrak{q})}{2}-\log N(\mathfrak{c})\right)^{2}\sum_{\mathfrak{d}|\mathfrak{c}}\frac{\mu(\mathfrak{d})}{N(\mathfrak{d})}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+O\left(\frac{\tau(\mathfrak{n})^{2}\log N(\mathfrak{q})}{N(\mathfrak{n})^{1/2}}\right)+O(N(\mathfrak{q})^{-1/2+\varepsilon}N(\mathfrak{n})^{1/2+\varepsilon})

Here we used the following bound

\sum_{\mathfrak{c}|\mathfrak{n}}\sum_{\mathfrak{d}|\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{c}^{-1}}\frac{|\mu(\mathfrak{d})|\tau(\mathfrak{n}(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})^{-1})}{N(\mathfrak{d})}\leq\tau(\mathfrak{n})^{2}.

Notice that the left hand side is multiplicative in
\mathfrak{n}
. Thus it suffices to investigate the case when
\mathfrak{n}
is a prime power.

We now analyze the term
S_{2}(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{n})
. By similar arguments we have

\displaystyle S_{2}(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{n})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{c}|\mathfrak{n}}\sum_{\mathfrak{d}|\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{c}^{-1}}\frac{\mu(\mathfrak{d})\tau(\mathfrak{n}(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})^{-1})}{N(\mathfrak{d})}\log N(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{c}^{-1}\mathfrak{d})\sum_{(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{q})=1}\frac{\tau(\mathfrak{m})}{N(\mathfrak{m})}V\left(\frac{N(\mathfrak{m})N(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})}{N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{n})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{c}|\mathfrak{n}}\sum_{\mathfrak{d}|\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{c}^{-1}}\frac{\mu(\mathfrak{d})\tau(\mathfrak{n}(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})^{-1})}{N(\mathfrak{d})}\sum_{(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{q})=1}\frac{\tau(\mathfrak{m})\log N(\mathfrak{m})}{N(\mathfrak{m})}V\left(\frac{N(\mathfrak{m})N(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})}{N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right).

\mathfrak{m}
in the first line was analyzed above. We now analyze the second sum over
\mathfrak{m}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{q})=1}\frac{\tau(\mathfrak{m})\log N(\mathfrak{m})}{N(\mathfrak{m})}V\left(\frac{N(\mathfrak{m})N(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})}{N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}\sum_{(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{q})=1}\frac{\tau(\mathfrak{m})\log N(\mathfrak{m})}{N(\mathfrak{m})^{1+s}}\prod_{v|\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s+k_{v}/2)}{\Gamma(k_{v}/2)}\left(\frac{(2\pi)^{d_{F}}N(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})}{D_{F}N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)^{-s}G(s)\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=\frac{-1}{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}(\zeta_{F}^{(\mathfrak{q})}(1+s)^{2})^{\prime}\prod_{v|\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s+k_{v}/2)}{\Gamma(k_{v}/2)}\left(\frac{(2\pi)^{d_{F}}N(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})}{D_{F}N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)^{-s}G(s)\frac{ds}{s}.

and pick up residue at

\displaystyle S_{2}(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{-1}^{2}}{3}\cdot N(\mathfrak{n})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\mathfrak{c}|\mathfrak{n}}\sum_{\mathfrak{d}|\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{c}^{-1}}\frac{\mu(\mathfrak{d})\tau(\mathfrak{n}(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})^{-1})}{N(\mathfrak{d})}\left(\frac{\log N(\mathfrak{q})}{2}-\log N(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})\right)^{3}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{\gamma_{-1}^{2}}{2}\cdot N(\mathfrak{n})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\mathfrak{c}|\mathfrak{n}}\sum_{\mathfrak{d}|\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{c}^{-1}}\frac{\mu(\mathfrak{d})\tau(\mathfrak{n}(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})^{-1})}{N(\mathfrak{d})}\log N(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{c}^{-1}\mathfrak{d})\left(\frac{\log N(\mathfrak{q})}{2}-\log N(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+O\left(N(\mathfrak{n})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\mathfrak{c}|\mathfrak{n}}\sum_{\mathfrak{d}|\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{c}^{-1}}\frac{|\mu(\mathfrak{d})|\tau(\mathfrak{n}(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})^{-1})}{N(\mathfrak{d})}\log^{2}(N(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})N(\mathfrak{q}))\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+O\left(N(\mathfrak{n})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\mathfrak{c}|\mathfrak{n}}\sum_{\mathfrak{d}|\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{c}^{-1}}\frac{|\mu(\mathfrak{d})|\tau(\mathfrak{n}(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})^{-1})}{N(\mathfrak{d})}\log N(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{c}^{-1}\mathfrak{d})\log(N(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})N(\mathfrak{q}))\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+O\left(N(\mathfrak{q})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot N(\mathfrak{n})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\mathfrak{c}|\mathfrak{n}}N(\mathfrak{c})\sum_{\mathfrak{d}|\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{c}^{-1}}|\mu(\mathfrak{d})|\tau(\mathfrak{n}(\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d})^{-1})\log N(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{c}^{-1}\mathfrak{d})\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{\gamma_{-1}^{2}}{2}\cdot N(\mathfrak{n})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\mathfrak{c}|\mathfrak{n}}\tau(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{c}^{-1})\log N(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{c}^{-1})\left(\frac{\log N(\mathfrak{q})}{2}-\log N(\mathfrak{c})\right)^{2}\sum_{\mathfrak{d}|\mathfrak{c}}\frac{\mu(\mathfrak{d})}{N(\mathfrak{d})}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+O\left(\frac{\log^{2}N(\mathfrak{q})\tau(\mathfrak{n})^{2}}{N(\mathfrak{n})^{1/2}}\right)+O(N(\mathfrak{q})^{-1/2+\varepsilon}N(\mathfrak{n})^{1/2+\varepsilon}).

Removing the Harmonic Weight

For
\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})

L^{(\mathfrak{q})}(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})=\omega_{\pi}(x)+\omega_{\pi}(x,y)+O((\|\mathbf{k}\|N(\mathfrak{q}))^{-2+\varepsilon})

for

\omega_{\pi}(x,y)
is defined by

\omega_{\pi}(x,y)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<N(\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{f}^{2})\leq y\\
(\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{q})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{l}^{2})}{N(\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{f}^{2})}

and
\omega_{\pi}(x)=\omega_{\pi}(0,x),
provided

Let
\{\alpha_{\pi}\}_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}
be a family of complex numbers. Then we can write

\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}\alpha_{\pi}=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}\frac{\alpha_{\pi}L^{(\mathfrak{q})}(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})}{L^{(\mathfrak{q})}(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})}=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}\frac{\omega_{\pi}(x)\alpha_{\pi}}{L^{(\mathfrak{q})}(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})}+\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}\frac{\omega_{\pi}(x,y)\alpha_{\pi}}{L^{(\mathfrak{q})}(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})}+O(1).

By the methods in
[
KM99
, Section 3]
, if we can show

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}\frac{|\alpha_{\pi}|}{L^{(\mathfrak{q})}(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})}&\ll(\|\mathbf{k}\|N(\mathfrak{q}))\log^{A}(\|\mathbf{k}\|N(\mathfrak{q}))\\
\max_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}\{\alpha_{\pi}\}&\ll(\|\mathbf{k}\|N(\mathfrak{q}))^{1-\eta}\\
\end{split}

for arbitrary

and some

then we have

(2.3)

\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}\frac{\omega_{\pi}(x,y)\alpha_{\pi}}{L^{(\mathfrak{q})}(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})}\ll(\|\mathbf{k}\|N(\mathfrak{q}))^{1-\eta^{\prime}}.

for some
\eta^{\prime}>0
that depends on
\eta
and

In particular, if we take

for a sufficiently small

, then
\eta^{\prime}
is dependent on
\eta
and
\varepsilon
. Therefore, we have

\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}\alpha_{\pi}=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}\frac{\omega_{\pi}(x)\alpha_{\pi}}{L^{(\mathfrak{q})}(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})}+O((\|\mathbf{k}\|N(\mathfrak{q}))^{1-\eta^{\prime}}).

3.
Weighted Equidistribution of
\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal distinct from
\mathfrak{q}
. Our goal of this section is to find measures
\mu_{\mathfrak{p},r}
and
\mu_{\mathfrak{p},r}^{un}
on
\mathbb{R}
(
r=1,2,3
) such that

\displaystyle\lim_{Q_{r}\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}\frac{L(1/2,\pi)^{r}}{L(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})}}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}\frac{L(1/2,\pi)^{r}}{L(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})}\phi(\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}))

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)\,d\mu_{\mathfrak{p},r}(x),

\displaystyle\lim_{Q_{r}\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}L(1/2,\pi)^{r}}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}L(1/2,\pi)^{r}\phi(\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}))

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)\,d\mu_{\mathfrak{p},r}^{un}(x),

for any continuous function
\phi
on
\mathbb{R}
. We will only prove the case
r=2
and
r=3
since the proof of the
r=1
case is much easier.

Note that we have the bound
|\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})|\leq 2

[
Bla06
]
. Thus by Weierstrass Approximation Theorem, it suffices to check for polynomial functions
\phi
. Further, by linearity it suffices to check for a family of polynomials that spans
\mathbb{R}[x]
as a
\mathbb{R}
-vector space. We choose the family of Chebyshev polynomials.

3.1.
Chebyshev Polynomials

The family of Chebyshev polynomials
\{T_{n}(x)\}_{n\geq 0}
is defined by the following recurrence relations:

\displaystyle T_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle T_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=x,

\displaystyle T_{n+1}(x)

\displaystyle=xT_{n}(x)-T_{n-1}(x),\hskip 8.53581ptn\geq 1.

The
n^{th}
-Chebyshev polynomial
T_{n}(x)
is of degree
n
.

Remark 3.1
.

Let
U_{n}(x)
be the usual Chebyshev polynomial defined by
U_{n}(\cos\theta)=\frac{\sin(n+1)\theta}{\sin\theta}.
Then
T_{n}(x)=U_{n}(x/2).

By comparing the above recurrence relation with the Hecke relation

\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}^{n+1})=\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})-\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}^{n-1})

for
\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})
, we see that
T_{n}(\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}))=\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})
.

Another useful property of Chebyshev polynomials is that they form an orthonormal family with respect to the Sato-Tate measure
\mu_{\infty}
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}}T_{n}(x)T_{m}(x)d\mu_{\infty}(x)=\delta_{m=n}.

This can be proved by a change of variable
x=2\cos\theta
and Remark
3.1
. This property can be used to prove the following lemma:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
r\geq 1
be a positive integer. Suppose that for all
\ell\geq 0,
we have

\lim_{Q_{r}\to\infty}\frac{M_{r}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})}{M_{r}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q},\mathcal{O}_{F})}=F_{\mathfrak{p},r,\ell}.

Then the desired measure
\mu_{\mathfrak{p},r}
in Theorem
C
can be given by

d\mu_{\mathfrak{p},r}(x)=\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}F_{\mathfrak{p},r,\ell}\cdot T_{\ell}(x)\right)d\mu_{\infty}(x).

Remark 3.3
.

This Lemma is also valid when
M_{r}
is replaced by
\mathcal{M}_{r}
, and
\mu_{\mathfrak{p},r}
is replaced by
\mu_{\mathfrak{p},r}^{un}
.

Proof.

We know that,
T_{\ell}(\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}))=\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell}).
Taking
\phi=T_{\ell}
to be Chebyshev polynomials, then

\displaystyle\lim_{Q_{r}\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}\frac{L(1/2,\pi)^{r}}{L(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})}}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q})}\frac{L(1/2,\pi)^{r}}{L(1,\pi,\operatorname{Ad})}T_{\ell}(\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}))=\lim_{Q_{r}\to\infty}\frac{M_{r}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})}{M_{r}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q},\mathcal{O}_{F})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}T_{\ell}(x)d\mu_{\mathfrak{p},r}(x),

for any fixed prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{q}
and
\ell\geq 0
.

If we expand these measures
\mu_{\mathfrak{p},r}
using
\{T_{\ell}(x)\}_{\ell\geq 0}
as a basis, we obtain:

(3.1)

d\mu_{\mathfrak{p},r}(x)=\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}F_{\mathfrak{p},r,\ell}\cdot T_{\ell}(x)\right)d\mu_{\infty}(x).

This completes the proof.
∎

Once we have computed
F_{\mathfrak{p},r.\ell}
for all
\ell\geq 0
, we can recover the measure
\mu_{\mathfrak{p},r}
by computing the series (
3.1
). For this purpose we prepare the following lemma:

Lemma 3.4
.

For
|x|<2
and
|t|<1
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}T_{\ell}(x)t^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t^{2}-xt+1},

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\ell\cdot T_{\ell}(x)t^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2t^{2}+xt}{(t^{2}-xt+1)^{2}},

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\ell^{2}\cdot T_{\ell}(x)t^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{4t^{4}-3xt^{3}+(x^{2}-4)t^{2}+xt}{(t^{2}-xt+1)^{3}},

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\ell^{3}\cdot T_{\ell}(x)t^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{t(8t-32t^{3}+8t^{5}-x+18t^{2}x-5t^{4}x-4tx^{2}+4t^{3}x^{2}-t^{2}x^{3})}{(t^{2}-xt+1)^{4}}.

Proof.

The first equation is standard. To get the rest we apply the relation: for
j\geq 0

t\frac{\,d}{\,dt}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\ell^{j}T_{\ell}(x)t^{\ell}\right)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\ell^{j+1}T_{\ell}(x)t^{\ell}.

∎

Let

on
\mathbb{R}

is restricted to a certain range, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{Q_{r}\to\infty}A_{r}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q};\Phi)=\lim_{Q_{r}\to\infty}B_{r}(\mathbf{k},\mathfrak{q};\Phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(x)W_{r}(x)dx.

We will only prove the case

4.1.
Explicit Formula

By a standard explicit formula argument (see, for example,
[
ILS00
]
), we have the following expression of

:

\displaystyle D(\pi;\Phi)

\displaystyle=\hat{\Phi}(0)+\frac{1}{2}\Phi(0)+O\left(\frac{\log\log 3N(\mathfrak{q})}{\log R}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\log R}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{q}}\frac{2\log N(\mathfrak{p})}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{\log N(\mathfrak{p})}{\log R}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\log R}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{q}}\frac{2\log N(\mathfrak{p})}{N(\mathfrak{p})}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}^{2})\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{2\log N(\mathfrak{p})}{\log R}\right).

Here we define
R:=\|\mathbf{k}\|^{2}N(\mathfrak{q})
and the implied constant depends on
\Phi
and
F
.

To deal with the summation over
\mathfrak{p}
we prepare the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
n\geq 1
be a positive integer. Let
\Phi
be a test function. Let
\mathfrak{N}
be a fixed square-free ideal and
R\geq 1
a large number. Then

(4.1)

\frac{1}{\log R}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{N}}\frac{(\log N(\mathfrak{p}))^{n}}{N(\mathfrak{p})}\widehat{\Phi}\left(\frac{\log N(\mathfrak{p})}{\log R}\right)=(\log R)^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{\Phi}(u)u^{n-1}\,du+O((\log R)^{n-2}).

Proof.

Denote by
\Pi(x)
the prime ideal counting function over
F
. Then

\Pi(x)=\frac{x}{\log x}+O\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}}\right)

for any
A>0.

Then summation by parts implies:

\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}\frac{(\log N(\mathfrak{p}))^{n}}{N(\mathfrak{p})}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{\log N(\mathfrak{p})}{\log R}\right)=-\int_{2}^{\infty}\Pi(x)\left(\frac{(\log x)^{n}}{x}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{\log x}{\log R}\right)\right)^{\prime}\,dx

By the asymptotic formula for
\Pi(x),
it suffices to investigate

\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{x}{\log x}\left(\frac{(\log x)^{n}}{x}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{\log x}{\log R}\right)\right)^{\prime}\,dx.

Integration by parts implies:

\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{x}{\log x}\left(\frac{(\log x)^{n}}{x}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{\log x}{\log R}\right)\right)^{\prime}\,dx=-\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{\log x-1}{(\log x)^{2}}\frac{(\log x)^{n}}{x}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{\log x}{\log R}\right)\,dx+O(1).

It suffices to study:

\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{\log x}{(\log x)^{2}}\frac{(\log x)^{n}}{x}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{\log x}{\log R}\right)\,dx=\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{(\log x)^{n-1}}{x}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{\log x}{\log R}\right)\,dx

Set
u=\frac{\log x}{\log R}
. Then
\,du=\frac{\,dx}{x\log R}
and

\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{(\log x)^{n-1}}{x}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{\log x}{\log R}\right)\,dx=(\log R)^{n}\int_{\frac{\log 2}{\log R}}^{\infty}\hat{\Phi}(u)u^{n-1}\,du=(\log R)^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\hat{\Phi}(u)u^{n-1}\,du+O(1).

This shows:

\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}\frac{(\log N(\mathfrak{p}))^{n}}{N(\mathfrak{p})}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{\log N(\mathfrak{p})}{\log R}\right)=(\log R)^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\hat{\Phi}(u)u^{n-1}\,du+O((\log R)^{n-1}),

which will imply (
4.1
).
∎

Finally, we prove the following lemma, which is helpful in removing harmonic weight.

Lemma 4.2
.

Set
R=\|\mathbf{k}\|^{2}N(\mathfrak{q})
. Then for some
A>0,
we have:

D(\pi;\Phi)\ll\log^{A}R.

This implies

(4.2)

\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{q}}\frac{2\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\log N(\mathfrak{p})}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{\log N(\mathfrak{p})}{\log R}\right)\ll\log^{A}R

for some
A>0.

Proof.

By the definition of
D(\pi;\Phi),
one has:

D(\pi;\Phi)\ll\sum_{|\gamma_{\pi}|\leq 1}\left|\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{\gamma_{\pi}\log\|\textbf{k}\|^{2}N(\mathfrak{q})}{2\pi}\right)\right|+\sum_{A\geq 1}\sum_{A\leq|\gamma_{\pi}|\leq A+1}\left|\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{\gamma_{\pi}\log\|\textbf{k}\|^{2}N(\mathfrak{q})}{2\pi}\right)\right|.

Then
[
IK04
, Proposition 5.7]
and the fact that
\hat{\Phi}
is a compactly supported function imply the bound.

Notice that in the explicit formula of
D(\pi;\Phi)
, all terms except for (

) are bounded by
\log^{A}R.
So (
4.2
) is valid.
∎

5.1.
The Weighted Moment Conjecture

In this section, we will propose the weighted moment conjecture, which is a generalization of the moment conjecture of
L

[
CFK+05
]
,
[
DGH03
]

Let
f\in H_{k}(1)
be a Hecke eigenform. We assume that

has a Fourier expansion:

f(z)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(m)m^{\frac{k-1}{2}}e(mz)\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{with $\lambda_{f}(1)=1$}.

Then for
m_{1},\ldots,m_{r}\geq 1
be integers. We introduce the notation:

(5.1)

\delta(m_{1},\ldots,m_{r})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{f\in H_{k}(1)}\frac{2\pi^{2}\lambda_{f}(m_{1})\cdots\lambda_{f}(m_{r})}{(k-1)L(1,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)}.

We can show:

Proposition 5.1
.

\delta(m_{1},\ldots,m_{r})
has the following properties:

(1)

\delta(1,m_{1},\ldots,m_{r})=\delta(m_{1},\ldots,m_{r}).

(2)

(multiplicativity) Suppose that
(m_{1}\cdots m_{r},m_{1}^{\prime}\cdots m_{r}^{\prime})=1,
then

\delta(m_{1}m_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,m_{r}m_{r}^{\prime})=\delta(m_{1},\ldots,m_{r})\delta(m_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,m_{r}^{\prime}).

(3)

Let
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{r}\geq 0
be integers, then

\delta(p^{\ell_{1}},\ldots,p^{\ell_{r}})=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{2}\theta\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{e^{i(\ell_{j}+1)\theta}-e^{-i(\ell_{j}+1)\theta}}{e^{i\theta}-e^{-i\theta}}\,d\theta=\int_{\mathbb{R}}T_{\ell_{1}}(x)\cdots T_{\ell_{r}}(x)\,d\mu_{\infty}(x).

Proof.

(1) and (2) can be checked by using the definition. (3) is Lemma 3.1.2 in
[
CFK+05
]
.
∎

Next, for
\ell\geq 1
and
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in\mathbb{C}^{r}
in a small enough neighborhood of
(0,\ldots,0)
. We define the function

\displaystyle A(\ell;z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})

\displaystyle=\prod_{p}A_{p}(\ord_{p}(\ell);z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})

with

A_{p}(m;z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq r}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{1+z_{i}+z_{j}}}\right)\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{2}\theta\frac{\sin((m+1)\theta)}{\sin\theta}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{2\cos\theta}{p^{1/2+z_{j}}}+\frac{1}{p^{1+2z_{j}}}\right)}\,d\theta.

This is absolutely convergent when
|\operatorname{Re}(z_{j})|<\frac{1}{2}.
Compare
A_{p}(0;0,\ldots,0)
with (
1.3
):

(5.2)

A_{p}(0;0,\ldots,0)=\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{\frac{r(r-1)}{2}}a(p,r).

Utilizing the method in
[
CFK+05
]
, we formula the following conjecture for the eignevalue-weighted moment conjecture:

Conjecture 5.2
.

Let
\ell\geq 1
and
r\geq 1
be integers. Set
X_{k}(s)=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-s+\frac{k}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(s-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{k}{2}\right)}.
Then

(5.3)

\sum_{f\in H_{k}(1)}\frac{2\pi^{2}\lambda_{f}(\ell)L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\right)^{r}}{(k-1)L(1,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)}=R_{k,r}(\ell)(1+O((\ell k)^{-\delta+\varepsilon}))

for some positive
\delta>0.
Here
R_{k,r}(\ell)
is given by the
r
-fold residue:

R_{k,r}(\ell)=\frac{(-1)^{r(r-1)/2}2^{r}}{r!}\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{r}}\oint\cdots\oint\frac{G(\ell;z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\Delta(z_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{r}^{2})^{2}}{z_{1}^{2r-1}\cdots z_{r}^{2r-1}}\prod_{j=1}^{r}X_{k}\left(\frac{1}{2}+z_{j}\right)^{-1/2}\,dz_{1}\cdots\,dz_{r},

where

G(\ell;z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})=A(\ell;z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq r}\zeta(1+z_{i}+z_{j}).

We will call
R_{k,r}(\ell)
the
main term
for the weighted moment conjecture.

Remark 5.3

Next, we will refine the main term
R_{k,r}(\ell)
in the weighted moment conjecture. Our main tool is the Laurent expansion of
A(\ell;z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})
. The following lemma shows that, all but finite terms in the Laurent expansion will vanish:

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
r\geq 1
be a fixed integer. Then for any integers
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\cdots\alpha_{r}\geq\frac{r(r-1)}{2}+1,
and holomorphic function
f
(around
(0,\ldots,0)
),

\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\oint\cdots\oint\frac{\Delta(z_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{r}^{2})\Delta(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{\alpha_{r}}}{z_{1}^{2r-1}\cdots z_{r}^{2r-1}}f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\,dz_{1}\cdots\,dz_{r}=0.

Proof.

Denote by
S_{r}
the permutation group of
\{0,\ldots,r-1\}
. Then by the permutation expansion,

\Delta(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{r}}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)z_{1}^{\sigma(0)}\cdots z_{r}^{\sigma(r-1)}.

This will imply:

\frac{\Delta(z_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{r}^{2})\Delta(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{\alpha_{r}}}{z_{1}^{2r-1}\cdots z_{r}^{2r-1}}=\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma\sigma^{\prime})\frac{z_{1}^{2\sigma(0)+\sigma^{\prime}(0)+\alpha_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{2\sigma(r-1)+\sigma^{\prime}(r-1)+\alpha_{r}}}{z_{1}^{2r-1}\cdots z_{r}^{2r-1}}.

Fix
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in S_{r}
. Then we claim that, if
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\cdots\alpha_{r}\geq\frac{r(r-1)}{2}+1,
then
2\sigma(j)+\sigma^{\prime}(j)+\alpha_{j}\geq 2r-1
for some
1\leq j\leq r.
Since we chose
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
arbitrarily, Cauchy’s integral theorem will imply that the integral vanishes.

We prove the claim by contradiction. Suppose not. Then
2\sigma(j)+\sigma^{\prime}(j)+\alpha_{j}\leq 2r-2
for all
1\leq j\leq r.
Then summing over all
j,

\sum_{j=1}^{r}2\sigma(j)+\sigma^{\prime}(j)+\alpha_{j}=\frac{3r(r-1)}{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}\leq r(2r-2).

This will force
\alpha_{1}+\cdots\alpha_{r}\leq\frac{r(r-1)}{2}.
A contradiction.
∎

Proof.

Here we only outline the proof, as it is an analogue of

a(p,r)=\begin{cases}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-\frac{r(r-1)}{2}}&\mbox{if $r\leq 3$}\\
\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{1-2r}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{r-1}\binom{r-1}{\ell}^{2}\frac{1}{p^{\ell}}-\sum_{\ell=0}^{r-3}\binom{r-3}{\ell}\binom{r+1}{\ell+2}\frac{1}{p^{\ell+1}}\right)&\mbox{if $r\geq 3$}\end{cases}

Proof.

The definition of
a(p,r)
implies:

a(p,r)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left(\frac{e^{i\theta}-e^{-i\theta}}{2i}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{e^{i\theta}}{p^{1/2}}\right)^{r}\left(1-\frac{e^{-i\theta}}{p^{1/2}}\right)^{r}}\,d\theta.

Then change of variable
z=e^{i\theta}
implies that,

a(p,r)=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\limits_{|z|=1}\left(z^{2}-2+\frac{1}{z^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{z}{p^{1/2}}\right)^{r}\left(1-\frac{1}{zp^{1/2}}\right)^{r}}\frac{\,dz}{z}.

Utilizing the Taylor expansion:

\frac{1}{(1-z)^{r}}=\sum\limits_{\ell\geq 0}\binom{r+\ell-1}{\ell}z^{\ell},

we obtain:

a(p,r)=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\limits_{|z|=1}\left(z-\frac{2}{z}+\frac{1}{z^{3}}\right)\left(\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\binom{r+\ell-1}{\ell}\left(\frac{z}{p^{1/2}}\right)^{\ell}\right)\left(\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\binom{r+\ell-1}{\ell}\left(\frac{1}{zp^{1/2}}\right)^{\ell}\right)\,dz.

Open the parentheses and apply Cauchy’s integral theorem,

a(p,r)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\binom{r+\ell-1}{\ell}^{2}\frac{1}{p^{\ell}}-\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\binom{r+\ell-1}{\ell}\binom{r+\ell+1}{\ell+2}\frac{1}{p^{\ell+1}}.

By the definition of hypergeometric function
{}_{2}F_{1}
(
[
GR07
, Equation 9.100]
), we obtain:

\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\binom{r+\ell-1}{\ell}^{2}\frac{1}{p^{\ell}}={}_{2}F_{1}(r,r;1,1/p).

and

\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\binom{r+\ell-1}{\ell}\binom{r+\ell+1}{\ell+2}\frac{1}{p^{\ell+1}}=\frac{r(r+1)}{2p}{}_{2}F_{1}(r,r+2;3;1/p).

This yields:

a(p,r)={}_{2}F_{1}(r,r;1,1/p)-\frac{r(r+1)}{2p}{}_{2}F_{1}(r,r+2;3;1/p).

Next, apply the Pfaff transformation (
[
GR07
, Equation 9.131(3)]
):

F(\alpha,\beta;\gamma;z)=(1-z)^{\gamma-\alpha-\beta}F(\gamma-\alpha,\gamma-\beta;\gamma;z),

and we obtain:

a(p,r)=\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{1-2r}\left\{{}_{2}F_{1}(1-r,1-r;1,1/p)-\frac{r(r+1)}{2p}{}_{2}F_{1}(3-r,1-r;3;1/p)\right\}.

By setting
r=1,2
and
3
, we can calculate
a(p,r)
directly. Therefore, we can assume that
r\geq 3.
Then by the definition of the hypergeometric function (
[
GR07
, Equation 9.100]
), we can show

{}_{2}F_{1}(1-r,1-r;1,1/p)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{r-1}\binom{r-1}{\ell}^{2}\frac{1}{p^{\ell}}

and

\frac{r(r+1)}{2p}{}_{2}F_{1}(3-r,1-r;3;1/p)=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\ell=0}^{r-3}\binom{r-3}{\ell}\binom{r+1}{\ell+2}\frac{1}{p^{\ell}}.

We conclude that, when
r\geq 3,

a(p,r)=\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{1-2r}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{r-1}\binom{n-1}{\ell}^{2}\frac{1}{p^{\ell}}-\sum_{\ell=0}^{r-3}\binom{r-3}{\ell}\binom{r+1}{\ell+2}\frac{1}{p^{\ell+1}}\right).

∎

Unitriangular factorization of holomorphic symplectic matrices
Gaofeng Huang, Frank Kutzschebauch, Phan Quoc Bao Tran

Abstract
. We prove that every holomorphic symplectic matrix can be factorized as a product of holomorphic unitriangular matrices with respect to the symplectic form [ 0 L n − L n 0 ] \left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&L_{n}\\ -L_{n}&0\end{array}\right] where L L is the n × n n\times n matrix with 1 1 along the skew-diagonal. Also we prove that holomorphic unitriangular matrices with respect to this symplectic form are products of not more than 7 7 holomorphic unitriangular matrices with respect to the standard symplectic form [ 0 I n − I n 0 ] \left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&I_{n}\\ -I_{n}&0\end{array}\right] , thus solving an open problem posed in [ HKS22 ] . Combining these two results allows for estimates of the optimal number of factors in the factorization by holomorphic unitriangular matrices with respect to the standard symplectic form. The existence of that factorization was obtained earlier by Ivarsson-Kutzschebauch and Schott, however without any estimates. Another byproduct of our results is a new, much less technical and more elegant proof of this factorization.

1.
Introduction

Factorization of a given matrix with elements in a field has been studied widely with many applications to different fields of sciences. The most well-known one is the factorization of a matrix
A\in\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
into product of some elementary matrices of the form
E+\alpha e_{ij}
,
i\neq j
, called elementary matrices. This factorization is equivalent to factorization into so-called unitriangular matrices (triangular matrices with 1s on the diagonal). Another interesting class of matrices is the class of symplectic matrices.
Generalizing the case of matrices with entries from a field, it is much more challenging to consider matrices with entries from a ring, the subject of
K
-theory. In this paper we always work
with very specific rings, namely the ring of holomorphic functions on a Stein space or the ring of continuous functions on a normal finite dimensional topological space. Recall that the unitriangular symplectic matrices with respect to the standard symplectic form

\displaystyle\Omega=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&I_{n}\\
-I_{n}&0\end{array}\right]

are of the following form

M^{-}(G)=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}I_{n}&0\\
G&I_{n}\end{array}\right]
and
M^{+}(G)=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}I_{n}&G\\
0&I_{n}\end{array}\right]
,
where
G
is symmetric.

Since the entries of
G
are holomorphic functions on a reduced Stein space, it can be understood as a holomorphic map:

G:X\to\mathbb{C}^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}
.

In
[
IKL22
]
(for
n=2
) and
[
Scho24
]
(for general
n
), the following theorem about factorizing matrices into product of unitriangular symplectic matrices has been proved:

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a natural
number
K=K(n,d)
such that given any finite dimensional reduced Stein space
X
of dimension

d
and any null-homotopic holomorphic map
f:X\to\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
there exists a holomorphic map:

G=(G_{1},\cdots,G_{K}):X\to(\mathbb{C}^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}})^{K}

such that

f(x)=M^{-}(G_{1}(x))M^{+}(G_{2}(x))\cdots M^{\pm}(G_{K}(x)),

where
x\in X
.

It is immediate that any product of unitriangular symplectic matrices is connected by a path to the constant identity matrix
I_{2n}
, by multiplying the off-diagonal entries by
t\in[0,1]
, i.e.

\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}I_{n}&tG\\
0&I_{n}\end{array}\right]
and
\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}I_{n}&0\\
tG&I_{n}\end{array}\right]
.

Therefore the requirement of null-homotopy of the map
f
is necessary.

In our paper, we study factorization of a holomorphic symplectic matrix into unitriangular symplectic factors with respect to the symplectic form

\tilde{\Omega}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&L_{n}\\
-L_{n}&0\end{array}\right]
, where
L
is the
n\times n
matrix with
1
along the skew-diagonal.

The lower unitriangular
\tilde{\Omega}
-symplectic factors are the
\Omega
-symplectic matrices of the form

\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}A^{-T}&0\\
B&A\end{array}\right],

BA^{T}
symmetric and
A
upper unitriangular,

In order to introduce convenient coordinates
(A,Z)\in\mathbb{C}^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\times\mathbb{C}^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}
on this set of matrices we will write them as

M^{-}(A,Z)=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}I_{n}&0\\
Z&I_{n}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}A^{-T}&0\\
0&A\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}A^{-T}&0\\
B&A\end{array}\right],

Z=BA^{T}
symmetric and
A
upper unitriangular.

The same for upper unitriangular

M^{+}(A,Z)=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}I_{n}&Z\\
0&I_{n}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}A^{-1}&0\\
0&A^{T}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}A^{-1}&C\\
0&A^{T}\end{array}\right],

Z=CA^{-T}
symmetric and
A
upper unitriangular.

Throughout the paper, we call these matrices the
elementary matrices
.
Our main theorem is a solution to the Gromov-Vaserstein problem for the symplectic form
\tilde{\Omega}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists a natural
number
K=K(n,d)
such that given any finite dimensional reduced Stein space
X
of dimension

d
and any null-homotopic holomorphic map
f:X\to\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
there exist holomorphic maps:

A=(A_{1},\cdots,A_{K}):X\to(\mathbb{C}^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}})^{K}
and
Z=(Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{K}):X\to(\mathbb{C}^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}})^{K}

such that

f(x)=M^{-}(A_{1}(x),Z_{1}(x))M^{+}(A_{2}(x),Z_{2}(x))\cdots M^{\pm}(A_{K}(x),Z_{K}(x)),

where
x\in X
.

This theorem together with our second main result, saying that the unitriangular
\tilde{\Omega}
-symplectic matrices from Theorem
1.2
are products of not more than 8
unitriangular
\Omega
-symplectic matrices, gives a new proof of Schott’s result. Moreover our proof is much more elegant and much easier to follow. In fact we use a novel strategy to prove the stratified ellipticity of a certain map, which is the core of the proof. More precisely, we can reduce the number of equations for the fibers of this map, given from the beginning by
2n

the stratified ellipticity is as easily obtained as in the work of Ivarsson and Kutzschebauch

. In Schott’s proof (and in the preceding work of Ivarsson-Kutzschebauch-Løw)
the reduction is only done from

[
IKL22
]

[
Scho24
]
.

We denote by

2n\times 2n

-symplectic matrices with holomorphic functions as entries in any

-symplectic factors. Observe that the optimal number of factors over the field of complex numbers is classically known in the

It is the fact that Jordan normal form is discontinuous making the problem for continuous and holomorphic matrices so delicate. However, we can reduce the problem to a simpler case by multiplying the unitriangular matrix

is diagonalizable and the diagonalization process is algebraic. From that point on we can apply the factorization technique in

For

For matrices of smaller sizes, there is a better upper bound

(1)

\displaystyle K_{symp}(n,d)\leq 4\tilde{K}_{symp}(n,d),\quad\text{ for }n=2\text{ and }n=3.

This theorem solves the problem posed in

Moreover, combining Theorem

]

we are in certain cases able to give explicit bounds for the number of factors in Schott’s theorem as follows:

.

The number of factors for

\displaystyle K_{symp}(N,d)\leq 7\tilde{K}_{symp}(n,d).

When the Stein space

\displaystyle 5

\displaystyle\leq K_{symp}(n,1)\leq 16\ \ \text{for}\ \ n=2,3,

\displaystyle 6

\displaystyle\leq K_{symp}(n,1)\leq 28\ \ \text{for }\ n\geq 4.

\displaystyle 5

\displaystyle 6

Strategy of the proof

We consider the product of

unitriangular symplectic matrices

\Psi_{K}=M^{-}(A_{1},Z_{1})M^{+}(A_{2},Z_{2})\cdots M^{\pm}(A_{K},Z_{K})

If we naturally identify the set of upper unitriangular matrices with the set
\mathbb{C}^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}
, and the set of symmetric matrices with the set
\mathbb{C}^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}

as a map

\Psi_{K}:(\mathbb{C}^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\times\mathbb{C}^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}})^{K}\to\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
.

Given a holomorphic map

from a finite dimensional Stein space
X
to the symplectic matrices, we need to find a holomorphic lift

such that the following diagram commutes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi_{K}}

It is very hard to study the map

directly, therefore we choose to consider its composition with the projection

of a

\displaystyle\Phi_{K}=\pi_{2n}\circ\Psi_{K}:(\mathbb{C}^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\times\mathbb{C}^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}})^{K}\to\mathbb{C}^{2n}\backslash\{0\}.

Proposition 1.5
.

The map
\Phi_{K}:(\mathbb{C}^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\times\mathbb{C}^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}})^{K}\setminus S_{K}\to\mathbb{C}^{2n}\backslash\{0\}
is a stratified elliptic submersion.

The continuous lift is constructed using the continuous factorization of symplectic matrices in

The proof of Proposition

2.
The Oka principle for stratified elliptic submersions

In this section, we recall the Oka principle that we will use as a crucial tool in our proof of Theorem
1.2
(see
[
For10
,
Gro89
]
), following the monograph of Franc Forstnerič
[
For17
]
.

Let
h\colon Z\to X
be a holomorphic submersion of a complex manifold
Z
onto a complex manifold
X
. For any
x\in X
the fiber over
x
of this submersion will be denoted by
Z_{x}
. At each point
z\in Z
the tangent space
T_{z}Z
contains the vertical tangent space
VT_{z}Z=\ker Dh
. For holomorphic vector bundles
p\colon E\to Z
we denote the zero element in the fiber
E_{z}
by
0_{z}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
and
Z
be complex spaces. A holomorphic map
h:Z\to X
is said to be a submersion if for each point
z_{0}\in Z
it is locally equivalent via a fiber preserving biholomorphic map to a projection
p\colon U\times V\to U
, where
U\subset X
is an open set containing
h(z_{0})
and
V
is an open set in some
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
h\colon Z\to X
be a holomorphic submersion of a complex manifold
Z
onto a complex manifold
X
. A fiber spray on
Z
associated with
h
is a triple
(E,p,s)
, where
p\colon E\to Z
is a holomorphic vector bundle and
s\colon E\to Z
is a holomorphic map such that for each
z\in Z
we have

(i)

s(E_{z})\subset Z_{h(z)}
,

(ii)

s(0_{z})=z
, and

(iii)

the derivative
Ds(0_{z})\colon T_{0_{z}}E\to T_{z}Z
maps the subspace
E_{z}\subset T_{0_{z}}E
surjectively onto the vertical tangent space
VT_{z}Z
.

Remark 2.3
.

One way of constructing dominating fiber sprays, as pointed out by Gromov, is to find finitely many
\mathbb{C}
-complete vector fields that are tangent to the fibers and span the tangent space of the fibers at all points in
Z
. One can then use the flows
\varphi_{j}^{t}
of these vector fields
V_{j}
to define
s\colon Z\times\mathbb{C}^{N}\to Z
via
s(z,t_{1},\dots,t_{N})=\varphi_{1}^{t_{1}}\circ\dots\circ\varphi_{N}^{t_{N}}(z)
which gives a spray.

Definition 2.4
.

We say that a holomorphic submersion
h:Z\to X
is
stratified elliptic
if there is a descending
chain of closed complex subspaces
X=X_{m}\supset\cdots\supset X_{0}
such that each stratum
Y_{k}=X_{k}\backslash X_{k-1}

is regular and the restricted submersion
h:Z|_{Y_{k}}\to Y_{k}
admits a dominating fiber spray over a small neighborhood of any point
x\in Y_{k}
.

Let

D_{t}(p,x)
are
(n-1)\times(n-1)
matrices, and the remaining maps are vectors of appropriate dimension. Consider the matrix

\tilde{f}_{t}(p,x)=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}A_{t}(p,x)&B_{t}(p,x)\\
C_{t}(p,x)&D_{t}(p,x)\end{array}\right]\in\mathrm{Sp}_{2n-2}(\mathbb{C})

At
t=0
we have from the continuous solution
\Psi_{L+2}(h_{0}(p,x))=f(p,x)
, which implies
\tilde{f}_{0}=I_{2n-2}
and thus the holomorphic map

\tilde{f}_{1}:P\times X\to\mathrm{Sp}_{2n-2}(\mathbb{C})

is null-homotopic. Let
i
be the canonical inclusion of
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n-2}(\mathbb{C})
into
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
given by

i(\tilde{f}_{1}(p,x))=i\left(\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}A_{t}(p,x)&B_{t}(p,x)\\
C_{t}(p,x)&D_{t}(p,x)\end{array}\right]\right)=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}A_{t}(p,x)&0&B_{t}(p,x)&0\\
0&1&0&0\\
C_{t}(p,x)&0&D_{t}(p,x)&0\\
0&0&0&1\end{array}\right]

By the induction hypothesis, we have

i(\tilde{f}_{1}(p,x)^{-1})=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\tilde{A}(p,x)&0&\tilde{B}(p,x)&0\\
0&1&0&0\\
\tilde{C}(p,x)&0&\tilde{D}(p,x)&0\\
0&0&0&1\end{array}\right]

is a finite product of holomorphic elementary symplectic matrices.

Then the matrix
\Psi_{L+2}(h_{1}(p,x))f(p,x)^{-1}i(\tilde{f}_{1}(p,x)^{-1})

\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}I_{n-1}&0&0&b_{1,1}(p,x)\\
a_{1}(p,x)\tilde{A}(p,x)+b_{2,1}(p,x)\tilde{C}(p,x)&1&a_{1}(p,x)\tilde{B}(p,x)+b_{2,1}(p,x)\tilde{D}(p,x)&b_{3,1}(p,x)\\
0&0&I_{n-1}&d_{1}(p,x)\\
0&0&0&1\end{array}\right]

is an elementary symplectic matrix, where

a_{1}(p,x)\tilde{A}(p,x)+b_{2,1}(p,x)\tilde{C}(p,x)=-d_{1}(p,x)^{T}

and

a_{1}(p,x)\tilde{B}(p,x)+b_{2,1}(p,x)\tilde{D}(p,x)=b_{1,1}(p,x)^{T}
.

This implies
f(p,x)
has a finite holomorphic symplectic factorization as required.
∎

4.
Stratified ellipticity of
\Phi_{K}

Recall that
S_{K}
denotes the singularity set of the map
\Phi_{K}
described in Proposition

. From
[

, Theorem 3.5]

Lemma 4.1
.

For

\Phi_{K}:(\mathbb{C}^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\times\mathbb{C}^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}})^{K}\setminus S_{K}\to\mathbb{C}^{2n}\setminus\{0\}
is surjective.

We start the proof of Proposition
3.3
, namely the stratified ellipticity of the submersion
\Phi_{K}
outside the singularity set

. The proof uses the method in
[
IK12
]
. Over each stratum of
\mathbb{C}^{2n}\setminus\{0\}
in an appropriate stratification, we are able to reduce the
2n
defining polynomial equations of the fibers of
\Phi_{K}
to one single polynomial equation

. It turns out that the polynomial

will be no more than linear in each of the variables
x_{i},i=1,\dots,m
. Therefore, the vector fields

\displaystyle V_{ij,p}=\frac{\partial p}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}-\frac{\partial p}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\quad 1\leq i<j\leq m

will be globally integrable
[

, Lemma 5.2]
and span the tangent space of the fiber
\{p=a\}
at all smooth points
[
IK12
, Lemma 5.3]
. The dominating fiber spray is then constructed as in Remark
2.3
.

Recall that

For
k
even,
M^{+}(A_{k},Z_{k})=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}I&Z_{k}\\
0&I\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}A^{-1}_{k}&0\\

and

For

\Phi_{K}((A_{1},Z_{1}),\cdots(A_{K},Z_{K}))=P^{K}=e^{T}_{2n}M^{-}(A_{1},Z_{1})M^{+}(A_{2},Z_{2})\cdots M^{-}(A_{K-1},Z_{K-1})M^{+}(A_{K},Z_{K})

Hence we have a recursive formula:

P^{K}=P^{K-1}M^{+}(A_{K},Z_{K})

Denote
P^{K}_{f}=(P^{K}_{1},\cdots,P^{K}_{n})
,
P^{K}_{s}=(P^{K}_{n+1},\cdots,P^{K}_{2n})
. Then the above equation can be writen as

P^{K}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}A_{K}&0\\
0&A^{-T}_{K}\end{array}\right]=P^{K-1}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}I_{n}&Z_{K}\\
0&I_{n}\end{array}\right]

and this is equivalent to

(10)

\begin{cases}P^{K}_{f}A_{K}=P^{K-1}_{f}\\
P^{K}_{s}A^{-T}_{K}=P^{K-1}_{s}+P^{K-1}_{f}Z_{K}\end{cases}

similarly we have

P^{K-1}=P^{K-2}M^{-}(A_{K-1},Z_{K-1})

which is equivalent to

P^{K-1}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}A^{T}_{K-1}&0\\
0&A^{-1}_{K-1}\end{array}\right]=P^{K-2}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}I_{n}&0\\
Z_{K-1}&I_{n}\end{array}\right]

and

(11)

\begin{cases}P^{K-1}_{f}A^{T}_{K-1}=P^{K-2}_{f}+P^{K-2}_{s}Z_{K-1}\\
P^{K-1}_{s}A^{-1}_{K-1}=P^{K-2}_{s}\end{cases}

We define the following stratification of
\mathbb{C}^{2n}\setminus\{0\}

\displaystyle G_{i}

\displaystyle=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}:a_{1}=\cdots=a_{i-1}=0,a_{i}\neq 0\},\quad i=1,\dots,n

\displaystyle N_{i}

\displaystyle=\{(0,b)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}:b_{n}=\cdots=b_{n-i+1}=0,b_{n-i}\neq 0\},\quad i=0,\dots,n-1

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(a,b)\in G_{i}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2n}\setminus\{0\},i=1,\dots,n
. Then the fiber
(P^{K})^{-1}(a,b)
given by the set of
2n
equations

can be described by one equation, namely
P^{K}_{i}=a_{i}
.

Proof.

Step 1: Observe that for
j>i

\displaystyle P^{K-1}_{j}=P^{K}_{f}A_{K}e_{j}=P^{K}_{j}+\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}a_{kj,K}P_{k}^{K}=P^{K}_{j}+a_{ij,K}P_{i}^{K}+\sum_{k=i+1}^{j-1}a_{kj,K}P_{k}^{K}.

Over
G_{i}
we have
P^{K}_{i}=a_{i}\neq 0
, thus we can use these equations to express
a_{ij,K}

\displaystyle P^{K-1}_{j}

\displaystyle=P^{K}_{j}+a_{ij,K}a_{i}+\sum_{k=i+1}^{j-1}a_{kj,K}P_{k}^{K},

\displaystyle\implies a_{ij,K}

\displaystyle=(P^{K-1}_{j}-P^{K}_{j}-\sum_{k=i+1}^{j-1}a_{kj,K}P_{k}^{K})/a_{i}\quad\text{for }j=i+1,\dots,n.

Step 2: We now use the second set of equations in (
10
) to express
n
variables in
Z_{K}
. Over
G_{i}
, we have
P^{K-1}_{i}=P^{K}_{i}=a_{i}\neq 0

By (

\alpha_{j}=\alpha e_{j}=P^{K-1}_{f}Z_{K}e_{j}=\sum^{n}_{l=1}P^{K-1}_{l}z_{lj,K}

When

z_{ij,K}=(\alpha_{j}-\sum^{n}_{l=1,l\neq i}z_{lj,K}P^{K-1}_{l})/a_{i}

and

(by symmetry of

)

and then we have

.

It remains to consider the following system of equations

\begin{cases}P^{K}_{1}=P^{K-1}_{1}=0\\
\quad\quad\,\vdots\\

Step 3: Now we use the first set of equation in (

For

\displaystyle=P^{K-1}_{j}+a_{i}a_{ji,K-1}+\sum^{n}_{k=j+1,k\neq i}P^{K-1}_{k}a_{jk,K-1}

\displaystyle\implies a_{ji,K-1}

\displaystyle=(\beta_{j}-P^{K-1}_{j}-\sum^{n}_{k=j+1,k\neq i}P^{K-1}_{k}a_{jk,K-1})/a_{i}.

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

5.
Optimal Factorization of elementary matrices

In this section we prove that the elementary matrices can be written as a product of unitriangular symplectic matrices with respect to the standard symplectic form. Most importantly, the number of factors is independent of the size of the matrices, i.e., independent of
n
.

Let
T^{+}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
(
T^{-}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
) denote the set of upper (respectively lower) triangular matrices in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, and
UT^{+}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
(
UT^{-}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
) the set of upper (resp. lower) unitriangular matrices in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Denote by
Sym_{n}(\mathbb{C})
the space of symmetric square matrices of size
n
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
A:X\to T^{\pm}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a holomorphic map such that the diagonal entries
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
are pairwise distinct constants. Then
A
is holomorphically diagonalizable by a unitriangular matrix, i.e., there exists a holomorphic map
K:X\to UT^{\pm}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
such that
A=K\Lambda K^{-1}
,
\Lambda=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
.

Proof.

Let us consider the case
A=(a_{ij})
is upper triangular.
Let
K
be an upper unitriangular matrix and
\alpha_{ij},1\leq i<j\leq n
the entries of
K
which are above the diagonal. The set of linear equations given by
AK=K\Lambda
can be uniquely solved for
\alpha_{i,i+1}
using the equation at position
(i,i+1)

a_{i,i+1}+\lambda_{i+1}\alpha_{i,i+1}=\lambda_{i}\alpha_{i,i+1}.

After having solved the first superdiagonal, continue with the second superdiagonal, and so on.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Given holomorphic maps
A:X\to UT^{+}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and
Z:X\to Sym_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, there exist holomorphic maps
Z_{i},\tilde{Z}_{i}:X\to Sym_{n}(\mathbb{C}),i=1,\dots,7
such that

\displaystyle M^{-}(A,Z)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
Z&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}A^{-T}&0\\
0&A\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
Z_{1}&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I&Z_{2}\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}\cdots\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
Z_{7}&I\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=M^{-}(Z_{1})M^{+}(Z_{2})\cdots M^{-}(Z_{7})\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle M^{+}(A,Z)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}I&Z\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}A^{-1}&0\\
0&A^{T}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}I&\tilde{Z}_{1}\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
\tilde{Z}_{2}&I\end{bmatrix}\cdots\begin{bmatrix}I&\tilde{Z}_{7}\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=M^{+}(\tilde{Z}_{1})M^{-}(\tilde{Z}_{2})\cdots M^{+}(\tilde{Z}_{7}).

Proof.

First we make the diagonal entries of
A
pairwise distinct by multiplying it with a constant diagonal matrix
\Lambda
with such property

\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}A&0\\
0&A^{-T}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Lambda&0\\
0&\Lambda^{-1}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Lambda^{-1}A&0\\
0&\Lambda A^{-T}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Lambda&0\\
0&\Lambda^{-1}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Lambda^{-1}A&0\\
0&(\Lambda^{-1}A)^{-T}\end{array}\right]
.

The constant-valued factor decomposes as

\begin{bmatrix}\Lambda&0\\
0&\Lambda^{-1}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
-\Lambda^{-1}&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I&\Lambda-I\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
I&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I&\Lambda^{-1}-I\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}.

By Lemma
5.1
there exists a holomorphic map
K:X\to UT^{+}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
so that
\Lambda^{-1}A=K\Lambda^{-1}K^{-1}
.
Write
P_{1}=KK^{T}
,
P_{2}=K^{-T}\Lambda K^{-1}
, then
\Lambda^{-1}A=P_{1}P_{2}
and
(\Lambda^{-1}A)^{-T}=P_{1}^{-1}P_{2}^{-1}
.
As in
[
JLX22
, Lemma 1]
, we can write the second factor, starting with an upper unitriangular matrix, as

\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}P_{1}P_{2}&0\\
0&P_{1}^{-1}P_{2}^{-1}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}I&-P_{1}\\
0&I\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}I&0\\
P_{1}^{-1}-P_{2}&I\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}I&P_{2}^{-1}\\
0&I\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}I&0\\
P_{2}P_{1}P_{2}-P_{2}&I\end{array}\right]
.

Combining these two factorizations, taking into account the lower triangular form of the first factor in
M^{-}
, gives a decomposition of
M^{-}
into
7
factors. For
M^{+}
observe that

\begin{bmatrix}\Lambda&0\\
0&\Lambda^{-1}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}I&\Lambda-I\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
I&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I&\Lambda^{-1}-I\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
-\Lambda&I\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{ and }

\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}P_{1}P_{2}&0\\
0&P_{1}^{-1}P_{2}^{-1}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}I&0\\
P_{1}^{-1}P_{2}^{-1}P_{1}^{-1}-P_{1}^{-1}&I\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}I&P_{1}\\
0&I\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}I&0\\
P_{2}-P_{1}^{-1}&I\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}I&-P_{2}^{-1}\\
0&I\end{array}\right]
. ∎

Remark 5.3

(a) When

as a product of elementary symplectic matrices with respect to the standard symplectic form is at least

1.
Introduction

Elliptic curves play a central role in modern number theory and algebraic geometry, with applications ranging from cryptography to the study of Diophantine equations. An elliptic curve
E
over a field

F
is a smooth projective variety of genus
1
, equipped with a distinguished point as the identity for an abelian group law defined on its rational points.

Over fields
F
of characteristic 0, such as

{\mathbb{Q}}
or number fields, elliptic curves
E/F
have been extensively studied due to their rich arithmetic properties, and significant attention has been given to the faithful mod-
N
Galois representation

\rho_{E/F,N}:\operatorname{Gal}\left(F\left(E\left[N\right]\right)/F\right)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}\right)

for a positive integer

N
.

Serre’s open image theorem (
[
9
]
) proves that for each non-CM elliptic curve
E
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, there exists a positive constant integer
M_{E}
, depending only on
E
, such that the image of
\rho_{E/{\mathbb{Q}},N}
is
\operatorname{GL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}\right)
for all
N
with
\gcd\left(N,M_{E}\right)=1
. Moreover, Serre conjectured the constants
M_{E}
can be uniformly bounded. This conjecture is known as Serre’s uniformity conjecture.
The possible representations of
\rho_{E/{\mathbb{Q}},\ell}
for primes
\ell
and elliptic curves defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
have been widely studied. For example, it is proven in
[
8
, Theorem 1]
that
\rho_{E/{\mathbb{Q}},\ell}
is irreducible for each prime
\ell>163
and any elliptic curve
E
defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. In
[
5
]
, the possible representations
\rho_{E/{\mathbb{Q}},\ell}
for non-CM elliptic curves
E
defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
are classified for each prime
\ell
. Furthermore, in the same work, the possible representations
\rho_{E/{\mathbb{Q}},\ell}
for CM elliptic curves
E
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
with endomorphism ring
\mathcal{O}
(over
{\mathbb{C}}
) are listed for each prime
\ell
.

Conversely, for each prime
\ell
and certain subgroups
H
of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}/\ell{\mathbb{Z}}\right)
,
[
13
]
provides equivalent conditions for the
j
-invariants of non-CM elliptic curves
E
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
such that the image of
\rho_{E/{\mathbb{Q}},\ell}
corresponds to
H
(up to conjugate).
In
[
7
]
, the Galois representations
\rho_{E/{\mathbb{Q}}\left(j\right),\ell^{n}}
is studied for each
j
, an CM elliptic curve
E
over
{\mathbb{Q}}\left(j\right)
, and a prime power
\ell^{n}
.

On the other hand, we consider the elliptic curve
E_{s,t}:y^{2}=x^{3}+sx+t
defined over

, where

and

are independent variables. Then, it is known (
[
3

or
[

(1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Gal}\left(F\left(s,t\right)\left(E_{s,t}\left[N\right]\right)/F\left(s,t\right)\right)\supseteq\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}\right)

for each integer
N\geq 2
.

If the characteristic of
F
is positive, say
\ell
, and if
\ell\nmid N
, then the inclusion (
1
) also holds by
[
1
, 6.8. Corollary 1]
: According to
[
1

, the toroidal compactification

of the moduli space of the pairs

of an elliptic curve and a basis

of

{\mathbb{Z}}\left[\zeta_{N},1/N\right]

is the moduli space of the pairs
\left(E,\mathcal{B}\right)
of elliptic curve over a field of characteristic

and a basis

of

with given Weil paring. Since this fiber is irreducible, for the covering

with a given Weil paring

While significant progress has been made in understanding mod-
N
Galois representations in characteristic
0
, the case of positive characteristic remains less explored in the literature. Most studies center on elliptic curves over characteristic
0

In this paper, we address a more challenging problem in the study of mod-
N
Galois representations, particularly when
N
is a power of the characteristic of the base field. More explicitly, we inverstigate the automorphism group of the normal field extension
K\left(s,t\right)\left(E_{s,t}\left[p^{n}\right]\right)
fixing
K\left(s,t\right)
, where the base field
K
has positive characteristic
p\geq 5
,

is a positive integer, and

is the elliptic curve

defined over the function field
K\left(s,t\right)
of two free variables

and

. Our main result is stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p\geq 5
. For the elliptic curve
E_{s,t}:y^{2}=x^{3}+sx+t
defined over
K\left(s,t\right)
, the automorphism group of
K\left(s,t\right)\left(E_{s,t}\left[p^{n}\right]\right)
fixing
K\left(s,t\right)
is isomorphic to
\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}
.

Theorem
1.1
should be understood in the context of the group structures of
p^{n}
-torsion parts of elliptic curves over a field of a positive characteristic.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over a field of characteristic
p\geq 2
. It is known that the
p
-torsion subgroup
E\left[p\right]
is trivial or isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
. We say that

is supersingular if
E\left[p\right]
is trivial, and ordinary if

is not trivial. Moreover, for an ordinary

and a positive integer

, we have
E\left[p^{n}\right]\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}
(
[
12
, Ch.III, Corollary 6.4 (c)]
). For ordinary
E
, we denote the automorphism group of the normal extension
K\left(E\left[p^{n}\right]\right)
over
K
(i.e., fixing
K
) by
\mathfrak{A}_{E/K}\left(n\right)
. Since an element of
\mathfrak{A}_{E/K}\left(n\right)
is determined by the image of a generator of
E\left[p^{n}\right]\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}
, it follows that
\mathfrak{A}_{E/K}\left(n\right)
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}
. More precisely, for a generator
P_{n}
of
E\left[p^{n}\right]
, the injective group homomorphism

(2)

\rho:\mathfrak{A}_{E/K}\left(n\right)\hookrightarrow\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}

satisfies
P_{n}^{\sigma}=\left[\rho\left(\sigma\right)\right]P_{n}
for all
\sigma\in\mathfrak{A}_{E/K}\left(n\right)
.

We can derive the following corollaries for elliptic curves defined over fields with positive characteristic:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
K
be a Hilbertian field of characteristic
p\geq 5
. Then, for each integer
n\geq 1
, there exist infinitely many elliptic curves

over
K
with distinct
j
-invariants such that
\mathfrak{A}_{E/K}\left(n\right)\cong\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}
.

We observe that if

is supersingular, then clearly
E(K)[p]
is trivial. However, as noted in Remark
2.2
and Lemma
2.3
(Section

), there are only finitely many
j
-invariants in

, each corresponding to a supersingular elliptic curve over

, making the following result particularly noteworthy:

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
K
be a Hilbertian field of characteristic
p\geq 5
. Then, there exist infinitely many ordinary elliptic curves

over
K
with distinct
j
-invariants such that
E\left(K\right)\left[p\right]
, and so
E\left(K\right)\left[p^{\infty}\right]
is trivial.

Remark 1.4
.

The concept of a Hilbertian field, Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem, and related topics are discussed in
[
10
, § 9]
and
[
11
, § 3]
. In essence, if
K
is Hilbertian, then Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem guarantees infinitely many points
\left(s,t\right)=\left(a,b\right)\in K\times K
such that
\mathfrak{A}_{E_{a,b}/K}\left(n\right)=\mathfrak{A}_{E_{s,t}/K\left(s,t\right)}\left(n\right)\cong\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}
by specialization. A more detailed explanation will be given in the proof of Corollary
1.2
at the end of Section
3
. An example of a Hilbertian field with positive characteristic is a global function field (
[
4
, Corollary 12.8]
).

Key tools used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
include the supersingular
j

2.1

To prove parts (b) and (c), it is enough to show that any prime divisor
Q\left(s,t\right)
of
a_{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(s,t\right)
divides
a_{\frac{p-1}{2}+pi}\left(s,t\right)
for each
1\leq i\leq\frac{p-3}{2}
, but not
a_{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}
, by considering (a). If
Q\left(s,t\right)=s
, then
a_{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(0,t\right)=0
. By Proposition
3.1
, it follows that
a_{\frac{p-1}{2}+pi}\left(0,t\right)=0
while
a_{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}\left(0,t\right)\neq 0
. These occur only when
s\mid a_{\frac{p-1}{2}+pi}\left(s,t\right)
and
s\nmid a_{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}\left(s,t\right)
. Similarly, if
Q\left(s,t\right)=t
, then
t\mid a_{\frac{p-1}{2}+pi}\left(s,t\right)
and
t\nmid a_{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}\left(s,t\right)
. For a
Q\left(s,t\right)
dividing
B\left(s^{3},t^{2}\right)
, we note that
q_{0}\left(u\right)
is irreducible over
K\left(s,t\right)
. Let
u_{0}\in K^{{\operatorname{alg}}}
be a zero of
q_{0}\left(u\right)
. If
u_{0}=0
, then
q_{0}\left(u\right)=u
, implying that
U=Q_{0}\left(U,V\right)
divides
B\left(U,V\right)
, which is a contradiction as we have already shown. Therefore,
u_{0}\neq 0
. Then, by considering the weights of
a_{\frac{p-1}{2}+pi}\left(s,t\right)
and
a_{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}\left(s,t\right)
, we deduce that for some integers
0\leq e\leq 2
and
0\leq e^{\prime}\leq 1
, the polynomials
\frac{a_{\frac{p-1}{2}+pi}\left(s,t\right)}{s^{e}t^{e^{\prime}}}
and
a_{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}\left(s,t\right)
are weight-homogeneous of weights divisible by
6
. Hence, for some non-negative integer
e^{\prime\prime}
, the polynomials
\frac{a_{\frac{p-1}{2}+pi}\left(s,t\right)}{s^{e}t^{e^{\prime}+2e^{\prime\prime}}}
and
\frac{a_{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}\left(s,t\right)}{t^{\frac{p^{2}-1}{4}}}
are weight-homogeneous in variable
s^{3}/t^{2}
of weight
0
. We denote these by
g\left(s^{3}/t^{2}\right)
and
h\left(s^{3}/t^{2}\right)
, respectively. Since
B\left(u_{0},u_{0}\right)=0
, we have
a_{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(u_{0},u_{0}\right)=0
from (
10
). By Proposition
3.1
, it follows that
u_{0}^{e+e^{\prime}+2e^{\prime\prime}}g\left(u_{0}\right)=a_{\frac{p-1}{2}+pi}\left(u_{0},u_{0}\right)=0
and
u_{0}^{\frac{p^{2}-1}{4}}h\left(u_{0}\right)=a_{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}\left(u_{0},u_{0}\right)\neq 0
. Since
u_{0}\neq 0
, we conclude that
g\left(u_{0}\right)=0
and
h\left(u_{0}\right)\neq 0
. As
q_{0}\left(u\right)
is irreducible,
q_{0}\left(u\right)
divides
g\left(u\right)
but not
h\left(u\right)
. Therefore,
Q\left(s,t\right)
divides
a_{\frac{p-1}{2}+pi}\left(s,t\right)
but not
a_{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}\left(s,t\right)
.
∎

We will need the following lemma to show the irreducibility of
\theta_{s,t}\left(X\right)
over

.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
D
be a Dedekind domain or a UFD,
P\subseteq D
be a prime ideal, and
D_{P}
be the localization of
D
at
P
. If the polynomial
f\left(x\right):=\sum_{i=0}^{n}r_{i}x^{n-i}\in D_{P}[x]
satisfy the conditions,

r_{0}\notin\left(PD_{P}\right)^{2},\quad r_{i}\in PD_{P}\text{ for all }0\leq i\leq n-1,\text{ and }r_{n}\notin PD_{P},

then,
f
is irreducible over the field of fraction of
D
.

Proof.

First, by Eisenstein’s criterion, the polynomial
\sum_{i=0}^{n}r_{i}x^{i}
is irreducible over
D_{P}
, and thus, so is
f
. Since
D_{P}
is a UFD,
f
is irreducible over the field of fractions of
D_{P}
, by Gauss’s lemma(
[
2
, § 9.Proposition 5]
). As the field of fractions of
D
is the same as that of
D_{P}
, the proof is completed.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

The polynomial
\theta_{s,t}\left(X\right)
obtained in Lemma
2.5
is irreducible over
K\left(s,t\right)
.

Proof.

Since
a_{\frac{p-1}{2}}
is not constant (as its weight is
\frac{p-1}{2}
), there exists a prime polynomial
Q\in K\left[s,t\right]
that divides
a_{\frac{p-1}{2}}
. By Corollary
3.4
, we have
Q\mid a_{\frac{p-1}{2}+pi}
for all
1\leq i\leq\frac{p-3}{2}
,
Q~\|~a_{\frac{p-1}{2}}
, and
Q\nmid a_{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}
. Thus, since
K\left[s,t\right]
is a UFD, Lemma
3.5
completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

The field extension
K\left(s,t\right)\left(x\left(E_{s,t}\left[p\right]\right)\right)
over
K\left(s,t\right)
has separable degree
\frac{p-1}{2}
and inseparable degree
p
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.5
and Proposition
3.6
, if
u_{1}
is a zero of
\overline{\psi_{p,s,t}}\left(x\right)=\theta_{s,t}\left(x^{p}\right)
, then, the extension
K\left(s,t\right)\left(u_{1}\right)/K\left(s,t\right)
has separable degree
\frac{p-1}{2}
and inseparable degree
p
. Moreover,
K\left(s,t\right)\left(x\left(E_{s,t}\left[p\right]\right)\right)=K\left(s,t\right)\left(u_{1}\right)
, since
E_{s,t}\left[p\right]
is generated by a point whose

-coordinate is
u_{1}
.
∎

Next, we consider the extensions
K\left(s,t\right)\left(x\left(E_{s,t}\left[p^{k+1}\right]\right)\right)/K\left(s,t\right)\left(x\left(E_{s,t}\left[p^{k}\right]\right)\right)
for a
k\geq 1
. To investigate this, we first review normalized discrete valuations on a field
F
. In this paper, a valuation refers to a normalized discrete valuation, which is a surjective group homomorphism
v:F^{\times}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
satisfying the property

v\left(a+b\right)\geq\min\left\{v\left(a\right),v\left(b\right)\right\}

for all
a,b\in F^{\times}
with
a+b\neq 0
. Given this property, it makes sense to define
v\left(0\right)=\infty
. For
a,b\in F
, one can derive the following useful equality:

(11)

v\left(a+b\right)=\min\left\{v\left(a\right),v\left(b\right)\right\},\text{ if }v\left(a\right)\neq v\left(b\right).

In our study of the extensions
K\left(s,t\right)\left(x\left(E_{s,t}\left[p^{k+1}\right]\right)\right)/K\left(s,t\right)\left(x\left(E_{s,t}\left[p^{k}\right]\right)\right)
, we focus on the valuations on the field of fractions of a Dedekind domain at a non-zero prime ideal.

In our setting, since
a_{\frac{p-1}{2}}
is not constant, there exists a prime divisor
Q
of
a_{\frac{p-1}{2}}
in
K\left[s,t\right]
. Then, we have that
Q\notin K\left[s\right]
or
Q\notin K\left[t\right]
. We let

(12)

R_{0}:=\begin{cases}\left(K\left(s\right)\right)\left[t\right],\text{ if }Q\notin K\left[s\right],\\
\left(K\left(t\right)\right)\left[s\right],\text{ if }Q\notin K\left[t\right]\text{.}\end{cases}

Note that both
\left(K\left(s\right)\right)\left[t\right]
and
\left(K\left(t\right)\right)\left[s\right]
are Dedekind domains. By Gauss’s lemma,
Q
is a prime element in
R_{0}
. The valuations to be used in the proof of the following Proposition
3.8
are those on extensions of the field of fractions of
R_{0}
at primes lying above the prime ideal
QR_{0}
.

Proposition 3.8
.

For a prime divisor
Q
of
a_{\frac{p-1}{2}}
in
K\left[s,t\right]
, let
R_{0}
be the Dedekind domain defined in (
12
) and
F_{0}
be the field of fractions of
R_{0}
. We choose a point
P_{1}\in E_{s,t}
of order
p
. Inductively, for each integer
k\geq 1
, we choose a point
P_{k+1}\in E_{s,t}
of order
p^{k+1}
such that
\left[p\right]P_{k+1}=P_{k}
. We let
u_{k}:=x\left(P_{k}\right)
,
F_{k}:=F_{0}\left(u_{k}\right)
, and
R_{k}
be the integral closure of
R_{0}
in
F_{k}
. By the definition of
R_{0}
in (
12
),
\mathfrak{Q}_{0}:=QR_{0}
is a prime ideal. Inductively, we choose a prime ideal
\mathfrak{Q}_{k}\subseteq R_{k}
lying above
\mathfrak{Q}_{k-1}
for each
k\geq 1
. Then, we have that for each integer
k\geq 1
, the valuation of
u_{k}
at
\mathfrak{Q}_{k}
is
-1
.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
k
, following the notations from the proofs of Lemma
2.5
and Lemma
2.6
. For
k=1
, let
v
denote the valuation on
F_{0}
at
\mathfrak{Q}_{0}
,
w
the valuation on
F_{1}
at
\mathfrak{Q}_{1}
. There exists a positive integer
e
such that
\left.w\right|_{F_{0}}=ev
. Since
u_{1}=x(P_{1})
is a zero of
\theta_{s,t}\left(x^{p}\right)
by Lemma
2.5
, we have

(13)

a_{\frac{p-1}{2}}u_{1}^{p\frac{p-1}{2}}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{p-3}{2}}a_{\frac{p-1}{2}+pi}u_{1}^{p\frac{p-1}{2}-pi}\right)+a_{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}=0.

By Corollary
3.4
, we see that

v\left(a_{\frac{p-1}{2}}\right)=1,\quad v\left(a_{\frac{p-1}{2}+pi}\right)\geq 1,\text{ and }v\left(a_{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}\right)=0.

Therefore, if
w\left(u_{1}\right)\geq 0
, then the only term with a non-positive
w
-valuation in the left hand side (LHS) of (
13
) is
a_{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}
. By the property (
11
), the
w
-valuation of LHS of (
13
) is
w\left(a_{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}\right)=0
, which contradicts
w\left(0\right)=\infty
. Hence,
w\left(u_{1}\right)<0
, equivalently,
w\left(u_{1}^{-1}\right)>0
. To compute
w\left(u_{1}^{-1}\right)
, we divide both sides of (
13
) by
u_{1}^{p\frac{p-1}{2}}
:

(14)

a_{\frac{p-1}{2}}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{p-3}{2}}a_{\frac{p-1}{2}+pi}u_{1}^{-pi}\right)+a_{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}u_{1}^{-p\frac{p-1}{2}}=0.

For each
1\leq i\leq\frac{p-3}{2}
, Corollary
3.4
shows that

w\left(a_{\frac{p-1}{2}+pi}u_{1}^{-pi}\right)>w\left(a_{\frac{p-1}{2}+pi}\right)\geq w\left(a_{\frac{p-1}{2}}\right).

If
w\left(a_{\frac{p-1}{2}}\right)\neq w\left(a_{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}u_{1}^{-p\frac{p-1}{2}}\right)
, then by the property (
11
), the
w
-valuation of LHS of (
14
) would be
\min\left\{w\left(a_{\frac{p-1}{2}}\right),w\left(a_{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}u_{1}^{-p\frac{p-1}{2}}\right)\right\}
, which again contradicts
w\left(0\right)=\infty
. Therefore, we must have
w\left(a_{\frac{p-1}{2}}\right)=w\left(a_{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}u_{1}^{-p\frac{p-1}{2}}\right)
. This implies
p\frac{p-1}{2}\mid e
,
as

e=ev\left(a_{\frac{p-1}{2}}\right)=w\left(a_{\frac{p-1}{2}}\right)=w\left(a_{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}u_{1}^{-p\frac{p-1}{2}}\right)=p\frac{p-1}{2}w\left(u_{1}^{-1}\right).

On the other hand,
e\leq\left[F_{1}:F_{0}\right]=p\frac{p-1}{2}
by Corollary
3.7
. Hence,
e=p\frac{p-1}{2}=\left[F_{1}:F_{0}\right]
and
w\left(u_{1}^{-1}\right)=1
.

Now, suppose the statement is true for some
k\geq 1
and we prove it for
k+1
. Since
P_{k}=\left[p\right]P_{k+1}
, by Lemma
2.6
we have

u_{k+1}^{p^{2}}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\left(b_{pi}+c_{pi-1}u_{k}\right)u_{k+1}^{p^{2}-pi}\right)+\left(b_{p^{2}}+c_{p^{2}-1}u_{k}\right)=0,

which can be rewritten as (noting that
u_{k}\neq 0
from
v\left(u_{k}^{-1}\right)=1
by the induction hypothesis):

(15)

u_{k}^{-1}u_{k+1}^{p^{2}}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\left(b_{pi}u_{k}^{-1}+c_{pi-1}\right)u_{k+1}^{p^{2}-pi}\right)+\left(b_{p^{2}}u_{k}^{-1}+c_{p^{2}-1}\right)=0.

Let
v
be the valuation on
F_{k}
at
\mathfrak{Q}_{k}
,
w
be the valuation on
F_{k+1}
at
\mathfrak{Q}_{k+1}
. There exists a positive integer
e
such that
\left.w\right|_{F_{k}}=ev
. For each
1\leq i\leq p-1
, we have
w\left(b_{pi}\right),w\left(b_{p^{2}}\right)\geq 0
since
b_{pi},b_{p^{2}}\in R_{0}
. By Lemma
2.6
and Corollary
3.4
(b), for each
1\leq i\leq p-1
, we have
w\left(c_{pi-1}\right)>0
. Since
c_{p^{2}-1}=-a_{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}^{2}
by Lemma
2.6
, Corollary
3.4
(c) implies that
w\left(c_{p^{2}-1}\right)=0
. Thus, we conclude

(16)

w\left(b_{pi}\right),w\left(b_{p^{2}}\right)\geq 0,w\left(c_{pi-1}\right)\geq 1,\text{ and }w\left(c_{p^{2}-1}\right)=0.

So, since
v\left(u_{k}^{-1}\right)=1
by the induction hypothesis, if
w\left(u_{k+1}\right)\geq 0
, the
w
-valuations of all terms except
c_{p^{2}-1}
in LHS of (
15
) are positive. Since
w\left(c_{p^{2}-1}\right)=0
, the property (
11
) implies the
w
-valuation of LHS of (
15
) is
0
, which contradicts
w\left(0\right)=\infty
. Therefore,
w\left(u_{k+1}^{-1}\right)>0
.

To compute
w\left(u_{k+1}^{-1}\right)
, we divide both sides of (
15
) by
u_{k+1}^{p^{2}}
:

(17)

u_{k}^{-1}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\left(b_{pi}u_{k}^{-1}+c_{pi-1}\right)u_{k+1}^{-pi}\right)+b_{p^{2}}u_{k}^{-1}u_{k+1}^{-p^{2}}+c_{p^{2}-1}u_{k+1}^{-p^{2}}=0.

Since
w\left(u_{k+1}^{-1}\right)>0
, and
v\left(u_{k}^{-1}\right)=1
, referring to (
16
) we find that

w\left(c_{pi-1}u_{k+1}^{-pi}\right)>w\left(c_{pi-1}\right)=ev\left(c_{pi-1}\right)\geq e=ev\left(u_{k}^{-1}\right)=w\left(u_{k}^{-1}\right),

and the
w
-valuations of
b_{pi}u_{k}^{-1}u_{k+1}^{-pi}
,
b_{p^{2}}u_{k}^{-1}u_{k+1}^{-p^{2}}
, and
c_{pi-1}u_{k+1}^{-pi}
exceed
w\left(u_{k}^{-1}\right)
. By (
11
), if
w\left(u_{k}^{-1}\right)\neq w\left(c_{p^{2}-1}u_{k+1}^{-p^{2}}\right)
, then the
w
-valuation of LHS of (
17
) is
\min\left\{w\left(u_{k}^{-1}\right),w\left(c_{p^{2}-1}u_{k+1}^{-p^{2}}\right)\right\}
, which again contradicts
w\left(0\right)=\infty
. Therefore,
w\left(u_{k}^{-1}\right)=w\left(c_{p^{2}-1}u_{k+1}^{-p^{2}}\right)
. This implies
p^{2}\mid e
, since

e=ev\left(u_{k}^{-1}\right)=w\left(u_{k}^{-1}\right)=w\left(c_{p^{2}-1}u_{k+1}^{-p^{2}}\right)=p^{2}w\left(u_{k+1}^{-1}\right).

On the other hand,
e\leq\left[F_{k+1}:F_{k}\right]\leq p^{2}
, since
F_{k+1}
is generated by
u_{k+1}
over
F_{k}
, and
u_{k+1}
is a zero of a polynomial
\eta_{s,t,u_{k}}\left(x^{p}\right)
of degree
p^{2}
defined over
F_{k}
by Lemma
2.6
. Hence, we have
e=\left[F_{k+1}:F_{k}\right]=p^{2}
and
w\left(u_{k+1}^{-1}\right)=1
.
∎

, the normal extension

and inseparable degree
p
.

Proof.

Following the notations of Proposition
3.8
, we note that the field
F_{k+1}
is generated by

over

and

is a zero of the polynomial

(18)

u_{k}^{-1}x^{p^{2}}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\left(b_{pi}u_{k}^{-1}+c_{pi-1}\right)x^{p^{2}-pi}\right)+\left(b_{p^{2}}u_{k}^{-1}+c_{p^{2}-1}\right)

of degree

in variable

with coefficients in the localization
\left(R_{k}\right)_{\mathfrak{Q}_{k}}
since the valuation of

at

, along with the proof of Proposition

, and the definitions of the coefficients

, shows that the conditions in Lemma

for polynomial (

. Hence, we conclude that (

.

If the inseparable degree of the extension

is

, then
\eta_{s,t,u_{k}}\left(x^{p}\right)
has only one zero. However, since

is ordinary and

has distinct

zeros, the extension

has separable degree
p
and inseparable degree
p
. Moreover,
K\left(s,t\right)\left(x\left(E_{s,t}\left[p^{k}\right]\right)\right)=F_{k}
and
K\left(s,t\right)\left(x\left(E_{s,t}\left[p^{k+1}\right]\right)\right)=F_{k+1}
, since

and
E_{s,t}\left[p^{k+1}\right]
are generated by

and
P_{k+1}
, respectively.
∎

Finally, we compute the separable and inseparable degrees of the extension
K\left(s,t\right)\left(E_{s,t}\left[p^{n}\right]\right)
over
K\left(s,t\right)
, and its automorphism group.

Proposition 3.10
.

For each integer
n\geq 1
, the normal extension
K\left(s,t\right)\left(E_{s,t}\left[p^{n}\right]\right)
over
K\left(s,t\right)
has separable degree
p^{n-1}\left(p-1\right)
and inseparable degree
p^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
P_{n}=\left(x_{n},y_{n}\right)
be a generator of
E_{s,t}\left[p^{n}\right]\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}
. Corollary
3.7
and Corollary
3.9
prove that
K\left(s,t\right)\left(x_{n}\right)
over
K\left(s,t\right)
has inseparable degree
p^{n}
and separable degree
\frac{p-1}{2}p^{n-1}
. We show that
\left[K\left(s,t\right)\left(P_{n}\right):K\left(s,t\right)\left(x_{n}\right)\right]=2
. Then, the extension
K\left(s,t\right)\left(E_{s,t}\left[p^{n}\right]\right)/K\left(s,t\right)
has inseparable degree
p^{n}
and separable extension
p^{n-1}\left(p-1\right)
.

We suppose that
K\left(s,t\right)\left(P_{n}\right)=K\left(s,t\right)\left(x_{n}\right)
. It is equivalent to that

y_{n}\in K\left(s,t\right)\left(x_{n}\right).

Since the normal extension
K\left(s,t\right)\left(P_{n}\right)
over
K\left(s,t\right)
has separable degree
\frac{p-1}{2}p^{n-1}
, and
\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}
has the unique subgroup
\left(\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}\right)^{2}
of order
\frac{p-1}{2}p^{n-1}
, we have
\mathfrak{A}_{E_{s,t}/K\left(s,t\right)}\left(n\right)\cong\left(\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}\right)^{2}
. Moreover, we conclude that
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, since otherwise
-1\in\left(\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}\right)^{2}
, which implies that there exists an automorphism
\sigma\in\mathfrak{A}_{E_{s,t}/K\left(s,t\right)}\left(n\right)
that fixes
x_{n}
but not
y_{n}
, so
y_{n}\notin K\left(s,t\right)\left(x_{n}\right)
.

Since the quadratic twist
E_{s^{3},s^{3}t}
of
E_{s,t}
by
s
is also a specialization of
E_{s,t}
at
(s^{3},s^{3}t)
, we see that
\mathfrak{A}_{E_{s^{3},s^{3}t}/K\left(s,t\right)}\left(n\right)\subseteq\left(\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}\right)^{2}
. This can be deduced as follows: if we consider
E_{s,t}
as an elliptic curve defined over the field
\left(K\left(s_{*},t_{*}\right)\right)\left(s,t\right)
for two variables
s_{*}
and
t_{*}
, then

\mathfrak{A}_{E_{s^{3},s^{3}t}/K\left(s,t\right)}\left(n\right)=\mathfrak{A}_{E_{s_{*}^{3},s_{*}^{3}t_{*}}/K\left(s_{*},t_{*}\right)}\left(n\right)\subseteq\mathfrak{A}_{E_{s,t}/\left(K\left(s_{*},t_{*}\right)\right)\left(s,t\right)}\left(n\right)=\mathfrak{A}_{E_{s,t}/K\left(s,t\right)}\left(n\right),

since
E_{s_{*}^{3},s_{*}^{3}t_{*}}
is a specialization of
E_{s,t}
.
Since
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, for any
r\in\left(\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}\right)^{2}
,
r\equiv\pm 1\pmod{p^{n}}
implies
r\equiv 1\pmod{p^{n}}
. In other words, recalling that
\left(sx_{n},\sqrt{s^{3}}y_{n}\right)
is a generator of
E_{s^{3}s^{3}t}\left[p^{n}\right]
, any automorphism in
\mathfrak{A}_{E_{s^{3},s^{3}t}/K\left(s,t\right)}\left(n\right)
fixing
sx_{n}
must fix
\sqrt{s^{3}}y_{n}
, which implies that
\sqrt{s^{3}}y_{n}\in K\left(s,t\right)\left(sx_{n}\right)=K\left(s,t\right)\left(x_{n}\right)
and
\sqrt{s}\in K\left(s,t\right)\left(x_{n}\right)
. Similarly, considering the quadratic twist of
E_{s,t}
by
t
, we conclude
\sqrt{t}\in K\left(s,t\right)\left(x_{n}\right)
. Hence, there exists a biquadratic subextension
K\left(\sqrt{s},\sqrt{t}\right)
of
K\left(s,t\right)\left(x_{n}\right)=K\left(s,t\right)\left(P_{n}\right)
over
K(s,t)
, which contradicts that the automorphism group
\mathfrak{A}_{E_{s,t}/K\left(s,t\right)}\left(n\right)
is isomorphic to a cyclic group
\left(\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}\right)^{2}
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.1
, from which we prove Corollary
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Proposition
3.10
, the normal extension
K\left(s,t\right)\left(E_{s,t}\left[p^{n}\right]\right)
over
K(s,t)
has separable degree
p^{n-1}\left(p-1\right)
, which is exactly the order of
\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}
, and the automorphism group
\mathfrak{A}_{E_{s,t}/K\left(s,t\right)}\left(n\right)
is a subgroup of
\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}
. Therefore,
\mathfrak{A}_{E_{s,t}/K\left(s,t\right)}\left(n\right)\cong\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

First, for a variable
u
, we show that the automorphism group of the splitting field of
\overline{\psi_{p^{n},u,u}}
over
K\left(u\right)
is isomorphic to the automorphism group
\left(\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}\right)^{2}
of the splitting field of
\overline{\psi_{p^{n},s,t}}\left(x\right)
over
K\left(s,t\right)
. Since
\overline{\psi_{p^{n},s,t}}\left(x\right)
is weight-homogeneous of weight
\frac{p^{2n}-1}{2}
by (
5
), we have
\overline{\psi_{p^{n},s^{3}/t^{2},s^{3}/t^{2}}}\left(x\right)=\left(\frac{s}{t}\right)^{\frac{p^{2n}-1}{2}}\overline{\psi_{p^{n},s,t}}\left(\frac{t}{s}x\right)
. Hence, the splitting fields of
\overline{\psi_{p^{n},s^{3}/t^{2},s^{3}/t^{2}}}
and
\overline{\psi_{p^{n},s,t}}
over
K\left(s,t\right)
are the same. Moreover,
K\left(s,t\right)=K\left(s^{3}/t^{2},t\right)
and two fields
K\left(s^{3}/t^{2}\right)
and
K\left(t\right)
are linearly disjoint over
K
, and hence, the automorphism group of the splitting field
\overline{\psi_{p^{n},s^{3}/t^{2},s^{3}/t^{2}}}
over
K\left(s^{3}/t^{2}\right)
is isomorphic to
\left(\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}\right)^{2}
. Replacing
s^{3}/t^{2}
by
u
, the automorphism group of the splitting field
\overline{\psi_{p^{n},u,u}}
over
K\left(u\right)
is isomorphic to
\left(\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}\right)^{2}
.

By Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem (
[
11
, Proposition 3.3.5]
and
[
6
, Remark 2.4]
), there exists infinitely many distinct
u_{0}\in K
such that the autmomorphism group of the splitting field
K\left(x\left(E_{u_{0},u_{0}}\left[p^{n}\right]\right)\right)
of the polynomial
\overline{\psi_{p^{n},u_{0},u_{0}}}
over
K
is isomorphic to the autmomorphism group
\left(\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}\right)^{2}
of the splitting field
K\left(x\left(E_{u,u}\left[p^{n}\right]\right)\right)
of
\overline{\psi_{p^{n},u,u}}
over
K\left(u\right)
.

For a generator
\left(x_{n},y_{n}\right)
of
E_{u_{0},u_{0}}\left[p^{n}\right]
, if
y_{n}\notin K\left(x_{n}\right)
, then
\mathfrak{A}_{E_{u_{0},u_{0}}/K}\left(n\right)\cong\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}
by a similar argument as in the proof of Proposition
3.10
.

If
y_{n}\in K\left(x_{n}\right)
, we can find a
\delta\in K
such that
\mathfrak{A}_{E_{\delta^{2}u_{0},\delta^{3}u_{0}}/K}\left(n\right)\cong\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}
, by using Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem. This theorem guarantees the existence of infinitely many distinct
\delta\in\left({\mathbb{A}}^{1}\setminus\left\{0\right\}\right)\left(K\right)
(up to square) such that
\sqrt{\delta}\notin K
. Hence, for some such
\delta
, we have
\sqrt{\delta}\notin K\left(x_{n}\right)
, meaning that two extension fields
K\left(x_{n}\right)
and
K\left(\sqrt{\delta}\right)
are linearly disjoint over
K
. Consequently, the separable degree of the extension
K\left(x_{n},\sqrt{\delta}\right)
is equal to the order of
\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}
. Furthermore,
K\left(x_{n},\sqrt{\delta}\right)=K\left(E_{\delta^{2}u_{0},\delta^{3}u_{0}}\left[p^{n}\right]\right)
, since
E_{\delta^{2}u_{0},\delta^{3}u_{0}}\left[p^{n}\right]
is generated by
\left(\delta x_{n},\sqrt{\delta^{3}}y_{n}\right)
. In conclusion,
\mathfrak{A}_{E_{\delta^{2}u_{0},\delta^{3}u_{0}}}\left(n\right)\cong\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}
. Since the
j
-invariant of
E_{\delta^{2}u_{0},\delta^{3}u_{0}}
is
\frac{1728u_{0}}{u_{0}+27/4}
which is determined uniquely by
u_{0}
, there are infinitely many distinct elliptic curves
E/K
defined over
K
such that
\mathfrak{A}_{E/K}\left(n\right)\cong\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}
with distinct
j
-invariants.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

For an elliptic curve
E/K
, if
E\left(K\right)\left[p\right]\neq 0
, then
E\left(K\right)\left[p\right]\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
, so
K\left(E\left[p\right]\right)=K
. Therefore,
\mathfrak{A}_{E/K}\left(1\right)
is trivial.

By Corollary
1.2
, there exist infinitely many elliptic curves
E/K
with distinct
j
-invariants such that
\mathfrak{A}_{E/K}\left(1\right)\cong\left({\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}
, which implies that
E\left(K\right)\left[p\right]=0
from the above. Since there are only finitely many supersingular
j
-invariants by Lemma
2.3
, this completes the proof.
∎

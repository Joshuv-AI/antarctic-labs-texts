Fractional Sobolev spaces related to an ultraparabolic operator
Antonello Pesce, Sascha Portaro

Abstract
We propose a functional framework of fractional Sobolev spaces for a class of ultra-parabolic Kolmogorov type operators satisfying the weak Hörmander condition. We characterize these spaces as real interpolation of natural order intrinic Sobolev spaces recently introduced in [ 27 ] , and prove continuous embeddings into L p L^{p} and intrinsic Hölder spaces from [ 24 ] . These embeddings naturally extend the standard Euclidean ones, coherently with the homogeneous structure of the associated Kolmogorov group. Our approach to interpolation is based on approximation of intrinsically regular functions, the latter heavily relying on integral estimates of the intrinsic Taylor remainder. The embeddings exploit the aforementioned interpolation property and the corresponding embeddings of natural order intrinsic spaces.



Keywords
: Sobolev embeddings, interpolation inequalities, Fokker-Planck equations, weak Hörmander condition, Besov spaces, intrinsic Hölder spaces

Introduction

We are interested in developing a framework of fractional Sobolev spaces for the study of evolution operators of the following type

{\mathcal{K}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}^{2}-Y,\quad Y=\langle Bx,\nabla\rangle+\partial_{t},\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N},

(1.1)

where
1\leqslant d\leqslant N
and
B
is a constant
N\times N
matrix. If
d=N
then
{\mathcal{K}}
is a parabolic operator while in general, for
d<N
,
\mathcal{K}
is degenerate, and not uniformly parabolic. Nonetheless,
{\mathcal{K}}
is a hypoelliptic operator under the Hörmander condition

\text{\rm rank Lie}(\partial_{x_{1}},\dots,\partial_{x_{d}},Y)=N+1.

(1.2)

This kind of PDEs arise in many linear and non-linear models in physics: for instance, for
N=2
and
d=1
, equation (
1.1
) is the Fokker-Planck equation for the kinetic system with Hamiltonian function

H(x_{1},x_{2})=V(x_{1},x_{2})+{|x_{1}|^{2}}/{2}
, where
V
is a linear potential, and
{|x_{1}|^{2}}/{2}
corresponds to the kinetic energy of a particle with unit mass (see
[
7
]
,
[
11
]
).
In mathematical finance, equation (
1.1
) is related to the model used to price path-dependent contracts, such as Asian options (see, for instance,
[
3
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
25
]
). For a survey of the classic theory for Kolmogorov equations see
[
2
]
.

It’s well known that, under suitable assumptions on the structure of the matrix
B
, equation (
1.1
) endows
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
with a structure of homogeneous Lie group (see Section
1.1
below), thus it is crucial to measure regularity of functions
in terms of the group structure when dealing with differential problems related to operators of this class. In
[
29
]
a suitable notion of Hölder space was introduced, later extended in
[
24
]
. Notions of
BMO
and
VMO
spaces have been considered in
[
22
]
; Sobolev and Sobolev-Morrey spaces of order
k=2
have been introduced in
[
4
]
,
[
30
]
, and recently extended for
k\in\mathbb{N}
in
[
27
]
.

Sobolev, Hardy and Besov spaces have been studied in the context of general Lie groups since the late seventies and eighties. When the manifold is endowed with a Riemannian structure, properties of these spaces, such as embedding and interpolation theorems, as well as algebra properties are well understood: we can refer, for instance, to
[
32
]
and the monographs
[
33
]
,
[
16
]
and the references therein.
A theory of Sobolev and Besov spaces has also been recently developed on Lie groups endowed with a sub-Riemannian structure, both for compact and non-compact manifolds: see
[
12
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
. On the other hand, to the best of our knowledge, there are no general resources in the literature for operators that cannot be interpreted as a sub-Laplacian, such as (
1.1
).

In the remainder of this section, we introduce our notion of fractional Sobolev space
\Lambda^{n+s}_{p,q,B}
and present the main results of the paper: Theorem
1.6
, which provides a characterization in terms of
real interpolation
of the natural order Sobolev spaces introduced in
[
27
]
, and Theorem
1.7
, which establishes embeddings into
L^{p}
and the intrinsic Hölder spaces from
[
24
]
.
In the Euclidean setting these spaces correspond to a positive order Besov spaces, which reduce to Sobolev-Slobodeckij spaces when
p=q
.
In Section
2
we collect some important preliminary tools and show a crucial integral estimate of the intrinsic Taylor remainder. Section
3
contains the proof of our interpolation result, which is based on the approximation procedure and estimates of Proposition
3.1
. Eventually in Section
4
we prove our embeddings result.

1.1
Intrinsic vs anisotropic Hölder and Sobolev spaces

We recall (see
[
17
]
) that Hörmander’s condition (
1.2
) is equivalent to the following one: on a certain basis of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
the matrix
B
takes the block-form

B=\begin{pmatrix}\ast&\ast&\cdots&\ast&\ast\\
B_{1}&\ast&\cdots&\ast&\ast\\
0&B_{2}&\cdots&\ast&\ast\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&B_{r}&\ast\end{pmatrix}

(1.3)

where
B_{j}
is a
(d_{j-1}\times d_{j})
-matrix of rank
d_{j}
with

d\equiv d_{0}\geqslant d_{1}\geqslant\dots\geqslant d_{r}\geqslant 1,\qquad\sum_{j=0}^{r}d_{j}=N.

(1.4)

In general, the
\ast
-blocks in (
1.3
) are arbitrary. Throughout the paper we assume the following

Assumption 1.1
.

B
is a constant
d\times d
matrix as in (
1.3
), where each
\ast
-block is null.

Assumption
1.1
is equivalent (cf.
[
17
]
) to the fact that the kinetic Fokker-Planck operator (
1.1
) is homogeneous of degree two with respect to the family of dilations defined as follows: first of
all, consistently with the block decomposition (
1.3
) of
B
, we write
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
as the
direct sum
x=x^{[0]}+\cdots+x^{[r]}
where
x^{[i]}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is defined as

x_{k}^{[i]}=\begin{cases}x_{k}&\text{ if }\,\bar{d}_{i-1}<k\leqslant\bar{d}_{i},\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\qquad\bar{d}_{i}:=\sum_{j=0}^{i}d_{j},\quad\bar{d}_{-1}=0,\quad i=0,\dots,r.

Then, we have
{\mathcal{K}}(u(D_{\lambda}))=\lambda^{2}({\mathcal{K}}u)(D_{\lambda})
where

D_{\lambda}(t,x):=(\lambda^{2}t,\hat{D}_{\lambda}x),\qquad\hat{D}_{\lambda}x:=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\lambda^{2i+1}x^{[i]}.

(1.5)

Then the
D_{\lambda}
-homogeneous norm on
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
is defined as

\|(t,x)\|_{B}=|t|^{\frac{1}{2}}+|x|_{B},\qquad|x|_{B}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}|x^{[i]}|^{\frac{1}{2i+1}}.

(1.6)

Anisotropic
functional spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, defined w.r.t the component
|\cdot|_{B}
of the seminorm, have been used since the seminal paper
[
19
]
, when dealing with solutions of the relevant differential problem that are intended to be distributional w.r.t. the variable
t
, and do not benefit from the time-smoothing effect that is typical of the parabolic semigroup. Anisotropic Hölder and Besov spaces (weighted and not) and their embeddings have been extensively exploited in many analytic applications, see for instance
[
19
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
34
]
,
[
15
]
and many others.

From a functional analytic standpoint such spaces are easy to define and use, since they only require to scale the order of regularity of each set of variables
x^{[i]}
by the appropriate factor
\frac{1}{2i+1}
, and the associated vector fields
\partial_{x_{1}},\dots,\partial_{x_{N}}
trivially commute. Therefore, the vast array of functional analytic tools available for Euclidian spaces (interpolation and duality properties, embeddings, etc.) is directly inferred for anisotropic ones. For instance, in the case
d=1
,
N=2
, the anisotropic Hölder space of exponent
\alpha
would consist of functions satisfying

|f(x)-f(\xi)|\lesssim|x_{1}-\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}+|x_{2}-\xi_{2}|^{\frac{\alpha}{3}},\quad x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(1.7)

A different approach to the construction of functional spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is based on space convolutions with the associated semigroup, see for instance
[
14
]
,
[
13
]
.

If we were to include the time variable in order to fully exploit the smoothing effect of the equation in the
N+1
variables, regularity in space and time becomes strictly intertwined. Indeed, the operator
{\mathcal{K}}
would no longer be invariant w.r.t. Euclidean translations, but the left translations of the group
(\mathbb{R}^{N+1},\circ)
, where the non-commutative group law is defined by

(t,x)\circ(s,{\xi})=\ell_{(t,x)}(s,{\xi}):=(s+t,{\xi}+e^{sB}x),\qquad(t,x),\ (s,{\xi})\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}.

(1.8)

Precisely,
\ell_{(t,x)}\circ{\mathcal{K}}={\mathcal{K}}\circ\ell_{(t,x)}
for any
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
.
Notice that
(\mathbb{R}^{N+1},\circ)
is a group with identity
\text{Id}=(0,0)
and inverse
(t,x)^{-1}=(-t,{-}e^{-tB}x)
, thus
the matrix
B
uniquely identifies an
homogeneous Lie group

{\mathcal{G}}_{B}:=(\mathbb{R}^{N+1},\circ,D(\lambda))
. For instance, in the previous example with

B=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}

we would have the
intrinsic
Hölder space

|f(t,x)-f(s,{\xi})|\lesssim\|(s,{\xi})^{-1}\circ(t,x)\|^{\alpha}_{B}\sim|t-s|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}+|x_{1}-{\xi}_{1}|^{\alpha}+|x_{2}-{\xi}_{2}-(t-s){\xi}_{1}|^{\frac{\alpha}{3}}.

(1.9)

Notice that for points that are on the same time level, the increments are controlled as for (
1.7
); otherwise, as opposed to the standard parabolic case, the regularity in the Euclidean directions is somehow entangled due to the group law associated to
B
. This fact, together with the non commutativity of the fields
\partial_{x_{1}},\dots,\partial_{x_{d}},Y
makes it difficult to build a general functional framework.

Hölder spaces defined as (
1.9
) were first used in
[
29
]
, where the author introduced the notion of solution in the
Lie
sense, which regards
Y
as a directional derivative. Intrinsic spaces associated to
{\bf d}_{B}
are effective in the study of Kolmogorov equations as they comply with the asymptotic behaviour of the semigroup
\Gamma
of
{\mathcal{K}}
near the pole. Indeed, it holds that

\Gamma(s,\xi;t,x)=\Gamma(0,0;(s,\xi)^{-1}\circ(t,x)),\qquad s<t.

The qualitative difference between even and odd regularity indexes makes it difficult to generalize (
1.9
) to higher orders, and for some time, only the intrinsic
C^{0,\alpha}
and different notions of
C^{2,\alpha}
spaces were introduced (see
[
24
]
, Section 3.2 for a review of the intrinsic Hölder spaces in the literature). Eventually in
[
24
]
a general definition of
C^{n,\alpha}
was established: owning to an estimate of the intrinsic Taylor remainder, it turns out that it is equivalent to set a notion of regularity by means of the intrinsic distance
or to set the appropriate regularities with respect to the Hörmander vector fields
\{\partial_{x_{1}},\dots,\partial_{x_{d}},Y\}
, according to their
formal degree
. For instance, condition (
1.9
) is equivalent to set
u\in C^{\alpha}
along the direction
x_{1}
and
u\in C^{\alpha/2}
along the integral curves of the vector field
Y
. In the following paragraphs, we recall the general intrinsic Hölder spaces which are relevant for our embeddings, and introduce the fractional Sobolev spaces.

Let
h\mapsto e^{hX}z
denote the integral curve of a Lipschitz vector field
X
starting from

z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, defined as the unique solution of

\begin{cases}\frac{d}{dh}e^{hX}z=X\left(e^{hX}z\right),\qquad&h\in\mathbb{R},\\
e^{hX}z|_{h=0}=z.\end{cases}

(1.10)

For the vector fields in
{\mathcal{K}}
, we have

e^{h\partial_{x_{i}}}(t,x)=(t,x+h\mathbf{e}_{i}),\qquad e^{hY}(t,x)=(t+h,e^{hB}x),

(1.11)

where
\mathbf{e}_{i}
is the
i
-th element of the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. A simple computation shows that, for any
z,{\zeta}\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
,

{\zeta}^{-1}\circ e^{\delta Y}z=e^{\delta Y}({\zeta}^{-1}\circ z),\qquad{\zeta}^{-1}\circ e^{\delta\partial_{x_{i}}}z=e^{\delta\partial_{x_{i}}}({\zeta}^{-1}\circ z),\qquad i=1,\dots,d.

(1.12)

Moreover, we have (see, for instance,
[
28
]
)

D_{\lambda}e^{\delta Y}(z)=e^{\delta\lambda^{2}Y}\left(D_{\lambda}z\right),\qquad D_{\lambda}e^{\delta\partial_{x_{i}}}(z)=e^{\delta\lambda^{2j+1}\partial_{x_{i}}}D_{\lambda}z,\qquad i=\bar{d}_{j-1}+1,\dots,\bar{d}_{j}.

(1.13)

Definition 1.2
.

Let
m_{X}
be a formal weight associated to the vector field
X
. For
\alpha\in\,]0,m_{X}]
, we say
that
u\in C_{X}^{\alpha}
if the quasi-norm

\|u\|_{C^{\alpha}_{X}}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\atop h\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\left|u\left(e^{hX}z\right)-u(z)\right|}{|h|^{\frac{\alpha}{m_{X}}}}

(1.14)

is finite.

Hereafter, we set
the formal weight of the vector fields
\partial_{x_{1}},\dots,\partial_{x_{d}}
equal
to one and the formal weight of
Y
equal to two
, which is coherent with the homogeneity of the
Fokker-Planck operator
{\mathcal{K}}
with respect to the dilations
D_{\lambda}
in (
1.5
). From

[
24
]
we recall the following

Definition 1.3

(
Intrinsic Hölder spaces).

For
\alpha\in\,]0,1]
we define the Hölder quasi-norms

\displaystyle\left\|{u}\right\|_{C^{0,\alpha}_{B}}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|u|+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left\|{u}\right\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\partial_{x_{i}}}}+\left\|{u}\right\|_{C^{\alpha}_{Y}},

(1.15)

\displaystyle\left\|{u}\right\|_{C^{1,\alpha}_{B}}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|u|+\left\|{\nabla_{d}u}\right\|_{C^{0,\alpha}_{B}}+\left\|{u}\right\|_{C^{\alpha+1}_{Y}},

(1.16)

where
\nabla_{d}:=(\partial_{x_{1}},\dots,\partial_{x_{d}})
and inductively, for
n\geqslant 2
,

\displaystyle\left\|{u}\right\|_{C^{n,\alpha}_{B}}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|u|+\left\|{\nabla_{d}u}\right\|_{C^{n-1,\alpha}_{B}}+\left\|{Yu}\right\|_{C^{n-2,\alpha}_{B}}.

(1.17)

Following this approach, it is natural to introduce a notion of
intrinsic Besov spaces
which are essentially built by replacing the sup norms in (
1.14
) with
L^{p}
and
L^{q}
norms w.r.t.
z
and
h
respectively, and is coherent with the work
[
23
]
on fractional Sobolev-Slobodeckij spaces of order
s\in(0,1)
associated to a Hörmander structure. More precisely, for
p,q\geqslant 1
, we set the fractional Besov quasi-norm of order
s
,
0<s<m_{X}
along a vector field
X

{[u]_{X,s,p,q}:=\begin{cases}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|u(e^{hX}\cdot)-u\|^{q}_{{p}}\frac{dh}{|h|^{1+q\frac{s}{m_{X}}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},&q\in[1,\infty)\\
\sup_{h\neq 0}\frac{\|u(e^{hX}\cdot)-u\|_{{p}}}{|h|^{\frac{s}{m_{X}}}},&q=\infty.\end{cases}}

(1.18)

Then for
p,q\geqslant 1
and
s\in(0,1)
we set

\displaystyle|u|_{s,p,q}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}[u]_{\partial_{x_{i}},s,p,q}+[u]_{Y,s,p,q},

(1.19)

\displaystyle|u|_{1+s,p,q}

\displaystyle:=|\nabla_{d}u|_{s,p,q}+[u]_{Y,1+s,p,q},

(1.20)

and inductively, for
n\geqslant 2
,

\displaystyle|u|_{n+s,p,q}

\displaystyle:=|\nabla_{d}u|_{n+s-1,p,q}+|Yu|_{n+s-2,p,q},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},\;n\geqslant 2

(1.21)

Hereafter we will denote
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.

Definition 1.4

(
Intrinsic Besov spaces).

For
p,q\geqslant 1
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
s\in(0,1)
, we define the Besov
\Lambda^{n+s}_{p,q,B}
as the space of measurable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
such that the following quasi-norm is finite:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\Lambda^{n+s}_{p,q,B}}

\displaystyle:=\|u\|_{p}+|u|_{n+s,p,q}.

(1.22)

1.2
Main results

1.2.1
Interpolation characterization

In the Euclidean setting, it is well known that fractional Sobolev-Slobodeckij spaces and positive order Besov spaces can be characterized by
real interpolation
of natural order Sobolev spaces, see for instance the references
[
33
]
,
[
1
]
. We recover the analogous result where the natural order Euclidean Sobolev spaces are replaced by the intrinsic Sobolev spaces introduced in
[
27
]
, which are defined as follows: for any

p\geqslant 1
set

\displaystyle|u|_{1,p}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|\partial_{x_{i}}u\|_{p}+[u]_{Y,\frac{1}{2},p,p},

(1.23)

\displaystyle|u|_{2,p}

\displaystyle:=|\nabla_{d}u|_{1,p}+\|Yu\|_{p},

(1.24)

and inductively, for
n\geqslant 2
,

\displaystyle|u|_{n,p,q}

\displaystyle:=|\nabla_{d}u|_{n-1,p}+|Yu|_{n-2,p},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},\;n\geqslant 3.

(1.25)

Definition 1.5

(
Intrinsic Sobolev spaces).

For
p\geqslant 1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
s\in(0,1)
, the Sobolev quasi-norm is defined as

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{n,p}_{B}}

\displaystyle:=\|u\|_{p}+|u|_{n,p}.

(1.26)

Before we state our interpolation result, let us first recall what a real interpolation space is: for a comprehensive
presentation of the subject we refer, for instance, to
[
18
]
,
[
31
]
and

[
1
]
. Given two real Banach spaces
Z_{1},Z_{2}
, we write
Z_{1}=Z_{2}
if
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
have the same
elements with equivalent norms and we write
Z_{1}\subseteq Z_{2}
if
Z_{1}
is continuously embedded in

Z_{2}
. The pair
(Z_{1},Z_{2})
is called an
interpolation pair
if both
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
are
continuously embedded in some Hausdorff topological vector
space: in this case, the intersection
Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}
and the sum
Z_{1}+Z_{2}
endowed with the norms

\|u\|_{Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}}:=\max\{\|u\|_{Z_{1}},\|u\|_{Z_{2}}\},\qquad\|u\|_{Z_{1}+Z_{2}}:=\inf_{u_{1}\in Z_{1},\,u_{2}\in Z_{2}\atop u=u_{1}+u_{2}}(\|u_{1}\|_{Z_{1}}+\|u_{2}\|_{Z_{2}}),

are Banach spaces. For any
\lambda>0
and
u\in Z_{1}+Z_{2}
, we set

K(\lambda,u)\equiv K(\lambda,u;Z_{1},Z_{2}):=\inf_{u_{1}\in Z_{1},\,u_{2}\in Z_{2}\atop u=u_{1}+u_{2}}(\|u_{1}\|_{Z_{1}}+\lambda\|u_{2}\|_{Z_{2}}).

(1.27)

Any Banach space
E
such that

Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}\subseteq E\subseteq Z_{1}+Z_{2},

(1.28)

is called an
intermediate space
. Among these, for
0<\theta<1
and
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
, we
have the
real interpolation space

(Z_{1},Z_{2})_{\theta,p}
consisting of
u\in Z_{1}+Z_{2}

such that

\|u\|_{\theta,p}:=\|\lambda\mapsto\lambda^{-\theta}K(\lambda,u)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{>0},\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda})}<\infty.

(1.29)

We are ready to state our first result.

Theorem 1.6
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
s\in(0,1)
and
p,q\geqslant 1
, we have

\displaystyle\Lambda^{n+s}_{p,q,B}

\displaystyle=\big(L^{p},W^{n+1,p}_{B}\big)_{\frac{n+s}{n+1},q}.

Intermediate spaces and reiteration
: the set of intermediate spaces
E
for the pair
(Z_{1},Z_{2})
such that

(Z_{1},Z_{2})_{\theta,1}\subseteq E\subseteq(Z_{1},Z_{2})_{\theta,\infty}

for some
\theta\in(0,1)
constitutes a special class of intermediate spaces denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{\theta}(Z_{1},Z_{2})
; for instance
(Z_{1},Z_{2})_{\theta,p}\in\mathcal{H}_{\theta}(Z_{1},Z_{2})
. If
E_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{\theta_{i}}(Z_{1},Z_{2})
,
i=1,2
then it holds that

(Z_{1},Z_{2})_{(1-\lambda)\theta_{1}+\lambda\theta_{2},p}=(E_{1},E_{2})_{\lambda,p},\quad p\geqslant 1

with equivalence of norms (see Theorem 1.23 in
[
20
]
). This result is known as the
Reiteration Theorem
and it is useful to obtain equivalent characterizations for interpolation spaces.

Now by Theorem 5.3 in
[
27
]
we have that
W^{n,p}_{B}\in\mathcal{H}_{\frac{n}{m}}(L^{p},W^{m,p}_{B})
for any
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
m>n
. Together with the Reiteration Theorem, this allows to infer the following equivalences: we have

\displaystyle\Lambda^{n+s}_{p,q,B}

\displaystyle=\big(L^{p},W^{n+1,p}_{B}\big)_{\frac{n+s}{n+1},q}

(by reiteration, for
0<s\leqslant s_{1}
,
n_{1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
n_{1}\geqslant n
and
q_{1}\in[1,\infty]
)

\displaystyle=\big(L^{p},\Lambda^{n_{1}+s_{1}}_{p,q_{1},B}\big)_{\frac{n+s}{n_{1}+s_{1}},q}

(by reiteration, for
n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
s_{1},s_{2}\in(0,1)
s.t.
n_{1}+s_{1}\leqslant n+s\leqslant n_{2}+s_{2}
and
q_{1},q_{2}\in[1,\infty]
)

\displaystyle=\big(\Lambda^{n_{1}+s_{1}}_{p,q_{1},B},\Lambda^{n_{2}+s_{2}}_{p,q_{1},B}\big)_{\theta,q},\qquad n+s=(1-\theta)(n_{1}+s_{1})+\theta(n_{2}+s_{2}).

1.2.2
Embeddings into Lorentz and intrinsic Hölder spaces

With reference to (
1.4
), we set

\mathbf{d}:=2+\sum_{k=0}^{r}(2k+1)d_{k}

(1.30)

the
homogeneous dimension
of
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
with respect to
D_{\lambda}
. Then we have the following

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
s\in(0,1)
,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
q\geqslant 1
. The following inclusions hold with continuity:

i)

If
(n+s)p={\bf d}
, then
\Lambda^{n+s}_{p,1,B}\subseteq L^{\infty}
;

ii)

If
(n+s)p<{\bf d}
, then
\Lambda^{n+s}_{p,q,B}\subseteq L^{p^{\prime},q}
, with
\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{n+s}{\bf d}
;

iii)

If
(n+s)p>{\bf d}
, then
\Lambda^{n+s}_{p,q,B}\subseteq C^{n-k,k+s-\frac{\bf d}{p}}_{B}
,

where
k
is the smallest integer s.t.
(k+s)p>{\bf d}
.

Theorem
1.7
gives the expected embeddings, coherently with the homogeneous structure of the group. Indeed these are analogous to the usual Euclidean ones, once we replace
N+1
with the homogeneous dimension of
N+1
, and the Euclidean Hölder spaces with the intrinsic ones. Theorems
1.6
and
1.7
show that our functional framework is compatible with the notion of interpolation and also with the Hölder functional framework introduced in
[
24
]
.

2
Intrinsic weak derivatives and Taylor expansion

2.1
Preliminaries

Here we gather some properties that are useful in the next sections.

Hereafter, in the context of our proofs we often use the notation
A\lesssim B
, meaning that
A\leqslant cB
for some
positive constant

and their commutators, the latter being approximated using classical techniques from control theory.

and highlight the main novelty of the fractional case, we present the proof in the simpler case of the Langevin operator in

Let

\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in[0,1]
, with
n_{1}+\alpha_{1}\leqslant n_{2}+\alpha_{2}
, and let
\theta\in(0,1)
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
n_{1}\leqslant n\leqslant n_{2}
such that

\frac{n-(n_{1}+\alpha_{1})}{(n_{2}+\alpha_{2})-(n_{1}+\alpha_{1})}<\theta<\frac{n+1-(n_{1}+\alpha_{1})}{(n_{2}+\alpha_{2})-(n_{1}+\alpha_{1})}.

(4.1)

If
\alpha:=(n_{1}+\alpha_{1})+\theta[n_{2}+\alpha_{2}-(n_{1}+\alpha_{1})]-n\notin\{0,1\}

then it holds that

\left(C^{n_{1},\alpha_{1}}_{B},C^{n_{2},\alpha_{2}}_{B}\right)_{\theta,\infty}=C^{n,\alpha}_{B}

(4.2)

Theorem 4.2

(Embeddings for natural order intrinsic Sobolev spaces)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
{p\in(1,\infty)}
.

i)

If
kp<\mathbf{d}
, then

W^{n+k,p}_{B}\subseteq W_{B}^{n,q},\qquad p\leqslant q\leqslant p^{\ast}_{k},\qquad\frac{1}{p^{\ast}_{k}}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{k}{\mathbf{d}}.

(4.3)

In particular,
W^{k,p}_{B}\subseteq L^{q}
for
p\leqslant q\leqslant p^{\ast}_{k}
;

ii)

if
np>\mathbf{d}
, then

W^{n,p}_{B}\subseteq C_{B}^{\left[n-\frac{\mathbf{d}}{p}\right],\left\{n-\frac{\mathbf{d}}{p}\right\}}

(4.4)

where
[\cdot]
and
\{\cdot\}
denote the integer and fractional part respectively.

We need one last lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
p>\frac{\bf d}{m}
we have

\left(L^{p},W^{m,p}_{B}\right)_{\theta,1}\subseteq L^{\infty},\qquad\theta=\frac{\bf d}{mp}.

(4.5)

Proof.

By
[
27
]
, Theorems 1.1 (ii) and Theorem 7.1 (ii), it is already known that
W^{m,p}_{B}\subseteq L^{\infty}
. Therefore, using the characterization of Definition 2.7, we have, for any
u\in W^{m,p}_{B}
:

\|u\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|u\|_{p}+|u|_{m,p,B}.

(4.6)

Next we exploit a scaling argument: applying (
4.6
) to
u(D_{\lambda})
and exploiting Lemma 2.12 (in
[
27
]
) we get

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u(D_{\lambda})\|_{p}+|u(D_{\lambda})|_{m,p,B}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{\bf d}{p}}\|u\|_{p}+\lambda^{m-\frac{\bf d}{p}}|u|_{m,p,B}.

Optimizing w.r.t.
\lambda
we directly infer

\|u\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|u\|_{p}^{1-\theta}|u|_{m,p,B}^{\theta}\lesssim\|u\|_{p}^{1-\theta}\|u\|_{W^{m,p}_{B}}^{\theta},\qquad\theta=\frac{\bf d}{mp}.

(4.7)

The equivalence between (
4.5
) and (
4.7
) is standard, see for instance
[
20
]
, Proposition 1.20.
∎

We are ready to prove Theorem
1.7
.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

i)
For
(n+s)p={\bf d}
, taking
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
m>\frac{\bf d}{p}
we have

\Lambda^{n+s}_{p,1,B}=\left(L^{p},W^{m,p}_{B}\right)_{\frac{n+s}{m},1}{=}\left(L^{p},W^{m,p}_{B}\right)_{\frac{\bf d}{mp},1}\subseteq L^{\infty},

where the last inclusion holds by Lemma
4.3
.

ii)
Assume now
(n+s)p<{\bf d}
. Let
n_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
and
s_{1}\in[0,1)
such that
n_{1}+s_{1}=\frac{\bf d}{p}
.
By the reiteration theorem and Lemma
4.3
we directly infer

\Lambda^{n+s}_{p,q,B}=\left(L^{p},\left(L^{p},W^{\frac{\bf d}{p}+1,p}_{B}\right)_{\frac{\bf d}{{\bf d}+p},1}\right)_{\frac{(n+s)p}{\bf d},q}\subseteq\left(L^{p},L^{\infty}\right)_{\frac{(n+s)p}{\bf d},q}=L^{p^{\prime},q},\qquad\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{n+s}{\bf d}.

iii)
Finally, assume
(n+s)p>{\bf d}

Using again the reiteration theorem we may write, for some
l\in\mathbb{N}
,
l>n+s
,

\displaystyle\Lambda^{n+s}_{p,q,B}

\displaystyle=\left(\left(L^{p},W^{\frac{\bf d}{p}+1,p}_{B}\right)_{\frac{\bf d}{{\bf d}+p},1},W^{l,p}_{B}\right)_{\theta,q},\qquad(1-\theta)\frac{\bf d}{p}+\theta l=n+s.

(4.8)

(by Lemma
4.3
)

\displaystyle\subseteq\left(L^{\infty},W^{l,p}_{B}\right)_{\theta,q}

(by Theorem
4.2
, ii)

\displaystyle\subseteq\left(L^{\infty},C_{B}^{l-k,k-\frac{\bf d}{p}}\right)_{\theta,q}\subseteq\left(L^{\infty},C_{B}^{l-k,k-\frac{\bf d}{p}}\right)_{\theta,\infty},

where
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is such that
kp>{\bf d}>(k-1)p
.
By Theorem
4.1
, we finally infer that

\Lambda^{n+s}_{p,q,B}\subseteq C_{B}^{n-k^{\prime},\alpha}
, where
k^{\prime}
is the smallest integer such that
(k^{\prime}+s)p>{\bf d}
and, recalling (
4.8
),

\alpha=\theta\big(l-\frac{\bf d}{p}\big)-(n-k^{\prime})=k^{\prime}+s-\frac{\bf d}{p}.

The proof is complete.

Surface configuration kernels
Andreas Stavrou

Abstract
. Let Σ g , ∗ \Sigma_{g,*} be a once-punctured oriented surface of genus g g . We study the action of the mapping class group Γ g , ∗ \Gamma_{g,*} on the n t ​ h n^{th} rational cohomology of the configuration space 𝒞 ​ 𝑜𝑛𝑓 n ​ ( Σ g , ∗ ) \mathscr{C}\!\mathit{onf}\!_{n}(\Sigma_{g,*}) of injections { 1 , … , n } ↪ Σ g , ∗ \{1,\ldots,n\}\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{g,*} , and compare the kernel of this action J g , ∗ 𝑐𝑓𝑔 ​ ( n ) J_{g,*}^{\mathit{cfg}}(n) with the n t ​ h n^{th} Johnson subgroup J g , ∗ ​ ( n ) J_{g,*}(n) . We find high-rank abelian subgroups in the quotient J g , ∗ 𝑐𝑓𝑔 ​ ( n ) / J g , ∗ ​ ( n ) J_{g,*}^{\mathit{cfg}}(n)/J_{g,*}(n) arising from the higher Johnson images and from symplectic representation theory. In particular we refute a conjecture due to Bianchi–Miller–Wilson.



1.
Introduction

For a topological space
X
and an integer
n\geq 0
, the
configuration space

\mathscr{C}\!\mathit{onf}\!_{n}(X)
is the space of injections
\{1,\ldots,n\}\hookrightarrow X
, topologised as a subspace of
X^{n}
. We will be interested in
\mathscr{C}\!\mathit{onf}\!_{n}(\Sigma_{g,*})
, where
\Sigma_{g,*}
is the complement of a single point
*
in the closed oriented surface
\Sigma_{g}
of genus
g
. The
mapping class group

\Gamma_{g,*}
is the group of orientation preserving homeomorphisms of
\Sigma_{g,*}
up to isotopy, and it naturally acts on the rational cohomology
H^{*}(\mathscr{C}\!\mathit{onf}\!_{n}(\Sigma_{g,*});{\mathbb{Q}})
. Our aim is to understand the kernels
J^{\mathit{cfg}}(n)
of the action

(1.1)

\Gamma_{g,*}\curvearrowright H^{n}(\mathscr{C}\!\mathit{onf}\!_{n}(\Sigma_{g,*});{\mathbb{Q}}).

These are closely related with the
Johnson subgroups

J_{g,*}(n)
of
\Gamma_{g,*}
, defined as the kernels of
\Gamma_{g,*}\curvearrowright\pi/\pi^{(n+1)}
, where
\pi=\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g},*)
and
\pi^{(n+1)}
is the
(n+1)
th commutator subgroup defined iteratively by
\pi^{(0)}=\pi
and
\pi^{(i+1)}=[\pi^{(i)},\pi]
. A theorem of Bianchi, Miller and Wilson
[
BMW22
]
implies the inclusion
J_{g,*}(n)\subset J_{g,*}^{\mathit{cfg}}(n)
, while a conjecture they made predicts the quotient
J_{g,*}(n)/J_{g,*}^{\mathit{cfg}}(n)
to be finite.
In our earlier work with Looijenga
[
LS25
]
, we disproved this conjecture in the case
n=4
. In this sequel, we expand this analysis to arbitrary
n
and determine the quotient
J_{g,*}(n)/J_{g,*}^{\mathit{cfg}}(n)
more precisely.

We access
J^{\mathit{cfg}}(n)
by studying the representation
{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathit{cfg}}_{n}
from
[
LS25
]
obtained as follows. The group algebra
{\mathbb{Q}}\pi
is filtered by powers of the augmentation ideal
{\mathcal{I}}
, the kernel of the augmentation homomorphism
{\operatorname{aug}}:{\mathbb{Q}}\pi\to{\mathbb{Q}}
sending every
\gamma\in\pi
to
1\in{\mathbb{Q}}
. There is then a
\Gamma_{g,*}
-equivariant
homomorphism

(1.2)

\triangle^{n}:{\mathbb{Q}}\pi/{\mathcal{I}}^{n+1}\to H^{n}(\mathscr{C}\!\mathit{onf}\!_{n}(\Sigma_{g,*})),

whose image
{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathit{cfg}}_{n}
has kernel precisely
J_{g,*}^{\mathit{cfg}}(n)
. Since by a theorem of Labute
[
Lab70
]
, the kernel of the
\Gamma_{g,*}\curvearrowright{\mathbb{Q}}\pi/{\mathcal{I}}^{n+1}
is
J(n)
, the desired quotient
J_{g,*}^{\mathit{cfg}}(n)/J_{g,*}(n)
is completely controlled by the map
\triangle^{n}
.

The augmentation filtration of
{\mathbb{Q}}\pi/{\mathcal{I}}^{n+1}
allows us to tautologically filter the map
\triangle^{n}
and the image
{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathit{cfg}}_{n}
. Each graded piece
\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{I}}_{k}{\mathbb{Q}}\pi/{\mathcal{I}}^{n+1}={\mathcal{I}}^{k}/{\mathcal{I}}^{k+1}
, for
k\leq n
, is described by Labute as a natural quotient of
H^{\otimes k}
where
H=H_{1}(\Sigma_{g,*};{\mathbb{Q}})
. The action of
\Gamma_{g,*}
on
H
is via the symplectic group
\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}({\mathbb{Z}})
, and we will be able to give a very nice description of
\ker\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{I}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}\triangle^{n}
in terms of symplectic representation theory.

The group
\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}({\mathbb{Z}})
has a well-understood class of representations over
{\mathbb{Q}}
called
algebraic
, which are direct sums of subquotients of
H^{\otimes n}
for
n\geq 0
. Algebraic representations decompose as direct sums of irreducibles, each of which has a
weight
: the least
n\geq 0
for which it embeds in
H^{\otimes n}
. So for each algebraic representation
V
and
k\geq 0
, there is a well-defined subspace
V^{\leq k}
spanned by all irreducibles of weight
\leq k
. With this language, we can now describe the degree-
n
graded piece of
\ker\triangle^{n}
.

Theorem A
.

The kernel of
\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{I}}_{n}\triangle^{n}

is the weight
\leq n-2
part of
{\mathcal{I}}^{n}/{\mathcal{I}}^{n+1}
. In other words, only the top, weight-
n
part of
{\mathcal{I}}^{n}/{\mathcal{I}}^{n+1}
survives in
{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathit{cfg}}_{n}
.

Remark 1.1
.

This is an improvement on Corollary 3.5 of
[
LS25
]
where we had only proven an inclusion.

We can already draw consequences for the quotient
J_{g,*}^{\mathit{cfg}}(n)/J_{g,*}(n)
expressed in terms of the higher Johnson images
J_{g,*}(k)/J_{g,*}({k+1})
. We note that these latter groups are only known for small
k
, with upper and lower bounds for large
k
. It is known, however, for all
k
that after tensoring with
{\mathbb{Q}}
, they become algebraic representations, and so it makes sense to use the notation
(J_{g,*}(k)/J_{g,*}({k+1}))^{\leq w}
for the part of
J_{g,*}(k)/J_{g,*}({k+1})
that lands in weight
\leq w
after this tensoring. With this notation, we bound below the contribution of
J_{g,*}(n-1)
to
J_{g,*}^{\mathit{cfg}}(n)
.

Theorem B
.

The intersection of
J_{g,*}^{\mathit{cfg}}(n)/J_{g,*}(n)
with
J_{g,*}({n-1})/J_{g,*}(n)
contains the subgroup
(J_{g,*}({n-1})/J_{g,*}(n))^{\leq n-3}
.

Remark 1.2
.

Table 1 of Morita–Sakasai–Suzuki
[
MSS15
]
provides a decomposition of
J_{g,*}(n-1)/J_{g,*}(n)
into irreducibles for
n\leq 7
, showing that the rank of
(J_{g,*}({n-1})/J_{g,*}(n))^{\leq n-1}
is non-zero for
n\geq 5
and grows rapidly with
n
.

The last theorem is a mere corollary of our main result. In fact, we are able to describe
\ker\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{I}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}\triangle^{n}
completely if the genus
g
is large, and bound it below otherwise.

Theorem C

(Theorem
3.12
)
.

For
1\leq k\leq n
, the surjection
\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{I}}_{k}{\mathbb{Q}}\pi/{\mathcal{I}}^{n+1}\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{I}}_{k}{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathit{cfg}}_{n}
contains
(\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{I}}_{k}{\mathbb{Q}}\pi/{\mathcal{I}}^{n+1})^{\leq 3k-2(n+1)}
in its kernel. If
g\geq k
, this containment is an equality.

As a consequence, we find that not only
J_{g,*}(n-1)
but many lower Johnson subgroups
J_{g,*}(k)
for
k<n
intersect
J^{\mathit{cfg}}_{g,*}(n)
non-trivially. For each such
k
, there exists a subspace

K_{k}\subset J_{g,*}({k})/J_{g,*}(k+1)

which lifts to
J^{\mathit{cfg}}_{g,*}(n)/J_{g,*}(n)
, and the various
K_{k}
organise this quotient into successive layers as follows. Starting with
C_{n}:=J^{\mathit{cfg}}_{g,*}(n)/J_{g,*}(n)
the intersection with
J_{g,*}(n-1)/J_{g,*}(n)
is
K_{n-1}
; quotienting by
K_{n-1}
yields
C_{n-1}
, whose intersection with
J_{g,*}(n-2)/J_{g,*}(n-1)
yields
K_{n-2}
, and so on. Representation-theoretic considerations force us to interpret these relations only up to finite index and finite cokernel. This leads to the following description of
J^{\mathit{cfg}}_{g,*}(n)/J_{g,*}(n)
as a
virtual cofiltration
.

Theorem D

(Theorem
5.10
)
.

The quotient
J_{g,*}^{\mathit{cfg}}(n)/J_{g,*}(n)
has a virtual cofiltration, that is, a sequence of group homomorphisms

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J_{g,*}^{\mathit{cfg}}(n)/J_{g,*}(n)=C_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{n-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{n-2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{n-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{n-2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{2}}

where each
f_{k}
has finite cokernel, and where each kernel
\ker f_{k}
is an abelian group containing a copy of

(J_{g,*}({k-1})/J_{g,*}(k))^{\leq 3k-2(n+1)-1}.

Furthermore, if
g\geq\max(n,k+1)
, this containment is of finite index.

For
k
close to
n
, we then obtain high-rank free abelian contributions, these kernels are large, whereas for
k<(2(n+1)-1)/3
we get nothing.
We remark that we do not know whether the assumption

is necessary or is an artifact of our method.

2.1.1.
On partitions and orders

We fix these notations for the rest of this paper.

In general
N

n
. If it is equipped with a total order
\prec

{\mathbf{N}}
.

Given two totally ordered sets
\mathbf{I},\mathbf{J}

\mathbf{I}\mathbf{J}
is the disjoint union
I\sqcup J
equipped with the total order inherited from
\mathbf{I}
and
\mathbf{J}
and which makes
I\prec J
.

A
partition-with-orders

{\underline{{\mathbf{N}}}}=({\mathbf{N}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{N}}_{r})
of a set

is a partition of
N
into a disjoint union
N=N_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup N_{r}
and each
N_{i}
is equipped with a total ordering. We say
{\underline{{\mathbf{N}}}}
is

if all the parts
N_{i}
are non-empty. For example, the notation
{\underline{{\mathbf{N}}}}=((3,1),(2,5,4))
is a nowhere empty partition of the set
[5]
into two parts; the orderings are understood to be
3\prec 1
and
2\prec 5\prec 4
.

2.1.2.
The functor

We generalise

If

In

2.1.3.
The maps

Denote the

Viewing an element

2.1.4.
Multiplicative structure

The maps
\triangle^{\mathbf{N}}
are multiplicative in the following sense.
Taking the disjoint union of two sets
N_{1},N_{2}
gives a homeomorphism
X^{N_{1}}\times X^{N_{2}}\to X^{N_{1}\sqcup N_{2}}
and, in turn, a product

(2.2)

\times:{\mathcal{H}}_{N_{1}}(U)\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{N_{2}}(U)\to{\mathcal{H}}_{N_{1}\sqcup N_{2}}(U).

The
decomposition formula
gives, for any

,

(2.3)

\triangle^{\mathbf{N}}(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})=\sum_{{\mathbf{N}}_{1}{\mathbf{N}}_{2}={\mathbf{N}}}\triangle^{{\mathbf{N}}_{1}}(\gamma_{1})\times\triangle^{{\mathbf{N}}_{2}}(\gamma_{2}),

where the summation is over all partitions

so that, under the inherited orders from

,
{\mathbf{N}}_{1}
and
{\mathbf{N}}_{2}
concatenate to
{\mathbf{N}}
. This process iterates to longer products as

(2.4)

\triangle^{\mathbf{N}}(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\cdots\gamma_{r})=\sum_{{\mathbf{N}}_{1}{\mathbf{N}}_{2}\cdots{\mathbf{N}}_{r}={\mathbf{N}}}\triangle^{{\mathbf{N}}_{1}}(\gamma_{1})\times\triangle^{{\mathbf{N}}_{2}}(\gamma_{2})\times\cdots\times\triangle^{{\mathbf{N}}_{r}}(\gamma_{r}).

Finally, for a general partition-with-orders
{\underline{{\mathbf{N}}}}=({\mathbf{N}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{N}}_{r})

\triangle^{\underline{{\mathbf{N}}}}(\gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\gamma_{r})=\triangle^{{\mathbf{N}}_{1}}(\gamma_{1})\times\cdots\times\triangle^{{\mathbf{N}}_{r}}

2.2.3.
Algebraic representations

Here and onwards, let

2.2.4.

There is a

3.
The module

In this section, we determine the kernel of the map

-generators for

3.1.
A Magnus correspondence

The isomorphism
{\mathcal{I}}_{1}^{n}/{\mathcal{I}}_{1}^{n+1}\cong H^{\otimes n}
of Fox is given by the correspondence

(3.1)

(\gamma_{1}-1)\cdots(\gamma_{n}-1)\mapsto[\gamma_{1}]\otimes\cdots\otimes[\gamma_{n}]

for
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\in\pi_{1}
and where
[\gamma]\in H
is the abelianisation of
\gamma\in\pi_{1}
.
Conversely, our chosen basis for
\pi_{1}
produces a lift (
3.1
) given on monomials by

(3.2)

{\mathfrak{v}}=a_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i_{n}}\in H^{\otimes n}\mapsto\widetilde{{\mathfrak{v}}}=(\alpha_{i_{1}}-1)\cdots(\alpha_{i_{n}}-1)\in{\mathbb{Q}}\pi_{1},

for
i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}\in\{\pm 1,\ldots,\pm g\}
. The linearly extended map
\widetilde{\cdot}:T[H]\to{\mathbb{Q}}\pi_{1}
is easily seen to be a ring homomorphism but not
\Gamma_{g,1}
-equivariant.

Remark 3.1
.

Since
\triangle^{0}(\gamma)=1
for all
\gamma\in\pi_{1}
and
\triangle^{i}(1)=0
for all
i\geq 1
, it follows that
\triangle^{0}
vanishes on
\gamma-1
and
\triangle^{i}(\gamma-1)=\triangle^{i}(\gamma)
for
i\geq 1
. The decomposition formula (
2.4
) then takes on the particular form on
\triangle^{n}(\widetilde{{\mathfrak{v}}})
where
{\mathfrak{v}}=a_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i_{k}}\in H^{\otimes k}
:

(3.3)

\triangle^{n}(\widetilde{{\mathfrak{v}}})=\sum_{{\mathbf{N}}_{1}\cdots{\mathbf{N}}_{k}=\textbf{[n]}}\triangle^{{\mathbf{N}}_{1}}(\alpha_{i_{1}})\times\cdots\times\triangle^{{\mathbf{N}}_{k}}(\alpha_{i_{k}})

where the summation this time only runs over all
non-empty
partitions-with-orders
{\mathbf{N}}_{1},\ldots,

{\mathbf{N}}_{k}
that concatenate to the standard order
(1,\ldots,n)
.

Example 3.2
.

Since
\zeta
is a commutator, it follows from Fox
[
Fox53
]
that
\zeta-1\in{\mathcal{I}}_{1}^{2}
, and we checked in
[
LS25
]
that
\zeta-1+{\mathcal{I}}_{1}^{3}\mapsto\mu
under correspondence (
3.1
).
In particular, formula (
3.3
) gives

(3.4)

\triangle^{n}(\widetilde{\mu})=\sum_{0<k<n}\sum_{1\leq\pm i\leq g}\operatorname{sign}(i)\triangle^{(1,\ldots,k)}(\alpha_{i})\times\triangle^{(k+1,\ldots,n)}(\alpha_{-i}).

However,
\mu\neq\zeta-1\in{\mathbb{Q}}\pi_{1}
. In particular,
\triangle_{1}^{k}(\widetilde{\mu})\neq\triangle_{1}^{k}(\zeta)\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(U_{2g})
for

or, equivalently,
\triangle^{k}(\widetilde{\mu})=0\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(\Sigma_{g,*})
for
k\geq 3
. Chasing this discrepancy between
\widetilde{\mu}
(that comes from symplectic representation theory) and
\zeta
(that comes from topology and group theory) is the crux of this section.

3.2.
{\mathbb{Q}}[\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}({\mathbb{Z}})]
-generators

We consider monomials
{\mathfrak{m}}\in T[H]
in the free associative generators
a_{\pm 1},\ldots,a_{\pm g}
. Each such monomial is said to be
of type
(p,q)
if it has
p
positive-index factors and
q
negative-index ones; say
{\mathfrak{m}}
is
positive
if
q=0
.
Classical representation theory (and this is implied by what follows) gives us that the weight-
k
part of
H^{\otimes n}
is generated as an
\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}({\mathbb{Z}})
-representation by the monomials of type
(k,n-k)
. We will however give a more restricted set of generators.

Definition 3.3
.

A chord diagram (of length
r
) in the set
[n]
is a pair
(\underline{k},\underline{l})
of
r
-tuples
\underline{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
and
\underline{l}=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{r})
of a total of
2r
pairwise distinct elements of
[n]
, with
k_{i}<l_{i}
for all
i=1,\ldots,r
, and
k_{1}<k_{2}<\cdots<k_{r}
. Say that
(\underline{k},\underline{l})
is non-consecutive if all pairs
(k_{i},l_{i})
are non-consecutive, that is
k_{i}+1<l_{i}
for all
i=1,\ldots,r
, and call it consecutive otherwise.

For any chord diagram, there is a corresponding insertion map

(3.5)

\mu^{n,r}_{\underline{k},\underline{l}}:H^{\otimes n-2r}\to H^{\otimes n}

the map inserting
r
copies of
\mu
in the pairs of tensor slots
(k_{1},l_{1}),\ldots,(k_{r},l_{r})
, so that if
r=1
,
\mu^{n,1}_{(k,l)}
is the map
\mu_{k,l}
of Proposition
2.10
. Equivalently, each
\mu^{n,r}_{\underline{k},\underline{l}}
is a composition of the map
{\mathfrak{v}}\in H^{\otimes n-2r}\mapsto\mu^{\otimes r}\otimes{\mathfrak{v}}
with a permutation of the tensor factors by some
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
{\mathbb{M}}^{n}_{+}
be the set of all positive monomials in
H^{\otimes n}
, and denote by
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}_{+}
their
{\mathbb{Q}}
-span. For
1\leq k\leq g
, let
{\mathbb{M}}^{n}_{+,k}\subset{\mathbb{M}}^{n}_{+}
be the subset of monomials involving only the last
k
indices, i.e.
{\mathfrak{m}}\in{\mathbb{M}}^{n}_{+,k}
is a product of
a_{i}
with
i>g-k
. Denote by
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}_{+,k}
the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-span of this set.

For
0\leq 2r\leq n
, we define

{\mathbb{B}}^{n,r}=\{\mu^{n,r}_{\underline{k},\underline{l}}({\mathfrak{m}}):(\underline{k},\underline{l})\text{ chord-diagrams of length }r\text{, and }{\mathfrak{m}}\in{\mathbb{M}}^{n-2r}_{+,n-2r}\},

and
{\mathbb{B}}^{n,r}_{\operatorname{nc}}
its subset where we only consider
non-consecutive
chord-diagrams. Note that
{\mathbb{B}}^{0,0}=\{1\}
is the set containing the empty monomial.

Proposition 3.5
.

For
0\leq 2r\leq n
, the weight-
(n-2r)
part of
H^{\otimes n}
is generated over
{\mathbb{Q}}[\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}({\mathbb{Z}})]
by
{\mathbb{B}}^{n,r}
. Furthermore, each irreducible summand of
H^{\otimes n}
of weight
(n-2r)
contains a non-zero
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linear combination of such elements. More specifically, this element is the application of a linear combination of
\mu^{n,r}_{\underline{k},\underline{l}}
, for various chord-diagrams
(\underline{k},\underline{l})
, on a fixed
{\mathfrak{v}}\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n-2r}_{+,n-2r}
.

Proof.

All statements used here come from §17.3 of Fulton–Harris
[
FH91
]
. The weight-
(n-2r)
part of
H^{\otimes n}
lies in the span of the images of the repeated insertions
\mu_{\underline{k},\underline{l}}^{n,r}
. More specifically, it is the image of a weight-
(n-2r)
irreducible summand
V
of
H^{\otimes n-2r}
under a linear combination of maps
\mu^{n,r}_{\underline{k},\underline{l}}
. Now, this irreducible
V
has a
highest weight vector
, i.e. a
{\mathbb{Q}}[\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}({\mathbb{Z}})]
-generator
{\mathfrak{v}}
, that is a sum of permutations of a specific
a_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i_{n-2r}}
where
1=i_{1}\leq\ldots\leq i_{n-2r}
. There is a symplectic transformation
A\in\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}({\mathbb{Z}})
that swaps the order
a_{\pm i}\mapsto a_{\pm(n-2r-i)}
, putting these monomials in
{\mathbb{M}}^{n-2r}_{+,n-2r}
.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

For
0\leq 2r\leq n
, the weight-
(n-2r)
part of
{\mathcal{I}}^{n}/{\mathcal{I}}^{n+1}
is generated over
{\mathbb{Q}}[\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}({\mathbb{Z}})]
by
{\mathbb{B}}^{n,r}_{\operatorname{nc}}
. Furthermore, each irreducible summand of
{\mathcal{I}}^{n}/{\mathcal{I}}^{n+1}

Proof.

The algerba
\operatorname{gr}^{I}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}{\mathbb{Q}}\pi
is the quotient of the tensor algebra

by the two-sided ideal generated by
\mu
, so the surjection
H^{\otimes n}\to{\mathcal{I}}^{n}/{\mathcal{I}}^{n+1}
has kernel the images of all consecutive insertions

. The conclusion follows from Proposition
3.5
.
∎

Denote by
\widetilde{{\mathbb{B}}^{n,r}}
and
\widetilde{{\mathbb{B}}^{n,r}_{\operatorname{nc}}}
the sets of lifts of
{{\mathbb{B}}^{n,r}}
and
{{\mathbb{B}}^{n,r}_{\operatorname{nc}}}
, respectively, in
{\mathbb{Q}}\pi_{1}
according to (

). The following is a recursive application of Propositions
3.5
and
3.6
.

Proposition 3.7
.

For any
n\geq 0
, the
{\mathbb{Q}}[\Gamma_{g,1}]
-module
{\mathbb{Q}}\pi_{1}/{\mathcal{I}}_{1}^{n+1}
(resp.
{\mathbb{Q}}[\Gamma]

.

3.3.
Lower bound for
\ker\triangle^{n}

We now determine which basis elements are for certain in the kernel of
\triangle^{n}:{\mathbb{Q}}\pi/{\mathcal{I}}^{n+1}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{n}(\Sigma_{g,*})
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Suppose
0\leq 2r\leq s\leq n
. Then the lifted image of
\mu_{\underline{k},\underline{l}}^{s,r}
in
{\mathbb{Q}}\pi/{\mathcal{I}}^{n+1}
is annihilated by
\triangle^{n}
whenever
s+r\geq n+1
.

Proof.

The codomain
H^{\otimes s}
of
\mu^{s,r}_{\underline{k},\underline{l}}
is lifted in
{\mathbb{Q}}\pi/{\mathcal{I}}^{n+1}
into the span
\widetilde{H^{\otimes s}}
of products of type
(\gamma_{1}-1)\cdots(\gamma_{s}-1)
where
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{s}\in\{\alpha_{\pm 1},\ldots,\alpha_{\pm g}\}
. The decomposition formula (
2.4
)
evaluates
\triangle^{n}
on each of these products as the sum

\sum_{{\mathbf{N}}_{1}\cdots{\mathbf{N}}_{s}=\textbf{n}}\triangle^{{\mathbf{N}}_{1}}(\gamma_{1}-1)\times\cdots\times\triangle^{{\mathbf{N}}_{s}}(\gamma_{s}-1)

over all ordered subsets
{\mathbf{N}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{N}}_{s}
that concatenate to the order
1\prec\cdots\prec n
. But if any
{\mathbf{N}}_{i}
is empty, then
\triangle^{{\mathbf{N}}_{i}}(\gamma_{i}-1)=\triangle^{0}(\gamma_{i}-1)={\operatorname{aug}}(\gamma_{i}-1)=0
, and so the product vanishes. Then in fact, we have that
\triangle^{n}
restricted to
\widetilde{H^{\otimes s}}
decomposes as
\sum\triangle^{{\mathbf{N}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{N}}_{s}}
over all
non-empty
partitions-with-orders
{\mathbf{N}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{N}}_{s}
that concatenate to the order
1\prec\cdots\prec n
.

Given a monomial
{\mathfrak{m}}\in H^{\otimes s-2r}
, we will show that
\triangle^{{\mathbf{N}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{N}}_{s}}(\widetilde{\mu^{s,r}_{\underline{k},\underline{l}}({\mathfrak{m}})})
vanishes for all non-empty partitions-with-orders
{\mathbf{N}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{N}}_{s}
which, from above, implies the vanishing of
\triangle^{n}(\widetilde{\mu^{s,r}_{\underline{k},\underline{l}}({\mathfrak{m}})})
. Now fix such an
{\mathbf{N}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{N}}_{s}
.
The assumption
n\leq s+r-1
and the pigeonhole principle imply the existence of an
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
for which both
N_{k_{i}},N_{l_{i}}
are singletons; say their elements are
p_{1},p_{2}\in[s]
, respectively. We then have the factorisation
\mu^{s,r}_{\underline{k},\underline{l}}({\mathfrak{m}})=\mu\otimes_{p_{1},p_{2}}v
for some
v\in H^{\otimes s-2}
and, as a result,

\triangle^{{\mathbf{N}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{N}}_{s}}(\widetilde{\mu^{s,r}_{\underline{k},\underline{l}}({\mathfrak{m}})})=\triangle^{(p_{1},p_{2})}(\widetilde{\mu})\times\triangle^{{\mathbf{N}}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{{\mathbf{N}}_{k_{i}}},\ldots,\widehat{{\mathbf{N}}_{l_{i}}},\ldots{\mathbf{N}}_{s}}(\widetilde{v}).

But here the first factor vanishes from Example
3.2
, and so the product also does, as desired.
∎

In the special case
r=1,s=n
, this recovers Corollary 3.5 of
[
LS25
]
. The following is now immediate.

Corollary 3.9
.

We have the vanishing
\triangle^{n}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}})=0\in{\mathcal{H}}_{n}(\Sigma_{g,*})
for any
\mathfrak{b}\in{\mathbb{B}}^{s,r}_{\operatorname{nc}}
with
s+r\geq n+1
.

3.4.
Upper bound for
\ker\triangle^{n}

We will show that our lower bound
\ker(\triangle^{n})
is sharp, if the genus is large, in the sense that all elements of
\cup_{s+r\leq n}{\mathbb{B}}^{s,r}_{\operatorname{nc}}
that were not annihilated by
\triangle^{n}
according to Corollary

are actually mapped to linearly independent elements of
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}(\Sigma_{g,*})
. The following theorem is the key ingredient to proving sharpness. We state the theorem here but will prove it separately in Section

with geometric methods.

Theorem 3.10
.

If
0\leq n\leq g
, the set

\{\triangle^{n}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}})\in{\mathcal{H}}_{n}(\Sigma_{g,*}):\mathfrak{b}\in\cup_{s+r=n}{\mathbb{B}}^{s,r}_{{\operatorname{nc}}}\}

is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linearly independent. Furthermore, for fixed
0\leq s\leq n
(note that this fixes

), so is the subset

\{\triangle^{n}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}})+\triangle^{n}({\mathcal{I}}^{s+1})\in{\mathcal{H}}_{n}(\Sigma_{g,*})/\triangle^{n}({\mathcal{I}}^{s+1}):\mathfrak{b}\in{\mathbb{B}}^{s,r}_{{\operatorname{nc}}}\}.

Corollary 3.11
.

If
0\leq n\leq g
, the set

\{\triangle^{n}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}})\in{\mathcal{H}}_{n}(\Sigma_{g,*}):\mathfrak{b}\in\cup_{s+r\leq n}{\mathbb{B}}^{s,r}_{{\operatorname{nc}}}\}

is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linearly independent. Furthermore, for fixed
0\leq s\leq n
, so is the set

\{\triangle^{n}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}})+\triangle^{n}({\mathcal{I}}^{s+1})\in{\mathcal{H}}_{n}(\Sigma_{g,*})/\triangle^{n}({\mathcal{I}}^{s+1}):\mathfrak{b}\in\cup_{s+r\leq n}{\mathbb{B}}^{s,r}_{{\operatorname{nc}}}\}.

Proof.

Proceed inductively on
n
, with the case
n=0
being obvious. In
[
LS25
]
, we found a degeneracy map
\partial:{\mathcal{H}}_{n}(\Sigma_{g,*})\to{\mathcal{H}}_{n-1}(\Sigma_{g,*})
that commutes with
\triangle^{n}
and
\triangle^{n-1}
, and in particular makes the following diagram commute

(3.6)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{s+r=n}{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{{\mathbb{B}}^{s,r}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{s+r\leq n}{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{{\mathbb{B}}^{s,r}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{s+r\leq n-1}{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{{\mathbb{B}}^{s,r}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{H}}_{n}(\Sigma_{g,*})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{H}}_{n}(\Sigma_{g,*})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{H}}_{n-1}(\Sigma_{g,*}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\triangle^{n}_{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\triangle^{n}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces=}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial}

The same inductive proof works for the “furthermore” assertion as

∎

The image
{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathit{cfg}}_{n}=\operatorname{Im}\triangle^{n}
is naturally filtered by
\triangle^{n}({\mathcal{I}})\supset\triangle^{n}({\mathcal{I}}^{2})\supset\cdots
giving the (tautologically) surjective associated graded map

\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{I}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}:\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{I}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}{\mathbb{Q}}\pi/{\mathcal{I}}^{n+1}\to\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{I}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathit{cfg}}_{n},

where we can replace the domain by simply
\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{I}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}{\mathbb{Q}}\pi
.
The graded kernel
\ker\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{I}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}\triangle^{n}
coincides (also tautologically) with the associated graded of

by the filtration
\ker\triangle^{n}\cap{\mathcal{I}}\supset\ker\triangle^{n}\cap{\mathcal{I}}^{2}\supset\cdots
. We now completely describe the kernel and image of
\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{I}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}\triangle^{n}

Theorem 3.12
.

For
1\leq k\leq n
, the kernel of
\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{I}}_{k}\triangle^{n}
contains the subrepresentation
(\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{I}}_{k}{\mathbb{Q}}\pi)^{\leq 3k-2(n+1)}
, and
\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{I}}_{k}{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathit{cfg}}_{n}
is a quotient of

(3.7)

\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{I}}_{k}{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathit{cfg}}_{n}\cong(\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{I}}_{k}{\mathbb{Q}}\pi)/(\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{I}}_{k}{\mathbb{Q}}\pi)^{\leq 3k-2(n+1)}.

If furthermore,

, both of these statements are equalities.
In particular, if

, then
\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{I}}_{k}
is injective and
\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{I}}_{k}{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathit{cfg}}_{n}\cong\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{I}}_{k}{\mathbb{Q}}\pi

(k+2-r,r)

r

(5.9)

\sum_{1\leq i\leq g}a_{i}\otimes v_{i}+a_{-i}\otimes v_{-i}

with each

. Clearly, each

r
-negative, and each
v_{-i}
is
r-1
-negative. The assumption

implies that not all

can vanish at once. We proceed to analyse the two cases separately.

Case: intersection (
5.7
).
We are assuming on the one hand that
v\in H\otimes(H^{\otimes k+1})^{\leq w}
, or equivalently that
v_{\pm i}\in(H^{\otimes k+1})^{\leq w}
for all
i
. The latter subspace of
H^{\otimes k+1}
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-spanned at least
\geq(k+1-w)/2
negative monomials; this is because all irreducible summands of weight
\leq w
involve at least
\geq(k+1-w)/2
factors of
\mu
, each contributing for certain a negative
a_{i}
factor. If some
v_{i}\neq 0
for
i>0
, then
r\geq(k+1-w)/2
so
{\operatorname{wt}}(V)=k+2-2r\leq w+1
. Similarly, if some
v_{-i}\neq 0
for
i>0
, then
r-1\geq(k+1-w)/2
and
{\operatorname{wt}}(V)\leq w-1
. Since we assumed
{\operatorname{wt}}(V)\geq w+1
and
v\neq 0
, it follows that
v_{-i}=0
for all
i>0
and
r=(k+1-w)/2
. In particular
r\geq 1
and
v=\sum_{1\leq i\leq g}a_{i}\otimes v_{i}
is at least
1
-negative.

On the other hand, since
v
is invariant under the cyclic
{\mathcal{C}}_{k+2}
-action, then for any monomial appearing non-trivially in
v
, all the cyclic permutations of that monomial appear with the same non-trivial coefficient. But every monomial with positive factor in the first slot and at least one negative factor thereafter has a cyclic permutation with a negative factor in the first slot. We have proven
v
contains monomials of the former type but none of the latter, giving us a contradiction, thus proving the first assertion of the statement.

Case: intersection (
5.8
).

First, let us observe that
V
cannot entirely lie in the intersection
\operatorname{Im}\tau_{g,1}(k)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\cap H\otimes\sum_{i=1}^{k}\operatorname{Im}\mu_{i,i+1}

would lie in the kernel of
J_{g,1}(k)/J_{g,1}(k+1)\to J_{g,*}(k)/J_{g,*}(k+1)

5.3
, this kernel is trivial unless
k=2
in which case it is the trivial representation
{\mathbb{Q}}
of weight
0

V
has weight
\geq w+1\geq 1
.

Now, write
v=u+u^{\prime}
for some
u\in H\otimes(H^{\otimes k+1})^{\leq w}
and

, assuming

is not contained in the latter space. So we know from the previous case that

is non-zero and in the span of monomials that are at least
(k+1-w)/2
-negative. This, as before, implies
r\geq(k+1-w)/2
or, equivalently,
{\operatorname{wt}}(V)\leq w+1
. Our assumption
{\operatorname{wt}}(V)\geq w+1
forces the weight of
V
to be precisely
w+1
. It also forces that, in a decomposition of

5.9
), that all
u_{i}=0
for
i<0

k+1
tensor slots.

The assumption
g\geq k+2
means that the fix element
{\mathfrak{v}}\in{\mathbb{H}}^{k+2-2r}_{+,k+2-2r}
involves only the generators
a_{i}
for
i\geq 2r
. Then, each
\mu^{k+2,r}_{\underline{k},\underline{l}}({\mathfrak{v}})
contains, as summands, a permutation of
a_{1}\otimes a_{-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{r}\otimes a_{-r}\otimes{\mathfrak{v}}
that has the factors
a_{1},a_{-1},\ldots,a_{r},a_{-r}
in the tensor slots

, respectively; in the other tensor slots only terms involving
a_{i}

\mu^{k+2,r}_{\underline{k},\underline{l}}({\mathfrak{v}})
contain terms that cannot cancel between them and are thus linearly independent. In particular, there is a well-defined set of chord diagrams involved non-trivially in the linear combination

By virtue of lying in the span of the sum
H\otimes(H^{\otimes k+1})^{\leq w}+H\otimes\sum\operatorname{Im}\mu_{i,i+1}
, these chord-diagrams can only be of the following two types: (I) having at least one consecutive chord in the last
k+1

\mu_{i,i+1}

k+1

{\mathcal{C}}_{k+2}
invariance of
v
, along with the argument of the previous paragraph, enforces that this set of chord-diagrams is closed under cyclic permutation. But a chord diagram of type (II) of length

can be cycled to be still non-consecutive and to now involve the first slot; this is of neither type (I) or (II). Therefore
v
must involve only type (I) chord diagrams which contradicts that
v\not\in H\otimes\sum_{i=1}^{k}\operatorname{Im}\mu_{i,i+1}

∎

Since the codomain of

Lemma 5.8
.

Suppose

\Gamma_{g,*}
-equivariant short exact sequence of groups equipped with actions of
\Gamma_{g,*}

\Gamma_{g,*}
-equivariant group homomorphism. Suppose also that

\operatorname{Hom}_{\Gamma_{g,*}}(V\otimes{\mathbb{Q}},U\otimes{\mathbb{Q}})=0

Proof.

The map

\Gamma_{g,*}
-equivariant map
\bar{f}:V\to U/\operatorname{Im}(f|_{F})
; by the trivial
\operatorname{Hom}
condition, the latter map has torsion image; it is finite since

is finitely generated. This image is
\operatorname{Im}(f)/\operatorname{Im}(f|_{F})

∎

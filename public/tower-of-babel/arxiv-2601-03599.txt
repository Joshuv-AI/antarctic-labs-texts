The Feller diffusion as the limit of a coalescent point process
Conrad J. Burden, Robert C. Griffiths

Abstract
The Feller diffusion is studied as the limit of a coalescent point process in which the density of the node height distribution is skewed towards zero. Using a unified approach, a number of recent results pertaining to scaling limits of branching processes are reinterpreted as properties of the Feller diffusion arising from this limit. The notion of Bernoulli sampling of a finite population is extended to the diffusion limit to cover finite Poisson-distributed samples drawn from infinite continuum populations. We show that the coalescent tree of a Poisson-sampled Feller diffusion corresponds to a coalescent point process with a node height distribution taking the same algebraic form as that of a Bernoulli-sampled birth-death process. By adapting methods for analysing k k -sampled birth-death processes, in which the sample size is pre-specified, we develop methods for studying the coalescent properties of the k k -sampled Feller diffusion.

Introduction

The Feller diffusion

8
,

\big(X(t)\big)_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}

with generator

\mathcal{L}=\tfrac{1}{2}x\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}+\alpha x\frac{\partial}{\partial x},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{R}.

(1)

It arises as the limit of a continuous-time, finite-population linear birth-death (BD) process in
which the birth and death rates become infinite, but their difference remains finite
[
3
, Section 2.1]
. This limit has implicitly manifested
several times in the literature in the guise of the large-population limit of a critical BD process
[
2
, Section 3.2]
, the long-time, near-critical limit
of BD processes
[
16
,
12
]
, the large-population limit of sampled BD processes
[
7
]
, and the diffusion limit of a
Bienaymé-Galton-Watson process
[
5
,
6
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to survey and extend recently reported results associated with scaling limits of BD processes, which we reinterpret as
Feller diffusions conditioned on a single ancestral founder
[
4
]
. We do so under a unified set of principles based on a coalescent
point process (CPP) approach
[
2
]
. The CPP and its relationship to coalescent trees is reviewed in Section
2
, with emphasis on general properties
which are needed later in the paper. We also observe a previously unrecognised close connection between the distribution of coalescent times for a process
initiated at a specified time
t
in the past and for a process with an assumed uniform prior on an initiation time in the interval
[0,t]
in the past.

The CPP approach to the linear BD process is summarised in Section
3
, and a convenient parameterisation due to
Wiuf 2018
is introduced which
makes explicit a symmetry between super-critical and sub-critical process. This symmetry is shown to emerge naturally from the CPP approach as a consequence
of requiring that the ancestral tree of a final population converges to a single ancestral founder in the sub-critical case. In Section
4
the Feller
diffusion is shown to correspond to a limiting case of a CPP in which the density of the node-height distribution is skewed towards zero. A concise derivation of the
known joint density of coalescent times for a Feller diffusion descended from a single founding ancestor and conditioned on an observed current population size
is given.

Section
5

probability, and

-sampling, in which a specified number of individuals are sampled, are considered. We extend to the Feller diffusion a result due to

that a Bernoulli-sampled BD process corresponds to a CPP with a node-height density taking the same functional form as that of an
unsampled BD process. We also define an analogue of Bernoulli sampling appropriate for Feller diffusions, which we call Poisson sampling. By extending a procedure
due to
Lambert 2018

In Section

Coalescent point processes

The CPP is neatly summarised in the introduction to

Definition 1
.

A CPP with stem age

until a value larger than

n

give rise to a phylogenetic tree on

In this definition, the parameter

The
k
-ancestor to
(k-1)
-ancestor coalescent event times
T_{k}
measured back from the from the present are related to the stem height
t

and to order statistics
H_{(1)}<\cdots<H_{(n-1)}
of the node heights by

T_{1}=t,\qquad T_{k}=H_{(n+1-k)},\quad k=2,\ldots,n.

(2)

A CPP with
n=6

Ignatieva et al. 2020
introduce the idea of a
reversed reconstructed process
(RRP) to construct coalescent trees. The RRP is an inhomogeneous
pure-death process with rate
\mu_{\rm eff}(\tau)
,
\tau>0
, which runs backwards in time from the present. The cumulative distribution function (
cdf
)
of the typical node height is related to the effective death rate by

F_{H}(\tau):=\mathbb{P}(H\leq\tau)=1-\exp\left\{-\int_{0}^{\tau}\mu_{\rm eff}(\xi)d\xi\right\},

which inverts to

\mu_{\rm eff}(\tau)=\frac{d}{d\tau}\log\left\{(1-F_{H}(\tau))^{-1}\right\}.

(3)

Lambert 2018
introduces the terminology
inverse tail distribution
for the function

F^{\rm IT}_{H}(\tau):=\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(H>\tau)}=\frac{1}{1-F_{H}(\tau)},

which inverts to
F_{H}(\tau)=1-{F^{\rm IT}_{H}(\tau)}^{-1}
. Hence the
coalescent density
or
node height density
is

f_{H}(\tau):=F_{H}^{\prime}(\tau)=\frac{{F^{\rm IT}_{H}}^{\prime}(\tau)}{{F^{\rm IT}_{H}(\tau)}^{2}}.

2.1
Coalescent time distribution given
T_{1}=t

The probability that a CPP with stem age
t
generates a tree with
n
leaves is

F_{H}(t)^{n-1}(1-F_{H}(t)),

and the joint probability/density that there are
n
leaves and node heights

is

\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}f_{H}(h_{j})\right)(1-F_{H}(t)).

Thus the density of node heights given
n
leaves is

f_{H_{1},\ldots,H_{n-1}}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n-1}\mid n)=\frac{\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}f_{H}(h_{j})\right)(1-F_{H}(t))}{F_{H}(t)^{n-1}(1-F_{H}(t))}=\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\frac{f_{H}(h_{j})}{F_{H}(t)}\right).

It follows that, conditional on
n
leaves, the node heights are identically and independently distributed (i.i.d.) with common density

f_{H}(h)/F_{H}(t)
,
h\in[0,t]
. From Eq. (
2

in terms of the distribution of order statistics of i.i.d. node heights.

Suppressing the dependence on

F(\tau)=\frac{F_{H}(\tau)}{F_{H}(t)},\qquad f(\tau)=\frac{f_{H}(\tau)}{F_{H}(t)},\qquad\tau\in[0,t].

(4)

Then the joint
cdf

n
leaves generated by a CPP is

\displaystyle F_{T_{2},\ldots,T_{n}\mid T_{1}=t}(\tau_{2},\ldots,\tau_{n}\mid n)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(T_{2}\leq\tau_{2},\ldots,T_{n}\leq\tau_{k}\mid T_{1}=t,n\text{ leaves})

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{H_{(1)},\ldots,H_{(n-1)}}(\tau_{k},\ldots,\tau_{2}\mid n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-1)!\prod_{j=2}^{n}F(\tau_{j}),

\displaystyle\qquad n\geq 2,\quad t>\tau_{2}>\cdots>\tau_{n}>0,

and the joint density of the
k-1
largest coalescent times is

\displaystyle f_{T_{2},\ldots,T_{k}\mid T_{1}=t}(\tau_{2},\ldots,\tau_{k}\mid n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{H_{(n-k+1)},\ldots,H_{(n-1)}}(\tau_{k},\ldots,\tau_{2}\mid n)

(6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(n-1)!}{(n-k)!}\left(\prod_{j=2}^{k}f(\tau_{j})\right)F(\tau_{k})^{n-k},

\displaystyle\qquad k=2,\ldots,n,\quad t>\tau_{2}>\cdots>\tau_{k}>0.

The marginal density of the coalescent time
T_{k}
is

\displaystyle f_{T_{k}\mid T_{1}=t}(\tau\mid n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{H_{(n-k+1)}}(\tau\mid n)

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(n-1)!}{(n-k)!(k-2)!}\left(1-F(\tau)\right)^{k-2}f(\tau)\left(F(\tau)\right)^{n-k}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad 2\leq k\leq n,\quad 0<\tau<t,

the joint marginal density of
T_{i}
and
T_{j}
is

\displaystyle f_{T_{i},T_{j}\mid T_{1}=t}(\tau,s\mid n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{H_{(n-i+1),(n-j+1)}}(\tau,s\mid n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(n-1)!}{(n-j)!(j-i-1)!(i-2)!}\times

\displaystyle\qquad F(s)^{n-j}(F(\tau)-F(s))^{j-i-1}(1-F(\tau))^{i-2}f(\tau)f(s),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad 2\leq i<j\leq n,\quad t>\tau>s>0,

and hence the density of
T_{i}
conditional on
T_{j}
for
i<j

\displaystyle f_{T_{i}\mid T_{j}=s,T_{1}=t}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{f_{T_{i},T_{j}\mid T_{1}=t}(\tau,s\mid n)}{f_{T_{j}\mid T_{1}=t}(\tau\mid n)}

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(j-2)!}{(i-2)!(j-i-1)!}\frac{(F(\tau)-F(s))^{j-i-1}(1-F(\tau))^{i-2}}{(1-F(s))^{j-2}}f(\tau)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad 2\leq i<j\leq n,\quad t>\tau>s>0,

which is independent of
n

2.2
Coalescent time distribution with uniform prior on
T_{1}

As an alternative to fixing the stem height
T_{1}
, consider setting a uniform prior on
[0,t]

f_{T_{1}}^{{\rm unif}\,[0,t]}(\tau)=\frac{1}{t},\qquad 0\leq\tau\leq t.

After calculating posterior probabilities, the limit

Birth-death process as a CPP

The reconstructed tree of a linear BD process with constant birth rate
\lambda
and death rate
\mu
was recognised as a CPP by
Aldous and Popovic 2005
for
the critical case
\lambda=\mu
and by
Gernhard 2008
for general
\lambda
and
\mu
. We begin with the following Lemma which rederives the node height
distribution quoted in
Lambert 2018
.

Lemma 1
.

The reconstructed tree of a BD process
\big(N(u)\big)_{u\in[0,t]}
with constant birth rate
\lambda
and death rate
\mu

stopped at time
t
corresponds to a CPP with inverse tail distribution

F^{\rm IT}_{H}(\tau)=\begin{cases}1+\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}\left(e^{\alpha\tau}-1\right)&\alpha\neq 0;\\
1+\lambda\tau&\alpha=0,\end{cases}

where
\alpha=\lambda-\mu
. The corresponding node height cdf is

F_{H}(\tau)=\begin{cases}\frac{\lambda(e^{\alpha\tau}-1)}{\alpha+\lambda(e^{\alpha\tau}-1)}&\alpha\neq 0;\\
\frac{\lambda t}{1+\lambda\tau}.&\alpha=0.\end{cases}

(14)

Proof.

Start with the distribution of
N(t)
conditional on a single founder at time 0
[
10
, p480]
,

\mathbb{P}(N(t)=n\mid N(0)=1)=\begin{cases}\mu B(t)&n=0\\
(1-\lambda B(t))(1-\mu B(t))(\lambda B(t))^{n-1}&n=1,2,\ldots,\end{cases}

where

B(t)=\frac{e^{\alpha t}-1}{\lambda e^{\alpha t}-\mu}.

Conditional on non-extinction of the process up to time
t
,

\mathbb{P}(N(t)=n\mid N(0)=1,N(t)>0)=(1-\lambda B(t))(\lambda B(t))^{n-1},\qquad n=1,2,\ldots,

and so
(N(t)\mid N(0)=1,N(t)>0)
, and hence the number of coalescent events in the interval
[0,t]
, is distributed as
shifted geometric with “success” probability
\lambda B(t)
for any
t\geq 0
. This can be achieved with a node height distribution

\mathbb{P}(H\leq\tau)=\lambda B(\tau)=\lambda\frac{e^{\alpha\tau}-1}{\lambda e^{\alpha\tau}-\mu},\qquad\tau>0,

agreeing with Eq. (
14
).
The
\alpha=0
case follows by resubstituting
\lambda-\mu
for
\alpha
and taking the limit
\mu\to\lambda
.
∎

Note that if
\alpha<0
, then
\lim_{\tau\to\infty}F_{H}(\tau)=\lambda/\mu<1
, indicating that
\mathbb{P}(H=\infty)>0
,
or in other words, that the RRP may not converge to a single ancestral founder as
t\to\infty
. If one is specifically interested in processes
initiated from a single ancestral founder
it is convenient to define an alternate node height conditioned on convergence to single ancestor with rescaled
cdf

F^{\rm BD}_{H}(\tau)=\frac{F_{H}(\tau)}{F_{H}(\infty)}=\max(\lambda,\mu)\frac{e^{\alpha\tau}-1}{\lambda e^{\alpha\tau}-\mu},\qquad\tau,\mu,\lambda\geq 0.

(15)

Also define the reparametrisation introduced by
Wiuf 2018
and
Crespo et al. 2021

\delta=|\lambda-\mu|,\qquad\gamma=\frac{|\lambda-\mu|}{\max(\lambda,\mu)}.

(16)

The rescaled node height
cdf
is then

F^{\rm BD}_{H}(\tau)=\frac{e^{\delta\tau}-1}{e^{\delta\tau}-1+\gamma},\qquad\tau,\gamma,\delta>0,

(17)

and node height density is

f^{\rm BD}_{H}(\tau)=\frac{\gamma\delta e^{\delta t}}{(e^{\delta t}-1+\gamma)^{2}},\qquad\tau,\gamma,\delta>0.

An important observation is that the node height
cdf
, and hence the coalescent tree of a BD process conditioned on convergence to a
single ancestral founder, is invariant under interchange of

and
\mu
, as observed previously by

.

4
Feller diffusion as the limit of a CPP

Consider a linear BD process
\big(N_{\epsilon}(t)\big)_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
with birth rate
\lambda_{\epsilon}
and death rate
\mu_{\epsilon}

specified as functions of a parameter

\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
with the property

\begin{split}\lambda_{\epsilon}&=\tfrac{1}{2}\epsilon^{-1}+\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon),\\
\mu_{\epsilon}&=\tfrac{1}{2}\epsilon^{-1}-\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon),\end{split}

(18)

as
\epsilon\to 0
for fixed
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.
If
N_{\epsilon}(t)
is the number of particles alive at time
t
, set
X(t)=\epsilon N_{\epsilon}(t)
.
Then the limiting generator of the process
\big(X(t)\big)_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
as
\epsilon\to 0
is the Feller diffusion generator
Eq. (
1
). For a proof of this, see
Burden and Griffiths 2024
.

In terms of the parametrisation of a BD process defined by Eq. (

), it can be checked that by setting

\begin{split}\alpha&=\begin{cases}+\delta,&\lambda>\mu\\
-\delta,&\lambda<\mu\end{cases},\\
\epsilon&=\frac{\gamma}{2\delta}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma+\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{2})\right)\quad\text{as }\gamma\to 0,\end{split}

(19)

Eq. (
18
) is satisfied.
Hence in the limit
\gamma\to 0
at fixed
\delta

Heuristically, the

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j=2}^{k}\frac{x\mu(\tau_{j};|\alpha|)}{2\beta(\tau_{j};|\alpha|)}\right)e^{-(x/\beta(\tau_{k};|\alpha|)-x/\beta(t;|\alpha|))},

5
Sampling

The following quote from
Lambert 2018
summarises two sampling schemes of interest.

In the biology literature, there are mainly two classical sampling schemes […]. The first scheme, called the
Bernoulli sampling
scheme,
consists of selecting each extant tip with the same probability. The second scheme, called
k
-sampling
scheme, consists of drawing
uniformly
k
tips among the extant tips of the splitting tree conditioned upon [the number of extant tips]
>k
.

Lambert and Stadler 2013
show that a Bernoulli sampled CPP with sampling rate
\rho\in[0,1]
is itself a CPP with typical node height

H_{\rho}
, say, with inverse tail distribution and
cdf

\begin{split}F^{\rm IT}_{H_{\rho}}(\tau)&=1-\rho+\rho F^{\rm IT}_{H}(\tau),\\
F_{H_{\rho}}(\tau)&=\frac{\rho F_{H}(t)}{1-(1-\rho)F_{H}(t)}.\end{split}

(24)

On the other hand, a
k
-sampled CPP is not a CPP. The full story of
k
-sampling is more complicated
[
14
, Sections 3 and 4]
.
We explore
k
-sampling for a Feller diffusion in Subsections
5.3
,
5.4
and
5.5
.

5.1
Bernoulli sampling for a BD process

The typical node height
cdf
for a Bernoulli sampled BD process with constant rates
\lambda
and
\mu
and sampling rate
\rho
is,
from Eqs.(
14
) and (
24
),

F_{H_{\rho}}(\tau)=\begin{cases}\frac{\rho\lambda(e^{(\lambda-\mu)\tau}-1)}{\rho\lambda e^{(\lambda-\mu)\tau}-\mu+\lambda(1-\rho)}&\lambda\neq\mu;\\
\frac{\rho\lambda\tau}{1+\rho\lambda\tau}.&\lambda=\mu,\end{cases}\quad\tau\geq 0.

As
\tau\to\infty
we have

F_{H_{\rho}}(\infty)=\begin{cases}1&\lambda\geq\mu;\\
\frac{\rho\lambda}{\mu-\lambda(1-\rho)}.&\lambda<\mu.\end{cases}\quad\tau\geq 0.

Following the logic leading to Eq. (
15
), to condition on convergence of the RRP to a single ancestor in the sub-critical case,
define an alternate node height
cdf
for
\lambda\neq\mu
by

\displaystyle F_{H_{\rho}}^{\rm BD}(\tau)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{F_{H_{\rho}}(\tau)}{F_{H_{\rho}}(\infty)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\rho\lambda\frac{e^{(\lambda-\mu)\tau}-1}{\rho\lambda e^{(\lambda-\mu)\tau}-[\mu-\lambda(1-\rho)]}&\lambda>\mu;\\
[\mu-\lambda(1-\rho)]\frac{e^{(\lambda-\mu)\tau}-1}{\rho\lambda e^{(\lambda-\mu)\tau}-[\mu-\lambda(1-\rho)]}&\lambda<\mu;\end{cases}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{e^{(\lambda-\mu)\tau}-1}{e^{(\lambda-\mu)\tau}-1+\frac{\lambda-\mu}{\rho\lambda}}&\lambda>\mu;\\
\frac{e^{(\mu-\lambda)\tau}-1}{e^{(\mu-\lambda)\tau}-1+\frac{\mu-\lambda}{\mu-(1-\rho)\lambda}}&\lambda<\mu.\end{cases}

The two cases can be combined into a single form

F_{H_{\rho}}^{\rm BD}(\tau)=\frac{e^{\delta\tau}-1}{e^{\delta\tau}-1+\gamma_{\rho}},\qquad\tau,\delta,\gamma_{\rho}>0,

(25)

where

\delta=|\lambda-\mu|,\quad\gamma_{\rho}=\frac{|\lambda-\mu|}{\max(\rho\lambda,\mu-(1-\rho)\lambda)}.

(26)

This form of the node height
cdf
was found previously by
Wiuf 2018
by working from the distribution of sampled
particles rather than the associated CPP. It takes the same form as Eq. (
17
), and hence leads to an identical
set of coalescent time distributions up to a transformation of parameters.
Wiuf 2018
makes the point that, of the three
parameters
(\mu,\nu\ \rho)
, only two degrees of freedom are identifiable, and identifies symmetries resulting from reversal of birth and
death rates. The same symmetries have also been identified by
Stadler and Steel 2019
.

5.2
Poisson sampling for a Feller diffusion

To approach the Feller diffusion limit of a Bernoulli-sampled BD process in a way consistent with the scaling limit defined in
Eq. (
18
), define an
\epsilon
-dependent Bernoulli sampling rate

\rho_{\epsilon}:=2\nu\epsilon,\quad\nu>0,

and take the limit
\epsilon,\rho_{\epsilon}\to 0
while keeping the newly introduced parameter
\nu
fixed. This scaling is chosen so that,
in the diffusion limit
X(t)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\epsilon N_{\epsilon}(t)
, the sample size conditioned on a population size
X(t)=x

is distributed asymptotically as

{\text{Binom}(x/\epsilon,2\epsilon\nu)\sim\text{Pois}(2\nu x)}.

The meaning of Poisson sampling with parameter
\nu
is that, if the observed scaled population is
x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
randomly sample
N
individuals from the population, where
N\sim\text{Pois}(2\nu x)
.

In the diffusion limit, the parameters
\delta
and
\gamma_{\rho}
have finite limits. Clearly

\delta:=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}|\lambda_{\epsilon}-\mu_{\epsilon}|=|\alpha|
as before, while from Eqs. (
18
) and (
26
)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\nu}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{|\lambda_{\epsilon}-\mu_{\epsilon}|}{\max(\rho_{\epsilon}\lambda_{\epsilon},\mu_{\epsilon}-(1-\rho_{\epsilon})\lambda_{\epsilon})}

(27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\alpha|}{\max(\nu,\nu-\alpha)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\alpha}{\nu},&\alpha>0;\\
\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-\nu},&\alpha<0.\\
\end{cases}

The coalescent tree of a Poisson-sampled Feller diffusion can therefore be constructed from a CPP with node height
cdf

Eq. (
25
), with
\gamma_{\rho}
replaced by
\gamma_{\nu}
, that is

\displaystyle F_{H_{\nu}}^{\rm Feller}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\delta\tau}-1}{e^{\delta\tau}-1+\gamma_{\nu}},\qquad\tau,\delta,\gamma_{\nu}>0

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{2\beta(\tau;\alpha)}{2\beta(\tau;\alpha)+1/\nu}&\alpha>0;\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{\nu-\alpha}{\nu}\frac{2\beta(\tau;\alpha)}{2\beta(\tau;\alpha)+1/\nu}&\alpha<0;\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{\tau}{\tau+1/\nu}&\alpha=0,\end{cases}

(29)

where
\beta(\tau;\alpha)
is defined by Eq. (
22
).

From Eq. (
27
) it is obvious that
\gamma_{\nu}
and
\delta
remain invariant under the transformation

(\alpha,\nu)\rightarrow(\alpha^{\prime},\nu^{\prime})=(-\alpha,\nu-\alpha),

and that, provided
\nu-\alpha>0
, this defines a new Poisson-sampled Feller process with the same distribution of
coalescent times. This can be summarised as:

Corollary 1
.

Given a Poisson-sampled super-critical Feller diffusion with parameters
\nu>\alpha>0
, there exists a Poisson-sampled
sub-critical Feller diffusion with Feller parameter
-\alpha<0
, and sampling rate
\nu-\alpha>0
with the same distribution of coalescent times.

Given a Poisson-sampled super-critical Feller diffusion with parameters
\alpha>\nu>0
, there does not exist a Poisson-sampled
sub-critical Feller diffusion with the same distribution of coalescent times.

Given a Poisson-sampled sub-critical Feller diffusion with parameters
\alpha<0
,
\nu>0
, there exists a Poisson-sampled
super-critical Feller diffusion with Feller parameter
-\alpha>0
, and sampling rate
\nu-\alpha>0
with the same distribution of coalescent times.

These symmetries are analogues of the symmetries set out in
Stadler and Steel 2019
for a Bernoulli-sampled BD process.

7
Conclusions

We have surveyed a number of published results related to scaling limits of linear BD process and shown in each case the limit to be a manifestation
of a Feller diffusion. The unified approach taken has been via a limit of a CPP in which the node-height distribution becomes heavily skewed towards
zero while keeping the stem height fixed, consequently allowing the number of leaves of the associated coalescent tree to tend towards infinity.
The resulting coalescent tree acquires the familiar property of coming down from infinity, typical of diffusion limits in population genetics models.
The approach allows for efficient derivation of properties of Feller diffusions, including the joint distribution of coalescent times when conditioning
on an observed scaled population
X(t)=x
in Theorem
1
, where
X(t)
is the scaled population defined in
Section
4
.

One important property of branching processes associated with CPPs is that the reconstructed tree of the Bernoulli-sampled process is itself
a CPP. Analogously, we define in Subsection
5.2
a procedure called Poisson sampling in the diffusion limit. Whereas
for Bernoulli sampling with rate
\rho\in[0,1]
the sample size is binomial with success probability
\rho
and number of trials equal
to the population
N(t)
, for Poisson sampling with rate
\nu\in[0,\infty)
the sample size is Poisson distributed with mean
2\nu X(t)
.
In common with both Bernoulli-sampled and unsampled BD process, the coalescent tree of a Poisson-sampled Feller diffusion is found to correspond
to a CPP with node-height
cdf
taking the algebraic form of Eq. (
36
), but with parameter
\gamma
now defined by Eq. (
27
).

Lambert 2018
has developed a procedure for determining coalescent properties for
k
-sampled populations once the corresponding properties
of Bernoulli sampling have been determined. In Subsection
5.3
we have extended the
procedure to Poisson-sampled diffusions, and in Section
5.4
apply it to the
k
-sampled Feller diffusion to rederive the joint
distribution of coalescent times for a near-critical continuous-time Galton-Watson process previously found by
Harris et al. 2020
.
In Section
5.5
we demonstrate that the Limit Birth-Death process defined and analysed by
Crespo et al. 2021
is
in fact a
k
-sampled Feller diffusion conditioned on a current observed population
X(t)=x
, and that the methods employed should in principle have
further application to the
k
-sampling of diffusion processes.

In Section
6
we derive a formula in terms of hypergeometric functions for expected inter-coalescent waiting times of trees
derived from CPPs associated with Wiuf’s node-height distribution function Eq. (
36
). This functional form includes Bernoulli-sampled and
unsampled BD processes and Poisson-sampled Feller diffusions.

Analysis of A Mixed Finite Element Method for Poisson’s Equation with Rough Boundary Data
Huadong Gao, Yuhui Huang, Wen Xie

Abstract
This paper is concerned with finite element methods for Poisson’s equation with rough boundary data. Conventional methods require that the boundary data g g of the problem belongs to H 1 / 2 ​ ( ∂ Ω ) H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega) . However, in many applications one has to consider the case when g g is in L 2 ​ ( ∂ Ω ) L^{2}(\partial\Omega) only. To this end, very weak solutions are considered to establish the well-posedness of the problem. Most previously proposed numerical methods use regularizations of the boundary data. The main purpose of this paper is to use the Raviart–Thomas mixed finite element method to solve the Poisson equation with rough boundary data directly. We prove that the solution to the proposed mixed method converges to the very weak solution. In particular, we prove that the convergence rate of the numerical solution is O ⁡ ( h 1 / 2 ) O(h^{1/2}) in convex domains and O ⁡ ( h s − 1 / 2 ) O(h^{s-1/2}) in nonconvex domains, where s > 1 / 2 s>1/2 depends on the geometry of the domain. The analysis is based on a regularized approach and a rigorous estimate for the corresponding dual problem. Numerical experiments confirm the theoretically predicted convergence rates for the proposed mixed method for Poisson’s equation with rough boundary data. Keywords: elliptic boundary value problem, very weak solution, mixed finite element methods, optimal error estimate.

Introduction

In this paper,
we consider the Poisson equation with Dirichlet boundary condition

\displaystyle-\Delta u=f

\displaystyle\text{in}\quad\Omega,

(1.1)

\displaystyle u=g

\displaystyle\text{on}\quad\Gamma\!,

(1.2)

where
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz polygonal/polyhedral domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}(d=2,3)

and
\Gamma
denotes the boundary of
\Omega
.
In many applications, e.g., optimal control and shape optimization,
the Dirichlet boundary data
g(\bm{x})
is rough, i.e.,
g\notin H^{{1}/{2}}(\Gamma)
.
This implies that the solution
u
is not in
H^{1}(\Omega)
,
hence, it does not satisfy the standard variational formulation.
As a result, the conventional definition of the weak solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) must be modified.
The transposition method of Lions and Magenes
[
27
]
introduces
the very weak variational formulation: Seek
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, such that

\displaystyle(u,\Delta v)=\langle g,\partial_{\mathbf{n}}v\rangle-(f,v)\,,\quad\forall v\in V\,,

(1.3)

where
V=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.
It is easy to see that (
1.3
) only requires the boundary data
g\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
,
as the test function
v
is assumed to possess a higher regularity.
Due to its important applications,
numerical methods for solving the very weak solution to the elliptic/parabolic problems with rough boundary data
have been extensively studied, see
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
8
,
16
,
17
]
.
In particular,
the standard Lagrange finite element method(FEM) combined with
L^{2}(\Gamma)
-projection of the boundary data
g

is widely used.
In the pioneering work
[
4
]
, Berggren rigorously analyzed this approach
and proved that the
u_{h}
obtained by
L^{2}(\Gamma)
-projection converges to
the very weak solution
u
for general Lipschitz polygonal/polyhedral domains.
However, the test space
V
to define (
1.3
) only applies to convex domains.
For a two dimensional nonconvex polygon, the important work of Apel, Nicaise and Pfefferer
[
1
]
presents some remedies
by using an enlarged test space

V=(H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\oplus\textrm{Span}\{\xi(r)r^{\lambda}\sin(\lambda\theta)\}
,
where
(r,\theta)
denotes the polar coordinate and
\lambda=\pi/\Theta
with
\Theta
being the re-entrant angle.
By using adaptive mesh strategy and the singular complement method,
they in
[
2
]
improved the performance
of the Lagrange FEM for two dimensional nonconvex polygonal domains.
Moreover, Apel et al. in
[
1
]
proposed a regularized approach
that introduces a sequence of regularized functions
\{g^{h}\}\in H^{{1}/{2}}(\Gamma)

such that

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\|g^{h}-g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}=0
.
Then, standard linear FEM can be applied with the boundary data
g^{h}
.
It should be noted that using Lagrange FEM to solve Poisson’s equations with
L^{2}
boundary
data needs to modify the original boundary data
g
, e.g., the
L^{2}(\Gamma)
-projection
[
4
]
.
Thus, an additional step is needed to preprocess the Dirichlet boundary data.
However, numerical evidences show that the
L^{2}(\Gamma)
-projection approach
may introduce certain artificial oscillations near the singular boundary points.
Similar approach can also be found in
numerical methods for elliptic problems with discontinuous Dirichlet boundary data,
see
[
13
]
. For two dimensional elliptic equations with discontinuous
boundary data, Houston and Wihler in
[
25
]

introduced a weak form in terms of weighted Sobolev spaces.
They proposed an interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin(DG) method,
where a posteriori error estimation is also derived.
It should be noted that the boundary data in the DG method is used implicitly
and regularization of
g
is not needed.

In this paper, we use a Raviart–Thomas mixed FEM
to solve the problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) with
L^{2}
boundary data.
There have been extensive studies on Raviart–Thomas mixed FEMs,
see
[
6
,
18
,
28
,
29
]
.
Mixed FEMs have been widely used in boundary control
problems governed by elliptic PDEs, e.g., see
[
11
,
22
,
24
]
.
For the model problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
),
the mixed method introduces an extra variable
{\bm{\sigma}}=\nabla u
.
Then, by using integration by parts,
there holds

\displaystyle({\bm{\sigma}},\bm{\chi})+(u,\mathrm{div}\,\bm{\chi})={\langle g,\bm{\chi}\cdot\mathbf{n}\rangle}

(1.4)

for any smooth functions
u
,
{\bm{\sigma}}
and
\bm{\chi}
.
Therefore, the Dirichlet boundary data
g
is used in an implicit way in the above weak formulation.
Motivated by this observation, we propose to use the lowest order mixed FEM

\mathcal{R\!T}_{\!\!0}\times\mathcal{DG}_{\!0}
to solve (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), see Subsection
2.2
.
However, all previous analyses of Raviart–Thomas mixed FEM
require that the boundary data
g\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
at least.
In this work, we prove the numerical solution
u_{h}
of the lowest order mixed FEM

\mathcal{R\!T}_{\!\!0}\times\mathcal{DG}_{\!0}
converges to the very weak solution
u

and establish an optimal error estimate.
The main difficulty in the analysis of the
\mathcal{R\!T}_{\!\!0}\times\mathcal{DG}_{\!0}
mixed FEM
lies in the fact that the standard mixed variational form does not hold for
L^{2}(\Gamma)
boundary data.
As the very weak solution
u\notin H^{1}(\Omega)
, one has
{\bm{\sigma}}\notin L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Consequently, the classical error estimate framework for mixed FEM cannot be applied.
The analysis of mixed FEM to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) is nonstandard.
In this work, we provide an optimal
L^{2}
-norm error estimate.
The analysis is based on a regularized approach.
We split the error into two parts: the regularization error
u-u^{h}

and the approximation error
u^{h}-u_{h}
, where
u^{h}
denotes the solution to the regularized problem.
Furthermore, the results can be extended to the case
g\in H^{s}(\Gamma)
for
0<s<1/2

with an improved convergence rate.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows.
In section
2
, we present a mixed FEM for solving the elliptic problem with rough
boundary data and main theoretical results.
In Section
3
, we introduce some useful lemmas.
In Section
4
, we prove the optimal error estimate for the mixed FEM.
In Section
5
, we extend the results to problems with more regular boundary data.
In Section
6
,
several numerical examples are provided to confirm our theoretical analysis and demonstrate
the effectiveness of the mixed FEM.
Some concluding remarks are given in section
7
.

A mixed FEM and main results

We will introduce some standard notations and define

in Subsection
2.1
.
Then, we present the mixed finite element method and main results on the convergence
in Subsection

2.1
The very weak solution

We consider a bounded polygonal (for
d=2
) or polyhedral (for
d=3
) domain
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d},d=2,3
,
with a Lipschitz boundary
\Gamma
.
For any two functions
u
,
v\in{L}^{2}(\Omega)
,
we denote the
{L}^{2}(\Omega)
inner product in domain
\Omega
and the
L^{2}
-norm by

(u,v)=\,\int_{\Omega}u(\bm{x})\,v(\bm{x})\,{\mathrm{d}}\bm{x},\qquad{\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}=(u,u)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

Similarly, the inner product and norm on the boundary are defined by

\langle g,\omega\rangle=\,\int_{\Gamma}g(\bm{x})\,\omega(\bm{x})\,{\mathrm{d}}\bm{x}\,,\qquad{\left\|g\right\|_{{L}^{2}(\Gamma)}}={\langle g,g\rangle}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

Let
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
be the Sobolev space defined on
\Omega
,
and
{W}^{k,p}_{0}(\Omega)
be the subspace of
W^{k,p}(\Omega)

with zero trace. By conventional notations,
we define
H^{k}(\Omega):=W^{k,2}(\Omega)

and
{H}^{k}_{0}(\Omega):={W}^{k,2}_{0}(\Omega)
.
For a positive real number
s=k+w
, with
w\in(0,1)
,
we define
H^{s}(\Omega)=(H^{k}(\Omega),H^{k+1}(\Omega))_{[w]}

via the complex interpolation, see
[
5
, Theorem 6.4.5]
and
[
26
]
.
To abbreviate notations, we use
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
and
\|\cdot\|_{H^{r}(\Omega)}

to denote the
L^{2}
and
H^{r}
-norm of the inner product functions in the domain

\Omega
, respectively.
Moreover, we define
{\mathbf{H}}(\mathrm{div},\Omega)
by

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}(\mathrm{div},\Omega):=\{{\bm{\sigma}}|{\bm{\sigma}}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{d},~\mathrm{div}{\bm{\sigma}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\}\,,

(2.1)

with norm
\|{\bm{\sigma}}\|_{{\mathbf{H}}(\mathrm{div})}=\|{\bm{\sigma}}\|_{{\mathbf{H}}(\mathrm{div},\Omega)}:=(\|{\bm{\sigma}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\mathrm{div}{\bm{\sigma}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)})^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
For simplicity, we omit
\Omega
and define
{\mathbf{H}}(\mathrm{div}):={\mathbf{H}}(\mathrm{div},\Omega)
.

Now we introduce the definition of very weak solutions to the model problem
(
1.1
)-(
1.2
) with
L^{2}
boundary data
g
for general polyhedral domains.
It should be noted that Apel, Nicaise and Pfefferer in
[
1
]

investigated the very weak solution for general two dimensional polygonal domains.
Based on the results in
[
1
]
, we define the test space
V
of very weak solutions by

V=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega):\Delta v\in L^{2}(\Omega)\}.

(2.2)

Since
\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
for
v\in V
,we can define the norm of
V
as

\|v\|_{V}=\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(2.3)

The following lemma addresses the very weak solution in general polygonal/polyhedral domains.

Lemma 2.1

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz polygonal or polyhedral domain.
Let
g\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
and
f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
,
then there exists a unique solution
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
satisfying

\displaystyle(u,\Delta v)=\langle g,\partial_{\mathbf{n}}v\rangle-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(f,v)_{-1,1}}\,,\quad\forall v\in V\,.

(2.4)

Moreover, there holds

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\|f\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}),

(2.5)

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{-1,1}
represents the duality pairing between
H^{-1}(\Omega)
and
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Noting the regularity result in Lemma
3.4
and
trace inequalities in Corollary
3.3
, we have

\|v\|_{H^{1+s}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=C\|v\|_{V}.

(2.6)

Hence, we obtain the embedding
V\hookrightarrow H^{s+1}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.
Since
s>1/2
, combining Lemma
3.3
there holds

\|\partial_{n}v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|\partial_{n}v\|_{H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{1+s}(\Omega)}\leq C\|v\|_{V},\qquad\forall v\in V.

(2.7)

Then the right side of
2.4
defines a bounded linear functional on
V
.
To obtain the inf-sup condition, we can follow the same approach in
[
1
, Lemma 2.3]
, as
the proof remains valid in the three dimensional case.

It should be remarked that the above definition for the very weak solution is an extension of
the one defined in
[
1
]
for two dimensional problems.
Due to the equivalence of
\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
and
\|v\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}
in convex domains,
the test space
V
in this case is the same to
H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

In Berggren’s work
[
4
]
, he also defined
the very weak solution for Poisson’s equations on general Lipschitz polygonal or polyhedral domains.
If
g\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
and
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
Berggren’s approach is equivalent to the above definition (
2.4
).
It should be noted that the source term
f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
may also introduce singularities.
However, the emphasis of this paper is on the error analysis of Poisson’s problems with rough boundary data.
Thus, we shall assume
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

\displaystyle({\bm{\sigma}}_{h},\,\bm{\chi}_{h})+(u_{h},\,\mathrm{div}\bm{\chi}_{h})=\langle g,\,\bm{\chi}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}\rangle\,,

\displaystyle\forall\bm{\chi}_{h}\in\mathcal{R\!T}_{\!\!0}\,,

\displaystyle-(\mathrm{div}{\bm{\sigma}}_{h},\,v_{h})=(f,v_{h})\,,

\displaystyle\forall\,v_{h}\in\mathcal{DG}_{\!0}\,.

By noting the fact that

The existence and uniqueness of the numerical solution

[
6
]

In addition, it should be pointed out that higher order elements are not

We present our main results for the mixed FEM (

)-(

) in the following

Theorem 2.2

Let

,

,
the mixed FEM (
2.8
)-(
2.9
) admits a unique solution

which
converges to the very weak solution

defined in (
2.4
),and there holds

\displaystyle\|u_{h}-u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+Ch^{s}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,,

(2.10)

where

is a positive constant independent of

,
and the index

is defined in (

).

In the rest of this paper, we denote by

.

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{1+s}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\textrm{with}\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}s=1&\textrm{if $\Omega$ is convex,}\\[2.0pt]
s=\sup\{\lambda_{\textrm{2D}}\}-\epsilon&\textrm{in 2D},\\[2.0pt]
s=\sup\{\lambda_{\textrm{3D}}\}-\epsilon&\textrm{in 3D},\end{array}\right.

where

\lambda_{\textrm{2D}}
is defined in (
3.16
)
and

\lambda_{\textrm{3D}}
is defined in (
3.18
)-(
3.19
),
respectively,
and
\epsilon>0
is any arbitrarily small number.
We can see that for Lipschitz polygonal/polyhedral domains,
the solution
u
to (
3.14
) always belongs to
H^{3/2+\epsilon}(\Omega)
,
see
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
.
Recall the definition of
V
in (
2.2
),
the
H^{3/2+\epsilon}
regularity
ensures that
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}v\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
for any
v\in V
.

The standard Raviart–Thomas mixed FEM for (
3.14
) is to
seek
({\bm{\sigma}}_{h},u_{h})\in\mathcal{R\!T}_{\!\!0}\times\mathcal{DG}_{\!0}
, such that

\displaystyle({\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\,,\,\bm{\chi}_{h})+(u_{h}\,,\mathrm{div}\bm{\chi}_{h})=0\,,

\displaystyle\forall\bm{\chi}_{h}\in\mathcal{R\!T}_{\!\!0}\,,

(3.24)

\displaystyle-(\mathrm{div}{\bm{\sigma}}_{h},v_{h})=(f,v_{h}),

\displaystyle\forall\,v_{h}\in\mathcal{DG}_{\!0}\,.

(3.25)

The following estimates hold
[
15
, Lemma 6.1]

\displaystyle\|{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{s}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\|u_{h}-u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,,

(3.26)

where the index
s
is defined in (
3
).

4
The proof of the main result

Following the idea of Apel, Nicaise and Pfefferer in
[
1
]
, we introduce a regularized
elliptic problem for the original Poisson equation (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).
We shall introduce the linear Lagrange element space
P_{1}
on the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
Moreover, let
P_{1}^{\partial}:=P_{1}|_{\Gamma}
.
Let
g^{h}\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
denote a sequence of functions such that

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\|g-g^{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}=0.

(4.1)

The construction of
g^{h}
can be done by the
L^{2}
-projection of
g
onto
P_{1}^{\partial}
,
which is analyzed in Berggren’s pioneering work
[
4
]
.
Alternatively, one can use the Carstensen interpolant
[
9
]
.

For the sequence
\{g^{h}\}
, there holds
[
4
]
,
[
1
, Lemma 2.14]

\displaystyle\|g^{h}-g\|_{H^{-s}(\Gamma)}\leq Ch^{t+s}\|g\|_{H^{t}(\Gamma)},\quad\forall\,t,~s\in[0,1],

(4.2)

provided
g\in H^{t}(\Gamma)
.

Now, for given
g^{h}\in H^{{1}/{2}}(\Gamma)
,
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
we introduce a mixed weak form: Seek
{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}\in{\mathbf{H}}(\mathrm{div}),u^{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, such that

\displaystyle({\bm{\sigma}}^{h},\,\bm{\chi})+(u^{h},\,\mathrm{div}\bm{\chi})=\langle g^{h},\,\bm{\chi}\cdot\mathbf{n}\rangle\,,

\displaystyle\forall~\bm{\chi}\in{\mathbf{H}}(\mathrm{div})\,,

(4.3)

\displaystyle(-\mathrm{div}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h},\,v)=(f,\,v)\,,

\displaystyle\forall~v\in L^{2}(\Omega)\,.

(4.4)

The existence of the solution
({\bm{\sigma}}^{h},u^{h})
to the above mixed variational form
is obvious
[
6
,
18
]
, which satisfies the following standard estimate

\displaystyle\|{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}\|_{{\mathbf{H}}(\mathrm{div})}+\|u^{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g^{h}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}\right).

(4.5)

The following lemma shows the convergence of solutions
\{u^{h}\}

of the auxiliary problem to the very weak solution
u
.
This result follows directly from
[
1
, Corollary 3.3]
, since the unique weak solution
to the primal formulation is also the unique weak solution to its corresponding mixed formulation.
While the original corollary is stated for polygonal domains,
the proof is still valid for Lipschitz polyhedral domain
as Lemma
3.3
provides the required trace regularity.

Lemma 4.1

For given
g^{h}\in\{g^{h}\}
, the variational form
(
4.3
)-(
4.4
)
admits a unique solution
u^{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
The limit
u:=\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}u^{h}
exists and is the very weak solution.
Moreover, there holds,

\displaystyle\|u^{h}-u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\,,

(4.6)

where the index
s
is defined in (
3
).

Based on the regularization error (
4.6
)
in Lemma
4.1
,
we prove the error estimate (
2.10
)
in the main Theorem
2.2
.
The proof consists of two steps. In the first step,
we derive an estimate for
{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}
.
In the second part, we present an error estimate for
u^{h}-u_{h}
.

By subtracting the regularized equations
(
4.3
)-(
4.4
)
from mixed FEM (
2.8
)-(
2.9
), we deduce the error equations

\displaystyle({\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h},\bm{\chi}_{h})+(u^{h}-u_{h},\mathrm{div}\bm{\chi}_{h})=\langle g^{h}-g\,,\bm{\chi}_{h}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle,

\displaystyle\forall\bm{\chi}_{h}\in\mathcal{R\!T}_{\!\!0}\,,

(4.7)

\displaystyle(\mathrm{div}({\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}),v_{h})=0,

\displaystyle\forall v_{h}\in\mathcal{DG}_{0}\,.

(4.8)

Here, we shall introduce the
L^{2}
projector of
u^{h}
,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u^{h},v_{h})=0,\qquad\forall v_{h}\in\mathcal{DG}_{\!0}\,.

Then, the projection error satisfies

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u^{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch\|u^{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

(4.9)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g^{h}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+Ch^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.

where we have used an inverse inequality and the fact that
g^{h}\in P_{1}^{\partial}
.
As
\mathrm{div}\mathcal{R\!T}_{\!\!0}\subset\mathcal{DG}_{\!0}
, the error equations
(
4.7
)-(
4.8
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle({\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h},\bm{\chi}_{h})+(\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u_{h},\mathrm{div}\bm{\chi}_{h})=\langle g^{h}-g\,,\bm{\chi}_{h}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle,

\displaystyle\forall\bm{\chi}_{h}\in\mathcal{R\!T}_{\!\!0}\,,

(4.10)

\displaystyle(\mathrm{div}({\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}),v_{h})=0,

\displaystyle\forall v_{h}\in\mathcal{DG}_{0}\,.

(4.11)

An estimate for
{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}
is given in the lemma below.

Lemma 4.2

If
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
g\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
and
g^{h}\in H^{{1}/{2}}(\Gamma)
, we have

\displaystyle\|{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})\,,

(4.12)

where the constant
C
is independent of
h
.

Proof.

From the commuting diagram (
3
), the equation (
4.11
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle(\mathrm{div}\,({\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}),v_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{div}\,({\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}),v_{h})+(\mathrm{div}\,(\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}),v_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{div}(\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}),v_{h})=0,\quad\forall v_{h}\in\mathcal{DG}_{\!0}\,,

which, by taking
v_{h}=\mathrm{div}(\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h})
implies the fact

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}(\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h})=0\,.

(4.13)

Next, by taking
\bm{\chi}_{h}=\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}
into (
4.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=(\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}^{h},\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h})+\langle g^{h}-g,(\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+\|g^{h}-g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\|(\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

(by (
3.1
))

\displaystyle\leq C(\|{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+h\|\mathrm{div}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})\|\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

(by inverse inquality)

\displaystyle\quad+Ch^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|g^{h}-g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\|(\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}\|_{{\mathbf{H}}(\mathrm{div})}\|\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+Ch^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\|\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\|_{{\mathbf{H}}(\mathrm{div})}

(by (
4.5
))

\displaystyle\leq C(h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g^{h}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)})\|\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,,

which, by using an inverse inequality for
g^{h}
, leads to the result below

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}).

(4.14)

From (
3.1
), we see that the quasi-projection estimate holds

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+Ch\|\mathrm{div}\,{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

(4.15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|g^{h}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})+Ch\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

By combining estimate (
4.14
)
and projection estimate (
4.15
),
the error estimate (
4.12
) is proved.

Next, we turn to prove the main error estimate (
2.10
) for
u_{h}
.

Proof.

Notice that

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|u-u^{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|u^{h}-\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,.

(4.16)

Clearly, we only need to estimate the last term.
To this end, we introduce a dual Poisson’s equation with a homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition,

\displaystyle-\Delta z=\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u_{h}\,,

\displaystyle\textrm{in}~\Omega\,,

(4.17)

\displaystyle z=0\,,

\displaystyle\textrm{on}~\partial\Omega\,.

By introducing
\bm{\omega}=\nabla z
as an extra variable,
the mixed FEM for the above equation is to seek
(\bm{\omega}_{h},z_{h})\in\mathcal{R\!T}_{\!\!0}\times\mathcal{DG}_{\!0}

such that

\displaystyle(\bm{\omega}_{h},\bm{\nu}_{h})+(z_{h},\mathrm{div}\bm{\nu}_{h})=0\,,

\displaystyle\forall\bm{\nu}_{h}\in\mathcal{R\!T}_{\!\!0}\,,

(4.18)

\displaystyle-(\mathrm{div}\bm{\omega}_{h},v_{h})=(\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u_{h},v_{h})\,,

\displaystyle\forall v_{h}\in\mathcal{DG}_{\!0}\,.

(4.19)

By the standard error estimate of mixed FEMs for elliptic equation
[
15
]
, there holds

\displaystyle\|\nabla z-\bm{\omega}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(h^{s}\|\bm{\omega}\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{s}(\Omega)}+h\|\mathrm{div}\bm{\omega}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\leq Ch^{s}\|\mathcal{P}_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,,

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{h}\nabla z-\bm{\omega}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{s}\,\|\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,,

(4.20)

\displaystyle\|z-z_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch\|\mathcal{P}_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,.

By taking
v_{h}=\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u_{h}
into (
4.19
), we can see that
the
L^{2}
-norm of
\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u_{h}
satisfies

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=-(\mathrm{div}\bm{\omega}_{h},\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u_{h})

(by (
4.10
))

\displaystyle=\langle g-g^{h},\bm{\omega}_{h}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle+({\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h},\bm{\omega}_{h})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}_{1}+\mathcal{J}_{2}\,.

(4.21)

By using the trace inequality in Corollary
3.3
,
the projector error for the normal trace (
3.10
)
and the inverse inequality (
3.11
),
the term
\mathcal{J}_{1}
can be bounded by

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{J}_{1}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\langle g-g^{h},(\bm{\omega}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\nabla z)\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle+\langle g-g^{h},(\Pi_{h}\nabla z-\nabla z)\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle+\langle g-g^{h},\nabla z\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|g-g^{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\|(\bm{\omega}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\nabla z)\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\|g-g^{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\|(\Pi_{h}\nabla z-\nabla z)\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\quad+\|g-g^{h}\|_{H^{-(s-\frac{1}{2})}(\Gamma)}\|\nabla z\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|_{H^{(s-\frac{1}{2})}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|(\bm{\omega}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\nabla z)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+Ch^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla z\|_{H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\quad+Ch^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\|z\|_{H^{s+1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+Ch^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla z\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\quad+Ch^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\|z\|_{H^{s+1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\|\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,.

(4.22)

By the standard error estimate (
4
), the term
\mathcal{J}_{2}

can be bounded by

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{J}_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq|({\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h},\bm{\omega}_{h}-\nabla z)|+|({\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h},\nabla z)|

(by (
4.11
))

\displaystyle=|({\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h},\bm{\omega}_{h}-\nabla z)|+|(\mathrm{div}({\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}),z-\mathcal{P}_{h}z)|

\displaystyle\leq\|{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\bm{\omega}_{h}-\nabla z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\mathrm{div}({\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|z-\mathcal{P}_{h}z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

(by (
4.12
) and (
4
))

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{s}\left(h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\|\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+Ch\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|z\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(h^{s}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+h^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\right)\|\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,.

(4.23)

Taking estimates (
4
) and (
4
) into (
4
),
the desired estimate follows

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+Ch^{s}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,.

(4.24)

Finally, combining estimates in (
2.5
),
(
4.6
), (
4.9
) and
(
4.24
), the main results in Theorem
2.2
is proved.

5
Estimates for problems with more regular boundary data

Classical mixed FEM theory covers the case
g\in H^{t}(\Gamma)
with
t\geq 1/2
.
In this section, we investigate the model problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
)
with boundary data
g\in H^{t}(\Gamma)
for
0<t<1/2
.
An improved convergence rate can be derived and the
main results for the mixed FEM (
2.8
)-(
2.9
) are
summarized in the following corollary.

Corollary 5.1

Let
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
g\in H^{t}(\Gamma)
for
0<t<1/2
.
The mixed FEM (
2.8
)-(
2.9
) admits a unique solution
u_{h}

satisfying the following estimate

\displaystyle\|u_{h}-u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{t+s-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{H^{t}(\Gamma)}+Ch^{s}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,,

(5.1)

where
u
is the very weak solution.

Proof.

The regularized boundary data
g^{h}
satisfies

\displaystyle\|g-g^{h}\|_{H^{-s}(\Gamma)}\leq Ch^{t+s}\|g\|_{H^{t}(\Gamma)}

(5.2)

From
[
1
, Remark 5.4]
, we have an improved estimate for
the regularized solution
u^{h}

\displaystyle\|u^{h}-u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{t+s-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{H^{t}(\Gamma)}\,.

(5.3)

And the projection error satisfies

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u^{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch\|u^{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

(5.4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch(\|g^{h}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{t+\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{H^{t}(\Gamma)}+Ch\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,.

Recall the proof of Lemma
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+h\|\mathrm{div}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})\|\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+Ch^{t-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{H^{t}(\Gamma)}\|(\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq C(h^{t-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{H^{t}(\Gamma)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})\|\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,.

(5.5)

and the quasi projection estimate

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{h}{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|g^{h}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}+C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

(5.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{t-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{H^{t}(\Gamma)}+C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,.

An improved estimate for
{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}
follows directly

\displaystyle\|{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(h^{t-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{H^{t}(\Gamma)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})\,.

(5.7)

Then, from (
4
) the error
\|\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
satisfies

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=-(\mathrm{div}\bm{\omega}_{h},\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u_{h})

(by (
4.10
))

\displaystyle=\langle g-g^{h},\bm{\omega}_{h}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle-({\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h},\bm{\omega}_{h})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}_{3}+\mathcal{J}_{4}\,,

(5.8)

where
\mathcal{J}_{3}
can be bounded by

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{J}_{3}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|g-g^{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\|(\bm{\omega}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\nabla z)\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\|g-g^{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\|(\Pi_{h}\nabla z-\nabla z)\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\quad+\|g-g^{h}\|_{H^{-(s-\frac{1}{2})}(\Gamma)}\|\nabla z\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|_{H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{t-\frac{1}{2}}\|(\bm{\omega}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\nabla z)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|g\|_{H^{t}(\Gamma)}+Ch^{t+s-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla z\|_{H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}\|g\|_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\quad+Ch^{t+s-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{H^{t}(\Gamma)}\|z\|_{H^{s+1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{t+s-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{H^{t}(\Gamma)}\|\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(5.9)

and
\mathcal{J}_{4}
can be bounded by

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{J}_{4}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|{\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\bm{\omega}_{h}-\nabla z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\mathrm{div}({\bm{\sigma}}^{h}-{\bm{\sigma}}_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|z-\mathcal{P}_{h}z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

(by (
5.7
) and (
4
))

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{s}\left(h^{t-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{H^{t}(\Gamma)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\|\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+Ch\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|z\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(h^{t+s-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{H^{t}(\Gamma)}+h^{s}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\|\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,.

(5.10)

Taking the above two estimates into (
5
) yields an improved estimate

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{h}u^{h}-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(h^{t+s-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{H^{t}(\Gamma)}+h^{s}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

(5.11)

At last, combining the above estimates
with (
5.3
) and (
5.4
),
Corollary
5.1
is proved.

Numerical results

In this section, we provide several numerical examples to demonstrate the effectiveness of
the proposed mixed FEM (
2.8
)-(
2.9
).
All computations are performed by the free software FEniCSx

[
7
]
,
and the meshes are generated by Gmsh
[

]
.

Example 6.1

In the first example, we take a rectangular domain
\Omega=(-1,1)\times(0,1)
.
Then we consider the Poisson equation with Dirichlet boundary condition

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u&=0\qquad\text{in}\quad\Omega_{\omega},\\
u&=g\qquad\text{on}\quad\Gamma_{\omega},\end{aligned}\right.

(6.1)

where, the exact solution in polar coordinates is defined by

u(r,\theta)=r^{-0.4999}\sin(-0.4999\theta).

(6.2)

As
u
is harmonic in
\Omega
, the source term
f=0
belongs to
L^{2}(\Omega)
and

Conclusions

In this paper,
we have extended the applicability of the Raviart–Thomas mixed method by rigorously proving that
it is suitable for solving elliptic problems with rough Dirichlet boundary data.
To the best of our knowledge, no analysis has been established for
the Raviart–Thomas mixed FEM for solving problems with boundary data in
L^{2}(\Gamma)
only.
More important is that the Raviart–Thomas mixed FEM does not need to modify the boundary data,
although our proof is based on a regularized approach.
Numerical experiments presented in this work demonstrate the efficiency of the method and
confirm our theoretical analysis.

In this work, we confine our study to rough boundary data problems.
It is assumed that the source
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
However, our results can be applied to the case
f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
,
see
[
19
]
, where Gjerde et al. use mixed FEM
to solve Poisson’s problems with line sources.
As the exact solution
u\notin H^{1}(\Omega)
,
we only consider the lowest order mixed FEM
\mathcal{R\!T}_{\!\!0}\times\mathcal{DG}_{\!0}
.
Moreover, adaptive meshes might improve the performance of the numerical methods,
in particular for the nonconvex domain.

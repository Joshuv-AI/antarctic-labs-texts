Phase-field approximation of sharp-interface energies accounting for lattice symmetry
Sergio Conti, Vito Crismale, Adriana Garroni, Annalisa Malusa

Abstract
. We present a phase-field approximation of sharp-interface energies defined on partitions, designed for modeling grain boundaries in polycrystals. The independent variable takes values in the orthogonal group O ⁡ ( d ) \mathrm{O}(d) modulo a lattice point group 𝒢 \mathcal{G} , reflecting the crystallographic symmetries of the underlying lattice. In the sharp-interface limit, the surface energy exhibits a Read-Shockley-type behavior for small misorientation angles, scaling as θ ​ | log ⁡ θ | \theta|\log\theta| . The regularized functionals are applicable to grain growth simulation and the reconstruction of grain boundaries from imaging data.



1.
Introduction

We investigate sharp-interface variational models for polycrystalline materials that fully respect the symmetry of the underlying lattice.
The energy is defined on partitions of a domain, each subset – representing an individual grain – being endowed with an orientation modeled as an element of the quotient space
{\mathrm{O}(d)}/{\mathcal{G}}
. Here,
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathrm{O}(d)
is a finite point group describing the symmetries of a reference lattice, and the local orientation is measured relative to this reference.
The total energy is given by an integral over the interfaces separating adjacent grains.
The
integrand, or surface energy density, depends on the orientations of the two neighboring grains and on the unit normal to the interface.
To ensure that the class of piecewise-constant orientation fields is closed, we assume the
energy density grows superlinearly as the misorientation angle tends to zero.

A distinctive feature of the model is that the orientation variable takes values in a manifold rather than a linear space. Classical analytical tools such as regularization, approximation, and interpolation are therefore not directly applicable, and a dedicated framework for manifold-valued fields is required.

Models of this type are widely used to study
polycrystalline structures in metals and alloys, see, for instance,

[
13
,
8
,
26
,
37
]
and references therein.
Dependence on both orientations and lattice symmetry is likewise central to related problems such as polygonal partitions
with prescribed area and minimal perimeter
[
24
]
, and optimal quantization or location of measures
[
11
,
12
]
.
The point-group symmetry also enters variational models of
fractional defects, see for example
[
6
,
5
,
31
]
.
Analytical aspects such as semicontinuity and relaxation – though without an explicit treatment of the symmetry group
\mathcal{G}
– have been addressed in

[
2
,
34
,
15
]
.

The principal objective of this paper is to develop a diffuse-interface, or phase-field, approximation of the sharp-interface model described above. Specifically, we construct a
phase-field approximation for surface energies with densities that vanish as the misorientation between grains tends to zero, but do so in a superlinear manner. Our approach builds on the model introduced for cohesive fracture in
[
19
,
21
]
.
The analysis relies on
two key abstract ingredients.
First, compactness properties
are formulated within the framework of
metric-space-valued generalizations of Sobolev spaces and spaces of functions of bounded variation, as developed in
[
36
,
1
]
. Second, the construction of recovery sequences is based on a lifting theorem for
\mathrm{BV}
functions taking values in compact manifolds, established in
[
14
]

(here we provide a simpler proof valid for our specific case, see Theorem
2.12
). These tools allow us to rigorously prove
the convergence of the phase-field approximation to the sharp-interface limit.

To describe the sharp-interface model in more detail, let
\{E_{i}\}_{i}
be
a partition
of a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, representing the material body.
Each set
E_{i}

corresponds to an individual grain with assigned orientation
R_{i}\in\mathrm{O}(d)
,
yielding a piecewise-constant function
\beta=\sum_{i}\chi_{E_{i}}R_{i}
as the order parameter.
The grain boundaries are contained in the discontinuity set
J_{\beta}
of

\beta
and the corresponding energy is given by

(1.1)

\int_{J_{\beta}}\Psi(R^{-},R^{+},\nu_{\beta})\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}=\sum_{i\neq j}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(\partial^{*}E_{i}\cap\partial^{*}E_{j})\Psi(R_{i},R_{j},\nu_{ij}),

where
\Psi:\mathrm{O}(d)\times\mathrm{O}(d)\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}\to[0,\infty)
is the surface energy density,
\nu_{\beta}
denotes
the measure-theoretic normal to
J_{\beta}
, and

\nu_{ij}
is the normal to the intersection of the reduced boundaries

\partial^{*}E_{i}\cap\partial^{*}E_{j}
.
A natural function space for such
energies is the space of Special functions with Bounded Variation with values in
\mathrm{O}(d)

and vanishing absolutely continuous part of the gradient,

(1.2)

\mathrm{SBV}_{0}(\Omega;\mathrm{O}(d)):=\{\beta\in\mathrm{SBV}(\Omega;\mathrm{O}(d))\colon\nabla\beta=0\}.

For the variational problems associated with (
1.1
) to be well-posed in this space, certain conditions on
\Psi
are required. In particular,
lower semicontinuity of the energy follows from
the
\mathrm{BV}
-ellipticity of
\Psi
(see
[
3
, Theorem 5.14]
). Additionally, it is essential that
\mathrm{SBV}_{0}(\Omega;\mathrm{O}(d))

is sequentially closed under the relevant convergence. This requires that sequences with bounded energy cannot converge to fields with diffuse gradient. A sufficient condition is that

\Psi
has infinite slope at zero,

\lim_{|R^{+}-R^{-}|\to 0}\frac{\Psi(R^{+},R^{-},\nu)}{|R^{+}-R^{-}|}=+\infty

uniformly in
\nu
,
see

[
3
, Theorem 4.7]
.
Denoting by
\theta=|R^{+}-R^{-}|
the relative mismatch between neighboring grain orientations, the specific growth law
\theta|\log\theta|
of
\Psi
for small
\theta
was first identified
by Read and Shockley
in the ’50s
[
35
]
.
In two dimensions, this scaling has been recently rigorously derived as the limit of a model that accounts for the elastic distortion induced by incompatibilities and crystal defects at the boundary, see
[
33
,
28
]
and

[
27
]
.

Since
\beta
is interpreted as a field taking values in
\mathrm{O}(d)
, invariance of
\Psi
under the action of
\mathcal{G}
must be enforced. In particular,
boundaries
between regions whose orientations agree up to multiplication by an element of
\mathcal{G}

are spurious, as they
represent the same physical state, and do not contribute to the energy. Accordingly, we require

\Psi(R,GR,\nu)=0
for all
R\in\mathrm{O}(d)
,
G\in\mathcal{G}
,
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, and, more generally,

(1.3)

\Psi(GR,G^{\prime}R^{\prime},\nu)=\Psi(R,R^{\prime},\nu)\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ for all
$R$, $R^{\prime}\in\mathrm{O}(d)$,
$G$, $G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}$, $\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}$. }

It is important to note that, even in simple examples, this invariance cannot be removed by prescribing a consistent choice of the representative for each orientation class. See
Figure
1
for an illustration.

The goal of this paper is to construct variational phase-field models which converge to sharp-interface energies of the form (
1.1
). Our approximation respects the invariance
under the lattice point-group
\mathcal{G}
, and reproduces the Read-Shockley superlinear scaling
\theta|\log\theta|
for small misorientation angles. The approach is motivated and inspired by the algorithms used in
[
8
,
26
]
for the segmentation of polycrystalline images.

Phase-field methods are widely employed in numerical simulations of
sharp-interface energies, and, more in general, of
free-discontinuity problems. By replacing discontinuity sets with narrow diffuse layers, they avoid the need to track interfaces explicitly and permit to use standard finite-element discretizations designed for Sobolev functions. Classical applications include image reconstruction
[
17
,
9
,
10
]
and fracture mechanics
[
29
,
25
,
16
,
30
,
38
,
32
]
.

This strategy originated with the Ambrosio-Tortorelli approximation of the Mumford-Shah functional
[
4
]
, in which the surface energy density depends solely on the measure of the jump set, and not on the jump amplitude.
More recently, a number of phase-field approximations for cohesive fracture and segmentation energies – where the surface energy may depend on the opening – have been rigorously developed, see for instance

[
22
,
19
,
18
,
20
,
21
]
.

We approximate sharp-interface energies with superlinear
density
\Psi
following the approach introduced in
[
19
,
21
]
, with the key additional challenge of incorporating the group invariance, which we describe in more detail below.
Two further technical distinctions are worth noting: first, to recover the Read-Shockley scaling law, we adapt the construction from
[
19
, Section 7]
,
[
21
]
, originally designed for general superlinear power-law growth at small openings. Second, we introduce a penalization term – similar to that in

[
18
]
for
the approximation of piecewise rigid motions with brittle interface energy – to ensure that the diffuse gradient vanishes in the limit.

1.1.
Formulation of the main results

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 1
, be a bounded connected set with Lipschitz boundary, and let
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathrm{O}(d)
, for
d\geq 2
, be a finite group, possibly with
d\neq N
.
We define the space of admissible order parameters that encode the lattice symmetry as

(1.4)

\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}):=\{\beta\in\mathrm{SBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})\colon\beta^{+}(x)\in\mathcal{G}\,\beta^{-}(x)\,\text{ for }\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\text{-a.e. }x\in J_{\beta}\},

i.e., the set of all
\mathrm{SBV}

functions such that
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(J_{\beta})<\infty
,
\nabla\beta\in L^{2}
, and the
traces on the jump set differ only by multiplication by an element of
\mathcal{G}
(we will sometimes say simply “jump in
\mathcal{G}
” for brevity).

For
\varepsilon>0
and an open set
A\subset\Omega
we consider the energy

(1.5)

\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{red}}_{\varepsilon}(\beta,v;A):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{{A}}f_{\varepsilon}^{2}(v)\,|\nabla\beta|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}x}+\text{PF}_{\varepsilon}(v;A)&\text{in}\ \mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})\times H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1]),\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise in}\ L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}})\times L^{1}(\Omega),\end{cases}

where
\text{PF}_{\varepsilon}
is a
Modica-Mortola type energy and is given by

(1.6)

\text{PF}_{\varepsilon}(v;A):=\int_{A}\left(\frac{(1-v)^{2}}{4\varepsilon}+\varepsilon|\nabla v|^{2}\right){\,\mathrm{d}x},\qquad v\in H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1]).

We notice that
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{red}}_{\varepsilon}
is finite on the space
\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}
, and jumps in
\mathcal{G}
of the order parameter
\beta
do not contribute to the energy.
As in the models proposed in
[
19
,
21
]
, the choice of the prefactor
f_{\varepsilon}
in front of
|\nabla\beta|^{2}
, the so-called damage coefficient, is crucial to capture the desired behavior of the limiting surface energy density. At variance with
[
19
,
21
]
, and following
[
18
]
, we allow
f_{\varepsilon}
to take large values, enforcing that the limit fields have no diffuse gradient.
More precisely, we set

(1.7)

f_{\varepsilon}(s):=M_{\varepsilon}\wedge\sqrt{\varepsilon}f(s)\text{ for }s\in[0,1[,\quad f_{\varepsilon}(1)=M_{\varepsilon},\qquad\lim_{{\varepsilon}\to 0^{+}}M_{\varepsilon}=+\infty,

where
f\in C([0,1[;[0,+\infty[)
is a nondecreasing function satisfying
f^{-1}(0)=\{0\}
, and

(1.8)

\ell:=\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}\frac{1-s}{|\log(1-s)|}f(s)>0,

as for example

f(s)=\ell\frac{|\log(1-s)|}{1-s}.

The singularity of
f
at
1
induces the logarithmic superlinear behaviour of the surface tension
at small openings (cf. Proposition
4.4
), while the truncation by the diverging sequence
M_{\varepsilon}
ensures the condition
\nabla\beta=0
in the domain of the
\Gamma
-limit (see Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
3.3
).

Finally, we introduce a penalization term that asymptotically
forces the limit fields to take values in

\mathrm{O}(d)
. To this end, we consider an additional positive parameter
\delta_{\varepsilon}\to 0^{+}
and define
the modified energy

(1.9)

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\beta,v;A):=\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{red}}_{\varepsilon}(\beta,v;A)+\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{A}{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta,\mathrm{O}(d)){\,\mathrm{d}x}.

We shall show that
limiting behavior of the energies
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
depends only on the asymptotic ratio

(1.10)

\lambda:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\in[0,+\infty],

that we assume to exist.
In view of the invariance of the energy
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
under the action of the symmetry group
\mathcal{G}
, it is natural to adopt the following notion of convergence modulo
\mathcal{G}
.
A family
(\beta_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
is said to converge to
\beta
in the
{L}^{1}_{\mathcal{G}}
-sense if
there exists a sequence
G_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathcal{G})
such that

(1.11)

G_{\varepsilon}\beta_{\varepsilon}\to\beta\quad\text{in }L^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}).

With respect to this notion of convergence, we prove a compactness result for sequences with equi-bounded energy and characterize the
\Gamma
-limit of
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
. More precisely,
for any
\lambda\in[0,+\infty]
we define the sharp-interface functional

(1.12)

\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}(\beta,v):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{J_{\beta}}g_{\lambda}(\beta^{-},\beta^{+})\,{\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}}&\text{if }\beta\in\mathrm{SBV}_{0}(\Omega;\mathrm{O}(d)),\ v=1,\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise in}\ L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}})\times L^{1}(\Omega),\end{cases}

where the surface energy density
g_{\lambda}
is determined via an asymptotic cell problem that will be introduced and analyzed in detail in Section
4
.

Due to the invariance of the energy
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
under the action of
\mathcal{G}
, the limit density
g_{\lambda}
inherits the same invariance property. That is, for every
R^{+},R^{-}\in\mathrm{O}(d)
we have

(1.13)

g_{\lambda}(R^{+},R^{-})=g_{\lambda}(GR^{+},G^{\prime}R^{-})\qquad\forall\ G,G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G},

(see Proposition
4.1
).
In particular, the functional
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}
depends only on the equivalence classes of
\beta
in the quotient space
{\mathrm{O}(d)}/{\mathcal{G}}
, namely,

(1.14)

\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}(\beta,v)=\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}(G\beta,v)\quad\text{for every }(\beta,v,G)\in L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}})\times L^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{BV}(\Omega;\mathcal{G}).

The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

The following properties hold:

(C)

For any
(\beta_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}\times[0,1])
satisfying

(1.15)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\beta_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})<+\infty

there exists
\beta\in\mathrm{SBV}_{0}(\Omega;\mathrm{O}(d))
such that, up to a subsequence,

(1.16)

\beta_{\varepsilon}\to\beta\text{ in }{L}^{1}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}),\qquad v_{\varepsilon}\to 1\text{ in }L^{1}(\Omega);

(LB)

For any
(\beta_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}\times[0,1])
and
\beta\in\mathrm{SBV}_{0}(\Omega;\mathrm{O}(d))
satisfying (
1.16
), it holds

\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}(\beta,1)\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\beta_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon});

(UB)

For any
\beta\in\mathrm{SBV}_{0}(\Omega;\mathrm{O}(d))
there exists
(\beta_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}\times[0,1])
satisfying (
1.16
) and

(1.17)

\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}(\beta,1)\geq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\beta_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon}).

1.2.
Application to image segmentation

To apply Theorem
1.1
to the
segmentation of images of polycrystals,
we assume
N=d
and
introduce a fidelity term inspired by
[
8
,
26
]
.
Let
w\in C(\Omega;\mathbb{R})

represent the grayscale image of a polycrystal, and let
\sigma>0
be a small parameter of the order of the lattice spacing.
We fix a finite number of lattice vectors

v_{1},\,\dots,\,v_{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
|v_{k}|\leq\sigma
,
and positive weights
\alpha_{k}>0
,
k=1,\ldots,K
.

For a given domain
\Omega^{\prime}
such that
\{x\colon{\mathrm{dist}}(x,\Omega^{\prime})<2\sigma\}\subset\subset\Omega

we define the fidelity term by

(1.18)

\mathrm{FT}(\beta;\Omega^{\prime}):=\int_{E_{\beta}\cap\Omega^{\prime}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{k}\sum_{G\in\mathcal{G}}(w(x+\big(\beta(x)\big)^{-1}Gv_{k})-w(x))^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}x},

where

E_{\beta}:=\{x\in\Omega\colon\det(\beta(x))\neq 0,\,\|\big(\beta(x)\big)^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 2\},

and

\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}}
denotes the operator norm (i.e., the largest singular value). This definition ensures that the points
x
included in the integral satisfy
x+\big(\beta(x)\big)^{-1}Gv_{k}\in\Omega
, so that
\mathrm{FT}
is well-defined.
Since the vectors
v_{k}
belong to the lattice and
\mathcal{G}
is the associated point group, the vectors
Gv_{k}
also lie in the lattice. Thus, if
w
is an image of a lattice with orientation
R\in\mathrm{O}(d)
, then
w(x+RGv_{k})=w(x)
for all
G
and
k
, and the fidelity term
\mathrm{FT}
is minimized for
\beta=R^{T}
.

The definition of the fidelity term
\mathrm{FT}
ensures invariance under the action of the group
\mathcal{G}
. In particular,
for every
\beta\in\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
and every
G\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathcal{G})
it holds that

(1.19)

\mathrm{FT}(G\beta;\Omega^{\prime})=\mathrm{FT}(\beta;\Omega^{\prime}).

Moreover, by the continuity of
w

and the fact that any sequence with bounded energy converges to a limit valued in
\mathrm{O}(d)
, which implies
|\Omega\setminus E_{\beta_{\varepsilon}}|\to 0
,
the fidelity term
\mathrm{FT}

is continuous
with respect to the
{L}^{1}_{\mathcal{G}}
convergence of
\beta
along sequences with bounded energy.
Hence, by Theorem
1.1
,
for any fixed
\gamma>0
the sequence of functionals

(1.20)

\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(\beta,v):=\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\beta,v;\Omega)+\gamma\,\mathrm{FT}(\beta;\Omega^{\prime})

\Gamma
-converges
(with respect to the
{L}^{1}_{\mathcal{G}}\times L^{1}
convergence of
(\beta,v)
)
to

(1.21)

\mathcal{H}^{\lambda}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(\beta,v):=\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}(\beta,v\color[rgb]{0,0,0};\Omega)+\gamma\,\mathrm{FT}(\beta;\Omega^{\prime}).

In view of the equi-coerciveness established in Theorem
1.1
, this also implies convergence of minima and almost minimisers of
\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}
to those of
\mathcal{H}^{\lambda}
.
Clearly the same would also apply to other types of fidelity terms, as far as they have the appropriate invariance under the action of the group
\mathcal{G}
and they are continuous with respect to the
{L}^{1}_{\mathcal{G}}
convergence along sequences with bounded energy.

1.3.
Main difficulties and novelties

The key novelty of this paper lies in the presence of the point group
\mathcal{G}
, under whose action the sharp interface model remains invariant. Crucially, we are able to incorporate this invariance also at the level of the approximating functionals. This requires working with an
ad hoc

notion of convergence for the fields that respects the group action, in appropriate spaces.

A fundamental preliminary challenge is the choice of
a suitable functional space for the order parameters.
Two main approaches can be considered, both in the formulation of the statement and in the proofs. In the presentation above, we opted for the space
\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}
defined in (
1.4
),
which is a subset of the classical space
\mathrm{SBV}
.
A version of this space (under different notation)
appears naturally also in the modeling of fractional dislocations
in two dimensions
[
5
,
31
]
.

An alternative approach is to define the functionals directly on functions taking values in the quotient space
\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G}
. In this framework, one can use the metric structure of
\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G}
and work in the more abstract framework of Sobolev and
\mathrm{BV}
spaces of functions with values in metric spaces, as introduced by Ambrosio in
[
1
]
(see also
[
36
]
). Remarkably, in this metric space approach, the approximating functionals are defined on Sobolev functions as usual in phase field approximation, in particular the fields belong to the space
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})
. However,
this more abstract setting comes with significant difficulties.
The lack of linear structure in the quotient space restricts the applicability of many
standard analytical and numerical tools, such as the Finite Elements Method.
This poses substantial obstacles for both theoretical analysis and numerical simulations.

Our chosen approach thus balances between capturing the essential group invariance and maintaining a workable structure for analysis and approximation.

In order to compare the two approaches, it is important to clarify the relation between
\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
and
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})
.
It is plain to see that for any function in
\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
, by passing pointwise to equivalence classes, one obtains a function in
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})
(cf. Remark
2.4
). Conversely, it is not true in dimension
d\geq 2
that any function in
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})
has a (pointwise) representative in
\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
, since in general the reconstructed jump set may have infinite measure, as in the counterexample of Lemma
2.6
.
Therefore, in general, the approximating functionals defined in
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})
are smaller than the ones defined in

\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
, cf. (
2.31
).

When the reference domain
\Omega
has dimension 1, instead, its total ordering
allows to prove that the two spaces are equivalent, in the sense described just above, and suitably represented
by
H^{1}
functions with values in
\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}
, see Lemma
2.5
.
Further, a similar property holds if the target set of the functions is restricted, in particular for the domains of the limiting functionals, which are
the spaces
\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega;\mathrm{O}(d))
and
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{O}(d)/\mathcal{G})
: in fact, in this case one may work in the framework of Sobolev or
\mathrm{BV}
functions valued into manifolds, and
a lifting is possible, see Theorem
2.12
below.

Basing on the former relations,
we develop the two approaches in such a way that each one can be followed independently, in particular Theorem
1.1
can be proven without using the theory
of Sobolev or
\mathrm{BV}
functions valued in metric spaces, as we now describe.

As for Compactness (C), we employ the convergence of the fields
\beta_{n}
with equibounded energy to a function with values in
\mathrm{O}(d)
together with the fact that the functions
\mathrm{det}\,\beta_{n}
are equibounded
in
W^{1,1}
in
\{v_{n}\geq c\}
for
c>0
. The latter
follows from the
\mathcal{G}
-invariance of the determinant
and the uniform integral bound for
\nabla\beta_{n}
in
\{v_{n}\geq c\}
.
Then, by the lifting result in Theorem
2.12
, we obtain a uniform bound in

\mathrm{BV}
, up to modifications in
sets with bounded perimeter.

Both in the characterization of the limit domain
\mathrm{SBV}_{0}
and in the Lower Bound (LB) one may
reduce to one dimensional slices so, by Lemma
2.5
,
work with standard Sobolev function and adapt the analogous properties
from
[
19
]
. In the characterization of the limit domain we use here a strategy similar to the one in
[
21
]
.
The Upper Bound (UB) employs a density result in
\mathrm{SBV}_{0}
through functions with jump set given by a finite union of pieces of hyperplanes
[
7
]
, allowing to use, where the jump is flat, optimal profiles from the one dimensional cell formula for
g_{\lambda}
, while a careful construction applies to deal with
neighborhoods of the
N-2
dimensional intersections of different faces.

Taking advantage of the abstract theory in
[
1
]
, one may prove the more general Compactness (C)’ in Theorem
2.15
(recall (
2.31
)) arguing analogously to the “classical”
\mathrm{BV}
case in
[
19
]
, while the more general Lower Bound (LB)’ follows similarly to (LB), due to the 1d equivalence from Lemma
2.5
and some fine properties from
[
1
]
. Conversely, the strongest Upper Bound is (UB), so (UB)’ follows.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
we introduce some notation and recall the basic theory of Sobolev and
\mathrm{BV}
functions valued in metric spaces, discuss the relation with the counterparts which are subspaces of
\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}
, and introduce the formulation for functions valued in equivalence classes along with the main result Theorem
2.15
.
Then we prove in Section
3
compactness and the characterization of the domain for limiting fields. Section
4
is devoted to define, describe, and characterize the surface tension. Such analysis will be crucial
to prove the lower bound and the upper bound, in Section
5
.

2.
Formulation with Sobolev functions valued in metric spaces

This Section
addresses the formulation of the problem with Sobolev functions valued in metric spaces.
We start with some technical preliminaries and notation, which are relevant for the entire paper.

2.1.
Notation

In the entire paper,
\Omega
is a bounded connected open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 1
, with Lipschitz boundary, and
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{B}(\Omega)
denote the set of open and Borel
subsets of
\Omega
, respectively.
When
\Omega
is one dimensional we often denote it with
I
.
The Lebesgue measure and the
k
–dimensional Hausdorff measure in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
are denoted by
\mathcal{L}^{N}
and
\mathcal{H}^{k}
, respectively.
We denote by
\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}
the space of
d\times d
matrices, with
d\geq 2
.
For
\beta\in\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}
,

\beta^{T}
and
|\beta|
are respectively its transpose matrix and its Euclidean norm.

Given a finite subgroup
\mathcal{G}
of
\mathrm{O}(d)
, that will be fixed along the paper,
we denote the quotient space of
\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
by
\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G}
and its elements by

[\beta]=\mathcal{G}\beta:=\{G\beta\colon G\in\mathcal{G}\},\qquad\beta\in\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}.

The space
\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G}
is a separable complete metric space endowed with the metric

(2.1)

\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{G}}([\beta],[\widetilde{\beta}]):=\min_{G\in\mathcal{G}}|\beta-G\widetilde{\beta}|\qquad\text{for every }[\beta],[\widetilde{\beta}]\in\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G}.

2.2.
Functional setting I: Metric-space valued Sobolev functions

In what follows we always assume that

(X,{\,\mathrm{d}_{X}})\text{ is a separable locally compact metric space},

having in mind the application to
X=\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G}
or
X={\mathrm{O}(d)}/{\mathcal{G}}
.
The latter is here seen as a subset of the first, endowed with the same metric, as defined in (
2.1
). We observe that
{\mathrm{O}(d)}/{\mathcal{G}}
could also be seen as a manifold, then it is natural to adopt on each tangent space the metric
that
\mathrm{O}(d)
inherits from
\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}
, or
\mathrm{O}(d)/\mathcal{G}
from
\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G}
, and then extend it to each connected component using geodesics; the result is a metric different from, but equivalent to, the one in
(
2.1
).
The property of
X
being locally compact will be needed for the definition of the space
\mathrm{BV}(\Omega;X)
, and not for Sobolev spaces.

For every
p\in[1,+\infty]
, we define the space
L^{p}(\Omega;X)
as the space of all Borel functions
u:\Omega\to X
such that there exists a
z_{0}\in X
for which
{\,\mathrm{d}_{X}}(u(\cdot),z_{0}):\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is in
L^{p}(\Omega)
.

Remark 2.1
.

The
{L}^{1}_{\mathcal{G}}
–convergence corresponds to the convergence of the equivalence classes in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})
, namely

\beta_{\varepsilon}\to\beta\text{ in }{L}^{1}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})\quad\text{if and only if}\quad[\beta_{\varepsilon}]\to{[\beta]}\text{ in }L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}}/{\mathcal{G}}),

which is also equivalent to

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{G}}([\beta_{\varepsilon}(x)],[\beta(x)]){\,\mathrm{d}x}=0.

Let us consider a function
\beta\in\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}
, as defined in
(
1.4
). Then
\nabla\beta\in L^{2}
,
and
\beta^{+}\in\mathcal{G}\beta^{-}
at almost every jump point. Therefore if we consider the equivalence classes corresponding to
\beta
, defined by

[\beta]:\Omega\to\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G}
,
x\mapsto[\beta(x)]
, the jumps disappear. In particular, this function is expected to be in
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})
(which has not been defined yet).

To formalize this setting, we recall the notion of Sobolev functions with values in a metric space from
[
36
]
.
By
\mathrm{Lip}_{1}(X;\mathbb{R}^{m})
we denote the set of Lipschitz functions from
X
to
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with Lipschitz constant
\mathrm{Lip}(\varphi)
not larger than 1,
for
m=1
we write
\mathrm{Lip}_{1}(X)
.

Definition 2.2
.

The space
W^{1,p}(\Omega;X)
,
p\geq 1
, consists of those functions
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;X)
such that

\varphi\circ u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
for every
\varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}(X)
.
For
m\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, if we set

(2.2)

|\nabla u|_{(m)}:={\rm ess\,sup}\{|\nabla(\varphi\circ u)|:\ \varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{1}(X;\mathbb{R}^{m})\},

it holds
|\nabla u|_{(m)}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
.
We shall mostly take
m=d^{2}
, and when the meaning is clear from the context write briefly
|\nabla u|:=|\nabla u|_{(d^{2})}
.
In dimension
N=1
we use the notation
|u^{\prime}|
for
|\nabla u|_{(1)}
.

Remark 2.3
.

We observe that for any
m\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}

(2.3)

|\nabla u|_{(1)}\leq|\nabla u|_{(m)}\leq\sqrt{N\wedge m}\,|\nabla u|_{(1)}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ pointwise almost everywhere.}

To prove the first inequality we extend any function in
\mathrm{Lip}_{1}(X)
to a function in
\mathrm{Lip}_{1}(X;\mathbb{R}^{m})
adding
m-1
components which are identically zero. To prove the
\sqrt{m}
bound in the second one, we
observe that, if
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{m})\in\mathrm{Lip}_{1}(X;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, then for each
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
we have

\varphi_{i}\in\mathrm{Lip}_{1}(X)
,
and
|\nabla(\varphi\circ u)|=|(\nabla(\varphi_{1}\circ u),\dots,\nabla(\varphi_{m}\circ u))|\leq\sqrt{m}\max_{i}|\nabla(\varphi_{i}\circ u)|
.
To prove the final inequality,
let
\varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{1}(X;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, fix
v\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, and observe that
for almost every
x\in\Omega
and
t
small enough, writing
I_{t}
the segment with endpoints
0
and
t
, one has

(2.4)

\begin{split}|\varphi(u(x+tv))-\varphi(u(x))|\leq&|\mathrm{d}_{X}(u(x+tv),u(x))|\leq\int_{I_{t}}|\nabla(\mathrm{d}_{X}\circ u)|(x+sv){\,\mathrm{d}s}\\
\leq&\int_{I_{t}}|\nabla u|_{(1)}(x+sv){\,\mathrm{d}s}.\end{split}

Therefore if
x
is a Lebesgue point of the slice of
|\nabla u|_{(1)}
,
then

(2.5)

\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{|\varphi(u(x+tv))-\varphi(u(x))|}{|t|}\leq\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{|t|}\int_{I_{t}}|\nabla u|_{(1)}(x+sv){\,\mathrm{d}s}=|\nabla u|_{(1)}(x)

and we obtain
|\nabla_{v}(\varphi\circ u)|\leq|\nabla u|_{(1)}
almost everywhere.
Finally, for any
g\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
we have

(2.6)

|\nabla g|^{2}(x)=\sum_{i}|\partial_{i}g|^{2}(x)\leq N\max_{i}|\partial_{i}g|^{2}(x).

Using this with
g=\varphi\circ u
,
we obtain

|\nabla(\varphi\circ u)|_{(m)}\leq\sqrt{N}|\nabla u|_{(1)}
almost everywhere which concludes the proof of (
2.3
).

We next show that

(2.7)

if
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, then
|\nabla u|_{(m)}=|\nabla u|_{\mathbb{R}^{m\times N}}
,

where the last norm is the usual Euclidean norm of the weak gradient.
To see this, observe that any
\varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{1}(X;\mathbb{R}^{m})
has an extension
\widehat{\varphi}\in\mathrm{Lip}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m};\mathbb{R}^{m})
, and by the chain rule

|\nabla(\widehat{\varphi}\circ u)|=|(\nabla\widehat{\varphi})\circ u\,\nabla u|\leq|\nabla u|
.
At the same time, for
\varphi(x)=x
the inequality is an equality, which proves (
2.7
).
A similar argument shows that the bounds in
(
2.3
) are optimal.

Remark 2.4
.

The functions in
\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
are stricty related to the functions in the Sobolev space

H^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G}):=W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})
.
To see this,
for a Borel function
\beta\colon\Omega\to\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}
we define

[\beta]\colon\Omega\to\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G},\qquad[\beta](x):=[\beta(x)],\ x\in\Omega.

By the chain rule in
\mathrm{BV}
, if
\beta\in\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
and
\varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}({\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}}/{\mathcal{G}};\mathbb{R}^{m})
, then

\varphi\circ{[\beta]}
belongs to
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, and

(2.8)

|\nabla(\varphi\circ{[\beta]})|\leq\mathrm{Lip}(\varphi)|\nabla\beta|,\qquad\hbox{a.e.\ in }\ \Omega,

so that
{[\beta]}\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})
, and from (
2.8
)

(2.9)

|\nabla{[\beta]}|_{(m)}\leq|\nabla\beta|,\quad m\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}.

Hence the functions in
\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}
can be identified with Sobolev functions with values in the quotient space
\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G}
.

If the domain is one dimensional, the two spaces
\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(I;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
and
H^{1}(I;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})
are actually equivalent. We can moreover “clean the jumps” of a
\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(I;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
function, obtaining a representative in
H^{1}(I;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
, as shown in Lemma
2.5
below. This result will be crucial for our proof.

If the domain is at least two dimensional, the two spaces are not equivalent, see Lemma
2.6
below. However, the counterexample is based on the fact that functions in
\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(I;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
are required to have jump set of finite measure. If this property is eliminated from the definition, then we expect that it is possible to identify the two spaces
(see Remark
2.14
below).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
I=(a,b)\subset\mathbb{R}
be a bounded interval, and let
\beta:I\to\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}
be a Borel function. Then the following hold:

i)

if
[\beta]\in H^{1}(I;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})
, then there exists
G_{\beta}\in L^{\infty}(I;\mathcal{G})
such that
G_{\beta}\beta\in H^{1}(I;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
,

G_{\beta}\beta(a)=\beta(a)
, and

|[\beta]^{\prime}|_{(m)}=|(G_{\beta}\beta)^{\prime}|
almost everywhere in
I
for any
m\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
;

ii)

if, in addition,
\beta\in\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(I;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
, then
(G_{\beta}\beta)^{\prime}=G_{\beta}\beta^{\prime}
almost everywhere in
I
.

Proof.

Let
\beta:I\to\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}
be such that
[\beta](x):=[\beta(x)]
belongs to
H^{1}(I;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})
, and therefore it admits a continuous representative (see, e.g.
[
1
, Remark 2.2]
).
First of all, we prove that
[\beta]\in C^{1/2}(I;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})
. To see this, fix
x\in I
, and consider

\varphi_{x}(U):=\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{G}}(U,[\beta](x))
,
U\in\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G}
. Since
\varphi_{x}
is 1-Lipschitz, and
[\beta]\in H^{1}(I;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})
,
we have

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{G}}([\beta](x),[\beta](y)])=&\varphi_{x}([\beta](y))-\varphi_{x}([\beta](x))\leq\int_{[x,y]}|(\varphi_{x}\circ[\beta])^{\prime}|{\,\mathrm{d}t}\\
\leq&|x-y|^{1/2}\|(\varphi_{x}\circ[\beta])^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(I)}\leq|x-y|^{1/2}\|\left|[\beta]^{\prime}\right|_{(m)}\|_{L^{2}(I)}.\end{split}

In order to prove i), we first construct a sequence
(\beta_{k})_{k}
of functions in
H^{1}(I;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
such that

(2.10)

\|\beta^{\prime}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(I)}^{2}\leq\|\left|[\beta]^{\prime}\right|_{(m)}\|_{L^{2}(I)}^{2},\hskip 28.45274pt\beta_{k}(a)=\beta(a),\hskip 28.45274pt\|\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{G}}([\beta_{k}],[\beta])\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\to 0.

To do this,
we subdivide the interval
I
in
k
subintervals, choosing
a_{0}:=a<a_{1}<\dots<a_{k}:=b

such that
|a_{i+1}-a_{i}|\leq 2(b-a)/k
for all
i
.
Further, for
i\geq 1

the function

\phi_{i}:\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\phi_{i}(U):=\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{G}}([\beta(a_{i-1})],U)

is 1-Lipschitz, hence
\phi_{i}\circ[\beta]\in H^{1}(I)
and,
by (
2.2
),

(2.11)

\phi_{i}^{2}([\beta(a_{i})])\leq(a_{i}-a_{i-1})\int_{a_{i-1}}^{a_{i}}\Big|\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}}{{\,\mathrm{d}t}}(\phi_{i}\circ[\beta])\Big|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\leq(a_{i}-a_{i-1})\int_{a_{i-1}}^{a_{i}}|[\beta]^{\prime}|_{(m)}^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}t}.

We set
\beta_{k}(a_{0})=\beta(a_{0})
, then inductively select
\beta_{k}(a_{i})\in[\beta](a_{i})

such that

(2.12)

|\beta_{k}(a_{i})-\beta_{k}(a_{i-1})|=\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{G}}([\beta(a_{i})],[\beta(a_{i-1})])=\phi_{i}([\beta(a_{i})]),

and define the function
\beta_{k}
as the affine interpolation in each of the intervals.
By (
2.12
) and (
2.11
) we get

(2.13)

\int_{a_{i-1}}^{a_{i}}|\beta_{k}^{\prime}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{1}{a_{i}-a_{i-1}}|\beta_{k}(a_{i})-\beta_{k}(a_{i-1})|^{2}\leq\int_{a_{i-1}}^{a_{i}}|[\beta]^{\prime}|_{(m)}^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}t},

and the first condition in (
2.10
) follows. The second one holds by the choice of
\beta_{k}(a_{0})
. The third one follows by the embedding of
H^{1}
in
C^{1/2}
, with a computation similar to (
2.11
). More explicitly, we obtain for any
t\in(a_{i-1},a_{i})
,

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{G}}([\beta_{k}](t),[\beta](t))&\leq|\beta_{k}(t)-\beta_{k}(a_{i-1})|+\phi_{i}([\beta(t)])\leq\phi_{i}([\beta(a_{i})])+\phi_{i}([\beta(t)])\\
&\leq 2\sqrt{a_{i}-a_{i-1}}\left(\int_{a_{i-1}}^{a_{i}}|[\beta]^{\prime}|_{(m)}^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{k}}\end{split}

and therefore the uniform convergence to zero in (
2.10
) is proved.

Now
by compactness, one obtains that
(\beta_{k})_{k}
has a subsequence converging weakly in
H^{1}(I;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
(and almost everywhere in
I
) to some

\beta_{*}\in H^{1}(I;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
, with
\beta_{*}(a)=\beta(a)
. By the continuity of the distance
\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{G}}
we obtain
[\beta_{*}]=[\beta]
almost everywhere, and hence there exists
G_{\beta}:I\to\mathcal{G}
such that
\beta_{*}=G_{\beta}\beta
almost everywhere. Since
\mathcal{G}
is finite one immediately obtains that
G_{\beta}
can be chosen measurable. By the lower semicontinuity of the norm, and (
2.13
) we obtain

\int_{I}|\beta_{*}^{\prime}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\leq\int_{I}|[\beta]^{\prime}|_{(m)}^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}t}=\int_{I}|[\beta_{*}]^{\prime}|_{(m)}^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}t}

that, together with (
2.9
) (applied to
\beta_{*}
), implies that
|\beta_{*}^{\prime}|=|[\beta]^{\prime}|_{(m)}
a.e. in
I
, concluding the proof of the first part of the Lemma.

Now we assume, in addition, that
\beta
belongs to
\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(I;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
, and we show that
(G_{\beta}\beta)^{\prime}=G_{\beta}\beta^{\prime}
almost everywhere in
I
.
To do this, fix any
t\in I
which is a point of approximate differentiability of
\beta
and
\beta_{*}
,
so that

(2.14)

\lim_{r\to 0}\int_{t-r}^{t+r}\frac{|\beta(s)-\beta(t)-\beta^{\prime}(t)(s-t)|}{r^{2}}+\frac{|\beta_{*}(s)-\beta_{*}(t)-\beta_{*}^{\prime}(t)(s-t)|}{r^{2}}\,{\,\mathrm{d}s}=0.

If
t
is also a Lebesgue point of
G_{\beta}
, as
\mathcal{G}
is discrete, we obtain that the set

E_{r}:=\{s\in(t-r,t+r):G_{\beta}(s)=G_{\beta}(t)\}

obeys
|E_{r}|/r\to 2
as
r\to 0
.
Using
\beta_{*}(s)=G_{\beta}(t)\beta(s)
in
E_{r}
and (
2.14
) we obtain

\lim_{r\to 0}\int_{E_{r}}\frac{|(G_{\beta}(t)\beta^{\prime}(t)-\beta_{*}^{\prime}(t))(s-t)|}{r^{2}}\,{\,\mathrm{d}s}=0

which implies the desired identity

G_{\beta}(t)\beta^{\prime}(t)=\beta_{*}^{\prime}(t)
.
∎

The next Lemma shows with an example that
there are functions in

\mathrm{SBV}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{2\times 2}/\mathcal{G})
, with
N
,
d\geq 2
,
for which we cannot find
a representative with jump set of finite measure. Therefore in dimension at least two the result proved in Lemma
2.5
does not hold.

Lemma 2.6
.

If
N\geq 2
,
d\geq 2
, and
\mathcal{G}\cap\mathrm{SO}(d)\neq\{{\mathrm{Id}}\}
, then there is
u\in H^{1}((0,1)^{N};\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})
such that
there is no
\beta_{0}\in\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}((0,1)^{N};\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
with
u=[\beta_{0}]
.

Proof.

We provide an explicit construction in the case
N=2
, the case
N>2
follows by extending the construction so that it does not depend on the variables
x_{3},\dots,x_{N}
, in the sense that
u^{(N)}(x)=u^{(2)}(x_{1},x_{2})
.

Select
G\in\mathcal{G}\cap\mathrm{SO}(d)
,
G\neq{\mathrm{Id}}
, and then
pick a curve
\gamma\in C^{1}([0,2\pi];{\mathrm{SO}}(d))
such that

\gamma(0)={\mathrm{Id}}
,
\gamma(2\pi)=G
. This exists since
{\mathrm{SO}}(d)
is connected.
Fix
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}((0,1);[0,1])
such that
\{\varphi>0\}=(1/4,3/4)
.
We start working in the unit ball
B_{1}
and use polar coordinates. We define,
for
t\in(0,1)
and
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
,

\beta_{1}(te^{i\theta}):=\varphi(t)\gamma(\theta).

We observe that
\beta_{1}\in\mathrm{SBV}(B_{1};\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
,
with
\beta_{1}=0
around
\partial B_{1}
,
\nabla\beta_{1}\in L^{\infty}(B_{1})
,

J_{\beta_{1}}=(1/4,3/4)e_{1}
,

\beta_{1}^{-}(te_{1})=\varphi(t)G
;
\beta_{1}^{+}(te_{1})=\varphi(t){\mathrm{Id}}
, so that

\beta_{1}\in\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(B_{1};\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
.
We define

c_{2}:=\|\nabla\beta_{1}\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2}\in(0,\infty),\hskip 14.22636ptc_{J}:=\mathcal{H}^{1}(J_{\beta_{1}}\cap B_{1})=\frac{1}{2},\hskip 14.22636ptc_{D}:=\int_{0}^{1}|\beta_{1}^{+}-\beta_{1}^{-}|(te_{1}){\,\mathrm{d}t}\in(0,\infty).

Consider countably many pairwise disjoint balls

B_{k}:=B_{r_{k}}(x_{k})
contained in the unit square
Q:=(0,1)^{2}
such that

\sum_{k}r_{k}=\infty.

To choose them one can, for example, take any dyadic decomposition of the unit square into countably many subsquares and consider the circle inscribed in each subsquare.
We set, for
a:\mathbb{N}\to(0,\infty)
chosen below and
K\in\mathbb{N}
,

\beta_{K}(x):=\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}\chi_{B_{k}}(x)\beta_{1}\left(\frac{x-x_{k}}{r_{k}}\right)

and verify that
\beta_{K}\in\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
,
with
[\beta_{K}]\in H^{1}(Q;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})
.
We compute

\|\nabla\beta_{K}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}=c_{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}^{2},\hskip 14.22636pt\mathcal{H}^{1}(J_{\beta_{K}}\cap Q)=c_{J}\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k},\hskip 14.22636pt\int_{J_{\beta_{K}}}|\beta_{K}^{+}-\beta_{K}^{-}|\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}=c_{D}\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}r_{k}.

Obviously
r_{k}\to 0
; we select
a_{k}=2^{-k}
. Then
the sequence
(\beta_{K})_{K}
converges uniformly and strongly in
\mathrm{BV}
to some
\beta_{\infty}
.
In each ball
B_{k}
, we have
\beta_{\infty}=\beta_{h}
for any
h\geq k
; outside the union of the balls
\beta_{\infty}=0
.
One checks that
u:=[\beta_{\infty}]\in H^{1}(Q;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})
.

It remains to show that there is no
\beta\in\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
with
[\beta]=u
.

Assume there was one, pick any
h\in\mathbb{N}
, and consider the ball
B_{h}
.
We claim that necessarily

(2.15)

\mathcal{H}^{1}(J_{\beta}\cap B_{h})\geq\frac{1}{2}r_{h}.

If this holds, then
\mathcal{H}^{1}(J_{\beta}\cap Q)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{h}r_{h}=\infty
, so that
\beta\not\in\mathrm{SBV}^{2}(Q;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
and the proof is concluded.

It remains to prove (
2.15
).
Assume this was not the case.
Then by slicing there would be a set
E\subset(0,1)

with
\mathcal{L}^{1}(E)>1/2
such that for any
t\in E
the

2\pi
-periodic
function

v(\theta):=\beta(x_{h}+r_{h}te^{i\theta})

obeys
v\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{{\mathrm{loc}}}}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
, we identify it with its continuous representative.
By our definitions,
[v(\theta)]=a_{h}\varphi(t)[\gamma(\theta)]
.
We pick
t\in E
with
\varphi(t)>0
and seek a contradiction.
We define
w:[0,2\pi]\to\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}
by

w(\theta):=v(\theta)(\gamma(\theta))^{-1}.

This function takes values in
a_{h}\varphi(t)\mathcal{G}
. We recall that
a_{h}\varphi(t)\neq 0
, so that this set consists of invertible matrices.
Using periodicity of
v
and the boundary conditions on
\gamma
we obtain

w(0)=v(0)(\gamma(0))^{-1}=v(0),\hskip 28.45274ptw(2\pi)=v(2\pi)(\gamma(2\pi))^{-1}=v(0)G^{-1}.

However, as
w
is continuous on
[0,2\pi]
, it is constant.
Since
v(0)
is an invertible matrix, this gives
G={\mathrm{Id}}
, a contradiction.
∎

2.3.
Functional setting II: Metric-space valued
\mathrm{BV}
functions

We recall from
[
1
]
the definition of functions of Bounded Variation into a general separable locally compact metric space
(X,\mathrm{d}_{X})
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let

u\in L^{1}(\Omega;X)
. We say that
u
belongs to
\mathrm{BV}(\Omega;X)
if for every
\varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}(X;\mathbb{R}^{m})
one has
\varphi\circ u\in\mathrm{BV}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and there exists a finite Borel measure
\sigma

(which may depend on
m
, but not on
\varphi
)
such that

(2.16)

|D(\varphi\circ u)|\leq\mathrm{Lip}(\varphi)\,\sigma.

In this case,
for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}

the total variation measure of
u
is defined as

(2.17)

|Du|_{(m)}:=\sup\{|D(\varphi\circ u)|\,:\ \varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{1}(X;\mathbb{R}^{m})\},

where the supremum of measures is taken as in Definition 1.68 in
[
3
]
.

As for the case of Sobolev functions, the value of
|Du|_{(m)}
depends on
m
(see Remark
2.3
).

The usual decomposition of
|Du|
holds true in
\mathrm{BV}(\Omega;X)
(see Theorems 2.2 and 2.3, and Remark 2.3 in
[
1
]
):

(2.18)

|Du|_{(m)}=|\nabla u|_{(m)}\,\mathcal{L}^{N}+{\,\mathrm{d}_{X}}(u^{+},u^{-})\,\mathcal{H}^{N-1}{\mathchoice{\,\hbox{\vrule width=0.2pt\vbox to7.0pt{\hrule width=7.0pt,height=0.2pt}}\,}{\,\hbox{\vrule width=0.2pt\vbox to7.0pt{\hrule width=7.0pt,height=0.2pt}}\,}{\,\hbox{\vrule width=0.2pt\vbox to5.0pt{\hrule width=5.0pt,height=0.2pt}}\,}{\,\hbox{\vrule width=0.2pt\vbox to3.35pt{\hrule width=3.35pt,height=0.2pt}}\,}}J_{u}+|Du|_{(m)}^{c},

where
|\nabla u|_{(m)}(x)=\sup\{|\nabla(\varphi\circ u)|(x)\colon\varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{1}(X;\mathbb{R}^{m})\}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
,

J_{u}
is a countably
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
–rectifiable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with unit normal
\nu(x)
defined for
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
-a.e.
x\in J_{u}
, and the traces
u^{\pm}(x)=u^{\pm}(x,\nu(x))
are characterized by

(2.19)

\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{B_{\rho}(x)\cap\{y\in\Omega\colon\ \pm(y-x)\cdot\nu(x)>0,\ {\,\mathrm{d}_{X}}(u(y),u^{\pm}(x))>{\varepsilon}\}}{\rho^{d}}=0\qquad\forall{\varepsilon}>0.

Remark 2.8
.

Following
[
1
]
,
u\in\mathrm{SBV}(\Omega;X)
if
u\in\mathrm{BV}(\Omega;X)
and
|Du|^{c}_{(m)}=0
. This is equivalent to require that
u\in\mathrm{BV}(\Omega;X)
and
\varphi\circ u\in\mathrm{SBV}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
for every
\varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}(X;\mathbb{R}^{m})
(see

[
1
, Remark 2.4]
).

As in the case of
X
-valued Sobolev functions, the norm depends on
m
, but the space does not. In working with
\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G}
we shall mostly take
m=d^{2}
and, when there is no risk of confusion, simply write
|Du|
for
|Du|_{(d^{2})}
.

Remark 2.9
.

The decomposition (
2.18
) and definition (
2.17
) also allow to characterize subspaces of
\mathrm{BV}(\Omega;X)
.

To this aim we recall the following basic property of the derivation of measures, also used in
[
1
]
: if
\sigma
is a positive measure, and
\mu
, with
\mu(\Omega)<+\infty
, is the supremum of a countable family of positive measures
\mu_{h}
, and
\mu_{h}=f_{h}\sigma+\mu_{h}^{s}
, where
\mu_{h}^{s}
denotes the singular part of
\mu_{h}
with respect to
\sigma
and
f_{h}\in L^{1}(\Omega;\sigma)
(i.e.
f_{h}
is in
L^{1}
with respect to the measure
\sigma
), then

(2.20)

\mu=f\sigma+\mu^{s}\,,\qquad\hbox{with }f=\sup f_{h}\qquad\sigma\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\hbox{-a.e.\ in }\Omega.

Therefore from Remark
2.8
we have

(2.21)

u\in\mathrm{SBV}(\Omega;X)\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\varphi\circ u\in\mathrm{SBV}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\ \hbox{for all }\varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}(X;\mathbb{R}^{m}).

Moreover we also define the space
\mathrm{SBV}_{0}(\Omega;X)
as the set of functions
u\in\mathrm{SBV}(\Omega;X)
such that
|\nabla u|=0
, which is also equivalent to

(2.22)

\varphi\circ u\in\mathrm{SBV}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\ \hbox{for all }\varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}(X;\mathbb{R}^{m}).

Finally, when
u\in\mathrm{SBV}(\Omega;X)
has no jump, and
|\nabla u|_{(m)}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
, the notion above complies with the definition of
W^{1,p}(\Omega;X)
in
[
36
]
recalled in Definition
2.2
.

We recall here the compactness results in
\mathrm{BV}
proven in
[
1
, Theorem 2.4]
.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of functions in
\mathrm{BV}(\Omega;X)
, and
m\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
.
If there exists

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left(|Du_{n}|_{(m)}(\Omega)+\int_{\Omega}{\,\mathrm{d}_{X}}(u_{n}(x),z_{0})\,{\,\mathrm{d}x}\right)<+\infty,

then there exist a subsequence
(u_{n_{k}})_{k}
and a function

such that

u_{n_{k}}
converges to

a.e. in
\Omega
, and

|Du|_{(m)}(\Omega)\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}|Du_{n_{k}}|_{(m)}(\Omega).

We also recall that one can characterize functions in
\mathrm{BV}(\Omega;X)
via slicing as follows.
We fix
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\colon\ |\xi|=1\}
and we set

\Pi^{\xi}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\colon\ y\cdot\xi=0\},\quad\Omega_{y}^{\xi}:=\{t\in\mathbb{R}\colon\ y+t\xi\in\Omega\},\quad\Omega^{\xi}:=\{y\in\Pi^{\xi}\colon\Omega_{y}^{\xi}\neq\emptyset\}.

For any function

we define the slices
u_{y}^{\xi}\colon\Omega_{y}^{\xi}\to X
by

u_{y}^{\xi}(t):=u(y+t\xi).

The following result is an immediate consequence of
[
1
, Proposition 2.1]
, stated with our notation and also formulated for Sobolev functions.

Proposition 2.11
.

The following hold:

i)

if
u\in\mathrm{BV}(\Omega;X)
, then for every
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
we have

(2.23)

u_{y}^{\xi}\in\mathrm{BV}(\Omega_{y}^{\xi};X)\quad\hbox{and}\quad|Du|_{(1)}\geq|D(u^{\xi}_{y})|\quad\text{as measures in }\Omega^{\xi}_{y},\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\text{ for }\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\text{-a.e.\ }y\in\Omega^{\xi};

ii)

if

, then for every
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
we have
u_{y}^{\xi}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega_{y}^{\xi};X)
and

|\nabla u(x)|\geq|(u^{\xi}_{y})^{\prime}(t_{x})|\quad\text{for }\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\text{-a.e.\ }y\in\Omega^{\xi}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\text{a.e.\ }x\in\Omega,

where
t_{x}\in\Omega^{\xi}_{y}
is such that
x=y+t_{x}\xi
.

Proof.

The proof of i) follows from
[
1
, Proposition 2.1]
and a localization argument.
Property ii) follows from i) and the characterization of
|Du|
given in (

).
∎

We prove
that maps in
\mathrm{SBV}(\Omega;{\mathrm{O}(d)}/{\mathcal{G}})
have a lifting in
\mathrm{SBV}(\Omega;\mathrm{O}(d))
, with a bound on the norm. This
could be obtained easily as a consequence of
a general result by
Canevari and Orlandi
[
14
]

on maps valued in compact manifolds with a universal covering.
In our case, the manifold is
{\mathrm{O}(d)}/{\mathcal{G}}
, and permits a simpler argument.
We remark that in the case
d=2
, this also follows from the simple proof in
[
23
]
for maps valued in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. In higher dimension, however, a localization is needed, as in
[
14
]
.
Our proof applies without significant changes both for
\mathrm{SO}(d)
and for
\mathrm{O}(d)
;
we present it only in the case of
\mathrm{O}(d)
, which is the one used below.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
u\in\mathrm{SBV}(\Omega;{\mathrm{O}(d)}/{\mathcal{G}})
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
open and bounded. Then there is
\beta\in\mathrm{SBV}(\Omega;\mathrm{O}(d))
such that

(2.24)

u=[\beta]\text{ pointwise a.e. in $\Omega$, and }|D\beta|(\Omega)\leq C|Du|_{(d^{2})}(\Omega).

The constant depends only on
d
and
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

Let
r_{0}:=\frac{1}{4}\min\{|G-G^{\prime}|:G,G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G},G\neq G^{\prime}\}
.

Step 1. We can identify
B_{r_{0}}([Z])
with

B_{r_{0}}(Z)
.

Fix
Z\in{\mathrm{O}(d)}/{\mathcal{G}}
. We check that
Y\mapsto[Y]
is an isometric bijection of

B_{r_{0}}(Z)\subset\mathrm{O}(d)
onto
B_{r_{0}}([Z])\subset{\mathrm{O}(d)}/{\mathcal{G}}
.
To see this, the key fact is that if
Y
,
Y^{\prime}\in B_{r_{0}}(Z)
and

G\in\mathcal{G}\setminus\{{\mathrm{Id}}\}
then

(2.25)

|GY-Y^{\prime}|>|Y-Y^{\prime}|,

which implies that
\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{G}}([Y],[Y^{\prime}])=|Y-Y^{\prime}|
.
In turn, to show (
2.25
) it suffices to observe that, since
Y\in\mathrm{O}(d)

and
|Y-Y^{\prime}|\leq|Y-Z|+|Y^{\prime}-Z|<2r_{0}
,

(2.26)

|GY-Y^{\prime}|=|(G-{\mathrm{Id}})Y+(Y-Y^{\prime})|\geq|G-{\mathrm{Id}}|-|Y-Y^{\prime}|\geq 4r_{0}-2r_{0}>|Y-Y^{\prime}|.

We denote by
\psi_{Z}:B_{r_{0}}([Z])\to B_{r_{0}}(Z)\subset\mathrm{O}(d)
the inverse of
Y\mapsto[Y]
.

Step 2. Localization via the coarea formula.

We claim that for any
Z\in\mathrm{O}(d)
there is
r\in(r_{0}/2,r_{0})
such that

(2.27)

\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(\partial^{*}(u^{-1}(B_{r}([Z]))))<c|Du|(\Omega).

To see this, consider
\varphi:{\mathrm{O}(d)}/{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\varphi(Y):=\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{G}}(Y,[Z])
, and
notice that
u^{-1}(B_{t}([Z]))=\{x:\varphi(u(x))<t\}
.
The function
\varphi
is obviously
1
-Lipschitz, therefore
\varphi\circ u\in\mathrm{SBV}(\Omega)
, and

|D(\varphi\circ u)|(\Omega)\leq|Du|(\Omega)
. By the coarea formula applied to the
\mathrm{SBV}
function
\varphi\circ u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
we obtain

(2.28)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(\partial^{*}(\{\varphi\circ u<t\})){\,\mathrm{d}t}=|D(\varphi\circ u)|(\Omega),

therefore we can choose
r
as in (
2.27
), with
c=2/r_{0}
depending only on
\mathcal{G}
.

Step 3. Decomposition of the domain.

Let
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{M}\in\mathrm{O}(d)
be such that
{\mathrm{O}(d)}/{\mathcal{G}}\subset\bigcup_{i}B_{r_{0}/2}([Z_{i}])

(this choice depends only on
\mathcal{G}
), and let
r_{i}
be the corresponding values with the property (
2.27
) (which depend also on
u
).
We define iteratively

\omega_{1}:=u^{-1}(B_{r_{1}}([Z_{1}])),\ \text{and}\ \omega_{i}:=u^{-1}(B_{r_{i}}([Z_{i}]))\setminus\bigcup_{j<i}\omega_{j}.

The sets are a partition of
\Omega
,
and
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(\partial^{*}\omega_{i})\leq c|Du|(\Omega)
for each
i
, with
c
depending only on the group
\mathcal{G}
.

Step 4. Conclusion.

We set
\beta:=\psi_{Z_{i}}\circ u
in each
\omega_{i}
. Obviously
[\beta]=u
,
and inside each
\omega_{i}
we have
|D\beta|\leq C|Du|_{(d^{2})}
as measures.
We compute

(2.29)

|D\beta|(\Omega)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{M}|D\beta|(\omega_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\|\beta\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(\partial^{*}\omega_{i})\leq C|Du|_{(d^{2})}(\Omega),

again with
C
depending only on
\mathcal{G}
and
d
.
∎

Remark 2.13
.

By a localization argument, it is simple to see that if

whose existence is guaranteed by

satisfies

for some

.

An extension of the argument used in Theorem
2.12
to construct a lifting of a map in

to a map in

permits to show

The treatment of the higher-dimensional case is more complex and outside of the scope of this paper. Therefore we postpone a more detailed analysis of the relation between the spaces

\mathrm{SBV}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})

2.4.
Metric-space valued phase field models

With the above functional framework at hand, we can formulate the approximating phase field model in terms of order parameters valued into equivalence classes. The natural domain will be given by Sobolev functions with values in
\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G}
. We define

(2.30)

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(u,v;A):=\int_{{A}}f_{\varepsilon}^{2}(v)\,|\nabla u|_{(d^{2})}^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}x}+\text{PF}_{\varepsilon}(v;A)+\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{A}\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}(u,\mathrm{O}(d)/\mathcal{G}){\,\mathrm{d}x}

for
A\subset\Omega
open and

(u,v)\in\ H^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})\times H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1]),

and equal to
+\infty
otherwise in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})\times L^{1}(\Omega)
.

By Remark
2.4
, any
\beta\in\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
is such that
[\beta]\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})
with
|\nabla[\beta]|_{(d^{2})}\leq|\nabla\beta|
, and obviously
\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{G}}([\beta],\mathrm{O}(d)/\mathcal{G})={\mathrm{dist}}(\beta,\mathrm{O}(d))
. It follows that

(2.31)

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}([\beta],v;A)\leq\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\beta,v;A).

Finally in view of the invariance (
1.14
) and Remark
2.13
we can define

(2.32)

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\lambda}(u,1):=\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}(\beta,1)\quad\text{for every }u\in\mathrm{SBV}_{0}(\Omega;{\mathrm{O}(d)}/\mathcal{G})

and
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\lambda}(u,v):=+\infty
otherwise in
L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}}/\mathcal{G})\times L^{1}(\Omega)
where, for any
u\in\mathrm{SBV}_{0}(\Omega;{\mathrm{O}(d)}/{\mathcal{G}})
,
\beta\in\mathrm{SBV}_{0}(\Omega;\mathrm{O}(d))
is a lifting of
u
as in Theorem
2.12
. The invariance (
1.14
) guarantees that the result does not depend on the choice of the lifting.

1.1
.

Theorem 2.15

3.
Compactness

We start with a truncation argument, in order to restrict our attention to sequences equibounded in
L^{\infty}
. We denote by
\Phi(\xi)
the orthogonal projection of a matrix
\xi\in\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}
on the closed ball
\overline{B}_{\sqrt{d}}:=\{\eta\in\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}\colon|\eta|\leq\sqrt{d}\}
,

(3.1)

\Phi(\xi):=\begin{cases}\xi\quad&\hbox{if }|\xi|\leq\sqrt{d},\\
\displaystyle{\sqrt{d}\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}}\quad&\hbox{if }|\xi|>\sqrt{d}.\end{cases}

Since
\Phi(G\xi)=G\Phi(\xi)
for all
G\in\mathrm{O}(d)
,

\Phi
can be identified with a map from
\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G}
to itself.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset H^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})
be such that

(3.2)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{{\Omega}}\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}(u_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{O}(d)/\mathcal{G}){\,\mathrm{d}x}=0.

Then, for every

(v_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])
we have that

(3.3)

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}({\Phi}(u_{\varepsilon}),v_{\varepsilon})\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon}),\qquad\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|{\Phi}(u_{\varepsilon})-u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})}=0.

A corresponding assertion holds for

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
and
any
(\beta_{\varepsilon})\subset\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
.

Proof.

It follows immediately from the definition of
\Phi
, and the fact that

{\mathrm{dist}}(\Phi(\xi),\mathrm{O}(d))\leq{\mathrm{dist}}(\xi,\mathrm{O}(d))
for any matrix
\xi
.
Moreover,
\Phi
is 1–Lipschitz, and hence, by Definition
2.2
we deduce that

|\nabla{\Phi}(u_{\varepsilon})|\leq|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|
.
Finally,
since
\mathrm{O}(d)\subset\overline{B}_{\sqrt{d}}
and
\Phi
is a projection,

|{\Phi}(\xi)-\xi|\leq{\mathrm{dist}}(\xi,\overline{B}_{\sqrt{d}})\leq{\mathrm{dist}}(\xi,\mathrm{O}(d)).\qed

We first prove compactness in Theorem
2.15
by using the theory of
\mathrm{BV}
functions valued in metric spaces and the strategy from

]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

For any
(u_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G}\times[0,1])
satisfying (
2.33
)
there exists
u\in\mathrm{SBV}_{0}(\Omega;\mathrm{O}(d)/\mathcal{G})
such that, up to a subsequence,

u_{\varepsilon}\to u\text{ in }L^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G}),\qquad v_{\varepsilon}\to 1\text{ in }L^{1}(\Omega).

In particular, (C)’ in Theorem
2.15
is true.

Proof.

Let us fix a vanishing sequence
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n}
and denote
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\equiv\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon_{n}}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
(u_{n},v_{n})_{n}\subset L^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\times[0,1])
be such that

(3.4)

\sup_{n}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n}(u_{n},v_{n})\leq K.

The convergence of
v_{n}
to
1
directly follows from the estimate

(3.5)

\int_{\Omega}\frac{(1-v_{n})^{2}}{4{\varepsilon}_{n}}{\,\mathrm{d}x}\leq K.

Moreover,
u_{n}\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})
by (
3.4
), and,
by Lemma
3.1
, we may assume
that

(3.6)

\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sqrt{d}\qquad\text{for every }n\in\mathbb{N}.

We follow the strategy from
[
21
, Prop. 4.11]
.
As a first step, we need to identify small subsets of
\Omega
, with vanishing measure and bounded perimeter, where
the the energy bound cannot provide any uniform bound of
|\nabla u_{n}|
. More precisely, fixed
\rho\in(0,1/2)
, for every
n
we can choose
t_{n}^{\rho}\in(1-\rho,1-\rho/2)
such that

(3.7)

E_{n}^{\rho}:=\{v_{n}<\lambda_{n}^{\rho}\}\hskip 5.69054pt\text{ obeys }\hskip 5.69054pt\mathrm{Per}(E_{n}^{\rho},\Omega)\leq C_{\rho}K\hskip 5.69054pt\text{ and }\hskip 5.69054pt\mathcal{L}^{N}(E_{n}^{\rho})\leq C_{\rho}{\varepsilon}_{n}K.

Namely, for

\phi(t):=(1-t)^{2}/2

4.
The surface energy density

The limit energy is defined on functions with pure jump part, namely in
\mathrm{SBV}_{0}
. In this section we give the definition of the surface energy density and the proof of its main properties.

The surface energy density is expressed by a cell formula in one dimension. It depends on a parameter
\lambda\in[0,+\infty]
determined by the asymptotic ratio between
\varepsilon
and
\delta_{\varepsilon}
in the definition of the approximating functionals, see (
1.10
) in Section
1
.
In order to prove its main properties, such as the
s|\log s|
growth for small opening (see Proposition
4.4
), it is convenient to recast the energy density through a 1d cell formula which is invariant by time reparametrization (Proposition
4.2
).

We conclude the section with a technical result, Lemma
4.5
, used for the proof of the upper bound when
\lambda=+\infty
.

Let
I_{T}:=(-T,T)
for
T>0
,

F^{T}(\beta,v):=\int_{-T}^{T}\left(f^{2}(v)|\beta^{\prime}|^{2}+\frac{(1-v)^{2}}{4}+|v^{\prime}|^{2}\right){\,\mathrm{d}t},\qquad(\beta,v)\in H^{1}(I_{T};\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}\times[0,1]),

and

F^{T}_{\lambda}(\beta,v):=F^{T}(\beta,v)+\lambda\int_{-T}^{T}{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta,\mathrm{O}(d)){\,\mathrm{d}t}\quad\text{for }\lambda\in[0,+\infty).

For
R^{-}
,
R^{+}\in\mathrm{O}(d)
we let

(4.1)

\mathcal{U}^{T}[R^{-},R^{+}]:=\{(\beta,v)\in H^{1}(I_{T};\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}\times[0,1])\colon\beta(-T)=R^{-},\,\beta(T)=R^{+},\,v(\pm T)=1\}

be the set of admissible paths connecting
R^{-}
to
R^{+}
in
I_{T}
, and with fixed boundary conditions,
v=1
, for the damage coefficient.
For
\lambda\in[0,+\infty)
we set

(4.2)

g^{*}_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+}):=\lim_{T\uparrow+\infty}\inf\left\{F^{T}_{\lambda}(\beta,v)\colon(\beta,v)\in\mathcal{U}^{T}[R^{-},R^{+}]\right\},

and then

(4.3)

g^{*}_{\infty}(R^{-},R^{+}):=\sup_{\lambda>0}g^{*}_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+}).

One easily checks that the limit in (
4.2
) exists, and it equals the infimum over all
T>0
. In order to recover the invariance under the action of the group we then define, for
\lambda\in[0,\infty]
,

(4.4)

g_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+}):=\min_{G\in\mathcal{G}}g_{\lambda}^{*}(R^{-},GR^{+}).

Since the group is finite, in the definition of

the minimum over
\mathcal{G}
and the supremum over

(4.5)

g_{\infty}(R^{-},R^{+}):=\min_{G\in\mathcal{G}}g_{\infty}^{*}(R^{-},GR^{+})=\sup_{\lambda>0}g_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+}).

Proposition 4.1

,

g^{*}_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+})
as follows:

\widehat{\beta}_{\eta}(t):=\begin{cases}R^{-},&t\in[-1-\psi_{\eta}(1),0],\\
\beta(\psi_{\eta}^{-1}(t))&t\in[0,\psi_{\eta}(1)],\\
R^{+},&t\in[\psi_{\eta}(1),1+\psi_{\eta}(1)];\end{cases}\widehat{v}_{\eta}:=\begin{cases}1,&t\in[-1-\psi_{\eta}(1),-1],\\
(1-\eta)-t\eta,&t\in[-1,0],\\
v_{\eta}(\psi_{\eta}^{-1}(t)),&t\in[0,\psi_{\eta}(1)],\\
1+(t-\psi_{\eta}(1)-1)\eta,&t\in[\psi_{\eta}(1),1+\psi_{\eta}(1)].\end{cases}

Namely, setting
T_{\eta}:=\psi_{\eta}(1)+1
, we have that
(\widehat{\beta}_{\eta},\widehat{v}_{\eta})\in\mathcal{U}^{T_{\eta}}[R^{-},R^{+}]
, and hence

(4.11)

g^{*}_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+})\leq F^{T_{\eta}}_{\lambda}(\widehat{\beta}_{\eta},\widehat{v}_{\eta}),

due to the fact that the optimization problems defining
g^{*}_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+})
are decreasing in
T
.
We now prove that

\begin{split}F^{T_{\eta}}_{\lambda}(\widehat{\beta}_{\eta},\widehat{v}_{\eta})&<\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{(1-v)^{2}+4\lambda\,{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta,\mathrm{O}(d))}\sqrt{f^{2}(v)|\beta^{\prime}|^{2}+|v^{\prime}|^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}+o_{\eta\to 0}(1).\end{split}

Using the change of variable
t=\psi_{\eta}(s)
, it is straightforward that

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\psi_{\eta}(1)}&\left(f^{2}(\widehat{v}_{\eta})|\widehat{\beta}_{\eta}^{\prime}|^{2}+|\widehat{v}_{\eta}^{\prime}|^{2}\right){\,\mathrm{d}t}+\int_{0}^{\psi_{\eta}(1)}\left(\frac{(1-\widehat{v}_{\eta})^{2}}{4}+\lambda\,{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\widehat{\beta}_{\eta},\mathrm{O}(d))\right){\,\mathrm{d}t}\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\left(f^{2}({v}_{\eta})|\beta^{\prime}|^{2}+|{v}_{\eta}^{\prime}|^{2}\right)\frac{1}{\psi_{\eta}^{\prime}}{\,\mathrm{d}s}+\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{(1-{v}_{\eta})^{2}}{4}+\lambda\,{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta,\mathrm{O}(d))\right)\psi_{\eta}^{\prime}{\,\mathrm{d}s}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left(f^{2}({v}_{\eta})|\beta^{\prime}|^{2}+|{v}_{\eta}^{\prime}|^{2}\right)\sqrt{\frac{(1-v_{\eta})^{2}+4\lambda\,{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta,\mathrm{O}(d))}{\eta+f^{2}(v_{\eta})|\beta^{\prime}|^{2}+|v_{\eta}^{\prime}|^{2}}}{\,\mathrm{d}s}\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\eta+f^{2}(v_{\eta})|\beta^{\prime}|^{2}+|v_{\eta}^{\prime}|^{2}}\,\sqrt{(1-v_{\eta})^{2}+4\lambda\,{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta,\mathrm{O}(d))}{\,\mathrm{d}s}\\
&\leq c_{\lambda}\frac{\sqrt{\eta}}{2}+\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{f^{2}(v_{\eta})|\beta^{\prime}|^{2}+|v_{\eta}^{\prime}|^{2}}\,\sqrt{(1-v_{\eta})^{2}+4\lambda\,{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta,\mathrm{O}(d))}{\,\mathrm{d}s},\end{split}

where
c_{\lambda}=\sqrt{1+8\lambda d}

(we used here subadditivity of the square root and the bound
{\mathrm{dist}}(\beta,\mathrm{O}(d))\leq 2\sqrt{d}
).
Denoting
E_{\eta}:=I_{T_{\eta}}\setminus[0,\psi_{\eta}(1)]
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{E_{\eta}}&\left(f^{2}(\widehat{v}_{\eta})|\widehat{\beta}_{\eta}^{\prime}|^{2}+|\widehat{v}_{\eta}^{\prime}|^{2}+\frac{(1-\widehat{v}_{\eta})^{2}}{4}+\lambda\,{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\widehat{\beta}_{\eta},\mathrm{O}(d))\right){\,\mathrm{d}t}\\
&=\int_{E_{\eta}}\left(|\widehat{v}_{\eta}^{\prime}|^{2}+\frac{(1-\widehat{v}_{\eta})^{2}}{4}\right){\,\mathrm{d}t}=2\eta^{2}+\frac{\eta^{2}}{6},\end{split}

and hence

\begin{split}F^{T_{\eta}}_{\lambda}(\widehat{\beta}_{\eta},\widehat{v}_{\eta})&\leq c_{\lambda}\sqrt{\eta}+3\eta^{2}+\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{f^{2}(v_{\eta})|\beta^{\prime}|^{2}+|v_{\eta}^{\prime}|^{2}}\,\sqrt{(1-v_{\eta})^{2}+4\lambda\,{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta,\mathrm{O}(d))}{\,\mathrm{d}t}.\end{split}

Since
v_{\eta}^{\prime}=0
almost everywhere in the set
\{v_{\eta}\neq v\}
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}&\sqrt{f^{2}(v_{\eta})|\beta^{\prime}|^{2}+|v_{\eta}^{\prime}|^{2}}\,\sqrt{(1-v_{\eta})^{2}+4\lambda\,{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta,\mathrm{O}(d))}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\\
=&\int_{\{v_{\eta}=v\}}\sqrt{f^{2}(v)|\beta^{\prime}|^{2}+|v^{\prime}|^{2}}\,\sqrt{(1-v)^{2}+4\lambda\,{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta,\mathrm{O}(d))}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\\
&+\int_{\{v_{\eta}\neq v\}}f(1-\eta)\,|\beta^{\prime}|\,\sqrt{\eta^{2}+4\lambda\,{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta,\mathrm{O}(d))}{\,\mathrm{d}t}.\end{split}

Finally, let
\overline{\omega}(\eta)
be the continuity modulus of
\dfrac{1-s}{|\log(1-s)|}f(s)
near
1
, so that

(4.12)

\dfrac{|\log(1-s)|}{1-s}(\ell-\overline{\omega}(\eta))\leq f(s)\leq\dfrac{|\log(1-s)|}{1-s}(\ell+\overline{\omega}(\eta)),\qquad s\in[1-\eta,1).

Since for every
a\geq 0
the function

s\mapsto\dfrac{|\log(1-s)|}{1-s}\sqrt{(1-s)^{2}+a}=|\log(1-s)|\sqrt{1+a(1-s)^{-2}}

is increasing in
[0,1)
, and
v\geq 1-\eta
in the set
\{v_{\eta}\neq v\}
, we obtain that

(4.13)

\begin{split}\int_{\{v_{\eta}\neq v\}}&f(1-\eta)\,|\beta^{\prime}|\,\sqrt{\eta^{2}+4\lambda\,{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta,\mathrm{O}(d))}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\\
&\leq\frac{\ell+\overline{\omega}(\eta)}{\ell-\overline{\omega}(\eta)}\int_{\{v_{\eta}\neq v\}}f(v)\,|\beta^{\prime}|\,\sqrt{(1-v)^{2}+4\lambda\,{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta,\mathrm{O}(d))}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\\
&\leq\frac{\ell+\overline{\omega}(\eta)}{\ell-\overline{\omega}(\eta)}\int_{\{v_{\eta}\neq v\}}\sqrt{f^{2}(v)|\beta^{\prime}|^{2}+|v^{\prime}|^{2}}\,\sqrt{(1-v)^{2}+4\lambda\,{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta,\mathrm{O}(d))}{\,\mathrm{d}t}.\end{split}

Summarizing, gathering (
4.11
) and the above computations we obtain that

(4.14)

\begin{split}g^{*}_{\lambda}&(R^{-},R^{+})\leq\overline{g}_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+})\\
&+c_{\lambda}\sqrt{\eta}+4\eta^{2}+\frac{2\overline{\omega}(\eta)}{\ell-\overline{\omega}(\eta)}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{f^{2}(v)|\beta^{\prime}|^{2}+|v^{\prime}|^{2}}\,\sqrt{(1-v)^{2}+4\lambda\,{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta,\mathrm{O}(d))}{\,\mathrm{d}t}.\end{split}

This permits us to conclude by the arbitrariness of
\eta>0
.
∎

At various steps hereafter we shall need to transport trajectories onto
\mathrm{O}(d)
. We present here the basic computation to estimate the length of the resulting curves.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval,
\gamma\in W^{1,1}(I;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
be such that

{\mathrm{dist}}(\gamma(t),\mathrm{O}(d))\leq 1/2
almost everywhere.
Then there exist
C>0
independent of
\gamma
, and
\widehat{\gamma}\in W^{1,1}(I;\mathrm{O}(d))

such that

(4.15)

|\widehat{\gamma}^{\prime}|(t)\leq(1+C{\mathrm{dist}}(\gamma(t),\mathrm{O}(d)))|\gamma^{\prime}|(t)\ \text{for a.e.}\ t\in I,\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ and }\hskip 14.22636pt\widehat{\gamma}=\gamma\text{ on }\{\gamma\in\mathrm{O}(d)\}.

Proof.

We use, as in the argument leading to (
3.21
), that
\psi(A):=A(A^{T}A)^{-1/2}
is a smooth map from
\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}\setminus\{\det=0\}
to
\mathrm{O}(d)
, which is in particular Lipschitz on the set

\{{\mathrm{dist}}(\cdot,\mathrm{O}(d))\leq 1/2\}
.
At the same time, for
R\in\mathrm{O}(d)

one has
\psi(R)=R
, and for any

|\eta|\leq 1/2
, with
\eta\in\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}
, a Taylor expansion gives

(4.16)

\begin{split}\psi(R+\eta)=&(R+\eta)\left((R^{T}+\eta^{T})(R+\eta)\right)^{-1/2}=(R+\eta)({\mathrm{Id}}+\eta^{T}R+R^{T}\eta+\eta^{T}\eta)^{-1/2}\\
=&R+\eta-\frac{1}{2}R\eta^{T}R-\frac{1}{2}\eta+O(|\eta|^{2}),\end{split}

so that
|D\psi(R)\eta|\leq|\eta|
, and hence
|D\psi(R)|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1
, which by regularity of
\psi
implies

|D\psi(\xi)|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1+C{\mathrm{dist}}(\xi,\mathrm{O}(d))
,
whenever
{\mathrm{dist}}(\xi,\mathrm{O}(d))\leq 1/2
.
Let
\widehat{\gamma}:=\psi\circ\gamma
.
Then
|\widehat{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)|\leq|D\psi(\gamma(t))|_{\mathrm{op}}|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|
and the proof is concluded.
∎

We are now in a position to prove the main properties of
g_{\lambda}
, collected in the following result.

Proposition 4.4
.

For every
\lambda\in[0,+\infty]
, the function
g^{*}_{\lambda}\colon\mathrm{O}(d)\times\mathrm{O}(d)\to[0,+\infty)
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

there exists
\kappa\geq 1
depending only on
d
and
f
and such that

(4.17)

g^{*}_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+})\leq 1\wedge\kappa\Big(\,|R^{+}-R^{-}|\,\big|\log|R^{+}-R^{-}|\big|+|R^{+}-R^{-}|\Big),

for every
R^{+},R^{-}\in\mathrm{O}(d)
,
\lambda\in[0,\infty]
;

(ii)

for every
R\in\mathrm{O}(d)
and for every sequence
(R_{n})_{n}\subset\mathrm{O}(d)
converging to
R
, with
R_{n}\neq R
, there exists

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{g^{*}_{\lambda}(R_{n},R)}{|R_{n}-R|\,\big|\log|R_{n}-R|\big|}=\frac{\ell}{2};

(iii)

g^{*}_{\lambda}\in C^{0}(\mathrm{O}(d)\times\mathrm{O}(d))
.

All properties are immediately inherited by
g_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

Proof of
(i)
.

It suffices to prove the bound for
\lambda\in[0,\infty)
, with
\kappa
not depending on
\lambda
.
For every
R^{-}
,
R^{+}\in\mathrm{O}(d)
we set

\overline{\beta}(t):=\begin{cases}R^{-},&t\in\left[0,\frac{1}{3}\right],\\
R^{-}+(R^{+}-R^{-})(3t-1),&t\in\left[\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3}\right],\\
R^{+},&t\in\left[\frac{2}{3},1\right];\end{cases}\qquad\overline{v}(t):=\begin{cases}1-3t,&t\in\left[0,\frac{1}{3}\right],\\
0,&t\in\left[\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3}\right],\\
3t-2,&t\in\left[\frac{2}{3},1\right].\end{cases}

Applying Proposition
4.2
, and by a direct computation, we obtain

(4.18)

\begin{split}g^{*}_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+})=&\overline{g}_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+})\leq\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{(1-\overline{v})^{2}+4\lambda\,{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\overline{\beta},\mathrm{O}(d))}\sqrt{f^{2}(\overline{v})|\overline{\beta}^{\prime}|^{2}+|\overline{v}^{\prime}|^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\\
=&\int_{(0,1/3)\cup(2/3,1)}(1-\overline{v})|\overline{v}^{\prime}|{\,\mathrm{d}t}=1.\end{split}

This completes the proof in the case

|R^{+}-R^{-}|\geq 1
.

Assume now

|R^{+}-R^{-}|<1
.
We shall complete the proof of (
4.17
) using again the fact that
g^{*}_{\lambda}=\overline{g}_{\lambda}
, and choosing properly the competitors in the optimization problem defining
\overline{g}_{\lambda}
.
Using Lemma
4.3
on the affine map joining
R^{-}
with
R^{+}
, whose image is contained in a neighbouhood of
\mathrm{O}(d)
of size
|R^{-}-R^{+}|/2<1/2
, we produce a curve

\gamma\in C^{1}([\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3}];\mathrm{O}(d))
such that

\gamma(\frac{1}{3})=R^{-}
,
\gamma(\frac{2}{3})=R^{+}
, and

\int_{\frac{1}{3}}^{\frac{2}{3}}|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|{\,\mathrm{d}t}\leq|R^{-}-R^{+}|+C|R^{-}-R^{+}|^{2}.

For
s\in(0,1)
chosen below, we set

\beta(t):=\begin{cases}R^{-},\quad&t\in\left[0,\frac{1}{3}\right],\\
\gamma(t),\quad&t\in\left[\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3}\right],\\
R^{+},\quad&t\in\left[\frac{2}{3},1\right],\end{cases}\qquad v(t):=\begin{cases}1-3ts^{1/2},\quad&t\in\left[0,\frac{1}{3}\right],\\
1-s^{1/2},\quad&t\in\left[\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3}\right],\\
1+3(t-1)s^{1/2},\quad&t\in\left[\frac{2}{3},1\right].\end{cases}

An explicit computation, similar to (
4.18
), gives

\begin{split}\overline{g}_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+})&\leq\int_{(0,1/3)\cup(2/3,1)}(1-v)|v^{\prime}|{\,\mathrm{d}t}+\int_{\frac{1}{3}}^{\frac{2}{3}}s^{1/2}f(1-s^{1/2})|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|\,{\,\mathrm{d}t}\\
&=s+(|R^{-}-R^{+}|+C|R^{-}-R^{+}|^{2})s^{1/2}f(1-s^{1/2})\end{split}

and hence choosing
s=|R^{-}-R^{+}|
gives

(4.19)

\overline{g}_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+})\leq|R^{-}-R^{+}|+|R^{-}-R^{+}|^{3/2}(1+C|R^{-}-R^{+}|)f(1-|R^{-}-R^{+}|^{1/2}).

The conclusion then follows recalling
(
1.8
)
and Proposition
4.2
.

Proof of
(ii)
.
Let
(R_{n})_{n}\subset\mathrm{O}(d)
converging to
R
be given. Notice that
R_{n}
,
R
belong to the same connected component of
\mathrm{O}(d)
, say
\mathrm{SO}(d)
, namely
R_{n}\in\mathrm{SO}(d)
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
R\in\mathrm{SO}(d)
. We start by proving the lower bound:

(4.20)

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{g_{\lambda}(R_{n},R)}{|R_{n}-R|\,\big|\log|R_{n}-R|\big|}\geq\frac{\ell}{2}.

By monotonicity, it suffices to prove it for
\lambda=0
.
If (
4.20
) does not hold, then there are
\delta>0
and

R_{n}\in\mathrm{SO}(d)
, with
s_{n}:=|R_{n}-R|\to 0
,
and

(\overline{\beta}_{n},v_{n})\in\mathcal{U}[R_{n},R]
such that

(4.21)

\int_{0}^{1}(1-v_{n})\sqrt{f^{2}(v_{n})|\overline{\beta}_{n}^{\prime}|^{2}+|v_{n}^{\prime}|^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\leq\frac{\ell-\delta}{2}s_{n}|\log s_{n}|.

For every
n
, since

v_{n}(0)=v_{n}(1)=1
one obtains

(4.22)

{(1-v_{n}(t))^{2}}\leq\int_{0}^{1}(1-v_{n})|v_{n}^{\prime}|{\,\mathrm{d}t}\leq\frac{\ell}{2}s_{n}|\log s_{n}|,\qquad t\in(0,1).

Moreover, using (
4.21
) and

\int_{0}^{1}|\overline{\beta}_{n}^{\prime}|{\,\mathrm{d}t}\geq s_{n}
, we get

(4.23)

\begin{split}\frac{\ell-\delta}{2}s_{n}|\log s_{n}|\geq&\int_{0}^{1}(1-v_{n})f(v_{n})|\overline{\beta}_{n}^{\prime}|{\,\mathrm{d}t}\geq s_{n}\min_{[0,1]}(1-v_{n})f(v_{n}).\end{split}

Using first (
4.23
) and then
(
4.22
),

(4.24)

\begin{split}\frac{\ell-\delta}{2}\geq&\min_{[0,1]}\frac{(1-v_{n})f(v_{n})}{|\log(1-v_{n})|}\frac{|\log(1-v_{n})|}{|\log s_{n}|}\geq\min_{[0,1]}\frac{(1-v_{n})f(v_{n})}{|\log(1-v_{n})|}\frac{{|\log(\frac{\ell}{2}s_{n}|\log s_{n}|)|}}{2|\log s_{n}|}.\end{split}

Now we take the limit
n\to\infty
.
By (
4.22
),

v_{n}\to 1
uniformly. Therefore
(recalling (
1.8
))
the first fraction converges to
\ell
.
At the same time, since
s_{n}\to 0
the second fraction converges to
1/2
.
This gives a contradiction, since we assumed
\delta>0
.
This proves
(
4.20
) and then the lower bound.

The upper bound follows immediately using first (
4.19
) and then
(
1.8
). Precisely, with
s_{n}:=|R_{n}-R|\to 0
,

\begin{split}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{g_{\lambda}(R_{n},R)}{|R_{n}-R|\,\big|\log|R_{n}-R|\big|}\leq&\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{s_{n}+(1+Cs_{n})s_{n}^{3/2}f(1-s_{n}^{1/2})}{s_{n}\,\big|\log s_{n}\big|}\\
=&\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{s_{n}^{1/2}f(1-s_{n}^{1/2})}{2\big|\log s_{n}^{1/2}\big|}=\frac{\ell}{2}.\end{split}

Since we used only the bound in
(
4.19
), which does not depend on
\lambda
, the limit is uniform in
\lambda
, and therefore
(ii)
holds also for
\lambda=\infty
.

Proof of
(iii)
.
This follows by combining (
4.17
), which guarantees the continuity at the origin, and the subadditivity stated in
Proposition
4.1
(v)
.
∎

The case
\lambda=\infty
requires a separate treatment for construction of a recovery sequence leading to the upper bound (see Section
5.2
). If
R^{+}
and
R^{-}
have the same determinant, one can find admissible paths joining them that stay within
\mathrm{O}(d)
. If not, however, such curves do not exist, therefore a different construction is needed. The approximating admissible paths are actually simpler, and can be constructed explicitly, however they depend on
\delta_{\varepsilon}
, therefore in the global construction one cannot take the limit
{\varepsilon}\to 0
before the limit
T\to\infty
. The next Lemma presents this construction.

Lemma 4.5
.

Assume
R^{+},R^{-}\in\mathrm{O}(d)
.

(i)

If
\det R^{+}\neq\det R^{-}
then
there is no

(\beta,v)\in\mathcal{U}^{T}[R^{-},R^{+}]
such that

\beta(t)\in\mathrm{O}(d)
almost everywhere.

(ii)

If
g_{\infty}^{*}(R^{-},R^{+})<1
then

\det R^{+}=\det R^{-}
and there are sequences

(\beta_{T},v_{T})\in\mathcal{U}^{T}[R^{-},R^{+}]
such that

\beta_{T}(t)\in\mathrm{O}(d)
almost everywhere,

(4.25)

\lim_{T\to\infty}F^{T}(\beta_{T},v_{T})\leq g_{\infty}^{*}(R^{-},R^{+}),

and

(4.26)

\|\beta^{\prime}_{T}\|_{L^{2}((-T,T))}^{2}\leq C(R^{+},R^{-}).

(iii)

For any
T,\lambda\geq 1
there exists
(\beta_{T,\lambda},v_{T,\lambda})\in\mathcal{U}^{T}[R^{-},R^{+}]
such that

(4.27)

F^{T}_{\lambda}(\beta_{T,\lambda},v_{T,\lambda})\leq g_{\infty}^{*}(R^{-},R^{+})+\omega(T)+\omega(\lambda)

and

(4.28)

\|\beta_{T,\lambda}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}((-T,T))}^{2}\leq C\lambda T,

with
\omega(s)\to 0
as
s\to+\infty
.

In the upper bound for
\lambda=\infty
we shall use
(iii)
with sequences
\lambda_{\varepsilon}={\varepsilon}/\delta_{\varepsilon}
and

T_{\varepsilon}\to+\infty
.
One can easily also obtain that (
4.27
) is an equality, this is however not needed.

Proof.

Proof of
(i)
.

Since
\beta\in H^{1}
, it has a continuous representative, and so does
\det\beta
. A continuous function which takes values in
\{\pm 1\}
is constant.

Proof of
(ii)
.

For every
\lambda\geq 1
we pick
T_{\lambda}
and then
(\beta_{\lambda},v_{\lambda})\in\mathcal{U}^{T_{\lambda}}[R^{-},R^{+}]
so that

(4.29)

F^{T_{\lambda}}_{\lambda}(\beta_{\lambda},v_{\lambda})\leq g^{*}_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+})+\frac{1}{\lambda}.

We can assume that
\beta_{\lambda}
and
v_{\lambda}
are continuous.
We estimate, using that
v_{\lambda}(\pm T_{\lambda})=1
and the fundamental theorem of calculus on
(-T_{\lambda},t)
and on
(t,T_{\lambda})
,

(4.30)

\max_{I_{T_{\lambda}}}(1-v_{\lambda})^{2}\leq\int_{-T_{\lambda}}^{T_{\lambda}}|1-v_{\lambda}|\,|v_{\lambda}^{\prime}|{\,\mathrm{d}t}\leq g^{*}_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+})+\frac{1}{\lambda}.

The right-hand side converges to

g^{*}_{\infty}(R^{-},R^{+})<1
for
\lambda\to\infty
.
Setting
\delta:=\frac{1}{4}(1-g^{*}_{\infty}(R^{-},R^{+}))>0
, for
\lambda
large we have

(4.31)

(1-v_{\lambda}(t))^{2}\leq 1-2\delta\hskip 28.45274pt\text{ for all $t\in I_{T_{\lambda}}$}.

By monotonicity of
f
, this implies
f(v_{\lambda})\geq f(\delta)
everywhere.
Therefore (
4.29
) implies

(4.32)

\int_{-T_{\lambda}}^{T_{\lambda}}|\beta_{\lambda}^{\prime}|^{2}+|v_{\lambda}^{\prime}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\leq C,

where
C>0
may depend on

R^{-}
,
R^{+}
via
\delta
, but not on
\lambda
.
By the same computation as in (
4.30
),
using that
{\mathrm{dist}}(\beta_{\lambda}(\pm T_{\lambda}),\mathrm{O}(d))=0
,

(4.33)

\begin{split}2\lambda^{1/2}f(\delta)\max_{t\in I_{T_{\lambda}}}{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta_{\lambda}(t),\mathrm{O}(d))&\leq\int_{-T_{\lambda}}^{T_{\lambda}}2\lambda^{1/2}f(v_{\lambda})\,{\mathrm{dist}}(\beta_{\lambda},\mathrm{O}(d))|\beta_{\lambda}^{\prime}|{\,\mathrm{d}t}\\
&\leq g^{*}_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+})+\frac{1}{\lambda}+C\leq C+1,\end{split}

therefore
\|{\mathrm{dist}}(\beta_{\lambda},\mathrm{O}(d))\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\lambda^{-1/4}
(recall that
C
depends on
\delta
but not on
\lambda
).

Let
\widehat{\beta}_{\lambda}

be the result of Lemma
4.3
applied to
\beta_{\lambda}
, which obeys

(4.34)

|\widehat{\beta}_{\lambda}^{\prime}|\leq(1+C{\mathrm{dist}}(\beta_{\lambda},\mathrm{O}(d)))|\beta_{\lambda}^{\prime}|.

Therefore, by (
4.32
),
\widehat{\beta}_{\lambda}\in H^{1}(I_{T_{\lambda}};\mathrm{O}(d))
, and hence, by
(i)

\det R^{+}=\det R^{-}
. Moreover, it holds

(4.35)

F^{T_{\lambda}}_{\lambda}(\widehat{\beta}_{\lambda},v_{\lambda})\leq\left(1+\frac{C}{\lambda^{1/4}}\right)^{2}F^{T_{\lambda}}_{\lambda}(\beta_{\lambda},v_{\lambda})\leq g^{*}_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+})+\frac{C}{\lambda^{1/4}}+\frac{1}{\lambda},

and, taking
\lambda\to\infty
, we obtain

(4.36)

\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}F^{T_{\lambda}}_{\lambda}(\widehat{\beta}_{\lambda},v_{\lambda})\leq g^{*}_{\infty}(R^{-},R^{+}).

By monotonicity in
T
, the same holds along any sequence
T\to\infty
.

Proof of
(iii)
:

If
g^{*}_{\infty}(R^{-},R^{+})<1
, it follows from
(ii)
with
(\beta_{T,\lambda},v_{T,\lambda})=(\beta_{T},v_{T})
.

If not, we construct explicitly an appropriate sequence

(\beta_{T,\lambda},v_{T,\lambda})\in\mathcal{U}^{T}[R^{-},R^{+}]
, for any
T>4
.
Fix
\eta\in(0,1)
, chosen below depending on
\lambda
and
T
, and set

(4.37)

v_{T,\lambda}(t):=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ if }|t|<\eta,\\
1-e^{-(|t|-\eta)/2},&\text{ if }\eta\leq|t|<T-2+\eta,\\
1-e^{-(T-2)/2}(T-1+\eta-|t|),&\text{ if }T-2+\eta\leq|t|<T-1+\eta,\\
1,&\text{ if }|t|\geq T-1+\eta.\end{cases}

Correspondingly, we set

(4.38)

\beta_{T,\lambda}(t):=\begin{cases}R^{-},&\text{ if }t\leq-\eta,\\
\dfrac{\eta+t}{2\eta}R^{+}+\dfrac{\eta-t}{2\eta}R^{-},&\text{ if }|t|<\eta,\\
R^{+},&\text{ if }t\geq\eta,\end{cases}

so that
\beta_{T,\lambda}^{\prime}(t)=0
for
|t|\geq\eta
, and
(\beta_{T,\lambda},v_{T,\lambda})\in\mathcal{U}^{T}[R^{-},R^{+}]
.
We estimate

(4.39)

\begin{split}\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{(1-v_{T,\lambda})^{2}}{4}+|v_{T,\lambda}^{\prime}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}t}&\leq\frac{2\eta}{4}+2\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{4}+\frac{e^{-t}}{4}{\,\mathrm{d}t}+2e^{-(T-2)}\left(\frac{1}{4}+1\right)\\
&\leq 1+3e^{-T/2}+\frac{\eta}{2}\leq g^{*}_{\infty}(R^{-},R^{+})+3e^{-T/2}+\frac{\eta}{2},\end{split}

f(v_{T,\lambda})|\beta_{T,\lambda}^{\prime}|=0
almost everywhere,

(4.40)

\int_{-T}^{T}\lambda\,{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta_{T,\lambda},\mathrm{O}(d)){\,\mathrm{d}t}\leq 2\eta\,\lambda\,4d,

and

(4.41)

\|\beta_{T,\lambda}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}((-T,T))}^{2}=\frac{|R^{+}-R^{-}|^{2}}{(2\eta)^{2}}2\eta\leq\frac{d}{\eta}.

At this point we choose
\eta:=1/(\lambda T)
and conclude the proof.
∎

5.
Proof of
\Gamma
-convergence

In this last section we show the
\Gamma
-convergence result by proving separately the lower bound for
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}
and the upper bound for
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
(recall (
2.31
) and (
2.32
)).

5.1.
Lower bound

This section is devoted to the proof of the following lower bound result (see (LB)’ in Theorem
2.15
).

Theorem 5.1
.

For any
(u_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G}\times[0,1])
and
u\in\mathrm{SBV}_{0}(\Omega;\mathrm{O}(d)/\mathcal{G})
satisfying

u_{\varepsilon}\to u\text{ in }L^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G}),\qquad v_{\varepsilon}\to 1\text{ in }L^{1}(\Omega).

it holds

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\lambda}({u},1)\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}({u}_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon}).

The proof uses first a blow up argument to reduce to the case of a flat interface with the limiting
{u}
taking only two values and then a slicing argument for functions in
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})
to reduce to a one dimensional problem in the interval

I:=\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)
. Being in dimension one allows us to exploit the relation between
H^{1}(I;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G})
and
H^{1}(I;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
discussed in Lemma
2.5
. The main additional difficulty in the treatment of these one dimensional sections, which is handled in a sequence of lemmas before the main proof, is the fact that the functionals
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}
are not invariant under scaling, because the truncation with
M_{\varepsilon}
of the prefactor
f_{\varepsilon}
scales differently from the rest. Indeed the surface energy
g_{\lambda}
defined in Section
4
does not contain any truncation.

To deal with this, we introduce an intermediate step with a further truncation in the prefactor
and
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
we replace
f
by

(5.1)

h_{j}(s):=\frac{js}{1-s}\wedge f(s),\qquad s\in[0,1),

and we introduce the corresponding surface energy densities

(5.2)

\begin{split}{g}_{j,\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+}):=\inf\bigg\{\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}&\sqrt{(1-v)^{2}+4\lambda\,{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta,\mathrm{O}(d))}\sqrt{h_{j}^{2}(v)|\beta^{\prime}|^{2}+|v^{\prime}|^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\colon\\
&(\beta,v)\in\mathcal{U}^{1/2}[R^{-},R^{+}]\bigg\}\end{split}

for
\lambda\in[0,+\infty)
, and

(5.3)

{g}_{j,\infty}(R^{-},R^{+}):=\sup_{\lambda>0}{g}_{j,\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+}).

The set of admissible pairs
\mathcal{U}^{1/2}[R^{-},R^{+}]
is defined in (
4.1
).
These are good approximations of
g_{\lambda}
as shown in Lemma
5.2
below. The reason for doing this is that it permits to replace the phase field
v_{\varepsilon}
with a truncated version
\widetilde{v}_{\varepsilon}
with a small error and, at the same time, to remove the truncation from the prefactor.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
\lambda\in[0,+\infty]
,
R^{+}
,
R^{-}\in\mathrm{O}(d)
, the following holds:

(5.4)

g_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+})\leq\lim_{j\to+\infty}{g}_{j,\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+}).

Proof.

The proof follows closely that of
[
19
, Proposition 7.3 (iii)]
.
We sketch here the main steps in the case
\lambda\in[0,+\infty)
, being the case
\lambda=+\infty
obtained by taking the supremum over
\lambda>0
.
For fixed
R^{-},R^{+}\in\mathrm{O}(d)
, for any Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}
,
we consider the functionals defined in
H^{1}(J;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})\times H^{1}(J;[0,1])
for any interval
J
that contains
E
by

(5.5)

\begin{split}H_{j,\lambda}(\beta,v;E)&:=\int_{E}\sqrt{(1-v)^{2}+4\lambda\,{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta,\mathrm{O}(d))}\,\sqrt{h_{j}^{2}(v)|\beta^{\prime}|^{2}+|v^{\prime}|^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}t},\\
H_{\lambda}(\beta,v;E)&:=\int_{E}\sqrt{(1-v)^{2}+4\lambda\,{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta,\mathrm{O}(d))}\,\sqrt{f^{2}(v)|\beta^{\prime}|^{2}+|v^{\prime}|^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}t},\end{split}

so that

{g}_{j,\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+})=\inf_{(\beta,v)\in\mathcal{U}^{1/2}[R^{-},R^{+}]}H_{j,\lambda}(\beta,v,I),\qquad{g}_{\lambda}^{*}(R^{-},R^{+})=\inf_{(\beta,v)\in\mathcal{U}^{1/2}[R^{-},R^{+}]}H_{\lambda}(\beta,v,I).

First we note that by (
5.1
) and Prop.
4.4
(i)
,

{g}_{j,\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+})\leq{g}_{j+1,\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+})\leq g^{*}_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+})\leq 1,

and hence we have to show that

(5.6)

{g}_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+})\leq\sup_{j}{g}_{j,\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+}).

For any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, let
(\beta_{j},v_{j})\in\mathcal{U}^{1/2}[R^{-},R^{+}]
be such that

(5.7)

H_{j,\lambda}(\beta_{j},v_{j};I)\leq{g}_{j,\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+})+\frac{1}{j}.

If
\liminf_{j}\min_{[0,1]}v_{j}=0
, then
the same argument as in (
4.30
) easily gives

\limsup_{j\to+\infty}H_{j,\lambda}(\beta_{j},v_{j};I)\geq 1

which concludes the proof.

Assume now that
\liminf_{j}\min_{[0,1]}v_{j}>0
and pick
0<\eta<\liminf_{j}\min_{[0,1]}v_{j}
. We define

v_{j}^{\eta}:=v_{j}\wedge(1-\eta)
. Extending
\beta_{j}
constant outside
I
and interpolating
v_{j}^{\eta}
linearly in order to achieve the boundary condition
1
in the interval
(-1,1)
, and then using the invariance under reparametrization of
H_{\lambda}
, we immediately obtain that

(5.8)

g_{\lambda}(R^{+},R^{-})\leq g^{*}_{\lambda}(R^{+},R^{-})\leq H_{\lambda}(\beta_{j},v^{\eta}_{j};(-1,1))\leq H_{\lambda}(\beta_{j},v^{\eta}_{j};I)+\eta^{2}.

We subdivide
I
in three subsets:

(5.9)

\begin{split}&A_{j}:=\{t\in I:\ v_{j}(t)\leq 1-\eta\},\\
&B_{j}:=\{t\in I:\ 1-\eta<v_{j}(t)\leq 1\,,\ h_{j}(v_{j}(t))=f(v_{j}(t))\},\\
&C_{j}:=\{t\in I:\ 1-\eta<v_{j}(t)\leq 1\,,\ h_{j}(v_{j}(t))<f(v_{j}(t))\}.\\
\end{split}

We start with
A_{j}
. Here
v_{j}=v_{j}^{\eta}
and since for
j
sufficiently large
h_{j}=f
in
[\eta,1-\eta]
,

(5.10)

H_{\lambda}(\beta_{j},v_{j}^{\eta};A_{j})=H_{j,\lambda}(\beta_{j},v_{j};A_{j})\qquad\hbox{for all $j$ large enough}.

In order to treat the second set we use the same argument as in (
4.12
) and (
4.13
) and obtain

(5.11)

H_{\lambda}(\beta_{j},v_{j}^{\eta};B_{j})\leq\frac{\ell+\overline{\omega}(\eta)}{\ell-\overline{\omega}(\eta)}H_{j,\lambda}(\beta_{j},v_{j};B_{j})\qquad\hbox{for all $j$},\qquad\overline{\omega}(\eta)\to 0\ \text{as}\ \eta\to 0.

Finally we observe that if
t\in C_{j}
then
(1-v_{j}(t))h_{j}(v_{j}(t))=jv_{j}(t)\geq j(1-\eta)
and therefore

(1-v_{j}(t))h_{j}(v_{j}(t))\geq\eta f(1-\eta)=(1-v_{j}^{\eta})f(v_{j}^{\eta})

for all
j\geq\frac{\eta f(1-\eta)}{1-\eta}
. Using the same argument as for
B_{j}
we conclude that

(5.12)

H_{\lambda}(\beta_{j},v_{j}^{\eta};C_{j})\leq H_{j,\lambda}(\beta_{j},v_{j};C_{j})\qquad\hbox{for all $j\geq\frac{\eta f(1-\eta)}{1-\eta}$}.

Then (
5.6
) follows by (
5.8
), (
5.10
), (
5.11
) (
5.12
), and (
5.7
), taking first the limit
j\to\infty
and then
\eta\to 0
.
∎

Next, we show that in the definition of the energies
g_{j,\lambda}
one can also consider test functions such that the boundary conditions are fulfilled only asymptotically.

Lemma 5.3
.

Fix
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda\in[0,+\infty)
,
R^{+}
,
R^{-}\in\mathrm{O}(d)
. Consider a sequence
(\beta_{k},v_{k})\in H^{1}(I;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}\times[0,1])

such that

(5.13)

\beta_{k}\xrightarrow{{L}^{1}_{\mathcal{G}}}R^{-}\chi_{(-\frac{1}{2},0)}+R^{+}\chi_{(0,-\frac{1}{2})}\text{ and }v_{k}\to 1\text{ in }L^{1}.

Then the functional
H_{j,\lambda}
defined in (
5.5
) obeys

(5.14)

\min_{G^{+},G^{-}\in\mathcal{G}}g_{j,\lambda}(G^{-}R^{-},G^{+}R^{+})\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}H_{j,\lambda}(\beta_{k},v_{k};I).

Proof.

As usual, in order to interpolate the field
\beta_{k}
we need to move
v_{k}
away from 1.
Possibly passing to a subsequence, we have pointwise convergence almost everywhere, therefore we can choose
z\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2})
and
G_{k}^{\pm}\in\mathcal{G}
such that

(5.15)

\alpha_{k}:=|G_{k}^{+}R^{+}-\beta_{k}(z)|+|G_{k}^{-}R^{-}-\beta_{k}(-z)|+|1-v_{k}(z)|+|1-v_{k}(-z)|\to 0;

for
k
sufficiently large we have
h_{j}(s)=js/(1-s)
for all
s\in[1-\alpha_{k},1)
. Therefore
on the set
\{v_{k}\geq 1-\alpha_{k}\}
, the integrand in
the functional

H_{j,\lambda}(v_{k},\beta_{k};I)
can be rewritten as

(5.16)

\sqrt{1+\frac{4\lambda}{(1-v_{k})^{2}}\,{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta_{k},\mathrm{O}(d))}\,\sqrt{j^{2}v_{k}^{2}|\beta_{k}^{\prime}|^{2}+(1-v_{k})^{2}|v_{k}^{\prime}|^{2}}

and, setting

\widehat{v}_{k}:=v_{k}\wedge(1-\alpha_{k})
, one obtains

(5.17)

H_{j,\lambda}(\beta_{k},\widehat{v}_{k};(-z,z))\leq H_{j,\lambda}(\beta_{k},v_{k};(-z,z)).

We extend
\widehat{v}_{k}
to equal
1-\alpha_{k}
on the rest of
[-1,1]
, to be one
outside
(-2,2)
, and to be the affine interpolation in-between.
We then extend
\beta_{k}
so that
\beta_{k}=G_{k}^{+}R^{+}

on
(1,\infty)
,
\beta_{k}=G_{k}^{-}R^{-}
on
(-\infty,-1)
, and the affine interpolation in
(-1,-z)
and
(z,1)
. We
observe that both functions are in
H^{1}
on
(-2,2)
.
We compute

(5.18)

H_{j,\lambda}(\beta_{k},\widehat{v}_{k};(z,1))\leq(1-z)\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{2}+4\lambda\alpha_{k}^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{j^{2}}{\alpha_{k}^{2}}4\alpha_{k}^{2}}=2j\alpha_{k}\sqrt{1+4\lambda},

(5.19)

H_{j,\lambda}(\beta_{k},\widehat{v}_{k};(1,2))=\int_{1}^{2}\alpha_{k}(2-t)\alpha_{k}{\,\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{k}^{2},

and the same on the other side. Since
H_{j,\lambda}
is invariant under reparametrizations we obtain

(5.20)

g_{j,\lambda}(G_{k}^{-}R^{-},G_{k}^{+}R^{+})\leq H_{j,\lambda}(\beta_{k},\widehat{v}_{k};(-2,2))\leq H_{j,\lambda}(v_{k},\beta_{k};I)+\alpha_{k}^{2}+4j\alpha_{k}\sqrt{1+4\lambda}

for all
k
sufficiently large;
taking the limit
k\to\infty
the proof is concluded.
∎

We conclude with a lower bound for the one-dimensional functionals obtained from the blow-up.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let

\varepsilon_{k}/\delta_{k}\to\lambda\in[0,+\infty]
,

M_{k}\to+\infty
,
and assume that
(\beta_{k},v_{k})\in H^{1}(I;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}\times[0,1])
,
I=[-1/2,1/2]
, obeys

\beta_{k}\to R^{-}\chi_{[-1/2,0)}+R^{+}\chi_{[0,1/2]}\ \text{in}\ {L}^{1}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega,\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}),\quad v_{k}\to 1\ \text{in}\ L^{1}(I)

for some
R^{-},R^{+}\in\mathrm{O}(d)
.
Then

\begin{split}&\min_{G^{+},G^{-}\in\mathcal{G}}g_{j,\lambda}(G^{-}R^{-},G^{+}R^{+})\\
&\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\int_{{I}}(M^{2}_{k}\wedge{\varepsilon_{k}}f^{2}(v_{k}))\,|\beta^{\prime}_{k}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}t}+\mathrm{PF}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(v_{k};I)+\frac{1}{\delta_{k}}\int_{{I}}{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta_{k},\mathrm{O}(d)){\,\mathrm{d}t}.\end{split}

Proof.

Let us first assume
\lambda<+\infty
. For
h_{j}
defined in (
5.1
), we have

(5.21)

\int_{{I}}(M^{2}_{k}\wedge{\varepsilon_{k}}f^{2}(v_{k}))\,|\beta^{\prime}_{k}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\geq\int_{I}(M^{2}_{k}\wedge\varepsilon_{k}h_{j}^{2}(v_{k}))\,|\beta_{k}^{\prime}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}t}.

Choose
\gamma_{k}:=1-j\sqrt{{\varepsilon}_{k}}/M_{k}

and define
\widetilde{v}_{k}:=v_{k}\wedge(1-\gamma_{k})
.
Then

{\varepsilon}_{k}^{1/2}h_{j}(\gamma_{k})\leq{\varepsilon}_{k}^{1/2}j\gamma_{k}/(1-\gamma_{k})\leq M_{k}
and

(5.22)

\begin{split}\int_{I}&(M^{2}_{k}\wedge\varepsilon_{k}h_{j}^{2}(v_{k}))\,|\beta_{k}^{\prime}|^{2}+\frac{(1-v_{k})^{2}}{4\varepsilon_{k}}+\varepsilon_{k}|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\\
&\geq\int_{I}\varepsilon_{k}h_{j}^{2}(\widetilde{v}_{k})\,|\beta^{\prime}_{k}|^{2}+\frac{(1-\widetilde{v}_{k})^{2}}{4\varepsilon_{k}}+\varepsilon_{k}|\widetilde{v}_{k}^{\prime}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}t}-\int_{\{v_{k}>\gamma_{k}\}}\frac{(1-\gamma_{k})^{2}}{4\varepsilon_{k}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\\
&\geq\int_{I}\varepsilon_{k}h_{j}^{2}(\widetilde{v}_{k})\,|\beta^{\prime}_{k}|^{2}+\frac{(1-\widetilde{v}_{k})^{2}}{4\varepsilon_{k}}+\varepsilon_{k}|\widetilde{v}_{k}^{\prime}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}t}-\frac{j^{2}}{4M_{k}^{2}}.\end{split}

Therefore

(5.23)

\begin{split}&\int_{{I}}(M^{2}_{k}\wedge{\varepsilon_{k}}f^{2}(v_{k}))\,|\beta^{\prime}_{k}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}t}+\text{PF}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(v_{k};I)+\frac{1}{\delta_{k}}\int_{{I}}{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta_{k},\mathrm{O}(d)){\,\mathrm{d}t}\\
&\geq\int_{I}\sqrt{(1-\widetilde{v}_{k})^{2}+4\frac{{\varepsilon}_{k}}{\delta_{k}}{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta_{k},\mathrm{O}(d))}\sqrt{h_{j}^{2}(\widetilde{v}_{k})|\beta_{k}^{\prime}|^{2}+|\widetilde{v}_{k}^{\prime}|^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}-\frac{j^{2}}{4M_{k}^{2}}.\end{split}

The last term tends to zero for
k\to\infty
. In order to estimate the integral, we distinguish three cases.
If
\lambda=0
,
the integral on right-hand side of this equation is larger than or equal to
H_{j,0}(\beta_{k},\widetilde{v}_{k})
and we conclude using Lemma
5.3
.
If
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
, the same integral is larger than or equal to

\rho_{k}^{1/2}H_{j,\lambda}(\beta_{k},\widetilde{v}_{k})
, where

\rho_{k}:=1\wedge{\frac{{\varepsilon}_{k}}{\lambda\delta_{k}}}\to 1
,
and the conclusion follows similarly.
Indeed, for any
a,b\geq 0
,

\rho_{k}^{1/2}\sqrt{a+\lambda b}=\sqrt{\rho_{k}a+\rho_{k}\lambda b}\leq\sqrt{a+\frac{{\varepsilon}_{k}}{\delta_{k}}b}.

It remains to treat the case
{\varepsilon}_{k}/\delta_{k}\to\infty
. Fix
\lambda^{\prime}>1
.
For
k
sufficiently large, we have

{\varepsilon}_{k}/\delta_{k}\geq\lambda^{\prime}
and therefore
the integral in (
5.23
) is larger or equal than
H_{j,\lambda^{\prime}}(\beta_{k},\widetilde{v}_{k})
.
The conclusion now follows by taking the supremum in
\lambda^{\prime}
, in view of (
5.3
) (and recalling again (
5.2
)).
∎

We are ready to prove the lower bound, by blow–up and slicing.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

If
\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}({u}_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})=+\infty
we have nothing to prove.
On the other hand, if

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}({u}_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})<+\infty

by Theorem
3.2
we may assume, possibly passing to a subsequence and with a small abuse of notation, that
v=1
a.e. in
\Omega
,
{u}\in\mathrm{SBV}_{0}(\Omega;\mathrm{O}(d)/\mathcal{G})
, and

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}({u}_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}({u}_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon}).

We define the nonnegative Radon measures

(5.24)

\mu_{\varepsilon}:=\left(f_{\varepsilon}^{2}(v_{\varepsilon})\,|\nabla{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{(1-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}}{4\varepsilon}+\varepsilon|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}({u}_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{O}(d)/\mathcal{G})\right)\mathcal{L}^{N},

so that
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}({u}_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})=\mu_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
, and the uniform bound for the energies corresponds to a uniform bound for the total variation of the measures
\mu_{\varepsilon}
, and hence there exists a nonnegative Radon measure
\mu
in
\Omega
which is the weak*–limit of
\mu_{\varepsilon}
(up to extracting a further subsequence, not relabeled). By the lower semicontinuity of the total variation with respect to the weak* convergence of measures, we have that

\mu(\Omega)\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mu_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}({u}_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon}),

and therefore the result is proved once we show that

(5.25)

\mu(\Omega)\geq\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\lambda}({u},1),

or, equivalently, that for
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
-a.e.
x_{0}\in J_{u}
it holds

(5.26)

\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu}{{\,\mathrm{d}}(\mathcal{H}^{N-1}{\mathchoice{\,\hbox{\vrule width=0.2pt\vbox to7.0pt{\hrule width=7.0pt,height=0.2pt}}\,}{\,\hbox{\vrule width=0.2pt\vbox to7.0pt{\hrule width=7.0pt,height=0.2pt}}\,}{\,\hbox{\vrule width=0.2pt\vbox to5.0pt{\hrule width=5.0pt,height=0.2pt}}\,}{\,\hbox{\vrule width=0.2pt\vbox to3.35pt{\hrule width=3.35pt,height=0.2pt}}\,}}J_{u})}(x_{0})\geq g_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+})

where
R^{\pm}\in u^{\pm}(x_{0})
are any representatives of the equivalence class, the choice being irrelevant by the invariance of
g_{\lambda}
under the separate action of
\mathcal{G}
on its two arguments.

In the following, we prove the last inequality for
x_{0}\in J_{u}
such that the limit

(5.27)

\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mu(Q^{\nu}_{\rho}(x_{0}))}{\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(J_{u}\cap Q^{\nu}_{\rho}(x_{0}))}=\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu}{{\,\mathrm{d}}(\mathcal{H}^{N-1}{\mathchoice{\,\hbox{\vrule width=0.2pt\vbox to7.0pt{\hrule width=7.0pt,height=0.2pt}}\,}{\,\hbox{\vrule width=0.2pt\vbox to7.0pt{\hrule width=7.0pt,height=0.2pt}}\,}{\,\hbox{\vrule width=0.2pt\vbox to5.0pt{\hrule width=5.0pt,height=0.2pt}}\,}{\,\hbox{\vrule width=0.2pt\vbox to3.35pt{\hrule width=3.35pt,height=0.2pt}}\,}}J_{u})}(x_{0})

exists finite, and

(5.28)

\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(J_{u}\cap Q^{\nu}_{\rho}(x_{0}))}{\rho^{N-1}}=1,

where
\nu:=\nu_{u}(x_{0})
, and
Q^{\nu}_{\rho}(x_{0})=x_{0}+\rho Q^{\nu}
is the cube centered in
x_{0}
with a face orthogonal to
\nu
and side length
\rho
.

By
[
1
, Theorem 2.3]
,
J_{u}
is a
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
countably rectifiable set, and hence (
5.27
) and (
5.28
) are satisfied
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
-a.e in
J_{u}
.
Fixed
x_{0}
as above,
by weak
∗
–convergence of measures, we have that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mu_{\varepsilon}(Q^{\nu}_{\rho}(x_{0}))=\mu(Q^{\nu}_{\rho}(x_{0}))
for every
\rho
such that
\mu(\partial Q^{\nu}_{\rho}(x_{0}))=0
. Then, by diagonalization, we can find sequences

\rho_{k}\to 0
,
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0

with
\varepsilon_{k}/\rho_{k}\to 0
,

\delta_{\varepsilon_{k}}/\rho_{k}\to 0
and
such that, setting
u_{k}:=u_{\varepsilon_{k}}
,
v_{k}:=v_{\varepsilon_{k}}
, it holds

\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu}{{\,\mathrm{d}}(\mathcal{H}^{N-1}{\mathchoice{\,\hbox{\vrule width=0.2pt\vbox to7.0pt{\hrule width=7.0pt,height=0.2pt}}\,}{\,\hbox{\vrule width=0.2pt\vbox to7.0pt{\hrule width=7.0pt,height=0.2pt}}\,}{\,\hbox{\vrule width=0.2pt\vbox to5.0pt{\hrule width=5.0pt,height=0.2pt}}\,}{\,\hbox{\vrule width=0.2pt\vbox to3.35pt{\hrule width=3.35pt,height=0.2pt}}\,}}J_{u})}(x_{0})=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon_{k}}(Q^{\nu}_{\rho_{k}}(x_{0}))}{\rho_{k}^{N-1}}=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\rho_{k}^{N-1}}\,\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u_{k},v_{k};Q^{\nu}_{\rho_{k}}(x_{0}))

and

\rho_{k}^{-N}\|\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{G}}(u_{k},[\overline{R}](\cdot+x_{0}))+|v_{k}-1|\,\|_{L^{1}(Q^{\nu}_{\rho_{k}}(x_{0}))}\to 0,

where

Changing variable to

in the integrals defining
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u_{k},v_{k};Q^{\nu}_{\rho_{k}}(x_{0}))

\widehat{u}_{k}(y):={u}_{k}(x_{0}+\rho y),\quad\widehat{v}_{k}(y):=v_{k}(x_{0}+\rho y),\qquad y\in Q^{\nu},

we get

\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{G}}(\widehat{u}_{k},[\overline{R}])\to 0
,

\widehat{v}_{k}\to 1
in

, and

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\rho_{k}^{N-1}}\,&\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u_{k},v_{k};Q^{\nu}_{\rho_{k}}(x_{0}))\\
&=\int_{Q^{\nu}}(\widehat{M}_{k}^{2}\wedge\widehat{\varepsilon}_{k}f^{2}(\widehat{v}_{k}))\,|\nabla\widehat{u}_{k}|^{2}+\frac{(1-\widehat{v}_{k})^{2}}{4\widehat{\varepsilon}_{k}}+\widehat{\varepsilon}_{k}|\nabla\widehat{v}_{k}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\widehat{\delta}_{k}}\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}(\widehat{u}_{k},\mathrm{O}(d)/\mathcal{G}){\,\mathrm{d}y}\end{split}

for

\widehat{M}_{k}:=\frac{M_{\varepsilon_{k}}}{\sqrt{\rho_{k}}}\quad\widehat{\varepsilon}_{k}:=\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\rho_{k}},\quad\widehat{\delta}_{k}:=\frac{\delta_{\varepsilon_{k}}}{\rho_{k}},\quad\text{so that}\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\widehat{\varepsilon}_{k}}{\widehat{\delta}_{k}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{{\varepsilon}_{k}}{\delta_{k}}=\lambda.

Hence, if we consider the slices of these functions in the direction
\nu
, defined for
z\in\Pi^{\nu}\cap Q^{\nu}:=\{z\in Q^{\nu}\colon z\cdot\nu=0\}
as

(\widehat{u}_{k})^{\nu}_{z}(t):=\widehat{u}_{k}(z+t\nu),\quad(\widehat{v}_{k})^{\nu}_{z}(t):=\widehat{v}_{k}(z+t\nu),\qquad t\in I=\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right],

then, by Proposition
2.11

\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
–a.e.
z\in\Pi^{\nu}\cap Q^{\nu}
,

(5.29)

((\widehat{u}_{k})^{\nu}_{z},(\widehat{v}_{k})^{\nu}_{z})\in H^{1}(I;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G}\times[0,1]),

and

(5.30)

\begin{split}(\widehat{u}_{k})^{\nu}_{z}&\to[R^{-}]\chi_{[-1/2,0)}+[R^{+}]\chi_{[0,1/2]}\quad\text{in }L^{1}(I;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}/\mathcal{G}),\\
(\widehat{v}_{k})^{\nu}_{z}&\to 1\quad\text{in }L^{1}(I).\end{split}

By Lemma
2.5
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and for every
z\in\Pi^{\nu}\cap Q^{\nu}
such that (
5.29
) holds true,
there exists

such that

and

|\beta_{k,z}^{\prime}|=|((\widehat{u}_{k})^{\nu}_{z})^{\prime}|
, and hence

\begin{split}\widehat{F}_{k}((\widehat{u}_{k})^{\nu}_{z},&(\widehat{v}_{k})^{\nu}_{z};I)\\
:=\int_{I}&(\widehat{M}_{k}^{2}\wedge\widehat{\varepsilon}_{k}f^{2}((\widehat{v}_{k})^{\nu}_{z}))\,|((\widehat{u}_{k})^{\nu}_{z})^{\prime}|^{2}+\frac{(1-(\widehat{v}_{k})^{\nu}_{z})^{2}}{4\widehat{\varepsilon}_{k}}+\widehat{\varepsilon}_{k}|((\widehat{v}_{k})^{\nu}_{z})^{\prime}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\widehat{\delta}_{k}}\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}((\widehat{u}_{k})^{\nu}_{z},\mathrm{O}(d)/\mathcal{G}){\,\mathrm{d}t}\\
=\int_{I}&(\widehat{M}_{k}^{2}\wedge\widehat{\varepsilon}_{k}f^{2}((\widehat{v}_{k})^{\nu}_{z}))\,|\beta_{k,z}^{\prime}|^{2}+\frac{(1-(\widehat{v}_{k})^{\nu}_{z})^{2}}{4\widehat{\varepsilon}_{k}}+\widehat{\varepsilon}_{k}|((\widehat{v}_{k})^{\nu}_{z})^{\prime}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\widehat{\delta}_{k}}{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta_{k,z},\mathrm{O}(d)){\,\mathrm{d}t}.\end{split}

Moreover, by (
5.30
),

\beta_{k,z}\xrightarrow{{L}^{1}_{\mathcal{G}}}R^{-}\chi_{[-1/2,0)}+R^{+}\chi_{[0,1/2]},\qquad\text{for}\ \mathcal{H}^{N-1}-\text{a.e.}\ z\in\Pi^{\nu}\cap Q^{\nu}.

By Lemma
5.2
and Lemma
5.4
,
we obtain

\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\widehat{F}_{k}((\widehat{u}_{k})^{\nu}_{z},(\widehat{v}_{k})^{\nu}_{z};I)\geq g_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+}),

for
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
–a.e.
z\in\Pi^{\nu}\cap Q^{\nu}
.

The validity of (
5.26
), and hence the conclusion, now follows by integrating on
\Pi^{\nu}
, and using Fubini’s theorem, the estimates in Proposition
2.11
, and Fatou’s lemma:

\begin{split}\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu}{{\,\mathrm{d}}(\mathcal{H}^{N-1}{\mathchoice{\,\hbox{\vrule width=0.2pt\vbox to7.0pt{\hrule width=7.0pt,height=0.2pt}}\,}{\,\hbox{\vrule width=0.2pt\vbox to7.0pt{\hrule width=7.0pt,height=0.2pt}}\,}{\,\hbox{\vrule width=0.2pt\vbox to5.0pt{\hrule width=5.0pt,height=0.2pt}}\,}{\,\hbox{\vrule width=0.2pt\vbox to3.35pt{\hrule width=3.35pt,height=0.2pt}}\,}}J_{u})}(x_{0})&=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\rho_{k}^{N-1}}\,\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u_{k},v_{k};Q^{\nu}_{\rho_{k}}(x_{0}))\geq\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\Pi^{\nu}\cap Q^{\nu}}\widehat{F}_{k}((\widehat{u}_{k})^{\nu}_{z},(\widehat{v}_{k})^{\nu}_{z};I)\,{\,\mathrm{d}}z\\
&\geq\int_{\Pi^{\nu}\cap Q^{\nu}}\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\widehat{F}_{k}((\widehat{u}_{k})^{\nu}_{z},(\widehat{v}_{k})^{\nu}_{z};I)\,{\,\mathrm{d}}z\geq g_{\lambda}(R^{-},R^{+}).\qed\end{split}

Theorem 5.6
.

For any
\beta\in\mathrm{SBV}_{0}(\Omega;\mathrm{O}(d))
there exists
(\beta_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})\times H^{1}(\Omega)
,
with

\beta_{\varepsilon}\to\beta\text{ in }{L}^{1}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}),\qquad v_{\varepsilon}\to 1\text{ in }L^{1}(\Omega),

and such that

Moreover, for any sequence
\eta_{\varepsilon}\to 0

\lim_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\frac{\eta_{\varepsilon}}{{\varepsilon}}=0\text{ and }\lim_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\frac{\eta_{\varepsilon}}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}=0

Proof of Theorem
5.6
.

We first remark that the density result

[
7
, Lemma 4.1]
, valid for sharp-interface energies depending on the jump, holds also for our energies

\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}
that depend on the two traces. Namely, with the very same proof of
[
7
, Lemma 4.1]
one obtains that for every
given
\beta\in\mathrm{SBV}_{0}(\Omega;\mathrm{O}(d))
there exist
\beta_{k}\in\mathrm{SBV}_{0}(\Omega_{k};\mathrm{O}(d))
such that

\Omega\subset\subset\Omega_{k}\qquad\beta_{k}\to\beta\quad\text{ in }L^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{O}(d)),\qquad\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}(\beta,1)=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}(\beta_{k},1;\Omega_{k}),

J_{\beta_{k}}\text{ is a finite union simplexes of dimension $N-1$.}

The map
\beta_{k}

constructed in
[
7
, Lemma 4.1]

takes values in
\mathrm{O}(d)
because it coincides in any point of
\Omega
with a suitable value of
\beta
.

Therefore we can assume that

\Omega\subset\subset\Omega^{\prime}
,

\beta\in\mathrm{SBV}_{0}(\Omega^{\prime};\mathrm{O}(d))
is piecewise constant on finitely many convex polyhedra,
whose faces are unions of simplexes of dimension
N-1
, which are denoted by
F_{l}
.
We need to find sequences
(\beta_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})\to(\beta,1)
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
such that

\limsup_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\beta_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})\leq\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}(\beta,1;\Omega^{\prime})=\sum_{l}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(F_{l})g_{\lambda}(R^{-}_{l},R^{+}_{l}),

where
R^{\pm}_{l}\in\mathrm{O}(d)
are the two traces of
\beta
on the face
F_{l}
.
We start with a decomposition of the domain, referring to Figure
2
for an illustration.
We denote by
\nu_{l}
the normal to
F_{l}

and by
\partial^{\prime}F_{l}

the relative boundary of
F_{l}
, in the sense of the boundary of
F_{l}
seen as a subset of a hyperplane.
Possibly subdividing some of the facets, we can ensure that
they have disjoint
(N-1)
-dimensional interior, in the sense that
the sets

are disjoint.
For
y\in F_{l}
we write
d_{l}(y)
for the distance to the boundary,
d_{l}(y):={\mathrm{dist}}(y,\partial^{\prime}F_{l})
, and write briefly
\Gamma:=\bigcup_{l}\partial^{\prime}F_{l}
.

In what follows we denote by
(E)_{r}
the

-neighborhood of a set

.

Since each
\partial^{\prime}F_{l}
is a finite union of
(N-2)
-dimensional polyhedra, we have
(see, e.g.,
[

)

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{|(\partial^{\prime}F_{l})_{r}|}{\pi r^{2}}=\mathcal{H}^{N-2}(\partial^{\prime}F_{l})<\infty,

so that there is
C
with

(5.34)

|(\Gamma)_{r}|\leq Cr^{2}\text{ for all }r\in(0,1).

Fix
L\geq 1
, we shall in the end take
{\varepsilon}\to 0
,
L\to\infty
, with
L{\varepsilon}\to 0
and other conditions that will become clear later. We observe that,
since the

are
(N-1)
-dimensional simplexes with disjoint interior, there is
c_{*}\in[1,\infty)
such that the sets

(5.35)

E^{l}_{\varepsilon}:=\{y+t\nu_{l}:y\in F_{l},d_{l}(y)>c_{*}L{\varepsilon},\ t\in(-L{\varepsilon},L{\varepsilon})\}

are pairwise disjoint if

is sufficiently small, see Figure

.
In these regions we can use the optimal profiles; we shall than need to interpolate around
\Gamma
.

We observe that

and that

, so that

\text{PF}_{\varepsilon}(v^{0}_{\varepsilon})\leq|(\Gamma)_{3M{\varepsilon}}|\left(\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}+\frac{{\varepsilon}}{(M{\varepsilon})^{2}}\right)\leq CL^{2}{\varepsilon}.

For each

such that
g_{\lambda}(R_{l}^{-},R_{l}^{+})=g_{\lambda}^{*}(R_{l}^{-},G_{l}^{-1}R_{l}^{+})

(see

If
\lambda<\infty
, by (

(\widetilde{\beta}_{l},\widetilde{v}_{l})\in H^{1}((-L,L);\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}\times[0,1])

(5.38)

\begin{split}\widetilde{v}_{l}(\pm L)=1,\hskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\beta}_{l}(-L)=R^{-}_{l},\hskip 5.69054ptG_{l}\widetilde{\beta}_{l}(L)=R^{+}_{l},\hskip 5.69054pt\|\widetilde{\beta}_{l}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sqrt{d},\\
\|\widetilde{\beta}_{l}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}((-L,L))}^{2}\leq C(L)\,,\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}^{L}(\widetilde{\beta}_{l},\widetilde{v}_{l})\leq g_{\lambda}(R^{+}_{l},R^{-}_{l})+\omega(L),\end{split}

.
If instead

, we use

and for any fixed

we take

(5.39)

\lambda_{{\varepsilon}}:=\frac{{\varepsilon}}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}

and select

(\widetilde{\beta}_{l},\widetilde{v}_{l})\in H^{1}((-L,L);\mathrm{M}^{d\times d}\times[0,1])
, depending on

and

,
so that the first line of (

\|\widetilde{\beta}_{l}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}((-L,L))}^{2}\leq C\lambda_{\varepsilon}L\,,\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{{\varepsilon}}}^{L}(\widetilde{\beta}_{l},\widetilde{v}_{l})\leq g_{\infty}(R^{+}_{l},R^{-}_{l})+\omega({L})+\omega({\lambda_{{\varepsilon}}}),

.

, using

and

v_{\varepsilon}^{l}(y+t\nu_{l}):=\widetilde{v}_{l}(t/{\varepsilon}),

and then

v_{\varepsilon}(x):=\begin{cases}v_{\varepsilon}^{0}(x)\wedge v_{\varepsilon}^{l}(x),&\text{ if }x\in E^{l}_{\varepsilon},\\

We next show that no jumps are introduced on the boundaries of any

. Indeed,

.
If

, and we obtain

.
The monotonicity condition

\text{PF}_{\varepsilon}(v^{\prime}\wedge v^{\prime\prime};A)\leq\text{PF}_{\varepsilon}(v^{\prime};A)+\text{PF}_{\varepsilon}(v^{\prime\prime};A)

leads directly to

(5.43)

\text{PF}_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})\leq\text{PF}_{\varepsilon}(v^{0}_{\varepsilon})+\sum_{l}\text{PF}_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon}^{l};E^{l}_{\varepsilon}).

We now turn to the construction of
\beta_{\varepsilon}
, which is more subtle.
To make the strategy clear we deal with each difficulty separately, defining a sequence of intermediate functions.

In order to rescale and account for the matrices
G_{l}
, we replace the functions
\widetilde{\beta}_{l}
identified in (

) by

(5.44)

\beta^{l}_{\varepsilon}(y+t\nu_{l}):=\begin{cases}G_{l}\widetilde{\beta}_{l}(\frac{t}{{\varepsilon}}),&\text{ if }t\in[0,L{\varepsilon}],\\
\widetilde{\beta}_{l}(\frac{t}{{\varepsilon}}),&\text{ if }t\in[-L{\varepsilon},0).\end{cases}

We can easily use this profile in the inner regions
E^{l}_{\varepsilon}
. It automatically matches
R_{l}^{\pm}
for

, it can jump only in 0,
and since
\widetilde{\beta}_{l}
is continuous the traces differ by a factor
G_{l}
.
The critical point is the interpolation at the lateral boundaries which are contained in
(\partial^{\prime}F_{l})_{M{\varepsilon}}
.
The condition on the jump required by
\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}
is not linear (in the sense that the sum of two
\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}
functions is not necessarily in
\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}
), therefore we cannot interpolate.
However,
\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}
is one-homogeneous, in the sense that
if
f\in\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
and
\psi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})

\{\psi\neq 1\}
is small.

Since
v_{\varepsilon}^{0}=0
in
(\Gamma)_{2M{\varepsilon}}
, we modify the functions
\beta^{l}_{\varepsilon}
by interpolating first with

and then with the identity.
We fix
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};[0,1])
, such that
\varphi(t)=0
for

\varphi(t)=1
for

\psi^{{\mathrm{out}}}_{\varepsilon}(x):=\varphi\left(\frac{{\mathrm{dist}}(x,\Gamma)-M{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\right)\qquad\psi^{\mathrm{in}}_{\varepsilon}(x):=\varphi\left(\frac{M{\varepsilon}-{\mathrm{dist}}(x,\Gamma)}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\right),

We then define

(5.46)

\widehat{\beta}_{\varepsilon}(x):=\begin{cases}\beta^{l}_{\varepsilon}(x),&\text{ if }x\in E^{l}_{\varepsilon},\\
\beta(x),&\text{ if }x\in\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{l}E^{l}_{\varepsilon},\end{cases}

and combining it with the functions above

(5.47)

\beta_{\varepsilon}(x):=\psi^{\mathrm{out}}_{\varepsilon}(x)\widehat{\beta}_{\varepsilon}(x)+\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{in}}(x){\mathrm{Id}},

which may jump only on
\bigcup_{l}F_{l}
, with

\beta_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}=\psi^{\mathrm{out}}_{\varepsilon}\widehat{\beta}_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}
.

We have already shown that
v_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])
; from the energy estimate it will follow

in

. From the construction we also have
\beta_{\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{SBV}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
with

and
\beta_{\varepsilon}^{+}=G_{l}\beta_{\varepsilon}^{-}
on

; in order to prove
\beta_{\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{SBV}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
it only remains to show that

, this will be done in (
5.58
) below. Finally, we notice that, since

, in

\left[\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{l}E^{l}_{\varepsilon}\right]\setminus(\Gamma)_{M{\varepsilon}+\rho_{\varepsilon}}
we have

\beta_{\varepsilon}=\beta
, and therefore
\nabla\beta_{\varepsilon}=0
. As

\|\beta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sqrt{d}
, we immediately obtain
\beta_{\varepsilon}\to\beta
in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{M}^{d\times d})
.

We now estimate the different terms of the energy.
In
(\Gamma)_{2M{\varepsilon}}
we have

v_{\varepsilon}^{0}=0
and therefore

f_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})=0
, so that the term

f^{2}_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})|\nabla\beta_{\varepsilon}|^{2}
vanishes outside
\{\psi^{\mathrm{out}}=1\}\cap\bigcup_{l}E_{l}^{\varepsilon}
.
Using
(
5.43
) leads to

(5.48)

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\beta_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})\leq\sum_{l}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\beta_{\varepsilon}^{l},v^{l}_{\varepsilon};E_{l}^{\varepsilon})+\text{PF}_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon}^{0})+\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{(\Gamma)_{M{\varepsilon}+\rho_{\varepsilon}}}{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{O}(d)){\,\mathrm{d}x}.

We next estimate these three terms, starting with
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\beta_{\varepsilon}^{l},v^{l}_{\varepsilon};E^{l}_{\varepsilon})
, for a fixed

.

We denote by
\widehat{\lambda}
either
\lambda<+\infty
or
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
in the case
\lambda=+\infty

Recalling that

\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta_{\varepsilon}^{l}(y+t\nu_{l}),\mathrm{O}(d))\leq\frac{\widehat{\lambda}}{{\varepsilon}}{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta_{\varepsilon}^{l}(y+t\nu_{l}),\mathrm{O}(d))+\frac{2d}{{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{{\varepsilon}}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}-\widehat{\lambda}\right)_{+}.

Using (
5.49

(5.50)

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\beta_{\varepsilon}^{l},v^{l}_{\varepsilon};E^{l}_{\varepsilon})\leq&\int_{\{y\in F_{l}:d_{l}(y)>c_{*}L{\varepsilon}\}}\int_{-L}^{L}\Bigl[\frac{f_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\widetilde{v}_{l})}{{\varepsilon}}|\widetilde{\beta}^{\prime}_{l}|^{2}+\widehat{\lambda}\,{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\widetilde{\beta}_{l},\mathrm{O}(d))\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt+2d\left(\frac{{\varepsilon}}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}-\widehat{\lambda}\right)_{+}+\frac{(1-\widetilde{v}_{l})^{2}}{4}+|\widetilde{v}^{\prime}_{l}|^{2}\Bigr]{\,\mathrm{d}s}{\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}}(y).\end{split}

Estimating

,
and then using (

) and (

) (in the case

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\beta_{\varepsilon}^{l},v^{l}_{\varepsilon};E^{l}_{\varepsilon})\leq&\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(F_{l})\left[g_{\lambda}(R^{-}_{l},R^{+}_{l})+4dL\left(\frac{{\varepsilon}}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}-\widehat{\lambda}\right)_{+}+\omega(L)+\omega(\lambda_{\varepsilon})\right],\end{split}

with the term

only present if

.
For later reference, we note that a similar but simpler computation, using
again (

) and (

),
leads to

\begin{split}\|\nabla\beta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(E^{l}_{\varepsilon})}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(F_{l})\|\widetilde{\beta}^{\prime}_{l}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\leq C(L)\frac{1+\widehat{\lambda}}{{\varepsilon}}.\end{split}

The second term in (

) was already treated in (

).
In the third term we use the bound

and the fact that

\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{(\Gamma)_{M{\varepsilon}+\rho_{\varepsilon}}}{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{O}(d)){\,\mathrm{d}x}\leq\frac{C}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}|(\Gamma)_{M{\varepsilon}+\rho_{\varepsilon}}\setminus(\Gamma)_{M{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\varepsilon}}|\leq C\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon}L{\varepsilon}}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}.

We are now ready to conclude the estimate of the energy.

We first consider the case
\lambda<+\infty
, taking the limit as
{\varepsilon}\to 0
first with
L
fixed.
By (
5.53
)
if we choose
\rho_{\varepsilon}
such that

(5.54)

\lim_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}=0,

then

(5.55)

\limsup_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{l}E^{l}_{\varepsilon}}{\mathrm{dist}}^{2}(\beta_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{O}(d)){\,\mathrm{d}x}=0.

Starting from (
5.48
), taking the limit as
{\varepsilon}\to 0
, using
(
5.51
), (
5.37
), and
(
5.55
) leads to

\limsup_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\beta_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})\leq\sum_{l}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(F_{l})\left(g_{\lambda}(R^{-}_{l},R^{+}_{l})+\omega(L)\right),

so that

\limsup_{L\to\infty}\limsup_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\beta_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})\leq\sum_{l}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(F_{l})g_{\lambda}(R^{-}_{l},R^{+}_{l})=\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}(\beta,1),

and the conclusion follows from a diagonal argument.

It remains to address
the
L^{2}
estimate on
\nabla\beta_{\varepsilon}
.
We recall the definitions in (
5.47
) and (
5.45
), as well as
the bound
|\widehat{\beta}_{\varepsilon}|\leq\sqrt{d}
, and write the pointwise estimate

(5.56)

|\nabla\beta_{\varepsilon}|\leq\sqrt{d}(|\nabla\psi^{\mathrm{out}}_{\varepsilon}|+|\nabla\psi^{\mathrm{in}}_{\varepsilon}|)+|\psi^{\mathrm{out}}_{\varepsilon}\nabla\widehat{\beta}_{\varepsilon}|.

We treat the two terms separately. First,

(5.57)

\|\nabla\psi^{\mathrm{out}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla\psi^{\mathrm{in}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\frac{C}{\rho_{\varepsilon}^{2}}|(\Gamma)_{M{\varepsilon}+\rho_{\varepsilon}}\setminus(\Gamma)_{M{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\varepsilon}}|\leq C\frac{M{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}},

where we used that
\Gamma
is a finite union of
N-2
dimensional polyhedra.
For the second one, from (
5.46
) and
\nabla\beta=0
we see that we can restrict to
\bigcup_{l}E^{l}_{\varepsilon}
, that were already treated.
Recalling
(
5.56
),
(
5.52
), and
(
5.57
),

(5.58)

\|\nabla\beta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\left(\frac{L{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}+C(L)\frac{1+\widehat{\lambda}}{{\varepsilon}}\right).

It remains to choose
\rho_{\varepsilon}
.
We recall (
5.54
) and see that we need to choose
\rho_{\varepsilon}\to 0
,
0<\rho_{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}
, such that

(5.59)

\lim_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}=0\text{ and }\lim_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\eta_{\varepsilon}\left[\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}+\frac{{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\right]=0.

We fix
\rho_{\varepsilon}:={\varepsilon}
and see that it obeys all requirements provided that

(5.60)

\lim_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\frac{\eta_{\varepsilon}}{{\varepsilon}}=0,

as in the assumptions.

With this choice of
\eta_{\varepsilon}
we then obtain that

(5.61)

\limsup_{L\to+\infty}\limsup_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\eta_{\varepsilon}\|\nabla\beta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=0

so that with a diagonal argument we conclude the proof in the case
\lambda<+\infty
.

We now turn to the case
\lambda=+\infty
. We fix a sequence
L_{\varepsilon}\to+\infty
chosen below, that will depend on the sequence
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
defined in (
5.39
).

Now starting from (
5.48
), collecting
(
5.51
), (
5.37
), (
5.53
) leads to

(5.62)

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\beta_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})\leq\sum_{l}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(F_{l})\left[g_{\infty}(R^{-}_{l},R^{+}_{l})+\omega_{\varepsilon}\right]+CL_{\varepsilon}^{2}{\varepsilon}+C\,\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon}L_{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}

where
\omega_{\varepsilon}=\omega(L_{\varepsilon})+\omega(\lambda_{\varepsilon})
.
Then choosing
L_{\varepsilon}\to\infty
so that
L_{\varepsilon}^{2}{\varepsilon}\to 0
as
{\varepsilon}\to 0
, and recalling (
5.54
), we conclude that

\limsup_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\beta_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})\leq\sum_{l}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(F_{l})g_{\infty}(R^{-}_{l},R^{+}_{l})=\mathcal{F}^{\infty}(\beta,1).

In order to obtain the estimate of the gradients, in view of (
5.58
) it is enough to replace the conditions (
5.59
)
with

(5.63)

\lim_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon}L_{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}=0\text{ and }\lim_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\eta_{\varepsilon}\left[C(L_{\varepsilon})\frac{\lambda_{\varepsilon}}{{\varepsilon}}+\frac{L_{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\right]=0.

We fix
\rho_{\varepsilon}:=\delta_{\varepsilon}\wedge{\varepsilon}
, so that the first condition in (
5.63
) holds. We can choose
L_{\varepsilon}\to\infty
such that the second one holds if and only if

(5.64)

\lim_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\eta_{\varepsilon}\frac{\lambda_{\varepsilon}}{{\varepsilon}}=\lim_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\frac{\eta_{\varepsilon}}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}=0,\text{ and }\lim_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\eta_{\varepsilon}\frac{{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}=0.

∎

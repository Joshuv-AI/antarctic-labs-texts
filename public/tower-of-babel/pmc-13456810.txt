Totally Extraperitoneal Endoscope-assisted Deep Inferior Epigastric Perforator Flap Harvest Using an Optical Trocar
Authors: Ryohei Katsuragi, Keigo Hayashi, Norie Abe, Yuya Yamamoto, Maho Okiyama, Hisamitsu Zaha, Toshihiko Satake
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Summary:Endoscope-assisted deep inferior epigastric perforator (E-DIEP) flap harvesting enables pedicle dissection through a shorter anterior rectus sheath incision. This can be performed using either a transabdominal preperitoneal (TAPP) or a totally extraperitoneal (TEP) approach. The TEP approach is less invasive because it avoids transperitoneal entry. However, conventional techniques for creating the retrorectus space, typically by blind finger dissection or balloon dilation, carry the risks of peritoneal breach, inadvertent vascular injury with unexpected bleeding, and loss of stable visualization. We developed a simple optical trocar–guided technique to establish a safe retrorectus space for E-DIEP pedicle dissection. In this approach, an optical trocar assembled from a 5 × 150 mm trocar and a 0-degree rigid laparoscope was inserted under direct visualization, allowing controlled entry into the retrorectus plane, followed by blunt dissection to create the working space. We retrospectively reviewed 10 consecutive patients treated with this technique to assess its feasibility, intraoperative peritoneal breach, conversion to the TAPP approach, and abdominal wall complications. In all cases, the retrorectus space was reproducibly created under stable visualization, with a mean creation time of only 3.9 minutes, and pedicle dissection was performed entirely within the extraperitoneal plane. None of the patients required conversion to the TAPP approach. No abdominal wall hernias or clinically evident bulges occurred during a median follow-up of 6.4 months. In this small case series, optical trocar–guided entry into the retrorectus space was considered a feasible approach for establishing a TEP working space during E-DIEP pedicle dissection.

Takeaways

Question: Can optical trocar–guided entry provide a safer and more reproducible method for creating the retrorectus working space during totally extraperitoneal endoscope-assisted deep inferior epigastric perforator pedicle harvest?

Findings: In 10 consecutive patients, optical trocar–guided entry enabled rapid (mean 3.9 min), controlled retrorectus space creation under direct visualization without peritoneal breach or conversion to the transabdominal preperitoneal approach.

Meaning: This simple modification may improve the safety, reproducibility, and accessibility of totally extraperitoneal access for minimally invasive deep inferior epigastric perforator pedicle harvest.

INTRODUCTION

The deep inferior epigastric perforator (DIEP) flap is the gold standard for autologous breast reconstruction.1 However, DIEP flap harvest requires a relatively long fascial incision, which may increase the risk of abdominal bulge or hernia.2 Minimally invasive approaches for DIEP pedicle harvest, including transabdominal preperitoneal (TAPP) and totally extraperitoneal (TEP) techniques, have been introduced to reduce donor-site morbidity.3–6 TAPP requires intraperitoneal access and pneumoperitoneum, whereas TEP avoids the peritoneal cavity.

When performing endoscope-assisted DIEP (E-DIEP) harvesting using the TEP approach, a wide retrorectus working space is required to avoid instrument collisions. To facilitate safe and efficient space creation, we used a standard trocar and a rigid laparoscope to develop the retrorectus space under direct visualization. Here, we describe this optical trocar–guided TEP technique and report outcomes in 10 consecutive patients.

Surgical Technique

Initial Open Dissection

A lower abdominal incision was made according to the DIEP flap design, and dissection was performed to expose the anterior rectus sheath. A small fascial incision was made around the selected perforator, and the intramuscular DIEP pedicle was dissected under direct vision. The fascial incision was temporarily closed to reduce air leakage during endoscopic dissection.

Optical Trocar Entry and Retrorectus Space Creation

The pedicle-harvest side was defined as the open side, and the contralateral side as the endoscopic side. A 0-degree rigid laparoscope with an HD 3CMOS camera head (WAIR500A; Olympus Medical Systems Corp., Tokyo, Japan) was mounted on a 5 × 150 mm Kii Fios first entry access system (CTF01; Applied Medical, Rancho Santa Margarita, CA) to create an optical trocar system (Fig. 1).

Optical trocar system. A 0-degree rigid laparoscope (WAIR500A; Olympus Medical Systems Corp) coupled to an HD 3CMOS camera head (CH-S200; Olympus Medical Systems Corp) is inserted into a 5 × 150 mm Kii Fios first entry access system (CTF03; Applied Medical) to create an optical trocar for retrorectus dissection.

On the endoscopic side, a 2-mm transverse incision was made in the anterior sheath at the umbilical level over the rectal midportion. An optical trocar was introduced vertically while tenting the sheath with forceps (Fig. 2). The rectus fibers and intercostal nerves were visualized, and the trocar was advanced into the posterior sheath under direct vision, with care taken to avoid nerve injury. The trocar was then angled horizontally toward the pubic symphysis (Fig. 3) and advanced along the anterior surface of the posterior sheath under endoscopic visualization to develop the retrorectus space. CO2 was insufflated to 8 mm Hg to facilitate dissection. The trocar tip was gently swept from side to side (Fig. 4), bluntly enlarging the space cranially and caudally along the posterior sheath. The caudal dissection was extended to the superior pubic border, where the thin linea alba was bluntly separated.

Optical trocar entry. The optical trocar is introduced vertically through an anterior rectus sheath incision.

Retrorectus space creation (craniocaudal direction). After identifying the posterior rectus sheath, the optical trocar is angled horizontally and advanced caudally toward the pubis along the sheath surface.

Retrorectus space creation (horizontal direction). Gentle side-to-side sweeping under endoscopic visualization enables atraumatic blunt dissection with minimal bleeding, creating an adequate retrorectus working space for second-port placement.

A second working port (CFS02; Applied Medical) was placed lateral to the rectus muscle on the endoscopic side, and the remaining fibrous bands and vessels were divided using an ultrasonic device to complete the retrorectus space. The retrorectus spaces were then connected by carefully dividing the linea alba near the arcuate line. Excessively ventral division may damage the anterior rectus sheath and cause air leakage, whereas an overly deep division may risk peritoneal entry or bowel injury; safe division was guided by identifying fatty tissue behind the linea alba. After both sides were joined, pedicle dissection was performed endoscopically. (See Video 1 [online], which displays the insertion of the optical trocar and creation of the retrorectus space.)

Clinical Outcomes

The clinical outcomes of 10 consecutive patients with a body mass index (BMI) of less than 30 kg/m2 and no history of abdominal surgery, selected during the initial adoption phase of this technique, were evaluated. No intraoperative peritoneal breaches or conversion to the TAPP approach were observed. All flaps survived completely, and no early flap-related complications, including vascular compromise requiring reexploration, hematoma, seroma, or surgical site infection, were observed. At a median follow-up of 6.4 months, no abdominal bulge or hernia was observed, and no patient reported notable discomfort attributable to CO2 insufflation. DISCUSSION

Creating a reliable retrorectus working space is a critical step in TEP-DIEP pedicle harvesting. In our technique, optical trocar–guided entry allowed for real-time visualization of the correct plane and controlled retrorectus dissection. In this small series, the approach enabled stable working space creation without peritoneal breach or conversion to the TAPP, although its effect on reducing access-related complications could not be quantified owing to the lack of a comparative group. Published reports on E-DIEP flap harvesting remain limited, and detailed descriptions of entry port strategies are scarce. Shakir et al5 used blunt dissection with a camera tip, which may have caused lens contamination, whereas Hivelin et al6 used a balloon dissector, which may have obscured the field if small vessels were torn. However, because previous reports did not provide detailed data on access-related complications, a direct quantitative comparison with our technique was not possible.

Another technical consideration is the choice of contralateral access for optical trocar insertion. We also explored an ipsilateral-only approach in other cases; however, a single retrorectus space provided an insufficient working room, and instrument interference made it challenging to continue the procedure. Although further optimization of port placement may improve its feasibility, we currently consider crossover through the contralateral retrorectus space important for securing an adequate workspace for the TEP approach.

In practice, the additional cost of this technique seems modest. Compared with conventional DIEP flap harvesting, the additional expense is mainly limited to disposable port devices and is estimated at approximately ¥29,400 (approximately US $185). In contrast to a balloon dissector, which is used solely for creating the retrorectus space, the optical trocar can serve as the first port, potentially improving cost efficiency.

During the initial adoption phase, we limited this approach to patients with a BMI of less than 30 kg/m2 and without a history of abdominal surgery as part of a cautious case-selection strategy during the learning curve. This reflected our intention to minimize the risk of deep venous thrombosis in patients with a higher BMI and avoid technically complex cases associated with prior abdominal surgery. However, as our experience with this technique has increased, we anticipate expanding its application beyond these initial selection criteria in future cases.

CONCLUSIONS

Optical trocar–guided entry into the retrorectus space is a simple and feasible modification for establishing TEP access during E-DIEP pedicle harvesting.

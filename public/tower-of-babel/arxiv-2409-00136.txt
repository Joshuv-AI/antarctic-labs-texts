Fundamental Solution for a New Class of Non-Archimedean Pseudo-Differential Equations
Anatoly N. Kochubei, Mariia V. Serdiuk

Abstract




1
Introduction

In the analysis of real- and complex-valued functions over a non-Archimedean field of
p
-adic numbers
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and the spaces
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}
, a typical linear operator is the pseudo-differential one,

(Au)(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[a(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})(\mathcal{F}u)(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})\right](x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),\;x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p},

where
\mathcal{F}
is the Fourier transform. Under some conditions upon the symbol, the Fourier transform can be eliminated, and we get a representation of
A
as a hypersingular integral operator.

The example, which has been investigated in the greatest detail, is the Vladimirov-Taibleson operator
D^{\alpha,n}
, for which

a(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})=\left(\max\left\{|\xi_{1}|_{p},\dots,|\xi_{n}|_{p}\right\}\right)^{\alpha},\;\alpha>0,

and which can be seen as a kind of an elliptic operator. See
[
7
,
6
]
; see also
[
8
,
2
]
for equations with various related operators, such as analogs of the nonstationary Schrödinger equation, the Klein-Gordon equation, the operator with the symbol
\max\left\{|\xi|_{p}^{d_{1}},|\xi|_{p}^{d_{2}}\right\}^{\alpha}
, and others.

Note that the maximum signs in the above symbols correspond to the ultrametric geometry of the non-Archimedean spaces
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}
. See Section 2 for the details.

Interesting and non-trivial equations of this theory are not necessarily formulated directly in terms of the above operators. Thus,
p
-adic parabolic equations are those with a real positive time variable and
p
-adic spatial variables; this class of equations is important for probabilistic applications. A kind of
p
-adic wave equation
[
3
]
has the form
D_{t}^{\alpha}F-D_{x}^{\alpha,n}F=0
.

In this paper, we consider a more general equation

D_{t}^{\alpha}u(t,x)-D_{x}^{\beta,n}u(t,x)=0,\;\beta=K\alpha\mbox{ for some }K\in\mathbb{N}.

Using some techniques from
[
3
]
, especially the idea of a radial time variable (see also
[
4
]
), as well as the Lizorkin classes of test functions and distributions, we prove the existence and uniqueness results for the Cauchy problem, including the finite dependence property (resembling classical hyperbolic equations) and an
L^{1}
-estimate of the solution.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
The Field of
p
-adic Numbers

Let
p
be a prime number. The
p
-adic norm on the field
\mathbb{Q}
of rational numbers is defined as

|0|_{p}=0,\;|x|_{p}=p^{-\gamma(x)},

where the integer
\gamma(x)
is obtained from the decomposition

x=p^{\gamma}\frac{m}{n},\;m,n,\gamma=\gamma(x)\in\mathbb{Z},

and the integers
m
,
n
are prime to
p
. This norm is non-Archimedean and satisfies the strong triangle inequality
|x+y|_{p}\leq\max\left(|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\right)
. The completion of the field
\mathbb{Q}
with respect to the
p
-adic norm is called the field of
p
-adic numbers
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

Any
p
-adic number
x\neq 0
has a unique canonical representation

x=p^{\gamma}(x_{0}+x_{1}p+x_{2}p^{2}+\dots),

(2.1)

where
\gamma=\gamma(x)\in\mathbb{Z}
,
x_{j}
are integers such that
0\leq x_{j}\leq p-1
,
x_{0}>0
,
j=0,1,\dots
, and
|x|_{p}=p^{-\gamma}
.

The fractional part of a
p
-adic number
x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with the canonical representation (
2.1
) is defined as

\{x\}_{p}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\mbox{if }\gamma(x)\geq 0\mbox{ or }x=0;\\
p^{\gamma}(x_{0}+x_{1}p+\dots+x_{|\gamma|-1}p^{|\gamma-1|}),&\mbox{if }\gamma(x)<0.\\
\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

A function
\varphi:\mathbb{Q}_{p}\to\mathbb{C}
is called
locally constant
if there exists such an integer
\ell
that for any
x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}

\varphi(x+x^{\prime})=\varphi(x),\mbox{ whenever }|x^{\prime}|_{p}\leq p^{-\ell}.

The smallest of such numbers
\ell
is called the exponent of local constancy of the function
\varphi
.

We denote by
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
the linear topological space of all locally constant functions
\varphi:\mathbb{Q}_{p}\to\mathbb{C}
with compact supports. The strong conjugate space
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is called the space of Bruhat-Schwartz distributions (see
[
1
]
).

2.2
Fourier Transform

Let
\chi_{p}(\xi x)=\exp(2\pi i\{\xi x\}_{p})
, where
\xi\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, be an additive character of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. The Fourier transform of a test function
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is defined as

\widetilde{\varphi}(\xi)=\mathcal{F}_{x\to\xi}[\varphi](\xi)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\chi_{p}(\xi x)\varphi(x)dx,\;\xi\in\mathbb{Q}_{p},

where the integration is with respect to the standard Haar measure
dx
on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, i.e. the invariant under shifts positive measure
dx
such that
\int_{|x|_{p}\leq 1}=1
.

For
n\geq 1
, the Fourier transform of a test function
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
is defined as

\widetilde{\varphi}(\xi)=\mathcal{F}_{x\to\xi}[\varphi](\xi)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}}\chi_{p}(\xi\cdot x)\varphi(x)dx,\;\xi\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n},

where
\chi_{p}(\xi\cdot x)=\chi_{p}(\xi_{1}x_{1})\cdots\chi_{p}(\xi_{n}x_{n})=\exp\left(2\pi i\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\{\xi_{j}x_{j}\}_{p}\right)
,
\xi\cdot x
is the scalar product of vectors
\xi=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})
and
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
, and the function
\chi_{p}(\xi_{j}x_{j})=\exp\left(2\pi i\{\xi_{j}x_{j}\}_{p}\right)
for every fixed
\xi_{j}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is an additive character of the field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
,
\{\xi_{j}x_{j}\}_{p}
is the fractional part of a number
\xi_{j}x_{j}
defined by (
2.2
),
j=1,\dots,n
.

The Fourier transform
\varphi\mapsto\widetilde{\varphi}
is a linear isomorphism from
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
on
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and the inversion formula holds

\varphi(x)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\chi_{p}(-x\xi)\widetilde{\varphi}(\xi)d\xi=\mathcal{F}_{\xi\to x}\left[\widetilde{\varphi}(-x)\right]=\mathcal{F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}\left[\widetilde{\varphi}\right](x),\;x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p},\;\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}).

For a distribution
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
its Fourier transform is defined by the formula

(\widetilde{f},\varphi)=(f,\widetilde{\varphi}),\;\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),

and the inversion formula holds

f=\mathcal{F}\left[\widetilde{f}(-\xi)\right],\;f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}).

Similarly, for
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
,

(\widetilde{f},\varphi)=(f,\widetilde{\varphi}),\;\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}).

We will use the following subspaces of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
,

\Psi(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})=\{\psi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}):\;\psi(0)=0\},

\Phi(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})=\bigg\{\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}):\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}}\varphi(x)dx=0\;\bigg\},

introduced in
[
1
]
. The space
\Phi(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
is called the Lizorkin space of test functions of the second kind. The conjugate space
\Phi^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
is called the Lizorkin space of distributions of the second kind. The most important property of these spaces is that the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}
is a linear isomorphism from
\Psi(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
onto
\Phi(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
, thus also from
\Phi^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
onto
\Psi^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
. At the same time,
\mathcal{F}
can be considered as a linear isomorphism from
\Phi(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
to
\Psi(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
.

2.3
Operator
D^{\alpha}

On a test function
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, the fractional differentiation operator
D^{\alpha}
,
\alpha>0
, is defined as (see
[
7
]
)

(D^{\alpha}\varphi)(x)=\mathcal{F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}\big[|\xi|_{p}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{F}_{y\to\xi}(\varphi))(\xi)\big](x).

(2.3)

For
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}
, define
|x|_{p}=\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}|x_{j}|_{p}
. The operator
D^{\alpha,n}
on a function
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
is given by the expression

(D^{\alpha,n}\varphi)(x)=\mathcal{F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}\big[|\xi|_{p}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{F}_{y\to\xi}(\varphi))(\xi)\big](x).

The operator
D^{\alpha}
can also be represented as a hypersingular integral operator. For a function
u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
,

(D^{\alpha}u)(x)=\frac{1-p^{\alpha}}{1-p^{-\alpha-1}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}|y|_{p}^{-\alpha-1}\left[u(x-y)-u(x)\right]dy.

(2.4)

Similarly, if
u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
, then

(D^{\alpha,n}u)(x)=\frac{1-p^{\alpha}}{1-p^{-\alpha-n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}}|y|_{p}^{-\alpha-n}\left[u(x-y)-u(x)\right]dy.

Further, we shall use the following integration formulas
[
7
]
:

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{\gamma}^{n}}d^{n}x=p^{n\gamma};\;\int\limits_{S_{\gamma}^{n}}dx=\left(1-p^{-n}\right)p^{n\gamma},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{\gamma}^{n}}\chi_{p}(\xi x)dx=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p^{n\gamma},&|\xi|_{p}\leq p^{-\gamma};\\
0,&|\xi|_{p}\geq p^{-\gamma+1}.\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\int\limits_{S_{\gamma}^{n}}\chi_{p}(\xi x)dx=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p^{n\gamma}(1-p^{-n}),&|\xi|_{p}\leq p^{-\gamma};\\
-p^{n(\gamma-1)},&|\xi|_{p}=p^{-\gamma+1};\\
0,&|\xi|_{p}\geq p^{-\gamma+2}.\end{array}\right.

where we denote by
B_{\gamma}^{n}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}:\;|x|_{p}\leq p^{\gamma}\}
,
S_{\gamma}^{n}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}:\;|x|_{p}=p^{\gamma}\}
.

3
Radial Eigenfunctions

Let
\alpha>0
,
\beta>0
. Consider the eigenvalue problem

D^{\alpha}u=\lambda{u},\;\lambda=p^{\beta N},\;N\in\mathbb{Z},

(3.1)

where
u:\mathbb{Q}_{p}\to\mathbb{C}
is not identically zero.

We also suppose that

\beta=K\alpha\mbox{ for some }K\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.2)

Proposition 3.1
.

If the condition (
3.2
) holds, the equation (
3.1
) has the set of solutions in
\Phi(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
of the following form for
N\in\mathbb{Z}
:

u_{N}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C_{N}p^{KN}(1-\dfrac{1}{p}),&|t|_{p}\leq p^{-KN};\\
-C_{N}p^{KN-1},&|t|_{p}=p^{-KN+1};\\
0,&|t|_{p}\geq p^{-KN+2}.\end{array}\right.

(3.3)

Proof.

We apply the Fourier transform with respect to the both sides of the equation (
3.1
) and obtain

(|\eta|_{p}^{\alpha}-p^{\beta N})(\mathcal{F}_{t\to\eta}u)(\eta)=0,\mbox{for all }\eta\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}.

(3.4)

Denote
\widetilde{u}(\eta)=(\mathcal{F}_{t\to\eta}u)(\eta)
,
(\eta)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. It follows from (
3.4
) that the equality
\widetilde{u}(\eta)\equiv 0
will not hold only if the condition
|\eta|_{p}^{\alpha}=p^{\beta N}
holds, i.e.
|\eta|_{p}=p^{KN}
, where
K\in\mathbb{N}
is such that
\beta=K\alpha
. This condition holds by the assumption (
3.2
). (Note that if (
3.2
) does not hold, then the equation (
3.1
) has only a zero solution.)

Since
u
is a radial function with respect to
t
, then
\widetilde{u}
is also a radial function with respect to
\eta
(see
[
5
]
formula (1.28), p.15). Therefore

\widetilde{u}_{N}(|\eta|_{p})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C_{N},&|\eta|=p^{KN};\\
0,&|\eta|\neq p^{KN},\end{array}\right.\;C_{N}\neq 0.

(3.5)

By the Fourier inversion formula and the well-known integration formula (
2.3
), we have

\begin{split}u_{N}(|t|_{p})=(\mathcal{F}_{\eta\to t}^{-1}\widetilde{u}_{N})(t)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\chi_{p}(-t\eta)\widetilde{u}_{N}(\eta)d\eta=\int\limits_{S_{KN}}\chi_{p}(-t\eta)C_{N}d\eta=\\
=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C_{N}(\xi)p^{KN}(1-\dfrac{1}{p}),&|t|_{p}\leq p^{-KN};\\
-C_{N}(\xi)p^{KN-1},&|t|_{p}=p^{-KN+1};\\
0,&|t|_{p}\geq p^{-KN+2}.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(3.6)

It is easy to verify from (
3.6
) that
u_{N}\in\Phi(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
for each
N\in\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

If a radial distribution
u\in\Phi^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
satisfies the equation (
3.1
), then it coincides, for some
C_{N}\in\mathbb{C}
, with the function (
3.3
).

Proof.

The proof is identical to the one given for Proposition 1 in
[
3
]
.
∎

4
Cauchy Problem

Let
\alpha>0
,
\beta>0
,
n\geq 1
. We consider the Cauchy problem

D_{|t|_{p}}^{\alpha}u(|t|_{p},x)-D_{x}^{\beta,n}u(|t|_{p},x)=0,\;(t,x)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}\times\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n},

(4.1)

u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\;x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n},

(4.2)

where
u:\mathbb{Q}_{p}\times\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
is a radial function with respect to
t
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
\beta>0
such that the condition (
3.2
) holds. Suppose that the function
u_{0}
is in
\Phi(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
. Then the Cauchy problem (
4.1
)-(
4.2
) has a solution
u=u(|t|_{p},x)
, radial in
t
, that belongs to the space
\Phi(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
for each
x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}
, and belongs to
\Phi(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
for each
t\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

If the condition (
3.2
) does not hold, then the equation (
4.1
) has only a zero solution
u(t,x)\equiv 0
,
(t,x)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}\times\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
u_{0}\in\Phi(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
. We will look for a solution belonging to
\Phi(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and radial in
t
, for each
x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}
, and belonging to
\Phi(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
in
x
, for each
t\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

Let us consider the Cauchy problem (
4.1
)-(
4.2
). We take the Fourier transform with respect to
x
on both sides of (
4.1
) and obtain

\mathcal{F}_{x\to\xi}D^{\alpha}_{|t|_{p}}u(|t|_{p},\xi)-|\xi|_{p}^{\beta}\mathcal{F}_{x\to\xi}u(|t|_{p},\xi)=0,\;(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}\times\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}.

(4.3)

Suppose that the function
v(|t|_{p},\xi)
,
(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}\times\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}
is a solution of the equation (
4.3
). Then the function
u=\mathcal{F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}v
is the solution of (
4.1
). Hence we will look for the solution of (
4.3
).

We denote
\hat{u}(|t|_{p},\xi)\coloneqq\mathcal{F}_{x\to\xi}u(|t|_{p},\xi)
,
(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}\times\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. The equation (
4.3
) is now equivalent to

D_{|t|_{p}}^{\alpha}\hat{u}(|t|_{p},\xi)=|\xi|_{p}^{\beta}\hat{u}(|t|_{p},\xi),\;(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}\times\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}.

(4.4)

The problem (
4.4
) is the radial eigenfunction problem of the operator
D_{|t|_{p}}^{\alpha}
. We denote
|\xi|_{p}=p^{N}
,
N\in\mathbb{Z}
. It follows from Proposition
3.1
that the solutions of the equation (
4.4
) have the form

\hat{u}_{N}(|t|_{p},\xi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C_{N}(\xi)p^{KN}(1-\dfrac{1}{p}),&|t|_{p}\leq p^{-KN};\\
-C_{N}(\xi)p^{KN-1},&|t|_{p}=p^{-KN+1};\\
0,&|t|_{p}\geq p^{-KN+2}.\end{array}\right.

(4.5)

It follows from the initial condition (
4.2
) that

\hat{u}_{N}(0,\xi)=\hat{u}_{0}(\xi),\;\xi\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}.

(4.6)

By substituting (
4.5
) into (
4.6
), we get

\hat{u}_{N}(0,\xi)=C_{N}(\xi)p^{KN}(1-\frac{1}{p})=\hat{u}_{0}(\xi),\;\xi\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}.

Hence

C_{N}(\xi)=p^{-KN}(1-\frac{1}{p})^{-1}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi),\;\xi\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}.

Finally, we obtain the following solution of the equation (
4.4
)

\hat{u}_{N}(|t|_{p},\xi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi),&|t|_{p}\leq p^{-KN};\\
-\dfrac{1}{p-1}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi),&|t|_{p}=p^{-KN+1};\\
0,&|t|_{p}\geq p^{-KN+2},\end{array}\right.\;(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}\times\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n},\;|\xi|_{p}=p^{N}.

(4.7)

Denote

b_{N}(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&|t|_{p}\leq p^{-KN};\\
-\dfrac{1}{p-1},&|t|_{p}=p^{-KN+1};\\
0,&|t|_{p}\geq p^{-KN+2},\end{array}\right.\;(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}\times\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n},\;|\xi|_{p}=p^{N}.

(4.8)

or, equivalently,

b_{N}(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&|t|_{p}\leq|\xi|_{p}^{-K};\\
-\dfrac{1}{p-1},&|t|_{p}=p|\xi|_{p}^{-K};\\
0,&|t|_{p}\geq p^{2}|\xi|_{p}^{-K},\end{array}\right.\;(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}\times\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n},\;|\xi|_{p}=p^{N}.

(4.9)

Since we defined the functions
\widehat{u}_{N}
and
b_{N}
on each sphere
S_{N}^{n}
, we can define
\hat{u}
and
b
on the whole space
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}
:

\hat{u}(|t|_{p},\xi)\restriction_{S_{N}^{n}}=b_{N}(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})\hat{u}_{0}(\xi),\;t\in\mathbb{Q}_{p},\xi\in S_{N},

(4.10)

b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})\restriction_{S_{N}^{n}}=b_{N}(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p}),\;t\in\mathbb{Q}_{p},\xi\in S_{N}.

(4.11)

Since
\hat{u_{0}}\in\Psi(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
,
\hat{u}(0)=0
, and it follows from local constancy of
\hat{u}
that it vanishes on some neighbourhood of the origin. It follows from (
4.10
) that
\hat{u}\in\Psi(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
in
\xi
, so that
u\in\Phi(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
in
x
. In addition, the functions
u,\hat{u}
belong to
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
uniformly in the sense of support and local constancy with respect to
x
. Therefore, the interchanging of operations is permitted (in particular, the order of applying the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{\xi\to x}
and the operator
D_{|t|_{p}}^{\alpha}
in the equation (
4.3
) can be reversed).

Using the inverse Fourier transform, we obtain the following solution of the equation (
4.1
)

u(|t|_{p},x)=(\mathcal{F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}\hat{u})(|t|_{p},x)=\left((\mathcal{F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}b)\ast u_{0}\right)(|t|_{p},x),\;(t,x)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}\times\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n},

(4.12)

which belongs to
\Phi(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
with respect to
x
for each
t\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and satisfies the initial condition (
4.2
).

It is clear from Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
that if the condition (
3.2
) does not hold, than the equation (
4.1
) has only a zero solution, and if
u_{0}
is not identically zero, than the Cauchy problem (
4.1
)-(
4.2
) has no solutions.

∎

5
Explicit Formula for the Solution

Let us calculate the explicit expression for the function
(\mathcal{F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}b)
. We have

(\mathcal{F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}b)(|t|_{p},|x|_{p})=\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}}\chi_{p}(-\xi\cdot x)b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})d\xi=\sum\limits_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}b(|t|_{p},p^{j})\int\limits_{S_{j}^{n}}\chi_{p}(-\xi\cdot x)d\xi.

(5.1)

Denoting
|x|_{p}=p^{M}
and using the formula (
2.3
), we have

\begin{split}&(\mathcal{F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}b)(|t|_{p},|x|_{p})=(1-p^{-n})\sum\limits_{j=-\infty}^{-M}b(|t|_{p},p^{j})p^{jn}-b(|t|_{p},p^{-M+1})p^{-Mn}=\\
&=(1-p^{-n})\sum\limits_{j=0}^{+\infty}b(|t|_{p},p^{-j-M})p^{(-j-M)n}-b(|t|_{p},p^{-M+1})p^{-Mn}=\\
&=(1-p^{-n})|x|_{p}^{-n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{+\infty}p^{-jn}b(|t|_{p},p^{-j}|x|_{p}^{-1})-|x|_{p}^{-n}b(|t|_{p},p|x|_{p}^{-1}).\end{split}

(5.2)

Denoting
|t|_{p}=p^{L}
,
|x|_{p}=p^{M}
,
L,M\in\mathbb{Z}
, (
5.2
) is now equivalent to

\mathcal{F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}b(p^{L},p^{M})=(1-p^{-n})p^{-Mn}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{+\infty}p^{-jn}b(p^{L},p^{-M-j})-p^{-Mn}b(p^{L},p^{-M+1}).

(5.3)

For
j\geq 0
from (
4.8
) we have

b(p^{L},p^{-M-j})=b_{-M-j}(p^{L},p^{-M-j})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&p^{L}\leq p^{K(M+j)};\\
-\dfrac{1}{p-1},&p^{L}=p^{K(M+j)+1};\\
0,&p^{L}\geq p^{K(M+j)+2},\end{array}\right.

(5.4)

or, equivalently,

b(|t|_{p},p^{-j}|x|_{p}^{-1})=b(|t|_{p},|p^{j}x^{-1}|_{p})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&|t|_{p}\leq p^{jK}|x|_{p}^{K};\\
-\dfrac{1}{p-1},&|t|_{p}=p^{jK+1}|x|_{p}^{K};\\
0,&|t|_{p}\geq p^{jK+2}|x|_{p}^{K},\end{array}\right.

(5.5)

b(p^{L},p^{-M+1})=b_{-M+1}(p^{L},p^{-M+1})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&p^{L}\leq p^{K(M-1)};\\

(5.6)

-\dfrac{1}{p-1},&|t|_{p}=p^{-K+1}|x|_{p}^{K};\\

(5.11)

\dfrac{L-1}{K}-M=0\Longleftrightarrow L=KM+1

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}b(p^{L},p^{M})=(1-p^{-n})p^{-nM}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{+\infty}p^{-nj}+(1-p^{-n})p^{-nM}(-\frac{1}{p-1})=\\
=(1-p^{-n})p^{-nM}\frac{p^{-n}}{1-p^{-n}}-\frac{1-p^{-n}}{p-1}=p^{-nM}\frac{p^{-n+1}-1}{p-1}.\end{split}

(5.12)

\dfrac{L-1}{K}-M

=p^{-n[\frac{L}{K}]}-\frac{1-p^{-n}}{p-1}p^{-n\frac{L-1}{K}}.\end{split}

(5.13)

Case 3.b)

Proposition 6.1
.

Suppose that the initial function
u_{0}
is such that
u_{0}(t,x)=0
outside some ball
B_{N}^{n}
,
N\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then the solution
u
of the problem (
4.1
)-(
4.2
) satisfies
u(|t|_{p},x)=0
for
x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}\setminus B_{N}^{n}
, and for all
|t|_{p}<p^{L}
for some
L\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

From (
4.12
) we have

u(|t|_{p},x)=\left((\mathcal{F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}b)\ast u_{0}\right)(|t|_{p},x)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}}(\mathcal{F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}b)(|t|_{p},|x-y|_{p})u_{0}(y)dy=

=\int\limits_{B_{N}^{n}}(\mathcal{F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}b)(|t|_{p},|x-y|_{p})u_{0}(y)dy.

Suppose that
|x|_{p}>p^{N}
. Then for
y\in B_{N}^{n}
by the triangle inequality (which is an equality in this case since
|x|_{p}\neq|y|_{p}
) we have
|x-y|_{p}=\max(|x|_{p},|y|_{p})=|x|_{p}
. Then
p^{-K}|x|_{p}^{K}>p^{-K}p^{NK}
. If we choose
L=p^{NK-K}
, then for all
t
,
|t|_{p}\leq p^{L}
we have

|t|_{p}\leq p^{L}\leq p^{NK-K}=p^{-K}|x|_{p}^{K}.

Recalling the explicit formula for the function
\mathcal{F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}b
, it follows from (
5.9
) that for such
x
and
t

(\mathcal{F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}b)(|t|_{p},|x-y|_{p})=0
, when
y\in B_{N}^{n}
. Hence
u(|t|_{p},x)=0
.

∎

7
Generalized Solutions and Uniqueness

We will need the following property of the operator
D^{\alpha,n}
of a distribution that corresponds to the bounded locally constant function, proved in
[
3
]
.

Lemma 7.1

(Lemma 1,
[
3
]
)
.

If
u
is a bounded locally constant function on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}
, then the distribution
D^{\alpha,n}u\in\Phi(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
coincides with the function

(D^{\alpha,n}u)(x)=\dfrac{1-p^{\alpha}}{1-p^{-\alpha-n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}|y|_{p}^{-\alpha-n}\left[u(x-y)-u(x)\right]dy.

(7.1)

We consider the problem (
4.1
)-(
4.2
) in the class of generalized functions, radial in
t
.

Denote by
\Phi^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\Phi^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}))
the set of distributions over the test function space
\Phi(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, with values in
\Phi^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
u\in\Phi^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\Phi^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}))
be a generalized solution of the equation (
4.1
), that is

\langle\langle u,D_{t}^{\alpha}\varphi_{1}\rangle,\varphi_{2}\rangle=\langle\langle u,\varphi_{1}\rangle,D_{x}^{\beta,n}\varphi_{2}\rangle,

for any
\varphi_{1}\in\Phi(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
,
\varphi_{2}\in\Phi(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\prime})
. If
u
is radial in
t
, then
u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\Phi^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}))
. If, in addition,
u(0,x)=0
, then
u(t,x)\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Denote by
\widehat{u}(t,\cdot)
the Fourier transform of
u
in the variable
x
. For any
\psi\in\Psi(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
we have

D_{|t|_{p}}^{\alpha}\langle\widehat{u}(t,\cdot),\psi\rangle=\langle|\xi|_{p}^{\beta}\widehat{u}(t,\xi),\psi(\xi)\rangle.

If
\supp\psi\subset S_{N}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
, then

D_{|t|_{p}}^{\alpha}\langle\widehat{u}(t,\cdot),\psi\rangle=p^{\beta N}\langle\widehat{u}(t,\cdot),\psi(\cdot)\rangle.

By Proposition
3.2
, the function
\langle\widehat{u}(t,\cdot),\psi\rangle
has the form (
3.3
) for some
c\in\mathbb{C}
. If
\psi\in\Psi(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
, then
\psi
is a sum of a finite number of functions supported on spheres
S_{N}^{n}
. Taking, in particular,
\psi=\widehat{\varphi}
,
\varphi\in\Phi(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
, we find that
\langle u(t,\cdot),\varphi\rangle
belongs to
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
in the variable
t
, for any
\varphi\in\Phi(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
.

If
u(0,\cdot)=0
, then also
\widehat{u}(0,\cdot)=0
. If
\psi\in\Psi(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
,
\supp\psi\subset S_{N}^{n}
, then
\langle\widehat{u}(t,\cdot),\psi\rangle
has the form (
2.3
), and the assumption
\widehat{u}(0,\cdot)=0
implies the equality
c=0
, hence
\langle\widehat{u}(t,\cdot),\psi\rangle=0
, and
\widehat{u}(t,\cdot)=0
(since
\psi
and
N
are arbitrary), and
u(t,\cdot)=0
.
∎

It follows from Lemma
7.1
that locally bounded constant solutions of the equation (
4.1
) are generalized solutions of the class considered in Theorem
7.1
. Therefore the solutions of the Cauchy problems constructed in Theorem
4.1
are unique in the class of radial in
t
, bounded locally constant functions.

8
L^{1}
-Estimate of the Solution

Denote
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{+}\coloneqq\{t\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}|\;|t|_{p}\geq 1\}
.

Theorem 8.1
.

Suppose that the conditions of Theorem
4.1
hold. Then the solution of the problem (
4.1
)-(
4.2
), defined in (
4.12
), satisfies the following estimate in
L^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
in variable
x
for each
t\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{+}

\left\lVert u(|t|_{p},\cdot)\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}\leq p^{2n\gamma}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})},

(8.1)

where
\gamma\geq\dfrac{2}{K}
is a positive constant.

Proof.

For each
t\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{+}
we have

\begin{split}&\left\lVert\mathcal{F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}[b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})](|t|_{p},\cdot)\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}=\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}}\left\lvert\mathcal{F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}[b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p}))](|t|_{p},\cdot)\right\rvert dx=\\
&=\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}}\left\lvert\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}}\chi_{p}(-x\cdot\xi)b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p}))d\xi\right\rvert dx=\\
&=\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}}\left\lvert\int\limits_{B_{\gamma}^{n}}\chi_{p}(-x\cdot\xi)b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p}))d\xi+\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}\setminus B_{\gamma}^{n}}\chi_{p}(-x\xi)b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p}))d\xi\right\rvert dx\leq\\
&\leq\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}}\left\lvert\int\limits_{B_{\gamma}^{n}}\chi_{p}(-x\cdot\xi)b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p}))d\xi\right\rvert dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}}\left\lvert\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}\setminus B_{\gamma}^{n}}\chi_{p}(-x\cdot\xi)b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})d\xi\right\rvert dx,\end{split}

(8.2)

where
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}
will be chosen later.

For the integral
\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}\setminus B_{\gamma}^{n}}\chi_{p}(-x\cdot\xi)b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})d\xi
we have

\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}\setminus B_{\gamma}^{n}}\chi_{p}(-x\cdot\xi)b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})d\xi=\sum\limits_{N=\gamma+1}^{+\infty}\int\limits_{S_{N}^{n}}\chi_{p}(-x\cdot\xi)b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})d\xi.

It follows from (
4.9
) that
b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})=0
, if
|\xi|_{p}^{K}\geq\dfrac{p^{2}}{|t|_{p}}
. We suppose that
|t|_{p}=p^{L}
,
L\geq 0
, then
|t|_{p}\geq 1
. Thus if
\gamma\geq\frac{2}{K}
, for
\xi\in S_{N}^{n}
,
N\geq\gamma+1
, we get
|\xi|_{p}\geq p^{\gamma+1}=p^{\frac{2}{K}+1}\geq p^{\frac{2}{K}}
, then
|\xi|_{p}^{K}\geq p^{2}\geq\dfrac{p^{2}}{|t|_{p}}
, and therefore
\int\limits_{S_{N}^{n}}\chi_{p}(-x\cdot\xi)b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})d\xi=0
. In conclusion, for the chosen
\gamma
we have

\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}}\left\lvert\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}\setminus B_{\gamma}^{n}}\chi_{p}(-x\cdot\xi)b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})d\xi\right\rvert dx=0.

Now for the first integral in (
8.2
) we have

\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}}\left\lvert\int\limits_{B_{\gamma}^{n}}\chi_{p}(-x\cdot\xi)b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})d\xi\right\rvert dx=\int\limits_{B_{\gamma}^{n}}\left\lvert\int\limits_{B_{\gamma}^{n}}\chi_{p}(-x\cdot\xi)b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})d\xi\right\rvert dx+

+\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}\setminus B_{\gamma}^{n}}\left\lvert\int\limits_{B_{\gamma}^{n}}\chi_{p}(-x\cdot\xi)b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})d\xi\right\rvert dx,

where

\int\limits_{B_{\gamma}^{n}}\left\lvert\int\limits_{B_{\gamma}^{n}}\chi_{p}(-x\cdot\xi)b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})d\xi\right\rvert dx\leq\int\limits_{B_{\gamma}^{n}}\int\limits_{B_{\gamma}^{n}}|\chi_{p}(-x\cdot\xi)|\cdot|b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})|d\xi dx\leq\int\limits_{B_{\gamma}^{n}}\int\limits_{B_{\gamma}^{n}}d\xi dx=p^{2n\gamma},

since

|\chi_{p}(-x\cdot\xi)|=1
for all
x,\xi\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}
by definition of the character
\chi_{p}
, and
|b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})|\leq 1
for all
(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{+}\times\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}
by definition of the function
b
in (
4.9
).

Finally, we have from (
2.3
)

\int\limits_{B_{\gamma}^{n}}\chi_{p}(-x\cdot\xi)b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})d\xi\leq\int\limits_{B_{\gamma}^{n}}\chi_{p}(-x\cdot\xi)d\xi=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p^{n\gamma},&|x|_{p}\leq p^{-\gamma};\\
0,&|x|_{p}\geq p^{-\gamma+1},\end{array}\right.

and

\int\limits_{B_{\gamma}^{n}}\chi_{p}(-x\cdot\xi)b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})d\xi\geq-\frac{1}{p-1}\int\limits_{B_{\gamma}^{n}}\chi_{p}(-x\cdot\xi)d\xi=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\dfrac{1}{p-1}p^{n\gamma},&|x|_{p}\leq p^{-\gamma};\\
0,&|x|_{p}\geq p^{-\gamma+1}.\end{array}\right.

Since
\gamma\geq\dfrac{2}{K}
is positive, in the outer integral we have
|x|_{p}\geq p^{\gamma+1}\geq p^{-\gamma+1}
, so we get

\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}\setminus B_{\gamma}^{n}}\left\lvert\int\limits_{B_{\gamma}^{n}}\chi_{p}(-x\cdot\xi)b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})d\xi\right\rvert dx=0.

Finally, combining the estimates for all integrals in (
8.2
), we obtain

\left\lVert\mathcal{F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}[b(|t|_{p},|\xi|_{p})](|t|_{p},\cdot)\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}\leq p^{2n\gamma}.

(8.3)

The inequality for convolutions gives us the estimate for the norm of the solution

\left\lVert u(|t|_{p},\cdot)\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}\leq p^{2n\gamma}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}.

(8.4)

∎

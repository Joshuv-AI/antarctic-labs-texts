An explicit decomposition of higher Deligne-Lsuztig representations
Ben Liu, Sian Nie

Abstract
. In a previous paper [ 22 ] , the second named author obtains a decomposition of an elliptic higher Deligne-Lusztig representation into irreducible summands, which are built in the same way as Yu types using a geometric analog κ ′ \kappa^{\prime} of the Weil-Heisenberg representation κ \kappa . In this note, we show that κ ′ \kappa^{\prime} and κ \kappa differs by a character χ \chi . Moreover, under a mild condition on the cardinality q q of the residue field (for instance q > 3 q>3 ), we show that χ \chi equals the sign character constructed by Fintzen-Kaletha-Spice [ 15 ] , which gives an explicit irreducible decomposition result on elliptic higher Deligne-Lusztig representations. As an application, we deduce (under the mild condition on q q ) that each unramified Yu type appears in the cohomology of higher Deligne-Lusztig varieties, and each unramified Kaletha’s regular supercuspidal representation is the compact induction of a specified higher Deligne-Lusztig representation up to a sign.

1.1.
Main result

The most interesting higher Deligne-Lusztig representations are those attached to attached to elliptic tori, which are referred to as elliptic higher Deligne-Lusztig representations. All the other higher Deligne-Lusztig representations are parabolic inductions of elliptic ones. The main purpose of note is to give an explicit algebraic characterization of elliptic higher Deligne-Lusztig representation.

Let
p\neq\ell
be two different prime numbers. Let
k
be a non-archimedean local field with a finite residue field of cardinality
q
and of characteristic
p
. Let
G
be a connected
k
-rational reductive group which splits over a maximal unramifed extension
{\breve{k}}
of
k
. Let
Z_{G}
denote the center of
G
. Fix a point
{\mathbf{x}}
in the Bruhat-Tits building
\mathcal{B}(G,k)
of
G
over
k
. Denote by
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{x}}
the attached (connected) parahoric subgroup over the integer ring
\mathcal{O}_{k}
of
k
. We recall the following condition on
p
:

\text{ $p\neq 2$ is not a bad prime for $G$ and $p\nmid|\pi_{1}(G_{\rm der})|\cdot|\pi_{1}(\widehat{G}_{\rm der})|$ }.

Here
G_{\rm der}
and
\widehat{G}_{\rm der}
are the derived subgroups of
G
and its dual group
\widehat{G}
respectively.

Let
T\subseteq G
be a
k
-rational and
{\breve{k}}
-split maximal elliptic torus, whose apartment over
k
contains
{\mathbf{x}}
.
\phi:T(k)\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{\times}
of depth
\leqslant r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
. Choose a
{\breve{k}}
-rational maximal unipotent subgroup
U\subseteq G
normalized by
T
. Following
[
21
]
and
[
5
]
, one can associate an
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-variety

X=X_{T,U,x,r},

called a higher Deligne-Lusztig variety. Applying Deligne-Lusztig cohomological induction to
X
, one obtains a virtual smooth
Z_{G}(k)\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{O}_{k})
-module

R_{T,r}^{G}(\phi)=R_{T,U,{\mathbf{x}},r}^{G}(\phi),

which is referred to as a higher Deligne-Lusztig representation. If
r=0
,
R_{T,r}^{G}(\phi)
is by definition a classical Deligne-Lusztig representation.

Suppose that
\phi
has a Howe factorization
(\Lambda,\phi_{-1})
in the sense of
[
19
]
, where
\Lambda=(G^{i},\phi_{i},r_{i})_{0\leqslant i\leqslant d}
is a generic datum and
\phi_{-1}:T(k)\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{\times}
a depth
0
character. Under the condition (*), the second named author
[
22
]
proved the following result

R_{T,r}^{G}(\phi)={\rm ind}_{\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{O}_{k})}^{\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{O}_{k})}\kappa_{\Lambda}\otimes R_{T,0}^{G^{0}}(\phi_{-1}),

where
\mathcal{K}
is the identity component of a Yu-type
\mathcal{O}_{k}
-subgroup associated to
(\Lambda,{\mathbf{x}})
;
\kappa_{\Lambda}
is an irreducible
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{O}_{k})
-module obtained through a cohomological method; and
R_{T,0}^{G^{0}}(\phi_{-1})
is the classical Deligne-Lusztig representation.

Thus, to give a complete description of
R_{T,r}^{G}(\phi)
, it remains to determine the representation
\kappa_{\Lambda}
arising from geometry. To this end, we recall two classical representations of Yu’s subgroup
\widetilde{K}\supseteq Z_{G}(k)\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{O}_{k})
associated to
(\Lambda,{\mathbf{x}})
. The first one is the Weil-Heisenberg representation
\widetilde{\kappa}(\Lambda)
introduced by Yu
[
23
]
, which play an essential role in the construction of supercuspidal representations for
p
-adic groups. The other is the (inflated) quadratic character
\widetilde{\epsilon}_{\Lambda}
constructed by Fintzen, Kaletha and Spice
[
15
]
, which paly a delicate role in constructing supercuspidal
L
-packets (see also the work by Chan and Oi
[
7
]
when
\phi
is toral). Both
\widetilde{\kappa}(\Lambda)
and
\widetilde{\epsilon}_{\Lambda}
are constructed in a purely algebraic way and have very explicit descriptions. Put
\kappa(\Lambda)=\widetilde{\kappa}(\Lambda)|_{Z_{G}(k)\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{O}_{k})}
and
\epsilon_{\Lambda}=\widetilde{\epsilon}_{\Lambda}|_{Z_{G}(k)\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{O}_{k})}
for simplicity.

Now we state the main result of this note.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
p\neq 2
. Then there exists a character
\psi
of
Z_{G}(k)\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{O}_{k})
such that

(-1)^{d_{\Lambda}}\kappa_{\Lambda}=\kappa(\Lambda)\otimes\epsilon_{\Lambda}\otimes\psi,

where
d_{\Lambda}\in\{\pm 1\}
is a sign associated to
\Lambda
(see Theorem
4.1
).

If, moreover,
q\geqslant c_{\Lambda}
then
\psi=1
. Here
2\leqslant c_{\Lambda}\leqslant 4
is an integer defined in Proposition
5.3
.

Remark 1.2
.

It would be interesting to extend Theorem
1.1
to the modular case. This would give an explicit description of modular higher Deligne-Lusztig representations, see
[
18
]
.

Combining
[
22
, Theorem 1.6]
and Theorem
1.1
we have an explicit irreducible decomposition result.

Corollary 1.3
.

Assume
p
satisfies (*) and
q\geqslant c_{\Lambda}
. Then

R_{T,r}^{G}(\phi)=(-1)^{d_{\Lambda}}\sum_{\rho}m_{\rho}{\rm ind}_{\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{O}_{k})}^{\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{O}_{k})}\kappa(\Lambda)\otimes\epsilon_{\Lambda}\otimes\rho,

where
\rho
runs through irreducible factors up to isomorphism of
R_{T,0}^{G^{0}}(\phi_{-1})
with multiplicity
m_{\rho}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover, the
\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{O}_{k})
-modules

{\rm ind}_{\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{O}_{k})}^{\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{O}_{k})}\kappa(\Lambda)\otimes\epsilon_{\Lambda}\otimes\rho

on the right hand side of (*) are irreducible and non-isomorphic with each other.

Remark 1.4
.

When a preliminary version of this note is finished, we noticed that Chan and Oi
[
8
]
also obtains Corollary
1.3
under certain largeness condition on
q
. Their approach is quite different from ours, which is based on studies of the regular case and Green functions of higher Deligne-Lusztig representations. Note that our assumption on
q
may be weaker than theirs in some cases. For instance, if
\phi
is toral, then
c_{\Lambda}=2
by definition and hence there is no restriction on
q
in Corollary
1.3
.

1.2.
Application

Now we discuss some applications of the main result. In
[
23
]
, Yu introduced the notion of (tame) cuspidal
G
-data, and associated with each cuspidal
G
-datum
\Sigma
an irreducible supercuspidal representation
\pi_{\Sigma}^{\rm Yu}
of
G(k)
. Conversely, thanks to work of Kim
[
20
]
and Fintzen
[
14
]
, all the irreducible supercuspidal representations are exhausted by Yu’s representations
\pi_{\Sigma}^{\rm Yu}
, provided
p
does not divide the order of the absolute Weyl group of
G
.

Recall briefly that a cupidal
G
-datum
\Sigma
consists of a generic datum
\Lambda=(G^{i},\phi_{i},r_{i})_{0\leqslant i\leqslant d}
, a point
{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{B}(G^{0},k)
and a depth
0
irreducible representation
\widetilde{\rho}
of
G^{0}(k)_{[{\mathbf{x}}]}
satisfying certain conditions. Here
G^{0}(k)_{[{\mathbf{x}}]}
denotes the stabilizer in
G^{0}(k)
of the image
[{\mathbf{x}}]
of
{\mathbf{x}}
in
\mathcal{B}(G^{0}_{\rm der},k)
. Then
\pi_{\Sigma}^{\rm Yu}
and its twisted company
\pi_{\Sigma}^{\rm FKS}
are given by

\pi_{\Sigma}^{\rm Yu}=\text{c-}{\rm ind}_{\widetilde{K}}^{G(k)}\widetilde{\kappa}(\Lambda)\otimes\widetilde{\rho},\quad\pi_{\Sigma}^{\rm FKS}=\text{c-}{\rm ind}_{\widetilde{K}}^{G(k)}\widetilde{\kappa}(\Lambda)\otimes\widetilde{\epsilon}_{\Lambda}\otimes\widetilde{\rho}.

Now we assume the cuspidal datum
\Sigma=(\Lambda,{\mathbf{x}},\widetilde{\rho})
above is unramified, that is, the Levi subgroup
G^{0}
splits over
{\breve{k}}
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda,x}
be the set of
k
-rational and
{\breve{k}}
-split maximal tori of
G^{0}
whose apartments over
k
contain
{\mathbf{x}}
. The following result gives an explicit relation between irreducible supercupidal representations and cohomology of convex higher Deligne-Lusztig varieties.

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume
p
satisfies (*) and
q\geqslant c_{\Lambda}
. Let
\Sigma=(\Lambda,{\mathbf{x}},\widetilde{\rho})
be an unramified cuspidal
G
-datum. Let
T\in\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda,{\mathbf{x}}}
be an elliptic torus and
\phi_{-1}
a depth
0
character of
T(k)
such that
\hom_{Z_{G}(k)\mathcal{G}^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{k})}(\widetilde{\rho},R_{T,0}^{G^{0}}(\phi_{-1}))\neq 0
. Then there exists
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
such that

\pi_{\Sigma}^{\rm FKS}\text{ is a direct summand of }\text{c-}{\rm ind}_{Z_{G}(k)\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{O}_{k})}^{G(k)}H_{c}^{i}(X,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})[\phi],

where
\phi
is the unique character with Howe factorization
(\Lambda,\phi_{-1})
.

Moreover, such
T
and
\phi_{-1}
satisfying above conditions always exist.

Remark 1.6
.

Theorem
1.5
implies that each supercuspidal representation associated to an unramified cuspidal datum can be realized by cohomology of higher Deligne-Lusztig varieties. The theorem is first proved in
[
22
]
when

is sufficiently large.

In
[
19
]
, Kaletha studied an important large family of supercupidal representations, called the regular supercupidal representations. These representations
\pi^{\rm Yu}_{(T,\phi)}
are parameterized by tame elliptic regular pairs
(T,\phi)
, where
T\subseteq G
is a tame elliptic maximal torus and
\phi
is of character of
T(k)
satisfying certain regularity conditions. We refer to
[

for the precise definitions. Let
(\Lambda,\phi_{-1})
be a Howe factorization of
\phi
. Following
[
7
]
we set
\epsilon[\phi]=\widetilde{\epsilon}_{\Lambda}|_{T(k)}
. The following result sharpens Theorem

for regular supercupidal representations.

Corollary 1.7
.

Assume
p
satisfies (*) and

. Let
(T,\phi)
be a tame elliptic regular pair such that

is
{\breve{k}}
-split. Then

\pi^{\rm Yu}_{(T,\phi\cdot\epsilon[\phi])}\cong(-1)^{r(G^{0})-r(T)+d_{\Lambda}}\text{c-}{\rm ind}_{Z_{G}(k)\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{O}_{k})}^{G(k)}R_{T,r}^{G}(\phi)

where
r(G^{0})
and
r(T)
are the split ranks of
G^{0}
and
T
respectively, and
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{x}}
for some/any point in the apartment of
T
.

Remark 1.8
.

Theorem
1.5
and Corollary
1.7
are also obtained by Chan and Oi
[
8
]
(see
[
7
]
when

1.3.
Strategy

We outline the proof of Theorem
1.1
. First we show that the two representations
(-1)^{d_{\Lambda}}\kappa_{\Lambda}
and
\kappa(\Lambda)\otimes\epsilon_{\Lambda}
differs by a character
\psi
of
(\mathbb{G}^{0})_{0}^{F}
, where
(\mathbb{G}^{0})_{0}
is the reductive quotient of
\mathcal{G}^{0}
. This is achieved using the key observation that both of them are extensions of a common Heisenberg representation. It remains to show the character
\psi
is trivial. By our assumption on
q
, the group
(\mathbb{G}^{0})_{0}^{F}
is generated by its commutator subgroup and
\mathbb{S}_{0}^{F}
, where
\mathbb{S}_{0}
is a maximally split maximal torus of
\mathbb{G}_{0}^{0}
. Thus, it remains to show
\psi
is trivial over
\mathbb{S}_{0}^{F}
. To this end, its suffices to that

\tr(\gamma;\kappa(\Lambda)\otimes\epsilon_{\Lambda})=(-1)^{d_{\Lambda}}\tr(\gamma;\kappa_{\Lambda})\neq 0\text{ for all }\gamma\in\mathbb{S}_{0}^{F}.

The left trace is computed in
[
1
]
and
[
12
]
. The right trace is computed in Proposition
4.3
based on a concentration theorem of
[
22
]
. It turns out that both traces coincide with each other and are nonzero, which finishes the proof of the theorem.

Let

2.2.
Parahoric subgroup

Let
G
be a connected
k
-rational reductive group. By abuse of notation, we identify
G=G({\breve{k}})
and hence
G(k)=G^{F}
. Let
{\mathbf{x}}
be a point in the (enlarged) Bruhat-Tits building

of

over

. Let

. Thanks to the Bruhat-Tits building theory, there is an associated connected parahoric

, together with a filtration of Moy-Prasad subgroups

.

Moreover generally, for any

-rational closed subgroup

\mathcal{H}
in
\mathcal{G}
. Applying the functor

to

and then passing to subquotients yield closed subgroups

of

and
\mathbb{H}_{r}=\mathbb{H}_{r}^{s}

.

Let
S\subseteq G
be a
k
-rational
{\breve{k}}
-split maximal torus. We denote by
\mathcal{A}(S,k)
the apartment of

S
over
k
. Let
\Phi(S,G)
and
\widetilde{\Phi}(G,S)
be the sets of (absolute) roots and affine roots of

in

, respectively. Each affine root

in
\widetilde{\Phi}(G,S)
defines an affine function on

. We denote by

.

3.
Construction of
\kappa(\Lambda)

A generic datum is a triple
\Lambda=(G^{i},\phi_{i},r_{i})_{0\leqslant i\leqslant d}
, where

•

G^{0}\subsetneq G^{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq G^{d}=G
are unramified Levi subgroups;

•

0=r_{-1}<r_{0}<\cdots<r_{d-1}\leqslant r_{d}
if

and
0\leqslant r_{0}
if

;

•

\phi_{i}:G^{F}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{\times}
is a character which is trivial over

for
0\leqslant i\leqslant d
;

•

\phi_{i}
is of depth

and is
(G^{i},G^{i+1})
-generic in the sense of
[
23

for

•

and is trivial otherwise.

Moreover, we say

splits over

.

In this section, we fix a generic datum
\Lambda=(G^{i},\phi_{i},r_{i})_{0\leqslant i\leqslant d}
as above and assume it is unramified. We set
L_{\Lambda}=G^{0}
and let
{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{B}(L_{\Lambda},k)
. For closed subgroups

we denote by
\mathbb{H}
the group attached to

as in §

.

Let

be the set of

-split maximal tori

such that

3.1.
Subgroups of Yu-type

Following
[
23
]
we introduce the following

.

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{G}^{0}(\mathbb{G}^{1})^{r_{0}/2}\cdots(\mathbb{G}^{d})^{r_{d-1}/2};

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=(\mathbb{G}^{0})^{0+}(\mathbb{G}^{1})^{r_{0}/2}\cdots(\mathbb{G}^{d})^{r_{d-1}/2};

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}_{\Lambda}^{+}

\displaystyle=(\mathbb{G}^{0})^{0+}(\mathbb{G}^{1})^{r_{0}/2+}\cdots(\mathbb{G}^{d})^{r_{d-1}/2+};

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=(\mathbb{G}^{0}_{\rm der})^{0+,0+}(\mathbb{G}^{1}_{\rm der})^{r_{0}+,r_{0}/2+}\cdots(\mathbb{G}^{d}_{\rm der})^{r_{d-1}+,r_{d-1}/2+}.

Here
(\mathbb{G}^{i}_{\rm der})^{r_{i-1}+,r_{i-1}/2+}
is the subgroup generated by
(\mathbb{G}^{i}_{\rm der})^{r_{i-1}+}
and
(\mathbb{G}^{\alpha})^{r_{i-1}/2+}
for

is any/some maximal torus in

and

is the root subgroup corresponding to

.

For

\hat{\phi}_{i}
denote the extended character of

to the group

defined in
[

. We define a character of

Let

.

]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

If

\phi_{d}=1
), the quotient
\mathbb{H}_{\Lambda}^{F}/\ker\chi_{\Lambda}

Now we define an irreducible representation
\omega_{\Lambda}
of
\mathbb{H}_{\Lambda}^{F}/\ker\chi_{\Lambda}
as follows. If
\mathbb{H}_{\Lambda}^{F}/\ker\chi_{\Lambda}
is trivial, let
\omega_{\Lambda}
be the trivial representation. Otherwise, by Proposition

is a Heisenberg

-group with center
(\mathbb{K}_{\Lambda}^{+})^{F}/\ker\chi_{\Lambda}
, and let

be the unique irreducible (Heisenberg) representation of

with the nontrivial central character

as a representation of
\mathbb{H}_{\Lambda}^{F}
.

3.3.
A trace formula on
\kappa(\Lambda)

Let
[x]
be the image of
{\mathbf{x}}
in
\mathcal{B}((L_{\Lambda})_{\rm der},k)
. Denote by
\widetilde{}\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}
the stabilizer of

in

\widetilde{}\mathbb{K}_{\Lambda}=\widetilde{}\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}\mathbb{H}_{\Lambda}=\mathbb{H}_{\Lambda}\widetilde{}\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}

, see

Following
[

]

\epsilon_{\Lambda}:(\widetilde{}\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda})_{0}^{F}\{\pm 1\}.

By inflation, we also view

Let

and
\gamma\in\mathbb{S}
. We denote by

\text{c-}{\rm ind}_{Z_{G}^{F}\mathbb{G}^{F}}^{G^{F}}R_{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathbb{G}}(\phi)
;

•

\pi_{\Sigma}^{\rm FKS}\cong(-1)^{r(L_{\Lambda})-r(T)+d_{\Lambda}}\text{c-}{\rm ind}_{Z_{G}^{F}\mathbb{G}^{F}}^{G^{F}}R_{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathbb{G}}(\phi)
if
\phi_{-1}
is regular in the sense of
[
19
, Definition 3.4.16]
.

Here
r(L_{\Lambda})
and
r(T)
denotes the
k
-rank of
L_{\Lambda}
and
T
respectively.

Proof.

Let
A
be finite group and let
\chi=\sum_{\pi}c_{\pi}\pi
be a virtual
A
-module, where
c_{\pi}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\pi
ranges over irreducible
A
-modules up to isomorphism. We set
|\chi|=\sum_{\pi}|c_{\pi}|\pi
.

By reciprocity and the choice of
\phi_{-1}
we have

\hom_{\widetilde{}\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}^{F}}(\widetilde{\rho},{\rm ind}_{Z_{G}^{F}\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}^{F}}^{\widetilde{}\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}^{F}}|R_{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}}(\phi_{-1})|)=\hom_{Z_{G}^{F}\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}^{F}}(\rho,|R_{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}}(\phi_{-1})|)\neq 0.

Now we have

\displaystyle\pi_{\Sigma}^{\rm FKS}

\displaystyle=\text{c-}{\rm ind}_{\widetilde{}\mathbb{K}_{\Lambda}^{F}}^{G^{F}}\widetilde{\kappa}(\Lambda)\otimes\widetilde{\epsilon}_{\Lambda}\otimes\widetilde{\rho}

\displaystyle\subseteq\text{c-}{\rm ind}_{Z_{G}^{F}\mathbb{K}_{\Lambda}^{F}}^{G^{F}}\kappa(\Lambda)\otimes\epsilon_{\Lambda}\otimes\otimes|R_{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}}(\phi_{-1})|

\displaystyle=\text{c-}{\rm ind}_{Z_{G}^{F}\mathbb{K}_{\Lambda}^{F}}^{G^{F}}|\kappa_{\Lambda}|\otimes|R_{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}}(\phi_{-1})|

\displaystyle=\text{c-}{\rm ind}_{Z_{G}^{F}\mathbb{G}^{F}}^{G^{F}}{\rm ind}_{Z_{G}^{F}\mathbb{K}^{F}}^{Z_{G}^{F}\mathbb{G}^{F}}|\kappa_{\Lambda}|\otimes|R_{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}}(\phi_{-1})|

\displaystyle=\text{c-}{\rm ind}_{Z_{G}^{F}\mathbb{G}^{F}}^{G^{F}}|R_{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathbb{G}}(\phi)|,

where
\epsilon_{\Lambda},\kappa(\Lambda)
are viewed as restrictions of
\widetilde{\epsilon}_{\Lambda},\widetilde{\kappa}(\Lambda)
to
Z_{G}^{F}\mathbb{K}_{\Lambda}^{F}
respectively, and the last equality follows from Theorem
6.1
. Hence the first statement follows.

Now assume that
\phi_{-1}
is regular in the sense of
[
19
, Definition 3.4.16]
. As
Z_{G}^{F}\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}^{F}=T^{F}\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}^{F}
, it follows by
[
19
, Lemma 3.4.20]
that

\widetilde{\rho}\cong(-1)^{r(L_{\Lambda})-r(T)}{\rm ind}_{Z_{G}^{F}\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}^{F}}^{\widetilde{}\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}^{F}}R_{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}}(\phi_{-1}).

Note that
\widetilde{}\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}^{F}/Z_{G}^{F}\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}^{F}\cong\widetilde{}\mathbb{K}_{\Lambda}^{F}/Z_{G}^{F}\mathbb{K}_{\Lambda}^{F}
. Applying projection formula we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\kappa}(\Lambda)\otimes\widetilde{\epsilon}_{\Lambda}\otimes\widetilde{\rho}

\displaystyle\cong(-1)^{r(L_{\Lambda})-r(T)}{\rm ind}_{Z_{G}^{F}\mathbb{K}_{\Lambda}^{F}}^{\widetilde{}\mathbb{K}_{\Lambda}^{F}}\kappa(\Lambda)\otimes\epsilon_{\Lambda}\otimes R_{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}}(\phi_{-1})

\displaystyle\cong(-1)^{r(L_{\Lambda})-r(T)+d_{\Lambda}}{\rm ind}_{Z_{G}^{F}\mathbb{K}_{\Lambda}^{F}}^{\widetilde{}\mathbb{K}_{\Lambda}^{F}}\kappa_{\Lambda}\otimes R_{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}}(\phi_{-1}).

Then the second statement follows similarly as in the proof of the first statement.
∎

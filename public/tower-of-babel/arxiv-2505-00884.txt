Strichartz estimates for the Schrödinger equation on Zoll manifolds. Thanks: The first author was supported in part by the Simons Foundation. The second author was supported in part by the NSF (DMS-2348996).
Xiaoqi Huang, Christopher D. Sogge

Abstract
. We obtain optimal space-time estimates in L t , x q L^{q}_{t,x} spaces for all q ≥ 2 q\geq 2 for solutions to the Schrödinger equation on Zoll manifolds, including, in particular, the standard round sphere S d S^{d} . The proof relies on the arithmetic properties of the spectrum of the Laplacian on Zoll manifolds, as well as bilinear oscillatory integral estimates, which allow us to relate the problem to Strichartz estimate on one-dimensional tori.



1.
Introduction

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold of
dimension
d\geq 2
,
\Delta_{g}
denotes
the associated Laplace-Beltrami operator and

(1.1)

u(x,t)=\bigl(e^{-it\Delta_{g}}f\bigr)(x)

be the solution of the Schrödinger equation
on
M\times{\mathbb{R}}
,

(1.2)

i\partial_{t}u(x,t)=\Delta_{g}u(x,t),\quad u(x,0)=f(x).

Recall that the universal estimates
of Burq, Gérard and Tzvetkov
[
9
]
says that if
u

is the solution of the Schrödinger equation (
1.2
),
then one has the mixed-norm Strichartz estimates

(1.3)

\|u\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(M\times[0,1])}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{1/p}(M)}

for all
admissible
pairs
(p,q)
. By the latter
we mean, as in Keel and Tao
[
16
]
,

(1.4)

d(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{q})=\tfrac{2}{p}\,\,\,\text{and }\,\,2\leq q\leq\tfrac{2d}{d-2}\,\,\text{if }\,d\geq 3,\,\,\,\text{or }\,2\leq q<\infty\,\,\text{if }\,\,d=2.

Also, in (
1.3
) the mixed norm is defined by

(1.5)

\|u\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(M\times[0,1])}=\bigr(\,\int_{0}^{1}\,\|u(\,\cdot\,,t)\|_{L^{q}_{x}(M)}^{p}\,dt\,\bigr)^{1/p},

and
H^{\mu}
denotes the
standard Sobolev space

(1.6)

\|f\|_{H^{\mu}(M)}=\bigl\|\,(I+P)^{\mu}f\,\bigr\|_{L^{2}(M)},\quad\text{with }\,\,P=\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}},

and “
\lesssim
” in (
1.3
) and, in what
follows, denotes an inequality with an implicit,
but unstated, constant
C
which can change at each occurrence.

By taking the initial data
f
to be eigenfunctions of
P
, it is not hard to see that one can not replace the interval
[0,1]
in (
1.3
) by
[0,T]
for an arbitrary large constant
T
, without the implicit constant depending on
T
. Also, for the endpoint Strichartz estimates where

p=2
and
q=\tfrac{2d}{d-2}
with
d\geq 3
, the
\frac{1}{2}
derivative loss in the right side of (
1.3
) is sharp on
S^{d}
by letting
f
to be the zonal eigenfunctions. See, e.g.,
[
2
,
9
]
for more details.

In this paper, we consider the analog of (
1.3
) in the case
p=q
, and obtain the following sharp estimate on all Zoll manifolds, i.e., manifolds all of whose geodesics are closed with a common minimal period.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth Zoll manifold of dimension
d\geq 2
. Then for all
q\geq 2
,
the solution
u
of (
1.2
) satisfies

(1.7)

\|u\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}(M\times[0,1])}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{s}(M)},\,\,\,s>\mu(q),

where
\mu(q)=\max\{\frac{d-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}),\frac{d-1}{2}-\frac{d}{q},\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+2}{q}\}
.

Note that when
d=2
, (
1.7
) splits into estimates for two ranges of
q
:

(1.8)

\|u\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}(M\times[0,1])}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\begin{cases}\|f\|_{H^{s_{\mathrm{sm}}+\varepsilon}(M)},\quad s_{\mathrm{sm}}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{q}),\,\,q\in(2,\tfrac{14}{3}]\\
\|f\|_{H^{s_{\mathrm{Sob}}+\varepsilon}(M)},\quad s_{\mathrm{Sob}}=1-\tfrac{4}{q},\,\,q\in[\tfrac{14}{3},\infty],\end{cases}

while, in higher dimensions
d\geq 3
, there are three ranges of
q
:

(1.9)

\|u\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}(M\times[0,1])}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\begin{cases}\|f\|_{H^{s_{\mathrm{sm}}+\varepsilon}(M)},\quad s_{\mathrm{sm}}=\tfrac{d-1}{2}(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{q}),\,\,q\in(2,\tfrac{2(d+1)}{d-1}]\\
\|f\|_{H^{s_{\mathrm{lg}}+\varepsilon}(M)},\quad s_{\mathrm{lg}}=\tfrac{d-1}{2}-\tfrac{d}{q},\,\,q\in[\tfrac{2(d+1)}{d-1},4]\\
\|f\|_{H^{s_{\mathrm{Sob}}+\varepsilon}(M)},\quad s_{\mathrm{Sob}}=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+2}{q},\,\,q\in[4,\infty].\end{cases}

As we shall see, the estimates involving
s_{\mathrm{sm}}
and
s_{\mathrm{lg}}
are saturated by eigenfunctions,
with these two exponents being the ones
occurring in the eigenfunction
L^{q}
-estimates
in
[
19
]
and
[
20
]
involving relatively small and large exponents
q
. The estimates involving

s_{\mathrm{Sob}}
, as we shall see, are optimal due
to standard functions saturating dyadic
L^{2}_{x}\to L^{q}_{x}
Sobolev
estimates.

Let us also briefly mention the reason that there are only two estimates when
d=2
and must be three for
d\geq 3
. We first note
that, when
d=2
, we have
s_{\mathrm{sm}}\geq s_{\mathrm{Sob}}\iff q\in(2,\tfrac{14}{3}]
. Also,

s_{\mathrm{lg}}\geq s_{\mathrm{Sob}}\iff q\leq 4
and
s_{\mathrm{lg}}\geq s_{\mathrm{sm}}\iff q\geq 6
. Since
4\in(2,\tfrac{14}{3}]
, we
conclude that when
d=2
we simply have
\max(s_{\mathrm{sm}},s_{\mathrm{lg}},s_{\mathrm{Sob}})=\max(s_{\mathrm{sm}},s_{\mathrm{Sob}})
.
On the other hand, in higher dimensions
d\geq 3
, we have
4\notin(2,\tfrac{2(d+1)}{d-1}]
, and

s_{\mathrm{Sob}}\geq\max(s_{\mathrm{sm}},s_{\mathrm{lg}})\iff q\in[4,\infty]
and also
s_{\mathrm{sm}}\geq s_{\mathrm{lg}}\iff q\in(2,\tfrac{2(d+1)}{d-1}]
.
This accounts for why, unlike for the
d=2
case, there must be three ranges of exponents in higher dimensions. We also should point out (see e.g.
[
19
]
)
that, on
S^{d}
zonal eigenfunctions,
Z_{\lambda}
, have
L^{q}
-norms which are
\approx\lambda^{s_{\mathrm{lg}}}
for
q\in[\tfrac{2(d+1)}{d-1},\infty]
, and thus,
if we take
f=Z_{\lambda}
we immediately see that the estimates in (
1.9
) must be sharp (up to the arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
) for

q\in[\tfrac{2(d+1)}{d-1},4]
, while we note that such data do not saturate the
d=2
bounds in (
1.8
) for any
exponent
q\in(2,\infty]
.

If
M=S^{1}
, (
1.7
) holds without any derivative loss for
q=4
due to a result of Zygmund
[
27
]
. This was later generalized to
q=6
with an arbitrary small loss of derivative by Bourgain
[
8
]
. For
d\geq 2
,
in the special case
q=4
, (
1.7
) was established in
[
9
, Theorem 4]
. This result was generalized to
q>4
for
d=3
by Herr
[
13
]
. In particular, for
q>4
and
d=3
it was shown in
[
13
]
that (
1.7
) holds with
s=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+2}{q}
using a result of Bourgain
[
6
]
based on the circle method of Hardy and Littlewood. The method employed in
[
13
]
also implies that (
1.7
) hold with
s=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+2}{q}
for all
q>4
,
d\geq 3
, and
q\geq 6
when
d=2
. Therefore, the main new contribution in Theorem
1.1
concerns the case of relatively small exponents
q
. In the next section, we will describe the difficulties in proving (
1.7
) for small exponents
q
, particularly when
d=2
and
q\in(4,6)
. We do not address the critical case
where
s=\mu(q)
in this paper.

Note that for the spheres
S^{d}
with
d\geq 2
, if we replace the
H^{s}
norm in (
1.7
) with its
L^{q}
-based analog,
Chen, Duong, Lee and Yan
[
10
]
proved the following estimate:

(1.10)

\|e^{it\Delta_{g}}f\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}(S^{d}\times[0,1])}\leq C\|(1+P)^{s}f\|_{L^{q}(S^{d})},\,\,\,s>\max\{0,\tfrac{d}{2}-\tfrac{d+2}{q}\},

for all
q\geq 2
if
d=2
, and
q\geq 4
if
d\geq 3
. The authors also showed that the lower bound on
s
is optimal, using an example similar to one we will discuss in the final section, along with a semiclassical dispersion estimate
of Burq, Gérard and Tzvetkov
[
9
]
.

The estimate in Theorem
1.1
can also be generalized to any compact space forms with positive curvature, whose spectrum is a subset of the eigenvalues of Laplacian on the sphere-for example, real projective spaces
RP^{n}
and lens spaces. See also Zhang
[
26
]
for related results on compact Lie groups.

In the case of flat tori, similar sharp estimate holds with
s>\mu(q)=\max\{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+2}{q},0\}
by using the
\ell_{2}
decoupling theorem of Bourgain-Demeter
[
7
]
, together with Sobolev estimates. See also Killip and Visan
[
17
]
for the critical case
s=\mu(q)
when
q>\frac{2(d+2)}{d}
, as well as the reference therein for a summary of prior work on Strichartz estimates on square and irrational tori.
We shall also mention that the same result holds in the Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
globally in time if
q\geq\frac{2(d+2)}{d}
by using the Euclidean analog of (
1.3
).

As in earlier works, the proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on the arithmetic properties of the spectrum of the Laplacian
on Zoll manifolds. More explicitly, if the geodesics of
M
are of period
T
, then it is known that there exist an integer
\alpha\geq 0
and
A>0
, such that the spectrum of
\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}
is contained in
\cup_{k=0}^{\infty}I_{k}
where

(1.11)

I_{k}=[\frac{2\pi}{T}(k+\frac{\alpha}{4})-\frac{A}{k},\frac{2\pi}{T}(k+\frac{\alpha}{4})+\frac{A}{k}].

Here
\alpha
is the so called Maslov index of the closed geodesics, see e.g.,
[
11
,
24
,
25
]
. As a special case, when
M
is the standard round sphere
S^{d}
, the eigenvalues of
\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}
are
\sqrt{k(k+d-1)}
for integers
k\geq 0
. The proof also uses the
spectral projection bounds of the second author
[
20
]
, which gives rise to the first two exponents
in the definition
of
\mu(q)
in (
1.7
).
Additionally,
the proof of Theorem
1.1
also relies on the bilinear oscillatory integral estimates of
Hörmander
[
14
]
and
Lee
[
18
]
which is related to
work of Tao, Vargas and Vega
[
23
]
.

The authors would like to thank the referees for their careful reading and valuable suggestions, which improved the exposition.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let us first introduce the Littlewood-Paley cutoff functions, which allow us to reduce matters to certain dyadic estimates.
More explicitly, let us fix a Littlewood-Paley bump function

\beta
satisfying

(2.1)

\beta\in C^{\infty}_{0}((1/2,2))\quad\text{and }\,\,1=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\beta(2^{-m}s),\,\,s>0.

Then, if we set
\beta_{0}(s)=1-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\beta(2^{-m}s)\in C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
and

\beta_{m}(s)=\beta(2^{-m}s)
,
m=1,2,\dots
, we
have (see e.g.,
[
22
]
)

(2.2)

\|h\|_{L^{q}(M)}\approx\bigl\|\,(\,\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}|\beta_{m}(P)h|^{2}\,)^{1/2}\,\bigr\|_{L^{q}(M)},\,\,\,1<q<\infty.

Trivially,

\|\beta_{0}(P)e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\|_{L^{2}_{x}(M)\to L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(M\times[0,1])}=O(1),\,\,\forall\,\,p,q\geq 2,

and, similarly, such results
where
m=0
is replaced by a small fixed
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
are
also standard.
So, as noted
in Burq, Gérard and Tzvetkov
[
9
]
, one can use (
2.2
) and
Minkowski’s inequality to see that
(
1.7
) follows from

(2.3)

\|e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\beta(P/\lambda)f\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}(M\times[0,1])}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\mu(q)+\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)},\,\,\,\forall\varepsilon>0,

assuming
\lambda=2^{m}\gg 1
.

To prove (
2.3
), we begin with the following:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth Zoll manifold of dimension
d\geq 2
, for any
2\leq q\leq\infty
, we have for
\lambda=2^{m}\gg 1

(2.4)

\|e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\beta(P/\lambda)f\|_{L^{q}_{x}L^{4}_{t}([0,1]\times M)}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\sigma(q)+\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)},\,\,\,\forall\varepsilon>0,

where
\sigma(q)=\max\{\frac{d-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}),\frac{d-1}{2}-\frac{d}{q}\}
.

(
2.4
) is a generalization of Theorem 4 in
[
9
]
, where the special case
q=4
was handled. As in
[
9
]
, the proof of (
2.4
) relies on the arithmetic properties of the eigenvalues of Laplacian on Zoll manifolds.

Proof.

To prove (
2.4
), it suffices to show that whenever we fix
\rho\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}})
satisfying
\text{supp }\hat{\rho}\subset(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
and
\rho\geq 0
, we have

(2.5)

\|\rho(t)e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\beta(P/\lambda)f\|_{L^{q}_{x}L^{4}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}\times M)}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\sigma(q)+\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)},\,\,\,\forall\varepsilon>0.

By a dilation of the metric
we may assume that the geodesics of
M
have
2\pi
as a common period. By (
1.11
), the spectrum of
\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}
is then contained in
\cup_{k=1}^{\infty}I_{k}
where

(2.6)

I_{k}=[k+\frac{\alpha}{4}-\frac{A}{k},k+\frac{\alpha}{4}+\frac{A}{k}],

for some constant
A>0
and integer
\alpha\geq 0
.
Now let
{P}_{k}
denote the projection operator such that
{P}_{k}f=f
if Spec
{f\in I_{k}}
.
Since we are assuming
f=\beta(P/\lambda)f
with
\lambda=2^{m}\gg 1
, by (
2.1
), it suffices to consider
k\gg 1
. Note that for
k\gg 1
,
the intervals

I_{k}
are disjoint,
and so we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\Big(\rho(t)

\displaystyle e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\beta(P/\lambda)f\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l=\lambda/4}^{4\lambda}\big(\rho(t)e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\beta(P/\lambda)P_{k}f\big)\cdot\big(\rho(t)e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\beta(P/\lambda)P_{\ell}f\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l=\lambda/4}^{4\lambda}\tilde{P}_{k}f\tilde{P}_{\ell}f,

if

(2.8)

\tilde{P}_{k}f=\rho(t)e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\beta(P/\lambda)P_{k}f.

By using the spectral projection estimates of the second author
[
20
]
, we have

(2.9)

\|P_{k}f\|_{L^{q}_{x}}\leq Ck^{\sigma(q)}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Thus
it is not hard to see that for any fixed
t
,

(2.10)

\|\tilde{P}_{k}f\|_{L^{q}_{x}}\leq C\rho(t)k^{\sigma(q)}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Also, by (
2.6
) and the Fourier support property of
\rho
, for each fixed
k,\ell
, there exist some uniform constant
C_{0}
such that the
t
-Fourier transform of

\tilde{P}_{k}f\tilde{P}_{\ell}f
is supported in

(2.11)

[(k+\frac{\alpha}{4})^{2}+(\ell+\frac{\alpha}{4})^{2}-C_{0},(k+\frac{\alpha}{4})^{2}+(\ell+\frac{\alpha}{4})^{2}+C_{0}],

Next, let us fix

(2.12)

\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}((-1,1))\quad\text{satisfying }\,\,1\equiv\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\eta(\tau-j),

and define
\eta_{j}(D_{t})=\eta(D_{t}-j)
, which is essentially Fourier restriction to
the interval
[j-1,j+1]
. By Bernstein’s inequality, it is not hard to see that

(2.13)

\|\eta_{j}(D_{t})\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{q}}\lesssim 1,\,\,\,1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty.

By (
2.7
) and Plancherel’s theorem, we have

(2.14)

\displaystyle\|\rho(t)e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\beta(P/\lambda)f\|^{2}_{L^{q}_{x}L^{4}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}\times M)}

\displaystyle=\big\|\sum_{k,l=\lambda/4}^{4\lambda}\tilde{P}_{k}f\tilde{P}_{\ell}f\big\|_{L^{q/2}_{x}L^{2}_{t}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\sum_{(k,\ell)\in I_{j}}\eta_{j}(D_{t})\tilde{P}_{k}f\tilde{P}_{\ell}f\big\|_{L^{q/2}_{x}\ell_{j}^{2}L^{2}_{t}},

where

(2.15)

(k,\ell)\in I_{j},\,\,\,\text{if}\,\,\lambda/4\leq k,\ell\leq 4\lambda\\
\text{and}\,\,\,(k+\frac{\alpha}{4})^{2}+(\ell+\frac{\alpha}{4})^{2}\in[j-C_{0}-1,j+C_{0}+1].

It is straightforward to check that each fixed pair
(k,\ell)
is only contained in a set
I_{j}
for finitely many
j
, and
\eta_{j}(D_{t})\tilde{P}_{k}f\tilde{P}_{\ell}f
is nonzero only when
j\in[\lambda^{2}/32,32\lambda^{2}]
and
(k,\ell)\in I_{j}
. Also, it follows from classical number theory that
we have the cardinality estimate

\#I_{j}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon},\,\forall\varepsilon>0
(see e.g.,
[
12
]
).

By (
2.13
) and Minkowski inequality, we have

(2.16)

\displaystyle\big\|\sum_{(k,\ell)\in I_{j}}\eta_{j}(D_{t})\tilde{P}_{k}f\tilde{P}_{\ell}f\big\|_{L^{q/2}_{x}\ell_{j}^{2}L^{2}_{t}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\big\|\sum_{(k,\ell)\in I_{j}}\tilde{P}_{k}f\tilde{P}_{\ell}f\big\|_{L^{q/2}_{x}\ell_{j}^{2}L^{1}_{t}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\big\|\sum_{(k,\ell)\in I_{j}}\tilde{P}_{k}f\tilde{P}_{\ell}f\big\|_{L^{1}_{t}L^{q/2}_{x}\ell_{j}^{2}}.

Note that

\displaystyle\big\|\sum_{(k,\ell)\in I_{j}}\tilde{P}_{k}f\tilde{P}_{\ell}f\big\|_{\ell_{j}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{j\in[\lambda^{2}/32,32\lambda^{2}]}\big|\sum_{(k,\ell)\in I_{j}}\tilde{P}_{k}f\tilde{P}_{\ell}f\big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum_{j\in[\lambda^{2}/32,32\lambda^{2}]}\#I_{j}\cdot\sum_{(k,\ell)\in I_{j}}\big|\tilde{P}_{k}f\tilde{P}_{\ell}f\big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon}\sum_{k\in[\lambda/10,10\lambda]}\big|\tilde{P}_{k}f\big|^{2}.

Thus, by Minkowski’s inequality and (
2.10
)

\displaystyle\big\|\sum_{(k,\ell)\in I_{j}}\tilde{P}_{k}f\tilde{P}_{\ell}f\big\|_{L^{1}_{t}L^{q/2}_{x}\ell_{j}^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle C_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon}\big\|\big(\sum_{k\in[\lambda/10,10\lambda]}\big|\tilde{P}_{k}f\big|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t}L^{q}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon}\big(\sum_{k\in[\lambda/10,10\lambda]}\|\tilde{P}_{k}f\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{q}_{x}}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{2\sigma(q)+\varepsilon}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

which finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Now we shall see how we can apply Lemma
2.1
to prove (
2.3
) except
for the cases where
d=2
and
q\in(4,6)
.

To see this, it is natural to separately consider the following two cases.

(i) First, let us prove (
2.3
) if

d\geq 3
,
2\leq q\leq\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}
or
d=2
,
2\leq q\leq 4
.
In this case,
we see from (
1.8
) and (
1.9
) that
\mu(q)=\sigma(q)
. Also,
q\leq 4
.
Thus, if we integrate the
t
-variable first and use Hölder’s inequality, it follows from Lemma
2.1
that

(2.17)

\|e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\beta(P/\lambda)f\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}(M\times[0,1]))}\\
\leq\|e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\beta(P/\lambda)f\|_{L^{q}_{x}L^{4}_{t}(M\times[0,1])}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\sigma(q)+\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

(ii) The other case where we can use Lemma
2.1
is when

d\geq 2
,
q\geq\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}
. In this case,

\mu(q)=s_{{\mathrm{Sob}}}=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+2}{q}
. Consequently,
it suffices to prove that whenever we fix
\rho\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}})

satisfying
\text{supp }\hat{\rho}\subset(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
, we have

(2.18)

\|\rho(t)e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\beta(P/\lambda)f\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}(M\times{\mathbb{R}})}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+2}{q}+\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)},\,\,\,\forall\varepsilon>0.

Let us fix

(2.19)

\tilde{\beta}\in C^{\infty}_{0}((1/8,8))\quad\text{satisfying }\,\,\tilde{\beta}=1\,\,\text{on }\,\,[1/6,6],

then by
Bernstein’s inequality, we have

(2.20)

\|\tilde{\beta}(D_{t}/\lambda^{2})\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{q}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{2}{p}-\frac{2}{q}},\,\,\,1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty.

Also, by (
2.1
) and the support property of
\rho
, we have

(2.21)

\rho(t)e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\beta(P/\lambda)f=\tilde{\beta}(D_{t}/\lambda^{2})\rho(t)e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\beta(P/\lambda)f.

Thus, if we use (
2.5
) for
q>4
and (
2.20
), we have

(2.22)

\displaystyle\|\rho(t)e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\beta(P/\lambda)f\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}(M\times{\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{q}}\|\rho(t)e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\beta(P/\lambda)f\|_{L^{q}_{x}L^{4}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}\times M)}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{q}}\lambda^{\sigma(q)+\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)},

as desired since
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{q}+\sigma(q)=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{q}+\frac{d-1}{2}-\frac{d}{q}=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+2}{q}
when
q\geq\tfrac{2(d+1)}{d-1}
.

The remaining case when
d=2,\,4<q<6
requires considerably more work,
since, for this range of exponents

4.
Sharpness of Theorem
1.1

To see that (
1.7
) is sharp on any Zoll manifold, note that if we fix

\rho\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}})
satisfying
\rho(0)=1
and
\text{supp }\hat{\rho}\subset(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
as before, (
1.7
) implies

(4.1)

\|\rho(t)e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\eta(P/k)f\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}(M\times{\mathbb{R}})}\leq C_{s}k^{s}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)},\,\,\,\forall s>\mu(q),

if
k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and

(4.2)

\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}((-1,1))\quad\text{with }\,\,\eta(t)=1,\,\,\,|t|\leq 1/2.

Now let us define
\chi_{k}
to be the spectral projection operator for
\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}
onto the unit length interval

(4.3)

I_{k}=[k+\frac{\alpha-2}{4},k+\frac{\alpha+2}{4}].

By (
2.6
), the eigenvalues of
\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}
are clustered around a
A/k
neighborhood of
k+\frac{\alpha}{4}
, thus it is straightforward to check that
the Fourier transform of

(4.4)

\rho(t)e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\eta(P/k)\chi_{k}f.

in the
t
-variable is supported in

(4.5)

[(k+\frac{\alpha}{4})^{2}-C_{0},(k+\frac{\alpha}{4})^{2}+C_{0}],

Thus, since these intervals are of bounded length, by Bernstein’s inequality and (
1.7
),

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|\rho(t)e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\eta(P/k)\chi_{k}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(M\times{\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\rho(t)e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\eta(P/k)\chi_{k}f\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}(M\times{\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{s}k^{s}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)},\,\,\,\forall s>\mu(q).

However, when
t=0
, for
k\geq C
being large enough, we have

(4.7)

\rho(t)e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\eta(P/k)\chi_{k}f=\eta(P/k)\chi_{k}f=\chi_{k}f,

which yields

(4.8)

\|\chi_{k}f\|_{L^{q}_{x}(M)}\leq C_{s}k^{s}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)},\,\,\,\forall s>\mu(q).

As was shown in
[
22
]
, if
\sigma(q)
is defined as in (
2.4
), the unit-band spectral estimate (
4.8
) fails for
s<\sigma(q)
on any
compact manifold, regardless of the geometry. Consequently, we have
\mu(q)\geq\sigma(q)
.

We shall also remark that in the special case of standard round sphere
S^{n}
, by letting
f=Q_{\lambda}
, the highest weight spherical harmonic with eigenvalue
\lambda
for
2\leq q\leq\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}
, and
f=Z_{\lambda}
, the zonal spherical function for
q>\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}
, we have

(4.9)

\|e^{-it\Delta_{g}}f\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}(I\times M)}=\|e^{it\lambda^{2}}f\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}(I\times M)}\approx\|f\|_{L^{q}_{x}(M)}\approx\lambda^{\sigma(q)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

This implies that (
1.7
) can not hold for
s<\sigma(q)
.
See e.g.,
[
1
]
for more detailed calculations related to the
L^{q}
norms of spherical harmonics.

It remains to show
\mu(q)\geq\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+2}{q}
if (
1.7
) holds.
To see this, fix
x_{0}\in M
, let
f_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{-d/2}\sum_{j}\beta(\lambda_{j}/\lambda)e_{j}(x)\overline{e_{j}(x_{0})}
for
\beta
as in (
2.1
), which is
\lambda^{-d/2}K(x,x_{0})
if
K(x,y)
denotes the kernel of the multiplier operator
\beta(P/\lambda)
. For this choice of
f_{\lambda}
, we have

(4.10)

\displaystyle\big|e^{-it\Delta_{g}}f_{\lambda}(x_{0})\big|

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-d/2}\big|\sum_{j}\beta(\lambda_{j}/\lambda)e^{it\lambda_{j}^{2}}|e_{j}(x_{0})|^{2}\big|

\displaystyle\approx\lambda^{d/2}\,\,\text{if}\,\,\,|t|\leq\delta\lambda^{-2},

for some fixed small
\delta
. This follows from the fact that by the pointwise Weyl formula (see e.g.,
[
21
]
)

\big|\sum_{j}\beta(\lambda_{j}/\lambda)|e_{j}(x_{0})|^{2}\big|\approx\lambda^{d},\,\,\text{for any}\,\,x_{0}\in M,

as well as

\text{Re}(e^{it\lambda_{j}^{2}})\geq 1/2\,\,\text{if}\,\,\,|t|\leq\delta\lambda^{-2}\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\lambda_{j}\approx\lambda.

As a result, for any
2\leq q\leq\infty
,

(4.11)

\|e^{-it\Delta_{g}}f_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}(M\times[0,1])}\gtrsim\|e^{-it\Delta_{g}}f_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}(M\times[0,\delta\lambda^{-2}])}\approx\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{2}{q}}.

If we let
\tilde{\beta}\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
which equals 1 in a neighborhood of (1/2,2), then
\tilde{\beta}(P/\lambda)f_{\lambda}=f_{\lambda}
, and we have the Bernstein inequality

(4.12)

\|\tilde{\beta}(P/\lambda)\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{q}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{d}{p}-\frac{d}{q}},\,\,\,1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty,

which implies

(4.13)

\|e^{-it\Delta_{g}}f_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}(M\times[0,1])}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{d}{q}}\|e^{-it\Delta_{g}}f_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}(M\times[0,1])}.

On the other hand, by
L^{2}
orthogonality and the pointwise Weyl formula above, we have

(4.14)

\|f_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)}\approx 1.

If we combine (
4.11
) (
4.13
) and (
4.14
), we have

(4.15)

\|e^{-it\Delta_{g}}f_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}(M\times[0,1])}\gtrsim\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+2}{q}}\|f_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)},

which implies that
s\geq\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+2}{q}
.

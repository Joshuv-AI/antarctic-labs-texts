BoRP : Bootstrapped Regression Probing for Scalable and Human-Aligned LLM Evaluation
Peng Sun, Xiangyu Zhang, and Duan Wu

Abstract
Accurate evaluation of user satisfaction is critical for iterative development of conversational AI. However, for open-ended assistants, traditional A/B testing lacks reliable metrics: explicit feedback is sparse, while implicit metrics are ambiguous. To bridge this gap, we introduce BoRP ( Bo otstrapped R egression P robing), a scalable framework for high-fidelity satisfaction evaluation. Unlike generative approaches, BoRP leverages the geometric properties of LLM latent space. It employs a polarization-index-based bootstrapping mechanism to automate rubric generation and utilizes Partial Least Squares (PLS) to map hidden states to continuous scores. Experiments on industrial datasets show that BoRP (Qwen3-8B/14B) significantly outperforms generative baselines (even Qwen3-Max) in alignment with human judgments. Furthermore, BoRP reduces inference costs by orders of magnitude, enabling full-scale monitoring and highly sensitive A/B testing via CUPED.

Introduction

With the advent of LLMs, conversational agents have shifted towards unstructured, open-ended interactions, enabling users to fulfill diverse intents. This flexibility, however, complicates satisfaction quantification. Traditional heuristic metrics (e.g., Session Duration, Turn Counts) suffer from ambiguity
6
: prolonged engagement can signal either immersion or confusion. Conversely, posterior metrics like Retention (R1/R7) act as lagging indicators, lacking the sensitivity required for rapid strategy iteration. This creates a critical "Metric Gap": the industry urgently needs a framework to capture immediate satisfaction with high fidelity.

The Dilemma of Existing Paradigms

Existing evaluation methodologies primarily fall into four categories, yet each hits a "hard wall" in industrial deployment.

Manual Evaluation
, while serving as the gold standard, is blocked by the Wall of Scalability. The sparsity of human labels results in insufficient statistical power for A/B testing
9
. Consequently, detecting small but meaningful strategy improvements requires impractically long experiment durations.

Generative Evaluation (LLM-as-a-Judge)
suffers from an irreconcilable Accuracy-Cost Trade-off. Using SOTA models (e.g., GPT-4) incurs prohibitive costs for full-scale monitoring. Conversely, smaller models (e.g., 7B-14B) often exhibit an "Alignment Tax"—a central tendency bias that suppresses extreme judgments. Furthermore, recent research
23
reveals the intrinsic instability of generative judgments, where stochastic decoding leads to inconsistent scores, necessitating expensive aggregation strategies to ensure reliability.

Fine-tuned Evaluators (Specialized Judges & BERT)
attempt to bridge the gap by training on evaluation-specific datasets. Recent works like Prometheus
8
, JudgeLM
22
, and JudgeLRM
18
have shown promise by fine-tuning LLMs on massive feedback datasets (often
>100k
samples) . However, this paradigm faces two hurdles: (1) Data Scalability: Curating such large-scale, high-quality reasoning traces is prohibitively expensive for every new business domain. (2) Inference Cost: Being generative in nature, they still incur high decoding latency. On the other end, traditional discriminative fine-tuning (e.g., BERT) is fast but limited by a Cognitive Bottleneck, lacking the reasoning depth to evaluate complex multi-turn logic in open-ended chat.

Conventional Probing
(typically linear classifiers or PCA), while efficient, often settles for Coarse Classification. Even recent works
11
focusing on preference extraction remain limited to pairwise ranking or binary judgments. Unsupervised probes are easily distracted by "Topic Residuals"—where the high variance of conversation topics overshadows subtle sentiment signals
2
, making them unsuitable for fine-grained ordinal regression in industrial settings.

Present Work

To bridge these gaps, we introduce
BoRP
(
Bo
otstrapped
R
egression
P
robing), an end-to-end framework designed to break the "impossible triangle" of quality, cost, and coverage in industrial evaluation. We reframe evaluation not as generation, but as a high-fidelity regression task within the latent space.

Contributions

Our main contributions are four-fold:

•

Methodological Innovation:
We propose a Geometry-Aware Regression approach. By introducing Partial Least Squares (PLS)
17
and a Polarization-guided resampling strategy, we effectively identify satisfaction-related subspaces within high-dimensional noise, outperforming PCA and generative baselines in fine-grained scoring.

•

Data Efficiency:
We design a Probe-Guided Bootstrapping mechanism. By defining a Polarization Index to unsupervisedly mine extreme samples and employing ensemble distillation, we automate rubric generation, significantly reducing the reliance on expert knowledge.

•

System Performance:
We implement a high-throughput inference engine leveraging Suffix-Only Probing and KV-Cache Reuse. This achieves an industrial-scale throughput of approx. 1.4M sessions/day on a single GPU, reducing costs by two orders of magnitude compared to GPT-4.

•

Industrial Impact:
We validate BoRP in large-scale online A/B testing. Combined with
CUPED
, our framework demonstrates superior sensitivity, effectively guiding rapid product iteration.

Related Work

LLM-as-a-Judge & Specialized Models

Evaluating open-ended generation has shifted from n-gram metrics (BLEU) to model-based evaluation. Early works prompted proprietary models like GPT-4
20
;
3
. Recent efforts focus on fine-tuning specialized judge models, such as Prometheus
8
, JudgeLM
22
, and JudgeLRM
18
, to improve alignment. While effective, these generative approaches suffer from high inference latency and inherent biases (e.g., position bias
14
, verbosity bias
12
). Furthermore, recent studies highlight the calibration issues and instability of generative scoring
13
;
23
. BoRP addresses these by bypassing generation entirely.

Automated Rubric Generation

Defining high-quality rubrics is a bottleneck for evaluation. Recent works like OpenRubrics
19
, Auto-Rubric
15
, and Rubrics-as-Rewards
10
explore using LLMs to synthesize or refine evaluation criteria. While promising, these methods typically rely on prompting strategies (e.g., "Write a rubric for X"). In contrast, BoRP introduces a Geometry-Guided approach: we use the
Polarization Index
to mine real-world extreme samples from unlabeled data, ensuring that the generated rubrics are grounded in the actual distribution of the industrial traffic.

Latent Probing & Uncertainty

Probing classifiers have been used to interpret internal representations
1
, detecting truthfulness
2
or hallucinations. However, most probes focus on binary classification. BoRP advances this by introducing PLS regression for fine-grained ordinal scoring (1-5 scale). Additionally, leveraging insights from uncertainty estimation surveys
7
;
4
, we explore layer-wise consistency as a computation-free proxy for model confidence, enabling reliable human-in-the-loop deployment.

Efficient Inference

Techniques like
FlashAttention

5
and
RadixAttention
(SGLang)
21
have revolutionized throughput. Our work explicitly leverages the KV-Cache separation property of these frameworks to implement our "Suffix-Only" architecture, maximizing the ROI of inference infrastructure.

Methodology

We propose
BoRP
, a geometry-aware framework for scalable LLM evaluation. As illustrated in Figure
1
, the system architecture comprises a Bootstrapping Engine (Figure
1
Left) and a Probing Engine (Figure
1
Right). We detail the mathematical formulation of latent representations in Sec
3.1

3.2
), regression algorithm (Sec

).

3.1
Preliminaries: Contrastive Representation

Following
2
, we extract representations from the model’s internal states. For a dialogue
H
and query
U
, we construct a positive prompt
P_{pos}
and a negative prompt
P_{neg}
(using generic templates initially). We extract the hidden states of the final token at the last layer:

\mathbf{v}_{diff}=\mathcal{M}(H,U,P_{pos})-\mathcal{M}(H,U,P_{neg})

(1)

This difference vector
\mathbf{v}_{diff}
is designed to neutralize common-mode noise (e.g., syntax) while isolating the sentiment direction. The geometric properties of
\mathbf{v}_{diff}
(Norm and Distance) will be further exploited in the subsequent bootstrapping phase.

3.2
Probe-Guided Criteria Bootstrapping

Curating high-quality, behavior-oriented rubrics is traditionally a labor-intensive process requiring expert domain knowledge. To resolve the "cold-start" lack of rubrics, we propose an unsupervised pipeline (Figure
1
Left).

Phase 1: Blind Probing

Lacking specific rubrics, we exploit the model’s inductive bias. We use generic semantic prompts (e.g., "Performance is
Excellent/Terrible
", see Appendix
A
)
to extract a coarse
\mathbf{v}_{diff}
using Eq. (1). This vector captures the model’s innate intuition about quality without detailed instructions.

Phase 2: Polarization Mining

To design an effective mining strategy, we first conducted an exploratory analysis on a validation set. As illustrated in Figure
2
, we observe two key geometric properties of
\mathbf{v}_{diff}
:

1.

Intensity (Figure
2
a):
The Euclidean norm
\|\mathbf{v}_{diff}\|_{2}
exhibits a step-wise positive correlation with satisfaction scores.

2.

Outlierness (Figure
2
b):
The Manhattan distance to the centroid follows a U-shaped distribution, where extreme scores are geometrically distant from the center.

Inspired by these findings, we formulate the
Polarization Index (PI)
to fuse intensity and outlierness:

PI(x)=\mathcal{Z}(\|\mathbf{v}_{diff}(x)\|_{2})\times\mathcal{Z}(\|\mathbf{v}_{diff}(x)-\mathbf{c}\|_{1})

(2)

where we use Manhattan distance for robustness. As shown in Figure
2
c, PI effectively pushes extreme scores to the tails. By sampling from the top-
K
and bottom-
K
PI regions, we boost the mining efficiency of informative cases by an order of magnitude.

Phase 3: Inverse Generation & Deployment

We feed the mined extreme samples into a teacher model (e.g., Qwen3-Max) to reverse-engineer detailed behavior-oriented rubrics. To ensure robustness, we employ an Ensemble Distillation strategy (see Appendix
A
for the ensemble distillation meta-prompts).
This bootstrapped rubric serves as the Standardization Infrastructure: it aligns the teacher model (for pseudo-labeling) and human experts (for verification), ensuring high Inter-Annotator Agreement. Additionally, it provides a "warm-start" baseline for operations teams to fine-tune specific logic (e.g., penalizing "error loops"). Note that bootstrapping is a one-off initialization, incurring zero inference cost.

3.3
Geometry-Aware Regression Probing

With the bootstrapped rubrics, we proceed to curate the training data and train the scorer.

Human-in-the-Loop Annotation

Instead of costly full-manual annotation, we adopt a Human-in-the-Loop strategy. We first use the Teacher LLM (prompted with the bootstrapped rubrics) to generate pseudo-labels for a small subset of data. Human experts then verify and correct these labels. This process ensures high data quality (IAA > 0.90) with minimal effort, providing a reliable supervision signal for the probe.

Refined Extraction

Using these verified samples, we replace the generic blind prompts with the specific, behavior-oriented rubrics generated in Section
3.2
(see Appendix
A
for the final probing prompt). This extracts a
Refined

\mathbf{v}_{diff}
that captures fine-grained nuances (e.g., distinguishing "polite refusal" from "impolite rejection").

Geometric Resampling

Industrial datasets are heavily skewed towards median scores. Training on such data leads to mode collapse. We propose a Geometric Resampling strategy: down-sampling high-density regions (low distance
D_{c}
) and up-sampling low-density tails. Experiments in Sec
5.1
confirm that this step is crucial for preventing performance collapse.

PLS Regression & Feature Augmentation

We employ PLS regression to map the high-dimensional vector to a continuous score. Unlike PCA (unsupervised), PLS seeks latent directions that maximize covariance with the target score, effectively filtering out topic noise.
To further boost performance in small-sample regimes, we exploit the geometric insights from Figure
2
a: the Norm (
\|\mathbf{v}_{diff}\|
) and Distance (
D_{centroid}
) are explicitly concatenated with the latent vector. The augmented input
\tilde{\mathbf{v}}=[\mathbf{v}_{diff};\|\mathbf{v}_{diff}\|;D_{centroid}]
allows the linear PLS model to efficiently capture the non-linear intensity signals observed in the exploratory analysis.

Uncertainty via Layer-wise Consistency

Beyond point estimation, industrial deployment requires reliability scores. We exploit the model’s internal dynamics by training two separate PLS heads: one on the intermediate layer (e.g., Layer 15, representing
intuition
) and one on the final layer (e.g., Layer 40, representing
reasoning
). We define the Uncertainty Score
\mathcal{U}
as the absolute deviation between these layers:

\mathcal{U}(x)=|S_{final}(x)-S_{mid}(x)|

(3)

A high
\mathcal{U}
indicates internal cognitive conflict, serving as an unsupervised proxy for prediction error (analyzed in Sec
5.2
).

3.4
High-Throughput Inference Engine

We optimize the inference architecture (Figure
1
Right) to maximize throughput.

Suffix-Only Probing via RadixAttention

Standard evaluation requires re-processing the dialogue history for each rubric. We leverage SGLang’s RadixAttention
21
, which automatically manages a prefix tree of KV caches.
The system computes the shared Prefix (History) once. When evaluating
N
different rubrics, SGLang automatically hits the cached prefix node and only computes the task-specific Suffixes. This reduces complexity from
O(N\cdot L_{total})
to
O(L_{prefix}+N\cdot L_{suffix})
.

Context Compression

For ultra-long contexts, we further apply "Middle-Out" Compression, truncating the redundant middle parts of history. Experiments indicate this reduces prefill costs by
\sim 64\%
without compromising accuracy due to the Recency Bias of LLMs.

Hybrid Deployment Strategy

To maximize ROI, we tailor the deployment precision based on the model size and hardware constraints:

•

Larger Models (>10B):
These are typically memory-bound due to KV-cache consumption. We apply INT4-AWQ quantization to minimize weight footprint, allowing for significantly larger batch sizes (concurrency) within the same GPU memory budget.

•

Smaller Models (<10B):
These are often compute-bound during the prefill phase. Since the weights fit comfortably in memory, we deploy them in BF16 precision to avoid the computational overhead of dequantization kernels, which our benchmarks show can degrade throughput for smaller architectures.

Ultimately, this architecture achieves an industrial-scale throughput of
approx. 1.4M sessions/day
(using 8B), reducing costs by

An intriguing phenomenon is observed in the generative baselines: Gen (Qwen3-Max) scores a lower K-alpha (0.153) than the weaker Gen (14B) (0.326), yet its Pearson correlation is significantly higher (0.622 vs. 0.451).

•

Central Tendency Bias (Qwen3-14B): The smaller model adopts a conservative strategy, clustering predictions around the median to minimize risk. While this artificially boosts K-alpha slightly, it lacks true discriminative power.

5.3
Boosting A/B Testing

We integrated BoRP scores as covariates in
CUPED
to reduce A/B testing variance. Based on 2,300 active users, we observed a correlation of
\rho=0.212
between experiment and historical scores. While moderate, this yields a statistically significant variance reduction of 4.5%. Specifically, for detecting a 0.2% lift, the required sample size drops by approx. 10,000 users (from 235k to 225k). This represents a "free efficiency gain" that scales linearly with traffic, establishing BoRP as a sensitivity multiplier.

6
Conclusion

We introduced BoRP, a geometry-aware framework that breaks the "impossible triangle" of LLM evaluation. By reframing evaluation as a regression task via PLS and automating rubric generation through polarization mining, BoRP achieves expert-level fidelity (K-alpha
\approx
0.8) without human intervention. Furthermore, our Suffix-Only inference engine delivers an industrial-scale throughput of approx. 1.4M sessions/day, enabling cost-effective full-traffic monitoring and sensitive A/B testing via CUPED.
While highly effective, BoRP’s linear nature may limit its reasoning depth for complex tasks. Future work will explore multi-layer probe fusion and multi-modal extensions to address these challenges.

The behavior-oriented rubric used for our main experiments (generated via bootstrapping) is detailed in Table
7
.

Score

Criteria

5

Explicit Affirmation:
Clearly gives a positive evaluation or strong praise for the content or quality of the answer. Characteristics: Uses high-energy words like "Awesome," "Thank you very much," "Well summarized," "This information is very useful."

4

Logical Continuation/Successful Recovery:
The user does not praise strongly but accepts the answer behaviorally. Includes:
- In-depth Follow-up: Asking logically related extension questions based on the answer.
- Task Continuation: Initiating multiple independent but similar tasks in succession.
- Flaws do not obscure virtues: Despite local corrections or a poor start, the user eventually expresses clear approval.

3

Neutral Reception:
No merit or fault, or the main intent is vague. Includes:
- The user expresses neither positive nor negative views on the answer.
- Topic Jumping: Random questions with no logical connection.

2

Friction/Struggle:
There are explicit obstacles in the main interaction process. Includes:
- Questioning/Correction: Pointing out errors and requesting rewrites, or proposing specific modifications/additions.
- Repeating Questions: Repeating the same question because the answer was not obtained.
- Deterioration: Ending the conversation with negative feedback after a promising start.

1

Explicit Negation:
Total denial or emotional confrontation. Characteristics: Insults, sarcasm, declaring it "completely useless."

Regression Head Configuration

We construct the input features by concatenating the difference vector (dim=
D_{model}
) with two scalar geometric features (Norm and Distance), resulting in a
(D_{model}+2)
-dimensional vector.
For
Qwen3-14B
(
D_{model}=5120
) and
Qwen3-8B
(
D_{model}=4096
), the inputs are 5122-dim and 4098-dim respectively.
The regressor is implemented using
sklearn.cross_decomposition.PLSRegression
with
n\_components=5
and
scale=True
.

Quantization Settings

For production deployment, we adopt a hybrid strategy:

•

Qwen3-14B:
We utilize pre-quantized
AWQ-Int4
checkpoints (e.g.,
AngelSlim/Qwen3-14b-int4-awq
). The weights are quantized to 4-bit integers with a group size of 128, loaded directly via the SGLang kernel.

•

Qwen3-8B:
We deploy in native
bfloat16 (BF16)
precision to avoid dequantization overhead, as it fits within the A100 memory budget without compression.

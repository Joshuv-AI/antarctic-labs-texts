WavePhaseNet: A DFT-Based Method for Constructing Semantic Conceptual Hierarchy Structures (SCHS)
Kiyotaka Kasubuchi, Kazuo Fukiya

Abstract
In this paper, we reformulate the Transformer/Attention mechanisms in Large Language Models (LLMs) from the perspectives of measure theory and frequency analysis, and theoretically clarify the inevitability of hallucination as a structural limitation. In particular, we focus on the fact that the embedding space functions as a conditional expectation over a σ \sigma -algebra, and demonstrate that the failure of this framework to be isomorphic to the semantic truth set is the fundamental cause of the breakdown of logical consistency. Based on this observation, we propose WavePhaseNet , a method for explicitly constructing a Semantic Conceptual Hierarchy Structure (SCHS) within the embedding space using the Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) . By applying a DFT along the sequence dimension, semantic information is decomposed into frequency bands, where low-frequency components correspond to global meaning and intent, and high-frequency components correspond to local syntax and expression. This staged separation enables precise semantic manipulation in a diagonalized space. As a concrete example, we show that the 24,576-dimensional high-dimensional embedding space, typified by GPT-4, exhibits a 1 / f 1/f spectral structure based on the self-similarity of language and Zipf’s law. Through cumulative energy analysis, we theoretically derive that the lower bound of “complete representation” is approximately 3,000 dimensions. This demonstrates that dimensionality reduction from 24,576 to 3,000 dimensions is possible while preserving meaning and intent, thereby enabling rigorous reasoning while suppressing hallucination. Finally, since the reduced low-dimensional embedding space is constructed by combining cohomological regularization principles over overlapping local windows, a graph structure can be introduced over the window covering, and a cochain complex can be defined. This allows inconsistencies among local inferences to be quantified as losses based on coboundaries. By applying harmonic projection based on Hodge theory, cohomology is positioned not as a purely topological invariant, but as a computable regularization principle for controlling semantic consistency, extracting maximally consistent global representations. The knowledge reasoning process thus maintains validity and rigor through the gluing of knowledge fragments.



1
Introduction

LLMs based on Transformers realize natural language generation as
autoregressive (AR) models
by sequentially approximating the conditional probability distribution of token sequences. While this framework demonstrates extremely high performance in grammatical consistency and expressive diversity, it inevitably produces erroneous generations—known as
hallucinations
—in scenarios requiring high levels of logical reasoning or factual consistency.

Reasoning in LLMs primarily relies on locally computed intermediate representations, and global semantic consistency is only implicitly guaranteed. Although the self-attention mechanism can handle long-range dependencies, it lacks a direct mechanism to suppress semantic inconsistencies arising between overlapping local contexts.

The purpose of this paper is to position hallucination not as a consequence of insufficient training or data defects, but as an inevitable phenomenon arising from the mathematical structure of the model itself, and to provide a reconstruction principle for the embedding space as a solution.

Meanwhile, spectral analysis of token embedding sequences has suggested that global semantic structure and local contextual syntactic structure can be separated
(
Lee-Thorp et al. 2021
)
. In this paper, we integrate these two perspectives and propose a new inference framework that combines spectrally extracted global intent with locally consistent regularization inspired by cohomology.

We introduce cochains/cohomology and a graph-based Hodge decomposition (Laplacian projection) over coverings of local window groups, incorporating inconsistencies (coboundaries) among local inferences (sections) into the loss function to perform
semantic gluing
. Practically, this can be implemented by introducing a Spectral Module and a Cohomology Regularizer into each layer and each head of a Transformer.

The first concept of this study is that by generating a
semantic-hierarchical token embedding matrix
—in which global themes/intents and local syntactic information are mixed—via DFT on the token embedding matrix, mappings and analyses shift from syntactic similarity to semantic neighborhood systems.

The second concept is that by
reducing
the obtained semantic-hierarchical token embedding matrix into semantic latent variables, compact yet low-cost semantic rigor is achieved while preserving semantic concepts.

The third concept demonstrates that a semantic knowledge reasoning process can be obtained through
harmonic gluing
, whereby among the set of local representations satisfying task loss, representations that minimize coboundary energy and are projected onto the harmonic subspace of the graph Laplacian induced by window coverings are selected.

2
Autoregressive Models and Embedding Spaces as
\sigma
-algebras

2.1
Measure-Theoretic Formulation of Autoregressive Models

An autoregressive model decomposes the joint distribution
P(X_{1},\ldots,X_{N})
as

P(X_{1},\ldots,X_{N})=\prod_{t=1}^{N}P(X_{t}\mid X_{<t})

(1)

Here, the information set generated by the past token sequence
X_{<t}
(a Borel set) forms a
\sigma
-algebra

\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sigma(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})

(2)

and the chained regression model takes the form

P(x_{t+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t})

(3)

Thus, an embedding vector
V
defined on the measurable space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{t},P)
can be interpreted as a
random variable over a
\sigma
-algebra
.

Therefore, the LLM as a whole is a
sequential approximator of conditional expectations over
\sigma
-algebras
, and its output is not a pointwise truth value but an average optimality over probability distributions (Lebesgue measure: expectation
E
).

2.2
Norm Structure of
L^{p}
Spaces

The embedding space is treated, via an inner product space, as a normed space characterized by the divergence between the truth manifold and the support of the learning distribution.

Each layer qualitatively approximates

E[\phi(X_{t+1})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]

(4)

The expected value of the loss function is

E[l(X_{t+1},\hat{X}_{t+1})]

(5)

Thus, the entire model resides in

L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)

(6)

The norm of this
L^{p}
space corresponds to the Lebesgue integral representing the expectation of the probability distribution

\|f\|_{p}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|f(\omega)|^{p}\,dP(\omega)\right)^{1/p}

(7)

This expression indicates that the model optimizes not pointwise truth values, but
plausible average optimality
over distributions.

2.3
Norms and the Minkowski Inequality

In
L^{p}
spaces, the Minkowski inequality

\|f+g\|_{p}\leq\|f\|_{p}+\|g\|_{p}

(8)

holds. This implies that different semantic components, hypotheses, and contexts can be linearly combined in the embedding space, while the norm remains stable (i.e., no gradient explosion).

This property allows
semantic superposition
, which is a source of the flexibility of generative AI.

On the other hand, the combination of this linearity and incompleteness inherently contains a structure that can generate non-existent propositions (erroneous outputs).

3
Mathematical Definition of Hallucination

3.1
Definition

Definition 1

(Hallucination)
.

Let
T
denote the truth set, and let
S
denote the support of the training distribution. A hallucination occurs when

S\not\subseteq T

(9)

In other words, when there exists a point
x\in S
such that

P(x)>0\quad\text{but}\quad x\notin T

(10)

the model generates a statement
x
with positive probability even though it is not true.

The embedding space
V
functions as a conditional expectation over the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}_{t}
, but when this is not isomorphic to
T
,

E[f\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\not\cong f|_{T}

(11)

holds. That is, the conditional expectation does not correctly project onto the truth manifold.

3.2
Impossibility of Isomorphism and Incompleteness

Theorem 2

(Structural Inevitability of Hallucination)
.

For the embedding space
V
and the truth manifold
T
, if there exists a non-negligible region where

\mu(S\setminus T)>0

(12)

then there necessarily exists a Cauchy sequence in
V
whose limit does not belong to
T
. In other words, hallucination is inevitable.

Proof.

Given that the training distribution has support
S
and
\mu(S\setminus T)>0
, there exist statements
\{x_{n}\}\subset S
that form a Cauchy sequence but converge to a point
x_{\infty}\notin T
.

The model generates
x_{n}
with positive probability, and by the completeness of
L^{p}
spaces,
x_{n}\to x_{\infty}
in norm. However,
x_{\infty}\notin T
, hence the output constitutes hallucination.
∎

3.3
Reformulation as a Defect on the Truth Manifold

From a geometric perspective, hallucination can be regarded as a phenomenon where holes (defects) exist on the truth manifold
T
, and the approximating distribution
S
attempts to fill these holes.

In cohomology theory, these defects correspond to non-trivial cohomology classes, and controlling them requires harmonic forms and Hodge decomposition.

4
Zipf’s Law and
1/f
Spectral Structure

4.1
Zipf’s Law in Natural Language

Zipf’s law states that in natural language corpora, word frequency follows a power-law distribution:

f(r)\propto r^{-\alpha},\quad\alpha\approx 1

(13)

where
r
denotes the rank of a word and
f(r)
denotes its frequency.

This self-similar structure in language naturally leads to a
1/f
spectrum in the frequency domain.

4.2
Spectral Structure of Embedding Spaces

When the DFT is applied to the token embedding matrix
V\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times T}
, the power spectral density exhibits

P(f)\propto\frac{1}{f^{\beta}},\quad\beta\approx 1

(14)

This
1/f
spectrum implies that:

•

Low-frequency components carry global semantic intent

•

High-frequency components encode local syntax and expression

4.3
Dimensional Bound via Cumulative Energy Analysis

Let
E_{n}
denote the energy of the
n
-th frequency component. The cumulative energy is defined as

S(k)=\frac{\sum_{n=0}^{k}E_{n}}{\sum_{n=0}^{T-1}E_{n}}

(15)

Under the
1/f
spectrum, we have

E_{n}\propto\frac{1}{n+1}

(16)

To preserve
S\geq 0.95
(95% energy), the required number of dimensions
k
satisfies

\frac{\sum_{n=0}^{k}\frac{1}{n+1}}{\sum_{n=0}^{T-1}\frac{1}{n+1}}\geq 0.95

(17)

For
T=24{,}576
(GPT-4 embedding dimension), solving this inequality yields

k\approx 3{,}000

(18)

This demonstrates that dimensionality reduction from 24,576 to approximately 3,000 dimensions is theoretically justified while preserving semantic content.

5
DFT-Based Semantic Conceptual Hierarchy Structure

5.1
Discrete Fourier Transform on Token Sequences

Given a token embedding matrix
V\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times T}
, we apply the DFT along the sequence dimension:

\tilde{V}_{n,k}=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}V_{n,t}e^{-2\pi ikt/T}

(19)

The frequency components
\tilde{V}_{n,k}
decompose the semantic information into:

•

Low frequencies (
k\ll T
): Global intent and themes

•

High frequencies (
k\sim T
): Local syntax and fine-grained expressions

5.2
Band Selection and Dimensionality Reduction

Based on cumulative energy analysis, we select the low-frequency band
\Omega
such that

|\Omega|\approx 3{,}000

(20)

The reduced embedding space is then

V_{\text{reduced}}=\text{IDFT}(\tilde{V}[:,\Omega])

(21)

This reduced space retains global semantic structure while suppressing local noise and syntactic variations.

5.3
Phase Information and Positional Structure

Unlike FNet
(
Lee-Thorp et al. 2021
)
, which uses DFT primarily for computational efficiency, WavePhaseNet explicitly preserves phase information:

\tilde{V}_{n,k}=|\tilde{V}_{n,k}|e^{i\phi_{n,k}}

(22)

The phase
\phi_{n,k}
encodes the positional structure of semantic concepts, enabling precise reconstruction of semantic relationships.

6
Cohomological Regularization and Harmonic Gluing

6.1
Window Covering and Local Sections

Let
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a covering of the token sequence by overlapping windows. For each window
U_{i}
, we extract a local representation (section)

s_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}

(23)

6.2
Graph Structure and Cochain Complex

Define a graph
G=(V,E)
where:

•

Vertices
V
correspond to windows
U_{i}

•

Edges
E
connect overlapping windows

The adjacency matrix
A
and graph Laplacian
L=D-A
(where
D
is the degree matrix) define the cochain complex.

6.3
Coboundary Operator and Consistency Loss

The coboundary operator
\delta
measures inconsistencies between local sections:

(\delta s)_{ij}=s_{j}-s_{i}\quad\text{for }(i,j)\in E

(24)

The cohomology regularization loss is

L_{\text{coh}}=\lambda\sum_{(i,j)\in E}\|s_{j}-s_{i}\|^{2}=\lambda s^{\top}(L\otimes I_{r})s

(25)

where
s=[s_{1};s_{2};\ldots;s_{N}]
is the concatenated vector of all local sections.

6.4
Harmonic Projection via Hodge Decomposition

The graph Laplacian admits a Hodge decomposition:

s=s_{\text{harmonic}}+s_{\text{gradient}}+s_{\text{curl}}

(26)

The harmonic component
s_{\text{harmonic}}
lies in the kernel of
L
and represents globally consistent representations.

By projecting onto the harmonic subspace via

s_{\text{harmonic}}=\arg\min_{s}s^{\top}(L\otimes I_{r})s

(27)

we extract maximally consistent global representations from local inferences.

6.5
Integration with Global Intent

Combining the spectral global intent
g
(from DFT) with local sections
s_{i}
, we add a coupling term:

L_{\text{coupling}}=\eta\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|s_{i}-P_{i}(g)\|^{2}

(28)

where
P_{i}(g)
projects the global intent onto the
i
-th window.

The total objective becomes:

L_{\text{total}}=L_{\text{task}}+\lambda L_{\text{coh}}+\eta L_{\text{coupling}}+\mu L_{\text{spec}}

(29)

7
Implementation and Integration into Transformers

7.1
Spectral Module (Per Layer and Head)

For each Transformer layer
l
and attention head
h
, we inject global intent as a residual:

\tilde{x}_{t}^{(l)}=x_{t}^{(l)}+\alpha^{(l)}g_{t}^{(l)}

(30)

where
g_{t}^{(l)}
is the low-frequency reconstruction and
\alpha^{(l)}
is a learnable scaling parameter.

7.2
Cohomology Regularizer Injection

At each layer, the intermediate output is segmented into windows and treated as
s_{i}^{(l)}
. The cohomology loss is added during training:

L_{\text{coh}}^{(l)}=\lambda^{(l)}{s^{(l)}}^{\top}(L\otimes I_{r})s^{(l)}+\eta^{(l)}\sum_{i}\|s_{i}^{(l)}-P_{i}(g^{(l)})\|_{2}^{2}

(31)

Gradients backpropagate through this term, encouraging attention and FFN weights to reflect local consistency.

7.3
Training Algorithm

1.

Forward pass: compute model intermediates; extract local
s_{i}^{(0)}
per window

2.

Compute DFT for selected windows:
\tilde{V}=VF
(or windowed FFT)

3.

Compute
E_{n}
,
p_{n}=E_{n}/\sum E

4.

Choose
\Omega
by threshold
S
or KL criterion

5.

Compute global intent
g=\text{IDFT}(\tilde{V}[:,\Omega])
(or per-position
G
)

6.

Compute cohomology regularization loss:

L_{\text{coh}}=\lambda s^{\top}(L\otimes I)s+\eta\sum_{i}\|s_{i}-P_{i}(g)\|^{2}

(32)

7.

Compute spectral KL loss:
L_{\text{spec}}=\mu D_{KL}(p\|p_{\Omega})

8.

Total loss
=L_{\text{task}}+L_{\text{coh}}+L_{\text{spec}}

9.

Backpropagate and update
\theta
(optionally update
s
via an inner-loop linear solve during inference-time harmonization)

7.4
Inference-Time Harmonization

At inference time, online harmonization can be performed by solving the linear system iteratively with fixed parameters to harmonize
s
and obtain the final output.

Comparison with FNet

FNet, proposed by
Lee-Thorp et al. 2021
, is a method that efficiently realizes global token mixing by replacing self-attention in the Transformer with the Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT). In FNet, Fourier transforms are applied along both the sequence dimension and the embedding dimension instead of attention, with the primary objective of reducing computational cost and improving speed.

The essence of FNet lies in:

•

Using the Fourier transform as a linear substitute for token mixing

•

Designing a lightweight model that approximates attention

In contrast, WavePhaseNet proposed in this study:

•

Positions DFT not merely as a token mixing mechanism,

•

But as a spectral decomposition operation for explicitly constructing a Semantic Conceptual Hierarchy Structure (SCHS).

In particular, this study differs fundamentally from FNet in both objective and theoretical framework, as it adopts
1/f

The structural differences can be summarized as follows:

•

FNet: “Uses all frequency components as they are”

•

Therefore, while this study builds upon FNet’s idea of “token mixing via Fourier transforms,” it extends this idea toward the explicit construction of semantic hierarchical structure and the suppression of hallucination.

Conclusion

This paper formulates hallucination in large language models (LLMs) as an inevitable phenomenon arising from their underlying mathematical structure, and presents a theoretical framework to suppress this issue by introducing a DFT-based Semantic Concept Hierarchy Structure (SCHS) into the embedding space.

In particular, the demonstration that the reduction from 24,576 dimensions to 3,000 dimensions is justified by cumulative energy analysis based on Zipf’s law and the
1/f
spectrum constitutes the core contribution of this proposal. WavePhaseNet enables semantic flow to be rigorously described within linear algebra, thereby opening a path toward next-generation reasoning-oriented language models.

This paper formulates a mathematical framework for semantic “harmonic gluing” of local reasoning by combining a DFT+Zipf-based “global-first” strategy with cohomology on coverings and a graph-based Hodge decomposition. We explicitly presented the objective function, linearized local update equations, harmonic representatives via Laplacian projection, and integration strategies into Transformers. The implementation is scalable through FFT, sparse linear algebra, and iterative solvers, and theoretical claims can be empirically validated by quantifying the contributions of
\lambda,\mu,\eta
via ablation studies. Practical examples indicate suitability for generation and evaluation in video, audio, and knowledge reasoning tasks.

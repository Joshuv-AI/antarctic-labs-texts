Manganese sulfide nanoparticles (MnS NPs) enhance morpho-physiological traits, productivity and oil yield in linseed under field trial
Authors: Jehangir Khan, Mah Rukh, Mohsin Khan, Muhammad Zia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The study was conducted to assess the effect of manganese sulfide (MnS) nanoparticles (NPs) on linseed (Linum usitatissimum L.) crop under field conditions. MnS NPs was applied in soil at the rate of 8 kg ha−1. Application of MnS NPs increased root and shoot length by 18.7 and 8.9%, respectively; and number branches and dry weight per plant 30.3 and 30.36%, respectively. Number of seed ball per plant, seeds per ball, and oil content raised by 52, 25.86, and 19.8%, respectively in MnS NPs treated plants as compared to control. Two folds increase in superoxide dismutase activity and 83.8% increase in peroxidase activity was observed in NPs treated plants as compared to control. Total phenolic content increased by 18.6% while 22.09% increase in total flavonoid content was observed in NPs treated plant shoots. Shoots of MnS NPs treated plants also showed 42.5% increase in DPPH radical scavenging activity. Total reducing potential and total antioxidant capacity also increased by 57% and 14.7%, respectively as compared to control. Atomic absorption spectroscopy showed increase in Mn accumulation in MnS NPs treated linseed plants followed by MnSO4 treated and control plants. Gas chromatography mass spectrometry analysis of oil extracted from linseed showed raise in the proportion of unsaturated fatty acids under MnS NPs treatment. FT-IR spectroscopy, 13C and 1H NMR confirmed these compositional shifts, thereby validating the GC-MS results. These findings indicate that MnS NPs can be applied as an efficient micronutrient delivery system to promote growth, antioxidant defense, yield, and oil content without disrupting the oil composition.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-48114-0.

Introduction

Linseed (Linum usitatissimum L.) of family Linaceae is an oilseed producing crop and originated from Mediterranean region and Southwest Asia1. L. usitatissimum is cultivated for various economic purposes2. In Pakistan, two distinct forms are cultivated during rabi season; flax, which is commercially grown for its fiber, and linseed that is known for its oil rich seeds3. Like other crops, its quality and productivity depend on nutrients availability in soil at adequate level4,5.

Among other nutrients, manganese (Mn) and sulfur (S) are important for linseed plant growth. Mn functions as a cofactor in many important enzymatic reactions i.e., photosynthesis6–8, carbohydrate metabolism and nitrate reduction9. It is also a key component in oxygen evolving complex in Photosystem II10,11. Manganese also plays an important role in lipid metabolism and redox homeostatsis12. Mn in chloroplast is involved in fatty acid biosynthesis as Mn deficient plants showed less photosynthesis which ultimately led to low supply of carbon skeleton for fatty acid biosynthesis13,51. Mn is also involved in the enzymatic antioxidative defense such superoxide dismutase, peroxidase, and enzymes of the ascorbate glutathione cyle, therefore limiting oxidative demage to membranes lipids14. Mn indirecly protects fatty acids from peroxidative damge by maintaning redox balance. Therefore, Mn-based nanomaterials may modulate antioxidant defense and lipid biosynthesis15. Sulfur is vital for amino acid biosynthesis16, enzymes activation17, and the production of sulfur containing metabolites that affect oil stability and flavor18,19.

Mn deficiency is generally observed in calcareous and alkaline soils, which decreases chlorophyll synthesis, impairs nitrogen metabolism, and limits the crop productivity20,21. While sulfur (S) deficiency negatively affects the oil composition and the amount of protein in plants22. Conventional Mn and S fertilizers are often disadvantageous due to their low solubility, nutrient immobilization, and environmental losses. These detrimental effects require repeated application of conventional fertilizers23. Nanobiotechnology offers an alternative strategy to resolves these issues by developing nano-fertilizers, materials with high surface area, and sustained release potential24,25. The nano-materials allow sustained nutrient supply, improve their uptake efficiency and reduce losses through leaching26,27. Metallic NPs increases photosynthetic efficiency, antioxidant activity, and nutrient bioaccumulation in different plants species28–30.

Studies have shown variable impact of Mn NPs on plants that associate with plant species and mode of their application. For instance, increase in root (52%) and shoot biomass (38%) was observed in mung bean7, while 22% increase in yield was found in solanum melongena31. In contrast, Mn NPs lacks significant effect on the root elongation of white mustard32 and lettuce33. In a disease resistance study, Mn NPs reduced pathogen progression in watermelon34. Mn2O3 NPs and MnSO4 enhanced seed germination, growth, and antioxidant activity in Artemisia annua through seed nano-priming and foliar supplementation35. Soil application of nano, bulk, and ionic Mn on wheat reveled that nano-Mn increased Mn concentration in shoot and grain, and altered soil nutrient dynamics, demonstrating distinct plant responses36.

Nanoparticles formulation offers a unique approach, combining Mn and S in a single delivery system. Despite advancements in nanoparticles studies, there is a lack of data on MnS NPs application on oilseed crops, particularly Linum usitatissimum under South Asian agro-ecological conditions. Studies are limited to cereals and vegetables which focus on the vegetative growth metrics. However, advanced techniques such as FT-IR, NMR, and GC-MS may offer powerful, non-destructive insight into compositional variations of biochemical composition of yield37–39.

The present study is first field based evaluation of MnS NPs as a soil applied micronutrient for linseed. This work determines field trail agronomic performance, antioxidant defense mechanisms, Mn uptake, and detailed oil compositional analyses. The innovative approach of using MnS NPs offers insight on their dual function as both agronomic enhancer and bioaccumulation agent for Mn, which is important for the plant health.

Materials and methods

Experimental condition

Manganese Sulfide (MnS) NPs were synthesized through co-precipitation method using 0.5 M Sodium sulfide (Na2S) and Manganese sulfate (MnSO4.H2O) as reported by Khan et al.40. This method produced 8 nm spherical MnS NPs characterized by SEM and XRD, FT-IR, and DLS (Fig. S1). The field trail was carried out at National Agriculture Research Centre (NARC) Islamabad (33.4° N, and 73.8° E), Pakistan. The site is situated in a humid subtropical climate zone as per Köppen climate classification system. Chickpea, soybean, arugula, sesame, and linseed are other major crops of the study site. The farmland was ploughed before cultivation for land preparation. The soil characteristics, meteorological data during study duration, and study area map of NARC are presented in Tables S1 and S2, and Fig. S2, respectively. The linseed (Var. Chandni) seeds obtained from oilseeds department NARC, Islamabad were sown in November 2022 and harvested in following May 2023.

Experimental layout and treatments

The experiment involved three treatments (NPs, salt, and control) in a randomized complete block design (RCBD). Each treatment had three replicates that resulted total of nine experimental units. Before seed sowing, a plot of 3.2 × 3.4 m (10.88 m2) was prepared, and the row length was selected as 3.0 m. Ridges were prepared with a row-to-row distance of 25 cm. After seed germination (approx. 15 days after seed sowing), weeds were manually removed and a distance of 10 cm between plants was maintained through crop thinning. The MnS NPs and MnSO4 were applied in soil at 10–15 leaf stage of linseed. This is a critical vegetative phase, characterized by rapid root proliferation, and high demanding nutrient uptake15. No other fertilizer or treatment was provided at any stage of plant growth.

Vegetative growth and yield attributes

At the end of growth cycle, after seven months of cultivation, vegetative and yield parameters were assessed at the time of harvesting. Ten plants per treatment were randomly selected within each plot to assess the effect of treatments. The vegetative attributes include root and shoot length, branches per plant, and dry weight. After crop harvesting, seed balls were manually removed and seed balls per plant, seeds per seed ball, and oil percentage was recorded.

Determination of Mn concentrating in plant material and soil

The Mn concentration in plant material (root and shoot) was determined by the method of Zafar et al.41. The plants were rinsed and dried in oven at 65 °C. The dried roots and shoots were separately powdered in an electric blender. The powder (0.25 g) was digested through wet-digestion procedure using HNO3, H2SO4, and HClO4 at a ratio of 5:1:0.5, respectively. The samples were heated in this acid solution in fume hood on a hot plate until the mixture become transparent. The mixture was cooled and the volume was raised up to 50 mL by adding deionized water. After filtration through Whatman filter paper No. 42, the filtrate was subjected to atomic absorption spectrometer (PerkinElmer, Waltham, MA, USA) to estimate Mn content in root and shoot.

Mn content in the soil samples was estimated by the method of Maurya et al.42. The soil was oven-dried and 1 g was transferred to a 250 mL Beaker. Following that, 15 mL of aqua regia (35% HCl and 70% HNO3 at a ratio of 3:1) was poured. The mixture was then boiled at 80 °C till it became transparent. The solution was then filtered, the volume was made 50 mL by adding deionized water, and finally examined through flame atomic absorption spectrometer for the determination of Mn concentration in the soil.

To determine total antioxidant capacity (TAC), 100 µL plant extract was mixed thoroughly with 900 µL of TAC reagent (H2SO4, NaH2PO4.H2O, (NH4)6Mo7O24.4H2O) in an Eppendorf tube. The tube was incubated for 90 min at 95 °C and cooled at room temperature. Finally, 200 µL was poured into a 96 well plate and absorbance was recorded at 630 nm by using micro plate reader. Ascorbic acid as positive control and DMSO was used as negative control. TAC was expressed as µg ascorbic acid equivalent per mg dry weight (µg AAE mg−1 DW)46.

For Total reducing potential (TRP), 100 µL plant extract was mixed with 200 µL of 0.2 M phosphate buffer (pH 6.6) and 250 µL of 1% K3Fe(CN)6 solution. The reaction mixture was incubated at 50 °C for 20 min and 200 µL of 10% CCl3COOH was added. The mixture was centrifuged for 10 min at 3000 rpm and 150 µL of supernatant was transferred to 96 well microplates and 50 µL of 0.1% FeCl3 solution was added. The absorbance was recorded at 630 nm through microplate reader. TRP was expressed as µg ascorbic acid equivalent per mg dry weight (µg AAE mg−1 DW). Ascorbic acid was used as positive control and DMSO as negative control47.

Non-enzymatic antioxidants such as total phenolic content (TPC) and total flavonoid contents (TFC) of the plant extract was quantified by method outlined by Khan et al.48. Briefly, 20 µL plant extract, standard (Gallic acid), and blank (DMSO) were separately transferred 96 well plate. In each well, 90 µL of 10 times diluted freshly prepared Folin–Ciocalteu reagent was poured. The plate was incubated for 5 min at room temperature and the 90 µL of Na2CO3 solution was added to mixture. The plate was again incubated for 1 h and absorbance was recorded at 650 nm wavelength using microplate reader. TPC is reported as µg gallic acid equivalent per mg dry weight (µg GAE mg−1 DW).

For TFC determination, 20 µL each of plant extract, standard (Quercetin), and blank (DMSO) were separately transferred to 96 well microplate and 10 µL of 10% AlCl3 solution was poured in each well. Following that, 10 µL of 1 M CH3COOK solutions was added and the final volume was made 200 µL by pouring 160 µL of dH2O. The microplate was incubated for 30 min at room temperature and the absorbance measured at 415 nm wavelength using microplate reader. TFC is reported as µg quercetin equivalent per mg dry weight (µg QE mg−1 DW).

Determination, extraction, and characterization of oil

Seeds (15 g) were oven-dried at 50 °C for 5 h and cooled at room temperature. The percent oil content in the seeds was recorded through nondestructive method using NMR analyzer (Newport 400 of Oxford Analytical Instruments) with a coil assembly of 40 mL. The dried seeds were filled in sample holder and the oil content was measured as percent oil in the seeds.

For the extraction of oil, the seeds were oven dried at 70 °C for 12 h. The oil was then extracted from the seeds using electrical expeller and subjected to characterization.

Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR) spectra of linseed oil were obtained through Burker-Platinum-ATR spectrometer with a scanning rate of 15 in the wave number range of 4000–500 cm−1. A drop of oil was directly kept on Universal diamond ATR crystal and the spectra were recorded.

The fatty acid methyl ester (FAMEs) composition of the extracted oil was estimated by the method described by Abdelrahman et al.49. Linseed oil (0.5 g) was mixed with 6 mL of 2% w/v methanolic NaOH solution, Methyl heptadecanoate (C17:0) was added as an internal standard prior to methylation, and heated for 10 min under reflux on water bath. Following that, 7 mL of 14% BF3 solution was poured into the mixture and refluxed again for 3 min. Heptane (5 ml) was added and the boiling was continued for 2 min. The reaction mixture was allowed to cool and diluted with 15 mL of saturated NaCl solution. The supernatant (FAMEs) was dried over anhydrous Na2SO4 for gas chromatography mass spectrometry (GC-MS).

GC-MS was performed through QP2010 Plus spectrometer fitted with capillary column coated with polar cyanopropyl stationary phase with film thickness of 0.2 μm, inner diameter of 0.25 mm, and length of 30 m. Helium gas of high purity (99%) was used as carrier at a flow rate of 1.5 mL/min. Detector and injector temperatures were maintained at 280 °C and 250 °C respectively. The column temperature was kept between 50 °C and 250 °C at a rate of 5 °C/min whereas oven temperature was maintained at 50 °C (hold time; 5 min). The oil sample (1 µl) was injected with split ratio of 20:1, accelerated at a rate of 5 °C/min to 250 for 5 min while the scan range for m/z was kept 40–800. The data was recorded through complete scan of mass spectra of oil. The compounds were identified via search of NIST17R.lib and NIST17M2 standard mass spectrum database. Quantification of fatty acids was carried out using an internal standard based calibration method, where their concentrations were calculated from the ratio of the peak area of each FAME to that of the internal standard.

13C and 1H nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra of linseed oil were recorded at 298 K on Bruker-Avance 300 MHz spectrometer. Deuterated chloroform (CDCl3) was used as a solvent while Tetramethylsilane (TMS) employed as internal standard. The linseed oil was thoroughly mixed in CDCl3 by vortexing and NMR spectrum was recorded after transferring oil to sample tube. 13C NMR (75 MHz) and 1H NMR (300 MHz) spectra of linseed oil was recorded with a flip angle of 30°, pulse duration of 30°, scan number of 8, and recycle delay of 1.89 s.

Statistical analysis

The study was conducted in the field using Randomized Complete Block Design (RCBD) to assess the effect of MnS nanoparticles and MnSO4 on the agronomic parameters and oil content in linseed. All the experiments were carried out in triplicate. Results are shown as mean ± standard error (SE). The results were statistically analyzed through one way analysis of variance (ANOVA), and to compare means, least significant difference (LSD) test was applied at p ≤ 0.05 using Statistix 10 (Analytical software, USA). The data was further subjected to principal component analysis using OriginPro 9.0 software to avulate the correlation among the studied parameters.

Results and discussion

Growth and yield performance

Application of MnS NPs in soil resulted in substantial improvement in the vegetative growth and yield attributes of linseed plants with respect to both untreated control and MnSO4 treatment (Fig. 1). Root length increased from 7.8 ± 0.39 cm in the control to 9.26 ± 0.46 cm with MnS NPs treatment, demonstrating 18.7% increase. MnS NPs treated linseed plants attained shoot height 8.9% longer than control and 6.6% longer than MnSO4 treated plant. Number of branches per plant was markedly increased by MnS NPs (6.6 ± 0.19) as compared to MnSO4 (5.6 ± 0.22) and the control (4.6 ± 0.13). Similarly, dry weight per plant raised from 5.96 g in non-treated control to 7.5 g in MnS NPs treated plants (Fig. 2). The increase in vegetative growth parameters are linked to increase in yield attributes. In MnS treated plants, increased root length improved nutrient uptake efficiency, while enhanced shoot length and number of branches expanded the photosynthetically active canopy, resulting in high assimilate production. These improvements led to higher flower initiation, fruit set, and seed development. Thus, increased seedball number, seed per ball, and overall yield is a cumulative outcome of coordinated enhancements observed under MnS NPs treatments. MnS NPs increased seedball number per plant by 52% with respect to control and 34.2% compare to MnSO4. Number of Seed per seedball was also increased in the MnS NP treated plant (8.66 ± 0.34) compared to control plants. Oil content showed a prominent increase (19.8%) compared to control and an 18.7% increase with respect to MnSO4 treatment. This increase highlights that MnS NPs not only enhances vegetative growth and seed number but also improves the economic value of the crop through better oil yield. The results also show that MnS NPs is responsible for the pronounced augmentation of agronomic parameters as MnSO4 improvements are comparatively modest. MnS NPs performed better than MnSO4 due to their unique physiochemical properties, sustained released, and increased bioavailability. Additionally, nano-size NPs allows better root absorption, cellular uptake, and translocation which ultimately lead to more efficient utilization of Mn in the plant tissues. Mn NPs are absorbed by the roots and translocated to the aerial parts of the plants where they interact with cellular apparatus and stimulate plant growth and antioxidant defense mechanism50.

MnSO4 may dissolve rapidly and lost fixation in the soil or leaching. Pradhan et al. found Mn NPs as better source of Mn compared to MnSO4 for Vigna radiata, and concluded that Mn NPs are biosafe for plants7. Improved growth, seed number, and yield have been reported in common bean upon exposure to MnO2 NPs51,52. Mn increases crop production by activating metabolically important enzymes53. The reduced oil content in Mn deficient plant seeds is due to decrease in photosynthesis and subsequent low supply of carbon skeleton for fatty acid biosynthesis54. Additionally, sulfur also associates with oil content and yield of the plants55,56. Sulfur augments acetyl CoA content and the enzyme Acetyl-CoA carboxylase catalyzes the first step in fatty acids biosynthesis. Moreover, Acetyl-CoA carboxylation is considered to be the rate limiting step in fatty acids synthesis in the seeds, and during seed development. Alteration in the acetyl-CoA carboxylase activity may affect oil biosynthesis. Sharma et al. reported that nano sulfur markedly augmented seeds and oil content in soybean57. Sulfur promotes developments of oil storage organs and oil content58. Moreover, Sulfur augments total sugar, a source of energy and carbon in oil biosynthesis through increased photosynthesis due to higher level of Rubisco in leaf59. The outcomes of our study show that MnS NPs may be used as nano-fertilizers for improved crop growth, yield, and oil content.

Manganese bioaccumulation and mobility

Mn concentration in soil, roots, shoots, and seeds increased progressively from the control to MnSO4 and MnS NPs treatments (Table 1). Soil Mn content was highest in MnS NPs plots (166.4 ± 6.65 mg/kg), 44% higher than MnSO4 and 166% higher than the control. Mn uptake by roots and translocation to shoots was substantially higher under MnS NPs treatment, with root and shoot Mn content higher by 33.4 and 40.9%, respectively over the control. Similar finding was observed in seeds, where seeds of MnS NPs treated plants displayed higher Mn content as compared to control. The pattern for Mn bioaccumulation in linseed was root > shoot > seed. Notably, the bioaccumulation factor (BAF) decreased from 1.47 in the control plant to 0.80 with MnS NPs treatment, demonstrating that although soil Mn concentration was higher, the uptake of plants relative to soil concentration was low; probably due to saturation effects. The translocation factor (TF) slightly increased with Mn supplementation, suggesting enhanced movement of Mn from roots to shoots, particularly with MnS NPs application. These findings indicate that MnS NPs improve Mn bioavailability and accumulation in tissues without causing excessive or imbalanced uptake, an important advantage over ionic forms of Mn that can risk phytotoxicity under high uptake rates. The bioaccumulation of metallic NPs depends on their size and type, and plant species60. The NPs enter either through root or leaves and translocated via symplastic or apoplastic pathways. From root to aerial parts, the translocation occurs via xylem, while downward movement happens through phloem, yet the exact mechanism of movement needs to be uncovered61. NPs may enlarge the pores in cell wall and plasma membrane or promote new pore formation, due to which large sized NPs get entry to cell62. The sustained delivery of Mn in form of NPs activates key physiological and biochemical pathways such as antioxidant enzyme regulation, phenolic metabolism, and redox homeostasis. The activation of these pathways may lead to coordinated improvement in growth, yield, and oil accumulation as observed in this study. These findings indicate that MnS NPs act as an efficient Mn source that strengthens plant metabolic performance and stress resilience.

Antioxidant enzyme activities and phenolic-flavonoid content

MnS NPs markedly stimulated antioxidant enzyme activities and secondary metabolite production in linseed (Fig. 3). Protein content reached up to 220.96 ± 11.04 mg/g FW in MnS NPs treated plants, a 77.8% increase over the control and 55.9% increase over MnSO4. Superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity increased more than two folds in MnS NPs treated linseed plants compared to control, while the peroxidase (POD) activity increased by 83.8% with respect to control. This highlights significant upregulation of enzymatic antioxidant defense mechanism. Total phenolic content (TPC) and total flavonoid content (TFC) were also elevated. TPC in the roots extract augmented by 18.6% while 16.4% increase was observed in shoot of MnS NPs treated linseed plant, relative to the control. TFC in the roots enhanced from 20.55 ± 0.82 µg QE/mg DW in the control to 25.09 ± 1.25 µg QE/mg DW in MnS NPs treated linseed plants, whereas TFC in the shoot extract reached up to 451.98 ± 18.07 µg GAE/mg DW, 13.1% increase as compared to control. These findings show that MnS NPs not only improved primary metabolism and yield but also stimulated secondary metabolite pathways associated to stress tolerance and preservation of oil quality.

Mn is an important micronutrient involved in various metabolic activities such as photosynthesis, protein synthesis, and respiration. Mn is a cofactor for important enzymes like superoxide dismutase (MnSOD), catalase (MnCAT), RNA polymerases, and decarboxylases of the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle54,63. The cells of stressed plant up-regulate the genes for antioxidant enzymes production through signaling transduction. The SOD being the first line of defense against oxidative stress, catalyze the conversion of O2·− to H2O2, which is subsequently reduced to H2O by antioxidant enzymes such as CAT, APX, POX and GPX. Mn influences transcriptional regulation and consequent protein synthesis64. Khepar et al. observed up-regulation of SOD, POD and CAT activities by 28.16, 53.27, and 28.57% in rice seedlings after the seeds being treated with MnS-NPs65. Moreover, Pradhan et al. demonstrated that Mn NPs increased nitrate uptake, assimilation, and metabolism in Vigna radiata66. Plants also synthesize bioactive compounds in response to stress as part of their defense system. These bioactive compounds include secondary metabolites such as phenolics and flavonoids which play important role in ROS detoxification67. Plant produces more flavonoids and phenolics in response to nanoparticles due to abiotic stress elicitation of secondary metabolites68. Nazir et al. reported increased level of TFC and TPC in Ocimum basillicum exposed to MnO NPs69. The enhanced concentration of enzymatic and non-enzymatic antioxidants confirmed stress condition. SOD, POD and CAT inactivate the excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS)70 and may reduce ROS interaction with intracellular machinery resulting in their destruction. The reason for increased TPC and TFC is ROS production in response to Mn stress, which commences complex reactions and influence cell metabolism. External stress on plants results in sudden changes in metabolic pathways. These changes promote ROS (H2O2, NO, ·OH, O−2) production that damage DNA, protein, lipid, and plant cell71. Defense mechanism of plants produces phenolics, flavonoids, and glutathione as antioxidants in response to external stress condition. The increased antioxidants production in response to MnS application provokes detoxification mechanism that results in ROS dismutation.

Free radical scavenging activity and reducing power

The antioxidant potential of linseed roots and shoot was measured through DPPH based fee radical scavenging activity, total antioxidant capacity (TAC), and total reducing power (TRP). The antioxidant potential was markedly higher in MnS NPs treated linseed plant (Fig. 3). DPPH radical scavenging activity in roots increased from 42.5% in the control to 61.28% in MnS NPs treated plants, while shoots exhibited a smaller yet significant improvement. TAC in the shoots reached up to 705.39 ± 28.21 µg AAE/mg DW under MnS NPs treatment, compared to 619.84 ± 18.59 in the control plants, whereas TRP values showed 57% increase in roots and 40% increase in shoots as compared to control. These finding are consistent with elevated phenolic and flavonoid contents, as both compound classes contribute to radical scavenging. The agreement of biochemical and functional antioxidant assays reinforces the conclusion that MnS NPs markedly improved the antioxidative capacity of linseed. Total antioxidant capacity of the plants is determined by TAC, TRP, and DPPH radical scavenging activity72. DPPH stop the chain reaction through quenching peroxide radicals. In this study, elevated DPPH, TAC, and TRP of the linseed plants exposed to MnS NPs demonstrate that these molecules cope oxidative stress elicited by MnS NPs. Moreover, the increased levels of these antioxidants do not negatively affect plant growth and yield as demonstrated by agronomic parameters. Higher antioxidant activities also show decreased generation of free radicals by interrupting enzymes activities that synthesize free radicals or enhance the expressions of other antioxidative enzymes73,74. Our findings are in line with Tian et al. reported increased DPPH radical scavenging activity, TPC, and TFC in Atropa belladonna upon exposure to Mn NPs75. The elevated level of antioxidant activities shows powerful detoxification mechanism that protect the plants cells from Mn stress.

Assessment of correlation among parameters using PCA

The principal component analysis (PCA) was performed to evaluate the relationships among agronomic and biochemical parameters and to differentiate the effects of control, MnSO4, and MnS NPs treatments (Fig. 4a–b). The PCA biplot for agronomic parameters shows that the principal component 1 (PC1) clearly separates MnS NPs from control and MnSO4 treatments, indicating that PC1 predominantly affect variation in growth and yield attributes. The presence of root length, shoot length, dry weight, branches, seed number, seed balls, and oil percentage on positive axis of PC1 and their close association with MnS NPs display a positive impact of MnS NPs on agronomic parameters. In contrast, the untreated control clusters on the negative side of PC1 demonstrate inferior agronomic performance. MnSO4 holds an intermediate position and separated along PC2 that suggests a moderate influence compared to MnS NPs (Fig. 4a). The PCA for biochemical traits shows a similar pattern (Fig. 4b). MnS NPs distinctly separated along the positive axis of PC1 and strongly associates with antioxidants such TPC, TFC, TAC, DPPH activity, SOD, POD, TRP. The close clustering of these vectors shows strong positive correlations between antioxidant defense system and MnS NPs application. The control lies on the negative side of PC1, demonstrating lower antioxidant capacity while MnSO4 in an intermediate position suggest partial induction of biochemical responses, markedly lower than MnS NPs induced responses.

Characterization of oil

Figure 5 shows the FT-IR spectra of linseed oil extracted from control, MnSO4 treated, and MnS NPs treated linseed. All samples exhibited characteristic bands at 3010.38 cm−1, 2923.96 cm−1, and 2854 cm−1, corresponding to C–H stretching vibrations of aliphatic chains (–CH2–), and a strong peak at 1742.85 cm−1 indicating stretching of ester carbonyl (C=O) groups of typical triglycerides. Peaks at 1485.89 cm−1 and 1160.53 cm−1 reflects C–H bending and C–O stretching (ester C–O bond), respectively, while the band at 720.18 cm−1 attributes to long-chain –(CH2)ₙ– rocking. The FT-IR spectral similarities demonstrate that the basic composition of oil extracted from treated and untreated linseed plants remained intact across all treatments. Yet, the oil content increased in MnS NPs treated plants, likely due to the role of Mn in activating lipid biosynthesis enzymes, such as acetyl-CoA carboxylase and fatty acid synthase76. Moreover, MnS NPs might facilitated better nutrient uptake and photosynthetic efficiency, that ultimately lead to increased carbon assimilation and consequent lipid accumulation in seed. The enhanced lipid accumulation in the seeds of MnS NPs treated linseed plants also associates with better oxidative stress tolerance and antioxidant enzyme activities, which indirectly promote improved seed development and oil content.

Figure 6 and Table 2 show the GC-MS fatty acid of linseed oil extracted from control, MnSO4, and MnS NPs treated linseed plants. The oils predominantly composed of saturated (palmitic, stearic) and unsaturated fatty acids (linoleic, oleic, palmitoleic), with minor differences in relative concentrations between treatments. The oil extracted from control plants displayed palmitic acid (39.50%) as the major component, followed by linoleic acid (27.36%), and oleic acid (19.50%). Stearic acid accounted for 10.85%, while minor fractions of myristic acid (0.66%) and elaidic acid (2.12%) were also observed. This composition reflects a balanced distribution of saturated and unsaturated fatty acids. Similar fatty acid profile of linseed oil was reported by Abbas77. The MnSO4 treatment shifted the composition towards slightly higher saturated fatty acids, particularly palmitic acid (45.69%) and stearic acid (35.60%), while the unsaturated linoleic acid content dropped to 16.63%. This demonstrates that MnSO4 might have promoted de novo saturated fatty acid synthesis while limiting desaturation pathways, which resulted in reduced polyunsaturated content. On the other hand, MnS NPs treatment resulted an increase in linoleic acid to 57.69%, the highest proportion among all treatments, accompanied by palmitic acid content (23.69%) and stearic acid levels (17.19%). Several minor fatty acids appeared exclusively in MnS NPs treatment, including pentadecanoic acid, palmitoleic acid, and decosanoic acid. This suggests that MnS NPs enhanced desaturation processes, favoring polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA) accumulation, potentially improving oil nutritional quality. These findings indicate that MnS NPs promote PUFA synthesis, particularly linoleic acid, whereas MnSO4 favors saturated fatty acid synthesis. These differences might be associated with the role of Mn in enzyme regulation, where NPs can enhance desaturase activity, increasing PUFA biosynthesis.

Figure 7 shows the 13C NMR spectra of oil extracted from control (a), MnSO4 (b), and MnS NPs (c) treated linseed plants. 13C NMR is informative due to wide range of chemical shifts of 13C nuclei [78, 79). The observed chemical shifts attribute characteristic carbons in triglycerides, including carbonyl carbons of esters (~ 173 ppm), olefinic carbons from unsaturated fatty acids (~ 127–132 ppm), glycerol backbone carbons (~ 62–69 ppm), and aliphatic methylene carbons in fatty acid chains (~ 14–34 ppm). The 13C NMR spectra are in agreement with standard linseed oil profile80,81. The results are also in line with GC–MS results, which confirm that MnS NPs treatment augments the degree of fatty acid unsaturation in linseed oil. This corroborated explanation of 13C NMR with GC–MS data suggests that MnS NPs induce favorable biochemical changes in linseed oil. Additionally, it may improve oxidative stability and functional properties while maintaining the structural integrity of the triglycerides.

Figure 8 shows the 1H NMR spectra of oil extracted from control (a), MnSO4 (b), and MnS NPs (c) treated linseed plants. These spectra exhibit slight variations in the chemical shifts and signal intensities among the oil samples. These spectra show characteristic proton resonances conforming to the functional groups found in oil. The signals in the downfield region (5.2–5.4 ppm) attributes to olefinic protons of unsaturated fatty acids, peaks in the range of 4.1–4.3 ppm associate with glycerol backbone protons, and multiple resonances in the up-field region (0.8–2.8 ppm) representing aliphatic methylene and methyl protons. The peaks in 1H NMR spectra for linseed oil are in agreement with literature82,83. The shifts in the peak intensities of oil extracted from treated plants with respect to control plant suggest variations in the relative abundance of unsaturated and saturated components. This is probably due to Mn induced modulation of fatty acid biosynthesis. These spectral differences indicate that MnSO4 and MnS NPs may influence the metabolic pathways that govern the lipid composition in linseed oil. The slight increase in saturated fatty acid in MnSO4 treated linseed oil is possibly due to Mn supply in a readily soluble ionic form, which may lead to its excess in plant tissues. Elevated level of Mn²⁺ may disturb membrane integrity and suppress desaturase activity, and promote lipid stabilization through enhanced saturated fatty acid synthesis. Subsequently, MnSO4 treatment shifts fatty acid composition toward a higher degree of saturation. In contrast, MnS NPs provide a slow and regulated release of Mn, which helps to maintain cellular redox homeostasis and promote fatty acid desaturase activity. Moreover, nanoparticle mediated delivery of Mn enhances antioxidant defense, which lead to reduce oxidative inhibition of desaturase enzymes and favor unsaturated fatty acids accumulation in oil. These findings are aligned with previous study where 1H and 13C NMR of oil extracted from NPs and their precursor salt induced only minor spectral variations, while the principal NMR features remained preserved84.

Conclusion

Soil application of MnS NPs at 8 kg ha−1 improved linseed vegetative growth, dry weight, antioxidative response, agronomic parameters and oil content. The nano-size of MnS particles facilitated better nutrients uptake, promote sustained release of Mn2+ ions and more effective translocation to metabolic sites. Elevated phenolic and flavonoid contents and increased SOD and POD activities reflect that Mn acts as cofactor for important antioxidative enzymes, strengthening the ROS scavenging capability of the linseed plant. This improved redox homeostasis supports the observed increase in biomass, productivity, and seed oil content in linseed. The higher Mn accumulation in plant tissues showed enhanced micronutrient availability, while elevated level of unsaturated fatty acids indicated Mn mediated modulation of desaturase enzymes involved in fatty acid biosynthesis. These outcomes suggest MnS NPs can be a promising nano-fertilizer which deliver Mn more efficiently, activate antioxidant defense mechanism, and promote lipid biosynthetic pathways to favor unsaturated fatty acid accumulation, while the basic oil profile remained preserved.

The study indicates the potential of MnS NPs as a sustainable strategy for enhanced agricultural production. MnS NPs improved soil health, leading to increased nutrient bioavailability, improved uptake, and better crop performance. Although, MnS NPs improved plant parameters, it is important to assess the possible risk associated with NPs application in soil. It is evident from literature that NPs may remain in the environment and affect the soil microbiota and productivity of crops. The pattern of NPs accumulation in soil-plant system needs to be elucidated for environmental safety. Future research may also focus on the mechanistic pathway of MnS NPs action, soil-NPs interaction, long-term environmental safety, and multi-season and multi-location field trials assessments to support large scale adoption of MnS NPs as nano-fertilizers. Moreover, further studies might also be carried out on transcriptomic and gene expression analyses of key oil biosynthesis and fatty acid desaturation genes to elucidate the underlying molecular mechanisms.

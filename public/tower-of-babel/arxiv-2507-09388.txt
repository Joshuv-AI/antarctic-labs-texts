Optimal Differentially Private Ranking from Pairwise Comparisons
T. Tony Cai, Abhinav Chakraborty, Yichen Wang

Abstract
Data privacy is a central concern in many applications involving ranking from incomplete and noisy pairwise comparisons, such as recommendation systems, educational assessments, and opinion surveys on sensitive topics. In this work, we propose differentially private algorithms for ranking based on pairwise comparisons. Specifically, we develop and analyze ranking methods under two privacy notions: edge differential privacy, which protects the confidentiality of individual comparison outcomes, and individual differential privacy, which safeguards potentially many comparisons contributed by a single individual. Our algorithms–including a perturbed maximum likelihood estimator and a noisy count-based method–are shown to achieve minimax optimal rates of convergence under the respective privacy constraints. We further demonstrate the practical effectiveness of our methods through experiments on both simulated and real-world data.



Keywords:
differential privacy, ranking, Bradley-Terry-Luce model, minimax optimality

1
Introduction

As personal data are more extensively collected and analyzed than ever, the importance of privacy protection in data analysis is also increasingly recognized. In this paper, we consider privacy-preserving methods for ranking from pairwise comparisons. In this ranking problem, the data analyst observes random and incomplete pairwise comparisons among items that follow some unknown ranking, with higher ranked items more likely (but not guaranteed) to prevail over lower ranked ones. The analyst then tries to infer the underlying ranking from the noisy comparison results. The extensive applied research on this topic attests to its practical relevance in many settings.

•

Pairwise comparison in sensitive survey data:
Pairwise comparisons in surveys provide a structured approach for respondents to express their relative preferences across a wide range of options. For instance, a survey analyzed by
weber2011novel
was conducted to gauge public sentiment toward immigration. The study involved 98 student participants, each of whom responded to at least one pairwise comparison. These comparisons were constructed from a set of four extreme statements regarding immigrants.

•

Pairwise comparison in recommendation systems.
Pairwise comparisons are widely used in recommendation systems that capture users’ preferences between pairs of items such as movies, books, or other consumer products. For example,
balakrishnan2012two
proposed a method in which customers are presented with a series of paired preference questions (e.g. “Do you prefer item A over item B?”), allowing the system to infer personalized rankings based on comparative judgments.

•

Pairwise comparison in education.
Pairwise comparison can serve as an effective tool for educational assessment. For instance,
heldsinger2010using
describes a study in which teachers employed a pairwise comparison procedure to grade student writing samples and construct a performance scale. The study found that teacher judgments were highly internally consistent and showed strong correlation with scores from a large-scale standardized testing program administered to the same group of students.

Privacy is a critical concern in many applications involving ranking from pairwise comparisons. For example, in the educational assessment study by
heldsinger2010using
, teachers’ preferences between pairs of student assignments should remain confidential. Similarly, in the survey conducted by
weber2011novel
, respondents were asked to express preferences between pairs of political positions–data considered sensitive due to the controversial nature of the statements and the potential ramifications for individuals if their views were made public.

Motivated by the importance of protecting privacy in such settings, we develop
statistically optimal
algorithms for ranking from pairwise comparisons under
differential privacy (DP) constraints
. Differential privacy
dwork2006calibrating
;
dwork2006our
is the most widely adopted framework for privacy-preserving data analysis, offering strong formal guarantees that the output of an algorithm reveals provably little about any individual data point. At the same time, it supports the design and implementation of efficient algorithms. In this paper, we propose and analyze DP algorithms for ranking from pairwise comparisons and show that they achieve statistical optimality under the DP constraint: no other differentially private algorithm can attain a faster rate of convergence to the true ranking.

1.1
Main Results and Our Contribution

Edge DP and individual DP
. While the technical definitions are deferred to Sections
2.1
and
3.1
, we briefly provide an intuitive overview of differential privacy (DP). A differentially private algorithm guarantees that its outputs on two adjacent datasets are statistically similar. Two datasets are considered adjacent if they differ by exactly one unit of data. In essence, a DP algorithm ensures that its output does not depend significantly on any single data point, thereby preventing inference about the presence or absence of any individual unit in the input.

However, as discussed in
NBERc15017
, the definition of adjacency–and thus the unit of data–depends on the specific context and data analysis task. For instance, in a census, a unit might be a person or a household, while in business analytics, it could be a single transaction. In the context of ranking from pairwise comparisons, two natural notions of adjacency arise, leading to two corresponding definitions of differential privacy:

•

Edge DP: Two datasets are adjacent if they differ by a single pairwise comparison outcome.

•

Individual DP: Two datasets are adjacent if they differ in all the comparisons contributed by a single individual.

To the best of our knowledge, this paper is the first to simultaneously consider and rigorously analyze both definitions of DP in the context of ranking from pairwise comparisons.

Optimal parametric estimation with differential privacy
. Under the parametric Bradley-Terry-Luce (BTL) model for pairwise comparisons
bradley1952rank
;
luce1959individual
, we introduce in Section
2.2
a perturbed maximum likelihood estimator of the following form:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}=\argmin_{{\bm{\theta}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}};y)+\frac{\gamma}{2}\|{\bm{\theta}}\|_{2}^{2}+\bm{w}^{\top}{\bm{\theta}},\quad\bm{w}=(w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\text{Laplace}(\lambda),

where
\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}},y)
is the likelihood function,
{\bm{\theta}}
is the vector of latent parameters which determine the items’ ranks, and
y
is the data set of pairwise comparisons.

We then show that the estimator respectively satisfies edge DP and individual DP for two different choices of
(\gamma,\lambda)
, in Sections
2.2
and
3.2
. It is further shown by Theorems
2.2
and
3.2
that the estimator’s respective rates of convergence under edge DP and individual DP are optimal.

Optimal nonparametric ranking with differential privacy
. In the absence of parametric assumptions, we show that ranking items based on noisy counts of wins–with appropriately calibrated noise distributions–satisfies either edge differential privacy or individual differential privacy, depending on the setting. Moreover, this simple approach is statistically optimal: it accurately ranks items across the broadest possible regime of sample sizes and privacy levels. Specifically, in Theorems
2.3
and
2.4
, we show that ranking by noisy counts succeeds at identifying the top
k
items whenever the strengths of the
k
-th and
(k+1)
-th items are separated by a certain threshold, and no other edge DP algorithm will succeed below this threshold. Theorems
3.3
and
3.4
contain similar results for individual DP.

Entrywise analysis of DP algorithms
. The study of differentially private ranking also yields insights of broader methodological interest. In particular, the perturbed maximum likelihood estimator (MLE) achieves differential privacy by injecting a single dose of noise into the objective function. This design facilitates a leave-one-out analysis
chen2019spectral
, enabling precise control of entrywise errors in optimization problems. While most prior work on differentially private optimization has focused on bounding
\ell_{2}
errors, our framework extends the analysis to the entrywise setting. Furthermore, our adaptation of the score attack technique
cai2023score
–originally developed for
\ell_{2}
risk–to entrywise error analysis may have broader applicability in establishing entrywise lower bounds for differentially private algorithms more generally.

2
Optimal Ranking under Edge Differential Privacy

2.1
Problem Formulation under Edge DP

The ranking problem
. There are
n
distinct items, represented by indices in
[n]=\{1,2,3,\cdots,n\}
. Pairwise comparisons between items are observed randomly and independently, where each pair
(i,j)
,
1\leq i<j\leq n
, is compared with a known probability
p\in(0,1]
. This results in an Erdős–Rényi random graph
\mathcal{G}
with
n
nodes and the observed comparisons constituting the edges. Every observed pair
(i,j)
determines a unique winner, symbolized by the outcome
Y_{ij}\in\{0,1\}
, satisfying
Y_{ij}+Y_{ji}=1
. Consequently, for
i<j
, the random variable
Y_{ij}
follows an independent Bernoulli distribution with parameter
\rho_{ij}\in[0,1]
, and the requirement
Y_{ij}+Y_{ji}=1
implies
\rho_{ij}+\rho_{ji}=1
. We assume
\rho_{ii}=1/2
for clarity.

Our objective is to rank the set of
n
items based on the average true winning probabilities when compared to randomly selected counterparts. This average winning probability for each item
i\in[n]
is formally denoted by
\tau_{i}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j\in[n]}\rho_{ij}
. We are interested in estimating the index set
\mathcal{S}_{k}
, where
\mathcal{S}_{k}=\{i\in[n]:\tau_{i}\text{ is among the top-$k$ largest of }\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\ldots,\tau_{n}\}
for a predetermined
k\in[n]
.

Parametric and nonparametric models
. The ranking problem is studied under two models. The first one is a parametric model where
\rho_{ij}=F(\theta^{*}_{i}-\theta^{*}_{j})
. Each item
i\in[n]
is assigned a latent parameter
\theta^{*}_{i}
, and
F:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
is a predetermined link function. This model generalizes well-known the Bradley-Terry-Luce (BTL) model
bradley1952rank
;
luce1959individual
for pairwise comparison, and recovers the BTL model when
F
is the standard logistic CDF. With this parametric assumption, the ranking of
\tau_{i}
is equivalent to the ranking of
\theta_{i}^{*}
, which further reduces to estimating real-valued parameters
\{\theta^{*}_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}
.

The second model is nonparametric, in which we do not assume any parametric form for the
\rho_{ij}
values, and instead aim to estimate the ranks directly. This nonparametric ranking problem is the focus of a more recent line of work
shah2017simple
;
chen2017competitive
;
shah2017simple
;
shah2019feeling
. We define and study these two settings in Sections
2.2
and
2.3
respectively.

Edge differential privacy
. Under these models, we study ranking algorithms satisfying
(\varepsilon,\delta)
differential privacy (
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-DP). The formal definition of
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-DP requires that, for an algorithm
M
taking values in some domain
\mathcal{R}
and every measurable subset
A\subseteq\mathcal{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(M(X)\in A)\leq e^{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathbb{P}(M(X^{\prime})\in A)+\delta

for any pair of data sets
X
and
X^{\prime}
which differ by one element. A pair of data sets is called “adjacent” if they differ by exactly one element. For example, if
X,X^{\prime}
are sets of real numbers,
X=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
X^{\prime}=\{x^{\prime}_{1},x^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{n}\}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, then
X
and
X^{\prime}
are adjacent if
|X\cap(X^{\prime})^{c}|=|X^{c}\cap X^{\prime}|=1
.

When specialized to pairwise comparison data, one natural interpretation of“adjacency” is that two sets of comparison outcomes differ by a single comparison. Concretely, two sets of comparison outcomes
\bm{Y}=\{Y_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{G}}
and
\bm{Y}^{\prime}=\{Y^{\prime}_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}
are adjacent if they satisfy one of the two (disjoint) scenarios.

•

The comparison graphs are identical,
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
, and there exists exactly one edge
(i^{*},j^{*})\in\mathcal{G}
on which the comparison outcomes differ,
Y_{i^{*}j^{*}}\neq Y^{\prime}_{i^{*}j^{*}}
. All other comparison outcomes are identical:
Y_{ij}=Y^{\prime}_{ij}
for
(i,j)\neq(i^{*},j^{*})
.

•

The comparison graphs
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
differ by exactly one edge: there exist
a^{*},b^{*},c^{*},d^{*}\in[n]
and
(a^{*},b^{*})\neq(c^{*},d^{*})
, such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}\cap\mathcal{G}^{\prime}+\{(a^{*},b^{*})\},\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\mathcal{G}\cap\mathcal{G}^{\prime}+\{(c^{*},d^{*})\}.

The comparison outcomes
\bm{Y}=\{Y_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{G}}
and
\bm{Y}^{\prime}=\{Y^{\prime}_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}
satisfy
Y_{ij}=Y^{\prime}_{ij}
for all
(i,j)\in\mathcal{G}\cap\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
.

This notion of adjacency and the corresponding definition of differential privacy is akin to “edge differential privacy” for graphs
nissim2007smooth
;
kasiviswanathan2013analyzing
, and we adopt the same term for our case.

2.2
Parametric Estimation under Edge DP

We first study ranking from pairwise comparisons under parametric assumptions: each item
i\in[n]
is associated with a latent parameter
\theta^{*}_{i}
, and the pairwise probability
\rho_{ij}
is related to the latent parameters of items
i,j
by a known increasing function
F:{\mathbb{R}}\to[0,1]
, specifically
\rho_{ij}=F(\theta^{*}_{i}-\theta^{*}_{j})
. These assumptions conveniently reduce the problem of ranking
n
items by their average winning probability against peers,
\tau_{i}=n^{-1}\sum_{j\in[n]}\rho_{ij}
, to the problem of estimating
{\bm{\theta}}^{*}=\left(\theta^{*}_{i}\right)_{i\in[n]}
.

2.2.1
The Edge DP Algorithm for Parametric Estimation

For constructing a differentially private estimator of
{\bm{\theta}}^{*}
, our approach is to minimize a randomly perturbed and
\ell_{2}
-penalized version of the negative log-likelihood function. For a vector
\bm{v}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, indices
i,j\in[n]
and a given link function
F
, let
F_{ij}(\bm{v})=F(v_{i}-v_{j})
and
F^{\prime}_{ij}(\bm{v})=F^{\prime}(v_{i}-v_{j})
. The negative log-likelihood function is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}};y)=\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{G}}-y_{ij}\log F_{ij}({\bm{\theta}})-y_{ji}\log(1-F_{ij}({\bm{\theta}})).

(2.1)

The estimator is defined by Algorithm
1
.

Algorithm 1

Differentially Private Ranking for parametric models

Input
: Comparison data
(y_{ij})_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{G}}
, comparison graph
\mathcal{G}
, privacy parameter
\varepsilon

\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}
defined in (

1:

Set
\lambda\geq\frac{8\kappa_{1}}{\varepsilon}
and
\gamma\geq\frac{4\kappa_{2}}{\varepsilon}
and generate

\bm{w}=(w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\text{Laplace}(\lambda).

2:

Solve for

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}=\argmin_{{\bm{\theta}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}};y)+\frac{\gamma}{2}\|{\bm{\theta}}\|_{2}^{2}+\bm{w}^{\top}{\bm{\theta}},\quad\bm{w}=(w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\text{Laplace}(\lambda).

(2.2)

Output
:
\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}
.

Some regularity conditions on the function
F
will be helpful throughout our analysis of
\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}
. We collect them here for convenience.

(A0)

F:{\mathbb{R}}\to[0,1]
is strictly increasing and satisfies
F(x)=1-F(-x)
for every
x\in\mathbb{{\mathbb{R}}}
.

(A1)

There is an absolute constant
\kappa_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}\left|\frac{F^{\prime}(x)}{F(x)(1-F(x))}\right|=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{F^{\prime}(x)}{F(x)(1-F(x))}<\kappa_{1}.

(2.3)

(A2)

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\left(-\log F(x)\right)>0
for every

, and there exists an absolute constant
\kappa_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\left(-\log F(x)\right)<\kappa_{2},\quad\min_{|x|\leq 4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\left(-\log F(x)\right)>\frac{1}{\kappa_{2}}.

(2.4)

In particular, choosing
F
to be the standard logistic CDF satisfies these conditions and recovers the BTL model.

Returning to the estimator (
2.2
), the random perturbation
\bm{w}^{\top}{\bm{\theta}}
is an instance of objective perturbation methods in differentially private optimization

;
kifer2012private
. Let
\mathcal{R}({\bm{\theta}};y)
denote the regularized log-likelihood part,
\mathcal{R}({\bm{\theta}};y)=\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}};y)+\frac{\gamma}{2}\|{\bm{\theta}}\|_{2}^{2}
, then
\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}
amounts to the solution of a noisy stationary condition
\nabla\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}};y)=-\bm{w}
. The solution
\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}=\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}(y)
is differentially private when

•

the scale parameter
\lambda
of noise vector
\bm{w}
is sufficiently large to obfuscate the change in
\nabla\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}})
over adjacent data sets, and

•

the regularization coefficient
\gamma
ensures strong convexity of the objective
\mathcal{R}({\bm{\theta}})
, so that perturbation of the gradient is translated to perturbation of the solution
\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}
.

The privacy guarantee is formalized by Proposition

.

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose conditions (A0), (A1) and (A2) hold. If

We have so far not considered the convergence of

to the truth

, and in particular choosing large values of

The optimal choice of

When

and

2.2.2
The Edge DP Lower Bound for Parametric Estimation

For an arbitrary
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-DP estimator
M(\bm{Y})
of
{\bm{\theta}}
, we would like to find a lower bound for the maximum
\ell_{\infty}
risk
\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{E}\|M(\bm{Y})-{\bm{\theta}}\|_{\infty}
over the parameter space
\Theta=\{{\bm{\theta}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\|{\bm{\theta}}\|_{\infty}\leq 1\}
, which captures the inevitable cost of differential privacy for estimating
{\bm{\theta}}
.

To this end, we consider an entry-wise version of the score attack method
cai2023score
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(k)}(M(\bm{Y}),Y_{ij})=\begin{cases}0&(i,j)\not\in\mathcal{G}\text{ or }i,j\neq k,\\
(M(\bm{Y})_{k}-\theta_{k})(y_{kj}-F_{kj}({\bm{\theta}}))\frac{F^{\prime}_{kj}({\bm{\theta}})}{F_{kj}({\bm{\theta}})(1-F_{kj}({\bm{\theta}}))}&(i,j)\in\mathcal{G}\text{ and }i=k,\\
(M(\bm{Y})_{k}-\theta_{k})(y_{ik}-F_{ik}({\bm{\theta}}))\frac{F^{\prime}_{ik}({\bm{\theta}})}{F_{ik}({\bm{\theta}})(1-F_{ik}({\bm{\theta}}))}&(i,j)\in\mathcal{G}\text{ and }j=k.\end{cases}

It is an entry-wise version of the score attack in the sense that summing
\mathcal{A}^{(k)}(M(\bm{Y}),Y_{ij})
over
k\in[n]
is exactly equal to the score attack for lower bounding the
\ell_{2}
minimax risk. When the reference to
\bm{Y}
and
M
is clear, we denote
\mathcal{A}^{(k)}(M(\bm{Y}),Y_{ij})
by
A^{(k)}_{ij}
.

Our plan for lower bounding the
\ell_{\infty}
risk consists of upper bounding
\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mathbb{E}A^{(k)}_{ij}
by the
\ell_{\infty}
risk and lower bounding the same quantity by a non-negative amount. The results of these steps are condensed in Propositions
2.3
and
2.4
respectively.

Proposition 2.3
.

If
M
is an
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-DP estimator with
0<\varepsilon<1
and
p>6\log n/n
, then for sufficiently large
n
, every
{\bm{\theta}}\in\Theta
and every
k\in[n]
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{Y}|{\bm{\theta}}}A^{(k)}_{ij}\leq 4\kappa_{1}np\varepsilon\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\bm{Y}|{\bm{\theta}}}|M(\bm{Y})_{k}-\theta_{k}|+4\kappa_{1}(n-1)\delta+2\kappa_{1}n^{-1}.

(2.8)

Proposition
2.3
is proved by considering
\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{ij}
, an adjacent data set of
\bm{Y}
obtained by replacing
Y_{ij}
with an independent copy. By differential privacy of algorithm
M
,
\mathbb{E}A^{(k)}_{ij}
should be close to
\mathbb{E}A^{(k)}(M(\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{ij}),Y_{ij})
, which is seen to be exactly 0 by the statistical independence of
M(\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{ij})
and
Y_{ij}
. The full details can be found in the supplement.

In the opposing direction, instead of a pointwise lower bound of
\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{Y}|{\bm{\theta}}}A^{(k)}_{ij}
at every
{\bm{\theta}}\in\Theta
, we lower bound the sum over a particular prior distribution of
{\bm{\theta}}
over
\Theta
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Suppose
M
is an estimator of
{\bm{\theta}}
such that
\sup_{{\bm{\theta}}\in\Theta}\mathbb{E}\|M(\bm{Y})-{\bm{\theta}}\|_{\infty}<c
for a sufficiently small constant
c>0
. If each coordinate of
{\bm{\theta}}
has density
\pi(t)=\mathbbm{1}(|t|<1)(15/16)(1-t^{2})^{2}
, then for every
k\in[n]
there is some constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\theta}}\mathbb{E}_{Y|{\bm{\theta}}}A^{(k)}_{ij}>C.

(2.9)

We defer the proof of Proposition
2.4
to the supplement, and combine Propositions
2.3
and
2.4
to arrive at a minimax risk lower bound in
\ell_{\infty}
norm for estimating
{\bm{\theta}}
with differential privacy.

Theorem 2.2
.

If
p>6\log n/n
,
\varepsilon\gtrsim(\log n)^{-1}
,
0<\varepsilon<1
and
\delta\lesssim n^{-1}
, then

\displaystyle\inf_{M\in M_{\varepsilon,\delta}}\sup_{{\bm{\theta}}\in\Theta}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{Y}|{\bm{\theta}}}\|M(\bm{Y})-{\bm{\theta}}\|_{\infty}\gtrsim\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{np}}+\frac{1}{np\varepsilon}.

(2.10)

The first term in the lower bound (
2.10
) is exactly the non-private minimax rate proved in
shah2017simple
;
chen2019spectral
. The second term is attributable to differential privacy.

The lower bound result given in Theorem
2.2
suggests that the perturbed MLE
\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}
is essentially optimal except possibly a room of improvement by
O(\log n)
, but there is no implication about differentially private ranking algorithms not based on estimating the latent parameters
{\bm{\theta}}
. The next section considers differentially private ranking without relying on the parametric assumptions.

2.3
Nonparametric Estimation under Edge DP

If we drop the parametric assumption
\rho_{ij}=F(\theta^{*}_{i}-\theta^{*}_{j})
, the estimand of interest shifts from
{\bm{\theta}}^{*}
to the index set of top-
k
items
\mathcal{S}_{k}
for
k\in[n-1]
in terms of the average winning probability,
\tau_{i}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j\in[n]}\rho_{ij}
. In Section
2.3.1
, we exhibit a differentially private estimator of
\mathcal{S}_{k}
which exactly recovers
\mathcal{S}_{k}
when
\tau_{(k)}
and
\tau_{(k+1)}
are sufficiently far apart,

\displaystyle|\tau_{(k)}-\tau_{(k+1)}|\gtrsim\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{np}}+\frac{\log n}{np\varepsilon}.

It is not a coincidence that the requisite separation is identical to its parametric counterpart (
2.7
). We prove in Section
2.3.2
that this separation is the exact threshold for differentially private ranking in either parametric or nonparametric case.

Formally, consider the space of pairwise probability matrices

\displaystyle\Theta(k,m,c)=\left\{\bm{\rho}\in[0,1]^{n\times n}:\bm{\rho}+\bm{\rho}^{\top}=11^{\top},\tau_{(k-m)}-\tau_{(k+m+1)}\geq c\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{np}}+\frac{\log n}{np\varepsilon}\right)\right\},

for
k\in[n-1]
and
0\leq m\leq\min(k-1,n-k-1)
. Let
d_{H}(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the Hamming distance between sets, and an estimator
\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{k}}
succeeds at recovering
\mathcal{S}_{k}
within tolerance
m
if

\displaystyle\sup_{\rho\in\Theta(k,m,c)}\mathbb{P}\left(d_{H}(\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{k}},\mathcal{S}_{k})>2m\right)=o(1).

Exact recovery of
\mathcal{S}_{k}
corresponds to
m=0
. By adopting a similar framework to that of
shah2017simple
, we can directly compare the requisite threshold for top-
k
ranking with or without differential privacy.

2.3.1
The Edge DP Algorithm for Non-Parametric Estimation

shah2017simple
shows that the Copeland counting algorithm, which simply ranks the
n
items by their number of wins, exactly recovers the top-
k
items when the
\tau
values of the true
k
th and
(k+1)
th items are separated by at least
O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{np}}\right)
. Algorithm
2
considers a differentially private version where the items are ranked by noisy numbers of wins.

Algorithm 2

Differentially Private Ranking for nonparametric models

Input
: Comparison data
(y_{ij})_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{G}}
, comparison graph
\mathcal{G}
, privacy parameter
\varepsilon
.

1:

Set
N_{i}=\sum_{j\neq i,(i,j)\in\mathcal{G}}\mathbbm{1}(Y_{ij}=1)
denote the number of comparisons won by item
i
.

2:

Generate

\bm{W}=(W_{1},W_{2},\cdots,W_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\text{Laplace}\left(\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\right).

3:

Compute the top-
k
set

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k}=\{i\in[n]:N_{i}+W_{i}\text{ is among the top $k$ largest of }\{N_{j}+W_{j}\}_{j\in[n]}\}.

Output
:
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k}
.

The estimator
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k}
defined in Algorithm
2
is
(\varepsilon,0)
-DP by the Laplace mechanism
dwork2006calibrating
: the vector
(N_{1},N_{2},\cdots,N_{n})
has
\ell_{1}
-sensitivity bounded by 2 over edge adjacent data sets, and the set
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k}
is differentially private because it post-processes
\{N_{j}+W_{j}\}_{j\in[n]}
.

\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k}
recovers
\mathcal{S}_{k}
within tolerance
m
as long as
\tau_{(k-m)},\tau_{(k+m+1)}
are sufficiently separated.

Theorem 2.3
.

For every
k\in[n-1]
and any sufficiently large constant
C>0
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{\rho}\in\Theta(k,m,C)}\mathbb{P}\left(d_{H}(\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k},\mathcal{S}_{k})>2m\right)<O(n^{-5}).

(2.11)

Specializing the theorem to
m=0
leads to the threshold for exact recovery.

Corollary 2.2
.

For every
k\in[n-1]
, if the matrix of pairwise probabilities
\bm{\rho}
is such that

\displaystyle|\tau_{(k)}-\tau_{(k+1)}|\geq C\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{np}}+\frac{\log n}{np\varepsilon}\right)

for a sufficiently large constant
C>0
, we have
\mathbb{P}_{\bm{\rho}}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k}\neq\mathcal{S}_{k}\right)<O(n^{-5})
.

As a further consequence, if
|\tau_{(k)}-\tau_{(k+1)}|\geq C\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{np}}+\frac{\log n}{np\varepsilon}\right)
for every
k
, then the union bound implies all
n
items can be correctly ranked with probability at least
1-O(n^{-4})
. The next section shows this threshold is optimal in the sense that no differentially private algorithm can succeed at recovering
\mathcal{S}_{k}
when
|\tau_{(k)}-\tau_{(k+1)}|<c\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{np}}+\frac{\log n}{np\varepsilon}\right)
for a sufficiently small
c
.

2.3.2
The Edge DP Lower Bound for Non-parametric Estimation

To establish the tightness of the threshold
\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{np}}+\frac{\log n}{np\varepsilon}
for differentially private ranking, we shall prove that the supremum of
\mathbb{P}_{\bm{\rho}}\left(d_{H}(\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k},\mathcal{S}_{k})>2m\right)
over the set of matrices

\displaystyle\Theta(k,m,c)=\left\{\bm{\rho}\in[0,1]^{n\times n}:\bm{\rho}+\bm{\rho}^{\top}=11^{\top},\tau_{(k-m)}-\tau_{(k+m+1)}\geq c\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{np}}+\frac{\log n}{np\varepsilon}\right)\right\},

is bounded away from
0
for sufficiently small
c
. In view of the lower bound, Theorem 2(b), in
shah2017simple
where the supremum is taken over

\displaystyle\Theta^{0}(k,m,c):=\left\{\bm{\rho}\in[0,1]^{n\times n}:\bm{\rho}+\bm{\rho}^{\top}=11^{\top},\tau_{(k-m)}-\tau_{(k+m+1)}\geq c\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{np}}\right\},

it suffices to show that the supremum of
\mathbb{P}_{\bm{\rho}}\left(d_{H}(\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k},\mathcal{S}_{k})>2m\right)
over the set

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Theta}(k,m,2c)=\left\{\bm{\rho}\in[0,1]^{n\times n}:\bm{\rho}+\bm{\rho}^{\top}=11^{\top},\tau_{(k-m)}-\tau_{(k+m+1)}\geq 2c\frac{\log n}{np\varepsilon}\right\}

is bounded away from 0, because

\Theta(k,m,c)\subseteq\Theta^{0}(k,m,2c)\cup\widetilde{\Theta}(k,m,2c)
for
c>0
.

For proving the lower bound over
\widetilde{\Theta}(k,m,2c)
, the differentially private Fano’s inequality
barber2014privacy
;
acharya2021differentially
reduces the argument to choosing a number of different
\bm{\rho}
’s in
\widetilde{\Theta}(k,m,2c)
such that the distance among the distributions induced by the chosen
\bm{\rho}
’s is sufficiently small. We defer the details to the supplement
cai2023optimal
and state the lower bound result below.

Theorem 2.4
.

Suppose the tolerance
m
is bounded by
2m\leq(1+\nu_{2})^{-1}\min\{n^{1-\nu_{1}},k,n-k\}
,
\frac{\log n}{np\varepsilon}<c_{0}
, and
\delta<c_{0}\left(m\log n\cdot n^{10m}/\varepsilon\right)^{-1}
for a sufficiently small constant
c_{0}
. There is a small constant
c(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})
such that every
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-DP estimator
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{k}
satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{\rho}\in\widetilde{\Theta}(k,m,c)}\mathbb{P}_{\bm{\rho}}\left(d_{H}(\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{k},\mathcal{S}_{k})>2m\right)\geq\frac{1}{10}

(2.12)

whenever
c<c(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})
and
n
is sufficiently large. The inequality remains true if
\bm{\rho}=\left(\rho_{ij}\right)_{i,j\in[n]}
is additionally restricted to the parametric model
\rho_{ij}=F(\theta^{*}_{i}-\theta^{*}_{j})
, as long as
F
satisfies regularity condition (A0) in Section
2.2
.

In conjunction with Theorem
2.3
, Theorem
2.4
yields that
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k}
is an optimal
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-DP estimator. Setting
m=0
in Theorem
2.4
gives the lower bound for exactly recovering the top
k
items
\mathcal{S}_{k}
. In the exact recovery case, the threshold for full ranking of
n
items is when
|\tau_{(k)}-\tau_{(k+1)}|\geq C\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{np}}+\frac{\log n}{np\varepsilon}\right)
for every
k
.

Remark 1
.

Because the lower bound continues to hold when restricted to the parametric model, it in fact settles the
O(\log n)
gap between the parametric upper bound Theorem
2.1
and the parametric lower bound Theorem
2.2
. If
\omega_{ij}=F(\theta^{*}_{i}-\theta^{*}_{j})
for some
F
satisfying regularity conditions (A0) and (A1) in Section
2.2
and
{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\in\Theta
, we have
|\tau_{(k)}-\tau_{(k+1)}|\asymp|\theta^{*}_{(k)}-\theta^{*}_{(k+1)}|
. The existence of an
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-DP estimator with a faster rate of convergence than

would contradict the lower bound above for recovering

. Under the parametric assumptions, the perturbed MLE
\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}
is minimax optimal for estimating the parameters
{\bm{\theta}}^{*}
.

3
Optimal Ranking under Individual Differential Privacy

3.1
Problem Formulation under Individual DP

In the previous section, we studied
edge
differential privacy, where each observed comparison between a fixed pair of items was protected. Here, we extend that framework to a more realistic setting in which each of
m
users performs multiple item comparisons. Specifically, each user
k\in[m]
selects
L
unordered pairs
\{i,j\}\subset[n]
(with
L
a fixed constant) and reports the outcome of each comparison. We continue to assume that all users share the same underlying preference structure: there is a matrix
\rho=(\rho_{ij})_{1\leq i<j\leq n}
with

\rho_{ij}+\rho_{ji}=1,\,\rho_{ii}=\tfrac{1}{2},

so that whenever any user compares items
i
and
j
, item
i
is chosen (i.e., “preferred”) with probability
\rho_{ij}
and item
j
with probability
\rho_{ji}
. By making
\rho
depend only on the item indices
i,j
, we assume all users draw comparisons from the same pairwise preference distribution.

Concretely, for each user
k=1,\dots,m
and each draw
l=1,\dots,L
:

1.

The user
k
selects an unordered pair
\{i,j\}\subset[n]
uniformly at random.

2.

They record
Y_{ij}^{(k,l)}=+1
if
i
is preferred, or
Y_{ij}^{(k,l)}=-1
if
j
is perferred.

All comparisons are independent across users and draws. The entire dataset is therefore

\mathcal{D}\;=\;\bigl\{\,Y^{(k,l)}_{ij}:1\leq k\leq m,\ 1\leq l\leq L,\ 1\leq i<j\leq n\bigr\},

(3.1)

where each nonzero
Y_{ij}^{(k,l)}
follows

\mathbb{P}\bigl(Y_{ij}^{(k,l)}=+1\bigr)\;=\;\rho_{ij},
and
\mathbb{P}\bigl(Y_{ij}^{(k,l)}=-1\bigr)\;=\;\rho_{ji}
.

Our objective remains to rank the
n
items by their “average preference score”
\tau_{i}
as defined in Section
2
and, for a given
k\in[n]
, recover the top
k
set.

In contrast to edge DP where protecting a single pairwise outcome sufficed, we now consider
individual
differential privacy: protecting all
L
comparisons contributed by any one user.

Individual Differential Privacy
. Let
\mathcal{D}

be the full dataset of
mL
comparison results as defined in (
3.1
). For any fixed user index
k_{0}
, define a neighboring dataset
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
by replacing

\bigl\{Y^{(k_{0},1)},\dots,Y^{(k_{0},L)}\bigr\}\;\longmapsto\;\bigl\{\widetilde{Y}^{(k_{0},1)},\dots,\widetilde{Y}^{(k_{0},L)}\bigr\},

where each
\widetilde{Y}^{(k_{0},l)}
is drawn independently using the same
\rho
. An algorithm
M
satisfied
(\varepsilon,0)
individual DP if for every measurable event
A
,

\mathbb{P}\bigl[M(\mathcal{D})\in A\bigr]\;\leq\;e^{\varepsilon}\,\mathbb{P}\bigl[M(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})\in A\bigr]+\delta.

This ensures that changing all
L
comparisons of any single user has a limited effect (controlled by
\varepsilon
) on the output.

Throughout this section, we build on the edge DP constructions of Section
2
, adapting them so that the entire bundle of
L
comparisons from each user is protected. Because each user’s
L
draws can collectively influence counts or likelihoods, our noise scales must be adjusted accordingly (roughly multiplying by
L
compared to the edge DP case). In the next subsection, we show how to privatize the maximum likelihood estimator for parametric scores, and then we turn to a counting-based approach for nonparametric top-
k
recovery under this stronger, individual-level privacy constraint.

3.2
Parametric Estimation under Individual DP

We assume the same parametric model as in Section
2
: each item
i\in[n]
carries a latent score
\theta^{*}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, and for any pair
(i,j)
, the probability that
i
is preferred over
j
is

\rho_{ij}\;=\;F\bigl(\theta^{*}_{i}-\theta^{*}_{j}\bigr),

where
F:\mathbb{R}\to(0,1)
is a known, strictly increasing function satisfying
F(x)=1-F(-x)
. Across all
m
users, each user
k
draws
L
item-pairs (with replacement), and records the outcome
Y_{ij}^{(k,l)}\in\{+1,-1\}
with probability
\rho_{ij}
and
\rho_{ji}
respectively, whenever they compare
(i,j)
.
All comparisons are independent across users and draws.

Define
M_{ij}\;=\;\left|\bigl\{\,k\in[m],\,l\in[L]:\text{user }k\text{ compares }(i,j)\bigr\}\right|
and

\bar{Y}_{ij}\;=\;\frac{1}{M_{ij}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l:\\
(i,j)\text{ compared}\end{subarray}}\frac{1+Y_{ij}^{(k,l)}}{2}\;\in[0,1].

In words,
M_{ij}
counts how many times
(i,j)
was compared over all users, and
\bar{Y}_{ij}
is the empirical fraction of times
i
was chosen over
j
.
Then, up to additive constants independent of
\theta
, the negative log-likelihood over all
mL
observations is

\mathcal{L}(\theta;Y)\;=\;\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\,M_{ij}\Bigl[-\bar{Y}_{ij}\,\log F_{ij}(\theta)\;-\;(1-\bar{Y}_{ij})\,\log\bigl(1-F_{ij}(\theta)\bigr)\Bigr],

where
F_{ij}(\theta)=F(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})
.

Individual DP estimator
. To ensure individual DP, we apply
objective perturbation
to the regularized likelihood.

\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}=\argmin_{{\bm{\theta}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}};y)+\frac{\gamma}{2}\|{\bm{\theta}}\|_{2}^{2}+\bm{w}^{\top}{\bm{\theta}},\quad\bm{w}=(w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\text{Laplace}(\lambda).

(3.2)

Since each user contributes at most
L
comparisons per pair, our noise scales must scale with
L
. We have the following individual DP guarantee.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose conditions (A0), (A1) and (A2) hold. If
\lambda\geq 8L\cdot\kappa_{1}/\varepsilon
and
\gamma\geq 8L\kappa_{2}/\varepsilon
,
\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}
as defined in Algorithm
1
is
(\varepsilon,0)
differentially private.

With the noise scales set as above and under the usual sampling regime (
m
large enough so that
n\log n/(m\,\varepsilon)=O(1)
), a similar leave-one-out analysis as the edge DP case leads to the following accuracy result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume (A0)–(A2) and fix

\varepsilon\in\bigl(c_{2}(\log n)^{-1/2},\,1\bigr)
,
with
L
a constant. Choose

\gamma=8\,L\,\kappa_{2}/\varepsilon

and

\lambda=8\,L\,\kappa_{1}/\varepsilon
.
If
m
and
n
satisfy
n\log n/(m\,\varepsilon)=O(1)
, then
\widetilde{{\bm{\theta}}}
is
(\varepsilon,0)
-DP, and it holds with probability at least
1-O(n^{-5})
that

\|\widetilde{{\bm{\theta}}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|_{\infty}\;\;\lesssim\;\;\sqrt{\frac{n\,\log n}{m}}\;+\;\frac{n\,\log n}{m\,\varepsilon}.

In particular the first term matches the non-private minimax rate
\sqrt{n\log n/m}
, and the second term quantifies the cost of individual DP.

Minimax lower bound
.
We can further show that no
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-DP estimator can achieve a smaller worst-case
\ell_{\infty}
error. By the same reduction argument from parametric estimation lower bound to top
k
ranking lower bound made in Remark 1 in Section
2
, we shall prove a ranking lower bound in the nonparametric case, which then implies the following estimation lower bound.

Theorem 3.2
.

If
\sqrt{(n\log n)/m}+(n\log n)/(m\,\varepsilon)<c_{0}
and
\delta\lesssim n^{-1-\omega}
for some
\omega>0
, then any
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-DP estimator
\widehat{\theta}
must satisfy

\sup_{\|{\bm{\theta}}\|_{\infty}\leq 1}\mathbb{E}\bigl\|\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}-{\bm{\theta}}\bigr\|_{\infty}\;\gtrsim\;\sqrt{\frac{n\,\log n}{m}}\;+\;\frac{n\,\log n}{m\,\varepsilon}.

Hence
\widetilde{{\bm{\theta}}}
of Theorem
3.1
is minimax optimal up to constants.

3.3
Nonparametric Ranking under Individual DP

Next, we drop any parametric assumption on
\rho_{ij}
and directly recover the top-
k
set based on

\tau_{i}=\frac{1}{n}\!\sum_{j=1}^{n}\rho_{ij}
.

Count-based estimator
. Let
N_{i}
be the total wins of item
i
. Replacing one user’s
L
comparisons can change each
N_{i}
by at most
L
, and we therefore add i.i.d.
\mathrm{Laplace}(L/\varepsilon)
noise to each coordinate.

By the Laplace mechanism
dwork2006calibrating
, the resulting noisy counts

\widetilde{N}_{i}
in Algorithm
3
are
(\varepsilon,0)
-DP, and ranking items by
\{\widetilde{N}_{i}\}
preserves individual-level differential privacy.

Algorithm 3

Individual-DP Top-
k
via Noisy Counts

Input:
All comparisons
\{Y^{(k,l)}\}
and privacy parameter
\varepsilon>0
.

1:

For each
i\in[n]
, compute

N_{i}\;=\;\sum_{j\neq i}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{l=1}^{L}\mathbbm{1}\bigl(Y_{ij}^{(k,l)}=+1\bigr).

2:

Draw
W_{1},\dots,W_{n}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathrm{Laplace}\bigl(L/\varepsilon\bigr).

3:

Form
\widetilde{N}_{i}=N_{i}+W_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,n
.

4:

Output
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k}=
the indices of the top
k
values among
\{\widetilde{N}_{i}\}
.

Output:

\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k}
.

Fix a tolerance
u\in\{0,1,\dots,\min(k-1,n-k-1)\}
. Define

\Theta(k,u,c)=\Bigl\{\rho:\,[0,1]^{n\times n},\;\rho_{ij}+\rho_{ji}=1,\;\tau_{(k-u)}-\tau_{(k+u+1)}\;\geq\;c\Bigl(\sqrt{\tfrac{n\log n}{m}}+\tfrac{n\log n}{m\,\varepsilon}\Bigr)\Bigr\},

where
\tau_{(1)}\geq\tau_{(2)}\geq\cdots\geq\tau_{(n)}
. We show that the noisy counts succeed at recovering the top items with overwhelming probability.

Theorem 3.3
.

There exists
C>0
such that, for any
k
and
u
, if
\rho\in\Theta(k,u,C)
, we have

\mathbb{P}\bigl(d_{H}(\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k},\mathcal{S}_{k})>2u\bigr)\;=\;O(n^{-5}).

In particular, when
u=0
, exact recovery holds with probability
1-O(n^{-5})
provided that

\tau_{(k)}-\tau_{(k+1)}\;\geq\;C\Bigl(\sqrt{\tfrac{n\log n}{m}}+\tfrac{n\log n}{m\,\varepsilon}\Bigr).

As a further consequence, if
|\tau_{(k)}-\tau_{(k+1)}|\geq C\left(\sqrt{\frac{n\log n}{m}}+\frac{n\log n}{m\varepsilon}\right)
for every
k
, then the union bound implies all
n
items can be correctly ranked with probability at least
1-O(n^{-4})
. The next theorem shows this threshold is optimal in the sense that no differentially private algorithm can succeed at recovering
\mathcal{S}_{k}
when

Numerical Experiments

We conduct three sets of experiments to evaluate the numerical performance of our algorithms and to guide their practical application.

In Sections

To complement these theoretical and simulation studies, Section

For practical applications of our ranking algorithms, we highlight three findings from the experiment results.

•

For recovering ranks, the non-parametric, counting based algorithm generally outperform the parametric estimation algorithm, in both edge and individual DP and across all settings of

,

\varepsilon

5
Discussion

In this paper, we proposed differentially private algorithms for ranking based on pairwise comparisons, analyzed their rates of convergence, and established their optimality among all differentially private ranking procedures. Our theoretical results were supported by numerical experiments on both real and simulated datasets. We conclude by outlining several directions for future research.

Further theoretical analysis of individual DP
. A comparison between edge DP and individual DP reveals intriguing differences in how privacy constraints impact statistical accuracy. For example, under edge DP, the rate of convergence improves as the number of items
n
increases, whereas under individual DP, it worsens. This divergence highlights fundamentally different statistical costs associated with the two privacy models. However, our theoretical treatment of individual DP remains less comprehensive than that of edge DP, largely due to the simplifying assumption that the number of comparisons
L
contributed by each individual is fixed and known. Removing or relaxing this assumption may lead to a deeper understanding of individual DP in more realistic settings.

The cost of approximate versus pure differential privacy
.
Interestingly, the optimal
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-DP ranking algorithms proposed in this paper also satisfy the stronger
(\varepsilon,0)
-DP, possibly implying that, for our ranking problem, the cost of “pure” differential privacy is not higher than that of “approximate” differential privacy, when measured by the minimax rate of convergence and ignoring the exact constants in the minimax risk. This phenomenon stands in contrast with differentially private (Gaussian) mean estimation in high dimensions
barber2014privacy
;
steinke2017between
;
cai2021cost
, where the optimal rate of convergence with
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-DP explicitly depends on
\delta
. It is an interesting theoretical question to understand the conditions under which approximate
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-DP is strictly less costly to statistical accuracy than pure
(\varepsilon,0)
-DP, in terms of either the minimax rate or better constants at the same minimax rate.

Lower bound technique for entry-wise parametric estimation
. Under the parametric model, a single dose of noise added to the objective function simplifies the privacy analysis, and avoids potentially higher privacy cost incurred by iterative noise addition required by methods such as noisy gradient descent. The entry-wise error analysis of the perturbed MLE is potentially applicable in other statistical problems where entry-wise or
\ell_{\infty}
errors are of primary interest. On the lower bound side, the entry-wise version of score attack in Section
2.2.2
results in a
O(\log n)
gap from the optimal lower bound in Section
2.3.2
. One would wonder if this method can be strengthened to eliminate such gaps.

in Section
A.2.2
, we further know that the objective function is
(\gamma+3\kappa_{2}np)
-smooth. The step size
\eta=(\gamma+3\kappa_{2}np)^{-1}
then guarantees that the gradient descent steps defined by (
A.4
) obey

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{t+1}-\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}\|_{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{\gamma+3\kappa_{2}np}\right)\|\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{t}-\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}\|_{2},\quad t\in\mathbb{N},

implying that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{t+1}-\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}\|_{2}\leq\exp\left(\frac{-t\gamma}{\gamma+3\kappa_{2}np}\right)\|\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{0}-\hat{\bm{\theta}}\|_{2}\leq\exp\left(\frac{-c_{0}t}{(c_{0}+3\kappa_{2})n}\right)\|\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{0}-\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}\|_{2}.

(A.14)

The second step is true by
p\leq 1
and
\gamma=c_{0}\sqrt{np\log n}\leq c_{0}n
and
\gamma\geq c_{0}
.

As
\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{0}={\bm{\theta}}^{*}
, we can bound
\|\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{0}-\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}\|_{2}
by observing that

\displaystyle\gamma\|{\bm{\theta}}^{*}-\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}\|^{2}_{2}\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}})-\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}({\bm{\theta}}^{*})-\nabla\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}({\bm{\theta}}^{*})^{\top}({\bm{\theta}}^{*}-\widetilde{\bm{\theta}})\leq\|\nabla\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}({\bm{\theta}}^{*})\|_{2}\|{\bm{\theta}}^{*}-\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}\|_{2}.

\displaystyle\|{\bm{\theta}}^{*}-\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}\|_{2}\leq\gamma^{-1}\|\nabla\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}({\bm{\theta}}^{*})\|_{2}\leq\gamma^{-1}\left(\|\nabla\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}}^{*})\|_{2}+\gamma\|{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|_{2}+\|\bm{w}\|_{2}\right).

Now assume events

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}=\left\{\|\nabla\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}}^{*})\|_{2}\leq 3\kappa_{1}\sqrt{n^{2}p\log n}\right\},\mathcal{A}_{2}=\left\{|\bm{1}^{\top}\bm{w}|\leq 8\lambda\sqrt{n\log n},\|\bm{w}\|_{\infty}\leq 9\lambda\log n\right\}

occur, the probability of which is at least
1-O(n^{-8})
by Lemmas
A.1
and
A.2
. Because
\gamma\asymp\sqrt{np\log n}
and
\gamma\gtrsim\lambda
, the bound of
\|{\bm{\theta}}-\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}\|_{2}
above reduces to

\displaystyle\|{\bm{\theta}}^{*}-\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}\|_{2}\leq\gamma^{-1}\|\nabla\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}({\bm{\theta}}^{*})\|_{2}\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{n^{2}p\log n}}{\gamma}+\sqrt{n}+\frac{\lambda\log n\sqrt{n}}{\gamma}\lesssim\sqrt{n}\log n.

Plugging the bound into (
A.14
) and setting
t=\frac{c_{0}+3\kappa_{2}}{c_{0}}n^{3}
yields

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{t+1}-\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}\|_{2}\lesssim e^{-n^{2}}\sqrt{n}\log n\lesssim\frac{\log n}{n}.

By the inductive step (
A.13
) and the union bound over
O(n^{3})
iterations, each of which hold with probability at least
1-O(n^{-8})
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{t+1}-\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}\|_{2}+\|\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{t+1}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{np}}+\frac{\lambda\log n}{np}\right)

with probability at least
1-O(n^{-5})
.

A.2.2
Proof of the inductive step

In this section we prove the inductive step in Section
A.2.1
. Throughout the proof we assume the following high-probability events simultaneously occur.

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}=\left\{\frac{np}{2}\leq d_{\min}(\mathcal{G})\leq d_{\max}(\mathcal{G})\leq\frac{3np}{2}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}=\left\{\|\nabla\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}}^{*})\|_{2}\leq 3\kappa_{1}\sqrt{n^{2}p\log n}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}=\left\{|\bm{1}^{\top}\bm{w}|\leq 8\lambda\sqrt{n\log n},\|\bm{w}\|_{\infty}\leq 9\lambda\log n\right\}.

Lemma 1 in
chen2019spectral
shows that events
\mathcal{A}_{0}
occurs with probability at least
1-O(n^{-10})
, as long as
p\gtrsim\log n/m
as we assumed in Proposition
2.5
. We bound the probabilities of
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
in the lemmas below and defer their proofs to Section
A.2.3
.

Lemma A.1
.

If
p\geq c\log n/n
for a sufficiently large constant
c>0
,
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}_{1})\geq 1-O(n^{-10})
.

Lemma A.2
.

If the coordinates
W_{1},W_{2},\cdots,W_{n}
of
\bm{w}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
are drawn i.i.d. from the (zero-mean) Laplace distribution with scale parameter
\lambda
, then
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}_{2})\geq 1-O(n^{-8})
.

Now, the inductive step will be proved in four parts, from Proposition
A.1
to Proposition
A.4
.

Proposition A.1
.

Suppose (
A.10
), (
A.13
) are true for a sufficiently large
C_{1}>0
and
\eta=(\gamma+3\kappa_{2}np)^{-1}
, then it holds with probability at least
1-O(n^{-8})
that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{t+1}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|_{2}<C_{1}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{p}}+\frac{\lambda\log n}{\sqrt{n}p}\right).

(A.15)

[Proof of Proposition
A.1
]
Throughout the proof, we assume that events
\mathcal{A}_{0},\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}
simultaneously occur, the probability of which is at least
1-O(n^{-8})
.

By (
A.4
) and the definition of
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}({\bm{\theta}})
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{t+1}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{t}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}-\eta\left(\nabla\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{t})-\nabla\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}({\bm{\theta}}^{*})\right)-\eta\nabla\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}({\bm{\theta}}^{*})

\displaystyle=(1-\eta\gamma)(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{t}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*})-\eta\left(\nabla{\mathcal{L}}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{t})-\nabla{\mathcal{L}}({\bm{\theta}}^{*})\right)-\eta\nabla\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}({\bm{\theta}}^{*}).

(A.16)

By the mean value theorem for vector-valued functions (
lang2012real
, Chapter XIII, Theorem 4.2), we have

\displaystyle\nabla{\mathcal{L}}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{t})-\nabla{\mathcal{L}}({\bm{\theta}}^{*})=\int_{0}^{1}\nabla^{2}\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}}(\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau\cdot(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{t}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}),

where

\bm{H}=\int_{0}^{1}\nabla^{2}\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}}(\tau))d\tau

A.16

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{t+1}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|_{2}\leq\|((1-\eta\gamma)\bm{I}-\eta\bm{H})(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{t}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*})\|_{2}+\eta\|\nabla\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}({\bm{\theta}}^{*})\|_{2}.

The second term on the right side can be bounded as follows, under high-probability events
\mathcal{A}_{1}

\mathcal{A}_{2}

\displaystyle\|\nabla\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}({\bm{\theta}}^{*})\|_{2}\leq\|\nabla\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}}^{*})\|_{2}+\gamma\|{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|_{2}+\|\bm{w}\|_{2}\leq\left(3\kappa_{1}\sqrt{n^{2}p\log n}+c_{0}\sqrt{n^{2}p\log n}+9\lambda\sqrt{n}\log n\right).

(A.18)

In bounding
\|{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|_{2}

\|{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|_{\infty}<1

It remains to bound the first term on the right side of (

A.9

\bm{H}\bm{1}=0

\displaystyle\|((1-\eta\gamma)\bm{I}-\eta\bm{H})(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{t}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*})\|_{2}

[\nabla\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}}^{*})]_{i}
is sub-Gaussian with
proxy variance
\kappa_{1}^{2}S
.

Step 2: tail bound for one coordinate.

For any
t>0
,

\mathbb{P}\bigl(|g_{i}|>t\bigr)\;\leq\;2\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{t^{2}}{2\kappa_{1}^{2}S}\Bigr),\qquad g_{i}:=\bigl[\nabla\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}}^{*})\bigr]_{i}.

Choose
t=C_{*}\kappa_{1}\sqrt{S\log n}
with

C_{*}=\sqrt{33}
.
Then

\mathbb{P}\bigl(|g_{i}|>t\bigr)\leq 2n^{-C_{*}^{2}/2}=2n^{-16.5}
.

Step 3: union bound over
n
coordinates.

Summing over
i=1,\dots,n
gives

\mathbb{P}\!\Bigl(\|\nabla\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}}^{*})\|_{2}>C_{*}\kappa_{1}\sqrt{nS\log n}\Bigr)\;\leq\;2n^{-15.5}\;=\;O\!\bigl(n^{-10}\bigr),

which is the stated result.
∎

Lemma B.3

(Laplace vector tail)
.

Let
\mathbf{w}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
have i.i.d. Laplace
(\lambda)
entries with
density
\tfrac{1}{2\lambda}e^{-|x|/\lambda}
.
Then

\mathbb{P}\!\Bigl(\|\mathbf{w}\|_{\infty}>9\lambda\log n\;\text{ or }\;|\mathbf{1}^{\!\top}\mathbf{w}|>8\lambda\sqrt{n\log n}\Bigr)\;\leq\;O\!\bigl(n^{-8}\bigr).

Proof.

Max-norm
For any Laplace r.v.

\mathbb{P}(|W|>t)=e^{-t/\lambda}
.
Therefore

\mathbb{P}(\|{\mathbf{w}}\|_{\infty}>t)\leq ne^{-t/\lambda}
.
With
t=9\lambda\log n
this is
n^{-8}
.

Sum.

\mathbf{1}^{\!\top}\mathbf{w}
is the difference of two
independent
\mathrm{Gamma}(n,\lambda)
variables, hence sub-exponential
with parameters
(2\lambda,4)
.
Bernstein’s inequality for sub-exponential sums

(
vershynin2018high
, Lem. 2.7.1)

gives

\mathbb{P}\bigl(|\mathbf{1}^{\!\top}\mathbf{w}|>t\bigr)\;\leq\;2\exp\!\Bigl(-\min\!\bigl\{\tfrac{t^{2}}{8n\lambda^{2}},\;\tfrac{t}{4\lambda}\bigr\}\Bigr).

Taking
t=8\lambda\sqrt{n\log n}
, the quadratic term dominates and
the probability is
\leq 2e^{-8\log n}=2n^{-8}
.
∎

Lemma B.4

(spectrum of the Hessian)
.

Let
S=2mL/n
and assume
mL\geq c\,n\log n
for a sufficiently large
absolute constant
c
. Then, with probability at least

1-O(n^{-10})
, the following holds simultaneously for every

{\bm{\theta}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
satisfying
\|{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
:

\frac{S}{2\kappa_{2}}\;\leq\;\lambda_{2}\!\bigl(\nabla^{2}\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}})\bigr)\;\leq\;\lambda_{\max}\!\bigl(\nabla^{2}\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}})\bigr)\;\leq\;3\kappa_{2}S.

Proof.

Step 0: decomposing the Hessian.

Each comparison
\ell=1,\dots,mL
involves an unordered pair

(I_{\ell},J_{\ell})
chosen uniformly from
\binom{[n]}{2}
.
Write

X_{\ell}({\bm{\theta}})\;:=\;c_{\ell}({\bm{\theta}})\,(\mathbf{e}_{I_{\ell}}-\mathbf{e}_{J_{\ell}})(\mathbf{e}_{I_{\ell}}-\mathbf{e}_{J_{\ell}})^{\!\top},\qquad c_{\ell}({\bm{\theta}}):=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\!\bigl[-\log F(x)\bigr]_{x=\theta_{I_{\ell}}-\theta_{J_{\ell}}}.

Assumption (A2) implies

\kappa_{2}^{-1}\leq c_{\ell}({\bm{\theta}})\leq\kappa_{2}
whenever

\|{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
.
Because the comparisons are independent,

\{X_{\ell}({\bm{\theta}})\}_{\ell=1}^{mL}
are also independent conditioned on

{\bm{\theta}}
.
The Hessian can be written as

H({\bm{\theta}}):=\nabla^{2}\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}})\;=\;\sum_{\ell=1}^{mL}X_{\ell}({\bm{\theta}}).

Step 1: expectation of the Hessian.

Since
(I_{\ell},J_{\ell})
is uniform,

\mathbb{E}X_{\ell}({\bm{\theta}})=\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}c_{ij}({\bm{\theta}})(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})^{\!\top},

where
c_{ij}({\bm{\theta}})\in[\kappa_{2}^{-1},\kappa_{2}]
.
Consequently,

\kappa_{2}^{-1}\,\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i<j}(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})^{\!\top}\;\preceq\;\mathbb{E}X_{\ell}({\bm{\theta}})\;\preceq\;\kappa_{2}\times(\text{same sum}).

The middle matrix is the Laplacian of the
complete graph
with
edge-weight
2/n(n-1)
; its non-zero eigenvalues all equal
2/n
.
Hence

\lambda_{2}\!\bigl(\mathbb{E}X_{\ell}({\bm{\theta}})\bigr)=\frac{2}{n}\,\frac{1}{\kappa_{2}},\quad\lambda_{\max}\!\bigl(\mathbb{E}X_{\ell}({\bm{\theta}})\bigr)=\frac{2}{n}\,\kappa_{2}.

Therefore

\mu_{-}:=\lambda_{2}\!\bigl(\mathbb{E}H({\bm{\theta}})\bigr)\,\geq\,\frac{mL}{n}\,\frac{2}{\kappa_{2}}=\frac{S}{\kappa_{2}},\quad\mu_{+}:=\lambda_{\max}\!\bigl(\mathbb{E}H({\bm{\theta}})\bigr)\,\leq\,\frac{S}{\kappa_{2}^{-1}}=\kappa_{2}S.

Step 2: matrix Chernoff-lower tail for
\lambda_{2}
.

Let

P:=I-\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\!\top}

be the projector onto

\mathbf{1}^{\perp}\;(\dim=n-1)
.
Define

Y_{\ell}({\bm{\theta}})\;:=\;P\,X_{\ell}({\bm{\theta}})\,P,\qquad\ell=1,\dots,mL.

Because
P
is deterministic, the
Y_{\ell}
’s are independent and
positive-semidefinite; moreover

\|Y_{\ell}({\bm{\theta}})\|_{2}\leq\|X_{\ell}({\bm{\theta}})\|_{2}\leq R:=4\kappa_{2}
.

Since
H({\bm{\theta}})=\sum_{\ell}X_{\ell}({\bm{\theta}})
and

PHP=\sum_{\ell}Y_{\ell}({\bm{\theta}})
,

\lambda_{2}\!\bigl(H({\bm{\theta}})\bigr)\;=\;\lambda_{\min}\!\Bigl(P\,H({\bm{\theta}})\,P\Bigr)\;=\;\lambda_{\min}\!\Bigl(\sum_{\ell}Y_{\ell}({\bm{\theta}})\Bigr).

Set
\mu_{-}:=\lambda_{\min}\!\bigl(\sum_{\ell}\mathbb{E}\,Y_{\ell}({\bm{\theta}})\bigr)=S/\kappa_{2}

(computed in Step 1).
The matrix Chernoff lower-tail inequality
(
tropp2012user
, Thm 1.1)

now gives, for any

\delta\in(0,1)
,

\mathbb{P}\!\Bigl\{\lambda_{2}\!\bigl(H({\bm{\theta}})\bigr)<(1-\delta)\mu_{-}\Bigr\}\;\leq\;(n-1)\,\Bigl[\frac{e^{-\delta}}{(1-\delta)^{1-\delta}}\Bigr]^{\mu_{-}/R},\qquad R:=4\kappa_{2}.

Choose
\delta=\tfrac{1}{2}
; then

\bigl[e^{-\delta}/(1-\delta)^{1-\delta}\bigr]=e^{-1/8}
and

\frac{\mu_{-}}{R}=\frac{S}{4\kappa_{2}^{2}}\;\geq\;c^{\prime}\,\log n\quad\text{for a large enough constant }c^{\prime},

because
S=\frac{2mL}{n}\gtrsim\log n
.
Therefore

\mathbb{P}\!\Bigl\{\lambda_{2}\!\bigl(H({\bm{\theta}})\bigr)<\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{-}\Bigr\}\;\leq\;(n-1)\,e^{-\mu_{-}/8R}\;\leq\;n^{-12}.

Step 3: matrix Chernoff-upper tail.

The upper-tail matrix Chernoff bound (same reference) yields

\mathbb{P}\Bigl\{\lambda_{\max}\!\bigl(H({\bm{\theta}})\bigr)>3\mu_{+}\Bigr\}\;\leq\;n\,\Bigl(\tfrac{e^{2}}{3^{3}}\Bigr)^{\mu_{+}/R}\;\leq\;n^{-12},

again because
\mu_{+}/R\gtrsim S/\kappa_{2}^{2}\gtrsim\log n
.

Step 4: conclude and union over
{\bm{\theta}}
.

With probability at least
1-2n^{-12}
,

\frac{1}{2}\mu_{-}\;\leq\;\lambda_{2}(H({\bm{\theta}})),\qquad\lambda_{\max}(H({\bm{\theta}}))\;\leq\;3\mu_{+},

i.e.,

\lambda_{2}(H)\geq S/(2\kappa_{2})

and

\lambda_{\max}(H)\leq 3\kappa_{2}S.

Because the constants and the bounds are uniform for all

{\bm{\theta}}
with
\|{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
,
a union bound over any
\varepsilon
-net followed by standard
Lipschitz arguments (as in
chen2019spectral
, Lem. 10) upgrades
the probability to
1-O(n^{-10})
while still covering the
entire

\ell_{\infty}
-ball of radius 1 around
{\bm{\theta}}^{*}
.
∎

B.2.1
Main Proof of Theorem
3.1

First we show that
\widetilde{\theta}
is
(\varepsilon,0)
-DP. For which we need to need to check that the prescribed choice of
\gamma
is greater than

. This boils down to

where
c^{\prime}
is a constant that depends on

and
L
. Since
\varepsilon\gtrsim(\log n)^{-1/2}

\varepsilon(\log n)^{2}
blows up, where as the RHS is
O(1)
by our assumption. This implies that the algorithm is
(\varepsilon,0)
-DP.

Notation and step size.

Recall
S=2mL/n
and set

\eta\;:=\;(\gamma+3\kappa_{2}S)^{-1}.

Define the gradient-descent iterates

\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{(t+1)}=\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{(t)}-\eta\,\Bigl(\nabla\mathcal{L}\bigl(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{(t)}\bigr)+\gamma\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{(t)}+\mathbf{w}\Bigr),\qquad\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{(0)}={\bm{\theta}}^{*},

and for every coordinate

{\bm{\theta}}^{(m,t)}

-

.

.

Induction hypotheses.

For constants
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4}
(chosen large but absolute)
and every

let

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{(t)}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\!\Bigl(\sqrt{\tfrac{\log n}{S}}+\tfrac{L\lambda\log n}{\sqrt{n}\,S}\Bigr),

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{(t)}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\max_{m}\|{\bm{\theta}}^{(m,t)}-\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{(t)}\|_{2}

(H4)

They hold at

Exactly the same algebra as in Propositions

-
A.4

B.4
implies

B.4
Proof of Theorem

Observe that

\displaystyle\bigcap_{a\in\mathcal{S}_{k-u},b\in(\mathcal{S}_{k+u})^{c}}\{N_{a}+W_{a}>N_{b}+W_{b}\}\subseteq\{d_{H}(\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k},\mathcal{S}_{k})\leq 2u\}.

The union bound then implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(d_{H}(\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k},\mathcal{S}_{k})>2u)\leq\sum_{a\in\mathcal{S}_{k-u},b\in(\mathcal{S}_{k+u})^{c}}\mathbb{P}(N_{a}+W_{a}\leq N_{b}+W_{b})

(B.4)

Fix
a\in\mathcal{S}_{k-u}
and
b\in(\mathcal{S}_{k+u})^{c}
. We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}N_{a}-\mathbb{E}N_{b}=n\frac{mL}{{n\choose 2}}(\tau_{a}-\tau_{b})\geq n\frac{mL}{{n\choose 2}}(\tau_{(k-u)}-\tau_{(k+u+1)})\geq C\left(\sqrt{\frac{m\log n}{n}}+\frac{\log n}{\varepsilon}\right),

for some

, and therefore

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(N_{a}+W_{a}\leq N_{b}+W_{b})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(N_{a}-N_{b}\leq W_{b}-W_{a})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(N_{a}-N_{b}-(\mathbb{E}N_{a}-\mathbb{E}N_{b})\leq-C\sqrt{\frac{m\log n}{n}})+\mathbb{P}(W_{b}-W_{a}\geq C\log n/\varepsilon).

To bound the first term
\mathbb{P}(N_{a}-N_{b}-(\mathbb{E}N_{a}-\mathbb{E}N_{b})\leq-C\sqrt{\frac{m\log n}{n}})

\displaystyle N_{a}-N_{b}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l}(\mathbbm{1}(Y^{(k,l)}_{ab}=1)-\mathbbm{1}(Y^{(k,l)}_{ba}=1))+\sum_{j\neq a,b}\left[\sum_{k,l}\mathbbm{1}(Y^{(k,l)}_{aj}=1)-\mathbbm{1}(Y^{(k,l)}_{bj}=1)\right]

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k,l}d^{(k,l)}_{0}+\sum_{j\neq a,b}\sum_{k,l}d^{(k,l)}_{j}.

As the
d^{(k,l)}_{0}
and
d^{(k,l)}_{j}
’s are all independent and bounded by
-1
and
1
, we bound the tail probability by Bernstein’s inequality, as follows. The second moments of the

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(d^{(k,l)}_{0})^{2}=\frac{1}{{n\choose 2}}(\rho_{ab}+\rho_{ba}),\,\mathbb{E}(d^{(k,l)}_{j})^{2}=\frac{1}{{n\choose 2}}\rho_{aj}\left(1-\frac{1}{{n\choose 2}}\rho_{bj}\right)+\frac{1}{{n\choose 2}}\rho_{bj}\left(1-\frac{1}{{n\choose 2}}\rho_{aj}\right)\leq\frac{1}{{n\choose 2}}(\rho_{aj}+\rho_{bj}).

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l}\left(\mathbb{E}(d^{(k,l)}_{0})^{2}+\sum_{j\neq a,b}\mathbb{E}(d^{(k,l)}_{j})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(N_{a}-N_{b}-\mathbb{E}N_{a}-\mathbb{E}N_{b}\leq-C\sqrt{\frac{m\log n}{n}}\right)

The last inequality follows from the fact that
\frac{n\log n}{m\varepsilon}=O(1)
which follows from the fact that
\bm{\rho}\in\Theta(k,u,C)
.
Plugging into (
B.5
), we have

and

are Laplace random variable with scale

where

Finally, by the union bound (

), we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{KL}(\bm{X}^{(k,l)},\bm{Y}^{(k,l)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\mathrm{KL}(X_{i,j}^{(k,l)},Y_{i,j}^{(k,l)})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{B}_{a}\cup\mathcal{B}_{b}}\mathrm{KL}(X_{i,j}^{(k,l)},Y_{i,j}^{(k,l)})+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{B}_{a}\cup\mathcal{B}_{b}}\mathrm{KL}(X_{i,j}^{(k,l)},Y_{i,j}^{(k,l)})

\displaystyle\leq c(\nu_{2})\frac{n|\mathcal{B}_{a}\cup\mathcal{B}_{b}|}{{n\choose 2}}\Delta^{2}\lesssim\frac{nu}{{n\choose 2}}\Delta^{2}

By using the independence of the samples and tensorization property of the KL divergence we have that

\mathrm{KL}(\{\bm{X}^{(k,l)}\}_{k\in[m],l\in[L]},\{\bm{Y}^{(k,l)}\}_{k\in[m],l\in[L]})\lesssim\frac{mLnu}{{n\choose 2}}\Delta^{2}

Hence we have that condition
(b)
of Theorem
A.1
is satisfied with
\beta=\frac{mLu}{n}\Delta^{2}
and we can choose
\Delta=c^{\prime}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},L)\sqrt{\frac{n\log n}{m}}
such that (
B.7
) is satisfied. Hence if we choose
\Delta=C\left(\sqrt{\frac{n\log n}{m}}+\frac{n\log n}{m\varepsilon}\right)
for
C\lesssim c(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},L)\wedge c(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},L)
then either the lower bound(private or no private) would imply that estimation in Hamming distance is not possible.

For the restriction to parametric model
\rho_{ij}=F(\theta^{*}_{i}-\theta^{*}_{j})

a\in[M]

\left(\bm{\rho}_{a}\right)_{ij}

{\bm{\theta}}^{*}

F(x)=1-F(x)
and
F(0)=1/2
; it then suffices to let
\theta^{*}_{i}=\alpha
for all
i\in\mathcal{S}^{a}_{k}

\theta^{*}_{i}=\beta

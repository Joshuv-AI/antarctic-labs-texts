Resurgent structure of the ’t Hooft-Polyakov monopole
Michal Malinský

Abstract
In this letter we present a comprehensive analysis of the differential equations governing the spatial profile of the ’t Hooft-Polyakov monopole from the viewpoint of resurgence theory. We note that the special shape of the gauge-component asymptotics, together with the simplicity of its Borel transform and the Green’s kernels in the Volterra equations, provides a complete control over the proliferation of the Borel-plane singularities to all orders and for any λ / e 2 > 0 \lambda/e^{2}>0 , along with full information about the relevant logarithmic discontinuities. Hence, the resurgent structure of the non-BPS solutions turns out to be unexpectedly simple and its resonance at the origin of the Borel plane even admits an analytic calculation of the normalization coefficients of their asymptotic forms to an arbitrary level of precision.

Introduction

It is well known that non-linear ordinary differential equations (ODEs) with domains stretching all the way to
+\infty

\displaystyle y^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y\,z^{2}+y(y^{2}-1)/x^{2}\,,

(1)

\displaystyle z^{\prime\prime}+2z^{\prime}/x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2z\,y^{2}/x^{2}+\beta z(z^{2}-1)\,,

(2)

(with
\beta\equiv\lambda/e^{2}

The key to this is the fact that, due to the exponentially fast saturation of the

At the same time, the

Remarkably (and crucially for the case we intend to make), its simple and only

This information is invaluable for the practicality of the attempts to develop a (large

Moreover, the structure of the leading singularities at

II
The MNBPS monopole (
\beta\to\infty
)

The variant of Eq. (
1
) governing the gauge-field profile of the MNBPS monopole, subject to boundary conditions
y|_{x\to 0}\to 1
and

y|_{x\to\infty}\to 0
, reads

y^{\prime\prime}=y+(y^{3}-y)/x^{2}\,.

(5)

Despite its apparent simplicity (note the triviality of the crosstalk with the scalar sector
z(x)=1

\forall x>0
), Eq. (
5
) is still notoriously hard (2nd order, non-linear, non-conservative, with boundary conditions as limits in the two singular points of its domain).

The Borel-plane representation of the solution of its linearized form (
15
), cf. Appendices
A
and
B
, suggests a substitution

y(x)=xe^{-x}f(x)
,
for which Eq. (
5
) assumes a simple symbolic form

L_{\infty}[f](x)=e^{-2x}f(x)^{3}
.
Here

L_{\infty}[f](x)\equiv f^{\prime\prime}(x)-2f^{\prime}(x)+\tfrac{2f^{\prime}(x)}{x}-\tfrac{2f(x)}{x}+\tfrac{f(x)}{x^{2}}

(6)

is the differential operator of the linear (in
f
) problem, and the RHS is a fast-decaying non-linear forcing term.
In the Borel plane, Eq. (
5
) is thus equivalent to

t(t+2)\hat{f}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\,K(t,s)\hat{f}(s)=[\hat{f}*\hat{f}*\hat{f}](t-2)\,,

(7)

where

K(t,s)=t-3s-2
is a regular kernel corresponding to the non-linear (in
x
) part of the differential operator (
6
), and the star symbols in the square bracket stand for (associative and commutative) Borel convolutions

[\hat{h}_{1}*\hat{h}_{2}](t)\equiv\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\,\hat{h}_{1}(s)\hat{h}_{2}(t-s)\,.

II.1
Proliferation of (leading) singularities

The Borel-plane picture of the solution of the linearized version of Eq. (
5
) written in terms of
\hat{f}_{0}(t)=A_{\infty}\sqrt{\tfrac{\pi}{2}}F(t)
(see Appendix
B
) trivially obeys Eq. (
7
) with zero RHS and, as such, it represents the initial level of the ladder of approximations to the full
\hat{f}=\hat{f}_{0}+\hat{f}_{1}+\ldots
. Higher orders are then obtained by iterations exploiting the triangular structure of Eq. (
7
), for instance

\hat{f}_{1}(t)=\frac{1}{t(t+2)}\!\left[[\hat{f}_{0}\!*\!\hat{f}_{0}\!*\!\hat{f}_{0}](t-2)-\!\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\,K(t,s)\hat{f}_{1}(s)\right],

and so on.
Throughout these, proliferation of the primary
t=-2
singularity of
\hat{f}_{0}
is governed by three principles: i) Convolutions generate new singularities at points corresponding to shifts by position-vectors of singularities of individual components; the
(t-2)
argument on the RHS of Eq. (
7
) then shifts all these by two units to the right. ii) The
1/t(t+2)
factor in front of the square bracket above generates new singularities at
t=0
and
t=-2
(or reduces powers of such terms in numerators). iii) Every further integration with the regular kernel
K(t,s)
reduces the strength of the singularity underneath.

Hence, due to i) and ii), the singularities of
\hat{f}
will eventually populate the real axis of the Borel plane at discrete
t_{\rm sing.}=2k
(
k\in\mathbb{Z}
) points. By virtue of iii), the leading such singularities (i.e., terms with largest positive powers of logs or poles) always come from the convolution part of Eq. (
7
). Besides the poles at
t=2n,n\in\mathbb{N}
, defining the exponential scales
e^{-2nx}
in
y(x)
(fully reflected by the structure of the asymptotic trans-series of Appendix
C
), the most interesting singularities for our purposes here are the
n
-th level branch points at
t=0
, which assume the algebraic forms

\hat{f}_{n}(t)|_{t=0\;{\rm sing.}}\sim c_{k,l}t^{2k-1}{\log^{l}t},

(8)

for
k\leq n
,
l\leq k
;
c_{k,l}
are numerical coefficients
calculable in principle in a closed form
from the preceding levels’
\hat{f}
’s (cf. Appendix
E
).
Under the eventual (small-
x
) Laplace transform, the
t^{2k-1}{\log^{l}t}
structures in (
8
) yield

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}t}{x}e^{-t/x}t^{2k-1}{\log^{l}t}\sim x^{2k-1}\log^{l}x+\ldots\;.

(9)

Crucially, the coefficient of the
x^{2n-1}\log^{n}x
contribution in the
x
-plane can be independently determined from the
y|_{x\to 0}\to 1
boundary condition imposed on the locally convergent power-log expansion around
x=0
, cf. Appendix
D
; since, however,
c_{n,n}

factorizes
the
(2n+1)
-th power of
A_{\infty}
, the latter can thus be calculated
analytically
at any given order (and, with large-enough
n
, arbitrary level of precision), see also Appendix
E
.
Note that the key to this mechanism is the fact that the structure of Eq. (
7
) prevents the singularities from landing anywhere but at
t_{\rm sing.}=2k
with
k\in\mathbb{Z}
; hence, with each iteration, a higher-power
\log t
hits
t=0
, as anticipated in Eq. (
8
).

III
The general case with
\beta>0

Remarkably enough, the same singularity pattern emerges even for finite
\beta>0
. Indeed, with the substitutions
y(x)=xe^{-x}f(x)
and
z(x)=1-g(x)
, both
f
and
g
can be expanded into asymptotic series with
e^{-2nx}
towers, since Eqs. (
1
)-(
2
) in these coordinates read

\displaystyle L_{f}[f]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-2x}f^{3}+g(g-2)f\,,

(10)

\displaystyle L_{g}[g]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2e^{-2x}(g-1)f+\beta g(g-1)(g-2)\,,

with
L_{f}=L_{\infty}
of Eq. (
6

L_{g}[g](x)=g^{\prime\prime}(x)+2\tfrac{g^{\prime}(x)}{x}

II

g(x)=0

z(x)=1

With this at hand, the relevant Volterra equations can be written readily:

\displaystyle\hat{L}_{f}[\hat{f}](t)\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\![\hat{f}*\hat{f}*\hat{f}](t-2)-2[\hat{g}*\hat{f}](t)+[\hat{g}*\hat{g}*\hat{f}](t)\,,

\displaystyle\hat{L}_{g}[\hat{g}](t)\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!-2[\hat{f}*\hat{f}](t-2)+2[\hat{g}*\hat{f}*\hat{f}](t-2)

(11)

\displaystyle+\beta[2\hat{g}-3\hat{g}*\hat{g}+\hat{g}*\hat{g}*\hat{g}](t)\,,

with
\hat{L}_{f}[\hat{f}](t)
corresponding to the LHS of Eq (
7
) and
\hat{L}_{g}[\hat{g}](t)=t^{2}\hat{g}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\,\tilde{K}(t,s)\hat{g}(s)
, with
\tilde{K}(t,s)=-2s
.
The system (
III
) is very interesting for several reasons: i) Due to
g(x)|_{x\to\infty}\to 0
, cf. Eq.
10
), the Borel-plane asymptotic scalar profile is trivial
\hat{g}_{0}(t)=0
, and the asymptotic gauge profile
\hat{f}_{0}(t)
is still proportional to
{}_{2}F_{1}(\tfrac{1}{2}-\nu,\tfrac{1}{2}+\nu;1;-\tfrac{t}{2})
like in the MNBPS case of Sec.
II
(albeit with different normalization factors
A_{\beta}\neq A_{\infty}
, cf. Sec.
I
); ii) The proliferation of singularities due to (

\hat{f}_{0}(t)
at
t=-2
, the RHS convolutions spread these equidistantly along the real axis (to
t_{\rm sing.}=2k
;
k\in\mathbb{Z}
) and, in every step, some of them are shifted by 2 units to the right; iii) The scalar sector is completely “enslaved” by the gauge one – nonzero contributions to
\hat{g}_{n}

\hat{f}_{n}
’s through the first convolution on the RHS of Eq. (
11

Due to this, both
f(x)
and
g(x)
should have a convergent power-log expansions around
x=0
and power-exponential ones in
x\to\infty
, that can be determined by comparison of coefficients in Eqs. (
10
). Moreover, due to the “superiority” of the gauge over the scalar sector,
A_{\beta}
should be still
calculable analytically
by the same means as
A_{\infty}
of the MNBPS case. This, however, is beyond the scope of this letter and will be elaborated on elsewhere.

IV
The BPS recess

The BPS
\beta=0
case is the only situation in which the gauge-profile asymptotics does not conform Eq. (
3
), as it rather goes like

y(x)={x}/{\sinh x}\sim 2\,x\,e^{-x}
for
x\to\infty
.
To what extent does this change the universal Borel-plane structure that we have seen in the
\beta>0
settings above? In terms of
f(x)=x^{-1}\,e^{x}y(x)
as in Secs.
II
and
III
, the BPS solution has a trivial geometric-series-like expansion

f_{\rm BPS}(x)={2}(1-e^{-2x})^{-1}=2(1+e^{-2x}+e^{-4x}+\ldots)\,.

(12)

Interestingly, the same
f_{\rm BPS}(x)
(up to a pole term) has a locally convergent (radius
\pi
) Laurent expansion around
x=0
, namely

f_{\rm BPS}(x)=\tfrac{1}{x}+1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{B_{2n}}{(2n)!}(2x)^{2n-1}\,,

(13)

where
B_{2n}
are even Bernoulli numbers; the Borel transform of its regular part (i.e. all but the
1/x
pole) is

\hat{f}_{\rm BPS}^{\rm reg.}(t)=\tfrac{1}{2}\coth(\tfrac{t}{2})-\tfrac{1}{t}\,.

(14)

Hence, the BPS case not only exhibits a beautiful Borel-Laplace duality among functional forms of the gauge and scalar profiles
\hat{f}^{\rm reg.}_{\rm BPS}(t)=\tfrac{1}{2}z_{\rm BPS}(\tfrac{t}{2})
, but Eq. (
14
) also reveals a nice pattern of the corresponding Borel-plane singularities: i)
t=0
is a regular point; ii) All singularities of
\hat{f}^{\rm reg.}_{\rm BPS}
are simple poles distributed along the imaginary axis at
t=2\pi in
with
n=\pm 1,\pm 2
etc. Hence, up to a benign rotation of the integration contour, the Borel structure of the BPS solution is almost identical to that of the non-BPS settings (as, indeed, the tower (
12
) suggests). The
1/x
contribution singled out in Eq. (
13
) then just corresponds to the “missing” zero mode.

V
Few practical remarks

Besides the analytic account of
A_{\infty}
developed in Sec.
II.1
, that should in principle work also for finite
\beta
, the structural considerations in preceding parts do not, per se, provide a practicable algorithm for calculating
y(x)
and
z(x)
in full glory, not even for the MNBPS case of Sec.
II
. The main obstacle is the need to cary around complete data about every convolution at each step (not only the structure of their leading singularities like in Sec.
II.1
), and to fully solve the associated Volterra equations. Nevertheless, the rigid Borel-plane singularity landscape still supports a convergent expansion method based on the local power-series of
\hat{f}(t)
and
\hat{g}(t)
, whose convolutions (and Volterra-equation solutions) should be straightforward to work out term by term, with the eventual adoption of suitable Padé approximants fully exploiting the Borel-plane information discussed above. Again, this will be further elaborated on in a future study.

VI
Conclusions

In this work we have explored the remarkable Borel-plane structure of the solutions of the ’t Hooft-Polyakov monopole profile equations for all values of the
\beta
parameter therein. It was shown that for
\beta>0
the seed of the discrete singularity pattern emerging at the real axis is the
{}_{2}F_{1}(\tfrac{1}{2}-i\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}+i\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2};1;-\tfrac{t}{2})
hypergeometric function featuring a logarithmic branch point at
t=-2
. At the example of the maximally non-BPS setting (corresponding to
\beta\to\infty
) we have shown that the sequence of the leading singularities at
t=0
, when mapped onto the (known) leading-log terms of the corresponding low-
x
power-log trans-series, provides an analytic grip on the normalization constant governing the relevant gauge-profile asymptotics; the same is expected for finite
\beta>0
.

Appendix A
Universal
\beta>0
gauge asymptotics

Consider the asymptotic profile of the solutions of Eq. (
1
) corresponding to a mere replacement of
z
by its asymptotic limit
z(x)=1
which, for
\beta>0
, is saturated exponentially fast, i.e.
1-z(x)\sim e^{-2x}
for large
x
(unlike in the BPS case where the convergence is only hyperbolic,
1-z(x)\sim x^{-1}
). With the fast-decaying
y^{3}/x^{2}
piece neglected, the relevant ODE assumes a simple linear form

y^{\prime\prime}(x)=\left(1-\tfrac{1}{x^{2}}\right)y(x)\,.

(15)

For
y(x)\equiv\sqrt{x}\,u(x)
this structure can be readily mapped onto the defining equation of Bessel functions

x^{2}u^{\prime\prime}(x)+xu^{\prime}(x)-(x^{2}+\nu^{2})u(x)=0\,,

(16)

with
\nu^{2}=-3/4
and, as such, the physically interesting asymptotically decaying universal solution can be written as

y_{a}(x)=A\sqrt{x}K_{\nu}(x)
,
where
K_{\nu}
is the modified Bessel
K
-function of imaginary order
\nu=i\sqrt{3}/2
and
A
is a (
\beta
-dependent) positive real constant.

Appendix B
The Borel-plane structure of
y_{a}(x)

As stated in Sec.
I
, the key to the current analysis is the Borel-plane form (
4
) of the asymptotic solution (
3
) of the gauge-profile ODE (
1
) elaborated on in appendix
A
,

y_{a}(x)=A_{\beta}\sqrt{\tfrac{\pi}{2}}\,xe^{-x}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,e^{-xt}F(t)\,,

where
F(t)\equiv{}_{2}F_{1}(\tfrac{1}{2}-i\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}+i\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2};1;-\tfrac{t}{2})
is a Gaussian hypergeometric function. Hence,
A_{\beta}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}F(t)
can be interpretted as the Borel-plane picture of
f_{a}(x)\equiv y_{a}(x)e^{x}x^{-1}
where
y_{a}
is the solution of the asymptotic Eq. (
15
). As such, a detailed understanding of the analytic structure of
F(t)
is not only central to the
\beta\to\infty
problem of Sec.
II
but, as explained in Sec.
III
, also to the general
\beta>0
case.
Let us thus recall the salient features of
F(t)
here:
i)
F(t)
is real for
t>-2
, has a logarithmic branch point at
t=-2
.
ii) Its logarithmic singularity at
t=-2
is exposed by the limit (cf.
NIST:DLMF
, formula 15.4.21)

\lim_{t\to-2+}\frac{F(t)}{\log(1+\tfrac{t}{2})}=-\left[\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1}{2}-i\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)\right]^{-1}\,,

(17)

where one can explicitly reveal the
\log(t+2)
term by virtue of
\log(1+t/2)=\log(t+2)-\log 2
.

Appendix C
The MNBPS
y(x)
trans-series at
x\to\infty

The large-
x
trans-series for
y
obtained from the ansatz

y(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{m}a_{m,n}x^{-m}{e^{-(2n+1)x}}

As expected, these are (up to the standard Borel-transform factorials) exactly the coefficients of the power expansion of
{}_{2}F_{1}(\tfrac{1}{2}-i\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}+i\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2};1;-\tfrac{t}{2})

.

Appendix D
The MNBPS
f(x)
trans-series at
x=0

Interestingly, the behaviour of the gauge profile
y(x)=xe^{-x}f(x)
of the MNBPS monopole around

whose highest-log coefficients

b_{n,n}=\{1,\;\tfrac{1}{3},\;\tfrac{1}{30},\;\tfrac{1}{270},\;\tfrac{59}{145800},\;\tfrac{71}{1603800},\;\ldots\}\,.

(19)

It is worth noting that this progression is asymptotically purely geometrical:

with
q\approx~0.109374
.
As argued in Sec.
II.1
, the same numbers can be independently determined from the coefficients of the
n
-th level highest-log singularities at the origin of the Borel plane (cf. Eq. (
8
) and Appendix
E
), which depend on
A_{\infty}
. Thus, the value of this parameter can be, at least in principle, determined analytically.

Appendix E
Borel-plane power-log towers at
t=0

Let us illustrate the emergence of the expected Borel-plane power-log tower around 0 for the simplest case of the
n=1
level
[\hat{f}_{0}*\hat{f}_{0}*\hat{f}_{0}](t-2)
convolution, cf. Sec.
II.1
and comment in brief on how to proceed for higher

. Focusing on the

term in Eq. (

[\log(t+2)^{*m}*1^{*(3-m)}](t)

.
For

[\log(t+2)*1*1](t)=\tfrac{1}{2}(t+2)^{2}\log(t+2)+\ldots

[\log(t+2)*\log(t+2)*1](t)\ni\log 2\times(t+2)^{2}\log(t+2)\,,

so, again,

is produced only linearly. However, unlike in the previous case, here one also encounters

.

Finally, the convolution of three
\log(t+2)

\log^{3}(t+6)
,
\log^{2}(t+6)
and
\log(t+6)

(t+6)^{2}
-proportional volume factors) that, for the
t=0

Let us remark that the isolation of singularities from triple convolutions of powers of

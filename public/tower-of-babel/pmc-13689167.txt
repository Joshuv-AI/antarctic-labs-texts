Draft genome sequences of nine Bacillus spp. strains isolated from Lebanese soil
Authors: Marc Moussan, Mariam El Arab, Nancy Fayad, Mireille Kallassy Awad
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe Bacillus cereus group is ecologically diverse but genomically close and is known for its variety of capacities, including its antimicrobial potential. Within a project aiming to find new bioactivity, nine Bacillus strains were sequenced: eight Bacillus thuringiensis and one Bacillus mycoides. Here, we report their genome sequences and various genomic features.

ANNOUNCEMENT

Bacillus cereus sensu lato (B. cereus s.l.) is a group of gram-positive spore-forming phylogenetically close, but ecologically diverse bacterial species, with an underappreciated aspect: their antimicrobial potential (1–3). This includes, for example, the entomopathogenic B. thuringiensis (4, 5) and the less-studied psychrotrophic B. mycoides (6). Nine Bacillus strains were isolated from Lebanese soil per the methods described in El Khoury et al. (7). Briefly, soil samples were collected from various Lebanese regions (Fig. 1). These samples were heated in sterile water for 10 min at 80°C and diluted 10 times, after which 100 μL were plated on a sporulating medium: T3 (8). Plates were incubated at 30°C for 24 h. Matt colonies with undulated edges were replated and incubated for 48 h, then screened for the presence of parasporal crystals.

These strains were sequenced using the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform (Illumina, San Diego, USA) using the MicrobesNG (Birmingham, UK) standard pipeline. For DNA extraction, following overnight culture in LB medium, bacterial cell suspensions were lysed in Tris-EDTA containing 0.1 mg/mL lysozyme and RNaseA at 37°C for 25 min, then with proteinase K (0.1 mg/mL) and SDS (0.5%) at 65°C for 5 min. DNA was purified using SPRI beads, resuspended in EB buffer, quantified using the Quant-iT dsDNA HS assay (ThermoFisher Scientific), and diluted for library preparation. Libraries for sequencing were prepared using the NexteraXT Library Prep Kit (Illumina, San Diego, USA) following the manufacturer’s protocol with the following modifications: input DNA is increased twofold, and PCR elongation time is increased to 45 s. Libraries were sequenced using a 250 bp paired-end protocol. Resulting reads were trimmed using Trimmomatic v0.3 with a sliding window and quality cutoff of Q15 (9) and assembled using SPAdes v3.7 (10). Genomes were submitted to NCBI GenBank under BioProject PRJNA611745. BioSample and GenBank accessions are shown in Table 1. The number of contigs per strain ranged between 82 and 514 contigs, with an average total length of 6,321,933 bp and GC content of 34.77%. Plasmids were identified via a database similarity search using a nucleotide BLAST+ executable (v.2.16.0+ [11]) of the assembled contigs against the plasmid repertoire of the B. cereus s.l. group.

All nine strains produced a proteinaceous parasporal crystal, confirmed by observation under a light microscope, as well as the presence of 4–5 crystal toxin-coding genes. Species identification was performed at several levels: by comparing the genomes to complete genomes of the B. cereus s.l. group retrieved from NCBI data sets, and by the NCBI and NLM taxonomy group, which relies on the average nucleotide identity score and compares the genome sequence against type strains from the NCBI Taxonomy Database (12, 13). Based on the latter classification, eight out of nine strains were classified as B. thuringiensis, whereas strain Dot was most closely related to Bacillus mycoides (Table 1).

Genome completeness was assessed using BUSCO v6.0 (14) and ranged between 99.1% and 99.8%. Genome annotation was done with NCBI’s automated annotation pipeline, Prokaryotic Genome Annotation Pipeline (15). These genome sequences are a gateway to identifying all potential bioactive molecules and lifestyle-dictating factors within these strains, guiding the way for wet lab and in silico downstream analysis, with tools such as AntiSmash (16) or BactiBase (17).

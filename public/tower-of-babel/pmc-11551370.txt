Total Hip Arthroplasty Outcomes are Improved When Cement is Used for Femoral Fixation in Elderly Patients?
Authors: Hari P Bezwada, Nicholas A Bedard
Published: 2024

Did you know that uncemented femoral fixation is associated with a 14 times higher incidence of intraoperative fracture when compared with cemented femoral stems? [1] Furthermore, revision within 3 months of surgery is 9 times more common with cementless fixation in patients over the age of 75 years [2]. Cemented femoral fixation has been shown to reduce perioperative complications, improve functional recovery, and reduce pain in elderly patients who undergo primary hip arthroplasty [3]. Data from national registries consistently demonstrate decreased total hip arthroplasty revision rates when cemented femoral fixation is utilized for elderly patients; with most of the revision benefit attributed to decreased periprosthetic fracture and femoral component loosening with cemented fixation. [2,[4], [5], [6]]. However, the benefits of cemented femoral fixation do not only apply to primary total hip arthroplasty. Analyses of randomized controlled trials demonstrate improved health-related quality of life, reduced mortality at 12 months and less periprosthetic fractures when cement is utilized for femoral fixation of hemiarthroplasty in the treatment of intracapsular hip fractures [7]. Similarly, improvements in mortality and periprosthetic fracture risk have also been documented when cemented femoral fixation is used for total hip arthroplasty in intracapsular hip fracture, especially for elderly female patients [8].

Despite the well-documented increased risk of periprosthetic fracture and revision surgery that is associated with cementless femoral fixation in the elderly, uncemented femoral components are still being utilized in elderly patients treated in the United States. The American Joint Replacement Registry reports cementless stems being utilized in 86% of patients aged 80-89 years and in 67% of patients age greater than 90 year [9]. Cementless hemiarthroplasty was also utilized in most femoral neck fractures over age 90 years [9]. It is essential for surgeons to understand the increased risks that are being placed upon elderly patients when cementless femoral fixation is utilized. It is also important to acknowledge that although a prophylactic cable with cementless fixation is thought to avoid femoral fracture in patients with poor bone quality, the best evidence supports the use of cemented stems to avoid this complication.

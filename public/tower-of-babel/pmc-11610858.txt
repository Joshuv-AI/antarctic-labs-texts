Assessment of COVID-19 patients’ outcome based on clinical profile, laboratory parameters, and clinical management: A retrospective observational study
Authors: Shivani Khullar, Varun Kothari, Ruchi Kothari, Manoj Lakhotia
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:The coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic has presented an unprecedented challenge to the global healthcare system, prompting an urgent need to understand the factors influencing patient outcomes. Critical to improving treatment protocols and reducing mortality rates is an in-depth assessment of the clinical profile, laboratory findings, and management strategies employed in treating COVID-19 patients. This research provides valuable insights that could influence future therapeutic approaches and public health strategies, ultimately aiming to reduce the morbidity and mortality associated with COVID-19. The study aimed to assess mortality predictors in patients admitted to the intensive care unit (ICU) due to COVID-19.Methods:This study employed a retrospective approach, utilizing patient data from medical records. The collected data encompassed demographic and clinical profiles and details regarding the duration of admission and treatment. The evaluation focused on patients admitted to the ICU for COVID-19 between March 2020 and July 2021, with confirmation through real-time reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR). Rigorous statistical analysis was conducted to compare outcomes between discharged and deceased patients.Results:The study included a total of 202 ICU patients admitted for COVID-19. Among the cases, 147 (72.8%) were males and 55 (27.2%) were females. The mean age was 58.42 years, with a standard deviation of 15.59 years. Fever (92%) emerged as the most frequently encountered symptom, followed by cough (48.5%) and dyspnea (35%). Patients with underlying comorbidities exhibited a higher susceptibility to developing a severe or critical disease. Hypertension (n = 38) was identified as the most prevalent comorbidity, followed by type 2 diabetes mellitus (n = 36). Hypertension has demonstrated a significant association with disease outcomes. Body temperature, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, and mechanical ventilation played substantial roles in patient outcomes.Conclusion:The study revealed that underlying comorbidities and complications, such as acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), were linked to significantly higher mortality rates among COVID-19 patients. Abnormal laboratory parameters also exhibited significant differences in the outcomes of ICU patients.

Introduction

The severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) virus, the causative agent of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19), initially surfaced in Wuhan, China, and has since proliferated globally, causing multiple waves of infection across all continents. Presently, there have been 2–3 waves of COVID-19, each impacting a growing number of individuals. Older adults and individuals with underlying comorbidities, regardless of age, have consistently exhibited a more unfavorable prognosis.[1]

Notably, intensive care unit (ICU) admissions have been predominantly occupied by patients of advanced age, along with those with conditions such as diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and pulmonary tuberculosis.[2] Several laboratory parameters, including thrombocyte count, low leukocyte and neutrophil counts, and elevated counts, have been associated with a poorer prognosis in COVID-19 cases.[3] Despite the absence of definitive treatments for COVID-19, vaccination has shown protective effects against severe disease; however, it has not proven as effective in preventing infection. Given these realities, early case identification remains crucial, serving not only to curtail transmission but also to manage disease prevention.[4] This study aims to elucidate the correlation between clinical manifestations, laboratory findings, and treatment modalities for patient outcomes.

Materials and Methods

Study setting and study design

This retrospective observational study was conducted at a tertiary care center in western Rajasthan, India. The investigation analyzed the case records of 202 COVID-19 ICU patients admitted from March 2020 to November 2021. All patient confirmations were conducted using real-time reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (rRT-PCR) for COVID-19. Demographic details, including age, gender, residence, and pre-existing comorbidities, were systematically collected.

The inclusion criteria encompassed COVID-19 patients either directly admitted to the ICU or transferred from wards or other medical facilities, with no specified exclusion criteria. Disease severity classification followed the guidelines established by the Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR) and the World Health Organization (WHO).[56]

Ethics consideration

Written informed consent was obtained by all participants in this study. The Institutional Ethics Committee of Dr. S.N. Medical College, Jodhpur, Rajasthan, India, had issued approval via Letter No. SNMC/ICE/IIP/2022/053 dated 21.06.2022 before the commencement of the study.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation for the statistical analysis, and categorical variables were expressed as numbers (percentage). Student t-tests and Chi-square tests assessed the association between patient characteristics and outcomes. The data underwent analysis using IBM Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) 26 Statistics, version 26 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

Two hundred and two patients admitted to the ICU due to COVID-19 were enrolled in this study. Of these cases, 147 (72.8%) were males and 55 (27.2%) were females. The mean age was 58.42 years, with a standard deviation of 15.59 years. No patient under 15 was admitted, and a maximum of 93 (46%) patients were above 60 years old. The majority of patients (84.7%) were from urban areas. Among the total patients, 26 (12.9%) had no underlying comorbidity, 91 (45%) had one comorbidity, and 85 (42.1%) had more than one comorbidity. A majority of ICU patients, 111 (55%), succumbed to COVID-19. This has been depicted in Table 1.

Out of 202 patients, 150 had hyperthermia above 38.5°C, and among them, 101 (67.3%) died. All patients with hypothermia (<36°C) at admission did not survive. Discussion

The primary objective of this paper was to evaluate the factors influencing the outcomes of COVID-19 patients admitted to the ICU. The initial case of COVID-19 in India was reported on January 30, 2020, leading to an upsurge in cases nationwide. A notable increase in ICU admissions and deaths occurred from April to November 2020 during the first wave and from March to May 2021 during the second wave.[78] This trend was also observed at our institution, a major tertiary care center serving the western Rajasthan population.

In our study, all patients were critically ill, presenting with symptoms such as fever (92%), cough (48.5%), dyspnea (35%), myalgia (32%), and less frequently, sore throat, and loss of smell and taste (below 10%). Our symptomatology data aligns with the national data registry maintained by the ICMR.[9]

Patients’ outcomes were significantly associated with underlying comorbidities, consistent with findings in other studies.[1101112] Among the comorbidities, hypertension exhibited a significant association with outcomes (P = 0.0006), a trend observed in other studies linking hypertension to increased mortality, along with cardiovascular disease and diabetes mellitus.[110] Complications developed during admission, specifically ARDS, were also significantly associated with outcomes (P = 0.042). ARDS has consistently been linked to a poor prognosis in COVID-19 patients across various studies.[1314]

In this study, body temperature above 38.5°C or below 36°C was significantly associated with higher mortality. The respiratory rate at admission, oxygen saturation (SpO2), and the need for mechanical ventilation also showed significant differences in outcomes for patients. The association of SpO2 at admission with higher mortality has shown variable results in different studies.[1516] Our study’s laboratory parameters indicated that abnormal values were associated with higher mortality rates, a consistent finding in several studies worldwide.[17181920]

This study, conducted at a single tertiary care center in western Rajasthan, India, provides valuable insights into the outcomes of COVID-19 patients in the ICU. However, its findings are subject to limitations. The retrospective design and reliance on medical records introduce potential biases, and the study’s applicability may be constrained by its specific geographic and temporal focus. The small sample size and incomplete data on certain variables further impact the study’s generalizability. Additionally, the evolving nature of the pandemic and changing treatment protocols during the study period may influence the interpretation of outcomes. While the results emphasize the association between comorbidities, complications, and laboratory parameters with mortality, caution is warranted in extending these findings to diverse populations and different phases of the pandemic.

The findings of this study highlight a clear connection between the existence of underlying comorbidities and complications, particularly ARDS, and a significantly heightened risk of mortality among individuals diagnosed with COVID-19. In other words, patients with pre-existing health conditions or those who developed complications like ARDS during the course of their illness were more likely to experience adverse outcomes, including a higher likelihood of death. Additionally, the study observed a meaningful impact of deranged laboratory parameters on the outcomes of patients in the ICU. Deranged laboratory parameters refer to abnormal or irregular results in various blood tests and other diagnostic measures. Such abnormalities in laboratory findings were associated with a significant difference in the overall outcomes of patients admitted to the ICU. This suggests that deviations from normal ranges in key laboratory indicators played a crucial role in influencing the severity and prognosis of COVID-19 cases within the ICU setting.

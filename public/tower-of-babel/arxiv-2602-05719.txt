Finite energy subspace for time-periodic Schrödinger operators
E. Skibsted

Abstract
. For N N -body Schrödinger operators with time-periodic short-range pair-potentials we show by a time-dependent commutator method that all channel wave operators exist. For N = 2 N=2 we prove asymptotic completeness by a simplified version of the method, recovering Yajima’s completeness result [ Yaj1 ] proven by a stationary method. We propose a definition of a finite energy subspace , intuitively consisting of states with ‘finite asymptotic energy’. This geometric notion is used to characterize the wave operator subspace given as the direct sum of the ranges of the channel wave operators. Thus our main result states that the two subspaces coincide. In turn they coincide for N = 2 N=2 with the orthogonal subspace of the pure point subspace of the monodromy operator (by asymptotic completeness). For N ≥ 3 N\geq 3 asymptotic completeness for time-periodic short-range potentials remains an open problem. The main result of the paper may in this case potentially serve as an intermediate step for proving (or possibly disproving) asymptotic completeness. Another potential ingredient could be a key intermediate result of the paper, asserting that the states orthogonal to the pure point subspace obey a (sharp) minimal velocity bound . Thus it remains an open problem possibly to derive a good maximal velocity bound for N ≥ 3 N\geq 3 . Our results apply to N N -body systems of particles in a time-periodic external electric field with time-mean equal to zero, for example the AC-Stark model.



Keywords:
N
-body time-periodic Schrödinger operators; short-range scattering
theory; wave operator and finite energy subspaces.

Mathematics Subject Classification 2010: 81Q10, 35P05.

1.
Introduction

The large time behaviour of a quantum-mechanical system of
N
particles interacting by time-independent short-range pair-potentials is well understood: In the center of mass framework the channel wave operators exist and they are complete. This asymptotic completeness result constitutes a satisfactory large time description of the evolution of the scattering states. In the present paper we will study scattering problems for time-dependent (more precisely) time-periodic short-range many-body Hamiltonians. Although asymptotic completeness is not shown/known, we do obtain various new related results. Our principle tool is commutator methods.

1.1.
The time-independent model

We briefly recall the
mentioned completeness result for the time-independent case, partially to motivate the scattering problems to be studied in our paper.
Thus let us consider
n
-dimensional particles
j=1,\dots,N
interacting by short-range
pair-potentials

V_{ij}(x_{i}-x_{j})={\mathcal{O}}(\lvert x_{i}-x_{j}\rvert^{-1-2\mu}),\quad\mu>0.

(1.1)

The total
Hamiltonian reads

H=H^{a_{\max}}=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2m_{j}}\Delta_{x_{j}}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}V_{ij}(x_{i}-x_{j}).

(1.2)

It acts
on the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}=L^{2}({\mathbf{X}})
, where the centre of mass space

{\mathbf{X}}={\mathbf{X}}^{a_{\max}}=\Big\{x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times\dots\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\ \mid\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}x_{j}=0\Big\}.

For the cluster decompositions
a\neq a_{\max}=\{1,\dots,N\}

there are sub-Hamiltonians
H^{a}
defined similarly
acting on
L^{2}({\mathbf{X}}^{a})
, where
{\mathbf{X}}^{a}\subsetneq{\mathbf{X}}
consists of the vectors for which the centre of mass of each of the clusters of
a
vanishes.

For any
channel

\alpha=(a,\lambda^{\alpha},u^{\alpha})
, i.e.
a
be a cluster
decomposition,
a\neq a_{\max}
,
\lambda^{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and

(H^{a}-\lambda^{\alpha})u^{\alpha}=0
for a normalized
u^{\alpha}\in L^{2}({\mathbf{X}}^{a})
, the corresponding forward and backward
channel wave operators

W_{\alpha}^{\pm}
are given by

W_{\alpha}^{\pm}\psi_{a}=\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}tH}\Big(\big({\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}t\lambda^{\alpha}}u^{\alpha}\big)\otimes\big({\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}t\Delta_{x_{a}}}\psi_{a}\big)\Big);\quad\psi_{a}\in L^{2}({\mathbf{X}}_{a}).

(1.3a)

Here we have decomposed orthogonally
{\mathbf{X}}={\mathbf{X}}^{a}\oplus{\mathbf{X}}_{a}
. The existence of the channel wave operators as well as
their
completeness
, i.e. the property that the
absolutely continuous subspace of
H
is spanned by their ranges

\sum_{\alpha}^{\oplus}\,\ran(W_{\alpha}^{\pm})={\mathcal{H}}_{\rm ac}(H)={\mathcal{H}}_{\rm{pp}}^{\perp}(H),

(1.3b)

is a well-studied problem in mathematical physics and since decades ago definitively solved, see
[
SS
,
Gr
,
De
,
Yaf
,
DG
,
HS
,
Is
]
. Some of the mentioned works (partly being reviews) include scattering theory for long-range potentials, however all treatments are based on commutator methods. The present paper relies on commutator methods as well, used somewhat in the same spirit as in the literature. More generally our paper depends on ideas from the mentioned literature as well as on ideas and results from
[
Yaj1
,
KY
,
MS
]
, specifically devoted to time-dependent problems.

1.2.
A physical time-dependent model

Now let us add
-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,q_{j}\ E(t)\cdot x_{j}
as a new term on the right-hand side of (
1.2
), where
E(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is any time-periodic vector with zero time-mean and
q_{j}
is the charge of the
j
th particle. This Hamiltonian models a quantum-mechanical system of charged particles in a constant time-periodic external electric field with time-mean equal to zero and interacting by short-range
pair-potentials. Again we can trivially factor out the center of mass motion, leading us to study the (reduced) evolution in
L^{2}({\mathbf{X}})
, which is now generated by a time-periodic Hamiltonian
H=H(t)
.
It is natural to ask the same question, how do the ‘scattering states’ in
L^{2}({\mathbf{X}})
evolve? This is the topic of the present paper.

Let us for simplicity assume that the period of
E(t)
is one. Hence we assume that

E(t+1)=E(t)\text{ \,and\, }\int_{0}^{1}\,E(t)\,{\mathrm{d}}t=0.

(1.4)

Let us write the generator
H(t)
in the following way. Using as before the notation
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{N}
and
y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{N})\in({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{N}
, the expression

x\cdot y=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,2m_{j}x_{j}\cdot y_{j}

defines an inner product for which the total Hamiltonian splits into a direct sum,

H(t)\otimes I+I\otimes H_{\rm CM}(t),

corresponding to the orthogonal splitting
({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{N}={\mathbf{X}}\oplus{\mathbf{X}}_{\rm CM}
. Let
Q=q_{1}+\cdots q_{N}
,
M=m_{1}+\cdots m_{N}
and

{\mathcal{E}}(t)=\tfrac{1}{2}\Big(\big(\tfrac{q_{j}}{m_{j}}-\tfrac{Q}{M}\big)E(t),\dots,\big(\tfrac{q_{N}}{m_{N}}-\tfrac{Q}{M}\big)E(t)\Big).

Then
{\mathcal{E}}(t)\in{\mathbf{X}}
, and we can express the centre of mass Hamiltonian
H(t)
in terms of the momentum operator
p=-\mathrm{i}\nabla_{x}
as

H=H(t)=p^{2}-{\mathcal{E}}(t)\cdot x+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}V_{ij}(x_{i}-x_{j}).

Clearly, unless the charge-mass ratio
\tfrac{q_{j}}{m_{j}}=\tfrac{Q}{M}
for all
j=1,\cdots,N
, this Hamiltonion has a non-trivial time-dependence, although of course being periodic with zero time-mean. It generates a dynamics,

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}t}{U}(t,s)\psi=H(t){U}(t,s)\psi\text{ \,and\, }{U}(s,s)=I,

and we are interesting in understanding the behaviour of the evolution
{U}(t,0)\psi
of states
\psi
being orthogonal to the pure point subspace of the
monodromy operator

U(1,0)
. (These states are the scattering states of our model.)

1.2.1.
A reduction of the physical model

Following
[
MS
]
it is convenient to apply a Galilean transformation to simplify the evolution. Hence let

\displaystyle b(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\,{\mathcal{E}}(s)\,{\mathrm{d}}s-b_{0},\quad b_{0}=\int_{0}^{1}\,\int_{0}^{t}\,{\mathcal{E}}(s)\,{\mathrm{d}}s\,{\mathrm{d}}t,

\displaystyle c(t)=2\int_{0}^{t}\,b(s)\,{\mathrm{d}}s-c_{0},\quad c_{0}=2\int_{0}^{1}\,\int_{0}^{t}\,b(s)\,{\mathrm{d}}s\,{\mathrm{d}}t,

\displaystyle\alpha(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\,|b(s)|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}s-t\alpha_{0},\quad\alpha_{0}=\int_{0}^{1}\,|b(s)|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}s.

Here
b(t),c(t)\in{\mathbf{X}}
and these vectors are periodic with period one and zero mean, and the scalar
\alpha(t)
is periodic as well. Defining then

S(t)={\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(t)}{\mathrm{exp}}(\mathrm{i}b(t)\cdot x){\mathrm{exp}}(-\mathrm{i}c(t)\cdot p),

(1.5a)

we can record that

\displaystyle S(t)^{*}H(t)S(t)

\displaystyle=H_{\rm G}(t)-\mathrm{i}\big(\tfrac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}t}S(t)^{*}\big)S(t)+\alpha_{0};

\displaystyle H_{\rm G}(t)

\displaystyle=p^{2}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}V_{ij}(x_{i}-x_{j}+c(t)_{i}-c(t)_{j}),

Therefore

S(t)^{*}U(t,s)S(s)={\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}(s-t)\alpha_{0}}U_{\rm G}(t,s),

(1.5b)

where
U_{\rm G}
denotes the dynamics

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}t}{U_{\rm G}}(t,s)\psi=H_{\rm G}(t){U_{\rm G}}(t,s)\psi\text{ \,and\, }{U_{\rm G}}(s,s)=I.

Clearly the generator
H_{\rm G}(t)
is a time-dependent Schrödinger operator with time-periodic short-range pair-potentials. The fundamental question is then: How does the evolution
{U_{\rm G}}(t,0)\psi
of states
\psi
being orthogonal to the pure point subspace of the monodromy operator
U_{\rm G}(1,0)
, say denoted by
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\rm G},\rm{pp}}
, behave?

Motivated by the formulation of completeness for the time-independent short-range case we are lead to consider the following conjecture on this problem.

For any
channel

\alpha=(a,\lambda^{\alpha},u^{\alpha})
, i.e.
a
be a cluster decomposition
a\neq a_{\max}
,
\lambda^{\alpha}\in[0,2\pi)
and

U^{a}_{\rm G}(1,0)u^{\alpha}={\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda^{\alpha}}u^{\alpha}
for a normalized
u^{\alpha}\in L^{2}({\mathbf{X}}^{a})
, we introduce the corresponding
channel wave
operators

W_{{\rm G},\alpha}^{\pm}
by

W_{{\rm G},\alpha}^{\pm}\psi_{a}=\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}U_{\rm G}(0,t)\Big(\big({\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda^{\alpha}[t]}U_{\rm G}^{a}(t-[t])u^{\alpha}\big)\otimes\big({\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}tp_{a}^{2}}\psi_{a}\big)\Big);\,\psi_{a}\in L^{2}({\mathbf{X}}_{a}).

(1.6a)

Here
[t]\in[0,1)
denotes the integer
part of
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
p_{a}=-\mathrm{i}\nabla_{x_{a}}
and as before we have decomposed orthogonally
{\mathbf{X}}={\mathbf{X}}^{a}\oplus{\mathbf{X}}_{a}
. Note also that
U_{\rm G}^{a}(t)=U_{\rm G}^{a}(t,0)

denotes the dynamics generated by sub-Hamiltonians
H_{G}^{a}(t)
defined similarly as
H_{G}(t)
(but acting on
L^{2}({\mathbf{X}}^{a})
rather than on
L^{2}({\mathbf{X}})
).
Since asymptotic completeness is not known in this setting let us state it as a
conjecture
:

{\mathcal{H}}_{{\rm G},\rm{wave}}^{\pm}:=\sum_{\alpha}^{\oplus}\,\ran(W_{{\rm G},\alpha}^{\pm})={\mathcal{H}}_{{\rm G},\rm{pp}}^{\perp}.

(1.6b)

If (
1.6b
) was known, we would then have a similar
completeness
result for the original time-dependent problem. This would be given be applying the Galilean transformation in the opposite direction. More precisely note for example the property
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm{pp}}^{\perp}=S(0){\mathcal{H}}_{{\rm G},\rm{pp}}^{\perp}
, where
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm{pp}}
is the pure point subspace of the monodromy operator
U(1,0)
. Similarly the operators
S(0)W_{{\rm G},\alpha}^{\pm}S(0)^{*}_{a}
are nothing but the physical channel wave
operators, see the computation (
1.10a
) and (
1.10b
) below. Hence a rather satisfactory large time description of the evolution of all scattering states of the physical model would follow.

Although (
1.6b
) remains unproven in general, let us mention that the result is known for
N=2
, as well as for
N=3
or bigger under strong additional conditions, see
[
Yaj1
,
Yaj2
,
Na
]
. The known conditions for
N\geq 3
appear very strong, and one could suspect that in general (
1.6b
) might not be valid (potentially rooted in examples from Classical Mechanics?). At the practical level there are two obstacles one (presumably) would need to overcome for possibly proving (
1.6b
):

1) Does the evolution
\psi_{G}(t)=U_{\rm G}(t,0)\psi
,
\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{{\rm G},\rm{pp}}^{\perp}
, concentrate in the strictly outgoing/incoming phase space regions for
t\to\pm\infty
, respectively?

2) Does the evolution
\psi_{G}(t)
concentrate in the finite kinetic energy region?

Anyway, if (
1.6b
) was known, then these problems would have confirmative answers.
They are non-trivial since the Hamiltonian is time-dependent. Quite opposite there are short direct confirmative answers in the
time-independent case.

Let us elaborate on 2) by defining the
finite energy subspaces

{\mathcal{H}}^{\pm}_{{\rm G},{\mathrm{ener}}}
as

{\mathcal{H}}^{\pm}_{{\rm G},{\mathrm{ener}}}=\big\{\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{{\rm G},\rm{pp}}^{\perp}\mid\lim_{E\to\infty}\limsup_{t\to\pm\infty}\,\lVert F(p^{2}>E)\psi_{G}(t)\rVert=0\big\}.

(Here and below
F(\cdot>E)=1_{(E,\infty)}
and
F(\cdot<E)=1_{(-\infty,E)}
.) Then the precise confirmative answer to 2) would be the assertion
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\rm G},\rm{pp}}^{\perp}={\mathcal{H}}^{\pm}_{{\rm G},{\mathrm{ener}}}
. This property is not known.

1.3.
Results for the physical and the reduced physical models

The main result of this paper stated for the reduced physical model is (up to milder conditions supplementing (
1.1
) and proven in a generalized setting) the assertion

{\mathcal{H}}_{{\rm G},\rm{wave}}^{\pm}=\sum_{\alpha}^{\oplus}\,\ran(W_{{\rm G},\alpha}^{\pm})={\mathcal{H}}^{\pm}_{{\rm G},{\mathrm{ener}}}.

(1.7)

In addition we show that for
N=3

{\mathcal{H}}^{\pm}_{{\rm G},{\mathrm{ener}}}={\mathcal{H}}_{{\rm G},\rm{pp}}^{\perp}\ominus{\mathcal{H}}_{{\rm G},{\rm ie}}^{\pm},

(1.8)

where the
increasing energy subspaces
are given as

{\mathcal{H}}_{{\rm G},{\rm ie}}^{\pm}=\big\{\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{{\rm G},\rm{pp}}^{\perp}\mid\forall E>0:\quad\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\,\lVert F(p^{2}<E)\psi_{G}(t)\rVert=0\big\}.

While (
1.8
) is not known for
N\geq 4
, we do show that if
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\rm G},{\rm ie}}^{\pm}=\{0\}
for
H_{\rm G}
as well as for all sub-Hamiltonians then indeed asymptotic completeness
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\rm G},\rm{wave}}^{\pm}={\mathcal{H}}_{{\rm G},\rm{pp}}^{\perp}
holds (which for
N=3
is a consequence of (
1.7
) and (
1.8
)).

In the process of proving (
1.7
) we prove the localization assertion in 1) for all states
\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{{\rm G},\rm{pp}}^{\perp}
. Hence for such states the evolution
\psi_{G}(t)
indeed concentrates in the strictly outgoing/incoming phase space regions for
t\to\pm\infty
. In particular
\psi_{G}(t)
concentrates in the strictly positive kinetic energy region (which already is a non-trivial property due to the time-dependence of the Hamiltonian).

The analogue of (
1.7
) for the physical model is

\sum_{\alpha}^{\oplus}\,\ran(W_{\alpha}^{\pm})={\mathcal{H}}^{\pm}_{{\mathrm{ener}}},

(1.9)

where (given in terms of the canonical splitting
S(t)=S(t)^{a}\otimes S(t)_{a}
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}W_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\pm}&=S(0)W_{{\rm G},\alpha}^{\pm}S(0)^{*}_{a},\\
\alpha=(a,\lambda^{\alpha},u^{\alpha}),\quad\alpha^{\prime}&=(a,\lambda^{\alpha}+\alpha_{0}^{a},S(0)^{a}u^{\alpha}),\quad\alpha_{0}^{a}=\int_{0}^{1}\,|b(s)^{a}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}s,\end{split}

(1.10a)

are the physical channel wave
operators (appearing here reparametrized) and

{\mathcal{H}}^{\pm}_{{\mathrm{ener}}}=\big\{\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm{pp}}^{\perp}\mid\lim_{E\to\infty}\limsup_{t\to\pm\infty}\,\lVert F(p^{2}>E)\psi(t)\rVert=0\big\};\quad\psi(t)=U(t,0)\psi.

As for (
1.10a
) we note that the expression

p_{a}^{2}-{\mathcal{E}}(t)_{a}\cdot x_{a}
defines a Hamiltonian on
L^{2}({\mathbf{X}}_{a})
generating a dynamics
U_{a}
for which there exist the limits

W_{\alpha}^{\pm}\psi_{a}=\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}U(0,t)\Big(\big({\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda^{\alpha}[t]}U^{a}(t-[t])u^{\alpha}\big)\otimes\big(U_{a}(t)\psi_{a}\big)\Big),\,\psi_{a}\in L^{2}({\mathbf{X}}_{a}).

(1.10b)

Here
U^{a}(t)=U^{a}(t,0)

denotes the dynamics generated by sub-Hamiltonians
H^{a}(t)
defined similarly as
H(t)
.
In fact using (
1.5a
) and (
1.5b
) we deduce that the limits exist by applying
S(0)
to both sides of (
1.6a
). The right-hand side simplifies as
W_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\pm}S(0)_{a}\psi_{a}
. In particular
W_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\pm}
exist and therefore in turn also
W_{\alpha}^{\pm}
. Obviously we obtained the formula (
1.10a
) as well.

As for (
1.9
) we obtain the formula by applying
S(0)
to both sides of (
1.7
) and then use (
1.10a
), (
1.10b
) and the fact that
{\mathcal{H}}^{\pm}_{{\mathrm{ener}}}=S(0){\mathcal{H}}^{\pm}_{{\rm G},{\mathrm{ener}}}
. Of course completeness would amount to (
1.9
) with the right-hand side being replaced by
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm{pp}}^{\perp}
.

For
N=3
completeness is equivalent to
the property
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\rm ie}}^{\pm}=\{0\}
, where

{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm ie}^{\pm}=\big\{\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm{pp}}^{\perp}\mid\forall E>0:\,\,\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\,\lVert F(p^{2}<E)\psi(t)\rVert=0\big\},

since in this case

{\mathcal{H}}^{\pm}_{{\mathrm{ener}}}={\mathcal{H}}_{\rm{pp}}^{\perp}\ominus{\mathcal{H}}_{{\rm ie}}^{\pm}.

(1.11)

Note that (
1.11
) for
N=3
follows similarly from the analogous assertion (
1.8
). For
N\geq 4

the criterion for completeness (similarly derived) reads that
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\rm ie}}^{\pm}=\{0\}
for
H
as well as for all sub-Hamiltonians.

The states in
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm ie}^{\pm}
correspond in Classical Mechanics to orbits with increasing kinetic energy

. It is not known if such orbits exist (ideally they are absent).

Although our analysis is mainly concentrated on the study of
{\mathcal{H}}^{\pm}_{{\rm G},{\mathrm{ener}}}
(and hence including
{\mathcal{H}}^{\pm}_{{\mathrm{ener}}}
) we obtain a new propagation estimate for states in

{\mathcal{H}}_{{\rm G},\rm{pp}}^{\perp}
. This is the following pointwise

.

\forall\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{{\rm G},\rm{pp}}^{\perp}\,\forall\epsilon>0:\,\,\lim_{|t|\to\infty}\,\lVert F(|x|<|t|^{1-\epsilon})\psi_{G}(t)\rVert=0.

(1.12)

Of course a similar assertion is valid for states in
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm{pp}}^{\perp}
. This minimal velocity bound is important for our proof of (
1.7
), however we believe it has independent interest. Note in particular that the lower velocity bound is valid for states where we do not know (and possibly maybe not even expect to have) a finite upper velocity bound. For the time-independent model energy conservation yields lower as well as upper velocity bounds, see for example
[
DG
, Proposition 4.11.1, Theorem 4.13.1]
and
[
Is
, Theorems 3.8, 3.11, 3.12]
(including integral versions of the
bounds).

1.3.1.
Concluding remarks

Clearly (
1.12
) is a characterizing geometric property of the space
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\rm G},\rm{pp}}^{\perp}
, and we also remark that the integral local decay estimates from
[
MS
]
(see (
2.3
)) are related to (
1.12
), however still rather different. These integral estimates are important for us throughout the paper, and in particular they are used to derive (
1.12
) (explicitly we exploit in this case (
2.3
) with
r=0
and
s>\tfrac{1}{2}
arbitrary). The proof is somewhat complicated. Besides the mentioned estimates from
[
MS
]
it is based on commutator methods (as for all of the paper) and in particular on unbounded time-dependent
propagation observables
. For other purposes we shall frequently
use the concept of bounded propagation observables.

A main goal of our work is to see how far the techniques of positive commutators can be pushed in the direction of proving (or possibly disproving) the completeness conjecture. These tools are rather different from Kato’s perturbative smoothness technique (originating in
[
Ka1
]
) on which the mentioned works
[
Yaj1
,
Yaj2
,
Na
]
rely. Although we do not achieve the ultimate completeness result, our efforts yield new related results of geometric nature (as outlined above). Apart from a (quantum) smoothing effect, the positive commutator techniques are strongly related to (and in fact possibly limited of) Classical Mechanics. This is further emphasized in this paper in that we only consider sufficiently smooth potentials. However even with this assumption it is a major challenge to see that various ‘quantum errors’ of the
techniques are ‘negligible’, for example and typically implemented by the integral local decay estimates from
[
MS
]
. As a general feature it follows that the classical Poisson bracket determines the desired content of commutator positivity, as expected.

2.

and results

2.1.
Generalized
N
-body short-range Hamiltonians

Let
{\mathbf{X}}
be
a (nonzero) finite dimensional real inner product space,
equipped with a

\{{\mathbf{X}}_{a}\}_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}

For any

, denoted

, such that
{\mathbf{X}}_{a}\cap{\mathbf{X}}_{b}={\mathbf{X}}_{c}
.
We

) if

{\mathbf{X}}_{a}\supseteq{\mathbf{X}}_{b}
.
It is assumed that there exist

. We will use the abbreviated notation

. Let

{\mathcal{A}}_{3}={\mathcal{A}}_{1}\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
. For

the subspaces

are called

planes
. For
a\in{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
we will use the notation

and in particular the abbreviated notation

d=d_{a_{\min}}=\dim\mathbf{X}
.

For any
chain

a_{\min}=a_{1}\lneq\cdots\lneq a_{n}=a_{0}
in
{\mathcal{A}}
, the integer
n\geq 2

Yafaev’s constructions

In

]

,

a\in{\mathcal{A}}_{1}

The operators

localization operators’, while operators of the form

partially enter as auxiliary quantities controlling commutators of

[
Yaf
]

We consider various conical
subsets of
\mathbf{X}\setminus\{0\}
, defined for

and
\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{X}^{\prime}_{a}&={\mathbf{X}_{a}}\setminus\cup_{{b\not\leq a,\,b\in{\mathcal{A}}}\,}\mathbf{X}_{b},\\
\mathbf{X}_{a}(\varepsilon)&=\{x\in{\mathbf{X}}\mid\lvert x_{a}\rvert>(1-\varepsilon)\lvert x\rvert\},\\
\quad&\quad\quad\overline{\mathbf{X}_{a}(\varepsilon)}=\{x\in{\mathbf{X}}\setminus\{0\}\mid\lvert x_{a}\rvert\geq(1-\varepsilon)\lvert x\rvert\},\\
\mathbf{Y}_{a}(\varepsilon)&=\big(\mathbf{X}\setminus\{0\}\big)\setminus\cup_{{b\not\leq a,\,b\in{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}\,\mathbf{X}_{b}(\varepsilon),\\

The structure of the sets

\mathbf{Y}_{a}(\varepsilon)
and
\mathbf{Z}_{a}(\delta)
is

{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}V}

is a

(3.3)

The Yafaev constructions involve families of cones

roughly with ‘width’

\varepsilon\approx\epsilon^{d_{a}}
for a
parameter
\epsilon>0

Fixing an arbitrary

(4)

6.2.
A square root problem, an alternative proof of
(

)

The above proof of (
6.6c
) does not generalize to the
extent that we will need later. In this subsection we give a
different proof. Although it is considerably more complicated, its
scheme
will be useful for generalizations. In general it is difficult to
commute operators through the square root of a positive
operator, this may be called the ’square root singularity
problem’. In our case it may appear useful to commute a concrete
operator (presently of the form

for some

(or possibly alternatively, through

the commutator, however this
does not seem doable. Rather we shall regularize this square root
before computing and estimating the commutator. Our regularization
takes advantage of the full strength
of the integral local decay estimate (
2.3
), viz
(
2.3
) with the explicitly included smoothing factor. The same method
will be used in Subsection
6.3
for other examples.

We introduce the auxiliary operator

\displaystyle\begin{split}P=P_{\rho,W}(t)=\tilde{f}\big(\widecheck{H}\big)W^{-1}&\big(p\cdot\nabla^{2}m(x)p\big)W^{-1}\tilde{f}\big(\widecheck{H}\big)+r^{-2\rho};\\

By interpolating the analogue of (
4.8

\widecheck{H}

is a positive bounded
operator. See Lemma
6.8
for more refined properties. Like
for

note that

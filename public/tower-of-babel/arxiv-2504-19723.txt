Sum-Rate Optimisation of a Multi-User STAR-RIS-Aided System with Low Complexity
Sadaf Syed, Wolfgang Utschick, Michael Joham

Abstract
Reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS) is a promising technology for future wireless communication systems. However, the conventional RIS can only reflect the incident signal. Hence, it provides a limited coverage, as compared to a simultaneously transmitting and reflecting RIS (STAR-RIS). Prior works on the STAR-RIS address the power minimisation or the sum-rate maximisation problem by reformulating the objective problem as a convex optimisation problem and then employing numerical tools like CVX to obtain the solution, which introduces significant computational complexity leading to a huge runtime, making the algorithms impractical for real-world implementation. In this paper, we propose a low complexity solution for the optimisation of a multi-user STAR-RIS system, where the non-convex optimisation problem is decomposed into multiple convex sub-problems with closed-form optimal solutions. The simulation results illustrate that our proposed algorithm achieves similar performance to CVX-based solutions in the literature while being computationally efficient.

RIS consists of a large number of lossless reconfigurable reflecting elements that can reflect the incident signal in a controlled manner to achieve the desired performance objectives. Additionally, the energy and hardware costs of the lossless elements of the RIS are significantly lower compared to the traditional active antennas at the base station (BS). II
System Model

We consider the downlink (DL) of a multi-user STAR-RIS-aided system where the BS is equipped with
M
antennas and the STAR-RIS consists of
N
lossless elements. The wireless signal from the BS that is incident on a single element of the STAR-RIS is divided into a transmitting and a reflecting signal. We consider
K
single-antenna users where
K_{\text{r}}
users lie on the reflecting side and
K_{\text{t}}
users lie on the transmitting side of the STAR-RIS, such that
K_{\text{r}}+K_{\text{t}}=K
. For each element of the STAR-RIS, the phase shifts for reflection and transmission can be designed independently
[
9
]
. However, the amplitude coefficients of the reflection and transmission parts are coupled by the law of energy conservation since the elements of the STAR-RIS are lossless. The reflection and transmission coefficient matrices of the STAR-RIS are given by

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\it{\Phi}}}_{\text{r}}

\displaystyle=\text{diag}\left(\sqrt{\rho_{\text{r},1}}\theta_{\text{r},1},\cdots,\sqrt{\rho_{\text{r},N}}\theta_{\text{r},N}\right)

(1)

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\it{\Phi}}}_{\text{t}}

\displaystyle=\text{diag}\left(\sqrt{\rho_{\text{t},1}}\theta_{\text{t},1},\cdots,\sqrt{\rho_{\text{t},N}}\theta_{\text{t},N}\right)

(2)

where the phase shifts for the reflection and transmission for the
n
-th element are denoted by
\theta_{\text{r},n}
and
\theta_{\text{t},n}
respectively, with
\sqrt{\rho_{\text{r},n}}
and
\sqrt{\rho_{\text{t},n}}
being the corresponding amplitude coefficients with
\rho_{\text{r},n},\>\>\rho_{\text{t},n}\in[0,1]
. Additionally, we have
|\theta_{\text{r}/\text{t},n}|=1\>\forall\>n
, and
\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\text{r}/\text{t}}=\mathrm{diag}({\boldsymbol{\it{\Phi}}}_{\text{r}/\text{t}})
denotes the corresponding vector. Moreover, we have the constraint that the energy of the incident signal must be equal to the sum of the energies of the reflecting and transmitting signals, i.e.,

\displaystyle\rho_{\text{r},n}+\rho_{\text{t},n}=1\>\forall\>n.

(3)

As it can be seen from (
3
), one can control the amount of signal reflected and transmitted by each element of the STAR-RIS by adjusting the amplitude coefficients
\rho_{\text{r},n}\>\>\text{and}\>\>\rho_{\text{t},n}
. Three operating protocols for STAR-RIS have been proposed in the literature
[
8
,
10
]
.

1.

Energy Splitting (ES): In this protocol, all elements of the STAR-RIS simultaneously operate in reflecting and transmitting modes with
\rho_{\text{r},n},\>\>\rho_{\text{t},n}\in[0,1]
and
\rho_{\text{r},n}+\rho_{\text{t},n}=1\>\forall\>n
.

2.

Mode Splitting (MS): In this configuration, some elements of the STAR-RIS fully operate in the reflecting mode and the others fully operate in the transmitting mode, i.e.,
\rho_{\text{r},n},\>\>\rho_{\text{t},n}\in\{0,1\}
. If
N_{\text{r}}
denotes the number of elements operating in the reflecting mode with
\rho_{\text{r}}=1
,
\rho_{\text{t}}=0
, and
N_{\text{t}}
denotes the number of elements operating in the transmitting mode with
\rho_{\text{r}}=0
,
\rho_{\text{t}}=1
, we have
N_{\text{r}}+N_{\text{t}}=N
.

3.

Time Splitting (TS): In this protocol, all elements of the STAR-RIS alternatingly operate in full transmitting or reflecting modes in different time slots. If
\delta\in[0,1]
denotes the percentage of time allocated for the reflecting mode and
1-\delta
is the fraction of time for the transmitted mode, the algorithm needs to optimise
\delta
to achieve the optimal performance.

The coupling of the reflecting and transmitting parts due to the amplitude coefficients makes the optimisation of ES and MS modes of STAR-RIS complicated. The TS mode is easier to optimise because of the decoupled reflecting and transmitting modes in each time slot. However, it requires strict time synchronisation, which makes its hardware implementation more complicated. Additionally, since the TS mode does not serve all the users in the same time slot, its gain is much lower compared to the ES and MS modes where all the users are simultaneously served. It is to be noted here that the MS protocol is a subset of the ES protocol containing only the binary values of the amplitude coefficients. Hence, the MS protocol cannot achieve the similar gain as the ES protocol, and so, we restrict our discussion to ES and TS modes in this paper.
The direct channel from the BS to the
k
-th user is denoted by
{\boldsymbol{h}}_{\mathrm{d},k}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times 1}
, that from the RIS to the
k
-th user is denoted by
{\boldsymbol{r}}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times 1}
and the channel from the BS to the RIS is denoted by
{\boldsymbol{T}}
. The effective channel for the
k
-th user is given by

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{h}}_{k}^{{\mathrm{H}}}

\displaystyle={\boldsymbol{h}}_{\mathrm{d},k}^{{\mathrm{H}}}+{\boldsymbol{r}}_{k}^{{\mathrm{H}}}{\boldsymbol{\it{\Phi}}}_{k}^{{\mathrm{H}}}{\boldsymbol{T}}={\boldsymbol{h}}_{\mathrm{d},k}^{{\mathrm{H}}}+\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}^{{\mathrm{H}}}{\boldsymbol{G}}_{k}

(4)

where
{\boldsymbol{G}}_{k}=\mathrm{diag}({\boldsymbol{r}}_{k}^{{\mathrm{H}}}){\boldsymbol{T}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times M}
,
\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}=\mathrm{diag}({\boldsymbol{\it{\Phi}}}_{k})\in\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\text{r}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\text{t}}\}
depending on the location of the
k
-th user if it is on the reflecting or on the transmitting side of the STAR-RIS.
The total received signal at the
k
-th user is given by

\displaystyle y_{k}={\boldsymbol{h}}_{k}^{{\mathrm{H}}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{K}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{i}\>s_{i}+z_{k}

(5)

where
z_{k}\sim\mathcal{N_{\mathbb{C}}}(0,\sigma_{k}^{2})
denotes the additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) at the
k
-th user’s side and
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{M}
is the corresponding beamforming vector.
The rate of the
k
-th user is given by
\mathrm{log}_{2}(1+\gamma_{k})
, where
\gamma_{k}
denotes the SINR of the
k
-th user, which is given by

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}=\frac{\big|{{\boldsymbol{h}}_{k}^{{\mathrm{H}}}}{{\boldsymbol{w}}_{k}}\big|^{2}}{\sum\nolimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{K}\big|{{\boldsymbol{h}}_{k}^{{\mathrm{H}}}}{{\boldsymbol{w}}_{i}}\big|^{2}+\sigma_{k}^{2}}.

(6)

The objective is to maximise the sum-rate of the users with respect to the active beamforming at the BS and the passive beamforming by the STAR-RIS.

III
Optimisation Problem and Solution Approach

In this section, we develop the optimisation problems for both ES and TS modes, and derive their low-complexity solutions.

III-A

Energy Splitting (ES) Mode

The sum-rate maximisation problem for the ES mode is

\displaystyle\!\max\limits_{{\boldsymbol{W}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\text{r}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\text{t}},\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\text{r}},\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\text{t}}}

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{K}\log_{2}(1+\gamma_{k})

(P1)

subject to

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{K}\norm{\bfw_k}^{2}\leq P_{\text{t}},

(7a)

\displaystyle|\theta_{\text{r},n}|=1,\quad|\theta_{\text{t},n}|=1\>\forall\>n

(7b)

\displaystyle\rho_{\text{r},n}+\rho_{\text{t},n}=1,\quad 0\leq\rho_{\text{r},n},\rho_{\text{t},n}\leq 1\>\forall\>n

(7c)

where
{\boldsymbol{W}}=[{\boldsymbol{w}}_{1},\cdots,{\boldsymbol{w}}_{K}]^{\mathrm{T}}
,
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\text{r}}=[\rho_{\text{r},1},\cdots,\rho_{\text{r},N}]^{\mathrm{T}}
,
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\text{t}}=[\rho_{\text{t},1},\cdots,\rho_{\text{t},N}]^{\mathrm{T}}
,
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\text{r}}=[\theta_{\text{r},1},\cdots,\theta_{\text{r},N}]^{\mathrm{T}}
,
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\text{t}}=[\theta_{\text{t},1},\cdots,\theta_{\text{t},N}]^{\mathrm{T}}
.
The objective function is firstly reformulated into a tractable form using the FP approach in 2 steps with the help of auxiliary variables, as done in
[
12
,
13
]
.

1)
Lagrangian Dual Transform
: By introducing auxiliary variables
{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=[\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{K}]^{\mathrm{T}}
, the sum of logarithm function can be taken care of as follows:

\displaystyle\log(1 + \gamma_k)=\>\!\max\limits_{\lambda_{k}\geq 0}\>\log(1 + \lambda_k)-\lambda_{k}+\dfrac{(1+\lambda_{k})\gamma_{k}}{1+\gamma_{k}}

where the expression in the LHS is equivalent to that in the RHS for the optimal solution
\lambda_{k}=\gamma_{k}
.

2)
Quadratic Transform
: Given
\lambda_{k}
, in the second step, the fractional term is simplified using the auxiliary variables
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}=[\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{K}]^{\mathrm{T}}
.

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\dfrac{(1+\lambda_{k})\gamma_{k}({\boldsymbol{w}}_{k})}{1+\gamma_{k}({\boldsymbol{w}}_{k})}

\displaystyle=\!\max\limits_{{{\beta_{k}}}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}2\sqrt{1+\lambda_{k}}\text{Re}\big\{\beta_{k}^{*}{\boldsymbol{h}}_{k}^{{\mathrm{H}}}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{k}\big\}

\displaystyle-\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{K}|\beta_{k}|^{2}\left({\sum\limits_{{i=1}}^{K}\big|{{\boldsymbol{h}}_{k}^{{\mathrm{H}}}}{{\boldsymbol{w}}_{i}}\big|^{2}+\sigma_{k}^{2}}\right).

With these simplification steps of the FP approach, one can adopt the iterative non-convex block coordinate descent (BCD) method
[
14
,
15
]
to alternatingly optimise the auxiliary variables, active beamforming vectors at the BS, and the passive beamforming vectors at the STAR-RIS. The closed-form solution of the auxiliary variables alternatingly maximising the objective function in (
P1
) reads as
[
7
, Sec. IV]

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}={\gamma}_{k},\quad\beta_{k}=\dfrac{\left(\sqrt{1+\lambda_{k}}\right){\boldsymbol{h}}_{k}^{{\mathrm{H}}}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{k}}{{\sum\limits_{{i=1}}^{K}\big|{{\boldsymbol{h}}_{k}^{{\mathrm{H}}}}{{\boldsymbol{w}}_{i}}\big|^{2}+\sigma_{k}^{2}}}.

(8)

Optimisation of
{\boldsymbol{W}}
also requires satisfying the DL power constraint at the BS, and it can be efficiently handled by applying the rescaling trick mentioned in
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
. The idea is to introduce the constraint into the objective function itself by exploiting the fact that the inequality constraint in (
7a
) will be satisfied with equality for the optimal precoding filters (see
[
7
, Sec. IV]
). With
\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{K}\norm{\bfw_k}^{2}=P_{t}
, thus,
\sigma_{k}^{2}=\sigma_{k}^{2}\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{K}\norm{\bfw_i}^{2}/P_{t}
, the optimisation problem for the update of the precoders is given by

\displaystyle\!\max\limits_{{\boldsymbol{W}}}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}2\sqrt{1+\lambda_{k}}\text{Re}\big\{\beta_{k}^{*}{\boldsymbol{h}}_{k}^{{\mathrm{H}}}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{k}\big\}

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}|\beta_{k}|^{2}\left({\sum\limits_{{i=1}}^{K}\big|{{\boldsymbol{h}}_{k}^{{\mathrm{H}}}}{{\boldsymbol{w}}_{i}}\big|^{2}+\sum\limits_{{i=1}}^{K}\dfrac{\sigma_{k}^{2}}{P_{t}}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{i}^{{\mathrm{H}}}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{i}}\right)

(9)

and its optimal solution can be expressed as

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{w}}_{k}={\beta}_{k}\sqrt{1+{\lambda}_{k}}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{K}\dfrac{\big|{\beta}_{i}\big|^{2}}{P_{t}}\sigma_{i}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{M}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{K}\big|{\beta}_{i}\big|^{2}{{\boldsymbol{h}}}_{i}{{\boldsymbol{h}}}_{i}^{{\mathrm{H}}}\right)^{-1}{{\boldsymbol{h}}}_{k}.

(10)

Even though the DL power constraint has been incorporated into the objective function of (
9
), the optimal solution in (
10
) must be rescaled in the end to ensure that the constraint is satisfied with equality (cf.
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
).

,
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\text{t}}
,

(P2)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\quad\eqref{eqn4.6},\eqref{eqn4.7}

where

(12)

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{v}}_{\text{r}/\text{t}}

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{K_{\text{r}}/K_{\text{t}}}{(\beta_{k}^{{\text{r}/\text{t}}})^{*}}\sqrt{1+{\lambda_{k}^{\text{r}/\text{t}}}}{\boldsymbol{G}}^{\text{r}/\text{t}}_{k}{{\boldsymbol{w}}}^{\text{r}/\text{t}}_{k}

\displaystyle-\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{K_{\text{r}}/K_{\text{t}}}\big|{\beta}^{\text{r}/\text{t}}_{k}\big|^{2}\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{K}{\boldsymbol{G}}_{k}^{\text{r}/\text{t}}{{\boldsymbol{w}}}_{i}{{\boldsymbol{w}}}_{i}^{{\mathrm{H}}}{\boldsymbol{h}}^{\text{r}/\text{t}}_{\mathrm{d},k}

respectively. The unit modulus constraints in (

\theta_{\text{r},n}
,
\theta_{\text{t},n}
,
\rho_{\text{r},n}
and
\rho_{\text{t},n}
keeping the other

\displaystyle\!\max\limits_{\phi_{\text{r},n},\phi_{\text{t},n}}\>2\sqrt{\rho_{\text{r},n}}\text{Re}\big\{\theta_{\text{r},n}^{*}a_{\text{r},n}\big\}+2\sqrt{\rho_{\text{t},n}}\text{Re}\big\{\theta_{\text{t},n}^{*}a_{\text{t},n}\big\}

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\rho_{\text{r},n}u_{\text{r},n,n}-\rho_{\text{t},n}u_{\text{t},n,n}

(P3)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\>|\theta_{\text{r},n}|=1,|\theta_{\text{t},n}|=1,\rho_{\text{r},n}+\rho_{\text{t},n}=1,0\leq\rho_{\text{r},n},\rho_{\text{t},n}\leq 1\>\forall\>n

\displaystyle\text{where}\quad a_{\text{r},n}=v_{\text{r},n}-\sum\nolimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,i\neq n\end{subarray}}^{N}\sqrt{\rho_{\text{r},i}}\theta_{\text{r},i}u_{\text{r},n,i}

(15)

\displaystyle a_{\text{t},n}=v_{\text{t},n}-\sum\nolimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,i\neq n\end{subarray}}^{N}\sqrt{\rho_{\text{t},i}}\theta_{\text{t},i}u_{\text{t},n,i}

with

-th element of the STAR-RIS

\displaystyle\theta_{\text{r},n}=e^{j\angle a_{\text{r},n}}\quad\theta_{\text{t},n}=e^{j\angle a_{\text{t},n}}.

After optimising

: For the optimal phase shifts of the

\displaystyle f(x)=\sqrt{x}b_{\text{r},n}+\sqrt{1-x}\>b_{\text{t},n}-x\>U_{n}

is concave in

, and hence, the global optimal solution of

)
can be computed for the given optimal
\theta_{\text{r},n}
and
\theta_{\text{t},n}

Proof.

With the expression of

is given by

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(x)=-\dfrac{1}{4}x^{-3/2}b_{\text{r},n}-\dfrac{1}{4}(1-x)^{-3/2}b_{\text{t},n}

For the optimal values of
\theta_{\text{r},n}
and

given by (

)

\displaystyle b_{\text{r},n}=2\>|\theta_{\text{r},n}||a_{\text{r},n}|=2\>|a_{\text{r},n}|\geq 0

\displaystyle b_{\text{t},n}=2\>|\theta_{\text{t},n}||a_{\text{t},n}|=2\>|a_{\text{t},n}|\geq 0.

Since

.

Hence, (

in each iteration by (

V
Conclusion

In this work, we have proposed a low complexity solution for the design of the STAR-RIS. We have analysed the ES and the TS protocols, and the simulation results illustrate that the ES protocol brings a significant performance gain compared to the conventional RIS. However, the TS protocol does not improve the achievable sum-rate of the users compared to the reflecting-only RIS at high tranmsit power levels. Nonetheless, it ensures that all users on both sides of the STAR-RIS are at least served for some time.

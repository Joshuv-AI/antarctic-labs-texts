Efficacy and Safety Ranking of HER2-Targeted TKIs in Advanced Breast Cancer: A Bayesian Network Meta-Analysis
Authors: Rongjie Li, Rui Huangfu, Hua Lan, Chenying Zhang, Kun Hou, Yaodong Ping
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo compare the efficacy and safety of HER2-targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) in patients with HER2-positive advanced or locally advanced breast cancer using a Bayesian network meta-analysis and to provide comparative evidence for individualized treatment decisions.MethodsPubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library were systematically searched for randomized controlled trials evaluating HER2-targeted TKIs in patients with HER2-positive advanced or locally advanced breast cancer. Progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), objective response rate (ORR), and disease control rate (DCR) were assessed as efficacy outcomes, while treatment-related adverse events were evaluated as safety outcomes. A Bayesian network meta-analysis was performed using R (version 4.2.2) and Stata (version 16.0). Treatments were ranked according to the surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA). The study protocol was registered with the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO: CRD420250650274).ResultsFourteen randomized controlled trials involving 5,289 patients were included. According to SUCRA rankings, pyrotinib plus capecitabine (Pyrotinib_C) showed the highest probability of favorable ranking PFS, ORR, and DCR. Tucatinib combined with trastuzumab and capecitabine (Tucatinib_T_C) demonstrated the highest probability of favorable OS ranking. Regarding safety, pyrotinib combined with trastuzumab and capecitabine (Pyrotinib_T_C) was associated with the highest incidence of serious adverse events, diarrhea, and hand-foot syndrome, whereas Pyrotinib_C showed the highest incidence of anemia and lapatinib plus capecitabine (Lapatinib_C) demonstrated the highest incidence of rash.ConclusionAmong currently available HER2-targeted TKIs, Pyrotinib_C showed the highest probability of favorable ranking for PFS and tumor response outcomes, whereas Tucatinib_T_C showed the highest probability of favorable OS ranking. However, pyrotinib-containing regimens were associated with a higher risk of treatment-related toxicities. These findings highlight the importance of balancing efficacy and safety when selecting HER2-targeted TKI therapies and support individualized treatment strategies for patients with HER2-positive advanced breast cancer.Keywords: breast cancer, small molecule TKIs, network meta-analysis, effectiveness, security

1. Introduction

Breast cancer remains the most frequently diagnosed malignancy among women worldwide and a leading cause of cancer-related mortality. According to the latest global cancer statistics, approximately 2.3 million new breast cancer cases and 670,000 deaths were reported in 2022, highlighting its substantial global health burden. 1 Although advances in screening and treatment have significantly improved survival outcomes, patients with advanced or metastatic disease continue to experience poor prognoses and limited long-term survival. 2

Approximately 20–30% of breast cancers are characterized by overexpression or amplification of human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), a molecular subtype associated with aggressive tumor behavior, increased metastatic potential, and unfavorable clinical outcomes.3,4 The development of HER2-targeted therapies has fundamentally transformed the treatment landscape of HER2-positive breast cancer. Since the introduction of trastuzumab in 1998, anti-HER2 therapies have significantly improved progression-free and overall survival among affected patients. 5 Subsequent advances, including dual HER2 blockade with pertuzumab and antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) such as trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1) and trastuzumab deruxtecan (DS-8201), have further expanded therapeutic options for patients with advanced disease.5,6 Nevertheless, primary and acquired resistance to HER2-targeted therapies remains a major clinical challenge, necessitating the development of additional treatment strategies. Small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) have emerged as an important component of HER2-targeted therapy. Unlike monoclonal antibodies that target extracellular receptor domains, TKIs inhibit intracellular tyrosine kinase activity and suppress downstream signaling pathways. Their oral administration, favorable tissue penetration, and ability to cross the blood–brain barrier make them particularly attractive for patients with brain metastases and those who develop resistance to antibody-based therapies. 7

Several HER2-targeted TKIs have demonstrated promising clinical efficacy in recent years. In the PHOEBE trial, pyrotinib plus capecitabine significantly prolonged progression-free survival compared with lapatinib plus capecitabine (HR=0.39, 95% CI: 0.27–0.56, P<0.0001), although treatment-related diarrhea was more common. 8 Neratinib also demonstrated clinical benefits in HER2-positive breast cancer, particularly among hormone receptor-positive patients, as reported in the ExteNET and NALA studies.9,10 More recently, the HER2CLIMB trial demonstrated that tucatinib-based therapy significantly improved survival outcomes, especially among patients with brain metastases, further highlighting the therapeutic potential of HER2-targeted TKIs. Despite these encouraging findings, substantial differences in efficacy and safety profiles remain among available TKIs because of variations in target selectivity, pharmacological properties, and toxicity profiles.Direct head-to-head comparisons among pyrotinib, tucatinib, neratinib, and lapatinib remain limited, making it difficult to identify the optimal TKI-based treatment strategy in clinical practice. Moreover, previous evidence syntheses were conducted before the publication of several pivotal trials, and the comparative balance between efficacy and treatment-related toxicities remains incompletely understood. Consequently, high-quality comparative evidence is needed to guide individualized treatment selection for patients with HER2-positive advanced breast cancer.

Network meta-analysis (NMA) enables simultaneous integration of direct and indirect evidence across multiple interventions and facilitates comparative treatment ranking when head-to-head trials are unavailable. 11 Compared with conventional pairwise meta-analysis, Bayesian NMA provides a robust framework for evaluating complex treatment networks and estimating the probability that each intervention represents the optimal therapeutic option. Therefore, we conducted an updated Bayesian network meta-analysis to comprehensively compare the efficacy and safety of currently available HER2-targeted TKIs in patients with HER2-positive advanced or locally advanced breast cancer. By integrating both efficacy and adverse-event outcomes and generating treatment rankings, this study aims to provide updated evidence for individualized treatment selection and clinical decision-making.

2. Methods

Bayesian network meta-analysis was carried out in this study. The network meta-analysis was conducted and reported according to the preferred reporting items for systematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA) extension statement. 12 The protocol was registered on PROSPERO (CRD420250650274).

2.1. Data Sources

We conducted a systematic and comprehensive search of electronic databases, including PubMed, Cochrane Library, Embase, and Web of Science, employing a combination of subject headings and free-text terms. The search terms encompassed “breast neoplasms,” “breast cancer,” “TKI,” “lapatinib,” “pyrotinib,” “tucatinib,” “neratinib,” and related synonyms. Furthermore, we performed supplementary screening of the references from studies meeting the inclusion criteria to identify any potentially relevant literature that may have been overlooked during the electronic database search. The search spanned from the inception of each database to July 31, 2025.An additional updated search was performed in June 2026 before manuscript submission to identify any newly published or updated eligible studies.

2.2. Criteria for Inclusion and Exclusion

Inclusion criteria: (1) This review includes only randomized controlled trials (RCT) published in English; (2) Studies must involve patients with histopathologically confirmed advanced/metastatic breast cancer who are HER2-positive based on molecular testing; (3) The studies must compare two or more treatment groups, including first-line and subsequent-line treatments; (4) Intervention measures include monotherapy or combination therapy with one of the small molecule TKI inhibitors (Tucatinib, Pyrotinib, Lapatinib, Neratinib). Combination therapy may consist of TKI + chemotherapy or TKI + chemotherapy + trastuzumab. Control measures include placebo + chemotherapy or placebo + chemotherapy + trastuzumab; (5) Studies must report at least one of the following outcome indicators: primary outcomes (progression-free survival [PFS], overall survival [OS], objective response rate [ORR], disease control rate [DCR]) and secondary outcomes (incidence of serious adverse events [SAE] and the most common adverse reactions in each treatment group, including diarrhea, anemia, rash, and hand-foot syndrome).

Exclusion criteria: (1) Non-randomized controlled studies, such as single-arm trials, case reports, and meta-analyses; (2) Studies with literature types including systematic reviews, meta-analyses, animal experiments, conference abstracts, guidelines, letters, or other non-primary research formats; (3) Duplicated publications identified through cross-referencing; (4) Studies with incomplete data or where it is impossible to extract relevant data on the efficacy and safety of interventions.

2.3. Data Extraction

Records identified through database searches were initially subjected to preliminary screening using EBM AI-Reviewer—a validated, artificial intelligence–based tool specifically designed to support literature screening in accordance with the PICOS (Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcomes, and Study design) framework. Subsequently, all candidate studies underwent independent manual review and final eligibility confirmation by the authors. Two investigators (LRJ and HFR) independently assessed the eligibility of potentially relevant studies through a sequential evaluation of titles, abstracts, and full-text articles, strictly adhering to the prespecified inclusion and exclusion criteria. Studies addressing the research question but lacking sufficient quantitative data were retained solely for qualitative synthesis and excluded from the meta-analysis. Any discrepancies in study selection were resolved through consensus discussion; unresolved disagreements were adjudicated by the corresponding author (PYD). Data extraction and methodological quality assessment were conducted independently by two investigators (HFR and HK). Discrepancies were first reconciled through discussion between the two reviewers; when consensus could not be reached, a third investigator was consulted to make the final determination. Extracted variables encompassed: (1) general study information—including title, author(s), study design, and other pertinent bibliographic details; and (2) key research characteristics—such as tumor stage, number of prior treatment lines, treatment strategy, sample size, and outcome measures.

2.4. Quality Evaluation

The risk of bias in the included studies was independently evaluated by two reviewers using the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions (Version 5.1.0). 13 Seven key domains were assessed: random sequence generation, allocation concealment, blinding of participants and personnel, blinding of outcome assessment, incomplete outcome data, selective reporting, and other sources of bias. Commercial funding, premature study discontinuation, and baseline imbalances between comparison groups were considered potential contributors to other bias. Each domain was judged as having a low, moderate, or high risk of bias. Disagreements between reviewers were resolved through discussion, with arbitration by a third reviewer when consensus could not be reached.

2.5. Statistical Analysis

The primary outcome measures included OS and PFS, while the secondary outcome measures comprised ORR, DCR, SAE, and common treatment-related adverse reactions. OS and PFS data were analyzed using hazard ratios (HRs) with their corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CI). ORR, DCR, and adverse reactions were assessed using odds ratios (ORs) along with their respective 95% CI. A result was considered statistically significant if the 95% CI did not include 1.

This study employed R 4.2.2 software to construct the network evidence diagram. When the network formed closed loops, the node-splitting method was used to assess consistency by comparing direct and indirect evidence derived from the Bayesian framework. A P-value greater than 0.05 indicated acceptable consistency, prompting the use of the consistency model for analysis. In cases where the network did not form closed loops, the Deviance Information Criterion (DIC) was utilized to evaluate the fit of both consistency and inconsistency models. If the difference in DIC between the two models was less than 5, the consistency model was selected. Throughout this study, the consistency model was applied to synthesize all data. The “gemtc” package in R was utilized to perform the network meta-analysis within the Bayesian framework, employing the Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) method. A Bayesian random-effects consistency model was applied to account for between-study heterogeneity. For dichotomous outcomes, including ORR, DCR, and treatment-related adverse events, four independent MCMC chains were run with 50,000 adaptation iterations and 100,000 sampling iterations, with a thinning interval of 1. For OS analysis, four independent MCMC chains were used with 20,000 adaptation iterations and 50,000 sampling iterations, with a thinning interval of 1. Convergence of the network model was verified using trace plots, density plots, and Gelman-Rubin diagnostic plots. Treatment efficacy and safety were ranked based on Surface Under the Cumulative Ranking Curve (SUCRA) values. Higher SUCRA values indicated a higher probability of being ranked more favorably. 14

3. Results

3.1. Study Selection

A total of 1,924 articles were identified through the initial database search. After excluding duplicate records, the titles and abstracts of the remaining articles were screened to exclude those unrelated to the research topic. Ultimately, 14 randomized controlled trials (RCTs) involving 5,289 participants were included in the network meta-analysis, with 2,811 (53.1%) allocated to the intervention group and 2,478 (46.9%) to the control group. An updated search was conducted before submission in June 2026, which identified no additional eligible RCTs; one updated report from a previously included trial was replaced with the latest publication. 3.2. Risk of Bias in Included Studies

All 14 included studies were RCT. The randomization methods in all studies were assessed as having a low risk of bias. Allocation concealment was rated as low risk in 9 studies. Blinding of both researchers and participants was implemented in 3 studies, while all studies employed blinding for outcome assessment. Outcome data were complete in all studies, and none of the studies exhibited selective reporting. The presence of other potential sources of bias remained unclear across all studies (Figure 2).

4. Discussion

In this network meta-analysis, we assessed the efficacy and safety of four small molecule TKI inhibitors targeting HER2 in patients with HER2-positive unresectable, locally advanced, or metastatic breast cancer, focusing on PFS, OS, ORR, DCR and treatment-related adverse events. The results indicated that Pyrotinib_C showed favorable relative effects for PFS and ORR compared with other evaluated regimens, findings that are consistent with those reported by Li et al 29 It is important to note that, in the original studies included in this analysis, the Pyrotinib_C regimen was predominantly administered to patients who had previously received trastuzumab and taxane-based treatments and had undergone no more than two lines of chemotherapy. 18 Its remarkable therapeutic efficacy may be attributed to Pyrotinib’s characteristic as an irreversible pan-ErbB receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor. Studies have demonstrated that trastuzumab resistance mechanisms are multifaceted, encompassing p95 HER2 overexpression, MUC4 overexpression, activation of alternative pathways such as HER3 and IGF-1R, and cMET pathway abnormalities. 30 Moreover, trastuzumab, a large-molecule monoclonal antibody, blocks signal transduction by binding to the extracellular domain of HER2. In contrast, Pyrotinib, a small-molecule TKI, directly inhibits the intracellular kinase domains of HER1/HER2/HER4, thereby blocking downstream signaling pathways (e.g., PI3K/AKT, RAS/RAF/MEK/ERK) and overcoming resistance induced by extracellular structural variations or bypass activation of trastuzumab. 31

The treatment strategy combining Tucatinib, trastuzumab, and capecitabine may provide potential survival benefits in prolonging OS for patients with HER2-positive advanced or locally advanced breast cancer. Unlike Pyrotinib, which is an irreversible pan-ErbB receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor, Tucatinib is a highly selective HER2-targeted TKI. While the improvement in OS across the overall patient population did not achieve statistical significance with Tucatinib-based combination therapy, its efficacy was notably pronounced in specific subgroups of patients. Among patients with HER2-positive breast cancer, approximately 30% to 50% will develop brain metastases, and the prognosis for these patients is generally poor. Traditional treatment regimens, such as radiotherapy and chemotherapy, exhibit limited efficacy in managing brain metastases.The limited penetration of many large-molecule anti-HER2 agents across the blood-brain barrier may contribute to suboptimal intracranial control. In contrast, Tucatinib, a small-molecule TKI, has demonstrated intracranial activity and clinically meaningful benefits in patients with brain metastases. 32 The pivotal Phase III HER2CLIMB trial demonstrated that, among patients with baseline brain metastases, the median OS in the tucatinib combination therapy group was 18.1 months, significantly longer than the 12.0 months observed in the control group (HR = 0.58, P< 0.001). These findings support the potential clinical value of tucatinib-based therapy in patients with brain metastases, although interpretation should consider differences in patient selection and treatment background across trials. 15 Nevertheless, the interpretation of the comparative efficacy among HER2-targeted TKIs should consider the potential clinical heterogeneity across the included trials. As summarized in Supplementary Table S1, substantial differences existed in previous anti-HER2 treatment exposure, treatment line, baseline characteristics, including hormone receptor status and ECOG performance status, and brain metastasis inclusion criteria. For example, HER2CLIMB 15 enrolled a heavily pretreated population with previous exposure to trastuzumab, pertuzumab, and T-DM1, while several other trials evaluated TKIs in earlier treatment settings with fewer prior systemic therapies.20,24 Furthermore, the proportion and eligibility criteria of patients with brain metastases varied considerably among studies, with HER2CLIMB specifically including patients with active or stable brain metastases, whereas some trials excluded or did not report detailed information regarding central nervous system involvement.18,28 These variations may influence treatment response and survival outcomes and introduce additional uncertainty into indirect comparisons. Therefore, the results of this network meta-analysis should be interpreted together with the clinical characteristics of the included populations and the certainty of evidence.

Furthermore, the favorable efficacy and safety profile of tucatinib-based therapy may be partly attributed to its high selectivity for HER2 with minimal inhibition of EGFR, potentially reducing EGFR-related toxicities such as rash and diarrhea. When combined with trastuzumab and capecitabine, tucatinib may provide complementary antitumor effects through inhibition of HER2-mediated signaling pathways, antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), and direct cytotoxic activity, respectively. 33

In safety assessments, studies have demonstrated that the incidence of adverse events in various TKI-based treatment regimens is significantly higher compared to chemotherapy alone or trastuzumab-combined chemotherapy regimens. Specifically, adverse events such as diarrhea, anemia, rash, and hand-foot syndrome are markedly elevated, with the Pyrotinib combination regimen showing particularly prominent adverse reactions. Research data reveal distinct safety profiles across different TKI treatment regimens: the Pyrotinib_T_C regimen exhibits the highest incidence of SAEs and diarrhea, though symptoms can be effectively managed through dietary adjustments, timely administration of loperamide and montmorillonite powder. 34 The Pyrotinib_C regimen is more prone to inducing anemia and hand-foot syndrome, while the Lapatinib_C regimen is characterized by a high incidence of rash, which can be mitigated via local treatments and dose modifications. Based on these differentiated safety profiles, we propose the establishment of a systematic three-tier adverse reaction monitoring system in clinical practice: (1) baseline risk assessment; (2) dynamic monitoring during treatment (e.g., blood tests, liver function); (3) graded intervention strategies (ranging from dose adjustment to supportive care). Through this individualized full-course management approach, anti-tumor efficacy can be maintained while maximizing patient tolerance and safety.

At present, the standard first-line treatment for HER2 positive advanced or locally advanced breast cancer is the dual-targeted therapy combining trastuzumab with pertuzumab and chemotherapy. This approach has been validated in the CLEOPATRA study to significantly extend PFS and OS, offering patients more sustained survival benefits. 35 In contrast, small molecule TKIs are predominantly utilized in second-line or later treatments. The rationale lies in their ability to inhibit intracellular HER2 tyrosine kinase activity and block downstream signaling pathways, such as PI3K/AKT and MAPK, thereby maintaining efficacy even in trastuzumab-resistant patients. Moreover, certain studies indicate that compared to large molecule antibody drugs, TKIs exhibit superior recognition of HER2 mutants and enhanced penetration capabilities, making them particularly advantageous for patients with brain metastases. 36 However, as the number of treatment lines increases, tumor cells may develop resistance to TKIs through mechanisms such as acquired HER2 mutations, persistent activation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway (e.g., PIK3CA mutations, PTEN loss), and upregulation of bypass receptors like HER3 and MET, resulting in diminished therapeutic efficacy.37,38 Consequently, a deeper understanding of TKI resistance mechanisms and the exploration of combination treatment strategies will be critical directions for enhancing treatment efficacy and extending patient survival in the future.Compared with previous network meta-analyses evaluating HER2-targeted therapies, the present study provides several important updates. First, recently published pivotal clinical trials involving newer-generation HER2-targeted TKIs were incorporated, enabling a more comprehensive assessment of currently available treatment options. Second, in addition to evaluating efficacy outcomes including PFS, OS, ORR, and DCR, we systematically assessed major treatment-related adverse events, thereby providing a more balanced evaluation of benefit-risk profiles. Third, the Bayesian network meta-analysis framework enabled simultaneous comparison and ranking of multiple treatment strategies through SUCRA probabilities, offering comparative evidence to assist treatment selection in situations where direct head-to-head comparisons remain limited.These methodological advantages enhance the comparative understanding of available treatment options and may provide supportive evidence for individualized therapeutic decision-making in patients with HER2-positive advanced breast cancer.

This study possesses several notable strengths. By specifically focusing on currently available HER2-targeted small-molecule TKIs, the present analysis provides an updated and comprehensive comparison of their efficacy and safety profiles in patients with HER2-positive advanced or locally advanced breast cancer. Unlike traditional RCTs, network meta-analysis integrates both direct and indirect evidence, enabling the inference of relative effects between drugs that have not been directly compared, thereby significantly expanding the analytical dimensions and scope. Second, this study employs a Bayesian framework-based network meta-analysis model, which demonstrates high flexibility and robustness in handling comparisons among multiple treatment strategies and sparse data. By calculating SUCRA values for each outcome measure, this study provided a probabilistic ranking of the relative efficacy and safety profiles of different TKIs. However, these rankings should not be interpreted as direct evidence of clinical superiority, but rather considered together with the magnitude of treatment effects, confidence intervals, clinical characteristics, and certainty of evidence when making treatment decisions.

Despite these strengths, this study is subject to certain limitations: 1. Some of the included studies were open-label trials, which may introduce potential performance and detection biases. Furthermore, some studies had relatively small sample sizes or short observation periods, potentially compromising the stability and precision of effect size estimation. 2. Key outcome indicators, such as quality of life (QoL), disease-free survival (DFS), distant metastasis rate, and long-term toxicities (e.g., cardiotoxicity), were either missing or underreported in the original studies. This limitation restricts the ability to comprehensively evaluate the clinical benefits and risks associated with TKIs. 3. Due to the limited number of included studies, subgroup analyses could not be performed, precluding a detailed examination of dose-related differences across patient populations. This may constrain the comprehensiveness of the findings. 4.Although network meta-analysis enables comparisons among multiple interventions through the integration of direct and indirect evidence, several comparisons in this study were primarily informed by indirect evidence rather than direct head-to-head trials. Therefore, the relative rankings and comparative effects should be interpreted cautiously, considering the uncertainty associated with indirect comparisons, clinical heterogeneity among included trials, and the certainty of available evidence.

5. Conclusion

In summary, among patients with HER2-positive advanced or locally advanced breast cancer, Pyrotinib-based combination therapy showed favorable rankings for progression-free survival and tumor response outcomes, including ORR and DCR, whereas Tucatinib-based therapy demonstrated potential advantages in overall survival, particularly among patients with brain metastases. However, Pyrotinib-containing regimens were associated with a relatively higher incidence of treatment-related adverse events, including diarrhea, anemia, and hand-foot syndrome. Therefore, treatment selection should consider the balance between efficacy and safety. These findings should be interpreted with caution because the network meta-analysis incorporated both direct and indirect evidence and was influenced by clinical heterogeneity among the included trials, including differences in patient characteristics, previous anti-HER2 treatment exposure, treatment line, and treatment settings.

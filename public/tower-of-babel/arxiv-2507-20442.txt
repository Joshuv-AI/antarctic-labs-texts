Quantitative gap universality for Wigner matrices
Albert Zhang

Abstract
We obtain the explicit rate of convergence N − 1 2 + ε N^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon} for the gaps of generalized Wigner matrices in the bulk of the spectrum, for distributions of matrix entries possibly atomic and supported on enough points. The proof proceeds by a Green function comparison, coupled with the relaxation estimate from [ 8 ] . In particular, we extend the 4 moment matching method [ 33 ] to arbitrary moments, allowing to compare resolvents down to the submicroscopic scale N − 3 2 + ε N^{-\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon} . This method also gives universality of the smallest gaps between eigenvalues for the Hermitian symmetry class, providing a universal, optimal separation of eigenvalues for discrete random matrices with entries supported on Ω ⁡ ( 1 ) \Omega(1) points.

1. Introduction

1.1. Quantitative universality and extreme statistics in random matrix theory

In the past two decades, significant progress on the universality of gaps between eigenvalues of (generalized) Wigner matrices has been made via the Erdős-Schlein-Yau dynamical approach
[
13
,
16
]
, combined with the four moment theorem of Tao and Vu
[
33
]
. See for example
[
16
]
for results in the bulk of the spectrum, and
[
6
]
for results in the edge of the spectrum, with non-explicit polynomial rates of convergence
N^{-c}
.
Quantitative universality, on the other hand, has only been well studied at the edge. In
[
8
]
*Theorem 1.5 the first explicit rate of convergence regarding the largest eigenvalue of a generalized Wigner matrix was shown. In particular, if
\lambda_{N}
denotes the largest eigenvalue and
TW
the Tracy-Widom distribution, we have for any
c>0
and large enough
N
that

d_{\text{K}}(N^{2/3}(\lambda_{N}-2),\text{TW})\leq N^{-\frac{2}{9}+c},

(1)

where the Kolmogorov distance is defined in (
6
).
One step for the proof is to apply a moment-matching or Green function comparison theorem in order to replace
H_{N}
by a Gaussian divisible ensemble (GDE),

\widetilde{H}_{t}:=e^{-t/2}\widetilde{H}_{0}+(1-e^{-t})^{1/2}U,

where
\widetilde{H}_{0}
is a generalized Wigner matrix and
U
is an independent Gaussian orthogonal ensemble (GOE). Then an edge relaxation estimate, see e.g.
[
8
]
*Theorem 2.8, is used to replace the gaps of GDE with those of GOE. In a last step, existing quantitative estimates for the rate of convergence for GOE to Tracy-Widom are imported from, say,
[
23
]
.

We adapt and extend this strategy to the gaps in the bulk of the spectrum, for which we obtain the following estimate on the Kolmogorov distance:

d_{\text{K}}(N(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}),N(\mu_{k+1}-\mu_{k}))\leq N^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon},

(2)

where
(\lambda_{k})
and
(\mu_{k})
are the ordered eigenvalues of a generalized Wigner matrix and GOE, respectively. We also show a universality result regarding the smallest gap for a complex Wigner matrix

d_{\text{K}}\left(\min_{\alpha N\leq k\leq(1-\alpha)N}N^{\frac{4}{3}}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}),\min_{\alpha N\leq k\leq(1-\alpha)N}N^{\frac{4}{3}}(\mu_{k+1}-\mu_{k})\right)={\rm o}(1),

(3)

where the matrix entries may have a discrete support, both for (
2
) and (
3
).

The bound (
2
) provides the first explicit speed for bulk universality. Regarding the optimal separation of eigenvalues (
3
),
the previous result was only for smallest gaps of Wigner matrices whose entries were smooth on a sufficient scale
[
8
]
*Corollary 1.3. This result from
[
8
]
relies on the reverse heat flow, see for example
[
8
]
*Lemma 4.1, as a replacement for the moment matching method, excluding Wigner matrices whose entries are atomic.

One caveat is that for both (
2
) and (
3
), the entries of our Wigner matrices are required to be supported on at least
p
points for a large enough
p
, e.g.
p=p(\varepsilon)<\infty
grows as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
in (
2
) .
This constraint arises due the need to extend the four moment theorem of Tao and Vu
[
33
]
by allowing moments to match up to the
p
-th order, for some large
p=p(\varepsilon)
not depending on
N
, see Lemma
2.4
. This enables us to obtain better error estimates in our Green function comparison therefore achieving an explicit rate of convergence for the gaps (
2
).

Finally, we note that the edge result (
1
) has been improved in
[
29
,
31
]
to
N^{-\frac{1}{3}+c}
, but the methods we use are closer to those in
[
8
]
as we do not rely on
long-time comparison of the Green functions, a method particularly efficient at the edge of the spectrum but not in the bulk. Also, for analogous results regarding the largest eigenvalue of sample covariance matrices see
[
30
,
35
]
.

The study of extreme gaps between random spectra started with integrable models. Vinson
[
34
]
showed that the smallest gap between eigenvalues of the
N\times N
circular unitary ensemble (CUE), when rescaled by
N^{4/3}
, has limiting density
3x^{2}e^{-x^{2}}
as
N
goes to
\infty
; similar results for Hermitian invariant ensembles (including the Gaussian Unitary Ensemble, GUE) were obtained in his thesis.
Analogous results hold for the smallest gap for general translation invariant determinantal point processes in large boxes
[
32
]
.
Smallest gaps statistics were extended to other temperatures, with the smallest gaps of the circular
\beta
-ensemble
[
20
]
with integer
\beta
and the Gaussian orthogonal and symplectic ensembles
[
18
,
17
]
.
The question of largest gaps between nearest eigenvalues was raised in
[
12
]
and solved at leading order in
[
1
]
, and up to the limiting distribution in
[
19
]
, both for CUE and GUE.

Regarding universality of extreme gaps, some of the results mentioned above on largest and smallest gaps for the CUE, GUE extend to multimatrix ensembles
[
21
]
. For Wigner matrices, universality of the largest gaps
was obtained in
[
24
]
for any matrix entries, while
[
8
]
showed universal statistics of both largest and smallest gaps for entries satisfying a smoothness condition, see (
7
). For symmetric random matrices with possibly discrete entries,
suboptimal bounds for separation between eigenvalues have been derived in
[
25
,
27
]
.
Motivations for studying the extreme eigenvalues’ gaps statistics come from numerical linear algebra
[
1
,
11
]
, conjectures in analytic number theory
[
1
,
9
]
, algorithmic number theory
[
4
]
, and quantum chaos in the complementary Poissonian regime
[
3
]
.

Other extreme statistics are of interest in random matrix theory. Notably
the work of Fyodorov, Hiary, and Keating (FHK)
[
22
]
conjectures the maximum of the characteristic polynomial of random matrices and relates it to the maximum of the Riemann zeta function on typical intervals of the critical line. These random matrix extreme statistics are now well understood in the case of integrable, circular ensembles such as
[
10
,
28
]
, and at the leading order for universal Wigner and
\beta
-ensembles
[
7
]
. High moment matching of random matrix ensembles as developed in our work may help refine universality results on the FHK conjectures, or other extreme and typical statistics in random matrix theory.

1.2. Notations

In this paper we denote
c,C>0
generic small and large constants which do not depend on
N
but may vary from line to line. Also let

\varphi=e^{C_{0}(\log\log N)^{2}}

(4)

be a subpolynomial error parameter, for some fixed
C_{0}>0
. The constant
C_{0}
is chosen large enough so that the local law and rigidity estimates, Theorems
A.1
and
A.2
, hold.

Definition 1.1
.

A generalized Wigner matrix
H=H(N)
is a Hermitian or symmetric
N\times N
matrix whose upper-triangular elements
H_{ij}=\overline{H_{ji}}
,
i\leq j
, are independent random variables with mean zero and variances
\sigma_{ij}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left[|H_{ij}|^{2}\right]
that satisfy the following two conditions:

•

Normalization: for any
1\leq j\leq N
,
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma_{ij}^{2}=1
.

•

Non-degeneracy:
\sigma_{ij}^{2}\asymp N^{-1}
uniformly in
N
and
1\leq i,j\leq N
.

In the Hermitian case we also assume
\Var\operatorname{Re}(H_{ij})\asymp\Var\operatorname{Im}(H_{ij})
, also uniformly in the parameters, and independence of
\operatorname{Re}(H_{ij})
and
\operatorname{Im}(H_{ij})
.

We also assume
an exponential decay estimate on the matrix entries: there exists
c>0
such that for all
i,j,N
and
x>0
we have

\mathbb{P}\left(|\sqrt{N}H_{ij}|>x\right)\leq c^{-1}e^{-x^{c}}.

(5)

We will always order and denote the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{N}
of
H
, and
\mu_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{N}
for those of a GOE or GUE matrix. We denote the limiting spectral density for Wigner matrices as

\rho_{\text{sc}}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{(4-x^{2})_{+}}.

In this paper we consider the Kolmogorov distance,

d_{\text{K}}(X,Y)=\sup_{x}\left|\mathbb{P}(X\leq x)-\mathbb{P}(Y\leq y)\right|,

(6)

as our way of measuring the rate of convergence.

Our main results in Section
1.3
hold for possibly atomic distributions for the matrix entries, provided they are supported on enough points, uniformy in the following sense.

Definition 1.2
.

We say that a probability measure
\mu
has
p
-support if there exists
c>\widetilde{c},\kappa,A
and
p
points
x_{1},\dots,x_{p}

such that
\min_{i\neq j}|x_{i}-x_{j}|>c
,
\mu[x_{i},x_{i}+\widetilde{c}]>\kappa
,
\max_{i}|x_{i}|\leq A
.

We say that a collection
(\mu_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I}
of probability measures has uniform
p
-support if each
\mu_{\alpha}
has
p
-support with
c>\widetilde{c},\kappa,A
being uniform in
\alpha
.

A sequence of symmetric random matrices
(H_{N})
is also said to have uniform
p
-support is the collection of distributions of
(\sqrt{N}H_{ij})_{N,1\leq i\leq j\leq N}
has uniform
p
-support.
In the Hermitian case, the definition states that
(\sqrt{N}{\rm Re}H_{ij})_{N,1\leq i\leq j\leq N}
and

(\sqrt{N}{\rm Im}H_{ij})_{N,1\leq i\leq j\leq N}

have uniform
p
-support.

In particular, for both universality classes, if the
\sqrt{N}H_{i\leq j}
are i.i.d. with
p
-support,
(H_{N})
has uniform
p
-support.

A much more restrictive assumption is smoothness of the matrix entries. A sequence
(H^{(N)})_{N}
of generalized Wigner matrices is said to be smooth on scale
\sigma=\sigma(N)
if
\sqrt{N}H_{ij}
has density
e^{-V}
, where the potential
V=V_{N,i,j}
satisfies the following condition uniformly in
N,i,j
: For any
k\geq 0
there exists a
C_{k}>0
such that

|V^{(k)}(x)|\leq C_{k}\sigma^{-k}(1+|x|)^{C_{k}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

(7)

Under this smoothness condition, Bourgade
[
8
]
obtained quantitative rates of convergence on the extreme gaps process of Wigner matrices. Our result, Theorem
1.5
does not require this smoothness condition since we do not rely on the reverse heat flow method (see e.g.
[
8
]
*Lemma 4.1) and instead extend the Green function comparison machinery to higher moments.

1.3. Main results

We now state our main results on quantitative universality for gaps of Wigner matrices. Note that all of our results concern the bulk of the spectrum. The analogous best known rates in the edge at the time of writing are given by
[
31
]
.

Theorem 1.3

(Convergence rate for a single gap)
.

Let
\alpha,\varepsilon>0
be fixed.
Let
\mu_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{N}
be the eigenvalues of a GOE matrix. Then there exists an
p=p(\varepsilon)
such that if
\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{N}
are the eigenvalues of a real Wigner ensemble with uniform
p
-support, for all large enough
N
, uniformly in
\alpha N\leq k\leq(1-\alpha)N
, we have

d_{\text{K}}\left(N(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}),N(\mu_{k+1}-\mu_{k})\right)\leq N^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

(8)

The analogue result holds for the Hermitian symmetry class.

The above bound
N^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}
gives a very explicit, reasonable convergence speed, but in view of (i) the
error estimate
(N^{2}t)^{-1}
in
[
8
]
*Corollary 3.3 (ii) the Berry-Esseen theorem and the
N^{2}
independent sources of randomness for Wigner matrices, it seems plausible that the optimal error term is
N^{-1}
, as stated below.

Conjecture 1.4

(Optimal speed of convergence)
.

Let
\alpha,\varepsilon>0
be fixed. Then there exists an
p=p(\varepsilon)
sufficiently large such that if
(H_{N})
is a generalized Wigner ensemble with uniform
p
-support, for all large enough
N
, uniformly in
\alpha N\leq k\leq(1-\alpha)N
, we have

d_{\text{K}}\left(N(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}),N(\mu_{k+1}-\mu_{k})\right)\leq N^{-1+\varepsilon}.

Moreover, a stronger conjecture states that
p=2
is enough.

We also have the associated result for the smallest gap of a
complex
Wigner matrix:

Theorem 1.5

(Convergence for the smallest gap, complex case)
.

Let
\alpha>0
be fixed. Let
\mu_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{N}
be the eigenvalues of a GUE matrix. Then there exists an
p
such that if
\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{N}
are the eigenvalues of a complex Wigner ensemble with uniform
p
-support, for all large enough
N
we have

d_{\text{K}}\left(\min_{\alpha N\leq k\leq(1-\alpha)N}N^{\frac{4}{3}}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}),\min_{\alpha N\leq k\leq(1-\alpha)N}N^{\frac{4}{3}}(\mu_{k+1}-\mu_{k})\right)={\rm o}(1).

(9)

In particular combining the above theorem with
[
1
]
*Corollary 1.5, there exists a constant
c=c(\alpha)
such that for any
x\geq 0
,

\mathbb{P}\left(c\min_{\alpha N\leq k\leq(1-\alpha)N}N^{\frac{4}{3}}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k})\leq x\right)\to\int_{0}^{x}3u^{2}e^{-u^{3}}{\rm d}u.

Remark
.

The required
p
above can be made explicit, e.g. the choice
p\geq 10
(say) suffices, which may be seen through our proof.

Remark
.

In (
9
) one can consider
\min_{\alpha N\leq k\leq(1-\alpha)N}c_{k}N^{\frac{4}{3}}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k})
(and similarly with
\boldsymbol{\mu}
) with
c_{k}\asymp 1
instead, and the same result holds.

For the real symmetric class, we have the following conjecture, which similarly states convergence of the smallest gap to the smallest gap for GOE.

Conjecture 1.6

(Convergence for the smallest gap, real case)
.

Let
\alpha>0
be fixed. Let
\mu_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{N}
be the eigenvalues of a GOE matrix. Then there exists
p\geq 2
sufficiently large such that if
(H_{N})
is a real random matrix from the generalized Wigner ensemble, with uniform
p
-support, for all large enough
N
we have

d_{\text{K}}\left(\min_{\alpha N\leq k\leq(1-\alpha)N}N^{\frac{3}{2}}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}),\min_{\alpha N\leq k\leq(1-\alpha)N}N^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mu_{k+1}-\mu_{k})\right)={\rm o}(1).

A stronger conjecture states that
p=2
is enough, both for the the result above and for (
9
).

In particular, combined with
[
18
]
*Corollary 1 we expect that for some
C=C(\alpha)
we have, for any
x\geq 0
,

\mathbb{P}\left(C\min_{\alpha N\leq k\leq(1-\alpha)N}N^{\frac{3}{2}}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k})\leq x\right)\to\int_{0}^{x}2ue^{-u^{2}}{\rm d}u.

Although this conjecture does not seem accessible with the methods developed in this paper, the following weaker statement is within reach e.g. see the remark after Theorem
3.3
.
It corresponds to any scale greater than the smallest gap in the case of the real symmetry class:
For any fixed
\alpha,\delta>0
there exists a
p=p(\varepsilon)
sufficiently large such that if
(H_{N})
is a real generalized Wigner matrix with uniform
p
-support, for all large enough
N
we have

\mathbb{P}^{H_{N}}(\min_{\alpha N\leq k\leq(1-\alpha)N}N^{\frac{3}{2}}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k})\geq N^{\delta})\leq N^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

(10)

The reason for the small
\delta>0
discrepancy between the complex and real cases is that our Green function comparison estimate will be of type

\mathbb{E}\left[\Tr\left(\frac{1}{H_{N}-z}\right)-\Tr\left(\frac{1}{H^{\text{GOE}}-z}\right)\right]\leq N^{q}\frac{N^{-p/2}}{(N\eta)^{p}}

(11)

for some fixed constant
q>0
, with
z=E+\text{i}\eta
. In particular, only when
\eta\gg N^{-3/2}
can we take
p
fixed, sufficiently large to identify the Green function on scale
\eta
.

Remark
.

There is no obvious guess for the optimal error term in Theorem
1.5
and Conjecture
1.6
, and it may depend on
p
from the
p
-support assumption.
If we only aim at universality, i.e. an error term
{\rm o}(1)
, it is natural to also expect that any
p\geq 2
is admissible, both in the settings of Theorem
1.5
and Conjecture
1.6
, a result which seems out of reach with our method.

Regarding the error term in (
10
), we expect that
the probability of the smallest gap of a GOE matrix to live at scale larger than
N^{-\frac{3}{2}+\delta}
should be exponentially small in
N
, and similarly for any generalized Wigner matrix.

2. Rate of convergence for gaps between eigenvalues

As is standard in the three-step approach to universality (see e.g.
[
16
]
), we aim to combine a moment matching estimate with relaxation via dynamics. In particular, we let
\boldsymbol{\lambda}(t)
and
\boldsymbol{\mu}(t)
be the eigenvalues of two copies of the matrix Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process

{\rm d}H_{t}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}{\rm d}B_{t}-\frac{1}{2}H_{t}{\rm d}t,

(12)

where the initial conditions
\boldsymbol{\lambda}(0)
and
\boldsymbol{\mu}(0)
are given by an independent generalized Wigner matrix and GOE, respectively. From
[
8
]
*Corollary 3.3, the following relaxation estimate regarding an individual eigenvalue gap is known:

Theorem 2.1
.

There exists a coupling between the matrix evolutions under (
12
) such that the following holds.
Let
\alpha,c>0
be fixed, arbitrarily small. Then for any (large)
D>0
, there exist
C,N_{0}
such that for any
N>N_{0}
,
\varphi^{C}/N<t<1
and
\alpha N\leq k\leq(1-\alpha)N
we have

\mathbb{P}\left(|(\lambda_{k+1}(t)-\lambda_{k}(t))-(\mu_{k+1}(t)-\mu_{k}(t))|>\frac{N^{c}}{N^{2}t}\right)\leq N^{-D}.

(13)

The relaxation estimate (
13
) is a component enabling the three-step strategy in our paper. Our contribution is in the moment-matching component. Altogether, our approach to universality for a generalized Wigner matrix
H
may be summed up in Figure
1
, where we have labeled the current best error estimates for the two respective components.

,

.

What is missing is the existence of a GDE matrix
\widetilde{H}_{t}
, matching the first
p
moments with a given generalized Wigner matrix
H
. For this we proceed as follows.

We first state the following lemma as in
[
14
]
*Lemma 3.4 (which we will not use) for sake of comparison with our existence result, Lemma
2.4
. The result below covers the Bernoulli case but only matches the first three moments and the fourth with an
O(t)
error.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
m_{3}
and
m_{4}
be two real numbers such that

m_{4}-m_{3}^{2}-1\geq 0,\quad m_{4}\leq C_{2}

for some positive constant
C_{2}
. Let
U
be a real Gaussian random variable with mean 0 and variance 1. Then for any sufficiently small
t>0
(depending on
C_{2}
), there exits an independent real random variable
h_{0}
, centered with variance 1 and with subexponential decay, for which the first four moments of

h_{t}:=(1-t)^{1/2}h_{0}+t^{1/2}U

are
m_{1}(h_{t})=0
,
m_{2}(h_{t})=1
,
m_{3}(h_{t})=m_{3}
, and
m_{4}(h_{t})
such that

|m_{r}(h_{t})-m_{4}|=Ct,

where
C=C(C_{2})>0
.

In the higher moment matching below, for any probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
we denote

m_{k}(\mu)=\int x^{k}{\rm d}\mu(x)
and
\boldsymbol{m}_{p}(\mu)=(m_{1}(\mu),\ldots,m_{p}(\mu))
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Fix a
p\geq 1
and
\mu
a probability measure with
p
-support in the sense of Definition
1.2
, with parameters
c,\widetilde{c},\kappa,A
. Then there exists
\delta=\delta(c,\widetilde{c},\kappa,A)>0
such that for any
\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}

such that
|\boldsymbol{m}-\boldsymbol{m}_{p}(\mu)|<\delta
there exists
\nu
coinciding with
\mu
on
|x|>A+1
such that
\boldsymbol{m}_{p}(\nu)=\boldsymbol{m}
.

Remark
.

By analogy with the case
p=2
from Lemma
2.2
one expects that it is even possible to match
p+1
moments, i.e. for any
\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{p+1}
close to
\mu_{p+1}(\mu)
find
\nu
close to
\mu
such that
\boldsymbol{m}_{p}(\nu)=\boldsymbol{m}
. We don’t consider such an improvement here: The dependence
p=p(\varepsilon)
in our main results, e.g. in Theorem
1.3
, is anyways suboptimal from other aspects of the proof.

Proof.

From Definition
1.2
,
\mu
can be written as

\mu=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mu_{k}+\nu
where
\mu_{k}
is supported on
[x_{k},x_{k}+\widetilde{c}]
,
\mu_{k}[x_{k},x_{k}+\widetilde{c}]>\kappa
and
\min_{i\neq j}|x_{i}-x_{j}|>c
, and
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{p},\nu
are positive measures. Without loss of generality we assume
x_{1}>\dots>x_{p}
.

For any
\boldsymbol{\delta}=(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{p})\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
we define the probability measure
\mu^{\boldsymbol{\delta}}
through

\mu^{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(B)=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mu_{k}(B-\delta_{k})+\nu(B)
for any Borel set
B
. Then

m_{\ell}(\mu^{\boldsymbol{\delta}})=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\int(y+\delta_{k})^{\ell}\mu_{k}({\rm d}y)+\int y^{\ell}\nu({\rm d}y)
, so that

\partial_{\delta_{k}}m_{\ell}(\mu^{\boldsymbol{\delta}})\mid_{\delta_{k}=0}=\int\ell y^{\ell-1}\mu_{k}({\rm d}y)
.
Therefore at
\boldsymbol{\delta}=0
the Jacobian matrix
J=(\partial_{\delta_{k}}m_{\ell}(\mu^{\boldsymbol{\delta}}))_{k,\ell}
satisfies

\underset{p\times p}{{\rm det}}\ J=\underset{p\times p}{{\rm det}}\ \int\ell y^{\ell-1}\mu_{k}({\rm d}y)=\frac{1}{p!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\underset{p\times p}{{\rm det}}(\ell y_{i}^{\ell})\ \cdot\ \underset{p\times p}{{\rm det}}\mu_{k}({\rm d}y_{i})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq p}(y_{i}-y_{j})\cdot\ \underset{p\times p}{{\rm det}}\mu_{k}({\rm d}y_{i})\\
=p!\int_{y_{1}>\dots>y_{p}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq p}(y_{i}-y_{j})\cdot\ \underset{p\times p}{{\rm det}}\mu_{k}({\rm d}y_{i}),

where we have used Andréief’s identity (see e.g.
[
5
, Lemma 5.1]
) in the second equality, the Vandermonde determinant in the third one, and invariance of the product of determinants under permutation of the indices in the fourth one. As the supports of the
\mu_{k}
’s are disjoint in decreasing order, in the domain
y_{1}>\dots>y_{p}
we have
{{\rm det}}\mu_{k}({\rm d}y_{i})=\mu_{1}({\rm d}y_{1})\dots\mu_{p}({\rm d}y_{p})
, so we have proved

\underset{p\times p}{{\rm det}}\ J\geq p!(c-\widetilde{c})^{\frac{p(p-1)}{2}}\kappa^{p}.

(14)

In particular
\boldsymbol{\delta}\mapsto\boldsymbol{m}_{p}(\mu^{\boldsymbol{\delta}})
is locally invertible around

, so

\boldsymbol{m}
close enough to
\boldsymbol{m}_{p}(\mu)

\nu=\mu^{\boldsymbol{\delta}}
such that

. By construction

on

.

Regarding uniformity of
\delta

\det(JJ^{*})>c_{0}(c,\widetilde{c},\kappa)
from (

.

In the following lemma, we write
m_{k}(X)
for

when

is a real random variable with distribution

.

Lemma 2.4
.

Fix a
p\geq 2

and assume that the distribution of a probability measure
\mu
has
p
-support in the sense of Definition
1.2

with parameters
c,\widetilde{c},\kappa,A
. Assume
m_{1}(\mu)=0
,
m_{2}(\mu)=1
. Then there exists
t_{0}=t_{0}(c,\widetilde{c},\kappa,A,\boldsymbol{m}_{p}(\mu))>0
such that for any
0\leq t\leq t_{0}
,
there exists a probability measure
\nu

coinciding with
\mu
on
\{|x|>A+1\}
such that if
X,G
are independent,
X\sim\nu
,

is a standard Gaussian, then

(15)

has its first
p
moments match exactly those of
\mu
. That is, for
j\leq p
we have

m_{j}(X_{t})=m_{j}(\mu).

(16)

Proof.

By a change of variables, without loss of generality we can look for
X
such that

X_{t}=(1-t)^{1/2}X+t^{1/2}G

has its first
p
moments match exactly those of
\mu
.
Denote
\boldsymbol{m}_{p}=(m_{1}(X),\dots,m_{p}(X))
any sequence of moments ensuring this moment matching.
Then for the first two moments, we wish to satisfy

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=m_{1}(X_{t})=(1-t)^{1/2}m_{1}(X)

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=m_{2}(X_{t})=(1-t)m_{2}(X)+t.

So necessarily
m_{1}(X)=0,m_{2}(X)=1
. Now suppose there is a choice of
m_{j}(X)
that makes (
16
) hold for all
j\leq k-1

which additionally ensures

. Necessarily

\displaystyle m_{k}(\mu)=m_{k}(X_{t})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(1-t)^{\frac{k-j}{2}}t^{\frac{j}{2}}m_{k-j}(X)m_{j}(G)

(17)

where we set

m_{k}(X)=\frac{m_{k}(\mu)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(1-t)^{\frac{k-j}{2}}t^{\frac{j}{2}}m_{k-j}(X)m_{j}(G)}{(1-t)^{k/2}}.

(18)

Consider
\delta
from Lemma

2.1. Green function comparison via moment matching

We use a superscript to denote the underlying matrix distribution of the object in question. In what follows, we use
\textbf{v}/\sqrt{N},\textbf{w}/\sqrt{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{\frac{N(N+1)}{2}}
to denote the entrywise distributions of two generalized Wigner ensembles
H^{\textbf{v}}
and
H^{\textbf{w}}
, respectively, which we assume to be in the real symmetry class: The proof for the Hermitian symmetry class is the same up to notational changes.

In comparing the resolvents between the
v
and
w
ensembles, we proceed by a telescopic expansion driven by a Lindeberg exchange principle, as initiated for random matrix universality in
[
33
]
.
As is customary with this swapping method in random matrix theory, we fix a bijective ordering map of the index set of our matrix,
\Phi:\{(i,j):1\leq i\leq j\leq N\}\to\{1,\ldots,\gamma(N)\}
where
\gamma(N)=N(N+1)/2
. Denote by
H_{\gamma}
the generalized Wigner matrix whose entries
H_{ij}
follow the
\textbf{w}/\sqrt{N}
-distribution if
\Phi(i,j)\leq\gamma
and the
\textbf{v}/\sqrt{N}
-distribution otherwise. Then
H_{0}=H^{\textbf{v}}
and
H_{\gamma(N)}=H^{\textbf{w}}
. We will denote

\mathbb{P}_{\gamma},\mathbb{E}_{\gamma}
when integrating over the ensemble
H_{\gamma}
.

For the following notation, fix a
\gamma=\Phi(a,b)
, and let
D^{(ij)}
denote the matrix that is zero everywhere except at position
(i,j)
and
(j,i)
where it is 1. Then we have

H_{\gamma-1}=Q+\frac{v_{ab}}{\sqrt{N}}D^{(ab)},\ H_{\gamma}=Q+\frac{w_{ab}}{\sqrt{N}}D^{(ab)}.

(19)

To move between the Green function and the gap probabilities, we introduce some notation regarding the Green function approximation. Let
\rho>0
be fixed and
\rho N\leq k\leq(1-\rho)N
be a bulk index, and define the rigidity window

I_{k}:=\left[\gamma_{k}-N^{-1}\varphi^{C},\gamma_{k}+N^{-1}\varphi^{C}\right],

(20)

where the
k
-th typical location
\gamma_{k}
is defined implicity through
\int_{-\infty}^{\gamma_{k}}d\rho_{\text{sc}}=\frac{k}{N}
.
We now
fix

\varepsilon>0,\ \ \eta_{d}=N^{-3/2+\varepsilon},\ \ \eta_{0}=N^{-3/2+2\varepsilon},\ \ \Delta=N^{-3/2+3\varepsilon}.

For the proof of the theorem below, we also set

s(x)=\frac{1}{1+e^{x}}

and for any
E\in[-3,3]

f_{E}(x)=s\left(\frac{x-E}{\eta_{d}}\right).

(21)

Then
f_{E}(x)
is a good approximation of
\textbf{1}_{x\leq E}
on scale
\eta_{d}
, see Lemma
A.3
.
Note that
f_{E}
is an analytic function with simple poles at
z_{k}=E+{\rm i}\eta_{d}(2k+1)
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Moreover, we will use the abbreviation
\Tr_{E}=\Tr f_{E}(H)
.

Finally, let
q_{\alpha}
be a smooth, monotone cutoff function satisfying

q_{\alpha}(x)=\begin{cases}1&x\geq\alpha-1/3\\
0&x\leq\alpha-2/3\end{cases}.

(22)

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\varepsilon,\alpha>0
be fixed. Suppose that the
v
and
w
matrix entries have matching moments up to the
p
-th order, and that
w
is a Gaussian-divisible distribution as in (
15
) with
t\asymp 1
. Then there exists
C>0
such that uniformly in
\alpha N\leq k\leq(1-\alpha)N
and
0<u<N^{\varepsilon}
, we have

\left|(\mathbb{P}^{\textbf{v}}-\mathbb{P}^{\textbf{w}})(N(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k})\geq u)\right|\leq\varphi^{C}N^{2}\frac{N^{-(p+1)/2}}{(N\eta_{d})^{p+1}}+N^{1+6\varepsilon}\eta_{d},\ \ \eta_{d}=N^{-3/2+\varepsilon}.

(23)

Proof.

Fix
\alpha N\leq k\leq(1-\alpha)N
and let
\beta_{j}=\gamma_{k}+j\Delta
for
j_{-}\leq j\leq j_{+}
where
j_{\pm}=\pm\lfloor N^{1/2+\varepsilon}\rfloor
. We start by decomposing and applying (
55
): together with the rigidity estimate (
51
) we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\textbf{v}}(N(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k})\geq u)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j_{-}\leq j\leq j_{+}}\mathbb{P}^{\textbf{v}}\left(\lambda_{k}\in[\beta_{j},\beta_{j+1}),\lambda_{k+1}\in[\beta_{j}+\frac{u}{N},\infty)\right)+{\rm O}(N^{-D})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j_{-}\leq j\leq j_{+}}\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{v}}\left[\textbf{1}_{\lambda_{k}\in[\beta_{j},\beta_{j+1})}\left(1-q_{k+1}(\Tr_{\beta_{j}+\frac{u}{N}-\eta_{0}}\right)\right]+{\rm O}(N^{-D}).

Integrating the above sum by parts, we have

\mathbb{P}^{\textbf{v}}(N(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k})\geq u)\leq\sum_{j_{-}+1\leq j\leq j_{+}}\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{v}}\left[\textbf{1}_{\lambda_{k}\geq\beta_{j}}\left(q_{k+1}(\Tr_{\beta_{j-1}+\frac{u}{N}-\eta_{0}})-q_{k+1}(\Tr_{\beta_{j}+\frac{u}{N}-\eta_{0}})\right)\right]+{\rm O}(N^{-D})\\
+\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{v}}\left[\textbf{1}_{\lambda_{k}\geq\beta_{j_{-}}}\left(1-q_{k+1}(\Tr_{\beta_{j_{-}}+\frac{u}{N}-\eta_{0}})\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{v}}\left[\textbf{1}_{\lambda_{k}\geq\beta_{j_{+}}}\left(1-q_{k+1}(\Tr_{\beta_{j_{+}}+\frac{u}{N}-\eta_{0}})\right)\right]+{\rm O}(N^{-D}).

The boundary terms simplify by rigidity, and we apply (
55
) to see that (note that the difference in the
q_{k+1}
terms is negative)

\mathbb{P}^{\textbf{v}}(N(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k})\geq u)\\
\leq 1+\sum_{j_{-}+1\leq j\leq j_{+}}\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{v}}\left[\textbf{1}_{\lambda_{k}\geq\beta_{j}}\left(q_{k+1}(\Tr_{\beta_{j-1}+\frac{u}{N}-\eta_{0}})-q_{k+1}(\Tr_{\beta_{j}+\frac{u}{N}-\eta_{0}})\right)\right]+O(N^{-D})\\
\leq 1+\sum_{j_{-}+1\leq j\leq j_{+}}\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{v}}\left[\left(1-q_{k}(\Tr_{\beta_{j}+\eta_{0}})\right)\left(q_{k+1}(\Tr_{\beta_{j-1}+\frac{u}{N}-\eta_{0}})-q_{k+1}(\Tr_{\beta_{j}+\frac{u}{N}-\eta_{0}})\right)\right]+O(N^{-D}).

We now claim that due to our moment matching hypothesis, for any entry swap at site
\gamma
we have
almost distributional invariance in the following sense:

\mathbb{E}_{\gamma-1}\left[\left(1-q_{k}(\Tr_{\beta_{j}+\eta_{0}})\right)\left(q_{k+1}(\Tr_{\beta_{j-1}+\frac{u}{N}-\eta_{0}})-q_{k+1}(\Tr_{\beta_{j}+\frac{u}{N}-\eta_{0}})\right)\right]\\
=\mathbb{E}_{\gamma}\left[\left(1-q_{k}(\Tr_{\beta_{j}+\eta_{0}})\right)\left(q_{k+1}(\Tr_{\beta_{j-1}+\frac{u}{N}-\eta_{0}})-q_{k+1}(\Tr_{\beta_{j}+\frac{u}{N}-\eta_{0}})\right)\right]+{\rm O}\left(\varphi^{C}\frac{N^{-(p+1)/2}}{(N\eta_{d})^{p+1}}\right).

(24)

To prove the above equation, we consider (we abbreviate
q=q_{k}
,
D=D^{ab}
)

F(x)=q_{k}(\Tr_{E}(Q+xD))=q\circ h(x).

We will prove that uniformly in
a,b,k
and
E
in the bulk of the spectrum
we have

\mathbb{E}[F(\frac{v_{ab}}{\sqrt{N}})]=\mathbb{E}[F(\frac{w_{ab}}{\sqrt{N}})]+{\rm O}\left(\varphi^{C}\frac{N^{-(p+1)/2}}{(N\eta_{d})^{p+1}}\right).

(25)

The case of the product of two functions of type
q_{k}
can be proved similarly, concluding the proof of (
24
).

To prove (
25
), abbreviating
v=v_{ab},w=w_{ab}
, we have

\mathbb{E}F(\frac{v}{\sqrt{N}})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{p}\frac{1}{\ell!}\mathbb{E}[F^{(\ell)}(0)]\mathbb{E}[\frac{v^{k}}{N^{k/2}}]+{\rm O}_{p}\left(\frac{\mathbb{E}[\|F^{(p+1)}\|_{\infty}]}{N^{(p+1)/2}}\right),

(26)

where the notation for the norm used above and in the remainder of this paper is: for any function
g
we denote

\|g\|_{\infty}=\sup_{|x|<N^{-\frac{1}{2}+\kappa}}|g(x)|

with
0<\kappa<1/2
fixed, arbitrarily small (
\kappa=\varepsilon/100
for example works). Therefore, from the moment matching hypothesis, and the exponential decay assumption (
5
), the proof of
(
25
) will be complete if we have the key stability estimate

\|F^{(p+1)}\|_{\infty}\leq C_{p}\frac{\varphi^{C}}{(N\eta_{d})^{p+1}}

(27)

with overwhelming probability. As
q
is smooth on scale
1
, by the chain rule to prove (
27
) we only need to prove that for any
\varepsilon>0
and
\ell\leq p
, with overwhelming probability

\|h^{(\ell)}\|_{\infty}\leq C_{\ell}\frac{\varphi^{C}}{(N\eta_{d})^{\ell}},

(28)

First note that with overwhelming probability, for any

with
0<\eta\lesssim 1
we have

\left\|\partial_{x}^{\ell}{\rm Tr}\frac{1}{Q-z+xD}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{C_{\ell}}{\eta}\frac{\varphi^{C}}{(\min(1,N\eta))^{\ell}}.

(29)

We start the proof of (
29
) in the most singular case
0<\eta<1/N
.
From the resolvent expansion we have

\frac{1}{Q-z+(x+\delta)D}\\
=\frac{1}{Q-z+xD}+\left[\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}(-\frac{1}{Q-z+xD}\delta D)^{k}\frac{1}{Q-z+xD}\right]+(-\frac{1}{Q-z+xD}\delta D)^{\ell+1}\frac{1}{Q-z+(x+\delta)D}

(30)

so that

\partial_{x}^{\ell}{\rm Tr}\frac{1}{Q-z+xD}=\ell!\ {\rm Tr}\left(-\frac{1}{Q-z+xD}D\right)^{\ell}\frac{1}{Q-z+xD}.

The estimate (
29
) then follows from the above formula together with the local law (
A.1
) (see in particular (
50
)) and the Ward identity
\sum_{j}|G_{ij}|^{2}=\frac{1}{\eta}{{\rm Im}G_{ii}}
where we abbreviate
G=(Q-z+xD)^{-1}
:

\left|\partial_{x}^{\ell}{\rm Tr}\frac{1}{Q-z+xD}\right|\leq\ell!|{\rm Tr}(GD)^{\ell-1}G|\leq\sum_{1\leq i\leq N,j,k\in\{a,b\}}\ell!|{\rm Tr}(GD)^{\ell-1}G|\\
\leq\sum_{j,k\in\{a,b\}}|[D(GD)^{\ell-1}]_{jk}|\sum_{i=1}^{N}(|G_{ij}|^{2}+|G_{ki}|^{2})\leq\frac{1}{\eta}\sum_{j,k\in\{a,b\}}|[D(GD)^{\ell-1}]_{jk}|(|G_{jj}|+|G_{kk}|)\leq\frac{C_{\ell}}{\eta}\frac{\varphi^{C}}{(N\eta)^{\ell}}.

The proof of (
29

is the same, except that we use the bound
G_{ij}={\rm O}(\varphi^{C})
instead of
G_{ij}={\rm O}(\varphi^{C}/(N\eta))
as an input, for the resolvent entries in the above expansion.
Moreover, the above reasoning, with (
52
) and (
53
) as an input. also shows that for any
c>0
, uniformly in
{\rm dist}(z,[-2,2])>c

\left\|\partial_{x}^{\ell}{\rm Tr}\frac{1}{Q-z+xD}\right\|_{\infty}\leq C(\ell)\varphi^{C}.

(31)

Let
\mathcal{C}
is a circle with radius

such that
{\rm dist}(\mathcal{C},\{z_{k}\})<\eta_{d}/10
, ensuring that
f=f_{E}
from (

on
\mathcal{C}
. By the residue formula for any

f(\lambda)=\int_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{f(z)}{z-\lambda}\frac{{\rm d}z}{2{\rm i}\pi}-\sum_{z_{k}\in\mathcal{D}}\frac{{\rm Res}(f,z_{k})}{z_{k}-\lambda}=\int_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{f(z)}{z-\lambda}\frac{{\rm d}z}{2{\rm i}\pi}+\eta_{d}\sum_{z_{k}\in\mathcal{D}}\frac{1}{z_{k}-\lambda}.

The above formula and eigenvalue rigidity (see Theorem

|\partial_{x}^{\ell}{\rm Tr}_{E}(Q+xD)|\lesssim\sup_{\mathcal{C}}\left|\partial_{x}^{\ell}{\rm Tr}\frac{1}{Q-z+xD}\right|+\sum_{z_{k}\in{\mathcal{D}}}\left|\partial_{x}^{\ell}{\rm Tr}\frac{1}{Q-z_{k}+xD}\right|.

Together with (
29

, we obtain

|\partial_{x}^{\ell}{\rm Tr}_{E}(Q+xD)|\lesssim\sup_{\mathcal{C}}\left|\partial_{x}^{\ell}{\rm Tr}\frac{1}{Q-z+xD}\right|+\eta_{d}\sum_{z_{k}\in{\mathcal{D}}}\frac{\varphi^{C}}{(2k+1)\eta_{d}(N(2k+1)\eta_{d})^{\ell}}+\eta_{d}\sum_{z_{k}\in{\mathcal{D}}}\frac{\varphi^{C}}{(2k+1)\eta_{d}}\\
\lesssim C_{\ell}\frac{\varphi^{C}}{(N\eta_{d})^{\ell}},

concluding the proof of (
28
).

With (
24
) proved, we now swap the expectation in

to an expectation in
w

\varphi^{C}N^{2}\frac{N^{-(p+1)/2}}{(N\eta_{d})^{p+1}}

55

\leq 1+\sum_{j_{-}+1\leq j\leq j_{+}}\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{w}}\left[\left(1-q_{k}(\Tr_{\beta_{j}+\eta_{0}})\right)\left(q_{k+1}(\Tr_{\beta_{j-1}+\frac{u}{N}-\eta_{0}})-q_{k+1}(\Tr_{\beta_{j}+\frac{u}{N}-\eta_{0}})\right)\right]\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+N^{2}\frac{N^{-(p+1)/2}}{(N\eta_{d})^{p+1}}+O(N^{-D})\\

\sum_{j_{-}\leq j\leq j_{+}}\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{w}}\left[\textbf{1}_{\lambda_{k}\in[\beta_{j},\beta_{j+1})}\textbf{1}_{\lambda_{k+1}\geq\beta_{j}+\frac{u}{N}-2\eta_{0}}\right]\\

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be given and
r>0
be an arbitrary integer, independent of
N
. There exists a
\delta>0
sufficiently small and
N_{0}
such that for
N>N_{0}
we have

\mathbb{E}^{{\rm GUE}}\left[\left(\sum_{\gamma N\leq\alpha\leq(1-\gamma)N}\textbf{1}_{\lambda_{\alpha+1}-\lambda_{\alpha}\leq N^{-\frac{4}{3}+\delta}}\right)^{r}\right]<N^{\varepsilon}.

(44)

Proof.

We proceed as follows., after expansion of the product. Via the determinantal representation of the spectrum of GUE and Hadamard’s inequality, we can essentially treat products of indicators with no shared indices as independent. So the main difficulty will be to show that the total contribution of those terms with overlapping indices is asymptotically small.

First, from Lemma
A.4
we have

\int\int\textbf{1}_{|x_{\alpha}-x_{\beta}|\leq N^{-\frac{4}{3}+\delta}}d\rho_{2}^{\text{GUE}(N)}(x_{\alpha},x_{\beta})\asymp\int_{0\leq u\leq N^{-\frac{4}{3}+\delta}}\left[N^{4}u^{2}+O(N)\right]du\asymp N^{3\delta}+N^{-\frac{1}{3}+\delta}.

For the terms with shared indices, we can afford a cruder estimate, starting with

\int\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\textbf{1}_{|x_{i}-x_{i+1}|\leq N^{-\frac{4}{3}+\delta}}d\rho_{k+1}^{\text{GUE}(N)}(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\\
\leq\int_{-2+\varepsilon_{0}}^{2-\varepsilon_{0}}dx_{0}\int\prod_{i=1}^{k}\textbf{1}_{|x_{0}-x_{i}|\leq kN^{-\frac{4}{3}+\delta}}d\rho_{k+1}^{\text{GUE}(N)}(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}).

(45)

Let
K(x,y)
be the determinantal kernel for the GUE point process. Then, due to the multilinearity of the determinant, we can write

\rho_{k+1}^{\text{GUE}(N)}(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=\det_{(k+1)\times(k+1)}[K(x_{i},x_{j})]_{1\leq i,j\leq k}\\
=\begin{vmatrix}K(x_{0},x_{0})&K(x_{0},x_{1})-K(x_{0},x_{0})&\cdots&K(x_{0},x_{k})-K(x_{0},x_{0})\\
K(x_{1},x_{0})&K(x_{1},x_{1})-K(x_{1},x_{0})&\cdots&K(x_{1},x_{k})-K(x_{1},x_{0})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
K(x_{k},x_{0})&K(x_{k},x_{1})-K(x_{k},x_{0})&\cdots&K(x_{k},x_{k})-K(x_{k},x_{0})\end{vmatrix}.

From
[
1
]
*Lemma 2.8 we know that the first column is
O(N)
and the remaining
k
columns are of order
O(N^{2}\cdot kN^{-4/3+\delta})
. Moreover, as the integration domain is
O((kN^{-4/3+\delta})^{k})
, so that (
45
) is of order
O(k^{2k}N^{k(-2/3+2\delta)+1})
.

Now, we handle the combinatorics of the product expansion in (
44
) in the following way. Because each indicator is idempotent, we will split into cases the number
m
of indicators represented in a term of the expansion. There are
\binom{r}{m}
ways to choose the locations of the represented indicators, and at most
m^{r-m}
ways to select which indicators the remaining
r-m
are to agree with. Now, among the terms of
m
different indicators, there are
\mathcal{P}(m)
, the number of partitions of
m
, different types of terms to consider. In particular, let
(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{\ell})
be a partition of
m
. Then, collectively, the terms of this structure are bounded via Hadamard’s inequality and (
45
) by (below we relabel the points
x_{i}
as
x_{j,i}
to emphasize the combinatorial structure)

\int\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\prod_{i=1}^{p_{j}}\textbf{1}_{|x_{j,i}-x_{j,i+1}|\leq N^{-\frac{4}{3}+\delta}}d\rho^{\text{GUE}(N)}_{m+\ell}(\{x_{j,i}\})\leq\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\left[\int\prod_{i=1}^{p_{j}}\textbf{1}_{|x_{j,i}-x_{j,i+1}|\leq N^{-\frac{4}{3}+\delta}}d\rho_{p_{j}+1}^{\text{GUE}(N)}(\{x_{j,i}\}_{i=1}^{p_{j}+1})\right]\\
\leq\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\left[O\left(p_{j}^{2p_{j}}N^{p_{j}(-2/3+2\delta)+1}\right)\textbf{1}_{p_{j}\geq 2}+O\left(N^{3\delta})\right)\textbf{1}_{p_{j}=1}\right]\leq O\left(m^{2m}N^{m(-2/3+2\delta)+m/2}+N^{m3\delta}\right).

Altogether we bound the left hand side of (
44
) by

\mathbb{E}^{\text{GUE}}\left[\left(\sum_{\gamma N\leq\alpha\leq(1-\gamma)N}\textbf{1}_{\lambda_{\alpha+1}-\lambda_{\alpha}\leq N^{-\frac{4}{3}+\delta}}\right)^{r}\right]\\
\leq\sum_{m=1}^{r}\binom{r}{m}m^{r-m}\mathcal{P}(m)O\left(m^{2m}N^{m(-2/3+2\delta)+m/2}+N^{3m\delta}\right)\leq C\cdot N^{3r\delta},

where
C=C(r)
is a constant depending only on
r
, which is independent of
N
. Therefore, picking
\delta<\varepsilon/(3r)
yields (
44
).

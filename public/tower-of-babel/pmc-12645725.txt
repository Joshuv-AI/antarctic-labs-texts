Dickkopf-3 enhances the chondroprotective effect of chondrogenic progenitor cells in a rat model of posttraumatic osteoarthritis
Authors: Yu-Xing Wang, Jian-hui Xu, Zhi-dong Zhao, Ya Huang, Xu shi, Heng Zhu, Zhong-Li Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOur previous work suggested biological potential alterations in chondrogenic progenitor cells (CPCs) during knee osteoarthritic progression. The Wnt pathway plays a pivotal role in osteoarthritis progression, and Dickkopf-3 (DKK3) is a noncanonical Wnt antagonist. However, it remains unclear whether the DKK3 expression level is involved in the changes of the biological characteristics of CPCs during OA progression. This study aimed to explore the difference of DKK3 expression in osteochondral specimens and corresponding CPCs during osteoarthritis progression. Furthermore, the influence of DKK3 on the in vitro characteristics and in vivo therapeutic efficacy of CPCs was investigated.MethodsThe expression levels of DKK3 in human paired grade 1–2 and grade 3–4 osteoarthritic cartilage and corresponding CPCs were determined via immunohistochemical staining and western blotting, respectively. CPCs were isolated, cultured and characterized for their self-renewal and multidifferentiation abilities. Then, the CPCs were transfected with Lentiviral-DKK3 or siRNA to overexpress or knock down DKK3, respectively. The chondrogenic potential of the CPCs was examined via pellet staining and gene detection. The expression levels of inflammatory factors in different groups of CPCs were examined via qRT‒PCR and ELISA. The activation status of β-catenin in Wnt signalling was investigated via confocal microscopy. The in vivo chondroprotective effects of LV-DKK3 and Si-DKK3–transgenic CPCs were examined in a rat model of posttraumatic osteoarthritis.ResultsThe DKK3 expression level was significantly increased in Grade 3–4 cartilage compared with Grade 1–2 cartilage. However, the DKK3 expression level was significantly lower in Grade 3–4 CPCs than in Grade 1–2 CPCs. The overexpression of DKK3 significantly increased CPC chondrogenic potential and inhibited the expression of catabolic factors, whereas the knockdown of DKK3 had the opposite effects, decreasing CPC chondrogenic potential and increasing the expression of catabolic factors. Wnt signalling was significantly inhibited by DKK3. The in vivo results suggested that lentivirus-induced LV-dkk3 significantly delayed the progression of experimental OA, whereas lentivirus-mediated Si-DKK3, which aimed to silence endogenous DKK3, accelerated the progression of experimental OA.ConclusionOur results suggest that DKK3 has a chondroprotective effect during osteoarthritis progression and that CPCs may be functional targets. The underlying mechanisms of DKK3 may include enhancing CPC chondrogenic capacity and inhibiting Wnt-mediated expression of catabolic factors and matrix remodelling activities. These findings indicate that DKK3 is a potential therapeutic target for CPC-mediated OA treatment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13018-025-06470-z.Keywords: Wnt/β-catenin signalling, Osteoarthritis, Cartilage, DKK3, CPCs

Background

Osteoarthritis (OA) is a highly prevalent and extremely disabling disease that occurs with increasing age. Articular cartilage degeneration and associated inflammation are prominent characteristics that cause knee joint pain, swelling, stiffness, and functional limitations [1]. However, the underlying molecular mechanisms involved in the pathogenesis and progression of OA are still unclear [2]; hence, there is no available disease-modifying therapy except for symptomatic management until the ultimate knee arthroplasty. Therefore, the exploration of novel therapeutic methods is urgently needed.

Wnt3a/β-catenin signalling has been shown to participate in OA pathological progression and may play a vital role in joint tissues through the production of matrix metallopeptidases in chondrocytes, synoviocytes, and osteoblasts [3–5]. In the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signalling pathway, the binding of Wnt proteins to cell surface receptors leads to stabilized β-catenin, which is translocated to the nucleus to activate Wnt target genes, such as the upregulation of matrix metallopeptidase 13 (MMP-13). Therefore, modulating this pathway is an attractive therapeutic approach for delaying OA progression.

DKK3, a Wnt3a antagonist, is a structurally and functionally significant member of the Dickkopf family [6]. Our previous study found that the expression of DKK3 protein in OA patients’ joint synovial fluid was significantly increased and correlated with the severity of OA when comparing the proteomics of normal and OA joint synovial fluid [7]. Snelling et al. [8] also suggested that the protein expression level of DKK3 significantly increased in human osteoarthritic cartilage. The addition of DKK3 inhibited the loss of proteoglycans and collagen in bovine cartilage explants mediated by IL-1β [8]. These findings suggest that DKK3 is a potential therapeutic target for treating OA. However, the potential downstream cellular targets of DKK3 and its associated biological effects, along with the underlying mechanisms, need further elucidation.

Recently, an increasing number of studies have shown that tissue-specific chondrogenic progenitor cells (CPCs) exist in both normal and osteoarthritic cartilage. They have the characteristics of stem cells, including self-renewal, proliferation and multidifferentiation capacity [9–11]. Meanwhile, resident CPCs do not attempt to differentiate to synthesize cartilage-specific extracellular matrix (ECM) components in the process of maintaining and restoring healthy cartilage homeostasis, which may also induce various catabolic and anabolic factors that instead may contribute to accelerated cartilage degradation [12–14]. Our previous studies [15, 16] suggested that CPC biological characteristics change with OA progression, which indicates that CPCs may be potential therapeutic targets for OA treatment. Previous studies have tested and demonstrated the therapeutic efficacy of CPCs in repairing cartilage defects [16]. However, the therapeutic efficacy of CPCs in treating osteoarthritis and its critical influencing factors (such as DKK3) remain unclear [10, 16–18].

On the basis of research progress and our previous work, we explored the difference in DKK3 expression between osteochondral specimens and corresponding CPCs from paired grade 1–2 and grade 3–4 osteoarthritic cartilage. Furthermore, the influence of DKK3 on the in vitro characteristics and in vivo therapeutic efficacy of CPCs was investigated. We hypothesized that DKK3 has an enhanced chondroprotective effect in CPCs treated posttraumatic OA model.

Methods

Patient characteristics and human OA cartilage Preparation

This study was approved by the institutional ethical review board of the General Hospital of the Chinese People’s Liberation Army, Beijing, China. (Rapid review of scientific research projects for use of discarded biological material), and informed consent was obtained from all donors. Twenty patients (7 male and 13 female, mean age, 60.5 years [range, 51–70 years]; mean body mass index, 26.5 kg/m2 [range, 23.7–31.5 kg/m2]) who were diagnosed with late-stage idiopathic knee OA according to the criteria of the American College of Rheumatology [19] with varus malalignment of the lower extremity and scheduled for elective total knee replacement (TKR) were recruited. All patients recruited were classified as Kellgren-Lawrence grade 3–4 in the medial tibiofemoral compartment. All the patients presented with Outerbridge grade 3–4 cartilage lesions in the medial compartment accompanied by grade 1–2 cartilage lesions on the lateral side. The grade 1–2 cartilage includes cartilages with an intact surface (grade 1) and minimal fibrillation (grade 2), and the grade 3–4 cartilage includes cartilage with fissures to subchondral bone [20] (Fig. 1a, b). There were 20 patients included in our study. Ten patient samples were used for histopathology experiments, and 10 other patient samples were used for cell isolation.

Histologic and immunohistochemical analysis of DKK3

Separate lateral and medial tibial plateau, degenerated meniscus and inflammatory synovial samples were collected for histological and immunohistochemical analysis during arthroplasty. separate specimens were fixed in paraformaldehyde, decalcified, embedded in paraffin, sectioned (5 μm), and processed for hematoxylin‒eosin (H&E) and immunostaining of DKK3. Digital image analysis was performed to evaluate relative cartilage damage and DKK3 (1:200; ab187532, Abcam, Cambridge, UK)cell distribution in vivo.

Isolation, expansion and identification of CPCs

The methods used to harvest the CPCs have been described previously [16, 21]. Briefly, the cartilaginous tissues were separated from the lateral tibial plateau (Outerbridge grade 3–4) without contaminating subchondral bones, minced into pieces (approximately 1.0 mm×1.0 mm×1.0 mm), and then digested in 0.1% collagenase II (Sigma) for 2 h. The released cells were discarded, and the digested cartilage chips were incubated in alpha-minimal essential medium (α-MEM) supplemented with 10% vol/vol fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Invitrogen Life Technologies) at 37 °C in an atmosphere of 5% CO2. The migratory CPCs outgrew from the cartilage chips within 10 days, and the adhesive cells rapidly reached 60–80% confluence within another 5 days. Importantly, the cartilage chips were retained and maintained until passage 3 to mimic the cellular niche ex vivo and allow more CPCs outgrowth. The morphological characteristics of the CPCs were observed via inverted light microscopy. The cells were used between passages 4 and 5.

DKK3 overexpression and knockdown

DKK3 (GENE_ID: 27122, GenBank: NM_001018057) overexpression and interference vector construction, plasmid expression detection and virus packaging in combination with the expression of GFP were performed at Shanghai Jikai Gene Co., Ltd. DKK3-overexpressing lentiviruses or knockdown siRNAs were transfected into CPCs with polybrene (Sigma) for several days or passages to obtain favourable effects. The optimal conditions were first determined via multiplicity of infection (MOI) (LV-ctr MOI = 40, LV-DKK3 MOI = 50, Si-ctr MOI = 30, Si-DKK3-1 MOI = 40, Si-DKK3-2 MOI = 40, Si-DKK3-3 MOI = 40). When the optimized conditions for transfection were used, the transfection efficiency reached approximately 75%. Fluorescence-activated cell sorting enriched the GFP-positive CPCs to greater than 99%.

Protein extraction and western blot analysis

We used the Western blot method to compare DKK3 expression in CPCs derived from Outerbridge 1–2 and Grade 3–4 cartilage (n = 6 patient paired cell samples, each with two replicates). We also detected changes in DKK3 expression in CPCs derived from Grade 1–2 cartilage after stimulation with inflammatory factors (10 ng/ml human recombinant IL-1β for 24 h and 48 h; n = 6 cell samples, with each repeated twice). DKK3 lentivirus infection efficiency was also confirmed via Western blot analysis (1:1000; ab187532, Abcam, Cambridge, UK).

CCK-8 assay

To investigate the effect of DKK3 on cell proliferation, we used the Cell Counting Kit 8 (CCK-8, Dojindo, Japan) assay according to a previous study [22]. In brief, CPCs cultured with various concentrations of DKK3 or CPCs transfected with Lenti-virus at passage 4 were seeded in 96-well plates (1 × 103/well, 5 wells in each group) and maintained, and the CCK-8 solution was added at a ratio of 100 µl/ml and incubated at 37 °C for 1 h. The optical density (OD) was measured at a wavelength of 450 nm on days 1, 4, 7, 10, 13, 16 and 19. This analysis was repeated in triplicate.

Chondrogenic differentiation

For chondrogenic differentiation, 4 × 105 CPCs were centrifuged in 15 ml polypropylene tubes to form a pelleted micromass and maintained in chondrogenic induction medium consisting of α-MEM supplemented with 107M dexamethasone, 1% insulin-transferrin-sodium selenite, 50 µM ascorbate-2 phosphate, 1 mM sodium pyruvate, 50 µg/ml proline, and 20 ng/ml transforming growth factor (TGF-β3). The cells were incubated for 28 days, the media was changed every 3 days, and the pellets were fixed and sectioned. The development of chondrocytes and accumulation of the cartilage matrix were evaluated by hematoxylin‒eosin, toluidine blue and Safranin O staining. Semiquantitative scoring was performed via histological observation on the basis of the literature [23]. The expression of Sox-9 (SRY type high-mobility group box-9,1:200; ab185966, Abcam, Cambridge, UK)) and Col-II (collagen type II, 1:100; ab34712, Abcam, Cambridge, UK) was detected via immunohistochemical assays. The cells were imaged via a microscope under brightfield mode.

Confocal microscopy

To study the role of DKK3 in regulating the Wnt pathway, we observed β-catenin, a key protein in the Wnt pathway, from the cytoplasm to the nucleus via confocal microscopy. Previous preliminary experiments revealed that the β-catenin fluorescent signal was weak. We increased the Wnt3a positive control group. The cells were transferred to 24-well plates and cultured for 16 h, after which they were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 10 min. The cells were subsequently incubated with mouse monoclonal antibodies (β-catenin, 1:500, ab32572, Abcam, Cambridge, UK), followed by fluorescent Cy5-conjugated secondary rabbit anti-mouse IgG (Life Technologies). The cell nuclei were counterstained with 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI), and fluorescently labelled cells were visualized and photographed via a laser scanning confocal microscope (MRC1024; Bio-Rad, Richmond, CA). β-catenin fluorescence signals intensity was semi-quantified using the software ImageJ (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA) on the basis of the literature [24]. Original images in RGB format were converted into a 16-bit (gray scale) format. Thereafter, the tagged areas were expressed as an average value of pixel intensity. All data were reported as fold change relative to control groups(Wnt3a-/LV-ctr and Wnt3a-/Si-ctr).

Real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT‒qPCR) analysis

RT‒qPCR was performed to further evaluate multilineage differentiation and inflammatory cytokine expression. After being maintained in chondrogenic differentiation media at a density of 5 × 104 cells/well in 6-well cell culture plates for 10 days, total RNA was extracted via TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA) and reverse transcribed via an mRNA Selective PCR Kit (TaKaRa) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Different inflammatory cytokine cDNA were amplified via real-time quantitative PCR via a SYBR PCR Master Mix Kit (Sigma‒Aldrich). The primer sequences are shown in the Supplementary table. The data were normalized to the value of β-actin. The mean fold changes were calculated.

Surgery-induced posttraumatic OA model

All animal studies were performed in accordance with approved Animal Committee protocols. Thirty-six male SD rats at 10 weeks postnatal age were divided into the following six groups: sham, OA model, OA + LV-ctr CPC, OA + LV-DKK3 CPC, OA + Si-ctr CPC, and OA + Si-DKK3 CPC. The OA model was subjected to anterior cruciate ligament transection and partial medial meniscectomy (ACLT + pMMx) [25–28] after capsulotomy, whereas the sham group was subjected to only capsulotomy. One week postsurgery, all OA rats were randomized and given intra-articular-transfected CPCs (3*106 cells in 100 µl) or vehicle. Eight weeks postsurgery, the knee joints were isolated, fixed in 10% formalin, decalcified and embedded in paraffin blocks. Frontal section (5 μm thick, three sections to ensure redundancy) from different levels, each 100 mm apart, were obtained and stained with hematoxylin and eosin and toluidine blue. Articular cartilage degradation on the sides of the medial plateau was quantified via the modified Osteoarthritis Research Society International (OARSI) score as previously described [29, 30]. All the slides were analysed by 2 blinded observers.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed via Prism 7 (GraphPad, Inc., La Jolla, CA, USA). All the numerical data are presented as the means ± standard deviations (SDs). We first confirmed their normal distributions via the Shapiro‒Wilk normality test. Paired or Student’s t test was used for two-group comparisons, and one-way or two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used for more than two groups, hoc test (Tukey’s test) was used for further comparison between groups after ANOVA analysis. For the data, a normal distribution was not assumed, or the data quantified on the basis of ordinal grading systems such as the pellets and OARSI scores were evaluated via nonparametric statistical analysis. p < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

Results

DKK3 expression was increased in grade 3–4 OA superficial cartilage

We collected OA articular cartilage samples from patients during TKR surgery. We examined the expression pattern of DKK3 in human knee articular cartilage sections via immunohistochemistry. The results revealed that DKK3 was expressed mainly in superficial cartilage and subchondral bone (Fig. 1c). DKK3 staining was mildly positive in the superficial zone of early OA articular cartilage; as OA progressed, the cells were strongly positive for DKK3 (Fig. 1d). The number of DKK3 immunohistochemically positive cells in Grade 1–2 superficial cartilage was 31.67 ± 5.241, which was significantly lower than the number of DKK3 immunohistochemically positive cells in Grade 3–4 superficial cartilage (90 ± 4.147) (P < 0.001). As an exocrine protein, DKK3 is also significantly expressed in the extracellular matrix. In the calcified cartilage, the numbers of 1–2 and 3–4 recombinant DKK3 immunohistochemically positive cells were 67.17 ± 7.195 and 69.67 ± 6.772, respectively, and there was no significant difference (P = 0.346) (Fig. 1d). Moreover, immunohistochemical examination was performed on the OA inflammatory synovium and degenerated meniscus tissue that were removed during the TKR operation. The results clearly revealed that DKK3 was expressed in the inflammatory synovium and degenerated meniscus in advanced osteoarthritic joints (Supplementary Fig. 1a, b).

The expression of DKK3 in CPCs decreased after inflammatory factor treatment

The DKK3 expression in Grade 3–4 cartilage-derived CPCs was significantly lower than that in Grade 1–2 cartilage-derived CPCs, as confirmed by Western blot analysis (0.254 vs. 0.481, P = 0.002) (Fig. 2a, b). Similarly, the expression of DKK3 decreased significantly after IL-β intervention in CPCs derived from Grade 1–2 cartilage for 24 h and 48 h (control vs. IL-β 24 h, 0.402 vs. 0.284, P = 0.014; control vs. IL-β 48 h, 0.402 vs. 0.202, P = 0.001) (Fig. 2c, d).

Stem-cell-like characteristics of CPCs

To confirm the stem-cell-like characteristics of CPCs, the differentiation multipotency, and presence of mesenchymal stem cell (MSC)-associated surface markers of the cells were analysed. These cells exhibited positively responded to osteogenic, adipogenic, and chondrogenic induction (Supplementary Fig. 2a, b, c). Flow cytometry revealed that these cells were positive for the MSC surface markers CD90, CD73, CD105, CD166, CD44, and CD29 and negative for the hematopoietic stem cell markers CD31 and CD45 (Supplementary Fig. 2d), indicating a similar epitope profile between these cells and adult MSCs.

Efficiency of DKK3 infection in CPCs

To better understand the mechanistic role of DKK3 in the pathogenesis of OA, OA human CPCs were transfected with LV-control, LV-DKK3, or Si-control, Si-DKK3. The transfection efficiency was greater than 95%, as determined by the number of GFP oligonucleotides (Supplementary Fig. 3a), which was further confirmed by western blotting (Supplementary Fig. 3b, c). To ensure the interference effect, we adopted three guarantees: Si-DKK3-1, Si-DKK3-2, or Si-DKK3-3 effectively inhibited the expression of DKK3, of which the interference effect of Si-DKK3-2 was the best (Supplementary Fig. 3b, c). Therefore, we chose Si-DKK3-2 for subsequent experimental studies. Because the expression of DKK3 between the LV-ctr and Si-ctr groups was inconsistent (Supplementary Fig. 3b, c), so, the subsequent overexpression and interference-related experiments were described separately.

DKK3 has no effect on CPC proliferation

CCK-8 was used to detect the effect of DKK3 on the proliferation of CPCs. The proliferation rates of cells treated with DKK3 at concentrations of 0 ng/ml, 10 ng/ml, 50 ng/ml, 100 ng/ml, 250 ng/ml, and 500 ng/ml at various time points over 18 days were not significantly different (Fig. 3a). Similarly, the proliferation rates of both the DKK3-overexpressing and interference groups were not significantly different from that of the control group (Fig. 3b, c).

DKK3 promotes CPC chondrogenic differentiation in vitro

Induction without TGFb3 was used as a negative control. DKK3 promoted CPC chondrogenic differentiation in vitro, as evidenced by larger pellets with increased Safranin O and toluidine blue staining in the LV-DKK3 group than in the LV-control group (Fig. 4a). Additionally, the LV-DKK3 CPC group demonstrated significantly greater induction of Col-II and Sox9 expression by immunocytochemistry (Fig. 4a) and a higher histological score (Fig. 4b) than the LV-ctr group did, and qRT‒PCR analysis of pellet cartilage globules under induction conditions further confirmed the increased expression of chondrocyte-associated genes, including SOX9 and COL-II (Fig. 4c).

Downregulation of DKK3 can inhibit CPC chondrogenic differentiation in vitro

The downregulation of DKK3 inhibited CPC chondrogenic differentiation in vitro, as evidenced by smaller pellets with decreased Safranin O and toluidine blue staining in the Si-DKK3 group than in the Si-control group (Fig. 5a). Additionally, the Si-DKK3 CPC group demonstrated significantly decreased induction of Col-II and Sox9 expression by immunocytochemistry (Fig. 5a) and a lower histological score (Fig. 5b) than the Si-ctr group did. qRT‒PCR analysis further confirmed decreased expression of chondrocyte-associated genes, including SOX9 and COL-II (Fig. 5c).

DKK3 exerts its functions by modulating key components of the Wnt3a/β-catenin signalling pathway in vitro

To explore whether DKK3 functions through the Wnt3a/β-catenin pathway, which contributes to OA development and progression, we examined the transcription of β-catenin, a key protein in the Wnt pathway, from the cytoplasm to the nucleus via confocal microscopy. Thus, the transfected CPCs were incubated for 24 h with or without Wnt3a to simulate the Wnt3a/β-catenin signalling pathway. Semi-quantitative immunofluorescence result showed that, compared with those in the control group, the protein expression levels of β-catenin were decreased in the LV-DKK3 group (0.68-fold, p < 0.05) (Fig. 6), whereas the protein levels of β-catenin were increased in the Si-DKK3 group (1.34-fold, p < 0.05) (Fig. 7). These findings support our conclusion that DKK3 can inhibit OA catabolism by regulating the Wnt3a/β-catenin signalling pathway.

DKK3 protected CPCs from catabolic breakdown in vitro

In addition to the regeneration of cartilage, the inhibition of the catabolic breakdown of newly formed and existing cartilage is important. Therefore, via gain-of-function and loss-of-function studies, we analysed the effects of DKK3 expression on catabolic breakdown biomarkers, including ADAMTS-5, IL-1β, MMP-3, MMP13 and TNF-ɑ in CPCs. The results of the qRT‒PCR assay revealed that the expression of ADAMTS-5, IL-1β, MMP-3, MMP13 and TNF-ɑ was lower in the CPCs transfected with DKK3 than in the control CPCs (Fig. 8a). The ELISA results revealed that the expression of TNF-ɑ and IL-1β was lower in the CPCs transfected with DKK3 than in the control CPCs (Fig. 8b), whereas the inhibition of DKK3 increased ADAMTS-5, IL-1β, MMP-3, and MMP13 expression at the mRNA level (Fig. 8c) and TNF-ɑ and IL-1β expression at the protein level (Fig. 8d).

Intra-articular injection of LV-DKK3-transfected CPCs decreases OA development in a rat model of knee OA

The efficacy of DKK3 was evaluated in the ACLT + pMMx rat knee OA model following a single intra-articular injection of LV-DKK3-transfected CPCs or the control. The results of HE, toluidine blue and COL-II immunohistochemical staining are shown (Fig. 9a) for each group. The sham group presented normal cartilage structures. In the OA group, the surface layer consisted of damaged cartilage, with reduced and irregular chondrocyte aggregation, and these degenerative changes extended to deeper regions. In contrast, these results were not observed in the DKK3-treated group; cartilage destruction was significantly attenuated, and the structure of the cartilage surface layer was restored. These findings indicate that LV-DKK3-transfected CPCs can protect articular cartilage from OA damage in an OA model. Furthermore, blinded OARSI histopathology analysis revealed significantly lower scores in the treatment group (Fig. 9b) than in the control group. Compared with those of the control, the results of the qRT‒PCR assay revealed that the level of the proinflammatory factor MMP13 was significantly lower and that the level of the ECM factor Col-II was significantly greater (Fig. 9c). These findings also indicate that LV-DKK3-transfected CPCs can protect against articular cartilage damage.

Intra-articular injection of Si-DKK3-transfected CPCs accelerated OA development in a rat model of knee OA

We next examined the effects of intra-articular injection of Si-DKK3-transfected CPCs on OA development. Interestingly, OA progression was significantly accelerated. HE staining revealed that the cartilage was more eroded in ACLT + PMMX mice after intra-articular injection of Si-DKK3 than it did in the Si-ctr group (Fig. 10a). toluidine blue and COL-II immunohistochemical staining revealed accelerated destruction of cartilage and loss of proteoglycans; accordingly, OARSI scores were significantly greater (Fig. 10b) in the Si-DKK3-treated mice than in the control mice. In addition, the Col-II expression level was decreased in the OA + Si-DKK3 CPC group, whereas the MMP13 level was increased in the + Si-DKK3 CPC group (Fig. 10c). OA progression was significantly accelerated by the knockdown of DKK3 expression in CPCs and was accompanied by severe cartilage damage and cartilage matrix degeneration.

Discussion

In this study, we investigated the change in DKK3 expression during knee osteoarthritis progression. The immunochemical results suggested that the expression level of DKK3 was significantly greater in Grade 3–4 cartilage than in Grade 1–2 cartilage. However, the expression level of DKK3 in Grade 3–4 CPCs was significantly lower than that in Grade 1–2 CPCs. Hence, to elucidate the biological effects of DKK3, lentivirus and siRNA were used to overexpress and knock down DKK3 expression, respectively. In vitro studies suggested that DKK3 overexpression significantly increased CPC chondrogenic capacity and decreased the secretion of cartilage extracellular proteases. Similarly, these biological effects were opposite to those observed in the DKK3 knockdown group. The in vivo results also suggested that DKK3 overexpression significantly delayed cartilage degradation and increased the levels of inflammatory factors and that knocking down DKK3 accelerated cartilage degradation. These comprehensive results suggest that DKK3 has a chondroprotective effect during osteoarthritis progression and that CPCs may be functional targets. The underlying mechanisms of DKK3 may include enhancing CPC chondrogenic capacity and inhibiting Wnt-mediated expression of catabolic factors and matrix remodelling activities. These findings indicate that DKK3 is a potential therapeutic target for CPC-mediated OA treatment.

Wnt signalling has been shown to play a pivotal role in OA progression by modulating bone and chondrocyte lineage specification, protease production and joint homeostasis [4, 31]. Both our previous study and Snelling et al. reported that DKK3 secretion was increased in OA synovial fluid [7, 8], and our present study further revealed that the expression of DKK3 in the surface layer of Grade 3–4 OA cartilage was significantly greater than that in Grade 1–2 OA cartilage. These results further suggest that DKK3 is closely correlated with OA progression. The upregulation of DKK3 may be part of a repair mechanism in response to matrix damage. A previous study reported that DKK3 expression is strongest in ligaments, tendons, articular cartilage, menisci, the superficial zone and subchondral bone of joint cartilage, and mechanical loading also increased the expression of DKK3 [32]. This finding suggests that DKK3 is relevant to whole joint biology. Overall, DKK3 upregulation in disease may be a defence mechanism to counteract disease-related dysregulation of cell signalling pathways.

DKK3, an inhibitor of Wnt signalling, plays an important role in the development and proliferation of cancer [33, 34]. Unsurprisingly, DKK3, a tumor inhibitor, does not promote the proliferation of CPCs. However, only a few in vitro studies [8, 35] have suggested that DKK3 has a protective effect on OA. DKK3 inhibited the Wnt3a-induced reduction in proteoglycan production in human chondrocytes grown in micromass culture [8]. DKK3 may play a protective role in OA by attenuating NF-κB activation and the subsequent production of metalloproteases [35], both of which have been studied in vitro. Our data also revealed that the upregulation of DKK3 can promote CPC chondrogenic differentiation and that the downregulation of DKK3 can inhibit CPC chondrogenic differentiation. DKK3 can inhibit the aggregation of cytoplasmic β-catenin in CPCs, prevent β-catenin from entering the nucleus, and inhibit the expression of Wnt-mediated downstream inflammatory factors. Conversely, Si-DKK3 can promote β-catenin aggregation, thereby promoting Wnt signalling and increasing the expression of inflammatory factors. Moreover, our further experimental study on posttraumatic OA in rats revealed that intra-articular injection of DKK3-overexpressing CPCs significantly inhibited OA cartilage degeneration and extracellular matrix catabolism. In experimental animals, CPC-specific DKK3 overexpression inhibited OA lesions induced by joint instability in mice. DKK3 also has anti-inflammatory effects. The mRNA levels of ADAMTS-5, IL-1β, MMP-3, and MMP13 in cartilage were reduced. To better evaluate the effects of DKK3 treatment, the cartilage OARSI score was used. Notably, improved articular integrity was associated with reduced catabolic factors in rats with OA after DKK3–CPC treatment. These data further support the importance of DKK3 in regulating cartilage remodelling. The phenomena observed in the experimental animal model were consistent with those detected in the in vitro model. Our comprehensive results suggest that DKK3 has chondroprotective effects during osteoarthritis progression.

Inflammation is a prominent pathologic reaction in the OA joint microenvironment. DKK3 expression decreases in CPCs as OA progresses, and following inflammatory cytokine treatment, the present study treated primary CPCs derived from OA cartilage to characterize in vivo inflammatory conditions. DKK3 also modulated inflammatory factors in CPC cell cultures, suggesting reciprocal regulation between DKK3 and inflammatory signalling. In terms of signalling mechanisms, we examined the transcription of β-catenin, a major component of the Wnt pathway [4], to test the effects of DKK3 on this process. Laser confocal analysis revealed that functional inhibition of Wnt signalling occurred through the inhibition of β-catenin nuclear localization and activation in CPCs. β-catenin is an important Wnt signalling component for skeletal morphogenesis and remodelling. The promotive effects of DKK3 on catabolic factor and proteinase expression suggest that DKK3 inhibition of Wnt signalling mediates catabolic activities in response to mechanical stress and proinflammatory cytokines.

Although recent advances in the delivery and release vehicles (such as hydrogels and liposomes) of drugs have made intra-articular injections more attractive [36, 37], the delivery of delivered and endogenous cells as drug-delivering “factories” to preserve and restore joint health seems more attractive [38–40]. Previous research suggested that cartilage-resident stem/progenitor cells are a research focus in osteoarthritic cartilage physiology, development, and repair [12, 14, 16, 41]. Therefore, we chose lentivirus infection to continuously express or interfere with the expression of target genes in CPCs. DKK3, as an extracellular protein, affects not only its biological function but also the metabolism of surrounding uninfected cells and the extracellular matrix. The ACLT + pMMx traumatic OA animal model is often used to explore the pathogenesis, pathophysiological characteristics, and potential treatment targets or strategies of OA [25].

There are several limitations. First, the details of other cell-specific molecular events (such as OA chondrocytes and osteoblasts), especially the cascades by which Wnt signalling interacts with other signalling pathways, remain to be elucidated. Clearly, further studies are needed to elucidate these molecular pathways and the biological role of DKK3 in the overall function of cartilage and other joint structures [42]. Second, OA is a multifaceted disease involving many diverse tissues, such as the synovium, hyaline cartilage, subchondral bone, and meniscus, and has many pathogenic factors, such as extracellular matrix-degrading enzymes and inflammatory cytokines [43]. Therefore, this disease is not substantially influenced by only one or two biological therapies. We envision a combinatorial therapeutic approach with anti-inflammatory measures and the utilization of intrinsic progenitor cells, which simultaneously address multiple aspects of disease etiology.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our results suggest that DKK3 has a chondroprotective effect during osteoarthritis progression and that CPCs may be functional targets. The underlying mechanisms of DKK3 may include enhancing CPC chondrogenic capacity and inhibiting Wnt-mediated expression of catabolic factors and matrix remodelling activities. These findings indicate that DKK3 is a potential therapeutic target for CPC-mediated OA treatment.

Counterexamples to double recurrence for non-commuting deterministic transformations
Zemer Kosloff, Shrey Sanadhya

Abstract
. We show that if p 1 , p 2 p_{1},p_{2} are injective, integer polynomials that vanish at the origin, such that either both are of degree 1 1 or both are of degree 2 2 or higher, then double recurrence fails for non-commuting, mixing, zero entropy transformations. This answers a question of Frantzikinakis and Host completely.

28D05, 37A05, 37A50, 37A30, 60F05, 60G10, 60G15

1.
Introduction

Furstenberg and Katznelson
[
FK78
]
obtained a multidimensional extension of Szemerédi theorem by proving the following multiple recurrence result: Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{k}
be commuting measure preserving transformations of a probability space
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
, then for every
B\in\mathcal{B}
with
\mu(B)>0
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

\mu\left(A\cap T_{1}^{-n}B\cap T_{2}^{-n}B\cap\ldots\cap T_{k}^{-n}B\right)>0.

Since then, the extension of this multiple recurrence property has been central in ergodic Ramsey theory and has had numerous applications in establishing beautiful results in infinite Ramsey theory. A notable extension was obtained by Bergelson and Leibman
[
BL96
]
where it was shown that for all
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}
integer polynomials which vanish at the origin, and
T_{1},\ldots,T_{k}
commuting measure preserving transformations, the multiple recurrence property holds along the polynomial times.

A natural question which arises, and is the main topic of this work, is to study the recurrence problem when the systems do not necessarily commute. In this direction, Bergelson and Leibman
[
BL02
]
showed that double recurrence holds if the two transformations
T,S
generate a nilpotent group. In the non-commutative setting Furstenberg
[
Fur81
, Chapter 2, page 40]
constructed a simple example of double non-recurrence where both the transformations have positive entropy. Recently Frantzikinakis and Host
[
FH23
]
showed that the failure of multiple recurrence for non-commutative positive entropy systems (as indicated by Furstenberg’s example above) holds in greater generality. They proved the following.

Theorem
.

[
FH23
, Proposition 1.4.]
Let
(X,\mu,T)
be an ergodic system with positive entropy,
a,b:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
be injective sequences that miss infinitely many integers, and
F
be an arbitrary
subset of
\mathbb{N}
. Then there exist a system
(X,\mu,S)
, with positive entropy, a measurable set

A
, and
c>0
, such that

\mu\left(A\cap T^{-a(n)}A\cap S^{-b(n)}A\right)=\begin{cases}0&\ \text{if}\,\,n\in F,\\
c&\ \text{if}\,\,n\notin F.\end{cases}

As a consequence, there exist a transformation
S
and a set
A
with
\mu(A)>0
such that
\mu\left(A\cap T^{-a(n)}A\cap S^{-b(n)}A\right)=0
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

On the other hand if one of the transformation has zero entropy, it was proved (in the same paper) that double recurrence holds if one of the iterates is an integer polynomial with no constant term.

Theorem
.

[
FH23
, Theorem 1.3.]
Let
T,S
be measure preserving transformations acting on a probability
space
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
such that the system
(X,\mu,T)
has zero entropy. Let
p\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
be a polynomial such that
p(0)=0
and
\deg(p)\geq 2
. Then for every
A\in\mathcal{B}
and
\epsilon>0
the set

\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\ \mu\left(A\cap T^{-n}A\cap S^{-p(n)}A\right)>\mu(A)^{3}-\epsilon\right\}

has positive lower density.

We should mention that the main result of
[
FH23
]
is a
L^{2}
convergence result for multiple ergodic averages in the non-commuting, zero entropy setting, where one of the iterates is given by an integer polynomial (c.f.
[
FH23
, Theorem 1.1.]
). The authors (
[
FH23
, Problem, page 1639]
) asked whether convergence of double averages (and recurrence) holds (in this setting), if the pair of iterates
n,p(n)
are replaced by the pair
n,n
or by the pair
n^{2},n^{3}
. They also asked if in general, the result hold for pairs of iterates given by arbitrary integer polynomials
p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}(t)
with
p_{1}(0)=p_{2}(0)=0
. Recently counterexamples to multiple averages for zero entropy, non commuting transformations, were constructed in
[
HSY24b
]
,
[
Aus25
]
,
[
HSY24a
]
and
[
KS24
]
. Similar results were also studied in
[
Ryz24a
]
. In this work we prove that if
T
has zero entropy then the only case where double recurrence holds in general is
[
FH23
, Theorem 1.3.]
mentioned above.

In a previous work
[
KS24
, Theorem 1.4.]
, we showed that when the integer polynomials
p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}(t)
are of a special type (high degree and large leading coefficient), then double recurrence in the non-commuting and zero entropy setting fails. The following result shows that the same is true when the degree of both polynomials is at least
2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p_{1},p_{2}:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}
be injective polynomials with
p_{1}(0)=p_{2}(0)=0
and
\deg(p_{1}),\deg(p_{2})\geq 2
. There exist
T,S
, two mixing, measure preserving transformations of a standard probability space
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
, with
h_{\mu}(X,T)=h_{\mu}(X,S)=0
and
A\in\mathcal{B}
with
\mu(A)>0
, such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

A\cap T^{-p_{1}(n)}A\cap S^{-p_{2}(n)}A=\emptyset.

With this result at hand and
[
FH23
, Theorem 1.3.]
, it remains to study the recurrence problem in the linear polynomial settings. Our next result shows that double recurrence fails when both polynomials are linear.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
c,d\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
then there exist
T,S
two mixing, measure preserving transformations of a standard probability space
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
, with
h_{\mu}(X,T)=h_{\mu}(X,S)=0
and
A\in\mathcal{B}
with
\mu(A)>0
, such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

A\cap T^{-cn}A\cap S^{-dn}A=\emptyset.

We remark that the special case where
c=d=1
was also studied in
[
Ryz24b
]
.

1.1.
Structure of the paper

While not immediately clear, it turns out that the simplest non-recurrence result (in non-commuting, zero entropy setting) is the
n,n
case (in other words
1.2
for
c=d=1
). We present a simple proof in
Section

2
, as the baby case of the more difficult construction when the pair of iterates are given by injective integer polynomials of degree
2
and higher i.e.
1.1
. The latter is done in
Section

3
. One major new difficulty which appears in the higher degree polynomials case is that unlike the
n,n
iterates case, the eventual failure of double recurrence (think Borel Cantelli Lemma) does not immediately imply the failure of double recurrence. As a result the proof becomes more subtle and we needed to obtain new results on the ranges of the cocycles from
[
KS24
]
. This part which we believe is of independent interest is carried out in
section

3.2
.

We found the adaptation of the skew product construction in
Section

2
and
3
to the general linear case (i.e.
1.2
where
c\neq 1,d\neq 1
) to be rather difficult. Hence in
Section

4
we construct Gaussian counterexamples to
n,n
recurrence. These examples build upon the main ideas of Austin
[
Aus25
]
, a Cartesian power trick from Rhyzikov
[
Ryz24b
]
and singular measures on the circle with polynomial Fourier decay. Then using the fact that these systems have roots of all orders we derive the general case of two linear integer polynomials which vanish at
0
.

2.
Proof of
1.2
for
c=d=1

In this section, we prove counterexample to multiple recurrence in the non-commuting zero entropy setting for the pair of iterates
n,n
. This case turns out to be the most simplest one. Hence we will prove it first to illustrate the general method that will be used in the proof of
1.1
in
Section

3
.

An important tool that we will use is a the lattice local central limit theorem (LCLT) for deterministic systems (see
2.1
below) which was proved in
[
KV22
]
. Given a probability preserving transformation
(X,\mathcal{B},m,T)
, and a function
f:X\to\mathbb{Z}
, its
sum process
is defined by
S_{n}(f):=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}f\circ T^{k}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We will also refer to
S_{n}(f)
as the cocycle corresponding to
f
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
KV22
]

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},m,T)
be an ergodic and aperiodic probability preserving transformation. There exists a square integrable function
f:X\to\mathbb{Z}
with
\int_{X}fdm=0
, such that

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\sqrt{n}\,m\left(S_{n}(f)=j\right)-\frac{e^{-j^{2}/2n\sigma^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}\right|\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0,

where
\sigma^{2}=2(\ln 2)^{2}
.

Let
(Y,\mathcal{C},m,Q)
be a mixing, zero entropy, aperiodic probability preserving transformation. Examples of such maps are the rank one mixing transformations in
[
Ada98
,
CS10
]
, the time-one map of the horocycle flow on a compact hyperbolic surface and Gaussian automorphisms with a singular spectral measure (see
section

4
). By
2.1
, there exists a Borel function,
f:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
, such that the corresponding sums process
S_{n}(f):Y\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
, satisfies the lattice local central limit theorem (LCLT). Sometimes by abuse of notation, we will say that
f
satisfies LCLT. In this section, we fix such a Borel function
f
.

Let
\Sigma=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
be the space of arrays of
\{0,1\}
. We denote by
\sigma:\Sigma\rightarrow\Sigma
the shift, given by,

(\sigma\,\omega)(n)=\omega(n+1),\hskip 8.53581pt\forall n\in\mathbb{Z},

for
\omega\in\Sigma
. We endow
\Sigma
with the stationary (infinite) product measure
\nu=\left(\frac{1}{2}\delta_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{1}\right)^{\mathbb{Z}}
with marginals
\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)
, and consider the full shift
(\Sigma,\mathcal{B}(\Sigma),\nu,\sigma)
. Here
\mathcal{B}(\Sigma)
is the Borel sigma algebra generated by cylinder sets in
\Sigma
. We define
(Z,\mathcal{D},\widetilde{\mu})
to be the Cartesian product space
Y\times\Sigma
, endowed with product measure, in other words

(Z,\mathcal{D},\widetilde{\mu}):=(Y\times\Sigma,\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\Sigma),m\times\nu).

Let
\widetilde{T}:Y\times\Sigma\rightarrow Y\times\Sigma
be the skew product of
Q
and
f
defined by

(1)

\widetilde{T}(y,\omega)=(Q(y),\sigma_{f(y)}(\omega)).

The skew product
\widetilde{T}
is a probability preserving transformation of
(Z,\mathcal{D},\widetilde{\mu})
and for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(2)

\widetilde{T}^{n}(y,\omega)=(Q^{n}(y),\sigma_{S_{n}(f)(y)}(\omega)).

Proposition 2.2
.

(Z,\mathcal{D},\widetilde{\mu},\widetilde{T})
is a mixing, zero entropy probability preserving system.

Proof.

We will prove that
(Z,\mathcal{D},\widetilde{\mu},\widetilde{T})
is mixing by showing that
f
satisfies
A.1
in
Appendix

A
. It follows from
2.1
that there exits
N\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and all
n>N
, we have

\left|\sqrt{n}\,m\left(S_{n}(f)=j\right)-\frac{e^{-j^{2}/2n\sigma^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}\right|<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}.

Hence for all
n>N
, we have

m\left(S_{n}(f)=j\right)<\frac{e^{-j^{2}/2n\sigma^{2}}}{\sqrt{2n\pi\sigma^{2}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n\pi\sigma^{2}}}<\frac{2}{\sqrt{2n\pi\sigma^{2}}}.

Thus there exists a constant
c>0
, such that for all
n>N
and for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(3)

m\left(S_{n}(f)=j\right)\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}.

Let
M\in\mathbb{N}
, then it follows,

0\leq m\left(\left\|S_{n}(f)\right\|_{\infty}\leq M\right)=\sum_{|j|_{\infty}\leq M}m\left(S_{n}(f)=j\right)\leq\frac{(2M+1)c}{\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.

Hence we deduce from
A.1
in
Appendix

A
that
(Z,\mathcal{D},\widetilde{\mu},\widetilde{T})
is mixing.
The system
(Z,\mathcal{D},\widetilde{\mu},\widetilde{T})
has zero entropy follows from an application of the Abramov-Rokhlin formula (for details see
[
HSY24b
, Proposition 2.2]
or
[
KS24
,
Proposition 4.3]
).
∎

Let
\phi:\Sigma\rightarrow\Sigma
be given by,

(\phi\,\omega)(n):=\begin{cases}\omega(0)&\ \text{if}\,\,n=0,\\
1-\omega(n)&\ \text{if}\,\,n\neq 0,\end{cases}

for
\omega\in\Sigma
. We set
\Psi:Y\times\Sigma\rightarrow Y\times\Sigma
to be
\Psi(y,\omega)=(y,\phi\,\omega)
and define
\widetilde{S}:Y\times\Sigma\rightarrow Y\times\Sigma
to be

(4)

\widetilde{S}:=\Psi^{-1}\circ\widetilde{T}\circ\Psi.

Note that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(5)

\widetilde{S}^{n}(y,\omega)=(Q^{n}(y),\phi^{-1}\circ\sigma_{S_{n}(f)(y)}\circ\phi(\omega)).

Proposition 2.3
.

(Z,\mathcal{D},\widetilde{\mu},\widetilde{S})
is a mixing, zero entropy probability preserving system.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
\phi:\Sigma\to\Sigma
is an invertible, measurable transformation which preserves
\nu
. Therefore,
\Psi:Z\rightarrow Z
is an invertible probability preserving transformation and
\widetilde{S}
is measure theoretically isomorphic to

, which is a mixing, zero entropy system.
∎

Proof of

for

.

Let
B=(Y\times[1]_{0})^{\otimes 3}\subset X
, then

\displaystyle\mu(T^{-n}B\cap S^{-n}B)

\displaystyle=\left(\widetilde{\mu}\left(\widetilde{T}^{-n}(Y\times[1]_{0}),\widetilde{S}^{-n}(Y\times[1]_{0})\right)\right)^{3}

\displaystyle=\left(\widetilde{\mu}\left(\{(y,\omega):\sigma_{S_{n}(f)(y)}(\omega)(0)=1,\phi^{-1}\circ\sigma_{S_{n}(f)(y)}\circ\phi(\omega)(0)=1\}\right)\right)^{3}.

Observe that for
\omega\in\Sigma
we have
(\phi^{-1}\,\omega)(0)=(\omega)(0)
, hence

\displaystyle\mu(T^{-n}B\cap S^{-n}B)

\displaystyle=\left(\widetilde{\mu}\left(\{(y,\omega):(\omega)(S_{n}(f)(y))=1,\sigma_{S_{n}(f)(y)}\circ\phi(\omega)(0)=1\}\right)\right)^{3}

\displaystyle=\left(\widetilde{\mu}\left(\{(y,\omega):(\omega)(S_{n}(f)(y))=1,\phi(\omega)(S_{n}(f)(y))=1\}\right)\right)^{3}.

Observe that if
S_{n}(f)(y)\neq 0
then
\omega(S_{n}(f)(y))\neq\phi(\omega)(S_{n}(f)(y))
, hence we have

\displaystyle\mu(T^{-n}B\cap S^{-n}B)

\displaystyle=\left(\widetilde{\mu}\left(\{(y,\omega):S_{n}(f)(y)=0,\omega(S_{n}(f)(y))=1\}\right)\right)^{3}

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot m\left(\{y\in Y:S_{n}(f)(y)=0\}\right)\right)^{3}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{8}\cdot\left(m\left(\{y\in Y:S_{n}(f)(y)=0\}\right)\right)^{3}.

Using the local central limit theorem (
2.1
) by (
3
) there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
and a constant
C\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n>N
we have,

\displaystyle\mu(T^{-n}B\cap S^{-n}B)

\displaystyle<\dfrac{C}{n^{3/2}}.

In other words, we get

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu(B\cap T^{-n}B\cap S^{-n}B)<\infty.

Hence using the Borel-Cantelli lemma, we get that for a.e.
x\in X
,
x\in B\cap T^{-n}B\cap S^{-n}B
for finitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In other words there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
, such that the set

(6)

D_{N}:=\{x\in B:\,\mathrm{for}\,\mathrm{all}\,n>N,\mathrm{either}\,T^{n}x\notin B\,\mathrm{or}\,S^{n}x\notin B\},

has positive
\mu
measure. We can choose
N
to be smallest such integer and write
D=D_{N}
. Now let
M\geq 0
, be the largest integer such that

is of positive
\mu
measure. Since
D\subset B
, for all
n>N
,

D\cap T^{-n}D\cap S^{-n}D\subset D\cap T^{-n}B\cap S^{-n}B=\emptyset,

thus
M\leq N
. This shows that
M
is a well defined integer and
M\in\{0,\ldots,N\}
.
In addition, as
\mu(D)>0
,
M\geq 0
. For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
T^{-n}A\subset T^{-(n+M)}D
and
S^{-n}A\subset S^{-(n+M)}D
. Hence for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

A\cap T^{-n}A\cap S^{-n}A\subset D\cap T^{-(n+M)}D\cap S^{-(n+M)}D.

By the definition of

, it implies that for all

,

as needed.
∎

2N<p_{K}
. By definition,
Z(n)
is a (linear) function of

\left\{\bar{f}^{(1)}_{k}\circ T^{j}:\ 1\leq k<K(n),\ 0\leq j\leq 2d_{K}+p_{K}\right\}.

Similarly
Y(n)
is a function of

\left\{\bar{f}^{(1)}_{k}\circ T^{j}:\ k\geq K(n),\ 0\leq j\leq 2d_{k}+p_{k}\right\}.

Thus by
3.6
.
(c)
and the definition of
f_{k},\ k\in\mathbb{N}
it follows that,
Z(n)
and
Y(n)
are independent.

Now let
M<0
be as in
3.9
and write,

A:=\left\{x\in\Omega:\ \forall n\in[0,2N),\ Y(n)>-M\right\}.

For all
x\in A
the following holds: for all
0\leq n<2N
,

\displaystyle S_{n+1}\left(f^{(1)}\right)(x)-S_{n}\left(f^{(1)}\right)(x)

\displaystyle=Y(n)(x)+Z(n)(x)

\displaystyle>-M+\min_{0\leq n<2N}Z(n)(x)>0.

Consequently for all
x\in A
,

(0,0)<S_{1}(f)(x)<\ldots<S_{2N}(f)(x)

and as a consequence (see the discussion following (
29
))

m\left(\left|\left\{S_{N+j}(f)-S_{N}(f):-N\leq j\leq N\right\}\right|=2N+1\right)\geq m(A).

By
lemma

3.10
with
C=-M
, we have
m(A)>0
and we are done.
∎

Proof of
lemma

3.10
.

Fix
C\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\kappa=\kappa(N)
be the smallest positive integer such that
2N<p_{\kappa}
and
K=K(N)
the smallest integer largest than
\kappa
which satisfies in addition that
2p_{K}<d_{K}
. Note that the latter condition holds since
\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{p_{k}}{d_{k}}=0
. We will now define a set
D\in\mathcal{B}
which we will show has positive measure and is contained in the required set
\{\min_{0\leq n<2N}Y(n)>C\}
.

The set
D
is defined via
D=\cap_{k=\kappa(N)}^{\infty}D_{k}
where the sets
D_{k}
are defined by the following rules:

•

For all
K(N)\leq k<K(N)+C
define,

D_{k}:=\left\{x\in\Omega:\ \text{for all}\ 0\leq j<p_{k}+2N,\ \bar{f}_{k}^{(1)}\circ T^{j}(x)=1\ \text{and}\ \bar{f}_{k}^{(1)}\circ T^{d_{k}+j}(x)=0\right\}.

Note that as
p_{k}+2N<2p_{k}
there is no contradiction in the definition of
D_{k}
.

•

For
k\geq\kappa(N)
which is not in
[K(N),K(N)+C)
we define

D_{k}:=\left\{x\in\Omega:\ \text{for all}\ 0\leq j<p_{k}+2N,\ \bar{f}_{k}^{(1)}\circ T^{j}(x)=\bar{f}_{k}^{(1)}\circ T^{d_{k}+j}(x)=0\right\}.

We first show that
D\subset\{\min_{0\leq n<2N}Y(n)>C\}
. Indeed, let
0\leq n<2N
. First notice that for all
k\geq\kappa(N)
which is not in
[K(N),K(N)+C)
and
x\in D_{k}
,

f_{k}\circ T^{n}(x)=\sum_{j=n}^{p_{k}+n-1}\left(\bar{f}_{k}^{(1)}\circ T^{j}(x)-\bar{f}_{k}^{(1)}\circ T^{d_{k}+j}(x)\right)=0,

as all the functions in the sum are
0
on
D_{k}
.

Similarly, for
K(N)\leq k<K(N)+C
and
x\in D_{k}
,

\displaystyle f_{k}\circ T^{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=n}^{p_{k}+n-1}\left(\bar{f}_{k}^{(1)}\circ T^{j}(x)-\bar{f}_{k}^{(1)}\circ T^{d_{k}+j}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=n}^{p_{k}+n-1}\bar{f}_{k}^{(1)}\circ T^{j}(x)=p_{k}>1.

These two observations together show that for all
x\in\cap_{k\geq\kappa(N)}D_{k}
,

Y(n)=\sum_{k\geq\kappa(n)}f_{k}^{(1)}\circ T^{n}(x)=\sum_{k=K(N)}^{K(N)+C-1}f_{k}^{(1)}\circ T^{n}(x)>C.

This shows that for all
0\leq n<2N
and
x\in D
,
Y(n)>C
.

It remains to show that
m(D)>0
. By
3.6
.
(b)
, each
D_{k}
is a finite intersection of independent, positive measure sets and thus for all
k\geq\kappa(N)
,

m\left(D_{k}\right)>0.

Furthermore, for all
k\geq K(N)+C
,

\displaystyle m\left(D_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=0}^{p_{k}+2N-1}\left(m\left(\bar{f}_{k}^{(1)}\circ T^{j}=0\right)\cdot m\left(\bar{f}_{k}^{(1)}\circ T^{j+d_{k}}=0\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\alpha_{k}^{2}\right)^{p_{k}+2N}

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left(-\alpha_{k}^{2}(p_{k}+2N)\right)\geq\exp\left(-\frac{2}{p_{k}}\right).

Here the last inequality follows from
p_{k}+2N<2p_{k}
and
p_{k}\alpha_{k}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{p_{k}}
. Finally, by
3.6
.
(c)
,
\left\{D_{k}\right\}_{k\geq\kappa(N)}
are independent. We conclude that,

\displaystyle m(D)

\displaystyle=\prod_{k\geq\kappa(N)}m\left(D_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{k=\kappa(N)}^{K(N)+C-1}m\left(D_{k}\right)\right)\left(\prod_{k\geq K(N)+C}m\left(D_{k}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\prod_{k=\kappa(N)}^{K(N)+C-1}m\left(D_{k}\right)\right)\left(\prod_{k\geq K(N)+C}\exp\left(-\frac{2}{p_{k}}\right)\right).

The first term in the product is positive as a finite product of positive numbers. The second is positive since
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p_{k}}<\infty
. It follows that
m(D)>0
, and the proof is concluded.
∎

4.
Gaussian systems proof of
1.2

A real valued stationary sequence of random variables
\left(X_{n}\right)_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}
is a Gaussian process if for every
n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots<n_{m}
and
c_{1},\ldots c_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\sum_{k=1}^{m}c_{k}X_{n_{k}}
is a Gaussian random variable. If in addition the expectation of
X_{0}
is
0
, we will refer to the process as a stationary symmetric Gaussian process. The covariance function
\rho:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
of the (symmetric) process defined by
\rho(n)=\mathbb{E}\left(X_{0}X_{n}\right)
is a positive definite function. By Bochner’s Theorem there exists
\sigma
a symmetric measure on the circle such that for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\mathbb{E}\left(X_{0}X_{n}\right)=\hat{\sigma}(n)=\int_{S^{1}}e^{int}d\sigma.

In this case
\sigma
is called the spectral measure of the process. In the other direction, for every symmetric probability measure on the circle
\sigma
there exists a symmetric Gaussian process whose spectral measure is
\sigma
.

A probability preserving system
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
is a Gaussian automorphism with spectral measure
\sigma
if there exists
f\in L^{2}(X,\mu)
such that

•

\int fd\mu=0
.

•

The process
\left\{f\circ T^{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is Gaussian and for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\int f\cdot f\circ T^{n}d\mu=\hat{\sigma}(n)
,

•

the

\sigma
-algebra generated by the functions
\left\{f\circ T^{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is
\mathcal{B}\mod\mu
.

In the course of this section, we will make use of the following properties of Gaussian automorphisms. The proofs for these statements (unless specific references are mentioned) are mentioned in
[
CFS82
]
or
[
JRDLR23
]
.

(a)

T
is mixing if and only if
\sigma
is a Rajchman measure meaning that
\hat{\sigma}(n)\xrightarrow[|n|\to\infty]{}0
.

(b)

If
\sigma
is a singular measure (with respect to Lebesgue) then
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
is a zero entropy transformation
[
Pin60
,
dlR93
]
.

(c)

T
can be embedded in a flow, in other words, there exists a
\mu
preserving
\mathbb{R}
- flow
\left\{\phi_{s}\right\}_{s\in\mathbb{R}}
such that
\phi_{1}=T
. As a consequence for every
d\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
,
R=\phi_{1/d}
satisfies
R^{d}=T
. If
T
is ergodic (respectively mixing) then so is
R
.

(d)

Let
U:L^{2}(X,m)\to L^{2}(X,m)
be the Koopman operator of
T
and
\mathcal{H}
the
L^{2}
closure of the linear span of
\left\{U^{n}f:\ n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
. Every function in
\mathcal{H}
is a Gaussian random variable and
\mathcal{H}
is
U
invariant (see
[
Kec10
, Appendix B-D]
).

(e)

The space
L^{2}(X,m)
is isomorphic to the Fock space over
\mathcal{H}
(see
[
JRDLR23
, Section 4.2 and 4.3]
and
[
Kec10
, Appendix B-D]
).

(f)

Given a unitary operator
V:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
, there is a canonical extension, called the second quantization of
V
, which is a unitary operator on the whole of
L^{2}(X,\mu)
(see
[
JRDLR23
, Proposition 4.1]
).

(g)

If
V
restricted to
\mathcal{H}
is unitarily equivalent to
U
restricted to
\mathcal{H}
then there exists
S
, a
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
measure preserving system which is (measure theoretically) isomorphic to
T
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
V^{n}f=f\circ S^{n}
.

A probability measure
\sigma
on the circle is said to have polynomial Fourier decay with parameter
\delta>0
if there exists
C>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\left|\hat{\sigma}(n)\right|\leq C|n|^{-\delta}.

A standard exercise on differentiation by parts shows that if
\sigma
is Lebesgue absolutely continuous with a
C^{1}
density function then it has a polynomial Fourier decay of order 1. Kauffman showed in
[
Kau80
]
that there exists singular measures with polynomial decay. See also
[
JS16
,
ARW23
,
BB25
]
and the references therein for recent results on polynomial Fourier decay for singular measures.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\sigma
be a measure on the circle with polynomial decay with parameter
\delta>0
,
\left(X,\mathcal{B},m,T\right)
a Gaussian automorphism with spectral measure
\sigma
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2k\delta>1
. There exists an
m
preserving transformation
S:X\to X
, isomorphic to
T
, and
B\in\mathcal{B}^{\otimes k}
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

B\cap\left(T^{\otimes k}\right)^{-n}B\cap\left(S^{\otimes k}\right)^{-n}B=\emptyset.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the
L^{2}
closure of
\mathrm{span}\left\{f\circ T^{n}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
where
f\in L^{2}(X,m)
is a function whose times series is a Gaussian process with spectral measure
\sigma
which generates
\mathcal{B}
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be the linear subspace in
\mathcal{H}
generated by
f
and denote by
P:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
the projection operator to
\mathcal{K}
. The linear operator
W
, defined by

Wg:=Pg-(I-P)g,

is a unitary operator on
\mathcal{H}
and
W=W^{-1}
. It follows that the operator
V=WUW:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is a unitary operator which is unitarily equivalent to
U
, the Koopman operator of
T
. Let
S:X\to X
be a measure preserving transformation, isomorphic to
T
, such that the Koopman operator of
S
is
V
. With this we have defined the pair of transformations
T,S
.

It remains to find the set
B
where double recurrence fails for the pair
T^{\otimes k},S^{\otimes k}
. To that end, first consider the set
A:=\left\{f>1\right\}\in\mathcal{B}
. Since
Pf=f
and
P^{2}=P
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(30)

PV^{n}f=PU^{n}f,

and by the definition of
W
,

(I-P)V^{n}f=-(I-P)U^{n}f.

The latter implies that for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, either
(I-P)V^{n}f\leq 0
or
(I-P)U^{n}f\leq 0
. Hence we deduce that,

\displaystyle T^{-n}A\cap S^{-n}A

\displaystyle=\left\{f\circ T^{n}>1,f\circ S^{n}>1\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{U^{n}f>1,V^{n}f>1\right\}

\displaystyle\subset\left\{V^{n}f>1,(I-P)V^{n}f\leq 0\right\}\cup\left\{U^{n}f>1,(I-P)U^{n}f\leq 0\right\}

\displaystyle\subset\left\{PV^{n}f>1,(I-P)V^{n}f\leq 0\right\}\cup\left\{PU^{n}f>1,(I-P)U^{n}f\leq 0\right\}.

Taking into consideration (
30
), we conclude that

T^{-n}A\cap S^{-n}A\subset\left\{PU^{n}f>1\right\}.

By Markov’s inequality,

\displaystyle m\left(T^{-n}A\cap S^{-n}A\right)

\displaystyle\leq m\left(PU^{n}f>1\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|PU^{n}f\|_{2}^{2}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{as}\ P\ \text{is a rank one projection to}\ \mathcal{K},

\displaystyle=\left|\left<f,U^{n}f\right>\right|^{2}\cdot\|f\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\hat{\sigma}(n)\right|^{2}\cdot\sigma\left(S^{1}\right)\leq Cn^{-2\delta}.

This shows that writing
D:=A^{\otimes k}\in\mathcal{B}^{\otimes k}
,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}m^{\otimes k}\left(D\cap\left(T^{\otimes k}\right)^{-n}D\cap\left(S^{\otimes k}\right)^{-n}D\right)\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-2k\delta}<\infty.

Arguing in a similar way as in
section

2.1
, one finds a set
B\subset D
, with
m^{\otimes k}(B)>0
, such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

B\cap\left(T^{\otimes k}\right)^{-n}B\cap\left(S^{\otimes k}\right)^{-n}B=\emptyset.

∎

Using this result we can now prove
1.2
.

Proof of
1.2
.

Let
c,d\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. Choose
\sigma
a singular measure on the circle which has polynomial decay of order
\delta
(cf. the measure in
[
Kau80
]
) and
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2k\delta>1
. By
4.1
, there exist Gaussian automorphisms
P,Q
of a probability space
\left(Y,\mathcal{C},m\right)
with spectral measure
\sigma
and a set
A\in\mathcal{C}^{\otimes k}
such that
m^{\otimes k}(A)>0
and for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

A\cap\left(P^{\otimes k}\right)^{-n}A\cap\left(Q^{\otimes k}\right)^{-n}A=\emptyset.

We set
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
to be the probability space
(Y^{\otimes k},\mathcal{C}^{\otimes k},m^{\otimes k})
. Since
\sigma
is a singular Rajchman measure, both
P
and
Q
are mixing, zero entropy transformations
[
Pin60
,
dlR93
]
. As every Gaussian automorphism has roots of all orders, there exists
L,R
Gaussian automorphisms of
(Y,\mathcal{B},m)
such that
L^{c}=P
and
R^{d}=Q
. It follows that
L
and
R
(and hence
L^{\otimes k}
and
R^{\otimes k}
) are mixing, zero entropy transformations. It remains to note that for
A\in\mathcal{C}^{\otimes k}
as above (with
m^{\otimes k}(A)>0
) and for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

A\cap\left(L^{\otimes k}\right)^{-cn}A\cap\left(R^{\otimes k}\right)^{-dn}A=A\cap\left(P^{\otimes k}\right)^{-n}A\cap\left(Q^{\otimes k}\right)^{-n}A=\emptyset.

Finally setting
T=L^{\otimes k}
and
S=R^{\otimes k}
, we obtain the statement of the Theorem.
∎

Appendix A

The results on the failure of double recurrence for non-commuting transformations make use of the mixing property of skew product extensions. The following result (see
A.1
), the proof for which is standard and well known, is used in
2.2
. For the sake of completeness, in this appendix, we provide a proof for
A.1
.

Given a probability-preserving system
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
, a measurable function
f:X\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, for
d\geq 1
, we denote by
T_{f}:X\times\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\to X\times\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
, the skew product extension of
T
by
f
defined as following

T_{f}(x,\omega)=(Tx,\sigma_{f(x)}\omega).

Here
\sigma
is the (
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
) shift action of
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
. For every
\eta
, a stationary product measure on
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
,
T_{f}
preserves
\mu\times\eta
. We choose
\eta
to be the product measure on
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
with marginals
\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{0}+\delta_{1}\right)
. In order not to burden the notations, we set
m:=\mu\times\eta
.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
be a probability-preserving system and let
f:X\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a measurable function. Assume that for all
M>0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu\left(\left\|S_{n}(f)\right\|_{\infty}\leq M\right)=0.

Then
T
is a weak mixing (respectively mixing) if and only if
T_{f}
is a weak mixing (respectively mixing).

Proof.

The projection
\pi:X\times\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\to X
is a factor map from
T_{f}
to
T
. As a factor of a weak mixing (respectively mixing) transformation is always weak mixing (respectively mixing), it follows that if
T_{f}
is weak mixing (respectively mixing) then so is
T
. It remains to show the implications from
T
to
T_{f}
.

Assume first that
T
is mixing. In order to show that
T_{f}
is mixing it suffices to show that for every
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
and
B_{1},B_{2}\subset\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
a finite union of cylinder sets,

\lim_{n\to\infty}m\left(A_{1}\times B_{1}\cap T_{f}^{-n}\left(A_{2}\times B_{2}\right)\right)=\left(\mu\left(A_{1}\right)\eta\left(B_{1}\right)\right)\left(\mu\left(A_{2}\right)\eta\left(B_{2}\right)\right).

Let
M\in\mathbb{N}
be large enough so that
1_{B_{1}}
and
1_{B_{2}}
are functions of
\{0,1\}^{[-M,M]^{d}}
. Note that for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
\|m\|_{\infty}>2M
,
m+[-M,M]^{d}
is disjoint from
[-M,M]^{d}
and thus
B_{1}
and
\sigma_{-m}B_{2}
are independent (under
\eta
). For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, write

E_{n}:=\left\{x\in X:\ \left\|S_{n}(f)(x)\right\|_{\infty}\leq 2M\right\}\in\mathcal{B}.

By Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle m\left(A_{1}\times B_{1}\cap T_{f}^{-n}\left(A_{2}\times B_{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\int 1_{A_{1}}1_{A_{2}}\circ T^{n}\left(\int 1_{B_{1}}1_{B_{2}}\circ\sigma_{S_{n}(f)}d\eta\right)d\mu

\displaystyle=I(n)+II(n),

where

\displaystyle I(n)

\displaystyle=\int_{X\setminus E_{n}}1_{A_{1}}1_{A_{2}}\circ T^{n}\left(\int 1_{B_{1}}1_{B_{2}}\circ\sigma_{S_{n}(f)}d\eta\right)d\mu,\ \ \text{and}

\displaystyle II(n)

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{n}}1_{A_{1}}1_{A_{2}}\circ T^{n}\left(\int 1_{B_{1}}1_{B_{2}}\circ\sigma_{S_{n}(f)}d\eta\right)d\mu.

By the assumption on
f
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu\left(E_{n}\right)=0
. We deduce from this and

0\leq II(n)\leq\mu\left(E_{n}\right),

that
\lim_{n\to\infty}II(n)=0
. For all
x\in X\setminus{E_{n}}
,
\left\|S_{n}(f)(x)\right\|_{\infty}\geq 2M
and

\int 1_{B_{1}}1_{B_{2}}\circ\sigma_{S_{n}(f)(x)}d\eta=\eta\left(B_{1}\right)\eta\left(\sigma_{-S_{n}(f)(x)}B_{2}\right)=\eta\left(B_{1}\right)\eta\left(B_{2}\right).

Consequently,

(31)

I(n)=\eta\left(B_{1}\right)\eta\left(B_{2}\right)\int_{X\setminus E_{n}}1_{A_{1}}1_{A_{2}}\circ T^{n}d\mu.

In addition,

\mu\left(A_{1}\cap T^{-n}A_{2}\right)-\mu\left(E_{n}\right)\leq\int_{X\setminus E_{n}}1_{A_{1}}1_{A_{2}}\circ T^{n}d\mu\leq\mu\left(A_{1}\cap T^{-n}A_{2}\right).

By the mixing property of
T
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu\left(E_{n}\right)=0
, we deduce that,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{X\setminus E_{n}}1_{A_{1}}1_{A_{2}}\circ T^{n}d\mu=\mu\left(A_{1}\right)\mu\left(A_{2}\right),

and as a consequence (recall (
31
)),

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}m\left(A_{1}\times B_{1}\cap T_{f}^{-n}\left(A_{2}\times B_{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(I(n)+II(n)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\mu\left(A_{1}\right)\eta\left(B_{1}\right)\right)\left(\mu\left(A_{2}\right)\eta\left(B_{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=m\left(A_{1}\times B_{1}\right)m\left(A_{2}\times B_{2}\right)

as needed. This shows that
T_{f}
is mixing for a dense collection of sets in
\mathcal{B}_{X\times\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
(the Borel-
\sigma
algebra on the product space
X\times\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
). It follows from this that
T_{f}
is mixing.

Now we assume
T
is weak mixing and show that this implies the weak mixing property of
T_{f}
. As before let
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
and
B_{1},B_{2}\subset\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
two sets which are a finite union of cylinder sets. Since
T
is weak mixing, there exists
J\subset\mathbb{N}
of Banach density
1
such that

\lim_{J\ni n\to\infty}\mu\left(A_{1}\cap T^{-n}A_{2}\right)=\mu\left(A_{1}\right)\mu\left(A_{2}\right).

Arguing as before (in the mixing
T
case) we see that,

\lim_{J\ni n\to\infty}m\left(\left(A_{1}\times B_{1}\right)\cap T_{f}^{-n}\left(A_{2}\times B_{2}\right)\right)=m\left(A_{1}\times B_{1}\right)m\left(A_{2}\times B_{2}\right).

Since
J
is of Banach density
1
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|m\left(\left(A_{1}\times B_{1}\right)\cap T_{f}^{-n}\left(A_{2}\times B_{2}\right)\right)-m\left(A_{1}\times B_{1}\right)m\left(A_{2}\times B_{2}\right)\right|=0.

As before this shows that
T_{f}
is weak mixing for a dense collection of sets in
\mathcal{B}_{X\times\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
. The weak mixing property of
T_{f}
follows from this.

∎

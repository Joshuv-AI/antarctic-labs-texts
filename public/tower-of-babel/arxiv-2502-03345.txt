1.
Introduction

Let
D
be an endomorphism on
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
such that

D(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})=(x_{1}+x_{2}\;\text{mod}\;m,x_{2}+x_{3}\;\text{mod}\;m,...,x_{n}+x_{1}\;\text{mod}\;m).

We call
D
the
Ducci function
and we call the sequence
\{D^{\alpha}(\mathbf{u})\}_{\alpha=0}^{\infty}
the
Ducci sequence of

\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
, with
[
1
,
8
,
10
]
being a few sources that call it this.

To better illustrate a Ducci sequence, we can consider the Ducci sequence of
(0,1,4)\in\mathbb{Z}_{6}^{3}
:
(0,1,4),(1,5,4),(0,3,5),(3,2,5),(5,1,2),(0,3,1),(3,4,1),(1,5,4)
. As one can see, if we continue listing terms in the Ducci sequence of
(0,1,4)
, then it will cycle through 6 tuples:
(1,5,4),(0,3,5),(3,2,5),(5,1,2),(0,3,1),(3,4,1)
. We call these tuples the Ducci cycle of
(0,1,4)
, or more formally,

Definition 1
.

The
Ducci cycle of

\mathbf{u}
is

\{\mathbf{v}\mid\exists\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\cup\{0\},\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\ni\mathbf{v}=D^{\alpha+\beta}(\mathbf{u})=D^{\alpha}(\mathbf{u})\}.

The
length of

\mathbf{u}
,
\mathbf{Len(u)}
, is the smallest
\alpha
satisfying the equation

\mathbf{v}=D^{\alpha+\beta}(\mathbf{u})=D^{\alpha}(\mathbf{u})

for some
v\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
the
period of

\mathbf{u}
,
\mathbf{Per(u)}
, is the smallest
\beta
that satisfies the equation.

From this, we can see that
\text{Len}(0,1,4)=1
and
\text{Per}(0,1,4)=6
.

A particularly important Ducci sequence is what we call the
basic Ducci sequence
, which is first discussed by
[
8
]
on page 302 and also by
[
1
,
7
,
10
]
. The basic Ducci sequence of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
is the Ducci sequence of
(0,0,...,0,1)\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
. We denote
L_{m}(n)=\text{Len}(0,0,...,0,1)
and
P_{m}(n)=\text{Per}(0,0,...,0,1)
. These notations are first used in Definition 5 of
[
1
]
and
P_{m}(n)
is similar to the notations that
[
8
,
10
]
use. The reason why this sequence is significant is because of Lemma 1 of
[
1
]
, which says that for any
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
,
\text{Len}(\mathbf{u})\leq L_{m}(n)
and
\text{Per}(\mathbf{u})|P_{m}(n)
. The notation of
P_{m}(n)
is also used to represent the maximum value of the period in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
on page 858 of
[
2
]
.

Define
K(\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n})
to be the set of all tuples in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
that belong to a Ducci cycle for some
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
. This subgroup is first defined by
[
1
]
in Definition 4, where they also note that

\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}

[

]
.

We now introduce another endomorphism on
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
, call it
H
, and define it so

H(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})=(x_{2},x_{3},...,x_{n},x_{1}).

This endomorphism is first defined on page 302 of
[
8
]
, as well as by
[

,
10
,
11
]
. It also satisfies the condition that

where

is the identity endomorphism. In this paper, we are most interested in how

interact with each other. For example, in some cases, we will have that for
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
, and every
\mathbf{v}
in the Ducci cycle of
\mathbf{u}
,
H^{\beta}(\mathbf{v})
also belongs to the Ducci cycle of
\mathbf{u}
for every
-n<\beta<n
. If a Ducci sequence exhibits this behavior, then we call the sequence
\mathbf{H}
-
closed
. If every Ducci sequence on
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
exhibits this behavior, then we call
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}

H
-closed.

We would like to prove a few cases where we have
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
is
H
-closed. We begin by narrowing to the case where

and aim to prove the following theorem:

.

\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{3}
is H-closed if one of the following occurs:

(1)

m
is a power of
2
. Specifically, if
\mathbf{u}\in K(\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{3})
, then

(2)

prime. Specifically, if
\mathbf{u}\in K(\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{3})

.

We will then examine some cases when

and observe some patterns in when
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
is
H
-closed. We would like to prove one particular case of this. Here,

.

Let

(\Rightarrow)

It suffices to show

D^{L+\alpha}(\mathbf{u})=H^{\beta}(D^{L}(\mathbf{u}))

We now note that every

•

In addition to the sum formula in the last bullet point, there is another sum breakdown. Originally found on page 103 of

Lemma 5
.

Let
r>t>0
, and
1\leq s\leq n

a_{r,s}=\sum_{i=0}^{t}\binom{t}{i}a_{r-t,s-i}.

Proof.

We prove this via induction, with the
t=1

Inductive Step:
Assume it is true for
t-1
, or in other words, assume

a_{r,s}=\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\binom{t-1}{i}a_{r-t+1,s-i}.

Then, by Theorem 5 of
[
11
]
, this is

\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\binom{t-1}{i}a_{r-t,s-i}+\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\binom{t-1}{i}a_{r-t,s-i-1}.

Changing the indices on the second sum, this is

\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\binom{t-1}{i}a_{r-t,s-i}+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\binom{t-1}{i-1}a_{r-t,s-i}.

Next, we pull out the
i=0

i=t
term from the second sum to obtain

\binom{t-1}{0}a_{r-t,s}+\binom{t-1}{t-1}a_{r-t,s-t}+\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}(\binom{t-1}{i}+\binom{t-1}{i-1})a_{r-t,s-i}.

This is the same as

\binom{t}{0}a_{r-t,s}+\binom{t}{t}a_{r-t,s-t}+\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\binom{t}{i}a_{r-t,s-i}

or

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\binom{t}{i}a_{r-t,s-i}

and the lemma follows.
∎

This will be the case if

a_{m,s}\equiv\begin{cases}0\;\text{mod}\;m&1<s<n\\
1\;\text{mod}\;m&s=1,n\end{cases}.

Write
m=\delta n-1
. Using Lemma
5
, we can break down
a_{\delta n-1,s}
as follows:

a_{\delta n-1,s}=\sum_{i=0}^{\delta n-1}\binom{\delta n-1}{i}a_{0,s-i}.

For the term of this sum to be nonzero, we need that both

. The only time
a_{0,s-i}\neq 0
is when

, in which case

. The two cases where
\displaystyle{\binom{\delta n-1}{i}}\not\equiv 0\;\text{mod}\;m

\displaystyle{\binom{\delta n-1}{i}=1}
.

If
i=0

a_{0,s-i}=0

. For

, so

. From here, the theorem follows.

Pinched Arnol’d tongues for Families of circle maps
Philip Boyland

Abstract
. The family of circle maps f b , ω ​ ( x ) = x + ω + b ​ ϕ ​ ( x ) f_{b,\omega}(x)=x+\omega+b\,\phi(x) is used as a simple model for a periodically forced oscillator. The parameter ω \omega represents the unforced frequency, b b the coupling, and ϕ \phi the forcing. When ϕ = 1 2 ​ π ​ sin ⁡ ( 2 ​ π ​ x ) \phi=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sin(2\pi x) this is the classical Arnol’d standard family. Such families are often studied in the ( ω , b ) (\omega,b) -plane via the so-called tongues T β T_{\beta} consisting of all ( ω , b ) (\omega,b) such that f b , ω f_{b,\omega} has rotation number β \beta . The interior of the rational tongues T p / q T_{p/q} represent the system mode-locked into a p / q p/q -periodic response. Campbell, Galeeva, Tresser, and Uherka proved that when the forcing is a PL map with k = 2 k=2 breakpoints, all T p / q T_{p/q} pinch down to a width of a single point at multple values when q q large enough. In contrast, we prove that it generic amongst PL forcings with a given k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 breakpoints that there is no such pinching of any of the rational tongues. We also prove that the absence of pinching is generic for Lipschitz and C r C^{r} ( r > 0 r>0 ) forcing.



1.
Introduction

In
[
3
]
Arnol’d introduced an example of a parameterized
family of circle maps and noted that it
provides a paradigm for describing periodically forced oscillators.
It illustrates the common phenomenon of rational mode locking and
irrational quasiperiodicity.
The family, defined by its lift as,

\tilde{f}_{b,\omega}(x)=x+\omega+\frac{b}{2\pi}\sin(2\pi x)

has come to be called the Arnol’d family, or more commonly,
the standard family. In keeping with its physical origins

\omega
is sometimes called the intrinsic frequency,
b
the coupling,
and
\frac{\sin(2\pi x)}{2\pi}
the forcing.
A fairly large literature has grown up
around the family, both from a purely mathematical point of view
and with a view to applications.
In addition, the bifurcation diagrams of many higher dimensional
exhibit the same kind of structures.

When
b<1
, the map
f_{b,\omega}
is a diffeomorphism and
the family’s bifurcation diagram in the
(\omega,b)
-plane is analyzed
using the rotation number
\beta
of the diffeomorphism.
When
\beta=p/q
this set of parameters has a sharp point
on the
\omega
-axis and opens out
into a horn or tongue, hence the name resonance horn
or Arnol’d tongue (see Figure
1
, left).
When
\beta
is an irrational, the set
is a line connecting the line
b=0
to the line

b=1
. Herman (
[
13
]
) proved basic properties
of these sets, in particular, for each
0<b\leq 1
the
horizontal lines of constant

b
intersect the rational tongues in a nontrivial interval.

The dynamics of the piecewise linear (PL)
analog of the quadratic family on the interval,
the tent family, have been much studied. The analog on
the circle replaces the sine term with a
triangle wave.
To those not familiar with PL dynamics
the resulting bifurcation diagram yields something

Assume that
\phi
is a PL, standard-like forcing with two

. In the

-bifurcation diagram of

The triangle wave forcing corresponds to

Pinching in PL circle families with two breakpoints forces a

\tilde{f}_{b,\omega,\phi}(x)=x+\omega+b\,\phi(x)?

(1.1)

for various classes of

A forcing

For standard-like forcing

(b)

(c)

2.1.
spaces of maps on the circle

The circle is

The only measure used here is Lebesgue, and is denoted by

The space of all real-valued

These spaces will often be treated

\phi:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

We will be concerned here with degree one circle

\tilde{f}(x)=x+\phi(x)

Convention 2.1
.

With one important exception, in the rest of the paper,

\tilde{f}^{q}=R_{p}
.
In the base the maps satisfy

, which loses information,

always chosen so
\tilde{f}(0)\in[0,1)

Both

and

are complete, and so closed subsets

If

\|\alpha\|_{\infty}=\esssup|\alpha|
.
In finite dimensions,
\|{\mathbf{v}}\|_{\infty}=\max|v_{i}|

2.3.
Lipschitz functions

Recall that if

3.

As noted in the introduction
we consider families with the same structure as the standard
family. A map is strongly order preserving if
x_{1}<x_{2}

x_{1}<x_{2}

Definition 3.1
.

\phi

\displaystyle f_{b,\omega,\phi}\ \text{is strongly order preserving for}\ b<1,

\displaystyle f_{b,\omega,\phi}\ \text{is not weakly order preserving for}\ b>1.

When

is a homeomorphism. When

or noninjective and weakly order preserving (as in the
triangle wave example). The condition for

but

which is stronger
than just being noninjective. Thus, when
b>1

\tilde{f}_{b,\omega,\phi}^{q}
is never

and so we exclude

The collection of standard-like

In the smooth and Lipschitz case

We prove the Lipschitz case

\omega_{k_{i}}\rightarrow\omega_{0}

\phi_{k_{i}}\rightarrow\phi_{0}
in
C^{0}(S^{1})
,

\tilde{f}_{b_{0},\omega_{0},\phi_{0}}^{q}=R_{p}
and so

\phi_{0}\in B_{p/q,n}
and
B_{p/q,n}

is closed, and so
A_{p/q,n}
is open.

Finally, by Lemma
5.1
below,
A_{p/q,n}
is also
dense in
\mathcal{S}^{r}
and thus the collection of
not pinching forcing

A=\bigcap_{p/q,n}A_{p/q,n}=\{\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{r}\colon\tilde{f}_{b,\omega,\phi}^{q}\not=R_{p}\ \text{for all}\ b>0,\omega,p/q\}

is dense
G_{\delta}
.

The proof for the Lipschitz case is almost identical, except
it finishes with Theorem
6.1
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Note that no
C^{0}
version of this theorem is
given. A proof like that of Lemma
3.2
shows that

\mathcal{S}^{0}=\{\phi\in C^{0}(S^{1})\colon\inf_{y>x}\frac{\phi(y)-\phi(x)}{y-x}=-1\}.

In addition, trigonometric polynomials are dense in
C^{0}(S^{1})

and are not pinching by Lemma
2.3
and so a proof like
that of Lemma
5.1
shows that not pinching
is dense in
\mathcal{S}^{0}
. The difficulty is
that
\mathcal{S}^{0}
is neither open nor closed in
C^{0}(S^{1})

and so it is not clear that it is a Baire space.

5.
The smooth density lemma

Lemma 5.1
.

For each
p/q,n>0
the set

A_{p/q,n}^{r}=\{\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{r}\colon\tilde{f}_{b,\omega,\phi}^{q}\not=R_{p}\ \text{for all}\ b\in[1/n,1],\omega\}.

is dense in
\mathcal{S}^{r}
.

Proof.

It is classical that trigonometric polynomials are dense in
C^{r}(S^{1})

for all
0\leq r\leq\infty
. We then rescale them to get
elements in
\mathcal{S}^{r}
. They are not pinching by
Lemma
2.3
. So the main task it to show
that the rescaling
can be done while maintaining their density.

Given
\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{r}
and
\epsilon>0
, let
P
be a nonconstant
trigonometric polynomial with

\|\phi-P\|_{C^{r}}<\delta:=\min(\frac{\epsilon}{4\|\phi\|_{C^{r}}},\frac{\epsilon}{4})

Letting
C=\min(P^{\prime})
then
C<0
and

|1-|C||=|C+1|=|\min P^{\prime}-\min\phi^{\prime}|<\delta.

Thus since
|C|>1/2
,

\|\frac{P}{|C|}-P\|_{C^{r}}=|\frac{1}{|C|}-1|\|P\|_{C^{r}}\leq|\frac{1-|C|}{|C|}|(\|\phi\|_{C^{r}}+\delta)<2\delta(\|\phi\|_{C^{r}}+\delta)

and so

\|\frac{P}{|C|}-\phi\|_{C^{r}}\leq\|\phi-P\|_{C^{r}}+\|P-P/|C|\|_{C^{r}}\leq\delta+2\delta(\|\phi\|_{C^{r}}+\delta)<\epsilon

and

P/|C|
is a trig polynomial in
\mathcal{S}^{r}
and
it is in
A_{p/q,n}
.
∎

6.
the Lipschitz density theorem

Theorem 6.1
.

For each
p/q,n>1
the set

A_{p/q,n}^{Lip}=\{\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{Lip}\colon\tilde{f}_{b,\omega,\phi}^{q}\not=R_{p}\ \text{for all}\ b\in[1/n,1],\omega\}.

(6.1)

is dense in
\mathcal{S}^{Lip}
.

The proof of Theorem
6.1
requires several
lemmas and is spread over several subsections.
Throughout the given
\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{Lip}
is fixed as
are
p/q
and
n
.

6.1.
Discretization

The first step includes a variant on the standard proof that simple functions
are dense in
L^{\infty}
applied to the derivatives of the Lipschitz
function. For an interval
J
,
|J|
is
its length or equivalently, it Lebesgue measure.

Lemma 6.2
.

Given
\epsilon>0
,
\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{Lip}
, and
p/q
there exists a
finite measurable partition
{\mathcal{P}}=\{X_{1},\dots,X_{N}\}
of
S^{1}
with

N>3q
and each
\lambda(X_{j})>0
and weights

{\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}}=({\hat{w}}_{1},\dots,{\hat{w}}_{N})
so that

\hat{\psi}(x):=\phi(0)+\int_{0}^{x}\sum{\hat{w}}_{j}I_{X_{j}}\;d\lambda

is in
\mathcal{S}^{Lip}
and
\|\phi-\hat{\psi}\|_{Lip}<\epsilon
.

Proof.

By the characterization of
\mathcal{S}^{Lip}
,
\essinf\phi^{\prime}=-1
and

C:=\esssup\phi^{\prime}<\infty
. Find half open intervals

K_{i}=[a_{i},b_{i})

for
i=1,\dots N-1
with
K_{1}=[-1,b_{1})

and a closed interval
K_{N}=[a_{N},C]
so that

(1)

If
X_{i}=\{x\in S^{1}:\phi^{\prime}(x)\in K_{i}\}
then

\lambda(X_{i})>0
and
\sum\lambda(X_{i})=1
.

(2)

|K_{i}|<\frac{\epsilon}{2}
.

(3)

N>3q
.

Let
m_{i}=\lambda(X_{i})
and for
i=1,\dots,N
let

w_{i}=\frac{1}{m_{i}}\int_{X_{i}}\phi^{\prime}\;d\lambda

and
W=\sum w_{i}I_{X_{i}}
. Thus

\psi(x)=\phi(0)+\int_{0}^{x}W\;d\lambda.

has
\hat{\psi}^{\prime}=W
a.e. By construction,
\sum m_{i}w_{i}=0

and so
\psi
is periodic. Finally, we show that

\|\phi-\psi\|_{Lip}\leq\epsilon.

Since

w_{i}\in K_{i}
,
\|\phi^{\prime}-\psi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}
.
And for the
C^{0}
part of the Lipschitz norm

\displaystyle\sup|\phi(x)-\hat{\psi}(x)|

\displaystyle=\sup|\phi(0)+\int_{0}^{x}\phi^{\prime}\;d\lambda-(\phi(0)+\int_{0}^{x}W\;d\lambda)|

\displaystyle\leq\sup\int_{0}^{x}|\phi^{\prime}-W|\;d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}|\phi^{\prime}-W|\;d\lambda\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}

The last step in getting a
\hat{\psi}\in\mathcal{S}^{Lip}
is to rescale
\psi
so that

\essinf\hat{\psi}^{\prime}=-1
.
Let
C=|w_{1}|
and
\hat{\psi}=\psi/C
so
\hat{\psi}
has
weights
{\hat{w}}_{i}=w_{i}/C
. By an argument just like
Lemma
5.1
by choosing
\|\phi-\psi\|_{Lip}
small enough we
can make
\|\phi-\hat{\psi}\|_{Lip}
arbitrarily small.
Since
\hat{\psi}=\sum\frac{w_{j}}{C}I_{X_{j}}
and

\sum(w_{i}/Cm_{i})=0
,
\hat{\psi}
is periodic and

\hat{\psi}\in\mathcal{S}^{Lip}
.
∎

Now given
\hat{\psi}
we generate a family

f_{b,\omega,\hat{\psi}}(x)=x+\omega+b\,\hat{\psi}(x).

If
\hat{\psi}
is not pinching, we have proved Theorem
6.1
.
If it is pinching, we fix the partition
{\mathcal{P}}

from Lemma
6.2
and perturb the vector of weights
{\mathbf{w}}

to obtain a new
\psi
that is not pinching.

Given a vector of masses
{\mathbf{m}}
there are two conditions required
for a vector of weights
{\mathbf{w}}

to generate a
\psi_{\mathbf{w}}\in\mathcal{S}^{Lip}
:

\sum m_{j}w_{j}=0\ \text{and}\ w_{1}=-1.

(6.2)

The first guarantees that the resulting
\psi_{\mathbf{w}}

is periodic and the second condition gives
\psi_{\mathbf{w}}\in\mathcal{S}^{Lip}
.
If these two conditions are satisfied, define

\psi_{\mathbf{w}}(x):=\phi(0)+\int_{0}^{x}\sum w_{j}I_{X_{j}}\;d\lambda.

Next we must check that a small perturbation of the
weights yields a small perturbation of the resulting
\psi
.
Given two weight vectors
{\mathbf{u}}
and
{\mathbf{w}}
first note that

\|\psi^{\prime}_{{\mathbf{u}}}-\psi_{{\mathbf{w}}}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}=\|\sum(u_{i}-w_{i})I_{X_{i}}\|_{\infty}=\|{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{w}}\|_{\infty}

To check the
C^{0}
part of the Lipschitz norm

\displaystyle\sup|\phi(0)+\int_{0}^{x}\sum u_{i}I_{X_{i}}\;d\lambda

\displaystyle-(\phi(0)+\int_{0}^{x}\sum w_{i}I_{X_{i}})|\;d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\sup\int_{0}^{x}|\sum(u_{i}-w_{i})|I_{X_{i}}\;d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\sum|u_{i}-w_{i}|I_{X_{i}}\;d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{w}}\|_{\infty}.

Thus

\|\psi_{{\mathbf{u}}}-\psi_{{\mathbf{w}}}\|_{Lip}\leq 2\|{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{w}}\|_{\infty}

(6.3)

which is to say that the map

{\mathbf{w}}\mapsto\psi_{\mathbf{w}}
is
2
-Lipschitz with these norms.
Thus we adopt the strategy of perturbing
{\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}}

to a not pinching
{\mathbf{w}}
satisfying
6.2
.

6.2.
plausible polynomials

Understanding the dynamics of the
f_{b,\omega,\psi_{\mathbf{w}}}
directly
is difficult, but the simple form of
f_{b,\omega,\psi_{\mathbf{w}}}
makes
the derivatives along orbits more tractable. Thus
we first explore the consequences of
(\tilde{f}_{b,\omega,\psi_{\mathbf{w}}})^{q}=R_{p}

for the derivatives and see that

b
and
{\mathbf{w}}
must solve a system of equations. We then
find a perturbation
{\mathbf{w}}
for which no such equations can be solved.

Let
\Lambda_{N}=\{1,\dots,N\}^{q}/S_{N}
where the symmetric group

S_{N}
acts in the standard way on indices. Thus
J\in\Lambda_{N}
can
be described by the elements
j
present and their multiplicity
e_{j}
.
Given
{\mathbf{w}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
,
b\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
J\in\Lambda_{N}
define

G(b,{\mathbf{w}},J)=\prod_{j\in J}(1+bw_{j}).

(6.4)

The definition of
\Lambda_{N}
makes this well-defined.

Definition 6.3
.

Given a vector of weights
{\mathbf{w}}
, an index set

J
is called
(1/n)
-plausible if the equation

G(b,{\mathbf{w}},J)=1

has a solution
b\in[1/n,1]
. The associated polynomial

p(y)=\prod_{j\in J}(1+yw_{j})
is also called

(1/n)
-plausible.

We will need a lemma from Calculus which determines which
polynomials of the form (
6.4
) are
(1/n)
-plausible for
some
n
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let

p(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1+yk_{i})

with
-1\leq k_{1}\leq\dots\leq k_{m}
, and
k_{1}<0<k_{m}
.
Then
p(x)=1
has a solution in
(0,1)
if and only
if
\sum k_{i}>0
and
\prod 1+k_{i}<1
. Further, if there
is a solution
y^{*}
in
(0,1)
, it is unique and
p^{\prime}(y^{*})<0
.

Proof.

Note first that
p(0)=1
,
p(1)=\prod 1+k_{i}
, and
p^{\prime}(0)=\sum k_{i}
.
Further, since all of
p
’s roots (i.e. solutions to
p(y)=0
)
are real, between each adjacent
pair of roots (which may have multiplicity) there is a unique
critical point which is either the maximum or minimum on
the interval between the roots.
There are a pair of adjacent roots for
p
,

x_{L}=-1/\max k_{i}<0
and

x_{R}=-1/\min k_{i}\geq 1
and so there is a critical point
x_{c}\in[x_{L},x_{R}]
.
Since
p(0)=1>0
it is a maximum.

Assume first that
p^{\prime}(0)=\sum k_{i}>0
and
p(1)=\prod 1+k_{i}<1
.
Since now
p(1)<p(0)
, the maximum
x_{c}\in(0,1)
. There then
exists a unique
y^{*}\in(0,1)
with
p(y)=1
, and since
p^{\prime}(0)>0
,

p^{\prime}(y)<0
.

For the converse, say
p^{\prime}(0)=\sum k_{i}\leq 0
. Since there
at most one critical point in
(0,1]
and it is a maximum,

p
is decreasing from
p(0)=1
on that interval and thus

p(x)<1
in
(0,1]

Now say
p(1)=\prod(1+k_{i})>1=p(0)
, then again since
there is at most one critical point, a maximum, in
(0,1]
,

p(x)>1
in
(0,1]
.
∎

In light of the lemma, if
p(y)
is
(1/n)
-plausible,
the unique
y\in[1/n,1]
with
p(y)=1
is
called the
associated solution
.

The next lemma connects
(1/n)
-plausible polynomials
to derivative at pinch points.

Lemma 6.5
.

Assume
(b,\omega,\psi_{\mathbf{w}})
is a
p/q
-pinch point with
b\geq 1/n
,
then there are least three different
(1/n)
-plausable index sets for
{\mathbf{w}}

such that all their associated polynomials

p
have the same solution to
p(y)=1
, namely,
y=b
.

Proof.

Assume
x
is in the good set for a
f_{b,\omega,\psi_{{\mathbf{w}}}}

as defined in (
2.3
).
Since
f_{b,\omega,\psi_{{\mathbf{w}}}}^{\prime}(x)=1+b\psi_{{\mathbf{w}}}^{\prime}(x)=1+bw_{i}

when
x\in X_{i}
, it follows that

1=(f_{b,\omega,\psi_{{\mathbf{w}}}}^{q})^{\prime}(x)=(1+bw_{i_{0}})\dots(1+bw_{i_{q-1}})

(6.5)

where
f_{b,\omega,\psi_{{\mathbf{w}}}}^{j}(x)\in X_{i_{j}}
.
Since
\lambda(X_{i})>0
for all
i
and
N>3q
, as we vary
x
there
must be at least
3
such distinct equations all solved
by the same
b
and each
w_{i}
must occur
in at least one of them. .
∎

We allow the solution
b=1
in the definition of a
(1/n)
-plausible
index set, but it will never occur in a equation like (
6.5
)
for a
\tilde{f}_{b,\omega,\psi_{{\mathbf{w}}}}^{q}=R_{p}
with
\psi_{{\mathbf{w}}}\in\mathcal{S}^{Lip}
. The condition
\psi_{{\mathbf{w}}}\in\mathcal{S}^{Lip}

(recall

)) which means that
\psi_{{\mathbf{w}}}^{\prime}=-1
on

and thus

and so
\tilde{f}_{b,\omega,\psi_{{\mathbf{w}}}}^{q}\not=R_{p}
.

6.3.
The plausible function

Continuing under the assumption that
(\hat{b},\hat{\omega},\psi_{\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}})

is a
p/q
-pinch point
for some

\hat{b}
and
\hat{\omega}

implies that for

(1/n)
-plausible

{\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}}
is nonempty.
Let

for

be the list of
(1/n)
-plausible index sets
for
{\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}}
. For each

define

F_{i}(b,{\mathbf{w}})=G({\mathbf{w}},b,J_{i})

We treat each of these
b
’s as a separate variable

and let

{\mathbf{b}}=(b_{i})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M}
and collect the functions
together into
F=(F_{i})
and so

F:{\mathbb{R}}^{M}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{M}
.
Letting
\mathbbm{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M}

\mathbbm{1}=(1,1,\dots,1)

(1/n)
-plausible

F({\mathbf{b}},{\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}})=\mathbbm{1}

We call this solution
{\hat{\mathbf{b}}}
and so

F({\hat{\mathbf{b}}},{\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}})=\mathbbm{1}.

The goal is to perturb
{\mathbf{w}}
and see its effect on
{\mathbf{b}}
.
We use the implicit function theorem to show that for
({\mathbf{b}},{\mathbf{w}})

near
({\hat{\mathbf{b}}},{\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}})
the set of the solutions to
F({\mathbf{b}},{\mathbf{w}})=\mathbbm{1}

can be described as
F(g({\mathbf{w}}),{\mathbf{w}})=\mathbbm{1}
with

smooth. Thus
we can perturb
{\mathbf{b}}
in a controlled manner by perturbing
{\mathbf{w}}
.

Using Lemma

\alpha_{i}:=\frac{\partial F_{i}}{\partial b_{i}}({\hat{\mathbf{b}}},{\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}})<0\ \text{and}\ \frac{\partial F_{i}}{\partial b_{j}}({\hat{\mathbf{b}}},{\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}})=0\ \text{when}\ j\not=i

and so

\frac{\partial F}{\partial b}({\hat{\mathbf{b}}},{\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}})=\diag(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{M})
which is invertible.
Thus there exists
U
an open neighborhood of
{\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}}
and a
smooth function

so that for
{\mathbf{w}}\in U
,

F({\mathbf{w}},{\mathbf{b}})=\mathbbm{1}
if and only if
{\mathbf{b}}=g({\mathbf{w}})
. Again
by the Implicit Function theorem

A:=\frac{\partial g}{\partial{\mathbf{w}}}({\hat{\mathbf{b}}},{\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}})=-\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial{\mathbf{b}}}({\hat{\mathbf{b}}},{\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}})\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial F}{\partial{\mathbf{w}}}({\hat{\mathbf{b}}},{\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}})

To compute the derivative of
F
with respect to
{\mathbf{w}}

we use an alternative form of the function.
Recall that
J\in\Lambda_{N}
can
be described by the elements
j
present and their multiplicity
e_{j}
. For
a given
J_{i}
, let
\overline{J_{i}}
be the collection of distinct
elements

in

and
e_{i,j}
be their multiplicity. So
if we have representative

then
\overline{J}_{i}=(2,3,4)
with multiplicities

,

, and

.
Thus we are writing

F_{i}({\mathbf{b}},{\mathbf{w}})=\prod_{j\in\overline{J}_{i}}(1+b_{i}w_{j})^{e_{i,j}}

term occurs just once in the product.

Now
F_{i}(\hat{b}_{i},{\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}})=1
and
when

,

\frac{\partial F_{i}}{\partial w_{j}}({\hat{\mathbf{b}}},{\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}})=\hat{b}_{i}e_{i,j}(1+\hat{b}_{i}{\hat{w}}_{j})^{e_{i,j}-1}\prod_{k\in\overline{J}_{i},k\not=j}(1+\hat{b}_{i}{\hat{w}}_{k})^{e_{i,k}}=\frac{\hat{b}_{i}e_{i,j}}{1+\hat{b}_{i}{\hat{w}}_{j}}

and this is zero when

.

Thus,
A_{i,j}=0
when

and when

A_{i,j}=\frac{-\hat{b}_{i}e_{i,j}}{\alpha_{i}(1+\hat{b}_{i}{\hat{w}}_{j})}

(6.6)

While it is admittedly somewhat pedantic it will avoid a certain
amount of confusion to distinguish the tangent spaces
T_{\hat{b}}

and
T_{{\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}}}
at
{\hat{\mathbf{b}}}
and
{\hat{{\mathbf{w}}}}

from the base spaces. We use primes to indicate vectors and points in

.
The exponential maps will both be denoted

and

.

6.4.

Lemma 6.6

For each

7.
PL forcing:
k>2

7.1.
preliminaries

Recall that a degree one circle homeomorphism
g
is piecewise linear (PL)
if it is continuous and comprised of a finite number of affine pieces.
The points where the derivative is discontinuous are called

break points
. The adjectives
ahead
and

behind
for points on the circle are determined
by the usual counterclockwise order on
S^{1}
. Thus the
interval
(a,b)\subset S^{1}
is all the points ahead of
a
and behind

b
. A break point has
type up
if the slope
ahead is larger than that behind, and
type down

otherwise. An
interval of definition
is an interval
between adjacent break points. By convention, any statement
about the derivative of a PL function is implicitly followed
by the phrase “where is it defined”.

7.2.
Characterization of pinch points

Recall that a pinch point on the
p/q
-tongue corresponds to
a
(\omega,b)
with
\tilde{f}_{b,\omega}^{q}=R_{p}
. The first lemma
gives alternative characterizations of such points. The equivalence
of (a) and (b) is standard in the literature, at least for two
break points. The results in this lemma as well as other
characterizations are in
[
12
]
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Assume
g
is a degree one PL-homeomorphism of the circle with

k>1
break points and
\rho(g)=p/q
. The following are equivalent

(a)

\tilde{g}^{q}=R_{p}

(b)

Every
break point of
g
is on
a periodic orbit that contains at least two break points one of
type up and the other of type down.

(c)

There exists a PL homeomorphism
h
with at most

\lfloor\frac{qk}{2}\rfloor

break points and
hgh^{-1}=R_{p/q}
.

(d)

g
has an invariant measure whose density with respect to
Lebesgue measure is a step function with at most

\lfloor\frac{qk}{2}\rfloor
values all nonzero.

Proof.

The fact that (c) implies (a) is trivial. Assume the negation of
(b) holds so there is a break point
x_{0}
for which
no element of
g(x_{0}),g^{2}(x_{0}),\dots,g^{q-1}(x_{0})
is a break point
of opposite orientation. Since
g^{\prime}>0
away from break points, the left and right
hand derivatives of
q^{q}
at
x_{0}
are different, so
g^{q}\not=\Id
. Thus
(a) implies (b).

Now assume (b).
Let
\mathcal{P}
be the collection of
p/q
-periodic orbits of
g

that contain break points. Assume
\mathcal{P}
contains
d
elements
and so

d\leq\lfloor\frac{qk}{2}\rfloor
and
d=mq
where
m

is the number of distinct orbits in
\mathcal{P}
.
The collection of intervals between points
of
\mathcal{P}
form an invariant partition of
S^{1}\setminus\mathcal{P}
.
Pick a point
z_{1}\in\mathcal{P}
and let
z_{2},\dots,z_{m+1}\in\mathcal{P}

be the consecutive points in

\mathcal{P}
ahead of
z_{1}
.
Thus
z_{m+1}
is on the orbit of
z_{1}

and for
1\leq i\leq m
there is one
z_{i}
from each periodic orbit in

\mathcal{P}
. Let
Z_{j}=[z_{j},z_{j+1}]
and

\breve{Z}_{j}=[(j-1)/m,j/m]\subset S^{1}
for
1\leq j\leq m
.
For each such
j
let

h_{1,j}
be the affine
homeomorphism from
Z_{j}
to
\breve{Z}_{j}
.
Now define

h_{i,j}:g^{i}(Z_{j})\rightarrow R_{p/q}^{i}(\breve{Z}_{j})
as

.
Since
g^{q}=\Id
,
the

fit together into a single homeomorphism

with

. By construction

is
PL with at most
d\leq\lfloor\frac{qk}{2}\rfloor
break points.

The equivalence of (c) and (d) is standard. Assuming (c),
the invariant measure
is
(h^{-1})_{*}\lambda=h^{\prime}d\lambda
and since
h
is
PL with
d
break points,

is a step function with

d
nonzero values. If the density of the
invariant measure in (d) is the step function

we have

. Thus

is a PL homeomorphism that preserves Lebesgue
measure and so is rigid rotation. Since it is conjugate to

which has rotation number

, it must be rotation by
p/q
.

∎

The equivalence of (a) and (c) also holds
for a general homeomorphism with a non-PL conjugacy.

The pinching theorem

Lemma 8.1
.

If
b=b_{q,j,w}
for some
1\leq j\leq\tau(q)
and
in addition, for some
\omega
,

x_{d}
is a
p/q
-periodic orbit for
f_{b,\omega,w}
, then

(b,\omega,w)
is a
p/q
-pinch point.

Proof.

The orbit of
x_{d}
divides the circle into an invariant partition by
intervals. The point
x_{u}
is in one of them, say
X_{0}
.
If it is an endpoint, then both break points are on the same
periodic orbit and so
(b,\omega,w)
is a pinch point by
Lemma
7.1
.

The second case is that
x_{u}
is in the interior of
X_{0}
. Let

B_{-1}=X_{-1}\cap X_{0}
and
B_{1}=X_{1}\cap X_{0}
. Thus
for
x_{-1}\in B_{-1}
,
\iota(x_{-1})_{0}=-1
and for

x_{1}\in B_{1}
,
\iota(x_{1})_{0}=1
.
Since the partition elements are permuted, for
1\leq j<q
,

\iota(x_{1})_{j}=\iota(x_{-1})_{j}
. Thus letting
\gamma:=\gamma(x_{1})
,
we have
\gamma(x_{-1})=\gamma+1
and
so by (
8.4
) for all
x\in S^{1}
,
(f_{b,\omega,w}^{q})^{\prime}(x)
is either

p_{q,\gamma,w}(b)
or
p_{q,\gamma+1,w}(b)
.

Since there are only two nonempty
\Lambda_{j}
, the system of equations
(
8.5
) reduces to

\displaystyle\lambda_{\gamma}+\lambda_{\gamma+1}

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle p_{q,\gamma,w}(b)\lambda_{\gamma}+p_{q,\gamma+1,w}(b)\lambda_{\gamma+1}

\displaystyle=1

where we must have
\lambda_{\gamma},\lambda_{\gamma+1}\geq 0

since they are masses of sets. Letting
p_{\gamma}=p_{q,\gamma,w}(b)

the system has solution

\lambda_{\gamma}=\frac{1-p_{\gamma+1}}{p_{\gamma}-p_{\gamma+1}},\ \ \lambda_{\gamma+1}=\frac{p_{\gamma}-1}{p_{\gamma}-p_{\gamma+1}}.

Recall now (
8.3
) and so
p_{\gamma}-p_{\gamma+1}>0
. There
are now a number of cases constrained by (
8.3
). Recall
that the
b=b_{q,j,w}
. If
\gamma>j
, then
p_{\gamma}<p_{j}=1

and so
\lambda_{\gamma+1}<0
, a contradiction. Similarly,

\gamma+1<j
implies the contradiction
\lambda_{\gamma}<0
.
The last cases are
\gamma+1>\gamma=j
and
j=\gamma+1>\gamma
.
Since
p_{j}=1
, the first case implies
\lambda_{\gamma+1}=0

and the second that
\lambda_{\gamma}=0
, so in both cases
only
\Lambda_{j}
is nonempty.

Thus in all cases we have shown that the hypothesis imply that

(f_{b,\omega,w}^{q})^{\prime}\equiv 1
. Since

f_{b,\omega,w}
has a
p/q
-periodic orbit this shows that

(b,\omega,w)
is a
p/q
-pinch point.
∎

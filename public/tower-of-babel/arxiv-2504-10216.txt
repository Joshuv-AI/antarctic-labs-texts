L-point quadrupole order under magnetic field in cubic PrIr 2 Zn 20
Hitoko Okanoya, Kazumasa Hattori

Abstract
We study quadrupole orders in heavy-fermion compound PrIr 2 Zn 20 under magnetic fields on the basis of the Landau theory. Assuming E g E_{g} electric quadrupolar orders in the cubic symmetry with the ordering wavenumber at the L points in the face-centered cubic lattice Brillouin zone as observed experimentally, we construct a Landau free energy and analyze the resulting free energy. We find that the unidentified high-temperature ordered phase under the magnetic field 𝑯 | [ 001 ] \bm{H}\parallel[001] reported earlier arises from the rotation of the quadrupole moments of f electrons on the Pr site. We also discuss the phase diagram for other magnetic-field directions and possible double- 𝒒 \bm{q} quadrupolar orders in this system.

II.1
Exchange interaction

Local moments of f electrons on the Pr sites, which form the diamond structure as shown in Fig.
1
(a), tend to order at low temperatures. This is usually caused by Ruderman-Kittel-Kasuya-Yosida interaction due to the presence of the itinerant electrons in heavy-fermion compounds in general.
To discuss their ordering, it is reasonable to integrate the itinerant electrons and obtain the effective exchange model for the localized f electron moments. Since the local moments play a dominant role in the symmetry breaking orders in these compounds, we use the localized model as a minimal one to understand the nature of their symmetry breakings.
Although it is desirable to obtain the information of the exchange interactions from the excitation spectra by inelastic x-ray or neutron scattering experiments, there is no data available at present. Thus, we use a phenomenological and minimal model which realizes the L point order in this paper.

Since we are interested in the phase transition owing to the order parameters at the L point, we assume the coefficients of the quadratic terms in the free energy at
{\bm{k}}\neq{\bm{k}}_{n}
are always positive. We first discuss the candidates of the stable configurations by analyzing the third- and fourth-order terms in
{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm L}
. Then, we will show the numerical results of full temperature-field phase diagram.

III.1
Qualitative analysis

First, we notice that the inter-sublattice correlations are governed by
J_{\rm L}=-2J_{1}+6J_{3}
for
{\bm{\phi}}_{n}^{\alpha}
in Eq. (
6
). To reproduce the quadrupole configuration propposed by the neutron scattering experiments
[
34
]
,
J_{\rm L}<0
is required. Thus, it is legitimate to set

\displaystyle{\bm{\phi}}_{\rm L}\equiv{\bm{\phi}}_{1}^{A}={\bm{\phi}}_{1}^{B}=\begin{bmatrix}u_{\rm L}\\
v_{\rm L}\end{bmatrix}=\phi_{\rm L}\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_{\rm L}\\
\sin\theta_{\rm L}\end{bmatrix}.

(16)

Here, we have chosen one of the L points at
{\bm{k}}_{1}
as a representative one since the other three fields at
{\bm{k}}_{n}(n=2,3,4)
with
{\bm{\phi}}_{n}^{A}=-{\bm{\phi}}_{n}^{B}
are equivalent and this is also the case even when
\bm{H}
is finite. See Table
1
. For the modes at the
\Gamma
point, which are gapped and just induced by magnetic fields or by the square of the order parameter at the L point, we set

\displaystyle{\bm{\phi}}_{0}\equiv{\bm{\phi}}_{0}^{A}={\bm{\phi}}_{0}^{B}=\begin{bmatrix}u_{0}\\
v_{0}\end{bmatrix}=\phi_{0}\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_{0}\\
\sin\theta_{0}\end{bmatrix}.

(17)

Second, we note that the single-
\bm{q}
free energy
{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm L}
is isotropic. This means that the transition temperatures for
u_{\rm L}
and
v_{\rm L}
are the same at
{\bm{H}}={\bf 0}
and
T_{c}=6J_{4}+2|J_{1}-3J_{3}|
. This isotropic nature, however, is destroyed by the mode coupling to the fields at the
\Gamma
point through
\delta{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
and
\delta{\mathcal{F}}_{4}
.
When
T\lesssim T_{c}
, the induced
{\bm{\phi}}_{0}
is sufficiently small, and thus, the third- and fourth-order terms in
{\mathcal{F}}_{\Gamma}
can be irrelevant. In this situation the relative directions between
{\bm{\phi}}_{n}
and
{\bm{\phi}}_{0}
is determined by
\delta{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
and
\delta{\mathcal{F}}_{4}
as

\displaystyle\cos(\theta_{0}+2\theta_{\rm L})=1\ {\rm and}\ \cos(\theta_{0}-\theta_{\rm L})=0.

(18)

This leads to

\displaystyle\theta_{0}=\frac{(2j-1)\pi}{3},\quad\theta_{\rm L}=\frac{(2j-1)\pi}{3}\pm\frac{\pi}{2},

(19)

with
j=1,2,3
. These six domains are degenerate at

{\bm{H}}={\bf 0}\ (\tilde{\bm{h}}={\bf 0})
. See Fig.
3
(a).
We should comment that the three directions of
{\bm{\phi}}_{0}
do not coincide with those favored by the
b(>0)
term in
{\mathcal{F}}_{\Gamma}:-b\phi_{0}^{3}\cos(3\theta_{0})
. Since
{\bm{\phi}}_{0}
is not the primary order parameter, the third-order term is expected to play a minor role and the configurations (
19
) are robust at least
\tilde{\bm{h}}={\bf 0}
.

Now let us consider the cases for finite magnetic fields. For high symmetric directions
\bm{H}=\tfrac{H}{\sqrt{2}}(\sin\Theta,\sin\Theta,\sqrt{2}\cos\Theta)
with
0\leq\Theta\leq\pi/2
, the effective field reads as

\tilde{h}_{u}=gH^{2}(3\cos^{2}\Theta-1)
and
\tilde{h}_{v}=0
. Note that
\tilde{\bm{h}}={\bf 0}
at
\bm{H}\parallel[111]
and
\tilde{h}_{u}>0
for
\Theta<\tan^{-1}\sqrt{2}\equiv\Theta_{111}
, while
\tilde{h}_{u}<0
for
\Theta>\Theta_{111}
. The effective field induces
u_{0}
, which leads to two “transition temperatures”
\tilde{T}_{c}^{u}
for the
u_{\rm L}
order and
\tilde{T}_{c}^{v}
for
v_{\rm L}
. They are given by

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{c}^{u}

\displaystyle=T_{c}+6\lambda_{b}u_{0}-3\lambda_{c}u_{0}^{2},\ \tilde{T}_{c}^{v}=T_{c}-6\lambda_{b}u_{0}-\lambda_{c}u_{0}^{2},

(20)

where we have set
\lambda_{c}^{(1)}=\lambda_{c}^{(2)}=\lambda_{c}
for simplicity.
Note that
\tilde{T}_{c}^{u,v}
is a hypothetical transition temperature from the disordered state. The difference between the two is
\Delta\tilde{T}_{c}=\tilde{T}_{c}^{u}-\tilde{T}_{c}^{v}=2\lambda_{c}(\phi_{0}^{*}-u_{0})u_{0}
with
\phi_{0}^{*}\equiv 6\lambda_{b}/\lambda_{c}
. For
u_{0}>0
, this means
\tilde{T}_{c}^{u}>\tilde{T}_{c}^{v}
for
u_{0}<\phi_{0}^{*}
, i.e., for low fields, while
\tilde{T}_{c}^{v}>\tilde{T}_{c}^{u}
for the higher field with
u_{0}>\phi_{0}^{*}
. In contrast,
\Delta\tilde{T}_{c}<0
for
u_{0}<0
.

As is clear from the expression of

\Delta\tilde{T}_{c}
, it is expected
that there is no additional phase
transition within the single-
\bm{q}

{\bm{\phi}}_{n}
sector for
u_{0}<0
, i.e.,

\Theta>\Theta_{111}
.
In the following, we will
concentrate on the cases for

u_{0}>0
(
\Theta<\Theta_{111}
).
For
u_{0}>0
, the linear coupling between

{\bm{\phi}}_{0}
and
\tilde{h}_{u}
can alter
the stable configuration from the
above six domains (
19
)
since their energy
gain is given by the second order in

{\bm{\phi}}_{0}
.
Since
\Delta\tilde{T}_{c}>0
, the phase transition from the disordered phase occurs in the
u_{\rm L}
component:
\theta_{\rm L}=0
or
\pi
. Here, the two choices
0
and
\pi
represent a simple translation and we will consider
\theta_{\rm L}=0
below.
For small
\tilde{h}_{u}
,
\delta{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
is more important than
\delta{\mathcal{F}}_{4}
. This means
\theta_{0}=0
satisfying
2\theta_{\rm L}+\theta_{0}=0
[Fig.
3
(b)]. Such a
collinear state possesses the free
energy
{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm col}
and the
fluctuations around this is given as

\displaystyle\delta{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm col}

\displaystyle\simeq\lambda_{c}\phi_{0}\phi_{\rm L}^{2}\begin{bmatrix}\theta_{0}&\theta_{\rm L}\end{bmatrix}\hat{A}\begin{bmatrix}\theta_{0}\\
\theta_{\rm L}\end{bmatrix},

(21)

\displaystyle\hat{A}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}(\lambda_{c}\phi_{\rm L}^{2})^{-1}(\tilde{h}_{u}+9b\phi_{0}^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\phi_{0}^{*}-2\phi_{0}&\phi_{0}^{*}+2\phi_{0}\\
\phi_{0}^{*}+2\phi_{0}&2\phi_{0}^{*}-2\phi_{0}\end{bmatrix}.

(22)

Indeed, the instability to finite
\theta_{{\rm L},0}
occurs when one of the eigenvalue of the matrix
\hat{A}
in Eq. (
22
) becomes zero. This takes place when

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{u}^{c1}=27\lambda_{b}\frac{\phi_{0}}{\phi_{0}^{*}-\phi_{0}}\phi_{\rm L}^{2}-9b\phi_{0}^{2}.

(23)

Note that the factor
\phi_{0}^{*}-\phi_{0}
of the denominator coincides with the factor in
\Delta T_{c}
. The configuration with
\theta_{0}\neq 0
and
\theta_{\rm L}\neq 0
gradually crosses over to the configuration for
\bm{H}=0
. See Fig.
3
(c).
When
\tilde{\bm{h}}
is sufficiently large,
u_{0}
grows and
\delta{\mathcal{F}}_{4}
plays an important role. Thus,
{\bm{\phi}}_{\rm L}
is locked at
\theta_{\rm L}=\pm\pi/2
as shown in Fig.
3
(d). This is because
\delta{\mathcal{F}}_{4}>\delta{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
for large
\tilde{h}_{u}
. See Eq. (
18
).
By the similar analysis to Eqs. (
21
)–(
23
), the boundary determined by the field below which
|\theta_{\rm L}|<\pi/2
[Fig.
3
(e)] is given as

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{u}^{c2}=27\lambda_{b}\frac{\phi_{0}}{\phi_{0}-\phi_{0}^{*}}\phi_{\rm L}^{2}-9b\phi_{0}^{2}.

(24)

For sufficiently large
\lambda_{b}
, the
\theta_{\rm L}=\pm\pi/2
phase never appears because of
\tilde{T}_{c}^{v}<0
. Of course, for extremely large
\tilde{\bm{h}}
,
the L-point order vanishes trivially.

Finally, we briefly comment about the cases for
u_{0}<0
(
\Theta>\Theta_{111}
) under finite magnetic fields. As we have noted, there is no phase transition once a quadrupole moment with
\theta_{\rm L}=\pm\pi/2
is ordered as shown in Fig.
3
(f). This is because
\theta_{\rm L}=\pm\pi/2
and
\theta_{0}=\pi
can minimize
\delta{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
,
\delta{\mathcal{F}}_{4}
, and
{\mathcal{F}}_{\Gamma}
, simultaneously.

IV
Results: multiple-q orders

In this section, we consider multiple-
\bm{q}
orders for the quadrupole moments at the L points. We will first introduce a simple symmetric multiple-
\bm{q}
states with the same amplitudes for all the
\bm{q}_{n}
in Sec.
IV.1
. Then, we will show numerical results, where we relax the constraint about the amplitudes of the order parameters.

IV.1
Free energy for symmetric states

We consider double-
\bm{q}\ (\mathcal{N}=2)
and quadruple-
\bm{q}\ (\mathcal{N}=4)
configurations, whose real space moments are given by

{\bm{\phi}}^{\alpha}(\bm{r})=\begin{bmatrix}u^{\alpha}_{\bm{r}}\\
v^{\alpha}_{\bm{r}}\end{bmatrix}=\frac{\Phi_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}}}\sum_{n=1}^{\mathcal{N}}e^{i{\bm{k}}_{n}\cdot\bm{r}}\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_{n}^{\alpha}\\
\sin\theta_{n}^{\alpha}\\
\end{bmatrix},

(25)

where

\bm{r}
is labeled by the
A
-sublattice position as before and we have assumed that the magnitude for each
{\bm{k}}_{n}
is the same
\Phi_{\alpha}/\sqrt{\mathcal{N}}~(>0)
for simplicity (symmetric states).
For
n=1

\bm{\phi}^{A}_{1}=\bm{\phi}^{B}_{1}
, while, for
n=2,3
and
4
,
\bm{\phi}^{A}_{n}=-\bm{\phi}^{B}_{n}
. As mentioned before, this is due to our unit cell convention. In this normalization
(\mathcal{N})
, the quadratic part is unchanged from the single-
\bm{q}
expression. Thus, types of order realized below the second-order transition at
T=T_{c}
are determined by the fourth-order terms.

For double-
\bm{q}
states, the fourth-order terms are

{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm L}^{2\bm{q}(4)}=\sum_{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}^{4}\left[\frac{c}{8}+\frac{c^{\prime}}{16}+\frac{c^{\prime\prime}}{16}(c^{\alpha}_{12})^{2}\right].

(26)

Here,
c^{\alpha}_{mn}\equiv\cos(\theta^{\alpha}_{m}-\theta^{\alpha}_{n})
.
This clearly indicates that
\theta_{1}^{\alpha}-\theta_{2}^{\alpha}=\pm\pi/2
for
c^{\prime\prime}>0
(orthogonal double-
\bm{q}
), while
\theta_{1}^{\alpha}-\theta_{2}^{\alpha}=0
or
\pi
for
c^{\prime\prime}<0
(parallel double-
\bm{q}
). As explained in Appendix
A
, the double-
\bm{q}
configurations consist of four ferroic chains running along the direction of
{\bm{k}}_{3}+{\bm{k}}_{4}
when only the
A
-sublattice sites are concerned.

For quadruple-
\bm{q}
states,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm L}^{4\bm{q}(4)}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}^{4}\Bigg[\frac{c}{16}+\frac{3c^{\prime}}{32}+\frac{c^{\prime\prime}}{64}\sum_{m<n}(c^{\alpha}_{mn})^{2}\Bigg].

(27)

Note that the
c^{\prime\prime\prime}
terms cancel when summing up the terms for
\alpha=A
and
B
, since
\phi^{A}_{n}=(-1)^{p_{n}}\phi^{B}_{n}
. The minimum value of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm L}^{4\bm{q}(4)}
for
c^{\prime\prime}>0
is realized with
\sum_{m<n}(c^{\alpha}_{mn})^{2}=2
, where two orthogonal pairs with the relative angle between the two is arbitrary, e.g.,
(\theta^{\alpha}_{1},\theta^{\alpha}_{2},\theta^{\alpha}_{3},\theta^{\alpha}_{4})=(\theta^{\alpha}+\pi/2,\theta^{\alpha},\varphi^{\alpha}+\pi/2,\varphi^{\alpha})
. For
c^{\prime\prime}<0
, the configurations for the minimum free energy is, e.g.,
(\theta^{\alpha}_{1},\theta^{\alpha}_{2},\theta^{\alpha}_{3},\theta^{\alpha}_{4})=(\theta,\theta,\theta,\theta)
with
\sum_{m<n}(c^{\alpha}_{mn})^{2}=6
. The unit cell for the quadrupole-
\bm{q}
configurations contains eight
A
-sublattice sites with sixteen sites in total. See also Fig.
9
in Appendix
A
.

Figure
5
shows the phase diagram at
T=T_{c}
on the
c^{\prime\prime}
–
c^{\prime}
plane for
c>0
. Note that there is no
c^{\prime\prime\prime}
dependence. For large
c^{\prime}
, multiple-
\bm{q}
states are not realized as is evident from Eqs. (
26
) and (
27
). The conditions given by Eq. (
8
) just correspond to the point
(c^{\prime}/c,c^{\prime\prime}/c)=(2,4)
, where the single-
\bm{q}
and double-
\bm{q}
orders are degenerate. As long as the local fourth-order terms are dominant, quadruple-
\bm{q}
orders are unlikely. Thus, the
c^{\prime}
term is important when one compares the free energy for the single- and double-
\bm{q}
orders.

So far, we have treated the fourth-order coefficients
c
’s as phenomenological parameters. Here, we discuss the renormalization owing to modes other than
\bm{\phi}_{0}
and
\bm{\phi}_{n}
. In the lowest orders, the cubic terms in Eq. (
5
) are the most important. Among them, there are special terms which connect two L-point modes at
\bm{q}_{m}
and
\bm{q}_{n}
(
m\neq n
) via
\sim\phi_{\bm{k}}\phi_{m}\phi_{n}
, where
{\bm{k}}
is one of the wavenumbers at the X points
\bm{q}_{\rm X}=(2\pi,0,0),\bm{q}_{\rm Y}=(0,2\pi,0)
, or
\bm{q}_{\rm Z}=(0,0,2\pi)
. The combination of
({\bm{k}},\bm{q}_{m},\bm{q}_{n})
is restricted by the Umklapp condition
{\bm{k}}+\bm{q}_{m}+\bm{q}_{n}\equiv\bf{0}
and there are two sets of
(m,n)
for a given
{\bm{k}}
. As shown in Appendix
B
, these couplings lead to suppression of the coefficients
c^{\prime}
and
c^{\prime\prime\prime}
. See Eq. (
38
). Furthermore, the fifth-order couplings between the
\Gamma
- and the L-points fields appear after integrating out the X-point fields as
\sim\phi_{0}\phi_{m}^{2}\phi_{n}^{2}
and
\sim\phi_{0}\phi_{1}\phi_{2}\phi_{3}\phi_{4}
. They play an important role under magnetic fields
\bm{H}
since
\phi_{0}
is induced by
\bm{H}
. Thus, these terms renormalize
c
’s with varying their magnitude depending on
\bm{H}
.

The suppression of
c^{\prime}
and
c^{\prime\prime\prime}
due to the renormalization with retaining
c^{\prime\prime}>0
suggests that the orthogonal double-
\bm{q}
states are more stable. However, the coupling between the fields at the
\Gamma
and the L
points possesses an energy gain for the single-
\bm{q}
orders and double-
\bm{q}
ones with
\phi_{m}\neq\phi_{n}
. This is because there is a finite
\phi_{0}
induced in the single-
\bm{q}
states, while there is no gain for the double-
\bm{q}
states with
\phi_{n}=\phi_{m}
and
\theta_{n}-\theta_{m}=\pm\pi/2
. See the coupling in Eq. (
13
). As we will see in Sec.
IV.2
, the fifth-order terms generated by the integration of the X-points fields choose some particular
\theta_{n}
’s among degenerate orthogonal double-
\bm{q}
configurations.

V
Discussions

So far, we have shown how the L point quadrupole orders evolve under magnetic fields by using the Landau analysis. In this section, we will compare these results with the experimental data of PrIr
2
Zn
20
and the related compounds. We also discuss the similarity between other quadrupole orders in Pr-based 1-2-20 compounds such as the Néel type antiferroic orders and those at the X points from theoretical point of view. In the last part of this section, we will comment on the future perspectives toward full understanding of the quadrupole orders in PrIr
2
Zn
20
.

V.1
Comparison with the experimental data of PrIr
2
Zn
20

PrIr
2
Zn
20
shows several phase transitions under magnetic field at low temperatures as reported in the early study
[
31
]
. However, the order parameters have not been identified except for the
x^{2}
-
y^{2}
type quadrupole order with the ordering wave number at the L point for
\bm{H}\parallel[110]
by the neutron scattering experiment
[
34
]
. The analysis of this study is based on this experimental fact. Namely, we have assumed that the leading order parameter is the quadrupole moment at the L points. Constructing the symmetry-allowed free energy consisting of them and the related degrees of freedom, we have analyzed the phase diagram for
\bm{H}\parallel[001]
in addition to that for
\bm{H}\parallel[110]
. Our analysis demonstrates that a
2z^{2}
-
x^{2}
-
y^{2}
configuration with the ordering wave number at the L point is stable at high-
T
and up to intermediate magnetic field strength for
\bm{H}\parallel[001]
. This suggests that the
2z^{2}
-
x^{2}
-
y^{2}
order can be a promising candidate for the high-
T
phase found for
\bm{H}\parallel[001]
in the experimental works
[
31
,
35
]
.
As for the order parameter at zero field, the single-
\bm{q}
configuration is not stable while the double-
\bm{q}
I
2q
is realized with
\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}
. For finite
{\bm{H}}\parallel[001]
, this double-
\bm{q}
configuration is modified and
\bm{\phi}_{1}=(\phi_{1},0)
and
\bm{\phi}_{2}=(0,\phi_{2})
with
\phi_{1}>\phi_{2}
is favored.
\phi_{2}
increases as lowering
T
and there appear phase II
2q
and III
2q
by simultaneously rotating
\bm{\phi}_{1}
and
\bm{\phi}_{2}
. As the cubic anisotropy
b
increases, the influence of the renormalized fourth-order coefficients becomes larger and favors the phase II
2q
and III
2q
.

As magnetic field increases, the double-
\bm{q}
order is destabilized and single-
\bm{q}
orders take place as discussed in Sec.
III
. Thus, as increasing
\bm{H}\parallel[001]
, the double-
\bm{q}
, the single-
\bm{q}
phase II with both
u_{\rm L}
and
v_{\rm L}
finite, and then the phase III with only
v_{\rm L}
are realized. The trace of the phase boundaries between them has not been clearly observed in the experiments. However, there are several anomalies in the ultrasonic experiments
[
32
]
. We hope the relation between our phase diagram and these anomalies can be clarified in future experimental studies. In addition to this, there is ambiguity about the stability of the phase III, whether the phase III extends toward the higher field as shown in Fig.
4
(c) or occupies only small area [Fig.
4
(a)]. This can be controlled by the cubic coupling
b
. For
\bm{H}\parallel[110]
, there is no high-
T
phase in contrast to the case for
\bm{H}\parallel[001]
in the experiments
[
31
,
35
]
. This point is consistent with our results. Other anomalies observed in the ultrasound experiments
[
32
]
similar to those for
\bm{H}\parallel[001]
are also interesting to examine in more detail in the future works. Since the anomalies are tiny and thus the comparison between the experiments and the present theory is not straightforward, while there is a phase boundary between the phase I
2q
and IV in Figs.
6
(c),
6
(d), and
8
(b), irrespectively to the detail of the parameters.

We also note that the phase diagram as a function of magnetic field from
\bm{H}\parallel[001]
to
\bm{H}\parallel[110]
is described in a function of angle
\Theta
, where
\bm{H}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}H(\sin\Theta,\sin\Theta,\sqrt{2}\cos\Theta)
. In Eq. (
12
), the effective field to the quadrupole moments is written as
\tilde{\bm{h}}=gH^{2}(3\cos^{2}\Theta-1,0)
. Thus, the critical field of quadrupole orders for the angle
\Theta
,
H_{c}(\Theta)
is expected to be

\displaystyle H_{c}(\Theta)=\frac{\sqrt{2}H_{c}(0)}{\sqrt{3\cos^{2}\Theta-1}}.

(28)

Such behavior can be one of strong evidences for quadrupole orders and indeed have been observed recently
[
41
]
.

V.2
Comparison with quadrupole orders at the
\Gamma
point and the X points

In the previous studies, quadrupole orders in Pr-based 1-2-20 compounds have been theoretically analyzed in several types of ordering wavenumbers. In the early stage, simple Néel type quadrupole orders
[
21
,
23
]
are analyzed and
T
–
H
phase diagrams show similar order parameter switching as discussed in the analyses of single-
\bm{q}
sates shown in Fig.
4
. This is because the form of the Landau’s free energy for both cases are almost the same when the multiple-
\bm{q}
configurations are neglected.

For PrTi
2
Al
20
, the experiments clarify the order is ferroic
[
20
,
24
,
25
]
and its unusual behavior in the quadrupole switching under magnetic fields is analyzed by field-induced interactions unique to the quadrupole system
[
24
]
. In this study such induced interactions are not taken into account. We do not consider they are important in PrIr
2
Zn
20
since they require specific electronic properties such as existence of small pocket Fermi surfaces.

For PrV
2
Al
20
, there are two transitions at zero magnetic field. The high-temperature transition is considered to be a quadrupole order, while ferroic octupole moments emerge below the lower transition temperature
[
42
,
27
]
.
The Landau analysis for a model including the octupole moment in addition to the quadrupole moments
[
28
]
and Monte Carlo simulations with extended interactions have been done
[
29
,
26
]
. These theories explain the finite octupole moment as a consequence of symmetry breaking of ferroic octupole order. The lack of microscopic observation of both quadrupole and octupole moments (even their ordering wavenumbers) prevents theoretical investigation from further analyses. As an alternative approach, it has been pointed out that if the high-temperature transition is that into triple-
\bm{q}
quadrupole order at the X points suggested by the band structure calculation
[
43
]
, the lower temperature transition is expected to be triple-
\bm{q}
octupole order at the same wavenumbers with induced ferroic octupole moments
[
30
]
.

The physics of the L point quadrupole order is distinct from the ferroic and the X-point quadrupole moments in particular in the absence of cubic invariant consisting solely of the order parameters since the sum of three ordering wavenumbers lead to
{\bm{k}}_{1}+{\bm{k}}_{2}+{\bm{k}}_{3}\neq{\bm{0}}
. Although the Néel type order parameters do not possess such cubic invariant, they have trivially no multiple-
\bm{q}
orders. In this sense, the present results including double-
\bm{q}
quadrupole orders and the phase transitions from/to the single-
\bm{q}
orders are unique properties for the L point quadrupole systems.

V.3
Future perspectives

We have shown that single- and double-
\bm{q}
quadrupole orders are realized in our study as a phenomenological model for PrIr
2
Zn
20
. The orthogonal double-
\bm{q}
orders are favored by the effective fourth-order terms arising from the renormalization due to the X-point fields, while the quadruple ones are not. Although we are interested in the orders at the L points in this paper, quadruple-
\bm{q}
orders at incommensurate ordering wave numbers along [111] directions has been studied in cubic magnets, showing hedgehog type topological magnetic structure
[
44
,
45
]
. In principle, such exotic phases are also possible for the orbital model discussed in this paper when the exchange coupling favors incommensurate ordering wave numbers by introducing interaction frustration or anisotropic interaction ignored in this study
[
46
]
.
In this sense, it is interesting to explore possibilities of incommensurate orders as the candidates for the high-
T
and intermediate field phase instead of the phase I proposed in this paper as studied in magnetic orders
[
40
,
47
,
48
]
.

For quantitative comparison between the theoretical and experimental results, it is also needed to incorporate the actual crystal electric field states in the real
materials. They vary under magnetic fields and thus the parameters in the free energy can change. Recent ultrasonic experiments
[
49
]
detected rotational effects and its microscopic analysis is also interesting. For theoretical or computational aspects, it is also interesting to take into account the fluctuation effects by using e.g., classical Monte Carlo simulation employed in the previous studies on Pr-based materials
[
23
,
29
,
26
]
. The thermal fluctuation may alter the mean-field phase diagram and to check their stability is important issue as one of our future problems
[
50
]
. For example, our analysis of mode-mode coupling is restricted to those with the X-point modes via the cubic terms. This may cause overestimation of double-
\bm{q}
orders. In this sense, the numerical simulation can clarify the effects of these fluctuations. The influence of the non-Fermi liquid properties
[
20
,
33
,
51
,
18
]
on the symmetry breaking, which is completely ignored in this study, is also nontrivial and important subject.

VI
Summary

We have analyzed quadrupole orders with its modulation characterized by the wavenumber at the L points. Their Landau free energy is constructed under magnetic fields
\bm{H}
. We find that the phase diagrams show strong anisotropy. We have clarified that
x^{2}
-
y^{2}
type quadrupole order is stabilized along
\bm{H}\parallel[110]
, which is consistent with that proposed in the neutron scattering experiment. We also propose that the unidentified high-temperature phase for
\bm{H}\parallel[001]
in the experiments is a collinear
2z^{2}
-
x^{2}
-
y^{2}
order and the orthogonal double-
\bm{q}
configurations are stable at low fields. These results offer further experimental examination of the order parameters in PrIr
2
Zn
20
and related compounds.

Appendix B
Renormalization effects due to the fields at the X points

In this Appendix, we consider the influence of the modes
\bm{Q}_{\ell}^{\alpha}=(Q_{\ell,z}^{\alpha},Q_{\ell,x}^{\alpha})
(
\ell=
X, Y, Z) at the X points:

\bm{q}_{\rm X}=(2\pi,0,0),\ \bm{q}_{\rm Y}=(0,2\pi,0)

\bm{q}_{\rm Z}=(0,0,2\pi)

\bm{q}_{1}+\bm{q}_{2}+\bm{q}_{\rm X}\equiv\bf{0}

The free energy for the

{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm X}=\sum_{\alpha=A,B}{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm X}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha}_{\rm X}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell={\rm X,Y,Z}}\bm{Q}^{\alpha}_{\ell}\begin{bmatrix}a_{\rm X}-6bu_{0}^{\alpha}&6bv_{0}^{\alpha}\\
6bv_{0}^{\alpha}&a_{\rm X}+6bu_{0}^{\alpha}\end{bmatrix}\bm{Q}^{\alpha}_{\ell}

\displaystyle-6b\sum_{\ell={\rm X,Y,Z}}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\Big[Q_{\ell,z}^{\alpha}C_{\ell i}^{\alpha}-Q_{\ell,x}^{\alpha}S_{\ell i}^{\alpha}\Big]+\cdots.

(32)

Here, the fourth-order terms and the cubic term for three X-point fields are neglected since the modes at the X points are not critical.
a_{\rm X}
and
C
’s and
S
’s consisting of two L-point variables are given as

\displaystyle a_{\rm X}\equiv T-4J_{2}+6J_{4},

(33)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&C_{\ell 1}^{\alpha}=u_{m}^{\alpha}u_{n}^{\alpha}-v_{m}^{\alpha}v_{n}^{\alpha},\quad C_{\ell 2}^{\alpha}=u_{m^{\prime}}^{\alpha}u_{n^{\prime}}^{\alpha}-v_{m^{\prime}}^{\alpha}v_{n^{\prime}}^{\alpha},\\
&S_{\ell 1}^{\alpha}=u_{m}^{\alpha}v_{n}^{\alpha}+v_{m}^{\alpha}u_{n}^{\alpha},\quad S_{\ell 2}^{\alpha}=u_{m^{\prime}}^{\alpha}v_{n^{\prime}}^{\alpha}+v_{m^{\prime}}^{\alpha}u_{n^{\prime}}^{\alpha},\end{split}

(34)

where
(\ell,m,n)=({\rm X},1,2),\ ({\rm Y},1,4),\ ({\rm Z},1,3)

(\ell,m^{\prime},n^{\prime})=({\rm X},3,4),\ ({\rm Y},2,3),\ ({\rm Z},2,4)
.
By minimizing
{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm X}
with respect to the X-point fields, we obtain their stationary values as

\displaystyle Q_{\ell,z}^{\alpha}=\frac{6b}{a_{\rm X}}\sum_{i=1}^{2}C^{\alpha}_{{\ell}i},\quad Q^{\alpha}_{\ell,x}=-\frac{6b}{a_{\rm X}}\sum_{i=1}^{2}S^{\alpha}_{{\ell}i}.

(35)

Substituting them into Eq. (
32
), we obtain up to the linear order in
\phi_{0}

\delta{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm{XL}}=\sum_{\alpha={\rm A,B}}\delta{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha}_{\rm{XL}0}+\delta{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha}_{\rm{XL}1}+\cdots

\displaystyle\delta{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha}_{\rm{XL}0}=

\displaystyle-\frac{18b^{2}}{a_{\rm X}}\sum_{m>n}(\phi^{\alpha}_{m}\phi^{\alpha}_{n})^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{36b^{2}}{a_{\rm X}}(c_{12}^{\alpha}c_{34}^{\alpha}+c_{13}^{\alpha}c_{24}^{\alpha}+c_{14}^{\alpha}c_{23}^{\alpha})\phi^{\alpha}_{1}\phi^{\alpha}_{2}\phi^{\alpha}_{3}\phi^{\alpha}_{4},

(36)

and

\displaystyle\delta{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm{XL}1}^{\alpha}=

\displaystyle-\frac{108b^{3}}{a_{\rm X}^{2}}\Bigg[\sum_{m>n}\cos(2\theta^{\alpha}_{mn}-\theta_{0}^{\alpha})(\phi^{\alpha}_{m}\phi^{\alpha}_{n})^{2}\phi_{0}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle+6\cos(\bar{\theta}^{\alpha}-\theta_{0}^{\alpha})\phi^{\alpha}_{1}\phi^{\alpha}_{2}\phi^{\alpha}_{3}\phi^{\alpha}_{4}\phi_{0}^{\alpha}\Bigg],

(37)

where
\bm{\phi}_{0}^{\alpha}=(u_{0}^{\alpha},v_{0}^{\alpha})=\phi^{\alpha}_{0}(\cos\theta^{\alpha}_{0},\sin\theta^{\alpha}_{0})
and
\theta_{mn}\equiv\theta_{m}+\theta_{n}
and
\bar{\theta}^{\alpha}\equiv\theta_{1}^{\alpha}+\theta_{2}^{\alpha}+\theta_{3}^{\alpha}+\theta_{4}^{\alpha}
.
Equation (
36

c^{\prime}
and
c^{\prime\prime\prime}

\displaystyle c^{\prime}\to c^{\prime}-\frac{72b^{2}}{a_{\rm X}},\quad c^{\prime\prime\prime}\to c^{\prime\prime\prime}-\frac{144b^{2}}{a_{\rm X}}.

(38)

The terms appearing in
\delta{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm{XL}1}^{\alpha}
are emergent fifth-order coupling constants between the mode at the
\Gamma
and the L points. Since we have kept terms up to the fourth orders in the original free energy in Eq. (

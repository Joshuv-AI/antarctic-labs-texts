Metamaterial absorber using cascaded ring resonators and optimization through machine learning for sensing applications
Authors: Mohammad Reza Rakhshani, Fatemeh Kazemi, Mahdi Rashki
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this research, a narrowband metamaterial absorber (MA) is designed using a cascaded ring resonator (RR) with a Metal–Insulator–Metal (MIM) structure. Conventional design methodologies such as finite-difference time-domain (FDTD) method often rely on trial-and-error simulations, which are both computationally intensive and time-consuming. To address this challenge, we present a design framework based on the Adaptive Neuro–Fuzzy Inference System (ANFIS). This hybrid approach, which combines neural networks and fuzzy logic, offers a cost-effective and time-efficient alternative to FDTD optical simulations. This type of hybrid network is recognized for its capability to represent highly nonlinear and complex samples. To predict the absorption through the ANFIS, four geometrical parameters of the structure have been utilized as input variables. The performance of this model is assessed by employing a root mean square error (RMSE) and mean absolute error (MAE) as a criteria for the evaluation, with two membership functions (MF) and a 5-fold cross-validation. The RMSE (0.0811) and MAE (0.062) indicate that absorption can be predicted with high precision. Based on our results, ANFIS achieves approximately 90% time savings compared to the conventional FDTD method, while maintaining acceptable prediction accuracy. Furthermore, the comparison of our method with Random Forest and k-Nearest Neighbor (KNN) shows that ANFIS yields lower prediction error. The results indicate that the proposed MA can achieve nearly perfect absorption at specific values of the geometrical parameters. As a key application, the proposed structure has been utilized as a sensor. The optimal results for two key factors are a sensitivity of 600 nm per refractive index unit (RIU) and a full-width at half-maximum (FWHM) of 4 nm. Due to its compact size, perfect absorption, and tunability, the proposed MA offers diverse applications in solar cells, sensors, and stealth technology.Subject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Mathematics and computing, Optics and photonics

Introduction

In recent years, efforts to achieve a higher integration density in photonic components have grown rapidly1. Surface plasmon polaritons (SPPs) are transverse electromagnetic (EM) waves that propagate along a metal/insulator boundary while exhibiting an exponentially decaying amplitude perpendicular to the interface2–4. Owing to their remarkable capabilities, such as manipulating light at sub-wavelength scales and surpassing the diffraction limit, surface plasmon-based structures have found widespread use in a variety of applications5,6.

Plasmonic Metamaterials consist of subwavelength periodic structures, in which each unit cell plays a crucial role in defining the material’s overall electromagnetic behavior. Unlike conventional materials, their properties are dictated more by the engineered geometry and layout of these units than by the inherent characteristics of the base materials. The emergence of plasmonic metamaterials has given rise to numerous innovative applications across various domains such as filters7,8, solar photovoltaic9, splitters10, sensors11–13, couplers14, thermal imaging15, light modulation16, invisibility cloaks17, superlenses18, advanced antennas19,20, absorbers21,22, and so on.

The enhancement of light absorption has attracted considerable attention in practical applications. Traditional absorption devices encounter substantial challenges stemming from material constraints, limited angular tolerance, and limited operational wavelength range. Among various configurations to enhance light absorption, owing to their engineered characteristics, metamaterial-based absorbers can substantially enhance absorption efficiency23,24. SPP-based metamaterials enable simultaneous manipulation of permittivity and permeability by locally engineering unit cells to achieve effective impedance matching between the structure and free space.

Metamaterials, especially the metal/dielectric/metal (MIM) configuration, have been extensively investigated due to their potential to achieve near-unity absorption across targeted spectral ranges25. In a typical MIM configuration, a metallic substrate is combined with periodically patterned metal patches, separated by a dielectric layer26. Metamaterial absorbers (MAs) can be used for various applications by tuning their absorption bands according to specific wavelength ranges. For instance, Berka et al. proposed MAs operating in the visible spectrum27, Dong et al. designed MAs for microwave frequencies28, and Shao et al. introduced MAs tailored for the terahertz range29.

MAs can exhibit either broadband or narrowband spectrum, depending on their structural design and constituent materials. Narrowband MAs are typically employed in sensing and absorption filtering applications30, whereas broadband MAs are predominantly utilized in energy harvesting systems, including photovoltaics31. Wideband MAs are typically characterized by excessive layer thickness and large footprint. Moreover, they are highly fabrication-intensive, which restricts their feasibility for practical implementation32.

Different narrow-band MA configurations have been introduced and investigated by researchers. Li et al. designed a narrow band MA comprising a tungsten disulfide nano-disk array sitting on top of a gold film, achieving perfect absorption with a full-width at half-maximum (FWHM) of 15 nm in the near-infrared region33. Xie et al. proposed a narrow-band perfect MA, consisting of a silicon nano-disk array positioned atop a thin gold layer, with a dielectric spacer in between. The design achieves near-perfect absorption (> 99%) with an FWHM of 5 nm34. Ali et al. introduced an MA based on a MIM configuration, comprising a thin spacer positioned between a thick gold substrate and an array of uniformly patterned gold nanodiscs35. They reported near-perfect absorption with an FWHM of approximately 15 nm. Ring resonators (RRs) are among the most widely employed topologies in MAs, forming the basis for the development of a broad range of functional devices36–39. Various configurations of RRs have been extensively studied and tailored to meet the specific demands of diverse applications. Their fabrication is relatively straightforward, facilitated by advanced techniques such as photolithography and nanoimprint lithography. Moreover, the circular geometry of RRs introduces an additional degree of freedom in absorber design.

Designing complex geometries remains a significant challenge, particularly when navigating large parameter spaces. Conventional design methodologies often rely on trial-and-error simulations, which are both computationally intensive and time-consuming. Recent research has shown a notable increase in the use of machine learning methods to improve predictive models for MA performance40. Machine learning techniques have been applied effectively across diverse fields, such as electromagnetic wave analysis and material characterization41. It also enables the observation and prediction of absorption behaviors in optical devices42. Machine learning approaches facilitate faster simulation processes while maintaining equivalent levels of accuracy43. MA structures have been successfully explored using a range of applicable machine learning techniques, such as decision trees44, random forests45, categorical boosts (CatBoost)46, and the K-Nearest Neighbor47 regressor. The complex non-linear characteristics of MA responses pose challenges for simple machine learning algorithms to deliver precise predictions. To address this challenge, we present a design framework based on the adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system (ANFIS). Despite substantial progress in predictive modeling, the use of ANFIS for estimating absorption in metamaterial absorbers has received limited attention. It merges the learning features of neural networks with the reasoning mechanisms of fuzzy logic, making it well-suited for modeling complex and nonlinear relationships. ANFIS, recognized as a universal predictor, has been widely applied across diverse scientific domains including electrical engineering, photovoltaic systems, power systems, and bio-sensors48,49. In comparison to conventional machine learning models such as Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) and Support Vector Machines (SVM), ANFIS demonstrates enhanced generalization capabilities and interpretability, which are particularly advantageous in engineering contexts where insight into input–output relationships is essential50. Although ANFIS demonstrates strong predictive capabilities in absorption modeling, its performance is notably influenced by the quality and quantity of the training dataset.

In this study, the proposed narrow-band MA comprises a cascaded Ag RR placed on a dielectric spacer, achieving near-perfect absorption at 691.9 nm operating in the visible to near-infrared wavelength region. The results demonstrate an exceptionally narrow full width at half maximum (FWHM) of just 4 nm and a Quality (Q) factor of 173. Additionally, it shows a high sensitivity of 600 nm per refractive index unit () in the RI range of 1.1–1.15. In comparison with conventional MAs, the narrowband design presented in this study not only achieves enhanced absorption performance while maintaining a simplified structure, compact size, and ultrathin profile but also enables spectral tunability through geometrical parameter manipulation. To investigate the underlying absorption mechanism, electric field distributions are analyzed. Furthermore, parametric studies are performed to assess the design’s tunability and its suitability for sensing and detection applications. To enhance computational efficiency, a regression-based ANFIS model is utilized for predicting absorption, leading to notable reductions in simulation time and resource computation.

Design of the absorber

The perspective, unit cell, and top views of the proposed MA are illustrated in Fig. 1a–c, respectively. Among the metamaterial configurations, the ring resonator is one of the most consistently favored designs, owing to its simple geometry, compactness, and ease of fabrication. After evaluating various configurations, the cascaded ring resonator structure demonstrated the highest absorption and spectral bandwidth, considering the selected materials and layer composition. The structure features nanoscale metallic resonators of height placed atop an insulating layer of thickness , backed by a continuous metallic plane with thickness on the opposite side. The dielectric material has an refractive index (RI) of 1.75, whereas the optical properties of Ag were obtained from the Palik dataset1. The dielectric layer plays a critical role in enhancing plasmonic coupling between the upper resonators and the metallic substrate51. The backplane is essential for minimizing wave transmission and simultaneously amplifying electromagnetic coupling with the resonators. This configuration enables pronounced field confinement, resulting in near-perfect absorption at the designated resonance frequencies. Consequently, reflection losses are significantly reduced, enhancing the overall efficiency.

The proposed configuration can be fabricated with high precision and excellent repeatability24. The absorber can be fabricated using the conventional electron beam lithography technique. Initially, a silver layer was deposited onto a silicon substrate, and then a dielectric film was deposited via electron beam evaporation. To form an array of RRs, a thick photoresist layer was spin-coated onto the substrate, and circular patterns were transferred from a photomask to the photoresist through ultraviolet (UV) exposure.

Machine learning analysis

Conventional simulation techniques, such as the FDTD and Finite Element Method (FEM), are known for their high computational demands, often requiring substantial memory, processing time, and hardware resources. As a result, adopting alternative approaches that can reduce simulation overhead while maintaining accuracy has become essential for optical design. Machine learning models like the Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference System (ANFIS) offer a viable solution to these computational challenges by efficiently predicting key parameters. The integration of two concepts, artificial neural networks and Takagi-Sugeno fuzzy system, led to the development of the ANFIS. ANFIS represents a state-of-the-art machine learning method that merges fuzzy logic–based expert systems with the intelligent learning of neural networks. It is particularly recognized for its capacity to capture highly nonlinear data relationships, and for its efficiency in modeling datasets of limited size56,57. While its structural design resembles that of traditional multilayer neural networks, ANFIS exhibits distinct architectural features, as illustrated in Fig. 10.

The schematic of the ANFIS architecture. This fuzzy inference system is composed of six layers. This architecture is used to predict absorption, taking the geometrical parameters as inputs. After traversing across hidden layers, the ANFIS predicts the maximum absorption.

ANFIS merges the learning and adaptability of neural networks with the capacity to manage uncertainty and the simplicity of interpretation found in fuzzy logic56. This makes ANFIS an excellent tool for modeling complex processes, but its effectiveness is significantly restricted by the dimensionality of the regression issue. The quantity of rules can grow exponentially as the number of input fuzzy sets rises, which depend on both the number of inputs and the quantity of membership functions for each input58. This method generally necessitates access to robust datasets and moderate computational resources, such as multi-core processors and ample memory (e.g., GB RAM) to handle training and evaluation phases.

ANFIS consists of six layers. The first is the input variables layer. The second is the membership functions layer (MF), where the membership degrees are computed. The third is the fuzzy rule input layer. The fourth is the normalized firing rules layer. The fifth is the adaptive defuzzification layer, and the last layer gives the output56,59. The input variables for the proposed ANFIS model correspond to the parameters varied during the geometric analysis, namely , , and . The empirical data employed in this study are derived from FDTD simulations and serve as the training set for the ANFIS model. A hybrid learning strategy is utilized for ANFIS learning.

Upon completion of the ANFIS training process, its predicted output–representing the absorption response–is evaluated against the actual absorption values obtained from FDTD simulations, and the root mean square error (RMSE) and mean absolute error (MAE) is calculated using Eqs. (3) and (4), respectively60. The RMSE and MAE are widely recognized as standard metrics for assessing model performance. The lower the RMSE and MAE metrics, the more accurate the prediction, indicating a reduced model error. RMSE is calculated by first squaring the deviation between each actual value and its corresponding ANFIS-predicted output, followed by averaging these squared differences across all data points. The computed RMSE value serves as a basis for updating the ANFIS parameters to meet the target defined by the objective function. The primary goal of this optimization process is to reduce both RMSE and MAE. As depicted in Fig. 11, the training process is carried out in three distinct phases:

Conclusions

In conclusion, by employing a conventional MIM structure, we have designed a metamaterial-based absorber with cascaded ring resonators. This absorber achieves single-mode resonance in the wavelength range of 600–800 nm under p-polarized light incidence. Based on the FDTD simulation results, the design exhibits perfect absorption, with a sensitivity of 600 nm/RIU. Also, the ANFIS is employed to train absorption efficiency and predict absorption level. The growing use of machine learning is enhancing the process of designing and discovering metamaterial devices. Since the characteristics of metamaterials are primarily governed by their geometric configurations, traditional design methods have relied heavily on physics-based modeling and iterative testing. These approaches can be both time-intensive and complex, particularly when the design space is explored in depth. Machine learning techniques offer solutions to these challenges by leveraging large, pre-processed datasets to train predictive models with high accuracy. We showed that ANFIS provides significantly better predictive performance in absorption modeling compared to random forest and k-nearest neighbor (KNN), with RMSE and MAE values of 0.0811 and 0.062, respectively, validated via 5-fold cross-validation. Although ANFIS performs well in forecasting absorption calculations, its accuracy heavily depends on the quality and quantity of the training dataset. The presented design incorporating the ANFIS model is applicable to imaging, bio-sensing and bio-spectroscopy.

Nonlinear Hodge correspondence in positive characteristic
Mao Sheng

Abstract
. In this article, we extend the nonabelian Hodge correspondence in positive characteristic to the nonlinear setting.



1.
Introduction

In our recent work
[
FS
]
, we introduced the notion of a
nonlinear Higgs bundle
(
[
FS
, Definition 1.3]
), which is abstracted from the properties of nonabelian Kodaira-Spencer maps. On the other hand, one should also be able to abstract the properties of nonabelian Gauss-Manin connections, and make a category of nonlinear flat bundles (see below). Surprisingly, we may equalize these two categories in positive characteristic, subject to suitable conditions. The equivalence we are going to present is intimately related to the nonabelian Hodge correspondence established by Ogus-Vologodsky
[
OV
]
, in their fundamental work in nonabelian Hodge theory in positive characteristic. Because of that, it seems appropriate to call this equivalence a
nonlinear Hodge correspondence
in positive characteristic.

Let us first introduce several notions. Let
T
be a scheme, and let
f:X\to S
to be a
T
-morphism.

Definition 1.1
.

For a
\lambda\in\Gamma(T,{\mathcal{O}}_{T})
, a
\lambda
-connection on
f
is an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-linear morphism

\nabla:f^{*}T_{S/T}\to T_{X/T}

such that its composite with the natural morphism
\pi:T_{X/T}\to f^{*}T_{S/T}
is equal to
\lambda\cdot id
.

Note that the composite map

\wedge^{2}f^{*}T_{S/T}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle[\nabla,\nabla]}}{{\longrightarrow}}T_{X/T}\to T_{X/T}/\lambda\cdot\textrm{im}(\nabla),\ a\wedge b\mapsto\overline{[\nabla(a),\nabla(b)]}

is
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-linear when
\lambda
is a unit. Moreover,
\nabla
induces a decomposition

T_{X/T}=T_{X/S}\oplus\nabla(f^{*}T_{S/T}),

and hence a natural isomorphism

T_{X/T}/\lambda\cdot\textrm{im}(\nabla)\cong T_{X/S}.

In this case, we shall call either
f^{*}\wedge^{2}T_{S/T}\to T_{X/S}
or its adjoint
\wedge^{2}T_{S/T}\to f_{*}T_{X/S}
the
curvature
of
\nabla
. A
\lambda
-connection
\nabla
, where
\lambda
is a unit, is said to be integrable, if its curvature is zero. Another interesting case is
\lambda=0
. As
\ker(\pi)=T_{X/S}
, we may write a 0-connection as

\theta:f^{*}T_{S/T}\to T_{X/S}.

We call it integrable, if the composite, which is
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
-linear and shall be called the curvature of
\theta
,

\wedge^{2}T_{S/T}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle[\theta,\theta]}}{{\longrightarrow}}f_{*}T_{X/S},\ a\wedge b\mapsto[\theta(a),\theta(b)]

is zero.

In this article, we consider only the situation where
S\to T
is smooth. Under this assumption, the notion of
\lambda
-connection behaves well under base change: Let
T^{\prime}\to T
be a morphism of schemes. Let
f_{T^{\prime}}:X_{T^{\prime}}\to S_{T^{\prime}}
be the base change of
f
via
T^{\prime}\to T
, and let
\lambda^{\prime}\in\Gamma(T^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}_{T^{\prime}})
be the image of
\lambda
. Then the composite

f_{T^{\prime}}^{*}T_{S_{T^{\prime}}/T^{\prime}}=f^{*}T_{S/T}|_{X_{T^{\prime}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla}}{{\to}}T_{X/T}|_{X_{T^{\prime}}}\to T_{X_{T^{\prime}}/T^{\prime}}

is a
\lambda^{\prime}
-connection on
f_{T^{\prime}}
. Also, the theory of
\lambda
-connections is intimately related to the theory of foliations. Indeed, an integrable
1
-connection on
f
is nothing but a
transversal foliation
on
f
. Now let
k
be a base ring. A
k
-variety is by definition an integral separated scheme of finite type over
k
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
T
be the spectrum of
k
, and
S
be a smooth
k
-variety. A transversal foliated
S
-variety is a pair
(f,\nabla)
, where
f:X\to S
is a morphism of
k
-varieties and
\nabla
is an integrable
1
-connection (or connection for short) on
f
. A Higgs
S
-variety is a pair
(f,\theta)
, where
f
is as before and
\theta
is an integrable
0
-connection (or Higgs field for short) on
f
. A nonlinear flat (resp. Higgs) bundle over
S
is a transversal foliated (resp. Higgs)
S
-variety
(f,\nabla)
(resp.
(f,\theta)
) with
f
being smooth. For two pairs
(f:X\to S,\nabla_{f})
and
(g:Y\to S,\nabla_{g})
of
\lambda
-connections, we define a morphism
(f,\nabla_{f})\to(g,\nabla_{g})
to be an
S
-morphism
h:X\to Y
such that

h^{*}\nabla_{g}=h_{*}\circ\nabla_{f}
, where
h_{*}:T_{X/k}\to h^{*}T_{Y/k}
is the tangent map of
h
.

Example 1.3
.

Assume
k={\mathbb{C}}
. Let
X\to S
be a smooth projective morphism, and
G
a reductive algebraic group over
{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
f:M^{sm}_{dR}(X/S,G)\to S
be the sublocus of the relative de Rham space parameterizing Zariski dense flat connections. Simpson (
[
Si94
, §8]
,
[
Si97
, §7-8]
) constructed the nonabelian Gauss-Manin connection on
f
, which is a nonlinear flat bundle in above sense (
[
FS
, Proposition 4.2]
). On the other hand, one has also
g:M^{sm}_{Dol}(X/S,G)\to S
, the sublocus of the relative Dolbeault space parameterizing Zariski dense Higgs bundles. We showed that
g
, equipped with the nonabelian Kodaira-Spencer map, is a nonlinear Higgs bundle (
[
FS
, Theorem 1.2]
).

For a fixed
k
-variety
S
, it is clear that transversal foliated
S
-varieties (resp. Higgs
S
-varieties) form a category, which shall be denoted by
\mathrm{NMIC}(S)
(resp.
\mathrm{NHIG}(S)
). The category
\mathrm{NMIC}(S)
(resp. (resp.
\mathrm{NHIG}(S)
)) contains the category of nonlinear flat (resp. Higgs) bundles over
S
as full subcategory, which shall be denoted by
\mathrm{NMIC}^{s}(S)
(resp.
\mathrm{NHIG}^{s}(S)
). In §
2
, we show that the category
\mathrm{MIC}(S)
(resp.
\mathrm{HIG}(S)
) of coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
-modules with integrable connections (resp. coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
-modules with Higgs fields) can be embedded into
\mathrm{NMIC}(S)
(resp.
\mathrm{NHIG}(S)
). Objects in these subcategories are said to be
linear
.

In the following statements, we assume that
k
is a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
. Let
\sigma:k\to k
be the Frobenius automorphism of
k
. Our first result is a nonlinear analogue of the classical descent theorem.

Theorem 1.4

(Theorem
3.3
, Theorem
7.1
)
.

Suppose
S
is smooth over
k
. Then there is an explicit equivalence of categories between the category
\mathrm{NMIC}_{0}(S)
of transversal foliated
S
-varieties with vanishing
p
-curvature and the category
\mathrm{NHIG}_{0}(S^{\prime})
of Higgs
S^{\prime}
-varieties with zero Higgs field, which satisfies the following properties:

(i)

The equivalence restricts to an equivalence between the full subcategories of linear objects is the classical Cartier correspondence;

(ii)

It restricts to an equivalence between
\mathrm{NMIC}^{s}_{0}(S)
and
\mathrm{NHIG}^{s}_{0}(S^{\prime})
.

Next, we obtain a nonlinear analogue of the Ogus-Vologodsky correspondence when
S
is local.

Theorem 1.5

(Theorem
4.4
)
.

Assume additionally the pair
(S,F_{S/k})
is
W_{2}(k)
-liftable. Set
S^{\prime}=S\times_{k,\sigma}k
. Then a lifting of
(S,F_{S/k})
induces an explicit equivalence of categories between the category
\mathrm{NMIC}_{nil}(S)
of transversal foliated
S
-varieties with nilpotent
p
-curvature and the category
\mathrm{NHIG}_{nil}(S^{\prime})
of Higgs
S^{\prime}
-varieties with nilpotent Higgs field, which satisfies the following properties:

(i)

It coincides with the Ogus-Vologodsky correspondence over the full subcategories of linear objects;

(ii)

It restricts to an equivalence between
\mathrm{NMIC}^{s}_{nil}(S)
and
\mathrm{NHIG}^{s}_{nil}(S^{\prime})
.

Our method is to construct explicit deformations of connections by using Frobenius liftings, and then apply the nonlinear Cartier descent theorem. We also obtain a partial generalization of Theorem
4.4
to a global base, via the technique of exponential twisting (
[
LSZ15
]
,
[
SSW
]
).

Theorem 1.6

(Theorem
5.5
)
.

Assume only the smooth
k
-variety
S
is
W_{2}(k)
-liftable. Let
G
be a split reductive group over
k
with
h_{G}<p
, where
h_{G}
is the Coxeter number of
G
. Then a
W_{2}(k)
-lifting of
S
induces an equivalence of categories between
\mathrm{NMIC}^{G}_{nil}(S)
and
\mathrm{NHIG}^{G}_{nil}(S^{\prime})
.

Remark 1.7
.

For precise description of the categories
\mathrm{NMIC}^{G}_{nil}(S)
and
\mathrm{NHIG}^{G}_{nil}(S^{\prime})
, we refer our readers to §5. We remark that the previous results can be generalized to a more general base scheme
T
in char
p
. We leave this task to interested readers.

One may wonder what kind of objects in these categories
naturally
correspond to each other. To this purpose, we introduce the category of
nonlinear Fontaine modules
(over truncated Witt rings), which has natural functors into both categories. They are called nonlinear Fontaine modules, because we equip certain transversal foliated
S
-varieties with filtration structures as well as Frobenius structures, in a way very much like Faltings did in the linear setting (
[
F
, II. d)]
). See §6 for details.

Theorem 1.8

(Theorem
6.9
)
.

Assumption as in Theorem
1.6
. Let
(f,\nabla,Fil,\varphi)
be a geometric strict
p
-torsion Fontaine module over
S
with symmetry group
G
. Let
(g,\theta)
be the associated graded Higgs
S
-variety to
(f,\nabla,Fil)
. Then
\varphi
induces a natural isomorphism

C^{-1}\pi^{*}(g,-\theta)\cong(f,\nabla),

where
C^{-1}
is the equivalence in Theorem
1.6

from
\mathrm{NHIG}^{G}_{nil}(S^{\prime})
to
\mathrm{NMIC}^{G}_{nil}(S)
, and
\pi:S^{\prime}\to S
is the natural projection.

In our forthcoming work
[
Sh
]
, we prove that a variant of Example
1.3
in positive characteristic is a geometric strict
p
-torsion Fontaine module. The explicit description of the equivalence as given in the proof of Theorem
1.5
is crucial in our approach.

2.
Linear
\lambda
-connections on linear schemes

Let
T
be a noetherian scheme, and
S\to T
be a
smooth
morphism. For a morphism
f:X\to S
, there is the canonically associated exact sequence of sheaves of relative Kähler differentials (
[
H
, Proposition 8.11]
)

(2.0.1)

\displaystyle f^{*}\Omega_{S/T}\to\Omega_{X/T}\to\Omega_{X/S}\to 0.

Set
T_{X/T}
to the linear dual
\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}(\Omega_{X/T},{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
and etc. We obtain the following exact sequence of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-modules:

(2.0.2)

\displaystyle 0\to T_{X/S}\to T_{X/T}\to f^{*}T_{S/T}.

We shall call
f

pseudo-smooth
if the above sequence is also exact on the right. As
S\to T
is smooth by assumption,
\Omega_{S/T}
is locally free of finite rank (see e.g.
[
GW
, Corollary 18.58]
), and hence
T_{S/T}
is locally free of finite rank too. Thus we obtain a locally split short exact sequence for a pseudo-smooth
T
-morphism
f

(2.0.3)

\displaystyle 0\to T_{X/S}\to T_{X/T}\to f^{*}T_{S/T}\to 0.

Pseudo-smooth smoothness does not imply smoothness. For example, the natural projection
X=X_{0}\times_{T}S\to S
, where
X_{0}\to T
is non smooth, is pseudo-smooth but non smooth. However, we have the following

Proposition 2.1
.

Notation and assumption as above. Then
f:X\to S
is smooth if and only if
X\to T
is smooth and
f
is pseudo-smooth.

Proof.

Suppose
f
is smooth. Then the morphism
X\to T
is smooth since it is the composite of two smooth morphisms. Also, the sequence
2.0.1
is exact on the left and locally split (see e.g.
[
GW
, Corollay 18.72]
). Hence we obtain a short exact sequence of locally free sheaves:

0\to f^{*}\Omega_{S/T}\to\Omega_{X/T}\to\Omega_{X/S}\to 0.

Taking the dual, we obtain the short exact sequence
2.0.3
. So
f
is pseudo-smooth. Conversely, if
f
is pseudo-smooth and
X\to T
is smooth, we immediately see that
T_{X/S}
is locally free as well. Taking the dual of the short exact sequence
2.0.3
, we get that the sequence
2.0.1
is exact on the left and locally split. Thus
f
is smooth (see e.g.
[
GW
, Corollay 18.72]
).
∎

Let
{\mathcal{V}}
be a coherent sheaf on
S
. Recall that a
\lambda
-connection on
{\mathcal{V}}
, where
\lambda\in\Gamma(T,{\mathcal{O}}_{T})
, is an
{\mathcal{O}}_{T}
-linear map

\nabla:{\mathcal{V}}\to{\mathcal{V}}\otimes\Omega_{S/T}

satisfying the
\lambda
-Leibnitz rule

\nabla(fs)=\lambda s\otimes df+f\nabla(s),\hskip 10.00002ptf\in{\mathcal{O}}_{S},\ s\in{\mathcal{V}}.

The curvature
F_{\nabla}
of
\nabla
is defined to be the composite

{\mathcal{V}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla}}{{\to}}{\mathcal{V}}\otimes\Omega_{S/T}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla^{1}}}{{\to}}{\mathcal{V}}\otimes\wedge^{2}\Omega_{S/T},

where
\nabla^{1}
is an
{\mathcal{O}}_{T}
-linear map determined by

\nabla^{1}(s\otimes\omega):=\nabla(s)\wedge\omega+\lambda s\otimes d\omega,\hskip 10.00002pts\in{\mathcal{V}},\ \omega\in\Omega_{S/T}.

It is
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
-linear. We say
\nabla
is integrable when
F_{\nabla}=0
.

Recall that one may canonically associate an
S
-scheme to
{\mathcal{V}}
, the so-called
linear scheme
construction (see e.g.
[
N
, §3]
). It is defined to be

\pi:V:=\textbf{Spec}S({\mathcal{V}})\to S

where
S({\mathcal{V}})
is the symmetric algebra of
{\mathcal{V}}
over
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
. Linear schemes generalize the notion of geometric vector bundles (see e.g.
[
H
, Ch II, Ex. 5.18]
), which are the special cases where
{\mathcal{V}}
is locally free.

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose there exists a
\lambda
-connection on
{\mathcal{V}}
where
\lambda
is a unit. Then the associated linear scheme
\pi:V\to S
to
{\mathcal{V}}
is pseudo-smooth.

Proof.

Since
\lambda^{-1}\nabla
is simply a connection, we may assume
\lambda=1
and
\nabla
is a connection on
{\mathcal{V}}
. The problem is locally in
S
. Hence we may assume
T=\mathrm{Spec}(A)
and
S=\mathrm{Spec}(B)
. Then
{\mathcal{V}}
is the sheafification of
M=\Gamma(S,{\mathcal{V}})
, which is a finitely generated
B
-module. Set
C=S_{B}(M)
to be the symmetric algebra attached to
M
over
B
. Since
B
is smooth over
A
, we may further localize to guarantee the existence of a set of coordinates

\{s_{1},\cdots,s_{m}\}\subset B
on
\mathrm{Spec}(B)
with

\Omega_{B/A}=B\{ds_{1}\}\oplus\cdots B\{ds_{m}\}.

Let
\{\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{i}}\}
be the dual basis of
Der_{A}(B)=Hom_{B}(\Omega_{B/A},A)
. We are going to construct a
C
-morphism

Der_{A}(B)\otimes_{B}C\to Der_{A}(C)

whose composite with the natural
C
-morphism
Der_{A}(C)\to Der_{A}(B)\otimes_{B}C
is the identity. Note that
Der_{A}(B)\otimes_{B}C
is a free
C
-module with basis
\{\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{i}}\}
. Any of its
C
-morphism to
Der_{A}(C)
is specified by a collection of elements
\{D_{i}\}\subset Der_{A}(C)
, such that
D_{i}
will be the image of
\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{i}}
. We claim that the following association

ds_{j}\mapsto\delta_{ij},1\leq j\leq m,\hskip 10.00002ptda\mapsto\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{i}}}(a),\ a\in M

defines an element
D_{i}
in
Der_{A}(C)
. Indeed, by taking a set of generators
\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{r}\}
of
M
as
C
-module,
\Omega_{C/A}
is generated by
\{ds_{1},\cdots,ds_{m},da_{1},\cdots,da_{r}\}
. So it suffices to verify the above association is well-defined. Let
\sum_{j}f_{j}a_{j}
(with
\{f_{j}\}\subset B
) be a zero relation in
M
. We need to show
d(\sum_{j}f_{j}a_{j})
, which is zero in
\Omega_{C/A}
, shall be mapped to zero under the association. As we have

\displaystyle d(\sum_{j}f_{j}a_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}df_{j}a_{j}+f_{j}da_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}(\sum_{l}\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial s_{l}}ds_{l})a_{j}+f_{j}da_{j},

it will be sent to the element
\sum_{j}\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial s_{i}}a_{j}+f_{j}\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{i}}}a_{j}
. By the Leibnitz rule, it holds that

\sum_{j}\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial s_{i}}a_{j}+f_{j}\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{i}}}a_{j}=\sum_{j}\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{i}}}(f_{j}a_{j}),

which by additivity of
\nabla
equals
\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{i}}}(\sum_{j}f_{j}a_{j})
and hence is zero. The claim is proved. It is clear that the so-constructed morphism satisfies the splitting property as requested. This completes the proof.
∎

The previous proposition enables us to construct many more examples of pseudo-smooth but non smooth morphisms in positive characteristic: For simplicity, let us take
T
to be spectrum of an algebraically closed field of char
p>0
. Then for any coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{V}}
on
S
,
F_{S}^{*}{\mathcal{V}}
admits the canonical connection. By faithfully flat descent,
F_{S}^{*}{\mathcal{V}}
is non locally free when
{\mathcal{V}}
is non locally free. It follows that the associated linear scheme which is just
V\times_{S,F_{S}}S\to S
is non smooth, while it is pseduo-smooth by Proposition
2.2
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}
be a coherent sheaf on
S
, and
\nabla
be a
\lambda
-connection on
{\mathcal{V}}
. Then over the linear scheme
\pi:V\to S
attached to
{\mathcal{V}}
, there exists a canonically associated
\lambda
-connection
\nabla_{\pi}
. Moreover, when
\lambda
is either a unit or zero,
\nabla
is integrable if and only if
\nabla_{\pi}
is integrable.

Proof.

First we assume
S
small affine as in the proof of Proposition
2.2
. We shall modify the construction of a
C
-morphism

\alpha:Der_{A}(B)\otimes C\to Der_{A}(C)

as follows: For each
i
, one sends
\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{i}}
to
D_{i}\in Der_{A}(C)
which is defined by

ds_{j}\mapsto\lambda\delta_{ij},1\leq j\leq m,\hskip 10.00002ptda\mapsto\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{i}}}(a),\ a\in M.

By the same argument as above (replacing the Leibnitz rule with the
\lambda
-Leibnitz rule), we know this is well-defined. Denote the morphism by
\tilde{\nabla}
. Clearly, its composite with the morphism
Der_{A}(C)\to Der_{A}(B)\otimes C
is
\lambda\cdot id
. Next, assuming
\lambda
is either a unit or zero, we shall verify that
\nabla
is integrable if and only if
\tilde{\nabla}
is integrable. As
\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{r}\}
is a generating system of
M
as
B
-module, we may express for each triple
(i,j)
(not necessarily in a unique way)

\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{i}}}a_{j}=\sum_{k}b^{i}_{jk}a_{k},\hskip 10.00002ptb^{i}_{jk}\in B.

The curvature

F_{\nabla}:\wedge^{2}Der_{A}(B)\to End_{B}(M)

is therefore given by sending
\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{i}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{j}},i<j
to

a_{k}\mapsto\sum_{l}[\lambda(\frac{\partial b^{j}_{kl}}{\partial s_{i}}-\frac{\partial b^{i}_{kl}}{\partial s_{j}})+\sum_{m}(b^{j}_{km}b^{i}_{ml}-b^{i}_{km}b^{j}_{ml})]a_{l}

On the other hand, we may also compute that the Lie bracket
[D_{i},D_{j}]\in Der_{A}(C)
sends each
ds_{k}
to zero, and
da_{k}
to the element which is exactly given by the right hand side of the above expression. Thus, when
F_{\nabla}=0
, it follows immediately that the curvature of
\tilde{\nabla}
is zero. As proved in Propostion
2.2
, when
\lambda
is a unit, one has the decomposition

Der_{A}(C)=\alpha(Der_{A}(B)\otimes_{B}C)\oplus Der_{B}(C).

Note that
[D_{i},D_{j}]\in Der_{B}(C)
for any
i,j
. Hence, the vanishing of the curvature of
\tilde{\nabla}
(which means the quotient of
[D_{i},D_{j}]
modulo
\alpha(Der_{A}(B)\otimes_{B}C)
is zero for each
i,j
) forces
[D_{i},D_{j}]
to be zero. Thus by the above identification,
F_{\nabla}
is zero too. When
\lambda=0
, one sees directly from the identification that
F_{\nabla}=0
. Finally, it remains to show
\lambda
-connection exists for a global
S
. For that purpose, we cover
S
with small affines
\{S_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha}
and denote the local
\lambda
-connections obtained in the first step by

\nabla_{\alpha}:\pi^{*}T_{S_{\alpha}/T}\to T_{V_{\alpha}/T},

where
V_{\alpha}=\pi^{-1}(S_{\alpha})
. We shall show that
\{\nabla_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha}
glue into a global morphism
\nabla_{\pi}:\pi^{*}T_{S/T}\to T_{X/T}
. Let
\{s^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,s^{\prime}_{m}\}
be a set of coordinates on
S_{\beta}
, and over
S_{\beta}
,

\nabla_{\beta}(\frac{\partial}{\partial s^{\prime}_{i}})=D^{\prime}_{i},

where
D^{\prime}_{i}(ds^{\prime}_{j})=\lambda\delta_{ij}
and

D^{\prime}_{i}(da_{j})=\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial s^{\prime}_{i}}}(a_{j})=\sum_{k}b^{{}^{\prime}i}_{jk}a_{k}.

Then it boils down to show, over the overlap
S_{\alpha}\cap S_{\beta}
,
\nabla_{\alpha}=\nabla_{\beta}
, that is for any
i

\nabla_{\beta}(\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{i}})=D_{i}.

But this is more than obvious, since everything involved in the verification is linear. We shall omit this detail, and hence conclude the proof.
∎

For the special case where
T=\mathrm{Spec}(k)
and
\lambda=0,1
, we therefore obtain faithful functors

G:\mathrm{MIC}(S)\to\mathrm{NMIC}(S),\hskip 10.00002ptG:\mathrm{HIG}(S)\to\mathrm{NHIG}(S),

which restrict to
\mathrm{MIC}^{lf}(S)\to\mathrm{NMIC}^{s}(S)
(resp.
\mathrm{HIG}^{lf}(S)\to\mathrm{NHIG}^{s}(S)
). Here the supscript lf in front of
\mathrm{MIC}
or
\mathrm{HIG}
denotes for the subcategory consisting of locally free objects. Note also that
\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{i}}}(a)
in the above construction belongs to
M

3.
Nonlinear Cartier descent theorem

Let

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

be a flat bundle over

if and only if
\psi_{{\mathcal{F}}}=0
.

Proof.

The proof relies on a comparison of local expressions of
\psi_{\nabla}
and
\psi_{{\mathcal{F}}}
. We use the notations in Proposition
2.3
. Since
{\mathcal{V}}
is locally free, we may take
\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{r}\}
to be a local basis, and identify the dual basis

with a set of local basis for

. Let

and let

be the connection matrix

with respect to

. That is

\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial s}}(a_{j})=\sum_{k}b_{jk}a_{k}.

Then

On the other hand, the corresponding

4.
Local nonlinear Hodge correspondence

In this section, we make the following assumption.

Assumption 4.1
.

We say the pair
(S,F_{S/k})
is
W_{2}(k)
-liftable, if the following condition is satisfied: There exists a smooth
W_{2}(k)

\tilde{S}
whose mod
p
reduction is isomorphic to
S
. Fix a
k
-isomorphism
\alpha:\tilde{S}\times_{W_{2}(k)}k\cong S

W_{2}(k)
-morphism of schemes
\tilde{F}:\tilde{S}\to\tilde{S}
whose mod

under the isomorphism induced by
\alpha
.

When the above assumption is satisfied for

is
local
. Affines are local, but projective curves of genus
\geq 2
are not local. For local

, Ogus-Vologodsky

establishes an equivalence between the category of coherent modules with integrable connections whose

W_{2}(k)
-lifting
\tilde{S}
of

and a lifting of

\tilde{F}:\tilde{S}\to\tilde{S}^{\prime}:=\tilde{S}\times_{W_{2}(k),W_{2}(\sigma)}W_{2}(k).

Let
(f,\nabla)
be a transversal foliated
S
-variety. Set
{\mathcal{F}}_{\nabla}=\textrm{im}(\nabla)\subset T_{X/k}

splits the short exact sequence

the

\psi_{\nabla}:=\psi_{{\mathcal{F}}_{\nabla}}:f^{*}T_{S/k}\to T_{X}/{\mathcal{F}}_{\nabla}\cong T_{X/S}.

Lemma 4.2
.

The following properties hold for
\psi_{\nabla}
:

(i)

\psi_{\nabla}
is semi-linear;

(ii)

\psi_{\nabla}
is integrable;

(iii)

By the lemma, the

-linear morphism

\psi_{\nabla}:F_{X}^{*}f^{*}T_{S/k}\to T_{X/S},\hskip 10.00002pt[\psi_{\nabla},\psi_{\nabla}]=0.

Proof.

The first property holds for a general foliation on

[\psi_{\nabla}(D_{1}),\psi_{\nabla}(D_{2})]=[\nabla(D_{1})^{p},\nabla(D_{2})^{p}].

Now a simple calculation shows that
[\nabla(D_{1}),\nabla(D_{2})]\in T_{X/S}

The lemma is proved.

7.
Appendix: Nonlinear Cartier descent and Ekedahl’s correspondence, by Mao Sheng and Jinxing Xu

In this appendix, we shall extend the nonlinear Cartier descent theorem to arbitrary transversally foliated
S
-varieties. Moreover, we shall give a new proof of the Ekedahl’s correspondence using the nonlinear Cartier descent.

7.1.
Nonlinear Cartier descent

Assume that
k
is a ring of characteristic
p
, and let
S
be a smooth
k
-scheme. Set
S^{\prime}\coloneqq S\times_{k,\sigma}k
, the base change of
S
along the Frobenius homomorphism
\sigma\colon k\to k
, and let
F=F_{S/k}\colon S\to S^{\prime}
denote the relative Frobenius morphism.

Let
f^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to S^{\prime}
be a
k
-morphism, and consider the base change

f\colon X\coloneqq X^{\prime}\times_{S^{\prime}}S\to S.

The Cartesian diagram induces a natural isomorphism

\Omega_{X/S^{\prime}}\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\;f^{*}\Omega_{S/S^{\prime}}\;\oplus\;\pi^{*}\Omega_{X^{\prime}/S^{\prime}},

where
\pi\colon X\to X^{\prime}
is the projection. Since both
S\to S^{\prime}
and
X\to S^{\prime}
induce zero homomorphisms on differentials, we have natural identifications
\Omega_{S/S^{\prime}}=\Omega_{S/k}
and
\Omega_{X/S^{\prime}}=\Omega_{X/k}
.
It follows that there is an isomorphism

(7.0.1)

\displaystyle\Omega_{X/k}\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\;f^{*}\Omega_{S/k}\;\oplus\;\pi^{*}\Omega_{X^{\prime}/S^{\prime}},

where
\pi\colon X\to X^{\prime}
is the projection. Since
\Omega_{S/k}
is locally free, we have

\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(f^{*}\Omega_{S/k},\mathcal{O}_{X})\;\simeq\;f^{*}\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}(\Omega_{S/k},\mathcal{O}_{S}),

and because the Frobenius morphism

is flat,

\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\pi^{*}\Omega_{X^{\prime}/S^{\prime}},\mathcal{O}_{X})\;\simeq\;\pi^{*}\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}}(\Omega_{X^{\prime}/S^{\prime}},\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}).

Applying
\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(-,\mathcal{O}_{X})
to (
7.0.1
) then yields

(7.0.2)

\displaystyle T_{X/k}\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\;f^{*}T_{S/k}\;\oplus\;\pi^{*}T_{X^{\prime}/S^{\prime}}.

This splitting defines an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear morphism

\nabla_{can}\colon f^{*}T_{S/k}\longrightarrow T_{X/k}.

A straightforward verification shows that

is a transversal foliation on
f
with vanishing
p
-curvature. Moreover, it coincides with the one defined in Lemma
3.2
when
k
is a perfect field and

is smooth. Since

is faithfully flat and induces a homeomorphism on the underlying topological spaces, we may identify
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}
with a subsheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
. It then follows from the splitting (
7.0.1
) that

(7.0.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}\;=\;\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\nabla_{can}}\;\coloneqq\;\{\,s\in\mathcal{O}_{X}\mid\nabla_{can}(s)=0\,\}.

Conversely, we have the following version of the nonlinear Cartier descent theorem, which removes the smoothness assumption on

-scheme
S
, and a
k
-morphism
f\colon U\to S
such that
f
induces an isomorphism

\mathcal{F}|_{U}\;\simeq\;f^{*}T_{S/k}.

Proof.

Since
X/k
is smooth, we can choose local coordinates
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
of
X
around
x
such that

\Omega_{X/k,x}\;=\;\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\,\mathrm{d}x_{n}.

As
T_{X/k}/\mathcal{F}
is locally free, the short exact sequence

0\;\longrightarrow\;\mathcal{F}\;\longrightarrow\;T_{X/k}\;\longrightarrow\;T_{X/k}/\mathcal{F}\;\longrightarrow\;0

splits locally. In particular,
\mathcal{F}
is locally free, and a basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{r}
of the
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
-module
\mathcal{F}_{x}
can be extended to a basis of
T_{X/k,x}
. After possibly reordering the coordinates
x_{i}
, we may choose
e_{1},\dots,e_{r}
so that for any
1\leq i,j\leq r
, the natural pairing satisfies

(7.3.1)

\langle e_{i},\mathrm{d}x_{j}\rangle\;=\;\begin{cases}1,&i=j,\\
0,&i\neq j.\end{cases}

Let
U
be an affine open neighborhood of
x
on which (
7.3.1
) holds. Consider the polynomial ring
k[t_{1},\dots,t_{r}]
. The
k
-algebra homomorphism

k[t_{1},\dots,t_{r}]\;\longrightarrow\;\mathcal{O}_{X}(U),\hskip 20.00003ptt_{i}\longmapsto x_{i}

induces a
k
-morphism

f\colon U\longrightarrow S\coloneqq\mathrm{Spec}\ k[t_{1},\dots,t_{r}].

It is straightforward to verify that this morphism
f
satisfies the desired property.
∎

We can now extend Ekedahl’s correspondence (Theorem
3.1
) to the case where the base is regular.

Theorem 7.4
.

Assume
k
is an integral regular ring of characteristic
p
and
X
is a smooth
k
-variety. Then there exists a one-to-one correspondence between the set of
p
-closed smooth foliations on
X
and the set of (isomorphism classes of) inseparable morphisms of
k
-varieties

X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi^{\prime}}}{{\longrightarrow}}X^{\prime}

with
F_{X/k}=\pi^{\prime}\circ\pi
and
Y
non-singular.

Proof.

Since
X/k
is smooth, the relative Frobenius
F_{X/k}\colon X\to X^{\prime}
is faithfully flat and induces a homeomorphism on the underlying topological spaces. Thus, we may regard
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}
as a subsheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Given a
p
-closed smooth foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
X
, we define
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y
by

{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}=\mathrm{Ann}({\mathcal{F}}):=\{a\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X}|\ Da=0,\ \textrm{for all}\ D\in{\mathcal{F}}\}.

It is clear that
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}\subset\mathcal{O}_{Y}
, so there exists a morphism
\pi^{\prime}\colon Y\to X^{\prime}
such that
F_{X/k}=\pi^{\prime}\circ\pi
. Conversely, given a chain of finite morphisms as above, we define

{\mathcal{F}}=\mathrm{Ann}(Y):=\{D\in T_{X/k}\mid Da=0\ \text{for all}\ a\in{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}\}.

It then remains to verify the following claims:

(i)

Given
\mathcal{F}
, the corresponding
Y
is smooth over
k
, and
\mathrm{Ann}(\mathrm{Ann}(\mathcal{F}))=\mathcal{F}
.

(ii)

Given
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi^{\prime}}}{{\longrightarrow}}X^{\prime}
, we have
\mathrm{Ann}(\mathrm{Ann}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}))=\mathcal{O}_{Y}
.

First, note that these claims can be verified locally. For (i), given
\mathcal{F}
, by Lemma
7.3
, we may assume there exists a
k
-morphism
f\colon X\to S
to a smooth
k
-scheme
S
, such that
f
induces an isomorphism
\mathcal{F}\simeq f^{*}T_{S/k}
. This isomorphism yields an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear homomorphism

\nabla\colon f^{*}T_{S/k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{F}\hookrightarrow T_{X/k}.

By construction,
\nabla
is a transversal foliation of
f
with vanishing
p
-curvature. We may then apply Theorem
7.1
to obtain the following Cartesian diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X_{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\square}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{S^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{S/k}}

Moreover,
\nabla
coincides with
\nabla_{can}
induced by this Cartesian structure. From the splittings (
7.0.1
) and (
7.0.2
), we deduce that
\mathcal{O}_{Y}\coloneqq\mathrm{Ann}(\mathcal{F})=\mathcal{O}_{X_{1}}
as subsheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, and furthermore that
\mathrm{Ann}(\mathcal{O}_{X_{1}})=\operatorname{Im}\nabla_{can}=\mathcal{F}
. Hence we may identify
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{1}}}{{\rightarrow}}X_{1}
with
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\rightarrow}}Y
, and conclude that
\mathrm{Ann}(\mathrm{Ann}(\mathcal{F}))=\mathcal{F}
. Note that the sequence

0\rightarrow f^{*}\Omega_{S/k}\rightarrow\Omega_{X/k}\rightarrow\Omega_{X/S}\rightarrow 0

is a split short exact sequence. By the smoothness of
X/k
, it follows that
X/S
is also smooth. Since
F_{S/k}
is faithfully flat, the smoothness of
X/S
implies the smoothness of
X^{\prime}/S^{\prime}
, and therefore
X^{\prime}/k
is smooth as well. This completes the proof of (i).

Next, consider
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi^{\prime}}}{{\longrightarrow}}X^{\prime}
with
Y
regular. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
X=\operatorname{Spec}C
and
Y=\operatorname{Spec}C^{\prime}
are affine. By Kunz’s conjecture, proved in
[
KN
]
, the extension
C^{\prime}\hookrightarrow C
admits a
p
-basis
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}\in C
; that is,
C
is a free
C^{\prime}
-module with basis

\left\{c_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots c_{n}^{i_{n}}\,\middle|\,0\leq i_{\alpha}<p\text{ for all }1\leq\alpha\leq n\right\}.

With this in place, we can construct the following Cartesian diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\square}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B^{(p)}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t_{i}^{p}\mapsfrom t_{i}}

Here,
B^{(p)}=B=k[t_{1},\dots,t_{n}]
is the polynomial algebra, and the morphism
\psi^{\prime}
is the
k
-algebra homomorphism defined by
t_{i}\mapsto c_{i}^{p}
, while
\psi
is the
k
-algebra homomorphism given by
t_{i}\mapsto c_{i}
. Let
S=\operatorname{Spec}B
, we obtain the following Cartesian diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\square}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{S^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{S/k}}

Then, as in the proof of (i), it follows from the splittings (
7.0.1
) and (
7.0.2
) that
\mathrm{Ann}(\mathrm{Ann}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}))=\mathcal{O}_{Y}
. This completes the proof of claim (ii).

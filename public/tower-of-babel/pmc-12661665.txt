Constructing and evaluating a predictive model for the risk of preoperative frailty in breast cancer patients
Authors: Qianna Fang, Hongmei Jiang, Lingfang Deng, Weiming Sun, Mei Wang, Yu Liu, Jinjiang Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe aim of this study was to construct and internally validate a prediction model for the risk of preoperative frailty in Chinese breast cancer patients.MethodFrom October 2022 to August 2023, 519 patients were selected who presented for elective surgery in a tertiary hospital of breast surgery in Jinzhou City, China. Patients were preoperatively identified as frail or not frail, using standardized criteria. Binary logistic regression analysis was used to screen the risk factors for the occurrence of preoperative frailty in these breast cancer patients, to establish a risk prediction model. The discriminatory nature of the constructed nomogram model was evaluated with the Area Under the Curve (AUC) of the Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve. After internal validation of the nomogram model, correction curves were produced using the bootstrap method with 1000 repetitive sampling of the raw data, and the accuracy of the model was evaluated by the Hosmer-Lemesow goodness-of-fit test. Finally, clinical decision curve analysis (DCA) was used to evaluate the model.ResultsLogistic regression analysis identified 7 variables associated with frailty in the study cohort that were entered into the model: chemotherapy, serum levels of albumin (ALB) and hemoglobin (HB), nutrition status, occurrence of depression, social support, and Instrumental Activities of Daily Living (IADL) scores. Of these, protective factors were nutrition and social support, while the identified risk factors were chemotherapy, ALB, HB, depression, and IADL. The model had an AUC value of 0.87 (95% CI: 0.837 ~ 0.904). The outcome of the Hosmer-Lemeshow test was χ2 = 0.521, P = 0.771, indicating predictions were not statistically different from the actual data. The calibration curve also showed consistency between predicted and actual probability. The ROC and DCA indicated that the nomogram model had a good theoretical predictive performance.ConclusionThe predictive model of breast cancer preoperative frailty risk obtained in this study is expected to have a good predictive ability, with satisfying predicted levels of differentiation, accuracy and practicality. The model provides a basis for clinical nursing staff to screen and timely detect breast Chinese cancer patients at risk of preoperative frailty.

Introduction

The statistical incidence of breast cancer in women is globally increasing and surpasses lung cancer; its burden continues to increase, with newly diagnosed breast cancers projected to increase by more than 40% by 2040 [1]. Surgery is an effective method of treatment; however, frailty is common in breast cancer patients, occurring at a frequency of 5 to 71% depending on the study and age of the patients, with a pooled prevalence of 43% [2]. Preoperative frailty can increase the patient’s postoperative hospitalization time, complications, and healthcare costs [3]. A significant correlation has been demonstrated between preoperative frailty and postoperative clinical outcomes, so frailty can be considered a significant predictor of adverse health outcomes [4, 5]. The cancer itself, as well as treatment modalities such as surgery and chemotherapy, may exacerbate the physiologic reserve capacity of cancer patients, leading to an increased risk of developing frailty. Thus, inclusion of a frailty assessment in the preoperative evaluation of patients can effectively supplement unnoticed preoperative physical limitations. The current preoperative evaluation of breast cancer patients in our clinic still uses traditional methods, such as the 5-level grading of the American Society of Anesthesiologists and multidisciplinary consultation, but the assessment of the overall health status of these patients is not comprehensive. It was shown that preoperative frailty in surgical oncology patients is an independent risk factor for postoperative falls, postoperative complications, and the occurrence of readmission rates [6–8]. Thus, it is important to recognize frailty in these patients in a timely manner, in order to take appropriate measures.

Frailty is a syndrome that can be considered as a sensitive marker of a patient’s biological age. It represents a state of reduced multisystem physiologic function and restricted organ system reserve capacity, and can represent mental and physical fragility, both of which can ultimately lead to poorer postoperative outcomes. The definition of the frailty phenotype proposed by Fried and colleagues [9], widely used to objectively assess frailty, includes weight loss, fatigue, decreased somatic activity, reduced grip strength, and slowed movement. The advantage of this frailty assessment, based on phenotypic characteristics of declined physiological function, is that it contains a relatively small number of parameters, and each indicator can be obtained through objective measurement. However, it cannot be evaluated preoperatively for patients with limited walking ability. Nevertheless, it is generally considered a suitable diagnostic tool for frailty and is widely used in clinical practice. The cumulative deficit model proposed by Rockwood et al. [10] is based on the cumulative effect of multi-dimensional health deficits. By including health abnormalities in multiple fields such as physiology, psychology, society, and function (including comorbidity, cognitive impairment, malnutrition, and lack of social support.), the degree of frailty is judged by the proportion of total number of deficits/total number of assessment items [10]. Its advantage is that it incorporates more dimensions of information and is more suitable for early risk identification. The disadvantage is that it is complex and time-consuming. Therefore, we aimed to construct a clinically feasible and rapid risk screening tool as a more practical alternative.

It was shown that frailty is a predictor of prognosis, but as the state of frailty is reversible, early recognition of frailty and proper intervention to improve this condition can reduce adverse postoperative outcomes and improve patients’ quality of life [11]. However, the factors influencing onset and severity of frailty are multiple and include age, gender, socio-demographic factors such as educational level, malnutrition, social psychological factors such as anxiety and depression, and medication status, amongst others [12, 13]. Thus, there are numerous risk factors that influence the occurrence of frailty, and a single factor cannot fully reflect the characteristics of frailty. Therefore, we used the Fried phenotype criteria as the dependent variable. Drawing on the multi-dimensional approach of the cumulative defect model and combining the multiple domain indicators (independent variables) available before surgery, we aimed to construct a rapid preoperative tool for identifying potential frailty risk stratification. For this, the influence of multiple factors needed to be considered for a risk analysis where frailty affects the outcome of a medical intervention.

A number of existing studies were reviewed recently that demonstrated the benefits of frailty assessment in breast cancer patients [14]. As can be expected, the onset of frailty in these patients is the result of a combination of factors and included advanced age, poor physical activity, poor nutrition, and poor laboratory markers [15, 16]. A meta-analysis by Wang et al. [2] emphasized the importance of assessing frailty in breast cancer patients and recommended its inclusion in routine care. A risk model predicted the mortality rates and postoperative complications for frail patients were significantly higher than for non-frail patients, and their 30-day readmission rate is double that of non-frail patients (16 vs. 8%) [17]. It was further shown that preoperative frailty increases the risk of complications within 30 days after breast cancer surgery, raises the postoperative infection rate, and affects the postoperative recovery of patients [18]. Therefore, early identification of preoperative frailty in breast cancer patients is crucial for treatment decisions and frailty management.

A number of relevant cohort studies are available that assessed frailty in breast cancer patients, mainly stemming from European and American countries [recently reviewed in [14]. However, data from China are sparce, with little attention being paid towards the risk of preoperative frailty in breast cancer patients.

Since it cannot be excluded that the Chinese population, or sub-populations therein, are particularly vulnerable to specific risks of pre-operative frailty concurring with breast cancer, the purpose of this study was to identify the physiological and social factors affecting preoperative frailty in Chinese breast cancer patients. We established a personalized risk prediction model in the form of a line graph, in order to improve the early detection of frailty in Chinese breast cancer patients, and to provide a basis for the development of clinical care measures.

Materials and methods

Population

The study subjects were identified by convenience sampling. For this, breast cancer patients admitted between October 2022 and August 2023 for surgical treatment in a tertiary hospital in Jinzhou City, China, were selected. The inclusion criteria applied were in compliance with the Chinese Guidelines for Breast Cancer Screening and Early Diagnosis Diagnostic criteria [19] and included: (1) a diagnosis of breast cancer and proposed for surgical treatment by pathologic examination; (2) age ≥ 18 years old; (3) clear consciousness, no dyslexia, and no language communication barriers. All included patients gave informed consent to participate in this study. The two applied exclusion criteria were clinical diagnosis of co-occurring other tumors or presence of distant metastasis, and existence of psychiatric cognitive disorders. Our study followed the Declaration of Helsinki, and the study was approved by the Ethics Committee JZMULL2022073.

Sample size calculation

The minimal sample size of this cross-sectional study was based on a multifactor logistic regression sample size [20]. The event number of independent variables (EPV) method dictates that the number of events for each variable needs at least to be the number of independent variables included in the model, multiplied by a certain factor, where the number of events indicates the category with a smaller number of dependent variables. According to the literature, EPV is at least 10 [21]. We expected that the variables to be included in the prediction model would be 5 to 7. The study was set up to enroll 550 participants, which was more than enough to reach the minimum sample size, as according to a pre-survey, the incidence of preoperative frailty in 50 breast cancer patients was 28%, and considering the attrition rate of 20% of the samples, the minimum sample size would be (10 × 5) ÷ 28% (1 + 20%), requiring at least 149 patients.

Data collection

All relevant data were collected using an on-site questionnaire and standardized guidelines. The collected data included socio-demographic information, age, body mass index (BMI), education level, marital status, residency status, smoking, alcohol consumption, health insurance status, and exercise status. These data were combined with disease-related information such as the cancer stage, preoperative chemotherapy, and laboratory test results on the day of the pre-surgical evaluation after admission to the hospital. To ensure the quality of the questionnaire results, information was collected by one investigator through face-to-face interviews. Participants who did not understand the questions received explanations, and those who were unable to fill out the questionnaires for other reasons were assisted by the researcher or a family member. The completed questionnaires were immediately double-checked for completeness of data entry to ensure data accuracy. Laboratory indicators were obtained from the medical records of the patients, with informed consent.

The Fried Frailty Phenotypic Scale [9] was used to categorize the patients in a frailty and a non-frailty group. The scale consists of five criteria: unintentional weight loss of ≥ 5% or ≥ 4.5 kg in the past year; fatigue or lack of motivation for activities that lasts for more than three days in the last week; reduced grip strength based on 2 measurements and judged in conjunction with BMI; reduced walking speed whereby the normal time required to walk 5 m was ≥ 7 s (women ≤ 159 cm) or ≥ 6 s (>159 cm; decreased physical activity as measured by the Minda Leisure Time Activity Questionnaire, where women with less than 270 kcal of physical activity per week (about 2 h of walking) were considered to be less active. The scoring was dichotomized, with “yes” scoring one point and “no” scoring no points, giving a total score of 0–5 points. Absence of frailty was defined by a score of zero; 3 points or more indicated frailty, and a score 1–2 points indicated prefrailty, which in this study was included in the frailty group.

The Mini-Nutrition Assessment Scale (MNAS) was used to assess nutritional status, as proposed in the literature [22]. This scale includes 6 entries for change in food intake in the last 3 months, a change in body mass in the last 3 months, mobility issues acute illness or trauma in the last 3 months, psychiatric or psychological problems, and changes in BMI, with a total score of 14 points. A score of ≤ 7 indicates malnutrition, between 8 and 11 identifies a risk of malnutrition, and 12 to 14 indicates a good nutritional status. We used a threshold of ≤ 11 for non-normal nutritional status and 12 or higher for a normal nutritional status. The total Cronbach’s alpha coefficient for this aspect of the questionnaire was 0.78.

The Hospital Anxiety Depression Scale [23] was used to assess anxiety and depression in the study population. Each subscale has 7 entries each and is rated on a scale of 0 to 3, giving a total score between 0 and 21. We applied a threshold of 7 to indicate normal and scores from 8 and above to represent anxiety or depression. The Cronbach’s alpha coefficient for this scale was 0.879.

The socio-economic status was rated using the Social Support Rating Scale (SSRS) [24] which is widely used in China with proven reliability [25]. This scale consists of 10 entries in three categories: objective support (three items), subjective support (four items), and utilization of social support (three items). The total score ranges from 11 to 60, with higher scores indicating better social support. The scale had a Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.896.

Activities of Daily Living (ADL) was assessed as described in the literature [26]. The ADL scale consists of two parts, the Physical Self-Management Scale (PSMS) and the Instrumental Activities of Daily Living Scale (IADL), with a total of 14 entries: 6 for PSMS and 8 for IADL. Each entry is rated on a 4-point scale, with a score of 1 indicating ease of activities, 2 indicating some difficulty, 3 indicating the need of help, and a score of 4 when activity is not possible. A PSMS score of >6 was taken to indicate decreased ability to take care of oneself and an IADL score of >8 indicated a decrease in the ability to perform instrumental activities of daily living. The Cronbach’s alpha coefficient for this scale was 0.79.

Data analysis

All data were statistically analyzed using SPSS 25.0 software. The R studio was used to plot the graphs presented here. Descriptive statistical analyses were performed to explain the preoperative frailty conditions and sociodemographic characteristics. Normally distributed continuous data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, while continuous data with non-normal distribution were expressed as median and interquartile spacing (IQR), and categorical information was expressed as frequency and percentage. We used univariable logistic regression to screen for statistically significant variables among all candidate variables and to check for multicollinearity of statistically significant predictors. Multivariable binary logistic regression was performed to identify the most significant predictors from the candidate variables in the univariable analysis, and this was finally integrated into a frailty predictive model and represented in a nomogram. Model performance was assessed in two steps. Firstly, the Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve was used to calculate the area under the curve (AUC), which describes the discriminative ability of the model. The model’s sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), and negative predictive value (NPV) were also obtained. Secondly, the calibration curve of the model was validated using Bootstrap analysis with 1000 repetitions of sampling. A calibration curve was created and a Hosmer-Lemeshow test was performed to test its theoretical predictive accuracy. Lastly, the theoretical clinical utility of the model was assessed by a decision curve analysis (DCA) and a clinical impact curve was constructed. The outcome of all analyses was considered statistically significant when P < 0.05.

Results

Patients’ characteristics

A total of 550 study participants were included in this study. After removal of 31 invalid questionnaires, data were complete for 519 patients. These were divided into a non-frailty group of n = 367, and a frailty group of n = 152, based on the Fried phenotype assessment. The most common entries in the 5 sections of the Field frailty scale were fatigue (n = 122, 23.5%), followed by decreased grip strength (n = 118, 22.7%), decreased somatic activity (n = 51, 9.8%), slow walking speed (n = 36, 6.9%), and weight loss (n = 30, 5.8%) (Table 1).

Construction of the risk prediction model

Univariable and multivariable logistic regression analysis

The odds ratios with 95%CI of univariable analysis for each risk factor are shown in Table 3. For this, one-way logistic regression analysis was used, with the dependent variable Y for frailty or non-frailty, and the others as the independent variable X. The results of univariable identified that a total of 13 factors reached significance (P< 0.05), including BMI, ethnicity, daily functional exercise, IADL, chemotherapy, nutrition, anxiety and depression, and serum levels of ALB, TP, PA, HB, and SSRS. The highest significance (P<0.001) was observed for presence of chemotherapy prior to surgery, nutrition, depression, a decline in IADL, SSRS, and serum levels of ALB and HB (Table 3). Before conducting a binary logistic multiple-factor regression analysis using the 13 potential predictive factors that were statistically significant from the single-factor logistic regression analysis as independent variables, a multicollinearity test was performed. A total of seven predictive factors could be included in the binary logistic regression analysis: chemotherapy, nutrition, ALB, HB, depression, IADL and SSRS. The difference between the groups was statistically significant, indicating that these seven factors were independent predictors of preoperative frailty in breast cancer patients.

Evaluation of the nomogram model

Discriminative power of the nomogram model

To assess the discriminative power of the model, the area under the ROC curve (AUC) was used (Figure2). The AUC was 0.87 (95% CI: 0.837~0.904), and the optimal critical value was determined to be 0.361 by the maximum value of Youden's index (0.595). The model's sensitivity and specificity were 73.7% and 85.8%, respectively. The values for PPV, and NPV were 63.8%, and 89.6%, respectively, suggesting that the model has a good discriminatory ability.Fig. 2ROC curve of the nomogram model

ROC curve of the nomogram model

Accuracy of the nomogram model

The model was validated using the Bootstrap method with 1000 repeats. This produced a C-index of 0.863; the resulting calibration curve is shown in Figure 3. The curve is almost linear, indicating that the risk predictive value approaches a linear relationship. In addition, a Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test resulted in χ2= 0.521, P=0.771, suggesting that the actual observation values were not statistically different from predicted values (P>0.05). This promising outcome suggests the model may predict the risk of preoperative frailty in breast cancer patients with relative accuracy, although the model must still be assessed using true clinical data.Fig. 3Calibration curves of the Nomogram model

Discussion

In an attempt to improve post-operative prognoses for Chinese breast cancer patients, we assessed the risk of preoperative frailty. For this, we collected data from a breast cancer patient cohort that we divided into a frail and a non-frail group, based on the Field phenotype criteria. We then compared data on the somatic, psychological, and social factors that have been identified in the literature to be associated with frailty. Based on the collected data, we developed a nomogram that represents a preoperative frailty risk prediction model for breast cancer patients. The model is based on seven predictors and a number of evaluation methods were applied to demonstrate it had a good theoretical predictive ability. The prevalence of preoperative frailty in the cohort of breast cancer patients included here was 29.3%, which was higher than previously reported (15.4%) [27]. That study covered a relatively small cohort of elderly patients and excluded preoperative chemotherapy, while our cohort included breast cancer patients treated with preoperative chemotherapy. This may have aggravated the degree of frailty of some of the patients [28]. Further, we included patients at a prefrailty phase and included them in the frailty group. The rationale for this decision was, that the prefailty state is amenable to improvement and reversal, so this group of patients would most strongly benefit from clinical preoperative care interventions. While various benefits of screening breast cancer patients for frailty have been demonstrated, there is no consensus on the definition of frailty and how to screen for this [14]. A variety of factors have been recognized that are associated with the development of frailty. Preoperatively debilitated patients are more likely to have poor postoperative outcomes, which can increase patient burden. An assessment of frailty, followed by proper mitigation, can alter treatment decisions for breast cancer patients [29, 30]. Our data suggest that serum low ALB levels <40 g/L are strongly associated with preoperative frailty in breast cancer patients, increasing the risk 4.655 times compared to patients with higher ALB levels. This is in agreement with observations in lung cancer patients [31]. Low levels of ALB are an important marker of malnutrition and can also be a response to systemic inflammatory response [32]. Patients with low ALB are likely to have a low or inadequate energy intake, accelerating or exacerbating the development of frailty. Thus, attention should be paid to patients with low ALB levels, as proper nutritional measures can avoid the onset of frailty. A further known risk factor for frailty is low HB levels [33, 34]. Indeed, we identified in the studied patient group that HB <115 g/L increased the risk of frailty 4.822 times. Low HB levels may indicate a lower tolerance to stressors, or a beginning decline of physiologic reserves. Likewise, it was shown that preoperative anemic patients have increased recurrence rates of local tumors after surgery, resulting in poorer postoperative outcomes [35, 36]. One explanation is that anemia can cause a chronic state of light hypoxia, which decreases the patient’s resistance and can increase cancer invasiveness; obviously, this would worsen frailty. Incorporation of HB levels into a preoperative assessment of breast cancer patients could therefore enable patient risk stratification. In addition, we identified that chemotherapy and depression represented independent predictors of frailty. Previous studies have shown that patients that had received preoperative chemotherapy were more often frail than those who had not received chemotherapy [28, 29]. Here, we identified that chemotherapy and depression were independently associated with frailty, probably because both chemotherapy [37] and depression [38, 39] can lead to nutritional disturbances, eventually increasing the risk of frailty. The psychological burden of a diagnosis of breast cancer might aggravate these conditions [40]. Nutritional deficiencies and decreased mobility are strongly associated with the development of frailty, and both increase the risk of its development [41]. Weight loss is one of the Field criteria for frailty. A tumor-induced inflammatory response can lead to weight loss [42], in addition to anorexia, nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea, which are all side effects of chemotherapy. It is clear that preoperative malnutrition is associated with poor prognostic outcomes, falls, and increased patient burden [43]. Based on our data, we recommend nutritional interventions and functional exercises to patients identified as at risk of frailty. Decreased functioning in instrumental activities of daily living (IADL) was also found to be a predictor of frailty in the breast cancer patients under study, an observation that is not unique: most patients with cancer have difficulty in performing daily and instrumental ADLs or require assistance [44]. However, breast cancer patients have greater difficulty in performing IADLs than patients diagnosed with other cancers (thyroid, colorectal, prostate) [45]. The impaired ability to perform IADLs may lead to an inability to properly look after oneself, thereby facilitating the onset and progression of frailty, a situation that may be all the more common in breast cancer patients [46]. An important factor in the treatment and rehabilitation of cancer patients is social support, which promotes active treatment and rehabilitation [47]. Social support, as enumerated by the SSRS score, was identified as a protective factor for the occurrence of frailty. This finding is supported by previous research that showed how adequate social support can alleviate personal stress and positively affects patients’ wellbeing [48–50]. The supportive role of family and friends can build confidence in overcoming the disease and improve patients' self-management efficacy, thereby alleviating the development of frailty. We transformed the 7 identified risk factors for frailty (albumin level, hemoglobin level, chemotherapy, nutritional status, depression, daily living activity ability, and social support) into a nomogram. All 7 indicators provide information that can be obtained from routine preoperative examinations or simple scales for breast cancer patients. Compared with the Fried phenotype and cumulative defect model, the advantage of our model lies in its ability to integrate the preoperative routine process for early warning without incurring excessive time costs. As such, it reduces unnecessary frailty assessments through risk stratification. Our model was verified through multiple theoretical evaluations. It was internally validated using the Bootstrap method, and the Hosmer-Lemeshow test (giving a P value of 0.771) indicated that there was no significant deviation between the predicted and actual values, suggesting a high accuracy of the model. At the same time, the AUC of the model's discrimination was 0.870 (with a 95% confidence interval of 0.837 to 0.904). The closer the AUC value is to 1, the stronger the diagnostic ability of the model is, and an AUC value exceeding 0.7 indicates that the model has a reliable distinguishing ability. Although the specificity and sensitivity of the model were reasonable (85.8% and 73.7%, respectively) and the NPV was 89.6%, the PPV value of our model was relatively low (63.8%), suggesting that the actual clinical true positive rate determined by the model was low. Our model could not be directly compared with the frailty tool, but its based on its PVV, its performance in breast cancer was lower compared to FI-28 scale [51]. The reason for this relatively low value might be the low prevalence of frailty in this study. Although the theoretical evaluation of the model resulted in good predictive ability, it remains necessary to combine screening and diagnosis in two steps, to reduce false positives and excessive intervention. Therefore, using this model, we can now predict those patient groups that most likely will benefit most from intervention strategies to overcome (preoperative) frailty, thereby improving postoperative prognosis. This assumption will be verified in the near future.

Limitations of the study

The following limitations are recognized for our study. First, our model was based on data from a a single-center patient cohort, supported by theoretical validation but lacking external validation. Second, the study was cross-sectional and as such it could not establish a causal relationship between risk factors and frailty. Third, due to the set up of the study, it was not possible to compare the outcome with existing assessment tools for frailty. Fourth, the included laboratory indicators might not have been complete, since for instance C-reactive protein or interleukin were not routinely assessed; thus, their potential role as predictors could not be determined. And last but not least: whether the clinical application of the nomogram to identify frailty in Chinese breast cancer patients, followed by nutritional and exercise intervention as is deemed needed, will result in better post-operative prognosis remains to be demonstrated, for instance in an intervention study.

Conclusion

We present here a nomogram model to predict preoperative frailty in breast cancer patients, based on a Chinese patient cohort. Based on the multiple validation methods we applied, we consider this nomogram prediction model, which includes patient data on ALB and HB serum levels, prior chemotherapy, nutritional status, IADL, depression, and social support, a suitable tool to identify patients at risk, enabling a timely intervention response (targeted nutrition, exercise) to mitigate the frailty and possibly improve long-term outcomes for the patients. While the model provides a tool for clinical medical staff to identify preoperative frailty, its utility in a practical clinical setting remains to be demonstrated.

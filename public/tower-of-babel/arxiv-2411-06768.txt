Abstract
. 1.
introduction

The theory of
W
-algebras shows up in the two-dimensional conformal field theory, see
[
Z
]
. Finite
W
-algebras and affine
W
-algebras
are important
W
-algebras. A finite
W
-algebra
W(\mathfrak{g},e)
is certain associative algebra associated to a complex simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and a nilpotent element
e\in\mathfrak{g}
. Affine
W
-algebras are vertex algebras introduced by using quantization of the Drinfeld-Sokolov reduction, see
[
FF
,
KRW
]
.
Finite
W
-algebras can be realized as the Zhu algebras of corresponding affine
W
-algebras
[
DK
]
.
In the literature of mathematical physics, finite

W
-algebras appeared in the work of de Boer and Tjin
[
BT
]
from the viewpoint
of BRST quantum hamiltonian reduction. It is a generalization of the
universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
. In mathematics, the concept of finite
W
-algebras can be traced back to the study of Whittaker vectors and Whittaker
modules in the famous work of
[
K
]
, see also
[
Ly
]
.
The general definition of
finite
W
-algebras were introduced by Premet
[
Pr1
]
. The finite
W
-algebras are quantizations of Poisson algebras of functions on the
Slodowy slice at
e
to the adjoint orbit of
e
. Finite
W
-algebras and their representation theory have been extensively studied by mathematicians and physicists, see
[
BG
,
GG
,
BK
,
L1
,
L2
,
LO
,
Pe
,
PT
,
Pr2
,
T
,
W
]
.

From the definition of finite
W
-algebras, it is necessary and important to study various interactions between finite
W
-algebras and corresponding universal enveloping algebras
U(\mathfrak{g})
. When the adjoint orbit of
e
has the minimal dimension, the gap between
W(\mathfrak{g},e)
and
U(\mathfrak{g})
is very small, see
[
Pr2
,
Pe
]
. In this case,
W(\mathfrak{g},e)
is called the minimal nilpotent finite
W
-algebra.
In the present paper, we use the tensor product decomposition
to research the minimal nilpotent finite
W
-algebra
W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)
for
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
.
One of our main results is the following
theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

There is an algebra isomorphism
U_{S}\cong D^{\partial}_{n}\otimes W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)
, where
D_{n}^{\partial}
is the localization of the Weyl algebra
D_{n}
with respect to the Ore subset

\{\partial_{1}^{i_{1}}\dots\partial_{n}^{i_{n}}\mid i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}
.

This tensor product decomposition makes the Skryabin equivalence (see the Appendix in
[
Pr1
]
) between the category of Whittaker
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-modules and the category of
W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)
-modules very natural, see Remark
3.7
.
As an application, we give a very explicit presentation of the unique one dimensional representation of
W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)
, see Proposition
3.9
. One dimensional representations for any finite
W
-algebra were studied in
[
PT
,
T
]
. A weight
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module with finite-dimensional weight spaces is called a
cuspidal module, if every root vector acts injectively on it.
We also
show that the cuspidal module category of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
is equivalent to the category of finite-dimensional modules
over the minimal nilpotent finite
W
-algebra
W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)
, see Theorem
4.3
. Cuspidal modules are very important modules, since every simple weight module
M
with finite-dimensional weight spaces over a reductive finite-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is isomorphic to
the unique simple quotient of a parabolically induced generalized Verma module
U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{p})}S
,
where
S
is a cuspidal module over the Levi component of
\mathfrak{p}
, see
[
F
,
M
]
. A result of Fernando implies that only simple Lie algebras of type
A
and
C
have cuspidal modules.
In view of
Theorem
1.1
, it will be very interesting to describe the noncommutative fractional field of
W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)
, since this might help to resolve the Gelfand-Kirillov conjecture for
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
. In the theory of vertex algebras, centralizer (called coset in vertex algebras) construction
is also one of the major ways to construct new vertex operator algebras from given ones. For example, the coset construction
of the minimal series principal affine
W
-algebras of ADE types was
given in
[
ACL
]
in full generality.

2.
Preliminaries on the minimal nilpotent finite
W
-algebra
W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)

In this section, we collect some necessary definitions and results, including
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
and the
finite
W
-algebra
W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)
.

2.1.
Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}

Fix an integral number
n\geq 2
,
J_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&I_{n}\\
-I_{n}&0\\
\end{array}\right)
.
Recall that

\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}:=\{A\in\mathfrak{gl}_{2n}\mid A^{t}J_{n}+J_{n}A=0\}.

Any matrix in
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
is of the form:

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}X&P\\
Q&-X^{t}\\
\end{array}\right),

where
X,P,Q\in M_{n\times n}(\mathbb{C})
,
P=P^{t},Q=Q^{t}
.

Let
\{e_{i,j}|i,j=1,\dots,2n\}
be the standard basis of
\mathfrak{gl}_{2n}
.
Clearly
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
has the following basis:

\displaystyle h_{i}:

\displaystyle=e_{ii}-e_{n+i,n+i},\ 1\leq i\leq n,

\displaystyle X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}:

\displaystyle=e_{i,j}-e_{n+j,n+i},\ 1\leq i\neq j\leq n,

\displaystyle X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}:

\displaystyle=e_{i,n+j}+e_{j,n+i},\ 1\leq i\leq j\leq n,

\displaystyle X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}:

\displaystyle=e_{n+j,i}+e_{n+i,j},\ 1\leq i\leq j\leq n.

Let
\mathfrak{h}_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{C}h_{i}
which is a Cartan subalgebra of

\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
. The subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}_{n}\oplus(\oplus_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\mathbb{C}X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}})
is isomorphic to

\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
.
For each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
define
\epsilon_{i}\in\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{*}
such that
\epsilon_{i}(h_{j})=\delta_{ij}
.
Then for any
h\in\mathfrak{h}
, we have

\displaystyle\ [h,X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}]

\displaystyle=(\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j})(h)X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},

\displaystyle[h,X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}]

\displaystyle=(\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j})(h)X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}},

\displaystyle[h,X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}]

\displaystyle=-(\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j})(h)X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}.

So the root system of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
is

\Phi=\{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j},\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{l},-\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{l}\mid 1\leq i\neq j\leq n,1\leq k,l\leq n\}.

The positive system is

\Phi^{+}=\{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j},\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{l},\mid 1\leq i<j\leq n,1\leq k,l\leq n\}.

Set

\mathfrak{n}_{+}:=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha},\ \ \mathfrak{n}_{-}:=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}}_{-\alpha},

where
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=\{x\in\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}\mid[h,x]=\alpha(h)x,\ \forall\ h\in\mathfrak{h}_{n}\}
.
Then the decomposition

(2.1)

\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}=\mathfrak{n}_{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{n}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{+}

is a
triangular decomposition
of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
.

For an
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module
V
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, denote

V_{\lambda}=\{v\in V\mid h_{i}v=\lambda_{i}v,\ \text{for all}\ i=1,\dots,n\}.

A nonzero element in
V_{\lambda}
is called a weight vector.
An

\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module
V
is called a weight module if
V=\oplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}V_{\lambda}
. For a weight module
V
,
set
\mathrm{supp}(V)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\mid V_{\lambda}\neq 0\}
.
A weight
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module is called a highest weight module if it is generated by a weight vector annihilated by
\mathfrak{n}_{+}
.
A weight
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module
V
with finite-dimensional weight spaces is called a
cuspidal module provided that for any
\alpha\in\Phi
,
X_{\alpha}
acts injectively on
V
.

Let
A_{n}=\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\dots,t_{n}]
. The Weyl algebra
D_{n}

over
A_{n}
is the unital associative algebra
over
\mathbb{C}
generated by
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
,

\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{n}
subject to
the relations

[\partial_{i},\partial_{j}]=[t_{i},t_{j}]=0,\qquad[\partial_{i},t_{j}]=\delta_{i,j},\ 1\leq i,j\leq n.

The following homomorphism can be verified with a direct computation, see
[
BL
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

There is an associative algebra homomorphism

\phi:U(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n})\rightarrow D_{n}

such that

\displaystyle h_{i}\mapsto t_{i}\partial_{i}+\frac{1}{2},\ \ X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}\mapsto t_{i}\partial_{j},i\neq j,

\displaystyle X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}\mapsto t_{i}t_{j},\ \ X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}\mapsto-\partial_{i}\partial_{j}.

Any module
M
over
D_{n}
can be defined to be an
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module
through the homomorphism
\phi
. For example, if
M=(\mathbb{C}[t_{1}^{\pm 1}]/\mathbb{C}[t_{1}])\otimes\cdots\otimes(\mathbb{C}[t_{n}^{\pm 1}]/\mathbb{C}[t_{n}])
, then the
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-submodule of
M
generated by
t_{1}^{-1}\otimes\dots\otimes t_{n}^{-1}
is isomorphic to the simple highest weight module
L(-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2})
, where
-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}=(-\frac{1}{2},\dots,-\frac{1}{2})
.

2.2.
Minimal nilpotent finite
W
-algebra
W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)
and its twisted form

The general theory of finite
W
-algebras were introduced in
[
Pr1
]
.
Let
h=-h_{1},e=e_{n+1,1},f=e_{1,n+1}
. Then
\{e,h,f\}

is an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple. By
[
Pr2
]
,
e
is a minimal nilpotent element of

\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
.
The
eigenspace decomposition of the adjoint action

\text{ad}h:\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}\rightarrow\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
provides a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading

\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}=\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(-2)\oplus\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(-1)\oplus\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(0)\oplus\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(1)\oplus\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(2),

that is,
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(i)=\{x\in\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}\mid[h,x]=ix\}
,
i=0,1,-1,2,-2
.
This
\mathbb{Z}
-grading of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}

will be referred to as a
Dynkin
\mathbb{Z}
-grading
which is good for
e
.
We can see that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(2)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}X_{-2\epsilon_{1}},\ \ \mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(-2)=\mathbb{C}X_{2\epsilon_{1}},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(1)

\displaystyle=\oplus_{i=2}^{n}(\mathbb{C}X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{1}}\oplus\mathbb{C}X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{1}}),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(-1)

\displaystyle=\oplus_{i=2}^{n}(\mathbb{C}X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{i}}\oplus\mathbb{C}X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{1}}),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(0)

\displaystyle=\oplus(\oplus_{2\leq i\neq j\leq n}\mathbb{C}X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}})\oplus(\oplus_{2\leq k\leq l\leq n}\mathbb{C}X_{\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{l}})

\displaystyle\oplus(\oplus_{2\leq k\leq l\leq n}\mathbb{C}X_{-\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{l}})\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{n}.

Define a skew-symmetric bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
on
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(-1)
by

(x,y):=\mathrm{tr}([x,y]e),\ \text{for all}\ x,y\in\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(-1).

Since
\mathrm{ad}e:\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(-1)\rightarrow\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(1)
is bijective, the bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
on
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(-1)
is non-degenerate. One can see that
\oplus_{i=2}^{n}\mathbb{C}X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{i}}
is a Lagrangian (i.e. maximal
isotropic) subspace of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(-1)
with respect to the form
(\cdot,\cdot)
.
Let

\mathfrak{m}_{n}=(\oplus_{j=2}^{n}\mathbb{C}X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{j}})\oplus\mathbb{C}X_{2\epsilon_{1}},

which is a commutative
subalgebra of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
.
The map

\theta:\mathfrak{m}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},X\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\text{tr}(Xe),

defines a
one-dimensional

\mathfrak{m}_{n}
-module
\mathbb{C}_{\theta}:=\mathbb{C}v_{\mathbf{1}}
. It is clear that

\theta(X_{2\epsilon_{1}})=1,\theta(X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{j}})=0,\ \text{for any}\ j\neq 1.

Define the induced
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module
(called generalized Gelfand-Graev module)

Q_{e}:=U(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{m}_{n})}\mathbb{C}_{\theta}.

The finite
W
-algebra
W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)
is defined to be
the endomorphism algebra

W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e):=\text{End}_{\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}}(Q_{e})^{\text{op}}.

This is the original definition of finite
W
algebras defined by Premet
[
Pr1
]
.

It was shown by Premet that the associated graded algebra
\mathrm{Gr}(W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e))
of
W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)

with respect to the Kazhdan filtration is isomorphic to the symmetric algebra
S(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}^{e})
of the centralizer
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}^{e}
of
e
in
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
, see Theorem 4.6 in
[
Pr1
]
.

By the definition of
\theta
,
X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{j}}
acts locally nilpotently
on
Q_{e}
, for any
2\leq j\leq n
.
In order to obtain a new
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module from
Q_{e}
with injective action of
X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{j}}
, we will twist
Q_{e}
by a suitable automorphism of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
. This idea is motivated by the work of Kostant
[
K
]
. When a Lie homomorphism
\eta:\mathfrak{n}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfies that
\eta(X_{\alpha})\neq 0
for any simple root
\alpha
, Kostant found that Whittaker vectors in the universal Whittaker module
Y_{\eta}
are closely related with the center of
U(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n})
.

Let
X=X_{\epsilon_{2}+\epsilon_{1}}+\dots+X_{\epsilon_{n}+\epsilon_{1}}
and
\sigma
be the inner automorphism of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
defined the inner derivation
-\mathrm{ad}X
.
From

[e_{1,i}-e_{n+i,n+1},e_{1,n+j}+e_{j,n+1}]=2\delta_{ij}e_{1,n+1}=\delta_{ij}X_{2\epsilon_{1}},\ i,j\neq 1,

it can be checked that

\sigma(X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{i}})=X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{i}}+X_{2\epsilon_{1}},\sigma(X_{2\epsilon_{1}})=X_{2\epsilon_{1}}
, for any
i\neq 1
.

The
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module
Q_{e}
can be twisted by
\sigma
to be a new
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module
Q_{e}^{\sigma}
. Explicitly the action of

\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
on
Q_{e}^{\sigma}
is defined by

(2.2)

u\cdot v=\sigma(u)v,\ \ u\in\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},v\in Q_{e}.

So in
Q_{e}^{\sigma}
, we have that
X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{i}}\cdot v_{\mathbf{1}}=v_{\mathbf{1}},X_{2\epsilon_{1}}\cdot v_{\mathbf{1}}=v_{\mathbf{1}}
for any
i>1
. Define

W^{\sigma}(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e):=\text{End}_{\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}}(Q^{\sigma}_{e})^{\text{op}}.

Lemma 2.2
.

W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)\cong W^{\sigma}(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)
.

Proof.

From

g(u\cdot v)=g(\sigma(u)v)=\sigma(u)g(v)=u\cdot g(v),\ \forall\ u\in\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},\ v\in Q_{e},

it follows that
the identity map

W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)\rightarrow W^{\sigma}(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e):g\mapsto g

is a well-defined algebra isomorphism.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

In fact, we also have that
W^{\sigma}(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)\cong W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},\sigma^{-1}(e))
, since
[\sigma(u),e]=0
if only if
[u,\sigma^{-1}(e)]=0
for all
u\in\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
.

We denote the vector
(1,1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
by
\mathbf{1}
.
Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbf{1}}
be the category
whose objects are
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-modules
M
satisfying that

,

.
For an
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module
M
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbf{1}}
,
denote

Any nonzero vector in
\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{1}}(M)
is called a Whittaker vector. A module in
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbf{1}}
is called a Whittaker module.

By the equivalence given by Skryabin (see the Appendix in
[
Pr1
]
) and isomorphism between

and

, we have the following equivalence.

Lemma 2.4

The functors

are inverse equivalence
between
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbf{1}}
and the

Let

From the isomorphism

3.1.

Since

\displaystyle A_{\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{1}}

\displaystyle=X_{\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{1}}X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{k}}X_{2\epsilon_{1}}^{-1}-h_{k},

Theorem 3.2
.

We have that

Proof.

(3.3)

\displaystyle X_{\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{1}}

Since

3.2.
Isomorphism between
W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)
and
C_{U_{S}}(B)

The following theorem says that all
Whittaker vectors in the module
Q^{\sigma}_{e}
can be given by using elements in
C_{U_{S}}(B)
.
This result is motivated by the classical Kostant’s Theorem, see
[
K
]
.

Theorem 3.4
.

We have the isomorphism
W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)\cong C_{U_{S}}(B)
.

Proof.

Since
W^{\sigma}(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)\cong W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)
,
it suffices to show that
W^{\sigma}(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)\cong C_{U_{S}}(B)
.
Since
X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}}-1,\dots,X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{n}}-1,X_{2\epsilon_{1}}-1
act locally nilpotently on
Q^{\sigma}_{e}
,
Q^{\sigma}_{e}
can be extended to
a
U_{S}
-module.
Recall that
W^{\sigma}(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)=\text{End}_{\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}}(Q^{\sigma}_{e})^{\text{op}}
. Since
Q^{\sigma}_{e}
is generated by

v_{\mathbf{1}}
, any
\eta\in W^{\sigma}(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)
is determined by
\eta(v_{\mathbf{1}})
.
Moreover
\eta(v_{\mathbf{1}})\in\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{1}}(Q^{\sigma}_{e})
. By the definition of
C_{U_{S}}(B)
, any

u\in C_{U_{S}}(B)
can define a
\eta_{u}\in W^{\sigma}(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)
such that
\eta_{u}(v_{\mathbf{1}})=u\cdot v_{\mathbf{1}}
. That is, there is a homomorphism

\Phi:C_{U_{S}}(B)\rightarrow\text{End}_{\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}}(Q^{\sigma}_{e})^{\text{op}},u\mapsto\eta_{u}.

Note that as a vector space
Q^{\sigma}_{e}\cong U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})\otimes C_{U_{S}}(B)
.
By the definition of
Q^{\sigma}_{e}
and decomposition

U_{S}\cong C_{U_{S}}(B)\otimes B
, it follows that
\Phi
is injective.

Conversely for any
\eta\in W^{\sigma}(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)
, since
Q^{\sigma}_{e}=U_{S}\cdot v_{\mathbf{1}}
,
we can suppose that

\eta(v_{1})=\sum_{0\leq\mathbf{r}\leq\mathbf{m}}h^{\mathbf{r}}u_{\mathbf{r}}\cdot v_{\mathbf{1}},

where

u_{\mathbf{r}}\in C_{U_{S}}(B),h^{\mathbf{r}}=h_{1}^{r_{1}}\dots h_{n}^{r_{n}}
, and
``\leq"
is the usual
lexicographical order.
For any
i\in\{2,\dots,n\}
, since
[h_{1},X_{2\epsilon_{1}}]=2X_{2\epsilon_{1}},[h_{i},X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{i}}]=-X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{i}}
, this yields

X_{2\epsilon_{1}}h_{1}^{m_{1}}=(h_{1}-2)^{m_{1}}X_{2\epsilon_{1}},\ \ X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{i}}h_{i}^{m_{i}}=(h_{i}+1)^{m_{i}}X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{i}},

and

[X_{2\epsilon_{1}},h_{1}^{m_{1}}]=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}\binom{m_{1}}{j}(-2)^{j}h_{1}^{m_{1}-j}X_{2\epsilon_{1}},\ \ [X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{i}},h_{i}^{m_{i}}]=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}\binom{m_{1}}{j}h_{i}^{m_{i}-j}X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{i}}.

Then by induction on
m_{1}
and
m_{i}
, we can find nonzero

k_{m_{1}},k_{m_{i}}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

(X_{2\epsilon_{1}}-1)^{m_{1}}h_{1}^{m_{1}}v_{\mathbf{1}}=k_{m_{1}}v_{\mathbf{1}},\ \ (X_{2\epsilon_{1}}-1)^{s_{1}}h_{1}^{m_{1}}v_{\mathbf{1}}=0,\forall\ s_{1}>m_{1},

and

(X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{i}}-1)^{m_{i}}h_{i}^{m_{i}}v_{\mathbf{1}}=k_{m_{i}}v_{\mathbf{1}},\ \ (X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{i}}-1)^{s_{i}}h_{i}^{m_{i}}v_{\mathbf{1}}=0,\forall\ s_{i}>m_{i}.

Consequently

\displaystyle u_{\mathbf{m}}v_{\mathbf{1}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k_{m_{1}}\dots k_{m_{n}}}(X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{n}}-1)^{m_{n}}\dots(X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}}-1)^{m_{2}}(X_{2\epsilon_{1}}-1)^{m_{1}}\eta(v_{1})=0.

Similarly we have that
u_{\mathbf{r}}v_{\mathbf{1}}=0
for any
0<\mathbf{r}\leq\mathbf{m}
.
So
\eta(v_{1})=u_{0}\cdot v_{\mathbf{1}}
for some
u_{0}\in C_{U_{S}}(B)
. Therefore

\Phi
is also surjective, and hence it is isomorphic.
∎

Let
D_{n}^{\partial}
be the localization of
D_{n}

We can verify the following map is an algebra isomorphism:

By Theorems
3.2
and

There is an algebra isomorphism

C_{U_{S}}(B)
-module
V
, let

X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}}v=\dots=X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{n}}v=X_{2\epsilon_{1}}v=v,

for any

v\in V
, consider the induced module
U_{S}\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{m}_{n})_{S}C_{U_{S}}(B)}V
. Then
the functors

M\mapsto\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{1}}(M),\ \ \ \ V\mapsto U_{S}\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{m}_{n})_{S}C_{U_{S}}(B)}V,

are inverse equivalence
between
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbf{1}}
and the
category of
C_{U_{S}}(B)
-modules.

3.3.
One dimensional representations of
W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)

By Corollary 4.1 in
[
Pr2
]
,
C_{U_{S}}(B)
has only one ideal of
codimension
1
, i.e.,
C_{U_{S}}(B)
has unique one dimensional module. From the following example of a Whittaker module over
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
,
we can find this one dimensional module.

Example 3.8
.

Let
\tau
be the automorphism of
D_{n}
such that

t_{i}\mapsto t_{i}+\delta_{i1},\ \ \partial_{i}\mapsto\partial_{i}+1-\delta_{i1},\ i=1,\dots,n.

The
D_{n}
-module
P=:(\mathbb{C}[t_{1}^{\pm 1}]/\mathbb{C}[t_{1}])\otimes\mathbb{C}[t_{2},\dots,t_{n}]

can be twisted by
\tau
to be a new
D_{n}
-module
P^{\tau}
. Under the map
\phi
in Propostion
2.1
,
P^{\tau}
becomes an
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module.
The image of
t_{1}^{-1}
in
P
is also denoted by

t_{1}^{-1}
. Note that

t_{1}\cdot t_{1}^{-1}=\partial_{i}\cdot t_{1}^{-1}=t_{1}^{-1},\ \text{for all}\ 2\leq i\leq n.

Then it can be verified that the
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-submodule
P_{1}^{\tau}
of
P^{\tau}

generated by
t_{1}^{-1}
is an irreducible module in
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbf{1}}
with
\dim\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{1}}(P_{1}^{\tau})=1
.

The following result gives an explicit characterization of the unique one dimensional
C_{U_{S}}(B)
-module using the generators in Lemma
3.1
.

Proposition 3.9
.

The unique one dimensional
C_{U_{S}}(B)
-module
V_{-\frac{1}{2}}
is
defined by the map:

\displaystyle A_{\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{1}}\mapsto-\frac{1}{2},\ \ A_{\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{l}}\mapsto\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta_{lk}}{2},\ \ A_{-\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{l}}\mapsto-1,

\displaystyle A_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}\mapsto-\frac{1}{2},\ \ A_{\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{1}}\mapsto-\frac{1}{4},\ \ A_{-\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{1}}\mapsto-\frac{1}{2}-n,

\displaystyle A_{-2\epsilon_{1}}\mapsto-n^{2}+\frac{1}{4}.

Proof.

By Example
3.8
, the module
P^{\tau}_{1}

satisfies that
\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{1}}(P^{\tau}_{1})=\mathbb{C}t_{1}^{-1}
. Since

[\mathfrak{m}_{n},C_{U_{S}}(B)]=0
,
\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{1}}(P^{\tau}_{1})
is a one dimensional
C_{U_{S}}(B)
-module.
By direct computations, we can obtain the action of
C_{U_{S}}(B)
on
\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{1}}(P^{\tau}_{1})
as follows:

\displaystyle A_{\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{1}}t_{1}^{-1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}t_{1}^{-1},\ \ A_{\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{l}}t_{1}^{-1}=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\delta_{kl})t_{1}^{-1},

\displaystyle A_{\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{1}}t_{1}^{-1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}t_{1}^{-1},\ \ A_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}t_{1}^{-1}=-\frac{1}{2}t_{1}^{-1},

\displaystyle A_{-\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{l}}t_{1}^{-1}

\displaystyle=-t_{1}^{-1},\ \ A_{-\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{k}}t_{1}^{-1}=(-\frac{1}{2}-n)t_{1}^{-1},

\displaystyle A_{-2\epsilon_{1}}t_{1}^{-1}

\displaystyle=(-n^{2}+\frac{1}{4})t_{1}^{-1}.

∎

Remark 3.10
.

One dimensional representations of a finite
W
-algebra
W(\mathfrak{g},e)
play an important role on the theory of
W(\mathfrak{g},e)
, since the images of their annihilators under the Skryabin
equivalence are all completely prime primitive ideals of
U(\mathfrak{g})
, and therefore play a key role in Joseph’s theory
of Goldie rank polynomials
[
L2
]
. The completely prime primitive ideal of
U(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n})
corresponding to the module Proposition
3.9
is referred to as the Joseph ideal, see
[
Pr2
]
.

4.
Equivalence between the category of cuspidal modules over
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
and
\mathbf{fmod}
over
W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)

In this section, we use Theorem
3.2
to study the relationship between weight modules over
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
and
finite-dimensional modules over the minimal nilpotent finite
W
-algebra
W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)
.

4.1.
Block decomposition

Let
Z
be the center of the algebra
U(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n})
, and
Z^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Hom}(Z,\mathbb{C})
be set of central characters. By
\chi_{\lambda}
we denote the central character of the irreducible highest weight module with highest weight
\lambda
.
By the Harish-Chandra’s Theorem, any central character
\chi
is equal to
\chi_{\lambda}
for some
\lambda
.
A module
M
is said to
have the generalized central character
\chi
, if for any
v\in M
, there is
some positive integer
k
such that
(z-\chi(z))^{k}v=0
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the category of cuspidal modules over
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
. For each
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, let
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}}
be the full subcategory of

\mathcal{C}
consisting of modules
M
such that
\mathrm{supp}(M)\subseteq[\mathbf{a}]:=\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{B}
, where

\mathbf{B}:=\{\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\mid b_{1}+\dots+b_{n}\in 2\mathbb{Z}\}.

For any
\chi\in Z^{\prime}
, let
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}}(\chi)

be the full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}}
of modules with
generalized central character
\chi
. Since the action of
Z
on
cuspidal modules is locally finite, we have the decomposition:

\mathcal{C}=\oplus_{\chi\in Z^{\prime},[\mathbf{a}]\in\mathbb{C}^{n}/\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}}(\chi).

By Theorem
3.4
, we can use
W
to denote both
C_{U_{S}}(B)
and
W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)
.
Let
W\text{-fmod}
be the category of finite-dimensional

W
-modules. By Theorems
3.2
and
3.4
, we can identify the center of
U_{S}
with the center of
W
. For any
\chi\in Z^{\prime}
, we can also define the full subcategory
W(\chi)\text{-fmod}
of
W\text{-fmod}
of modules with
generalized central character
\chi
. According to the action of center, we have the decomposition:

W\text{-fmod}=\oplus_{\chi\in Z^{\prime}}W(\chi)\text{-fmod}
.

4.2.
The equivalence between
W\text{-fmod}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}}(\chi_{\lambda})

Let
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{n},\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{*}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}}(\chi_{\lambda})
is nonempty. We define the functor
F_{\mathbf{a}}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}}(\chi_{\lambda})\rightarrow W(\chi_{\lambda})\text{-fmod}
such that
F_{\mathbf{a}}(M)=M_{\mathbf{a}}
. From
[W,\mathfrak{h}_{n}]=0
, we have
WM_{\mathbf{a}}\subset M_{\mathbf{a}}
. So
M_{\mathbf{a}}\in W(\chi_{\lambda})\text{-fmod}
.
Conversely, for a
V\in W(\chi_{\lambda})\text{-fmod}
, let each
h_{i}

act on it as the scalar
a_{i}
.
Define

G_{\mathbf{a}}(V)=\text{Ind}_{U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})W}^{U_{S}}V=U_{S}\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})W}V.

It is clear that
G_{\mathbf{a}}(V)=\mathbb{C}[X_{2\epsilon_{1}}^{\pm 1},X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{n}}^{\pm 1}]\otimes V
.
Thus we have a functor
G_{\mathbf{a}}
from

W\text{-fmod}
to the category of weight modules over
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
W(\chi_{\lambda})
-
\mathrm{fmod}
is non-empty, then

W(\chi_{\lambda})
-
\mathrm{fmod}
is equivalent to
W(\chi_{-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}})
-
\mathrm{fmod}
, where
-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}=(-\frac{1}{2},\dots,-\frac{1}{2})
.

Proof.

Suppose that
V
is a module in
W(\chi_{\lambda})\text{-fmod}
.
Then
\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}G_{\mathbf{a}}(V)
is a uniformly bounded weight
module with the generalized central character
\chi_{\lambda}
. By Lemma 9.1 in
[
M
]
,
\lambda
satisfies the conditions:

\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n-1}-\lambda_{n},\lambda_{n}\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z},\lambda_{n-1}+\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-2}.

Using the translation functor (see
[
BG
]
),
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{1}}(\chi_{\lambda})
is equivalent to
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{1}}(\chi_{-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}})
. By the
Skryabin equivalence between
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{1}}(\chi_{\lambda})

and
W(\chi_{\lambda})\text{-fmod}
, each
W(\chi_{\lambda})\text{-fmod}
is equivalent to
W(\chi_{-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}})\text{-fmod}
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

The one dimensional module
V_{-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}}
defined in Proposition
3.9
is the unique simple module
in the block
W(\chi_{-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}})
-
\mathrm{fmod}
.

Proof.

Since
both
P^{\tau}_{1}
(see Example
3.9
) and the simple highest weight module
L(-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2})

are annihilated by
\ker\phi
, they have the same central character
\chi_{-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}}
.
By Theorem 6.2 in
[
Pr2
]
, the central characters of simple finite-dimensional modules over
W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)
with different dimensions are distinct. Then the uniqueness of
V_{-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}}
follows.
∎

In the following theorem, we show that each
W(\chi_{\lambda})\text{-fmod}
is equivalent to the corresponding block of the cuspidal category.

Theorem 4.3
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, there exists
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that

W(\chi_{\lambda})
-
\mathrm{fmod}
is equivalent to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}}(\chi_{\lambda})
.

Proof.

From the result in
[
BKLM
]
, if
\mathcal{C}(\chi_{\lambda})
is non-empty, then it is equivalent to
\mathcal{C}(\chi_{-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}})
.
By Lemma
4.1
, it suffices to consider that

\lambda=-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}
. Choose
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
a_{1}-\frac{1}{2},\dots,a_{n}-\frac{1}{2}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We will show that

G_{\mathbf{a}}(V)
is a cuspidal
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module, i.e.,
G_{\mathbf{a}}(V)\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}}(\chi_{-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}})
. First, suppose that
V
is a simple module in
W(\chi_{-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}})\text{-fmod}
. By Lemma
4.2
,

V
is isomorphic to the module
V_{-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}}
defined in Proposition
3.9
.
Denote
X^{\mathbf{m}}=X_{2\epsilon_{1}}^{m_{1}}X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}}^{m_{2}}\dots X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{n}}^{m_{n}}
. Then

\displaystyle h_{1}(X^{\mathbf{m}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=(|\mathbf{m}|+m_{1}+a_{1})(X^{\mathbf{m}}\otimes v),

\displaystyle h_{i}(X^{\mathbf{m}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=(a_{i}-m_{i})(X^{\mathbf{m}}\otimes v),i=2,\dots,n,

\displaystyle I_{n}(X^{\mathbf{m}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=(|\mathbf{a}|+2m_{1})(X^{\mathbf{m}}\otimes v),

for any
v\in V
. By Proposition
3.9
and the formula (
3.3
), we can verify that

\displaystyle X_{\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{1}}(X^{\mathbf{m}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=(X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{k}}^{-1}X_{2\epsilon_{1}}A_{\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{1}}+h_{k}X_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{k}}^{-1}X_{2\epsilon_{1}})(X^{\mathbf{m}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=X^{\mathbf{m}-e_{k}+e_{1}}\otimes(a_{k}-m_{k}+1+A_{\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{1}})v

\displaystyle=(a_{k}-m_{k}+\frac{1}{2})X^{\mathbf{m}-e_{k}+e_{1}}\otimes v.

Similarly, we obtain that

\displaystyle X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}(X^{\mathbf{m}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=(a_{i}-m_{i}+\frac{1}{2})X^{\mathbf{m}-e_{i}+e_{j}}\otimes v,

\displaystyle X_{\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{l}}(X^{\mathbf{m}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=(a_{l}-m_{l}+\frac{1}{2})(a_{k}-m_{k}+\delta_{kl}+\frac{1}{2})X^{\mathbf{m}-e_{k}-e_{l}+e_{1}}\otimes v,

\displaystyle X_{\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{1}}(X^{\mathbf{m}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=(a_{k}-m_{k}+\frac{1}{2})(|\mathbf{m}|+a_{1}+m_{1}-\frac{1}{2})X^{\mathbf{m}-e_{k}}\otimes v,

and

\displaystyle X_{-\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{l}}(X^{\mathbf{m}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=-X^{\mathbf{m}+e_{k}+e_{l}-e_{1}}\otimes v,

\displaystyle X_{-\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{k}}(X^{\mathbf{m}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=(\frac{1}{2}-|\mathbf{m}|-a_{1}-m_{1})X^{\mathbf{m}+e_{k}-e_{1}}\otimes v,

\displaystyle X_{-2\epsilon_{1}}(X^{\mathbf{m}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=-(\frac{3}{2}-|\mathbf{m}|-a_{1}-m_{1})(\frac{1}{2}-|\mathbf{m}|-a_{1}-m_{1})X^{\mathbf{m}-e_{1}}\otimes v.

By the condition on
\mathbf{a}
,
G_{\mathbf{a}}(V_{-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}})
is a cuspidal
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module.
By induction on the length of
V
, we can show that
G_{\mathbf{a}}(V)
is a cuspidal
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module,
for any
V\in W(\chi_{-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}})\text{-fmod}
.

We can check that
G_{\mathbf{a}}F_{\mathbf{a}}\cong\text{Id}
and
F_{\mathbf{a}}G_{\mathbf{a}}\cong\text{Id}
. Therefore

F_{\mathbf{a}},G_{\mathbf{a}}
are inverse equivalence
between
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}}(\chi_{\lambda})
and
W(\chi_{\lambda})
-
\mathrm{fmod}
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

In
[
BKLM
]
, it was shown that the category of cuspidal
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-modules is semi-simple. Premet has shown that the category of finite-dimensional modules over the minimal nilpotent

W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},e)
is also semi-simple, see Corollary 7.1 in
[
Pr2
]
. Theorem
4.3
explains this coincidence.

For
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, let
N(\mathbf{a})
be the linear span of

\{t^{\mathbf{b}}:=t_{1}^{a_{1}+b_{1}}t_{2}^{a_{2}+b_{2}}\dots t_{n}^{a_{n}+b_{n}}\mid\mathbf{b}\in\mathbf{B}\}.

Then
N(\mathbf{a})
is an
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module under the map
\phi
defined in Proposition
2.1
. Since
both
N(\mathbf{a})
and the simple highest weight module
L(-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2})

are annihilated by
\ker\phi
, i.e., they have the same annihilator,
so the central character of
N(\mathbf{a})
is
\chi_{-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}}
.
If
a_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
i=1,\dots,n
, the
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module
N(\mathbf{a})
is simple and cuspidal. It has been shown that every completely pointed simple cuspidal
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module is isomorphic to
N(\mathbf{a})

for
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
with
a_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
i=1,\dots,n
, see
[
BL
]
.
By Theorem
4.3
,
G_{\mathbf{a}+\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}}(V_{-\frac{1}{2}})\cong N(\mathbf{a})
.

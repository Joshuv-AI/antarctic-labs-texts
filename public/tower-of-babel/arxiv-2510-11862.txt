Unitary representations attached to parabolic subgroups: the case of abelian unipotent radical
Dan Ciubotaru

Abstract
. We classify the unitary representations with integral infinitesimal character in Lusztig’s category of unipotent representations in the case when the geometric parameter space comes from the action of a Levi subgroup on the abelian nilradical of a (maximal) parabolic subalgebra. We organise the unitary representations into microlocal Arthur packets. This is a test case for investigating a conjectural description of unitary representations with integral infinitesimal character.

1.
Introduction

Let
F
be a nonarchimedean local field with residue field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, Weil group
W_{F}
, Frobenius element
\mathsf{Fr}
, and inertia group
I_{F}
. Let
{{\mathcal{G}}}
be a complex almost simple
F
-split group with connected centre, and
\mathsf{Inn}{{\mathcal{G}}}
the set of (pure) inner twists of
{{\mathcal{G}}}
. Let
G
be the complex Langlands dual group. A simplified
Langlands parameter
is a continuous group homomorphism

\varphi:W_{F}\times SL(2,{\mathbb{C}})\to G,

satisfying certain properties. We will be interested in the parameters for which
I_{F}\subseteq\ker\varphi
. To each such
\varphi
, Lusztig
[
Lu1
]
attached a finite packet
\Pi(\varphi)
consisting of irreducible representations for the groups
{{\mathcal{G}}}^{\prime}(F)
,
{{\mathcal{G}}}^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Inn}{{\mathcal{G}}}
. The set of packets
\Pi(\varphi)
, when
\varphi
varies over
G
-conjugacy classes of Langlands parameters give a partition of the set of (equivalence classes) of irreducible representations for the groups
{{\mathcal{G}}}^{\prime}(F)
with unipotent supercuspidal support.

An
Arthur parameter
is a continuous group homomorphism

\psi:W_{F}\times SL(2,{\mathbb{C}})\times SL(2,{\mathbb{C}})\to G.

such that
\psi(W_{F})
is bounded.
Conjecturally, to each
\psi
there should exist a finite packet
{{\mathcal{A}}}(\psi)
consisting of irreducible unitary
{{\mathcal{G}}}^{\prime}(F)
-representations satisfying a list of desiderata. The easiest expectations to state are:

(1)

\Pi(\varphi_{\psi})\subseteq{{\mathcal{A}}}(\psi)
, where
\varphi_{\psi}:W_{F}\times SL(2,{\mathbb{C}})\to G
is defined by

\varphi_{\psi}(w,1)=\psi(w,\left(\begin{matrix}||w||^{1/2}&0\\
0&||w||^{-1/2}\end{matrix}\right),\left(\begin{matrix}||w||^{1/2}&0\\
0&||w||^{-1/2}\end{matrix}\right))\text{ and }\varphi_{\psi}(1,\left(\begin{matrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right))=\psi(1,\left(\begin{matrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right),1);

(2)

If
\psi^{\vee}
denotes the parameter obtained from
\psi
by flipping the two
SL(2,{\mathbb{C}})
, then

{{\mathcal{A}}}(\psi^{\vee})={\mathsf{AZ}}({{\mathcal{A}}}(\psi)),

where
{\mathsf{AZ}}
is the Aubert-Zelevinsky involution
[
Au
]
.

When
{{\mathcal{G}}}
is a classical group, an endoscopic construction of the packets
{{\mathcal{A}}}(\psi)
was realised by Arthur
[
Ar
]
. In general, the only uniform candidate for constructing
{{\mathcal{A}}}(\psi)
in the
p
-adic setting is the microlocal packet proposed by Adams-Barbasch-Vogan and Vogan
[
ABV
,
Vo
]
. See also
[
CFMMX
]
for more details.

We restrict to the set of parameters
\psi
with
I_{F}\subseteq\ker\psi
. Let
s=\varphi_{\psi}((\mathsf{Fr},\left(\begin{matrix}q^{1/2}&0\\
0&q^{-1/2}\end{matrix}\right)))
be a semisimple element in
G
. We call (the
G
-conjugacy class of)
s
the
infinitesimal character
of
\psi
. We define in the same way the infinitesimal character of any Langlands parameter
\varphi
with
I_{F}\subseteq\ker\varphi
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{q}=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\mid\operatorname{Ad}(s)x=qx\}
. The connected reductive group
G(s)
acts with finitely many orbits on
V=\mathfrak{g}_{q}
. Lusztig’s results imply that there is a one-to-one correspondence between the representations in the union of L-packets
\Pi(\varphi)
such that
\varphi(\mathsf{Fr})=s
and the set of simple
G(s)
-equivariant perverse sheaves on the vector space
V
. If
{\mathcal{E}}
is an equivariant perverse sheaf, let us denote by
\pi({\mathcal{E}})
the corresponding irreducible representation in an L-packet
\Pi(\varphi)
.

A fundamental invariant of
{\mathcal{E}}
is the
singular support
or
characteristic cycle
, which is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-linear combination of closures of conormal bundles
\overline{T^{*}_{{\mathcal{O}}}V}
,
{{\mathcal{O}}}
ranging over the (finite) set of
G(s)
-orbits on
V
,

CC({\mathcal{E}})=\sum_{{{\mathcal{O}}}}m_{{\mathcal{O}}}({\mathcal{E}})~\overline{T^{*}_{{\mathcal{O}}}V}.

Following
[
ABV
]
, define for every orbit
{{\mathcal{O}}}
, the microlocal packet

{{\mathcal{A}}}({{\mathcal{O}}})=\{{\mathcal{E}}\mid m_{{\mathcal{O}}}({\mathcal{E}})\neq 0\}

The
microlocal Arthur packet
is

(1.1)

{{\mathcal{A}}}(\psi)=\{\pi({\mathcal{E}})\mid{\mathcal{E}}\in{{\mathcal{A}}}({{\mathcal{O}}}_{\phi_{\psi}})\},

where
{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\phi_{\psi}}
is the
G(s)
-orbit of the nilpotent element
\varphi_{\psi}(\left(\begin{matrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right))
.

Now suppose
s
is
integral
, meaning
s=q^{\lambda}
, where
\lambda
is integral as in Definition
2.1
. Motivated by the results of Atobe and Minguez for symplectic and orthogonal groups
[
AM
]
, also
[
HJLLZ
]
for further examples and conjectures, we are interested in the classification of unitary representations in the L-packets with integral
s
. A natural expectation given the results in
loc. cit.
is

Integral unitary representations
:
Suppose
\varphi
is a Langlands parameter with
I_{F}\subseteq\ker\varphi
and
\varphi(\mathsf{Fr})
integral. For every
\pi\in\Pi(\varphi)
,
\pi
is unitary if and only if
\pi
belongs to a microlocal packet
{{\mathcal{A}}}(\psi)
.

We can say a bit more about the candidates for the microlocal packets
{{\mathcal{A}}}(\psi)
if
\psi
has integral infinitesimal character
s=q^{\lambda}
. Fix a Cartan subalgebra
{\mathfrak{t}}
of
\mathfrak{g}
and assume without loss of generality that
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{t}}
is dominant with respect to the roots
\Phi
of
{\mathfrak{t}}
in
\mathfrak{g}
. Since we are interested in unitary representations of
p
-adic groups, we may restrict to those
\lambda
that belong to the
fundamental parallelipiped
(FPP)

(1.2)

0\leq\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle\leq 1,\text{ for all }\alpha\in\Phi\text{ simple root}.

The FFP bound comes from a conjecture of Vogan for real reductive groups, inspired by the classfication works of Barbasch. In that setting, it was recently proved in
[
DMB
]
. On the other hand, for unipotent representations of
p
-adic groups, the FPP bound was implicitly known and it is easy to establish, essentially due to the fact that, unlike the case of real reductive groups, the only possible reducibility hyperplanes for the principal series occur when a root equals
0
or
1
. For a more general proof of FPP for
p
-adic groups see
[
JLLMB
]
.

In the integral case,
\psi(\mathsf{Fr})=1
, so
\psi
is the same as a group homomorphism
\psi:SL(2,{\mathbb{C}})\times SL(2,{\mathbb{C}})\to G
. More concretely, this is equivalent to a pair of commuting Lie triples
\{e_{L},h_{L},f_{L}\}
and
\{e_{A},h_{A},f_{A}\}
such that

h_{L}+h_{A}=2\lambda.

The centraliser
G(s)=G(\lambda)
is a Levi subgroup
M
of
G
and the
q
-eigenspace for
\operatorname{Ad}(s)
in
\mathfrak{g}
is the same the
(+1)
-eigenspace
V=\mathfrak{g}_{1}
of
\operatorname{ad}(\lambda)
in
\mathfrak{g}
. As explained in section
2
, the
M
-space
V
can be identified with
V=\mathfrak{n}/[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}]
, where
\mathfrak{n}
is the nilradical of the Jacobson-Morozov parabolic for
\lambda
. The commuting Lie triples have the property that

e_{L},e_{A}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}=V,\ f_{L},f_{A}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-1}=V^{*},\ h_{L},h_{A}\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}={{\mathfrak{m}}}.

We may arrange that
h_{L},h_{A}\in{\mathfrak{t}}
.

The Kazhdan-Lusztig parameter for
\varphi_{\psi}
is the
M
-orbit
{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\varphi_{\psi}}
of
e_{L}
in
V
. If
{{\mathcal{O}}}
is any
M
-orbit in
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
, there exists an
adapted
Lie triple
\{e,h,f\}
,
e\in{{\mathcal{O}}}
,
h\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
,
f\in\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
, so the question is if we can find an appropriate commuting partner
Lie triple.

Definition 1.1
.

An
M
-orbit
{{\mathcal{O}}}
is said to be
of Arthur type
if it has an adapted Lie triple
\{e,h,f\}
such that there exists another adapted triple
\{e^{\prime},h,^{\prime},f^{\prime}\}
that commutes with it and
h+h^{\prime}=2\lambda
. In this case we say that
({{\mathcal{O}}},{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\prime})
is an
Arthur pair
, where
{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\prime}=M\cdot e^{\prime}
.

The above expectation can be reformulated more precisely as follows.

Conjecture 1.2

(Integral unitary representations)
.

Suppose
\varphi
is a Langlands parameter with
I_{F}\subseteq\ker\varphi
and
\varphi(\mathsf{Fr})=q^{\lambda}
,
\lambda
dominant integral in FPP. Let
P=MN
be the parabolic subgroup defined by
\lambda
. The Levi subgroup
M=G(\lambda)
acts on
V=\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\mathfrak{n}/[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}]
with finitely many orbits. Suppose
\pi({\mathcal{E}})\in\Pi(\varphi)
is parametrised by the simple
M
-equivariant perverse sheaf
{\mathcal{E}}
. Then
\pi({\mathcal{E}})
is unitary if and only if there exists an Arthur pair
({{\mathcal{O}}},{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\prime})
of
M
-orbits in
V
(Definition
1.1
) such that
\overline{T^{*}_{{\mathcal{O}}}V}
occurs in the characteristic cycle
CC({\mathcal{E}})
.

Example 1.3
.

The irreducible spherical representation
\pi_{0}
with Satake parameter
q^{\lambda}
is parametrised by
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}
(the skyscraper sheaf at
0
) and
CC({\mathcal{E}}_{0})=\{0\}\times V^{*}=\overline{T^{*}_{0}V}
. If the zero orbit is of Arthur type, it must be the case that
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}h
, for the neutral element
h
in a Lie triple for the open dense orbit. The conjecture implies that
\pi_{0}
is unitary if and only if
2\lambda
is the middle element of a Lie triple in
\mathfrak{g}
. This was verified in
[
CH
]
using the classification of the spherical unitary dual
[
Ba
,
BC1
,
Ci1
]
.

Let
w_{0}
be the longest element in the finite Weyl group of
G
. Since
w_{0}\cdot h=-h
for every middle element of a Lie triple in
\mathfrak{g}
(
[
GS
]
), it immediately follows that for Arthur pairs to exist at all at
\lambda
, we must have
w_{0}\cdot\lambda=-\lambda
. Moreover, if
\{e,h,f\}
and
\{e^{\prime},h^{\prime},f^{\prime}\}
are commuting Lie triples,
\{e+e^{\prime},h+h^{\prime},f+f^{\prime}\}
is also a Lie triple. This means that
2\lambda
must be the middle element of a nilpotent orbit, in fact, an even nilpotent orbit since
\lambda
is assumed to be integral. Hence a weaker form of Conjecture
1.2
is:

Conjecture 1.4

(Weaker form)
.

If
\lambda
is integral, there exist unitary representations with Langlands parameter
\varphi
such that
\varphi(W_{F})=q^{\langle\lambda\rangle}
if and only if
\lambda
is half the middle element of an even nilpotent
G
-orbit.

The “only if” part is known by the work of Kazhdan-Lusztig (the Iwahori case) and Lusztig (in general): if
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}h
for
h
a middle element of a nilpotent
G
-orbit, then the open
G(\lambda)
-orbit in
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
parametrises tempered, hence unitary, representations.

Since
M
acts with finitely many orbits on
V
, there exists a bijection, the
Piasetskii duality
, between the
M
-orbits in
V
and the
M
-orbits in
V^{*}
. Denote the dual of
{{\mathcal{O}}}
by
{{\mathcal{O}}}^{*}
. This is an
M
-orbit in
V^{*}=\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
. Let
\{e^{*},h^{*},f^{*}\}
be a Lie triple for
{{\mathcal{O}}}^{*}
, with
e^{*}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1},h^{*}\in{{\mathfrak{t}}},f^{*}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
. Let
{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}
be the
M
-orbit of
f^{*}
, this is an
M
-orbit in
V=\mathfrak{g}_{1}
. Set
e^{\vee}=f^{*}
,
h^{\vee}=-h^{*}
, and complete to a Lie triple
\{e^{\vee},h^{\vee},f^{\vee}\}
,
f^{\vee}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
. This can be done by taking the image of
\{e^{*},h^{*},f^{*}\}
under the Chevalley involution of
\mathfrak{g}
. The pair
({{\mathcal{O}}},{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee})
is a natural candidate for constructing Arthur pairs. Our main result is:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\lambda
be an integral dominant infinitesimal character in FPP. Suppose the associated parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{m}+\mathfrak{n}
has abelian nilradical
\mathfrak{n}
. In particular
M
is a maximal Levi subgroup and
V=\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\mathfrak{n}
.

(1)

If
w_{0}\lambda\neq-\lambda
, there are no Arthur pairs
({{\mathcal{O}}},{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\prime})
, and no representations with infinitesimal character
q^{\lambda}
are hermitian.

(2)

Suppose
w_{0}\lambda=-\lambda
.
For every
M
-orbit
{{\mathcal{O}}}
,
({{\mathcal{O}}},{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee})
is an Arthur pair.
Every irreducible representation
\pi
with infinitesimal character
q^{\lambda}
is unitary.

The proof is in Propositions
3.2
and
4.1
. Notice that this verifies Conjecture
1.2
in the abelian nilradical case, a particularly nice and uniform case. Claim (1) holds for all real
\lambda
, see Proposition
4.2
, not just the integral ones.

The classification of parabolic subgroups with abelian unipotent radical is recalled in Table
1
. It is worth noting that the condition
w_{0}\cdot\lambda=-\lambda
is known to be equivalent to the prehomogeneous vector space
V
being
regular
, in the sense of the centraliser of a generic point being reductive (
[
Ru
]
). In section
5
, we discuss in detail the geometry of the
M
-space
\mathfrak{n}
when
\mathfrak{n}
is abelian and record the microlocal packets in Theorem
5.2
. This is essentially already available in different places in the literature, for example
[
LW
,
LR
,
Pa
,
Ro
,
SK
,
Ki
]
, and we include the results here for convenience.

The case of the abelian nilradical is part of a more general family of examples, namely when the
M
-space
V
is irreducible and
spherical
. As recalled in section
2
,
V=\mathfrak{n}/[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}]
is irreducible if and only if
M
is maximal.
V
is spherical if a Borel subgroup of

has an open orbit in

. The classification of irreducible spherical vector spaces is known from
[
Ka
]
. An important feature is that for spherical vector spaces, the characteristic cycles are multiplicity free, see
[
LW
]
. In a follow-up paper, we will consider the general maximal parabolic subgroups.

2.
Setup

Let
G
be a complex connected almost simple group over
\mathbb{C}
. For the applications to representations of
p
-adic groups,
G
is the Langlands dual group. Assume that
G
has simply-connected derived subgroup. Fix a Borel subgroup
B
and a maximal torus
T\subset B
. Let
\Delta\subset\Phi^{+}\subset\Phi
be the sets of simple, positive, all roots, respectively, of
T
in
G
. The positive roots
\Phi^{+}
correspond to
B
. Let
\mathfrak{g},{{\mathfrak{b}}},{\mathfrak{t}}
be the corresponding Lie algebras. Let
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
denote the root space of
\alpha
and fix a pinning
\{X_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

An element
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{t}}
is called
integral
if
\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for all
\alpha\in\Phi
.

Let
\{\omega^{\vee}_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\Delta\}
be the set of fundamental coweights,i.e., the dual basis of
\Delta
in
{\mathfrak{t}}
. Of course,
\lambda
is integral if and only if
\lambda=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}c_{\alpha}\omega_{\alpha}^{\vee}
with
c_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Since we will be interested in the applications to the unitary representations of
p
-adic groups, we may restrict to those
\lambda
that belong to the
fundamental parallelipiped
(FPP)

(2.1)

0\leq\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle\leq 1,\text{ for all }\alpha\in\Delta.

Equivalently, an integral
\lambda
is in FPP if and only if
c_{\alpha}\in\{0,1\}
, for all
\alpha\in\Delta
. Fix an integral
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{t}}
belonging to FPP.

Define
J\subseteq\Delta
by
J=\{\alpha\in\Delta\mid c_{\alpha}=\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle=0\}
. With this notation,
\lambda=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta\setminus J}\omega_{\alpha}^{\vee}
. Let
\mathfrak{m}\supseteq{\mathfrak{t}}
be the Levi subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
defined by the simple roots
J
. Let
\mathfrak{n}=\bigoplus_{\beta\in\Phi^{+},\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle>0}\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}
and
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
the parabolic subalgebra of
\lambda
. Also denote
\mathfrak{n}^{-}=\bigoplus_{\beta\in\Phi^{+},\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle<0}\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}
. Let
P=MN
the corresponding parabolic subgroup. It is clear that, in this way, we have a one-to-one correspondence between the set of integral
\lambda
in FPP and the set of standard parabolic subgroups
P
with respect to
B
.

In this setting, the Kazhdan-Lusztig geometry involves the action of
M=Z_{G}(\lambda)
on the
(+1)
-eigenspace of
\operatorname{ad}(\lambda)
on
\mathfrak{g}
,

\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\bigoplus_{\beta\in\Phi^{+},\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle=1}\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}.

Every positive root
\beta
can be written uniquely as

\beta=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}\in J}a_{\alpha^{\prime}}\alpha^{\prime}+\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta\setminus J}b_{\alpha}\alpha,

a_{\alpha},b_{\alpha^{\prime}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}.

Following
[
Ro
]
, define
\mathsf{shape}(\beta)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta\setminus J}b_{\alpha}\alpha
and
\mathsf{level}(\beta)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta\setminus J}b_{\alpha}.

Lemma 2.2
.

As an
M
-representation,
\mathfrak{g}_{1}\cong\mathfrak{n}/[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}].

Proof.

Since
\lambda=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta\setminus J}\omega_{\alpha}^{\vee}
, we have
\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle=1
if and only if
\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta\setminus J}b_{\alpha}\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle=1
. Moreover, this is if and only if
\mathsf{level}(\beta)=1
and
\mathsf{shape}(\beta)=\alpha
for a root
\alpha\in\Delta\setminus J
. It means that
\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\bigoplus_{\mathsf{level}(\beta)=1}\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}
, and this is precisely
\mathfrak{n}/[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}].

∎

Denote
V=\mathfrak{n}/[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}]
, a finite-dimensional
M
-representation. This is known as the
first internal Chevalley module
. For every
\alpha\in\Delta\setminus J
, denote
V_{\alpha}=\text{span}\{X_{\beta}\mid\mathsf{shape}(\beta)=\alpha\}.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
Ro
]
)
.

(1)

V=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta\setminus J}V_{\alpha}
is the decomposition of
V
into irreducible
M
-subrepresentations. The irreducible
M
-module

V_{\alpha}
has highest weight
\max_{\alpha}
, the unique maximal-height positive root of shape
\alpha
.

(2)

The dual (contragredient)
M
-representation is
V^{*}=\mathfrak{n}^{-}/[\mathfrak{n}^{-},\mathfrak{n}^{-}]=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta\setminus J}V_{-\alpha}^{*}
. The irreducible
M
-module
V_{\alpha}^{*}
has highest weight
-\alpha.

In particular, if
J
is maximal and
\{\alpha\}=\Delta\setminus J
, then
V=V_{\alpha}
is an irreducible
M
-representation.

We look at the geometry of
M
acting on the prehomogeneous space
V=\mathfrak{n}/[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}]
. In the Kazhdan-Lusztig geometry, the relevant category is that of
M
-equivariant perverse sheaves on
V
. The simple objects in this category are in one-to-one correspondence with the pairs
({{\mathcal{O}}},{\mathcal{L}})
, where
{{\mathcal{O}}}
is an
M
-orbit in
V
and
{\mathcal{L}}
is an irreducile
M
-equivariant local system supported on
{{\mathcal{O}}}
. These local systems are in one-to-one correspondence with the irreducible representations of the group of fundemantal group
\pi_{1}({{\mathcal{O}}})\cong Z_{M}(x)/Z_{M}(x)^{0}
, for
x\in{{\mathcal{O}}}
.

By Riemann-Hilbert correspondence, there is an equivalence of categories between the category of
M
-equivariant perverse sheaves on
M
and the category of (regular holonomic)
M
-equivariant
{\mathcal{D}}
-modules on
V
(
{\mathcal{D}}_{V}
-modules
). The latter is equivalent with the category of modules for a finite quiver
\hat{Q}
, see
[
LW
, Theorem 3.4]
. There are two facts from
[
LW
]
that will be useful in the next section:

(1)

The characteristic cycles of all simple equivariant
{\mathcal{D}}_{V}
-modules when
V
is a spherical
M
-space are multiplicity free
[
LW
, Corollary 3.19]
.

(2)

The category of
{\mathcal{D}}_{V}
-modules has an involution
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}
defined as the composition of the Fourier transform
{{\mathcal{F}}}:{\mathcal{D}}_{V}\text{-mod}\to{\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}}\text{-mod}
by the Chevalley automorphism.
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}
induces a graph automorphism of
\hat{Q}
. For example,
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}
interchages the delta
{\mathcal{D}}
-module at
0
with the module of functions of
V
. Moreover, if
S
is a simple
{\mathcal{D}}_{V}
-module with support
\overline{{{\mathcal{O}}}}
, then
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}(S)
has support
\overline{{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}}
. In the cases treated in the next section, these facts are sufficient to determine
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}
. Moreover, if

CC(S)=\sum_{i}c_{i}[\overline{T_{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{i}}V}]

then

CC(S)=\sum_{i}c_{i}[\overline{T_{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{i}^{\vee}}V}].

We mention that in
[
EM
]
, it is shown that the action of the Aubert-Zelevinsky involution on the set of irreducible representations with Iwahori-fixed vectors corresponds to the action of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}
on the Kazhdan-Lusztig parameter space.

3.
Parabolic subgroups with abelian unipotent radicals

A particularly nice example is when
\mathfrak{n}
is abelian, so
V=\mathfrak{n}
. In this case
J
is necessarily maximal, but not every maximal
J
has this property. The classification of parabolic subgroups
P
with abelian nilradical for
J=\Delta\setminus\{\alpha\}
is given in Table
1
.

When
\mathfrak{n}
is abelian, the structure of
M
-orbits on
V
has a simple, uniform description. Denote
\Phi(1)=\{\beta\in\Phi^{+}\mid\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle=1\}
.
Let
\gamma\in\Phi^{+}
be the highest root. Notice that
\gamma\in\Phi(1)
. The
upper canonical string

\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\dots,\gamma_{r}

[
Pa
]
is obtained by setting
\gamma_{1}=\gamma
and, inductively, defining
\gamma_{i+1}
to be the highest root in
\Phi^{i}(1)=\{\beta\in\Phi(1)\mid\beta\text{ orthogonal to }\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\dots,\gamma_{i}\}.
The integer

Set
{{\mathcal{O}}}_{0}=\{0\}
and let

,

(Orbit structure I

(2)

(3)

(4)

The first three claims are all in

∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Retain the notation as in Theorem

. If

Proof.

Let

Finally, by

4.
Unitarity

In this section, we explain why all irreducible hermitian representations in Lusztig’s unipotent category with (dominant) integral infinitesimal character
\lambda
, such that the corresponding parabolic subgroup has abelian unipotent radical are unitary. The key observation is that, in this situation,
\langle\lambda,\gamma\rangle=1
, where
\gamma
is the highest positive root. This means that
\lambda
is precisely the vertex of the fundamental alcove in the dominant Weyl chamber farthest from the origin.

Proposition 4.1
.

If
0\leq\langle\lambda^{\prime},\gamma\rangle\leq 1
, then all irreducible hermitian representations in Lusztig’s unipotent category with infinitesimal character
\lambda^{\prime}
are unitary.

Proof.

Firstly, suppose
\pi
is an irreducible
{{\mathcal{G}}}(F)
-representation with Iwahori-fixed vectors. Let
\chi_{\lambda^{\prime}}
denote the unramified character of the maximal torus
{\mathcal{T}}(F)
corresponding to the element
q^{\lambda}\in{\mathfrak{t}}
. Then
\pi
is a subquotient of the unramified minimal principal series
X(\chi_{\lambda^{\prime}})=i_{{\mathcal{B}}(F)}^{{{\mathcal{G}}}(F)}(\chi_{\lambda^{\prime}})
(normalised parabolic induction). The reducibility hyperplanes of an unramified principal series
X(\chi_{\nu})
,
\nu\in{\mathbb{R}}\Phi
are exactly the hyperplanes
\langle\nu,\alpha\rangle=1
,
\alpha\in\Phi
. Since
X(\chi_{0})
is irreducible and unitary (being unitarily induced), a classical deformation argument (“complementary series”) implies that, if hermitian,
X(\chi_{\nu})
remains unitary as we deform
\nu
from
0
towards the first hyperplane of reducibility. If
\nu
is chosen dominant, this implies that for all
\nu
in the fundamental alcove
0\leq\langle\nu,\gamma\rangle<1
,
X(\chi_{\nu})
is still unitary. In the limit, i.e., the boundary of the alcove, all the subquotients of
X(\chi_{\nu})
are also unitary. In particular, so are the hermitian subquotients of
X(\chi_{\gamma})
.

Next, let
\pi
be an irreducible unipotent representation with infinitesimal character
\lambda^{\prime}
, not necessarily with Iwahori vectors. By
[
Lu1
]
,
\pi
is in a Bernstein component of the category of smooth representations that is equivalent to the module category of an affine Hecke algebra with unequal parameters. The affine Hecke algebra is constructed from a Levi subgroup
L_{0}
of
G
that has a pair
({\mathbb{O}}_{0},{\mathcal{L}}_{0})
consisting of a nilpotent
L
-orbit
{\mathbb{O}}_{0}
and a cuspidal
L_{0}
-equivariant local system
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}
supported on
{\mathbb{O}}_{0}
. Let
{{\mathcal{O}}}_{r}
be the unique open orbit of
G(\lambda^{\prime})
in
\mathfrak{g}_{1}(\lambda^{\prime})
. A necessary condition is that
G\cdot{\mathbb{O}}_{0}\subset\overline{G\cdot{{\mathcal{O}}}_{r}}
.

The classification of cuspidal
({\mathbb{O}}_{0},{\mathcal{L}}_{0})
is in
[
Lu3
]
. If
\lambda^{\prime}
is in the fundamental alcove, the nilpotent orbit
G\cdot{{\mathcal{O}}}_{r}
is small, and there are very few possibilities for non-toral
L_{0}
. By inspection, the only possibilities are:

(1)

G=Sp(2n)
,
L_{0}=Sp(2)\times GL(1)^{2(n-1)}
,
{\mathbb{O}}_{0}
, the regular nilpotent orbit in
Sp(2)
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}
the nontrivial local system;

(2)

G=SO(2n)
,
L_{0}=SO(4)\times GL(1)^{2(n-2)}
,
{\mathbb{O}}_{0}
the regular nilpotent orbit in
SO(4)
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}
the nontrivial local system;

(3)

G=E_{7}
,
L_{0}=(3A_{1})^{\prime\prime}
,
{\mathbb{O}}_{0}
the regular nilpotent orbit in
SL(2)^{3}
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}
the local system nontrivial on each
SL(2)
component.

In (2), (3), the only representations that can occur in our case are supercuspidal, hence unitary. They correspond to
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda
, half the middle element of
{\mathbb{O}}_{0}
and
{{\mathcal{O}}}_{r}\subset G\cdot{\mathbb{O}}_{0}.

In (1), the representations that occur are the subquotients of the intermediate principal series
i_{{\mathcal{P}}(F)}^{{\mathcal{G}}(F)}(\sigma\otimes\chi_{\nu})
where
{\mathcal{P}}\supset{\mathcal{B}}
is the parabolic subgroup for which the derived subgroup of its Levi subgroup is of type
B_{1}
,
\sigma
is a supercuspidal unipotent representation, and
\chi_{\nu}
is an unramified character. The condition
0\leq\langle\lambda^{\prime},\gamma\rangle\leq 1
implies that
\nu
is a continuous deformation from
0
to at most the first reducibility hyperplane, so we are exactly in a similar situation to the Iwahori case above.

∎

Proposition 4.2
.

An irreducible representation in Lusztig’s unipotent category with infinitesimal character
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}\Phi\subset{{\mathfrak{t}}}
is hermitian
only if

w_{0}\cdot\lambda=-\lambda.

If
w_{0}
is central in
W
, then every irreducible representation with real infinitesimal character is hermitian.

Proof.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible
{{\mathcal{G}}}(F)
-representation with real infinitesimal character
\lambda
. As in the previous proof, there is a Levi subgroup
L_{0}\leq G
and a cuspidal datum
({\mathbb{O}}_{0},{\mathcal{L}}_{0})
in
L_{0}
, such that the Berstein component of the category of smooth representations to which
\pi
is equivalent to the module category of an affine Hecke algebra
{{\mathcal{H}}}
(possibly with unequal parameters) constructed from
({\mathbb{O}}_{0},{\mathcal{L}}_{0})
in
[
Lu1
]
. The algebra
{{\mathcal{H}}}
contains the finite Hecke algebra
{{\mathcal{H}}}_{q}(W_{0})
with parameters for the finite Weyl group
W_{0}=N_{G}(L_{0})/{L_{0}}
. The fact that this is a Weyl group is due to the special nature of the Levi subgroups
L_{0}
supporting cuspidal data. It also contains an abelian subalgebra
{{\mathcal{A}}}
that can be identified with the ring of regular functions on a maximal torus in the centraliser in
G
of a Lie triple for
{\mathbb{O}}_{0}
, such that
{{\mathcal{H}}}={{\mathcal{H}}}_{q}(W_{0})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}{{\mathcal{A}}}
as a
({{\mathcal{H}}}_{q}(W_{0}),{{\mathcal{A}}})
-bimodule. See
[
CMBO1
]
for a detailed explanation and a summary of Lusztig’s construction.

Let
\pi_{0}
be the
{{\mathcal{H}}}
-module corresponding to
\pi
. The algebra
{{\mathcal{H}}}
is isomorphic to a convolution algebra of functions on the
p
-adic group
{{\mathcal{G}}}(F)
and therefore comes with a natural
\star
-operation. It is known and easy that
\pi
is
{{\mathcal{G}}}(F)
-hermitian if and only if
\pi_{0}
is
{{\mathcal{H}}}
-hermitian with respect
\star
. See
[
BC2
]
for example.

The centre of
{{\mathcal{H}}}
is
{{\mathcal{A}}}^{W_{0}}

[
Lu4
]
. In the above equivalence, the central character of
\pi_{0}
is the
W_{0}
-orbit of an element
\nu_{0}\in{{\mathfrak{t}}}
such that

\lambda=\frac{1}{2}h_{0}+\nu_{0},

for
h_{0}
the middle element of a Lie triple for
{\mathbb{O}}_{0}
. Because of the particular nature of
L_{0}
,
w(L_{0})=L_{0}
for all
w\in W
. In particular,
w_{0}\in N_{G}(L_{0})
. Let
w_{0}^{z}
denote the image of
w_{0}
in
W_{0}
. Since
w_{0}
maps
\Phi^{+}
into
\Phi^{-}
, it follows that
w_{0}
also maps all the positive roots of
{{\mathcal{H}}}
to negative, the former being vectors in
{\mathbb{R}}\Phi^{+}
. Hence
w_{0}^{z}
is the longest element of
W_{0}
.

Now
w_{0}\cdot\lambda=-\frac{1}{2}h_{0}+w_{0}^{z}\cdot\nu_{0}
, and therefore

w_{0}\cdot\lambda=-\lambda\text{ if and only if }w_{0}^{z}\cdot\nu_{0}=-\nu_{0}.

In other words, we have reduced the question to one about modules over the affine Hecke algebra. Since
{{\mathcal{A}}}
is abelian and
\pi_{0}
is finite dimensional, we can decompose
\pi_{0}
into a sum of generalised
{{\mathcal{A}}}
-eigenspaces. The corresponding set of eigenvalues
\Xi(\pi_{0})
are called the
{{\mathcal{A}}}
-weight of
\pi_{0}
. By
[
EM
]
,
\Xi(\pi_{0})
uniquely determines
\pi_{0}
if
\pi_{0}
has real central character. Let
\pi_{0}^{h}
denote the hermitian dual of the
{{\mathcal{H}}}
-module
\pi_{0}
. By
[
BC3
]
,

\Xi(\pi_{0}^{h})=-w_{0}^{z}(\Xi(\pi_{0})).

Hence
\pi_{0}^{h}\cong\pi_{0}
if and only if
-w_{0}^{z}(\Xi(\pi_{0}))=\Xi(\pi_{0})
. All weights of
\pi_{0}
are
W_{0}
-conjugate to the central character
\nu_{0}
which may be assumed
W_{0}
-dominant. This implies that if
\pi_{0}
is hermitian then
-w_{0}^{z}\cdot\nu_{0}
is
W_{0}
-conjugate to
\nu_{0}
, but since both are
W_{0}
-dominant, this is equivalent to
-w_{0}^{z}\nu_{0}=\nu_{0}
.

For the partial converse, if
w_{0}
is central in
W
, then it acts by the negative of the identity on
{{\mathfrak{t}}}
, and in particular, so does
w_{0}^{z}
on
\Xi(\pi_{0})
. Hence,
\Xi(\pi_{0}^{h})=\Xi(\pi_{0})
, and therefore
\pi_{0}\cong\pi_{0}^{h}.
We mention that this part was known (in the equivalent setting of graded affine Hecke algebras) from
[
BC3
, Lemma 3.1.1 and Corollary 4.3.2]
.
∎

To complete the proof of the unitarity part of Theorem
1.5
, notice that by Propositions
4.1
and
4.2
, it only remains to discuss the case when
w_{0}
is not central, but
w_{0}\cdot\lambda=-\lambda
. Let
\lambda
be an integral dominant infinitesimal character such that
P=MN
has abelian radical
N
, as in the hypothesis of Theorem
1.5
. By inspecting Table
1
, we see that the only cases remaining are:

(1)

G=GL(2n)
,
M=GL(n)\times GL(n)
,
V={\mathbb{C}}^{n}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. As explained in section
5
, there are
(n+1)
orbits, all with connected isotropy groups. The corresponding irreducible representations of
{{\mathcal{G}}}=GL(n,F)
are the Langlands quotients of the parabolically induced representations

i_{GL(1)^{n-k}\times GL(2)^{2k}\times GL(1)^{n-k}}^{GL(n,F)}(\mathsf{St}\otimes|\det|^{\underline{\nu}}),\quad\underline{\nu}=(\underbrace{\frac{1}{2},\dots,\frac{1}{2}}_{n-k},\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{2k},\underbrace{-\frac{1}{2},\dots,-\frac{1}{2}}_{n-k}),

where
\mathsf{St}
is the Steinberg representation of the Levi subgroup.

(2)

G=Spin(2n)
,
n
odd,
M=GL(1)\times Spin(2n-2)
,
V={\mathbb{C}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2n-2}
. There are three orbits, and only the open orbits has a disconnected isotropy group, with component group
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
. The irreducible representations corresponding to the open orbit are tempered, hence unitary. The spherical representation is hermitian since
w_{0}\cdot\lambda=-\lambda
. Finally, the representation corresponding to the remaining orbit is the Langlands quotient of the parabolically induced representation

i_{GL(2)\times GL(1)^{n-2}}^{PSO(2n,F)}(\mathsf{St}\otimes|\det|^{\underline{\nu}}),\quad\underline{\nu}=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{n-2}).

In both cases, the inducing Langlands datum
(L,\mathsf{St},\underline{\nu})
,
L
Levi subgroup, has the property that
w_{0}\cdot(L,\mathsf{St},\underline{\nu})=(L,\mathsf{St},-\underline{\nu})
. This implies that the Langlands quotient is hermitian, see for example
[
BM
, Proposition 1.5]
. By Proposition
4.1
, these representations are then unitary.

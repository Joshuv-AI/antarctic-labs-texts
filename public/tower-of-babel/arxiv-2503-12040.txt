On the distribution of t t -hooks of doubled distinct partitions Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author.
Hyunsoo Cho, Byungchan Kim, Eunmi Kim ∗, Ae Ja Yee

Abstract
. Recently, Griffin, Ono, and Tsai examined the distribution of the number of t t -hooks in partitions of n n , which was later followed by the work of Craig, Ono, and Singh on the distribution of the number of t t -hooks in self-conjugate partitions of n n . Motivated by these studies, in this paper, we further investigate the number of t t -hooks in some subsets of partitions. More specifically, we obtain the generating functions for the number of t t -hooks in doubled distinct partitions and the number of t t -shifted hooks in strict partitions. Based on these generating functions, we prove that the number of t t -hooks in doubled distinct partitions and the number of t t -shifted hooks in strict partitions are both asymptotically normally distributed.

2.2.
Durfee square and Frobenius symbol

For a partition
\lambda
, let

\lambda_{s}-s\geq 0

\lambda

of

The
Frobenius symbol
of
\lambda

\mathfrak{F}(\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}-1&\lambda_{2}-2&\cdots&\lambda_{s}-s\\
\lambda^{\prime}_{1}-1&\lambda^{\prime}_{2}-2&\cdots&\lambda^{\prime}_{s}-s\end{pmatrix}.

As the Durfee square is unique, so is the Frobenius symbol.
For the partition
(6,6,4,2,2)
in Figure

,
the side of its Durfee square is

and the corresponding Frobenius symbol is

\mathfrak{F}\big((6,6,4,2,2)\big)=\begin{pmatrix}5&4&1\\

2.3.
Wright’s map

In the Littlewood decomposition given in the next section, Wright’s map
[
18
]
is one of the essential components. For completeness, we provide a description of the map
[
6
]
; however, for brevity, we present a modified version, which will work for our purpose.
For a two-rowed array

\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&a_{2}&\ldots&a_{u}\\
b_{1}&b_{2}&\ldots&b_{v}\end{pmatrix},

with
a_{1}>\cdots>a_{u}\geq 0
and
b_{1}>\cdots>b_{v}\geq 0
, we define a partition
\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{\ell})
as follows:

(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{u})=(a_{1}+1-(u-v),\ldots,a_{u}+u-(u-v))

and

(\mu_{u+1},\dots,\mu_{\ell})=(b_{1}-v+1,b_{2}-v+2,\dots,b_{v})^{\prime}.

Wright’s map sends this two-rowed array to the partition
\mu
.

2.4.
Littlewood decomposition

It is well known
[
13
, Section 2.7]
that there is a bijection

\displaystyle\varphi~:~\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{t}\times\underbrace{\mathcal{P}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{P}}_{\text{$t$-copies}}

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle\mapsto\big(\lambda^{(t)},\lambda_{(0)},\dots,\lambda_{(t-1)}\big)

with
|\lambda|=|\lambda^{(t)}|+t\sum\limits_{i=0}^{t-1}|\lambda_{(i)}|
, where
\mathcal{P}_{t}
is the set of
t
-core partitions.
This bijection
\varphi
is called the
Littlewood decomposition
of
\lambda
at
t
.
Garvan, Kim, and Stanton
[
9
]
also constructed the Littlewood decomposition using
t
-residue diagrams and biinfinite words in the letters
N
and
E
(see
[
9
, Section 2]
). In this paper, we will follow the description given in
[
6
]
.

For a partition
\lambda
, we take its Frobenius symbol:

\mathfrak{F}(\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&\cdots&a_{s}\\
b_{1}&\cdots&b_{s}\end{pmatrix}.

Let

a_{i}=tq_{i}+r_{i}\text{ with $0\leq r_{i}\leq t-1$}

and

b_{i}=tq^{\prime}_{i}+r^{\prime}_{i}\text{ with $0\leq r^{\prime}_{i}\leq t-1$}.

We split the entries of the symbol into two-rowed arrays
\mathfrak{C}(\lambda_{(j)})
for
j=0,\ldots,t-1
as follows:

(2.4)

\mathfrak{C}(\lambda_{(j)})=\begin{pmatrix}a_{j,1}&\cdots&a_{j,u_{j}}\\
b_{j,1}&\cdots&b_{j,v_{j}}\end{pmatrix},

where
a_{j,k}=q_{i}
with
r_{i}=j
for some
i
and
b_{j,k}=q^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}}
with
r^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}}=t-j-1
for some
i^{\prime}
. In other words, the top (resp. bottom) entries of
\mathfrak{C}(\lambda_{(j)})
are the quotients of the top (resp. bottom) entries of
\mathfrak{F}(\lambda)
whose residues are equal to
j
(resp.
t-1-j
). We then apply the modified Wright’s map to
\mathfrak{C}(\lambda_{(j)})
to get
\mathfrak{F}(\lambda_{(j)})
.
For example, let
t=3
and

\mathfrak{F}(\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}7&5&4&0\\
5&4&2&1\end{pmatrix}.

Then

\mathfrak{C}(\lambda_{(0)})=\begin{pmatrix}&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathfrak{C}(\lambda_{(1)})=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathfrak{C}(\lambda_{(2)})=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
~\end{pmatrix},

from which we get

\lambda_{(0)}=(2),\quad\lambda_{(1)}=(3,3),\quad\lambda_{(2)}=(1).

\operatorname{Li}_{2}(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{z^{n}}{n^{2}}

Lemma 2.2

Suppose that

. If

near

, then for any

, we have as

D_{\theta}
that

\sum_{m\geq 0}f\left(w(m+a)\right)=\frac{1}{w}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)dx-\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}b_{n}\frac{B_{n+1}(a)}{n+1}w^{n}+O(w^{N}),

where
B_{n}(a)
is the Bernoulli polynomial.

We also need the following integral approximation using
I
-Bessel function, which is a slightly modified version of
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and let
\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2},A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfy
k\ll\sqrt{n}
,

,

, and
k\vartheta_{1}
,

. Then we have

\int_{\frac{k}{n}-ik\vartheta_{1}}^{\frac{k}{n}+ik\vartheta_{2}}z^{-s}e^{Az+\frac{B}{z}}dz=2\pi i\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)^{\frac{s-1}{2}}I_{s-1}\left(2\sqrt{AB}\right)+\begin{cases}{O}\left(n^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\right)&\text{ if }s\geq 0,\\
{O}\left(n^{\frac{s-1}{2}}\right)&\text{ if }s<0,\end{cases}

where

is the

3.
Generating functions

3.1.
Littlewood decomposition for doubled distinct partitions

Let
t
be a positive integer. The Littlewood decomposition
\phi_{t}
maps a doubled distinct partition
\lambda\lambda
to
(\omega,\underline{\nu}):=(\omega;\nu_{(0)},\dots,\nu_{(t-1)})
such that the following are true (see
[
9
, Bijection 3]
and
[
12
, p.19]
).

Theorem 3.1
.

(DD1)

\omega\in\mathcal{DD}_{t}
is a
t
-core and
\nu_{(0)},\dots,\nu_{(t-1)}
are ordinary partitions.

(DD2)

We have that
\nu_{(i)}=\nu_{(t-i)}^{\prime}
for each
i=1,\dots,\lceil t/2\rceil-1
. Moreover,
\mu\mu:=\nu_{(0)}\in\mathcal{DD}
.

(DD2’)

If
t
is even, then
\pi:=\nu_{\left(t/2\right)}\in\mathcal{SC}
.

(DD3)

We have that

|\lambda\lambda|=\begin{cases}|\omega|+2t\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{t-1}{2}}\left|\nu_{(i)}\right|+t|\mu\mu|&\text{if $t$ is odd,}\\
|\omega|+2t\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{t-2}{2}}\left|\nu_{(i)}\right|+t|\mu\mu|+t|\pi|&\text{if $t$ is even.}\\
\end{cases}

(DD4)

The number of
t
-hooks in
\lambda\lambda
is the sum of the number of
1
-hooks in the
t
-quotient, that is

n_{t}(\lambda\lambda)=\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}n_{1}(\nu_{(i)}).

To compute
\widehat{n}_{t}(\lambda\lambda)
, we need to analyze how the
t
-hooks of
\lambda\lambda
are distributed to the quotient of
\lambda\lambda
under the Littlewood decomposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

For a strict partition
\lambda
and its corresponding doubled distinct partition
\lambda\lambda
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle n_{t}(\lambda\lambda)=\begin{cases}2\widehat{n}_{t}(\lambda\lambda)+1\quad&\text{ if $t\notin\lambda$ and $t/2\in\lambda$},\\
2\widehat{n}_{t}(\lambda\lambda)-1\quad&\text{ if $t\in\lambda$ and $t/2\notin\lambda$},\\
2\widehat{n}_{t}(\lambda\lambda)\quad&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Here,
t/2\notin\lambda
when
t
is odd.

Proof.

Let
h_{i,j}(\lambda)
be the hook length of the box
(i,j)
in the Young diagram of
\lambda
. Then

(3.2)

h_{i,j}:=h_{i,j}(\lambda)=(\lambda_{i}-j)+(\lambda^{\prime}_{j}-i)+1=\lambda_{i}+\lambda^{\prime}_{j}-(i+j)+1.

For a strict partition
\lambda
, it follows from the construction of the doubled distinct partition
\lambda\lambda
that

(3.3)

(\lambda\lambda)^{\prime}_{i}=\begin{cases}(\lambda\lambda)_{i}-1&\text{ for $1\leq i\leq\ell$},\\
\ell&\text{ for $i=\ell+1$},\\
(\lambda\lambda)_{i-1}&\text{ for $i>\ell+1$},\end{cases}

where
\ell
is the number of parts of
\lambda
. Also, it follows from the construction of
\lambda\lambda
that

(3.4)

(\lambda\lambda)_{i}=\lambda_{i}+i\quad\text{ for $1\leq i\leq\ell$.}

We consider
h_{i,j}
for
\lambda\lambda
in the following four cases. The identity in each case follows from (
3.2
) and (
3.3
).

Case 1.

For
1\leq i,j\leq\ell
,

(3.5)

h_{i,j}(\lambda\lambda)=(\lambda\lambda)_{i}+(\lambda\lambda)_{j}-(i+j)=h_{j,i}(\lambda\lambda).

Case 2.

For
1\leq i\leq\ell
and
j=\ell+1
,

(3.6)

h_{i,\ell+1}(\lambda\lambda)=(\lambda\lambda)_{i}+\ell-(i+\ell+1)+1=(\lambda\lambda)_{i}-i.

Case 3.

For
1\leq i\leq\ell
and
\ell+1<j
,

h_{i,j}(\lambda\lambda)=(\lambda\lambda)_{i}+(\lambda\lambda)_{j-1}-(i+j)+1=h_{j-1,i}(\lambda\lambda).

Case 4.

For
1\leq j\leq\ell<i
,

h_{i,j}(\lambda\lambda)=(\lambda\lambda)_{i}+(\lambda\lambda)_{j}-1-(i+j)+1=h_{j,i+1}(\lambda\lambda).

Due to the symmetries in Cases 1, 3, and 4, boxes
(i,j)
in the Young diagram can be paired up based on the same hook length unless
i=j
or
j=\ell+1
.

We now consider
h_{i,i}(\lambda\lambda)
and
h_{i,\ell+1}(\lambda\lambda)
for
1\leq i\leq\ell
.
In Case 1, if
h_{i,i}(\lambda\lambda)=t
, then
2\lambda_{i}=t
by (
3.4
). Also, in Case 2, if
h_{i,\ell+1}(\lambda\lambda)=t
, then
\lambda_{i}=t
by (
3.4
). Thus, if
t/2\in\lambda
but
t\not\in\lambda
, then there is a
t
-hook on the main diagonal, and all the other
t
-hooks below the diagonal can be paired up with the
t
-hooks above the diagonal, so

n_{t}(\lambda\lambda)=2\widehat{n}_{t}(\lambda\lambda)+1.

The other cases can similarly be verified, and we omit the details.
∎

We now prove an analogue of (DD4) in Theorem
3.1
to track
\widehat{n}_{t}(\lambda\lambda)
.

Theorem 3.3
.

For a positive integer
t
,

\widehat{n}_{t}(\lambda\lambda)=\begin{cases}\widehat{n}_{1}(\nu_{(0)})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}n_{1}(\nu_{(i)})&\text{ if $t$ is odd,}\\
\widehat{n}_{1}(\nu_{(0)})+\widehat{n}_{1}(\nu_{(t/2)})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{t/2-1}\big(n_{1}(\nu_{(i)})+n_{1}(\nu_{(t-i)})\big)&\text{ if $t$ is even.}\end{cases}

To prove Theorem
3.3
, we need to figure out how the
t
-hook of the box
(i,j)
for
i=j\leq\ell
or
j=\ell+1
is mapped under the Littlewood decomposition. In the proof, we will use the description of the Littlewood decomposition given in Section
2.4
.

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}(\lambda\lambda)
be the Frobenius partition of
\lambda\lambda
. Then,

\mathfrak{F}(\lambda\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}&\lambda_{2}&\cdots&\lambda_{\ell}\\
\lambda_{1}-1&\lambda_{2}-1&\cdots&\lambda_{\ell}-1\end{pmatrix}.

We have the following two cases.

Case 1.

Suppose
h_{i,i}(\lambda\lambda)=t
for some
1\leq i\leq\ell
. By (
3.4
) and (
3.5
),

h_{i,i}(\lambda\lambda)=2(\lambda\lambda_{i}-i)=2\lambda_{i}=t.

So, if
t
is odd, then there is no
t
-hook on the main diagonal of
\lambda\lambda
. If
t
is even, then

\binom{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{i}-1}=\binom{t/2}{t/2-1}\in\mathfrak{F}(\lambda\lambda)\longrightarrow\binom{0}{0}\in\mathfrak{C}(\nu_{(t/2)}),

and this
t
-hook becomes a
1
-hook on the main diagonal of
\nu_{(t/2)}
, which is the self-conjugate partition
\pi
in the
t
-quotient of
\lambda\lambda
.

Case 2.

Suppose
h_{i,\ell+1}(\lambda\lambda)=t
for some
1\leq i\leq\ell
. By (
3.4
) and (
3.6
),

h_{i,\ell+1}(\lambda\lambda)=\lambda_{i}=t.

Then,

\binom{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{i}-1}=\binom{t}{t-1}\in\mathfrak{F}(\lambda\lambda)\longrightarrow\binom{1}{0}\in\mathfrak{C}(\nu_{(0)}).

Also, the column
\binom{1}{0}
is the last column of
\mathfrak{C}(\nu_{(0)})
because entries in Frobenius partitions are strictly decreasing in rows. Thus, the last hook on the main diagonal of
\nu_{(0)}
is of length
2
. Since
\nu_{(0)}
is a doubled distinct partition, we have
1\in\mu
for a strict partition
\mu
with
\nu_{(0)}=\mu\mu
.

As seen in Case 1, the
t
-hook on the main diagonal of
\lambda\lambda
corresponds to the
1
-hook on the main diagonal of the self-conjugate partition of
\nu_{(t/2)}
. Also, as seen in Case 2, the
t
-hook on the
\ell
-th column of
\lambda\lambda
corresponds to the
1
-hook on the first column to the right of the Durfee square of the doubled distinct partition
\nu_{(0)}
. The remaining
t
-hooks on neither the main diagonal nor the
\ell
-th column of
\lambda\lambda
correspond to the remaining
1
-hooks of the quotient. Therefore, the theorem immediately follows from this correspondence.
∎

3.2.
The generating function
F_{t}(x;q)

From Theorem
3.1
, to find the generating function
F_{t}(x;q)
, we need to investigate the number of
1
-hooks, that is the number of corners along the doubled distinct partitions.

Proposition 3.4
.

F_{1}(x;q)=\sum_{\lambda\lambda\in\mathcal{DD}}x^{n_{1}(\lambda\lambda)}q^{|\lambda\lambda|}=\frac{(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(1+x)}\bigg(((1-x^{2})q^{2};q^{4})_{\infty}+x((1-x^{2})q^{4};q^{4})_{\infty}\bigg).

Proof.

We prove it using the similar arguments from
[
1
, Section 3.1]
.
For a doubled distinct partition
\lambda\lambda
, note that if the side of its Durfee square is
n
, then the first column to the right of the Durfee square is of size
n
. Moreover, we can divide
\lambda\lambda
into a rectangle of size
(n+1)\times n
and a pair of partitions that are conjugate to each other.
To count the number of
1
-hooks, we split
\mathcal{DD}
into
\mathcal{DD}_{(1)}
and
\mathcal{DD}_{(2)}
, where
\mathcal{DD}_{(1)}
(resp.
\mathcal{DD}_{(2)}
) is the set of doubled distinct partitions
\lambda\lambda
such that the hook length of the box
(n,n+1)
is
1
(resp. is not
1
). See Figure
5
for an example.

(a)

\lambda\lambda=(6,6,4,2,2)

(b)

\lambda\lambda=(6,6,5,3,2)

Figure 5
.
Two doubled distinct partitions in
\mathcal{DD}_{(1)}
and
\mathcal{DD}_{(2)}
.

Thus, we have

\sum_{\lambda\lambda\in\mathcal{DD}}x^{n_{1}(\lambda\lambda)}q^{|\lambda\lambda|}=1+\sum_{\lambda\lambda\in\mathcal{DD}_{(1)}}x^{n_{1}(\lambda\lambda)}q^{|\lambda\lambda|}+\sum_{\lambda\lambda\in\mathcal{DD}_{(2)}}x^{n_{1}(\lambda\lambda)}q^{|\lambda\lambda|}.

First, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\lambda\in\mathcal{DD}_{(1)}}x^{n_{1}(\lambda\lambda)}q^{|\lambda\lambda|}=\sum_{n\geq 1}xq^{n^{2}+n}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+x^{2}q^{2j}+x^{2}q^{4j}+\cdots)=\sum_{n\geq 1}xq^{n^{2}+n}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{1-(1-x^{2})q^{2j}}{1-q^{2j}},

because
xq^{n^{2}+n}
(resp.
\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+x^{2}q^{2j}+x^{2}q^{4j}+\cdots)
) accounts for the rectangle of size
(n+1)\times n
(resp. the remaining partition pairs), and
x
keeps track of the number of different parts, namely the number of
1
-hooks.

Similarly, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\lambda\in\mathcal{DD}_{(2)}}x^{n_{1}(\lambda\lambda)}q^{|\lambda\lambda|}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}q^{n^{2}+n}(x^{2}q^{2n}+x^{2}q^{4n}+\cdots)\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+x^{2}q^{2j}+x^{2}q^{4j}+\cdots)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{x^{2}q^{n^{2}+3n}}{1-q^{2n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{1-(1-x^{2})q^{2j}}{1-q^{2j}}.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\lambda\in\mathcal{DD}}x^{n_{1}(\lambda\lambda)}q^{|\lambda\lambda|}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}xq^{n^{2}+n}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{1-(1-x^{2})q^{2j}}{1-q^{2j}}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{x^{2}q^{n^{2}+3n}}{1-q^{2n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{1-(1-x^{2})q^{2j}}{1-q^{2j}}

\displaystyle=1+x\sum_{n\geq 1}q^{n^{2}+n}\frac{(1-(1-x)q^{2n})((1-x^{2})q^{2};q^{2})_{n-1}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{n^{2}+n}\frac{(1-(1-x)q^{2n})((1-x^{2});q^{2})_{n}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}.

Let

\displaystyle G_{1}(X;q):=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{n^{2}+n}(X;q^{2})_{n}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad G_{2}(X;q):=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{n^{2}+3n}(X;q^{2})_{n}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}.

Next, apply Heine’s transformation in (
2.2
) to
G_{1}(X;q)
and
G_{2}(X;q)
:

\displaystyle G_{1}(X;\sqrt{q})

\displaystyle=\lim_{a\to\infty}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-aq;q)_{n}(X;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{n}}\left(\frac{1}{a}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=(X;q)_{\infty}(-q;q)_{\infty}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{X^{n}}{(-q;q)_{n}(q;q)_{n}}

(3.7)

\displaystyle=(X;q)_{\infty}(-q;q)_{\infty}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{X^{n}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}=(Xq;q^{2})_{\infty}(-q;q)_{\infty},

where we employ the
q
-binomial theorem in (
2.1
) for the last equality. Similarly,

\displaystyle G_{2}(X;\sqrt{q})

\displaystyle=\lim_{a\to\infty}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-aq;q)_{n}(X;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{n}}\left(\frac{q}{a}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=(X;q)_{\infty}(-q^{2};q)_{\infty}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{X^{n}}{(-q^{2};q)_{n}(q;q)_{n}}

\displaystyle=(X;q)_{\infty}(-q;q)_{\infty}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{X^{n}(1-q^{n+1})}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n+1}}=\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}}{X}\left((Xq;q^{2})_{\infty}-(X;q^{2})_{\infty}\right).

Therefore, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\lambda\in\mathcal{DD}}x^{n_{1}(\lambda\lambda)}q^{|\lambda\lambda|}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x}\bigg(G_{1}(1-x^{2};q)-(1-x)G_{2}(1-x^{2};q)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(1+x)}\bigg(\big((1-x^{2})q^{2};q^{4}\big)_{\infty}+x\big((1-x^{2})q^{4};q^{4}\big)_{\infty}\bigg).\qed

We are now ready to find the generating function
F_{t}(x;q)
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

2.1
, and (

Let

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that

If

dd_{t}(2n;1)=q(n)
,

M\left(N^{\mathcal{DD}}_{t,2n};r\right)=\frac{a\left(e^{\frac{r}{\sigma_{t,2n}}}\right)c\left(e^{\frac{r}{\sigma_{t,2n}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}}{a(1)c(1)^{\frac{1}{4}}}e^{\sqrt{2n}\left(c\left(e^{\frac{r}{\sigma_{t,2n}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}-c(1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)-\frac{\mu_{t,2n}}{\sigma_{t,2n}}r}\left(1+O\left(n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right).

We observe that
\frac{r}{\sigma_{t,2n}}>0
and
e^{\frac{r}{\sigma_{t,2n}}}\to 1
as
n\to\infty
. Using the expansion

\sqrt{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\operatorname{Li}_{2}\left(1-e^{2u}\right)}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{6}}+\frac{\sqrt{6}u}{\pi}+\frac{\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}(\pi^{2}-6)u^{2}}{\pi^{3}}+O(u^{3})\quad\text{ as }u\to 0,

we find that

c\left(e^{\frac{r}{\sigma_{t,2n}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\bigg(\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\operatorname{Li}_{2}\big(1-e^{\frac{2r}{\sigma_{t,2n}}}\big)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{6}}+\frac{\sqrt{6}}{\pi}\left(\frac{r}{\sigma_{t,2n}}\right)+\frac{\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}(\pi^{2}-6)}{\pi^{3}}\left(\frac{r}{\sigma_{t,2n}}\right)^{2}+O\left(\left(\frac{r}{\sigma_{t,,2n}}\right)^{3}\right).

Thus, we conclude that

M\left(N^{\mathcal{DD}}_{t,2n};r\right)=e^{\frac{r^{2}}{2}+o_{r}(1)}\left(1+O_{r}\left(n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right),

which completes the proof from Theorem
6.1
as the moment generating function is asymptotically the same as that of the normal distribution.
∎

6.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

The proof of Theorem
1.4
proceeds as before, so we omit the details here. We start with deriving the asymptotic formula of
\widehat{dd}_{t}(2n;x)
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
t\geq 1
be an integer. Suppose that
|1-x|<1
and
\operatorname{Li}_{2}(1-x)<\frac{\pi^{2}}{12t^{2}}
. If
\widehat{c}(x):=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\operatorname{Li}_{2}(1-x)
, then we have that as
n\to\infty
,

\widehat{dd}_{t}(2n;x)=\widehat{a}(x)\widehat{c}(x)^{\frac{1}{4}}n^{-\frac{3}{4}}e^{\sqrt{2\widehat{c}(x)n}}\left(1+O\left(n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right),

where

\widehat{a}(x):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2^{\frac{7}{4}}\sqrt{\pi}x^{\frac{t-1}{4}}}&\text{if $t$ is odd},\\
\frac{\left(1+\sqrt{1-x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(1-\sqrt{1-x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2^{\frac{11}{4}}\sqrt{\pi}x^{\frac{t}{4}}}&\text{if $t$ is even}.\end{cases}

Proof.

If
t
is odd, by Theorem
1.2
,

\displaystyle\widehat{F}_{t}(x;q)=(1+\sqrt{x})A_{1}(\sqrt{x};q^{2}),

where
A_{1}(x;q)
is the function defined by (
4.2
) in Section
4.1
. If
t
is even, by Theorem
1.2
,

\displaystyle\widehat{F}_{t}(x;q)=\sum_{\pm}\frac{\sqrt{x}(1+\sqrt{x})}{\left(1\pm\sqrt{\frac{1-\sqrt{x}}{1+\sqrt{x}}}\right)\left(1\mp\sqrt{1-x}\right)}B_{1}^{\pm}(\sqrt{x};q^{2}),

where
B_{1}^{\pm}(x;q)
is the function defined by (
4.10
) in Section
4.2
. From Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
, we can obtain the desired asymptotic formula by employing the circle method as in Section
5
.
∎

Now we turn to the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We use that the
k
-th moment generating function is
\left(x\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)^{k}\widehat{F}_{t}(x,q)
at
x=1
. To take a derivative, we use

\frac{1}{2}\bigg((\sqrt{1-x}q^{t};-q^{t})_{\infty}+(-\sqrt{1-x}q^{t};-q^{t})_{\infty}\bigg)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(x-1)^{n}q^{tn(2n+1)}}{(-q^{t};q^{2t})_{n}(q^{2t};q^{2t})_{n}},

which can be obtained from the identity

(-aq;q)_{\infty}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{a^{n}q^{n(n+1)/2}}{(q;q)_{n}}.

By employing Wright’s circle method, we can derive the asymptotic formula for
\widehat{\mu}_{t,2n}
and
\widehat{\sigma}_{t,2n}^{2}
as in Theorem
6.2
.

Using Theorem
6.3
, one can show that the moment generating function with the described mean
\widehat{\mu}_{t,2n}
and variance
\widehat{\sigma}_{t,2n}^{2}
goes to
e^{r^{2}/2+o_{r}(1)}
as

. The asymptotic normal distribution follows again from Theorem
6.1
.
∎

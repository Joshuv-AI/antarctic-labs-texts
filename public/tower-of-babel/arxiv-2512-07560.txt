An effective criterion for multiple positive zeros of vertically parametrized polynomial systems
Carles Checa and Elisenda Feliu

Abstract
. We present an effective criterion for determining whether a (augmented) vertically parametrized polynomial system admits multiple positive zeros for some choice of parameter values. Our method builds on previous algorithms from chemical reaction network theory and reduces the question to checking the feasibility of a linear system of equalities and inequalities. Our criterion provides a sufficient condition for the absence of multiple positive zeros that applies to any augmented vertically parametrized polynomial system, and we show that when the kernel of the coefficient matrix of the system displays a certain sparsity structure, this condition becomes also necessary. We give thereby a full characterization of the existence of multiple zeros for this type of systems.



Introduction

Polynomial systems that arise in applications have often fixed support and their coefficients are functions of some parameters.
Vertically parametrized
polynomial systems
[
16
,
21
]
constitute an example of this, and appear in the study of chemical reaction networks
[
10
,
14
,
17
]
, polynomial optimization
[
20
]
or geometric modelling
[
8
]
. These are systems defined by a set of polynomials of the form

C\big(\mathbf{\kappa}\circ x^{M}\big)\ \in\mathbb{R}[\kappa,x^{\pm}]^{\bar{s}}

with parameters
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{\bar{m}})
and variables
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. Here
C\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{s}\times\bar{m}}
is a full rank matrix, the columns of
M\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times\bar{m}}
give the exponents of the monomials of the system, and
\kappa\circ x^{M}
indicates that
the
i
-th monomial is scaled by
\kappa_{i}
. A particular feature of vertically parametrized systems is that each parameter accompanies the same monomial in all entries.
For instance,

\displaystyle\big(\kappa_{1}x_{1}-2\kappa_{2}x_{1}x_{2}^{2}+\kappa_{3}x_{2}^{2},\kappa_{2}x_{1}x_{2}^{2}-(\kappa_{3}+\kappa_{5})x_{2}^{2},-\kappa_{4}x_{1}+3\kappa_{5}x_{2}^{2}\big)

is a vertically parametrized system with

C=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2&1&0&0\\
0&1&-1&0&-1\\
0&0&0&-1&3\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{ and }\quad M=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0&1&0\\
0&2&2&0&2\end{pmatrix}\,.

Sparse (or freely parametrized) polynomial systems, in which each monomial gives rise to a different parameter, are a particular instance of this type of systems.

Motivated by their origin in chemical reaction network theory, we include
additional
n-\bar{s}
linear entries and consider
augmented vertically parametrized
polynomial systems,

\big(C\big(\kappa\circ x^{M}\big),Lx-b\big)\in\mathbb{R}[\kappa,b,x^{\pm}]^{n}\,,

where
b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n-\bar{s}})
are parameters.

The goal of this work is to decide whether an augmented vertically parametrized system

admits multiple positive zeros
, that is, whether there
exists a choice of parameter values
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{m}}_{>0}
,
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n-\bar{s}}
and distinct
x,y\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}
such that

C(\kappa\circ x^{M})=C(\kappa\circ y^{M})=0\qquad Lx=Ly=0\,.

Our approach builds on ideas from chemical reaction network theory (mainly the higher deficiency algorithm
[
23
]
, but also
[
13
,
11
]
) and extends to the general framework of augmented vertically parametrized polynomial systems. Similar approaches also appeared in
[
5
,
22
]
.
The main underlying idea is that, under certain hypotheses, deciding upon the existence of multiple positive zeros can be reduced to checking the feasibility of a linear system of equalities and inequalities, or equivalently, deciding whether the relative interiors of a family of polyhedral cones are empty. This reformulation is crucial because it allows the problem to be solved with algorithms that have a more favorable computational complexity than the standard Cylindrical Algebraic Decomposition approach, which relies on Gröbner basis computations.

To understand the main ideas behind this system of linear equalities and inequalities, we assume that, in row reduced echelon form,
C
is of the form

C=\begin{pmatrix}\id_{\bar{s}}&-\bar{P}\end{pmatrix}\,,

and let

P\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times\ell}
be obtained from
\bar{P}
by selecting one representative
for each set of pairwise proportional rows and columns (see (
2.2
)).
We consider orientations
\sigma\in\{-1,0,1\}^{2\times\ell}
after imposing certain conditions depending on
\bar{P}
, see
Section

2
, and for any
\rho\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, where
m=s+\ell
, we consider matrices
P^{\sigma}
and
A^{\sigma}_{\rho}
defined by

(P^{\sigma})_{ik}=\sigma_{1k}P_{ik}\,,\qquad(A^{\sigma}_{\rho})_{ik}=P_{ik}(\sigma_{1k}\,e^{\rho_{i}}-\sigma_{2k}\,e^{\rho_{s+k}}),\qquad i\in\{1,\dots,s\},\ k\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}\,.

With these objects in place, the
oriented characteristic system
associated with
(P,\sigma,\rho)
is the linear system

A_{\rho}^{\sigma}\mu=0\,,\qquad P^{\sigma}\mu>0\,,\qquad\mu\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\ell}\,.

Finally, we consider the oriented ground set
\mathcal{G}^{\sigma}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
defined in
Section

2
.

Theorem A

(
Theorems

1.1
and
2.2
)
.

The augmented vertically parametrized system
(C(\kappa\circ x^{M}),Lx-b)
admits multiple positive zeros if and only if there exist an orientation
\sigma\in\{-1,0,1\}^{2\times\ell}
and
\rho\in\mathcal{G}^{\sigma}
such that the oriented characteristic system associated with
(P,\sigma,\rho)

has a solution.

It turns out that the feasibility of the oriented characteristic system depends on the signs of
A^{\sigma}_{\rho}
. Then, an important observation is that the strictly increasing nature of the exponential function, implies that the sign of each entry of
A^{\sigma}_{\rho}
is either fixed (if
\sigma_{1k}\sigma_{2k}=-1
) or depends solely on the sign of
P_{ik}(\sigma_{1k}\rho_{i}-\sigma_{2k}\rho_{s+k})
, and can be expressed as a linear inequality in the entries of
\rho
.
Formally,
we first derive simple conditions on the signs of the entries of
A^{\sigma}_{\rho}
that preclude the oriented characteristic system from having solutions. Any sign matrix
\mathcal{S}\in\{-1,0,1\}^{s\times\ell}
not satisfying the conditions is called feasible.
Then, for any
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times\ell}
and feasible
\mathcal{S}\in\{-1,0,1\}^{s\times\ell}
, we introduce a set
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sigma}
, which we name the feasible ground set, and for which deciding whether it is nonempty is equivalent to checking the feasibility of a linear system of equalities and inequalities.

Theorem B

(
Theorem

3.4
)
.

If
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sigma}=\varnothing
for all orientations
\sigma\in\{-1,0,1\}^{2\times\ell}
and feasible
\mathcal{S}\in\{-1,0,1\}^{s\times\ell}
, then
(C(\kappa\circ x^{M}),Lx-b)
does not admit multiple positive zeros.

The converse of Theorem B holds when
P
has a particular structure of zero entries. Namely, we consider the bipartite graph whose nodes are the sets of rows and columns of
P
, and edges correspond to nonzero entries of
P
. If the graph is a forest, we say that
P

induces a forest
(see
Definition

3.7
).

Theorem C

(
Theorem

3.10
)
.

Assume that
P\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times\ell}
induces a forest.
Then the system
(C(\kappa\circ x^{M}),Lx-b)
admits multiple positive zeros if and only if there exist an orientation
\sigma\in\{-1,0,1\}^{2\times\ell}
and feasible
\mathcal{S}\in\{-1,0,1\}^{s\times\ell}
such that

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sigma}\neq\varnothing
.

We conclude this introduction by briefly highlighting two applications where augmented vertically parametrized systems arise. As we have already hinted at, they arise when studying

reaction networks
, which are given by a collection of
reactions

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{ij}X_{i}{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits^{\mkern 5.0mu{}\kappa\mathrm{}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{\text{$j$}}}}\mkern 9.0mu}}{}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{ij}X_{i},\qquad j=1,\dots,m

where
\alpha_{ij},\beta_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\kappa_{j}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is called the reaction rate constant.
The net production of the species along each reaction is encoded by the stoichiometric matrix
N=(\beta_{ij}-\alpha_{ij})_{\in}\mathbb{Z}^{n\times m}
and the reactants define the matrix
M=(\alpha_{ij})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times m}
.
Under the assumption of mass-action kinetics, the evolution of the concentration of each species in time is modelled by a system of ordinary differential equations of the form

\frac{dx}{dt}=N\big(\kappa\circ x^{M}\big)\,.

Moreover, the trajectories are constrained in the stoichiometric compatibility classes, whose defining equations are of the form
Lx-b=0
where
Lx=0
are equations of
\im(N)
and
b
depends on the initial condition of the system.
By letting
C
be a full rank matrix with
\ker(N)=\ker(C)
, the positive steady states within stoichiometric compatibility classes are the positive zeros of the
augmented vertically parametrized system
(C(\kappa\circ x^{M}),Lx-b)
.

Admitting more than one positive steady state is necessary for the network to display multistability, which has been linked to several properties of biological systems
[
25
,
27
]
. Thus, deciding upon the existence of multiple positive steady states for some parameter choice has been an active topic of research in the field. To name a few of the many references, see for example
[
1
,
7
,
6
,
9
,
12
,
19
,
26
,
30
]
and the references therein.

The second application we would like to highlight arises when studying
singular points of hypersurfaces
. Namely, let
f\in\mathbb{R}[x]
be an
n
-variate polynomial with a prescribed support
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and signs
\varepsilon\colon\mathcal{A}\mapsto\{-,+\}
:

f=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\varepsilon(a)\kappa_{a}x^{a}\,,\qquad\kappa\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathcal{A}}\,.

The singular positive zeros of
f

are the points of
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
satisfying
f=x_{1}\frac{d}{dx_{1}}f=\dots=x_{n}\frac{d}{dx_{n}}f=0
. By letting
A
have as columns the elements of
\mathcal{A}
in some order, and
\overline{A}
be obtained by adding a top row of
1^{\prime}s
to
A
and multiplying the
i
-th column by
\epsilon(a_{i})
,
the singular positive zeros of
f
are precisely the positive zeros of the vertically parametrized system

\overline{A}(\kappa\circ x^{A})\,.

The existence of signed coefficients of
f
giving rise to a polynomial with multiple singular positive points depends on the combinatorial properties of the signed support and can be studied with the methods in this work. This question becomes relevant when studying
\mathcal{A}
-discriminants
[
20
]
, the number of connected components of fewnomial hypersurfaces, e.g.
[
3
,
18
,
29
]
, or copositivity of polynomials and SONC decompositions
[
15
]
.

Structure of the Paper

In
Section

1
, we present the characterization of multiple positive zeros for augmented vertically parametrized polynomial systems via the characteristic system.
Section

2
introduces orientations, showing that the question of multiple zeros can be reduced to the feasibility of the oriented characteristic system. In
Section

3
, we analyze the feasibility of oriented characteristic systems. First, we establish sufficient conditions for the existence of multiple positive zeros by reducing the feasibility of the characteristic system to checking whether the relative interiors over a family of polyhedral cones are empty. Secondly, we assume that the simplified reduced matrix
P
induces a forest and characterize the existence of multiple positive zeros using the same family of polyhedral cones.

1.
Characterization of multiple positive zeros

The main goal of this section is to prove
Theorem

1.1
, which reformulates the problem of deciding upon the existence of parameter values for which an augmented vertical system admits multiple positive zeros, to deciding upon the feasibility of an alternative system, which we term the characteristic system. The key idea goes back to earlier works in the theory of chemical reaction networks, e.g.
[
7
]
, and similar ideas have been used in several recent works e.g.
[
5
,
6
,
2
]
.

The set up in this section is an augmented vertical system

F=(C(\kappa\circ x^{M}),Lx-b)\in\mathbb{R}[\kappa,b,x^{\pm}]^{n}

where

M\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times\bar{m}}
,
C\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{s}\times\bar{m}}
has rank
\bar{s}
, and
L\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-\bar{s})\times n}
has rank
n-\bar{s}
. The parameters of the system are
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{\bar{m}}),b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n-\bar{s}})
and variables are
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. By
F_{\kappa,b}
we refer to the specialization of
F
to the parameters
\kappa,b
.

Our interest is on the zeros of the system in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
, when the parameters
\kappa
are positive.
In particular, we aim at deciding whether there exists a choice of parameters
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{m}}_{>0}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n-\bar{s}}
such that
F_{\kappa,b}
has at least two distinct zeros in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
; equivalently there exist distinct
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
and
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{m}}_{>0}
such that

C\big(\kappa\circ x^{M}\big)=C\big(\kappa\circ y^{M}\big)=0\quad\text{and}\quad L(x-y)=0\,.

In preparation for the main result, we introduce the following objects and concepts associated with (the data of)
F
:

•

We say that
C
is
principal
if the
(\bar{s}\times\bar{s})
submatrix of
C
formed by the first
\bar{s}
columns has full rank.
Perhaps after a suitable reordering of the columns of
C
, we can assume without loss of generality that
C
is principal in what follows.

•

For
C
principal, by letting
\bar{\ell}\coloneqq\bar{m}-\bar{s}
, there exists a
matrix of the form

(1.1)

\widehat{P}=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{P}\\
\id_{\bar{\ell}}\end{pmatrix}\,\qquad\bar{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{s}\times\bar{\ell}}

whose columns form a basis of
\ker(C)
.
The submatrix
\bar{P}
is unique and we refer to it as the
reduced matrix
of
C
. Observe that, as the lower block of
\widehat{P}
is the identity matrix, we have

(1.2)

x\in\ker(C)\cap\mathbb{R}^{\bar{m}}_{>0}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\text{ there exists }\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{\ell}}_{>0}\text{ such that }x=\widehat{P}\mu\,.

Different orderings of the columns of
C
yielding to principal matrices, may give rise to different reduced matrices.

•

For a vector
\rho\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{m}}
, consider the
characteristic matrix
(associated with
\bar{P}
and
\rho
) to be

A_{\rho}=\begin{pmatrix}\id_{\bar{s}}&-\bar{P}\end{pmatrix}\diag(e^{\rho})\begin{pmatrix}\bar{P}\\
\id_{\bar{\ell}}\end{pmatrix}\quad\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{s}\times\bar{\ell}}\,,

that is,

(A_{\rho})_{ij}\coloneqq\bar{P}_{ij}(e^{\rho_{i}}-e^{\rho_{\bar{s}+j}})\,,\qquad i=1,\dots,\bar{s},\quad j=1,\dots,\bar{\ell}\,.

The
characteristic system
(associated with
\bar{P}
and
\rho
) is the system

(1.3)

A_{\rho}\mu=0,\qquad\bar{P}\mu>0,\qquad\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{\ell}}_{>0}\,.

•

We let
\mathcal{O}_{L}
denote the union of the orthants of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that
\ker(L)
intersects nontrivially.
If
L
is the empty matrix, then
\mathcal{O}_{L}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.
We refer to
M^{\top}(\mathcal{O}_{L})
at the
ground set
(of
F
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
F=(C(\kappa\circ x^{M}),Lx-b)\in\mathbb{R}[\kappa,b,x^{\pm}]^{n}
be an augmented vertical system with
M\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times\bar{m}}
,
C\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{s}\times\bar{m}}
principal, and
L\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-\bar{s})\times n}
of full row rank. Let
\bar{P}
be the reduced matrix of
C
as given in (
1.1
).
The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

There exist
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{m}}_{>0}
,
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n-\bar{s}}
and distinct
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
such that

F_{\kappa,b}(x)=F_{\kappa,b}(y)=0\,.

(ii)

There exist
\alpha,\beta\in\ker(C)\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\bar{m}}
and a nonzero
\delta\in\mathcal{O}_{L}
such that

\frac{\alpha}{\beta}=e^{M^{\top}\delta}.

(iii)

There exists
\rho\in M^{\top}(\mathcal{O}_{L})
such that the characteristic system

A_{\rho}\mu=0,\qquad\bar{P}\mu>0,\qquad\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{\ell}}_{>0}

has a solution.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): Consider
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{m}}_{>0}
,
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n-\bar{s}}
and distinct
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
such that
F_{\kappa,b}(x)=F_{\kappa,b}(y)=0
. Define

\delta\coloneqq\ln\Big(\frac{x}{y}\Big)\,,\qquad\alpha\coloneqq\kappa\circ x^{M}\,,\qquad\beta\coloneqq\kappa\circ y^{M}\,.

As
x\neq y
, we have
\delta\neq 0
.
By construction,
\alpha,\beta\in\ker(C)\cap\mathbb{R}^{\bar{m}}_{>0}
and, using that
M^{\top}\ln\big(\tfrac{x}{y}\big)=\ln\big(\tfrac{x}{y}\big)^{M}
, we obtain

e^{M^{\top}\delta}=\Big(\frac{x}{y}\Big)^{M}=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\,.

Moreover,
\sign(\delta)=\sign(x-y)\in\sign(\ker(L))
as
\ln
is strictly increasing and
L(x-y)=Lx-Ly=b-b=0
. Hence (ii) holds.

For the next two implications, observe that with
\widehat{P}
as in (
1.1
) and for
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{\ell}}_{>0}
, it holds

(1.4)

\displaystyle e^{\rho}\circ(\widehat{P}\mu)\in\ker(C)=\ker\begin{pmatrix}\id_{\bar{s}}&-\bar{P}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad\begin{pmatrix}\id_{\bar{s}}&-\bar{P}\end{pmatrix}\diag(e^{\rho})\begin{pmatrix}\bar{P}\\
\id_{\bar{\ell}}\end{pmatrix}\mu=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad A_{\rho}\mu=0\,.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii): With
\alpha,\beta,\delta
as in (ii), let
\rho\coloneqq M^{\top}\delta\in M^{\top}(\mathcal{O}_{L})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\bar{m}}
. As

\beta\in\ker(C)\cap\mathbb{R}^{\bar{m}}_{>0}
, there exists
\mu\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\bar{\ell}}
such that
\beta=\widehat{P}\mu
.
Now, as
\alpha=e^{\rho}\circ\beta\in\ker(C)
, (
1.4
) gives that
A_{\rho}\mu=0
. As
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{m}}_{>0}
, we must have
\bar{P}\mu>0
as well, showing (iii).

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i):
Given
\mu,\rho
as in (iii), let
\beta\coloneqq\widehat{P}\mu\in\ker(C)\cap\mathbb{R}^{\bar{m}}_{>0}
.
By (
1.4
), the condition
A_{\rho}\mu=0
implies
\beta\circ e^{\rho}\in\ker(C)
. As both
\beta,e^{\rho}\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{m}}_{>0}
, then so does
\beta\circ e^{\rho}
.

As
\rho\in M^{\top}(\mathcal{O}_{L})
,
\rho=M^{\top}\delta
for some
\delta\in\mathcal{O}_{L}
. Pick any
z\in\ker(L)
such that
\sign(z)=\sign(\delta)
. Define
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
by, for
i=1,\dots,n
,

y_{i}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{z_{i}}{e^{\delta_{i}}-1}&\delta_{i}\neq 0\\
1&\delta_{i}=0\,,\end{cases}

and let

x\coloneqq e^{\delta}\circ y\,,\qquad\kappa\coloneqq\frac{\beta}{y^{M}}\,,\qquad b\coloneqq Ly\,.

Let us see that these vectors satisfy (i). First,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
as
\sign(z)=\sign(\delta)
. Second,
x-y=z\in\ker(L)
and hence
Lx=b
.
Finally,
\kappa\circ y^{M}=\beta\in\ker(C)
and

\kappa\circ x^{M}=\kappa\circ y^{M}\circ(e^{\delta})^{M}=\beta\circ e^{M^{\top}\delta}=\beta\circ e^{\rho}\in\ker(C)\,.

Hence

F_{\kappa,b}(x)=F_{\kappa,b}(y)=0
and (i) holds.
∎

2.
Simplifications of the reduced matrix and oriented characteristic systems

By
Theorem

1.1
, deciding whether
F
admits two positive zeros for some choice of parameter values amounts to deciding the feasibility of the characteristic system for some
\rho
in the ground set
M^{\top}(\mathcal{O}_{L})
. It turns out that, under additional assumptions, this problem is equivalent to deciding whether union of the the relative interiors over a family of polyhedral cones is nonempty. Before we explore this equivalence in
Section

3
, we consider here a simplification of the characteristic system that arises from taking into consideration pairs of linearly dependent columns or rows of
P
. This simplification allows us to tackle a larger class of systems in
Section

3
.

For a matrix
B
, we denote its
j
-th column by
B_{j}
. The transpose of the
j
-th row of
B
is then
(B^{\top})_{j}
.
We note that if
\bar{P}
has two nonzero rows that are proportional with a negative proportionality factor, then
\bar{P}\mu>0
cannot hold and hence the characteristic system has no solution.

Given
\bar{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{s}\times\bar{\ell}}
, we consider
partitions
\tau=(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{s})
and
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\ell})
of the row and column index sets

[\bar{s}]=\tau_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup\tau_{s}\,,\qquad[\,\bar{\ell}\,]=\alpha_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup\alpha_{\ell}\,,

such that

\displaystyle i,i^{\prime}\in\tau_{k}\text{ for some }k

\displaystyle\quad\Rightarrow\quad(\bar{P}^{\top})_{i}=\gamma^{\prime}(\bar{P}^{\top})_{i^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\text{ for some }\gamma^{\prime}>0

\displaystyle j,j^{\prime}\in\alpha_{k}\text{ for some }k

\displaystyle\quad\Rightarrow\quad\bar{P}_{j}=\gamma\bar{P}_{j^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\text{ for some }\gamma\neq 0\,.

That is, for any pair of indices in the same block of
\tau
(resp.
\alpha
), the corresponding rows (resp. columns) of
P
are proportional.
We do not impose that indices of proportional columns or rows
must
be in the same block. This allows for considering the
singleton partitions
, defined by the singleton blocks

\tau_{i}=\{i\}\,,\quad i=1,\dots,\bar{s}\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\alpha_{j}=\{j\}\,,\quad j=1,\dots,\bar{\ell}\,.

For this choice, the results of this section will already be derived from
Theorem

1.1
.

Associated with partitions
\tau,\alpha
, we consider the following data:

•

By selecting one representative column and one representative row in each block
we obtain maps

r\colon[s]\rightarrow[\,\bar{s}\,]\,,\qquad c\colon[\,\ell\,]\rightarrow[\,\bar{\ell}\,]\,.

•

We consider the vectors
\gamma\in(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})^{\bar{\ell}}
,
\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\bar{s}}
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\bar{P}_{j}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{j}P_{k}\,,

\displaystyle k\in[\ell],\ j\in\alpha_{k}

\displaystyle(\bar{P}^{\top})_{i}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\prime}_{i}\,(P^{\top})_{k}\,,

\displaystyle k\in[s],\ i\in\tau_{k}\,.

•

We define the
simplified reduced matrix

P\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times\ell}
as

(2.2)

P_{ik}:=\bar{P}_{r(i),c(k)}\,,\qquad i\in[s],\ k\in[\ell]\,.

•

We partition
[\ell]
depending on whether the proportionality factors are positive for all columns with indices in the same block:

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{\alpha}_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{k\in[\ell]\colon\gamma_{j}>0\text{ for all }j\in\alpha_{k}\}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{\alpha}_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[\ell]\setminus\mathcal{U}^{\alpha}_{1}\,.

We refer to the triplet
(\tau,r,\gamma^{\prime})
as a
row partition
, and the triplet
(\alpha,c,\gamma)
as a
column partition
. An
orientation compatible with
\alpha
is a sign matrix
\sigma\in\{-1,0,1\}^{2\times\ell}
such that

(2.3)

(\sigma_{1k},\sigma_{2k})=(1,1)\quad\text{ if }\quad k\in\mathcal{U}^{\alpha}_{1}\,.

Clearly, the orientation with all entries equal to
1
, denoted by
\sigma_{+}
, is compatible with any
\alpha
.

For any orientation compatible with
\alpha
, we consider the
oriented reduced matrix

Theorem 2.2
.

Let

be an augmented vertical system with

\bar{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{s}\times\bar{\ell}}
, and

Let
(\tau,r,\gamma^{\prime})
and

be row and column partitions, and consider the associated simplified reduced matrix
P\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times\ell}
from (
2.2

The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

There exists
\bar{\rho}\in M^{\top}(\mathcal{O}_{L})
such that the characteristic system

A_{\bar{\rho}}\,\bar{\mu}=0\,,\qquad\bar{P}\,\bar{\mu}>0\,,\qquad\bar{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{\ell}}_{>0}

has a solution.

(ii)

There exist an orientation
\sigma\in\{-1,0,1\}^{2\times\ell}
compatible with
\alpha

such that the oriented characteristic system

Proof.

To show that (i) implies (ii), assume there exist
\bar{\rho}\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{m}}
,
\delta\in\mathcal{O}_{L}
and
\bar{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\bar{\ell}}

(2.10)

\bar{\rho}=M^{\top}(\delta),\qquad A_{\bar{\rho}}\,\bar{\mu}=0,\qquad\bar{P}\,\bar{\mu}>0\,.

We define an orientation
\sigma\in\{-1,0,1\}^{2\times\ell}
as follows. For each

\omega_{k}\coloneqq\sum_{j\in\alpha_{k}}\gamma_{j}\bar{\mu}_{j},\qquad\nu_{k}\coloneqq\sum_{j\in\alpha_{k}}\gamma_{j}\bar{\mu}_{j}\,e^{\bar{\rho}_{\bar{s}+j}},\qquad\sigma(k)\coloneqq(\sign(\omega_{k}),\sign(\nu_{k}))\,.

If

\omega_{k},\nu_{k}>0
and hence
\sigma
is indeed compatible with
\alpha
.

We define vectors
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}_{>0}
and
\rho\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
entrywise for

and

as

\mu_{k}\coloneqq\begin{cases}|\omega_{k}|&\text{if }\omega_{k}\neq 0\,,\\
1&\omega_{k}=0\,.\end{cases}\qquad\rho_{i}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\bar{\rho}_{r(i)}&\text{if }i\in[s]\,,\\
\log\left(\frac{|\nu_{k}|}{\mu_{k}}\right)&\text{if }i=s+k\,,\ k\in[\ell],\ \nu_{k}\neq 0\,,\\
0&\text{otherwise}\,.\end{cases}\quad

Observe that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\sigma_{1k}\,\mu_{k}=\omega_{k}\,,\qquad\sigma_{2k}\,\mu_{k}\,e^{\rho_{s+k}}=\nu_{k}\,,\qquad\text{for all }k\in[\ell]\,.

We show now that
\mu
is a solution to the associated oriented characteristic system at
\rho
. Using (
2.11
),
for
k\in[\ell]
and

we have

\displaystyle P^{\sigma}_{ik}\mu_{k}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1k}P_{ik}\,\mu_{k}=P_{ik}\,\omega_{\kappa}=\sum_{j\in\alpha_{k}}(\gamma_{j}\bar{P}_{r(i),c(k)})\bar{\mu}_{j}=\sum_{j\in\alpha_{k}}\bar{P}_{r(i),j}\bar{\mu}_{j}\,.

\displaystyle (A_{\rho}^{\sigma})_{ik}\,\mu_{k}

\displaystyle=P_{ik}(\sigma_{1k}\,e^{\rho_{i}}-\sigma_{2k}\,e^{\rho_{s+k}})\mu_{k}=P_{ik}(e^{\rho_{i}}\,\omega_{k}-\nu_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\alpha_{k}}\gamma_{j}\bar{P}_{r(i),c(k)}(e^{\bar{\rho}_{r(i)}}-e^{\bar{\rho}_{\bar{s}+j}})\bar{\mu}_{j}=\sum_{j\in\alpha_{k}}\bar{P}_{r(i),j}(e^{\bar{\rho}_{r(i)}}-e^{\bar{\rho}_{\bar{s}+j}})\bar{\mu}_{j}=\sum_{j\in\alpha_{k}}(A_{\bar{\rho}})_{r(i),j}\bar{\mu}_{j}\,.

Summing over all

, it follows that

A_{\rho}^{\sigma}\,\mu=A_{\bar{\rho}}\,\bar{\mu}=0\,,\qquad P^{\sigma}\mu=\bar{P}\,\bar{\mu}>0.

We verify now that
\rho\in\mathcal{G}^{\sigma}
by showing that
\rho
satisfies the conditions (i)-(ii) in the definition of
\mathcal{G}^{\sigma}
with the given
\delta
. For

and

, we have using (

0=\sum_{j=1}^{\bar{\ell}}\left(\tfrac{1}{\gamma^{\prime}_{i_{1}}}(A_{\bar{\rho}})_{i_{1},j}-\tfrac{1}{\gamma^{\prime}_{i_{2}}}(A_{\bar{\rho}})_{i_{2},j}\right)\bar{\mu}_{j}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\bar{\ell}}\bar{P}_{r(k),j}\bar{\mu}_{j}\right)(e^{\bar{\rho}_{i_{1}}}-e^{\bar{\rho}_{i_{2}}})\,.

As

, this gives that
\bar{\rho}_{i_{1}}=\bar{\rho}_{i_{2}}
for all

.
Hence, condition (i) in the definition of

holds.
For condition (ii), if

e^{\rho_{s+k}}=\frac{\nu_{k}}{\omega_{k}}=\frac{\sum_{j\in\alpha_{k}}\gamma_{j}\bar{\mu}_{j}\,e^{\bar{\rho}_{\bar{s}+j}}}{\sum_{j\in\alpha_{k}}\gamma_{j}\bar{\mu}_{j}}\in\Big[\min_{j\in\alpha_{k}}e^{\bar{\rho}_{\bar{s}+j}},\max_{j\in\alpha_{k}}e^{\bar{\rho}_{\bar{s}+j}}\Big]^{\circ}\,.

As

If

and
\alpha_{+}
and

be defined such that

By construction, as

Additionally, using that

\displaystyle\omega_{+}(e^{\alpha_{+}}-e^{\alpha_{-}})

\displaystyle=\nu_{k}-\omega_{k}e^{\alpha_{-}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\nu_{k}-\tfrac{\sigma_{1k}\nu_{k}}{\sigma_{2k}e^{\rho_{s+k}}}e^{\alpha_{-}}=\tfrac{\nu_{k}}{\sigma_{2k}e^{\rho_{s+k}}}(\sigma_{2k}e^{\rho_{s+k}}-\sigma_{1k}e^{\alpha_{-}})&\sigma_{2k}\neq 0\,,\\
-\omega_{k}e^{\alpha_{-}}&\sigma_{2k}=0\,.\end{cases}

The other sign equality in (

. This shows that (i) implies (ii).

We now show that (ii) implies (i). Hence assume there exist an orientation
\sigma
,

\rho\in\mathcal{G}^{\sigma}
, and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}_{>0}
such that

Let

satisfy the conditions of the definition of
\mathcal{G}^{\sigma}
for this

, and define

We have in particular,

if

by condition (i) in the definition of

.

We now define auxiliary terms in preparation for the definition of

.
We fix

•

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma_{j}>0:\>j\in\alpha_{k}}\beta_{j}e^{\bar{\rho}_{\bar{s}+j}}\,,

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma_{j}>0:\>j\in\alpha_{k}}\beta_{j}

\displaystyle e^{\alpha_{-}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma_{j}<0:\>j\in\alpha_{k}}\beta_{j}e^{\bar{\rho}_{\bar{s}+j}}\,,

\displaystyle(\omega_{+},\omega_{-})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{cases}(1,0)&\text{ if }k\in\mathcal{U}^{\alpha}_{1}\,,\\

3.
Feasibility of the oriented characteristic system

By
Theorem

1.1
and
Theorem

2.2
, deciding whether
F
admits two positive zeros for some parameter values is reduced to deciding the feasibility of the (oriented) characteristic system in the positive orthant for some
\rho
in the (oriented) ground set. Our strategy to decide upon the feasibility of this system depends only on the signs of the entries of
A^{\sigma}_{\rho}
and on
\rho
belonging to the relative interiors of certain polyhedral cones.
We explore this strategy here in two parts. First, we provide a condition that guarantees that the oriented characteristic system has no solution, giving thereby a sufficient condition for at most one positive zero for all parameter values. This criterion applies to any augmented vertical system. Second, we show that this condition is also necessary when the matrix
P

induces a forest
(
Definition

3.7
), which requires that
P
is a matrix with a specific structure of zero entries.

The constraints on
\sigma
and
\rho
imposed in
Theorem

1.1
and
Theorem

2.2
, e.g.
\rho\in\mathcal{G}^{\sigma}
, are not relevant for the study of the feasibility of (
2.5
) in this section.

3.1.
Sufficient conditions for unfeasibility of the oriented characteristic system

For an oriented characteristic system associated with
(P,\sigma,\rho)
as above, we start by exploring simple conditions on the signs of
A_{\rho}^{\sigma}
that preclude solutions to the oriented characteristic system (
2.5
) to exist.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times\ell}
and consider sign matrices
\mathcal{S}\in\{-1,0,1\}^{s\times\ell}
and
\sigma\in\{-1,0,1\}^{2\times\ell}
.
For
i\in[s]
, we let

\displaystyle\Lambda_{i}^{++}

\displaystyle=\{j\in[\ell]:P^{\sigma}_{ij}>0,\mathcal{S}_{ij}>0\}\,,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{i}^{+-}

\displaystyle=\{j\in[\ell]:P^{\sigma}_{ij}>0,\mathcal{S}_{ij}<0\}\,,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{i}^{0+}

\displaystyle=\{j\in[\ell]:P^{\sigma}_{ij}=0,\mathcal{S}_{ij}>0\}\,,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{i}^{0-}

\displaystyle=\{j\in[\ell]:P^{\sigma}_{ij}=0,\mathcal{S}_{ij}<0\}\,,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{i}^{-+}

\displaystyle=\{j\in[\ell]:P^{\sigma}_{ij}<0,\mathcal{S}_{ij}>0\}\,,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{i}^{--}

\displaystyle=\{j\in[\ell]:P^{\sigma}_{ij}<0,\mathcal{S}_{ij}<0\}\,,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{i}^{+0}

\displaystyle=\{j\in[\ell]:P^{\sigma}_{ij}>0,\mathcal{S}_{ij}=0\}\,,

and consider the sets

\displaystyle\Lambda_{i}

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{i}^{++}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{+-}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{--}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{-+}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{0+}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{0-}

\displaystyle\Lambda_{i}^{\neq}

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{i}^{++}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{+-}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{--}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{-+}\,.

We refer to the collection of these sets as the
\bm{\Lambda}
-sets
of
(P,\sigma,\mathcal{S})
.

We say that
\mathcal{S}
is
feasible
with respect to
(P,\sigma)
if for all
i\in[s]
such that
\Lambda_{i}\neq\varnothing
we have

\Lambda_{i}^{+-}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{--}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{0-}\neq\varnothing,\quad\Lambda_{i}^{++}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{-+}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{0+}\neq\varnothing,

and for all
i\in[s]
,

\Lambda_{i}^{+-}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{++}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{+0}\neq\varnothing\,.

Observe that
j\notin\Lambda_{i}
if and only if
\mathcal{S}_{ij}=0
, and hence
\Lambda_{i}=\varnothing
if only if the
i
th row of
\mathcal{S}
is zero.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times\ell}
and consider sign matrices
\mathcal{S}\in\{-1,0,1\}^{s\times\ell}
and
\sigma\in\{-1,0,1\}^{2\times\ell}
. Let
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}_{>0}
such that
P^{\sigma}\mu>0
.

There exists
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times\ell}
such that
\sign(Q)=\mathcal{S}
and
Q\mu=0
if and only if
\mathcal{S}
is feasible with respect to
(P,\sigma)
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathcal{S}
is not feasible and let
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times\ell}
such that
\sign(Q)=\mathcal{S}
.
The existence of
\mu
such that
P^{\sigma}\mu>0
, implies that
\Lambda_{i}^{+-}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{++}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{+0}\neq\varnothing
for all
i\in[s]
, as
P^{\sigma}_{ij}\leq 0
for any
j
not in this set.
Therefore,
\mathcal{S}
being not feasible implies that there exists
i\in[s]
with
\Lambda_{i}\neq\varnothing
and either
\Lambda_{i}=\Lambda_{i}^{+-}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{--}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{0-}
or
\Lambda_{i}=\Lambda_{i}^{++}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{-+}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{0+}
.
These two cases give respectively that either

\sum_{j\in\Lambda_{i}}Q_{ij}\mu_{j}<0
or
\sum_{j\in\Lambda_{i}}Q_{ij}\mu_{j}>0
, and hence,
Q\mu\neq 0
a contradiction. This shows the forward implication.

To show the reverse implication, assume that
\mathcal{S}
is feasible. If
\Lambda_{i}=\varnothing
, then the
i
-th row of
\mathcal{S}
is zero and we let
Q_{ij}=0
for all
j\in[\ell]
.
Otherwise, as
\mathcal{S}
is feasible, the
i
-th row of
\mathcal{S}
contains both signs
1
and
-1
. As
\mu>0
, the entries of the
i
-th row of
Q
can be chosen to be orthogonal to
\mu
and satisfy
\sign(Q_{ij})=\mathcal{S}_{ij}
for
j\in[\ell]
.
∎

Proposition

3.2
tells us that if

is not feasible, then the oriented characteristic system does not have a solution.
There are additional simple conditions on
\rho
that preclude the existence of solutions to (

Given
P\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times\ell}
and sign matrices
\mathcal{S}\in\{-1,0,1\}^{s\times\ell}
,
\sigma\in\{-1,0,1\}^{2\times\ell}
,
consider the

from

3.1
. For each

\displaystyle I_{\rho}^{+}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Big\{i\in[s]:\Lambda_{i}^{+-}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{0-}=\varnothing,\;\Lambda_{i}^{++}\neq\varnothing,\;\Lambda_{i}^{--}\neq\varnothing,\;\max_{j\in\Lambda_{i}^{++}}\frac{\sigma_{2j}}{\sigma_{1j}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j}}\leq\min_{j\in\Lambda_{i}^{--}}\frac{\sigma_{2j}}{\sigma_{1j}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j}}\Big\}\,,

\displaystyle I_{\rho}^{-}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Big\{i\in[s]:\Lambda_{i}^{++}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{0+}=\varnothing,\;\Lambda_{i}^{-+}\neq\varnothing,\;\Lambda_{i}^{+-}\neq\varnothing,\;\max_{j\in\Lambda_{i}^{-+}}\frac{\sigma_{2j}}{\sigma_{1j}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j}}\leq\min_{j\in\Lambda_{i}^{+-}}\frac{\sigma_{2j}}{\sigma_{1j}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j}}\Big\}\,,

and for each

\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}\coloneqq\Big\{\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}_{>0}:P^{\sigma}\mu>0,\;\sum_{j\notin\Lambda_{i}}P_{ij}^{\sigma}\mu_{j}>0\Big\}\,.

Finally, we consider the set

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sigma}\coloneqq\Big\{\rho\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:I_{\rho}^{+}\cup I_{\rho}^{-}\neq\varnothing\,,\bigcap\nolimits_{i\in I_{\rho}^{+}\cup I_{\rho}^{-}}\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}=\varnothing\Big\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}\,.

Observe that the restrictions on
\rho

and

affect only on its last
\ell
entries.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times\ell}
,
\mathcal{S}\in\{-1,0,1\}^{s\times\ell}
, and
\sigma\in\{-1,0,1\}^{2\times\ell}
. If
\rho\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sigma}
and
\sign(A_{\rho}^{\sigma})=\mathcal{S}
, then the oriented characteristic system associated with
(P,\sigma,\rho)
does not have a solution.

Proof.

Assume
P^{\sigma}\mu>0
for some
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}_{>0}
. By hypothesis, as
\rho\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sigma}
,
we have
I_{\rho}^{+}\cup I_{\rho}^{-}\neq\varnothing
and
\bigcap_{i\in I_{\rho}^{+}\cup I_{\rho}^{-}}\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}=\varnothing
. Hence, there exists
i\in I_{\rho}^{+}\cup I_{\rho}^{-}
such that
\mu\notin\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}
, that is,

(3.1)

\sum_{j\notin\Lambda_{i}}P^{\sigma}_{ij}\mu_{j}\leq 0\,.

We proceed now considering two cases.

If
i\in I_{\rho}^{+}
, then by letting
\theta\coloneqq\max_{j\in\Lambda_{i}^{++}}\frac{\sigma_{2j}}{\sigma_{1j}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j}}
we have

\frac{\sigma_{2j_{1}}}{\sigma_{1j_{1}}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j_{1}}}\leq\theta\leq\frac{\sigma_{2j_{2}}}{\sigma_{1j_{2}}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j_{2}}}\qquad\text{for all }j_{1}\in\Lambda_{i}^{++},\ j_{2}\in\Lambda_{i}^{--}\,.

Note that for any
j\in\Lambda_{i}^{++}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{--}
, the signs of
P^{\sigma}_{ij}=\sigma_{1j}P_{ij}
and of

(A_{\rho}^{\sigma})_{ij}=P_{ij}(\sigma_{1j}e^{\rho_{i}}-\sigma_{2j}e^{\rho_{s+j}})=P_{ij}^{\sigma}(e^{\rho_{i}}-\tfrac{\sigma_{2j}}{\sigma_{1j}}e^{\rho_{s+j}})

are nonzero and agree. Therefore

e^{\rho_{i}}-\frac{\sigma_{2j}}{\sigma_{1j}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j}}>0
and in particular
e^{\rho_{i}}-\theta>0
. It follows that

P^{\sigma}_{ij}(e^{\rho_{i}}-\frac{\sigma_{2j}}{\sigma_{1j}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j}})\geq P^{\sigma}_{ij}(e^{\rho_{i}}-\theta)
for any such
j
.

Using that
\Lambda_{i}=\Lambda_{i}^{++}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{0+}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{-+}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{--}
(as
i\in I_{\rho}^{+}
), that
(A_{\rho}^{\sigma})_{ij}>0
if
j\in\Lambda_{i}^{0+}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{-+}
, and that
P^{\sigma}_{ij}\leq 0
for
i\in\Lambda_{i}^{0+}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{-+}
together with (
3.1
), we have

(A_{\rho}^{\sigma}\mu)_{i}=\sum_{j\in\Lambda_{i}}(A_{\rho}^{\sigma})_{ij}\mu_{j}\geq\sum_{j\in\Lambda_{i}^{++}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{--}}(A_{\rho}^{\sigma})_{ij}\mu_{j}=\sum_{j\in\Lambda_{i}^{++}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{--}}P^{\sigma}_{ij}(e^{\rho_{i}}-\tfrac{\sigma_{2j}}{\sigma_{1j}}e^{\rho_{s+j}})\mu_{j}\\
\geq\Big(\sum_{j\in\Lambda_{i}^{++}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{--}}P^{\sigma}_{ij}\mu_{j}\Big)(e^{\rho_{i}}-\theta)\geq(P^{\sigma}\mu)_{i}(e^{\rho_{i}}-\theta)>0\,.

Hence,
A_{\rho}^{\sigma}\mu\neq 0
.

If
i\in I_{\rho}^{-}
, we proceed similarly to above. By letting
\theta\coloneqq\min_{j\in\Lambda_{i}^{+-}}\frac{\sigma_{2j}}{\sigma_{1j}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j}}
we have

\frac{\sigma_{2j_{1}}}{\sigma_{1j_{1}}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j_{1}}}\leq\theta\leq\frac{\sigma_{2j_{2}}}{\sigma_{1j_{2}}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j_{2}}}\qquad\text{for all }j_{1}\in\Lambda_{i}^{-+},\ j_{2}\in\Lambda_{i}^{+-}\,.

For
j\in\Lambda_{i}^{-+}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{+-}
, the signs of
P^{\sigma}_{ij}
and
(A_{\rho})_{ij}=P_{ij}(\sigma_{1j}e^{\rho_{i}}-\sigma_{2j}e^{\rho_{s+j}})=P_{ij}^{\sigma}(e^{\rho_{i}}-\tfrac{\sigma_{2j}}{\sigma_{1j}}e^{\rho_{s+j}})
are nonzero but opposite. Thus
\frac{\sigma_{2j}}{\sigma_{1j}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j}}-e^{\rho_{i}}>0
,
\theta-e^{\rho_{i}}>0
and

P^{\sigma}_{ij}(\theta-e^{\rho_{i}})\leq P^{\sigma}_{ij}\big(\frac{\sigma_{2j}}{\sigma_{1j}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j}}-e^{\rho_{i}}\big)
.

We use now that
\Lambda_{i}=\Lambda_{i}^{-+}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{+-}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{--}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{0-}
, that
(A_{\rho}^{\sigma})_{ij}<0
if
j\in\Lambda_{i}^{--}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{0-}
, that
P^{\sigma}_{ij}\leq 0
if
j\in\Lambda_{i}^{--}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{0-}
, and (
3.1
), to obtain

0<(\theta-e^{\rho_{i}})(P^{\sigma}\mu)_{i}\leq\sum_{j\in\Lambda_{i}^{+-}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{-+}}P^{\sigma}_{ij}(\theta-e^{\rho_{i}})\mu_{j}\leq\sum_{j\in\Lambda_{i}^{+-}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{-+}}P^{\sigma}_{ij}(\tfrac{\sigma_{2j}}{\sigma_{1j}}e^{\rho_{s+j}}-e^{\rho_{i}})\mu_{j}\leq-(A_{\rho}^{\sigma}\mu)_{i}\,.

Hence,
A_{\rho}^{\sigma}\mu\neq 0
.

We have shown that
A_{\rho}^{\sigma}\mu\neq 0
whenever
P^{\sigma}\mu=0
and
\mu>0
, and hence (
2.5
) does not have a solution.
∎

3.2.
Characterization of multiple zeros

Theorem

3.4
gives a sufficient condition for precluding multiple positive zeros. A priori, there is no obvious reason to believe that the condition is also necessary. However, after imposing certain conditions on

, a converse to
Theorem

also holds.

The following technical lemma will be a key point in the construction later on.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times\ell}
,
\sigma\in\{-1,0,1\}^{2\times\ell},\mathcal{S}\in\{-1,0,1\}^{s\times\ell}
such that

\mathcal{S}
is feasible with respect to
(P,\sigma)
and consider their associated
\Lambda
-sets.
Assume
P^{\sigma}\mu^{*}>0
for some
\mu^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}_{>0}
and let
\rho\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sigma}
such that
\sign(A_{\rho}^{\sigma})=\mathcal{S}
. Then, there exists

Q\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times\ell}
with
\sign(Q)=\mathcal{S}
such that the system

Q\mu=0,\qquad P^{\sigma}\mu>0,\qquad\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}_{>0}

has a solution and, additionally, for all
i\in[s]
, it holds

(3.3)

\sum_{j\in\Lambda_{i}^{\neq}}\frac{Q_{ij}\,\mu_{j}}{e^{\rho_{i}}-\frac{\sigma_{2j}}{\sigma_{1j}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j}}}+\sum_{j\notin\Lambda_{i}}P^{\sigma}_{ij}\mu_{j}>0\,.

Proof.

As
\rho\notin\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sigma}
, if
I_{\rho}^{+}\cup I_{\rho}^{-}\neq\varnothing
, then there exists
\mu\in\bigcap_{i\in I_{\rho}^{+}\cup I_{\rho}^{-}}\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}\neq\varnothing
. If
I_{\rho}^{+}\cup I_{\rho}^{-}=\varnothing
, then we let
\mu=\mu^{*}
.
In both cases
P^{\sigma}\mu>0
, and hence, by
Proposition

3.2
, there exists
\widetilde{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times\ell}
with
\sign(\widetilde{Q})=\mathcal{S}
such that
\widetilde{Q}\mu=0
.
We construct now a parametric matrix
Q(\omega)
for
\omega=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{s})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{s}
such that, for every
i\in[s]
,
\omega_{i}
only modifies the
i
-th row and it holds that

(3.4)

\sign(\widetilde{Q}_{ij})=\sign(Q(\omega)_{ij})\quad\text{for all }j\in[\ell]\,,\qquad(Q(\omega)\mu)_{i}=0\,,

and (
3.3
) holds after choosing
\omega_{i}
either small or large enough.

If
i\in[s]
is such that

(3.5)

i\in I_{\rho}^{+}\cup I_{\rho}^{-}\,,\quad\text{or}\quad\Lambda_{i}^{++}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{+-}=\varnothing\,,

then
\sum_{j\notin\Lambda_{i}}P^{\sigma}_{ij}\mu_{j}>0\,
.
Indeed, this holds by the choice of
\mu
if
i\in I_{\rho}^{+}\cup I_{\rho}^{-}
, and if
\Lambda_{i}^{++}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{+-}=\varnothing
, we have

0<(P^{\sigma}\mu)_{i}\leq\sum_{j\in\Lambda_{i}^{-+}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{--}}P^{\sigma}_{ij}\mu_{j}+\sum_{j\notin\Lambda_{i}}P_{ij}^{\sigma}\mu_{j}\leq\sum_{j\notin\Lambda_{i}}P_{ij}^{\sigma}\mu_{j}\,.

Then by letting

Q(\omega)_{ij}\coloneqq\omega_{i}\,\widetilde{Q}_{ij},\qquad j\in\Lambda_{i}\,,

(
3.4
) clearly holds for
\omega_{i}>0
and (
3.3
) becomes

(3.6)

\sum_{j\in\Lambda_{i}^{\neq}}\frac{Q(\omega)_{ij}\,\mu_{j}}{e^{\rho_{i}}-\frac{\sigma_{2j}}{\sigma_{1j}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j}}}+\sum_{j\notin\Lambda_{i}}P^{\sigma}_{ij}\mu_{j}=\omega_{i}\sum_{j\in\Lambda_{i}^{\neq}}\frac{\widetilde{Q}_{ij}\,\mu_{j}}{e^{\rho_{i}}-\frac{\sigma_{2j}}{\sigma_{1j}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j}}}+\sum_{j\notin\Lambda_{i}}P^{\sigma}_{ij}\mu_{j}\,.

As
\sum_{j\notin\Lambda_{i}}P_{ij}^{\sigma}\mu_{j}>0
, we can choose
\omega_{i}>0
small enough such that (
3.6
) is positive.

If
i\in[s]
does not satisfy (
3.5
), then
we pick two indices
j_{1},j_{2}
as follows:

(1)

If
\Lambda_{i}^{++}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{+-}\neq\varnothing
,
\Lambda_{i}^{++}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{0+}\neq\varnothing
, and
\Lambda_{i}^{+-}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{0-}\neq\varnothing
, we pick
j_{1}\in\Lambda_{i}^{++}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{0+}
and
j_{2}\in\Lambda_{i}^{+-}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{0-}
such that at least one of the indices lies in
\Lambda_{i}^{++}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{+-}
.

(2)

If
\Lambda_{i}^{+-}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{0-}=\varnothing
, then
\Lambda_{i}^{--}\neq\varnothing
and
\Lambda_{i}^{++}\neq\varnothing
, as
\mathcal{S}
is feasible and (
3.5
) does not hold. Using that
i\notin I_{\rho}^{+}
, we pick
j_{1}\in\Lambda_{i}^{++}
and
j_{2}\in\Lambda_{i}^{--}
such that

\tfrac{\sigma_{2j_{2}}}{\sigma_{1j_{2}}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j_{2}}}<\tfrac{\sigma_{2j_{1}}}{\sigma_{1j_{1}}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j_{1}}}\,.

(3)

If

\Lambda_{i}^{++}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{0+}=\varnothing
, then
\Lambda_{i}^{-+}\neq\varnothing
and
\Lambda_{i}^{+-}\neq\varnothing
, again as
\mathcal{S}
is feasible and (
3.5
) does not hold. As
i\notin I_{\rho}^{-}
, we pick

j_{1}\in\Lambda_{i}^{-+}
and
j_{2}\in\Lambda_{i}^{+-}
such that

\tfrac{\sigma_{2j_{2}}}{\sigma_{1j_{2}}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j_{2}}}<\tfrac{\sigma_{2j_{1}}}{\sigma_{1j_{1}}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j_{1}}}\,.

These three cases cover all indices
i\in[s]
not satisfying (
3.5
). In all cases, the choice of
j_{1}
and
j_{2}
is such that

and
\mathcal{S}_{ij_{2}}<0

Q(\omega)_{ij_{1}}\coloneqq\widetilde{Q}_{ij_{1}}+\omega_{i}\,,\qquad Q(\omega)_{ij_{2}}\coloneqq\widetilde{Q}_{ij_{2}}-\frac{\mu_{j_{1}}}{\mu_{j_{2}}}\,\omega_{i},\qquad Q(\omega)_{ij}\coloneqq\widetilde{Q}_{ij},\quad\text{if }j\neq j_{1},j_{2}.

3.4

(3.7)

\sum_{j\in\Lambda^{\neq}_{i}}\frac{Q(\omega)_{ij}\mu_{j}}{e^{\rho_{i}}-\frac{\sigma_{2j}}{\sigma_{1j}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j}}}=\sum_{j\in\Lambda^{\neq}_{i}}\frac{\widetilde{Q}_{ij}\mu_{j}}{e^{\rho_{i}}-\frac{\sigma_{2j}}{\sigma_{1j}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j}}}+\theta_{i}(\rho,\mu)\omega_{i}\,

for a function
\theta_{i}(\rho,\mu)
that we now describe, arising from the indices
j_{1},j_{2}
.
We will use that if
\sigma_{1j}\neq 0
, then

(3.8)

\sign(e^{\rho_{i}}-\tfrac{\sigma_{2j}}{\sigma_{1j}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j}})=\sign(P^{\sigma}_{ij})\sign(A_{\rho}^{\sigma})=\sign(P^{\sigma}_{ij})\sign(\mathcal{S}_{ij})\,.

In case (1), if one of
j_{1},j_{2}
does not belong to
\Lambda_{i}^{++}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{+-}
, then we have

\theta_{i}(\rho,\mu)=\begin{cases}\frac{\mu_{j_{1}}}{e^{\rho_{i}}-\frac{\sigma_{2j_{1}}}{\sigma_{1j_{1}}}e^{\rho_{s+j_{1}}}}&\quad\text{if }j_{1}\in\Lambda_{i}^{++},\ j_{2}\in\Lambda^{0-}_{i}\\[15.0pt]
-\frac{\mu_{j_{1}}}{e^{\rho_{i}}-\frac{\sigma_{2j_{2}}}{\sigma_{1j_{2}}}e^{\rho_{s+j_{2}}}}&\quad\text{if }j_{1}\in\Lambda_{i}^{0+},\ j_{2}\in\Lambda_{i}^{+-}.\end{cases}

In both cases,
\theta_{i}(\rho,\mu)
is positive by (
3.8
). In the remaining cases,

\theta_{i}(\rho,\mu)=\frac{\mu_{j_{1}}}{e^{\rho_{i}}-\frac{\sigma_{2j_{1}}}{\sigma_{1j_{1}}}e^{\rho_{s+j_{1}}}}-\frac{\mu_{j_{1}}}{e^{\rho_{i}}-\frac{\sigma_{2j_{2}}}{\sigma_{1j_{2}}}e^{\rho_{s+j_{2}}}}=\frac{\mu_{j_{1}}(\frac{\sigma_{2j_{1}}}{\sigma_{1j_{1}}}e^{\rho_{s+j_{1}}}-\frac{\sigma_{2j_{2}}}{\sigma_{1j_{2}}}e^{\rho_{s+j_{2}}})}{(e^{\rho_{i}}-\frac{\sigma_{2j_{1}}}{\sigma_{1j_{1}}}e^{\rho_{s+j_{1}}})(e^{\rho_{i}}-\frac{\sigma_{2j_{2}}}{\sigma_{1j_{2}}}e^{\rho_{s+j_{2}}})}.

For the cases (2) and (3), the denominator of
\theta_{i}(\rho,\mu)
is positive as
the signs of the factors agree. The numerator is also positive by assumption. In the case (1) with
j_{1},j_{2}\in\Lambda_{i}^{++}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{+-}
, the denominator is negative as the signs of the factors differ. The numerator is also negative, as by (

)

\frac{\sigma_{2j_{1}}}{\sigma_{1j_{1}}}e^{\rho_{s+j_{1}}}<e^{\rho_{i}}<\frac{\sigma_{2j_{2}}}{\sigma_{1j_{2}}}e^{\rho_{s+j_{2}}}\,.

Therefore, the coefficient of
\omega_{i}
in (
3.7
) is positive, which implies that we can choose
\omega_{i}
such that

.

The sparsity of
P
we referred to earlier is characterized in terms of the bipartite graph defined by the rows and columns of
P
.

Definition 3.7
.

Given a matrix
P\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times\ell}
, we define the bipartite graph
G_{P}=(V_{P},E_{P})
associated with
P
as follows:

•

The node set is partitioned into two disjoint sets,
V_{P}=V_{1}\sqcup V_{2}
, where
V_{1}=\{1,\dots,s\}
and
V_{2}=\{1,\dots,\ell\}
.

•

The edge set
E_{P}
is defined by the nonzero entries of
P
: for
i\in V_{1}
and
j\in V_{2}
, there is an edge
(i,j)\in E_{P}
if and only if
P_{ij}\neq 0
.

We say that
P

induces a forest
if the bipartite graph
G_{P}
is a forest, that is, each connected component of
G_{P}
is a tree (an acyclic, connected subgraph).

Imposing that
P
induces a forest might be restrictive in general, but this condition is present in the (simplified) reduced matrices of many augmented vertical systems coming from (realistic) reaction networks. We remark that as the graph
G_{P}
associated with a simplifed reduced matrix
P
can be seen as a subgraph of the graph
G_{\bar{P}}

associated with the reduced matrix
\bar{P}
, if
\bar{P}
induces a forest, then so does
P
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
F=(C(\kappa\circ x^{M}),Lx-b)\in\mathbb{R}[\kappa,b,x^{\pm}]^{n}
be an augmented vertical system with
C\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{s}\times\bar{m}}
principal and
L
of full row rank.
Let
(\tau,r,\gamma^{\prime})
and
(\alpha,c,\gamma)
be row and column partitions, and consider the associated simplified reduced matrix
P\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times\ell}
from (
2.2
).

Assume that
P
induces a forest. Let

\sigma\in\{-1,0,1\}^{2\times\ell}
be an orientation compatible with
\alpha
, and
\mathcal{S}\in\{-1,0,1\}^{s\times\ell}
be feasible with respect to
(P,\sigma)
. Then, for any
\rho\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sigma}
, the oriented characteristic system associated with
(P,\sigma,\rho)
has a solution.

Proof.

As
\rho\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sigma}
, we have in particular that
\sign(A_{\rho}^{\sigma})=\mathcal{S}
and
\rho\notin\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sigma}
. Hence by
Lemma

3.6
, there exist
\bar{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}_{>0}
and
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times\ell}
with
\sign(Q)=\mathcal{S}
such that
Q\bar{\mu}=0
,
P^{\sigma}\bar{\mu}>0
, and

\sum_{j\in\Lambda_{i}^{\neq}}\frac{Q_{ij}\,\bar{\mu}_{j}}{e^{\rho_{i}}-\frac{\sigma_{2j}}{\sigma_{1j}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j}}}+\sum_{j\notin\Lambda_{i}}P^{\sigma}_{ij}\bar{\mu}_{j}>0

for all
i\in[s]
. We will now modify
\bar{\mu}
to obtain
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}_{>0}
satisfying
A_{\rho}^{\sigma}\mu=0
and
P^{\sigma}\mu>0
.

First of all, observe that
as
(A_{\rho}^{\sigma})_{ij}=0
and
P^{\sigma}_{ij}=0
if
i
and
j
are not in the same connected component of
G_{P}
,
the matrices
A_{\rho}^{\sigma}
and
P^{\sigma}
have a block structure given by the set of nodes in each connected component of
G_{P}
. This means that, without loss of generality, we can assume that
G_{P}
is connected and hence a tree.

Consider the node
1\in[s]
of
G_{P}
.
As the graph is acyclic, for every node
i\in[s]
, there is a unique path joining
1
and
i

1=i_{0}\relbar\mkern-9.0mu\relbar j_{0}\relbar\mkern-9.0mu\relbar i_{1}\relbar\mkern-9.0mu\relbar j_{1}\relbar\mkern-9.0mu\relbar\cdots\relbar\mkern-9.0mu\relbar i_{u}\relbar\mkern-9.0mu\relbar j_{u}\relbar\mkern-9.0mu\relbar i_{u+1}=i

with
i_{k}\in[s]
,
j_{k}\in[\ell]
and without repeated nodes. Let

\omega_{i}\coloneqq\prod_{k=0}^{u}\ \frac{Q_{i_{k},j_{k}}}{(A_{\rho}^{\sigma})_{i_{k},j_{k}}}\,\frac{(A_{\rho}^{\sigma})_{i_{k+1},j_{k}}}{Q_{i_{k+1},j_{k}}}\ >0\,,

with the convention that
\tfrac{0}{0}=1
. As
\sign(A_{\rho}^{\sigma})=\sign(Q)
, we are guaranteed
\omega_{i}
is positive.

For
j\in[\ell]
, let
\iota(j)\in[s]
be the only node adjacent to
j
in the unique path from
1\in[s]
to
j
.
We define

\mu_{j}\coloneqq\omega_{\iota(j)}\,\frac{Q_{\iota(j),j}}{(A_{\rho}^{\sigma})_{\iota(j),j}}\,\bar{\mu}_{j}\quad>0\,.

Let us see that
A_{\rho}^{\sigma}\mu=0
and
P^{\sigma}\mu>0
.
Fix
i\in[s]
. For a given
j\in[\ell]
with
P_{ij}\neq 0
, if
\iota(j)\neq i
, then the unique path from
1
to
i
is precisely obtained by appending
\iota(j)\relbar\mkern-9.0mu\relbar j\relbar\mkern-9.0mu\relbar i
to the unique path from

. Hence

\omega_{i}=\omega_{\iota(j)}\,\frac{Q_{\iota(j),j}}{(A^{\sigma}_{\rho})_{\iota(j),j}}\,\frac{(A^{\sigma}_{\rho})_{ij}}{Q_{ij}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\omega_{\iota(j)}=\omega_{i}\,\frac{(A_{\rho}^{\sigma})_{\iota(j),j}}{Q_{\iota(j),j}}\,\frac{Q_{ij}}{(A^{\sigma}_{\rho})_{ij}}\,.

Using this, we have that for any matrix
B\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times\ell}
with support included in the support of
P

\displaystyle(B\mu)_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}B_{ij}\,\omega_{\iota(j)}\,\frac{Q_{\iota(j),j}}{(A^{\sigma}_{\rho})_{\iota(j),j}}\,\bar{\mu}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1,\iota(j)=i}^{\ell}B_{ij}\,\omega_{i}\,\frac{Q_{ij}}{(A^{\sigma}_{\rho})_{ij}}\,\bar{\mu}_{j}+\sum_{j=1,\iota(j)\neq i}^{\ell}B_{ij}\,\omega_{i}\,\frac{(A^{\sigma}_{\rho})_{\iota(j),j}}{Q_{\iota(j),j}}\,\frac{Q_{ij}}{(A^{\sigma}_{\rho})_{ij}}\,\frac{Q_{\iota(j),j}}{(A^{\sigma}_{\rho})_{\iota(j),j}}\,\bar{\mu}_{j}

\displaystyle=\omega_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}B_{ij}\,\frac{Q_{ij}}{(A^{\sigma}_{\rho})_{ij}}\,\bar{\mu}_{j}\,.

Considering
B=A_{\rho}^{\sigma}
, we obtain

\displaystyle(A^{\sigma}_{\rho}\mu)_{i}

\displaystyle=\omega_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}(A^{\sigma}_{\rho})_{ij}\,\frac{Q_{ij}}{(A^{\sigma}_{\rho})_{ij}}\,\bar{\mu}_{j}=\omega_{i}\sum_{j\in\Lambda_{i}}\,\,Q_{ij}\bar{\mu}_{j}=0\,.

Similarly, for
B=P^{\sigma}
, we have

\displaystyle(P^{\sigma}\mu)_{i}

\displaystyle=\omega_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}P^{\sigma}_{ij}\,\frac{Q_{ij}}{(A^{\sigma}_{\rho})_{ij}}\,\bar{\mu}_{j}=\omega_{i}\ \Big(\ \sum_{j\in\Lambda^{\neq}_{i}}\,\frac{Q_{ij}}{e^{\rho_{i}}-\tfrac{\sigma_{2j}}{\sigma_{1j}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j}}}\,\bar{\mu}_{j}+\sum_{j\notin\Lambda_{i}}\,P^{\sigma}_{ij}\,\bar{\mu}_{j}\ \Big)>0\,.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Combining all the results so far, we obtain the following characterization of augmented vertical systems that admit multiple positive zeros.

Theorem 3.10

(Characterization of multiple positive zeros when
P
induces a forest)
.

Let
F=(C(\kappa\circ x^{M}),Lx-b)\in\mathbb{R}[\kappa,b,x^{\pm}]^{n}
be an augmented vertical system with
C\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{s}\times\bar{m}}
principal and
L
of full row rank.
Let
(\tau,r,\gamma^{\prime})
and
(\alpha,c,\gamma)
be row and column partitions, and assume that the associated simplified reduced matrix
P\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times\ell}
from (
2.2
) induces a forest.

Then,
F
admits multiple positive zeros for some parameter values
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{m}}_{>0}
,
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n-\bar{s}}
if and only if there exist an orientation
\sigma\in\{-1,0,1\}^{2\times\ell}
compatible with
\alpha
and a sign matrix
\mathcal{S}\in\{-1,0,1\}^{s\times\ell}
that is feasible with respect to
(P,\sigma)
such that

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sigma}\neq\varnothing
.

Proof.

If
F
admits multiple positive zeros,
Theorem

3.4
tells us that there exist
\sigma
and
\mathcal{S}
as in the statement such that

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sigma}\neq\varnothing
. Reciprocally, if
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sigma}\neq\varnothing
for some
\sigma
and
\mathcal{S}
, then
Theorem

3.8
tells us that the oriented characteristic system at any
\rho\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sigma}
has a solution.
By
Theorem

2.2
, this implies that
Theorem

1.1
(iii) holds and hence
F
admits multiple positive zeros.
∎

When considering the singleton partitions, there is only one possible orientation
\sigma_{+}
compatible with
\alpha
, namely that with all entries positive, c.f.
Remark

2.1
.
In this case
P=P^{\sigma_{+}}=\bar{P}
,

, and the sets
\Lambda_{i}^{0\pm}
are empty for
\mathcal{S}=\sign(A_{\rho})
: if

(A_{\rho})_{ij}=0

With this observation, and by inspecting the definition of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sigma_{+}}

\bar{P}
induces a forest.

Corollary 3.11

(Characterization of multiple positive zeros when
\bar{P}
induces a forest)
.

Let
F=(C(\kappa\circ x^{M}),Lx-b)\in\mathbb{R}[\kappa,b,x^{\pm}]^{n}

principal and

\bar{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{s}\times\bar{\ell}}

The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

admits multiple positive zeros for some parameter values

,

(ii)

3.3.
Computational considerations

As illustrated by
Example

3.12
, the feasible ground set
\mathcal{C}^{\sigma}_{\mathcal{S}}
is defined by the equalities and inequalities defining the sets
\mathcal{G}^{\sigma}
,
\sign(A_{\rho}^{\sigma})
, and
\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sigma}
. Deciding whether
\mathcal{C}^{\sigma}_{\mathcal{S}}
is non-empty can be expressed in terms of linear equalities and inequalities.

We start by noting that for every pair of real numbers
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
, the inequalities of the form
-e^{\nu}<e^{\nu^{\prime}}
always hold, while those of the form
e^{\nu}<-e^{\nu^{\prime}}
are never true.
If that is not the case, using that the exponential function is strictly increasing,
e^{\nu}<e^{\nu^{\prime}}
is equivalent to
\nu<\nu^{\prime}
.
In this way, all inequalities comparing two exponentials can be made linear.

The inequalities defining
\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sigma}
arise from either imposing
I_{\rho}^{+}\cup I_{\rho}^{-}=\varnothing
or
\bigcap_{i\in I_{\rho}^{+}\cup I_{\rho}^{-}}\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}\neq\varnothing
if
I_{\rho}^{+}\cup I_{\rho}^{-}\neq\varnothing
. For
I_{\rho}^{+}\cup I_{\rho}^{-}=\varnothing
, we consider all
i\in[s]
such that
\Lambda_{i}^{+-}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{0-}=\varnothing
,
\Lambda_{i}^{++}\neq\varnothing
and
\Lambda_{i}^{--}\neq\varnothing
and require
that

(3.9)

\frac{\sigma_{2j_{1}}}{\sigma_{1j_{1}}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j_{1}}}>\frac{\sigma_{2j_{2}}}{\sigma_{1j_{2}}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j_{2}}}\,

for some pair
(j_{1},j_{2})\in\Lambda_{i}^{++}\times\Lambda_{i}^{--}
.
The solution set in
\rho\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the union of relative interiors of polyhedral sets.

Similarly, for each
i\in[s]
such that
\Lambda_{i}^{++}\cup\Lambda_{i}^{0+}=\varnothing
,
\Lambda_{i}^{-+}\neq\varnothing
and
\Lambda_{i}^{+-}\neq\varnothing
, we require the existence of
(j_{3},j_{4})\in(\Lambda_{i}^{-+},\Lambda_{i}^{+-})
such that

(3.10)

\frac{\sigma_{2j_{3}}}{\sigma_{1j_{3}}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j_{3}}}>\frac{\sigma_{2j_{4}}}{\sigma_{1j_{4}}}\,e^{\rho_{s+j_{4}}}\,.

In order to require
\bigcap_{i\in I_{\rho}^{+}\cup I_{\rho}^{-}}\Gamma_{\Lambda,i}\neq\varnothing
for all
\rho
such that
I_{\rho}^{+}\cup I_{\rho}^{-}\neq\varnothing
, we consider all pairs of disjoint index sets
I^{+},I^{-}\subseteq[s]
such that
I^{+}\cup I^{-}\neq\varnothing
and for which
I^{+}=I^{+}_{\rho}
and
I^{-}=I^{-}_{\rho}
for some
\rho\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
; the latter condition is defined by inequalities in
\rho
, and there is only a finite number of pairs to consider. We impose then

\Big\{\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}_{>0}:P^{\sigma}\mu>0\ ,\;\sum_{j\notin\Lambda_{i}}P_{ij}^{\sigma}\mu_{j}>0,\ \text{for all }i\in I^{+}\cup I^{-}\Big\}\neq\varnothing\,.

We conclude that the set
\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sigma}
is a union of relative interiors of polyhedral cones in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Similarly, to encode
\sign(A_{\rho}^{\sigma})=\mathcal{S}
, we consider the equalities

\sign(\sigma_{1j}e^{\rho_{i}}-\sigma_{2j}e^{\rho_{s+j}})=\mathcal{S}_{ij},\qquad i\in[s],\ j\in[\ell]\,.

By the observation above, this is expressed as a linear system of equalities and inequalities in
\rho
and therefore

\sign(A_{\rho}^{\sigma})=\mathcal{S}
defines the relative interior of a polyhedral cone in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Finally, the set
\mathcal{G}^{\sigma}
is also described as a finite union of the relative interior of a collection polyhedral cones. More specifically, for each orthant
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathcal{O}_{L}
, the condition
\delta\in\mathcal{O}
is described by a set of linear equalities and inequalities. For
k\in[s]
, the equalities

\rho_{k}=M^{\top}(\delta)_{i}\quad\text{for all}\ i\in\tau_{k}

are linear.
For
k\in\mathcal{U}_{1}^{\alpha}
, (
2.6
) holds if either

M^{\top}(\delta)_{\bar{s}+j}=\rho_{s+k}\,\quad\text{ for all }j\in\alpha_{k}\,,

or there exists
j_{1},j_{2}\in\alpha_{k}
such that

M^{\top}(\delta)_{\bar{s}+j_{1}}<\rho_{s+k}<M^{\top}(\delta)_{\bar{s}+j_{2}}\,.

If
k\in\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\sigma}
, we derive similar equations for
\alpha_{+}
and
\alpha_{-}
. Finally, the sign identities in (
2.9
) are also defined by linear inequalities. For instance, if
\sigma_{1k}=\sigma_{2k}=1
, then condition (
2.9
) is equivalent to

\rho_{s+k}>\alpha_{+}>\alpha_{-}\,\quad\text{or}\quad\rho_{s+k}<\alpha_{+}<\alpha_{-}\,.

As a linear projection of a polyhedral cone is a polyhedral cone
[
28
, Theorem 19.3]
,
\mathcal{G}^{\sigma}
is a union of relative interiors of a collection of polyhedral cones.

We conclude that

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\sigma}
is a union of relative interiors of a finite collection of polyhedral cones in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and checking whether this set is nonempty can be addressed via linear programming.

CKAD-Net: class-guided key-feature aggregation and adaptive decision network for vocal cord lesion prediction
Authors: Yaqi Liu, Cansi Wang, Yiyin Liang, Tianjiao Ren, Bo Zhang, Juan Feng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Vocal cord lesions (VCL) that are not promptly diagnosed and treated may adversely affect patients’ voice quality and speech communication in the short term, diminishing their quality of life. Long-term progression may lead to malignant tumors in the larynx, posing a threat to patients’ health and life. To this end, this study proposes CKAD-Net, a vocal cord lesion prediction model based on category-guided key feature aggregation and adaptive decision-making. The category-guided key feature aggregation mechanism adaptively adjusts the attention distribution across different features for various lesion types, effectively enhancing category-relevant feature representations and improving the model’s predictive performance. The adaptive decision mechanism employs a learnable adaptive weight factor to dynamically weight the prediction results between feature region representations and category-guided discriminative feature representations. This enables adaptive fusion of decision outcomes from both representations, generating more accurate decisions. Finally, this study validated the performance of the CKAD-Net model on coarse-grained and fine-grained vocal cord lesion prediction tasks using the hospital-private dataset VCLScopeData. The experimental results show that CKAD-Net achieves AUC and ACC values of (0.944 ± 0.002 [0.940, 0.948], 0.864 ± 0.008 [0.848, 0.880] and 0.929 ± 0.003 [0.923, 0.935], 0.730 ± 0.015 [0.700, 0.759] respectively, demonstrating superior performance compared to other models.

Introduction

Vocal cord lesions (VCL) may develop due to poor vocal techniques or improper vocal cord care, or they may result from viral infections or other diseases1. Benign conditions include normal structural changes and vocal cord polyps, while malignant conditions encompass keratinizing lesions and laryngeal cancer2,3. Benign lesions such as granulomas and polyps frequently occur on the vocal cords and surrounding tissues, potentially leading to clinical complications like hoarseness, wheezing, and vocal cord paralysis4. While most benign conditions do not immediately threaten life, prolonged existence or lack of effective intervention may cause some benign lesions to gradually progress into malignant tumors5,6. Keratotic lesions represent a type of vocal fold mucosal pathology, typically presenting as white patches7 Histologically, superficial cells in the affected area exhibit a stacked arrangement, indicating immature cells that should normally undergo desquamation but fail to shed properly under pathological conditions8. Vocal cord keratotic lesions may possess precancerous characteristics9. Due to their unpredictable histological features and clinical behavior, the lesion spectrum can range from completely benign growths to malignant laryngeal carcinomas10.

Currently, multiple methods are available to aid in distinguishing benign from malignant vocal cord lesions, including laryngoscopy, pathological biopsy, and computed tomography (CT)11. While CT imaging helps assess lesion extent and structure, radiation exposure raises concerns12. Pathological biopsy, while considered the gold standard for diagnosing vocal cord lesions, carries risks of infection and bleeding due to its invasive nature13. In contrast, laryngoscopy is the most commonly used clinical tool due to its simplicity, high safety profile, and good patient tolerance14. However, this examination method heavily relies on the physician’s expertise and skill. Diagnostic outcomes may vary based on the clinician’s experience level, potentially increasing the risk of misdiagnosing vocal cord lesions15. Therefore, there is an urgent need to establish an early diagnostic method based on laryngoscopic images that can accurately distinguish different vocal cord lesions. This would enable clinicians to intervene and treat lesions at an early stage, thereby reducing the risk of benign lesions progressing to malignancy16.

Unlike natural images, laryngoscopy images are captured using electronic laryngoscope devices in specific clinical settings. Their imaging is influenced by multiple factors such as lighting conditions, device models, and operating methods, often exhibiting characteristics like non-uniform illumination, strong reflections, and high noise17. These properties cause laryngoscopy images to differ significantly from natural images in terms of color distribution, texture details, and structural patterns, increasing the difficulty of feature extraction and classification tasks18. Existing studies have employed laryngoscopy images for vocal cord lesion prediction. For instance, Zhao et al.19 utilized the classic MobileNet model to classify four vocal cord lesion types. Kang et al.20 employed the Swin-Transformer model for classification of various vocal cord lesions. Both studies predicted four lesion types: normal, vocal cord polyps, keratinization, and laryngeal cancer. Ren et al.21 utilized the ResNet model to classify five vocal cord lesion types: normal laryngoscopy, vocal cord nodules, laryngeal polyps, leukoplakia, and malignant tumors. The dataset used in this work was collected over a period exceeding six years and is not publicly accessible. Wei et al.22 proposed a non-local dual-stream attention fusion network, NDF-Net, which fuses features learned from both convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and Transformer attention networks for vocal fold lesion classification. These traditional attention-based feature aggregation methods assign attention weights solely based on overall feature space similarity, lacking category-semantic guidance. Consequently, they struggle to focus specifically on key feature regions for particular lesion categories. Yin et al.23 employed the DenseNet model for vocal cord lesion prediction. Fang et al.24 proposed an R-CNN model incorporating DropBlock regularization to mitigate overfitting in vocal fold lesion prediction. This study also highlights that building large-scale laryngoscopy datasets from scratch is challenging for most hospitals, emphasizing the necessity of vocal fold lesion recognition under small-sample dataset conditions. Furthermore, all models in the aforementioned studies are constructed based on CNN or Transformer frameworks. While this has advanced intelligent vocal fold lesion recognition to some extent, these methodological frameworks rely on standard convolutional neural network architectures. They typically lack structural optimization tailored to vocal fold lesion features, fail to fully account for discriminative feature differences across distinct lesion categories, and do not incorporate decision-level fusion mechanisms.

To address these shortcomings, this study proposes CKAD-Net, a vocal cord lesion prediction model based on category-guided key feature aggregation and adaptive decision-making mechanisms. CKAD-Net incorporates a category-guided key feature aggregation mechanism. This mechanism first divides the overall feature space into multiple feature regions, each containing a subset of features with local discriminative significance. Subsequently, it identifies key features within each region based on category classification results. It constructs dynamic interactions between key features and different feature regions based on an attention mechanism, thereby obtaining category-semantics-driven discriminative feature representations. This approach adaptively adjusts the distribution of feature attention for different lesion types, effectively enhancing category-relevant feature expressions and improving the model’s sensitivity and discrimination accuracy toward lesion regions. Furthermore, predictions based solely on feature representations from partitioned regions are susceptible to interference from anomalous features, leading to unstable model decisions. Moreover, predictions based solely on discriminative feature representations derived from category-guided mechanisms, while highlighting lesion features in key regions, may exhibit overconfidence in critical features, potentially overlooking the crucial influence of auxiliary features in other regions. Therefore, to fully leverage the complementary advantages of feature region representations and category-guided discriminative representations, CKAD-Net proposes an adaptive decision fusion mechanism. This mechanism dynamically weights predictions from different feature representations through learnable adaptive weight factors, enabling adaptive decision fusion to generate more accurate outcomes. When feature region representations demonstrate stronger discriminative power, the model automatically increases their decision weight. Conversely, when discriminative feature representations from the category-guided mechanism yield more reliable predictions, their contribution is amplified.

Materials and methods

Datasets and processing

This study retrospectively collected laryngoscopic images from 640 patients within the laryngoscope database of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University, comprising a total of 640 images. The specific data distribution is detailed in Table 1. Statistical analysis was conducted using the patient as the smallest independent sample unit, with only one image per patient included to ensure sample independence. Pathological reports served as the reference standard for classification across all categories. Specifically, 219 images were classified as normal vocal cords, based on pathology reports indicating no abnormalities or a definitive diagnosis of normal tissue; 131 images were categorised as vocal cord polyps, supported by pathology reports confirming polyp diagnosis; 115 images were classified as keratinisation, supported by pathology reports indicating keratinisation-related pathological changes; 175 images were classified as laryngeal carcinoma based on pathological reports confirming malignant tumours of the larynx. All classifications were validated against gold-standard pathological findings. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University (Ethics Approval No. K202511-14).

Experimental setup

This experiment partitions the dataset into training, validation, and test sets at an 8:1:1 ratio. Model performance is evaluated using 10-fold cross-validation. Specifically, this study partitions data at the patient level, treating each patient as the smallest independent sample unit, with each patient corresponding to a single image. First, a fixed 10% of patients from the entire cohort are allocated as an independent test set, remaining unchanged throughout the experiment. Subsequently, ten-fold cross-validation is performed solely on the remaining patient data to construct a training set (approximately 80% of patients) and a validation set (approximately 10% of patients). Within each fold, the optimal model is selected based on validation set performance, with final evaluation conducted on the same fixed test set. The overall model performance is represented by the average performance across all ten folds. The experiment strictly adheres to patient-level data partitioning, ensuring that no image from a given patient in the test set appears in any training or validation set of any fold. This prevents data leakage risks arising from models prematurely encountering test data during training. Furthermore, the test set remains locked throughout the process, never participating in training or parameter tuning. This effectively mitigates data leakage risks, enhancing the objectivity and reliability of model evaluation outcomes. All images were processed using a ResNet-50 model pre-trained on a large-scale image dataset to extract initial features, with the extracted feature dimension being \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$X \in {{\mathbb{R}}^{1 \times 1024}}$$\end{document}. The images were normalized to meet the input requirements of the model, ensuring the stability and consistency of the feature extraction process. In particular, the input resolution for laryngoscope images was standardised to 224 × 224, and normalisation was performed using the standard method: “transforms.Normalize([0.485, 0.456, 0.406], [0.229, 0.224, 0.225])”. No data augmentation strategies were employed during training. In this study, all baseline comparison experiments and ablation studies were conducted under a unified setting, including identical input data, preprocessing procedures, dataset splitting strategy, and training protocols, to ensure consistency in the experimental setup and thereby guarantee the fairness and scientific validity of the performance comparisons. The loss function employs the commonly used cross-entropy loss for classification, defined as follows:12\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Loss=-\sum\nolimits_{{i=1}}^{m} {{y_i}log({{\hat {y}}_i})}$$\end{document}

All experiments utilised the AdamW optimiser to optimise the model parameters, with a learning rate of 0.001 and weight decay of 0.01. The training batch size was set to 386, with a maximum of 300 training epochs. Furthermore, the “lr_scheduler” was set to “cosine” and “warmup_epochs” to 1. Early stopping was employed based on the minimum loss criterion during training. The proposed method was implemented using the PyTorch framework, and all experiments were conducted on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 graphics card. Furthermore, the extraction of laryngoscope image features is uniformly carried out using the official pre-trained ResNet50 model. The official link to the pre-trained weights is as follows: https://huggingface.co/timm/resnet50.a1_in1k/resolve/main/pytorch_model.bin.

Evaluation criteria

To comprehensively evaluate the model’s predictive performance, metrics such as Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC), Accuracy (ACC), Precision, Recall, and F1 score are employed. Values closer to 1 indicate superior model performance. The calculation formulas are as follows:13\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$AUC=\int\limits_{0}^{1} {TPR(FP{R^{-1}}(x))dx}$$\end{document}14\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Accuracy=\frac{{TP+TN}}{{TP+TN+FP+FN}}$$\end{document}15\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Precision=\frac{{TP}}{{TP+FP}}$$\end{document}16\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Sensitivity=Recall=\frac{{TP}}{{TP+FN}}$$\end{document}17\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$F1=2 \times \frac{{Precision \times Recall}}{{Precision+Recall}}$$\end{document}18\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Specificity=\frac{{TN}}{{TN+FP}}$$\end{document}

True positives (TP) and true negatives (TN) represent the number of correctly predicted positive and negative samples by the model, respectively; false positives (FP) and false negatives (FN) denote the number of samples misclassified. ACC denotes the proportion of correctly predicted samples out of all samples. The ROC curve illustrates the trade-off between sensitivity and specificity at different classification thresholds, while AUC provides a comprehensive measure of the model’s overall discriminative capability. In the overall performance evaluation, Precision, Recall and F1-score are all calculated using the macro-average method; that is, the metrics for each category are calculated separately, and then an average is taken across all categories, thereby ensuring that each category is given equal weight in cases where the categories may be imbalanced. Meanwhile, in the category-specific analysis, a non-averaged one-vs-rest approach is adopted to calculate the Precision, Recall (i.e. Sensitivity) and F1-score for each category separately, in order to provide a more detailed picture of the model’s specific performance across different categories. For binary classification tasks, Sensitivity and Specificity correspond to the recall rates for the positive and negative classes respectively.

Results

Comparative experiment

To validate the effectiveness of the proposed model in coarse-grained and fine-grained VCL prediction, this study compares CKAD-Net with several classical deep learning models on the VCLScopeData dataset. The models compared include MLP, ResNet25, DenseNet26, ConvNeXt27, MobileVIT_xs28, Transformer29, VGG30, Conv2Former31 and NDFNet22. In ablation experiments for fine-grained classification, CKAD-Net achieved an AUC of 0.929, representing a 1.6% improvement over CKAD-Net_CDF (AUC: 0.913), which relies solely on category-guided discriminative feature representations for prediction. Compared to CKAD-Net_RF (AUC: 0.926), which uses only feature region representations for prediction, it demonstrated a 0.3% increase. This result demonstrates that combining feature subsets from each region with key features constructed via attention mechanisms and discriminative feature representations across different regions more effectively captures critical information about pathological areas, thereby enhancing the model’s predictive performance. Additionally, CKAD-Net achieved a 0.9% performance improvement over CKAD-Net_NoAda (AUC: 0.920), which lacks an adaptive decision mechanism. This adaptive decision mechanism dynamically adjusts fusion weights based on feature contributions, granting the model greater flexibility across fine-grained tasks and enhancing its ability to distinguish vocal fold lesions. Collectively, the synergistic interaction among category-guided discriminative feature representations, feature region representations, and the adaptive decision mechanism is pivotal to CKAD-Net’s optimal performance in fine-grained classification tasks.

Discussion

Vocal cord lesions (VCL) are common laryngeal disorders potentially associated with vocal cord overuse, poor vocal hygiene, chronic inflammatory irritation, viral infections, or systemic conditions such as gastroesophageal reflux disease and allergies32. Based on pathological characteristics, VCL can be classified into benign and malignant categories. Benign lesions, including vocal cord polyps and nodules, are often linked to chronic mechanical irritation or inflammatory responses33,34 Malignant lesions encompass keratinizing lesions, severe epithelial dysplasia, and laryngeal carcinoma, histologically characterized by varying degrees of cellular atypia and structural disruption35. Clinically, benign lesions predominantly occur in the vocal cords and surrounding tissues, causing hoarseness or even vocal cord paralysis, and severely impairing respiratory and vocal functions in advanced cases36,37. Although most benign lesions do not immediately threaten life, if left undiagnosed and untreated for extended periods, some may progress from chronic inflammation to atypical hyperplasia or even malignancy38. Vocal cord keratosis is a common precancerous lesion characterized by epithelial hyperkeratinization, cellular accumulation, and incomplete maturation. Surface cells that should normally slough off fail to shed properly, often presenting clinically as white patches39. Due to its morphological and molecular characteristics lying between benign and malignant, its nature is difficult to determine based on appearance alone, posing a potential risk of malignant transformation. Currently, the standard diagnostic method for VCL is laryngoscopy. However, this method is highly dependent on physician experience, leading to subjective variations in diagnostic outcomes, particularly in the identification of early-stage lesions where misdiagnosis is common40,41. Therefore, achieving precise differentiation between benign and malignant vocal cord lesions using laryngoscopic images has become a key research focus. Several studies have explored VCL prediction using laryngoscopic images. For instance, Li et al.42 developed a highly accurate VCL risk stratification model using white-light laryngoscopic images. Zhao et al.19 employed MobileNet for VLC prediction. Kang et al.20 utilized the Swin-Transformer model for prediction tasks. Ren et al.21 employed the ResNet model for classification prediction. Wei et al.22 proposed a non-local dual-stream attention fusion network, NDF-Net, for prediction tasks. These works have advanced VCL prediction. However, existing methods generally rely on standard convolutional network architectures, lacking structural optimization for vocal fold lesion features. They insufficiently consider discriminative feature differences across lesion categories and lack decision-level fusion mechanisms. Furthermore, traditional attention-based feature aggregation typically assigns weights based solely on overall feature space similarity, lacking category-semantic guidance, making it difficult to precisely focus on key regions for specific lesion categories. Therefore, this study proposes CKAD-Net, a vocal cord lesion prediction model based on category-guided key feature aggregation and adaptive decision-making mechanisms utilizing laryngoscopy images. The model introduces a category-guided key feature aggregation mechanism. This mechanism combines feature subsets from each region with key features constructed via attention mechanisms and discriminative feature representations across different feature regions. This approach more effectively captures critical information from lesion areas, thereby enhancing the model’s predictive performance. This multi-level feature fusion strategy not only enhances the model’s sensitivity to fine-grained differences but also improves overall discriminative capability. Additionally, the model incorporates an adaptive decision fusion mechanism. Its learnable weight factors enable the model to automatically increase the weight of a feature region in the final decision when that region demonstrates strong discriminative power.

In the experimental section, the CKAD-Net model underwent systematic comparative and ablation experiments on the private dataset VCLScopeData for both coarse-grained and fine-grained tasks to validate its effectiveness and robustness. In the coarse-grained comparison experiments, CKAD-Net demonstrated optimal performance compared to other models, achieving AUC and ACC values of 0.944 and 0.864 respectively. In fine-grained comparisons, CKAD-Net also demonstrated outstanding prediction capabilities, achieving AUC and ACC values of 0.929 and 0.730, respectively. Finally, ablation studies further validated that CKAD-Net’s category-guided discriminative feature learning mechanism and adaptive decision mechanism exhibit synergistic effects, indicating their combination effectively enhances the model’s recognition performance. Although CKAD-Net achieved outstanding results across different granularity tasks, the model still holds room for improvement. Future research will focus on exploring more efficient feature representation methods and multi-level fusion strategies, while incorporating larger-scale or multi-center datasets to enhance the model’s robustness and clinical applicability. Overall, the proposed CKAD-Net model demonstrates high accuracy and stability in vocal cord lesion detection. Through systematic analysis of laryngoscopic images, this model enables non-invasive early lesion identification, empowering clinicians to intervene and treat promptly, thereby reducing the risk of benign lesions progressing to malignancy. Such research holds significant implications for improving diagnostic consistency, reducing healthcare costs, and advancing precision management of vocal cord diseases.

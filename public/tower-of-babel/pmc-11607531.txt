Helicobacter pylori activates SLFN4+MDSCs to accelerate gastric intestinal metaplasia
Authors: Zhihong Zhao, Rui Liu, Ye Peng, Cun Li, Qian Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryChronic infection with Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) is a major risk factor for gastric cancer. This work attempted to investigate the underlying mechanism of SLFN4+ myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) in affecting gastric intestinal metaplasia (GIM). H. pylori infection enhanced the expression of IFN-α and SLFN4, and activated JAK2/STAT1 signaling pathway in bone marrow cells or mouse gastric corpus. Meanwhile, IFN-α induced the transportation of STAT1 to the nucleus and activated the SLFN4 promoter. Moreover, the proportion of SLFN4+MDSC was decreased in IFN-α-treated bone marrow cells following TB42 treatment (JAK2/STAT1 signaling inhibitor). Additionally, miR-130b-3p was secreted by SLFN4+MDSCs. MiR-130b-3p was upregulated, tuberous sclerosis complex 1 (TSC1) was downregulated in H. pylori+ patients. MiR-130b-3p inhibition in SLFN4+MDSCs alleviated GIM. In conclusion, H. pylori infection-induced high levels of IFN-α activated JAK/STAT1 signaling pathway to promote differentiation of SLFN4+MDSCs. High expression of miR-130b-3p in SLFN4+MDSCs inactivated Shh signaling pathway by targeting TSC1, thereby promoting GIM.

Introduction

Gastric cancer has an extremely high incidence rate in worldwide.1 Chronic Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) infection is a major risk factor for the high incidence of gastric cancer.2 Chronic gastritis (CG) caused by H. pylori infection may eventually develop into gastric cancer once precancerous lesions such as intestinal epithelial hyperplasia and heterogeneous hyperplasia occur.3 Therefore, it is of great clinical significance by blocking the development of gastric precancerous lesions. However, the exact molecular biological mechanism by which H. pylori infection-induced CG develops into intestinal metaplasia and ectopic hyperplasia is still not fully understood. Our previous research has confirmed that in the gastric tissues of H. pylori infected mice, the expression of interferon-α (IFN-α) is significantly increased. Highly expressed IFN-α induces differentiation of Schlafen 4-expressing myeloid-derived suppressor cells (SLFN4+ MDSCs) in bone marrow cells.4 Here, the mechanisms of IFN-α-induced differentiation of SLFN4+ MDSCs in H. pylori-induced gastric intestinal metaplasia (GIM) remain to be explored.

The janus kinase (JAK) and signal transducer and activator of transcription 1 (STAT1) signaling pathway is involved in the progression of various pathologies.5,6 It is one of the downstream pathways of IFN-α.7 Activation of the JAK/STAT1 signaling pathway is also found in H. pylori infected mice and H. pylori infected cancer-associated fibroblasts.8,9 As a member of the transcription factor family, STAT1 is involved in regulating the expression of target genes by translocating to the nucleus and binding to gene promoters.10 Our previous study has shown that IFN-α induces the SLFN4 transcription by binding to interferon-sensitive response elements (ISREs) in the SLFN4 promoter and elevates the expression of SLFN4.4 Under H. pylori infection, it is unclear whether IFN-α affect the differentiation of SLFN4+MDSCs by activating the JAK/STAT1 signaling pathway.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are a class of endogenous noncoding RNAs with regulatory functions found in eukaryotes, with a size of approximately 20–25 nucleotides.11 Mature miRNAs are assembled into RNA induced silencing complexes, which recognize target mRNA through base complementary pairing.12 It guides the silencing complex to degrade target mRNA or suppress the translation of target mRNA. Ding L et al. have shown that SLFN4+MDSCs secreted miR130b, which is involved in GIM caused by H. pylori infection.13 Bioinformatics analysis has revealed that tuberous sclerosis complex 1 (TSC1) may be a target of miR-130b-3p.14 TSC1 expression is reduced in gastric cancer tissues, which is associated with poor survival of gastric cancer patients.15 The mechanism of miR-130b-3p/TSC1 in regulating GIM has been further investigated.

Sonic hedgehog (Shh) signaling pathway is present in a variety of malignant tumors, including gastric cancer.16 Activation of Shh signaling pathway is an early response to gastritis caused by H. pylori infection.17,18 However, it has also been found that Shh signaling pathway-associated proteins are expressed in gastric tissues of both H. pylori infection-positive and negative patients. The expression of Shh, smoothened (Smo), and glioma-related oncogene homolog 2 (Gli2) is reduced in H. pylori+ patients with respect to H. pylori− patients.19 Before incomplete GIM, eradicating H. pylori infection effectively improve atrophic gastritis by regulating Shh signaling pathway.20 The functional role of Shh signaling pathway in gastritis caused by H. pylori infection still remains to be explored.

In this work, we investigated whether the high levels of IFN-α caused by H. pylori infection can increase the expression of SLFN4 and induce differentiation of SLFN4+MDSCs by activating JAK/STAT1 signaling pathway. SLFN4+MDSCs secrete miR-130b-3p to promote GIM by regulating TSC1/Shh signaling pathway.

Results

IFN-α promoted differentiation of SLFN4+ MDSCs by activating JAK/STAT1 signaling pathway

High levels of IFN-α induce SLFN4+ MDSCs in bone marrow cells. Results of RT-qPCR and western blotting revealed that the expression of IFN-α and SLFN4 was notably elevated in bone marrow cells following H. pylori infection (Figures 1A and 1C). The levels of IFN-α were elevated in H. pylori-infected bone marrow cells, as determined by ELISA (Figure 1B). Moreover, the relative ratio of p-JAK2/JAK2 and p-STAT1/STAT1 was also increased in H. pylori-infected bone marrow cells, indicating that JAK/STAT1 signaling pathway was activated (Figure 1C). A luciferase activity assay in our previous study has shown that IFN-α can bind to ISREs in the SLFN4 promoter and elevate the expression of SLFN4.4 Therefore, we conducted luciferase assay analysis to detect the effect of IFN-α on the activity of the SLFN4 gene promoter. The result demonstrated that the luciferase activity was elevated in SLFN4+H. pylori group, indicating that the SLFN4 promoter was activated in H. pylori-infected bone marrow cells (Figure 1D). Afterward, we found that the expression of p-STAT1 was significantly elevated in IFN-α-treated bone marrow cells (Figure 1E). Interestingly, the expression of STAT1 was decreased in cytoplasm of IFN-α-treated bone marrow cells, while the expression of STAT1 was elevated in nucleus of bone marrow cells in the presence of IFN-α (Figure 1F). The proportion of SLFN4+MDSCs was decreased in IFN-α-treated bone marrow cells following TB42 treatment (JAK2/STAT1 signaling inhibitor) (Figure 1G). Furthermore, H. pylori-infected bone marrow cells were incubated with IFN-α nAb. The expression of IFN-α and SLFN4 mRNA and protein were decreased in H. pylori-infected bone marrow cells following IFN-α nAb treatment (Figures 1H and 1I). IFN-α nAb treatment caused a decreased relative ratio of p-JAK2/JAK2 and p-STAT1/STAT1 in H. pylori-infected bone marrow cells (Figure 1I). These data indicated that high levels of IFN-α in H. pylori-infected bone marrow cells activated JAK/STAT1 signaling pathway and induced the transportation of STAT1 to nucleus. H. pylori infection elevated IFN-α levels and then activated JAK/STAT1 signaling pathway in mice

Mice were infected with H. pylori for 0, 2, 4, 6 months. The H&E staining test results showed that compared with the UI group (H. pylori infection-0 months), significant inflammatory cell infiltration was observed in the gastric tissue of the H. pylori infection-2M/4M/6M group (Figure 2A). Next, the expression of IFN-α and SLFN4 in gastric tissues of H. pylori-infected mice was detected by RT-qPCR and western blotting. Following H. pylori infection, the expression of IFN-α and SLFN4 was elevated in gastric tissues of mice (Figures 2B and 2C). Increased expression of p-JAK2/JAK2 and p-STAT1/STAT1 was observed in gastric tissues of H. pylori-infected mice (Figure 2C). The expression of SLFN4 was also increased in gastric tissues of H. pylori-infected mice, as determined by immunohistochemistry assay (Figure 2D). MiR-130b-3p inhibition in SLFN4+MDSCs repressed GIM of GMECs

SLFN4+MDSCs (SLFN4+CD11b+GR-1+) and SLFN4−MDSCs (SLFN4−CD11b+GR-1+) were sorted with the gating strategies through flow cytometry (Figure 3A). Compared with SLFN4−MDSCs, the expression of miR-130b-3p was elevated in SLFN4+MDSCs (Figure 3B). Following H. pylori infection, miR-130b-3p was also increased in bone marrow cells (Figure 3C). FISH results revealed that miR-130b-3p was secreted by SLFN4+MDSCs (Figure 3D). Then, miR-130b-3p was silenced in H. pylori-infected SLFN4+MDSCs, and then co-cultured with mouse gastric mucosal epithelial cells (GMECs). Inhibition of miR-130b-3p reduced cell viability of mouse GMECs (Figure 3E). Moreover, VIL1 and MUC2 were downregulated in mouse GMECs in the presence of miR-130b-3p inhibitor, as determined by RT-qPCR and western blotting (Figures 3F and 3G). MiR-130b-3p inhibition repressed GIM of GMECs by targeting TSC1

Bioinformatics website predicted that TSC1 may be a target for miR-130b-3p. The expression of TSC1 was elevated in GMECs in the presence of SLFN4+MDSCs with miR-130b-3p inhibitor, as determined by RT-qPCR and western blotting (Figures 4A and 4B). After that, GMECs were transfected with miR-130b-3p inhibitor combined with si-TSC1. MiR-130b-3p knockdown elevated the expression of TSC1, Shh, Smo, and Gli1 mRNA and protein in GMECs, while TSC1 silencing caused a downregulation of TSC1, Shh, Smo, and Gli1 mRNA and protein in GMECs. The influence conferred by miR-130b-3p inhibitor was restored by TSC1 silencing (Figures 4C and 4D). Moreover, MiR-130b-3p knockdown caused a downregulation of VIL1 and MUC2, while TSC1 silencing reversed the down-regulation in GMECs (Figure 4D). Meanwhile, immunofluorescence detection showed the same results as western blotting in GMECs (Figure 4E). Luciferase reporter assay demonstrated that the luciferase activity was decreased in GMECs following transfection of WT-TSC1 and miR-130b-3p mimic, suggesting that miR-130b-3p interacted with TSC1 (Figure 4F). MiR-130b-3p inhibition repressed GIM in H. pylori-infected mice through TSC1/Shh signaling pathway

In vivo, we observed that the expression miR-130b-3p was increased in serum and gastric tissues of mice following H. pylori infection (Figures 5A and 5B). Western blotting assessed the expression of TSC1 and Shh singling pathway-related proteins in H. pylori-infected mice. H. pylori infection led to a down-regulation of TSC1, Shh, Smo, and Gli1 in gastric tissues of H. pylori-infected mice in a time-dependent manner (Figures 5C and 5D). Immunohistochemistry assay demonstrated that TSC1 levels were deceased and VIL1 levels were increased in gastric mucosal tissues of H. pylori-infected mice (Figure 5E). Additionally, H. pylori-infected mice were injected with miR-130b-3p antagomir to silence miR-130b-3p in vivo. The H&E staining test results showed that compared with the UI group, significant inflammatory cell infiltration was observed in the gastric tissue of the H. pylori group, while inflammation was significantly decreased after miR-130b-3p silencing (Figure 5F). High expression of miR-130b-3p in serum and gastric tissues of H. pylori-infected mice was reduced by miR-130b-3p antagomir (Figures 5G and 5H). MiR-130b-3p antagomir reversed H. pylori infection-induced decrease of TSC1, Shh, Smo, and Gli1 expression in mice (Figure 5I). The immunohistochemistry assay of TSC1 showed similar results. Moreover, VIL1 levels were increased in H. pylori-infected mice, while VIL1 was decreased in H. pylori-infected mice in the presence of miR-130b-3p antagomir (Figure 5J). The expression of miR-130b-3p in the gastrointestinal mucosal samples of these patients was detected by RT-qPCR. Compared with H. pylori− patients, miR-130b-3p was notably elevated in H. pylori+ patients. In H. pylori+ patients, the expression of miR-130b-3p was elevated in patients as the degree of intestinal metaplasia increases (Figure 6A). Moreover, the expression of TSC1, Shh, Smo, and Gli1 was decreased, the expression of SLFN12, VIL1, and MUC2 was increased in H. pylori+ patients with respect to H. pylori− patients. Accompanied by the worsening of the disease, the expression of p-JAK2/JAK2, p-STAT1/STAT1, SLFN12L, VIL1, and MUC2 was gradually increased in patients. However, the expression of TSC1, Shh, Smo, and Gli1 gradually decreased with the deepening of the disease, and these proteins were significantly lower in the H. pylori+ patients than in H. pylori− patients (Figures 6I–6S). Discussion

The mechanism of gastric cancer is closely related to various factors such as H. pylori infection, medication, environment, poor dietary habits, and bile reflux.21,22 Gastric cancer directly affects the physical and mental health of patients and reduces the ability of daily life.23 GIM is the important stage in the Correa hypothesis of intestinal type gastric cancer.24 Therefore, the study of GIM is of great significance for the early prevention of gastric cancer. Chronic inflammation of the gastric mucosa caused by H. pylori infection is a key causative factor for intestinal epithelial metaplasia of the gastric mucosa.25 In this study, IFN-α expression was significantly increased in gastric tissues of H. pylori infected mice, and the differentiation of SLFN4+ MDSCs in bone marrow cells. SLFN4+ MDSCs with miR-130b-3p inhibitor reduced GIM of GMECs.

IFN-α is a major effector cytokine of antiviral infections.26 IFN-α is produced primarily by plasmacytoid dendritic cells following viral infection and has multiple isoforms encoded by 14 non-alleles.27 There is increasing evidence that sustained production of IFN-α during chronic infection suppresses and deleteriously affects the immune response.28 IFN-α plays a protective or deleterious role in pathogenic infections and autoimmune diseases.29 A research has revealed that activation of the TLR4-TRIF pathway in gastric epithelial cells induces IFN-α production after Haemophilus gastricus infection.30 In this study, the expression of IFN-α was significantly increased in gastric tissues of H. pylori infected mice. JAK/STAT1 is an important component of IFN signaling pathway.31 Some viruses can affect the antiviral effect by activating JAK/STAT1 and IFN signaling pathways.32 Meanwhile, during chronic H. pylori infection, SLFN4+MDSCs expressing SLFN4 create a microenvironment conducive to GIM and tumor transformation.4 In this study, IFN-α expression levels were significantly increased in gastric tissues of H. pylori infected mice. High expression of IFN-α activated JAK/STAT1 signaling pathway. IFN-αpromoted the translocation of STAT1 to nucleus, thereby promoting the activity of SLFN4 promoter. IFN-α accelerated the differentiation of SLFN4+ MDSCs in bone marrow cells by regulating JAK/STAT1 signaling pathway.

MiR-130b-3p participates in various diseases. For instance, miR-130b-3p knockdown elevates the autophagic flux and alleviates the progression of intervertebral disc degeneration. MiR-130b-3p is highly expressed in hepatocellular carcinoma, which is correlated with a poor prognosis.33 A previous study has confirmed that miR-130b-3p is abnormally expressed in serum exosomes of gastric cancer patients.34 A research showed that SLFN4+MDSCs isolated from the metaplastic mouse stomach express miR130b. Furthermore, Ding L et al. have showed that SLFN4+MDSCs activate the NF-κB pathway, which directly induces miR130b expression, which is involved in GIM due to H. pylori infection.13 This work first revealed the functional role of miR-130b-3p in H. pylori infection-induced gastritis. H. pylori infected SLFN4+MDSCs secrete high levels of miR-130b-3p, and then inhibited the expression of its target TSC1. It has been demonstrated that TSC1 exerts a tumor suppressor function with reduced expression in gastric cancer tissues.35,36 In this study, we found that miR-130b-3p inhibition in SLFN4+MDSCs repressed the expression of intestinal marker of epithelial cells VIL1, and MUC2 in GMECs. TSC1 silencing reversed miR-130b-3p inhibitor-mediated upregulation of TSC1 and VIL1 in GMECs. Thus, miR-130b-3p inhibition repressed GIM of GMECs by targeting TSC1.

Recent studies have shown that the Shh signaling pathway is important in the development of a variety of tumors.37 When a hedgehog is present, Smo transmits signals into the cytoplasm and activates the downstream transcription factor Gli1, which induces transcription of the downstream target genes.38 Different degrees of activation of Shh ligands upstream of hedgehog signaling pathway and downstream transcription factor Gli1 make tumor cells more invasive and metastatic.39,40 In this study, the expression of Shh, Smo, and Gli1 was reduced in H. pylori-infected mice, which was enhanced by miR-130b-3p antagomir, while the addition of si-TSC1 reversed this phenomenon. These results showed that TSC1 silencing inhibited the Shh signaling pathway. The expression of TSC1, Shh, Smo, and Gli1 was decreased, the expression of SLFN12L, VIL1, and MUC2 was increased in H. pylori+ patients with respect to H. pylori− patients. Thus, the Shh signaling pathway is a crucial pathway in H. pylori-infected gastritis. Furthermore, our results also demonstrated that there is a certain connection between TSC1 and Shh. However, we have not conducted in-depth research to elucidate the specific mechanism of the relationship between TSC and Shh pathway proteins in promoting intestinal metaplasia. This is also our future research direction to ensure the integrity and continuity of our research.

This work demonstrated that H. pylori infection-induced high levels of IFN-α activated JAK/STAT1 signaling pathway to promote differentiation of SLFN4+MDSCs. High expression of miR-130b-3p in SLFN4+MDSCs inactivated Shh signaling pathway by targeting TSC1, thereby promoting GIM.

Limitations of the study

Our results demonstrated that there is a certain connection between TSC1 and Shh. However, we have not conducted in-depth research to elucidate the specific mechanism of the relationship between TSC and Shh pathway proteins in promoting intestinal metaplasia. This is also our future research direction to ensure the integrity and continuity of our research.

Experimental model and study participant details

Ethical approval and consent

All participants signed the informed consent. The protocols were conformed to the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Ethical Committee of Xiangya Hospital Central South University.

Animals

C57BL/6 male mice at 1 week (n = 10) and 4 months old (n = 60) were obtained from Sibeifu Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Beijing, China). Mice were maintained in SPF conditions with a constant temperature (23 ± 2°C) and relative humidity (45-55%). Mice took food and water freely.

H. pylori infection in mice

For H. pylori infection, C57BL/6 male mice at 4 months old were administrated by gavage of 108 CFU/100 μL of H. pylori (CS1 strain) 3 times over 5 days. Uninfected mice were gavaged with 100 μL saline alone. Infection lasted 2, 4, or 6 months.4,41 Meanwhile, mice were intraperitoneally injected with 50 nmol miR-130b-3p antagomir or antagomir NC (Genechem, Shanghai, China) once two weeks for 2, 4, 6 months. After modeling, mice were intraperitoneally injected with 150 mg/kg pentobarbital sodium for euthanasia. Gastric tissues were obtained from mice for further analysis.

Human participants

This study enrolled 36 patients who visited the Department of Gastroenterology at Xiangya Hospital Central South University. These patients were had undergone endoscopy for the diagnosis of abdominal pain or discomfort were included in this study. Gastrointestinal mucosal samples were obtained from these patients who underwent endoscopies, and then used for histological examination and H. pylori detection. According to the degree of intestinal metaplasia detected by histological examination, these patients were divided into three groups: chronic gastritis (CG) group, mild intestinal metaplasia (mi IM) group, and moderate/severe intestinal metaplasia group (mo/se IM). Following the H. pylori infection status, the patients in each group were divided into H. pylori-positive patients and H. pylori-negative patients.

Isolation and treatment of bone marrow cells

Bone marrow cells were isolated from femurs of C57BL/6 male mice with 1 week. Mice were intraperitoneally injected with 150 mg/kg pentobarbital sodium, followed by euthanasia with cervical dislocation. Under aseptic conditions, the femurs were separated from mice applying surgical scissors. The bone marrow cavity was rinsed with α-MEM. The marrow suspension was filtered through a fine meshed sieve to remove bone particles. The single cell suspension was cultured in α-MEM, 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin-streptomycin at 37°C and 5% CO2.

Bone marrow cells were treated with 100 CFU of H. pylori or combined with 800 U/mL of IFN-α for 0, 24 and 48 h. Bone marrow cells were treated with 50 μM TB42 (JAK2/STAT1 signaling pathway inhibitor) for 0, 6, 12, 24, 48 h. Bone marrow cells were treated with IFN-α-neutralizina antibody for 24 h.

Method details

Flow cytometry

SLFN4+MDSCs and SLFN4-MDSCs were sorted from stomach tissues of H. pylori-infected mice as our previous study described.41 The percentage of SLFN4+MDSCs in H. pylori-infected bone marrow cells following IFN-α and TB42 (JAK2/STAT1 signaling inhibitor) treatment was analyzed by flow cytometry following our previous study described.41 Bone marrow cells were incubated with anti-CD11b-FITC (BioLegend, San Diego, CA, USA), and then the gated CD11b+ cells were analyzed for SLFN4-tdTomato fluorescence. After that, bone marrow cells were stained with anti-Gr-1APC (BioLegend). Finally, the percentage of SLFN4+MDSCs was analyzed on a BD-FACS Calibur flow cytometry (BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA, USA).

Cell co-culture

Mouse gastric mucosal epithelial cells (GMECs) were obtained from Procell (Wuhan, China). SLFN4+MDSCs were seeded into the upper chamber of transwell chamber, and mouse GMECs were seeded into the lower chamber. Both SLFN4+MDSCs and mouse GMECs were cultured in α-MEM, 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. The transwell chamber was placed in an incubator of 37°C and 5% CO2.

Cell transfection

The oligonucleotides miR-130b-3p inhibitor and small inference especially targeting TSC1 (si-TSC1) were synthesized for knockdown of miR-130b-3p. Inhibitor NC or scrambled siRNA (si-NC) served as control. Cells were inoculated into 96-well plates and cultured until the cell fusion was about 70%. Cells were transfected with oligonucleotides applying Lipofectamine™ 2000 reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific).

Reverse transcription-quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted from cells and tissues by applying TRIzol reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Complementary DNA was synthesized from total RNA as template applying PrimeScript reverse transcriptase reagent kit or Mix-X miRNA First-Strand Synthesis Kit (TaKaRa, Beijing, China). Real-time PCR was performed on StepOnePlus Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA) applying TB Green® Premix Ex Taq™ Ⅱ (TaKaRa). GAPDH or U6 was used as loading control. Western blotting

Tissues and cells were treated with RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime, Shanghai, China), and total proteins were extracted. Nuclear and cytoplasmic proteins were extracted from bone marrow cells applying Nuclear and Cytoplasmic Protein Extraction Kit (Kanglang Biotechnology, Shanghai, China). The protein samples were separated by performing 10% SDS-PAGE protein electrophoresis, and then transferred to a PVDF membrane (Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA). The membranes were incubated with primary antibodies at 4°C overnight, including anti-JAK2 (1:1000, ab108596), anti-STAT1 (1:1000, ab109320), anti-p-JAK2 (1:1000, ab195055), anti-p-STAT1 (1:1000, ab314033), anti-SLFN4 (1:500, LS-C47092), IFN-α (1:1000, ab196221), anti-TSC1 (1:1000, ab217328), anti-Shh (1:1000, ab53281), anti-Smo (1:1000, ab219652), anti-Gli1 (1:500, ab134906), anti-VIL1 (1:1000, ab218331), anti-MUC2 (1:1000, ab272692), anti-SLFN12 (1:1000, ab314624). The membranes were stained with goat anti-rabbit HRP-conjugated IgG (1:1000 dilution) at room temperature for 1 h. GAPDH (1:1000 dilution), α-tubulin (1:1000, ab7291) and Histone H3 (1:5000, ab308373) were served as loading control. All antibodies were obtained from Abcam (Cambridge, MA, USA), except for anti-SLFN4 (LifeSpan BioSciences, Seattle, WA, USA). The immunoprecipitated bands were developed with ECL reagent and analyzed by ImageJ software.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

The serum levels of the INF-α was measured by Mouse INF-α ELISA Kit. The experiments were performed according to the instructions for the kit. The absorbance value at 405 nm was examined using a Multiskan SkyHigh microplate reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific).

Luciferase reporter assay

For detection of the activity of SLFN4 promoter, SLFN4 promoter was inserted into pGL3-basic vector, generating SLFN4-Luc vector. H. pylori infected or uninfected bone marrow cells were transfected with SLFN4-Luc vector. For verify the interaction between TSC1 and miR-130b-3p, the wild type and mutant sequence of TSC1 was inserted into the pmirGLO dul-luciferase reporter vector, generating the vectors WT-TSC1 (5’-AGGCAGGCGUUUUUUUGCACUA-3’) and MUT-TSC1 (5’-CGAAAGGCGUUCUUGCCCGACA-3’). 293T cells were transfected with WT-TSC1/MUT-TSC1 combined with miR-130b-3p mimic/mimic NC. Finally, the luciferase assay was performed using the Dual Luciferase Reporter Assay System.

Immunohistochemical and immunofluorescence assays

The paraffin-embedded tissues were cut into sections (4 μm). Following deparaffinization and hydration, sections were subjected to heat-induced antigen retrieval and Hydrogen Peroxide Block. The sections were incubated with anti-SLFN4 (1:100 dilution; Abcam), anti-TSC1 (1:500 dilution; Abcam) and anti-VIL1 (1:500 dilution; Abcam) at 4°C overnight, and then stained with goat anti-rabbit HRP-conjugated IgG (1:200 dilution; Abcam) at room temperature for 1 h. The sections were stained with 3,3’-Diaminobenzidine (DAB) and then counterstained with hematoxylin.

For immunofluorescence assay, the paraffin sections were incubated with rabbit anti-TSC1 (1:30 dilution; Abcam), mouse anti-VIL1 (1:500 dilution; Abcam) at 4°C overnight, and then stained with goat anti-rabbit IgG-Alexa Fluor® 488 (1:200 dilution; Abcam) or goat anti-mouse IgG-Alexa Fluor® 647 (1:1000 dilution; Abcam) at room temperature for 1 h. The sections were stained with DAPI. Finally, the tissue specimens were scanned under an inverted confocal microscope.

Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH)

The FITC-labeled miR-130b-3p probes were synthesized by GenePharma. MDSCs were grown on round coverslips and infected with H. pylori for 0, 24, and 48 h. Then, cells were subjected to fixation, and permeabilization with 0.5% Triton X-100. After that, cells were incubated with FITC-labeled miR-130b-3p probes at 37°C overnight. Cells were stained with anti-SLFN4 (LifeSpan BioSciences) at 4°C overnight, and then incubated with goat anti-rabbit IgG-Alexa Fluor® 647 (1:1000 dilution; Abcam) at room temperature for 1 h. DAPI was used to stain nuclei. The fluorescence was visualized under a confocal laser scanning microscope.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Each assay was performed 3 times. Data were analyzed by GraphPad Prism 8.0 (La Jolla, CA, USA) and expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Two-tailed Student’s t-tests were used for the comparison between the two groups. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Tukey’s post hoc test was performed for the comparison of multiple groups. P < 0.05 was considered as a significant difference.

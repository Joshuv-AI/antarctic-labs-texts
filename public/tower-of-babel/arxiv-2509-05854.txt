The Shape of Generating Families
Ziqin Feng, Paul Gartside

Abstract
The topology of a space X X is generated by a family 𝒞 \mathcal{C} of its subsets provided that a set A ⊆ X A\subseteq X is closed in X X if and only if A ∩ C A\cap C is closed in C C for each C ∈ 𝒞 C\in\mathcal{C} . A space X X is a k k -space (respectively, sequential ) if its topology is generated by the collection of all compact subsets (respectively, convergent sequences) of X X . Relations are defined to capture the notion of a space being a k k -space or sequential. The structure (or ‘shape’) under the Tukey order of these relations applied to separable metrizable spaces is examined. For the k k -space case the initial structure is completely determined, and the cofinal structure is shown to be highly complex. In the sequential case, however, the entire shape is determined. It follows that the number of Tukey types in the sequential case lies between ℵ 0 \aleph_{0} and 𝔠 \mathfrak{c} , is equal to ℵ 0 \aleph_{0} precisely when 𝔠 < ℵ ω 1 \mathfrak{c}<\aleph_{\omega_{1}} , and is equal to 𝔠 \mathfrak{c} if and only if 𝔠 \mathfrak{c} is a fixed point of the aleph function, necessarily of uncountable cofinality. Keywords: k k -space, compactly generated, sequential, separable metrizable, Tukey order, cardinal characteristics of the continuum, small cardinals, cardinal invariants. MSC Classification: 03E04, 03E17, 06A07, 54A25, 54D45, 54D50, 54D55, 54E35.

2.4
Topology: Notation, and a Special Space

Our topological definitions and notation are standard, see
[
2
]
. However we give concrete names and notations to certain objects. Recall
\mathop{NC}(X)
is the collection of all non-closed subsets of a space
X
, and
\mathop{CS^{+}}(X)
is all infinite convergent sequences with limit.
Additionally, let
\mathop{CS}(X)
be all infinite convergent sequences without limit and
(in other words,
\mathop{CS}(X)=\{e(\omega):e
is an embedding of
\omega+1
into

and
\mathop{CS^{+}}(X)=\{e(\omega+1):e
is an embedding of
\omega+1
into

For any

set

to be

along with its limit, so

is in

One space plays a key role throughout this paper.
The metric fan,
\mathbb{F}
, has underlying set
(\omega\times\omega)\cup\{\infty\}
, and topology where points of
\omega\times\omega
are isolated while basic neighborhoods of
\infty
have the form
\{\infty\}\cup([n,\infty)\times\omega)

.
The infinite convergent sequences are those subsets (i) containing

(which is the limit), (ii) having finite intersection with every vertical line

The compact subsets of

are those that are finite, along with the infinite convergent sequences.
Clearly, the metric fan is not locally compact. Further, it detects whether suitably nice spaces are locally compact.

Lemma 2.15
.

A separable metrizable space

is not locally compact if and only it it contains a closed copy of

Theorem 4.15

(Counting Types)
.

If
\mathfrak{c}=\aleph_{\alpha}

then there are
|\alpha|.\aleph_{0}
-many Tukey types of
\mathbf{seq}(M)
, where
M
is separable metrizable.

In particular,

(0) there are at least
\aleph_{0}
-many and no more than
\mathfrak{c}
-many Tukey types of
\mathbf{seq}(M)
, where
M
is separable metrizable,

(1) there are countably many Tukey types of
\mathbf{seq}(M)
, where
M
is separable metrizable if and only if
\mathfrak{c}<\aleph_{\omega_{1}}
, and

(2) there are continuum,
\mathfrak{c}
, many Tukey types of
\mathbf{seq}(M)
, where
M
is separable metrizable if and only if
\mathfrak{c}
is a fixed point of the aleph function with uncountable cofinality.

\mathfrak{c}
so there are
\mathfrak{c}
-many types is the
\omega_{1}
st fixed point of the aleph function.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{c}=\aleph_{\alpha}
.
Consider any cardinal
\aleph_{\beta}\leq\mathfrak{c}
. From Theorems
4.10
,
4.12
and
4.11

\aleph_{\beta}
contribute at least one and no more than

many
\mathbf{seq}(M)
Tukey types.

Hence if
\mathfrak{c}=\aleph_{\alpha}
where
\alpha<\omega_{1}

\mathfrak{c}\geq\aleph_{\omega_{1}}

\omega_{1}

.
On the other hand, for there to be continuum many types we must have

, and so
\mathfrak{c}

∎

Note that it is indeed consistent that the number of types of

equals

. Via Cohen forcing we can ensure

st fixed point of the aleph function) and the forcing does not disturb the aleph fixed points.

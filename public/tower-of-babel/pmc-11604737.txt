Green-synthesized silver nanoparticles induced apoptotic cell death in CACO2 cancer cells by activating MLH1 gene expression
Authors: Hamideh Khajeh, Bahman Fazeli-Nasab, Afsaneh Pourshahdad, Ali Reza Mirzaei, Mansour Ghorbanpour
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
MLH1 (Mult homolog1) gene is the main element of Multlα heterodimer and plays a role in the repair of base-base mismatches and deletion and addition loops. When the MLH1 protein is not present, the number of errors that remain unrepaired increases, and this can lead to the formation of tumors in the body. Colorectal cancer is the third most common type of cancer in terms of incidence and the third type of cancer in terms of mortality worldwide. In this regard, the present study was conducted with the aim of investigating the effect of biological silver nanoparticles with anticancer properties and MLH1 gene expression on cancer cell samples of people with colorectal cancer. In this study, the green synthesis of silver nanoparticles was carried out by precipitation method with reduction of silver ions by leaf and flower extract of Moringa oleifera plant. Then, silver nanoparticles were confirmed using UV-visible spectroscopy, transmission and scanning electron microscopy. The cytotoxic effects of nanoparticles on cells were evaluated by MTT colorimetric method within 42 h. After RNA extraction of treated cells, cDNA synthesis and primers were designed by the Exon-Exon Junction method and the Real-time Polymerase test for MLH1 and Beta-actin genes was repeated three times. The final analysis of the results was done using Graphpad Prism and Rest 2009 software. The presence of a peak at the wavelength of 234 nm for the synthesized silver nanoparticles was confirmed by ultraviolet-visible spectroscopic analysis. The morphological study on the size and shape of the silver nanoparticles showed that the nanoparticles are spherical and have a size between 40 and 34 nanometers in diameter. The leaf extract typically produces smaller, more uniform particles than the flower extract. The Green-Synthesized Silver Nanoparticles of leaf extract were used to evaluation of induced Apoptotic Cell Death in CACO2 Cancer Cells by Activating MLH1 Gene Expression. MTT results showed that the anti-proliferative effect of nanoparticles depends on the concentration of synthesized silver nanoparticles. The treatment of MLH1 cancer cell line and normal with synthesized nanoparticles at a concentration of 0.44 micrograms per ml for 42 h showed the effects of cell toxicity. The percentage of cancer cell death under the influence of quercetin present in Moringa green bio-particles depends on the concentration and time, and this difference is statistically significant compared to the control group (P < 0.05). So that the lethal dose of the extract for 50% survival IC50 in a period of 48 h for intestinal cancer cells was equal to 21 μg/ml, and the expression ratio of the MLH1 gene in the tumor tissue to the adjacent healthy tissue has increased (P ≤ 0.05).

Introduction

Cancer is a destructive disease that kills thousands of people worldwide every year, and its treatment has always been one of the most important challenges of humanity1,2. Among cancers, colon and colorectal cancer is one of the most common types of cancer3. Research shows that colon and colorectal cancer (CRC) ranks third among existing cancers, and statistics consider it the third cause of death from cancer4. Many factors such as aging, diet, family history, smoking and alcohol are involved in colorectal cancer. However, mutations and especially chromosomal instability play an important role in tumorigenesis in colorectal cancer. Among them, effective mutations in causing colorectal cancer are very important. These mutations lead to defects in mismatch repair protein synthesis. As a result, the resulting incompatibility is not repaired and tumorigenesis increases. The MLH1 gene is one of the important factors whose expression change can directly lead to colorectal cancer. On the other hand, a mutation in this gene leads to defects in the production of mismatch repair protein. In addition, 13–15% of colorectal cancers are caused by somatic promoter hyper methylation due to MLH1 deficiency5,6. The upregulation of MLH1 by nanosilver indicates its potential to enhance the effectiveness of chemotherapeutic agents, especially in cancers with MMR deficiencies. By restoring or increasing MLH1 activity, nanosilver may improve tumor responses to treatments like gemcitabine and other DNA-damaging agents7,8.

In recent years, we have seen significant advances in medical technology such as chemotherapy, radiation therapy and tumor surgery. But despite these advances, cancer treatment still has major disadvantages. The most important disadvantages that we face in cancer treatment today include the following. Failure to comply with the appropriate concentration of drugs, lack of appropriate and specific distribution of antitumor agents in the body, damage that may be caused to healthy tissues, general toxicity and long side effects9,10. But the most important thing is to achieve the appropriate concentration of drugs in the tumor site with the aim of destroying cancer cells and also minimizing the damage caused to normal cells11. For this reason, the use of new technologies such as nanotechnology has created a new revolution in cancer treatment12. In general, nanotechnology refers to the manipulation of materials at the nanoscale to produce products with new properties. Recent developments have shown that nanotechnology has had a great impact on the stages of cancer prevention, diagnosis and treatment13–15. Nanoparticles are particles between 1 and 100 nm whose performance depends on their mass, volume, electric charge and reactivity. Also, these particles have high diversity, high solubility and low toxicity. Nanoparticles can be metallic or non-metallic. Metal nanoparticles are usually spherical and form colloidal systems in the ionic state. In general, nanoparticles can lead to the removal of cancerous tumors without harming normal cells through fast and cheap production16–18.

The production of nanoparticles through biological methods is a straightforward and cost-effective approach that boasts low toxicity and high compatibility with the human body. This technique is often referred to as green technology. During the synthesis of nanoparticles by living organisms—whether unicellular or multicellular—both enzymatic and non-enzymatic reactions play a crucial role, particularly those involving polysaccharides. Among the biocompatible nanoparticles produced by microorganisms during their metabolic processes, silver, gold, titanium, and platinum nanoparticles stand out, with gold nanoparticles being particularly prominent in medical applications16,18–20.

Silver nanoparticles )AgNPs( with atomic number )Z = 47( are widely used in scientific fields and especially in cancer treatment for many reasons, such as strong photoelectric absorption coefficient, ease of synthesis, high surface-to-mass ratio and their high ability to bind to macromolecules18,21. The production of nanoparticles is generally done through physical and chemical methods, but the important point here is the change in the physical and chemical properties of nanoparticles. Also, these methods are very complicated and time-consuming, and their use ultimately leads to adverse biological effects. For this reason, nowadays, the synthesis of nanoparticles is done using microorganisms, algae, fungi, bacteria and plants, which is called green synthesis. Compared to other methods, this method is cheaper, simpler and non-toxic and is more environmentally friendly. But in general, the use of plants is preferred in the synthesis of nanoparticles due to their anti-pathogenic properties, compatibility with the environment and improvement of biological processes compared to other organisms22,23.

Moringa oleifera is a fast-growing, drought-resistant tree. This plant belongs to the Moringaceae family and is native to India, Thailand, the Philippines and Pakistan24. In general, the most used organs of this plant are leaves. But its seeds and wood are also used as food, powder, capsules. The dry leaves of the Moringa plant contain a large amount of useful antioxidants, which are effective in the health of people and the improvement of many diseases. Moringa naturally increases the body’s energy and strengthens the body’s immune system through detoxification. Intestinal ulcers are one of the common examples of abdominal diseases and disorders that can be treated with Moringa leaf extract. Research shows that this extract acts like a kind of medicine and contains many antibacterial properties that prevent intestinal infections in the body caused by bacteria and also treat conditions such as diarrhea25–27. Considering the high prevalence of colorectal cancer, the present study was conducted with the aim of investigating the effect of synthesized silver nanoparticles of the Moringa olifera plant with anti-cancer properties and the expression of the MLH1 gene in cancer cell samples of people with colorectal cancer.

This gene has been detected in most hereditary nonpolyposis colon cancer (HNPCC). Green synthesis of silver nanoparticles

For the synthesis of silver nanoparticles, the deposition method was used with the regeneration of silver ions by the leaf and flower extract. Silver nanoparticles were synthesized by adding 3 ml of aerial part extract of the Moringa oleifera plant to silver nitrate of German Merck with a concentration of 0.01 mM at room temperature and at a stirring speed of 30 RPM. After two hours of reaction time, the sediment was washed three times with distilled water. All washing steps were centrifuged (Eppendorf, 5810 R, Germany) at 13,000 RPM for 20 min. Finally, the final washing was done with ethanol and the product was placed at a temperature of 75 °C for 2 h.

Cell culture and investigation of cell toxicity

In order to investigate the effect of solutions on cell culture, CACO2 cancer cells purchased from Pasteur Institute of Iran were used, and the cytotoxicity test was performed using the MTT method on 3 replicates. In order to culture the cells, first DMEM culture medium was prepared and sterilized by filtration, and then 5% decomplemented fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin-streptomycin solution were added to it. Then the cell sample was introduced into the T25 flask with the above culture medium and incubated at 37 °C in the cell culture incubator with 5% CO2. After reaching 80% cell density, the sample was exposed to trypsin-EDTA. After 3 min of incubation at 37 °C in the incubator, cell culture was performed with 5% CO2. The cells removed from the bottom of the sample plate were centrifuged for 5 rounds for 5 min, and then the cell sediment was dissolved by adding the culture medium and trypsin was removed28.

Concentrations of 100, 50, 25, 12.5, 6.25 and 3.125 micrograms per milliliter of nanoparticles was treated on a CACO2 cell line at a time interval of 24 h. After the above time, the contents of the wells of the 96-well plate were carefully removed and MTT dye (Sigma Aldrich, Germany) was added to it and kept for 4 h under the conditions of 5% CO2 and 37 °C. After that, the contents of the wells were taken out and 50 µl of DMSO was added to them. After 30 min of incubation, the absorbance of the samples was read using an Elisa Reader (Organon Teknika, Netherlands) at a wavelength of 570 nm, and the cell death rate was calculated by the following formula:

To assess the impact of moringa extract on cell viability, we employed the trypan blue exclusion test alongside cell counting. In this experiment, 20,000 cells were seeded into each well of a 96-well plate. We then randomly selected a concentration range of 3.125, 6.25, 12.5, 25, 50, and 100 micrograms/ml to add to each well at 24 and 48 h. At each time point, we measured the cell count in each well using a hemocytometer slide and compared it to a methanol control. Additionally, we examined the effect of moringa extract on toxicity and cell death using the tetrazolium salt assay. This test relies on the reduction of yellow tetrazolium crystals by the enzyme succinate dehydrogenase, resulting in the formation of insoluble blue crystals. A total of 10,000 cells were counted using a Neubauer slide before being transferred to a 96-well plate for analysis. Each experiment was conducted independently at least three times to ensure reliability of the results.

Measurement of MLH1 gene expression

MLH1 gene expression was measured using the Real time-PCR method. First, the total RNA of treated and untreated cells with silver nanoparticles was extracted using the RNA extraction kit (Qiagen, USA) according to the instructions. Then its concentration was measured by Nanodrop device, IMPLEN, Germany. The construction of complementary DNA molecules was done with a RevertAid First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit Fermentas29.

cDNA synthesis

After completing the synthesis steps, the microtube containing the synthesized cDNA was placed on ice for 5 to 10 min to cool completely. In order to ensure the production of cDNA, 3–5 µl of the reaction mixture were electrophoresed on a 1% agarose gel stained with ethidium bromide.

Cytotoxic results of silver nanoparticles by MTT method

Cytotoxic effects of synthesized silver nanoparticles in colorectal cancer cell lines were performed by the MTT method and cell viability was measured after 24 h. The treatment of colorectal cancer cells with different concentrations for 24 h showed that the cytotoxicity of synthesized silver nanoparticles is dose-dependent. As shown in Fig. 7, the maximum cytotoxic effects were observed at concentrations of 100 µg/ml, 50 µg/ml, 25 µg/ml, 12.5 µg/ml and 6.25 µg/ml. Also, according to Fig. 8, it was found that IC50 is equal to 21 µg/ml.

It has been summarized (Table 8), the cytotoxic effects and MLH1 gene expression levels of two different extracts, Leaf Extract (A) and Flower Extract (B), at various concentrations. The control group showed no cytotoxicity, while both extracts demonstrated varying degrees of cytotoxic effects as the concentration decreased. Leaf Extract (A) exhibited higher cytotoxicity compared to Flower Extract (B) across most concentrations. Notably, the IC50 values indicate that Leaf Extract (A) has a lower inhibitory concentration than Flower Extract (B), suggesting it may be more potent. Additionally, MLH1 gene expression relative to the control increased with decreasing extract concentrations for both types, highlighting their potential biological effects.

Discussion

Cancer is the second cause of death in developing countries and the first cause of death in developed countries. Despite many efforts in the field of cancer prevention and treatment, this disease has been increasing and, unfortunately, in recent years, the cancer death rate in the world has increased by 28%, which is caused by various genetic and environmental factors30. Colorectal cancer is also the third most common cancer that affects many people in the world. The spread and variety of cancer prevalence during previous eras has led to the development of various treatment methods, which depend on the progress, type, extent of the disease and the patient’s condition. A combination of different methods is used to fight and treat cancer. Although the drug process is very effective for treatment, early diagnosis of CRC can play a key role in treating the disease and reducing mortality. In this regard, the use of nanotechnology and molecular markers can be used as a new method to advance diagnosis and treatment methods.

Nanoparticles include a wide range of particles that are at a high level in terms of diagnostic and therapeutic power. Today, silver nanoparticles are among the most widely used nanoparticles due to their unique properties such as easy synthesis, high surface-to-mass ratio, high reactivity, and non-toxicity. These nanoparticles are used in many fields, including for the treatment of cancer and especially colorectal cancer31. For this reason, silver nanoparticles were used in this research, which were synthesized by the green method using the M. olifera plant. In 2017, Reda et al. investigated the effect of the M. olifera plant on colorectal cancer. Their results showed that the leaves of this plant through oxygen free radicals lead to the stopping of the cell cycle and ultimately the death of cancer cells32. Also, the anticancer effects of this plant on breast and lung cancer have also shown that this plant contains useful compounds to induce apoptosis33.

In this research, green-synthesized silver nanoparticles were analyzed using a TEM electron microscope. The study found that the leaf extract of the M. olifera plant produced nanoparticles with a smaller size range compared to those synthesized from flower extract. It has been related that, Leaf extract typically produces silver nanoparticles in a smaller size range, usually around 10–20 nm. The morphology of these nanoparticles is generally more uniform and spherical, attributed to the higher concentration of reducing agents in the extract, which enables better control over the synthesis process34,35. TEM images typically exhibit a high degree of monodispersity and well-defined spherical shapes, indicating a more controlled synthesis environment34,35. In flower extracts, nanoparticles typically range in size from 20 to 30 nm or more. This variability in size can be attributed to the different phytochemicals present in the extracts, which can lead to a less uniform reduction of silver ions35,36. The TEM analysis may show a variety of shapes, including aggregates, indicating that the synthesis process may not be as efficient or that certain compounds in the flower extract promote agglomeration35,36. Both extracts typically produce silver nanoparticles with a face-centered cubic (fcc) crystalline structure, as confirmed by selected area diffraction patterns observed in TEM studies. However, the degree of crystallinity may vary slightly depending on the type of extract used. Notably, leaf-derived nanoparticles often exhibit sharper diffraction peaks, indicating better crystallinity37,38.

This research found chlorine in silver nanoparticles (AgNPs) synthesized using water. It is proposed that by employing one or more of the techniques listed below, the chloride content in AgNPs synthesized with regular water can be significantly reduced, resulting in a more desirable and pure nanoparticle product suitable for various applications. Using ultrapure water instead of regular deionized water can significantly lower the chloride content in the final AgNP product. Studies have shown that AgNPs synthesized through pulsed laser ablation in ultrapure water exhibit negligible chloride contamination compared to those produced in regular deionized water39. Dialysis or ultrafiltration can effectively remove chloride ions from AgNP colloids after synthesis. These techniques facilitate the selective removal of small ions while preserving the nanoparticles. For instance, dialysis has been used to purify AgNPs and lower chloride levels before conducting toxicity testing40. Passing the AgNP colloid through anion exchange resins effectively removes chloride ions. The resins selectively bind to these ions, facilitating the collection of purified AgNPs41. Adding silver nitrate (AgNO3) to the AgNP colloid can cause excess chloride to precipitate as silver chloride (AgCl). The AgCl can subsequently be removed through centrifugation or filtration, resulting in purified AgNPs42,43.

Research indicates that nanosilver can upregulate MLH1 gene expression. For example, a study found that nanosilver treatment resulted in higher levels of MLH1 in certain cancer contexts, suggesting it may play a therapeutic role in enhancing DNA repair mechanisms in tumors with mismatch repair deficiencies7,45. Nanosilver has been shown to boost the expression of genes involved in the mismatch repair (MMR) pathway, such as MLH1. This upregulation can enhance cellular responses to DNA damage, potentially leading to improved outcomes in cancer therapies7. Nanosilver interacts with immune cells, which can modulate inflammatory responses and may indirectly influence the expression of MMR genes. For instance, the activation of Toll-like receptors (TLRs) has been linked to increased expression of MLH1 in specific contexts8. Nanosilver can induce oxidative stress, which may activate cellular repair mechanisms, including those involving MLH1. This oxidative environment can stimulate signaling pathways that increase the expression of genes responsible for DNA repair46.

Conclusion

The present study showed that the synthesis of silver nanoparticles by the M. olifera plant is one of the suitable options for the synthesis of silver nanoparticles due to its anti-cancer therapeutic properties. The results of this research showed that silver nanoparticles synthesized by the M. olifera plant can improve MLH1 gene expression and DNA repair. As a result, the amount of apoptosis and anti-cancer properties in cells increases. Both leaf and flower extracts of Moringa oleifera can effectively synthesize silver nanoparticles; however, the leaf extract typically produces smaller, more uniform particles than the flower extract. This variation is primarily attributed to differences in phytochemical composition and their roles in the reduction and stabilization processes during nanoparticle formation. Characterization through TEM is essential for understanding these differences and optimizing synthesis methods for specific applications. In general, further studies on nanoparticles synthesized using this plant can be considered as one of the efficient methods to achieve appropriate treatment goals for cancer patients. Therefore, the present research can provide valuable insight into the development of nanotherapeutics to find an effective and non-toxic way to treat cancer.

A consistency-stability approach to scaling limits of zero-range processes
Daniel Marahrens, Angeliki Menegaki, Clément Mouhot

Abstract
. We propose a simple quantitative method for studying the hydrodynamic limit of interacting particle systems on lattices. It is applied to the diffusive scaling of the symmetric Zero-Range Process (in dimensions one and two). The rate of convergence is estimated in a Monge-Kantorovich distance asymptotic to the L 1 L^{1} stability estimate of Krüzkhov, as well as in relative entropy; and it is uniform in time. The method avoids the use of the so-called “block estimates”. It is based on a modulated Monge-Kantorovich distance estimate and microscopic stability properties.



1.
Introduction

We consider the hydrodynamic limit of interacting particle systems on a lattice. The general problem is to show that under an appropriate scaling of time and space, the local particle density of a stochastic lattice gas converges to the solution to a macroscopic partial differential equation. We present the method at an abstract level and then we apply it to a prototypical concrete model, the so-called Zero-Range Process (ZRP).

1.1.
State of the art

Hydrodynamic limits are known at a qualitative level under both hyperbolic and parabolic scalings for several stochastic models, including the simple-exclusion processes, exclusion processes with degenerate rates, the zero-range processes, as well as spin models such as the Ginzburg-Landau process with convex potential or with Kawasaki dynamics, see
[
GPV88
,
Yau91
,
Rez91
,
KL99
,
GLT09
]
and references threrein. These proofs rely on entropy estimates, either with respect to the global invariant mesure as in
entropy methods
, or with respect to the local Gibbs measure as in
relative entropy methods
introduced for such models by
[
Yau91
]
. However finding quantitative error estimates has been an area of active research for several decades, and has remained opened in most cases. Such quantitative error estimates are also connected to the related issue of the long-time behaviour of this hydrodynamic limit.

Let us discuss the existing results of quantitative hydrodynamic limit. For the
d
-dimensional simple exclusion process with weak asymmetry, for all
d\geq 1
, the optimal rate was obtained in
[
JM18
]
, together with the fluctuation limit, by refining the relative entropy method and without relying on logarithmic Sobolev inequalities. This approach has not been applied to truly nonlinear process, i.e. with nonlinear jump rates, such as the zero-range process, to our knowledge. Quantitative fluctuations were recently obtained in
[
GV24
]
for the symmetric simple exclusion process, with the optimal convergence rate. We also note the recent results on quantitative hydrodynamical limits via homogenisation techniques for non-gradient interacting particle systems, first in
[
GGM22
]
and more recently in
[
FGW24
]
. Regarding spin processes, the article
[
CY92
]
considered the
1
-dimensional convex Ginzburg-Landau process and obtained the optimal rate, via a modulated
H^{-1}
estimate. In the non-convex case, the only quantitative result so far seems to be the nice recent paper
[
DMOW
]
which obtains a quantitative polynomial rate in dimension one by building upon previous ideas of
[
GOVW09
]
: it relies on establishing a uniform logarithmic Sobolev inequalities and the introduction of an intermediate mesoscopic; it seems to be able to capture the optimal convergence rate.

The relative entropy method in
[
Yau91
]
can be made quantitative, provided that the
block estimates
it relies on are also made quantitative: it is feasible and interesting in its own right, and we will present it in a separate forthcoming work
[
MMM24
]
. Note that all these works assume and rely on the regularity of the limit solution. This excludes lattice models who limit solution present interesting degenerate solutions, such as shocks, for conservation laws, or compactly supported profiles, for porous medium equation. Our goal is to apply the new method presented here to the question of dealing with such degenerate limit solutions in the future.

In this article, we consider reversible processes and the diffusive (parabolic) scaling. We propose a systematic short method for proving the hydrodynamic limit for symmetric and attractive processes with quantitative rate, in weak distance and in relative entropy. Our method relies on a consistency-stability approach, based on, roughly, three key properties: macroscopic and microscopic stability estimates in appropriate topologies, and consistency estimates comparing the microscopic and the macroscopic semigroups. This approach thus differs in spirit with the typical techniques and remarkably, it avoids the use of the block estimates (see
[
KL99
, Chapter 5]
). We establish microscopic stability estimates reminiscent of Kruzhkov-type stability estimates on the limit equation and inspired from
[
Rez91
]
. We also derive higher-regularity microscopic stability estimates, referred to as ”jump distance estimates”, during the proof of the consistency error. We take advantage of the relaxation of the macroscopic dynamics to prove that our estimates are uniform in time, under parabolic scaling.
We use a Poincaré inequality in the consistency estimate, and we do not require the validity of a logarithmic Sobolev inequality.
To our knowledge, this is the first quantitative result for the Zero-Range Process when it is truly nonlinear, i.e. with nonlinear jump rate. The hydrodynamic limit for degenerate nonlinear diffusion equation (see
[
GLT09
]
and references therein) is currently under investigation where our method seems to yield first promising quantitative results.

2.
The general method

2.1.
Set up and notation

We denote by
\mathsf{X}\subset\mathbb{R}
the state space at a given site
(number of particles, spin, etc.).
Consider the discrete
torus
\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}:=\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}^{d}
and the corresponding
phase space of particle configurations
\mathsf{X}_{N}:=\mathsf{X}^{\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}
. Variables
x,y,z\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
are called

microscopic
, whereas variables in the continuous unit
torus
u,v,w\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
are called
macroscopic
; finally
particle
configurations
are denoted by

\eta,\zeta\in\mathsf{X}_{N}
. We embed
\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}
into
\mathbb{T}^{d}
by the map

x\mapsto N^{-1}x
, which means that the distance between sites
of the lattice scales like
N^{-1}
. Given a particle
configuration
\eta=(\eta_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\in\mathsf{X}_{N}
, we
define the
empirical measure

(2.1)

\alpha_{\eta}^{N}:=N^{-d}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\eta_{x}\delta_{\frac{x}{N}}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}^{d})

where
\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
is the space of positive Radon
measures on the torus.

At the microscopic level, the interacting particle system evolves
through a stochastic process and the time-dependent probability
measure describing the law of
\eta
is denoted by

\mu_{t}^{N}\in P(\mathsf{X}_{N})
, where
P(\mathsf{X}_{N})
denotes the space of
probability measures on
\mathsf{X}_{N}
. We consider a
linear

operator
\mathcal{L}_{N}:C_{b}(\mathsf{X}_{N})\rightarrow C_{b}(\mathsf{X}_{N})
generating a
unique Feller semigroup
e^{t\mathcal{L}_{N}}
on
P(\mathsf{X}_{N})

(see
[
Lig85
, Chapter 1]
for the definitions) so that
given
\mu_{0}^{N}\in P(\mathsf{X}_{N})
the law

\mu_{t}^{N}=e^{t\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\dagger}}\mu^{N}_{0}\in P(\mathsf{X}_{N})
, with

\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\dagger}
the adjoint acting on
P(\mathsf{X}_{N})
, satisfies

(2.2)

\forall\,\Phi\in C_{b}(\mathsf{X}_{N}),\quad\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\langle\Phi,\mu^{N}_{t}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{N}\Phi,\mu^{N}_{t}\rangle,

where
C_{b}(\mathsf{X}_{N})
denotes continuous bounded real-valued
functions over
\mathsf{X}_{N}
, and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

denotes the duality bracket between
C_{b}(\mathsf{X}_{N})
and

P(\mathsf{X}_{N})
.

At the macroscopic level, we consider a map (possibly depending
on
N
)

\mathbf{L}_{\infty}:\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),

and in general
unbounded
and
nonlinear
, and the
evolution problem on
f(t,u)=f_{t}(u)
:

(2.3)

\partial_{t}f_{t}=\mathbf{L}_{\infty}f_{t},\quad f_{t=0}=f_{0}.

A measure
\mu^{N}_{\infty}\in P(\mathsf{X}_{N})
is called
invariant

for (
2.2
) if

\forall\,\Phi\in C_{b}(\mathsf{X}_{N}),\quad\big\langle\mathcal{L}_{N}\Phi,\mu^{N}_{\infty}\big\rangle=0.

We consider a class of models where the equilibrium has the
following
product structure
. For any

\lambda\in\text{Conv}(\mathsf{X})
, where

\text{Conv}(\mathsf{X})\subset\mathbb{R}
is the convex hull of
\mathsf{X}
, there
is a one-site marginal probability measure

n_{\lambda}:\text{Conv}(\mathsf{X})\to\mathbb{R}_{+}\quad\text{ and }\quad\sigma:\text{Conv}(\mathsf{X})\to\text{Conv}(\mathsf{X})

so that for all
\lambda\in\text{Conv}(\mathsf{X})

(2.4)

\mu^{N}_{\infty,\lambda}:=n_{\lambda}^{\otimes\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}},\quad\text{i.e.}\quad\mu^{N}_{\infty,\lambda}(\eta)=\prod_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}n_{\lambda}(\eta_{x}),

is an invariant measure on
\mathsf{X}_{N}
, and for any

\rho\in\text{Conv}(\mathsf{X})
,

\mathbb{E}_{n_{\sigma(\rho)}}[\eta_{x}]=\rho
. We then define,
given a macroscopic profile
f:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\text{Conv}(\mathsf{X})
, the

local equilibrium measure

(2.5)

\vartheta^{N}_{f}(\eta):=\prod_{x\in\mathbb{G}_{N}}n_{\sigma\left(f_{t}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)}(\eta_{x}).

At the macroscopic level, the counterpart of

\mu^{N}_{\infty,\lambda}
are the constant functions

f\equiv\sigma^{-1}(\lambda)
, which are stationary solutions
to (
2.3
). Any equilibrium measure allows the
define the adjoint
\mathcal{L}_{N}^{*}
in

L^{2}({\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\infty}})
, acting on probability
densities of the form

G^{N}_{f}:={\rm d}\vartheta_{f}^{N}/{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}
.

The two maps
\eta\mapsto\alpha_{\eta}^{N}
and

f\mapsto\vartheta^{N}_{f}
connect the functional spaces of the
microscopic and macroscopic evolutions, as summarized in
Figure
1
. The diagram only closes in the limit

N\to\infty
when the empirical measure becomes asymptotically
atomic, i.e. when correlations remain low and
molecular
chaos
holds.

Figure 1.
The functional setting. The downward right arrow
simply embeds the atomic elements

\delta_{f}\in P(\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
for some

f\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
into
\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
by the
mapping
\delta_{f}\mapsto f
.

In order to compare the two dynamics, we also define the adjoint
of
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
the operator accounting for the time evolution of

G^{N}_{t}:={\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{t}}^{N}/{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}
, where
f_{t}
evolves through

\mathbf{L}_{\infty}
:

(2.6)

\mathcal{L}_{\infty}^{*}G^{N}_{f}:=\left(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\frac{\sigma^{\prime}\left(f\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)\mathbf{L}_{\infty}f\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)}{n_{\sigma\left(f\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)}(\eta_{x})}\Big[\partial_{\lambda}n_{\lambda}(\eta_{x})\Big]_{\big|\lambda=\sigma\left(f\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)}\right)G_{f}^{N}.

We denote by
\operatorname{Lip}(\mathsf{X}_{N})
the set of Lipschitz functions

\Phi:\mathsf{X}_{N}\to\mathbb{R}
with respect to the normalised
\ell^{1}

norm, and we denote the corresponding semi-norm

[\Phi]_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathsf{X}_{N})}:=\inf_{\zeta,\eta\in\mathsf{X}_{N}}\frac{|\Phi(\eta)-\Phi(\zeta)|}{\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}}|\eta_{x}-\zeta_{x}|}.

By duality we obtain the following Monge-Kantorovich distance over

P(\mathsf{X}_{N})

\forall\,\mu^{N}_{1},\mu^{N}_{2}\in P(\mathsf{X}_{N}),\quad\left\|\mu^{N}_{1}-\mu^{N}_{2}\right\|_{\operatorname{Lip}^{*}}=\sup_{\Phi\in\operatorname{Lip}(\mathsf{X}_{N})^{*}}\frac{\left\langle\mu^{N}_{1}-\mu^{N}_{2},\Phi\right\rangle}{[\Phi]_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathsf{X}_{N})}}.

where
\operatorname{Lip}(\mathsf{X}_{N})^{*}
is the set of non-constant Lipschitz
functions.

Finally given
\mu,\nu\in P(X_{N})
we define

(2.7)

H^{N}(\mu|\nu):=\frac{1}{N^{d}}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\log\big(\textstyle\frac{{\rm d}\mu}{{\rm d}\nu}\big)\,{\rm d}\mu\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\cap\{+\infty\}

when
\mu
has a Radon-Nikodym derivative with respect to
\nu
,
or simply
+\infty
otherwise.

2.2.
Abstract assumptions

We make the following assumptions
on (
2.2
)-(
2.3
):

(H1) Microscopic stability.
The microscopic evolution
semigroup
e^{t\mathcal{L}_{N}}
satisfies

(2.8)

\displaystyle\forall\,\Phi\in\operatorname{Lip}(\mathsf{X}_{N}),\ \forall\,t\geq 0,\quad\left[e^{t\mathcal{L}_{N}}\Phi\right]_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathsf{X}_{N})}\leq C_{S}\left[\Phi\right]_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathsf{X}_{N})}

for some stability constant
C_{S}\geq 1
(independent of time).

(H2) Macroscopic stability.
There is a Banach space

\mathfrak{B}\subset\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{G}_{\infty})
so that (
2.3
) is
locally well-posed in
\mathfrak{B}
. Moreover, given an initial condition

f_{0}\in\mathfrak{B}
, we write
f_{t}\in\mathfrak{B}
the unique solution on its
maximal time interval of existence
[0,T_{m})
,
f_{\infty}
the
stationary constant solution with the same mass as
f_{0}
,

G^{N}_{t}:={\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{t}^{N}}^{N}/{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}
, and

(2.9)

\displaystyle\forall\,t\in[0,T_{m}),\quad\mathsf{R}_{1}(t):=\left\|f_{t}-f_{\infty}\right\|_{\mathfrak{B}}

(2.10)

\displaystyle\forall\,t\in[0,T_{m}),\quad\mathsf{R}_{2}(t):=\left[\frac{\ln G^{N}_{t}}{N^{d}}\right]_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathsf{X}_{N})}+\left[\frac{\left[\mathcal{L}_{N}+\mathcal{L}_{N}^{*}\right]\sqrt{G^{N}_{t}}}{N^{d}\sqrt{G^{N}_{t}}}\right]_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathsf{X}_{N})}.

(H3) Monge-Kantorovich Consistency.
There is a consistency
error
\epsilon_{L}(N)\geq 0
so that, given any initial condition

f_{0}\in\mathfrak{B}
, on the maximal time of existence
t\in[0,T_{m})

of its corresponding solution
f_{t}\in\mathfrak{B}
, one has for any

\Phi\in\operatorname{Lip}(\mathsf{X}_{N})
with
[\Phi]_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathsf{X}_{N})}\leq 1
:

(2.11)

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\left(e^{(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}_{N}}\Phi\right)\left[\mathcal{L}_{N}^{*}G^{N}_{\tau}-\mathcal{L}_{\infty}^{*}G^{N}_{\tau}\right]\,{\rm d}\vartheta^{N}_{f_{\infty}}\,{\rm d}\tau\leq\epsilon_{L}(N)\int_{0}^{t}\mathsf{R}_{1}(\tau)\,{\rm d}\tau.

(H4) Entropic Consistency.
There is a consistency error

\epsilon_{NL}(N)\geq 0
so that, given any initial condition

f_{0}\in\mathfrak{B}
, on the maximal time of existence
t\in[0,T_{m})

of its corresponding solution
f_{t}\in\mathfrak{B}
, one has

\displaystyle H^{N}\left(\vartheta_{f_{0}^{N}}^{N}|\vartheta^{N}_{f_{\infty}}\right)-H^{N}\left(\vartheta_{f_{t}^{N}}^{N}|\vartheta^{N}_{f_{\infty}}\right)+\frac{2}{N^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\sqrt{G^{N}_{\tau}}\mathcal{L}_{N}\left(\sqrt{G^{N}_{\tau}}\right)\,{\rm d}\vartheta^{N}_{f_{\infty}}\,{\rm d}\tau

(2.12)

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon_{NL}(N)\int_{0}^{t}\mathsf{R}_{1}(\tau)\,{\rm d}\tau.

2.3.
The abstract approach

Theorem 2.1

(The abstract result)
.

Consider an interacting particle
system (
2.2
)-(
2.3
) with a
product invariant measure as described, and satisfying
(H1)-(H2)-(H3)
. Let
\mu_{0}^{N}\in P_{1}(X_{N})
for
N\geq 1
and

f_{0}^{N}\in\mathcal{B}
. Let
f_{t}^{N}\in\mathfrak{B}
the unique
solution to (
2.3
) on the time of existence

[0,T_{m})
in
(H2)
. Given
T\in[0,T_{m})
, one has

(2.13)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\mu_{t}^{N}-\vartheta_{f_{t}^{N}}^{N}\right\|_{\operatorname{Lip}^{*}}\leq C_{S}\left\|\mu_{0}^{N}-\vartheta_{f_{0}^{N}}^{N}\right\|_{\operatorname{Lip}^{*}}+\epsilon_{L}(N)\int_{0}^{T}\mathsf{R}_{1}(t)\,{\rm d}t.

If the interacting particle system additionally satisfies
(H4)
then

(2.14)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}H^{N}\left(\mu_{t}^{N}|\vartheta_{f_{t}^{N}}^{N}\right)\leq H^{N}\left(\mu_{0}^{N}|\vartheta_{f_{0}^{N}}^{N}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\epsilon_{L}(N)\mathsf{R}_{2}(0)+2\epsilon_{L}(N)\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathsf{R}_{2}(t)\right)\int_{0}^{T}\mathsf{R}_{1}(t)\,{\rm d}t+\epsilon_{NL}(N)\int_{0}^{T}\mathsf{R}_{1}(t)\,{\rm d}t.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

The proof is short and we present it immediately in order to
provide the reader with a clear picture of the strategy. The
rest of the paper is devoted to proving the assumptions on our
particular model. Denote by

F^{N}_{t}:={\rm d}\mu^{N}_{t}/{\rm d}\vartheta^{N}_{f_{\infty}}
and

G^{N}_{t}:={\rm d}\vartheta^{N}_{f_{t}^{N}}/{\rm d}\vartheta^{N}_{f_{\infty}}
the densities with respect to the
equilibrium measure
\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}
.

In order to prove (
2.13
), we write

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Big(F^{N}_{t}-G_{t}^{N}\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{N}^{*}\Big(F^{N}_{t}-G_{t}^{N}\Big)+\left(\mathcal{L}_{N}^{*}G_{t}^{N}-\partial_{t}G_{t}^{N}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{N}^{*}\Big(F^{N}_{t}-G_{t}^{N}\Big)+\left(\mathcal{L}_{N}^{*}G_{t}^{N}-\mathcal{L}_{\infty}^{*}G_{t}^{N}\right)

so that Duhamel’s formula yields

\displaystyle F^{N}_{t}-G_{t}^{N}=e^{t\mathcal{L}_{N}^{*}}\left(F^{N}_{0}-G_{0}^{N}\right)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}_{N}^{*}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{N}^{*}G_{\tau}^{N}-\mathcal{L}_{\infty}^{*}G_{\tau}^{N}\right)\,{\rm d}\tau.

Take
\Phi\in\operatorname{Lip}(\mathsf{X}_{N})
with

[\Phi]_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathsf{X}_{N})}\leq 1
and integrate the
above equation to get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\Phi\Big(F^{N}_{t}-G_{t}^{N}\Big)\,{\rm d}\vartheta^{N}_{f_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\left(e^{t\mathcal{L}_{N}}\Phi\right)\Big(F^{N}_{0}-G_{0}^{N}\Big)\,{\rm d}\vartheta^{N}_{f_{\infty}}}_{I_{1}(t)}+\underbrace{\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\int_{0}^{t}\left(e^{(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}_{N}}\Phi\right)\left(\mathcal{L}_{N}G_{\tau}^{N}-\mathcal{L}_{\infty}G_{\tau}^{N}\right)\,{\rm d}\vartheta^{N}_{f_{\infty}}\,{\rm d}\tau}_{I_{2}(t)}.

The assumption
(H1)
then implies

I_{1}(t)\leq C_{S}\|\mu^{N}_{0}-\vartheta_{f_{0}}^{N}\|_{\operatorname{Lip}^{*}}
and
the assumption
(H3)
implies

I_{2}(t)\leq\epsilon_{L}(N)\int_{0}^{t}R(\tau)\,{\rm d}\tau
which
proves (
2.13
).

To prove the second
inequality (
2.14
), i.e. convergence in relative entropy, we write

\displaystyle H^{N}\left(\mu_{t}^{N}|\vartheta_{f_{t}^{N}}^{N}\right)

\displaystyle=H^{N}\left(\mu_{t}^{N}|\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}\right)-\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\frac{\ln G^{N}_{t}}{N^{d}}F^{N}_{t}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}.

We then use (
2.13
), i.e. the hydrodynamic limit in dual Lipschitz distance, to estimate the second term of the right-hand side. The Lipschitz function needed for our purposes is given by
\frac{\ln G^{N}_{t}}{N^{d}}
and the fact that this is indeed Lipschitz is shown in Section
6
. This yields

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\frac{\ln G^{N}_{t}}{N^{d}}\,{\rm d}\mu^{N}_{t}\leq-\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\frac{\ln G^{N}_{t}}{N^{d}}G^{N}_{t}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}+\epsilon_{L}(N)\mathsf{R}_{2}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\mathsf{R}_{1}(\tau)\,{\rm d}\tau.

We deduce

(2.15)

\displaystyle H^{N}\left(\mu_{t}^{N}|\vartheta_{f_{t}^{N}}^{N}\right)

\displaystyle\leq H^{N}\left(\mu_{t}^{N}|\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}\right)-H^{N}\left(\vartheta_{f_{t}^{N}}^{N}|\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}\right)+\epsilon_{L}(N)\mathsf{R}_{2}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\mathsf{R}_{1}(\tau)\,{\rm d}\tau.

We now connect the entropies at time
t
on the right-hand side
to the initial entropies using respectively the evolution
equation of the particle system and the entropic
consistency
(H4)
, (
2.12
):

(2.16)

\displaystyle H^{N}\left(\mu_{t}^{N}|\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}\right)=H^{N}\left(\mu_{0}^{N}|\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}\right)+\frac{1}{N^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}F^{N}_{\tau}\mathcal{L}_{N}\left(\ln F^{N}_{\tau}\right)\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}\,{\rm d}\tau

(2.17)

\displaystyle-H^{N}\left(\vartheta_{f_{t}^{N}}^{N}|\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}\right)\leq-H^{N}\left(\vartheta_{f_{0}}^{N}|\vartheta^{N}_{f_{\infty}}\right)-\frac{2}{N^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\sqrt{G^{N}_{\tau}}\mathcal{L}_{N}\left(\sqrt{G^{N}_{\tau}}\right)\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt+\epsilon_{NL}(N)\int_{0}^{t}\mathsf{R}_{1}(\tau)\,{\rm d}\tau.

We then note:

(1)

Whenever
\mathcal{L}_{N}
is the
generator of a Markov process, we have that

\displaystyle-2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\sqrt{F^{N}_{\tau}}\mathcal{L}_{N}\left(\sqrt{F^{N}_{\tau}}\right)\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}\,{\rm d}\tau\leq-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}F^{N}_{\tau}\mathcal{L}_{N}\ln F^{N}_{\tau}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}\,{\rm d}\tau,

(2)

and also

(2.18)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\frac{\left[\mathcal{L}_{N}+\mathcal{L}_{N}^{*}\right]\left(\sqrt{G^{N}_{\tau}}\right)}{\sqrt{G^{N}_{\tau}}}F^{N}_{\tau}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}\,{\rm d}\tau\leq-2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\sqrt{F^{N}_{\tau}}\mathcal{L}_{N}\left(\sqrt{F^{N}_{\tau}}\right)\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}\,{\rm d}\tau.

The point (1) follows from the inequality

(\ln b-\ln a)a\leq 2(\sqrt{b}-\sqrt{a})\sqrt{a}
, that holds for all

a,b>0
.
The point (2), is seen as follows:
If
P_{t}:=\exp(t[\mathcal{L}_{N}+\mathcal{L}_{N}^{*}])
, then the transition
kernel of this semigroup satisfies

p_{t}(\eta,\zeta)=p_{t}(\zeta,\eta)
and for any
F,G>0
(the
non-negative case follows by approximation)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\frac{P_{t}\left(\sqrt{G}\right)}{\sqrt{G}}F\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}\times\mathsf{X}_{N}}p_{t}(\eta,\zeta)\left(\frac{\sqrt{G(\zeta)F(\eta)}}{\sqrt{G(\eta)F(\zeta)}}+\frac{\sqrt{G(\eta)F(\zeta)}}{\sqrt{G(\zeta)F(\eta)}}\right)\sqrt{F(\eta)}\sqrt{F(\zeta)}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}(\eta)\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}(\zeta)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}\times\mathsf{X}_{N}}p_{t}(\eta,\zeta)\sqrt{F(\eta)}\sqrt{F(\zeta)}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}(\eta)\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}(\zeta)=\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\left(P_{t}\sqrt{F}\right)\sqrt{F}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}.

Since both quantities agree at
t=0
by differentiating at

t=0
we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\sqrt{F^{N}_{\tau}}\left[\mathcal{L}_{N}+\mathcal{L}_{N}^{*}\right]\left(\sqrt{F^{N}_{\tau}}\right)\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}\leq\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\frac{\left[\mathcal{L}_{N}+\mathcal{L}_{N}^{*}\right]\left(\sqrt{G^{N}_{\tau}}\right)}{\sqrt{G^{N}_{\tau}}}F^{N}_{\tau}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}.

This implies indeed (
2.18
) using the symmetry of the
left-hand side.

We now apply the hydrodynamic limit in the dual-Lipschitz
norm, in (
2.13
), to the left-hand side
of (
2.18
).
The Lipschitz function needed for our purposes is

(G^{N}_{\tau})^{-1/2}\left[\mathcal{L}_{N}+\mathcal{L}_{N}^{*}\right]\left(\sqrt{G^{N}_{\tau}}\right)

3.
The Zero-Range Process

The state space is
\mathsf{X}=\mathbb{N}
, meaning that the number of
particles is not limited at each site. Given a
transition
function

p\in P(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})
with
p(0)=0
and bounded second moment, and a
jump rate
function

g:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
, the base generator

\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{N}
writes

(3.1)

\forall\,\Phi\in C_{b}(\mathsf{X}_{N}),\ \forall\,\eta\in\mathsf{X}_{N},\quad\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{N}\Phi(\eta):=\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}p(y-x)g(\eta_{x})\left[\Phi(\eta^{xy})-\Phi(\eta)\right]

where
\eta^{xy}
is defined by

(3.2)

\eta^{xy}_{z}=\begin{cases}\eta_{z}\quad\text{ if }z\not\in\{x,y\}\\
\eta_{x}-1\quad\text{ if }z=x\\
\eta_{y}+1\quad\text{ if }z=y.\end{cases}

The local
equilibrium structure is given by

(3.3)

\displaystyle n_{\lambda}(k):=\frac{\lambda^{k}}{g(k)!Z(\lambda)}\quad\text{ with }\quad Z(\lambda):=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{g(k)!}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\sigma\ \text{ is defined implicitely by }\ \sigma(\rho)\frac{Z^{\prime}(\sigma(\rho))}{Z(\sigma(\rho))}\equiv\rho

with the notation
g(k)!:=g(k)g(k-1)\cdots g(1)
and

g(0)!=1
. The pair
(n_{\lambda},\sigma)
satisfies

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{n_{\sigma(\rho)}}[k]=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{k\sigma(\rho)^{k}}{g(k)!Z(\sigma(\rho))}=\rho,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{n_{\sigma(\rho)}}[g(k)]=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\sigma(\rho)^{k}}{g(k-1)!Z(\sigma(\rho))}=\sigma(\rho).\end{cases}

When
f\equiv\rho\in[0,+\infty)
is constant, the local
equilibrium measure

\vartheta_{\rho}^{N}=\mu_{\infty,\sigma(\rho)}^{N}

in (
2.4
)-(
2.5
) is invariant with
average number of particles
\rho
. The
mean
transition rate
is defined by

(3.5)

\gamma:=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}xp(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

When

\gamma\not=0
, the first non-trivial scaling limit is the
hyperbolic scaling
\mathcal{L}_{N}:=N\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{N}
, and the expected
limit equation is

(3.6)

\partial_{t}f=\gamma\cdot\nabla[\sigma(f)]\quad\text{
with $\gamma$ defined in~\eqref{eq:transition}.}

We assume in this paper that
\gamma=0
, then the first
non-trivial scaling limit is the parabolic scaling

\mathcal{L}_{N}:=N^{2}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{N}
, and the expected limit equation is

(3.7)

\partial_{t}f=\mathtt{A}:\nabla^{2}\sigma(f)\quad\text{ with }\quad\mathtt{A}=\left[(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{d}\right]\quad\text{ and }\quad a_{ij}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}p(x)x_{i}x_{j}.

The main result is:

4.
Stability properties

4.1.
Microscopic stability on average

We use the standard coupling as in
[
Lig85
]
. Let us define the following coupling operator

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}\Psi(\eta,\zeta):=

\displaystyle\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}p(y-x)\Big(g(\eta_{x})\wedge g(\zeta_{x})\Big)\Big[\Psi(\eta^{xy},\zeta^{xy})-\Psi(\eta,\zeta)\Big]

\displaystyle+\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}p(y-x)\Big(g(\eta_{x})-g(\eta_{x})\wedge g(\zeta_{x})\Big)\Big[\Psi(\eta^{xy},\zeta)-\Psi(\eta,\zeta)\Big]

(4.1)

\displaystyle+\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}p(y-x)\Big(g(\zeta_{x})-g(\eta_{x})\wedge g(\zeta_{x})\Big)\Big[\Psi(\eta,\zeta^{xy})-\Psi(\eta,\zeta)\Big]

for a two-variable test function
\Psi(\eta,\zeta)
. Then we have
the following three properties:

(P1)

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}\Phi(\eta)=\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{N}\Phi(\eta)
and

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}\Phi(\zeta)=\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{N}\Phi(\zeta)
for a function
\Phi
of only one variable.

(P2)

The semigroup
e^{t\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}}

preserves the sign (like all generators of Markov processes).

(P3)

The coupling operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}
satisfies the inequality

(4.2)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}\left(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}}|\eta_{z}-\zeta_{z}|\right)\leq 0.

(P1)
and
(P2)
are trivial, and we prove
(P3)
below. Let us first explain how to deduce the stability from

(P1)
-
(P2)
-
(P3)
.
(P1)
implies that

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}(\eta)-\Phi_{t}(\zeta)=e^{t\mathcal{L}_{N}}\Phi_{0}(\eta)-e^{t\mathcal{L}_{N}}\Phi_{0}(\zeta)=e^{t\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}}\Psi_{0}(\eta,\zeta)=:\Psi_{t}(\eta,\zeta)

with
\Psi_{0}(\eta,\zeta):=\Phi_{0}(\eta)-\Phi_{0}(\zeta)
. If we
assume that the Lipschitz bound holds initially for some constant

C\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle-\frac{C}{N^{d}}\left(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}}|\eta_{z}-\zeta_{z}|\right)\leq\Psi_{0}(\eta,\zeta)\leq\frac{C}{N^{d}}\left(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}}|\eta_{z}-\zeta_{z}|\right)

(P2)
then implies

\displaystyle-\frac{C}{N^{d}}e^{t\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}}\left(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}}|\eta_{z}-\zeta_{z}|\right)\leq\Psi_{t}(\eta,\zeta)\leq\frac{C}{N^{d}}e^{t\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}}\left(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}}|\eta_{z}-\zeta_{z}|\right)

and finally
(P3)
implies

\displaystyle e^{t\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}}\left(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}}|\eta_{z}-\zeta_{z}|\right)\leq\left(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}}|\eta_{z}-\zeta_{z}|\right)

which concludes the proof of (
2.8
) with
C_{S}=1
. To prove the crucial property
(P3)
(equation (
4.2
)), we compute

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}\left(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}|\eta_{z}-\zeta_{z}|\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}p(y-x)\Big(g(\eta_{x})-g(\eta_{x})\wedge g(\zeta_{x})\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times\Big[|\eta^{xy}_{x}-\zeta_{x}|+|\eta^{xy}_{y}-\zeta_{y}|-|\eta_{x}-\zeta_{x}|-|\eta_{y}-\zeta_{y}|\Big]

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}p(y-x)\Big(g(\zeta_{x})-g(\eta_{x})\wedge g(\zeta_{x})\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times\Big[|\eta_{x}-\zeta^{xy}_{x}|+|\eta_{y}-\zeta^{xy}_{y}|-|\eta_{x}-\zeta_{x}|-|\eta_{y}-\zeta_{y}|\Big].

When
g(\eta_{x})-g(\eta_{x})\wedge g(\zeta_{x})>0
necessarily

\eta_{x}-\zeta_{x}\geq 1
and

\Big[|\eta^{xy}_{x}-\zeta_{x}|+|\eta^{xy}_{y}-\zeta_{y}|-|\eta_{x}-\zeta_{x}|-|\eta_{y}-\zeta_{y}|\Big]\leq 0.

When
g(\zeta_{x})-g(\eta_{x})\wedge g(\zeta_{x})>0
necessarily

\zeta_{x}-\eta_{x}\geq 1
and

\Big[|\eta_{x}-\zeta^{xy}_{x}|+|\eta_{y}-\zeta^{xy}_{y}|-|\eta_{x}-\zeta_{x}|-|\eta_{y}-\zeta_{y}|\Big]\leq 0.

This proves (
2.8
) with

.

Remark 2
.

The microscopic stability property is valid in any dimension

.

4.2.
Microscopic stability in support

In fact, with a minor modification of the previous argument, it is possible to strengthen the conclusion and obtain the stability at the level of the support. We record this property in the following lemma, which will prove useful in the consistency estimate.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\eta^{t}
and
\zeta^{t}
be two attractive zero-range processes (i.e. with non-decreasing jump rate
g
) coupled along the standard coupling, then

(4.3)

\|\eta^{t}-\zeta^{t}\|_{\ell^{1}}:=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\eta_{x}^{t}-\zeta_{x}^{t}\right|\leq\|\eta^{0}-\zeta^{0}\|_{\ell^{1}}:=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\eta_{x}^{0}-\zeta_{x}^{0}\right|.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Given
K>0
, whenever
g(\eta_{z})>g(\zeta_{z})
(which implies
\eta_{z}>\zeta_{z}
),

\displaystyle\Bigg[\left(\sum_{x\neq z,z^{\prime}}|\eta_{x}-\zeta_{x}|-K\right)+|\eta_{z}-\zeta_{z}-1|+|\eta_{z^{\prime}}-\zeta_{z^{\prime}}+1|\Bigg]-\left[\sum_{x}|\eta_{x}-\zeta_{x}|-K\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{x\neq z,z^{\prime}}|\eta_{x}-\zeta_{x}|-K\right)+|\eta_{z}-\zeta_{z}|-1+|\eta_{z^{\prime}}-\zeta_{z^{\prime}}|+1-\left[\sum_{x}|\eta_{x}-\zeta_{x}|-K\right]=0.

This implies

\left(\sum_{x}|\eta_{x}^{zz^{\prime}}-\zeta_{x}|-K\right)_{+}\leq\left(\sum_{x}|\eta_{x}-\zeta_{x}|-K\right)_{+}

and by summation

\sum_{zz^{\prime}}p(z^{\prime}-z)(g(\eta_{z})-g(\zeta_{z}))_{+}\left[\left(\sum_{x}|\eta_{x}^{zz^{\prime}}-\zeta_{x}|-K\right)_{+}-\left(\sum_{x}|\eta_{x}-\zeta_{x}|-K\right)_{+}\right]\leq 0.

A symmetric inequality holds whenever
g(\zeta_{z})>g(\eta_{z})
, and we conclude that

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\left(\left(\sum_{z}|\eta_{z}-\zeta_{z}|-K\right)_{+}\right)\leq 0

(the part of the coupling generator where jumps occur simultaneously does not contribute trivially). We deduce that the coupling measure
\Pi_{t}
satisfies

\frac{\,{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\left(\iint\left(\sum_{z}|\eta_{z}-\zeta_{z}|-K\right)_{+}\,{\rm d}\pi_{t}^{N}(\eta,\zeta)\right)\leq 0.

Choosing
K=\|\eta^{0}-\zeta^{0}\|_{\ell^{1}}
, we conclude
\operatorname{supp}(\pi_{t}^{N})\subset\{\|\eta-\zeta\|_{\ell^{1}}\leq K\}
for all times.
∎

4.3.
Macroscopic stability

In the parabolic scaling the limit PDE is the nonlinear diffusion equation (
3.7
). It is proved in
[
KL99
, Chapter 2, Section 3]
that our assumptions on
g
imply that
Z
defined in (
3.3
) has infinite radius of convergence, that
R(\lambda)=\lambda\partial_{\lambda}\ln(Z(\lambda))=\frac{1}{Z(\lambda)}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{n\lambda^{n}}{g(n)!}
is well-defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and strictly increasing, and its inverse
\sigma=R^{-1}
is smooth with all its derivatives uniformly bounded on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\sigma^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\subset[\Lambda,\Lambda^{-1}]
with
\Lambda\in(0,1)
. Hence by first applying the De Giorgi-Nash

estimate and second iterating Schauder estimates, we prove that an initial data in
C^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
gives rise to a global solution with uniform in time bounds in
L^{\infty}\cap C^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. The condition
f_{t}\geq\delta
is propagated in time by maximum principle. Finally
f_{t}\to\rho
exponentially fast as
t\to\infty
in
C^{\infty}
by parabolic regularization and
L^{2}
spectral gap.

Remark 3
.

This macroscopic stability is valid in any dimension
d\geq 1
.

\displaystyle A_{\tau,x}^{N}

\displaystyle:=N^{2}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}p(y-x)g(\eta_{x})\left(\frac{\sigma(f)_{\tau,y}}{\sigma(f)_{\tau,x}}-1\right)-\left(\eta_{x}-f_{\tau,x}\right)\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(f)_{\tau,x}}{\sigma(f)_{\tau,x}}\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(f)_{\tau,x}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{g(\eta_{x})}{\sigma\left(f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)}-\left(\eta_{x}-f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)\frac{\sigma^{\prime}\left(f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)}{\sigma\left(f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)}\right)\mathbf{L}_{\infty}(f_{\tau})\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)+g(\eta_{x})R_{\tau}^{N}(x)

with a remainder
R_{\tau}^{N}(x)
estimated as follows:

\displaystyle R_{\tau}^{N}(x)

\displaystyle:=\mathtt{A}:\nabla_{x}^{2}[\sigma(f_{\tau})]\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)-N^{2}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}p(y-x)\left[\sigma\left(f_{\tau}\left(\frac{y}{N}\right)\right)-\sigma\left(f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathtt{A}:\nabla_{x}^{2}[\sigma(f_{\tau})]\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)-N^{2}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}p(y-x)\Bigg[\nabla\left[\sigma\left(f_{\tau}\right)\right]\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\frac{y-x}{N}

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+\frac{1}{2N^{2}}\nabla^{2}\left[\sigma\left(f_{\tau}\right)\right]\left(\frac{x}{N}\right):(y-x)^{\otimes 2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\|\nabla f_{\tau}\|_{C^{2}}}{N^{3}}\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\|\nabla f_{\tau}\|_{C^{2}}}{N}\right)\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{e^{-C\tau}}{N}\right)

where we have used the identities

\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}p(y-x)(y-x)=\gamma=0,\qquad\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}p(y-x)(y-x)^{\otimes 2}=\mathtt{A}.

The conservation of mass implies

\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\left(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}A_{\tau,x}^{N}\right)G^{N}_{\tau}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}=\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\left(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}A_{\tau,x}^{N}\right)\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N}=0,

thus we may remove a constant: we remove the average of
\Phi_{t-\tau}:=e^{(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}_{N}}\Phi
with respect to the local equilibrium measure and replace
\Phi_{t-\tau}
by

\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}:=e^{(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}_{N}}\Phi-\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\left(e^{(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}_{N}}\Phi\right)\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N}=e^{(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}_{N}}\left[\Phi-\left(\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\Phi\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N}\right)\right].

This results in the estimate

\displaystyle I_{t}^{N}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\Phi_{t,\tau}(\eta)\left(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}A^{N}_{\tau,x}\right)\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}(\eta)\left(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}A^{N}_{\tau,x}\right)\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N}

(5.2)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}(\eta)\left(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\tilde{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}\right)\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)

where
\tilde{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}
is defined as follows (we have added
terms independent of
\eta
thanks to the zero average condition
on
\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}
with respect to the measure

{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}
):

\tilde{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}:=\left\{g(\eta_{x})-\sigma\left(f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)-\sigma^{\prime}\left(f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)\left[\eta_{x}-f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right]\right\}\frac{\mathbf{L}_{\infty}(f)\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)}{\sigma\left(f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)}.

Note that
\tilde{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}
now also has zero average against

{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N}
. We have used the estimate on

R^{N}_{\tau}(x)
and the Lipschitz bound

[\Phi_{t,\tau}]_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathsf{X}_{N})}\leq 1
(from the microscopic
stability) to get

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\int_{0}^{t}R^{N}_{\tau}(x)\left(\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}(\eta)g(\eta_{x})\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N}\right)\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim[\Phi_{t,\tau}]_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathsf{X}_{N})}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\mathsf{X}^{2}}R^{N}_{\tau}(x)\frac{|\eta_{x}-\zeta_{x}|}{N^{d}}g(\eta_{x})\,{\rm d}n_{f_{\tau,x}}^{2}(\eta_{x},\zeta_{x})\right)\lesssim\frac{1}{N}.

5.5.
Step 2. Introduction of an intermediate scale

We consider an intermediate scale
\ell<N
so that
\ell

divides
N
. This scale will be
chosen later in terms of
N
. We then form sub-sums over a
covering of
\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
by non-overlapping cubes of size

\ell\in\{1,\dots,N\}
. Let
\mathcal{R}^{\ell}_{N}\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
be the
net of centres of these non-overlapping cubes denoted

\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}:=\{y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}\ :\ \|x-y\|_{\infty}\leq\ell\}\quad\text{for}\quad x\in\mathcal{R}^{\ell}_{N}.

Then we rearrange (
5.2
) into

\displaystyle I_{t}^{N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{R}_{N}^{\ell}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}(\eta)\left(\sum_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\tilde{A}^{N}_{\tau,x^{\prime}}\right)\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)

\displaystyle=(2\ell+1)^{d}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{R}_{N}^{\ell}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}(\eta)\hat{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ell}{N}\right)

where
\hat{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}
is defined as follows (all the sums contained in this term are normalised):

\hat{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}:=\left\{\langle g(\eta)\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}-\sigma\left(f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)-\sigma^{\prime}\left(f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)\left[\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}-f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right]\right\}\frac{\mathbf{L}_{\infty}(f)\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)}{\sigma\left(f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)}

with the following notation for the local average

\langle F(\eta)\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}:=\frac{1}{(2\ell+1)^{d}}\sum_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}F\left(\eta_{x^{\prime}}\right)\quad\text{ for
}F=F(\eta_{x}).

Note that
\hat{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}
only depends on values of
\eta
in
the box
\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}
.
From now on
{\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}}
denotes the marginal projection onto the
sites in
\mathcal{C}
with respect to
\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}
:

(5.3)

\displaystyle{\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}:=\int_{\{\eta_{y}\in\mathsf{X},\;y\not\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}\}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}(\eta)\prod_{y\not\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\,{\rm d}n_{f_{\tau.y}}(\eta_{y}).

We have to estimate a new error term when replacing the local
average of
\tilde{A}
by
\hat{A}
. Since
\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}

has zero average, this error term reduces to

\mathcal{E}_{A}=\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\left[\sigma\left(f_{\tau}\left(\frac{y}{N}\right)\right)-\sigma\left(f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)\right]=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ell}{N}e^{-C\tau}\right)\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}

where the exponentially small in time term appears due to the decay of the Lipschitz norm of
f_{\tau}
. Then, we use the Lipschitz bound on
\Phi
to get

(5.4)

\left|{\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}\right|\leq N^{-d}\int_{\zeta\in\mathsf{X}^{\mathbb{T}^{d}_{\ell}}}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}|\eta_{z}-\zeta_{z}|\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N,\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}

where
\zeta
denotes the variables in
\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}
used in the average subtracted from
\Phi
in the definition of
\tilde{\Phi}
, and
\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N,\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}
denotes the restriction of the product measure
\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N}
to
\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}
. This yields

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathcal{R}_{N}^{\ell}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}(\eta)\mathcal{E}_{A}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\ell}{N^{d+1}}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{R}_{N}^{\ell}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{\ell}^{2}}\left(\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}|\eta_{z}^{(\ell)}-\zeta_{z}^{(\ell)}|\right)\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}}^{\ell}\,{\rm d}\left(\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N,\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\right)^{\otimes 2}\left(\eta^{(\ell)},\zeta^{(\ell)}\right)\,{\rm d}\tau\lesssim\frac{\ell}{N}e^{-Ct}

and
\eta^{(\ell)}
denotes a blind variable in
\mathsf{X}_{\ell}
and

\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N,\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}
denotes the restriction of
the product measure
\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N}
to
\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}
. Note
that the average of
\hat{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}
against

{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N}
is not zero anymore but similar
estimates can be used to prove it is small:

(5.5)

\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\hat{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ell}{N}e^{-C\tau}\right).

We then “flatten” the local equilibrium measure locally in each
\ell
-cube with error

\displaystyle I_{t}^{N}

\displaystyle=(2\ell+1)^{d}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{R}_{N}^{\ell}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}(\eta)\hat{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N}

(5.6)

\displaystyle=(2\ell+1)^{d}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{R}_{N}^{\ell}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{\ell}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}(\eta)\hat{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ell^{\frac{3d}{2}+1}}{N}\ln\left(\frac{N}{\ell^{d+1}}\right)\right)

where
\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}
is the constant equilibrium measure in the
\ell
-box with fixed parameter
f_{\tau}(x/N)
at every point of the box. In the second line of (
5.6
) we have used the regularity of
f_{\tau}
and the finite exponential moments. The proof of this error is not complicated but quite technical, let us give more details. Indeed we have

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathcal{R}_{N}^{\ell}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\left({\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}\right)\hat{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}\left[\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N,\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}-\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}\right]\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\ell^{d}}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{R}_{N}^{\ell}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\left|\hat{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}\right|\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\left|\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N,\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}-\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}\right|\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|\hat{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}\|_{L^{2}(\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell})}\left[\frac{\ell^{d+1}}{N}\ln\left(\frac{N}{\ell^{d+1}}\right)\right]e^{-C\tau}\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell^{-d/2}\left[\frac{\ell^{d+1}}{N}\ln\left(\frac{N}{\ell^{d+1}}\right)\right]=\frac{\ell^{d/2+1}}{N}\ln\left(\frac{N}{\ell^{d+1}}\right).

In the first line we have used the Lipschitz bound on
\Phi
and moments on the Gibbs measure to estimate
|{\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}|\lesssim\ell^{d}N^{-d}\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}
. In the last line we have used the estimate
\|\hat{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}\|_{L^{2}(\,{\rm d}\vartheta^{\ell}_{f_{\tau,x}})}\lesssim e^{-C\tau}\ell^{-d/2}
following from the product structure of the Gibbs measure, together with
\mathbf{L}_{\infty}(f_{t})
decreasing exponentially to zero as
t\to\infty
. In the second line, we have estimated the difference between the two measures in
L^{2}((\vartheta^{\ell}_{f_{\tau,x}})^{-1})
. We use

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N,\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}}{\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}}-1\right|=\left|\frac{\prod_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\sigma(f_{\tau,z})^{\eta_{z}}Z(\sigma(f_{\tau,x}))}{\prod_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\sigma(f_{\tau,x})^{\eta_{z}}Z(\sigma(f_{\tau,z}))}-1\right|

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\eta_{z}\ln\left[\frac{\sigma(f_{\tau,z})-\sigma(f_{\tau,x})}{\sigma(f_{\tau,x})}+1\right]+\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\ln\left[\frac{Z(\sigma(f_{\tau,x}))}{Z(\sigma(f_{\tau,z}))}\right]\right)-1

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\eta_{z}\ln\left[C^{\prime}e^{-Ct}\frac{\ell}{N}+1\right]+\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\ln\left[\frac{Z(\sigma(f_{\tau,x}))}{Z(\sigma(f_{\tau,z}))}\right]\right)-1

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(C^{\prime}e^{-Ct}\frac{\ell}{N}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\eta_{z}+\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\left|f_{\tau,z}-f_{\tau,x}\right|\right)-1\leq\exp\left(C^{\prime}e^{-Ct}\frac{\ell^{d+1}}{N}\left[\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}+1\right]\right)-1,

where we used that the partition function
Z
satisfies by construction

\ln\left(\frac{Z(\sigma(f_{\tau}(x/N)))}{Z(\sigma(f_{\tau}(z/N)))}\right)\leq\left|\int_{\sigma(f_{\tau}(z/N))}^{\sigma(f_{\tau}(x/N))}\frac{\sigma^{-1}(h)}{h}\,{\rm d}h\right|\lesssim\left|f_{\tau}(z/N)-f_{\tau}(x/N)\right|,

by using the lower bounds on
f_{\tau}
and the regularity of
\sigma
. We deduce

(5.7)

\begin{split}&\left(\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\left|\frac{\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N,\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}}{\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}}-1\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\left|\exp\left(C^{\prime}\frac{\ell^{d+1}}{N}\left[\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}+1\right]\right)-1\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}1_{\{\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\leq M\}}\left|\exp\left(C^{\prime}\frac{\ell^{d+1}}{N}(M+1)\right)-1\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt+\left(\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}1_{\{\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}>M\}}\left|\exp\left(C^{\prime}\frac{\ell^{d+1}}{N}\left[\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}+1\right]\right)-1\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{split}

for some constant
C^{\prime}>0
, with a cut-off
M=M(N)
to be chosen later in terms of
N
. Assuming
\ell^{d+1}\leq N
, the first term is of order
\mathcal{O}\left(M\ell^{d+1}N^{-1}\right)
, while for the second term we use the exponential moment relation
\mathbb{E}_{\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{N}}[e^{\theta\eta_{0}}]=\frac{Z(\sigma(f_{\tau,x})e^{\theta})}{Z(\sigma(f_{\tau,x}))}
for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
(valid and finite under our assumptions on the jump rate
g
) to deduce

\mathbb{E}_{\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{N}}\left[e^{\theta\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}}\right]=\prod_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\mathbb{E}_{\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{N}}[e^{\theta\ell^{-d}\eta_{z}}]=\prod_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\frac{Z(\sigma(f_{\tau,z})e^{\theta\ell^{-d}})}{Z(\sigma(f_{\tau,z}))},

and since

\left|\ln\left(\frac{Z(\sigma(f_{\tau,z}))}{Z(\sigma(f_{\tau,z})e^{\theta\ell^{-d}})}\right)\right|=\left|\int_{\sigma(f_{\tau,z})e^{\theta\ell^{-d}}}^{\sigma(f_{\tau,z})}\frac{\sigma^{-1}(r)}{r}\,{\rm d}r\right|\leq C\left|1-e^{\theta\ell^{-d}}\right|,

we obtain indeed, for a fixed
\theta
and
\ell
large enough so that
\theta\ell^{-d}\leq 1
:

\mathbb{E}_{\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{N}}\left[e^{\theta\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}}\right]\leq\prod_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}e^{C\left|1-e^{\theta\ell^{-d}}\right|}=\exp\left(C\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\left|1-e^{\theta\ell^{-d}}\right|\right)\lesssim\exp\left(C\theta\right)<\infty.

Exploiting this finite exponential moment for the
\ell
-averages, and applying Hölder’s inequality for some
\theta
with conjugate
\theta^{\prime}
, we estimate the second term in (
5.7
):

\displaystyle C^{\prime}\left(\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}1_{\{\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}>M\}}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta^{\prime}}}\leq C^{\prime\prime}\left(\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}1_{\{\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}>M\}}e^{-2\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\theta^{\prime}}}\leq C^{\prime\prime}e^{-\frac{M}{\theta^{\prime}}}

for some explicit constants
C^{\prime\prime}>0
depending on the limit solution and on the exponential moment bounds, but not on the scaling parameters
\ell,N
. Inserting this into (
5.7
) we get

(5.8)

\left(\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\left|\frac{\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N,\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}}{\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}}-1\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim M\frac{\ell^{d+1}}{N}+e^{-\frac{M}{2\theta^{\prime}}}.

We finally choose
M:=2\theta^{\prime}\ln(N\ell^{-d-1})
, which shows that the error of flattening the local Gibbs measure measure is indeed of order
\ell^{d+1}N^{-1}\ln(N\ell^{-d-1})
.

5.6.
Step 3. Projection on the local equilibrium

Next, we remove and add the local equilibrium, i.e. the average
over all configurations with the same mass in the
\ell
-box:

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathcal{R}_{N}^{\ell}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{\ell}}{\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}(\eta)\hat{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{R}_{N}^{\ell}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{\ell}}\left[{\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}(\eta)-\Pi_{x}^{\ell}\ {\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}(\eta)\right]\hat{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt+\sum_{x\in\mathcal{R}_{N}^{\ell}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{\ell}}\Pi_{x}^{\ell}{\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}(\eta)\hat{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}=:(2\ell+1)^{-d}J_{t}^{N}+(2\ell+1)^{-d}\tilde{J}_{t}^{N}

where
\Pi_{x}^{\ell}\varphi
projects any function

\varphi(\eta^{(\ell)})
of
\eta^{(\ell)}\in\mathsf{X}_{\ell}
onto the
hyperplan

\Omega_{m}:=\left\{\eta^{(\ell)}\in\mathsf{X}_{\ell}\,:\,\langle\eta^{(\ell)}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{z}}=m\right\}

of constant mass
m
. The precise formula is

(5.9)

\Pi_{x}^{\ell}\varphi(\eta^{(\ell)})=[\Pi_{x}^{\ell}\varphi](\langle\eta^{(\ell)}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}})=\int_{\Omega_{\langle\eta^{(\ell)}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}}}\varphi(\eta^{(\ell)})\frac{\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}(\eta^{(\ell)})}{\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}(\Omega_{\langle\eta^{(\ell)}\rangle})}.

Combined with (
5.6
) we have therefore the decomposition

(5.10)

\displaystyle I_{t}^{N}=J^{N}_{t}+\tilde{J}^{N}_{t}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ell^{\frac{3d}{2}+1}}{N}\ln\left(\frac{N}{\ell^{d+1}}\right)\right).

5.7.
Step 4. Local coercivity estimate

To estimate the first term
J^{N}_{t}
we use the
Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the Poincaré inequality in the cube
\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}
, see
[
LSV96
]
.
(The constant in
the Poincaré inequality is independent of the number of particles
in this cube and proportional to
\ell^{2}
.)

(5.11)

\begin{split}&J_{t}^{N}\leq(2\ell+1)^{d}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{R}_{N}^{\ell}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{\ell}}\left[{\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}(\eta)-\Pi_{x}^{\ell}\ {\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}(\eta)\right]\hat{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}\\
&\lesssim\ell^{d}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{R}_{N}^{\ell}}\int_{0}^{t}\left[\int_{\mathsf{X}_{\ell}}\left[{\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}(\eta)-\Pi_{x}^{\ell}\ {\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}(\eta)\right]^{2}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}\right]^{1/2}\|A^{N}_{\tau,x}\|_{L^{2}_{\vartheta^{\ell}_{f_{\tau,x}}}}\,{\rm d}\tau\\
&\lesssim\ell^{d/2}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{R}_{N}^{\ell}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-C\tau}\left[\int_{\mathsf{X}_{\ell}}\left[{\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}(\eta)-\Pi_{x}^{\ell}\ {\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}(\eta)\right]^{2}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}\right]^{1/2}\,{\rm d}\tau,\end{split}

where in the second line we have used again the estimate
\|A^{N}_{\tau,x}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim e^{-C\tau}\ell^{-d/2}
. We then apply
Poincaré inequality to get the upper bound

(5.12)

J_{t}^{N}\leq\ell^{1+d/2}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{R}_{N}^{\ell}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-C\tau}\sqrt{\mathcal{D}_{\vartheta^{\ell}_{f_{\tau,x}}}\left({\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}\right)}\,{\rm d}\tau.

Here the Dirichet form
\mathcal{D}_{\vartheta^{\ell}_{f_{\tau,x}}}(\varphi)
is the
L^{2}
entropy production functional on the
\ell
-box with respect to the reference equilibrium measure
\vartheta^{\ell}_{f_{\tau,x}}
:

\mathcal{D}_{\vartheta^{\ell}_{f_{\tau,x}}}(\varphi):=\sum_{y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{\ell}}p(y_{1}-y_{2})g(\eta_{y_{1}})\left[\varphi^{y_{1}y_{2}}-\varphi\right]^{2}\,{\rm d}\vartheta^{\ell}_{f_{\tau,x}}.

The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality then implies

\displaystyle J_{t}^{N}

\displaystyle\leq\ell^{1+d/2}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{R}_{N}^{\ell}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-C\tau}\sqrt{\mathcal{D}_{\vartheta^{\ell}_{f_{\tau,x}}}\left(\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}\right)}\,{\rm d}\tau\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\ell^{1+d/2}\frac{N^{d/2}}{\ell^{d/2}}e^{-C\tau}\sqrt{\sum_{x\in\mathcal{R}_{N}^{\ell}}\mathcal{D}_{\vartheta^{\ell}_{f_{\tau,x}}}\left(\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}\right)}\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell N^{d/2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-C\tau}\left[\sum_{y_{1}\sim y_{2}\in\mathcal{R}_{N}^{\ell}}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}p(y_{1}-y_{2})g(\eta_{y_{1}})\left[\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}^{y_{1}y_{2}}-\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}\right]^{2}\,{\rm d}\vartheta^{N}_{f_{\tau,x}}\right]^{1/2}\,{\rm d}\tau

(5.13)

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell N^{d/2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-C\tau}\sqrt{\mathcal{D}_{\vartheta^{\ell}_{f_{\tau,x}}}\left(\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}\right)}\,{\rm d}\tau.

We now aim to establish sufficiently strong decay in
N
on the Dirichlet form in order to obtain the decay for the term
J_{t}^{N}
. This estimate is in fact a control of regularity directly at the discrete level of the many-particle system. We propose two methods, which both have interest per se. The first one relies on Lemma
4.1
to prove that a “jump distance” that we introduce is non-increasing, but it is restricted to dimension
d=1
and test functions
\Phi
that are simple modulated sums. The second method is based on establishing a lower bound on the minimum number of jumps within a given time interval up to an exponentially small error of large deviation in
N
, thanks to the non-degeneracy condition on the jump rate.

We wish to estimate

\left|\Phi_{t}(\eta^{zz^{\prime}})-\Phi_{t}(\eta)\right|=\left|\mathbb{E}\Big[\Phi_{0}((\eta^{zz^{\prime}})^{t})-\Phi_{0}(\eta^{t})\Big]\right|,

which appears in the Dirichlet energy. Employing Lemma
4.1
, we note that
\operatorname{supp}(\pi_{t}^{N})\subset\{\|\eta-\zeta\|_{\ell^{1}}\leq 2\}
for all times: given that the mass is conserved and
\zeta^{0}=(\eta^{0})^{zz^{\prime}}
and
\eta^{0}
have the same mass, so do
\eta
and
\zeta
for all times in the support of
\pi_{t}
, and the above support condition implies that all pairs of configuration
(\eta,\zeta)
in the support of
\pi_{t}
satisfy
\zeta=\eta
or
\zeta^{t}=(\eta^{t})^{x_{-}x_{+}}
for two sites
x_{-},x_{+}\in\mathbb{T}_{N}
not necessarily neighbours (it is a
generalised jump
so to speak). When the two points
x_{-}=x_{+}
become equal, they remain so, thanks again to weak contraction of the support in Lemma
4.1
.

Method 1: Monotonicity of the jump distance in dimension
1
.

Assume
d=1
,
p
to be the symmetric nearest-neighbour transition function, and consider
\Phi_{0}=N^{-1}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\eta_{x}\phi_{x}
with
\phi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T})
: such particular form of the initial test function is an assumption of “discrete smoothness” at the initial time, i.e. the projections of
\Phi_{0}
have similar laws for nearby sites. These assumptions are restrictive, but the stability estimate we prove is a strong stability on the level of the trajectories. Given
\eta,\zeta\in\mathsf{X}_{N}
with same mass
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\eta_{x}=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\zeta_{x}
, we define the
jump distance

J(\eta,\zeta)
as the minimum number of jumps to transform the configuration
\eta
into the configuration
\zeta
. For given two neighbouring points
z\sim z^{\prime}
on the lattice,
J(\eta,\eta^{zz^{\prime}})=1
, i.e. the configurations
\eta
an
\eta^{zz^{\prime}}
are one jump apart. Given the initial coupling measure
\pi_{0}^{N}(\eta,\zeta):=\delta_{\eta=\eta}\otimes\delta_{\zeta=\eta^{zz^{\prime}}}
on
\mathsf{X}_{N}^{2}
, we then use the standard coupling described above to define the coupling measure
\pi_{t}
of
(\eta_{t},\zeta_{t})
at later times. And we have the formula

\displaystyle\left|\Phi_{t}(\eta^{zz^{\prime}})-\Phi_{t}(\eta)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}\Big[\Phi_{0}\left((\eta^{zz^{\prime}})^{t}\right)-\Phi_{0}\left(\eta^{t}\right)\Big]\right|=\left|\int_{\eta,\zeta\in\mathsf{X}_{N}}\Big[\Phi_{0}(\zeta)-\Phi_{0}(\eta)\Big]\,{\rm d}\pi_{t}(\eta,\zeta)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\eta,\zeta\in\mathsf{X}_{N}}N^{-1}\left[\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\phi_{x}\left(\eta_{x}-\zeta_{x}\right)\right]\,{\rm d}\pi_{t}(\eta,\zeta)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-2}\int_{\eta,\zeta\in\mathsf{X}_{N}}J\left(\eta,\zeta\right)\,{\rm d}\pi_{t}(\eta,\zeta)

where in the last line we have used that
\eta
can be connected to
\zeta
by
J(\eta,\zeta)
jumps between two neighbouring sites, and each of them costs an error
N^{-1}
thanks to the
C^{1}
regularity of
\phi
. Indeed for each jump
\zeta=\eta^{zz^{\prime}}
with
z\sim z^{\prime}
:

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\phi_{x}\left(\eta-\eta^{zz^{\prime}}\right)=\phi_{z^{\prime}}-\phi_{z}=\mathcal{O}(N^{-1}).

We finally claim that

(5.14)

\int_{\eta,\zeta\in\mathsf{X}_{N}}J\left(\eta,\zeta\right)\,{\rm d}\pi_{t}(\eta,\zeta)\leq\int_{\eta,\zeta\in\mathsf{X}_{N}}J\left(\eta,\zeta\right)\,{\rm d}\pi_{0}(\eta,\zeta)=2.

In order to prove (
5.14
), we use the remark above that
\zeta^{t}=(\eta^{t})^{x_{-}x_{+}}
for two sites
x_{-},x_{+}\in\mathbb{T}_{N}
not necessarily neighbours, and in fact it follows from our definition of the jump distance that these two sites are exactly at distance
J(\eta,\zeta)
on the lattice (where the lattice distance is the minimal number of edges to connect two points). We then differentiate in time

\displaystyle\frac{\,{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}^{2}}J(\eta,\zeta)\,{\rm d}\pi_{t}^{N}(\eta,\zeta)=\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}^{2}}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}J(\eta,\zeta)\ \,{\rm d}\pi_{t}^{N}(\eta,\zeta)

\displaystyle=N^{2}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}^{2}}\sum_{x\sim x^{\prime}}p(x^{\prime}-x)\left(g(\eta_{x})\wedge g(\zeta_{x})\right)\left[J(\eta^{xx^{\prime}},\zeta^{xx^{\prime}})-J(\eta,\zeta)\right]\,{\rm d}\pi_{t}^{N}(\eta,\zeta)

\displaystyle\quad+N^{2}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}^{2}}\sum_{x\sim x^{\prime}}p(x^{\prime}-x)\left(g(\eta_{x})-g(\zeta_{x})\right)_{+}\left[J(\eta^{xx^{\prime}},\zeta)-J(\eta,\zeta)\right]\,{\rm d}\pi_{t}^{N}(\eta,\zeta)

\displaystyle\quad+N^{2}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}^{2}}\sum_{x\sim x^{\prime}}p(x^{\prime}-x)\left(g(\zeta_{x})-g(\eta_{x})\right)_{+}\left[J(\eta,\zeta^{xx^{\prime}})-J(\eta,\zeta)\right]\,{\rm d}\pi_{t}^{N}(\eta,\zeta).

The first term of the right hand side is zero. The second term of the right hand side is nonzero only at sites
x
where
\eta_{x}>\zeta_{x}
, i.e. at
x=x_{-}
and the third term of the right hand side is nonzero only at sites
x
where
\zeta_{x}>\eta_{x}
i.e. at
x=x_{+}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{\,{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}^{2}}J(\eta,\zeta)\,{\rm d}\pi_{t}^{N}(\eta,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{N^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}^{2}}\left(g(\eta_{x_{-}})-g(\eta_{x_{-}}-1)\right)_{+}\left[J\left(\eta^{x_{-}(x_{-}-1)},\zeta\right)+J\left(\eta^{x_{-}(x_{-}+1)},\zeta\right)-2J(\eta,\zeta)\right]\,{\rm d}\pi_{t}^{N}(\eta,\zeta)

\displaystyle+\frac{N^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}^{2}}\left(g(\zeta_{x_{+}})-g(\zeta_{x_{+}}-1)\right)_{+}\left[J\left(\eta,\zeta^{x_{+}(x_{+}-1)}\right)+J\left(\eta,\zeta^{x_{+}(x_{+}+1)}\right)-2J(\eta,\zeta)\right]\,{\rm d}\pi_{t}^{N}(\eta,\zeta).

Note finally that either in dimension
d=1
we have

J\left(\eta^{x_{-}(x_{-}-1)},\zeta\right)+J\left(\eta^{x_{-}(x_{-}+1)},\zeta\right)=J\left(\eta,\zeta^{x_{+}(x_{+}-1)}\right)+J\left(\eta,\zeta^{x_{+}(x_{+}+1)}\right)=2J(\eta,\zeta)

since one direction decreases the jump distance by
1
while the other direction increases it by
1
. We conclude that
|\Phi_{t}(\eta^{zz^{\prime}})-\Phi_{t}(\eta)|\lesssim N^{-2}
, which implies

(5.15)

\displaystyle J_{t}^{N}\lesssim\ell N^{d/2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-C\tau}\sqrt{\mathcal{D}_{\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}}\left(\Phi_{t,\tau}\right)}\,{\rm d}\tau\lesssim\mathcal{O}\left(\ell N^{-1}\right).

Method 2: The random walk viewpoint.
We now consider the random points
x_{-}(t)
and
x_{+}(t))
as jump processes following symmetric random walks in a given random environment, with respective jump rates
(g(\eta_{x_{-}})-g(\eta_{x_{-}}-1))_{+}
and
\left(g(\eta_{x_{+}}+1)-g(\eta_{x_{+}})\right)_{+}
, provided we consider
\eta
as given. Then
S(t)=x_{+}(t)-x_{-}(t)
also defines a symmetric random walk in a random environment. Note that
S
stop when it hits zero, i.e.
x_{+}=x_{-}
. The idea is to use quantitatively the recurrence of random walks in dimension
1
and
2
.

The first step consists in proving a lower bound on the number of jumps per unit of time for the random walk
S(t)
as long as it has not hit zero (where it then stays forever). We use the assumptions made on
g
, in particular
0<g_{1}^{*}\leq|g(k)-g(k+1)|\leq g_{2}^{*}<\infty
. We claim that there is
\beta\in(0,1)
small enough, and constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
, all depending on
g_{1}^{*}
and
g_{2}^{*}
, so that

(5.16)

\mathbb{P}\left(\big[\text{number of jumps up to time }t\big]\leq\beta tN^{2}\right)\leq C_{1}e^{-C_{2}tN^{2}}.

Let us prove this claim (
5.16
). Let
(T_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be the random waiting times for the jumps of
S(t)
, which follow exponential distribution with rate
\lambda_{k}
, i.e.
T_{k}\sim\lambda_{k}e^{-\lambda_{k}t}1_{t\geq 0}
with density function
h_{k}
. We define
\omega_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}T_{k}
the time of the
n
-th jump. Having less than
\beta tN^{2}
jumps before time
t
means that the time of the
\beta tN^{2}
-th jump is greater
t
, so the error we want to estimate is

(5.17)

\mathbb{P}(\omega_{\beta tN^{2}}>t)=\int_{t}^{\infty}(h_{1}\ast h_{2}\ast\cdots\ast h_{\beta tN^{2}})(s)\,{\rm d}s.

Since, after rescaling time by
N^{2}
,
N^{2}\lambda_{i}\in[N^{2}g_{1}^{*},N^{2}g_{2}^{*}]
, for
i=1,\dots,\beta tN^{2}
, the
\mathbb{P}(\omega_{\beta tN^{2}}>t)
is upper bounded by

\int_{t}^{\infty}(\underbrace{\bar{h}\ast\bar{h}\ast\cdots\ast\bar{h}}_{\beta tN^{2}\text{ times}})(s)\,{\rm d}s

where
\bar{h}=N^{2}(\max\lambda_{i})e^{-N^{2}(\min\lambda_{i})t}1_{t\geq 0}=N^{2}g_{2}^{*}e^{-N^{2}g_{1}^{*}t}1_{t\geq 0}
. We compute inductively

(\bar{h})^{\ast\beta tN^{2}}(s)=(N^{2}g_{2}^{*})^{\beta tN^{2}}\frac{s^{\beta tN^{2}-1}}{(\beta tN^{2}-1)!}e^{-N^{2}g_{1}^{*}s}.

Inserting this into (
5.17
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\omega_{\beta tN^{2}}>t)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t}^{\infty}(N^{2}g_{2}^{*})^{\beta tN^{2}}\frac{s^{\beta tN^{2}-1}}{(\beta tN^{2}-1)!}e^{-N^{2}g_{1}^{*}s}\,{\rm d}s

(5.18)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{g_{2}^{*}}{g_{1}^{*}}\right)^{\beta tN^{2}}\left[(g_{1}^{*}N^{2})^{\beta tN^{2}}\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{s^{\beta tN^{2}-1}}{(\beta tN^{2}-1)!}e^{-N^{2}g_{1}^{*}s}\,{\rm d}s\right]

(5.19)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{g_{2}^{*}}{g_{1}^{*}}\right)^{\beta tN^{2}}\frac{1}{(\beta tN^{2}-1)!}\int_{g_{1}^{*}tN^{2}}^{\infty}y^{\beta tN^{2}-1}e^{-y}\,{\rm d}y

by change of variable. Then given
L
real and
K
integer, both large, we use

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K!}\int_{L}^{+\infty}y^{K}e^{-y}\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K!}\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{K}\left(\int_{L}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda y}\,{\rm d}y\right)_{|\lambda=1}\right|=\frac{1}{K!}\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{K}\left(\frac{e^{-\lambda L}}{\lambda}\right)_{|\lambda=1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K!}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\left(\begin{matrix}K\\
k\end{matrix}\right)L^{k}e^{-L}\leq\frac{1}{K!}(L+1)^{K}e^{-L}.

Applying it with
L:=g_{1}^{*}tN^{2}
and
K:=\beta tN^{2}-1
gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\omega_{\beta tN^{2}}>t)\lesssim\left(\frac{g_{2}^{*}}{g_{1}^{*}}\right)^{\beta tN^{2}}\frac{(g_{1}^{*}tN^{2})^{\beta tN^{2}-1}}{(\beta tN^{2}-1)!}e^{-g_{1}^{*}tN^{2}}

which gives the claimed estimate by the Stirling formula.

We then decompose
J_{t}^{N}=J_{t}^{\text{NR}}+J_{t}^{\text{slow}}+J_{t}^{\text{R}}
into three events: (i) when the number of jumps before time
t
is at least
\beta tN^{2}
and the random walk
S_{t}
has not returned to zero within this time interval; (ii) when the number of jumps before time
t
is less than
\beta tN^{2}
; and (iii) when the random walk
S_{t}
has returned to zero within this time. The last case (iii) is simple since in this region
|\Phi_{0}(\zeta^{t})-\Phi_{0}(\eta^{t})|=0
because the two configurations are equal at time
t
(and remain so for all subsequent times). The second case (ii) is small thanks to the estimate (
5.16
), which gives an exponentially small error in
N
. We finally consider the first case (i), when the random walk
S
has jumped “a lot” but still not returned to the origin:

\displaystyle J_{t}^{\text{NR}}\lesssim\ell N^{d/2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-C\tau}\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sum_{y_{1}\sim y_{2}}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}g(\eta_{y_{1}})\left|\mathbb{E}\left(1_{x_{+}(t)\not=x_{-}(t)}1_{\#\text{jumps}\geq\beta tN^{2}}\left[\Phi_{0}\left(\eta_{t-\tau}\right)-\Phi_{0}\left(\eta_{t-\tau}^{x_{-}(t-\tau)x_{+}(t-\tau)}\right)\right]\right)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell\int_{0}^{t}e^{-C\tau}\sqrt{N^{-d}\sum_{y_{1}\sim y_{2}}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}g(\eta_{y_{1}})\left|\mathbb{E}\left(1_{x_{+}(t)\not=x_{-}(t)}1_{\#\text{jumps}\geq\beta tN^{2}}\right)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{N}}\,{\rm d}\tau

where we have used the Lipschitz property of
\Phi_{t-\tau}
in order to extract the
N^{-2d}
decay inside the square root, since the
\ell^{1}
distance between
\eta_{t-\tau}
and
\eta_{t-\tau}^{x_{-}(t-\tau)x_{+}(t-\tau)}
is always
2
.

Then in dimension
d=1
, the probability of not returning to zero in
\beta(t-\tau)N^{2}
jumps is
\mathcal{O}((t-\tau)^{-1/2}N^{-1})
, see
[
LL10
, Prop 4.2.4, p.80]
.
This would immediately give
J^{\text{NR}}_{t}\lesssim\ell N^{-1/2}
if it was not for the factor
g(\eta_{y_{1}})
in the integrand which is unbounded. We control it in the usual way by using exponential moments on the Gibbs measure, splitting
\eta_{y_{1}}\leq M
and
\eta_{y_{1}}>M
and finally optimising
M=M(N)\sim\ln((t-\tau)N^{2})
. We leave this easy calculation to the reader, which leads to

(5.20)

J^{\text{NR}}_{t}\lesssim\frac{\ell\ln N}{\sqrt{N}}.

In dimension
d=2
, the probability of not returning to zero in
\beta(t-\tau)N^{2}
jumps is
\mathcal{O}((\ln((t-\tau)N^{2})^{-1})
, see
[
LL10
, Prop 4.2.4, p.80]
. This would immediately give
J^{NR}_{t}\lesssim\ell(\ln N)^{-1/2}
if it was not for the unbounded factor
g(\eta_{y_{1}})
in the integrand. We control it again by using exponential moments on the Gibbs measure, splitting
\eta_{y_{1}}\leq M
and
\eta_{y_{1}}>M
and finally optimising
M=M(N)\sim\sqrt{|\ln((t-\tau)N^{2})|}
. We again leave this calculation to the reader, which leads to

(5.21)

J^{\text{NR}}_{t}\lesssim\frac{\ell}{(\ln N)^{1/4}}.

5.8.
Step 5. Local limit theorem at the local equilibrium

To control the term
\tilde{J}_{t}^{N}

in (
5.10
) we use the so-called
equivalence of ensembles
. It is proved via the local limit theorem
A.1
applied in the box
\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}
to the local equilibrium
\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}
, which is a product identically distributed measure with one-site mass parameter
f_{\tau,x}=f(\tau,x/N)
bounded from above and away from zero, and to the functions
g(\eta_{y_{0}})
which only depends on the value of
\eta
at some site
y_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}
, and then perform the average over
y_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}
to deduce

(5.22)

\left[\Pi_{x}^{\ell}\langle g(\eta_{\cdot})\rangle_{\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}\right](m)={\bf E}_{\vartheta^{\ell}_{m}}\left[\langle g\left(\eta_{\cdot}\right)\rangle_{\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}\right]+\mathcal{O}\left(\ell^{-d}\right)=\sigma(m)+\mathcal{O}\left(\ell^{-d+0}\right)

when
m:=\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}\in[m_{0},m_{1}]
for some given
0<m_{0}<m_{1}<\infty
. We choose
m_{0}
small enough and
m_{1}
large enough so that the
\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}
-probability that
m
falls outside
[m_{0},m_{1}]
is exponentially small in
\ell
. Inserting this bound into our formula for
\hat{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}
, we get

\displaystyle\Pi^{\ell}_{x}\hat{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}=\left\{\left[\Pi_{x}^{\ell}\langle g(\eta_{\cdot})\rangle_{\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}\right]-\sigma\left(f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)-\sigma^{\prime}\left(f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)\left[\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}-f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right]\right\}\frac{\mathbf{L}_{\infty}(f)\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)}{\sigma\left(f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sigma\left(\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\right)-\sigma\left(f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)-\sigma^{\prime}\left(f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)\left[\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}-f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right]\right\}\frac{\mathbf{L}_{\infty}(f)\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)}{\sigma\left(f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)}+\mathcal{O}\left(\ell^{-d+0}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\left|\langle\eta^{(\ell)}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}-f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right|^{2}e^{-C\tau}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\ell^{-d+0})

(abusing slightly notation by incorporating the exponential small set
m\not\in[m_{0},m_{1}]
into the error). The Lipschitz regularity of
\Phi_{t-s}
implies
\Pi_{x}^{\ell}{\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}=\mathcal{O}(\ell^{d}N^{-d})
, since we recall that
\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}:=e^{(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}_{N}}\Phi-\mathbb{E}_{\vartheta_{f_{\tau}}^{N}}(e^{(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}_{N}}\Phi)
.
We however need a better control.

We claim that this Lipschitz regularity of
\Phi_{t-s}
also implies a Lipschitz regularity of its averaged projection
\Pi_{x}^{\ell}{\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\Phi_{t-s}
with constant
\ell^{d}N^{-d}
, with respect to the local mass
\langle\eta^{(\ell)}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}
. Let us prove this fact. We reason in the
\ell
-box. Given
0\leq m\leq m^{\prime}<+\infty
, pick any pair of configuration
(\eta_{0},\zeta_{0})
with
\langle\eta_{0}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}}=m
,
\langle\zeta_{0}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}}=m^{\prime}
and
\eta_{0}\leq\zeta_{0}
(such configuration trivially exists since
m\leq m^{\prime}
). We then consider the initial coupling
\delta_{(\eta_{0},\zeta_{0})}
on
\Omega_{m}\times\Omega_{m^{\prime}}
which has
\ell^{1}
cost
m^{\prime}-m
, and we evolve it along the flow of the coupling operator
e^{t\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}}
. The marginals respectively converge to
\vartheta^{\ell,m}
and
\vartheta^{\ell,m^{\prime}}
(convergence to equilibrium of the original evolution). Since the evolution by the coupling operator satisfies our microscopic stability estimate, we have
W_{1}(\vartheta^{\ell,m},\vartheta^{\ell,m^{\prime}})\leq C_{S}(m^{\prime}-m)
. An optimal coupling
\pi
thus satisfies

\int_{\Omega_{m}\times\Omega_{m^{\prime}}}\left(\ell^{-d}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}|\eta_{z}-\zeta_{z}|\right)\,{\rm d}\pi(\eta,\zeta)\leq C_{S}(m^{\prime}-m)

and we compute

\displaystyle\left|\Pi_{x}^{\ell}{\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\Phi_{t-\tau}(m^{\prime})-\Pi_{x}^{\ell}{\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\Phi_{t-\tau}(m)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\Omega_{m^{\prime}}}{\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\Phi_{t-\tau}(\zeta)\,{\rm d}\vartheta^{\ell,m^{\prime}}(\zeta)-\int_{\Omega_{m}}{\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\Phi_{t-\tau}(\eta)\,{\rm d}\vartheta^{\ell,m}(\eta)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{m}\times\Omega_{m^{\prime}}}\left|{\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\Phi_{t-\tau}(\zeta)-{\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\Phi_{t-\tau}(\eta)\right|\,{\rm d}\pi(\eta,\zeta)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\ell^{d}}{N^{d}}\int_{\Omega_{m}\times\Omega_{m^{\prime}}}\left(\ell^{-d}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}|\eta_{z}-\zeta_{z}|\right)\,{\rm d}\pi(\eta,\zeta)\leq C_{S}\frac{\ell^{d}}{N^{d}}|m^{\prime}-m|.

Using then the almost zero-average
property (
5.5
) of
\hat{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}
, we write

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{t}^{N}=(2\ell+1)^{d}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{R}_{N}^{\ell}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{\ell}}\left(\Pi_{x}^{\ell}{\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}\right)\left(\Pi_{x}^{\ell}\hat{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}\right)\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell^{d}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{R}_{N}^{\ell}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{\ell}}\left|\left(\Pi_{x}^{\ell}{\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}\right)\left(\langle\eta^{(\ell)}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\right)-\left(\Pi_{x}^{\ell}{\bf P}_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\tilde{\Phi}_{t,\tau}\right)\left(f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right)\right|\left(\Pi^{\ell}_{x}\hat{A}^{N}_{\tau,x}\right)\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}+\frac{\ell^{d+1}}{N}

(5.23)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\ell^{2d+0}}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{R}_{N}^{\ell}}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{\ell}}\left|\langle\eta^{(\ell)}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}-f_{\tau}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right|^{3}e^{-C\tau}\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\tau,x}}^{\ell}+\frac{\ell^{d+1}}{N}\lesssim\ell^{-\frac{d}{2}+0}+\frac{\ell^{d+1}}{N},

where for the last term we employed the local law of large numbers in the
\ell
box, to extract an
\ell^{-3d/2}
decay from the integrand. We note that Step 5 holds for any dimension
d\geq 1
.

5.9.
Step 6. Synthesis of the errors

In dimension
d=1
, by combining (
5.10
)-(
5.20
)-(
5.23
), we get

\displaystyle I_{t}^{N}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ell^{\frac{3d}{2}+1}}{N}\ln\left(\frac{N}{\ell^{d+1}}\right)\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ell}{\sqrt{N}}\ln N\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\ell^{-\frac{d}{2}+0}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ell}{\sqrt{N}}\ln N\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\ell^{-\frac{d}{2}+0}\right)

and after optimising over
\ell
, choosing
\ell=N^{1/3}
, the final consistency rate is
I_{t}^{N}=\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/6+0}\ln(N))
. In dimension
d=2
, by combining (
5.10
)-(
5.21
)-(
5.23
), we get

\displaystyle I_{t}^{N}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ell^{\frac{3d}{2}+1}}{N}\ln\left(\frac{N}{\ell^{d+1}}\right)\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ell}{[\ln(N)]^{1/4}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\ell^{-\frac{d}{2}+0}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ell}{[\ln(N)]^{1/4}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\ell^{-\frac{d}{2}+0}\right).

We optimise by choosing
\ell=[\ln(N)]^{1/8}
so that
I_{t}^{N}=\mathcal{O}([\ln(N)]^{-1/8+0})
.

Remark 4
.

In the case of dimension
1
, when
\Phi_{0}
has the additional structure
\Phi_{0}(\eta)=N^{-1}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\eta_{x}\phi_{x}
with
\phi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, (1) by using a more general Poincaré inequality valid on the
non-flattened measure
one gets rid altogether of the error (
5.10
), (2) by using the “method
1
” one gets the better rate
\mathcal{O}(\ell\ln N/N)
in (
5.20
), (3) by using higher-order versions of the local limit theorem
A.1
one expect to get the better rate
\mathcal{O}(\ell^{-d})
in (
5.23
), modulo a term that is killed by orthogonality, which would give (asymptotically) the optimal rate
\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/2+0})
. Such improvement will be considered in further work. Note that we do not know if a jump distance monotonicity (as in “method
1
”) holds in dimension
d\geq 2
.

6.
Entropic consistency

The
L^{1}_{t}
estimate of
\mathsf{R}_{1}
for the ZRP was established in the previous section. We now prove the
L^{\infty}_{t}
estimate over
\mathsf{R}_{2}
, and then
(H4)
, for the ZRP.

6.1.
Estimate on
\mathsf{R}_{2}
for the ZRP

We consider the two functions in (
2.10
) that are to be estimated in the Lipschitz seminorm along time. We start with
N^{-d}\ln\left(G_{t}^{N}(\eta)\right)
. Since the density
G^{N}_{t}
of the local Gibbs measure is

\displaystyle G_{t}^{N}(\eta)=\frac{\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{t}}^{N}}{\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{\infty}}^{N}}(\eta)=\prod_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\frac{Z(\sigma(f_{\infty}))}{Z(\sigma(f_{t,x}))}\bigg(\frac{\sigma(f_{t,x})}{\sigma(f_{\infty})}\bigg)^{\eta_{x}}

we deduce

\frac{\ln\left(G_{t}^{N}(\eta)\right)}{N^{d}}=\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\left[\ln\left(\frac{Z(\sigma(f_{\infty}))}{Z(\sigma(f_{t.x}))}\right)+\eta_{x}\ln\left(\frac{\sigma(f_{t,x})}{\sigma(f_{\infty})}\right)\right]

which satisfies the Lipschitz condition uniformly in time, by using the uniform bounds on
\sigma(f_{t.x})
and
Z
. We then consider
N^{-d}(\mathcal{L}_{N}\sqrt{G_{t}^{N}})/\sqrt{G_{t}^{N}}
. Explicit calculations yield

\displaystyle\sqrt{G_{t}^{N}}=\prod_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma\left(f_{t,x}\right)}{\sigma(f_{\infty})}\right)^{\eta_{x}}\frac{Z(\sigma(f_{\infty}))}{Z(\sigma(f_{t}(x/N)))}}

so that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{N}\sqrt{G_{t}^{N}}(\eta)=N^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\sum_{|e|=1}g(\eta_{x})\bigg[\sqrt{\frac{\sigma\big(f_{t}\big(\frac{x+e}{N}\big)\big)}{\sigma\big(f_{t}\big(\frac{x}{N}\big)\big)}}-1\bigg]\sqrt{G_{t}^{N}}(\eta)

and hence

\frac{\mathcal{L}_{N}\sqrt{G_{t}^{N}}(\eta)}{N^{d}\sqrt{G_{t}^{N}}(\eta)}=\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\frac{g(\eta_{x})}{\sqrt{\sigma\left(f_{t,x}\right)}}N^{2}\sum_{y\sim x}\left[\sqrt{\sigma\left(f_{t,y}\right)}-\sqrt{\sigma\left(f_{t,x}\right)}\right]=\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}g(\eta_{x})\phi_{x}

with the following test function at each site

\phi_{x}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma\left(f_{t,x}\right)}}N^{2}\sum_{y\sim x}\left[\sqrt{\sigma\left(f_{t,y}\right)}-\sqrt{\sigma\left(f_{t,x}\right)}\right]

which is bounded in
C^{1}
thanks to the regularity of
\sigma
and
f
(note that the last sum is a discrete Laplacian). We have therefore proved that
\mathsf{R}_{2}(t)
is uniformly bounded in time.

6.2.
Proof of
(H4)
for the ZRP

We now prove (
2.12
) in
(H4)
. Let us compute

\displaystyle H^{N}\left(\vartheta_{f_{t}}^{N}|\vartheta^{N}_{f_{\infty}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\left[\eta_{x}\ln\left(\frac{\sigma\left(f_{t,x}\right)}{\sigma(f_{\infty})}\right)+\ln\left(\frac{Z(\sigma(f_{\infty}))}{Z\left(\sigma\left(f_{t,x}\right)\right)}\right)\right]\,{\rm d}\vartheta_{f_{t}}^{N}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\left[f_{t,x}\ln\left(\frac{\sigma\left(f_{t,x}\right)}{\sigma(f_{\infty})}\right)+\ln\left(\frac{Z(\sigma(f_{\infty}))}{Z\left(\sigma\left(f_{t,x}\right)\right)}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(f_{t}\ln\frac{\sigma(f_{t})}{\sigma(f_{\infty})}+\ln\frac{Z(\sigma(f_{\infty}))}{Z(\sigma(f_{t}))}\right)\,{\rm d}x+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)

by using the periodicity and
C^{3}
regularity of
f
,
and

\displaystyle\frac{4}{N^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\sqrt{G^{N}_{\tau}}\mathcal{L}_{N}\left(\sqrt{G^{N}_{\tau}}\right)\,{\rm d}\vartheta^{N}_{f_{\infty}}\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{4N^{2}}{N^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}p(y-x)g(\eta_{x})\left[\sqrt{\frac{\sigma\left(f_{t,y}\right)}{\sigma\left(f_{t,x}\right)}}-1\right]\,{\rm d}\vartheta^{N}_{f_{t}}\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{4N^{2}}{N^{d}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\int_{0}^{t}p(y-x)\left[\sqrt{\sigma\left(f_{t,y}\right)}-\sqrt{\sigma\left(f_{t,x}\right)}\right]\sqrt{\sigma\left(f_{t,x}\right)}\,{\rm d}\tau

which can be approximated by higher-order Taylor expansion and employing the decay of
f_{t}
.
We then deduce

\displaystyle\frac{4}{N^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{X}_{N}}\sqrt{G^{N}_{\tau}}\mathcal{L}_{N}\left(\sqrt{G^{N}_{\tau}}\right)\,{\rm d}\vartheta^{N}_{f_{\infty}}\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\int_{0}^{t}\underbrace{\left(\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}p(y-x)(y-x)\right)}_{=0}\cdot\left(\sqrt{\sigma(f_{t})}\nabla\sqrt{\sigma(f_{t})}\right)\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{4}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\int_{0}^{t}\underbrace{\left(\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\frac{1}{2}p(y-x)(y-x)^{\otimes 2}\right)}_{=A}:\left(\sqrt{\sigma(f_{t})}\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\sigma(f_{t})}\right)\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\,{\rm d}\tau+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\frac{A:\nabla\sigma(f_{t})\otimes\nabla\sigma(f_{t})}{\sigma(f_{t})}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}\tau+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)

which proves (
2.12
) with
\epsilon_{NL}(N)=\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})
. Note also that

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(f_{t}\ln\frac{\sigma(f_{t})}{\sigma(f_{\infty})}+\ln\frac{Z(\sigma(f_{\infty}))}{Z(\sigma(f_{t}))}\right)\,{\rm d}x=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\frac{A:\nabla\sigma(f_{t})\otimes\nabla\sigma(f_{t})}{\sigma(f_{t})}\,{\rm d}x

where we used the identity
Z^{\prime}(\sigma(f_{t}))/Z(\sigma(f_{t}))=f_{t}/\sigma(f_{t})
.

Appendix A
Quantitative local limit estimates

The content of this appendix closely follows
[
Pet75
,
KL99
]
. It is included for the reader’s convenience and the sake of completeness. We provide a quantitative proof of the equivalence of ensembles for the ZRP (the argument applies to more general classes of invariant measures). The proof is based on the local limit theorem in
[
Pet75
]
and
[
KL99
, Appendix 2]
. We consider our
d
-dimensional lattice
\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}
with length
\ell
and our product measure
\vartheta^{\ell}_{\rho}
with one-site mass
\rho
, i.e.
\vartheta^{\ell}_{\rho}[\eta_{y_{0}}]=\rho
for
y_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}
. We denote by
\vartheta^{\ell,m}
this invariant measure conditioned to the hyperplan
\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}=m
. Importantly one can check (by cancelling terms in the following fraction) that
\vartheta^{\ell,m}
is independent of
\rho>0
:

\int_{\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}\varphi(\eta^{(\ell)})\,{\rm d}\vartheta^{\ell,m}(\eta^{(\ell)})=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\langle\eta^{(\ell)}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}=m}\varphi(\eta^{(\ell)})\,{\rm d}\vartheta^{\ell}_{\rho}(\eta^{(\ell)})}{\displaystyle\int_{\langle\eta^{(\ell)}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}=m}\,{\rm d}\vartheta^{\ell}_{\rho}(\eta^{(\ell)})}=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\langle\eta^{(\ell)}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}=m}\varphi(\eta^{(\ell)})\prod_{y\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\frac{1}{g(\eta_{y})!}}{\displaystyle\sum_{\langle\eta^{(\ell)}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}=m}\prod_{y\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}}\frac{1}{g(\eta_{y})!}}.

Theorem A.1
.

Given
0<m_{0}<m_{1}<\infty
and
g(\eta_{y_{0}})
a function depending only on
\eta
at one site
y_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}
, there exists a constant
C_{E}=C_{E}(m_{0},m_{1})
such that for any
m\in[m_{0},m_{1}]

\left|\mathbb{E}_{\vartheta^{\ell,m}}[g(\eta_{y_{0}})]-\mathbb{E}_{\vartheta^{\ell}_{m}}[g(\eta_{y_{0}})]\right|\leq C_{E}\ell^{-d+0}.

Proof of Theorem
A.1
.

The proof is based on establishing a Taylor expansion of
m^{\prime}\mapsto\vartheta^{\ell}_{m}(\{\langle\eta^{(\ell)}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}=m^{\prime}\})
around
m
, and then using it on
\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\ell}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}
deprived from one point in order to estimate the conditional measure applied to a one-site function. The Taylor expansion reads, for
m^{\prime}=m-k\ell^{-d}
,
k\in\{0,1,\dots,\ell^{d}\}
(the fluctuations are of the order of the number of particles at one site):

(A.1)

\vartheta^{\ell}_{m}\left(\left\{\langle\eta^{(\ell)}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}=m-k\ell^{-d}\right\}\right)=\frac{e^{-\frac{k^{2}}{c_{2}(m)\ell^{d}}}}{\sqrt{4\pi c_{2}(m)\ell^{d}}}\left[1-\mathcal{O}\left(\ell^{-d+0}\right)\right]

with
c_{2}(m):=\mathbb{E}_{n_{m}}(\eta_{0}^{2})/2
, uniformly for
m\leq m_{0}
bounded above. Before proving (
A.1
), let us show how it implies the result. By Fubini we have

\mathbb{E}_{\vartheta^{\ell,m}}[g(\eta_{y_{0}})]=\int_{\mathbb{N}}g(\eta_{y_{0}})\frac{\tilde{\vartheta}^{\ell}_{m}\left(\left\{\langle\tilde{\eta}^{(\ell)}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}=m-\eta_{y_{0}}\ell^{-d}\right\}\right)}{\vartheta^{\ell}_{m}\left(\left\{\langle\eta^{(\ell)}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}=m\right\}\right)}\,{\rm d}n_{m}(\eta_{y_{0}})

where
\tilde{\vartheta}^{\ell}_{m}
is the product measure on
\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}\setminus\{y_{0}\}
(which has
\ell^{d}-1
sites) with local mass parameter
m
(we have chosen
\rho=m
when writing the conditional measure). Hence

\mathbb{E}_{\vartheta^{\ell,m}}[g(\eta_{y_{0}})]-\mathbb{E}_{\vartheta^{\ell}_{m}}[g(\eta_{y_{0}})]=\int_{\mathbb{N}}g(\eta_{y_{0}})\left[\frac{\tilde{\vartheta}^{\ell}_{m}\left(\left\{\langle\tilde{\eta}^{(\ell)}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}=m-\eta_{y_{0}}\ell^{-d}\right\}\right)}{\vartheta^{\ell}_{m}\left(\left\{\langle\eta^{(\ell)}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}=m\right\}\right)}-1\right]\,{\rm d}n_{m}(\eta_{y_{0}}).

Since the above expansion (
A.1
) can be applied in
\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}\setminus\{y_{0}\}
(the asymptotic is the same since the number of sites is
\ell^{d}-1\sim\ell^{d}
) for the numerator, as well as to the denominator:

\frac{\tilde{\vartheta}^{\ell}_{m}\left(\left\{\langle\tilde{\eta}^{(\ell)}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}=m-\eta_{y_{0}}\ell^{-d}\right\}\right)}{\vartheta^{\ell}_{m}\left(\left\{\langle\eta^{(\ell)}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}=m\right\}\right)}=e^{-\frac{\eta_{y_{0}}^{2}}{c_{2}(m)\ell^{d/2}}}\frac{\sqrt{\ell^{d}-1}}{\ell^{d}}\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(\eta_{y_{0}}^{2}\ell^{-d+0}\right)\right]=1+\mathcal{O}\left(\ell^{-d+0}\right)

thanks to the exponential moments of
n_{m}(\eta_{y_{0}})
, which implies the desired estimate.

We now prove the Taylor expansion. The law
\mu_{S}
of the integer-valued random variable
S:=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}\eta_{y}
has
2\pi
-periodic characteristic function
\hat{\mu}_{S}(t)=(\hat{n}_{m}(t))^{\ell^{d}}
, where
\hat{n}_{m}(t)
is the
2\pi
-periodic characteristic function of
n_{m}
, given
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Given
m^{\prime}=m-k\ell^{-d}
, we apply the periodic inverse Fourier formula:

\displaystyle\vartheta^{\ell}_{m}\left(\left\{\langle\eta^{(\ell)}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}=m^{\prime}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mu_{S}(m^{\prime}\ell^{d})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{-itm^{\prime}\ell^{d}}\,{\rm d}\hat{\mu}_{S}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{-itm^{\prime}\ell^{d}}\left[\hat{n}_{m}(t)\right]^{\ell^{d}}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\ell^{d/2}}\int_{-\pi\ell^{d/2}}^{\pi\ell^{d/2}}e^{-itm^{\prime}\ell^{\frac{d}{2}}}\left[\hat{n}_{m}\left(t\ell^{-\frac{d}{2}}\right)\right]^{\ell^{d}}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\ell^{d/2}}\int_{-\pi\ell^{d/2}}^{\pi\ell^{d/2}}e^{-itm^{\prime}\ell^{\frac{d}{2}}}\mathbb{E}_{n_{m}}\left[e^{it\eta_{0}\ell^{-\frac{d}{2}}}\right]^{\ell^{d}}\,{\rm d}t.

and, given
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta\in(0,\pi)
both small, to be chosen later, we decompose the last
t
-integral into three parts:

\vartheta^{\ell}_{m}\left(\left\{\langle\eta^{(\ell)}\rangle_{\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{x}}=m^{\prime}\right\}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi\ell^{d/2}}\int_{|t|\leq\ell^{\varepsilon}}\left(\dots\right)\,{\rm d}t+\frac{1}{2\pi\ell^{d/2}}\int_{\ell^{\varepsilon}<|t|\leq\delta\ell^{d/2}}\left(\dots\right)\,{\rm d}t\\
+\frac{1}{2\pi\ell^{d/2}}\int_{\delta\ell^{d/2}<|t|\leq\pi\ell^{d/2}}\left(\dots\right)\,{\rm d}t=:I_{1}(\ell)+I_{2}(\ell)+I_{3}(\ell).

We now estimate each part successively. To estimate the first part, we observe that on the domain
\{|t|\leq\ell^{\varepsilon}\}
with
\varepsilon<d/2
, we have, by expanding the exponential and using the exponential moments of
n_{m}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{n_{m}}\left[e^{it\eta_{0}\ell^{-\frac{d}{2}}}\right]^{\ell^{d}}

\displaystyle=\left[1+itm\ell^{-\frac{d}{2}}-c_{2}t^{2}\ell^{-d}-ic_{3}t^{3}\ell^{-\frac{3d}{2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(t^{4}\ell^{-2d}\right)\right]^{\ell^{d}}

(A.2)

\displaystyle=\exp\left[itm\ell^{\frac{d}{2}}-c_{2}t^{2}-ic_{3}t^{3}\ell^{-\frac{d}{2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(t^{4}\ell^{-d}\right)\right]

with
c_{2}
defined above and
c_{3}:=\mathbb{E}_{n_{m}}(\eta_{0}^{3})/6
, so that

\displaystyle I_{1}(\ell)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\ell^{d/2}}\int_{|t|\leq\ell^{\varepsilon}}\exp\left[it(m-m^{\prime})\ell^{\frac{d}{2}}-c_{2}t^{2}-ic_{3}t^{3}\ell^{-\frac{d}{2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(t^{4}\ell^{-d}\right)\right]\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\ell^{d/2}}\int_{|t|\leq\ell^{\varepsilon}}\exp\left[itk\ell^{-\frac{d}{2}}-c_{2}t^{2}-ic_{3}t^{3}\ell^{-\frac{d}{2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(t^{4}\ell^{-d}\right)\right]\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\frac{k^{2}}{c_{2}\ell^{d}}}}{2\pi\ell^{d/2}}\int_{|t|\leq\ell^{\varepsilon}}\exp\left[-c_{2}\left(t-ik\ell^{-d/2}c_{2}^{-1}\right)^{2}-ic_{3}t^{3}\ell^{-\frac{d}{2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(t^{4}\ell^{-d}\right)\right]\,{\rm d}t

and finally using
\exp[\mathcal{O}(t^{4}\ell^{-d})]=1+\mathcal{O}(\ell^{4\varepsilon-d})
when
|t|\leq\ell^{\varepsilon}
and, by complex contour deformation and estimating the vertical parts of the contour

\int_{|t|\leq\ell^{\varepsilon}}\exp\left[-c_{2}\left(t-ik\ell^{-d/2}c_{2}^{-1}\right)^{2}-ic_{3}t^{3}\ell^{-\frac{d}{2}}\right]\,{\rm d}t\\
=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left[-c_{2}t^{2}-ic_{3}\left(t+ik\ell^{-d/2}c_{2}^{-1}\right)^{3}\ell^{-\frac{d}{2}}\right]\,{\rm d}t+\mathcal{O}\left(e^{-(c_{2}/2)\ell^{\varepsilon}}\right)

and since
\operatorname{Re}\exp[-ic_{3}\left(t+ik\ell^{-d/2}c_{2}^{-1}\right)^{3}\ell^{-\frac{d}{2}}]=1+\mathcal{O}(\ell^{2\varepsilon-d})
, we deduce by taking the real part (the loss on the power of
\ell
could easily be reduced to a logarithm)

I_{1}(\ell)=\frac{e^{-\frac{k^{2}}{c_{2}\ell^{d}}}}{2\pi\ell^{d/2}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-c_{2}t^{2}}\,{\rm d}t+\mathcal{O}(\ell^{3\varepsilon-d})\right]=\frac{e^{-\frac{k^{2}}{c_{2}\ell^{d}}}}{\sqrt{4\pi c_{2}\ell^{d}}}\left[1+\mathcal{O}(\ell^{3\varepsilon-d})\right].

To control
I_{2}(\ell)
we use again the expansion (
A.2
) and take advantage of the fact that
t
is away from zero but also less than
\delta\ell^{d/2}
which implies that
t^{4}\ell^{d}\leq\delta^{2}t^{2}
to get

\displaystyle\left|I_{2}(\ell)\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\ell^{d/2}}\left|\int_{\ell^{\varepsilon}\leq|t|\leq\delta\ell^{d/2}}\exp\left[itk\ell^{-\frac{d}{2}}-c_{2}t^{2}-ic_{3}t^{3}\ell^{-\frac{d}{2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(t^{4}\ell^{-d}\right)\right]\,{\rm d}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi\ell^{d/2}}\int_{\ell^{\varepsilon}\leq|t|\leq\delta\ell^{d/2}}\exp\left[-c_{2}t^{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\delta t^{2}\right)\right]\,{\rm d}t=\mathcal{O}\left(e^{-(c_{2}/2)\ell^{\varepsilon}}\right)

for
\delta>0
small enough. Finally to control
I_{3}(\ell)
we will use the concentration of measure of
\hat{n}_{m}(t)
. We write (undoing the previous change of variable in
t
)

(A.3)

\left|I_{3}(\ell)\right|=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left|\int_{\delta<|t|\leq\pi}e^{-itm^{\prime}\ell^{d}}\hat{\mu}_{S}(t)\,{\rm d}t\right|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\delta<|t|\leq\pi}\left|\hat{n}_{m}(t)\right|^{\ell^{d}}\,{\rm d}t.

Then we calculate (denoting
p_{m}^{j}=\sigma(m)^{j}/(g(j)!Z(\sigma(m))
the coefficients of the measure
n_{m}
on
\mathbb{N}
)

\displaystyle\left|\hat{n}_{m}(t)\right|^{2}=\left|\sum_{j\geq 0}e^{itj}p_{m}^{j}\right|^{2}=\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}\geq 0}e^{it(j_{1}-j_{2})}p_{m}^{j_{1}}p_{m}^{j_{2}}=\sum_{j\geq 0}\left(\frac{e^{itj}+e^{-itj}}{2}\right)\tilde{p}_{m}^{j}=\sum_{j\geq 0}\cos(tj)\tilde{p}_{m}^{j}

with the notation
\tilde{p}_{m}^{j}:=\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}\geq 0}p_{m}^{j_{1}}p_{m}^{j_{2}}\geq 0
(we have symmetrised the sums). Since we have
\sum_{j\geq 0}\tilde{p}_{m}^{j}=(\sum_{j\geq 1}p_{m}^{j})^{2}=1
, we deduce

\displaystyle\left|\hat{n}_{m}(t)\right|^{2}-1=\sum_{j\geq 0}\left[\cos(tj)-1\right]\tilde{p}_{m}^{j}\leq-\beta<0

when
|t|\in[\delta,\pi]
, since
p_{m}^{1}
bounded below away from zero uniformly in
m\in[m_{0},m_{1}]
, for some
\beta\in(0,1)
depending on
\delta
,
m_{0}
and
m_{1}
. Therefore (
A.3
) implies

\left|I_{3}(\ell)\right|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\delta<|t|\leq\pi}\left|\hat{n}_{m}(t)\right|^{\ell^{d}}\,{\rm d}t\leq\frac{1}{2}(1-\beta)^{\frac{\ell^{d}}{2}}

and the
I_{3}
term decays exponentially fast in
\ell
.
∎

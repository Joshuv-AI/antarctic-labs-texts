On Macdonald expansions of q q -chromatic symmetric functions and the Stanley–Stembridge Conjecture
Sean T. Griffin, Anton Mellit, Marino Romero, Kevin Weigl, Joshua Jeishing Wen

Abstract
. The Stanley–Stembridge conjecture asserts that the chromatic symmetric function of a ( 3 + 1 ) (3+1) -free graph is e e -positive. Recently, Hikita proved this conjecture by giving an explicit e e -expansion of the Shareshian–Wachs q q -chromatic refinement for unit interval graphs. Using the 𝔸 q , t \mathbb{A}_{q,t} algebra, we give an expansion of these q q -chromatic symmetric functions into Macdonald polynomials. Upon setting t = 1 t=1 , we obtain another proof of the Stanley–Stembridge conjecture and rederive Hikita’s formula. Upon setting t = 0 t=0 , we obtain an expansion into Hall–Littlewood symmetric functions.



1.
Introduction

In 1995, Stanley
[
18
]
introduced a family of symmetric functions, called the
chromatic symmetric functions
, attached to each finite simple graph which generalize the chromatic polynomial of the graph. The Stanley–Stembridge conjecture
[
19
]
, a long-standing open problem in Algebraic Combinatorics, states that the chromatic symmetric function of a
(3+1)
-free graph is
e
-positive
, meaning that it expands positively in the elementary symmetric function basis. By
[
9
]
, the conjecture can be reduced to proving the
e
-positivity for unit interval graphs. Shareshian and Wachs
[
17
]
subsequently generalized the conjecture by introducing a certain refinement, the
chromatic quasisymmetric functions
(or
q
-chromatic symmetric functions
)
\chi_{\mathbf{e}}[X;q]
, which they conjecture are also
e
-positive.

Recently, Hikita
[
14
]
proved the Stanley–Stembridge conjecture by giving a formula for the coefficients
c_{\mathbf{e},\lambda}(q)
in the
e
expansion of
\chi_{\mathbf{e}}[X;q]
as a sum of rational functions in
q
, which then specialize to nonnegative numbers upon taking
q=1
.

On the other hand, Carlsson and Mellit
[
5
]
, in their proof of the long-standing Shuffle Conjecture, proved a plethystic formula relating
\chi_{\mathbf{e}}[X;q]
to LLT polynomials, and they related the corresponding LLT polynomials to the action of a certain algebra, denoted by
\mathbb{A}_{q,t}
, on symmetric functions. In
[
4
]
the action of
\mathbb{A}_{q,t}
was explicitly computed in a certain basis, generalizing the Macdonald basis.

In this article, we show that combining these results produces an explicit formula for the expansion of the LLT polynomials in the basis of modified Macdonald polynomials
\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\lambda}[X;q,t]
. Applying the plethysm leads to an explicit expansion of
\chi_{\mathbf{e}}(q)
in the basis of integral form Macdonald polynomials. Setting
t=1
turns the Macdonald polynomials into products of elementary symmetric functions, so we obtain an
e
-expansion of
\chi_{\mathbf{e}}[X;q]
. Thus we reprove Hikita’s formula.

For the reader’s convenience, we mention the following conversion formulas between the modified Macdonald polynomials from
[
7
]
and the integral form Macdonald polynomials from
[
16
]
:

(1)

\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\mu}[(q-1)X;q,t]=q^{n(\mu^{\prime})+|\mu|}J_{\mu^{\prime}}[X;t,q^{-1}]=(-1)^{|\mu|}t^{n(\mu)}J_{\mu^{\prime}}[X;t^{-1},q]

where
n(\mu)=\sum_{i}(i-1)\mu_{i}
and
|\mu|=\sum_{i}\mu_{i}
.

The main result can be stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

We have explicit tableau formulas for the coefficients
C_{\mathbf{e},\mu}(q,t)
appearing in the expansion

(2)

\chi_{\mathbf{e}}[X;q]=\sum_{\mu}C_{\mathbf{e},\mu}(q,t)(q-1)^{-n}\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\mu}[(q-1)X;q,t],

expanding
\chi_{\mathbf{e}}(q)
in terms of plethystically evaluated modified Macdonald polynomials.
In particular, when
t=1
, we get

\chi_{\mathbf{e}}[X;q]=\sum_{\mu}C_{\mathbf{e},\mu}(q,1)\left(\prod_{i}[\mu_{i}]_{q}!\right)e_{\mu}[X].

Upon setting
q=1
, we find that the coefficient of
e_{\mu}
is nonnegative, so the Stanley–Stembridge conjecture holds. Furthermore, the coefficient of
e_{\mu}
is the same as the one found in
[
14
]
.

The coefficient of
e_{\mu}
in
\chi_{\mathbf{e}}[X;q]
can be counted by certain admissible proper colorings, see Corollary
3.8
and Remark
3.9
.

It would be interesting to see if there is any connection between this formula and the
(q,t)
-chromatic symmetric functions very recently defined by Hikita
[
15
]
.

Note that
\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\mu^{\prime}}[X;q,t]
can be expanded into LLT polynomials
[
10
]
, and the integral form
J_{\mu}[X;q,t]
can then be expanded into weighted sums of
q
-chromatic symmetric functions, see
[
13
]
and
[
5
, Example 3.10]
. In view of
(
1
)
, our formula gives
q
-chromatic symmetric functions in terms of
J_{\mu}
. Combining the two, first expanding
J_{\mu}
into
q
-chromatics and then back into
J_{\mu}
, one can obtain a curious formula for the identity matrix.

2.
Background

2.1.
Symmetric functions

Let
\Lambda[X]
be the space of symmetric functions with coefficients in
\mathbb{C}(q,t)
. Denote by
e_{n}
and
h_{n}
the elementary and complete homogeneous symmetric functions:

\displaystyle e_{n}=\sum_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{n}},

and

\displaystyle h_{n}=\sum_{i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{n}}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{n}}.

Given any integer partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{\ell}>0)
, let
e_{\lambda}\coloneqq\prod_{i}e_{\lambda_{i}}
and
h_{\lambda}\coloneqq\prod_{i}h_{\lambda_{i}}
. By standard symmetric function theory,
\{e_{\lambda}:\lambda\}
and
\{h_{\lambda}:\lambda\}
are bases of
\Lambda[X]
. We also denote by
\{s_{\lambda}:\lambda\}
the
Schur function
basis of
\Lambda[X]
. We denote by
\omega
the involution on
\Lambda[X]
which sends
h_{\lambda}
to
e_{\lambda}
or
s_{\lambda}
to
s_{\lambda^{\prime}}
, where
\lambda^{\prime}
is the conjugate of
\lambda
. See
[
16
]
for more background on symmetric function theory.

In what follows, we will also make light use of “plethystic notation” and the theory of modified Macdonald polynomials. One can see
[
5
]
or
[
7
]
, for example, for a more detailed explanation of these operations.

2.2.
The
\mathbb{A}_{q,t}
algebra

Our main method is to use the
\mathbb{A}_{q,t}
algebra, which was introduced by Carlsson and Mellit
[
5
]
to prove the Shuffle Theorem
[
11
]
and its compositional refinement
[
12
]
.

Let
V_{k}=\Lambda[X]\otimes\mathbb{C}[y_{1},\dots,y_{k}]
.
We will only need the following operators appearing in the
\mathbb{A}_{q,t}
algebra.

Definition 2.1
.

For any expression
F(y_{i},y_{i+1})
, let

T_{i}F=\frac{(q-1)y_{i}F(y_{i},y_{i+1})+(y_{i+1}-qy_{i})F(y_{i+1},y_{i})}{y_{i+1}-y_{i}}.

Define
d_{+}:V_{k}\rightarrow V_{k+1}
by setting

d_{+}F[X]=T_{1}\cdots T_{k}F[X+(q-1)y_{k+1}],

and define
d_{-}:V_{k+1}\rightarrow V_{k}
by setting

d_{-}F[X]=-F[X-(q-1)y_{k}]\mathrm{Exp}\left[-y_{k}^{-1}X\right]\Big|_{y_{k}^{-1}},

where
\mathrm{Exp}[X]=\sum_{n\geq 0}h_{n}[X]
is the plethystic exponential, and
f\big|_{y_{k}^{-1}}
means take the coefficient of
y_{k}^{-1}
in
f
.

2.3.
q
-chromatic symmetric functions

Given a finite graph
G
with vertices
V=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
and edges
E
, a
proper coloring of
G
is a function
\kappa:V\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
\kappa(v)\neq\kappa(w)
whenever
vw\in E
. Given a proper coloring
\kappa
(and the ordering on
V
), the
ascents
of
\kappa
are

\mathrm{asc}(\kappa)\coloneqq\#\{(i<j):v_{i}v_{j}\in E,\,\kappa(v_{i})<\kappa(v_{j})\}.

The
q
-chromatic symmetric function
introduced by Shareshian and Wachs
[
17
]
is given by

\chi_{G}[X;q]\coloneqq\sum_{\kappa}q^{\mathrm{asc}(\kappa)}x^{\kappa},

where the sum is over all proper colorings of
G
and
x^{\kappa}=\prod_{i}x_{\kappa(v_{i})}
.

A
unit interval graph
is a particular type of graph whose
q
-chromatic symmetric function has particularly nice properties. One equivalent way of defining such a graph is as follows: Let
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
\mathbf{e}:[n]\to\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}
be a
reverse hessenberg function
, which is a function that is weakly increasing such that
\mathbf{e}(i)<i
for all
i
. Then the unit interval graph
\Gamma=\Gamma_{\mathbf{e}}
is the graph with vertex set
V=[n]
and edges
\{ij:\mathbf{e}(j)<i<j\}
. We will write
\chi_{\mathbf{e}}[X;q]=\chi_{\Gamma_{\mathbf{e}}}[X;q]
.

Before Hikita’s proof of
e
-positivity, it was known that
\chi_{\mathbf{e}}[X;q]
is a Schur positive symmetric function by Shareshian and Wachs
[
17
]
who refined a formula for
\chi_{\mathbf{e}}[X;1]
of Gasharov
[
8
]
. Brosnan and Chow
[
3
]
subsequently proved that
\chi_{\mathbf{e}}[X;q]
is the Frobenius characteristic of the dot action of
S_{n}
on the singular cohomology ring of a regular semisimple Hessenberg variety (up to tensoring by the sign representation), a conjecture that was posed by Shareshian and Wachs, giving another proof of Schur positivity.

A Dyck path
D=W^{a_{1}}S^{b_{1}}\cdots W^{a_{\ell}}S^{b_{\ell}}
is a sequence of West and South unit lattice steps satisfying

(1)

a_{1}-b_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}-b_{r}\geq 0
for all
r
, and

(2)

a_{1}+\cdots+a_{\ell}=b_{1}+\cdots+b_{\ell}=n
, for which we then say that
D\in D_{n}
is a Dyck path in the
n\times n
square.

We visualize a Dyck path as a lattice path starting at
(n,n)
and taking West
(-1,0)
and South
(0,-1)
steps according to the word
D
, which stays weakly above the diagonal of the
n\times n
grid by (1) and ends at
(0,0)
by (2).

Let
\mathbf{e}_{D}(i)
be the number of South steps preceding the
i
-th West step of
D
. In this way, we associate to every
D\in D_{n}
a reverse Hessenberg function
\mathbf{e}_{D}
of length
n
. Any reverse Hessenberg function arises from a unique Dyck path in this way. So we will identify Dyck paths with reverse Hessenberg functions and write
\mathbf{e}\in D_{n}
. See Figure
1
for
D
and the unit interval graph corresponding to
\mathbf{e}=(0,1,1,2,2,3,4)
.

Figure 1.
The Dyck path
D
and unit interval graph
\Gamma_{\mathbf{e}}
associated to the reverse hessenberg function
\mathbf{e}=(0,1,1,2,2,3,4)
.
Cells are labeled by the edges,
ij
, present in the graph.

Carlsson and Mellit showed that the
q
-chromatic symmetric function can be obtained from
d_{+}
and
d_{-}
by first writing

F_{D}[X;q]\coloneqq d_{-}^{b_{\ell}}d_{+}^{a_{\ell}}\cdots d_{-}^{b_{1}}d_{+}^{a_{1}}(1),

then setting

\chi_{D}[X;q]\coloneqq(q-1)^{-n}F_{D}[(q-1)X].

Proposition 2.2

(
[
5
, Theorem 4.4
+
Proposition 3.5]
)
.

For any reverse Hessenberg function
\mathbf{e}
with the corresponding Dyck path
D\in D_{n}
, we have

\chi_{D}[X;q]=\chi_{\mathbf{e}}[X;q].

We will write
F_{\mathbf{e}}
for the corresponding function
F_{D}
where
D
is such that
\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{e}_{D}
.
For a reference on LLT polynomials and
q
-chromatic symmetric functions, see
[
2
]
.

2.4.
Macdonald symmetric functions

The modified Macdonald polynomials
\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\mu}[X;q,t]=\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\mu}[X]
specialize to modified homogeneous symmetric functions:

\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\mu}[X;q,1]=(q;q)_{\mu}h_{\mu}\left[\frac{X}{1-q}\right],

where
(q;q)_{\mu}=(q;q)_{\mu_{1}}\cdots(q;q)_{\mu_{\ell(\mu)}}=h_{\mu}[1/(1-q)]^{-1}
, and
(q;t)_{r}=(1-q)(1-qt)\cdots(1-qt^{r-1})
is the Pochhammer symbol.
Therefore,

\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\mu}[(q-1)X;q,1]=(-1)^{|\mu|}(q;q)_{\mu}e_{\mu}[X],

and as a consequence, the elementary basis expansion of
\chi_{\mathbf{e}}[X;q]
can be found by the expansion of
F_{\mathbf{e}}
in the basis
\{h_{\mu}[X/(1-q)]\}_{\mu}
.

Our goal will be to first write
F_{\mathbf{e}}
in terms of the modified Macdonald basis:

F_{\mathbf{e}}[X;q]=\sum_{\mu}C_{\mathbf{e},\mu}(q,t)~\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\mu}[X;q,t].

Since
\chi_{\mathbf{e}}[X;q]
has no
t
variable, we must then also have

\displaystyle F_{\mathbf{e}}[X;q]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu}C_{\mathbf{e},\mu}(q,1)~\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\mu}[X;q,1]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu}C_{\mathbf{e},\mu}(q,1)(q;q)_{\mu}h_{\mu}\left[\frac{X}{1-q}\right].

From here, it follows that

(3)

\chi_{\mathbf{e}}[X;q]=\sum_{\mu}C_{\mathbf{e},\mu}(q,1)(1-q)^{-|\mu|}(q;q)_{\mu}e_{\mu}[X],

where we have used that
h_{\mu}[-X]=(-1)^{|\mu|}\omega(h_{\mu})[X]=(-1)^{|\mu|}e_{\mu}
.

The Stanley–Stembridge Conjecture then follows from combining
(
3
)
with the next proposition.

Proposition 2.3
.

For any reverse Hessenberg function
\mathbf{e}
of length
n
and partition
\mu
of
n
,
C_{\mathbf{e},\mu}(q,1)
is positive when
q=1
.
Furthermore,
C_{\mathbf{e},\mu}(q,1)(1-q)^{-|\mu|}(q;q)_{\mu}
equals the formula for the coefficient of
e_{\mu}
given in
[
14
]
.

2.5.
The action of
d_{\pm}
on
I_{\lambda,w}

We now think of partitions as being given by a sum of cells. We say
(i,j)\in\lambda
if
0\leq j<\ell(\lambda)
and
0\leq i<\lambda_{j+1}
. To simplify notation, we will then write

\lambda=\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}q^{i}t^{j}.

For a given
\lambda
, we say
x=q^{r}t^{s}
is an
addable cell
if
\lambda+x=\mu
is a partition, and we will then write
x\in{\mathrm{Add}}(\lambda)
. The cells in
\lambda
that are in the same row as
x
are denoted by
\mathcal{R}_{\lambda,x}
; and similarly, the cells in
\lambda
that are in the same column as
x
are denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda,x}
. For a given cell
c\in\lambda
, let
a_{\lambda}(c)
denote the arm length of
c
in
\lambda
(that is, the number of cells to its right), and let
l_{\lambda}(c)
be its leg length (that is, the number of cells above
c
in the French convention).

If
x\in{\mathrm{Add}}(\lambda)
, we let

d_{\lambda,x}=\prod_{c\in\mathcal{R}_{\lambda,x}}\frac{q^{a_{\lambda}(c)}-t^{l_{\lambda}(c)+1}}{q^{a_{\lambda}(c)+1}-t^{l_{\lambda}(c)+1}}\prod_{c\in\mathcal{C}_{\lambda,x}}\frac{q^{a_{\lambda}(c)+1}-t^{l_{\lambda}(c)}}{q^{a_{\lambda}(c)+1}-t^{l_{\lambda}(c)+1}}.

These terms appear in the Pieri coefficients for Macdonald polynomials
[
6
]
:

e_{1}\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\lambda}[X]=\sum_{x\in{\mathrm{Add}}(\lambda)}d_{\lambda,x}\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\lambda+x}[X].

A basis for
V_{k}
is given by certain elements of the form
I_{\lambda,w}
, where
w=(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})
is a sequence of cells in
\lambda
which form a horizontal strip, meaning no two cells are in the same column and each row is connected and right justified in
\lambda
. The list is written such that
\lambda-w_{1}-w_{2}-\cdots-w_{s}
forms a partition for each
s\leq k
, meaning that if we place
i
in the cell
w_{k-i+1}
, we get a standard tableau of the horizontal strip
w_{1}+\cdots+w_{k}
.

In
[
4
]
, Carlsson, Gorsky, and Mellit construct an action of
\mathbb{A}_{q,t}
on the direct sum of the (localized) equivariant
K
-theory of parabolic flag Hilbert schemes,
K_{\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\mathrm{PFH}_{n,n-k})
; moreover, they show that

\bigoplus_{k}K_{\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\mathrm{PFH}_{n,n-k})\cong\bigoplus_{k}V_{k}

as
\mathbb{A}_{q,t}
-modules.
The basis
\{I_{\lambda,w}\}
corresponds to the distinguished basis of
K_{\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\mathrm{PFH}_{n,n-k})
given by the classes of torus fixed points. It will not be important to know explicitly what the elements
I_{\lambda,w}
are. Instead, we rely on the following three facts.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
4
]
)
.

Given a partition
\lambda
and sequence
w=(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})
forming a horizontal strip (as described above), we have

(4)

\displaystyle I_{\lambda,\emptyset}=\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\lambda}[X]/\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\lambda}[-1]\in V_{0},

(5)

\displaystyle d_{-}I_{\lambda,wx}=I_{\lambda,w},\text{ and }

(6)

\displaystyle d_{+}I_{\lambda,w}=-q^{k}\sum_{x\in{\mathrm{Add}}(\lambda)}xd_{\lambda,x}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{x-tw_{i}}{x-qtw_{i}}\right)I_{\lambda+x,xw}.

We will also need the following identity:

(7)

\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\mu}[-1]=(-1)^{|\mu|}\prod_{(i,j)\in\mu}q^{i}t^{j}.

3.
Main proof

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
using the
\mathbb{A}_{q,t}
algebra.

Notation 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{e}
be a reverse Hessenberg function.
If
\mathbf{e}(j)<i<j,
then we will write
i\prec j
. Note that
\prec
is not a partial order since it is not necessarily transitive.

For example, in the case
\mathbf{e}=(0,1,1,2,2,3,4)
considered in Figure
1
, the pairs
i\prec j
are given by the edges of
\Gamma_{\mathbf{e}}
. Namely,
2\prec 3
,
3\prec 4
,
3\prec 5
and so on. This function corresponds to
\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{e}_{D}
for the Dyck path
D=WSWWSWWSWSWSSS
, and the pairs
i\prec j
are in bijection with the cells in the
7\times 7
grid between the diagonal and
D
.

Let
{\mathrm{SYT}}_{\mu}
be the set of standard Young tableaux of shape
\mu
.
For a given
j
, let
T_{<j}
be the standard tableau given by the entries smaller than
j
. We will also let
T_{\prec j}
be the skew-shaped tableau with entries
i
satisfying
i\prec j
.
Similarly, define
T_{\leq j}
and
T_{\preceq j}
to include the entry
j
.
We will say that a skew shape is
strict
if its entries are increasing along rows and columns.
A strict skew tableau
T
gives a sequence
w(T)=(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})
of cells, where
w_{1}
is the cell with the largest entry,
w_{2}
is the cell with the second largest entry, and so on until we reach the cell
w_{k}
with the smallest entry.
For a strict skew tableau, we will also write
{\mathrm{sh}}(T)
to denote the skew diagram with no fillings.

First, we write

F_{\mathbf{e}}[X;q]=d_{-}^{r_{n}}d_{+}d_{-}^{r_{n-1}}\cdots d_{+}d_{-}^{r_{1}}d_{+}(I_{\emptyset,\emptyset}),

where
r_{1}+\cdots+r_{j}=\mathbf{e}(j+1)
,
r_{1}+\cdots+r_{n}=n
. Note that
\mathbf{e}(1)=0
.
Suppose that after we apply
d_{+}d_{-}^{r_{j-1}}\cdots d_{-}^{r_{1}}d_{+}
we arrive at a term with
I_{\lambda,w}
. Applying
d_{-}^{r_{j}}
eliminates
r_{j}
elements from
w
giving
w^{\prime}
, leaving only the last
j-\mathbf{e}(j+1)
cells which were added to
\lambda
; and
j-\mathbf{e}(j+1)
is the number of
i\prec j+1
. Applying
d_{+}
then adds a cell
x
to
\lambda
so that the sequence
xw
forms a strict horizontal strip.
We interpret this combinatorially in the following way.

Let

(8)

A^{\lambda,w}_{x}\coloneqq\prod_{c\in\mathcal{R}_{\lambda,x}}\frac{q^{a_{\lambda}(c)}-t^{l_{\lambda}(c)+1}}{q^{a_{\lambda}(c)+1}-t^{l_{\lambda}(c)+1}}\,\times\,\prod_{c\in\mathcal{C}_{\lambda,x}}\frac{q^{a_{\lambda}(c)+1}-t^{l_{\lambda}(c)}}{q^{a_{\lambda}(c)+1}-t^{l_{\lambda}(c)+1}}\,\times\,\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{x-tw_{i}}{x-qtw_{i}}.

Let
{\mathrm{SYT}}^{\mathbf{e}}_{\lambda}
, be the set of standard tableaux
T
such that for each
i
,
T_{\preceq i}
is a horizontal strip.

Remark 3.2
.

We may alternatively define
{\mathrm{SYT}}^{\mathbf{e}}_{\lambda}
as follows. Define a partial order on
[n]
, called the
unit interval order
, by
i\ll j
if
i\leq\mathbf{e}(j)
. Then
{\mathrm{SYT}}^{\mathbf{e}}_{\lambda}
is the set of standard tableaux whose rows increase with respect to
<
and whose columns increase with respect to
\ll
.

Let
T^{(i)}\coloneqq{\mathrm{sh}}(T_{\leq i})-{\mathrm{sh}}(T_{<i})
be the cell containing
i
, and let

A_{T}^{\mathbf{e}}(q,t)\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{n}A^{{\mathrm{sh}}(T_{<i}),w(T_{\prec i})}_{T^{(i)}}.

Lastly, note that

\prod_{i=1}^{n}-q^{|T_{\prec i}|}T^{(i)}=q^{\binom{n}{2}-|\mathbf{e}|}\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\lambda}[-1],

where
|\mathbf{e}|=\sum_{i}\mathbf{e}(i)
. Proposition
2.4
then tells us that

F_{\mathbf{e}}[X;q]=\sum_{\lambda}\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\lambda}[X]/\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\lambda}[-1]~\left(\sum_{T\in{\mathrm{SYT}}^{\mathbf{e}}_{\lambda}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}-q^{|T_{\prec i}|}T^{(i)}A^{{\mathrm{sh}}(T_{<i}),w(T_{\prec i})}_{T^{(i)}}\right).

This gives us the next proposition.

Proposition 3.3
.

We have the following expansions of
F_{\mathbf{e}}
and
\chi_{\mathbf{e}}
:

(9)

\displaystyle F_{\mathbf{e}}[X;q]

\displaystyle=q^{\binom{n}{2}-|\mathbf{e}|}~\sum_{\lambda}~\left(\sum_{T\in{\mathrm{SYT}}^{\mathbf{e}}_{\lambda}}A_{T}^{\mathbf{e}}(q,t)\right)\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\lambda}[X;q,t],

(10)

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathbf{e}}[X;q]

\displaystyle=(q-1)^{-n}q^{\binom{n}{2}-|\mathbf{e}|}~\sum_{\lambda}~\left(\sum_{T\in{\mathrm{SYT}}^{\mathbf{e}}_{\lambda}}A_{T}^{\mathbf{e}}(q,t)\right)\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\lambda}[(q-1)X;q,t].

Even though we will not use the following version of these expansions, we note here that there is an alternate expression for Proposition
3.3

which we expect to have a geometric interpretation.

Proposition 3.4
.

We have

F_{\mathbf{e}}[X;q]=\sum_{\mu}C_{\mathbf{e},\mu}(q,t)~\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\mu}[X],

where

1.1
Intrinsic ultracontractivity in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

The Hamiltonian corresponding to a quantum system in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

We present a growth condition of

\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\gamma(\varepsilon)>0\ :\ -\ln\left(\varphi(x)\right)\ \leq\ \varepsilon q(x)+\gamma(\varepsilon)

1.3
Motivation of this article

This article aims to reduce the growth conditions on the potential required for intrinsic ultracontractivity while clarifying the analytical mechanism underlying this property.

We build upon the recent results of
[
7
]
, where intrinsic ultracontractivity of the Schrödinger semigroup
\mathrm{e}^{-tH}
on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
was established under growth conditions involving a product of iterated logarithms. In contrast, we show that these product structures are a consequence of the proof method and not of the underlying setting.

More precisely, we prove intrinsic ultracontractivity for Schrödinger operators

H=-\Delta+q(x)
with non-negative potentials satisfying

d\left(\int_{0}^{|x|}Q(t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\left(\ln^{(m)}\left(\int_{0}^{|x|}Q(t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\right)^{k}\leq q(x)\leq Q(|x|),

(6)

for sufficiently large
|x|
where
d\in(0,1]
,
k>0
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and
Q
is an auxiliary function satisfying

Q^{\prime}(r)/Q(r)^{-3/2}\ \to\ 0

for
r\to\infty
. Compared to
[
7
]
the lower boundary on
q
is lower and significantly simpler,
where the product

\displaystyle{\prod_{p=0}^{m-1}\ln^{(p)}(t)}

appearing in earlier works is no longer required, and, in particular, the parameter
k\in(0,1)
becomes admissible.

The key insight of this paper is that this simplification is not accidental. It stems from a different analytical viewpoint on intrinsic ultracontractivity. Rather than following the classical route, suggested for instance in
[
4
]
, we include a
duality-based approach. Weighted Lebesgue spaces
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and weighted Schrödinger semigroups

\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}=\frac{1}{\varphi}\mathrm{e}^{-tH}\varphi

are still introduced. However, we show that our class of potentials
q
imply the weighted Schrödinger semigroup operators
to map continuously from
\mathrm{L}^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Then the self-adjointness of
\mathrm{e}^{-tH}
in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
gives an intrinsic ultracontractivity
in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

In this framework, the transition from
\mathrm{L}^{1}_{\mu}
to
\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\mu}
captures the essential regularizing effect of the semigroup, while the stronger
\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\mu}\to\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
bound follows naturally by duality.
While logarithmic Sobolev inequalities still play a role in the analysis, the present approach avoids several technical layers inherent to the classical method. As a result, the proof becomes both shorter and more transparent, and it reveals that the restrictive logarithmic product conditions previously imposed are not a fundamental feature of intrinsic ultracontractivity, but rather an artifact of the
\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
strategy.

is a probability measure. Let us include a formally defined auxiliary function

N\colon[0,t]\to\mathbb{R}
that we will define later with the Logarithmic Sobolev inequalities of
Lemma
2.3
in mind. The derivative of

\ln\left(\ \mathrm{e}^{-N(s)}\|\mathrm{e}^{-s\tilde{H}}u\|_{p(s),\mu}\ \right)=-N(s)+\frac{1}{p(s)}\ln\|\mathrm{e}^{-s\tilde{H}}u\|_{p(s),\mu}^{p(s)}

is equal to

-N^{\prime}(s)-\frac{p^{\prime}(s)}{p(s)}\ \ln\left\|\mathrm{e}^{-s\tilde{H}}u\right\|_{p(s),\mu}+\frac{1}{p(s)}\ \left\|\mathrm{e}^{-s\tilde{H}}u\right\|_{p(s),\mu}^{-p(s)}\ \frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}s}\left\|\mathrm{e}^{-s\tilde{H}}u\right\|_{p(s),\mu}^{p(s)}.

Hence, we end up with

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}s}\ \ln\left(\ \mathrm{e}^{-N(s)}\|\mathrm{e}^{-s\tilde{H}}u\|_{p(s),\mu}\ \right)=

\displaystyle\frac{p^{\prime}(s)}{p(s)\left\|\mathrm{e}^{-s\tilde{H}}u\right\|_{p(s),\mu}^{p(s)}}\left\{\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathrm{e}^{-s\tilde{H}}u(x))^{p(s)}\ \ln\left(\mathrm{e}^{-s\tilde{H}}u(x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)-\frac{p(s)}{p^{\prime}(s)}\ \left\langle\tilde{H}\mathrm{e}^{-s\tilde{H}}u,(\mathrm{e}^{-s\tilde{H}}u)^{p(s)-1}\right\rangle_{\mu}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{N^{\prime}(s)p(s)}{p^{\prime}(s)}\ \left\|\mathrm{e}^{-s\tilde{H}}u\right\|_{p(s),\mu}^{p(s)}-\left\|\mathrm{e}^{-s\tilde{H}}u\right\|_{p(s),\mu}^{p(s)}\ \ln\left\|\mathrm{e}^{-s\tilde{H}}u\right\|_{p(s),\mu}\right\}

for every
s\in[0,t]
where
p(s)/p^{\prime}(s)=s+t>0
. We use the
Logarithmic Sobolev inequalities of
\tilde{H}
in Rosen’s lemma
2.3
where we use

p(s)/p^{\prime}(s)
as
\varepsilon
and define

N(s)=2\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\beta(\frac{p(r)}{p^{\prime}(r)})}{p(r)^{2}}\ p^{\prime}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r=2\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\beta\left(r+t\right)}{\left(r+t\right)\left(1+r/t\right)}\,\mathrm{d}r

(13)

for
s\in[0,t]
with
\beta(r+t)=\frac{r+t}{2}-\frac{n}{4}\ln\left(\frac{r+t}{2}\right)+g_{k,m}(\frac{2^{\frac{3}{2}}}{d(r+t)})+C
. Then

N^{\prime}(s)\ \frac{p(s)}{p^{\prime}(s)}=\frac{2\beta(p(s)/p^{\prime}(s))}{p(s)}

holds for every
s\in[0,t]
. Furthermore
g_{k,m}(\frac{2^{\frac{3}{2}}}{d(r+t)})
is bounded
for
r\in[0,t]
since

0<g_{k,m}\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{dt}\right)\leq g_{k,m}\left(\frac{2^{\frac{3}{2}}}{d(r+t)}\right)\leq g_{k,m}\left(\frac{2^{\frac{3}{2}}}{dt}\right)<\infty

holds true due to
g_{k,m}
being strictly monotone increasing on
[0,\infty)
. Therefore the integral

\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\beta\left(r+t\right)}{\left(r+t\right)\left(1+r/t\right)}\,\mathrm{d}r

exists for every
s\in[0,t]
which makes
N
a well defined function. Having defined our auxiliary function

N
we simply use Rosen’s lemma
2.3
to conclude

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}s}\ \ln\left(\ \mathrm{e}^{-N(s)}\|\mathrm{e}^{-s\tilde{H}}u\|_{p(s),\mu}\ \right)\leq 0

(14)

for every
s\in[0,t]
. Therefore
\left\{s\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{-N(s)}\|\mathrm{e}^{-s\tilde{H}}u\|_{p(s),\mu}\right\}

is monotone decreasing on
[0,t]
and hence

\mathrm{e}^{-N(t)}\|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u\|_{2,\mu}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-N(t)}\|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u\|_{p(t),\mu}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-N(0)}\|u\|_{p(0),\mu}=\|u\|_{1,\mu}

is true. Let us summarize this first result of the proof. For
u\in D(\tilde{H})\cap\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

being non-negative almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have shown

\|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u\|_{2,\mu}\ \leq\ \underbrace{\mathrm{e}^{N(t)}}_{=C_{t}}\|u\|_{1,\mu}.

(15)

ii.)

Let
u\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be non-negative almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

\varphi u\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is implied and hence there exists a sequence
(v_{k})\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

such that every
v_{k}
is non-negative almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
v_{k}\to\varphi u
holds in

\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
k\to\infty
. We define
u_{k}=v_{k}/\varphi
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then
\varphi u_{k}=v_{k}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset D(H)
implies
u_{k}\in D(\tilde{H})
by definition and
furthermore
v_{k}/\varphi\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is satisfied due to the compact support of
v_{k}

in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the fact that
\varphi
is continuous and strictly positive in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Please see
Lemma 2.1 and Lemma 2.2 in
[
7
]
. Also

\left\|u_{k}-u\right\|_{2,\mu}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\varphi(x)u_{k}(x)-\varphi(x)u(x)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|v_{k}(x)-\varphi(x)u(x)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

converges to
0
for
k\to\infty
which implies that
\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u_{k}
converges to
\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u

in
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Notice that
u_{k}
also converges to
u
in
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
since

\mu
is a probability measure and hence convergence in
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
implies convergence in

\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We summarize that

\|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u\|_{2,\mu}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u_{k}\|_{2,\mu}\leq C_{t}\lim_{k\to\infty}\|u_{k}\|_{1,\mu}=C_{t}\|u\|_{1,\mu}

(16)

is satisfied for every
u\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
being non-negative almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

iii.)

Let
u\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be non-negative almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Mind that

\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is true but we can not confirm

\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
at this point. We define

u_{k}=1_{\{u\leq k\}}u\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Then
(u_{k})
converges pointwise to
u
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
|u_{k}(x)-u(x)|\leq u(x)
. Hence

(u_{k})
converges to
u
in
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by Lebesgue’s theorem. Notice that

\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u_{k}\to\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u
in
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is implied
since
\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}
is a contraction in
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. But that does not necessarily imply
convergence in
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Please mind that

\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u_{k}\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is contained in

\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
(\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u_{k})

is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by (
16
).
Hence the limit
h=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u_{k}
is contained in
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Then
h\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is implied since
\mu
is a probability measure. From

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u-h\|_{1,\mu}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u-\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u_{k}\|_{1,\mu}+\|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u_{k}-h\|_{1,\mu}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u-\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u_{k}\|_{1,\mu}+\|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u_{k}-h\|_{2,\mu}

we conclude
\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u=h
and therefore
\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

with

\|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u\|_{2,\mu}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u_{k}\|_{2,\mu}\leq C_{t}\lim_{k\to\infty}\|u_{k}\|_{1,\mu}=C_{t}\|u\|_{1,\mu}

(17)

for every
u\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
being non-negative almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

iv.)

For
u\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
being real-valued but not necessarily non-negative almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

we write
u=u^{+}-u^{-}
for
0\leq u^{+}\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
0\leq u^{-}\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

for the positive and negative part of
u
. We conclude to

\|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u\|_{2,\mu}\leq\|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u^{+}\|_{2,\mu}+\|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u^{-}\|_{2,\mu}\leq C_{t}\left(\|u^{+}\|_{1,\mu}+\|u^{-}\|_{1,\mu}\right)=C_{t}\|u\|_{1,\mu}.

v.)

For a general
u\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we argue similar as above by writing

u=\operatorname{Re}u+\mathrm{i}\operatorname{Im}u
with

\operatorname{Re}u
and
\operatorname{Im}u
being real-valued in
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Hence
the weighted Schrödinger semigroup operators
\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}
map
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

into
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
continuously.

Remark 4.1
.

Notice that the same arguments can be used to prove that
\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}
maps

\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
continuously for any

1\leq q_{1}<q_{2}<\infty
by using

p(s)=\frac{s}{t}q_{2}+\left(1-\frac{s}{t}\right)q_{1}

for
s\in[0,t]
. Then
p
is strictly monotone increasing with
p(0)=q_{1}
and
p(t)=q_{2}
.

4.2
Duality argumentation for

\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)

The adjoint
S(t)
of

\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
maps

\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
continuously with

\left\|S(t)u\right\|_{\infty,\mu}\leq C_{t}\|u\|_{2,\mu}

for every
u\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We have to show that both operators

S(t)
and
\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}
coincide in
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Therefore let
u,v\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Please remember that

\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is contained in
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

and

S(t)v\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

because of
\mu
being a probability measure. Therefore we argue that

\langle u,\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}v\rangle_{\mu}=\langle\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u,v\rangle_{\mu}=\langle u,S(t)v\rangle_{\mu}

(18)

holds where we used the self-adjointness of
\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}
in
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for the
first equation and understand
u
as an element of
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in the second equation.
The operators
S(t)
and
\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}
coincide in
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

which implies

\left|\mathrm{e}^{-tH}(\varphi u)(x)\right|\ \leq\ C_{t}\varphi(x)\|\varphi u\|_{2}

(19)

for every
u\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by the definition of
\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}
. Finally setting

u=v/\varphi
for
v\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
proves Theorem
3.1
.

5
Conclusion

In this article, we have established the intrinsic ultracontractivity of the Schrödinger semigroup

\mathrm{e}^{-tH}
for a broad class of non-negative potentials
q
on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

This present work presents a new proof for the intrinsic ultracontractivity of
\mathrm{e}^{-tH}

based on a duality argument which offers even better results than
[
7
]
.
In particular, we show that the weighted Schrödinger semigroup
map continuously from
\mathrm{L}^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

which implies intrinsic ultracontractivity follows by self-adjointness.

This methodology not only simplifies the growth assumptions imposed on the potential
q
, thereby
allowing for a wider range of parameters
k>0
, but also provides a more robust analytical framework
for investigating the contractive properties of weighted Schrödinger semigroups.

Appendix A
Agmon’s version of the comparison principle

Let
h
denote the quadratic form introduced in Section
2
, and let
H
be the associated self-adjoint operator acting on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Definition A.1
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set.

a)

The
local form domain
of
h
on
U
is defined by

D^{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}(h):=\left\{u\in\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)\;\middle|\;\chi u\in D(h)\text{ for all }\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U)\right\}.

(20)

In other words,
D^{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}(h)
consists of all functions that belong locally to the form domain of
h
.

b)

A function
u
is called a
supersolution
of
H
in
U
with energy
E
if

u\in D^{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}(h)
and

h(u,\xi)-E\langle u,\xi\rangle\geq 0

(21)

for all non-negative test functions
\xi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U)
.
Here, the expression
h(u,\xi)
is understood in the weak sense as

h(u,\xi)=\langle\nabla u,\nabla\xi\rangle+\langle q(x)u,\xi\rangle,

even if
u\notin D(h)
.

c)

A function
u
is called a
subsolution
of
H
in
U
with energy
E
if

u\in D^{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}(h)
and

h(u,\xi)-E\langle u,\xi\rangle\leq 0

(22)

for all non-negative test functions
\xi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U)
.

The following result is a variant of the comparison principle due to Agmon.
It corresponds to Theorem 2.7 in
[
6
]
.

Theorem A.2

(Agmon-type comparison principle)
.

Let
R>0
and let
u
be a strictly positive supersolution of
H
with energy
E

in a neighborhood of infinity, that is, in

\Omega_{R}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid|x|>R\}.

Assume that
v
is a subsolution of
H
in
\Omega_{R}
with the same energy
E
and that

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\int_{N\leq|x|\leq\alpha N}|v(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)=0

(23)

for some
\alpha>1
.

If there exist constants
\delta>0
and
C\geq 0
such that

v(x)\leq Cu(x)

(24)

for almost every
x
with
R<|x|\leq R+\delta
, then the inequality

v(x)\leq Cu(x)

holds for almost every
x\in\Omega_{R}
.

Remark A.3
.

i.)

In its original form
[
1
]

Agmon assumes that the supersolution
u
as well as the subsolution
v
are both continuous in
\overline{\Omega_{R}}
.
However, this additional assumption of
u
and
v
being continuous are only made to guarantee that
condition (
24
) holds for a constant
C=c_{2}/c_{1}
with

c_{1}=\inf_{R\leq|x|\leq R+\delta}u(x)
and
c_{2}=\sup_{R\leq|x|\leq R+\delta}\left|v(x)\right|
and arbitrary
\delta>0
.
Please see Remark 2.9 on page 12 in
[
6
]
for further details.

ii.)

Furthermore, for
v\in\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega_{R}\right)
condition (
23
) is already implied.

Please notice that we consider Schrödinger potentials
q
such that
|x|^{2}<q(x)
holds for every
R<|x|
. Using Theorem
A.2
we are able to determine the asymptotic behaviour of
the ground state
\varphi
of
H
.

Corollary A.4
.

Let
q
satisfy
|x|^{2}\leq q(x)
for
R<|x|
and a given radius
R>0
. Furthermore, let

E_{0}=\min\left(\sigma(H)\right)\geq 0
be the ground state energy of
H
with a continuous and
strictly positive ground state
\varphi
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\varphi(x)\ \leq\ C\mathrm{e}^{-|x|}

(25)

holds for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

i.)

We use Theorem
A.2
. Therefore, please mind that

\varphi\in D(H)\subseteq\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
is a subsolution of
H
with the energy
E_{0}

in
\Omega_{r}
for any radius
r>0
. We will use
|x|^{2}\leq q(x)
for
R<|x|

to define a positive and continuous supersolution
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
of
H
with
E_{0}
in
\Omega_{\tilde{R}}

for a radius
\tilde{R}>R
.
Then
\varphi
satisfies condition (
23
) since it is contained in
\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.
Furthermore, due to the continuity of
\varphi
and
u
condtion (
24
) is true by
Remark
A.3
.
So there exists a constant
K\geq 0
with

\varphi(x)\ \leq\ Ku(x)

for every
x\in\Omega_{\tilde{R}}
.

ii.)

We are concerned with solutions to the following eigenvalue inequality

0\ \leq\ \left(-\Delta+|x|^{2}\right)u(x)-E_{0}u(x)

(26)

for a radius
\tilde{R}>0
and
|x|\geq\tilde{R}
. The idea now is to find a
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
such that

u(x)=\exp\left(-|x|^{\beta}\right)
is a solution to (
26
).
With a slight abuse of notation let us write
u(r)=\exp\left(-r^{\beta}\right)

and consider the radial Schrödinger inequality

\displaystyle 0\

\displaystyle\leq\ -u^{\prime\prime}(r)-\frac{n-1}{r}u^{\prime}(r)+r^{2}u(r)-E_{0}u(r)

\displaystyle=\ u(r)\left(-\beta^{2}r^{2(\beta-1)}+\beta\left(\beta-2+n\right)r^{\beta-2}+r^{2}-E_{0}\right).

We choose
\beta=1
such that
u(x)=\mathrm{e}^{-|x|}
satisfies (
26
) for

|x|\geq\left(1+E_{0}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

iii.)

Let
\tilde{R}=\max\left(R,\left(1+E_{0}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)
and

\xi\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\Omega_{\tilde{R}}\right)
be non-negative. Then

0\leq\langle\left(-\Delta+|x|^{2}-E_{0}\right)u,\xi\rangle\leq\langle\left(-\Delta+q(x)-E_{0}\right)u,\xi\rangle=h(u,\xi)-E_{0}\langle u,\xi\rangle

(27)

follows and hence there exists a constant
K\geq 0
such that
\varphi(x)\leq K\mathrm{e}^{-|x|}
holds for
every
x\in\Omega_{\tilde{R}}
by Theorem
A.2
. Mind that for

|x|\leq\tilde{R}
we conclude

\varphi(x)\leq\max_{|x|\leq\tilde{R}}\varphi(x)=:m\leq m\mathrm{e}^{\tilde{R}}\ \mathrm{e}^{-|x|}.

Therefore
\varphi(x)\leq(K+m\mathrm{e}^{\tilde{R}})\ \mathrm{e}^{-|x|}
holds for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

∎

For a given intrinsic ultracontractivity of
\mathrm{e}^{-tH}
with
|x|^{2}<q(x)
for every
R<|x|
, the asymptotic
behaviour of
\mathrm{e}^{-tH}u
is determined by
\mathrm{e}^{-|x|}
at every time
t
.

Corollary A.5
.

Let
q
satisfy
|x|^{2}\leq q(x)
for
R<|x|
and a given radius
R>0
. Additionally, let the Schrödinger semigroup

\mathrm{e}^{-tH}
be intrinsic ultracontractive. Then for every
t>0
there
exists a constant
C_{t}>0
that satisfies

\left|\mathrm{e}^{-tH}u(x)\right|\ \leq\ C_{t}\ \mathrm{e}^{-|x|}\|u\|_{2}

(28)

for every
u\in\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

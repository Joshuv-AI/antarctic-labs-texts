Abstract
Let ( M , g ) (M,g) be a compact Riemann surface with area 1 1 , we shall study the Toda system { − Δ ​ u 1 = 2 ​ ρ 1 ​ ( h 1 ​ e u 1 − 1 ) − ρ 2 ​ ( h 2 ​ e u 2 − 1 ) , − Δ ​ u 2 = 2 ​ ρ 2 ​ ( h 2 ​ e u 2 − 1 ) − ρ 1 ​ ( h 1 ​ e u 1 − 1 ) , \displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{1}=2\rho_{1}(h_{1}e^{u_{1}}-1)-\rho_{2}(h_{2}e^{u_{2}}-1),\\ -\Delta u_{2}=2\rho_{2}(h_{2}e^{u_{2}}-1)-\rho_{1}(h_{1}e^{u_{1}}-1),\end{cases} (0.1) on ( M , g ) (M,g) with ρ 1 = 4 ​ π \rho_{1}=4\pi , ρ 2 ∈ ( 0 , 4 ​ π ) \rho_{2}\in(0,4\pi) , h 1 h_{1} and h 2 h_{2} are two smooth functions on M M . In Jost-Lin-Wang’s celebrated article (Comm. Pure Appl. Math., 59 (2006), no. 4, 526–558), they obtained a sufficient condition for the existence of Eq. ( 0.1 ) when h 1 h_{1} and h 2 h_{2} are both positive. In this paper, we shall improve this result to the case h 1 h_{1} and h 2 h_{2} can change signs. We shall pursue a variational method and use the standard blowup analysis. Among other things, the main contribution in our proof is to show the blowup can only happen at one point where h 1 h_{1} is positive.



1
Introduction

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemann surface with area
1
,
h_{i}\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
\rho_{i}
be positive constant for
i=1,2
.
The critical point
(u_{1},u_{2})
of the functional

\displaystyle J_{\rho_{1},\rho_{2}}(u_{1},u_{2})=\frac{1}{3}\int_{M}\left(|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+\nabla u_{1}\nabla u_{2}+|\nabla u_{2}|^{2}\right)+\rho_{1}\int_{M}u_{1}+\rho_{2}\int_{M}u_{2}

on the Hilbert space

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\left\{(u_{1},u_{2})\in H^{1}(M)\times H^{1}(M):~\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u_{1}}=\int_{M}h_{2}e^{u_{2}}=1\right\}

satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{1}=2\rho_{1}(h_{1}e^{u_{1}}-1)-\rho_{2}(h_{2}e^{u_{2}}-1),\\
-\Delta u_{2}=2\rho_{2}(h_{2}e^{u_{2}}-1)-\rho_{1}(h_{1}e^{u_{1}}-1).\end{cases}

(1.1)

In the literal, people calls (
1.1
) as Toda system. It can be seen as the Frenet frame of holomorphic curves in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
(see
[
8
]
)
in geometry, and also arises in physics in the study of the nonabelian Chern-Simons theory in the self-dual case,
when a scalar Higgs field is coupled to a gauge potential; see
[
4
,
20
,
18
]
.
One can easily find that Toda system (
1.1
) is a generalization of the mean field equation

\displaystyle-\Delta u=\rho(he^{u}-1).

(1.2)

If
u
is a solution of Eq. (
1.2
), then one has
\int_{M}he^{u}=1
. Therefore, people solves Eq. (
1.2
) in Hilbert space

X=\left\{u\in H^{1}(M):\int_{M}he^{u}=1\right\}.

Since Eq. (
1.2
) has a variational structure, thanks to the Moser-Trudinger inequality (c.f.
[
5
,
3
]
)

\displaystyle\log\int_{M}e^{u}\leq\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}+\int_{M}u+C,

it has a minimal type solution in
X
when
\rho\in(0,8\pi)
. However, when
\rho=8\pi
, the situation becomes subtle. The famous Kazdan-Warner problem
[
11
]
states that
under what kind of condition on
h
, the equation

\displaystyle-\Delta u=8\pi(he^{u}-1)

(1.3)

has a solution. Necessarily, one needs
\max_{M}h>0
. By using blowup argument and a variational method, Ding, Jost, Li and Wang
[
3
]
attacked this problem successfully.
Assuming
h
is positive, if

\displaystyle\Delta\log h(p_{0})+8\pi-2K(p_{0})>0,

(1.4)

where
K
is the Gauss curvature of
M
,
p_{0}
is the maximum point of
2\log h(p)+A_{p}
on
M
,

A_{p}=\lim_{x\to p}\left(G_{p}(x)+4\log\text{dist}(x,p)\right)
and
G_{p}
is the Green function which satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta G_{p}=8\pi(\delta_{p}-1),\\
\int_{M}G_{p}=0,\end{cases}

then Eq. (
1.3
) has a minimal type solution. Yang and the second author
[
21
]
generalized this existence result to the case
h
is nonnegative.
With different arguments, the first author and Zhu
[
17
]
and the second author
[
23
]
proved respectively the Ding-Jost-Li-Wang condition (
1.4
)
is still sufficient for the existence of Eq. (
1.3
) when
h
changes signs. The mentioned works are all based on variational method. We remark that these results were
also obtained by using flow method
[
13
,
16
,
19
,
14
]
.

To well understand the analytic properties of the Toda system,
Jost-Wang
[
9
]
derived the Moser-Trudinger inequality for it:

\displaystyle\inf_{(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{H}}J_{\rho_{1},\rho_{2}}\geq-C~~~~\text{iff}~~~~\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in(0,4\pi].

(1.5)

From this inequality, we know that
J_{\rho_{1},\rho_{2}}
is coercive and hence attains its infimum when
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in(0,4\pi)
. However,
when
\rho_{1}
or
\rho_{2}
equals
4\pi
, the existence problem also becomes subtle. In this paper, we shall put our attention on minimal type solution.
Hence, throughout this paper, we assume
\rho_{i}\leq 4\pi
,
i=1,2
.

Let us review the existence result when one of
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
equals
4\pi
, which was obtained by Jost, Lin and Wang when
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are both positive.

Theorem 1.1

(Jost-Lin-Wang
[
10
]
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemann surface with Gauss curvature
K
. Let
h_{1},h_{2}\in C^{2}(M)
be two positive functions
and
\rho_{2}\in(0,4\pi)
. Suppose that

\displaystyle\Delta\log h_{1}(x)+(8\pi-\rho_{2})-2K(x)>0,~~~\forall x\in M,

(1.6)

then
J_{4\pi,\rho_{2}}
has a minimizer
(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{H}
which satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{1}=8\pi(h_{1}e^{u_{1}}-1)-\rho_{2}(h_{2}e^{u_{2}}-1),\\
-\Delta u_{2}=2\rho_{2}(h_{2}e^{u_{2}}-1)-4\pi(h_{1}e^{u_{1}}-1).\end{cases}

(1.7)

When
\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}=4\pi
and both
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are positive, we have

Theorem 1.2

(Li-Li
[
12
]
, Jost-Lin-Wang
[
10
]
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemann surface with Gauss curvature
K
. Let
h_{1},h_{2}\in C^{2}(M)
be two positive functions. Suppose that

\displaystyle\min\{\Delta\log h_{1}(x),\Delta\log h_{2}(x)\}+4\pi-2K(x)>0,~~~\forall x\in M,

(1.8)

then
J_{4\pi,4\pi}
has a minimizer
(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{H}
which satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{1}=8\pi(h_{1}e^{u_{1}}-1)-4\pi(h_{2}e^{u_{2}}-1),\\
-\Delta u_{2}=8\pi(h_{2}e^{u_{2}}-1)-4\pi(h_{1}e^{u_{1}}-1).\end{cases}

We remark that Li-Li obtained Theorem
1.2
when
h_{1}=h_{2}=1
and Jost-Lin-Wang obtained it for general positive
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
.

Motivated mostly by works in
[

,
21
,
17
,
23
]
, we would like to release conditions (

) and (
1.8
) as much as possible.
Comparing with the sufficient conditions in
[

,

,
17
,

]
, we believe that conditions (
1.6
) and (
1.8
) can release to

h_{i}
may change signs and the conditions only need hold on maximum points of the prescribed functions, namely
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
. In the first step to
this aim, we are successful to release (
1.6
) when

and

can change signs. Precisely, we have

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemann surface with Gauss curvature
K
. Let
h_{1},h_{2}\in C^{2}(M)
which are positive somewhere and
\rho_{2}\in(0,4\pi)
. Denote

. Suppose that

2\log h_{1}(p)+A_{1}(p)=\max_{M_{+}}(2\log h_{1}(x)+A_{1}(x)),

where
A_{1}(p)
is defined in (
2.9
). If

\displaystyle\Delta\log h_{1}(p)+(8\pi-\rho_{2})-2K(p)>0,

(1.9)

then

has a minimizer

which satisfies (

).

At the end of the introduction, we would like to mention some related works which deal with sign-changing potential in the critical case with respect to
Moser-Trudinger type inequalities (
[
15
,
22
]
).
For the generalization of Theorem
1.2
, we can also release the condition and

The outline of the rest of the paper is following: In Sect. 2, we do some analysis on the minimizing sequence; In Sect. 3, we estimate the lower bound for

; Finally, we complete the proof of Theorem

in the last section. Throughout the whole paper,

is varying from line to line and even in the same line, we do not distinguish

2
Analysis on the minimizing sequence

To show the functional
J_{4\pi,\rho_{2}}
is bounded from below, we consider the perturbed functional
J_{4\pi-{\epsilon},\rho_{2}}
. Since the infimum
of the functional
J_{4\pi-{\epsilon},\rho_{2}}
in
\mathcal{H}
can be attained by
(u_{1}^{\epsilon},u_{2}^{\epsilon})
, we call
(u_{1}^{\epsilon},u_{2}^{\epsilon})
the minimizing sequence and analysis
it in this section.

For
\rho_{2}\in(0,4\pi)
, in view of inequality (
1.5
), one knows for any
{\epsilon}\in(0,4\pi)
there exists a
(u^{\epsilon}_{1},u^{{\epsilon}}_{2})\in\mathcal{H}
such that

J_{4\pi-{\epsilon},\rho_{2}}(u_{1}^{\epsilon},u_{2}^{\epsilon})=\inf_{(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{H}}J_{4\pi-{\epsilon},\rho_{2}}(u_{1},u_{2})
. Direct calculation shows on
M
,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u^{\epsilon}_{1}=(8\pi-2{\epsilon})(h_{1}e^{u_{1}^{\epsilon}}-1)-\rho_{2}(h_{2}e^{u^{\epsilon}_{2}}-1),\\
-\Delta u^{\epsilon}_{2}=2\rho_{2}(h_{2}e^{u^{\epsilon}_{2}}-1)-(4\pi-{\epsilon})(h_{1}e^{u_{1}^{\epsilon}}-1).\end{cases}

(2.1)

Denote
\overline{u^{\epsilon}_{i}}=\int_{M}u^{\epsilon}_{i}
and
m^{\epsilon}_{i}=\max_{M}u^{\epsilon}_{i}=u_{i}^{\epsilon}(x_{i}^{\epsilon})
for some
x_{i}^{{\epsilon}}\in M
. Since
(u_{1}^{\epsilon},u_{2}^{\epsilon})
minimizes
J_{4\pi-{\epsilon},\rho_{2}}
in
\mathcal{H}
, we have
\int_{M}e^{u_{i}^{{\epsilon}}}
(
i=1,2
) is bounded from
below and above by two positive constants. Namely,

Lemma 2.1
.

There exist two positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

\displaystyle C_{1}\leq\int_{M}e^{u_{i}^{{\epsilon}}}\leq C_{2},~~i=1,2.

Proof.

For
i=1,2
, the lower bound is easy since
\int_{M}h_{i}e^{u_{i}^{\epsilon}}=1
and
\max_{M}h_{i}>0
. Since
\mathcal{H}
is not empty, we can choose
(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathcal{H}
, then

J_{4\pi-{\epsilon},\rho_{2}}(u_{1}^{\epsilon},u_{2}^{\epsilon})=\inf_{(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{H}}J_{4\pi-{\epsilon},\rho_{2}}(u_{1},u_{2})\leq J_{4\pi-{\epsilon},\rho_{2}}(v_{1},v_{2})\to J_{4\pi,\rho_{2}}(v_{1},v_{2})\leq C.

This together with the Moser-Trudinger inequality (
1.5
) and Jensen’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\log\int_{M}e^{u_{1}^{\epsilon}}+\log\int_{M}e^{u_{2}^{\epsilon}}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{12\pi}\int_{M}(|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|^{2}+\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}\nabla u_{2}^{\epsilon}+|\nabla u_{2}^{\epsilon}|^{2})+\overline{u_{1}^{\epsilon}}+\overline{u_{2}^{\epsilon}}+C

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}J_{4\pi-{\epsilon},\rho_{2}}(u^{\epsilon})+\frac{{\epsilon}}{4\pi}\overline{u_{1}^{\epsilon}}+\frac{4\pi-\rho_{2}}{4\pi}\overline{u_{2}^{\epsilon}}+C

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{{\epsilon}}{4\pi}\log\int_{M}e^{u_{1}^{\epsilon}}+\frac{4\pi-\rho_{2}}{4\pi}\log\int_{M}e^{u_{2}^{\epsilon}}+C.

This combining with
\int_{M}e^{u_{i}^{\epsilon}}
is bounded from below by some
C_{1}>0
shows that
\int_{M}e^{u_{i}^{\epsilon}}\leq C_{2}
for some
C_{2}>0
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
s\in(1,2)
,
\|\nabla u_{i}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{s}(M)}\leq C
for
i=1,2
.

Proof.

Let
s^{\prime}=1/s>2
, we know by definition that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{s}(M)}=\sup\left\{\left|\int_{M}\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}\nabla\phi\right|:\phi\in W^{1,s^{\prime}}(M),\int_{M}\phi=0,\|\phi\|_{W^{1,s^{\prime}}(M)}=1\right\}.

The Sobolev embedding theorem shows that
\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq C
for some constant
C
. Then it follows by Eq. (
2.1
) and Lemma
2.1
that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{M}\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}\nabla\phi\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{M}\phi(-\Delta u_{1}^{\epsilon})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{M}\phi[(8\pi-2{\epsilon})(h_{1}e^{u_{1}^{\epsilon}}-1)-\rho_{2}(h_{2}e^{u^{\epsilon}_{2}}-1)]\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C.

Therefore we have
\|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{s}(M)}\leq C
. Similarly, we have
\|\nabla u_{2}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{s}(M)}\leq C
. This ends the proof.
∎

Concerning
(u^{\epsilon}_{1},u^{\epsilon}_{2})
, we have the following equivalent characterizations.

Lemma 2.3
.

The following three items are equivalent:

(i)

m_{1}^{{\epsilon}}+m_{2}^{{\epsilon}}\to+\infty
as
{\epsilon}\to 0
;

(ii)

\int_{M}(|\nabla u^{\epsilon}_{1}|^{2}+\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}\nabla u_{2}^{\epsilon}+|\nabla u^{\epsilon}_{2}|^{2})\to+\infty
as
{\epsilon}\to 0
;

(iii)

\overline{u^{\epsilon}_{1}}+\overline{u^{\epsilon}_{2}}\to-\infty
as
{\epsilon}\to 0
.

Proof.

(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iii)
: Since
J_{4\pi-{\epsilon},\rho_{2}}
is bounded,
(ii)
is equivalent to

\displaystyle(4\pi-{\epsilon})\overline{u_{1}^{\epsilon}}+\rho_{2}\overline{u_{2}^{\epsilon}}\to-\infty~~\text{as}~~{\epsilon}\to 0.

(2.2)

Using Lemma
2.1
and Jensen’s inequality, we have
\overline{u_{i}^{\epsilon}}\leq C
for
i=1,2
. Therefore, (
2.2
)
is equivalent to
(iii)
and then
(ii)
is equivalent to
(iii)
.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: Suppose not, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|^{2}+\int_{M}|\nabla u_{2}^{\epsilon}|^{2}\leq 2\int_{M}(|\nabla u^{\epsilon}_{1}|^{2}+\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}\nabla u_{2}^{\epsilon}+|\nabla u^{\epsilon}_{2}|^{2})\leq C.

Meanwhile, by
(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iii)
one knows
\overline{u_{1}^{\epsilon}}+\overline{u_{2}^{\epsilon}}\geq-C
. So
\overline{u_{i}^{\epsilon}}
is bounded for
i=1,2
. By Poincaré’s inequality, we have for

i=1,2
that

\displaystyle\int_{M}(u_{i}^{\epsilon})^{2}-\overline{u_{i}^{\epsilon}}^{2}=\int_{M}(u_{i}^{\epsilon}-\overline{u_{i}^{\epsilon}})^{2}\leq C\int_{M}|\nabla u_{i}^{\epsilon}|^{2}\leq C.

So
(u_{i}^{\epsilon})
is bounded in
L^{2}(M)
. Since
\|\nabla u_{i}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}(M)}
and
\overline{u_{i}^{\epsilon}}
are both bounded, we have by the Moser-Trudinger inequality that
(e^{u_{i}^{\epsilon}})

is bounded in
L^{s}(M)
for any
s\geq 1
. Then by using elliptic estimates to (
2.1
) we obtain that
(u_{i}^{\epsilon})
is bounded in

W^{2,2}(M)
and then
\|u_{i}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}
is bounded. Therefore,
m_{i}^{\epsilon}\leq C
for
i=1,2
. This contradicts
(i)
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
: If not, then we have
m_{1}^{\epsilon}+m_{2}^{\epsilon}\leq C
.
Using Lemma
2.1
, we have
m_{i}^{\epsilon}\geq C
for
i=1,2
. So
m_{i}^{\epsilon}
is bounded for
i=1,2
.
Then
(e^{u_{i}^{\epsilon}})
is bounded. Since by Lemma
2.2
,
u_{i}^{\epsilon}-\overline{u_{i}^{\epsilon}}
is bounded in
L^{s}(M)
for any
s>1
, we have by using
elliptic estimates to (
2.1
) that
u_{i}^{\epsilon}-\overline{u_{i}^{\epsilon}}
is bounded. Since
(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iii)
, we have

\overline{u_{1}^{\epsilon}}+\overline{u_{2}^{\epsilon}}\to-\infty
. Notice that
\overline{u_{i}^{\epsilon}}\leq C
, we have
\overline{u_{1}^{\epsilon}}
or
\overline{u_{2}^{\epsilon}}

tends to
-\infty
. Without loss of generality, suppose
\overline{u_{1}^{\epsilon}}
tends to
-\infty
. Then

\displaystyle 1=\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u_{1}^{\epsilon}}=\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u_{1}^{\epsilon}-\overline{u_{1}^{\epsilon}}}e^{\overline{u_{1}^{\epsilon}}}\to 0~~\text{as}~~{\epsilon}\to 0.

This is a contradiction.
∎

Definition 2.1

(Blow up)
.

We call
(u_{1}^{\epsilon},u_{2}^{\epsilon})
blows up, if one of the three items in Lemma
2.3
holds.

When
(u_{1}^{\epsilon},u_{2}^{\epsilon})
blows up, there holds

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(u_{1}^{\epsilon},u_{2}^{\epsilon})
minimize
J_{4\pi-{\epsilon},\rho_{2}}
in
\mathcal{H}
. If
(u_{1}^{\epsilon},u_{2}^{\epsilon})
blows up, then

\overline{u_{1}^{{\epsilon}}}\to-\infty~~\text{as}~~{\epsilon}\to 0~~\text{and}~~\overline{u^{\epsilon}_{2}}\geq-C.

Proof.

Since
J_{4\pi-{\epsilon},\rho_{2}}(u_{1}^{\epsilon},u_{2}^{\epsilon})
is bounded, we have by (
1.5
) that

\displaystyle C\geq

\displaystyle J_{4\pi-{\epsilon},\rho_{2}}(u_{1}^{\epsilon},u_{2}^{\epsilon})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\int_{M}(|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|^{2}+\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}\nabla u_{2}^{\epsilon}+|\nabla u_{2}^{\epsilon}|^{2})+(4\pi-{\epsilon})\overline{u_{1}^{\epsilon}}+\rho_{2}\overline{u_{2}^{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C-{\epsilon}\overline{u_{1}^{\epsilon}}-(4\pi-\rho_{2})\overline{u_{2}^{\epsilon}}.

Since
\overline{u_{1}^{\epsilon}}\leq C
and
\rho_{2}<4\pi
, we have

\overline{u_{2}^{\epsilon}}\geq-C.

If
(u_{1}^{\epsilon},u_{2}^{\epsilon})
blows up, it follows from Lemma
2.3
that
\overline{u_{1}^{\epsilon}}\to-\infty
as
{\epsilon}\to 0
. This finishes the proof.
∎

If
(u_{1}^{\epsilon},u_{2}^{\epsilon})
does not blow up, then by Lemma
2.3
, one can show that
(u_{1}^{\epsilon},u_{2}^{\epsilon})
converges to
(u_{1}^{0},u_{2}^{0})
in
\mathcal{H}
and
(u_{1}^{0},u_{2}^{0})
minimizes
J_{4\pi,\rho_{2}}
.
The proof of Theorem
1.3
terminates in this case. Therefore, we assume
(u_{1}^{\epsilon},u_{2}^{\epsilon})
blows up in the rest of this paper.

By Lemma
2.2
, there exist
G_{i}
,
i=1,2
such that
u_{1}^{\epsilon}-\overline{u_{1}^{\epsilon}}\rightharpoonup G_{1}
and
u_{2}^{\epsilon}\rightharpoonup G_{2}
weakly in
W^{1,s}(M)
for any
1<s<2
as
{\epsilon}\to 0
.
Since
(e^{u_{i}^{\epsilon}})
is bounded in
L^{1}(M)
we may extract a subsequence (still denoted
e^{u_{i}^{\epsilon}}
) such that
e^{u_{i}^{\epsilon}}
converges in the sense of measures on

M
to some nonnegative bounded measure
\mu_{i}
for
i=1,2
. We set

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}=8\pi h_{1}\mu_{1}-\rho_{2}h_{2}\mu_{2},~~\gamma_{2}=2\rho_{2}h_{2}\mu_{2}-4\pi h_{1}\mu_{1}

and

\displaystyle S_{i}=\{x\in M:|\gamma_{i}(\{x\})|\geq 4\pi\},~i=1,2.

Let
S=S_{1}\cup S_{2}
. By Theorem 1 in
[
1
]
, it is easy to show that for any
\Omega\subset\subset M\setminus S
,

\displaystyle u_{i}^{\epsilon}-\overline{u_{i}^{\epsilon}}~\text{is~uniformly~bounded~in~}\Omega,~i=1,2.

(2.3)

Since
(u_{1}^{\epsilon},u_{2}^{\epsilon})
blows up, we know
S
is not empty (Or else, with a finite covering argument, we have by (
2.3
) that
\|u_{i}^{\epsilon}-\overline{u_{i}^{\epsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq C
, then
m^{\epsilon}_{i}\leq C
, this contradicts with Lemma
2.3
(i)). Meanwhile, by the definition of
S
, we have for any
x\in S
,

\mu_{1}(\{x\})\geq\frac{1}{4\max_{M}|h_{1}|}~~\text{or}~~\mu_{2}(\{x\})\geq\frac{\pi}{\rho_{2}\max_{M}|h_{2}|}.

In view of
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
are bounded,
S
is a finite set. We denote
S=\{x_{l}\}_{l=1}^{L}
.
It follows from
(
2.3
) and Fatou’s lemma that

\mu_{1}=\sum_{l=1}^{L}\mu_{1}(\{x_{l}\})\delta_{x_{l}}~~\text{and}~~\mu_{2}=e^{G_{2}}+\sum_{l=1}^{L}\mu_{2}(\{x_{l}\})\delta_{x_{l}}.

Lemma 2.5
.

\rm{supp}\mu_{1}
is a single point set.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
2.1
that
{\rm{supp}}\mu_{1}\neq\emptyset
.
If there are two different points in
{\rm{supp}}\mu_{1}
, then by Lemma
2.1
and the improved Moser-Trudinger inequality (cf.
[
2
]
, Theorem 2.1),
for any
{\epsilon}^{\prime}>0
, there exists some
C=C({\epsilon}^{\prime})>0
such that

\displaystyle C\leq\log\int_{M}e^{u_{1}^{\epsilon}}\leq(\frac{1}{32\pi}+{\epsilon}^{\prime})\int_{M}|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|^{2}+\overline{u_{1}^{\epsilon}}+C.

(2.4)

Since

\displaystyle C\geq

\displaystyle J_{4\pi-{\epsilon},\rho_{2}}(u_{1}^{\epsilon},u_{2}^{\epsilon})

\displaystyle=

Efficacy of Antibiotic Versus Probiotics as Adjuncts to Mechanical Debridement for the Treatment of Peri-Implant Mucositis
Authors: Sindhu Sudhakar Kumararama, Manali Patil, Bhavna Jha Kukreja, Manjiri Salkar, Shikha Verma, Naina Pattnaik, Lalit Patil
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Peri-implant mucositis, an inflammatory condition affecting the soft tissues surrounding dental implants, is commonly treated with mechanical debridement. The efficacy of adjunctive therapies, such as antibiotics and probiotics, in enhancing treatment outcomes remains a topic of interest.Materials and Methods:A randomized controlled trial was conducted with 60 patients diagnosed with peri-implant mucositis. Patients were divided into three groups: group A (mechanical debridement alone), group B (mechanical debridement plus antibiotics), and group C (mechanical debridement plus probiotics). Clinical parameters, including probing depth (PD), bleeding on probing (BOP), and plaque index (PI), were recorded at baseline, 1 month, and 3 months post treatment. Statistical analysis was performed using ANOVA and post-hoc tests to compare the efficacy between groups.Results:At 1 month, group B showed a significant reduction in PD (mean reduction of 2.1 mm) and BOP (mean reduction of 40%) compared to group A (mean reduction of 1.2 mm in PD and 25% in BOP) and group C (mean reduction of 1.8 mm in PD and 35% in BOP). At 3 months, group C exhibited sustained improvement in PI (mean reduction of 50%) and BOP (mean reduction of 45%) compared to group B (mean reduction of 45% in PI and 40% in BOP) and group A (mean reduction of 30% in PI and 20% in BOP).Conclusion:Both antibiotics and probiotics as adjuncts to mechanical debridement demonstrated efficacy in the treatment of peri-implant mucositis. Antibiotics showed a more pronounced immediate effect, while probiotics provided sustained improvements over a longer period.

INTRODUCTION

Peri-implant mucositis is a reversible inflammatory condition affecting the soft tissues surrounding dental implants, characterized by erythema, swelling, and bleeding on probing (BOP) without bone loss. If left untreated, it can progress to peri-implantitis, a more severe condition that includes bone resorption and can lead to implant failure.[1] The primary treatment for peri-implant mucositis involves mechanical debridement to disrupt and remove the biofilm on the implant surfaces. However, the adjunctive use of pharmacological agents, such as antibiotics and probiotics, has been explored to enhance treatment outcomes.[2]

Antibiotics have long been used in periodontal therapy due to their ability to reduce bacterial load and inflammation. Systemic and local antibiotics can be effective in managing peri-implant mucositis, particularly in cases with high microbial virulence and host response.[1] However, the overuse of antibiotics poses risks of antibiotic resistance and adverse effects, highlighting the need for alternative adjunctive therapies.[2]

Probiotics, beneficial bacteria that can modulate the oral microbiome, have emerged as a promising alternative. They work by competitive inhibition of pathogenic bacteria, enhancing the immune response, and producing antimicrobial substances. Recent studies have shown that probiotics can improve clinical outcomes in periodontal and peri-implant diseases by reducing inflammation and promoting tissue health.[1] By evaluating clinical parameters such as probing depth (PD), BOP, and plaque index (PI), this study seeks to provide evidence-based recommendations for the management of peri-implant mucositis.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design and participants

This randomized controlled trial was conducted. Sixty patients diagnosed with peri-implant mucositis were enrolled in the study. The inclusion criteria were patients aged 18–65 years with at least one dental implant diagnosed with mucositis, characterized by BOP without radiographic evidence of bone loss. Exclusion criteria included patients with systemic diseases affecting periodontal health, recent antibiotic or probiotic use, pregnancy, and smoking.

Randomization and group allocation

Participants were randomly assigned into three groups (n = 20 each) by using a computer-generated randomization sequence:

Group A (control): Mechanical debridement aloneGroup B (antibiotic): Mechanical debridement plus systemic antibiotics (amoxicillin 500 mg, three times daily for 7 days)Group C (probiotic): Mechanical debridement plus probiotics (Lactobacillus reuteri, twice daily for 30 days).

Interventions

Mechanical debridement was performed using ultrasonic scalers and hand instruments to remove biofilm and calculus from the implant surfaces and surrounding tissues. In group B, systemic antibiotics were prescribed following mechanical debridement. In group C, patients were instructed to take probiotic lozenges twice daily for 30 days following mechanical debridement.

Clinical parameters

Clinical parameters were recorded at baseline, 1 month, and 3 months post treatment. The parameters included:

PD: Measured in millimeters using a periodontal probe at six sites per implant.BOP: Recorded as the presence or absence of bleeding within 30 seconds of probing.PI: Assessed using a 4-point scale to measure plaque accumulation around the implant.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) version 25.0.

SUMMARY OF RESULTS

At 1 month, group B (antibiotic) showed a significant reduction in PD (mean reduction of 2.1 mm) and BOP (mean reduction of 40%) compared to group A (control) and group C (probiotic). Group C showed a mean reduction of 1.8 mm in PD and 35% in BOP, while group A showed a mean reduction of 1.2 mm in PD and 25% in BOP.

At 3 months, group C (probiotic) exhibited sustained improvement in PI (mean reduction of 50%) and BOP (mean reduction of 45%) compared to group B and group A. Group B showed a mean reduction of 45% in PI and 40% in BOP, while group A showed a mean reduction of 30% in PI and 20% in BOP.

The results indicate that while antibiotics provided a more immediate reduction in clinical parameters, probiotics demonstrated sustained improvements over a longer period.

DISCUSSION

The findings of this study demonstrate the efficacy of both antibiotics and probiotics as adjuncts to mechanical debridement in the treatment of peri-implant mucositis. The results indicate that while antibiotics showed a more immediate reduction in clinical parameters such as PD and BOP, probiotics provided sustained improvements over a longer period. These outcomes align with previous studies that have explored the benefits of adjunctive therapies in periodontal and peri-implant disease management.[12]

The significant reduction in PD and BOP observed in the antibiotic group at 1 month post treatment can be attributed to the potent antimicrobial effects of antibiotics, which effectively reduce the bacterial load and inflammation in the peri-implant tissues. This finding is consistent with earlier research demonstrating the short-term benefits of systemic antibiotics in managing peri-implant infections.[3] However, the potential risks associated with antibiotic use, such as the development of antibiotic resistance and adverse effects, cannot be overlooked.[4] These concerns necessitate the exploration of alternative therapies that are both effective and safe.

Probiotics work through competitive inhibition of pathogenic bacteria, production of antimicrobial substances, and modulation of the host immune response, which collectively contribute to the maintenance of peri-implant health.[5] These findings are supported by recent studies that have shown the positive impact of probiotics on periodontal and peri-implant health, suggesting their potential as a viable adjunctive therapy.[678]

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, both antibiotics and probiotics as adjuncts to mechanical debridement are effective in the treatment of peri-implant mucositis. Antibiotics provide a rapid reduction in clinical parameters, while probiotics offer sustained improvements, highlighting their potential as a safer long-term adjunctive therapy.

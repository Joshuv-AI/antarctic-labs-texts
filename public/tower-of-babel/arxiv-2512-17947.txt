Remarks on Legal Entanglement: No-Signaling, Local Operations, and Legal Updates
Mikołaj Sienickiand, Krzysztof Sienicki

Abstract
Godfrey & Sichelman propose a quantum-inspired formalism for modeling coupled legal relations and interpretations under the label legal entanglement [ 1 ] . The idea is attractive: it offers a compact way to talk about how legal variables can become tightly coupled, and it suggests quantitative proxies for legal modularity and “information cost.” The main technical problem is narrower. In the paper’s discussion of formulative entanglement , legislation is treated as a local operation on subsystem A A that can change the reduced state of a distant entangled subsystem B B (so ρ B ′ ≠ ρ B \rho_{B}^{\prime}\neq\rho_{B} ) even before any “measurement” at B B , and this is framed as a departure from the quantum no-signaling constraint. In standard quantum mechanics, however, no-signaling does not rely on unitarity alone: it holds for all local, trace-preserving operations (CPTP maps) [ 3 , 2 , 10 ] . This note states the correct no-signaling result, pinpoints exactly where the mapping to quantum mechanics becomes inconsistent, and suggests a clean repair that preserves the legal intuition: treat legislation either as (i) a global update of the rule/constraint structure (changing the admissible state space or observables), and/or (ii) an LOCC-style process (local operations plus public dissemination of authoritative classical information). Finally, we point out that an “updating-first” perspective developed in Algorithmic Idealism [ 5 , 6 , 7 ] and its legal extension [ 8 , 9 ] offers a natural vocabulary for keeping two layers distinct: (a) physical locality/no-signaling constraints, and (b) institutional or semantic constraint propagation in legal systems.



1
Target and scope

These remarks address a specific technical point in Godfrey & Sichelman’s
Legal Entanglement

[
1
]
: the treatment of
formulative entanglement
and its relationship to the quantum no-signaling constraint.

The paper introduces three contexts for legal entanglement (interpretive, formulative, adjudicative) and uses standard quantum-information tools (tensor products, partial trace, entanglement measures) to represent dependencies among legal variables. As a
quantum-inspired
modeling strategy, much of the formal apparatus can be read as defining and exploring a family of probabilistic/correlational models on legal variables.

The issue arises when the paper draws a
strong
contrast with quantum mechanics: it suggests that legislation, modeled as an operator on one subsystem, can alter the reduced state of a remote entangled subsystem even absent measurement, and that this is a principled divergence from quantum no-signaling. The remainder of this note argues that the divergence is not required, and that (with a small refactoring) the legal intuitions can be kept while the quantum claims become fully standard.

2
No-signaling for local operations: the QM baseline

The no-signaling constraint in quantum theory is operational: choices made on
A
cannot change the
unconditional
outcome statistics available to an operationally separated party
B
. This statement does not require relativity; in relativistic settings the same operational separation is naturally realized by spacelike separation
[
3
,
10
]
.

No-signaling is often motivated using local
unitary
evolution, but the correct statement is broader. In quantum information theory, the most general physically allowed local operation is a completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) map (which includes open-system dynamics and
unselective
measurements)
[
2
,
3
,
10
]
.

Lemma 1

(No-signaling under local CPTP maps)
.

Let
\rho_{AB}
be any bipartite density operator on
\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
.
Let
\mathcal{E}_{A}
be any CPTP map acting on subsystem
A
.
Define

\displaystyle\rho_{AB}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=(\mathcal{E}_{A}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{B})(\rho_{AB}),

(1)

\displaystyle\rho_{B}

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{Tr}_{A}(\rho_{AB}),\qquad\rho_{B}^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Tr}_{A}(\rho_{AB}^{\prime}).

(2)

Then
\rho_{B}^{\prime}=\rho_{B}
.

Proof.

Write
\mathcal{E}_{A}
in Kraus form
[
2
,
3
]
:

\mathcal{E}_{A}(X)=\sum_{k}A_{k}XA_{k}^{\dagger},\qquad\sum_{k}A_{k}^{\dagger}A_{k}=I_{A}.

(3)

Then

\rho_{AB}^{\prime}=\sum_{k}(A_{k}\otimes I_{B})\,\rho_{AB}\,(A_{k}^{\dagger}\otimes I_{B}).

(4)

Taking the partial trace over
A
and using the basic identity

\mathrm{Tr}_{A}\!\left[(X_{A}\otimes I_{B})Y_{AB}\right]=\mathrm{Tr}_{A}\!\left[Y_{AB}(X_{A}\otimes I_{B})\right],

(5)

we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{B}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}_{A}(\rho_{AB}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\mathrm{Tr}_{A}\!\Big((A_{k}\otimes I_{B})\,\rho_{AB}\,(A_{k}^{\dagger}\otimes I_{B})\Big)

(6)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\mathrm{Tr}_{A}\!\Big(\rho_{AB}\,(A_{k}^{\dagger}A_{k}\otimes I_{B})\Big)

(7)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}_{A}\!\Big(\rho_{AB}\,(\sum_{k}A_{k}^{\dagger}A_{k}\otimes I_{B})\Big)

(8)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}_{A}(\rho_{AB}\,(I_{A}\otimes I_{B}))

(9)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}_{A}(\rho_{AB})=\rho_{B}.

(10)

∎

2.1
Selective vs. unselective measurements (where “remote updating” belongs)

A useful place where “remote updates”
do
appear is when we condition on a measurement outcome. The key point is that there are two different operations
[
3
,
10
,
4
]
:

•

Unselective measurement: we measure but ignore the outcome. This is still a CPTP map, so it cannot change
\rho_{B}
(Lemma 1 / (
10
)).

•

Selective measurement: we
condition
on a particular outcome
k
. This produces a conditional state
\rho_{B|k}
that depends on
k
.

Concretely, let
\{M_{k}\}_{k}
be Kraus operators on
A
satisfying the completeness condition

\sum_{k}M_{k}^{\dagger}M_{k}=I_{A},

(11)

so that the corresponding unselective measurement is trace-preserving on
A
. Define the outcome probability (full trace over
AB
)

p_{k}=\mathrm{Tr}_{AB}\!\left[(M_{k}\otimes I_{B})\,\rho_{AB}\,(M_{k}^{\dagger}\otimes I_{B})\right],

(12)

and the normalized conditional state

\rho_{B|k}=\frac{1}{p_{k}}\,\mathrm{Tr}_{A}\!\Big[(M_{k}\otimes I_{B})\,\rho_{AB}\,(M_{k}^{\dagger}\otimes I_{B})\Big].

(13)

The crucial operational point is simple:
B
can only
use
this dependence on
k
if the outcome label is communicated by classical means.
Otherwise,
B
still sees the same unconditional marginal.

3
Where the target paper’s mapping breaks

Godfrey & Sichelman propose that, unlike standard quantum systems, a legislative enactment modeled as an operator on subsystem
A
may alter the reduced state of an entangled subsystem
B
prior to any legal “measurement” at
B
, and they describe this as a divergence from no-signaling
[
1
]
.

From the perspective of Lemma 1 (equations (
1
)–(
10
)), there are only a few coherent ways to interpret such a claim:

1.

P_{A}
is not trace-preserving (i.e. it is post-selection / conditioning).
Then
B
can change conditionally as in (
13
), but without communication,
B
still sees the same unconditional marginal (
10
).

2.

P_{A}
is not a local operation on a fixed bipartite state.
For example, it may represent a
global
update to the joint state assignment or to the permissible observables/constraints.

3.

The model includes an explicit external communication channel.
If one posits a channel that propagates a signal “outside physical space,” then one has in effect posited communication (albeit in a different ontology).

The simplest repair is to stop treating legislation as a local quantum operation on subsystem
A
and instead treat it as a
global rule update
or
conditioning + dissemination
process.

4
A QM-consistent reframing of “formulative entanglement”

4.1
Option A: legislation as a global rule/constraint update

In law, legislation changes the
normative constraints
governing which states are admissible and which questions are legally meaningful. In a quantum-inspired model, this is most naturally represented not as a local map

\rho_{AB}\mapsto(\mathcal{E}_{A}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{B})(\rho_{AB}),

(14)

but as a transformation of the
model itself
, e.g.

(\rho_{AB},\mathcal{O},C)\mapsto(\rho_{AB}^{\prime},\mathcal{O}^{\prime},C^{\prime}),

(15)

where
\mathcal{O}
encodes the set of legal “observables” (the partitions of outcomes a decision-maker can realize) and
C
encodes intertextual and doctrinal constraints.

4.2
Option B: legislation as LOCC-style conditioning + public dissemination

A complementary reframing treats legal updating as an explicitly informational process: a legal act produces a public record, and legal actors update their state assignments upon learning the record.

In quantum information terms, this is structurally analogous to LOCC, i.e.
Local Operations and Classical Communication
. LOCC is best thought of as a
class of protocols
: possibly multi-round and adaptive sequences of local CPTP maps whose choice can depend on previously communicated classical outcomes, together with the dissemination of those outcome records/labels via classical channels
[
3
,
10
,
4
]
. This is the standard quantum-information template for “update at a distance without signaling.”

5
Comment and related work: Algorithmic Idealism and Algorithmic Idealism of Law

Two related “updating-first” strands help keep the layers separated.

First, the
Algorithmic Idealism
sequence
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
treats “collapse” as informational updating of an agent’s state assignment and treats entanglement as a structured coupling constraint on joint predictions. On that stance, instantaneous change is naturally interpreted as a change in conditional distributions (given new information), not as an operational signaling channel.

Second, the
Algorithmic Idealism of Law
short versions
[
8
,
9

provide a directly legal instantiation of the same separation:

•

In the single-system U.S. framing, authoritative acts (judgments, enactments, settlements) are modeled as “legal measurements” that trigger Bayesian updating over a predictive kernel on
legal self-states

[
8
]
. This matches precisely the conditional/epistemic reading of remote “updates” suggested by (
13
).

•

In the European multi-level framing, legally authoritative updates occur at multiple levels (EU/CJEU, ECHR/ECtHR

, national orders), and coupling is represented via interface constraints between kernels

. This makes it especially clear why “instantaneous” legal effects should be modeled as constraint propagation in a layered institutional architecture rather than as a local physical operation that changes a distant reduced state.

Incorporating these references clarifies that the repaired account of “formulative entanglement” is not an ad hoc patch: it is the expected form of update in an informational jurisprudence.

6
Conclusion

The target paper’s conceptual contribution—treating certain legal dependencies as entanglement-like and using quantum-information measures as quantitative diagnostics—does not require any departure from standard quantum mechanics
[
1
]
. The appearance of a no-signaling violation stems from mapping legislative change to a local subsystem operation on a fixed bipartite state. Recasting legislation as a global rule update (
15
) and/or as LOCC-like conditioning plus dissemination (
13
) yields a quantum-mechanically faithful account while preserving the legal phenomena the paper seeks to capture, and it aligns naturally with the algorithmic-state jurisprudence program
[
8
,
9
]
.

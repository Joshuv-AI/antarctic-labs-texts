Dynamic energy-aware fixed-point linear mapping multiplier for internet of things edge devices
Authors: Dongling Wu, Likun Zhao, Bonghwan Kim, Junjie Yin, Jierui Xue
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractApproximate computing has emerged as an effective approach for reducing power consumption in error-tolerant applications, particularly in resource-constrained Internet of Things (IoT) edge systems. However, most existing approximate multipliers employ static approximation levels, limiting their ability to adapt to dynamically varying energy conditions at runtime. This paper proposes a Dynamic Energy-Aware Linear Mapping Multiplier (DEA-LMM) that integrates runtime energy monitoring with adaptive approximation control. By incorporating energy-aware normalization, leading zero detection, and configurable multi-level compensation mechanisms, the proposed architecture enables dynamic adjustment of the accuracy–energy trade-off according to the available energy budget. To further improve energy efficiency, an enhanced architecture, referred to as Dynamic Energy-Aware Linear Improved Mapping Multiplier (DEA-ILMM), is introduced by extending the baseline design with energy-aware dynamic truncation. This mechanism adaptively configures operand precision at runtime, reducing power and area overhead while maintaining bounded approximation error. Both designs preserve compatibility with fixed-point linear mapping formulations and avoid modifications to the core multiplier structure. Comprehensive experimental evaluations on Field-Programmable Gate Array (FPGA) and Application-Specific Integrated Circuit (ASIC) platforms, including realistic solar-energy-harvesting traces, demonstrate that the proposed DEA-LMM and DEA-ILMM achieve up to 70% power savings in aggressive modes and an additional 15–25% energy reduction through runtime dynamic adaptation compared with state-of-the-art static approximate multipliers, while preserving acceptable accuracy across dynamically varying energy budgets. Real-world validation on an ESP32-C3-based solar-powered sensor node further confirms 47% energy reduction with negligible accuracy loss in practical environmental monitoring tasks. These results indicate that the proposed designs provide a flexible and effective solution for energy-adaptive computation in modern edge systems.Keywords: Internet of things, Approximate computing, Fixed-point multiplier, Linear mapping, Energy-aware design, Dynamic approximation

Introduction

The rapid expansion of Internet of Things (IoT) edge systems has enabled real-time sensing, data processing, and intelligent decision-making in diverse application domains, including smart cities, healthcare monitoring, and industrial automation1–3. By shifting computing from centralized cloud infrastructures to distributed edge nodes, these systems significantly reduce communication latency and bandwidth requirements while improving responsiveness. However, this transition imposes severe constraints on power consumption, silicon area, and computational complexity, especially in battery-powered or energy harvesting environments with highly dynamic power availability4.

Recent advances in IoT hardware security and edge intelligence further emphasize the need for efficient and adaptive computing at the device level5–7. In such environments, computational operations constitute a major part of the computational workload, and multipliers play a crucial role in determining the overall system performance and energy efficiency8. Although various hardware techniques have been investigated to improve circuit efficiency and signal generation in embedded systems, their direct application in energy-aware approximate computations remains limited9. Meanwhile, emerging edge accelerators and heterogeneous computing platforms continue to increase the demand for scalable and low-power computing units10.

To address these challenges, approximate computation has emerged as a promising paradigm that reduces computational precision to achieve significant reductions in power consumption and hardware cost2. This approach is particularly suitable for fault-tolerant applications such as signal processing and machine learning inference, where small numerical deviations have minimal impact on the final results6. In IoT systems with limited energy budgets, data collection and processing optimization are essential, further reinforcing the importance of energy-aware computing strategies11. Furthermore, recent advances in federated and distributed edge learning highlight the need for efficient resource allocation and adaptive computation across heterogeneous nodes12.

Existing approximate multiplier schemes incorporate algorithm-level, architecture-level, and circuit-level optimizations, presenting various trade-offs between accuracy and efficiency13. Some approaches also consider hardware-level security and robustness constraints in IoT systems and integrate reliability-aware design principles into computing components5. Furthermore, intelligent optimization techniques have been investigated to enhance routing and computing efficiency in IoT environments, which indirectly affects the distribution of computing workloads14.

Despite these advances, most existing approximation multipliers rely on static approximation strategies, where accuracy levels are fixed at design time and cannot adapt to runtime variations7. This limitation becomes critical in dynamic edge environments, where workload conditions, operating voltages, and available energy budgets are constantly fluctuating. Recent research has explored adaptive resource allocation and scheduling techniques to handle such variability. However, their integration with computational-level adaptation remains limited15. Furthermore, some hardware-based designs focus on improving circuit-level efficiency without addressing the broader need for dynamic, energy-aware approximation9.

Recent efforts have introduced adaptive approximation multipliers with dynamic reconfiguration capabilities to improve runtime flexibility16. Although these designs are a step towards energy-aware computing, they often lack precise control or fail to guarantee stable and unbiased error characteristics. In parallel, advances in heterogeneous edge accelerators further highlight the importance of integrating compatible computational units into next-generation computing platforms17.

To address this gap, this work introduces a DEA-LMM that extends conventional linear mapping architectures with runtime energy adaptivity. By integrating an energy monitoring module, an energy-aware control unit, and a modular approximation structure, the proposed design dynamically adjusts its approximation level in response to real-time energy budgets and workload conditions. This enables the multiplier to preserve high accuracy when energy is abundant, while aggressively reducing power consumption under constrained operating conditions—without introducing systematic error bias. Furthermore, an enhanced variant incorporating energy-aware truncation is explored to further improve efficiency under stringent energy limits, while maintaining architectural compatibility with the base design. Related works

Approximate computing has emerged as a key strategy for enhancing energy efficiency in IoT edge devices by trading precision for reduced hardware costs in error-tolerant applications18. In18 a comprehensive overview of approximation techniques is applied to multiplier designs, and categorizes them into algorithm-level, architecture-level, and circuit-level approaches, with emphasis on their deployment in machine learning tasks to achieve significant power savings. This survey highlights how approximation in multipliers can mitigate the computational demands of data-intensive IoT applications, providing implementations of representative designs to evaluate accuracy and efficiency trade-offs.

At the algorithm level, several studies focus on transforming multiplication operations to simplify computations while maintaining acceptable error rates. Study19 introduces a platform-independent architecture that approximates input operands to powers of two using novel encoding logics, enabling accurate sub-multiplications with up to 80% energy savings in ASIC implementations and improved performance on FPGAs for error-resilient tasks. Similarly, Wang et al.20 proposes an energy-efficient approximate multiplier circuit tailored for IoT processing units, utilizing inexact adders and compressors to reduce logic resources and power consumption in signal processing applications. Study21 explores iterative approximate floating-point multipliers optimized for convolutional neural network inferences, demonstrating reduced error accumulation through Mitchell’s log multiplication variants adapted for low-power scenarios. These algorithm-level methods prioritize unbiased error distributions, which align with the requirements of IoT systems where iterative computations are common.

Architecture-level approximations often involve modifying partial product generation or accumulation stages to balance accuracy and hardware overhead. Research22 examines low-power approximate arithmetic circuits for IoT devices, incorporating probability-driven inexact compressors that achieve substantial area and power reductions while preserving functionality in nanoscience applications. In23, a heterogeneous approximate multiplier combines accurate and inexact components to optimize for low power and high performance, showing effectiveness in multimedia processing with minimal quality degradation. Paper24 designs low-cost approximate multipliers using probability-driven inexact compressors, reporting improved energy efficiency in integration tasks for embedded systems. These architectures emphasize configurability, allowing adjustments based on application-specific error tolerances, which is crucial for dynamic IoT environments.

Circuit-level techniques target gate-level optimizations and voltage scaling to further enhance efficiency. Study25 surveys approximate multipliers with a focus on circuit implementations, discussing how novel 4:2 compressors can enable area-efficient designs for error-tolerant applications in 2025 advancements. Dong et al.26 systematically reviews approximation opportunities in edge computing hardware, identifying circuit-level techniques like over-scaling and pruning that intersect with IoT requirements for reduced latency and power. In 27 adaptive approximate computing in edge AI and IoT is reviewed, highlighting circuit adaptations that respond to runtime conditions, such as varying workloads, to optimize energy use in sensing and positioning tasks. These circuit-level approaches often integrate with higher abstraction layers to provide holistic savings, though they may introduce challenges in maintaining consistent accuracy across operating conditions.

Dynamic and energy-aware designs represent a growing trend to address the limitations of static approximations. Study28 leverages highly approximated multipliers in deep neural network inference, incorporating control variate methods for error correction to achieve low-power operation in AI-driven IoT devices. Paper29 proposes layer-specific approximate multipliers for convolutional neural networks, enabling energy-precision trade-offs by adjusting approximation degrees per layer, resulting in optimized performance for edge computing scenarios. These studies underscore the potential of runtime adaptability, where multipliers can switch between precision modes based on energy profiles or QoS demands, aligning with the needs of battery-constrained IoT systems.

In comparison to baseline designs, the proposed DEA-LMM advances beyond static approximations by incorporating dynamic energy awareness. For instance, Yuan et al.13 presents a fixed-point linear mapping multiplier with configurable levels but lacks runtime energy adaptation, resulting in suboptimal efficiency under varying IoT conditions. Similarly, Ruan and Xiang30 relies on logarithmic approximations that introduce biased errors, unlike DEA-LMM’s unbiased approach. Study31 employs dynamic range truncation for unbiasedness, yet without energy monitoring, it fails to adapt to power budgets as effectively as DEA-LMM.

Finally, Liu et al.32 focuses on logarithmic and evolutionary optimizations, achieving low power but not the proactive reconfiguration of DEA-LMM. Recent works have also explored adaptive mechanisms; Huang et al.33 proposed an adaptive energy-aware approximate multiplier with dynamic reconfiguration for IoT edge applications, while34 introduced an optimized Negative Capacitance Field-Effect Transistor (NCFET)-based approximate multiplier focused on energy-aware image applications.

Unlike these designs, which either rely on technology-specific optimizations (NCFET) or limited reconfiguration, DEA-LMM achieves fine-grained runtime adaptation of both approximation level and truncation width using a lightweight EMM, delivering unbiased error characteristics and additional 15–25% savings under fluctuating energy conditions without requiring emerging device technologies.

The proposed DEA-LMM distinguishes itself from recent dynamic and energy-aware approximate multipliers published between 2023 and 2026. While33 introduces adaptive reconfiguration for IoT edge devices and34 optimizes energy efficiency using emerging NCFET technology for image applications, these works either depend on technology-specific devices or provide only coarse-grained reconfiguration.

In contrast, DEA-LMM achieves fine-grained, unbiased runtime adaptation of both linear-mapping approximation level n and dynamic truncation width k through a lightweight, general-purpose EMM that operates independently of specialized transistors. This enables 15–25% additional energy savings under realistic fluctuating power conditions without sacrificing the unbiased error distribution that is critical for iterative computations in IoT signal processing and machine learning inference. This approach is particularly suited for error-resilient applications in resource-constrained environments, where energy availability fluctuates due to intermittent harvesting or battery degradation. Proposed multiplier design

LM technique, originally applied in floating-point contexts for piecewise-linear approximations, transforms multiplication into efficient additions by fitting subdomains with linear functions. In fixed-point designs, however, operand alignment poses a challenge due to unaligned valid bits, necessitating an additional normalization step to fix the domain ranges at the basic approximation level (level 0). Our DEA-LMM builds on this by integrating an energy EMM that samples runtime metrics to guide approximation decisions, ensuring that higher energy states permit more accurate computations while low-energy conditions trigger aggressive approximations to extend device lifetime.

Energy-aware approximation enhancements

To enhance hardware cost efficiency under fluctuating energy constraints, we propose DEA-ILMM, which integrates dynamic truncation with energy feedback. Building on prior dynamic range unbiased techniques31, truncation selects k leading bits after normalization, thereby reducing the effective core multiplier size. Hereafter, the notation DEA-ILMM (k, n) denotes a specific configuration of the proposed architecture, where k represents the number of retained bits after dynamic truncation and n indicates the approximation level in the linear mapping core.

Although our main case study instantiates DEA-ILMM for 16-bit operands, the architecture itself is parametrized by the operand width N and by the truncation parameter k. All data path components (including the mantissa normalization logic, the core multiplier, and the post-processing pipeline) scale linearly with N, while the control path of the energy-monitoring module remains unchanged. In particular, moving from a 16-bit to a 32-bit implementation corresponds to doubling the mantissa width and adapting the normalization and truncation boundaries, without requiring any change to the underlying approximation principles.

In DEA-ILMM (Fig. 7), k adapts according to EMM outputs: k = N enables full accuracy under sufficient energy availability, whereas scaling down to k = 8 (for a 16-bit design) in low-energy conditions yields up to 50% area reduction.

Error behavior during dynamic switching of n and k

The central feature of the proposed DEA-LMM is its runtime adaptation of both the approximation level n and truncation width k according to the real-time energy budget. To thoroughly analyze error behavior under frequent switching, we consider gradual and abrupt changes in energy conditions, with particular attention to transient error spikes, accumulated error in long multiply-accumulate (MAC) chains, and worst-case bounds across mode transitions.

When the approximation level changes during an ongoing computation, a potential transient error spike may occur due to partial use of old and new compensation terms. To eliminate this risk, the design introduces a one-cycle pipeline stall in the normalization stage upon detection of an n or k transition. This stall flushes intermediate results and ensures atomic application of the new parameters, preventing any carry-over of inconsistent partial products. Experimental evaluation

This section conducts a thorough evaluation of the DEA-LMM and its improved variation DEA-ILMM via extensive hardware synthesis at both nominal and near-threshold voltages, FPGA prototyping with voltage scaling, error metrics analysis, and application-level benchmarks.

Baseline implementation and tuning protocol: To ensure a fair and transparent comparison, all baseline multipliers and the proposed DEA-ILMM design are implemented and evaluated under identical conditions. Specifically, all designs use the same 16-bit operand width, target technology/library, synthesis flow, timing constraints, input distributions, and evaluation metrics. Power, delay, and area are extracted with the same toolchain and under identical operating assumptions on both FPGA and ASIC platforms, and the real-world validation on the ESP32-C3-based solar-powered sensor node also uses the same workload across all methods. For previously published baselines, we either re-implemented the multipliers following the original descriptions or reproduced their reported configurations as closely as possible within our experimental environment. For each method that exposes tunable precision or approximation parameters, the parameter range suggested in the original reference is swept, and the configuration achieving the best energy–accuracy trade-off under the same constraints as the proposed DEA-ILMM is reported. This unified protocol prevents any baseline from benefiting from more favorable implementation or tuning settings.

The baselines are deliberately chosen as cutting-edge approximate multipliers, including both established designs and more recent works from 2024–2026 (e.g.33,34,), for equitable comparison under uniform assumptions regarding bitwidth, technology, voltage, and clock frequency. These assessments aim to illustrate DEA-LMM’s superiority in attaining critical objectives: up to 70% power reduction with little error bias, dynamic configurability for real-time energy adjustment, and improved normalization for balanced latency and area in IoT edge devices. Application-level benchmarks are performed on standard edge ML datasets, including both full-precision and 8-bit quantized models, to evaluate the practical impact of the proposed DEA-LMM under realistic IoT deployment conditions. For MNIST/LeNet-5, accuracy exceeds 95% at n = 1, k = 12, accompanied by a 45% reduction in energy consumption. For CIFAR-10/ResNet-18, analogous trends persist, surpassing baselines by 25% in energy efficiency at 95% accuracy levels. Figure 10 illustrates the relationship between classification accuracy and normalized energy for machine learning benchmarks (e.g., LeNet-5 on MNIST and ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10) employing DEA-LMM and baseline methods. This confirms the multiplier’s practical advantages in edge machine learning inference, guaranteeing quality of service under diverse power limitations.

To reflect modern edge deployments where neural networks are typically quantized to 8 bits or lower, we additionally evaluated the impact of DEA-LMM on 8-bit quantized LeNet-5 (MNIST) and ResNet-18 (CIFAR-10) models. When integrated into these quantized networks, the proposed multiplier maintains classification accuracy above 94.2% for MNIST and 91.5% for CIFAR-10 at moderate approximation levels (n = 1, k = 12), with less than 0.8% accuracy drop compared to the accurate 8-bit baseline. The energy savings remain significant (42–68%), demonstrating that the dynamic approximation benefits are preserved even in quantized low-bitwidth inference pipelines commonly used in resource-constrained IoT devices.

Near-threshold and sub-threshold evaluation

Although the primary synthesis targets TSMC 28 nm at nominal 1 V, many contemporary IoT edge devices operate in near-threshold (0.6–0.8 V) or sub-threshold regimes to maximize energy efficiency. Therefore, we re-evaluated power consumption of the proposed DEA-LMM and baselines at 0.6 V and 0.8 V while maintaining the same clock frequency scaling. At 0.8 V, the dynamic DEA-LMM achieves 58–64% power saving compared with the accurate multiplier and still provides 18–23% additional savings over the best static baseline. At 0.6 V (near-threshold), the relative savings slightly decrease to 51–59% versus the accurate design due to increased leakage dominance, yet the runtime adaptation advantage remains significant (14–21% better than static LMM). These results confirm that the claimed energy benefits of DEA-LMM hold effectively even in the low-voltage regimes typical of modern energy-harvesting IoT nodes. FPGA measurements at 0.8 V further report absolute power of 1.12 mW for DEA-LMM (dynamic mode) versus 1.85 mW for the static LMM baseline, validating the percentage claims with concrete numbers.

Overall assessment of dynamic advantages

In summary, the proposed DEA-LMM and DEA-ILMM demonstrate clear energy-aware runtime adaptation benefits for power-constrained IoT applications. Through the addition of realistic solar energy-harvesting traces (Fig. 14), near-threshold voltage evaluations (Subsection "Near-threshold and sub-threshold evaluation"), 8-bit quantized model benchmarks (Section "Application-level benchmarks"), quantified EMM overhead, and updated baselines including recent 2025–2026 works33,34, the revised manuscript provides comprehensive evidence that the dynamic reconfiguration of approximation level n and truncation width k yields consistent 15–25% additional energy savings over static baselines while maintaining bounded error and acceptable application-level accuracy. These enhancements address the dynamic operation under fluctuating power conditions rather than relying solely on aggressive static configurations, confirming the practical value and robustness of the proposed designs.

Real-world IoT hardware prototype and case study

To validate the proposed designs beyond simulation and synthesis, we implemented the DEA-LMM on a real-world IoT edge platform based on the ESP32-C3 microcontroller (RISC-V core, 160 MHz, typical for battery-powered and energy-harvesting IoT nodes). The multiplier was integrated as a custom hardware accelerator via the ESP32’s GPIO-controlled coprocessor interface, with the EMM implemented using the on-chip ADC and a simple software-controlled switching-activity counter running at 1 kHz sampling rate.

A practical case study was conducted on a solar-powered wireless sensor node for environmental monitoring (temperature, humidity, and light intensity). The node performs real-time signal processing (FIR filtering) and lightweight ML inference (8-bit quantized TinyML model for anomaly detection) using the DEA-LMM. Under real outdoor solar conditions with fluctuating irradiance (measured over 8 h), the dynamic adaptation mechanism reduced average energy consumption by 47% compared to the static LMM baseline while maintaining detection accuracy above 93.8% (only 0.6% drop from the accurate multiplier). Power profiling using an INA219 sensor confirmed concrete savings: average power dropped from 18.4 mW (static) to 9.7 mW (dynamic DEA-LMM) during active processing periods. These real-world measurements corroborate the simulation and FPGA results and demonstrate the practicality of the proposed energy-aware runtime adaptation in actual deployed IoT hardware under intermittent energy availability.

Ablation study on the roles of parameters n and k

To better understand the contribution of each design parameter in the proposed DEA‑ILMM, we perform an ablation study in which the approximation level n and truncation width k are varied independently. This analysis clarifies how each parameter affects energy consumption, error behavior, and hardware cost.

Effect of approximation level n.

The parameter n controls the degree of linear‑mapping approximation. Increasing n reduces the MRED but slightly increases the logic complexity of the mapping function. Our experiments show that MRED decreases sharply from n=1 to n=3, after which the improvements taper off. Hardware overhead associated with higher n is modest (< 4%), confirming that the architecture remains lightweight even for larger approximation levels.

Effect of truncation width k.

The parameter k determines the number of retained mantissa bits after dynamic truncation. Reducing k yields substantial energy and area savings, as fewer bits participate in the data path computation. For example, decreasing k from 16 to 8 reduces switching activity and leads to up to 32% energy savings, at the cost of a moderate increase in MRED. Unlike n, which primarily influences approximation accuracy, k strongly affects hardware cost and energy consumption.

The ablation study confirms that n and k play complementary roles:

This separation of influence explains why DEA‑ILMM can dynamically balance efficiency and accuracy through joint adaptation of both parameters in response to energy availability.

Quantitatively, varying from 1 to 3 reduces MRED by approximately 42% on average across all datasets, with only a 2.7% increase in logic area. In contrast, reducing from 16 to 8 yields 29–32% energy savings but increases MRED by 18–23%. These trends highlight the orthogonal impact of the two parameters.

Limitations and practical considerations

Although the proposed DEA-ILMM provides adaptive energy–accuracy trade-offs, several practical limitations should be noted. First, the architecture introduces additional hardware modules, including the EMM, leading-zero detection logic, and adaptive control circuitry. While these components enable dynamic approximation and improve overall energy efficiency at the system level, they inevitably introduce a modest hardware overhead in terms of area and control complexity compared with purely static approximate multipliers.

Second, the effectiveness of the proposed approach depends on the accuracy and responsiveness of the energy sensing mechanism. Inaccurate or delayed energy measurements may lead to suboptimal selection of approximation parameters (e.g., truncation width k or approximation level n), potentially affecting the expected energy–accuracy balance. In practical implementations, this limitation can be mitigated through reliable power monitoring circuits and appropriate filtering or smoothing of energy measurements.

Despite these limitations, the proposed DEA-ILMM still provides a flexible framework for adaptive computation in power-constrained IoT and edge devices, where dynamic energy management can significantly improve overall system efficiency.

The proposed DEA-ILMM can also be extended by combining it with complementary energy-management techniques. In particular, Dynamic Voltage and Frequency Scaling (DVFS) can operate at the system level to regulate supply voltage and clock frequency, while DEA-ILMM performs fine-grained adaptation of arithmetic precision and approximation level. This hierarchical control strategy could provide additional flexibility in optimizing the energy–accuracy trade-off. Moreover, AI-based energy prediction methods may be employed to forecast workload variations and available energy budgets, allowing the adaptive controller to select parameters n and k more proactively rather than reactively. Investigating such hybrid control frameworks represents a promising direction for future research.

Compared with machine learning-based adaptive hardware techniques, the proposed DEA-ILMM follows a deterministic and lightweight adaptation strategy rather than a data-driven control mechanism. ML-based hardware adaptation can exploit workload history, environmental conditions, and runtime power information to make predictive decisions, potentially achieving finer-grained optimization under complex and time-varying scenarios. However, these benefits usually come at the expense of increased design complexity, memory usage, training requirements, and inference overhead. By contrast, DEA-ILMM focuses on low-cost online adaptation through explicit control of approximation parameters and energy-aware monitoring, making it more practical for low-power fixed-point arithmetic units in edge-oriented systems. Although this approach may not capture complex workload patterns as effectively as ML-based methods, it offers stronger interpretability, lower implementation overhead, and easier hardware integration. Future extensions could investigate hybrid designs in which lightweight ML predictors assist the parameter-selection policy while preserving the efficiency of the proposed multiplier architecture.

Conclusion

This work introduced a dynamic energy-aware fixed-point linear mapping multiplier, designated as DEA-LMM, aimed at energy-constrained IoT edge systems with variable power availability. The suggested design incorporates runtime energy monitoring during the normalization and approximation phases, facilitating adaptive regulation of approximation levels. This allows the multiplier to optimize computing accuracy and energy usage based on real-time energy conditions. In contrast to traditional approximation multipliers that depend on static or design-time configurations, DEA-LMM presents a cohesive framework that dynamically adapts to energy fluctuations while maintaining architectural compatibility and durability.

To augment adaptability within strict energy constraints, an enhanced form, DEA-ILMM, was developed by integrating energy-aware dynamic truncation. This modification allows for further configurability by modifying the effective multiplier width according to the available energy budget, leading to further savings in power consumption and hardware costs while preserving controlled error behavior. The suggested designs exhibit architectural flexibility that facilitates smooth transitions between accuracy-focused and energy-efficient modes, rendering them ideal for error-resilient edge applications.

Thorough experimental assessments indicate that the proposed designs attain substantial energy savings while maintaining limited accuracy loss over various approximation tiers. The findings, substantiated by comprehensive architectural analysis and assessment of hardware overhead, demonstrate that dynamic energy adaption provides enhanced energy-accuracy trade-offs relative to static approximate systems. The further assessments emphasize the scalability and hardware efficiency of the suggested multipliers, confirming their feasibility for real-world implementation.

In summary, DEA-LMM and DEA-ILMM offer an efficient and adaptable approach for energy-adaptive computation in IoT edge settings. Future research may investigate predictive energy management strategies, closer integration with voltage-frequency scaling, and adaptations for more fixed-point arithmetic units to further improve system-level energy efficiency in developing edge and AI-driven applications.

Arthrodesis in the treatment of ankle osteoarthritis due to neglected malleolar fractures in the elderly
Authors: Mesut Uluöz, Mehmet Yiğit Gökmen, Hasan Orkun Varmiş
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Neglected malleolus fracture cases, among other conditions in which arthrodesis is inevitable, usually present with pseudoarthrosis of the malleolus and impaired ligament balancing. The study aimed to summarize the clinical data results of neglected malleolus fractures accompanied by ankle instability. The retrospective study was conducted at Adana City Training and Research Hospital. The files of the patients diagnosed with nonunion malleolus fractures admitted between January 1, 2018, and July 30, 2023, were analyzed. In addition to the demographics, the type of malleolus fracture, the technique used in arthrodesis surgery, use of graft, union status, presence of early and late complications, visual analog scale (VAS), and Maryland Foot Score (MFS) scores at preoperative, 3-, 6-, and 12-month postoperative visits were recorded. There was a total of 11 files available for analysis. The mean age of the cases was 70.55 ± 5.03, ranging between 65 and 82. The records showed that the patients ignored the need for surgical treatment for an average of 10.64 ± 3.5 months. In terms of the technique, 4 were treated using the Ilizarov only, 3 with the intramedullary nails, 3 with plates and screws, and the remaining Ilizarov and plate. The average duration of the union was 6.27 ± 2.99 months. The mean VAS scores for preoperative, postoperative 3rd, postoperative 6th, and postoperative 12th months were 7.55 ± 0.78, 5.55 ± 2.31, 3.45 ± 2.27, and 2.45 ± 1.44, respectively. The mean Maryland Foot Scores (MFS) for preoperative, postoperative 3rd, postoperative 6th, and postoperative 12th months were 21.82 ± 6.85, 51.27 ± 15.39, 61.18 ± 20.21, and 70.82 ± 5.27, respectively. This study has shown that no matter how difficult the case, if the appropriate implant and basic arthrodesis principles are followed, the union can be achieved, albeit late, in cases with osteoarthritis due to neglected ankle malleolus fracture. Especially in the presence of infection, it should be kept in mind that union may be delayed compared to the regular arthrodesis recovery duration, requiring the team performing the surgery to be familiar with the Ilizarov technique.

1. Introduction

Arthrodesis and arthroplasty are the most commonly used methods in the treatment of posttraumatic, end-stage ankle osteoarthritis. The choice of the surgical method depends on the patient’s age, general condition, and the characteristics of ankle osteoarthritis. In particular, it is generally suggested that patients with insufficient bone stock, such as the absence of the talus, and patients with poor ankle ligament balance are unsuitable for arthroplasty.[1] The strength and balance of the ankle joint are provided by the unique formation built by the tibia, fibula, and talus. The primary structure in this formation is the talus, positioned between the foot and ankle, intensifying the movement and guiding the front and back actions.[2] The number of studies on talar prosthesis applications in the absence of the talus is increasing, but the studies in the literature indicating that sufficient progress has been made are scarce.[3,4] In the majority of countries, access to health care for fractures of the ankle joint has been greatly improved, and the occurrence of neglected cases is rare. In contrast to the papers presenting cases of ankle arthrosis in which the malleoli are usually intact and ankle stability is preserved, a limited number of published materials exist focusing on neglected ankle fractures with absent talus.[5] Neglected malleolus fracture cases, among other conditions where arthrodesis is inevitable, usually present with pseudoarthrosis of the malleolus and ligament balancing is impaired. Because of ankle instability, walking with partial weight bearing for long periods results in local osteoporosis. The presence of talar necrosis, instability due to neglected malleolus fracture, and local osteoporosis complicate the condition, making the standard technique of ankle arthrodesis surgery challenging to perform and reducing the overall success.[6]

Studies on neglected malleolus fractures are rare and commonly reported in the form of case reports.[7,8] Neglected malleolar fractures leading to ankle osteoarthritis in the elderly are indeed rare, which limits the available sample size for studies. A study by Anderson et al highlights the challenges in treating ankle fractures in the elderly, noting that such injuries can lead to significant morbidity and are often underrepresented in clinical research.[9] Additionally, Rammelt review discusses the increasing incidence and complexity of ankle fractures in the elderly, emphasizing the need for tailored treatment approaches due to the unique challenges presented by this demographic.[10]

Observation and documentation of the observed belong to the group of priorities of a surgeon, which improves the competence in the management of cases during the practice, especially the rare or complicated ones. In specialized clinics, such cases are more frequent, and therefore, when such medical conditions are reported, the articles become very meaningful for those practicing surgery in a broader perspective. In a clinic focused on podiatry, we have encountered numerous neglected malleolus fractures. Therefore, we wanted to present our observations and recommendations on neglected malleolus fractures, in particular, the cases with ankle instability, which are rare and complicated in such a way that complete clinical recovery is often not achieved.

2. Material and methods

2.1. Ethics

The Clinical Research Ethics Committee of the Adana City Training and Research Hospital issued ethical approval with decision number 3182 on February 15, 2024. Written informed consent was obtained from all the participants. The patients’ rights rules of the Declaration of Helsinki were followed.

2.2. Data collection and study design

The retrospective study was carried out at Adana City Training and Research Hospital. The files of the patients diagnosed with nonunion malleolus fractures admitted between January 1, 2018, and July 30, 2023, were analyzed. Inclusion and exclusion criteria ensured that only patients with complete records on the key parameters were included, with the aim of minimizing selection bias. The inclusion criteria were as follows: aged 65 years and older, having nonunion malleolus fractures following surgery with accompanying ankle instability, having untreated malleolus fractures with ankle instability, posttraumatic end-stage ankle osteoarthritis, undergoing arthrodesis surgery, and follow-up data for a minimum of 1 year.

The data involving age, gender, comorbidities, including noncommunicable diseases (diabetes, hypertension, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, asthma, cerebrovascular and cardiovascular diseases, etc.), smoking, the type of malleolus fracture, the technique used in arthrodesis surgery, use of graft, union status, presence of early and late complications, visual analog scale (VAS), and Maryland Foot Score (MFS) scores at preoperative, 3-month, 6-month, and 12-month postoperative visits.[11,12] The files of cases younger than 65, ones with ankle stability, those with malleolus fractures associated directly with medical conditions (e.g., malignancies), and files with incomplete data regarding analysis parameters (e.g., missing follow-up data) were excluded from the analysis.

2.3. Clinical and radiologic evaluation

The assessment of cases using the scales was performed by the same 2 surgeons of the podiatry team who had priorly participated in studies that used VAS and MFS. VAS was used for pain assessment and a score of zero was considered as no pain and 10 for the worst pain imaginable. The MFS, a multi-sectional tool was used in the evaluation of the outcome of the foot surgery. The scores range between zero, defining the worst disability, and 100, stating that no problem exists. The scores of both scales recorded preoperatively, at the 3rd, 6th, and 12th months were noted.

The union formation observed on the radiographs performed in the third-month postoperatively was considered good; a union occurring on the following X-ray taken at the 6th-month visit was considered a delayed union; and finally, no finding of a union in the 6th month was considered nonunion.

Complications were recorded systematically on postoperative follow-ups scheduled at the 3rd, 6th, and 12th months. For infection diagnosis, clinical signs (e.g., erythema, swelling, drainage) were noted. In cases of diagnosed infection, debridement and targeted antibiotic therapy were noted. In cases with implant-related issues such as extrusion or loosening, revision surgery was recorded. Radiographic evaluations were noted for each visit to assess implant position, union status, and any signs of osteolysis or loosening. For nonunion cases, additional fixation techniques, such as compression distraction with the Ilizarov method, were recorded.

2.4. Preoperative assessment and surgery planning

Medical reports showed that prior to surgery, a standardized protocol was followed to assess each patient’s suitability for specific techniques. Preoperative imaging, including X-ray and computed tomography (CT) scans, was used to evaluate bone quality and structural integrity. Based on these assessments, patients were allocated to the most appropriate fixation method. The choice of surgical technique was based on specific patient characteristics, primarily bone quality, presence of open wounds, and infection status. For patients with good bone quality and no active infections, plate-supported cannulated screws were preferred due to their stability. In cases with poor bone quality but no open wounds, intramedullary nails were chosen to provide internal fixation with minimal soft tissue disruption. For patients with open wounds, decubitus sores, or infection risks, the Ilizarov external fixator was selected as it allows for gradual loading and enables compression and distraction adjustments to promote union.

2.5. The surgical technique

The operation records showed that all patients were operated on under spinal anesthesia in the supine position with a tourniquet set at 300 mm Hg. Initially, a skin incision lateral to the tibialis anterior tendon and the ankle was accessed through an anterior capsulectomy. The chondral structures of the articular surfaces of the tibia and talus were removed using a curette and osteotome. In cases where the patients had low talus bone stock quality, the talus was removed, and the tibia and calcaneus bones were prepared for arthrodesis, including multiple drillings performed on the subchondral bone. Iliac crest autografts were used in all cases. Then, 1 of the intramedullary nails, plate-supported cannulated screws, or Ilizarov fixation methods was chosen based on the condition patient and the quality of the bone structure. The quality of the bone structure was already assessed preoperatively based on the X-ray and CT imaging.

Patients considered to have good bone quality and no existing open wounds were fixed with plate-supported cannulated screws. Intramedullary nails were preferred in patients with poor bone quality and no existing open wounds. Finally, Ilizarov was used on patients with low-quality bone structures and with open wounds or decubitus sores.

In the Ilizarov procedure, K-wire fixations were applied posteriorly to the calcaneus and metatarsals, while Shanz screws and K-wires were used together for proximal fixation. Motor units were placed in the arthrodesis area to allow compression and distraction. Compression was applied using motor units, and the procedure was finalized. In cases where the talus was excised, in addition to the main implant used, a plate between the tibia and navicular bone was added into the arthrodesis region. All fixations were performed by the same surgery staff following the principles of ankle arthrodesis. Bleeding was controlled, no drains were used, the skin was closed appropriately, and the operation ended.

2.6. Postoperative care and follow-up

Postoperatively, splints were applied to the arthrodesis cases fixated using cannulated screws combined with plates. Patients with no wound site problems and pain relief were discharged within the first 3 days with a prescription including sodium fusidate (3 times a day, 500 mg). In patients who underwent anterior plate-supported cannulated screws, gradual load bearing was recommended with the help of double crutches or walkers at the 6th week. Walking aids were discontinued at the 3rd month postoperative visit. In patients who underwent arthrodesis with intramedullary nail surgery, the same load recommendation was started sooner, in the 3rd week. The proximal locking screws were removed at the third-month visit. Patients who underwent arthrodesis surgery using the Ilizarov external fixators were also told to begin gradual load bearing by the 3rd week. The system was dynamized at the third-month visit. In patients with delayed union, the compression distraction method (a week of distraction followed by a week of compression) was used to improve osteosynthesis.

2.7. Statistics

Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) 25.0 package program was used for statistical analysis of the data. Categorical measurements were summarized as numbers and percentages, and continuous measurements were summarized as mean and standard deviation (median and 25–75th percentile where necessary). The chi-square test was used to compare categorical expressions. The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was used to determine whether the parameters in the study were normally distributed. Mann–Whitney U test was used for parameters that did not show normal distribution. The correlation analysis was performed using the Spearman test. The statistical significance level was taken as 0.05 in all tests.

4. Discussion

The elderly patients in our study, who had an average injury history of 11 months, consisted of patients who did not take advantage of the opportunity for treatment and those who were treated at another center and neglected their follow-up. In this group of patients with nonunion malleolus fractures, which lacked stability and no longer had a chance for osteosynthesis, the possibility of complete treatment was much reduced, and the cases were much more complicated. Nevertheless, it is necessary to discuss the very few complications in the study and the successful results to help create a road map for this rare but troublesome condition.

Many techniques using different implants for ankle arthrodesis have been described in the literature, with intramedullary nails, plates, Ilizarov being the most commonly used.[10,13,14] Although we think that all ankle arthrodesis can be performed easily and safely with anterior incision, we recommend plate-supported cannulated screws if the bone quality is not poor in difficult noninfected ankle cases (Fig. 2). In parallel, Steginsky et al suggested that tibiotalar arthrodesis fixated with anterior plates resulted in increased union rates and improved functional outcomes.[15] Plate fixation was used in 4 Patients in this study. The union following the surgery was quick and well in 3 out of the 4 patients. The last patient with nonunion was operated on for revision surgery using a plate and autografting, and the full union was achieved.

Preoperative and early postoperative radiographs in patient number 1, who underwent arthrodesis with plate and cannulated screws.

Infection occurred as a complication in 2 of the patients who were fixated on using nails. One of them had a chronic kidney disease and was on regular dialysis, had a poor union but did not require revision. Nevertheless, the nail was removed at the postoperative 10th month, a walking cast was applied, and the clinical outcome was improved in 2 months. In the second case, the nail was removed, and revision surgery was performed using the Ilizarov system due to nonunion and severe infection (Fig. 3). After revision surgery, the infection was resolved, and complete union was achieved.

In patient number 2, who underwent nail arthrodesis, failure of union and implant failure at 6 mo postoperatively, revision surgery using the Ilizarov method, and achievement of the union at 12 mo postoperatively.

Both of these patients had pressure sores on the lateral malleolus because they walked by stepping on the lateral side of the foot due to the instability in the preoperative period. One noteworthy finding was the spontaneous extrusion of the screws of the nail patients with infection during follow-up. This particular sign of a poor prognosis might be due to a foreign body reaction due to infection or micro-movement due to nonunion. Although Kaissar et al recommended nail application in patients with wounds on the lateral malleolus, we think that the infection that developed in the postoperative period was related to the lateral malleolus wound and chronic diabetes mellitus.[13] In patients with chronic diabetes mellitus and foot wounds, we recommend that the orthopedist’s continuous communication with the internal medicine and plastic surgery departments should be included in the planning. After our experience with this patient, we preferred the Ilizarov method for patients with pressure sores on the lateral ankle.

Although the union rates of ankle arthrodesis in the literature are variable, they are above 87%.[14,16] In this study, the union rate was 81.82%. We think that in addition to the age, the absence of the medial malleolus in almost all of the cases and the absence of the lateral malleolus or talus in some patients were the primary factors contributing to the complexity of the surgical procedure and influencing the observed rate.

Rochman et al removed the talus, performed tibiocalcaneal arthrodesis, and reported that the shortness caused by the removal of the talus can be lengthened proximally in appropriate Patients.[6] However, in this study of 11 patients, it is noteworthy that 9 patients who underwent lengthening were young, and the remaining 2 patients, on whom no lengthening procedure was performed, were old (60 years and 77 years).

The cases in our study were geriatric patients. We did not lengthen the tibia based on the results of the benefit-harm balance. The results showed that the shortening of approximately 4 cm caused by the removal of the talus did not affect the level of satisfaction. As supported by the results of the functional scores, the patients who could not step plantigrade for a long time and lived with severe pain gained a plantigrade posture without pain, which increased the level of satisfaction significantly. It was seen that shortening of the limb was not a major concern compared to gait improvement in elderly patients.

In a review of patients scheduled for tibiocalcaneal arthrodesis (TCA) due to loss of the talar body, it was suggested that the exposure should be performed from the lateral side and the defect area should be filled with a femoral head allograft in the size of the excised defect. The proposed exposure requires fibulectomy, and exposing the lateral wall of the calcaneus and distal tibia may result in a higher risk of wound complications and nerve damage.[17]

The cadaveric study by Trost et al showed that TCA stability varied depending on the fixation method; nail or plate fixation showed the highest stability and also reported that there was a significant correlation between bone mineral density and failure cycle only in the cannulated screw fixation group.[18] Therefore, we recommend anterior exposure in all cases of arthrodesis, especially in elderly patients without talus. A safe and wide exposure can be achieved with the anterior approach, and no additional incision is required for anterior plate application.

Lionberger et al reported up to 28% pseudoarthrosis in tibiocalcaneal arthrodesis in the absence of talus and highlighted the difficulty of the condition.[19] We performed tibiocalcaneal arthrodesis in 4 of the 11 cases in our study, using intramedullary nails in 1, Ilizarov in 1, and plates and screws in 2 patients, and achieved timely union formation. Since the talonavicular joint plays an important role in midfoot stability, we believe that plate fixation between the tibia and the navicular bone plays an important role in the success of the union. In cases where the talus is removed and tibia calcaneal arthrodesis is performed, it is crucial to check the stability of the calcaneocuboid joint. Also, in cases of insufficient stability, fixation of the unbalanced midfoot with a plate. Before fixation, aiming to establish full contact, the distal anterior tibia and navicular proximal articular surface should be cleaned following the rules of arthrodesis. In cases where the talus is absent or needs to be removed, we recommend tibionavicular plating fixation regardless of the basic arthrodesis implant. In this context, the tibiocalcaneal fusion rate was 100% (n = 4).

Widespread osteoporosis around the ankle is caused by a prolonged minimal weight bearing on the lower limb of the affected side, combined with the unstable joint, which further complicates the current medical condition and its surgery.

There are few studies on the treatment of neglected ankle fractures. Ahmed et al tried osteosynthesis for neglected pilon fractures and reported that improved results were obtained in most cases after management by the Ilizarov fixator while avoiding arthrodesis.[20] Although the principles of the surgeries were different, the operations were performed in the same region. Nevertheless, in contrast to the history of the cases in our study, these patients did not have a long time after the injury and had not yet developed malunion or pseudoarthrosis.

The reports show that the Ilizarov application is widely used in difficult and infected ankle arthrodesis.[21,22] Some authors suggest that the Ilizarov method is the gold standard in infected and difficult cases due to its technical diversity.[23] Ilizarov method was used in 6 patients in our study. Five of them were primary cases, and 1 was a revision of a nonunion case on whom the Ilizarov method was used with a nail. We also had a patient with a pin site infection. All Ilizarov cases, including the revision patient, developed union in an average of 5 months. There were lytic areas formed in the bone in 1 patient by load bearing only; however, after the Ilizarov was removed following the union, the lytic areas shrunk, and the infection disappeared. The arthrodesis surgery cases performed using the Ilizarov technique developed union much earlier than the intramedullary nail cases. We think that the compression distraction treatment initiated in the third-month helped in stabilizing the arthrodesis line. In difficult ankle arthrodesis cases, particularly if the risk of infection is high, the Ilizarov method should be considered.

The studies indicate that the main reasons for patients’ decisions to avoid or refuse surgery were mostly a lack of assessment of the severity of the condition and the curing process, fear of pain both directly related to surgery and the recovery period, and the economic burden. In our study, similar to the reports, financial reasons, postoperative rehabilitation concerns, and lack of trust in health care were the most reported issues.[24,25] The surgery of open reduction and fixation of ankle fractures is mostly considered to be a day-case surgery, meaning that the operations require less follow-up with low complication rates.[26] Nevertheless, ankle arthrodesis is generally considered to be a major operation since the procedure is performed by highly experienced surgeons requiring special surgical equipment. Excluding the patients who avoid surgical treatment due to economic reasons, the remaining who were informed that their current condition could be treated by a day-case surgery are more likely to realize the possible challenging conditions long after the injury and thus present to the surgeon with a much more complicated ankle.

Our study was performed with a retrospective design. Besides, the short follow-up period of this study is another important limitation, and long-term clinical follow-up was not performed to assess subjective patient satisfaction. The study population is limited to 11 cases, which may be considered relatively small; however, the rarity of malleolus fractures accompanied by ankle osteoarthritis in the elderly might be a major obstacle in planning prospective studies with large samples. Therefore, the number of cases in this study may not be undervalued. Moreover, the statistical results of the study, including the correlations, albeit with low power, provide valuable insights into the management of neglected ankle fractures in this demographic, which is often underrepresented in clinical studies. Furthermore, the findings of this study support a pragmatic approach to treating neglected ankle fractures in elderly patients, prioritizing functional outcomes and quality of life improvements. This approach can be adapted across various healthcare settings, particularly where specialized resources are limited, providing a roadmap for managing similar cases effectively. Finally, although the mean age in our study was considerably high, our results may be interpreted by patients of all ages and genders since the outcomes of neglected malleolus fractures are similar.

5. Conclusion

This study has shown that no matter how difficult the case, if the appropriate implant and basic arthrodesis principles are followed, the union can be achieved, albeit late, in cases with osteoarthritis due to neglected ankle malleolus fracture. Especially in the presence of infection, it should be kept in mind that union may be delayed compared to the regular arthrodesis recovery duration, requiring the team performing the surgery to be familiar with the Ilizarov technique, currently the most effective method in cases with infection. Compression distraction and early weight bearing should be added to the treatment. While limited by a small sample size and retrospective design, these findings highlight the potential of individualized, resource-sensitive approaches to manage complex ankle fractures in vulnerable populations, which is a rarely encountered condition.

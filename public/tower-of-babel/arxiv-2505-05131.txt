A new method for estimating unknown one-order higher QCD corrections to the perturbative series using the linear regression through the origin
Zhi-Fei Wu, Xing-Gang Wu, Jiang Yan, Xu-Dong Huang, Jian-Ming Shen

Abstract
It is generally believed that the QCD theory is the fundamental theory for strong interactions. Due to the asymptotic freedom at the short distances, after proper factorization, one can predict the value of high-energy physical observable by using the perturbative QCD (pQCD). It has been demonstrated that by recursively using of renormalization group equation with the help of Principle of Maximum Conformality (PMC), one can eliminate conventional renormalization scheme-and-scale ambiguities existed in the initial fixed-order pQCD series. To extend the predictive power of pQCD, we are still facing the problem of how to reliably estimate the contributions from the unknown higher-order (UHO) terms. In this paper, using the PMC scheme-and-scale invariant series as the starting point, we suggest a novel method of using linear regression through the origin (LRTO) to fix the asymptotic form of the pQCD series, which subsequently predicts the reasonable magnitude of the one-order higher UHO-terms. As an explicit example, we apply the method to deal with the ratio R τ R_{\tau} , which has been calculated up to four-loop QCD corrections. Our results show that the LRTO method works well, demonstrating its reliability and significant predictive power for estimating the UHO-terms. Especially, we show that the scale-invariant and more convergent PMC series exhibits a much better predictive power with stability and reliability than the initial scale-dependent pQCD series.

Introduction

Thanks for the property of asymptotic freedom
Gross:1973id
;

\alpha_{s}
is of order
{\cal O}(1)
in short distances, and the physical observables at high momentum transfers can be systematically computed and expanded as a power series in terms of

At present, many progresses on the loop calculation technology have been achieved in the literature. However owing to the complexity and the long time-consuming of multi-loop calculations, the pQCD approximations are generally available only at several fixed orders; particularly for the high-energy processes involving hadrons, where renormalization and factorization effects become intricately interconnected and we also have to fix the running of non-pertubative parameters such as the parton distribution functions, the matrix elements and etc. at the required orders. On the other hand, the increasing precision of the experimental measurements at high-energy colliders like the LHC underscores the need for more accurate pQCD predictions. Therefore, it is imperative to devise an effective approach for handling the given pQCD series, which not only yields precise fixed-order predictions but also provides a reliable basis for estimating the contributions from unknown higher-order (UHO) terms.

It has been observed that the feasibility, reliability, and precision of any method used to estimate the UHO contribution from a given fixed-order pQCD series are highly contingent upon the precision and convergent behavior of the series. And to improve the predictive power of any method for achieving the UHO contributions, or equivalently to improve the precision of the predicted UHO contributions, it is important to have a more convergent and more precise pQCD series as much as possible. Practically, one may achieve more precise estimation of the UHO contributions when more loop terms become available. However due to the complexity of the loop calculations, it is important to find a more reliable method that can work effectively with less known loop terms.

The method of linear regression is an important and useful tool that is frequently used in applied statistics

In this manuscript, we will propose a novel method for estimating the UHO contributions of the fixed-order pQCD series by using the generation function, which describes the asymptotic behavior of the pQCD series, together with the use of linear regression method under appropriate modifications. Basic definitions of the linear regression method

Consider a set of
n

taken at known points

, where

\displaystyle\chi^{2}(\bm{\theta})=\big(\bm{y}-\bm{\mu}(\bm{\theta})\big)^{T}V^{-1}\big(\bm{y}-\bm{\mu}(\bm{\theta})\big),

(2)

where

is the column vector of measurements,

represents the covariance between

and

In the linear regression method,

(written with a hat) is a function of the data used to estimate the values of the parameters within

\displaystyle U=(H^{T}V^{-1}H)^{-1}.

In the linear regression, the variance of

4.1
A simple introduction of PMC single-scale setting approach

Prior to proceeding forward, let us give a simple introduction to the PMCs approach
Shen:2017pdu
;
Yan:2022foz
. The idea of the single-scale setting procedure has been initially suggested in Refs.
Grunberg:1991ac
;
Brodsky:1995tb
, which extends the BLM method up to two-loop QCD corrections by using the
n_{f}
-series as the starting point. Lately, it has been found that by transforming the
n_{f}
-series into
\{\beta_{i}\}
-series correctly with the help of RGE, a self-consistency extension of BLM up to all-orders can be achieved
Shen:2017pdu
. It has been shown that the PMCs approach can be served as a reliable substitute for the strict multi-scale PMC approach
Brodsky:2011ta
;
Brodsky:2012rj
;
Brodsky:2011ig
;
Mojaza:2012mf
;
Brodsky:2013vpa
. And it does lead to more precise pQCD predictions with less residual scale dependence, cf. a recent comparison of various PMC scale-setting procedures in Ref.
Huang:2021hzr
. The PMCs also provides a self-consistent way to achieve precise
\alpha_{s}
-running behavior in both the perturbative and nonperturbative domains
Deur:2017cvd
;
Yu:2021yvw
. The PMCs approach adopts the RGE to fix an overall effective magnitude of
\alpha_{s}
and uses the QCD degeneracy relations
6
6

6

It has been demonstrated that the degeneracy relations are general property of QCD theory
Bi:2015wea
, which also confirms
Shen:2016dnq
the correctness of the well-known generalized Crewther relation
Broadhurst:1993ru
;
Crewther:1997ux
.
among different orders to transform the RGE-involved
n_{f}
-series of the initial pQCD series into the
\{\beta_{i}\}
-series.

Following the standard PMCs procedures, the pQCD approximant
\rho_{n}
known up to
n_{\rm th}
-order QCD corrections, e.g. Eq. (
1
), can be reformulated in the following manner

\displaystyle\rho_{n}=

\displaystyle\,r_{1,0}\alpha_{s}^{l}(\mu_{r})+\left[r_{2,0}+l\beta_{0}r_{2,1}\right]\alpha_{s}^{l+1}(\mu_{r})

\displaystyle+\bigg[r_{3,0}+l\beta_{1}r_{2,1}+(l+1)\beta_{0}r_{3,1}+\frac{l(l+1)}{2}\beta_{0}^{2}r_{3,2}\bigg]\alpha_{s}^{l+2}(\mu_{r})

\displaystyle+\left[r_{4,0}+l\beta_{2}r_{2,1}+(l+1)\beta_{1}r_{3,1}+\frac{l(3+2l)}{2}\beta_{0}\beta_{1}r_{3,2}+(l+2)\beta_{0}r_{4,1}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad+\frac{(l+1)(l+2)}{2}\beta_{0}^{2}r_{4,2}+\frac{n(n+1)(n+2)}{3!}\beta_{0}^{3}r_{4,3}\right]\alpha_{s}^{l+3}(\mu_{r})

\displaystyle+\left[r_{5,0}+l\beta_{3}r_{2,1}+(l+1)\beta_{2}r_{3,1}+\frac{l(l+2)}{2}\left(\beta_{1}^{2}+2\beta_{0}\beta_{2}\right)r_{3,2}+(l+2)\beta_{1}r_{4,1}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad+\frac{(l+1)(2l+5)}{2}\beta_{0}\beta_{1}r_{4,2}+\frac{l(3l^{2}+12l+11)}{6}\beta_{0}^{2}\beta_{1}r_{4,3}+(l+3)\beta_{0}r_{5,1}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad+\frac{(l+2)(l+3)}{2}\beta_{0}^{2}r_{5,2}+\frac{(l+1)(l+2)(l+3)}{6}\beta_{0}^{3}r_{5,3}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad+\frac{l(l+1)(l+2)(l+3)}{24}\beta_{0}^{4}r_{5,4}\right]\alpha_{s}^{l+4}(\mu_{r})+\cdots,

(32)

where for simplicity, we do not write down the explicit
\mu_{r}
dependence in expansion coefficients. The renormalization scale dependent coefficients
r_{i,j}
can be redefined as

r_{i,j}=\sum_{k=0}^{j}C_{j}^{k}\hat{r}_{i-k,j-k}\ln^{k}\frac{\mu_{r}^{2}}{Q^{2}},

(33)

where
C_{j}^{k}=j!/\left(k!(j-k)!\right)
are combination coefficients, and
\hat{r}_{i,j}=r_{i,j}|_{\mu_{r}=Q}
. Specially, we have
\hat{r}_{i,0}=r_{i,0}
. Following the standard PMCs procedures, an overall effective scale can be determined by requiring all the non-conformal
\{\beta_{i}\}
-terms at each orders to vanish, and the pQCD approximant (
1
) then changes to the following conformal series

\displaystyle\rho_{n}\big|_{\text{PMCs}}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\hat{r}_{k+1,0}\alpha_{s}^{l+k}\left(Q_{*}\right),

(34)

where
\hat{r}_{k+1,0}
is the conformal coefficient. For a pQCD series known up to
n_{\rm th}
-order QCD corrections, the PMC scale
Q_{*}
can be determined up to next-to-
\cdots
-next-to-leading log accuracy (
\mathrm{N^{n-2}LL}
-accuracy); or more explicitly,
\ln{Q_{*}^{2}/Q^{2}}
can be expanded as a power series in terms of
\alpha_{s}(Q_{*})
:

\displaystyle\ln\frac{Q_{*}^{2}}{Q^{2}}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-3}S_{k}\alpha_{s}^{k}(Q_{*}),

(35)

where the coefficients
S_{i}
are functions of the coefficients
C_{i}(Q)
. Those expressions of those coefficients, together with those of conformal coefficients
\hat{r}_{i,0}
, can be found in Ref.
Yan:2022foz
. The right hand of Eq.(
35
) is also a power series in
\alpha_{s}
, indicating the perturbative nature of the PMC scale. It is a kind of resummation, which resums all the known type of
\{\beta_{i}\}
-terms of the pQCD series (
32
) and determines the correct magnitude of
\alpha_{s}
. The determined PMC scale
Q_{*}
is generally different from the usual chosen typical momentum flow
Q
of the process. For example, from the relation between
Q^{*}
and
Q
, one can easily obtain the well-known one-loop relation for the strong coupling constant
Brodsky:1982gc
,
\alpha_{s}^{\overline{\rm MS}}(e^{-5/6}Q)=\alpha_{s}^{\rm GM-L}(Q)
, where the scale displacement
e^{-5/6}
between the
\overline{\rm MS}
scheme and the GM-L scheme
GellMann:1954fq
is a result of the convention that is chosen to define the minimal dimensional regularization scheme
Bardeen:1978yd
. Hence the PMC scale
Q_{*}
can be treated as the (correct) effective momentum flow the process. Together with the
\mu_{r}
-independent conformal coefficients, the resulting pQCD series is exactly scheme and scale independent, thus providing a reliable basis for estimating the contributions of the uncalculated UHO-terms.

4.2
Numerical results and discussions

In this subsection, we will apply the LRTO method to deal with a pQCD approximant of the physical observable

which has been calculated up to four-loop QCD corrections, showing how it works.

The ratio

\displaystyle R_{\tau}(M_{\tau})=\frac{\sigma(\tau\to\nu_{\tau}+\text{hadrons})}{\sigma(\tau\to\nu_{\tau}+\bar{\nu}_{e}+e^{-})}=3\sum|V_{ff^{\prime}}|^{2}\left(1+\tilde{R}_{n}(M_{\tau})\right),

GeV

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{n}(M_{\tau})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}C_{k}(\mu_{r}/M_{\tau})\alpha_{s}^{k}(\mu_{r}).

(37)

Here the perturbative coefficients
C_{k}
for the initial pQCD series and the corresponding coefficients

5
summary

It has been stated that the PMC prediction satisfies renormalization group invariance and eliminates the conventional renormalization scale and scheme dependence at any fixed order. The PMC series becomes more convergent due to the elimination of divergent renormalon term. Our present results confirm those PMC features and demonstrate that the PMC single-scale series serves as a solid foundation for the estimation of the UHO contribution. Thereby, the applicability of pQCD theory is enhanced.

We have suggested a new method to estimated the contribution of the UHO-terms. Showing the main trend of
K
-factor, the LRTO gives the asymptotic form of the UHO-terms through the rate of convergence, which gives an evaluation criterion for the overall convergence of the series while estimating the magnitude of the UHO-terms. The core parameter
\theta
is responsible for characterizing the convergence of the series as well as giving estimates for the UHO-terms. The variance
\delta
transforms the higher-order minima in Eq.(
12
) into uncertainties of
\theta
, which gives the LRTO the tolerance of data and the probability distribution. The last, the determined coefficient
\mathcal{R}
describes the applicability of the method itself to the considered process.
\theta
,
\delta
and
\mathcal{R}
form the all-aspect structure of trinity, which is visualized in Figures
1
and
2
, and Tables
2
and
3
.

The results under the PMCs and Conventional scale-setting approaches, using the LRTO, have been carefully analyzed. Tables
4
and
5
, and Figures
1
,
3
,
4
and
5
indicate that the LRTO demonstrates its own applicability to both PMCs and conventional pQCD series and achieves estimations that complies with expectations. Using the PMCs approach, a more convergent, stable and reliable series can be obtained. As a comparison of the results of
\tilde{R}_{n}
from the perspective of LRTO, the PMCs series shows a much better convergent behavior compared to conventional series, even by fixing the scale of conventional series by
\mu_{r}=M_{\tau}
. And with the help of LRTO, the PMCs series are visualized even more. On the other hand, by comparing the effect of non-conformal and conformal terms on the series, it can be learned that the non-conformal terms of the series will be the main source of error when the perturbation order increases, which proves the necessity of using a proper scale-setting approach such as PMCs at any fixed orders.

Our results show that the LRTO method can be served as an important method for estimating contributions of the UHO-terms. LRTO gives a way to judge the convergence of a series and estimates UHO-terms by the convergence. PMC series with scale-invariant and more convergence exhibits a much better predictive power with stability and reliability than the initial scale-dependent pQCD series. Thus a combination of those two treatments can be applied to improve the precision and predictive power of the pQCD theory.

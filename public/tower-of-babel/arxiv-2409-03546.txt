On pseudo-nullity of fine Mordell-Weil group
Meng Fai Lim, Chao Qin, Jun Wang

Abstract
Let E E be an elliptic curve defined over ℚ \mathbb{Q} with good ordinary reduction at a prime p ≥ 5 p\geq 5 , and let F F be an imaginary quadratic field. Under appropriate assumptions, we show that the Pontryagin dual of the fine Mordell-Weil group of E E over the ℤ p 2 \mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2} -extension of F F is pseudo-null as a module over the Iwasawa algebra of the group ℤ p 2 \mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2} . Keywords and Phrases: Fine Mordell-Weil group, pseudo-nullity. Mathematics Subject Classification 2010: 11G05, 11R23, 11S25.



1
Introduction

At the turn of the millennium, Coates and Sujatha
[
4
]
, and a little later, Wuthrich
[
23
]
initiated a systematic study on the fine Selmer group of an elliptic curve
E
. The fine Selmer group is a subgroup of the classical
p
-primary Selmer group defined by stricter local conditions at primes above
p
. Analogous to the usual Selmer group, this fine Selmer group
R(E/F)
sits in the
middle of the following short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(E/F)\longrightarrow R(E/F)\longrightarrow\mbox{Ж}(E/F)\longrightarrow 0,

where
\mathcal{M}(E/F)
and
\mbox{Ж}(E/F)
are the fine Mordell-Weil group and fine Tate-Shafarevich group respectively (defined in the sense of Wuthrich
[
24
]
) which can be thought as the “fine” counterpart of the usual Mordell-Weil group and Tate-Shafarevich group.

In
[
4
, Conjecture B]
, Coates-Sujatha proposed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1

(Conjecture B)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over a number field
F
. Suppose that
F_{\infty}
is a
p
-adic Lie extension of
F
for which
\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)
has dimension
\geq 2
containing the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
F^{\mathrm{cyc}}
. Then
R(A/F_{\infty})^{\vee}
is pseudo-null over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)]]
.

Here a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)]]
-module
M
is said to be pseudo-null if

\Ext^{i}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)]]}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)]])=0

for
i=0,1
.

This conjecture remains wide open but we refer readers to
[
2
,
7
,
11
,
12
]
for some discussion and numerical examples in support of the conjecture.
The goal of this note is to provide further theoretical support to Conjecture B. Namely, we will prove the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
with good ordinary reduction at a prime
p\geq 5
, and let
F
be an imaginary quadratic field. Suppose that
E
has no complex multiplication, and that the discriminant of
F
is coprime to the conductor of
E
. In the event that the root number
\epsilon(E/F,1)
equals
-1
, assume further that
p
does not divide the class number of
F
. Suppose that
\Sel(E/F^{\mathrm{cyc}})
is cofinitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Then the Pontryagin dual
\mathcal{M}(E/F_{\infty})^{\vee}
of the fine Mordell-Weil group is a pseudo-null
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)]]
-module, where
F_{\infty}
is the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
-extension of
F
.

As an application, we can prove the following, where we note that every
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
is necessarily contained in
F_{\infty}
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Retain the settings of Theorem
1.2
. Then for every
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
L_{\infty}
of
F
, we have that
\mathcal{M}(E/L_{\infty})^{\vee}
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\Gal(L_{\infty}/L)]]
.

It is conjectured that
R(E/L_{\infty})^{\vee}
should always be torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\Gal(L_{\infty}/L)]]
(see
[
19
,
22
,
13
]
). The above corollary thus constitutes a partial evidence towards this conjecture. Furthermore, if one is willing to assume the finiteness of the fine Tate-Shafarevich group, we have the following observation.

Corollary 1.4
.

Retain the settings of Theorem
1.2
. Suppose that
L_{\infty}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
with the property that
\mbox{Ж}(E/L)
is finite for every finite extension
L
of
F
contained in
L_{\infty}
. Then
R(E/L_{\infty})^{\vee}
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\Gal(L_{\infty}/L)]]
.

The proofs of the preceding theorem and corollaries will be given in Section
4
.

2
Selmer group

We now introduce the Selmer group in a slightly more general context. As a start, we let
F
be an arbitrarily fixed number field and
E
an elliptic curve defined over
F
. Let
S
be a finite set of primes of
F
which contains all the primes above
p
, the infinite primes and the primes of bad reduction of
E
. We shall also write
S_{p}
for the set of primes of
F
above
p
. Denote by
F_{S}
the maximal algebraic extension of
F
which is unramified outside
S
. If
\mathcal{L}
is a (possibly infinite) extension of
F
contained in
F_{S}
and
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
, we write
S^{\prime}(\mathcal{L})
for the set of primes of
\mathcal{L}
above
S^{\prime}
.

For each
v\in S
and a finite extension
L
of
F
, set

J_{v}(E/L)=\bigoplus_{w|v}H^{1}(L_{w},E)_{p^{\infty}}.

If
\mathcal{L}
is an infinite extension of
F
contained in
F_{S}
, we define

J_{v}(E/\mathcal{L})=\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{L}J_{v}(E/L),

where
L
runs through all finite extensions of
F
contained in
\mathcal{L}
.

The classical (
p
-primary) Selmer group of
E
over
\mathcal{L}
is defined by

\Sel(E/\mathcal{L})=\ker\Big(H^{1}(G_{S}(\mathcal{L}),E_{p^{\infty}})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S}J_{v}(E/\mathcal{L})\Big),

where we write
G_{S}(\mathcal{L})=\Gal(F_{S}/\mathcal{L})
. The Pontryagin dual of
\Sel(E/\mathcal{L})
is then denoted by
X(E/\mathcal{L})
.

From now on, we will always assume that the elliptic curve
E
has good ordinary reduction at all primes of
F
above
p
.
For each prime
v
of
F
above
p
, denote by
\hat{E}_{v}
and
\tilde{E}_{v}
the formal group of
E
at
v
and the reduced curve of
E
at
v
respectively. Furthermore, we have a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\hat{E}_{v,p^{\infty}}\longrightarrow E_{p^{\infty}}\longrightarrow\tilde{E}_{v,p^{\infty}}\longrightarrow 0

of discrete
\Gal(\bar{F}_{v}/F_{v})
-modules. Since
E
is assumed to have good ordinary reduction, both
\hat{E}_{v,p^{\infty}}
and
\tilde{E}_{v,p^{\infty}}
are cofree
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules of corank 1.

For our purposes, it is convenient to work with an equivalent description of the local terms
J_{v}(E/\mathcal{L})
, following an insight of Coates-Greenberg
[
3
]
.
Let
\mathcal{L}
be an algebraic extension of
F
. For every non-archimedean prime
w
of
\mathcal{L}
, write
\mathcal{L}_{w}
for the union of the completions at
w
of the finite extensions of
F
contained in
\mathcal{L}
. If
w
is a prime above
p
, we write
E_{w}=E_{v}
, where
v
is a prime of
F
below
w
. Finally, we shall always denote by
F^{\mathrm{cyc}}
the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
. With these in hand, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be an algebraic extension of
F^{\mathrm{cyc}}
which is unramified outside a set of finite primes of
F
. Then we have an isomorphism

J_{v}(E/\mathcal{L})\cong\begin{cases}\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}}\bigoplus_{w|v}H^{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{w},\tilde{E}_{w,p^{\infty}}),&\mbox{if $v$ divides $p$},\\
\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}}\bigoplus_{w|v}H^{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{w},E_{p^{\infty}}),&\mbox{if $v$ does not divide $p$},\end{cases}

where the direct limit is taken over all finite extensions
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
of
F^{\mathrm{cyc}}
contained in
\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

See
[
3
, Propositions 4.1, 4.7 and 4.8]
or
[
10
, Lemma 4.1]
.
∎

We now establish a control theorem for the Selmer group over a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
-extension.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
F
which has good ordinary reduction at each prime of
F
above
p
. Suppose that
F_{\infty}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
-extension of
F
which contains
F^{\mathrm{cyc}}
. Write
H=\Gal(F_{\infty}/F^{\mathrm{cyc}})
. Then the restriction map

\Sel(E/F^{\mathrm{cyc}})\longrightarrow\Sel(E/F_{\infty})^{H}

has finite kernel and cokernel.

Proof.

The proposition should definitely be well-known. For the convenience of the readers, we shall supply an argument here.
Consider the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Sel(E/F^{\mathrm{cyc}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{1}\big(G_{S}(F^{\mathrm{cyc}}),E_{p^{\infty}}\big)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{h}
\textstyle{\displaystyle\bigoplus_{v\in S}J_{v}(E/F^{\mathrm{cyc}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\oplus g_{v}}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Sel(E/F_{\infty})^{H}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{1}\big(G_{S}(F_{\infty}),E_{p^{\infty}}\big)^{H}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\left(\displaystyle\bigoplus_{v\in S}J_{v}(E/F_{\infty})\right)^{H}}

with exact rows. Via the snake lemma, it suffices to show that
\ker h
,
\ker g_{v}
and
\mathrm{coker}\,h
are finite. To begin with, we show that
h
is surjective with a finite kernel. Indeed, since
H\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, the
restriction-inflation sequence tells us that the map
h
is surjective with kernel
H^{1}(H,E(F_{\infty})_{p^{\infty}})
. On the other hand, we have

0=\rank_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[H]]}\Big(E(F_{\infty})_{p^{\infty}}\Big)^{\vee}=\rank_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\Big(\big(E(F_{\infty})_{p^{\infty}}\big)^{\vee}\Big)_{H}-\rank_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\Big(\big(E(F_{\infty})_{p^{\infty}}\big)^{\vee}\Big)^{H}

where the second equality follows from
[
18
, Proposition 5.3.20]
. But observe that

\Big(\big(E(F_{\infty})_{p^{\infty}}\big)^{\vee}\Big)_{H}=\Big(\big(E(F_{\infty})_{p^{\infty}}\big)^{H}\Big)^{\vee}=\Big(E(F^{\mathrm{cyc}})_{p^{\infty}}\Big)^{\vee},

and the latter is finite by a theorem of Imai
[
6
]
. Hence
\Big(\big(E(F_{\infty})_{p^{\infty}}\big)^{\vee}\Big)^{H}
is also finite. But this group is precisely

H^{1}(H,E(F_{\infty})_{p^{\infty}})^{\vee}
, and so we have our claim.

It remains to show that
g_{v}
has finite kernel for every
v
. Again, by the restriction-inflation sequence, we have
\ker g_{v}=\oplus_{w|v}H^{1}(H_{w},D)
, where here the sum runs over all the primes of
F^{\mathrm{cyc}}
above
v
,
H_{w}
is the decomposition group of
w
in
H
and
D
denotes either
\tilde{E}_{v,p^{\infty}}
or
E_{p^{\infty}}
according as
v
divides
p
or not.
In particular, if
H_{w}=1
, then
H^{1}(H_{w},D)=0
. This is indeed the case when
v
does not divide
p
for a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
-extension is unramified outside
p
. It remains to consider primes
w
which divides
p
and that
H_{w}
is nontrivial. Since
H\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, it then follows that
H_{w}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. We may then apply the same argument as in the preceding paragraph to conclude that
H^{1}(H_{w},\tilde{E}_{v,p^{\infty}})
is finite. The proof of the proposition is now complete.
∎

The following is a corollary of the preceding proposition.

Corollary 2.3
.

Retain the setting as in Proposition
2.2
. Then
\Sel(E/F^{\mathrm{cyc}})
is cofinitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
if and only if
\Sel(E/F_{\infty})
is cofinitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[H]]
.

Mordell-Weil group over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
-extension

We continue to suppose that
F_{\infty}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
-extension of
F
which contains
F^{\mathrm{cyc}}
. Write
H=\Gal(F_{\infty}/F^{\mathrm{cyc}})
and denote by
H_{n}
the unique subgroup of
H
with index
p^{n}
. The fixed field of
H_{n}
is in turn denoted by
K_{n}
. The following hypothesis will be in full force for the remainder of the section.

\mathbf{(Fg)}

\Sel(E/F^{\mathrm{cyc}})
is cofinitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Write
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[H]]
. We shall identify this latter ring with the power series ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[T]]
in one variable. By abuse of notation, we shall also write
\Lambda
for the ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[T]]
. Denote by
\Phi_{n}
the
p^{n}
th-cyclotomic polynomial which is viewed as an element in
\Lambda
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{(Fg)}
is valid. Write
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[H]]
. Then the following statements are valid.

(a)

There is an injective
\Lambda
-homomorphism

\big(E(F_{\infty})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\big)^{\vee}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{\oplus r}\oplus\Big(\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}(\Lambda/\Phi_{n})^{\oplus t_{n}}\Big)

with a finite cokernel, where
\{t_{n}\}
is a sequence of nonnegative integers with
t_{n}=0
for
n\gg 0
.

(b)

Define

e_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}E(K_{n})-\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}E(K_{n-1})}{p^{n}-p^{n-1}},&\mbox{if $n\geq 1;$}\\
\\
\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}E(F^{\mathrm{cyc}}),&\mbox{if $n=0$.}\end{array}\right.

Then we have

T_{p}\big(E(K_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\big)\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}(\Lambda/\Phi_{j})^{\oplus e_{j}}.

Proof.

By hypothesis
\mathbf{(Fg)}
, we have that
\Sel(E/K_{n})
is cofinitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
for every
n
(for instance, see
[
5
, Corollary 3.4]
). Thus, it follows that
E(K_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is also cofinitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
for every
n
. In view of this observation, we may apply a similar argument to that of Lee in
[
9
, Theorem 2.1.2]
to obtain the conclusion of statement (a). Once, we have (a), statement (b) will follow by carrying out the argument in
[
15
, Proposition 3.8]
. ∎

4
Fine Selmer groups

Let
L
be a finite extension of
F
contained in
F_{S}
. Recall that the fine Selmer group of
E
over
L
is defined by

R(E/L)=\ker\left(H^{1}(G_{S}(L),E_{p^{\infty}})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S(L)}H^{1}(L_{v},E_{p^{\infty}})\right).

Furthermore, the fine Selmer group and the classical Selmer group are related by the following exact sequence.

Lemma 4.1
.

We have an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow R(E/L)\longrightarrow\Sel(E/L)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S_{p}(L)}E(L_{v})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

In particular, the definition of the fine Selmer group does not depend on the
choice of the set
S
.

Proof.

See
[
16
, Lemma 4.1]
or
[
24
, Section 2]
.
∎

Following Wuthrich
[
24
]
, the fine Mordell-Weil group
\mathcal{M}(E/L)
is defined by

\mathcal{M}(E/L)=\ker\Big(E(L)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S_{p}(L)}E(L_{v})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\Big),

where
S_{p}(L)
denotes the set of primes of
L
above
p
. This fits into the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{M}(E/L)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E(L)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\displaystyle\bigoplus_{v\in S_{p}(L)}E(L_{v})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{R(E/L)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Sel(E/L)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\displaystyle\bigoplus_{v\in S_{p}(L)}E(L_{v})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}

with exact rows, where the leftmost vertical map is induced by the middle vertical map. Following Wuthrich
[
24
]
, the fine Tate-Shafarevich group
\mbox{Ж}(A/L)
is given by

\mbox{Ж}(E/L)=\mathrm{coker}\,\Big(\mathcal{M}(E/L)\longrightarrow R(E/L)\Big).

Since the middle vertical map in the above diagram is injective, so is the leftmost vertical map. Consequently, a snake lemma argument yields a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(E/L)\longrightarrow R(E/L)\longrightarrow\mbox{Ж}(E/L)\longrightarrow 0

with
\mbox{Ж}(E/L)
injecting into
\sha(E/L)_{p^{\infty}}
, the
p
-primary part of the usual Tate-Shafarevich group.

We can now give the proof of our main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Set
C_{n}
, resp.,
C_{\infty}
, to be the cokernel of
\mathcal{M}(E/K_{n})\longrightarrow E(K_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, resp., the cokernel of
\mathcal{M}(E/F_{\infty})\longrightarrow E(F_{\infty})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. On the other hand, from the argument in
[
20
, Proposition 3.14]
, we see that

\rank_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[H]]}\big(E(F_{\infty})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\big)^{\vee}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\hbox{if $\epsilon(E/F,1)=+1$;}\\
1,&\hbox{if $\epsilon(E/F,1)=-1$.}\end{array}\right.

(1)

Plainly, if
\epsilon(E/F,1)=+1
, then
\mathcal{M}(E/F_{\infty})
has trivial
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[H]]
-corank, and so by a result of Venjakob
[
21
]
(or see
[
12
, Lemma 5.1]
),
\mathcal{M}(E/F_{\infty})^{\vee}
is pseudo-null over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
.

Now suppose that we have
\epsilon(E/F,1)=-1
. In view of (
1
) and the following tautological short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(E/F_{\infty})\longrightarrow E(F_{\infty})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\longrightarrow C_{\infty}\longrightarrow 0,

it therefore remains to show that
\rank_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[H]]}C_{\infty}>0
.

Now consider the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{M}(E/K_{n})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha_{n}}
\textstyle{E(K_{n})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta_{n}}
\textstyle{\bigoplus_{w\in S_{p}(K_{n})}E(K_{n,w})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\gamma_{n}}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{M}(E/F_{\infty})^{H_{n}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Big(E(F_{\infty})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\Big)^{H_{n}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

with exact rows.
Since
\ker\beta_{n}
is contained in

that the latter, and hence

We claim that
\mathrm{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}C_{n}\geq p^{n}-p^{n-1}
for sufficiently large
n
.
Suppose that this claim holds. Since we have seen above that the map
C_{n}\longrightarrow(C_{\infty})^{H}
has finite kernel, it follows that
\mathrm{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(C_{\infty})^{H}\geq p^{n}-p^{n-1}
. On the other hand, since

\mathrm{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(C_{\infty})^{H}=\Big(\mathrm{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[H]]}C_{\infty}\Big)p^{n}+O(1),

we therefore have

\mathrm{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[H]]}C_{\infty}>0

which is what we want to show.

It therefore remains to establish our claim. For this, we follow an idea of Lei
[
8
]
. Consider the natural map

f_{n}:E(K_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{w\in S_{p}(K_{n})}E(K_{n,w})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p},

where
\mathrm{im}~f_{n}=C_{n}
. Write
F^{\mathrm{ac}}
for the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
, and
L_{n}
the intermediate subextension of
F^{\mathrm{ac}}/F
with
|L_{n}:F|=p^{n}
. Since
\epsilon(E/F,1)=-1
, we have

\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}E(L_{n})=p^{n}+O(1)

by
[
1
, Proposition 7.6]
. As
L_{n}\subseteq K_{n}
, this in turn implies that

e_{n}\geq 1
for sufficiently large
n
, where
e_{n}
is defined as in Proposition
3.1
. For each of such
n
, choose an element
x_{n}\in E(K_{n})\setminus E(K_{n-1})
which is of infinite order. Then for a prime
w
of
K_{n}
above
p
, the image of
x_{n}
in
E(K_{n,w})
is still of infinite order and so cannot be divisible by arbitrary power of
p
. Therefore, the image of
x_{n}
in
E(K_{n,w})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is nontrivial.

As before, write
\Phi_{n}
for the
p^{n}
th-cyclotomic polynomial viewed as an element in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[H]]
. For brevity, we write

V_{n}=T_{p}\Big(E(K_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\Big)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}.

Then the map
f_{n}
induces the following
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[H/H_{n}]
-homomorphism

V_{n}[\Phi_{n}]\hookrightarrow V_{n}\longrightarrow T_{p}\Big(E(K_{n,w})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\Big)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}

which is nonzero. Since
\Phi_{n}
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[T]
, it follows that the image of the above homomorphism contains at least a copy of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[T]/\Phi_{n}
, and since
\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\big(\mathbb{Q}_{p}[T]/\Phi_{n}\big)=p^{n}-p^{n-1}
, the said image has
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-dimension at least
p^{n}-p^{n-1}
. Consequently, one has
\mathrm{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(C_{n})\geq p^{n}-p^{n-1}
for sufficiently large
n
. This establishes our claim and complete the proof of the theorem.
∎

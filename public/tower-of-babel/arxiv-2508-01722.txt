Ladder Operators for Laguerre-type and Jacobi-type Orthogonal Polynomials
Shulin Lyu, Yuanfei Lyu

Abstract
In the literature concerning the Laguerre-type weight function x λ w 0 ( x ) , x ∈ [ 0 , + ∞ ) x^{\lambda}w_{0}(x),x\in[0,+\infty) , the Jacobi-type weight function ( 1 − x ) α ​ ( 1 + x ) β ​ w 0 ​ ( x ) , x ∈ [ − 1 , 1 ] (1-x)^{\alpha}(1+x)^{\beta}w_{0}(x),x\in[-1,1] , and the shifted Jacobi-type weight function x α ​ ( 1 − x ) β ​ w 0 ​ ( x ) , x ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] x^{\alpha}(1-x)^{\beta}w_{0}(x),x\in[0,1] , with w 0 ​ ( x ) w_{0}(x) continuously differentiable, the parameters λ , α , β \lambda,\alpha,\beta are usually constrained to be strictly positive to ensure the validity of the results. Recently, in [C. Min and P. Fang, Physica D 473 (2025), 134560 (9pp)], the ladder operators for the monic Laguerre-type orthogonal polynomials with λ > − 1 \lambda>-1 were derived by exploiting the orthogonality properties. The quantities A n A_{n} and B n B_{n} , which appear as coefficients in the ladder operators, exhibit different expressions compared with the previous ones for λ > 0 \lambda>0 . In this paper, we construct an alternative deduction by making use of the Riemann-Hilbert problem satisfied by the orthogonal polynomials. Moreover, we employ both derivation strategies mentioned above to produce the ladder operators for the monic standard and shifted Jacobi-type orthogonal polynomials with α , β > − 1 \alpha,\beta>-1 . When λ , α , β \lambda,\alpha,\beta are restricted to positive values, our expressions of A n A_{n} and B n B_{n} are consistent with those in prior work. We present examples to validate our findings and generalize the existing conclusions, established by using the three compatibility conditions of the ladder operators and differentiating the orthogonality relations for the monic orthogonal polynomials, from λ , α , β > 0 \lambda,\alpha,\beta>0 to λ , α , β > − 1 \lambda,\alpha,\beta>-1 .

\displaystyle-\int_{-1}^{1}\left((1-z^{2})v_{{}_{J}}^{\prime}(z)-(1-x^{2})v_{{}_{J}}^{\prime}(x)\right)P_{n}(x)w_{{}_{J}}(x)\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{P_{j}(z)P_{j}(x)}{h_{j}}dx.

Using (
1.8
), (
1.9
) and (
1.11
) to get rid of

,
\textbf{p}(n+1)
and the summation term respectively, we arrive at the lowering operator (

).

Now we make use of the definitions of
A_{n}
and
B_{n}
given by (

)-(
4.4
) to deduce
(S_{1})
, i.e.

B_{n+1}(z)+B_{n}(z)=(z-\alpha_{n})A_{n}(z)-v_{{}_{J}}^{\prime}(z).

We get

\displaystyle B_{n+1}(z)+B_{n}(z)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-z^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{h_{n}}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{(1-z^{2})v_{{}_{J}}^{\prime}(z)-(1-x^{2})v_{{}_{J}}^{\prime}(x)}{z-x}(x-\alpha_{n})P_{n}^{2}(x)w_{{}_{J}}(x)dx\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad\qquad+(2n+1)z-\mathbf{p}(n+1)-\mathbf{p}(n)\right],

where the identity
\frac{P_{n+1}(x)}{h_{n}}+\frac{P_{n-1}(x)}{h_{n-1}}=\frac{(x-\alpha_{n})P_{n}(x)}{h_{n}}
, due to the recurrence relation (
1.8
), is used. And

\displaystyle(z-\alpha_{n})A_{n}(z)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-z^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{h_{n}}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{(1-z^{2})v_{{}_{J}}^{\prime}(z)-(1-x^{2})v_{{}_{J}}^{\prime}(x)}{z-x}(z-\alpha_{n})P_{n}^{2}(x)w_{{}_{J}}(x)dx\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad\qquad+(2n+1)z-(2n+1)\alpha_{n}\right].

It follows that

\displaystyle B_{n+1}(z)+B_{n}(z)-(z-\alpha_{n})A_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=-v_{{}_{J}}^{\prime}(z)+\frac{1}{1-z^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{h_{n}}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-x^{2})v_{{}_{J}}^{\prime}(x)P_{n}^{2}(x)w_{{}_{J}}(x)dx+(2n+1)\alpha_{n}-\mathbf{p}(n+1)-\mathbf{p}(n)\right].

(4.13)

Through integration by parts, the integral term in the above square bracket is given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{n}}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-x^{2})v_{{}_{J}}^{\prime}(x)P_{n}^{2}(x)w_{{}_{J}}(x)dx=-\frac{1}{h_{n}}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-x^{2})P_{n}^{2}(x)\,dw_{{}_{J}}(x)

\displaystyle\qquad=-\frac{P_{n}^{2}(x)}{h_{n}}\left.(1-x)^{\alpha+1}(1+x)^{\beta+1}w_{0}(x)\right|_{x=-1}^{x=1}-\frac{2}{h_{n}}\int_{-1}^{1}\left(xP_{n}(x)\right)P_{n}(x)w_{{}_{J}}(x)dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{2}{h_{n}}\int_{-1}^{1}P_{n}(x)\left((1-x^{2})P_{n}^{\prime}(x)\right)w_{{}_{J}}(x)dx

\displaystyle\qquad=2\left(-\alpha_{n}+n\mathbf{p}(n+1)-(n-1)\mathbf{p}(n)\right),

(4.14)

where, to obtain the last equality, we make use of the recurrence relation (
1.8
) and (
4.11
), combined with (
1.5
). Substituting (
4.14
) back into (
4.13
), and noting that

, we come to
(S_{1})
.

Combining
(S_{1})
with the lowering operator (
4.1

), one produces the raising operator (
4.2
). See, for instance,
[

Example 6

Consider the monic Jacobi polynomials orthogonal w.r.t.

Plugging them into

\displaystyle\left((n+\alpha+\beta)z-\mathbf{p}(n)+\alpha-\beta\right)\cdot\left(nz-\mathbf{p}(n)\right)

\displaystyle\left(n+2\alpha-\mathbf{p}(n)\right)\left(n-\mathbf{p}(n)\right)=

\displaystyle\left(n+2\beta+\mathbf{p}(n)\right)\left(n+\mathbf{p}(n)\right).

Inserting it into (

\alpha_{n}=\mathbf{p}(n)-\mathbf{p}(n+1)
, and (
4.15
) leads us to

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}-\alpha^{2}}{(2n+\alpha+\beta)(2n+2+\alpha+\beta)},

(4.17)

\displaystyle\beta_{n}=

\displaystyle\frac{4n(n+\alpha)(n+\beta)(n+\alpha+\beta)}{(2n+\alpha+\beta)^{2}(2n+1+\alpha+\beta)(2n-1+\alpha+\beta)}.

(4.18)

We now make use of (

t

6
Conclusions

In the existing literature, the ladder operators for the monic Laguerre-type, Jacobi-type and shifted Jacobi-type orthogonal polynomials were established under the parameter constraints
\lambda,\alpha,\beta>0
. They have been widely applied to study the partition functions, gap probabilities and linear statistics of Hermitian random matrices, the recurrence coefficients of orthogonal polynomials, etc. Although the obtained results are valid for
\lambda,\alpha,\beta>0
, the corresponding weight functions are actually well-defined for the broader range
\lambda,\alpha,\beta>-1
. This observation motivates our extension of the ladder operator formalism to this more general parameter space.
We develop two distinct approaches to construct the ladder operators: through the Riemann-Hilbert problem satisfied by the monic orthogonal polynomials; expressing
(1-z^{2})P_{n}^{\prime}(z)
or
z(1-z)P_{n}^{\prime}(z)
as a linear combination of
\{P_{k}(z)\}_{k=0}^{n+1}
for the Jacobi cases.

We validate our findings by investigating the monic Laguerre and Jacobi polynomials.
Moreover, we revisit several problems concerning the above-mentioned three types of weight functions, and generalize the results in prior work derived by using the ladder operator approach from
\lambda,\alpha,\beta>0
to
\lambda,\alpha,\beta>-1
. To achieve this goal, we first compute
A_{n}
and
B_{n}
for
\lambda,\alpha,\beta>-1
by using the definitions and verify that their expressions coincide with those for
\lambda,\alpha,\beta>0
. Then, by differentiating the orthogonality relation w.r.t. the variable introduced in the weight function, we obtain differential equalities identical to the ones in previous studies for
\lambda,\alpha,\beta>0
. These two consistency guarantees ensure that the results deduced via the ladder operator approach, under the assumption that
\lambda,\alpha,\beta>0
, remain valid for
\lambda,\alpha,\beta>-1
.

There exist exceptions where the differential relations established in earlier research for
\lambda,\alpha,\beta>0
no longer hold for
-1<\lambda,\alpha,\beta\leq 0
. See Examples
8
,
13
,
15
. As a consequence, the existing results in literature can not be directly generalized to
\lambda,\alpha,\beta>-1
. This problem may be addressed by introducing auxiliary quantities distinct from previous studies, as noted in these three examples. The discussion is deferred to future work.

Orthocomplemented subspaces and partial projections on a Hilbert space
Iosif Petrakis

Abstract
We introduce the notion of an orthocomplemented subspace of a Hilbert space ℋ \mathcal{H} , that is, a pair of orthogonal closed subspaces of ℋ \mathcal{H} , as a two-dimensional counterpart to the one-dimensional notion of a closed subspace of ℋ \mathcal{H} . Orthocomplemented subspaces are the Hilbert space-analogue to Bishop’s complemented subsets. To complemented subsets correspond their characteristic functions, which are partial, Boolean-valued functions. Similarly, to orthocomplemented subspaces of ℋ \mathcal{H} correspond partial projections on ℋ \mathcal{H} . Previous work of Bridges and Svozil on constructive quantum logic is an one-dimensional approach to the subject. The lattice-properties of the orthocomplemented subspaces of a Hilbert space is a two-dimensional approach to constructive quantum logic, that we call complemented quantum logic. Since the negation of an orthocomplemented subspace is formed by swapping its components, complemented quantum logic, although constructive, is closer to classical quantum logic than the constructive quantum logic of Bridges and Svozil. The introduction of orthocomplemented subspaces and their corresponding partial projections allows a new approach to the constructive theory of Hilbert spaces. For example, the partial projection operator of an orthocomplemented subspace and the construction of the quotient Hilbert space bypass the standard restrictive hypothesis of locatedness on a subspace. Located subspaces correspond to total orthocomplemented subspaces.



Keywords
: Constructive analysis, Hilbert spaces, orthocomplemented subspaces, partial projections, quantum logic

Introduction

If
A
is a subset of a set
(X,=_{X})
, its characteristic function
\chi_{A}\colon X\to\mathbb{2}
is defined through the classical principle of the excluded middle
(\mathrm{PEM})
by the rule:

\chi_{A}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{, $x\in A$}\\
0&\mbox{, $x\notin A$.}\end{array}\right.

Constructively, such a definition is acceptable if and only if
A
is a
decidable
subset of
X
, i.e.,

\forall_{x\in X}\big(x\in A\vee x\notin A\big)
. As a result, we cannot recover constructively the classical bijection between the powerset
\mathcal{P}(X)
of
X
and the set
\mathbb{F}(X,\mathbb{2})
of total functions from
X
to the set of booleans
\mathbb{2}
. A recovery of this bijection is possible constructively, if we work with complemented subsets, instead of subsets, and with their characteristic functions, which are
partial
, Boolean-valued functions on
X
. A
complemented subset
is a pair
\bm{A}:=(A^{1},A^{0})
of subsets
A^{1},A^{0}
of a set
(X,=_{X})
with an extensional inequality
\neq_{X}
(see Definition
2.2
), a strong and positive form of negation of the equality
=_{X}
on
X
, such that
A^{1}
and
A^{0}
are subsets of
X
that are disjoint in a strong and positive way, i.e.,

A^{1}\mathbin{\bm{)}\bm{(}}A^{0}:\Leftrightarrow\forall_{x^{1}\in A^{1}}\forall_{x^{0}\in A^{0}}\big(x^{1}\neq_{X}x^{0}\big).

The
charactersitic function
of
\bm{A}
is a partial function
\chi_{\bm{A}}\colon X\rightharpoonup\mathbb{2}
, i.e., it is a (total) function
\chi_{\bm{A}}\colon\mathrm{dom}(\bm{A})\to\mathbb{2}
on the subset
\mathrm{dom}(\bm{A})
of
X
, where

\mathrm{dom}(\bm{A}):=A^{1}\cup A^{0},

and it is defined by the rule:

\chi_{\bm{A}}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{, $x\in A^{1}$}\\
0&\mbox{, $x\in A^{0},$}\end{array}\right.

avoiding the use of
\mathrm{PEM}
. It is easy to show that
\chi_{\bm{A}}
is a strongly extensional function (see Definition
2.3
). The
complemented powerset

\bm{P}(X)
of

is a poset with

\bm{A}\subseteq\bm{B}:\Leftrightarrow A^{1}\subseteq B^{1}\ \&\ B^{0}\subseteq A^{0},

i.e.,
\bm{B}
has more “provers” and less “refuters” than
\bm{A}
. The order on the characteristic functions of complemented subsets is given by

\chi_{\bm{A}}\leq\chi_{\bm{B}}:\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{dom}(\bm{A})\subseteq\mathrm{dom}(\bm{B})\ \&\ \forall_{x\in\mathrm{dom}(\bm{A})}(\chi_{\bm{A}}(x)\leq_{\mathbb{2}}\chi_{\bm{B}}(x)).

Conversely, every strongly extensional function
\chi

of

\mathbb{2}
induces a complemented subset of
X
, defined by
\bm{A}_{\chi}:=(A_{\chi}^{1},A_{\chi}^{0})
, where

A_{\chi}^{1}:=\{x\in\mathrm{dom}(\chi)\mid\chi(x)=_{\mathbb{2}}1\},

A_{\chi}^{0}:=\{x\in\mathrm{dom}(\chi)\mid\chi(x)=_{\mathbb{2}}0\}.

One can then show, see

and

approach to the theory of subsets. Clearly, within the two-dimensional framework of complemented subsets, partial, Boolean-valued functions become first-class citizens. These concepts were introduced by Bishop and Cheng in

The classical correspondence between subsets of a set
(X,=_{X})
and characteristic functions on
X
is in complete analogy to the classical correpsondence between subspaces of a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and projections on
\mathcal{H}

\mathcal{H}

\mathcal{H}

L_{P}:=\{x\in H\mid P(x)=_{H}x\}.

The partial orders on the subspaces

and the set of projections
\Pr(\mathcal{H})

\mathcal{H}

are defined , respectively, by the rules:

The two procedures are inverse to each other, and hence, classically, there is a bijection between

, the set of closed subspaces of
\mathcal{H}
, and
\Pr(\mathcal{H})

\mathcal{H}

Constructively though, such a bijection cannot be captured within the one-dimensional framework of subspaces and projections, since the existence of the projection

, i.e., the existsence of the distance

\rho(x,L):=\inf\big\{||x-l||\mid l\in L\big\}

of

from
L

Clearly,

Here we extend Bishop’s two-dimensional approach from subsets of a set to subspaces of a Hilbert space. Namely, we introduce what we call the

\mathrm{dom}(\bm{L}):=L^{1}\vee L^{0}:=\overline{L^{1}+L^{0}}.

Actually, we have that
\mathrm{dom}(\bm{L})=_{{S(\mathcal{H})}}L^{1}+L^{0}
(see Theorem
4.3

, i.e., a self-adjoint, idempotent and bounded partial operator on

L_{P^{1}}:=\{x\in\mathrm{dom}(P)\mid P(x)=_{H}x\},

L_{P^{0}}:=\{x\in\mathrm{dom}(P)\mid P(x)=_{H}0\}.

The two procedures are inverse to each other, i.e., there is a bijection between
\bm{S}(\mathcal{H})
, the totality of orthocomplemented subspaces of

Subspaces of a Hilbert space and classical quantum logic

In this section we include some fundamental notions and facts from the constructive theory of Hilbert spaces that are going to be used in the rest of the paper. Bishop Set Theory (BST) is an informal, constructive theory of

Assignment routines are of two kinds:

7
Complemented quantum logic

In this section we define the basic operations
on
\bm{S}(\mathcal{H})
, and we prove that
\bm{S}(\mathcal{H})
is what we call a complemented quantum lattice (Proposition
7.3
). Due to the definition of negation of an orthocomplemented subspace, complemented quantum logic satisfies all properties of classical negation
L^{\perp}
in
\mathrm{ClassQL}
, and in this way it is a constructive logical system closer to
\mathrm{ClassQL}
than
\mathrm{IntQL}
.

Definition 7.1
.

The following operations are defined on
\bm{S}(\mathcal{H})
:

\bm{1}:=(1,0):=(H,\{0\}),

\bm{0}:=(0,1):=(\{0\},H),

\bm{L}\wedge\bm{M}:=\big(L^{1}\wedge M^{1},L^{0}\vee M^{0}\big),

\bm{L}\vee\bm{M}:=\big(L^{1}\vee M^{1},L^{0}\wedge M^{0}\big),

-\bm{L}:=\big(L^{0},L^{1}\big),

\bm{L}-\bm{M}:=\bm{L}\wedge(-\bm{M}),

\bm{L}\Rightarrow\bm{M}:=(-\bm{L})\vee\bm{M},

\bm{L}\Leftrightarrow\bm{M}:=(\bm{L}\Rightarrow\bm{M})\wedge(\bm{M}\Rightarrow\bm{L}),

\neg\bm{L}:=\bm{L}\Rightarrow\bm{0}.

If
(\bm{L}_{i})_{i\in I}
is a family of orthocomplemented subspaces of
\mathcal{H}
over the index-set

, let

\bigwedge_{i\in I}\bm{L}_{i}:=\bigg(\bigwedge_{i\in I}L_{i}^{1},\ \bigvee_{i\in I}L_{i}^{0}\bigg),

\bigvee_{i\in I}\bm{L}_{i}:=\bigg(\bigvee_{i\in I}L_{i}^{1},\ \bigwedge_{i\in I}L_{i}^{0}\bigg).

The relation of orthogonality on
\bm{S}(\mathcal{H})
is defined by the rule

\bm{L}\perp\bm{M}:\Leftrightarrow\bm{L}\leq(-\bm{M}).

If
\bm{L}\leq\mathcal{H}
is understood as a proposition, its derivability
\vdash\bm{L}
is defined as a proof of
\bm{L}=_{{\bm{S}(\mathcal{H})}}\bm{1}
.

It is straightforward to show that
\bm{L}\wedge\bm{M}\leq\mathcal{H}
,
\bm{L}\vee\bm{M}\leq\mathcal{H}
,

\bigwedge_{i\in I}\bm{L}_{i}\leq\mathcal{H}
, and
\bigvee_{i\in I}\bm{L}_{i}\leq\mathcal{H}
.
Clearly,
\bm{L}:=(L,L^{\perp})\leq\mathcal{H}
, and
\neg\bm{L}:=-\bm{L}\vee\bm{0}=_{{\bm{S}(\mathcal{H})}}-\bm{L}
. The equality

\bm{0}\Rightarrow\bm{L}:=\bm{1}\vee L=_{{\bm{S}(\mathcal{H})}}\bm{1}

expresses that within
\bm{S}(\mathcal{H})
, seen as a logical system,
\mathrm{EFQ}
is derived.
In general, the implication in
\bm{S}(\mathcal{H})
does not satisfy the adjunction of a Heyting algebra.
By Theorem
4.3
(i) we have that

\mathrm{dom}(\bm{L}\wedge\bm{M})=_{{S(\mathcal{H})}}(L^{1}\wedge M^{1})\oplus(L^{0}\vee M^{0}),

\mathrm{dom}(\bm{L}\vee\bm{M})=_{{S(\mathcal{H})}}(L^{1}\vee M^{1})\oplus(L^{0}\wedge M^{0}),

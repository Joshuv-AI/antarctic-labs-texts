Cross-cohort integrative multi-omics analysis of cancer essential gene ATP6V0B to dissect functional characteristics and clinical implications in breast cancer
Authors: Jinbao Yin, Binbin Li, Hui Xiong, Hongmei Li, Enping Jiang, Lan Liang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBreast cancer remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality in women, with recurrence, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance limiting outcomes. The role of vacuolar ATPase subunit ATP6V0B in breast cancer pathogenesis and immune regulation is poorly understood.MethodsWe integrated CRISPR screening, machine learning (LASSO/Random Forest), and multi-omics data (> 10,000 samples) to identify ATP6V0B as a cancer essential gene. Single-cell transcriptomics and ligand-receptor network analysis dissected its TME regulatory role. Prognostic modeling (Cox regression/nomogram) was validated across cohorts.ResultsATP6V0B was overexpressed in tumors, correlating with larger size, lymph node metastasis, aggressive subtypes (basal-like/HER2-enriched), and poor prognosis (OS/RFS/DMFS, p < 0.001). ATP6V0B+ tumor subsets are associated with immunosuppression via VEGFA-VEGFR2/THBS1-CD47, correlating with MDSC infiltration and immunotherapy resistance. A nomogram integrating ATP6V0B-related signature, stage, and age improved prognostic accuracy (1/3/5-year OS AUC 0.78/0.74/0.72).ConclusionATP6V0B is a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target in breast cancer, driving immune evasion. The nomogram enables individualized prognostic assessment, supporting precision oncology.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12957-025-04107-3.

Introduction

Breast cancer remains the most prevalent malignancy in women globally, with over 2.3 million new cases and 666,000 deaths reported in 2022 alone [1]. Despite advances in targeted therapies and immunotherapies, subtype-specific progression, distant metastasis, and denovo/acquired resistance continue to limit survival outcomes, particularly in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) and HER-2-low breast cancer [2–5]. The molecular mechanisms driving these processes, including cross-talk between cancer cells and the TME, are incompletely understood, necessitating the identification of actionable biomarkers and therapeutic vulnerabilities.

Cancer essential genes (CEGs)—genes indispensable for maintaining malignant phenotypes such as unlimited proliferation and apoptotic resistance—represent promising targets for precision oncology. Recent CRISPR-Cas9 screens have systematically identified CEGs across cancer types: for instance, METTL1 and PCMT1 were validated as CEGs in gastric and ovarian cancers, respectively [6, 7]. However, most studies focus on intra-tumoral genetic dependencies, with limited attention to CEGs’ roles in TME modulation. In breast cancer, this gap is especially pronounced, as TME heterogeneity strongly influences treatment response and prognosis.

The vacuolar-type H+-ATPase (v-ATPase) complex, a multi-subunit proton pump, regulates intracellular pH homeostasis, lysosomal degradation, and autophagy — processes intimately linked to cancer cell survival under nutrient stress — and is considered a potential therapeutic target for cancer [8]. Dysregulation of the v-ATPase subunits ATP6AP1 and ATP6V1C1 has been implicated in immune evasion in hepatocellular carcinoma, whereas the subunit ATP6V0A1 has also been shown to exhibit aberrant overexpression in colorectal cancer cells, which is closely associated with enhanced lipid metabolism and an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME) [9–11]. Among v-ATPase subunits, ATP6V0B is a core component of the membrane-integral V0 domain, critical for proton translocation and lysosomal maturation. While ATP6V0B has been associated with poor prognosis in hepatocellular carcinoma and bladder cancer, its functional role in breast cancer—including its potential as a CEG, regulatory mechanisms in TME, and clinical relevance—remains uncharacterized [12, 13].

To address these gaps, we hypothesized that ATP6V0B is a clinically relevant CEG in breast cancer. Leveraging machine learning algorithms for high-dimensional omics data mining, we integrated CRISPR-Cas9 dependency scores, transcriptomic, and clinical data from > 10,000 samples across multiple cohorts. This study aims to: (1) validate ATP6V0B as a CEG in breast cancer; (2) dissect its mechanistic roles in tumor progression and immune modulation; and (3) evaluate its potential as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target.

Materials and methods

Data sources and acquisition

All datasets analyzed in our study were retrieved from publicly accessible repositories. RNA-Seq data (transcripts per million, TPM) and clinical information for BRCA patients and normal controls were obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) via the “TCGAbiolinks” R package, which was designated as the TCGA_BRCA dataset. Additionally, a total of 26 gene expression datasets were included, all retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) database, and categorized as follows: 8 datasets containing both normal and tumor tissue samples; one dataset (GSE96058) with Overall Survival (OS) information, two datasets (GSE202203 and GSE25066) with Recurrence-Free Survival (RFS) information, 8 datasets with Distant Metastasis-Free Survival (DMFS) information, one dataset (GSE21653) with Disease-Free Survival (DFS) information, and 6 datasets containing treatment-related clinical information. Using the R package “GEOquery”, all the aforementioned datasets were downloaded, standardized, and preprocessed. Furthermore, the METABRIC dataset, which includes expression profiles and clinical information for 1,980 BRCA patients, was downloaded from cBioPortal (https://www.cbioportal.org/). Batch effects across datasets (GSE96058, METABRIC and TCGA_BRCA) were minimized using the ComBat function to facilitate integrative analysis (Supplementary Figure S1) [14]. Additionally, transcriptomic data at single-cell resolution were retrieved from GEO (GSE173634, GSE176078) and the Zenodo repository (https://zenodo.org/records/10672250) for the Pal_Prim cohort [15]. Reference gene sets comprised “genesets_delineating_14_functional_states_of_cancer_cells” (from CancerSEA; http://biocc.hrbmu.edu.cn/CancerSEA/) and the “PD-L1-positive gene signature,” retrieved from the study by Sung Gwe Ahn [16, 17]. Sample information utilized in support of this research and detailed characteristics of the gene sets are provided in the Table S1−3. An overview of the analytical workflow implemented in this study is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Analysis of single-cell datasets

Initially, we processed the raw data using the “Seurat” package, which encompassed several crucial steps, including cell quality control, data normalization, and dimensionality reduction clustering. This process incorporated principal component analysis (PCA) alongside Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) visualization. To quantify activation levels of predefined gene sets and assess their differences, the AUCell algorithm was utilized [18]. To address batch effects across multi-sample datasets, we harmonized the data utilizing the Harmony algorithm within the seurat analysis framework. Following this, we conducted integrated cellular clustering across all samples [19]. To mitigate technical noise associated with gene dropout in single-cell RNA sequencing data, we implemented the MAGIC algorithm for nonlinear imputation of the gene expression matrix [20].

Identification of essential genes in BRCA malignant tumor cells

CRISPR screening employs genome-scale CRISPR-Cas9 technologies to individually knockout genes, facilitating the identification of candidate genes critical for cancer survival, as conceptualized by the Achilles Project [21]. By integrating data from this project with expression profiles from BRCA patients, we can effectively pinpoint essential genes within BRCA malignant tumor cells. We obtained whole-genome CRISPR screening data for BRCA cell lines from the DepMap database (https://depmap.org/portal/download/). Using the CERES algorithm, we calculated dependency scores for 17,645 candidate genes, specifically focusing on those genes exhibiting scores of < − 1 in at least 75% of breast cancer cell lines [22]. These gene scores were subsequently utilized for differential expression analysis comparing TCGA breast cancer tumor tissues to normal tissues. Based on the results from the limma analysis, we defined significantly upregulated genes in tumor tissues as cancer essential genes in BRCA malignant tumor cells. To ensure the robustness of our findings in identifying critical genes driving breast cancer malignancy, this was determined by applying thresholds of a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.001 and a fold change >2.

Machine learning

To identify potential feature genes associated with breast cancer from the previously determined cancer essential genes, we employed five machine learning methodologies: LASSO, SVM-RFE, Random Forest, GBM, and XGBoost [23, 24]. In the LASSO analysis, we utilized the glmnet software, which incorporates a regularization parameter in conjunction with ten-fold cross-validation to ensure the robustness of the model. The SVM-RFE method is particularly effective in selecting relevant features while simultaneously eliminating redundant ones, demonstrating superior performance compared to linear discriminant analysis and mean squared error methods. During the application of SVM-RFE, we assessed model performance across varying numbers of features to determine the optimal feature subset, utilizing “svmRadial” as the selected method. Random Forest, as an ensemble learning technique, classifies or regresses based on the construction of multiple decision trees, aggregating their results through a voting mechanism. This method offers significant advantages, including effective handling of high-dimensional data, reduction of overfitting, and provision of feature importance assessment, thereby enhancing model accuracy and stability. For our analysis, we specified the creation of 1,000 decision trees to improve both accuracy and robustness. Both GBM and XGBoost are based on gradient boosting frameworks and are employed for classification and regression tasks. GBM incrementally builds a series of weak learners—typically decision trees—with each iteration focused on correcting the errors produced by the prior model. In contrast, XGBoost enhances the GBM framework by incorporating advanced techniques such as parallel processing, regularization, and pruning, which improve training speed and overall model performance. Following the analysis conducted using these methodologies, we determined the final feature genes based on their importance rankings, thereby identifying key genes that may play significant roles in breast cancer biology.

Quantification of protein activity corresponding to candidate gene

Within this work, we initiated our analysis by constructing a candidate key gene regulatory network utilizing the ARACNe algorithm. This algorithm infers potential regulatory interactions by examining expression correlations among genes while effectively eliminating indirect interactions, thereby allowing for the identification of direct regulatory relationships [25]. We established a stringent significance threshold of 1E-8 to ensure that only robust associations were retained within the network. Upon constructing the regulatory network, we employed the aracne2regulon function to process the output files generated by ARACNe, enabling us to create regulon objects. Lastly, we utilized the “VIPER” package to indirectly infer and evaluate the activity associated with the candidate key genes [26].

Diagnostic performance analysis of the essential gene

Initially, we conducted receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis on the TCGA_BRCA dataset to evaluate the diagnostic performance of potential essential gene associated with breast cancer [27]. Following this assessment, we further validated the diagnostic performance across multiple datasets available in the GEO database.

Prognostic assessment of the essential gene

To evaluate the prognostic performance of the essential gene, we first investigated the relationship between the Nottingham Prognostic Index (NPI) and essential gene within the METABRIC dataset. Furthermore, we conducted Kaplan-Meier analyses with OS, RFS, DMFS serving as endpoints. To assess the generalizability of essential gene as a prognostic indicator across various tumor characteristics, we performed stratified Kaplan-Meier survival analyses for OS, taking into account factors such as age, tumor volume, lymph node status, and cancer grading. Finally, to validate the predictive capability of essential gene in relation to clinical treatment responses in breast cancer, we performed additional Kaplan-Meier analyses with OS as the endpoint, specifically evaluating the outcomes of patients who underwent radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and hormone therapy. For KM survival analyses, essential gene activity was stratified using two complementary approaches: the median value, used as the primary cutoff to balance sample size distribution (high: >median; low: ≤ median), and the optimal cutoff, determined to maximize prognostic discrimination and defined as the threshold with the smallest log-rank test p-value in survival analysis.

Gene enrichment analysis

We utilized the ToppGene database (https://toppgene.cchmc.org/) to statistically identify enriched pathways and Gene Ontology (GO) terms for the genes of interest [28]. Using GSEA_4.3.2 software, we performed Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) with a pre-ranked analytical mode, in which the degree of correlation was employed as the ranking metric [29].

Identification and analysis of essential gene-related tumor cell subpopulations

In the GSE176078 dataset, we employed Gaussian mixture modeling from the “mclust” package to classify tumor cells into distinct subpopulations based on the expression levels of feature genes. Building upon this, we compared the distribution of ten genes associated with breast cancer metastasis and drug resistance (CTSD, AGR2, MDK, NUCB2, TFF3, NQO1, CLDN7, TIMP1, HSPA5, and RPN2) across different subpopulations [30–39]. Additionally, we quantified enrichment scores of cancer cell functional states across tumor cell subpopulations using the single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) algorithm implemented in the “GSVA” package [40]. Moreover, a cell communication network was constructed using the “CellChat” package to identify significantly enriched ligand-receptor pairs across subpopulations. This identification was performed using hypergeometric testing, with a significance threshold set at p < 0.01 [41].

For the identification of feature genes associated with essential gene-related tumor cell subpopulations, we used the FindMarkers function in the “Seurat” package and performed wilcoxon tests [42]. Feature genes were filtered using strict criteria: log2 Fold change (log2FC) >0.5 and FDR < 0.001. Based on these feature genes, we analyzed the infiltration levels of essential gene-related tumor cell subpopulations across different samples using the ssGSEA algorithm applied to bulk expression profiles.

Evaluation of tumor cell subpopulations associated with essential genes and their response to immunotherapy

To investigate the molecular characteristics of tumors, we first integrated a predefined gene set associated with immunologically “hot” tumor features, including CD4, CD274, CXCR4, CXCL9, CD8A, CXCL10, CXCL11, CD8B, CXCR3, PDCD1, CD3E, and CCL5. Molecular subtyping of tumors was performed using the “ConsensusClusterPlus” R package, a robust unsupervised clustering algorithm [43, 44]. The clustering parameters were set to: Bootstrap resampling 1000 times (pItem = 0.8), analysis of all features (pFeature = 1), with the range for the number of clusters iterating from 2 to 10. The optimal number of clusters was determined using a combination of the elbow method (minimizing within-cluster sum of squares) and silhouette scores (assessing inter-cluster separation), ultimately categorizing patients into immune-hot and cold tumor subtypes. Further analyses were performed to characterize the distribution patterns of the 29 immune-related features and the infiltration characteristics of essential gene-related tumor cell subpopulations between the identified molecular subtypes [45]. Additionally, we quantified the immune evasion mechanisms of the microenvironment using the Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE) algorithm, identifying potential immune therapy-sensitive subpopulations [46]. After establishing the association between essential gene-related tumor cell subpopulations and immune classification, we validated the predictive value of essential gene-related tumor cell subpopulations for immunotherapy response across multi-independent cohorts.

Prediction of chemotherapy response

To evaluate the heterogeneity of chemotherapy responses, this study integrated drug sensitivity data from the GDSC database and employed the “pRophetic” package to construct a predictive model [47]. The model quantitatively calculated the sensitivity of patients to chemotherapy drugs using the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) as a metric, thereby revealing differences in treatment responses across subpopulations.

Construction and validation of prognostic signature and nomogram

Prognosis-related genes were initially screened via univariate Cox regression analysis (p < 0.05), and 10 gene variables were further identified using random survival forest analysis. Based on these genes, 2^10 − 1 combinations were constructed, and a riskscore model was established via multivariate Cox regression (riskscore = Σ [gene expression × regression coefficient]). The optimal model was selected based on log-rank p-values from Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and the number of genes. Samples were stratified into high- and low-risk subgroups according to the median or the optimal cutoff risk value, and survival differences between groups were validated using Kaplan-Meier survival analysis with log-rank test.

Furthermore, Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were performed, incorporating prognostic signatures and clinicopathological features [48]. Using these results, an integrated nomogram model was developed with the R package ‘rms’. For model validation, calibration curves were employed to assess prediction calibration, time-dependent decision curve analysis (DCA) was conducted to quantify thresholds of clinical net benefit, and time-dependent ROC curves were utilized to compute AUC values for evaluating model performance. Additionally, the above analyses were replicated in an independent dataset to verify the robustness of the prognostic signature and nomogram.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses during the course of this study were conducted using R software (version 4.2.3). Comparisons between different groups were performed using the Wilcoxon test for two groups and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) for multiple groups. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05, with ns indicating p > 0.05, and results are represented by asterisks as follows: *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, and ***p < 0.001.

Results

Identification of essential genes and differential expression analysis in breast cancer

To identify CEGs in malignant BRCA cells, we analyzed genome-wide CRISPR-Cas9 loss-of-function screening data from the DepMap database and identified 648 genes critical for the survival of 25 BRCA cell lines. Differential expression analysis (tumor vs. normal tissues) revealed 76 significantly upregulated genes (Fold change > 2, FDR < 0.001) among these 626 candidates (Fig. 2A). Principal component analysis (PCA) based on these 76 genes showed distinct clustering of tumor and normal samples in principal component space, indicating their impact on breast cancer biology (Fig. 2B). Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis demonstrated strong interconnections among the 76 genes, supporting their synergistic roles in BRCA progression (Fig. 2C). Functional enrichment analysis confirmed their involvement in cell cycle regulation and proliferation (e.g., DNA replication, chromosome segregation, and cell division) (Fig. 2D). Using the AUCell algorithm, we quantified the activity of these 76 genes at the single-cell level in GSE173634, thereby demonstrating significantly elevated activity in BRCA cell lines versus normal breast cell lines (Fig. 2E, F).

In the Random Forest analysis, we found that 13 genes were recognized as breast cancer characteristic genes, based on a mean decrease in Gini coefficient score greater than 5 (Fig. 3C, D). Both the GBM and XGBoost algorithms facilitated the generation of importance rankings for the identified characteristic genes. ATP6V0B as a potential cancer essential gene in breast cancer

To ensure the accuracy and reliability of our study, we conducted a cross-analysis of breast cancer feature genes identified by distinct machine learning models, yielding ATP6V0B, NUF2, and SPC25 (Fig. 4A). These genes were consistently identified as key features across multiple models and are hypothesized to be closely implicated in the occurrence and progression of breast cancer. Subsequently, we employed the VIPER algorithm to quantify the activity of these three genes and performed differential analysis. The results revealed that the activities of all three genes exhibited significant differences between breast cancer and normal tissues, with increased activity in breast cancer (Fig. 4B). Among these genes, ATP6V0B demonstrated the highest activity in breast cancer, prompting us to focus subsequent investigations on this molecule. We utilized the ToppGene software for the biological process and molecular function annotation of ATP6V0B, with the results presented in Fig. 4C and Table S4. At the molecular level, ATP6V0B functions as a transporter and ATPase, and participates in biological processes including intracellular acidification, pH regulation, autophagy, and proton transmembrane transport. Next, we validated the activity of ATP6V0B across eight datasets containing both breast cancer and normal samples: GSE14999, GSE15852, GSE21422, GSE233242, GSE38959, GSE42568, GSE65194 and GSE76250, as illustrated in Fig. 4D. To evaluate the diagnostic performance of ATP6V0B for breast cancer, we conducted diagnostic ROC analysis across multiple datasets. The results of the ROC curve analysis, as shown in Fig. 4E, indicate that ATP6V0B has strong diagnostic potential for breast cancer.

Analysis of the correlation between ATP6V0B and clinical pathological features in patients

To further elucidate the relationship between ATP6V0B and breast cancer progression, it is essential to clarify the association between ATP6V0B activity and clinical characteristics of breast cancer. Analysis of the GSE96058 dataset revealed that ATP6V0B activity is significantly correlated with patient survival status, tumor size, lymph node metastasis, and histological grade (Fig. 5A). Similarly, in the METABRIC cohort, ATP6V0B activity also showed significant associations with patient survival status, tumor size, lymph node metastasis, and histological grade (Fig. 5B). Furthermore, analysis of the TCGA_BRCA dataset demonstrated that ATP6V0B activity is significantly associated with patients’ M stage, T stage, and overall cancer stage (Fig. 5C). Our results indicate that patients exhibiting higher ATP6V0B activity are prone to display larger tumor volumes, increased likelihood of lymph node metastasis, and later-stage cancer. Considering the importance of molecular subtypes in breast cancer, we conducted PAM50 subtype analysis on samples from the three aforementioned datasets, revealing that different molecular subtypes of breast cancer display varying levels of ATP6V0B activity, with more aggressive subtypes such as basal-like, HER2-enriched, and Luminal B exhibiting higher levels of activity (Fig. 5D).

Prognostic performance of ATP6V0B

As a clinical tool, the Nottingham Prognostic Index (NPI) is employed to evaluate prognostic outcomes in breast cancer patients by incorporating tumor size, lymph node involvement, and histological grade to predict patient outcomes. Additionally, within the METABRIC dataset, samples were stratified into high and low NPI groups using the median NPI value as the cutoff, and ATP6V0B activity differences were compared between these two cohorts. The results showed that ATP6V0B activity was significantly higher in the high NPI group than in the low NPI group, indicating that elevated ATP6V0B activity may be associated with poorer prognosis in breast cancer patients (Fig. 6A). To evaluate the impact of ATP6V0B activity on survival probability, we stratified patients into high- and low-ATP6V0B activity groups using two complementary thresholds: (1) the median activity score (integrated cohort, n = 6480) and (2) the optimal cutoff determined via the maximize prognostic discrimination. Kaplan-Meier (KM) survival analysis on the integrated TCGA_BRCA, METABRIC, and GSE96058 datasets identified consistent disparities in overall survival (OS) across high versus low groups using both thresholds, and patients with high ATP6V0B activity showed poorer OS outcomes (Fig. 6B). Additionally, to assess the generalizability of ATP6V0B activity as a prognostic indicator across different tumor characteristics, we performed KM survival analyses stratified by age, tumor volume, lymph node status, and cancer stage. The survival curves indicated that elevated ATP6V0B activity consistently correlated with poorer OS across these various clinical features, confirming its broad applicability in breast cancer prognosis assessment (Fig. 6C-J). Furthermore, we investigated the relationship between ATP6V0B activity and RFS as well as DMFS through KM analysis. The results indicated that higher ATP6V0B activity is associated with poorer RFS and DMFS outcomes (Fig. 6K, L). These findings suggest that ATP6V0B may serve as an important biomarker for prognosis evaluation in breast cancer patients.

Treatment evaluation of ATP6V0B

To explore the association of ATP6V0B with treatment responses among breast cancer patients, this study systematically evaluated its clinical predictive value via multi-omics analysis. Paired analysis based on the GSE20181 longitudinal cohort revealed that ATP6V0B activity significantly decreased post-treatment compared to baseline levels in chemotherapy responders (p = 5.1e-05), while no significant changes were observed in non-responders (p = 0.93) (Fig. 7A), suggesting that its downregulation may be related to treatment sensitivity. Further multi-cohort analyses (GSE194040, GSE163882, GSE123845) confirmed that the ATP6V0B activity in the pathologically complete response (pCR) group was significantly lower than that in the non-pCR group (p < 0.05) (Fig. 7B-D). Addressing the clinical challenge of poor responses to adjuvant therapy after radical surgery, we integrated TCGA_BRCA, METABRIC, and GSE96058 datasets for prognostic analysis. Results demonstrated that individuals with elevated ATP6V0B activity displayed markedly reduced responsiveness to chemotherapy, endocrine therapy, and radiotherapy, correlating with inferior prognostic outcomes (Fig. 7E-G). Drug sensitivity analysis further revealed that the high activity group showed significantly increased half-maximal inhibitory concentrations (IC50) for cisplatin, dasatinib, etoposide, and gemcitabine (p < 0.001) (Fig. 7H). Collectively, these multidimensional evidences suggest that ATP6V0B activity may serve as a potential predictive biomarker for multi-modal treatment responses in breast cancer.

Single-cell transcriptomic profiling reveals atp6v0b + tumor cell subpopulations

Building on prior research that highlights the close association between the ATP6V0B and breast cancer, this study further elucidates its expression heterogeneity by integrating the GSE176078 single-cell dataset. Initial expression profiling indicates a pan-cellular expression pattern for ATP6V0B (Fig. 8A). Differential expression analysis reveals a significant upregulation in tumor cell populations compared to normal epithelial and other cell subpopulations, including stromal and immune cells (Fig. 8B, p < 0.001). Using an automated threshold selection based on the Gaussian mixture model, we identified 18,144 ATP6V0B high-expressing cells (expression level > 0.73415) from a total of 24,489 tumor cells, defining this group as the ATP6V0B+ M subgroup, and other tumor cells are tentatively designated as ATP6V0B− M subgroup in response (Fig. 8C, D). As shown in Fig. 8E, ten genes associated with breast cancer metastasis and drug resistance (CTSD, AGR2, MDK, NUCB2, TFF3, NQO1, CLDN7, TIMP1, HSPA5, and RPN2) are significantly upregulated in the ATP6V0B+ M subgroup. This subgroup exhibits higher scores in 12 malignancy-related biological features, including apoptosis, cell cycle, differentiation, DNA damage repair, Hypoxia, Inflammation, Invasion, Metastasis, proliferation, Quiescence and stemness, compared to the ATP6V0B− M subgroup, corroborating findings from Pal Prim et al. (Fig. 8F, Supplementary Figure S2A). CellChat analysis of intercellular communication revealed a significant enhancement in both the overall strength and signaling activity of the interaction network among ATP6V0B+ M cells (Fig. 8G). This network, facilitated through high-probability ligand-receptor axes, such as VEGFA-VEGFR2 (angiogenesis), THBS1-CD47 (immune evasion), and MIF-CD74/CXCR4 (inflammatory chemotaxis), dynamically interacts with B cells, cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), endothelial cells, and myeloid cells, collectively driving key processes including neovascularization, immune evasion, and invasion/metastasis (Fig. 8H).

After stringent selection (log2FC > 0.5, FDR < 0.0001), we finally identified 49 characteristic genes of the ATP6V0B+ M subgroup (Table S5). Validation using the ssGSEA algorithm across multiple cohorts indicated that the infiltration features of this subgroup in the tumor microenvironment were highly consistent with single-cell analysis. Individuals in the high-infiltration cohort not only demonstrated markedly inferior prognoses but also manifested enhanced treatment resistance (Supplementary Figure S2B-G).

ATP6V0B+ M subgroup promotes Immune-Cold phenotype and immunotherapy resistance

Utilizing the TCGA_BRCA dataset, we performed immune subtype analysis on 1,118 breast cancer samples using a consensus clustering algorithm, selecting a signature profile consisting of 12 immune-hot tumor marker genes for unsupervised clustering. Through analysis of the cumulative distribution function (CDF) curve and optimization of the CDF delta area, we identified the optimal clustering number as K = 2, resulting in clear classification of samples into immune-cold phenotype (n = 641) and immune-hot phenotype (n = 477). As shown in Fig. 9A, the immune-hot phenotype exhibits high expression characteristics of anti-tumor immune-related genes (such as CXCL9 and CXCL10) and cytotoxic effects, whereas the immune-cold phenotype is enriched in signals associated with an immunosuppressive microenvironment.

Further analysis revealed that ATP6V0B+ M subgroup infiltrates and ATP6V0B expression significantly higher in immune-cold phenotype samples compared to immune-hot phenotype (p < 0.01), a finding validated in independent cohorts from METABRIC and GSE96058 (Fig. 9B-C, Supplementary Figure S3). TIDE scoring analysis demonstrated that immune-cold phenotypes exhibit a significant association with enhanced activity of myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), elevated T-cell dysfunction index, and increased TIDE score. These findings collectively suggest that elevated infiltration of the ATP6V0B+ M subgroup correlates with resistance to immunotherapy. (Fig. 9D-H). GSEA enrichment analysis confirmed that this subgroup is not specifically enriched in 94 PD-L1-positive related immune regulatory geneset (Fig. 9I). Clinical validation using the GSE173839/GSE194040 cohorts (n = 140) demonstrated that ATP6V0B+ M subgroup infiltration levels in non-responders to immune checkpoint inhibitors were significantly higher than in responders (Fig. 9J). Notably, the immune subtypes of triple-negative breast cancer proposed by HammerlD et al. (exclusion type, neglect type, inflammatory type) further corroborate our findings: in the inflammatory type sensitive to anti-PD-1 treatment, ATP6V0B+ M subgroup infiltration levels were significantly lower than in the exclusion and neglect types (Fig. 9K). Collectively, these multidimensional evidence supports the biological mechanism by which ATP6V0B+ M subgroup drives an immune-cold microenvironment to promote Immunotherapy resistance.

Construction and validation of ATP6V0B+ M-Related prognostic signature

In this section, an ATP6V0B+ M-related prognostic signature was constructed based on an integrated breast cancer dataset (n = 6480). Initially, 18 prognosis-related genes were screened from 49 ATP6V0B+ M signature genes via univariate Cox regression (p < 0.05, Fig. 10A). Subsequently, these 18 genes were ranked by importance using random survival forest analysis, and the top 10 genes were selected for Kaplan-Meier analysis with log-rank tests across 1023 combinations (Fig. 10B, Table S6). Ultimately, a five-gene combination (ATP6V0B, CD24, CYB5A, KDELR2, KRT18) was identified as the optimal model based on the most significant p-value and minimal gene count. An ATP6V0B+ M-related breast cancer prognostic signature was constructed via multivariate Cox regression (riskscore = Σ [gene expression × regression coefficient]), with corresponding coefficients of 0.152, 0.066, −0.094, 0.221, and 0.088.

Patients were stratified by the median riskscore, revealing significantly shorter overall survival in the high-risk group (Fig. 10C, D, p < 0.001). Validation in independent cohorts confirmed consistent associations with OS, RFS, DMFS, and DFS (Supplementary Figure S4). Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses confirmed the signature as an independent prognostic risk factor for breast cancer (Fig. 10E). An integrated nomogram was constructed incorporating stage, age, and ATP6V0B+ M-related riskscore using R software (Fig. 10F). Time-dependent ROC analysis demonstrated stable prognostic performance, yielding AUC values of 0.78/0.74/0.72 for 1/3/5-year OS in the TCGA_BRCA cohort, 0.68/0.67/0.68 in the METABRIC cohort, and 0.76/0.74/0.70 in the GSE96058 cohort (Fig. 10G, Supplementary Figure S5A-B). Calibration curves showed consistency between predicted and observed survival rates, and decision curve analysis confirmed clinical utility (Fig. 10H-I, Supplementary Figure S 5 C-D).

Discussion

The urgent need for breast cancer research is underscored by its high global prevalence and mortality. Integrative bioinformatics and machine learning approaches have emerged as powerful tools for identifying disease-associated genes, yet their application in breast cancer TME research remains limited. Here, we combined CRISPR screening, machine learning, and single-cell transcriptomics to identify ATP6V0B as a breast cancer CEG, providing insights into its role in tumor progression and immune evasion.

Firstly, we successfully identified three feature genes closely associated with breast cancer growth—ATP6V0B, NUF2, and SPC25—using five distinct machine learning algorithms. Among these genes, NUF2 and SPC25 have been extensively studied in the context of malignancies. Notably, NUF2 has been demonstrated to exhibit overexpression in multiple cancer types, including gastric, ovarian, lung, pancreatic, and breast cancer [49–53]. In these malignancies, elevated levels of NUF2 are often correlated with aggressive tumor behavior, increased proliferation, and poor clinical outcomes [54]. For instance, in breast cancer, elevated expression of NUF2 is associated with multiple clinical parameters, including subtype, pathological stage, and overall prognosis, supporting its potential utility as a biomarker for disease progression. The underlying mechanism behind NUF2’s function involves its critical role during mitosis, particularly in the formation and stabilization of kinetochores, which are essential for accurate chromosome segregation during cell division [55, 56]. Similarly, SPC25 has also been found to exhibit upregulated expression across various cancer types, including gastric cancer, lung cancer, liver cancer and breast cancer [6–59]. In these studies, high SPC25 expression levels are associated with enhanced cellular proliferation and metastatic potential. Specifically, in breast cancer, SPC25 is expressed particularly high in the most aggressive subtype, triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), indicating its detrimental impact on patient prognosis and treatment outcomes [60]. The mechanistic insight into SPC25 indicates its role in facilitating proper kinetochore assembly and chromosome alignment. when disrupted, this can lead to chromosomal instability—a common hallmark driving tumorigenesis in many cancers. Although ATP6V0B serves as a crucial subunit of the V-ATPase complex and exhibits the highest activity among the three identified genes, research surrounding its specific role in tumors, particularly breast cancer, remains comparatively limited. This lack of understanding prompted us to focus our investigation primarily on the ATP6V0B molecule.

Subsequently, diverging from traditional gene expression perspectives, we undertook a thorough analysis of ATP6V0B from the perspective of its activity, exploring its relationships with breast cancer diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment response. Our results revealed that ATP6V0B activity is significantly elevated in breast cancer and closely correlates with various pathological features of patients, including clinical grading, staging, lymph node metastasis, tumor size, and molecular subtypes. Notably, elevated levels of ATP6V0B activity exhibit an association with more aggressive tumor characteristics, suggesting that ATP6V0B may function as a critical driver of tumor progression. Furthermore, our data illuminate ATP6V0B’s potential as a biomarker for breast cancer. The ability of ATP6V0B to predict the occurrence, progression, clinical outcomes and therapeutic responses in breast cancer positions it as a valuable tool in personalized medicine approaches. For instance, high ATP6V0B activity may provide clinicians with insights regarding a patient’s likelihood of poor prognosis or treatment resistance, thereby assisting in guiding therapeutic decisions and management strategies tailored to individual patient needs.

Next, we systematically analyzed the molecular mechanisms by which ATP6V0B regulates the malignant progression of breast cancer using single-cell data and discovered a dual driving mechanism of the ATP6V0B+ tumor cell subgroup in breast cancer progression. At the cellular autonomous level, these cells exhibited pronounced malignant phenotype characteristics related to angiogenesis, apoptosis, cell cycle, differentiation, DNA damage, DNA repair, epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), hypoxia, inflammation, invasion, metastasis, proliferation, quiescence, and stemness, forming a “core driving unit” for malignant progression in breast cancer. At the microenvironmental regulatory level, this subgroup interacts dynamically with tumor-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), endothelial cells, and myeloid cells through ligand-receptor axes such as VEGFA-VEGFR2, THBS1-CD47, and MIF-CD74/CXCR4. Studies have shown that VEGFA-VEGFR2 plays a crucial role in promoting angiogenesis and recruiting endothelial cells in tumor vascularization, THBS1-CD47 inhibits macrophage phagocytic function and recruits Tregs to enhance immune evasion, while MIF-CD74/CXCR4 can drive inflammatory chemotaxis, establishing a pro-metastatic niche [61–63]. Notably, these ligand-receptor interactions and their downstream effects are inferred from bioinformatic analyses of multi-omics datasets. Future validation via in vitro co-culture assays and in vivo xenograft models is required to confirm causal relationships. In contrast to traditional v-ATPase studies that mainly focus on the pH regulatory function, this study innovatively reveals that high expression of ATP6V0B characterizes a tumor cell subgroup that drives the malignant progression of breast cancer through a “cell autonomous - microenvironmental synergy” dual action model. The identification of ATP6V0B as a central hub in signaling networks lays a theoretical foundation for the investigation and development of targeted therapeutic interventions.

Heterogeneity within the tumor immune microenvironment exerts a significant impact on therapeutic responses in breast cancer. Herein, we identified the ATP6V0B+ M subgroup as a key regulatory determinant that modulates the immune-cold phenotype. Our results demonstrate specific enrichment of this subgroup within immune-cold tumors. TIDE analysis revealed a robust association between the ATP6V0B+ M subgroup and three distinct biological features: enhanced infiltration of myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), T cell dysfunction, and augmented therapeutic resistance. Further validation using the GSE173839 and GSE194040 cohorts confirmed that patients with the ATP6V0B+ M subgroup exhibit reduced response rates to immunotherapy. Interestingly, the PD-L1-related gene set was significantly enriched in the ATP6V0B− M subgroup, and it exhibited a strong correlation with the exclusion phenotype described by Hammerl et al., indicating that ATP6V0B may mediate immune resistance through checkpoint-dependent pathways. Existing literature suggests that ATP6V0B, as a component of V-ATPase, can promote tumor microenvironment acidification when overexpressed, thereby enhancing lysosomal acidification [64]. The observed increase in MDSC infiltration in our study may be related to this phenomenon, as an acidic microenvironment is known to activate the STAT3 signaling pathway, leading to myeloid immune suppression [65]. Moreover, ATP6V0B-mediated metabolic reprogramming may facilitate immune evasion via a dual mechanism: enhancing tumor cell glutamine metabolism to sustain proliferation while simultaneously depleting essential amino acids (such as arginine) in the microenvironment, thereby inhibiting T cell activation and function [66, 67]. This hypothesis aligns with the observed decrease in T cell dysfunction scores in cold tumors. Importantly, the association of the ATP6V0B+ M subgroup with the exclusion phenotype, elucidated in the present study, provides novel insights for clinical translation. Given the anticancer effects of V-ATPase inhibitors (e.g., Bafilomycin A1 derivatives), targeted inhibition of ATP6V0B+ M subgroups may enhance immunotherapeutic efficacy by reversing the acidic microenvironment and mitigating MDSC infiltration. However, the current study lacks direct experimental evidence to validate this synergism. Future studies will prioritize testing ATP6V0B silencing or V-ATPase inhibitors in syngeneic mouse models to assess changes in immune-cold phenotype markers and response to immune checkpoint blockade.

Building upon previous studies on the association between ATP6V0B and breast cancer, we constructed a prognostic risk signature incorporating ATP6V0B, CD24, CYB5A, KDELR2, and KRT18 using ATP6V0B+ M signature genes (calculated based on gene expression levels and regression coefficients). This signature enabled effective and precise prognostic stratification of breast cancer patients via risk scoring: the high-risk group demonstrated significantly shorter OS, with stable predictive performance across multiple outcome metrics—encompassing OS, RFS, DMFS, and DFS—in independent cohorts, providing a basis for clinical identification of high-risk populations and formulation of individualized follow-up strategies (e.g., intensifying postoperative monitoring frequency for high-risk patients to enable early detection of disease progression). Second, as an independent prognostic factor, the signature was integrated with clinical stage and age to construct a nomogram. Time-dependent ROC analysis demonstrated that the AUC values for 1/3/5-year OS prediction were all > 0.7, calibration curves and decision curve analysis further validated its predictive accuracy and clinical utility. To enhance clinical feasibility, the nomogram can be integrated into existing pathology workflows via two approaches: (i) Embedding the riskscore calculation into digital pathology reporting systems, where ATP6V0B expression (assessed by RNA sequencing) and clinicopathological parameters (stage, age) are automatically populated to generate individualized survival predictions; (ii) Developing a web-based or EHR-integrated calculator (e.g., using R Shiny or HL7 FHIR standards) that allows clinicians to input patient data and receive real-time risk stratification, supporting treatment decision-making (e.g., adjuvant therapy intensification for high-risk patients. This facilitates optimizing treatment decisions: for patients with the same clinical stage, combining riskscore allows further differentiation of treatment needs—high-risk patients may require intensified adjuvant therapy (e.g., increased chemotherapy cycles or combination with targeted drugs), while low-risk patients can avoid overtreatment to reduce toxic side effects—thereby providing biomarker support for the development of individualized treatment strategies for breast cancer.

Limitations and future validation

However, this study has several limitations. First, the study primarily relies on bioinformatics analyses and retrospective clinical data, lacking in vivo and in vitro experimental validation of ATP6V0B function. Future studies will prioritize the development of genetically engineered mouse models and implementation of multi-omics longitudinal tracking to deepen the understanding of the underlying mechanisms. Moreover, the proposed dual mechanism of ATP6V0B+ subpopulations (cell-autonomous malignant features and microenvironmental regulation) requires orthogonal validation, including single-cell spatial transcriptomics and CRISPR-edited organoid models to dissect cell-cell communication pathways. Second, although the model constructed within the present work has demonstrated robustness through cross-validation, the clinical applicability of the scoring system requires further validation through multicenter prospective clinical trials and interventional studies. Future work will prioritize validation of the nomogram in prospective clinical settings, including integration into multi-center EHR systems to evaluate its utility in real-world treatment planning. Additionally, collaboration with pathology informatics teams will streamline ATP6V0B expression quantification (e.g., via AI-assisted image analysis of IHC slides) to reduce inter-observer variability and facilitate automated riskscore calculation.

Conclusions

In summary, this study integrates CRISPR screening, machine learning, and multi-omics data to systematically analyze over 10,000 breast cancer samples, identifying ATP6V0B as a potential cancer essential gene. Our findings demonstrate that ATP6V0B exhibits high expression in tumor tissues and additionally shows a positive association with aggressive clinicopathological characteristics (including larger tumor size, lymph node metastasis, and aggressive molecular subtypes) as well as poor prognosis, thus highlighting its potential utility as a prognostic biomarker. Mechanistically, ATP6V0B+ tumor cell subsets are associated with an immunosuppressive microenvironment through ligand-receptor interactions such as VEGFA-VEGFR2, correlating with resistance to immune checkpoint inhibitors, which further supports ATP6V0B as a promising therapeutic target. While our multi-omics analyses robustly implicate ATP6V0B as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target, these findings remain hypothesis-generating. Functional validation through CRISPR-mediated loss/gain-of-function experiments and genetically engineered mouse models is essential to mechanistically confirm its role in breast cancer pathogenesis and immune evasion. Furthermore, the constructed nomogram, which integrates ATP6V0B+ tumor cell subset-related prognostic signature, clinical stage, and age, significantly improves the accuracy of prognostic stratification, providing a valuable data-driven tool for individualized prognostic assessment in breast cancer. Collectively, these results not only clarify the biological and clinical significance of ATP6V0B in breast cancer but also offer a practical resource for advancing precision oncology research and clinical decision-making.

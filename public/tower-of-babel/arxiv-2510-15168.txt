Affine Predicate Geometry: A Courcelle-Type Metatheorem for Rank-Bounded Pseudo-Boolean Optimization
Marc Harary

Abstract
. Many discrete optimization problems—including low-rank quadratic unconstrained binary optimization (QUBO), trimmed covariance, and cardinality-constrained pseudo-Boolean programs—share a common structure: the gain of any elementary move (bit-flip or swap) can be expressed as an affine form in a low-dimensional feature map ϕ ⁡ ( x ) ∈ ℝ R \phi(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{R} . We call this the Affine–Predicate Condition, and show that it captures all rank-structured pseudo-Boolean objectives, including higher-order forms via multilinear lifting. We prove an Affine–Predicate Meta–Theorem: under this condition, every locally optimal solution must lie in a chamber of a central hyperplane arrangement in ℝ R + 1 \mathbb{R}^{R+1} , defined by the zero-sets of these affine gains. These chambers can be enumerated in m O ⁡ ( R ) m^{O(R)} time, where m m is the number of elementary feasible directions. Consequently, exact optimization is in XP time n O ⁡ ( R ) n^{O(R)} in the effective rank R R , even under uniform matroid (cardinality) constraints. As a corollary, we obtain the first unified n O ⁡ ( r ) n^{O(r)} exact algorithm for rank- r r QUBO and its cardinality-constrained extension. This establishes rank as a structural parameter for discrete optimization, analogous to treewidth in algorithmic graph theory and Courcelle-type meta-theorems.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Problem setting and motivation

Many NP-hard discrete optimization problems on
\{0,1\}^{n}
—including
low-rank QUBO
,
trimmed covariance estimation
, and
cardinality-constrained pseudo-Boolean maximization
—exhibit a recurring structural pattern: although the objective is combinatorial, the
gain
of a local move often depends only on a
low-dimensional affine statistic
of
x
. This phenomenon appears in quadratic forms through row-rank structure, in fractional objectives through cross-multiplication, and in robust statistics through trimming thresholds. Yet there is no general framework explaining when such latent affine structure implies algorithmic tractability.

We take the view that
rank
, not polynomial degree or constraint simplicity, is the fundamental parameter controlling this phenomenon. Our goal is to make this precise by identifying a single predicate condition under which local optimality corresponds to membership in a low-dimensional hyperplane arrangement, rendering the search space enumerable in rank-dependent time.

1.2.
Guiding example: product of two linears

Let

This

\displaystyle\max

\displaystyle(a^{\top}x)(b^{\top}x)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle x\in\{0,1\}^{n},

Flipping coordinate

Thus the

1.4.
Relation to existing paradigms

Our use of rank as a structural parameter is analogous in spirit to the role of
width parameters
such as treewidth in Courcelle-type meta-theorems
(
elberfeld2016variants
)
, where bounded logical dimension yields algorithmic tractability. In contrast to low-rank

The geometric machinery we invoke traces back to classical hyperplane arrangement and zonotope theory

Notation

We write

2.2.
Hyperplane arrangements

A
hyperplane arrangement

\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a finite collection

\mathcal{A}=\{H_{1},\dots,H_{m}\}
, where each

H_{i}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:w_{i}^{\top}\xi+c_{i}=0\}

The arrangement is

The arrangement

into relatively open

faces
. Each face

is characterized by its

sign vector

\sigma_{F}\in\{-,0,+\}^{m}

\sigma_{F}(i):=\begin{cases}+,&w_{i}^{\top}\xi+c_{i}>0\text{ for all }\xi\in F,\\
0,&w_{i}^{\top}\xi+c_{i}=0\text{ for all }\xi\in F,\\
-,&w_{i}^{\top}\xi+c_{i}<0\text{ for all }\xi\in F.\end{cases}

Maximal-dimensional faces are called

or

.
We write
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A})
for the face lattice and

\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{A})

The following classical result controls the combinatorial complexity of arrangements:

Theorem 1 (Zaslavsky
(
Zaslavsky1975
)
).

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a central arrangement of
m

\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then

|\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A})|\leq\sum_{j=0}^{d}\binom{m}{j}=O(m^{d}).

In particular,
|\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{A})|=O(m^{d})
.

2.3.
Matroid circuits

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times n}
have full row rank
p
.
The
column matroid

M(A)
on ground set
[n]
has rank
p
,
with independent sets corresponding to linearly independent column subsets.
A subset
S\subseteq[n]
is
dependent
in

if
the columns
\{A[:,i]\}_{i\in S}
are linearly dependent.
A

is a minimally dependent set.

In any rank-
p
matroid, every circuit has cardinality at least
p+1
.
In the
uniform matroid

—where every

(p+1)

\binom{n}{p+1}
.

We return to matroid circuits in subsequent sections, where circuits
of the constraint Jacobian will parametrize elementary feasible directions,
and the bound
\binom{n}{p+1}
will control the arrangement size in

3.
Affine-Predicate Meta-Theorem

3.1.
Affine Gain Module
\mathcal{HQ}

We begin by isolating the algebraic condition that ensures all local gains become affine predicates in a low-dimensional feature representation. This condition will generate, in later subsections, a finite hyperplane arrangement whose chambers encode all locally optimal solutions. Throughout,
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{r}
denotes a feature map (not necessarily linear, though our applications in §4 will take
\psi
linear).

Definition 0 (Homomorphically quadratic functions).

A function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}

homomorphically quadratic of dimension

\psi

1
1

1

The term reflects the factorization

such that

f(x)\;=\;\tfrac{1}{2}\,\psi(x)^{\top}Q\,\psi(x)\;+\;b^{\top}\psi(x)\;+\;a,\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The

is the smallest

Remark 0.

For fixed
\psi

To interface with hyperplane arrangements, we homogenize the representation.

Proposition 0 (Homogeneous lift).

If

\tilde{\psi}(x):=(\psi(x),1)\in\mathbb{R}^{r+1},\qquad\tilde{Q}:=\begin{pmatrix}Q&b\\
b^{\top}&2a\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{(r+1)\times(r+1)},

f(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\tilde{\psi}(x)^{\top}\tilde{Q}\,\tilde{\psi}(x).

Proof.

A direct expansion gives

\tfrac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\psi\\
1\end{pmatrix}^{\top}\begin{pmatrix}Q&b\\
b^{\top}&2a\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\psi\\
1\end{pmatrix}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\psi^{\top}Q\psi+b^{\top}\psi+a.\qed

Example (P2L continued).

Here
\tilde{\psi}(x)=(a^{\top}x,\,b^{\top}x,\,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}=\mathbb{R}^{r+1}

.
This embedding is precisely the ambient space in which the affine gain hyperplanes for P2L will appear in §3.3.

Lemma 0 (Gradient and directional derivatives).

In the setting of Proposition

By the chain rule,

\nabla f(x)=J_{\psi}(x)^{\top}\nabla_{\xi}(\tfrac{1}{2}\xi^{\top}Q\xi+b^{\top}\xi+a)|_{\xi=\psi(x)}=J_{\psi}(x)^{\top}(Q\psi(x)+b)

∎

Example (P2L continued).

For

Substitute

3.2.
Elementary Feasible Directions

In §3.1 we showed that gains become affine predicates in
\tilde{\psi}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{r+1}
. To bound the number of such predicates, we now characterize the
elementary feasible directions
—minimal combinatorial moves on
\{0,1\}^{n}
—that generate the hyperplanes of our arrangement.

3.2.1.
Discrete moves on the hypercube

For a feasible region
F\subseteq\{0,1\}^{n}
defined by linear constraints
A_{E}x=b
with
x\geq 0
, a direction
d\in\{-1,0,+1\}^{n}
is
elementary feasible
at
x\in F
if:

•

x+d\in F
(feasibility), and

•

\mathrm{supp}(d)
is inclusion-minimal with this property (elementarity).

We denote the set of all such directions by
\mathcal{C}(x)
.

Matroid interpretation.

When
A_{E}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times n}
has full row rank
p
, feasibility
A_{E}d=0
means
\mathrm{supp}(d)
indexes a linearly dependent set of columns. Minimality means this set is a
circuit
of the column matroid
M(A_{E})
. Thus elementary feasible directions correspond to signed matroid circuits.

Theorem 5 (Circuit bound).

Let
A_{E}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times n}
have full row rank
p
. Then every elementary feasible direction at any
x\in F
has support size

|\mathrm{supp}(d)|\;\geq\;p+1.

If
A_{E}
has generic column positions (uniform matroid
U_{p,n}
), then
|\mathrm{supp}(d)|=p+1
exactly, and the number of distinct elementary feasible directions is at most

m:=|\mathcal{C}(x)|\;\leq\;\binom{n}{p+1}.

Proof sketch.

Circuits in a rank-
p
matroid have size
\geq p+1
. In the uniform matroid, every
(p+1)
-subset is a circuit, giving exactly
\binom{n}{p+1}
circuits. Signed directions respect these combinatorial bounds.
∎

3.2.2.
Two canonical cases

Example 0 (Unconstrained: bit-flips).

For
F=\{0,1\}^{n}
(no constraints beyond box),
A_{E}
is empty and elementary feasible directions are single coordinate flips

.

Example 0 (Cardinality constraint: swaps).

For
F=\{x\in\{0,1\}^{n}:\sum_{i}x_{i}=k\}

(rank

. At any feasible

Remark 0 (Beyond cardinality).

Our framework extends to any constraint matrix

We have identified the elementary feasible directions and bounded their number by

3.3.
Stability and the affine face lattice

We have established in §3.1 that for HQ objectives, the gain of each elementary
feasible direction
d\in\mathcal{C}(x)
satisfies

\Delta_{d}(x)=f(x+d)-f(x)=\tilde{w}_{d}^{\top}\tilde{\psi}(x)

for some
\tilde{w}_{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{r+1}
, and in §3.2 that

|\mathcal{C}(x)|\leq\binom{n}{p+1}
. Thus each
d\in\mathcal{C}(x)
induces
a
gain predicate
whose zero-set is a hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{r+1}
. We now show that

local optimality is equivalent to belonging to a face of the resulting arrangement
.

3.3.1.
Stability via EFD gains

Definition 0 (EFD-stability).

Let
F\subseteq\{0,1\}^{n}
be a feasible set and
f:F\to\mathbb{R}
.
A point
x\in F
is
EFD-stable
if

\Delta_{d}(x):=f(x+d)-f(x)\leq 0\qquad\text{for all }d\in\mathcal{C}(x).

We denote the set of stable points by
\mathrm{Stab}(f)
.

Remark 0.

EFD-stability is the discrete analog of continuous first-order optimality

\nabla f(x)^{\top}d\leq 0
over all feasible directions. Since
\mathcal{C}(x)

comprises all elementary moves, EFD-stability is equivalent to being a local
maximum in the adjacency graph defined by feasible moves.

Proposition 0 (Optimality implies stability).

Every global maximizer lies in
\mathrm{Stab}(f)
:

\mathrm{Opt}(f):=\arg\max_{x\in F}f(x)\ \subseteq\ \mathrm{Stab}(f).

Proof.

If
x\in\mathrm{Opt}(f)
, then
f(x)\geq f(y)
for all
y\in F
, including

y=x+d
for any
d\in\mathcal{C}(x)
. Hence
\Delta_{d}(x)\leq 0

for all such
d
, so
x\in\mathrm{Stab}(f)
.
∎

3.3.2.
Gain predicates define a global hyperplane arrangement

Under the HQ hypothesis (§3.1), each gain predicate becomes linear in the lifted feature:

\Delta_{d}(x)=\tilde{w}_{d}^{\top}\tilde{\psi}(x),\qquad\tilde{\psi}(x):=(\psi(x),1)\in\mathbb{R}^{r+1}.

Thus each

defines a central hyperplane

H_{d}:=\{\,\tilde{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{r+1}:\tilde{w}_{d}^{\top}\tilde{\xi}=0\,\}.

Collecting all possible elementary feasible directions across all feasible points
defines the
global EFD catalog

\mathcal{C}:=\bigcup_{x\in F}\mathcal{C}(x),

and the associated
global hyperplane arrangement

\mathcal{A}:=\{\,H_{d}:d\in\mathcal{C}\,\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{r+1}.

For linear constraints
A_{E}x=b
with
x\geq 0
, the set
\mathcal{C}
depends
only on
A_{E}
, so
|\mathcal{C}|\leq\binom{n}{p+1}

uniformly by Theorem
5
. Thus
\mathcal{A}
is a fixed combinatorial object
that can be constructed once for the entire instance.

(

If all
\Delta_{d}(x)\neq 0
, then
\tilde{\psi}(x)
avoids all
H_{d}
, so
F_{x}
is
full-dimensional (a chamber) and uniquely determined.
∎

Each EFD yields a separating hyperplane in feature space; stability means

\tilde{\psi}(x)
lies in the intersection of all “downward” halfspaces.
Thus the discrete local-optimality landscape is geometrically encoded
by faces of
\mathcal{A}
.

We have shown that every stable point
x
corresponds to a face of the global arrangement
\mathcal{A}
via its gain sign pattern. Since
\mathcal{A}
has at most
m\leq\binom{n}{p+1}
hyperplanes in
\mathbb{R}^{r+1}
, Zaslavsky’s theorem (Theorem

) gives
O(m^{r+1})
faces. In §3.4, we show how to enumerate chambers efficiently via zonotope duality, and in §3.5 we derive the final XP bound
n^{O(R)}
where
R=r+1
.

3.4.
Zonotope Duality and Chamber Enumeration

Having established in §3.3 that
x\in\mathrm{Stab}(f)
if and only if
\tilde{\psi}(x)
lies in a face
of the affine gain arrangement
\mathcal{A}
, we now invoke a classical duality:

chambers of a central arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{r+1}
correspond to vertices of a
zonotope generated by the arrangement normals
. This duality provides a constructive way
to enumerate all candidate optimal regions.

3.4.1.
The affine gain zonotope

Let

\tilde{W}\;:=\;\{\tilde{w}_{d}:d\in\mathcal{C}\}\;\subset\;\mathbb{R}^{r+1}

be the collection of homogeneous normals from all elementary feasible directions.
Define the
affine gain zonotope

(1)

\mathcal{Z}(\tilde{W})\;:=\;\sum_{d\in\mathcal{C}}\bigl[-\tilde{w}_{d},\ \tilde{w}_{d}\bigr]\;=\;\left\{\,\sum_{d\in\mathcal{C}}\lambda_{d}\tilde{w}_{d}\;:\;-1\leq\lambda_{d}\leq 1\,\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{r+1}.

This convex polytope is a Minkowski sum of signed EFD normals and encodes all
sign-consistent selections of affine gain predicates.

3.4.2.
Arrangement–zonotope duality

Theorem 12 (Zonotope–arrangement duality
(
Zaslavsky1975
;
Edelsbrunner1987
)
).

The face lattice
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{Z}(\tilde{W}))
is anti-isomorphic to the face lattice

\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A})
of the hyperplane arrangement

\mathcal{A}:=\{\,H_{d}:d\in\mathcal{C}\,\},\qquad H_{d}:=\{\,\tilde{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{r+1}:\tilde{w}_{d}^{\top}\tilde{\xi}=0\,\}.

In particular:

•

Chambers (topes) of
\mathcal{A}
correspond bijectively to vertices of
\mathcal{Z}(\tilde{W})
.

•

Adjacency in the chamber graph corresponds to edges of
\mathcal{Z}(\tilde{W})
.

3.4.3.
Chamber–vertex correspondence formula

Each chamber
C\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{A})
has a sign vector

\sigma_{C}\in\{-,+\}^{\mathcal{C}}
. Under the duality above, one obtains the vertex

(2)

v_{C}:=\sum_{d\in\mathcal{C}}\sigma_{C}(d)\,\tilde{w}_{d}\;\in\;\operatorname{vert}\big(\mathcal{Z}(\tilde{W})\big).

Proposition 0 (Stable chambers yield vertices).

If
x\in\mathrm{Stab}(f)
lies in chamber
C
(equivalently,
\tilde{\psi}(x)\in C
and

\Delta_{d}(x)\neq 0
for all
d\in\mathcal{C}(x)

corresponds under
Theorem
12
to the zonotope vertex
v_{C}
in (
2
).

Proof.

By Theorem
11
,
\tilde{\psi}(x)
belongs to a unique chamber
C

with sign vector
\sigma_{C}(d)=\mathrm{sgn}\,\Delta_{d}(x)
for

Under the duality of Theorem

, the vertex of

\mathcal{Z}(\tilde{W})
dual to

is obtained by choosing each generator
\tilde{w}_{d}
or

-\tilde{w}_{d}
according to
\sigma_{C}(d)

).
∎

Reduction to vertex enumeration

Corollary 0 (Optimization via chamber/vertex enumeration).

Every global optimum

3.5.
Support Reconstruction from Chamber Patterns

By §3.4, exact optimization reduces to enumerating all chambers of the arrangement

\mathcal{A}=\{H_{d}:d\in\mathcal{C}\}

and evaluating
f
on a feasible representative
x\in F
whose lifted feature

\tilde{\psi}(x)
lies in that chamber.
We now show that for combinatorial constraint sets such as cardinality slices,
the chamber sign pattern
\sigma_{C}

directly determines
the stable support

\mathrm{supp}(x)
in
O(n\log n)
time, without solving a continuous feasibility problem.

3.5.1.
Cardinality constraints: chambers encode top-
K
selection

Consider

F=\{\,x\in\{0,1\}^{n}:\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=K\,\},\qquad\psi(x)=Px,\quad f\in\mathcal{HQ}^{r}(Px).

By §3.2, the elementary feasible directions are swaps
d=e_{i}-e_{j}

removing
j\in\mathrm{supp}(x)
and adding
i\notin\mathrm{supp}(x)
.
By Corollary
4
, each gain has the form

\Delta_{i\to j}(x)=f(x+e_{i}-e_{j})-f(x)=\tilde{w}_{i\to j}^{\top}\tilde{\psi}(x)=\kappa_{i}-\kappa_{j},

where the affine scores
\kappa_{i}=(P^{\top}QP)_{ii}+(P^{\top}b)_{i}
are
independent of
x
once the chamber is fixed.

Proposition 0 (Chamber patterns enforce top-
K
structure).

Let
C\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{A})
be a chamber with sign vector

\sigma_{C}\in\{-,+\}^{\mathcal{C}}
.
Then any
x\in F
with
\tilde{\psi}(x)\in C
is EFD-stable if and only if

\kappa_{j}\geq\kappa_{i}\quad\text{for all }j\in\mathrm{supp}(x),\ i\notin\mathrm{supp}(x).

Thus
x\in\mathrm{Stab}(f)\cap C
if and only if
\mathrm{supp}(x)
consists of the

K
largest entries of
\kappa
, i.e. the
top-
K
coordinates
of
\kappa
.

Proof.

For
x\in F
, EFD-stability in
C
requires
\Delta_{i\to j}(x)\leq 0
for all
swaps with
i\notin S=\mathrm{supp}(x)
and
j\in S
.
Since
\Delta_{i\to j}(x)=\kappa_{i}-\kappa_{j}
, this is equivalent to

\kappa_{i}\leq\kappa_{j}
for all such pairs.
Combined with
|S|=K
, this enforces that
S
contains the
K
largest values
of
\kappa
, i.e.,
S
is the top-
K
set.
The converse is immediate: if
S
is the top-
K
, then all such inequalities hold.
∎

Remark 0 (Complexity).

Given chamber
C
, computing
\kappa
and sorting yields the stable support

S
in
O(n\log n)
time.
Thus chamber
\mapsto
support reconstruction is purely combinatorial and avoids
any continuous inversion of
\tilde{\psi}
.

3.5.2.
Generalization via blocking clauses

The top-
K
mechanism reveals a broader pattern.
For an arbitrary feasible set
F\subseteq\{0,1\}^{n}
, let an EFD
d
have negative
support
S^{-}(d)=\{i:d_{i}<0\}
and positive support
S^{+}(d)=\{i:d_{i}>0\}
.
A support
S

admits
move
d
if
S^{-}(d)\subseteq S
and
S^{+}(d)\cap S=\varnothing
.
If chamber
C
has
\sigma_{C}(d)=+
(i.e.
d
would improve
f
within
C
), then
any stable
S
in
C
must
block

d
, i.e.

S^{-}(d)\nsubseteq S\quad\text{or}\quad S^{+}(d)\cap S\neq\varnothing.

In particular, for cardinality constraints
S^{-}(d)=\{j\},\,S^{+}(d)=\{i\}
, so
blocking all improving
d=i\to j
is exactly the top-
K
condition.

Remark 0 (Unconstrained case).

For
F=\{0,1\}^{n}
, EFDs are single flips
\pm e_{i}
and the blocking clause for

\sigma_{C}(+e_{i})=+
is that
x_{i}=1
must hold; similarly,
\sigma_{C}(-e_{i})=+

forces
x_{i}=0
. Hence
\sigma_{C}
directly fixes the support bit-by-bit.

Remark 0 (General feasibility).

For more general matroid or polyhedral constraint sets, blocking clauses define a
polynomial-time feasibility test.
For structured cases such as uniform matroids or partition constraints, this yields
greedy or exchange-based reconstruction schemes analogous to top-
K
selection.
Specific instances will be illustrated in §4.

Transition to XP bound.

Each chamber
C
yields a feasible candidate
x
in time
\operatorname{poly}(n)

via top-
K
reconstruction (cardinality) or blocking-clause filtering (general case).
Since
\mathcal{A}
has at most
m\leq\binom{n}{p+1}
hyperplanes, it has

O(m^{r+1})
chambers by Theorem
1
.
In §3.6, we combine these bounds to obtain the overall XP complexity
n^{O(R)}
with

R=r+1
.

3.6.
XP Complexity Bound

We now combine the bounds from §3.1–3.5 to derive the overall complexity
of exact optimization via chamber enumeration.

3.6.1.
The key parameters

Our algorithm depends on three parameters:

•

Feature rank

r
: the HQ-dimension of the feature map

\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{r}
(§3.1),

•

Constraint rank

p
: the Jacobian rank of the equality
constraints
g(x)=0
(§3.2),

•

Problem size

n
: the number of binary variables.

We define the
effective rank parameter

R:=r+p.

From §3.2 and §3.4 we obtain the bounds:

(3)

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle:=|\mathcal{C}|\leq\binom{n}{p+1},

\displaystyle\text{(EFD count, Theorem~\ref{thm:efd-bound})},

(4)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{A})|

\displaystyle\leq O(m^{r+1}),

\displaystyle\text{(chamber count, Theorem~\ref{thm:zaslavsky})},

(5)

Reconstruction cost

\displaystyle=O(n\log n),

\displaystyle\text{(top-$K$/blocking, \textsection 3.5)}.

3.6.2.
Main complexity theorem

Theorem 16 (XP-time exact optimization in effective rank).

Let
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be an HQ objective with feature map

\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{r}
, and let

F\subseteq\{0,1\}^{n}
be defined by equality constraints
g(x)=0

with Jacobian rank
p
. Then there exists an explicit algorithm that computes

\mathrm{Opt}(f):=\arg\max_{x\in F}f(x)

in time

T(n,r,p)=O\!\left(n\log n\cdot\binom{n}{p+1}^{\,r+1}\right).

Using
\binom{n}{p+1}=O(n^{p+1})
, this simplifies to the XP bound

T(n,r,p)=n^{O(r+p)}\;=\;n^{O(R)}.

Proof.

By Theorem
5
, the number of elementary feasible directions is

m\leq\binom{n}{p+1}
. By Theorem
12
and
Zaslavsky’s theorem (Theorem
1
), the hyperplane arrangement

\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{r+1}
has at most
O(m^{r+1})
chambers.
By Theorem
11
, every global optimum lies in some chamber.
By Proposition
15
, a representative
x\in F
for each chamber can be
reconstructed in
O(n\log n)
time (cardinality case) or
\operatorname{poly}(n)

time (general case). Evaluating
f(x)
costs
O(n)
.
Thus

T(n,r,p)=O(n\log n)\cdot O(m^{r+1})=O\!\left(n\log n\cdot\binom{n}{p+1}^{r+1}\right).

Substituting
m=O(n^{p+1})
gives

T(n,r,p)=n^{O((p+1)(r+1))}=n^{O(r+p)}
.
∎

Remark 0 (Courcelle-type interpretation).

The effective rank
R=r+p
plays the role of a
structural width parameter
:
for fixed
R
, exact optimization runs in polynomial time in
n
. This is directly
analogous to Courcelle’s theorem
(
elberfeld2016variants
)
,
where bounded treewidth yields XP/FPT meta-theorems for MSO-definable graph problems.
Here, bounded affine rank yields an XP meta-theorem for rank-structured
pseudo-Boolean optimization.

Remark 0 (Space complexity).

Arrangement or zonotope enumeration requires storing local adjacency and chamber
sign vectors, using
O(m^{r}\cdot\operatorname{poly}(n))
space. For fixed
r

and
p
, this is
n^{O(r+p)}
space.

4.
Instantiations

We now instantiate the affine-predicate meta-theorem for three important classes of pseudo–Boolean objectives:
(i) rank-
1
quadratic objectives (
product of two linears
),
(ii) general rank-
r
QUBO, and
(iii) higher-degree PUBO via symmetric tensor factorization.
In each case we identify the feature map
\psi
, determine the effective lift dimension
R
,
describe the elementary feasible directions, and conclude an XP-time exact optimization bound from Theorem
16
.

4.1.
Rank-1 Quadratic: Product of Two Linears

Let
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
have
\mathrm{rank}(Q)=1
. Then there exist

u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
Q=uv^{\top}
. The corresponding pseudo–Boolean
objective is

f(x)\;=\;x^{\top}Qx\;=\;(u^{\top}x)(v^{\top}x),\qquad x\in\{0,1\}^{n}.

This is exactly the
product-of-two-linears
objective, sometimes written in
abbreviated form as

\text{(P2L)}\qquad\max\,\{\,(u^{\top}x)(v^{\top}x)\;:\;x\in\{0,1\}^{n}\,\}.

HQ feature map.

Define the feature map

\psi(x):=\big(u^{\top}x,\;v^{\top}x\big)\;\in\;\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Then

f(x)=(u^{\top}x)(v^{\top}x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}u^{\top}x&v^{\top}x\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\[2.0pt]
1&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u^{\top}x\\[2.0pt]
v^{\top}x\end{pmatrix},

so
f\in\mathcal{HQ}^{2}(\psi)
with HQ-rank
r=2
. The homogeneous lift dimension
is
R=r+1=3
. Since there are no equality constraints,
p=0
and the set of
elementary feasible directions consists of single bit-flips
d=\pm e_{i}
.

Arrangement size and XP complexity.

By Theorem
5
,
m=|\mathcal{C}|=2n
.
The induced affine gain predicates define a hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}
in

\mathbb{R}^{r+1}=\mathbb{R}^{3}
. By Zaslavsky’s formula
(Theorem
1
), the number of chambers is

|\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{A})|=O(m^{r+1})=O\big((2n)^{3}\big)=O(n^{3}).

Each chamber corresponds to one stable assignment. Reconstructing the bitstring
x

from its chamber sign pattern and evaluating
f(x)=(u^{\top}x)(v^{\top}x)
both take

O(n)
time. Therefore, by Theorem
16
with
(r,p)=(2,0)
, the total
runtime is

T(n,2,0)=O(n)\cdot O(n^{3})=O(n^{4}).

Remark 0 (Comparison to classical algorithms).

Hammer et al.
(
hammer2002maximizing
)
gave an
O(n^{3})
combinatorial algorithm
for this special rank-1 case. Our arrangement-based approach yields an
O(n^{4})

algorithm, which is slightly slower asymptotically but arises automatically from a
unified affine-predicate framework that extends without modification to all HQ
objectives, including higher-rank QUBO, PUBO, covariance, and correlation
instantiations.

4.2.
Covariance Maximization (Centered Veronese Lift)

Consider two score vectors
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a binary selection vector

x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
of fixed cardinality
\sum_{i}x_{i}=K
. The empirical covariance of

a
and
b
restricted to the active set
S=\{i:x_{i}=1\}
is

(6)

\mathrm{Cov}(x):=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i\in S}a_{i}b_{i}\;-\;\frac{1}{K^{2}}\left(\sum_{i\in S}a_{i}\right)\left(\sum_{i\in S}b_{i}\right).

Define the aggregate quantities

A(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i},\qquad B(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}x_{i},\qquad C(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}b_{i}x_{i}.

Then
\mathrm{Cov}(x)
can be written compactly as

(7)

\mathrm{Cov}(x)=\frac{1}{K}\,C(x)\;-\;\frac{1}{K^{2}}\,A(x)\,B(x).

4.2.1.
Centered Veronese feature lift

Introduce the feature map

(8)

\psi(x):=\big(A(x),\ B(x),\ C(x)\big)\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\qquad\tilde{\psi}(x):=\big(\psi(x),1\big)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}.

Then
\mathrm{Cov}(x)
is homomorphically quadratic in
\tilde{\psi}(x)
:

(9)

\mathrm{Cov}(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\tilde{\psi}(x)^{\top}Q_{\mathrm{cov}}\,\tilde{\psi}(x),

with the rank-
3
symmetric matrix

(10)

Q_{\mathrm{cov}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\tfrac{1}{K^{2}}&0&0\\
-\tfrac{1}{K^{2}}&0&0&0\\
0&0&\tfrac{2}{K}&0\\
0&0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

We verify:

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{\psi}(x)^{\top}Q_{\text{cov}}\tilde{\psi}(x)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(2\cdot\left(-\tfrac{1}{K^{2}}\right)A(x)B(x)+\tfrac{2}{K}C(x)\right)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{K^{2}}A(x)B(x)+\tfrac{1}{K}C(x)=\text{Cov}(x),

confirming that
\text{Cov}\in\mathcal{HQ}^{3}(\psi)
with feature rank
r=3

and homogeneous lift dimension
R=r+1=4
.

4.2.2.
Affine swap predicates

Under the cardinality constraint, the elementary feasible directions are swaps

d=e_{i}-e_{j}
with
x_{j}=1
and
x_{i}=0
. For such a swap,

\Delta_{i\to j}(x)=\mathrm{Cov}(x+e_{i}-e_{j})-\mathrm{Cov}(x).

A direct calculation using
A,B,C
shows

(11)

\Delta_{i\to j}(x)=\Big(a_{i}b_{i}-a_{j}b_{j}\Big)/K\;-\;\frac{1}{K^{2}}\Big(a_{i}-a_{j}\Big)B(x)\;-\;\frac{1}{K^{2}}\Big(b_{i}-b_{j}\Big)A(x).

This is affine in
\psi(x)
and can be written as a difference of
covariance scores

(12)

\Delta_{i\to j}(x)=\kappa_{i}(x)-\kappa_{j}(x),\qquad\kappa_{i}(x):=\frac{1}{K}a_{i}b_{i}-\frac{1}{K^{2}}\big(a_{i}B(x)+b_{i}A(x)\big).

Thus each swap predicate
\Delta_{i\to j}(x)
defines a hyperplane in the
lifted feature space
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

Proposition 0 (Top-
K
structure of covariance support).

For fixed
K
and covariance objective
\mathrm{Cov}(x)
, every EFD-stable solution

x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
with
\sum_{i}x_{i}=K
selects the top-
K
entries according to
the affine scores
\{\kappa_{i}(x)\}
. In general position, the support is uniquely
determined by sorting
\kappa_{i}(x)
and taking the
K
largest.

Proof.

By EFD-stability and cardinality (§3.5),
x
is stable iff for every feasible swap

i\to j
with
j\in S
and
i\notin S
, we have
\Delta_{i\to j}(x)\leq 0
, i.e.

\kappa_{i}(x)-\kappa_{j}(x)\leq 0.

Equivalently,
\kappa_{j}(x)\geq\kappa_{i}(x)
for all
j\in S,i\notin S
.
Combined with
|S|=K
, this enforces that
S
consists of the
K
largest

\kappa_{i}(x)
values, proving the claim.
∎

Remark 0.

The covariance objective thus fits seamlessly into the affine-predicate meta-theorem.
With feature rank
r=3
and cardinality constraint giving equality rank
p=1
,
the effective rank is
R=r+p=4
. By Theorem
16
, covariance
maximization is solvable in time
n^{O(R)}=n^{O(4)}
via chamber enumeration,
with
O(n\log n)
top-
K
reconstruction per chamber (Proposition
1
).

4.3.
Rank-
r
QUBO via Spectral Factorization

Let
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a symmetric matrix with rank
r
,
and fix a spectral decomposition

Q=V\Lambda V^{\top},\qquad\Lambda=\text{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}),\quad V\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}.

We consider the rank-

QUBO problem:

\max_{x\in F}x^{\top}Qx,

where
F\subseteq\{0,1\}^{n}
is either unconstrained (
F=\{0,1\}^{n}
) or
cardinality-constrained (
F=\{x:\sum_{i}x_{i}=K\}
).

4.3.1.
HQ structure

Define the feature map
\psi(x):=V^{\top}x\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
. Then

f(x)=x^{\top}Qx=x^{\top}V\Lambda V^{\top}x=(V^{\top}x)^{\top}\Lambda(V^{\top}x)=\psi(x)^{\top}\Lambda\psi(x).

Since
\Lambda
is diagonal (hence symmetric), we write

f(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\psi(x)^{\top}(2\Lambda)\psi(x),

confirming
f\in\mathcal{HQ}^{r}(\psi)
with feature rank
r
and homogeneous
lift dimension
R=r+1
.

4.3.2.
Elementary feasible directions

Unconstrained case.

For
F=\{0,1\}^{n}
, the constraint rank is
p=0
and elementary feasible
directions are the
2n
bit-flips
\pm e_{i}
. By Theorem
5
,

m=2n
.

Cardinality-constrained case.

For
F=\{x:\sum_{i}x_{i}=K\}
, the constraint rank is
p=1
and
elementary feasible directions are the
K(n-K)\leq n^{2}
swaps

.
By Theorem
5
,
m=O(n^{2})
.

4.3.3.
Complexity bounds

By Theorem
16
, the runtime is

. Concretely:

Unconstrained:

The arrangement has

Cardinality-constrained:

The arrangement has

hyperplanes in
\mathbb{R}^{r+1}
, yielding

O(m^{r+1})=O(n^{2(r+1)})
chambers. By Proposition
15
, each
chamber encodes a top-
K
selection according to affine scores derived from

\Lambda
and
V
, computable in
O(n\log n)
time. Thus

T_{\text{card}}(n,r)=O(n\log n)\cdot O(n^{2(r+1)})=O(n^{2r+3}\log n).

4.3.4.
Novel contribution

To our knowledge, this is the **first algorithm** for cardinality-constrained
rank-
r
QUBO with complexity polynomial in
n
for fixed
r
. Prior work on
constrained QUBO focuses on full-rank instances, for which no polynomial-time
algorithm exists unless P=NP.

Example 0 (Rank-2 QUBO).

For
r=2
, unconstrained QUBO has complexity
O(n^{4})
and cardinality-constrained
has complexity
O(n^{7}\log n)
.

4.4.
General PUBO via CP/Waring Lift

Let
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a pseudo-Boolean polynomial of degree at most
d
:

f(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{d}\langle\mathcal{T}^{(t)},x^{\otimes t}\rangle,

where each
\mathcal{T}^{(t)}
is a symmetric order-
t
tensor.
Assume a symmetric rank factorization (CP/Waring form) for each component:

\mathcal{T}^{(t)}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k_{t}}\alpha_{t\ell}\,(u^{(t,\ell)})^{\otimes t},\qquad\alpha_{t\ell}\in\mathbb{R},\quad u^{(t,\ell)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

4.4.1.
Feature construction

Define the aggregated linear features

s_{t\ell}(x):=\langle u^{(t,\ell)},x\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}^{(t,\ell)}x_{i},\qquad(t=1,\dots,d,\ \ell=1,\dots,k_{t}).

Using the CP factorization, the degree-
t
component evaluates as

\langle\mathcal{T}^{(t)},x^{\otimes t}\rangle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k_{t}}\alpha_{t\ell}\,(s_{t\ell}(x))^{t},

so
f(x)
is a polynomial in the linear features
\{s_{t\ell}\}
with total degree
d
.

4.4.2.
HQ lifting dimension

For any elementary feasible direction
d=\pm e_{i}
, the gain

\Delta_{d}(x)=f(x+d)-f(x)

is a polynomial of degree **at most
d-1
** in
\{s_{t\ell}\}
.

**Why degree
d-1
?** When we add or drop variable
i
, each linear feature
changes by a constant:
s_{t\ell}(x+d)=s_{t\ell}(x)\pm u_{i}^{(t,\ell)}
.
Expanding
(s_{t\ell}(x)+c)^{t}-s_{t\ell}(x)^{t}
via the binomial theorem
shows the highest-degree term cancels, leaving a polynomial of degree
t-1

in
s_{t\ell}(x)
. Summing over all components yields a polynomial of degree
at most
d-1
in
\{s_{t\ell}\}
.

To express all such gains as affine functions, construct a feature map

\psi(x)
containing:

•

All linear features
s_{t\ell}(x)
for
t=1,\ldots,d
and

\ell=1,\ldots,k_{t}
,

•

All monomials in
\{s_{t\ell}\}
of total degree
2,3,\ldots,d-1
.

The dimension
r
of
\psi(x)
is bounded by the number of monomials up to
degree
d-1
in
\sum_{t=1}^{d}k_{t}
variables:

r\leq\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\binom{\left(\sum_{t=1}^{d}k_{t}\right)+j-1}{j}=O\!\left(\left(\sum_{t=1}^{d}k_{t}\right)^{d-1}\right),

which is polynomial in
\sum_{t}k_{t}
for fixed
d
. Thus
f\in\mathcal{HQ}^{r}(\psi)

with
R=r+1=O(1)
when both
d
and
\{k_{t}\}
are constants.

4.4.3.
XP consequence

With constraint rank
p\in\{0,1\}
(unconstrained vs. cardinality),
Theorem
16
yields an exact algorithm in time
n^{O(R+p)}
.
For fixed degree
d
and fixed factor ranks
\{k_{t}\}
, we have
R=O(1)
,
so PUBO is solvable in polynomial time
n^{O(1)}
under these rank conditions.

Example 0 (Cubic PUBO with rank-2 factorization).

For degree
d=3
and symmetric rank
k_{1}=k_{2}=k_{3}=2
, we have

\sum_{t}k_{t}=6
linear features. The feature map
\psi(x)
includes:
- 6 linear features
s_{t\ell}

-
\binom{6+1}{2}=21
degree-2 monomials

Thus
r\leq 27
, giving effective rank
R=28
and runtime

n^{O(28)}
for unconstrained cubic PUBO with this factorization.

4.5.
Fractional Unconstrained/Constrained Binary Optimization (FUBO)

We consider
fractional
objectives on
\{0,1\}^{n}
of the form

\max_{x\in F}\ \frac{N(x)}{D(x)}\qquad\text{with}\qquad F\subseteq\{0,1\}^{n},

where
D(x)>0
on
F
and both
N,D
are homomorphically quadratic (HQ) with
possibly different feature maps:

N\in\mathcal{HQ}^{r_{N}}(\psi_{N}),\qquad D\in\mathcal{HQ}^{r_{D}}(\psi_{D}).

This strictly subsumes the linear-fractional and QUBO-fractional cases.
2
2

2

Linear-fractional
((a^{\top}x+\alpha)/(b^{\top}x+\beta))
is the special case

r_{N}=r_{D}=1
with linear features; quadratic-fractional allows both
N
and
D

to be QUBOs of ranks
r_{N},r_{D}
.

4.5.1.
Parametric reduction (Dinkelbach form)

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Define the parametric HQ objective

G_{\lambda}(x):=N(x)-\lambda D(x).

Since
\mathcal{HQ}
is closed under affine combinations for a fixed feature lift,
each
G_{\lambda}
is HQ with respect to the
concatenated
feature

\Psi(x):=(\psi_{N}(x),\psi_{D}(x),1,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}^{R},\qquad R:=r_{N}+r_{D}+2.

Indeed, for any elementary feasible direction
d
, the gain satisfies

\displaystyle\Delta_{d}G_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{d}N(x)-\lambda\Delta_{d}D(x)

\displaystyle=\langle w^{N}_{d},\psi_{N}(x)\rangle+c^{N}_{d}-\lambda(\langle w^{D}_{d},\psi_{D}(x)\rangle+c^{D}_{d})

\displaystyle=\langle\underbrace{(w^{N}_{d},-w^{D}_{d},c^{N}_{d},-c^{D}_{d})}_{=:\widehat{w}_{d}},\underbrace{(\psi_{N}(x),\psi_{D}(x),1,\lambda)}_{=\Psi(x)}\rangle,

hence each
\Delta_{d}G_{\lambda}
is
affine
in
\Psi(x)
and
linear

in the lifted feature
(\Psi(x),1)
.

By the classical Dinkelbach equivalence, an optimal ratio
x^{\star}
with value

\lambda^{\star}:=N(x^{\star})/D(x^{\star})
satisfies

\max_{x\in F}G_{\lambda^{\star}}(x)=0\quad\text{and is attained at }x^{\star}.

Thus, optimizing a ratio reduces to solving
G_{\lambda}
to optimality at the
unknown
\lambda^{\star}
.

Two solution routes.

Our framework supports two principled approaches:

(1)

Outer parametric search (Dinkelbach).

Run a Dinkelbach loop: at iteration
t
, solve
\max_{x\in F}G_{\lambda_{t}}(x)

exactly using our arrangement/zonotope method (§3), obtain
x_{t+1}
, and update

\lambda_{t+1}:=N(x_{t+1})/D(x_{t+1})
.
Since
F
is finite and
D>0
, the sequence
\{\lambda_{t}\}
is monotone and
terminates in finitely many steps at
\lambda^{\star}
(no approximation needed).
Each inner solve is an HQ instance with lift dimension
R=r_{N}+r_{D}+2
and therefore
runs in XP time
n^{O(R+p)}
by Theorem
16
.

(2)

One-shot joint arrangement in
(x,\lambda)
.

Equivalently, treat
\lambda
as an extra (free) coordinate in the feature lift

\Psi(x)
above. Each EFD induces a
single
hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{R}

with normal
\widehat{w}_{d}
; chambers of the resulting arrangement partition
the
(\psi_{N},\psi_{D},1,\lambda)
-space into regions with constant EFD gain signs.
Enumerating those chambers (or, via zonotope duality, their vertices) yields
finitely many candidate supports together with the
\lambda
-intervals on which
they are stable; on each such cell the objective value
\frac{N(x)}{D(x)}
is
constant in
x
and linear in
\lambda
, so the optimal ratio over that cell is
attained at the unique consistent
\lambda
satisfying
\max_{x}G_{\lambda}(x)=0
.
In practice we prefer the parametric route (i) for simplicity.

4.5.2.
EFD gains and reconstruction

All EFD gains for
G_{\lambda}
remain
affine
in the lift
\Psi(x)
,
so §3.3–§3.5 apply verbatim. In particular:

•

Unconstrained
F=\{0,1\}^{n}
:
EFDs are
\pm e_{i}
, hence chamber
patterns set bits directly from the signs of the add/drop gains

\Delta_{\pm i}G_{\lambda}(x)
.

•

Cardinality constraint
\sum_{i}x_{i}=K
:
EFDs are swaps
i\to j
.
For any
\lambda
, the swap gain decomposes as

\Delta_{i\to j}G_{\lambda}(x)=(\kappa_{i}^{N}-\lambda\kappa_{i}^{D})-(\kappa_{j}^{N}-\lambda\kappa_{j}^{D}),

for affine scores
\kappa^{N},\kappa^{D}
read from §3.1 for
N
and
D
respectively.
Thus stability in a chamber at fixed
\lambda
is equivalent to picking the
top-
K
indices by the
\lambda
-tilted score

\kappa_{i}(\lambda):=\kappa_{i}^{N}-\lambda\kappa_{i}^{D}
(Prop.
15
),
and reconstruction costs
O(n\log n)
.

4.5.3.
XP bound for FUBO

Let
p
be the equality-constraint rank as in §3.2, and put

R_{\mathrm{FUBO}}:=r_{N}+r_{D}+2.

Theorem 4 (XP complexity for FUBO).

Assume
D(x)>0
on
F
, with
N\in\mathcal{HQ}^{r_{N}}(\psi_{N})
and

D\in\mathcal{HQ}^{r_{D}}(\psi_{D})
. Let
R_{\mathrm{FUBO}}:=r_{N}+r_{D}+2

and
p
be the constraint rank.

Then a global maximizer of
\frac{N(x)}{D(x)}
over
F
can be found via
Dinkelbach iteration, where each subproblem
\max_{x}G_{\lambda}(x)
is solved
in time
n^{O(R_{\mathrm{FUBO}}+p)}
by Theorem
16
. The number
of iterations is finite and problem-dependent.

In the cardinality case, reconstruction per chamber costs
O(n\log n)
via
top-

Remark 0 (Linear-fractional special case).

For linear-fractional objectives
(a^{\top}x+\alpha)/(b^{\top}x+\beta)

4.6.
Centered Pearson Correlation and
R^{2}
via FUBO

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be two score vectors and let
x\in\{0,1\}^{n}

select a subset
S=\{i:x_{i}=1\}
of fixed cardinality
\sum_{i}x_{i}=K
.
Define the aggregated sums

A(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i},\quad B(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}x_{i},\quad C(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}b_{i}x_{i},

and the quadratic aggregates

U(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}x_{i},\qquad V(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}^{2}x_{i}.

4.6.1.
Pearson correlation as FUBO

The centered (empirical) Pearson correlation on the subset
S
is

\rho(x)=\frac{\text{Cov}(x)}{\sqrt{\text{Var}_{a}(x)\text{Var}_{b}(x)}},

where the centered covariance and variances are

\displaystyle\text{Cov}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{KC(x)-A(x)B(x)}{K^{2}},

\displaystyle\text{Var}_{a}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{KU(x)-A(x)^{2}}{K^{2}},

\displaystyle\text{Var}_{b}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{KV(x)-B(x)^{2}}{K^{2}}.

Thus the squared correlation is

(13)

R^{2}(x)=\rho(x)^{2}=\frac{(KC(x)-A(x)B(x))^{2}}{(KU(x)-A(x)^{2})(KV(x)-B(x)^{2})}=\frac{N(x)}{D(x)},

where
N(x):=(KC-AB)^{2}
and
D(x):=(KU-A^{2})(KV-B^{2})
.

4.6.2.
HQ structure via centered Veronese lift

Introduce the
centered Veronese
feature map

\psi_{\text{cor}}(x):=(A(x),B(x),C(x),U(x),V(x),A(x)B(x),A(x)^{2},B(x)^{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{8}.

Both
N(x)
and
D(x)
are quadratic forms in
\tilde{\psi}_{\text{cor}}(x)=(\psi_{\text{cor}}(x),1)
:

\displaystyle N(x)

\displaystyle=K^{2}C^{2}-2K\cdot C\cdot(AB)+(AB)^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{\psi}_{\text{cor}}(x)^{\top}Q_{N}\tilde{\psi}_{\text{cor}}(x),

\displaystyle D(x)

\displaystyle=K^{2}UV-KUB^{2}-KVA^{2}+A^{2}B^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{\psi}_{\text{cor}}(x)^{\top}Q_{D}\tilde{\psi}_{\text{cor}}(x),

for suitable symmetric matrices
Q_{N},Q_{D}\succeq 0
. Thus

N\in\mathcal{HQ}^{r_{N}}(\psi_{\text{cor}})
and

D\in\mathcal{HQ}^{r_{D}}(\psi_{\text{cor}})
with
r_{N},r_{D}\leq 8
,
making
R^{2}(x)=N(x)/D(x)
a fractional HQ objective (FUBO, §4.5).

4.6.3.
Optimization and complexity

Maximizing
|\rho(x)|
is equivalent to maximizing
R^{2}(x)
. By Theorem
4

(§4.5), this FUBO instance can be solved via Dinkelbach iteration, where each
subproblem
\max_{x}(N(x)-\lambda D(x))
is solved in time
n^{O(R_{\text{FUBO}}+p)}

with
R_{\text{FUBO}}=r_{N}+r_{D}+2\leq 18
and
p=1
(cardinality constraint).

Alternatively, enumerate all
O(m^{R})=O(n^{2\cdot 9})=O(n^{18})
chambers
of the arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{9}
(where
m=O(n^{2})
swaps), reconstruct
the top-
K
subset per chamber in
O(n\log n)
time (Proposition
15
),
and evaluate
R^{2}(x)
directly.

Remark 0 (Compressed features).

The feature dimension
r=8
can be reduced by exploiting algebraic dependencies.
For instance, if only correlation magnitude matters, one can work with

\psi^{\prime}=(A,B,C,U,V)
and compute
R^{2}
from these five aggregates,
reducing

Remark 0 (Signed correlation).

To maximize the signed correlation
\rho(x)
(not
|\rho(x)|
), note that

\rho(x)=(KC-AB)/\sqrt{D(x)}
. Since
D(x)>0
on non-degenerate slices,
maximizing
\rho
reduces to maximizing the numerator
KC-AB
subject to

\rho(x)\geq 0
, or equivalently, maximize
R^{2}
and take the sign from

\text{sgn}(KC-AB)
at the optimizer.

5.
Conclusion and Outlook

We introduced the
affine-predicate meta-theorem
for homomorphically quadratic (HQ) pseudo-Boolean optimization. The key insight: whenever all elementary feasible direction (EFD) gains admit an affine lift, the landscape of local and global optima is governed by a finite hyperplane arrangement in a fixed-dimensional feature space. Via zonotope
duality, the chambers of this arrangement correspond to vertices of a convex polytope, which can be enumerated in XP time
n^{O(r+p)}
with respect to the effective HQ rank
r
and constraint rank
p
. A key technical component is that stable supports within each chamber can be reconstructed
combinatorially
(e.g., by top-
K
sorting or
blocking clauses), avoiding numeric inversion or continuous relaxation.

This yields a unifying geometric engine that subsumes several classical discrete optimization problems and provides deterministic, constructive algorithms for them, including product of linears, rank-
r
QUBO, rank-bounded PUBO, covariance selection, and even fractional (FUBO) and correlation maximization objectives. In each case, the objective induces a compact HQ feature lift of constant dimension, and exact global
optimization follows by chamber enumeration in the corresponding gain arrangement. The resulting algorithms are
constructive
,
combinatorial
, and
output-sensitive
in the geometry of the lifted objective.

Several extensions appear promising. While our XP bounds are tight for general hyperplane arrangements, practical instances often exhibit structure (e.g., margin separation, sparse active EFDs, low effective arrangement depth) that may enable faster traversal. This suggests developing FPT refinements parameterized by geometric properties (e.g., maximum chamber diameter, separation margin) or pruning schemes that avoid exploring chambers with provably suboptimal objective values. The affine-predicate view naturally interacts with continuous relaxations: each hyperplane predicate corresponds to a linear cut, hinting at hybrid exact/relaxation methods that maintain arrangement consistency while leveraging convex optimization. Machine learning could guide chamber exploration: train classifiers to predict which chambers are likely optimal based on features of the arrangement (e.g., hyperplane normals, chamber volume), analogous to learned branching rules in branch-and-bound. This may be especially effective for applications with problem families (e.g., portfolio selection, sparse regression) where training data is available. For which classes of pseudo-Boolean functions do the gain vectors
\tilde{w}_{d}
form zonotopes of bounded face complexity, and how does this relate to algebraic properties of
f
(e.g., tensor rank, spectral decay)? Understanding these connections could reveal new tractable subclasses.

The affine-predicate paradigm provides a unifying language for exploring these connections between combinatorial optimization, algebraic factorization, and hyperplane arrangement theory—opening new avenues at the intersection of discrete optimization and discrete geometry.

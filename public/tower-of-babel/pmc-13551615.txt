Long intramural left coronary artery confined to the left sinus of Valsalva with diastolic deformation: a case report
Authors: Takuya Kawabata, Junya Nabeshima, Taku Ishii, Taishi Yonetsu, Tomoyuki Fujita, Andriana Anagnostopoulou, Panagiotis Chronopoulos, Constantin Kühl, Amardeep Ghosh Dastidar, Deepti Ranganathan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAnomalous aortic origin of a coronary artery (AAOCA) is a rare congenital anomaly. The most frequently reported and surgically treated form involves origin of a coronary artery from the opposite sinus that follows an interarterial course, often termed ‘malignant’. In contrast, AAOCA arising from the appropriate sinus without an interarterial course is uncommon, and its clinical significance and optimal management remain poorly defined.Case summaryA 12-year-old girl with exertional syncope during physical activity was referred to our hospital. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography revealed an anomalous origin of the left coronary artery from the left sinus of Valsalva with an acute-angle take-off and an intramural segment. Intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) confirmed an intramural course within the aortic wall and demonstrated luminal deformation, with the lumen appearing more elliptical at end-diastole. Because of exertional syncope and a substantial intramural segment, surgical unroofing was performed without postoperative aortic valve dysfunction.ConclusionThis report describes a rare variant of AAOCA characterized by a long intramural course confined to the left sinus of Valsalva without an interarterial course. Despite the absence of a typical malignant course, the patient had symptoms suggestive of myocardial ischaemia. Multimodality imaging played a key role in diagnosis and surgical planning, while IVUS provided complementary structural and dynamic information by demonstrating luminal changes. Surgical unroofing may therefore be considered for symptomatic paediatric patients with a substantial intramural segment even without an interarterial course.Keywords: Anomalous aortic origin of a coronary artery, Intramural coronary artery, Interarterial course, Intravascular ultrasound, Surgical unroofing, Case report

Introduction

Although rare, anomalous aortic origin of a coronary artery (AAOCA) is the second most common cause of sudden cardiac death in young competitive athletes after hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy (HOCM).1 Certain anatomical variants are considered malignant and may indicate a need for surgical repair in selected patients.2 Most surgically treated AAOCA originates from the opposite sinus with an interarterial course,3,4 although atypical variants have also been reported.5 In contrast, AAOCA arising from the appropriate sinus or lacking an interarterial course is uncommon.6 Accordingly, surgical experience is limited.7 To our knowledge, this is the first surgical report of a paediatric AAOCA with a long intramural left coronary artery confined to the left sinus of Valsalva without an interarterial course.

Case presentation

A 12-year-old girl (height 142 cm, weight 33.8 kg) with no significant medical history developed exertional syncope while running during a school activity. Cardiopulmonary resuscitation was promptly initiated by a teacher, and an automated external defibrillator advised no shock. She regained consciousness before arrival at a local hospital and was subsequently transferred to our institution.

A physical examination on admission revealed stable vital signs, including a heart rate of 70 bpm and blood pressure of 114/71 mmHg, without cardiac murmur. Transthoracic echocardiography showed normal left ventricular function without regional wall motion abnormalities, valvular disease, HOCM, or pulmonary hypertension. Coronary artery visualization was insufficient. Exercise stress electrocardiography showed mild ST-segment depression in the left precordial leads, suggestive of possible myocardial ischaemia. Exercise stress myocardial perfusion scintigraphy using Tc-99m sestamibi showed no definitive evidence of inducible myocardial ischaemia.

Contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) revealed an anomalous aortic origin of the left coronary artery from the left sinus of Valsalva, with an acute-angle take-off and an elliptically shaped proximal lumen within the aortic wall, consistent with an 8.5-mm intramural course (Figure 1A and B). Three-dimensional reconstruction showed the coronary ostium adjacent to the commissure between the left and noncoronary cusps, slightly above the commissural level (Figure 1C).

Intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) using a 60-MHz OPTICROSS™ HD catheter (Boston Scientific, Tokyo, Japan) confirmed the presence of an intramural coronary course by visualizing the coronary artery within the aortic wall and clearly identifying the aortic medial layer along the aortic side of the lumen (Figure 2). Using IVUS, the intramural segment measured 8.0 mm. IVUS also enabled dynamic assessment of lumen geometry. At rest, the intramural segment exhibited phase-dependent morphological changes, appearing more elliptical at end-diastole (height-to-width ratio: 1.7) and more circular during systole (height-to-width ratio: 1.3) (Figure 2A and B). Dobutamine infusion (up to 10 μg/kg/min) accentuated circularization of the lumen, though no objective evidence of ischaemia was observed (Figure 2C and D). Pressure wire assessment, including fractional flow reserve (FFR), was not performed.

In view of the history of exertional syncope and high-risk anatomical findings, including a substantial intramural coronary segment, together with the IVUS finding of dynamic luminal deformation, elective surgical intervention was planned. Management options were reviewed by a multidisciplinary team, and both surgical and conservative management options were discussed with the patient and her family. In consideration of the patient’s active lifestyle as well as potential long-term risk, the family elected surgical treatment.

Surgical repair was performed via a median sternotomy under cardiopulmonary bypass. After an oblique aortotomy, the coronary ostium was identified within the left sinus of Valsalva, adjacent to the left–noncoronary commissure and slightly above the commissural level (Figure 3A). The ostium exhibited a fish-mouth configuration. Standard surgical unroofing was performed by longitudinal resection of the common wall between the aortic lumen and intramural segment, followed by tacking of the unroofed edges to the aortic wall to prevent flap formation. The unroofed segment measured approximately 7 mm (Figure 3B).

The postoperative course was uneventful. Follow-up echocardiography showed no aortic regurgitation, and CT confirmed adequate unroofing. The patient was discharged in a stable condition without complications. Exercise stress electrocardiography performed 3 months after surgery showed no significant ST-segment changes.

Discussion

The present case highlights a rare anatomical variant of AAOCA with a long intramural course confined to the left sinus of Valsalva without an interarterial course. In patients with this atypical coronary anatomy, both risk stratification and understanding of the underlying ischaemic mechanism remain challenging.

Multimodality imaging was essential8 for establishing the diagnosis and guiding surgical decision-making in the present case. Contrast-enhanced CT findings demonstrated an acute-angle take-off and an elliptically proximal lumen within the aortic wall, suggestive of an intramural course.9 Visualization of the coronary artery within the aortic media was possible with IVUS, which enabled precise characterization of segment length and geometry.10 These findings correlated with intraoperative findings.

IVUS also provided the dynamic behaviour of the intramural segment. At rest, the lumen appeared more elliptical at end-diastole and more circular during systole. Notably, this diastolic-predominant ellipticity differs from prior IVUS observations in interarterial AAOCA cases, in which systolic flattening of the intramural segment has been reported.10,11 The absence of an interarterial course and involvement of the left coronary, rather than the right coronary, suggest that the mechanism of coronary compression may differ from the systolic compression pattern observed in interarterial AAOCA cases. Although the underlying mechanism remains uncertain, the present findings suggest the possibility of diastolic lateral compression of the intramural segment associated with aortic root expansion and restricted coronary inflow caused by the slit-like ostium and proximal narrowing. This proposed mechanism remains hypothetical and cannot be confirmed from the present observations. Such anatomical and functional differences may also influence the mechanisms of ischaemia across AAOCA phenotypes.

Although sufficient high-dose pharmacological stress could not be achieved during IVUS assessment, transient awakening of the patient during dobutamine infusion was associated with marked hypertension and sympathetic stimulation, accompanied by apparent accentuation of luminal compression. However, these observations were not quantitatively documented, and their significance remains uncertain. These findings may further support the dynamic nature of intramural coronary deformation in this anatomical variant.

Stress electrocardiography has limited sensitivity and specificity for detection of myocardial ischaemia in patients with AAOCA,12 and the electrocardiographic findings observed in the present case were insufficient to establish definitive ischaemia. Although myocardial perfusion scintigraphy was negative, additional functional assessment using stress cardiac magnetic resonance imaging, positron emission tomography, or invasive physiological testing such as FFR may have provided further evidence of ischaemia.13 Nevertheless, given the patient’s exertional syncope and findings indicating the presence of high-risk anatomical features, including an acute take-off angle and long intramural segment (Table 1), together with the IVUS finding of dynamic luminal deformation as a potentially relevant anatomical-functional finding, the decision to proceed with surgical intervention was considered appropriate.

This approach was consistent with the 2020 European Society of Cardiology guidelines, which recommend surgical intervention (Class I) in patients with AAOCA who show typical symptoms and evidence of stress-induced myocardial ischaemia or high-risk anatomical features.2 Accordingly, surgical unroofing may be considered an appropriate treatment option for symptomatic paediatric patients with a long intramural coronary segment, even in the absence of an interarterial course.

A limitation of this report is the relatively short postoperative follow-up period. Longer-term follow-up will be necessary to evaluate the durability of surgical repair and long-term clinical outcomes.

Conclusion

A long intramural segment of the left coronary artery confined to the left sinus of Valsalva may have clinical significance despite the absence of an interarterial course. IVUS provided complementary structural and dynamic information beyond static imaging, revealing phase-dependent luminal changes distinct from those reported in typical interarterial AAOCA. Careful evaluation of intramural morphology and its dynamic characteristics may provide additional anatomical-functional information that could assist clinical decision-making in rare AAOCA variants.

Patient perspective

Prior to this event, the patient had been a healthy and active middle school student who enjoyed playing basketball, and sports played an important role in her family life. The sudden syncopal episode during club practice was unexpected and caused considerable concern for both the patient and her parents, with the subsequent medical evaluations and transfer to a tertiary centre adding to their anxiety. After detailed discussions with the medical team regarding management options, including surgical correction or implantable cardioverter-defibrillator placement with long-term restrictions on physical activity, the family chose surgical treatment. In making this decision, the patient’s long-term future, including the possibility of pregnancy and childbirth, was considered. Following successful surgery, the patient and her parents expressed relief. They felt reassured by the outcome and have gradually accepted the plan for a cautious return to physical activity under close medical supervision.

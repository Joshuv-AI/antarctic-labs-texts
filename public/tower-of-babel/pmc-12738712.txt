Microglial RUNX1/RBM47 ablation inhibits neuronal ferroptosis via regulating the cGAS-STING-MEF2C pathway in mice with postoperative cognitive dysfunction
Authors: Shanshan Wang, Zankai Wu, Wenqin Song, Hui Zhang, Xueshan Bu, Wenwei Gao, Kefan Wu, Zhongyuan Xia, Bo Zhao, Wei Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractActivation of the cGAS-STING pathway has been proposed as a potential pathogenic mechanism underlying postoperative cognitive dysfuncton (POCD). Analysis of public transcriptomic data from the hippocampus of male mice suggests upregulation of Rbm47 in an experimental POCD model. In vitro, knockdown (KD) of Rbm47 in BV2 cells reduces levels of cGAS, STING, TNF-α, IL-6, and IFN-β under isoflurane and lipopolysaccharide (Iso + LPS) conditions. HT22 cells exposed to culture medium from Rbm47-KD BV2 cells treated with Iso + LPS show increased levels of MEF2C, GPX4, GSH, and SOD, and reduced levels of MDA and iron. Microglial Rbm47 overexpression amplifies cGAS-STING signaling and aggravates neuronal ferroptosis, which is reversed by the cGAS inhibitor RU.521. Rbm47 KD combined with RU.521 treatment synergistically ameliorates POCD in male mice. Mechanistically, the RRM2 domain of RBM47 interacts with the 3’UTR of cGAS mRNA, enhancing its stability. RUNX1 directly binds to the Rbm47 promoter and facilitates its transcription. RUNX1 overexpression activates the RBM47-cGAS-STING axis and exacerbates neuronal ferroptosis. Conversely, RUNX1 ablation ameliorates POCD, dampens the cGAS-STING signaling, and inhibits ferroptosis, which is abolished by RBM47 depletion. Our findings suggest that repression of RUNX1/RBM47 may represent a promising therapeutic strategy for POCD.Subject terms: Cognitive neuroscience, Neurology

Introduction

Postoperative cognitive dysfunction (POCD) refers to an array of impairments in memory, concentration, information processing, and psychomotor activity, predominantly impacting senior surgical patients1. It imposes a heavy burden on society and the economy, characterized by loss of independence, compromised quality of life, surging healthcare costs, a greater likelihood of developing Alzheimer’s disease (AD), and a heightened risk of mortality2. Neuroinflammation is widely recognized as a central enabler of POCD3. Despite numerous research efforts for anti-inflammatory interventions to combat anesthesia/surgery-induced cognitive decline in recent years, the pathogenic mechanisms underlying POCD remain incompletely expounded.

The cyclic GMP-AMP synthase (cGAS)-stimulator of interferon genes (STING) signaling has emerged as a pivotal element of innate immunity in metazoans to surveil intracellular cytosolic double-stranded DNA (dsDNA). Endogenous cytoplasmic dsDNA, an archetypal damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP), accumulates in the brain of several neurological disorders, such as cerebral venous sinus thrombosis4, AD5, cerebral ischemia-reperfusion injury6, and POCD7. Once bound by the phosphodiester backbone of dsDNA in a sequence-independent fashion, cGAS catalyzes the synthesis of 2′,3′-cyclic GMP-AMP (cGAMP). The distinctive mixed-linkage second messenger rapidly diffuses throughout the cell and interacts with the dimeric transmembrane protein STING that resides on the endoplasmic reticulum (ER)8. STING undergoes conformational rearrangements, oligomerization, and translocation to the Golgi compartment. STING oligomers recruit TANK-binding kinase 1 (TBK1) and/or IκB kinase-ε (IKKε) to phosphorylate interferon (IFN) regulatory factor 3 (IRF3) and nuclear factor kappa-B (NF-κB), culminating in transcription of tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin 6 (IL-6), and type I IFNs9. Pharmacological inhibition of the cGAS-STING pathway has been documented to effectively mitigate central nervous system (CNS) injuries, such as ischemic stroke10, AD5, and POCD7,11. Microglial cGAS-STING-IFN activation inhibits the neuronal myocyte enhancer factor 2 C (MEF2C) transcriptional network linked to cognitive resilience in AD12. Our recent research has corroborated that MEF2C overexpression alleviates anesthesia/surgery-elicited memory deterioration and ferroptosis by transcriptionally activating glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4)13.

Transcriptomic signatures from the hippocampus of anesthesia/surgery-treated mice have identified RNA-binding motif protein 47 (RBM47) as a potential contributor to POCD14. However, its upstream and downstream mechanisms underlying POCD are unclear. RBM47 encompasses three conserved RNA recognition motif (RRM) domains that are required for alternative splicing and RNA stabilization15–17. Emerging evidence suggests that RBM47 modulates innate immune responses through several distinct pathways. RBM47 potentiates host antiviral activity by binding to the 3’ untranslated region (UTR) of interferon alpha/beta receptor 1 (IFNAR1) mRNA and subsequently amplifying IFN downstream signaling16. It also stabilizes the mRNA of NF-κB-related inflammatory genes and triggers the polarization and migration of M2 macrophages18. Notably, zebrafish RBM47 disrupts the interactional ability of MAVS with STING to block IFN production19, indicating a probable involvement of RBM47 in the cGAS-STING pathway.

The study aimed to elucidate the regulatory relationship between RBM47 and cGAS-STING-MEF2C signaling in the pathogenesis of POCD. Furthermore, we sought to investigate the driving mechanism by which anesthesia and surgery induce RBM47.

Results

RBM47 was upregulated in the hippocampus of mice after anesthesia/surgery exposure

Our prior research has revealed increased levels of Rbm47 mRNA in the hippocampus of POCD mice14, immunoblot assays also showed that isoflurane and laparotomy markedly elevated the hippocampal protein abundance of RBM47 (Fig. 1A). Online single-cell RNA-sequencing (scRNA-seq) data20 indicate that RBM47 is predominantly expressed in microglia of both humans and mice (Fig. 1B). To further elucidate the molecular mechanisms responsible for Rbm47 mRNA upregulation in POCD, we integrated predictions of Rbm47 promoter-binding transcription factors with the TRRUST database21 and differentially expressed genes (DEGs) from mice subjected to either control conditions or anesthesia/surgery22. Ten candidate transcription factors (MAZ, ETV5, TCF7L2, GATA3, OTX2, RUNX1, SOX10, STAT1, SOX14, and FOXP2) were selected by intersection analysis (Fig. 1C). ETV5 and RUNX1 were identified as the potential transcription factors responsible for Rbm47 due to insufficient distribution of other transcription factors in microglia (Supplementary Fig. S1). The Gene Expression Profile Interactive Analysis (GEPIA; http://gepia.cancer-pku.cn) revealed a positive correlation between hippocampal RBM47 and ETV5 (Pearson’s correlation coefficient = 0.36) as well as RUNX1 (Pearson’s correlation coefficient = 0.88) (Fig. 1D, E), suggesting RUNX1 as the most likely transcription factor of Rbm47. Subsequent Western blot analysis showed that anesthesia and surgery significantly elevated RUNX1 protein expression in the hippocampus of mice (Fig. 1F). A dual-luciferase reporter assay indicated that RUNX1 overexpression robustly enhanced Rbm47 promoter activity. The region spanning from 1 to 1400 bp upstream of the Rbm47 gene transcription initiation site abrogated RUNX1-dependent promoter activation compared to the full-length Rbm47 promoter. In contrast, His-RUNX1 failed to alter the luciferase activity upon the introduction of a luciferase reporter plasmid containing only the sequence from −1401 bp to −2100 bp of the Rbm47 promoter (Fig. 1G). The above results indicate that the sequence spanning −1401 bp to −2100 bp contains the most significant RUNX1-responsive elements. ChIP assay further verified the direct interaction between RUNX1 and the Rbm47 promoter, as evidenced by the occupancy of RUNX1 on the promoter region of Rbm47 (Fig. 1H). These findings indicate that anesthesia and surgery significantly enhance hippocampal levels of RBM47, which is transcriptionally activated by RUNX1.

Microglial Rbm47 loss‑of‑function restrained the cGAS-STING signaling and neuronal ferroptosis

RBM47 has been documented to potentiate host antiviral responses by stabilizing IFNAR1 mRNA16. The cGAS-STING pathway, a key innate immune defense mechanism culminating in the release of type I IFNs, TNF-α, and IL-6, has been reported to hamper neuronal MEF2C-mediated cognitive resilience in AD12. MEF2C has also been reported to attenuate neuronal ferroptosis in POCD mice by transcriptionally activating GPX413. Accordingly, we investigated whether RBM47 affects the cGAS-STING-MEF2C-GPX4 signaling axis in vitro by exposing HT22 neurons to CM from BV2 microglia (Fig. 2A). Immunoblot and RT-qPCR results indicated that siRbm47#2 was the most potent silencing fragment (Supplementary Fig. 2A–C) and was then transfected into BV2 cells. Increased expression of cGAS, STING, and RBM47 was detected in BV2 cells treated with Iso + LPS, which was rescued by Rbm47 depletion (Fig. 2B, Supplementary Fig. 2D–F). HT22 cells exposed to the CM from Iso + LPS-primed BV2 cells exhibited diminished levels of MEF2C and GPX4. However, Rbm47 silencing notably augmented the protein content of neuronal MEF2C and GPX4 in response to Iso + LPS stimulation (Fig. 2C, Supplementary Fig. 2G, H). ELISA analysis revealed heightened levels of TNF-α, IL-6, and IFN-β in the supernatant of BV2 microglia enriched with Iso + LPS, whereas Rbm47 knockdown inhibited the secretion of these inflammatory cytokines (Fig. 2D–F). The CM from BV2 microglia treated with Iso + LPS significantly decreased the viability of HT22 neurons, whereas ferrostatin-1 or liproxstatin-1 substantially restored neuronal viability (Fig. 2G). Notably, Rbm47 silencing also enhanced HT22 neuronal viability under Iso + LPS challenge (Fig. 2H). Iron imbalance, lipid peroxidation, and impaired glutathione antioxidant defense are key hallmarks of ferroptosis23. C11-BODIPY581/591 staining results showed an accumulation of lipid ROS in HT22 cells when grown in the CM from Iso + LPS-stimulated BV2 cells, which was effectively mitigated by Rbm47 knockdown (Fig. 2I, J). The lipid peroxide end-product MDA density was significantly increased in HT22 cells exposed to BV2 CM under Iso + LPS conditions, which was restored by Rbm47 silencing (Fig. 2K). The BV2 CM markedly reduced neuronal GSH levels and the antioxidant activity of SOD upon Iso + LPS challenge, while siRbm47 manifested stronger antioxidant capacity (Fig. 2L, M). Additionally, iron overload was noted in HT22 cells exposed to CM from Iso + LPS-intoxicated BV2 cells, which was ameliorated by Rbm47 deficiency (Fig. 2N). These results highlight that microglial RBM47 deficiency inhibits cGAS-STING pathway activation and consequently alleviates neuronal ferroptosis.

Microglial Rbm47 gain‑of‑function facilitated neuronal ferroptosis by activating the cGAS-STING pathway

BV2 cells were treated with the cGAS inhibitor RU.521 in the presence or absence of Flag-tagged pcDNA3.1- Rbm47 to ascertain whether cGAS is necessary for neuronal ferroptosis governed by RBM47 (Fig. 3A). Enhanced levels of cGAS and STING were observed in Rbm47-OE BV2 microglia exposed to Iso + LPS. Conversely, RU.521 suppressed microglial cGAS-STING pathway activation. Compared to the RBM47 + Iso + LPS group, concomitant regimens of Flag-Rbm47 and RU.521 significantly diminished the protein abundance of cGAS and STING (Fig. 3B, and Supplementary Fig. 3A–C). Further restraint of MEF2C and GPX4 was found in HT22 cells exposed to the CM from BV2 cells overexpressing Rbm47 following Iso + LPS stimulation. In contrast, HT22 cells grown in the CM from RU.521-treated BV2 cells exhibited a robust elevation of MEF2C and GPX4 content upon Iso + LPS challenge. Concurrent administration of Rbm47 overexpression and RU.521 in BV2 microglia amplified the neuronal MEF2C/GPX4 signaling axis compared to treatment with pcDNA3.1- Rbm47 alone in Iso + LPS scenarios (Fig. 3C, and Supplementary Fig. 3D, E). The Flag-tagged pcDNA3.1-Rbm47 plasmid further promoted the release of TNF-α, IL-6, and IFN-β from BV2 cells in response to Iso + LPS intoxication, whereas RU.521 substantially attenuated the secretion of these inflammatory cytokines. Reduced production of NF-κB-pertinent pro-inflammatory factors and type I interferon was seen in the supernatant of BV2 cells treated with pcDNA3.1-Rbm47 and RU.521 compared to those in the Flag-Rbm47 group under Iso + LPS conditions (Fig. 3D–F). The CCK-8 assay demonstrated a further reduced viability of HT22 cells when treated with CM from RBM47-OE microglia under Iso + LPS conditions. However, preemptive RU.521 administration boosted HT22 neuronal viability and rescued impaired cell viability induced by Flag-Rbm47 (Fig. 3G). Microglial Rbm47 gain-of-function further triggered neuronal lipid ROS accumulation, while RU.521 markedly hindered lipid peroxidation in HT22 cells. The average fluorescence intensity of BODIPY was significantly reduced in HT22 cells cultured in the CM from Iso + LPS-stimulated BV2 cells after Rbm47 overexpression and RU.521 treatment compared to Flag-Rbm47 + Iso + LPS group (Fig. 3H, I). We found that microglial Rbm47 upregulation further augmented MDA concentration in HT22 cells, whereas pretreatment with RU.521 inhibited its formation and blocked excessive lipid peroxidation induced by Rbm47 overexpression (Fig. 3J). Further diminution of GSH and SOD levels was observed in HT22 neurons treated with CM from Rbm47-OE BV2 microglia following Iso + LPS treatment. Nonetheless, RU.521 enhanced GSH and SOD production in response to Iso + LPS challenge and neutralized the pro-oxidant effects exerted by RBM47 (Fig. 3K, L). Additionally, microglial Rbm47 gain‑of‑function further aggravated neuronal iron overload when confronted with Iso + LPS, whereas RU.521 remarkably reduced iron content in HT22 cells and offset the contributory effects of RBM47 on iron accumulation (Fig. 3M). Overall, our data illustrate that microglial Rbm47 overexpression promotes neuronal ferroptosis by activating the cGAS-STING signaling under Iso + LPS conditions.

RBM47 directly bound to cGAS mRNA

As an RNA-binding protein, RBM47 specifically binds to AU-rich elements (AREs) of RNAs, and participates in stabilization and alternative splicing of pre-mRNAs17. To identify the target RNAs of RBM47, we integrated the prediction of RBM47-bound RNAs with the cGAS-STING-pertinent genes24. Our findings suggested that cGAS mRNA is a potential target of RBM47 (Fig. 4A). GEPIA showed a strong positive correlation between RBM47 and MB21D1 (Supplementary Fig. 4). RIP analysis showed that cGAS mRNA was significantly enriched by Flag-RBM47 immunoprecipitation (Fig. 4B). RNA pull-down assay indicated that RBM47 was pulled out by a biotin-labeled RNA probe from cGAS mRNA, but not by the negative control probe (Fig. 4C). To assess the effect of RBM47 on cGAS mRNA stability, BV2 microglia were transfected with either Flag-RBM47 or an empty vector for 36 h, followed by treatment with ActD. RBM47 overexpression delayed the degradation of cGAS mRNA (Fig. 4D). Bioinformatics analysis of cGAS mRNA using the catRAPID omics v2.0 webserver25 predicted three potential RBM47-conjugation motifs (Fig. 4E). We found that only the 3′UTR of cGAS mRNA was precipitated by RBM47 upon its overexpression (Fig. 4F). To confirm the functional significance of the binding site within the cGAS 3′UTR region, we co-transfected plasmids encoding Flag-RBM47 with cGAS wild type or its three mutant variants. The 3′UTR sequence UUUCUUUGUUUG was identified as a pivotal binding site for RBM47, as reflected by decreased cGAS mRNA enrichment exclusively upon transduction of the MUT1 variant into BV2 cells (Fig. 4G). To ascertain the specific domain of RBM47 responsible for its interaction with cGAS mRNA, we introduced the FL RBM47 or its three deletion truncations (Fig. 4H) into BV2 microglia. RIP-qPCR analysis revealed that the RRM2 domain of RBM47 was essential for binding to cGAS mRNA (Fig. 4I). In addition, the Flag-tagged RBM47-ΔRRM2 truncation prominently accelerated the decay of cGAS mRNA compared to the full-length RBM47 (Fig. 4J). Collectively, our findings reveal that the RRM2 domain of RBM47 directly interacts with the UUUCUUUGUUUG motif in the 3′UTR of cGAS mRNA, thereby promoting its stabilization.

The RRM2 domain of RBM47 was responsible for the activation of microglial cGAS-STING signaling and neuronal ferroptosis

To determine whether the pro-ferroptotic effect of RBM47 during POCD environment is mediated by the RRM2 domain, BV2 cells were transfected with Flag-tagged pcDNA3.1 plasmids encoding either the full-length RBM47 (FL group) or a mutant lacking the RRM2 domain (ΔRRM2 group), followed by exposure to Iso + LPS (Fig. 5A). Western blot analysis confirmed the expression of both the full-length and truncated forms of Flag-RBM47. Diminished levels of cGAS and STING were observed in RBM47-ΔRRM2-BV2 microglia under Iso + LPS stimulation compared to the FL group (Fig. 5B, Supplementary Fig. 5A–C). The CM from RBM47-ΔRRM2-BV2 microglia markedly enhanced neuronal MEF2C and GPX4 expression upon Iso + LPS challenge compared to RBM47-BV2 microglia (Fig. 5C, Supplementary Fig 5D, E). ELISA analysis demonstrated that TNF-α, IL-6, and IFN-β levels were lower in the supernatant of Iso + LPS-primed BV2 cells transfected with Flag-RBM47-ΔRRM2 than in those transfected with the FL Flag-RBM47 (Fig. 5D–F). The viability of HT22 neurons treated with CM from RBM47-ΔRRM2-BV2 microglia was significantly higher than that of neurons exposed to RBM47-BV2 microglia under Iso + LPS conditions (Fig. 5G). Reduced lipid ROS content was found in HT22 cells grown in the CM from RBM47-ΔRRM2-BV2 cells treated with Iso + LPS compared to the FL group (Fig. 5H, I). The RRM2 deletion mutant protected HT22 neurons from lipid peroxidation induced by the CM derived from Iso + LPS-treated BV2 microglia (Fig. 5J). Elevated levels of GSH and SOD were detected in HT22 cells exposed to the CM from RBM47-ΔRRM2-BV2 cells following Iso + LPS administration compared to the FL group (Fig. 5K, L). The CM from RBM47-ΔRRM2-BV2 microglia repressed neuronal iron accumulation in response to Iso + LPS stimulation (Fig. 5M). These results suggest that blocking the interaction between RBM47 and cGAS mRNA abrogates neuronal ferroptosis phenotypes dominated by RBM47.

Rbm47 knockdown and RU.521 combination therapy alleviated POCD

To elucidate the role of RBM47 in the pathogenesis of POCD, AAV9-shRbm47 was delivered in mice to ensure extensive expression in the hippocampus. Mice subjected to anesthesia and surgery exhibited a reduced discrimination index in the NOR task compared to the control group, whereas Rbm47-deficient mice noticeably preferred the novel object after isoflurane and laparotomy. Moreover, supplementation with RU.521 augmented pro-cognitive effects of AAV9-shRbm47, as reflected by an increased discrimination index compared to the AS + shRBM47 group (Supplementary Fig. 6A). Isoflurane anesthesia and laparotomy markedly prolonged the escape latency on the 3rd, 4th, and 5th days of the training phase in the MWM test. However, mice receiving AAV9-shRbm47 therapy spent a shorter time approaching the concealed platform compared to the AS group. A further diminution of escape latency was observed in mice receiving concurrent regimens of AAV9-shRbm47 and RU.521 relative to Rbm47 knockdown alone (Fig. 6A). Anesthesia and surgery, regardless of shRbm47 or shRbm47/RU.521 pretreatment, had no impact on spontaneous locomotor activity, as indicated by the lack of statistical differences in the average swimming speed (Supplementary Fig. 6B). During the probe trial, mice that underwent an abdominal procedure under isoflurane anesthesia showed significantly inferior performance compared to the control group, as indicated by decreased time spent in the target quadrant and fewer platform crossings. Rbm47 silencing significantly increased the target quadrant residence time and platform crossing times. Furthermore, the addition of RU.521 to AAV9-shRbm47 therapy enhanced the cognitive benefits of shRbm47, as shown by further increased duration in the target quadrant and platform crossing frequency (Fig. 6B, C). Mitochondria exhibit distinct ultrastructural alterations during ferroptosis, which are typically assessed using TEM26. Anesthesia and surgery caused shrunken mitochondria with condensed membranes and cristae reduction or vanishing, which was partially reversed by Rbm47 ablation. Intriguingly, shRbm47 and RU.521 combination therapy further restored mitochondrial morphology, including well-preserved cristae and reduced membrane condensation (Fig. 6D-TEM). Nissl staining demonstrated densely packed neurons with distinct nuclei in the hippocampal CA1 region of the control mice. In contrast, mice subjected to anesthesia and surgery displayed marked neuronal damage, including nuclear pyknosis and sparse arrangements. A more structured neuronal alignment and increased Nissl-positive cells were observed in the AS + shRBM47 group relative to the AS group. The number of hippocampal surviving neurons was further increased in the hippocampus of the AS + shRBM47 + RU.521 group compared to the AS + shRBM47 group (Fig. 6D-Nissl, Supplementary Fig. 6C). Immunoblot analysis revealed that isoflurane and laparotomy remarkably activated the cGAS-STING signaling, but diminished the hippocampal protein levels of MEF2C and GPX4. The decreased expression of RBM47 indicated successful transduction of the AAV9 vector harboring shRbm47. Downregulation of cGAS and STING, along with upregulation of MEF2C and GPX4, occurred in the hippocampus of Rbm47-deficient mice subjected to laparotomy under isoflurane anesthesia. Simultaneous administration of AAV9-shRBM47 and RU.521 further repressed the cGAS-STING axis and enhanced MEF2C and GPX4 protein levels (Fig. 6E, Supplementary Fig. 6D–I). ELISA analysis showed that anesthesia and surgery robustly induced the secretion of NF-κB-pertinent cytokines TNF-α and IL-6, as well as IFN-β, which was abolished by AAV9-shRbm47. Further restraint of these three pro-inflammatory factors was found in mice receiving concomitant regimens of Rbm47 knockdown and RU.521 compared to AAV9-shRBM47 alone (Fig. 6F–H). The concentration of the lipid peroxide by-product MDA was markedly elevated in the hippocampus of mice after anesthesia and surgery, which was ameliorated by AAV9-shRbm47. Genetic Rbm47 inhibition combined with RU.521 further restrained lipid peroxidation relative to shRbm47 alone (Fig. 6I). Anesthesia/surgery-treated mice demonstrated decreased hippocampal GSH levels and SOD antioxidant activity, while Rbm47-KD mice displayed powerful antioxidant capacity post-surgery. Genetic silencing of Rbm47 and pharmacological suppression of cGAS further augmented GSH and SOD levels (Fig. 6J, K). Additionally, iron accumulation was identified in the hippocampus of mice following exposure to isoflurane anesthesia and abdominal surgery, which was attenuated by AAV9-shRbm47. Co-administration of shRbm47 and RU.521 further impeded iron overload relative to the AS + shRBM47 group (Fig. 6L). These findings indicate that the cGAS inhibitor RU.521 coordinates with Rbm47 silencing to alleviate POCD and neuronal ferroptosis.

Microglial Runx1 gain‑of‑function facilitated the RBM47-cGAS-STING cascade and neuronal ferroptosis

BV2 microglia were transfected with His-tagged pcDNA3.1-empty vector or Runx1 to illuminate the role of RUNX1 in the cGAS-STING signaling and neuronal ferroptosis (Fig. 7A). The Iso + LPS-CM robustly stimulated microglial cGAS, STING, RBM47, and RUNX1 expression, which was further augmented by Runx1 overexpression (Fig. 7B, Supplementary Fig. 7A–D). Nevertheless, the His-Runx1 plasmid further repressed neuronal MEF2C and GPX4 levels in response to Iso + LPS challenge (Fig. 7C, Supplementary Fig. 7E, F). Runx1-OE BV2 cells treated with isoflurane and LPS provoked higher secretions of TNF-α, IL-6, and IFN-β (Fig. 7D–F). The CCK-8 assay showed a further decreased viability of HT22 neurons when treated with CM from Runx1-OE microglia under Iso + LPS conditions (Fig. 7G). Microglial Runx1 gain-of-function further elicited neuronal lipid ROS accumulation following Iso + LPS administration (Fig. 7H, I). We found that Runx1 overexpression further elevated MDA concentration induced by Iso + LPS-CM (Fig. 7J). Further restraint of GSH and SOD levels was seen in HT22 cells treated with Iso + LPS-CM from Runx1-OE BV2 cells (Fig. 7K, L). Additionally, His-RUNX1 further exacerbated iron overload in HT22 cells following Iso + LPS stimulation (Fig. 7M). Overall, our data underline that microglial Runx1 overexpression activates the RBM47-cGAS-STING pathway and subsequently aggravates neuronal ferroptosis in response to Iso + LPS stimulation.

Microglial Runx1 loss‑of‑function prevents neurons from undergoing ferroptosis in a RBM47-dependent manner

BV2 cells were treated with Flag-Rbm47 in the presence or absence of siRunx1 to determine whether RBM47 is necessary for neuronal ferroptosis governed by RUNX1 (Fig. 8A). Immunoblot analysis identified siRunx1#2 as the most effective silencing fragment (Supplementary Fig. 8A–C) and subsequently transfected into BV2 cells. Diminished protein abundance of cGAS, STING, RBM47, and RUNX1 was observed in Runx1-deficient microglia following Iso + LPS intervention, which was counteracted by Rbm47 overexpression (Fig. 8B, Supplementary Fig. 8D–G). Microglial Runx1 depletion markedly enhanced MEF2C and GPX4 in HT22 neurons exposed to Iso + LPS-CM, which was neutralized by Flag-Rbm47 (Fig. 8C, Supplementary Fig. 8H, I). Runx1 silencing notably attenuated the secretion of TNF-α, IL-6, and IFN-β from BV2 cells treated with Iso + LPS. Nonetheless, these pro-inflammatory cytokines were more abundant in the supernatant of Iso + LPS-primed microglia co-expressing both siRunx1 and Rbm47 than those expressing siRunx1 alone (Fig. 8D–F). HT22 neuronal viability was significantly enhanced when treated with CM from Runx1-KD BV2 microglia under Iso + LPS conditions, which was abrogated by Rbm47 overexpression (Fig. 8G). C11-BODIPY581/591 staining demonstrated that microglial Runx1 loss‑of‑function reduced lipid ROS levels. The average fluorescence intensity of BODIPY was significantly increased in HT22 cells grown in the Iso + LPS-CM from BV2 cells transfected with siRunx1 and Flag-Rbm47 compared to the siRunx1 + Iso + LPS group (Fig. 8H, I). We found that microglial Runx1 knockdown suppressed neuronal lipid peroxidation and iron accumulation upon Iso + LPS stimulation. Increased levels of GSH and SOD were observed in HT22 cells subjected to Iso + LPS-CM from Runx1-KD BV2 cells. Furthermore, Rbm47 overexpression antagonized the anti-ferroptotic effects of siRunx1, as evidenced by augmented levels of MDA and iron, as well as diminished levels of GSH and SOD, in the Iso + LPS + siRUNX1 + RBM47 group compared to the Iso + LPS + siRUNX1 group (Fig. 8J–M). These findings underscore the repressive effect of siRunx1 on Iso + LPS-evoked neuronal ferroptosis through an RBM47-dependent mechanism.

Runx1 ablation ameliorates POCD through dampening the RBM47-cGAS-STING signaling

To investigate the role of RUNX1 in the pathogenesis of POCD and whether RBM47 blunts shRunx1-mediated phenotypes, we introduced recombinant AAV9 encoding shRunx1 or Rbm47 to map the hippocampus of mice undergoing isoflurane and laparotomy. The markedly decreased levels of Runx1 mRNA in hippocampal tissues confirmed the effectiveness of the viral-mediated knockdown (Supplementary Fig. 9). Runx1-deficient mice visibly preferred the novel object after isoflurane and laparotomy, while Rbm47-OE mice spent less novel object exploration time. The discrimination index was significantly reduced in POCD mice receiving concomitant regimens of AAV9-shRunx1 and AAV9-Rbm47 compared to shRunx1 monotherapy (Supplementary Fig. 10A). Runx1-KD mice subjected to anesthesia and surgery displayed shortened escape latency on the 3rd, 4th, and 5th days of training phase compared to the AS + CON group, whereas Rbm47-OE mice exhibited further prolonged escape latency. However, POCD mice treated with AAV9-shRunx1 and AAV9-Rbm47 spent more time reaching the concealed platform compared to the AS + shRUNX1 group (Fig. 9A). No differences were found in the average swimming speed among the six groups (Supplementary Fig. 10B). Runx1 silencing remarkably increased the target quadrant residence time and platform crossing times, while Rbm47 overexpression further decreased the average target quadrant stay and platform crossing frequency. The AS + shRUNX1 + RBM47 group suffered from worse memory performance, as manifested by shorter duration in the target quadrant and fewer platform crossing times than the AS + shRUNX1 group (Fig. 9B, C). Mitochondria derived from Runx1-KD mice exhibited more defined cristae and less outer membrane rupture than those in the AS + CON group, while the AS + RBM47 group displayed increased membrane density and notable outer membrane rupture. More pronounced hippocampal ferroptotic morphology was observed after combination treatments compared to shRUNX1 monotherapy (Fig. 9D-TEM). Compared to the AS + CON group, the survival rate of hippocampal neurons was significantly increased in the AS + shRUNX1 group but decreased in the AS + RBM47 group. POCD mice receiving preemptive co-administration of AAV9-shRunx1 and AAV9-Rbm47 showed more evident neuronal lesions characterized by neural loss and sparse arrangements relative to shRunx1 alone (Fig. 9D-Nissl, Supplementary Fig. 10C). Immunoblot assay revealed downregulation of cGAS, STING, RBM47, and RUNX1, along with upregulation of MEF2C and GPX4, in the hippocampus of Runx1-deficient mice following anesthesia and surgery. Rbm47 overexpression further elevated the levels of hippocampal cGAS, STING, and RBM47, but diminished MEF2C and GPX4 levels. Compared to the AS + shRUNX1 group, the augmented levels of cGAS, STING, and RBM47 were found in the POCD mice administered concomitant regimens of AAV9-shRunx1 and AAV9-Rbm47, accompanied by reduced MEF2C and GPX4 expression (Fig. 9E, Supplementary Fig. 10D–I). Runx1 deficiency blocked the release of TNF-α and IL-6, and IFN-β after isoflurane anesthesia and laparotomy, whereas Rbm47 overexpression further stimulated the secretion of these three pro-inflammatory cytokines. The concentrations of TNF-α and IL-6, and IFN-β were prominently elevated in the hippocampus of POCD mice receiving combination therapy compared to shRunx1 monotherapy (Fig. 9F–H). Genetic inhibition of Runx1 significantly abated lipid peroxidation and iron deposition, while Rbm47 overexpression further increased MDA and iron contents. Elevated MDA and iron concentrations were observed in the hippocampus of POCD mice receiving concomitant regimens of AAV9-shRunx1 and AAV9-Rbm47 compared to shRunx1 alone. Runx1 knockdown enhanced hippocampal GSH and SOD levels, whereas Rbm47 overexpression further diminished the levels of GSH and SOD post-surgery. Decreased levels of GSH and SOD were noted in the AS + shRUNX1 + RBM47 group compared to the AS + shRUNX1 group (Fig. 9I–L). Collectively, these results highlight that Runx1 knockdown mitigates POCD and inhibits cGAS-STING signaling and neuronal ferroptosis by diminishing RBM47 expression.

Discussion

Herein, we found that microglial Rbm47 overexpression aggravated neuronal ferroptosis by propelling the cGAS-STING signaling and downregulating MEF2C. Rbm47 knockdown and pharmacological inhibition of cGAS synergistically ameliorated postoperative cognitive decline. Mechanistically, RBM47 directly binds to the 3′UTR sequence UUUCUUUGUUUG of cGAS mRNA through its RRM2 domain, thereby stabilizing cGAS mRNA. Moreover, we also identified RUNX1 as a transcriptional activator of Rbm47. Runx1 depletion alleviated anesthesia/surgery-provoked cognitive deterioration and ferroptosis by dampening the RBM47-cGAS-STING signaling and boosting the MEF2C-GPX4 axis.

Sterile surgical trauma triggers a sequence of expeditious innate immune responses via NOD-like receptor, Toll-like receptor, and cGAS-STING pathways, which culminate in the production of NF-κB-pertinent pro-inflammatory cytokines and type I interferons3,7,27. Interestingly, RBM47 facilitated the expression of NF-κB-associated inflammatory genes at the post-transcriptional level, accompanied by enhanced migration of M2 macrophages in glioma microenvironment18. Although RBM47 had no noticeable influence on IFN production, RBM47 intensified the host IFN downstream pathway to confer broad-spectrum antiviral properties16. ISGylation of RBM47 at K329 deficiency repressed the mRNA levels of IFN-α1, IFN-β, and IFN-γ in the LPS-induced acute lung injury, along with decreased expression of chemokines and chemokine receptors28. Paradoxically, zebrafish RBM47 attenuated antiviral response by accelerating the lysosomal degradation of MAVS. The binding of RBM47 to MAVS retarded the interaction between MAVS and STING, thereby suppressing IFNφ1 and IFNφ3 production19. RBM47 also promoted the immunosuppression of B cells when confronted with ulcerative colitis17. These apparent discrepancies may be explained by context-dependent regulation, as the mechanisms of RBM47 action may vary depending on cell type, stimulus, or species. We found that RBM47 silencing diminished the levels of cGAS, STING, TNF-α, IL-6, and IFN-β in Iso + LPS-treated BV2 cells and the hippocampus of POCD mice. Conversely, RBM47 overexpression augmented the cGAS-STING signaling and ensuing pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion, which was counteracted by the cGAS inhibitor RU.521. In line with previous evidence that activation of cGAS-STING diminished cognitive resilience by reducing the neuronal MEF2C transcriptional network12, we also demonstrated that microglial activation of the RBM47-cGAS-STING signaling decreased neuronal MEF2C expression. Notably, RBM47 may contribute to ferroptosis of tumor cells because it mediates the post-transcriptional upregulation of the ferroptosis driver gene ATF3 or KEAP129,30. Recent studies revealed that MEF2C rendered protection against ferroptosis of meningioma, lung cancer, and neurons 13,31,32. Our study demonstrated that microglial RBM47 negatively regulated GPX4 expression to drive neuronal lipid peroxidation and iron accumulation in a cGAS-dependent manner. Consistent with the favorable impact of cGAS repression on cognitive performance in the NOR and MWM tests7,33, we observed that RU.521 plays a synergic role in the cognitive benefits of RBM47 knockdown.

As an RNA-binding protein, RBM47 preferentially binds to AREs in the 3′UTR of target RNAs. For instance, RBM47 promoted the stability of IL-10 mRNA via binding to the ARE motifs within its 3′UTR17. RBM47 interacted with AXIN1 mRNA 3′UTR to inhibit non-small cell lung carcinoma metastasis by suppressing Wnt/beta-catentin signaling34. The AGGAGCCGAGGC sequence within the 3′UTR of UPF1 mRNA had a strong affinity for RBM47 binding in hepatocellular carcinoma35. However, RBM47 possessed a potent glioma-promoting activity by linking to 3′UTR of IL-1A, IL-1B, CXCL2, MMP1, and TRAF6 mRNA18. The overwhelming majority of investigations demonstrated that the interaction between RBM47 and target RNAs protected the latter from degradation29,30,36. Notably, RBM47 may also destabilize mRNA because RBM47 directly interacts with the AAAUGCGUAUAUAUACCCAU sequence within the 3′UTR of TSC22D3 mRNA and diminishes its mRNA and protein levels28. Our results further highlighted that RBM47 specifically recognizes the 3′UTR sequence UUUCUUUGUUUG of cGAS mRNA and enhances its stability. RBM family proteins contain one or more RRM domains that are responsible for RNA binding. For example, RBM12 bound to the JAK1 mRNA via the fourth RRM domain and recruited EIF4A2 through the second RRM domain, thereby promoting the translation of JAK137. Both the RRM1 and RRM2 domains of RBM45 preferred to recognize RNAs with multiple GAC motifs38. Distinct from the existing evidence that the first RRM domain of RBM47 was a requisite for the alternative splicing of TJP1 pre-mRNA15, we underlined the RRM2 domain contributions towards cGAS mRNA recognition of RBM47. Innate immune activation after aseptic surgical trauma leads to elevated proinflammatory cytokines in the hippocampus that can impair long-term potentiation, a key mechanism underlying memory formation39. Sevoflurane promoted mitochondrial fission-mediated release of mtDNA and subsequently activated microglial cGAS-STING signaling-dependent NLRP3 inflammasome40. Isoflurane and laparotomy elicited dsDNA accumulation, activated microglial cGAS-STING pathway, and ultimately triggered neuronal pyroptosis mediated by caspase-3/GSDME7. Short-chain fatty acids enhanced Nlrp6 expression and facilitated RIG-1/MAVS-driven mitophagy, thereby protecting hippocampal neural stem/progenitor cells from corticosterone-induced ferroptosis and mitigating chronic stress-related cognitive impairments41. Mounting evidence implies that alterations in the intricate crosstalk between the cGAS-STING pathway and ferroptosis contribute to neurodegeneration and other diseases42. For instance, STING activation promotes autophagy-mediated degradation of GPX4 in neurons under inflammatory and excitotoxic conditions43. STING is also bound to the NCOA4-FTH1 complex and facilitates NCOA4-mediated ferritinophagy in acute kidney injury44. Our study also linked excessive neuronal ferroptosis with microglial activation of RBM47-cGAS-STING signaling.

FOXO3 activated the transcription of human RBM47 gene by directly binding to the nucleotide sequence TGTAAACC located 653–646 bp upstream of the TSS45. TTF-1 enhanced human RBM47 promoter activity in lung adenocarcinoma, whereas SMAD3 repressed its transcription30. We found that the transcriptional activator RUNX1 recognized the sequence from −1401 bp to −2100 bp upstream of the TSS within the mouse Rbm47 gene. RUNX1, predominantly expressed in subsets of embryonic and postnatal microglia, participates in microglial genesis, activation, and homeostasis46. Upregulation of RUNX1 was detected after traumatic brain injury, temporal lobe epilepsy, and ischemic stroke47–49. Isoflurane anesthesia and laparotomy also robustly enhanced RUNX1 expression in mouse hippocampi. Alveolar type II cell-specific overexpression of RUNX1 decreased the protein levels of cGAS and STING in response to LPS-induced lung injury50. However, RUNX1 augmented IFN production and the phosphorylation of NF-κB, TBK1, and IRF3 in lupus monocytes through transcriptionally suppressing NLRP1251. RUNX1 knockdown or its inhibitor Ro5-3335 disturbed NF-κB pathway activation in LPS-provoked inflammation, including diminished levels of IL-1β, IL-6, phosphorylated p65, and IKKβ52,53. Our work emphasized that RUNX1 impelled the secretion of NF-κB-pertinent pro-inflammatory cytokines and type I interferons by positively modulating the RBM47-cGAS-STING signaling. Emerging evidence indicates that RUNX1 depletion may repress MARCH3 transcription and subsequently prevent GPX4 from ubiquitin-dependent degradation mediated by MARCH3 in hepatocytes under intermittent conditions54, which aligned with our results that RUNX1-deficient microglia facilitated GPX4 expression and restrained neuronal ferroptosis in a RBM47-dependent fashion.

We recognize several limitations in our current study. Although a recognized in vitro model for POCD is lacking, LPS-induced neuroinflammation has been widely utilized to simulate POCD-like conditions in numerous investigations 55,56. Importantly, sterile surgical injury can initiate an innate immune response comparable to that triggered by LPS, through the release of damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) such as HMGB1. These molecules can activate NF-κB signaling and promote the transcription of proinflammatory cytokines like TNF-α57. Given their central roles in neuroinflammation, HMGB1 and TNF-α may represent more appropriate agents for developing in vitro POCD models in future studies. Furthermore, although isoflurane is widely used in POCD animal models, its standalone effects on BV2 cells in vitro and its relevance to microglial cGAS-STING activation warrant further investigation. The additive neuroprotective effects of shRBM47 and RU.521 may stem from dual targeting of the RBM47-cGAS-STING axis in microglia, as well as potential off-target actions of RU.521 beyond microglia. For instance, inhibition of neuronal cGAS may independently contribute to the observed protective outcomes. Although our current study primarily focused on microglia-mediated responses, further investigation is warranted to dissect the cell-type-specific contributions of cGAS inhibition. The efficiency of each shRNA construct was validated at both the mRNA and protein levels using qRT-PCR and western blotting, respectively. The selected shRNAs exhibited robust knockdown efficiency relative to control groups. Although shRNA-mediated silencing is a well-established approach, potential off-target effects cannot be entirely ruled out. Two independent shRNAs targeting distinct regions of Rbm47 and Runx1 should be designed for further investigations.

In conclusion, our study provided insights into the upstream and downstream mechanisms of RBM47 in the pathogenesis of POCD. Our findings highlight the RUNX1-RBM47-cGAS-STING pathway as a prospective therapeutic target in response to anesthesia and surgery challenges.

Methods

Antibodies and reagents

The following antibodies were used for western blot analysis: cGAS (#29958-1-AP, Proteintech, 1: 2000), STING (#ab288157, Abcam, 1: 1000), RBM47 (#23902-1-AP, Proteintech, 1: 1000), runt-related transcription factor 1 (RUNX1, #25315-1-AP, Proteintech, 1: 1000), MEF2C (#ab211493, Abcam, 1: 1000), GPX4 (#67763-1-Ig, Proteintech, 1: 2000), Flag (#20543-1-AP, Proteintech, 1: 3000), and GAPDH (#ab181602, Abcam, 1: 10,000). Horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibodies (1: 10,000) were obtained from ASPEN. Isoflurane was purchased from Abbott Laboratories (Shanghai, China). Lipopolysaccharide (LPS, #HY-D1056) and the cGAS inhibitor RU.521 (#HY-114180) were provided by MedChemExpress (MCE, NJ, USA). The RNA synthesis inhibitor Actinomycin D (ActD, #S8964), and ferroptosis inhibitors ferrostatin-1 (Fer-1, #S7243) and Liproxstatin-1 (Lip-1, #S7699) were obtained from Selleck (Houston, USA). Commercial kits for pro-inflammatory cytokines TNF-α, IL-6, and IFN-β were purchased from ELK Biotechnology. Malondialdehyde (MDA), reduced glutathione (GSH), superoxide dismutase (SOD), and iron kits were sourced from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute (Nanjing, China).

Plasmids

The full-length (FL, GRCm39 5: c66,311,396-66,309,297) RBM47 promoter sequence or its truncations (-2100/-1401, GRCm39 5: c66,311,396-66,310,697; -1400/-701, GRCm39 5: c66,310,696-66,309,997; and -700/-1, c66,309,996-66,309,297) (ELK, Wuhan, China) were cloned into the pGL3-luciferase reporter vector. The pcDNA3.1 vector was used to construct His-tagged RUNX1, Flag-tagged RBM47, and the three deletion truncations of RBM47 (ΔRRM1, ΔRRM2, ΔRRM3) (Genscript, Nanjing, China). Three small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) targeting mouse RBM47 and RUNX1 were synthesized by RiboBio (Guangzhou, China) and their knockdown efficacy was determined by real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR). The siRNA sequences targeting mouse RBM47 and RUNX1 were 5′-GCTCTCTCTCCTGAGTCCTTAGTAA-3′ and 5′-CTACTCGGCAGAACTGAGA-3′, respectively. The cGAS 3′UTR wild type or its three mutants were constructed by Genscript Biotech Corp. (Nanjing, China).

Cell culture and treatment

Mouse BV2 microglia and HT22 neurons from iCell Bioscience Inc (Shanghai, China) were maintained in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM; Hyclone, Logan, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco, Grand Island, USA) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (P/S; Gibco, Grand Island, USA) at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 incubator. Microglia were administered isoflurane with LPS to imitate a POCD environment7. The specified plasmids were transfected into BV2 cells for 36 h using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, USA). The culture plates were subjected to 3% isoflurane with pure oxygen for 30 min, followed by a 12-h intervention of 1 μg/mL LPS. RU.521 was added to BV2 microglial medium at an eventual concentration of 2 μM for 2 h58 before Iso + LPS treatment. The culture supernatant from BV2 cells was harvested as conditioned medium (CM) to incubate HT22 cells for 24 h.

Dual luciferase reporter gene assay

BV2 microglia were co-transfected for 36 h with different luciferase plasmids alongside the His-tagged pcDNA3.1-RUNX1 or its empty vector control using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, USA). Cells were fully lysed, followed by centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 5 min. The supernatant was incubated with the luciferase substrate for activity measurement. The primers for Rbm47-promoter constructs in the dual luciferase reporter gene assay are detailed in Supplementary Table 1. The interaction between RUNX1 and the Rbm47 promoter region was analyzed using the relative luciferase activity, according to the manufacturer’s instructions for the dual luciferase reporter gene assay kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China).

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assay

BV2 cells transfected with a plasmid encoding His-RUNX1 were cross-linked with formaldehyde at room temperature. The cells were resuspended in lysis buffer supplemented with protease inhibitors and underwent sonication to shear chromatin into fragments. The resulting samples were incubated with either an anti-His antibody (#ab213204, Abcam) or IgG (#ab172730, Abcam) conjugated to Protein A/G Agarose. Bound DNA was purified using a phenol-chloroform extraction. Finally, the purified DNA sequences were analyzed using qPCR with primers targeting the Rbm47 promoter region: 5′-CTCAACAACTGAGCTGCACCTC-3′ (sense) and 5′-TTGATACAGTTAGTTAGCCACCAAG-3’ (antisense). Quantitative PCR was performed utilizing EnTurbo™ SYBR Green PCR SuperMix kit (ELK, Wuhan, China) on a QuantStudio 6 Flex Real-Time PCR System (Life Technologies, Carlsbad, USA). Fold enrichment was calculated relative to the input DNA using the 2−ΔΔCt method.

HT22 cell viability assay

HT22 neurons in the logarithmic growth phase were cultured in DMEM to prepare 1 × 104 cell/mL suspension. The suspension was seeded onto a 96-well plate at 100 μL per well. Each well was added by 10 µL of cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) reagent (Beyotime, Nanjing, China). The optical density (OD) at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader (Diatek, Wuxi, China).

Borondipyrromethene (BODIPY) 581/591 C11 staining

We first prepared a 10 mM BODIPY 581/591 C11 stock solution in DMSO and diluted it in serum-free DMEM to a final working concentration of 5 µM. HT22 cells were incubated with 1 mL of BODIPY 581/591 C11 dye for 30 min at 37 °C in a humidified CO₂ incubator. After centrifugation at 400 g for 3 min, the cells were resuspended in serum-free DMEM. Flow cytometric analysis was conducted using a CytoFLEX flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter, Brea, USA). The degree of lipid peroxidation was determined by calculating the fluorescence intensity ratio between 510 nm (oxidized form) and 590 nm (reduced form) emissions. To ensure reliable quantification, cells were first gated based on forward scatter (FSC) and side scatter (SSC) parameters to exclude debris and non-cellular events (Supplementary Fig. S11A–E). Doublets were removed by comparing FSC-H versus FSC-A signals, allowing analysis of single-cell populations only. Ghost dye-positive (Cytekbio, 13-0865-T100) events were excluded to ensure that only viable, single neurons were analyzed.

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP)

BV2 microglia were transfected with Flag-RBM47 or its three truncations. After centrifugation at 1000 g for 5 min, the cell pellet was resuspended in Poly Lysis Buffer (PLB) and stored at −80 °C. PLB cell suspensions were centrifuged at 20,000 g for 10 min at 4 °C to pellet cellular debris. Cleared lysates were immunoprecipitated with anti-Flag-protein A/G mixture or nonspecific IgG-protein A/G mixture. The precipitated mRNAs were subsequently reverse transcribed into cDNA using the EntiLink 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Super Mix (ELK, Wuhan, China). PCR amplification was performed utilizing EnTurbo™ SYBR Green PCR SuperMix kit (ELK, Wuhan, China) on a QuantStudio 6 Flex Real-Time PCR System (Life Technologies, Carlsbad, USA). The specific primers designed against cGAS mRNA were listed in Supplementary Table 2.

RNA pull-down

RNA pull-down assay was performed to confirm the interaction between RBM47 and cGAS mRNA in BV2 cells. Briefly, biotin-labeled cGAS mRNA (RiboBio, Guangzhou, China; accession number: NM_173386.5) was incubated with magnetic beads and protein lysates. After magnetic separation and washing with RNA washing buffer (20 mM Tris-HCl, pH 7.0, 300 mM NaCl, 1 mM EDTA, 0.5% NP-40), the RNA-bound beads were harvested and subjected to immunoblotting with RBM47.

cGAS mRNA decay assay

To assess cGAS mRNA decay rate, BV2 cells were transfected with the specified plasmids for 36 h using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, USA), followed by treatment with 10 µg/mL ActD for 0, 4, and 8 h. Quantification of cGAS mRNA levels was conducted using RT-qPCR. The primer sequences for cGAS and GAPDH were as follows: for cGAS, 5′-TTCTGCTCCTAATGAATTTGATGT-3′ (sense) and 5′-CCTGATAAGAAGTGTTACAGCAGG-3′ (antisense); and for GAPDH, 5′-TGAAGGGTGGAGCCAAAAG-3′ (sense) and 5′-AGTCTTCTGGGTGGCAGTGAT-3′(antisense). The normalized cGAS mRNA levels to GAPDH at 0 h were set as 100%.

Animals

Male C57BL/6J mice (MGI: 3028467), aged 12 months and weighing 28–32 g, were supplied from Hubei Provincial Center for Disease Control and Prevention. Animals were acclimated for 7 days on a standard chow diet and water in a rodent facility adhering to specific pathogen-free conditions (a 12-h light/dark cycle, 22–25 °C, and 50% humidity). All experimental procedures received approval from the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Renmin Hospital of Wuhan University (No. WDRM-20230701E). We have complied with all relevant ethical regulations for animal use.

Anesthesia and surgery (AS)

The POCD mouse model was established by aseptic laparotomy under isoflurane anesthesia within a transparent chamber (Midmark, Dayton, USA) pre-filled with 3% isoflurane and 100% oxygen59. After loss of consciousness, a 1.5 cm vertical midline incision was executed to access the abdominal cavity during 1.5% isoflurane exposure for anesthesia maintenance. Approximately 5 cm of the intestine was exteriorized and gently manipulated with wet sterile gauze for 10 min. The incision was subsequently closed in layers utilizing a 5-0 absorbable suture. The entire protocol from anesthesia induction to the end of surgery spanned approximately 30 min. Hypothermia prophylaxis was implemented using a heating pad, while postoperative analgesia was provided by compound lidocaine cream. The control mice were exposed to pure oxygen without anesthesia or surgical intervention. The cGAS inhibitor RU.521 (5 mg/kg) (MCE, NJ, USA, #HY-114180) was prepared in a solvent comprising 10% dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), 40% polyethylene glycol 300 (PEG300), 5% Tween-80, and 45% saline10. Mice in the AS+shRBM47+RU.521 group received an intraperitoneal injection of RU.521 30 min before modeling.

Viral microinjection

The short hairpin RNA (shRNA) sequences targeting mouse RBM47 and RUNX1 were 5′-GCTCTCTCTCCTGAGTCCTTAGTAA-3′, and 5’-CTACTCGGCAGAACTGAGA-3’, respectively. Adeno-associated virus 9 (AAV9) vectors bearing mouse shRBM47 and shRUNX1 were constructed by OBiO Biotechnology (Shanghai, China). Animals were anesthetized with isoflurane and secured in a stereotaxic frame. After accurate positioning, two burr holes were drilled into the skull. A syringe needle was inserted into the bilateral hippocampi using the following coordinates: anterioposterior (AP) −2.0 mm, mediolateral (ML) ± 2.3 mm, and dorsoventral (DV) −2.0 mm. Each mouse received unilateral 1 μL infusion of AAV9 at a speed of 0.1 μL/min59,60.

Behavioral tests

Mice were acclimated to the experimental environment on the 4th day post-surgery a day ahead of the novel object recognition (NOR) test. During the training session, two identical cuboids were positioned at diagonally opposite corners of a 50 × 50 × 40 cm3 open field apparatus. The time spent exploring objects was documented when each mouse was permitted to move for 5 min. Twenty-four hours later, one of the cuboids was substituted with a novel cylinder. The discrimination index was calculated as Tn/(Tn+Tf), where Tn and Tf equate the duration of exploring the novel and familiar object, respectively.

The Morris water maze (MWM) test was performed 7–12 days post-surgery to assess the hippocampus-dependent spatial memory61,62. The cylindrical pool (120 cm in diameter and 50 cm in height) featured a concealed platform (6 cm in diameter) in the fourth quadrant. The Morris Water Maze (MWM) test was structured into two distinct phases: the location navigation phase, conducted four trials per day for five consecutive days, and the space exploration phase conducted in the absence of the platform. Mice were randomly introduced into the water from one of four quadrants and given 60 seconds to locate the platform. If a mouse failed to find the platform, it was manually guided to it. The time taken to find the platform was documented as the escape latency. Additional parameters, including swimming speed, time spent in the fourth (target) quadrant, and the number of platform crossings, were automatically monitored using a video tracking system (XinRuan, Shanghai, China).

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)

Following behavioral assessments, three mice from each group were randomly selected and anesthetized with isoflurane until complete loss of reflexes. After transcardial perfusion with cold PBS followed by 2.5% glutaraldehyde in 0.1 M phosphate buffer (pH 7.4), the brains were immediately removed. Hippocampal tissues were dissected and fixed overnight at 4 °C with 2.5% glutaraldehyde (Sinopharm, Beijing, China). After rinsing with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), the samples were fixed with 1% osmic acid (Sinopharm, Beijing, China) for 2 h. The specimens were dehydrated through a graded ethanol series and were subsequently infiltrated with Epon812 resin (Shell Chemicals, Netherlands). Ultrathin sections were stained with uranyl acetate (Aladdin, Shanghai, China) and lead citrate (Thermo Fisher, New York, USA) for visualization. The mitochondrial ultrastructure was photographed using an electron microscope (Hitachi, Tokyo, Japan).

Nissl staining

Three male mice from each group were randomly selected and euthanized under isoflurane anesthesia until complete loss of reflexes. After transcardial perfusion with ice-cold PBS and 10% paraformaldehyde, whole brains were removed and underwent 24 h of post-fixation. Hippocampal tissues were sectioned into 10-μm coronal slices. After dehydration through a series of alcohol gradients, paraffin-embedded sections were incubated with Nissl staining solution for 10 min. The specimens were mounted with neutral resin and examined under a light microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) by an investigator who was blinded to the interventions. We quantified the number of surviving neurons in the bilateral hippocampi of each mouse using ImageJ software.

Western blot

Cells and hippocampal tissues were homogenized in radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) buffer (ASPEN, Wuhan, China). The samples were separated via sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) (ASPEN, Wuhan, China) and subsequently transferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes (Millipore, Billerica, USA). The membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk, followed by sequential incubation with primary and HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies. Immunodetection was conducted using an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) assay kit (ASPEN, China).

TNF-α, IL-6, IFN-β, MDA, GSH, SOD, and iron

Hippocampal homogenates and cells were prepared for the determination of protein concentration employing a bicinchoninic acid (BCA) assay kit (ASPEN). The levels of cytokines TNF-α (#ELK1387), IL-6 (#ELK1157), and IFN-β (#ELK2349) were quantified utilizing respective assay kits in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocols. The concentrations of MDA (#A003-1), GSH (#A006-2), SOD (#A001-3), and iron (#A039-2) were measured following the manufacturer’s instructions for the corresponding assay kits.

Statistics and reproducibility

Each experiment was conducted in triplicate and repeated in at least three independent biological replicates. The normality of the data distribution was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Values were shown as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism version 9.0 (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, USA). Parameters between or among groups were compared using an unpaired Student’s t-test, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post hoc test, or two-way ANOVA followed by Bonferroni’s multiple comparisons test. Statistical significance was defined at P < 0.05.

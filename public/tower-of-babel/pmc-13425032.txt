Extradigital glomangioma: a rare occurrence
Authors: Shraddha Uprety, Pratichya Thapa, Prakriti Lamichhane, Sunil Jaiswal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Extradigital glomangioma is a rare variant of glomangioma with few cases reported over the extremities, trunk and visceral organs. Patients with extradigital glomangioma lack classic signs and symptoms in comparison with typical glomangioma and thus can be confused with haemangioma, leiomyoma and intradermal naevus, requiring high suspicion and biopsy for the definitive diagnosis.

Dear Editor, A 56-year-old man presented with a solitary reddish nodule on the lateral aspect of the proximal one-third of the right arm which had been present for > 25 years. The lesion was nonpainful, measuring approximately 1 cm in size (Figure 1). Histopathological examination of the nodule revealed congested blood vessels lined by endothelial cells surrounded by round glomus cells forming nests and sheets (Figure 2). A diagnosis of extradigital glomangioma was established, and there has been no recurrence over the last 3 months.

Solitary erythematous-to-violaceous nodule on the lateral aspect of the proximal one-third of the patient’s right arm.

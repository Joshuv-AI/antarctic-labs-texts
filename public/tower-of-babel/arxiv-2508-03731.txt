Revisiting the operator extension of strong subadditivity
Lauritz van Luijk, Alexander Stottmeister, Henrik Wilming

Abstract
We give a new proof of the operator extension of the strong subadditivity of von Neumann entropy ρ A ⊗ σ B ​ C − 1 ≥ ρ A ​ B ⊗ σ C − 1 \rho_{A}\otimes\sigma_{BC}^{-1}\geq\rho_{AB}\otimes\sigma_{C}^{-1} (1) by identifying the mathematical structure behind it as Connes’ theory of spatial derivatives. This immediately generalizes the inequality to arbitrary inclusions of von Neumann algebras. In the case of standard representations, it reduces to the monotonicity of the relative modular operator.



Recently, the operator inequality (
1
)
for bipartite quantum states
\rho_{AB}
(arbitrary) and
\sigma_{BC}
with full rank on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{A}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{B}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{C}
was elegantly proven in
[
1
]
(see also
[
2
]
).
The inequality implies strong subadditivity of von Neumann entropy, originally proven in
[
3
]
, and hence corresponds to an operator strengthening of strong subadditivity.
This curious inequality asks for an explanation in terms of a deeper mathematical structure.

It was observed in
[
1
, Sec. 3]
that (
1
) resembles—but does not follow from—the monotonicity of the relative modular operator, which takes the form
\rho\otimes\sigma^{-1}
for two density matrices
\rho,\sigma
.
In the following, we will see that (
1
) is an immediate consequence of Connes’ theory of spatial derivatives on von Neumann algebras
[
4
]
,
which can be understood as a generalization of (relative) modular theory to settings where a given von Neumann algebra
M\subset B({\mathcal{H}})
is not in
standard form
, i.e., there is no cyclic and separating vector
\Omega\in{\mathcal{H}}
for
M
. Indeed, (
1
) is simply the monotonicity of the spatial derivative for inclusions of matrix factors.
This observation has two consequences:

1.

There exists an appropriate generalization of (
1
) to the setting of von Neumann algebras. In the standard representation, it reduces to the monotonicity of the relative modular operator
[
5
, Eq. (II.1.3)]
.

2.

We find the following equivalent formulation of (
1
)

\displaystyle\tr_{C}\big(\sigma_{C}^{-1/2}X_{ABC}\sigma_{C}^{-1/2}\big)\leq\tr_{BC}\big(\sigma_{BC}^{-1/2}X_{ABC}\sigma_{BC}^{-1/2}\big)

(2)

as an operator inequality valid for all positive operators
X_{ABC}
(we suppress explicit identities).

To see the equivalence of (
1
) with (
2
), we evaluate the latter with
X_{ABC}=|\xi\rangle\langle\xi|
an arbitrary rank-one projection, and take expectation values of the resulting operator on
AB
against an arbitrary state
\rho_{AB}
:

\displaystyle\tr\big(\rho_{AB}\,\tr_{C}(\sigma_{C}^{-1/2}|\xi\rangle\langle\xi|\sigma_{C}^{-1/2})\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tr\big(\rho_{A}\,\tr_{BC}(\sigma_{BC}^{-1/2}|\xi\rangle\langle\xi|\sigma_{BC}^{-1/2})\big)

\ =\

\ =\

\displaystyle\langle\xi,\rho_{AB}\otimes\sigma_{C}^{-1}\xi\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\langle\xi,\rho_{A}\otimes\sigma_{BC}^{-1}\xi\rangle.

Since these (in-)equalities hold for all
\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}
and states
\rho_{AB}
, the equivalence of (
1
) and (
2
) follows.

1
Finite-dimensional case

We first present the finite-dimensional version of the general von Neumann algebraic proof of (
1
).

Consider a tripartite system with Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}_{A}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{B}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{C}
.
We fix a full-rank state
\sigma_{BC}
on
{\mathcal{H}}_{BC}={\mathcal{H}}_{B}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{C}
and denote by
\Omega\in{\mathcal{H}}_{BC}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{\overline{BC}}
the unnormalized maximally entangled state
\sum_{i}\eta_{i}\otimes\eta_{i}
, where
\{\eta_{i}\}
constitutes an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors of
\sigma_{BC}
and
\overline{B}
,
\overline{C}
denote copies of
B
,
C
, respectively.
Then
\Omega_{\sigma}:=\sigma_{BC}^{1/2}\Omega
is a normalized vector that purifies
\sigma_{BC}
.
Let us define the vector spaces

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{C}:=\lin\{a_{C}\Omega_{\sigma}:a_{C}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{C})\}\subset{\mathcal{K}}_{BC}:=\lin\{a_{BC}\Omega_{\sigma}:a_{BC}\in B({\mathcal{H}}_{BC})\}.

(4)

Since
\sigma_{BC}
has full rank, we have
{\mathcal{K}}_{BC}={\mathcal{H}}_{BC}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{\overline{BC}}
.
For each
\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}
we now define a linear operator
R^{X}(\xi):{\mathcal{K}}_{X}\to{\mathcal{H}}
via

\displaystyle R^{X}(\xi)a_{X}\Omega_{\sigma}=a_{X}\xi,\quad a_{X}\in B({\mathcal{H}}_{X}),

(5)

where
X=C
or
X=BC
.
Evidently,
R^{X}(\xi)a_{X}=a_{X}R^{X}(\xi)
for
a_{X}\in B({\mathcal{H}}_{X})
. Moreover,
R^{C}(\xi)

is simply the restriction of
R^{BC}(\xi)
to
{\mathcal{K}}_{C}
.
Denoting by
P
the orthogonal projection onto
{\mathcal{K}}_{C}
(which commutes with the action of
B({\mathcal{H}}_{C})
on
{\mathcal{K}}_{BC}
), we thus find

\displaystyle\theta^{C}(\xi,\xi):=R^{C}(\xi)R^{C}(\xi)^{*}=R^{BC}(\xi)PR^{BC}(\xi)^{*}\leq R^{BC}(\xi)R^{BC}(\xi)^{*}=:\theta^{BC}(\xi,\xi),

(6)

where
\theta^{C}(\xi,\xi)\in B({\mathcal{H}}_{C})^{\prime}=B({\mathcal{H}}_{AB})
and
\theta^{BC}(\xi,\xi)\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{BC})^{\prime}=B({\mathcal{H}}_{A})
.
A useful way to explicitly represent
R^{X}
and
\theta^{X}
is via tensor-network diagrams (which we draw from right to left):

\displaystyle R^{BC}(\xi)=\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[]{figures/R.pdf}}}\quad\implies\quad\theta^{BC}(\xi,\xi)=\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[]{figures/theta.pdf}}}\ ,

(7)

where an arc denotes an (unnormalized) maximally entangled state.
Inspecting the diagram for
\theta^{BC}(\xi,\xi)
we see that indeed

\displaystyle\theta^{BC}(\xi,\xi)=\tr_{BC}\big(\sigma_{BC}^{-1/2}|\xi\rangle\langle\xi|\sigma_{BC}^{-1/2}\big).

(8)

The isometry
V:{\mathcal{K}}_{C}\to{\mathcal{K}}_{BC}
such that
P=VV^{*}
can be written as

\displaystyle V=\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[]{figures/V.pdf}}}\ ,

(9)

which leads to

\displaystyle\theta^{C}(\xi,\xi)=\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[]{figures/theta-C-P.pdf}}}=\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[]{figures/theta-C.pdf}}}\ .

Hence

\displaystyle\theta^{C}(\xi,\xi)=\tr_{C}\big(\sigma_{C}^{-1/2}|\xi\rangle\langle\xi|\sigma_{C}^{-1/2}\big),

(10)

showing (
2
).
Note we only needed to use the inequality
APA^{*}\leq AA^{*}
, which is true for arbitrary operators
A
and projections
P
.

2
General case

We now discuss the general case of inclusions of von Neumann algebras
N\subset M
on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
.
We directly formulate the results in terms of
weights
, which are positive, but generally unbounded, linear functionals on a von Neumann algebra that generalize the notion of a state on a von Neumann algebra. They can be thought of as non-commutative versions of measures on an infinite measure space (e.g., the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
). We will assume familiarity with the basic terminology for weights, see for example
[
6
]
.

Let
\phi,\psi
be normal semifinite (ns) weights on
N^{\prime}
and
M
, respectively.
We assume that the restrictions
\phi|_{M^{\prime}}
and
\psi|_{N}
to the respective subalgebras
N\subset M
and
M^{\prime}\subset N^{\prime}
are again semifinite (and hence ns weights) and that
\phi
is faithful.
The setting of the introduction corresponds to
N=B({\mathcal{H}}_{A})\subset M=B({\mathcal{H}}_{A}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{B})\subset B({\mathcal{H}}_{A}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{B}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{C})
and
\psi=\tr(\rho_{AB}{\,\cdot\,})
,
\phi=\tr(\sigma_{BC}{\,\cdot\,})
are normal states on the respective algebras.

This section aims to explain how the spatial theory of von Neumann algebras, developed by Connes in
[
4
]
, gives rise to the operator inequality

\frac{d\psi}{d\phi|_{M^{\prime}}}\,\leq\,\frac{d\psi|_{N}}{d\phi},

(11)

where the derivatives denote Connes’ spatial derivatives, explained below.
In the setting of finite-dimensional quantum information theory discussed above the spatial derivatives are
d\psi/d(\phi|_{M^{\prime}})=\rho_{AB}\otimes\sigma_{C}^{-1}
and
d(\psi|_{N})/d\phi=\rho_{A}\otimes\sigma_{BC}^{-1}
, so that (
11
) reduces to (
1
).

We need to recall some preliminaries.
For now, we fix a von Neumann algebra
A
on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
together with a normal semifinite faithful (nsf) weight
\omega
.
We will later consider the cases
(A,\omega)=(N^{\prime},\phi)
and
(A,\omega)=(M^{\prime},\phi|_{M^{\prime}})
, in the notation introduced above.

We denote the GNS representation by
(\pi_{\omega},L^{2}(A,\omega),\eta_{\omega})
.
Here,
L^{2}(A,\omega)
is defined as the Hilbert space completion of the left-ideal
\mathfrak{n}_{\omega}=\{a\in A:\omega(a^{*}a)<\infty\}\subset A
with respect to the inner product
(a,b)\mapsto\omega(a^{*}b)
,
\eta_{\omega}:\mathfrak{n}_{\omega}\to L^{2}(A,\omega)
denotes the induced injection, and
\pi_{\omega}
denotes the representation defined by
\pi_{\omega}(a)\eta_{\omega}(b)=\eta_{\omega}(ab)
.
If
\omega
is a normal faithful state, then
1\in\mathfrak{n_{\omega}}
, the GNS vector is given as
\Omega_{\omega}=\eta_{\omega}(1)
and
\eta_{\omega}=\pi_{\omega}({\,\cdot\,})\Omega_{\omega}
.

Following
[
4
]
, we use the GNS representation to define, for each
\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}
, an operator
R^{\omega}(\xi):L^{2}(A,\omega)\supset D(R^{\omega}(\xi))\to{\mathcal{H}}
by

D(R^{\omega}(\xi))=\eta_{\omega}(\mathfrak{n}_{\omega})
,
R^{\omega}(\xi)\eta_{\omega}(a)=a\xi
,
a\in\mathfrak{n}_{\omega}
.
We note the following intertwining relation:

aa^{\prime}R^{\omega}(\xi)=R^{\omega}(a^{\prime}\xi)\pi_{\omega}(a),\qquad a\in A,\ a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}.

(12)

A vector
\xi
is called
\omega
-bounded if
R^{\omega}(\xi)
is a bounded operator, i.e., there is a constant
C_{\xi}>0
such that
\lVert a\xi\rVert\leq C_{\xi}\,\omega(a^{*}a)^{1/2}
for all
a\in A
.
The set of
\omega
-bounded vectors is a dense subspace in
{\mathcal{H}}

[
7
, Lemma 11.2]
. If
\xi
is
\omega
-bounded we denote the bounded extension to an operator
L^{2}(A,\omega)\to{\mathcal{H}}
again by
R^{\omega}(\xi)
, and we set

\theta^{\omega}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=R^{\omega}(\xi_{1})R^{\omega}(\xi_{2})^{*}.

(13)

for a pair
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}
of
\omega
-bounded vectors.
Note that
\theta^{\omega}
is linear in the first and conjugate linear in the second entry.
Importantly, (
12
) implies that
\theta^{\omega}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
is an element of
A^{\prime}
.
Let us also note that
\theta^{\omega}(\xi,\xi)\geq 0
.

Before we describe the spatial derivatives appearing in (
11
), we prove the von Neumann algebraic version of (
2
) from which we will later infer (
11
).
In fact, we will see that both inequalities are equivalent.

Lemma 1
.

Let
B\subset A
be a von Neumann subalgebra such that
\omega|_{B}
is semifinite (this is automatic if
\omega
is a normal faithful state).
Then every
\omega
-bounded vector
\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}
is
\omega|_{B}
-bounded and we have

\theta^{\omega|_{\hskip-0.5ptB}}(\xi,\xi)\leq\theta^{\omega}(\xi,\xi).

(14)

To prove
Lemma

1
, we note that in the setting of the Lemma, the inclusion
B\hookrightarrow A
induces an inclusion on the level of GNS spaces
L^{2}(B,\omega|_{B})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(A,\omega)
.
We can regard
L^{2}(B,\omega|_{B})
as a closed subspace of
L^{2}(A,\omega)
.
We have
\eta_{\omega|_{B}}(a)=P\eta_{\omega}(a)
for
a\in\mathfrak{n}_{\omega|_{B}}
and
\pi_{\omega|_{B}}(b)=P\pi_{\omega}(b)=\pi_{\omega}(b)P
,
b\in B
, where
P\in\pi_{\omega}(B)^{\prime}
denotes the orthogonal projection of
L^{2}(A,\omega)
onto
L^{2}(B,\omega|_{B})
.
It thus follows that
R^{\omega|_{B}}(\xi)=R^{\omega}(\xi)P
, which shows that
\xi
is
\omega|_{B}
-bounded if it is
\omega
-bounded.

The monotonicity (
14
) is now an immediate consequence:

\theta^{\omega|_{\hskip-0.5ptB}}(\xi,\xi)=R^{\omega|_{\hskip-0.5ptB}}(\xi)R^{\omega|_{\hskip-0.5ptB}}(\xi)^{*}=R^{\omega|_{\hskip-0.5ptB}}(\xi)PR^{\omega|_{\hskip-0.5ptB}}(\xi)^{*}\leq R^{\omega}(\xi)R^{\omega}(\xi)^{*}=\theta^{\omega}(\xi,\xi).

This concludes the proof of
Lemma

1
.

We now come to the definition of Connes’ spatial derivatives.
For this, we consider a von Neumann algebra
M
, and we consider an ns weight
\psi
on
M
and an nsf weight
\phi
on
M^{\prime}
.
We obtain a quadratic form
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\mapsto\psi(\theta^{\phi}(\xi_{2},\xi_{1}))
, defined on the subspace of
\phi
-bounded vectors.
This form is closable, and the positive self-adjoint operator corresponding to its closure is the spatial derivative
d\psi/d\phi
:

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{1},\frac{d\psi}{d\phi}\xi_{2}\rangle=\psi(\theta^{\phi}(\xi_{2},\xi_{1})).

(15)

The spatial derivatives are useful tools that allow one to apply modular theory in other representations than the standard representation, in which the spatial derivative reduces to the relative modular operator:

\frac{d\psi}{d\phi}=\Delta_{\psi|\phi^{\top}},

where
\phi^{\top}
denotes the nsf weight
x\mapsto\phi(Jx^{*}J)
on
M
, where
J
is the modular conjugation associated with the standard form of
M

[
8
]
, which fulfills
JMJ=M^{\prime}
.

Applying
Lemma

1
to the case
A=N^{\prime}
,
B=M^{\prime}
,
\omega=\phi
, and evaluating the resulting operator inequality with the weight
\psi
, we get:

Corollary 2
.

Let
N\subset M
be von Neumann algebras on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
.
Let
\psi
be an ns weight on
M
and let
\phi
be an nsf weight on
N^{\prime}
.
If the restrictions
\psi|_{N}
and
\phi|_{M^{\prime}}
are semifinite, then (
11
) holds.

We have deduced (
11
) from (
14
).
As a final remark, we note that the two inequalities are, in fact, equivalent.
Indeed, in the setting of
Lemma

1
,
Eq.

11
implies

\langle\chi,\theta^{\omega|_{\hskip-0.5ptB}}(\xi,\xi)\chi\rangle=\langle\xi,\frac{d\langle\chi,{\,\cdot\,}\chi\rangle|_{B^{\prime}}}{d\omega|_{B}}\xi\rangle\leq\langle\xi,\frac{d\langle\chi,{\,\cdot\,}\chi\rangle|_{A^{\prime}}}{d\omega}\xi\rangle=\langle\chi,\theta^{\omega}(\xi,\xi)\chi\rangle,\qquad\chi\in{\mathcal{H}},

(16)

and, thus, (
14
) implies (
11
) as well.

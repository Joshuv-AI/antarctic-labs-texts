Abstract
We define the families of Kuramoto models (KM) related to bounded symmetric domains. The families include the Lohe unitary model and the spherical models as special cases. Our approach is based on the construction proposed by Watanabe and Strogats WS. We replace the Poincare disc and its S 1 S^{1} boundary in the WS construction on the bounded symmetric domains and on the its Bergman-Shilov (BS) boundaries. In Cartan classifications there are four classical domains of types I-IV. Here we consider the domains of types I,II and III. For a fixed domain there is a decreasing chain of the BS boundaries components. This leads to the KM families we described here.

Let us schematically define

(m,n)

Consider the set of rectangular matrices

It is a diffeomorphism of

The action of group

As in that example the center

(m)

By this action

Therefore, the components are equivalent to the type I domains

This component can be described as the

It follows from this definition that the BS boundary is the quotient

This quotient is

Equivalently, the component

It follows from (

Summarizing,

In the following to denote the components we will use their

By means of (

Consider the boundaries of

(t=1,\ldots,n-1)

Thus, the domain

3.2
Kuramoto families

Define a flow on the domain
D^{I}_{mn}
(
3.1
) corresponding to the Lie algebra action (
3.17
)

\dot{\bf z}=J^{z}{\bf z}

\dot{\bf z}=b+a{\bf z}-{\bf z}d-{\bf z}b^{\dagger}{\bf z}\,.

(3.27)

Here
(a,b,d)\in{\mathfrak{g}}=
Lie(SU
(m,n))
(
3.7
).
The equation (
3.27
) has the form of the matrix Riccati equation.

Consider
N
oscillators
{\bf z}^{K}\,,~~K=1,\ldots N
on the bounded domain
D^{I}_{mn}
.
In other words we pass to the configuration space

\mathcal{X}=\times_{K=1}^{N}(D_{mn}^{I})^{K}
and to the
K
flows

\dot{\bf z}^{K}=b+a{\bf z}^{K}-{\bf z}^{K}d-{\bf z}^{K}b^{\dagger}{\bf z}^{K}\,,~~K=1,\ldots N\,.

(3.28)

It is a system of non-interacting oscillators. In order for the oscillators to interact with each other
we assume that the matrices
a
,
b
and
d
depend on some mean-field quantities.
In particular, consider the moment

\mathcal{Z}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{J=1}^{N}{\bf z}^{J}\,.

(3.29)

and take
b=\mathcal{Z}
while
a
and
d
remain
{\bf z}^{J}
-independent.
The system (
3.28
) becomes

\dot{\bf z}^{K}=a{\bf z}^{K}-{\bf z}^{K}d+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{J=1}^{N}({\bf z}^{J}-{\bf z}^{K}({\bf z}^{\dagger})^{J}{\bf z}^{K})\,.

(3.30)

\frac{d}{dt}(z^{\alpha}_{j})^{K}=a^{\alpha}_{\beta}(z^{\beta}_{j})^{K}-(z^{\alpha}_{k})^{K}d^{k}_{j}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{J=1}^{N}((z^{\alpha}_{j})^{J}-(z^{\alpha}_{k})^{K}(z^{k}_{\beta})^{J}(z^{\beta}_{j})^{K})

(3.31)

with initial data
(z^{\alpha}_{j})^{K}(t)_{t=0}=(z^{\alpha}_{j})^{K}(p)
.
The Einstein summation rule is assumed in (
3.31
).
We call the system (
3.31
)
the type I Kuramoto model
KM_{mn}(I)
,
or taking into account the dual symmetric space the Grassmannian model Gr
n,m+n
.
For
m=n=1
put
z^{K}=e^{\imath\theta^{K}}
. Then the system (
3.31
) coincides with the Kuramoto system (
1.1
).
This system is the
{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
model.

Due to the Witt theorem
[
2
]
the group
G=
SU
(m,n)
as well as its subgroup SU
(m-t,n-t)
acts
on the components

\mathcal{F}^{mn}_{t}\sim D_{m-t,n-t}
,(
3.19
) of the
boundary.
One can restrict the flow (
3.31
) on them. We
assume that the coefficients
a,b,d
in (
3.28
) belongs to the
subalgebra Lie
(G_{m-t,n-t})
.
These components are in general open. But the Kuramoto type flows should be defined
on compact spaces. For this reason we should restrict the flows on the defined above the BS boundaries or the Stiefel manifolds (
3.26
).
Thus we come to the family of
n
KM(I) models related to the domain
D^{I}_{mn}

KM_{mn}(I)\,,\,KM_{m-1,n-1}(I)\,,\ldots,KM_{m-n+1,1}(I)\,.

(3.32)

Their configuration spaces are the product of the Stiefel manifolds

\times_{J=1}^{N}(St^{\mathbb{C}}_{m-t,n-t})
.
The equations of motion have the form (
3.30
), where
{\bf z}^{K}
are local coordinates
on the Stiefel manifold
St^{\mathbb{C}}_{m-t,n-t}=
U
(m-t)/
U
(m-n)

(t<n)
.

We can pass to some fixed element
KM_{m-t,n-t}
of the family. For this purpose assume that the initial configuration belongs to some component of the boundary

(z^{\alpha}_{j})^{I}(p)\in D_{m-t,n-t}
.
It means that for this configuration the group of symmetries
G_{m,n}

is broken up to the subgroup
G_{m-t,n-t}
.
In this case the flows remain localized at the component

D^{I}_{m-t,n-t}
. The flows on its BS boundary are just
KM_{m-t,n-t}(I)
i.e on the
Stiefel varieties
St^{\mathbb{C}}_{m-t,n-t}
,
\,\dim\,(St^{\mathbb{C}}_{m-t,n-t})=(n-t)(2m-n-t)
.
The symmetry can be broken down step-by-step

G_{mn}\to G_{m-t_{1},n-t_{1}}\to\ldots\to G_{m-t_{k},n--t_{k}}\,.

If
m=n
the Stiefel manifold is the unitary group
St^{\mathbb{C}}_{mn}=
U
(m)
,
the Kuramoto family is the Lohe unitary family. The end of the family is the standard
Kuramoto model
KM_{1,1}(I)
. If
n<m
the family ends with
the
{\mathbb{C}}P^{n}
model or
KM_{m-n,1}(I)
, or the spherical
S^{2n-1}
model.

Consider the Kuramoto flows
KM_{m-t,n-t}(I)
(
3.30
).
It can be rewritten in term of the group element
h\in G_{m-t,n-t}

\dot{z}^{K}=\dot{h}h^{-1}z^{K}\,,~~(K=1,\ldots,N)\,,~~{\bf z}^{K}\in St^{\mathbb{C}}_{m-t,n-t}\,,

(3.33)

if the flow on
G_{m-t,n-t}
has the form

\dot{h}=xh\,,~~x=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&\mathcal{Z}\\
\mathcal{Z}^{\dagger}&d\\
\end{array}\right)\,~~({\rm tr}\,a+{\rm tr}\,d=0)\,.

(3.34)

In this way we come to the reduction from the
N
flows on the Stiefel manifold

St^{\mathbb{C}}_{m-t,n-t}
to the flow on the group
G_{m-t,n-t}

\times_{J=1}^{N}(St^{\mathbb{C}}_{m-t,n-t})\to G_{m-t,n-t}\,.

Note that

\dim\,(\times_{J=1}^{N}(St^{\mathbb{C}}_{m-t,n-t}))=N(n-t)(2m-n-t)
and

\dim\,(G_{m-t,n-t})=(m+n)^{2}-4t(m+n)+4t^{2}-1
.

4.1
Symmetric space

Consider the symmetric space

corresponding to the domain

. This is

with

{}^{*}(2n)
and

(n)

The group SO
{}^{*}(2n)
is a real form of the complex group
SO
(2n,{\mathbb{C}})
. We define the latter as the matrices from SL
(2n,{\mathbb{C}})
preserving the
symmetric form
s_{1}

gs_{1}g^{T}=s_{1}\,,~~s_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&Id_{n}\\

\end{array}\right)\,.

It means that the matrices from SO
(2n,{\mathbb{C}})

g=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
C&D\\
\end{array}\right)\,,~~A^{T}C=-C^{T}A\,,~D^{T}B=-B^{T}D\,,~A^{T}D+C^{T}B=Id_{n}\,.

(4.2)

The real subgroup SO
{}^{*}(2n)

gs_{2}g^{\dagger}=s_{2}\,,~~s_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-Id_{n}&0\\
0&Id_{n}\\
\end{array}\right)\,,

which means that

(2n,{\mathbb{C}})\cap

(n,n)

In this way the group SO

is a fixed point set in the group SO

6
Concluding remarks

There are few aspects that have not discussed here.

It would be interesting to understand whether such properties
as synchronisation phenomena hold in general for the type I, II and III Kuramoto models.
It seems that the simplest case to test this is the type I Kuramoto model Gr
(2,5)
model or
KM^{I}_{23}
.
It contains the BS boundary St
{}^{\mathbb{C}}_{32}\sim
U
(3)/
U
(1)\sim
SU
(3)
.
The Kuramoto models on this configuration space were not considered before.
Also it would be interesting to analyzed
the impact of the involutions described for the type II and III Kuramoto models.

We have not carried out a study of the Kuramoto models related to
the Cartan domain of type IV as well as for
the two exceptional domains of types V and VI. In particular,
the group action for the type IV domains is no longer the Möbius transform. For this
reason the Kuramoto equations should be different from the equations (
3.30
),(
4.14
) and (
5.10
).

We have not considered here the limit
N\to\infty
. In this case the ensemble of oscillators
on the BS boundary is replaced on some distribution that evolves in time. The Poisson kernel
associated with the
corresponding domain allows one to construct special distributions on the domain itself. We plan to return
to this problem later.

Enhancing workflow efficiency with a modified Firefly Algorithm for hybrid cloud edge environments
Authors: Deafallah Alsadie, Musleh Alsulami
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractEfficient scheduling of scientific workflows in hybrid cloud-edge environments is crucial for optimizing resource utilization and minimizing completion time. In this study, we evaluate various scheduling algorithms, emphasizing the Modified Firefly Optimization Algorithm (ModFOA) and comparing it with established methods such as Ant Colony Optimization (ACO), Genetic Algorithm (GA), and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO). We investigate key performance metrics, including makespan, resource utilization, and energy consumption, across both cloud and edge configurations. Scientific workflows often involve complex tasks with dependencies, which can challenge traditional scheduling algorithms. While existing methods show promise, they may not fully address the unique demands of hybrid cloud-edge environments, potentially leading to suboptimal outcomes. Our proposed ModFOA integrates cloud and edge computing resources, offering an effective solution for scheduling workflows in these hybrid environments. Through comparative analysis, ModFOA demonstrates improved performance in reducing makespan and completion times, while maintaining competitive resource utilization and energy efficiency. This study highlights the importance of incorporating cloud-edge integration in scheduling algorithms and showcases ModFOA’s potential to enhance workflow efficiency and resource management across hybrid environments. Future research should focus on refining ModFOA’s parameters and validating its effectiveness in practical hybrid cloud-edge scenarios.Keywords: Scientific workflows, Cloud computing, Scheduling algorithms, Firefly Optimization Algorithm, Resource utilizationSubject terms: Mathematics and computing, Computer science

Introduction

Scientific research is increasingly relying on complex workflows, sequences of computational tasks where each step depends on the previous1. The efficiency of scheduling these workflows is crucial as it directly impacts productivity by minimizing the overall duration or makespan. However, optimizing workflow scheduling involves balancing resource utilization and mitigating data transfer bottlenecks between various nodes2–5.

Cloud computing offers scalability and flexibility, allowing researchers to dynamically adjust resources to meet workflow demands6,7. However, cloud usage can lead to high costs and latency issues, as extensive data transfer between cloud servers can hinder performance8–10.

The hybrid cloud-edge paradigm presents a promising solution by integrating the extensive storage and computational power of the cloud with the real-time processing capabilities of edge computing6,11. This approach decentralizes tasks between cloud and edge nodes, improving performance by reducing latency and leveraging localized processing11–13. This hybrid model enables more efficient resource allocation and workload distribution, optimizing both performance and cost14–16.

However, the dynamic nature of hybrid cloud edge environments requires advanced scheduling algorithms to effectively manage the complexities of resource allocation and data transfer. Traditional algorithms, such as Shortest Job First (SJF) or Heterogeneous Earliest Finish Time (HEFT), may not adapt well to the unique challenges of these environments17,18. Metaheuristic algorithms such as genetic algorithms and particle swarm optimization also fail to address these complexities19,20.

The Firefly Algorithm (FA), inspired by the behavior of fireflies, offers a promising approach due to its adaptive nature and the ability to explore complex solution spaces effectively21. To address the specific needs of hybrid cloud-edge environments, we propose modifications to FA, including adjustments to solution representation, fitness function design, and movement mechanisms to account for cloud and edge resource characteristics. These enhancements aim to improve the effectiveness of the algorithm in scheduling workflows within this hybrid paradigm21–23.

Related work

The surge in data volume and the quest for faster processing have elevated task scheduling in cloud computing to a critical position. To address this challenge, a variety of methods have been developed with the goal of reducing task completion times by executing sub-tasks simultaneously while maintaining their predetermined sequence. Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs) have become a popular choice for representing task dependencies, portraying tasks as nodes and depicting dependencies as directed edges. This graphical strategy facilitates efficient scheduling of diverse workloads, striking a balance between comprehensive functionality and computational efficiency.

Kakkottakath et al.22 introduced an improved version of the ant-lion optimization algorithm (ALO), which was combined with the particle swarm optimization (PSO) algorithm to optimize workflow scheduling specifically tailored for cloud computing environments. To enhance security, the Data Encryption Standard (DES) approach was employed for encrypting cloud information during the scheduling process. The main objective of this research was to develop a more secure workflow scheduling framework compared to existing methods. The effectiveness of the enhancements was evaluated based on cost, load balancing, and makespan metrics. Experiments were performed using the CloudSim tool to simulate the proposed hybrid optimization approach, which was then benchmarked against well-known methods including round-robin (RR), ALO, and PSO. The results demonstrated that the proposed technique achieved significant reductions in cost, load balancing, and makespan compared to the existing methods, with improvements ranging from 8% to 45%. Moreover, the proposed method demonstrated satisfactory performance in terms of both energy consumption and reliability.

Ijaz et al.23 introduced a fresh scheduling algorithm, termed Energy Makespan Multi-Objective Optimization, with a two-phase operation. Initially, they formulated the problem as a multi-objective optimization task, aiming to strike a balance between conflicting objectives while assigning fog and cloud resources. Subsequently, they allocated latency-sensitive tasks (with lower computational demands) to fog resources and computationally intensive tasks (with strict latency requirements) to cloud resources. Moreover, they incorporated the Deadline-Aware stepwise Frequency Scaling technique to further reduce energy consumption by utilizing idle time slots between two consecutively scheduled tasks on a single node. Their evaluation, encompassing both synthesized and real-world applications, illustrated that their approach significantly decreased energy consumption by up to 50% compared to existing methods, with negligible impact on completion times.

Mokni et al.18 introduced a hybrid Cloud-Fog multi-agent strategy for scheduling a sequence of interdependent IoT tasks depicted as a workflow. The notable strength of their approach is its capacity to conceptualize IoT workflow planning as a multi-objective optimization challenge, enabling the derivation of a well-rounded planning solution that factors in response time, cost, and makespan. Besides accounting for data communications between workflow tasks during the planning phase, their approach offers two additional benefits: (1) maximizing the utilization of Fog Computing to minimize response time, and (2) leveraging elastic cloud computing resources at minimal cost. They implemented the MAS-GA (Multi-Agent System based Genetic Algorithm) within this framework and conducted a series of experiments on various datasets. The analysis of the acquired results validated the feasibility and effectiveness of their methodology. Particularly noteworthy is their approach’s performance in terms of cost, exhibiting an average enhancement of 21.38% compared to Fog and 25.49% compared to Cloud. It also achieved a 14.13% improvement in makespan compared to Fog and a slight 5.24% increase compared to Cloud. Furthermore, their method bolstered response time by an average improvement of 46.66% compared to Cloud, with a marginal 6.66% increase compared to Fog, thus promoting collaboration between Fog computing and Cloud computing.

Further, the authors proposed a novel approach that integrates partitioning, sequencing, and scheduling algorithms to optimize conflicting requirements of users and providers in a multi-objective framework24. They integrate distributed resolution through a Multi-Agent System (MAS) to alleviate problem complexity and utilize a fuzzy inference system to tackle uncertainty. To evaluate their approach, they conducted experiments using various datasets and compared it against existing approaches. The results demonstrated the effectiveness of their approach, showcasing significant improvements over studied methods. Their solution notably attained a 48% decrease in Makespan compared to the Energy Makespan Multi-Objective Optimization (EM-MOO) approach and a 41% reduction compared to the Multi-Agent System-based Genetic Algorithm (MAS-GA), all within the specified time constraints.

Javaheri et al.25 introduced an architecture featuring multiple fog computing providers and introduced a Hidden Markov Model (HMM) to forecast the availability of each fog computing provider. This prediction relied on factors such as incoming request volumes, workflow missed deadlines, and tasks offloaded from fogs to cloud computing. They trained the HMM model using the unsupervised Baum-Welch algorithm and computed fog provider availability probabilities using the Viterbi algorithm. Subsequently, these probabilities were utilized for fog computing provider selection and IoT workflow scheduling. Additionally, they improved the Harris Hawks Optimization (HHO) algorithm and proposed a Discrete Opposition-based variant, known as DO-HHO, tailored for scheduling scientific workflows. Through extensive experiments conducted using iFogSim, they demonstrated that their proposed approach significantly reduced the number of tasks offloaded to cloud computing, instances of missed workflow deadlines, and SLA violations, outperforming existing methods.

Xu et al.26 introduced a fresh problem model for DWSFC (Dynamic Workflow Scheduling in Fog Computing) and unveiled a new simulator tailored precisely for this model. The simulator considered mobile devices, edge, and cloud servers as an integrated system capable of task execution. It incorporated two types of decision points: routing decision points and sequencing decision points. To tackle this issue, a pioneering approach called Multi-Tree Genetic Programming (MTGP) was devised to autonomously evolve scheduling heuristics capable of making real-time decisions at these junctures. The MTGP method, as proposed, employs a multi-tree representation, enabling it to concurrently manage routing and sequencing decision points. Experimental results demonstrated that the proposed MTGP outperformed existing state-of-the-art methods, achieving significantly improved test performance with up to a 50% reduction in makespan across all tested scenarios.

Choudhary and Rajak9 introduced a modified version of the Min-Min heuristic that incorporates load-balancing principles to optimize both task completion time and resource load distribution in workflow scheduling. Through extensive simulations utilizing various benchmark workflow datasets, including randomly generated and Montage workflows, they evaluated the efficacy of the proposed load-balancing approach. The outcomes revealed that the modified Min-Min heuristic surpassed existing heuristics such as HEFT and PETS across several performance metrics, including load balancing, makespan, speedup, efficiency, and resource utilization. By achieving a more equitable allocation of resources and reducing workflow completion time, the proposed method enhanced overall system performance within cloud computing environments. This study significantly contributes to the field of workflow scheduling in Cloud Computing Environments (CCE) by introducing a load-balancing approach that enhances resource allocation efficiency. The findings emphasize the significance of integrating load-balancing principles into task scheduling strategies to optimize performance within cloud computing environments. The proposed method provides a valuable solution for practitioners and researchers involved in workflow scheduling within CCE, facilitating improved resource utilization and reduced execution time.

Nazeri et al.27 introduced a predictive energy-aware scheduling framework structured around a MAPE-K control model, which comprises the Monitor, Analyzer, Planner, and Executer components with a shared Knowledge base in fog computing. Initially, they introduced a prediction method utilizing an Adaptive Network-based Fuzzy Inference System (ANFIS) within the Analyzer component to forecast future resource load. Subsequently, they presented a resource management strategy based on the predicted resource load to mitigate energy consumption. Lastly, they integrated the Improved Ant Lion Optimizer (ALO) and weighted Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO) into a planner component termed I-ALO-GWO for workflow scheduling. Finally, decisions derived from the preceding steps were executed on the fog cells. The I-ALO-GWO evolutionary algorithm’s effectiveness underwent evaluation using the IEEE CEC2019 benchmark functions, while the proposed approach underwent practical assessment on numerous renowned scientific workflows utilizing the iFogSim tool. Experimental outcomes showcased that I-ALO-GWO elevated the makespan by up to 18%, curtailed energy consumption by up to 17%, slashed total execution cost by up to 11%, and boosted efficiency by 26% when juxtaposed with the second-best results.

Khaleel12 devised a methodology to address scientific workflow scheduling by formulating it as a multi-objective optimization problem, aiming to strike a balance between energy consumption and scheduling reliability. This approach unfolded in two phases. Initially, fog resources were designated for tasks with lower computational demands, while cloud resources were tasked with computationally intensive assignments, all while avoiding resources with elevated failure rates. Subsequently, the Reliability-Aware stepwise Performance-to-Power Ratio method was employed to further reduce energy usage. This method involved sampling the utilization levels of machines using a distinguished Performance-to-Power Ratio (PPR), calculated as the ratio of transactions executed at a specific time to the active power consumed. Simulation outcomes, conducted on both synthesized and real-world workflow applications, demonstrated that our approach (referred to as RMEE) achieved significantly higher reliability values under constrained energy conditions compared to several competing workflow mapping algorithms.

Dougani and Dennai28 introduced a pioneering approach named Bandwidth-Aware Workflow Allocation (BW-AWA), which prioritizes task scheduling based on resource bandwidth while simultaneously minimizing the total execution time across the heterogeneous computing system, all without compromising on power usage. Task selection considered task dependencies to ensure efficient allocation. We validated our approach through a case study on road safety, comparing the results with established methods to showcase its effectiveness in reducing Makespan.

Shukla and Pandey7 presented a technique termed Differential Evolution-Grey Wolf Optimization (DE-GWO) designed to enhance the scheduling of scientific workflows within cloud-fog environments.. The DE-GWO algorithm addressed issues of slow convergence and low accuracy inherent in the classical GWO algorithm by incorporating the Differential Evolution (DE) method as the evolutionary pattern of wolves. Additionally, a weighted sum based objective function was formulated, integrating makespan, cost, and energy consumption criteria. The DE-GWO technique was evaluated and compared with various conventional and hybrid optimization algorithms using five scientific workflow datasets including Montage, Cybershake, Epigenomics, LIGO, and SIPHT. Results demonstrated superior performance of the DE-GWO algorithm across multiple scientific workflows and performance criteria compared to conventional algorithms. This approach demonstrated competitiveness by harnessing the evolutionary and elimination mechanisms of DE while concurrently maintaining a balance between exploration and exploitation, a characteristic of GWO.

Adhikari et al.21 proposed a novel workflow scheduling strategy utilizing the Firefly algorithm (FA) to address multiple conflicting objectives such as cloud server workload, makespan, resource utilization, and reliability. The FA aimed to allocate suitable cloud servers for each workflow, ensuring their requirements were met while balancing server loads and resource utilization. Additionally, a rule-based approach was devised to assign tasks to appropriate VM instances, minimizing workflow makespan while meeting deadlines. We evaluated the proposed strategy using Google cluster traces through various simulation runs and thoroughly investigated FA’s control parameters for enhanced performance. Experimental analysis showcased that our strategy surpassed state-of-the-art algorithms across various Quality-of-Service (QoS) parameters, encompassing makespan, reliability, resource utilization, and cloud server loads.

Preliminary concepts

This section serves as an introduction to the fundamental concepts necessary for grasping the discussions that follow regarding scientific workflow scheduling and optimization algorithms. It covers the basics of scientific workflows and their scheduling, the modeling of workflows using Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs), and an overview of the Firefly Optimization Algorithm (FOA). Understanding these foundational concepts is crucial for comprehending the intricacies involved in scientific workflow scheduling and the application of optimization algorithms in cloud computing environments.

Scientific workflow

Scientific workflows are the backbone of computational research, orchestrating a sequence of tasks that transform raw data into valuable insights across a myriad of disciplines21,29. In the evolving landscape of cloud-edge computing, these workflows take on a new dimension of complexity and opportunity. Cloud-edge environments seamlessly integrate centralized cloud servers with decentralized edge devices, offering a distributed computing paradigm that optimizes resource utilization and enhances computational capabilities. Within this framework, scientific workflows are essential for leveraging the combined strengths of both cloud and edge resources.

Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs) serve as a powerful representation of scientific workflows in cloud-edge computing, capturing the intricate dependencies between tasks and facilitating efficient task allocation across diverse computing nodes30. These workflows play a pivotal role in addressing the escalating computational demands of modern research endeavors. By breaking down complex problems into smaller, more manageable tasks, workflows enable researchers to navigate the vast expanse of data and computational resources available in cloud-edge environments.

The need for scientific workflows in cloud-edge computing arises from the ever-increasing complexity and scale of computational tasks in contemporary research27. These workflows provide a structured approach to problem-solving, allowing researchers to systematically analyze data, run simulations, and derive meaningful insights. Moreover, by leveraging both cloud and edge resources, workflows offer unparalleled scalability and flexibility, enabling researchers to adapt their computational processes to evolving research requirements.

There are various types of scientific workflows tailored to specific research domains and computational demands within cloud-edge environments25,30. Linear workflows follow a sequential execution pattern, where each task depends on the completion of the preceding one. Branching workflows introduce conditional execution paths, branching out based on intermediate results or external conditions. Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs) offer a more sophisticated representation, allowing for complex task dependencies and parallel execution of independent tasks, thereby maximizing computational efficiency.

The benefits of employing scientific workflows in cloud-edge computing are manifold31,32. These workflows optimize resource utilization, ensuring efficient allocation of computing resources across both cloud and edge environments. They also offer scalability, enabling researchers to dynamically adjust resource allocation to meet fluctuating computational demands. Moreover, by exploiting the proximity to data sources and real-time processing capabilities of edge devices, workflows minimize latency and enhance responsiveness, facilitating rapid data analysis and decision-making.

However, scheduling scientific workflows in cloud-edge environments presents several challenges. Resource heterogeneity across cloud and edge nodes requires sophisticated scheduling algorithms capable of optimizing resource allocation based on computational requirements and constraints32,33. Data transfer overhead between cloud and edge nodes introduces latency and bandwidth constraints, necessitating efficient data management strategies to minimize performance degradation. Furthermore, the dynamic nature of workflows poses challenges for static scheduling approaches, demanding algorithms capable of adapting to changing conditions and optimizing resource allocation in real-time.

Addressing these challenges requires the development of advanced scheduling algorithms specifically tailored to the unique characteristics of scientific workflows in cloud-edge environments31,33. These algorithms must strike a delicate balance between optimizing makespan, resource utilization, and data transfer overhead, ultimately enabling researchers to fully leverage the potential of cloud-edge computing for scientific discovery and innovation.

Scientific workflow scheduling

Scientific workflow scheduling is pivotal for optimizing computational tasks, enhancing resource utilization, and meeting performance objectives21,34. Various algorithms contribute to this process by determining task execution order, managing dependencies, and allocating resources effectively. However, each approach comes with its own set of advantages and disadvantages.

Heuristic algorithms offer simplicity and ease of implementation, serving as baseline approaches for comparison with more complex algorithms35. While lacking optimality, they provide minimal computational overhead and are suitable for scenarios with straightforward scheduling requirements. However, heuristic algorithms may struggle to handle complex dependencies or dynamic workloads, limiting their scalability and effectiveness in large-scale or heterogeneous computing environments. On the other hand, metaheuristic algorithms offer flexibility and adaptability to diverse optimization objectives and constraints17. They excel at handling large-scale and combinatorial optimization problems, providing robustness and scalability for complex workflows and distributed computing environments. Despite their computational overhead, metaheuristic algorithms deliver near-optimal solutions and can effectively adapt to changing workload conditions. However, parameter tuning and optimization technique selection may be necessary to achieve optimal performance. Workflow-specific algorithms are tailored to the unique characteristics and constraints of scientific workflows, leveraging domain-specific knowledge to optimize task execution and handle data dependencies effectively36. While providing better performance and resource utilization for specific research domains, they may lack flexibility outside of the targeted domain and require expertise in workflow design and optimization for effective implementation. Machine learning-based approaches offer the potential for automation and self-optimization of scheduling strategies by learning from past scheduling decisions and adapting to changing workload conditions4. They can capture complex patterns and relationships in workflow data, providing insights for efficient scheduling. However, they require large amounts of training data and computational resources for model training, and the interpretability of decision-making processes may be limited. In conclusion, the selection of an appropriate scheduling algorithm depends on the specific requirements, characteristics, and constraints of the scientific workflow and computing environment. While each approach offers distinct advantages, researchers must carefully consider trade-offs between optimization objectives, computational complexity, and scalability to achieve efficient and effective workflow scheduling.

This involves formulating the problem to consider essential metrics such as makespan, flowtime, and cost, each representing different aspects of workflow execution.

Makespan (Cmax): Makespan indicates the total time required to complete all tasks within the workflow. When considering the application of the Firefly Optimization Algorithm to scientific workflow scheduling in cloud-edge computing environments, several strengths and limitations become apparent. One of its key strengths lies in its ability to efficiently search for optimal solutions in complex and dynamic optimization landscapes, which aligns well with the intricacies of scheduling scientific workflows. The algorithm’s simplicity in implementation also proves advantageous in environments where computational resources may be limited or heterogeneous. Moreover, its fast convergence properties can facilitate the timely scheduling of workflows, particularly in scenarios where real-time processing is crucial, such as edge computing environments.

However, the Firefly Optimization Algorithm’s performance may be sensitive to certain factors inherent in cloud-edge computing settings. Moreover, the algorithm’s effectiveness may diminish in high-dimensional optimization spaces, which are common in scientific workflow scheduling problems involving numerous tasks and dependencies. In summary, while the Firefly Optimization Algorithm offers promising capabilities for optimizing scientific workflow scheduling in cloud-edge computing environments, its application must be carefully considered in light of the specific characteristics and constraints of these environments. By leveraging its strengths and addressing its limitations through tailored adaptations and parameter tuning, the algorithm can contribute to the efficient and effective scheduling of scientific workflows in increasingly complex computing landscapes.

Modified Firefly Algorithm for hybrid cloud-edge scheduling

Based on the existing literature, it’s apparent that the original version of the Firefly Algorithm (FA) lacks a clear diversification mechanism31–33. The algorithm endeavors to strike a balance between intensification and exploration primarily through the randomization parameter α, which functions as the third component of the search equation. The magnitude of α significantly influences the algorithm’s global search capability, with larger values emphasizing diversification. In its initial implementation, α remained constant throughout the runtime, typically within the range [0, 1], without considering the decreasing distance between individuals as iterations advance. While some enhanced versions of FA introduce dynamic adjustments to α by gradually decreasing it over time, this alone fails to establish a balanced trade-off between exploitation and exploration, often favoring the former.

Furthermore, addressing these shortcomings requires additional enhancements to the intensification process within the original FA framework. Relying solely on a dynamic parameter α proves insufficient to overcome these deficiencies. Hence, there’s a need for supplementary mechanisms to effectively enhance the intensification aspect of the algorithm.

To overcome the limitations identified in the original Firefly Algorithm (FA) and to enhance the intensification process, our proposed upgraded version integrates several key enhancements. These enhancements encompass the inclusion of genetic operators, particularly uniform crossover and Gaussian mutation, and the integration of a quasi-reflection-based learning mechanism. These modifications aim to address the deficiencies related to the lack of explicit diversification and the imbalance between intensification and diversification inherent in the basic FA, ultimately improving the efficiency of the exploitation process.

The incorporation of genetic operators aims to augment exploration during the initial iterations of the algorithm, especially when the search process has not yet converged to the vicinity of the optimal solution26. This adjustment has a direct impact on the convergence behavior, ensuring a more robust exploration of the solution space. Conclusion

This study presents a comprehensive evaluation of scheduling algorithms for scientific workflows within hybrid cloud-edge computing environments, focusing on key metrics such as makespan, resource utilization, and energy consumption. Through extensive simulations, we compared the Modified Firefly Optimization Algorithm (ModFOA) with Ant Colony Optimization (ACO), Genetic Algorithm (GA), and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO).

The results demonstrate that ModFOA consistently outperforms the other algorithms. For instance, in scenarios with 1000 tasks across 20 hosts and 150 virtual machines, ModFOA achieved a 15% lower makespan compared to ACO, 12% lower than GA, and 18% lower than PSO, highlighting its efficiency in workflow execution. Additionally, ModFOA showed an 85% resource utilization rate, outperforming ACO (80%), GA (78%), and PSO (82%), which is crucial in hybrid environments where resource availability varies.

Energy consumption analysis also favored ModFOA, with it consuming 10% less energy on average compared to ACO, 8% less than GA, and 12% less than PSO. This is particularly important in hybrid cloud-edge environments where energy efficiency is vital.

In conclusion, ModFOA proves to be an effective scheduling algorithm for hybrid cloud-edge environments, offering significant improvements in makespan, resource utilization, and energy efficiency. Future research could refine ModFOA’s parameters and validate its effectiveness in real-world scenarios, further establishing its practical utility in these integrated environments.

ABSTRACT NUMBER: ESOC2026A1823 A DETAILED ANALYSIS OF FIRST PASS REPERFUSION AND NUMBER OF PASSES IN MEVO STROKE THROMBECTOMY
Authors: Alexander Stebner, Salome Bosshart, David Volders, Shahid Nimjee, Dar Dowlatshahi, Mouhammad Jumaa, Markus Moehlenbruch, Michael Hill, Mayank Goyal, Johanna Ospel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and aimsAcute stroke due to medium-vessel occlusion (MeVO) represents a distinct subgroup with unproven benefit of endovascular treatment (EVT). Reperfusion success after EVT and the number of EVT passes may be associated with clinical and imaging outcomes in MeVO strokes.MethodsSecondary analysis of patients randomized to EVT in the ESCAPE-MeVO trial. Final reperfusion was assessed with the MeVO-expanded Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction (MeVO-eTICI) Score. First pass near-complete reperfusion was defined as MeVO-eTICI 2c-3 after 1 pass and multi-pass near-complete reperfusion as MeVO-eTICI 2c-3 after >1 pass. The primary outcome was the modified Rankin Score (mRS) at 90 days. Secondary outcomes included 90-day mRS 0-1, mRS 0-2, mortality, 24-hour infarct volume and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage. Associations were assessed using regression models.ResultsOf 255 patients randomized to EVT (48.3%), 206 remained for the final analysis. First pass near-complete reperfusion was associated with decreased infarct volume (median 12.8ml [IQR 0.15-25.3] versus: 22.4ml [6.71-56.38 ml], adjusted beta-coefficient-1.68 [95%CI:-2.54–(-0.81)]), but not with clinical outcomes. Similarly, improved reperfusion status was associated with lower infarct volumes (adjusted beta-coefficient -0.98[95%CI:-1.38–(-0.57)]) only. Number of EVT passes was associated with higher 90-day mRS (acOR 1.41[95%CI:1.04-1.91] per 1-additional pass) and larger infarct volume (adjusted beta-coefficient 1.12[95%CI:0.63-1.61]).ConclusionsAchieving first pass near-complete reperfusion with EVT in MeVO stroke patients was associated with reduced infarct volumes without resulting in improved clinical outcomes.

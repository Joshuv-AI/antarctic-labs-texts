Some inequalities for the beta function and its ratios
Jean − - Marcel T. Dje, Eyram A. K. Schwinger, Benoit F. Sehba

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove some inequalities for the differences and ratios of the beta function.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 26A48, 33B15; Secondary 26D07, 33C47.

1.
Introduction

Recall that the beta function is defined by

(1)

B(x,y):=\int_{0}^{1}t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-1}dt,\quad x>0,y>0.

A related function is the gamma function defined by

\Gamma(x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{x-1}e^{-t}dt,\quad x>0.

Indeed, it is not difficult to check that

B(x,y)=\frac{\Gamma(x)\Gamma(y)}{\Gamma(x+y)}.

For more information on the above functions and related functions, see
[
1
]
.

An interesting question on the beta function is the problem of finding ’good or best’ functions
f
and
g
such that

(2)

f(x,y)\leq B(x,y)\leq g(x,y)

and recently,

(3)

f(a,b,y_{1},y_{2})\leq\frac{B(b,y_{2})}{B(a,y_{1})}\leq g(a,b,y_{1},y_{2}).

For the first problem (
2
), one of the noticeable first results is the following inequality by S. Dragomir et al.
[
7
, Theorem 3]
:

(4)

\frac{1}{xy}-\frac{1}{4}\leq B(x,y)\leq\frac{1}{xy}.

The above inequality has since been improved and extended by various authors
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
6
,
8
,
9
]
. In this direction, let us mention the following estimates obtained by P. Ivady in
[
10
]
:

(5)

\frac{x+y-xy}{xy}\leq B(x,y)\leq\frac{x+y}{xy(1+xy)}\quad\text{for}\quad 0<x,y\leq 1.

Several one-sided (lower or upper) inequalities were obtained in
[
6
,
8
,
9
]
.

The problem (
3
) was recently considered in
[
8
, Theorem 3.12, 3.16]
where several estimates were proved. Let us mention two of these inequalities:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0<a<b
and
y>0
. Then

(6)

\frac{b^{b-1}(a+y)^{a+y-1}}{a^{a-1}(b+y)^{b+y-1}}\leq\frac{B(b,y)}{B(a,y)}\leq\frac{b^{b}(a+y)^{a+y}}{a^{a}(b+y)^{b+y}}.

Let mention the following result recently proved in
[
5
, Theorem 4.12]
as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
a,b,y>0
with
a<b
, the following holds.

(7)

\frac{b+y}{a+y}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{y+1}<\frac{B(b,y)}{B(a,y)}<\frac{b+y}{a+y}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)\left(\frac{a+y+1}{b+y+1}\right)^{y}.

We recall that the lower bound in (
6
) is better than the one in (
7
) but the inequality between the two upper bounds depends on values of
a,b
and
y
.

The following estimate was also obtained in
[
8
, Theorem 3.17]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
0<a<b<\infty
,
y>0
. Let

c=1-\frac{y}{b-a}\ln\left(\frac{b+y}{a+y}\right),\quad\alpha=\ln c-1,\quad\beta=\frac{1}{c}.

Then

(8)

\frac{B(b,y)}{B(a,y)}\leq e^{(\alpha+\beta)(b-a)}\left(\frac{a+y}{b+y}\right)^{y\beta}.

In this paper, our objective is to prove new inequalities of the form (
3
) that improve (
6
), (
7
), and (
8
), and estimates of the differences of the beta function. By the latter, we mean inequalities of the form

(9)

f(a,b,y)\leq B(b,y)-B(a,y)\leq g(a,b,y),\quad 0<a<b,y>0.

Note that (
2
) can be obtained from (
9
) for some parameters
a,b,y
.

We find ranges of
a,b,y
where our results improve (
6
) and (
7
). We prove this manually. For the analogue of (
8
), using Python, we show in a figure the area of the parameters where our result is an improvement. The associated Python code is provided in Section 5. Our results are presented and proved in the next section.

2.
Main results

A key lemma in this work is the following.

Lemma 2.1

(Generating lemma)
.

Let
k
be a positive integer, and let
h
and
l
be real numbers. Then for
x,y>0
, the following identity holds.

(10)

\int_{0}^{1}t^{x+k-l-1}(1-t)^{y-h}dt+(x+k-l)\int_{0}^{1}t^{x+k-l-1}(1-t)^{y-h}\ln tdt=(y-h)\int_{0}^{1}t^{x+k-l}(1-t)^{y-h-1}\ln tdt.

Proof.

Put

I=\int_{0}^{1}t^{x-l}(1-t)^{y-h}(t^{k}\ln t)^{\prime}dt.

We compute
I
in two different ways. First, we have

(11)

I=\int_{0}^{1}t^{x-l}(1-t)^{y-h}(kt^{k-1}\ln t+t^{k-1})dt.

Second, using integration by parts, we obtain

(12)

I=-(x-l)\int_{0}^{1}t^{x+k-l-1}(1-t)^{y-h}\ln tdt+(y-h)\int_{0}^{1}t^{x+k-l}(1-t)^{y-h-1}\ln tdt.

It follows from (
10
) and (
15
) that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}t^{x-l}(1-t)^{y-h}(kt^{k-1}\ln t+t^{k-1})dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(x-l)\int_{0}^{1}t^{x+k-l-1}(1-t)^{y-h}\ln tdt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(y-h)\int_{0}^{1}t^{x+k-l}(1-t)^{y-h-1}\ln tdt.

Rearranging the terms, we then obtain

\int_{0}^{1}t^{x+k-l-1}(1-t)^{y-h}dt+(x+k-l)\int_{0}^{1}t^{x+k-l-1}(1-t)^{y-h}\ln tdt=(y-h)\int_{0}^{1}t^{x+k-l}(1-t)^{y-h-1}\ln tdt.

∎

We have the following first result.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
0<a<b
. The following hold.

(1)

If
0<y\leq 1
, then

(13)

\left(\frac{b+y}{a+y}\right)^{1-y}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)\leq\frac{B(b,y)}{B(a,y)}\leq\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{y}.

(2)

If
y>1
, then

(14)

\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{y}\leq\frac{B(b,y)}{B(a,y)}\leq\left(\frac{b+y}{a+y}\right)^{1-y}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right).

Proof.

Taking
k=l
and
h=1
in (
10
), we obtain

(15)

\int_{0}^{1}t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-1}dt+x\int_{0}^{1}t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-1}\ln tdt=(y-1)\int_{0}^{1}t^{x}(1-t)^{y-2}\ln tdt.

We assume that
0<y\leq 1
. Then we read the left hand side of (
15
) as

\int_{0}^{1}t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-1}dt+x\int_{0}^{1}t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-1}\ln tdt=B(x,y)+x\partial_{x}B(x,y).

Let

I=(y-1)\int_{0}^{1}t^{x}(1-t)^{y-2}\ln tdt.

We recall that for
0<t\leq 1
,
\frac{t-1}{t}\leq\ln t\leq t-1
. It follows that

-(y-1)\int_{0}^{1}t^{x}(1-t)^{y-1}dt\leq I\leq-(y-1)\int_{0}^{1}t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-1}dt.

We deduce that

(16)

\displaystyle-(y-1)B(x+1,y)\leq B(x,y)+x\partial_{x}B(x,y)\leq-(y-1)B(x,y)

or equivalently,

(17)

-\frac{y-1}{x+y}-\frac{1}{x}\leq\frac{\frac{\partial B(x,y)}{\partial x}}{B(x,y)}\leq-\frac{y}{x}.

Integrating the above equation from
x=a
to
x=b
, we obtain

-(y-1)\ln\left(\frac{b+y}{a+y}\right)-\ln\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)\leq\ln\left(\frac{B(b,y)}{B(a,y)}\right)\leq-y\ln\left(\frac{b}{a}\right).

That is

\left(\frac{b+y}{a+y}\right)^{1-y}\frac{a}{b}\leq\frac{B(b,y)}{B(a,y)}\leq\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{-y}.

In the case
y>1
, since
(y-1)>0
, one has to rather use

-(y-1)\int_{0}^{1}t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-1}dt\leq I\leq-(y-1)\int_{0}^{1}t^{x}(1-t)^{y-1}dt.

The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

(a)

For
0<a<b
and
0<y<1
, we have

\left(\frac{b+y}{a+y}\right)^{1-y}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)>\frac{b^{b-1}(a+y)^{a+y-1}}{a^{a-1}(b+y)^{b+y-1}}

because the function
x\mapsto x\ln\left(1+\frac{y}{x}\right)
is increasing.
Also observing that the function
x\mapsto(x+y)\left[\ln(x+y)-\ln x\right]
is decreasing, one obtains

\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{y}\leq\frac{b^{b}(a+y)^{a+y}}{a^{a}(b+y)^{b+y}}.

Thus (
13
) improves (
6
) when
0<y<1
.

(b)

In the same way, one obtains that for
0<y<1
, and
\frac{1}{y}-y<a<b
,

\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{y}\leq\frac{b+y}{a+y}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)\left(\frac{a+y+1}{b+y+1}\right)^{y}.

Thus (
13
) also improves (
7
) at least for
0<y<1
and
\frac{1}{y}-y<a<b
.

(c)

We have that for
0<a<b<1+\sqrt{y+1}
, and
y\geq 1
,

\left(\frac{b+y}{a+y}\right)^{1-y}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)<\frac{b^{b}(a+y)^{a+y}}{a^{a}(b+y)^{b+y}}.

Thus the upper bound in (
14
) improves the one in (
6
) for
0<a<b<1+\sqrt{y+1}
.

Proof.

The above inequality is equivalent to

1\leq\frac{A(b)}{A(a)}\quad\text{where}\quad A(x)=\frac{x^{x+1}}{(x+y)^{x+1}}.

Put

f(x)=\ln A(x)=(x+1)\left[\ln x-\ln(x+y)\right].

Then

f^{\prime}(x)=\ln x-\ln(x+y)+\frac{1}{x}+\frac{y-1}{x+y}

and

f^{\prime\prime}(x)=\frac{yx^{2}-2xy-y^{2}}{x^{2}(x+y)^{2}}.

For
0<x<1+\sqrt{y+1}
, one has that
f^{\prime\prime}(x)<0
and as
\displaystyle\lim_{x\mapsto\infty}\,f^{\prime}(x)=0
, we conclude that
f^{\prime}(x)>0
. Thus
f(x)
is increasing. Hence
f(b)>f(a)
or equivalently,
\frac{A(b)}{A(a)}>1
. The proof is complete.
∎

(d)

We also obtain that for
0<a<b<\frac{y^{2}-y}{2-y}
with
1<y<2
,

\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{y}\geq\frac{b^{b-1}(a+y)^{a+y-1}}{a^{a-1}(b+y)^{b+y-1}}.

Hence the lower bound in (
14
) is better than the one in (
6
) for this range of the parameters.

Proof.

As above, the inequality is equivalent to

1\leq\frac{A(b)}{A(a)}\quad\text{where}\quad A(x)=\left(\frac{x}{x+y}\right)^{x+y-1}.

Again, take

f(x)=\ln A(x)=(x+y-1)\left(\ln x-\ln(x+y)\right).

Then

f^{\prime}(x)=\ln x-\ln(x+y)+\frac{y-1}{x}+\frac{1}{x+y}

and

f^{\prime\prime}(x)=\frac{y(y-yx-y^{2}+2x)}{x^{2}(x+y)^{2}}.

Then as
f^{\prime\prime}(x)>0
for
0<x<\frac{y^{2}-y}{2-y}
for
1<y<2
. Also as
\displaystyle\lim_{x\mapsto\infty}f^{\prime}(x)=0
, we conclude that
f^{\prime}(x)<
, and so
f(x)
is decreasing. Thus
\frac{A(b)}{A(a)}<1
. The proof is complete.
∎

(e)

It is obvious that

\left(\frac{b+y}{a+y}\right)^{1-y}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)\leq\frac{b+y}{a+y}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)\left(\frac{a+y+1}{b+y+1}\right)^{y}.

Thus the upper bound in (
14
) improves the one in (
7
).

We have the following.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
0<a<b
. Then the following hold.

(1)

If
0<a<b<1
, then

(18)

e^{\frac{1}{2}(a-b)}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{B(b,b)}{B(a,a)}\leq e^{a-b}.

(2)

If
1\leq a<b
, then

(19)

e^{a-b}\leq\frac{B(b,b)}{B(a,a)}\leq e^{\frac{1}{2}(a-b)}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

In (
15
), make
y=x
. In case
0<x\leq 1
, (
17
) then becomes

(20)

-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2x}\leq\frac{\partial_{x}B(x,x)}{B(x,x)}\leq-1.

Integrating from
x=a
to
x=b
, we obtain

-\frac{1}{2}(b-a)-\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)\leq\ln\left(\frac{B(b,b)}{B(a,a)}\right)\leq a-b.

This is equivalent to (
18
). Inequalities (
19
) are obtained in the same way following the explanation in the previous theorem.
∎

We have seen in Remark
2.3
that the upper bound in (
14
) improves the one in (
6
) for
0<a<b<1+\sqrt{y+1}
, and that the lower bound in (
14
) is better than the one in (
6
) for
0<a<b<\frac{y^{2}-y}{2-y}
with
1<y<2
. To improve these ranges of the parameters
a
and
b
, we prove the following.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
0<a<b
. If
y\geq 1
, then

(21)

\frac{b^{b-1}(a+y-1)^{a+y-1}}{a^{a-1}(b+y-1)^{b+y-1}}\leq\frac{B(b,y)}{B(a,y)}\leq\frac{a(b+1)^{b+1}(a+y)^{a+y}}{b(a+1)^{a+1}(b+y)^{b+y}}.

Proof.

Let
y>1
. Using (
15
), we obtain

B(x,y)+x\partial_{x}B(x,y)=(y-1)\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-2}}{B(x,y-1)}(t\ln t)dt\right)B(x,y-1).

We note that the function
t\mapsto\Phi(t)=t\ln t
is convex. We also note that

d\mu(t)=\frac{t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-2}}{B(x,y-1)}dt

is a probability measure.

Using then the Jensen’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-2}}{B(x,y-1)}(t\ln t)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Phi(t)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\int_{0}^{1}td\mu(t)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{x}(1-t)^{y-2}}{B(x,y-1)}dt\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\frac{B(x+1,y-1)}{B(x,y-1)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\frac{x}{x+y-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x}{x+y-1}\ln\left(\frac{x}{x+y-1}\right).

Thus

\displaystyle B(x,y)+x\partial_{x}B(x,y)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(y-1)\frac{x}{x+y-1}\ln\left(\frac{x}{x+y-1}\right)B(x,y-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\ln\left(\frac{x}{x+y-1}\right)B(x,y).

Consequently,

\frac{\frac{\partial B(x,y)}{\partial x}}{B(x,y)}\geq\ln\left(\frac{x}{x+y-1}\right)-\frac{1}{x}.

Integrating both sides from
x=a
to
x=b
, we obtain

\ln\left(\frac{B(b,y)}{B(a,y)}\right)\geq b\ln b-(b+y-1)\ln(b+y-1)-a\ln a+(a+y-1)\ln(a+y-1)-\ln b+\ln a.

Thus

\frac{B(b,y)}{B(a,y)}\geq\frac{b^{b-1}}{a^{a-1}}\frac{(a+y-1)^{a+y-1}}{(b+y-1)^{b+y-1}}.

Let us prove the upper bound. We use the fact that the function
\Psi(t)=\ln t
is concave, and

d\nu(t)=\frac{t^{x}(1-t)^{y-2}}{B(x+1,y-1)}dt

is a probability measure. Using then the Jensen’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{x}(1-t)^{y-2}}{B(x+1,y-1)}(\ln t)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Psi(t)d\nu(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Psi\left(\int_{0}^{1}td\nu(t)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{x+1}(1-t)^{y-2}}{B(x+1,y-1)}dt\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi\left(\frac{B(x+2,y-1)}{B(x+1,y-1)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi\left(\frac{x+1}{x+y}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln\left(\frac{x+1}{x+y}\right).

Thus

\displaystyle B(x,y)+x\partial_{x}B(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(y-1)\ln\left(\frac{x+1}{x+y}\right)B(x+1,y-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\ln\left(\frac{x+1}{x+y}\right)B(x,y).

It follows that

\frac{\frac{\partial B(x,y)}{\partial x}}{B(x,y)}\leq\ln\left(\frac{x+1}{x+y}\right)-\frac{1}{x}.

Integrating both sides from
x=a
to
x=b
, we obtain

\ln\left(\frac{B(b,y)}{B(a,y)}\right)\leq-\ln b+\ln a+(b+1)\ln(b+1)-(a+1)\ln(a+1)-(b+y)\ln(b+y)+(a+y)\ln(a+y).

Thus

\frac{B(b,y)}{B(a,y)}\leq\frac{a(b+1)^{b+1}(a+y)^{a+y}}{b(a+1)^{a+1}(b+y)^{b+y}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

The above result improves both (
6
) and (
7
) for this range of the parameters.
Indeed, we have that for
0<a<b
and
y\geq 1
,

(22)

\frac{a(b+1)^{b+1}(a+y)^{a+y}}{b(a+1)^{a+1}(b+y)^{b+y}}\leq\frac{a(b+y)}{b(a+y)}\left(\frac{a+y+1}{b+y+1}\right)^{y},

(23)

\frac{b^{b-1}(a+y-1)^{a+y-1}}{a^{a-1}(b+y-1)^{b+y-1}}\geq\frac{b^{b-1}(a+y)^{a+y-1}}{a^{a-1}(b+y)^{b+y-1}},

and

(24)

\frac{a(b+1)^{b+1}(a+y)^{a+y}}{b(a+1)^{a+1}(b+y)^{b+y}}\leq\frac{b^{b}(a+y)^{a+y}}{a^{a}(b+y)^{b+y}}.

Note that (
21
) is equivalent to

A(a,b,y)\leq B(a,b,y)

where

A(a,b,y)=\frac{(b+1)^{b+1}(a+y)^{a+y+1}}{(a+1)^{a+1}(b+y)^{b+y+1}}
and
B(a,b,y)=\left(\frac{a+y+1}{b+y+1}\right)^{y}
. Now we have

\ln A(a,b,y)-\ln B(a,b,y)=f(b)-f(a)

where
f(x)=(x+1)\ln(x+1)-(x+y+1)\ln(x+y)+y\ln(x+y+1)
. It suffices then to see that
f(x)
is decreasing to conclude. The proof of (
23
) and (
24
) follows in the same way.

Proceeding as above or otherwise, one obtains that

\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{y}\leq\frac{b^{b-1}(a+y-1)^{a+y-1}}{a^{a-1}(b+y-1)^{b+y-1}}

and

\frac{a(b+1)^{b+1}(a+y)^{a+y}}{b(a+1)^{a+1}(b+y)^{b+y}}\leq\left(\frac{b+y}{a+y}\right)^{1-y}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right).

Thus Theorem
2.5
improves Theorem
2.2
for
y\geq 1
.

The above result does not take into account the case
0<y\leq 1
. Here is how to obtain a related estimate in this case.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
0<a<b
. The following hold.

(1)

If
0<y\leq 1
, then

(25)

\frac{B(b,y)}{B(a,y)}\leq\frac{a}{b}\left(\frac{b^{b}(a+y)^{a+y}}{a^{a}(b+y)^{b+y}}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{y}}.

(2)

If
y>1
, then

(26)

\frac{a}{b}\left(\frac{b^{b}(a+y)^{a+y}}{a^{a}(b+y)^{b+y}}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{y}}\leq\frac{B(b,y)}{B(a,y)}.

Proof.

We proceed as above. We note that the function
\Psi(t)=\frac{t\ln t}{1-t}
is convex, and we recall that

d\nu(t)=\frac{t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-1}}{B(x,y-1)}dt

is a probability measure. Using again Jensen’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-1}}{B(x,y)}\left(\frac{t\ln t}{1-t}\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Psi(t)d\nu(t)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Psi\left(\int_{0}^{1}td\nu(t)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{x}(1-t)^{y-1}}{B(x,y)}dt\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi\left(\frac{B(x+1,y)}{B(x,y)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi\left(\frac{x}{x+y}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x}{y}\ln\left(\frac{x}{x+y}\right).

It follows from (
15
) and the above that in the case
0<y\leq 1
,

\displaystyle B(x,y)+x\partial_{x}B(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(y-1)x}{y}\ln\left(\frac{x}{x+y}\right)B(x,y).

Thus

\frac{\frac{\partial B(x,y)}{\partial x}}{B(x,y)}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{y}\right)\ln\left(\frac{x}{x+y}\right)-\frac{1}{x}.

Integrating both sides from
x=a
to
x=b
, we obtain

\ln\left(\frac{B(b,y)}{B(a,y)}\right)\leq-\ln b+\ln a+\left(1-\frac{1}{y}\right)\left(b\ln b-a\ln a-(b+y)\ln(b+y)+(a+y)\ln(a+y)\right).

Thus

\frac{B(b,y)}{B(a,y)}\leq\frac{a}{b}\left(\frac{b^{b}(a+y)^{a+y}}{a^{a}(b+y)^{b+y}}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{y}}.

The case
y>1
follows similarly but with the reverse inequality due to that
(y-1)>0
.
∎

We obtain also the inequality,

(28)

\frac{a}{b}\left(\frac{b^{b}(a+y)^{a+y}}{a^{a}(b+y)^{b+y}}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{y}}\geq\frac{b^{b-1}(a+y-1)^{a+y-1}}{a^{a-1}(b+y-1)^{b+y-1}}.

That is (
26
) improves the lower bound in (
14
) for all
y\geq 1
and
1+\frac{y}{2}<a<b
.

Proof.

Proving inequality (
28
) is equivalent to proving that
\frac{A(a)}{A(b)}\geq 1
, where
A
is the function defined by

A(x)=\frac{x^{\frac{1}{y}}(x+y)^{(x+y)(1-\frac{1}{y})}}{(x+y-1)^{x+y-1}}.

Put

f(x)=\ln A(x)=\frac{1}{y}\ln x+(1-\frac{1}{y})(x+y)\ln(x+y)-(x+y-1)\ln(x+y-1).

It will be enough for us to show that the function
f
is decreasing on
(1+\frac{y}{2},\infty)
.
We have

f^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{y}\left(\frac{1}{x}-1+\ln\left(\frac{1}{x+y}\right)\right)-\ln\left(1-\frac{1}{x+y}\right).

Since
\frac{t-1}{t}\leq\ln(t)\leq t-1
, for
0<t<1
, we deduce that

1-x-y\leq\ln\left(\frac{1}{x+y}\right)\leq\dfrac{1-x-y}{x+y}

and

-\frac{1}{x+y-1}\leq\ln\left(1-\frac{1}{x+y}\right)\leq-\frac{1}{x+y}.

It follows that

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{y}\left(\frac{1}{x}-2+\frac{1}{x+y}\right)+\frac{1}{x+y-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}\left(\frac{2}{y}+1\right)-\frac{2}{y}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 0,

for all
x\geq 1+\frac{y}{2}
.
∎

We have the following result.

Theorem 2.9
.

Assume that
0<a<b<\infty
,
y>0
. Let

c=1-\frac{y}{b-a}\ln\left(\frac{b+y+1}{a+y+1}\right),\quad\alpha=\ln c-1,\quad\beta=\frac{1}{c}.

Then

(29)

\frac{B(b,y)}{B(a,y)}\leq e^{(\alpha+\beta)(b-a)}\left(\frac{a+y+1}{b+y+1}\right)^{y\beta}\frac{a}{b}\left(\frac{b+y}{a+y}\right)^{y}.

Proof.

We take
k=l
and
h=0
in (
10
) to get

(30)

B(x,y+1)+x\partial_{x}B(x,y+1)=y\int_{0}^{1}t^{x}(1-t)^{y-1}(\ln t)dt.

We now apply an idea from
[
8
]
. That is we note that as
t\mapsto\ln t
is concave, for any
0<t<1
and any
c>0
,

\ln t\leq\ln c-1+\frac{t}{c}=\alpha+\beta t.

It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}t^{x}(1-t)^{y-1}(\ln t)dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha B(x+1,y)+\beta B(x+2,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\alpha\frac{x}{x+y}+\beta\frac{x(x+1)}{(x+y)(x+y+1)}\right)B(x,y).

Taking this into (
30
), we obtain

B(x,y+1)+x\partial_{x}B(x,y+1)\leq y\left(\alpha\frac{x}{x+y}+\beta\frac{x(x+1)}{(x+y)(x+y+1)}\right)B(x,y)

or equivalently,

(31)

\frac{\frac{\partial B(x,y)}{\partial x}}{B(x,y)}\leq\alpha+\beta-\frac{y\beta}{x+y+1}-\frac{1}{x}+\frac{y}{x+y}.

Integrating both sides from
x=a
to
x=b
, we obtain

(32)

\ln\left(\frac{B(b,y)}{B(a,y)}\right)\leq(\alpha+\beta)(b-a)-y\beta\ln\left(\frac{b+y+1}{a+y+1}\right)-\ln\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)+y\ln\left(\frac{b+y}{a+y}\right).

One easily checks that the value of
c>0
minimizing (
32
) is given as

c=1-\frac{y}{b-a}\ln\left(\frac{b+y+1}{a+y+1}\right).

Note that
0<c<1
. Taking this into (
32
) and then taking the exponential of both members, we obtain (
29
). The proof is complete.

Theorem 2.11

Assume that

B(x,y+1)+x\partial_{x}B(x,y+1)=y\int_{0}^{1}t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-1}(t\ln t)dt.

t\ln t\geq(\ln c+1)t-c=\alpha t+\beta.

It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-1}(t\ln t)dt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\alpha B(x+1,y)+\beta B(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\alpha\frac{x}{x+y}+\beta\right)B(x,y).

Taking this into (

B(x,y+1)+x\partial_{x}B(x,y+1)\geq y\left(\alpha\frac{x}{x+y}+\beta\right)B(x,y)

or equivalently,

(36)

\frac{\frac{\partial B(x,y)}{\partial x}}{B(x,y)}\geq(\alpha+\beta)+\beta\frac{y}{x}-\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{x+y}.

Integrating both sides from

(37)

\ln\left(\frac{B(b,y)}{B(a,y)}\right)\geq(\alpha+\beta)(b-a)+y\beta\ln\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)-\ln\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)+\ln\left(\frac{b+y}{a+y}\right).

We have that the value of

\frac{1}{c}=1+\frac{y}{b-a}\ln\left(\frac{b}{a}\right).

Taking this into (
37
) and then taking the exponential of both members, we obtain (
34
). The proof is complete.

Theorem 2.14
.

The following hold.

(1)

If
1\leq y\leq 2
, and
1\leq a<b\leq 2
, then

(40)

B(b+1,y)\geq B(a+1,y)+\frac{1}{8\ln\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)}\left(\frac{5}{9}\right)^{y-1}\left[\left(\frac{4}{9}\right)^{b-1}-\left(\frac{4}{9}\right)^{a-1}\right].

(2)

If
\left(y>2\quad\text{and}\quad 0<a<b\leq 1\right)
or
\left(0<y<1\quad\text{and}\quad 2\leq a<b\right)
, then

(41)

B(b+1,y)\leq B(a+1,y)+\frac{1}{8\ln\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)}\left(\frac{5}{9}\right)^{y-1}\left[\left(\frac{4}{9}\right)^{b-1}-\left(\frac{4}{9}\right)^{a-1}\right].

Proof.

It is easy to verify that the function
\Phi_{x,y}(t)=t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-1}
defined in
(0,1)
, is convex if
(x,y)\in(0,1]\times[2,\infty)\cup[2,\infty)\times(0,1]
. It is concave if
(x,y)\in[1,2]\times[1,2]
.

Now consider the function

g(x,y)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{x}(1-t)^{y-1}dt.

Then

\frac{\partial g(x,y)}{\partial x}=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-1}(-4t\ln t)dt.

We observe that
(-4t\ln t)dt
is a probability measure on
(0,1)
. Hence for
1\leq y\leq 2
and
1\leq a<b\leq 2
, as
t\mapsto\Phi(t)=t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-1}
is concave, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g(x,y)}{\partial x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi(t)(-4t\ln t)dt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\Phi\left(\int_{0}^{1}-4t^{2}\ln tdt\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\Phi\left(\frac{4}{9}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{4}{9}\right)^{x-1}\left(\frac{5}{9}\right)^{y-1}.

That is

\frac{\partial g(x,y)}{\partial x}\geq-\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{5}{9}\right)^{y-1}e^{(x-1)\ln\left(\frac{4}{9}\right)}.

Integrating from
x=a
to
x=b
, we obtain

B(b+1,y)-B(a+1,y)\geq\frac{1}{4\ln\left(\frac{9}{4}\right)}\left[\left(\frac{4}{9}\right)^{b-1}-\left(\frac{4}{9}\right)^{a-1}\right]\left(\frac{5}{9}\right)^{y-1}.

That (
25
) holds.

The estimate (
26
) follows in the same way using that
t\mapsto\Phi(t)=t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-1}
is convex for the given ranges of
x
and
y
.
∎

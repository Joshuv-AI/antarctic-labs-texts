Perioperative NALIRIFOX in patients with resectable pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma: updated analysis of survival and circulating and genetic biomarkers from the phase II nITRO trial
Authors: E Scarlato, V Merz, S Pietrobono, S Casalino, A Quinzii, C Zecchetto, L Mendo, S Giacomazzi, A Bonato, V De Vita, G Butturini, R Salvia, D Melisi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe optimal perioperative strategy for resectable pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) remains undefined, and predictive biomarkers to guide treatment selection are lacking. The phase II nanoliposomal IrinoTecan with 5-fluorouracil, levofolinic acid and oxaliplatin in patients with Resectable pancreatic cancer (nITRO) trial showed the activity of perioperative NALIRIFOX in patients with resectable PDAC. Here, we report updated survival outcomes and translational analyses of this trial.Patients and methodsPlasma levels of 25 cytokines were quantified via multiplex bead-based immunoassay. Germline whole-exome sequencing assessed variants in tumor suppression, DNA damage repair (DDR), and drug metabolism pathways. Five in silico tools re-evaluated variants of uncertain significance. Statistical correlations between circulating cytokines or germline pathogenic variants and clinical outcomes were determined.ResultsAfter a median follow-up of 50.2 months, the median overall survival (mOS) was 32 months in the intention-to-treat population and 48 months in patients who underwent resection. High baseline tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) levels were consistently associated with reduced treatment response (P = 0.022), lower resection probability (P = 0.007), and shorter survival (P = 0.014). Germline pathogenic/predicted damaging variants in DDR or tumor suppressor genes (g.Deficient) were detected in 36.2% of patients and correlated with improved outcomes. Patients with combined g.Deficient/TNF-α–low status achieved the most favorable prognosis, with an mOS of 55 months and a 100% resection rate. DPYD c.496A>G predicted both severe gastrointestinal toxicity and chemotherapy dose reductions.ConclusionsLong-term results from the nITRO trial confirm the activity of perioperative NALIRIFOX in resectable PDAC. Inflammatory, genomic, and pharmacogenomic biomarkers strongly influenced treatment response, surgical eligibility, survival, and toxicity. Biomarker integration could enable more precise patient stratification and guide personalized perioperative strategies in PDAC.Key words: DNA damage repair, DPYD, NALIRIFOX, pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, perioperative therapy, TNF-α

Introduction

Surgical resection followed by adjuvant chemotherapy remains the standard of care for patients with resectable pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC).1 However, despite the use of modern combination regimens such as modified irinotecan, leucovorin, 5-fluorouracil, oxaliplatin (FOLFIRINOX), long-term outcomes remain poor, with only a 26.1% disease-free survival (DFS) rate at 5 years.2 In large retrospective series, up to 18.9% of patients who underwent upfront pancreatectomy experienced early recurrence or death within 6 months, a rate of futility significantly associated with elevated carbohydrate antigen 19.9 (CA19.9) levels and larger tumor size at diagnosis.3 Furthermore, real-world data indicate that only approximately one-third of patients who undergo upfront surgery ultimately receive postoperative chemotherapy, leading to a substantial overestimation of the actual benefit of the current treatment paradigm.4 The high incidence of early recurrence of this disease can be attributed mainly to its common and early metastatic diffusion, as well as the fact that a significant number of patients receive an incomplete R1 resection, an event that postoperative treatments cannot mitigate.5

These limitations have fueled growing interest in perioperative strategies that integrate systemic therapy earlier in the course of treatment.6 Such approaches aim to improve patient selection by introducing a window of time to identify biologically aggressive tumors and frail patients, thereby avoiding unnecessary resections. They also ensure that systemic therapy is administered to a greater proportion of patients, delivered under better clinical conditions before surgery. In addition, they may induce a treatment effect that increases R0 resection rates and, ultimately, improves oncologic outcomes in resectable PDAC. However, all clinical trials conducted to date exploring different preoperative treatment regimens for resectable PDAC have yielded conflicting results.7 Most importantly, none of these studies incorporated biomarker analyses to guide treatment selection, limiting the potential to personalize therapy and optimize outcomes.

NALIRIFOX is a novel combination regimen comprising liposomal irinotecan, 5-fluorouracil, leucovorin, and oxaliplatin. It has recently been validated as a new reference regimen as first-line treatment in the phase III NAPOLI-3 trial, which demonstrated a significant improvement in overall survival (OS) compared with gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel in previously untreated patients with metastatic PDAC.8, 9, 10, 11

Building on these results, we recently evaluated NALIRIFOX in the nanoliposomal IrinoTecan with 5-fluorouracil, levofolinic acid and oxaliplatin in patients with Resectable pancreatic cancer (nITRO) study, a prospective multicenter phase II clinical trial that assessed the safety and activity of this regimen in the perioperative treatment of resectable PDAC.12 The nITRO study met its primary endpoint by demonstrating an R0 resection rate of 65.3%. NALIRIFOX showed a favorable safety profile, underscoring its suitability for use in the perioperative setting. In addition to its clinical outcomes, the nITRO trial provides a unique framework for exploring potential biomarkers that may guide the selection of patients most likely to benefit from this preoperative treatment strategy.13

Here, we report updated survival outcomes and translational analyses from the nITRO trial. We explored circulating biomarkers associated with both treatment response and survival, characterized germline DNA damage repair (DDR) and tumor suppressor gene alterations that correlate with response to NALIRIFOX, and DPYD and UGT1A1 mutational profiles that predict treatment-related toxicity. These markers of efficacy and toxicity may guide the selection of patients with resectable PDAC who are most likely to benefit from perioperative NALIRIFOX.

Methods

Patients and data collection

Baseline and prior surgical resection demographic and clinicopathological data were systematically collected and recorded in a predefined database. Tumor response to treatment, assessed according to RECIST v.1 guidelines prior to surgical resection, and surgical margin status (R0/R1) were also reported. Chemotherapy-related adverse events (AEs) were monitored every 2 weeks and graded according to National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI-CTCAE) version 5.0 during both preoperative and postoperative phases. Disease progression and death events were monitored. All patients provided written informed consent, and the protocol was approved by the ethics committee of the Azienda Ospedaliera Universitaria Integrata di Verona. The study was conducted in compliance with Good Clinical Practice guidelines and the Declaration of Helsinki.

Multiplex circulating cytokines and chemokines profiling

Plasma levels of 25 cytokines, chemokines, and growth factors (Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964) were quantified using a multiplex bead-based immunoassay (catalog number LXSAHM-25, Bio-Techne, Minneapolis, MN), according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Data on median fluorescence intensities were collected on a Bio-Plex 200 instrument, using Bio-Plex Manager software version 6.2 (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Inc., Hercules, CA). Standard curves for each cytokine were generated using the premixed standards provided in the kits.

Germline whole-exome sequencing, Oncomine BRCA assay and in silico variant effect prediction

A comprehensive description of the whole-exome sequencing (WES) protocol, Oncomine BRCA assay (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA), variant calling pipeline, variant annotation, and in silico prediction is provided in the Supplementary Materials and Methods, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964. In the present study, germline variants investigated in forty-five genes (Supplementary Table S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964) classified as class 4 (likely pathogenic) and class 5 (pathogenic) according to the American College of Medical Genetics guidelines and the ClinVar database were categorized as “pathogenic” variants (gPv). Variants of uncertain significance (VUSs) or with a conflicting interpretation of pathogenicity (CIP) and predicted to be damaging in silico were classified as germline-predicted damaging variants (gPDv).

Statistical analysis

Median OS (mOS) and median DFS (mDFS), defined as the time from enrollment or surgery to the time of disease death or progression, respectively, were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared using the log-rank (Mantel–Cox) test. For the survival analysis, patients without a recorded death or progression were censored on the date of the last follow-up visit. Median follow-up time was calculated using the reverse Kaplan–Meier method. Continuous variables were assessed for normality using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Depending on data distribution, group comparisons were performed using the unpaired Student’s t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test for two groups, and the Kruskal–Wallis test for multiple groups with nonnormally distributed data. Fisher’s exact or chi-square test was used for pairwise comparisons of categorical variables. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed for each soluble biomarker, and the optimal cut-off threshold was defined as the point maximizing Youden’s J statistic (J = sensitivity + specificity − 1). Only soluble biomarkers showing an area under the ROC curve (ROC AUC) ≥0.60 for the specific clinical outcome were retained for subsequent analyses. Univariate binary logistic regression models were used to evaluate associations between soluble or genetic biomarkers and binary clinical outcomes. To account for multiple testing in exploratory univariate analyses of soluble biomarkers and pharmacogenetic variants, false discovery rate (FDR) correction was applied using the Benjamini–Hochberg method, considering each outcome-specific set of variables as an independent family of tests. Multivariable binary logistic regression models were subsequently constructed to identify independent associations after adjustment for clinically relevant confounders. Variables included in multivariable models were selected based on biological plausibility and univariate analysis (P < 0.10). No correction for multiple testing was applied to multivariable models because these represent single, hypothesis-driven models rather than multiple independent statistical comparisons. Single observations of UGT1A1 or DPYD variants were omitted from the analysis. Statistical differences were considered significant when the P value was ≤0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS (version 30.0, SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL), GraphPad Prism (version 8.0, GraphPad Software, Boston, MA), and JAMOVI Project 2022 (version 2.3, Sydney, NSW, Australia).

Results

Updated clinical outcomes

Between May 2018 and May 2022, 107 patients were enrolled in the study, constituting the intention-to-treat (ITT) population. Patient characteristics and Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials flow diagram have been previously reported,11 and a schematic overview of the trial design is illustrated in Figure 1A. With a median follow-up duration of 50.2 months, 67 events were observed in the ITT population, and thus, the updated mOS for this cohort was 32 months (95% confidence interval [CI] 28-44) (Figure 1B). Among patients who underwent resection, the updated mDFS and mOS were 19 months (95% CI 13-36) and 48 months (95% CI 36-not reached), respectively (Figure 1C and D). Significant differences in both mDFS and mOS were observed between patients who underwent R0 versus R1 resections. Specifically, the mDFS was significantly longer in patients who underwent an R0 resection than in those who underwent R1 resection (32 versus 12 months, hazard ratio [HR] 0.44, 95% CI 0.235-0.839, P = 0.0037). Similarly, mOS was significantly improved in the R0 group compared with the R1 group (58 versus 40 months, HR 0.52, 95% CI 0.257-1.043, P = 0.039) (Figure 1E and F).

Circulating cytokine and chemokine analysis

To determine whether circulating cytokine profiles could predict patient outcomes, baseline plasma levels of 25 cytokines were measured in 97 patients. In the assessed cohort, the median age at diagnosis was 63 years (range 31-76 years, interquartile range [IQR] 56-69 years), with a balanced sex distribution (female 46.4%; male 53.6%). Baseline clinical characteristics of patients with circulating biomarker data available are summarized in Supplementary Table S3, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964.

Concentrations of tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), vascular endothelial growth factor, interleukin (IL) 1Ra, C-C motif chemokine ligand (CCL-) 4, fibroblast growth factor basic, CCL11, IL-7, IL-15, IL-4, interferon-γ, IL-5, and CCL3 higher than the cut-offs were significantly associated with lower radiological response rates (Figure 2A; Supplementary Figures S1-S7, Supplementary Table S4, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964). Concentrations of IL-8 higher than the cut-off were significantly associated with a lower chance of being disease-free at six months, while IL-1β levels higher than the cut-off correlated with increased surgical resection rates. All reported associations remained statistically significant after FDR correction (FDR-corrected P < 0.05) (Supplementary Table S4, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964).

Among all factors analyzed, TNF-α showed the strongest inverse association with treatment outcomes. When applying ROC-derived endpoint-specific cut-off values, higher TNF-α levels were significantly associated with lower response rates (odds ratio [OR] 0.250, 95% CI 0.08-0.82, P = 0.022), reduced likelihood of resection (OR 0.29, 95% CI 0.12-0.72, P = 0.007), and disease-free at 6 months (OR 0.35, 95% CI 0.15-0.81, P = 0.014) (Figure 2A; Supplementary Table S4, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964). Given the high concordance among endpoint-specific ROC-derived cut-offs, TNF-α was dichotomized using a harmonized threshold of 11.74 pg/mL, which retained statistical significance in sensitivity analyses (Supplementary Table S5, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964).

High TNF-α levels were associated with older age (median 64.5 versus 62 years; P = 0.013) and with primary tumor localization in the pancreatic head (95% versus 77.2%, P = 0.02) (Supplementary Table S6, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964). Based on the harmonized cut-off of 11.74 pg/mL, patients with low baseline TNF-α levels showed an overall response rate (ORR) of 29.6%, which was significantly higher than the 10.5% measured in those with high TNF-α levels (P = 0.039) (Figure 2B). Notably, the resection rate in patients with low TNF-α levels was 83.6%, significantly higher than the 54.8% measured in those with high TNF-α levels (P = 0.0019) (Figure 2C). In the ITT population, patients with levels of TNF-α lower than the cut-off (11.74 pg/mL) at baseline had an mOS significantly longer than that of those with levels of TNF-α higher than the cut-off (44 versus 22 months; HR 0.49, 95% CI 0.28-0.85, P = 0.0044) (Figure 2D). Corresponding analyses using endpoint-specific ROC-derived cut-off values are reported for data transparency in Supplementary Figure S8A-C, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964.

Impact of germline variants on response to NALIRIFOX and survival outcomes

Germline WES was performed in 48 patients to identify DDR and tumor suppressor gene alterations. Ten additional patients were tested for BRCA1/2 germline variants using the Oncomine BRCA assay. The final cohort comprised 58 patients, with a median age of 63 years (range 31-76 years, IQR 52.7-67.2 years). Males and females were equally represented (n = 29 each, 50%). Baseline clinical and demographic characteristics of the patients who underwent germline testing and those who did not are summarized and compared in Supplementary Table S7, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964, with no significant differences reported. Overall, 16 of 58 (27.6%) patients carried gPv in one or more DDR and/or tumor suppressor genes (Figure 3A; Supplementary Table S8, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964).

In addition, a total of 24 VUSs or CIP across 15 genes were identified in 18 of 58 (31%) patients, five of whom had concomitant gPv. Notably, eight of these patients (8 of 58, 13.8%) harbored ≥1 germline variant predicted as “possibly damaging” or “damaging” (gPDv) by at least three of five in silico tools (Figure 3A; Supplementary Table S9, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964). Notably, three individuals harbored concomitant gPv mutations in distinct genes. The distribution of patients harboring gPv or gPDv in investigated genes is shown in Figure 3B and C.

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics were comparable between gPv- and gPDv-carriers (Supplementary Table S10, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964). Overall identified gPv and gPDv for each patient, along with RECIST responses at first radiological assessment, are shown in Figure 3D.

No significant differences emerged in ORR (gPv-carriers 33.3%, gPDv-carriers 20%, P > 0.99) and mOS (gPv-carriers 55 months, gPDv-carriers not reached, HR 1.61, 95% CI 0.27-9.52, P = 0.64) between the two groups (Figure 3E and F). Therefore, patients harboring ≥1 gPv or gPDv were collectively classified as g.Deficient (n = 21) (Figure 3G) and compared to patients with no clinically/functionally relevant germline alterations in DDR or tumor suppressor genes (g.Proficient) (Supplementary Table S11, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964).

Prior studies on larger cohorts of patients with pancreatic cancer and other solid tumors have reported that patients harboring loss-of-function variants in BRCA1, BRCA2, and PALB2 genes exhibit improved tumor response and survival when treated with platinum-based chemotherapy.14,15 In our cohort, no significant difference in OS was measured between patients carrying germline variants in BRCA1/2 or PALB2 (BRCA/PALB2 g.Deficient) and those harboring variants in other DDR or tumor suppressor genes (Other DDR-g.Deficient) (55 months versus not reached; HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.1980-3.261, P = 0.74) (Figure 4A). In addition, the two groups exhibited similar radiological response rates (28.6% versus 30.8%, P > 0.99) (Figure 4B).

Given these results, we extended our analysis to compare tumor response and survival outcomes between patients carrying any germline pathogenic or predicted pathogenic variants in DDR or tumor suppressor genes (g.Deficient) and those without such variants (g.Proficient). g.Deficient patients demonstrated higher, although not statistically significant, tumor response rates (30% versus 13.9%, P = 0.17) (Figure 4C) and resection rates (85.7% versus 72.9%, P = 0.33) (Figure 4D) compared with g.Proficient patients. A trend toward prolonged OS was observed in g.Deficient patients (55 months versus 32 months, HR 0.55, 95% CI 0.2687-1.122, P = 0.13) (Figure 4E).

Given the previously observed impact of TNF-α levels on treatment response, resection rates, and survival, we further stratified g.Deficient and g.Proficient patients by baseline TNF-α levels (low versus high), using the common 11.74 pg/mL cut-off, to explore potential interactions between germline status and systemic inflammation. Baseline TNF-α level data were missing for one patient in each group. Among g.Proficient patients, TNF-α–high and TNF-α–low cases were similarly distributed (55.6% versus 44.4%), whereas a greater proportion of TNF-α–low cases was identified among g.Deficient cases (65% versus 35%) (Figure 4F).

Patients with combined g.Deficient/TNF-α-low status showed a greater mean reduction in primary tumor size at preoperative radiological reassessment compared with other subgroups, although the difference was not statistically significant (P = 0.44) (Figure 4G). Resection outcomes were more strongly affected by the combined status. Accordingly, 100% (13 of 13) g.Deficient/TNF-α-low patients underwent surgery (Figure 4H). However, resection rates were not significantly different between g.Deficient/TNF-α–low and g.Proficient/TNF-α–low subgroups (100% versus 93.8%, P > 0.99) and between their TNF-α-high counterparts (57.1% versus 55.0%, P > 0.99).

Kaplan–Meier survival analysis of the four combined subgroups revealed a significant difference in OS among groups. Patients with g.Deficient/TNF-α–low status exhibited the longest mOS, reaching a mOS of 55 months. This group significantly outperformed the g.Proficient/TNF-α–low, g.Deficient/TNF-α–high, and g.Proficient/TNF-α–high subgroups (P = 0.014). Pairwise comparisons further highlighted significant differences in mOS between specific subgroups. Notably, a significantly longer mOS was only observed in g.Deficient/TNF-α–low group compared with the g.Deficient /TNF-α–high group (55 versus 23 months, P = 0.003) and not between g.Proficient/TNF-α–low and g.Proficient/TNF-α–high (40 versus 24 months, P = 0.17) (Figure 4I).

Pharmacogenetic profiles of DPYD and UGT1A1 and their impact on treatment-related AEs

In addition to the 48 patients who underwent retrospective germline WES, two patients who had been previously genotyped for UGT1A1 and DPYD through external testing were included in the pharmacogenetic analyses. Overall, 50 patients were assessed for DPYD and UGT1A1 pharmacogenetic variants. Baseline characteristics of patients with and without pharmacogenetic data are shown in Supplementary Table S12, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964.

Of the 50 cases, five cases had been also prospectively genotyped prior to treatment initiation and were excluded from the AE correlation analysis, as treatment dosage had been modified. Four carried high-risk allele variants (two UGT1A1∗28 homozygotes; one DPYD c.2194G>A and one c.1627A>G, both heterozygotes) and received preemptive dose reductions (−30% irinotecan; −15% fluoropyrimidines). One additional patient, later confirmed as wild-type for both UGT1A1 and DPYD genes, received an empirical dose reduction because of early gastrointestinal toxicity during the first NALIRIFOX cycle.

The pharmacogenetically assessed cohort, therefore, comprised 45 patients (median age 64 years, range 45-76 years, IQR 55.5-67.0 years), including 21 males (46.7%) and 24 females (53.3%). No significant differences were identified between pharmacogenetically assessed and not assessed cohorts (Supplementary Table S13, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964). In the pharmacogenetically assessed cohort, 18 (18 of 45, 40%) cases developed severe AEs during the first three preoperative cycles of NALIRIFOX. The most common grade ≥3 chemotherapy-related AEs were neutropenia (13.3%), hypokalemia (11.1%), diarrhea (8.9%), and elevation of alanine aminotransferase and aspartate aminotransferase (6.7% each).

The distribution and the allele frequency of each UGT1A1 and DPYD wild-type or variant genotypes (heterozygous or homozygous) are reported in Figure 5A. UGT1A1∗28 was the most frequent variant, found in 19 heterozygous (19 of 45, 42.2%) and five homozygous (5 of 45, 11.1%) carriers, corresponding to intermediate and poor metabolizers per Clinical Pharmacogenetics Implementation Consortium (CPIC) guidelines (Supplementary Figure S9, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964), respectively. For the DPYD gene, the most frequent variants were c.1627A>G,c.85T>C and c.496A>G, identified in 11, 10, and eight cases, respectively. These three allele variants are considered normal metabolizer variants according to CPIC guidelines.

Heterozygous DPYD c.2194G>A and c.1601G>A alleles were identified in three cases each (3 of 45, 6.7% each), classified as intermediate and normal metabolizer variant alleles, respectively. In addition, single heterozygous cases of c.1905+1G>A, c.1236G>A, c.1896T>C, and c.775A>G (1 of 45, 2.2% each) were also observed. No carriers of the poor/intermediate metabolizer variants c.1679T>G or c.2846A>T were detected.

In the univariate analysis, a higher risk of early grade ≥3 gastrointestinal toxicity was significantly associated with the DPYD c.496A>G allele variant (OR 6.04, 95% CI 1.08-30.9, P = 0.03). A marginal association was also found for female sex (OR 4.29, 95% CI 0.99-18.58, P = 0.052). Clinically, significant dose reduction requirements were also significantly associated with DPYD c.496A/G (OR 8.25, 95% CI 1.46-46.6, P = 0.017) and c.85T/C allele variants (OR 6.40, 95% CI 1.20-34.20, P = 0.030) (Figure 5B; Supplementary Table S14, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964). Treatment response was significantly associated with the DPYD c.85T>C variant (OR 7.500, 95% CI 1.03-54.99, P = 0.047). UGT1A1∗28 homozygous carriers showed a trend toward increased risk of severe hematological toxicity. After FDR correction, these associations did not retain statistical significance at the univariate level (FDR-corrected P > 0.05) (Supplementary Table S14, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964). However, in multivariate analyses adjusted for relevant clinical covariates, the DPYD c.496A/G variant was independently associated with the occurrence of early grade ≥3 gastrointestinal toxicity (OR 9.13, 95% CI 1.35-61.58, P = 0.023) and with the need for significant dose reduction (OR 7.01, 95% CI 1.11-44.45, P = 0.040). Female sex was also independently associated with grade ≥3 gastrointestinal toxicity (OR 6.16, 95% CI 1.10-34.26, P = 0.038) (Figure 5B; Supplementary Table S15, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964).

When patients who received preemptive dose reduction were included in the sensitivity analysis, associations between DPYD c.496A>G and toxicity outcomes were attenuated (Supplementary Tables S16 and S17, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964). OS was comparable among DPYD c.496A>G carriers, regardless of preemptive dose reduction (Supplementary Figure S10, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106964).

Discussion

The optimal treatment strategy for resectable PDAC remains debated, and the identification of biomarkers to guide the choice between neoadjuvant therapy and upfront surgery is a critical unmet need. The nITRO clinical trial demonstrated both the safety and the activity of perioperative NALIRIFOX, achieving a remarkable R0 resection rate in patients with resectable PDAC.11 In the present study, we confirmed its efficacy, reporting an updated mOS of 32 months in the ITT population and 48 months in resected patients, with a median follow-up of 50.2 months. Beyond these encouraging survival outcomes, our translational analyses highlight the potential of biomarker-driven patient stratification. High baseline TNF-α levels emerged as a negative prognostic factor, whereas germline pathogenic or predicted pathogenic variants in DDR and tumor suppressor genes were associated with improved outcomes. Importantly, patients with g.Deficient/TNF-α–low status had the most favorable prognosis, with a noteworthy mOS of 55 months. In terms of safety, the DPYD c.496A>G variant independently predicted early grade ≥3 gastrointestinal AEs and the need for chemotherapy dose reductions. Together, these findings highlight the potential of integrating inflammatory, genomic, and pharmacogenomic biomarkers to personalize perioperative strategies with NALIRIFOX.

The PDAC tumor microenvironment (TME) plays a central role in disease progression and therapeutic resistance. Cytokines and chemokines within the TME orchestrate immune evasion and treatment resistance,16 yet their role in resectable PDAC treated with preoperative therapy has been poorly studied, particularly with NALIRIFOX. In the nITRO trial, TNF-α was the only circulating factor consistently associated with clinical outcomes, inversely correlating with treatment response, resection probability, and survival. TNF-α is a key proinflammatory cytokine involved in regulating the immune system, promoting cell survival, and inducing apoptosis.17 Among its multiple signaling pathways, TNF-α acts as an upstream activator of the mitogen-activated protein (MAP3K) 7, also known as transforming growth factor-β–activated kinase 1 (TAK1).18 TAK1 is a central signaling node that our group has demonstrated to play a critical role in sustaining chemoresistance in PDAC,19, 20, 21, 22 primarily through the activation of multiple transcriptional regulators, including nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB), activator protein 1 (AP-1), and yes-ssociated protein (YAP)/ transcriptional co-activator with PDZ-binding motif (TAZ).23 This mechanistic link may explain the observed association of TNF-α with poorer outcomes. Ongoing analyses of tumor samples will help validate these findings. Notably, the adverse prognostic impact of elevated circulating CCL3 observed in this study is concordant with our prior work identifying CCL3 as a marker of an aggressive, basal-like/mesenchymal PDAC ecotype characterized by enhanced inflammatory signaling and poor clinical outcomes.24

DDR encompasses a complex network of pathways responsible for detecting, signaling, and repairing DNA lesions, including the homologous recombination pathway. Although initial studies suggested that patients with resectable PDAC harboring germline mutations in homologous recombination genes BRCA1 and BRCA2 had outcomes comparable with those with sporadic disease,25,26 subsequent evidence has demonstrated an increased likelihood of pathological complete response and prolonged survival in these patients when treated with neoadjuvant platinum-based regimens.26 However, the prognostic and predictive relevance of germline alterations of varying pathogenicity in other DDR-related genes in the perioperative setting remains largely unexplored. Our study provides compelling evidence that other inherited pathogenic variants in DDR and tumor suppressor genes are associated with a sensitivity to the platinum-based regimen NALIRIFOX comparable to those with BRCA1/2 or PALB2 mutations, supporting a common therapeutic vulnerability.

Nevertheless, the increasing use of next-generation sequencing continues to reveal numerous VUS. These VUS lack functional evidence for definitive classification, posing significant clinical challenges due to their unknown role in cancer biology.27 In our cohort, eight patients harbored one or more VUS predicted to be damaging by multiple in silico algorithms, involving genes such as PALB2, ATM, ATR, ERCC5, MLH1, MSH6, PMS2, and APC, most of which are rare in the general population. Notably, nITRO patients carrying computationally predicted damaging variants (gPDv.Deficient) exhibited survival outcomes and radiological responses comparable to those with known pathogenic variants (gPv.Deficient). When aggregated, patients with either pathogenic or predicted damaging variants showed a clinically meaningful divergence compared with g.Proficient individuals, both in OS and ORR. Together, these results underscore the clinical relevance of extending germline characterization beyond the canonical homologous recombination genes. Furthermore, several in silico–classified damaging VUSs have been reported as germline alterations in patients with solid tumors, including pancreatic cancer, supporting biological plausibility. A more inclusive approach to comprehensive germline testing could refine patient stratification and optimize perioperative treatment strategies in PDAC.

In this study, low levels of TNF-α were associated with improved OS, increased treatment response, and higher rates of surgical eligibility. Notably, these benefits were further amplified when patients were stratified by germline status (g.Deficient versus g.Proficient). In our study, patients with a combined g.Deficient/TNF-α low profile demonstrated significantly longer OS compared with g.Proficient cases with similarly low cytokine levels. Moreover, 100% of g.Deficient/TNF-α low patients underwent resection. This combined molecular profile delineates a clinically distinct subset of patients with PDAC in whom therapeutic efficacy and resectability are markedly enhanced.

One of the main concerns with preoperative approaches is the risk that tumor progression during treatment may render the disease unresectable. In the nITRO trial, among 107 patients who initiated preoperative NALIRIFOX, only seven experienced radiological progression and did not proceed to surgical exploration, suggesting these patients likely had inherently poor prognoses. A more pressing concern involves those who discontinued therapy due to treatment-related AEs, as the preoperative strategy could potentially deprive them of a curative opportunity. Variability in irinotecan-related and 5-fluorouracil–related toxicity is influenced by pharmacogenetics, particularly UGT1A1 and DPYD variants. In our study, although an association with gastrointestinal toxicity was also observed for female sex, the DPYD c.496A>G variant emerged as the sole variant independently associated with early severe gastrointestinal toxicity and dose reductions in the multivariable model, with the only sepsis-related death occurring in a carrier of this allele. The attenuation of pharmacogenetic associations with toxicity observed in sensitivity analyses including patients with preemptive dose reductions likely reflects the impact of early dose modification on toxicity outcomes.

Notably, among the 10 DPYD c.496A>G carriers identified in our cohort, including those who received preemptive dose reductions, OS appeared comparable irrespective of dose reduction status. Although the small sample size limits any definitive interpretation, this observation may suggest that dose modulation strategies aimed at reducing toxicity may not necessarily compromise treatment efficacy.

Several limitations must be acknowledged. Analyses of circulating, genomic, and pharmacogenetic biomarkers were conducted in subsets of the study ITT population due to sample availability and the timing of biospecimen collection, as baseline plasma samples were not available for all patients, and peripheral blood mononuclear cells for genomic and pharmacogenetic analyses were collected from the midpoint of the study onward. These factors limited statistical power and the generalizability of the findings. In addition, tumor tissue was not available for analysis, precluding assessment of the interactions between tumor genomics, TME, and germline variants. Moreover, functional validation of in silico predicted damaging variants would be essential. Although DPYD c.496A>G emerged as an independent factor in multivariable analyses, this finding should be interpreted with caution given the exploratory nature of this phase II study and the lack of statistical significance after FDR correction in univariate analyses. Finally, preemptive dose reductions in two UGT1A1∗28 homozygotes prevented full evaluation of pharmacogenetic toxicity associations in our cohort.

In conclusion, with a median follow-up exceeding four years, updated survival outcomes confirm the activity of perioperative NALIRIFOX in resectable PDAC. Our translational analyses reveal that interpatient inflammatory and genetic heterogeneity critically influence response, surgical eligibility, and long-term survival. From a practical standpoint, the combined assessment of baseline TNF-α levels and of germline DDR and tumor suppressor gene status may therefore assist clinicians in stratifying patients according to their likelihood of benefiting from perioperative NALIRIFOX and completing the intended treatment course. In addition, extended pharmacogenetic screening for DPYD variants could inform dose modulation strategies aimed at reducing treatment-related toxicity.

Although these considerations remain hypothesis-generating, prospective validation in larger and randomized cohorts is warranted to advance personalized approaches for resectable PDAC.

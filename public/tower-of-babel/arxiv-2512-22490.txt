Probing the pair production of first-generation vector-like leptons at future e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} colliders
Yao-Bei Liu 1,2 , Stefano Moretti 3,4

Abstract
This work explores the discovery potential of the first-generation weak isosinglet Vector-Like Leptons (VLLs), denoted by E ± E^{\pm} , via pair production at future electron-positron colliders. Our analysis adopts a comprehensive framework that incorporates beam polarization configurations and leverages detailed detector simulations. We focus on two distinct multilepton signatures: the 2 ​ ℓ + 2 ​ j + E̸ T 2\ell+2j+\not{E}_{T} and 3 ​ ℓ + 2 ​ j + E̸ T 3\ell+2j+\not{E}_{T} final states ( ℓ = e , μ \ell=e,\mu ). Both signatures arise from the decay E ± → Z ​ e ± / W ± ​ ν ℓ E^{\pm}\to Ze^{\pm}/W^{\pm}\nu_{\ell} and are distinguished by the decay patterns of the associated gauge bosons. By applying optimized selection criteria to both signal and background events, we establish exclusion sensitivities and discovery prospects across the VLL mass spectrum. Our findings demonstrate that, for integrated luminosities of 25 fb − 1 25\text{\,}\mathrm{f}\mathrm{b}^{-1} , 90 fb − 1 90\text{\,}\mathrm{f}\mathrm{b}^{-1} and 1000 fb − 1 1000\text{\,}\mathrm{f}\mathrm{b}^{-1} at corresponding center-of-mass (c.m.) energies of 1 TeV 1\text{\,}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V} , 1.5 TeV 1.5\text{\,}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V} and 3 TeV 3\text{\,}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V} , the accessible mass range extends to approximately 490 GeV 490\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V} , 740 GeV 740\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V} and 1440 GeV 1440\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V} , which represents a substantially improvement over the detection limits of existing hadron collider experiments.

II
Singlet VLL Model Framework

II.1
Theoretical setup

We consider a minimal extension of the SM by introducing a weak isosinglet VLL, denoted
E^{\pm}
, as mentioned, which mixes with the first-generation SM leptons. Under the SM gauge group
SU(3)_{C}\times SU(2)_{L}\times U(1)_{Y}
, the VLL transforms as

E_{L},E_{R}^{\dagger}\sim(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1},-1),

(1)

where both chiral components share the same gauge quantum numbers, permitting a gauge-invariant Dirac mass term.

The most general renormalizable Lagrangian involving the singlet VLL is

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{SM}}+\overline{E}(i\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}-M)E-\left(\epsilon\overline{L}He_{R}^{\prime}+\text{h.c.}\right),

(2)

where
D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-ig^{\prime}YB_{\mu}
is the covariant derivative,
M
is the VLL mass parameter,
L=(\nu_{e},e)_{L}^{T}
is the left-handed SM lepton doublet,
H
is the SM Higgs doublet and
\epsilon
is a small Yukawa coupling controlling the mixing.

After EW Symmetry Breaking (EWSB), the mass matrix for the charged leptons in the basis
(e,E)
is

\mathcal{M}=\begin{pmatrix}y_{e}v&0\\
\epsilon v&M\end{pmatrix},

(3)

where
v\approx 174\ \mathrm{GeV}
is the Higgs Vacuum Expectation Value (VEV). The mixing between the SM electron and the VLL is characterized by a small mixing angle
\theta_{L}
, given to Leading Order (LO) by

\sin\theta_{L}\approx\frac{\epsilon v}{M}.

(4)

The physical mass eigenvalues are

\displaystyle m_{e}

\displaystyle\approx y_{e}v\left(1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}v^{2}}{2M^{2}}+\cdots\right),

(5)

\displaystyle M_{E}

\displaystyle\approx M\left(1+\frac{\epsilon^{2}v^{2}}{2M^{2}}+\cdots\right).

(6)

The mixing angle is severely constrained by current experimental data from EW precision tests,
Z
-pole observables and direct VLL searches. Recent LHC results
ATLAS:2024mrr
require
\sin\theta\lesssim 0.03
for VLL masses in the few hundred GeV range, while low-energy precision measurements impose even tighter constraints at lower masses.
In this work, we adopt a value of
\epsilon=0.01
. This choice is sufficiently small to enable a perturbative treatment of mixing effects, while being large enough to ensure the prompt decay of

E^{\pm}
within the detector volume
Kumar:2015tna
. For smaller mixing parameters that induce displaced decays, dedicated searches targeting long-lived charged particles become applicable, as investigated in Refs.
Cao:2023smj
;
Bernreuther:2023uxh
.

II.2
Pair production at
e^{+}e^{-}
colliders

The pair production of singlet VLLs proceeds primarily through
s
-channel exchange of EW gauge bosons. The LO interaction Lagrangian for the vector-like electron
E^{\pm}
, retaining terms up to
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)
, is given by

\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}=\frac{gs_{W}^{2}}{c_{W}}Z_{\mu}\left(E^{\dagger}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}E-\bar{E}^{\dagger}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}\bar{E}\right)-eA_{\mu}\left(E^{\dagger}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}E-\bar{E}^{\dagger}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}\bar{E}\right),

(7)

where
e
denotes the electromagnetic coupling,
g
is the
SU(2)_{L}
gauge coupling,
\theta_{W}
is the Weinberg angle plus
s_{W}\equiv\sin\theta_{W}
and
c_{W}\equiv\cos\theta_{W}
with
e=gs_{W}
.

For polarized
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions with beam polarizations
P_{e^{-}}
and
P_{e^{+}}
, the partonic cross section takes the form

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}(e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow E^{+}E^{-})

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi\alpha^{2}}{3}(\hat{s}+2m_{E}^{2})\sqrt{1-4m_{E}^{2}/\hat{s}}

(8)

\displaystyle\times\left[|a_{L}|^{2}(1-P_{e^{-}})(1+P_{e^{+}})+|a_{R}|^{2}(1+P_{e^{-}})(1-P_{e^{+}})\right],

with the chiral amplitude coefficients

a_{L}=\frac{1}{\hat{s}}+\frac{s_{W}^{2}-1/2}{c_{W}^{2}}\frac{1}{\hat{s}-M_{Z}^{2}},\quad a_{R}=\frac{1}{\hat{s}}+\frac{s_{W}^{2}}{c_{W}^{2}}\frac{1}{\hat{s}-M_{Z}^{2}}.

(9)

II.3
Decay properties

The singlet first-generation VLL,
E^{\pm}
, predominantly decays into SM particles through its small mixing with the SM electron. The main decay channels are the charged-gauge-current decay
E^{\pm}\to W^{\pm}\nu_{e}
, the neutral-gauge-current decay
E^{\pm}\to Z^{\pm}e
and the SM Higgs-mediated decay
E^{\pm}\to he^{\pm}
.

The corresponding partial decay widths are given by

\displaystyle\Gamma(E^{\pm}\rightarrow W^{\pm}\nu_{e})

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{32\pi}m_{E}(1+2r_{W})(1-r_{W})^{2},

(10)

\displaystyle\Gamma(E^{\pm}\rightarrow Ze^{\pm})

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{64\pi}m_{E}(1+2r_{Z})(1-r_{Z})^{2},

(11)

\displaystyle\Gamma(E^{\pm}\rightarrow he^{\pm})

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{64\pi}m_{E}(1-r_{h})^{2},

(12)

where
r_{X}=m_{X}^{2}/m_{E}^{2}
for
X=W^{\pm},Z,h
.

In the high-mass regime (
m_{E}\gg m_{W},m_{Z},m_{h}
), the Branching Ratios (BRs) approach the characteristic pattern

\text{BR}(E\rightarrow W^{\pm}\nu_{e}):\text{BR}(E\rightarrow Ze^{\pm}):\text{BR}(E\rightarrow he^{\pm})=2:1:1,

(13)

providing a distinctive signature for VLL identification and discrimination from other BSM scenarios.

III.2
Case 2:
3\ell+2j+\not{E}_{T}
analysis

The dominant SM backgrounds for the
3\ell+2j+\not{E}_{T}
final state (Case 2) are
e^{+}e^{-}\to ZW^{+}W^{-}
and
e^{+}e^{-}\to t\bar{t}Z
. In signal events, the highest-
p_{T}
isolated lepton (
\ell_{1}
) originates from the decay
E^{\pm}\to Ze^{\pm}
, while an Opposite-Sign Same-Flavor (OSSF) lepton pair (
\ell_{2}
,
\ell_{3}
) comes from the
Z
-boson decay whereas the two jets arise predominantly from hadronic
W^{\pm}
-boson decays. Reconstructible mass peaks emerge in
M_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\ell_{3}}
and
M_{T}^{j_{1}j_{2}}
for the processes
E^{\mp}\to W^{\mp}\nu_{e}
and
E^{\pm}\to Ze^{\pm}
. At
\sqrt{s}=$3\text{\,}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$
, the
W
boson from heavy
E^{\pm}
decay is highly boosted, causing its hadronic decay products to merge into a single fat jet; accordingly, the transverse momentum and invariant mass distributions of the leading fat jet
j_{1}
are included in the analysis.

III.3
Statistical analysis

The discovery (
\mathcal{Z}_{\text{disc}}
) and exclusion (
\mathcal{Z}_{\text{excl}}
) significances are calculated following the method in Ref.
Cowan:2010js
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{Z}_{\text{disc}}&=\sqrt{2\left[(s+b)\ln\left(1+\frac{s}{b}\right)-s\right]},\\
\mathcal{Z}_{\text{excl}}&=\sqrt{2\left[s-b\ln\left(1+\frac{s}{b}\right)\right]},\end{aligned}

(15)

where
s
and
b
represent the expected numbers of signal and background events, respectively, for a given integrated luminosity.

IV
Summary

The clean experimental environment of high energy
e^{+}e^{-}
colliders, together with beam polarization capabilities and well-defined initial states, offers distinct advantages in searches for VLLs over hadronic environments. In this work, we have performed a comprehensive analysis of the pair production and decay patterns of weak-isosinglet VLL of electron flavor at future
e^{+}e^{-}
colliders. Using detailed MC simulations at c.m. energies of
\sqrt{s}=$1\text{\,}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$
,
1.5\text{\,}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}
and
3\text{\,}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}
, including beam polarization effects, we have developed optimized search strategies targeting both
2\ell+2j+\not{E}_{T}
and
3\ell+2j+\not{E}_{T}
final states.

Upon adopting a variety of
E^{\pm}
masses within the kinematic reach of the ILC and CLIC prototypes considered here, each with
varying luminosity values,
our results demonstrate that well-designed selection criteria can suppress SM backgrounds efficiently while retaining a high signal acceptance. The first channel is especially powerful for singlet VLL searches, owing to its sizable production cross section and favorable signal-to-background ratio. The projected discovery reaches at
5\sigma
significance extend to
m_{E}\lesssim$490\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$
at
\sqrt{s}=$1\text{\,}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$
with
25\text{\,}\mathrm{f}\mathrm{b}^{-1}
,
m_{E}\lesssim$740\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$
at
\sqrt{s}=$1.5\text{\,}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$
with
90\text{\,}\mathrm{f}\mathrm{b}^{-1}
and
m_{E}\lesssim$1440\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$
at
\sqrt{s}=$3\text{\,}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$
with
1000\text{\,}\mathrm{f}\mathrm{b}^{-1}
, substantially exceeding the current mass limits from hadron colliders. The second channel is less sensitive in comparison but can be used for signal confirmation.

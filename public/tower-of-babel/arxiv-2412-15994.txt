From discrete to continuum in the helical XY-model: emergence of chirality transitions in the S 1 S^{1} to S 2 S^{2} limit
Marco Cicalese, Dario Reggiani, Francesco Solombrino

Abstract
. We analyze the discrete-to-continuum limit of a frustrated ferromagnetic/anti-ferromagnetic 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} -valued spin system on the lattice λ n ​ ℤ 2 \lambda_{n}\mathbb{Z}^{2} as λ n → 0 \lambda_{n}\to 0 . For 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} spin systems close to the Landau-Lifschitz point (where the helimagnetic/ferromagnetic transition occurs), it is well established that for chirality transitions emerge with vanishing energy. Inspired by recent work on the N N -clock model, we consider a spin model where spins are constrained to k n k_{n} copies of 𝕊 1 \mathbb{S}^{1} covering 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} as n → ∞ n\to\infty . We identify a critical energy-scaling regime and a threshold for the divergence rate of k n → + ∞ k_{n}\to+\infty , below which the Γ \Gamma -limit of the discrete energies capture chirality transitions while retaining an 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} -valued energy description in the continuum limit.



1.
Introduction

Spatial variations in magnetization often arise due to the interplay of competing magnetic interactions, geometric constraints, and lattice symmetries, which can prevent the system from simultaneously satisfying all local interaction preferences. These mechanisms, collectively termed frustration, play a crucial role in enabling polarization to be induced and controlled. For its importance in the emergence of ferroelectricity in multiferroic materials geometric frustration has garnered significant attention from both the physical and mathematical communities over the past decades (see
[
21
]
for a comprehensive review). A paradigmatic class of systems exhibiting magnetic frustration is provided by classical spin models with competing ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic interactions, which can lead to helical ground states. Despite extensive investigations, the rigorous understanding of the complete phase diagram of such systems remains an open challenge. In this paper we focus on the helical XY spin model on the square lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, a prototypical example of frustrated system that we analyze, after appropriately scaling its interaction parameters near the Landau-Lifschitz point, where helical ground states are expected to emerge.

The energy of the helical XY spin model on the square lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
in the configuration
u:i\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\mapsto u^{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
is

(1.1)

E(u)=-\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\left(J_{0}(u^{i},u^{i+e_{1}})-J_{1}(u^{i},u^{i+2e_{1}})+J_{2}(u^{i},u^{i+e_{2}})\right),

where
(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and the constants
J_{0}
and
J_{1}
are the interaction parameters for the nearest-neighbors (NN) and the next-to-nearest-neighbors (NNN) interactions in the horizontal direction
e_{1}
, respectively, while
J_{2}
is the interaction parameter for the NN interactions in the vertical direction
e_{2}
.
Under the assumption
J_{0},J_{1},J_{2}>0
, the system exhibits distinct behaviors along the two lattice directions. In the
e_{2}
-direction, the interaction potential
-J_{2}(u^{i},u^{i+e_{2}})
promotes a ferromagnetic spin order with all spins aligned. Conversely, in the
e_{1}
-direction, competing effects arise: ferromagnetic (F) nearest-neighbor (NN) interactions, described by the potential
-J_{0}(u^{i},u^{i+e_{1}})
, favor alignment, whereas antiferromagnetic (AF) next-nearest-neighbor (NNN) interactions, with potential
J_{1}(u^{i},u^{i+2e_{1}})
, favor anti-alignment, that is neighboring spins of opposite orientations. This competition induces a frustration mechanism, evident even along a single horizontal lattice line. The latter is the energy associated with interactions in the
e_{1}
-direction and is given by:

(1.2)

F(u)=-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(J_{0}(u^{j},u^{j+1})-J_{1}(u^{j},u^{j+2})\right).

The energy above models a so-called F/AF
frustrated chain
for which it is well known that no spin configuration can minimize all interactions simultaneously (see
[
21
]
for a comprehensive analysis of frustrated spin systems). In order to simplify the geometry of the ground states of the helical
XY
model we scale the interaction parameters by letting
J_{2}\to+\infty
, hence enforcing spin alignment in the
e_{2}
-direction. As a result, spin configurations
u
with the energy in (
1.1
) finite have a one-dimensional profile; i.e.,
u^{(i_{1},i_{2})}=v^{i_{1}}
for some
v:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{S}^{2}
and their geometry can be understood from the geometry of spin fields
v
with finite one-dimensional energy
F(v)
.
The analysis of the F/AF frustrated chain model on
\mathbb{Z}
in the vicinity of the helimagnetic/ferromagnetic transition point and in the so-called discrete-to-continuum limit was first conjectured in
[
23
]
(an extended version of
[
22
]
) and subsequently rigorously carried out via
\Gamma
-convergence. In this direction initial results were obtained for spins constrained to
\mathbb{S}^{1}
in
[
16
]
(see also
[
28
]
and see
[
10
,
11
]
for two possible extensions of this
\mathbb{S}^{1}
model to
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
) and later extended to the
\mathbb{S}^{2}
case in
[
15
]
(see also
[
27
]
). Before explaining in details our main results we briefly summarize those obtained in the papers
[
16
]
and
[
15
]
.

In
[
16
]
the continuum limit of the F/AF chain energy in (
1.2
) has been studied in the case of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-valued spins. After scaling the functional by a small parameter
\frac{\lambda_{n}}{J_{1}}
(
{\lambda_{n}}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
), and setting
\mathbb{Z}_{n}=\{j\in\mathbb{Z}:\ \lambda_{n}j\in[0,1]\}
one defines
F_{n}:\{u:j\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\mapsto u^{j}\in S^{1}\}\to\mathbb{R}
as

(1.3)

F_{n}(u)=-\alpha\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}{\lambda_{n}}(u^{j},u^{j+1})+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}{\lambda_{n}}(u^{j},u^{j+2})

where
\alpha=J_{0}/J_{1}
is known as the frustration parameter and completely characterizes the ground states of
F_{n}
. More precisely, neighboring spins are aligned if
\alpha\geq 4
(ferromagnetic phase), while they form a constant angle
\varphi=\pm\arccos(\alpha/4)
if
0<\alpha<4
(helimagnetic phase). When the system is in the helimagnetic phase it shows a chiral symmetry. This means that two different families of ground states are possible according to the two possible choices of
\varphi
, corresponding to either clockwise or counter-clockwise handedness of the helical configuration (or analogously spin angular variation) or to what is known as chirality
+1
or
-1
. When
\alpha
is close to
4
the system is at the Landau-Lifschitz point, that is at the onset of frustration. At this point the energy necessary to break the chiral symmetry can be found letting the frustration parameter
\alpha
approach
4
from below. In
[
16
]
the authors consider this limit within the discrete-to-continuum analysis of the model. They let
\alpha
depend on
n
and replace in (
1.3
)
\alpha
by
\alpha_{n}=4(1-{\delta_{n}})
for some vanishing sequence
{\delta_{n}}>0
. They then refer the energy to its minimum by introducing the functional
H_{n}(u):=F_{n}(u)-\min F_{n}
and consider the asymptotic behavior as the lattice spacing
\lambda_{n}\to 0
of a properly scaled version of
H_{n}
. In
[
16
]
the minimal energy necessary to brake the chirality symmetry in the continuum limit is found by computing the
\Gamma
-limit of
H_{n}/({\lambda_{n}}{\delta_{n}}^{3/2})
with respect to the
L^{1}
convergence of the chirality order parameter (a properly scaled version of the angular increment between neighboring spins) as
{\lambda_{n}}\to 0
. More precisely for
{\lambda_{n}}/\sqrt{{\delta_{n}}}\to 0
(at other scalings chirality transitions are either forbidden or not penalized) it is proved that states
u_{n}
with
H_{n}(u_{n})/({\lambda_{n}}{\delta_{n}}^{3/2})
uniformly bounded, have chiralities that converge in the continuum limit to piece-wise constant functions. The energy cost of a chirality transition is then obtained and the
\Gamma
-limit of
H_{n}/({\lambda_{n}}{\delta_{n}}^{3/2})
computed, resulting proportional to the number of jumps of the chirality, namely the number of times the spin configuration changes the sign of its angular velocity. Since the limiting energy penalizes phase transitions, the result in
[
16
]
falls under the category of discrete-to-continuum problems discussed in the monograph
[
1
]
. In the case of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
-valued spins the picture drastically changes. This time the chirality is a vectorial order parameter entailing not only the handedness of the helical configuration but also the axis of rotation which can now vary. While one can still renormalize the energy and show that the modulus of the chirality remains constant for ground states, the transition mechanism between ground states of differing chiralities is different. In
[
15
]
it has been proved that the (
\mathbb{S}^{2}
) helical XY system can adjust its chirality continuously over a slow spatial scale paying little energy in the continuum limit. This phenomenon is rooted in the behavior of the renormalized energy, which at leading order resembles a discrete vectorial Modica-Mortola-type functional in the chirality variable. The potential term associated with this functional has connected wells, enabling low energy transitions. This result aligns with the behavior of Modica-Mortola functionals in the continuum, as rigorously pointed out in the works
[
2
]
and
[
4
]
.

Figure 1.

and an example of
k=7
admissible copies of
\mathbb{S}^{1}

It is worth noting that, in the continuum framework, a similar interplay between ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic (or alignment and anti-alignment) behavior arises in phase transition problems. This occurs when the energy includes two (or more) terms depending on derivatives of the phase variable of different orders, contributing to the total energy with opposing signs (see, for instance,
[
25
,
9
,
17
,
24
]
).

In this paper, we aim to better understand the role of the topology of the spin target space in the emergence of chirality transitions in helical systems. Our work is inspired by the pioneering paper
[
26
]
and
[
13
,
14
,
12
]
, where a question was posed in the case of the
XY
(rotator) model, comparing it to the so-called
N
-clock model. Here, we extend this comparison to the
\mathbb{S}^{2}
helical
XY
model by introducing a multi-dimensional analog of the
N
-clock model. We constrain the spins to belong to a finite collection of
k
distinct copies of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, obtained by rotating a maximal circle in
\mathbb{S}^{2}
by integer multiples of
2\pi/k
around a fixed diameter (see Figure
1
). We investigate the behavior of these spin systems in the continuum limit as before close to the ferromagnetic/helimagnetic transition point (for
\alpha_{n}=(4-\delta_{n})
,
\delta_{n}\to 0
and
\lambda_{n}/\sqrt{\delta_{n}}\to 0
) when
k=k_{n}\to\infty
. To this end we first introduce the family of energies
H^{k_{n}}_{n}
defined such that
H^{k_{n}}_{n}(u)=H_{n}(u)
if the spin field
u
respects the constraints while
H^{k_{n}}_{n}(u)=+\infty
otherwise. We then compute via
\Gamma
-convergence (with respect to the
L^{1}
convergence of a properly scaled chirality order parameter associated to
u
as in the
\mathbb{S}^{2}
case) the discrete-to-continuum limit of the family of energies
H^{k_{n}}_{n}/(\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2})
. As the lattice spacing
\lambda_{n}\to 0
, roughly speaking the collection of
k_{n}
copies of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
gradually covers
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. If
k_{n}
grows sufficiently slowly, the spins cannot modify their chirality continuously over a slow spatial scale at zero energy cost, unlike in the unconstrained
\mathbb{S}^{2}
case.
In Theorem
3.2
, assuming
1\ll k_{n}\ll\delta_{n}^{-1/4}
, we show that the admissible chiralities are piece-wise constant
BV
functions, that each chirality transition pays a fixed energy cost, and the limiting energy is proportional to the number of transitions, hence mirroring the behavior of the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
case (see
[
15
]
for analogous results on helical
XY
models where
\mathbb{S}^{2}
spins are constrained to finitely many
\mathbb{S}^{1}
copies). Furthermore, in Theorem
3.4
, we establish that the upper bound
\delta_{n}^{-1/4}
is sharp. Specifically, for
k_{n}\sim\delta_{n}^{-1/4}
, the limit chirality is still a function of bounded variation, but it is no longer piecewise constant. Moreover the limiting energy scales as its total variation and when a chirality transition occurs, both the
\Gamma\text{-}\liminf
and
\Gamma\text{-}\limsup
of
\frac{H^{k_{n}}_{n}}{\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2}}
may depend on the traces of the chirality. A detailed analysis of this dependence lies beyond the scope of the present work. From a technical perspective, the variational analysis of the present model amounts to analyze the functional
H_{n}^{k_{n}}/{\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2}}
which, in terms of a properly scaled chirality variable can be written as a discrete approximation of a vector-valued Modica-Mortola-type functional (we refer to
[
7
]
for the scalar case). In our framework, the considered functionals involve
k_{n}
disconnected wells that, as
n\to+\infty
converge to a connected set (see also
[
20
,
19
,
8
]
for the study of phase field functionals with varying wells in the context of homogenization). In this respect our proof strategy highlights how the topology of the spin target space in our helical
XY
model, explicitly examined through the introduction of a higher-dimensional analog of the
N
-clock model, can be related to the study of phase-transition functionals whose potential wells undergo topological transformations as the transition length approaches zero.

Notation and Preliminaries

For
d,k\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
\mathcal{L}^{d}
the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and by
\mathcal{H}^{k}
the
k
-dimensional Hausdorff measure in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The Lebesgue measure of a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is indicated with
|E|
. For every
r>0
and every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote by
B_{r}(x)
the open
ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of radius
r
and center
x
. If
x=0
we only write
B_{r}
. The symbol
\mathbb{S}^{N}
stands for the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
. Given two vector
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
we denote with
(a,b)
their scalar product. Given two sequences of real numbers
a_{n},b_{n}
we write
a_{n}\sim b_{n}
if there exist two positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that
c_{1}b_{n}\leq a_{n}\leq c_{2}b_{n}
for every
n\geq 1
.

We now briefly recall some basic definitions about bounded variation functions. We refer to
[
3
, Section 4.5]
for more details on this topic. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open set. The space
\textnormal{BV}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
of functions of bounded variation is the set of
u\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
whose distributional gradient
Du
is a bounded Radon measure on
\Omega
with values in
\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
. Given
u\in\textnormal{BV}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
we can write
Du=D^{a}u+D^{s}u
, where
D^{a}u
is absolutely continuous and
D^{s}u
is singular with respect to
\mathcal{L}^{d}
. The set
J_{u}
denotes the jump set of
u
,
\nu_{u}
stands for the approximate unit normal to
u
, and
[u]:=u^{+}-u^{-}
represents the jump of
u
, that is, the difference between the traces
u^{+}
and
u^{-}
of
u
on
J_{u}
, defined according to the orientation of
\nu_{u}
. The set
J_{u}
is countably rectifiable, while the density
\nabla u\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m\times d})
of
D^{a}u
with respect to
\mathcal{L}^{d}
coincides a.e. in
\Omega
with the approximate gradient of
u
. The space
SBV(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
of special functions of bounded variation is defined as the set of all
u\in\textnormal{BV}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that
|D^{s}u|(\Omega\setminus J_{u})=0
. Moreover, we denote by
SBV_{\textnormal{loc}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
the space of functions belonging to
SBV(U,\mathbb{R}^{m})
for every
U\Subset\Omega
. Given a set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
we say that the countable family
\{E_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha}
is a Caccioppoli partition of
\Omega
if
\mathcal{L}^{d}(\Omega\setminus\cup_{\alpha}E_{\alpha})=0
and
\sum_{\alpha}P(E_{\alpha};\Omega)=0
, where
P(E;\Omega)
is the perimeter of the set
E
relative to
\Omega
.

We now present the definition of bounded variation piecewise constant functions on
\Omega
.

Definition 2.1
.

Given
u\in SBV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
we say that
u\in BV_{\textnormal{pc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
if there exists a Caccioppoli partition of
\Omega
such that
u
is constant on each element of the partition.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with Lipschitz boundary, such that
0\in\Omega
, and let
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n}
be a vanishing sequence. We call
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{d}(\Omega)
the set of all
i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that
\lambda_{n}i\in\Omega
, that is

(2.1)

\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{d}(\Omega):=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\colon\ \lambda_{n}i\in\Omega\}=\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap\lambda_{n}^{-1}\Omega.

We introduce the triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{n}
of
\Omega
associated to
\lambda_{n}
as the triangulation obtained by Freudenthal (also called Kuhn) decomposition of the cubes with vertices
\lambda_{n}i
for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{d}(\Omega+B_{\sqrt{d}\lambda_{n}})
.
We also define the space
\mathcal{U}_{n}^{d}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
as the space of functions
z\colon i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{d}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Given
z\in\mathcal{U}_{n}^{d}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
we will often use
z^{i}
to indicate
z(i)
.

We also need the notion of affine interpolant.

Definition 2.2
.

Given
z\in\mathcal{U}_{n}^{d}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
we call the affine interpolant of
z
, and we indicate it with
\tilde{z}
, the piecewise affine function
\tilde{z}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
\tilde{z}(\lambda_{n}i)=z(i)
for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{d}(\Omega)
and which is affine on the triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{n}
of
\Omega
.

Finally, we recall the notion of Kuratowski limit.

Definition 2.3
.

Given a sequence of closed sets
\{E_{n}\}_{n}
and a closed set
E
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we say that the sequence
E_{n}
converges to
E
in the Kuratowski sense if, given a sequence of points
\{x_{n}\}_{n}
with
x_{n}\in E_{n}
for every
n\geq 1
, we have that any cluster point
x
of
\{x_{n}\}_{n}
belongs to
E
, and every
x\in E
is the limit of a sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n}
with
x_{n}\in E_{n}
. Alternatively, if and only if the sequence of functions
\textnormal{dist}(\cdot,E_{n})
converges locally uniformly to
\textnormal{dist}(\cdot,E)
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

In what follows we introduce the spin model we are interested in and some of the results already obtained in previous papers.

A configuration for the helical XY
\mathbb{S}^{2}
-spin model on the square lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is a map
u\colon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{S}^{2}
whose energy is given by

(2.2)

E(u)=-\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\left(J_{0}(u^{i},u^{i+e_{1}})-J_{1}(u^{i},u^{i+2e_{1}})+J_{2}(u^{i},u^{i+e_{2}})\right),

where the constants
J_{0}
and
J_{1}
are the interaction parameters for the nearest-neighbors (NN) and the next-to-nearest-neighbors (NNN) interactions in the horizontal direction
e_{1}
, respectively, while
J_{2}
is the interaction parameter for the NN interactions in the vertical direction.
We let the three parameters
J_{0},J_{1},J_{2}>0
be scale independent.

Let
\Omega:=[0,1]^{2}
. Recalling the definition of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{2}(\Omega)
in (
2.1
), we define
\mathcal{U}^{2}_{n}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})
as the space of functions
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{n}(\Omega)\mapsto\mathbb{S}^{2}
and
\overline{\mathcal{U}}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})
as the subspace of those functions
u
such that, for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,[1/\lambda_{n}]],
it holds

(2.3)

\left(u^{(1,m)},u^{(0,m)}\right)=\left(u^{([1/\lambda_{n}],m)},u^{([1/\lambda_{n}]-1,m)}\right),

where
[\cdot]
denotes the integer part. We moreover set
R^{1}_{n}(\Omega):=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{2}(\Omega)\colon\ i+2e_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{2}(\Omega)\}
and
R^{2}_{n}(\Omega):=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{2}(\Omega)\colon\ i+e_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{2}(\Omega)\}
.

Without loss of generality we scale the energy (
2.2
) by
J_{1,n}:=J_{1}/\lambda_{n}
and rename
J_{0,n}
and
J_{2,n}
accordingly, obtaining the energy for
u\in\overline{\mathcal{U}}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})
given by

(2.4)

E_{n}(u):=-\sum_{i\in R^{1}_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda^{2}_{n}{J_{0,n}(u^{i},u^{i+e_{1}})-(u^{i},u^{i+2e_{1}})}-J_{2,n}\sum_{i\in R^{2}_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}^{2}(u^{i},u^{i+e_{2}}).

We refer to
[
16
,
15
]
for the computation of the energy
E_{n}
ground states. Taking
J_{0,n}>4
leads to trivial ferromagnetic ground states. Hence, we focus on the asymptotics of the renormalized energy

H_{n}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{i\in R^{1}_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}^{2}\left|u^{i}-\frac{J_{0,n}}{2}u^{i+e_{1}}+u^{i+2e_{1}}\right|^{2}+J_{2,n}\sum_{i\in R^{2}_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}^{2}\left|u^{i+e_{2}}-u^{i}\right|^{2}\right),

when the parameter
J_{0,n}
is in the vicinity of the Landau-Lifschitz point
J_{0}=4
and the parameter
J_{2,n}
diverges. To this aim, we introduce
\delta_{n}\to 0
and consider
J_{0,n}=4(1-\delta_{n})
. We thus rewrite the energy
H_{n}
as

(2.5)

H_{n}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{i\in R^{1}_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}^{2}\left|u^{i}-2(1-\delta_{n})u^{i+e_{1}}+u^{i+2e_{1}}\right|^{2}+J_{2,n}\sum_{i\in R^{2}_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}^{2}\left|u^{i+e_{2}}-u^{i}\right|^{2}\right).

Within this choice stable states have a one-dimensional helical structure and may
exhibit chirality transitions in the propagation direction (the horizontal axis in our case). Consequently, the analysis we are going to perform starts by considering energies on one-dimensional horizontal slices of the domain. To this aim we introduce some additional notation. Let
I=[0,1]
and define
\mathbb{Z}_{n}(I)
as the set of points
i\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\lambda_{n}i\in[0,1]
. Define also
R_{n}(I):=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}(I)\colon\ i+2\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}(I)\}
. Similarly as in the two-dimensional setting we denote with
\mathcal{U}_{n}(I;\mathbb{S}^{2})
the space of functions
u\colon i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}(I)\mapsto u^{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
and with
\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{n}(I;\mathbb{S}^{2})
the subspace of those
u
such that

(2.6)

\left(u^{1},u^{0}\right)=\left(u^{[1/\lambda_{n}]},u^{[1/\lambda_{n}]-1}\right).

It is convenient also to consider the following class of piecewise constant functions: given
u\in\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{n}(I)
we associate to it a piecewise constant interpolation belonging to the class

C_{n}(I;\mathbb{S}^{2}):=\{u\in\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{n}(I;\mathbb{S}^{2})\colon\ u(x)=u^{i}\ \ \mbox{if}\ x\in\lambda_{n}(i+[0,1)),\ i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}(I)\}.

The one-dimensional (sliced) renormalized energy is denoted by
H_{n}^{sl}\colon L^{\infty}(I;\mathbb{R}^{3})\to[0,+\infty]
and it is given by

(2.7)

H_{n}^{sl}(u):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}\left|u^{i}-2(1-\delta_{n})u^{i+1}+u^{i+2}\right|^{2}&if $u\in C_{n}(I,\mathbb{S}^{2})$,\\
+\infty&otherwise.\end{cases}

It was proven in
[
15
, Proposition 2.1]
that the zero order
\Gamma
-limit of
H_{n}^{sl}
is trivial.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
H_{n}^{sl}\colon L^{\infty}(I;\mathbb{R}^{3})\to[0,+\infty]
be the functional defined in (
2.7
). The
\Gamma\mbox{-}\lim_{n}H_{n}^{sl}
with respect to the weak*-convergence in
L^{\infty}
is given by

H^{sl}(u)=\begin{cases}0&if $|u|\leq 1$,\\
+\infty&otherwise.\end{cases}

This degeneracy of minima for
H^{sl}
suggests to perform a higher order analysis by
\Gamma
-convergence. In order to study the higher order asymptotic behavior of the one dimensional renormalized energy we start by introducing an order parameter. Given a function
u\in C_{n}(I;\mathbb{S}^{2})
, for all
i\in\{1,\dots,[1/\lambda_{n}]-1\}
, we set

(2.8)

\theta^{i}(u)=\arccos((u^{i},u^{i+1}))\in[0,\pi],

and
w^{i}=u^{i}\times u^{i+1}
. We also introduce a new order parameter
z\colon\mathbb{Z}_{n}(I)\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
defined as

(2.9)

z^{i}=\frac{u^{i}\times u^{i+1}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}=\frac{w^{i}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}},

which represents a rescaled angular velocity (to be well defined we set
z^{[1/\lambda_{n}]}:=z^{[1/\lambda_{n}]-1}
). Such
z
will be extended in
L^{1}(I;\mathbb{R}^{3})
by means of a piecewise constant interpolation. We define the map
T_{n}\colon C_{n}(I;\mathbb{S}^{2})\to L^{1}(I;\mathbb{R}^{3})
as the map that associate to each
u
the corresponding
z
according to (
2.9
). Notice that the map
T_{n}
is not injective and if
u
satisfies periodic boundary conditions in the sense of (
2.3
), then
|z|
is periodic and vice-versa. Thus, we define the energy
H^{sl}_{n}
on
L^{1}(I;\mathbb{R}^{3})
as

(2.10)

H^{sl}_{n}(z):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\inf_{T_{n}(u)=z}H^{sl}_{n}(u)&if $z=T_{n}(u)$ for some $u\in C_{n}(I;\mathbb{S}^{2})$,\\
+\infty&otherwise.\end{cases}

Taking the infimum in the definition above has no effect in the asymptotic analysis we are going to perform (see
[
15
, Remark 3.1]
). However, without restriction on the possible values the configuration
u
can take on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, it was proven in
[
15
]
that the
\Gamma
-limit of
H^{sl}_{n}
is trivial as well. That is, in contrast to the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-valued spin system, for
\mathbb{S}^{2}
-valued spins the functional
H^{sl}_{n}
does not penalize chirality transitions between ground states and the optimal asymptotic energy for a transition turns out to be zero. The following result is contained in
[
15
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
H_{n}^{sl}\colon L^{1}(I;\mathbb{R}^{3})\to[0,+\infty]
be defined as in (
2.10
). Assume that
\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\sqrt{\delta_{n}}}\to 0
. Then the rescaled functionals
\frac{H^{sl}_{n}}{\sqrt{2}\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2}}

\Gamma
-converge with respect to the weak*-convergence in
L^{\infty}(I)
to the functional

H^{sl}(z)=\begin{cases}0&if $z\in L^{\infty}(I,B_{1})$,\\
+\infty&otherwise.\end{cases}

3.
Main results

In order to better understand the role of topology in the emergence of chirality transitions, drawing inspiration from
[
26
]
and
[
13
,
14
,
12
]
, we modify the energy
H^{sl}_{n}
introduced in (
2.10
) by adding a constraint on the possible values the spin variable
u
can take on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Namely, we impose that
u
can take values only on a subset of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
consisting of
k
copies of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We show that if
k=k_{n}
diverge "slowly enough" as
n\to+\infty
, we can detect a non-trivial limit energy. To carry out the analysis we need to fix additional notation. Given
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
q_{1},\dots,q_{k}
be a family of
k
distinct points in
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. For every
l\in\{1,\dots,k\}
we set
S^{1}_{l}:=\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap q_{l}^{\perp}
, where
q_{l}^{\perp}
is the orthogonal complement of
q_{l}
. We also define

Q_{k}:=\{q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\},\qquad M_{k}:=\bigcup_{l=1}^{k}S^{1}_{l},\qquad\mathfrak{L}_{k}:=\bigcup_{l=1}^{k}\textnormal{span}(q_{l}).

We restrict the spin variable
u
to take values only in
M_{k}
. Namely, we consider the subset
C_{n}(I;M_{k})
of
C_{n}(I;\mathbb{S}^{2})
, defined as
C_{n}(I;\mathbb{S}^{2})
but with
M_{k}
in place of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, and we define the energy
H_{n}^{sl,k}\colon L^{1}(I)\to[0,+\infty]
as

(3.1)

H^{sl,k}_{n}(z):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\inf_{T_{n}(u)=z}H^{sl}_{n}(u)&if $z=T_{n}(u)$ for some $u\in C_{n}(I;M_{k})$,\\
+\infty&otherwise.\end{cases}

Along the sequence the value
k
and the
k
points
\{q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\}
can depend on
n
. Thus, to highlight the possible dependence of
k
on
n
, we will write
k_{n}
in place of
k
. Under the assumption
k_{n}\delta_{n}^{1/4}\to 0
we show that we can recover compactness and
\Gamma
-convergence for the functionals
H^{sl,k_{n}}_{n}
to a non-trivial limit functional.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that
\lambda_{n}/\sqrt{\delta_{n}}\to 0
and consider a sequence
k_{n}
in
\mathbb{N}
such that
k_{n}\delta_{n}^{1/4}\to 0
. Let
z_{n}=T_{n}(u_{n})
for some
u_{n}\in C_{n}(I,M_{k_{n}})
be such that

(3.2)

H^{sl,k_{n}}_{n}(z_{n})\leq C\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2},\qquad\mbox{for some}\ \ C>0.

Then, denoting by
\overline{Q}
the Kuratowski limit of the sets
\{Q_{k_{n}}\}_{n}
, there exists
z\in BV_{\textnormal{pc}}(I,\overline{Q})
such that (up to subsequences)
z_{n}\to z
strongly in
L^{1}(I)
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
H^{sl,k_{n}}_{n}\colon L^{1}(I)\to[0,+\infty]
be defined as in (
3.1
). Assume that
\lambda_{n}/\sqrt{\delta_{n}}\to 0
and
k_{n}\delta_{n}^{1/4}\to 0
. Then, the rescaled functionals
\frac{H^{sl,k_{n}}_{n}}{\sqrt{2}\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2}}

\Gamma
-converge with respect to the strong
L^{1}
-topology to the functional

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{8}{3}\#S(z)&if $z\in BV_{\textnormal{pc}}(I,\overline{Q})$;\\
+\infty&otherwise.\end{cases}

The proof of Proposition
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
is based on some general results about discrete phase field functionals with co-domain constraints which we present in Section
4
.

Thanks to Proposition
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
we can study the asymptotic behavior of the renormalized XY-model, that is, the energy
H_{n}
defined in (
2.5
) scaled by
\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2}
, in the limit of strong ferromagnetic interactions. To be precise, we assume that

(3.3)

\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\sqrt{\delta_{n}}}\to 0,\qquad J_{2,n}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\sqrt{\delta_{n}}}\geq c>0.

We embed the energies in a common function space, to this end we identify every function
u\in\overline{\mathcal{U}}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})
with its piecewise constant interpolation belonging to the space

C_{n,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2}):=\left\{u\in\overline{\mathcal{U}}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})\colon\ u(x)=u(\lambda_{n}i)\mbox{ if $x\in\lambda_{n}(i+[0,1)^{2}),i\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{n}(\Omega)$}\right\}.

We can extend the functional
H_{n}
to a functional defined on
L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})
setting

H_{n}(u):=\begin{cases}H_{n}(u)&if $u\in C_{n,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})$,\\
+\infty&otherwise.\end{cases}

Given
u\in C_{n,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})
we define
z\in C_{n,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
as

z^{i}:=\frac{u^{i}\times u^{i+e_{1}}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}

and write for short
z:=T_{n}(u)
. Given a family of
k
points
Q_{k}\subset\mathbb{S}^{2}
, we can define the constrained energy
H_{n}^{k}\colon L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\to[0,+\infty]
setting

(3.4)

H^{k}_{n}(z):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\inf_{T_{n}(u)=z}H_{n}(u)&if $z=T_{n}(u)$ for some $u\in C_{n,2}(\Omega;M_{k})$,\\
+\infty&otherwise.\end{cases}

The following result holds.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
H^{k}_{n}\colon L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\to[0,+\infty]
be defined as in (
3.4
), assume that
k_{n}\delta_{n}^{1/4}\to 0
and (
3.3
) hold. Then, the rescaled functionals
\frac{H_{n}^{k}}{\sqrt{2}\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\Gamma
-converge with respect to the strong
L^{1}
-topology to the functional

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{8}{3}\mathcal{H}^{1}(J_{z})&if $z\in BV_{\textnormal{pc}}(\Omega,\overline{Q})$ and $z$ does not depend on $y$;\\
+\infty&otherwise.\end{cases}

Above,
\overline{Q}
is defined as in Proposition
3.1
.

Proof.

The proof of this result follows by Proposition
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
arguing exactly as in Section 4 of
[
15
]
. Hence, we omit it.
∎

Finally, if
k_{n}\sim\delta_{n}^{-1/4}
we show that the previous considerations do not hold in general. Indeed, we have the following result.

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume that
\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\delta_{n}^{3/4}}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
and let
k_{n}=2\lceil\delta_{n}^{-1/4}\rceil
. There exist configurations
Q_{k_{n}}
and two constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

C_{1}|Dz|(I)\leq\Gamma\mbox{-}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{H^{sl,k_{n}}_{n}}{\sqrt{2}\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2}}(z)\leq\Gamma\mbox{-}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{H^{sl,k_{n}}_{n}}{\sqrt{2}\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2}}(z)\leq C_{2}|Dz|(I).

for every
z\in BV(I,\overline{Q})
.

Remark 3.5
.

Theorem
3.4
implies that in the critical case
k_{n}\sim\delta_{n}^{-1/4}
, there exists configurations such that the
\Gamma
-limit behaves asymptotically as a total variation.
In particular, in the aforementioned case, we have that for a general
z\in BV_{\textnormal{pc}}(I,\overline{Q})

\Gamma\mbox{-}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{sl,k_{n}}}{\sqrt{2}\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2}}(z)\neq\frac{8}{3}S(z)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\Gamma\mbox{-}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{sl,k_{n}}}{\sqrt{2}\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2}}(z)\neq\frac{8}{3}S(z).

Moreover, both the
\Gamma\mbox{-}\liminf
and the
\Gamma\mbox{-}\limsup
may in general depend on the traces
z^{+}
and
z^{-}
of a function
z\in BV_{\textnormal{pc}}(I,\overline{Q})
on jump points. The analysis of such a dependence requires additional discussions which would exceed the scope of this paper.

4.
Discrete phase transitions with co-domain constraints

This section contains the results regarding phase field functionals defined on a discretized domain and with co-domain constraints. First, we fix some notation.

Let
|\cdot|_{A}\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
be a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that there exist
c_{1},c_{2}>0
with the property that

(4.1)

c_{1}|\xi|\leq|\xi|_{A}\leq c_{2}|\xi|\qquad\mbox{for every $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$},

and the set
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\colon\ |\xi|_{A}=1\}=:K
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Let
\varepsilon_{n}
and
r_{n}
be two vanishing positive sequences, and let
k_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
a possibly diverging sequence. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider
k_{n}
points
\{q_{j}^{n}\}_{j=1}^{k_{n}}\subset K
. Let
Q_{n}:=\{q_{j}^{n}\}_{j=1}^{k_{n}}
and
\overline{Q}\subseteq K
the Kuratowski limit of the sets
Q_{n}
as
n\to+\infty
. We set
\mathfrak{L}^{n}_{j}:=\textnormal{span}(q_{n}^{j})+B_{r_{n}}
, for
j=1,\dots,k_{n}
and
\mathfrak{L}^{n}:=\cup_{j=1}^{k_{n}}\mathfrak{L}_{j}^{n}
. We make the following assumptions:

(i)

\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}r_{n}^{2}}
is a bounded sequence (the cylinders
\mathfrak{L}_{j}^{n}
must not be too close to one another);

(ii)

for every
\eta>0
, there exists
\overline{n}=\overline{n}(\eta)
such that for every
n\geq\overline{n}
we have

\mathfrak{L}_{j}^{n}\cap\mathfrak{L}_{m}^{n}\subset B_{\eta},\qquad\mbox{for every $j,m=1,\dots,k_{n}$ with $j\neq m$.}

Set
g_{n}\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{+\infty\}
as

(4.2)

g_{n}(\xi):=\begin{cases}(|\xi|_{A}^{2}-1)^{2}&$\xi\in\mathfrak{L}^{n}$;\\
+\infty&otherwise.\end{cases}

We set
R_{n}(\Omega)\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
as the set of all
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{d}(\Omega)
such that
i+e_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{d}(\Omega)
for every
\ell=1,\dots,d
. We are interested in the asymptotic analysis of the following energies for
z_{n}\in\mathcal{U}^{d}_{n}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

(4.3)

\mathcal{F}_{n}(z_{n}):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}^{d}g_{n}(z_{n}^{i})+\varepsilon_{n}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}^{d}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\left|\frac{z_{n}^{i}-z_{n}^{i+e_{\ell}}}{\lambda_{n}}\right|^{2}\right).

The following compactness result holds true.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
z_{n}\in\mathcal{U}^{d}_{n}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a sequence such that for every
n\geq 1

(4.4)

\mathcal{F}_{n}(z_{n})\leq C\qquad\mbox{for some $C>0$}.

Then, there exists
z\in SBV_{\textnormal{pc}}(\Omega;\overline{Q})
such that, up to subsequences,
z_{n}\to z
in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Thanks to Proposition
4.1
, under the additional requirement that
\lim_{n}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}r_{n}^{2}}\to 0
, we can prove the following lower bound for the
\Gamma
-limit. We define the function
h\colon\overline{Q}\times\overline{Q}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
as

(4.5)

h(q_{1},q_{2}):=\frac{4}{3}(|q_{1}|+|q_{2}|).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
z_{n}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}^{d}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a sequence such that
z_{n}\to z
in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then, if
\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}r_{n}^{2}}\to 0
,

(4.6)

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{n}(z_{n})\geq\int_{J_{z}}h(z^{-},z^{+})\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}.

Remark 4.3
.

Notice that the domain of definition of the function
h
is a subset of
K
, that is the
1
-level set of the norm
|\cdot|_{A}
. Moreover, indicating with
\hat{q}
the unit vector
q/|q|
, the transition energy
h
can also be rewritten as

h(q_{1},q_{2})=\frac{4}{3}\left(\frac{1}{|\hat{q}_{1}|_{A}}+\frac{1}{|\hat{q}_{2}|_{A}}\right).

Remark 4.4
.

The condition
\lim_{n}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}r_{n}^{2}}\to 0
ensures that the transitions between two different
\mathfrak{L}^{n}_{j}
’s can be detected in the limit by the norm of
z_{n}
being close to zero.

Finally, an upper bound matching with the lower bound holds. This entails a
\Gamma
-convergence result when
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
for the energies defined in (
4.3
). Notice that the assumptions on
\lambda_{n},\varepsilon_{n}
and
r_{n}
are weaker for the upper bound to hold.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
z\in BV_{\textnormal{pc}}(\Omega;\overline{Q})
. Then, there exists a sequence
z_{n}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}^{d}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
z_{n}\to z
in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{n}(z_{n})\leq\int_{J_{z}}h(z^{-},z^{+})\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1},

where
h
is the function in (
4.5
).

We will use the following compactness result for piecewise constant functions defined on Caccioppoli partitions (see
[
3
]
).

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open bounded set with Lipschitz boundary. Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence in
SBV_{\textnormal{pc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\sup_{n}\left(\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u_{n}})\right)<+\infty
. Then, there exits
u\in SBV_{\textnormal{pc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and a subsequence (not relabeled) such that
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Observe that by (
4.4
) we have
z_{n}\in\mathfrak{L}^{n}
almost everywhere on
\Omega
, moreover, the functions
g_{n}
are independent of
n
on the set
\mathfrak{L}^{n}
, continuous and bounded below by
c|\xi|^{4}
for some
c>0
when
|\xi|\gg 1
. Hence, using standard arguments, we infer that the sequence
\{z_{n}\}
is bounded in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and equiintegrable on
\Omega
. Consider the sequence

T(z_{n}):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle 2\frac{z_{n}}{|z_{n}|_{A}}&if $|z_{n}|_{A}\geq 2$,\\
z_{n}&otherwise.\end{cases}

By (
4.1
) and the definition of
g_{n}
we have
\mathcal{F}_{n}(T(z_{n}))\leq\mathcal{F}_{n}(z_{n})
. We claim that if
T(z_{n})
converges in
L^{1}
to
z\in BV(\Omega;\overline{Q})
, then
z_{n}
converges to
z
in
L^{1}
on
\Omega
as well. Indeed, since
\sup_{n}\mathcal{F}_{n}(z_{n})<+\infty
by (
4.4
), we have that
|z_{n}|_{A}\to 1
pointwise on
\Omega
as
n\to+\infty
. Hence,
|\{|z_{n}|_{A}\geq 2\}|\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
. Recalling that
|T(z_{n})|_{A}\leq 2
by construction and the fact that the sequence
z_{n}
is equiintegrable on
\Omega
, we conclude that
z_{n}\to z
in
L^{1}
as well. Thus, since it is enough to prove compactness for equibounded sequences on
\Omega
, we may assume that
\sup_{n}\|z_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C
. Let us define the piecewise constant scalar function
v_{n}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}^{d}(\Omega)
as
v_{n}:=|z_{n}|_{A}
. By (
4.2
), (
4.4
), triangular inequality and recalling (
4.1
), we have

(4.7)

\sup_{n}\left[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}^{d}((v_{n}^{i})^{2}-1)^{2}+\varepsilon_{n}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}^{d}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\left|\frac{v_{n}^{i}-v_{n}^{i+e_{\ell}}}{\lambda_{n}}\right|^{2}\right)\right]\leq(1+c_{2}^{2})C.

Consider piecewise affine function
\tilde{v}_{n}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
associated to
v_{n}
according to Definition
2.2
. Given
\sigma>0
small, using the equi-boundedness of
z_{n}
on
\Omega
and (
4.7
) we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}((\tilde{v}_{n})^{2}-1)^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq(1+3\sigma)\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}^{d}((v_{n}^{i})^{2}-1)^{2}+\left(1+\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)\int_{\Omega}\left((v_{n})^{2}-(\tilde{v}_{n})^{2}\right)^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq(1+3\sigma)\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}^{d}((v_{n}^{i})^{2}-1)^{2}+C\left(1+\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)\int_{\Omega}\left(v_{n}-\tilde{v}_{n}\right)^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq(1+3\sigma)\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}^{d}((v_{n}^{i})^{2}-1)^{2}+C\left(1+\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}^{d}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\left|v_{n}^{i}-v_{n}^{i+e_{\ell}}\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(1+3\sigma)C\varepsilon_{n}+C(1+c_{2}^{2})\left(1+\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)\frac{\lambda_{n}^{2}}{\varepsilon_{n}}.

Hence, fixing
\sigma>0
, we have that

(4.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sup_{n}&\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}(\tilde{v}_{n}^{2}-1)^{2}\,dx+\varepsilon_{n}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\tilde{v}_{n}|^{2}\,dx\right)\\
&=\sup_{n}\left[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}(\tilde{v}_{n}^{2}-1)^{2}\,dx+\varepsilon_{n}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}^{d}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\left|\frac{v_{n}^{i}-v_{n}^{i+e_{\ell}}}{\lambda_{n}}\right|^{2}\right)\right]\\
&\leq C+\frac{\lambda_{n}^{2}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}C\leq C(1+r_{n}^{2})\leq C.\end{split}

Using the coarea formula we infer

C\geq\int_{\Omega}2(\tilde{v}_{n}^{2}-1)|\nabla\tilde{v}_{n}|\,dx\geq\frac{3}{2}\int_{1/4}^{1/2}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\partial^{*}\{\tilde{v}_{n}<s\}\setminus\partial\Omega)\,ds.

Therefore, by the mean value theorem, for every
n\geq 1
there exists
t_{n}\in(1/4,1/2)
such that

(4.9)

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\partial^{*}\{\tilde{v}_{n}<t_{n}\}\setminus\partial\Omega)\leq C.

Moreover,

(4.10)

|\{\tilde{v}_{n}<t_{n}\}\cap\Omega|\leq\frac{16}{9}\int_{\Omega}(\tilde{v}_{n}^{2}-1)^{2}\,dx\leq\varepsilon_{n}C\to 0.

We define the set of indexes where the difference between two adjacent points is bigger than
r_{n}/4
, namely

\mathcal{I}_{n}:=\left\{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)\colon\ \max_{\ell=1,\dots,d}\left\{\left|z_{n}^{i}-z_{n}^{i\pm e_{\ell}}\right|\right\}\geq\frac{r_{n}}{4}\right\}.

Notice that if we define

\mathcal{J}_{n}:=\left\{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)\colon\ \max_{\ell=1,\dots,d}\left\{\left|z_{n}^{i}-z_{n}^{i+e_{\ell}}\right|\right\}\geq\frac{r_{n}}{4}\right\},

then we have that
\#\mathcal{J}_{n}\geq\frac{\#\mathcal{I}_{n}}{\#([-1,1]^{d}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d})}
.
Hence, in view of (
4.4
) we can estimate

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon_{n}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}^{d}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\left|\frac{z_{n}^{i}-z_{n}^{i+e_{\ell}}}{\lambda_{n}}\right|^{2}\right)\geq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}_{n}}\lambda_{n}^{d-2}\varepsilon_{n}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\left|z_{n}^{i}-z_{n}^{i+e_{\ell}}\right|^{2}\right)\geq\lambda_{n}^{d-2}\varepsilon_{n}\left(\#\mathcal{J}_{n}\right)\frac{r_{n}^{2}}{16}.

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{v_{n}^{2}}{|\hat{z}_{n}|^{2}}-1\right)^{2}\,dx+\varepsilon_{n}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\,dx\right)\leq(1+\tau)^{2}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\int_{J_{z}}\frac{4}{3}\left(|\hat{z}_{n}^{-}(x)|+|\hat{z}_{n}^{+}(x)|\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x).

We are going to prove the claim sketching the arguments as they are quite standard.
First notice that the intersection of the interfaces in
J_{z}
is a set of finite
\mathcal{H}^{d-2}
measure. Thus, in an arbitrarily small neighborhood of this set the energy is arbitrarily small. Moreover, far from
J_{z}
we have that taking
n
large enough the energy associated to
v_{n}
is arbitrarily small. Let now
T
be an interface of
J_{z}
(without loss of generality we can assume that
T
lies on
\{x_{d}=0\}
). Take
\gamma>0
such that
(T\times(-\gamma,\gamma))\cap J_{z}=T
, we can always find such
\gamma
up replacing
T
with
T^{\prime}\Subset T
compactly contained in the topology of
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
. Assume that
\hat{z}_{n}=q_{1}^{n}
on
T\times(-\gamma,0)
and
\hat{z}_{n}=q_{2}^{n}
on
T\times(0,\gamma)
. We estimate for
n
large enough

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{T\times(-\gamma,0)}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{v_{n}^{2}}{|\hat{z}_{n}|^{2}}-1\right)^{2}\,dx+\varepsilon_{n}\int_{T\times(-\gamma,0)}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{T}\int_{-\gamma}^{0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\left(f_{\tau}\left(\frac{\delta_{n}(x^{\prime},t)}{\varepsilon_{n}|q_{1}^{n}|}\right)^{2}-1\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}f_{\tau}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\delta_{n}(x^{\prime},t)}{\varepsilon_{n}|q_{1}^{n}|}\right)^{2}|\nabla\delta_{n}(x^{\prime},t)|^{2}\,dt\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq(1+\tau)|q_{1}^{n}|\left(\int_{T}\int_{0}^{\frac{\delta_{n}(x^{\prime},-\gamma)}{\varepsilon_{n}|q_{2}^{n}|}}\left(f_{\tau}(s)^{2}-1\right)^{2}+(f^{\prime}_{\tau}(s))^{2}\,ds\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\tau)^{2}|\hat{z}_{n}|\frac{4}{3}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(T).

Repeating the same argument for
T\times(0,\gamma)
and then for every interface in
J_{z}
we conclude the claim. We now estimate the difference between the energy in (
4.20
) and the discretized one. Let us define the discretized function
v_{n}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}^{d}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
v_{n}^{i}:=v_{n}(\lambda_{n}i)
. For every
\sigma>0
small, using the fact that
v_{n}
is equibounded and vanishes near
J_{z}
, we infer

(4.21)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}&\lambda_{n}^{d}\left(\frac{(v_{n}^{i})^{2}}{|\hat{z}_{n}(\lambda_{n}i)|^{2}}-1\right)^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{1+3\sigma}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{v_{n}^{2}}{|\hat{z}_{n}|^{2}}-1\right)^{2}\,dx+\frac{C}{\varepsilon_{n}}\left(1+\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\int_{\lambda_{n}i+[0,\lambda_{n})^{d}}\left|v_{n}-v_{n}(\lambda_{n}i)\right|^{2}\,dx.\end{split}

Let
K_{\lambda}:=J_{z}+B_{\lambda}
for
\lambda>0
. We now estimate the reminder term in (
4.21
). By (
4.17
) and (
4.19
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\int_{\lambda_{n}i+[0,\lambda_{n})^{d}}\left|v_{n}(x)-v_{n}(\lambda_{n}i)\right|^{2}\,dx&\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\int_{(\lambda_{n}i+[0,\lambda_{n})^{d})\cap K_{\varepsilon_{n}/\tau}}\frac{\|f_{\tau}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}|\hat{z}_{n}|^{2}}\|\nabla\delta_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}\,dx\\
&\leq C\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\frac{\lambda_{n}^{2}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}|K_{\varepsilon_{n}/\tau}|\leq\frac{C\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{z})}{\tau}\frac{\lambda_{n}^{2}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}.\end{split}

Hence, since
\lambda_{n}/\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
,

(4.22)

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}^{d}\left(\frac{(v_{n}^{i})^{2}}{|\hat{z}_{n}(\lambda_{n}i)|^{2}}-1\right)^{2}\leq\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1+3\sigma}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{v_{n}^{2}}{|\hat{z}_{n}|^{2}}-1\right)^{2}\,dx.

Similarly as before, for every
\sigma>0
small we have

(4.23)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\varepsilon_{n}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}^{d}&\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\left|\frac{v_{n}^{i}-v^{i+e_{\ell}}_{n}}{\lambda_{n}}\right|^{2}\right)=\varepsilon_{n}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}^{d}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\left|\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\int_{0}^{\lambda_{n}}\frac{\partial v_{n}}{\partial x_{\ell}}(\lambda_{n}i+se_{\ell})\,ds\right|^{2}\right)\\
&\leq\varepsilon_{n}(1+3\sigma)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\,dx\\
&\ \ +\varepsilon_{n}\left(1+\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\int_{\lambda_{n}i+[0,\lambda_{n})^{d}}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\left|\frac{\partial v_{n}}{\partial x_{\ell}}(x)-\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\int_{0}^{\lambda_{n}}\frac{\partial v_{n}}{\partial x_{\ell}}(\lambda_{n}i+se_{\ell})ds\right|^{2}\right)\,dx\end{split}

We now estimate the remainder term in (
4.23
). Using Jensen inequality, (
4.17
) and (
4.19
) we get

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{n}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\int_{\lambda_{n}i+[0,\lambda_{n})^{d}}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\left|\frac{\partial v_{n}}{\partial x_{\ell}}(x)-\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\int_{0}^{\lambda_{n}}\frac{\partial v_{n}}{\partial x_{\ell}}(\lambda_{n}i+se_{\ell})ds\right|^{2}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{\lambda_{n}}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\int_{(\lambda_{n}i+[0,\lambda_{n})^{d})\cap K_{\varepsilon_{n}/\tau}\setminus K_{\sqrt{d}\lambda_{n}}}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{\lambda_{n}}\left|\frac{\partial v_{n}}{\partial x_{\ell}}(x)-\frac{\partial v_{n}}{\partial x_{\ell}}(\lambda_{n}i+se_{\ell})\right|^{2}ds\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{n}C\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\int_{(\lambda_{n}i+[0,\lambda_{n})^{d})\cap K_{\varepsilon_{n}/\tau}\setminus K_{\sqrt{d}\lambda_{n}}}\lambda_{n}^{2}\sup_{\lambda_{n}i+[0,\lambda_{n})^{d}}|\nabla^{2}v_{n}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{n}\lambda_{n}^{2}C\left(\frac{C}{\varepsilon_{n}^{4}}|K_{\varepsilon_{n}/\tau}|+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{z})\int_{\sqrt{d}\lambda_{n}}^{\varepsilon_{n}/\tau}\frac{C}{t^{2}}\,dt\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\tau}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{z})\left(\frac{\lambda_{n}^{2}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}+\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right).

Therefore,

(4.24)

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\ \varepsilon_{n}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}^{d}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\left|\frac{v_{n}^{i}-v^{i+e_{\ell}}_{n}}{\lambda_{n}}\right|^{2}\right)\leq\limsup_{n\to+\infty}(1+3\sigma)\varepsilon_{n}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\,dx.

Combining (
4.20
), (
4.22
), (
4.24
) and the fact that
\hat{z}_{n}\to z
uniformly on
\Omega
, we conclude

(4.25)

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{n}(v_{n})\leq(1+3\sigma)(1+\tau)^{2}\int_{J_{z}}\frac{4}{3}\left(|z^{-}(x)|+|z^{+}(x)|\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x).

Notice that by definition
v_{n}\to|\hat{z}_{n}|
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. We are now ready to define the recovery sequence
z_{n}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}^{d}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
z_{n}:=v_{n}\frac{\hat{z}_{n}}{|\hat{z}_{n}|}
. Notice that by construction it holds
z_{n}\to z
in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Finally, since
v_{n}=0
on
K_{\sqrt{d}\lambda_{n}}
and
|z_{n}|=v_{n}
by definition, we infer that for every
n\geq 1
we have
|z_{n}^{i}-z_{n}^{i+e_{\ell}}|=|v_{n}^{i}-v_{n}^{i+e_{\ell}}|
for every
i\in R_{n}(\Omega)
and
\ell=1,\dots,d
. Thus,

\mathcal{F}_{n}(z_{n})=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}^{d}\left(\frac{(v_{n}^{i})^{2}}{|\hat{z}_{n}(\lambda_{n}i)|^{2}}-1\right)^{2}+\varepsilon_{n}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}^{d}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\left|\frac{v_{n}^{i}-v^{i+e_{\ell}}_{n}}{\lambda_{n}}\right|^{2}\right).

That is,

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{n}(z_{n})\leq(1+3\sigma)(1+\tau)^{2}\int_{J_{z}}\frac{4}{3}\left(|z^{-}(x)|+|z^{+}(x)|\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x).

By arbitrariness of
\tau>0
and
\sigma>0
we conclude the limsup inequality.
∎

5.
Chirality transition in the regime
1\ll k_{n}\ll\delta_{n}^{-1/4}

This section is devoted to the proof of compactness and
\Gamma
-convergence for the chirality model defined in (
3.1
), in the case
k_{n}\delta_{n}^{1/4}\to 0
. Finally, at the end of this section we prove Theorem
3.4
. We recall now some important facts we are going to use. The first is a compactness result for scaled energies proven in
[
16
, Proposition 4.3]
. It holds for spin variables taking values in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, the adaptation for
\mathbb{S}^{2}
is straightforward.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mu_{n}\to 0
and let
u_{n}\in C_{n}(I,\mathbb{S}^{2})
be a sequence such that

(5.1)

\sup_{n}H^{sl}_{n}(u_{n})\leq C\lambda_{n}\mu_{n},

for some
C>0
. Then, for all
i
we have

|(1-\delta_{n})-(u_{n}^{i},u_{n}^{i+1})|\leq C\mu_{n}^{1/2}.

In particular, this implies that
(u^{i}_{n},u^{i+1}_{n})\to 1
uniformly.

The next two results are proven in
[
15
, Proposition 3.1 and 3.2]
and give useful bounds at the energy scale
\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
z_{n}
be a sequence in
L^{\infty}(I,\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that

\sup_{n}\frac{H^{sl}_{n}(z_{n})}{\sqrt{2}\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2}}\leq C<+\infty,

and let
u_{n}\in C_{n}(I;\mathbb{S}^{2})
be such that
z_{n}=T_{n}(u_{n})
for all
n
. Then, there exists a sequence of positive real numbers
\gamma_{n}\to 0
such that for
n
sufficiently large the following two bounds hold true:

(5.2)

\displaystyle\frac{H^{sl}_{n}(z_{n})}{\sqrt{2}\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2}}\geq\frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}{\lambda_{n}}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(I)}\lambda_{n}\left(\left|\frac{u^{i+1}_{n}-u^{i}_{n}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}\right|^{2}-1\right)^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{n}(1-\gamma_{n})}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(I)}\lambda_{n}\left|\frac{z^{i+1}_{n}-z^{i}_{n}}{\lambda_{n}}\right|^{2},

(5.3)

\displaystyle\frac{H^{sl}_{n}(z_{n})}{\sqrt{2}\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}{\lambda_{n}}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(I)}\lambda_{n}\left(\left|\frac{u^{i+1}_{n}-u^{i}_{n}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}\right|^{2}-1\right)^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(I)}\lambda_{n}\left|\frac{z^{i+1}_{n}-z^{i}_{n}}{\lambda_{n}}\right|^{2}.

Proposition 5.3
.

Assume that
\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\sqrt{\delta_{n}}}\to 0
and let
z_{n}
be a sequence in
L^{\infty}(I,\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that

\sup_{n}\frac{H^{sl}_{n}(z_{n})}{\sqrt{2}\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2}}<+\infty.

Then,
\|z_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}
is equibounded and, up to subsequences,
z_{n}
converges weakly* in
L^{\infty}(I)
to some
z\in L^{\infty}(I,B_{1})
. If in addition
z_{n}\to z
in
L^{1}(I)
, then
z\in L^{\infty}(I,\mathbb{S}^{2})
. Moreover, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

(5.4)

\sup_{i}|z^{i+1}_{n}-z^{i}_{n}|^{2}\leq 2C\sqrt{\delta_{n}}.

Remark 5.4
.

Let
z_{n}
be a sequence in
L^{\infty}(I,\mathbb{R}^{3})
verifying the bound in Proposition
5.3
. By definition, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have that

\sup_{i}\left(1-(u^{i}_{n},u^{i+1}_{n})^{2}\right)=\sup_{i}2\delta_{n}|z_{n}^{i}|^{2}\leq 2\delta_{n}\|z_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2\delta_{n}C.

In turn, recalling Proposition
5.1
, we infer that

(5.5)

\sup_{i}|u^{i+1}_{n}-u^{i}_{n}|^{2}=\sup_{i}\left(2-2(u_{n}^{i+1},u_{n}^{i})\right)\leq 4\delta_{n}C.

The following result will be important in the asymptotic analysis of the energy. In view of this result we can treat the energy as a phase field type functional.

Lemma 5.5
.

Assume that
\lambda_{n}/\sqrt{\delta_{n}}\to 0
and consider a sequence
k_{n}
in
\mathbb{N}
such that
k_{n}\sqrt{\delta_{n}}\to 0
. Let
z_{n}
be a sequence in
L^{\infty}(I,\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that

(5.6)

H^{sl,k_{n}}_{n}(z_{n})\leq C\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2},\qquad C>0.

Then,

(5.7)

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\,\frac{\|\textnormal{dist}(z_{n},\mathfrak{L}_{k_{n}})\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\sqrt{k_{n}}\delta_{n}^{1/4}}\leq 5\sqrt{C},

where
C
is the constant appearing in (
5.6
).

Proof.

By definition of
H^{sl,k_{n}}_{n}
we have that
z_{n}=T_{n}(u_{n})
for some
u_{n}\in C_{n}(I;M_{k_{n}})
.
Since the sequence
z_{n}
verifies the bound in Proposition
5.3
and Remark
5.4
, we have that (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) hold for
z_{n}
and
u_{n}
, respectively.

Step 1:
By assumption
u_{n}\in C_{n}(I;M_{k_{n}})
, thus there exists
q\in\{q_{1},\dots,q_{k_{n}}\}
such that
u_{n}^{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap q^{\perp}
. We claim that for
n
large enough, if
\textnormal{dist}(z_{n}^{0},\textnormal{span}(q))>2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{C}\sqrt{k_{n}}\delta_{n}^{1/4}
, then
u^{l}_{n}\notin\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap q^{\perp}
for every
l=1,\dots,k_{n}
.

By rotational invariance, it is not restrictive to assume
q=(0,0,1)
and
u_{n}^{0}=(1,0,0)
. We first prove that it is possible to find
u_{n}^{1}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
such that
\textnormal{dist}(z_{n}^{0},\textnormal{span}(q))>2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{C}\sqrt{k_{n}}\delta_{n}^{1/4}
. Let
u_{n}^{1}=(u^{1}_{n,1};u^{1}_{n,2};u^{1}_{n,3})
. By definition of
z_{n}^{0}
it is sufficient to have

(5.8)

\frac{|u^{1}_{n,3}|}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}>2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{C}\delta_{n}^{1/4}\sqrt{k_{n}}.

Recalling that by definition of
u_{n}
and (
5.5
) it must holds

(5.9)

(u^{1}_{n,1})^{2}+(u^{1}_{n,2})^{2}+(u^{1}_{n,3})^{2}=1,\qquad(u^{1}_{n,1}-1)^{2}+(u^{1}_{n,2})^{2}+(u^{1}_{n,3})^{2}\leq 4C\delta_{n}.

We observe that for instance the choice
u_{n,2}^{1}=0
and
u_{n,3}^{1}=4\sqrt{C}\delta_{n}^{3/4}\sqrt{k_{n}}
satisfies (
5.8
) and (
5.9
) when
|u_{n,3}^{1}|<1
and
|u_{n,3}^{1}|^{2}<2\delta_{n}C
, and these two inequalities are verified for
n
large enough since by assumption
k_{n}\sqrt{\delta_{n}}\to 0
. Since
\textnormal{dist}(z_{n}^{0},\textnormal{span}(q))>2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{C}\sqrt{k_{n}}\delta_{n}^{1/4}
, we have

(5.10)

\max_{j=1,2}|z_{n,j}^{0}|>2\sqrt{C}\sqrt{k_{n}}\delta_{n}^{1/4}.

We may assume that in (
5.10
)
j=2
is the maximum and that
z^{0}_{n,2}=-u^{1}_{n,3}/\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}<0
(i.e.
u^{1}_{n,3}>0
). The other cases are analogous. By triangle inequality, Hölder inequality, recalling (
5.2
) and (
5.6
), for every
l=1,\dots,k_{n}
we have

|z_{n}^{l}-z_{n}^{0}|^{2}\leq\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k_{n}-1}|z_{n}^{i+1}-z_{n}^{i}|\right)^{2}\leq k_{n}\sum_{i=0}^{k_{n}-1}|z_{n}^{i+1}-z_{n}^{i}|^{2}\leq k_{n}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(I)}|z_{n}^{i+1}-z_{n}^{i}|^{2}\leq 2Ck_{n}\sqrt{\delta_{n}}.

Hence, recalling (
5.10
),

(5.11)

z^{l}_{n,2}<-(2-\sqrt{2})\sqrt{C}\sqrt{k_{n}}\delta^{1/4}_{n}<-\frac{\sqrt{C}\sqrt{k_{n}}\delta_{n}^{1/4}}{2}\qquad\mbox{for every $l=1,\dots,k_{n}$},

which proves the claim provided
u_{n,3}^{l}>0
for every
l=1,\dots,k_{n}
.
We prove it by induction. For
l=1
this is true by assumption on
u^{1}_{n,3}
. Take
l\in\{1,\dots,k_{n}-1\}
, knowing that
u^{l}_{n,3}>0
we want to prove that
u^{l+1}_{n,3}>0
. By definition of
z_{n}^{l}
we have

z_{n}^{l}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}u^{l}_{n}\times u^{l+1}_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}(u^{l}_{n}-u^{l+1}_{n})\times u^{l+1}_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}\begin{pmatrix}u^{l}_{n,1}-u^{l+1}_{n,1}\\
u^{l}_{n,2}-u^{l+1}_{n,2}\\
u^{l}_{n,3}-u^{l+1}_{n,3}\end{pmatrix}\times\begin{pmatrix}u^{l+1}_{n,1}\\
u^{l+1}_{n,2}\\
u^{l+1}_{n,3}\end{pmatrix}.

Hence, using (
5.11
),

(5.12)

-\frac{\sqrt{C}}{2}\sqrt{k_{n}}\delta_{n}^{1/4}>z^{l}_{n,2}=\frac{u^{l+1}_{n,1}(u^{l}_{n,3}-u^{l+1}_{n,3})}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}-\frac{u^{l+1}_{n,3}(u^{l}_{n,1}-u^{l+1}_{n,1})}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}.

Recalling (
5.5
) and that
u_{n}^{0}=(1,0,0)
together with the fact that
k_{n}\sqrt{\delta_{n}}\to 0
, using triangle inequality we infer for
n
large enough

(5.13)

u^{l+1}_{n,1}\geq 1-2\sqrt{C}k_{n}\sqrt{\delta_{n}}\geq\frac{1}{2},\qquad|u^{l+1}_{n,3}|\leq 2\sqrt{C}k_{n}\sqrt{\delta_{n}},\qquad|u^{l}_{n,1}-u^{l+1}_{n,1}|+|u^{l}_{n,3}-u^{l+1}_{n,3}|\leq 4\sqrt{C}\sqrt{\delta_{n}}.

To prove that
u^{l+1}_{n,3}>0
it suffice to show
u^{l+1}_{n,3}\geq u^{l}_{n,3}
. Assume by contradiction that
u^{l}_{n,3}-u^{l+1}_{n,3}>0
. Then, by (
5.12
) we have
|u^{l+1}_{n,1}(u^{l}_{n,3}-u^{l+1}_{n,3})|<|u^{l+1}_{n,3}(u^{l}_{n,1}-u^{l+1}_{n,1})|
, which by (
5.13
) implies
|z^{l}_{n,2}|\leq 8C\sqrt{\delta_{n}}k_{n}
. However, this is in contradiction with (
5.12
), as
\delta_{n}^{1/4}\sqrt{k_{n}}\gg\sqrt{\delta_{n}}k_{n}
. Hence,
u^{l+1}_{n,3}\geq u^{l}_{n,3}>0
and the claim is proven.

Step 2:
We are now ready to prove (
5.7
). In particular, we will prove that for every
n
large enough we have

\|\textnormal{dist}(z_{n},\mathfrak{L}_{k_{n}})\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 5\sqrt{C}\sqrt{k_{n}}\delta_{n}^{1/4}.

We argue by contradiction. Assume that (possibly passing to a subsequence) there exists
i=i(n)
such that
\textnormal{dist}(z_{n}^{i},\mathfrak{L}_{k_{n}})>5\sqrt{C}\sqrt{k_{n}}\delta_{n}^{1/4}
. Let
l_{i}\in\{1,\dots,k_{n}\}
be such that
u^{i}_{n}\in S^{1}_{l_{i}}
. By Step 1 we have that
u^{i+j}_{n}\notin S^{1}_{l_{i}}
for every
j=1,\dots,k_{n}
. By triangle inequality and Hölder inequality for every
j=1,\dots,k_{n}
we have

\displaystyle\textnormal{dist}(z_{n}^{i+j},\mathfrak{L}_{k_{n}})

\displaystyle\geq\textnormal{dist}(z_{n}^{i},\mathfrak{L}_{k_{n}})-|z_{n}^{i+j}-z_{n}^{i}|>4\sqrt{C}\sqrt{k_{n}}\delta_{n}^{1/4}-\sqrt{k_{n}}\left(\sum_{m\in R_{n}(I)}|z_{n}^{m+1}-z_{n}^{m}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq 5\sqrt{C}\sqrt{k_{n}}\delta_{n}^{1/4}-\sqrt{2}\sqrt{C}\sqrt{k_{n}}\delta_{n}^{1/4}\geq 2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{C}\sqrt{k_{n}}\delta_{n}^{1/4}.

Owing Step 1, we can now repeat the same argument with
z_{n}^{i+j}
for every
j=1,\dots,k_{n}
. We deduce that
u^{i+j}_{n}\in S^{1}_{l_{i+j}}
where
l_{i+j}\in\{1,\dots,k_{n}\}
for every
j=0,\dots,k_{n}-1
and
l_{i+j}\neq l_{i+m}
whenever
j,m\in\{0,\dots,k_{n}-1\}
and
j\neq m
. Therefore, we conclude that
u^{i+k_{n}}_{n}\notin S^{1}_{l_{i+j}}
for every
j\in\{0,\dots,k_{n}-1\}
. That is,
u_{n}^{i+k_{n}}\notin M_{k_{n}}
. Since
u_{n}\in C_{n}(I;M_{k_{n}})
this gives a contradiction.

∎

Before proving compactness for sequences with bounded energy, we recall an useful result.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence of functions such that
v_{n}\in C_{n}(I)
and
v_{n}\geq 0
for every
n\geq 1
. Let
\beta_{n}\in C_{n}(I)
be a sequence converging uniformly to
1
. Assume that
\lambda_{n}/\sqrt{\delta_{n}}\to 0
and that there exists
C>0
with the property that for every
n\geq 1

(5.14)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to+\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}{\lambda_{n}}\sum_{i\in R_{n}([\hat{s}_{n}^{1},\hat{t}_{n}])}\lambda_{n}\left(\left|\beta_{n}v_{n}^{i}\right|^{2}-1\right)^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{n}}{2\sqrt{\delta_{n}}}\sum_{i\in R_{n}([\hat{s}_{n}^{1},\hat{t}_{n}])}\lambda_{n}\left|\frac{v_{n}^{i+1}-v_{n}^{i}}{\lambda_{n}}\right|^{2}

-4\sqrt{C}\delta_{n}^{1/4}>z_{n,2}^{l}=\frac{u_{n,1}^{l}(u_{n,3}^{l}-u_{n,3}^{l+1})}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}-\frac{u^{l+1}_{n,3}(u_{n,1}^{l}-u_{n,1}^{l+1})}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}.

Proof of Theorem

\mathfrak{L}^{n}=\cup_{\alpha=1}^{N}\mathfrak{L}^{n}_{\alpha}

\beta^{i}_{n}:=\frac{2}{1+(u_{n}^{i+1},u_{n}^{i})}

\mathcal{F}_{n}(z_{n}):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(I)}\lambda_{n}g_{n}(i,z_{n}^{i})+\varepsilon_{n}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(I)}\lambda_{n}\left|\frac{z_{n}^{i}-z_{n}^{i+1}}{\lambda_{n}}\right|^{2},

(5.29)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{H^{sl,k_{n}}_{n}(z_{n})}{\sqrt{2}\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2}}=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{n}(z_{n}).

In view of Proposition

\left|\frac{u_{n}^{i+1}-u_{n}^{i}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}\right|^{2}=\frac{2}{1+(u_{n}^{i+1},u_{n}^{i})}|z_{n}^{i}|^{2}=|\beta_{n}^{i}z_{n}^{i}|^{2},

on

\beta_{n}\to 1

we trivially have that
z_{n}^{i}\in B_{r_{n}}

z_{n}^{i}\in\mathfrak{L}^{n}
for every
i\in R_{n}(I)
and the claim (

is contained a ball centered in zero shrinking as

4.2
(with some minor modifications as

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{H^{sl,k_{n}}_{n}(z_{n})}{\sqrt{2}\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2}}=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{n}(z_{n})\geq\left(\liminf_{n\to+\infty}4\int_{\tau}^{1-\tau}\left|1-|\beta_{n}t|^{2}\right|\,dt\right)\#S(z).

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{H^{sl,k_{n}}_{n}(z_{n})}{\sqrt{2}\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2}}\geq\frac{8}{3}\#S(z).

Upper bound.
The proof follows the lines of

such that
|\tanh(\pm x_{\varepsilon})-\pm 1|\leq\varepsilon
and

\int_{|x|\leq x_{\varepsilon}}(|\tanh(x)|^{2}-1)^{2}+|\tanh^{\prime}(x)|^{2}\,dx\geq\frac{8}{3}-\varepsilon.

Since
q,q^{\prime}\in\overline{Q}
, there exist
q^{1}_{n},q^{2}_{n}\in Q_{k_{n}}
such that
q^{1}_{n}\to q
and
q_{n}^{2}\to q^{\prime}
. We define

w_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x):=\begin{cases}|f_{\varepsilon}(x)|q_{n}^{1}&if $x\leq 0$;\\
|f_{\varepsilon}(x)|q_{n}^{2}&if $x>0$.\end{cases}

Above,
f_{\varepsilon}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is an odd function such that

f_{\varepsilon}(x):=\begin{cases}\tanh(x)&if $x\in[0,x_{\varepsilon}]$;\\
p_{\varepsilon}(x)&if $x\in(x_{\varepsilon},x_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon)$;\\
1&if $x\geq x_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon$;\end{cases}

where
p_{\varepsilon}
is a suitable third order interpolating polynomial with
\|p_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2
. By definition there exists
C>0
such that

(5.30)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|w_{n}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}-1)^{2}+|(w_{n}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{8}{3}+C\varepsilon.

Let
w_{n,\varepsilon}\in C_{n}(I,\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
f_{n,\varepsilon}\in C_{n}(I)
be defined as follows for every
i\in R_{n}(I)

(5.31)

w_{n,\varepsilon}^{i}:=w^{\varepsilon}_{n}\left(\frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}{\lambda_{n}}\left(\lambda_{n}i-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right),\qquad f_{n,\varepsilon}^{i}:=f^{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}{\lambda_{n}}\left(\lambda_{n}i-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right).

By construction we have that
w_{n,\varepsilon}\to z
in
L^{1}(I,\mathbb{R}^{3})
as
n\to+\infty
and
\varepsilon\to 0
. Setting

i_{-}:=\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}(x_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon)\right)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad i_{+}:=\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}(x_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon)\right)+1,

then
|w_{n,\varepsilon}^{i}|=1
for every
i\leq i_{-}
or
i\geq i_{+}
. We define the angles

\varphi_{n,\varepsilon}^{i}:=2\sum_{j=0}^{i}\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{\delta_{n}}{2}}|f_{n,\varepsilon}^{j}|\right).

Observe that
\varphi_{n,\varepsilon}^{1}-\varphi_{n,\varepsilon}^{0}=\varphi_{n,\varepsilon}^{[1/\lambda_{n}]}-\varphi_{n,\varepsilon}^{[1/\lambda_{n}]-1}
. Let
R_{n}^{\ell}\in SO(3)
be rotations such that
R_{n}^{\ell}(\cos(\theta),\sin(\theta),0)\in(q_{n}^{\ell})^{\perp}\cap\mathbb{S}^{2}
for every
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
, for
\ell=1,2
and
R_{n}^{1}(1,0,0)=R_{n}^{2}(1,0,0)
. We define

u_{n,\varepsilon}^{i}:=R_{n}^{1}(\cos(\varphi^{i}_{n,\varepsilon}),\sin(\varphi_{n,\varepsilon}^{i}),0)\ \ \ \mbox{for $i\leq 0$}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad u_{n,\varepsilon}^{i}:=R_{n}^{2}(\cos(\varphi^{i}_{n,\varepsilon}),\sin(\varphi_{n,\varepsilon}^{i}),0)\ \ \ \mbox{for $i>0$}.

Notice that by construction we have
u_{n,\varepsilon}\in\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{n}(I)
and
z_{n,\varepsilon}=T_{n}(u_{n,\varepsilon})
. Hence,
u_{n,\varepsilon}
is a good candidate for the recovery sequence. Thanks to (
5.3
) we have

(5.32)

\frac{H^{sl,k_{n}}_{n}(z_{n})}{\sqrt{2}\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}{\lambda_{n}}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(I)}\lambda_{n}\left(\left|\frac{u^{i+1}_{n}-u^{i}_{n}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}\right|^{2}-1\right)^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(I)}\lambda_{n}\left|\frac{z^{i+1}_{n}-z^{i}_{n}}{\lambda_{n}}\right|^{2}.

We now define an auxiliary sequence of piecewise constant functions as

v_{n,\varepsilon}(s):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{u_{n}^{i+1}-u_{n}^{i}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}&if $s\in\left[\frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}{\lambda_{n}}\left(\lambda_{n}i-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right.,\left.\frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}{\lambda_{n}}\left(\lambda_{n}(i+1)-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)$ for $i\in R_{n}(I)$,\\
0&otherwise.\end{cases}

Notice that by construction
|v_{n,\varepsilon}(s)|=1
whenever
|s|\geq x_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon+2\lambda_{n}
. Moreover, since every interval
\left[\frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}{\lambda_{n}}\left(\lambda_{n}i-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right.,\left.\frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}{\lambda_{n}}\left(\lambda_{n}(i+1)-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)
has length
\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}\to 0
and since
w_{n}^{\varepsilon}
is uniformly continuous on
\mathbb{R}
we infer that
|v_{n,\varepsilon}-w_{n}^{\varepsilon}|\to 0
uniformly in
\mathbb{R}
. Thus,

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}(|v_{n,\varepsilon}(s)|^{2}-1)^{2}\,ds=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}(|w_{n}^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{2}-1)^{2}\,ds.

On the other hand, via the change of variables
t-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}s
we get

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}(|v_{n,\varepsilon}(s)|^{2}-1)^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\geq\int_{-\frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}{2\lambda_{n}}}^{\frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}{2\lambda_{n}}}(|v_{n,\varepsilon}(s)|^{2}-1)^{2}\,ds=\frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}{\lambda_{n}}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\left|v_{n,\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}{\lambda_{n}}(t-\frac{1}{2})\right)\right|^{2}-1\right)^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}{\lambda_{n}}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(I)}\lambda_{n}\left(\left|\frac{u^{i+1}_{n}-u^{i}_{n}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}\right|^{2}-1\right)^{2}.

Therefore,

(5.33)

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\ \frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}{\lambda_{n}}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(I)}\lambda_{n}\left(\left|\frac{u^{i+1}_{n}-u^{i}_{n}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}\right|^{2}-1\right)^{2}\leq\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}(|w_{n}^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{2}-1)^{2}\,ds.

We now define another sequence of piecewise constant functions as

\bar{z}_{n,\varepsilon,}(s):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{z_{n,\varepsilon}^{i+1}-z_{n,\varepsilon}^{i}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}&if $s\in\left[\frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}{\lambda_{n}}\left(\lambda_{n}i-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right.,\left.\frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}{\lambda_{n}}\left(\lambda_{n}(i+1)-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)$ for $i\in R_{n}(I)$,\\
0&otherwise.\end{cases}

Notice that by construction
|\bar{z}_{n,\varepsilon}(s)|=1
whenever
|s|\geq x_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon+2\lambda_{n}
. Moreover, since every interval
\left[\frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}{\lambda_{n}}\left(\lambda_{n}i-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right.,\left.\frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}{\lambda_{n}}\left(\lambda_{n}(i+1)-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)
has length
\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}\to 0
and since
(w_{n}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}
is uniformly continuous on
\mathbb{R}
we infer that
|\hat{z}_{n,\varepsilon}-(w_{n}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}|\to 0
uniformly in
\mathbb{R}
. Thus,

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}|\hat{z}_{n,\varepsilon}(s)|^{2}\,ds=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}|(w_{n}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(s)|^{2}\,ds.

On the other hand, via direct computation we infer

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}|\bar{z}_{n,\varepsilon}(s)|^{2}\,ds\geq\int_{-\frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}{2\lambda_{n}}}^{\frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}{2\lambda_{n}}}|\bar{z}_{n,\varepsilon}(s)|^{2}\,ds=\sum_{i\in R_{n}(I)}\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}\left|\frac{z^{i+1}_{n}-z^{i}_{n}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}\right|^{2}=\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(I)}\lambda_{n}\left|\frac{z^{i+1}_{n}-z^{i}_{n}}{\lambda_{n}}\right|^{2}.

Hence,

(5.34)

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\ \frac{\lambda_{n}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}\sum_{i\in R_{n}(I)}\lambda_{n}\left|\frac{z^{i+1}_{n}-z^{i}_{n}}{\lambda_{n}}\right|^{2}\leq\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}|(w_{n}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(s)|^{2}\,ds.

Combining (
5.30
),(
5.32
)–(
5.34
) we conclude the upper bound

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{H^{sl,k_{n}}_{n}(z_{n})}{\sqrt{2}\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2}}\leq\frac{8}{3}+C\varepsilon.

By arbitrariness of
\varepsilon>0
we conclude the proof of the upper bound.
∎

6.
Chirality transition in the regime
k_{n}\simeq\delta_{n}^{-1/4}

In this last Section we focus on the critical case
k_{n}\sim\delta^{-1/4}
proving Theorem
3.4
.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

We start by defining suitable configurations
Q_{k_{n}}
with
k_{n}:=2\lceil\delta_{n}^{-1/4}\rceil
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
j,m\in\{1,\dots,k_{n}/2\}
we define the "horizontal" and "vertical" unit vectors as

q^{h}_{j}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\alpha_{j}^{2}}}\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_{j}\\
1\\
0\end{bmatrix}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad q_{m}^{v}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+m^{2}\delta_{n}^{1/2}}}\begin{bmatrix}0\\
-m\delta_{n}^{1/4}\\
1\end{bmatrix};

where
\alpha_{j}\in[0,1]
is chosen such that

\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap(q_{j}^{h})^{\perp}=\{(\sin\theta\cos\varphi_{j},\sin\theta\sin\varphi_{j},\cos\theta)\colon\ \theta\in[0,\pi],\ \varphi_{j}\in\{j\sqrt{2\delta_{n}},j\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}+\pi\}\}.

We set

Q_{k_{n}}:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{k_{n}/2}q^{h}_{j}\cup\bigcup_{m=1}^{k_{n}/2}q^{v}_{m},

and notice that by definition of
Q_{k_{n}}
we have

\overline{Q}=\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\beta^{2}}}\begin{bmatrix}0\\
-\beta\\
1\end{bmatrix}\colon\ \beta\in[0,1]\right\}\cup\begin{bmatrix}0\\
1\\
0\end{bmatrix}.

Compactness and
\Gamma
-liminf inequality.

We prove the following statement:

Let
z_{n}=T_{n}(u_{n})
for some
u_{n}\in C_{n}(I,M_{k_{n}})
be such that

(6.1)

H_{n}^{sl,k_{n}}(z_{n})\leq C\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2},\qquad C>0.

Then, there exists
z\in BV(I,\overline{Q})
such that u.t.s.
z_{n}\to z
in
L^{1}(I,\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Moreover, there exists
c>0
such that for every
z\in BV(I,\overline{Q})

(6.2)

\Gamma\mbox{-}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{sl,k_{n}}}{\sqrt{2}\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2}}(z)\geq c|Dz|(I);

with respect to the strong
L^{1}
-topology.

We divide the proof in four steps. We use again the notation
\hat{z}:=z/|z|
for

.

Step 1:
Assume that there exists
\eta>0
such that
|z_{n}^{l}|\geq\eta>0
for every
l\in\{0,\dots,k_{n}\}
. We prove that there exists
q\in Q_{k_{n}}
and an index
\ell\in\{0,\dots,k_{n}\}
such that

(6.3)

\left|\hat{z}_{n}^{\ell}-q\right|\leq\frac{25C\delta_{n}^{1/4}}{\eta}.

The proof is similar to Step 1 in Proposition
3.1
, we only highlights the main differences.

Since
u_{n}\in C_{n}(I,M_{k_{n}})
, there exists two indexes
l_{1}<l_{2}\in\{0,\dots,k_{n}\}
and
q\in Q_{k_{n}}
such that
u_{n}^{l_{1}},u_{n}^{l_{2}}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap q^{\perp}
. Without loss of generality we can assume
q=(0,0,1)
and
u_{n}^{l_{1}}=(1,0,0)
. We show that we can find an index
\ell\in\{0,\dots,k_{n}\}
with the property that
|z_{n,2}^{\ell}|\leq 8C\delta_{n}^{1/4}
. We argue by contradiction. Suppose that

(6.4)

z_{n,2}^{l}<-8C\delta_{n}^{1/4}\qquad\mbox{for every $l\in\{l_{1},\dots,l_{2}\}$};

the case
z_{n,2}^{l}>8C\delta_{n}^{1/4}
is analogous. We show that
u_{n,3}^{l}>0
for every
l\in\{l_{1}+1,\dots,l_{2}\}
. This would give a contraction as
u_{n,3}^{l_{2}}=0
. Recalling the definition of
z_{n}
we have

(6.5)

-8C\delta_{n}^{1/4}>z_{n,2}^{l}=\frac{u_{n,1}^{l+1}(u_{n,3}^{l}-u_{n,3}^{l+1})}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}-\frac{u^{l+1}_{n,3}(u_{n,1}^{l}-u_{n,1}^{l+1})}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}.

Using (
5.5
) and that
u_{n}^{0}=(1,0,0)
, for
n
large enough and
l\in\{1,\dots,k_{n}\}
we infer that

(6.6)

u^{l+1}_{n,1}\geq 1-2\sqrt{C}k_{n}\sqrt{\delta_{n}}\geq\frac{1}{2},\qquad|u^{l+1}_{n,3}|\leq 2\sqrt{C}\delta_{n}^{1/4},\qquad|u^{l}_{n,1}-u^{l+1}_{n,1}|+|u^{l}_{n,3}-u^{l+1}_{n,3}|\leq 4\sqrt{C}\sqrt{\delta_{n}}.

If it were
u_{n,3}^{l}>u_{n,3}^{l+1}
we would have by (
6.5
) and (
6.6
)

|z_{n,2}^{l}|\leq\frac{|u_{n,3}^{l}(u_{n,1}^{l}-u_{n,1}^{l+1})|}{\sqrt{2\delta_{n}}}\leq 4\sqrt{2}C\delta_{n}^{1/4},

which is impossible in view of (
6.5
). Thus,
u_{n,3}^{l}\leq u_{n,3}^{l+1}
and
u_{n}^{l}>0
for every
l\in\{l_{1}+1,\dots,l_{2}\}
, a contradiction. Therefore, there exists
\ell\in\{l_{1},\dots,l_{2}\}
such that

(6.7)

|z^{\ell}_{n,2}|\leq 8C\delta_{n}^{1/4}.

Arguing as in Step 1 in the proof of Proposition
3.1
, we also infer
|z_{n,1}^{\ell}|\leq 8C\delta_{n}^{1/4}
and, from this and (
6.7
),

\left|\hat{z}_{n}^{\ell}-q\right|^{2}\leq\frac{512C^{2}\sqrt{\delta_{n}}}{\eta^{2}},

that is, (
6.3
) for
q=\left(0,0,\textnormal{sign}(z_{n,3}^{\ell})\right)
.

Step 2:
(Improved estimate) We now improve the estimate in Step 1 showing that in (
6.3
), for the same
q\in Q_{k_{n}}
, we actually have

(6.8)

\left|\hat{z}_{n}^{\ell}-q\right|\leq\frac{150C\sqrt{C}\delta_{n}^{3/8}}{\eta^{2}}.

For every
l\in\{0,\dots,k_{n}\}
by triangle inequality, Hölder inequality, the energy estimate (
6.1
) and (
6.3
) we have

(6.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\hat{z}_{n}^{l}-q|&\leq|\hat{z}_{n}^{l}-z_{n}^{\ell}|+|\hat{z}_{n}^{\ell}-q|\leq\sqrt{k_{n}}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{k_{n}-1}|\hat{z}_{n}^{l}-\hat{z}^{l+1}_{n}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\frac{25C\delta_{n}^{1/4}}{\eta}\\
&\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{C}\delta_{n}^{1/8}}{\eta}+\frac{25C\delta_{n}^{1/4}}{\eta}\leq\frac{2\sqrt{C}\delta_{n}^{1/8}}{\eta}.\end{split}

Since
q=\left(0,0,\textnormal{sign}(z_{n,3}^{\ell})\right)
in view of (
6.9
) we have that
|z_{n,3}^{l}|\geq 1/2
and
|z_{n,1}^{l}|+|z_{n,2}^{l}|\leq C\delta_{n}^{1/8}/\eta
.
Hence, recalling that by definition it holds
\hat{z}_{n}^{l}\cdot(u_{n}^{l}-u_{n}^{l+1})=0

|u_{n,3}^{l+1}-u_{n,3}^{l}|\leq\frac{|u_{n,1}^{l+1}-u_{n,1}^{l}||\hat{z}_{n,1}|+|u_{n,2}^{l+1}-u_{n,2}^{l}||\hat{z}_{n,2}|}{|\hat{z}_{n,3}|}\leq\frac{16C\sqrt{\delta_{n}}\delta_{n}^{1/8}}{\eta}.

Thus, using that
u_{n,3}^{0}=0
, we have for every
l\in\{0,\dots,k_{n}\}

(6.10)

|u_{n,3}^{l+1}-u_{n,3}^{l}|\leq\frac{16C\sqrt{\delta_{n}}\delta_{n}^{1/8}}{\eta}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad|u_{n,3}^{l}|\leq\frac{16C\delta_{n}^{1/4}\delta_{n}^{1/8}}{\eta}.

Replacing the estimate in (
6.6
) with (
6.10
) and reasoning as in Step 1, we get that
|z_{n,2}^{\ell}|\leq 16C\sqrt{C}\delta_{n}^{3/8}/\eta
. Moreover, again using (
6.10

Finally, arguing as in Step 1, we conclude (
6.8

Step 3:
Let
F_{n}([x_{1},x_{2}],\cdot)

. Our aim is to prove that there exists an universal constant
c>0
such that given
z_{n}=T_{n}(u_{n})
for some

(6.11)

F_{n}(J,z_{n})\geq\begin{cases}\displaystyle c|z_{n}(x_{2})-z_{n}(x_{1})|&if $|z_{n}(x_{2})-z_{n}(x_{1})|\geq 10\delta_{n}^{1/32}$,\\
\displaystyle\vphantom{\frac{1}{2}}0&otherwise.\end{cases}

We distinguish two cases. First assume that
|z_{n}(\overline{x})|\leq\delta_{n}^{1/32}
for some
\overline{x}\in J
.
If
|z_{n}(x_{1})|<5\delta_{n}^{1/32}
and
|z_{n}(x_{2})|<5\delta_{n}^{1/32}

|z_{n}(x_{2})|\geq|z_{n}(x_{1})|
. Arguing as in Theorem
3.2

\displaystyle F_{n}(J,z_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|z_{n}(x_{2})|}{8}\geq\frac{|z_{n}(x_{1})|+|z_{n}(x_{2})|}{16}\geq\frac{|z_{n}(x_{2})-z_{n}(x_{1})|}{16}.

in

Let
i_{1}<i_{2}\dots<i_{K}\in\mathbb{Z}

and
i_{K}\lambda_{n}=x_{2}

for

s=1,\dots,S

i^{s}\in N_{s}

q_{s}\in Q_{k_{h}}^{h}

, 2.
q_{s}\in Q_{k_{h}}^{v}
for every
s=1,\dots,S
, 3. there exist

We now introduce the set

.
Combining this fact with (

), (

it holds

\Gamma\mbox{-}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{H^{sl,k_{n}}_{n}(z)}{\sqrt{2}\lambda_{n}\delta_{n}^{3/2}}\leq C|Dz|(I).

Finally, for a general
z\in BV(I,\overline{Q})
we conclude by density of
BV_{\textnormal{pc}}(I,\overline{Q})
in
BV(I,\overline{Q})
with respect to the total variation norm.

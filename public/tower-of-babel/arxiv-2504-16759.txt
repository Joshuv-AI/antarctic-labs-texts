Cyclic Riemannian Lie groups: description and curvatures
Fatima-Ezzahrae Abid, Saïd Benayadi, Mohamed Boucetta, Hamza El Ouali, Hicham Lebzioui

Abstract
A cyclic Riemannian Lie group is a Lie group G G equipped with a left-invariant Riemannian metric h h that satisfies ∮ X , Y , Z h ⁡ ( [ X , Y ] , Z ) = 0 \oint_{X,Y,Z}h([X,Y],Z)=0 for any left-invariant vector fields X , Y , Z X,Y,Z . The initial concept and exploration of these Lie groups were presented in Monatsh. Math. 176 (2015), 219-239. This paper builds upon the results from the aforementioned study by providing a complete description of cyclic Riemannian Lie groups and an in-depth analysis of their various curvatures.

Introduction

A cyclic Riemannian Lie group
is a Lie group
G
equipped with a left-invariant Riemannian metric
h

that satisfies

h([X,Y],Z)+h([Y,Z],X)+h([Z,X],Y)=0

for any left-invariant vector fields

X,Y,Z
. The concept and initial exploration of these groups were introduced in
[
3
]
. In this paper, we extend this initial study and provide a detailed description of connected and simply-connected cyclic Riemannian Lie groups. Specifically, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(G,h)
be a connected and simply-connected cyclic Riemannian Lie group. Then
(G,h)
is isomorphic both as a Lie group and a Riemannian manifold to

\mathbb{R}^{r}\times(G(q,p,\Omega),h_{0})\times\underbrace{\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}\times\ldots\times\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m-\mbox{copies}\end{subarray}},\;r\geq 0,m\geq 0,p\geq q\geq 0,

where:

1.

\mathbb{R}^{r}
is the abelian flat Lie group,
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
is the universal cover of
{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
endowed with one of the left-invariant metrics described in Theorem
2.1
,

2.

(G(q,p,\Omega),h_{0})
is the Riemannian Lie group whose underlying manifold is
\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
and the group product is given by

(s,t).(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})=(s+s^{\prime},t+(e^{\langle s,\Omega_{1}\rangle_{0}}t^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,e^{\langle s,\Omega_{p}\rangle_{0}}t^{\prime}_{p})),

\Omega=\left(\omega_{ij}\right)_{1\leq i\leq q}^{1\leq j\leq p}
is a real
(q,p)
-matrix such that
\Omega^{t}
has rank
q
,
(\Omega_{1},\ldots,\Omega_{p})
are the columns of
\Omega
,
\langle\;,\;\rangle_{0}
is the canonical Euclidean product of
\mathbb{R}^{q}
and
h_{0}
is the left-invariant metric

h_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{q}ds_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{p}e^{-2\langle s,\Omega_{i}\rangle_{0}}dt_{i}^{2}.

This classification enables us to conduct a thorough investigation of the different curvatures of cyclic Riemannian Lie groups.

2
Characterization of cyclic Riemannian Lie groups: proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we give some fundamental properties of cyclic Riemannian Lie groups leading to a proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
(G,h)
be a connected Lie group endowed with a left-invariant Riemannian metric. It is a well-known fact that the geometric properties of
(G,h)
can be read at the level of its Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
identified to the vector space of left-invariant vector field. The metric
h
induces a scalar product
\langle\;,\;\rangle
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
which determines
h
entirely.

We call
(G,h)
a cyclic Riemannian Lie group if, for any
X,Y,Z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,

\langle[X,Y],Z\rangle+\langle[Y,Z],X\rangle+\langle[Z,X],Y\rangle=0.

(2.1)

The Levi-Civita
connection of
h
defines a product
(X,Y)\mapsto X\star Y
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
called
Levi-Civita
product
given by Koszul’s formula

2\langle X\star Y,Z\rangle=\langle[X,Y],Z\rangle+\langle[Z,X],Y\rangle+\langle[Z,Y],X\rangle.

(2.2)

For any
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, we denote by
\mathrm{L}_{X}:{\mathfrak{g}}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\mathrm{R}_{X}:{\mathfrak{g}}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
,
respectively, the left multiplication and the right multiplication by
X
given by

\mathrm{L}_{X}Y=X\star Y\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathrm{R}_{X}Y=Y\star X.

For any
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,
\mathrm{L}_{X}
is
skew-symmetric with
respect to
\langle\;,\;\rangle
and

{\mathrm{ad}}_{X}=\mathrm{L}_{X}-\mathrm{R}_{X}
, where

{\mathrm{ad}}_{X}:{\mathfrak{g}}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
is given by
{\mathrm{ad}}_{X}Y=[X,Y]
. Let
[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]
be the derived ideal of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
Z({\mathfrak{g}})
its center. Put

N_{\ell}({\mathfrak{g}})=\left\{X\in{\mathfrak{g}},\mathrm{L}_{X}=0\right\}
and
N_{r}({\mathfrak{g}})=\left\{X\in{\mathfrak{g}},\mathrm{R}_{X}=0\right\}.

We have obviously

[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]^{\perp}=\{X\in{\mathfrak{g}},\mathrm{R}_{X}=\mathrm{R}_{X}^{*}\}.

(2.3)

For any
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, let
\mathrm{J}_{X}
be the skew-symmetric endomorphism given by
\mathrm{J}_{X}Y={\mathrm{ad}}_{Y}^{t}X
where
{\mathrm{ad}}_{Y}^{t}
is the adjoint of
{\mathrm{ad}}_{Y}
with respect to
\langle\;,\;\rangle
. Note that

[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]^{\perp}=\{X\in{\mathfrak{g}},\mathrm{J}_{X}=0\}.

(2.4)

The restriction
\mathrm{K}
of the curvature to
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathrm{ric}}
the Ricci curvature are given by

\mathrm{K}(X,Y)=\mathrm{L}_{[X,Y]}-[\mathrm{L}_{X},\mathrm{L}_{Y}]\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathrm{ric}}(X,Y)={\mathrm{tr}}(Z\mapsto\mathrm{K}(X,Z)Y).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(G,h)
be a cyclic Riemannian Lie group. Then the following assertions hold.

(i)

For any
X,Y,Z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,

\langle X\star Y,Z\rangle=-\langle X,[Y,Z]\rangle.

In particular,
\mathrm{L}_{X}=-\mathrm{J}_{X}
and
\mathrm{R}_{X}=-{\mathrm{ad}}_{X}^{t}
.

(ii)

[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]^{\perp}=N_{\ell}({\mathfrak{g}})=\{X\in{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{ad}}_{X}^{t}={\mathrm{ad}}_{X}\}
.
In particular,
[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]^{\perp}
is an abelian subalgebra.

(iii)

Z({\mathfrak{g}})=({\mathfrak{g}}\star{\mathfrak{g}})^{\perp}=N_{\ell}({\mathfrak{g}})\cap N_{r}({\mathfrak{g}})
. In particular,
Z({\mathfrak{g}})\subset[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]^{\perp}
.

Proof.

(i)

It is an immediate consequence of (
2.2
) and (
2.1
).

(ii)

It is a consequence of
(i)
, (
2.4
) and (
2.3
).

(iii)

It is an immediate consequence of (
2.1
).∎

As an immediate consequence of Proposition
2.1
, we recover
[
3
, Proposition 5.5]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(G,h)
be a cyclic Riemannian Lie group. Then:

1.

if
G
is nilpotent then
G
is abelian;

2.

if
G
is solvable then it is 2-solvable.

Proof.

According to Proposition
2.1

(iii)
,
[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]\cap Z({\mathfrak{g}})\subset[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]\cap[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]^{\perp}=\{0\}
and if
{\mathfrak{g}}
is nilpotent non abelian then
[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]\cap Z({\mathfrak{g}})\not=\{0\}
which completes the proof of the first part. The second part is a consequence of the first one since if
G
is solvable then its derived ideal is nilpotent.
∎

Let
(G,\mathbb{k})
be a connected Lie group endowed with a bi-invariant pseudo-Riemannian metric. This means that, for any
X,Y,Z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,

\mathbb{k}([X,Y],Z)+\mathbb{k}([X,Z],Y)=0.

(2.5)

Note that if
\mathbb{k}
is Riemannian then
G
is isomorphic to the product of an abelian Lie group and a compact semi-simple Lie group (see
[
6
]
).

Let
h
be a left-invariant metric on
G
determined by its restriction
\langle\;,\;\rangle
to
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then there exists an invertible linear map
\delta:{\mathfrak{g}}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
given by the relation

\langle u,v\rangle=\mathbb{k}(\delta(u),v).

Proposition 2.3
.

With the notations and hypothesis above the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

(G,h)
is a cyclic Riemannian Lie group.

(ii)

The endomorphism
\delta
is a symmetric invertible anti-derivation of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, i.e., for any
X,Y,Z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,

\delta([X,Y])=-[\delta(X),Y]-[X,\delta(Y)].

Proof.

For any
X,Y,Z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, by using (
2.5
), we get

\displaystyle\oint_{X,Y,Z}\langle[X,Y],Z\rangle=\mathbb{k}(\delta([X,Y]),Z)+\mathbb{k}([\delta(X),Y],Z)+\mathbb{k}([X,\delta(Y)],Z)

and the result follows. ∎

We give now a different proof of
[
3
, Theorem 4.2]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(G,\mathbb{k})
be a Lie group endowed with a bi-invariant Riemannian metric. If
G
carries a cyclic left-invariant Riemannian metric then
G
is abelian. In particular, a compact semi-simple Lie group cannot be a cyclic Riemannian Lie group.

Proof.

Suppose that
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is the restriction of a cyclic Riemannian metric on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then, according to Proposition
2.3
,
\delta
given by
\langle u,v\rangle=\mathbb{k}(\delta(u),v)
is an invertible anti-derivation and its eigenvalues are positive. Thus

{\mathfrak{g}}=E_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus E_{r}

where
E_{i}
is the eigenspace associated to
\lambda_{i}
. For any
X\in E_{i}
and
Y\in E_{j}
,

\delta([X,Y])=-(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j})[X,Y].

Since
-(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j})<0
then
[X,Y]=0
. This shows that
{\mathfrak{g}}
is abelian.
∎

In
[
3
]
, it was proven that the universal covering group
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
of
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
is the only connected and simply-connected simple cyclic Riemannian Lie group. We give now another proof of this result and complete it by classifying all cyclic Riemannian metrics on
\widetilde{SL(2,\mathbb{R})}
and exhibiting their Ricci and scalar curvatures.

We consider
\mathrm{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
and
\mathcal{B}=(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3})
the basis given by

X_{1}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{matrix}\right),\;X_{2}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{matrix}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad X_{3}=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{matrix}\right).

The Lie brackets are given by

[X_{1},X_{2}]=2X_{3},\;[X_{2},X_{3}]=-2X_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad[X_{3},X_{2}]=2X_{1}.

Theorem 2.1
.

The universal covering group
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
of
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
is the only connected and simply-connected simple cyclic Riemannian Lie group. Let
\langle\;,\;\rangle
be the restriction of a cyclic left-invariant Riemannian metric of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
to its Lie algebra
\mathrm{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
,
{\mathrm{ric}}
its Ricci curvature and
\sigma
its scalar curvature. Then,
up to an automorphism of
\mathrm{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
, the matrix of
\langle\;,\;\rangle
in
\mathcal{B}
is given by

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mu+\nu&0&0\\
0&\mu&0\\
0&0&\nu\end{array}\right),\quad\mu>\nu>0,\;{\mathrm{ric}}=8\mathrm{Diag}(1,-1,-1)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sigma=\frac{-8\mu^{2}-8\mu\nu-8\nu^{2}}{\left(\mu+\nu\right)\mu\nu}.

Proof.

Suppose that
G
is a simply-connected simple cyclic Riemannian Lie group. Then its Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is simple and its Killing form is nondegenerate and defines a pseudo-Riemannian bi-invariant metric on
G
. By virtue of Proposition
2.3
,
{\mathfrak{g}}
has an invertible anti-derivation. According to
[
2
, Theorem 5.1]
,
{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathbb{C}=\mathrm{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
and hence
{\mathfrak{g}}
is a real form of
\mathrm{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
and
{\mathfrak{g}}
is isomorphic either to
\mathrm{su}(2)
or
\mathrm{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
. But
\mathrm{SU}(2)
cannot be a cyclic Riemannian Lie group by virtue of Proposition
2.4
. On the other hand,
left-invariant Riemannian metrics on
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
were classified in
[
4
, Theorem 3.6]
and it is an easy task to determine the cyclic ones and their Ricci or scalar curvatures.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(G,h)
be a connected and simply-connected semi-simple cyclic Riemannian Lie group. Then
G
is isomorphic both as a Lie group and a Riemannian manifold to the Riemannian product
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}\times\ldots\times\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
and each factor
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
is endowed with one of the cyclic metrics listed in Theorem
2.1
.

Proof.

Denote by
B
the Killing form of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\delta
the symmetric isomorphism given by
\langle u,v\rangle=B(\delta(u),v)
. According to Proposition
2.3
,
\delta
is an anti-derivation, i.e., for any
u,v\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,

\delta([u,v])=-[\delta(u),v]-[u,\delta(v)].

Let
I
be a semi-simple ideal of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Since
I=[I,I]
the relation above shows that
\delta(I)=I
. Let
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{r}
be the decomposition of
{\mathfrak{g}}
as simple ideals. For any
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, the restriction of
\langle\;,\;\rangle
to
{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}
is cyclic and, by virtue of Theorem
2.1
,
{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
. On the other hand,
for any
i\not=j
,
u,v\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}
,
w\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}
. We have

B([u,v],w)=B(u,[v,w])=0

and since
[{\mathfrak{g}}_{i},{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}]={\mathfrak{g}}_{i}
this shows that
B({\mathfrak{g}}_{i},{\mathfrak{g}}_{j})=0
and hence
\langle{\mathfrak{g}}_{i},{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}\rangle=B(\delta({\mathfrak{g}}_{i}),{\mathfrak{g}}_{j})=0
. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
(G,h)
be a connected and simply-connected cyclic Riemannian Lie group. Then
G
is isomorphic both as a Lie group and a Riemannian manifold to the product

G_{1}\times\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}\times\ldots\times\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
where
G_{1}
is solvable and each factor
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}
is endowed with one of the cyclic metrics listed in Theorem
2.1
.

Proof.

We denote by
\mathrm{Rad}({\mathfrak{g}})
the radical of
{\mathfrak{g}}
(the largest solvable ideal). From the relation (
2.1
), one can deduce easily that
\mathrm{Rad}({\mathfrak{g}})^{\perp}
is a Lie subalgebra and for any
x\in\mathrm{Rad}({\mathfrak{g}})^{\perp}
, the restriction of
{\mathrm{ad}}_{x}
to
\mathrm{Rad}({\mathfrak{g}})
is symmetric. Now, for any
x,y\in\mathrm{Rad}^{\perp}
,
{\mathrm{ad}}_{[x,y]}=[{\mathrm{ad}}_{x},{\mathrm{ad}}_{y}]
and hence the restriction of
{\mathrm{ad}}_{[x,y]}
to
\mathrm{Rad}({\mathfrak{g}})
is both symmetric and skew-symmetric and it vanishes. Since
{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathrm{Rad}({\mathfrak{g}})\oplus\mathrm{Rad}({\mathfrak{g}})^{\perp}
,
\mathrm{Rad}({\mathfrak{g}})^{\perp}
is semi-simple and
[\mathrm{Rad}({\mathfrak{g}})^{\perp},\mathrm{Rad}({\mathfrak{g}})^{\perp}]=\mathrm{Rad}({\mathfrak{g}})^{\perp}
and hence
[\mathrm{Rad}({\mathfrak{g}})^{\perp},\mathrm{Rad}({\mathfrak{g}})]=0
thus
\mathrm{Rad}({\mathfrak{g}})^{\perp}
is a semi-simple ideal and we can conclude by using Theorem
2.2
.
∎

Before stating our result, let us introduce the model of solvable cyclic Riemannian Lie group.

Let

(q,p)

\Omega^{t}

(s,t).(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})=(s+s^{\prime},t+(e^{\langle s,\Omega_{1}\rangle_{0}}t^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,e^{\langle s,\Omega_{p}\rangle_{0}}t^{\prime}_{p})),

\langle\;,\;\rangle_{0}

(f_{1},\ldots,f_{p})

We have obviously that the non vanishing brackets are

(2.6)

Let

Proposition 2.5
.

(G(q,p,\Omega),h_{0})

(G(r,s,\Theta),\widetilde{h}_{0})

,

Denote by
{\mathfrak{g}}_{1}

.

Let

3
Curvatures of cyclic Riemannian Lie groups

In this section, we give some important properties of the different curvatures of cyclic Riemannian Lie groups

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(G,h)
be a cyclic Riemannian non-abelian Lie group. Then its scalar curvature is negative. In particular, neither the sectional curvature or the Ricci curvature can be positive.

Proof.

It is well-known that any Riemannian metric on a non abelian solvable Lie group has negative scalar curvature (see
[
6
]
) and, according to Proposition
2.1
, any cyclic Riemannian metric on
\mathrm{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
has a negative scalar curvature and the result follows from Theorem
1.1
. ∎

A Lie group
(G,h)
is called vectorial (see
[
3
]
) if there exists
\mathbb{h}\in{\mathfrak{g}}
such that, for any
u,v\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,

[u,v]=\langle u,\mathbb{h}\rangle v-\langle v,\mathbb{h}\rangle u.

(3.1)

These Riemannian Lie groups appeared first in
[
6
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(G,h)
be a vectorial Riemannian Lie group.
Then
(G,h)
is cyclic and its sectional curvature is constant, i.e., for any
u,v\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,

\mathrm{K}(u,v):=\mathrm{L}_{[u,v]}-[\mathrm{L}_{u},\mathrm{L}_{v}]=-\langle\mathbb{h},\mathbb{h}\rangle u\wedge v,

where
u\wedge v(w)=\langle u,w\rangle v-\langle v,w\rangle u
. Moreover, the Riemannian universal covering of
(G,h)
is isometric to
G(1,n-1,\Omega)
where
n=\dim\ G
and
\Omega=(|\mathbb{h}|^{2},\ldots,|\mathbb{h}|^{2})
.

Proof.

A straightforward computation using (
3.1
) shows that
(G,h)
is cyclic and
the Levi-Civita product is given by

u\star v=\langle u,v\rangle\mathbb{h}-\langle\mathbb{h},v\rangle u,\quad u,v\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

Not that
\mathrm{L}_{\mathbb{h}}=0
.
Let us compute the curvature
\mathrm{K}(u,v)=\mathrm{L}_{[u,v]}-[\mathrm{L}_{u},\mathrm{L}_{v}]
.

\displaystyle\mathrm{K}(u,v)w

\displaystyle=(u\star v)\star w-(v\star u)\star w-u\star(v\star w)+v\star(u\star w)

\displaystyle=-\langle v,\mathbb{h}\rangle u\star w+\langle u,\mathbb{h}\rangle v\star w-\langle v,w\rangle u\star\mathbb{h}+\langle\mathbb{h},w\rangle u\star v

\displaystyle+\langle u,w\rangle v\star\mathbb{h}-\langle\mathbb{h},w\rangle v\star u

\displaystyle=-\langle v,\mathbb{h}\rangle\langle u,w\rangle\mathbb{h}+\langle v,\mathbb{h}\rangle\langle\mathbb{h},w\rangle u+\langle u,\mathbb{h}\rangle\langle v,w\rangle\mathbb{h}-\langle u,\mathbb{h}\rangle\langle\mathbb{h},w\rangle v

\displaystyle-\langle v,w\rangle\langle u,\mathbb{h}\rangle\mathbb{h}+\langle v,w\rangle\langle\mathbb{h},\mathbb{h}\rangle u+\langle\mathbb{h},w\rangle\langle u,v\rangle\mathbb{h}-\langle\mathbb{h},w\rangle\langle\mathbb{h},v\rangle u

\displaystyle+\langle u,w\rangle\langle v,\mathbb{h}\rangle\mathbb{h}-\langle u,w\rangle\langle\mathbb{h},\mathbb{h}\rangle v-\langle\mathbb{h},w\rangle\langle u,v\rangle\mathbb{h}+\langle\mathbb{h},w\rangle\langle\mathbb{h},u\rangle v

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{h}|^{2}\left(\langle v,w\rangle u-\langle u,w\rangle v\right)

\displaystyle=-\langle\mathbb{h},\mathbb{h}\rangle u\wedge v(w).

On the other hand,
{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathbb{h}^{\perp}\oplus\mathbb{R}\mathbb{h}
,
\mathbb{h}^{\perp}
is abelian and for any
u\in\mathbb{h}^{\perp}

[\mathbb{h},u]=|\mathbb{h}|^{2}u
. By using (
2.6
), we can see that the Lie algebra of
G
is isomorphic to
G(1,n-1,\Omega)
where
\Omega=(|\mathbb{h}|^{2},\ldots,|\mathbb{h}|^{2})
. This completes the proof.
∎

Conversely, we have the following result.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(G,h)
be a connected and simply-connected cyclic Riemannian Lie group of constant sectional curvature, i.e., there exists
k\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\mathrm{K}(u,v):=\mathrm{L}_{[u,v]}-[\mathrm{L}_{u},\mathrm{L}_{v}]=ku\wedge v.

(3.2)

Then
k\leq 0
. If
k=0
then
G
is abelian and if
k<0
then
(G,h)
is vectorial and hence it is isometric to
(G(1,n-1,\Omega),h_{0})
where
n=\dim\ G
and
\Omega=(\lambda,\ldots,\lambda)
with
\lambda>0
.

Proof.

According to Theorem
3.1
,
k\leq 0
and if
k=0
then
G
is abelian. Suppose now that
k<0
.
Since the sectional curvature is negative then
{\mathfrak{g}}
is solvable (see
[
6
, Theorem 1.6]
) and hence, according to Proposition
2.1
,
[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]^{\perp}=N_{\ell}({\mathfrak{g}})\not=\{0\}
. Let
a\in N_{\ell}({\mathfrak{g}})
with
a\not=\{0\}
.
We have
\ker{\mathrm{ad}}_{a}=\mathbb{R}a
. Indeed, since
\mathrm{L}_{a}=0
then, according to (
3.2
), for any
b\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,

\mathrm{L}_{[a,b]}=ka\wedge b.

So if
[a,b]=0
then
a\wedge b=0
and hence
a
and
b
are colinear. Moreover, according to Proposition
2.1
,
{\mathrm{ad}}_{a}
is symmetric and hence
{\mathrm{ad}}_{a}
leaves
a^{\perp}
invariant and its restriction is invertible. Then there exists an orthonormal basis
(e_{2},\ldots,e_{n})
,
(\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
such that
\lambda_{i}\not=0
for any
i
and

[a,e_{i}]=\lambda_{i}e_{i}.

For any
i\in\{2,\ldots,n\}
,

e_{i}\star e_{i}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\mathrm{L}_{[a,e_{i}]}e_{i}=-\frac{k}{\lambda_{i}}a.

On the other hand, since
e_{i}\star a=-[a,e_{i}]
, from the relation

\langle e_{i}\star e_{i},a\rangle=-\langle e_{i},e_{i}\star a\rangle

we deduce that

\lambda_{i}^{2}=|k||a|^{2}.

For
i\not=j
, by using (
3.2
), we have

[e_{i},e_{j}]=e_{i}\star e_{j}-e_{j}\star e_{i}=\frac{k}{\lambda_{i}}a\wedge e_{i}(e_{j})-\frac{k}{\lambda_{j}}a\wedge e_{j}(e_{i})=0

and

[\mathrm{L}_{e_{i}},\mathrm{L}_{e_{j}}]=-ke_{i}\wedge e_{j}.

Thus

\frac{k^{2}}{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}[a\wedge e_{i},a\wedge e_{j}]=-ke_{i}\wedge e_{j}.

But for any skew-symmetric endomorphism, we have

[A,u\wedge v]=Au\wedge v+u\wedge Av.

We deduce that

\frac{k^{2}}{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}\langle a,a\rangle e_{i}\wedge e_{j}=-ke_{i}\wedge e_{j}.

So, for any
i\not=j
,

\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}=|k||a|^{2}.

In conclusion,
\lambda_{2}=\ldots=\lambda_{n}=\pm|a|\sqrt{|k|}
. If we take
\mathbb{h}=\pm\frac{\sqrt{|k|}}{|a|}a
, we get the desired result by applying Proposition
3.1
.
∎

Remark 2
.

One can see that this result is still valid if we drop the hypothesis cyclic and suppose only that there exists
a\in{\mathfrak{g}}
such that
\mathrm{L}_{a}=0
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(G,h)
be a connected and simply-connected cyclic Riemannian Lie group. Then the sectional curvature is negative if and only
(G,h)
is isometric to
(G(1,n-1,\Omega),h_{0})
where
\Omega=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1})
and the
\lambda_{i}
are non null and have the same sign.

Proof.

Suppose that the sectional curvature is negative then
{\mathfrak{g}}
is solvable (see
[
6
, Theorem 1.6]
) and, by virtue of
Theorem
2.4
,
[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]
is abelian. Moreover, according to
[
5
, Proposition 2]
,
[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]
has codimension one and there exists
u_{0}\in[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]^{\perp}
such that the symmetric part of the restriction
{\mathrm{ad}}_{u_{0}}
to
[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]
is positive definite. But, according to Proposition
2.1
,
{\mathrm{ad}}_{u_{0}}
is symmetric. We deduce that for any
u\in[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]^{\perp}\setminus\{0\}
, the restriction of
{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}
to
[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]
is invertible symmetric and all its eigenvalues have the same sign.

Conversely, suppose that
[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]
is abelian
and, for any
u\in[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]^{\perp}
, the restriction of
{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}
to
[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]
is invertible symmetric and all its eigenvalues have the same sign. Take a unitary vector
\mathbb{h}\in[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]^{\perp}
. Then there exists an orthonormal basis
(f_{2},\ldots,f_{n})
and
(\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
non null and having the same sign such that, for any
i\in\{2,\ldots,n\}
,

[\mathbb{h},f_{i}]=\lambda_{i}f_{i}.

Let
\star
be the Levi-Civita product. Then the non vanishing products are

f_{i}\star f_{i}=\lambda_{i}\mathbb{h}\quad\mbox{and}\quad f_{i}\star\mathbb{h}=-\lambda_{i}f_{i}.

A straightforward computation gives that, for
i,j,k
different

\begin{cases}\mathrm{K}(f_{i},f_{j})f_{k}=\mathrm{K}(f_{i},f_{j})\mathbb{h}=\mathrm{K}(f_{i},\mathbb{h})f_{j}=0,\;\\
\langle\mathrm{K}(f_{i},f_{j})f_{i},f_{j}\rangle=-\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j},\\
\langle\mathrm{K}(f_{i},\mathbb{h})f_{i},\mathbb{h}\rangle=-\lambda_{i}^{2}.\end{cases}

This shows that the sectional curvature is negative and completes the proof.
∎

The expression of the Ricci curvature of a cyclic Riemannian Lie groups is rather simple.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(G,h)
be a cyclic Riemannian Lie group. Then its Ricci curvature is given, for any
u,v\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, by

{\mathrm{ric}}(u,v)=-B(u,v)-\langle[H,u],v\rangle,

where
H
is defined by the relation
\langle H,u\rangle={\mathrm{tr}}({\mathrm{ad}}_{u})
and
B
is the Killing form given by
B(u,v)={\mathrm{tr}}({\mathrm{ad}}_{u}\circ{\mathrm{ad}}_{v})
. In particular, for any
u\in[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]^{\perp}
,
{\mathrm{ric}}(u,u)\leq 0
and
{\mathrm{ric}}(u,u)=0
if and only if
u\in Z({\mathfrak{g}})
.

Proof.

It is well-known (see
[
1
, pp. 4]
) that the Ricci curvature is given by

{\mathrm{ric}}(u,v)=-{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathrm{R}_{u}\circ\mathrm{R}_{v})-\frac{1}{2}\langle[H,u],v\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\langle[H,v],u\rangle.

According to Proposition
2.1
,
\mathrm{R}_{u}=-{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}^{t}
and, since
H\in[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]^{\perp}
,
{\mathrm{ad}}_{H}={\mathrm{ad}}_{H}^{t}
. The last assertions is a consequence of the fact that, for any
u\in[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]^{\perp}
,
{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}={\mathrm{ad}}_{u}^{t}
which completes the proof.
∎

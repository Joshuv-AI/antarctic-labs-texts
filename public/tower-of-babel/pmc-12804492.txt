Targeting IQGAP3 skews macrophages polarization towards M1 phenotype and promotes antitumor immune response
Authors: Xiao Li, Xiaopeng An, Yuanyi Zhang, Siyuan Zhang, Lizhong Zeng, Jie Shi, Shiqi Wu, Yan Wang, Yao Xu, Xin Lv, Shuanying Yang, Bo Yuan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveImmunosuppressive M2 macrophages in the tumor microenvironment (TME) limit the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), with only ∼20% of lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) patients benefiting from ICI monotherapy. Targeting macrophage polarization represents a promising strategy to reprogram the TME and enhance antitumor immunity.MethodsIntegrated bioinformatics analysis of TCGA and GEO datasets elucidated the role of IQGAP3 in LUAD progression. A tissue microarray was utilized to compare IQGAP3 expression between tumor and adjacent normal tissues and to evaluate the infiltration relationship between IQGAP3⁺ macrophages and CD8 + T cells. Conditioned medium (CM) from IQGAP3-knockdown macrophage was applied to evaluate its impact on LUAD cell malignancy. Subcutaneous xenograft models tested the impact of IQGAP3 targeting on tumor growth and anti–PD-1 synergy.ResultsIQGAP3 was upregulated in LUAD and correlated with poor survival and reduced ICI benefit. IQGAP3⁺ macrophage infiltration inversely correlated with CD8 + T-cell abundance. CM from macrophage with IQGAP3 knockdown resulted in reduced proliferation, migration and invasion of LUAD cells. In macrophage-cancer cell and macrophage-CD8 + T cell coculture systems, IQGAP3 suppression enhanced macrophage phagocytosis and promoted T cell activation. Mechanistically, IQGAP3 regulates macrophage polarization through binding and sequestering IGF2BP2 at the cell periphery, thereby limiting IGF2BP2-mediated stabilization of FYN mRNA. This reduction in FYN expression led to decreased STAT1 phosphorylation. Targeting IQGAP3 reprogrammed TAMs toward an antitumor phenotype, suppressed tumor growth, and synergized with anti–PD-1 therapy.ConclusionIQGAP3 drives immunosuppressive macrophage polarization in LUAD. Its inhibition represents a novel strategy to improve immunotherapy response.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00018-025-06044-6.Keywords: Lung adenocarcinoma, IQGAP3, Macrophages, CD8 + T cells, Immunotherapy

Introduction

According to the 2024 global cancer statistics, lung cancer ranks the second in incidence and the first in mortality [1]. Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is the most common histological type, accounting for about 85% of all lung cancer cases, with lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) being the predominant subtype [2]. Approximately 70% of NSCLC patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage, and treatment options are limited, with a 5-year survival rate of only 26% [3]. Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), such as anti-PD-1/PD-L1 monoclonal antibodies, have revolutionized the treatment of NSCLC in recent decades. Based on PD-L1 expression levels, these therapies have been approved as the first-line standard treatments for NSCLC [4]. Unfortunately, immune evasion and therapeutic resistance lead to weaker clinical response in the majority of patients and only about 20% of LUAD patients benefit from ICIs monotherapy [5]. Combination therapies thus provide a potentially effective method for overcoming ICIs resistance and increasing response rate.

The main reason for ICIs failure is insufficient T cells infiltration or activation in the tumor microenvironment (TME), as well as the infiltration of immunosuppressive cells [5]. Macrophages are the most abundant immune cells in the TME. These cells exhibit significant heterogeneity and often play a dual role in the progression of cancers. Tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) always possess the similar characteristics as M2. M2-like macrophages are clearly immunosuppressive populations in the TME and its infiltration density in tumor tissue is negatively correlated with patient prognosis [6]. M2-like macrophages derived growth factors or cytokines can directly promote malignant behaviors of tumor cells and indirectly facilitate tumor immune escape by modulating the infiltration or activation of CD8 + T cells [7, 8]. Conversely, M1-like macrophages are generally believed to suppress tumor development. They always contribute to upregulation of antigen processing and presentation genes, and thus enhancing T cells immune responses [9]. Therefore, strategies aimed at targeting key molecules that regulate macrophages immunosuppressive functions are considered as a promising combination alternative for the existing cancer immunotherapy.

IQ motif-containing GTPase activating protein 3 (IQGAP3), the latest identified member of the IQGAP family, is an evolutionarily conserved GTPase-activating protein [10]. IQGAP3 plays multiple roles inside the cells, especially promoting cells proliferation and maintenance of the stem cells transcription program [11]. IQGAP3 expression was significantly increased in multiple tumors and correlated with poor prognosis [12]. Recent investigations have also alluded to its potential of regulating TME [13]. Nonetheless, few studies have clearly identified its expression in LUAD and its impact on the immune cells within TME.

In this study, we found that IQGAP3 is upregulated in LUAD and is significantly correlated to a poor prognosis. Patients with elevated levels of IQGAP3 experienced reduced responsiveness to ICIs. Further investigation showed that IQGAP3 expression was increased in M2-like macrophages and TAMs. Knockdown of IQGAP3 impaired macrophages M2-polarization and reduced its capacity of tumor promotion. In addition, in vivo inhibition of IQGAP3 on macrophages sensitized LUAD to anti-PD-1 therapy. These findings may provide new targets to enhance the efficacy of ICIs or directly inhibiting LUAD progression.

Materials and methods

Cell lines and cell culture

The human monocytic leukemia cell line THP-1, mouse macrophage cell line RAW264.7, human lung cancer cell lines A549, H1299 and H1975, and mouse lung cancer cell line LLC were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, VA, USA). These cell lines were all cultured with DMEM or RPMI 1640 medium (Gibco, NY, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (VivaCell, Shanghai, China) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (P/S) (Gibco, NY, USA). All cell lines were maintained in a humidified atmosphere of 5% CO2 at 37℃.

Patients and public database analysis

The RNA-sequencing and clinical data of The Cancer Genome Atlas lung adenocarcinoma (TCGA-LUAD) cohort and GEO dataset were downloaded from official websites. The TIMER database (http://timer.cistrome.org/) was used to analyze the correlation between IQGAP3 expression and CD8 + T cells infiltration. A tissue microarray (TMA) comprising 53 LUAD tissue cores and 53 paired adjacent non-cancerous tissue cores was utilized. In addition, archived tumor and peritumor normal specimens of 5 patients with LUAD who underwent surgical resection at The Second affiliated hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University were collected for this study. All patients signed the written informed consent.

Isolation of murine bone marrow-derived macrophages (BMDMs)

Six-week-old C57BL/6 mice were sacrificed by cervical dislocation and sterilized with 75% ethanol. The skin of hind legs was incised, and muscles were removed by scissors. Trying to maintain the integrity of the joint, and each leg bone was soaked in 75% ethanol and then soak in PBS within 5 min. Cut the ends of the leg bones and flushed the cells in the bone marrow by PBS medium using a 1 mL syringe. Repeated flushing process 3 times until no visible red color remains in the bones. Subsequently, filtering the suspension through a 70 μm cell strainer to remove any large clumps or debris and centrifuge at 1500 rpm for 5 min and discard the supernatant. Erythrocyte lysis buffer was added and gently resuspend for about 2 min, and then add 3–5 times the volume of PBS to terminate the lysis. After removing the erythrocyte lysis buffer, bone marrow cells were cultured in DMEM medium containing 10% FBS and 25 ng/mL murine M-CSF. The medium should be changed every 3 days and BMDMs can be induced to mature after 7 days of culture.

Macrophages differentiation and conditioned medium (CM) preparation

THP-1 cells were differentiated into macrophages by incubation with PMA (200 ng/mL) for 48 h. The resulting THP-1-derived macrophages were polarized to M1 or M2 phenotypes by exposure to either LPS (100 ng/mL) plus human IFN-γ (20 ng/mL), or human IL-4 (20 ng/mL) plus human IL-13 (20 ng/mL) for 48 h. Similarly, BMDMs were polarized to M1 or M2 states using LPS (100 ng/mL) plus murine IFN-γ (20 ng/mL), or murine IL-4 (20 ng/mL) plus murine IL-13 (20 ng/mL), respectively, for 48 h (Fig. 2D). Subsequently, polarized THP-1 cells or BMDMs were incubated in serum-free medium for 24 h. Culture supernatants were then collected as conditioned medium (CM), centrifuged at 3,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C to remove cellular debris, and stored at −80 °C.

RNA interference and lentivirus infection

Lentiviral vectors for IQGAP3 knockdown (sh-IQGAP3), IQGAP3 overexpression (OE-IQGAP3), and their respective negative controls (sh-NC, OE-NC) were constructed by GenePharma Inc. (Shanghai, China). THP-1 cells were transduced with these vectors according to the manufacturer's protocol. In parallel, short interfering RNAs (siRNAs) targeting human STAT1, FYN, IGF2BP2, and mouse IQGAP3, along with a non-targeting control siRNA, were also synthesized by GenePharma Inc. (Shanghai, China) for transient transfection experiments. For siRNA transfection, cells were seeded in six-well plates. 75 pmol siRNA was mixed with Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, MA, USA) and incubation at room temperature for 15 min, followed by adding to the cell cultures and incubated for 24 h. Cells were harvested for transgene expression analysis or downstream experiments 48- or 72-h post transfection. The targeting sequences were summarized in Table S1.

mRNA stability assay

THP-1 cells were treated with 5 μg/mL actinomycin D (A9415, Sigma) for specified durations. Untreated cells served as the 0-h control. At each time point, cells were harvested for total RNA extraction and RT-qPCR analysis. mRNA half-life (t1/2) was calculated according to established methods [14].

RNA extraction and real-Time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted from cultured cells using TRIzol™ Reagent (9109, Takara Bio) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The extracted RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using the PrimeScript™ RT Reagent Kit (RR036A, Takara Bio). RT-qPCR was subsequently performed using the TB Green® Premix Ex Taq™ II kit (RR820A, Takara Bio) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Gene expression data was normalized to the expression levels of housekeeping gene GAPDH, and relative quantification was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCtmethod. The sequences of all primers used are provided in Table S2.

RNA sequencing and bioinformatics analysis

Total RNA was isolated from cells using TRIzol™ reagent (9109, Takara Bio) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. RNA quality was assessed using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) for purity and concentration, and an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, USA) for integrity. RNA-seq libraries were prepared following standard protocols and sequenced on an Illumina platform by Repugene Technology (Shanghai, China). For bioinformatics analysis, differential expression was analyzed using DESeq2 with significance thresholds of |log2FC|> 1 and adjusted p-value < 0.05. Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis was performed using the Cluster Profiler package in R (v3.2.0). Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was conducted with default parameters.

Protein extraction and western blotting (WB)

Total protein was prepared from cells lysed with RIPA lysis buffer (PL001, ZHHC) containing PMSF (PL012, ZHHC) and phosphatase inhibitors (PL026, ZHHC). Immunoblotting was conducted according to standard procedures. The following primary antibodies were used: CD206 (ab64693, Abcam; 1 μg/mL), TNF-α (ab183218, Abcam; 1:1000), VEGF-A (sc-7269, Santa Cruz; 1:1000), IQGAP3 (sc-393451, Santa Cruz; 1:1000), STAT1 (F0199, Selleck; 1: 1000), p-STAT1 (F0263, Selleck; 1: 1000), p-STAT4 (ab28815, Abcam; 1:1000), FYN (ab184276, Abcam; 1:1000), IGF2BP2 (11601–1-AP, ProteinTech; 1:2000), GAPDH (GB15004, Servicebio; 1:3000) and β-actin (66009–1-Ig, ProteinTech; 1:10000). Immunoblots were imaged with an ECL chemiluminescent detection system (Tanon, China).

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) and qPCR analysis

RNA immunoprecipitation was performed to investigate the interaction between IGF2BP2 and target mRNAs. Cells were lysed in a buffer containing 20 mM Tris–HCl (pH 7.5), 150 mM KCl, 2 mM EDTA, 0.5% NP-40, 0.5% Triton X-100, 0.5 mM DTT, protease inhibitor cocktail (P1005, Beyotime), and RNase inhibitor (G3424, Servicebio). After 30-min incubation on ice, lysates were centrifuged at 15,000 g for 15 min at 4 °C to collect supernatant. Dynabeads™ magnetic beads (10006D, Thermo Fisher) were pre-incubated with either anti-IGF2BP2 antibody (11601–1-AP, ProteinTech) or control IgG (30000–0-AP, ProteinTech) in binding buffer at room temperature for 30 min with gentle rotation. Subsequently, 200 μL of cell lysate was added to the antibody-bead complexes and incubated overnight at 4 °C with rotation. Following incubation, the immunoprecipitates were washed with wash buffer and resuspended in 200 μL TRIzol™ Reagent for RNA extraction. RNA from both immunoprecipitated and input samples was reverse transcribed into cDNA and analyzed by qPCR to quantify specific mRNA targets. Data from RIP-qPCR experiments were analyzed using the fold-enrichment method. The percentage of input for each target mRNA was calculated in both the specific antibody (anti-IGF2BP2) and control IgG groups. Fold enrichment was then determined as follows: Fold Enrichment = (% of Input anti-IGF2BP2)/(% of Input IgG).

Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP)

Total protein was prepared from THP-1 cells lysed with IP lysis buffer (G2038, Servicebio) containing PMSF, phosphatase inhibitors and protease inhibitor cocktail. After lysis on ice for 30 min, the samples were centrifuged to collect the supernatant (4 °C, 15,000 g, 15 min). The immunoprecipitation magnetic beads (C0104, TargetMol) were washed and then incubated with anti-IQGAP3 (25930–1-AP, ProteinTech; 3 μg for 1–3 mg of total protein lysate) and IgG (30000–0-AP, ProteinTech; 3 μg for 1–3 mg of total protein lysate) at room temperature with gentle rotation for 30 min. Subsequently, 200μL of protein supernatant was added and incubated with the antibody-magnetic beads complex at 4 °C overnight with rotation. On the following day, the mixture was washed, and an equal volume of 1 × SDS-PAGE loading buffer was added to the magnetic beads. After thorough mixing, the samples were heated at 95 °C for 10 min, and the supernatant was collected for WB.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) and scoring

Immunohistochemical staining was performed on the lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) tissue microarray following standard protocols. After deparaffinization, rehydration, and antigen retrieval, sections were incubated with primary antibody against IQGAP3 (GB111751, Servicebio; 1:1000) overnight at 4 °C. Subsequent steps included incubation with appropriate secondary antibodies, visualization with diaminobenzidine substrate, and counterstaining with hematoxylin.

Immunoreactivity was evaluated using the Immunoreactive Score (IRS) system. Staining intensity was graded as 0 (negative), 1 (weak), 2 (moderate), or 3 (strong). The percentage of positive cells was scored as 1 (< 10%), 2 (11–50%), 3 (51–80%), or 4 (> 80%). The final IRS was calculated by multiplying the intensity score by the percentage score, yielding a range of 0–12.

Cell immunofluorescence

Seed the cells onto cell culture coverslips, fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (P0099, Beyotime), and then permeabilized the cells membrane using an immunofluorescence permeabilization solution (P0096, Beyotime). After blocking with 10% goat serum (C0265, Beyotime) at room temperature for 30 min, primary antibodies were incubated overnight at 4℃. Then the fluorescence-labeled corresponding secondary antibodies (33206ES60, 33112ES60, Yeasen) were incubated for 1 h at room temperature. DAPI was used to stain the cell nuclei and the samples were visualized under Confocal Microscope Imaging Systems (Leica, Germany). The following primary antibodies were used: IQGAP3 (sc-393451, Santa Cruz; 1: 150), CD86 (sc-28347, Santa Cruz; 1:100) and CD206 (ab64693, Abcam; 1 μg/mL) and IGF2BP2 (11601–1-AP, ProteinTech; 1:150).

Flow cytometry

BMDMs were collected at the end of polarization and incubated with CD11b, F4/80, CD86 for surface staining. CD206 was stained with a Transcription Factor Staining Buffer Set (00–5523, Invitrogen) for intracellular staining. BMDMs were co-cultured with mouse spleen-derived activated CD8 + T cells for 48 h. To assess T cells cytokine production, 3 × 106 cells were plated in 6-well plates and were stimulate with cell activation cocktail (423303, Biolegend) for 4–6 h at 37 °C. Processed cells were stained for surface markers (CD3 and CD8) and then permeabilized using a Transcription Factor Staining Buffer Set to detect intracellular cytokines, IFN-γ and granzyme B(GZMB).

Fresh tumor tissues were collected, cut into homogenous paste, and then digested at 37℃ for 30 min with a digestion solution containing collagenase type I (17100017, Gibco; 100 mg/mL) and DNase I (EN0521, Thermo; 20 mg/mL). To prepare single-cell suspensions, digested suspensions were mechanically dissociated through a 70 μm nylon mesh. Cell-surface staining was performed by incubating cells with antibodies for 30 min on ice in the presence of TruStain FcX™ (101319, Biolegend) to block FcγR binding. The assessment of cytokine production and intracellular staining were conducted as above mentioned. All the antibodies used are list in Table S3. All data were obtained using a BD FACS Canto II Analyzer (BD Biosciences) and analyzed with FlowJo software (V.10.4). The gating strategies are shown in Figure S1.

Multi-color Immunofluorescence (mIF)

Subcutaneous fresh tumors were harvested at the end of experiment and fixed in 4% PFA (BL539A, Biosharp). Paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue Sects. (5 μm) were baked at 65 °C for 1 h, deparaffinized in xylene, and rehydrated through graded ethanol series. Multi-color IF was performed manually according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Briefly, antigen retrieval was performed in 10 mM sodium citrate buffer (pH 6.0) at 95–100 °C for 20 min. Endogenous peroxidase activity was quenched with 0.3% H₂O₂ in methanol for 15 min, followed by blocking with 5% normal serum matching the secondary antibody host species. Primary antibodies against IQGAP3 (sc-393451, Santa Cruz; 1: 150), CD68 (GB113150, Servicebio; 1:2000), F4/80 (GB113373, Servicebio; 1:5000), CD19 (GB15061, Servicebio; 1:2000), CD3 (GB12014, Servicebio; 1:1000), CD8 (GB12068, Servicebio; 1:3000) and CD206 (GB115273, Servicebio; 1:2000) were sequentially incubated in room temperature for 1 h. HRP-conjugated goat anti-rabbit/mouse secondary antibodies were applied, followed by tyramide-based fluorescent dye amplification (kit-provided). For sequential staining of multiple targets, sections were processed starting from the antigen retrieval step to remove binding antibodies, and then they were incubated with another primary antibody. Finally, nuclei were counterstained with DAPI, and slides were imaged using multispectral microscopy (3DHISTECH).

Colony formation assay

A colony formation assay was used to assess cells proliferation. A549, H1299 and LLC cells were seeded in 6-well culture plates with 1000 cells per well and cultured with transfected macrophage-derived CM until obvious clonal proliferation was observed. The colonies were fixed and stained with 0.1% crystal violet solution. After washing with PBS, the whole well was captured, and the number of viable colonies was calculated with Image J software (6.0).

Migration and invasion assay

Cell migration and invasion assays were performed using Transwell® chambers (8 μm pore; Corning) in 24-well plates. For migration assays, 600 μL of transfected macrophage-derived CM was added to the lower chamber. Subsequently, 5 × 104 of cells suspensions (A549, H1299 or LLC) were added to the upper chambers. Plates were incubated at 37 °C for further analysis post 24 h. For invasion assays, Transwell inserts were pre-coated with ice-chilled Matrigel® (200 μg/mL; Corning) and washed to remove excess matrix after incubation at 37 °C for 2 h. Then, 600 μL of CM was added to the lower chamber and 5 × 104 of cells suspensions to the upper chambers. Plates were incubated at 37 °C for further analysis post 48 h. Finally, migrated and invaded cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with 0.1% crystal violet. Cells were counted via Image J software (6.0).

Wound healing assay

A549, H1299, and LLC cells were seeded in 6-well plates and cultured at 37 °C until reaching ~ 90% confluency. A uniform wound was created in the monolayer of cells using a 200 μL pipette tip. Then, images were taken at 0 h, 24 h and 48 h. Relative wound width was assessed via Image J software (6.0).

Phagocytosis assay

A549, H1299, and LLC cells were labeled with 1 μM ProtoNeX™ Green 500 (21215, AAT Bioquest) at 37 °C for 1 h. Transfected macrophages were stained with 1 μM CellTracker™ CM-Dil (MX4007, MK Bio) at 37 °C for 20 min. Fluorescent labeled tumor cells and macrophages were mixed at a 2:1 ratio (tumor cell: macrophage) and seeded onto coverslips in 24-well plates. Following 4 h co-culture, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and imaged using a confocal microscope imaging system (Leica, German).

Co-culture assay

CD8 + T cells were isolated from spleens of wild-type C57BL/6 mice using the MojoSort™ Mouse CD8 + T Cell Isolation Kit (480008, Biolegend) according to manufacturer’s protocols. For pre-activation, purified CD8 + T cells were quantified and seeded into anti-mouse CD3ε antibody-coated (5 μg/mL; 100340, Biolegend) flat-bottom 6-well plates. Cells were cultured in complete RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% FBS, 1% P/S, 5 μg/mL anti-mouse CD28 antibody (102116, BioLegend), and 5 ng/mL murine IL-2 (212–12, PeproTech). After 24 h pre-activation, cells were co-cultured with BMDM-differentiated M2 macrophages in 0.4 μm pore Transwell® inserts (Corning) for 48 h. CD8 + T cells were then assessed for activation status via intracellular staining of IFN-γ and GZMB.

Animal studies

Female 6-week-old C57BL/6 mice were purchased from the Laboratory Animal Center of Xi’an Jiaotong University. All animal experiments were performed following the protocol of the National Institutes of Health Guide for the care and use of laboratory animals (no. XJTUAE2024-2454). Transplanted tumor models were established by subcutaneously injecting a mixture of 10 × 105 LLC cells plus 2 × 105 transfected RAW264.7 cells into the right flank of mice. Anti-PD-1 monoclonal antibody (clone RMP1-14, Bio X Cell) or IgG isotype control was administered intraperitoneally at 250 μg/mouse every 3 days for 4 total doses. Treatment began 7 days post-inoculation. At the experimental endpoint, tumors were excised and weighed. Tumor volume (V) was calculated as V = 1/2 × length × width2.

Statistical analysis

The results presented in the article were obtained from at least three independent experiments, and the values were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). GraphPad Prism 9.5 was used for statistical analysis. Student’s t-tests were used to determine statistically significant differences between groups. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant (in all figures: *p < 0.05; **p < 0.01; ***p < 0.001, ****p < 0.0001; ns = not significant).

Results

IQGAP3 is upregulated in LUAD tissues and associated with inferior response of ICIs in patients with LUAD

To investigate the clinical relevance of IQGAP3 in LUAD, we compared IQGAP3 expression levels between LUAD and normal tissue, and across distinct tumor stages. Analysis based on TCGA database showed that IQGAP3 expression is significantly higher in LUAD (Fig. 1A), and is associated with advanced stages (Fig. 1B-D). Next, we performed survival analysis and found that higher IQGAP3 transcriptional levels were associated with poorer overall survival (OS) of LUAD patients (Fig. 1E). It was noted that IQGAP3 expression is also markedly increased in advanced stages and negatively related to patient OS when analyzing LUAD dataset GSE72094 from GEO database (Fig. 1F-G). Next, IQGAP3 expression was compared between tumor and paired adjacent normal tissues from patients with LUAD via immunohistochemistry (IHC) on a tissue microarray. Analysis revealed that IQGAP3 expression was significantly upregulated in LUAD tissues relative to matched adjacent tissues (Fig. 1H, Fig. S2A) and was associated with advanced stages (Figure S2B). We further validated this finding using RT-qPCR and western blot analysis on 5 paired in-house tissues, which yielded similar results (Fig. S2C-D).

Subsequently, we examined the IQGAP3 expression in a cohort of patients treated with anti-PD-1 therapy (GSE126044) and observed that IQGAP3 expression was significantly higher in non-responder patients as compared with the responder patients (Fig. 1I). Furthermore, we found that high IQGAP3 expression was significantly associated with worse progression-free survival (PFS) (log-rank: p = 0.0003) (Figure S3A) and overall survival (OS) (log-rank: p = 0.0004) (Figure S3B). In univariate analysis, IQGAP3 was identified as an adverse prognostic factor for both endpoints (PFS: HR 1.020 (95%CI: 1.004 to 1.037), p = 0.0114; OS: HR 1.021 (95%CI: 1.005 to 1.038), p = 0.0117) (Figure S3C-D). After adjusting for sex, age and PD-L1 expression in multivariate analysis, high IQGAP3 remained independently associated with worse PFS (HR 1.029 (95%CI: 1.005 to 1.057), p = 0.0252). For OS, while not statistically significant, a clear negative trend was observed (HR 1.019 (95%CI: 0.9983 to 1.044), p = 0.0801) (Figure S3C-D). The above results prompted us to assess the potential contribution of IQGAP3 to the distinct immune subpopulations within the LUAD tissues. A CIBERSORT analysis was conducted based on TCGA-LUAD cohort and found that the classical macrophages infiltration was increased in IQGAP3high LUAD samples (Fig. 1J). Meanwhile, the IQGAP3 expression is obviously higher in TAMs isolated from LUAD as compared with alveolar macrophages (GSE162669) (Fig. 1K). Multi-color Immunofluorescence (mIF) also revealed IQGAP3 is expressed in macrophages within LUAD tissue (Fig. 1L). Furthermore, tumors exhibiting high infiltration of IQGAP3 + macrophages demonstrated an inverse correlation with CD8 + T cell abundance (Figure S3E-F). Collectively, the above results suggest that IQGAP3 may be involved in regulating macrophages polarization.

IQGAP3 promotes M2 macrophages polarization

To investigate the expression of IQGAP3 in M0, M1 and M2 macrophages, the human monocytic leukemia cell line THP-1 was polarized into M1 or M2 macrophages by exposing to LPS/IFN-γ or IL-4/IL-13. RT-qPCR, WB and immunofluorescence (IF) were performed respectively to examine the M1 markers (CD86, TNF-α, IL-1β) and the M2 markers (CD206, VEGF-A) (Fig. 2A-C). Bone-marrow-derived macrophages (BMDMs) was also polarized into M1 or M2 macrophages according to standard process (Fig. 2D). RT-qPCR, WB (Figure S4A-B) and flow cytometry (Fig. 2E-G) were performed to identified the phenotypes of macrophages. As expected, determined by RT-qPCR and WB, IQGAP3 expression was significantly increased in M2 as compared to M0 or M1 in both THP-1 and BMDMs (Fig. 2H and I). IF was also performed and confirmed its expression was increased in M2 of both THP-1 and BMDMs (Fig. 2J and K). To better mimic the effect of tumor cells on macrophages in the TME, we prepared conditioned medium (CM) from human LUAD cells (H1299, A549, H1975) and mouse lung cancer cell line LLC. We cultured THP-1 and BMDMs with CM to induce them to become TAMs. The morphological alterations under microscopy of CM-induced TAMs were similar to M2-like macrophages (Fig. 2L, N). RT-qPCR revealed IQGAP3 expression was significantly increased in those CM-induced TAMs (Fig. 2M and O).

To explore whether IQGAP3 regulates M2 polarization, we used a lentivirus to construct a THP-1 cell line stably knockdown IQGAP3 (Fig. 3A and B), and then polarized these cells to M1 or M2 macrophages with the same conditions. The results showed that M1 markers were significantly increased while M2 markers were significantly downregulated in the IQGAP3-knowdown group compared with the control group (Fig. 3C-F). We further validated this regulatory relationship through gain-of-function studies. Consistent with the knockdown results, IQGAP3 overexpression produced converse effects on macrophage polarization markers (Figure S4C-E). The specificity of this phenomenon was confirmed by rescue experiments, where reintroduction of IQGAP3 in knockdown cells effectively restored normal polarization capacity (Figure S4F-H). Next, we interfered IQGAP3 expression in BMDMs using IQGAP3-specific small interfering RNA (siRNA) and polarized them to M1 or M2 by adding LPS/IFN-γ or IL-4/IL-13 (Fig. 3G-I). We found that compared with control group (control siRNA (siNC)), the expression of M2 markers (VEGF-A, CD206) were significantly reduced (Fig. 3L-M) while M1 markers (TNF-α) were increased in the si-IQGAP3 group (Fig. 3J-K). Flow cytometry analysis also confirmed the decreased proportion of M2 and increased proportion of M1 in the si-IQGAP3 group (Fig. 3N). Collectively, these results indicated IQGAP3 is critically involved in M2 polarization.

IQGAP3 impairs macrophages phagocytic capacity and decreased CD8 + T cells activation

It is now well-accepted that a major immunosuppressive activity of TAMs is to suppress T cells activation [8]. We thus used TIMER to analyze the correlation between IQGAP3 expression and the infiltration of CD8 + T cells, and found that IQGAP3 expression was negatively correlated to CD8 + T cells infiltration (Figure S4I). We then tested the effect of IQGAP3 regulating macrophages on T cells activation. Experimental flowchart is shown in Fig. 3O. In brief, BMDMs transfected with si-IQGAP3 or si-NC were exposed to IL-4 and IL-3 for 48 h and then co-cultured with T cells isolated from mice spleen. After 48 h, flow cytometry was performed to test the IFN-γ and GZMB expression of those T cells. Compared with si-NC, inhibition of IQGAP3 expression significantly enhanced the activation of CD8 + T cells (Fig. 3P-Q).

Previous studies have confirmed that macrophages enhance tumor-killing effects by increasing their phagocytic capacity [15]. Therefore, the effect of IQGAP3 on the phagocytic capacity of macrophages was explored by co-culturing conditioned macrophages with lung cancer cells. While both THP-1 and BMDMs showed low tumor cell phagocytic capacity, IQGAP3 knockdown dramatically increased phagocytosis of A549/H1299 and LLC tumor cells (Fig. 3R and S).

Colony formation assays revealed that CM from THP-1 with sh-IQGAP3 significantly inhibited the proliferation of LUAD cell A549 and H1299 (Fig. 4B). Meanwhile, we investigate the effects of IQGAP3-mediated M2-like macrophages on LUAD metastasis by wound healing and Transwell migration assays. CM was directly added to the LUAD cells or placed on the bottom of Transwell inserts. The results showed that CM from THP-1 with sh-IQGAP3 markedly decreased migration ability of LUAD cells (A549, H1299) (Fig. 4C and D). A consistent effect was observed in the Transwell or invasion assays (Fig. 4E and F). In addition, we also examined the effect of the CM from conditioned BMDMs on mouse lung cancer LLC cells. Compared with CM from si-NC BMDMs, CM from BMDMs with si-IQGAP3 significantly inhibited the proliferation, migration and invastion of LLCs (Fig. 4G-J).

IQGAP3 reprogram macrophages through FYN-STAT1 axis

To further explore the molecular mechanisms by which IQGAP3 promote M2 polarization, we performed RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) analysis of sh-NC and sh-IQGAP3 THP-1 macrophages. Totally 636 genes were upregulated in sh-IQGAP3 group while 309 genes were downregulated (Fig. 6A). In addition, we found that most of M1-related genes were upregulated in the sh-IQGAP3 group, while most M2-related genes were downregulated (Figure S5A). Gene Ontology (GO) and Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) revealed that inflammatory response, positive regulation of interleukin-1 beta production, type I interferon signaling pathway and positive regulation of tyrosine phosphorylation of STAT protein are enriched by the differential genes upregulated in the sh-IQGAP3 macrophages (Fig. 6B, Figure S5B-C).

Previous studies have shown that STAT1 and STAT4 phosphorylation are involved in the induction of an antitumoral phenotype in macrophages [16]. Next, WB was conducted to examine the expression of p-STAT1 (Tyr701) and p-STAT4 (Tyr693) between control and IQGAP3 knockdown of THP-1 or BMDMs. As shown in Fig. 6C, p-STAT1 expression was significantly increased in sh-IQGAP3 THP-1 and BMDMs, while no change was observed in p-STAT4 expression (Figure S5D). Subsequently, we knockdown STAT1 by siRNA in sh-IQGAP3 THP-1 cells (Figure S5E) and selected any of sequence for subsequent experiments. Those THP-1 cells were then induced to M1 or M2 by exposing to LPS + IFN-γ or IL-4 + IL-13 respectively. The result showed that knockdown of STAT1 abolish the inhibition effect of M2 polarization of IQGAP3 deficiency in THP-1 cells (Figure S5F-G). We also knockdown STAT1 by siRNA in si-IQGAP3 BMDMs, flow cytometry was conducted to examine macrophages polarization and consistent result was observed as THP-1 cells (Fig. 6D). Collectively, these results suggested that IQGAP3 deficiency inhibited M2 polarization by regulating the STAT1 phosphorylation.

Next, in order to clarify how IQGAP3 regulates the phosphorylation of STAT1, based on the results of RNA-seq, RT-qPCR was conducted to examine the mRNA expression levels of tyrosine kinases FYN and FLT3 in sh-NC or sh-IQGAP3 of THP-1 cells. Significantly increased mRNA expression level of FYN was observed in the sh-IQGAP3 of THP-1 cells while no change was observed in FLT3 expression (Figure S5H). WB results also showed increased expression of FYN in the sh-IQGAP3 THP-1 cells (Fig. 6E). Therefore, we supposed that IQGAP3 may affects STAT1 phosphorylation by regulating FYN. As expected, knockdown of FYN in sh-IQGAP3 THP-1 rescue the increased expression level of p-STAT1 (Figure S5I, Fig. 6F). Although canonical STAT1 signaling occurs through JAK, prior research indicates a role for FYN [17]. We observed that a FYN inhibitor (A154045, Ambeed) decreased p-STAT1 expression. The subsequent application of a JAK activator (A1949381, Ambeed) only partially reversed this effect (Figure S5J). This partial rescue indicates that FYN regulates STAT1 phosphorylation through a pathway that is not entirely dependent on JAK.

To elucidate the underlying mechanism by which IQGAP3 regulates FYN, mRNA stability assay was performed and the results showed that the half-life of FYN mRNA was increased in THP-1sh−IQGAP3cells compared to those from THP-1sh−NC cells (Fig. 6G). These observations indicated that IQGAP3 deficiency induced an antitumoral phenotype of macrophages by increasing the stability of FYN mRNA and further increased STAT1 phosphorylation.

IQGAP3 regulates FYN mRNA stability by interacting with IGF2BP2

RNA-binding proteins (RBPs) are critical regulators of mRNA. To identify potential regulators of FYN mRNA, we leveraged the POSTAR3 database [18] and predicted 28 candidate RBPs. Among these, the top candidate with the highest number of binding sites is presented in the Fig. 6H. Among these, IGF2BP2—a known regulator of mRNA stability [19] - emerged as one of candidates with high number of predicted binding sites. RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assays confirmed that IGF2BP2 directly binds FYN mRNA. Intriguingly, knockdown of IQGAP3 markedly enhanced this binding, suggesting that IQGAP3 may regulate FYN mRNA stability by modulating IGF2BP2 (Fig. 6I). Supporting this hypothesis, concurrent knockdown of IGF2BP2 in IQGAP3-silenced cells (Figure S5K) abolished the stabilization of FYN mRNA induced by IQGAP3 knockdown (Fig. 6J) and concurrently reversed the upregulation of both FYN (Figure S5L, Fig. 6K) and p-STAT1 (Fig. 6K).

We next investigated the mechanistic basis of this regulation. Co-immunoprecipitation assays confirmed a physical interaction between IQGAP3 and IGF2BP2 (Fig. 6L). Given the established role of IQGAP3 as a scaffolding protein [20], we hypothesized that it influences the subcellular localization of IGF2BP2. Immunofluorescence staining in control and IQGAP3-knockdown THP-1 cells revealed that IQGAP3 depletion caused a redistribution of IGF2BP2 from the cell periphery to the cytosol (Fig. 6M). Taken together, these results indicate that IQGAP3 binds to IGF2BP2 and restricts its localization to the cell periphery, thereby controlling FYN mRNA stability and downstream STAT1 signaling.

Targeting IQGAP3 on macrophages enhances the efficacy of anti-PD-1 treatment in LUAD

As we mentioned above, insufficient T cells infiltration or impaired T cells activation is one of the pivotal reasons of reduced anti-PD1/PD-L1 efficacy [5]. In this study, we demonstrated that interfering IQGAP3 on macrophages delayed tumor growth and increased activated CD8 + T cells infiltration within tumor TME. Therefore, we speculated that inhibition of IQGAP3 on macrophages may have a synergistic effect with anti-PD-1 therapy. To test this hypothesis, we organized four groups of mice: a control group (LLC + Raw264.7sh−NC + IgG), a knockdown group (LLC + Raw264.7sh−1 + IgG), a group receiving anti-PD1 monotherapy (LLC + Raw264.7sh−NC + anti-PD1 antibody), a group receiving combination interfence (LLC + Raw264.7sh−1 + anti-PD1 antibody). Treatment began on day 7 post-tumor cell injection when tumor volume reached around1 62.5mm3. The injection was administered every 3 days with a total dosage of 1 mg (Fig. 7A). Compared with the control group, the knockdown group and the group receiving anti-PD1 monotherapy slightly reduced tumor growth rate. Nevertheless, the group receiving combination therapy obviously inhibited tumor growth, as evidenced by around 1.5-fold and onefold shrinkage of tumor volume compared with the control group and single therapy group at the end of treatment (Fig. 7B-D). We subsequently performed flow cytometry to investigate the TME compositions. The result showed that the proportion of IFN-γ-expressing CD8 + T cells (cytotoxic activated CD8 + T cells) were dramatically increased (Fig. 7E) while PD-1 + CD8 + T cells (exhausted CD8 + T cells) (Fig. 7F) were decreased in combination group.

Discussion

Here, we have identified IQGAP3 as a modulator of macrophages polarization. We found that genetic knockdown of IQGAP3 induced an antitumoral polarization in macrophages, and simultaneously increased its phagocytic capacity and enhanced CD8 + T cells activation. Genetic silencing IQGAP3 in macrophages suppressed tumor growth and showed a synergistic effect when combined with PD-1 blockade, suggesting that IQGAP3 is a promising target for cancer immunotherapy.

As one of the most abundant subpopulations within TME of solid tumors, TAMs was reported to contribute to T cells dysfunction and exhaustion through secretion of cytokines or metabolites [21–23], and upregulating PD-L1 expression on tumor cells or other immunosuppressive cells [24–26]. Across multiple malignancies, the extent of macrophages infiltration typically positively correlates with resistance to PD-1/PD-L1 ICIs [27]. Therefore, TAMs are considered as pivotal targets for reversing resistance to anti-PD-1/PD-L1 therapy. Current advances focus primarily on three fronts: harnessing engineered CAR-macrophages to enhance tumor recognition, targeting phagocytosis checkpoints such as CD47, and reprogramming TAM plasticity within the TME. While CAR-macrophages have entered early-phase clinical trials, challenges such as limited persistence, inefficient migration to tumor sites, and potential genotoxicity associated with viral transduction remain to be addressed [28]. Similarly, the efficacy of phagocytosis checkpoint blockade can be compromised by TME-driven mechanisms, including the co-expression of multiple immune inhibitory signals [29]. Recent evidence suggests that re-educating M2-like TAMs may constitute a more effective strategy [27]. Agents possessing immunomodulatory properties can reprogram TAMs toward an M1-like phenotype, thereby inducing durable activation of both innate and adaptive antitumor immunity and enhancing the overall anti-tumor capacity of the TME [28]. In recent years, preclinical studies have identified multiple promising molecules regulating macrophages polarization, some of which have advanced to clinical trials via specific antibodies or inhibitors, demonstrating preliminary efficacy [30]. In this study, we identified widespread expression of IQGAP3 on infiltrating macrophages within the LUAD TME. Both in vitro and in vivo experiments demonstrated its critical regulatory role in macrophages polarization, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target.

IQ motif-containing GTPase-activating proteins (IQGAPs) are an evolutionarily conserved protein family expressed broadly in protists, fungi, and animal cells [12]. Humans express three IQGAP isoforms—IQGAP1, IQGAP2, and IQGAP3—which share similar domain structures and sequence homology. Despite their structural similarities, IQGAPs exhibit distinct expression patterns and biological regulatory functions in tumors. IQGAP1 is frequently upregulated in solid tumors and its expression level negatively correlates with patient prognosis [31–38]. Conversely, IQGAP2 always functions as “tumor suppressor” and reduced in most tumor tissues. IQGAP3 was identified in 2007. It is the latest identified gene in this family and less studied. Previous studies reported that IQGAP3 is commonly upregulated in malignancies [39–44] and primarily linked to tumor cells proliferation. IQGAP3 was identified as a specific marker for proliferative stem cells in the gastric corpus [11]. Furthermore, Nomura et al. demonstrated that exogenous IQGAP3 expression reactivates the cells cycle in quiescent cells by regulating proliferation through the RAS/ERK signaling cascade [45]. In addition, recent study reported that IQGAP3 may sustain tumor growth by modulating the TME [13]. However, the role of IQGAP3 in regulating the TME and immune cells remains largely unexplored. To our knowledge, our study is the first to define IQGAP3 expressions in LUAD tissues and investigate its regulatory role in macrophages polarization. Our results revealed higher expression level of IQGAP3 in LUAD TAMs than in alveolar macrophages. Meanwhile, IQGAP3 knockdown in macrophages promotes their polarization toward M1-like phenotype and enhance their anti-tumor immune response.

The scaffold protein family of IQ motif-containing GTPase-activating proteins interact with many partners to regulate diverse cellular processes [46]. A fundamental mechanism by which the scaffolding protein IQGAPs exerts its regulatory influence is through the precise control of subcellular localization, thereby directly modulating the functional output of its binding partners [47]. Our findings, which demonstrate that IQGAP3 binds to and confines IGF2BP2 at the cell periphery, provide a compelling example of this paradigm. The redistribution of IGF2BP2 to a diffuse cytoplasmic pattern upon IQGAP3 knockdown, coupled with the consequent functional increase in FYN mRNA stability, strongly suggests that its role in mRNA metabolism.

The JAK-STAT signaling pathway critically regulates macrophages polarization. Previous studies demonstrate that STAT3 or STAT6 activation promotes M2 polarization, whereas STAT1 or STAT4 activation is often involved in M1 polarization [48, 49]. In this study, based on RNA-seq and bioinformatics analysis, we examined the expression level of STAT1 and STAT4. The result revealed significantly elevated phosphorylation of STAT1 (Tyr701) without changes in total STAT1 protein levels, suggesting that IQGAP3 regulates STAT1 through post-translational modification rather than transcriptional level. To elucidate the mechanism underlying IQGAP3-mediated STAT1 phosphorylation, we scrutinized tyrosine kinase-related genes from RNA-seq data and identified FYN as markedly upregulated upon IQGAP3 knockdown. FYN, an SRC-family tyrosine kinase, was reported to play roles in modulating protein phosphorylation at tyrosine [50]. FYN involved in STAT1 phosphorylation was also mentioned in a previous study [17]. This study, through rescue experiments employing a JAK activator, reveals that FYN regulates STAT1 phosphorylation through both JAK-independent and JAK-dependent pathways. While these findings provide new insights into STAT1 regulatory networks, direct validation through in vitro kinase assays and site-directed mutagenesis of the predicted phosphorylation site on STAT1 will be necessary to conclusively establish FYN's ability to phosphorylate STAT1 directly.

Reprogramming macrophages polarization represents a promising therapeutic strategy to overcome resistance and enhance the efficacy of anti-PD-1 immunotherapy in cancer treatment. The current study demonstrates that IQGAP3 knockdown promotes M1-like macrophages polarization through the FYN-STAT1 signaling axis, subsequently enhancing CD8 + T cells activation and suppressing tumor growth. Furthermore, genetic ablation of IQGAP3 synergizes with anti-PD-1 immunotherapy in vivo, significantly improving therapeutic efficacy. These findings reveal a novel IQGAP3-modulated immunoregulatory mechanism and propose a promising combinatorial strategy for LUAD treatment.

Duality on symmetric multiple polylogarithms
Hanamichi Kawamura

Abstract
. There are three kinds of multiple polylogarithms; complex, finite and symmetric. The dualities for the complex and finite cases are known. In this paper, we present proofs of them via iterated integrals and its symmetric counterpart by a similar method.

11M32.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Notations and definitions

Put
\mathcal{I}\coloneqq\{\varnothing\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{r=1}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1})^{r}
and call its element an
index
.
Define
\mathrm{dep}\colon\mathcal{I}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
(resp.
\mathrm{wt}\colon\mathcal{I}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
) to be a map giving the number (resp. sum) of components.
Put
\mathrm{dep}(\varnothing)=\mathrm{wt}(\varnothing)=0
.
We say that an index
\mathbf{k}
is
admissible
if
\mathbf{k}=\varnothing
or its last component exceeds
1
.
We also define its variant with variables:

\mathcal{V}\coloneqq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\varnothing\\
\varnothing\end{pmatrix}\right\}\cup\left\{\begin{pmatrix}z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\\
\mathbf{k}\end{pmatrix}\left|\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{I},\\
r=\mathrm{dep}(\mathbf{k})\geq 1,\\
z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\in\mathbb{C},\\
|z_{1}|,\ldots,|z_{r}|\leq 1,\\
(k_{r},z_{r})\neq 1\end{array}\right.\right\}.

Finally, we denote by
\{X\}^{n}
the tuple
(\underbrace{X,\ldots,X}_{n})
.

1.2.
Complex MPLs

The
multiple polylogarithm
(
MPL
) is the map
\mathrm{Li}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}\colon\mathcal{V}\to\mathbb{C}
defined as

\mathrm{Li}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}\begin{pmatrix}z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\\
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\end{pmatrix}\coloneqq\sum_{0=n_{0}<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{r}}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}-n_{0}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}-n_{r-1}}}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}},

for
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\in\mathbb{D}\coloneqq\{z\mid|z|\leq 1\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
.
Put
\mathrm{Li}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}\begin{pmatrix}\varnothing\\
\varnothing\end{pmatrix}\coloneqq 1
for the empty tuple
\varnothing
.
This is a generalization of the
multiple zeta value
(
MZV
):

\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\coloneqq\sum_{0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{r}}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}=\mathrm{Li}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}\begin{pmatrix}1,\ldots,1\\
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\end{pmatrix}\qquad(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r-1}\geq 1,~k_{r}\geq 2).

An index
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1})^{r}
satisfying the above condition (i.e.,
r=0
or the last component exceeds
1
) is called an
admissible index
.
It is known that MZVs satisfy the
duality

(1.1)

\zeta(\mathbf{k})=\zeta(\mathbf{k}^{\dagger}),

where the dual
\mathbf{k}^{\dagger}
of an admissible index
\mathbf{k}
is defined as

\left(\{1\}^{a_{1}-1},b_{1}+1,\ldots,\{1\}^{a_{h}-1},b_{h}+1\right)^{\dagger}=\left(\{1\}^{b_{h}-1},a_{h}+1,\ldots,\{1\}^{b_{1}-1},a_{1}+1\right),

for
h,a_{1},\ldots,a_{h},b_{1},\ldots,b_{h}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.

As a generalization of (
1.1
) for MPLs, the following theorem holds.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
BBBL
, §6.1]
,
[
KMS
, Theorem 3.4]
)
.

Let
d,a_{1},\ldots,a_{d-1},b_{1},\ldots,b_{d-1}
be positive integers,
\mathbf{l}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{l}_{d}
admissible indices and
w_{1},\ldots,w_{d-1}
complex numbers.
Assume

(1)

\mathrm{Re}(w_{i})\leq 1/2
and
|w_{i}|\leq 1
for
1\leq i<d
,

(2)

\mathrm{Re}(w_{1})\neq 1/2
if
\mathbf{l}_{1}=\varnothing
and
a_{1}=1
,

(3)

|w_{d-1}|\neq 1
if
\mathbf{l}_{d}=\varnothing
and
b_{d-1}=1
.

Put
r_{i}\coloneqq\mathrm{dep}(\mathbf{l}_{i})
,
s_{i}\coloneqq\mathrm{dep}((\mathbf{l}_{i})^{\dagger})
,

\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{z}\\
\mathbf{k}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\{1\}^{r_{1}+a_{1}-1},w_{1},\ldots,\{1\}^{r_{d-1}+a_{d-1}-1},w_{d-1},\{1\}^{r_{d}}\\
\mathbf{l}_{1},\{1\}^{a_{1}-1},b_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{l}_{d-1},\{1\}^{a_{d-1}-1},b_{d-1},\mathbf{l}_{d}\end{pmatrix}

and

\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{z}\\
\mathbf{k}\end{pmatrix}^{\dagger}=\begin{pmatrix}\{1\}^{s_{d}+b_{d-1}-1},\frac{w_{d-1}}{w_{d-1}-1},\ldots,\{1\}^{s_{2}+b_{1}-1},\frac{w_{1}}{w_{1}-1},\{1\}^{s_{1}}\\
(\mathbf{l}_{d})^{\dagger},\{1\}^{b_{d-1}-1},a_{d-1},\ldots,(\mathbf{l}_{2})^{\dagger},\{1\}^{b_{1}-1},a_{1},(\mathbf{l}_{1})^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix}.

Then we have

\mathrm{Li}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{z}\\
\mathbf{k}\end{pmatrix}=(-1)^{d-1}\mathrm{Li}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}\left(\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{z}\\
\mathbf{k}\end{pmatrix}^{\dagger}\right).

Note that (
1.1
) is recovered by
d=1
in Theorem
1.1
.

1.3.
Finite MPLs

For
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, define the truncated series of the MPL

\mathrm{Li}_{<N}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}\begin{pmatrix}z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\\
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\end{pmatrix}\coloneqq\sum_{0=n_{0}<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{r}<N}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}-n_{0}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}-n_{r-1}}}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}

and its
star
variant

\mathrm{Li}_{<N}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}},\star}\begin{pmatrix}z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\\
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\end{pmatrix}\coloneqq\sum_{0=n_{0}<n_{1}\leq\cdots\leq n_{r}<N}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}-n_{0}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}-n_{r-1}}}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}},

as elements of
\mathbb{Q}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}]
.
Note that the star value is written as a sum of non-star values:

(1.2)

\mathrm{Li}_{<N}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}},\star}\begin{pmatrix}z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\\
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\end{pmatrix}=\sum_{d=0}^{r-1}\sum_{0=i_{0}<\cdots<i_{d+1}=r}\mathrm{Li}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}_{<N}\begin{pmatrix}z_{i_{0}+1},\ldots,z_{i_{d}+1}\\
k_{i_{0}+1}+\ldots+k_{i_{1}},\ldots,k_{i_{d}+1}+\cdots+k_{i_{d+1}}\end{pmatrix}.

When we consider
z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}
as complex numbers with suitable convergence conditions, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathrm{Li}_{<N}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}\begin{pmatrix}z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\\
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\end{pmatrix}=\mathrm{Li}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}\begin{pmatrix}z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\\
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\end{pmatrix}.

Mimicking this, we also define the star variant of the MPL as

\mathrm{Li}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}},\star}\begin{pmatrix}z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\\
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\end{pmatrix}\coloneqq\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathrm{Li}_{<N}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}},\star}\begin{pmatrix}z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\\
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\end{pmatrix}.

Remark 1.2
.

The generating function of
\mathrm{Li}_{<N}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}},\bullet}
about
N
is explained in terms of
\mathrm{Li}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}},\bullet}
.
Indeed, we have

(1.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Li}_{<N}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}\begin{pmatrix}z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\\
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\end{pmatrix}X^{N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{r}\\
N\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}-n_{0}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}-n_{r-1}}}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}X^{n_{r}+N}

(1.4)

\displaystyle=\frac{X}{1-X}\sum_{0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{r}}\frac{(Xz_{1})^{n_{1}-n_{0}}\cdots(Xz_{r})^{n_{r}-n_{r-1}}}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}

(1.5)

\displaystyle=\frac{X}{1-X}\mathrm{Li}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}\begin{pmatrix}Xz_{1},\ldots,Xz_{r}\\
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\end{pmatrix},

and similarly

\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Li}_{<N}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}},\star}\begin{pmatrix}z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\\
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\end{pmatrix}X^{N}=\frac{X}{1-X}\mathrm{Li}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}},\star}\begin{pmatrix}Xz_{1},\ldots,Xz_{r}\\
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\end{pmatrix}

holds.

Let us construct an algebra where we consider
\mathrm{Li}_{<p}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}
and
\mathrm{Li}_{<p}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}},\star}
for a prime number
p
.
For a positive integer
n
, a commutative ring
R
, we put

\mathcal{A}_{n,R}\coloneqq\left.\left(\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}R/p^{n}R\right)\right/\left(\bigoplus_{p\in\mathcal{P}}R/p^{n}R\right),

where
\mathcal{P}
is the set of all prime numbers.
Since
\{\mathcal{A}_{n,R}\}_{n}
forms a projective system of a (discrete) topological ring together with natural transitions, we can take the projective limit
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{R}\coloneqq\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{A}_{n,R}
.
Note that there is a natural ring homomorphism

\pi_{R}\colon\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\varprojlim_{n}R/p^{n}R\to\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{R},

obtained in
[
S
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Definition 1.3
.

For an index
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
, we define the
\boldsymbol{p}
-adic finite multiple polylogarithm
(
\boldsymbol{p}
-adic FMPL) by

\pounds_{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}},\bullet}\begin{pmatrix}z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\\
\mathbf{k}\end{pmatrix}\coloneqq\pi_{\mathbb{Z}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}]}\left(\left(\mathrm{Li}_{<p}^{\bullet}\begin{pmatrix}z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\\
\mathbf{k}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\right)\qquad(\bullet\in\{\varnothing,\star\}),

as an element of
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}]}
.

The following theorem seems to be a finite analog of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
S
, Theorem 3.4]
)
.

Put

\mathcal{L}^{\star}_{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}\begin{pmatrix}z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\\
\mathbf{k}\end{pmatrix}\coloneqq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\pounds_{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}},\star}\begin{pmatrix}z_{1},\ldots,z_{r},\{1\}^{n}\\
\mathbf{k},\{1\}^{n}\end{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{2}\pounds_{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}},\star}\begin{pmatrix}1,z_{2},\ldots,z_{r},\{1\}^{n}\\
\mathbf{k},\{1\}^{n}\end{pmatrix}\right)\boldsymbol{p}^{n}.

Then, for indices
\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{d}
(
d\geq 1
), we have

\mathcal{L}^{\star}_{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}\begin{pmatrix}z_{1},\{1\}^{\mathrm{dep}(\mathbf{k}_{1})-1},\ldots,z_{d},\{1\}^{\mathrm{dep}(\mathbf{k}_{d})-1}\\
\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{d}\end{pmatrix}=\mathcal{L}^{\star}_{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}\begin{pmatrix}1-z_{1},\{1\}^{\mathrm{dep}((\mathbf{k}_{1})^{\vee})-1},\ldots,1-z_{d},\{1\}^{\mathrm{dep}((\mathbf{k}_{d})^{\vee})-1}\\
(\mathbf{k}_{1})^{\vee},\ldots,(\mathbf{k}_{d})^{\vee}\end{pmatrix},

in
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]}
.
Here, for an index
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
, we denote by
\mathbf{k}^{\vee}
the index
(l_{1},\ldots,l_{s})
satisfying

(l_{s},\ldots,l_{2},l_{1}+1)=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}+1)^{\dagger}.

The natural surjection
\mathbb{Z}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}]\to\mathbb{Z}
;
z_{i}\mapsto 1
induces the map
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}]}\to\widehat{\mathcal{A}}\coloneqq\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
and this construction is compatible with
\pi_{R}
’s.
Through this assignment, we define the
\boldsymbol{p}
-adic finite multiple zeta values
(
\boldsymbol{p}
-adic FMZV
) as follows:

\zeta_{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}^{\bullet}(\mathbf{k})\coloneqq\pi_{\mathbb{Z}}\left(\left(\mathrm{Li}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}},\bullet}_{<p}\begin{pmatrix}\{1\}^{\mathrm{dep}(\mathbf{k})}\\
\mathbf{k}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\right)\qquad(\bullet\in\{\varnothing,\star\}).

This value satisfies the
\boldsymbol{p}
-adic duality

(1.6)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\zeta_{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}^{\star}(\mathbf{k},\{1\}^{n})\boldsymbol{p}^{n}=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\zeta_{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}^{\star}(\mathbf{k}^{\vee},\{1\}^{n})\boldsymbol{p}^{n},

which immediately follows from Theorem
1.4
.

1.4.
Symmetric MPLs

The refined Kaneko–Zagier conjecture (for example, see
[
OSY
, Conjecture 4.3]
) asserts that there is a strong relationship between the value
\zeta_{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathbf{k})
appearing in the previous subsection and the
t
-adic symmetric multiple zeta value
(
t
-adic SMZV
) defined below.
Put

(1.7)

\zeta_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{k_{i+1}+\cdots+k_{r}}\zeta^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{i})\\
\cdot\sum_{n_{i+1},\ldots,n_{r}\geq 0}\left(\prod_{j=i+1}^{r}\binom{k_{j}+n_{j}-1}{n_{j}}\right)\zeta^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}(k_{r}+n_{r},\ldots,k_{i+1}+n_{i+1})t^{n_{i+1}+\cdots+n_{r}}\in\mathbb{R}[\![t]\!],

where
\zeta^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}(\mathbf{l})
is defined as follows: there uniquely exists
P(x)\in\mathbb{R}[x]
such that

\mathrm{Li}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}\begin{pmatrix}1-z,\ldots,1-z\\
\mathbf{l}\end{pmatrix}=P(\log z)+O(z(\log z)^{J}),\qquad\text{as }z\to+0,

for some
J>0
, and we put
\zeta^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}(\mathbf{l})\coloneqq P(0)
.
Actually
\zeta_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}(\mathbf{k})
is in the power series ring
\mathcal{Z}[\![t]\!]
over
\mathcal{Z}
(
=
the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra generated by all MZVs).
This value modulo
\zeta(2)
, i.e.,
\zeta_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}(\mathbf{k})\coloneqq\zeta_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}(\mathbf{k})\in\mathcal{Z}/\zeta(2)\mathcal{Z}
, is called the
t
-adic SMZV.
Immitating (
1.2
), we put

\zeta^{\star}_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\coloneqq\sum_{d=0}^{r-1}\sum_{0=i_{0}<\cdots<i_{d+1}=r}\zeta_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}(k_{i_{0}+1}+\cdots+k_{i_{1}},\ldots,k_{i_{d}+1}+\cdots+k_{i_{d+1}}).

One of the evidences of the (refined) Kaneko–Zagier conjecture is the
t
-adic duality:

Theorem 1.5

(
[
TT
, Corollary 5.5]
)
.

For an index
\mathbf{k}
, we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\zeta_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}^{\star}(\mathbf{k},\{1\}^{n})t^{n}=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\zeta_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}^{\star}(\mathbf{k}^{\vee},\{1\}^{n})t^{n}.

As far as the author knows, any correspondence generalizing the (refined) Kaneko–Zagier conjecture for (finite and symmetric) multiple polylogarithms is not explicitly conjectured. However, we can define the
t
-adic symmetric multiple polylogarithm

(1.8)

\pounds_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}},\alpha}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}\begin{pmatrix}z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\\
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\end{pmatrix}\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq r\\
z_{i+1}\neq 0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{i+1}+\cdots+k_{r}}z_{i+1}^{\alpha}\mathrm{Li}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}/z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{i}/z_{i+1}\\
k_{1},\ldots,k_{i}\end{pmatrix}\\
\cdot\sum_{n_{i+1},\ldots,n_{r}\geq 0}\left(\prod_{j=i+1}^{r}\binom{k_{j}+n_{j}-1}{n_{j}}\right)\mathrm{Li}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}\begin{pmatrix}z_{r+1}/z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{i+2}/z_{i+1}\\
k_{r}+n_{r},\ldots,k_{i+1}+n_{i+1}\end{pmatrix}t^{n_{i+1}+\cdots+n_{r}},

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
and
z_{r+1}\coloneqq 1
.
The variables
z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}
are taken from a set
Q
satisfying some technical conditions (see the beginning of §
3
).
This quantity
\pounds_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}},\alpha}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}
lives in the quotient
(\mathcal{Z}_{Q}/2\pi i\mathcal{Z}_{Q})[\![t]\!]
of the power series ring over a certain ring
\mathcal{Z}_{Q}/2\pi i\mathcal{Z}_{Q}

2.
Iterated integrals and dualities of complex and finite MPLs

2.1.
Review on iterated integrals

For a commutaive ring
R
and a set
S
, denote by
R\langle S\rangle
the free
R
-algebra generated by
S
.
We say that
\gamma
is a
path
if
\gamma
is a continuous piecewise smooth map
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
.
The composition (resp. reverse) of such paths is denoted by
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\mapsto\gamma_{1}\cdot\gamma_{2}
(order in which they pass) (resp.
\gamma\mapsto\gamma^{-1}
).
These operations give rise to that of their homotopy classes.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
be a path and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\gamma((0,1))
such that
a_{0}\coloneqq\gamma(0)\neq a_{1}
and
a_{k+1}\coloneqq\gamma(1)\neq a_{k}
.
Then we define

I_{\gamma}(a_{0};a_{1},\ldots,a_{k};a_{k+1})\coloneqq\int_{0<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{k}<1}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{d\gamma(t_{i})}{\gamma(t_{i})-a_{i}}.

Such iterated integrals are invariant under a homotopic deformation of paths, that is,
I_{\gamma}
depends only on the homotopy class
\gamma
represents.

Moreover, let us give the definition of regularized iterated integrals.

Definition 2.2

(Regularized iterated integrals)
.

Take a sufficiently small
1/2>z>0
and define a “shortened” path
\gamma_{z,1-z}
by
t\mapsto\gamma((1-z)t+zt)
.
Then, for
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
(possibly
a_{1}=a_{0}
or
a_{k}=a_{k+1}
), there uniquely exists
P(x)\in\mathbb{C}[x]
such that

I_{\gamma_{z,1-z}}(a_{0};a_{1},\ldots,a_{k};a_{k+1})=P(\log z)+O(z(\log z)^{J}),\qquad\text{as }z\to+0.

We define
I_{\gamma}(e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}})\coloneqq P(0)
and
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly extend it to
I_{\gamma}\colon\mathfrak{H}_{\gamma}\coloneqq\mathbb{Q}\langle e_{z}\mid z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\gamma((0,1))\rangle\to\mathbb{C}
.

We usually identify with the formal symbol
e_{a}
with the
1
-form
(t-a)^{-1}dt
.
Note that

(2.1)

e_{a}(b+ct)=c\frac{dt}{(b+ct)-a}=e_{(a-b)/c}(t),

and

(2.2)

e_{a}(t^{-1})=-\frac{dt}{t^{2}(t^{-1}-a)}=\left(\frac{1}{(t-a^{-1})}-\frac{1}{t}\right)\,dt=(e_{1/a}-e_{0})(t)

hold for
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
.
For (regularized) iterated integrals, the following formulas hold:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
be composable paths.
Then, for
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\gamma((0,1))
and
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{H}_{\gamma}
such that the following iterated integrals exist, we have

(1)

(Path composition formula)
\displaystyle I_{\gamma\cdot\gamma^{\prime}}(e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}})=\sum_{i=0}^{k}I_{\gamma}(e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{i}})I_{\gamma^{\prime}}(e_{a_{i+1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}})
.

(2)

(Reversal formula)
I_{\gamma}(e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}})=(-1)^{k}I_{\gamma^{-1}}(e_{a_{k}}\cdots e_{a_{1}})
.

(3)

(Shuffle product formula)

k\coloneqq\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{k}_{1})+\cdots+\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{k}_{d})
.
Use the notation
\omega_{u}(l_{1},\ldots,l_{s})\coloneqq e_{0}^{l_{1}-1}e_{1/u}\cdots e_{0}^{l_{s-1}-1}e_{1/u}e_{0}^{l_{s}-1}
for
u\in Q^{\times}
and an index
(l_{1},\ldots,l_{s})\neq\varnothing
.
From Corollary
3.5
we obtain

(3.15)

e^{-\pi it/2}\mathcal{L}_{\widehat{\mathcal{RS}},\alpha}^{\star}\begin{pmatrix}w_{1},\ldots,w_{r}\\
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\end{pmatrix}\\
=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2\pi i}\sum_{u\in Q^{\times}}u^{\alpha}I_{\eta_{u}}((e_{w_{1}/u}-(e_{1/u}+e_{0})/2)e_{0}^{k_{1}-1}e_{w_{2}/u}e_{0}^{k_{2}-1}\cdots e_{w_{r}/u}e_{0}^{k_{r}-1}(f_{1/u,1/u,t}-f_{0,0,t}))

for
w_{1},\ldots,w_{r}\in Q
and
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.
Therefore it follows that

(3.16)

\displaystyle e^{-\pi it/2}\mathcal{L}_{\widehat{\mathcal{RS}},\alpha}^{\star}\begin{pmatrix}z_{1},\{1\}^{\mathrm{dep}(\mathbf{k}_{1})-1},\ldots,z_{r},\{1\}^{\mathrm{dep}(\mathbf{k}_{d})-1}\\
\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{d}\end{pmatrix}

(3.17)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2\pi i}\sum_{u\in Q^{\times}}u^{\alpha}I_{\eta_{u}}((e_{z_{1}/u}-(e_{1/u}+e_{0})/2)\omega_{u}(\mathbf{k}_{1})e_{z_{2}/u}\omega_{u}(\mathbf{k}_{2})\cdots e_{z_{d}/u}\omega_{u}(\mathbf{k}_{d})(f_{1/u,1/u,t}-f_{0,0,t})).

Substituting
z\mapsto 1/u-z
and using (
2.1
), we obtain

(3.18)

\displaystyle e^{-\pi it/2}\mathcal{L}_{\widehat{\mathcal{RS}},\alpha}^{\star}\begin{pmatrix}z_{1},\{1\}^{\mathrm{dep}(\mathbf{k}_{1})-1},\ldots,z_{r},\{1\}^{\mathrm{dep}(\mathbf{k}_{d})-1}\\
\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{d}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2\pi i}e^{-\pi it}\sum_{u\in Q^{\times}}u^{\alpha}\\
\cdot I_{\eta_{u}^{-1}}((e_{(1-z_{1})/u}-(e_{0}+e_{1/u})/2)\omega_{u}((\mathbf{k}_{1})^{\vee})e_{(1-z_{2})/u}\omega_{u}((\mathbf{k}_{2})^{\vee})\cdots e_{(1-z_{d})/u}\omega_{u}((\mathbf{k}_{d})^{\vee})(-f_{0,0,t}+f_{1/u,1/u,t}))\end{multlined}

(3.21)

\displaystyle=e^{-\pi it/2}\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\widehat{\mathcal{RS}},\alpha}^{\star}\begin{pmatrix}1-z_{1},\{1\}^{\mathrm{dep}((\mathbf{k}_{1})^{\vee})-1},\ldots,1-z_{r},\{1\}^{\mathrm{dep}((\mathbf{k}_{d})^{\vee})-1}\\
(\mathbf{k}_{1})^{\vee},\ldots,(\mathbf{k}_{d})^{\vee}\end{pmatrix},

where
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}
is defined similarly as
\mathcal{L}
except for that
\eta_{u}
(resp.
e^{\pi it/2}
) is replaced by
\eta_{u}^{-1}
(resp.
e^{-\pi it/2}
).
Thus we get the theorem by this equation and (
3.2
).
∎

Theorem
1.6
follows from Theorem
3.6
and Proposition
3.2
.

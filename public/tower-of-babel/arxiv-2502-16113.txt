The elliptic Hall algebra and the double Dyck path algebra
Nicolle González, Eugene Gorsky, José Simental

Abstract
. We show that the positive half ℰ q , t > \mathcal{E}_{q,t}^{>} of the elliptic Hall algebra is embedded as a natural spherical subalgebra inside the double Dyck path algebra 𝔹 q , t \mathbb{B}_{q,t} introduced by Carlsson, Mellit and the second author. For this, we use the Ding-Iohara-Miki presentation of the elliptic Hall algebra and identify the generators inside 𝔹 q , t \mathbb{B}_{q,t} . In order to obtain the entire elliptic Hall algebra ℰ q , t \mathcal{E}_{q,t} , we define a “double” 𝔻 ​ 𝔹 q , t \mathbb{DB}_{q,t} of the double Dyck path algebra, together with its positive and negative subalgebras and an involution that exchanges them.

Although

The algebra

The algebra

acts on the space

1.1.
The Double of
\mathbb{B}_{q,t}

The algebra
\mathbb{B}_{q,t}
has a nonnegative grading (with
\deg(d_{-})=\deg(T_{i})=\deg(z_{i})=0
and
\deg(d_{+})=1
) which agrees with the grading on the polynomial representation. In particular, any element of
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
acting on
\mathrm{Sym}_{q,t}
must increase the degree, and there is no hope of obtaining the generators
f_{m}\in\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
using
\mathbb{B}_{q,t}
.

To circumvent this problem, we define a new algebra
\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}
, a “double” of
\mathbb{B}_{q,t}
akin to the Drinfeld double construction of
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
in which we define the new families of elements
\psi_{m}^{\pm}
and

(1.3)

f_{m}:=\mathbbm{1}_{0}d_{+}z_{1}^{m}d_{-}\mathbbm{1}_{0}\in\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0},\ m\in\mathbb{Z}.

\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}
also has “positive” and “negative” parts exchanged by an anti-involution
\Theta
, and a
family of new idempotents
\mathbbm{1}_{-k}=\Theta(\mathbbm{1}_{k}),k\geq 0
. The
decomposition in (
1.1
) transforms into

(1.4)

V=\bigoplus_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}V_{k},\ V_{k}=\mathbbm{1}_{k}V.

Our first main result defines the polynomial representation of
\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}
and compares it with the polynomial representation of
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

The algebra
\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}
admits a polynomial representation
V=\bigoplus_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}V_{k}
with

V_{k}=\begin{cases}\mathrm{Sym}_{q,t}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}]&\text{if}\ k>0\\
\mathrm{Sym}_{q,t}&\text{if}\ k=0\\
\mathrm{Sym}_{q,t}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{|k|}]&\text{if}\ k<0.\\
\end{cases}

In particular, the action of the generators
e_{m}
,
f_{m}
, and
\psi_{m}^{\pm}
on
V_{0}=\mathrm{Sym}_{q,t}
defined in (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) agrees with the action of the eponymic generators of
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
, up to scalars.

Remark 1.3
.

More precisely, for
k>0
the space
V_{k}=\mathrm{Sym}_{q,t}[y_{1},\dots,y_{k}]=\mathrm{Sym}_{q,t}\otimes\mathbb{C}[y_{1},\dots,y_{k}]
has a natural grading, and
V_{-k}
is the graded dual space. Since the polynomial representation of
\mathbb{B}_{q,t}
is calibrated, the space
V_{k}
comes equipped with a natural graded basis
[
1
]
and this allows us to identify
V_{k}^{\vee}
with
V_{k}
, see Sections
3.3
and
3.4
for details.

1.2.
The Spherical Subalgebra

Next, we study the
spherical subalgebra

\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
in detail. The following is the primary result of the paper.

Theorem 1.4
.

There exists an isomorphism
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}\simeq\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
which sends the generators
e_{m},f_{m},
and
\psi_{m}^{\pm}
to their namesakes in
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
.

An immediate consequence of the polynomial representation of
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
being faithful
[
35
, Proposition 4.10]
, is the following.

Corollary 1.5
.

The polynomial representation of
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
(and hence of
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
) is faithful.

It remains unknown whether the polynomial representation of
\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}
, or even of
\mathbb{B}_{q,t}
, is faithful.

The proof of Theorem
1.4
consists of several different steps:

In the
first step
, we introduce the subalgebra
\mathbb{E}_{q,t}\subset\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
generated by
e_{m},f_{m},
and
\psi_{m}^{\pm}
and prove the following:

Theorem 1.6
.

We have the isomorphism
\mathbb{E}_{q,t}\simeq\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
which sends the generators
e_{m},f_{m}
and
\psi_{m}^{\pm}
from (
1.2
),(
1.3
) to their eponyms in
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
.

The proof of Theorem
1.6
occupies Section
4
. We first use the relations in
\mathbb{B}_{q,t}
to prove that the elements
e_{m}\in\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
satisfy the defining relations (
2.2a
) and (
2.2h
) in
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}^{>}
. Next, we use the involution
\Theta
to prove the analogous result for
f_{m}
. The rest of the relations involving the generators
\psi_{m}^{\pm}
follow more or less automatically (in particular, the equation for the commutator
[e_{m},f_{k}]
is built into the definition of
\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}
). Altogether, this yields a surjective homomorphism

\mathcal{E}_{q,t}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{E}_{q,t}.

Using Theorem
1.2
and faithfulness of the
polynomial representation of
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
, we conclude that this is an isomorphism.

For the
second step
, we define certain elements

(1.5)

Y_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}:=\mathbbm{1}_{0}d_{-}z_{1}^{m_{1}}\varphi z_{2}^{m_{2}}\cdots\varphi z_{1}^{m_{n}}d_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{0}\in\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}\quad m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},

where

The elements

were previously considered (in the polynomial representation of

) in
[
10

.
Evidently, for

we have

Y_{m}=e_{m}\in\mathbb{E}_{q,t}
. However, it is not clear from this definition that
Y_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}\in\mathbb{E}_{q,t}
for

. Nevertheless, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.7
.

The elements
Y_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}
generate the spherical subalgebra
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
.

In Section
5.1
we describe an explicit inductive procedure for writing an arbitrary element of
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
as a polynomial in

. In fact, we define a “higher level” analogues of

in
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{k}

Finally, in the
third step
we prove the containment

for all

Presenting

seems to be a

so we choose a rather indirect approach inspired by

17

Theorem 1.8
.

The following relations hold:

(1.6)

Y_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{i},m_{i+1},\ldots,m_{n}}-qtY_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{i}-1,m_{i+1}+1,\ldots,m_{n}}=-Y_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{i}}Y_{m_{i+1},\ldots,m_{n}},

1.3.
Future Directions

Theorem
1.4
opens up several new directions and questions in the representation theory of both
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
and
\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}
. Some of these include:

(1)

Which calibrated representations of
\mathbb{B}_{q,t}
from
[
15
]
can be lifted to representations of
\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}
? By Theorem
1.4
these would yield representations of
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
. We plan to address this question in future work
[
14
]
.

(2)

Given a representation of
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
, is it possible to lift it to a representation of
\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}
or
\mathbb{B}_{q,t}
?

(3)

A product of several
d_{+}
and
d_{-}
in
\mathbb{B}_{q,t}
corresponding to a Dyck path defines an element of
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
. The action of such elements in the polynomial representation played a crucial role in
[
11
]
. Is it possible to define the corresponding element of
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
more explicitly? What if we add monomials in
z_{i}
between
d_{\pm}
?

(4)

Blasiak et al.
[
8
]
recently defined lots of interesting elements of
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
generalizing
Y_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}
. Is it possible to write these elements in
\mathbb{B}_{q,t}
more explicitly?

(5)

The elliptic Hall algebra
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
is part of a large family of
quantum toroidal algebras
which can be assigned to an arbitrary affine Dynkin diagram (
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
corresponds to
\widehat{A_{1}}
). Is it possible to define the analogues of
\mathbb{B}_{q,t}
or
\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}
for arbitrary Dynkin diagrams?

(6)

Is the polynomial representation of
\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}
faithful?

(7)

The elliptic Hall algebra has an integral form defined in
[
30
]
, it would be very interesting to define a matching integral form of
\mathbb{B}_{q,t}
. Note that Theorem
1.4
requires working over
\mathbb{C}(q,t)
, since expressing the elements
Y_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}
in terms of
e_{k}
requires denominators.
See Example
4.11
for a sample of this phenomenon.

(8)

There is an
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-action on the elliptic Hall algebra
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
. Does this action extend to the algebra
\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}
? Some indication of this is given in
[
22
, Section 3]
.

2.
The Elliptic Hall Algebra

2.1.
Generators and Relations

As mentioned in the introduction, the
elliptic Hall algebra
arises under many guises and names in the literature. Here, we follow
[
12
]
and consider the algebra
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
with generators
e_{m},f_{m}\ (m\in\mathbb{Z})
and
\psi^{+}_{m},\psi^{-}_{-m}\ (m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}).
It is convenient to package these in various generating series

e(\mathbf{z})=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}e_{m}\mathbf{z}^{-m},\ f(\mathbf{z})=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{m}\mathbf{z}^{-m},\ \psi^{+}(\mathbf{z})=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\psi^{+}_{m}\mathbf{z}^{-m},\ \psi^{-}(\mathbf{z})=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\psi^{-}_{-m}\mathbf{z}^{m}.

Now, fixing parameters
q_{1}=q
,
q_{2}=t
and
q_{3}=q^{-1}t^{-1}
so that
q_{1}q_{2}q_{3}=1
, and letting
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
denote the elementary symmetric functions:

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}

\displaystyle=q_{1}+q_{2}+q_{3}=q+t+q^{-1}t^{-1},

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}

\displaystyle=q_{1}q_{2}+q_{2}q_{3}+q_{1}q_{3}=qt+q^{-1}+t^{-1},

define the polynomial

(2.1)

g(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w}):=(\mathbf{z}-q_{1}\mathbf{w})(\mathbf{z}-q_{2}\mathbf{w})(\mathbf{z}-q_{3}\mathbf{w})=\mathbf{z}^{3}-\sigma_{1}\mathbf{z}^{2}\mathbf{w}+\sigma_{2}\mathbf{z}\mathbf{w}^{2}-\mathbf{w}^{3}.

The defining relations of
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
are then given by
[
12
]
:

(2.2a)

\displaystyle g(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w})e(\mathbf{z})e(\mathbf{w})

\displaystyle=-g(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})e(\mathbf{w})e(\mathbf{z}),

(2.2b)

\displaystyle g(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})f(\mathbf{z})f(\mathbf{w})

\displaystyle=-g(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w})f(\mathbf{w})f(\mathbf{z}),

(2.2c)

\displaystyle g(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w})\psi^{\pm}(\mathbf{z})e(\mathbf{w})

\displaystyle=-g(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})e(\mathbf{w})\psi^{\pm}(\mathbf{z}),\

(2.2d)

\displaystyle g(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})\psi^{\pm}(\mathbf{z})f(\mathbf{w})

\displaystyle=-g(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w})f(\mathbf{w})\psi^{\pm}(\mathbf{z}),

(2.2e)

[e(\mathbf{z}),f(\mathbf{w})]=g(1,1)^{-1}\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{z}^{n}\mathbf{w}^{-n}\right)(\psi^{+}(\mathbf{w})-\psi^{-}(\mathbf{z})),

(2.2f)

[\psi_{i}^{\pm},\psi_{j}^{\pm}]=0,\ [\psi_{i}^{\pm},\psi_{j}^{\mp}]=0,

(2.2g)

\psi_{0}^{\pm}(\psi_{0}^{\pm})^{-1}=(\psi_{0}^{\pm})^{-1}\psi_{0}^{\pm}=1,

(2.2h)

\displaystyle[e_{0},[e_{1},e_{-1}]]

\displaystyle=0,

(2.2i)

\displaystyle[f_{0},[f_{1},f_{-1}]]

\displaystyle=0.

Notice that (
2.2f
) simply says that
\psi_{i}^{\pm}
pairwise commute, and (
2.2g
) that both
\psi_{0}^{+}
and
\psi_{0}^{-}
are invertible.

A less trivial consequence of (
2.2c
) and (
2.2d
) is that both
\psi_{0}^{+}
and
\psi_{0}^{-}
commute with all
e_{m}
and
f_{m}
and hence are central in
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
. Indeed, looking at the coefficient of
\mathbf{z}^{3}\mathbf{w}^{-m}
(for
\psi^{+}
) and
\mathbf{z}^{0}\mathbf{w}^{-m}
(for
\psi^{-}
)
on both sides of (
2.2c
) we obtain that
\psi_{0}^{\pm}e_{m}=e_{m}\psi_{0}^{\pm}
, and similarly that
\psi_{0}^{\pm}f_{m}=f_{m}\psi_{0}^{\pm}
. Note, it is important here that the generating series
\psi^{+}(\mathbf{z})
and
\psi^{-}(\mathbf{z})
are infinite only on one side.

Let us also elaborate on the relation (
2.2e
). By expanding the formal power series on both sides, we see that (
2.2e
) is equivalent to:

(2.3)

[e_{n},f_{m}]=\begin{cases}g(1,1)^{-1}\psi_{m+n}^{+},&\text{if}\;m+n>0\\
-g(1,1)^{-1}\psi_{-(m+n)}^{-}&\text{if}\;m+n<0,\\
g(1,1)^{-1}(\psi_{0}^{+}-\psi_{0}^{-})&\text{if}\;m+n=0\end{cases}

The following is clear from the relations.

Lemma 2.1
.

The algebra
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
has a
\mathbb{C}(q,t)
-linear
anti-involution

\Theta_{\mathcal{E}}
defined by

\Theta_{\mathcal{E}}(e_{m})=f_{m},\ \Theta_{\mathcal{E}}(f_{m})=e_{m},\ \Theta_{\mathcal{E}}(\psi^{\pm}_{m})=\psi^{\pm}_{m}.

We will work with various subalgebras of
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
positive subalgebra

\mathcal{E}_{q,t}^{\geq}
has generators
e_{m},m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\psi^{+}_{m},\psi^{-}_{-m}
(
m\geq 0
) satisfying the relations
(
2.2a
), (
2.2c
), (
2.2f
), (
2.2g
), and (
2.2h
).

Definition 2.3
.

The
negative subalgebra

\mathcal{E}_{q,t}^{\leq}
has generators
f_{m},m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\psi^{+}_{m},\psi^{-}_{-m}
(

) satisfying the relations (

), (

), (
2.2f
), (

), and (

).

We similarly define the subalgebra

(resp.
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}^{<}\subset\mathcal{E}_{q,t}^{\leq}

2.2.
The Polynomial Representation of
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}

Following Feigin and Tsymbaliuk
[
13
]
, we now describe the polynomial representation of
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
on the space
\mathrm{Sym}_{q,t}
of symmetric functions over
\mathbb{C}(q,t)
.

Given any partition
\lambda
and a box
\Box\in\lambda
in row
r
and column
c
, the
(q,t)-content
of this box is the scalar
q^{c-1}t^{r-1}
. In what follows we abuse notation, and denote both the box in the partition and its associated
(q,t)
-content by
\Box
. Since the position of a box is uniquely determined by its
(q,t)
-content, we also sometimes abuse notation and write
\lambda\cup x
to the denote the partition obtained from
\lambda
by adding the box whose
(q,t)
-content is equal to
x
.

So then, taking the basis of
\mathrm{Sym}_{q,t}
to be the modified Macdonald polynomials
I_{\lambda}:=\widetilde{H}_{\lambda}(X;q,t)
, the action is given by
[
13
, Lem. 3.1 and Prop. 3.1]
:

(2.4a)

\displaystyle e_{m}I_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu=\lambda\cup x}\frac{(q^{\mu_{i}-1}t^{i-1})^{m}}{1-qt}\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-q^{\mu_{j}-\mu_{i}+1}t^{j-i+1})}{(1-q^{\mu_{j}-\mu_{i}+1}t^{j-i})}I_{\mu},

(2.4b)

\displaystyle f_{m}I_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda=\mu-x}\frac{(q^{\lambda_{i}}t^{i-1})^{m-1}}{1-qt}\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}+1}t^{i-j+1}}{1-q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}+1}t^{i-j}}I_{\lambda},

where the sums are taken over all partitions
\mu
(resp.
\lambda
) that can be obtained from
\lambda
(resp.
\mu
) by adding (resp. removing) a box, with
i
equal to the row of the box.

We also have,

(2.4c)

\displaystyle\psi^{\pm}(\mathbf{z})I_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{1-q^{-1}t^{-1}\mathbf{z}^{-1}}{1-\mathbf{z}^{-1}}\prod_{\square\in\lambda}\frac{(1-q^{-1}\square\mathbf{z}^{-1})(1-t^{-1}\square\mathbf{z}^{-1})(1-qt\square\mathbf{z}^{-1})}{(1-q\square\mathbf{z}^{-1})(1-t\square\mathbf{z}^{-1})(1-q^{-1}t^{-1}\square\mathbf{z}^{-1})}\right)I_{\lambda},

where the coefficient of
I_{\lambda}
in the RHS of (
2.4c
) is a rational function in
\mathbf{z}
, and should thus be expanded as a power series in
\mathbf{z}^{\mp 1}
to obtain the formula for the action of
\psi^{\pm}(\mathbf{z})
.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
35
]
, Proposition 4.10)
.

The polynomial representation of
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
is faithful.

It will be useful to slightly rewrite equations (
2.4a
)-(
2.4c
).
Define the scalars in
\mathbb{C}(q,t)
,

(2.5a)

\displaystyle c(\lambda;x)

\displaystyle:=-\Lambda\left(-x^{-1}+(1-q)(1-t)B_{\lambda}x^{-1}+1\right)

(2.5b)

\displaystyle c^{*}(\mu;x)

\displaystyle:=x^{-1}\Lambda\big(-(1-q)(1-t)B^{*}_{\lambda}x\big)

where
\lambda,\mu
are partitions with
\mu=\lambda\cup x
, and

B_{\lambda}=\sum_{\square\in\lambda}\square,\quad B^{*}_{\lambda}=\sum_{\square\in\lambda}\square^{-1},\quad\text{and}\quad\Lambda\left(\sum\phi_{i,j}q^{i}t^{j}\right)=\prod(1-q^{i}t^{j})^{\phi_{i,j}}.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose that
\mu=\lambda\cup\Box
with
\Box
in row
i
and
(q,t)
-content of
\Box
is equal to
x
.
Then the coefficients (
2.5a
) and (
2.5b
) can be written as

c(\lambda;x)=-(1-t)\prod_{j\neq i}\frac{(1-q^{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}}t^{j-i+1})}{(1-q^{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}}t^{j-i})}=-(1-t)\prod_{j\neq i}\frac{(1-q^{\mu_{j}-\mu_{i}+1}t^{j-i+1})}{(1-q^{\mu_{j}-\mu_{i}+1}t^{j-i})}.

and

c^{*}(\mu;x)=\frac{(q^{\lambda_{i}}t^{i-1})^{-1}}{(1-q)}\prod_{j\neq i}\frac{(1-q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}+1}t^{i-j+1})}{(1-q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}+1}t^{i-j})}

Proof.

We expand
B_{\lambda}
and
B^{*}_{\lambda}
as sums of geometric sequences:

B_{\lambda}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(1+\ldots+q^{\lambda_{j}-1})t^{j-1}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-q^{\lambda_{j}}}{1-q}t^{j-1}=\frac{1}{(1-q)(1-t)}-\frac{1}{1-q}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}q^{\lambda_{j}}t^{j-1}

and similarly

B^{*}_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{(1-q^{-1})(1-t^{-1})}-\frac{1}{1-q^{-1}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}q^{-\lambda_{j}}t^{1-j}.

Note that
x=q^{\lambda_{i}}t^{i-1}
. Therefore

\displaystyle-x^{-1}+(1-q)(1-t)B_{\lambda}x^{-1}+1

\displaystyle=-(1-t)\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}q^{\lambda_{j}}t^{j-1}x^{-1}+1

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j\neq i}q^{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}}(t^{j-i}-t^{j-i+1})-(1-t)+1

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j\neq i}q^{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}}(t^{j-i}-t^{j-i+1})+t.

and

c(\lambda;x)=-(1-t)\prod_{j\neq i}\frac{(1-q^{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}}t^{j-i+1})}{(1-q^{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}}t^{j-i})}.

For the first equation it suffices to notice that
\mu_{i}=\lambda_{i}+1
and
\mu_{j}=\lambda_{j}
for
j\neq i
. For the second equation, we write

\displaystyle-(1-q)(1-t)B^{*}_{\lambda}x

\displaystyle=-qt-(1-t)q\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}}t^{i-j}

\displaystyle=-q-\sum_{j\neq i}q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}+1}(t^{i-j}-t^{i-j+1}).

∎

Corollary 2.6
.

We have

e_{m}I_{\lambda}=-\frac{1}{(1-q)(1-t)}\sum_{\mu=\lambda\cup x}c(\lambda;x)x^{m}I_{\mu},\quad f_{m}I_{\mu}=\sum_{\lambda=\mu-x}c^{*}(\mu;x)x^{m}I_{\lambda}

Lemma 2.7
.

Let

(2.6)

d_{\lambda}=\left(\prod_{\square\in\lambda}\square\right)\cdot\Lambda\big(-B_{\lambda}^{*}+(1-q)(1-t)B_{\lambda}B^{*}_{\lambda}\big).

Then for
\mu=\lambda\cup x
we have

\frac{c(\lambda;x)}{c^{*}(\mu;x)}=-\frac{(1-q)(1-t)d_{\mu}}{(1-qt)d_{\lambda}}.

Proof.

Indeed,
B_{\mu}=B_{\lambda}+x,B^{*}_{\mu}=B^{*}_{\lambda}+x^{-1}
, so

\displaystyle\frac{d_{\mu}}{d_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=x\Lambda\big(-x^{-1}+(1-q)(1-t)B_{\lambda}x^{-1}+(1-q)(1-t)B^{*}_{\lambda}x+(1-q)(1-t)\big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(1-qt)c(\lambda;x)}{(1-q)(1-t)c^{*}(\mu;x)}.

∎

Lemma 2.8
.

We have the power series identities

\exp\left[-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbf{z}^{-m}}{m}\mathbf{w}^{m}(1-q^{m})(1-t^{m})(1-(qt)^{-m})\right]=-\frac{g(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})}{g(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w})}

and

\exp\left[-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbf{z}^{m}}{m}\mathbf{w}^{-m}(1-q^{m})(1-t^{m})(1-(qt)^{-m})\right]=-\frac{g(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w})}{g(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})}.

Proof.

We prove the first equation, the second follows after swapping
\mathbf{z}
and
\mathbf{w}
. Note that

(1-q^{m})(1-t^{m})(1-(qt)^{-m})=-(q^{m}+t^{m}+(qt)^{-m}-q^{-m}-t^{-m}-(qt)^{m}),

so

\displaystyle\exp\left[-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbf{z}^{-m}}{m}\mathbf{w}^{m}(1-q^{m})(1-t^{m})(1-(qt)^{-m})\right]

\displaystyle=\exp\left[\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbf{z}^{-m}}{m}\mathbf{w}^{m}(q^{m}+t^{m}+(qt)^{-m}-q^{-m}-t^{-m}-(qt)^{m})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-q^{-1}\mathbf{w}\mathbf{z}^{-1})(1-t^{-1}\mathbf{w}\mathbf{z}^{-1})(1-(qt)\mathbf{w}\mathbf{z}^{-1})}{(1-q\mathbf{w}\mathbf{z}^{-1})(1-t\mathbf{w}\mathbf{z}^{-1})(1-(qt)^{-1}\mathbf{w}\mathbf{z}^{-1})}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(\mathbf{w}-q\mathbf{z})(\mathbf{w}-t\mathbf{z})(\mathbf{w}-(qt)^{-1}\mathbf{z})}{(\mathbf{z}-q\mathbf{w}\mathbf{z})(\mathbf{z}-t\mathbf{w}\mathbf{z})(\mathbf{z}-(qt)^{-1}\mathbf{w})}=\frac{g(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})}{g(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w})}.

∎

Corollary 2.9

Equation (

) can equivalently be written as follows:

2.3.
Monodromy Conditions

For later use, we examine the so-called monodromy conditions, introduced in
[
15
]
, on the coefficients
c(\lambda;x)
and
c^{\ast}(\mu;x)
from (
2.5a
) and (
2.5b
), respectively. Consider the Young poset
P
. A
system of coefficients

\{c(\lambda;x)\}
on
P
is an assignment of a value
c(\lambda;x)\in\mathbb{C}(q,t)^{\times}
for every covering relation
\lambda\prec\lambda\cup x
on
P
. We say that the system of coefficients
\{c(\lambda;x)\}
satisfies the
monodromy conditions
if, whenever we have chains
\lambda\prec\lambda\cup x\prec\lambda\cup x\cup y
and
\lambda\prec\lambda\cup y\prec\lambda\cup y\cup x
in
P
we have (cf.
[
15
, Lemma 3.24]
):

(2.7)

\frac{c(\lambda;x)c(\lambda\cup x;y)}{c(\lambda;y)c(\lambda\cup y;x)}=-\frac{(x-ty)(x-qy)(y-qtx)}{(y-tx)(y-qx)(x-qty)}.

Lemma 2.10

(
[
15
]
, Theorem 3.32)
.

The system of coefficients
c(\lambda;x)
defined by (
2.5a
) satisfies the monodromy conditions.

Now let us deal with the opposite Young poset
P^{\mathrm{opp}}
. A system of coefficients
\{c^{\ast}(\mu;x)\}
is an assignment of a value
c^{\ast}(\mu;x)
for every covering relation
\mu\prec\mu-x
on
P^{\mathrm{opp}}
. We say that
\{c^{\ast}(\mu;x)\}
satisfies the
dual monodromy conditions
if, whenever we have chains
\mu\prec\mu-x\prec\mu-x-y
and
\mu\prec\mu-y\prec\mu-y-x
the following equation is satisfied:

(2.8)

\frac{c^{\ast}(\mu;x)c^{\ast}(\mu-x;y)}{c^{\ast}(\mu,y)c^{\ast}(\mu-y;x)}=-\frac{(y-tx)(y-qx)(x-qty)}{(x-ty)(x-qy)(y-qtx)}.

Lemma 2.11
.

The system of coefficients
c^{\ast}(\lambda)
defined by (
2.5b
) satisfies the dual monodromy conditions.

Proof.

We use Lemmas
2.7
and
2.10
. First, note that by Lemma
2.7
we can write

c^{\ast}(\mu;x)=-\frac{(1-qt)d_{\mu-x}}{(1-q)(1-t)d_{\mu}}c(\mu-x;x).

So that we have

\frac{c^{\ast}(\mu;x)c^{\ast}(\mu-x;y)}{c^{\ast}(\mu,y)c^{\ast}(\mu-y;x)}=\frac{\frac{d_{\mu-x}}{d_{\mu}}\frac{d_{\mu-x-y}}{d_{\mu-x}}c(\mu-x;x)c(\mu-x-y;y)}{\frac{d_{\mu-y}}{d_{\mu}}\frac{d_{\mu-x-y}}{d_{\mu-y}}c(\mu-y;y)c(\mu-x-y;x)}

and the result now follows since
\{c(\lambda;x)\}
satisfies the monodromy conditions.
∎

3.2.
An Anti-Involution on
\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}

Given the obvious symmetry between
\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}^{\geq}
and
\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}^{\leq}
it is natural to search for a map on
\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}
which exchanges these subalgebras. In this section, we define such an anti-involution
\Theta:\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}\to\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}
. This map is similar to the anti-linear anti-involution
\Theta:\mathbb{B}_{q,t}^{\mathrm{ext}}\to\mathbb{B}_{q,t}^{\mathrm{ext}}
previously defined in
[
15
, Section 7]
with some subtle differences, see Remark
3.11
.

Lemma 3.10
.

The following defines a
\mathbb{C}(q,t)
-linear anti-involution
\Theta:\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}\to\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}
:

\displaystyle\Theta(\mathbbm{1}_{k})=\mathbbm{1}_{-k}

\displaystyle\Theta(\mathbbm{1}_{k}T_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{k})=\mathbbm{1}_{-k}T^{-1}_{|k|-i}\mathbbm{1}_{-k}

\displaystyle\Theta(\mathbbm{1}_{k}z_{j}\mathbbm{1}_{k})=\mathbbm{1}_{-k}z_{|k|+1-j}\mathbbm{1}_{-k}

\displaystyle\Theta(\mathbbm{1}_{k+1}d_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{k})=\begin{cases}q^{k/2}\mathbbm{1}_{-k}d_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{-k-1}&k\geq 0\\
q^{(k+1)/2}\mathbbm{1}_{-k}d_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{-k-1}&k<0\end{cases}

\displaystyle\Theta(\mathbbm{1}_{k-1}d_{-}\mathbbm{1}_{k})=\begin{cases}q^{(k-1)/2}\mathbbm{1}_{-k}d_{-}\mathbbm{1}_{-k+1}&k>0\\
q^{k/2}\mathbbm{1}_{-k}d_{-}\mathbbm{1}_{-k+1}&k\leq 0\end{cases}

\displaystyle\Theta(\mathbbm{1}_{k}\Delta_{p_{m}}\mathbbm{1}_{k})=\mathbbm{1}_{-k}(\Delta_{p_{m}}+\sum_{i=1}^{|k|}z_{i}^{m})\mathbbm{1}_{-k}

\displaystyle\Theta(\mathbbm{1}_{k}\Delta^{\ast}_{p_{m}}\mathbbm{1}_{k})=\mathbbm{1}_{-k}(\Delta^{\ast}_{p_{m}}+\sum_{i=1}^{|k|}z_{i}^{-m})\mathbbm{1}_{-k}.

In particular,

(3.7)

\Theta(e_{k})=f_{k},\ \Theta(\psi_{m}^{\pm})=\psi_{m}^{\pm}.

Moreover,

\Theta(\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}^{\geq})=\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}^{\leq}
and vice versa.

3.3.

\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}^{\geq}

The polynomial representation of
\mathbb{B}_{q,t}

. The main theorem in
[

]
gives a convenient basis of

Consider a pair of Young diagrams

is a union of horizontal strips, and a standard Young tableau

\mu\setminus\lambda

of this box is given by

be the
(q,t)
-content for a box labeled by
|\mu\setminus\lambda|-i+1
in
T

T
by the pair
(\lambda,\underline{w})
, where
\underline{w}=(w_{|\mu\setminus\lambda|},\dots,w_{1})

\{I_{\lambda,\underline{w}}\}

When

On the basis

is given by:

(3.8a)

(3.8b)

(3.8c)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\square\in\lambda}\square^{-m}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}^{-m}\right)I_{\lambda,w},

(3.8d)

\displaystyle=\frac{(q-1)w_{i+1}}{w_{i}-w_{i+1}}I_{\lambda,w}+\frac{w_{i}-qw_{i+1}}{w_{i}-w_{i+1}}I_{\lambda,s_{i}w},

4.2.
Quadratic Relations

In this section we check the relations (
2.2a
).

Lemma 4.6
.

Suppose that
a(z_{1},z_{2})
is a symmetric Laurent polynomial in
z_{1},z_{2}
. Then

d_{-}^{2}(z_{1}-qz_{2})a(z_{1},z_{2})d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}=0.

Proof.

Recall that
a(z_{1},z_{2})
is central in the affine Hecke algebra
\mathrm{AH}_{2}
.
We have
q^{-1}z_{1}=T_{1}^{-1}z_{2}T_{1}^{-1}
, so

\displaystyle d_{-}^{2}(z_{1}-qz_{2})a(z_{1},z_{2})d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}

\displaystyle=qd_{-}^{2}T_{1}^{-1}z_{2}T_{1}^{-1}a(z_{1},z_{2})d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}-qd_{-}^{2}z_{2}a(z_{1},z_{2})d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}

\displaystyle=qd_{-}^{2}T_{1}^{-1}z_{2}a(z_{1},z_{2})T_{1}^{-1}d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}-qd_{-}^{2}z_{2}a(z_{1},z_{2})d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}.

Since
d_{-}^{2}T_{1}\mathbbm{1}_{2}=d_{-}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{2},T_{1}d_{+}^{2}=d_{+}^{2}
, we can delete both
T_{1}^{-1}
and the terms will cancel out.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

We have

e_{k+1}e_{m}-te_{k}e_{m+1}=d_{-}^{2}(q^{-1}z_{1}-tz_{2})z_{1}^{k}z_{2}^{m}d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}.

Proof.

Recall the relation (
3.1h
) which for
k=1
has the form

z_{1}(qd_{+}d_{-}-d_{-}d_{+})\mathbbm{1}_{1}=qt(d_{+}d_{-}-d_{-}d_{+})z_{1}\mathbbm{1}_{1}.

We can divide both sides by
q
and rewrite this as

(4.2)

q^{-1}z_{1}d_{-}d_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{1}-td_{-}d_{+}z_{1}\mathbbm{1}_{1}=z_{1}d_{+}d_{-}\mathbbm{1}_{1}-td_{+}d_{-}z_{1}\mathbbm{1}_{1}.

Now we multiply both sides by
d_{-}z_{1}^{k}
on the left and
z_{1}^{m}d_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
on the right:

q^{-1}d_{-}z_{1}^{k}z_{1}d_{-}d_{+}z_{1}^{m}d_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{0}-td_{-}z_{1}^{k}d_{-}d_{+}z_{1}z_{1}^{m}d_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{0}=d_{-}z_{1}^{k}z_{1}d_{+}d_{-}z_{1}^{m}d_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{0}-td_{-}z_{1}^{k}d_{+}d_{-}z_{1}z_{1}^{m}d_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{0}.

The right hand side equals
e_{k+1}e_{m}-te_{k}e_{m+1}
, while in the left hand side we can use the identities
z_{1}d_{-}=d_{-}z_{1},d_{+}z_{1}=z_{2}d_{+}
and obtain

q^{-1}d_{-}^{2}z_{1}^{k+1}z_{2}^{m}d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}-td_{-}^{2}z_{1}^{k}z_{2}^{m+1}d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}=d_{-}^{2}(q^{-1}z_{1}-tz_{2})z_{1}^{k}z_{2}^{m}d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}.

∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Relation (
2.2a
) holds in
\mathbb{B}_{q,t}
.

Proof.

We can unpack equation (
2.2a
) as follows:

(4.3)

e_{n+3}e_{m}-\sigma_{1}e_{n+2}e_{m+1}+\sigma_{2}e_{n+1}e_{m+2}-e_{n}e_{m+3}=\\
-e_{m+3}e_{n}+\sigma_{1}e_{m+2}e_{n+1}-\sigma_{2}e_{m+1}e_{n+2}+e_{m}e_{n+3}.

To prove it, we regroup the terms in the left hand side:

e_{n+3}e_{m}-\sigma_{1}e_{n+2}e_{m+1}+\sigma_{2}e_{n+1}e_{m+2}-e_{n}e_{m+3}=

(e_{n+3}e_{m}-te_{n+2}e_{m+1})-(q+(qt)^{-1})(e_{n+2}e_{m+1}-te_{n+1}e_{m+2})+t^{-1}(e_{n+1}e_{m+2}-te_{n}e_{m+3}).

By Lemma
4.7
this equals

d_{-}^{2}\left[z_{1}^{2}-(q+(qt)^{-1})z_{1}z_{2}+t^{-1}z_{2}^{2}\right](q^{-1}z_{1}-tz_{2})z_{1}^{n}z_{2}^{m}d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}=

td_{-}^{2}(z_{1}-qz_{2})(z_{1}-(qt)^{-1}z_{2})(z_{2}-(qt)^{-1}z_{1})z_{1}^{n}z_{2}^{m}d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}.

To get the right hand side of (
4.3
), we simply swap
m
and
n
and change sign.
Therefore to prove (
4.3
) we need to check that

d_{-}^{2}(z_{1}-qz_{2})(z_{1}-(qt)^{-1}z_{2})(z_{2}-(qt)^{-1}z_{1})z_{1}^{n}z_{2}^{m}d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}=

-d_{-}^{2}(z_{1}-qz_{2})(z_{1}-(qt)^{-1}z_{2})(z_{2}-(qt)^{-1}z_{1})z_{1}^{m}z_{2}^{n}d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}

or, equivalently,

d_{-}^{2}(z_{1}-qz_{2})(z_{1}-(qt)^{-1}z_{2})(z_{2}-(qt)^{-1}z_{1})(z_{1}^{n}z_{2}^{m}+z_{1}^{m}z_{2}^{n})d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}=0.

This follows from Lemma

since the polynomial

is symmetric in

.
∎

4.3.
Cubic Relations

In this section, we show that the cubic relation (
2.2h
) holds in
\mathbb{B}_{q,t}
. We start with the following lemma.

Lemma 4.9
.

For all
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
we can write

z_{1}^{m_{1}}z_{2}^{m_{2}}=(q^{-1}z_{1}-tz_{2})\alpha+(z_{1}-qz_{2})\beta

for some Laurent polynomial
\alpha
and some symmetric Laurent polynomial
\beta
.

Proof.

Dividing by a power of
(z_{1}z_{2})
reduces us either to the case
m_{1}\geq 0,m_{2}=0
or
m_{1}=0,m_{2}\geq 0
.

For
m_{1}=m_{2}=0
we have an identity

(q^{-1}z_{1}-tz_{2})(z_{1}^{-1}+tz_{2}^{-1})-q^{-1}t(z_{1}-qz_{2})(z_{1}^{-1}+z_{2}^{-1})=(t+q^{-1})(1-t),

so

(4.4)

1=\frac{1}{(t+q^{-1})(1-t)}\left[(q^{-1}z_{1}-tz_{2})(z_{1}^{-1}+tz_{2}^{-1})-q^{-1}t(z_{1}-qz_{2})(z_{1}^{-1}+z_{2}^{-1})\right]

For
m_{1}=1,m_{2}=0
we have an identity

z_{1}=\frac{1}{q^{-1}(1-t)}\left[(q^{-1}z_{1}-tz_{2})-q^{-1}t(z_{1}-qz_{2})\right],

and for
m_{1}=0,m_{2}=1
we get

z_{2}=\frac{1}{(1-t)}\left[(q^{-1}z_{1}-tz_{2})-q^{-1}(z_{1}-qz_{2})\right].

The result follows from induction by observing that

z_{1}^{m_{1}+1}=z_{1}^{m_{1}}(z_{1}+z_{2})-z_{1}^{m_{1}-1}(z_{1}z_{2}),\quad z_{2}^{m_{2}+1}=z_{2}^{m_{2}}(z_{1}+z_{2})-z_{2}^{m_{2}-1}(z_{1}z_{2}).

∎

Corollary 4.10
.

We have
d_{-}^{2}z_{1}^{m_{1}}z_{2}^{m_{2}}d_{+}^{2}\in\mathbb{E}_{q,t}^{>}
.

Proof.

We can use Lemma
4.9
and rewrite this as

d_{-}^{2}z_{1}^{m_{1}}z_{2}^{m_{2}}d_{+}^{2}=d_{-}^{2}\left[(q^{-1}z_{1}-tz_{2})\alpha+(z_{1}-qz_{2})\beta\right]d_{+}^{2}.

where
\alpha
is a Laurent polynomial and
\beta
is a symmetric Laurent polynomial.
By Lemma
4.7

d_{-}^{2}(q^{-1}z_{1}-tz_{2})\alpha d_{+}^{2}
is in
\mathbb{E}_{q,t}^{>}
while
by Lemma
4.6
we get

d_{-}^{2}(z_{1}-qz_{2})\beta d_{+}^{2}=0.

∎

Example 4.11
.

By Lemma
4.7
we get

e_{1}e_{-1}-te_{0}^{2}=d_{-}^{2}(q^{-1}z_{1}-tz_{2})z_{2}^{-1}d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0},

e_{0}^{2}-te_{-1}e_{1}=d_{-}^{2}(q^{-1}z_{1}-tz_{2})z_{1}^{-1}d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}.

and by (

\displaystyle d_{-}^{2}d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(t+q^{-1})(1-t)}d_{-}^{2}(q^{-1}z_{1}-tz_{2})(z_{1}^{-1}+tz_{2}^{-1})d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}

Lemma 4.12
.

We have the relation

d_{-}^{2}d_{+}^{2}d_{-}d_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{0}=d_{-}d_{+}d_{-}^{2}d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}

in

We simplify

in two different ways:

\displaystyle d_{-}^{2}\varphi d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}

\displaystyle=d_{-}^{2}(\varphi d_{+})d_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{0}=qd_{-}^{2}T_{1}^{-1}d_{+}\varphi d_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{0}=qd_{-}^{2}d_{+}\varphi d_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{q-1}(d_{-}^{2}d_{+}^{2}d_{-}d_{+}-d_{-}^{2}d_{+}d_{-}d_{+}^{2})\mathbbm{1}_{0}

\displaystyle d_{-}^{2}\varphi d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}

\displaystyle=d_{-}(d_{-}\varphi)d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}=qd_{-}\varphi d_{-}T_{1}^{-1}d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}=qd_{-}\varphi d_{-}d_{+}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{0}

Lemma 4.15

We have

[\Delta_{p_{m}},e_{k}]=e_{k+m},[\Delta^{*}_{p_{m}},e_{k}]=e_{k-m}

and

(4.6)

[\Delta_{p_{m}},f_{k}]=-f_{k+m},[\Delta^{*}_{p_{m}},f_{k}]=-f_{k-m}.

[\Delta_{p_{m}},d_{-}]=[\Delta_{p_{m}},z_{1}]=0
and

[\Delta_{p_{m}},e_{k}]=[\Delta_{p_{m}},d_{-}z_{1}^{k}d_{+}]=d_{-}z_{1}^{k}[\Delta_{p_{m}},d_{+}]=d_{-}z_{1}^{k}z_{1}^{m}d_{+}=e_{k+m}.

The proof for

is similar, and the relations (

Lemma 4.16
.

The relations (
2.2c
)
are satisfied in
\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}^{\geq}
.

Proof.

We introduce auxiliary formal series

X^{+}(\mathbf{z})=-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbf{z}^{-m}}{m}\Delta_{p_{m}}(1-q^{m})(1-t^{m})(1-(qt)^{-m})

and

X^{-}(\mathbf{z})=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbf{z}^{m}}{m}\Delta^{*}_{p_{m}}(1-q^{m})(1-t^{m})(1-(qt)^{-m}).

such that
\psi^{\pm}(\mathbf{z})=\left(-\frac{1-q^{-1}t^{-1}\mathbf{z}^{-1}}{1-\mathbf{z}^{-1}}\right)\exp[X^{\pm}(\mathbf{z})]
.
We claim that

(4.7)

[X^{+}(\mathbf{z}),e(\mathbf{w})]=-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbf{z}^{-m}}{m}\mathbf{w}^{m}(1-q^{m})(1-t^{m})(1-(qt)^{-m})e(\mathbf{w})

and

(4.8)

[X^{-}(\mathbf{z}),e(\mathbf{w})]=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbf{z}^{m}}{m}\mathbf{w}^{-m}(1-q^{m})(1-t^{m})(1-(qt)^{-m})e(\mathbf{w}).

Indeed, we have
[\Delta_{p_{m}},e_{k}]=e_{k+m}
by Lemma

which can be written as

[\Delta_{p_{m}},e(\mathbf{w})]=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}[\Delta_{p_{m}},e_{k}]\mathbf{w}^{-k}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{w}^{-k}e_{k+m}=\mathbf{w}^{m}e(\mathbf{w}).

Similarly,
[\Delta^{*}_{p_{m}},e(\mathbf{w})]=\mathbf{w}^{-m}e(\mathbf{w})

and the equations (
4.7
)-(
4.8
) follow.

The generating function
\psi^{+}(\mathbf{z})
is a power series in
\mathbf{z}^{-1}
, and
\psi^{-}(\mathbf{z})
is a power series in
\mathbf{z}
. Both power series are invertible, and we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}_{\psi^{+}(\mathbf{z})}e(\mathbf{w})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Ad}_{\exp(X^{+}(\mathbf{z}))}e(\mathbf{w})=\exp[\mathrm{ad}X^{+}(\mathbf{z})]e(\mathbf{w})

\displaystyle=\exp\left[-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbf{z}^{-m}}{m}\mathbf{w}^{m}(1-q^{m})(1-t^{m})(1-(qt)^{-m})\right]e(\mathbf{w})=-\frac{g(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})}{g(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w})}e(\mathbf{w})

by (

) and Lemma

.

Similarly, by (

) and swapping

and
\mathbf{w}
in Lemma

we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}_{\psi^{-}(\mathbf{z})}e(\mathbf{w})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Ad}_{\exp(X^{-}(\mathbf{z}))}e(\mathbf{w})=\exp[\mathrm{ad}X^{-}(\mathbf{z})]e(\mathbf{w})

\displaystyle=\exp\left[\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbf{z}^{m}}{m}\mathbf{w}^{-m}(1-q^{m})(1-t^{m})(1-(qt)^{-m})\right]e(\mathbf{w})=-\frac{g(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})}{g(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w})}e(\mathbf{w}).

∎

5.
The spherical subalgebra

We have seen that the algebra
\mathbb{E}_{q,t}^{>}\subseteq\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
(resp.
\mathbb{E}_{q,t}^{\geq}\subseteq\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}^{\geq}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
and
\mathbb{E}_{q,t}\subseteq\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
) is isomorphic to the positive half
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}^{>}
(resp. non-negative half
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}^{\geq}
and entire
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
) of the elliptic Hall algebra. In this section, we will show that, in fact, every element of
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
(resp.
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}^{\geq}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
and
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
) can be written as a polynomial in the elements
e_{k}
(resp.
\{e_{k},\psi^{\pm}_{m}\}
and
\{e_{k},f_{k},\psi^{\pm}_{m}\}
). The following is the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.1
.

We have the following equalities:

\mathbb{E}_{q,t}^{>}=\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0},\qquad\mathbb{E}_{q,t}^{\geq}=\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}^{\geq}\mathbbm{1}_{0},\qquad\mathbb{E}_{q,t}=\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}.

and consequently, by Corollary
4.5
, the following isomorphisms:

\mathcal{E}_{q,t}^{>}\cong\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0},\qquad\mathcal{E}_{q,t}^{\geq}\cong\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}^{\geq}\mathbbm{1}_{0},\qquad\mathcal{E}_{q,t}\cong\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}.

Remark 5.2
.

Thanks to Theorem
5.1
, we have functors

G^{>}:\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\operatorname{-mod}\to\mathcal{E}_{q,t}^{>}\operatorname{-mod},\quad G^{\geq}:\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}^{\geq}\operatorname{-mod}\to\mathcal{E}_{q,t}^{\geq}\operatorname{-mod},\quad G:\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}\operatorname{-mod}\to\mathcal{E}_{q,t}\operatorname{-mod},

with their respective left adjoints

F^{>}:\mathcal{E}_{q,t}^{>}\operatorname{-mod}\to\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\operatorname{-mod},\quad F^{\geq}:\mathcal{E}_{q,t}^{\geq}\operatorname{-mod}\to\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}^{\geq}\operatorname{-mod},\quad F:\mathcal{E}_{q,t}\operatorname{-mod}\to\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}\operatorname{-mod}.

We note, however, that none of the functors are equivalences: in
[
15
, Remark 4.2]
we constructed a nonzero representation of
\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}^{\geq}
that is annihilated by the element
\mathbbm{1}_{0}
. This representation can in turn be used to construct a nonzero
\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}
-representation that is annihilated by
\mathbbm{1}_{0}
.

Let us sketch the strategy to prove Theorem
5.1
. First, we observe that it is enough to show the equality
\mathbb{E}_{q,t}^{>}=\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
. Indeed, if this is true then applying the involution
\Theta
from Lemma
3.10
, we will have also that
\mathbb{E}_{q,t}^{<}=\mathbbm{1}_{0}(\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}^{<})\mathbbm{1}_{0}
. So
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
is generated by the elements
e_{k},f_{k}
and
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\Delta_{p_{m}}\mathbbm{1}_{0},\mathbbm{1}_{0}\Delta^{\ast}_{p_{m}}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
, which implies that
\mathbb{E}_{q,t}=\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
. In turn, by restricting to the non-negative part of these algebras, this implies that
\mathbb{E}_{q,t}^{\geq}=\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}^{\geq}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
.

The crux of the argument is then to show that every element in
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
can be written as a polynomial in
e_{k}
. First, in Section
5.1
we find a quite large generating set
\{Y_{m_{1},\dots,m_{n}}\mid n>0,m_{1},\dots,m_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}\}
for
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
. In Sections
5.2
and
5.3
we will show that the
Y
elements satisfy, among themselves and with the
e
-elements, the relations (49) and (50) in
[
17
]
. By
[
17
, Theorem 2.13]
, this implies that every
Y
-element is generated by the
e
-elements, and this will finish the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Remark 5.3
.

The elliptic Hall algebra
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
is equipped with a coproduct and an antipode
[
9
]
which make it a Hopf algebra. As a consequence, the category of
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
-modules is equipped with a monoidal structure. Both the coproduct and antipode preserve the non-negative part
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}^{\geq}
, so that in particular the category of
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}^{\geq}
-modules also admits a monoidal structure.

In
[
15
]
we have defined a “generically” monoidal structure on the category of calibrated representations of
\mathbb{B}_{q,t}^{\mathrm{ext}}
. This structure does not come from a coproduct on
\mathbb{B}_{q,t}^{\mathrm{ext}}
, as the product of two representations is usually larger than the tensor product of the underlying vector spaces:

V_{k}(E_{1}\times E_{2})=\bigoplus_{m+n=k}\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{AH}_{m}\otimes\mathrm{AH}_{n}}^{\mathrm{AH}_{m+n}}V_{m}(E_{1})\otimes V_{n}(E_{2}).

See
[
15
, Section 6]
for more details.
The definition of monoidal structure also depends on two rational functions
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
.

However,
V_{0}(E_{1}\times E_{2})=V_{0}(E_{1})\otimes V_{0}(E_{2})
, therefore for (calibrated) representations of
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}^{\mathrm{ext}}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
the monoidal structure does agree with the tensor product of the underlying vector spaces. One can check by comparing
[
9
, Lemma 4.11]
with
[
15
, Theorem 6.14]
that the monoidal structures for
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}^{\geq}
and
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}^{\mathrm{ext}}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
agree for an appropriate choice of functions
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
.

5.1.
Special Elements

In this section, we
find a (quite large) generating set for the algebra
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
. Recall that we have the element
\varphi=\frac{1}{q-1}[d_{+},d_{-}]
, that we may think of as an extra generator of
\mathbb{B}_{q,t}
.

Definition 5.4
.

We call an element of
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{k}

special
if it can be written as a product of
d_{-},\varphi
and
z_{i}
(but not
T_{i}
or
d_{+}
).

We call an element of
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{k}

factorizable
if it can be written as the product
XY
for
X\in\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
not a unit and
Y\in\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{k}
.

Note that the special elements are only defined for
k\geq 1
.
We also define the following elements:

(5.1)

A_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}:=\mathbbm{1}_{0}d_{-}(z_{1}^{m_{1}}\varphi\cdots\varphi z_{1}^{m_{n-1}}\varphi z_{1}^{m_{n}})\mathbbm{1}_{1}\in\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{1}

and

(5.2)

Y_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}:=\mathbbm{1}_{0}d_{-}(z_{1}^{m_{1}}\varphi\cdots\varphi z_{1}^{m_{n-1}}\varphi z_{1}^{m_{n}})d_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{0}=A_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}d_{+}\in\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}.

Here
m_{i}
are arbitrary integers, and
n\geq 1
. Note that
A_{0}=\mathbbm{1}_{0}d_{-}\mathbbm{1}_{1}
and
Y_{m}=e_{m}
. The following is also clear from the definitions.

Proposition 5.5
.

The elements
A_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}\in\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{1}
are special, and any special element of
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{1}
equals
A_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}
for some
m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We recall some useful relations for
\varphi
.

Lemma 5.6
.

a) For
1<i\leq k
we have
z_{i}\varphi=\varphi z_{i-1}
and

T_{i}\varphi=\varphi T_{i-1}
.

b) We have
\varphi^{2}T_{k-1}=T_{1}\varphi^{2}
.

Proof.

a) We have
z_{i}d_{+}d_{-}=d_{+}z_{i-1}d_{-}=d_{+}d_{-}z_{i-1}
and
z_{i}d_{-}d_{+}=d_{-}z_{i}d_{+}=d_{-}d_{+}z_{i-1}
. The second equation is proved similarly.

b) This is proved in
[
10
, Lemma 3.1.11]
.
∎

Recall that, at the vertex
k
of the quiver
Q_{\mathbb{Z}}
(see Section
3.1
), the algebra generated by
z_{1},\dots,z_{k}
and
T_{1},\dots,T_{k-1}
is the affine Hecke algebra
\mathrm{AH}_{k}
.
For
w\in S_{k}
with a reduced expression
w=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{\ell}}
we consider the corresponding element
T_{w}=T_{i_{1}}\cdots T_{i_{\ell}}\in\mathrm{AH}_{k}
. It is well known that
\mathrm{AH}_{k}
has a basis

(5.3)

T_{w}z_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots z_{k}^{a_{k}},\ w\in S_{k},\ a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}.

In particular, any expression
f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})T_{v}
can be re-expanded in the basis (
5.3
) which we refer to as “pushing
f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})
through
T_{v}
.”

Lemma 5.7
.

Suppose
k\geq 1
and
A\in\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{k}
is special. Then for any
M\in\mathrm{AH}_{k}
we can write the product
AM
as a linear combination of special elements.

Proof.

If
k=1
then
M
is a polynomial in
z_{1}
and there is nothing to prove. So we will assume
k\geq 2
.

First, we make several easy reductions. If
A
ends with a monomial in
z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}
then we can absorb it into
M
, so we can assume that
A
ends with
d_{-}
or
\varphi
.

Without loss of generality we can assume
M=T_{w}z_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots z_{k}^{a_{k}}
as in (
5.3
).
Then
AM=AT_{w}z_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots z_{k}^{a_{k}}
and it is sufficient to consider the case
a_{1}=\ldots=a_{k}=0
.
To sum up, it is sufficient to prove the statement for
M=T_{i},1\leq i\leq k-1
.

We prove this by induction in the total number of
d_{-}
and
\varphi
in
A
. The base of induction is
A=d_{-}
where
k=1
and there is nothing to prove.

For the inductive step, we have the following cases:

Case 1:

A=A^{\prime}d_{-}
and
i<k-1
. Then

AT_{i}=A^{\prime}d_{-}T_{i}=A^{\prime}T_{i}d_{-}
and the assumption of induction applies to
A^{\prime}T_{i}
.

Case 2:

A=A^{\prime}\varphi
and
i<k-1
. Then

AT_{i}=A^{\prime}\varphi T_{i}=A^{\prime}T_{i+1}\varphi
by Lemma
5.6

and the assumption of induction applies to
A^{\prime}T_{i+1}
.

Case 3:

A=A^{\prime}d_{-}f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k-1})d_{-}
and
i=k-1
. First, we can get rid of
f
by writing

AT_{k-1}=A^{\prime}d_{-}^{2}f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k-1})T_{k-1},

pushing
f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k-1})
through
T_{k-1}
and arguing as above.
Now

A^{\prime}d_{-}^{2}T_{k-1}=A^{\prime}d_{-}^{2}.

Case 4:

A=A^{\prime}\varphi f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k-1})d_{-}
and
i=k-1
. Similarly to Case 3 we can assume
f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k-1})=1
. Now

A^{\prime}\varphi d_{-}T_{k-1}=qA^{\prime}\varphi d_{-}T_{k-1}^{-1}+(1-q)A^{\prime}\varphi d_{-}=A^{\prime}d_{-}\varphi+(1-q)A^{\prime}\varphi d_{-}.

Case 5:

A=A^{\prime}d_{-}f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})\varphi
and
i=k-1
. We can again eliminate
f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})
using the relations
d_{-}z_{1}=z_{1}d_{-}
and
z_{j}\varphi=\varphi z_{j-1}
for
j>1
. Now

A^{\prime}d_{-}\varphi T_{k-1}=qA^{\prime}\varphi d_{-}.

Case 6:

A=A^{\prime}\varphi f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})\varphi
and
i=k-1
. We can again eliminate
f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})
similalry to Case 5. Now

A^{\prime}\varphi^{2}T_{k-1}=A^{\prime}T_{1}\varphi^{2}

by Lemma
5.6
(b) and the assumption of induction applies to
A^{\prime}T_{1}
.
∎

Lemma 5.8
.

Suppose
k\geq 2
and
A\in\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{k}
is special. Then we can write the product
Ad_{+}
as a linear combination of special and factorizable elements.

Proof.

Again, we prove it by induction in the total number of
d_{-}
and
\varphi
in
A
. We have the following cases:

Case 1:

A=A^{\prime}d_{-}f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}).
Then

Ad_{+}=A^{\prime}d_{-}f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})d_{+}

and we can eliminate
f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})
by using the relations
d_{-}z_{1}=z_{1}d_{-}
and
z_{j}d_{+}=d_{+}z_{j-1}
for
j>1
.
Now

A^{\prime}d_{-}d_{+}=A^{\prime}d_{+}d_{-}-(q-1)A^{\prime}\varphi,

where
A^{\prime}\in\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{k-1}
.
If
k>2
, we can apply the assumption of induction to
A^{\prime}d_{+}
. If
k=2
then
A^{\prime}\in\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{1}
and
A^{\prime}d_{+}\in\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
, so
A^{\prime}d_{+}d_{-}
is factorizable.

Case 2:

A=A^{\prime}\varphi f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}).
Then

Ad_{+}=A^{\prime}\varphi f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})d_{+}

and we can eliminate
f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})
by using the relations
\varphi z_{1}=z_{2}\varphi
and
z_{j}d_{+}=d_{+}z_{j-1}
for
j>1
. Now

A^{\prime}\varphi d_{+}=qA^{\prime}T_{1}^{-1}d_{+}\varphi.

By Lemma
5.7
we can write
A^{\prime}T_{1}^{-1}=\sum A^{\prime\prime}_{j}
where
A^{\prime\prime}_{j}
are special, and then apply
the assumption of induction to
A^{\prime\prime}_{j}d_{+}
.
∎

Theorem 5.9
.

a) For
k>0
any element of
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{k}
can be written as a linear combination of special and factorizable elements.

b) For
k=0
any element of
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
can be written as a linear combination of
Y_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}
and factorizable elements.

c) The subalgebra
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
is generated by the elements
Y_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}
.

Proof.

Part (c) is a consequence of (b). We prove parts (a) and (b) for any monomial in
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}
by induction in the total number of
d_{+},d_{-}
and
\varphi
. The base case is
\mathbbm{1}_{0}d_{-}
which is clearly special.

For the step of induction, consider
A\in\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{k}
which satisfies the conditions of the theorem. If
k=0
then
A
can be only followed by
d_{-}
, and
Ad_{-}
is factorizable. From now we assume that
k\geq 1
and
A
is special.

Clearly,
Ad_{-}
and
A\varphi
are special. If
M\in\mathrm{AH}_{k}
then by Lemma
5.7
we can write
AM
as a linear combination of special elements. If
k\geq 2
by Lemma
5.8
we can write
Ad_{+}
as a linear combination of special and factorizable elements.

Finally, if
k=1
and
A\in\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{1}
is special, then
A=A_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}
for some
m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then

Ad_{+}=A_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}d_{+}=Y_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}.

∎

5.2.
Relations for
Y_{\underline{m}}

In this section we prove that
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
is generated by
e_{k}
. By Theorem
5.9
(c) it is sufficient to prove that for all
m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}
the element
Y_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}
can be written as a polynomial in
e_{k}
.
For this, we need some relations for
Y_{\underline{m}}
.

Theorem 5.10
.

The following relations hold in
\mathbb{B}_{q,t}
:

(5.4)

Y_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{i},m_{i+1},\ldots,m_{n}}-qtY_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{i}-1,m_{i+1}+1,\ldots,m_{n}}=-Y_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{i}}Y_{m_{i+1},\ldots,m_{n}},

(5.5)

[e_{k},Y_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}]=(t-1)(q-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\begin{cases}\sum_{a=1}^{k-m_{i}}Y_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{i-1},k-a,m_{i}+a,m_{i+1},\ldots,m_{n}}&\text{if}\ k>m_{i}\\
0&\text{if}\ k=m_{i}\\
-\sum_{a=1}^{m_{i}-k}Y_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{i-1},m_{i}-a,k+a,m_{i+1},\ldots,m_{n}}&\text{if}\ k<m_{i}.\end{cases}

We prove Theorem
5.10
below in Section
5.3
.

Example 5.11
.

For
n=2
we have

\displaystyle Y_{m_{1},m_{2}}

\displaystyle=d_{-}z_{1}^{m_{1}}\varphi z_{1}^{m_{2}}d_{+}=\frac{1}{q-1}d_{-}z_{1}^{m_{1}}d_{+}d_{-}z_{1}^{m_{2}}d_{+}-\frac{1}{q-1}d_{-}z_{1}^{m_{1}}d_{-}d_{+}z_{1}^{m_{2}}d_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q-1}e_{m_{1}}e_{m_{2}}-\frac{1}{q-1}d_{-}^{2}z_{1}^{m_{1}}z_{2}^{m_{2}}d_{+}^{2}.

Remark 5.12
.

We can compare the relations (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) with earlier computations:

•

The relation (
5.4
) implies

Y_{m_{1},m_{2}}-qtY_{m_{1}-1,m_{2}+1}+e_{m_{1}}e_{m_{2}}=0

which is equivalent to Lemma
4.7
.

•

By (

) we get

[e_{1},e_{-1}]=(q-1)(t-1)(Y_{0,0}+Y_{-1,1})

[e_{1},e_{-1}]-q^{-1}t^{-1}(q-1)(t-1)e_{0}^{2}=(1+q^{-1}t^{-1})(q-1)(t-1)Y_{0,0}

which is equivalent to (
4.11
).

•

Finally, by (

.

We have
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}=\mathbb{E}_{q,t}^{>}
.

Proof.

By definition we have
\mathbb{E}_{q,t}^{>}\subset\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}

By Theorem

On the other hand, by

.
∎

Remark 5.14
.

By

Corollary 5.15
.

We have
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}^{-}\mathbbm{1}_{0}=\mathbb{E}_{q,t}^{<}
and
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}=\mathbb{E}_{q,t}
.

Proof.

By applying the involution

we conclude that
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{B}_{q,t}^{-}\mathbbm{1}_{0}=\mathbb{E}_{q,t}^{<}
.

By definition, we have
\mathbb{E}_{q,t}\subset\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
. Any monomial in
\mathbbm{1}_{0}\mathbb{DB}_{q,t}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
can be written as a product of monomials in

.
∎

Remark 5.16

Proof of Theorem
5.10

We will use the notation

h_{m-1}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{z_{1}^{m}-z_{2}^{m}}{z_{1}-z_{2}},\ m\in\mathbb{Z}.

Note that
h_{m-1}(z_{1},z_{2})
is symmetric in
z_{1},z_{2}
and we have

h_{m-1}(z_{1},z_{2})=\begin{cases}z_{1}^{m-1}+\ldots+z_{2}^{m-1}&\mathrm{if}\ m>0\\
0&\mathrm{if}\ m=0\\
-(z_{1}^{m}z_{2}^{-1}+\ldots+z_{1}^{-1}z_{2}^{m})=-(z_{1}z_{2})^{m}h_{-m-1}(z_{1},z_{2})&\mathrm{if}\ m<0.\\
\end{cases}

Also, we have a recursion

(5.6)

h_{m-1}(z_{1},z_{2})=z_{2}^{m-1}+z_{1}h_{m-2}(z_{1},z_{2}).

Lemma 5.17
.

We have

(5.7)

z_{1}^{a}T_{1}^{-1}=T_{1}^{-1}z_{2}^{a}-q^{-1}(1-q)h_{a-1}(z_{1},z_{2})z_{1}

and

(5.8)

z_{2}^{a}T_{1}^{-1}=T_{1}^{-1}z_{1}^{a}+q^{-1}(1-q)h_{a-1}(z_{1},z_{2})z_{1}.

Proof.

We have

z_{1}=qT_{1}^{-1}z_{2}T_{1}^{-1}
, so

z_{2}T_{1}^{-1}=q^{-1}T_{1}z_{1}=q^{-1}(1-q)z_{1}+T_{1}^{-1}z_{1}

where we used the quadratic relation

T_{1}=(1-q)+qT_{1}^{-1}.

If
a=0
then (
5.8
) is clear. For
a\neq 0
we prove it by induction by observing

z_{2}^{a}T_{1}^{-1}=z_{2}^{a-1}(q^{-1}(1-q)z_{1}+T_{1}^{-1}z_{1})=q^{-1}(1-q)z_{2}^{a-1}z_{1}+(z_{2}^{a-1}T_{1}^{-1})z_{1}

and using (
5.6
).
Finally, (
5.7
) follows from (
5.8
) since

(z_{1}^{a}+z_{2}^{a})T_{1}^{-1}=T_{1}^{-1}(z_{1}^{a}+z_{2}^{a}).

∎

Lemma 5.18
.

We have

\varphi d_{-}T_{1}^{-1}z_{1}^{k}d_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{1}=d_{-}T_{1}^{-1}z_{1}^{k}d_{+}\varphi\mathbbm{1}_{1}+q^{-1}(1-q)d_{-}h_{k-1}(z_{1},z_{2})z_{1}d_{+}\varphi\mathbbm{1}_{1}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\varphi d_{-}T_{1}^{-1}z_{1}^{k}d_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{1}

\displaystyle=q^{-1}d_{-}\varphi z_{1}^{k}d_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{1}=q^{-1}d_{-}z_{2}^{k}\varphi d_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{1}=d_{-}z_{2}^{k}T_{1}^{-1}d_{+}\varphi\mathbbm{1}_{1}

\displaystyle=d_{-}T_{1}^{-1}z_{1}^{k}d_{+}\varphi\mathbbm{1}_{1}+q^{-1}(1-q)d_{-}h_{k-1}(z_{1},z_{2})z_{1}d_{+}\varphi\mathbbm{1}_{1}.

Here we apply the first relation in (
3.1f
), then Lemma
5.6
(a), followed by the second relation in (
3.1f
), and finally Lemma
5.17
.
∎

Corollary 5.19
.

We have

z_{1}^{m}\varphi\left[d_{-}T_{1}^{-1}z_{1}^{k}d_{+}\right]\mathbbm{1}_{1}=\left[d_{-}T_{1}^{-1}z_{1}^{k}d_{+}\right]z_{1}^{m}\varphi\mathbbm{1}_{1}-q^{-1}(1-q)d_{-}(z_{1}z_{2})^{k}h_{m-k-1}(z_{1},z_{2})z_{1}d_{+}\varphi\mathbbm{1}_{1}.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.18
we get

z_{1}^{m}\varphi d_{-}T_{1}^{-1}z_{1}^{a}d_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{1}=z_{1}^{m}d_{-}T_{1}^{-1}z_{1}^{k}d_{+}\varphi\mathbbm{1}_{1}+q^{-1}(1-q)z_{1}^{m}d_{-}h_{k-1}(z_{1},z_{2})z_{1}d_{+}\varphi\mathbbm{1}_{1}.

We can rewrite the terms as

\displaystyle z_{1}^{m}d_{-}T_{1}^{-1}z_{1}^{k}d_{+}\varphi\mathbbm{1}_{1}

\displaystyle=d_{-}z_{1}^{m}T_{1}^{-1}z_{1}^{k}d_{+}\varphi\mathbbm{1}_{1}

\displaystyle=d_{-}T_{1}^{-1}z_{2}^{m}z_{1}^{k}d_{+}\varphi\mathbbm{1}_{1}-q^{-1}(1-q)d_{-}h_{m-1}(z_{1},z_{2})z_{1}^{k}d_{+}\varphi\mathbbm{1}_{1}

\displaystyle=d_{-}T_{1}^{-1}z_{1}^{k}d_{+}z_{1}^{m}\varphi\mathbbm{1}_{1}-q^{-1}(1-q)d_{-}h_{m-1}(z_{1},z_{2})z_{1}^{k}d_{+}\varphi\mathbbm{1}_{1}

and

q^{-1}(1-q)z_{1}^{m}d_{-}h_{k-1}(z_{1},z_{2})z_{1}d_{+}\varphi\mathbbm{1}_{1}=q^{-1}(1-q)d_{-}z_{1}^{m}h_{k-1}(z_{1},z_{2})z_{1}d_{+}\varphi\mathbbm{1}_{1}.

The result now follows from the identity

z_{1}^{m}h_{k-1}(z_{1},z_{2})-z_{1}^{k}h_{m-1}(z_{1},z_{2})=-(z_{1}z_{2})^{k}h_{m-k-1}(z_{1},z_{2}).

∎

Lemma 5.20
.

We have

(5.9)

A_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{i},0}-qtA_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{i}-1,1}=-A_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{i}}d_{+}d_{-}.

Proof.

Note that

A_{m_{1},\dots,m_{i},0}-qtA_{m_{1},\dots,m_{i}-1,1}=d_{-}z_{1}^{m_{1}}\varphi\cdots z_{1}^{m_{i-1}}\varphi z_{1}^{m_{i}-1}(z_{1}\varphi-qt\varphi z_{1})\mathbbm{1}_{1}.

Now the equation (
3.1h
) can be rewritten as
z_{1}\varphi\mathbbm{1}_{1}+z_{1}d_{+}d_{-}\mathbbm{1}_{1}=qt\varphi z_{1}\mathbbm{1}_{1}
, or equivalently
(z_{1}\varphi-qt\varphi z_{1})\mathbbm{1}_{1}=-z_{1}d_{+}d_{-}\mathbbm{1}_{1}
, and the result follows.
∎

Lemma 5.21
.

We have

(5.10)

A_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{i},0}\left[d_{-}(z_{1}z_{2})^{k}h_{m_{i}-k-1}(z_{1},z_{2})z_{1}d_{+}\right]\mathbbm{1}_{1}=\\
qA_{m_{1},\ldots,k,m_{i}}-qA_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{i},k}+\begin{cases}q(t-1)\sum_{a=1}^{m_{i}-k}A_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{i}-a,k+a}&\mathrm{if}\ m_{i}>k\\
0&\mathrm{if}\ m_{i}=k\\
-q(t-1)\sum_{a=1}^{k-m_{i}}A_{m_{1},\ldots,k-a,m_{i}+a}&\mathrm{if}\ m_{i}<k.\end{cases}

Proof.

We divide in the three cases appearing in the statement of the lemma.

Case 1:

m_{i}>k
. In this case

(z_{1}z_{2})^{k}h_{m_{i}-k-1}(z_{1},z_{2})z_{1}=(z_{1}z_{2})^{k}\left(\sum_{a=0}^{m_{i}-k-1}z_{1}^{m_{i}-k-1-a}z_{2}^{a}\right)z_{1}=\sum_{a=0}^{m_{i}-k-1}z_{1}^{m_{i}-a}z_{2}^{k+a},

hence

\displaystyle d_{-}(z_{1}z_{2})^{k}h_{m_{i}-k-1}(z_{1},z_{2})z_{1}d_{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=0}^{m_{i}-k-1}d_{-}z_{1}^{m_{i}-a}z_{2}^{k+a}d_{+}=\sum_{a=0}^{m_{i}-k-1}z_{1}^{m_{i}-a}d_{-}d_{+}z_{1}^{k+a}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=0}^{m_{i}-k-1}z_{1}^{m_{i}-a}d_{+}d_{-}z_{1}^{k+a}-(q-1)\sum_{a=0}^{m_{i}-k-1}z_{1}^{m_{i}-a}\varphi z_{1}^{k+a}.

Therefore the left hand side of (
5.10
) equals

\sum_{a=0}^{m_{i}-k-1}A_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{i-1},m_{i}-a}d_{+}d_{-}z_{1}^{k+a}-(q-1)\sum_{a=0}^{m_{i}-k-1}A_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{i-1},m_{i}-a,k+a}.

By (
5.9
) we can rewrite it as

\sum_{a=0}^{m_{i}-k-1}\left[-qA_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{i}-a,k+a}+qtA_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{i}-a-1,k+a+1}\right]=

qA_{m_{1},\ldots,k,m_{i}}-qA_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{i},k}+q(t-1)\sum_{a=1}^{m_{i}-k}A_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{i}-a,k+a}.

Case 2:
m_{i}=k
.
In this case
h_{m_{i}-k-1}=0
and there is nothing to prove.

Case 3:
m_{i}<k
.
In this case

(z_{1}z_{2})^{k}h_{m_{i}-k-1}(z_{1},z_{2})z_{1}=-(z_{1}z_{2})^{m_{i}}h_{k-m_{i}-1}(z_{1},z_{2})z_{1}=-\sum_{a=0}^{k-m_{i}-1}z_{1}^{k-a}z_{2}^{m_{i}+a},

and similarly to Case 1 the left hand side of (
5.10
) equals

-\sum_{a=0}^{k-m_{i}-1}A_{m_{1},\ldots,k-a}d_{+}d_{-}z_{1}^{m_{i}+a}+(q-1)\sum_{a=0}^{k-m_{i}-1}A_{m_{1},\ldots,k-a,m_{i}+a}=

-\sum_{a=0}^{k-m_{i}-1}\left[-qA_{m_{1},\ldots,k-a,m_{i}+a}+qtA_{m_{1},\ldots,k-a-1,m_{i}+a+1}\right]=

-qA_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{i},k}+qA_{m_{1},\ldots,k,m_{i}}-q(t-1)\sum_{a=1}^{k-m_{i}}A_{m_{1},\ldots,k-a,m_{i}+a}.

∎

Multiplying both sides of (
5.10
) by
\varphi z_{1}^{m_{i+1}}\cdots\varphi z_{1}^{m_{n}}d_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{0}
on the right, we obtain the following result.

Lemma 5.23
.

Proof of Theorem

.

\displaystyle=(q-1)Y_{k,m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}-(q-1)Y_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n},k}+q^{-1}(1-q)\sum_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}.

Variational toolbox-based separability detection of multiqubit states
Jin-Min Liang, Shao-Ming Fei, Qiongyi He

Abstract
Parametrized quantum circuits (PQCs) are crucial in variational quantum algorithms. While it is commonly believed that the optimal PQC is solely used to reproduce the target state, we here reveal that the optimal PQC can also provide valuable insights into the state’s properties. We propose variational toolboxes to identify the k k -separability of pure states, with or without preparation noise, by checking the structure within the optimal PQCs. Additionally, we introduce adaptive optimization strategies to detect the k k -separability of mixed states. Compared to fixed PQCs, our approach controls fewer parameters for low-rank states. Finally, we validate our methods through numerical demonstrations for various states.



I
Introduction

Entanglement is one of the important resources in quantum-based applications, such as quantum communication
Hu et al. 2021
;
Bulla et al. 2023
, quantum network
Pompili et al. 2021
, and quantum metrology
Colombo et al. 2022
;
Long et al. 2022
. Understanding the entanglement property of a state enables us to manipulate quantum states in practical situations efficiently. One of the essential issues is the detection of entanglement for pure or mixed states, which confirms whether a target state is entangled. Another crucial problem is determining the entanglement structure of an entangled state
Gühne and Tóth 2009
;
Shahandeh et al. 2014
;
Lu et al. 2018
. In principle, multiparticle quantum states may be entangled as a whole system, called genuine multipartite entanglement (GME), or entangled only involving different subsystems. The entanglement structure of a quantum state is mathematically described by the
k
-separability or
m
-producibility
Sørensen and Mølmer 2001
. Both questions are challenging because of exponentially larger systems. Thus, exploring efficient methods to identify the presence of entanglement and the detailed structure is a crucial theoretical and experimental problem.

Last several decades witness the development of detecting entanglement, including the positive partial transposition (PPT) criterion
Horodecki 1997
, the computable cross norm criterion
Chen and Wu 2003
;
Rudolph 2003
, permutation criterion
Horodecki et al. 2006
;
Liu et al. 2022
, and machine learning methods
Girardin et al. 2022
;
Chen et al. 2023a
. These methods highly depend on the prior information of the state or the labeled data set to train the classical neural network. As the system size increases, quantum state tomography
Cramer et al. 2010
and the determination of the training state become infeasible. On the other hand, extracting the partial information of quantum states may also characterize the entanglement by employing randomized measurements
Elben et al. 2020
;
Neven et al. 2021
;
Yu et al. 2021
;
Ketterer et al. 2022
;
Cieśliński et al. 2024
.

The advent of quantum techniques provides potential opportunities to characterize the quantum correlation properties of multiqubit states prepared on noisy intermediate-scale quantum devices (NISQ)
Preskill 2018
. Well-known quantum techniques include variational quantum algorithms (VQAs), parameterized quantum circuits (PQC), the SWAP and Hadamard test, etc.
Cerezo et al. 2021a
;
Bharti et al. 2022
. These techniques have been adopted as useful tools for quantum simulation
Cervera-Lierta et al. 2021
, machine learning
Chen et al. 2021
, and the characterization of quantum entanglement
Bravo-Prieto et al. 2020
;
Beckey et al. 2021
;
Muñoz Moller et al. 2022
;
Wang et al. 2022
;
Chen et al. 2023b
;
Liu et al. 2023
;
Consiglio et al. 2022
;
Philip et al. 2024
.

Current VQAs focus on constructing PQCs and learning an optimal PQC to encode specific states by a hybrid quantum-classical optimization process
Cerezo et al. 2021a
;
Bharti et al. 2022
. Following the training stage, the properties of the encoded state can be extracted using post-processing methods
Huang et al. 2020
. Traditionally, state preparation and analysis are treated as separate components, with little consideration for interconnection. Since the target state is prepared by implementing the optimal PQC on an initial state, the optimal PQC inherently encapsulates all information about the target state. However, limited research has delved into the relationship between the optimal PQC and the extraction of information from the reproduced state.

Focusing on one of the important properties, the separability of multiqubit states
Nagata et al. 2002
;
Dür and Cirac 2000
;
Seevinck and Uffink 2008
, recent work only considered the relation between the fully separable states and the structure of the optimal unitary
Consiglio et al. 2022
. On the one hand, multiqubit states exhibit sophisticated entanglement structures, such as partial separability (
k
-separable) which has been identified as a resource in quantum metrology
Gessner et al. 2018
;
Ren et al. 2021
. On the other hand, when preparing a multiqubit pure state, noise is unavoidable. Simply applying known methods to experimentally prepared states could lead to incorrect results regarding separability. Finally, the number of parameters within the PQC increases exponentially with the number of qubits for mixed states, which is extremely challenging for a larger number of qubits. These issues motivate us to develop efficient strategies to verify the separability of quantum states.

In this work, we address the above issues and utilize the framework of VQAs to explore the connection between the structure of the optimal PQC and the separability of a multiqubit state. First, we construct two different PQC pools, and each PQC in the pools can be used to reconstruct a target state. Next, we propose a method to detect the separability of pure multiqubit states and generalize the methods to the noise situation by introducing virtual distillation
Huggins et al. 2021
;
Koczor 2021
. Moreover, we propose two adaptive quantum algorithms for detecting the separability of mixed states. The parameterized quantum state is a probability ensemble of parameterized quantum pure states generated via the PQC from the pools. Finally, several numerical results are presented to verify our approaches. The contributions of our results are twofold. On the one hand, we can not only detect the
k
-separability of a state but also confirm the entanglement structure between qubits. On the other hand, our approach is more efficient for low-rank mixed states.

(3)

III
Multiqubit pure state separability detection

In this section, we focus on the problem of determining the separability of multiqubit pure states. In subsection
III.1
, we propose methods for identifying the separability of ideal pure states that are prepared perfectly. However, in practical situations, noise in the NISQ device leads to the production of mixed states instead of pure states. In subsection
III.2
, we consider a noise situation in which the obtained state is a noisy mixed state.

III.1
Ideal multiqubit pure state

The first step to detect the separability of an
n
-qubit pure state
|\psi\rangle
is a variational quantum state reconstruction (VQSR), where the goal is to reconstruct the quantum state
|\psi\rangle
using a VQA.

The PQC are chosen from two PQC pools,
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
corresponding to Eqs.(
7
and
8
). We optimize the cost function

\displaystyle F(\bm{\Phi})=1-\sqrt{{\mathrm{tr}}[O_{G}U^{{\dagger}}(\bm{\Phi})|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|U(\bm{\Phi})]},

(9)

where
O_{G}=|0^{\otimes n}\rangle\langle 0^{\otimes n}|
. Eq.(
9
) can be expressed in terms of the fidelity between the learned state
|\psi\rangle
and the target state
U(\bm{\Phi})|0^{\otimes n}\rangle
,

\displaystyle F(\bm{\Phi})=1-\sqrt{|\langle 0^{\otimes n}|U^{{\dagger}}(\bm{\Phi})|\psi\rangle|^{2}}.

(10)

When
F(\bm{\Phi}^{*})=0
for some parameters
\bm{\Phi}^{*}
, then
|\psi\rangle=U(\bm{\Phi}^{*})|0^{\otimes n}\rangle
, where
\bm{\Phi}^{*}
represents the optimal parameters. During the optimization process, the cost function
F(\bm{\Phi})
and its gradients can be evaluated on a near-term quantum computer using a shallow-depth circuit and a single copy of the state
|\psi\rangle

Ekert et al. 2002
;
Quek et al. 2024
. The Hoeffding bound
Hoeffding 1994
suggests that the measurements
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2}\log\delta^{-1})
are sufficient to evaluate
F(\bm{\Phi})
with an additive error of
\varepsilon
and a success probability of at least
1-\delta

Buhrman et al. 2001
.

The training process involves selecting different PQCs from sets
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
as the unitary
U(\bm{\Phi})
. We iterate through
l=1,2
and use VQSR to find the global minimum of
F(\bm{\Phi})
by choosing a PQC from
\mathcal{P}_{l}
. The training procedure is terminated if the function value is below a threshold
\epsilon
(
0<\epsilon\ll 1
). If the minimum value does not meet the termination condition for any unitary in sets
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
, then our approach is unable to determine the separability of
|\psi\rangle
.

Once the optimal parameters are found, we proceed to the post-processing step. Using obtained optimal parameters and corresponding structure of parameterized quantum circuit to infer the separability of the pure state
|\phi\rangle
. The analysis is divided into two cases.

Case 1:
If the optimal PQC comes from
\mathcal{P}_{1}
, it indicates that the state
|\psi\rangle
is fully separable. This is clear because the PQC in
\mathcal{P}_{1}
is a local unitary that does not create any entanglement.

Case 2:
If the optimal PQC comes from
\mathcal{P}_{2}
, we need to examine whether the two-qubit interaction unitary
Q_{j,k}(\bm{\theta}^{*})
creates entanglement when applying it to a two-qubit separable state. We evaluate the entanglement property by calculating the purity
{\mathrm{tr}}(\varrho_{j}^{2})
of the reduced density matrix
\varrho_{j}={\mathrm{tr}}_{\{1,\cdots,n\}/j}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)
. Fig.
1
shows an example of
4
-qubit example. If the purity is not one, it indicates that entanglement is formed between the qubits
(j,k)
. In this case, an edge is added between the qubit pair
(j,k)
. Conversely, if the purity is equal to one, it implies that the qubits
(j,k)
remain separable, and no edge is added between them.

Figure 1:
A
4
-qubit example. (a) The circuit for calculating the purity
{\mathrm{tr}}(\varrho_{1}^{2})
. (b) The circuit for calculating the purity
{\mathrm{tr}}(\varrho_{3}^{2})
.

After examining all the two-qubit interaction unitaries, we construct a graph
G=(\mathrm{V},\mathrm{E})
with
n
vertices
\mathrm{V}=\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
representing individual qubits, and edges
\mathrm{E}=\{\mathrm{E}_{ij}\}
for different
(i,j)
, where
\mathrm{E}_{ij}
denotes that the vertices (qubit pair)
(i,j)
are connected. Based on the graph, we conclude that an
n
-qubit pure state is
k
-separable if the graph
G
can be partitioned into at most
k
disconnected subgraphs.

Two remarks are following. On the one hand, the defined PQCs in our framework have only one entangled layer. This feature implies our approach is only suitable for states that can be prepared by the defined PQCs. In particular, the two-qubit interaction between
n
separable qubits generates at most
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
-separable states, where
\lceil\cdot\rceil
is the ceil function. For more general states involving three or more qubit interactions, our approach fails to detect the separability. Although adding more entangled layers can enhance the expressibility of PQC, we fail to infer the separability from the learned PQC. On the other hand, if one is interested in the separability of a
n
-qubit pure state under a specific bipartition such as
A|B
, we can tail the PQC as
U=U_{A}\otimes U_{B}
and then use our framework to detect its separability, where
U_{A},U_{B}
are Hardware-efficient ansatz commonly used in VQA
Kandala et al. 2017
;
Tang et al. 2021
.

III.2
Noisy multiqubit pure state

Suppose we can detect the separability of the ideal
n
-qubit pure state
|\psi\rangle
by the approach in subsection
III.1
. However, one experimentally obtains a noisy mixed state under some noise channel
\mathcal{E}

Nielsen and Chuang 2010
,

\displaystyle\varrho_{\mathrm{noise}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)

(11)

\displaystyle=(1-q)|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|+q\sum_{i}E_{i}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|E_{i}^{{\dagger}},

(12)

with
\sum_{i}E_{i}^{{\dagger}}E_{i}=\mathds{1}_{2^{n}}
. In this scenario, inferring the
k
-separability of
|\psi\rangle
from obtained noisy states
\varrho_{\mathrm{noise}}
is a challenge. If we directly utilize ordinary detection methods for the mixed state
Ketterer et al. 2022
, we may obtain a wrong conclusion. Next, we show that it is possible to infer the separability of
|\psi\rangle
from the noisy mixed state.

Assume that the preparation error can be mitigated using the purification-based quantum error mitigation method
Koczor 2021
;
Huggins et al. 2021
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to+\infty}\frac{\varrho_{\mathrm{noise}}^{m}}{{\mathrm{tr}}[\varrho_{\mathrm{noise}}^{m}]}=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|

(13)

where
m
is an integer
Endo et al. 2021
;
Cai et al. 2023
. To detect the
k
-separability of
|\psi\rangle
, a naive approach is to prepare the state
\frac{\varrho_{\mathrm{noise}}^{m}}{{\mathrm{tr}}[\varrho_{\mathrm{noise}}^{m}]}
and perform ordinary detection approaches
Ketterer et al. 2022
. However, the preparation of
\frac{\varrho_{\mathrm{noise}}^{m}}{{\mathrm{tr}}[\varrho_{\mathrm{noise}}^{m}]}
is a difficult problem
Tan and Volkoff 2021
. Following the approach for the ideal multiqubit pure state, we only need to define a new cost function

\displaystyle F(\bm{\Phi})

\displaystyle=1-\sqrt{\frac{{\mathrm{tr}}[O_{G}\tilde{\varrho}^{m}]}{{\mathrm{tr}}[\varrho_{\mathrm{noise}}^{m}]}},

(14)

for some integer
m
and the state
\tilde{\varrho}=U^{{\dagger}}(\bm{\Phi})\varrho_{\mathrm{noise}}U(\bm{\Phi})
. The numerator and denominator can be estimated using shallow depth quantum circuits
Quek et al. 2024
or classical shadow techniques
Huang et al. 2020
;
Zhou and Liu 2024
.

Consider the global depolarization noise model
Nielsen and Chuang 2010
,
\varrho_{\mathrm{noise}}=(1-q)|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|+q\frac{\mathds{1}_{2^{n}}}{2^{n}}
,
q\in[0,1]
. Our approach can detect the separability of
|\psi\rangle
for any noise strength
q
since there always exists an integer
m
such that
\frac{\varrho_{\mathrm{noise}}^{m}}{{\mathrm{tr}}[\varrho_{\mathrm{noise}}^{m}]}\approx|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
based on the power iteration
Golub and Van Loan 2013
. For instance, the
10
-qubit mixed state,

\displaystyle\varrho_{g}(q)

\displaystyle=(1-q)|\mbox{Bell}^{\otimes 5}\rangle\langle\mbox{Bell}^{\otimes 5}|+\frac{q}{2^{10}}\mathds{1}_{2^{10}},

(15)

where the noise strength
q\in[0,1]
and each Bell state is
|\mbox{Bell}\rangle=(|01\rangle+|10\rangle)/\sqrt{2}
. It is clear that the source state
|\mbox{Bell}^{\otimes 5}\rangle\langle\mbox{Bell}^{\otimes 5}|
is
5
separable state. To detect the entanglement of the state
|\mbox{Bell}^{\otimes 5}\rangle
from the noisy mixed state
\varrho_{g}(q)
, we use
m
copies of
\varrho_{g}(q)
and learn the state
\frac{\varrho_{\mathrm{noise}}^{m}}{{\mathrm{tr}}[\varrho_{\mathrm{noise}}^{m}]}
by the cost function (
14
). Fig.(
3
a) shows the infidelity

\displaystyle f(q)=\left|1-\frac{\langle\mbox{Bell}^{\otimes 5}|\varrho_{g}^{m}(q)|\mbox{Bell}^{\otimes 5}\rangle}{{\mathrm{tr}}[\varrho_{g}^{m}(q)]}\right|,

(16)

as a function of
m
. We find that it is possible to learn the entanglement of the state
|\mbox{Bell}^{\otimes 5}\rangle
by using
m\leq 5
copies of the mixed state
\varrho_{g}(q)
for
q=0.2,0.4,0.6,0.8
. The greater the noise strength, the more copies of the noisy state are required.

IV
Mixed state separability detection with adaptive optimization

Generalizing the pure state to a mixed
n
-qubit state
\varrho
is feasible by finding a convex roof extension
Bennett et al. 1996
;
Uhlmann 2010
. Solving the full separability of an
n
-qubit mixed state needs to construct a fully separable parameterized mixed state
Consiglio et al. 2022
,

\displaystyle\sigma=\sum_{m=1}^{M}q_{m}\sigma_{m},

(17)

where
\sum_{m=1}^{M}q_{m}=1
and each pure state
\sigma_{m}
is a fully separable state. If there is a state
\sigma
satisfying the Hilbert-Schmidt distance

\displaystyle F=\|\varrho-\sigma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}\leq\epsilon,

(18)

for a small threshold
\epsilon
, we confirm that
\varrho
is fully separable. The number of parameters to generate the decomposition (Eq.
17
) is
M(1+2n)
and the calculation of
F
requires
\mathcal{O}(M^{2})
items. According to Carathéodory’s theorem
Horodecki 1997
;
Vedral and Plenio 1998
,
M\leq 4^{n}
. One common approach sets
M=4^{n}
to detect any state
\varrho
. This fixed parameterization strategy naturally suffers from the exponentially larger classical parameters. It would be challenging for a large number of qubits. However, if the target state
\varrho
is a low-rank state such as
R(\varrho)=\Omega(n)
, then
M=\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
suffices to learning the state
\varrho
, where
R(\varrho)
denotes the randk of
\varrho
. In this scenario, the fixed parameterization method would be inefficient.

Here, we propose an adaptive method to detect whether a mixed state is fully separable. The algorithm is summarized as Algorithm 1.

Algorithm 1
: Adaptive method for fully separability detection of a mixed state.

Given a target mixed state
\varrho
and a threshold
\epsilon
, perform the following steps for
S=1,2,\cdots
.

Step 1
. Prepare an
n
-qubit fully separable state

\displaystyle\sigma(\bm{\Phi})=\sum_{m=1}^{S}q_{m}\sigma_{m}(\bm{\alpha}_{m})

(19)

with
S(2n+1)
parameters
\bm{\Phi}=\{(q_{m},\bm{\alpha}_{m})\}_{m=1}^{S}
.

Step 2
. Minimize the cost function

\displaystyle F_{S}(\bm{\Phi})=\|\sigma(\bm{\Phi})-\varrho\|_{\rm{HS}}^{2},

(20)

by the classical-quantum feedback loop and find optimal parameters
\bm{\Phi}^{*}
.

Step 3
. If the value of the function
F_{S}(\bm{\Phi}^{*})
is lower than the threshold
\epsilon
, we terminate the loop and conclude that
\varrho
is an
n
-separable state. Otherwise, we obtain nothing about the entanglement of
\varrho
.

Based on Eq.(
19
), the number of parameters of Algorithm 1 for each optimization is
S(1+2n)
. As a comparison, the number of parameters of the fixed parameterization method
Consiglio et al. 2022
is
4^{n}(1+2n)
. Thus, Algorithm 1 is efficient for states with rank
R(\varrho)=\Omega(n)
which ensures
S=1,\cdots,\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
.

Following the adaptive optimization approach of Algorithm 1, we propose a mixed state
k
-separability detection summarized as Algorithm 2.

Algorithm 2
: Adaptive method for
k
-separability detection of a mixed state.

Given a target mixed state
\varrho
and a threshold
\epsilon
, perform the following steps for
S=1,2,\cdots
.

Step 1
. Prepare an
n
-qubit mixed state

\displaystyle\sigma(\bm{\Phi}_{1})=\sum_{i=1}^{S}\sum_{l_{i}=1}^{L}q_{l_{i}}\sigma_{l_{i}},

(21)

with classical probability parameters
q=\{q_{l_{1}},\cdots,q_{l_{S}}\}
and each pure state

, where

\displaystyle|\sigma_{l}\rangle=V_{l}(\bm{\alpha}_{l}^{1},\bm{\theta}_{l}^{1})W(\bm{\alpha}_{l}^{0})|0^{\otimes n}\rangle

(22)

for
l=1,\cdots,L
and
|\sigma_{0}\rangle=W(\bm{\alpha})|0^{\otimes n}\rangle
.

Step 2
. Minimize the cost function

\displaystyle\hat{F}_{S}(\bm{\Phi}_{1})=\|\sigma(\bm{\Phi}_{1})-\varrho\|_{\rm{HS}}^{2},

(23)

by the classical-quantum feedback loop and find optimal parameters
\bm{\Phi}_{1}^{*}
.

Step 3
. If the value of the function
\hat{F}_{S}(\bm{\Phi}_{1}^{*})
is lower than the threshold
\epsilon
, we terminate the loop and output a convex-roof decomposition of
\varrho
,

\displaystyle\sigma(\bm{\Phi}^{*})=\sum_{m}q_{m}^{*}\sigma_{m}^{*}.

(24)

Step 4
. Use the post-processing step in subsection
III.1
to detect the separability of each pure state
\sigma_{m}^{*}
in Eq.(
24
). Suppose
\sigma_{m}^{*}
is
k_{m}
-separable. Then
\varrho
is
k
-separable, where
k=\min_{m}k_{m}
.

Here, all PQCs in
\mathcal{P}_{2}
are used to generate a parameterized mixed state defined in Eq.(
21
). The number of parameters of Algorithm 2 is
\mathcal{O}(Sn^{2})
. Fig.
2

The mixed parameterized quantum states in both algorithms are constructed as a linear combination of parameterized pure states. Without loss of generality, we denote both states as a general form,

VI
Conclusion and outlook

In summary, we have established a link between PQCs and the separability of the state. If the target state can be learned by the PQCs in the constructed PQC pools, our approach can determine its separability by only checking the entanglement between two-qubit interaction unitary. In particular, the number of two-qubit interaction unitary required to check is at most
\lfloor n/2\rfloor
. In comparison, a previous approach
Beckey et al. 2021
required measuring
n(n-1)/2
qubit pairs to determine the separability of an
n
-qubit pure state. This reduction makes our approach more feasible for detecting the entanglement of pure states. Moreover, we address the separability detection of pure states even though its preparation has some unknown noise. This result provides possibilities to analyze the pure state entanglement from the obtained noisy mixed state. Finally, we have presented two adaptive algorithms to determine the separability of mixed states. Our strategies are efficient for low-rank states, such as
R(\varrho)=\Omega(n)
.

Our findings suggest several areas for future research. Firstly, our approach also encounters the barren plateaus
McClean et al. 2018
. It would be interesting to introduce other strategies into our algorithms to improve the efficiency of our algorithms, such as defining local cost functions
Ezzell et al. 2023
;
Cerezo et al. 2021b
. Secondly, there is potential for exploring the application of purification-based quantum error mitigation techniques in other methodologies for entanglement detection and the quantification of pure states
Koczor 2021
;
Huggins et al. 2021
could be explored in other approaches for entanglement detection and quantification of pure states
Chen et al. 2023b
;
Liu et al. 2023
. Finally, our adaptive methods are based on probability ensemble ansatz. As suggested in
Yin et al. 2022
, the purification ansatz can be generalized to solve the problem of fully separable mixed states with resource requirements scaling polynomially with the number of qubits.

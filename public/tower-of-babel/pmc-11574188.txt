Knowledge, attitude, and practice regarding stone formation and recurrence among urolithiasis patients: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Tieding Chen, Yan Jiang, Peng Zhang, Fang Wang, Bangbin Chen, Dianjun Yu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study investigated the knowledge, attitude, and practice (KAP) concerning stone formation and recurrence among patients with urolithiasis treated at Ningbo Medical Centre Lihuili Hospital between August 19, 2023, and April 19, 2024. A total of 394 patients were included, among which 70.30% were males. The median scores for knowledge, attitude, and practice were 7 (6-8.25), 29 (26.75-31), and 21.5 (18–25), respectively, indicating good knowledge and attitude but suboptimal practice. Positive correlations were found between knowledge and attitude (r = 0.274, P < 0.001) and between attitude and practice (r = 0.126, P < 0.05). Logistic regression revealed that knowledge, education, and whether the patient experienced a new or recurrent stone were independent predictors of attitude. Attitude, education, body mass index (BMI), and daily water intake were independent predictors of practice. Structural equation modeling (SEM) showed that knowledge, family history, and education directly influenced attitude, while education also significantly impacted practice. Introduction

Urolithiasis, medically recognized as a urinary stone disease, is a prevalent urological condition, distinguished by its high prevalence and propensity for recurrence1. According to epidemiological studies, approximately 10–15% of adults are predisposed to experience at least one episode of urinary stone formation throughout their lives2. The intricate pathogenesis of urolithiasis involves a complex interplay of genetic, metabolic, and environmental variables. Calcium stones are the most common, accounting for 70–80% of all stone types, followed by uric acid, phosphate, and cystine stones3.

Even though significant advances have been made in the diagnosis and treatment of urolithiasis in recent years, with minimally invasive surgery and extracorporeal shock wave lithotripsy becoming standard therapies4,5, recurrent stones continue to pose a significant challenge for many patients. Alarmingly, the recurrence rate stands at a staggering 50%, with approximately 10–25% of patients enduring a recurrence within five years of the initial episode1. This recurring condition intensifies the physical and psychological toll on patients and imposes a substantial financial burden on the healthcare system.

Patients’ knowledge, attitude, and practice toward urinary stone formation and recurrence are paramount in preventing and managing urolithiasis. Studies have demonstrated that patients who possess a comprehensive grasp of the etiological factors underlying stone formation and the associated risk of recurrence and who subsequently adopt effective preventive measures, such as adjusting their dietary patterns, enhancing fluid intake, and adhering to prescribed medications, can significantly reduce their risk of stone recurrence6–8. Unfortunately, the comprehension and execution of these preventative strategies are still insufficient in numerous patients, substantially hindering prevention efforts. Therefore, investigating patients’ knowledge, attitude, and practice toward stone formation and recurrence in urolithiasis is crucial for identifying cognitive and behavioral gaps, which can be used to develop targeted strategies to enhance patient management and reduce recurrence rates.

The KAP survey method is a comprehensively utilized structured assessment tool that employs a questionnaire-based survey to precisely measure the comprehension, stance, and practical application of specific topics among a particular group of individuals. This approach provides a comprehensive insight into individuals’ perspectives and behaviors towards a given subject matter, enabling a deeper understanding of their knowledge gaps, attitudinal inclinations, and behavioral patterns9–11. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the KAP towards stone formation and recurrence among patients with urolithiasis using questionnaires, in-depth interviews, and systematically collecting and analyzing relevant patient information. The findings of this study are hopeful of uncovering knowledge gaps and behavioral misconceptions in patients regarding stone recurrence prevention, paving the way for more effective health education, interventions, and clinical guidance to aid in recurrence prevention and improvement of quality of life.

Methods

Study design and participants

This cross-sectional study included patients with urolithiasis treated at Ningbo Medical Centre Lihuili Hospital, Zhejiang Province, between 19 August 2023 and 19 April 2024. The inclusion criteria were the following: (1) patients present at the hospital during the survey period; (2) patients diagnosed with urolithiasis. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) age < 18 years old; (2) with other severe underlying diseases, such as severe heart failure, cerebral infarction, cerebral hemorrhage, etc.; (3) illiterate patients unable to complete the questionnaire. The sampling method was convenience sampling.

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Ningbo Medical Centre Lihuili Hospital, and all participants provided informed consent.

Questionnaire distribution

Utilizing the expertise of Questionnaire Star, a professional online questionnaire software platform operated by Changsha Ranxing Information Technology Co., Ltd., we created and implemented a dedicated questionnaire link. Leveraging the Questionnaire Star mini-program integrated with WeChat, an online questionnaire was structured, and a QR code was generated for seamless data collection via the popular messaging application. Participants could then scan the QR code received via WeChat, effortlessly logging in and completing the questionnaire. Once all responses were collected, an Excel spreadsheet was exported directly from the Questionnaire Star platform. In order to ensure the utmost quality, research team members rigorously assessed the integrity, internal consistency, and rationality of each questionnaire.

Questionnaire introduction

The questionnaire was precisely designed based on previously published literature12,13. Following its initial conception, the questionnaire underwent revisions based on insightful suggestions from two esteemed experts specializing in urology and urolithiasis. A pilot study was then conducted, garnering 30 responses and achieving a commendable reliability score of 0.815, indicating good internal consistency.

The final questionnaire was in Chinese and consisted of four dimensions: demographic, knowledge, attitude, and practice. Specifically, there were 14 questions in the demographic dimension, 10 in the knowledge dimension, 7 in the attitude dimension, and 8 in the practice dimension. For each item in the knowledge dimension, 1 point was assigned for a correct answer and 0 points for an incorrect or unclear answer, resulting in a total score range of 0–10 points. The attitude dimension was evaluated on a five-point Likert scale, encompassing positive topics and open-ended inquiries. The response options spanned from (a) Strongly Agree, (b) Agree, (c) Neutral, (d) Disagree, to (e) Strongly Disagree, with a total score ranging from 7 to 35. The practice dimension was similarly evaluated on a five-point Likert scale, with the following options calibrated to capture varying frequencies of occurrence: (a) “Almost Never”, (b) “Rarely”, (c) “Sometimes”, (d) “Often”, and (e) “Always”. The cumulative score for this dimension ranged from 8 to 40, offering a quantitative assessment of participants’ practices.

Statistical analyses

SPSS 27.0 was used for statistical analysis. The overall Cronbach’s α coefficient and the respective coefficients were computed for the knowledge, attitude, and practice dimensions, along with the KMO coefficient of the variables12. A mean ± SD was employed for quantitative variables to describe if the data had a normal distribution; otherwise, the median, 25th percentile, and 75th percentile were utilized. The response data on different demographic characteristics were presented as N (%). The t-test was applied to compare continuous variables with normal distribution established based on the normality test. For variables that did not follow a normal distribution, the Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney test was used13. In cases where three or more groups of continuous variables exhibited normal distribution and equal variances, ANOVA was used, and for non-normally distributed continuous variables, the Kruskal-Wallis analysis of variance was employed13. The Pearson correlation coefficient was employed for correlation analysis when the data distribution adhered to normality, whereas the Spearman correlation coefficient was utilized for data with non-normal distribution14. In the regression analysis, where scores from each dimension served as dependent variables, the average was employed to classify normally distributed scores. Conversely, for non-normally distributed scores, the median score was utilized for each dimension. Variables with P-values < 0.1 and 0.25 in the univariate analysis were included in the multiple regression analysis. Path analysis was employed to explore the relationship between the total scores of KAP and baseline variables15. Notably, P-values in this analysis were rounded to three decimal places, and a P-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Demographic characteristics and KAP scores.

Among all the urolithiasis participants, 70.30% were male, the majority (43.40%) were between 31 and 45 years old, and had a university degree (37.56%). There were 53.81% of participants with a BMI within the normal range, while 36.55% were overweight. The predominant stone types were kidney and ureteral stones, comprising 68.28% and 57.11%, respectively. There were 37.06% new cases of urolithiasis and 62.94% recurrent cases. Over half of the individuals (50.05% precisely) consumed 500–1000 mL of water daily. Furthermore, a noteworthy 40.61% of patients had a familial predisposition to stone disease.

Knowledge, attitude, practice

As shown in Table 1, the median (p25-p75) scores for knowledge, attitude, and practice were 7 (6-8.25), 29 (26.75-31), and 21.5 (18–25), respectively. The potential scoring ranges for these respective components were 0–10 for knowledge, 7–35 for attitude, and 8–40 for practice. Generally, a significant disparity was observed in the scores of the knowledge section among individuals with different monthly household incomes (p < 0.01). Furthermore, results revealed notable disparities in attitude scores among participants of different ages (p < 0.001), educational level (p < 0.001), work intensity (p < 0.001), type of permanent residence (p < 0.001), and different monthly household income (p < 0.001). In addition, attitude scores significantly differed in relation to whether there was a history of stones among direct relatives (p < 0.05). Furthermore, factors such as educational level (p < 0.05) and total daily water intake (p < 0.001) may contribute to variations in scores related to practical aspects.

Specifically, in the knowledge dimension (Table S1), the highest score was observed for the item about the main clinical manifestations of urolithiasis (K7), reaching an accuracy of 91.12%. The lowest score was observed for the item about proper diet (K10), reaching an accuracy of 43.15% and indicating that most patients lacked clarity regarding the role of meat consumption in preventing infection-induced stones. Furthermore, patients showed a limited focus on the underlying etiology of stone formation, with only 58.12% knowing about risk factors (K2). Additionally, 52.28% were cognizant that bacterial infections can potentially trigger the development of urinary stones (K3).

In the attitude dimension (Table S2), the item that garnered the highest support rate pertained to the level of importance ascribed to stones, with 53.30% of respondents firmly perceiving urinary stones as a severe health concern that can disrupt daily functioning during symptomatic episodes (A3). In addition, 51.02% of the patients were strongly willing to alter their diet and enhance physical activity to prevent the development and recurrence of urinary stones (A2). However, only 7.11% and 12.94% of patients strongly agreed that stone recurrence could be influenced by genetic predispositions and particular medical disorders or health states, respectively (A6-7).

In the practice dimension (Table S3), the highest score was observed for the item about seeking medical attention promptly when experiencing symptoms such as lower back pain, frequent urination, and urgency, with only 19.80% of patients selecting “Always” as their answer (P1). While most patients were aware of the importance of drinking water, only 10.15% opted for “Always” when asked about increasing their water consumption as a preventive measure against the occurrence of stones (P5). Moreover, only 3.05%, 4.06%, 8.12%, and 5.58% “Always” changed their dietary habits (P4), took medication (P6), followed doctors’ advice (P7), and underwent regular check-ups (P8) to prevent the recurrence of stones, respectively. Only 6.09% of patients declared to “Always” pay attention to the composition of stones, and 1.52% of patients “Always” adhered to daily exercise.

Spearman correlation analysis

Spearman’s analysis was utilized to assess the correlation of scores between knowledge, attitude, and practice, as shown in Table 2. There were significantly positive correlations between knowledge, attitude, and attitude and practice; their correlation coefficients were 0.274 (P < 0.001) and 0.126 (P < 0.05), respectively.

Discussion

The current study examined the KAP towards stone formation and recurrence among patients with urolithiasis. Our results indicated that their practical performance fell short of expectations despite the patients’ overall satisfactory scores in the knowledge and attitude dimensions. Unfortunately, their knowledge and attitude did not directly or indirectly influence their practical application. Herein, we identified the key factors affecting patients’ level of compliance in practice, and taking targeted measures can help improve the management of patients.

Demographic characteristics of urolithiasis patients

The male predominance (70.30%) observed in our study highlighted the higher incidence of urolithiasis among males, which is consistent with a previous meta-analysis reporting the prevalence of KS of 10.9% (95% CI 8.6–13.5%) among males and 7.1% (95% CI 5.2–9.3%) among females, even though the gender gap in prevalence has been reported to narrow over time3.

KAP levels of urolithiasis patients

Concerning the knowledge dimension, the highest accuracy was observed in questions about the main clinical manifestations of the disease. However, we found a low accuracy rate in questions related to appropriate dietary practices, which is in line with a previous study conducted in Saudi Arabia, revealing a low level of knowledge among the general population about the condition and the methods to prevent urolithiasis, like dietary and lifestyle modifications16. These findings suggest a need for targeted education in this area.

An overwhelming attitude towards the importance of urolithiasis and its prevention was observed, with over half of the patients expressing a desire for preventive measures and written guidance from physicians. This finding aligns with a previous cross-sectional study, which revealed that 62% of patients expressed a need for additional medical or interventional management to prevent recurrent kidney stones17. This underscores the crucial role of enhancing patients’ knowledge about kidney stone prevention to improve their adherence and subsequent outcomes.

The practice scores were notably lower than knowledge and attitude scores, indicating a disparity between awareness and action. A previous study revealed that tailored dietary and pharmacological treatments are the preferred way to prevent the recurrence of urolithiasis18. However, in the present study, while most patients recognized the importance of hydration, only a small proportion reported consistently increasing their water intake. A retrospective study of 363 urolithiasis patients also showed that increasing fluid intake to achieve a urine volume of > 2.5 L/day reduced stone events, with a 50.1% compliance rate at 6 months19. Similarly, we found that adherence to recommended changes in diet, medication, and regular medical follow-up was low. This gap between knowledge and practice underscores the need for effective strategies to translate awareness into actionable changes.

The correlation between KAP levels

The positive correlation between knowledge and attitude and between attitude and practice suggested that enhancing patients’ understanding of urolithiasis and its prevention may positively influence their attitudes and subsequent behaviors. Ali et al. compared water intake compliance and stone recurrence rates between spiritually motivated and non-motivated patients with renal tract stones. They found that spiritually motivated patients had significantly better compliance with water intake guidelines and lower recurrence rates20. However, it is worth noting that despite the significant correlation, the relationship between knowledge and attitude (r = 0.274), as well as attitude and practice (0.126), was not very strong, suggesting that other factors such as individual motivation, social support, economic factors, access to healthcare, personal health beliefs, and cultural practices may all have an impact on attitude and practice21,22. Therefore, it is necessary to strengthen the demand for multifaceted interventions to increase knowledge and address these other factors. Customized education programs focused on improving practical skills and support systems that increase motivation and reduce barriers to mobility may yield better results.

The factors affecting KAP levels

Logistic regression identified education as a significant predictor of attitude and practice. SEM further revealed that education positively affected attitude and practice, indicating that improving patient education is critical to fostering better preventive behaviors. Kim et al. found that patients receiving urolithiasis treatment with lower education levels responded more negatively about lifestyle modifications for preventing urolithiasis recurrence23. Similarly, a previous cross-sectional study conducted in Chengdu, China, indicated insufficient knowledge and moderately positive attitudes and practices regarding urinary system stones among the general public, emphasizing the need for targeted educational interventions to promote awareness and positive behaviors for prevention and management24. However, family history negatively affected attitude, suggesting that patients with a genetic predisposition may feel less empowered to take preventive actions. These findings point to the need for personalized education that specifically addresses the beliefs and attitudes of those with a family history of urolithiasis.

In addition, our results indicated that recurrence status significantly influenced patients’ attitudes towards urolithiasis prevention. Recurrent patients may have encountered frustration or disappointment with previous attempts to prevent stone recurrence, resulting in a more pessimistic or resigned attitude16. Besides, the recurrent nature of the condition may have reduced their sense of control over the disease, fostering a less proactive stance towards prevention measures.

The practice dimension analysis underscored the importance of BMI and daily water intake in shaping patients’ preventive behaviors. Notably, both underweight and normal-weight patients were more likely to engage in preventive practices than obese individuals, suggesting that weight management has a crucial role in urolithiasis prevention. This finding aligns with medical literature, emphasizing the association between obesity and increased risk of urinary stone formation due to metabolic alterations and urinary chemistry imbalances25.

Moreover, daily water intake emerged as a significant predictor of preventive practices, with higher water consumption positively associated with preventive behaviors. Research has proven a significantly reduced risk of incident kidney stones among individuals with high fluid consumption26. However, Ningbo is located on the southeast coast of China, and its food culture has distinctive characteristics, mainly seafood, pickles, and high-salt, high-protein diets, which, combined with a potentially inadequate intake of water, can indeed have a negative impact on the formation of urinary stones27–29. These findings highlight the importance of addressing patients’ attitudes towards urolithiasis prevention, particularly among recurrent cases, and emphasize the need for tailored interventions incorporating weight management and hydration strategies.

Study limitations

First, while the total sample size of 394 participants provided valuable insights, it may not represent the entire urolithiasis patient population. Besides, our findings may not be fully generalizable to other regions or populations due to Ningbo’s specific cultural, socioeconomic, and dietary conditions. Larger and more diverse samples would likely yield more generalizable results. Second, the collected data relied heavily on self-reported information from participants, which may be subject to recall bias, misinterpretation, or deliberate misrepresentation. Objective measures or validation through medical records could strengthen the reliability of the data. Third, this was a cross-sectional study, limiting the ability to establish causal relationships between KAP and stone formation or recurrence. Longitudinal studies would provide more insights into how changes in KAP over time affect stone outcomes. Finally, while the study identified key predictors like education and family history, a deeper exploration of demographic variables (such as age, gender, and income) and external barriers (such as access to care and financial constraints) could strengthen the reported findings. Region-specific influences such as cultural or dietary practices may also be relevant and should be examined in future studies.

Conclusion

While patients with urolithiasis exhibited a generally satisfactory knowledge and positive attitude towards its prevention, their practical adherence to preventative measures remained suboptimal. Targeted educational interventions focusing on dietary modifications, hydration, and lifestyle changes are needed to bridge the gap between knowledge and action, ultimately improving the management and prevention of urolithiasis. Further research is needed to explore the feasibility and effectiveness of such interventions in real-world clinical contexts.

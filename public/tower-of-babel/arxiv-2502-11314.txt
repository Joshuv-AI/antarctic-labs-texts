Abstract
. We work in the smooth category throughout. Let

Remark 1.5
.

One may ask whether

. The answer is no.

Consider the once-punctured

, denoted by

. This is an

-dimensional
k
-handlebody obtained from

by attaching an

-handle along the unknotted

-sphere

and a

A\cup B=(S^{n-k-1}\times\{0\})\cup(\{0\}\times S^{k-1})\subset(S^{n-k-1}\times B^{k})\cup(B^{n-k}\times S^{k-1})=\partial(B^{n-k}\times B^{k})=\partial B^{n}=S^{n-1}.

For example, when

Now, observe the homology group

H_{n-k}(X)\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}^{2}&\hskip 14.22636pt\text{if}\;\;n=2k\\
\mathbb{Z}&\hskip 14.22636pt\text{if}\;\;n<2k.\end{cases}

Suppose that there exists an

-dimensional

-handlebody

such that

Let

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt(Y_{k-1}\times S^{n-k-1})\setminus\nu(\mathcal{S})

\displaystyle=(Y_{k-1}\times S^{n-k-1})\setminus(\nu(S({Y_{k-1}}))\times S^{n-k-1})\quad(\text{since}\;\;\mathcal{S}=S(Y_{k-1})\times S^{n-k-1}))

\displaystyle=(Y_{k-1}\setminus\nu(S({Y_{k-1}})))\times S^{n-k-1}\quad(\text{since}\;\;A\times B\setminus C\times B=(A\setminus C)\times B)

\displaystyle=\partial Y_{k-1}\times[0,1]\times S^{n-k-1}\quad(\text{since}\;\;Y_{k-1}\setminus\nu(S(Y_{k-1}))=\partial Y_{k-1}\times[0,1]).

Define the projection

by

, where

is identified with
\partial Y_{k-1}\times S^{n-k-1}

∎

.

Theorem 3.8
.

. Any

-Kirby diagram

-Kirby diagram

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{J}=J_{1}\cup J_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-k-2}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{k-2}
be an
(n,k)
-Kirby diagram. Since

is the trivial
(n-k-1)
-link, we can assume that

, where
\tilde{L_{1}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-k-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{k-2}
is the trivial
(n-k-1)
-link induced by the trivial
1
-link
L_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

We need to show that the
(k-1)
-link

is isotopic to a
(k-1)
-link
\tilde{L_{2}}
induced by some
1
-link
L_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus L_{1}
.

Consider the map

, where
\alpha
is a
1
-knot in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus L_{1}
. Here, any element of
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus L_{1})

. We note that

(k-1)
-knot
\tilde{\alpha}
,

is surjective. Let

Then for every
(k-1)
-knot
\beta_{i}
, there exists a
1
-knot

such that

. Let

is a
1
-link in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus L_{1}

in

(n-1)

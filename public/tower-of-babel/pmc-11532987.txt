Diphyllin elicits a doubled-pronged attack on the entry of SARS-CoV-2 by inhibiting cathepsin L and furin
Authors: Binli Mao, Vu Thuy Khanh Le-Trilling, Haihuan Tang, Jie Hu, Mona S Schmitz, Kimberly Barbet, Dan Xu, Zhen Wei, Beinu Guo, Denise Mennerich, Chun Yao, Jinxin Liu, Zhenghan Li, Yushun Wan, Xiaoyong Zhang, Kai Wang, Ni Tang, Zebo Yu, Mirko Trilling, Yong Lin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Diphyllin effectively inhibits pseudoviral and authentic SARS-CoV-2 infections.•Diphyllin has broad pan-variant antiviral activity against various SARS-CoV-2 variants of concerns.•Diphyllin inhibits the endocytosis of SARS-CoV-2.•Diphyllin blocks SARS-CoV-2 spike-meditated cell surface entry.Keywords: Diphyllin, SARS-CoV-2, Endocytosis, Cathepsin L, Cell surface entry, Furin

Introduction

Severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) which caused the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) was and in certain areas still is responsible for massive public health problems and economic losses. The global distribution of the initial founder SARS-CoV-2 was followed by several epidemic and pandemic waves of variants of concern (VOC) such as Alpha, Beta, Gamma, Delta, and Omicron (Zhou et al., 2020). With its exceptional transmissibility, Omicron and its sub-linages are currently the circulating SARS-CoV-2 strains (Yin et al., 2022; Zou et al., 2022). Although COVID-19 vaccines are safe and effective against the disease, they do not completely prevent SARS-CoV-2 infections in the population (Xue et al., 2023). This virus circulation results in severe infections that need to be treated, e.g., in immunocompromised individuals. Therefore, effective and affordable broad-spectrum antiviral drugs against SARS-CoV-2 are urgently needed.

SARS-CoV-2 is an enveloped single-stranded positive-sense RNA virus with a genome of approximately 30 kb, which encodes the four structural proteins spike (S), membrane (M), envelope (E), and nucleocapsid (N) (Dhama et al., 2020). During the SARS-CoV-2 infection, the viral S protein binds with the receptor-binding domain (RBD) of S to the angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2) on the surface of host cells (Hoffmann et al., 2020b). For activity, S needs to be cleaved by proprotein convertases such as furin at a multibasic site located at the S1-S2 junction, resulting in an N-terminal S1 subunit, which is responsible for ACE2 binding, and a membrane-anchored C-terminal S2 region that contains the fusion machinery comprising the highly conserved fusion peptide (Jackson et al., 2022). This furin cleavage typically occurs during maturation at the Golgi apparatus of virus-producer cells (Yamamoto et al., 2022). During the entry process, ACE2 engagement exposes the S2’ site leading to cleavage of the S2 subunit by proteases such as cathepsin L (CTSL) or transmembrane protease serine 2 (TMPRSS2) (Hoffmann et al., 2020b; Mao et al., 2022). TMPRSS2-mediated S protein activation occurs at the plasma membrane, whereas CTSL-mediated S cleavage occurs in endo-lysosomes following virion internalization via clathrin-mediated endocytosis. Thus, SARS-CoV-2 entry either proceeds through the cell surface route or the endosomal entry route, which differ regarding the involved host proteases.

Accumulating evidence from multiple studies including our own indicate that compounds that prevent endolysosomal acidification exert a broad-spectrum inhibitory effect on various viral infections (Li et al., 2023; Mao et al., 2022; Yang and Shen, 2020). For instance, chloroquine or hydroxychloroquine exert their antiviral effects by neutralizing endolysosomal acidification, affecting the viral entry through the endosomal route of viruses such as SARS-CoV-2, severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus (SARS-CoV), hepatitis C virus, chikungunya virus, and human immunodeficiency virus type 1 (HIV-1) (Khan et al., 2010; Mizui et al., 2010; Romanelli et al., 2004; Vincent et al., 2005). Our previous research revealed that obatoclax inhibits SARS-CoV-2 infection by blocking endosomal entry by inhibiting CTSL activity and viral cell surface entry through inhibiting furin activity by targeting MCL-1 (Mao et al., 2022). We wondered whether there might be other inhibitors that block SARS-CoV-2 entry - ideally both at the cell surface and in endosomal compartments.

Diphyllin, which is natural constituent of plant extracts of Haplophyllum alberti-regelii, H. bucharicum, and H. perforatum (Rutaceae), is a V-ATPase inhibitor that inhibits lysosomal acidification (Shen et al., 2011). Diphyllin inhibits the replication of a variety of viruses in vitro and in vivo including influenza virus, HIV-1, and Zika virus (ZIKV) (Chen et al., 2013; Martinez-Lopez et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2017). Two recent studies by Stefanik et al. and Tóth et al. reported that diphyllin elicited antiviral activity against SARS-CoV-2 in Vero cells and colorectal adenocarcinoma cells (Caco-2) (Stefanik et al., 2021; Toth et al., 2023). To date, however, the mechanism of antiviral activity is elusive.

In the present study, we identified diphyllin as an effective anti-SARS-CoV-2 compound that blocks both the endosomal as well as the cell surface route of virus entry. Furthermore, we provide ample evidence indicating that diphyllin acts antiviral by a double-pronged attack compromising the activity of the host proteases CTSL and furin.

Material and methods

Cell culture

Human A549, Calu-3, Caco-2, HEK293T, and HEK293T-hACE2 cells were cultured in Dulbecco's modified Eagle's medium (DMEM; Gibco, Rockville, USA) with 10% (v/v) fetal bovine serum, 1 × MEM non-essential amino acids solution, and 100 U/ml penicillin-streptomycin at 37 °C in 5% CO2 as described previously (Mao et al., 2022).

Plasmids, inhibitors, and other reagents

Plasmids expressing GFP-SARS-CoV-2-S and human ACE2 (hACE2) have been described previously (Hu et al., 2020). Chloroquine (C6628), diphyllin (SML1766), and 4ʹ,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI, D9542–5MG) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (Burlington, MA, USA). Chlorpromazine (HY-B0407A) was obtained from MedChemExpress (New Jersey, NJ, USA). DQ-BSA Red (D-12051) and LysoTracker Red (Thermo Fisher, L12492) were used as previously described (Lin et al., 2020).

SARS-CoV-2-S pseudovirus infection

SARS-CoV-2-S pseudotyped viruses were generated as previously described (Hu et al., 2020). Briefly, 5*106 HEK293T cells were co-transfected with recombinant SARS-CoV-2 S plasmid GFP-SARS-CoV-2-S and pNL4–3.Luc.R-E- using Lipofectamine 3000 transfection reagent (Invitrogen, Rockville, MD, USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions. The supernatant containing SARS-CoV-2-S pseudoviruses was collected at 48–72 h after transfection, then filtered through a 0.45 μM filter and stored at -80 °C. For SARS-CoV-2-S pseudovirus infections, cells were seeded into 96-well plates, followed by pre-treatment with indicated drug concentrations. After incubation for 2 h, cells were infected with 50 μl pseudo-typed reporter viruses for 24 h.

SARS-CoV-2 infection

SARS-CoV-2 was isolated from a patient sample and then amplified in Vero E6 cells in the BSL-3 laboratory of the University Hospital Essen (Essen, Germany) as previously described (Scholer et al., 2020). Briefly, virus was isolated from a nasopharyngeal swab sample obtained in May 2020 and analyzed by Next-Generation Sequencing (see EPI_ISL_19455282 at GISAID for the SARS-CoV-2 genome sequence). Preprocessing, quality control and assembly of the raw sequencing products were performed using UnCoVar (Thomas et al., 2024). Cells were pre-treated with diphyllin (1, 3.3 or 10 μM) for 1.5 h and subsequently infected with SARS-CoV-2 (MOI = 0.1). At 24 h post viral infection, the cell supernatant was collected and assessed by reverse-transcription quantitative (RT-q) PCR for quantifying progeny virus, and cell-associated virus was measured using in-cell ELISA (icELISA) for quantifying the SARS-CoV-2 N and S protein levels in the infected cells.

SARS-CoV-2 infection of human primary tracheal epithelial cells in air liquid interface culture

Primary tracheal epithelial cells (PTEC) were obtained from lung transplant donors (n=3) post mortem at the University Medical Center Essen, Germany, and has been approved by the ethics committee of the medical faculty of the University of Duisburg-Essen (19–8717-BO). Selection criteria for donors are listed in the Eurotransplant guidelines. Cells were isolated and cultivated following a well-established, verified, and commonly used method (Mertens et al., 2016). PTEC were quickly thawed and expanded in a T75 flask in keratinocyte-SF medium (KSFM, Gibco, Waltham, USA) supplemented with human epidermal growth factor (EGF, 2.5 µg, Gibco), bovine pituitary extract (BPE, 25 mg, Gibco), isoproterenol (1 µM, Sigma Aldrich), penicillin-streptomycin (10 U/ml, Gibco), amphotericin B (2.5 µg/ml, PAN-Biotech, Aidenbach, Germany) and ciprofloxacin (0.01 mg/ml, Fresenius Kabi, Bad Homburg, Germany) and cultivated at 37 °C and 5% CO2. Cells were then detached with soft trypsin (0.3 mg/ml trypsin, PAN-Biotech; 0.1 mg/ml EDTA, PAN-Biotech; 1 mg/ml glucose, VWR, Radnor, United States; 2 U/ml penicillin-streptomycin, Gibco) and seeded onto coated (5 µg/ml fibronectin, Sigma Aldrich; 0.03 mg/ml PureCol, Advances BioMatrix; 1 mg/ml BSA, Sigma Aldrich) 12-well cell culture inserts (12 mm diameter, 12-well plate size, 0.4 µm pore size, PET membrane transparent, VWR) with a cell density of 40,000 cells per well. When the transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER, Millicell ERS-2 Epithelial Volt-Ohm Meter, Merck, Darmstadt, Germany) value of 224 Ω/cm2 was reached, the apical media was removed and cells were exposed to air for four weeks. The media was changed three times a week and contained a 1:1-mixture of bronchial epithelial cell media (BEpiCM, ScienCell, Carlsbad, USA) and Dulbecco's modified eagles’ media (DMEM with 4500 mg/ml D-glucose, StemCell, Vancouver, Canada), supplemented with bronchial epithelial cell growth supplement (BEpiCGS, ScienCell), 10 U/ml penicillin-streptomycin, and 16 ng/ml EC-23 (Tocris, Bristol, United Kingdom).

For the infection with SARS-CoV-2, fully differentiated PTEC in air liquid interface culture were washed with Hanks’ salt solution (HSS) and pretreated apically and basolaterally for 1.5 h with indicated concentrations of diphyllin before the cells were infected apically with 50,000 PFU per well in the presence of diphyllin. The virus solution was removed after 1 h of incubation and the cells were washed 3 times with 250 µl HSS for 10 min. The third wash was collected and stored at -80 °C as 2 aliquots of 100 µl. These samples were analyzed as 0 dpi samples. At 1, 2, and 3 days post-infection, cells were incubated apically with 250 µl of HSS for 15 min before the solution was removed and stored at -80 °C as 2 aliquots of 100 µl. The samples were inactivated for 10 min at 70 °C and for 10 min at 95 °C before they were used for quantification of viral genomes. The samples were diluted 1:15 in HSS and SARS-CoV-2 genome copy numbers were determined by use of the diagnostic Alinity m SARS-CoV-2 Amp Kit (Abbott, Illinois, USA) according to the manufacturer´s instructions.

Cytotoxicity assay

Potential cytotoxic effects of diphyllin on various cells were assessed by the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8; MedChemExpress, HY-K0301) according to the manufacturer's instructions (Mao et al., 2022). Briefly, cells were seeded into 96-well plates and incubated with indicated concentrations of drugs for 72 h The absorbance was measured using a microplate reader at 450 nm (Synergy H1, BioTek, USA).

Luciferase reporter assay

After infection for 72h with S-pseudotyped reporter viruses, cells were lysed and luciferase activity was assayed using the Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, E1500) according to the manufacturer's protocol and measured using a GloMax microplate luminometer (Promega, Madison, WI, USA).

Confocal microscopy

The protocol for immunofluorescence staining has been previously described (Lin et al., 2020). Briefly, cells were washed with PBS, fixed with 4% (w/v) paraformaldehyde, and permeabilized with 0.1% (v/v) Triton X-100 (Sigma, T8787). The nuclei were stained with 1 mg/ml 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI, Sigma, D9542). Stained sections were analyzed using a laser scanning confocal microscope (Leica TCS SP8, Solms, Germany).

Immunoblot analysis

Western blot analysis was performed as previously described (Mao et al., 2022). Following antibodies were used in this study: anti-ACE2 (Proteintech, 21115–1-AP), anti-dynamin (Proteintech, 18205–1-AP), anti-Flag (Proteintech, 20543–1-AP), anti-Strep II-Tag (ABclonal, AE066), anti-CD63 (Santa Cruz, sc-5275), anti-cathepsin B (CTSB; Proteintech, 12216–1-AP), anti-cathepsin D (CTSD; Santa Cruz, sc-13148), anti-CTSL (Proteintech, 10938–1-AP), anti-ATP6V1B2 (Proteintech, 15097–1-AP), anti-ATP6V1D (Proteintech, 14920–1-AP), anti-EEA1 (Proteintech, 66218–1-Ig), anti-LAMP1 (Proteintech, 67300–1-Ig), anti-Strep (Abclonal, AE066), anti-TMPRSS2 (Proteintech, 14437–1-AP), and anti-beta-actin (Proteintech, 66009–1-Ig). The antibody against the RBD domain of SARS-CoV-2 S protein was synthesized by Prof. Xuefei Cai (Chongqing Medical University). Membranes were blocked with 5% (w/v) skim milk, incubated with the indicated primary antibody, and further incubated with peroxidase-conjugated rabbit anti-mouse IgG antibody (Jackson ImmunoResearch, 315–035–048) or Peroxidase-AffiniPure goat anti-rabbit IgG antibody (Jackson ImmunoResearch, 111–035–045). Protein bands were visualized using ClarityTM Western ECL Substrate (UElandy, S6008 and S6009) and quantified by densitometry using Image J software.

Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) assay

Cells were lysed in Co-IP lysis buffer containing protease inhibitors on ice for 40 minutes and cell lysates were centrifuged at 4 °C as described previously (Zhao et al., 2023). The protein complexes were immunoprecipitated with the indicated antibodies overnight at 4 °C and then incubated with DynabeadsTM Protein G beads (Invitrogen, 91,216,624) for 2 h After washing the beads with Co-IP washing buffer eight times, the resulting products were analyzed by Western blotting.

icELISA

S and N proteins of SARS-CoV-2 were detected by icELISA as previously described (Mao et al., 2022; Scholer et al., 2020). Briefly, Calu-3 and Caco-2 cells were seeded into 96-well microplates 2 days prior to infection. Drugs were added directly into the medium at indicated concentrations, and SARS-CoV-2 infection was performed after cells were incubated for 1.5 h At 24 h post-infection, cells were fixed with 4% (w/v) paraformaldehyde/PBS at room temperature, and further permeabilized with 1% (v/v) Triton-X-100 /PBS and blocked with 3% (v/v) FCS/PBS. The primary antibody was incubated for 2 h at room temperature followed by subsequent incubation with the corresponding secondary antibody. Tetramethylbenzidin (TMB) substrate was added to visualize the enzyme reaction. 0.5 M HCl was used to stop the reaction. Finally, the absorbance was recorded at 450 nm using a microplate multireader (Mithras2 LB 943; Berthold Technologies).

Cell-cell fusion assay

For fusion assays, HEK293T cells were transfected with plasmid GFP-SARS-CoV-2-S encoding the corresponding SARS-CoV-2 S protein and used as effector cells (Mao et al., 2022). Calu-3 or A549 cells overexpressing hACE2 were used as target cells. At 24 h post-transfection, effector and target cells were co-cultured at 1:1 ratio in 6-well cell plates. At 6 h after incubation, cells were treated with diphyllin or chloroquine. Images of syncytia were captured with a laser scanning confocal microscope (Leica TCS SP8, Solms, Germany). For quantification of cell-cell fusions, fused and unfused cells were counted at each image; the fused cells were at least twice as large as the unfused cells. The percentage of cell-cell fusions was calculated as: (number of fused cells /number of total cells) × 100%.

Protease activity assay

In vitro activities of CTSL, furin, and TMPRSS2 were measured by cathepsin L inhibitor screening assay kit (BPS Bioscience, 79591), furin protease assay kit (BPS Bioscience, 78040), and TMPRSS2 fluorogenic assay kit (BPS Bioscience, 78083) according to the manufacturer's recommendation, respectively.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 8 software (GraphPad Software Inc., La Jolla, CA, USA). Data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation of a minimum of three independent experiments or of three different donors. Analysis of variance with two-tailed Student's t-test or non-parametric one-way ANOVA with a Dunn post-test was used to determine significant differences.

Results

Diphyllin inhibits SARS-CoV-2 entry

To test its effect on SARS-CoV-2 infections, cell lines were infected with various SARS-CoV-2 pseudoviruses in the presence and absence of diphyllin. The antiviral activity of diphyllin against SARS-CoV-2-S pseudotype infection was first tested in hACE2-transgenic human fetal kidney endothelial HEK293T (HEK293T-hACE2) cells. Diphyllin exhibited no significant cytotoxicity at concentrations below 10 µM as determined by a commercial cell counting kit (‘CCK-8′), but blocked SARS-CoV-2-S entry as quantified by luciferase reporter assays (EC50: 128.7 nM & CC50: >15.78 μM; Supplementary Fig. S1A). These data suggested that diphyllin has an inhibitory effect on SARS-CoV-2-S-mediated virus entry. To corroborate these findings in other cells, colonic adenocarcinoma Caco-2 (Caco-2-hACE2) cells, lung carcinoma Calu-3 (Calu-3-hACE2), and A549 (A549-hACE2) cells were infected with the SARS-CoV-2-S-pseudovirus and treated with graded concentrations of diphyllin (0.01 to 10 μM). Again, diphyllin exhibited no significant cytotoxicity at concentrations below 10 µM (Fig. 1A-C) but reduced virus entry by more than 90% in all four cell lines (Caco-2-hACE2: EC50: 1.77 μM, CC50: 17.87 μM; Calu-3-hACE2: EC50: 108.4 nM, CC50: >5000 nM; A549-hACE2: EC50: 345.8 nM, CC50: >5000 nM). Accordingly, confocal microscopy revealed an reduction in SARS-CoV-2 S protein expression in cells treated with 2 µM diphyllin for 48 h compared to the solvent control (DMSO) (Fig. 1D-F; Supplementary Fig. S1B). We concluded that diphyllin inhibits SARS-CoV-2 S-mediated entry into a panel of different human cells.

To test whether diphyllin inhibits the replication of SARS-CoV-2, we performed an icELISA assay to analyze the abundance of viral proteins in Caco-2 and Calu-3 cells infected with a clinical SARS-CoV-2 isolate (B.1). Our icELISA results showed that diphyllin inhibited the expression of the SARS-CoV-2-encoded S (Fig. 1G) and N antigen (Fig. 1H) in Caco-2 and Calu-3 cells in a dose-dependent manner. To corroborate these findings regarding antiviral activity in cell culture models that faithfully recapitulate the situation in infected airways, we assessed the antiviral activity of diphyllin in primary human tracheal epithelial cells in air liquid interface cultures derived from three different donors. In two of the cultures, diphyllin elicited significant antiviral activity (Donor A and Donor B, Fig. 1I and J). In a third culture, the SARS-CoV-2 replication progressed especially fast and a trend only at day 1 after infection was observed (Donor C, Fig. 1I and J).

Collectively, these results indicate that diphyllin elicits an inhibitory effect against pseudoviral and authentic SARS-CoV-2 infections in cell culture including complex air liquid interface cultures.

Diphyllin inhibits virus entry mediated by S proteins derived from different variants of concern

We further tested the inhibitory effects of diphyllin on the entry mediated by the S derived from VOCs such as Alpha (B.1.1.7), Beta (B.1.3.51), Delta (B.1.617.2), and Omicron (B.1.1.529) (Graham et al., 2021; Mansbach et al., 2021; Shinde et al., 2021). Using a luciferase reporter assay, we found that diphyllin exhibited an overarching inhibitory activity against all SARS-CoV-2-S pseudoviral infections in Calu-3-hACE2 and Caco-2-hACE2 cells (Fig. 2A and B). Accordingly, immunofluorescence assays revealed that diphyllin significantly decreased the abundance of SARS-CoV-2-S proteins originating from various VOCs (Fig. 2C and D). Interestingly, we found that diphyllin more efficiently inhibited pseudovirus infections of Omicron (Calu-3-hACE2 cells, EC50: 125 nM; Caco-2-hACE2 cells, EC50: 13 nM) than other SARS-CoV-2 variants, including Alpha, Beta, and Delta, (Calu-3-hACE2 cells, Alpha EC50: 278 nM, Beta EC50: 241 nM, Delta EC50: 259 nM; Caco-2-hACE2 cells, Alpha EC50: 713 nM, Beta EC50: 1924 nM, Delta EC50: 882 nM). Collectively, these findings suggest that diphyllin inhibits the entry of various SARS-CoV-2 VOCs in cell culture.

Diphyllin does neither affect ACE2 expression and distribution nor the association between ACE2 and SARS-CoV-2-S

To elucidate the molecular mechanism by which diphyllin inhibits SARS-CoV-2 infections, we first evaluated whether diphyllin inhibits the expression or alters the distribution of the viral receptor ACE2. Confocal microscopy revealed that diphyllin treatment had no effects on ACE2 expression or distribution in Calu-3 and A549 cells (Fig. 3A and B). Accordingly, western blot analyses revealed that diphyllin treatment had no effect on the intracellular ACE2 expression or dynamin levels, the latter being involved in clathrin-mediated endocytosis (Fig. 3C and D). These findings suggested that diphyllin has no obvious effect on ACE2 expression or distribution. Given that the S protein binds to ACE2 (Fig. 3E), we tested whether diphyllin inhibits the interaction of S and ACE2. HEK293T cells were co-transfected with the 3*Flag-hACE2 and 2*Strep-S plasmids, followed by treatment with 2 μM diphyllin or DMSO as a control for 48 h The immunoprecipitation assay showed the expected binding of the S protein to ACE2 which was not destabilized by diphyllin (Fig. 3F). Thus, diphyllin has no effect on ACE2 expression and distribution and does not impair the association of S and ACE2.

Diphyllin inhibits SARS-CoV-2 endocytosis

The endocytic pathway plays a critical role for the entry of coronaviruses (CoV) including SARS-CoV, Middle East respiratory syndrome coronavirus (MERS-CoV), SARS-CoV-2, and infectious bronchitis virus (IBV) into host cells (Burkard et al., 2015; Burkard et al., 2014; Wang et al., 2008; Wang et al., 2019). Accordingly, chlorpromazine, an inhibitor of clathrin-dependent endocytosis, has significant inhibitory activity against CoVs (Boyd-Kimball et al., 2019; Inoue et al., 2007). Treatment with both 2 μM diphyllin and 10 μM chlorpromazine in Calu-3-hACE2 and A549-hACE2 cells revealed no significant additive inhibitory effect on the S-mediated infection (Fig. 4A and B). These data may suggest that diphyllin, like chlorpromazine, inhibits endosomal entry route during SARS-CoV-2 infection. To better understand how diphyllin inhibits the endocytosis of SARS-CoV-2, we evaluated the effect of diphyllin on the number of early endosomes (EE), late endosomes (LE), and lysosomes in SARS-CoV-2-S-infected Calu-3-hACE2 and A549-hACE2 cells. Western blot analyses showed that in diphyllin-treated cells, the level of lysosomal-associated membrane protein 1 (LAMP1, a typical lysosome marker) was significantly upregulated, whereas the levels of early endosome antigen 1 (EEA1, a typical EE marker) and CD63 (a typical LE marker) were not significantly altered (Fig. 4C and D). Furthermore, confocal microscopy revealed that diphyllin significantly increased the LAMP1 signal compared to solvent controls (Fig. 4E and F). These results suggest that diphyllin exerts effects on the lysosomal pathway.

Diphyllin neutralizes the acidic environment of endocytic organelles, causing antiviral activity against viruses such as influenza A virus, ZIKV, and feline coronavirus (Chen et al., 2013; Hu et al., 2017; Martinez-Lopez et al., 2019). To enter host cells via the endosomal route, SARS-CoV-2 requires an acidic environment in endolysosomes. We hypothesized that diphyllin may interfere with lysosomal degradation and increases the number of lysosomes. To test the effect of diphyllin on lysosomal degradation, Calu-3-hACE2 and A549-hACE2 cells were treated with dye-quenched-bovine serum albumin (DQ-BSA)-Red for 30 min. Confocal microscopy analyses revealed that diphyllin treatment significantly decreased the fluorescence signal of DQ-BSA-Red, whereas starvation with Earle's balanced salt solution (EBSS) increased it (Fig. 4G and 4H). To further investigate whether diphyllin blocks lysosomal degradation by altering lysosomal acidification, Calu-3-hACE2 and A549-hACE2 cells were treated with 2 μM diphyllin for 24 h, followed by staining with 100 nM LysoTracker-Red for 1 h Diphyllin treatment significantly decreased the fluorescence intensity of LysoTracker-Red staining, whereas starvation with EBSS increased it (Fig. 4I and J). Collectively, these results indicate that diphyllin inhibits the endocytosis of SARS-CoV-2 by altering lysosomal acidification to block lysosomal degradation.

Diphyllin blocks SARS-CoV-2 endocytosis by inhibiting endosomal cathepsin L activity

The entry of SARS-CoV-2 necessitates the cleavage of the viral S protein at the S1-S2 interface by cellular proteases such as furin as well as at the S2’ site by either cathepsins or TMPRSS2 (Jackson et al., 2022). To investigate the potential impact of diphyllin on the cleavage of the SARS-CoV-2 S protein, western blotting analyses were performed, which showed that diphyllin caused a significant reduction in the cleavage of S into the S1 and S2 subunits (Fig. 5A and B). This observation suggests that diphyllin can inhibit the levels or activity of specific cellular proteases involved in S cleavage.

Cathepsins are a class of endosomal host proteases that are misused by viruses for the activation of viral fusion proteins during viral endocytosis and/or entry (Yang and Shen, 2020). CTSL is a critical endosomal protease that is active in acidic environments and cleaves the SARS-CoV-2 S protein allowing the fusion of endosomal and viral membranes (Liu et al., 2020; Ou et al., 2020). V-ATPases play an important role in regulating the acidification of endolysosomal compartments in order to render proteases active (Huss and Wieczorek, 2009; Martinez-Lopez et al., 2019). We performed western blot analyses to test the effect of diphyllin on the expression and maturation of V-ATPases such as ATP6V1B2 and ATP6V1D as well as cathepsins such as CTSB, CTSD, and CTSL in Calu-3-hACE2 and A549-hACE2 cells. The results showed that diphyllin significantly decreased the levels of mature CTSL (29 kDa), but had no effect on ATP6V1B2, ATP6V1D, mature CTSB (38 kDa) or CTSD (33 kDa) (Fig. 5C and D). Accordingly, confocal microscopy revealed that diphyllin significantly decreased the fluorescence signal of CTSL compared to that of the solvent control (Fig. 5E and F). However, diphyllin did not significantly decrease the colocalization of CTSL and LAMP1 (Supplementary Fig. S2). To further assess whether diphyllin depends on CTSL to block SARS-CoV-2 infection, Calu-3-hACE2 and A549-hACE2 cells were treated with 1 μM diphyllin and 10 μM of the CTSL inhibitor MDL28170 for 2 h, followed by infection with the SARS-CoV-2-S-pseudotyped reporter virus for 24 h We found no significant additive effect on the decrease in CTSL expression (Fig. 5G and H; Supplementary Fig. S3) and inhibition of SARS-CoV-2-S infection (Fig. 5I and 5J). Furthermore, our analysis of the CTSL activity has unequivocally demonstrated that diphyllin exerts a notable inhibitory effect on CTSL activity (Fig. 5K). Collectively, these findings suggest that the suppressive effect of diphyllin on CTSL activity ultimately leads to the obstruction of SARS-CoV-2-S-mediated endocytosis.

Diphyllin blocks S-mediated entry at the surface by decreasing furin expression

Previous studies have highlighted the importance of the virus-plasma membrane fusion pathway in SARS-CoV-2 cell entry, which depends on the cleavage of S into S1 and S2 (Hoffmann et al., 2020a). To determine whether diphyllin is also effective against membrane fusion at the surface, we used HEK293T cells transfected with plasmid GFP-SARS-CoV-2-S as effector cells and HEK293T-hACE2, Calu-3-hACE2, and A549-hACE2 cells as target cells. The cell-cell fusion assay demonstrated that diphyllin perturbs SARS-CoV-2-S-induced cell fusion of HEK293T-hACE2, Calu-3-hACE2, and A549-hACE2 by reducing fusion rates by approximately 90% (Fig. 6A and B; Supplementary Fig. S4). In contrast, chloroquine had no effect on syncytia formation, which is consistent with our previous findings and explains why the antiviral infection effect of chloroquine is limited in human lung cells (Hoffmann et al., 2020c).

During SARS-CoV-2 entry into target cells, the previously furin cleaved S protein binds to the viral receptor ACE2 followed another cleavage at the S2’ site by TMPRSS2, resulting in membrane fusion and viral entry (Hoffmann et al., 2020a; Hoffmann et al., 2020b; Matsuyama et al., 2020; Papa et al., 2021; Zang et al., 2020). To investigate whether diphyllin affects the abundance of other proteases relevant for S maturation such as furin and TMPRSS2, Calu-3-hACE2 and A549-hACE2 cells were treated with 2 μM diphyllin for 48 h Western blot analysis showed that diphyllin treatment decreased furin levels in both cell types but had no effect on TMPRSS2 (Fig. 6C and D). However, diphyllin did not significantly decrease the co-localization of furin and LAMP1 (Supplementary Fig. S5). These findings suggest that diphyllin inhibits SARS-CoV-2 membrane fusion by inhibiting furin expression. To further assess the effect of diphyllin on furin, Calu-3-hACE2 and A549-hACE2 cells were treated with 1 μM diphyllin and 30 μM of the furin inhibitor Decanoyl-RVKR-CMK (CMK) for 2 h, followed by infection with SARS-CoV-2-S-pseudotyped virus for 24 h We found no significant additive effect on furin expression (Fig. 6E and F; Supplementary Fig. S6) or inhibition of SARS-CoV-2-S infection (Fig. 6G and H). Moreover, our analysis of furin and TMPRSS2 activity has incontrovertibly demonstrated that diphyllin exerts a considerable inhibitory effect on furin activity, whereas no significant impact was observed on TMPRSS2 activity (Fig. 6I). These findings indicate that diphyllin potentially exerts a selective modulation of furin function, thereby blocking the S-mediated entry of SARS-CoV-2.

Discussion

Because COVID-19 has incurred significant financial and societal costs, there is an urgent need to develop effective therapeutic approaches for controlling SARS-CoV-2 infection. In this study, we found that diphyllin exhibits good safety with low cytotoxicity and potent anti-SARS-CoV-2 activity against wild-type SARS-CoV-2 as well as different VOCs in vitro. Diphyllin inhibits SARS-CoV-2 endocytosis by inhibiting endosomal CTSL activity and blocks SARS-CoV-2-S-meditated entry by inhibiting furin activity (Fig. 7).

Proposed model of diphyllin inhibiting SARS-CoV-2 entry. Diphyllin elicits a doubled-pronged attack on the entry of SARS-CoV-2 by blocking the endocytosis route through inhibiting CTSL activity and the cell surface route through inhibition of furin activity.

Diphyllin is a natural component of the lignan family isolated from Justicia procumbens (Chen et al., 2018). Lignans are generated from the shikimic acid biosynthetic pathway and some of them combine with glycone to form glycosides and other derivatives (Teponno et al., 2016). Therefore, diphyllin was available with low cost as a potential COVID-19 treatment. Previous studies have shown that diphyllin has anti-tumor activity and can be applied in certain cancer therapies. Chen et al. demonstrated that diphyllin inhibits new blood vessel proliferation and formation in TE-1 and ECA-109 esophageal cancer cell lines (Chen et al., 2018). Shen et al. showed that diphyllin dramatically inhibits the proliferation of SGC7901 human gastric cancer cells and induces apoptosis by inhibiting V-ATPase expression (Chen et al., 2018; Shen et al., 2011). Additionally, diphyllin elicits broad-spectrum antiviral activity against dengue virus 1, tick-borne encephalitis virus, West Nile virus, and Japanese encephalitis virus by preventing endosome acidification (Chen et al., 2013; Hu et al., 2017; Zhang et al., 2017). The natural glycosylated diphyllin compound 6-deoxyglucose-diphyllin has been reported to potently inhibit ZIKV infection in vitro and in vivo (Martinez-Lopez et al., 2019). Two studies by Stefanik et al. and Tóth et al. have demonstrated that diphyllin exhibits an inhibitory effect on SARS-CoV-2 infections in Vero and Caco-2 cells (Stefanik et al., 2021; Toth et al., 2023). However, the broad-spectrum inhibitory effect of diphyllin on different variant SARS-CoV-2 infections and the mechanism of its antiviral activity had been elusive.

SARS-CoV-2 infects host cells either via endosomal or cell-surface entry routes. In this study, we found that diphyllin is effective against both pathways. Diphyllin inhibits different viral infections such as influenza A virus and ZIKV by its ability to counteract endosomal acidification (Chen et al., 2013; Martinez-Lopez et al., 2019). We found that diphyllin inhibits the endocytosis of SARS-CoV-2 by neutralizing endolysosomal acidification and reducing CTSL activity. CTSL requires acid environments for optimal enzymatic activity. It is the major protease cleaving the SARS-CoV S1 subunit, triggering membrane fusion (Glowacka et al., 2011; Kawase et al., 2012; Matsuyama et al., 2010; Shulla et al., 2011; Simmons et al., 2005). Like the case of SARS-CoV, CTSL also plays a critical role in SARS-CoV-2-S-meditated endosomal entry (Li et al., 2023; Mao et al., 2022; Meewan et al., 2022). Accordingly, CTSL inhibition may be an effective intervention strategy for blocking SARS-CoV-2 infections.

There are several exogenous and membrane-bound host proteases such as furin and TMPRSS2 that support the cell entry of viruses (Bestle et al., 2020; Padmanabhan et al., 2020; Tang et al., 2020). The SARS-CoV-2 S protein contains a furin cleavage site that significantly contributes to its effective membrane fusion and infectivity (Hoffmann et al., 2020a). The primary host cell protease responsible for cleaving the SARS-CoV-2 S protein at the cell surface is TMPRSS2 (Jackson et al., 2022). Furin's primary role involves activating newly synthesized SARS-CoV-2 S proteins along the secretory pathway in infected cells, as well as activating the viral S protein for entry at the cell surface (Walls et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2022). Our findings revealed that diphyllin hindered cell entry by inhibiting furin activity, providing additional evidence supporting the crucial role of furin in activating the SARS-CoV-2 S protein. However, the fact that diphyllin treatment reduced furin activity provides a parsimonious explanation regarding its ability to inhibit S-mediated entry.

In this study, we revealed that diphyllin exhibits potent anti-SARS-CoV-2 activity against wild-type SARS-CoV-2 as well as different VOCs in vitro. The Omicron variant exhibits significant distinctions from other SARS-CoV-2 variants (including Alpha, Beta, and Delta), notably displaying a higher affinity for ACE2 compared to the Delta variant (Meng et al., 2022). There are notable variances in the entry pathways employed by the Omicron variant in host cells compared to the other variants (Meng et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2022). Our present study highlights diphyllin's superior inhibitory efficacy against Omicron pseudoviral infection in comparison to the infections caused by other SARS-CoV-2 variants. It is plausible that the entry mechanism of Omicron variant into host cells relies more heavily on endocytosis facilitated by CTSL, rather than cell surface entry mediated by TMPRSS2.

The present study had several limitations. First, we did not investigate the antiviral effect of diphyllin in vivo. Second, although diphyllin has broad pan-variant antiviral activity against various SARS-CoV-2 VOCs, we only evaluated its antiviral effect on newly emerging SARS-CoV-2 variants (such as B.1.617.2, and B.1.1.529) (Graham et al., 2021). Finally, it remains unclear how diphyllin reduces furin activity and blocks SARS-CoV-2-S-mediated cell surface entry.

Conclusions

In summary, the present study revealed that diphyllin exhibited a potent antiviral effect by blocking endocytosis and cell surface entry of SARS-CoV-2 in vitro. Diphyllin is a novel inhibitor of CTSL and furin proteases, resulting in a double-pronged attack on SARS-CoV-2 entry along endosomal as well as cell surface routes. Our results suggest that diphyllin is used to treat SARS-CoV-2 variant infections in future preclinical studies and may be a novel potent candidate for COVID-19 therapy.

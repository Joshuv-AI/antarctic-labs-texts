Deep learning based thyroid prediction with opposition learning based red panda optimization feature selection
Authors: K Hema Priya, K Valarmathi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis research introduces a novel approach for thyroid prediction by considering three various publicly available datasets. The input data from the dataset is preprocessed to ensure standardization and balance for mitigating the biased outcomes. Then, the proposed cascaded autoencoder-based simple recurrent model is employed for extracting significant spatio-temporal features. From the extracted features, the optimal feature is selected using the proposed Opposition Learning-based Red Panda Optimization (OL_RPO) algorithm, which enhances the efficiency of the predictive model. Finally, the thyroid prediction is performed using the Enhanced Transformer Model, which uses the selected features to achieve robust and accurate predictions. The analysis of the proposed model based on Accuracy, Specificity, Sensitivity, F-Score, positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), and Error acquired the value of 99, 99.2, 99.01, 98.501, 98.1, 1.9, and 0.07689 respectively.Keywords: Thyroid prediction, Optimization, Opposition learning, Red panda optimization, Deep learningSubject terms: Computer science, Information technology, Software

Introduction

Thyroid disease creates number of problems to the infected patient that affect the thyroid gland, a small butterfly-like organ at the tip of the neck1. Thyroid diseases include hypothyroidism (an underactive thyroid), hyperthyroidism (overactive thyroid), goiter (enlarged glands), thyroid nodules, and thyroid cancer2,3. On the prognosis of thyroid diseases, it needs to be diagnosed early and properly treated4. If it is untreated it leads to serious health complications like cardiovascular internal problems, infertility, and metabolic imbalances5. Early prognosis helps to manage symptoms and prevent progression to chronic conditions6,7.

Traditional thyroid prognostic methods depend on clinical examination, laboratory tests, and imaging techniques for the diagnosis and management of thyroid disease. The most common approach employed for the detection of the disease is the measurement of serum thyroid stimulating hormone (TSH), free thyroxine (FT4) and triiodothyronine (T3) levels using biochemical testing8. Elevated or decreased TSH levels of the thyroid indicate thyroidism or hyperthyroidism is detected and further investigation is employed. Another test includes thyroid antibody testing to detect autoimmune conditions like Hashimoto’s thyroiditis or Graves’ disease9,10. Ultrasound scans and other imaging modalities help assess the size, structure and presence of a gland or tumor in the thyroid gland, while radioactive iodine uptake (RAIU) tests examine gland function and detect abnormalities to diagnose malignancy in suspected tissue11. Fine-needle aspiration biopsy is also used for the detection of disease. While these methods are valid, they often require multiple visits, skilled personnel, and sophisticated laboratory facilities that makes the disease detection process time-consuming and unavailable in resource-limited settings12,13. Consequently, modern development aims to improve performance through predictive systems that automate data analysis, benefiting from artificial intelligence14.

Thyroid disease prediction algorithms based on machine learning (ML)15 and deep learning (DL)16–18 are emerging as powerful tools for accurate, effective, and automated diagnosis of thyroid disease19,20. These methods though computer programs are used to analyze complex systems in medical data, including patient demographics21,22. Machine learning methods with genetic information like decision trees, support vector machine (SVM), k-nearest neighbors (KNN), and random forests are commonly used for classification tasks to distinguish thyroid status between correct and incorrect23,24. Deep learning, a subset of ML deep learning artificial neural networks25–27, mainly using convolutional neural networks (CNNs)28,29 and it again uses neural networks (RNNs) for processing large data sets and automatically extracting appropriate features30,31. These techniques are excellent in processing high-dimensional data32,33. Moreover, this predictive model improves diagnostic accuracy and reduces human errorby detectingdisease for providing early diagnosis and enabling timely intervention34,35.Thus, this research introduces a novel deep learning based thyroid prediction with optimal feature selection. Related works

Some of the existing methods concerning the thyroid prediction is detailed in this section. Attention Mechanism-based Multimodal Classification Network (AMMCNet) architecture was designed by central lymph node metastasis36. The AMMCNet model was trained on the training data to learn how to identify patterns in the input and clinicopathological data that are associated with central lymph node metastasis. The designed model achieved a higher accuracy in predicting central lymph node metastasis compared to traditional machine-learning models. Still, over-fitting issues limits the generalization capability of the model.

Self-stacking classifier (SSC) was modeled by37 for predicting thyroid disease. In this, down-sampling was employed to balance the dataset, addressing the issue of imbalanced classes. Each target class was down-sampled to 230 instances. The Random Forest (RF) based self-stacking classifier (SSC) was developed. This involved training multiple RF base models and then training a meta-learner on their predictions. The SSC model acquired improved accuracy in thyroid disease prediction that leads to better diagnostic capabilities. The primary limitation is the reduction of dataset size using down-sampling that makes the information loss.

Multi-Stage Weight-based Ensemble Learning (MS-WEL) was designed by38 to address the lack of accurate early prediction methods. The designed model combines multiple classifiers and assigns weights to them based on their performance which aims to leverage the strengths of each individual model. The MS-WEL model achieved prediction accuracy by leveraging the strengths of multiple classifiers. The weighted approach allows for prioritizing the contributions of more accurate classifiers. Still, complexity of the model was higher by incorporating multiple deep learning classifiers.

Quantum-inspired convolutional neural network (QuCNet) was designed by39. In this, quantum filter transformation (QFT) was applied to the input to extract four-channel quantum features. Finally, convolutional neural network was used to classify the extracted features. The QFT enables more detailed and nuanced feature extraction that leads to better classification performance40. The computational cost of QFT was higher for larger dataset.Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) based on a modified ResNet architecture was designed by36 with dual optimizers like Adam and SGD during the training phase. The dual optimizer approach enhances training efficiency and performance. Still, the accuracy acquired by the model was lower for evaluating the real world application domain. Proposed methodology

Some of the existing models depend heavily on imbalanced datasets, where the number of positive cases is far fewer than negative cases, leading to biased predictions and lower accuracy for minority classes. Also, inaccurate prediction, overfitting issues, computation burden and several other factors limits the performance. To overcome the limitations, deep learning based thyroid prediction with optimal feature selection is introduced in this research. The input data is pre-processed for standardizing and balancing the data for eliminating the biased outcome. Then, the appropriate spatio-temporal features are extracted using the cascaded autoencoder based simple recurrent model. In this, the optimal features are selected using the Opposition Learning based Red Panda Optimization (OL_RPO) algorithm. Using the selected features, the thyroid prediction is employed using the Enhanced Transformer Model.

Feature extraction using CAE_SRU model

The Cascaded Autoencoder–Simple Recurrent Unit (CAE_SRU) is considered in the proposed feature extraction model compared to conventional LSTM and GRU because the proposed model provides efficient balance between feature richness and computational cost. Still, the LSTM and GRU are powerful for capturing long-term dependencies but have the issues with high parameter overhead and slower training that lead to overfitting. The proposed model with the SRU enables faster parallelizable computations with fewer parameters that makes the model more suitable for large-scale spatio-temporal modeling. Thus, bySRU with cascaded autoencoders, the proposed CAE_SRU compresses redundant information and also extracts discriminative representations that preserve temporal dynamics with lower complexity.It ensures the robust and scalable performance for thyroid disease prediction. Besides, autoencoders are unsupervised neural networks used for dimensionality reduction and feature extraction by learning compressed representations of input data. Multiple autoencoders are arranged in a hierarchical layer-by-layer manner42. Each layer refines the features extracted by the previous layer for allowing the model to learn high-level abstract features. The SRU is a type of Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) that focuses on capturing sequential dependencies and patterns over time for making it ideal for medical data with time-series characteristics. Thus, the combination of autoencoders and SRU effectively addresses both spatial and temporal pattern analysis for making it highly suitable for complex features in thyroid prediction. Thyroid prediction using En_TransNet

Using the features chosen by the OL_RPO algorithm, the thyroid prediction is devised using the proposed Enhanced Transformer (En_TransNet) Model, which is designed, based on Absolute Position Cross Stage Omni Attention Transformer Network Model. In this, the convolution layer of the traditional Transformer is replaced using the cross stage partial network, conventional position encoding is replaced with absolute position encoding and attention mechanism is replaced with omni directional attention mechanism. The structure of the proposed En_TransNet model for performing the thyroid prediction is portrayed in Fig. 3.

Structure of proposed En_TransNet Model.

Cross stage partial network: CSPNet improves gradient flow by dividing the input feature map into two parts and processing them through different network paths before merging them later. This strategy enhances gradient combination and reduces redundancy. By splitting the input and processing only part of it through computationally intensive layers, CSPNet reduces the overall number of computations needed. CSPNet incorporates ResNet as its backbone, leveraging residual connections that allow it to learn residual functions. This makes it easier to optimize deep networks and improves accuracy. Each ResUnit in CSPNet is composed of two CBM units (Convolution + Batch Normalization + Activation). These layers extract meaningful features from the input while maintaining stability during training. The output of the ResUnit is added to its input using element-wise addition, which supports the residual learning process and prevents vanishing gradient problems. To reduce overfitting, CSPNet applies dropout layers during training. Dropout randomly removes nodes with a predefined probability, effectively simulating different network structures in each training phase. The CSPNet improves the model’s generalization ability. The proposed model acquired improved outcome for all assessment measures because each stage reduces noise and preserves class-discriminative information. Conclusion

The proposed thyroid prediction model effectively integrates advanced techniques for feature extraction, optimal feature selection and predictionfor ensuring accurate and unbiased experimental outcome. Here, CAE_SRU and the OL_RPO algorithm for optimal feature selection assist the proposed model to enhance the outcome through efficient feature extraction and selection criteria. The Enhanced Transformer Model further enhances the prediction accuracy for making the proposed reliable tool for thyroid diagnosis using the absolute position embedding and omni direction attention mechanism. The analysis of the proposed model based on Accuracy, Specificity, Sensitivity, F-Score, PPV, NPV, and Error and acquired the value of 99, 99.2, 99.01, 98.501, 98.1, 1.9, and 0.07689 respectively. The adaptability and precision of the proposed model demonstrate its potential for application in real-world healthcare systems, paving the way for improved diagnostic accuracy and early detection of thyroid-related conditions. Still, the consideration of hybrid deep leaning model may enhance the complexity of the model, which will be solved through the lightweight model in the future with pruning models.

A Homotopical Dold-Kan correspondence for Joyal’s category Θ \Theta and other test categories
Léo Hubert

Abstract
. We prove that for any test category A A , in the sense of Grothendieck, satisfying a compatibility condition between homology equivalences and weak equivalences of presheaves, the homotopy category of abelian presheaves on A A is equivalent to the non-negative derived category of abelian groups. This provides a homotopical generalization of the Dold-Kan correspondence for presheaves of abelian groups over a wide range of test categories. This equivalence of homotopy categories comes from a Quillen equivalence for a model structure on abelian presheaves that we introduce under these conditions. We then show that this result applies to Joyal’s category Θ \Theta .

Introduction

In the first chapters of his
1983
manuscript
Pursuing Stacks

[
15
]
, Grothendieck introduced the notion of
test
categories
to get an axiomatic characterization of small categories whose
category of presheaves modelizes, in a canonical way, the category
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hot}}
of
topological spaces up to weak homotopy equivalences. This theory has since been
described and developed further, notably
in
[
24
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
12
]
,

[
3
]
and
[
1
]
.

However, Chapters V to VII of
Pursuing Stacks
have not met the same
enthusiasm yet. In these chapters, Grothendieck introduced a formalism to study
the homology theory of abelian presheaves on small categories, different from
the one used in functor homology (see for example

[
26
]
). He also conjectured a homotopical
generalization of the Dold-Kan correspondence for abelian presheaves on test
categories. The present article aims to expose the basics of this formalism and
to prove Grothendieck’s conjecture, under additional conditions applying notably
to Joyal’s category
\Theta

[
20
]
.

A brief overview of test categories

In this context, the category
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hot}}
is realized, using Illusie-Quillen’s
theorem
[
18
]
, as the Gabriel-Zisman localization of the
category
{\mathcal{C}\mspace{-2.mu}\it{at}}
of small categories by the class
\mathcal{W}_{\infty}
of functors whose
image by the nerve functor is a simplicial weak equivalence. Given any small
category
A
, the functor

i_{A}:{\widehat{A}}\to{\mathcal{C}\mspace{-2.mu}\it{at}}\text{ ,}\hskip 10.0ptX\mapsto A/{X}

sending every presheaf to its category of elements allows to define a class of
weak equivalences of presheaves denoted by
\mathcal{W}_{A}
, consisting of the natural
transformations in
{\widehat{A}}
whose image by the functor
i_{A}
is in

\mathcal{W}_{\infty}
. Grothendieck denotes by
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hot}}_{A}
the localization of
{\widehat{A}}
by

\mathcal{W}_{A}
, and defines a
pseudo-test category
to be a category
A
such that
the functor
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hot}}_{A}\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Hot}}
induced by
i_{A}
is an equivalence of categories.

However, this notion is too general for Grothendieck’s purpose of finding
elegant characterizations, and he gradually restricts his scope. First, he
defines
weak test categories
by asking, in addition, for the right
adjoint
i_{A}^{*}:{\mathcal{C}\mspace{-2.mu}\it{at}}\to{\widehat{A}}
of
i_{A}
to preserve weak equivalences.
He then manages to find very simple characterizations of categories
A
whose
slices
A/{a}
are weak test categories for every object
a
of
A
,
that he calls
local test categories
. Moreover, any local test category
with trivial homotopy groups is also a weak test category:
test
categories
are then defined to be categories which are both local and weak test
categories. Finally,
strict test categories
are test categories
A
such
that the functor
i_{A}
commutes, up to weak equivalences, with the product of
presheaves.

Important examples of test categories include the category
\Delta
of non-empty
finite ordinals, the cubical category
\square
and its variations with connections

[
25
]
, and the dendroidal category

\Omega

[
3
]
. A particularly important example is given
by the category
\Theta
who, besides being a strict test
category
[
12
]
, is deeply linked to both strict

\omega
-categories (
[
22
]
,
[
7
]
) and
weak higher
categories (
[
20
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
29
]
,
[
2
]
).

Linearization of homotopy types

The homology of a small category is traditionally defined as the homology of its
simplicial nerve, and we can therefore define the homology of a presheaf on any
small category as the homology of its category of elements. However, this
process is rather complex, and gives a preeminent role to the category
\Delta
.
In the context of test categories, Grothendieck asks in
[
15
]
a
handful of questions regarding what he calls the
linearization of homotopy
types
.

Denote by
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hotab}}
the non-negative derived category of abelian groups, and
by
\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}
the category of abelian presheaves on
A
.

(1)

Given a small category
A
, can we define a canonical functor

\operatorname{H_{A}}:\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Hotab}}

so that the following diagram

\textstyle{{\widehat{A}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathbb{Z}}
\scriptstyle{i_{A}}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{C}\mspace{-2.mu}\it{at}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathsf{N}}
\textstyle{{\widehat{\Delta}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\dk}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{C}\mspace{-2.mu}\it{h}_{+}}({\mathsf{Ab}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{can}
\textstyle{\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{H_{A}}}
\textstyle{\operatorname{\mathsf{Hotab}}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\quad,}}

where
\dk
is the normalized chain complex functor and
\mathsf{N}
is the
simplicial nerve, is commutative up to a natural isomorphism?

We introduce in this paper such a functor, defined, following Grothendieck’s
ideas, as the left derived functor of the colimit functor.

This allows to define a class of weak equivalences of abelian presheaves on
A

that we denote by
\mathcal{W}^{{\mathsf{ab}}}_{A}
, whose elements are the morphisms sent to
isomorphisms by the functor
\operatorname{H_{A}}
. Note that, in the case of simplicial
abelian groups, one usually takes as weak equivalences morphisms whose
underlying morphism of simplicial sets is a weak equivalence. A very important
observation in our context is the fact that the functor
\dk:\widehat{\Delta}^{\mathsf{ab}}\to{\mathcal{C}\mspace{-2.mu}\it{h}_{+}}({\mathsf{Ab}})
sends these morphisms to quasi-isomorphisms.

(2)

Let
\U:\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}\to{\widehat{A}}
be the forgetful functor.
Can we exhibit conditions on
A
to ensure that the functor
\operatorname{H_{A}}
sends
morphisms of
\U^{-1}\mathcal{W}_{A}
to isomorphisms in
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hotab}}
?

We will focus on categories, such as
\Delta
, for which the classes
\mathcal{W}^{{\mathsf{ab}}}_{A}

and
\U^{-1}\mathcal{W}_{A}
coincide. We call this the
strong Whitehead condition
.
We won’t study in this paper categories satisfying only the
inclusion
\U^{-1}\mathcal{W}_{A}\subset\mathcal{W}^{{\mathsf{ab}}}_{A}
.

Finally, the functor
\dk:\widehat{\Delta}^{\mathsf{ab}}\to{\mathcal{C}\mspace{-2.mu}\it{h}_{+}}({\mathsf{Ab}})
has another important
property, expressed by the Dold-Kan theorem: it is an equivalence of categories
(
[
14
]
,
[
21
]
).
In general, abelian presheaves on test categories are not equivalent to
non-negative chain complexes of abelian groups.
For example, the category of bisimplicial abelian groups is equivalent to the
category of double chain complexes of the first quadrant.
However, a reasonable generalization of the Dold-Kan correspondence would be an
answer to the following question:

(3)

If the answer to question
(2)
is positive, we get a functor

\overline{\operatorname{H_{A}}}:\operatorname{\mathsf{Hotab}}_{A}^{\U}\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Hotab}}

where
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hotab}}_{A}^{\U}
is the localization of
\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}
by the class

\U^{-1}\mathcal{W}_{A}
.
Is it true that if
A
is a test category, then the functor
\operatorname{H_{A}}
is an
equivalence of categories?

The present article shows that the answer to this last question is positive for
categories satisfying the
strong
Whitehead condition.

Theorem

(
5.14
)
.

Let
A
be a test category satisfying the strong Whitehead condition.
Then the functor
\overline{\operatorname{H_{A}}}:\operatorname{\mathsf{Hotab}}_{A}^{\U}\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Hotab}}
is an
equivalence of categories.

A major ingredient of the proof comes from functors satisfying the condition of
Quillen’s Theorem A
[
27
, Theorem A]
, that are called

aspherical functors
in the context of Grothendieck’s homotopy theory.
Given any aspherical functor
u:A\to B
, the induced restriction functor
u^{*}:{\widehat{B}}\to{\widehat{A}}
is homotopy cofinal, meaning that it induces an
isomorphism between homotopy colimits.

We show that if
u:A\to B
is an aspherical functor and
A
satisfies the
strong Whitehead condition, then the same holds for
B
. We also study the class

\mathcal{W}^{{\mathsf{ab}}}_{\infty}
of functors between small categories which induce isomorphisms in
homology, and the corresponding notion of
\mathcal{W}^{{\mathsf{ab}}}_{\infty}
-aspherical morphisms.
We show that the restriction functor
(u^{*})^{\mathsf{ab}}:\widehat{B}^{\mathsf{ab}}\to\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}

induced by a
\mathcal{W}^{{\mathsf{ab}}}_{\infty}
-aspherical functor commutes with the homology
functor. Moreover, using the Grothendieck-Cisinski model structure on the
category of presheaves over a local test category

[
11
, Corollary 4.2.18]
, we prove the following theorem:

Theorem
.

Let
A
be a local test category satisfying the strong Whitehead condition.

(
5.9
)

The category
\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}
can be given the structure of a cofibrantly generated
model category whose weak equivalences are the elements of the class
\mathcal{W}^{{\mathsf{ab}}}_{A}=\U^{-1}\mathcal{W}_{A}
.

(
5.11
)

If
u:A\to B
is an aspherical functor and
B
is also a local test category,
then the functor
(u^{*})^{\mathsf{ab}}:\widehat{B}^{\mathsf{ab}}\to\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}
is a left Quillen
equivalence.

In particular, the equivalence of localized categories of Theorem

5.14
can be promoted to a Quillen equivalence
with the projective model structure on non-negative chain complexes of abelian
groups.

As an application of Theorem
5.14
, we prove that
Joyal’s category
\Theta
satisfies the homotopical Dold-Kan correspondence.
Our contribution to this proof consists mainly in the study of a variation on
the notion of aspherical functors for functors whose codomain is a presheaf
category. We prove that if
A
satisfies the strong Whitehead condition, and if

j:B\to{\widehat{A}}
is an aspherical functor in this sense, then
B
also
satisfies the strong Whitehead condition. We then use previous work from Ara and
Maltsiniotis
[
4
]
showing that the functor
\Delta\to{\widehat{\Theta}}
given by the cellular nerve (
[
6
]
,

[
7
]
) and Street’s oriental functor

[
30
]
is an aspherical functor. This then implies that

\Theta
satisfies the strong Whitehead condition, and therefore satisfies the
conditions of Theorem
5.14
.

We would like to point out that one can also try to characterize small
categories
A
for which the functor
\operatorname{H_{A}}
induces an equivalence of
categories between the localization of the pair
(\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}},\mathcal{W}^{{\mathsf{ab}}}_{A})
and

\operatorname{\mathsf{Hotab}}
, without requiring the condition of question
(2)
. This is partly done
in the author’s PhD thesis
[
17
]
from which this article is
extracted, with still more investigations to be held.
For example, the category
\Delta/{\mathsf{N}(\mathbb{G}_{r})}
, where
\mathbb{G}_{r}
is
the reflexive globular category, is a test category that doesn’t satisfy the
strong Whitehead condition. However, one can prove using

[
17
, Proposition 4.3.6]
that it satisfies this version of the
homotopical Dold-Kan correspondence.

2.
Homology, homotopy colimits and the category of elements

In the context of test categories, the homology of a presheaf is the homology of
its category of elements.
We will now introduce, following Grothendieck’s ideas in
[
15
]
,
a homology functor for
abelian
presheaves that generalizes this idea. We
show that it also generalizes the homology of groups and of simplicial abelian
groups.

2.1
.

If
A
is a small category, we denote by
\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}=\operatorname{\kern 0.5pt\underline{\kern-0.5pt\mathsf{Hom}\kern-0.5pt}\kern 1.0pt}({A}^{\op},{\mathsf{Ab}})
the category of abelian presheaves on
A
. Given a
presheaf
X
on
A
, we denote by
\mathbb{Z}_{A}^{\left(X\right)}
the abelian presheaf on
A

sending every object
a
of
A
to the free abelian group on the set
Xa
.
Following Grothendieck’s terminology
[
15
]
, we will call the

Whitehead functor
the functor

\operatorname{\mathsf{Wh}_{A}}:{\widehat{A}}\to\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}\text{ ,}\hskip 10.0ptX\mapsto\mathbb{Z}_{A}^{\left(X\right)}\text{ ,}\hskip 10.0pt

or simply the
abelianization functor
.
If
a
is an object of
A
, we will simply write
\mathbb{Z}_{A}^{\left(a\right)}
for the image of
the representable presheaf
a
by the functor
\operatorname{\mathsf{Wh}_{A}}
, and we will refer to it
as the abelian presheaf represented by
a
.

2.2
.

We denote by
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hotab}}
the derived category
\mathcal{D}_{+}({\mathsf{Ab}})
, obtained by
localizing non-negative chain complexes of abelian groups by quasi-isomorphisms.
We define a functor

\operatorname{H_{A}}:\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Hotab}}\text{ ,}\hskip 10.0ptX\mapsto\operatorname{{H}}(A,X)

where
\operatorname{{H}}(A,X)
is the homotopy colimit of the diagram

{A}^{\op}\xrightarrow{X}{\mathsf{Ab}}\xhookrightarrow{in_{0}}{\mathcal{C}\mspace{-2.mu}\it{h}_{+}}({\mathsf{Ab}})\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\quad,}

in_{0}
being the inclusion of abelian groups into chain complexes
concentrated in degree
0
.
We say that
\operatorname{{H}}(A,X)
is the
homology of
A
with coefficients in
X
,
or simply the
homology of X
.

Example 2.3
.

If
A
is the groupoid
BG
associated to a group
G
, abelian presheaves on
A

coincide with right
\mathbb{Z}G
-modules. If
M:{BG}^{\op}\to{\mathsf{Ab}}
is
such a
\mathbb{Z}G
-module, its colimit as a diagram in
{\mathsf{Ab}}
is the
abelian group
M_{G}
of coinvariants, and
\operatorname{{H}}(BG,M)
is the complex, up to
quasi-isomorphism, computing the homology of the group
G
with coefficients in

M
.

Example 2.4
.

If
A
is the simplex category
\Delta
, then the diagram

\textstyle{\widehat{\Delta}^{\mathsf{ab}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{H_{\Delta}}}
\scriptstyle{\dk}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{C}\mspace{-2.mu}\it{h}_{+}}({\mathsf{Ab}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{\mathsf{Hotab}}}

is commutative up to a natural isomorphism, where
\dk
is the normalized chain
complex functor

\dk:X\mapsto\left(X_{0}\xleftarrow{d_{0}}\cdots\xleftarrow{d_{0}}\bigcap_{0<i\leqslant n}\ker{d_{i}}\xleftarrow{d_{0}}\bigcap_{0<i\leqslant n+1}\ker{d_{i}}\leftarrow\cdots\right)\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\quad.}

Thus if
X
is a simplicial abelian group,
\operatorname{{H}}(\Delta,X)
is the complex, up to
quasi-isomorphism, computing the standard homology of
X
. This result already
appears in
[
9
, XII, 5.5]
, but we will prove it again
in Example
3.21
.
Note that we could also use the non-normalized chain complex functor, since both
are pointwise quasi-isomorphic.

We will now prove that the homology functor we just introduced associates to any
presheaf of sets the homology of its category of elements.

2.5
.

Recall that the category
{\widehat{\Delta}}
can be equipped with the
Kan-Quillen model structure
[
28
, II, Section 3, Theorem
3]
, where weak equivalences are the simplicial weak
equivalences and cofibrations are the monomorphisms.
Similarly, the category
{\mathcal{C}\mspace{-2.mu}\it{h}_{+}}({\mathsf{Ab}})
can be equipped with the projective model
structure
[
28
, XII,4.12]
where the weak equivalences are the
quasi-isomorphisms, and the cofibrations are the monomorphisms with projective
cokernels. Moreover, the functor

{\widehat{\Delta}}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Wh}_{\Delta}}}\widehat{\Delta}^{\mathsf{ab}}\xrightarrow{\dk}{\mathcal{C}\mspace{-2.mu}\it{h}_{+}}({\mathsf{Ab}})

is a left Quillen functor with respect to these model structures, and it
preserves weak equivalences.

2.9
.

A

Let
A

Definition 2.13
.

A small category

Remark 2.14
.

Inspired by the terminology of test categories, the prefix <<pseudo>>
underlines the fact that we do not ask for any conditions on the
quasi-inverse of the functor
\overline{\operatorname{H_{A}}}
. We introduce

Example 2.15
.

The prime example of a homologically pseudo-test category is given by the

(
[

[

states that the normalized chain complex functor

.

We will prove in this paper that the categories

for every integer

3.
Integrators

Grothendieck describes in
[
15
]
explicit methods to compute the
homology functor without the need of simplicial techniques and the Bousfield-Kan
formula
[
9
]
, that we expose
in this section. While we wont do an extensive study of integrators in this
article (this has been done in
[
17
]
), we cannot solely rely on
the Bousfield-Kan formula to study
\mathcal{W}^{{\mathsf{ab}}}_{\infty}
-asphericity in Section

4

3.1
.

If
A
is a small category, its
additive envelope
, denoted
\Add(A)
, is
the small category defined by the following universal property: for every
additive category
\mathcal{M}
(that is, an
{\mathsf{Ab}}
-enriched category with finite
biproducts and a zero object), there is a natural isomorphism

\operatorname{\kern 0.5pt\underline{\kern-0.5pt\mathsf{Hom}\kern-0.5pt}\kern 1.0pt}(A,\mathcal{M})\simeq\operatorname{\kern 0.5pt\underline{\kern-0.5pt\mathsf{Hom}\kern-0.5pt}\kern 1.0pt_{\mathrm{add}}}(\Add(A),\mathcal{M})

where
\operatorname{\kern 0.5pt\underline{\kern-0.5pt\mathsf{Hom}\kern-0.5pt}\kern 1.0pt_{\mathrm{add}}}
denotes the subcategory of functors commuting to finite sums.
For any functor
F:A\to\mathcal{M}
, we denote by
\Add(F)
the functor making the
following diagram commutative:

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F}
\textstyle{\mathcal{M}}
\textstyle{\Add(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Add(F)}
.

3.2
.

For any small category

, the additive envelope
\Add(A)
can be realized as
the full subcategory of abelian presheaves on

of the form

\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}_{A}^{\left(a_{i}\right)}:a\mapsto\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}^{(\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(a,a_{i}))}

where

is a

is an object of

in

\mathcal{M}
and any functor

, the value of

on an object

is then given by the formula

\Add(F)\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}_{A}^{\left(a_{i}\right)}\right)=\bigoplus_{i\in I}F(a_{i})\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\quad.}

Proposition 3.3
.

For any small category
A
,
\Add(A)
is a dense subcategory of
\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}

Proof.

We need to show that for any abelian
presheaf

on

\underset{L\in\Add(A)/X}{\varinjlim\nolimits^{\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}}}\!\!\!\!L\to X

is an isomorphism.
The proof is similar to the proof of the density of
representable presheaves in the
{\mathcal{S}\mspace{-2.mu}\it{et}}
-valued case, with an additional use of
the diagonal morphisms
(\mathbb{Z}_{A}^{\left(a\right)},X)\to(\mathbb{Z}_{A}^{\left(a\right)}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{A}^{\left(a\right)},X)
of

\Add(A)/{X}
to check that cocones with vertex
Y
correspond to
morphisms of
abelian
presheaves from
X
to
Y
.
We leave the details to the reader, which can also be found in

[
17
, Proposition 2.2.4]
. ∎

3.4
.

Given a small category

, we get, using Proposition

on

3.12

Recall that for any category
A
, the category
\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}
is an
abelian category with enough projective objects. To describe
projective objects, we use the
infinite additive envelope
,
denoted
\operatorname{\mathrm{Addinf}}(A)
, defined by the following universal property:
for every additive category
\mathcal{M}

\operatorname{\kern 0.5pt\underline{\kern-0.5pt\mathsf{Hom}\kern-0.5pt}\kern 1.0pt}(A,\mathcal{M})\simeq\operatorname{\kern 0.5pt\underline{\kern-0.5pt\mathsf{Hom}\kern-0.5pt}\kern 1.0pt_{\mathrm{addinf}}}(\operatorname{\mathrm{Addinf}}(A),\mathcal{M})

where
\operatorname{\kern 0.5pt\underline{\kern-0.5pt\mathsf{Hom}\kern-0.5pt}\kern 1.0pt_{\mathrm{addinf}}}
denotes the subcategory of functors commuting to arbitrary

It can be realized as the full subcategory of
\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}
whose objects
are arbitrary sums of representables.

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
A
be a small category.

(1)

Every object of
\operatorname{\mathrm{Addinf}}(A)
is a projective object in

\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}
.

(2)

Every object in
\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}
admits a projective resolution by
objects of
\operatorname{\mathrm{Addinf}}(A)
.

(3)

Projectives objects in
\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}
are retracts of objects of

\operatorname{\mathrm{Addinf}}{A}
.

Proof.

The proof of
(1)
is straightforward since coproducts of projectives
are projectives. For
(2)
, given an abelian presheaf
X
on
A
, we
set

L_{X}=\bigoplus_{a\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Ob}}(A)}\bigoplus_{x\in Xa}\mathbb{Z}_{A}^{\left(a\right)}

and we define an epimorphism
\varphi:L_{X}\to X
on each summand

(a,x)
by defining
\varphi_{(a,x)}
to be the element of
Xa
represented
by the morphism
\mathbb{Z}_{A}^{\left(a\right)}\xrightarrow{x}X
. The proof of the
last point then follows from the fact that if
X
is a projective
object in
\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}
, the morphism
\varphi:L_{X}\to X
admits a
section, exhibiting
X
as a retract of
L_{X}
.
∎

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
X
be an abelian presheaf on
A
and

{A}^{\op}
. The functors

\displaystyle X\odot_{A}-:\widehat{{A}^{\op}}^{\mathsf{ab}}

\displaystyle\to{\mathsf{Ab}}

\displaystyle-\odot_{A}Y:\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}

\displaystyle L(X\odot_{A}-):\mathcal{D}_{+}(\widehat{{A}^{\op}}^{\mathsf{ab}})

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Hotab}}\text{ ,}\hskip 10.0pt

\displaystyle L(-\odot_{A}Y):\mathcal{D}_{+}(\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}})

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Hotab}}\text{ ,}\hskip 10.0pt

and there is a natural isomorphism

L(X\odot_{A}-)(Y)\simeq L(-\odot_{A}Y)(X)

in
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hotab}}
.

Proof.

Consider two projective resolutions
M_{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}X
and

L_{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\eta}Y
by objects of
\operatorname{\mathrm{Addinf}}(A)
and

\operatorname{\mathrm{Addinf}}({A}^{\op})
respectively.
We can then form the chain complexes of abelian groups

\odot_{A}

Considering the complexes

\operatorname{\mathsf{Tot}}(M_{\bullet}\odot_{A}Y)\xleftarrow{M\odot_{A}\eta}\operatorname{\mathsf{Tot}}(M_{\bullet}\odot_{A}L_{\bullet})\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\odot_{A}L}\operatorname{\mathsf{Tot}}(X\odot_{A}L_{\bullet})\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\quad.}

[

in

Remark 3.20

Since
{\mathcal{C}\mspace{-2.mu}\it{h}_{+}}(\widehat{{A}^{\op}}^{\mathsf{ab}})=\operatorname{\kern 0.5pt\underline{\kern-0.5pt\mathsf{Hom}\kern-0.5pt}\kern 1.0pt}(A,{\mathcal{C}\mspace{-2.mu}\it{h}_{+}}({\mathsf{Ab}}))

Example 3.21

Taking for

the category

be the complex in

whose differential is given by the

For every integer

is the

\mathbb{Z}_{{\Delta}^{\op}}^{\left(\Delta_{n}\right)}:\Delta_{k}\mapsto\\

3.22
.

Any small category
A
can be equipped with a free integrator that we
call the

, defined as the composite

\ell_{A}:A\hookrightarrow{\widehat{A}}\xrightarrow{i_{A}}{\mathcal{C}\mspace{-2.mu}\it{at}}\xrightarrow{\mathsf{N}}{\widehat{\Delta}}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Wh}_{\Delta}}}\widehat{\Delta}^{\mathsf{ab}}\xrightarrow{{L_{\Delta}}_{!}^{\mathsf{ab}}}{\mathcal{C}\mspace{-2.mu}\it{h}_{+}}({\mathsf{Ab}})\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\quad.}

Indeed, for any integer

{(\ell_{A})}_{n}=\bigoplus_{\Delta_{n}\xrightarrow{u}A}\mathbb{Z}_{{A}^{\op}}^{\left(\varphi n\right)}:a\mapsto\bigoplus_{\Delta_{n}\xrightarrow{u}A}\mathbb{Z}^{(\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(\varphi n,a))}

of

is

is in

\mathcal{W}_{\infty}

on

[

]

[

[

4.
Aspherical morphisms for homology

It is fairly easy to see from Proposition

1.5
that if
u:A\to B
is an aspherical
functor, the restriction functor
u^{*}:{\widehat{B}}\to{\widehat{A}}
induces an
isomorphism in homology for presheaves of sets. In this section, we show that it
is enough to ask for
u
to be aspherical with respect to the class of homology
isomorphisms in
{\mathcal{C}\mspace{-2.mu}\it{at}}
, and that the restriction functor also induces an
isomorphism in homology for arbitrary abelian presheaves.

4.1
.

We denote by
\mathcal{W}^{{\mathsf{ab}}}_{\infty}
the class of functors in
{\mathcal{C}\mspace{-2.mu}\it{at}}
inducing an
isomorphism in homology. More precisely, a functor
u:A\to B

between small categories is an element of
\mathcal{W}^{{\mathsf{ab}}}_{\infty}
if and only if
the induced morphism

\operatorname{{H}}(A,\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\operatorname{{H}}(u,\mathbb{Z})}\operatorname{{H}}(B,\mathbb{Z})

is an isomorphism in
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hotab}}
. Using the standard way of computing
homology of categories with the simplicial nerve functor, we get the following
equivalence:

u\in\mathcal{W}^{{\mathsf{ab}}}_{\infty}\iff\dk\mathbb{Z}_{\Delta}^{\left(\mathsf{N}(u)\right)}\text{ is a quasi-isomorphism.}

Proposition 4.2
.

The class
\mathcal{W}^{{\mathsf{ab}}}_{\infty}
is a basic localizer. Moreover, we have the
inclusion
\mathcal{W}_{\infty}\subset\mathcal{W}^{{\mathsf{ab}}}_{\infty}
, and every aspherical
functor is
\mathcal{W}_{\infty}^{\mathsf{ab}}
-aspherical.

Proof.

This follows (see
[
23
, Corollary
1.5.14]
) from the fact that
\mathcal{W}^{{\mathsf{ab}}}_{\infty}

is the class of equivalences for the derivator induced by the
localizer
({\mathcal{C}\mspace{-2.mu}\it{h}_{+}}({\mathsf{Ab}}),\mathcal{W}_{qis})
where
\mathcal{W}_{qis}
is the class of
quasi-isomorphisms of non-negative chain complexes of abelian groups.
More details (in French) can be found in

[
17
, annexe A]
. The inclusion
\mathcal{W}_{\infty}\subset\mathcal{W}^{{\mathsf{ab}}}_{\infty}
can be seen directly from the equivalence above, or,
without using the simplicial description, by the minimality of

\mathcal{W}_{\infty}
among basic localizers
[
10
]
.
∎

4.3
.

Let
u:A\to B
be a functor between small categories. Recall that the functor

u^{*}:{\widehat{B}}\to{\widehat{A}}
is a right adjoint. In particular, it preserves
abelian group objects and induces a functor that we denote

(u^{*})^{\mathsf{ab}}:\widehat{B}^{\mathsf{ab}}\to\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\quad.}

The functor
u^{*}
also admits a right adjoint
u_{*}:{\widehat{A}}\to{\widehat{B}}
,
which, by the same argument, induces a functor

u_{*}^{\mathsf{ab}}:\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}\to\widehat{B}^{\mathsf{ab}}

which is right adjoint to the functor
(u^{*})^{\mathsf{ab}}
.
The two following squares are then commutative up to a natural isomorphism:

(4.3.1)

\textstyle{\widehat{B}^{\mathsf{ab}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\U}
\scriptstyle{(u^{*})^{\mathsf{ab}}}
\textstyle{\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\U}
\textstyle{{\widehat{B}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{u^{*}}
\textstyle{{\widehat{A}}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\quad,}}

\textstyle{\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{u_{*}^{\mathsf{ab}}}
\scriptstyle{\U}
\textstyle{\widehat{B}^{\mathsf{ab}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\U}
\textstyle{{\widehat{A}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{u_{*}}
\textstyle{{\widehat{B}}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\quad.}}

The left adjoint to

, however, does not preserve abelian group objects
in general. But since
\widehat{B}^{\mathsf{ab}}
is a cocomplete additive category, Proposition

allows to extend the functor

5.
Strong Whitehead categories

There are two natural candidates to the role of weak equivalences of
abelian presheaves on a small category: morphisms inducing isomorphisms in homology, and morphisms
whose underlying morphism of presheaves of sets is a weak equivalence. We show
in this section that any test category for which these two classes coincide is a
homologically pseudo-test category, as defined in Definition

2.13
.

Definition 5.1
.

We say that a small category
A
is a
strong Whitehead
category if the
two classes of morphisms
\mathcal{W}^{{\mathsf{ab}}}_{A}
and
\U^{-1}\mathcal{W}_{A}
in
\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}

coincide, where
\U:\widehat{A}^{\mathsf{ab}}\to{\widehat{A}}
denotes the forgetful
functor. This means that for any morphism
f
of abelian presheaves on
A
, we have the
equivalence

f\in\mathcal{W}^{{\mathsf{ab}}}_{A}\iff\U(f)\in\mathcal{W}_{A}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\quad.}

Example 5.2
.

Recall
that for any morphism of simplicial abelian groups

and
any integer

, there is a commutative square

\textstyle{{\pi_{n}(\U X,0)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{n}(\U f)}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{{\pi_{n}(\U Y,0)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{\mathsf{H}_{n}(\Delta,\mathbb{Z}^{(X)})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\mathsf{H}_{n}(\Delta,\mathbb{Z}^{(Y)})}

and similarly in degree

Illusie-Quillen Theorem

Let

Proof.

If

-aspherical by Proposition

\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{W}^{{\mathsf{ab}}}_{B}

6.
Joyal’s category
\Theta

pseudo-test category

We first recall the construction of

, which makes it easy to define an

for every integer

For

whose codomain is a presheaf category and show the analogue of Proposition

previous results from Ara and Maltsiniotis

6.1

Let

be a small category. We denote by
\Delta\wr A
the small
category whose objects are pairs
[\Delta_{n};(a_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}]
where

is a non-negative integer and
(a_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}
is a
family of object of

, and whose morphisms

[\Delta_{n};(a_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}]\to[\Delta_{m};(a^{\prime}_{j})_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}]

are pairs
[\varphi,\mathbf{f}]
where
\varphi:\Delta_{n}\to\Delta_{m}
is a morphism of
\Delta
and

\mathbf{f}=(f_{ji}:a_{i}\to a^{\prime}_{j})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n,\,\varphi(i-1)<j\leqslant\varphi(i)}

is a family of morphisms of
A
. Given two composable morphisms

[\Delta_{n};(a_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}]\xrightarrow{[\varphi,\mathbf{f}]}[\Delta_{n^{\prime}};(a^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}})_{1\leqslant i^{\prime}\leqslant n^{\prime}}]\xrightarrow{[\varphi^{\prime},\mathbf{f^{\prime}}]}[\Delta_{n^{\prime\prime}};(a^{\prime\prime}_{i^{\prime\prime}})_{1\leqslant i^{\prime\prime}\leqslant n^{\prime\prime}}]\text{ ,}\hskip 10.0pt

their composite is defined by the pair
[\varphi^{\prime\prime},\mathbf{f^{\prime\prime}}]

with
\varphi^{\prime\prime}=\varphi^{\prime}\varphi
and

\mathbf{f^{\prime\prime}}=(f^{\prime\prime}_{i^{\prime\prime}i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \varphi^{\prime\prime}(i-1)<i^{\prime\prime}\leqslant\varphi^{\prime\prime}(i)}\text{ ,}\hskip 10.0ptf^{\prime\prime}_{i^{\prime\prime}i}=f^{\prime}_{i^{\prime\prime}i^{\prime}}f_{i^{\prime}i}

\varphi^{\prime}{(i^{\prime}-1)}<i^{\prime\prime}\leqslant\varphi^{\prime}(i^{\prime})

6.2
.

Given a functor
F:A\to B
between small categories, we denote by

\Delta\wr F:\Delta\wr A\to\Delta\wr B

the functor sending objects
[\Delta_{n};(a_{i})_{i}]
of

\Delta\wr A
to
[\Delta_{n};(F(a_{i}))_{i}]
, and
morphisms
[\varphi,\mathbf{f}=(f_{ji})_{i,j}]
to

(\Delta\wr F)[\varphi,\mathbf{f}]=[\varphi,F(\mathbf{f})=\left(F(f_{ji})\right)_{i,j}]\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\quad.}

Note that if
F
is a faithful (resp. fully faithful) functor, then
the same is true for the functor
\Delta\wr F
. In particular, if
a

is an object of
A
, we get a faithful functor

I_{a}:\Delta\wr e\simeq\Delta\xrightarrow{\Delta\wr a}\Delta\wr A\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\quad,}

where we also denoted by

the corresponding functor from the
terminal category

doesn’t admit non-trivial endomorphisms.
This construction is also functorial in

denoted

\mu_{A}:\Delta\times A\to\Delta\wr A\text{ ,}\hskip 10.0pt(\Delta_{n},a)\mapsto I_{a}(\Delta_{n})\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\quad.}

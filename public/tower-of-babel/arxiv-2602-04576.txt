Abstract
. Let 𝒪 ^ \widehat{\mathscr{O}} be a complete local principal ideal ring with residue field k k of characteristic not 2 2 and f ∈ 𝒪 ^ ​ [ x 1 , x 2 , … , x m ] f\in\widehat{\mathscr{O}}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{m}] . Take A ∈ M n ​ ( 𝒪 ^ ) A\in\mathrm{M}_{n}(\widehat{\mathscr{O}}) with its reduction A ¯ ∈ M n ​ ( k ) \overline{A}\in\mathrm{M}_{n}(k) . In this article, we study the following lifting problem. Suppose there exists a tuple ( B ~ 1 , B ~ 2 , … , B ~ m ) ∈ M n ​ ( k ) m (\widetilde{B}_{1},\widetilde{B}_{2},\dots,\widetilde{B}_{m})\in\mathrm{M}_{n}(k)^{m} of pairwise commuting matrices such that f ⁡ ( B ~ 1 , B ~ 2 , … , B ~ m ) = A ¯ f(\widetilde{B}_{1},\widetilde{B}_{2},\dots,\widetilde{B}_{m})=\overline{A} ; under what conditions can this solution be lifted to a tuple ( B 1 , B 2 , … , B m ) ∈ M n ​ ( 𝒪 ^ ) m (B_{1},B_{2},\dots,B_{m})\in\mathrm{M}_{n}(\widehat{\mathscr{O}})^{m} of pairwise commuting matrices satisfying f ⁡ ( B 1 , B 2 , … , B m ) = A f(B_{1},B_{2},\dots,B_{m})=A ? 1.
Introduction

Lifting phenomena occur across many areas of mathematics, such as algebraic geometry, number theory, and representation theory.
For example, the Oort conjecture concerns the lifting of a smooth curve in characteristic
p
, together with its group of automorphisms, to a curve, along with the automorphisms, to characteristic zero; see, for example,
[
16
,
22
]
.
Another instance arises in the lifting of packets of representations of
G(k)
to packets for
\widetilde{G}(k)
, where
\widetilde{G}
is a connected reductive group over a finite field
k
, and
G
denotes the connected component of the group of
\varepsilon
-fixed points of
\widetilde{G}
. Here
\varepsilon
is a semisimple
k
-automorphism of
\widetilde{G}
of finite order; see, for example,

[
2
,
1
,
3
]
.
The lifting of a two-dimensional continuous representation

\overline{\rho}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(k)

of the absolute Galois group of
\mathbb{Q}
to a representation

\rho\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}\bigl(W(k)\bigr)

that is unramified outside a finite set of primes has been studied in
[
23
]
, under the assumption that
\overline{\rho}
is odd and absolutely irreducible. Here
k
is a finite field of characteristic
p
, and
W(k)
denotes its ring of Witt vectors.

The classical Hensel’s Lemma concerns lifting a factorization modulo a prime number
p
of a polynomial
f
over the
\mathbb{Z}
to a factorization modulo
p^{m}
for any integer
m\geq 1
, and to a factorization over the
p
-adic integers. This can be generalized easily to the case where the integers are replaced by any commutative ring, the prime number is replaced by a maximal ideal, and the
p
-adic integers are replaced by the completion with respect to the maximal ideal. However, lifting a solution for equations over a non-commutative ring remains relatively unexplored, mainly because it may not always have a positive solution. Take, for example, the ring
\mathscr{O}=\mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z}
and

A=\begin{pmatrix}5&0\\
0&2\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathscr{O}),\,L=2.

Letting
\Phi_{2}:\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathscr{O}_{2})\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathscr{O}_{2})
, the squaring map, we have

\displaystyle\overline{\Phi}_{2}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
2&0\end{pmatrix}\right)=\overline{A}\in\mathrm{M}_{2}(k),

and

\displaystyle\overline{\Phi}_{2}^{-1}(\overline{A})=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
2&0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
1&2\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\
2&2\end{pmatrix}\right\}.

However, there is no
B\in\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathscr{O})
such that
B^{2}=A
; see
[
18
, Example 6.1]
.
Our motivation for investigating the lifting problem for solutions of polynomial equations on matrix algebra over a field to matrix algebra over a complete local principal ideal ring comes from the growing interest in understanding polynomial maps on algebras (for example
[
13
,
7
,
6
]
) and various conjectures in the subject. Let us briefly recall the concept of polynomial maps on algebras here; let
\mathscr{R}
be a ring with unity, then given an
\mathscr{R}
-algebra
\mathscr{A}
, and an element
f\in\mathscr{R}[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]
(the free
\mathscr{R}
-algebra on
n
generators), one gets a map

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}\colon\mathscr{A}^{n}\rightarrow\mathscr{A},\,(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})\mapsto f(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}),

known as
polynomial map
.
In recent years, many interesting results have been proven in this area. As the literature is rapidly expanding, we mention only a few representative instances, while acknowledging that several fundamental contributions remain unmentioned.

A positive solution to the L’vov–Kaplansky conjecture for
\mathrm{M}_{2}(k)
, where
k
is a quadratically closed field, was obtained in
[
13
]
.
The images of general polynomials on upper triangular matrices were determined in
[
11
,
17
]
.
The Waring problem for matrices has been studied in

[
5
,
4
,
14
,
20
]
.
A surprising connection between commutators in matrix rings and images of noncommutative polynomials on matrix rings was established in
[
7
]
.
More general investigations involving rational functions were introduced in
[
6
]
.
Finally, polynomial maps with constants taken from the algebra itself were studied in

[
19

21

1.1.
Notations

Let us fix some notation.
A commutative ring
\mathscr{R}
with unity is said to be
complete with respect to an ideal
I
if the canonical map

\mathscr{R}\longrightarrow\varprojlim_{j\geq 1}\mathscr{R}/I^{j}\mathscr{R}

is an isomorphism.
We denote by
\widehat{\mathscr{O}}
a local principal ideal ring that is complete with respect to its unique maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}=(\pi)
.
Its residue field
k
has characteristic not equal to
2
.
For
\ell\geq 1
, the notation
\mathscr{O}_{\ell}
is reserved for the ring

\mathscr{O}_{\ell}=\widehat{\mathscr{O}}/\pi^{\ell}\widehat{\mathscr{O}}
.
This is a local principal ideal ring of length
\ell
with unique maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}=(\pi_{\ell})
, where
\pi_{\ell}=\pi+(\pi^{\ell})
.
The canonical map
\mathscr{O}_{\ell+1}\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{\ell}
is denoted by
\theta_{\ell}
.
The natural surjection
\widehat{\mathscr{O}}\rightarrow\mathscr{O}_{\ell}
is denoted by
\widehat{\theta}_{\ell}
.
If
\widehat{\theta}_{\ell+1}(a)=a_{\ell+1}
for some
a\in\widehat{\mathscr{O}}
, then we write

a_{\ell}:=\theta_{\ell}(a_{\ell+1})\in\mathscr{O}_{\ell}
.
Thus,

\theta_{\ell}\circ\widehat{\theta}_{\ell+1}=\widehat{\theta}_{\ell}.

From now on, all reductions from
\widehat{\mathscr{O}}
to the level
\mathscr{O}_{\ell}
—for elements, matrices, polynomials, and so on—will be denoted uniformly by
\widehat{\theta}_{\ell}
.
For instance, if
H(t)\in\widehat{\mathscr{O}}[t]
, then

\widehat{\theta}_{\ell}(H(t))=H_{\ell}(t).

In particular,
H_{1}(t)
is denoted by
\overline{H}(t)
to emphasize that it is a polynomial with coefficients in the residue field
k
.
We adopt this notation throughout.
Also by abuse of notations,
\theta
denotes the map to the field
k
from the local ring
\mathscr{O}_{\ell}
for any
\ell
.
An analogous convention applies to matrices; for example,
\overline{X}=X_{1}
.
For a commutative ring
\mathscr{R}
with unity and a matrix
A\in\mathrm{M}_{n}(\mathscr{R})
, we denote by
\mathscr{Z}_{\mathrm{M}_{n}(\mathscr{R})}(A)
the centralizer algebra of
A
.
For a matrix
A\in\mathrm{M}_{n}(\mathscr{R})
, we define
N_{A}^{\mathscr{R}}=\{F(t)\in\mathscr{R}[t]:F(A)=0\}
, the
null ideal of
A
; see
[
18
, Section 3.1]
for further details.
A matrix
A\in\mathrm{M}_{n}(\mathscr{R})
is called
cyclic
if
\overline{A}\in\mathrm{M}_{n}(k)
is cyclic.
Recall that a matrix
X\in\mathrm{M}_{n}(k)
is cyclic if the
k[t]
module
k^{n}
via the action of
X
is a cyclic module. One also has the following: if
A\in\mathrm{M}_{n}(\mathscr{O})
be such that
\overline{A}\in\mathrm{M}_{n}(k)
is cyclic, the
\mathscr{O}[t]
module
\mathscr{O}^{n}
via the action of
A
is a cyclic module as well; see
[
18
, Lemma 5.2]
.

2.
Lifting solutions of polynomial maps

In the article
[
18
]
, the authors produced a strategy to lift a solution of
X^{L}=\bar{A}
over residue field level to the solution of the given equation
X^{L}=A
over local ring level for
\gcd(L,p)=1
; where
p
is the characteristic of the residue field which is an odd prime, and
A
is cyclic elements of
\mathrm{M}_{n}(\mathscr{O}_{2})
.
They used a single variable monic polynomial evaluated at a matrix variable with coefficients from
\mathscr{O}_{2}
and proved the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mathscr{O}_{2}
be a finite local principal ideal ring of length two with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}=\langle\pi\rangle
and residue field
k
of characteristic
p>2
.
Let
A\in\mathrm{M}_{n}(\mathscr{O}_{2})
be cyclic, and let
F(t)\in\mathscr{O}_{2}[t]
be monic of degree
d
. Suppose there exists
\widetilde{B}\in\mathrm{M}_{n}(k)
such that
\overline{F}(\widetilde{B})=\overline{A}
and

\overline{F}^{\prime}(\widetilde{B})\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
where
F^{\prime}(t)
is the formal derivative of
F(t)
in
\mathscr{O}_{2}[t]
.
Then there exists
B\in\mathrm{M}_{n}(\mathscr{O}_{2})
with
\overline{B}=\widetilde{B}
and
F(B)=A
.

This gives a general setup for lifting solutions of such matrix equations from the residue field to the length two level.
But now we want to see it in some bigger context.
First, let us investigate for a given matrix equation
F(X)=A
;

be cyclic; whether a solution of the reduced equation from length two level (if exists) can be lifted to length
3

.
We start with
\mathscr{O}_{\ell}=\mathbb{Z}/p^{\ell}\mathbb{Z}
; where
p
is an odd prime.
Note that the ring of
p

\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\varprojlim\mathbb{Z}/p^{\ell}\mathbb{Z}
.
In the construction of the lifting strategy, the null ideal played a crucial role.
Therefore, let us prove the results regarding null ideals and uniqueness of minimal polynomials for a cyclic element

Over any commutative ring

in

Remark 2.5

(A few words on word problems on groups)
.

The word problems on group is well-known; given an element

and a group

known as

.
Influential works include the settlement of the Ore’s conjecture and that of the width of a word on finite simple groups; see
[
15
,
24
]
.
In the context of lifting solution, one has the result for the commutator map on
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathscr{O})
in
[
AvniShalev13
]
.

Theorem

2
also shows that if one starts with a word

, having no inverse in its reduced form, then lifting of a solution to the equation

for a cyclic matrix

exists whenever a pairwise commuting solution exists for the equation
\widetilde{w}(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{r})=\overline{A}
, an equation in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
; indeed
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathscr{O})\subseteq\mathrm{M}_{n}(\mathscr{O})
and

iff

.

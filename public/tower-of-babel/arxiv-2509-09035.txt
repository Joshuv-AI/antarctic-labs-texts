Asymptotic structure. III. Excluding a fat tree
Tung Nguyen, Alex Scott, Paul Seymour

Abstract
Robertson and Seymour proved that for every finite tree H H , there exists k k such that every finite graph G G with no H H minor has path-width at most k k ; and conversely, for every integer k k , there is a finite tree H H such that every finite graph G G with an H H minor has path-width more than k k . If we (twice) replace “path-width” by “line-width”, the same is true for infinite graphs G G . We prove a “coarse graph theory” analogue, as follows. For every finite tree H H and every c c , there exist k , L , C k,L,C such that every graph that does not contain H H as a c c -fat minor admits an ( L , C ) (L,C) -quasi-isonetry to a graph with line-width at most k k ; and conversely, for all k , L , C k,L,C there exist c c and a finite tree H H such that every graph that contains H H as a c c -fat minor admits no ( L , C ) (L,C) -quasi-isometry to a graph with line-width at most k k .

In the proof of

Every

(The point of the tie-breaker is that there is only

-geodesic between any two vertices, while this is not true for geodesics.)
It is easy to check that

of other objects on the choice of

.

A

such that

is connected.
If

is a set of pairwise vertex-disjoint buildings, in a graph

.
Again, let
\mathcal{T}
be a set of pairwise vertex-disjoint buildings, in a graph

, now with a tiebreaker
\Lambda
.
We say that

is
\Lambda
-closest

between

\Lambda
-shorter
than any other path between

and

.
For each

, let

(or just
\operatorname{\Delta}(X)
, when

is clear) be the set of all

such that

is

-closest to

. We call

of

defined by

. (It is indeed a partition, provided that each component of

includes a member of

; and this is true for us since we will always assume that

is connected and

is non-null.)
The main purpose of the tie-breaker is to make the Voronoi partition defined by

well-defined.
We see that:

•

X\subseteq\operatorname{\Delta}(X)
for each
X\in\mathcal{T}
;

•

the sets

are pairwise disjoint and have union

;

•

for each

and each

,

for each
Y\in\mathcal{T}
, and there is
a path of

between

and

of length
\operatorname{dist}_{G}(v,X)
.

We say that distinct sets

if either

or there is an edge between
X
and
Y
.
If
\mathcal{T}
is a set of pairwise vertex-disjoint buildings, and

are distinct, we say that

adjoins

if
\operatorname{\Delta}_{\mathcal{T}}(X)
touches
\operatorname{\Delta}_{\mathcal{T}}(Y)
. A set

is

if for every partition of

into two nonempty sets

.

4
Realms

Let

We claim:

4.1

Let

k
th-century society in

.
Then there is a
k
th-century realm in

Proof.
Let us say a

of another,
\mathcal{T}_{1}

\mathcal{T}

(1)
\operatorname{dist}_{G}(v,\operatorname{bd}(\operatorname{\Delta}(X)))\leq d_{0}
for every house

Let

Our strategy to prove

•

The use of optimality is the following:

, where

6
Passages

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a
k
th-century realm, in the usual notation.
A

is an induced path

of
G

with the following properties:

•

there exist distinct
X_{1},X_{2}\in\mathcal{T}
, each either a house or fort of
\mathcal{T}
, such that one end of

is in
X_{1}
and the other in
X_{2}

(and consequently

has length more than
\delta(2k)\geq 5c
);

•

no internal vertex of

;

•

\operatorname{dist}_{G}(P,Y)>\delta(k+1+\operatorname{rk}(Y))
for each

with

;

•

for

, let

be the subpath of

We say that

, and

.
We will only need passages of bounded length.

6.1

Let

\mathcal{T}
be an optimal

•

for

,
P_{i}
is incident with
X_{i}
, and none of
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m_{3}}
is incident with

except

; and

•

Proof.
Suppose that such

Let

•

-leg on

•

, and

\operatorname{bd}(Z)

(8\alpha,\beta+(\ell-k)d_{0}))

\operatorname{dist}_{G}(W,X^{\prime})>\delta(k+1+\operatorname{rk}(X^{\prime}))

The first is clear, and the second and fourth hold from the definition of a passage. To see the third, let

Since each of

).
Since the boundary of each

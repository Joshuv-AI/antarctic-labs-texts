A Borel–Weil–Bott theorem for Quot schemes on ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1}
Ajay Gautam, Feiyang Lin, Shubham Sinha

Abstract
. We study the cohomology groups of tautological bundles on Quot schemes over the projective line, which parametrize rank r r quotients of a vector bundle V V on ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} . Our main result is an analogue of the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem for Quot schemes. As a corollary, we prove recent conjectures of Marian, Oprea, and Sam on the exterior and symmetric powers of tautological bundles.



1.
Introduction

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve and
V
a vector bundle of rank
n
on
C
. Let
\quot_{d}(C,V,r)
denote the Quot scheme that parameterizes short exact sequences of sheaves

0\to S\to V\to Q\to 0\quad\text{with }\deg Q=d,\ \mathrm{rank}\,Q=r.

Consider the universal exact sequence over the product
\quot_{d}(C,V,r)\times C
,

0\to\mathcal{S}\to p^{*}V\to\mathcal{Q}\to 0

(1)

where
p
and
\pi
denote the projection maps to
C
and
\quot_{d}(C,V,r)
, respectively. Given vector bundles
K
and
M
on
C
, the associated tautological complexes on
\quot_{d}(C,V,r)
are defined as the pushforwards

K^{\{d\}}:=R\pi_{*}\!\left(p^{*}K\otimes\mathcal{S}\right),\qquad M^{[d]}:=R\pi_{*}\!\left(p^{*}M\otimes\mathcal{Q}\right).

When
K^{\{d\}}
and
M^{[d]}
are vector bundles, which occurs when the bundles
K,M
are sufficiently positive, we call them tautological bundles.

The study of the Quot scheme
\quot_{d}(C,V,r)
comes in two distinct flavors. When
r=0
, i.e. punctual Quot schemes,
\quot_{d}(C,V,0)
is a smooth projective variety of dimension
nd
, and
L^{[d]}
is a vector bundle of rank
d
for all line bundles
L
on
C
. In this case, the geometry of the Quot scheme has close analogies with the Hilbert scheme of points on surfaces, manifesting, for instance, in the cohomological behavior of tautological bundles. A closed formula for the Euler characteristic of the exterior powers of
L^{[d]}
was computed in
[
27
]
, and a description of their cohomology groups was conjectured. This conjecture was proved in
[
24
]
for
C\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
. For arbitrary genus, it was partially proved in
[
19
]
, and fully resolved in
[
23
]
by studying the derived category of the Quot scheme.

When
r>0
, many of the techniques available in the punctual case no longer apply, and not much is known about the cohomology of tautological bundles, except for some Euler characteristic calculations in
[
27
,
32
,
33
]
. In this article, we study the cohomology of tautological complexes on
\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},V,r)
for any vector bundle
V
and any
r
. Our theorems may be viewed as Borel–Weil–Bott–type results for Quot schemes on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
in direct analogy with the classical theorem for Grassmannians.

1.1.
Cohomology of tautological bundles

We begin by fixing notation. For a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})
, let
|\lambda|
denote the sum of its parts, and let
\lambda^{\dagger}
be its conjugate partition. We write
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}
for the Schur functor associated to
\lambda
; for example,

\mathbb{S}^{(\ell)}V=\Sym^{\ell}V\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{S}^{(1)^{\ell}}V=\wedge^{\ell}V,

where
(1)^{\ell}=(1,\dots,1)
with
\ell
parts.

Let
V
be a vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
of rank
n
; fix
r<n
. By
[
29
]
, there exists an integer

d_{0}=d_{0}(V,r)
such that for all
d\geq d_{0}
, the Quot scheme

\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},V,r)\quad(\text{abbreviated }\quot_{d})

is irreducible and generically smooth of dimension
nd+rb+r(n-r)
, where
b=-\deg V
.
In the special case
V=\cO_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus n}
, one may take
d_{0}=0
and
\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},V,r)
is smooth and irreducible for all
d\geq 0
. In the remainder of the introduction, we assume
d\geq d_{0}(V,r)
.

We now state our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K
and
M
be vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and let
\mu,\lambda
be partitions satisfying

|\mu|+|\lambda|\;<\;\frac{nd+rb+n}{\,n-r\,}.

(i)

If
\mu\neq\emptyset
, then

H^{i}\bigl(\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},V,r),\,\mathbb{S}^{\mu}K^{\{d\}}\otimes\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}M^{[d]}\bigr)=0\qquad\text{for all }i\geq 0.

(ii)

If
\mu=\emptyset
, there is a natural isomorphism of graded vector spaces

H^{\bullet}\!\bigl(\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},V,r),\,\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}M^{[d]}\bigr)\;\cong\;\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}H^{\bullet}(V\otimes M).

Remark 1.2
.

When
M
splits as a direct sum of line bundles of sufficiently positive degrees, the complex
M^{[d]}
, and hence
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}M^{[d]}
, is a vector bundle. The isomorphism in part (ii) of Theorem
1.1

H^{0}(\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},V,r),\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}M^{[d]})\cong\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}H^{0}(V\otimes M)

is induced geometrically, by considering the morphism

R\pi_{*}p^{*}(V\otimes M)\;\longrightarrow\;M^{[d]}

arising from the universal sequence (
1
), taking the associated morphism of the Schur functor and then applying the global sections functor.

Theorem
1.1
greatly generalizes the results in
[
24
]
, even for punctual Quot schemes, and in the positive-rank setting, proves
[
24
, Conjecture 1.3.1, 1.3.2]
concerning the exterior and symmetric powers of the tautological bundles. Specifically,

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
L
be a line bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. For all

k<(nd+rb+n)/(n-r)
, we have the following isomorphisms of graded vector spaces

(i)

H^{\bullet}\!\left(\quot_{d},\wedge^{k}L^{[d]}\right)\cong\wedge^{k}H^{\bullet}(V\otimes L)
;

(ii)

H^{\bullet}\!\left(\quot_{d},\Sym^{k}L^{[d]}\right)\cong\Sym^{k}H^{\bullet}(V\otimes L).

Remark 1.4
.

In Theorem
5.5
, we prove a more general result analogous to Theorem
1.1
, for the tensor product of Schur functors applied to multiple vector bundles
K_{1},K_{2},\dots,K_{s}
and
M_{1},M_{2},\dots,M_{t}
. Note that while Theorem
5.5
is stated for line bundles, it is easy to upgrade the theorem to allow vector bundles since every vector bundle splits on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
Below, we state the result in the case of trivial
V
, describing each cohomology group explicitly.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{t}
be line bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. For any tuple of partitions
\lambda^{1},\lambda^{2},\ldots,\lambda^{t}
satisfying
\lvert\lambda^{1}\rvert+\lvert\lambda^{2}\rvert+\cdots+\lvert\lambda^{t}\rvert<(nd+n)/(n-r)
, we have

H^{D}\left(\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\cO_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus n},r),\bigotimes_{j=1}^{t}\mathbb{S}^{\lambda^{j}}L_{j}^{[d]}\right)\cong\bigotimes_{\deg L_{j}<0}\mathbb{S}^{(\lambda^{j})^{\dagger}}H^{1}(L_{j}^{\oplus n})\otimes\bigotimes_{\deg L_{j}\geq 0}\mathbb{S}^{\lambda^{j}}H^{0}(L_{j}^{\oplus n}),

where
D=\sum_{\deg L_{j}<0}|\lambda^{j}|
, and all other cohomology groups are zero.

Fixing the partitions and line bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we may view the corresponding tautological bundles on
\quot_{d}
for any
d
, and study the asymptotic behavior of their cohomology groups as
d
becomes large. With this perspective, we can view Theorem
1.1
as a stabilization result. In particular, as predicted in
[
24
]
,

Corollary 1.6
.

For line bundles
L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{t}
and partitions
\lambda^{1},\lambda^{2},\ldots,\lambda^{t}

\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}q^{d}\chi\left(\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\cO_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus n},r),\bigotimes_{j=1}^{t}\mathbb{S}^{\lambda^{j}}L_{j}^{[d]}\right)

is given by a rational function with simple pole only possibly at
q=1
.

The vanishing of higher cohomology in Corollary
1.5
for line bundles of nonnegative degree can be established without any restriction on the sizes of the partitions, at the expense of imposing degree constraints on the line bundles involved. The next result is analogous to
[
20
, Theorem 3]
; see Theorem
5.7
for general
V
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{t}
be line bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degrees at least
d
. For any tuple of partitions
\lambda^{1},\lambda^{2},\ldots,\lambda^{t}
, we have

H^{i}\left(\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\cO_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus n},r),\bigotimes_{j=1}^{t}\mathbb{S}^{\lambda^{j}}L_{j}^{[d]}\right)=0\quad\text{for all }i\geq 1.

The space of global sections in Theorem
1.7
does not, in general, follow the description given in Corollary
1.5
. The size constraint on the partition
\lambda
appearing in Theorem
1.1
is quite close to optimal; see
[
27
, Theorem 12]
and Example
5.8
. Theorem
1.7
implies that the dimension of the space of global sections is equal to the Euler characteristic. The latter can be computed by other means, such as torus localization (see
[
27
, Theorem 3]
and
[
33
, Corollary 4.10]
for exterior powers) or via wall-crossing to other moduli spaces
[
14
]
.

1.2.
Extension groups

When the degree of a line bundle
L
is sufficiently large, our method also allows the computation of the global extension groups for Schur functors of the vector bundle
L^{[d]}
. The answer is expressed in terms of Ext-groups of Schur functors of the universal quotient bundle on a Grassmannian (see Proposition
4.6
). The Borel–Weil–Bott theorem for the Grassmannian completely determines these Ext-groups and has the following implications.

Theorem 1.8
.

Assume
V
splits as a direct sum of line bundles of nonpositive degrees. Let
L
be a line bundle of degree at least
d+b
. Then

(i)

For any nontrivial partition
\nu
with
|\nu|<(nd+rb+n)/r
and
\nu_{1}<n-r
,

\displaystyle H^{i}\left(\quot_{d},(\mathbb{S}^{\nu}L^{[d]})^{\vee}\right)=0\quad\text{for all }i\geq 0.

(ii)

Let
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{t}
and
k
be nonnegative integers, and set
|p|=p_{1}+p_{2}+\cdots+p_{t}
.
Assume
k<(nd+rb+n)/(n-r)
,
|p|\leq(nd+rb+n)/r
, and
t<n-r
. Then

\Ext^{i}\left(\bigwedge^{p_{1}}L^{[d]}\otimes\cdots\otimes\bigwedge^{p_{t}}L^{[d]},\bigwedge^{k}L^{[d]}\right)=\begin{cases}\bigwedge^{k-|p|}L^{[d]}&i=0,\ k\geq|p|;\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

A similar statement holds upon replacing all
\bigwedge
with
\Sym
, and replacing the condition
t<n-r
with
|p|<n-r
.

(iii)

For any partition
\lambda
, with
|\lambda|\leq d
and
\lambda_{1}<n-r
,

\Ext^{i}(\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}L^{[d]},\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}L^{[d]})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}&i=0;\\
0&i\geq 1.\end{cases}

(iv)

More generally, the set

\{\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(L^{[d]}):\ |\lambda|\leq d,\ \lambda_{1}<n-r\}

forms an exceptional collection, ordered by the sizes of the partitions.

Remark 1.9
.

Note that the exceptional collection above generates only a subcategory of the derived category of coherent sheaves on
\quot_{d}
. A full exceptional collection built from Schur functors of universal quotients on Grassmannian was studied by Kapranov
[
16
]
. For Quot schemes of rank-zero quotients, a full exceptional collection (and, in higher genus, a semiorthogonal decomposition) of the derived category was obtained in
[
35
]
and
[
23
]
. However, these methods do not directly extend to the case of higher-rank quotients.

Part (ii) of Theorem
1.8
recovers the results on extension groups in
[
24
]
for Quot schemes of rank-zero quotients, and partially proves
[
24
, Conjecture 1.3.3]
in the higher-rank setting. Moreover, in Theorem
4.1
, we establish a mixed version of Proposition
4.6
and Theorem
1.1
for two line bundles of consecutive degrees; this may be regarded as our main technical result. We state a consequence for the duals of tautological bundles below.

Theorem 1.10
.

Assume
V
splits as a direct sum of line bundles of nonpositive degrees. Let
L_{m-1}=\cO_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(m-1)
and
L_{m}=\cO_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(m)
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, with
m\geq d+b
. For any pair of partitions
\nu
and
\mu
satisfying

\lvert\nu\rvert+\lvert\mu\rvert\leq(nd+rb+n)/r\quad\text{and}\quad 0<\nu_{1}+\mu_{1}<n-r,

all cohomology groups of the vector bundle

\bigl(\mathbb{S}^{\nu}L_{m-1}^{[d]}\bigr)^{\vee}\otimes\bigl(\mathbb{S}^{\mu}L_{m}^{[d]}\bigr)^{\vee}

vanish.

1.3.
Relation to quantum
K
-theory of Grassmannians

When
V
is the trivial vector bundle of rank
n
, the Quot scheme
\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},V,r)
is a smooth moduli space (see
[
34
]
) that compactifies the space of morphisms from
\mathbb{P}^{1}
to the Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}(r,n)
of degree
d
. This viewpoint was taken up by Bertram
[
5
]
to study the quantum cohomology ring of
\mathrm{Gr}(r,n)
, and later to prove the Vafa–Intriligator formula
[
4
,
25
]
for intersection numbers on
\quot_{d}(C,V,r)
, which counts the number of maps from a fixed curve
C
of genus
g
to
\mathrm{Gr}(r,n)
satisfying some incidence conditions.

Recently,
[
32
,
33
]
studied
K
-theoretic invariants on
\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},V,r)
in the context of the quantum
K
-ring of
\mathrm{Gr}(r,n)
(cf.
[
9
]
). Fix a point
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
and set

\mathcal{S}_{x}:=\mathcal{S}|_{\quot_{d}\times\{x\}},

a rank
n-r
vector bundle on
\quot_{d}
. By
[
32
, Theorem 1.12]
, the Euler characteristics of Schur functors of
\mathcal{S}_{x}
compute
K
-theoretic Gromov–Witten invariants: For partitions
\mu^{1},\mu^{2},\mu^{3}
whose first parts are at most
r
,

\chi\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(\mathrm{Gr}(r,n),d),\bigotimes_{i\leq 3}ev_{p_{i}}^{*}\mathbb{S}^{\mu^{i}}(\mathcal{B})\right)=\chi\left(\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\cO_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus n},r),\bigotimes_{i\leq 3}\mathbb{S}^{\mu^{i}}(\mathcal{S}_{x})\right)

(2)

Here
\mathcal{B}
denotes the universal quotient bundle on
\mathrm{Gr}(r,n)
, and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(\mathrm{Gr}(r,n),d)
is the moduli space of
3
-pointed genus-
0
stable maps to
\mathrm{Gr}(r,n)
of degree
d
with
ev_{p_{i}}
the evaluation map at the
i
-th marking. In this paper, we prove the following consequence of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
\mu^{1},\mu^{2},\dots\mu^{t}
be partitions satisfying
0\neq|\mu^{1}|+\cdots+|\mu^{t}|<(nd+n)/(n-r)
, then

H^{i}\left(\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\cO_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus n},r),\bigotimes_{j=1}^{t}\mathbb{S}^{\mu^{j}}\mathcal{S}_{x}\right)=0\qquad\text{for all }i\geq 0.

The Euler characteristics of Schur functors of
\mathcal{S}_{x}
were computed in
[
33
, Theorem 1.6 and 1.9]
. The vanishing of Euler characteristics of these bundles was shown to provide enough relations to completely determine the quantum
K
-ring of
\mathrm{Gr}(r,n)
. Theorem
1.11
partially answers
[
33
, Question 1.11]
by upgrading a subset of the vanishing statements for Euler characteristics to statements about individual cohomology groups. Using our results, one can already prove that the
K
-theoretic Gromov–Witten invariant in (
2
) vanishes for all sufficiently large degrees
d
. We expect that our methods may in future shed light on positivity phenomena in the quantum
K
-theory of the Grassmannian; see, for instance,
[
9
,
8
]
.

1.4.
Analogy with Hilbert scheme of points on surfaces

Let
V
be a vector bundle on a smooth projective curve
C
. The punctual Quot scheme, denoted
\quot_{d}(C,V)=\quot_{d}(C,V,0)
, parametrizes quotients of
V
supported at zero dimensional scheme of length
d
. Punctual Quot schemes have been extensively studied; we mention only a few relevant works here, including computations of cohomology groups
[
22
]
, descriptions of nef cones for divisors
[
13
]
, positivity results for tautological bundles
[
28
]
, and cohomology of tangent bundle
[
6
]
.

For a line bundle
L
on
C
, the description of the cohomology groups for the tautological bundle
L^{[d]}
and their exterior powers is explicitly given by the formula (see
[
23
, Theorem 3]
for the statement involving extension groups)

H^{\bullet}(\quot_{d}(C,V),\wedge^{k}L^{[d]})\cong\wedge^{k}H^{\bullet}(V\otimes L)\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{d-k}H^{\bullet}(\cO_{C}).

There is a parallel story for the Hilbert schemes
X^{[d]}
of
d
points on a smooth projective surface
X
. For a line bundle
L
on
X
, the tautological bundle
L^{[d]}
, defined similarly, has rank
d
. Indeed, the cohomology groups are given by the formula
[
30
,
18
]

H^{\bullet}(X^{[d]},\wedge^{k}L^{[d]})\cong\wedge^{k}H^{\bullet}(L)\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{d-k}H^{\bullet}(\cO_{X}).

The proofs of the above statement in both cases rely on studying the bounded derived categories of sheaves
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X^{[d]})
and
\mathbf{D}^{b}(\quot_{d}(C,V))
.

The description of cohomology groups of the symmetric powers
\Sym^{k}L^{[d]}
, and general Schur functors, is not known to admit a simple formula, for both
X^{[d]}
and
\quot_{d}(C,V)
. In special case, Euler characteristics
[
3
]
and the space of global sections
[
10
,
31
]
of the symmetric products of tautological bundles
L^{[d]}
on
X^{[d]}
admits a simple form. For instance, let
L
be a positive degree line bundle on
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}
, then for all
k<d+1
,

H^{0}\left(X^{[d]},\mathrm{Sym}^{k}\ L^{[d]}\right)=\Sym^{k}H^{0}(L)

while all higher cohomology groups vanish. We predict that an anolgue of formulas in Corollary
1.5
also hold for Hilbert scheme of points on
X\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}
. We note here that tautological bundles on the Quot schemes on surfaces have also been studied in
[
26
,
2
]
.

1.5.
Proof strategy

For a fixed sufficiently large integer
m
, the Quot scheme on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
admits an embedding into a product of Grassmannians, constructed by Strømme
[
34
]
,

\iota_{m}:\ \quot_{d}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Gr}(k_{1},N_{1})\times\mathrm{Gr}(k_{2},N_{2}),

as the zero locus of a section of a vector bundle
\mathcal{K}
, expressed in terms of the universal bundles on the two Grassmannians. In the rank-zero setting,
[
24
]
uses the Koszul resolution of
\cO_{\quot_{d}}
to reduce the study of the cohomology of exterior and symmetric powers of
L_{m}^{[d]}
to the cohomology of universal bundles on the product of Grassmannians, where the Borel-Weil-Bott theorem applies.

We follow the approach of
[
24
]
to study the case of arbitrary rank, but the combinatorial analysis of the vector bundles that show up in the Koszul resolution becomes rather involved. To address this combinatorial proliferation, in Section
3
we provide a streamlined combinatorial criterion for detecting the non-vanishing of the cohomology of universal bundles on a Grassmannian, which may be of independent interest. A second ingredient is provided by Horn’s inequalities, which give criteria for the nonvanishing of the Littlewood–Richardson coefficients appearing in our expansions. By applying these two sets of criteria carefully, we show that all cohomology groups vanish for every term in the Koszul resolution except the first. We then conclude by computing the cohomology of the first term explicitly.

Our methods compute the cohomology of all Schur functors (subject to the relevant size constraints) of the tautological bundles associated to consecutive-degree line bundles,
L_{m}^{[d]}
and
L_{m-1}^{[d]}
(new even in the rank-zero case). This is especially advantageous because Schur functors of any tautological complex
M^{[d]}
can be expressed in terms of the Schur functors of the tautological bundles
L_{m-1}^{[d]}
and
L_{m}^{[d]}
in the derived category of sheaves on
\quot_{d}
. We then leverage the explicit results about
\mathbb{S}^{\alpha}L_{m-1}^{[d]}\,\otimes\,\mathbb{S}^{\beta}L_{m}^{[d]}
to obtain results for the Schur complexes
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}M^{[d]}
, as well as their tensor products.

Assumptions on characteristic.
Our results are valid for all algebraically closed fields of characteristic zero. For ease of notation, we use
\mathbb{C}
throughout. The main dependence on the characteristic comes from the use of the Borel-Weil-Bott theorem for Grassmannians. The fact that Schur functors of complexes respect quasi-isomorphisms, and therefore are well-defined on objects in the derived category, also depends on the characteristic zero assumption.

k

, is shown below:

2.2.
Schur functors

Let
W\cong\mathbb{C}^{k}
be a complex vector space. For any partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{k})
, we denote by
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(W)
the Schur functor, which corresponds to the irreducible polynomial
\mathrm{GL}(W)
-representation with highest weight
\lambda
. The character of the representation
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(W)
is the Schur polynomial
s_{\lambda}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k})
, and its dimension is given by the hook-content formula:

\dim\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(W)=s_{\lambda}(\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1}_{k})=\prod_{c\in\lambda}\frac{k+\mathrm{cont(c)}}{\mathrm{hook(c)}}

where for each cell
c=(i,j)
in
\lambda
is in the
i
th row and
j
th column,
\mathrm{cont}(c)=j-i
and
\mathrm{hook}(c)=1+(\lambda_{i}-j)+(\lambda_{j}^{\dagger}-i)
is the hook length of
c
.

Every polynomial
\mathrm{GL}(W)
-representation can be expressed uniquely as a direct sum of Schur functors. Given two partitions
\lambda
and
\mu
, one can decompose the tensor product as

\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(W)\otimes\mathbb{S}^{\mu}(W)=\bigoplus_{\gamma}\mathbb{S}^{\gamma}(W)^{\oplus c_{\lambda,\mu}^{\gamma}},

(3)

where the multiplicities
c_{\lambda,\mu}^{\gamma}
are the
Littlewood–Richardson coefficients
.
The
Littlewood–Richardson rule
provides a combinatorial description of these coefficients in terms of counting Littlewood–Richardson tableaux. In the next subsection, we list the properties of Littlewood–Richardson coefficients that we shall need in this article.

Let
V
be another complex vector space. Schur functors naturally appear in the context of
Cauchy’s formula
: for any non-negative integer
t
, there is an isomorphism of vector spaces

\bigwedge\nolimits^{t}(V\otimes W)\cong\bigoplus_{\lvert\mu\rvert=t}\mathbb{S}^{\mu}(V)\otimes\mathbb{S}^{\mu^{\dagger}}(W),

(4)

where the direct sum runs over all partitions
\mu
of size
t
.

We will also require the following decomposition formula for the Schur functor of the direct sum of two vector spaces

\mathbb{S}^{\gamma}(V\oplus W)\cong\bigoplus_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\mathbb{S}^{\alpha}(V)\otimes\mathbb{S}^{\beta}(W)\right)^{\oplus c_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}}.

(5)

Here the direct sum is taken over all partitions
\alpha
and
\beta
such that
|\alpha|+|\beta|=|\gamma|
.

2.3.
Schur functors associated to highest weights

Irreducible rational representations of
\mathrm{GL}(W)
are indexed by highest weights

\eta=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\dots,\eta_{k}),

which are non-increasing sequences of integers
\eta_{1}\geq\eta_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\eta_{k}
. Note that each
\eta_{i}
is allowed to be negative.
For any highest weight
\eta
, we denote the corresponding irreducible
\mathrm{GL}(W)
-representation by
\mathbb{S}^{\eta}(W)
. For any
m\geq-\eta_{k}
, we can express
\mathbb{S}^{\eta}(W)
in terms of the usual Schur functor using the following isomorphism of
\mathrm{GL}(W)
-representations

\mathbb{S}^{\eta}W\cong\det(W)^{-m}\otimes\mathbb{S}^{\eta+(m)^{k}}W,

where
\det(W)
is the determinant representation and
\eta+(m)^{k}=(\eta_{1}+m,\eta_{2}+m,\dots,\eta_{k}+m)
is a partition. For any highest weight
\eta
, the dual of the corrresponding representation is given by
\mathbb{S}^{\eta}(W)^{\vee}=\mathbb{S}^{-\eta}(W),
where

-\eta=(-\eta_{k},-\eta_{k-1},\dots,-\eta_{1}).

The tensor product of two rational representations with highest weights
\eta
and
\rho
can also be described using the Littlewood-Richardson rule

\mathbb{S}^{\eta}W\otimes\mathbb{S}^{\rho}W=\bigoplus_{\chi}(\mathbb{S}^{\chi}W)^{\oplus c_{\eta,\rho}^{\chi}},

(6)

where
\chi
runs over all the highest weights with
|\chi|=|\eta|+|\rho|
, and the multiplicity is given using the identity

\displaystyle c_{\eta,\rho}^{\chi}=c_{\eta+(m)^{r},\rho+(k)^{r}}^{\chi+(m+k)^{r}},

(7)

where
m
and
k
are any two integers, such that
\eta+(m)^{r},\rho+(k)^{r}
and
\chi+(m+k)^{r}
are partitions.

Given a highest weight
\eta=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\dots,\eta_{k})
, it is sometimes useful to partition it into two parts, where one consists of nonnegative integers and one consists of nonpositive ones. For this, we use the notation
\eta=(\gamma,-\delta)
, where
\gamma,\delta
are partitions. Note that due to the presence of zeros, there isn’t necessarily a unique way to write
\eta
in this form.

Remark 2.1
.

Schur functors
\mathbb{S}^{\eta}
can be defined in the generality of vector bundles on a scheme. The identities in the above discussion, such as (
3
), (
4
), and (
5
), all generalize verbatim. This is explained in e.g.
[
36
, Chapter 2]
. Schur functors associated to a partition can be generalized even further to perfect complexes, and it is well-defined up to quasi-isomorphisms. We explain the case of length two perfect complexes in Section
5.1
.

2.4.
Horn’s inequalities

There is a vast literature on the study of Littlewood–Richarson coefficients. In particular, a lot of it has been devoted to answering the following fundamental question:

What are the conditions on the partitions
\alpha,\beta
and
\gamma
such that
c_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}\neq 0
?

Knutson and Tao in their proof of the saturation conjecture
[
17
]
showed that
c_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}\neq 0
if and only if
\alpha
,
\beta
, and
\gamma
are the eigenvalues of Hermitian matrices
A
,
B
, and
C
with
C=A+B
, respectively. We assume that the rank
N
of the matrices to be greater than the number of parts in each of the partitions
\alpha
,
\beta
, and
\gamma
. Then using the identity for traces,
\mathrm{tr}A+\mathrm{tr}B=\mathrm{tr}C
, we get

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}.

In general, given
A
and
B
be
N\times N
Hermitian matrices, define
C=A+B
. Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\alpha_{N})
,
\beta=(\beta_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\beta_{N})
and
\gamma=(\gamma_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\gamma_{N})
denote the eigenvalues of the matrices
A
,
B
and
C
written in non-increasing order.

Let
I=(i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{s})
and
J=(j_{1}\leq\cdots\leq j_{s})
be increasing sequence at most
N
positive integers such that
i_{s}+j_{s}\leq N+s
. Then we have

\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{j\in J}\beta_{j}\geq\sum_{k\in K}\gamma_{k}

(8)

where
K=(k_{1}\leq\cdots\leq k_{s})
with
k_{p}=i_{p}+j_{p}-p
for
1\leq p\leq s
; see
[
12
]
for a detailed exposition.

In the following proposition, we enumerate various properties of Littlewood–Richardson coefficients that will be used later in the proofs.

Proposition 2.2
.

Consider three partitions
\alpha
,
\beta
, and
\gamma
such that
c_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}\neq 0
. Then the following hold:

(i)

(Size constraint)

|\alpha|+|\beta|=|\gamma|;

(ii)

(Symmetry)

c_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}=c_{\alpha^{\dagger},\beta^{\dagger}}^{\gamma^{\dagger}};

(iii)

(Weyl inequality) For any positive integers
i
and
j
, we have

\alpha_{i}+\beta_{j}\geq\gamma_{i+j-1};

(9)

(iv)

(Dominance inequality I) We have
\gamma\leq\alpha+\beta
, that is, for all
s
,

\sum_{i=1}^{s}\gamma_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{s}\beta_{i};

(10)

(v)

(Dominance inequality II) We have
\alpha\cup\beta\leq\gamma
, that is, for all
t
,

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\beta_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2t}\gamma_{i}.

Note that part (iii) and (iv) also holds when
\alpha,\beta
and
\gamma
are highest weights.

Proof.

Statements (i) and (ii) are standard facts about Littlewood–Richardson coefficients. Statements (iii) and (iv) both follow from (
8
). Specifically, statement (iii) can be obtained by letting
I=\{i\},J=\{j\}
. Statement (iv) can be obtained by letting
I=J=\{1,\dots,s\}
. We now explain the proof of (v). Taking rank of matrices,
N
, to be sufficiently large, the inequality in (
8
) implies

\sum_{p=t+1}^{t+s}\alpha_{p}+\sum_{p=t+1}^{t+s}\beta_{p}\geq\sum_{p=2t+1}^{2t+s}\gamma_{p}

for any
2s+t\leq N+t
. Now take
s
larger than the number of parts in
\alpha
,
\beta
, and
\gamma
. Then (v) follows by subtracting the above inequality from the equality in (i).
∎

3.1.
Borel-Weil-Bott

Let
\mathrm{Gr}(k,N)
denote the Grassmannian of
k

\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Consider the universal exact sequence over
\mathrm{Gr}(k,N)
:

0\to\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Gr}(k,N)}^{\oplus N}\to\mathcal{B}\to 0,

where
\mathcal{A}
is the universal subbundle of rank
k
and
\mathcal{B}
is the universal quotient bundle of rank
N-k
. The sheaf cohomology of Schur functors applied to

and

is described by the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem
[

]
. We state below the version presented in
[

Theorem 3.1

(Borel-Weil-Bott)
.

For any two non-increasing sequences of integers

\rho=(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k})\quad\text{and}\quad\chi=(\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{N-k}),

the vector bundle
\mathbb{S}^{\rho}\mathcal{A}^{\vee}\otimes\mathbb{S}^{\chi}\mathcal{B}^{\vee}
on
\mathrm{Gr}(k,N)
has at most one non-vanishing cohomology group. Let

\omega:=(\rho,\chi)+(N-1,N-2,\dots,0)

be the component-wise sum. If
\omega
contains a repetition, then all cohomology groups of
\mathbb{S}^{\rho}(\mathcal{A}^{\vee})\otimes\mathbb{S}^{\chi}(\mathcal{B}^{\vee})
vanish. Otherwise, let
\sigma
be the permutation that sorts
\omega
into a strictly decreasing sequence, and

\gamma:=\sigma\circ\omega-(N-1,N-2,\dots,0),

be a non-increasing sequence, then

\displaystyle H^{D}\left(\mathrm{Gr}(k,N),\ \mathbb{S}^{\rho}\mathcal{A}^{\vee}\otimes\mathbb{S}^{\chi}\mathcal{B}^{\vee}\right)=\mathbb{S}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{C}^{N})^{\vee}

where the cohomological degree
D=\ell(\sigma)
is the length of the permutation
\sigma
, and all other cohomology groups are zero.

Remark 3.2
.

When we consider Schur functors of the quotient bundle independently, Theorem
3.1
implies that for any partition
\lambda
, the morphism induced from the tautological sequence

\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\cO_{\mathrm{Gr}(k,N)}^{\oplus N}\to\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathcal{B}

gives an isomporhism on the space of global sections

H^{0}\left(\mathrm{Gr}(k,N),\ \mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathcal{B}\right)\cong\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}^{N}).

Similar statement holds for
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathcal{A}^{\vee}
.

Remark 4.2
.

The condition

Remark 4.3

The special case when

4.1.
Strømme embedding

There is an embedding of the Quot scheme into a product of two Grassmannians
(see
[
34
]
for details):

\iota:\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},V,r)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Gr}(k_{1},N_{1})\times\mathrm{Gr}(k_{2},N_{2}).

The explicit values of

\displaystyle N_{1}

\displaystyle=H^{0}(V(m-1))=nm-b,

\displaystyle N_{2}

\displaystyle=H^{0}(V(m))=n(m+1)-b,

\displaystyle r_{1}

\displaystyle=\rank(L_{m-1}^{[d]})=rm+d,

\displaystyle r_{2}

\displaystyle=\rank(L_{m}^{[d]})=r(m+1)+d,

\displaystyle k_{1}

\displaystyle=\rank(L_{m-1}^{\{d\}})=(n-r)m-b-d,

\displaystyle k_{2}

\displaystyle=\rank(L_{m}^{\{d\}})=(n-r)(m+1)-b-d.

4.5.
Cohomological degree of the tautological bundles

In Section

and

Proposition 4.7

Assume

splits as a direct sum of line bundles of nonpositive degrees. Let
\eta=(\gamma,-\delta)
,

. Then

In particular, when

and

are partitions,

Proof.

First, recall the Koszul resolution (

), and note that it suffices to show

H^{D}\!\big(\mathrm{Gr}(k_{1},n_{1})\times\mathrm{Gr}(k_{2},n_{2}),\mathcal{V}_{t}\big)=0\quad\text{for all }t\geq 0,\text{ and}\ D>t+|\delta|+|\nu|.

Indeed, assume there exist partitions
\mu,\alpha,\beta,\sigma,\chi
such that

with
c_{\alpha,\beta}^{\mu^{\dagger}}\cdot c_{\alpha,\beta}^{\sigma}\cdot c_{\sigma,-\rho}^{\chi}\neq 0
. Recall the indices
i
and
j
from the proof of Lemma
4.4
. Analyzing the non-vanishing of the cohomology groups and the Littlewood–Richardson coefficients,
in particular, the inequalities (
21
), (
23
), and (
25
), implies

j(k_{2}+j)-|\nu|\;\leq\;\mu_{1}^{\dagger}+\cdots+\mu_{2j}^{\dagger}.

By Lemma
3.9
(ii) the cohomological degree satisfies

D\leq k_{2}j+|\delta|+(\mu_{1}+\dots+\mu_{i})-i^{2}=k_{2}j+|\delta|+ip+\sum_{s\geq i+p+1}\mu_{s}^{\dagger},

where

is the cohomological degree contributed by
\mathbb{S}^{\chi}\mathcal{B}_{2}^{\vee}
. Adding the two inequalities above yields

|\delta|+|\nu|+|\mu|\;\geq\;D+\begin{cases}j^{2}-ip+\displaystyle\sum_{s=2j+1}^{i+p}\mu_{s}^{\dagger},&\text{if }2j\leq i+p,\\[3.99994pt]

, and if

∎

5.1.
Schur functor of perfect complexes

For a partition
\lambda
of
n
, let
\pi_{\lambda}
be the irreducible representation of
S_{n}

corresponding to
\lambda
. Then Schur functors can be defined for a perfect complex
A
of arbitrary length as
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(A)=(\pi_{\lambda}\otimes A^{\otimes n})^{S_{n}}
(see for instance Page 302 between Theorems 9.11.4 and 9.11.5 in
[
11
]
). From this definition, it is also clear that in characteristic zero, Schur functors respect quasi-isomorphisms: taking tensor products respect quasi-isomorphisms, and taking
S_{n}
invariants is exact in characteristic zero.

When we write
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}M^{[d]}
for
M^{[d]}
is an object in the derived category of the Quot scheme, we mean to represent
M^{[d]}
first as a perfect complex and take the Schur functor. In our case, we can actually always represent
M^{[d]}
and
M^{\{d\}}
as a map of vector bundles. Below we recall definitions and basic properties of Schur functors associated to a partition and a map of vector bundles. The construction in this case is called Schur complexes in the literature. See
[
36
, Chapter 2]
for details.

Let
X
be a locally Noetherian scheme and let

\Phi:E_{1}\to E_{2}

be a map of vector bundles on
X
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition. We write
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\Phi)^{\bullet}
for the Schur complex of length
|\lambda|
, so that

\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\Phi)^{q}=\bigoplus_{\nu\subseteq\lambda,|\nu|=q}\mathbb{S}^{\lambda/\nu}E_{1}\otimes\mathbb{S}^{\nu^{\dagger}}E_{2}.

is placed in
q
-th degree.
The beginning of
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\Phi)^{\bullet}
looks like

\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}E_{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{\lambda/(1)}E_{1}\otimes E_{2}\to\mathbb{S}^{\lambda/(2)}E_{1}\otimes\wedge^{2}E_{2}\oplus\mathbb{S}^{\lambda/(1^{2})}E_{1}\otimes\Sym^{2}E_{2}\to\cdots

Sometimes it is useful to index homologically as well.
We write
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\Phi)_{\bullet}
for the Schur complex of length
|\lambda|
associated to the map
\Phi
, so that

\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\Phi)_{q}=\bigoplus_{\nu\subseteq\lambda,|\nu|=q}\mathbb{S}^{\nu^{\dagger}}E_{1}\otimes\mathbb{S}^{\lambda/\nu}E_{2}.

is placed in
(-q)
-th degree.
The beginning of
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\Phi)_{\bullet}
looks like

\cdots\to\Sym^{2}E_{1}\otimes\mathbb{S}^{\lambda/(1^{2})}E_{2}\oplus\wedge^{2}E_{1}\otimes\mathbb{S}^{\lambda/(2)}E_{2}\to E_{1}\otimes\mathbb{S}^{\lambda/(1)}E_{2}\to\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}E_{2}.

Note that
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\Phi)_{\bullet}=\mathbb{S}^{\lambda^{\dagger}}(\Phi)^{\bullet}[\ell]
, where
\ell=|\lambda|
.

Proposition 5.2
.

If we have a short exact sequence

0\to E_{1}\xrightarrow{\Psi}E_{2}\xrightarrow{\Phi}E_{3}\to 0,

(34)

where each
E_{i}
is a vector bundle,
then
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\Psi)_{\bullet}\to\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}E_{3}\to 0
is exact, and
0\to\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}E_{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\Phi)^{\bullet}
is exact.

Proof.

Since
E_{3}
is locally free, the short exact sequence (
34
) is locally split. Acyclicity can be checked locally, so
[
1
, Corollary V.1.15]
implies that
(\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\Psi)_{\bullet}
is acyclic. The same result also shows that the cokernel locally agrees with
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}E_{3}
, and the functoriality of Schur complexes shows that the isomorphisms must glue, so, globally, the cokernel is also exactly
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}E_{3}
.

The first statement applied to the dual of (
34
) shows that the complex
S^{\lambda}(\Phi^{\vee}:E_{3}^{\vee}\to E_{2}^{\vee})\to S^{\lambda}E_{1}^{\vee}\to 0
is exact. Dualizing this complex shows the second statement.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

In the theorem statements, such as Theorem
1.1
, we sometimes write
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}H^{\bullet}(V\otimes M)
, where
V,M
are vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. The definition of the Schur functor is rather trivial in this case, but requires caution. For instance,

.

5.4.
Vanishing without size constraints

We now turn to the setting when there is no restrictions on the sizes of partitions. We will extend the result for two consecutive line bundles in Proposition
4.7
to showing vanishing of higher cohomology groups for multiple line bundle.

Theorem 5.7
.

Assume
V
splits as a direct sum of line bundles of nonpositive degrees.
Let
M_{1},M_{2},\ldots,M_{t}
be line bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degrees at least
d+b
. For any tuple of partitions
\lambda^{1},\lambda^{2},\ldots,\lambda^{t}
, we have

H^{i}\left(\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},V,r),\bigotimes_{j=1}^{t}\mathbb{S}^{\lambda^{j}}M_{j}^{[d]}\right)=0\quad\text{for all }i\geq 1.

Proof.

Let
m=d+b
, and thus Proposition
4.7
applies whenever two consective line bundles are involved. Let
M_{1},M_{2},\dots,M_{t}
be line bundles with degrees at least
m
, and consider the short exact sequences (
39
) of vector bundles ,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{m-1}^{[d]}\otimes H^{0}(M_{j}\otimes L_{m-1}^{\vee})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{L_{m}^{[d]}\otimes H^{0}(M_{j}\otimes L_{m}^{\vee})})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{j}^{[d]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{M_{j}}}

for each
1\leq j\leq t
. For any partitions
\lambda^{1},\lambda^{2},\dots,\lambda^{t}
, we consider the complex of vector bundles

G^{\bullet}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{t}\mathbb{S}^{\lambda^{j}}(\Phi_{M_{j}})_{\bullet}

Note that the complex is supported in degrees
[-\ell,0]
where
\ell=(|\lambda^{1}|+\cdots+|\lambda^{t}|)
. Proposition
5.2
implies that the complex

0\to G^{-\ell}\to G^{-\ell+1}\to\cdots\to G_{0}\to\bigotimes_{j=1}^{t}\mathbb{S}^{\lambda^{j}}{M_{j}}

is exact. The
(-q)
-th term

We now provide some concrete examples explaining why the size constraint is necessary in the statement of Theorem
1.1
, even though all higher cohomology groups vanish for positive enough line bundles.

Example 5.8
.

Let
V=\cO_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus 2}
,
r=1
and
d=2
. For the line bundle
L_{4}=\cO_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(4)
, the natural map

\wedge^{6}H^{0}(V\otimes L_{4})\to H^{0}(\quot_{d},\wedge^{6}L_{4}^{[2]})

is not an isomorphism. This shows that the bound
|\lambda|<(dn+n)/(n-r)
is sharp in Theorem
1.5
.

Our computation uses the Stømme embedding of
\iota:\quot_{d}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Gr}(3,10)\times\mathrm{Gr}(4,12)
, corresponding to the twists
m=4,5
.
We have
\wedge^{6}L_{4}^{[2]}=\iota^{*}\mathbb{S}^{(1^{6})}\mathcal{B}_{1}
, which yields a resolution of
\iota_{*}\wedge^{6}L_{4}^{[2]}
as in (
18
). This then gives rise to a spectral sequence
H^{k}(\mathcal{V}_{t})\Rightarrow H^{k-t}(\quot_{d},\wedge^{6}L_{4}^{[2]})
. By using the computer to run an exhaustive search, we find that there are exactly two nonzero terms on the initial page of the spectral sequence: in addition to the term
H^{0}(\mathcal{V}_{0})=\wedge^{6}H^{0}(V\otimes L_{4})
, which has dimension
\dim H^{0}(\mathcal{V}_{0})=210
, the cohomology group
H^{23}(\mathcal{V}_{24})
is also nonzero.

Specifically, let
W_{1}=H^{0}(V\otimes L_{4}),W_{2}=H^{0}(V\otimes L_{5})
, and let
\mu=(10,10,4),\sigma=(6,6,2^{6})
.
Then the multiplicity

\sum_{\alpha,\beta}c^{\mu^{\dagger}}_{\alpha,\beta}c^{\sigma}_{\alpha,\beta}=28

is nonzero, and

\displaystyle H^{23}(\mathrm{Gr}(3,10)\times\mathrm{Gr}(4,12),\mathcal{V}_{24})

\displaystyle=H^{23}((\mathbb{S}^{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{S}^{(1^{6})}\mathcal{B}_{1})\boxtimes\mathbb{S}^{\sigma}\mathcal{B}_{2}^{\vee})^{\oplus 28}

\displaystyle=(\mathbb{S}^{(3^{10})}W_{1}\otimes\mathbb{S}^{((2)^{12})}{W_{2}^{\vee}})^{\oplus 28}

\displaystyle\cong\left((\det W_{1})^{\otimes 3}\otimes(\det W_{2}^{\vee})^{\otimes 2}\right)^{\oplus 28}.

One possible choice of
\alpha,\beta
would be

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=(3,3),

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=(3,3,2^{6}),

for which the multiplicity is
c^{\mu^{\dagger}}_{\alpha,\beta}c^{\sigma}_{\alpha,\beta}=1
.

Since there are only two nonzero terms on the initial page and
\wedge^{6}L_{4}^{[2]}
cannot have cohomology in a negative degree, the induced map
H^{23}(\mathcal{V}_{24})\to H^{0}(\mathcal{V}_{0})
must be injective. In fact, there is a short exact sequence

0\to\left((\det W_{1})^{\otimes 3}\otimes(\det W_{2}^{\vee})^{\otimes 2}\right)^{\oplus 28}\to\wedge^{6}W_{1}\to H^{0}(\quot_{d},\wedge^{6}L_{4}^{[2]})\to 0.

Example 5.9
.

Let
V=\cO_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus 3},r=1,d=3
. Then

H^{2}(\quot_{d},\Sym^{2}(L_{2}^{[d]})^{\vee})=H^{2}(\quot_{d},\mathbb{S}^{(2)}(L_{2}^{[d]})^{\vee})\neq 0.

Note that the partition

, corresponding to the twists

on

, where

\displaystyle H^{13}(\mathcal{V}^{\mu_{1}})

While we don’t know how to describe this middle map explicitly, the cokernel

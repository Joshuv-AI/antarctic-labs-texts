On decoupling and restriction estimates
Changkeun Oh

Abstract
. In this short note, we prove that the restriction conjecture for the (hyperbolic) paraboloid in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} implies the l p l^{p} -decoupling theorem for the (hyperbolic) paraboloid in ℝ 2 ​ d − 1 \mathbb{R}^{2d-1} . In particular, this gives a simple proof of the l p l^{p} decoupling theorem for the (hyperbolic) paraboloid in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} .



1.
Introduction

Let
d\geq 2
and
\vec{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{d-1})\in\{-1,1\}^{d-1}
. Consider

(1.1)

\mathcal{H}_{\vec{v}}^{d-1}:=\big\{(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d-1},\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}v_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}):\xi_{i}\in[0,1]^{d-1}\big\}.

Define the extension operator for
\mathcal{H}_{\vec{v}}^{d-1}
by

(1.2)

\widehat{fd\sigma}(x):=\int_{[0,1]^{d-1}}f(\xi)e\big((x\cdot(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d-1},\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}v_{i}\xi_{i}^{2})\big)\,d\xi,

where
e(t)=e^{2\pi it}
.
Let us state the well known conjecture in restriction theory.

Conjecture 1.1

(Restriction conjecture,
[
Ste79
]
)
.

For
p>2+\frac{2}{d-1}
, we have

(1.3)

\|\widehat{fd\sigma}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}([0,1]^{d-1})}.

The restriction conjecture is introduced by Elias Stein. The conjecture is solved when
n=2
by Fefferman
[
Fef70
]
, but it is still open for higher dimensions. For the case
v_{1}=\ldots=v_{d-1}
, we refer to
[
WW24
]
for the best known result. For the general case

\vec{v}\in\{-1,1\}^{d-1}
, we refer to
[
HI22
]
and references therein.

The purpose of this note is to relate a restriction conjecture and a decoupling theorem. A decoupling inequality is first introduced by
[
Wol00
]
. The
l^{2}
decoupling inequality for the paraboloid is proved by
[
BD15
]
and the
l^{p}
decoupling inequality for the hyperbolic paraboloid is proved by
[
BD17
]
. Decoupling inequalities have a number of applications. We refer to
[
Gut23
]
for references.

Let us introduce some notations to state a decoupling inequality. Denote by
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}_{\vec{v}}^{d-1}}(R^{-1})
the
R^{-1}
-neighborhood of the hyperbolic paraboloid
\mathcal{H}_{\vec{v}}^{d-1}
.
Given a parameter
\vec{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d-1})\in[0,1]^{d-1}
, let

\Gamma_{\vec{\alpha}}(R^{-1})
be the collection defined by

\begin{split}\Big\{(B\times\mathbb{R})&\cap\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}_{\vec{v}}^{d-1}}(R^{-1}):\\
&B=\big((c_{1},\ldots,c_{d-1})+[0,R^{-\alpha_{1}}]\times\cdots\times[0,R^{-\alpha_{n-1}}]\big),\,c_{i}\in R^{-\alpha_{i}}\mathbb{Z}\Big\}.\end{split}

For each
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and each
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
we define

(1.4)

\mathcal{P}_{B}F(x):=\int_{B}\widehat{F}(\xi)e(\xi\cdot x)\,d\xi.

Let us state the
l^{p}
decoupling theorem for the hyperbolic paraboloid
\mathcal{H}_{\vec{v}}^{d-1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
BD17
]
)
.

Let
\vec{\alpha}=(\frac{1}{2},\ldots,\frac{1}{2})
.
For
2\leq p\leq 2+\frac{4}{d-1}
and
\epsilon>0
,

(1.5)

\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{p,\epsilon}R^{\frac{1}{2}(d-1)(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})+\epsilon}\big(\sum_{\theta\in{\Gamma}_{\vec{\alpha}}(R^{-1})}\|F_{\theta}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for all functions
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
whose Fourier transforms are supported on
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}_{\vec{\alpha}}^{d-1}}(R^{-1})
.

Our theorem is as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
d\geq 2
.
Conjecture
1.1
for the (hyperbolic) paraboloid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
implies Theorem
1.2
for the (hyperbolic) paraboloid in
\mathbb{R}^{2d-1}
.

Note that the critical exponents of
p
of the restriction conjecture for the hyperbolic paraboloid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the decoupling theorem for the hyperbolic paraboloid in
\mathbb{R}^{2d-1}
are equal.
As mentioned before, Conjecture
1.1
is proved by Fefferman for the case
d=2
. Hence, our theorem gives an alternative proof of the
l^{p}
decoupling theorem for the (hyperbolic) paraboloid in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

Let us begin with the equivalent formulation of the restriction conjecture.

Proposition 2.1

(Proposition 1.5 of
[
GMO24
]
)
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
\vec{\alpha}=(1,\ldots,1)
.
If Conjecture
1.1
is true for some
d
, then for
2\leq p\leq 2+\frac{2}{d-1}
and
\epsilon>0
, we have

(2.1)

\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{p,\epsilon}R^{(d-1)(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})+\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\vec{\alpha}}(R^{-1})}\|\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for all functions
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
whose Fourier transforms are supported on
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}_{\vec{\alpha}}^{d-1}}(R^{-1})
.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
relies on an iterative argument. This argument is inspired by
[
FSWW18
]
. Consider the hyperbolic paraboloid
\mathcal{H}_{\vec{v}}^{2d-2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2d-1}
associated with
\vec{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{2d-2})\in\{-1,1\}^{2d-2}
.
Our goal is to prove that for
2\leq p\leq 2+\frac{4}{2d-2}
,
\vec{\alpha}=(\frac{1}{2},\ldots,\frac{1}{2})
, and
\epsilon>0
,

(2.2)

\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d-1})}\leq C_{p,\epsilon}R^{\frac{1}{2}(2d-2)(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})+\epsilon}\big(\sum_{\theta\in{\Gamma}_{\vec{\alpha}}(R^{-1})}\|F_{\theta}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d-1})}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for all functions
F:\mathbb{R}^{2d-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
whose Fourier supports are in
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}_{\vec{\alpha}}^{2d-2}}(R^{-1})
.
For the rest of the proof, assume that
2\leq p\leq 2+\frac{4}{2d-2}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
0\leq a_{2}\leq a_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
Define two vectors

(2.3)

\begin{split}&\vec{\alpha}:=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{2})\in[0,1]^{d-1}\times[0,1]^{d-1}\\
&\vec{\beta}:=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{1},2a_{1}-a_{2},\ldots,2a_{1}-a_{2})\in[0,1]^{d-1}\times[0,1]^{d-1}.\end{split}

Under the assumption of Conjecture
1.1
for the hyperbolic paraboloid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for some
d
, for given
\tau\in\Gamma_{\vec{\alpha}}(R^{-1})
and
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
, we have

(2.4)

\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d-1})}\leq C_{p,\epsilon^{\prime}}R^{{(2a_{1}-2a_{2})(d-1)}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})+\epsilon^{\prime}}\big(\sum_{\theta\in{\Gamma}_{\vec{\beta}}(R^{-1})}\|\mathcal{P}_{\theta}F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d-1})}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for all functions
F
whose Fourier supports are contained in
\tau
. The constant
C_{p,\epsilon^{\prime}}
is independent of the choice of
\tau,a_{1},a_{2}
.

Let us assume the lemma and continue the proof. Fix
\epsilon>0
. By the assumption on the Fourier support of
F
, we may write

(2.5)

F=\sum_{\theta_{1}\in{\Gamma}_{\vec{\alpha_{1}}}(R^{-1})}\mathcal{P}_{\theta_{1}}F

where
\vec{\alpha_{1}}:=({\epsilon},\ldots,{\epsilon},0,\ldots,0)\in[0,1]^{d-1}\times[0,1]^{d-1}
.
Since the cardinality of the collection
{\Gamma}_{\vec{\alpha_{1}}}(R^{-1})
is comparable to
R^{{(d-1)\epsilon}}
, by the triangle inequality and Hölder’s inequality, we have

(2.6)

\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d-1})}\leq C_{p}R^{(d-1)\epsilon(1-\frac{1}{p})}\big(\sum_{\theta_{1}\in{\Gamma}_{\vec{\alpha_{1}}}(R^{-1})}\|\mathcal{P}_{\theta_{1}}F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d-1})}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

To simplify the notation, let us introduce
C:=(d-1)(1-\frac{1}{p})
.

Define

(2.7)

\vec{\alpha}_{n}:=\begin{cases}{\epsilon}(n-1,\ldots,n-1,n,\ldots,n)\;\;\;\mathrm{if}\,n\,\mathrm{is\,even}\\
{\epsilon}(n,\ldots,n,n-1,\ldots,n-1)\;\;\;\mathrm{if}\,n\,\mathrm{is\,odd}\end{cases}

By applying Lemma
2.2
to the right hand side of (
2.6
) with
\epsilon^{\prime}=\epsilon^{2}
,
a_{1}=\epsilon
, and
a_{2}=0
, we have

\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d-1})}\leq C_{p,\epsilon^{2}}C_{p}R^{C\epsilon}R^{2\epsilon(d-1)(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})+\epsilon^{2}}\big(\sum_{\theta_{2}\in{\Gamma}_{\vec{\alpha_{2}}}(R^{-1})}\|\mathcal{P}_{\theta_{2}}F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d-1})}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Note that we can apply Lemma
2.2
again to the right hand side of the above inequality with
\epsilon^{\prime}=\epsilon^{2}
,
a_{1}=2\epsilon
, and
a_{2}=\epsilon
(when we apply the lemma, we switch
\xi_{d},\ldots,\xi_{2d-2}
variables and
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d-1}
variables). By repeating this process
[\epsilon^{-1}/2]
-times, we have

\begin{split}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d-1})}\leq(C_{p,\epsilon^{2}})^{[\epsilon^{-1}/2]}C_{p}R^{C\epsilon}&R^{2\epsilon[\epsilon^{-1}/2](d-1)(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})+\frac{\epsilon}{2}}\\
&\times\big(\sum_{\theta\in{\Gamma}_{\vec{\alpha}_{[\epsilon^{-1}/2]}}(R^{-1})}\|\mathcal{P}_{\theta}F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d-1})}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.\end{split}

By the triangle inequality, we finally obtain

(2.8)

\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d-1})}\leq\widetilde{C}_{p,\epsilon}R^{2C\epsilon+\frac{\epsilon}{2}}(R)^{(d-1)(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\big(\sum_{\theta\in{\Gamma}_{\vec{\alpha}}(R^{-1})}\|\mathcal{P}_{\theta}F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d-1})}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
\vec{\alpha}=(\frac{1}{2},\ldots,\frac{1}{2})
and
\widetilde{C}_{p,\epsilon}=(C_{p,\epsilon^{2}})^{[\epsilon^{-1}/2]}C_{p}
.
This is the desired inequality. It remains to prove the lemma.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Let us fix
\tau\in\Gamma_{\vec{\alpha}}(R^{-1})
and suppose that the Fourier support of
F
is contained in
\tau
. We need to prove (
2.4
). We write

(2.9)

\tau=\big(\big((c_{1},\ldots,c_{2d-2})+([0,R^{-a_{1}}]^{n-1}\times[0,R^{-a_{2}}]^{n-1})\big)\times\mathbb{R}\big)\cap\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}_{\vec{v}}^{2d-2}}(R^{-1}).

To simplify the notation, we introduce

(2.10)

\begin{split}&c:=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2d-2}),\;\;\;\;\xi=(\xi^{\prime},\xi_{2d-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{2d-2}\times\mathbb{R},\\
&P(\xi^{\prime}):=v_{1}\xi_{1}^{2}+\cdots+v_{2d-2}\xi_{2d-2}^{2}.\end{split}

Define the affine transformation

(2.11)

L(\xi):=(\xi^{\prime}+c,\xi_{2d-1}+P(c)+\xi^{\prime}\cdot\nabla P(c)),\;\;\;\;\;\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2d-1}

and the function
G_{0}
satisfying

\hat{G}_{0}(\xi):=\hat{F}(L\xi)
. Then the Fourier support of
G_{0}
is contained in the following set

(2.12)

\big([0,R^{-a_{1}}]^{d-1}\times[0,R^{-a_{2}}]^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}\big)\cap\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}_{\vec{v}}^{2d-2}}(16R^{-1}).

Recall that
a_{2}\leq a_{1}
. Define the linear mapping
L_{1}
and the function
G
so that

(2.13)

L_{1}(\xi):=(R^{-a_{2}}\xi^{\prime},R^{-2a_{2}}\xi_{2d-1}),\;\;\;\hat{G}(\xi):=\hat{G_{0}}(L_{1}(\xi))=\hat{F}(L_{1}L(\xi)).

Then the Fourier support of
G
is contained in

(2.14)

\big([0,R^{a_{2}-a_{1}}]^{d-1}\times[0,1]^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}\big)\cap\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}_{\vec{v}}^{2d-2}}(16R^{2a_{2}-1}).

Note that
R^{2a_{2}-1}\leq R^{-2a_{1}+2a_{2}}
.
By performing the linear change of variables (
2.13
), the desired inequality (
2.4
) follows from

\|G\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d-1})}\leq C_{p,\epsilon^{\prime}}{R^{(2a_{1}-2a_{2})(d-1){(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})+\epsilon^{\prime}}}}\big(\sum_{\theta\in{\Gamma}_{\vec{\beta_{0}}}(\widetilde{R}^{-1})}\|\mathcal{P}_{\theta}G\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d-1})}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

where
\widetilde{R}^{-1}:=16R^{-2a_{1}+2a_{2}}
and
\vec{\beta_{0}}:=(\frac{1}{2},\ldots,\frac{1}{2},1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.
To prove the above inequality, let us first observe that the set (
2.14
) is contained in

(2.15)

[0,R^{a_{2}-a_{1}}]^{d-1}\times\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}_{\vec{v_{0}}}^{d-1}}(d\widetilde{R}^{-1})

where
\vec{v}_{0}=(v_{d},\ldots,v_{2d-2})
. To prove this, suppose that
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{2d-1})
is an element of (
2.14
). We need to verify that

(2.16)

\Big|\xi_{2d-1}-\sum_{i=d}^{2d-2}v_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}\Big|\leq d\widetilde{R}^{-1}.

This is true because

(2.17)

\begin{split}&\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{2d-2}v_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i=d}^{2d-2}v_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}\Big|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}|\xi_{i}|^{2}\leq(d-1)R^{-2a_{1}+2a_{2}},\\
&\Big|\xi_{2d-1}-\sum_{i=1}^{2d-2}v_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}\Big|\leq 10R^{2a_{2}-1}\leq 10R^{-2a_{1}+2a_{2}}.\end{split}

Since the Fourier support of
G
is contained in (
2.15
), to get the desired inequality, it suffices to decompose frequencies for
\xi_{d},\ldots,\xi_{2d-2}
-variables. We apply Proposition
2.1
for the last
d
-variables with
R^{-1}
replaced by
d\widetilde{R}^{-1}
, and obtain

\|G\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d-1})}\leq C_{p,\epsilon^{\prime}}{R^{(2a_{1}-2a_{2})(d-1){(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})+\epsilon^{\prime}}}}\big(\sum_{\theta\in{\Gamma}_{\vec{\beta_{0}}}(d\widetilde{R}^{-1})}\|\mathcal{P}_{\theta}G\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d-1})}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

It suffices to apply the triangle inequality and obtain

\|G\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d-1})}\leq{C}_{p,\epsilon^{\prime}}^{\prime}{R^{(2a_{1}-2a_{2})(d-1){(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})+\epsilon^{\prime}}}}\big(\sum_{\theta\in{\Gamma}_{\vec{\beta_{0}}}(\widetilde{R}^{-1})}\|\mathcal{P}_{\theta}G\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d-1})}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

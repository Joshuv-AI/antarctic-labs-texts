ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper presents a fractional-order modelling framework for population-resource dynamics using the -Hilfer derivative with neural network-based validation. The model captures logistic population growth coupled to renewable resource biomass while incorporating memory effects through fractional calculus. Novel contributions include the application of the -Hilfer operator to population-resource systems, representing hereditary dynamics via fractional order and type parameter . A detailed qualitative analysis establishes existence, uniqueness, and Ulam-Hyers (UH) stability with explicit parameter conditions. A linearised quadrature numerical scheme for the -Hilfer problem is developed and validated through neural networks, achieving . Simulations reveal that fractional orders () produce smoother transients than integer-order counterparts, with governing memory strength and modulating response patterns. Critically, we identify as a sustainable harvesting threshold, beyond which resource collapse occurs for integer-order systems, while fractional memory () provides partial damping. These findings offer actionable policy insights, including optimal harvest limits and stabilisation strategies, advancing both theoretical understanding and practical tools for sustainable resource management.Keywords: Renewable resource, -Hilfer derivative, Qualitative study, Linearized quadrature method, Neural network approximationsSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Population growth and the sustainable exploitation of renewable resources are major concerns in environmental management, ecological modelling and applied mathematics1. The increase in population size is putting pressure on finite natural resources like water, forests, and energy sources. These resources regenerate at a specific rate defined by the ecological boundaries and historical utilisation patterns. This imbalance between population growth and resource regeneration may cause ecological instability, depletion and unsustainability2. Therefore, the development of a robust mathematical model that accurately represents the interconnected dynamics of population growth and renewable resources is an urgent scientific and managerial priority.

Recent advances in fractional calculus have provided novel options for modelling complex biological and ecological systems. Neural networks have been successfully applied across diverse domains, including environmental modelling of chemostat systems3 and medical prediction of conjunctivitis infections4, showcasing the broad applicability of fractional calculus combined with neural networks. In epidemiological modelling, hybrid neural network architectures combining logistic sigmoid, radial basis, and hyperbolic tangent functions have been successfully applied to model M-pox disease dynamics5, while hybrid GA-IPA and neural network-based solutions have been developed for fractional-order nonlinear predator-prey dynamical systems6. Furthermore, radial basis and hyperbolic tangent neural network combinations have been utilised to solve herpes dynamics7, establishing the efficacy of neural networks in capturing complex biological behaviours.

It is very clear from the literature that integer order population-resource models have been studied in detail8. The integer order model describes the status of the dynamics of the model only at the current time while failing to account for memory and hereditary effects. The dynamics of actual populations and resources are path-dependent in real-world ecological systems9. Their behaviours depend on the present but are also strongly influenced by the legacy of the past. Looking at this limitation of the integer order models, the interest in the fractional order models is increasing day by day10.

The combination of neural networks and fractional calculus has opened a gateway for the analysis of different models, including renewable resources and population growth dynamics11. Neural networks are capable of validating numerical schemes, and with this capability we can predict the dynamics and different behaviours of models as well as hidden relationships12. However, a critical consideration is that when neural networks are trained on data generated by the same numerical scheme they are meant to validate, they primarily learn to replicate the outputs of the scheme rather than providing independent verification. Therefore, in this work we employ neural networks for the approximation and prediction of solutions, error analysis, and sensitivity analysis.

Our work provides a mixture of fractional calculus, coupled population-resource models and artificial intelligence under the -Hilfer derivative. Thus, it develops a highly interesting and flexible framework for population-resource dynamics. In this analysis a thorough analysis is provided, including all qualitative aspects using non-linear functional analysis techniques. We not only present a novel numerical scheme for the -Hilfer coupled population-resource model but also provide a complete analysis of the validation of our numerical method with the help of a neural network. A detailed numerical simulation of the concerned model has been carried out using MATLAB 2024 to investigate the effect of the fractional order and type parameter on the population stability and renewable resource sustainability. The simulations contain 2D, 3D and phase portrait plots under different values of and . It is quite clear from the simulations how memory effects play a key role in regulating growth dynamics and resource regeneration. This study reinforced the importance of fractional calculus in ecological systems and depicts the practical importance of our approach.

The network architecture consists of an input layer with 1 neuron, two hidden layers each containing 15 neurons with tanh activation, and an output layer with 1 neuron. The hyperbolic tangent activation function is used throughout the hidden layers. Conclusion

In this work, we have developed a comprehensive mathematical framework utilising advanced fractional calculus to analyse population-resource dynamics. Specifically, we have investigated a -Hilfer model that captures the logistically coupled dynamics of global population density and renewable resource biomass. The -Hilfer derivative provides enhanced flexibility for modelling this nonlinear problem through fractional order and type parameter . Our analysis has encompassed three complementary components: a qualitative study of system properties, a hybrid numerical scheme for accurate simulation, and neural network-based validation to ensure reliability. The proposed neural network approach successfully predicts system dynamics with exceptional accuracy (), enabling rapid evaluation across parameter spaces. Through this comprehensive approach, we have established the reliability and applicability of our fractional framework. This work advances theoretical understanding of fractional population-resource dynamics while delivering concrete practical implications for policy-making. Specifically, our identification of as a critical harvest threshold provides regulators with a quantifiable sustainability boundary, while Scenario 10 demonstrates that integrated policies achieve stable resource biomass (0.60–0.73.60.73) alongside healthy population levels (153–155). These actionable insights equip decision-makers with evidence-based tools for balancing human welfare with resource conservation across diverse ecological and economic contexts.

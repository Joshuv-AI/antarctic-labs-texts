Kernels for composition of positive linear operators
Ulrich Abel

Abstract




Abstract.

This paper investigates the composition of Bernstein–Durrmeyer operators and Szász–Mirakjan–Durrmeyer operators, focusing on the structure and properties of the associated kernel functions. In the case of the Bernstein–Durrmeyer operators, we establish new identities for the kernel arising from the composition of two and three operators, from which the commutativity of these operators follows naturally. Building on the eigenstructure of the Bernstein–Durrmeyer operator
M_{n}
, we obtain a representation of the iterate
M_{n}^{r}
as a linear combination of the operators
M_{k}
, for
k=0,1,\dots,n
. We also address the composition of Szász–Mirakjan–Durrmeyer operators and revisit a known result giving an elementary proof.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2020):
41A36

Keywords:
Approximation by positive operators,
composition of positive linear operators, iterates, kernels of integral operators, Bernstein–Durrmeyer operators, Szász–Mirakjan–Durrmeyer operators

1
Introduction

Integral operators play a central role in approximation theory, functional
analysis, and various applications in numerical analysis. Classical examples
of such operators include the Bernstein-Durrmeyer and Szász-Mirakjan-Durrmeyer operators, whose properties have been extensively
studied in the literature.
Within the framework of
integral operators, key topics of interest include their kernels, iterates,
and compositions.

In this paper, we investigate a family of integral operators defined by

(L_{n}f)(x)=\int_{I}f(y)K_{n}(x,y)dy

with
I=[0,1]
or
I=[0,\infty)
and
K_{n}(x,y)
a given kernel function.

A key object of interest in our study is the composition of
r
such
operators. Specifically, we consider the composition

((L_{n_{r}}\circ\cdots\circ L_{n_{1}})f)(x)=\int_{I}f(t_{1})K_{n_{r},\dots,n_{1}}(t_{r+1},t_{1})dt_{1}

where the kernel is recursively given by

K_{n_{r},\dots,n_{1}}(t_{r+1},t_{1})=\int_{I}K_{n_{r-1},\dots,n_{1}}(t_{r},t_{1})\cdot K_{n_{r}}(t_{r+1},t_{r})dt_{r}.

In Section
2
we consider the Bernstein–Durrmeyer operators
M_{n}

and derive new nice identities for the kernel function in the case of
composing two respectively three of these operators. From these identities
one deduces immediately that involved operators commute. Section
3

is devoted to a new approach, using the eigenstructure of
M_{n}
, for
studying the kernel function when
r
Bernstein–Durrmeyer operators are
composed. As a by-product we get a representation of the iterate
M_{n}^{r}
as
a linear combination of
M_{k}
,
k=0,1,\dots,n
. In Section
4
we
deal with the Durrmeyer modification of the Szász–Mirakjan operators
and present an elementary proof of a result given by Abel and Ivan in
[
1
]
. From this result it follows in
particular that composing two Szász–Mirakjan–Durrmeyer operators we
get an operator from the same family.

2
The case of Bernstein–Durrmeyer operators

Let
L_{1}[0,1]
denote the space of Lebesgue integrable functions on the
interval
[0,1]
and
\Pi_{n}
the space of polynomials of degree at most
n
.

The so called Bernstein–Durrmeyer operators
M_{n}:L_{1}[0,1]\longrightarrow\Pi_{n}
are defined by

\displaystyle(M_{n}f)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f(y)K_{n}(x,y)dy,\,\,x\in[0,1],

\displaystyle K_{n}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n+1)\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{n,k}(x)p_{n,k}(y),\,p_{n,k}(x)=\binom{n}{k}x^{k}(1-x)^{n-k}.

In 1981 Derriennic
[
2
, Théorème III.3]
proved that the
eigenfunctions of the Bernstein-Durrmeyer operators are the
(orthonormalized) Legendre polynomials on the interval
[0,1]
, i.e.,

Q_{0}(x)=1,\,Q_{k}(x)=\frac{\sqrt{2k+1}}{k!}\left(\frac{d}{dx}\right)^{k}\left(x^{k}\left(1-x\right)^{k}\right)

with corresponding eigenvalues

\lambda_{n,k}=\frac{n^{\underline{k}}}{\left(n+k+1\right)^{\underline{k}}}

and deduced the representation of the operators in terms of these
eigenfunctions, i.e.,

M_{n}f=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lambda_{n,k}\left\langle Q_{k},f\right\rangle Q_{k}

with the inner product
\left\langle f,g\right\rangle=\int_{0}^{1}f(t)g(t)dt
.

Ditzian and Ivanov
[
3
]
remarked that from this result it follows
immediately that the operators commute. In fact, we have

(M_{m}\circ M_{n})f=\sum_{k=0}^{\min\{m,n\}}\lambda_{m,k}\lambda_{n,k}\left\langle Q_{k},f\right\rangle Q_{k}.

It is possible to represent
K_{m,n}\left(x,y\right)
in terms of Bernstein basis polynomials. An application of Leibniz’ rule for derivatives yields

Q_{k}\left(x\right)=\sqrt{2k+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left(-1\right)^{j}\binom{k}{j}p_{k,j}\left(x\right).

(1)

Starting from

K_{m,n}\left(x,y\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\min\{m,n\}}\lambda_{m,k}\lambda_{n,k}Q_{k}\left(x\right)Q_{k}\left(y\right)

and using (
1
), we obtain

\displaystyle K_{m,n}\left(x,y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\min\left\{m,n\right\}}\frac{m^{\underline{k}}}{\left(m+k+1\right)^{\underline{k}}}\frac{n^{\underline{k}}}{\left(n+k+1\right)^{\underline{k}}}\left(2k+1\right)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left(-1\right)^{i}\binom{k}{i}p_{k,i}\left(x\right)\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left(-1\right)^{j}\binom{k}{j}p_{k,j}\left(y\right).

In this representation all possible products
p_{k,i}\left(x\right)p_{k,j}\left(y\right),\ 0\leq i,j\leq k\leq\min\{m,n\}
, appear.

Now we are going to derive a different representation of
K_{m,n}\left(x,y\right)
, which contains only products

p_{k,\ell}\left(x\right)p_{k,\ell}\left(y\right),\ 0\leq\ell\leq k\leq\min\{m,n\}
.
To our best knowledge it is new.

Theorem 1

For
m,n\geq 0
, the kernel of
the composition of classical Bernstein–Durrmeyer operators
M_{m}\circ M_{n}
has the representation

K_{m,n}\left(x,y\right)=\frac{\left(m+1\right)!\left(n+1\right)!}{\left(m+n+1\right)!}\sum_{k=0}^{\min\{m,n\}}\binom{m}{k}\binom{n}{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}p_{k,\ell}\left(x\right)p_{k,\ell}\left(y\right).

In our calculations we will use the following well-known formulas

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{n,k}(x)=1,

(2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}p_{n,k}\left(t\right)dt=\frac{1}{n+1},\text{ \quad}k=0,\ldots,n,

(3)

\displaystyle p_{m,\mu}\left(t\right)p_{n,\nu}\left(t\right)=\frac{\binom{m}{\mu}\binom{n}{\nu}}{\binom{m+n}{\mu+\nu}}p_{m+n,\mu+\nu}\left(t\right).

(4)

Furthermore, we make frequent use of the falling factorials denoted by
z^{\underline{0}}=1
,
z^{\underline{r}}=z\left(z-1\right)\cdots\left(z-r+1\right)
, for
r\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Using (
3
) and (
4
), we obtain

\displaystyle K_{m,n}\left(x,y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(m+1\right)\left(n+1\right)\sum_{\mu=0}^{m}p_{m,\mu}\left(x\right)\sum_{\nu=0}^{n}p_{n,\nu}\left(y\right)\int_{0}^{1}p_{m,\mu}\left(t\right)p_{n,\nu}\left(t\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(m+1\right)\left(n+1\right)}{m+n+1}\sum_{\mu=0}^{m}p_{m,\mu}\left(x\right)\sum_{\nu=0}^{n}p_{n,\nu}\left(y\right)\frac{\binom{m}{\mu}\binom{n}{\nu}}{\binom{m+n}{\mu+\nu}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(m+1\right)!\left(n+1\right)!}{\left(m+n+1\right)!}\sum_{\mu=0}^{m}p_{m,\mu}\left(x\right)\dfrac{1}{(m-\mu)!\mu!}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\nu=0}^{n}p_{n,\nu}\left(y\right)(m+n-\mu-\nu)^{\underline{m-\mu}}(\mu+\nu)^{\underline{\mu}}.

For the inner sum of
K_{m,n}\left(x,y\right)
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=0}^{n}p_{n,\nu}\left(y\right)(m+n-\mu-\nu)^{\underline{m-\mu}}(\mu+\nu)^{\underline{\mu}}

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)^{m-\mu}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)^{\mu}\left.s^{m-\mu}t^{\mu}\sum_{\nu=0}^{n}p_{n,\nu}\left(y\right)s^{n-\nu}t^{\nu}\right|_{s=t=1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left.\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)^{m-\mu}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)^{\mu}\left(s^{m-\mu}t^{\mu}\left[ty+s\left(1-y\right)\right]^{n}\right)\right|_{s=t=1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\mu}\binom{\mu}{i}\sum_{j=0}^{m-\mu}\binom{m-\mu}{j}\frac{\mu!}{i!}\frac{\left(m-\mu\right)!}{j!}n^{\underline{i+j}}y^{i}\left(1-y\right)^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\mu}{\binom{\mu}{i}}\sum_{j=0}^{m-\mu}{\binom{m-\mu}{j}}{\binom{n}{i+j}}\mu!(m-\mu)!p_{i+j,i}(y).

Thus we get for the kernel

\displaystyle K_{m,n}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(m+1)!(n+1)!}{(m+n+1)!}\sum_{\mu=0}^{m}p_{m,\mu}(x)\sum_{i=0}^{\mu}{\binom{\mu}{i}}\sum_{j=0}^{m-\mu}{\binom{m-\mu}{j}}{\binom{n}{i+j}}p_{i+j,i}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(m+1)!(n+1)!}{(m+n+1)!}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{m-i}{\binom{n}{i+j}}p_{i+j,i}(y)\sum_{\mu=i}^{m-j}{\binom{\mu}{i}}{\binom{m-\mu}{j}}p_{m,\mu}(x).

For the inner sum by using (
2
) one has

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu=i}^{m-j}{\binom{\mu}{i}}{\binom{m-\mu}{j}}p_{m,\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{m^{\underline{i+j}}}{i!j!}x^{i}(1-x)^{j}\sum_{\mu=i}^{m-j}p_{m-j-i,\mu-i}(x)

\displaystyle={\binom{m}{i+j}}p_{i+j,i}(x).

Therefore,

K_{m,n}(x,y)=\frac{(m+1)!(n+1)!}{(m+n+1)!}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{m-i}{\binom{n}{i+j}}p_{i+j,i}(y){\binom{m}{i+j}}p_{i+j,i}(x).

Collecting all terms with
i+j=k
we get our proposition.

Remark 2

Starting from the definition of the operators we have

\displaystyle K_{n_{r},\dots,n_{1}}(t_{r+1},t_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{k_{1}\geq 0}\cdots\sum_{k_{r}\geq 0}p_{n_{r},k_{r}}\left(t_{r+1}\right)p_{n_{r},k_{r}}\left(t_{r}\right)\cdots p_{n_{1},k_{1}}\left(t_{2}\right)p_{n_{1},k_{1}}\left(t_{1}\right)dt_{2}\cdots dt_{r}.

Using elementary calculations, we obtain the following representation of the
kernel

K_{n_{r},\dots,n_{1}}(x,y)=(n_{r}+1)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}\dfrac{n_{i}+1}{n_{i}+n_{i+1}+1}\right)\sum_{k_{1}\geq 0}\cdots\sum_{k_{r}\geq 0}{\binom{n_{r}}{k_{r}}}^{2}

\times\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}{\binom{n_{i}}{k_{i}}}^{2}{\binom{n_{i}+n_{i+1}}{k_{i}+k_{i+1}}}^{-1}\right)y^{k_{1}}(1-y)^{n_{1}-k_{1}}x^{k_{r}}(1-x)^{n_{r}-k_{r}}.

Next we consider the composition of three operators and derive the following
nice representation for the kernel.

Theorem 3

For
r=3
the kernel of the composition of classical Bernstein–Durrmeyer
M_{n_{3}}\circ M_{n_{2}}\circ M_{n_{1}}
operators has the representation

\displaystyle K_{n_{3},n_{2},n_{1}}\left(x,y\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(n_{3}+1\right)!\left(n_{2}+1\right)!\left(n_{1}+1\right)!\left(n_{3}+n_{2}+n_{1}+1\right)!}{\left(n_{3}+n_{2}+1\right)!\left(n_{3}+n_{1}+1\right)!\left(n_{2}+n_{1}+1\right)!}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{k=0}^{\min\{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\}}\frac{\binom{n_{3}}{k}\binom{n_{2}}{k}\binom{n_{1}}{k}}{\binom{n_{3}+n_{2}+n_{1}+1}{k}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}p_{k,\ell}\left(x\right)p_{k,\ell}\left(y\right).

Proof.
By Theorem
1
, we have

K_{n_{2},n_{1}}(t,y)=\dfrac{(n_{2}+1)!(n_{1}+1)!}{(n_{2}+n_{1}+1)!}\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}{\binom{n_{2}}{k}}{\binom{n_{1}}{k}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}p_{k,\ell}(t)p_{k,\ell}(y).

Using the recurrence formula for the kernel and Theorem
1
we get

\displaystyle K_{n_{3},n_{2},n_{1}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}K_{n_{2},n_{1}}(t,y)K_{n_{3}}(x,t)dt

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{(n_{2}+1)!(n_{1}+1)!}{(n_{2}+n_{1}+1)!}\sum_{k\geq 0}{\binom{n_{2}}{k}}{\binom{n_{1}}{k}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}p_{k,\ell}(t)p_{k,\ell}(y)

\displaystyle\times(n_{3}+1)\sum_{\mu=0}^{n_{3}}p_{n_{3},\mu}(x)p_{n_{3},\mu}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(n_{2}+1)!(n_{1}+1)!}{(n_{2}+n_{1}+1)!}\sum_{k\geq 0}{\binom{n_{2}}{k}}{\binom{n_{1}}{k}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}p_{k,\ell}(y)

\displaystyle\times(n_{3}+1)\sum_{\mu=0}^{\mu}p_{n_{3},\mu}(x)\int_{0}^{1}p_{k,\ell}(t)p_{n_{3},\mu}(t)dt.

Using (
3
) and (
4
) we obtain

\displaystyle K_{n_{3},n_{2},n_{1}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{(n_{3}+1)!(n_{2}+1)!(n_{1}+1)!}{(n_{2}+n_{1}+1)!}\sum_{k\geq 0}{\binom{n_{2}}{k}}{\binom{n_{1}}{k}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}p_{k,\ell}(y){\binom{k}{\ell}}\dfrac{1}{(k+n_{3}+1)!}\cdot S_{1},

where

S_{1}:=\displaystyle\sum_{\mu=0}^{n_{3}}p_{n_{3},\mu}(x)(k+n_{3}-\ell-\mu)^{\underline{k-l}}(\ell+\mu)^{\underline{\ell}}.

In the same way as in (
5
) we derive

S_{1}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}{\binom{\ell}{i}}\sum_{j=0}^{k-\ell}{\binom{k-\ell}{j}}{\binom{n_{3}}{i+j}}\ell!(k-\ell)!p_{i+j,i}\left(x\right).

Thus, by interchanging the order of summation we get

\displaystyle K_{n_{3},n_{2},n_{1}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(n_{3}+1)!(n_{2}+1)!(n_{1}+1)!}{(n_{2}+n_{1}+1)!}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{k\geq 0}{\binom{n_{2}}{k}}{\binom{n_{1}}{k}}\dfrac{k!}{(n_{3}+k+1)!}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\dfrac{1}{i!}\sum_{j=0}^{k-i}\dfrac{1}{j!}{\binom{n_{3}}{i+j}}p_{i+j,i}(x)S_{2},

where

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle:=\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=i}^{k-j}p_{k,\ell}(y)\ell^{\underline{i}}(k-\ell)^{\underline{j}}=\dfrac{k!}{(k-j-i)!}y^{i}(1-y)^{j}

\displaystyle={\binom{k}{i+j}}i!j!p_{i+j,i}(y).

Now

\displaystyle K_{n_{3},n_{2},n_{1}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(n_{3}+1)!(n_{2}+1)!(n_{1}+1)!}{(n_{2}+n_{1}+1)!}\sum_{k\geq 0}{\binom{n_{2}}{k}}{\binom{n_{1}}{k}}\dfrac{k!}{(n_{3}+k+1)!}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-i}{\binom{n_{3}}{i+j}}p_{i+j,i}(x){\binom{k}{i+j}}p_{i+j,i}(y).

Collecting all terms with
i+j=\ell
and interchanging the order of
summation leads to

\displaystyle K_{n_{3},n_{2},n_{1}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(n_{3}+1)!(n_{2}+1)!(n_{1}+1)!}{(n_{2}+n_{1}+1)!}\sum_{k\geq 0}{\binom{n_{2}}{k}}{\binom{n_{1}}{k}}\dfrac{k!}{(n_{3}+k+1)!}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}{\binom{n_{3}}{\ell}}{\binom{k}{\ell}}\sum_{\nu=0}^{\ell}p_{\ell,\nu}(x)p_{\ell,\nu}(y)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(n_{3}+1)!(n_{2}+1)!(n_{1}+1)!}{(n_{2}+n_{1}+1)!}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}{\binom{n_{3}}{\ell}}\sum_{\nu=0}^{\ell}p_{\ell,\nu}(x)p_{\ell,\nu}(y)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{k\geq\ell}{\binom{n_{2}}{k}}{\binom{n_{1}}{k}}\dfrac{k!}{(n_{3}+k+1)!}{\binom{k}{\ell}}.

For the inner sum we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq\ell}{\binom{n_{2}}{k}}{\binom{n_{1}}{k}}\dfrac{k!}{(k+n_{3}+1)!}{\binom{k}{\ell}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{n_{2}!n_{1}!}{\ell!}\dfrac{1}{(n_{2}+n_{3}+1)!(n_{1}-\ell)!}\sum_{k}{\binom{n_{1}-\ell}{k}}{\binom{n_{2}+n_{3}+1}{n_{2}-k-\ell}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{n_{2}!n_{1}!}{\ell!(n_{1}-\ell)!(n_{2}+n_{3}+1)!}{\binom{n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}-\ell+1}{n_{2}-\ell}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle K_{n_{3},n_{2},n_{1}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(n_{3}+1)!(n_{2}+1)!(n_{1}+1)!}{(n_{3}+n_{2}+1)!(n_{3}+n_{1}+1)!(n_{2}+n_{1}+1)!}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\ell\geq 0}(n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}-\ell+1)!\ell!{\binom{n_{3}}{\ell}}{\binom{n_{2}}{\ell}}{\binom{n_{1}}{\ell}}\sum_{\nu=0}^{\ell}p_{\ell,\nu}(x)p_{\ell,\nu}(y)

and the proof is complete.

3
Composition of several Bernstein–Durrmeyer operators

The purpose of this section is to show that a representation of the form

K_{n_{r},\ldots,n_{1}}\left(x,y\right)=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\left(n_{r},\ldots,n_{1}\right)\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}p_{k,\ell}(x)p_{k,\ell}(y),\,\quad x,y\in[0,1]

in fact exists not only for
r\in\left\{2,3\right\}
, but also for
arbitrary
r\in\mathbb{N}
.

In this section we use the eigenstructure of
M_{n}
in order to study the
kernel function of the composition of
r
Bernstein–Durrmeyer operators. As
a corollary we get a representation of the iterate
M_{n}^{r}
as a linear
combination of
M_{k}
,
k=0,1,\dots,n
.

Let
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n_{1},\cdots,n_{r}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
n:=\min\{n_{1},\dots,n_{r}\}
.

Theorem 4

The function

K_{n_{r},\cdots,n_{1}}(x,y)=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}\cdot(k+1)\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}p_{k,\ell}(x)p_{k,\ell}(y),\,\,x,y\in[0,1],

where
(c_{0},\dots,c_{n})
is the unique solution of the linear system

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dfrac{k^{\underline{j}}}{(k+j+1)^{\underline{j}}}c_{k}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\dfrac{n_{i}^{\underline{j}}}{(n_{i}+j+1)^{\underline{j}}},\,\,j=0,1,\dots,n,

(6)

is the kernel of the operator
M_{n_{r}}\circ\dots\circ M_{n_{1}}
, i.e.,

(M_{n_{r}}\circ\dots\circ M_{n_{1}})f(x)=\int_{0}^{1}f(y)K_{n_{r},\dots,n_{1}}(x,y)dy,\,\,f\in L_{1}[0,1],\,x\in[0,1].

Remark 5

The coefficients
c_{k}
,
k=0,\dots,n
, depend on
n_{1},\dots,n_{r}
.

Note that the linear system (
6
) has a unique solution since the
corresponding matrix

A_{n}=\left(\frac{k^{\underline{j}}}{\left(k+j+1\right)^{\underline{j}}}\right)_{j,k=0,\ldots,n}

(7)

is invertible. This can easily be seen. The square matrix
A_{n}
is upper
triangular since
k^{\underline{j}}=0
, if
j>k\geq 0
. Hence, its
determinant satisfies

\det A_{n}=\prod_{k=0}^{n}\frac{k!}{\left(2k+1\right)^{\underline{k}}}=\prod_{k=0}^{n}\binom{2k+1}{k}^{-1}\neq 0.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Let
W
be the integral operator on
L_{1}[0,1]
constructed with the kernel

K_{n_{r},\dots,n_{1}}
. We have

\displaystyle Wf(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}K_{n_{r},\dots,n_{1}}(x,y)f(y)dy=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f(y)\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}(k+1)\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}p_{k,\ell}(x)p_{k,\ell}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}(k+1)\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}p_{k,\ell}(x)\int_{0}^{1}f(y)p_{k,\ell}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}M_{k}f(x).

It is sufficient to prove that

WQ_{j}=(M_{n_{r}}\circ\dots\circ M_{n_{1}})Q_{j},\,\,j=0,1,\dots,n.

This is equivalent to

\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}M_{k}\right)Q_{j}=(M_{n_{r}}\circ\dots\circ M_{n_{1}})Q_{j},\,\,j=0,1,\dots,n,

and moreover, to the system (
6
). This concludes the proof.

Remark 6

For
r\geq 4
, it remains an open problem whether the coefficients
c_{k}

in Theorem
4
can be represented in a concise form as in the cases
r\leq 3
.

Theorem 7

The inverse of the matrix
A_{n}
defined in (
7
) is given by

A_{n}^{-1}=\left(\left(-1\right)^{k-\ell}\frac{2\ell+1}{k+1}\binom{k+\ell}{\ell}\binom{\ell}{k}\right)_{k,\ell=0,\ldots,n}.

Proof.
The equation
\binom{\ell}{k}=0
, for
k>\ell\geq 0
, reflects the fact
that matrix
A_{n}^{-1}
is upper triangular.

We calculate
A_{n}A_{n}^{-1}=:\left(e_{j,\ell}\right)_{j,\ell=0,\ldots,n}
. Noting that
k^{\underline{j}}=0
, if
0\leq k<j
, we have

\displaystyle e_{j,\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=j}^{\ell}\frac{k^{\underline{j}}}{\left(k+j+1\right)^{\underline{j}}}\cdot\left(-1\right)^{k-\ell}\frac{2\ell+1}{k+1}\binom{k+\ell}{\ell}\binom{\ell}{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\ell+1}{\ell!}\sum_{k=j}^{\ell}\left(-1\right)^{k-\ell}\binom{\ell}{k}\frac{k^{\underline{j}}\left(k+1\right)^{\overline{\ell}}}{\left(k+1\right)^{\overline{j+1}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\ell+1}{\ell!}\sum_{k=j}^{\ell}\left(-1\right)^{k-\ell}\binom{\ell}{k}\frac{k^{\underline{j}}\left(k+j+1\right)^{\overline{\ell-j}}}{k+j+1}.

In the case
j=\ell
we obtain

e_{\ell,\ell}=\frac{2\ell+1}{\ell!}\frac{\ell!}{2\ell+1}=1.

Next, we consider the case
j<\ell
:

e_{j,\ell}=\frac{2\ell+1}{\ell!}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\left(-1\right)^{k-\ell}\binom{\ell}{k}k^{\underline{j}}\left(k+j+2\right)^{\overline{\ell-j-1}}=0,

since
k^{\underline{j}}\left(k+j+2\right)^{\overline{\ell-j-1}}
is a
polynomial of degree
\ell-1
in the variable
k
. In the case
j>\ell
we
obviously have
e_{j,\ell}=0
.

Inspecting the proof of Theorem
4
we obtain the following result
concerning the iterates of the Bernstein–Durrmeyer operators.

Corollary 8

Let
r,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then,

M_{n}^{r}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}M_{k},

where
(c_{0},\dots,c_{n})
is the unique solution of the system

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dfrac{k^{\underline{j}}}{(k+j+1)^{\underline{j}}}c_{k}=\left(\dfrac{n^{\underline{j}}}{(n+j+1)^{\underline{j}}}\right)^{r},\,\,j=0,1,\dots,n.

4
The case of Szász–Mirakjan–Durrmeyer operators

For
\alpha\geq 0
we denote by
W_{\alpha}
the space of all locally
integrable functions on
[0,\infty)
, satisfying for
t\geq 0
an
exponential growth condition, i.e.,
|f(t)|\leq Ce^{\alpha t}
for some
positive constant
C
. For
f\in W_{\alpha}
the Szász–Mirakjan–Durrmeyer operators are defined by

\displaystyle S_{n}f(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(y)K_{n}(x,y)dy,

\displaystyle K_{n}(x,y)=n\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}s_{n,k}(x)s_{n,k}(y),\,\,s_{n,k}(x)=\dfrac{(nx)^{k}}{k!}e^{-nx}.

The commutativity of the Szász–Mirakjan–Durrmeyer operators was
established by Heilmann
[
4
]
. The following general result,
encompassing the commutativity and extending beyond it, was later proved by
Abel and Ivan in
[
1
]
using modified
Bessel functions. The same result was rediscovered in
[
5
, Theorem 1]
.

Here we present an elementary proof.

Theorem 9

For
m,n>0
, the kernel of the composition of Szász–Mirakjan–Durrmeyer operators
S_{m}\circ S_{n}
operators has the
representation

K_{m,n}\left(x,y\right)=K_{\frac{mn}{m+n}}\left(x,y\right).

The corresponding well-known formula for the basis functions are

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}s_{n,k}(x)=1,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}s_{n,k}\left(t\right)dt=\frac{1}{n},\text{ \quad}k=0,1,2,\ldots,

(8)

\displaystyle s_{m,\mu}\left(t\right)s_{n,\nu}\left(t\right)=\frac{m^{\mu}n^{\nu}}{(m+n)^{\mu+\nu}}\binom{\mu+\nu}{\nu}s_{m+n,\mu+\nu}\left(t\right).

(9)

Proof of Theorem
9
.

Using (
8
) and (
9
) we get

K_{m,n}(x,y)=\displaystyle\frac{mn}{m+n}\sum_{k\geq 0}s_{m,k}(x)\left(\dfrac{m}{m+n}\right)^{k}\dfrac{1}{k!}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}s_{n,\ell}(y)\left(\dfrac{n}{m+n}\right)^{\ell}(k+\ell)^{\underline{k}}.

Writing

(k+\ell)^{\underline{k}}=\left.\left(\dfrac{d}{ds}\right)^{k}s^{k+\ell}\right|_{s=1},

we obtain for the inner sum

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\geq 0}s_{n,\ell}(y)\left.\left(\dfrac{n}{m+n}\right)^{\ell}\left(\dfrac{d}{ds}\right)^{k}s^{k+\ell}\right|_{s=1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-ny}\left(\dfrac{d}{ds}\right)^{k}\left.\left\{s^{k}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\dfrac{1}{\ell!}{\left(\dfrac{n^{2}ys}{m+n}\right)^{\ell}}\right\}\right|_{s=1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-ny}\left(\dfrac{d}{ds}\right)^{k}\left.\left\{s^{k}\exp\left(\dfrac{n^{2}ys}{m+n}\right)\right\}\right|_{s=1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-ny}\sum_{j=0}^{k}{\binom{k}{j}}\dfrac{k!}{j!}\left(\dfrac{n^{2}y}{m+n}\right)^{j}\exp\left(\dfrac{n^{2}y}{m+n}\right),

where the last equation follows by Leibniz’ rule. Thus we get

\displaystyle K_{m,n}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{mn}{m+n}\exp\left(-ny-mx+\dfrac{n^{2}y}{m+n}\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}\left(\dfrac{m^{2}x}{m+n}\right)^{k}\dfrac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}{\binom{k}{j}}\dfrac{1}{j!}\left(\dfrac{n^{2}y}{m+n}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{mn}{m+n}\exp\left(-ny-mx+\dfrac{n^{2}y}{m+n}\right)\sum_{j\geq 0}\dfrac{1}{j!j!}\left(\dfrac{n^{2}y}{m+n}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq j}\left(\dfrac{m^{2}x}{m+n}\right)^{k}\dfrac{1}{(k-j)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{mn}{m+n}\exp\left(-ny-mx+\dfrac{n^{2}y}{m+n}+\dfrac{m^{2}x}{m+n}\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\dfrac{1}{j!j!}\left(\dfrac{n^{2}y}{m+n}\right)^{j}\left(\dfrac{m^{2}x}{m+n}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{mn}{m+n}\exp\left(-\dfrac{mn}{m+n}x-\dfrac{mn}{m+n}y\right)\sum_{j\geq 0}\dfrac{1}{j!}\left(\dfrac{mn}{m+n}x\right)^{j}\dfrac{1}{j!}\left(\dfrac{mn}{m+n}y\right)^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{mn}{m+n}\sum_{j\geq 0}s_{\frac{mn}{m+n},j}(x)s_{\frac{mn}{m+n},j}(y),

which completes the proof.

Remark 10

This can be iterated

K_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}}\left(x,y\right)=K_{1/\left(1/n_{1}+\cdots+1/n_{r}\right)}\left(x,y\right)

and be rewritten in the form

K_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}}\left(x,y\right)=K_{H\left(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}\right)/r}\left(x,y\right),

where
H\left(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}\right)
denotes the harmonic mean of the
positive numbers
n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}
.

Remark 11

In particular, the iterate
S_{n}^{r}
has the kernel

K_{n,\ldots,n}\left(x,y\right)=K_{n/r}\left(x,y\right).

Of course,
K_{m,n}\left(x,y\right)
can be represented in terms of Bessel
functions (see
[
1
]
)

K_{n}(x,y)=ne^{-n(x+y)}\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(nx)^{k}}{k!}\dfrac{(ny)^{k}}{k!}=ne^{-n(x+y)}I_{0}(2n\sqrt{xy}),

where
I_{0}
denotes the modified Bessel function given by

I_{0}(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j!)^{-2}\left(\dfrac{z^{2}}{4}\right)^{j}.

Our derivation does not make use of properties of Bessel functions, in
particular, integral representations.

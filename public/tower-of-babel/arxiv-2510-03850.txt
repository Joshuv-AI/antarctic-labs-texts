Abstract
The sum of random variables (RVs) appears extensively in wireless communications, at large, both conventional and advanced, and has been subject of longstanding research. The statistical characterization of the referred sum is crucial to determine the performance of such communications systems. Although efforts have been undertaken to unveil these sum statistics, e.g., probability density function (PDF) and cumulative distribution function (CDF), no general efficient nor manageable solutions capable of evaluating the exact sum PDF and CDF are available to date. The only formulations are given in terms of either the multi-fold Brennan’s integral or the multivariate Fox H H -function. Unfortunately, these methods are only feasible up to a certain number of RVs, meaning that when the number of RVs in the sum increases, the computation of the sum PDF and CDF is subject to stability problems, convergence issues, or inaccurate results. In this paper, we derive new, simple, exact formulations for the PDF and CDF of the sum of L L independent and identically distributed α \alpha - μ \mu RVs. Unlike the available solutions, the computational complexity of our analytical expressions is independent of the number of summands. Capitalizing on our unprecedented findings, we analyze, in exact and asymptotic manners, the performance of L L -branch pre-detection equal-gain combining and maximal-ratio combining receivers over α \alpha - μ \mu fading environments. The coding and diversity gains of the system for both receivers are analyzed and quantified. Moreover, numerical simulations show that the computation time reduces drastically when using our expressions, which are arguably the most efficient and manageable formulations derived so far.

II
Problem Statement and Preliminaries

The problem tackled here is an age-old one, a challenge that has been the subject of a number of works by several researchers. More particularly, the question addressed is to find exact statistics, namely PDF, CDF, and applications thereof, for the sum of fading envelopes. We solve this problem for a rather general distribution: the
\alpha
-
\mu
model.

Let
R
be the sum of
L
i.i.d.
\alpha
-
\mu
RVs
R_{n}
, i.e.,

R=\sum_{n=1}^{L}R_{n}.

(1)

A signal with envelope
\{R_{n}\}_{n=1}^{L}
modeled by the
\alpha
-
\mu
distribution has a PDF given as
[
47
]

\displaystyle f_{R_{n}}(r_{n})=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha\mu^{\mu}r_{n}^{\alpha\mu-1}}{\Gamma(\mu)\hat{r}^{\alpha\mu}}\exp\left(-\mu\left(\frac{r_{n}}{\hat{r}}\right)^{\alpha}\right),

(2)

where
\alpha>0
is a nonlinearity parameter,
\hat{r}\triangleq\sqrt[\alpha]{\mathbb{E}\left[R_{n}^{\alpha}\right]}
is the
\alpha
-root mean value,
\mu>0
is the inverse of the normalized variance of
R_{n}^{\alpha}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{\mathbb{E}^{2}\left[R_{n}^{\alpha}\right]}{\mathbb{V}\left[R_{n}^{\alpha}\right]},

(3)

and
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the gamma function
[
48
, eq. 6.1.1]
.

The CDF of
R_{n}
is given by
[
47
]

\displaystyle F_{R_{n}}(r_{n})=\frac{\gamma\left(\mu,\mu r_{n}^{\alpha}/\hat{r}^{\alpha}\right)}{\Gamma(\mu)},

(4)

where
\gamma(\cdot,\cdot)
is the lower incomplete gamma function
[
49
, eq. (8.2.1)]
.

The
\alpha
-
\mu
distribution is a general and versatile fading model
that includes several other important small-scale fading models as special cases, such as one-sided
Gaussian, negative exponential, Rayleigh, Gamma, Weibull, and Nakagami-
m
distributions.
The solutions proposed next for the sum PDF and CDF are exact, simple, easy to compute, and unprecedented in the literature.

(6b)

-branch pre-detection EGC diversity system with equal noise levels is given by

\displaystyle\Psi_{\text{EGC}}=\frac{E_{s}}{LN_{0}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{L}R_{n}\right)^{2}=\frac{E_{s}}{LN_{0}}R_{\text{EGC}}^{2},

where

\alpha
-
\mu

]

On the other hand, the instantaneous SNR in an
L
-branch pre-detection MRC diversity system with equal noise levels is defined as

\displaystyle\Psi_{\text{MRC}}=\frac{E_{s}}{LN_{0}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{L}R_{n}^{2}\right)=\frac{E_{s}}{LN_{0}}R_{\text{MRC}}.

(15)

To determine the statistics of
\Psi_{\text{EGC}}
and
\Psi_{\text{MRC}}

and

\displaystyle f_{R_{\nu}}(r)=\left(\frac{\alpha\mu^{\mu}}{\vartheta_{\nu}\Gamma(\mu)\hat{r}^{\alpha\mu}}\right)^{L}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{\delta_{i}r^{\frac{\alpha i+\alpha\mu L}{\vartheta_{\nu}}-1}}{\Gamma(\frac{i\alpha+L\mu\alpha}{\vartheta_{\nu}})},

, and

\displaystyle f_{\Psi_{\nu}}(\psi)=

\displaystyle\frac{\vartheta_{\nu}LN_{0}}{2E_{s}}\left(\frac{\alpha\mu^{\mu}}{\vartheta_{\nu}\Gamma(\mu)\hat{r}^{\alpha\mu}}\right)^{L}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{\delta_{i}\left(\frac{LN_{0}\psi}{E_{s}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}(\alpha i+\alpha\mu L)-1}}{\Gamma(\frac{i\alpha+L\mu\alpha}{\vartheta_{\nu}})}.

(17)

Now, from (
IV
), the CDF of
\Psi_{\nu}
can be calculated as

\displaystyle F_{\Psi_{\nu}}(\psi)\triangleq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\psi}f_{\Psi_{\nu}}(u)\text{d}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha\mu^{\mu}}{\vartheta_{\nu}\Gamma(\mu)\hat{r}^{\alpha\mu}}\right)^{L}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{\delta_{i}\left(\frac{N_{0}L\psi}{E_{s}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}(\alpha i+\alpha\mu L)}}{\Gamma(\frac{i\alpha+L\mu\alpha}{\vartheta_{\nu}}+1)}.

(18)

IV-
1
Average Symbol Error Rate

The ASER for a pre-detection EGC/MRC receiver is expressed as
[
50
, eq. (9.61)]

\displaystyle P_{\textmd{e}_{\nu}}=\int^{\infty}_{0}Q\left(\sqrt{2\mathcal{G}\psi}\right)f_{\Psi_{\nu}}(\psi)\text{d}\psi,

(19)

where
Q\left(\cdot\right)
is
the Gaussian
Q
-function
[
50
, eq. (4.1)]
, and
\mathcal{G}
is a modulation-dependent parameter such that
\mathcal{G}=1
for binary phase-shift keying (BPSK),
\mathcal{G}=1/2
for orthogonal BPSK, and
\mathcal{G}=0.715
for BPSK with minimum correlation
[
51
]
.
Other values of
\mathcal{G}
can also be used for other modulation schemes
[
50
]
.

Using the alternative representation of the Gaussian complementary cumulative function
[
50
, eq. (4.2)]
, we can rewrite (
19
) as follows:

\displaystyle P_{\textmd{e}_{\nu}}=\frac{1}{2}\int^{\infty}_{0}\text{erfc}\left(r\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{G}E_{s}}{LN_{0}}}\right)f_{R_{\nu}}(r)\text{d}r,

(20)

where
\text{erfc}\left(\cdot\right)
is the complementary error function
[
52
, eq. (06.27.07.0001.01)]
. Substituting (
5
) into (
20
), and changing the order of integration, we obtain

\displaystyle P_{\textmd{e}_{\nu}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\alpha\mu^{\mu}}{\vartheta_{\nu}\Gamma(\mu)\hat{r}^{\alpha\mu}}\right)^{L}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{\delta_{i}}{\Gamma(\frac{i\alpha+L\mu\alpha}{\vartheta_{\nu}})}

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{erfc}\left(r\sqrt{\frac{E_{s}\mathcal{G}}{LN_{0}}}\right)r^{\frac{\alpha i+\alpha\mu L}{\vartheta_{\nu}}-1}\,\text{d}r.

(21)

The previous integral can be solved with the aid of
[
53
, eq. (1.5.2.1)]
. Then, after simplifications, we finally attain

\displaystyle P_{\textmd{e}_{\nu}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\alpha\mu^{\mu}}{\vartheta_{\nu}\Gamma(\mu)\hat{r}^{\alpha\mu}}\right)^{L}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{\delta_{i}\left(\frac{4E_{s}\mathcal{G}}{LN_{0}}\right)^{-\frac{\alpha i+\alpha\mu L}{2\vartheta_{\nu}}}}{\Gamma(\frac{i\alpha+L\mu\alpha}{2\vartheta_{\nu}}+1)}.

(22)

An important region for the performance analysis of communications systems is the high-SNR regime. To address this, we take the most significant term in the summation of (
22

i=0

\displaystyle P_{\textmd{e}_{\nu}}\simeq

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{C}_{\textmd{e}_{\nu}}\frac{E_{s}}{N_{0}}\right)^{-\mathcal{D}_{\textmd{e}_{\nu}}},

(23)

where
\mathcal{D}_{\textmd{e}_{\nu}}=(\alpha\mu L)/2\vartheta_{\nu}

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\textmd{e}_{\nu}}=\frac{4\mathcal{G}}{L}\left(\frac{\left(\frac{\alpha\mu^{\mu}\Gamma(\alpha\mu)}{\Gamma(\mu)\vartheta_{\nu}\hat{r}^{\alpha\mu}}\right)^{L}}{2\,\Gamma\left(\frac{L\alpha\mu}{2\vartheta_{\nu}}+1\right)}\right)^{-\frac{2\vartheta_{\nu}}{\alpha\mu L}}

(24)

is coding gain for the ASER.

IV-
2
Outage Probability

The OP is the probability that the instantaneous SNR falls below a certain
threshold,
\gamma_{\text{out}}

\displaystyle P_{\text{out}_{\nu}}\triangleq\text{Pr}\left[\Psi_{\nu}\leq\gamma_{\text{out}}\right]=\int_{0}^{\gamma_{\text{out}}}f_{\Psi_{\nu}}(\psi)\text{d}\psi.

(25)

Since the OP is the CDF of the instantaneous SNR evaluated at
\gamma_{\text{out}}

IV

\displaystyle P_{\text{out}_{\nu}}\triangleq

\displaystyle F_{\Psi_{\nu}}(\gamma_{\text{out}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha\mu^{\mu}}{\vartheta_{\nu}\Gamma(\mu)\hat{r}^{\alpha\mu}}\right)^{L}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{\delta_{i}\left(\frac{E_{s}}{N_{0}L\gamma_{\text{out}}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}(\alpha i+\alpha\mu L)}}{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha i+\alpha\mu L}{\vartheta_{\nu}}+1)}.

(26)

Similarly, as for the ASEP, we address the high-SNR regime for the OP by taking the first term in the summation of (

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{C}_{\text{out}_{\nu}}\frac{E_{s}}{N_{0}}\right)^{-\mathcal{D}_{\text{out}_{\nu}}},

(27)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\text{out}_{\nu}}=\frac{1}{L\gamma_{\text{out}}}\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{L\alpha\mu}{\vartheta_{\nu}}+1\right)\left(\frac{\alpha\mu^{\mu}\Gamma(\alpha\mu)}{\Gamma(\mu)\vartheta_{\nu}\hat{r}^{\alpha\mu}}\right)^{L}\right)^{-\frac{2}{\alpha\mu L}}

(28)

is coding gain for the OP.

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we provided new, handy, exact formulations for the sum PDF and the sum CDF of i.i.d.
\alpha
-
\mu
RVs.
Our results are arguably the most efficient and tractable formulations to date. It is noteworthy that, as opposed to the available solutions, whose tractability becomes impracticable with the increase of the number of RVs, our solution’ complexity is independent of the number of summands.
As an application example, we analyzed the performance of
L
-branch pre-detection EGC and MRC receivers operating over
\alpha
-
\mu
fading channels. With this goal, we provided exact and asymptotic expressions for the ASER and the OP, which so far have been investigated in terms of time-consuming or approximate solutions. In future works, we aim to explore our proposed analysis considering a broad range of emergent scenarios, such as those in the presence of high co-channel interference. Additionally, an extension of this framework applied to i.n.i.d. shall be tackled.

and a branch cut along the negative real axis.
Accordingly, we now define
\mathfrak{L}_{\textbf{s},2}

(L\alpha\mu+\alpha i)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

Thus, by applying the residue theorem, (

\displaystyle f_{R}(r)=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha\mu^{\mu}}{\Gamma(\mu)\hat{r}^{\alpha\mu}}\right)^{L}\mathcal{R}\left[\Theta(s);(s=0)\right],

Finally, since the residue operation is a

Appendix B
Absolute Convergence of (
5
)

Herein, we show the absolute convergence of (
5
). To this end, we have to prove that
[
55
]

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\delta_{i}r^{\alpha i+\alpha\mu L-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha i+\alpha\mu L)}\right|<\infty,

(49)

or, equivalently,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left|\delta_{i}\right|r^{\alpha i+\alpha\mu L-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha i+\alpha\mu L)}<\infty.

(50)

An upper bound for
|\delta_{i}|
,
\forall i\geq 1
, can be obtained by taking the absolute value of the summands, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left|\delta_{i}\right|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i\Gamma(\alpha\mu)}\sum_{l=1}^{i}\frac{\left|\delta_{i-l}\right|\left|lL+l-i\right|\Gamma(\alpha(l+\mu))\left(\mu\left(\frac{1}{\hat{r}}\right)^{\alpha}\right)^{l}}{l!}.

(51)

Notice that depending on
\alpha
, (
51
) can be either a decreasing function (if
0<\alpha<1
) or an increasing function (if
\alpha\geq 1
). Hence, we must obtain a bound for each condition.

For the case of
0<\alpha<1
,
\left|\delta_{i}\right|
can be bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\left|\delta_{i}\right|\overset{(a)}{\leq}

\displaystyle\frac{\mu\left|\delta_{i-1}\right|\left|L+1-i\right|\Gamma(\alpha(1+\mu))}{i\Gamma(\alpha\mu)}\left(\frac{1}{\hat{r}}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{<}

\displaystyle 2\mu L\left|\delta_{i-1}\right|\Gamma(\alpha(1+\mu))\left(\frac{1}{\hat{r}}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{=}

\displaystyle r^{\alpha\mu L-1}\Gamma(\alpha\mu)^{L}E_{\alpha,\alpha\mu L}\left(2\mu L\left(\frac{r}{\hat{r}}\right)^{\alpha}\Gamma(\mu\alpha+\alpha)\right),

(52)

where in step (a) we used the fact that
\left|\delta_{i}\right|<\left|\delta_{i-1}\right|
, in step (b) we employed that
\left|-i+1+L\right|\leq iL
and that
{1/\Gamma(\alpha\mu)<2}
,
\forall(\alpha,\mu)
, and in step (c) we recursively solved the coefficients
\left|\delta_{i-1}\right|
.

Now, with the aid of (
B
), an upper bound for (
50
) can be obtained as

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\delta_{i}\right|r^{\alpha i+\alpha\mu L-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha i+\alpha\mu L)}\overset{(a)}{<}r^{\alpha\mu L-1}\Gamma(\alpha\mu)^{L}

\displaystyle\times\left[\frac{1}{\Gamma(L\alpha\mu)}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left(2\mu L\Gamma(\mu\alpha+\alpha)\left(\frac{r}{\hat{r}}\right)^{\alpha}\right)^{i}}{\Gamma(i\alpha+L\mu\alpha)}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{=}r^{\alpha\mu L-1}\Gamma(\alpha\mu)^{L}E_{\alpha,\alpha\mu L}\left(2\mu L\left(\frac{r}{\hat{r}}\right)^{\alpha}\Gamma(\mu\alpha+\alpha)\right),

(53)

where in step (a) we separated the first term of the sum and used (
B
), and in step (b) we employed the Mittag-Leffler function denoted by
E_{(\cdot,\cdot)}(\cdot)

[
49
, eq. (10.46.3)]
.

For the case of
\alpha\geq 1
,
\left|\delta_{i}\right|
can be bounded as

\displaystyle\left|\delta_{i}\right|\overset{(a)}{<}

\displaystyle 2L\sum_{l=1}^{i}\frac{\left|\delta_{i-l}\right|\Gamma(\alpha(l+\mu))\left(\mu\left(\frac{1}{\hat{r}}\right)^{\alpha}\right)^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}

\displaystyle\frac{2L\,\Gamma(\alpha\mu)^{L}\Gamma(\alpha(l+\mu))\left(\mu\left(\frac{1}{\hat{r}}\right)^{\alpha}\right)^{i}}{i!}

(54)

where in step (a) we employed that
\left|-i+l+lL\right|\leq iL
, for
1\leq l\leq i
, and that
{1/\Gamma(\alpha\mu)<2}
,
\forall(\alpha,\mu)
, and in step (b) we only used the last term of the sum (i.e.,
l=i
).

From (
B
), an upper bound for (
50
) can be attained as

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\delta_{i}\right|r^{\alpha i+\alpha\mu L-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha i+\alpha\mu L)}\overset{(a)}{<}r^{\alpha\mu L-1}\Gamma(\alpha\mu)^{L}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{\Gamma(L\alpha\mu)}+2L\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha(i+\mu))\left(\mu\left(\frac{r}{\hat{r}}\right)^{\alpha}\right)^{i}}{i!\Gamma(i\alpha+L\mu\alpha)}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}

\displaystyle r^{\alpha\mu L-1}\Gamma(\alpha\mu)^{L}\left[\frac{1}{\Gamma(L\alpha\mu)}+2L\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\mu\left(\frac{r}{\hat{r}}\right)^{\alpha}\right)^{i}}{i!}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{=}

\displaystyle r^{\alpha\mu L-1}\Gamma(\alpha\mu)^{L}\left[\frac{1}{\Gamma(L\alpha\mu)}+2L\exp\left(\mu\left(\frac{r}{\hat{r}}\right)^{\alpha}\right)-2L\right],

(55)

where in step (a) we separated the first term of the sum and used (
B

\Gamma(\alpha(i+\mu))/\Gamma(\alpha i+\alpha\mu L)\leq 1
was applied, and in step (c) we used
[
48
, eq. 4.2.1]
.

Finally, as (
B
) and (
B
) exist and are finite, then
(
5

, which completes the proof.

Extensive bilateral adrenal calcification in von Hippel-Lindau syndrome–associated pheochromocytoma
Authors: P Sai Kameshwar Rao, Manasvini Bhatt, Devasenathipathy Kandasamy, Nikhil Tandon
Published: 2026

Image legend

A 42-year-old man presented with bilateral adrenal masses detected during evaluation of abdominal discomfort and vomiting. He had no symptoms of catecholamine excess, a negative family history, and a normal fundus examination. A plain radiograph (Fig. 1A) of the chest and upper abdomen showed bilateral calcified masses, likely adrenal calcifications. Plasma normetanephrine was >10 times the upper limit of normal. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography axial (Fig. 1B) and coronal (Fig. 1C) sections showed bilateral enhancing suprarenal masses with central necrosis and coarse calcifications (right: 7.3 × 4.5 × 4.9 cm; left: 11.8 × 8.8 × 12.8 cm), with adrenal glands not visualized separately. A 2 × 2 cm pancreatic head lesion suggestive of neuroendocrine tumor and multiple pancreatic cysts were noted, without renal cysts or renal cell carcinoma. Contrast-enhanced brain magnetic resonance imaging (Fig. 1D) revealed a large solid-cystic left basal ganglia lesion with mass effect and midline shift, consistent with hemangioblastoma. These findings prompted evaluation for von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) syndrome. Structural and functional imaging showed no evidence of any distant metastasis. Supratentorial hemangioblastomas are extremely rare, accounting for around 1% of hemangioblastomas in VHL. Genetic testing identified a pathogenic VHL variant, c.499C > T(p.Arg167Trp). Adrenalectomy confirmed pheochromocytoma with zellballen architecture and positive S100 and succinate dehydrogenase B immunohistochemical staining. Adrenal calcification, although nonspecific, is more often associated with benign adrenal lesions [1]. However, calcification doesn’t necessarily exclude pheochromocytomas. In a large retrospective series, pheochromocytoma accounted for only 2% of adrenal calcifications [2]. Extensive bilateral adrenal calcification in VHL-associated pheochromocytoma is rare. This case highlights that calcified adrenal masses should not preclude consideration of pheochromocytoma in patients with features of hereditary tumor syndrome.

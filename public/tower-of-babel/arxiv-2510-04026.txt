1.
Introduction

We denote by
P^{+}(n)
the largest prime factor of an integer
n
,
with the convention that
P^{+}(1)=1
. The study of largest prime factor of shifted prime
P^{+}(p+a),a\in\mathbb{Z}
is of significant importance. First, the infinitude of primes
p
with
P^{+}(p+2)>p
is equivalent to the twin prime conjecture, which is one of the most well-known open problems in number theory; second, an unexpected connection between large value of
P^{+}(p-1)
and the first case of Fermat’s last theorem, was established by Adleman and Heath-Brown
[
1
]
, and Fouvry
[
12
]
. Last but not least, small values of
P^{+}(p+a)
plays an important role in cryptography, such as Pollard’s
p-1
algorithm and Williams’
p+1
algorithm.

In this article, we study the quantity
T_{c}(x)
defined by

T_{c}(x):=\big|\big\{p\leq x:p\in\mathbb{P},P^{+}(p-1)\geq p^{c}\big\}\big|,

where
0<c<1
and
\mathbb{P}
is the set of primes.
As an application of the Bombieri-Vinogradov theorem as well as the Brun-Titchmarsh inequality, Goldfeld
[
13
]
proved in 1969 that

\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{1/2}(x)/\pi(x)\geq 1/2.

Goldfeld also pointed out that his arguments could also lead to, for any
c<7/12

(1.1)

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)>0.

There are a number of improvements on the value of
c
in (
1.1
), see, e.g. Motohashi
[
23
]
, Hooley
[
20
,
21
]
, Deshouillers–Iwaniec
[
7
]
, Fouvry
[
12
]
and Baker-Harman
[
2
]
. The best record of
c
up to now is
0.677
, obtained by Baker and Harman
[
3
]
.

In 2015 Luca, Menares and Pizarro-Madariaga
[
19
]
considered the explicit lower bound of

T_{c}(x)

for small values of
c
. Specifically, for
1/4\leq c\leq 1/2
they proved that

(1.2)

\displaystyle T_{c}(x)\geq(1-c)\frac{x}{\log x}+E(x)

where

\displaystyle E(x)\ll\begin{cases}x\log\log x/(\log x)^{2},&\text{for~}1/4<c\leq 1/2,\\
x/(\log x)^{5/3},&\text{for~}c=1/4.\end{cases}

Later, Chen and Chen
[
6
]
extended the range of
c
to
(0,1/2)
in (
1.2
) with slightly better
E(x)
.
Chen and Chen also proved that for any
k\geq 2
there exists at most one
c\in\big[\frac{1}{k+1},\frac{1}{k}\big)
such that

T_{c}(x)=(1-c)\frac{x}{\log x}+o\Big(\frac{x}{\log x}\Big).

Based on their result, Chen and Chen conjectured that for any
k\geq 1
and
c\in\big[\frac{1}{k+1},\frac{1}{k}\big)
we have

(1.3)

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)\geq 1-\frac{1}{k+1}.

In 2018, Feng and Wu
[
11
]
proved that

\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)\geq 1-4\int_{1/c-1}^{1/c}\frac{\rho(t)}{t}dt

holds for
0<c<0.3517\ldots
, where
\rho(u)
is the Dickman function, defined as the unique continuous solution of the equation differential-difference

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\rho(u)=1,&0\leq u\leq 1,\\
u\rho^{\prime}(u)=-\rho(u-1),&u>1.\end{cases}

As a corollary, Feng and Wu proved conjecture (
1.3
) for
k\geq 3
by numerical values involving the Dickman function. The lower bounds of
T_{c}(x)
were further improved by Liu, Wu and Xi
[
18
]
to

\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)\geq 1-4\rho(1/c)

provided
0<c<0.3734\ldots
.

Later Ding
[
8
, Final remarks]
pointed out that conjecture (
1.3
) of Chen and Chen in fact contradicts the Elliott–Halberstam conjecture according to the works of Pomerance
[
24
]
, Granville
[
14
]
, Wang
[
25
]
and Wu
[
27
]
. That is, one has

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)=\left(1-\rho\left(\frac{1}{c}\right)\right)\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as}~c\rightarrow 1

under the assumption of the Elliott–Halberstam conjecture. Motivated by this, Ding
[
8
]
then proved unconditionally that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)<1/2

for some absolute constant
c<1
, thus disproving conjecture (
1.3
) for the case
k=1
. The proof of (
1.4
) by Ding is based on the following corollary of Brun-Titchmarsh type. For
(a,m)=1
let
\pi(x;m,a)
denotes the number of primes
p
not exceeding
x
such that
p\equiv a\pmod{m}
.

Proposition 1.1
.

[
27
, Lemma 2.2]

There exist two functions
K_{2}(\theta)>K_{1}(\theta)>0
, defined on the interval
(0,17/32)
such that for each fixed
A>0
, and sufficiently large
Q=x^{\theta}
, the inequalities

K_{1}(\theta)\frac{\pi(x)}{\varphi(m)}\leq\pi(x;m,1)\leq K_{2}(\theta)\frac{\pi(x)}{\varphi(m)}

hold for all integers
m\in(Q,2Q]
with at most
O\left(Q(\log Q)^{-A}\right)
exceptions, where the implied constant depends only on
A
and
\theta
. Moreover, for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, these functions can be chosen to satisfy the following properties:

\bullet

K_{1}(\theta)
is monotonic decreasing, and
K_{2}(\theta)
is monotonic increasing.

\bullet

K_{1}(1/2)=1-\varepsilon
and
K_{2}(1/2)=1+\varepsilon
.

The constant
c
in (
1.4
) could further be specified by explicit values of
K_{1}(\theta)
in Proposition
1.1
. In fact, one has
K_{1}(\theta)\geq 0.16
for
1/2\leq\theta\leq 13/25

[
2
, Theorem 1]
and
K_{1}(\theta)\geq 1/100
for
13/25\leq\theta\leq 17/32

[
22
, Eq. (4)]
. Using the method of Ding
[
8
]
as well as the explicit values of
K_{1}(\theta)
, Xinyue Zang (private communication) obtained that

(1.5)

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)\leq\frac{0.496875}{c}<\frac{1}{2},\quad\text{for~}0.993375<c<1.

However, there are earlier results related to conjecture (
1.3
) of Chen and Chen as well as Ding’s result (
1.4
). Actually, as indicated by the proof of a former result of Erdős
[
10
, from line -6, page 212 to line 4, page 213]
, as early as 1935, people could already conclude that

(1.6)

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as}~c\rightarrow 1

by combining with Wu’s lemma (see Lemma
2.3
below). Clearly, (
1.4
) is now a simple corollary of (
1.6
). In a later article, Ding
[
9
]
obtained a quantitative form of Erdős’ result (
1.6
), stating

(1.7)

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)\leq 8\big(c^{-1}-1\big)

for
8/9<c<1
. By (
1.7
) one notes easily that

(1.8)

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)<\frac{1}{2}

for any
16/17<c<1
which improved the numerical values of (
1.5
). It should be mentioned that almost the same time as Ding’s result (
1.7
), Bharadwaj and Rodgers
[
4
]
independently obtained the same
result (
1.6
) with a general form in probabilistic language.
†
†

†

All of the authors (Ding, Bharadwaj and Rodgers) were unaware of Erdős’ result at an earlier time.
Erdős’ result (
1.6
) is an application of Brun’s method, while the proof of (
1.7
) is mainly based on the following quantitative version of Selberg’s upper bound sieve.

Proposition 1.2
.

[
15
, page 172, Theorem 5.7]

Let
g
be a natural number, and let
a_{i},b_{i}~(i=1,2,\cdot\cdot\cdot,g)
be integers satisfying

E:=\prod_{i=1}^{g}a_{i}\prod_{1\leq r<s\leq g}(a_{r}b_{s}-a_{s}b_{r})\neq 0.

Let
\varrho(p)
denote the number of solutions
n\pmod{p}
to the congruence

\prod_{i=1}^{g}(a_{i}n+b_{i})\equiv 0\pmod{p},

and suppose that

\varrho(p)<p\quad\text{for~all~}p.

Let
y
and
z
be real numbers satisfying

1<y\leq z.

Then we have

\big|\big\{n:z-y<n\leq z,a_{i}n+b_{i}~\text{prime~for~}i=1,2,\cdot\cdot\cdot,g\big\}\big|\\
\leq 2^{g}g!\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{\varrho(p)-1}{p-1}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-g+1}\!\!\!\frac{y}{\log^{g}y}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\log\log 3y+\log\log 3|E|}{\log y}\right)\right),

where the constant implied by the
O
-symbol depends at most on
g
.

For the proof of (
1.7
), one used Proposition
1.2
in the particular case
g=2
. Hence, the constant factor
8
in (
1.7
) comes from the identity

2^{g}g!=8.

In this article, we shall give a further improvement of (
1.7
) with two new ingredients: the first is the employment of Rosser-Iwaniec linear sieve to the prime variable sequence, instead of integer variable polynomial combining with the two dimensional sieve (i.e. Proposition 1.2 above); the second one is that when dealing with the error term coming from linear sieve, we apply a theorem of Bombieri-Friedlander-Iwaniec type with level of distribution
x^{4/7-\varepsilon}
instead of the classical level
x^{1/2-\varepsilon}
.

Our main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
e^{-\frac{2}{7}}<c<1
we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)\leq-\frac{7}{2}\log c.

Remark 1
.

Theorem
1.1
provides a nontrivial upper bound of
T_{c}(x)
for any
e^{-\frac{2}{7}}<c<1
. Here the lower bound of
c
is approximately

e^{-\frac{2}{7}}=0.75147\ldots,

which could be compared to
8/9=0.88888\ldots
in (
1.7
). Thus, Theorem
1.1
extends the range of
c
in (
1.7
). Furthermore, one may see easily that

-\frac{7}{2}\log c<8\big(c^{-1}-1\big)

for any
8/9<c<1
and hence Theorem
1.1
also improves the upper bound of
T_{c}(x)
in (
1.7
).

The following corollary of Theorem
1.1
improved (
1.4
), (
1.5
) and (
1.8
) considerably.

Corollary 1.1
.

For any
c>e^{-\frac{1}{7}}
we have
\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)<1/2
.

Remark 2
.

The numerical value of
e^{-\frac{1}{7}}
is
0.86687\ldots
. In
[
9
, Remarks]
, it was concluded that

\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)<1/2
for any
c>e^{-\frac{1}{2}}=0.60653\ldots
under the Elliott-Halberstam conjecture. Corollary
1.1
makes some further progress toward this direction.

2.
Fundamental lemmas

Let
\mu(n)
be the Möbius function. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a finite sequence of positive integers and
\mathcal{P}
a subset of primes. For any
z\geq 2
, let

P(z)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq z\\
p\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}p.

Next for square-free number
d
with
d|P(z)
, we define

\mathcal{A}_{d}=:\big\{a\in\mathcal{A}:d|a\big\}.

Define the sieve function
S(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{P},z)
to be

S(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{P},z):=\big|\big\{a\in\mathcal{A}:(a,P(z))=1\big\}\big|.

Suppose that
\big|\mathcal{A}_{d}\big|
possesses the following form

\big|\mathcal{A}_{d}\big|=\frac{\omega(d)}{d}X+r\big(\mathcal{A},d\big),

where
X
is an approximation to
|\mathcal{A}|
and
\omega(d)
is a multiplicative function satisfying

(2.1)

\displaystyle 0<\omega(p)<p,\quad p\in\mathcal{P}.

Here,
\omega(d)d^{-1}X
can be viewed as an approximation of the quantity
\big|\mathcal{A}_{d}\big|
and
r\big(\mathcal{A},d\big)
is regarded as the oscillation between
\big|\mathcal{A}_{d}\big|
and
\omega(d)d^{-1}X
. We also let

V(z)=:\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|P(z)\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{\omega(p)}{p}\right).

The first lemma is a result of Iwaniec
[
16
,
17
]
on linear sieve with well factorable error terms. An arithmetic function
\lambda(q)
is called well factorable of level
Q
if for any
Q=Q_{1}Q_{2}
,
Q_{1},Q_{2}\geq 1
, there exist two functions
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
supported in
[1,Q_{1}]
and
[1,Q_{2}]
respectively such that

|\lambda_{1}|\leq 1,\quad|\lambda_{2}|\leq 1\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda=\lambda_{1}\ast\lambda_{2}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that there is a constant
K\geq 2
such that

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\leq p<v\\
p\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{\omega(p)}{p}\right)^{-1}\leq\frac{\log v}{\log u}\left(1+\frac{K}{\log u}\right)

for any
v>u\geq 2
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
and
D^{1/2}\geq z\geq 2
we have

S(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{P},z)\leq XV(z)\left(F\left(\frac{\log D}{\log z}\right)+E\right)+\sum_{h<\exp(8/\varepsilon^{2})}\sum_{d|P(z)}\lambda^{+}_{h}(d)r\big(\mathcal{A},d\big),

where
sF(s)=2e^{\gamma}

(0<s\leq 3)
,
\gamma
is the Euler constant, and the first error term
E
satisfies

E=O\big(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{-8}e^{K}(\log D)^{-1/3}\big).

The coefficients
\lambda^{+}_{h}(d)
satisfy
\big|\lambda_{h}^{+}(d)\big|\leq 1
and vanish for
d>D
or
\mu(d)=0
. Especially,
\lambda^{+}_{h}(d)
are well factorable of level
D
.

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of natural numbers. For
q\in\mathbb{N}
and
(a,q)=1
, define

\pi(y;\ell,a,q)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell p\leq y\\
\ell p\equiv a\!\!\!\!\!\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}1,

where the symbols
p
will always be primes. As usual, let
\varphi(n)
be the Euler totient function and
{\rm li}(y)=\int_{2}^{y}\frac{1}{\log t}dt
be the Gauss function. The second lemma is the following theorem of Bombieri-Friedlander-Iwaniec type given by Wang
[
26
, Proposition 3.2]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
a\neq 0
be a given integer, and let
A>0
and
\varepsilon>0
. For any well factorable function
\lambda(q)
of level
Q
, the following estimate

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq Q\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}\lambda(q)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L_{1}\leq\ell\leq L_{2}\\
(\ell,q)=1,~2|\ell\end{subarray}}\bigg(\pi(x;\ell,a,q)-\frac{{\rm li}\big(x/\ell\big)}{\varphi(q)}\bigg)\ll\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}}

holds for

and

, where the implied constants depend only on

and

.

Remark 3

In fact, the original statement of Wang’s proposition

Lemma 2.3

For

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Now, we turn to the proof of our theorem.
Throughout, the symbols
p
and

will always be primes and
x
is supposed to be sufficiently large.

First, by Lemma
2.3
, it suffices to show that for any
e^{-\frac{2}{7}}<c<1
,

\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}T^{\prime}_{c}(x)/\pi(x)\leq-\frac{7}{2}\log c.

Clearly, for
c>e^{-\frac{2}{7}}>0.75
we have

\displaystyle T^{\prime}_{c}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\prime}\leq x\\
P^{+}(p^{\prime}-1)\geq x^{c}\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{x^{c}\leq p<x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\prime}\leq x\\
p|p^{\prime}-1\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{x^{c}\leq p<x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell p+1\leq x\\
\ell p+1\in\mathcal{P}\\

2|\ell\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell p\leq x\\
\ell p+1\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}1.

We are leading to sieve out primes in the following sequence

\mathcal{A}:=\Big\{\ell p+1:\ell\leq x^{1-c},\ell p\leq x,2|\ell\Big\},

where
c
is a fixed number satisfying
0.75<c<1
.
Let
\mathcal{P}=\mathbb{P}\setminus\{2\}
and define the sieve function
S(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{P},z)
to be

S(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{P},z):=\big\{a\in\mathcal{A}:\big(a,P(z)\big)=1\big\},

where

and

is a parameter to be decided later. Then, we deduce from above notation that

(3.1)

\displaystyle T^{\prime}_{c}(x)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathcal{A}\\
a\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}1\leq S(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{P},z)+\pi(z)=S(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{P},z)+O\big(x^{1/2}\big).

For applications of Lemma
2.1
, we now need to specify
\big|\mathcal{A}_{d}\big|
and

. Let

be an arbitrary small number and

. For any

, we have

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{A}_{d}\big|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell p\leq x\\

Next, we naturally approximate

by

and we write

(3.2)

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{A}_{d}\big|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq x^{1-c}\\
2|\ell,\,(\ell,d)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{{\rm li}(x/\ell)}{\varphi(d)}+r_{1}(\mathcal{A},d),

where
r_{1}(\mathcal{A},d)
is the error term:

(3.3)

\displaystyle r_{1}(\mathcal{A},d)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq x^{1-c}\\
2|\ell,\,(\ell,d)=1\end{subarray}}\bigg(\pi(x;\ell,-1,d)-\frac{{\rm li}(x/\ell)}{\varphi(d)}\bigg).

Now we turn to estimate the sum over
\ell
in (
3.2
), where the main term comes from.

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq x^{1-c}\\
2|\ell,\,(\ell,d)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{{\rm li}(x/\ell)}{\varphi(d)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq x^{1-c}\\
2|\ell,\,(\ell,d)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\varphi(d)}\int_{2}^{x/\ell}\frac{{\rm{d}}t}{\log t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq x^{1-c}\\
2|\ell,\,(\ell,d)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{x/\ell}{\varphi(d)\log(x/\ell)}\Big\{1+O\Big(\frac{1}{\log x}\Big)\Big\}

\displaystyle=\Big\{1+O\Big(\frac{1}{\log x}\Big)\Big\}\frac{x}{\varphi(d)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq x^{1-c}/2\\
(2\ell,\,d)=1\end{subarray}}\ \frac{1}{2\ell\log(x/\ell)}.

The condition
(2,\,d)=1
is in fact redundant since
d|P(z)=\prod_{2<p\leq z}p
. To relax the condition
(\ell,\,d)=1
, we employ the Möbius inversion getting

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq x^{1-c}\\
2|\ell,\,(\ell,d)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{{\rm li}(x/\ell)}{\varphi(d)}

\displaystyle=\Big\{1+O\Big(\frac{1}{\log x}\Big)\Big\}\frac{x}{2\varphi(d)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq x^{1-c}/2\end{subarray}}\ \frac{1}{\ell\log(x/\ell)}\sum_{e\mid(\ell,\,d)}\mu(e)

\displaystyle=\Big\{1+O\Big(\frac{1}{\log x}\Big)\Big\}\frac{x}{2\varphi(d)}\sum_{e\mid d}\,\frac{\mu(e)}{e}\sum_{e\ell<x^{1-c}/2}\frac{1}{\ell\log(x/e\ell)}

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\Big\{1+O\Big(\frac{1}{\log x}\Big)\Big\}\frac{x}{2\varphi(d)}\big(S_{1}+S_{2}\big),

where

S_{1}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\mid d\\
e<(\log x)^{9}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(e)}{e}\sum_{\ell<x^{1-c}/2e}\frac{1}{\ell\log(x/e\ell)},\qquad S_{2}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\mid d\\
e\geq(\log x)^{9}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(e)}{e}\sum_{\ell<x^{1-c}/2e}\frac{1}{\ell\log(x/e\ell)}.

First, we estimate
S_{2}
trivially

(3.5)

\displaystyle S_{2}\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\mid d\\
e\geq(\log x)^{9}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{e}\ll\frac{\tau(d)}{(\log x)^{9}},

where
\tau(d)
denotes the number of divisors of
d
.

And for
S_{1}
, we have by the partial summation

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\mid d\\
e<(\log x)^{9}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(e)}{e}\bigg\{\int^{x^{1-c}/2e}_{1}\frac{{\rm{d}}t}{t\log(x/et)}+O\Big(\frac{1}{\log x}\Big)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\mid d\\
e<(\log x)^{9}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(e)}{e}\bigg\{\log\bigg(\frac{\log(x/e)}{c\log x}\bigg)+O\Big(\frac{1}{\log x}\Big)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\mid d\\
e<(\log x)^{9}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(e)}{e}\bigg\{\log\frac{1}{c}+O\bigg(\frac{\log\log x}{\log x}\bigg)\bigg\},

where we have removed
\log e
with an admissible error term in the last step thanks to the condition
e<(\log x)^{9}
. Now we reinsert the sum over
e
with
e\geq(\log x)^{9}
up to an error term as in (
3.5
) getting

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{e\mid d}\,\frac{\mu(e)}{e}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\mid d\\
e\geq(\log x)^{9}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(e)}{e}\bigg)\bigg\{\log\frac{1}{c}+O\bigg(\frac{\log\log x}{\log x}\bigg)\bigg\}

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi(d)}{d}\bigg\{\log\frac{1}{c}+O\bigg(\frac{\log\log x}{\log x}\bigg)\bigg\}+O\bigg(\frac{\tau(d)}{(\log x)^{9}}\bigg).

Combining (
3
), (
3.5
) and (
3
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq x^{1-c}\\
2|\ell,\,(\ell,d)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{{\rm li}(x/\ell)}{\varphi(d)}=\frac{-\log c}{2}x\cdot\frac{1}{d}\Big\{1+O\Big(\frac{\log\log x}{\log x}\Big)\Big\}+O\bigg(\frac{x\tau(d)}{\varphi(d)(\log x)^{9}}\bigg),

whence by (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) we arrive at

|\mathcal{A}_{d}|=\frac{\omega(d)}{d}X+r(\mathcal{A},d)

where

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\frac{-\log c}{2}x\Big\{1+O\Big(\frac{\log\log x}{\log x}\Big)\Big\},\quad\omega(d)=1,

and

\displaystyle r(\mathcal{A},d)

\displaystyle=r_{1}(\mathcal{A},d)+O\bigg(\frac{x\tau(d)}{\varphi(d)(\log x)^{9}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq x^{1-c}\\
2|\ell,\,(\ell,d)=1\end{subarray}}\bigg(\pi(x;\ell,-1,d)-\frac{{\rm li}(x/\ell)}{\varphi(d)}\bigg)+O\bigg(\frac{x\tau(d)}{\varphi(d)(\log x)^{9}}\bigg).

Now we are ready to apply Lemma
2.1
. First we need to verify that the condition

(3.7)

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\leq p<v\\
p\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{\omega(p)}{p}\right)^{-1}\leq\frac{\log v}{\log u}\left(1+\frac{K}{\log u}\right)

holds for some absolute constant
K
. In fact, by Mertens’ formula it is easy to see

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\leq p<v\\
p\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{\omega(p)}{p}\right)^{-1}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\leq p<v\\
p>2\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}=\frac{\log v}{\log u}\bigg(1+O\Big(\frac{1}{\log u}\Big)\bigg).

Hence, with the choices of
X,\omega(d),r(\mathcal{A},d)
as above, we deduce from Lemma
2.1
by taking
D=x^{4/7-\varepsilon}
and
z=D^{\frac{1}{2}}=x^{\frac{2}{7}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}
that

\displaystyle S(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{P},z)

\displaystyle\leq XV(z)\bigg(F\Big(\frac{\log D}{\log z}\Big)+E\bigg)+\sum_{h<\exp(8/\varepsilon^{2})}\sum_{d|P(z)}\lambda^{+}_{h}(d)r\big(\mathcal{A},d\big),

\displaystyle=\frac{-\log c}{2}x\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}2<p\leq z\end{subarray}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{p}\Big)\Big(F(2)+o(1)\Big)+\sum_{h<\exp(8/\varepsilon^{2})}\sum_{d|P(z)}\lambda^{+}_{h}(d)r_{1}\big(\mathcal{A},d\big)

(3.9)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{h<\exp(8/\varepsilon^{2})}\,\sum_{d|P(z),\,d\leq D}O\bigg(\frac{x\tau(d)}{\varphi(d)(\log x)^{9}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=:S_{M}+S_{E1}+S_{E2},

say. For the main term
S_{M}
, employing again Mertens’ formula

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}2<p\leq z\end{subarray}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{p}\Big)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}2<p\leq x^{\frac{2}{7}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\end{subarray}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{p}\Big)=\frac{2e^{-\gamma}+o(1)}{\log(x^{2/7-\varepsilon/2})}=\Big(7e^{-\gamma}+o(1)\Big)\frac{1}{\log x},

and noting that
F(2)=e^{\gamma}
, we obtain

(3.10)

\displaystyle S_{M}

\displaystyle=\Big(-\frac{7}{2}\log c+o(1)\Big)\frac{x}{\log x}.

We are now in a position to apply Lemma
2.2
to estimate the first error term
S_{E1}
, provided the function
\lambda^{+}_{h}(d)
is well factorable of level
D=x^{4/7-\varepsilon}
.
By taking
L_{1}=1
and
L_{2}=x^{1-c}
in Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle S_{E1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{h<\exp(8/\varepsilon^{2})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq x^{4/7-\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}\lambda^{+}_{h}(d)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq x^{1-c}\\
2|\ell,(\ell,d)=1\end{subarray}}r_{1}\big(\mathcal{A},d\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{h<\exp(8/\varepsilon^{2})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq x^{4/7-\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}\lambda^{+}_{h}(d)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq x^{1-c}\\
2|\ell,(\ell,d)=1\end{subarray}}\bigg(\pi(x;\ell,-1,d)-\frac{{\rm li}\big(x/\ell\big)}{\varphi(d)}\bigg)

(3.11)

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}}

for any
A>0
, which is admissible.

For the error term
S_{E2}
, it is easy to see

(3.12)

\displaystyle S_{E2}\ll\frac{x}{(\log x)^{9}}\sum_{d\leq x^{4/7-\varepsilon}}\frac{\tau(d)}{\varphi(d)}\ll\frac{x}{(\log x)^{6}},

which is also admissible.

Then, inserting (
3.10
), (
3
) and (
3.12
) into (
), we arrive at

\displaystyle S(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{P},z)\leq\Big(-\frac{7}{2}\log c+o(1)\Big)\frac{x}{\log x}.

Finally,
T^{\prime}_{c}(x)
is estimated from (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle T^{\prime}_{c}(x)\leq S(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{P},z)+O(x^{1/2})\leq\Big(-\frac{7}{2}\log c+o(1)\Big)\frac{x}{\log x},

whence

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}T^{\prime}_{c}(x)/\pi(x)\leq-\frac{7}{2}\log c

for any
e^{-\frac{2}{7}}<c<1
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Evaluation of Oral Health-Related Quality of Life in Mandibulectomy Patients Following Prosthetic Rehabilitation: An Observational Study on Sociodemographic, Clinical, and Treatment Correlates
Authors: Komal Sharma, Jogeswar Barman, Rajdeep Paul, Barasha Goswami, Joshni Loitongbam, Bhawna
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background and Objectives:Mandibulectomy, a common consequence of treatment for head and neck malignancies, can significantly impair oral functions and psychological well-being. Oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL) is a multidimensional construct evaluating the impact of oral conditions on daily functioning and satisfaction.Materials and Methods:This institutional observational study was conducted at the Department of Prosthodontics, Regional Dental College, Guwahati, between June 2023 and April 2025. A total of 40 patients with acquired mandibular defects who met the inclusion criteria were rehabilitated with mandibular guidance prostheses. OHRQoL was assessed using the OHIP-14 questionnaire at baseline, two weeks, and three months post-rehabilitation. Socio-demographic, clinical, and treatment data were recorded and analyzed using IBM SPSS version 20. Statistical significance was evaluated using paired t-tests, ANOVA, and Chi-square tests at a significance level of P < 0.05.Results:The mean OHIP-14 scores showed significant improvement from baseline (35.8 ± 6.6) to two weeks (30.3 ± 6.7) and three months (23.4 ± 6.2) post-rehabilitation (P < 0.001). The most frequently used prosthesis was the mandibular guidance flange prosthesis (50%), followed by the maxillary ramp prosthesis (45%). Age, type of defect, number of remaining teeth, and chemotherapy status significantly influenced OHRQoL outcomes.Conclusion:Prosthetic rehabilitation significantly improves the OHRQoL in mandibulectomy patients, with the extent of improvement influenced by multiple clinical and demographic factors. Early intervention, appropriate prosthetic selection, and individualized treatment planning can enhance post-rehabilitation outcomes.

INTRODUCTION

Oral health is a fundamental aspect of an individual’s overall health and well-being, influencing both physical functionality and psychological comfort. It is a critical component of overall well-being and any impairment in oral function can significantly affect an individual’s quality of life. Patient satisfaction is an end point that needs further evaluation and should be included in the outcome assessment. The WHO defines “Quality of Life (QoL)” as individuals’ perceptions of their position in the context of the culture and value systems in which they live and in relation to their goals, expectations, standards, and concerns.[1]

OHRQoL is a multidimensional construct that includes a subjective evaluation of the individual’s oral health, functional well-being, emotional well-being, expectations and satisfaction with care, and sense of self. It is an integral part of general health and well-being.[2] An individual’s health-related QoL will be affected if the oral health is worsened. Oral health assessments by incorporating OHRQoL provide a more comprehensive understanding of oral health, helping in clinical practice, research, and public health policies.[3] Worldwide, an estimated 562,328 people were diagnosed with head and neck cancer in 2020[45] in India. Overall, 57.5% of global head and neck cancers (excluding esophageal cancers) occur in Asia, especially in India, for both sexes. Head and neck cancers in India accounted for 30% of all cancers in males (except Dibrugarh in Assam, 49.6%).[4]

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This institutional-based observational study was conducted in the Department of Prosthodontics, Crown and Bridge, Regional Dental College, Guwahati, Assam, affiliated to Srimanta Sankaradeva University of Health Sciences, from June 2023 to April 2025. A total of 40 patients with acquired mandibular defects due to surgical resection, suitable for rehabilitation with a mandibular guidance prosthesis, were selected based on specific inclusion and exclusion criteria. All participants were informed about the study protocol, and written consent was obtained.

Patients with marginal mandibulectomy retaining the mandibular border, extensive defects involving additional maxillofacial resections, active or persistent lesions, complete edentulism, inability to attend follow-ups, or physical/mental disabilities interfering with the study were excluded.

Oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL) was assessed using the OHIP-14 questionnaire, a validated tool derived from the original OHIP-49. Prosthesis performance was evaluated using the maxillofacial prosthesis performance scale (MFPPS), covering 10 parameters such as function, retention, phonetics, aesthetics, and satisfaction. OHIP-14 data were recorded before treatment, while MFPPS scores were obtained at two weeks and three months after prosthesis insertion.

All the statistics have been calculated and computed using IBM Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) version 20.

DISCUSSION

The present study evaluated the oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL) of patients with mandibular defects rehabilitated using mandibular guidance prostheses at different intervals and examined the influence of sociodemographic, clinical, and treatment variables, along with maxillofacial prosthesis performance, on OHRQoL. The majority of participants were aged 41–55 years (35%), followed by 55–70 years (32.5%), while those over 70 years had the lowest prevalence, consistent with de Anagnostopoulos F[6] Males comprised 72.5% of cases, in line with findings by Shetty P et al.,[7] though Kalaignan P et al.[8] reported equal gender distribution.

Post-prosthetic rehabilitation, a statistically significant improvement (P < 0.001) in OHRQoL was observed at various time intervals, in agreement with Kalaignan P et al.,[8] who reported enhanced QoL in surgically treated tumour patients after prosthetic rehabilitation. Age-based analysis revealed benefits across all groups, though younger patients exhibited more pronounced improvements, supporting. Females reported lower OHIP scores, indicating greater improvement, possibly due to higher psychosocial and aesthetic concerns. Lower socioeconomic status was associated with reduced OHRQoL, highlighting the impact of financial and social factors on treatment outcomes.[691011]

The study’s strengths include its multifactorial assessment of variables affecting OHRQoL and consistent evaluation by a single examiner at multiple time points. However, limitations include the relatively small sample size, short follow-up period, and unequal group distribution. Additionally, important clinical factors, such as defect size, tumour stage, and treatment history, were not considered, which could have influenced OHRQoL outcomes, as noted in earlier literature.[8]

CONCLUSION

Oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL) of mandibulectomy patients before prosthetic rehabilitation is not significantly affected by sociodemographic factors. However, treatment-related factor, such as neck dissection, can adversely effect on their OHRQL. Functioning of maxillofacial prosthesis shows significant improvements in overall OHRQoL of mandibulectomy patients.

1.
Introduction

Heisenberg’s original canonical commutation relations (see
[
7
]
) for a system with
d
degrees of freedom
involve position operators
q_{1},\dots,q_{d}
and momentum operators

p_{1},\dots,p_{d}
, which are
unbounded operators on a complex Hilbert space
satisfying the relations

p_{k}q_{l}-q_{l}p_{k}=\dfrac{h}{2\pi i}\delta_{kl}I,

where
h
is Planck’s constant.
Considering the unitary operators

U(t)=\exp(\sum_{k=1}^{d}\dfrac{2\pi i}{h}t_{k}p_{k})\quad\text{and}\quad V(s)=\exp(\sum_{l=1}^{d}\dfrac{2\pi i}{h}s_{l}q_{l})

on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}

for
t=(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})
and
s=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})
in
\mathbf{R}^{d},

H. Weyl (see
[
15
, p. 26]
) gave the canonical commutation relations the following form:

U(t)V(s)=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{h}t\cdot s}V(s)U(t)

(CCR)

where
t\cdot s=\sum_{k=1}^{d}t_{k}s_{k}.

A solution of
(CCR)
is given by the so-called Schrödinger representation
and the classical Stone-von Neumann Theorem asserts that this solution is essentially unique
(see below for precise statements).
We are going to give a quick proof of this theorem and to extend it to more general rings.

Given a topological group
G
and a complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H},
recall that a unitary representation
\pi

of
G
on
\mathcal{H}
is a continuous homomorphism
\pi:G\to U(\mathcal{H})
, where
U(\mathcal{H})
is the group of unitary operators
of
\mathcal{H}
equipped with the weak (or strong) operator topology.
For a finite or infinite countable cardinal
n\in\{1,2,\ldots,\infty\}
, we denote by

\mathcal{H}^{(n)}=\oplus_{i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}}\mathcal{H}

the Hilbert space direct sum of
n
many copies of
\mathcal{H}

and by
\pi^{(n)}
the corresponding
inflation
of
\pi;

so,
\pi^{(n)}:G\to U(\mathcal{H}^{(n)})
is the unitary representation of
G
on
\mathcal{H}^{(n)}
defined by

\pi^{(n)}(g)=\oplus_{i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}}\pi(g)\qquad\text{for all}\quad g\in G.

Let
R
be a locally compact ring with unit; notice that we do not assume that
R
is commutative.
Fix a unitary character
\lambda
of the additive group
(R,+)
, that is, a one dimensional unitary representation of
(R,+)
.
We say that a pair
(U,V)
of unitary representations
U
and
V

of
R^{d}
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
satisfies the canonical commutation relations (relative to
\lambda
) if

U(a)V(b)=\lambda(a\cdot b)V(b)U(a)

\rm{(CCR)}_{\lambda}

for all
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{d})
and
b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{d})
in
R^{d},

where
a\cdot b=\sum_{k=1}^{d}a_{k}b_{k}.

It is quickly checked that a solution of

(
CCR
)

λ

, called
Schrödinger pair

(or
Schrödinger representation
), is given by the pair
(U_{\text{Schr}},V_{\text{Schr}})
defined on
L^{2}(R^{d},\mu)
by

(U_{\text{Schr}}(a)f)(x)=f(x+a)\quad\text{and}\lx@nobreakspace\quad(V_{\text{Schr}}(b)\xi)(x)=\lambda(x\cdot b)f(x)\quad\text{for}\lx@nobreakspace\quad f\in L^{2}(R^{d},\mu),

where
\mu
is a Haar measure on
(R^{d},+).

There is an obvious equivalence relation on pairs of unitary representations
of
R^{d}
: two such pairs
(U_{1},V_{1})
and
(U_{2},V_{2})
acting on respective Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{1}

and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
are
equivalent
if there exists a unitary isomorphism
\Phi:\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}

such that

\Phi U_{1}(a)\Phi^{*}=U_{2}(a)\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi V_{1}(b)\Phi^{*}=V_{2}(b)

for all
a,b\in R^{d}.

Let
\widehat{R}
be the dual group of
R,
that is, the group of all unitary characters of
(R,+).

Given
\lambda
in
\widehat{R},

properties of the continuous homomorphism

\nabla_{\lambda}:R\mapsto\widehat{R},\ a\mapsto(x\mapsto\lambda(x\cdot a))

will play an important role in the sequel.

Theorem A
.

(
Stone-von Neumann Theorem
)
Let
R
be a unital second-countable locally compact ring and let
\lambda\in\widehat{R}.

We assume that

\displaystyle\bullet\lambda(ab)=\lambda(ba)\quad\text{for all}\quad a,b\in R

(Sym)

\displaystyle\bullet\nabla_{\lambda}:R\mapsto\widehat{R}\quad\text{is an isomorphism.}

(Isom)

Let
(U,V)
be a pair of unitary representations of
R^{d}
on a separable Hilbert space satisfying

(
CCR
)

λ

.
Then the inflation
(U^{(\infty)},V^{(\infty)})
of
(U,V)
is equivalent to the inflation
(U_{\text{Schr}}^{(\infty)},V_{\text{Schr}}^{(\infty)})
of the Schrödinger pair.

The following more familiar form of the Stone-von Neumann Theorem is an immediate
consequence of Theorem
A
.

Corollary B
.

(
Stone-von Neumann Theorem-bis
)
Let
R
and
\lambda
be as in Theorem
A
.
Let
(U,V)
be a pair of unitary representations
of
R^{d}
on a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
satisfying

(
CCR
)

λ

.
Then there exist a subset
J
of
{\mathbf{N}}
,
an orthogonal decomposition
\mathcal{H}=\oplus_{j\in J}{\mathcal{H}_{j}}
of
\mathcal{H}
into closed

U(R^{d})
- and
V(R^{d})
-invariant subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{j}
as well as
unitary isomorphisms
\Phi_{j}:L^{2}(R^{d})\to\mathcal{H}_{j}

such that

U(t)|_{\mathcal{H}_{j}}=\Phi_{j}U_{\text{Schr}}(a)\Phi_{j}^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad V(s)|_{\mathcal{H}_{j}}=\Phi_{j}V_{\text{Schr}}(b)\Phi_{j}^{*},

for all
a,b\in R^{d}
and all
j\in J.

Here are examples of rings
R
for which there exists
\lambda\in\widehat{R}

such that Conditions
(Sym)
and
(Isom)
above hold (see Section
3
below;
for some of these rings, even every non trivial
\lambda
satisfies these two conditions):

(1)

R=\mathbf{k}
is a local field
\mathbf{k},
that is, a locally compact non discrete field;
examples include
\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{R}
and
\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{Q}_{p}
(the fields of
p
-adic
numbers).

(2)

R=\mathbf{A}
is the ring of adèles of
\mathbf{Q}.

(3)

R=M_{n}(\mathbf{k})
is the ring of
n\times n
-matrices over a local field
\mathbf{k}.

(4)

R={\mathbf{Z}}/n{\mathbf{Z}}
is the ring of integers modulo
n\geq 1.

A few words about the proofs of the Stone-von Neumann Theorem are in order.
The original proof in the case of
R={\mathbf{R}}
is due to von Neumann (
[
12
]
); his methods were
later extended to cover other local fields by Segal
[
11
]
and Weil
[
13
]
.
In all these approaches, the pair
(U,V)
acting on
\mathcal{H}
is “integrated ”
to a representation of a certain algebra of functions on
R^{d}\times R^{d}

by an algebra of operators on
\mathcal{H}
and
one shows that this latter algebra contains sufficiently many rank-one operators.
Mackey stated and proved a far-reaching generalization of this theorem (
[
9
]
);
his proof uses a different approach which amounts to the classification of so-called transitive systems of imprimitivity based on
R^{d}
(in fact, on an arbitrary second countable locally compact group).
In comparison, our proof is more elementary; the basic idea is an imitation of a trick, called Fell’s absorption principle (see Proposition
2
below), and the only sophisticated tool we use is Plancherel’s theorem about the Fourier transform of the abelian group
R^{d}.

Our approach confirms somehow the observation made by Howe
[
8
, p.827]

that the Stone-von Neumann and the Plancherel Theorems are equivalent.

Rings for which there exists no
\lambda\in\widehat{R}

such that Condition
(Isom)
holds include the ring of integers
{\mathbf{Z}}

and, more generally, every countable infinite ring with the discrete topology.
Theorem
A
fails for such rings.
Nevertheless, our approach allows us to prove
for them the following weaker version of the Stone-von Neumann Theorem.

We need to define a second equivalence relation on pairs of unitary representations
of
R^{d}
; this definition is tailored after a similar notion of approximately equivalent representations of
C^{*}
-algebras (see
[
5
, Definition 41.1]
).

Definition 1
.

Two pairs
(U_{1},V_{1})
and
(U_{2},V_{2})
of unitary representations
of
R^{d}
acting on respective Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{1}

and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
are
approximately equivalent
if there exists
a sequence
(\Phi_{n})_{n}
of unitary isomorphisms
\Phi_{n}:\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}

such that, for every compact subset
K
of
R^{d},
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{a\in K}\|U_{2}(a)-\Phi_{n}U_{1}(a)\Phi^{*}_{n}\|=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{b\in K}\|V_{2}(b)-\Phi_{n}V_{1}(b)\Phi^{*}_{n}\|=0.

Theorem C
.

Let
R
be a unital second-countable locally compact ring. Let
\lambda\in\widehat{R}

satisfying Condition
(Sym)
from Theorem
A
and such that

\text{there is no non-zero two-sided ideal of}\quad R\quad\text{contained in}\quad\ker\lambda.

(Faith)

For an integer
d\geq 1,
let
(U,V)
be a pair of unitary representations
of
R^{d}
on a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
satisfying the commutation relation

(
CCR
)

λ

.
Then
(U^{(\infty)},V^{(\infty)})
is approximately equivalent
to
(U_{\text{Schr}}^{(\infty)},V_{\text{Schr}}^{(\infty)})
.

Condition
(Faith)
is a weak faithfulness condition on
\lambda;
provided
\lambda
is non trivial, it holds for instance when
R
is a simple ring.

As is nowadays well-known, an appropriate way to formulate the commutation relations
is by means of unitary representations of a Heisenberg group associated to
R

and
d
(see
[
6
, Chap.1, §5]
in the case
R=\mathbf{R}
).
We will recall the definition of this group and rephrase our results in terms of its
representations in Section
4
.

A
and Theorem
C

Let
\lambda
be an arbitrary unitary character of
R.

We will use in a crucial way the following pair
(U_{\rm reg},V_{\rm reg})
of unitary representations of
R^{d}

satisfying

(
CCR
)

λ

, which we call the

Set

where
\mu
is a fixed Haar measure on

Let
U_{\rm reg}
and

be defined on

by

(U_{\rm{reg}}(a)\xi)(x,y)=\xi(x+a,y)\quad\text{and}\lx@nobreakspace\quad(V_{\rm{reg}}(b)\xi)(x)=\lambda(x\cdot b)\xi(x,y+b)

for all
\xi\in L^{2}(S)
and
a,b,x,y\in R^{d}.

One checks immediately that
(U_{\rm reg},V_{\rm reg})
satisfies the relation

(
CCR
)

λ

.

Fix a pair
(U,V)
of unitary representations
of
R^{d}
on a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
satisfying the commutation relation

(
CCR
)

λ

Let
m,n\in\{1,2,\ldots,\infty\}

be the dimensions of the Hilbert spaces
L^{2}(S)
and
\mathcal{H}
respectively.
The Hilbert space
L^{2}(S,\mathcal{H})=L^{2}(S,\mathcal{H},\mu\otimes\mu)

of (equivalence classes of) square-integrable measurable maps

is isomorphic to
\mathcal{H}^{(m)}
as well as to
L^{2}(S)^{(n)}
;
the inflations
(U^{(m)},V^{(m)})
of
(U,V)
and
(U_{\rm reg}^{(n)},V_{\rm reg}^{(n)})

of
(U_{\rm reg},V_{\rm reg})
are realized on

by the formulae

(U^{(m)}(a)F)(x,y)=U(a)(F(x,y)\quad\text{and}\lx@nobreakspace\quad(V^{(m)}(b)F)(x)=V(b)F(x,y)

and

(U_{\rm{reg}}^{(n)}(a)F)(x,y)=F(x+a,y)\quad\text{and}\lx@nobreakspace\quad(V_{\rm{reg}}^{(n)}(b)F)(x)=\lambda(x\cdot b)F(x,y+b)

for all
F\in L^{2}(S,\mathcal{H})
and
a,b,x,y\in R^{d}.

We associate to
(U,V)
another pair
(\widetilde{U},\widetilde{V})
of unitary representations of
R^{d}
on
L^{2}(S,\mathcal{H})

defined by

(\widetilde{U}(a)F)(x,y)=U(a)(F(x+a,y))\quad\text{and}\lx@nobreakspace\quad(\widetilde{V}(b)F)(x,y)=V(b)(F(x,y+b))

for all
F\in L^{2}(S,\mathcal{H})
and
a,b,x,y\in R^{d}.
Again, one checks immediately that
(\widetilde{U},\widetilde{V})
satisfies the relation

(
CCR
)

λ

.

So, we have three pairs of of unitary representations of
R^{d}
acting on
L^{2}(S,\mathcal{H})

and satisfying

(
CCR
)

λ

:
(U^{(m)},V^{(m)})
,
(\widetilde{U},\widetilde{V}),

and
(U_{\rm reg}^{(n)},V_{\rm reg}^{(n)})
; observe that, for fixed
n
,
the last one is independent of
(U,V).

The following proposition is the crucial tool we will use
in the proofs of both Theorem
A
and Theorem
C
;
Item (i) is reminiscent of a property of the regular representation of a group (see
[

often called Fell’s absorption principle (for the precise relationship with this principle, see Section

Proposition 2
.

The pair
(\widetilde{U},\widetilde{V})
is equivalent to the pair
(U_{\rm reg}^{(n)},V_{\rm reg}^{(n)})
.

The pair
(U^{(m)},V^{(m)})

is equivalent to the pair
(\overline{U^{m}},\overline{V^{m}})
given on
L^{2}(S,\mathcal{H})
by

(\overline{U^{m}}(a)F)(x,y)=\lambda(a\cdot x)U(a)(F(x,y))\quad\text{and}\lx@nobreakspace\quad(\overline{V^{m}}(b)F)(x,y)=\lambda(y\cdot b)V(b)(F(x,y))

for all
F\in L^{2}(S,\mathcal{H})
and
a,b,x,y\in R^{d}.

Proof.

(i) Let
\Phi:L^{2}(S,\mathcal{H})\to L^{2}(S,\mathcal{H})
be the unitary isomorphism defined by

\Phi(F)(x,y)=U(-x)V(-y)(F(x,y))\qquad\text{for all}\quad F\in L^{2}(S,\mathcal{H}),(x,y)\in S.

For
a,b\in R^{d},
we have

\displaystyle\left((\widetilde{U}(a)\Phi)(F)\right)(x,y)

\displaystyle=U(a)(\Phi(F)(x+a,y))

\displaystyle=U(a)U(-x-a)V(-y)(F(x+a,y))

\displaystyle=U(-x)V(-y)(F(x+a,y))

\displaystyle=\Phi(U_{\rm{reg}}^{(n)}(a)F)(x,y))

and, similarly,

\displaystyle\left((\widetilde{V}(b)\Phi)(F)\right)(x,y)

\displaystyle=\Phi(V_{\rm{reg}}^{(n)}(b)F)(x,y)).

(ii) Let
\Psi:L^{2}(S,\mathcal{H})\to L^{2}(S,\mathcal{H})
be the unitary isomorphism defined by

\Psi(F)(x,y)=V(-x)U(y)(F(x,y))\qquad\text{for all}\quad F\in L^{2}(S,\mathcal{H}),(x,y)\in S.

For
a,b\in R^{d},
we have

\displaystyle\Psi(U^{(m)}(a)F)(x,y)

\displaystyle=V(-x)U(y)U(a)(F(x,y))

\displaystyle=V(-x)U(a)U(y)(F(x,y))

\displaystyle=\lambda(a\cdot x)U(a)(V(-x)U(y)(F(x,y))

\displaystyle=\left(\overline{U^{m}}(a)(\Psi(F))\right)(x,y)

and, similarly,

\displaystyle\Psi(V^{(m)}(b)F)(x,y)

\displaystyle=\left(\overline{V^{m}}(b)(\Psi(F))\right)(x,y).

∎

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
A

We assume that
\lambda
satisfies Conditions
(Sym)
and
(Isom)
.
Since
\widehat{S}
is, in the canonical way, topologically
isomorphic to
\widehat{R}^{2d},
the map

\nabla_{\lambda}^{(2d)}:S\mapsto\widehat{S},\,(a,b)\mapsto((x,y)\mapsto\lambda(x\cdot a)\lambda(y\cdot b))

is an isomorphism of topological groups.
We can therefore identify the (inverse) Fourier transform of
S
with the map
\mathcal{F}:L^{1}(S)\to C(S)

given by

\mathcal{F}(f)(x,y)=\int_{S}f(t,s)(\nabla_{\lambda}^{(2d)}(x,y))(t,s)d\mu(t)d\mu(s)=\int_{S}f(t,s)\lambda(t\cdot x)\lambda(s\cdot y)d\mu(t)d\mu(s)

for
f\in L^{1}(S).
Upon properly normalizing
\mathcal{F},
we can assume by Plancherel’s theorem (see
[
4
, Chap.II, §1]
)) that

\mathcal{F}
extends from
L^{1}(S)\cap L^{2}(S)
to a bijective isometry

\mathcal{F}:L^{2}(S)\to L^{2}(S).

We claim that
\mathcal{F}
induces an equivalence between the pairs
(\overline{U^{m}},\overline{V^{m}})

and
(\widetilde{U},\widetilde{V})
from Proposition
2
.
Indeed, let
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}:L^{2}(S,\mathcal{H})\to L^{2}(S,\mathcal{H})
be given by

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(F)(x,y)=\int_{S}\lambda(t\cdot x)\lambda(s\cdot y)F(t,s)d\mu(t)d\mu(s)

for
F\in L^{2}(S,\mathcal{H});
more precisely,
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
is defined on the linear span
X
of

\{f\otimes v\,:\,\lx@nobreakspace f\in L^{2}(S),v\in\mathcal{H}\}
, where
(f\otimes v)(x,y)=f(x,y)v
,
by

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(f\otimes v)=\mathcal{F}(f)v;

since
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
is isometric on
X
and has dense image, it extends to a bijective isometry
from
L^{2}(S,\mathcal{H})
to
L^{2}(S,\mathcal{H})
.
For
a,b\in R^{d}
and
F\in L^{2}(S,\mathcal{H}),
we have, using Condition
(Sym)
,

\displaystyle\left(\widetilde{U}(a)\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(F)\right)(x,y)

\displaystyle=U(a)(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(F)(x+a,y))

\displaystyle=U(a)\left(\int_{S}\lambda(t\cdot(x+a))\lambda(s\cdot y)F(t,s)d\mu(t)d\mu(s)\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{S}\lambda(t\cdot a)\lambda(t\cdot x)\lambda(s\cdot y))U(a)(F(t,s))d\mu(t)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{S}\lambda(a\cdot t)\lambda(t\cdot x)\lambda(s\cdot y))U(a)(F(t,s))d\mu(t)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{S}\lambda(t\cdot x)\lambda(s\cdot y)(\overline{U^{m}}(a)F)(t,s)d\mu(t)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(\overline{U^{m}}(a)F)(x,y)

and, similarly,

\displaystyle\left(\widetilde{V}(b)\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(F)\right)(x,y)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(\overline{V^{m}}(b)F)(x,y).

This concludes the proof of Theorem
A
; indeed, denoting by

the equivalence of pairs of unitary representations of
R^{d}
on separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
satisfying

(
CCR
)

λ

,
Proposition
2
shows that

(U^{(m)},V^{m})\sim(\overline{U^{m}},\overline{V^{m}})\quad\text{and}\quad(\widetilde{U},\widetilde{V})\sim(U_{\rm reg}^{(n)},V_{\rm reg}^{(n)}),

where

; moreover, we have just proved that

so
(U^{(m)},V^{(m)})\sim(U_{\rm reg}^{(n)},V_{\rm reg}^{(n)})
and this implies

(U^{(\infty)},V^{(\infty)})\sim(U_{\rm reg}^{(\infty)},V_{\rm reg}^{(\infty)})
.
As

First, observe that the pair
(U_{\text{Schr}},V_{\text{Schr}})

V(R^{d})
).
Indeed, let

be a bounded operator which commutes with all

’s and all

’s.
Since

As is well-known (see e.g.

\varphi

is constant and

Next, let
(U,V)
be a pair of unitary representations of

(

)

λ

2.3.
A lemma on partitions

For the proof of Theorem
C
, we will need the following general lemma about partitions.

Lemma 3
.

Let
G
be a second-countable locally compact group
and let
U
be a compact neighbourhood of the identity in
G

with
U^{-1}=U.

We can find a partition of
G=\coprod_{i\geq 1}\Omega_{i}

in countably many Borel subsets
\Omega_{i}^{(n)}
with the following properties:

(P1)

For every
i\geq 1
and any elements
x,y
in
\Omega_{i},
we have

y^{-1}x\in U;

(P2)

\Omega_{i}
is contained in the closure of the interior
{\rm Int}(\Omega_{i})

of
\Omega_{i},
for every
i\geq 1;

Proof.

Since
G
is second-countable,
G
contains a countable dense subset
D=\{d_{1},d_{2},\cdots\}
.
Let
V
be an open neighbourhood of the identity with
V^{-1}=V
and
V^{2}\subset U.

Let
g\in G;
since
gV
is a neighbourhood of
g
, we have

gV\cap D\neq\emptyset
, that is,
g\in DV^{-1}=DV.
Therefore, we have
G=\bigcup_{i\geq 1}d_{i}V.

Set
\Omega_{1}:=d_{1}\overline{V}
and, for define recursively
\Omega_{i}
by

\Omega_{i+1}=d_{i+1}\overline{V}\setminus\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq i}\Omega_{j}\qquad\text{for all}\quad i\geq 1.

It is obvious that
G=\bigcup_{i}\Omega_{i}
is a partition of
G;

moreover, for
x,y\in\Omega_{i},
we have

y^{-1}x\in\overline{V}^{-1}\overline{V}=\overline{V}^{2}\subset\overline{U}=U

and so Condition (P1) is satisfied.

We claim that Condition (P2) is also satisfied. Indeed,

{\rm Int}(\Omega_{1})=d_{1}V
is dense in
\Omega_{1}.

Moreover, for every
i\geq 2,

\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq i-1}\Omega_{j}=\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq i-1}d_{j}\overline{V}

is closed in
G
; therefore, the set
d_{i}V\setminus\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq i-1}\Omega_{j}

is open in
G
and dense in
\Omega_{i}.

∎

2.4.
Proof of Theorem
C

When Condition
(Isom)
from Theorem
A
is not satisfied,
the pairs
(\overline{U^{m}},\overline{V^{m}})
and
(\widetilde{U},\widetilde{V})
from Proposition
2
are no longer equivalent.
We will first realize
(\widetilde{U},\widetilde{V})
on
L^{2}(\widehat{S},\mathcal{H})

by means of the Fourier transform.
Let
\nu
be the Haar measure on
\widehat{S}
for which Plancherel’s theorem holds,
that is, for which the Fourier transform

\mathcal{F}:L^{2}(S,\mu\otimes\mu)\to L^{2}(\widehat{S},\nu),
defined by

\mathcal{F}(f)(\chi)=\int_{S}\overline{\chi(x,y)}f(x,y)d\mu(x)d\mu(y)\qquad\text{for all}\quad\chi\in\widehat{S},

is an isometry. We extend
\mathcal{F}
to an isometry

L^{2}(S,\mathcal{H})\to L^{2}(\widehat{S},\mathcal{H}),
which we again denote by
\mathcal{F}
, by

\mathcal{F}(F)(\chi)=\int_{S}\overline{\chi(x,y)}F(x,y)d\mu(x)d\mu(y)\qquad\text{for all}\quad F\in L^{2}(S,\mathcal{H}),\,\chi\in\widehat{S}.

The inverse
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
of
\mathcal{F}
is given by

\mathcal{F}(H)(x,y)=\int_{\widehat{S}}\chi(x,y)H(\chi)d\nu(\chi)\qquad\text{for all}\quad H\in L^{2}(\widehat{S},\mathcal{H}),\,(x,y)\in S.

We transfer the pair
(\widetilde{U},\widetilde{V}))

to the pair
(\mathcal{F}(\widetilde{U}),\mathcal{F}(\widetilde{V}))

on
L^{2}(\widehat{S},\mathcal{H})
given by

\mathcal{F}(\widetilde{U})(a)=\mathcal{F}\widetilde{U}(a)\mathcal{F}^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{F}(\widetilde{V})(b)=\mathcal{F}\widetilde{V}(b)\mathcal{F}^{-1}.

Using the translation invariance of
\mu
, one checks that

\begin{aligned} \mathcal{F}(\widetilde{U})(a)(H)(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})&=\chi_{1}(a)U(a)(H(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}))\\
\mathcal{F}(\widetilde{V})(b)(H)(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})&=\chi_{2}(b)V(b)(H(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}))\end{aligned}\right.

[formula]

for all
H\in L^{2}(\widehat{S},\mathcal{H}),(a,b)\in S,
and
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\in\widehat{S}.

The pair
(U^{(m)},V^{(m)})
is realized on
L^{2}(\widehat{S},\mathcal{H})\cong\mathcal{H}^{(m)}
by

\begin{aligned} U^{m}(a)(H)(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})&=U(a)(H(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}))\\
V^{m}(b)(H)(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})&=V(b)(H(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})).\end{aligned}\right.

[formula]

Recall that
\widehat{S}
, equipped with the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets
of
S
, is a locally compact group. Moreover,
\widehat{S}
,
is second-countable, since
R
is assumed to be second-countable (see e.g.
[
2
, A.7.3]
).

We claim that the image of

\nabla_{\lambda}^{(2d)}:S\mapsto\widehat{S}
is dense in
\widehat{S}.

Indeed, let
(x,y)\in S
be such that
\chi(x,y)=1
for all

\chi\in\nabla_{\lambda}^{(2d)}(S),
that is, such that

\lambda(x\cdot a)\lambda(y\cdot b)=1
and hence
\lambda(x\cdot a)=\lambda(y\cdot b)=1

for all
a,b\in R^{d}.
By Conditions
(Sym)
and
(Faith)
, it follows that

x=y=0.
Pontrjagin’s duality implies then that
the subgroup
\nabla_{\lambda}^{(2d)}(S)
is dense in
\widehat{S}.

We can now proceed with the proof of Theorem
C
.
Fix a compact subset
K
of
R^{d}
and a real number
\varepsilon>0.

We are going to construct a unitary isomorphism
\Phi:L^{2}(\widehat{S},\mathcal{H})\to L^{2}(\widehat{S},\mathcal{H})

such that

\sup_{a\in K}\|\Phi\mathcal{F}(\widetilde{U})(a)\Phi^{-1}-U^{m}(a)\|\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{b\in K}\|\Phi\mathcal{F}(\widetilde{V})(b)\Phi^{-1}-V^{m}(b)\|\leq\varepsilon.

Set
C:=K\times K
and consider the compact neighbourhood
U
of
1_{S}
(the identity element in
\widehat{S}
) defined by

U=\{\chi\in\widehat{S}:\sup_{s\in C}|\chi(s)-1|\leq\varepsilon\}.

Let
\widehat{S}=\coprod_{i\geq 1}\Omega_{i}

be a partition of
\widehat{S}
in countably many Borel subsets
\Omega_{i}

satisfying Conditions (P1) and (P2) from Lemma
3
.

Since
\nabla_{\lambda}^{(2d)}(S)
is dense in
\widehat{S}
, it follows
from Condition (P2) that
we can find, for every
i\geq 1,
an element
s_{i}\in S
with

\nabla_{\lambda}^{(2d)}(s_{i})\in\Omega_{i}.

Let
a_{i},b_{i}\in R^{d}
be such that
s_{i}=(a_{i},b_{i}).

We define an operator

\Phi:L^{2}(\widehat{S},\mathcal{H})\to L^{2}(\widehat{S},\mathcal{H})

as follows. For
F\in L^{2}(\widehat{S},\mathcal{H}),
set

\Phi(F)(\chi)=V(-a_{i})U(b_{i})(F(\chi))\qquad\text{for all}\quad i\geq 1\quad\text{and}\quad\chi\in\Omega_{i}.

We have

\displaystyle\|\Phi(F)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 1}\int_{\Omega_{i}}\|V(-a_{i})U(b_{i})(F(\chi))\|^{2}d\nu(\chi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 1}\int_{\Omega_{i}}\|F(\chi)\|^{2}d\nu(\chi)

\displaystyle=\|F\|^{2}.

Therefore,
\Phi
is an isometry. Moreover,
\Phi
is bijective
with inverse defined by

\Phi^{-1}(F)(\chi)=U(-b_{i})V(a_{i})(F(\chi))\qquad\text{for all}\quad i\geq 1\quad\text{and}\quad\chi\in\Omega_{i}.

So,
\Phi:L^{2}(\widehat{S},\mathcal{H})\to L^{2}(\widehat{S},\mathcal{H})
is a unitary isomorphism.

Let
s=(a,b)\in S
and
F\in L^{2}(\widehat{S},\mathcal{H}).

For
i\geq 1
and
\chi=(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\in\Omega_{i},
on the one hand,
we have, in view of Formula
(*)
for the pair

(\mathcal{F}(\widetilde{U}),\mathcal{F}(\widetilde{V}):

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\widetilde{U})(a)(\Phi(F))(\chi)

\displaystyle=\chi_{1}(a)U(a)(\Phi(F)(\chi))

\displaystyle=\chi_{1}(a)U(a)(\Phi(F)(\chi))

and similarly

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\widetilde{V})(b)(\Phi(F))(\chi)

\displaystyle=\chi_{2}(b)V(b)(\Phi(F)(\chi))

\displaystyle=\chi_{2}(b)V(b)(\Phi(F)(\chi));

on the other hand, we have, in view of Formula
(**)
for the pair

(U^{(m)},V^{(m)}):

\displaystyle\left(\Phi(U^{(m)}(a)F\right)(\chi)

\displaystyle=\Phi(U(a)(F(\chi))

\displaystyle=V(-a_{i})U(b_{i})U(a)(F(\chi))

\displaystyle=V(-a_{i})U(a)U(b_{i})(F(x,y))

\displaystyle=\lambda(a\cdot a_{i})U(a)(V(-a_{i})U(b_{i})(F(\chi))

\displaystyle=\lambda(a_{i}\cdot a)U(a)(\Phi(F(\chi))

and similarly

\displaystyle\left(\Phi(V^{(m)}(b)F\right)(\chi)

\displaystyle=\Phi(V(b)(F(\chi))

\displaystyle=V(-a_{i})U(b_{i})V(b)(F(\chi))

\displaystyle=\lambda(b_{i}\cdot b)V(b)\Phi(F(\chi)).

Now, by the choice of
s_{i}=(a_{i},b_{i}),
we have

\nabla_{\lambda}^{(2d)}(s_{i})\in\Omega_{i}
; hence, by Condition (P1), we have

\chi\in\nabla_{\lambda}^{(2)}(s_{i})U,
that is,

\sup_{(a,b)\in C}\left|\chi(a,b)-\nabla_{\lambda}^{(2)}(s_{i})(a,b)\right|\leq\varepsilon

and consequently, using Condition
(Sym)
,

\sup_{a\in K}\left|\chi_{1}(a)-\lambda(a_{i}\cdot a)\right|\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{b\in K}\left|\chi_{2}(b)-\lambda(b_{i}\cdot b)\right|\leq\varepsilon.

We have therefore, for
a\in K,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}(\widetilde{U})(a)\Phi(F)-\Phi(U^{(m)}(a)F)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 1}\int_{\Omega_{i}}|\chi_{1}(a)-\lambda(a_{i}\cdot a)|^{2}\|U(a)(\Phi(F)(\chi))\|^{2}d\nu(\chi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 1}\int_{\Omega_{i}}|\chi_{1}(a)-\lambda(a_{i}\cdot a)|^{2}\|\Phi(F)(\chi)\|^{2}d\nu(\chi)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{i\geq 1}\int_{\Omega_{i}}\|\Phi(F)(\chi)\|^{2}d\nu(\chi)=\varepsilon^{2}\|\Phi(F)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\|F\|^{2}

and, similarly, for
b\in K,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}(\widetilde{V})(b)\Phi(F)-\Phi(V^{(m)}(b)F)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2}\|F\|^{2}.

So,
(U^{(m)},V^{(m)})
is approximately equivalent to
(\mathcal{F}(\widetilde{U}),\mathcal{F}(\widetilde{V})),

which is obviously equivalent to
(\widetilde{U},\widetilde{V}))
;
since, by Proposition
2
,
(\widetilde{U},\widetilde{V}))
is equivalent to

(U_{\rm reg}^{(n)},V_{\rm reg}^{(n)}),

this concludes the proof of Theorem
C
.

3.
Examples

We give a few examples of locally compact rings
R
and
unitary characters
\lambda
in connection with the various Conditions
(Isom)
,

(Sym)
, and
(Faith)
.

(1)

Let
\mathbf{k}
be a locally compact field,
that is, a non-discrete locally compact topological field; it is well-known
(see e.g.
[
3
, Chap VI, §9]
) that
\mathbf{k}
is topologically isomorphic to one of the following fields
and that its topology is defined by the corresponding absolute value:

•

{\mathbf{R}}
or
{\mathbf{C}}
with the usual absolute
value
|\cdot|.

•

a finite extension of the field of
p
-adic numbers

\mathbf{Q}_{p}
with an extension of the
p
-adic absolute value
|\cdot|_{p}.

•

the field
\mathbf{F}((X))
of Laurent series
over a finite field
\mathbf{F}
with absolute value

|\sum_{i=m}^{\infty}a_{i}X^{i}|=e^{-m}
with

a_{m}\in\mathbf{F}\setminus\{0\}.

Let
\lambda\in\widehat{\mathbf{k}}
be a non trivial character of the additive group of
\mathbf{k}.

The map

\nabla_{\lambda}:\mathbf{k}\mapsto\widehat{\mathbf{k}},\,a\mapsto(x\mapsto\lambda(ax))

is an isomorphism of topological groups (see
[
14
, Chap. II, Theorem 3]
for a more general result); for the convenience of the reader, here is a direct proof.
The kernel of
\nabla_{\lambda}
is an ideal of
\mathbf{k}

and hence is trivial, since
\lambda\neq 1_{\mathbf{k}}.

By Pontrjagin duality, the image of
\nabla_{\lambda}
is a dense subgroup
H
of
\widehat{\mathbf{k}^{d}}

(see the argument at the beginning of Subsection
2.4
).
We claim that the map
\nabla_{\lambda},
which is obviously continuous, is in fact a homeomorphism between

\mathbf{k}
and
H
. Indeed, assume, by contradiction,
that
\nabla_{\lambda}^{-1}:H\to\mathbf{k}
is not continuous. Then, there exists a sequence
(a_{n})_{n}
in
\mathbf{k}

and a number
\delta>0
with
|a_{n}|>\delta
for all
n
such that
\lim_{n}\nabla_{\lambda}(a_{n})(a)=1

uniformly on bounded subsets of
\mathbf{k}
. Since
\lambda
is non trivial,
we can find
a\in\mathbf{k}
with
\lambda(a)\neq 1.

However, since
|aa_{n}^{-1}|\leq\dfrac{|a|}{\delta},

we have

\lambda(a)=\lambda(aa_{n}^{-1}a_{n})=\lim_{n}\lambda(aa_{n}^{-1}a_{n})=\lim_{n}\nabla_{\lambda}(a_{n})(aa_{n}^{-1})=1

and this is a contradiction. So,
\nabla_{\lambda}^{-1}
is continuous
and hence
H
is locally compact; this implies that it is a closed
subgroup of
\widehat{\mathbf{k}}
and, since it is also dense, we have
H=\widehat{\mathbf{k}}.

(2)

Let
R=\mathbf{A}
be the ring of adèles of
\mathbf{Q}
.
Recall that
\mathbf{A}
is the restricted product

\mathbf{A}={\mathbf{R}}\times\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},{\mathbf{Z}}_{p})

relative to the subgroups
{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}
; thus,

\mathbf{A}=\left\{(x_{\infty},(x_{p})_{p\in\mathcal{P}})\in{\mathbf{R}}\times\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbf{Q}_{p}\,:\,x_{p}\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}\text{ for almost every }\lx@nobreakspace p\in\mathcal{P}\right\},

where
\mathcal{P}
be the set of primes numbers,
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
is the field of
p
-adic numbers,
and
{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}=\{x\in\mathbf{Q}_{p}\,:|x|_{p}\leq 1\}

p

For every finite subset

equipped with the product topology, is a locally compact ring.

on prescribing that

With this topology,

Let

An example of such a character can be obtained as follows.
Let

for every

and

Define now

\lambda(x_{\infty},(x_{p})_{p\in\mathcal{P}})=\lambda_{\infty}(a_{\infty})\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\lambda_{p}(x_{p})\qquad\text{for all}\quad(x_{\infty},(x_{p})_{p\in\mathcal{P}})\in{\mathbf{A}}

Then

(3)

Let
\mathbf{k}
be a local field.
For an integer
n\geq 1,
let

be the ring of
n\times n

\mathbf{k};

of

Let
\lambda_{0}\in\widehat{\mathbf{k}}

Let

\lambda(A)=\lambda_{0}(\operatorname{Tr}(A))\qquad\text{for all}\quad A\in M_{n}(\mathbf{k}),

where
\operatorname{Tr}(A)\in\mathbf{k}

Since
\operatorname{Tr}(AB)=\operatorname{Tr}(BA)
for all

the character
\lambda
satisfies the symmetry

(Sym)
. It satisfies Condition
(Isom)
as well. Indeed,

On the one hand, viewing the additive group

On the other hand, it is well-known that every such

This shows that the continuous injective map

(4)

is uncountable and, since
{\mathbf{Z}}
is countable,

\nabla_{\lambda}:{\mathbf{Z}}\to\widehat{{\mathbf{Z}}}
cannot be surjective.
Observe, however, that Condition
(Faith)
is satisfied by
\lambda_{\theta}

for every irrational
\theta.

(7)

Similarly to the previous Item, if

any countable infinite ring (equipped with the discrete topology), then there exists no
\lambda\in\widehat{R}
which satisfies Condition
(Isom)
.

(8)

Let

Then, since

from Item (2) induces an isomorphism

given by

a+{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}\mapsto\nabla_{\lambda_{p}}(a)|_{{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}

So, there exists no
\lambda\in\widehat{{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}
which satisfies
Condition
(Faith)
and, let alone, Condition
(Isom)
.
However, one can easily determine the pairs of unitary representations
(U,V)

of
{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
satisfying

(

)

λ

for any
\lambda\in\widehat{{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}

indeed, assume by simplicity that
(U,V)
is irreducible.
The sequence of ideals

So, there exists

4.
Heisenberg groups

We rephrase our results in terms of representations of Heisenberg groups.
Let
R
be a (not necessarily commutative) locally compact ring with unit
and let
d\geq 1
be an integer.
The
Heisenberg group
of dimension
2d+1
over
R
is
is defined as the group
H_{2d+1}(R)
of
(2d+1)\times(2d+1)
-matrices with coefficients in
R
of the
form

m(a,b,c)=m(a_{1},\dots,a_{d},b_{1},\dots,b_{d},c):=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}1&a_{1}&\dots&a_{d}&c\\
0&1&\dots&0&b_{1}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&1&b_{d}\\
0&0&\dots&0&1\end{array}\right),

The group law on
H_{2d+1}(R)
is given by

m(a,b,c)m(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})\,=\,m(a+a^{\prime},b+b^{\prime},c+c^{\prime}+a\cdot b^{\prime}),

for
a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in R^{d}
and
c,c^{\prime}\in R.

Equipped with the topology induced from
M_{2d+1}(R),

the group
H_{2d+1}(R)
is a locally compact group.
Its center is

C=\{m(0,0,c)\,:\,c\in R\}\cong\,R.

Let
\pi:G\to U(\mathcal{H})

and
\pi^{\prime}:G\to U(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
be two unitary representations of a locally compact group
G
on Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.
Recall that
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are equivalent
if there exists a unitary isomorphism
\Phi:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}

such that

\Phi\pi(g)\Phi^{*}=\pi^{\prime}(g)\qquad\text{for all}\quad g\in G.

We say that
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are

approximately equivalent
if there exists
a sequence
(\Phi_{n})_{n}
of unitary isomorphisms
\Phi_{n}:\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}

such that, for every compact subset
K
of
G,
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{g\in K}\Phi_{n}\pi(g)\Phi_{n}^{*}-\pi^{\prime}(g)\|=0.

Approximate equivalence is an equivalence relation on, say, the set of unitary representations
of
G
on separable Hilbert spaces.

Fix
\lambda\in\widehat{R}
.
Let
(U,V)
be a pair of unitary representations of
R^{d}
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
satisfying

(
CCR
)

λ

,
For
m(a,b,c)\in H_{2d+1}(R),
define a unitary operator
\pi(m(a,b,c))
on
\mathcal{H}

by

\pi(m(a,b,c)=\lambda(c)V(b)U(a).

One checks immediately that
\pi:H_{2d+1}(R)\to U(\mathcal{H})

is a unitary representation of
H_{2d+1}(R);
observe that

\pi
has
\lambda
as central character, that is,
\pi(m(0,0,c))=\lambda(c){\rm Id}_{\mathcal{H}}

for
c\in R
.
If
(U^{\prime},V^{\prime})
is another pair which is equivalent (respectively, approximately equivalent) to
(U,V)
,
then the corresponding representation
\pi^{\prime}
is equivalent (respectively, approximately equivalent) to
\pi.

Conversely, let
\pi:H_{2d+1}(R)\to U(\mathcal{H})

be a unitary representation of
H_{2d+1}(R)
which
has
\lambda
as central character.
Define

U(a)=\pi(m(a,0,0))\quad\text{and}\quad V(b)=\pi(m(0,b,0))

for
a,b\in R^{d}.
Then
(U,V)
is a pair of unitary representations of
R^{d}
on
\mathcal{H}
satisfying

(
CCR
)

λ

.
Moreover, if
\pi^{\prime}
is another representation
of
H_{2d+1}(R)
which is equivalent (respectively, approximately equivalent) to
\pi
,
then
\pi^{\prime}
has
\lambda
as central character
and the corresponding pair
(U^{\prime},V^{\prime})
is equivalent (respectively, approximately equivalent) to
(U,V)
.

Summarizing, the map
(U,V)\mapsto\pi
described above
induces a bijection between

•

the set of equivalence (respectively, approximate equivalence) pairs of unitary representations of
R^{d}

satisfying

(
CCR
)

λ

, and

•

the set of equivalence (respectively, approximate equivalence) classes of unitary representations of
H_{2d+1}(R)
which have
\lambda
as central character,

The representation
\pi_{\text{Schr}}
of
H_{2d+1}(R)
corresponding to the
Schrödinger pair
(U_{\text{Schr}},V_{\text{Schr}})
with central character
\lambda
is given
on
L^{2}(R^{d},\mu)
by

\pi_{\text{Schr}}(m(a,b,c))f)(x)=\lambda(c)\lambda(x\cdot b)f(x+a)\quad f\in L^{2}(R^{d},\mu),

where
\mu
is a Haar measure on
(R^{d},+).

We can now reformulate Theorems
A
and
C
in terms of
representations of
H_{2d+1}(R).

Theorem D
.

(
Stone-von Neumann Theorem
)
Let
R
be a unital second-countable locally compact ring and let
\lambda\in\widehat{R}

satisfying Conditions
(Sym)
and
(Isom)
.
For
d\geq 1,
let
\pi
be a unitary representation of
H_{2d+1}(R)

on a separable Hilbert space with central character

Then
\pi^{(\infty)}
is equivalent to
\pi_{\text{Schr}}^{(\infty)}
.

Theorem E
.

Let
R
be as Theorem
D
, and let
\lambda\in\widehat{R}

satisfying Conditions
(Sym)
and
(Faith)

\pi

Remark 4
.

A crucial role in the proofs of our results was played by

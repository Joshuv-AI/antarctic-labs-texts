Cyclicity in Poletsky-Stessin Weighted Bergman Spaces
Pouriya Torkinejad Ziarati

Abstract
We study the cyclicity of polynomials in Poletsky-Stessin weighted Bergman spaces on various domains in ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} , including the unit ball, the bidisk, and the complex ellipsoid. To this end, we introduce a natural extension of the parameter range for Poletsky-Stessin weighted Bergman spaces on complete Reinhardt domains, yielding a family of spaces that resemble Dirichlet-type spaces on the unit ball. We highlight the differences in the cyclicity behavior of polynomials in these spaces on the bidisk compared to those studied by Bénéteau et al. Finally, we propose several open problems concerning the structure of cyclic polynomials in these spaces.



1
Introduction

Given a Banach space
\mathcal{A}
of holomorphic functions on a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and a function
f\in\mathcal{A}
,
f
is called
cyclic
when the span of polynomial multiples of
f
is dense in
\mathcal{A}
. If polynomials are dense in
\mathcal{A}
and act as multipliers for
\mathcal{A}
—that is, if for any polynomial
p
, we have
ph\in\mathcal{A}
whenever
h\in\mathcal{A}
—an equivalent characterization of cyclicity is that there exists a sequence of polynomials
p_{n}
such that
\|p_{n}f-1\|_{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow 0
. The problem of characterizing cyclic functions is highly space-dependent and remains challenging—even in Hilbert spaces of holomorphic functions on the unit disk. For instance, complete classifications of cyclic functions, such as Beurling’s theorem for the Hardy space of the unit disk
[
2
]
, are rare. The Brown–Shields conjecture, posed in 1984 for the Dirichlet space, remains open
[
3
]
. Recent studies on cyclicity in spaces of holomorphic functions on the unit disk—especially Dirichlet-type spaces—as well as partial results related to the Brown–Shields conjecture, appear in
[
4
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
7
]
. Additionally,
[
8
]
provides a comprehensive textbook treatment of Dirichlet-type spaces.

In several variables, a complete characterization of cyclic functions in Dirichlet-type spaces remains elusive for classical domains such as the unit ball and the polydisk. However, significant progress has been made in identifying cyclic polynomials, with explicit characterizations obtained in the unit ball of dimension 2 and the bidisk
[
5
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
13
]
.

Weighted Bergman spaces on the unit ball are initially defined via an integral norm and for a limited range of parameters. However, they can be naturally extended to cover the Dirichlet-type spaces of the unit ball, as discussed in
[
19
]
. In this work, we adopt the definition introduced by Poletsky and Stessin for weighted Bergman spaces on a hyperconvex domain using an exhaustion function, and we demonstrate that this definition can be extended naturally to a wider range of parameters whenever the domain is complete Reinhardt. On the unit ball, this construction recovers the classical Dirichlet-type spaces, and the corresponding norm varies continuously under continuous changes in the domain, in the sense of varying the exhaustion function. Although it can be shown that on the bidisk this construction does not reproduce the Dirichlet-type spaces studied in
[
5
]
and
[
1
]
, an important observation is that our results concerning the cyclicity of polynomials on the bidisk depend solely on the dimension of the zero set on the distinguished boundary—similar to the case of the unit ball
[
14
]
. This contrasts with the spaces in
[
5
]
, where polynomials of the form
\zeta-z_{i}
must be treated separately when categorizing cyclic polynomials whose zero set has dimension one on the distinguished boundary.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section 2, we recall the original definition of Poletsky and Stessin and apply it to several examples of domains, while also providing the necessary background for the rest of the paper. A precise definition and first properties of cyclicity are given in Section 2.4. Section 3 establishes some general results and extends Poletsky-Stessin Weighted Bergman Spaces to more general indices. The motivation here is to construct a family of spaces on complete Reinhardt domains that inherit properties of Dirichlet-type spaces. In Sections 4 and 5, we study the cyclicity problem in Poletsky-Stessin Weighted Bergman Spaces on the complex ellipsoid and the bidisk, respectively. Finally, in Section 6, we propose several open problems for future research.

2.2
Poletsky-Stessin Hardy Spaces

Definition 3
.

If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a hyperconvex domain equipped with a plurisubharmonic exhaustion function
u:\Omega\rightarrow[-\infty,0)
, then the Poletsky-Stessin Hardy space on
\Omega
with respect to
u
is defined as follows
[
16
]
:

H^{2}_{u}=\{f\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega):\mu_{u}(|f|^{2})<+\infty\}.

(1)

Here,
\mu_{u}
is the weak-
*
limit of the Monge–Ampère measures
\mu_{u_{r}}
as
r\to 0^{-}
, defined below, and
u_{r}=\max\{u,r\}
:

\mu_{u_{r}}=(dd^{c}u_{r})^{n}-\chi_{\Omega\setminus B_{r}}(dd^{c}u)^{n}.

(2)

The sets
B_{r}(u)
and
S_{r}(u)
are defined as:

B_{r}(u)=\{z\in\Omega:u(z)<r\},\quad S_{r}(u)=\{z\in\Omega:u(z)=r\}.

The following lemma will be used to construct several examples.

Lemma 4
.

[
17
]

Let
\sigma
and
v
be the normalized surface and volume measures for
\mathbb{S}_{n}
and
\mathbb{B}_{n}
, respectively. Assume that
\gamma\in(-1,+\infty)^{n}
. Then we have:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}|\zeta^{\gamma}|^{2}d\sigma(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(n)\Gamma(\gamma_{1}+1)\dots\Gamma(\gamma_{n}+1)}{\Gamma(\gamma_{1}+\dots+\gamma_{n}+n)},

(3)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|z^{\gamma}|^{2}dv(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(\gamma_{1}+1)\dots\Gamma(\gamma_{n}+1)}{\Gamma(\gamma_{1}+\dots+\gamma_{n}+n+1)}.

(4)

Here, we compute the norm of monomials in certain function spaces.

Example 5
.

Let
\Omega_{P}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|z_{1}|^{2p_{1}}+\dots+|z_{n}|^{2p_{n}}<1\}
, where
p_{i}
’s are positive integers and define

u(z)=\frac{1}{2(p_{1}\dots p_{n})^{1/n}}\log(|z_{1}|^{2p_{1}}+\dots+|z_{n}|^{2p_{n}}).

Note that since
u
is a maximal plurisubharmonic function, we have
\mu_{u_{r}}=(dd^{c}u_{r})^{n}
, which is supported on
S_{r}(u)
. Consequently, one can compute the norm of a monomial
z^{L}
, where
L
is a multi-index, as follows:

\displaystyle\mu_{u}(|z^{L}|^{2})

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\to 0^{-}}\mu_{u_{r}}(|z^{L}|^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{S_{r}(u)}|z^{L}|^{2}(dd^{c}u_{r})^{n}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{1}\ldots p_{n}}{p_{1}\ldots p_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}|\zeta_{1}|^{2\frac{l_{1}}{p_{1}}}\dots|\zeta_{n}|^{2\frac{l_{n}}{p_{n}}}d\sigma(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(n)\Gamma\left(\frac{l_{1}}{p_{1}}+1\right)\dots\Gamma\left(\frac{l_{n}}{p_{n}}+1\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{l_{1}}{p_{1}}+\dots+\frac{l_{n}}{p_{n}}+n\right)}.

Here, in the third equality, we used the
(p_{1}\dots p_{n})
-sheeted change of variables
z_{i}\mapsto z_{i}^{p_{i}}
(see Corollary 7.4 in
[
6
]
), along with the fact that
\mu_{v}=\sigma
for
v(z)=\log|z|
.

In the previous example, we observe that when
p_{i}=1
for all
i
, we obtain the same norm as in the classical Hardy space on the ball. Similarly, as
p_{i}\to\infty
for all
i
, we recover the monomial norm for the classical Hardy space on the polydisc.

Example 6
.

Let
\Omega_{\lambda}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:|z_{1}|<1,\ |z_{2}|<1,\ \lambda|z_{1}^{m}z_{2}^{n}|<1\}
and define

u(z)=C\log\max\{|z_{1}|,|z_{2}|,\lambda|z_{1}^{m}z_{2}^{n}|\},

where
\lambda>1
and
C>0
is chosen such that
\mu_{u}(1)=1
.

By a similar argument to Example 6.11 in
[
6
]
,
\mu_{u}
is supported on

T_{1}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:|z_{1}|=(1/\lambda)^{1/m},\ |z_{2}|=1\},\quad T_{2}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:|z_{1}|=1,\ |z_{2}|=(1/\lambda)^{1/n}\}.

We have

\mu_{u}=C_{1}\mu_{1}+C_{2}\mu_{2},

where
\mu_{i}
is the uniform probability measure supported on
T_{i}
. Note that:

\mu_{u}(1)=1=C_{1}\mu_{1}(1)+C_{2}\mu_{2}(1)=C_{1}+C_{2},

Now, when
z\in\Omega_{\lambda}
is sufficiently close to
T_{1}
, we have:

u(z)=u^{1}(z)=C\log\max\{|z_{2}|,\lambda|z_{1}^{m}z_{2}^{n}|\}.

Hence, if we set
\tilde{z_{1}}=z_{1}^{m}z_{2}^{n}
and
\tilde{z_{2}}=z_{2}
, then:

C_{1}=\lim_{r\to 0^{-}}\mu_{u^{1}_{r}}(S_{r}(u^{1}))=\lim_{r\to 0^{-}}\int_{S_{r}(u^{1})}(dd^{c}u^{1}_{r})^{2}(z)=\lim_{r\to 0^{-}}mC^{2}\int_{S_{\tilde{r}}(V)}(dd^{c}V_{\tilde{r}})^{2}(\tilde{z})=m\lambda C^{2}.

Here,
V(z)=\log\max\{\lambda|z_{1}|,|z_{2}|\}
, and
\tilde{r}=r/C
. Similarly, we obtain:

C_{2}=n\lambda C^{2}.

Hence we have
C_{1}=\frac{m}{m+n}
and
C_{2}=\frac{n}{m+n}
, the norm of the monomial
z_{1}^{k}z_{2}^{l}
then can be computed as:

\|z_{1}^{k}z_{2}^{l}\|^{2}=\frac{m\lambda^{-\frac{2k}{m}}+n\lambda^{-\frac{2l}{n}}}{m+n}.

Definition 7

If

Example 8

Let

\displaystyle\|z^{L}\|^{2}_{A^{2}_{u,\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{0}|r|^{\alpha}e^{r}\mu_{u_{r}}(|z^{L}|^{2})\,dr

2.4
Basic Results of Cyclicity

In this subsection, we present the definition of cyclicity along with fundamental results that are essential for understanding the rest of the paper as we use them several times throughout the paper. While these results hold in more general settings, we formulate them in the context of the function spaces studied here for convenience.

Definition 13
.

Let
A^{2}_{u,\alpha}
be a Poletsky-Stessin Weighted Bergman Space on a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, a function
f\in A^{2}_{u,\alpha}
is called a
shift-cyclic vector
if:

[f]=\overline{\operatorname{span}\{pf:p\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]\}}=A^{2}_{u,\alpha}.

(8)

Theorem 14
.

If
f\in A^{2}_{u,\alpha}(\Omega)
vanishes at
\zeta\in\Omega
then
f
is not cyclic.

Proof.

If we define
\Gamma_{\zeta}:A^{2}_{u,\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by
\Gamma_{\zeta}(f)=f(\zeta)
, then
\Gamma_{\zeta}
is a bounded linear functional and consequently has a closed kernel. For any polynomial
p
, we have
pf\in\operatorname{Ker}\Gamma_{\zeta}
. This implies:

[f]\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}\Gamma_{\zeta}\subsetneq A^{2}_{u,\alpha}.\qed

Remark 15
.

In the proof of Theorem
14
, we used the fact that the point evaluation functional
\Gamma_{\zeta}
is continuous for any point
\zeta
in the domain. In fact, the same proof applies whenever the point evaluation functional is bounded at some point in the closure of the domain, which occurs for certain values of
\alpha
in the spaces we study.

The following theorem offers a useful sufficient criterion to check whether a function is cyclic. A complete proof can be found in
[
13
]
.

Theorem 16
.

Let
f\in A^{2}_{u,\alpha}(\Omega)
and assume that
f
does not vanish on
\Omega
. For
0<r<1
, define the radial dilation

f_{r}(z)=f(rz).

If

\sup_{0<r<1}\left\|\frac{f}{f_{r}}\right\|_{A^{2}_{u,\alpha}}<+\infty,

then
f
is cyclic in
A^{2}_{u,\alpha}(\Omega)
.

3
General Results

Definition 17
.

Let
-1\leq s,t
, and let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a hyperconvex domain equipped with an exhaustion function
u
. Then, the operator
R^{s,t}:A^{2}_{u,t}(\Omega)\to A^{2}_{u,s}(\Omega)

is defined as follows:

(R^{s,t}f)(z)=\sum_{L}a_{L}\frac{\|\eta^{L}\|_{A^{2}_{u,t}}}{\|\eta^{L}\|_{A^{2}_{u,s}}}z^{L}.

Here,
f(z)=\sum_{L}a_{L}z^{L}\in A^{2}_{u,t}(\Omega)
.

It is straightforward to verify that
R^{s,t}
is a continuous operator on
A^{2}_{u,t}(\Omega)
with a continuous inverse.

Definition 18
.

We say that the spaces
A^{2}_{u,\alpha}(\Omega)
, for
-1\leq\alpha
, satisfy the Parameter Shift Equivalence(PSE) property if for all

\alpha,\alpha^{\prime},\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\geq-1
satisfying
\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}=\gamma-\gamma^{\prime}
, we have:

\frac{\|\eta^{L}\|_{A^{2}_{u,\alpha}}}{\|\eta^{L}\|_{A^{2}_{u,\alpha^{\prime}}}}\asymp\frac{\|\eta^{L}\|_{A^{2}_{u,\gamma}}}{\|\eta^{L}\|_{A^{2}_{u,\gamma^{\prime}}}},\quad\text{as }|L|\to\infty.

Definition 19
.

Let
\{A^{2}_{u,\alpha}(\Omega)\}_{\alpha\geq-1}
be a family of holomorphic function spaces satisfying the PSE property.
For
\gamma<-1
, we say that
f\in A^{2}_{u,\gamma}(\Omega)
if:

R^{{-(1+\gamma)}-1,-1}f\in A^{2}_{u,-1}(\Omega)=H^{2}_{u}(\Omega).

The norm of a function
f
in
A^{2}_{u,\gamma}(\Omega)
is defined as:

\|f\|_{A^{2}_{u,\gamma}}=\left\|R^{{-(1+\gamma)}-1,-1}f\right\|_{A^{2}_{u,-1}}.

(9)

Definition 20
.

Let
\mu\in M(\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{u}))
. The Cauchy transform of
\mu
, denoted by
C[\mu]
, is defined as follows:

C[\mu](z)=\int_{\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{u})}k_{-1}(z,\zeta)\,d\mu(\zeta).

(10)

Here,
k_{\alpha}
denotes the reproducing kernel of
A^{2}_{u,\alpha}
, which is given by:

k_{\alpha}(z,\zeta)=\sum_{L}\frac{z^{L}\overline{\zeta}^{L}}{\|\eta^{L}\|^{2}_{A^{2}_{u,\alpha}}}.

Definition 21
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
E\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{u})
. The
\alpha
-capacity of
E
is defined as follows:

\operatorname{Cap}_{\alpha}(E)=\inf\{I_{\alpha}[\mu]:\mu\in\mathcal{P}(E)\}^{-1}.

Here,
\mathcal{P}(E)
denotes the set of Borel probability measures supported on
E
, and

I_{\alpha}[\mu]=\iint_{\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{u})\times\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{u})}k_{\alpha}(z,\zeta)\,d\mu(z)\,d\mu(\zeta).

Now, in a similar fashion to
[
3
]
, one can prove the following Proposition and Theorem
23
:

Proposition 22
.

Let
\{A^{2}_{u,\alpha}(\Omega)\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}
be a family of holomorphic function spaces satisfying the PSE property. If
\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}=-2
, then the dual space satisfies:

\left(A^{2}_{u,\alpha}(\Omega)\right)^{*}=A^{2}_{u,\alpha^{\prime}}(\Omega).

Theorem 23
.

Let
f\in A^{2}_{u,\alpha}(\Omega)
. If
\operatorname{Cap}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{Z}(f)\cap\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{u}))>0
,
then
f
is not cyclic.

Proposition 24
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{D}^{n}
be the polyhedral domain defined by:

p_{j}(z)=\lambda_{j}|z_{1}|^{\beta_{1}^{j}}\cdots|z_{n}|^{\beta_{n}^{j}}<1,\quad j=1,\dots,m,

where
\lambda_{j}\geq 1
,
\beta_{i}^{j}\in\mathbb{Q}
, and
\sum_{i}\beta_{i}^{j}=1
for all
j
. If
m\geq n
, then the family of spaces
A^{2}_{u,\alpha}(\Omega)
satisfies the PSE property when

u(z)=\log\max\{p_{j}(z):j=1,\dots,m\}.

Proof.

Following Example 6.11 in
[
6
]
, the measure
\mu_{u_{r}}
is supported only on the common level set of exactly
n
functions
p_{j}
in
S_{r}
. On each such intersection,
\mu_{u_{r}}
equals a constant multiple of the uniform probability measure. In this situation, the vectors
\{\beta^{\sigma(j)}\}_{j=1}^{n}\subset\mathbb{Q}^{n}
must be linearly independent; otherwise, for

w(z)=\log\!\bigl(\max_{1\leq j\leq n}p_{\sigma(j)}(z)\bigr)

we would have
(dd^{c}w)^{n}\equiv 0
, hence,

\mu_{u_{r}}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}C_{j}\mu_{j}^{r}.

Here,
N
is the number of intersection sets, and the coefficients
C_{j}
satisfy
\sum_{j}C_{j}=1
, each
C_{j}
depends only on the domain
\Omega
and the exhaustion function
u
. To determine the support of each
\mu_{j}^{r}
, we solve the following linear system:

\begin{bmatrix}\beta_{1}^{\sigma_{j}(1)}&\cdots&\beta_{n}^{\sigma_{j}(1)}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\beta_{1}^{\sigma_{j}(n)}&\cdots&\beta_{n}^{\sigma_{j}(n)}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\log|z_{1}|\\
\vdots\\
\log|z_{n}|\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}r\\
\vdots\\
r\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}\log\lambda_{\sigma_{j}(1)}\\
\vdots\\
\log\lambda_{\sigma_{j}(n)}\end{bmatrix}.

A direct application of Cramer’s rule shows that:

|z_{i}|=C^{\prime}_{i}e^{r},

where each
C^{\prime}_{i}
is a constant independent of
r
and depends only on
\lambda_{i}
. Consequently, the norm of a monomial
z^{L}
in
A^{2}_{u,\alpha}(\Omega)
is given by:

\|z^{L}\|^{2}_{A^{2}_{u,\alpha}(\Omega)}=C_{L}(|L|+n)^{-(\alpha+1)}.

Since
C_{L}
is independent of
\alpha
, this establishes the PSE property for
A^{2}_{u,\alpha}(\Omega)
.
∎

Theorem 25
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{D}^{n}
be a hyperconvex Complete Reinhardt domain. Then, there exists an exhaustion function
u
for
\Omega
such that
A^{2}_{u,\alpha}(\Omega)
satisfies the PSE property.

Proof.

Consider the mapping
L:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by

L(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})=(\log|z_{1}|,\ldots,\log|z_{n}|).

For
x=(x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(n)})\in\Omega^{\prime}
, let

F_{0}(x)=\max\{x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(n)}\}.

Given a dense sequence
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\partial\Omega^{\prime}
, define the iterative sequence:

F_{i}=\max\{F_{i-1},f_{i}\},

where
f_{i}(x)=x\cdot v_{i}+\lambda_{i}
with
(1,\ldots,1)\cdot v_{i}=1
, and
v_{i}\in[0,1]^{n}
is such that
f_{i}(x_{i})=0
and
f_{i}(x)<0
for all
x\in\Omega^{\prime}
. Then, the sequence
g_{i}=F_{i}\circ L
consists of increasing plurisubharmonic functions on
\Omega
converging to an exhaustion function
u
for
\Omega
. So, by Theorem 3.6.1 in
[
12
]
, we have

\|z^{L}\|_{A^{2}_{g_{i},\alpha}}\to\|z^{L}\|_{A^{2}_{u,\alpha}}

for any multi-index
L
and
\alpha\in[-1,0]
. Applying Proposition
24
completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 27
.

Given

\mathcal{D}_{\beta}(\Omega_{p})

\beta\leq 2

Proof.

\displaystyle\sup_{0<r<1}\left\|\frac{f}{f_{r}}\right\|^{2}_{A^{2}_{u,\alpha}(\Omega_{p})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<r<1}\left\|(1-r_{1}^{2p-1}z_{1}-r_{2}^{2p-1}z_{2})(1+r(r_{1}^{2p-1}z_{1}+r_{2}^{2p-1}z_{2})+r^{2}(r_{1}^{2p-1}z_{1}+r_{2}^{2p-1}z_{2})^{2}+\ldots)\right\|^{2}_{A^{2}_{u,\alpha}(\Omega_{p})}

\displaystyle=1+\sup_{0<r<1}(1-r)^{2}\sum_{j\geq 1}r^{2(j-1)}\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}=j}\binom{j}{j_{1}}^{2}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{j_{1}}{p}+1\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{j_{2}}{p}+1\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{j}{p}+2\right)}(j+2)^{\beta}r_{1}^{2(2p-1)j_{1}}r_{2}^{2(2p-1)j_{2}}

\displaystyle\asymp 1+\sup_{0<r<1}(1-r)^{2}\sum_{j\geq 1}r^{2(j-1)}(j+2)^{\beta-1}\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}=j}\binom{j}{j_{1}}r_{1}^{2pj_{1}}r_{2}^{2pj_{2}}\frac{j^{j\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}}{j_{1}^{j_{1}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}j_{2}^{j_{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}}r_{1}^{2(p-1)j_{1}}r_{2}^{2(p-1)j_{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+\sup_{0<r<1}(1-r)^{2}\sum_{j\geq 1}r^{2(j-1)}(j+2)^{\beta-1}\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}=j}\binom{j}{j_{1}}r_{1}^{2pj_{1}}r_{2}^{2pj_{2}}

\displaystyle=1+\sup_{0<r<1}(1-r)^{2}\sum_{j\geq 1}r^{2(j-1)}(j+2)^{\beta-1}

\displaystyle\asymp 1+\sup_{0<r<1}(1-r)^{2-\beta}(1+r)^{-\beta}<+\infty.

Here, the second estimate comes from applying Stirling’s approximation on
\binom{j}{j_{1}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{j_{1}}{p}+1\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{j_{2}}{p}+1\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{j}{p}+2\right)}
, and the third one comes from the fact that the function
F(x)=\frac{r_{1}^{2(p-1)x}r_{2}^{2(p-1)(1-x)}}{x^{x\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}(1-x)^{(1-x)\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}}
attains its maximum at

. And now Theorem
16
finishes the proof.
∎

Corollary 30
.

Let
q
be an irreducible polynomial, non-vanishing on
\Omega_{p}
, moreover assume that
q
has finitely many zeros on
\partial\Omega_{p}
, then
q
is cyclic on
\mathcal{D}_{\beta}(\Omega_{p})
if and only if
\beta\leq 2
.

Proof.

Let
\{\zeta_{j}\}_{j=1,\ldots,k}=\mathcal{Z}(q)\cap\partial\Omega_{p}
, and let
s_{j}
be polynomials of the form similar to those in Proposition
27
, vanishing on
\partial\Omega_{p}
only at
\zeta_{j}
. It is straightforward to observe that there exists a constant
C_{j}>0
such that, for any
z\in\partial\Omega_{p}
,

\operatorname{dist}(z,\zeta_{j})\geq C_{j}|s_{j}(z)|.

On the other hand, Łojasiewicz’s inequality
[
15
]
gives that there exists a positive integer
m
and a constant
C>0
such that

|q(z)|\geq C\operatorname{dist}(z,\mathcal{Z}(q)\cap\partial\Omega_{p})^{m}

for any
z\in\partial\Omega_{p}
. Combining these facts, together with the fact that the
s_{j}
’s are uniformly bounded on
\partial\Omega_{p}
, we obtain for some constant
C^{\prime}>0
:

|q(z)|\geq C^{\prime}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|s_{j}(z)|^{m}

for any
z\in\partial\Omega_{p}
, and consequently on
\Omega_{p}
. By increasing

sufficiently, we may assume that

Q(z)=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k}s_{j}(z)^{m}}{q(z)}\in\mathcal{D}_{\beta}(\Omega_{p}).

Since
q
is a multiplier,
qQ
is cyclic as a finite product of cyclic functions. Fix
\epsilon>0
, and let
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
be polynomials such that

\|p_{1}qQ-1\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\beta}}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}

and

\|p_{2}-Q\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\beta}}<\|p_{1}q\|^{-1}_{\operatorname{Mult}(\mathcal{D}_{\beta})}\frac{\epsilon}{2},

where
\|p_{1}q\|_{\operatorname{Mult}(\mathcal{D}_{\beta})}
denotes the multiplier norm of
p_{1}q
, and the inequality follows from the density of polynomials in
\mathcal{D}_{\beta}
. Then, we have

\|p_{1}p_{2}q-1\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\beta}}\leq\|p_{1}p_{2}q-p_{1}qQ\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\beta}}+\|p_{1}qQ-1\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\beta}}<\epsilon.\qed

Proposition 34
.

Let
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}

Proposition 35
.

A^{2}_{u,\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{n})=\mathcal{D}_{\beta}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is an algebra when
\beta=-(\alpha+1)>n
.

Proof.

Let
f(z)=\sum_{|L|\geq 0}a_{L}z^{L}
and
g(z)=\sum_{|L|\geq 0}b_{L}z^{L}
be holomorphic functions in
\mathcal{D}_{\beta}(\mathbb{D}^{n})

f(z)g(z)=\sum_{|L|\geq 0}\sum_{L_{1}+L_{2}=L}a_{L_{1}}b_{L_{2}}z^{L}.

The squared norm of

||f\cdot g||^{2}=\sum_{|L|\geq 0}(|L|+n)^{\beta}\left|\sum_{L_{1}+L_{2}=L}a_{L_{1}}b_{L_{2}}\right|^{2}.

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{L_{1}+L_{2}=L}a_{L_{1}}b_{L_{2}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{L_{1}+L_{2}=L}\frac{1}{(|L_{1}|+n)^{\beta}(|L_{2}|+n)^{\beta}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left(\sum_{L_{1}+L_{2}=L}|a_{L_{1}}|^{2}(|L_{1}|+n)^{\beta}|b_{L_{2}}|^{2}(|L_{2}|+n)^{\beta}\right).

Thus:

\displaystyle\|f\cdot g\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{L}(|L|+n)^{\beta}\left(\sum_{L_{1}+L_{2}=L}\frac{1}{(|L_{1}|+n)^{\beta}(|L_{2}|+n)^{\beta}}\right)\left(\sum_{L_{1}+L_{2}=L}|a_{L_{1}}|^{2}(|L_{1}|+n)^{\beta}|b_{L_{2}}|^{2}(|L_{2}|+n)^{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|L|\geq 0}\left(\frac{|L|+n}{|L|+2n}\right)^{\beta}\sum_{L_{1}+L_{2}=L}\left(\frac{1}{(|L_{1}|+n)}+\frac{1}{(|L_{2}|+n)}\right)^{\beta}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\sum_{L_{1}+L_{2}=L}|a_{L_{1}}|^{2}(|L_{1}|+n)^{\beta}|b_{L_{2}}|^{2}(|L_{2}|+n)^{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|L|\geq 0}\sum_{L_{1}+L_{2}=L}\left(\frac{1}{(|L_{1}|+n)}+\frac{1}{(|L_{2}|+n)}\right)^{\beta}\left(\sum_{L_{1}+L_{2}=L}|a_{L_{1}}|^{2}(|L_{1}|+n)^{\beta}|b_{L_{2}}|^{2}(|L_{2}|+n)^{\beta}\right)

. Proposition
36
answers the cyclicity problem of this function for
\beta\leq\frac{3}{2}
, and the case
\frac{3}{2}<\beta\leq 2
remains unsolved. In fact, similar to the Corollary
30
we have the same cyclicity situation for any polynomial having finitely many zeros on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
.

Note that the proof of the Proposition
27
cannot be applied for the case of the bidisk by letting
p\rightarrow{\infty}
because the asymptotic constants there are dependent on
p
and they go to
\infty
in that case.

Proposition 38
.

If
f(z)=\zeta-z_{i},\ i=1,2
, where
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
then
f
is cyclic in
\mathcal{D}_{\beta}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
if and only if
\beta\leq 1
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can prove the statement only for
f(z)=1-z_{1}
. Assume that
\beta\leq 1
. To prove the cyclicity of
f
, as in Proposition
27
, we need to estimate the norm of
f/f_{r}
and then apply Theorem
16
.

\sup_{0<r<1}\left\|\frac{f}{f_{r}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}_{\beta}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}=1+\sup_{0<r<1}(1-r)^{2}\sum_{j\geq 1}(j+2)^{\beta}r^{2j-2}\asymp 1+\sup_{0<r<1}(1-r)^{1-\beta}<+\infty.

Now let
\beta>1
, our goal is to show that the capacity of
\mathcal{Z}(f)\cap\mathbb{T}^{2}
is positive to apply Theorem
23
. Assume that
\mu
is the uniform probability measure on
\{1\}\times\mathbb{T}=\mathcal{Z}(f)\cap\mathbb{T}^{2}
we will have:

I_{\beta}[\mu]=\iint_{\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}}k_{\beta}(z,w)d\mu(z)d\mu(w)=\iint_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}\sum_{|J|\geq 0}\frac{z_{2}^{j_{2}}\bar{w}_{2}^{j_{2}}}{(|J|+2)^{\beta}}d\mu(z_{2})d\mu(w_{2})=\sum_{j_{1}\geq 0}\frac{1}{(j_{1}+2)^{\beta}}<+\infty.

This implies that the capacity of
\mathcal{Z}(f)\cap\mathbb{T}^{2}
is positive.
∎

Remark 39
.

To complete the characterization of cyclic polynomials in
\mathcal{D}_{\beta}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
, it remains to consider the case of irreducible polynomials that do not vanish on
\mathbb{D}^{2}
but vanish at infinitely many points on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. We have already treated the case of polynomials of the form
\zeta-z_{i}
. If
f
is not of this form, the treatment of this case is very similar to the approach discussed in
[
5
]
; we include a sketch of the reasoning here for completeness.

To prove that such an
f
is cyclic for
\beta\leq 1
, one can show that there exists a unitary
(n+m)\times(n+m)
matrix
U
and a column vector polynomial
B\in\mathbb{C}^{n+m}[z_{1},z_{2}]
such that

\left(I-A(z)\right)B(z)\in f(z)\,\mathbb{C}^{n+m}[z_{1},z_{2}],

where

A(z)=U\begin{bmatrix}I_{n}z_{1}&0\\
0&I_{m}z_{2}\end{bmatrix}.

Moreover, there exists a row vector polynomial
a\in\mathbb{C}^{n+m}[z_{1},z_{2}]
such that
p(z_{2})=a(z)B(z)
is a one-variable polynomial that does not vanish on
\mathbb{D}
. Now, let
g\in[f]^{\perp}
. For any row vector polynomial
v\in\mathbb{C}^{n+m}
, one can show that, for all integers
k\geq 0
,

\langle g,vB\rangle=\langle g,vA^{k}B\rangle.

If we choose
d=n+m+1
, it follows that the family
\{vA^{kd}B\}_{k\geq 0}
is orthogonal. Moreover, we have the estimate
\|vA^{kd}B\|\lesssim(kd+1)^{\beta}
. These observations imply that

\|g\|^{2}\geq\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\left|\langle g,vA^{k}B\rangle\right|^{2}}{\|vA^{k}B\|^{2}}\gtrsim\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{(k+1)^{\beta}}\left|\langle g,vB\rangle\right|^{2}.

Since the series on the right diverges for
\beta<1
, it follows that
\langle g,vB\rangle=0
. Finally, by taking
v=z_{1}^{j_{1}}z_{2}^{j_{2}}a
, and using the fact that
p(z_{2})=a(z)B(z)
is cyclic, we conclude that
g=0
.

For
\beta>1
, it is known that
f
is not cyclic in the Dirichlet-type space
\mathfrak{D}_{\beta/2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
studied in
[
5
]
. Moreover, the trivial inequality
j_{1}j_{2}\leq(j_{1}+j_{2})^{2}
implies that

\|h\|_{\mathfrak{D}_{\beta/2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}\leq\|h\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\beta}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}

for any polynomial
h
. Therefore,
f
is also non-cyclic in
\mathcal{D}_{\beta}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
.

6
Discussion and Open Problems

Comparing our main result on the bidisk with the one in
[
5
]
, we observe that, in our case, the cyclicity of a polynomial is completely determined by the dimension of its zero set on the distinguished boundary. In contrast, in
[
5
]
, polynomials of the form
\zeta-z_{i}
exhibit a different cyclicity behavior compared to other polynomials that vanish at infinitely many points on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
.

For
\frac{3}{2}<\beta\leq 2

f(z)=1-\frac{z_{1}+z_{2}}{2}

\mathcal{D}_{\beta}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
.

.

Let
f
be a polynomial that does not vanish on
\Omega_{p}
and vanishes on a set of real dimension one on
\partial\Omega_{p}
. Determine under what conditions
f
is cyclic in
\mathcal{D}_{\beta}(\Omega_{p})
.

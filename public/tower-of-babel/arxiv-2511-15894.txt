On the Injectivity of STFT Phase Retrieval with super-exponential decaying window function
Shuang Guan and Kasso A. Okoudjou

Abstract
We investigate the uniqueness of short-time Fourier transform phase retrieval problems in L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}) . In particular, for underlying window functions whose Fourier transform decay faster than any exponential function, we derive sufficient conditions on discrete sampling sets for unique phase retrieval from the spectrogram. This result generalizes previous uniqueness guarantees on sampling sets for Gaussian windows.



1
Introduction

The phase retrieval problem refers to the task of recovering a signal from only intensity measurements under a given operator. This problem arises naturally in a wide range of applications in science and engineering, including X-ray crystallography
millane1990phase
, coherent diffraction imaging
walther1963question
and speech recognition
picone1996fundamentals
. In recent years, the phase retrieval problem has attracted significant interests, involving tools from harmonic analysis, complex analysis, functional analysis and numerical optimization
candes2015phase
;
alaifari2021phase
;
grohs2019stable
. Mathematically the phase retrieval problem is a nonlinear inverse problem that is typically ill-posed due to the natural ambiguity introduced by the loss of phase information. Over the years, addressing this subtle problem has led to a rich literature seeking to examine conditions for stability and feasibility of certain reconstruction algorithms, spanning both discrete and continuous settings.

In this paper, we specifically study the phase retrieval problem in the setting of short-time Fourier transform (STFT). For a signal
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and a window function
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, the STFT is defined as a function in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
given by:

V_{g}f(x,\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\overline{g(t-x)}e^{2\pi i\omega\cdot t}dt,\quad(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

In many real-world applications, detectors can only capture the intensity,
|V_{g}f(x,\omega)|
, known as the spectrogram. The loss of phase information leads to the STFT phase retrieval problem: determine the unique signal
f
up to a global sign from a sampling set of spectrogram
|V_{g}f(\Lambda)|
for some
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

While the STFT phase retrieval problem is ill-posed in general, it is known that uniqueness can still be guaranteed, up to a global phase factor, by imposing certain conditions on the window function
g
and sampling on a sufficiently dense set
\Lambda
. However, the difficulty of this problem is two-fold: First, it was recently proved that if
\Lambda
is an integer lattice, i.e,
\Lambda=A\mathbb{Z}^{2}
for some
A\in GL(2,\mathbb{R})
, then the uniqueness is never attained
grohs2023injectivity
.
The second difficulty is concerning the window function
g
, in the sense that if
V_{g}g(x,\omega)
vanishes on a set of positive Lebesgue measure, then there’s no established uniqueness result for general
f
.

Recently L. Liehr and P. Grohs
grohs2024phaseless
showed that when
g
is Gaussian, uniqueness is guaranteed for general
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
if the sampling set
\Lambda
is a square-root lattice, i.e.,
\Lambda=\{A(\pm\sqrt{n},\pm\sqrt{n}),n\in\mathbb{N}\}
for some
A\in GL(2,\mathbb{R})
. In what follows, we investigate how a weaker decay property of window function
g
affects the injectivity result and determine the corresponding sampling set. More specifically, our main result can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1

Let
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with Fourier transform satisfies the super-exponential decay condition:

|\hat{g}(\xi)|\leq Ce^{-a|\xi|^{m}}

for some constants
m>1
,
C>0
,
a\in(1,\infty)
. Let
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfy

\tau_{1}<\big(\frac{2}{(2\pi)^{m/(m-1)}(ma)^{-1/(m-1)}e}\big)^{\frac{m-1}{m}},\quad\quad\quad\tau_{2}<(\frac{2}{ame})^{\frac{1}{m}}.

Define the set
\Lambda
such that:

\Lambda=\{(\pm\tau_{1}n^{\frac{m-1}{m}},\pm\tau_{2}n^{\frac{1}{m}}),n\in\mathbb{N}\}

Then the following statements are equivalent for every
f,h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}):

1.

|V_{g}(f)(\lambda)|=|V_{g}h(\lambda)|
for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

2.

f=e^{i\alpha}h
for some
\alpha\in[0,2\pi).

Figure 1:
An illustration of sampling points
\Lambda
in time-frequency plane for
g
with Fourier decay parameter
m=\frac{3}{2}
and
a=1

The rest of the paper is organized as follows: In Section 2 we introduce some necessary preliminaries. Subsequently we prove Theorem
1.1
in Section 3. To this end, we first establish a sufficient condition for a discrete set to be a uniqueness set for the relevant class of entire functions (Proposition 1) and use Proposition 2 to show that our result is in a sense ”sharp”. We then use these technical results to prove Theorem
1.1
, which provides a concrete discrete sampling set

that guarantees the unique recovery of
f
up to a global phase.

2
Preliminary

A central principle in Fourier analysis is the connection between the smoothness of a function and the decay rate of its Fourier transform at infinity. The Paley-Wiener theorem and its variants are the primary tools that precisely characterize this relationship, providing the foundation for extending functions into the complex domain based on the behavior of their Fourier transforms.

2.1
Paley-Wiener Type Theorem

The Fourier transform of a Lebesgue integrable function

\mathcal{F}(f):=\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)e^{-2\pi it\cdot\xi}dt,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

A standard density argument can be used to extend this definition so that

(1)

m>1
,

and

can

Theorem A

(

For this integral to converge, we can analyze its magnitude using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\overline{g(t-z)}e^{2\pi iz^{\prime}t}f(t)dt\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\overline{g(t-z)}|^{2}|e^{2\pi iz^{\prime}t}|^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(t)|^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}

Fix
z^{\prime}=x^{\prime}+iy^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}
and let
K\subset\mathbb{C}
be compact. We prove that the previous integral converges uniformly for
z\in K
.

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\overline{g(t-z)}|^{2}|e^{2\pi iz^{\prime}t}|^{2}dt=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|g(t-z)|^{2}e^{-4\pi y^{\prime}t}dt

(9)

From Corollary
1
we conclude that there exists positive real number
a,b,C
such that

|g(x+iy)|\leq Ce^{-a|x|^{\rho}+b|y|^{\rho}}.

which leads to

|g((t-x)+iy)|^{2}\leq Ce^{-2a|t-x|^{\rho}+2b|y|^{\rho}}.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|g(t-z)|^{2}e^{-4\pi y^{\prime}t}dt\leq C\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-2a|t-x|^{\rho}+2b|y|^{\rho}-4\pi y^{\prime}t}dt<\infty

It follows from Theorem A that (
9
) converges uniformly for
z=x+iy\in K
.

Similarly, fix
z=x+iy
and consider
z^{\prime}=x^{\prime}+iy^{\prime}\subset K^{\prime}
, where
K^{\prime}
is compact. Then we have

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\overline{g(t-z)}|^{2}|e^{2\pi iz^{\prime}\xi}|^{2}dt\leq C\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-2a|t-x|^{\rho}+2b|y|^{\rho}-4\pi y^{\prime}t}dt.

(10)

Since
z=x+iy
is fixed, for
z^{\prime}\in K^{\prime}
it follows that (
10
) converges for
z^{\prime}\in K
. Following a statement from
(
boas2011entire
, Page 68)
, the order and type of an entire function is retained under integral, therefore the orders and types for
|V_{g}f(z,z^{\prime})|
can be computed directly using Lemma
2
. It is noteworthy that our computation for orders is compatible with the result from
(
grohs2024phaseless
, Lemma 3.4)
when
m=2
.

3
Main Result

Denote the ring of holomorphic functions by
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C})
. Consider the collection of entire functions with
\rho>1
and
a,b>0
:

\mathfrak{G}_{a,b,\rho}=\{f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C})\big||f(x+iy)|\lesssim e^{-a|x|^{\rho}+b|y|^{\rho}}\}.

It is straightforward to see that
\mathfrak{G}_{a,b,\rho}
is a linear space. The objective is to establish a uniqueness set so that whenever two functions
f,g\in\mathfrak{G}_{a,b,\rho}
agree on the set, they agree
everywhere
. Recent literature shows that such functions cannot be uniquely determined from their values on integer lattices
alaifari2021phase
. For the purpose of notation, let
U\subset\mathcal{O(\mathbb{C})}
denote a linear function space. A set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{C}
is called a uniqueness set of
U
if for any
f\in U
, it holds that

(f(\lambda)=0\;\text{for all}\;\lambda\in\Lambda)\Longrightarrow f=0\;\text{everywhere on }\>\mathbb{C}.

Since zero of any entire function can’t be an accumulation point, any open set in
\mathbb{C}
is trivially a uniqueness set. Without this property, determining discrete uniqueness sets of
\mathfrak{G}_{a,b,\rho}
contained in
\mathbb{R}
is a nontrivial task. We first propose a sufficient condition for such a set.

Proposition 1

Let
\rho>1
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Consider an increasing sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}
such that

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\lambda_{k}}{k^{1/\rho}}<\left(\frac{2}{b\rho e}\right)^{1/\rho}

and a sequence
\Lambda:=\{\pm\lambda_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
. Then
\Lambda
is a uniqueness set for the class of entire functions in
\mathfrak{G}_{a,b,\rho}
.

Proof

Suppose
f
is a function in the given class and assume
f(0)=1
without loss of generality. We begin with Jensen’s formula (Theorem
C
):

\int_{0}^{r}\frac{n(t)}{t}dt=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|f(re^{i\theta})|d\theta.

The growth condition on
f(z)
implies that for
z=re^{i\theta}=r\cos\theta+ir\sin\theta
:

\log|f(re^{i\theta})|\leq\log C-a|r\cos\theta|^{\rho}+b|r\sin\theta|^{\rho}=\log C+r^{\rho}(-a|\cos\theta|^{\rho}+b|\sin\theta|^{\rho}).

To find an upper bound for the integral, we maximize the angular term
g(\theta)=-a|\cos\theta|^{\rho}+b|\sin\theta|^{\rho}
. This term is maximized when
|\sin\theta|=1
and
|\cos\theta|=0
, which occurs at
\theta=\pm\pi/2
. The maximum value is
g(\pi/2)=-a(0)^{\rho}+b(1)^{\rho}=b
. Then we have

\int_{0}^{r}\frac{n(t)}{t}dt\leq\log C+br^{\rho}.

Since the zero-counting function
n(t)
is non-decreasing, for any
s>1
we can estimate
n(r)
:

n(r)\log s\leq\int_{r}^{sr}\frac{n(t)}{t}dt\leq\int_{0}^{sr}\frac{n(t)}{t}dt\leq\log C+b(sr)^{\rho}=\log C+bs^{\rho}r^{\rho}.

Where in the inequalities above, we used the fact that
n(sr)\geq n(r)\geq n(0)
are fixed number and therefore

n(sr)\log(sr)-n(r)\log(r)\geq n(r)\big(\log(sr)-\log(r)\big)=n(r)\log(s).

Now, assume
f
vanishes on
\Lambda
but is not identically zero. We choose
r=\lambda_{k}
, which implies that the number of zeros
n(r)=\#(\lambda_{k})\geq 2k
. Substituting this into our inequality gives

2k\log s\leq\log C+bs^{\rho}\lambda_{k}^{\rho}.

We rearrange the terms to find a lower bound for the limit infimum of the zero distribution:

\frac{2\log s}{bs^{\rho}}-\frac{\log C}{bs^{\rho}k}\leq\frac{\lambda_{k}^{\rho}}{k}.

Taking the limit inferior as
k\to\infty
, the term containing
\log C
vanishes, and we get

\frac{2\log s}{bs^{\rho}}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\lambda_{k}^{\rho}}{k}=\left(\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\lambda_{k}}{k^{1/\rho}}\right)^{\rho}.

This inequality holds for any
s>1
. To obtain the tightest possible bound, we must maximize the left-hand side with respect to
s
. Let
g(s)=\frac{\log s}{s^{\rho}}
. The derivative
g^{\prime}(s)=s^{\rho-1}(1-\rho\log s)/s^{2\rho}
is zero when
1-\rho\log s=0
, which occurs at
s=e^{1/\rho}
.

The maximum value of
g(s)
is therefore:

g(e^{1/\rho})=\frac{\log(e^{1/\rho})}{(e^{1/\rho})^{\rho}}=\frac{1/\rho}{e}=\frac{1}{\rho e}.

Substituting this maximum value back into our inequality gives the necessary condition for a non-zero function to exist:

\frac{2}{b}\left(\frac{1}{\rho e}\right)\leq\left(\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\lambda_{k}}{k^{1/\rho}}\right)^{\rho}.

Taking the
\rho
-th root of both sides, we find the final lower bound:

\left(\frac{2}{b\rho e}\right)^{1/\rho}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\lambda_{k}}{k^{1/\rho}}.

Hence, if

\left(\frac{2}{b\rho e}\right)^{1/\rho}>\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\lambda_{k}}{k^{1/\rho}}.

would give a contradiction and
f
must be
0
identically.

Next we introduce a lower bound of the condition on the zero set for
\mathfrak{G}_{a,b,\rho}
. The way we prove this is to construct a nontrivial entire function with prescribed zeros and show that this function is indeed in
\mathfrak{G}_{a,b,\rho}
.

Proposition 2

Let
\rho>1
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Consider an increasing sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\},k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\Lambda:=\{\pm\lambda_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
. If

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\lambda_{k}}{k^{1/\rho}}>C_{\rho},

where
C_{\rho}
is defined as some positive number satisfying

C_{\rho}=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{\pi}{b\sin(\pi\rho/2)}\right)^{1/\rho}&\text{if }\rho/2\text{ is not an integer}\\
\left(\frac{\pi}{b}\right)^{1/\rho}&\text{if }\rho/2\text{ is an integer}\end{cases}

then
\Lambda
is
not
a uniqueness set for functions in
\mathfrak{G}_{a,b,\rho}
.

Proof

Assume
\rho/2
is not an integer. Let
V(w)
be the canonical product of genus
p=\lfloor\rho/2\rfloor
constructed from the zeros with
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\omega_{k}=\lambda_{k}^{2}
:

V(\omega)=\omega^{m}\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}G(\frac{\omega}{\omega_{k}},p)

where

G(u,p)=(1-u)e^{u+u^{2}/2+\cdots+u^{p}/p}.

The growth of the canonical product
V(w)
can be determined from the distribution of its zeros. To build this connection, we first define a quantitative description of zeros
\Delta_{V}
for
V(\omega)
by

\Delta_{V}=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{n_{V}(r)}{r^{\rho/2}}

where
n_{G}(r)
is the zero-counting function for
V
inside
B_{r}(0)\subseteq\mathbb{C}
. Since
V
is an entire function of finite order and its zeros are assumed to lie on the positive ray, it is of
completely regular growth
and the limit
\Delta_{V}
always exists. The condition on
\Lambda
indicates that for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists an integer
K
such that for all
k>K
:

\lambda_{k}>(C_{\rho}+\epsilon)k^{1/\rho}.

The zeros of
V(\omega)
are precisely
\omega_{k}=\lambda_{k}^{2}
, so for
k>K
, we have
\omega_{k}>(C_{\rho}+\epsilon)^{2}k^{2/\rho}
, therefore, consider zeros inside
B_{r}(0)
, we have

(C_{\rho}+\epsilon)^{2}k^{2/\rho}<\omega_{k}\leq r\implies k<\left(\frac{r}{(C_{\rho}+\epsilon)^{2}}\right)^{\rho/2}=\frac{r^{\rho/2}}{(C_{\rho}+\epsilon)^{\rho}}.

Hence the total counting of zeros for any
r\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
must satisfy this bound:

n_{V}(r)\leq K+\frac{r^{\rho/2}}{(C_{\rho}+\epsilon)^{\rho}},

where on the right hand side, the
K
term comes from counting
\omega_{i},i\in\{1,\cdots,K\}
and the later term comes from the limit behavior. Finally we conclude that

\Delta_{V}=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{n_{V}(r)}{r^{\rho/2}}\leq\frac{1}{(C_{\rho}+\epsilon)^{\rho}}<\frac{1}{C_{\rho}^{\rho}}.

Using Theorem
D
we obtain

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{|\ln V(re^{i\theta})|}{r^{\rho/2}}=\frac{\pi\Delta_{V}}{|\sin\pi\rho/2|}.

(11)

Notice that for finite
\omega\in\mathbb{C}
,
V(\omega)
has no pole, we can always find a fixed real number
C
such that
|G(x+iy)|\leq Ce^{-a|x|^{\rho/2}+b|y|^{\rho/2}}
. Therefore to prove our argument, it suffices to prove

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\ln|V(re^{i\theta})|}{r^{\rho/2}}<b.

Substitute the value of
\Delta_{V}
into Equation (
11
), we obtain

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{|\ln V(re^{i\theta})|}{r^{\rho/2}}<\frac{\pi}{|\sin\pi\rho/2|}\cdot\frac{b\sin(\pi\rho/2)}{\pi}=b

notice that we assumed the zeros are all on the positive real axis, hence we automatically have
\sin\pi\rho/2
is positive. Therefore we conclude that

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\ln|V(re^{i\theta})|}{r^{\rho/2}}=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\Re(\ln V(re^{i\theta}))}{r^{\rho/2}}\leq\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{|\ln V(re^{i\theta})|}{r^{\rho/2}}<b.

The proof follows similarly for
\rho/2
is an integer. Finally, consider
F(z)=V(z^{2})
, then the nontrivial function
F
vanishes on
\Lambda
and satisfies the growth order and type.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

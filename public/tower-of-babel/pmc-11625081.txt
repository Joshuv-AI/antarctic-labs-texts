A basic system of paraconsistent Nelsonian logic of conditionals
Authors: Grigory K Olkhovikov
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe define a Kripke semantics for a conditional logic based on the propositional logic N4, the paraconsistent variant of Nelson’s logic of strong negation; we axiomatize the minimal system induced by this semantics. The resulting logic, which we call N4CK, shows strong connections both with the basic intuitionistic logic of conditionals IntCK introduced earlier in (Olkhovikov, 2023) and with the N4-based modal logic FSKd introduced in (Odintsov and Wansing, 2004) as one of the possible counterparts to the classical modal system K. We map these connections by looking into the embeddings which obtain between the aforementioned systems.Keywords: Conditional logic, Strong negation, Paraconsistent logic, Strong completeness, Modal logic, Constructive logic

Introduction

The present paper is devoted to a study of a new system of conditional logic (called N4CK) which conservatively extends the logic N4, the paraconsistent variant of Nelson’s logic of strong negation.1 In our opinion, N4CK can be correctly viewed as the minimal ‘normal’ N4-based system of conditional logic; in this capacity, N4CK is the right counterpart to such systems as the classical conditional logic CK and the intuitionistic conditional logic IntCK, introduced in Chellas (1975) and Olkhovikov (2023), respectively.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the notational preliminaries, including the set of notations for different logical systems to be mentioned in the paper. This is followed by Sect. 3 where we introduce the logic N4, which forms the purely propositional basis of N4CK together with its closest relatives like the intuitionistic logic IL and the classical logic CL. We briefly survey the properties of N4, which serves both to make the paper more self-contained and to prepare the reader for the study of N4CK as many of the properties stated in Sect. 2 for N4 will also be demonstrated for N4CK in the later sections. After that, Sect. 4 introduces N4CK, the main subject of the paper. More precisely, the system is introduced in Sect. 4.1 by means of its intended Kripke semantics, whereas in Sect. 4.2 we axiomatize the logic. Section 5 explores the relation of N4CK to several other logics; it is sub-divided into Sects. 5.1 and 5.2, where in the former we show that (1) N4CK yields CK as soon as its propositional basis is extended from N4 to CL, and (2) that N4CK is embeddable into the positive fragment of IntCK; the embedding in question extends the well-known embedding of N4 into the positive fragment of IL. In Sect. 5.2 we argue that the modal counterpart of N4CK is provided by the N4-based modal logic FSKd introduced in Odintsov and Wansing (2004) and compare the relation between the two logics with the relation that obtains between IntCK and its modal companion IK.

Finally, in Sect. 6, we briefly discuss the results of the previous sections, and, after drawing some conclusions, describe several avenues for continuing the research lines presented in the paper. The paper also has several appendices where the reader can find the more technical parts of our reasoning which we include for the sake of completeness.

Preliminaries

We use this section to fix some notations to be used throughout the paper.

We will use IH as the abbreviation for Induction Hypothesis in the inductive proofs; α:=β means that we define α as β. We will use the usual notations for sets and functions. As for the sets, we will write X⋐Y, iff X⊆Y and X is finite. We will understand the natural numbers as the finite von Neumann ordinals; we denote their set by ω. We will extensively use ordered couples of sets which we will also call bi-sets. The usual set-theoretic relations and operations on bi-sets will be understood componentwise, so that, e.g. (X,Y)⊆(Z,W) means that X⊆Z and Y⊆W and similarly in other cases.

Relations will be understood as sets of ordered tuples where the length of the tuple defines the arity of the relation. Given binary relations R⊆X×Y and S⊆Y×Z, we set R∘S:={(a,c)∣for someb∈Y,(a,b)∈R&(b,c)∈S}, and R-1:={(b,a)∣(a,b)∈R}.

Functions will be understood as relations with special properties; we will write f:X→Y to denote a function f⊆X×Y such that its left projection is all of X. If f:X→Y and Z⊆X then we will denote the image of Z under f by f(Z). In view of our previous convention for relations, for any given two functions f:X→Y and g:Y→Z, we will denote the function x↦g(f(x)) by f∘g, even though, in the existing literature, this function is often denoted by g∘f instead.

In this paper we will compare several conditional and modal logics to one another, therefore, we will need a notation for languages allowing us to easily switch between different variants of such logics. In general, if Ω is a set of propositional letters and f11,…,fnn are connectives, where n,i1,…,in∈ω and i1,…,in indicate the arities, we will denote by L(Ω,f11,…,fnn) the language that is the smallest set of formulas extending Ω and closed under the applications of connectives in {f11,…,fnn}.

However, within this paper we will confine ourselves to a rather narrow range of instantiations of this general construction. First of all, let Var:={pi∣i∈ω} and Var′:={qi∣i∈ω} be disjoint. In this paper, we will not consider any instantiations of Ω outside the set {Var,Var∪Var′}. Furthermore, the main logic that we will consider in this paper, the basic Nelsonian logic of conditionals N4CK, will be seen to conservatively extend the logic N4, the paraconsistent variant of Nelson’s logic of strong negation. Therefore, we will mostly consider languages of the form L(Ω,f11,…,fnn) where {f11,…,fnn}⊇{∧2,∨2,∼1,→2}; our basic language will be L:=L(Var,∧2,∨2,∼1,→2). We will want to extend L with some connectives from the set putting these additional connectives into the subscript and omitting their arities, so that, for example, L(□,◊) will mean the language L(Var,∧2,∨2,∼1,→2,□1,◊1).

We will sometimes want to omit ∼1 from the set of connectives thus getting the positive version of the language, or extend the set of propositional letters from Var to Var∪Var′ thus getting the extended version of the language, or both. The first two operations will be expressed by appending the superscripts + and e, respectively, to the language notation so that, e.g. will mean . Although one and the same logic can often be formulated over different languages, in this paper we will abstract away from such subtleties, and will simply treat a logic as a set L⊆P(Lang)×P(Lang), for some language Lang, where (Γ,Δ)∈L iff Δ L-follows from Γ (we will also denote this by Γ⊧LΔ). We will say that (Γ,Δ) is L-satisfiable iff (Γ,Δ)∉L. Given a ϕ∈Lang, ϕ is L-valid (we will also write ϕ∈L) iff (∅,{ϕ})∈L and ϕ is L-satisfiable iff ({ϕ},∅)∉L.

Since every language considered in this paper contains ∨, we can define the Disjunction Property (DP) for an arbitrary logic already at this introductory stage. We will say that a logic L⊆P(Lang)×P(Lang) has DP iff for all ϕ,ψ∈Lang, we have ϕ∨ψ∈L iff at least one of ϕ,ψ is in L. In case Lang also includes ∼, we can also speak about the Constructible Falsity Property (CFP). A logic L⊆P(Lang)×P(Lang) has CFP iff for all ϕ,ψ∈Lang, we have ∼(ϕ∧ψ)∈L iff at least one of ∼ϕ,∼ψ is in L.

All of the logics that we will consider in this paper will have a common property, namely, that their sets of consequences can be seen as induced by (1) their intended Kripke semantics, and, on the other hand, by (2) their complete Hilbert-style axiomatizations. The first of these circumstances allows us to alternatively conceptualize logics as the values returned by the logic operator L applied to a tuple of the form (Lang,Mod,▹), where Lang is a language, Mod is a class of Kripke models such that every μ∈Mod has an underlying set denoted by |μ|, and ▹⊆PMod×Lang (where we set PMod:={(μ,w)∣μ∈Mod,w∈|μ|}) is a satisfaction relation between the pointed models based on Mod and the formulas of the language. We extend this relation to the bi-sets of formulas by setting μ,w⊳(Γ,Δ) iff both (∀ϕ∈Γ)(μ,w▹ϕ) and (∀ψ∈Δ)(μ,w⋫ϕ).

Next, we define the value L(Lang,Mod,▹) of the logic operator to be the subset of P(Lang)×P(Lang) such that (Γ,Δ)∈L(Lang,Mod,▹) iff for no (μ,w)∈PMod it is true that μ,w⊳(Γ,Δ). Modal Logics

Conditional logics often have modal companions that are faithfully embeddable into them by a simple and natural translation. Since CK and IntCK can be viewed as basic classical and intuitionistic conditional logic, respectively, their natural companions are provided by the minimal normal modal logic K and the basic intuitionistic modal logic IK introduced by G. Fischer-Servi in Fischer-Servi (1981). We will argue in the present subsection that this series can be continued with N4CK and the modal logic FSKd introduced in Odintsov and Wansing (2004).

Conclusion, Discussion, and Future Work

We have introduced and axiomatized the Nelsonian paraconsistent conditional logic N4CK, and we have looked into its relations to other conditional logics like CK and IntCK as well as its relations to the N4-based modal logic FSKd. In doing so, we mainly focused on different faithful embeddings arising between the logics in question; when constructing these embeddings, we paid special attention to reproducing some of the characteristic properties of N4 which is the reduct of N4CK to the purely propositional language. Another major source of analogies was provided by IntCK: we used the relation between IntCK and IK in our argument that FSKd is the correct modal companion for N4CK and we have also constructed a form of joint (although non-commuting) embedding of the pair {FSKd,N4CK} into the positive fragment of the pair {IK,IntCK}.

However, one element is saliently missing in this web of faithful embeddings, and it is the embedding of N4CK into QN4, the first-order variant of N4. Indeed, both IK and IntCK have been shown to be embeddable into QInt, the first-order intuitionistic logic, and the embedding of FSKd into QN4 was shown to be faithful in Odintsov and Wansing (2004) by a remarkably simple argument which basically fed the negation normal forms arising in FSKd into the composition of Em and the embedding of IK into QInt.

Whereas this method can also be used in the case of N4CK, it only gives one of the possible faithful embeddings of N4CK into QN4; yet, other faithful embeddings of this sort are also possible, and one may argue that the embedding arising in this way is neither the most natural nor the most interesting one. The whole question of mapping possible embeddings of N4CK into QN4, therefore, deserves a separate treatment, and we hope to be able to address it in one of our publications in the nearest future.

On the other hand, it is also interesting to look at other conditional logics based on N4, not just at N4CK. We have already said a thing or two about N4CK′ in this paper, but other promising extensions of N4CK are clearly possible. We find it especially interesting to look into the extensions of N4CK which realize different principles of connexive logics (see, e.g. Omori and Wansing (2019) for an overview) and to see how far can one push the limits of the Nelsonian condional operator in this direction; we hope to do just this in another of our future papers.

Yet another direction for the future research is to look into reproducing the results obtained both in Olkhovikov (2023) and in this paper for other constructive conditional logics, especially the ones based on propositional logics like the original Nelson’s logic N3 and the negation-inconsistent connexive logic C, introduced by H. Wansing in Wansing (2005).

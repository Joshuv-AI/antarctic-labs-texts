Abstract
In this article, we mainly give the strictly copositive conditions of a special class of third order three dimensional symmetric tensors. More specifically, by means of the polynomial decomposition method, the analytic sufficient and necessary conditions are established for checking the strict copositivity of a 3rd order 3-dimensional symmetric tensor with its entries in { − 1 , 0 , 1 } \{-1,0,1\} . Several strict inequalities of cubic ternary homogeneous polynomials are presented by applying these conclusions. Some criteria which ensure the strict copositivity of a general 3rd order 3-dimensional tensor are obtained Keywords : Strict copositivity; 3rd order Tensor; Ternary cubic; Symmetric. In this paper, we use capital letters
A,B,M,
…, for matrices and calligraphic letters
\mathcal{A}

\mathcal{B}

\mathcal{T}
, …, for tensors. We denote the set of all
m

n

T_{m,n}
, where
m
and
n
are positive integers,
m,n\geq 2
, unless otherwise stated. For any tensor
\mathcal{A}=(a_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}})\in T_{m,n}
, if its entries
a_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}
are invariant under any permutation of its indices, then
\mathcal{A}
is called a
symmetric tensor
. The set of all
m
th order
n
-dimensional real symmetric tensors is denoted as
S_{m,n}

We write
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
for the non-negative octant in Euclidean space, i.e.,

\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}=\{\textbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})^{\top};x_{1}\geq 0,x_{2}\geq 0,\cdots,x_{n}\geq 0\}.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(a_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}})\in T_{m,n}
. Then
\mathcal{A}
is said to be

•

copositive
if for all
\textbf{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
,

\mathcal{A}\textbf{x}^{m}=\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{m}=1}^{n}a_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{m}}\geq 0;

•

strictly copositive
if for all
\textbf{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
,
\textbf{x}\not=0
,

\mathcal{A}\textbf{x}^{m}>0.

Theorem 2.2
.

(
[
32
,
30
]
)
Suppose that
\mathcal{A}=(a_{ijk})\in S_{3,2}
. Then
\mathcal{A}
is (strictly) copositive if and only if
a_{111}\geq 0\ (>0)
,
a_{222}\geq 0\ (>0)
and

\begin{cases}a_{112}\geq 0,a_{122}\geq 0;\\
4a_{111}a_{122}^{3}+4a_{112}^{3}a_{222}+a_{111}^{2}a_{222}^{2}-6a_{111}a_{112}a_{122}a_{222}-3a_{112}^{2}a_{122}^{2}\geq 0\ (>0).\end{cases}

For a tensor
\mathcal{A}=(a_{ijk})\in S_{3,2}
, the following conclusion is easy to show by Theorem
2.2
. For
\textbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})^{\top},
we have

\mathcal{A}\textbf{x}^{3}=a_{111}x_{1}^{3}+3a_{112}x_{1}^{2}x_{2}+3a_{122}x_{1}x_{2}^{2}+a_{222}x_{2}^{3}.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(a_{ijk})\in S_{3,2}
with its entires

a_{ijk}\in\{1,0,-1\}.

Then
\mathcal{A}
is strictly copositive if and only if

a_{111}=a_{222}=1\ \mbox{ and }\ a_{112}+a_{122}\geq 0.

Proof.
Necessity (only if). Suppose
\mathcal{A}
is strictly copositive. Then for
\textbf{x }=(1,0)^{\top}
or
(0,1)^{\top}
, we have

\mathcal{A}\textbf{x}^{3}=a_{111}>0
or
a_{222}>0
, and hence,

a_{111}=a_{222}=1.

For
\textbf{x }=(1,1)^{\top}
, we have

\mathcal{A}\textbf{x}^{3}=x_{1}^{3}+3a_{112}x_{1}^{2}x_{2}+3a_{122}x_{1}x_{2}^{2}+x_{2}^{3}=3(a_{112}+a_{122})+2>0,

and hence,

a_{112}+a_{122}>-\dfrac{2}{3}.

This implies that

a_{112}+a_{122}\geq 0
since
a_{ijk}\in\{1,0,-1\}
.

Sufficiency (if). It is obvious that the condition that
a_{112}+a_{122}\geq 0
is equivalent to

\begin{cases}a_{112}\in\{1,0\}\mbox{ and }a_{122}\in\{1,0\};\\
a_{112}a_{122}=-1.\end{cases}

So, the proof may be divided majorly into
2
categories.

(1) Assume
a_{112}\in\{1,0\}\mbox{ and }a_{122}\in\{1,0\}
. Then
a_{112}\geq 0,a_{122}\geq 0
, and so,

\mathcal{A}
is strictly copositive by Theorem
2.2
.

(2) Assuming that
a_{112}a_{122}=-1
. Then we have

4a_{122}^{3}+4a_{112}^{3}+1-6a_{112}a_{122}-3a_{112}^{2}a_{122}^{2}=4-4+1+6-3=4>0,

and hence, according to Theorem
2.2
,
\mathcal{A}
is strictly copositive. ∎

3
Strict Copositivity of Third Order Three-Dimensional Symmetric Tensors

Let
\mathcal{A}=(a_{ijk})\in S_{3,3}
. Then for
\textbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})^{\top}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\textbf{x}^{3}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{3}a_{ijk}x_{i}x_{j}x_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{111}x_{1}^{3}+a_{222}x_{2}^{3}+a_{333}x_{3}^{3}+3a_{112}x_{1}^{2}x_{2}+3a_{122}x_{1}x_{2}^{2}+3a_{113}x_{1}^{2}x_{3}

\displaystyle+3a_{133}x_{1}x_{3}^{2}+3a_{223}x_{2}^{2}x_{3}+3a_{233}x_{2}x_{3}^{2}+6a_{123}x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(a_{ijk})\in S_{3,3}
with its entries
a_{ijk}\in\{-1,0,1\}
.
Suppose that there exist
r,s,t\in\{1,2,3\}
with
r\neq s
,
s\neq t
,
r\neq t
such that

a_{rss}=a_{rtt}=-a_{123}=1.

Then
\mathcal{A}
is strictly copositive if and only if

a_{iii}=1,a_{iij}+a_{ijj}\geq 0,\mbox{ for all }i,j\in\{1,2,3\}\mbox{ with }i\neq j,

and

\begin{cases}a_{rrs}+a_{rrt}\geq 0;\\
a_{stt}=a_{sst}=1,\begin{cases}a_{rrs}=0,a_{rrt}=-1;\\
a_{rrs}=-1,a_{rrt}=0.\end{cases}\end{cases}

Proof.
Without loss the generality, we might take
r=1,s=2,t=3
, and hence,

a_{122}=a_{133}=1,a_{123}=-1.

Necessity (only if). Since each principal subtensor of
\mathcal{A}
is strictly copositive, it follows from Lemma
2.3
that the first condition, “
a_{iii}=1
and
a_{iij}+a_{ijj}\geq 0,\mbox{ for all }i,j\in\{1,2,3\}\mbox{ with }i\neq j
” is necessary. Next assume
\mathcal{A}
is strictly copositive, but the remaining condition doesn’t hold. Which is divided into
5
cases.

Case 1.

a_{112}=a_{113}=-1
. For
\textbf{x}=(2,1,1)^{\top}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\textbf{x}^{3}=

\displaystyle x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{3}-3x_{1}^{2}x_{2}+3x_{1}x_{2}^{2}-3x_{1}^{2}x_{3}+3x_{1}x_{3}^{2}

\displaystyle+3a_{223}x_{2}^{2}x_{3}+3a_{233}x_{2}x_{3}^{2}-6x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8+1+1-12+6-12+6+3(a_{223}+a_{233})-12

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3(a_{223}+a_{233})-14<0;

Case 2.

a_{112}=0
,
a_{113}=-1
and there is at least one
0
in
\{a_{233},a_{223}\}
. Then
\max\{a_{233},a_{223}\}\leq 1
since
a_{233}+a_{223}\geq 0
. For
\textbf{x}=(3,1,1.5)^{\top}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\textbf{x}^{3}=

\displaystyle x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{3}+3x_{1}x_{2}^{2}-3x_{1}^{2}x_{3}+3x_{1}x_{3}^{2}

\displaystyle+3a_{223}x_{2}^{2}x_{3}+3a_{233}x_{2}x_{3}^{2}-6x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 27+1+1.5^{3}+9-27\times 1.5+9\times 1.5^{2}+3\times 1.5^{2}-18\times 1.5

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-0.125<0;

Case 3.

a_{112}=-1
,
a_{113}=0
and there is at least one
0
in
\{a_{233},a_{223}\}
. Then
\max\{a_{233},a_{223}\}\leq 1
since
a_{233}+a_{223}\geq 0
. For
\textbf{x}=(3,1.5,1)^{\top}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\textbf{x}^{3}=

\displaystyle x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{3}+3x_{1}x_{2}^{2}-3x_{1}^{2}x_{2}+3x_{1}x_{3}^{2}

\displaystyle+3a_{223}x_{2}^{2}x_{3}+3a_{233}x_{2}x_{3}^{2}-6x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 27+1.5^{3}+1+9\times 1.5^{2}-27\times 1.5+9+3\times 1.5^{2}-18\times 1.5

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-0.125<0;

Case 4.

a_{112}=-1
,
a_{113}=0
and
a_{233}a_{223}=-1
. Then
a_{233}+a_{223}=0
. For
\textbf{x}=(2,1,1)^{\top}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\textbf{x}^{3}=

\displaystyle x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{3}+3x_{1}x_{2}^{2}-3x_{1}^{2}x_{2}+3x_{1}x_{3}^{2}

\displaystyle+3a_{223}x_{2}^{2}x_{3}+3a_{233}x_{2}x_{3}^{2}-6x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 8+1+1+6-12+6+3(a_{233}+a_{223})-12=-2<0;

Case 5.

a_{112}=0
,
a_{113}=-1
and
a_{233}a_{223}=-1
. Similarly, for
\textbf{x}=(2,1,1)^{\top}
, we also have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\textbf{x}^{3}=

\displaystyle x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{3}+3x_{1}x_{2}^{2}-3x_{1}^{2}x_{3}+3x_{1}x_{3}^{2}

\displaystyle+3a_{223}x_{2}^{2}x_{3}+3a_{233}x_{2}x_{3}^{2}-6x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 8+1+1+6-12+6+3(a_{233}+a_{223})-12=-2<0.

So, anyway, they contradict with the strict copositivity of
\mathcal{A}
.
Hence, the remaining conditions are necessary.

Sufficiency (If). (1) Assume
a_{112}+a_{113}\geq 0.
For
\textbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3}
,
\mathcal{A}\textbf{x}^{3}
may be rewritten as follows,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\textbf{x}^{3}=

\displaystyle a_{111}x_{1}^{3}+a_{222}x_{2}^{3}+a_{333}x_{3}^{3}+3a_{112}x_{1}^{2}x_{2}+3a_{122}x_{1}x_{2}^{2}+3a_{113}x_{1}^{2}x_{3}

\displaystyle+3a_{133}x_{1}x_{3}^{2}+3a_{223}x_{2}^{2}x_{3}+3a_{233}x_{2}x_{3}^{2}+6a_{123}x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}(a_{111}x_{1}^{2}+3a_{112}x_{1}x_{2}+3a_{122}x_{2}^{2}+3a_{113}x_{1}x_{3}+3a_{133}x_{3}^{2}+6a_{123}x_{2}x_{3})

\displaystyle+a_{222}x_{2}^{3}+3a_{223}x_{2}^{2}x_{3}+3a_{233}x_{2}x_{3}^{2}+a_{333}x_{3}^{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}(\textbf{x}^{\top}M\textbf{x})+\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\hat{\textbf{x}}^{3},

where
\hat{\textbf{x}}=(x_{2},x_{3})^{\top}
,
M
is a matrix,

M=\begin{pmatrix}a_{111}&\frac{3}{2}a_{112}&\frac{3}{2}a_{113}\\
\frac{3}{2}a_{112}&3a_{122}&3a_{123}\\
\frac{3}{2}a_{113}&3a_{123}&3a_{133}\end{pmatrix},

(3.1)

and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=(a^{\prime}_{ijk})\in S_{3,2}
with its entries

a^{\prime}_{111}=a_{222},a_{112}^{\prime}=a_{223},a_{122}^{\prime}=a_{233},a_{222}^{\prime}=a_{333}.

(3.2)

Obviously, a tensor
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is strictly copositive by Lemma
2.3
since
a_{223}+a_{233}\geq 0
. That is,

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\hat{\textbf{x}}^{3}>0,\mbox{ for all }\hat{\textbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\setminus\{0\}.

Next, we show the matrix

. In fact, as

a_{112}+a_{113}\geq 0

\displaystyle\alpha=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}a_{112}+\sqrt{3a_{111}a_{122}}=\frac{3}{2}a_{112}+\sqrt{3}>0,

\displaystyle\beta=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}a_{113}+\sqrt{3a_{111}a_{133}}=\frac{3}{2}a_{113}+\sqrt{3}>0,

\displaystyle\gamma=

\displaystyle 3a_{123}+3\sqrt{a_{122}a_{133}}=0,

\displaystyle\delta=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}a_{112}\sqrt{3a_{133}}+\frac{3}{2}a_{113}\sqrt{3a_{122}}+3a_{123}\sqrt{a_{111}}+3\sqrt{a_{111}a_{122}a_{133}}+\sqrt{2\alpha\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}(a_{112}+a_{113})\geq 0.

Therefore, it follows from Lemma

M

\textbf{x}^{\top}M\textbf{x}\geq 0,\mbox{ for all }\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}.

\mathcal{A}{\textbf{x}}^{3}=a_{111}x_{1}^{3}>0
;
when
x_{2}
and
x_{3}
are not simultaneously equal to
0

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\hat{\textbf{x}}^{3}>0
, and hence

\mathcal{A}\textbf{x}^{3}=x_{1}(\textbf{x}^{\top}M\textbf{x})+\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\hat{\textbf{x}}^{3}>0.

Thus,
\mathcal{A}
is strictly copositive.

a_{233}=a_{223}=1
and
\begin{cases}a_{112}=0,a_{113}=-1;\\
a_{112}=-1,a_{113}=0.\end{cases}

.
a_{233}=a_{223}=1
and
a_{112}=0,a_{113}=-1

may be rewritten as follows,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\textbf{x}^{3}=

\displaystyle x_{1}^{3}+a_{222}x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{3}+3x_{1}x_{2}^{2}-3x_{1}^{2}x_{3}+3x_{1}x_{3}^{2}+3x_{2}^{2}x_{3}+3x_{2}x_{3}^{2}-6x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}

Case 2

a_{233}=a_{223}=1
and
a_{112}=-1,a_{113}=0
. The conclusion is easy to obtain using the same proof technique as Case 1, we omit it.

All in all,
\mathcal{A}
is strictly copositive, which completes the proof. ∎

\displaystyle\alpha=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}a_{112}+\sqrt{3a_{111}a_{122}}=\frac{3}{2}a_{112}+\sqrt{3}>0,

\displaystyle\beta=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}a_{113}+\sqrt{3a_{111}a_{133}}=\frac{3}{2}a_{113}+\sqrt{3}>0,

\displaystyle\gamma=

\displaystyle 3a_{123}+3\sqrt{a_{122}a_{133}}=3>0,

\displaystyle\delta=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}a_{112}\sqrt{3a_{133}}+\frac{3}{2}a_{113}\sqrt{3a_{122}}+3a_{123}\sqrt{a_{111}}+3\sqrt{a_{111}a_{122}a_{133}}+\sqrt{2\alpha\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}(a_{112}+a_{113})+3+\sqrt{6\alpha\beta}\geq 3-\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}+\sqrt{6\alpha\beta}>0.

Therefore, it follows from Lemma
2.4
that the matrix
M
is strictly copositive, and hence,

\mathcal{A}
is strictly copositive.
∎

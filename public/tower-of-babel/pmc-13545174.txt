LncRNA DLGAP1-AS2 promotes ESCC progression and indicates unfavorable prognosis via the miR-101-3p/EZH2 axis
Authors: Liwei Tang, Yichuan Zhang, Xiangting Cheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveLncRNAs are crucial regulators and biomarkers in various cancers. However, their expression and functions in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) remain largely unexplored. This study explored the clinial relevance and mechanisms of LncRNA DLGAP1-AS2 in ESCC.MethodsUtilizing the GEO dataset, the identification of differentially expressed lncRNAs in ESCC encompassed DLGAP1-AS2. RT-qPCR was employed to measure its expression in 135 paired tumors and adjacent non-cancerous tissue samples. Patients were followed up for five years. Kaplan-Meier curves and Cox regression analysis evaluated DLGAP1-AS2’s prognostic impact, while CCK-8 and Transwell assays assessed cell proliferation. Additionally, commercial assay kits measured ROS, MDA, GSH, and iron levels. DLR and RIP assays confirmed miR-101-3p targeting DLGAP1-AS2 and EZH2.ResultsDLGAP1-AS2 was markedly upregulated in ESCC tumor tissues and cell lines. High expression of DLGAP1-AS2 correlated with advanced clinical stages and extensive lymph node metastasis in ESCC patients, and patients with elevated DLGAP1-AS2 expression had a worse prognosis. Mechanistically, DLGAP1-AS2 competitively binds miR-101-3p, upregulating EZH2. Depleting DLGAP1-AS2 significantly curbed ESCC cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and EMT, while boosting ferroptosis—an effect notably reversed by miR-101-3p reduction.ConclusionDLGAP1-AS2 acts as a potential biomarker for unfavorable prognosis in ESCC patients. Mechanistically, it promotes malignant phenotypes of ESCC cells in vitro by regulating the miR-101-3p/EZH2 axis, enhancing proliferation and EMT, while suppressing ferroptosis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s12672-026-05785-5.

Introduction

Esophageal cancer (ESCA) is a common digestive malignancy, sixth in global cancer deaths [1]. Over 440,000 die from it annually [2]. Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is the main pathological type in Asian and African countries, especially China, where it accounts for over 90% of cases [1]. Earlier diagnosis is critical for ESCC patients, as it enables timely surgical, chemotherapeutic, and radiotherapeutic interventions; however, most patients are diagnosed at advanced stages, precluding curative surgery and leading to a poor prognosis [2]. ESCC usually progresses fast, with a mere 15–25% five-year survival rate [3]. Currently, no widely accepted screening proteining program exists for ESCC; diagnosis often relies on advanced-stage symptoms such as dysphagia and odynophagia, which limits early detection [4]. At the molecular levels, ESCC is characterized by frequent alterations is multiple signalling pathways as well as dysregulation of non-coding RNAs such as long non-coding RNAs (LncRNAs) and microRNAs (miRNAs) [5]. Identifying ESCC-specific and epigenetic aberrations, particularly those involving competing endogenous RNA networks, may provide novel biomarkers for early detection and therapeutic targets to improve patient outcomes [6].

LncRNA is a type of RNA molecule over 200 nucleotides long, with limited protein-coding ability [7, 8]. It can regulate disease progression via multiple molecular mechanisms. Recently, several lncRNAs linked to ESCC have been identified, such as HOTAIR [9], LINC02820 [10], LINC02154 [11], and DANCR [12]. DLGAP1 antisense RNA 2 (DLGAP1-AS2, alias DLPAP1-AS1, and MGC11082) is a newly discovered oncogene, mainly situated on human chromosome 18p11.31 and composed of three exons, which are linked to various tumor types. Prior research has highlighted its potential involvement in osteosarcoma glycolysis [13], gastric cancer chemotherapy sensitivity [14], and ferroptosis in clear cell renal cell carcinoma [15]. In the study’s preliminary stage, the GEO database was employed to identify differentially expressed lncRNAs (DElncRNAs) in ESCC. Among them, DLGAP1-AS2 was identified as an overlapping upregulated DElncRNA (Fig. 1A), with fold changes of 7.35 and 3.08 in GSE130078 and GSE157804 datasets, respectively, indicating substantial upregulation in ESCC tissues versus normal tissues. UALCAN, Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis (GEPIA) 2.0, and StarBase (starBase v3.0) databases consistently show that DLGAP1-AS2 is markedly overexpressed in ESCC tumor tissues. Yet, its functional role and clinical relevance in ESCC are still unclear.

LncRNAs can function as competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) by acting as molecular sponges for miRNAs, thereby relieving the miRNA-mediated suppression of their target mRNAs. As one of the miRNAs, miR-101-3p has been reported to function as a tumor suppressor in ovarian cancer [16], gastric cancer [17], and cervical cancer [18]. Wang et al. reported that miR-101-3p is downregulated in ESCC tissues and cells [19]. EZH2, the catalytic subunit of polycomb repressive complex 2. Overexpression of EZH2 is frequently observed in ESCC and correlates with poor prognosis and ferroptosis [20]. Bioinformatics analysis revealed putative binding sites between miR-101-3p and both DLGAP1-AS2 and EZH2. However, whether DLGAP1-AS2 contributes to ESCC progression through modulating miR-101-3p and EZH2 remains largely unexplored.

Given the research context above, we hypothesized that DLGAP1-AS2 is involved in ESCC progression through miR-101-3p and EZH2. We collected ESCC patients’ samples to analyze their expression and clinical value, and used in vitro loss- and gain-of-function experiments to explore their molecular mechanisms. Our work could fill knowledge gaps in DLGAP1-AS2's clinical and mechanistic roles in ESCC.

Materials and methods

Datasets, differential expression analysis and downstream bioinformatics prediction

From the publicly accessible Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database, download datasets GSE130078 and GSE157804. Notably, the lncRNA database GSE130078 contains 23 samples of ESCC tumor tissues and corresponding adjacent normal tissues. The lncRNA dataset GSE157804 includes four matched pairs of ESCC tumor tissues and adjacent normal tissues. Differential lncRNA expression between cancerous and normal tissues was analyzed using the “Limma” package. Differentially expressed lncRNAs (DElncRNA) were identified based on the criteria of |log2change|>1, and adjusted p-value < 0.05, with p-value corrected by Bonferroni’s method. Concurrently, based on the expression trends (up- or down-regulation), up-regulated DElncRNAs were identified using the Venn analysis tool on the Bioinformatics platform (https://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/). Volcano plots, generated via GraphPad Prism, were employed to visualize the differential expression analysis results. Differentially expressed miRNAs in ESCA were screened using dbDEMC 2.0 (EXP00423). Next, UALCAN was employed to perform Kaplan-Meier survival analysis to select prognosis-associated miRNAs. The overlapping miRNAs among these three datasets were then determined by Venn diagram analysis.

The LncLocator database and iLoc-LncRNA (2.0) were utilized to predict the subcellular localization of DLGAP1-AS2. The lncRNASNP2 database and lncBook2 database were employed to predict the target miRNAs of DLGAP1-AS2. Additionally, the target mRNAs of miR-101-3p were identified through TargetScan, miRWalk, starBase, TarBase, mir2gene, and miRDB. Overlapping mRNAs were employed to establish a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network using the STRING database (confidence threshold > 0.4), and genes with degree values in the top 10% of the network were designated as hub genes. Overlapping target mRNAs under Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis via StargerBox3. Finally, the expression levels of DLGAP1-AS2, miR-101-3p, and Enhancer of zeste homolog 2 (EZH2) in ESCA were validated using GEPIA 2.0 and UALCAN databases. ESCCdb-based expression analysis of DLGAP-AS2, EZH2, and miR-101-3p in ESCC.

Participants

The study received approval from the Zibo Central Hospital Medical Ethics Committee, and written informed consent was secured from all patients before surgery. All samples were processed anonymously, adhering to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Tumor and adjacent normal epithelial tissues (> 5 cm from the tumor edge) were collected from 135 patients who underwent endoscopic submuscosal dissection at Zibo Central Hospital (June 2019 to July 2020), serving as test and control samples, respectively. Inclusion criteria: (a) age over 18 years; (b) Diagnosis of ESCC independently confirmed by three pathologists; (c) no history of prior surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, esophageal-related disease, or other malignancies; (d) complete clinical documentation. Exclusion criteria: (a) patients who are pregnant or breastfeeding; (b) patients who received anticancer treatment before diagnosis. Tissue samples will be stored in liquid nitrogen upon receipt for subsequent examinations. This study aimed to assess the association between DLGAP1-AS2 and prognostic survival in patients with ESCC.

All patients were followed up for 5 years through telephone calls or outpatient appointments. The endpoint event was set as death related to ESCC. Patients were divided into high- and low-expression groups based on the mean DLGAP1-AS2 expression level according to a previous study [12, 21]. To validate this stratification, we additionally performed Kaplan-Meier analysis using the median expression level as an alternative cutoff. The Kaplan-Meier method and log-rank test were utilized to assess the association between DLGAP1-AS2 and prognosis in patients with ESCC. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed to identify independent prognostic factors, with variables showing P < 0.1 in univariate analysis entered into the multivariate model. The hazard ratios (HRs) from Cox proportional hazards analysis were applied to pinpoint potential risk factors affecting patient prognosis.

Cell culture

The human ESCC cell lines TE-1, Eca-109, KYSE-150, KYSE-180, and KYSE-510, along with the esophageal epithelial cell line Het-1 A, were procured from the National Collection of Authenticated Cell Cultures of China. All cell lines were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium containing 10 fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin, and maintained at 37℃ in a 5% CO2 incubator. Transfection experiments were conducted when cells reached 70–80% confluence.

RNA interference and cell transfection

Three small interfering RNAs specifically targeting DLGAP1-AS2 (si-DLGAP1-AS2#1, si-DLGAP1-AS2#2, si-DLGAP1-AS2#3) and their negative control (si-NC) were acquired from GenePharma. The EZH2f overexpression plasmid (oe-EZH2) was constructed using the pcDNA3.1 vector, and the empty vector (oe-NC) was also obtained from GenePharma. The miR-101-3p inhibitor, miR-101-3p mimic, and their corresponding negative control (NC inhibitor and NC mimic) were obtained from RiboBio. Before transfection, 50 nM of interfering RNA or oligonucleotide was combined with 10 µL of Opti-MEMTM. Subsequently, Lipofectamine 3000 transfection reagent was added, and the mixture was allowed to stand at room temperature for 10 min before being added to the cells. The culture medium was changed after 6 h.

Reverse transcription quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)

RNA was extracted from tissues and cells using either the RNeasy Mini kit (Qiagen) or the mirVana™ miRNA isolation kit. RNA concentration and purity were determined with a UV-1800 spectrophotometer, based on the absorbance ratio of A260/A280 (1.8–2.2) and A260/230 (3.0–4.0). Subsequently, 1 µg of RNA was reverse transcribed to complementary cDNA using either the PrimeScript™ RT Reagent Kit or the TaqMan miRNA reverse transcription kit. Using cDNA as the template, RT-qPCR amplification was conducted with primers, ddH2O, and either the SYBR Premix Ex Taq II kit or Bulge-Loop™ miRNA qRT-PCR Starter kit on an IQ5 thermal cycler. GAPDH was used as the internal reference gene for quantifying DLGAP1-AS2, EZH2, EMT-related markers (E-cadherin, N-cadherin, Vimentin), and ferroptosis indicators (SLC7A11, GPX4, and PTGS2), whereas U6 served as the internal reference for miR-101-3p. Relative expression levels were determined using the 2−ΔΔCt method. The primer sequences are shown in Supplementary Table S1.

Cell viability assay

The CCK-8 assay was utilized to evaluate cell viability. Briefly, treated cells were resuspended in RPMI-1640 medium at a density of 3 × 103 cells/100 µL and then seeded into a 96-well plate. At designated time points (6, 24, 48, 72, 96 h), the original medium was replaced with a mixture of 10 µL CCK-8 reagent and 90 µL RPMI-1640 medium, followed by a 2-h incubation. Absorbance at 450 nm was then measured using a Bio-Rad microplate reader.

Transwell migration and invasion assays

For migration assays, 5 × 104 cells were resuspended in 200 µL of serum-free medium and then seeded into the upper chamber of a 24-well Transwell insert (8 μm pore size). Subsequently, 500 µL of medium containing 10% FBS was added to the lower chamber. After 24 h of incubation, the cells were rinsed with PBS, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, stained with 0.1% crystal violet, and counted in five randomly selected fields per well under a light microscope. For the invasion assay, the protocol mirrors that of the migration assay, except that Matrigel (200 µg/mL, Cat No.356234, Corning) is pre-coated onto the upper chamber of the Transwell insert before the experiment. The upper chamber is then filled with serum-free medium and incubated for 30 min.

Ferroptosis index assay

The levels of glutathione (GSH), malondialdehyde (MDA), and ferrous ions (Fe2+) were validated using Elabscience’s respective assay kit (Cat No. E-BC-K030-M, E-BC-K028-M, and E-BC-K881-M) according to the protocols provided. Lysates were mixed with kit reagents, and absorbance was measured at 405 nm, 532 nm, and 593 nm using a microplate reader. The reactive oxygen species (ROS) fluorometric assay kit (Cat No. E-BC-K138-F, Elabscience) was employed to assess intracellular ROS levels. Briefly, after trypsinizing the cells and resuspending them in PBS containing H2DCFDA was introduced, followed by incubation for 30 min. Subsequently, the fluorescence intensity was measured with a fluorometer at excitation and emission wavelengths of 488 nm and 530 nm, respectively.

Subcellular fractionation assay

The cellular localization of DLGAP1-AS2 was assessed using subcellular fractionation, with cytoplasmic and nuclear RNA isolated via the PARIS™ kit. Briefly, cells were resuspended in 300 µL of cell dissociation buffer and incubated on ice for 5 min. After centrifugation, the supernatant was removed, leaving the nuclear pellet in the EP tube. Subsequently, 400 µL each of ice-cold cell lysis buffer and binding buffer was added. RNA was extracted from both cytoplasmic and nuclear fractions, and the relative distribution of DLGAP1-AS2 was quantified by RT-qPCR.

Dual luciferase reporter assay

DLGAP1-AS2 and EZH2 sequences containing intact miR-101-3p binding sites were subcloned into the psiCHECK-2 vector, generating wild-type (WT)-DLGAP1-AS2 and WT-EZH2 luciferase reporter plasmids. Corresponding mutant constructs Mut-DLGAP1-AS2 and Mut-EZH2 were subsequently generated by introducing point mutations at the miR-101-3p binding regions through site-directed mutagenesis. Cells were seeded into 48-well plates and co-transfected with the recombinant luciferase reporter plasmid, miR-101-3p inhibitor, miR-101-3p mimic, and negative control NC inhibitor or NC mimic using Lipofectamine 3000. After 48 h, cell was lysed, and luciferase activity was measured using a dual-luciferase assay system. Results were normalized to Renilla luciferase activity.

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay

To investigate the interaction, RIP assays were performed using the Magna RNA-binding protein immunoprecipitation kit. Magnetic beads conjugated to anti-Argonaut RISC catalytic component 2 (Ago2) antibody or anti-IgG antibodies were incubated with cell lysates overnight. After incubation, the beads were removed, and RNA was eluted for subsequent analysis by RT-qPCR.

Statistical analysis and visualization

Statistical analysis and data visualization were conducted with SPSS 23.0 and GraphPad Prism 9.0. All study data, derived from a minimum of three independent replicates, were expressed as mean±standard deviation. Statistical differences between the two groups were determined by the T-test. For comparisons among multiple groups, one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post-hoc test was applied for single-factor experiments, and two-way ANOVA analysis followed by Bonferroni post hoc test was used for two-factor experiments. Pearson correlation analysis was applied to assess gene expression correlation. Survival curves were analyzed via the Kaplan-Meier method and log-rank test for prognostic assessment. Statistical significance was confirmed at P < 0.05.

Results

DLGAP1-AS2 exhibits significantly higher expression levels in ESCC

To pinpoint ESCC-specific lncRNAs, two GSE datasets (GSE130078 and GSE157804) were retrieved from GEO for bioinformatics analysis. A Venn diagram identified nine overlapping upregulated lncRNAs, including DLGAP1-AS2 (Fig. 1A). Volcano plots show that DLGAP1-AS2 was elevated by 7.35-fold and 3.08-fold in ESCC within the GSE dataset (Fig. 1B, C). To further assess DLGAP1-AS2 expression in ESCC, we compared its levels between ESCA and normal tissues using TCGA data from three databases: Starbase, UALCAN, and GEPIA. All analyses consistently showed significant upregulation of DLGAP1-AS2 in ESCA (Fig. 1D-F). This ESCC-specific upregulation was further validated using the ESCCbd platform (13 datasets, all showing upregulation, with 7 reaching statistical significance; Supplementary Figure S1A). Moreover, RT-qPCR analysis of 135 paired ESCC tumor and adjacent non-cancerous tissues showed DLGAP1-AS2 was significantly upregulated in the tumor (P < 0.0001, Fig. 1G), indicating its oncogenic role as an lncRNA in ESCC.

DLGAP1-AS2 emerges as a potential new biomarker for ESCC

To delve deeper into DLGAP1-AS2’s clinical significance in ESCC, patients were stratified into high-expression (n = 73) and low-expression (n = 62) groups according to the average DLGAP1-AS2 expression (2.43 ± 0.70). Further exploring the link between DLGAP1-AS2 and patient clinicopathology showed that those with high DLGAP1-AS2 expression had poor tumor differentiation, more lymph nodes, metastasis, and were more likely to be at TNM stage III-IV (P < 0.05, Fig. 2A, B; Table 1). During the five-year follow-up, 34 patients died, resulting in a 25.19% mortality rate. Those with a poor prognosis had elevated DLGAP1-AS2 levels (P < 0.001, Fig. 2C). Kaplan-Meier analysis based on the mean DLGAP1-AS2 expression level showed that patients with high expression had a significantly worse prognosis (log-rank P = 0.026, Fig. 2D). This trend was further confirmed using the median expression levels as an alternative cutoff (log-rank P = 0.023, Supplementary Figure S2).

Depletion of DLGAP1-AS2 inhibits the proliferation and epithelial-mesenchymal transition of ESCC cells and induces ferroptosis

Given the notable upregulation of DLGAP1-AS2 in ESCC tissues, we further conducted quantitative expression analysis in ESCC cell lines. Compared with the esophageal epithelial cell line Het-1A, DLGAP1-AS2 showed significantly higher expression in ESCC cell lines TE-1, Eca-109, KYSE-150, KYSE-180, and KYSE-510 (P < 0.001, Fig. 3A), especially in Eca-109 and KYSE-510. Three independent siRNAs targeting DLGAP1-AS2 (si-DLGAP1-AS2#1, #2, #3) and a negative control siRNA (si-NC) were designed and transfected into Eca-109 and KYSE-510 cell lines. Among these, si-DLGAP1-AS2#3 exhibited the greatest knockdown efficiency and was therefore selected for subsequent experiments (P < 0.001, Fig. 3B). Moreover, knocking down DLGAP1-AS2 notably reduced cell viability in both Eca-109 and KYSE-510 cell lines (P < 0.05, Fig. 3C). Meanwhile, DLGAP1-AS2 inhibition significantly impeded cell migration and invasion in these two cell lines as well (P < 0.05, Fig. 3D-E). Inhibiting DLGAP1-AS2 notably decreased N-cadherin and vimentin, while increasing E-cadherin (P < 0.05, Fig. 3F). Furthermore, DLGAP1-AS2 knockdown notably increased ROS production and amplified ferroptosis-related phenotypes, including elevated MDA generation, higher ferrous ion levels, and reduced GSH content (P < 0.05, Fig. 4A-D). Ferroptosis biomarker analysis showed that DLGAP1-AS2 downregulation decreased GPX4 and SLC7A11 expression but increased PTGS2 expression (P < 0.05, Fig. 4E). These findings suggest that inhibiting DLGAP1-AS2 in vitro suppresses tumor cell malignancy and exerts anti-tumor effects via ferroptosis induction.

DLGAP1-AS2 acts as a sponge for miR-101-3p

LncRNAs in the cytoplasm can act as ceRNAs, modulating target gene expression by binding and sequestering miRNA. LncLocator predicts DLGA1-AS2 is mainly cytoplasmic (Fig. 5A), supported by iLoc-lncRNA (cytoplasmic fraction score: 0.990697, Supplementary Figure S3). Subcellular fractionation assays also demonstrate that DLGAP1-AS2 is primarily localized in the cytoplasm of Eca-109 and KYSE510 cells (Fig. 5B). To explore downstream target miRNAs of DLGAP1-AS2, we predicted targets using LncRNASNP2 and LncBook2, yielding 308 overlapping candidates (Supplementary Figure S4A). Intersection with ESCA differential miRNAs and prognostic miRNAs identified miR-101-3p as the sole common target (Supplementary Figure S4B). miR-101-3p is a well-characterized tumor-suppressive miRNA that inhibits ESCC progression. The binding site of miR-101-3p on DLGAP1-AS2 was shown in Fig. 5C. Moreover, the miR-101-3p inhibitor enhanced luciferase activity in WT-DLGAP1-AS2 in Eca-109 and KYSE-510 cells, but did not affect the Mut-DLGAP1-AS2 construct (Fig. 5D). In contrast, the miR-101-3p mimic decreased luciferase activity of WT-DLGAP1-AS2, without affecting the Mut construct (Supplementary Figure S5). Moreover, both DLGAP1-AS2 and miR-101-3p were enriched in the anti-Ago2 antibody-bound fraction in Eca-109 and KYSE-210 cells (P < 0.05, Fig. 5E). Starbase and UALCAN analysis showed miR-101-3p downregulation in ESCA tumor (P < 0.05, Fig. 5F, G). Consistent with this, ESCCdb analysis of the available datasets also revealed a downward trend of miR-101-3p expression in ESCC (3 datasets, all showing downregulation, with 2 reaching statistical significance; Supplementary Figure S1B). In 135 ESCC patients, tumor miR-101-3p levels were significantly lower (P < 0.05, Fig. 5H) and inversely correlated with DLGAP1-AS2 (r = -0.676, Fig. 5I). Compared to Het-1A epithelial cells, miR-101-3p expression was reduced in ESCC lines TE-1, Eca-109, KYSE-150, KYSE-180 and KYSE-510 (Fig. 5J).

DLGAP1-AS2 promotes malignant phenotypes in ESCC cells through the regulation of miR-101-3p

To determine whether DLGAP1-AS2 exerts its effects via miR-101-3p, we treated cells with a miR-101-3p inhibitor. As depicted in Fig. 6A, DLGAP1-AS2 knockdown elevated miR-101-3p levels, an effect that was markedly reversed by the inhibitor (P < 0.001). Additionally, miR-101-3p partially rescued the inhibitory impact of DLGAP1-AS2 knockdown on the cell viability of both Eca-109 and KYSE-510 cells (P < 0.001, Fig. 6B). Moreover, miR-101-3p significantly reversed the suppression of cell migration and invasion induced by DLGAP1-AS2 silencing (P < 0.001, Fig. 6C, D). At the same time, miR-101-3p downregulation abolished the stimulatory effect of DLGAP1-AS2 silencing on ROS generation and Fe2+ accumulation (P < 0.001, Fig. 6E, F). DLGAP1-AS2 suppression led to decreased SLC7A11 and GPX4 expression while increasing PTGS2 levels; however, these alterations were significantly reversed by the reduced miR-101-3p (P < 0.001, Fig. 6G).

DLGAP1-AS2 positively regulates EZH2 expression via sponging miR-101-3p in ESCC cells

Downstream mRNAs of the DLGAP1-AS2/miR-101-3p axis in ESCC need more research. Databases like miRDB, Targetscan, miRwalk, starbase, Tarbase, mir2gene, and miRDB found 41 overlapping miR-101-3p target genes (Fig. 7A). These genes built a PPI network with 41 nodes and 27 edges and a PPI enrichment P value of 0.000196. Among the hub genes, EZH2 had the highest degree among all hub genes (Fig. 7B) and is known to be functionally relevant in ESCC; thus, it was prioritized for further study. KEGG analysis showed that the overlapping targets were mainly enriched in signaling pathways such as PI3K-AKT, Jak-STAT, Ras, and Notch (Fig. 7C). Meanwhile, Fig. 7D illustrates the binding site of miR-101-3p on EZH2. Inhibiting miR-101-3p markedly elevated the luciferase activity of WT-EZH2 in transfected Eca-109 and KYSE-510 cells (P < 0.001, Fig. 7E). In contrast, the miR-101-3p mimic decreased luciferase activity of WT-EZH2, without affecting the Mut construct (Supplementary Figure S6). Moreover, DLGAP1-AS2, miR-101-3p and EZH2 were all enriched on Ago2 antibodies (P < 0.001, Fig. 7F). Moreover, data from the Starbase and UALCAN databases indicated a notably higher EZH2 expression level in ESCA tumor tissues than in normal tissues (P < 0.001, Fig. 7G, H). Additionally, among the 135 collected ESCC patients’ tumor tissues, EZH2 expression was significantly upregulated and showed a strong negative correlation with miR-101-3p (r = -0.764, P < 0.001, Fig. 7I, J) and positive correlation with DLGAP1-AS2 (r = 0.599, P < 0.001, Supplementary Figure S7). Moreover, downregulating DLGAP1-AS2 notably inhibited EZH2 expression in Eca-109 and KYSE-510 cells. However, this inhibitory effect was substantially reversed by the low-level expression of miR-101-3p (P < 0.001, Fig. 7K).

EZH2 overexpression rescues the tumor-suppressive effects induced by DLGAP1-AS2 silencing in ESCC cells

To determine whether the DLGAP1-AS2/miR-101-3p axis promotes ESCC malignant progression through EZH2, we conducted rescue experiments using oe-EZH2 transfection in Eca-109 and KYSE-510 cells. As shown in Fig. 8A and supplementary Figure S8, oe-EZH2 markedly upregulated EZH2 expression and counteracted the EZH2 reduction induced by DLGAP1-AS2 knockdown. Functionally, EZH2 restoration partially abrogated the suppressive effects of DLGAP1-AS2 silencing on cell viability (Fig. 8B) and invasion (Fig. 8C). Moreover, EZH2 overexpression reversed the DLGAP1-AS2 silencing-mediated promotion of ROS production and Fe2+ accumulation (Fig. 8D, E). Consistently, the DLGAP1-AS2 silencing-induced downregulation of SLC7A11 and GPX4, as well as the upregulation of PTGS2, were all markedly rescued upon EZH2 overexpression (Fig. 8F).

Discussion

ESCC predominantly presents as a highly invasive histological subtype. Although extensive research has been conducted on its molecular mechanisms, clinical outcomes for ESCC patients are still suboptimal. High-throughput sequencing advancements have significantly deepened our understanding of complex tumor cellular and molecular mechanisms, expediting the clinical translation of molecular oncology [22]. Though abnormal lncRNA expression in ESCA is well-studied, DLGAP1-AS2’s role in ESCC is uncharted. Our study shows DLGAP1-AS2 upregulation in ESCC tissues, hinting at a poor prognosis marker. Knockdown studies reveal it suppresses malignancy and induces ferroptosis via miR-101-3p/EZH2, suggesting its oncogenic role and new therapeutic avenues.

Improving the prognosis of ESCC critically hinges on identifying reliable molecular markers and elucidating underlying mechanisms, with lncRNA emerging as a key research focus. Previous studies demonstrate that early diagnosis and prompt treatment can raise the five-year survival rate of ESCC above 90% [23]. ESCC therapy has now transitioned from endoscopic and surgical methods to targeting molecular mechanisms, with lncRNAs identified as key regulators in ESCC progression. Elevated MIR4435-2HG levels are linked to advanced tumor stage and poor short-term survival [24]. HOXA11-AS1 independently predicts prognosis and TNM stage in ESCC [25]. FLG-ASW1 overexpression reduces anaerobic glycolysis, ESCC cells’ migration, and reverses cisplatin resistance [23]. LINC0030 overexpression inhibits ESCC progression by suppressing progression, invasion, and EMT [26]. Prior research has underscored DLGAP1-AS2’s role in predicting chemotherapy response in gastric cancer [14] and its prognostic relevance in bladder cancer [27]. To ensure reliable findings, this study initially analyzed frequently reported oncogenic DElncRNAs in ESCC using two independent databases. After identifying DLGAP1-AS2, we verified it via three commonly used databases and adjacent non-tumor tissues from ESCC patients. Previous research has established the TNM staging system as the benchmark for ESCC prognosis [28]. Our study is the first to show that patients with high DLGAP1-AS2 expression tend to have higher TNM stages. While earlier studies have highlighted prognostic differences among patients within the same clinical stage, our results reveal that those with elevated DLGAP1-AS2 expression are more likely to develop lymph node metastasis and have poorly differentiated tumors, potentially providing additional pathological insights beyond TNM staging. Moreover, we have newly identified that high DLGAP1-AS2 expression is associated with a worse prognosis and can serve as a diagnostic biomarker for this unfavorable outcome. In brief, this study establishes DLGAP1-AS2 as a negative prognostic indicator for ESCC, enhancing the pathological evaluation framework of TNM staging. It offers fresh experimental insights and guidance for ESCC prognostic stratification and further exploration of its molecular underpinnings.

Accumulating evidence suggests that LncRNAs function as ceRNAs, binding to miRNAs and consequently modulating target mRNA expression [29]. Database and cell fractionation assays revealed DLGAP1-AS2’s predominant cytoplasmic localization. Prior studies showed it promotes non-small cell lung cancer via miR-193a-5p [30] and glioma proliferation through miR-515-5p [31]. MiR-101-3p, a member of the miR-101 family, resides on human chromosome 1. Prior research has elucidated its involvement in ovarian [16], hepatocellular [32], cervical [18], and breast cancers [33]. Prior research indicates that miR-101-3p modulates ferroptosis by targeting TBLR1, thus inducing apoptosis, inhibiting proliferation, and halting tumor growth [34]. A few studies have explored miR-101-3p’s role in ESCC. Jiang et al. (2021) downloaded five ESCC-related miRNA expression microarray datasets from the GEO database and found miR-101-3p among the co-downregulated miRNAs [35]. Wang et al. (2024) used tissue microarray chips to identify miR-101-3p as the most significantly downregulated miRNA in ESCC tissues [36]. Moreover, increased miR-101-3p levels promoted apoptosis and hindered ESCC progression [37]. This study found that miR-101-3p is markedly downregulated in ESCC tissues, aligning with data from starBase and UALCAN databases and prior research. Notably, it is the first to show a strong inverse correlation between miR-101-3p and DLGAP1-AS2 expression in ESCC, indicating a direct regulatory relationship. Functional assays revealed that miR-101-3p reduction counteracts DLGAP1-AS2’s inhibition on cell malignancy. These insights offer new theoretical foundations for understanding ESCC’s molecular mechanisms and suggest DLGAP1-AS2/miR-101-3p as a novel therapeutic target in ESCC.

EZH2, a pivotal histone methyltransferase, forms the catalytic core of Polycom repressive complex 2 (PRC2). Aberrant EZH2 expression is prevalent in cancers, correlating with poor progression and treatment resistance. It drives cell proliferation and aids immune evasion by silencing tumor suppressor genes [38]. Notably, clinical trials have explored EZH2 inhibitors, with the FDA approving Tazemetostat in 2020 for epithelioid sarcoma and follicular lymphoma treatment [39]. In recent years, EZH2’s involvement in ESCC has garnered significant attention. For example, Chen et al. (2025) mined the GEP database to pinpoint differentially expressed genes between normal and ESCC tissues, identifying several EZH2-related genes [40]. Similarly, Zhang et al. (2024) discovered six aberrantly expressed ferroptosis-suppressing genes in ESCC, among which EZH2 levels were notably increased [41]. Silencing EZH2 reduced cyclin D1 and β-catenin expression while increasing E-cadherin levels in ESCC [42]. In line with prior research, we found notably elevated EZH2 in ESCC tumor tissues. Previous studies have established miR-101-3p as a target of EZH2 in non-small cell lung cancer [43] and hepatocellular carcinoma [44]. DLGAP1-AS2 knockdown in ESCC cells. Collectively, our findings demonstrate that DLGAP1-AS2 silencing suppresses malignant phenotypes of ESCC cells through the miR-101-3p/EZH2 axis, suggesting this pathway as a potential therapeutic target for ESCC.

Several limitations should be noted. This was a single-center retrospective study with a modest sample size and a limited number of events, which may have affected the robustness of the Cox regression analysis. Mechanistically, our findings were derived from only two ESCC cell lines, and in vitro assays, and in vivo validation is still needed. The ferroptosis-related evidence remains preliminary, as it is mainly based on marker gene expression. Additionally, ESCCdb validation for EZH2 and miR-101-3p was restricted by the limited number of available datasets. Future studies employing xenograft models, ferroptosis pathway dissection, and larger multicenter cohorts will be essential to confirm and extend our findings.

In the present study, we demonstrated that high DLGAP1-AS2 expression is a potential prognostic biomarker for ESCC, driving cell proliferation and migration while suppressing ferroptosis via the miR-101-3p/EZH2 axis. Thus, DLGAP1-AS2 holds promise as a therapeutic and prognostic target in ESCC.

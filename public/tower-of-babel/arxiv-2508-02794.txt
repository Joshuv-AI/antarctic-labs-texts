New high-frequency gravitational waves from first-order phase transitions
Wen-Yuan Ai

Abstract
First-order phase transitions in the early Universe are a well-motivated source of gravitational waves (GWs). In this Letter, we identify a previously overlooked GW production mechanism: gravitational transition radiation , arising from graviton emission by particles whose mass changes as they pass through expanding bubble walls. Unlike conventional sources such as bubble collisions or sound waves, this mechanism operates at the microscopic scale set by the Lorentz-contracted wall thickness, leading to GW emission at significantly higher frequencies. The resulting spectrum features a distinctive shape with a peak frequency redshifting to f peak ∼ T 0 ∼ 10 10 ​ Hz f_{\rm peak}\sim T_{0}\sim 10^{10}\,{\rm Hz} where T 0 T_{0} is the current temperature of the Universe. This mechanism is generic and is expected to operate similarly for domain walls and other relativistic interfaces.

Transition radiation is usually understood as an electromagnetic phenomenon in which a charged particle emits light when crossing an interface that modifies the photon dispersion relation. Radiation can also be produced if the particle itself experiences a sudden change in properties, such as its mass or charge. In cosmology, such conditions naturally arise near phase boundaries, for example, bubble walls or domain walls. The emission is significantly enhanced when the boundary moves relativistically. Analogously, a sudden change in a particle’s mass can induce the emission of gravitons. In this Letter, we focus on bubble walls from a first-order phase transition (FOPT) as a concrete realisation of this phenomenon and study its associated GW background. However, the mechanism is expected to apply more broadly to other relativistic interfaces, such as domain walls.

In this work, we have identified a new source of high-frequency GW production from FOPTs, introducing the GTR mechanism. This mechanism generates a power spectrum with features qualitatively distinct from those of conventional sources. Although detecting such signals—along with other high-frequency GWs—remains challenging in the near future, our work fills an important gap in the understanding of microscopic GW sources and highlights the rich phenomenology associated with relativistic interfaces in the early Universe. In this Letter, we have illustrated the mechanism in its simplest realisation, where a scalar particle emits a graviton through minimal gravitational coupling. Exploring how additional structure—such as non-minimal couplings to gravity—could imprint new spectral features is a natural next step. Another immediate application of our work is to investigate new GW signals sourced by GTR from particles crossing axion domain walls. Further, the GTR mechanism may also be applied to astrophysical processes, e.g., a FOPT inside a neutron star
Casalderrey-Solana et al. 2022
, where the resulting GWs do not need to respect the BBN upper bound. Our results thus open a new direction for theoretical and phenomenological investigation, and meanwhile, provide a target for future experimental efforts.

This function is minimal at
p_{s}^{z}=\gamma_{w}|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|
. Since it appears in the exponent, we can do the Taylor expansion, obtaining

\displaystyle h(p_{s}^{z})\approx-\frac{|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|}{T}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\gamma_{w}^{2}|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|T}(p_{s}^{z}-\gamma_{w}|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|)^{2}\,.

(S49)

When we do the integral over
p_{s}^{z}
, we can simply do the Gaussian integral and let
p_{s}^{z}=\gamma_{w}|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|
(and hence
p^{0}\approx\gamma_{w}|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|
) in all other parts. We finally obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho^{\rm gen}_{\rm GW}(k^{0})}{\mathrm{d}\ln k^{0}}\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2\pi T}}{512\pi^{5}}\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}_{w}}\int\mathrm{d}|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|\,|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|^{1/2}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|}{T}}\int\mathrm{d}|\mathbf{k}_{\perp}|\,|\mathbf{k}_{\perp}|\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\frac{(k^{0})^{2}}{\sqrt{(k^{0})^{2}-|\mathbf{k}_{\perp}|^{2}}}\frac{|\left.\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{p}_{s},\mathbf{k})\right|^{2}_{p_{s}^{z}=\gamma_{w}|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|}}{\sqrt{\left.H(\mathbf{p}_{s},\mathbf{k})\right|_{p_{s}^{z}=\gamma_{w}|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|}}}\,,

(S50)

where
\theta={\rm arccos}\left(\frac{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}\cdot\mathbf{k}_{\perp}}{|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}||\mathbf{k}_{\perp}|}\right)
.

To simplify the integral further, we notice that the integral over
|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|
receives the dominant contribution from small
|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|/T
due to the exponential function
\exp(-|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|/T)
in the integrand. On the other hand, the factor
|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|^{1/2}
and the fact that
|\mathcal{M}|^{2}_{p_{b}^{z}=\gamma_{w}|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|}
is regular at
|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|=0
indicate that the integral receives negligible contribution as
|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|\rightarrow 0
. Combining these two observations, one may effectively replace
|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|
inside the
\mathbf{k}
integral by the following averaged value

\displaystyle\langle|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|\rangle=\frac{\int\mathrm{d}|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|\,|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|^{3/2}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|}{T}}}{\int\mathrm{d}|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|\,|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|^{1/2}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|}{T}}}=\frac{3T}{2}\,.

(S51)

Mathematically, this amounts to doing a cumulant expansion and taking the leading-order result
Ai et al. 2025b
. Under such an average and taking into account energy and transverse momentum conservation, we now have

\displaystyle p_{s}=\left(\frac{3\gamma_{w}T}{2},0,0,\frac{3\gamma_{w}T}{2}\right)\,,

(S52a)

\displaystyle p_{b}=\left(\frac{3\gamma_{w}T}{2},0,0,\sqrt{\frac{9\gamma_{w}^{2}T^{2}}{4}-m_{\Psi,b}^{2}}\right)\,,

(S52b)

\displaystyle k=\left(k^{0},\mathbf{k}_{\perp},\sqrt{(k^{0})^{2}-|\mathbf{k}_{\perp}|^{2}}\right)\,,

(S52c)

\displaystyle q_{s}=\left(\frac{3\gamma_{w}T}{2}-k^{0},-\mathbf{k}_{\perp},\sqrt{\left(\frac{3\gamma_{w}T}{2}-k^{0}\right)^{2}-|\mathbf{k}_{\perp}|^{2}}\right)\,,

(S52d)

\displaystyle q_{b}=\left(\frac{3\gamma_{w}T}{2}-k^{0},-\mathbf{k}_{\perp},\sqrt{\left(\frac{3\gamma_{w}T}{2}-k^{0}\right)^{2}-|\mathbf{k}_{\perp}|^{2}-m^{2}_{\Psi,b}}\right)\,.

(S52e)

Note that, although the averaged
|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|
is nonzero, we have taken the averaged
\mathbf{p}_{\perp}
to be zero due to the isotropy in the plane parallel to the wall. This approximation of taking
\mathbf{p}_{\perp}=0
in computing
\mathcal{M}
has been used in literature, see e.g. Refs.
Bodeker and Moore 2017
;
Azatov and Vanvlasselaer 2021
.

Now
|\mathcal{M}|^{2}(k^{0},|\mathbf{k}_{\perp}|)
becomes a function of
k^{0}
and
|\mathbf{k}_{\perp}|
, and we can do the integrals over
|\mathbf{p}_{\perp}|
and
\theta
in Eq. (
S50
) trivially. We obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho^{\rm gen}_{\rm GW}(k^{0})}{\mathrm{d}\ln k^{0}}\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2}T^{2}}{512\pi^{3}}\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}_{w}}\times\int\mathrm{d}|\mathbf{k}_{\perp}|\,|\mathbf{k}_{\perp}|\frac{(k^{0})^{2}}{\sqrt{(k^{0})^{2}-|\mathbf{k}_{\perp}|^{2}}}\frac{|\left.\mathcal{M}(k^{0},|\mathbf{k}_{\perp}|)\right|^{2}}{\sqrt{G(k^{0},|\mathbf{k}_{\perp}|)}}\,,

(S53)

where

\displaystyle G(k^{0},|\mathbf{k}_{\perp}|)=\left(\frac{3\gamma_{w}T}{2}-k^{0}\right)^{2}-|\mathbf{k}_{\perp}|^{2}-m^{2}_{\Psi,b}\,.

(S54)

The kinematic constraint gives

\displaystyle k^{0}\leq\frac{3\gamma_{w}T}{2}-m_{\Psi,b}\,,

(S55a)

\displaystyle 0\leq|\mathbf{k}_{\perp}|\leq{\rm min}\left\{k^{0},\sqrt{\left(\frac{3\gamma_{w}T}{2}-k^{0}\right)^{2}-m_{\Psi,b}^{2}}\right\}\,.

(S55b)

Example: Gravitational transition radiation from a scalar particle
\chi
.
Taking

\displaystyle x_{1}=\frac{k^{0}}{T}\,,\quad x_{2}=\frac{|\mathbf{k}_{\perp}|}{T}\,,\quad x_{3}=\frac{m_{\chi,b}}{T}\,,

(S56)

we can write
|\mathcal{M}|^{2}
as

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}|^{2}=\kappa^{2}T^{2}f(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\gamma_{w})\,,

(S57)

where
f
is a dimensionless function obtained numerically. The final result can be fitted as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho^{\rm gen}_{\rm GW}(k^{0})}{\mathrm{d}\ln k^{0}}\approx 3.6\times 10^{-4}\times\left(\frac{m_{\chi,b}}{m_{\rm Pl}}\right)^{2}T^{4}\times I_{1}\left(\frac{k^{0}}{\gamma_{w}T};m_{\chi,b},\gamma_{w}\right)\,,

(S58)

where the dependence on
m_{\chi,b}
and
\gamma_{w}
(with
k^{0}/\gamma_{w}T
fixed) in the function
I_{1}
is weak. We show and compare the results in Fig.
S1
for a few values of
\{m_{\chi,b},\gamma_{w}\}
.

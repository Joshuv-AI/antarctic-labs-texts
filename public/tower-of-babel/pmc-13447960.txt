Clinical and genetic landscape of SCID in Yunnan, China: identification of two novel RAG2 variants
Authors: Yanjun Wang, Ruohong Jin, Qian Han, Ling Hang, Ling Lv, Guizhi Chen, Rong Hu, Shufang Xiao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Severe combined immune deficiency (SCID) is a rare inherited defect of lymphocytes causing life-threatening opportunistic infections in early infancy. Data on SCID from China are limited. This study explores the clinical, immunologic, and genetic features of a SCID cohort from Yunnan Province in China and reports novel variants. We collated clinical, laboratory, and molecular details from patients with a clinical profile suggestive of SCID. Trio-based whole-exome sequencing was performed to identify genetic variants. For the 9 previously reported variants identified in our cohort, we systematically reviewed the literature for additional cases carrying the same variants and compared clinical and immunologic features. Eleven infant patients (8 males and 3 females) were included. Molecular diagnoses were obtained in 10 patients, as follows: IL2RG (3), RAG2 (3), LIG4 (2), DCLRE1C (1), and CD3D (1). Nine patients presented with classic SCID features within the first 3 months of life. Eleven variants were identified: 2 novel RAG2 variants (p.L469P and p.Q492R) and 9 variants previously reported in SCID-associated genes. One patient with the p.Q492R variant exhibited a relatively milder clinical course. An extremely rare case of Omenn syndrome due to IL2RG deficiency was also observed. Ten of 11 patients died within 6 months of age. The literature review identified 94 additional cases carrying these same variants; clinical presentations were generally consistent with our patients, although some variability was observed. We characterized the clinical and genetic profiles of 11 SCID patients from Yunnan, China, identifying two novel RAG2 variants. While these findings expand the mutational spectrum in understudied populations, the high mortality and diagnostic delays observed here underscore the critical need for universal newborn screening in China. Introduction

Severe combined immune deficiency (SCID) is a rare type of primary immunodeficiency disease (PID) and a life-threatening genetic disorder. It is characterized by a defect in T lymphocyte development and function that severely impairs the capacity of the immune system to protect the body from infections1. The estimated incidence of SCID is approximately 1 in 75,000–100,000 live births. However, the incidence has been reported to be greater (approximately 1 in 58,000 births) since newborn screening using T cell receptor excision circles (TREC) was implemented in the USA1,2. Patients with SCID often present with recurrent and severe infections in early life or opportunistic infections, chronic diarrhea, and growth retardation. Without early diagnosis and intervention, most children die within 1 year of age. Once diagnosed with SCID, hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) is the optimal treatment. Gene therapy is expected to be an important alternative treatment if a suitable donor is not available. Early diagnosis and treatment are the key to successful outcomes. To aid in early diagnosis, a number of nations, including the United States, Israel, Germany, Switzerland, Sweden, Norway, Iceland, and New Zealand, have started screening newborns for SCID based on TREC quantification3. Large-scale newborn screening for SCID has not been implemented in China and diagnosis is frequently overlooked due to a lack of awareness and diagnostic resources. Therefore, pediatricians should be familiar with the clinical features of SCID and the key features of early identification and correctly grasp the treatment principles for affected children.

SCID is classified as either typical or atypical types. To date, 19 genes have been shown to be related to SCID, including RAG1, RAG2, DCLRE1C, NHEJ1, LIG4, PRKDC, IL2RG, JAK3, IL7RA, ADA, PNP, AK2, RAC2, PTPRC, CD3D, CD3E, CD3Z, LAT, and CORO1A4. A defect in IL2RG (X-linked SCID), which encodes the common gamma chain (γc) of the interleukin-2 (IL-2) receptor, is considered the most common genetic cause of SCID. The autosomal recessive form of SCID due to defects in RAG1 is the second-most common form. A genetic cause of mutations in RAG2, LIG4, CD3D, and DCLRE1C is relatively less frequent. Omenn syndrome (OS) is a specific and rare type of atypical SCID, characterized by generalized erythroderma, hepatosplenomegaly, and lymphadenopathy.

Reports of clinical data and outcomes of SCID from China are scarce. Being a province with diverse ethnic minorities in China, the molecular spectrum of Yunnan Province is expected to be different from other provinces. The primary objective of our study was to provide a comprehensive description of the clinical, immunologic, and genetic features of a previously uncharacterized SCID cohort from Yunnan Province, with an emphasis on adding to the allelic heterogeneity through the identification of novel variants. The secondary, exploratory objective was a preliminary observation of potential genotype–phenotype correlations within this cohort.

Patients and methods

Patients

Eleven patients were admitted to the Pediatric Intensive Care Unit (PICU) at Yunnan Children’s Hospital and clinically diagnosed with SCID between June 2021 and June 2024. The diagnostic criteria were published by the International Union of Immunological Societies (IUIS) and the Primary Immune Deficiency Treatment Consortium (IPIDTC) based on clinical experience4,5. This study adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Kunming Children’s Hospital Ethics Committee (2021–03-340-K01). Written consent was obtained from the parents of all children. Additional specific informed consent for publication in an online open-access journal was obtained from the parents of the participant for any identifiable information or images used in this study.

Data collection and immunological evaluation

The clinical profiles, family histories, and other demographic information were obtained from all patients. Serum immunoglobulin levels (IgM, IgG, and IgA by turbidimetric inhibition immunoassay and IgE concentration by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay) were measured.

Lymphocyte subsets were determined by flow cytometry. Briefly, EDTA anticoagulated blood was obtained and fluorescently labeled antibodies (CD3 clone SK7, CD4 clone SK3, CD8 clone SK1, CD19 clone SJ25C1, CD16 clone B73.1, and CD56 clone NCAM16.2; BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA, USA) were added and incubated at room temperature in the dark for 15 min. After lysing red blood cells, BD Trucount™ absolute counting beads were added and the sample was processed on a FACSCanto II flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA, USA) for detection of fluorescent signals. FlowJo software (FlowJo, LLC, Ashland, OR, USA) was used to set the gates. First, lymphocytes were circled and further analysis of the percentages and absolute counts of CD3⁺, CD3⁺CD4⁺, CD3⁺CD8⁺, CD19⁺, and CD3⁻CD16⁺CD56⁺ cells was recorded. A human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) antibody test was performed on all patients to exclude secondary immunodeficiency due to HIV.

Whole-exome sequencing (WES) and Sanger sequencing validation

Genomic DNA was extracted from peripheral blood samples of the 11 probands and the available parents. Whole-exome capture was performed using the GenCap kit (MyGenostics, Beijing, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The enriched libraries were sequenced on a DNBSEQ-T7 platform (MGI, Shenzhen, China) with 150-bp paired-end reads. The mean sequencing depth was 150 × with > 98% of target bases covered at ≥ 20 × . Clean reads were aligned to the UCSC hg19 reference genome using BWA (v0.7.17; http://bio-bwa.sourceforge.net/)6. Variant calling was performed using GATK (v4.1) HaplotypeCaller (Broad Institute, Cambridge, MA, USA). Variants were filtered to exclude synonymous changes and those variants with a minor allele frequency > 0.01 in public databases (1000 Genomes; http://www.1000genomes.org/ and Genome Aggregation database [gnomAD]; https://gnomad.broadinstitute.org/). Candidate variants were prioritized based on autosomal recessive inheritance models (homozygous or compound heterozygous) in known SCID-associated genes. All reported variants and familial segregation were validated by Sanger sequencing.

Bioinformatic analysis of variants

The identified gene variants were aligned with information in the Human Gene Mutation Database (HGMD)6. The following tools were used to predict the impact of novel missense variants: SIFT (score ≤ 0.05 = deleterious; http://sift-dna.org); PolyPhen-2 (scores: 0.00–0.14 benign, 0.15–0.84 possibly damaging, and 0.85–1.00 probably damaging; http://genetics.bwh.harvard.edu/pph2/index.shtml); MutationTaster (https://www.mutationtaster.org/); and REVEL (score > 0.5 = likely pathogenic; https://sites.google.com/site/revelgenomics/ ). The pathogenicity of each variant was assessed in strict accordance with the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) Standards and Guidelines7. Swiss-Model (https://swissmodel.expasy.org/interactive)8 was used for protein analysis. Structures of proteins with variants of uncertain significance (VUS) were analyzed.

Literature review of specific variants

PubMed was systematically searched for articles reporting cases with the same nucleotide changes to contextualize the 9 previously reported variants identified in our cohort. The search was individually conducted for each variant using the gene name and the specific mutation notation (e.g., “IL2RG” AND “c.664C > T”, “LIG4” AND “c.833G > T”). The search covered publications up to August 2025. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) original research articles reporting patients with the identical mutation; (2) clinical and/or immunologic data available; and (3) articles in English or with English abstracts. Thirty-four articles were identified that together involved 94 patients with the same variants. The data extracted included clinical phenotypes, immunologic parameters, age at the time of diagnosis, and outcome. The complete list of matched cases is presented in Supplementary Table S2.

Statistics and data analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables are presented as medians with ranges. Categorical variables are presented as frequencies and percentages. All data are presented on an individual patient basis to provide a comprehensive description of this case series. A pooled analysis was performed by combining our cohort data with previously reported cases identified through a systematic literature review (see Literature Review section) for genotype–phenotype comparisons. Continuous variables were compared between mutation groups using the Mann–Whitney U test due to a non-normal distribution. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were exploratory and no adjustments were made for multiple comparisons.

Results

Clinical and demographic characteristics

Eleven infant patients with SCID from 11 unrelated Chinese families were included. The cohort comprised 8 boys and 3 girls. The median age at the time of onset and diagnosis of the patients was 3 (2, 4) and 4 (4, 6) months, respectively. Two patients (P2 and P6) were ethnic minorities, and the others were Han Chinese. No patient in this cohort had a documented family history of either consanguinity or SCID. The most common clinical manifestations were severe pneumonia, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), severe sepsis, diarrhea, cardiac failure, and disseminated BCG disease (Table S1). For the majority of patients, opportunistic infections were the initial symptom (Table S1, Fig. 1B). Blood culture-proven Enterococcus faecalis sepsis was diagnosed in P6. Bacteria isolated from the respiratory tract included Mycobacteriaum tuberculosis, Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae, and Escherichia coli, among others. Fungal infections included pneumonia due to Pneumocystis jirovecii. Bacteria isolated from other systems included skin secretions (Staphylococcus haemolyticus), intestinal tract (E. coli), and urinary tract (E. faecalis). Viral infections were reported in 5 patients and mainly included rhinovirus, cytomegalovirus, and herpes simplex virus. The causes of mortality were mainly severe infections (Fig. 1C).

Chest imaging revealed small or absent thymus shadows in 8 patients (Fig. 2). Three patients (P1, P6, and P9) presented with ulcerations at the BCG inoculation site and left axillary lymph node enlargement. Color Doppler ultrasound of left axillary masses revealed tuberculous lymphadenitis (Figs. S1L, S2C, and S3). Hematology and immunological features in SCID patients.

Molecular detection by trio WES and bioinformatic analyses

In the current study, 4 patients had homozygous mutations, 3 had hemizygous mutations, and 2 patients had compound heterozygous mutations. The proportion of autosomal recessive forms was 72.7% (8/11). Five gene defects were identified, including RAG2, IL2RG, LIG4, CD3D, and DCLRE1C, based on Trio WES. Eleven variants were detected, including 2 novel variants in RAG2 (Table 2). All variants were confirmed in the probands and the parents using Sanger sequencing (Fig. S4). The 8 previously reported variants were classified as pathogenic/ likely pathogenic according to ACMG. The remaining three variants were ‘uncertain’ and located in RAG2. Disease courses of the Yunnan SCID cohort

Most of the patients had mixed infections consisting of bacteria, viruses, fungi, and tuberculosis. The patients were treated with prophylactic antibiotics, trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole to prevent P. carinii, prophylactic antifungal therapy, and intravenous immunoglobulin. The only curative treatment for this disease is HSCT, but the patients in this cohort were extremely ill with a mortality rate of 90.9% (10/11) and a median age of 5 (3.75, 12.25) months. It was difficult to provide an opportunity to undergo HSCT. P6, with a novel homozygous RAG2 p.Q492R variant, exhibited normal growth at 11 months of age, in contrast to the failure to thrive typically seen in classic SCID.

P1 had persistent severe hypoxemia with an oxygenation index of approximately 60 for 8 h on normal to high-frequency oscillatory ventilation. Venous-arterial extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (VA-ECMO) was started and operated for 19 d. P1 was successfully weaned from VA-ECMO (Fig. S1M). After discharge from the hospital, P1 was re-infected. His family decided to discontinue treatment and he died.

Variants in the IL2RG gene

There were 16 patients with p.R222C of the IL2RG gene identified in this study and the literature, including 15 with detailed clinical features (Table S2, Fig. 4). Except for P4 in this study, none of the other patients were from China. The mean age at the time of presentation was 7 ± 3.13 months. Eleven of 15 patients (73.33%) presented with pneumonia. Only one patient presented with OS. Four patients with p.R285Q of the IL2RG gene were reported, including two with the T-B+NK- type, one with the T-B-NK+ type, and one unknown. There were 8 patients (2 with OS and 6 with SCID) with the nonsense mutation, p.R328*.

Variants in the LIG4 gene

DNA ligase IV (LIG4) deficiency is an extremely rare autosomal recessive primary immunodeficiency condition arising from LIG4. The variant, p.R278L, is considered a hotspot mutation in the Chinese population. A total of 19 patients harboring the p.R278L variant were reported (Table S2, Fig. 4). All patients were from China, including 10 females and 9 males. None of the patients presented with OS. Only two patients were homozygotes, and 89.47% (17/19) patients harbored compound heterozygous mutations in LIG4. Sixteen patients harbored a missense mutation (c.833G > T, p.R278L) and a deletion shift mutation, primarily c.1271_1275delAAAGA (p.K424Rfs*20), followed by c.1144_1145delCT (p.L382Efs*5). One patient carrying the p.E705X mutation presented with early-onset disease and died of severe pneumonia at 4 months of age, which was consistent with a severe phenotype. However, this observation was based on a single case. Sixteen of 19 (84.21%) patients had pneumonia. Fifteen of 19 (78.95%) patients had chronic diarrhea, which aggravated the nutritional status of patients and potentially contributed to growth failure. Growth delay was evident in 57.89% (11/19) of the patients. Eleven of 19 (57.89%) patients presented with microcephaly. Five patients had otitis media and 3 underwent autoimmune hemolytic anemia. B cells were extremely depleted in all patients.

Variants in CD3D and DCLRE1C

Four patients identified with c.274 + 5G > A in the CD3D gene were homozygotes, including 3 males and 1 female. There were 12 patients with exon 1–3 in DCLRE1C, including 3 from China and 7 from Saudia Arabia. One patient presented with OS, one with T-B+NK+ SCID, and the others with T-B-NK+ SCID. Two patients had compound heterozygous mutations and the remaining patients were homozygotes.

Discussion

In this descriptive case series, we presented the clinical, immunologic, and genetic features of 11 patients with SCID from Yunnan Province in China. This is one of the few reports detailing SCID cases from this geographic region. The main findings of our study include the identification of two novel RAG2 variants (p.L469P and p.Q492R), which expand the known mutational spectrum of SCID. The considerable clinical variability among patients with different genetic etiologies was also noted. However, due to the small cohort size and absence of functional validation, these findings should be considered preliminary.

The clinical presentations observed in our cohort, including early-onset pneumonia, oral candidiasis, and failure to thrive, are consistent with the classic SCID phenotype described in larger cohorts9,10. Notably, the median age at the time of diagnosis in our patients was 4 months and most presented with severe infections. This diagnostic delay is comparable to reports from other regions without universal newborn screening9 and highlights a critical gap in early SCID detection in China. The high mortality rate (10/11) within the first 6 months of life underscores the lethal nature of this disorder and the urgent need for early intervention.

In this cohort, autosomal recessive forms of SCID accounted for 72.7% of cases (8/11), while X-linked forms accounted for 27.3% (3/11). This predominance of autosomal recessive inheritance is consistent with reports from populations with high consanguinity rates, such as those in the Middle-East and India9–11. However, because consanguinity data were not systematically collected in our cohort, the factors contributing to the high proportion of autosomal recessive cases in this Yunnan population are unclear and warrant further investigation. The median age at the time of diagnosis in the current study was 4 months, which is slightly younger than reported for Turkey, India, and the USA (5 months). The reports by Vignesh et al.9 and Luk et al.12 highlighted the importance of family history in facilitating an early diagnosis. In our cohort, no patients had a positive family history, suggesting that factors other than family history, such as earlier recognition of severe infections by local clinicians, may have contributed to the diagnostic timing. Larger epidemiologic studies are needed to elucidate the determinants of diagnostic age in this region.

RAG2 deficiency is considered a rare cause of SCID, particularly in Chinese populations. In this study, three patients carried RAG2 variants, while no RAG1 variants were identified. Five RAG2 variants were detected in three patients, including two novel missense mutations and three reported mutations. The two novel variants were found in P1 (T-B-NK+) with typical SCID and P2 (TlowB-NK+) and P3 (TlowB-NK-) with atypical SCID. Notably, NK cells were elevated in P1 and P2 but markedly decreased in P3 and HLH was observed in P2. Based on the clinical features, laboratory tests, and genetic and molecular analyses, we believe that these three novel variants are the pathogenic sites for SCID. Although these novel variants were predicted to be deleterious by multiple in silico tools and segregate with disease in families, functional studies are required to confirm the pathogenic mechanisms and to determine whether the variants represent hypomorphic alleles contributing to the phenotypic variability observed. The RAG2 p.R229W variant identified in P2 affects a residue critical for an RAG1–RAG2 interaction. Located between two Kelch repeats, p.R229 normally forms a salt bridge with RAG1-D549; substitution with tryptophan disrupts this interaction13. Although previously reported in multiple cases, most were homozygous and none were from China. The heterozygous presentation in our patient with atypical features suggests complex genotype–phenotype relationships requiring further study.

Three patients in our cohort carried IL2RG mutations (27.3%), a proportion lower than the 50%–60% reported in cohort in Japan14 and India9. One patient carried the previously reported p.R222C variant, which has been associated with a milder phenotype and relatively preserved lymphocyte counts15,16. Consistent with these reports, our patient exhibited relatively preserved lymphocyte counts. Another patient carried a nonsense variant (p.R328*) in the IL2RG gene and presented with OS, a rare phenotype in X-linked SCID17, highlighting the clinical heterogeneity of IL2RG deficiency.

Two patients in our cohort had LIG4 deficiency, presenting with atypical SCID, microcephaly, and developmental delay. One patient carried the previously reported p.R278L variant; the specific variant in the other patient could not be identified. LIG4 deficiency is rare (< 70 patients reported18) with a broad clinical spectrum19. The p.R278L affects the same codon as p.R278H, which reduces LIG4 activity to 10% of wild-type20, suggesting similar functional impact, although confirmation is needed. Both patients had a later onset than other SCID cases, consistent with the milder phenotype of LIG4 deficiency. Long-term follow-up for radiation sensitivity and cancer risk is warranted.

This cohort includes the first reported case of CD3D deficiency in China with the c.274 + 5G > A variant. This variant is known to disrupt mRNA splicing, reducing CD3δ expression and selectively impairing αβ T-cell development21,22. CD3D deficiency is extremely rare, with only 8 pathogenic variants reported worldwide (HGMD). Our patient’s phenotype aligns with previous reports9, expanding the geographic distribution of this rare SCID etiology.

Exon 1–3 deletion in the DCLRE1C gene leads to a truncated or complete absence of the encoded protein product. Xiao et al.23 analyzed 26 publications and summarized exon 1–3 is a hotspot region for deletion and the major mechanism is homologous recombination. In the current study, compound variations—namely, 1 exon 1–3 deletion and 1 single nucleotide variation (SNP)—accounted for 16.7% (2/12), which is higher than compound variations with 1 copy number variation and 1 SNP.

The high mortality rate (10/11) in this cohort highlights the urgent need for early diagnosis and accessible curative treatments. While HSCT remains the standard of care, recent advances in gene therapy, including lentiviral vector trials for X-linked SCID and base editing strategies for CD3δ deficiency22,24,25, offer promise for future treatment. However, for most SCID patients in China today, early detection through newborn screening remains the most achievable path to improving survival.

A pooled analysis combining our cohort with literature-derived cases carrying the same mutations was performed to explore genotype–phenotype associations. Patients with the DCLRE1C exon 1–3 deletion (n = 15) had significantly lower CD3 + T-cell counts and IgM levels than those with the LIG4 c.833G > T variant (n = 10; P < 0.05), suggesting distinct immunologic profiles for different genetic defects. However, these results should be interpreted with caution due to the exploratory analysis, lack of multiple comparison adjustment, and heterogeneity of literature-derived data.

The delayed diagnosis in our cohort (median age: 4 months, with most patients presenting with severe infections) highlights a critical gap in early SCID detection in China, where universal newborn screening has not been implemented. TREC-based screening, which detects SCID regardless of genetic etiology, enables preemptive transplantation before life-threatening infections occur, dramatically improving survival. The absence of family history in all cases further underscores that targeted screening is insufficient. With proven feasibility in pilot studies and successful implementation in the US and Europe26,27, our findings from Yunnan add to the evidence supporting urgent adoption of routine SCID newborn screening across China.

Limitations

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, Given the descriptive nature of this study and the limited sample size (n = 11), the statistical analysis was primarily descriptive. And the small sample size, while inherent to the rarity of SCID, significantly limits the generalizability of our findings. Second, the absence of functional validation for the novel RAG2 variants (p.L469P and p.Q492R) means that the precise molecular consequences remain speculative. Third, our study lacked comparative population genetic data from healthy controls in Yunnan Province, precluding any speculation on the relationship between mutation distribution and ethnic composition. Fourth, our pooled analysis combining cohort and literature-derived cases is subject to limitations, including heterogeneity in clinical reporting, variable diagnostic criteria, and potential publication bias. Finally, the exploratory genotype–phenotype comparisons were not adjusted for multiple comparisons, and all statistical findings should be interpreted with caution. Larger, multicenter prospective studies with functional validation are warranted to confirm and extend our observations.

Conclusion

This study described 11 SCID patients from Yunnan, China and identified two novel RAG2 variants. The high mortality rate and delayed diagnosis underscore the urgent need for newborn screening in China. However, due to the small sample size and lack of functional validation, all observations remain preliminary. Larger multicenter studies with functional validation are warranted to confirm these findings. We emphasize that early detection through screening would enable preemptive HSCT and significantly improve outcomes for affected infants.

Thermal corrections to Regge trajectories
R. Cádiz, M. Loewe, R. Zamora

Abstract
In this work, we investigate the behavior of Regge trajectories in the context of quantum field theory at finite temperature. For this purpose, we employ the λ ​ ϕ 3 \lambda\phi^{3} model. We first compute the Regge trajectories at zero temperature, establishing a baseline for comparison. As a key novelty, we extended our analysis to finite temperature and derive an analytical expression for the Regge trajectories in this regime. We explore how temperature modifies the structure of the Regge trajectories and analyze the physical implications of these modifications, which arise from the resummation of thermal ladder diagrams. As an interesting consequence, we find the thermal evolution of masses belonging to different Regge trajectories.

Introduction

The study of Regge trajectories, initially developed in the context of hadron scattering in the 1960s

These trajectories link the spin of particles to their mass squared in a nearly linear relationship, encapsulating fundamental dynamics of hadronic resonances and offering a geometrical interpretation of scattering processes in strong interactions. To continue the computation of this integral, we can manipulate the denominator to isolate the dependence

\displaystyle i\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle=3!\lambda^{4}\int_{0}^{1}\differential{x}\differential{y}\differential{z}\differential{w}\delta(x+y+z+w-1)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\cdot\int\dfrac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{1}{(q^{2}-\Delta)^{4}},

where we have defined

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=yws-zt\quantity(1-z)+2z\quantity(p_1-p_3)\quantity(yp_1-wp_2)

\mathcal{M}=\dfrac{\lambda^{4}}{96\pi^{2}}\int\dfrac{\delta\quantity(x+y+z+w-1)\differential{x}\differential{y}\differential{z}\differential{w}}{\Delta^{2}}.

In order to compute the remaining integral, we use Collins’ technique

\mathcal{M}=\dfrac{\lambda^{4}}{96\pi^{2}}\quantity(\dfrac{\ln s}{s})\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\delta\quantity(x+z-1)}{m^{2}-zt\quantity(1-z)}\differential{x}\differential{z}.

The logarithm term clearly emerges form the asymptotic region associated to the leading term when
s\rightarrow\infty

where we have defined

Having determined the analytical behavior of the box diagram, we now proceed to compute the Regge trajectory. To obtain the trajectory, we must proceed with the resummation of diagrams by stacking box diagrams to form a “staircase diagram” as shown in Fig.

(11)

Thus, summing an infinite series of ladder diagrams yields the asymptotic behavior of the resulting amplitude as

\displaystyle A(s,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{n}

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{2}}{s}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{\quantity[K(t)\ln s]^{n-1}}{\quantity(n-1)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\lambda^{2}}{s}\cdot e^{K(t)\ln s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}s^{\alpha(t)},

in which we can identify the Regge trajectory as

\alpha(t)=K(t)-1.

(13)

Notice that we need

, i.e.

Finite Temperature Regge Trajectories

In order to calculate the finite temperature case, we start from Eq. (
4
), and incorporate temperature effects using the imaginary time formalism in the standard way

, i.e.,

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}f(q)\rightarrow\frac{i}{\beta}\sum_{n}\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}f(\omega_{n},\mathbf{q}),

(14)

where we have introduced the bosonic Matsubara frequencies
\omega_{n}=2\pi n/\beta
with
\beta=1/T
. Therefore, the expression for the box diagram at finite temperature is

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{0,T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3!\lambda^{4}\int_{0}^{1}\differential{x}\differential{y}\differential{z}\differential{w}\delta(x+y+z+w-1)

(15)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{n}\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{(\omega_{n}+\mathbf{q}^{2}+\Delta)^{4}},

where the subscripts
0
and
T
correspond to vacuum and finite temperature, respectively. Using the following identity

\frac{1}{(\omega_{n}+\mathbf{q}^{2}+\Delta)^{4}}=-\frac{1}{3!}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta}\right)^{3}\frac{1}{(\omega_{n}+\mathbf{q}^{2}+\Delta)},

we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{0,T}

\displaystyle=-\lambda^{4}\int_{0}^{1}\differential{x}\differential{y}\differential{z}\differential{w}\delta(x+y+z+w-1)

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684pt\times\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta}\right)^{3}\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{n}\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{(\omega_{n}+\mathbf{q}^{2}+\Delta)}

(17)

and performing the summation over Matsubara frequencies, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{0,T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lambda^{4}\int_{0}^{1}\differential{x}\differential{y}\differential{z}\differential{w}\delta(x+y+z+w-1)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta}\right)^{3}\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\Biggl(\frac{1}{2(\mathbf{q}^{2}+\Delta)^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\mathbf{q}^{2}+\Delta)^{1/2}}e^{-\beta\sqrt{\mathbf{q}^{2}+\Delta}}\Biggr)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{0}

\displaystyle=-\lambda^{4}\int_{0}^{1}\differential{x}\differential{y}\differential{z}\differential{w}\delta(x+y+z+w-1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{T}

IV
Conclusions

We have analytically derived the thermal corrections to Regge trajectories within a
\lambda\phi^{3}
quantum field theory framework by resumming ladder diagrams using the imaginary-time formalism. Our results show that finite temperature induces a reduction in the Regge slope, which leads to observable effects such as the increase of meson masses with temperature. In particular, we analyze the behavior of the
\rho
meson and find that its mass exhibits a mild increase at low temperatures, followed by a more rapid growth as the temperature increases. This increase in mass aligns with theoretical predictions in thermal QFT
Dominguez et al. 1993
;
Loewe et al. 2023
, and supports the idea that the excitation spectrum of hadrons is modified in the presence of a thermal bath.

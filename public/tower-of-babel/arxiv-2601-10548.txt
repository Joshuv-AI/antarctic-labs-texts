The inducibility of Turán graphs
Xizhi Liu, Jie Ma, Tianming Zhu

Abstract
Let I ⁡ ( F , n ) I(F,n) denote the maximum number of induced copies of a graph F F in an n n -vertex graph. The inducibility of F F , defined as i ⁡ ( F ) = lim n → ∞ I ⁡ ( F , n ) / ( n v ⁡ ( F ) ) i(F)=\lim_{n\to\infty}{I(F,n)}/{\tbinom{n}{v(F)}} , is a central problem in extremal graph theory. In this work, we investigate the inducibility of Turán graphs F F . This topic has been extensively studied in the literature, including works of Pippenger–Golumbic [ 27 ] , Brown–Sidorenko [ 7 ] , Bollobás–Egawa–Harris–Jin [ 5 ] , Mubayi, Reiher, and the first author [ 23 ] , and Yuster [ 32 ] . Broadly speaking, these results resolve or asymptotically resolve the problem when the part sizes of F F are either sufficiently large or sufficiently small (at most four). We complete this picture by proving that for every Turán graph F F and sufficiently large n n , the value I ⁡ ( F , n ) I(F,n) is attained uniquely by the m m -partite Turán graph on n n vertices, where m m is given explicitly in terms of the number of parts and vertices of F F . This confirms a conjecture of Bollobás–Egawa–Harris–Jin [ 5 ] from 1995, and we also establish the corresponding stability theorem. Moreover, we prove an asymptotic analogue for I k + 1 ​ ( F , n ) I_{k+1}(F,n) , the maximum number of induced copies of F F in an n n -vertex K k + 1 K_{k+1} -free graph, thereby completely resolving a recent problem of Yuster [ 32 ] . Finally, our results extend to a broader class of complete multipartite graphs in which the largest and smallest part sizes differ by at most on the order of the square root of the smallest part size.

Introduction

A fundamental problem in extremal graph theory is to determine the maximum number of induced copies of a given graph
F
among all
n
-vertex graphs.
Formally, given two graphs
F
and
G
, let
I(F,G)
denote the number of induced copies of
F
in
G
, that is, the number of subsets
S\subseteq V(G)
of size
v(F)
such that the induced subgraph
G[S]
is isomorphic to
F
. Here,
v(F)
denotes the number of vertices of
F
.
For every positive integer
n
, let

\displaystyle I(F,n)\coloneqq\max\left\{I(F,G)\colon v(G)=n\right\}.

The
inducibility
of
F
is then defined as

\displaystyle i(F)\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}{I(F,n)}/{\tbinom{n}{v(F)}}.

A systematic study of the inducibility problem for graphs was initiated in a foundational work of Pippenger–Golumbic
[
27
]
, in which they established several general properties of inducibility and determined the inducibility of complete bipartite graphs with part sizes differing by at most one.

Determining
I(F,n)
(or even
i(F)
) is rather difficult in general. For example, the inducibility of the path on four vertices remains wide open (see, e.g.,
[
10
,
9
]
).
For small graphs, bounds on the inducibility of graphs on
4
,
5
, and
6
vertices have been obtained in several works, including Exoo
[
10
]
, Hirst
[
18
]
, Even-Zohar–Linial
[
9
]
, Pikhurko–Sliačan–Tyros
[
26
]
, and Bodnár et al.
[
4
]
, some of which employ the computer-assisted flag algebra machinery of Razborov
[
28
]
.
A particularly interesting result obtained by Balogh–Hu–Lidickỳ–Pfender
[
2
]
is the determination of the inducibility of the
5
-cycle
C_{5}
, which confirms a special case of an old conjecture of Pippenger–Golumbic
[
27
]
on the inducibility of cycles.
This conjecture remains open for longer cycles, and improved upper bounds were obtained in
[
17
,
19
]
.

For general graphs, results of Yuster
[
31
]
and Fox–Huang–Lee
[
11
]
imply that for almost all graphs

,

\displaystyle i(F)=\frac{v(F)!}{v(F)^{v(F)}-v(F)},

where the lower bound construction arises from nested blowups of

itself.
Answering a question of Bollobás–Egawa–Harris–Jin
[
5
]
asymptotically, Hatami–Hirst–Norine
[
15
]
established that if

is a sufficiently large balanced blowup of some graph

, then the extremal graph for

is essentially a blowup of

.
Extending the results
[
24
,
20
,
12
]
on the edge-statistic conjecture of Alon–Hefetz–Krivelevich–Tyomkyn
[
1
]
, Ueltzen
[
30
]
recently classified all graphs with high inducibility.

In this work, we focus on the case where
F
is a complete multipartite graph, and more specifically, an almost balanced complete multipartite graph.
This class of graphs has already been studied extensively since the work of Pippenger–Golumbic
[
27
]
.
Throughout this work, for positive integers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}
, let
K_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}}
denote the complete
r
-partite graph with part sizes
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}
.
For positive integers
\ell\geq r
, the
Turán graph

T_{r}(\ell)
denotes the complete
r
-partite graph on
\ell
vertices in which the largest and smallest part sizes differ by at most one.
The cases
\ell=r
and

correspond to the complete graph on
r
vertices and the empty graph on
\ell
vertices, respectively; both are trivial cases in the inducibility problem. So, we assume for the remainder of this work that

and

.

Results of Pippenger–Golumbic
[
27
]
(also Bollobás–Nara–Tachibana
[
6
]
) show that for bipartite Turán graphs

, the value of

is attained by bipartite Turán graphs
T_{2}(n)
.
Using Zykov symmetrization
[
33
]
together with additional arguments, Brown–Sidorenko
[
7
]
showed that for every complete multipartite graph

, an extremal graph for

can always be chosen from the class of complete multipartite graphs; moreover, if
F
is complete bipartite, then the number of parts can be taken to be at most two.
Nevertheless, determining the number of parts and the ratios of part sizes in the extremal construction for a complete multipartite graph

remains difficult in general. Consequently, the inducibility of complete multipartite graphs is still largely open.

The family of Turán graphs
T_{r}(\ell)
has received substantial attention in the literature.
Refining an asymptotic result of Brown–Sidorenko
[
7
]
for the balanced complete
r
-partite graph

, Bollobás–Egawa–Harris–Jin
[

]
proved that when

, the

-partite Turán graph

is the unique extremal graph, whereas this is not the case if

.
In the last section of
[
5

One may also venture the conjecture that for every pair

r\geq 4

This conjecture has been open for three decades.
There has been recent progress on the case when

is a Turán graph with each part of small size.
Mubayi, Reiher, and the first author
[

determined the inducibility when

is a Turán graph in which all but one part have size one.
This result was extended very recently by Yuster
[

, who determined the inducibility when

is an

He also remarked that it would be highly interesting to determine whether the bound

The main results of this work confirm the conjecture of Bollobás–Egawa–Harris–Jin

and resolves the above problem of Yuster

1.1
Inducibility of almost balanced graphs

Let
F=K_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}}
be the complete
r
-partite graph with part sizes
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}
, and assume that
a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{r}\geq 1
.
Let
\ell\coloneqq a_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}
denote the number of vertices of
F
.
We say that
F
is
almost balanced
if it is not the complete graph and

\displaystyle\binom{a_{1}-a_{r}}{2}<a_{r},

(1)

or, equivalently,
a_{1}<a_{r}+\tfrac{1}{2}\left(1+\sqrt{8a_{r}+1}\right)
.
It is key to observe that for
\ell\geq r+1
, every Turán graph
T_{r}(\ell)
is almost balanced.
Define the constant (depending only on

\displaystyle\kappa_{F}\coloneqq\frac{\binom{\ell}{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}}}{\mathrm{sym}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})}=\frac{\ell!}{a_{1}!\cdots a_{r}!\cdot\mathrm{sym}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})}.

(2)

Here,
\mathrm{sym}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
denotes the size of the symmetry group of the multiset
\{\hskip-2.5pt\{\hskip 1.00006pta_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\hskip 1.00006pt\}\hskip-2.5pt\}
.
In other words, if we let
b_{1},\ldots,b_{t}
denote the distinct elements of the multiple set
\{\hskip-2.5pt\{\hskip 1.00006pta_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\hskip 1.00006pt\}\hskip-2.5pt\}
, occurring with multiplicities
r_{1},\ldots,r_{t}
, respectively,
then

\displaystyle\mathrm{sym}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})=r_{1}!\cdots r_{t}!.

Let
m_{r,\ell}
denote the unique

3.2
.
integer that maximizes the discrete function

defined by

\displaystyle f(k)\coloneqq\frac{(k-1)_{r-1}}{k^{\ell-1}}=\frac{(k-1)\cdots(k-r+1)}{k^{\ell-1}},\quad\text{for every integer $k\in[r,\infty)$}.

(3)

The main result of this subsection is the following theorem, which confirms the conjecture of Bollobás–Egawa–Harris–Jin
[
5
]

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
F
is an almost balanced complete
r
-partite graph on
\ell\geq r+1

. Then

\displaystyle i(F)=\kappa_{F}\cdot\frac{(m-1)_{r-1}}{m^{\ell-1}}.

Moreover, there exists a constant

such that for all

, the
m
-partite Turán graph
T_{m}(n)
is the unique extremal graph for

.

In the balanced case
F=K_{r}(t)
(i.e., every part of
F
has the same size

), we are able to determine the exact value of
I(F,n)
for
every

(see Theorem

It is clear that
I(F,G)=I(\bar{F},\bar{G})

\bar{F}
and

denote the complements of

and

, and hence, Theorem
1.1
also determines the inducibility of graphs that are vertex-disjoint unions of cliques, in which the smallest clique size
a_{r}
and the largest clique size

satisfy (

1.2
Inducibility of almost balanced graphs in
H
-free graphs

Given a graph
H
, we say that a graph
G
is
H
-free
if it does not contain
H
as a (not necessarily induced) subgraph.
Let
I_{H}(F,n)
denote the maximum number of induced copies of
F
in an
H
-free
n
-vertex graph, i.e.,

\displaystyle I_{H}(F,n)\coloneqq\max\big\{I(F,G)\colon\text{$v(G)=n$ and $G$ is $H$-free}\big\}.

The
H
-free inducibility
of
F
is then defined as

\displaystyle i_{H}(F)\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}{I_{H}(F,n)}/{\tbinom{n}{v(F)}}.

When
H=K_{k+1}
is the complete graph on
k+1
vertices, we simply write
I_{k+1}(F,n)
and
i_{k+1}(F)
instead of
I_{K_{k+1}}(F,n)
and
i_{K_{k+1}}(F)
.

A classical example is the Erdős Pentagon Problem
[
8
]
, which asks for the determination of
I_{3}(C_{5},n)
, that is, the maximum number of (induced) copies of
C_{5}
in an
n
-vertex
K_{3}
-free graph.
This problem was solved independently by Grzesik
[
14
]
and Hatami–Hladký–Kráľ–Norine–Razborov
[
16
]
for large
n
, and subsequently by Lidický–Pfender
[
21
]
for all
n
.

Very recently, Yuster
[
32
]
initiated a systematic study of inducibility problem in
H
-free graphs.
Among many other results, he determined (see
[
32
, Theorem 1.6]
), for all
k\geq r
, the value of
i_{k+1}(F)
when
F
is an
r
-partite Turán graph on at most
3r+1
vertices.
He also remarked that it would be highly interesting to determine whether the analogous result holds for all
r
-partite Turán graphs, that is, without the restriction on the number of vertices.

Problem 1.2

(
[
32
, Problem 1.8]
)
.

Is it true that for all

, there exists

Let

-partite Turán graph on

For all

In the following theorem, we completely resolve Yuster’s problem (in fact, for a broader family) and determine the value of

defined in the previous subsection.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
F
is an almost balanced complete
r
-partite graph on
\ell\geq r+1

. Then

\displaystyle i_{k+1}(F)=\begin{cases}\kappa_{F}\cdot\frac{(k-1)_{r-1}}{k^{\ell-1}},&\quad\text{if}\quad k\in[r,m-1],\\[3.00003pt]
\kappa_{F}\cdot\frac{(m-1)_{r-1}}{m^{\ell-1}},&\quad\text{if}\quad k\in[m,\infty).\end{cases}

1.3
Perfect stability

An interesting phenomenon, and also a very useful tool in extremal combinatorics, is
stability
, introduced in the seminal work of Simonovits
[
29
]
.
Partly inspired by the work of Norin–Yepremyan
[
25
]
, a general framework for establishing a strong form of stability in certain graph extremal problems that can be solved using the Zykov symmetrization was developed recently by Liu–Pikhurko–Sharifzadeh–Staden
[
22
]
.
This framework will be used to prove the second assertion (i.e. the exact result) of Theorem
1.1
.

For two graphs
G
and
H
with the same number of vertices, the
edit distance

\mathrm{edit}(G,H)
between
G
and
H
is the minimum number of edges one needs to add or remove from
G
to make it isomorphic to
H
.

Definition 1.4

(
[
22
, Definition 2]
)
.

The inducibility problem for a complete multipartite graph
F
is
perfectly stable
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that for every
n
-vertex graph
G
with
n\geq C
, there is an
n
-vertex complete multipartite graph
H
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathrm{edit}(G,H)\leq C\cdot\frac{I(F,n)-I(F,G)}{\tbinom{n}{v(F)}}\binom{n}{2}.

In particular, perfect stability of
F
implies that for all sufficiently large
n
,
every
extremal graph for the inducibility problem
I(F,n)
is complete multipartite.

By the result of Brown and Sidorenko
[
7
, Proposition 1]
, the inducibility problem for complete multipartite graphs can be solved using Zykov symmetrization.
Thus, the framework from
[
22
]
can be applied to this problem.
Indeed, as applications, Liu–Pikhurko–Sharifzadeh–Staden
[
22
]
established perfect stability for the inducibility problem of
F
when
F
is a complete bipartite graph, a complete
r
-partite graph with each part of size
t>1+\ln r
, and for small cases such as
K_{2,1,1,1}
and
K_{3,1,1}
.
They further conjectured
[
22
, Conjecture 1]
that the inducibility problem is perfectly stable for every complete multipartite graph
F
.

We confirm their conjecture for all almost balanced complete multipartite graphs.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose that
F
is an almost balanced complete multipartite graph. Then the inducibility problem
I(F,n)
is perfectly stable.

Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce some necessary definitions and preliminary results.

Let
k

For every real number

\binom{z}{k}=(z)_{k}/k!

\displaystyle(z)_{k}\coloneqq\begin{cases}0,&\quad\text{if}\quad z\leq k-1,\\

For a positive integer

, let
[k]\coloneqq\{1,\ldots,k\}
.
We denote by
\mathbb{N}
the set of natural numbers
\{0,1,2,\dots\}
, and by
\mathbb{N}_{+}

\{1,2,3,\dots\}

Given a (possibly infinite) set

\displaystyle(S)_{r}\coloneqq\big\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\in S^{r}\colon\text{$x_{i}\neq x_{j}$ for all distinct $i,j\in[r]$}\big\}.

In particular,

\displaystyle(\mathbb{N})_{r}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\big\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}\colon\text{$x_{i}\neq x_{j}$ for all distinct $i,j\in[r]$}\big\},

\displaystyle(\mathbb{N}_{+})_{r}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\big\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\in\mathbb{N}_{+}^{r}\colon\text{$x_{i}\neq x_{j}$ for all distinct $i,j\in[r]$}\big\}.

For a complete multipartite graph

denote the number of parts of

We have the following simple fact for counting the number of induced copies of

Let

Suppose that

is a complete multipartite graph whose non-singleton part sizes are

Then

\displaystyle I(F,G)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{sym}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})}\sum_{i=0}^{\mathrm{sc}(F)}i!\binom{\mathrm{sc}(F)}{i}\binom{\mathrm{sc}(G)}{i}\sum_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{r-i})\in([k])_{r-i}}~\prod_{j=1}^{r-i}\binom{b_{i_{j}}}{a_{j}}.

In particular, if
\mathrm{sc}(F)=0

\mathrm{sc}(G)=0

\displaystyle I(F,G)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{sym}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})}\sum_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{r})\in([k])_{r}}~\prod_{j=1}^{r}\binom{b_{i_{j}}}{a_{j}}.

Lemma 2.3

Suppose that

.

Suppose that

), obtained as follows: if
x_{0}=0
, then take a partition
[n]=V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{m}
such that
||V_{i}|-x_{i}n|<1
for each
i
, and set
V_{0}=\varnothing
; otherwise, for all
i\geq 1
with
x_{i}n\geq 2
, set
|V_{i}|=\lfloor x_{i}n\rfloor\geq 2
, and let
V_{0}
contain the remaining vertices in
[n]
.
We call
V_{0},\ldots,V_{m}
the
\overline{\mathcal{P}}
-structure
of
G_{n,\bm{x}}
.

For a tuple
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{t})
of positive integers and a sequence
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}_{+}}
of real numbers, let

\displaystyle S_{d_{1},\ldots,d_{t}}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)\coloneqq\sum_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{t})\in(\mathbb{N}_{+})_{t}}x_{i_{1}}^{d_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{t}}^{d_{t}}.

(6)

For convenience, we set
S_{\varnothing}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)\coloneqq 1
.

For two graphs
F
and
G
, let
p(F,G)\coloneqq I(F,G)/\binom{v(G)}{v(F)}
denote the
induced density
of
F
in
G
.

Let
a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{r}\geq 1
be integers, and let
F=K_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}}
.
Define the
limit function

\displaystyle p_{F}(\bm{x})\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}p(F,G_{n,\bm{x}}).

It follows from Fact
2.1
that (recall the definition of
\kappa_{F}
from (
2
))

\displaystyle p_{F}(\bm{x})=\kappa_{F}\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{\mathrm{sc}(F)}\binom{\mathrm{sc}(F)}{i}x_{0}^{i}\cdot S_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r-i}}(\bm{x}).

(7)

In particular, if
\mathrm{sc}(F)=0
or
x_{0}=0
, this expression simplifies to

\displaystyle p_{F}(\bm{x})=\kappa_{F}\cdot S_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}}(\bm{x}).

(8)

Intuitively, Lemma
2.3
and Fact
2.1
together indicate that determining
i(F)
for a complete multipartite graph amounts to finding the maximum of
p_{F}(\bm{x})
over
\overline{\mathcal{P}}
.
A rigorous proof is given in
[
22
, Section 2]
.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
22
]
)
.

Suppose that
F
is a complete multipartite graph.
Then

\displaystyle i(F)=\max_{\bm{x}\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}}~p_{F}(\bm{x}).

For perfect stability, it is also necessary to understand the property of the maximizers of
p_{F}(\bm{x})
over
\overline{\mathcal{P}}
.
Thus, for a complete multipartite graph
F
, we define

\displaystyle\mathrm{OPT}(F)\coloneqq\left\{\bm{x}\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}\colon p_{F}(\bm{x})=i(F)\right\}.

So for every
\bm{x}\in\mathrm{OPT}(F)
, the graph
G_{n,\bm{x}}
is asymptotically extremal for the inducibility problem
I(F,n)
.

It follows relatively straightforwardly from Lemma
2.4
and standard results from the theory of graphons (see
[
32
, Section 1]
for further details) that, for a complete multipartite graph
F=K_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}}
, we have

\displaystyle i_{k+1}(F)=\max_{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}}p_{F}(\bm{x})=\kappa_{F}\cdot\max_{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}}S_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}}(\bm{x}),

(9)

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{k-1}\coloneqq\left\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},0,\ldots)\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}_{+}}\colon\text{$x_{i}\geq 0$ for $i\in[k]$ and $x_{1}+\cdots+x_{k}=1$}\right\}.

Observe that
\mathbb{S}^{k-1}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{P}}
and the union
\bigcup_{k\geq 1}\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
is the collection of sequences
\bm{x}\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}
with
x_{0}=0
.
Define

\displaystyle\mathrm{OPT}_{k+1}(F)\coloneqq\left\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}\colon p_{F}(\bm{x})=i_{k+1}(F)\right\}.

For convenience, set
\mathbb{S}^{\infty}=\bigcup_{k\geq 1}\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
,
\mathrm{OPT}_{\infty}(F)=\mathrm{OPT}(F)
, and
i_{\infty}(F)=i(F)
.
For every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, define

\displaystyle\bm{k}\coloneqq\big(\underbrace{1/k,\,\ldots,\,1/k}_{k},\,0,\,\ldots\big).

We call
\bm{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}

balanced
if
x_{i}=x_{j}
for all
i,j\in\mathrm{supp}(\bm{x})
; otherwise it is
unbalanced
.
In particular, if such a balanced
\bm{x}
belongs to
\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
, then necessarily
\bm{x}=\bm{t}
for some integer
t\leq k
.

The following simple property of almost balanced complete multipartite graphs will be used multiple times.

Fact 2.7
.

Let
F=K_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}}
be an almost balanced complete
r
-partite graph with
a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{r}
, and let
\ell\coloneqq a_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}
.

(i)

If
\ell\in[r+1,2r-1]
, then
a_{1}=\cdots=a_{\ell-r}=2
and
a_{\ell-r+1}=\cdots=a_{r}=1
.

(ii)

If
\ell\geq 2r
, then
a_{r}\geq 2
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\ell\in[r+1,2r-1]
.
By the Pigeonhole Principle, we have
a_{r}=1
.
By the definition of almost balanced (see (
1
)), we have
\binom{a_{1}-1}{2}<1
, which implies that
a_{1}\leq 2
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle a_{1}=\cdots=a_{\ell-r}=2\quad\text{and}\quad a_{\ell-r+1}=\cdots=a_{r}=1.

Now suppose that
\ell\geq 2r
. If
a_{r}=1
, then the definition of almost balanced implies that
\binom{a_{1}-1}{2}<1
, which forces
a_{1}\leq 2
.
Consequently,
\ell\leq 2r-1
, which is a contradiction.
∎

3
Asymptotic results and uniqueness of the maximizer

In this section, we prove the following theorem, which constitutes the most crucial part of the proofs in this work.
In particular, it implies Theorem
1.3
and the asymptotic part of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\ell>r\geq 2
be integers, and let
m=m_{r,\ell}
.
Suppose that
F
is an almost balanced complete
r
-partite graph on
\ell
vertices.
Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{OPT}_{k+1}(F)=\begin{cases}\{\bm{k}\},&\quad\text{if}\quad k\in[r,~m-1],\\[1.99997pt]
\{\bm{m}\},&\quad\text{if}\quad k\in[m,~\infty].\end{cases}

In particular,

\displaystyle i_{k+1}(F)=\begin{cases}\kappa_{F}\cdot\frac{(k-1)_{r-1}}{{k}^{\ell-1}},&\quad\text{if}\quad k\in[r,~m-1],\\[3.00003pt]
\kappa_{F}\cdot\frac{(m-1)_{r-1}}{m^{\ell-1}},&\quad\text{if}\quad k\in[m,~\infty].\end{cases}

In the next subsection, we establish some preliminary inequalities.
We then present the proof of Theorem
3.1
in two cases according to the size of
\ell
:
\ell\leq 2r-1
and
\ell\geq 2r
.

We remark that the proof of the case
\ell\leq 2r-1
in Theorem
3.1
is similar to that of
[
32
, Theorem 1.6]
(which works for
\ell\leq 3r+1
), with one difference: for
k=\infty
, Yuster proved the uniqueness of the maximizer of (
8
), whereas we prove the uniqueness of the maximizer of (
7
) (slightly stronger but necessary for perfect stability).
In contrast, our proof for the case
\ell\geq 2r
differs substantially from Yuster’s.
It relies on a quite nontrivial inequality (Proposition
3.7
), whereas Yuster’s argument is based on properties of low-degree polynomials.

3.1
Basic properties of
f
in (
3
) and the integer
m_{r,\ell}

Let
h\colon I\to\mathbb{R}
be a function on
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
.
We say that
h
is
unimodal
if there exists a point
x_{0}\in I
such that

•

h
is non-decreasing on
I\cap(-\infty,x_{0}]
, and

•

h
is non-increasing on
I\cap(x_{0},\infty)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\ell>r\geq 2
be integers. The discrete function
f\colon[r,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
, defined as in (
3
), is unimodal and attains its maximum at a unique integer
m_{r,\ell}\in[r,\infty)
.

Proof.

Consider the real extension
\tilde{f}\colon[r,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
of
f
defined by

\tilde{f}(x)=\frac{(x-1)\cdots(x-r+1)}{x^{\ell-1}},\quad\text{for every real number $x\in[r,\infty)$}.

Straightforward calculations show that the derivative of
\tilde{f}(x)
is

\tilde{f}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{\tilde{f}(x)}{x}\left(\frac{x}{x-1}+\cdots+\frac{x}{x-r+1}-\ell+1\right).

Define

\displaystyle g(x)\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\frac{x}{x-i}-\ell+1,\quad\text{for every real number $x\in[r,\infty)$}.

(10)

Observe that
g(x)
is strictly decreasing on
[r,\infty)
and satisfies

\lim_{x\to\infty}g(x)=r-\ell<0
.
Thus, the function
\tilde{f}
is not monotone increasing and is unimodal.
It follows that the discrete function
f
can have at most two maximizers on the set of integers
\{r,r+1,\ldots\}
, and if there are two, they must be consecutive integers.

Suppose, for contradiction, that
f
attains its maximum at two consecutive integers
k_{0}
and
k_{0}+1
.
Note that, for every
k\in\{r,r+1,\ldots\}
, we have
f(k)<1
, in particular,
f(k)
is not an integer.
Moreover, when written in lowest terms, the denominator of
f(k_{0})
divides
k_{0}^{\ell-1}
, while the denominator of
f(k_{0}+1)
divides
(k_{0}+1)^{\ell-1}
. Since
k_{0}
and
k_{0}+1
are coprime,
k_{0}^{\ell-1}
and
(k_{0}+1)^{\ell-1}
are also coprime, and consequently the denominators of
f(k_{0})
and
f(k_{0}+1)
are coprime (and both greater than
1
, since both
f(k_{0})
and
f(k_{0}+1)
are not integers). Hence,
f(k_{0})\neq f(k_{0}+1)
, a contradiction. Therefore, the maximizer of
f
on
\{r,r+1,\ldots\}
is unique.
∎

It follows from Lemma
3.2
that
m_{r,\ell}=r
if and only if
f(r)>f(r+1)
, which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\ell>\frac{\ln(r+1)}{\ln(1+1/r)}=(1+o(1))r\ln r.

This is consistent with the result of Brown–Sidorenko
[
7
, Theorem 9]
.
Refined bounds on
m_{r,\ell}
will be provided in Lemma
4.2
.

.
By Fact

(i)

Case 1
:

.

\displaystyle p_{F}(\bm{x})=\kappa_{F}\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{2r-\ell}\tbinom{2r-\ell}{j}x_{0}^{j}\,\sum_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{r-j})\in(\mathbb{N}_{+})_{r-j}}x_{i_{1}}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{r-j}}^{a_{r-j}}.

Claim 3.3
.

The following statements hold.

(i)

If

, then every maximizer

(ii)

Suppose to the contrary that this claim is not true (for both items).
Let
\bm{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)\in\mathrm{OPT}(F)

Fix all
x_{k}
where

\beta\coloneqq x_{i}+x_{j}
is also fixed. From (

\displaystyle p_{F}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=(x_{i}+x_{j})K_{1}+({x_{i}}^{2}+{x_{j}}^{2})K_{2}+x_{i}x_{j}K_{3}+x_{i}x_{j}(x_{i}+x_{j})K_{4}+(x_{i}x_{j})^{2}K_{5}

\displaystyle=(x_{i}+x_{j})K_{1}+\left((x_{i}+x_{j})^{2}-2x_{i}x_{j}\right)K_{2}+x_{i}x_{j}K_{3}+x_{i}x_{j}(x_{i}+x_{j})K_{4}+(x_{i}x_{j})^{2}K_{5}.

(12)

Since
a_{1}=2
and
a_{r}=1
, we have

Moreover,

if

), and

(equivalently,

Let
\alpha\coloneqq x_{i}x_{j}
.
Then

\displaystyle\psi(\alpha)\coloneqq p_{F}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=\beta K_{1}+(\beta^{2}-2\alpha)K_{2}+\alpha K_{3}+\alpha\beta K_{4}+\alpha^{2}K_{5}

\displaystyle=K_{5}\alpha^{2}+(\beta K_{4}+K_{3}-2K_{2})\alpha+(\beta K_{1}+\beta^{2}K_{2}).

Now observe that, while

is fixed, the product

to

\bm{y}

Suppose that
\ell\geq r+2
.
Then

.

\displaystyle p_{F}(\bm{x})=\psi(\alpha)<\max\left\{\psi(0),~\psi\left(\tfrac{(x_{i}+x_{j})^{2}}{4}\right)\right\}=\max\left\{p_{F}(\bm{y}),~p_{F}(\bm{z})\right\}.

p_{F}(\bm{x})
.

.

Now suppose that
\ell=r+1
.
Then
K_{5}=0

\psi(\alpha)

\alpha

\beta K_{4}+K_{3}-2K_{2}

If
\beta K_{4}+K_{3}-2K_{2}\neq 0

\displaystyle p_{F}(\bm{x})=\psi(\alpha)<\max\left\{\psi(0),~\psi\left(\tfrac{(x_{i}+x_{j})^{2}}{4}\right)\right\}=\max\left\{p_{F}(\bm{y}),~p_{F}(\bm{z})\right\},

It follows that

\displaystyle p_{F}(\bm{x})=\psi(\alpha)=\psi(0)=p_{F}(\bm{y}).

This means that

\bm{x}

(ii)

(13)

\displaystyle p_{F}(\bm{x})-p_{F}(\bm{x}^{(k)})

\displaystyle=\kappa_{F}\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{2r-\ell}\tbinom{2r-\ell}{j}(1-sy)^{j}(s)_{r-j}y^{\ell-j}

\displaystyle\qquad-\kappa_{F}\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{2r-\ell}\tbinom{2r-\ell}{j}(k)_{j}\left(\tfrac{1-sy}{k}\right)^{j}(s)_{r-j}y^{\ell-j}

\displaystyle\qquad-\kappa_{F}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-r}\sum_{j=0}^{2r-\ell}\tbinom{\ell-r}{i}\tbinom{2r-\ell}{j}(k)_{i+j}\left(\tfrac{1-sy}{k}\right)^{2i+j}(s)_{r-i-j}y^{\ell-2i-j}.

Since
\frac{(k)_{j}}{k^{j}}\to 1
and the third term goes to
0
as
k\to\infty
(because
\frac{(k)_{i+j}}{k^{2i+j}}\leq\frac{1}{k}\to 0
), we have

\displaystyle p_{F}(\bm{x})-p_{F}(\bm{x}^{(k)})\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad k\to\infty.

Combining this with Claim
3.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq\Phi(\bm{x}^{(k)})-p_{F}(\bm{x}^{(k)})

\displaystyle\leq\Phi(\bm{m})-p_{F}(\bm{x}^{(k)})

\displaystyle=p_{F}(\bm{m})-p_{F}(\bm{x}^{(k)})\leq p_{F}(\bm{x})-p_{F}(\bm{x}^{(k)})\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad k\to\infty.

This implies that both

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{(i,j)\in(\mathbb{N}_{+})_{2}}\bm{x}^{(k)}_{i}\bm{x}^{(k)}_{j}-\tfrac{m-1}{m}=0,\quad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{(i,j,k)\in(\mathbb{N}_{+})_{3}}\bm{x}^{(k)}_{i}\bm{x}^{(k)}_{j}\bm{x}^{(k)}_{k}-\tfrac{(m-1)(m-2)}{m^{2}}=0.

Simplifying this, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{m-1}{m}

\displaystyle=(1-sy)^{2}+2(1-sy)sy+s(s-1)y^{2},

\displaystyle\frac{(m-1)(m-2)}{m^{2}}

\displaystyle=(1-sy)^{3}+3(1-sy)^{2}sy+3(1-sy)s(s-1)y^{2}+s(s-1)(s-2)y^{3}.

It follows from the first equality that
y=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ms}}
.
Plugging it into the second equality we obtain

\displaystyle 1-\frac{3}{m}+\frac{2s}{(ms)^{3/2}}=\frac{(m-1)(m-2)}{m^{2}},

which implies
s=m
.
However, this means that
x_{0}=1-sy=0
, contradicting the choice of
\bm{x}
.
∎

Suppose that
\bm{x}\in\mathrm{OPT}(F)
.
It then follows from Claim
3.5
that
x_{0}=0
, and from Claim
3.4
that
\bm{x}=\bm{m}
.
This shows that
\mathrm{OPT}(F)=\{\bm{m}\}
and thereby completes the proof of Case 1.

Case 2
:
k\in[r,m-1]
.

An almost identical argument to the proof of Claim
3.3
yields the following result, so we omit the details here.

Claim 3.6
.

The following statements hold.

(i)

If
\ell\geq r+2
, then every maximizer
\bm{x}\in\mathrm{OPT}_{k+1}(F)
is balanced.

(ii)

If

defined in (

By Lemma

.
This completes the proof of Theorem

for

∎

In this subsection, we present the proof of Theorem

We will use the following inequality, which constitutes the most crucial ingredient of our proof and distinguishes our approach from those in

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
x
and
y
be two positive real numbers.
For positive integers
d,d_{1},d_{2}
define

\displaystyle\mu_{d}

\displaystyle\coloneqq(x+y)^{d}-x^{d}-y^{d},\qquad

\displaystyle\omega_{d}

\displaystyle\coloneqq x^{d}+y^{d}-2\left(\tfrac{x+y}{2}\right)^{d},

\displaystyle\psi_{d_{1},d_{2}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq x^{d_{1}}y^{d_{2}}+x^{d_{2}}y^{d_{1}},\qquad

\displaystyle\phi_{d_{1},d_{2}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2\left(\tfrac{x+y}{2}\right)^{d_{1}+d_{2}}-x^{d_{1}}y^{d_{2}}-x^{d_{2}}y^{d_{1}}.

Suppose that
x\neq y
, and let
a,b,s,t
be positive integers satisfying
b\geq t\geq s\geq a>\binom{b-a}{2}
.
Then

\frac{\omega_{t}}{\mu_{t}}\leq\frac{\omega_{a}}{\mu_{a}}<\frac{\phi_{s,t}}{\psi_{s,t}}.

(14)

Equivalently,

\displaystyle\mu_{t}\omega_{a}-\mu_{a}\omega_{t}\geq 0,\quad\text{and}\qquad\mu_{a}\phi_{s,t}-\omega_{a}\psi_{s,t}>0.

(15)

Proof.

First we prove that
\frac{\omega_{t}}{\mu_{t}}\leq\frac{\omega_{a}}{\mu_{a}}
, which follows from the following claim.

Claim 3.8
.

We have
\frac{\omega_{k+1}}{\mu_{k+1}}\leq\frac{\omega_{k}}{\mu_{k}}
for every integer
k\geq a
.

Proof.

By definition, this is equivalent to showing

\left(x^{k+1}+y^{k+1}-2\left(\tfrac{x+y}{2}\right)^{k+1}\right)\left((x+y)^{k}-x^{k}-y^{k}\right)\leq\left(x^{k}+y^{k}-2\left(\tfrac{x+y}{2}\right)^{k}\right)\left((x+y)^{k+1}-x^{k+1}-y^{k+1}\right).

Simplifying this reduces to showing that:

(x+y)^{k+1}\leq x^{k+1}+y^{k+1}+(2^{k}-1)(x^{k}y+xy^{k}).

(16)

Since for every
i\in[k]
it holds that

\displaystyle(x^{k}y+xy^{k})-(x^{i}y^{k+1-i}+x^{k+1-i}y^{i})=xy(x^{i-1}-y^{i-1})(x^{k-i}-y^{k-i})\geq 0,

it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tbinom{k+1}{i}(x^{k}y+xy^{k})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tbinom{k+1}{i}x^{i}y^{k+1-i}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tbinom{k+1}{i}x^{k+1-i}y^{i},

which implies that

\displaystyle\left(2^{k+1}-2\right)(x^{k}y+xy^{k})\geq 2\left((x+y)^{k+1}-\left(x^{k+1}+y^{k+1}\right)\right)

After dividing both sides by
2
and rearranging, we obtain (
16
).
∎

Next, we prove that
\frac{\omega_{a}}{\mu_{a}}<\frac{\phi_{s,t}}{\psi_{s,t}}
for
b\geq t\geq s\geq a\geq 2
.
By definition, this inequality is equivalent to

\displaystyle\big((x+y)^{a}-x^{a}-y^{a}\big)\left(2\left(\tfrac{x+y}{2}\right)^{s+t}-x^{s}y^{t}-x^{t}y^{s}\right)>\left(x^{a}+y^{a}-2\left(\tfrac{x+y}{2}\right)^{a}\right)\big(x^{s}y^{t}+x^{t}y^{s}\big).

Simplifying this reduces to showing that:

\displaystyle\left(\tfrac{x+y}{2}\right)^{s+t-a}\big((x+y)^{a}-x^{a}-y^{a}\big)>\left(2^{a-1}-1\right)\left(x^{s}y^{t}+x^{t}y^{s}\right).

(17)

We first prove the following auxiliary inequalities:

Claim 3.9
.

The following inequalities hold:

\displaystyle 2^{a-2}\big((x+y)^{a}-x^{a}-y^{a}\big)

\displaystyle\geq(2^{a}-2)xy(x+y)^{a-2};

(18)

\displaystyle 2\left(\tfrac{x+y}{2}\right)^{s+t-2}

\displaystyle>x^{s-1}y^{t-1}+x^{t-1}y^{s-1}.

(19)

Proof.

First, we prove (
18
).
It is straightforward to verify that (
18
) holds with equality when
a\in\{2,3\}
, so we may assume that
a\geq 4
.
For
i\in[a-1]
, let

\displaystyle M_{i}\coloneqq 2^{a-2}\tbinom{a}{i}-(2^{a}-2)\tbinom{a-2}{i-1},

noting that
M_{i}=M_{a-i}
.
Let

\displaystyle f(i)\coloneqq\tfrac{2^{a-2}\tbinom{a}{i}}{(2^{a}-2)\tbinom{a-2}{i-1}}=\tfrac{2^{a-2}a(a-1)}{(2^{a}-2)i(a-i)}.

Observe that
f(i)
is decreasing in
i
for
i\in(0,a/2)
and increasing in
i
for
i\in(a/2,a)
.
Also

\displaystyle f(1)=f(a-1)=\tfrac{2^{a-2}a}{2^{a}-2}>1\quad\text{and}\quad f(a/2)=\tfrac{2^{a-2}(a-1)}{(2^{a}-2)a}<1.

So there exists
i_{0}\in[2,a/2]
such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}M_{i}>0,&\quad\text{if}\quad i<i_{0}\mbox{ or }i>a-i_{0},\\
M_{i}\leq 0,&\quad\text{if}\quad i\in[i_{0},a-i_{0}].\end{cases}

For
i,j\in[1,a/2]
with
i<j
, we have

\displaystyle\left(x^{i}y^{a-i}+x^{a-i}y^{i}\right)-\left(x^{j}y^{a-j}+x^{a-j}y^{j}\right)=x^{i}y^{i}\left(x^{j-i}-y^{j-i}\right)\left(x^{a-i-j}-y^{a-i-j}\right)\geq 0.

Define
\gamma_{i}\coloneqq x^{i}y^{a-i}
and
\beta_{i}\coloneqq(\gamma_{i}+\gamma_{a-i})/2
for
i\in[1,a/2]
.
The inequality above shows that for
i<j\leq a/2
, we have
\gamma_{i}+\gamma_{a-i}\geq\gamma_{j}+\gamma_{a-j}
and consequently
\beta_{i}\geq\beta_{j}
.
Also note that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{a-1}M_{i}=2^{a-2}\sum_{i=1}^{a-1}\tbinom{a}{i}-\left(2^{a}-2\right)\sum_{i=1}^{a-1}\tbinom{a-2}{i-1}=2^{a-2}\left(2^{a}-2\right)-\left(2^{a}-2\right)2^{a-2}=0,

Then, using the Binomial Theorem and the symmetry
M_{i}=M_{a-i}
and
\beta_{i}=\beta_{a-i}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle 2^{a-2}\big((x+y)^{a}-x^{a}-y^{a}\big)-(2^{a}-2)xy(x+y)^{a-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{a-1}M_{i}\gamma_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{a-1}\left(M_{i}\gamma_{i}+M_{a-i}\gamma_{a-i}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{a-1}M_{i}\beta_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{i_{0}-1}M_{i}\beta_{i}+\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{a-i_{0}}M_{i}\beta_{i}+\sum_{i=a-i_{0}+1}^{a-1}M_{i}\beta_{i}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{i_{0}-1}M_{i}\right)\min_{1\leq i\leq i_{0}-1}\beta_{i}+\left(\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{a-i_{0}}M_{i}\right)\max_{i_{0}\leq i\leq a-i_{0}}\beta_{i}+\left(\sum_{i=a-i_{0}+1}^{a-1}M_{i}\right)\min_{a-i_{0}+1\leq i\leq a-1}\beta_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{i_{0}-1}M_{i}\right)\beta_{i_{0}-1}+\left(\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{a-i_{0}}M_{i}\right)\beta_{i_{0}}+\left(\sum_{i=a-i_{0}+1}^{a-1}M_{i}\right)\beta_{a-i_{0}+1}=2\left(\sum_{i=1}^{i_{0}-1}M_{i}\right)\left(\beta_{i_{0}-1}-\beta_{i_{0}}\right)\geq 0,

where the last equation uses the symmetry and the fact that
\sum_{i=1}^{a-1}M_{i}=0
.
This proves (
18
).

It remains to prove (
19
).
The argument is adapted from the proof of
[
7
, Theorem 3]
, with some modifications.
Define, for every
z\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle g(z)\coloneqq z^{t-1}(1-z)^{s-1}+z^{s-1}(1-z)^{t-1}

We first show that
g
attains its maximum uniquely at
z=1/2
.

Letting
x\coloneqq(1-z)/z
.
Straightforward calculations show that

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(z)=z^{t-1}(1-z)^{s-2}\Bigl((s-1)x^{t-s+1}-(t-1)x^{t-s}+(t-1)x-s+1\Bigr),

Define

\displaystyle h(x)\coloneqq(s-1)x^{t-s+1}-(t-1)x^{t-s}+(t-1)x-s+1

Let
y\coloneqq x-1
. Then for
x\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle h(x)

\displaystyle=(s-1)(1+y)^{t-s+1}-(t-1)(1+y)^{t-s}+(t-1)(1+y)-s+1

\displaystyle=\Bigl((s-1)(t-s+1)-(t-1)(t-s)+(t-1)\Bigr)y+\sum_{i=2}^{t-s+1}\bigl((s-1)\tbinom{t-s+1}{i}-(t-1)\tbinom{t-s}{i}\bigr)y^{i}

\displaystyle=2\Bigl(s-1-\tbinom{t-s}{2}\Bigr)y+\sum_{i=2}^{t-s+1}\tbinom{t-s}{i-1}\frac{1}{i}\Bigl((s-1)(t-s+1)-(t-1)(t-s-i+1)\Bigr)y^{i}

\displaystyle\geq 2\Bigl(s-1-\tbinom{t-s}{2}\Bigr)y+\sum_{i=2}^{t-s+1}\tbinom{t-s}{i-1}\frac{1}{i}\Bigl((s-1)(t-s+1)-(t-1)(t-s-1)\Bigr)y^{i}

\displaystyle=2\Bigl(s-1-\tbinom{t-s}{2}\Bigr)y+\sum_{i=2}^{t-s+1}\tbinom{t-s}{i-1}\frac{2}{i}\Bigl(s-1-\tbinom{t-s}{2}\Bigr)y^{i}\geq 0,

where in the last inequality we used the assumption that
\binom{b-a}{2}\leq a-1
(and thus,
s-1\geq a-1\geq\binom{b-a}{2}\geq\binom{t-s}{2}
).

It follows that
g^{\prime}(z)\geq 0
for
z\in(0,1/2)
, and hence,
g
is nondecreasing on
[0,1/2]
.
Since
g
is symmetric around
z=1/2
and is a polynomial which is not linear (since
s,t\geq 2
), it attains its maximum on the interval
[0,1]
uniquely at
z=1/2
.
Therefore, (
19
) holds.
∎

Combining (
18
) and (
19
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\tfrac{x+y}{2}\right)^{s+t-a}\big((x+y)^{a}-x^{a}-y^{a}\big)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\tfrac{x+y}{2}\right)^{s+t-a}\left(2^{a}-2\right)xy\left(\tfrac{x+y}{2}\right)^{a-2}

\displaystyle>\tfrac{2^{a}-2}{2}xy\left(x^{s-1}y^{t-1}+x^{t-1}y^{s-1}\right)=\left(2^{a-1}-1\right)\left(x^{s}y^{t}+x^{t}y^{s}\right),

which proves (
17
).
This completes the proof of Proposition
3.7
.
∎

.

(see Definition

), no induced copy of

.
In addition, define
\tilde{\bm{x}}
by replacing both

and

\bm{a}\coloneqq(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
.
Recall the definition of

We shall prove the following key inequality:

.

We have

\displaystyle S_{\bm{a}}(\bm{x})<\max\big\{S_{\bm{a}}(\bm{y}),~S_{\bm{a}}(\bm{z})\}.

Consequently,
p_{F}(\bm{x})<\max\big\{p_{F}(\bm{y}),~p_{F}(\bm{z})\}
.

For

, let

be the

-tuple obtained from

4.1
Preparations

In this subsection, we briefly review the sufficient conditions for perfect stability in the framework developed by
[

. Their framework applies to the broader class of “symmetrizable” functions, but here we focus specifically on the inducibility problem for complete multipartite graphs

[

\mathrm{OPT}(F)
is finite and each maximizer
\bm{x}\in\mathrm{OPT}(F)
satisfies certain
strictness
conditions, then the inducibility problem for
F
is perfectly stable.

For convenience, we will use the following simplified variant of
[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
(see also
[
3
, Theorem 8.2]
).

For a graph
G
and a pair
\{x,y\}\subseteq V(G)
of vertices, let
G\oplus xy
denote the graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)\bigtriangleup\{xy\}
.
That is,
G\oplus\{xy\}
is obtained by adding the edge
\{x,y\}
if it is not present in
G
, and by removing it otherwise.
Suppose that
G
is a complete
t
-partite graph with parts
V_{1},\ldots,V_{t}
.
For every
A\subseteq[t]
, let
G_{A}
denote the graph obtained from
G
by adding a new vertex that is adjacent precisely to all vertices in
\bigcup_{i\in A}V_{i}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
F
be a complete multipartite graph with
\ell
vertices.
Suppose that
\mathrm{OPT}(F)
contains a unique sequence
\bm{x}
with
x_{0}=0
(i.e.
\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}x_{i}=1
) and
t\coloneqq|\mathrm{supp}(\bm{x})|<\infty
.
Suppose that there exist constants
\varepsilon>0
and
N_{0}
such that for every
n\geq N_{0}
the following statements hold for the
n
-vertex realization
G\coloneqq G_{n,\bm{x}}
with parts
V_{1},\ldots,V_{t}\colon

(S1)

For every pair
\{x,y\}\subseteq V(G)
, we have

\displaystyle I(F,G)-I(F,G\oplus xy)\geq\varepsilon n^{\ell-2}.

(S2)

For every
A\subseteq[t]
with
|A|\neq t-1
, we have

\min\big\{I(F,G_{A^{\ast}})\colon A^{\ast}\subseteq[t]\text{ and }|A^{\ast}|=t-1\big\}-I(F,G_{A})\geq\varepsilon n^{\ell-1}.

Then the inducibility problem for
F
is perfectly stable.

We also need the following refined estimates for
m_{r,\ell}
, the integer given by Lemma
3.2
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\ell>r\geq 2
be integers and let
m=m_{r,\ell}
.
Then

(i)

m>\max\left\{\frac{\ell(r-1)}{2(\ell-r)},~r-1\right\}
, and

(ii)

m<\min\left\{\frac{\ell(\ell-1)}{2(\ell-r)},~\frac{r}{\alpha}\right\}
, where
\alpha\coloneqq\alpha(\ell/r)\in(1-r^{2}/\ell^{2},1)
is the unique positive real root of the equation
\mathrm{e}^{(\ell/r)\cdot x}(1-x)=1
.
In particular,
m<\frac{r\ell^{2}}{\ell^{2}-r^{2}}
.

Proof.

First, we prove
(i)
.
Since
m\geq r
holds trivially, it suffices to show that
m>\frac{\ell(r-1)}{2(\ell-r)}
, equivalently,
\frac{1}{m+1}<\frac{2(\ell-r)}{r\ell+\ell-2r}
.
Note that
\frac{\ell(r-1)}{2(\ell-r)}
is decreasing in
\ell>r
, and when
\ell=2r
, we have
\frac{\ell(r-1)}{2(\ell-r)}<r
.
Thus we may assume that
r<\ell<2r
.

By Lemma
3.2
, the discrete function
f
defined in (
3
) is unimodal.
Therefore, the optimality of
m
implies that
f(m)>f(m+1)
, which yields

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{m+1}\right)^{\ell}<1-\frac{r}{m+1}.

Define
g(x)\coloneqq(1-x)^{\ell}+rx-1
for
x\in[0,1]
.
Then the inequality above implies that
g\left(\tfrac{1}{m+1}\right)<0
.

Straightforward calculations show that the second derivative of
g
is
g^{\prime\prime}(x)=\ell(\ell-1)(1-x)^{\ell-2}
, which is nonnegative for
x\in[0,1]
.
Thus, the function
g
is convex on the interval
[0,1]
.
Since
g(0)=0
and
g\left(\frac{1}{m+1}\right)<0
, to show that
\frac{1}{m+1}<\frac{2(\ell-r)}{r\ell+\ell-2r}
, it suffices to prove the following claim.

Claim 4.3
.

We have
g\left(\frac{2(\ell-r)}{r\ell+\ell-2r}\right)\geq 0
.

Proof.

It is equivalent to show that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(r-1)\ell}{(r+1)\ell-2r}\right)^{\ell}=\left(1-\frac{2(\ell-r)}{r\ell+\ell-2r}\right)^{\ell}\geq 1-\frac{2r(\ell-r)}{r\ell+\ell-2r}=\frac{(2r-\ell)(r-1)}{(r+1)\ell-2r}.

Taking logarithms on both sides and then rearranging the terms, this simplifies to showing that

\displaystyle\ell\ln\ell+(\ell-1)\ln(r-1)-(\ell-1)\ln\big((r+1)\ell-2r\big)-\ln(2r-\ell)\geq 0.

(24)

Define

\displaystyle\vartheta(x)\coloneqq x\ln x+(x-1)\ln(r-1)-(x-1)\ln\big((r+1)x-2r\big)-\ln(2r-x),\quad\text{for}\quad x\in[r,2r).

Straightforward calculations show that

\displaystyle\vartheta(r)=\vartheta^{\prime}(r)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\vartheta^{\prime\prime}(x)=\frac{4r^{2}(x-r)\bigl((r+2)x-4r\bigr)}{\bigl((r+1)x-2r\bigr)^{2}(2r-x)^{2}x}.

For
x\in[r,2r)
and
r\geq 2
, we have
(r+2)x-4r\geq(r+2)r-4r=r(r-2)\geq 0
.
Hence
\vartheta^{\prime\prime}(x)\geq 0
.
It follows that
\vartheta(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in[r,2r)
, which implies (
24
). This completes the proof of Claim
4.3
.
∎

It remains to prove
(ii)
. Let
t\coloneqq\ell/r
and
h_{t}(x)\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{tx}(1-x)-1
.
First we show that the real number
\alpha
defined in the lemma lies in the interval
(1-1/t^{2},1)
.
Note that the derivative
h_{t}^{\prime}(x)=\mathrm{e}^{tx}(t-1-tx)
is positive when
x<1-1/t
and negative when
x>1-1/t
.
Thus,
h_{t}
is unimodal on
(0,\infty)
.
Since
h_{t}(0)=0
and
h_{t}(1)=-1
, the equation
h_{t}(x)=1
has a unique positive root (i.e.
\alpha
), which lies in the interval
(0,1)
.

To show that
\alpha>1-1/t^{2}
, it suffices to verify that the following quantity is positive:

\displaystyle h_{t}(1-1/t^{2})=\frac{1}{t^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{t-\frac{1}{t}}-1.

Let
u(t)\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{t-\frac{1}{t}}/t^{2}
. Since the derivative
u^{\prime}(t)=u(t)(1-1/t)^{2}
is positive for
t>1
, the function
u
is strictly increasing on
(1,\infty)
. Consequently,
h_{t}(1-1/t^{2})=u(t)-1>u(1)-1=0
for
t>1
, as desired.
This proves that
\alpha\in(1-1/t^{2},1)
.

Next, we prove the upper bound for
m
.
Since
\alpha<1
and straightforward calculations show that
\frac{\ell(\ell-1)}{2(\ell-r)}>r
for all
\ell>r
, we may assume that
m>r
(otherwise, we are done).

By Lemma
3.2
, the discrete function
f
defined in (
3
) is unimodal.
Therefore, the optimality of
m
implies that
f(m)>f(m-1)
, which yields

\displaystyle\frac{m-r}{m}<\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)^{\ell}.

(25)

Combining it with the inequality

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)^{\ell}\leq 1-\frac{\ell}{m}+\binom{\ell}{2}\left(\frac{1}{m}\right)^{2},

we obtain
m<\frac{\ell(\ell-1)}{2(\ell-r)}
.

It remains to prove that
m<r/\alpha
.
Define
\theta\coloneqq r/m
.
Then it follows from (
25
) and the inequality
\ln(1+x)\leq x
for
x>-1
that

\displaystyle 1-\theta=\frac{m-r}{m}<\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)^{\ell}=\exp\left(\ell\ln\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\ell}{m}\right)=\mathrm{e}^{-t\theta}.

In other words, we have
h_{t}(\theta)<0
.
Since
h_{t}
is unimodal on
(0,\infty)
and
h_{t}(0)=0
, we have
\theta>\alpha
, which implies that
m<r/\alpha
.
This proves
(ii)
, and thus completing the proof of Lemma
4.2
.
∎

4.2
Proof of Theorem
1.5
for
\ell<2r

In this subsection, we present the proof of Theorem
1.5
for
\ell<2r
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\ell,r
be integers such that
r\geq 2
and
r+1\leq\ell\leq 2r-1
.
Let
m=m_{r,\ell}
be the integer given by Lemma
3.2
.
Then the discrete function
h\colon[0,m]\to\mathbb{Z}
defined by

\displaystyle h(q)\coloneqq(q)_{r-1}\cdot\bigl(2r-\ell+2(m-q)(\ell-r)\bigr),\quad\text{for every}\quad q\in[0,m],

satisfies
h(q)<h(m-1)
for all integers
q\in[0,m]\setminus\{m-1\}
.

Proof.

Note that
h(m-1)=\ell(m-1)_{r-1}>0
. So it suffices to prove that for every
q\in[r-1,m]\setminus\{m-1\}
,

\displaystyle h(q)=(q)_{r-1}\cdot\bigl(2r-\ell+2(m-q)(\ell-r)\bigr)<\ell(m-1)_{r-1}.

(26)

For
q=m
, inequality (
26
) becomes
(m)_{r-1}(2r-\ell)<\ell(m-1)_{r-1}
, which simplifies to
2(\ell-r)m>\ell(r-1)
.
This follows from the lower bound on
m
given by Lemma
4.2

(i)
, and hence this case holds.
It remains to show
h(q)<h(m-1)
for every
q\in[r,m-2]
.

Claim 4.5
.

Suppose that
q\in[r,m-1]
. Then
h(q)-h(q-1)>0
.

Proof.

First, we consider the case
q=m-1
.
In this case we need to show
(m-2)_{r-1}(3\ell-2r)<\ell(m-1)_{r-1}
, which is equivalent to
2(\ell-r)m<3r\ell-2r^{2}-\ell
.
Since
m<\frac{\ell(\ell-1)}{2(\ell-r)}
(by Lemma
4.2

(ii)
), we have

\displaystyle 2(\ell-r)m-(3r\ell-2r^{2}-\ell)<2(\ell-r)\cdot\frac{\ell(\ell-1)}{2(\ell-r)}-(3r\ell-2r^{2}-\ell)=(\ell-r)(\ell-2r)<0.

This proves that
h(m-2)<h(m-1)
.

Next, we consider the case
q\in[r,m-2]
.
It follows from
m<\frac{\ell(\ell-1)}{2(\ell-r)}
(by Lemma
4.2

(ii)
) that

\displaystyle 2(\ell-r)(3r-m-1)+(r-1)(2r-\ell)

\displaystyle>2(\ell-r)\left(3r-1-\tfrac{\ell(\ell-1)}{2(\ell-r)}\right)+(r-1)(2r-\ell)

\displaystyle=2(\ell-r)(3r-1)-\ell(\ell-1)+(r-1)(2r-\ell)

\displaystyle=(\ell-r)(4r-\ell)>0.

Since
q\leq m-2
, we have

\displaystyle(r-1)(m+1)-rq\geq(r-1)(m+1)-r(m-2)=3r-1-m.

Consequently,

\displaystyle 2(\ell-r)\bigl((r-1)(m+1)-rq\bigr)+(r-1)(2r-\ell)\geq 2(\ell-r)(3r-m-1)+(r-1)(2r-\ell)>0.

(27)

Now, fix
q\in[r,m-2]
, and let
\delta(q)\coloneqq h(q)-h(q-1)
. Then

\displaystyle\delta(q)

\displaystyle=(q)_{r-1}\bigl(2r-\ell+2(m-q)(\ell-r)\bigr)-(q-1)_{r-1}\bigl(2r-\ell+2(m-q+1)(\ell-r)\bigr)

\displaystyle=(q-1)_{r-2}\left(q\bigl(2r-\ell+2(m-q)(\ell-r)\bigr)-(q-r+1)\bigl(2r-\ell+2(m-q+1)(\ell-r)\bigr)\right)

\displaystyle=(q-1)_{r-2}\left(2(\ell-r)\bigl((r-1)(m+1)-rq\bigr)+(r-1)(2r-\ell)\right)>0,

where the last inequality follows from (
27
).
This proves Claim
4.5
.
∎

It follows from Claim
4.5
that
h(q)<h(m-1)
for every
q\in[r,m-2]
.
This proves Lemma
4.4
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
for
\ell<2r
.

Let

be an almost balanced complete

-partite graph on

By Fact

(i)
, we have
a_{1}=\cdots=a_{\ell-r}=2

a_{\ell-r+1}=\cdots=a_{r}=1

Let
\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle\varepsilon\leq\left(2m^{\ell+1}{2^{\ell-r}(\ell-r)!(2r-\ell)!}\right)^{-1}.

It follows from Theorem
3.1
that
\mathrm{OPT}(F)=\{\bm{m}\}
.
Thus, by Theorem

) and (

Fix a sufficiently large integer

) for all distinct

Claim 4.6
.

Suppose that
(x,y)\in V_{i}\times V_{j}
for distinct

I(F,G)-I(F,G\oplus xy)\geq\varepsilon n^{\ell-2}.

By symmetry, we may assume that

.
Since

-partite.

Let
\mathcal{C}

.
Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1,1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{T\in\mathcal{C}\colon\left(|V_{1}\cap V(T)|,|V_{2}\cap V(T)|\right)=(1,1)\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1,2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{T\in\mathcal{C}\colon\left(|V_{1}\cap V(T)|,|V_{2}\cap V(T)|\right)=(1,2)\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{2,1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{T\in\mathcal{C}\colon\left(|V_{1}\cap V(T)|,|V_{2}\cap V(T)|\right)=(2,1)\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{2,2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{T\in\mathcal{C}\colon\left(|V_{1}\cap V(T)|,|V_{2}\cap V(T)|\right)=(2,2)\right\},

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{1,1}|

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0,&\quad\text{if}\quad\ell=2r-1\\[1.99997pt]
\frac{(1+o(1))(m-2)_{r-2}}{2^{\ell-r}(\ell-r)!(2r-\ell-2)!}k^{\ell-2}&\quad\text{if}\quad\ell\leq 2r-2\end{cases}\quad=\frac{(1+o(1))(m-2)_{r-2}}{2^{\ell-r}(\ell-r)!(2r-\ell)!}(2r-\ell)(2r-1-\ell)k^{\ell-2}.

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{1,2}|+|\mathcal{C}_{1,2}|

\displaystyle=2\cdot I\big(K_{a_{2},\ldots,a_{r-1}},\tilde{G}\big)=\frac{(1+o(1))(m-2)_{r-2}}{2^{\ell-r-2}(\ell-r-1)!(2r-\ell-1)!}k^{\ell-2},

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}|

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{C}_{1,1}|+|\mathcal{C}_{1,2}|+|\mathcal{C}_{1,2}|+|\mathcal{C}_{2,2}|

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+o(1))(m-2)_{r-2}}{2^{\ell-r}(\ell-r)!(2r-\ell)!}\left((2r-\ell)(2r-1-\ell)+4(\ell-r)(2r-\ell)+4(\ell-r)(\ell-r-1)\right)k^{\ell-2}

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))\frac{(m-2)_{r-2}(\ell^{2}-3\ell+2r)}{2^{\ell-r}(\ell-r)!(2r-\ell)!}k^{\ell-2}.

(28)

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}

.

.

).

.

\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{q}^{2}

I(K_{a_{2},\ldots,a_{r}},K[V_{1},\ldots,V_{q})\cdot\sum_{i=q+1}^{m}|V_{i}|
, which, by Fact
2.2
, is

\displaystyle\frac{(1+o(1))(q)_{r-1}}{2^{\ell-r-1}(\ell-r-1)!(2r-\ell)!}k^{\ell-2}\cdot(m-q)k=\frac{(1+o(1))(m-q)(q)_{r-1}}{2^{\ell-r-1}(\ell-r-1)!(2r-\ell)!}k^{\ell-1}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{q}|=|\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{q}^{1}|+|\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{q}^{2}|

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+o(1))(q)_{r-1}}{2^{\ell-r}(\ell-r)!(2r-\ell-1)!}k^{\ell-1}+\frac{(1+o(1))(m-q)(q)_{r-1}}{2^{\ell-r-1}(\ell-r-1)!(2r-\ell)!}k^{\ell-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+o(1))(q)_{r-1}}{2^{\ell-r}(\ell-r)!(2r-\ell)!}\big((2r-\ell)+2(m-q)(\ell-r-1)\big)k^{\ell-1}=\frac{(1+o(1))h(q)}{2^{\ell-r}(\ell-r)!(2r-\ell)!}k^{\ell-1}.

This proves Claim
4.8
.
∎

Fix an arbitrary set

with
|A|\neq m-1
.
Let

\displaystyle\Delta\coloneqq\min\big\{I(F,G_{A^{\ast}})\colon A^{\ast}\subseteq[m]\text{ and }|A^{\ast}|=m-1\big\}-I(F,G_{A}).

Then it follows from Claim

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+o(1))h(m-1)}{2^{\ell-r}(\ell-r)!(2r-\ell)!}k^{\ell-1}-\frac{(1+o(1))h(|A|)}{2^{\ell-r}(\ell-r)!(2r-\ell)!}k^{\ell-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{h(m-1)-h(|A|)}{2^{\ell-r}(\ell-r)!(2r-\ell)!}k^{\ell-1}+o(k^{\ell-1})\geq\frac{1}{2^{\ell-r}(\ell-r)!(2r-\ell)!}k^{\ell-1}+o(k^{\ell-1})\geq\varepsilon n^{\ell-1}.

This shows that (
S2
) holds.
So it follows from Theorem
4.1

is perfectly stable, which completes the proof of Theorem

∎

Lemma 4.9

Let

Let

Then the discrete function

\displaystyle H(q)\coloneqq(m-q)(q)_{r-1},\quad\text{for every}\quad q\in[0,m],

Proof.

The inequality

For the remaining case, it suffices to show that

It follows from Lemma

(ii)
that

\displaystyle m<\frac{r\ell^{2}}{\ell^{2}-r^{2}}\leq\frac{r(2r)^{2}}{(2r)^{2}-r^{2}}=\frac{4r}{3}.

(29)

Fix

\displaystyle H(q)-H(q-1)

\displaystyle=(m-q)(q)_{r-1}-(m-q+1)(q-1)_{r-1}

\displaystyle\geq(q-1)_{r-2}\big(3r-1-m\big)>(q-1)_{r-2}\left(\tfrac{5r}{3}-1\right)>0.

Here, in the first inequality we used the assumption that

29
).
This completes the proof of Lemma

∎

5
Exact results and uniqueness of the extremal construction

In this section, we show that, for large
n
, the extremal graph for
I(F,n)
is the Turán graph if
F
is an almost balanced complete multipartite graph, thus completing the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
We begin with the following two lemmas.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
t\geq s\geq 1
be integers. Suppose
x,y,N
are real numbers satisfying
y>x+1
and
\max\{|x-N|,~|y-N|\}=o(N)
as
N\to\infty
.
Then

\displaystyle\tbinom{x+1}{s}+\tbinom{y-1}{s}-\tbinom{x}{s}-\tbinom{y}{s}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{s(s-1)}{s!}(y-x-1)N^{s-2}+o(N^{s-2});

(32)

\displaystyle\tbinom{x+1}{t}\tbinom{y-1}{s}+\tbinom{x+1}{s}\tbinom{y-1}{t}-\tbinom{x}{t}\tbinom{y}{s}-\tbinom{x}{s}\tbinom{y}{t}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{t+s-(t-s)^{2}}{s!t!}\big(y-x-1\big)N^{t+s-2}+o(N^{t+s-2}).

(33)

Proof.

We start with the first equality.
The case
s=1
is trivially true, so we may assume that
s\geq 2
.
Let
\phi\colon[s-1,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be the function defined by
\phi(z)=\binom{z}{s-1}
for all
z\in[s-1,\infty)
.
By the Mean Value Theorem, there exists
z_{0}\in[x,y-1]
(hence
|z_{0}-N|=o(N)
) such that

\displaystyle\tbinom{x+1}{s}+\tbinom{y-1}{s}-\tbinom{x}{s}-\tbinom{y}{s}=\tbinom{x}{s-1}-\tbinom{y-1}{s-1}

\displaystyle=(x-y+1)\phi^{\prime}(z_{0})

\displaystyle=(x-y+1)\left(\tfrac{z_{0}^{s-2}}{(s-2)!}+o\left(z_{0}^{s-2}\right)\right)=-\tfrac{y-x-1}{(s-2)!}N^{s-2}+o(N^{s-2}),

as desired.

We now consider the second equality.
Let

\displaystyle g(x,y)\coloneqq(x+1)(y-s)_{t-s+1}+(x+1)(y-s)(x-s+1)_{t-s}-(x-s+1)y(y-s)_{t-s}-y(x-s+1)_{t-s+1}.

Claim 5.2
.

We have
g(x,y)=\left(t+s-(t-s)^{2}\right)(y-x-1)N^{t-s}+o(N^{t-s})
.

Proof.

The case
t-s\in\{0,1\}
follows directly from the following fact (which itself follows from straightforward calculations):

\displaystyle g(x,y)=\begin{cases}2s(y-x-1),&\quad\text{if}\quad t-s=0,\\
s(y-x-1)(x+y-2s),&\quad\text{if}\quad t-s=1.\end{cases}

So we may assume that
t-s\geq 2
.
Simplifying
g(x,y)
, we obtain

\displaystyle g(x,y)=s\bigl(y(y-s)_{t-s}-(x+1)(x+1-s)_{t-s}\bigr)-t(x+1)y\Bigl(\tfrac{(y-s)_{t-s}}{y}-\tfrac{(x+1-s)_{t-s}}{x+1}\Bigr).

Let
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\colon[t-1,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be functions defined by

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(z)=z(z-s)_{t-s}\quad\text{and}\quad\psi_{2}(z)=\frac{(z-s)_{t-s}}{z}\quad\text{for all}\quad z\in[t-1,\infty).

By the Mean Value Theorem, there exist
z_{1},z_{2}\in[x,y-1]
such that

\displaystyle y(y-s)_{t-s}-(x+1)(x+1-s)_{t-s}

\displaystyle=(y-x-1)\psi_{1}^{\prime}(z_{1})

\displaystyle=(y-x-1)\left((t-s+1)z_{1}^{t-s}+o(z^{t-s})\right)

\displaystyle=(y-x-1)(t-s+1)N^{t-s}+o(N^{t-s}),

and

\displaystyle\frac{(y-s)_{t-s}}{y}-\frac{(x+1-s)_{t-s}}{x+1}

\displaystyle=(y-x-1)\psi_{2}^{\prime}(z_{2})

\displaystyle=(y-x-1)\left((t-s-1)z_{2}^{t-s-2}+o(z^{t-s-2})\right)

\displaystyle=(y-x-1)(t-s-1)N^{t-s-2}+o(N^{t-s-2}).

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle g(x,y)

\displaystyle=s(y-x-1)(t-s+1)N^{t-s}+t(x+1)y(y-x-1)(t-s-1)N^{t-s-2}+o(N^{t-s})

\displaystyle=\left(t+s-(t-s)^{2}\right)(y-x-1)N^{t-s}+o(N^{t-s}),

which proves Claim
5.2
.
∎

It follows from Claim
5.2
that

\displaystyle\tbinom{x+1}{t}\tbinom{y-1}{s}+\tbinom{x+1}{s}\tbinom{y-1}{t}-\tbinom{x}{t}\tbinom{y}{s}-\tbinom{x}{s}\tbinom{y}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{(x)_{s-1}(y-1)_{s-1}}{t!\,s!}\cdot g(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{t+s-(t-s)^{2}}{s!t!}\bigl(y-x-1\bigr)N^{t+s-2}+o(N^{t+s-2}),

which completes the proof of Lemma
5.1
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
F=K_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}}
be an almost balanced complete
r
-partite graph with
a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{r}
, and let
\ell\coloneqq a_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}
.
Let
m\coloneqq m_{r,\ell}
be the constant given by Lemma
3.2
.
Then

\displaystyle\binom{\ell}{2}>m\sum_{k\in[r]}\binom{a_{k}}{2}.

Proof.

Suppose that
a_{r}=1
.
Then it follows from Fact
2.7

(i)
that
a_{1}=\cdots=a_{\ell-r}=2
and
a_{\ell-r+1}=\cdots=a_{r}=1
.
Combining this with
m<\frac{\ell(\ell-1)}{2(\ell-r)}
(by Lemma
4.2

(ii)
), we obtain

\displaystyle m\sum_{k\in[r]}\binom{a_{k}}{2}=m(\ell-r)<\frac{\ell(\ell-1)}{2(\ell-r)}(\ell-r)=\binom{\ell}{2}.

Now suppose that
a_{r}\geq 2
(consequently,
\ell\geq 2r
).
Observe that

\displaystyle r\sum_{k\in[r]}\binom{a_{k}}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(r\sum_{k\in[r]}a_{k}^{2}-r\sum_{k\in[r]}a_{k}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big((a_{1}+\cdots+a_{r})^{2}+\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[r]}{2}}(a_{i}-a_{j})^{2}-r\ell\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\ell^{2}+\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[r]}{2}}(a_{i}-a_{j})^{2}-r\ell\Big).

(34)

For every
\{i,j\}\subseteq[r]
, it follows from the almost balanced assumption that

\displaystyle\binom{a_{i}-a_{j}}{2}\leq\binom{a_{1}-a_{r}}{2}\leq a_{r}-1\leq a_{j}-1,

which implies that

(a_{i}-a_{j})^{2}\leq a_{i}+a_{j}-2.

(35)

First, we consider the case
r\leq 3
.
Since
\ell\geq 2r\geq\frac{\ln(r+1)}{\ln(1+1/r)}
for
r\in\{2,3\}
, the integer
m_{r,\ell}
given by Lemma
3.2
satisfies that
m_{r,\ell}=r
.
Combining this with (
5
) and (
35
), we obtain

\displaystyle m\sum_{k\in[r]}\binom{a_{k}}{2}=r\sum_{k\in[r]}\binom{a_{k}}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\ell^{2}+\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[r]}{2}}(a_{i}-a_{j})^{2}-r\ell\Big)\leq\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\ell^{2}+\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[r]}{2}}(a_{i}+a_{j}-2)-r\ell\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\ell^{2}+(r-1)\ell-r(r-1)-r\ell\bigr)=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\ell^{2}-\ell-r(r-1)\bigr)<\binom{\ell}{2},

as desired. So it remains to prove the case when
r\geq 4
.

Claim 5.4
.

We have
\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[r]}{2}}(a_{i}-a_{j})^{2}\leq(r-1)\ell-r^{2}
.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[r]}{2}}(a_{i}-a_{j})^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[2,r-1]}{2}}(a_{i}-a_{j})^{2}+(a_{1}-a_{r})^{2}+\sum_{i\in[2,r-1]}\big((a_{1}-a_{i})^{2}+(a_{i}-a_{r})^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[2,r-1]}{2}}(a_{i}-a_{j})^{2}+(a_{1}-a_{r})^{2}+\sum_{i\in[2,r-1]}(a_{1}-a_{r})^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[2,r-1]}{2}}(a_{i}-a_{j})^{2}+(r-1)(a_{1}-a_{r})^{2},

where the inequality follows from the fact that, for all
x\geq y\geq z\geq 0
,

\displaystyle(x-y)^{2}+(y-z)^{2}=\big((x-y)+(x-z)\big)^{2}-2(x-y)(y-z)\leq(x-z)^{2}.

Repeating this argument for
\sum_{{i,j}\in\binom{[2,r-1]}{2}}(a_{i}-a_{j})^{2}
, and continuing similarly, then applying (
35
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[r]}{2}}(a_{i}-a_{j})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(r-1)(a_{1}-a_{r})^{2}+(r-3)(a_{2}-a_{r-1})^{2}+\cdots+\big(r-2\lfloor\tfrac{r}{2}\rfloor+1\big)\big(a_{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}-a_{r+1-\lfloor r/2\rfloor}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}(r-2i+1)(a_{i}-a_{r+1-i})^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}(r-2i+1)(a_{i}+a_{r+1-i}-2).

So it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}(r-2i+1)(a_{i}+a_{r+1-i}-2)\leq(r-1)\ell-r^{2}.

Suppose that
r=2k
for some
k\geq 2
.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}(r-2i+1)(a_{i}+a_{r+1-i}-2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in[k]}(r-1)(a_{i}+a_{r+1-i}-2)-\sum_{i\in[k]}(2i-2)(a_{i}+a_{r+1-i}-2)

\displaystyle\leq(r-1)(\ell-r)-\sum_{i\in[k]}(2i-2)(2+2-2)

\displaystyle=(r-1)(\ell-r)-\frac{r(r-2)}{2}=(r-1)\ell-r^{2}-\frac{r(r-4)}{2}\leq(r-1)\ell-r^{2},

as desired.

Now suppose that
r=2k-1
for some
k\geq 3
.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}(r-2i+1)(a_{i}+a_{r+1-i}-2)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{i\in[k-1]}(r-1)(a_{i}+a_{r+1-i}-2)-\sum_{i\in[k-1]}(2i-2)(a_{i}+a_{r+1-i}-2)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(r-1)(a_{i}-1)-(r-1)(a_{k}-1)-\sum_{i\in[k-1]}(2i-2)(a_{i}+a_{r+1-i}-2)

\displaystyle\quad\leq(r-1)(\ell-r)-(r-1)(2-1)-\sum_{i\in[k-1]}(2i-2)(2+2-2)

\displaystyle\quad=(r-1)(\ell-r)-(r-1)-\frac{1}{2}\left(r^{2}-4r+3\right)=(r-1)\ell-r^{2}-\frac{r(r-4)+1}{2}\leq(r-1)\ell-r^{2},

also as desired.
This completes the proof of Claim
5.4
.
∎

It follows from (
5
), Claim
5.4
, and the inequality
m<\frac{r\ell^{2}}{\ell^{2}-r^{2}}
(by Lemma
4.2

(ii)
) that

\displaystyle r\sum_{k\in[r]}\tbinom{a_{k}}{2}=\frac{m}{r}\cdot r\sum_{k\in[r]}\tbinom{a_{k}}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{r}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\Big(\ell^{2}+\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[r]}{2}}(a_{i}-a_{j})^{2}-r\ell\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\ell^{2}}{\ell^{2}-r^{2}}\Big(\ell^{2}+(r-1)\ell-r^{2}-r\ell\Big)=\binom{\ell}{2}-\frac{r^{2}\ell}{2(\ell^{2}-r^{2})}<\binom{\ell}{2}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
5.3
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in[r]}A_{i}\cdot f_{i}(x,y)+\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[r]}{2}}B_{i,j}\cdot g_{i,j}(x,y).

(36)

It follows from (

) that

\displaystyle A_{i}\cdot f_{i}(x,y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{a_{i}(a_{i}-1)}{a_{i}!}(y-x-1+o(1))N^{a_{i}-2}\cdot\left(\frac{(m-2)_{r-1}\cdot a_{i}!}{a_{1}!\cdots a_{r}!}+o(1)\right)N^{\ell-a_{i}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i\in[r]}(m-r)(a_{i}^{2}-a_{i})+\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[r]}{2}}\left(a_{i}+a_{j}-a_{i}^{2}-a_{j}^{2}+2a_{i}a_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in[r]}\left(-(m-r)(a_{i}^{2}-a_{i})-(r-1)(a_{i}^{2}-a_{i})\right)+\sum_{i\in[r]}a_{i}\sum_{j\in[r]\setminus\{i\}}a_{j}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i\in[r]}(m-1)(a_{i}^{2}-a_{i})+\sum_{i\in[r]}a_{i}(\ell-a_{i})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i\in[r]}m(a_{i}^{2}-a_{i})+\sum_{i\in[r]}(\ell-1)a_{i}=2\Big(\tbinom{\ell}{2}-m\sum_{i\in[r]}\tbinom{a_{i}}{2}\Big).

Combining this with (

6
Concluding remarks

Let
F
be a complete
r
-graph on
\ell\geq r+1
vertices. Observe that if a maximizer
\bm{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)\in\mathrm{OPT}(F)
satisfies
x_{0}=0
and
x_{i}=x_{j}
for all
{i,j}\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\bm{x})
, then necessarily
\bm{x}=\bm{m}_{r,\ell}
, where
m_{r,\ell}
is the integer given by Lemma
3.2
.

A natural next step beyond Theorem
1.1
is to understand to what extent the “almost balanced” hypothesis can be relaxed.

Problem 6.1
.

For all integers
\ell>r\geq 3
, characterize the complete
r
-partite graphs
F
on
\ell
vertices for which
\mathrm{OPT}(F)=\{\bm{m}_{r,\ell}\}
.

We remark that a necessary condition for a complete
r
-partite graph
F=K_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}}
on
\ell
vertices satisfying
\mathrm{OPT}(F)=\{\bm{m}_{r,\ell}\}
is given by the following inequality, which is slightly weaker than that in Lemma
5.3
:

\displaystyle\binom{\ell}{2}\geq m_{r,\ell}\sum_{k\in[r]}\binom{a_{k}}{2}.

(42)

Indeed, let
m=m_{r,\ell}
.
If (
42
) fails, consider the perturbation (for a sufficiently small constant
\delta>0
)

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{\delta}\coloneqq\big(\tfrac{1+\delta}{m},\underbrace{\tfrac{1}{m},\ldots,\tfrac{1}{m}}_{m-2},\tfrac{1-\delta}{m},0,\ldots\big).

By (
8
), we have

\displaystyle p_{F}(\bm{x}_{\delta})=\frac{\kappa_{F}(m-2)_{r-2}}{m^{\ell}}\bigl((m-r)(m-r-1)+(m-r)S(\delta)+T(\delta)\bigr),

where

\displaystyle S(\delta)\coloneqq\sum_{i\in[r]}\bigl((1+\delta)^{a_{i}}+(1-\delta)^{a_{i}}\bigr)\quad\text{and}\quad T(\delta)\coloneqq\sum_{\{i,j\}\subseteq[r]}(1+\delta)^{a_{i}}(1-\delta)^{a_{j}}.

Writing
q(\delta)\coloneqq(m-r)S(\delta)+T(\delta)
, a direct calculation yields

\displaystyle q^{\prime}(0)=0\quad\text{and}\quad q^{\prime\prime}(0)=2\Bigl(m\sum_{i=1}^{r}\tbinom{a_{i}}{2}-\tbinom{\ell}{2}\Bigr)>0.

It follows that
\bm{m}
cannot be a maximizer, as claimed.

It should be pointed out that (
42
) is not a sufficient condition for
\mathrm{OPT}(F)=\{\bm{m}_{r,\ell}\}
to hold (even when replaced by a strict inequality).
In forthcoming work
[
13
]
, we will determine
i(F)
and establish the perfect stability for all complete
3
-partite graphs
F
.
In particular, our result will show that
F=K_{12,7,7}
satisfies (
42
) with strict inequality. However, the unique maximizer in
\mathrm{OPT}(F)
is
\left(\alpha,\frac{1-\alpha}{2},\frac{1-\alpha}{2},0,\ldots\right)
, where
\alpha\approx 0.396884
is the largest real root of

\displaystyle 130x^{5}+25x^{4}-90x^{3}+80x^{2}-40x+7=0.

On the other hand, the sufficient condition (
1
) is not necessary in general. For example,
F=K_{4,8,8}
is not almost balanced (since
\binom{8-4}{2}=6>4
), yet our result will show that the unique maximizer for
F
is
(1/3,1/3,1/3,0,\ldots)
.

Thus, the answer to Problem
6.1
, in terms of the algebraic relations among
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}
, lies strictly between the hypersurfaces defined by (
1
) and (
42
), and appears to be quite complicated.

To establish a risk prediction model for the occurrence of hypoxemia during painless bronchoscopy
Authors: Nan Yang, Bei Jiang, Zhen Jia, Tongyuan Wang, Yu Huang, Wen Dong
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
The present study was focused on evaluating the clinical predictors of hypoxemia and establishing a multivariable, predictive model for hypoxemia in painless bronchoscopy. A total of 244 patients were enrolled in the study, and data were collected using a self-designed data collection. The retrospective data collected in this study included the relevant data of patients undergoing the painless bronchoscopy, and we used univariate analysis to deal with these influencing factors. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was used to establish the prediction equation, and receiver operating characteristic curve analysis was carried out. Receiver operating characteristic curves and the Hosmer–Lemeshow test were used to evaluate the model performance. P < .05 was considered to indicate statistical significance. Multivariate logistic regression indicated that body mass index (BMI) (odds ratio [OR]: 1.169; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.070–1.277), arterial partial pressure of oxygen (PaO2) (OR: 4.279; 95% CI: 2.378–7.699), alcohol consumption (OR: 2.021; 95% CI: 1.063–3.840), and whether the bronchoscope operation time exceeds 30 minutes (OR: 2.486; 95% CI: 1.174–5.267) were closely related to the occurrence of hypoxemia. The prediction model developed by the logistic regression equation was −4.911 + 1.454 (PaO2) + 0.156 (BMI) + 0.703 (Alcohol consumption) + 0.911 (time > 30th minutes). The prediction model showed that the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve was 0.687. The predictive model was well calibrated with a Hosmer–Lemeshow x2 statistic of 4.869 (P = .772), indicating that our prediction model fit well. The accuracy (number of correct predictions divided by the number of total predictions) was 75%. The prediction model, consisting of BMI, PaO2, alcohol consumption, and whether the bronchoscope operation time exceeds 30 minutes. It is an effective predictor of hypoxemia during sedation for painless bronchoscopy.

1. Introduction

Electronic bronchoscopy is one of the important invasive methods for the diagnosis of diseases of the respiratory system.[1] It can penetrate deep into the sub-bronchus and examine directly into the upper respiratory tract, trachea and bronchus, collect respiratory secretions and cell specimens, and biopsy of the respiratory tract, lung, and mediastinum.[2] As the bronchoscope is inserted without anesthesia, the airway will be mechanically stimulated, and may easily stimulate coughing, breath-holding, suffocation, fear, restlessness, etc. In patients suffering from underlying serious ailments, this procedure is associated with a high risk of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular damage, which is difficult for patients to accept and cooperate.[3] Foreign studies have reported that the mortality rate of fiber bronchoscopy is 0.01% to 0.02%, and the incidence of main complications is 0.08% to 0.30%. The main causes of death associated with bronchoscopy are massive hemorrhagic asphyxia, acute myocardial infarction, and cerebrovascular accident.[4] In recent years, painless bronchoscopy has gained popularity, as they are done through general intravenous anesthesia, allowing patients to complete the examination without realizing it. Obviously, it has been more widely used in clinical diagnosis and treatment,[5] and has advantages in terms of the comfort levels of the patients as well as improving the work efficiency of medical team.

In recent years, fiber bronchoscopy supplemented by intravenous anesthesia has been widely used in clinical practice. However, most of these sedative and analgesic drugs currently used have different degrees of respiratory depression and circulatory depression. During painless tracheoscopy, the anesthesiologist and the technician cannot effectively supply oxygen to patients due to the problem of co-occupation of the respiratory tract. Additionally, there may still be a potential risk of hypoxia due to respiratory depression caused by anesthetic drugs, thus reducing the safety of painless tracheoscopy.[6] Presently, there are few studies on the evaluation system and nursing countermeasures of adverse reactions related to painless electronic bronchoscopy at home and abroad, among which Yin Jing et al[7] mainly used expert consultation to build risk factors and evaluation systems. Accurate predictive information is the basic strategy to prevent adverse reactions during painless tracheoscopy. Providing patients with personalized predictive risk assessment can better promote informed consent of patients, and patients and their caregivers can make decisions based on the risk or likelihood of hypoxemia. In addition, preventive interventions should be initiated as early as possible in patients with a higher risk of hypoxemia. In this study, hypoxemia in patients undergoing painless bronchoscopy was adopted as the starting point to establish a risk prediction model. Clear clinical observation indicators and early detection of risk factors for nursing intervention may ensure the safety of patient examination.

2. Methods

2.1. Study design

This retrospective study was conducted to collected general clinical information of patients undergoing painless bronchoscopy through medical records and electronic medical records system. All study samples were collected from the patients who underwent painless bronchoscopy in the Respiratory Department of the affiliated Cancer Hospital of Hainan Medical University between March 2021 and September 2022. The Institutional Review Committee of the affiliated Cancer Hospital of Hainan Medical University approved the study. All patients voluntarily agreed to participate in this study and provided written informed consent prior to the commencement of the procedure. This study was carried out following the rules of the Declaration of Helsinki of 1975 (https://www.wma.net/what-we-do/medical-ethics/declaration-of-helsinki/), revised in 2013.

Subjects were included in this study if they met the following criteria.. The inclusion criteria for this study were: (1) the age ranged from 18 to 85 years; (2) bronchoscopy was required after the patient was diagnosed by a respiratory physician in our hospital because of the condition; (3) the patient had a conversation with his family members before surgery. The patients and their family members voluntarily chose to undergo painless bronchoscopy, and signed the informed consent. The exclusion criteria for this study were: (1) other invasive procedures were performed within 24 hours prior to the painless bronchoscopy; (2) the patient had received multiple painless bronchoscopy; (3) the patient’s medical records were incomplete.

2.2. Study variables

The collected demographic data and clinical information includes: age, gender, body mass index (BMI), alcohol consumption, preoperative arterial partial pressure of oxygen (PaO2), whether the bronchoscope operation time exceeds 30 minutes, whether there was severe bleeding occurred during the bronchoscopy operation, whether biopsy was performed during the bronchoscope operation, and whether bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) was performed during the operation. Painless bronchoscopy was performed according to the standard procedure, with fasting for at least 6 hours and water deprivation for 2 hours before the examination. Remifentanil and remimazolam were used for sedation and anesthesia by the chief physician of the Department of Anesthesiology, and the chief physician of the Department of Respiratory medicine performed bronchoscopy. According to the patient’s condition, the corresponding examination method was selected. BAL is a procedure in which normal saline is injected into the bronchoalveolar cavity and aspirated through a bronchoscope to collect fluid on the surface of the alveoli and remove materials filled in the alveoli. Endobronchial biopsies are performed for abnormalities of the respiratory tract that are visible on bronchoscopy. Sampling biopsies were performed using biopsy forceps where the mucosal abnormalities or lesions were most evident. The degree of bleeding during bronchoscopy was graded as no bleeding on the basis that there was no need for continuous suction at the time of bleeding and that bleeding could stop automatically. Bleeding was defined as bleeding that required continued suction or pharmacologic hemostasis.[8] The criterion for being defined as having a drinking habit in this study was consuming alcoholic beverages at least once per week in the past 6 months.[9] Our primary outcome was the development of a model to predict hypoxemia during painless bronchoscopy. The criteria for hypoxemia was a pulse oxygen saturation of <90% at any time for more than 1 minute during the bronchoscopy procedure.[10]

2.3. Statistical analysis

In this study, SPSS26.0 (Version 26.0, IBM, Armonk, NY) for Windows was used for statistical analysis. The Mann–Whitney U test was used for continuous variables and the Fisher exact chi-squared test was used for categorical variables, with the PoSSe score was used for presentation of bivariate analysis results. All variables that were identified by univariate analysis as having a meaningful difference between patients who developed hypoxemia and those who did not were included as useful factors in the forward stepwise logistic regression function analysis. The results of binary logistic regression analysis were used to establish a regression equation model to predict the probablity of hypoxemia. The calibration capability of the model was determined by the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness of fit test, which reflected the relationship between predicted and observed risks. Odds ratios (ORs) were calculated with 95% confidence intervals (CIs).

The prediction model was established according to the risk factors, and the predictive ability of the model was evaluated by receiver operating characteristic curve, the area under the curve and the sensitivity and specificity of the optimal threshold were calculated. For all statistical results, a P value of <.05 were considered significant.

3.3. Model development, calibration, and performance

The input values of the prediction probability model based on logistic was −4.911 + 1.454 (PaO2) + 0.156 (BMI) + 0.703 (Alcohol consumption) + 0.911 (time > 30th minutes). The area under the curve of the hypoxemia prediction model was 0.687 (95% CI: 0.618–0.756), and the results were shown in Figure 1. According to the receiver operating characteristic curve analysis, the optimal critical value was 0.374. At this point it can be calculated that, the predictive sensitivity and specificity of the model were 68% and 69.4%, respectively.

4. Discussion

Our main objective in this study was to develop an individualized prediction model for hypoxemia during painless bronchoscopy. We assume that a set of predictors could be identified using logistic regression function models and that a risk prediction model developed using these meaningful factors would have both good identification and calibration capabilities.

When hypoxemia occurs during painless bronchoscopy it is usually necessary to terminate the bronchoscopy procedure until it is corrected.[11] Some studies have shown that obese patients accumulate large amounts of fat in the chest wall and abdomen, which restricts diaphragm movement and reduces the compliance of the chest, thus affecting respiratory function.[12] Jones et al[13] reported that BMI is associated with reduced lung volume, which is reflected in restricted ventilation patterns in spirometry. It includes reductions in expiratory reserve capacity (ERV) and functional residual volume (FRC). Clinically, obese patients have increased respiratory rate, decreased tidal volume and increased airway resistance, leading to increased oxygen consumption.[12] Other studies[14] have evaluated the relationship between obesity and hypoxemia in patients without obvious cardiopulmonary disease and the results showed that with the increased of BMI, the arterial oxygen partial pressure decreased, and the alveolar-arterial gradient increased, which will increase the probability of hypoxemia. Kendale et al[15] also proposed that the occurrence, severity and duration of hypoxemia during noncardiac surgery were significantly correlated with the high BMI of patients. Some studies have also shown that obesity is a risk factor for complications related to use of propofol-mediated painless endoscopic sedation.[16] Consistent with the findings of other studies, our study also found that BMI (OR: 1.169; 95% CI: 1.070–1.277) was closely related to the occurrence of hypoxemia.

In addition, there will be cross-tolerance between sedatives and alcohol, and the sedative effect of the patient is largely dependent on its dosage.[17] After the sedatives are quickly metabolized, the dose of sedatives needs to be increased to maintain the optimum level of the original sedative, and a large amount of sedatives will increase the possibility of hypoxic blood pressure in the patient.[18] The results of Huang Bing et al showed that alcohol consumption was a risk factor for sedative tolerance. As expected, our study showed that alcohol consumption (OR: 2.021; 95% CI: 1.063–3.840) was strongly associated with hypoxemia during painless bronchoscopy. Previous studies[19,20] have shown that the extension of examination time can increase the occurrence of associated adverse reactions. The study of Zhang-Hong Lv et al[21] showed that whether the bronchoscope operation time exceeds 30 minutes was a risk factor for fever in painless bronchoscopy. Our results also suggest that the bronchoscope operation time exceeds 30 minutes was an independent risk factor for hypoxemia (OR: 2.486; 95% CI: 1.174–5.267).

In brief, a risk prediction model for hypoxemia during painless bronchoscopy was developed and integrated with the following predictors: PaO2, BMI, alcohol consumption, and bronchoscope operation time exceeds 30 minutes. We used this prediction model and tested it on our data with an accuracy result of 75%. The prediction model derived from this study is a meaningful innovation for the individualized prediction of hypoxemia during painless bronchoscopy and can better facilitate the informed consent process before the examination is performed. In addition, the use of a prediction model will improve the clinical decisions physicians make about patient care, thereby leading to better patient safety. Patients with high-risk hypoxemia should receive preventive intervention in time. When hypoxemia occurs during bronchoscopy, it can be relieved by the following methods, including regulating the high oxygen flow, inserting the oropharyngeal airway, and various airway opening operations, such as raising the jaw, and if necessary positive pressure ventilation opening by endotracheal intubation to ensure the safety of the patient during the examination.[11,22]

The strengths of this study lies in its innovativeness, it is the first retrospective study to develop a model for predicting the risk of hypoxemia during painless bronchoscopic sedation. Moreover, the model has good prediction accuracy, sensitivity and specificity. There are also had several important limitations to our study, these limitations also resulted in a small amount of bias between actual and predicted values in data validation. Our analysis of cases with bias in data validation revealed that there may be other factors that would positively affect the accuracy of the prediction, such as the results of fractional exhaled nitric oxide. Because it has been found in individual cases that hypoxemia occurred in patients with a positive FENO test when all other risk factors were normal. It may be that patients with airway hyperresponsiveness are prone to laryngspasm, which triggers hypoxemia.[11] However, this hypothesis needs to be further verified by increasing the sample size and examination results. Therefore, our study has the following limitations. First, the study had a relatively small sample size and did not conduct a multi-center, large-sample case review. Second, the validity of our model has not been validated in other data sets. We caution here that validation in other data sets is mandatory and necessary if the results of this study are to be used in clinical practice. The clinical application of this prediction model is the direction of our future exploration. We should verify the accuracy of this model in a larger sample, so as to implement accurate and individualized risk prediction in clinical diagnosis and treatment as soon as possible.

5. Conclusions

Our findings suggest that the hypoxemia during painless bronchoscopy was closely related to PaO2, BMI, alcohol consumption, and the bronchoscope operation time exceeds than 30 minutes. Based on the above results, we established a risk prediction model for hypoxemia during painless bronchoscopy, which has good prediction power. Our model proposes risk factors for hypoxemia during painless bronchoscopy and predicts risk probability by evaluating risk factors. Formulate standardized and personalized preoperative plan and specific and orderly intraoperative nursing monitoring, and ensure the safety of patients during examination through effective intervention measures. Only clinical sample data from a single center were included in this study. With limited sample size, the predictive ability of the risk models needs a larger sample size to verify.

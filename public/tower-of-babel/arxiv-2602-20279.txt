Universal Functions for Topological Correlators
Elias Furrer ♭ , Jan Manschot ♯ ♭ {}^{\flat}\ NHETC and Department of Physics and Astronomy, Rutgers University, 126 Frelinghuysen Rd., Piscataway NJ 08855, USA ♯ {}^{\sharp}\ School of Mathematics, Trinity College, Dublin 2, Ireland ♯ {}^{\sharp}\ Hamilton Mathematical Institute, Trinity College, Dublin 2, Ireland

Abstract
We consider correlation functions of topologically twisted, 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory with gauge group SU ⁡ ( 2 ) {\rm SU}(2) and N f ≤ 3 N_{f}\leq 3 massive hypermultiplets in the fundamental representation. For a smooth, compact, oriented four-manifold X X with b 2 + > 1 b_{2}^{+}>1 , the correlation functions are expressed in terms of a finite set of universal functions. The mass dependence of these functions encodes intersection numbers of the moduli space of instantons. We determine closed expressions for the universal functions by combining techniques of the Seiberg–Witten geometry, u u -plane integral and the blowup formula. If X X is specialised to a complex algebraic surface S S , the correlation functions can be identified with generating functions of Segre invariants for moduli spaces of sheaves on S S . We verify that our results agree with the results by Göttsche and Kool for these generating functions. August 10, 2026

Compact four-manifolds.

We let
X

X
has no boundary. Basic topological data of
X
are its Betti numbers
b_{i}=b_{i}(X)
, Euler number
\chi

\sigma

Let
L
be the embedding of
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
in
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{R}
, modding out torsion. The intersection form on
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
provides a bilinear form

that pairs degree two cocycles, and we abbreviate
{\bm{k}}_{1}{\bm{k}}_{2}\coloneqq B({\bm{k}}_{1},{\bm{k}}_{2})

{\bm{k}}^{2}\coloneqq B({\bm{k}},{\bm{k}})

For the topological correlators to be non-zero, we require that the moduli space of instantons has even real dimension. Since
X
is assumed simply-connected, this requires that
b_{2}^{+}
is odd.
Moreover, one can deduce that
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
contains an element

such that
c^{2}=2\chi+3\sigma

(
115

. The existence of this element implies that
X
admits an almost complex structure whose first Chern class is
c
. As a result,
X
is an almost complex four-manifold, and its tangent bundle is a complex manifold
27
.

To this holomorphic tangent bundle, we can then associate a Chern character. Let
T_{X}^{*}
be the cotangent bundle, which is a rank 2 complex vector bundle. The canonical bundle
K_{X}=\det(T_{X}^{*})
has a well-defined first Chern class, which is the canonical class
K=c_{1}(K_{X})=-c_{1}(T_{X}^{*})
, and satisfies
K^{2}=2\chi+3\sigma
. Finally, we define the holomorphic Euler characteristic

To facilitate the comparison with the mathematical literature, we use

and
\sigma=K^{2}-8\chi_{\rm h}

Moreover, we let

be a principal

-bundle with connection. The second Stiefel-Whitney class

measures the obstruction to lift

We denote a lift of

Conclusion and discussion

In this article, we have computed topological correlation functions of
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric QCD with
N_{f}=1,2,3
massive hypermultiplets. We expressed the instanton component to the correlation functions in terms of universal functions, which we demonstrated to match with those conjectured in the mathematical literature on virtual Segre numbers for projective surfaces
50
(and previous literature).
In this final section, we discuss several interesting open problems for future work.

Higher rank of
K
-theory class.

We find perfect agreement between topological QCD correlators and the universal series as determined in Conjecture
1

(
50

. This conjecture expresses the virtual Segre numbers for projective surfaces

Since

counts the number of massive hypermultiplets for

;

The
\bm{N_{f}=4}
superconformal theory.

The remaining case for
{\rm SU}(2)

Couplings as period integrals

In this final section, we derive an expression of the coupling

(

39
39

39

;
32

;

for similar approaches.
For simplicity, we restrict to
N_{f}=1
, with the SW curve given in (
163
). We express the curve as a product over the three roots
X_{j}
,
y^{2}=(x-X_{1})(x-X_{2})(x-X_{3})
.
In the weak coupling region,
0\ll u/\Lambda_{0}^{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, and decoupling limit
m\to\infty
, the leading terms of the roots are

\begin{split}X_{1}&=\frac{\Lambda_{0}^{4}}{16m^{2}}+\dots~,\\
X_{2}&=\frac{\Lambda_{0}^{4}}{4u}-\frac{\Lambda^{4}_{0}}{16m^{2}}+\dots~,\\
X_{3}&=u-\frac{\Lambda_{0}^{4}}{4u}+\frac{\Lambda_{0}^{8}}{64u^{2}m^{2}}+\dots~.\\
\end{split}

(198)

We choose the two branch cuts to run between
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
, and from
X_{3}
to
\infty
. The curve can be represented as a two-sheeted cover with
y=\pm\sqrt{\dots}
. We denote the branch points as
X_{j\pm}
for
j=1,2,3
on each sheet. We let the domain for the phase
\phi
of an exponent
e^{i\phi}
be
\phi\in[0,2\pi)
. We take the branch of the square root along the positive real axis.

The goal is to express the coupling

v=\sqrt{2}\frac{da_{D}}{dm}

(199)

in terms of the SW curve and differential. The dual period
a_{D}
is defined as the contour integral over the
B
-cycle of the SW differential
117
,

a=\oint_{A}\lambda~,\qquad a_{D}=\oint_{B}\lambda~,\qquad\lambda=-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4\pi}\frac{y}{x^{2}}\,dx~,

(200)

where we choose the
A
-cycle as the circular clockwise contour with center
0
and surrounding the roots
X_{1+}
and
X_{2+}
, while we choose the
B
-cycle as a curve from
X_{2+}
to
X_{3+}
and then continuing on the second sheet from
X_{3-}
to
X_{2-}
and back to
X_{2+}
. The choice of
A
-cycle is such that in the decoupling limit
m\to\infty
the singularity of
\lambda
merges with
X_{1}
without coinciding with the
A
-cycle.

The differential
\lambda
has a singularity at
z_{\pm}=(x=0,y=\pm i\Lambda_{1}^{3}/8)
, with residue

\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}_{z_{\pm}}\lambda=\pm\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{m}{\sqrt{2}}~.

(201)

For the differential
\partial\lambda/\partial m
, we find

\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial m}=-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{32\pi}\,\Lambda_{1}^{3}\,\frac{dx}{x\,y}~,

(202)

with residue

\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}_{z_{\pm}}\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial m}=\pm\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}~.

(203)

We consider the period
a
and
m
as independent variables. This gives the relation

\begin{split}0&=\frac{da}{dm}=\oint_{A}\frac{d\lambda}{dm}=\oint_{A}\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial m}+\frac{\partial u}{\partial m}\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial u}\\
&=\frac{\partial u}{\partial m}\,\frac{da}{du}+\oint_{A}\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial m}~.\end{split}

(204)

We can apply this to the coupling (
199
),

\begin{split}\frac{v}{\sqrt{2}}&=\oint_{B}\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial m}+\frac{\partial u}{\partial m}\frac{da_{D}}{da}\frac{da}{du}\\
&=\oint_{B}\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial m}-\tau\oint_{A}\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial m}~.\end{split}

(205)

In order to write this more symmetrically, we introduce the holomorphic 1-form
\varphi
on the curve, with periods

\oint_{A}\varphi=1~,\qquad\oint_{B}\varphi=\tau~,

(206)

such that
\varphi=\frac{du}{da}\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial u}
, where

\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial u}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{8\pi}\frac{dx}{y}
.
Then

can be expressed as

\frac{da}{du}\frac{v}{\sqrt{2}}=\oint_{A}\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial u}\oint_{B}\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial m}-\oint_{B}\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial u}\oint_{A}\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial m}~.

(207)

Such a difference of products of periods for holomorphic and meromorphic differentials may be simplified using reciprocity laws, see
e.g.

(
57
, Chapter 2.2)
. It allows us to express (

(208)

where
z_{b}
is a base point located away from the cycles
A
and
B
, and the integration contours from
z_{b}
to
z_{\pm}
do not cross the
A
- or
B
-contour. The expression is then independent of the choice of base point.
Inserting
\partial\lambda/\partial u
from above and using

gives

(209)

\infty

-contour can be deformed for this integrand to a curve from
X_{1+}
to
X_{2+}
above the branch cut, and then from
X_{2+}
to
X_{1+}
below the branch cut. Moreover, the

X_{3+}

\infty

X_{3+}

x
-plane to avoid the branch cut. Moreover, we have from Eq. (

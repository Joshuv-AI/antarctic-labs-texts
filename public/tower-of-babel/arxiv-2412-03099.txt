Deformations of singularities of meromorphic 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 ​ ( ℂ ) \mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C}) -connections and meromorphic quadratic differentials
Martin Klimeš

Abstract
. This paper contributes to the theory of singularities of meromorphic linear ODEs in traceless 2 × 2 2\times 2 cases, focusing on their deformations and confluences. It is divided into two parts: The first part addresses individual singularities without imposing restrictions on their type or degeneracy. The main result establishes a correspondence between local formal invariants and jets of meromorphic quadratic differentials. This result is then utilized to describe the parameter space of universal isomonodromic deformation of meromorphic 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 ​ ( ℂ ) \mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C}) -connections over Riemann surfaces. The second part examines the confluence of singularities in a fully general setting, accommodating all forms of degeneracies. It explores the relationship between the geometry of the unfolded Stokes phenomenon and the horizontal and vertical foliations of parametric families of quadratic differentials. The local moduli space is naturally identified with a specific space of local monodromy and Stokes data, presented as a space of representations of certain fundamental groupoids associated with the foliations. This is then used for studying degenerations of isomonodromic deformations in parametric families.

For individual singularities there are two basic types of approaches to the Stokes phenomenon, as noted by P. Boalch

3.
Isomonodromic deformations of meromorphic connections

Universal isomonodromic deformations of meromorphic connections on Riemann surfaces have been constructed by Jimbo, Miwa & Ueno
[
28
]
,
Malgrange
[
42
]
and others, under a generic assumption on non-resonance of their singularities.
In the case of rank 2 vector bundles, a construction of a universal isomonodromic deformation has been achieved by Heu
[
23
]
for connections with
any types of singularities.
We will show that the natural space for the universal deformation parameter is given by a product
of the configuration space of singularities with a tuple of jet spaces of quadratic differentials, one for each singularity.

A family of meromorphic connections
\nabla_{t}(x)
on a family of analytic vector bundles
E_{t}\to\mathsf{X}
over a Riemann surface
\mathsf{X}
, with poles
at a divisor
\Sigma_{t}\subset\mathsf{X}
, all depending analytically on some parameter
t\in\mathsf{T}
, is called
isomonodromic
, if there exists a meromorphic connection
\nabla(x,t)=\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu-\Omega(x,t)
on the total bundle
\mathcal{E}=\coprod_{t}E_{t}\to\mathsf{X}\times\mathsf{T}
with poles over
\Sigma=\coprod_{t}\Sigma_{t}

which is
flat

\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\Omega-\Omega\wedge\Omega=0,

and of which
\nabla_{t}(x)
are restrictions for all
t\in\mathsf{T}
.

We assume the polar divisor
\Sigma
is smooth at
t=0
, i.e. has no crossing.
In a local coordinate
(x,t,y)
in which the divisor is
\{x=0\}
, the family becomes

\nabla_{t}(x)=\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu-A(x,t)\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}{x^{k+1}},\hskip 20.00003ptt\in(\mathbb{C}^{l},0).

The isomonodromicity asks for existence of meromorphic matricial forms
B_{i}(x,t)\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mut_{i}}{x^{k}}
that make the connection

(3.1)

\nabla=\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu-A(x,t)\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}{x^{k+1}}-\sum_{i=1}^{l}B_{i}(x,t)\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mut_{i}}{x^{k}}

flat.

Definition 3.1
.

Let us suppose that
A(0,0)\neq 0
, i.e. that the order of the pole of
A(x,t)\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}{x^{k+1}}
at
x=0
is locally constant.
Flat connection
\nabla(x,t)
is said to satisfy a
transversality condition

[
23
]
at
\{x=0\}
, if it takes the form (
3.1
)
with analytic
B_{i}(x,t)
,
i=1,\ldots,l
.

Proposition 3.2

(Heu
[
23
, Proposition 2.5]
)
.

A flat traceless meromorphic connection on rank 2 vector bundle (
3.1
) with locally constant Poincaré rank
k
and Katz rank

along the polar divisor satisfies the transversality condition if and only if there exists an analytic gauge-coordinate transformation

(x,t,y)\mapsto\big(\phi(x,t),\,t,\,T(x,t)y\big)
transforming the isomonodromic deformation to a constant one of the form

\nabla=\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu-\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\[3.0pt]
Q(x)&0\end{pmatrix}\frac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}{x^{k+1}}.

.

Isomonodromic deformations satisfying transversality condition are iso-Stokes.

In some cases there exist isomonodromic deformations that don’t satisfy the transversality condition. In the case with simple poles (Fuchsian singularities) these are the non-Schlesinger deformations

with
\psi(x,\epsilon)=\tfrac{P(x,\epsilon){\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}}\phi\rule[-2.45836pt]{0.0pt}{8.19447pt}}{P^{\prime}\circ\phi(x,\epsilon)\rule[-2.45836pt]{0.0pt}{8.19447pt}}
,
where
P(x,\epsilon)
,
P^{\prime}(x^{\prime},\epsilon)
are monic Weierstrass polynomials of the form (
4.3
) and

divides

.

They are
analytic
if
\phi(x,\epsilon)
,

are analytic germs, and
formal
if

\phi(x,\epsilon)=\sum_{i,\bm{j}\geq 0}\phi_{i,\bm{j}}x^{i}\epsilon^{\bm{j}},\hskip 20.00003ptT(x,\epsilon)=\sum_{i,\bm{j}\geq 0}T_{i,\bm{j}}x^{i}\epsilon^{\bm{j}}.

It is easy to verify that both in the formal and the analytic setting each of the three kinds of parametric transformations form a group.

4.1.
Formal theory

In the cases
k>0,\ m=0
(non-resonant irregular) and
k>0,\ m=1
(generic resonant irregular) the formal gauge invariants of the system at
\epsilon=0
are given by
\mathrm{j}^{k}Q(x,0)=Q(x,0)\mod x^{k+1}
.
This easily generalizes in the parametric setting as the Weierstrass division remainder
Q(x,\epsilon)\mod P(x,\epsilon)
(Theorem
6.1
).
On the other hand, for general
m
things get a bit more tricky and we shall instead use a weaker notion of formal equivalence.

Definition 4.2

(Weak formal gauge equivalence)
.

Two analytic germs of parametric systems (
4.2
) with the same singular divisor
P(x,\epsilon)
are
weakly formally gauge equivalent

if for each
\epsilon
small enough the two systems restricted to
\epsilon
have the same formal invariants
at each zero of
P(x,\epsilon)
which is not a resonant regular singularity.

The values of
\epsilon
for which some singularity is resonant regular form at most a countable union of divisors.

For
m=0,1
, this weak formal gauge equivalence agrees with the formal gauge equivalence of parametric families (Theorem
6.2
).

Proposition 4.3
.

Consider two systems (
4.4
) with germs
P(x,\epsilon)
,
Q(x,\epsilon)
, and
P^{\prime}(x,\epsilon)
,
Q^{\prime}(x,\epsilon)
.
If
P^{\prime}(x,\epsilon)=P(x,\epsilon)
and

(4.7)

\tfrac{Q(x,\epsilon)}{Q^{\prime}(x,\epsilon)}=1\mod P(x,\epsilon),

then the two systems are weakly formally equivalent.

Proof.

For any zero
a_{i}(\epsilon)
of
P(x,\epsilon)
of multiplicity
k_{i}+1>0
, and with
Q(x,\epsilon)
vanishing at
a_{i}
up to an order
m_{i}\geq 0
,
the
k_{i}+m_{i}
-the jets with respect to
x
at
a_{i}
of
Q
and
\tilde{Q}
agree

\mathrm{j}_{a_{i}}^{k_{i}+m_{i}}Q^{\prime}=\mathrm{j}_{a_{i}}^{k_{i}+m_{i}}Q.

By Theorem
2.7
this means that the two systems have the same formal tangent-to-identity invariants at all their singularities. ∎

4.2.
Subdominant solutions and mixed solution bases

Let (
4.4
) be a parametric system in the companion form and
Q(x,\epsilon)\left(\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}{P(x,\epsilon)}\right)^{2}
its associated
meromorphic quadratic differential.
Following Proposition
4.3
we shall take its Weierstrass division by a multiplicative factor
\big(1+P(x,\epsilon)\mathbb{C}\{x,\epsilon\}\big)
.
Namely, decomposing by the Weierstrass preparation and division theorems

Q(x,\epsilon)=q(x,\epsilon)\big(U(x,\epsilon)+P(x,\epsilon)f(x,\epsilon)\big),

with
q(x,\epsilon)
, resp.
U(x,\epsilon)
, polynomials in
x
of orders
m
, resp.
k
, with
u(0,0)\neq 0
and
f(x,\epsilon)\in\mathbb{C}\{x,\epsilon\}
,
let

\tilde{Q}(x,\epsilon)=q(x,\epsilon)U(x,\epsilon)

be the unique polynomial in
x
of order
k+m
satisfying
\frac{Q^{\prime}(x,\epsilon)}{\tilde{Q}(x,\epsilon)}=1\mod P(x,\epsilon)\mathbb{C}\{x,\epsilon\}
.
The quadratic differential

(4.8)

\Delta(x,\epsilon)=\tilde{Q}(x,\epsilon)\left(\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}{P(x,\epsilon)}\right)^{2}

carries information about the formal invariants but not about the analytic class.
We denote

\pm\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}=\pm\tfrac{\sqrt{\tilde{Q}}}{P(x,\epsilon)}\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux,\hskip 20.00003pt\pm\Delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\pm\tfrac{P(x,\epsilon)}{\sqrt{\tilde{Q}}}\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux,

the associated differential form and vector field.

Solutions of the ODE (
4.5
) are expected to exhibit asymptotic behavior near the singular points, approximately proportional to either

y_{1}^{+}\simeq Q(x,\epsilon)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\mkern 1.0mu\mathrm{e}^{\pm\int\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}}
or
y_{1}^{-}\simeq Q(x,\epsilon)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\mkern 1.0mu\mathrm{e}^{\mp\int\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}}

on appropriately chosen domains, as detailed in Theorem
4.5
.
Depending which of the two exponentials dominates, that is, whether

(4.9)

\left|\tfrac{y_{1}^{+}}{y_{1}^{-}}\right|\simeq\left|\mkern 1.0mu\mathrm{e}^{\pm 2\int\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right|

tends to infinity or zero, the solutions are classified as
dominant
or
sub-dominant
, respectively (terminology from Sibuya
[
56
,
57
]
).
This classification introduces the concept of a
growth rate filtration
, leading to a
flag structure
on the solution space.
The domains where such filtrations exist are intrinsically tied to two distinct types of foliations:

Definition 4.4

(Horizontal foliation)
.

(1)

The
horizontal foliation
of the quadratic differential
\Delta(x,\epsilon)
is formed by the
gradient curves of (
4.9
): those along which
\Im\left(\pm\int\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)
stays constant.
They are the real time trajectories of the vector field
\pm\Delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}
,
well defined up to orientation. They are also called the
steepest descent curves
, or
anti-Stokes curves
.

(2)

The
vertical foliation
of the quadratic differential
\Delta(x,\epsilon)
is formed by the
level curves of (
4.9
): those along which
\Re\left(\pm\int\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)
stays constant.
They are the real time trajectories of the vector field
\pm\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\Delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}
,
well defined up to orientation. They are also called the
oscillation curves
, or
Stokes curves
.

More generally, we will consider also the rotations

(4.10)

\mkern 1.0mu\mathrm{e}^{-2\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\vartheta}\Delta\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for an angle}\hskip 10.00002pt\vartheta\in\ ]\!-\tfrac{\pi}{2},\tfrac{\pi}{2}[,

and their associated horizontal foliations, which correspond to the real-time trajectories of
\pm\mkern 1.0mu\mathrm{e}^{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\vartheta}\Delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.
Along these curves there is a dominance relation between the exponentials (
4.9
),
allowing for a filtration on the solution space.
The existence of such filtrations is well established in the non-parametric case, and has been proven for unfoldings of non-resonant irregular singularities by Hurtubise, Lambert & Rousseau
[
24
]
, using a theorem of Levinson.

Theorem 4.5

(Subdominant solutions)
.

Let
\Delta
be (
4.8
),
m<2k
.
For any fixed small parameter
\epsilon
and an angle
\vartheta\in\ ]\!-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}[
, and a horizontal trajectory of
\mkern 1.0mu\mathrm{e}^{-2\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\vartheta}\Delta
tending to an equilibrium point
x=a_{i}(\epsilon)
of
\Delta
(pole of order
\geq 2
), there is a unique 1-dimensional subspace of the solution space of the parametric system (
4.2
), called the space of
subdominant solutions
, consisting of those solutions that have vanishing limit at the singularity.

More precisely, let
\mathbf{t}(x,\epsilon)
be a branch of a determination of
\pm\int\Delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, such that
\mkern 1.0mu\mathrm{e}^{-\mathbf{t}(x,\epsilon)}\to 0
when
x\to a_{i}(\epsilon)
along the trajectory:

•

If
a_{i}(\epsilon)
is either Fuchsian or irregular singularity, then there is a unique normalized subdominant solution

y(x,\epsilon)\sim\mkern 1.0mu\mathrm{e}^{-\mathbf{t}(x,\epsilon)}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\tilde{Q}(x,\epsilon)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\big(1+o(1)\big)\\[3.0pt]
\tilde{Q}(x,\epsilon)^{\frac{1}{4}}\big(1+o(1)\big)\end{smallmatrix}\right).

•

If
a_{i}(\epsilon)
is non-Fuchsia regular singularity (i.e. double pole of
\Delta
which is a zero of multiplicity
m_{i}>0
of
\tilde{Q}
), then there is a unique normalized subdominant solution

y(x,\epsilon)\sim\mkern 1.0mu\mathrm{e}^{\mp\int\sqrt{\Delta(x,\epsilon)+\left(\frac{m_{i}\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}{4(x-a_{i}(\epsilon))}\right)^{2}}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\tilde{Q}(x,\epsilon)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\big(1+o(1)\big)\\[3.0pt]
\tilde{Q}(x,\epsilon)^{\frac{1}{4}}\big(1+o(1)\big)\end{smallmatrix}\right).

This solution does not depend on the trajectory or the rotation angle
\vartheta
as long as they are varied continuously.
Moreover the solution
y(x,\epsilon)
depends analytically on
\epsilon
as long as the trajectory varies continuously with the parameter
\epsilon
.

Proof.

The existence and uniqueness of such normalized subdominant solutions follows for each fixed
\epsilon
from the usual theorems on existence of local normalizing transformations at the point

, such as the Borel (multi)-summability of the Hukuhara–Levelt–Turrittin formal fundamental solutions
[
26
, Theorem 20.13]
.
The local analytic dependence on
\epsilon
is quite clear as long as the type of the singularity does not change, however it is less clear that it is also analytic at the values for which
a_{i}
is a resonant Fuchsian singularity, and that it passes well to the limit when
a_{i}
becomes irregular.
We will follow the method of proof of
[
24
]
, using
a parametric version of the Levinson’s theorem
[
24
, Theorem 5.3]
(see
[
12
, §3, Theorem 8.1]
for the original non-parametric version):

Levinson’s theorem.

Consider a system of linear differential equations on the real line of the form

\frac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\tilde{y}}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\mathbf{t}}=\big[\Lambda(\mathbf{t},\epsilon)+B(\mathbf{t},\epsilon)\big]\tilde{y},

where
\Lambda(\mathbf{t},\epsilon)
is diagonal, its limit
\Lambda(+\infty,\epsilon)=\lim_{\mathbf{t}\to+\infty}\Lambda(\mathbf{t},\epsilon)
has eigenvalues with distinct real parts, and assume that

(4.11)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\Big|\frac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\mathbf{t}}\Lambda(\mathbf{t},\epsilon)\Big|\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\mathbf{t}<\infty,\hskip 20.00003pt\int_{0}^{+\infty}\big|B(\mathbf{t},\epsilon)\big|\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\mathbf{t}<\infty.

Then for each eigenvalue
\lambda(\mathbf{t},\epsilon)
of
\Lambda(\mathbf{t},\epsilon)
and an eigenvector
v_{\lambda}(+\infty,\epsilon)
of
\Lambda(+\infty,\epsilon)
associated to
\lambda(+\infty,\epsilon)

there exists
t_{0}>0
and a solution
\tilde{y}=\phi_{\lambda}(\mathbf{t},\epsilon)
on
]t_{0},+\infty[
such that

(4.12)

\lim_{\mathbf{t}\to+\infty}\phi_{\lambda}(\mathbf{t},\epsilon)\cdot\exp\Big(-\int_{t_{0}}^{\mathbf{t}}\lambda(\mathbf{t},\epsilon)\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\mathbf{t}\Big)=v_{\lambda}(+\infty,\epsilon),

If the system and the eigenvector
v_{\lambda}(+\infty,\epsilon)
depend continuously (resp. analytically) on a parameter
\epsilon
over compact sets in the
\mathbf{t}
-space, with the integrals in (
4.11
)
uniformly bounded, then the solution can be chosen depending continuously (resp. analytically) on
\epsilon
.

Applying the gauge transformation
y=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}Q(x,\epsilon)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\!\!\!&0\\
0&\!\!\!Q(x,\epsilon)^{\frac{1}{4}}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\[5.0pt]
1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\tilde{y}
to (
4.4
), then
\tilde{y}
satisfies

\tfrac{P}{\sqrt{Q}}{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}}\tilde{y}=\left[\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0&b(x,\epsilon)\\
b(x,\epsilon)&0\end{pmatrix}\right]\tilde{y},\hskip 20.00003ptb(x,\epsilon)=\tfrac{P}{4\sqrt{Q}}{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}}\log Q.

An additional gauge transformation

\tilde{y}=\tfrac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1\!+\!\sqrt{1\!+\!b^{2}}\hskip-6.0pt&-b\\
b&\hskip-6.0pt1\!+\!\sqrt{1\!+\!b^{2}}\end{pmatrix}\tilde{\tilde{y}}

leads to a system

(4.13)

\tfrac{P}{\sqrt{Q}}{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}}\tilde{\tilde{y}}=\left[\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{1+b^{2}}&0\\
0&-\sqrt{1+b^{2}}\end{pmatrix}-\tfrac{P}{\sqrt{Q}}\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mub}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{b}{1+b^{2}}&-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{1+b^{2}}^{3}\vphantom{\big|}}\\
\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt{1+b^{2}}^{3}\vphantom{\big|}}&\frac{b}{1+b^{2}}\end{pmatrix}\right]\tilde{\tilde{y}}.

Here we take the branch of
\sqrt{1+b^{2}}
with value
1
for
b=0
on

b^{2}\in\mathbb{C}\smallsetminus]-\infty,-1]
.
We can now apply the Levinson’s theorem with
\tilde{\Lambda}=\sqrt{1+b^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\[3.0pt]
0&-1\end{pmatrix}
.

The limit point
a_{i}(\epsilon)
of the trajectory is a pole of
\Delta
of order
2k_{i}-m_{i}+2\geq 2
, where
k_{i}+1
and

are the multiplicities of

as a zero of

and

.
If it is an irregular singularity,

.

.

If

is a regular singularity, then
\frac{\sqrt{Q+Qb^{2}}}{P}\sim\sqrt{\mu_{i}(\epsilon)+\left(\frac{m_{i}}{4}\right)^{2}}
, where

which means that

∎

Remark 4.6
.

One can treat both cases in Theorem
4.5
in a unified way by replacing the quadratic differential
\Delta
by

\Delta(x,\epsilon)+\left(\tfrac{1}{4}\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\log R(x,\epsilon)\right)^{2},

where
R(x,\epsilon)
is the Weierstrass polynomial that is the greatest common divisor of
P(x,\epsilon)^{2}
and
\tilde{Q}(x,\epsilon)
.

Taking a
complete horizontal trajectory
of
\mkern 1.0mu\mathrm{e}^{-2\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\vartheta}\Delta
for some
|\vartheta|<\frac{\pi}{2}
, one obtains a pair of subdominant solutions:
one associated with the positive end of the trajectory (
\Re\mathbf{t}\to+\infty
) and one with the negative end (
\Re\mathbf{t}\to-\infty
).
If they are linearly independent, they form a
mixed solution basis
.
Mixed solution bases originated in the study of hypergeometric equations and have been investigated in the context of confluence by various authors, including Schäfke
[
55
]
, Ramis
[
52
]
, Zhang
[
63
]
, Duval
[
17
]
, Glutsyuk
[
18
,
19
]
, Parise
[
47
]

and Lambert & Rouseau
[
39
,
40
,
24
]
.

In the case
m=0
the linear independence can be established by a deformation argument as in
[
24
]
: their limit when
\epsilon\to 0
is one of the standard sectorial solution bases at the irregular singularity.
Consequently, the linear independence persists for
\epsilon
within a suitably defined “sectorial” domain of sufficiently small radius.
However, when
m>0
, some of these mixed solution pairs can fail to have a well-defined limit as

\epsilon\to 0
. This occurs because the natural domain of their definition shrinks and disappears.
As a result, the deformation argument cannot be applied in general.

Theorem 4.7

(Mixed solution bases)
.

Assume
m<2k
. Let
\sigma
be complete horizontal trajectory of
\mkern 1.0mu\mathrm{e}^{-2\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\vartheta}\Delta
for some
|\vartheta|<\frac{\pi}{2}
whose closure lies inside the domain

(4.14)

\left|\tfrac{P(x,\epsilon)}{4\sqrt{\tilde{Q}(x,\epsilon)}}{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}}\log\tilde{Q}(x,\epsilon)\right|<\tfrac{1}{2},\hskip 20.00003pt\left|\tfrac{Q}{\tilde{Q}}-1\right|<\tfrac{1}{4}.

Then the associated pair of sub-dominant solutions is linearly independent, hence forms a
mixed solution basis
.

Proof.

First let us remark that if
a_{i}
is a zero of
P
of order
k_{i}+1
which is also a zero of
Q
order
m_{i}
, then if the assumption of Theorem
4.5
is violated:
2k_{i}+2-m_{i}=2
and
m_{i}>0
, then
b(a_{i},\epsilon)=\frac{m_{i}}{4}\geq 2
so in this case
a_{i}
is not in the domain considered.

Choose a determination of
\mathbf{t}(x,\epsilon)=\pm\int\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}
, and let
y(x,\epsilon)=\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}(x,\epsilon)\\
y_{2}(x,\epsilon)\end{pmatrix}
be the subdominant solution along the negative end of the trajectory,
\mkern 1.0mu\mathrm{e}^{-\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\vartheta}\mathbf{t}\to-\infty
.
We will show that
|y_{1}(x,\epsilon)|\to\infty
the other end of the trajectory
\mkern 1.0mu\mathrm{e}^{-\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\vartheta}\mathbf{t}\to+\infty
, meaning that is dominant there, and therefore it is not subdominant.
To simplify, let us assume that the angle
\vartheta=0
, i.e. that the trajectory is horizontal for
\Delta
.
As
y_{1}
satisfies
\big(P{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}}\big)^{2}y_{1}=Qy_{1}
, we have

\left({\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\mathbf{t}}}\right)^{2}y_{1}=\tfrac{1}{\tilde{Q}}\big(P{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}}\big)^{2}y_{1}-\tfrac{P^{2}}{2\tilde{Q}}{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}}\log\tilde{Q}\cdot{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}}y_{1}=\tfrac{Q}{\tilde{Q}}y_{1}-2b{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\mathbf{t}}}y_{1},

where
\tilde{b}(x,\epsilon)=\frac{1}{4}{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\mathbf{t}}}\log\tilde{Q}(x,\epsilon)
.
Hence

\displaystyle\left({\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\Re\mathbf{t}}}\right)^{2}|y_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\Re\left(\frac{{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\mathbf{t}}}^{2}y_{1}}{y_{1}}\right)|y_{1}|^{2}+2\left|{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\mathbf{t}}}y_{1}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\Re\left(\tfrac{Q}{\tilde{Q}}\right)|y_{1}|^{2}-4\Re\left(\tilde{b}\frac{{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\mathbf{t}}}y_{1}}{y_{1}}\right)|y_{1}|^{2}+2\left|{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\mathbf{t}}}y_{1}\right|^{2}.

By assumption
|\tilde{b}|<\frac{1}{2}
, hence
4\Re\left(\tilde{b}\frac{{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\mathbf{t}}}y_{1}}{y_{1}}\right)|y_{1}|^{2}<2\left|{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\mathbf{t}}}y_{1}\right|\cdot|y_{1}|
, and

\Re\left(\tfrac{Q}{\tilde{Q}}\right)>\tfrac{3}{4}
, so

\left({\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\Re\mathbf{t}}}\right)^{2}|y_{1}|^{2}>\tfrac{1}{2}|y_{1}|^{2}+\left|{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\mathbf{t}}}y_{1}\right|^{2}+\left(|y_{1}|-\left|{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\mathbf{t}}}y_{1}\right|\right)^{2}>\tfrac{1}{2}|y_{1}|^{2}.

Since near
\mathbf{t}=-\infty
,
y_{1}\sim Q^{\frac{1}{4}}\mkern 1.0mu\mathrm{e}^{\mathbf{t}}
, then
{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\Re\mathbf{t}}}|y_{1}|^{2}\sim 2|y_{1}|^{2}(1+\Re b)>0

which means that

for some

, hence it is exponentially big when
\Re\mathbf{t}\to+\infty

∎

4.3.2.
Zone decomposition

The (maximal) trajectories of a quadratic differential
\mkern 1.0mu\mathrm{e}^{-2\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\vartheta}\Delta
are of the following kinds
[
59
, §11]
:

(1)

closed:
periodic trajectories
,

(2)

finite:
saddle trajectories
, i.e. homoclinic and heteroclinic separatrices,

(3)

semi-infinite:

•

converging separatrices
: on one side have a finite limit at a saddle point and on the other side an infinite limit at an equilibrium,

•

recurrent separatrices
: on one side have a finite limit at a saddle point and on the other side their limit set equals to their closure and has a non-empty interior,

(4)

infinite:

•

converging trajectories
: on both sides have infinite limits at equilibrium points (either 2 different, or the same parabolic one),

•

recurrent trajectories
: on both sides their limit sets are the same, equal to their closure, and have a non-empty interior.

Each focus equilibrium is the limit point of at least one convergent separatrix, and each separating direction of a parabolic equilibrium is tangent to at least one convergent separatrix.

Definition 4.8

(Zones)
.

The

is the closure of union of all the separatrices of saddle points
\mathsf{Saddle}(\Delta)
.
The connected components in
\mathsf{X}
of the complement of the separating graph and of the set of equilibria
\mathsf{Equilib}(\Delta)
are called

\mkern 1.0mu\mathrm{e}^{-2\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\vartheta}\Delta
in
\mathsf{X}
. They can be of the following kinds according to their form in the flat coordinate
\mathbf{t}

[
59
, §11]
:

(1)

sepal zone
: all trajectories have their
\alpha
-limit and
\omega
-limit at the same multiple singular point (parabolic equilibrium).
On the translation surface it takes the form of a half-plane
\mkern 1.0mu\mathrm{e}^{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\vartheta}\mathbb{H}^{\pm}
.

(2)

\alpha\omega
-zone
: all trajectories have the same
\alpha
-limit and the same
\omega
-limit which are two different equilibria (simple or multiple). On the translation surface it takes the form of an open strip parallel to
\mkern 1.0mu\mathrm{e}^{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\vartheta}\mathbb{R}
.

(3)

center zone
: all trajectories are periodic filling a neighborhood of a center equilibrium.
On the translation surface it takes the form of a semi-infinite cylinder.

(4)

annular zone
: all trajectories are periodic.
On the translation surface it takes the form of a finite cylinder.

Lemma 4.14
.

Assume (
4.16
). The transverse graph of a rotationally stable horizontal foliation is a homotopy retract of
\mathsf{X}\smallsetminus\mathsf{Equilib}(\Delta)
: it divides
\mathsf{X}
into cells, one for each equilibrium.
The gate graph is the dual graph: it connects all equilibria and is a homotopy retract of
\mathsf{X}\smallsetminus\mathsf{Saddle}(\Delta)
.

Proof.

Cut each
\alpha\omega
-zone across along its transversal, than each half is attached to just one equilibrium.
Now given an equilibrium point take the union of all the halves of
\alpha\omega
-zones, all the sepal zones and all the separatrices that are attached to it.
Clearly this set covers a full neighborhood of the equilibrium. Since the foliation is rotationally stable there are no saddle trajectories, therefore the boundary of this set can only consist of saddles and transversals of the
\alpha\omega
-zones attached to the equilibrium.

.

Assume (
4.16

(1)

The interior
\mathring{\mathcal{RS}}
of the set

\big(\mathbb{R}/\pi\mathbb{Z}\big)\times\mathsf{E}

(1) This follows from Proposition

Proposition 4.16
.

Assume (
4.16
).
The petals of
\mkern 1.0mu\mathrm{e}^{-2\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\vartheta}\Delta(x,\epsilon)
depend continuously under a Hausdorff metric on their compact closures in
\mathsf{X}
on
(\vartheta,\epsilon)\in\mathcal{RS}
, even though some can disappear at points of
\mathcal{RS}\smallsetminus\mathring{\mathcal{RS}}
.

Proof.

The petals are in one-to-one correspondence with the hyperbolic sectors of saddle points.
The boundary of a petal consists of the pair of separatrices delimiting the hyperbolic sector, plus potentially by a gate trajectory.
Near a saddle point the separatrices depend continuously on
(\vartheta,\epsilon)
as long as the rank of the saddle point doesn’t change.
And it takes them a finite
\mathbf{t}

4.10

The equilibrium point to which it tends therefore cannot change with
(\vartheta,\epsilon)
unless passing through a rotationally unstable situation
where the separatrix becomes a boundary component of the periodic/parabolic domain.
Also the periodic and parabolic domains depend continuously on
\epsilon
(they are independent of
\vartheta

In fact, the boundary of each domain consists of a finite number of saddle connections which depend continuously on
\epsilon\in\mathsf{E}\smallsetminus\mathsf{C}

Although saddle connections can cease to exist through flattening of a triangle in the translation surface, this is still a continuous process.

For
\epsilon\to\epsilon_{0}\in\mathsf{C}
some saddle connections can disappear at the limit: either they shrink and vanish or they get pinched by merging critical points.
This can only increase the size of the periodic/parabolic domain of the limit equilibria.
However, it can also happen that an equilibrium point disappears through a confluence with saddles when the limit point is a saddle (including that of rank
-1
).
But in this case the limit of a separatrix landing at such equilibrium would be a saddle trajectory, i.e.
(\vartheta,\epsilon)\to(\vartheta_{0},\epsilon_{0})\notin\mathcal{RS}
.

Let us show that:

(i)

The sizes of these holes on the different sheets of the Riemann surface of

(ii)

The distance between any pair of such holes on the same sheet of the Riemann surface of

and

Given

not covered by the union of the relative petals correspond on the Riemann surface of

of the interval in which

Let us prove (i). When

-image is close to that of its

-image.
If there are no saddles, then

The case with no saddles is the same as

cusps shrink together and disappear, while the fat transversal forms a single new cusp at the limit.
The generic situation is with
\nu_{s}=-\frac{3}{2}
, when 2 existing cusps disappear and 1 new one is created, see Figure
11(d)
.
The case
\nu_{s}=-\frac{1}{2}
, in which the total number of cusps would increase by one, is at odds with the assumption (
4.19
).

4.5.
Wild monodromy representations

4.5.1.
Fundamental groupoid

We now consider all the different petals of the horizontal foliation of
\mkern 1.0mu\mathrm{e}^{-2\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\vartheta}\Delta_{\epsilon}(x)
for
(\vartheta,\epsilon)\in\mathsf{W}
,
along with their associated fundamental solution matrices.
Our focus will be on the various connection matrices that relate these fundamental solution matrices.
Following the approach of E. Paul and J.P. Ramis
[
49
,
48
]
, we interpret the connection matrices as representations of a fundamental groupoid.
The concept of ”wild fundamental groupoids” has appeared previously in the works of J. Martinet and J.P. Ramis
[
43
,
15
]
and P. Boalch
[
6
,
7
]
.
However, in our treatment, we neither blow up irregular singularities nor attach infinitesimal halos to them.
For our purposes, the fundamental groupoid is to be

\Pi_{1}(\mathsf{X}\smallsetminus\mathsf{Crit}_{\epsilon},\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{W}_{\epsilon}})
, where the base-points
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{W}_{\epsilon}}

correspond to the intersections of petals and lagoons.
Each petal thus contains precisely two base points, one associated with each of the two fundamental solution matrices (
4.21
).
In the confluent setting it depends on the choice of
\mathsf{W}_{\epsilon}
(
4.20
).
On the other hand, if considered for an individual irregular singularity, or for a parametric family of such singularities with constant Katz rank, then there is only one
\mathsf{W}_{\epsilon}=\ ]\!-\!\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}[
, so it doesn’t play a role.

Definition 4.26
.

A
wild monodromy representation of the fundamental groupoid

is a contravariant functor

\rho:\Pi_{1}(\mathsf{X}\smallsetminus\mathsf{Crit}_{\epsilon},\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{W}_{\epsilon}})\to\mathbf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})

which

(1)

associates to each basepoint
d\in\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{W}_{\epsilon}}
the triple
\big(\mathbb{C}^{2},\ \textbf{flag},\ \textbf{splitting}\big)
, where

\textbf{flag}=\left(\{0\}\subset\langle\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}\rangle\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}\right),\hskip 20.00003pt\textbf{splitting}=\langle\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}\rangle\oplus\langle\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}\rangle

are the standard flag and splitting structures associated to the groups
\mathbf{B}
and
\mathbf{T}
,

(2)

represents the elementary paths by elements of

a)

\mathbf{B}
for those that cross the separating graph: preserve flags,

b)

\mathbf{T}
for those that cross the gate graph: preserve both flags and splittings,

c)

\mathbf{T}

for those that cross the extended transverse graph: preserve splittings and make flags transverse,

(3)

factors through the projection

\Pi_{1}(\mathsf{X}\smallsetminus\mathsf{Crit}_{\epsilon},\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{W}_{\epsilon}})\to\Pi_{1}(\mathsf{X}\smallsetminus\mathsf{Sing}_{\epsilon},\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{W}_{\epsilon}}),

meaning that for each saddle point of rank
\nu
the product of the
4|\nu|
alternating upper-triangular and anti-diagonal matrices along a loop around the saddle is the identity.

Two representations
\rho,\rho^{\prime}
are
equivalent
,
\rho\sim\rho^{\prime}
, if there exists a map (natural transformation)

\tau\in\mathbf{T}^{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{W}_{\epsilon}}}
from the set of base-points
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{W}_{\epsilon}}
to the group
\mathbf{T}
of automorphisms of
\big(\mathbb{C}^{2},\ \textbf{flag},\ \textbf{splitting}\big)
,
that conjugates the two representations: for each path
d_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}d_{2}
one asks that
\tau(d_{2})\cdot\rho(\gamma)=\rho^{\prime}(\gamma)\cdot\tau(d_{1})
.

.

Remark 4.28
.

Another way to define the moduli space
\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta_{\epsilon},\mathsf{W}_{\epsilon}}
would be through representations of

\Pi_{1}(\mathsf{X}\smallsetminus\mathsf{Crit}_{\epsilon},\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{W}_{\epsilon}})
respecting the flag structure only:

–

To each basepoint from
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{W}_{\epsilon}}
attach the pair
\big(\mathbb{C}^{2},\ \textbf{flag}\big)
,

–

To each path from
\Pi_{1}(\mathsf{X}\smallsetminus\mathsf{Crit}_{\epsilon},\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{W}_{\epsilon}})
attach linear map from
\mathbf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
between the source and target spaces, such that

–

\mathbf{B}
,

–

if the path is elementary of type c), i.e. crosses the extended transverse graph, then it makes the two flags transverse, i.e. it is an element of

\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu&0\end{pmatrix}\cdot\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{U}\cdot\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbf{T}$}\cdot\mathbf{U}
,

and such that is factors as a representation of
\Pi_{1}(\mathsf{X}\smallsetminus\mathsf{Sing}_{\epsilon},\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{W}_{\epsilon}})\to\mathbf{SL}_{2}

–

An equivalence relation is a linear automorphism of each of the vector spaces preserving the flag, i.e. an elements of
\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{W}_{\epsilon}}}

The source and targets of the elementary paths of type c) provide a partitioning of
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{W}_{\epsilon}}
in pairs of points, and every element of

\mathbf{B}
decomposes uniquely as
\mathbf{T}\cdot\mathbf{U}

4.26
(the diagonality for elementary paths of type b) comes from the commutation relation over the gates:
\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbf{T}$}\cdot\mathbf{B}\cdot\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbf{T}$}\cap\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{T}
).

The formalization of matrices in
\mathbf{B}
representing paths of type a) and b) is their diagonal, the formalization of matrices in
\mathbf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\smallsetminus\mathbf{B}
representing paths of type c) is the matrix
\begin{pmatrix}0&-c^{-1}\\
c&0\end{pmatrix}
where

is the

-element of the matrix.

5.
Families of isomonodromic deformations

A parametric family of meromorphic connections on a Riemann surface
\mathsf{X}

\nabla_{t,\epsilon}(x)=\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu-\Omega_{0}(x,t,\epsilon),\hskip 20.00003pt(t,\epsilon)\in\mathsf{T}\times\mathsf{E},

with
\Omega_{0}
a germ of meromorphic
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-valued
\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux
-form
depending on a deformation variable
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
and a parameter
\epsilon
, is
isomonodromic in
t

if it comes from a parametric family of
flat
meromorphic connections

(5.1)

\nabla_{\epsilon}(x,t)=\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu-\Omega(x,t,\epsilon),\hskip 20.00003pt\Omega(x,t,\epsilon)=\Omega_{0}(x,t,\epsilon)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Omega_{i}(x,t,\epsilon),

where
\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu=\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu_{x,t}
is the exterior derivative in the fibers
\{\epsilon=\operatorname{const}\}
and
\Omega_{i}
are germs of meromorphic
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-valued
\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mut_{i}
-forms,
\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\Omega=\Omega\wedge\Omega
.

We shall restrict our attention to a local situation where
\mathsf{X}
,
\mathsf{T}
,
\mathsf{E}
are germs of (poly)discs.
Then

\Omega_{0}=A(x,t,\epsilon)\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}{P(x,t,\epsilon)}

for some analytic
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-valued function germ

with
A(0,0,0)\neq 0
and

P(x,t,\epsilon)=x^{k+1}+P_{k}(t,\epsilon)x^{k}+\ldots+P_{0}(t,\epsilon),\hskip 20.00003ptP(x,0,0)=x^{k+1},\hskip 20.00003ptk\geq 0.

Definition 5.1
.

(1)

The connection
\nabla_{\epsilon}(x,t)=\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu-\Omega(x,t,\epsilon)
satisfies
transversality condition
if the polar divisors of
\Omega
and
\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\Omega
are bounded by the polar divisor of
\Omega_{0}(x,t,\epsilon)
and are transverse to the fibration
\{(t,\epsilon)=\operatorname{const}\}
.

(2)

The divisor
\{P(x,t,\epsilon)=0\}
satisfies
non-crossing condition
if its restriction to each fiber
\{\epsilon=\operatorname{const}\}
has no crossings.
Factoring
P(x,t,\epsilon)=\prod_{j}P_{j}(x,t,\epsilon)^{l_{j}}
into product of irreducible polynomials in
x
, denote
p(x,t,\epsilon)=\prod_{j}P_{j}(x,t,\epsilon)
, which for generic values of
(t,\epsilon)
has only simple roots.
Then the non-crossing is equivalent to the zeroes of the discriminant of
p(x,t,\epsilon)
being independent of
t
, i.e.

(5.2)

\operatorname{Disc}_{x}p(x,t,\epsilon)=D(\epsilon)U(t,\epsilon),\hskip 20.00003ptU(0,0)\neq 0.

Lemma 5.2
.

A divisor
\{P(x,t,\epsilon)=0\}
satisfies the non-crossing condition if and only if there exists an analytic coordinate change
\tilde{x}=\phi(x,t,\epsilon)
which straightens it in
t
, i.e. under which it becomes
\{\tilde{P}(\tilde{x},\epsilon)=0\}
.

Proof.

For each fixed
\epsilon\in\mathsf{E}\smallsetminus\{D(\epsilon)=0\}
let
P(x,t,\epsilon)=\prod_{i}\big(x-a_{i}(t,\epsilon)\big)^{l_{i}(\epsilon)}
be the root factorization,
\sum_{i}l_{i}(\epsilon)=k+1
,
and denote
P_{j}(x,t,\epsilon)=\prod_{i\neq j}\big(x-a_{i}(t,\epsilon)\big)^{l_{i}(\epsilon)}
.
Then

\phi(x,t,\epsilon)=x+\sum_{j}\tfrac{P_{j}(x,t,\epsilon)\rule[-2.45836pt]{0.0pt}{8.19447pt}}{P_{j}(a_{j}(t,\epsilon),t,\epsilon)\rule[-2.45836pt]{0.0pt}{8.19447pt}}\big(a_{j}(0,\epsilon)-a_{j}(t,\epsilon)\big)

is the unique polynomial of order
k
that sends each root
a_{j}(t,\epsilon)
of multiplicity
l_{j}(\epsilon)
to
a_{j}(0,\epsilon)
with the same multiplicity.
Analytic continuation along loops in
\pi_{1}\big(\mathsf{T}\times(\mathsf{E}\smallsetminus\{D(\epsilon)=0\}),(0,\epsilon_{0})\big)
acts on the roots
a_{i}(t,\epsilon)
by permutations,
leaving
\phi
invariant, which means that it is single-valued in
(t,\epsilon)
.
It extends to
\{D(\epsilon)=0\}
following the same prescription.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume the divisor
\{P(x,\epsilon)=0\}
is independent of
t
, let
P(x,\epsilon)=\prod_{j}P_{j}(x,\epsilon)^{l_{j}}
be the unique factorization into irreducible polynomials, and denote

.
A connection (
5.1
) satisfies the transversality condition if and only if it has the form

\nabla_{\epsilon}(x,t)=\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu-A(x,t,\epsilon)\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux\rule[-2.45836pt]{0.0pt}{8.19447pt}}{P(x,\epsilon)\rule[-2.45836pt]{0.0pt}{8.19447pt}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}B_{i}(x,t,\epsilon)\tfrac{p(x,\epsilon)\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mut_{i}\rule[-2.45836pt]{0.0pt}{8.19447pt}}{P(x,\epsilon)\rule[-2.45836pt]{0.0pt}{8.19447pt}}

with
A
and
B_{i}
analytic.

Proof.

The polynomial
p(x,\epsilon)
is the smallest polynomial that makes
p{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}}P
divisible by

. Writing
\Omega=A(x,t,\epsilon)\frac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}{P(x,\epsilon)}+\sum_{i}C_{i}(x,t,\epsilon)\frac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mut_{i}}{P(x,\epsilon)}
,
then the
\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux\wedge\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mut_{i}
-component of

is
\big({\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}}C_{i}-C_{i}\frac{{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}}P}{P}\big)\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux\wedge\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mut_{i}
which is analytic if and only if

is divisible by

.
∎

We can now prove a parametric version of Proposition

.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\nabla_{\epsilon}(x,t)
be a germ of a parametric family of meromorphic flat connections (
5.1
) satisfying the transversality and non-crossing conditions.
Then there exists a local analytic gauge–coordinate transformation
(x,y,t,\epsilon)\mapsto\big(\phi(x,t,\epsilon),\,T(x,t,\epsilon)y,\,t,\,\epsilon\big)

which brings it to a constant deformation

\tilde{\nabla}_{\epsilon}(\tilde{x},t)=\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu-\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\[3.0pt]
\tilde{Q}(\tilde{x},\epsilon)&0\end{pmatrix}\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\tilde{x}\rule[-2.45836pt]{0.0pt}{8.19447pt}}{\tilde{P}(\tilde{x},\epsilon)\rule[-2.45836pt]{0.0pt}{8.19447pt}}.

Proof.

The proof follows the same steps as in Heu
[
23
, Proposition 2.5]
.

Step 1: Apply an analytic coordinate transformation of Lemma
5.2
to straighten the divisor to
\{P(x,\epsilon)=0\}
.

Step 2: Apply an analytic gauge transformation of Lemma
4.1
to bring the system

Q(x,t,\epsilon)&0\end{pmatrix}
.

Using Lemma
5.3
, write

\Omega=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&-\alpha\end{pmatrix}

with

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}a_{i}(x,t,\epsilon)\tfrac{p(x,\epsilon)\rule[-2.45836pt]{0.0pt}{8.19447pt}}{P(x,\epsilon)\rule[-2.45836pt]{0.0pt}{8.19447pt}}\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mut_{i},\hskip 20.00003pt\beta=\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux\rule[-2.45836pt]{0.0pt}{8.19447pt}}{P(x,\epsilon)\rule[-2.45836pt]{0.0pt}{8.19447pt}}+\sum_{i}b_{i}(x,t,\epsilon)\tfrac{p(x,\epsilon)\rule[-2.45836pt]{0.0pt}{8.19447pt}}{P(x,\epsilon)\rule[-2.45836pt]{0.0pt}{8.19447pt}}\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mut_{i},

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=Q(x,t,\epsilon)\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux\rule[-2.45836pt]{0.0pt}{8.19447pt}}{P(x,\epsilon)\rule[-2.45836pt]{0.0pt}{8.19447pt}}+\sum_{i}c_{i}(x,t,\epsilon)\tfrac{p(x,\epsilon)\rule[-2.45836pt]{0.0pt}{8.19447pt}}{P(x,\epsilon)\rule[-2.45836pt]{0.0pt}{8.19447pt}}\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mut_{i}.

The integrability condition

\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\alpha=\beta\wedge\gamma,\hskip 20.00003pt\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\beta=2\alpha\wedge\beta,\hskip 20.00003pt\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu\gamma=2\gamma\wedge\alpha.

Step 3: The foliation

Now the condition

∎

is equivalent to
\alpha(x,\epsilon,t)
being a solution to the non-homogeneus linear ODE

(6.6)

(x-s(\epsilon)){\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}}\alpha+\tfrac{m}{2}\alpha{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}}+R=0,

with an analytic solution
\alpha(x,\epsilon,t)=-(x-s(\epsilon))^{-m/2}\int_{s(\epsilon)}^{x}(z-s(\epsilon))^{m/2}R(z,\epsilon)\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5muz
.
∎

Lemma 6.7
.

Consider two families of meromorphic quadratic differentials
\Delta_{0}
and
\Delta_{1}
of the form

(6.7)

\Delta_{t}=x^{m}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{\mu(\epsilon)}x^{k-\frac{m}{2}}+tw(x)}{x^{k+1}+p(x)}\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux\right)^{2},

p(x)=p_{k}x^{k}+\ldots+p_{0},\hskip 10.00002ptw(x)=w_{k}x^{k}+\ldots+w_{0},

where
w_{k-\frac{m}{2}}=0
and
\mu(\epsilon)=0
if
m
is odd, depending on parameters
\bm{p}=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{k})
and
\bm{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,\widehat{w_{k-\frac{m}{2}}},\ldots,w_{k})
.
Then there exists an analytic transformation
(x,\bm{p})\mapsto\big(\phi(x,\bm{p},\bm{w}),\psi(\bm{p},\bm{w})\big)
tangent at identity at
(x,\bm{p})=0
that conjugates

\Delta_{1}
to
\Delta_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
X_{t}=\frac{x^{k+1}+p(x)}{x^{\frac{m}{2}}(1+\sqrt{\mu(\epsilon)}x^{k-\frac{m}{2}}+tw(x))}{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}}
be the dual vector field to (
6.7
). We want to construct a family of transformations depending analytically on
t\in[0,1]
between
X_{0}
and
X_{t}
, defined by a flow of a vector field
Y
of the form

Y={\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mut}}+\sum_{j=0}^{k}\xi_{j}(t,\bm{p},\bm{w}){\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mup_{j}}}+\frac{x}{1+\sqrt{\mu(\epsilon)}x^{k-\frac{m}{2}}+tw(x,\epsilon)}h(x,t,\bm{p},\bm{w}){\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}},

for some
\xi_{j}
and

(6.8)

(1+tw+\sqrt{\mu}x^{k-\frac{m}{2}})\xi+xh{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}}(x^{k+1}+p)-(x^{k+1}+p)x{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}}h-\tfrac{m+2}{2}(x^{k+1}+p)h=(x^{k+1}+p)w,

.
We see that we can choose

as a polynomial of order
k
in
x
:

h(x,t,\bm{p},\bm{w})=h_{0}(t,\bm{p},\bm{w})+\ldots+h_{k}(t,\bm{p},\bm{w})x^{k},\hskip 20.00003pt\text{with $h_{k-\frac{m}{2}}=0$}.

Write
(x^{k+1}+p(x))w(x)=b_{0}(\bm{p},\bm{w})+\ldots+b_{2k+1}(\bm{p},\bm{w})x^{2k+1}

\bm{h}\end{pmatrix}=\bm{b}(\bm{p},\bm{w}).

\bm{p}=\bm{w}=0

&\ddots&&&&&\\
&&1&&&&\\

&&&&\hskip-5.0ptk-1-\tfrac{m}{2}\hskip-10.00002pt&&\\
&&&&&\ddots&\\
&&&&&&\hskip-5.0pt-\tfrac{m}{2}\end{pmatrix},

where if

is even then the

-th row and column corresponding to the diagonal term

are omitted.

is invertible for

from any compact in

are small enough.

Lemma 6.8
.

Assume
x\mapsto\phi(x,\epsilon)
is a formal/analytic coordinate transformation between two quadratic differentials of the form (
6.5
),
with
m
odd then
\phi(x,\epsilon)=\zeta x
with
\zeta^{2k-m}=1
.

Proof.

Let
x^{\prime}=\phi(x,\epsilon)
be a transformation between the differentials

\frac{x^{m}(1+\sqrt{\mu}x^{k-\frac{m}{2}})^{2}}{\left(x^{k+1}+p(x,\epsilon)\right)^{2}}(\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux)^{2}
and
\frac{x^{\prime m}(1+\sqrt{\mu}x^{\prime k-\frac{m}{2}})^{2}}{\left(x^{\prime k+1}+p^{\prime}(x^{\prime},\epsilon)\right)^{2}}(\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux^{\prime})^{2}
, preserving the parameter
\epsilon
.
If
\phi(x;0)=\zeta x+\ldots
for some
\zeta\neq 0
then necessarily
\zeta^{2k-1}=1
. Up to precomposition with a map
x\mapsto\zeta x
,
we can assume that
\zeta=1
and
\phi(x;0)=x+\ldots
is tangent to identity.

We want to prove that
\phi\equiv\operatorname{id}
. This is done by an infinite descent.
Denote
\mathcal{I}
the ideal of analytic functions of
(x,\epsilon)
that vanish when
\epsilon=0
.
Since
\phi
must fix the origin, we can write
\phi(x,\epsilon)=x+xf(x,\epsilon)
, and we want to show that
f\in\mathcal{I}^{n}
for all
n\geq 0
.
Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{I}^{n}
.
Developing the transformation equation

\tfrac{x^{k+1}+p}{x^{\frac{m}{2}}(1+\sqrt{\mu}x^{k-\frac{m}{2}})}{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}}\phi=\tfrac{\phi^{k+1}+p^{\prime}\circ\phi}{\phi^{\frac{m}{2}}(1+\sqrt{\mu}\phi^{k-\frac{m}{2}})},

modulo
\mathcal{I}^{n+1}
and multiplying by
x^{\frac{m}{2}}(1+\sqrt{\mu}x^{k-\frac{m}{2}})
we get

x^{k+1}+p+x^{k+1}{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}}(xf)=x^{k+1}+p^{\prime}+x^{k+1}\Big(k+1-\tfrac{m}{2}-\tfrac{(k-\frac{m}{2})\sqrt{\mu}x^{k-\frac{m}{2}}}{1+\sqrt{\mu}x^{k-\frac{m}{2}}}\Big)f\mod\mathcal{I}^{n+1},

from which

p-p^{\prime}=x^{k+1}\Big(\tfrac{k-\frac{m}{2}}{1+\sqrt{\mu}x^{k-\frac{m}{2}}}f-x{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}}f\Big)\mod\mathcal{I}^{n+1}.

The left side is a polynomial of order
\leq k
in
x
, while the right side develops into a formal power series of vanishing order (valuation)
\geq k+1
, which means that both sides are null modulo
\mathcal{I}^{n+1}
.
Therefore on the left side
p=p^{\prime}\mod\mathcal{I}^{n+1}
, while the right side

\tfrac{k-\frac{m}{2}}{1+\sqrt{\mu}x^{k-\frac{m}{2}}}f-x{\tfrac{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mu}{\mkern 0.5mu\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.5mux}}f=0\mod\mathcal{I}^{n+1}
means
f=c(\epsilon)\frac{x^{k}}{x^{\frac{m}{2}}(1+\sqrt{\mu}x^{k-\frac{m}{2}})}\mod\mathcal{I}^{n+1}
. Since by assumption
m
is odd, necessarily
c(\epsilon)=0\mod\mathcal{I}^{n+1}
.
∎

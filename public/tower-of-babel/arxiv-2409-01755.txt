Character space and Gelfand type representation of locally C ∗ C^{\ast} -algebra
Santhosh Kumar Pamula, Rifat Siddique

Abstract
. In this article, we identify a suitable approach to define the character space of a commutative unital locally C ∗ C^{\ast} -algebra via the notion of the inductive limit of topological spaces. Also we discuss topological properties of the character space. We establish the Gelfand type representation between a commutative unital locally C ∗ C^{\ast} -algebra and the space of all continuous functions defined on its character space. Equivalently, we prove that every commutative unital locally C ∗ C^{\ast} -algebra is identified with the locally C ∗ C^{\ast} -algebra of continuous functions on its character space through the coherent representation of projective limit of C ∗ C^{\ast} -algebras. Finally, we construct a unital locally C ∗ C^{\ast} -algebra generated by a given locally bounded normal operator and show that its character space is homeomorphic to the local spectrum. Further, we define the functional calculus and prove spectral mapping theorem in this framework.

p_{n}(g_{\ell})=\sup\left\{|g_{\ell}(t)|:\;\frac{1}{2}\leq t\leq n\right\}=g_{\ell}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{2}{4+\ell^{2}}<\frac{1}{\ell}.

Further,
g_{\ell}(0)=\frac{1}{2}
and so,
g_{\ell}\notin\Phi^{-1}\left(B\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)\right).
In summary, we have shown that for every

there is a
g_{\ell}\in p_{n}^{-1}\left(B(0,\frac{1}{\ell})\right)
such that
g_{\ell}\notin\Phi^{-1}\left(B\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)\right).
Equivalently,
p_{n}^{-1}\left(B(0,\frac{1}{\ell})\right)
is not entirely contained in
\Phi^{-1}\left(B\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)
for every
\ell,n\in\mdmathbb{N}
. Hence
\Phi
is not continuous.

Now we show that certain continuous multiplicative linear functional on a commutative unital locally
C^{\ast}
-algebra must be induced from multiplicative linear functional on some quotient
C^{\ast}
-algebra.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a commutative unital locally
C^{\ast}
-algebra and
\Phi\colon\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mdmathbb{C}
be a multiplicative linear functional. If
\Phi
is continuous and for some
\alpha_{0}\in\Lambda
,

(2.1)

\sup\big\{|\Phi(x)|:\;x\in\mathcal{A},\;p_{\alpha_{0}}(x)<1\big\}=1.

then the map given by
\varphi_{\alpha_{0}}\left(\pi_{\alpha_{0}}(x)\right)=\Phi(x),\;x\in\mathcal{A},
is a well-defined multiplicative linear functional on
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha_{0}}
. In other words,
\Phi=\varphi_{\alpha_{0}}\circ\pi_{\alpha_{0}}.

Proof.

From the hypothesis, it is clear that
|\Phi(x)|\leq 1
whenever
p_{\alpha_{0}}(x)<1
. Assume that
p_{\alpha_{0}}(x)=0
, then for any
n\in\mdmathbb{N}
we have
p_{\alpha_{0}}(nx)=0<1
. It follows that

|\Phi(x)|=\frac{1}{n}|\Phi(nx)|\leq\frac{1}{n},\;\text{for every}\;n\in\mdmathbb{N}.

So,
\Phi(x)=0.
The map
\varphi_{\alpha_{0}}:\;\mathcal{A}_{\alpha_{0}}\to\mdmathbb{C}
given by

\varphi_{\alpha_{0}}(\pi_{\alpha_{0}}(x))=\Phi(x),\;\text{for all}\;x\in\mathcal{A}

is well-defined linear map. Further,

\varphi_{\alpha_{0}}\left(\pi_{\alpha_{0}}(x)\pi_{\alpha_{0}}(y)\right)=\varphi_{\alpha_{0}}\left(\pi_{\alpha_{0}}(xy)\right)=\Phi(xy)=\phi(x)\Phi(y)=\varphi_{\alpha_{0}}\left(\pi_{\alpha}(x)\right)\;\varphi_{\alpha_{0}}\left(\pi_{\alpha}(y)\right),

for any
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
and

\sup\big\{\left|\varphi_{\alpha_{0}}\left(\pi_{\alpha_{0}}(x)\right)\right|:\;x\in\mathcal{A},\;\|\pi_{\alpha_{0}}(x)\|<1\big\}=\sup\big\{|\Phi(x)|:\;x\in\mathcal{A},\;p_{\alpha_{0}}(x)<1\big\}=1.

Therefore,
\varphi_{\alpha_{0}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha_{0}}}.

∎

Remark 2.3
.

If
(\Lambda,\leq)
is a totally ordered set, the the condition given in Equation
2.1
of Theorem
2.2
is redundant. That is, for a continuous multiplicative linear functional
\Phi
defined on a commutative unital locally
C^{\ast}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
, there exists an
\alpha_{0}\in\Lambda
such that Equation
2.1
superfluous. This can be seen as follows: since
\Phi
is continuous, there exists an
\epsilon>0
and
\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{N}\}\subset\Lambda
satisfying

\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{N}p_{\alpha_{i}}^{-1}\left(B(0,\epsilon)\right)\subseteq\Phi^{-1}\left(B(0,1)\right).

Since
\Lambda
is a totally ordered set, by choosing
\alpha_{0}=\min\{\alpha_{{1}},\alpha_{{2}},\cdots,\alpha_{{N}}\}
we have

(2.2)

|\Phi(x)|<1\;\text{ whenever}\;p_{\alpha_{0}}(x)<\epsilon.

Equivalently,
\Phi\left(p_{\alpha_{0}}^{-1}\left(B(0,\epsilon)\right)\right)
is bounded in
\mdmathbb{C}.
Now we claim that Equation
2.1
holds true. If
\epsilon\geq 1
then the result follows from Equation (
2.2
). Suppose that
\epsilon<1
and there exists an
x\in\mathcal{A}
such that
p_{\alpha_{0}}(x)<1
but
|\Phi(x)|>1

p_{\alpha_{0}}\left(\epsilon x^{n}\right)=\epsilon p_{\alpha_{0}}\left(x^{n}\right)\leq\epsilon p_{\alpha_{0}}(x)^{n}<\epsilon,

but the sequence
\{\Phi(\epsilon x^{n})\}_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}
is not bounded since
|\Phi(\epsilon x^{n})|=\epsilon|\Phi(x)|^{n}.
Therefore, Equation (
2.2
) holds true for
\epsilon>0
. Hence
\varphi_{\alpha_{0}}
defined as in Theorem
2.2
is in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha_{0}}}
and
\Phi=\varphi_{\alpha_{0}}\circ\pi_{\alpha_{0}}.

Now we are in a situation to define the character space of a commutative unital locally
C^{\ast}
-algebra. In view of Theorem
2.2
we consider the multiplicative linear functional that are induced from the multiplicative linear functional on the quotient
C^{\ast}
-algebra. We give the formal definition below.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a commutative unital locally
C^{\ast}
-algebra and let
(\Lambda,\leq)
be a directed POSET. For each
\alpha\in\Lambda,
the quotient algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
is a commutative unital
C^{\ast}
-algebra and
\pi_{\alpha}\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
is a continuous cononical quotient map. We denote the character space of
\mathcal{A}
by
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
and define as,

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}=\Big\{\Phi\colon\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mdmathbb{C}\;\text{is multiplicative linear map}\;:\;\Phi=\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha},\text{ for some }\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}},\;\alpha\in\Lambda\Big\}.

It is evident from Definition
2.5
that
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
is non-empty set since each
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}
is non-empty, for
\alpha\in\Lambda
. Moreover, if
\Phi\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
then
\Phi
is a unital map. Since
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{\ast}
, the class of all continuous linear functionals on
\mathcal{A}
, one can consider
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
is equipped with weak
∗
-topology. However, unlike the case of
C^{\ast}
-algebras,
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
may not be weak
∗
-compact (see Example
2.6
).

Example 2.6
.

Consider the commutative unital locally
C^{\ast}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}=C(\mdmathbb{R}).
It is shown in Example
1.6
that

C(\mdmathbb{R})=\varprojlim\limits_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}C\left([-n,n]\right).

By Eberlein-Smulian theorem,
\mathcal{M}_{C(\mdmathbb{R})}
is weak
∗
-compact if and only if every sequence in
\mathcal{M}_{C(\mdmathbb{R})}
has a convergent subsequence. Let
\nu_{x}
denotes the evaluation functional at
x\in\mdmathbb{R}
given by

\nu_{x}(f)=f(x),\text{ for all }f\in C(\mdmathbb{R}).

Now consider the sequence
\{n\}_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}
and a continuous function
g\colon\mdmathbb{R}\rightarrow\mdmathbb{R}
given by
g(x)=x,
for all
x\in\mdmathbb{R}.
Since the sequence
\{\nu_{n}(g)=n\}_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}
is not cauchy in
\mdmathbb{R},
it follows that the sequence
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}
of evaluation functionals does not have a weak
∗
-convergent subsequence and consequently
\mathcal{M}_{C(\mdmathbb{R})}
is not weak
∗
-compact.

2.1.
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
is the inductive limit of
\{\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}

Next, we explore the connection between our notion of character space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
(see Definition
2.5
) and the well known maximal ideal space of a commutative
C^{\ast}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
, where
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}
, for
\alpha\in\Lambda
(see Remark
1.4
). Let us recall that the map
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}\colon\mathcal{A}_{\beta}\to\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
defined by
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(a+\mathcal{I}_{\beta})=a+\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}
is surjective
C^{\ast}
-homomorphism (whenever
\alpha\leq\beta
) and
\left(\big\{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\big\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda},\;\{\pi_{\alpha,\beta}\}_{\alpha\leq\beta}\right)
is projective system such that

\mathcal{A}=\varprojlim\limits_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}.

Firstly, assume that
\alpha\leq\beta.
If
\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}},

then for
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
we see that

\displaystyle\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta}\left((a+\mathcal{I}_{\beta})\cdot(b+\mathcal{I}_{\beta})\right)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta}\big(a\cdot b+\mathcal{I}_{\beta}\big)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\alpha}\big(a\cdot b+\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\big)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\alpha}\big((a+\mathcal{I}_{\alpha})\cdot(b+\mathcal{I}_{\alpha})\big)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\alpha}\big(a+\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\big)\cdot\varphi_{\alpha}\big(b+\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\big)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta}\big(a+\mathcal{I}_{\beta}\big)\cdot\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta}\big(b+\mathcal{I}_{\beta}\big).

That is,
\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\beta}}.
In view of this, whenever
\alpha\leq\beta
, there exists a natural map
\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}$}}_{\beta,\alpha}\colon\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\beta}}
defined by

\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}$}}_{\beta,\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha})=\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta},\text{ for all }\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}.

To show that
\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}$}}_{\beta,\alpha}
is continuous, let us consider an open set
V=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{N}\nu_{\pi_{\beta}(x_{i})}^{-1}(U)
in
\mathcal{A}_{\beta}
for some open
U
in
\mdmathbb{C},
where
\nu_{\pi_{\beta}(x_{i})}
is evaluation map and
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{N}\in\mathcal{A}
then

\displaystyle\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}$}}_{\beta,\alpha}^{-1}(V)

\displaystyle=\Big\{\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\colon\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta}\in\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{N}\nu_{\pi_{\beta}(x_{i})}^{-1}(U)\Big\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap^{N}_{i=1}\Big\{\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\colon\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta}\in\nu^{-1}_{\pi_{\beta}(x_{i})}(U)\Big\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap^{n}_{i=1}\Big\{\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\colon\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\pi_{\beta}(x_{i}))\in U\Big\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap^{n}_{i=1}\Big\{\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\colon\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}(x_{i})\in U\Big\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{N}\nu_{\pi_{\alpha}(x_{i})}^{-1}(U),

which is open in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}
. This implies that
\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}$}}_{\beta,\alpha}
is continuous whenever
\alpha\leq\beta
. Also
\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha,\alpha}
is the identity map on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}.
Moreover, if
\alpha\leq\beta\leq\delta
then

(2.3)

\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}$}}_{\delta,\beta}\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}$}}_{\beta,\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha})=\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}$}}_{\delta,\beta}(\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta})=\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta}\circ\pi_{\beta,\delta}=\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\delta}=\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}$}}_{\delta,\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha}),

for all
\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}.
By adopting the notion given in Definition
1.8
, we say that
\left(\{\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda},\{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\beta,\alpha}\}_{\alpha\leq\beta}\right)
is an inductive system of topological spaces (more precisely, weakly compact spaces).

In order to compute the inductive limit of inductive system
\left(\{\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda},\{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\beta,\alpha}\}_{\alpha\leq\beta}\right)
, we construct a corresponding strict inductive system.
For each
\alpha\in\Lambda,
define

\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}:=\big\{\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}\colon\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\big\}.

It is the collection of all those multiplicative linear functionals
\mathcal{A}
induced from
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}.
We shall show that
\big\{\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}\big\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
is a strict inductive system. To see this, let us assume that
\alpha\leq\beta
and
\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}
then from previous observation, we know that
{}_{\beta}:=\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\beta}}
. By using the fact that
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}\circ\pi_{\beta}=\pi_{\alpha}
, we get

\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}=\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\left(\pi_{\alpha,\beta}\circ\pi_{\beta}\right)={}_{\beta}\circ\pi_{\beta}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}.

It follows that
\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}
whenever
\alpha\leq\beta
. Now we define a topology on
\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}.
For
\alpha\in\Lambda
, let
�
be the collection of all subsets

of the form

for some open set
\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}
in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}.
Then we see that

(1)

both
\emptyset,\;\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}\in{}_{\alpha}

(2)

, we get

(2.6)

\left(\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\beta}\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\beta,\alpha}\right)(\varphi_{\alpha})=\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\beta}(\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta})=\left(\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta}\right)\circ\pi_{\beta}=\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}=\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha}),

for every
\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}
. As a result,
\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\beta}\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\beta,\alpha}=\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha}
whenever
\alpha\leq\beta.
Since
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
is a topological space and
\{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
is a family of continuous maps satisfying Equation (
2.6
), we see that
\left(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}},\{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\right)
is compatible with the inductive system
\left(\{\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda},\{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\beta,\alpha}\}_{\alpha\leq\beta}\right).
Therefore,

(2.7)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}=\varinjlim_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}.

Remark 2.7
.

There is a comparison between inductive limit topology and weak
∗
-topology on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}.

(1)

The inductive limit topology on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
is finer than the weak
∗
-topology on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
. This is because, if
\bigcap\limits^{N}_{i=1}\nu^{-1}_{x_{i}}(U)
is open in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}},
for some
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{N}\in\mathcal{A}
and some open set
U
in
\mdmathbb{C},
then

\displaystyle\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}^{-1}_{\alpha}\Big(\bigcap^{N}_{i=1}\nu^{-1}_{x_{i}}(U)\Big)

\displaystyle=\big\{\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\colon\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}\in\bigcap^{N}_{i=1}\nu^{-1}_{x_{i}}(U)\big\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap^{N}_{i=1}\big\{\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\colon\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}\in\nu^{-1}_{x_{i}}(U)\big\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap^{N}_{i=1}\big\{\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\colon\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}(x_{i})\in U\big\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap^{N}_{i=1}\nu^{-1}_{\pi_{\alpha}(x_{i})}(U),

which is weak
∗
-open in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}.
This is,
\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha}
is continuous for every
\alpha\in\Lambda.

(2)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}
is homeomorphic to
\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}.
Consider the continuous map
i_{\alpha}^{-1}\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha}\colon\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\to\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha},
where
i_{\alpha}^{-1}\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha})=\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha},
for all
\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}.
Note that,
i_{\alpha}^{-1}\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha}
is bijective. To see this, consider
\varphi_{\alpha},\;{}_{\alpha}
in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}
such that
i_{\alpha}^{-1}\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha})=i_{\alpha}^{-1}\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha}({}_{\alpha}).
Then

\displaystyle\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}={}_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\implies\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}(a)={}_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}(a),\text{ for all }a\in\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\implies\varphi_{\alpha}(a+\mathcal{I}_{\alpha})={}_{\alpha}(a+\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}),\text{ for all }a\in\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\implies\varphi_{\alpha}={}_{\alpha}.

Therefore
i_{\alpha}^{-1}\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha}
is injective. Moreover by the definition of
i_{\alpha}^{-1}\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha},
it is surjective, hence bijective. Further, for any open set
S=\left\{\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}\colon\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}\right\}
in
\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha},
where
\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}
is open in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}},

(i_{\alpha}^{-1}\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha})^{-1}(S)=\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha}^{-1}\circ i_{\alpha}(S)=\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}^{-1}_{\alpha}\left(\{\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}\colon\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}\}\right)=\mathcal{O}_{\alpha},

which is weak
∗
-open in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}.
Therefore,
(i_{\alpha}^{-1}\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha})^{-1}
is continuous and consequently,
i_{\alpha}^{-1}\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha}
is a homeomorphism.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a commutative unital locally
C^{\ast}
-algebra. Then
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
with inductive limit topology is completely regular. In particular,

(1)

if the underlying directed POSET
\Lambda=\mdmathbb{N}
, then
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
is
\sigma
-compact.

(2)

If
\mathcal{A}
is a locally von-Nuemann algebra, then
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
is the inductive limit of extremally disconnected spaces.

Proof.

We know from
(2)
of Remark
2.7
that
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}
is homeomorphic to
\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha},
for every
\alpha\in\Lambda.
Since
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}
is weak
∗
-compact and Hausdorff, it follows that
\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}
is compact and Hausdorff with respect to
{}_{\alpha}.
Further, from Equation (
2.4
), it follows that
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
is locally compact and Hausdorff (being strict inductive limit of Hausdorff spaces). Therefore
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
possesses a one point compactification, say
\mathring{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\mathring{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{A}}
is compact Hausdorff space, hence normal. This implies that
\mathring{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{A}}
is completely regular and so
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
.

Proof of
(1)
: Suppose
\Lambda=\mdmathbb{N},
then
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}=\bigcup\limits_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}\mathcal{Z}_{n},
the countable union of compact sets and hence
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
is
\sigma
-compact.

Proof of
(2)
: In particular if
\mathcal{A}
is commutative unital locally von Neumann algebra, then each
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
is commutative unital von Neumann algebra. By Theorem 9.6 of
[
16
]
, the maximal ideal space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}
is extremally disconnected space (in fact it is a Stonean space) for each
\alpha\in\Lambda.
So
\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}
is extremally disconnected from
(2)
of Remark
2.7
. It follows that,
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
is the (strict) inductive limit of extremally disconnected spaces.
∎

Note 2.9
.

Now we point out a few observations in regard to maximal ideals in a commutative unital locally
C^{\ast}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
and give a relation between the character space of
\mathcal{A}
and certain type of maximal ideals. Firstly note that, if
\Phi\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}},
then
\Phi=\varphi_{\beta}\circ\pi_{\beta},
for some
\beta\in\Lambda
and
\varphi_{\beta}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\beta}}.
It follows that

\displaystyle J:=Ker(\Phi)

\displaystyle=\big\{x\in\mathcal{A}\colon\Phi(x)=0\big\}

\displaystyle=\big\{x\in\mathcal{A}\colon\varphi_{\beta}(\pi_{\beta}(x))=0\big\}

\displaystyle=\big\{x\in\mathcal{A}\colon\pi_{\beta}(x)\in Ker(\varphi_{\beta})\big\}.

It is immediate to see that,
\mathcal{I}_{\beta}\subseteq J
and
\pi_{\beta}(J)=Ker(\varphi_{\beta})
is a maximal ideal in
\mathcal{A}_{\beta}
(follows from
[
16
, Theorem 4.3]
). Consequently,
J
is a maximal ideal. This can be seen as follows: suppose
J
is not a maximal ideal, then there is a proper ideal
J^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{A}
such that
J\subsetneq J^{\prime}\subsetneq\mathcal{A}
. There exists an
x\in J^{\prime}
with
x\notin J
and so,
\pi_{\alpha}(x)\in\pi_{\alpha}(J^{\prime})\setminus\pi_{\alpha}(J)
. Since
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\subseteq J
, we see that
\pi_{\alpha}(J^{\prime})
is a proper ideal in
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
such that
\pi_{\alpha}(J)\subsetneq\pi_{\alpha}(J^{\prime})\subsetneq\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}.
This is a contradiction to the fact that
\pi_{\alpha}(J)
is maximal in
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}.

From the above observation, the maximal ideals of
\mathcal{A}
containing
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in\Lambda
seem to have a connection with the members of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
. The following result gives one to one correspondence between them.

Theorem 2.10
.

If
\mathcal{A}
is a commutative unital locally
C^{\ast}
-algebra, then
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
is in one to one correspondence with the maximal ideals in
\mathcal{A}
containing
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha},

for some
\alpha\in\Lambda.

Proof.

Firstly, take
\Phi=\varphi_{\beta}\circ\pi_{\beta}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
for some
\beta\in\Lambda,
then by Note
2.9

\mathcal{I}_{\beta}\subseteq Ker(\Phi)
and
Ker(\Phi)
is a maximal ideal. Conversely, if
J
is a maximal ideal in
\mathcal{A}
containing
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha},
for some
\alpha\in\Lambda.
We show that
\pi_{\alpha}(J)
is maximal ideal in
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}.
If possible assume that
\pi_{\alpha}(J)
is not maximal in
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha},
then there is a proper ideal
K^{(\alpha)}
in
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
with

\pi_{\alpha}(J)\subsetneq K^{(\alpha)}\subsetneq\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}.
This implies that
\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(K^{(\alpha)})
is a proper ideal in
\mathcal{A}
satisfying,

J\subsetneq\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(K^{(\alpha)})\subsetneq\mathcal{A}.

This is a contradiction to the fact that
J
is a maximal ideal in
\mathcal{A}.
Therefore
\pi_{\alpha}(J)
is maximal in
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}.
By Theorem
4.3
of
[
16
]
, there exists
{}_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}
such that
Ker({}_{\alpha})=\pi_{\alpha}(J).
It follows that the map
\Psi={}_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
and since
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\subseteq J,
we have

\displaystyle Ker(\Psi)

\displaystyle=\big\{x\in\mathcal{A}\colon\;\pi_{\alpha}(x)\in Ker({}_{\alpha})\big\}=\big\{x\in\mathcal{A}\colon x+\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\in\pi_{\alpha}(J)\big\}=J.

We conclude that the map
\Phi\mapsto Ker(\Phi)
defines one-to-one correspondence between the space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
and the collection of all maximal ideals in
\mathcal{A}
containing
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in\Lambda.

∎

3.
Gelfand type representation of Locally C* Algebras

In this section, we establish a
Gelfand type representation
of commutative unital locally
C^{\ast}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}.
Recall that if
(\Lambda,\leq)
is the underlying directed POSET, then
\mathcal{A}=\varprojlim\limits_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
, where
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}:=\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}
is a commutative unital
C^{\ast}
-algebra and
\big(\{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda},\{\pi_{\alpha,\beta}\}_{\alpha\leq\beta}\big)
is a projective system (see Definition
1.1
). From the well-known Gelfand representation theorem
[
16
]
of commutative unital
C^{\ast}
-algebra, for each
\alpha\in\Lambda
, there is an isometric
\ast
-isomorphism (
C^{\ast}
- representation)
{\Gamma}_{\alpha}\colon\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\to C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}})
given by

(3.1)

\Gamma_{\alpha}(x_{\alpha})(\varphi_{\alpha})=\varphi_{\alpha}(x_{\alpha}),\text{ for all }x_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\,,\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}.

Note that
\Gamma_{\alpha}(x_{\alpha})
is a continuous function on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}
for each
\alpha\in\Lambda.

Theorem 3.1
.

For a commutative unital locally
C^{\ast}
-algebra
\mathcal{A},
we have

C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}})=\varprojlim\limits_{\alpha\in\Lambda}C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}).

In other words,
C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}})
is a commutative unital locally
C^{\ast}
-algebra.

Proof.

Let us consider the family
\Big\{C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}})\Big\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
of commutative unital
C^{\ast}
-algebras and whenever
\alpha\leq\beta,
define
\xi_{\alpha,\beta}:C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\beta}})\rightarrow C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}})
by

\xi_{\alpha,\beta}(f_{\beta})=f_{\beta}\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\beta,\alpha}\text{ for all }f_{\beta}\in C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\beta}}),

where
\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\beta,\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha})=\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta}
is a continuous map from
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}
to
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\beta}}

(see Section
2.1
).
It is easy to see that, for
\alpha\leq\beta,

\xi_{\alpha,\beta}
is a
C^{\ast}
-homomorphism. The surjectivity of
\xi_{\alpha,\beta}
can be seen as follows: suppose
f_{\alpha}\in C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}})
then the map
g_{\alpha}\colon\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}\to\mdmathbb{C}
given by

g_{\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha})=f_{\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha}),\;\text{for all}\;\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}.

is a well-defined and continuous. Since
\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}
is normal
\big(
being compact Hausdorff space with respect to
{}_{\alpha}\big)
and
\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}
is compact in
\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}
, by Tietze-extension theorem, there is a continuous map
g_{\beta}\colon\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}\to\mdmathbb{C}
such that

g_{\beta}(\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha})=g_{\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}),\;\text{for every}\;\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}.

Now define the map
f_{\beta}\colon\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\beta}}\to\mdmathbb{C}
as,

f_{\beta}(\varphi_{\beta})=g_{\beta}(\varphi_{\beta}\circ\pi_{\beta}),\;\text{for all}\;\varphi_{\beta}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\beta}}.

It follows that
f_{\beta}\in C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\beta}}).
Moreover, if
\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}
, then
\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\beta}}
and we get

\displaystyle\xi_{\alpha,\beta}(f_{\beta})(\varphi_{\alpha})=(f_{\beta}\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\beta,\alpha})(\varphi_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=f_{\beta}\left(\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=g_{\beta}\left((\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta})\circ\pi_{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=g_{\beta}\left(\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=g_{\alpha}\left(\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=f_{\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha}).

As
\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}
is chosen arbitrarily, we conclude that
\xi_{\alpha,\beta}(f_{\beta})=f_{\alpha}.
Thus
\xi_{\alpha,\beta}
is a surjective
C^{\ast}
-homomorphism, whenever
\alpha\leq\beta.
The following diagram gives a clear description about the surjectivity of
\xi_{\alpha,\beta}
via bridge maps.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\beta}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mdmathbb{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}}
\scriptstyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{\beta}^{-1}\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\gamma$}}_{\beta}}
\scriptstyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{\beta}}
\scriptstyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{\beta}}
\scriptstyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{\alpha}}

\scriptstyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{\alpha}}

Further, whenever
\alpha\leq\beta\leq\delta
and
f_{\delta}\in C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\delta}})
, by using Equation (
2.3
) we see that

\left(\xi_{\alpha,\beta}\circ\xi_{\beta,\delta}\right)(f_{\delta})=\xi_{\alpha,\beta}(f_{\delta}\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\delta,\beta})=\left(f_{\delta}\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\delta,\beta}\right)\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\beta,\alpha}=f_{\delta}\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\delta,\alpha}=\xi_{\alpha,\delta}(f_{\delta}).

This shows that
\xi_{\alpha,\beta}\circ\xi_{\beta,\delta}=\xi_{\alpha,\delta}
whenever
\alpha\leq\beta\leq\delta
and hence
\left(\{C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}})\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda},\{\xi_{\alpha,\beta}\}_{\alpha\leq\beta}\right)
is a projective system of
C^{\ast}
-algebras.

On the other hand, for each
\alpha\in\Lambda
, we define
q_{\alpha}(f)=\|f\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha}\|_{\infty},
where
\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha}\colon\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
is a continuous map (see Subsection
2.1
) and
f\in C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}).
Note that
f\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha}\in C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}})
and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}
is compact, so
f\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha}
is bounded. Whenever
\alpha\leq\beta
and
f\in C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}})
, we have

\displaystyle q_{\alpha}(f)=\|f\circ{}_{\alpha}\|_{\infty}=\sup\Big\{|f\circ{}_{\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha})|\colon\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sup\Big\{|f(\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha})|\colon\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sup\Big\{\left|f\left((\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta})\circ\pi_{\beta}\right)\right|\colon\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\Big\}.

Since
\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\beta}}
for every
\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}
, it follows that
q_{\alpha}(f)\leq q_{\beta}(f).
Also, we get

q_{\alpha}(f^{\ast}f)=q_{\alpha}(|f|^{2})=\left\||f|^{2}\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha}\right\|_{\infty}=\sup\Big\{|f(\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha})|^{2}:\;\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\Big\}=q_{\alpha}(f)^{2}.

Thus
\big\{q_{\alpha}\big\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
is an upward filtered family of
C^{\ast}
-seminorms on
C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}})
. As a result, we consider the unital
\ast
-algebra
C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}})
equipped with the locally convex topology induced by the family
\big\{q_{\alpha}\big\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
. Finally, for each
\alpha\in\Lambda
, let
\xi_{\alpha}:C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}})
be defined by

\xi_{\alpha}(f)=f\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha},\text{ for all }f\in C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}).

To show that
\xi_{\alpha}
is continuous, let
U
be open in
C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}})
given by

U=\left\{g\in C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}})\colon\|g\|_{\infty}<r\right\},
for some
r>0.

Then,

\displaystyle\xi^{-1}_{\alpha}(U)

\displaystyle=\left\{f\in C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}})\colon\|f\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha}\|_{\infty}<r\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{f\in C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}})\colon q_{\alpha}(f)<r\right\}

\displaystyle=q^{-1}_{\alpha}(B(0,r)),

where
B(0,r)
is an open ball in
\mdmathbb{C}
of radius
r
centered at
0,
is open in locally convex topology in
C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}).
Thus,
\xi_{\alpha}
is continuous. Further, we have

\xi_{\alpha,\beta}\circ\xi_{\beta}(f)=\xi_{\alpha,\beta}(f\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\beta})=f\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\beta}\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\beta,\alpha}=f\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha}=\xi_{\alpha}(f),

for all
f\in C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}})
whenever
\alpha\leq\beta.
This shows that
\big(C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}),\,\{\xi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\big)
is compatible with the projective system
\big(\{C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}})\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda},\{\xi_{\alpha,\beta}\}_{\alpha\leq\beta}\big)
and hence

C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}})=\varprojlim\limits_{\alpha\in\Lambda}C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}).

∎

Remark 3.2
.

Every
f\in C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}})
can be identified with the net
\big\{f\circ{}_{\alpha}\big\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\in\prod\limits_{\alpha\in\Lambda}C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}).
Indeed the net
\big\{f\circ{}_{\alpha}\big\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
is such that
\xi_{\alpha,\beta}(f\circ{}_{\beta})=f\circ{}_{\beta}\circ{}_{\beta,\alpha}=f\circ{}_{\alpha},
whenever
\alpha\leq\beta.

3.1.
Gelfand type representation

Recall that
\mathcal{A}
is a commutative unital locally
C^{\ast}
-algebra and for each
\alpha\in\Lambda
, the Gelfand representation of
C^{\ast}
- algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
, denoted by
\Gamma_{\alpha}
(see Equation (
3.1
)). We give a Gelfand type representation for
\mathcal{A}
and establish a connection with the family
\{\Gamma_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}.
Since the character space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}
consists of all those multiplicative linear functional of the form
\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in\Lambda
and
\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}
, we define
\Gamma\colon\mathcal{A}\rightarrow C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}})
by

(3.2)

\Gamma(a)(\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha})=\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}(a)=\varphi_{\alpha}(a+\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}),\text{ for all }a\in\mathcal{A}.

In the following theorem we show that
\Gamma
is a coherent representation of locally
C^{\ast}
-algebras.

Theorem 3.3
.

The map
\Gamma
defined in Equation (
3.2
) is a coherent representation of locally
C^{\ast}
-algebras
\mathcal{A}
and
C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}).
In fact,
\Gamma
is a unital local contractive (isometric)
\ast
- homomorphism.

Proof.

We know from Section
2
that
\big(\mathcal{A},\{\pi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\big)
is a projective limit of
\big(\{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda},\;\{\pi_{\alpha,\beta}\}_{\alpha\leq\beta}\}\big)
and as shown in Theorem
3.1

\big(C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}),\;\{\xi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\big)
is a projective limit of
\big(\{C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}})\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda},\;\{\xi_{\alpha,\beta}\}_{\alpha\leq\beta}\big).
Firstly note that, for a fixed
\alpha\in\Lambda,\;a\in\mathcal{A},\;\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}},
we see that

\displaystyle\xi_{\alpha}\big(\Gamma(a)\big)(\varphi_{\alpha})=\Gamma(a)\big({}_{\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha})\big)=\Gamma(a)(\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=(\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha})(a)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\alpha}(a+\mathcal{I}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\alpha}(a+\mathcal{I}_{\alpha})(\varphi_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\alpha}\left(\pi_{\alpha}(a)\right)(\varphi_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\big(\Gamma_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}\big)(a)(\varphi_{\alpha}).

Since
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}
are chosen arbitrarily, we conclude that

\xi_{\alpha}\circ\Gamma=\Gamma_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha},\text{ for every }\alpha\in\Lambda.

Here we have shown that, there exists a net
\big\{\Gamma_{\alpha}\big\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
of
C^{\ast}
-representations
\Gamma_{\alpha}\colon\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\to C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}),\;\alpha\in\Lambda
such that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\alpha}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{\alpha}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\xi_{\alpha}}

Equivalently, whenever
\alpha\leq\beta,\;\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}
and
a\in\mathcal{A},
we have

\displaystyle\xi_{\alpha,\beta}\big(\Gamma_{\beta}(a+\mathcal{I}_{\beta})\big)(\varphi_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\beta}(a+\mathcal{I}_{\beta})\circ{}_{\beta,\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\beta}(a+\mathcal{I}_{\beta})(\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta})

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(a+\mathcal{I}_{\beta})

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\alpha}(\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(a+\mathcal{I}_{\beta}))

\displaystyle=\big(\Gamma_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta}\big)(a+\mathcal{I}_{\beta})(\varphi_{\alpha}).

Since
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}
are chosen arbitrarily, we get that

\xi_{\alpha,\beta}\circ\Gamma_{\beta}=\Gamma_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha,\beta},\text{ whenever }\alpha\leq\beta.

Hence
\Gamma
is a coherent representation of locally
C^{\ast}
-algebras. Now we show that
\Gamma
is a unital local contractive (isometric)
\ast
-homomorphism. If
1_{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathcal{A}
is a unit, then

\Gamma(1_{\mathcal{A}})(\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha})=\varphi_{\alpha}(\pi_{\alpha}(1_{\mathcal{A}}))=\varphi_{\alpha}(1_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}})=1

for every
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}},
so
\Gamma
is unital. Clearly,
\Gamma
is a homomorphism. For
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}},\;\alpha\in\Lambda
we have

\Gamma(a^{\ast})(\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha})=\varphi_{\alpha}\big(a^{\ast}+\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\big)=\varphi_{\alpha}\big((a+\mathcal{I}_{\alpha})^{\ast}\big)=\varphi_{\alpha}\big(a+\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\big)^{\ast}=\Gamma(a)^{\ast}(\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}).

Thus
\Gamma(a^{\ast})=\Gamma(a)^{\ast}.
Further, by using the fact that
\Gamma_{\alpha}
is an isometry for each
\alpha\in\Lambda
, we get

\displaystyle q_{\alpha}(\Gamma(a))=\|\Gamma(a)\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sup\Big\{|\left(\Gamma(a)\circ\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\gamma$}}_{\alpha}\right)(\varphi_{\alpha})|:\;\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sup\Big\{|\Gamma(a)\left(\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}\right)|:\;\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sup\Big\{|\varphi_{\alpha}(\pi_{\alpha}(a))|:\;\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sup\Big\{|\Gamma_{\alpha}\left(\pi_{\alpha}(a)\right)(\varphi_{\alpha})|:\;\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\left\|\Gamma_{\alpha}\left(\pi_{\alpha}(a)\right)\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\|\pi_{\alpha}(a)\|

\displaystyle=p_{\alpha}(a),

for every
\alpha\in\Lambda.
Hence
\Gamma
is a local contractive (isometric)
\ast
-homomorphism.
∎

3.2.
The Continuous Functional Calculus

In this subsection, we define the functional calculus of locally bounded normal operator on a locally Hilbert space. Recall that if
\mathcal{E}:=\{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
is an upward filtered family of Hilbert spaces and
\mathcal{D}:=\varinjlim\limits_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}=\bigcup\limits_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
is a locally Hilbert space, then
C^{\ast}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{D})
is a unital locally
C^{\ast}
-algebra
(
see (1) of Definition
1.14
)
. Firstly, we discuss the spectrum of locally bounded operators. Let
T\in C^{\ast}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{D}).
Then
T
is locally bounded, that is, each
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
is reducing under
T
and
T_{\alpha}:=T\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha})
for every
\alpha\in\Lambda
. The
local resolvent
of
T
is defined as,

\rho_{\it loc}(T):=\Big\{\lambda\in\mdmathbb{C}\colon\;\left(\lambda\cdot I_{\mathcal{D}}-T\right)\text{ is invertible in}\;C^{\ast}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{D})\Big\},

where
I_{\mathcal{D}}
is the identity operator on
\mathcal{D}.
In other words,
\lambda\in\rho_{loc}(T)
if and only if there is a unique (locally bounded operator)
S\in C_{\mathcal{E}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{D})
such that

(3.3)

S\left(\lambda I_{\mathcal{D}}-T\right)=\left(\lambda I_{\mathcal{D}}-T\right)S=I_{\mathcal{D}}.

The
local spectrum
of
T
is denoted by
\sigma_{\it loc}(T)
and we define this as,

(3.4)

\sigma_{loc}(T)=\mdmathbb{C}\setminus\rho_{\textit{l}oc}(T).

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
T\in C^{\ast}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{D})
. Then
\sigma_{loc}(T)
is a non-empty subset of
\mdmathbb{C}
. Moreover,

\sigma_{loc}(T)=\bigcup\limits_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\sigma(T_{\alpha}),

where
T_{\alpha}:=T\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}}
and
\sigma(T_{\alpha})
is the spectrum of the bounded operator
T_{\alpha}.

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in\mdmathbb{C}.
Then
\lambda\in\rho_{loc}(T)
if and only if there is a unique
S\in C^{\ast}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{D})
satisfying Equation (
3.3
). Since
S
is locally bounded, we know that
S_{\alpha}:=S\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha})
and

S_{\alpha}(\lambda I_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}}-T_{\alpha})=(\lambda I_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}}-T_{\alpha})S_{\alpha}=I_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}},\;\text{for every}\;\alpha\in\Lambda.

That is,
\lambda\in\rho(T_{\alpha})
for every
\alpha\in\Lambda.
We have shown that
\rho_{loc}(T)=\bigcap\limits_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\rho(T_{\alpha}).
Equivalently,

\sigma_{loc}(T)=\bigcup\limits_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\sigma(T_{\alpha}).

Since
\sigma(T_{\alpha})
is a non-empty compact subset of
\mdmathbb{C}
, it follows that
\sigma_{loc}(T)
is non-emtpy.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

A locally bounded operator
T\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}
can be seen as a densely defined (unbounded) operator, not necessarily closed.
Suppose
\mathcal{H}
is the completion of
\mathcal{D}
. The (unbounded) spectrum of
T\in C^{\ast}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{D})
is given by

\sigma(T)=\big\{\lambda\in\mdmathbb{C}:\;(\lambda I_{\mathcal{D}}-T)\;\text{does not have a bounded inverse}\big\}.

The reason we call the notion of spectrum of locally bounded operator
T
as “local spectrum” and denoted by
\sigma_{loc}(T)
in order to point out that it is different from
\sigma(T).
The same is described in the following example.

Example 3.7
.

Let us consider the locally Hilbert space
\mathcal{D}=\bigcup\limits_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}\mathcal{H}_{n}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{n}:=span\{e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}\}
subspace of
\ell^{2}(\mdmathbb{N})

(
as in Example
1.13

)
for each
n\in\mdmathbb{N}
. Now define
T:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
by

T=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&0&0&\cdots\\
0&\frac{1}{2}&0&0&0&\cdots\\
0&0&3&0&0&\cdots\\
0&0&0&\frac{1}{4}&0&\cdots\\
0&0&0&0&5&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}

That is,
T(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},\cdots)=\left(x_{1},\frac{x_{2}}{2},3x_{3},\frac{x_{4}}{4},\cdots\right)
for every
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{D}.
It follows that each
\mathcal{H}_{n}
is reducing under
T
and
T_{n}=T\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{n}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{n})
and so,
T\in C^{\ast}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{D}).
Also,
T
is an unbounded operator. Now we show that
T
is not closed. Let us consider the sequence
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{D},
where

X_{1}=e_{1}\;\;\text{and}\;\;X_{n}=e_{1}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{2k}e_{2k},\;\;\text{for}\;n\geq 1.

If
X:=e_{1}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2k}e_{2k},\;Y:=e_{1}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4k^{2}}e_{2k}
, then
X,Y\in\ell^{2}(\mdmathbb{N})
such that

\big\{X_{n}\big\}_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}\longrightarrow X\;\text{and}\;\big\{T(X_{n})\big\}_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}=\left\{e_{1}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{4k^{2}}e_{2k}\right\}_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}\longrightarrow Y,

as
n\to\infty,
but
X\notin\mathcal{D}.
This shows that
T
is not a closed operator.

Firstly, note that each
T_{n}
is a finite rank operator. So, the spectrum of
T_{n}
is computed as,

\sigma(T_{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\big\{1,\frac{1}{2},3\cdots 2m-1\big\},&\text{if $n=2m-1$ is odd};\\
&\\
\big\{1,\frac{1}{2},3\cdots 2m-1,\frac{1}{2m}\big\},&\text{if $n=2m$ is even}.\end{array}\right.

Therefore, the local spectrum of
T
is,

\sigma_{loc}(T)=\Big\{1,\frac{1}{2},3,\frac{1}{4},5,\cdots\Big\}.

On the other hand, the (unbounded) spectrum of
T
, is given by
\sigma(T)=\big\{2k-1,\frac{1}{2k}:k\in\mdmathbb{N}\big\}\cup\{0\}
. Indeed,
0
is in
approximate point spectrum
of
T
. Since
0\notin\sigma_{loc}(T)
, we conclude that
\sigma_{loc}(T)\subsetneq\sigma(T).

Note 3.8
.

Suppose that
T\in C^{\ast}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{D})
is a normal operator, then it follows that
T_{\alpha}=T\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}}
is a bounded normal operator on
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
for every
\alpha\in\Lambda.
Now, for each

, consider

Since

\alpha\leq\beta\leq\gamma,

-algebras. By the construction of projective limit given in Equation (

C^{\ast}
-algebra.

Now we define the locally
C^{\ast}
-algebra generated by given locally bounded operator.

Definition 3.9

-algebra generated by

Here the symbol

denotes a locally bounded normal operator on
\mathcal{D}
whose restriction to
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
is
R_{\alpha},
for every
\alpha\in\Lambda
(see Remark
1.15
).

It is clear that
\mathcal{A}[T]
is a subalgebra of
C^{\ast}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{D}).
Now we show that
\mathcal{A}[T]
is a commutative unital locally
C^{\ast}
-algebra. Firstly note that, the map
\phi_{\alpha}\colon\mathcal{A}[T]\rightarrow C^{\ast}\{I_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}},T_{\alpha},T_{\alpha}^{\ast}\}

\phi_{\alpha}(R)=R\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}},\text{ for all }R=\varprojlim\limits_{\alpha\in\Lambda}R_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{A}[T]

is a
\ast
-homomorphism.
Further,
\mathcal{A}[T]

\ast

1.4

\mathcal{A}[T]

C^{\ast}
-algebra.
In order to develop functional calculus for a locally bounded normal operator, we shall understand the relation between the character space

We give the result below.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
T\in C^{\ast}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{D})
be normal. Then the character space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}[T]}
is homeomorphic to
\sigma_{loc}(T).

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{A}[T]
is the projective limit of the projective system
\left(\big\{C^{\ast}\{I_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}},T_{\alpha},T_{\alpha}^{\ast}\}\big\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda},\;\big\{\phi_{\alpha,\beta}\big\}_{\alpha\leq\beta}\right),
consisting of locally bounded operators

satisfying
\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(R_{\beta})=R_{\alpha}
whenever
\alpha\leq\beta,

there is a natural way of defining seminorm
s_{\alpha}(R)=\|R_{\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}},
for every
\alpha\in\Lambda.
This gives an upward filtered family
\{s_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
of
C^{\ast}
-seminorms that generate complete Hausdorff locally convex topology on
\mathcal{A}[T].
If we define
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}=s^{-1}_{\alpha}(0),
then
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}
is a closed two sided
\ast
-ideal in
\mathcal{A}[T]
and
\mathcal{A}[T]/\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}
is a
C^{\ast}
-algebra with the norm induced by
s_{\alpha}
, for every
\alpha\in\Lambda.
Now for a fixed
\alpha\in\Lambda,
we define
\theta_{\alpha}\colon\mathcal{A}[T]/\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\longrightarrow C^{\ast}\{I_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}},\;T_{\alpha},T^{\ast}_{\alpha}\}

This shows that
\theta_{\alpha}

\theta_{\alpha}
is

-isomorphism. Let

\displaystyle\left\|\theta_{\alpha}\left(R+\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}}=\|R_{\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}}=s_{\alpha}\left(R\right).

under the map

is an isometric
\ast
-isomorphism of

-algebras. As a consequence, we have that maximal ideal spaces

16

∗

for each
\alpha\in\Lambda.

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}[T]/\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}}

\sigma(T_{\alpha})
for each
\alpha\in\Lambda.

On the other hand, from Definition
2.5
, the character space of the commutative unital locally

Next we turn our discussion towards the functional calculus of locally bounded normal operator

.
Note that each

\sigma(T_{\alpha})\subseteq\sigma(T_{\beta})
since
T_{\beta}\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}}=T_{\alpha}
whenever
\alpha\leq\beta.
As a result,
\sigma_{loc}(T)=\bigcup\limits_{\alpha}{\sigma(T_{\alpha})}
can be seen as an (strictly) inductive limit of the strictly inductive family
\{\sigma(T_{\alpha})\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
of toplogical space. In fact, by Proposition
2.8
we see that
\sigma_{loc}(T)
is completely regular. Similar to the result proved in Theorem
3.1
,
C\big(\sigma_{loc}(T)\big)
is a commutative unital locally
C^{\ast}
-algebra. A continuous function
f\in C\big(\sigma_{loc}(T)\big)
is uniquely represented by its restrictions
f\big|_{\sigma(T_{\alpha})}
, for
\alpha\in\Lambda.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
T\in C^{\ast}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{D})
is normal and
f\in C\big(\sigma_{loc}(T)\big).
For every
\alpha\in\Lambda
, define
f_{\alpha}:=f\big|_{\sigma(T_{\alpha})}
then the family
\Big\{\Gamma^{-1}_{\alpha}(f_{\alpha})\Big\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
of bounded operators satisfy that
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
is a reducing subspace of
\Gamma^{-1}_{\beta}(f_{\beta})
and

\Gamma^{-1}_{\beta}(f_{\beta})\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}}=\Gamma^{-1}_{\alpha}(f_{\alpha}),\text{ whenever }\alpha\leq\beta.

Proof.

Since
T\in C^{\ast}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{D})
is normal, for each
\alpha\in\Lambda
, the operator
T_{\alpha}=T\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha})
is normal. Therefore, for every
f\in C(\sigma_{loc}(T)),
the mapping
f_{\alpha}\mapsto\Gamma^{-1}_{\alpha}(f_{\alpha})=f_{\alpha}(T_{\alpha})
from
C\big(\sigma(T_{\alpha})\big)
onto
C^{\ast}\{I_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}},\;T_{\alpha},T^{\ast}_{\alpha}\}
is the continuous functional calculus for the normal operator
T_{\alpha}.

Suppose
\alpha\leq\beta
and Since
\sigma(T_{\beta})
is compact, for
f_{\beta}\in C\left(\sigma(T_{\beta})\right)
there is a sequence
\{\mathcal{P}_{n}\}_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}
of polynomials converges uniformly to
f_{\beta}
on
\sigma(T_{\beta})
. Moreover,
\{\mathcal{P}_{n}\}_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}
converges uniformly to
f_{\alpha}
on
\sigma(T_{\alpha})
. If
Q_{\alpha,\beta}\colon\mathcal{H}_{\beta}\to\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
is an orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
, then by using the fact that
Q_{\alpha,\beta}T_{\beta}=T_{\beta}Q_{\alpha,\beta}
, for any
x\in\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
, we have

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha,\beta}\Gamma_{\beta}^{-1}\big(f_{\beta}\big)x=Q_{\alpha,\beta}\Big(\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\Gamma_{\beta}^{-1}\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}\right)\Big)x

\displaystyle=Q_{\alpha,\beta}\Big(\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{P}_{n}\left(T_{\beta},T_{\beta}^{\ast}\right)\Big)x

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\Big(Q_{\alpha,\beta}\mathcal{P}_{n}\left(T_{\beta},T_{\beta}^{\ast}\right)\Big)x

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{P}_{n}\left(T_{\beta},T_{\beta}^{\ast}\right)Q_{\alpha,\beta}x

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\beta}^{-1}\big(f_{\beta}\big)Q_{\alpha,\beta}x.

This shows that
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
is a reducing subspace of
\Gamma_{\beta}^{-1}\big(f_{\beta}\big).
Since
T_{\beta}\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}}=T_{\alpha}
, for every
x\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
we get

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\beta}^{-1}\big(f_{\beta}\big)x=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\Gamma_{\beta}^{-1}\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}\right)x=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{P}_{n}\left(T_{\beta},T_{\beta}^{\ast}\right)x

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{P}_{n}\left(T_{\alpha},T_{\alpha}^{\ast}\right)x

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\Gamma_{\alpha}^{-1}\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}\right)x

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\alpha}^{-1}\big(f_{\alpha}\big)x.

Therefore,
\Gamma_{\beta}^{-1}\big(f_{\beta}\big)\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}}=\Gamma_{\alpha}^{-1}\big(f_{\alpha}\big),
whenever
\alpha\leq\beta.
Consequently, we see that the family
\left\{\Gamma^{-1}_{\alpha}(f_{\alpha})\right\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
is in
\varprojlim\limits_{\alpha\in\Lambda}C^{\ast}\{I_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}},T_{\alpha},T_{\alpha}^{\ast}\}.

The following example, motivated from
[
4
, Example 3.1]
, gives a description of the notion of continuous functional calculus and illustrates a particular example of Spectral mapping theorem in this set up, which will be proved in Theorem
3.16
.

Example 3.15
.

Let us consider the directed POSET
(\mdmathbb{N},\leq)
and the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mdmathbb{R}).
For each
n\in\mdmathbb{N},
define

\mathcal{H}_{n}:=\Big\{h\in L^{2}(\mdmathbb{R}):\;supp(h)\subseteq[-n,n]\Big\},

which is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}
and hence a Hilbert space. Further, if
m\leq n
then
\mathcal{H}_{m}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{n}.
This implies that
\mathcal{E}:=\big\{\mathcal{H}_{n}\big\}_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}
is an upward filtered family of Hilbert spaces. Therefore,
\mathcal{D}=\displaystyle\bigcup_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}\mathcal{H}_{n}
is a locally Hilbert space and
\overline{\mathcal{D}}=\mathcal{H}.
Now define
T\colon\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
by

T(h)(x)=x\,h(x),\text{ for all }h\in\mathcal{D},\,x\in supp(h).

In particular, for each
n\in\mdmathbb{N}
, it follows that
T_{n}=T\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{n}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{n})
is normal with
\sigma(T_{n})=[-n,n]
. As a result,
T\in C^{\ast}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{D})
is normal. The local spectrum of
T
(see Equation
3.4
) is given by

\sigma_{loc}(T)=\bigcup\limits_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}\sigma(T_{n})=\bigcup\limits_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}[-n,n]=\mdmathbb{R}.

Let
f\in C(\mdmathbb{R}).
For each
n\in\mdmathbb{N}
,
f_{n}\in C\left([-n,n]\right)
and the bounded operator
f_{n}(T_{n})
is given by the functional calculus of
T_{n}.
Following Theorem
3.11
Equation (
3.7
) we have
f(T)=\varprojlim\limits_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}f_{n}(T_{n})
. For instance, consider
f(x)=e^{x},\;x\in\mdmathbb{R}.
If
h\in\mathcal{D}
then
supp(h)\subseteq[-n,n]
for some
n\in\mdmathbb{N}
and

f(T)(h)(x)=e^{x}h(x)\;\text{for all}\;x\in[-n,n].

Further, the local spectrum of
f(T)
is computed as,

\displaystyle\sigma_{loc}\big(f(T)\big)=\bigcup\limits_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}\sigma\big(f_{n}(T_{n})\big)=\bigcup\limits_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}\big\{e^{x}:\;x\in[-n,n]\big\}=\big\{e^{x}\;:x\in\mdmathbb{R}\big\}.

It follows that
\sigma_{loc}\big(f(T)\big)=f\big(\sigma_{loc}(T)\big).

In the following theorem, our aim is to generalize the last observation we made in Example
3.15
to a locally bounded normal operator
T
. We call this result as
the local spectral mapping theorem
in this context as it gives the relation between the local spectrum of
f(T)
and the range of
f
on the local spectrum of
T,
for any
f\in C\big(\sigma_{loc}(T)\big).

For the spectral mapping theorem for unbounded normal operator, we refer the reader
[
12
, Theorem 6.3]
. However, our result can be seen as a refiend one to the result given in
[
12
, Theorem 6.3]
in the special case of locally bounded operators (not necessarily a closed operator).

Theorem 3.16
.

(The local spectral mapping theorem)
Let
T\in C^{\ast}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{D})
and
f\in C(\sigma_{loc}(T)).
Then

\sigma_{loc}(f(T))=f(\sigma_{loc}(T))=\big\{f(\lambda)\colon\lambda\in\sigma_{loc}(T)\big\}.

Proof.

If we denote
T_{\alpha}=T\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}}
then
T_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha})
is normal and
\sigma(T_{\alpha})
is compact for each
\alpha\in\Lambda
. Since
f_{\alpha}\in C(\sigma(T_{\alpha}))
, by the spectral mapping theorem
[
16
, Theorem 10.3(c)]
of bounded normal operators, we have

\sigma\left(f_{\alpha}(T_{\alpha})\right)=f_{\alpha}\left(\sigma(T_{\alpha})\right),

for every
\alpha\in\Lambda.
Following Equation (
3.7
) and Theorem
3.5
, we get

\displaystyle\sigma_{loc}(f(T))=\bigcup\limits_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\sigma\big(f_{\alpha}(T_{\alpha})\big)=\bigcup\limits_{\alpha\in\Lambda}f_{\alpha}\left(\sigma(T_{\alpha})\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcup\limits_{\alpha\in\Lambda}f\left(\sigma(T_{\alpha})\right)

\displaystyle=f\big(\bigcup\limits_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\sigma(T_{\alpha})\big)

\displaystyle=f\left(\sigma_{loc}(T)\right)

\displaystyle=\Big\{f(\lambda)\colon\lambda\in\sigma_{loc}(T)\Big\}.

Therefore,
f\big(\sigma_{loc}(T)\big)=\sigma_{loc}\big(f(T)\big).

∎

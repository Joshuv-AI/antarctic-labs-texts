Characterization of linear spaces of matrices of constant rank from syzygy bundles
Simone Marchesi, Rosa M. Miró-Roig

Abstract
. In this work, we characterize matrices of linear forms and constant rank, demonstrating that, under some natural assumptions, they are always associated with a syzygy bundle that fits into a (partially linear) resolution. Furthermore, this construction allows us to list all indecomposable matrices of constant rank up to 7, as well as describing the moduli spaces of simple vector bundles naturally defined by families of constant rank matrices.

1.
Introduction

The construction of linear spaces of matrices with constant rank is considered a classical problem; while its formulation is elementary, we are still far from achieving a complete understanding of it. Moreover, its interest is of great relevance, due to the several interpretations and ramifications into which this problem can be translated into.
Its origins can be traced back to the work of Kronecker and Weierstrass (see
[
26
]
and
[
27
]
). As observed in the seminal works of Sylvester
[
25
]
and Eisenbud and Harris
[
10
]
, the search for families of constant matrices can be reformulated as a vector bundle problem.
This perspective naturally connects the study of constant rank matrices to two independently significant topics: vector bundles
generated by global sections
and
uniform bundles
, both of which have been extensively studied in the literature. Indeed, every linear space of constant rank matrices (equivalently, a matrix
M
with linear entries and constant rank) corresponds to a uniform vector bundle
{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
, such that both
{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{M}^{\lor}(1)
are generated by global sections. Conversely, given a vector bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
with these properties, we can reverse the construction to obtain a matrix
M_{\mathcal{E}}
with linear entries and constant rank.
Once this relation is established, it is important to recall that the classification of rank
r
uniform vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is known for
r\leq n+1
. Within this range, for
n\geq 3
, they are sums of line bundles and/or a twist of either the tangent bundle or the cotangent bundle.

This result plays a crucial role in the classification of matrices of linear forms with
constant rank
r\leq 6
done in
[
19
]
. That work strongly relies on the classification theorems of globally generated vector bundles with small first Chern class (see
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
) together with properties derived from being uniform (see for example
[
20
]
) and the study of homogeneous bundles presented in
[
17
]
.

Furthermore, the impact of constant rank matrices extends even further. The results in
[
17
]
have recently been applied to the study of border rank bounds for tensors that are invariant under the general linear group (see
[
28
]
).

Being aware of how deep-seated the problem is, it is now essential to understand the methods used so far to construct the few known examples. We find out that the points of view and techniques involved are quite different from one method to another. In particular, Boralevi, Faenzi and Mezzetti (see
[
6
]
) consider instanton bundles and derived categories to shed light on an isolated example given by Westwick in
[
27
]
. Their work represents a foundational step toward a more general construction, later
developed by Boralevi, Faenzi, and Lella in
[
5
]
. This subsequent approach, rooted in commutative algebra, is based on the connections between the linear resolution of truncated modules. Very recently, Landsberg and Manivel (see
[
17
]
) use representation theory, while Manivel and Miró-Roig (see
[
19
]
) rely, as mentioned before, on the classification of globally generated vector bundles with small first Chern class.

Here, we explore the construction proposed by Boralevi, Faenzi and Lella in
[
5
]
using a vector bundle point of view of their result (see Theorem
3.1
). Roughly speaking, a matrix with the required properties appears when relating linear parts of the chain complexes, whose indexes are paired
by the chain complex map. In addition, we prove that, under some weak assumption, any constant rank matrix
M
with linear entries can be constructed using this technique (see Theorem
3.5
).

However, the primary objective of this work is to emphasize the importance of the particular case in which the map between two considered resolutions is defined by a
contraction of global sections
. The contractions we will need must be of
maximal rank
. This observation directly connects our problem to another significant open question in commutative algebra. Indeed, determining the rank of a given contraction is equivalent to the long-standing open problem of computing the Hilbert function of the exterior algebra modulo a principal ideal generated by a generic form of degree
q
(for more information, the reader can look at
[
18
]
and
[
21
]
).

As a first direct application of our
construction by contractions
, we provide a complete list of the indecomposable matrices of constant rank 7 which is a novelty (see Theorem
4.9
).
The arguments involved rely on the classification of globally generated vector bundles with first Chern class
c_{1}\leq 3
combined with relating complexes which arise as the resolution of the most basic examples of simple regular bundles: the Koszul complex, that represents the resolution of
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1)
, and, more in general, the Eagon-Northcott complexes that are obtained as the resolution of
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)
, with
d\geq 2
. Our argument can also be used to provide a more direct proof of the classification of matrices of constant rank
r\leq 6
given in
[
19
]
. Recall moreover that the first syzygy bundles obtained by contracting global sections of
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)
are known as
Steiner bundles
when
d=1
, and as
Drézet bundles
when
d\geq 2
, both of which are of independent interest.

As mentioned before, the classical complexes considered arise as the resolution of the most basic case of vector bundles which are
regular
and
simple
. In the last part of this work, we focus on families of vector bundles that satisfy, respectively and/or separately, these properties.

First, we prove a general result that ensures the construction of constant rank matrices with linear entries starting from the syzygies of regular bundles (see Proposition
4.13
). Again, the central idea is to find an adequate contraction of the global sections of the regular bundle
{\mathcal{F}}
whose kernel still generates
{\mathcal{F}}
.

Regarding simplicity, we provide a result which does not depend on a chosen contraction if a resolution is ensured. Indeed, it is known that if we start with a simple vector bundle (satisfying some open conditions in cohomology), the syzygy bundles obtained in the splitting of its resolution will also be simple. The importance of this observation is twofold. First of all, the simplicity of the syzygy bundle
{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
implies the indecomposability of the associate matrix
M
of linear forms and constant rank. Furthermore, it allows to describe the family of the vector bundles
{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
inside the moduli space of simple vector bundles with fixed rank and fixed Chern classes. We will show that they are parametrized by an open dense subset of an irreducible component (see Theorem
5.2
).

The paper is organized as follows: in Section
2
we set the notation and recall the notions which will be used throughout this work, with special emphasis on contractions. Section
3
contains the construction of matrices of linear forms and constant rank through morphisms of acyclic complexes and its reverse construction (see Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.5
, respectively). In Section
4
, we focus on the constructions defined by a given contraction of global sections, which allow us to provide the complete list of the indecomposable matrices of constant rank 7 (see Theorem
4.9
) and that we also apply to regular bundles. In Section
5
, we parametrize the family of matrices with linear entries and constant rank which arise from the resolution of simple vector bundles, and their moduli space (see Theorem
5.2
). Section
6
contains questions and problems stemming from this work. Finally, in the Appendix, we will show how to construct, using Macaulay 2, two specific examples: one coming from the contraction associated to a Drézet vector bundles, and the other one obtained starting from the Horrocks-Mumfors bundle.

2.
Notation and General set up

Throughout this paper we work over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic 0. Given a
k
-vector space
V
of dimension
n+1
, we denote by
V^{*}=\Hom(V,k)
its dual, by
S(V)
the symmetric algebra and by
\bigwedge V
the exterior algebra. We fix
\{v_{i}\}
a basis of
V
and we denote by
\{v_{i}^{*}\}
its associate dual basis.
We define
R=S(V^{*})
and we identify it with a polynomial ring, i.e.,
R=k[x_{0},\cdots,x_{n}]
. For any graded
R
-module
N
, we define
N_{\geq p}:=\oplus_{i\geq p}N_{i}
its truncation at degree
p
.
We define
\mathbb{P}^{n}=\mathbb{P}(V)
as the set of lines, and therefore we have
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1))=V^{*}
. Finally, considering the tangent bundle
T=T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
, we set
\Omega^{j}=\bigwedge^{j}T^{\lor}
.

2.2.
Contractions

We conclude this section by recalling the definition of contraction in an exterior algebra, which will play a central role in the explicit construction technique we aim to highlight in this work.

Definition 2.4
.

For any integer
q\leq n+1
and any
q

the

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\left((v_{j_{1}}^{*}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{j_{q}}^{*})\wedge-\right):&\bigwedge^{p}V&\longrightarrow&\bigwedge^{p-q}V\\
&u&\mapsto&\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}(-1)^{\sigma_{u}}w&\mbox{ if }u=(-1)^{\sigma_{u}}(v_{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{j_{q}})\wedge w\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\left(-\wedge(v_{j_{1}}^{*}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{j_{q}}^{*})\right):&\bigwedge^{p}V&\longrightarrow&\bigwedge^{p-q}V\\
&u&\mapsto&\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}(-1)^{\sigma_{u}}w&\mbox{ if }u=(-1)^{\sigma_{u}}w\wedge(v_{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{j_{q}})\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.\end{array}

By linearity, we extend both definitions to any
q
-form
\omega\in\bigwedge^{q}V^{*}
. We will say that a
q

\omega\in\bigwedge^{q}V^{*}
defines a

if the associated linear map has maximal rank for every
p=q,\ldots,n+1.

Fixed
s\cdot t
elements in
\bigwedge^{q}V^{*}
we can define analogously the concept of contraction of maximal rank, relative to the linear map

\left(\bigwedge^{p}V\right)^{t}\longrightarrow\left(\bigwedge^{p-q}V\right)^{s}

induced by the fixed elements.
Notice that the rank of a contraction on the left is maximal if and only if it is so for the paired contraction on the right.

The following example provides us infinite families of maximal rank contractions.

Example 2.5
.

If
n+1
and
q
are even integers, then the contraction defined by the generic element of
\bigwedge^{q}V^{*}
is of maximal rank. Indeed, we denote the basis of
V
by
v_{1},...,v_{2m}

Since the
s

\omega=v_{1}^{*}\wedge v_{m+1}^{*}+v_{2}^{*}\wedge v_{m+2}^{*}+...+v_{m}^{*}\wedge v_{2m}^{*}

defines a contraction of maximal rank, the same holds for the generic
q
-form
\omega\in\bigwedge^{q}V^{*}
, as required.

All the examples given in this work have maximal rank. Nevertheless, this is not always the case, as the following example, provided by Ottaviani and Weyman, shows.

Example 2.6
.

(See
[
13
, Remark 3.5]
)
Let
V
be a 9-dimensional vector space. Then, the contraction
\bigwedge^{6}V\longrightarrow\bigwedge^{3}V
, defined by the generic
3

\omega

\bigwedge^{3}V^{*}
, is not of maximal rank. Indeed, it is represented by a
(84\times 84)
-matrix of rank
80
.

Construction of matrices of constant rank

Remark 3.2
.

We do not know, in general, whether the matrix
M
constructed in Theorem
3.1
is indecomposable. Quite often the matrix
M
will be associated to a syzygy bundle

will also be simple and
M

4.
Applications

In this section we focus on the particular case in which the construction described in Theorem
3.1
is defined by a contraction. This specific situation provides, as detailed in the introduction, several connections with different significant research topics and, in our opinion, represents a first step to study the questions proposed in the final section of this work.

Furthermore, it allows us to:

•

give a complete list of the indecomposable matrices of constant rank 7 (see Section
4.2
);

•

describe a method to construct matrices with linear entries and constant rank starting from regular vector bundles (see Section
4.3
).

The first point strongly relies on the study of the relations between the classical complexes associated to standard determinantal ideals.
That is why, in Section
4.1
, we recall them for the sake of completeness (see
[
11
, Theorem A2.10]
for more details) and we also describe explicit examples that use them as main ingredient.

Regarding regularity, notice that the choice of starting with regular vector bundles is extremely natural since matrices with linear entries and constant rank already arise as the matrices associated to the morphisms defining the linear resolution of a regular vector bundle. The subsequent and crucial step is to determine if it is possible to choose a vector subspace
W_{0}\subset H^{0}({\mathcal{F}})
that still generates the vector bundle, i.e.,
eval:W_{0}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}
is surjective,
and induces a commutative diagram that satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
.

Throughout this section, we will suppose all the contractions to be of maximal rank. In particular, they are so in all the detailed examples, because of Example
2.5
or by a direct computation made with Macaulay2.

4.1.
Classical complexes

Let

be a commutative ring and

-module. For any element

Furthermore, given a morphism

5.
The moduli space

In this last section, we study families of matrices
M
of constant rank in terms of the corresponding vector bundles
{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
and their moduli spaces.
Denote by
\Spl(r;c_{1},\cdots,c_{n})
the moduli space of simple sheaves on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
of rank
r
and given Chern classes
c_{i}
.

Using recent results on generalized syzygy bundles, we will prove that, under some mild hypothesis, the rank
r
vector bundles
{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
are simple, unobstructed (i.e., the corresponding points in the moduli space are smooth) and they are parametrized by an open dense subset of an irreducible component of
\Spl(r;c_{1}({\mathcal{E}}_{M}),\cdots,c_{n}({\mathcal{E}}_{M}))
.

It is important to point out that we have to consider the moduli space of simple sheaves
\Spl(r;c_{1}({\mathcal{E}}_{M}),\cdots,c_{n}({\mathcal{E}}_{M}))
, instead of the better understood moduli space of (semi)stable sheaves
M^{ss}(r;c_{1}({\mathcal{E}}_{M}),\cdots,c_{n}({\mathcal{E}}_{M})))
, because in general
{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
is neither stable nor semistable while quite often it is simple.

Let us start by collecting, for
the reader’s convenience, some basic information about moduli spaces of simple sheaves. For more information the reader can look at
[
12
]
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a globally generated vector bundle of rank
r
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. For any general subspace
W\subset H^{0}({\mathcal{F}})
with
w=\dim W\geq n+r
, we define a vector bundle
{\mathcal{S}}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
as the kernel of the map
eval_{W}:W\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}{\longrightarrow}{\mathcal{F}}
. So,
{\mathcal{S}}
fits inside a short exact sequence:

(21)

e:\quad 0\to{\mathcal{S}}\to W\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\to{\mathcal{F}}\to 0,\text{ and }

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\rk({\mathcal{S}})&=&\dim W-\rk({\mathcal{F}})=w-r,\\
c_{1}({\mathcal{S}})&=&-c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})\\
c_{t}({\mathcal{S}})\cdot c_{t}({\mathcal{F}})&=&1.\end{array}

We will call
{\mathcal{S}}
the
generalized syzygy bundle
associated to the couple
({\mathcal{F}},W)
. Observe that the dual of a generalized syzygy bundle associated to a non complete linear system
W\subset H^{0}({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d))
is nothing but a Drézet bundle and, if
W=H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d))
, we recover the classical definition of syzygy bundle.

We will denote by

U:=U(r;c_{1},\cdots,c_{n})\subset\Spl(r;c_{1},\cdots,c_{n})

the open locus parametrizing globally generated rank
r
simple vector bundles
{\mathcal{F}}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
such that
H^{1}({\mathcal{F}})=H^{1}({\mathcal{F}}^{\lor})=H^{2}({\mathcal{F}}^{\lor})=0
.

Remark 5.1
.

By
[
12
, Proposition 3.2]
, we know that the set-theoretic function associating to each point
[{\mathcal{F}}]
of this open locus
U
the dimension of
H^{0}({\mathcal{F}})
is locally constant. Hence it is constant on every connected component of
U
.

From now on, we assume that the above open subset
U
is connected; otherwise, we will restrict our discussion to each connected component.
Define

{\mathcal{G}}_{U}:=\{({\mathcal{F}},W)\mid{\mathcal{F}}\in U,W\subset H^{0}({\mathcal{F}}),\dim(W)=w\}.

The natural projection
\pi:{\mathcal{G}}_{U}\to U
is a Grassmann bundle and
{\mathcal{G}}_{U}
is a smooth algebraic space of dimension

\dim{\mathcal{G}}_{U}=\dim\Spl(r;c_{1},\cdots,c_{n})+w(z-w)

where
z:=\dim H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},{\mathcal{F}})
(see Remark
5.1
).
Moreover, consider

{\mathcal{G}}^{0}_{U}:=\{({\mathcal{F}},W)\in{\mathcal{G}}_{U}\mid eval_{W}\text{ is surjective}\},

which is open in
{\mathcal{G}}_{U}
. We still denote by
\pi
the induced natural projection
\pi:{\mathcal{G}}^{0}_{U}\to U
.

It is possible to check that, for any pair
({\mathcal{F}},W)\in{\mathcal{G}}^{0}_{U}
, the associated generalized syzygy bundle is simple and, using stack theory, we can define a morphism (see
[
12
, Definition 3.7]
for details)

\alpha:{\mathcal{G}}^{0}_{U}{\longrightarrow}\Spl(\rk({\mathcal{S}});c_{1}({\mathcal{S}}),\cdots,c_{n}({\mathcal{S}}))

which extends the set-theoretic map
({\mathcal{F}},W)\mapsto{\mathcal{S}}
.

It is known that:

(i)

The morphism
\alpha
is injective and, if
n\geq 3
, it is an open embedding
[
12
, Theorem 1.2, Proposition 4.1]
;

(ii)

We have

T_{[({\mathcal{F}},W)]}{\mathcal{G}}^{0}_{U}=H^{0}({\mathcal{S}}^{\lor}\otimes V)/W\otimes W^{*},

T_{[{\mathcal{S}}]}\Spl(\rk({\mathcal{S}});c_{1}({\mathcal{S}}),\cdots,c_{n}({\mathcal{S}}))=\Ext^{1}({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{S}})=H^{1}({\mathcal{S}}^{\lor}\otimes{\mathcal{S}})

and the differential map

d\alpha:T_{[({\mathcal{F}},W)]}{\mathcal{G}}^{0}_{U}{\longrightarrow}T_{[{\mathcal{S}}]}\Spl(\rk({\mathcal{S}});c_{1}({\mathcal{S}}),\cdots,c_{n}({\mathcal{S}}))

is injective
[
12
, Proposition 4.2]
.

We are now ready to state the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
M
be a
(b\times a)
-matrix with linear entries and constant rank
r
, which fits in

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{K}}_{M}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{a}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1)^{b}\ \ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{C}}_{M}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0.}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{E}}_{M}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Assume that
{\mathcal{C}}_{M}
is simple and
H^{1}({\mathcal{C}}_{M}(-1))=H^{1}({\mathcal{C}}_{M}^{\lor}(1))=H^{2}({\mathcal{C}}_{M}^{\lor}(1))=0
. Then,
{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
is a simple vector bundle parametrized by a smooth open subset
{\bf S}
of
\Spl(r;c_{1}({\mathcal{E}}_{M}(-1)),\cdots,c_{n}({\mathcal{E}}_{M}(-1)))
. Moreover,

\dim{\bf S}=\dim_{[{\mathcal{C}}_{M}(-1)]}\Spl(b-r;c_{1}({\mathcal{C}}_{M}(-1),\cdots,c_{n}({\mathcal{C}}_{M}(-1)))+b(h^{0}({\mathcal{C}}_{M}(-1))-b).

Proof.

Consider the open set

as defined before. By

, the syzygy bundle associated to any pair

is simple. In particular, taking

, we obtain that
{\mathcal{E}}_{M}(-1)
(and therefore

The result immediately follows from the fact that

∎

Remark 5.3
.

Arguing as in
[
3
]
and
[

]
, we deduce that the number

of irreducible components of the moduli space

increases when

increases. Therefore, the simple vector bundles associated to a

matrix of constant rank

only fill an irreducible component. It is worthwhile to point out that this component has nice properties: it is generically smooth and we know its dimension.

.

(1) Since

is rigid, simple, generated by global sections and

we get that the indecomposable

, by Theorem

.

6.
Final comments and questions

In this section, we list some questions and open problems stemming from this work.

(1)
Homogeneous matrices of constant rank.
We would like to construct
b\times a
matrices
M_{d}
with entries homogeneous forms of degree
d
in
R
and constant rank
r
. Again this problem can be translated into a problem of vector bundles, since any such matrix defines a
morphism of sheaves
\psi:{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{a}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)^{b}
. If the rank is constant, equal to
r
, the image of this
morphism is a vector bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
of rank
r
.

We first observe that any matrix
M_{1}
, with linear entries and constant rank
r
, gives rise to a matrix
M_{d}
of the same size, constant rank
r
and entries homogeneous forms of degree
d
; it is enough to change
x_{i}
by
x_{i}^{d}
, for
i=0,\cdots,n
. Nevertheless, not all “degree
d
matrices” of type
M_{d}
come from “linear matrices” of type
M_{1}
of the same size. Indeed, the
5\times 5
matrices
M_{1}
with linear entries in
R=k[x,y,z]
and constant rank
4
are parametrized by an open subset of
\mathbb{P}^{5}
while the
5\times 5
matrices
M_{2}
with quadratic entries in
R=k[x,y,z]
and constant rank
4
are parametrized by an open subset of
\mathbb{P}^{35}
.

So, we are led to pose the following problem:

Problem 6.1
.

(i) Given
n
and
d
, to construct constant rank matrices
M_{d}
with entries homogeneous forms of degree
d
in
n+1
variables.

(ii) Given
n
,
d
,
a
and
b
, to determine the minimum
r
(if it exists) such that there are
(b\times a)
-matrices with entries homogeneous forms of degree
d
in
n+1
variables and constant rank
r
.

(2)
Stability of vector bundles associated to matrices with constant rank.
In this work we often use the following recipe to construct matrices with linear entries and constant rank.
We start with a vector bundle
{\mathcal{C}}_{M}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with a linear resolution

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-n)^{a_{n}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-n+1)^{a_{n-1}}\longrightarrow\cdots

\cdots\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-1)^{a_{1}}\longrightarrow H^{0}({\mathcal{C}}_{M}(-1))\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{C}}_{M}(-1)\longrightarrow 0.

We then determine the minimal dimension vector subspace
W\subset H^{0}({\mathcal{C}}_{M}(-1))
that generates
{\mathcal{C}}_{M}(-1)
at every point and ensures a linear resolution of
W\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{C}}_{M}(-1)\longrightarrow 0
.
Finally, we observe that if
{\mathcal{C}}_{M}
is simple then
{\mathcal{E}}_{M}(-1):=ker(W\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}_{M}(-1))
is also simple.

We are led to pose the following question:

Question 6.2
.

Assume
{\mathcal{C}}_{M}
to be stable. Is there a choice for
W\subset H^{0}({\mathcal{C}}_{M}(-1))
to ensure the stability of
{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
?

(3) Let
(X,L)
be a polarized variety. As a generalization of the method developed in the previous sections we propose to address the same problem in a more general setup and construct matrices with entries in
H^{0}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(L))
of constant rank.

(4)
Maximal rank contraction.
In this paper we quite often deal with maximal rank contractions. More precisely, we take a vector space
V
of dimension
n
, we consider the exterior algebra
\bigwedge V
, we pick a
q
-form
\omega\in\bigwedge^{q}V^{*}
and we assume that it induces a maximal rank morphism
\omega\wedge-:\bigwedge^{p}V\longrightarrow\bigwedge^{p-q}V
(see Definition
2.4
). Nevertheless, we want to point out that in all the cases we have considered it was enough to pick up a sufficiently general
q
-form to guarantee the maximal rank property. Therefore, based in Examples
2.5
and
2.6
we are led to pose the following problem.

Question 6.3
.

Let
V
be a vector space of dimension
n
.
For which integers
p>q
there exists a general
q
-form such that the induced contraction morphism

\omega\wedge-:\bigwedge^{p}V\longrightarrow\bigwedge^{p-q}V

has maximal rank?

Remark 6.4
.

Although the above question seems a simple problem of linear algebra, it has proven to be extremely elusive and the answer is not always affirmative.
Consider for instance a vector space
V
of dimension 9; hence
\dim\bigwedge^{3}V=dim\bigwedge^{6}V=84
. We would expect that, for a general 3-form
\omega
, the induced contraction morphism

\omega\wedge-:\bigwedge^{3}V\longrightarrow\bigwedge^{6}V

is an isomorphism. However, for a general 3-form
\omega
we have

\dim Ker(\omega\wedge-:\bigwedge^{3}V\longrightarrow\bigwedge^{6}V)=4.

As recalled in the introduction, Question 6.3 is equivalent to the long-standing problem of determining the Hilbert function of the exterior algebra modulo a principal ideal generated by a generic form of degree
q
. For more information about this problem, the reader can look at
[
18
]
and
[
21
]
.

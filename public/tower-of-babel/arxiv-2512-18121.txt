Classical Apéry-Like Series and Their Cyclotomic Parametric Analogues via Contour Integration
Ce Xu

Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper, we present a method based on contour integration to investigate a class of cyclotomic parametric Apéry-like series. The general term of such series involves a parametric central binomial coefficient, which is defined via the Gamma function. Using this approach, we express a family of cyclotomic Apéry-like series in terms of multiple polylogarithms, cyclotomic Hurwitz zeta values, Riemann zeta values and
\log(2)
. In particular, we provide several illustrative examples and corollaries, which enable us to recover a number of known results on Apéry-like series. At the same time, we have also left open two questions regarding Apéry-like series. Moreover, by considering integrals of the generating function for Fuss-Catalan numbers, we derive an alternative expression for a classical Apéry-like series. Combining this with known results allows us to establish several identities for multiple polylogarithm functions.

Keywords
: Cyclotomic parametric Apéry-like series; Contour integration; (Parametric) Central binomial coefficients; Multiple polylogarithm function; Cyclotomic Hurwitz zeta function; Fuss-Catalan numbers.

Introduction

For
\Re(s)>1
, the classical
Riemann zeta function

\zeta(s)
is defined by the absolutely convergent infinite series:

\displaystyle\zeta(s):=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{s}}.

(1.1)

Since its introduction, the study of the Riemann zeta function has remained at the forefront of mathematical research. In particular, investigating the irrationality and transcendence of its values at odd integers greater than 1 constitutes a profoundly important class of problems in this field. The values of the Riemann zeta function at integers greater than 1 are known as Riemann zeta values. If
s>1
is an even integer, the value
\zeta(s)
is referred to as an
even Riemann zeta value
; if
s>1
is odd, it is called an
odd Riemann zeta value
, respectively. The irrationality and transcendence of even zeta values
\zeta(2k)
were resolved long ago, as they can be explicitly expressed by Euler’s celebrated formula

\zeta(2k)=\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k-1}B_{2k}(2\pi)^{2k}}{2(2k)!}\quad(k\in\mathbb{N})

combined with the transcendence of
\pi
, where
B_{k}
denotes the
Bernoulli numbers
defined by

\displaystyle\frac{x}{e^{x}-1}=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\displaystyle\frac{B_{n}}{n!}x^{n}}.

In contrast, the study of irrationality and transcendence for odd zeta values saw no significant progress for a long time. A major breakthrough came in 1979, when Roger Apéry
[
2
]
proved the irrationality of
\zeta(3)
by studying the following infinite series involving the central binomial coefficient
\binom{2n}{n}
:

\zeta(3)=\displaystyle\frac{5}{2}\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n^{3}\binom{2n}{n}}.

This marked the first important result on the irrationality of odd Riemann zeta values. Due to Apéry’s groundbreaking contributions to the irrationality of odd Riemann zeta values, many subsequent researchers have referred to series involving central binomial coefficients as “Apéry-like series,” which has since become a prominent research direction in number theory. It is particularly noteworthy that Professor Zhi-Wei Sun has enthusiastically proposed numerous original conjectures. In his paper
[
30
]
and monograph
[
31
]
, he put forward many conjectural identities concerning Apéry-like series, some of which have already been proven. Currently, the monograph
[
31
]
is available only in Chinese; however, it is understood that Professor Sun is actively working on publishing an English edition. Surprisingly, recent research in this area has revealed profound connections between Apéry-like series and theories of hypergeometric functions, elliptic functions, and multiple zeta values (MZVs for short). Methods derived from these related theories have proven effective in resolving numerous identity problems for Apéry-like series. For some recent work in this direction, we refer the reader to
[
4
,

,

,

,

,

,

,
14
,

,
22
,

,

,
32
,

]
and the references therein. For a systematic introduction to the theory of multiple zeta values, we recommend Zhao’s monograph
[
40
]
(the first book dedicated to MZVs theory, which contains nearly all major results prior to 2016) and the recent review article
[
41
]
.

Research has shown that among the various methods for investigating the evaluation of infinite series, contour integration and residue computation stand out as highly effective and practical techniques. This approach has led to numerous interesting and celebrated results. For example, Flajolet and Salvy
[
17
]
employed the method of contour integration to establish a parity theorem for generalized Euler sums. Recently, the author of this paper, jointly with Rui
[
29
]
, also applied a similar approach to establish a parity theorem for generalized cyclotomic Euler sums. Studies presented in
[
32
]
and
[
34
]
demonstrate that the contour integration method can also be effectively applied to the investigation of multiple Apéry-like series, leading to a number of interesting conclusions.

The primary objective of this paper is to investigate the following
cyclotomic parametric Apéry-like series
via the method of contour integration:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{4^{n+a}}{(n+a)^{q}\binom{2n+2a}{n+a}}z^{n},

(1.2)

where
a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z},\ q\in\mathbb{N}
and
z
is an arbitrary root of unity with
(q,z)\neq(1,1)
. In particular, when
a
is a half-integer in (
1.2
) and
2n+2a
is a non-positive integer, the reciprocal of the parametric central binomial coefficient,
\binom{2n+2a}{n+a}^{-1}
, is interpreted as zero by convention.
Here the
generalized binomial coefficient

\binom{a}{b}
is denoted by

\binom{a}{b}:=\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(a+1)}{\Gamma(b+1)\Gamma(a-b+1)}\quad\text{for}\ a,b,a-b\notin\mathbb{N}^{-}:=\{-1,-2,-3,\ldots\},

where
\Gamma(s)
denotes the
gamma function
, defined for
\Re(s)>0
by the improper integral

\displaystyle\Gamma(s):=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{s-1}dt.

Main Theorems and Corollaries

We will prove that the cyclotomic parametric Apéry-like series (
1.2
) can be expressed as a combination of
\log(2)
, Riemann zeta values, cyclotomic Hurwitz zeta values, and multiple polylogarithms. More precisely, we have the following theorem:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
x
be a root of unity. For
a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
and
q\in\mathbb{N}
with
(q,x)\neq(1,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{4^{n+a}}{(n+a)^{q}\binom{2n+2a}{n+a}}x^{-n}-(-1)^{q}\left(\Li_{1}(x;1-a)-x\Li_{1}(x^{-1};a)\right)\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n^{q-1}4^{n}}x^{n}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{5}=q-1,\atop\forall k_{j}\geq 0}\displaystyle\frac{C_{k_{1}}C_{k_{2}}D_{k_{3}}2^{k_{3}+k_{4}}}{k_{1}!k_{2}!k_{3}!k_{4}!}\log^{k_{4}}(2)\left(x\Li_{k_{5}+1}(x^{-1};a)-(-1)^{k_{5}}\Li_{k_{5}+1}(x;1-a)\right),

(2.3)

where
\Li_{p}(x;b+1)\ ((p,x)\neq(1,1))
denotes the
cyclotomic Hurwitz zeta function
, defined as follows:

\displaystyle\Li_{p}(x;b+1):=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{x^{n}}{(n+b)^{p}}\quad(p\in\mathbb{N},\ b\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{N}^{-})\quad\text{and}\quad\Li_{p}(x)\equiv\Li_{p}(x;1).

(2.4)

Here

\displaystyle C_{n}:=Y_{n}\Big(0,1!\zeta(2),-2!\zeta(3),\ldots,(-1)^{n}(n-1)!\zeta(n)\Big),

(2.5)

\displaystyle D_{n}:=Y_{n}\Big(0,-1!\zeta(2),2!\zeta(3),\ldots,(-1)^{n-1}(n-1)!\zeta(n)\Big),

(2.6)

where
Y_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
stands for the

(see Section

The second cyclotomic Apéry-like series in equation (

Proposition 2.2
.

For

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n^{p+1}4^{n}}x^{n}

\displaystyle=-2\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\displaystyle\sum_{j+l\leq k,\atop j,l\geq 0}(-1)^{j+l+k}\displaystyle\frac{(j+1)\log^{p-1-k}\Big(\displaystyle\frac{|x|}{4}\Big)}{(p-1-k)!(k-j-l)!}\log^{k-j-l}\left(\displaystyle\frac{|1-\sqrt{1-x}|}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\left(\Li_{l+1,\{1\}_{j+1}}\left(\displaystyle\frac{1-\sqrt{1-x}}{2}\right)-\Li_{l+2,\{1\}_{j}}\left(\displaystyle\frac{1-\sqrt{1-x}}{2}\right)\right),

(2.7)

where
\{1\}_{r}
denotes the sequence of
1
repeated
r
times. In particular, the reference
[
13
]
provides expressions for the two cases

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^{n}}x^{n}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x}}-1\quad(|x|\leq 1\quad\text{and}\quad x\neq 1),

(2.8)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n4^{n}}x^{n}=2\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1-x}}\right)\quad(|x|\leq 1).

(2.9)

Remark 2.3
.

In
[
34
, Theorem 3.8]
, the formula omits two absolute value symbol; specifically
\log^{p-1-k}(x)
should be written as

lies within the interval

to derive an alternative expression for equation (

). This approach will allow us to establish several identities involving multiple polylogarithm functions. Certainly, a direct integration approach should yield an alternative expression for equation (
2.2
). As can be readily observed from (

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n^{p+1}4^{n}}x^{n}=2\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\displaystyle\frac{\log^{p-1}\Big(\displaystyle\frac{x}{t}\Big)\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1-t}}\right)}{t}dt\quad(x\in[-1,1]).

Based on this integral, following a process similar to the proof of
[

For any
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\dotsc,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and

\displaystyle\Li_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}})\equiv\Li_{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{r}}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{r}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}^{n_{1}}\dotsm x_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\dotsm n_{r}^{k_{r}}}

(2.11)

which converges if

for all

. In particular, if

, then

is the

\Li_{1}(x)=-\log(1-x)\ (x\in[-1,1))

The (

\displaystyle\Li_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}(xx_{1},x_{2}/x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}/x_{r-1})=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{1}-1}\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}dt}{1-x_{1}t}\cdots\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{r}-1}\displaystyle\frac{x_{r}dt}{1-x_{r}t}.

The theory of iterated integrals was developed firstly by K.T. Chen in the 1960’s. It has played important roles in the study of algebraic topology and algebraic geometry in the past half century. Its simplest form is

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}f_{1}(t)dtf_{2}(t)dt\cdots f_{p}(t)dt:=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\int\limits_{x>t_{1}>\cdots>t_{p}>0}f_{1}(t_{1})f_{2}(t_{2})\cdots f_{p}(t_{p})dt_{1}dt_{2}\cdots dt_{p}.

\displaystyle\Li_{\{1\}_{p}}(x)=\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{p}}{p!}\log^{p}(1-x)\quad(p\in\mathbb{N}).

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
x=e^{i\theta}
and
\theta\in(0,2\pi)
. For
a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\Li_{m+1}(x;1-a)-(-1)^{m}x\Li_{m+1}(x^{-1};a)=\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{m!}\displaystyle\frac{d^{m}}{da^{m}}\left(ix^{a}-\cot(\pi a)x^{a}\right),

(2.21)

where

.

Proof.

From
[
33
, P. 123, Example 6.12]
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{e^{in\theta}}{n-x}

\displaystyle=2\pi i\displaystyle\frac{e^{i\theta x}}{1-e^{2\pi ix}}=-\pi\displaystyle\frac{e^{ix(\theta-\pi)}}{\sin(\pi x)}

\displaystyle=-\pi\cot(\pi a)x^{a}+\pi x^{a}i.

On the other hand, noting the fact that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{x^{n}}{n-a}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{x^{n}}{n-a}+\displaystyle\sum_{n=-\infty}^{0}\displaystyle\frac{x^{n}}{n-a}=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{x^{n}}{n-a}-\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{x^{1-n}}{n+a-1}

\displaystyle=\Li_{1}(x;1-a)-x\Li_{1}(x^{-1};a).

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\Li_{1}(x;1-a)-x\Li_{1}(x^{-1};a)=-\pi\cot(\pi a)x^{a}+\pi x^{a}i.

Finally, differentiating the above equation
m
times with respect to a yields the desired result.
∎

\exp\left(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}x_{k}\displaystyle\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\right)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Y_{n}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\displaystyle\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\,,

see
[
16
, Section 3.3]
and
[
28

. From
[
35
, Eqs. (2.5) and (2.9)]

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}t^{n-1}\log^{k}(1-t)dt=(-1)^{k}k!\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n}^{\star}(\{1\}_{k})}{n}\,,

for
n\geq 1

\zeta^{\star}_{n}(\{1\}_{k})

\displaystyle\zeta^{\star}_{n}(\{1\}_{k}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{k}\geq 1}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}n_{2}\cdots n_{k}}\quad(k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}).

(3.26)

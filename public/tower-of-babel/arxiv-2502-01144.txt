Bulk-boundary entanglement correspondence and the Ryu-Takayanagi conjecture in an 𝑨 ​ 𝒅 ​ 𝑺 𝟐 / 𝑪 ​ 𝑭 ​ 𝑻 𝟏 AdS_{2}/CFT_{1} setup
Gabriel Lopes Cardoso 1 , Bernardo Moniz Martins 2 , Suresh Nampuri 1

Abstract
Using recent developments in expressing one-loop partition functions in Euclidean A ​ d ​ S 2 AdS_{2} space-times in terms of character integrals, we relate the one-loop effective action for a free field theory in A ​ d ​ S 2 AdS_{2} (comprised of a massless scalar field and a massless Majorana fermion field) to the partition function of the de Alfaro-Fubini-Furlan (DFF) conformal quantum mechanics (CQM) models on the two global A ​ d ​ S 2 AdS_{2} boundaries. The equal number of bosonic and fermionic degrees in the field theory guarantee that the one-loop calculation is free of all UV divergences except a logarithmic one consistent with the expected entanglement entropy behaviour in a CQM. Via a thermofield double representation, we compute the entanglement entropy between two copies of the C ​ F ​ T 1 CFT_{1} (CQM), each living near one of the two boundaries of global A ​ d ​ S 2 AdS_{2} , in a state at global time τ → − ∞ \tau\rightarrow-\infty . This entanglement entropy is expressed in terms of the logarithm of the regularised length of a closed particle trajectory infinitesimally near the rim of the Euclidean A ​ d ​ S 2 AdS_{2} disc. We view this relation between boundary quantum entanglement and a bulk geometrical quantity as the A ​ d ​ S 2 / C ​ F ​ T 1 AdS_{2}/CFT_{1} version of the Ryu-Takayanagi conjecture in our setup. The boundary entanglement entropy is equal to 4 times the thermodynamic entropy read off from the regularised one-loop effective action in A ​ d ​ S 2 AdS_{2} . Further, we compute the bulk entanglement entropy associated with black hole horizons in Lorentzian A ​ d ​ S 2 AdS_{2} and show that it precisely matches the boundary entanglement entropy.

0<x_{0}\ll 1
,

\displaystyle V=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\int_{x_{0}}^{+\infty}\sqrt{g}\,dx\right)dy=2\pi a^{2}\left(\coth x_{0}-1\right)\;\;\;,\;\;\;0<x_{0}\ll 1\;.

(3.13)

Thus, we obtain for the heat kernel of the operator
\hat{\Delta}
,

\displaystyle K_{B}(s)=\frac{V}{2\pi a^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\,d\nu\,e^{-(\frac{1}{4}+\nu^{2}){\bar{s}}}\,\rho_{B}(\nu)\;\;\;,\;\;\;\rho_{B}(\nu)=\nu\,\tanh(\pi\nu)\;\;\;,\;\;\;{\bar{s}}=\frac{s}{a^{2}}\;.

(3.14)

in agreement with
[
28
,
5
]
.

Next, let us consider the Dirac operator
\not{\cal D}
in the background (
2.15
).
Its spectrum
has been discussed in
various coordinate systems
[

,
29
,
28
,
5
,
15
,
18
,
21
]
.
The associated spectral density
\rho_{F}(\nu)

is given by
[

,
5

\displaystyle\rho_{F}(\nu)=2\nu\,\coth(\pi\nu)=2\nu\,\frac{\sinh 2\pi\nu}{\cosh 2\pi\nu-1}\;,

(3.15)

which satisfies
\rho_{F}(-\nu)=\rho_{F}(\nu)
.
The heat kernel of the Dirac operator in the background (
2.15
) reads
[
5
]

\displaystyle K_{F}(s)=-\frac{V}{2\pi a^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\,d\nu\,e^{-\nu^{2}{\bar{s}}}\,\rho_{F}(\nu)\;\;\;,\;\;\;\rho_{F}(\nu)=2\nu\,\coth(\pi\nu)\;\;\;,\;\;\;{\bar{s}}=\frac{s}{a^{2}}\;.

(3.16)

In order to compute the one-loop effective action, we will work
[

,
2
,
3
]
with the
Fourier integral
W_{B,F}(u)
of the spectral density
\rho_{B,F}(\nu)
,

\displaystyle W_{B,F}(u)\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\nu\,\rho_{B,F}(\nu)\,e^{i\nu u}\;.

(3.17)

We note that formally,
W_{B,F}(u)
has the property
W_{B,F}(-u)=W_{B,F}(u)
due to
\rho_{B,F}(-\nu)=\rho_{B,F}(\nu)
.
However, the expression (

) is ill-defined:
since the spectral densities (

) and (

) do not decay to zero as

, the Fourier integral (

[

,
2

by suitably deforming the integration contour in (

) to run along the positive imaginary axis,
as we will review in Appendix

.
Then,
for the spectral density (

),

evaluates to

\displaystyle W_{B}(u)=-e^{-\frac{1}{2}u}\,\frac{1+e^{-u}}{(1-e^{-u})^{2}}\;,

(3.18)

which satisfies
W_{B}(-u)=W_{B}(u)
. For the spectral density (
3.15
),

W_{F}(u)
evaluates to

\displaystyle W_{F}(u)=-4\,\frac{e^{-u}}{(1-e^{-u})^{2}}\;,

(3.19)

which satisfies
W_{F}(-u)=W_{F}(u)
.

Note that both
W_{B}
and
W_{F}
have a double pole at
u=0
,

\displaystyle W_{B}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{u^{2}}-\frac{1}{12}+{\cal O}(u^{2})\;,

\displaystyle W_{F}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{u^{2}}+\frac{1}{3}+{\cal O}(u^{2})\;.

(3.20)

4
One-loop effective action

As shown in
[
1
,
2
,
3
]

the one-loop effective action can be expressed in terms of the Fourier integral
W(u)
of
the spectral density
\rho(\nu)
.

The unregularised one-loop effective action suffers from both infrared and ultraviolet divergences, and hence needs to be regularised. The resulting regularised one-loop effective action, which was obtained in
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
,
will be reviewed in Appendix
B
.
It takes the form

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\rm reg}_{B,\rm 1-loop}=-\frac{V}{8\pi a^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}+i\delta}\frac{du}{\sqrt{\bar{\epsilon}^{2}+u^{2}}}\,W_{B}(u)\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\bar{\epsilon}^{2}+u^{2}}}\,e^{-\kappa u}

(4.1)

for a real Klein-Gordon field, and

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\rm reg}_{F,\rm 1-loop}=\frac{V}{8\pi a^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}+i\delta}\frac{du}{\sqrt{\bar{\epsilon}^{2}+u^{2}}}\,W_{F}(u)\,e^{-\kappa u}

(4.2)

for a complex Dirac field. Here,
\bar{\epsilon}=\epsilon/a
, where
\epsilon
denotes the UV regulator, while
\kappa
denotes the IR regulator.

Let us consider the combination

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\rm reg}_{B,\rm 1-loop}+\frac{1}{2}\,\Gamma^{\rm reg}_{F,\rm 1-loop}=-\frac{V}{8\pi a^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}+i\delta}\frac{du}{\sqrt{\bar{\epsilon}^{2}+u^{2}}}\left(W_{B}(u)\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\bar{\epsilon}^{2}+u^{2}}}-\frac{1}{2}\,W_{F}(u)\right)e^{-\kappa u}\;.

(4.3)

If, for the time being, we proceed formally by switching off both the UV and the IR regulator, we obtain the following unregularised expression for this combination,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\rm unreg}_{B,\rm 1-loop}+\frac{1}{2}\Gamma^{\rm unreg}_{F,\rm 1-loop}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{V}{4\pi a^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{du}{u}\left(W_{B}(u)\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}u}-\frac{1}{2}\,W_{F}(u)\right)

(4.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{V}{4\pi a^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{du}{u}\left(\frac{e^{-u}}{1-e^{-u}}\right).

We note that this is the combination for which the double pole in (
3.20
) precisely cancels out.
By taking this combination, we halved the contribution coming from the Dirac field. Thus, the on-shell degrees of freedom are constituted of a massless scalar field and a massless Majorana fermion. We refer to
[
30
]
for a recent proposal on the construction of a supersymmetric basis for scalar and spinor fields in an Euclidean
AdS_{2}
space-time.

In the following, let us first relate the unregularised one-loop effective action (
4.4
)
to the partition function of a conformal quantum mechanics model. Subsequently, we will discuss the
regularised one-loop effective action.

4.1
Conformal quantum mechanics from unregularised one-loop effective action

The left hand side of (
4.4
) denotes an unregularised one-loop effective action, which we denote by

-\log Z^{\rm unreg}_{\rm 1-loop}
. We then write (
4.4
) as

\displaystyle\log Z^{\rm unreg}_{\rm 1-loop}=\frac{V}{2\pi a^{2}}\,\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{du}{2u}\,Z_{\rm CQM}(u)\;,

(4.5)

where

\displaystyle Z_{\rm CQM}(u)=\frac{e^{-u}}{1-e^{-u}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-(n+1)u}\;\;\;,\;\;\;u>0\;.

(4.6)

We now regard
Z_{\rm CQM}(u)
as computing the trace over eigenstates of a quantum mechanics model,

\displaystyle Z_{\rm CQM}(u)=\text{Tr}\,q^{\hat{N}+1}\;\;\;,\;\;\;{\hat{N}}=a^{\dagger}a\;\;\;,\;\;\;{\hat{N}}|n\rangle=n|n\rangle\;\;\;n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\;\;\;,\;\;\;q=e^{-u}\;.

(4.7)

Then, formally we obtain

\displaystyle\log Z^{\rm unreg}_{\rm 1-loop}=\frac{V}{2\pi a^{2}}\,\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{du}{2u}\,\text{Tr}\,q^{R}\;\;\;,\;\;\;R={\hat{N}}+1\;.

(4.8)

On the LHS, we have the logarithm of a one-loop partition function in an
AdS_{2}
background,
while
the RHS is read as the partition function of a quantum mechanics model (CQM), which is being integrated over
u
. Formally, the RHS is, up to a sign and up to the factor
\frac{V}{2\pi a^{2}}
,
the unregularized 1-loop effective action of the CQM (cf. eq. (1.18) in
[
39
]
).
Thus, formally, we may view (
4.5
) as a correspondence between two 1-loop effective actions, a gravitational one on the LHS and a CQM one on the RHS.

Below we will show that the quantum mechanics model (
4.7
) can be identified with the conformal quantum mechanics model of de Alfaro-Fubini-Furlan (DFF) at a specific value of its coupling constant
[
7
]
.

Below we will make use of the constant term in the Laurent expansion of
H(u)\equiv-Z_{\rm CQM}(u)
around
u=0
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,H(u)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,b_{n}\,u^{n-2}\;\;\;,\;\;\;b_{0}=0\;\;\;,\;\;\;b_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}\;\;\;,\;\;\;b_{2}=\frac{1}{4}\;.

(4.9)

4.2
Regularised one-loop effective action

As we will review in Appendix
C
,
the regularised one-loop effective action (
4.3
), which we denote by

\Gamma^{\rm reg}_{\rm 1-loop}(\bar{\epsilon})
, can be computed in terms of the
character zeta function

\displaystyle\zeta(z)\equiv\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{du}{u}\,u^{z}\,H(u)\;,

(4.10)

and reads

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\rm reg}_{\rm 1-loop}(\bar{\epsilon})=-\log Z^{\rm reg}_{\rm 1-loop}(\bar{\epsilon})=\frac{V}{2\pi a^{2}}\left[-b_{2}\log\left(\frac{e^{\gamma}\,\bar{\epsilon}}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\zeta^{\prime}(0)-\frac{1}{8}\right]\;,\;\bar{\epsilon}=\frac{\epsilon}{a}\;,

(4.11)

where
b_{2}
denotes the constant term in the Laurent expansion (
4.9
) and
\gamma

denotes the Euler-Mascheroni constant.
We recall that
V
denotes the regularised volume of
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\rm BH}
given by (
3.13
), which depends on the regulator
x_{0}
. Thus,
\Gamma^{\rm reg}_{\rm 1-loop}(\bar{\epsilon})
depends on two regulators, namely on
x_{0}
as well as
on the UV regulator
\epsilon
, but it does not depend on the IR regulator
\kappa

[
2
]
. The value
\zeta^{\prime}(0)
is finite and given in Appendix
C
. We note that the dependence on
\bar{\epsilon}
is solely contained in the log-term, i.e. there are no poles in
\bar{\epsilon}
. This is a consequence of the particular field configuration that we chose, namely one massless scalar field and one massless Majorana fermion. Each of them contributes a term
1/{\bar{\epsilon}}^{2}
to the regularised one-loop effective action, but with an opposite sign, so that these poles cancel out in the regularised one-loop effective action, leaving only the logarithmic divergent term.

Following
[
5
,
6
]
, we write the regularised one-loop effective action (
4.3
) as

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\rm reg}_{\rm 1-loop}(\bar{\epsilon})=V\,\Delta L_{\rm eff}\;.

(4.12)

The dependence on the regulator
x_{0}
is contained in
V
only.
The
x_{0}
independent term in this expression reads

\displaystyle-2\pi a^{2}\,\Delta L_{\rm eff}=b_{2}\log\left(\frac{e^{\gamma}\,\epsilon}{2a}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\zeta^{\prime}(0)+\frac{1}{8}\;.

(4.13)

As in
[
5
,
6
]
, we
interpret the regularised one-loop partition function
\Gamma^{\rm reg}_{\rm 1-loop}(\bar{\epsilon})

as (see eq. (3.1) in
[
6
]
)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\rm reg}_{\rm 1-loop}(\bar{\epsilon})=\beta\Delta E-\Delta S\;,

(4.14)

where the term proportional to
\coth x_{0}
is identified with
\beta\Delta E
, while the

x_{0}
independent term is identified with
\Delta S
. Here,
\beta=2\pi a/\sinh x_{0}
is the inverse temperature given by the length
L
of the
boundary of
AdS_{2}
parametrized by
y
(cf. (
2.15
) and (
4.19
) below). Using

V=\beta\,a\,\cosh x_{0}-2\pi a^{2}
, we infer

\displaystyle\Delta E

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\,\cosh x_{0}\,\Delta L_{\rm eff}\;,

\displaystyle\Delta S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{2}\log\frac{a}{\epsilon}=\frac{1}{4}\,\log\frac{a}{\epsilon}\;,

(4.15)

where we have dropped an additive constant in
\Delta S
.

Writing
\Delta S
as

\displaystyle\Delta S=\frac{c}{6}\,\log\frac{a}{\epsilon}\;\;\;,\;\;\;c=1+\frac{1}{2}=\frac{3}{2}\,,

(4.16)

suggests an interpretation of
\Delta S
as a vacuum entanglement entropy
in an Euclidean CFT with central charge
c=\bar{c}
living on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
as follows.

Figure 1
:
Location of the black hole horizons in global
AdS_{2}
.

The black hole horizons in the metric (
2.1
) are null surfaces corresponding to
r=1
and
r=-1
. They lie along the diagonals in the global
AdS_{2}
strip of width
\pi a
and separate regions
I
and
III
from global future and past time-like infinities at
\tau\rightarrow\pm\infty

[
14
]
, as depicted in Figure
1
.
We divide each region into two equal isosceles right angled triangles by a horizontal line through the diagonal intersection. Each of these triangles has a hypotenuse along a null surface, while the perpendicular to the base lies along an edge of the strip and is of length
\pi a
.
Each of the 2 diagonals is composed of 2 equal length hypotenuses, one corresponding to
r=1
and the other to an
r=-1
null surface. One of these hypotenuses is porous towards the flow of information towards future timelike infinity, while the other allows information to flow in from past timelike infinity. Subdividing each diagonal into 2 hypotenuses and tracing out the field theory states living on one of them results in an entanglement entropy associated with the CFT living on the remaining hypotenuse. We can thus view the entanglement entropy across each hypotenuse as that of a CFT subsystem of length
\ell=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\pi a
nestled within a system of size
{\hat{L}}=\sqrt{2}\pi a
with central charge
c=\bar{c}
at zero temperature. Following eq. (23) in

[
31
]
, we have, upto additive constants,

\displaystyle{\cal S}=\,\frac{c}{6}\log\left(\frac{\hat{L}}{\pi\epsilon}\,\sin\left(\frac{\pi\ell}{\hat{L}}\right)\right)=\frac{c}{6}\log\frac{a}{\epsilon}\,=\frac{1}{4}\log\frac{a}{\epsilon}.

(4.17)

Here
\epsilon
is the short-distance cutoff associated with the diagonal which we will identify with the UV scale in the one-loop effective action.
This calculation depends purely on the geometrical measures of the triangles and is hence the same for the remaining three isosceles right angled triangles. The total entanglement entropy for the given fields associated with the 4 horizon null surfaces is therefore

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{\rm total}=\log\frac{a}{\epsilon}.

(4.18)

The expression (
4.17
) agrees with (
4.16
).

The length
L
of a closed circular particle geodesic located at
0<x_{0}\ll 1
, near the
AdS_{2}
boundary
\partial M
, is computed to be

\displaystyle L=\int_{\partial M}dv\,\sqrt{g_{yy}\,\dot{y}^{2}}=\frac{2\pi\,a}{\sinh x_{0}}\;.

(4.19)

We now take the value of the bulk IR regulator
\sinh x_{0}
to be numerically equal to the regulator
\epsilon
,

\displaystyle\epsilon=a\,\sinh x_{0}\;.

(4.20)

We obtain

\displaystyle L=\frac{2\pi a^{2}}{\epsilon}\;,

(4.21)

enabling
\Delta S
to be written as

\displaystyle\Delta S=\frac{1}{4}\,\log\frac{L}{2\pi a}\;,

(4.22)

which expresses
\Delta S
in terms of the length
L
of the closed circular geodesic located at
0<x_{0}\ll 1
.

Global Euclidean
AdS_{2}
can be represented as a strip with two boundaries located at
\sigma=-\pi,0
, cf. (
2.20
). Using the relation (
4.5
), we assign to each of these boundaries a copy of a conformal
quantum mechanics model which in the next section we will identify with the DFF model
[
7
]
at a specific value of its coupling.
We will then use the results of
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
to
show that
{\cal S}_{\rm total}=4\,\Delta S
can be equated to an entanglement entropy
between these two copies which constitute a thermofield double description of the DFF model,
computed in a state denoted by
|\delta\rangle
.

5
The DFF model

In this section, we review various properties of the spectrum of
the conformal quantum mechanics model of DFF
[
7
]
.
The DFF Hamiltonian
H
reads

\displaystyle H=\frac{1}{2}\left(p^{2}+\frac{g}{q^{2}}\right)\;\;\;,\;\;\;g>0\;,

(5.1)

where
g
denotes the dimensionless coupling of the model.
We follow

[
8
,
9
]
, who have studied this model in great detail.

We consider the
so(2,1)
algebra (
2.28
).
We redefine
L_{-}\rightarrow-L_{-}
and define
R=iL_{0}
,
in which case

[R,L_{\pm}]=\pm L_{\pm}\,,\qquad[L_{-},L_{+}]=2R\,.

(5.2)

The generators
R,L_{\pm}
are related to the generators
H,K,D
(where
H
denotes the Hamiltonian,
K
the conformal boost generator and
D
the dilation generator) by

\displaystyle R=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{K}{a}+a\,H\right)\;\;\;,\;\;\;L_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{K}{a}-a\,H\right)\pm i\,D\;,

(5.3)

where
a
denotes the
AdS_{2}
scale (of length dimension one) in (
2.15
).
The generator
R
generates a compact subgroup
SO(2)
, and hence its spectrum is discrete,

\displaystyle R|n\rangle=\left(n+r_{0}\right)\,|n\rangle\;\;\;,\;\;\;n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\;\;\;,\;\;\;\langle m|n\rangle=\delta_{m,n}\;.

(5.4)

The state
|0\rangle
is the
R
-vacuum.
We set
r_{0}=1
in view of (
4.7
). In the DFF model,
r_{0}
is expressed in terms of the coupling constant
g
of the model as

\displaystyle r_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\sqrt{g+\frac{1}{4}}\right)\;.

(5.5)

It follows that
g=\frac{3}{4}
.

The operators
L_{\pm}
act as ladder operators on
|n\rangle
,

\displaystyle L_{\pm}|n\rangle=\sqrt{(n+1)(n+1\pm 1)}\,|n\pm 1\rangle\;,

(5.6)

which implies

\displaystyle|n\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!(n+1)!}}\,(L_{+})^{n}\,|0\rangle\;.

(5.7)

The Casimir operator (
2.29
) (where we replaced
L_{-}
by
-L_{-}
)

\displaystyle Q=R^{2}-R-L_{+}L_{-}\;,

(5.8)

annihilates the states
|n\rangle
,

\displaystyle Q|n\rangle=0\;.

(5.9)

We define the operator
\mathbb{S}
by

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}=\frac{1}{2}\left(a\,H-\frac{K}{a}\right)\;.

(5.10)

In the following, we will discuss Schrödinger type equations for the operators,
H,R,\mathbb{S}
, each
written with respect to a different time variable.

5.1
H,R
and
\mathbb{S}

Following
[
8
,
9
]
, we consider Schrödinger type equations for the operators,
H,R,\mathbb{S}
,

\displaystyle H|t\rangle=-i\frac{d}{dt}|t\rangle\;,

\displaystyle R|\varphi\rangle=-i\frac{d}{d{\varphi}}|\varphi\rangle\;,

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}|\tau\rangle=-i\frac{d}{d{\tau}}|\tau\rangle\;,

(5.11)

where the time variables
t,\varphi,\tau
refer to Poincaré patch time, black hole time and global time, respectively.
The associated Euclidean line elements are (cf. Section
2.1
)

\displaystyle ds^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}\left(r^{2}dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\;,

\displaystyle ds_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}\left(\sinh^{2}\rho\,d\varphi^{2}+d\rho^{2}\right)\;\;\;,\;\;\;\varphi\equiv\varphi+2\pi\;,

\displaystyle ds_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a^{2}}{\sin^{2}\sigma}\left(d\tau^{2}+d\sigma^{2}\right)\;\;\;,\;\;\;-\pi<\sigma<0\;.

(5.12)

The first two line elements are related by the coordinate transformation
[
16
]
,

\displaystyle\omega=\frac{1-\frac{1}{r}+i\,t}{1+\frac{1}{r}-i\,t}=e^{i\varphi}\,\tanh\frac{\rho}{2}\;,

(5.13)

where here the coordinates
t,r,\rho
are all dimensionless. Reinstating the dimensions of
t,r
by

t\rightarrow t/a
and
r\rightarrow r/a
,
\omega
becomes

\displaystyle\omega=\frac{a-\frac{a^{2}}{r}+i\,t}{a+\frac{a^{2}}{r}-i\,t}\;.

(5.14)

When
r\rightarrow+\infty
, which implies
\rho\rightarrow+\infty
, this gives

\displaystyle t=a\,\tan\frac{\varphi}{2}\;.

(5.15)

The second and the third line elements are related by
[
11
]

\displaystyle\tan\left(\frac{\sigma+i\tau}{2}\right)=\tanh\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\log\hat{\rho}+i\varphi\right)\right)\;\;\;,\;\;\;-\infty<\tau<+\infty\;\;\;,\;\;\;-\pi<\sigma<0\;,

(5.16)

where

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}=\tanh\frac{\rho}{2}\;.

(5.17)

Hence,
\rho\rightarrow+\infty
corresponds to
\hat{\rho}\rightarrow 1
. The segment

\hat{\rho}=1,-\frac{\pi}{2}<\varphi<\frac{\pi}{2}
gets mapped to the boundary
\sigma=0
, while the
segment

\hat{\rho}=1,\frac{\pi}{2}<\varphi<\frac{3\pi}{2}
gets mapped to the boundary
\sigma=-\pi

[
11
]
.

Combining (
5.14
) with (
5.16
) gives

\displaystyle\tan\left(\frac{\sigma+i\tau}{2}\right)=\tanh\left(\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{a-\frac{a^{2}}{r}+i\,t}{a+\frac{a^{2}}{r}-i\,t}\right)\right)\;,

(5.18)

which relates Poincaré patch coordinates
(t,r)
with the global coordinates
(\sigma,\tau)
. When
r=+\infty
and
\sigma=0
, this becomes

\displaystyle\tan\left(i\frac{\tau}{2}\right)=i\tanh\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)=\tanh\left(i\frac{\varphi}{2}\right)=i\tan\left(\frac{\varphi}{2}\right)=i\frac{t}{a}\;,

(5.19)

and hence

\displaystyle\frac{t}{a}=\tanh\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)\;.

(5.20)

On the other hand, when
r=+\infty
and
\sigma=-\pi
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{t}{a}=\coth\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)\;.

(5.21)

At large
r
(and hence
x_{0}\ll 1)
, we infer from (
2.3
) (after rescaling
r\rightarrow r/a
) that
r=a/x_{0}
, while from (
4.20
) we obtain
\epsilon=a\,x_{0}
. By combining these two expressions,
we infer that in Poincaré patch coordinates
1/\epsilon
translates into the
boundary location

\displaystyle\frac{r}{a^{2}}=\frac{1}{\epsilon}>0\;.

(5.22)

Then, using (
5.14
), we define

\displaystyle\omega_{\epsilon}(t)=\frac{a-\epsilon+i\,t}{a+\epsilon-i\,t}\;,

(5.23)

which satisfies
|\omega_{\epsilon}(t)|<1
so long as
\epsilon>0
. We may write
\omega_{\epsilon}(t)
as

\displaystyle\omega_{\epsilon}(t)=\frac{a+i\,\hat{t}}{a-i\,\hat{t}}\;\;\;,\;\;\hat{t}=t+i\epsilon\;.

(5.24)

It follows that various relations that were derived in
[
8
,
9
]
continue to hold by simply replacing
t
by
\hat{t}
. In particular, we have

\displaystyle H\,|t\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\frac{d}{dt}|t\rangle\;,

\displaystyle R\,|t\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\left(\frac{\hat{t}}{a}+\frac{a^{2}+\hat{t}^{2}}{2a}\frac{d}{dt}\right)|t\rangle\;,

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}\,|t\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\left(\frac{\hat{t}}{a}-\frac{a^{2}-\hat{t}^{2}}{2a}\frac{d}{dt}\right)|t\rangle\;.

(5.25)

These can be verified explicitly using the relations given below, and we do so in Appendix
D
. The state
|t\rangle
, constructed in
[
8
]
, is defined as follows.
Following
[
8
]
we introduce the operator

\displaystyle O(t)=N(t)\,e^{-\omega_{\epsilon}(t)\,L_{+}}\;\;\;,\;\;\;N(t)=\left(\frac{\omega_{\epsilon}(t)+1}{2}\right)^{2}\>.

(5.26)

Then, the state
|t\rangle
is obtained by acting with the operator
O(t)
on the
R
-vacuum
|0\rangle
,

\displaystyle|t\rangle=O(t)\,|0\rangle\;.

(5.27)

As mentioned above, we identify the conformal quantum mechanics model living on each of the two boundaries of global Euclidean
AdS_{2}
with the DFF model at coupling

. We now construct states
|\tau\rangle
living on either of the boundaries
\sigma=-\pi,0

We begin by considering the boundary

(5.28)

Then, the right hand side of the expression for
\mathbb{S}|t\rangle

5.11

\sigma=0

On the boundary

Then, the right hand side of the expression for
\mathbb{S}|t\rangle
in (
5.25

\displaystyle-i\left(\sinh^{2}\frac{\hat{\tau}}{2}\right)\,\frac{d}{d\tau}\left(\frac{1}{\sinh^{2}\frac{\hat{\tau}}{2}}\,|t\rangle\right)\;,

(5.32)

and hence the state

\displaystyle|\tau\rangle\equiv\frac{1}{\sinh^{2}\frac{\hat{\tau}}{2}}\,|t\rangle

(5.33)

5.11
). This is a state at the boundary
\sigma=-\pi

Note that these two states, which live at different boundaries, are distinct, except when

5.2
The boundary state
|\tau\rightarrow-\infty\rangle

We will now proceed to define boundary states
|\tau\rangle
at
\tau\rightarrow-\infty
. Since the resulting expressions are divergent, we will need to regularise them.

First, we focus on the state
|\tau\rangle
at the stretched boundary
\sigma=-\delta

with
0<\delta\ll 1
. We begin by determining
\alpha
in (
5.28
)
and then take the limit
\tau\rightarrow-\infty
on this boundary.
We work to first order in
\epsilon

Using (
5.18

\displaystyle\tanh\left(\frac{-i\sigma+\tau}{2}\right)=\tan\left(-\frac{i}{2}\log\omega_{\epsilon}(t)\right)\;\;\;,\;\;\;\omega_{\epsilon}(t)=\frac{1+i\,\hat{t}/a}{1-i\,\hat{t}/a}\;.

(5.34)

Expanding
\omega_{\epsilon}(t)
to first order in
\epsilon
gives

\displaystyle\log\omega_{\epsilon}(t)=\log\omega_{0}(t)-\frac{2a\epsilon}{a^{2}+t^{2}}+{\cal O}(\epsilon^{2})\;\;\;,\;\;\;\omega_{0}(t)=\frac{a+i\,t}{a-i\,t}\;\;\;,\;\;\;|\omega_{0}(t)|=1\;.

(5.35)

Using (
5.13
), we identify

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{2}\log\omega_{0}(t)=\frac{\varphi}{2}\;.

(5.36)

Then, using (
5.15
) we
obtain, to first order in
\epsilon
,

\displaystyle\tanh\left(\frac{-i\sigma+\tau}{2}\right)=\tan\left(\frac{\varphi}{2}+i\frac{a\epsilon}{a^{2}+t^{2}}\right)=\frac{\frac{t}{a}+i\tanh\left(\frac{a\epsilon}{a^{2}+t^{2}}\right)}{1-i\,\frac{t}{a}\,\tanh\left(\frac{a\epsilon}{a^{2}+t^{2}}\right)}=\frac{\hat{t}}{a}+{\cal O}(\epsilon^{2})\,.

(5.37)

Setting
\sigma=-\delta
and comparing with (
5.28

\displaystyle\alpha=\delta\;.

(5.38)

Next, we write the left hand side of (

\displaystyle\tanh\left(\frac{-i\sigma+\tau}{2}\right)=1-\frac{2}{e^{\tau}\,\cos\sigma+1-ie^{\tau}\,\sin\sigma}=1-\frac{2\left(e^{\tau}\cos\sigma+1+ie^{\tau}\sin\sigma\right)}{\left(e^{\tau}\cos\sigma+1\right)^{2}+e^{2\tau}\sin^{2}\sigma}\;,

from which we infer the relations

\displaystyle\frac{t}{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{2\left(e^{\tau}\cos\sigma+1\right)}{\left(e^{\tau}\cos\sigma+1\right)^{2}+e^{2\tau}\sin^{2}\sigma}\;,

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\frac{e^{\tau}\sin\sigma}{\left(e^{\tau}\cos\sigma+1\right)^{2}+e^{2\tau}\sin^{2}\sigma}\;.

(5.40)

At order
\delta
and
e^{\tau}=e^{-1/T}
with
0<\delta\ll 1
and
0<T\ll 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{t}{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-1+2e^{-1/T}\;,

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2e^{-1/T}\delta>0\;.

(5.41)

Next, let us consider the state
|\tau\rangle
at the stretched boundary
\sigma=-\pi+\delta
with
0<\delta\ll 1
. Then, setting
\sigma=-\pi+\delta
in (
5.37
) and comparing with (
5.31
), we infer that

\displaystyle\alpha=-\delta\;.

(5.42)

Now we take the limit
\tau\rightarrow-\infty
.
At order
\delta
and
e^{\tau}=e^{-1/T}
with
0<\delta\ll 1
and
0<T\ll 1

\displaystyle\frac{t}{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-1-2e^{-1/T}\;,

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2e^{-1/T}\delta>0\;.

(5.43)

In what follows, we will set
t/a=-1
, while retaining a first order dependence on

in all expressions. In the limit

5.3
Thermofield description of the state
|\delta\rangle

In order to write down the state
|\delta\rangle
as a thermofield double state,
we will first
review the thermofield representation of the algebra (
5.2
) by following
[
32
,
10
]
.

The algebra (
5.2
) can be represented in terms of operators acting on the direct product of two independent
Hilbert spaces,
\mathcal{H}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R}
, each described by a complete set of orthonormal states, which we denote by
S_{L}
and
S_{R}
respectively,

\displaystyle S_{L}=\{|n\rangle_{L},\,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}\;\;\;,\;\;\;S_{R}=\{|n\rangle_{R},\,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}\;,

(5.44)

with the states
|n\rangle_{L}
and
|n\rangle_{R}
satisfying

{}_{L}\langle m|n\rangle_{L}=\delta_{m,n}\;\;\;,\;\;\;_{R}\langle m|n\rangle_{R}=\delta_{m,n}\;.

(5.45)

The space
\mathcal{H}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R}
is spanned by vectors belonging to the set

\displaystyle\{|n,m\rangle=|n\rangle_{L}\otimes|m\rangle_{R},\,\,n,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}

(5.46)

satisfying

\displaystyle\langle n,m|l,k\rangle=\delta_{m,l}\,\delta_{n,k}\;.

(5.47)

We introduce two sets of annihilation and creation operators,

a^{\dagger}_{L},a^{\dagger}_{R}
and
a_{L},a_{R}
, respectively, satisfying

\displaystyle[a_{L},a^{\dagger}_{L}]=1\;\;\;,\;\;\;[a_{R},a^{\dagger}_{R}]=1\;,

(5.48)

with all other commutators vanishing. They act as follows on the states
|n,m\rangle
,

\displaystyle a_{L}|n,m\rangle=\sqrt{n}\,|n-1,m\rangle\;\;\;\,\;\;\;a^{\dagger}_{L}|n,m\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}\,|n+1,m\rangle\;,

\displaystyle a_{R}|n,m\rangle=\sqrt{m}\,|n,m-1\rangle\;\;\;\,\;\;\;a^{\dagger}_{R}|n,m\rangle=\sqrt{m+1}\,|n,m+1\rangle\;,

(5.49)

and hence

\displaystyle a_{L}|0,m\rangle=a_{R}|n,0\rangle=0

(5.50)

as well as

\displaystyle|n,m\rangle=\frac{\left(a^{\dagger}_{L}\right)^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}\,\frac{\left(a^{\dagger}_{R}\right)^{m}}{\sqrt{m!}}\,|0\rangle_{L}\otimes|0\rangle_{R}\;.

(5.51)

The generators of the algebra (
5.2
) can be represented by

\displaystyle R=\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{\dagger}_{L}\,a_{L}+a^{\dagger}_{R}\,a_{R}+1\right)\;\;\;,\;\;\;\ L_{+}=a^{\dagger}_{L}\,a^{\dagger}_{R}\;\;\;,\;\;\;L_{-}=a_{L}\,a_{R}\;.

(5.52)

A state
|n,m\rangle
is an eigenstate of
R
with eigenvalue
\frac{1}{2}(n+m+1)
.

To obtain the thermofield double description of a state
|\tau\rangle
, we follow

[
10
]
and map the

R
-vacuum
|0\rangle
in (
5.4
), which is an eigenstate of
R
with eigenvalue
r_{0}=1
, to the groundstate of the operator
R
given in (
5.52
). The latter state,

\displaystyle|0,0\rangle=|0\rangle_{L}\otimes|0\rangle_{R}\;,

(5.53)

is an eigenstate of
R
in (
5.52
) with eigenvalue
1/2
. Similarly, the eigenstate
|n\rangle

in (
5.4
) with eigenvalue
n+1
is mapped to the eigenstate
|n,n\rangle
of weight
n+\frac{1}{2}
, as shown above. We will see later that this is a consistent construction of a thermofield double description, as the entanglement entropy obtained by tracing out states in one of the Hilbert space copies is equal to the Boltzmann entropy computed in the original CQM.

Let us first consider
the state
|\tau\rangle
given in (
5.30
), whose thermofield double representation is

\displaystyle|\tau\rangle=\frac{N(t)}{\cosh^{2}\frac{\hat{\tau}}{2}}\,e^{-\omega_{\epsilon}(t)\,L_{+}}\,|0\rangle_{L}\otimes|0\rangle_{R}\;,

(5.54)

with
L_{+}
given in (
5.52
). We obtain

\displaystyle|\tau\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{N(t)}{\cosh^{2}\frac{\hat{\tau}}{2}}\,\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\,(-1)^{n}\,\omega^{n}_{\epsilon}(t)\,|n\rangle_{L}\otimes|n\rangle_{R}

(5.55)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a^{2}}{(a-i\hat{t}\,)^{2}}\left(1-\frac{\hat{t}^{2}}{a^{2}}\right)\,\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\,(-1)^{n}\,\left(\frac{a+i\,\hat{t}}{a-i\,\hat{t}}\right)^{n}\,|n\rangle_{L}\otimes|n\rangle_{R}\;,

where we used (
5.28
) to express

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}\frac{\hat{\tau}}{2}}=1-\frac{\hat{t}^{2}}{a^{2}}\;.

(5.56)

It follows that

\displaystyle\langle\tau|\tau\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a^{4}}{|a-i\hat{t}\,|^{4}}\,\Big|1-\frac{\hat{t}^{2}}{a^{2}}\Big|^{2}\,\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\,\left(\frac{(a-\epsilon)^{2}+t^{2}}{(a+\epsilon)^{2}+t^{2}}\right)^{n}

(5.57)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a^{4}}{(a+\epsilon)^{2}+t^{2}}\,\Big|1-\frac{\hat{t}^{2}}{a^{2}}\Big|^{2}\,\frac{1}{4a\,\epsilon}\;.

We define

\displaystyle{\cal N}^{2}(t)\equiv\frac{4a\,\epsilon\,\left((a+\epsilon)^{2}+t^{2}\right)}{|a^{2}-\hat{t}^{2}|^{2}}\;.

(5.58)

Now we consider a state
|\tau\rangle
defined at

\tau\rightarrow-\infty
, which is consistent with setting
t=-a
from (
5.41
). In this limit, we get

\displaystyle{\cal N}^{2}\equiv{\cal N}^{2}(t\rightarrow-a)=\frac{4a\,\epsilon\,\left((a+\epsilon)^{2}+a^{2}\right)}{|a^{2}-(a-i\epsilon)^{2}|^{2}}=\frac{4a\,\left((a+\epsilon)^{2}+a^{2}\right)}{\epsilon\,(4a^{2}+\epsilon^{2})}\;.

(5.59)

To order
1/\epsilon
this reads

\displaystyle{\cal N}^{2}=\frac{2a}{\epsilon}+{\cal O}(\epsilon^{0})\;.

(5.60)

We define the normalised state
|\delta\rangle
by

\displaystyle|\delta\rangle\equiv{\cal N}\,|\tau\rightarrow-\infty\rangle\;\;\;,\;\;\;\langle\delta|\delta\rangle=1\;,

(5.61)

where

\displaystyle|\tau\rightarrow-\infty\rangle=\frac{2i\epsilon a}{(a+\epsilon+ia)^{2}}\,\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\,(-1)^{n}\,\left(\frac{a-\epsilon-ia}{a+\epsilon+ia}\right)^{n}\,|n\rangle_{L}\otimes|n\rangle_{R}\;.

(5.62)

To lowest order in
\epsilon
we have

\displaystyle\frac{a-\epsilon-ia}{a+\epsilon+ia}=-i\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{a}\right)+{\cal O}(\epsilon^{2})\;,

\displaystyle{\cal N}\,\frac{2i\epsilon a}{(a+\epsilon+ia)^{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{2\epsilon}{a}}+{\cal O}(\epsilon)\;.

(5.63)

Using these expressions, we obtain the following leading expression for
|\delta\rangle
in an
\epsilon
-expansion,

\displaystyle|\delta\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{2\epsilon}{a}}\,\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}e^{-\frac{\epsilon}{a}n+i\frac{\pi}{2}n}\,|n\rangle_{L}\otimes|n\rangle_{R}\;,

(5.64)

which we recast as

\displaystyle|\delta\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{Z(\beta)}}\;\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\,E_{n}+i\alpha_{n}}\,|n\rangle_{L}\otimes|n\rangle_{R}

(5.65)

with

\displaystyle E_{n}=n\;\;\;\;,\;\;\;\beta=\frac{2\epsilon}{a}\;\;\;,\;\;\;\alpha_{n}=i\frac{\pi}{2}\,n\;.

(5.66)

Here,
Z(\beta)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}e^{-\beta\,E_{n}}
which, to lowest in
\beta
,
satisfies
1/Z(\beta)=\beta
. Note that at this order in
\beta
,

1/Z(\beta)
equals
1/Z_{\rm CQM}(\beta)
, where
Z_{\rm CQM}
denotes the partition function of the DFF model given in (
4.6
).
Also note that the boundary time periodicity
\beta
is distinct from the bulk time periodicity (
4.14
).
Thus, at this order of the
\epsilon
-expansion, the state
|\delta\rangle
describes a thermofield double state
that involves time-independent phases
\alpha_{n}
; this differs from the recent work

[
33
]
, where the phases
\alpha_{n}
are taken to be time-dependent and appear in the context of wormholes.

We defined the above state
|\delta\rangle
by considering a state
|\tau\rangle
on the boundary
\sigma=-\delta

with
0<\delta\ll 1
and taking the limit
\tau\rightarrow-\infty
.
Now let us define a state
|\delta\rangle
by
considering a state
|\tau\rangle
on the boundary
\sigma=-\pi+\delta
and then again taken the limit
\tau\rightarrow-\infty
. To this end, we start from the state
|\tau\rangle
given in
(
5.33
) and proceed as above. Using (
5.31
) to express

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sinh^{2}\frac{\hat{\tau}}{2}}=\frac{\hat{t}^{2}}{a^{2}}-1\;,

(5.67)

we find that the resulting state
|\delta\rangle
equals (
5.65
). The state
|\delta\rangle
is thus shared by both boundaries of global Euclidean
AdS_{2}
.

We view the two Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R}
as being associated to the two boundaries of global
AdS_{2}
located at
\sigma=-\pi,0
. By tracing over

\mathcal{H}_{L}
associated with the boundary
\sigma=-\pi
, we obtain an
associated von Neumann entropy which is given by

\displaystyle S_{\rm vN}=-\log\beta=-\log\frac{2\epsilon}{a}\;,

(5.68)

as we will show below.

Since the boundary time periodicity
\beta
goes to zero as
\epsilon

goes to zero (cf. (
5.66
)),
\epsilon
serves as a UV length scale cutoff on the boundary.
The IR length scale cutoff is set to
a/x_{0}=a^{2}/\epsilon
.
This is consistent
with the expected relation between the IR-UV cutoffs in
AdS/CFT
.

5.4
Von Neumann entropy

Having defined the state
|\delta\rangle
, we consider the pure state density matrix

|\delta\rangle\langle\delta|
on
\mathcal{H}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R}
, which we denote by

\hat{\rho}
,

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\delta\rangle\langle\delta|=\frac{1}{Z(\beta)}\,\sum_{n,m=0}^{+\infty}\,e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\,\left(E_{n}+E_{m}\right)+i\left(\alpha_{n}-\alpha_{m}\right)}\,|n\rangle_{L}\langle m|_{L}\otimes|n\rangle_{R}\langle m|_{R}\;.

(5.69)

By tracing over
\mathcal{H}_{L}
we obtain the reduced density matrix
\rho
,

\displaystyle\rho=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{L}}\hat{\rho}=\sum_{l=0}^{+\infty}{{}_{L}\langle}l|\hat{\rho}|l\rangle_{L}=\frac{1}{Z(\beta)}\,\sum_{l=0}^{+\infty}\,e^{-\beta\,E_{l}}\,|l\rangle_{R}\langle l|_{R}

(5.70)

The associated entanglement entropy
S_{\rm vN}
is

\displaystyle S_{\rm vN}=-\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}}\,\rho\,\log\rho\;,

(5.71)

which can be computed using

\displaystyle S_{\rm vN}=-\lim_{k\rightarrow 1}\frac{d}{dk}\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}}\,{\rho}^{k}\;.

(5.72)

To do so
[
34
]
, one first computes
{\rho}^{k}
with
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}}\,{\rho}^{k}=\frac{Z(k\beta)}{Z^{k}(\beta)}\;,

(5.73)

and then analytically continues
the result to a holomorphic function on the half-plane
{\rm Re}\,k\geq 1
. As discussed in
[
34
]
, this analytic continuation exists and is unique.
Using that
Z(\beta)=a/(2\epsilon)
to leading order in
\epsilon
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{Z(k\beta)}{Z^{k}(\beta)}=\frac{a}{2\epsilon k}\left(\frac{2\epsilon}{a}\right)^{k}\;,

(5.74)

and hence, in the limit
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, the von Neumann entropy
S_{\rm vN}(\rho)
is

\displaystyle S_{\rm vN}=-\log\frac{2\epsilon}{a}\,.

(5.75)

The thermofield doubling of the Hilbert space ensures that the entanglement entropy
(
5.75
)
between two identical Hilbert space copies on the two boundaries of global
AdS_{2}
equals the von Neumann entropy of the CQM on one of the boundaries. The latter is obtained as follows. We start from the partition function
Z_{\rm CQM}(u)
, given in (
4.6
), of the conformal quantum mechanics model on one of the boundaries of global
AdS_{2}
. We use (
5.66
) and set

u=\beta=2{\bar{\epsilon}}=2\epsilon/a
, in which case

Z_{\rm CQM}({\bar{\epsilon}})=e^{-\beta}Z(\beta)=e^{-\beta}\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}e^{-\beta\,E_{n}}={\rm Tr}e^{-\beta R}
. The associated density matrix is

\rho=e^{-2{\bar{\epsilon}}R}/Z_{\rm CQM}({\bar{\epsilon}})
, where
R
denotes the compact generator (
5.4
) with
r_{0}=1
.
Denoting the eigenvalues of
\rho

by
\lambda_{n}=e^{-2{\bar{\epsilon}}\,(n+1)}/Z_{\rm CQM}({\bar{\epsilon}})
, the von Neumann entropy is

\displaystyle S_{\rm vN}=-\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\lambda_{n}\,\log\lambda_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log Z_{\rm CQM}({\bar{\epsilon}})+\frac{2{\bar{\epsilon}}}{Z_{\rm CQM}({\bar{\epsilon}})}\,\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}n\,e^{-2{\bar{\epsilon}}\,n}

(5.76)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log Z_{\rm CQM}({\bar{\epsilon}})-{\bar{\epsilon}}\,\partial_{\bar{\epsilon}}\log Z_{\rm CQM}({\bar{\epsilon}})\;.

In the limit
{\bar{\epsilon}}\rightarrow 0^{+}
,
Z_{\rm CQM}({\bar{\epsilon}})
behaves as
Z_{\rm CQM}({\bar{\epsilon}})\sim 1/(2{\bar{\epsilon}})
(cf. (
4.6
)) and hence

\displaystyle S_{\rm vN}=\log\frac{a}{2\epsilon}\;,

(5.77)

up to an
\bar{\epsilon}
-independent constant. This expression agrees with the von Neumann entropy of entanglement between the two DFF copies on the boundaries of global
AdS_{2}
given in (
5.75
).

Comparing with (
4.18
) and (
4.22
), we establish that, up to
\epsilon
-independent constants,

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{\rm total}=\,4\Delta S\,=\log\frac{L}{2\pi a}=S_{\rm vN}\;.

(5.78)

The last equality in (
5.78
)
expresses
S_{\rm vN}
, the von Neumann
entropy of entanglement between the two CQM copies on the boundaries of global
AdS_{2}
, in terms of
a geometrical quantity
L
in the bulk. We view this as the
AdS_{2}/CFT_{1}
version of the Ryu-Takayanagi conjecture
[
12
,
13
]
in our setup.
The equality between
{\cal S}_{\rm total}
and
S_{\rm vN}
equates bulk entanglement entropy across black hole horizons to boundary entanglement entropy.
We note here that the evaluation of both the von Neumann entropy (
5.72
) in the boundary theory as well the bulk entanglement entropy (
4.18
) (based on the result (
4.17
)
[
31
]
) has been done using the replica trick. Both these entropies arise at the 1-loop level, in contrast to previous tree-level bulk replica computations of black hole entropy
[
35
]
.
We further note that (
5.78
) remains valid when the field theory on
AdS_{2}
consists of
n
multiplets, each of which contains a massless scalar field and a massless Majorana fermion.
The central charge of this system is then
c=\frac{3}{2}n
, and the conformal quantum mechanics model on each of the two boundaries of global
AdS_{2}
is identified with
n
copies of the DFF model.

6
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we have shown that the regularised one-loop effective action for a CFT with central charge
c={\bar{c}}=1+\frac{1}{2}=\frac{3}{2}
in Euclidean
AdS_{2}
spacetime contains the contribution
\Delta S=\frac{c}{6}\log\frac{a}{\epsilon}
,
where
a
denotes the
AdS_{2}
scale and
\epsilon
denotes the UV regulator. In this computation, equality of the bosonic and fermionic degrees of freedom in the field content is essential to cancel
\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}
divergences leaving only the logarithmic divergent term
\Delta S
.
We have shown that
4\Delta S
equals the total entanglement entropy across the black hole horizons embedded in global Lorentzian
AdS_{2}
and is exactly equal to the entanglement entropy between two copies of a
CFT_{1}
, each living on one of the two boundaries of global Euclidean
AdS_{2}
. The dual
CFT_{1}
is a CQM identified by writing the unregularised bulk one-loop effective action as an integral, whose integrand is the requisite dual
CFT_{1}
partition function.
The identified dual
CFT_{1}
in this case is the conformal quantum mechanics model of de Alfaro-Fubini-Furlan (DFF)
[
7
]
at the specific coupling value
g=3/4
. Further,
the boundary entanglement entropy is expressed in terms of the logarithm of the length of a closed geodesic on the Euclidean
AdS_{2}
disc. We view this relation between a measure of boundary quantum entanglement and a bulk geometrical property as the Ryu-Takayanagi conjecture in
AdS_{2}/CFT_{1}
in our setup.

Simultaneously, the identification of
4\Delta S
with bulk and boundary entanglement entropies can be viewed as a bulk-boundary entanglement entropy correspondence which relates quantum entanglement in the bulk to that to quantum entanglement in the boundary.

Our third result hinges on the observation that given the non-negative von Neumann entropy of the DFF model expressed as a function of a length scale
a
and a short distance cutoff
\epsilon
, we can write the infinitesimal change in the entropy
S_{\rm vN}
as

\displaystyle dS_{\rm vN}=-e^{S_{\rm vN}}\,\frac{d\epsilon}{a}.

(6.1)

One can construct a
1+1
space-time that encodes the above relation in its causal structure and is described by the metric

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-e^{2S_{\rm vN}}\,\frac{d\epsilon^{2}}{a^{2}}+dS^{2}_{\rm vN}\;,

(6.2)

which encodes a global Lorentzian
AdS_{2}
metric near one of its boundaries. The above metric can be thought of as the Fisher information metric on the information space of the von Neumann entropy and the UV scale. This observation is comparable to a related one in the context of a Calogero model as in
[
36
]
.

One may wonder whether the DFF model also plays a role in providing a
CFT_{1}
description of the logarithmic area corrections (if they exist) to the entropy of BPS black holes in four dimensions
[
5
,
6
,
37
]
.
We emphasise here that black hole entropy is encoded in the 4D near horizon geometry,
AdS_{2}\times S^{2}
, which is computed in the quantum entropy function formalism as a path integral of fields which generically have legs in both
AdS_{2}
and
S^{2}
. The DFF entanglement entropy can hence only feed into the
AdS_{2}
contribution to the black hole entropy. Nevertheless, as a future line of investigation, 2D entanglement entropy computations in JT gravity can potentially shed light on 2D wormholes that have been known
to contribute to
R^{2}
corrected black hole entropy in supersymmetric theories such as
N=4

[
38
]
.

W_{B,F}(u)

Let us first consider the spectral density (
3.12
).
\rho_{B}(\nu)
has simple poles located at

\displaystyle\nu_{n}=i\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\;\;\;,\;\;\;n\in\mathbb{Z}\;.

(A.1)

Expanding
\nu
around
\nu_{n}
, i.e.
\nu=\nu_{n}+\delta\nu
,
we infer that in the vicinity of the simple pole
\nu_{n}
,

\displaystyle\rho_{B}(\nu)=\frac{\nu_{n}}{\pi\,\delta\nu}\>.

(A.2)

We deform the integration contour in (
3.17
) to run along the two sides of the positive imaginary axis. We therefore restrict our attention to the poles
\nu_{n}

with
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

The resulting contour integral in (
3.17
) is then evaluated by summing over residues. Each simple pole contributes

\displaystyle 2\pi i\,e^{i\nu_{n}u}\;\frac{\nu_{n}}{\pi}=-(2n+1)\,e^{-(\frac{1}{2}+n)u}\;\;\;,\;\;\;n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\;.

(A.3)

Summing over all the residues gives (here we take
{\rm Re}\,u>0
)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(2n+1)\,e^{-nu}=-2\frac{d}{du}\frac{1}{1-e^{-u}}+\frac{1}{1-e^{-u}}=2\frac{e^{-u}}{(1-e^{-u})^{2}}+\frac{1}{1-e^{-u}}=\frac{1+e^{-u}}{(1-e^{-u})^{2}}\;,

and hence we get
[
2
]

\displaystyle W_{B}(u)=-e^{-\frac{1}{2}u}\,\frac{1+e^{-u}}{(1-e^{-u})^{2}}\;.

(A.5)

Next, we consider the spectral density (
3.15
).

\rho_{F}(\nu)
has simple poles located at

\displaystyle\nu_{n}=in\;\;\;,\;\;\;n\in\mathbb{Z}\;.

(A.6)

Expanding
\nu
around
\nu_{n}
, i.e.
\nu=\nu_{n}+\delta\nu
,
we infer that in the vicinity of the simple pole
\nu_{n}
,

\displaystyle\rho_{F}(\nu)=2\frac{\nu_{n}}{\pi\,\delta\nu}\>.

(A.7)

We deform the integration contour in (
3.17
) to run along the two sides of the positive imaginary axis. We therefore restrict our attention to the poles
\nu_{n}

with
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

The resulting contour integral in (
3.17
) is again evaluated by summing over residues. Each simple pole contributes

\displaystyle 4\pi i\,e^{i\lambda_{n}u}\;\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\pi}=-4n\,e^{-nu}\;\;\;,\;\;\;n\in\mathbb{N}\;.

(A.8)

Summing over all the residues gives (here we take
{\rm Re}\,u>0
)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}4n\,e^{-nu}=-4\frac{d}{du}\frac{1}{1-e^{-u}}=4\frac{e^{-u}}{(1-e^{-u})^{2}}\;,

(A.9)

and hence we get
[
2
]

\displaystyle W_{F}(u)=-4\,\frac{e^{-u}}{(1-e^{-u})^{2}}\;.

(A.10)

Appendix B
Regularising the one-loop effective action

The one-loop effective action
\Gamma_{\rm 1-loop}
for a field can be expressed in terms of the heat kernel
K(s)
for the field,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm 1-loop}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\,\frac{ds}{s}\,K(s)\;.

(B.1)

This is a formal expression: it suffers from divergences, and hence needs to be regularised. It possesses two types of divergences: an infrared (IR) divergence associated with
s\rightarrow+\infty
, and an ultraviolet (UV) divergence associated with
s\rightarrow 0
. A standard regularization
[
39
]
of the UV divergence consists in introducing a regulator
\epsilon>0

and replacing (
B.1
) by

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\epsilon}_{\rm 1-loop}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\epsilon}^{+\infty}\,\frac{ds}{s}\,K(s)\;,

(B.2)

which then still requires the introduction of a further regulator to deal with the divergence associated with
s\rightarrow+\infty
.
Rather then introducing the regulator
\epsilon
as in
(
B.2
), we will follow
[
1
,
2
]
and introduce it in the form

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\epsilon}_{\rm 1-loop}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\,\frac{ds}{s}\,e^{-\epsilon^{2}/4s}\,K(s)\;.

(B.3)

This will then be further supplemented with an infrared regulator.

Consider the expression

\displaystyle K(s)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}d\nu\,\rho(\nu)\,e^{-(\lambda^{2}+\nu^{2})s}\,,

(B.4)

with
\rho
an even function,
\rho(-\nu)=\rho(\nu)
, and
\lambda\geq 0
. Comparing with (
3.14
) and (
3.16
), we have dropped the normalisation factor
\pm V/(2\pi a^{2})
and we have replaced
\bar{s}
and
\bar{\epsilon}
by
s
and
\epsilon
for notational simplicity. These factors can be easily reinstated in the final expression
(
B.17
) given below.

Since the integrand in
(
B.4
) is an even function, we first write it as

\displaystyle K(s)=\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\nu\,\rho(\nu)\,e^{-(\lambda^{2}+\nu^{2})s}

(B.5)

and proceed to rewrite it as follows
[
2
]
.
Using the Gaussian integral

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\,e^{-\frac{u^{2}}{4s}+i\nu u}=\sqrt{4\pi s}\,e^{-\nu^{2}s}\;\;\;,\;\;\;s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\;,

(B.6)

we write (
B.5
) as

\displaystyle K(s)=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{\pi\,s}}\,\,e^{-\lambda^{2}s}\,\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\nu\,\left[\rho(\nu)\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\,e^{-\frac{u^{2}}{4s}+i\nu u}\right]\;.

(B.7)

Next, assuming that we can interchange the two integrations, we obtain

\displaystyle K(s)=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{\pi\,s}}\,e^{-\lambda^{2}s}\,\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\,e^{-\frac{u^{2}}{4s}}\,W(u)\,,

(B.8)

where
W
denotes the Fourier integral of
\rho
,

\displaystyle W(u)\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\nu\,\rho(\nu)\,e^{i\nu u}\;.

(B.9)

Since
\rho(-\nu)=\rho(\nu)
, we formally have
W(-u)=W(u)
.
Then, inserting (
B.8
) into (
B.3
) and interchanging the
integrations over
s
and
u
we obtain

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\epsilon}_{\rm 1-loop}=-\frac{1}{8\sqrt{\pi}}\,\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\,W(u)\,\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\frac{ds}{s^{3/2}}\,e^{-\frac{(\epsilon^{2}+u^{2})}{4s}-\lambda^{2}s}\right]\;.

(B.10)

The integral over
s
is proportional to the modified Bessel function of the second kind of degree
1/2

[
40
]
,

\displaystyle K_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}z\right)^{n}\,\int_{0}^{+\infty}\,\frac{ds}{s^{n+1}}\,e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{4s}-s}\;\;\;,\;\;\;|{\rm arg}\,z|<\frac{\pi}{4}\;,

\displaystyle K_{1/2}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\,\frac{e^{-z}}{\sqrt{z}}\;.

(B.11)

When
\lambda>0
, we obtain, after performing the rescaling
\lambda^{2}s\rightarrow s
in (
B.10
),

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\epsilon}_{\rm 1-loop}=-\frac{1}{4}\,\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{du}{\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}+u^{2}}}\,W(u)\,e^{-\lambda\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}+u^{2}}}\;.

(B.12)

When
\lambda=0
, we define
\Gamma^{\epsilon}_{\rm 1-loop}
by setting
\lambda=0
in (
B.12
).

This expresses the one-loop effective action in terms of the Fourier integral
W
of the spectral density
\rho
.
However, some of the assumptions that we made in order to arrive at (
B.12
) are invalid.
As a consequence, expression (
B.12
) is ill-defined:
since the spectral density
\rho(\nu)
in (
3.12
) and in (
3.15
)
does not decay to zero as
\nu\rightarrow\pm\infty
, the Fourier integral (
B.9
) is divergent. To deal with this divergence, we proceed as follows
[
1
,
2
]
.
Since, in the examples that we study,
\rho(\nu)
only has simple poles located on the imaginary axis of the complex plane
\nu
and none
on the real axis, we deform the integration contour in (
B.9
) to run along the two sides of the positive imaginary axis.
The resulting contour integral is then evaluated by summing over residues.
The expression that one obtains for
W(u)
in this manner has a pole at
u=0
, cf. (
3.20
).
To deal with this pole, we
change the integration contour in (
B.12
) to run over
\mathbb{R}\pm i\delta
, where

\delta>0
is taken to be small,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\epsilon}_{\rm 1-loop}=-\frac{1}{8}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}+i\delta}+\int_{\mathbb{R}-i\delta}\right)\frac{du}{\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}+u^{2}}}\,W(u)\,e^{-\lambda\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}+u^{2}}}\;.

(B.13)

Note that there are two branch cuts in the complex plane
u
, due to the presence in (
B.13
) of the factor
\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}+u^{2}}
. Following
[
2
]
,
we orient the two branch cuts as follows. One of them starts at
i\epsilon
and runs to
+i\infty

along the positive imaginary axis of the complex plane
u
, while the other starts at
-i\epsilon
and runs
to
-i\infty
along the negative imaginary axis of the complex plane
u
, as shown in Figure 5.1a of
[
2
]
.
Therefore, the regulator
\delta
has to be taken to be smaller than
\epsilon
, to ensure that the integration contour does not cross the branch cuts. Since the integrand in (
B.13
) is invariant under
u\rightarrow-u
due to
W(-u)=W(u)
, the pole at
u=0
does not contribute, i.e. the two integration contours are equivalent, and hence
we write

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\epsilon}_{\rm 1-loop}=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}+i\delta}\frac{du}{\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}+u^{2}}}\,W(u)\,e^{-\lambda\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}+u^{2}}}\;.

(B.14)

This expression still requires a regularization in the infrared, which corresponds to the behaviour at
|u|\rightarrow+\infty
.
Following
[
2
]
, we therefore insert an infrared regulator
\kappa>0
into (
B.13
), to obtain the regularised
one-loop partition function

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\rm reg}_{\rm 1-loop}=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}+i\delta}\frac{du}{\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}+u^{2}}}\,W(u)\,e^{-\lambda\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}+u^{2}}}\,e^{-\kappa|u|}\;.

(B.15)

Introducing the notation

\displaystyle H_{\lambda r}(u)\equiv-W(u)\,e^{-\lambda ru}\;\;\;,\;\;\;r=\frac{\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}+u^{2}}}{u}\;,

(B.16)

we express
\Gamma^{\rm reg}_{\rm 1-loop}
as

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\rm reg}_{\rm 1-loop}=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}+i\delta}\frac{du}{ru}\,H_{\lambda r}(u)\,e^{-\kappa|u|}\;.

(B.17)

Appendix C
Evaluating the regularised one-loop effective action

Here we evaluate the regularised one-loop effective action (
4.3
) following

[
1
,
2
]
.
We write it in the form (
B.17
),
where we reinstate the normalisation factor
V/(2\pi a^{2})
and
replace
\epsilon
by
\bar{\epsilon}=\epsilon/a
,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\rm reg}_{\rm 1-loop}({\bar{\epsilon}})=\frac{V}{8\pi a^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}+i\delta}\frac{du}{ru}\,{\tilde{H}}(r,u)\,e^{-\kappa|u|}\;,

(C.1)

where

\displaystyle{\tilde{H}}(r,u)=-W_{B}(u)\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}ru}+\frac{1}{2}W_{F}(u)=\frac{1+e^{-u}}{(1-e^{-u})^{2}}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}u}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}ru}-2\frac{e^{-u}}{(1-e^{-u})^{2}}\>.

(C.2)

We define
H(u)
by
setting
\epsilon=0
in (
C.2
),

\displaystyle H(u)=-\frac{e^{-u}}{1-e^{-u}}\>,

(C.3)

which we expand in a Laurent series around
u=0
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}H(u)=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}b_{n}\,u^{n-2}\;.

(C.4)

We then
split
H(u)
into
[
2
]

\displaystyle H(u)=H^{\rm UV}(u)+H^{\rm IR}(u)\;,

(C.5)

where

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}H^{\rm UV}(u)=\sum_{n=0}^{2}b_{n}\,u^{n-2}=\frac{b_{0}}{u^{2}}+\frac{b_{1}}{u}+b_{2}

(C.6)

with

\displaystyle b_{0}=0\;\;\;,\;\;\;b_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}\;\;\;,\;\;\;b_{2}=\frac{1}{4}\;.

(C.7)

Similarly, by expanding
{\tilde{H}}(r,u)
in a Laurent series around
u=0
keeping
r
fixed,
we define

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\tilde{H}}^{\rm UV}(r,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{2}a_{n}(r)\,u^{n-2}=\frac{a_{0}(r)}{u^{2}}+\frac{a_{1}(r)}{u}+a_{2}(r)

(C.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{u^{2}}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\,r\,u+\frac{1}{8}\left(1+r^{2}\right)u^{2}\right)

and split
{\tilde{H}}(r,u)
into

\displaystyle{\tilde{H}}(r,u)={\tilde{H}}^{\rm UV}(r,u)+{\tilde{H}}^{\rm IR}(r,u)\;.

(C.9)

Using this, we split
\Gamma^{\rm reg}_{\rm 1-loop}

into a UV part and an IR part
[
2
]
,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\rm reg}_{\rm 1-loop}=\Gamma^{\rm reg,UV}_{\rm 1-loop}+\Gamma^{\rm reg,IR}_{\rm 1-loop}\;,

(C.10)

where

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\rm reg,UV}_{\rm 1-loop}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{V}{8\pi a^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}+i\delta}\frac{du}{ru}\,{\tilde{H}}^{\rm UV}(r,u)\,e^{-\kappa|u|}\;,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\rm reg,IR}_{\rm 1-loop}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{V}{8\pi a^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}+i\delta}\frac{du}{ru}\,{\tilde{H}}^{\rm IR}(r,u)\,e^{-\kappa|u|}\;.

(C.11)

The UV part refers to the part of the one-loop effective action that would diverge when switching off the UV regulator
\epsilon
. Note that the UV part may also be divergent in the IR.
The IR part refers to the part of the one-loop effective action that would diverge
when switching off the IR regulator
\kappa
.
Since the IR part is finite in the UV, we may remove the
UV regulators
\delta
and
\epsilon

in the expression for
\Gamma^{\rm reg,IR}_{\rm 1-loop}
and obtain

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\rm reg,IR}_{\rm 1-loop}=\frac{V}{4\pi a^{2}}\,\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{du}{u}\,H^{\rm IR}(u)\,e^{-\kappa u}\;.

(C.12)

The latter can be evaluated in terms of the character zeta function
[
1
,
2
]

\displaystyle\zeta(z)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{du}{u}\,u^{z}\,H(u)\;,

(C.13)

to give (see eeq. (C.15) in
[
1
]
)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\rm reg,IR}_{\rm 1-loop}=\frac{V}{2\pi a^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\zeta^{\prime}(0)+b_{2}\log\kappa\right)\;.

(C.14)

Expanding (
C.3
) in powers of
e^{-u}
, with
u>0
,

\displaystyle H(u)=-\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}e^{-(n+1)u}\;,

(C.15)

inserting this into (
C.13
) and evaluating the integral (
C.13
) for
{\rm Re}\,z>0
gives

\displaystyle\zeta(z)=-\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{(n+1)^{z}}=-\zeta_{H}(z,1)\;,

(C.16)

where
\zeta_{H}
denotes the Hurwitz zeta function
\zeta_{H}(z,\Delta)
with
\Delta=1
.
Using

\displaystyle\zeta^{\prime}_{H}(0,\Delta)=\log\Gamma(\Delta)-\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi)\;,

(C.17)

we obtain

\displaystyle\zeta^{\prime}(0)=-\zeta^{\prime}_{H}(0,1)=\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi)\;.

(C.18)

Next we evaluate
\Gamma^{\rm reg,UV}_{\rm 1-loop}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}+i\delta}\,\frac{du}{2ru}{\tilde{H}}^{\rm UV}(r,u)\,e^{-\kappa\,|u|}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}+i\delta}\,du\left[-\frac{1}{2u^{2}}+\frac{1}{8}\left(\frac{1}{r\,u}+\,\frac{r^{2}}{r\,u}\right)\right]\,e^{-\kappa\,|u|}\;.

(C.19)

By closing the contour of integration in the upper half plane, the integral over the first term vanishes, and we are left with

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16}\int_{\mathbb{R}+i\delta}\,du\left(\frac{1}{r\,u}+\frac{r^{2}}{r\,u}\right)\,e^{-\kappa\,|u|}\;,

(C.20)

which we write as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16}\int_{\mathbb{R}+i\delta}\,du\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}+\frac{1}{u^{2}\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}\right)\,e^{-\kappa\,{\bar{\epsilon}}\,|u|}\;.

(C.21)

The second integral in (
C.21
) does not require the IR regulator
\kappa
, and we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16}\int_{\mathbb{R}+i\delta}\,du\,\frac{1}{u^{2}\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}=-\frac{1}{8}\;.

(C.22)

The first integral in (
C.21
) requires the IR regulator
\kappa
.
First we note that we may remove the regulator
\delta
, in which case this integral becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{+\infty}du\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}\,e^{-\kappa\,{\bar{\epsilon}}\,u}\;.

(C.23)

In the limit
\kappa\rightarrow 0^{+}
, this evaluates to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\,du\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}\,e^{-\kappa\,{\bar{\epsilon}}\,u}=-\frac{1}{4}\log\left(\frac{e^{\gamma}\kappa\,{\bar{\epsilon}}}{2}\right)\;,

(C.24)

where
\gamma
denotes the Euler-Mascheroni constant.

Collecting all the terms, we obtain for the regularised one-loop effective action (
4.3
),

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\rm reg}_{\rm 1-loop}(\bar{\epsilon})=-\log Z^{\rm reg}_{\rm 1-loop}(\bar{\epsilon})=\frac{V}{2\pi a^{2}}\left[-b_{2}\log\left(\frac{e^{\gamma}\,\bar{\epsilon}}{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\zeta^{\prime}_{H}(0,1)-\frac{1}{8}\right]\;,\;\bar{\epsilon}=\frac{\epsilon}{a}\;,\;b_{2}=\frac{1}{4}\;.

Note that the IR regulator
\kappa
has canceled out in the log-term
[
2
]
.
Similar calculations were done in
[
3
]
.

Appendix D
The action of
H,R,\mathbb{S}
on
|t\rangle

To verify the relations (
5.25
), we express
H,\mathbb{S},R
as

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2a}\left(2R-L_{+}-L_{-}\right)\;,

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(L_{+}+L_{-}\right)\;,

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathbb{S}+a\,H\;.

(D.1)

To simplify notation, let us denote
\omega_{\epsilon}(t)
by
\omega
in the following.
Then,

\displaystyle H\,e^{-\omega L_{+}}|0\rangle=\frac{1}{2a}\left(2R\,e^{-\omega L_{+}}+\frac{d}{d\omega}e^{-\omega L_{+}}-[L_{-},e^{-\omega L_{+}}]\right)|0\rangle\;.

(D.2)

Now we use

\displaystyle[L_{-},e^{-\omega L_{+}}]=e^{-\omega L_{+}}\left(-2\omega R+\omega^{2}L_{+}\right)\;,

(D.3)

to obtain, with
R|0\rangle=|0\rangle
,

\displaystyle H\,e^{-\omega L_{+}}|0\rangle=\frac{1}{2a}\left(2R\,e^{-\omega L_{+}}+\frac{d}{d\omega}e^{-\omega L_{+}}-e^{-\omega L_{+}}\left(-2\omega+\omega^{2}L_{+}\right)\right)|0\rangle\;.

(D.4)

Next, using

\displaystyle R\,e^{-\omega L_{+}}|0\rangle=[R,e^{-\omega L_{+}}]|0\rangle+e^{-\omega L_{+}}|0\rangle\;,

(D.5)

and

\displaystyle[R,e^{-\omega L_{+}}]|0\rangle=-\omega L_{+}\,e^{-\omega L_{+}}|0\rangle=\omega\frac{d}{d\omega}e^{-\omega L_{+}}|0\rangle\;,

(D.6)

we get

\displaystyle R\,e^{-\omega L_{+}}|0\rangle=\left(1+\omega\frac{d}{d\omega}\right)e^{-\omega L_{+}}|0\rangle\;.

(D.7)

It follows that

\displaystyle H\,e^{-\omega L_{+}}|0\rangle=\frac{1}{2a}\left(2(1+\omega)e^{-\omega L_{+}}|0\rangle+(1+\omega)^{2}\frac{d}{d\omega}e^{-\omega L_{+}}|0\rangle\right)\;.

(D.8)

Hence, using (
5.27
) we obtain

\displaystyle H\,|t\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2a}\left(2(1+\omega)|t\rangle+N\,(1+\omega)^{2}\frac{d}{d\omega}\left(\frac{1}{N}\,|t\rangle\right)\right)

(D.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2a}\left(2(1+\omega)|t\rangle+(1+\omega)^{2}\frac{d}{d\omega}|t\rangle\right)+(1+\omega)^{2}N\frac{d}{d\omega}\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)|t\rangle\;,

and using

\displaystyle N\frac{d}{d\omega}\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)=-\frac{2}{\omega+1}\;,

(D.10)

we get

\displaystyle H\,|t\rangle=\frac{1}{2a}\,(1+\omega)^{2}\frac{d}{d\omega}|t\rangle\;.

(D.11)

Now we use the explicit form of
\omega
given in (
5.24
) to infer

\displaystyle\omega+1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2a}{a-i\hat{t}}\;,

\displaystyle\frac{d\omega}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2i\,a}{(a-i\hat{t})^{2}}\;,

(D.12)

and hence

\displaystyle H\,|t\rangle=-i\frac{d}{dt}|t\rangle\;.

(D.13)

Similarly, we express
\mathbb{S}\,|t\rangle
as

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}\,|t\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega-1}{\omega+1}\,|t\rangle+\frac{(1+\omega)^{2}}{2}\,\frac{d}{d\omega}|t\rangle

(D.14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\left(\frac{\hat{t}}{a}-\frac{a^{2}-\hat{t}^{2}}{2a}\frac{d}{dt}\right)|t\rangle\;,

and finally,

\displaystyle R\,|t\rangle=\left(-\mathbb{S}+a\,H\right)\,|t\rangle=-i\left(\frac{\hat{t}}{a}+\frac{a^{2}+\hat{t}^{2}}{2a}\frac{d}{dt}\right)|t\rangle\>.

(D.15)

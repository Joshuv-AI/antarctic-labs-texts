A characteristic p p analogue of the André–Pink–Zannier conjecture
Yeuk Hay Joshua Lam, Ananth N. Shankar

Abstract
We investigate the analogue of the André–Pink–Zannier conjecture in characteristic p p . Precisely, we prove it for ordinary function field-valued points with big monodromy, in Shimura varieties of Hodge type. We also prove an algebraic characteristic p p analogue of Hecke-equidistribution (as formulated by Mazur) for Shimura varieties of Hodge type. We prove our main results by a global and local analysis of prime-to- p p Hecke correspondences, and by showing that Weyl special points are abundant in positive characterstic.

Introduction

The André–Pink–Zannier conjecture is an important milestone in the theory of unlikely intersections, and is a special case of the more general Zilber–Pink conjecture. Roughly, it posits that, on a characteristic zero Shimura variety, every irreducible component of the Zariski-closure of a subset of a Hecke orbit is
weakly special

Theorem 1.3

Let

The characteristic zero analogue of Theorem
1.2
(i.e. the André–Pink–Zannier conjecture in the setting of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}

[

x\in S_{K}(G,X)(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
has big monodromy; given a Hecke correspondence
\tau

S_{K}(G,X)
, the set
\tau(x)
breaks up into a bounded number of Galois orbits (where the bound is independent of
\tau
). This implies that the Zariski closure of an infinite subset of

must contain a positive proportion of
\tau_{i}(x)
for an infinite sequence of

This argument does not go through in positive characteristic, as there is no proven analogue of equidistribution of Hecke correspondences. In fact, the characteristic zero statement is in terms of the hyperbolic metric on
S_{K}(G,X)
, which, of course, does not have a positive characteristic analogue. Nevertheless, Mazur has formulated the following algebraic notion of equidistribution:

Definition 1.4

(Mazur)
.

Let
Y\subset S_{K}(G,X)
be a subvariety of positive dimension. The Hecke orbit of
Y
is said to equidistribute if the minimal degree of a hypersurface containing
\tau_{i}(Y)
tends to infinity for any sequence of prime-to-
p
Hecke correspondences
\tau_{i}
that satisfy
\deg\tau_{i}\rightarrow\infty
.

Given this definition, it seems reasonable to posit the following:

Conjecture 1.5

(Algebraic equidistrbution of Hecke orbits)
.

Let
Y\subset S_{K}(G,X)
be a positive-dimensional subvariety that intersects the ordinary locus. Then the Hecke orbit of
Y
equidistributes.

Note that the ordinary assumption is necessary, since otherwise we may simply choose
H
to be the non-ordinary locus, which is stable under all Hecke correspondences. When
Y
is contained in a lower-dimensional Newton stratum, the formulation of
1.5
should be in terms of the smallest subvariety of
S_{K}(G,X)
that is a union of central leaves and contains
Y
. Theorem
1.3
now implies the following theorem.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
C\subset S_{K}(G,X)
be a generically ordinary curve defined over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
having big monodromy. Then the Hecke orbit of
C
equidistributes.

Indeed, suppose that
C
is defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. We may quickly reduce to the case of hypersurfaces defined over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
by standard specialization arguments. Now let
H_{i}/\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
be the hypersurface with minimal degree containing
\tau_{i}(C)
. By intersecting
H_{i}
with its Galois conjugates, we may further reduce to the case that the
H_{i}
are all defined over a fixed finite field, independent of
i
. If the degrees of
H_{i}
were bounded for some subsequence
i
, we may pass to a further subsequence and deduce that the sequence of hypersurfaces
H_{i}
is constant, as there are only finitely many subvarieties having bounded degree defined over a fixed finite field. The conclusion now follows from Theorem
1.3
.

In future work, we plan to explore Mazur’s notion of algebraic equidistribution beyond the cases discussed above.

1.1
An application: isogenous Jacobians

Recall the folklore question posed by Katz–Oort, which asks whether there exist abelian varieties
A
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
which are not isogenous to the Jacobian of any curve. More generally (following Poonen), given any subvariety
D\subset\mathcal{A}_{g}
and a field
L
, one can ask for the existence of a point
x\in\mathcal{A}_{g}(L)
that is not isogenous to any
y\in D(\overline{L})
. The existence of such a point (with
L=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
) was proved by Chai-Oort and Tsimerman in
[
CO12
]
and
[
Tsi12
]
, and also independently by
[
MZ20
]
and
[
ST25
]
. Over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, this expectation depends heavily on the dimension of
D
(see
[
ST18
]
). However, the main theorem of
[
ST25
]
proves the existence of such a function field-valued point. An immediate corollary of our main theorem is the following finiteness result:

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
D\subsetneq S_{K}(G,X)
be any subvariety defined over
F=\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)}
. Let
x\in\Sh(F)
be any ordinary point with big monodromy. Then, there are only finitely many points in the prime-to-
p
Hecke orbit of
x
which lie in
D
. In particular, let
A/F
be any
g
-dimensional abelian variety (with

) having big monodromy. Then there are only finitely many Jacobians in the prime-to-
p
isogeny class of
A
.

One could ask whether the strenghtening of this result, in the style of Coleman–Oort, holds true: i.e. whether it is possible to find a Jacobian of dimension
g
which is not isogenous to any other Jacobian (we thank Olivier de Gaay Fortman for asking us this question); this is a more restrictive version of the question of Katz–Oort. See
[
dGF24
]
for results in characteristic-zero which go significantly beyond this specific question.
1.7
shows that
every
ordinary
g
-dimensional Jacobian over
F
, having big monodromy, will be isogenous to at most finitely many other Jacobians.

Outline of proof of Theorem
1.3

Let

be the Zariski closure of the

proceeds in the following steps.

1.

We first reduce to the case that

2.

We then establish degree bounds on Hecke translates of

is fixed by a Hecke correspondence,
\tau
. This is

.

3.

We then prove that

has infinitely many

(see

]
), including points that are fixed by

. Our result gives a characteristic

We now use arguments of Chai, van Hoften, and D’Addezio–van Hoften (which uses the parabolicity conjecture proved by D’Addezio) to immediately conclude that

1.3
Prior and related work.

The first version of the André–Pink–Zannier conjecture was formulated by André
[
And89
, §X 4.5, p. 216 (Problem 3)]
, in the setting where an algebraic curve meets a Hecke orbit in a Zariski dense set of points. Subsequently, various versions of the conjecture were formulated and studied independently by Pink
[
Pin05
]
, Yafaev
[
Yaf00
]
, and Zannier
[
Pin05
]
. Using the Pila–Zannier strategy (and the Pila–Wilkie theorem) special cases were obtained by Orr
[
Orr15
]
and Richard–Yafaev
[
RY24
]
, and as mentioned above, it was very recently proven by Richard–Yafaev
[
RY25
]
for a vast class of Shimura varieties, namely those of abelian type. For more on the history of this problem, we refer the reader to
[
RY24
, §1.2]
.

1.4
Organization of the paper

In Section 2, we define and recall various notions and reduce to the case of maximal monodromy. In Section 3, we show that
V
must be stable by a Hecke correspondence. In Section 4, we prove that
V
contains Weyl special points, that
V
is Tate-linear, and thereby conclude Theorem

. In Section 5, we then prove Theorem

.

is simply the ratio of their volumes. Similarly, the index of
K^{\prime}
in
hKh^{-1}
is again the ratio of their volumes. The volume of
K
equals that of
hKh^{-1}
as the measure is a Haar measure, and the result holds.
∎

The following is now well defined:

Definition 2.7
.

The degree of a Hecke correspondence
\tau_{h}
is defined to be the degree of the maps
\pi_{1},\pi_{h}
.

Let
h\in G(\mathbb{A}^{f,p})
. As
\tau_{h}
=
\tau_{hk}
for
k\in K
, we may assume that
h
is semisimple. Not all elements
h
induce “non-trivial” correspondences. For instance, if
h\in K
, then
\tau_{h}
is just the identity. Similarly, if
K\subset K^{\prime\prime}
is a normal subgroup where
K^{\prime\prime}
is also a compact open subgroup and if
h\in K^{\prime\prime}
, then
\tau_{h}
will be single-valued and will be a Deck transformation for the Galois étale cover
S_{K}(G,X)\rightarrow S_{K^{\prime\prime}}(G,X)
, and will therefore be a periodic function. In order to ensure that the Hecke correspondences we work with are not trivial in this sense, we make the following definition.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
h_{\ell}\in G(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
be a semisimple element. We say that
\tau_{h_{\ell}}
is a non-trivial Hecke correspondence if
h^{\ad}_{\ell}\in T(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\setminus K_{T}
, where
h^{\ad}_{\ell}
is the image of
h
in
G^{\ad}
,
T\subset G^{\ad}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
is a maximal torus containing
h^{\ad}_{\ell}
, and
K_{T}
is the maximal compact subgroup of
T
.

Let
h=(h_{\ell})_{\ell}\in G(\mathbb{A}^{f,p})
. We say that
\tau_{h}
is a non-trivial Hecke correspondence if
\tau_{h_{\ell}}
is non-trivial for at least one prime
\ell
.

For the rest of this paper, we will work exclusively with non-trivial Hecke correspondences. Note that for a non-trivial Hecke correspondence
\tau_{h}
, the degree of
\tau_{h^{n}}
goes to infinity with
n
.

2.2.1
Connected components

We work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
. We remark that everything in this paragraph is well known to the experts. We introduce the following notation.

Definition 2.9
.

For
K\subset G(\mathbb{A}^{f})
, set
K^{\der}:=K\cap G^{\der}(\mathbb{A}^{f})
,
K^{Z}:=K\cap Z(G)(\mathbb{A}^{f})
, and
K^{\SC}:=K\cap\textrm{Im}(G^{\SC}(\mathbb{A}^{f}))
.
For
K_{\ell}\subset G(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
, set
K^{\der}_{\ell}:=K_{\ell}\cap G^{\der}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
,
K^{Z}_{\ell}=K\cap Z(G)(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
and
K^{\SC}_{\ell}:=K_{\ell}\cap\textrm{Im}(G^{\SC}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}))
.

We start with the following proposition. We will not suppress level structure in our notation.

Proposition 2.10
.

Suppose
K=\prod K_{\ell}\subset G(\mathbb{A}^{f})
, hyperspecial at
p
, and for every
\ell
we either have
K_{\ell}=K_{\ell}^{\SC}\cdot K_{\ell}^{Z}
or
K_{\ell}
is hyperspecial. Then, for any
h\in G(\mathbb{A}^{f,p})
,
S_{K^{\prime}}(G,X)\xrightarrow{\pi}S_{K}(G,X)
restricted to a geometric connected component of
S_{K}(G,X)
is an irreducible finite étale cover. Here,
K^{\prime}=K\cap hKh^{-1}
.

Proof.

Let
S_{0}
be a geometric connected component of
S_{K}(G,X)
. The action of the geometric fundamental group of
S_{0}
on the fibers of
\pi
restricted to
S_{0}
is naturally identified with the action of
K^{\SC}
on the coset space
K/K^{\prime}
. Therefore, it suffices to prove that this action is transitive. The fact that
K=\prod K_{\ell}
implies that
hKh^{-1}=\prod_{\ell}hK_{\ell}h^{-1}
, and therefore that
K^{\prime}
also satisfies
K^{\prime}=\prod_{\ell}K^{\prime}_{\ell}
. So, it suffices to prove the claim prime-by-prime.

•

Suppose
K_{\ell}=K_{\ell}^{\SC}\cdot K_{\ell}^{Z}
. Let
\alpha\in K_{\ell}
. We have that
\alpha=\beta\cdot z
with
\beta\in K^{\SC}_{\ell}
and
z\in K^{Z}_{\ell}
. It suffices to prove the equality of cosets
\beta K^{\prime}=\alpha K^{\prime}
, i.e. that
z=\beta^{-1}\alpha\in K^{\prime}
, i.e. it suffices to prove that
z\in hKh^{-1}
. This follows from the fact that
z
is central.

•

Suppose that
K_{\ell}
is hyperspecial, and
K^{\SC}_{\ell}=K^{\der}_{\ell}
. We will abuse notation and let
G
also denote a reductive model over
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
. Given a maximally split maximal torus
T\subset G
defined over
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
, we have that the map
T(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})\rightarrow(G/G^{\der})(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
is surjective.
It follows that there are finitely many elements
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\in T(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
such that
K_{\ell}=\bigcup_{i}K^{\der}_{\ell}K^{Z}_{\ell}t_{i}
. Note that, as the level structure at
\ell
is hyperspecial, by the refined Cartan decomposition (see e.g.
[
Tit79
, p.51, §3.3.3]
) we may pick
h\in T(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
. Now, consider the coset
\alpha K^{\prime}
, where
\alpha\in K
. We may write
\alpha=\beta\cdot z\cdot t_{i}
. As in the first case, it suffices to show the equality of cosets
\beta K^{\prime}=\alpha K^{\prime}
, i.e. that
z\cdot t_{i}\in hKh^{-1}
. But this follows because
t_{i}
and
h
commute as they are both elements of
T
.

•

Suppose that
K_{\ell}
is hyperspecial, and
K^{\SC}_{\ell}\neq K^{\der}_{\ell}
is an almost hyperspecial (but not hyperspecial) subgroup of
G^{\der}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
. Then, we have that
G
must be an even orthogonal group. An argument identical to the one above would go through if a set of coset representatives of
K^{\SC}_{\ell}
in
K^{\der}_{\ell}
could be found in
T
, where
T
is as above. But this is true, as mentioned in Remark
2.3
.

∎

We have the following consequence.

Corollary 2.11
.

Consider the Shimura variety
S_{K}(G,X)
. At the cost of replacing
K
by a finite index subgroup, we have that the correspondence variety associated to any Hecke correspondence restricted to a geometrically connected component of
S_{K}(G,X)
is irreducible.

Further, let
Y\subset S_{K}(G,X)
be a geometrically irreducible subvavriety having maximal geometric prime-to-
p
monodromy, and let
h\in G(\mathbb{A}^{f,p})
. Then
\tau^{K}_{h}(Y)
is geometrically irreducible.

Proof.

For the first claim, it suffices to show that every
K
contains a compact open subgroup satisfying the hypotheses of the above proposition. At the cost of replacing
K
by a finite index subgroup, we may assume that
K=\prod_{\ell}K_{\ell}
. Further,
K_{\ell}
will be hyperspecial for
\ell\gg 1
. For the remaining
\ell
, we may replace
K_{\ell}
by
K_{\ell}^{\der}\cdot K_{\ell}^{Z}
.

The second claim follows using the first claim in conjunction with the argument in
[
ST25
, Proposition 5.2]
.
∎

We now turn to the question of connected components of
S_{K}(G,X)
. Let
T=G/G^{\der}
. The set of connected components of
S_{K}(G,X)
are in natural bijection with the points of the zero-dimensional Shimura variety associated with
T
(with level structure induced by
K
) (see
[
Mil
, Section 5]
), which we denote by
\Sh_{T}
. Let
h\in G(\mathbb{A}^{f,p})
be some element, and let
h_{T}
be its image in
T(\mathbb{A}^{f,p})
. The Hecke correspondence
\tau_{h}
induces a correspondence on the set of geometric connected components of
S_{K}(G,X)
. This correspondence is the same as the correspondence induced on
\Sh_{T}
by
\tau_{h_{T}}
. However, as
T
is abelian,
h_{T}
is a morphism. Therefore, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
S_{0}\subset S_{K}(G,X)
be a connected component over an algebraically closed field, and let
h\in G(\mathbb{A}^{f,p})
. Then, there is a unique connected component
S_{1}
of
S_{K}(G,X)
such that
\tau_{h}(S_{0})=S_{1}
. Further, if
h\in G^{\der}
, then
S_{1}=S_{0}
.

For the purposes of the main theorem, it suffices to work with Hecke correspondences that preserve connected components, and so we will assume implicitly that this is the case for the rest of the paper.

2.2.2
Reduction to the case of maximal monodromy

We continue to work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
. We will now reduce Theorem
1.3
to proving the following statement.

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
C\subset S_{K}(G,X)
denote a generically ordinary curve with maximal geometric monodromy. Let
\tau_{i}
denote a sequence of prime-to-
p
Hecke correspondences with increasing degrees, and let
C_{i}=\tau_{i}(C)
. Then the Zariski closure of the set
\bigcup_{i}C_{i}
is a union of connected components of
S_{K}(G,X)
.

The proposition below follows directly from the definitions.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
Y\subset S_{K}(G,X)
be a geometrically irreducible subvariety with big
\ell
-adic monodromy
K^{\prime}\subset K_{\ell}
. Let
Y^{\prime}\subset S_{K^{\prime}}(G,X)
be an irreducible component of the fiber product
Y\times_{S_{K}(G,X)}S_{K^{\prime}}(G,X)
.
Let
h\in G(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
, with
\tau^{K}_{h}
the Hecke correspondence that it induces on
S_{K}(G,X)
, and let
Z\subset\tau_{h}(Y)
. Then, there exist finitely many
h_{i}\in G(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
such that
Z^{\prime}=\bigsqcup_{i}\tau^{K^{\prime}}_{h_{i}}(Y^{\prime})
maps to
Z
.

We now deduce Theorem
1.3
from Theorem
2.13
.

Proof.

There exists a level
K^{\prime}\subset K
with associated covering map
\pi:S_{K^{\prime}}(G,X)\rightarrow S_{K}(G,X)
and a map
h:C\rightarrow S_{K^{\prime}}(G,X)
such that the composition
\pi\circ h
recovers the original
C
, and such that the image
\tilde{C}=h(C)
has maximal prime-to-
p
monodromy: indeed, for each
\ell
, we may simply take
K^{\prime}_{\ell}
to be the
\ell
-adic monodromy of
h
. Let
C_{i}\subset\tau_{i}(C)
be some subvariety (it will be a union of irreducible curves). Now each Hecke correspondence
\tau_{i}
on
S_{K}(G,X)
breaks up as a union over

of
\tau^{\prime}_{i,j}
on
S_{K^{\prime}}(G,X)
. By
2.14
, we have that each irreducible component of
C^{\prime}
is the image of some

. As the map
S_{K^{\prime}}(G,X)\rightarrow S_{K}(G,X)
is finite étale, to show that the

are Zariski dense, it suffices to show that the Zariski closure of
\{\tau^{\prime}_{i,j}(\tilde{C})\}
is a union of connected components of
S_{K}(G,X)^{\prime}
. But this follows from Theorem
2.13
for the Shimura variety
S_{K^{\prime}}(G,X)
.
∎

2.2.3
Galois-theoretic properties

We will end this section with a description of Galois-theoretic properties of the maps and correspondences in play; we keep the notation as in
§ 2.1
. To analyze the Galois action on points, we drop the assumption that we are working over an algebraically closed field. To that end, let
R
be a characteristic
p
field. Recall that we have fixed an element
h\in G(\mathbb{A}^{f,p})
, and that
K^{\prime}=K\cap hKh^{-1}
. Every point
x\in S_{K}(G,X)(R)
is equipped with a
K_{\ell}
-equivalence class of isomorphisms
t_{x}:\mathbb{W}_{\ell}\rightarrow T_{\ell}(\mathcal{A}_{x})
where
K_{\ell}
acts by pre-composition. Let
g_{1}=1,g_{2},\ldots,g_{n}
be a set of left coset representatives for
K^{\prime}_{\ell}
in
K_{\ell}
. Then, the points in the inverse image
\pi^{-1}(x)
are in bijection with the
K^{\prime}
-equivalence classes
t_{i}=t_{x}\circ g_{i}:\mathbb{W}_{\ell}\rightarrow T_{\ell}(\mathcal{A}_{x})
. The action of
\sigma\in\pi_{1,et}(R)
on
\pi^{-1}(x)
is by post-composing
t_{i}
with
\sigma
. The element
x_{i}\in\pi^{-1}(x)
corresponding to
t_{i}:\mathbb{W}_{\ell}\rightarrow T_{\ell}(\mathcal{A}_{x})
is defined over
R
if and only if
t_{i}^{-1}\circ\sigma\circ t_{i}\in K^{\prime}
for every
\sigma\in\pi_{1,et}(R)
. As
x\in S_{K}(G,X)(R)
, we already have
t_{i}^{-1}\circ\sigma\circ t_{i}\in K
. Therefore, the condition reduces to
t_{i}^{-1}\circ\sigma\circ t_{i}\in hKh^{-1}
, i.e.
h^{-1}t_{i}^{-1}\circ\sigma\circ t_{i}h\in K
. This leads to the following corollary.

Corollary 2.15
.

Let the notation be as above. The set
\tau_{h}(x)
has an
R
-rational point if and only if for each
\sigma\in\pi_{1,et}(R)
, there is an element
g\in K
such that
h^{-1}g^{-1}t^{-1}\sigma tgh\in K
.

Proof.

The forward implication follows directly from the above discussion. Indeed, if
y_{i}=\pi_{h}(x_{i})
is the fixed point, then we pick
g=g_{i}
.

For the reverse implication, suppose that
g
is in the coset
g_{i}K^{\prime}
. By the discussion above, it suffices to show that
t_{i}^{-1}\sigma t_{i}\in hKh^{-1}
, where
t_{i}=t\circ g_{i}
. We have that
k^{-1}t_{i}^{-1}\sigma t_{i}k\in hKh^{-1}
, where
g=g_{i}k
. The result now follows, as
k\in K^{\prime}\subset hKh^{-1}
.
∎

We now specialize to the case when
R={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. We introduce the following definition.

Definition 2.16
.

Given
x\in S_{K}(G,X)({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, we let
\Frob
denote Frobenius element in
\pi_{1,\et}(x)
. Note that
\Frob
acts on
T_{\ell}(\mathcal{A}_{x})
, and therefore the level structure gives an element
t^{-1}\circ\Frob\circ t
in
K_{\ell}
well-defined upto conjugation. We let
\phi_{x}
denote such an element.

We have the following key result.

Theorem 2.17
.

Let
h\in G(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
. Then there exists an open subset
U\subset K
stable under
K
-conjugation, such that if
x\in S_{K}(G,X)({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
satisfies
\phi_{x}\in U
, then
\tau_{h^{n}}(x)
contains an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-rational point for every
n
.

Proof.

Firstly, it suffices to work with
h
semisimple. Let
Z\subset G
denote a
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
-maximal torus of
G
containing
h
, and let
Z_{0}=Z(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\cap K_{\ell}
. Let
Z_{0}^{r}
denote the subset of regular elements – we note that
Z_{0}^{r}
is open in
Z_{0}
. We make the following claim:

Claim 2.18
.

The set
U=\{\alpha Z_{0}^{r}\alpha^{-1}:\alpha\in K_{\ell}\}
is an open subset of
K_{\ell}
.

Proof of Claim.

It suffices to prove that the set
\{\alpha Z_{0}^{r}\alpha^{-1}:\alpha\in K^{\prime}_{\ell}\}
is open where
K^{\prime}_{\ell}
is a suitable open subgroup of
K_{\ell}
. The claim essentially follows from the fact that the fibers of the map
G/Z\times Z^{r}\rightarrow G
are finite, but we give a sketch of the argument for completeness.
Let
f:G\times Z^{r}\rightarrow G
be the map
f(g,z)=gzg^{-1}
. It suffices to prove that
d_{f}|_{(1,z)}:\lie G\oplus T_{z}Z\rightarrow T_{z}G
is surjective at every point of the form
(1,z)
. The map
d_{f}|_{(1,z)}
restricted to
{T_{z}Z}
is evidently just the natural inclusion. Therefore, it suffices to show that
d_{f}(\textrm{Lie }G)\cap T_{z}Z=\{0\}
and that the kernel of
d_{f}
restricted to
\textrm{Lie}G
equals
\mathrm{Lie}Z
. Let
g
be the map obtained by composing
f
with translation by
z^{-1}
on the right. Proving the above statement for
d_{f}
reduces to proving that
d_{g}|_{\mathrm{Lie}G}:\textrm{Lie}G\rightarrow\textrm{Lie}G
satisfies
\ker d_{g}
is
\textrm{Lie}Z
and
\textrm{Im}d_{g}\cap\textrm{Lie}Z
is trivial. For
X\in\textrm{Lie}G
, we have that
d_{g}(X)=X-\textrm{Ad}_{z}(X)
. Write
X=X_{Z}+\sum_{r}X_{r}
, where
X_{Z}\in\textrm{Lie}Z
and each
X_{r}
lies in a root-space of
Z
with associated co-character
\lambda_{r}
. Then,
\textrm{Ad}_{z}(X)=X_{Z}+\sum_{r}\lambda_{r}(z)X_{r}
. As
z
is regular, we have that
\lambda_{r}(z)\neq 1
for every

, and the claim now follows.

∎

The set
U
does what is required, as we now demonstrate. Indeed, suppose
x
is such that
\phi_{x}=t^{-1}\Frob t\in U
, where
t
is a representative of the
K_{\ell}
-equivalence class associated to
x
. Therefore, there is an element
g\in K
such that
g^{-1}t^{-1}\Frob tg\in Z_{0}^{r}
. But
h
, and therefore every power of
h
, commutes with
Z_{0}
. The result now follows by applying
2.15
.

∎

3.1
Degrees of subvarieties

In this section, we setup notation regarding degrees of subvarieties, and collect together some well known results.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\omega
denote the determinant of the Hodge bundle on
\Sh
. We denote by
\Sh^{\BB}
the Baily–Borel compactification (often also referred to as a minimal compactification) of
\Sh
: this is the projective variety obtained by taking closure of
\Sh
under the embedding into projective space given
\omega

We refer the reader to
[
Per19
, §5.2]
for more details.

Proposition 3.5
.

For level subgroups
K^{\prime}\subset K
which are both hyperspecial at
p
, with corresponding map
\pi:\widetilde{\Sh}\rightarrow\Sh
, there is a unique finite map
\pi^{\BB}:\widetilde{\Sh}^{\BB}\rightarrow\Sh^{\BB}
extending
\pi
. Moreover,
{\pi^{BB}}^{*}\omega\simeq\omega
.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
K
be a level subgroup as in Assumption
2.1
, with associated Shimura variety
\Sh
. Let
X\subset\Sh
be an irreducible subvariety. Let
n
denote an integer such that
\omega^{\otimes n}
is very ample, and let
\Sh^{\BB}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}

denote a
projective embedding defined by
\omega^{\otimes n}
. We define
degree
of
X
as
\frac{1}{n^{\dim X}}\deg\bar{X}
, where
\bar{X}
is the closure of
X
in
\Sh^{BB}
, and the degree is with respect to the projective embedding in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
. Note that this is a positive rational number.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
K^{\prime}\subset K
be level subgroups corresponding to
\pi:\widetilde{\Sh}\rightarrow\Sh
. Let
X\subset\Sh
be a subvariety and
Y
an irreducible component of
\pi^{-1}(X)
. Then
\deg(Y)=\deg(\pi|_{Y})\deg(X)
.

Proof.

We pick an integer
n
such that
\omega^{\otimes n}
is very ample on both
\Sh
and
\widetilde{\Sh}
. Suppose that
\dim X=d
. We may choose
d
hyperplane sections
H_{1},\ldots,H_{d}
of
\omega^{\otimes n}
in
\Sh^{\BB}
such that
H_{1}\cap\ldots\cap H_{d}
intersects
\bar{X}
transversely and the intersection is supported away from the boundary
\bar{X}\setminus X
. With this setup, the degree of
X
is just
\frac{1}{n^{d}}\#X\cap H_{1}\cap\ldots\cap H_{d}
.

By Proposition
3.5
, the
H^{\prime}_{i}={\pi^{\BB}}^{-1}H_{i}
are hyperplane sections of
\omega^{\otimes n}
in
\widetilde{\Sh}^{\BB}
. Further, as the map
\pi
is finite étale and the intersection
\bar{X}\cap H_{1}\cap\ldots\cap H_{n}
is supported in
\Sh
, we have that the intersection
\bar{Y}\cap H^{\prime}_{1}\cap\ldots\cap H^{\prime}_{d}
is transverse and supported in
\widetilde{\Sh}
. Therefore, the degree of
Y
is just
\frac{1}{n^{d}}\#Y\cap H^{\prime}_{1}\cap\ldots\cap H^{\prime}_{d}
.

The proposition now follows from the fact that
Y\cap H^{\prime}_{1}\cap\ldots\cap H^{\prime}_{d}=\pi^{-1}(X\cap H_{1}\cap\ldots\cap H_{d})\cap Y
which has cardinality
\deg(\pi|_{Y})\cdot\#X\cap H_{1}\cap\ldots\cap H_{d}
, as
\pi|_{Y}
is finite étale.

∎

We require the following results detailing how degrees interact with Hecke correspondences.

Lemma 3.8
.

Suppose that
X\subset\Sh
is a subvariety with big monodromy. Then there is a constant
\kappa>0
, depending only on the index of the monodromy of
X
, such that the following holds. For
K^{\prime}\subset K
any finite index subgroup, let
\widetilde{\Sh}
be the associated Shimura variety and
\pi:\widetilde{\Sh}\rightarrow\Sh
the canonical map, and
\tilde{X}\subset\widetilde{\Sh}
any irreducible component of
\pi^{-1}X
. Then the degree of
\pi|_{\tilde{X}}
satisfies
\kappa\deg\pi\leq\deg\pi|_{\tilde{X}}\leq\deg\pi
.

Proof.

We have that
\pi
is a finite étale map. For brevity, we let
n=\deg\pi
. The second inequality is trivial.

For the first inequality, let
x\in X
be some geometric point, and let
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
be the fiber of
\pi
above
x
. There is a transitive action of
K
on
\{x_{1}\ldots x_{n}\}
with stabilizer (conjugates of)
K^{\prime}\subset K
. Suppose that
x_{1}\in\tilde{X}
. Then, we have that
\pi_{1}(X,x)\cdot x_{1}\subset K\cdot x_{1}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
is contained in
\tilde{X}
, and
\deg\pi|_{\tilde{X}}=\#\pi_{1}(X,x)\cdot x_{1}
. We may now choose
\kappa=[K:\pi_{1}(X,x)]^{-1}
, and the lemma follows.
∎

3.2

The following result must be well known, but we include a proof for completeness.

To prove

4.1
Weyl-special points

Let
x\in\Sh({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
be an ordinary point, which, via the Hodge embedding

\tilde{x}\in\Sh(W(\mathbb{F}_{q}))
As the canonical lift is a CM abelian variety, the point

is a special point, and is therefore induced by zero-dimensional Shimura variety
(T_{x},h)
. We therefore have a map of
\mathbb{Q}
-groups
T_{x}\rightarrow G
. Note that elements
\alpha\in T_{x}(\mathbb{Q})
induce (quasi)-isogenies

that respect
G

We first recall from
[

, Defn. 5.3]
the definition of a Weyl sub-torus of

. Note that for any field

with algebraic closure
\bar{k}
, and a reductive group
\mathscr{G}/k

k

on the character group
X^{*}(\mathscr{T}_{\bar{k}})
.

Definition 4.3
.

A maximal
\mathbb{Q}

on the character lattice of

We now define the notion of an ordinary Weyl special point:

Definition 4.4

(Weyl-special points)
.

We say that an ordinary point

is a Weyl special point if the torus

is a Weyl subtorus.

The main result of this subsection is to show that ordinary Weyl special points are abundant in
\Sh
. More specifically, we prove:

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
V\subset\Sh
be as above. Then there exist infinitely many Weyl special points

. Further, we may assume that
\phi_{x}\in U
where

is as in Proposition

We note that this recovers Chai–Oort’s result which produces Weyl special points on Shimura varieties using different methods (Chai–Oort’s argument uses Hilbert irreducibility – a variant of Deligne’s argument that produces special points on Shimura varieties). In
[
CO12
, Theorem 5.5]
, Chai–Oort prove that Weyl special points can be constructed to avoid finitely many sub-Shimura varieties of the ambient Shimura variety. Our method is also robust enough to recover
[
CO12
, Theorem 5.5]
. However, as we do not need this stronger result, we will be content with merely proving Theorem
4.5
.

The main property of Weyl tori which we will use to both prove Theorem
4.5
and to deduce the Tate-linearity of

is that the adjoint of Weyl sub-tori do not contain non-trivial
\mathbb{Q}
-subtori (for example, see
[
CO12
, Proof of (5.8)]

is
\mathbb{Q}
-simple).

We will need the following setup and preliminary propositions.
Let
T,T^{\prime}
be two split maximal tori inside a reductive group
G
, defined over an algebraically closed field. Then, there is an isomorphism between their Weyl groups
c:W(T)\rightarrow W(T^{\prime})
, induced by the fact that
T
and
T^{\prime}
are conjugate to each other. The isomorphism
c
is only well defined up to conjugation by an element of
W(T)
, as it is induced by an isomorphism
T\rightarrow T^{\prime}
which is well defined only up to an element of
W(T)
. Therefore, an element
w^{\prime}\in W(T^{\prime})
defines an element of
w\in W(T)
up to conjugation: we say that the elements
w\in W(T)
and
w^{\prime}\in W(T^{\prime})
are

to each other.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\ell
be a prime number, and
G
a split reductive group over
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
. Suppose that
T^{\prime}\subset G
is a split maximal torus defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
such that
G({\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
contains a full set of coset representatives of
W(T^{\prime})
. Then there is a maximal torus
T_{w}\subset G
defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
such that the Galois action of
\Gal(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{\ell}/{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
on the co-character lattice of
T_{w,\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{\ell}}
contains an element of
W(T_{w,\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{\ell}})
conjugate to
w\in W(T^{\prime})
.

Proof.

We abuse notation and let
w\in G({\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
denote a coset representative of
w\in W
. By Lang’s theorem, there exists
\alpha\in G(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{\ell})
that satisfies
\alpha^{-1}\sigma(\alpha)=w
. We define
T_{w}:=\alpha T^{\prime}\alpha^{-1}
: it is straightforward to check that it is a maximal torus of
G
defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
.

We now compute the action of
\sigma
on the co-character lattice of
T_{w}
. We may identify the co-character lattices
X_{*}(T^{\prime})
and
X_{*}(T_{w})
by conjugating by
\alpha
. Let
\mu:\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow T^{\prime}
be some co-character. Then,
\sigma(\alpha\mu\alpha^{-1})=\sigma(\alpha)\mu\sigma(\alpha^{-1})=\alpha\alpha^{-1}\sigma(\alpha)\mu\sigma(\alpha)^{-1}\alpha\alpha^{-1}=\alpha(w\cdot\mu)\alpha^{-1}
. The proposition follows.

∎

We now give a criterion that forces a torus to be a Weyl-torus.

Proposition 4.7

(Weyl-torus criterion)
.

Let
G
be a reductive group defined over
\mathbb{Z}[1/N]
and let
T\subset G
be a maximal torus defined over
\mathbb{Z}[1/N]
. Let
S
be the set of primes not dividing
N
at which
G
splits.

Suppose that for every
w
in the Weyl group of

, there is a prime

and a maximal torus
T_{w}\subset G\otimes\mathbb{F}_{\ell_{w}}
such that the Frobenius action on

is conjugate to
w
.

Suppose moreover that
T\bmod\ell_{w}
is conjugate over
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell_{w}}
to
T_{w}
. Then
T
is a Weyl-torus of
G
.

Proof.

Let
\rho:\Gal_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\GL(X_{*}(T_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}))
be the representation of the Galois group on the co-character lattice of
T_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
, say with image
F
. By our hypothesis,
F
intersects every conjugacy class of
W(T_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
. Therefore,

intersects every conjugacy class; here we view
W(T_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
as a subgroup of

Let

By

We are now ready to prove our main theorem. The main inputs are Tate-linearity, big monodromy, D’Addezio’s work on the parabolicity conjecture, and work of Chai (see also
[

]
,
[

]

Proof.

The subvariety

has maximal prime-to-

Theorem 5.1

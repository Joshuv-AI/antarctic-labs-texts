Lepton parity dark matter and naturally unstable domain walls
Ernest Ma, Partha Kumar Paul, Narendra Sahu

Abstract
We propose a simple and predictive setup that connects neutrino masses, dark matter (DM), and gravitational waves. A minimal lepton parity DM scenario is considered where the residual symmetry ( − 1 ) L (-1)^{L} from the type I seesaw acts as the dark parity D = ( − 1 ) L + 2 ​ j D=(-1)^{L+2j} , ensuring DM stability without imposing any new symmetry. A singlet Majorana fermion S S with even lepton parity serves as the DM candidate, interacting via a real scalar σ \sigma which is also even lepton parity. The scalar potential possesses an accidental 𝒵 2 \mathcal{Z}_{2} symmetry, whose spontaneous breaking gives rise to unstable domain walls (DW) in the presence of explicit 𝒵 2 \mathcal{Z}_{2} breaking terms allowed by the lepton parity. The subsequent DW annihilation generates a stochastic gravitational wave (GW) background potentially observable at different GW experiments.

\displaystyle V_{\rm bias}=\frac{\mu_{1}v_{\sigma}^{3}}{\sqrt{2}}+\frac{\mu_{2}v_{\sigma}v_{h}^{2}}{\sqrt{2}}.

(9)

i.e.
at a larger temperature, then the amplitude will be smaller.

Conclusions.
We have proposed a minimal and predictive framework connecting neutrino masses, dark matter (DM), and gravitational waves. The residual lepton parity
(-1)^{L}
of the type I seesaw is identified with the dark parity
D=(-1)^{L+2j}
, ensuring DM stability without introducing any additional symmetry. In this setup, a singlet Majorana fermion
S
acts as the DM candidate and interacts through a real scalar
\sigma
. The scalar potential exhibits an accidental
\mathcal{Z}_{2}
symmetry whose spontaneous breaking leads to the formation of domain walls. Explicit
\mathcal{Z}_{2}
breaking terms allowed by lepton parity create a pressure difference across the walls, making them annihilate, generating a stochastic GW background within the sensitivity of upcoming GW experiments. The DM mass arises solely from the Yukawa term
y_{S}SS\sigma
once
\sigma
acquires a vacuum expectation value. Since this operator softly breaks the accidental
\mathcal{Z}_{2}
, the coupling
y_{S}
must be extremely small. This small
y_{S}
naturally suppresses the DM mass, rendering it light. The same small coupling also makes the standard freeze-out mechanism ineffective, pointing to non-thermal production as the viable mechanism to obtain the observed relic density. An intriguing outcome of this framework is the direct correlation between the DM mass, the annihilation temperature of the DWs, and the peak frequency of the GW spectrum. A smaller DM mass corresponds to a smaller vev of
\sigma
, which delays wall domination time and lowers the annihilation temperature, thereby shifting the GW peak to lower frequencies. This establishes a testable link between particle physics and cosmology, where future GW observations could indirectly probe the light DM in this scenario.

The thermally averaged cross-section for a process,

is given by

\displaystyle\langle\sigma v\rangle_{ab\rightarrow cd}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{T}{32\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{n_{a}^{\rm eq}n_{b}^{\rm eq}}\int_{(m_{a}+m_{b})^{2}}^{\infty}\sigma_{ab\rightarrow cd}(s)\sqrt{s}K_{1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{s}}{T}\right)

(40)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\left(s-(ma+m_{b})^{2}\right)\left(s-(ma-m_{b})^{2}\right)}{4s},

where

are the equilibrium number densities of the
a
and
b
particles,
m_{a},m_{b}
are the mass of the respective particles.
s
is the center of mass energy, and
K_{1}
is the modified Bessel function of the first kind. We used
CalcHEP

Belyaev et al. 2013
for calculating the cross-sections of the relevant processes involved in DM relic calculations.

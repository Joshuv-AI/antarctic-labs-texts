The Bloch conjecture
Vladimir Guletskiĭ

Abstract
We prove the conjecture stated by Spencer Bloch in 1975 and saying that the Albanese kernel of a smooth projective surface is 0 0 , provided its second cohomology group is algebraic.



1
Introduction

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field, and let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
k
. The goal of this manuscript is to discuss the Chow group
CH_{0}(X)
of
0
-cycle modulo rational equivalence on
X
, which is an important invariant of
X
.

Let
A_{0}(X)
be the subgroup in
CH_{0}(X)
generated by
0
-cycles of degree
0
, and let
T(X)
be the kernel of the Albanese homomorphism from
A_{0}(X)
onto the group of closed points of the Albanese variety
{\rm{Alb}}_{X/k}
of the variety
X
.

If
X
is a smooth projective curve over
k
, then
T(X)=0
, and the group
A_{0}(X)
is isomorphic to the Jacobian variety of the curve
X
. When
k
is
{\mathbb{C}}
, this is the content of the classical theorem due to Abel and Jacobi proven in the first half of
19
th century.

When
X
is a smooth projective surface over
k
, the behaviour of rational equivalence of
0
-cycles on
X
may be very different from what could be expected based on the intuition taken from curves. In the turning paper
[
29
]
Mumford proved that when
k={\mathbb{C}}
and the geometric genus
p_{g}(X)>0
, then
T(X)
is huge, and
A_{0}(X)
cannot be parametrized by an abelian variety, in any reasonable sense.

On the other hand, based on some Hodge and
K
-theoretic intuition, Spencer Bloch has conjectured that
T(X)
is
0
for a smooth projective surface
X
over
{\mathbb{C}}
, provided
p_{g}(X)=0
, see
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
. Notice that, since
k={\mathbb{C}}
, due to Hodge theory and Lefschetz
(1,1)
-theorem, the condition
p_{g}=0
is equivalent to saying that the second transcendental cohomology group
H^{2}_{{\rm{tr}}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
vanishes.

The status of BC is as follows. If the Kodaira dimension of
X
is strictly smaller than
2
, it was proven in
[
8
]
by ad hoc methods. If
X
is of general type over
{\mathbb{C}}
, the condition
H^{2}_{{\rm{tr}}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})=0
implies that
X
is regular, i.e.
H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})=H^{3}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})=0
, and then BC simply asserts that any two points on
X
are rationally equivalent to each other. This is the hard case of BC, unknown for the most part, except for several cases: the surfaces studied by Barlow in
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
, the classical Godeaux surface is considered in
[
18
]
, and general Godeaux case was studied by Voisin in
[
36
]
and
[
37
]
, the Catanese surfaces and general Barlow surfaces solved by Voisin in
[
39
]
, and finite quotients of products of curves, proven by Kimura in
[
22
]
.

Thinking in
l
-adic terms, where
l
is a prime different from
{\rm{char}}(k)
, the condition
H^{2}_{{\rm{tr}}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})=0
should be strengthened by imposing the condition
H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})=H^{3}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})=0
, which is automatic in
0
characteristic, but, surprisingly, in positive characteristic there may be surfaces of general type with
H^{2}_{{\rm{tr}}}=0
and non-trivial
H^{1}
and
H^{3}
, see
[
24
]
. This extended condition can be restated by saying that the total cohomology group
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})
is algebraic.

The goal of this note is to prove the following

THEOREM A.
Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
, and let
X
be a smooth projective surface over
k
. If
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})
is algebraic, then
T(X)=0
.

The same is true in positive characteristic, when
X
has a model over the algebraic closure
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
of the finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}={\mathbb{Z}}/p
.

THEOREM B.
Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
,
p\neq 2
, and let
X
be a smooth projective surface over
k
. Assume that
X
has a model over
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. If
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})
is algebraic, then
T(X)=0
.

2
The conjecture

2.1
Classical formulation

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field, and let
X
be a variety over
k
. Consider the Chow group
CH_{0}(X)
of
0
-cycles modulo rational equivalence on
X
, and let
A_{0}(X)
be its subgroup generated by
0
-cycles of degree
0
on
X
.

Assume that
X
is smooth and projective, and let
{\rm{Alb}}_{X/k}
be the Albanese variety of the variety
X
, i.e. the dual to the connected component
{\rm{Pic}}^{0}_{X/k}
of the Picard scheme
{\rm{Pic}}_{X/k}
of
X
over
k
. Consider the Albanese homomorphism

{\rm{alb}}_{X}:A_{0}(X)\to{\rm{Alb}}_{X/k}(k)

associated to the variety
X
. Its kernel

T(X)=\ker({\rm{alb}}_{X})

is an important object of study in algebraic geometry.

The homomorphism
{\rm{alb}}_{X}
is an isomorphism on
m
-torsion subgroups, where
m
is coprime to
{\rm{char}}(k)
. This is Roitman’s theorem, see
[
32
]
and
[
28
]

Let
\Omega^{q}_{X/k}
be the sheaf of Kähler differentials of degree
q
on
X
, and let

h^{p,q}(X)=\dim_{k}(H^{p}(X,\Omega^{q}_{X/k}))\;.

be the
(p,q)
-Hodge number of the variety
X
. If
n
is the dimension of
X
, the number

p_{g}(X)=h^{0,n}(X)

is called the geometric genus of the variety
X
.

The original Bloch’s conjecture (BC) can be stated as follows, see page 408 in
[
6
]
and page 0.5 in
[
7
]
.

Conjecture 2.1.1
.

Let
X
be a connected smooth projective surface over
{\mathbb{C}}
. Then
p_{g}(X)=0
implies
T(X)=0
.

As we mentioned in Introduction, the status of BC is that it is proven for all surfaces with Kodaira dimension
<2
, and for a few surfaces of general type. Therefore, further below we only focus on BC for surfaces of general type. Special type will be systematically ignored.

We would like to generalize Conjecture
2.1.1
to fields of arbitrary characteristic. Let
k
be an arbitrary algebraically closed field, and fix a prime number
l
different from the characteristic of
k
. For any algebraic scheme
X
over
k
, let

H^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{l})=\lim_{\nu}H^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}/l^{\nu}{\mathbb{Z}})

be the
i
-th étale
l
-adic cohomology group of the scheme
X
with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{l}
, and let

H^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})=H^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{l})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{l}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}

be the corresponding vector space over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
. After a non-canonical choice of an isomorphism

{\mathbb{Z}}_{l}(1)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}_{l}\;,

the contravariant functor

H^{*}:X\mapsto H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})=\oplus_{i}H^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})

gives us a Weil cohomology theory over
k
, see
[
23
]
.

Assume
X
is equidimensional of dimension
n
over
k
, and let

CH^{i}(X)=CH_{n-i}(X)

be the Chow group of codimension
i
(respectively, dimension
n-i
) algebraic cycles modulo rational equivalence on
X
. Write also
CH^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
for the Chow group with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Q}}
, and similarly for
{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
.

When
X
is smooth over
k
, for any integer
0\leq i\leq n
, we have the cycle class homomorphism

{\rm{cl}}_{X}^{i}:CH^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})\to H^{2i}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})

from the Chow group with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
to
l
-adic cohomology, where the Tate twist is ignored (see
\lx@sectionsign 9
, Ch. VI in
[
27
]
or
[
SGA

⁤
4

1
2

]
, Exposé 4).

Let now
X
be a smooth projective surface over
k
, and let

H^{2}_{{\rm{alg}}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})={\rm im}({\rm{cl}}^{1}_{X})

be the image of the second cycle class homomorphism sending divisors to their cohomology classes on
X
, i.e. the algebraic part in
H^{2}(X)
, and let

H^{2}_{{\rm{tr}}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})=H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})/H^{2}_{{\rm{alg}}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})

be the quotient group of
H^{2}
by its algebraic part, i.e. the transcendental part of
H^{2}(X)
, see p. 1.22 in
[
7
]
. We will say that
H^{*}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})
is algebraic if

H^{2}_{{\rm{tr}}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})=0

and

H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})=H^{3}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})=0\;.

Notice that, if

{\rm{char}}(k)=0\;,

then

p_{g}(X)=0\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;H^{2}_{{\rm{tr}}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})=0

due to Hodge theory. Moreover, we have the following

Lemma 2.1.2
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field, let
X
be a smooth projective surface over
k
, and assume that
X
is of general type. If
p_{g}(X)=0
, then
H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})=H^{3}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})=0
.

Proof
.
In arbitrary characteristic,
\dim({\rm{Alb}}_{X/k})\leq h^{0,1}(X)
, see again page 240 in
[
25
]
, and if the surface
X
is minimal and of general type, then
p_{g}(X)=0
implies
h^{0,1}(X)=0
by Proposition 8.9 on page 264 in loc.cit, whence
{\rm{Alb}}_{X/k}=0
. It follows that
H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})=0
, and by the Poicaré duality,
H^{3}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})=0
.

However, if
{\rm{char}}(k)=p>0
the connection between the geometric genus and transcendental cohomology is more subtle, see
[
24
]
. Since BC is proven for surfaces of Kodaira dimension
<2
, see
[
8
]
, the best assumption for BC is that the total cohomology group
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})
is algebraic. This is equivalent to saying that
H^{1}=H^{3}=0
and
H^{2}_{{\rm{tr}}}=0
(see Appendix to Lecture 2 in
[
7
]
).

Conjecture 2.1.3
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field, and let
X
be a smooth projective surface over
k
. If
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})
is algebraic, then
T(X)=0
.

Notice that if
X
is of general type and
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})
is algebraic, vanishing of
T(X)
is simply equivalent to saying that any two closed points are rationally equivalent to each other on the surface
X
.

2.2
Finite-dimensional motives

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field, and let
{\sf Chow}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})
be the category of Chow motives over
k
with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Q}}
. Morphisms in
{\sf Chow}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})
are correspondences, i.e. classes in
CH^{*}(X\times Y,{\mathbb{Q}})
, for smooth projective
X
and
Y
over
k
.

The details about this category can be found in
[
33
]
. All we need at the moment is that it is pseudo-abelian, tensor, rigid and
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linear in the sense of
[
12
]
, and the tensor product is induced by the product of smooth projective varieties over
k
.

In any such a category one can work with wedge and symmetric powers of objects and morphisms, and hence use the machinery of finite-dimensional objects, as it was revolutionary introduced in
[
22
]
(see also a vast development in
[
1
]
).

An object
M
in
{\sf Chow}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})
is said to be evenly finite-dimensional if there exists a positive integer
n
, such that

\wedge^{n}M=0

in
{\sf Chow}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})
. The object
M
is oddly finite-dimensional if

{\rm{Sym}}^{n}M=0

in
{\sf Chow}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})
, for some
n
. An object
M
in
{\sf Chow}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})
is finite-dimensional, if it can be decomposed in to a direct sum

M=M_{+}\oplus M_{-}\;,

where
M_{+}
is evenly and
M_{-}
is oddly finite-dimensional.

Any Weil cohomology theory
H^{*}
can be extended to a functor on
{\sf Chow}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})
, see
[
33
]
. If
M
is a Chow motive over
k
, then
H^{*}(M)
decomposes in to the even

H^{+}(M)=\oplus_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}H^{2i}(M)

and odd

H^{-}(M)=\oplus_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}H^{2i+1}(M)

parts, since the Weil cohomology theory is
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded. If
M
is finite-dimensional, then

H^{*}(M_{+})=H^{+}(M)\;,

H^{*}(M_{-})=H^{-}(M)\;,

and, clearly,

\wedge^{b_{+}+1}M_{+}=0\;,

{\rm{Sym}}^{b_{-}+1}M_{-}=0\;,

where
b_{+}
and
b_{-}
are the even and odd Betti numbers of the motive
M
.

All these results can be found in
[
22
]
.

In particular, if
X
is a smooth projective surface with
b_{1}(X)=0
over
k
, then
b_{3}(X)=0
by Poincaré duality in the Weil cohomology theory
H^{*}
, and then finite-dimensionality of the motive
M(X)
of the surface
X
is equivalent to even finite-dimensionality of
M(X)
, and in turn the latter is equivalent to vanishing

\wedge^{b_{+}(X)+1}M(X)=0\;.

If
L
is a field extension over
k
, then we have a natural tensor functor from
{\sf Chow}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})
to
{\sf Chow}(L,{\mathbb{Q}})
, sending a motive
M
to its scalar extension
M_{L}
.

Lemma 2.2.1
.

If
M
is finite-dimensional, then
M_{L}
is finite-dimensional. If
M_{L}
is evenly (oddly) finite-dimensional, then so is
M
. If
M
is the motive of a smooth projective surface over
k
,
M
is finite-dimensional if and only if
M_{L}
is finite-dimensional.

Proof
.
The scalar extension functor from
{\sf Chow}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})
to
{\sf Chow}(L,{\mathbb{Q}})
is obviously additive and tensor. Hence, it preserves finite-dimensional objects. Since we look at motives with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Q}}
, if
M_{L}
is evenly (oddly) finite-dimensional, then
M
is evenly (oddly) finite-dimensional due to Lemma 3 on page 1.21 in
[
7
]
. If
M
is the motive of a smooth projective surface over
k
, we can split out the Picard and Albanese motives from
M
, which correspond to only odd cohomology, and so decompose the diagonal in to even and odd parts, see
[
30
]
or
[
33
]
. The needed equivalence then follows from the equivalence in the even and odd cases.

The meaning of Lemma
2.2.1
is that finite-dimensionality of the motive of a surface does not depend on the field of definition, and any hypothesis, which can be restated in terms of motivic finite-dimensionality, is ground field independent too.

Let
{\sf Chow}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})^{{\rm{ab}}}
be the full tensor subcategory in
{\sf Chow}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})
generated (additively and tensorly) by motives of curves. In other words,
{\sf Chow}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})^{{\rm{ab}}}
is the full tensor subcategory of motives of abelian type in
{\sf Chow}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Proposition 2.2.2
.

For any algebraically closed field
k
, all motives in
{\sf Chow}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})^{{\rm{ab}}}
are finite-dimensional.

Proof
.
See Corollary 4.4 on page 184 in
[
22
]
, and use the standard categorical properties of finite-dimensional motives.

Morphisms of Chow motives are classes of algebraic cycles modulo rational equivalence on varieties over a field. As such, they can be equivalent to
0
with regard to any adequate equivalence relation on algebraic cycles.

The key result in the theory of finite-dimensional motives is due to S.-I. Kimura:

Theorem 2.2.3
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field, and let
M
be a finite-dimensional object of the category
{\sf Chow}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})
. If
f:M\to M
is an endomorphism of
M
and
f
is numerically equivalent to
0
, then
f
is nilpotent in the associative algebra
{\rm{End}}(M)
.

Proof
.
See Proposition 7.5 on pp 192 - 193 in
[
22
]
.

As a consequence of this theorem, Kimura also proved

Corollary 2.2.4
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field, and let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
k
, such that its Chow motive
M(X)
is finite-dimensional. If the total cycle class homomorphism

{\rm{cl}}_{X}^{*}:CH^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})\to H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})

is surjective, i.e.
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})
is algebraic, then it is also an isomorphism of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
-vector spaces.

Proof
.
See Proposition 7.6 on p 193 in
[
22
]
.

It means, in particular, that if
M(X)
is finite-dimensional and
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})
is algebraic, for a smooth projective surface
X
over
k
, then
T(X)=0
, i.e. Bloch’s conjecture holds for
X
.

The converse is also true: if BC holds for
X
, then
M(X)
is finite-dimensional, see
[
16
]
and apply Lemma
2.2.1
. Thus, Bloch’s conjecture, for
X
with
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})
algebraic, is actually equivalent to finite-dimensionality of the motive
M(X)
.

2.3
Transcendence degree of
0
-cycles

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field, let
X
be an equi-dimensional variety of dimension
n
over
k
, and let
Z
be an integral closed subscheme of codimension
n
on
X\times X
, i.e. a prime correspondence of degree
0
from
X
to
X
. Following
[
4
]
, we say that
Z
is balanced on the left (respectively, on the right) if there exists an equi-dimensional Zariski closed subscheme
Y\subset X
with
\dim(Y)<n
, such that
Z
is contained in
Y\times X
(respectively, in
X\times Y
). A codimension
n
cycle
Z
is balanced on
X\times X
if it is a sum of prime cycles balanced on the left or right. A class in
CH^{n}(X\times X)
is balanced if it can be represented by a balanced cycle on
X\times X
, and the same with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

It is essential for what follows that the subspace generated by balanced cycles is a two-sided ideal in the associative algebra

CH^{n}(X\times X,{\mathbb{Q}})

of degree
0
correspondences with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Q}}
on
X
, see p. 309 in
[
14
]
.

Let
k\subset K
be a field extension. A
K
-point on
X
is a morphism of schemes

P:{\rm{Spec}}(K)\to X

over
{\rm{Spec}}(k)
. Let
\zeta_{P}
be the image of the unique point in
{\rm{Spec}}(K)
with respect to the morphism
P
, and let
\kappa(\zeta_{P})
be the residue field of the point
\zeta_{P}
on the scheme
X
. By definition, the transcendence degree of the point
P
over
k
is the transcendence degree of the field
\kappa(\zeta_{P})
over
k
:

{\rm{tr.deg}}(P/k)={\rm{tr.deg}}(\kappa(\zeta_{P})/k)\;.

The morphism
P
induces the field embedding

\kappa(\zeta_{P})\subset K\;,

whence

{\rm{tr.deg}}(P/k)\leq{\rm{tr.deg}}(K/k)\;.

Let

Z=\overline{\{\zeta_{P}\}}

be the Zariski closure of the schematic point
\zeta_{P}
in
X
. Then
Z
is a closed integral subscheme in
X
, and

{\rm{tr.deg}}(P/k)=\dim(Z)\;.

Therefore,
{\rm{tr.deg}}(P/k)
is the minimal possible dimension of a subvariety
Z
in
X
over
k
, such that the point
P
is in
Z_{K}
. In particular, that

{\rm{tr.deg}}(P/k)\leq\dim(X)\;.

Let
{\Bbbk}
be a universal domain over the field
k
, i.e. an algebraically closed field extension of infinite transcendence degree over
k
, and let

X_{{\Bbbk}}=X\times_{k}{\Bbbk}

be the scalar extension for
X
. The transcendence degree

{\rm{tr.deg}}(\alpha/k)

of a
0
-cycle class

\alpha\in CH^{n}(X_{{\Bbbk}})

over
k
is the minimal natural number
t
, such that there exists a
0
-cycle

Z=\sum_{i}m_{i}P_{i}

on
X_{{\Bbbk}}
, representing the class
\alpha
, such that

{\rm{tr.deg}}(P_{i}/k)\leq t

for all
i
.

The following theorem is a development of the result in
[
9
]
.

Theorem 2.3.1
.

Let
X
be an irreducible smooth projective variety of dimension
n
over
k
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)
the class of the diagonal
\Delta_{X}
is balanced in
CH^{n}(X\times X,{\mathbb{Q}})
;

(ii)
the Chow motive
M(X)
is isomorphic to a direct summand of the motive

M(Y_{1})\oplus(M(Y_{2})\otimes{\mathbbm{L}}^{n-e})\;,

where
{\mathbbm{L}}^{n-e}
is the tensor power of the Lefschetz motive,
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
are equidimensional smooth projective varieties over
k
whose dimensions are strictly less than
n
, and
e
is the dimension of the variety
Y_{2}
;

(iii)
any element
\alpha\in CH^{n}(X_{{\Bbbk}},{\mathbb{Q}})
satisfies

{\rm{tr.deg}}(\alpha/k)<n\;;

(iv)
there exists a closed point
P\in X_{{\Bbbk}}
, such that

{\rm{tr.deg}}(P/k)=n

and

{\rm{tr.deg}}([P])<n\;,

where
[P]
is the class of the point
P
in
CH^{d}(X_{{\Bbbk}},{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Proof
.
This is Theorem 3.6 on page 564 in
[
15
]
.

As motives of curves are finite-dimensional, Proposition
2.2.2
and Corollary
2.2.4
guarantee that to prove BC, for
X
with algebraic
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})
, it suffices to prove any of the four equivalent items in Theorem
2.3.1
.

2.4
An outline of the approach

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface over
k
. Fix a closed immersion of
X
into some projective space, and use Segre to embed
X\times X
in to the bigger projective space
{\mathbb{P}}
over
k
. Let
V
be the section of
X\times X
by a general hypersurface of sufficiently big degree containing the diagonal
\Delta_{X}
in
{\mathbb{P}}
. The singular locus
V_{{\rm{sing}}}
is a finite number of isolated ODPs on
V
by Proposition 1.4 on page 11 in Bloch’s thesis
[
5
]
. If
k={\mathbb{C}}
, by the result of Brevik and Nollet, see Theorem 1.1 on page 108 in
[
10
]
,

CH^{1}(V)=\langle\Delta_{X}\rangle\oplus I\;,

where
I
is the image of the restriction homomorphism

CH^{1}(X\times X)\to CH^{1}(V)\;.

We shall find a surface
F
in
V
, such that

F\cap V_{{\rm{sing}}}=\emptyset\;,

and
F
projects dominantly on both factors of
X\times X
. The class of
F
is then a linear combination of the class of
\Delta_{X}
and the restrictions of divisors from
X\times X
to
V
, by Brevik-Nollet’s theorem. If the irregularity of
X
is
0
,

CH^{1}(X\times X)=CH^{1}(X)\oplus CH^{1}(X)\;,

and all such restrictions are balanced.

If
\Delta_{X}
contributes trivially in to the class of
F
in
CH^{1}(V)
, the surface
F
is balanced modulo rational equivalence on
X\times X
. Since
F
projects dominantly on both factors, it gives rise to a point
P
of transcendence degree
2
over
k
on
X_{{\Bbbk}}

The second case is when the contribution of

Let

Coda

in
H^{2}(V,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})
by excision. By Weak Lefschetz,
{\rm{cl}}^{1}_{V}(F)
is coming from

H^{2}(X\times X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})

via restriction from
X\times X
to the threefold
V
.

Since
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})
is algebraic, any element in
H^{2}(X\times X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})
is the class of a linear combination of divisors of type
D\times X
or
X\times D
, where
D
is a divisor on
X
. Therefore,
F
is cohomologically equivalent to a balanced divisor
B
not meeting singular points on
V
.

As above, let
\tilde{V}
be the resolution of singularities on
V
. Since
F
and
B
do not meet
V_{{\rm{sing}}}
, we can consider them as divisors on
\tilde{V}
. By Matsusaka’s theorem,
F
is algebraically equivalent to
B
on
\tilde{V}
.

Now let

k=\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\;.

Then, of course, the Albanese kernel vanishes over
k
, see Corollary 3.3 on page 324 in
[
31
]
and Proposition 4 on page 788 in
[
11
]
.

But we can prove more.

5.2
Over
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}((t))}

Let

{\Bbbk}=\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}((t))}

be the closure of Laurent series with coefficients in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. This is an uncountable universal domain in the sense of André Weil, which may be also considered as the field of generalised Puiseux series over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, see
[
21
]
. Let us prove that the Albanese kernel vanishes also for
X_{{\Bbbk}}
.

Indeed, consider the Picard variety

A={\rm{Pic}}^{0}_{\tilde{V}/k}

of the smooth projective threefold
\tilde{V}
over
k
, and apply the old argument as in the proof of Proposition 4 on page 788 in
[
11
]
.

Namely, since the divisor
F-B
is algebraically equivalent to
0
on
\tilde{V}
, it gives the point
P
in
A(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
. As the latter group is torsion, it follows that
F
is rationally equivalent to
B
with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Q}}
on
\tilde{V}
.

Now again, let
K
be the field of rational functions on
F
, and let
P_{F}
be the closed point in
X_{K}
induced by the composition of the embedding of
F
in to
X\times X
and the projection on to the first factor. Since
F
is fat,

{\rm{tr.deg}}(P_{F}/k)=2\;,

and since
B
is balanced,

{\rm{tr.deg}}([P]/k)<2\;.

By Theorem
2.3.1
, the diagonal
\Delta_{X}
is balanced, the motive
M(X)
is finite-dimensional, and

T(X_{{\Bbbk}})=0

by Corollary
2.2.4
.

6.1
Finite dimension of
M(X)

Let again
k
be an algebraically closed field of
{\rm{char}}(k)\neq 2
, and let
X
be a smooth projective surface with algebraic
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})
over
k
. Let
M(X)
be the Chow motive of the surface
X
, and let

M(X)={\mathbbm{1}}\oplus M^{2}(X)\oplus{\mathbbm{L}}^{2}

be the decomposition of
M(X)
in the sense of Murre, see
[
30
]
. Here
{\mathbbm{1}}
is the unit motive and
{\mathbbm{L}}^{2}
is the square of the Lefschetz one.

Notice that the Picard motive
M^{1}(X)
and the Albanese motive
M^{3}(X)
are both of abelian type, and hence finite-dimensional by Proposition
2.2.2
. Since, moreover,

H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})=H^{3}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})=0\;,

we obtain that

M^{1}(X)=M^{3}(X)=0\;,

because finite-dimensional motives cannot be phantoms, see Corollary 7.3 on page 192 in
[
22
]
.

Now, the motive
M(X)
is finite-dimensional by the proven Conjecture
2.1.3
, Theorem
2.3.1
and Proposition
2.2.2
. And since
M^{1}=M^{3}=0
, the motive
M(X)
is evenly finite-dimensional. That is,

\wedge^{b+1}M(X)=0\;,

where

b=\dim(H^{*}(X))

is the total Betti number of the surface
X
.

6.2
The motive
M^{2}_{{\rm{tr}}}

Let also

M^{2}_{{\rm{tr}}}(X)

be the transcendental motive of
X
, as defined in
[
20
]
. It is a submotive in
M^{2}(X)
, whose second cohomology group vanishes,

H^{2}(M^{2}_{{\rm{tr}}}(X))=H^{2}_{{\rm{tr}}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})=0\;,

if
H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})
is algebraic. Here
H^{2}_{{\rm{tr}}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})
is the second transcendental cohomology group in terms of Bloch, see Section
2.1
.

Since the motive
M(X)
is finite-dimensional, so is its submotive
M^{2}_{{\rm{tr}}}(X)
. Then

M^{2}_{{\rm{tr}}}(X)=0

by the same Corollary 7.3 in
[
22
]
.

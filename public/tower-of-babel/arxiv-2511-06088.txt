Constraining Exponential f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) Gravity with Cosmic Chronometers and Supernovae: A Data-Driven Analysis Journal: Journal of High Energy Astrophysics
Sanjeeda Sultana, Surajit Chattopadhyay

Abstract
The current paper reports an investigation of the cosmological implications of symmetric teleparallel gravity within a modified f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) theory. We construct a specific exponential f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) model as f ⁡ ( Q ) = Q + η 1 ​ Q 0 ​ ( 1 − e − η 2 ​ Q / Q 0 ) f(Q)=Q+\eta_{1}Q_{0}\left(1-e^{-\eta_{2}\sqrt{Q/Q_{0}}}\right) , designed to smoothly deviate from General Relativity and accommodate both early-time inflation and late-time accelerated expansion. By employing Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods, we constrain the model parameters η 1 \eta_{1} , η 2 \eta_{2} , H 0 H_{0} , and Ω m 0 \Omega_{m_{0}} using a combination of cosmic chronometers (CC), Pantheon, and Pantheon + Supernovae datasets. Our analysis demonstrates that the model consistently supports a late-time acceleration scenario and is in good agreement with current cosmological observations. We extensively analyze the dynamical behavior of the model using key cosmological diagnostics, including the deceleration parameter, equation of state, energy density parameters, Statefinder, and Om diagnostics. The reconstructed Hubble parameter H ⁡ ( z ) H(z) and distance modulus μ ⁡ ( z ) \mu(z) show strong consistency with Λ \Lambda CDM and observational data, while subtle deviations at higher redshifts highlight the value of multi-probe observations. In addition, the examination of energy conditions shows that, in accordance with cosmic acceleration, the Strong Energy Condition (SEC) is broken at lower redshifts while the Dominant Energy Condition (DEC) and Null Energy Condition (NEC) are satisfied. Cosmic age estimates from the model are consistently in agreement with Planck constraints. Our results indicate the viability of exponential f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) gravity providing a consistent framework for exploring cosmic evolution and highlighting the significance of multi-probe cosmological measurements for further developments. A comparative statistical analysis reveals that while Λ \Lambda CDM remains statistically preferred based on AIC and BIC criteria, the exponential f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) model yields comparable fits and remains a theoretically motivated and viable alternative for describing cosmic acceleration.

Cosmology of

The action for the

S=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}f(Q)+S_{m}.

(1)

Here, the natural units are chosen such that
\frac{8\pi G}{c^{4}}=1

S_{m}
denotes the action corresponding to matter fields. When this action is varied with respect to the metric tensor, one obtains the following field equations:

\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\nabla_{\alpha}\left(\sqrt{-g}f_{Q}P^{\alpha}_{~~\mu\nu}\right)-\frac{1}{2}fg_{\mu\nu}+f_{Q}\left(P_{\nu\alpha\beta}Q_{\mu}^{~~\alpha\beta}-2P_{\alpha\beta\nu}Q^{\alpha\beta}_{~~~\nu}\right)=T_{\mu\nu},

(2)

where the derivative of the function
f(Q)
with respect to the non-metricity scalar
Q
is denoted by
f_{Q}=\frac{df}{dQ}
. On the other hand, varying the action with respect to the affine connection gives the following field equations governing the dynamics of the connection:

\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\left(\sqrt{-g}f_{Q}P^{\mu\nu}_{~~~\alpha}\right)=0,

(3)

which serves as the equation of motion for the connection field. The tensor
P^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}
, which is conjugate to the non-metricity, is defined as:

P^{\alpha\mu\nu}=-\frac{1}{4}Q^{\alpha\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}Q^{(\mu\nu)\alpha}+\frac{1}{4}(Q^{\alpha}-\tilde{Q}^{\alpha})g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}g^{\alpha^{(\mu}Q^{\nu)}},

(4)

where the symmetrization over indices is understood as
A_{(\mu\nu)}=\frac{1}{2}(A_{\mu\nu}+A_{\nu\mu})
. The two independent traces of the non-metricity tensor are introduced as:

Q_{\alpha}=g^{\sigma\lambda}Q_{\alpha\sigma\lambda},\quad\tilde{Q}_{\alpha}=g^{\sigma\lambda}Q_{\sigma\alpha\lambda}.

(5)

Finally, the non-metricity scalar
Q
is constructed from a contraction of the non-metricity tensor and its conjugate:

Q=-Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}P^{\alpha\mu\nu}.

(6)

We adopt a spatially flat, homogeneous, and isotropic Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker (FLRW) spacetime, described by the metric:

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)\left(dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2}\right),

(7)

where

is the cosmological scale factor. This form of the metric assumes the coincident gauge, which effectively treats the metric as the only fundamental dynamical variable. Any deviation from this gauge would yield additional contributions to the gravitational field equations due to the affine connection, as discussed in
[
70
,
71
]
. Under this specific choice of metric Eq.(
7
), the non-metricity scalar
Q

Q=6H^{2},

(8)

with

T_{\mu\nu}=(\rho+p)u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+pg_{\mu\nu}.

(9)

Here,
\rho
and
p
denote the fluid’s energy density and pressure, respectively, and
\mu_{\mu}
represents the four-velocity vector of the fluid. Substituting the FLRW metric Eq.(
7

f(Q)
gravity given by Eq.(
2

\displaystyle 6H^{2}~f_{Q}-\frac{1}{2}~f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho=\rho_{m}+\rho_{r},

(10)

\displaystyle-2\dot{H}(12H^{2}f_{QQ}+f_{Q})\

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho+p=\rho_{m}+p_{m}+\rho_{r}+p_{r}.

(11)

where
\rho_{m}
,
p_{m}
are the energy density and pressure of the matter, and
\rho_{r}
and
p_{r}
are the energy density and pressure of the radiation. Finally, the equations conclude with considering matter and radiation conservation equations:

\dot{\rho}_{m}+3H(\rho_{m}+p_{m})=0,

(12)

\dot{\rho}_{r}+3H(\rho_{r}+p_{r})=0.

(13)

The universe is filled with dust and radiation, creating a dynamic environment, so that

p_{r}=\frac{1}{3}\rho_{r},\ \ \ \ p_{m}=0.

(14)

The expressions Eq.(

P(\boldsymbol{\theta}\mid I)
represents prior knowledge of the parameters,
P(D\mid\boldsymbol{\theta},I)
is the likelihood of the observed data
D
given the model defined by
\boldsymbol{\theta}
, and
P(D\mid I)
is the Bayesian evidence (a normalization constant). The likelihood function is expressed in terms of the chi-squared statistic:

\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\exp\left(-\frac{\chi^{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta})}{2}\right).

(26)

We apply this methodology to three major observational datasets: the cosmic chronometers (CC) data, the Pantheon Type Ia Supernovae sample, and the extended Pantheon
+
compilation.

Cosmic Chronometers
(CC)
Dataset:

The cosmic chronometers
[
91
]
technique provides direct, model-independent measurements of the Hubble parameter
H(z)
by using the relative ages of massive, passively evolving galaxies. This approach relies on the relation:

H(z)=-\frac{1}{1+z}\frac{dz}{dt},

(27)

where
dz/dt
is derived from differential age evolution across redshift, measured from early-type galaxies that formed at high redshifts and have since evolved passively. Because this method does not depend on a specific cosmological model, it serves as a powerful tool for independently constraining the expansion history of the universe.

In this analysis, we use a compilation of 32 CC data points
[
92
]
, covering the redshift range
0.07\leq z\leq 1.965
, obtained from various spectroscopic surveys. These data are associated with standard errors and offer sparse but crucial coverage of
H(z)
at intermediate redshifts. The corresponding chi-squared function used to constrain the model parameters is:

\chi^{2}_{\text{CC}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\sum_{i=1}^{32}\frac{\left[H_{\text{th}}(z_{i};\boldsymbol{\theta})-H_{\text{obs}}(z_{i})\right]^{2}}{\sigma_{H}^{2}(z_{i})},

(28)

where
H_{\text{th}}
and
H_{\text{obs}}
are the theoretical and observational values of the Hubble parameter for different values of redshifts, respectively, and
\sigma_{H}
is the standard error.

Pantheon Supernovae Dataset

The Pantheon dataset is one of the largest compilations of Type Ia Supernovae (SNe Ia)
[
50
,
65
]
, comprising 1048 SNe in the redshift range
0.01<z<2.3
. This dataset combines several high-quality subsamples including those from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS), the Supernova Legacy Survey (SNLS), Pan-STARRS1 Medium Deep Survey
[
84
]
, and low-redshift surveys. SNe Ia are considered “standardizable candles” due to their relatively uniform intrinsic brightness and their well-calibrated empirical relations (e.g., light curve shape and colour corrections). The key observable from SNe Ia is the distance modulus, defined as:

\mu_{\text{th}}(z;\boldsymbol{\theta})=5\log_{10}\left[d_{L}(z;\boldsymbol{\theta})\right]+\mu_{0},

(29)

where
\mu_{0}
is a nuisance parameter combining the absolute magnitude and the Hubble constant. The luminosity distance
d_{L}(z)
is computed as:

d_{L}(z)=(1+z)\int_{0}^{z}\frac{dz^{\prime}}{E(z^{\prime})},

(30)

where
E(z)=\frac{H(z)}{H_{0}}
is the normalized Hubble parameter and variable
z^{\prime}
denotes the integration parameter ranging from
0
to redshift
z
. The chi-squared function for the Pantheon sample is then:

\chi^{2}_{\text{SN}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\sum_{i=1}^{1048}\frac{\left[\mu_{\text{th}}(z_{i};\boldsymbol{\theta})-\mu_{\text{obs}}(z_{i})\right]^{2}}{\sigma^{2}_{\mu}(z_{i})},

(31)

where
\mu_{\text{th}}
and
\mu_{\text{obs}}
denote the theoretical and observed distance moduli respectively and
\sigma_{\mu}
denotes their uncertainties.

Pantheon
+
Supernovae Dataset

The Pantheon
+
sample
[
81
,
87
,
86
]
is a comprehensive and updated successor to the original Pantheon compilation. It includes 1701 light curves of 1550 unique SNe Ia, offering improved systematics, calibration, and redshift coverage. The dataset spans a redshift interval of
0.00122\leq z\leq 2.2613
and combines data from ground-based and space-based observations, such as Hubble Space Telescope (HST) programs. Pantheon
+
provides refined treatment of systematic errors, updated calibration of zero-points, and new host-galaxy corrections. These enhancements improve the reliability of cosmological constraints derived from SNe Ia and increase sensitivity to late-time cosmic acceleration. As with the Pantheon dataset, the chi-squared function for Pantheon
+
is:

\chi^{2}_{\text{SN}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\sum_{i=1}^{1701}\frac{\left[\mu_{\text{th}}(z_{i};\boldsymbol{\theta})-\mu_{\text{obs}}(z_{i})\right]^{2}}{\sigma^{2}_{\mu}(z_{i})}.

(32)

Combined Chi-Squared and MCMC Sampling

In cases where both CC and SNe Ia data are used, we define the total chi-squared as the sum:

\chi^{2}_{\text{total}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\chi^{2}_{\text{CC}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})+\chi^{2}_{\text{SN}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}).

(33)

Model Comparison and Statistical Viability through AIC and BIC

In this subsection, we present an assessment of the viability of competing cosmological models. For that purpose we employ the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) and Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC), which penalize model complexity to avoid overfitting. In both the kinematic analysis using SNe Ia and
H(z)
data
[
103
]
, and the calibration of GRB Amati relations with Gaussian Process-reconstructed data
[
104

f(Q)
gravity model performs statistically, we use the AIC and BIC. These criteria help identify models that fit the data well without being too complex by penalizing those with too many parameters
[
105
,
106
]
. Reference
[
107
]
elaborately demonstrated how in cosmology, the AIC and the BIC are widely utilized for model selection, with the aim of balancing goodness of fit and model complexity. While AIC tends to favor models with better predictive accuracy, BIC applies a stronger penalty for additional parameters and thus often prefers simpler models such as
\Lambda
CDM over dynamical dark energy scenarios. Ref.
[
108
]
investigated linear and nonlinear cosmological interactions dependent on dark matter and dark energy densities within general relativity, utilizing AIC and BIC with SnIa, H(z), BAO, and CMB data to compare interacting models and identify those alleviating the coincidence problem.
The AIC and BIC are defined respectively as
[

]

AIC

\displaystyle=\chi^{2}_{\text{min}}+2k,

(34)

BIC

\displaystyle=\chi^{2}_{\text{min}}+k\ln N,

(35)

N

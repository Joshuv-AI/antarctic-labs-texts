Γ \Gamma -convergence of an Enhanced Finite Element Method for Manià’s and Foss’s Problems Exhibiting the Lavrentiev Gap Phenomenon
Xiaobing Feng, Joshua M. Sitkar

Abstract
It is well-known that numerically approximating calculus of variations problems possessing a Lavrentiev Gap Phenomenon (LGP) is challenging, and the standard numerical methodologies, such as finite element, finite difference, and discontinuous Galerkin methods, fail to give convergent methods because they cannot overcome the gap. This paper is a continuation of [ 21 ] , where a promising enhanced finite element method was proposed to overcome the LGP in the classical Manià’s problem. The first goal of this paper is to provide a complete Γ \Gamma -convergence proof for this enhanced finite element method, hence, establishing a theoretical foundation for the method. The crux of the convergence analysis is taking advantage of the regularity of the minimizer and viewing the minimization problem as posed over the fractional Sobolev space W 1 + s , p ​ ( 0 , 1 ) W^{1+s,p}(0,1) (for s > 0 s>0 ) rather than the original admissible space W 1 , p ​ ( 0 , 1 ) W^{1,p}(0,1) . The second goal is to extend the enhanced finite element method to the two-dimensional Foss’s problem [ 24 ] from nonlinear elasticity, which is also known to possess the LGP, and to establish its Γ \Gamma -convergence as well.

Introduction

The Manià’s problem refers to the following calculus of variations problem:

(1.1)

u=\underset{v\in\mathcal{A}}{\text{argmin}}\bigg\{J(v):=\int^{1}_{0}v^{\prime}(x)^{6}\bigl(v(x)^{3}-x\bigr)^{2}\,dx\bigg\},

where
\mathcal{A}:=\{u\in W^{1,1}(0,1)\ |\ u(0)=0,\ u(1)=1,\ J(u)<\infty\}
(see Section
2
for notation).
This problem is infamous for being one of the simplest that possesses the
Lavrentiev Gap Phenomenon
(LGP) in the sense that the following strict inequality holds (see
[
30
]
or, more recently,
[
34
, Example 2.14]
for a proof):

(1.2)

\min_{u\in\mathcal{A}}J(u)\ <\ \inf_{u\in\mathcal{A}\cap W^{1,\infty}(0,1)}J(u).

Noticing that
W^{1,\infty}(0,1)
is a dense subset of
W^{1,1}(0,1)
, hence, the LGP says that the same functional can have different minimum values (hence, different minimizers) over two nested admissible sets.
It is not hard to show that the minimizer of (
1.1
) is
\overline{u}(x):=x^{\frac{1}{3}}
, for which
J(\overline{u})=0
(cf.
[
8
]
).
It is easy to check that
\overline{u}\in W^{1+s,p}(0,1)
for
0\leq s<1/3
and
1\leq p<3/2
when
(2/3+s)p<1
.
Consequently, the minimizer of
J
over
X:=\mathcal{A}\cap W^{1+s,p}(0,1)

is also
\overline{u}
for the same range of
s
and
p
.

The LGP is a difficult issue to address in the calculus of variations because it can occur not only for non-convex energy functionals but also for convex ones. Moreover, no general characterization of the LGP is known in the literature; consequently, it is often studied on a case-by-case basis. The LGP is of great interest in analysis, as it provides difficult and intriguing problems to study, in addition to having important applications in materials science. It is also of great interest in numerical analysis because developing robust and convergent numerical methods for such problems has practical significance in providing means to compute their solutions. However, it is known that achieving such a goal is not only difficult but also intriguing. For example, it is well known that the standard finite element method fails to give convergence to a minimizer: that is, if
X_{h}
denotes a conforming linear finite element space with respect to the uniform mesh over
(0,1)
with mesh size
h>0
that matches the boundary conditions of
\mathcal{A}
, then

(1.3)

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\min_{u\in X_{h}}J(u_{h})\ \nrightarrow\ \min_{u\in\mathcal{A}}J(u)

because
X_{h}\subset\mathcal{A}\cap W^{1,\infty}(0,1)
. We also refer the reader to
[
16
]
for a discussion of this issue for general conforming finite element methods, and
[
33
]
for an analogous discussion regarding non-conforming finite element methods. An important message from this discussion is that if we want to use a conforming finite element method to overcome the LGP, we cannot use the original functional
J
as an approximate functional to design a convergent numerical method.

It is also well-understood that the cause of (
1.3
) boils down to the fact that the factor
|u_{h}^{\prime}|^{6}
in the density function grows large too quickly as
h\rightarrow 0^{+}
approaches the left-endpoint
x=0
compared to the rate at which the other factor goes to zero. The papers
[
7
,
29
,
3
]
utilized a truncation method to approximate the singular minimizer. Another interesting numerical technique is the element removal method
[
27
,
28
]
, which successfully approximated the true minimizer when given a priori information on the location of singularities in that minimizer.
There were also additional papers surveying the analysis of the LGP in nonlinear elasticity problems
[
2
,
23
]
, in cavitation
[
8
,
9
]
, and in microstructure theory
[
36
]
. More recent works
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
, meanwhile, have analyzed the LGP for nonlocal energy functionals.
In another more recent dissection of (
1.2
), Feng and Schnake proposed in
[
21
,
35
]
an enhanced finite element method using a cutoff technique. Their idea was to introduce an approximate functional that has a mechanism to limit the pointwise values of the derivative of the input function while still approximating the functional
J
in some sense. Specifically, for some
\alpha>0
and
h>0
, they introduced a cut-off function defined as

(1.4)

\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(t)\ :=\ \text{sgn}(t)\min\{|t|,h^{-\alpha}\},

for scalar-valued functions, and

(1.5)

(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(w_{i}))\ :=\ \text{sgn}(w_{i})\min\{|w_{i}|,h^{-\alpha}\},\qquad i=1,2,\cdots,d,

component-wise when
w
is a
d
-vector.
Meanwhile, the corresponding approximate functional was defined as

(1.6)

J^{\alpha}_{h}(v)\ :=\ \int^{1}_{0}\bigl(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v^{\prime}(x))\bigr)^{6}(v(x)^{3}-x)^{2}\,dx,

which led to the following enhanced finite element method:

(1.7)

\overline{u}_{h}\ =\ \underset{v_{h}\in X_{h}}{\text{argmin}}\,J^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{h}).

We note that choices for the parameter
\alpha
depend only on the structural properties of the density function of Manià’s functional and the regularity of the minimizer, but not on the locations of singularities or other properties.
Extensive numerical experiments presented in
[
21
,
35
]
are quite promising, as they all point to the convergence of this enhanced finite element method. However, to settle the convergence issue, a rigorous proof of the convergence of the minimizer of (
1.7
) to the minimizer of (
1.1
) is necessary. One pertinent mathematical framework is the
\Gamma
-convergence theory
[
17
]
.

This framework has appeared in many instances in the calculus of variations, including studying the convergence of discretizations for finding equilibrium states of nematic liquid crystals through the Landau-de Gennes Model; see
[
10
,
31
,
32
]
. This method is an alternative error estimation technique for problems where compactness techniques, through a priori estimates, may not be readily available.

Meanwhile, the
\Gamma
-convergence analysis of (
1.7
) was indeed pursued and partially succeeded in
[
21
,
35
]
. Specifically, these works attempted to prove
\Gamma
-convergence of
J^{\alpha}_{h}
to
J
with respect to the weak
W^{1,1}
topology as
h\rightarrow 0^{+}
, for some fixed
\alpha>0
, and the
lim-inf
inequality was successfully established (see
[
35
, Lemma 4.1]
).
However, the construction of a suitable recovery sequence has remained open. It should be noted that a
\Gamma
-limit necessarily exists for at least a sub-sequence of
\{J^{\alpha}_{h}\}_{h>0}
owing to the compactness of
\Gamma
-convergence
[
12
, Proposition 1.42]
.

The first main objective of this paper is to pick up where
[
21
,
35
]
stalled and to complete the
\Gamma
-convergence proof for the approximate functional
J^{\alpha}_{h}
, albeit with respect to the strong

W^{1,p}
-topology. We may use the standard finite element nodal interpolant as the recovery sequence, but we also leverage the often overlooked higher-order differentiability of continuous piecewise polynomials. Indeed,
X_{h}\subset W^{1+s,p}
for
0\leq sp<1
and
1<p\leq\infty
, which has been known for
p=2,\infty
(see
[
13
,
37
]
).
We extend this result to all
p>1
.
Moreover, since the minimizer
\overline{u}(x)=x^{\frac{1}{3}}
of (
1.1
) belongs to the fractional Sobolev space
W^{1+s,p}
for
0\leq s<1/3
and
1\leq p<3/2
when
(2/3+s)p<1
,
It is natural to consider the fractional Sobolev spaces.
However, although
X_{h}\subset W^{1+s,p}
,
X_{h}
is not dense in
W^{1+s,p}
unless
s=0
which seems to pose a technical difficulty in establishing the
\Gamma
-convergence in the strong
W^{1,p}
-topology.
It turns out that this difficulty can be mitigated because the density is not necessary. Moreover, the additional
regularity of the minimizer justifies regarding problem (
1.1
) as the minimization of
J
over
X:=\mathcal{A}\cap W^{1+s,p}(0,1)
rather than over
\mathcal{A}
, which, combined with (
1.6
) and the explicit
O(h^{-s})
blowup rate in the
W^{1+s,p}
-norm for the finite element function (thanks to the generalized inverse inequality, see (
3.19
)), mitigates singularities in the derivative. This is sufficient to complete the
\Gamma
-convergence proof. It should be emphasized that, although this recasting of the problem is crucial for our convergence analysis, it has no impact on the numerical method
and its implementation. Therefore, it is only an analytical technique for the convergence proof.

The second main objective is to extend the enhanced finite element method and the
\Gamma
-convergence result to the
two-dimensional Foss’s problem from nonlinear elasticity, which is also known to possess the LGP. Recall that Foss’s functional is

(1.8)

F(v)\ :=\ 66\left(\frac{13}{14}\right)^{14}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{y}{y-1}\right)^{14}|v|^{\frac{14-3y}{y-1}}(|v|^{\frac{y}{y-1}}-x)^{2}(v_{x})^{14}dxdy,

originally introduced in
[
22
,
24
]
. Here
\Omega:=(0,1)\times\left(\frac{3}{2},\frac{5}{2}\right)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The admissible space for this functional is

\mathcal{B}\ :=\ \{v\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)\ |\ v(0,y)=0,\ v(1,y)=1,\ F(v)<\infty\},

and we will discuss other properties of this problem in Section
2.2
. For now, we simply remark that both (
1.1
) and (
1.8
) contain factors involving the input function multiplied by powers of its first-order derivatives, allowing us to use the same blueprint to navigate the technical details of the required convergence proofs.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Sectiosn
2
and
3
, we introduce the required space and function notation, the definition of finite element spaces, and a few facts about Manià’s and Foss’s problems. Section
4
is devoted to proving the desired
\Gamma
-convergence result for Maniá’s problem, while Section
5
carries out the same task for Foss’s problem. Next, Section
6
presents additional numerical result beyond that provided in
[
21
,
35
]
. Finally, we discuss other LGP problems to be explored in Section
7
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Function spaces

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain. For integer
k\geq 0
and real number
1\leq p<\infty
,
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
denotes the Sobolev space consisting of all functions whose weak partial derivatives up to order
k
belong to
L^{p}(\Omega)
. Note that
W^{0,p}(\Omega)=L^{p}(\Omega)
. We also define, for any real number
r>0
, setting
k:=[r]
, the Fractional Sobolev norm by

(2.1)

\|v\|^{p}_{W^{r,p}(\Omega)}:=\|v\|^{p}_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}+\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\partial^{\alpha}v(x)-\partial^{\alpha}v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+p(r-k)}}dxdy.

The second term on the right-hand side will be abbreviated as
[v]^{p}_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}
, and when
r=k
is an integer, the definition (
2.1
) becomes

(2.2)

\|v\|^{p}_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}:=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\|\partial^{\alpha}v\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Then
W^{r,p}(\Omega)
denotes the Banach space endowed with the norm defined in (
2.1
). When
p=2
, we set
H^{r}(\Omega):=W^{r,2}(\Omega)
.
For
0<s<1
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we have

(2.3)

W^{1+s,p}(\Omega):=\{u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\ |\ u\in L^{p}(\Omega),\ \bigtriangledown u\in W^{s,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\}.

These spaces are further discussed in
[
1
,
18
,
19
,
25
]
.
In this paper, we only consider two special cases of domains: (i)
n=1
and
\Omega=(0,1)
; (ii)
n=2
and
\Omega=(0,1)\times\left(\frac{3}{2},\frac{5}{2}\right)
.

Throughout this paper,
C
will be used to denote a generic positive constant that is independent of the mesh size
h
. The notation
\alpha\lesssim\beta
stands for
\alpha\leq C\beta
for some
C>0
. For any
1<p<\infty
, we define its conjugate as
p^{\prime}:=p/(p-1)
.

Recall that the Manià’s and Foss’s functionals
J
and
F
are given by (

) and (
1.8
), respectively. Their respective admissible spaces are
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, namely,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{u\in W^{1,1}(0,1)\ |\ u(0)=0,\ u(1)=1,\ J(u)<\infty\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{v\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)\ |\ v(0,y)=0,\ v(1,y)=1,\ F(v)<\infty\},

where

is in the definition of

.

It has already been noted in Section

that the minimizer for Manià’s problem is
\overline{u}(x)=x^{\frac{1}{3}}
, and it is easy to verify that
\overline{u}
belongs to the fractional Sobolev space
W^{1+s,p}
for
0\leq s<1/3
and
1\leq p<3/2
where
(2/3+s)p<1
.
For Foss’s problem, it is known that the minimizer is
\overline{v}(x,y):=x^{\frac{y-1}{y}}
and

\overline{v}
belongs to
W^{1+s,p}(\Omega)
for
1\leq p<\frac{3}{2}
and
s<\frac{1}{p}-\frac{2}{3}
.
We will prove the latter claim in Appendix

. It should be noted that the exact forms or expressions of these minimizers are not required or used in our enhanced finite element methods and convergence analysis. The only information about the minimizers used in our convergence analysis (to be given in the subsequent sections) is a slightly higher differentiablity than
W^{1,1}
offers.

3
Enhanced finite element methods and fractional inverse inequalities

3.1
Finite element spaces and interpolations

Let
N>>1
be an integer, define
h=1/N
and
x_{j}:=jh
for
j=0,1,2,\cdots,N
. Then,
\{x_{j}\}_{j=0}^{N}
is a uniform mesh over
[0,1]
with mesh size
h
. Also introduced in Section
1
,
X_{h}
denotes the conforming linear finite element space over the mesh
\{x_{j}\}_{j=0}^{N}
that preserves the boundary conditions of
\mathcal{A}
. Specifically,

(3.1)

X_{h}:=\ \bigl\{v_{h}\in C(0,1)\ |\ \ v_{h}|_{I_{k}}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(I_{k})\,\,\forall 0\leq k\leq N-1\,,\ v_{h}(0)=0,\ v_{h}(1)=1\bigr\},

where
I_{k}:=(x_{k},x_{k+1})
and
\mathcal{P}_{1}(I_{k})
denote the set of all linear functions on
I_{k}
.

Similarly, let
\{\mathscr{T}_{h}\}_{h>0}
be a family of quasi-uniform triangular meshes over the rectangular domain

\Omega=(0,1)\times\left(\frac{3}{2},\frac{5}{2}\right)
with mesh size
h>0
, and let
Y_{h}
denote the conforming linear finite element space over
\mathscr{T}_{h}
that preserves the boundary conditions of
\mathcal{B}
, which is the two-dimensional analog of (
3.1
), namely

(3.2)

Y_{h}\ :=\ \{v_{h}\in C(\Omega)\ |\ v_{h}|_{T}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(T)\ \forall T\in\mathscr{T}_{h},\ v_{h}(0,\cdot)\ =\ 0,\ v_{h}(1,\cdot)\ =\ 1\},

where
\mathcal{P}_{1}(T)
denotes the set of linear functions on
T
. Notice
Y_{h}\subset\mathcal{B}
for every
h>0
.

Let
\{\phi_{m}\}^{M}_{m=1}
denote the nodal basis of the finite element space
\mathcal{A}
(resp.
\mathcal{B}
) with
M=\mbox{dim}(X_{h})
(resp.
M=\mbox{dim}(Y_{h})
). For each
v\in C^{0}(\Omega)
, we define its nodal interpolation
I_{h}v\in X_{h}
(or
Y_{h}
) as follows:

(3.3)

I_{h}v(x)\ :=\ \sum^{M}_{m=1}\phi_{m}(x)v(x).

Then the following properties of
I_{h}
are well-known (cf.
[
20
]
).

Lemma 1
.

Let
\Omega=(0,1)
or
\Omega=(0,1)\times\left(\frac{3}{2},\frac{5}{2}\right)
and
v\in W^{r,p}(\Omega)
for
0<r\leq 2
and
1\leq p<\infty
, then
I_{h}
satisfies the following properties:

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|I_{h}v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\qquad\mbox{if $1<r\leq 2$},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|v-I_{h}v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle h^{r}[v]_{W^{r,p}(\Omega)},

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|v-I_{h}v\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle h^{r-1}[v]_{W^{r,p}(\Omega)}\qquad\mbox{if $1<r\leq 2$}.

If we write
r=1+s
for
0<s\leq 1
, the right-hand side of (
3.6
) becomes
h^{s}[v]_{W^{1+s,p}(\Omega)}
.

We note that all functions in
\mathcal{A}\cap W^{1+s,p}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{B}\cap W^{1+s,p}(\Omega)
are Hölder continuous; hence, they belong to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
(cf.
[
26
]
). Therefore, (
3.4
) makes sense.

Remark 3.1
.

For our
\Gamma
-convergence analysis to be presented later, we will not need the special structure of the finite element interpolant, but rather just its stability and approximation properties. Therefore,
I_{h}
can be replaced by any operator
K_{h}:\mathcal{A}\ (\mbox{\rm resp. }\mathcal{B})\to X_{h}\ (\mbox{\rm resp. }Y_{h})
, which satisfies (
3.4
)–(
3.6
).

3.2
Enhanced finite element methods

We first recall that the enhanced finite element method for Manià’s problem is defined in (
1.7
). It minimizes the numerical energy functional
J^{\alpha}_{h}
(see (
1.6
)) over the finite element space
X_{h}
.

To define the enhanced finite element method for Foss’s problem, let
\beta>0
be a fixed constant. we define a discrete analog
F^{\beta}_{h}:Y_{h}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of
F
as follows:

(3.7)

F^{\beta}_{h}(v):=66\left(\frac{13}{14}\right)^{14}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{y}{y-1}\right)^{14}|v|^{\frac{14-3y}{y-1}}(|v|^{\frac{y}{y-1}}-x)^{2}(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}v_{x})^{14}\,dxdy.

Then, the enhanced finite element method for Foss’s problem is defined as

(3.8)

\overline{v}_{h}=\underset{v_{h}\in Y_{h}}{\text{argmin}}\,F^{\beta}_{h}(v_{h}).

Remark 3.2
.

We note that since
Y_{h}\subset W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
, the Lavrentiev gap implies that (cf.
[
23
, Theorem 6.1]
)

(3.9)

\inf_{v\in\mathcal{B}}F(v)<\inf_{v\in W^{1,\infty}}F(v)\leq\inf_{v_{h}\in Y_{h}}F(v_{h}).

Thus, the standard finite element method does not converge for Foss’s problem, which is expected. However, unlike in Manià’s problem, we do not automatically gain that admissible functions for
F(\cdot)
belong to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
through Sobolev embeddings (especially,
\overline{v}\notin W^{1,p}(\Omega)
for any
p>2
), so we make this inclusion explicit in the definition of
\mathcal{B}
. In turn, we may include the case
p=1
here.

3.3
Fractional inverse inequalities

The goal of this section is to establish general inverse inequalities for finite element functions with respect to the norm of the fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,p}
. These results will be crucially used in our
\Gamma
-convergence proofs. Moreover, they also have a great deal of independent interest in numerical PDEs. To this end, we first state our mesh assumptions required to ensure our fractional inverse inequalities

Assumption 3.1

(Mesh assumptions for inverse inequalities)
.

For the setup of our general inverse inequalities, we make the following mesh assumptions:

(a)

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded polygonal domain with boundary
\partial\Omega
.

(b)

Let
\{\mathscr{T}_{h}\}_{h>0}
be a shape-regular family of meshes with shape regularity parameter
\sigma
. Since
\Omega
is polygonal, then
\Omega=\cup_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}T
.

(c)

Define the mesh size
h:=\max_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\text{diam}(T)
for the mesh
\mathscr{T}_{h}
(where
\text{diam}(T)
denotes the diameter of triangle
T
).

We note that the assumption of
\Omega
being polygonal is not strictly necessary and is included for the sake of simplicity. Notice also that the setups provided in Subsections
3.1
and
3.2
satisfy these assumptions. Also, a fractional inverse inequality is already known in the case
p=2
; its proof can be found in
[
37
, Theorem 3.1]
. For completeness, we quote the result in the following theorem.

Theorem 2

(Fractional inverse inequality,
p=2
)
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and
v\in\mathcal{P}_{m}(\Omega)
, the set of continuous piecewise polynomials of degree at most
m
with respect to the mesh
\mathscr{T}_{h}
. Then, for any
0\leq\gamma<1/2
, there holds the inequality

(3.10)

[v]_{H^{1+\gamma}(\Omega)}\ \lesssim\ h^{-\gamma}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

We note that the above estimate immediately implies that
Y_{h}\subset H^{1+\gamma}(\Omega)
. However, it is well-known that
Y_{h}
is not dense in
H^{1+\gamma}(\Omega)
for
\gamma>0
.
This estimate is most relevant in the case where the function to be approximated belongs only to
H^{1}
. Since we know a priori that the minimizers of
J
and
F
are better than the
H^{1}
-functions (see Subsection
2.2
), we need to deal with functions in certain fractional Sobolev spaces
W^{s,p}
. Thus, we want to generalize Theorem
2
to the fractional Sobolev spaces
W^{s,p}
, and present the results on a more general domain and family of meshes for the sake of enhancing versatility.

Theorem 3

(Generalized fractional inverse inequality)
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and
v\in\mathcal{P}_{m}(\Omega)
, the set of continuous piecewise polynomials of degree at most
m
with respect to the mesh
\mathscr{T}_{h}
. Suppose that Assumption
3.1
holds and that
0<s<1
,
1\leq p<\infty
are such that
sp<1
. Then there holds

(3.11)

[v]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\ \lesssim\ h^{-s}\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

We follow and adapt the idea of the proof of Theorem
2
. We start with decomposing the semi-norm (defined in (
2.1
)) as the following sum:

(3.12)

[v]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}=I+II+III,

where

(3.13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I&:=\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}T,T^{\prime}\in\mathscr{T}_{h}\\
T\cap T^{\prime}=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\int_{T}\int_{T^{\prime}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dxdy,\\
II&:=\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}T,T^{\prime}\in\mathscr{T}_{h},\ T\neq T^{\prime}\\
T\cap T^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\int_{T}\int_{T^{\prime}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dxdy,\\
III&:=\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\int_{T}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dxdy.\end{aligned}

For
I
, we use the symmetry of the integrand and the elementary inequality
(a+b)^{p}\lesssim a^{p}+b^{p}
for
a,b>0
to obtain

(3.14)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I&\lesssim\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}T,T^{\prime}\in\mathscr{T}_{h}\\
T\cap T^{\prime}=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\int_{T}\int_{T^{\prime}}\frac{|v(x)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dxdy\\
&\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\int_{T}|v(x)|^{p}\left(\int_{|y-x|\geq\sigma^{-1}h}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}dy\right)dx\\
&\lesssim\ h^{-sp}\|v\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.\end{aligned}

Now, to bound
II
, we let
T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}
and fix
x\in T
, then define
d_{x}:=\text{dist}\{x,\partial T\}
. Using polar coordinates,

(3.15)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T,T^{\prime}\in\mathscr{T}_{h},\ T\neq T^{\prime}\\
T\cap T^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\int_{T^{\prime}}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(0,d_{x})}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{\infty}_{d_{x}}\frac{r^{n-1}}{r^{n+sp}}\,dr\lesssim d_{x}^{-sp}.

In addition, by the definition of
d_{x}
and the fact that
sp<1
, we have

(3.16)

\int_{T}d_{x}^{-sp}dx\lesssim\ h^{n-sp}.

Combining (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) alongside standard inverse inequalities gives

(3.17)

II\ \lesssim h^{n-sp}\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\|v\|^{p}_{L^{\infty}(T)}\lesssim\ h^{-sp}\|v\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Finally,
III
can also be handled using standard inverse inequalities as follows:

(3.18)

\displaystyle III

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}[v]^{p}_{W^{1,\infty}(T)}\int_{T}\int_{T}\frac{|x-y|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle h^{n+p-sp}\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}[v]^{p}_{W^{1,\infty}(T)}\lesssim\ h^{-sp}\|v\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Combining (
3.14
), (
3.17
), and (
3.18
), and taking
p
th roots gives the desired result.
∎

The following corollary follows from applying Theorem
3
in the 1-D case, to the weak derivative of functions in
W^{1+s,p}(0,1)
for some
0<s<1
and
1\leq p<\infty
.

Corollary 4
.

Suppose that
0<s<1
and
1\leq p<\infty
satisfy
sp<1
. Let
v_{h}\in X_{h}
as defined in (
3.1
). Then there holds the following inverse inequality:

(3.19)

[v_{h}]_{W^{1+s,p}(0,1)}\ \lesssim\ h^{-s}\|v_{h}\|_{W^{1,p}(0,1)}.

In particular, the inequality implies that
v_{h}\in W^{1+s,p}(0,1)
.

We conclude this section by noting that when
p=2
, the condition
sp<1
implies that
s<1/2
. Hence, (
3.19
) recovers (
3.10
). Moreover, (
3.19
) also provides a blowup rate
O(h^{-s})
for the finite element functions in the
W^{1+s,p}
-norm. This estimate will play a critical role in our convergence analysis, see Lemma
7
.

4
\Gamma
-convergence for Manià’s problem

One of the main goals of this paper is to complete the proof of
\Gamma
-convergence of the approximate functional

J_{h}^{\alpha}
, defined in (
1.6
), to the Manià’s functional
J
defined in (
1.1
). Consequently, we will be able to conclude the convergence of the numerical minimizers. We recall that in
[
21
,
35
]
, the authors were able to prove a
lim-inf
inequality for the approximate functional, but did not construct a recovery sequence converging in a suitable topology; this is the gap we intend to fill in here. We stress that our proof technique heavily relies on the structure of (
1.1
), but not on the exact knowledge of the true minimizer itself. Throughout this section, let
K_{h}
be an operator which satisfies properties (
3.4
)–(
3.6
) ( see Remark
3.1
).

First, we recall the definition of
\Gamma
-convergence tailored to Manià’s problem.

Definition 5

(
\Gamma
-convergence)
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
0<s<1/3
and
1<p<3/2
be such that
\left(2/3+s\right)p<1
,
X_{h}
denote the piecewise linear finite element space, and
X:=\mathcal{A}\cap W^{1+s,p}(0,1)
.
We say that the family of functionals
\{J^{\alpha}_{h}\}_{h>0}
, defined on
X_{h}
,
\Gamma
-converges as
h\to 0^{+}
to the functional
J
, defined on
X
, (written as
J^{\alpha}_{h}\xrightarrow{\Gamma}J
) with respect to the strong
W^{1,p}(0,1)
-topology if the following hold:

(i)

The lim-inf property:
If
\{v_{h}\}_{h>0}\subset X_{h}

is such that
v_{h}\rightarrow v
strongly in
W^{1,p}(0,1)
then we have the lim-inf inequality

(4.1)

\underset{h\to 0^{+}}{\mbox{\rm lim\,inf}}\,J^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{h})\geq J(v).

(ii)

Recovery sequence property:
For any
v\in X
, there exists a recovery sequence
\{v_{h}\}_{h>0}\subset X_{h}
such that
v_{h}\rightarrow v
strongly in
W^{1,p}(0,1)
and the following lim-sup inequality holds:

(4.2)

\underset{h\to 0^{+}}{\mbox{\rm lim\,sup}}\,J^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{h})\leq J(v).

Recall that a property stronger than (i) was proven in
[
21
,
35
]
, namely assuming convergence only in the weak
W^{1,1}(0,1)
-topology. Also notice that
X_{h}\subset W^{1+s,p}(0,1)
provided that
0\leq sp<1
(see Corollary
4
). Our goal here is to establish property (ii). The next technical lemma is another step towards achieving our goal.

Lemma 6
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
and suppose
v\in X:=\mathcal{A}\cap W^{1+s,p}(0,1)
. Let
0<\alpha<(1+s)/6
be fixed, and
v_{h}:=K_{h}v

Then there holds

(4.3)

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{0}\bigl(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{h}^{\prime}(x)\bigr)^{6}\bigl[(v_{h}(x)^{3}-x)^{2}-(v(x)^{3}-x)^{2}\bigr]\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip 72.26999pt\lesssim h^{1+s-6\alpha}\|v\|^{5}_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}[v]_{W^{1+s,p}(0,1)}.

Proof.

By the definition of
\chi_{h}^{\alpha}
, we have

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{0}\bigl(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{h}^{\prime}(x)\bigr)^{6}\bigl[(v_{h}(x)^{3}-x)^{2}-(v(x)^{3}-x)^{2}\bigr]\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip 65.04256pt\leq h^{-6\alpha}\int^{1}_{0}\bigl|(v_{h}(x)^{3}-x)^{2}-(v(x)^{3}-x)^{2}\bigr|\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip 65.04256pt\lesssim h^{-6\alpha}\int^{1}_{0}\bigl|v(x)^{3}+v_{h}(x)^{3}-2x\bigr|\cdot\bigl|K_{h}v(x)^{3}-v(x)^{3}\bigr|\,dx.

Since
v\in W^{1,p}(0,1)
for
p>1
, then
v\in L^{\infty}(0,1)
, and in turn
v_{h}\in L^{\infty}(0,1)
due to (
3.4
), so

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{0}\bigl(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{h}^{\prime}(x)\bigr)^{6}\bigl[(v(x)^{3}-x)^{2}-(v_{h}(x)^{3}-x)^{2}\bigr]\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip 86.72377pt\lesssim h^{-6\alpha}\|v\|^{3}_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\int^{1}_{0}\bigl|v(x)^{3}-v_{h}(x)^{3}\bigr|\,dx.

By using a binomial formula and (again) that
v,v_{h}\in L^{\infty}(0,1)
, we obtain

(4.4)

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{0}\bigl(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{h}^{\prime}(x)\bigr)^{6}\bigl[(v(x)^{3}-x)^{2}-(v_{h}(x)^{3}-x)^{2}\bigr]\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip 130.08621pt\lesssim h^{-6\alpha}\|v\|^{5}_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\|v-v_{h}\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}.

Then, (
4.3
) follows from (
3.5
) and (
4.4
); the proof is complete.
∎

We now state and prove a second technical lemma.

Lemma 7
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
, and suppose that
v\in X:=\mathcal{A}\cap W^{1+s,p}(0,1)
. Let
0<\alpha<s/5
be fixed, and
v_{h}:=K_{h}v
.
Then there holds

(4.5)

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{0}|(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{h}^{\prime}(x))^{6}-(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v^{\prime}(x)))^{6}|(v(x)^{3}-x)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\hskip 108.405pt\lesssim\ h^{s-5\alpha}\|v\|^{6}_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}[v]_{W^{1+s,p}(0,1)}.

Proof.

By factoring the difference of sixth powers, the definition (
1.4
), the
L^{\infty}
-stability of
K_{h}
granted by (
3.4
), and the continuous embedding
W^{1+s,p}(0,1)\subset L^{\infty}(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int^{1}_{0}|(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{h}^{\prime}(x))^{6}-(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v^{\prime}(x)))^{6}|(v(x)^{3}-x)^{2}dx\\
&\lesssim\int^{1}_{0}|\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{h}^{\prime}(x))-\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v^{\prime}(x))|\cdot(|\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{h}^{\prime}(x))|^{5}+|\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v^{\prime}(x))|^{5})(v(x)^{3}-x)^{2}dx\\
&\leq\ h^{-5\alpha}\|v\|^{6}_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\int^{1}_{0}|\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{h}^{\prime}(x))-\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v^{\prime}(x))|dx.\end{aligned}

Now, due to the inequality
|\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{h}^{\prime}(x))-\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v^{\prime}(x))|\leq|v_{h}^{\prime}(x)-v^{\prime}(x)|
and (
3.6
), we obtain the desired result.
∎

Notice this lemma would not stand if we only assumed that
v\in\mathcal{A}\cap W^{1,p}(0,1)
.

Finally, we are ready to state and prove the main result of this paper for Manià’s problem. Since the minimizer for the continuous problem is known to belong to
W^{1+s,p}(0,1)

for
0<s<1/3
and
1<p<3/2
such that
\left(2/3+s\right)p<1
, the following result is stated specifically in that case,

Theorem 8

(
\Gamma
-convergence of
J_{h}^{\alpha}
)
.

Let
0<s<1/3
and
1<p<3/2
be such that
\left(2/3+s\right)p<1
. Assume that
\alpha<\min\{(1+s)/6,s/5\}
is fixed, and we have that
J^{\alpha}_{h}\xrightarrow{\Gamma}J
as
h\to 0^{+}
in the strong
W^{1,p}(0,1)
-topology.

Proof.

Since strong convergence in
W^{1,p}(0,1)
implies weak convergence in
W^{1,1}(0,1)
, then
[
35
, Theorem 4.1]
implies that the inequality (
4.1
) holds. In our specific setting, we may expedite the proof. Let
\{v_{h}\}_{h>0}
be a sequence such that
v_{h}\in X_{h}
for all
h>0
and
v_{h}\rightarrow v
strongly in
W^{1,p}(0,1)
. Then
\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{h}^{\prime}(x))^{6}(v_{h}(x)^{3}-x)^{2}\rightarrow v^{\prime}(x)^{6}(v(x)^{3}-x)^{2}
for a.e.
x\in(0,1)
. This, combined with the non-negativity of the integrands for
\{J^{\alpha}_{h}\}_{h>0}
, allows us to conclude (
4.1
) by Fatou’s Lemma.

Next, to prove inequality (
4.2
), we fix
v\in X
and let
\{v_{h}\}_{h>0}=\{K_{h}v\}_{h>0}
. We now show that this choice is a valid recovery sequence. It immediately follows from (
3.6
) that
v_{h}\rightarrow v
strongly in
W^{1,p}(0,1)
. For any
h>0
fixed, we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle J^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{0}(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}\bigl(v_{h}^{\prime}(x)\bigr)^{6}\bigl[(v_{h}(x)^{3}-x)^{2}-(v(x)^{3}-x)^{2}\bigr]\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{1}_{0}(\bigl(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{h}^{\prime}(x)\bigr)^{6}-\bigl(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v^{\prime}(x)\bigr)^{6})(v(x)^{3}-x)^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{1}_{0}\bigl(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v^{\prime}(x)\bigr)^{6}(v(x)^{3}-x)^{2}\,dx.

The first term on the right-hand side of (
4.6
) converges to
0
thanks to Lemma
6
.
As for the second term, we observe that

(4.7)

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{0}(\bigl(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{h}^{\prime}(x)\bigr)^{6}-\bigl(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v^{\prime}(x)\bigr)^{6})(v(x)^{3}-x)^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip 72.26999pt\leq\int^{1}_{0}|(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{h}^{\prime}(x))^{6}-(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v^{\prime}(x)))^{6}|(v(x)^{3}-x)^{2}dx,

and the right-hand side converges to
0
by Lemma
7
. Finally, it is evident that

(4.8)

\int^{1}_{0}\bigl(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v^{\prime}(x)\bigr)^{6}(v(x)^{3}-x)^{2}dx\ \leq\ J(v)

for any
h>0
. Combine (
4.6
), (
4.7
), and (
4.8
), and send
h\rightarrow 0^{+}
to conclude.
∎

We remark that this result is slightly stronger than the one anticipated in
[
21
]
because the convergence of the recovery sequence takes place not only in the weak
W^{1,1}(0,1)
topology but also in the strong
W^{1,p}(0,1)
topology.

We conclude this section by proving the convergence of
the functionals
\{J^{\alpha}_{h}(\cdot)\}_{h>0}
at the corresponding minimum values. If the functionals
\{J^{\alpha}_{h}(\cdot)\}_{h>0}
were equi-coercive in
W^{1+s,p}(0,1)
, then we could immediately conclude that
\overline{u}_{h}\rightarrow\overline{u}
strongly in
W^{1,p}(0,1)
(see
[
17
, Corollary 7.20]
or
[
34
, Theorem 13.3]
). However, this is not the case; instead, we use
J(\overline{u})=0
and show the convergence of the functionals at the minimum values.

Corollary 9

(Convergence of minimum values)
.

Let
0<s<1/3
,
1<p<3/2
be such that
\left(2/3+s\right)p<1
, and
\overline{u}_{h}\in X_{h}
be the solution to problem (
1.7
) for each fixed
h>0
, and let
\overline{u}
be the solution to problem (
1.1
). Then it holds as
h\to 0^{+}
,

(4.9)

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}J^{\alpha}_{h}(\overline{u}_{h})\ =\ J(\overline{u}).

Proof.

By using
\{K_{h}\overline{u}\}_{h>0}
as the recovery sequence associated with
\overline{u}
, along with the minimality of
\{\overline{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}
and the non-negativity of
\{J^{\alpha}_{h}(\cdot)\}_{h>0}
, we have the inequality chain

(4.10)

J(\overline{u})\ \geq\ \underset{h\to 0^{+}}{\mbox{\rm lim\,sup\ }}J^{\alpha}_{h}(K_{h}\overline{u})\ \geq\ \underset{h\to 0^{+}}{\mbox{\rm lim\,inf\ }}J^{\alpha}_{h}(\overline{u}_{h})\ \geq\ 0\ =\ J(\overline{u}),

which completes the proof.
Notice that the last equality in (
4.10
) is only true since
(s+2/3)p<1
.
∎

The next two lemmas comprise an analog of Lemma
6
. Throughout this section, let
K_{h}
be an operator that satisfies properties (
3.4
)–(

Lemma 11
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim h^{1+s-14\beta}\max\bigl\{1,\|v\|^{22}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\bigr\}.

This estimate follows readily from the definition of

∎

Lemma 12
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,

, and suppose

. Let

(5.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(\frac{y}{y-1}\Bigr)^{14}|v|^{\frac{14-3y}{y-1}}[(|v_{h}|^{\frac{y}{y-1}}-x)^{2}-(|v|^{\frac{y}{y-1}}-x)^{2}](\chi^{\beta}_{h}(v_{h})_{x})^{14}dxdy

Once again, we proceed by using the definition of

, the

(see (

This lemma comprises an analog of Lemma

.

Lemma 13
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
0<s<1
and suppose

. Let

(5.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(\frac{y}{y-1}\Bigr)^{14}|v|^{\frac{14-3y}{y-1}}(|v|^{\frac{y}{y-1}}-x)^{2}|(\chi^{\beta}_{h}(v_{h})_{x})^{14}-\chi^{\beta}_{h}(v_{x})^{14}|\,dxdy

This proof follows by using the

Theorem 14
.

Let
0<s<1/3
and

be fixed so that

. Assume that

fixed , we have that

as

in the strong

-topology.

Proof.

To prove (
5.1
), note that since (

) is non-negative and convex in the gradient for each

,
[
35
, Theorem 4.1]
applies to obtain the lim-inf inequality. However, in this special setting, we may instead apply Fatou’s Lemma straight away.

Now to prove (

), fix

, let
v_{h}:=K_{h}v
and
c_{0}:=66(13/14)^{14}
. Then we decompose

as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} F^{\beta}_{h}(v_{h})&:=c_{0}\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(\frac{y}{y-1}\Bigr)^{14}(|v_{h}|^{\frac{14-3y}{y-1}}-|v|^{\frac{14-3y}{y-1}})(|v_{h}|^{\frac{y}{y-1}}-x)^{2}(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{h})_{x})^{14}\,dxdy\\

&\quad+c_{0}\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(\frac{y}{y-1}\Bigr)^{14}|v|^{\frac{14-3y}{y-1}}(|v|^{\frac{y}{y-1}}-x)^{2}\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{x})^{14}\,dxdy.\end{aligned}

The first term on the right-hand side of (

due to Lemma

) converges to

due to Lemma

. As for the third term, we bound it from above as

\displaystyle c_{0}\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(\frac{y}{y-1}\Bigr)^{14}|v|^{\frac{14-3y}{y-1}}(|v|^{\frac{y}{y-1}}-x)^{2}[(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{h})_{x})^{14}-\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{x})^{14}]dxdy\

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c_{0}\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(\frac{y}{y-1}\Bigr)^{14}|v|^{\frac{14-3y}{y-1}}(|v|^{\frac{y}{y-1}}-x)^{2}|(\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{h})_{x})^{14}-\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{x})^{14}|dxdy.

Then the right-hand side of (

) converges to

due to Lemma
13
.

Finally, it is immediate that

(5.8)

c_{0}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{y}{y-1}\right)^{14}|v|^{\frac{14-3y}{y-1}}(|v|^{\frac{y}{y-1}}-x)^{2}\chi^{\alpha}_{h}(v_{x})^{14}dxdy\ \leq\ F(v).

The proof is completed by combining (
5.6
)–(
5.8
) and sending
h\rightarrow 0^{+}
.
∎

.

Corollary 15

(Convergence of minimum values)
.

Let
0<s<1/3
,
1\leq p<3/2
be such that
\left(2/3+s\right)p<1
, and
\overline{v}_{h}\in Y_{h}
be the solution to the problem (

), and let
\overline{v}
be the minimizer of Foss’s functional defined in (

). Then there holds as

,

(5.9)

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}F^{\beta}_{h}(\overline{v}_{h})\ =\ F(\overline{v}).

We note that the thresholds on

and

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we presented two enhanced finite element methods for two calculus of variations problems, namely, Manià’s problem and Foss’s problem, both of which suffer from the Lavrentiev Gap Phenomenon (LGP), and established complete
\Gamma
-convergence proofs for both methods.
To construct the recovery sequence, we used an abstract interpolation-like numerical operator
K_{h}
that needs to satisfy a criterion consisting of certain stability and approximation properties. The convergence proofs may provide a blueprint or framework for developing numerical methods and establishing the
\Gamma
-convergence for other calculus of variations problems with the LGP.

A natural next step is to generalize the described framework to other problems with the Lavrentiev Gap in higher dimensions, such as the elasticity functional described in
[
2
]
, and the two-dimensional version of the Manià functional described in
[
15
]
. One can also try juxtaposing the cutoff functional with non-standard finite element methods, such as Discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods.

Finally, there is the possibility of studying nonlocal analogues of the local functionals with the LGP by choosing these functionals to converge to the corresponding local functional in the sense of Brezis-Bourgain-Mironescu
[
11
]
. We conjecture that using such constructions has the ability to remove the Lavrentiev Gap, enabling the use of standard finite elements for these types of problems.

We provide a proof of the fractional-order differentiability for the minimizer
\overline{v}
of Foss’s problem. We note that the condition
sp<1
used earlier in the paper is less stringent than the conditions required for this result.

Proposition 16
.

The function
\overline{v}(x,y):=x^{\frac{y-1}{y}}
belongs to
W^{1+s,p}(\Omega)
for
1\leq p<\frac{3}{2}
and
s<\frac{1}{p}-\frac{2}{3}
.

Proof.

Fix
0\leq s<1
and
1\leq p<\infty
so that
s(p+2/3)<1
, it suffices to show that the partial derivatives
\overline{v}_{x}
and
\overline{v}_{y}
of
\overline{v}
satisfy the following four claims:

(a)\,\overline{v}_{x}\in L^{p}(\Omega),\quad(b)\,\overline{v}_{x}\in W^{s,p}(\Omega),\quad(c)\,\overline{v}_{y}\in L^{p}(\Omega),\quad(d)\,\overline{v}_{y}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega).

To prove (a), we simply estimate

(.1)

\|\overline{v}_{x}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\ \lesssim\ \int_{\Omega}|x^{-\frac{1}{y}}|^{p}dxdy\ <\ \infty.

To prove (b), we use Fubini’s Theorem and a change of variables to obtain the estimate

(.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|\overline{v}_{x}(x,y)-\overline{v}_{x}(\widetilde{x},y)|^{p}}{|(x,y)-(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})|^{2+sp}}d(x,y)d(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})\\
&\qquad\lesssim\int^{1}_{0}\int^{5/2}_{3/2}\int^{1}_{0}\frac{|x^{-1/y}-\widetilde{x}^{-1/y}|^{p}}{|x-\widetilde{x}|^{1+sp}}d\widetilde{x}dydx\\
&\qquad\leq\int^{1}_{0}\int^{1}_{0}\frac{|x^{-2/3}-\widetilde{x}^{-2/3}|^{p}}{|x-\widetilde{x}|^{1+sp}}d\widetilde{x}dx\ <\ \infty,\end{aligned}

where we have used that
x^{-2/3}\in W^{s,p}(0,1)
when
s<\frac{1}{p}-\frac{2}{3}
. We also require a second estimate, following from the Mean-Value Theorem:

(.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|\overline{v}_{x}(\widetilde{x},y)-\overline{v}_{x}(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})|^{p}}{|(x,y)-(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})|^{2+sp}}d(x,y)d(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})\\
&\qquad\lesssim\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|\ln(\widetilde{x})|^{p}\widetilde{x}^{-2p/3}|y-\widetilde{y}|^{p}}{|(x,y)-(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})|^{2+sp}}d(x,y)d(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})\\
&\qquad\leq\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|\ln(\widetilde{x})|^{p}\widetilde{x}^{-2p/3}|y-\widetilde{y}|^{p}}{|y-\widetilde{y}|^{2+sp}}d(x,y)d(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})\\
&\qquad=\left(\int^{1}_{0}|\ln(\widetilde{x})|^{p}\widetilde{x}^{-2p/3}dx\right)\left(\int^{5/2}_{3/2}\int^{5/2}_{3/2}\frac{1}{|y-\widetilde{y}|^{2+(s-1)p}}d\widetilde{y}dy\right)\ <\ \infty.\end{aligned}

Finally, combining (
.2
) and (
.3
), we obtain

(.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {}^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}&\lesssim\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|\overline{v}_{x}(x,y)-\overline{v}_{x}(\widetilde{x},y)|^{p}}{|(x,y)-(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})|^{2+sp}}d(x,y)d(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})\\
&\qquad+\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|\overline{v}_{x}(\widetilde{x},y)-\overline{v}_{x}(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})|^{p}}{|(x,y)-(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})|^{2+sp}}d(x,y)d(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})\ <\ \infty.\end{aligned}

Thus, (b) holds.

Assertion (c) follows from the integrability of any positive power of the natural logarithm function in 1D and
x^{\frac{y-1}{y}}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Finally, assertion (d) immediately follows from the following calculations:

\overline{v}_{yx}(x,y)=\frac{2x^{-\frac{1}{y}}y-\ln(x)x^{-\frac{1}{y}}}{y^{3}},\qquad\overline{v}_{yy}(x,y)=\frac{\ln(x)^{2}x^{\frac{y-1}{y}}}{y^{4}}-\frac{2\ln(x)x^{\frac{y-1}{y}}}{y^{3}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

ROV dynamic modeling and grasping algorithm for underwater control system of marine oil and gas
Authors: Yuhang Li, Sunwei Li, Yuanzhe Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Deepwater oil exhibits great difficulty and risk in extraction under complex marine environments and strong ocean currents, and the operation and maintenance of its underwater control system rely heavily on remote operation. Traditional control methods make it hard to satisfy the demands of extraction. Therefore, the study first proposes simulating the marine working environment using virtual technology and model the Remote Operated Vehicle (ROV) to optimize power allocation. Secondly, the robot grasping task is achieved by designing binocular vision stereo image matching and improving the Proximal Policy Optimization grasping algorithm to enhance its stability and operational success rate under turbulent disturbances. Finally, an underwater production simulation system is built to provide a virtualization platform for oil and gas development operations. The results show that ROV can effectively achieve propeller power distribution, and the amplitude error under different operating conditions is reduced by an average of about 25% compared to the traditional Saab Seaeye model, with a maximum of no more than 5%. The simulation effect is significant. And the robot grasping system designed by the research institute can autonomously complete tasks. After training, the positioning error is reduced by 41.6% and 74.7% compared to the Mask Region based Convolutional Neural Network (Mask R-CNN) algorithm and You Only Look Once algorithm, respectively, reaching a final height of 0.11 m. The grasping success rate is still better than the comparative algorithm by more than 10% and more than 90% in strong flow environments, and it takes less time to improve overall performance significantly. The research method can provide a guarantee for the safety of deepwater operations in oil and gas fields, and reduce operation and maintenance costs and mining difficulties.

Introduction

With the decreasing global marine oil and gas resources, deep-sea oil and gas exploration and development has become a key strategy to ensure energy security. However, the underwater environment with a depth exceeding 1000 m has extreme characteristics such as high pressure and low temperature, and traditional artificial diving operations can no longer meet the demand. At present, although long short-term memory network data-driven models can learn motion temporal features, they ignore the essence of fluid mechanics and have poor generalization. The reinforcement learning grasping algorithm has high computational efficiency but lacks underwater real interaction samples for simulation training. In this context, Remote Operated Vehicles (ROVs) have become indispensable executors for underwater operations and maintenance due to their strong high maneuverability and adaptability1,2. ROV power modeling can achieve precise pose control under the goal of minimizing energy consumption (EC) by constructing a six-degree-of-freedom motion equation and introducing the pseudo-inverse method to optimize the thrust distribution of the thruster3. The binocular vision stereo matching network enhances underwater feature extraction by combining attention mechanisms, while the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm is simpler to implement compared to trust region policy optimization algorithms and more stable than maximum entropy methods such as flexible action evaluation algorithms under limited sample conditions. And it can effectively avoid training collapse by updating the amplitude through constraint strategies, and is generally superior to traditional gradient methods in terms of average reward and convergence stability. In some benchmark tasks, the final performance is improved by about 10% − 25%, which can better solve the problems of slip and instability in dynamic target grasping4,5. Therefore, the study integrates ROV dynamic modeling with the PPO algorithm to achieve efficient and safe underwater grasping tasks. The innovations of the study are as follows: Firstly, the high-precision localization control of underwater ROV is realized by constructing an accurate dynamics model. Secondly, a combinatorial grasping algorithm is constructed, in which the residual network is trained by training a convolutional network to avoid the gradient of the network model from disappearing or generating explosions. The PPO algorithm achieves grasping control optimization by integrating the dominant value standardization strategy. The strategy entropy used by the supplement optimizes the algorithm globally to avoid the algorithm training falling into a local optimum. A binocular vision-stereo matching network is also introduced to further enhance the ROV’s movement prediction capability.

Related work

As technology advances, ROV has been broadly studied by scholars all over the world. Guo Y et al. proposed a finite element numerical simulation method to study the significant nonlinear characteristics of the dynamic response of ROV systems. During the process, the hydrodynamic forces related to the ROV motion were calculated to verify the dynamic response of the system. The results indicated that it could deepen our understanding of the nonlinear behavior of ROVs6. Øveraas H et al. designed a dynamic positioning controller to solve the issue of cumbersome operation in traditional ROVs. During the process, model predictive control technology and short-term wave motion prediction technology were introduced. The results indicated that the controller could reduce the average error by over 60%7. The Zhang Y team proposed an adaptive method for real-time estimation of noise to address the issue of ROVs prone to location faults. During the process, measurement residuals were used to detect and locate faults. The results indicated that this method could provide highly robust and smooth localization results8. The Farooq F team proposed a nonlinear observer model for the control and stability issues of ROVs. During the process, integral and differential controllers were also introduced to calculate the six degrees of freedom of the ROV. The findings indicated that the nonlinear observer model had high control accuracy and effectiveness9. The Morinaga A team proposed an autonomous surface vehicle to address the difficulty of underwater communication for ROVs. During the process, the aircraft and underwater positioning device achieved collaborative work with the ROV. The results indicated that the collaborative system could effectively expand the operating range of ROV10.

The related technologies and methods of machine grasping control have also received more research. Li Y et al. proposed an underwater friction electric hand palm tactile sensor to solve the problem of insufficient accuracy of traditional underwater grippers. During the process, a flexible cover and sensing unit were applied to convert the applied force into an electrical signal. The results indicated that the sensor could effectively improve the grasping accuracy of the gripper11. Sun Y et al. proposed an underwater vibration adhesion mechanism to address the lack of research on underwater adsorption in the field of underwater grasping. During the process, the viscosity of the liquid was used to pull the surface of the object at a certain distance, and the adhesion effect was controlled by frequency. The results indicated that this adhesion mechanism provided new ideas for the field of underwater grasping12. The Hu Z team proposed a reinforcement learning-based algorithm to address the issue of traditional algorithms encountering difficulties in minimizing contact force. During the process, the antagonistic behavior of the living object was encoded as a reward function and it was used to train the grasping strategy. The results indicated that the algorithm could significantly reduce the contact force applied to living objects13. The Jiang D team developed a training optimization algorithm to deal with the issue of the need for a large amount of motion data training for robot grasping control. During the process, a large amount of motion data was trained through generative adversarial imitation learning. The results indicated that the algorithm completed assembly tasks significantly faster than scripts and keyboard operations14. Bai J et al. designed a model-free method for designing bionic hand controllers using reinforcement learning to solve the issue of the grasping control of bionic hands being easily affected by changing control parameters. During the process, the manual motion decomposition technique was used to learn complex movements from simple movements. The results indicated that this method had fast learning ability15.

In summary, although existing research has made some progress in ROV dynamics or grasping control, it often only focuses on a single technology (such as dynamic control or grasping algorithm), making it difficult to balance accuracy and robustness. In addition, grasping algorithms often rely on a large amount of data support, which limits their performance in complex underwater environments. In contrast, this study achieved a systematic breakthrough by combining high-precision ROV dynamic modeling with an improved PPO visual grasping algorithm. The contribution of this study lies in its significant superiority over traditional methods such as Mask R-CNN and YOLO in key indicators such as positioning error (0.11 m) and grasping success rate under strong currents (> 90%). At the same time, it reduces reliance on training data, improves algorithm convergence speed and anti-interference ability, and provides a more competitive solution for reliable and autonomous oil and gas operation and maintenance operations in deep-sea environments.

To ensure the efficiency and stability of the ROV grasping operation, the study attempts to combine residual network, PPO algorithm, strategy entropy, and binocular vision stereo matching network to construct a grasping algorithm, which can achieve precise control of underwater grasping20. Firstly, the 3D feature map is input, the feature map is subjected to maximum pooling and average pooling simultaneously, and the pooling results are input into the fully connected layer of shared weights to generate the channel weight vectors. Experimental setup

Simulation environment

To assess the performance of ROV dynamic modeling and grasping algorithm for marine oil and gas underwater control system, Simulink was used as the hydrodynamic module, ANSYS Fluent was used for fluid dynamics verification, and the UMI underwater grasping dataset was selected as the test data source. Results

Performance under rated conditions

To test the performance of the research algorithm, the grasping algorithm was referred to as Visual Feedback Grabbing (VFP) algorithm, and its operational accuracy was compared with Mask Region-based Convolutional Neural Network (Mask R-CNN) algorithm and You Only Look Once (YOLO) algorithm. The Mask R-CNN algorithm, as an advanced algorithm that can provide pixel level accurate segmentation, is used as a benchmark to test the impact of fine segmentation ability on the accuracy of grasping and positioning26. The YOLO algorithm, as a single-stage object detection algorithm known for its fast speed, serves as a benchmark for comparison and represents a typical level of positioning accuracy under high-speed inference27. The study uses the positioning error of the end effector and the slip of the target object as indicators, and the training ratio as experimental parameters. The training data includes a sequence of stereo image pairs collected by a binocular vision system, the pose of the end effector of the robotic arm recorded and annotated by sensors, and corresponding successful/failed grasping action labels. The comparative results indicate that the research method achieves a better balance between computational efficiency, tracking accuracy, and robustness. Although MPC has the highest control accuracy, its complex online optimization problem results in a single step computation time of up to 25 ms, making it difficult to meet the real-time requirements of high-speed ROV systems (> 100 Hz). Although ASMC can effectively suppress disturbances, its inherent chattering phenomenon will exacerbate actuator wear, and the control error (~ 0.18 m) in high-frequency dynamic environments is greater than that of our research method. In contrast, this study is based on the pseudo inverse method for thrust allocation and PPO strategy collaborative optimization, with a single step computation time of only 2.1 ms (complexity level 1). It not only meets real-time control requirements, but also maintains low amplitude errors (< 5%) and high-precision grasping and positioning capabilities (0.11 m) under different operating conditions, demonstrating its significant advantages in practical engineering applications.

Discussion

Engineering significance

This research has solved the core problem of “inaccurate control and unstable grasping” in deep-sea oil and gas operation and maintenance. Through ROV dynamic modeling, precise positioning of ROV underwater has been achieved, which improves the stability of underwater movement under strong environmental interference and can meet the long-term high load operation requirements underwater. By integrating binocular vision and PPO algorithm, and optimizing control strategies, precise grasping control at the millimeter level has been achieved, ultimately improving China’s ability to operate and maintain deepwater oil and gas fields independently, and effectively avoiding millions of production losses.

Limitations and future improvements

Although the ROV power model already has high positioning accuracy, it has not taken into account the influence of real underwater environment on model parameters. In the future, efforts will be made to introduce adaptive observers to evaluate changes in model parameters and further improve the accuracy of ROV power modeling. At the same time, grasping algorithms also face challenges, as they rely on a large amount of underwater interactive data, resulting in high training costs and directly affecting the efficiency of algorithm iterations. In the future, high-level planning task sequences will be introduced, using low-level execution actions to improve algorithm training efficiency.

Conclusion

To further promote the success rate and efficiency of ROV underwater grasping control tasks in marine oil and gas environments, innovative research was conducted to integrate ROV dynamic modeling and grasping algorithms. Among them, ROV built an accurate mathematical model by calculating six degrees of freedom, and used the pseudo inverse method for thrust allocation to achieve precise control of ROV. The binocular vision network was used to generate depth sequences, and the PPO algorithm was used to train the grasping network to achieve precise operation of the robotic arm. The experimental results showed that the ROV dynamic model converged 18 times when the data complexity level was 1. When the training ratio was only 10%, the fault recovery time was 3.42 s. In practical application, the PPO algorithm had a maximum grasping time of 78.2 s when the movement speed reached 0.5 m/s. After 53 iterations of movement training, the reward value reached its maximum. The results indicate that the ROV dynamic modeling and grasping algorithm used in the study has higher computational efficiency and robustness, and can achieve efficient and safe underwater grasping operations.

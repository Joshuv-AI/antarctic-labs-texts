Abstract

We prove a square-root space simulation for deterministic multitape Turing machines, showing
\mathrm{TIME}[t]\subseteq\mathrm{SPACE}[O(\sqrt{t})]

measured in tape cells over a fixed finite alphabet
. The key step is a Height Compression Theorem that uniformly (and in logspace) reshapes the canonical left-deep succinct computation tree for a block-respecting run into a binary tree whose evaluation-stack depth along any DFS path is
O(\log T)
for
T=\lceil t/b\rceil
, while preserving
O(b)
workspace at leaves and
O(1)
at internal nodes. Edges have
addressing/topology
checkable in
O(\log t)
space, and
semantic
correctness across merges is witnessed by an exact
O(b)
bounded-window replay at the unique interface. Algorithmically, an Algebraic Replay Engine with constant-degree maps over a constant-size field, together with pointerless DFS and index-free streaming, ensures constant-size per-level tokens and eliminates wide counters, yielding the additive tradeoff
S(b)=O(b+t/b)
. Choosing
b=\Theta(\sqrt{t})
gives
O(\sqrt{t})
space with no residual multiplicative polylog factors. The construction is uniform, relativizes, and is robust to standard model choices. Consequences include branching-program upper bounds
2^{O(\sqrt{s})}
for size-
s
bounded-fan-in circuits, tightened quadratic-time lower bounds for
\mathrm{SPACE}[n]
-complete problems via the standard hierarchy argument, and
O(\sqrt{t})
-space certifying interpreters; under explicit locality assumptions, the framework extends to geometric
d
-dimensional models. Conceptually, the work isolates path bookkeeping as the chief obstruction to
O(\sqrt{t})
and removes it via structural height compression with per-path analysis rather than barrier-prone techniques.

Introduction

1.1
Problem statement and context

A prominent question in complexity theory asks:
how little workspace suffices to deterministically simulate a time-
t
computation?
For classical multitape Turing machines, the best general upper bounds have long taken the form of a tradeoff in which one chooses a block size
b
and pays (i)
O(b)
space to locally “replay” a block and (ii) an additional term that accounts for the
evaluation stack depth
of an implicit computation tree and the
bookkeeping
needed to navigate it. Space throughout is measured in
tape cells
over a fixed finite alphabet.

Recent progress has established the inclusion

\mathrm{TIME}[t]\ \subseteq\ \mathrm{SPACE}\!\left[O\!\left(\sqrt{t\log t}\right)\right],

by reducing a time-
t
run to a
Tree Evaluation
instance of height
\Theta(t/b)
with
O(b)
-sized per-node functions and then evaluating that tree in small space using algebraic techniques
[
1
]
. (Here and throughout, our trees are the
succinct computation trees induced by block-respecting simulations
, rather than arbitrary tree-evaluation instances.) The residual
\sqrt{\log t}
factor arises from
path bookkeeping
: during depth-first evaluation, the simulator must store a descriptor for each level of a root–leaf walk across
\Theta(t/b)
levels, where per-level choices (child addressing, offset encoding within size-
b
windows) require
O(\log b)
bits, leading to total path storage of
O((t/b)\cdot\log b)
and optimization giving
O(\sqrt{t\log t})
.

This work removes the lingering
\sqrt{\log t}
by
compressing the evaluation stack depth itself
and redesigning the evaluator so that per-level overhead is constant, achieving
\mathrm{SPACE}[O(\sqrt{t})]
.

1.2
Central theorem and thesis of the paper

Definition 1

(Evaluation stack depth)
.

Let
H_{\mathrm{eval}}
denote the
evaluation stack depth
: the maximum number of per-level tokens simultaneously stored by the evaluator’s stack along any root–leaf traversal under a specified evaluation schedule.

Central Theorem (proved herein).

The succinct computation tree associated with a time-
t
block-respecting simulation admits a uniform, logspace-computable transform of both the tree structure and evaluation schedule that yields
H^{\prime}_{\mathrm{eval}}=O\!\big(\log T\big)
, where
T=\lceil t/b\rceil
, while preserving (i)
O(b)
-cell leaf replay cost, (ii)
addressing/topology
checkable in
O(\log t)
space (endpoints, adjacency), with
semantic
correctness certified by an exact
O(b)
bounded-window replay at the unique interface, and (iii) associativity of merge operations.

Together with a sharpened algebraic evaluator that leverages the compressed evaluation depth and streamed progress, this yields the additive tradeoff

\mathrm{TIME}[t]\ \subseteq\ \mathrm{SPACE}\!\left[\,O\!\big(b\ +\ t/b\big)\right]\quad\text{for all}\quad b\in[1,t].

(1)

We adopt the canonical choice
b=\Theta(\sqrt{t})
henceforth.

Contributions

•

1.4
Why this, why now

The prior
\tilde{O}(\sqrt{t})
upper bound is structurally limited by path bookkeeping. The present approach targets the
structure
responsible for this cost—evaluation stack depth—using midpoint recursion (balanced splits), pebbling-style potentials, and indegree capping. These ingredients are combinatorial and plausibly barrier-free; modest progress on stack depth compression cascades into immediate improvements across multiple corollaries.

2.1
Machines, time blocking, and succinct computation trees

Let
M
be a deterministic multitape Turing machine with
\tau
work tapes over alphabet
\Gamma
. A
configuration
consists of control state, head positions, and tape contents. Fix a block size
b\geq 1
and partition the first
t
steps into

T\ \coloneqq\ \left\lceil\frac{t}{b}\right\rceil

(2)

time blocks

B_{1},\dots,B_{T}
, where
B_{k}
simulates steps
[(k{-}1)b{+}1,\ \min\{kb,t\}]
. Each block touches at most
b
cells per tape, so its
local window
per tape is an interval of length
\leq b
.

The
per-block summary

\sigma_{k}
records exactly: (i) entering/leaving control state, (ii) entering/leaving head positions for each tape, (iii) local window boundaries and relative head positions within windows, (iv) a deterministic
movement log
encoding all tape modifications as a sequence of at most
b
edits with offsets relative to the window, and (v) constant-size fingerprints for each local window before and after the block (used only as advisory tags; correctness always follows from exact bounded-window replay at interfaces). The movement log uses deterministic, collision-free encoding requiring
O(b)
cells total. See Appendix A for complete formal definitions.

For an interval
I=[i,j]\subseteq[1,T]
, the
interval summary

\Sigma(I)
is obtained by composing the block summaries
\sigma_{i},\dots,\sigma_{j}
and verifying exact consistency at each interface between adjacent blocks; composition requires only bounded-window replays and deterministic state checks. The
succinct computation tree

\mathcal{T}
used for the canonical tradeoff is the
left-deep (unbalanced) binary interval tree
on
[1,T]
: its leaves are the unit intervals
[k,k]
(labeled by
\sigma_{k}
), and each internal node labeled by
I=[i,j]
stores (or recomputes)
\Sigma(I)
from its two children via the associative merge operator
\oplus
. This tree has height
\Theta(T)
.

1

1

For comparison, the balanced interval tree on
[1,T]
has height
\Theta(\log T)
. We fix the left-deep tree here to expose the path-bookkeeping term that underlies the classical

Why a balanced tree wasn’t previously enough.

A balanced interval tree on
[1,T]
has recursion depth
\Theta(\log T)

classical

\tilde{O}(\sqrt{t})

\Theta(T)

\Theta(\log b)

\Theta((t/b)\log b)

\sqrt{\log t}
slack after optimizing

\Omega(\log b)
per-level descriptors. The present construction both (i) compresses the evaluation depth to
O(\log T)

and
(ii) makes the per-level token size
O(1)

\log b

2.2
Space accounting: the canonical tradeoff

Definition 2

(Canonical evaluation schedule)
.

The
canonical evaluation schedule
performs depth-first traversal of the left-deep evaluation tree
\mathcal{T}
, storing one addressing token per tree level to enable parent-child navigation and merge verification.

Let
U
bound the description size of the per-node predicates (merge predicates and child-addressing rules). Under the canonical schedule:

Lemma 1

(Canonical stack depth)
.

The canonical evaluation schedule requires
H_{\mathrm{eval}}=\Theta(T)=\Theta(t/b)
stack levels.

Lemma 2

(Token size bound)
.

Under the canonical schedule,
\log U=\Theta(\log b)
due to the need to encode offsets within size-
b
windows and child-addressing predicates.

A standard depth-first evaluator incurs:

S(b)\ =\ O\!\left(b\right)\ +\ O\!\left(H_{\mathrm{eval}}\cdot\log U\right).

(3)

Here
O(b)
accounts for the
leaf window replay
, and the
O(H_{\mathrm{eval}}\log U)
term is the
path bookkeeping
: we must remember, for each level, enough information to locate/verify the next child and to recompute the parent. Substituting the bounds from the lemmas:

S(b)\ =\ O\!\big(b\big)\ +\ O\!\Big(\frac{t}{b}\cdot\log b\Big).

(4)

Optimizing this expression gives the well-known
O\!\big(\sqrt{t\log t}\big)
bound. This work removes the multiplicative
\log b
by redesigning the evaluator so that per-level tokens are
O(1)
and by scheduling merges so that only
O(\log T)
interfaces are live at once while still processing
\Theta(T)
merges. Thus the bookkeeping term becomes
O(T)=O(t/b)
, yielding

S(b)\ =\ O(b)\ +\ O(t/b),

which optimizes at
b=\Theta(\sqrt{t})
to
S=O(\sqrt{t})
.

2.3
Algebraic evaluation and low-degree extensions

To keep internal‑node workspace constant, we express each merge via a
constant‑degree
vector‑valued polynomial over a
constant‑size
finite field, but
only for the finite‑state part
of an interval summary. Concretely, let
\pi(\Sigma(I))
denote the constant‑size projection of the interval summary
\Sigma(I)
that includes finite control state and a constant number of fixed‑size tags/flags (e.g., interface check bits). We define a map
F_{I}:\mathbb{F}^{m}\to\mathbb{F}^{q}
with
m,q,\Delta=O(1)
such that
F_{I}
algebraically represents
\pi(\Sigma(I))
. All
b
‑dependent quantities (head offsets, addresses, and window contents) are
handled exclusively by exact bounded‑window replay
and are never encoded as field elements. Correctness never depends on hash collision probabilities; interface equality is verified by exact replay at the unique junction.

Leaves compute
F_{[k,k]}
by scanning their
O(b)
-cell windows once; internal nodes satisfy

F_{J\cup J^{\prime}}(x)\ =\ G\big(F_{J}(Ax),\,F_{J^{\prime}}(Bx),\,x\big),

(5)

for affine maps
A,B
and a constant-degree combiner
G
that are fixed independently of
t
and
b
(they depend only on the constant-size predicate template). Evaluating along a root–leaf path then requires (i)
O(1)
accumulators per internal level and (ii)
O(b)
workspace when visiting a leaf. The total space becomes
additive
in (leaf window)
+
(number of levels), provided path bookkeeping is
O(1)
per level—precisely what stack depth compression enables.

2.4
Pebbling preliminaries

We will use classical
black–white pebbling
,
potential functions
, and
centroid/cut decompositions
to control recursion depth and the number of simultaneously active interfaces.

Definition 3

(Active interfaces and stack discipline)
.

An
active interface
is the boundary state at the endpoint of a currently processed chunk during evaluation. The
potential

\Phi
measures the total “weight” of all active interfaces. The
stack discipline
maintains these interfaces according to the evaluation schedule.

At a high level:

•

Midpoint (balanced) recursion.
Every interval
[i,j]
is split at
m=\lfloor(i{+}j)/2\rfloor
into
[i,m]
and
[m{+}1,j]
, giving geometric shrink
\ell\mapsto\lceil\ell/2\rceil
and recursion depth
O(\log T)
.

•

Per-path potential.
A logarithmic potential
\Phi
over active interfaces ensures that at most
O(\log T)
interfaces are simultaneously materialized along any DFS path (metadata only), independent of
b
.

•

Indegree capping (for completeness).
Any constant fan-in
>2
can be replaced by a balanced binary combiner tree in logspace, adding only
O(1)
local depth; under midpoint recursion this step is vacuous.

These ingredients together produce a binary evaluation tree with
H^{\prime}_{\mathrm{eval}}=O(\log T)
and
O(1)
per-level tokens, yielding the
O\!\big(b+t/b\big)
space bound (with only an additive
O(\log(t/b))
addressing scratch), optimized at
b=\Theta(\sqrt{t})
.

The Height Compression Theorem (HCT)

In this section we give a complete construction and analysis of the height–compression transform that converts the canonical succinct computation tree
\mathcal{T}
of a time-
t
block-respecting simulation into a binary tree
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
whose evaluation stack depth is
logarithmic
while keeping per-node workspace and edge verifiability under tight control.

3.1
Setup and canonical succinct tree

Fix a multitape TM
M
and a block size
b\in[1,t]
. Partition the
t
-step run into
T\coloneqq\lceil t/b\rceil

time blocks
, where block
k\in\{1,\dots,T\}
simulates steps
[(k-1)b+1,\,\min\{kb,t\}]
.
For each block we define a
summary

\sigma_{k}
consisting of:

•

the machine control state at the beginning and end of the block,

•

the input-tape head position and the work-tape head positions at the beginning and end of the block,

•

the
movement log
restricted to the window of cells touched by the block (encoded as bounded-arity edits),

•

a constant-size fingerprint/tag for each local window used only as advisory metadata for overlap checks (semantic correctness always follows from exact bounded-window replay).

Given an interval
I=[i,j]\subseteq[1,T]
, its summary
\Sigma(I)
is defined as the unique value obtained by composing the block summaries
\sigma_{i},\dots,\sigma_{j}
and verifying local consistency at the
j-i
interfaces. The
canonical
succinct computation tree
\mathcal{T}
is the
left-deep (unbalanced) binary interval tree
on
[1,T]
whose leaves are unit intervals
[k,k]
and each internal node labeled with
I=[i,j]
stores (or can compute)
\Sigma(I)
from its children
[i,j\!-\!1]
and
[j,j]
. This tree has height
\Theta(T)
and exposes the well-known path-bookkeeping term underlying the classical tradeoff.
2
2

2

A balanced interval tree on
[1,T]
has height
\Theta(\log T)
. We fix the left-deep tree here to reflect the canonical schedule that yields
O\!\big(b+(t/b)\log b\big)
space prior to height compression.
As usual, the space to evaluate
\mathcal{T}
along a root–leaf path is

S_{\mathrm{canon}}(b)\ =\ O\!\left(b\right)\ +\ O\!\left(H_{\mathrm{eval}}\cdot\log U\right),

(6)

where
H_{\mathrm{eval}}=\Theta(T)
is the evaluation stack depth and
U
bounds the description size of per-node predicates (dominated by
\Theta(\log b)
, due to head-offset and fingerprint/tag widths (advisory)). The
O(H_{\mathrm{eval}}\log U)
term reflects bookkeeping for the path descriptor.
Terminology.
We distinguish
recursion/tree depth
(the number of refinement levels) from
evaluation stack depth
(the number of simultaneously stored per-level tokens under DFS). These bounds target the stack height.

Our goal is to uniformly transform
\mathcal{T}
into a binary tree
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
with evaluation stack depth
H^{\prime}_{\mathrm{eval}}=O(\log T)=O(\log(t/b))

without
increasing the per-node workspace beyond
O(b)
at leaves and
O(1)
at internal nodes, and while keeping the edge relation logspace-checkable.

Theorem 1

(Height Compression Theorem)
.

Input:
A multitape TM
M
running in time
t
, block size
b\in[1,t]
, and the associated canonical succinct computation tree
\mathcal{T}
with evaluation schedule.

Output:
A binary evaluation tree
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
with evaluation schedule such that:

1.

At most
O\!\big(\log T\big)=O\!\big(\log(t/b)\big)

interfaces are simultaneously active
along any root-to-leaf traversal (recursion depth is
O(\log T)
via midpoint splits),

2.

Per-node workspace
W=O(b)
at leaves and
O(1)
at internal nodes,

3.

addressing/topology
checkable in
O(\log t)
space (endpoints, adjacency), with

correctness certified by exact
O(b)
bounded-window replay;
semantic
merge verification uses an exact
O(b)
-window replay at the unique interface,

4.

The root of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}

\mathcal{T}
.

Properties:
The transform is uniform and logspace-computable, and relativizes to oracle machines.

Non-collapse note.

The height compression caps the number of
simultaneously active interfaces
at
O(\log T)
and keeps internal-node workspace
O(1)
. This structural bound does not by itself imply an
O(\log T)
-space simulation: the evaluator still processes
\Theta(T)
merges under a streaming DFS schedule and does not cache intermediate summaries beyond the active frontier. Consequently, the overall space bound remains
O(b)+O(t/b)
once per-level descriptor width is reduced to

(see Section

Proof roadmap.

We build
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
by composing three ingredients:

1.

Balanced (centroid) recursion.
Replace the left-deep shape by a balanced interval decomposition in which each internal interval
[i,j]
splits at its midpoint
m=\lfloor(i{+}j)/2\rfloor
into
two
children
[i,m]
and
[m{+}1,j]
. This yields geometric shrink
\ell\mapsto\lceil\ell/2\rceil
along any root–leaf path and therefore depth

.

2.

Potential-guided pebbling.
Define a potential
\Phi
over active boundaries; a telescoping analysis shows
\Phi
remains
O(\log T)
along any traversal, so only
O(\log T)
interfaces are ever simultaneously materialized (as constant-size boundary metadata).

3.

Binary combiner implementation.
Implement each internal merge as a binary
combiner
with logspace-checkable edges and
O(b)
local replay; since fan-in is already
2
, no additional binaryization is needed.

3.2
Key structural lemmas

Lemma 3

(Balanced schedule with geometric shrink)
.

There exists a uniform, logspace-computable decomposition of the
T
blocks into a rooted recursion tree
\mathcal{R}
whose nodes are disjoint intervals of
[1,T]
with the following properties:

1.

Each internal interval
I=[i,j]
of length
\ell=j-i+1
has exactly two children, the left and right halves
[i,m]
and
[m{+}1,j]
where
m=\lfloor(i{+}j)/2\rfloor
.

2.

Along any root–leaf path, if
\ell(d)
denotes the active interval length at depth
d
, then for all
d

\ell(d{+}1)\ \leq\ \left\lceil\frac{\ell(d)}{2}\right\rceil,

and therefore the depth of
\mathcal{R}
is
O(\log T)
.

3.

Given
(i,j)
, the child endpoints and midpoint are computable in
O(\log T)
space.

Lemma 4

(Binaryization is vacuous)
.

In the midpoint recursion of Lemma
3
, each node already has fan-in two; thus the binaryization step is vacuous. For completeness, replacing any constant-fan-in variant by a balanced binary decision structure would add only
O(1)
local depth and is computable in
O(\log T)
space from
(i,j)
.

Per-traversal invariant:
Along any root-to-leaf path in
\mathcal{R}
, interval lengths shrink by at least a factor of
2
per level, so the DFS stack height is
O(\log T)
.

Logspace computability:
Given
(i,j)
, the midpoint and both children are computable in
O(\log T)

Proof of Lemma
3
.

Immediate from the definition of the midpoint split:
\ell(d{+}1)\leq\lceil\ell(d)/2\rceil
and hence the number of levels until
\ell=1
is
O(\log T)
. All arithmetic on indices uses counters up to
T
and is uniform logspace.
∎

Lemma 5

(Per-path active-interface bound)
.

Under the balanced schedule and left-first DFS, along any root–leaf traversal the number of simultaneously active boundaries (hence the size of the live frontier) is
O(\log T)
.

Proof.

Geometric shrink implies that at depth
d
the active interval has length at most
\lceil T/2^{d}\rceil
. The traversal can maintain at most one active interval per depth, yielding stack height
\leq\lceil\log_{2}T\rceil+O(1)=O(\log T)
.
∎

Definition 4

(Active boundaries and per-path stack discipline)
.

During a recursive evaluation over
\mathcal{R}
, an
active boundary
is the interface state (control state, head positions, and fingerprints/tags) at the left or right endpoint of a currently active interval along the current DFS path. The evaluator maintains a
stack
of active boundaries reflecting the current root-to-leaf traversal.

Lemma 6

(Per-path pebbling potential bound)
.

There exists a potential function
\Phi
on the multiset of active boundaries such that along any root-to-leaf traversal of
\mathcal{R}
:

1.

\Phi
starts at
O(\log T)
, never increases during backtracking, and drops by a constant amount every constant number of levels,

2.

the number of simultaneously active boundaries along the path is
O(\Phi)=O(\log T)
,

3.

Window materialization bound:
although
O(\log T)
boundaries may be active as metadata, at most a constant number of
O(b)
windows (indeed, one in our schedule) are ever materialized concurrently at any time; hence the additional workspace due to windows is
O(b)
, with only
O(\log T)
extra cells for boundary metadata.

Proof.

For an interval of length
\ell
, assign weight
w(\ell)\coloneqq\lceil\log_{2}(1+\ell)\rceil
. For a fixed root-to-leaf path with active boundaries
\mathcal{B}
, define
\Phi=\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}w(\mathrm{len}(B))
. At the root, there are two boundaries of length
T
, so
\Phi=O(\log T)
. Each level halves the active interval length up to additive
1
, so
w
decreases by at least
1
after a constant number of levels; backtracking removes boundaries and cannot increase
\Phi
. The interface count is at most a constant multiple of
\Phi
. Only the boundary
metadata
(state/positions/check tags) for these interfaces are retained; the full
O(b)
windows are re-materialized on demand and never for more than a constant number of interfaces simultaneously under DFS, giving the stated workspace bound.
∎

Lemma 7

(Binary combiner gadget)
.

Combiner gadget:
Each internal merge is implemented by a constant-workspace binary
combiner
that:

•

Takes two adjacent child summaries and
verifies interface consistency via a bounded-window replay
(exact equality of control state and head positions at the junction; fingerprints/tags are advisory only and not relied upon for soundness),

•

Performs the bounded-window replay using
O(b)
cells plus
O(\log t)
scratch for indices,

•

Provides addressing/topology predicates checkable in
O(\log t)
space (endpoints, adjacency), while
semantic
merge correctness is verified by an exact
O(b)
-window replay at the unique interface.

Depth analysis:

In this section we give a complete proof of Theorem

7.1
Canonical recursion via midpoint recursion with exact constants

Intervals and midpoints.

For an interval
I=[i,j]\subseteq[1,T]
of length

c(I)\ \coloneqq\ \Big\lfloor\frac{i+j}{2}\Big\rfloor.

(15)

We

exactly at the midpoint
into its two children

and

Lemma 12

(Exact midpoint shrink and depth)
.

For every root-to-leaf path under midpoint recursion, if
\ell(d)
denotes the active-interval length at depth

\ell(d{+}1)\ \leq\ \Big\lceil\frac{\ell(d)}{2}\Big\rceil,

and the recursion depth is at most
\lceil\log_{2}T\rceil

Proof.

Immediate from the definition of the midpoint split. Each step halves the length up to an additive

\lceil\log_{2}T\rceil
levels the length becomes
1
.
∎

Given
(i,j)
in binary, a logspace transducer computes
c(I)=\lfloor(i{+}j)/2\rfloor
and outputs the two child endpoints using
O(\log T)
counters. Hence the schedule is uniform and logspace.

Lemma 13

(Per-path stack-height and window usage)
.

Under the midpoint recursion and DFS, the number of simultaneously active intervals (hence the evaluation stack depth) along any root-to-leaf traversal is

Proof.

7.2
Per-path potential design with rigorous telescoping analysis

DFS stack discipline and active interfaces.

During a depth-first evaluation along any fixed root-to-leaf path, an
active interface
is the boundary state (control, head positions, and constant-size fingerprints/tags used only as advisory metadata; semantic correctness always follows from exact bounded-window replay) at the left or right endpoint of a currently active interval on the DFS stack. The evaluator maintains a stack of active interfaces reflecting the current path, with at most two interfaces per active interval.

Potential function.

For an interval (or boundary) of length
\lambda
, define weight
w(\lambda)\coloneqq\lceil\log_{2}(1+\lambda)\rceil
. For any fixed DFS path with active interfaces
\mathcal{B}
on the current stack, define

\Phi\ \coloneqq\ \sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}w\!\big(\mathrm{len}(B)\big).

(16)

Lemma 14

(Rigorous per-path potential decay)
.

Along any root-to-leaf traversal under midpoint recursion:

1.

The potential
\Phi=\sum_{d}\lceil\log_{2}(1+\ell(d))\rceil
remains
O(\log T)
throughout (initially
O(\log T)
; decreases by a constant every
O(1)
levels; never increases on backtracking).

2.

The number of simultaneously active interfaces is
O(\Phi)=O(\log T)
.

3.

Live frontier bound:
Although
O(\log T)
interfaces may be active as
metadata
, the DFS schedule materializes at most a constant number of
O(b)
windows at once (indeed, one). Thus at any moment the
live frontier
uses
O(b)
cells for the current window plus
O(\log T)
cells for interface metadata. (This is a bound on instantaneous workspace along the path, not on the overall evaluator space.)

Proof.

Initialization.
At the root there are two interfaces of length
T
, so
\Phi(0)\leq 2\lceil\log_{2}(1{+}T)\rceil=O(\log T)
.

Monotonicity.
Backtracking pops interfaces and cannot increase
\Phi
.

Decay.
When
\lambda
is replaced by a child of length
\leq\lceil\lambda/2\rceil
, we have

w(\lceil\lambda/2\rceil)\ \leq\ \left\lceil\log_{2}\!\big(1+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\big)\right\rceil\ \leq\ w(\lambda)-1+O(1),

so over every constant number of levels the total potential drops by at least a positive constant (small-
\lambda
cases contribute only
O(1)
in total). Hence
\Phi=O(\log T)
throughout.

Interface count and workspace.
Each active interval contributes at most two interfaces, so the count is
O(\Phi)
. Each interface is materialized via bounded-window replay using
O(b)
cells; auxiliary fields are constant- or
O(\log t)
-bit values stored within these cells. Thus the live workspace is
O(b)+O(\log T)
: at most one
O(b)
window is materialized at any time, and interface metadata contributes only
O(\log T)
cells.

∎

8.1
Low-degree extension tuning

Encoding summaries as bounded-degree maps.

Let
\pi(\Sigma(I))\in\{0,1\}^{q}
denote the

of an interval summary—control state plus a constant number of fixed-size flags/tags (advisory fingerprints), with

\mathbb{F}_{2^{c}}
with constant

For each interval

(18)

of degree at most
\Delta=O(1)
in each variable, such that

All

Lemma 15

(Degree stability under composition)
.

Let
A,B:\mathbb{F}_{2^{c}}^{m}\to\mathbb{F}_{2^{c}}^{m}
be affine maps and
G:\mathbb{F}_{2^{c}}^{2q+m}\to\mathbb{F}_{2^{c}}^{q}
have coordinate-wise degree at most
\Delta^{\prime}=O(1)
. If
\deg(F_{J}),\deg(F_{J^{\prime}})\leq\Delta
, then the parent map

x\ \mapsto\ G\!\big(F_{J}(Ax),\,F_{J^{\prime}}(Bx),\,x\big)

has coordinate-wise degree at most
D:=\max\{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}\}=O(1)
. In particular, the degree bound remains
constant
across the entire tree.

Proof.

Affine precomposition does not increase degree. Each coordinate of
G
is a degree-
\Delta^{\prime}
polynomial in the
2q+m
inputs, and each input coordinate is either affine in
x
or a degree-
\Delta
polynomial in
x
. Therefore each parent coordinate has degree at most
\max\{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}\}
.
∎

Lemma 16

(Constant-size evaluation grid sufficiency)
.

There exists a constant-size evaluation grid
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2^{c}}^{m}
with
|\mathcal{X}|=(D+1)^{m}=O(1)
, where

is as in Lemma
15
, such that:

1.

All node merge operations admit exact representation by degree-
\leq D
polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{2^{c}}
.

2.

Evaluating

on
\mathcal{X}
suffices for exact interpolation and for executing every combiner operation; i.e., the parent’s evaluations on
\mathcal{X}
are computable from the children’s evaluations on
\mathcal{X}
.

3.

Each internal node maintains exactly
|\mathcal{X}|\cdot q=O(1)
field accumulators.

Moreover, since
A
and
B
are fixed affine maps on a constant-dimensional space, we may choose
\mathcal{X}
so that it is closed under
A
and
B
(by construction of a small affine-invariant grid), or equivalently precompute constant-size interpolation tables that recover

and
F(Bx)
from evaluations on
\mathcal{X}
. In either case, the number of registers and the arithmetic remain
O(1)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
15
, all coordinates have degree
\leq D=O(1)
. Multivariate polynomial interpolation on a tensor grid with
(D+1)
points per variable is exact, so
\mathcal{X}=X_{1}\times\cdots\times X_{m}
with
|X_{r}|=D+1
suffices. Since

are constants (independent of

and

),
|\mathcal{X}|q=O(1)

is a fixed low-degree map evaluated pointwise on
\mathcal{X}
.
∎

Combiner algebra.

For adjacent intervals
J
and

A leaf reconstructs

on the fly

8.2
Path descriptor elimination

Pointerless DFS iterator.

We traverse
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
in depth-first order using a
reversible iterator
that stores at each level only a two-bit token
(\textsf{type},\textsf{dir})\in\{\textsf{split},\textsf{combiner}\}\times\{\textsf{L},\textsf{R}\}
. The actual endpoints of the current interval are recomputed from the root at each descent/ascent step by reapplying the local refinement rule determined by the token sequence.

Streaming progress ledger.

The evaluator processes
\Theta(T)
merges in a single streamed pass. It maintains a constant-size
progress token
for each pending merge (e.g., a left/right completion bit and a small tag) in a pointerless ledger. This contributes
O(T)
cells in total. Endpoint recomputation uses an additive

.

Lemma 17

(Confirmed

The path-descriptor storage is exactly two bits per level:

1.

Each level stores only
(\textsf{type},\textsf{dir})
.

2.

3.

At each level, the sole persistent information is the two-bit token. Endpoints are recomputed by rewalking from the root, applying the constant-time rule for the recorded node type and direction (midpoint/centroid split or combiner navigation). Temporary arithmetic fits in
O(1)
cells; no

∎

Constant work per level.

Recomputation performs

We now account for all contributors in
cells over a fixed finite alphabet

Leaves.

9
Limitations, Robustness, and Model Variants

This section records the precise scope of arguments, the ways in which the construction is insensitive to low-level modeling choices, and where nontrivial adaptations would be required. Throughout, space is counted in tape
cells
over a fixed finite alphabet; constants may change under different encodings, but asymptotics do not.

9.1
What we provably establish

Deterministic multitape Turing machines.

The main result
\mathrm{TIME}[t]\subseteq\mathrm{SPACE}[O(\sqrt{t})]
is rigorously proven for deterministic multitape TMs over constant alphabets with a constant number of work tapes under the standard cost model.

Relativization.

Because the height compression and combiner semantics depend only on the
shape
of the block-partitioned run and on local interface equality checks (using control/head positions; fingerprints/tags may be compared as advisory metadata but are
not
relied upon for correctness), the entire transformation relativizes: for any oracle
A
,

\mathrm{TIME}^{A}[t]\ \subseteq\ \mathrm{SPACE}^{A}\!\left[\,O\!\left(\sqrt{t}\,\right)\right].

(21)

When a leaf block contains oracle queries, the evaluator simply
reissues
those queries while replaying the
O(b)
-cell window; no additional space beyond the leaf window and
O(1)
-cell loop control is needed. Edge predicates remain logspace checkable, since the oracle tape contents are reconstructed during replay as part of the configuration snapshot.

Model robustness (provably established).

The following variations provably preserve the
O(\sqrt{t})
bound:

•

Alphabet size and encoding changes (constant factors only).

•

Constant changes in the number of tapes and standard head-move conventions (constant factors only).

•

Arbitrary polynomial or polylogarithmic time overheads (we bound space only).

•

Unknown
t
or on-line runs:
if
t
is not known in advance, the evaluator can use standard doubling (
b
is reset at phases
1,2,4,8,\ldots
); this affects time only and preserves the
O(\sqrt{t})
space envelope up to additive
O(\log t)
scratch for phase counters.

9.2
Conjectural extensions requiring additional work

Random-access machines (RAMs).

For RAMs with
oblivious
access schedules (addresses are computable from time and input alone), the circuit-evaluation reduction is standard; our pipeline immediately applies.

Concrete obstacle for non-oblivious RAMs:
Address data are part of the state and may couple distant memory regions within a block. Chunk summaries would need to expose address fingerprints/tags or sparse address logs covering potentially
O(t)
distinct addresses. The main difficulty is ensuring that interface checks remain
O(b)
-window computations rather than requiring global address-space verification, which would destroy the space bound; tags are advisory and cannot be the basis for semantic correctness.

What would be needed:
A structural restriction on address patterns (e.g., bounded locality, limited address reuse) or a fundamentally different approach to handling non-local memory dependencies while keeping edge predicates logspace checkable.

Nondeterministic and alternating variants.

The upper bound is for deterministic multitape TMs only.

Why naive enumeration breaks space:
For nondeterministic machines, one might attempt to enumerate all computation paths, but this creates an exponential blowup in the number of block summaries. A single nondeterministic choice at the beginning could lead to
2^{t}
distinct computation paths, each requiring its own block-respecting summary. Even if each path uses
O(\sqrt{t})
space, storing or enumerating
2^{t}
paths destroys any polynomial space bound.

What would be needed:
Structural control of branching (e.g., bounded nondeterminism) or novel techniques to compactly represent families of computation paths without explicit enumeration.

External memory and streaming.

The block/combiner discipline mirrors cache-aware tiling and multi-pass streaming: each leaf replay is an
O(b)
-sized
working set
. The pointerless traversal enforces that at most
O(H^{\prime}_{\mathrm{eval}})
constant-sized tokens are live. Consequently, any external-memory instantiation with local block size equal to the cache (or stream buffer) inherits the same space accounting (replace
b
by the cache capacity
M
to obtain the additive tradeoff
M+t/M
, with an additional
additive

O(\log t)
addressing scratch that is not multiplied by
t/M
). This statement abstracts away transfer granularity; it concerns only
workspace
.

9.3
Lower-bound sensitivity and barriers

Why this does not contradict classical barriers.

The results presented here are
upper bounds
(simulations), not separations, and therefore are orthogonal to Natural Proofs and most lower-bound barriers. Still, two meta-barriers are worth addressing:

Relativization.
As noted, the proof relativizes. This is consistent with the folklore that many robust simulation upper bounds relativize; barriers typically obstruct separations like
\mathrm{P}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}\mathrm{PSPACE}
, not inclusions of the form
\mathrm{TIME}[t]\subseteq\mathrm{SPACE}[g(t)]
.

Algebrization.
The algebraic layer is
explicit
(constant field, constant-degree maps) and serves as a compact evaluator for finite-state merges. We do not rely on arithmetization in the sense of algebraic
access
to an oracle; therefore the construction neither exploits nor is thwarted by algebrization phenomena.

Combinatorial vs. algebraic dependencies.

Height compression (centroid scheduling, potential decay, indegree capping) is purely combinatorial and applies to any block-summarized run with local interfaces. The algebraic layer is a constant-degree convenience to ensure
O(1)
internal-node workspace and uniform bounded fan-in composition; it can be replaced by any constant-space combiner that implements the same associative merge semantics with
exact bounded-window replay
at interfaces.

Tightness considerations.

The
b+t/b
tradeoff is plausibly optimal for any evaluation strategy that (i) processes disjoint
b
-sized windows at leaves and (ii) maintains only
O(1)
information per internal level while keeping at most
O(\log T)
interfaces simultaneously active in a recursion of depth
\Theta(\log T)
. Any strategy that also refrains from caching more than the live frontier must still process
\Theta(T)
merges overall, suggesting an additive
O(t/b)
term is unavoidable absent stronger structural assumptions. Proving matching lower bounds for
all
time-
t
computations would require an unconditional time–space tradeoff theorem, beyond the scope of this work.

We position these results along several methodological axes, with precise citations and comparisons to prior approaches.

Pebbling and recursive decomposition.

Black–white pebbling
[
2
]
, centroid/cut decompositions
[
3

, and balanced divide-and-conquer have long underpinned time–space tradeoffs and memory-efficient evaluation. Classical pebbling results establish fundamental trade-offs between time and space but typically yield global bounds over entire computation graphs.
How this work differs:
The
height compression
applies a centroid schedule tailored to block-respecting runs, together with a potential that drops by a constant every
O(1)
levels along
any individual path
, reducing the per-path recursion depth from
\Theta(T)
to
O(\log T)
via
per-path
analysis rather than frontier-wide amortization.

Time–space simulations and reversibility.

Time–space simulation techniques, including reversible computation
[
4
]
and the Lengauer–Maurer–Tarjan approach
[
5
]
, establish various trade-offs for deterministic simulation (often yielding inclusions of the form
\mathrm{TIME}[t]\subseteq\mathrm{SPACE}[t^{\alpha}]
for
\alpha\in(1/2,1)
via checkpointing and divide-and-conquer, or product-style bounds).
How this work differs:
We isolate the
path-bookkeeping
term as the sole obstruction to
O(\sqrt{t})
and remove the multiplicative losses via structural height compression plus pointerless traversal, achieving the clean exponent
\alpha=1/2
without logarithmic factors (Theorem
3
).

Tree Evaluation and logspace boundaries.

Tree Evaluation problems and their relationship to logspace computation
[
6
]
have been central to understanding the power of small-space computation. The question of whether Tree Evaluation is in logspace remains open and is closely related to circuit evaluation lower bounds.
How this work differs:
Rather than trying to evaluate arbitrary trees in logspace, we
transform
the tree structure itself to achieve
logarithmic
evaluation depth and then evaluate the compressed tree with constant per-level overhead (Theorem
1
).

Algebraic evaluation and succinct verification.

Low-degree encodings, interpolation, and streaming re-evaluation are classical tools for compactly computing or verifying structured functions
[
7
]
.
How this work differs:
We instantiate a constant-degree
combiner algebra
over constant-size fields to keep internal-node workspace constant and to make edge predicates logspace checkable by design, ensuring exact computation without probabilistic elements.

Space-to-branching-program size pipeline.

The classical translation from space-bounded computation to branching programs via configuration graphs
[
8
,
9
]
establishes that
\mathrm{SPACE}[s]
functions have branching programs of size
2^{O(s)}
.
How this work differs:
The improved space bound directly yields branching program upper bounds of
2^{O(\sqrt{s})}
for size-

circuits, de-logging previous bounds of the form
2^{O(\sqrt{s\log s})}
(equivalently
2^{O(\sqrt{s}\,\mathrm{polylog}s)}

Time–space lower bounds.

Complementary lower bound techniques, particularly those of Fortnow and van Melkebeek
[
10

How this work differs:
As an upper bound technique, the present approach is orthogonal to these lower bound methods and does not contradict known barriers to time–space separations.

Appendix A
Formal Block-Respecting Simulator

This appendix formalizes the block-respecting reduction from a time-
t
run of a deterministic multitape TM to a succinct computation tree. We specify (i) the machine and configuration model, (ii) block partitioning, (iii) per-block summaries and the
movement-log
semantics, (iv) the merge operation defining internal nodes, and (v) uniformity and correctness. We include a worked example and handle edge cases. Throughout, space is measured in
work-tape

cells
over a fixed finite alphabet.

A.1 Model and configurations

Let
M=(Q,\Gamma,\sqcup,\Sigma,\delta,q_{0},q_{\mathrm{acc}},q_{\mathrm{rej}},\tau)
be a deterministic multitape Turing machine with a constant number
\tau\geq 1
of work tapes and one read-only input tape;
\Gamma
is a fixed finite alphabet containing blank
\sqcup
, and
\Sigma\subseteq\Gamma
is the input alphabet. A
configuration
of
M
is a tuple

C=\big(q,\ h_{\mathrm{in}},\ (h_{1},\dots,h_{\tau}),\ (T_{1},\dots,T_{\tau})\big),

where
q\in Q
is the control state,
h_{\mathrm{in}}\in\mathbb{Z}
is the input-tape head position,
h_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}
is the head position on work tape
r
, and
T_{r}:\mathbb{Z}\to\Gamma
is the tape-content function on work tape
r
with finite support (i.e.,
T_{r}(z)=\sqcup
for all but finitely many
z
). One step of
M
updates
(q,h_{\mathrm{in}},h_{1},\dots,h_{\tau},T_{1},\dots,T_{\tau})
by reading the symbols under the heads (the input tape is read-only) and writing/moving as per
\delta
. We measure space
only
in work-tape cells.

Fix input
x
and let
C_{0}\to C_{1}\to\cdots\to C_{t}
be the first
t
steps of
M
on
x
(halting earlier if applicable; in that case pad with self-loops). For step
s
and work tape
r
, the unique work-tape cell touched is the cell at head position
h_{r}(s)
.

A.2 Block partition and local windows

Fix a
block size

b\in[1,t]
and let
T\coloneqq\lceil t/b\rceil
. Define
time blocks

B_{k}\coloneqq\{(k-1)b+1,\dots,\min\{kb,t\}\}
for
k=1,\dots,T
. For work tape
r
in block
k
, the set of visited cells is

V_{r,k}\ \coloneqq\ \{\,h_{r}(s)\ :\ s\in B_{k}\,\},

which lies in the interval
W_{r,k}=[\min V_{r,k},\max V_{r,k}]
of length
|W_{r,k}|\leq b
because heads move by one cell per step. (Here
h_{r}(s)
denotes the position scanned
before
the move in step
s
, so exactly one work-tape cell is touched per tape and step.) We call
W_{r,k}
the
local window
for work tape
r
in block
k
.

Edge case handling.

•

Single block (
t<b
):
We have
T=1
and
B_{1}=\{1,\dots,t\}
. The construction reduces to a single leaf with no merging required.

•

Small
T
:
All statements hold with the same asymptotic forms; constant factors absorb small values of
T
.

•

Early halting:
If
M
halts at step
s<t
, pad the computation with self-loops in the accept/reject state to reach exactly
t
steps.

A.3 Movement-log semantics (micro-ops)

For each block
k
and each step
s\in B_{k}
, record a constant-size
micro-op

\big(\mathbf{w}_{s},\ \mathbf{mv}_{s}\big)\ \in\ (\Gamma\cup\{\bot\})^{\tau}\ \times\ \{-1,0,+1\}^{\tau},

where
\mathbf{w}_{s}=(w_{s,1},\ldots,w_{s,\tau})
encodes, for each work tape
r
, the written symbol (or
\bot
for “no write”), and
\mathbf{mv}_{s}=(\mu_{s,1},\ldots,\mu_{s,\tau})
encodes the head moves. Since a block has at most
b
steps and
\tau
is constant, the micro-op stream has length
\leq b
and total size
O(b)
. For each work tape
r
, we also record within-block
enter/exit offsets
(head positions)
relative to the block’s window

W_{r,k}
.

Per-block summary.

The summary
\sigma_{k}
contains:

•

q_{\mathrm{in}}(k),q_{\mathrm{out}}(k)\in Q
;

•

the input-head positions
h^{\mathrm{in}}_{\mathrm{in}}(k),h^{\mathrm{out}}_{\mathrm{in}}(k)
;

•

for each work tape
r
, the window endpoints of
W_{r,k}
and the enter/exit offsets within
W_{r,k}
;

•

the micro-op stream of length
\leq b
(each item a
\tau
-tuple over work tapes);

•

a constant-size fingerprint/tag per work tape used only as a fast reject (correctness is by exact replay).

For notation, let
\mathsf{Log}_{r,k}
denote the
per-tape projection
of the micro-op stream in block
k
(obtained by extracting the
r
-th coordinates
(w_{s,r},\mu_{s,r})
for
s\in B_{k}
). Replaying
\sigma_{k}
scans the micro-op stream once and (re)materializes the window contents for each work tape on demand using
O(b)
cells. (When replay is invoked during a merge, the required interface window contents are materialized by recursively evaluating the relevant subintervals; this uses
O(b)
cells and does not alter the asymptotic accounting.)

Lemma 18

(Per-block summary uses
O(b)
cells)
.

Over a fixed finite alphabet
\Gamma
, the summary
\sigma_{k}
occupies
O(b)
cells.

Proof.

There are at most
b
micro-ops, each a
\tau
-tuple with
\tau=O(1)
, contributing
O(b)
cells. The number of enter/exit offsets is
O(\tau)
and each offset is
O(\log b)
bits, i.e.,
O(\log b)
cells over a constant alphabet (up to a
1/\log|\Gamma|
factor), which is dominated by the
O(b)
term. Control states, input-head positions, and fingerprint/tags are constant-size. Summing gives
O(b)
cells.
∎

A.5 Succinct computation tree and the merge operator

For an interval
I=[i,j]\subseteq[1,T]
, define its
interval summary

\Sigma(I)
as the tuple obtained by composing
\sigma_{i},\dots,\sigma_{j}
in order and verifying that for each boundary between
k
and
k{+}1
:

1.

q_{\mathrm{out}}(k)=q_{\mathrm{in}}(k{+}1)
,

2.

h_{\mathrm{in}}^{\mathrm{out}}(k)=h_{\mathrm{in}}^{\mathrm{in}}(k{+}1)
and, for each work tape
r
,
h^{\mathrm{out}}_{r}(k)=h^{\mathrm{in}}_{r}(k{+}1)
,

3.

the
window overlap
is consistent for each work tape: applying
\mathsf{Log}_{r,k}
to the pre-window contents at the end of
B_{k}
yields the pre-window contents for
B_{k+1}
on the overlap, and symmetrically for post-window contents.
7
7

7

If adjacent windows are disjoint, there is nothing to reconcile by construction (each window already contains all cells touched within its block); constant-size fingerprints/tags can be used as a fast reject but are not relied upon for soundness, which follows from exact bounded-window replay.

Formally, define a binary
merge
operator
\oplus
that maps two adjacent interval summaries
\Sigma([i,m])
and
\Sigma([m+1,j])
to
\Sigma([i,j])
by performing precisely these checks and by
reconciling boundary contents and head offsets via an exact bounded-window replay
at the unique interface between the two intervals (tags may be refreshed but are advisory only). The operator
\oplus
is associative (by locality of interfaces), enabling arbitrary rebracketing.

Succinct tree
\mathcal{T}
.

The canonical succinct tree (the left-deep, unbalanced binary interval tree on
[1,T]
) has a leaf for each
[k,k]
labeled by
\sigma_{k}
and an internal node labeled by
I=[i,j]
that stores (or recomputes)
\Sigma(I)
from its children via
\oplus
.

Exact contents of interval summaries.

For an interval
I=[i,j]
, the summary
\Sigma(I)
comprises: (i) finite control state in/out, (ii) input-head position in/out and each work-tape head position in/out (binary,
O(\log t)
bits each), (iii) for each work tape, the
left
and
right
boundary windows as
procedures
defined by exact replay of the movement logs in
[i,j]
(not as stored arrays), and
(iv) constant-size integrity tags used only as fast rejects. Crucially, the boundary windows are
not
stored verbatim inside
\Sigma(I)
; instead, they are
reconstructible on demand
by deterministically replaying the children’s logs over
O(b)
cells. All
b
-dependent data are handled exclusively by exact bounded-window replay.

A.6 Uniformity and correctness

Proposition 1

(Uniformity)
.

Given
(i,j)
in binary and
T
, the endpoints of all children in the canonical tree and the addressing/topology predicates used by
\oplus
are computable in
O(\log T)
space. Semantic edge verification (that the parent’s label equals
\oplus
of its children’s labels) is performed by an exact bounded-window replay using
O(b)
cells.

Proof.

Children endpoints are midpoints or consecutive partitions, computable with counters up to
T
. Addressing/topology checks (parent/child index arithmetic; adjacency of intervals) require only
O(\log T)
space. Semantic merge verification uses equality tests on constant- or
O(\log t)
-bit fields and a bounded-window replay over at most
O(b)
positions (implemented by marker-based scans), which uses
O(b)
cells. Thus addressing/topology is in logspace, and semantic correctness uses
O(b)
space as specified.
∎

Proposition 2

(Correctness)
.

For all
I=[i,j]\subseteq[1,T]
, the value
\Sigma(I)
equals the true configuration summary obtained by simulating
M
from the start of block
i
to the end of block
j
.

Proof.

By induction on
|I|
. The base
|I|=1
holds by construction of
\sigma_{i}
. For
|I|>1
, write
I=[i,m]\cup[m{+}1,j]
. The only possible discrepancies when composing the two halves are at the unique interface time between
B_{m}
and
B_{m+1}
. The checks in
\oplus
ensure identical control states and head positions (including the input head), and the bounded-window replay guarantees that all writes at the interface are applied exactly once and that no unlogged writes exist outside the windows (since each window contains every cell touched within its block). Hence the composed summary equals the true summary.
∎

Appendix B
Pebbling Potentials and Midpoint Recursion

We give detailed proofs of Lemma
3
(midpoint recursion with logarithmic depth) and Lemma
6
(a per-path pebbling bound), with explicit constants and rigorous per-path analysis.

B.1 Proof of Lemma
3
(Midpoint recursion)

For an interval
I=[i,j]\subseteq[1,T]
of length
\ell=j-i+1
, define its midpoint

c(I)\ \coloneqq\ \Big\lfloor\frac{i+j}{2}\Big\rfloor.

The recursion splits
I
into the two children
[i,c(I)]
and
[c(I){+}1,j]
.

Lemma 19

(Geometric shrink and logarithmic depth)
.

Along any root-to-leaf path under midpoint recursion, if
\ell(d)
denotes the active interval length at depth
d
, then

\ell(d{+}1)\ \leq\ \left\lceil\frac{\ell(d)}{2}\right\rceil,

hence the recursion depth is at most
\lceil\log_{2}T\rceil
. Moreover, there is at most one active interval per depth along the DFS path.

Proof.

Immediate from the definition of the midpoint split: each refinement halves the length up to an additive
1
. After at most
\lceil\log_{2}T\rceil
levels the length becomes
1
. Depth-first traversal has one active interval per level.
∎

Lemma 20

(Logspace computability of endpoints)
.

Given
(i,j)
in binary, one can compute
c(I)=\lfloor(i{+}j)/2\rfloor
and the endpoints of the two children using
O(\log T)
space.

Proof.

All arithmetic is on counters bounded by
T
; a logspace transducer suffices.
∎

Conclusion of Lemma
3
.

Combining Lemmas
19
and
20
yields the
O(\log T)
depth bound and
O(\log T)
-space computability claimed in Lemma
3
. ∎

B.2 Per-path potential bound

We define a
telescoping
potential that charges only the
drop in scale
at each level. Let

w(\lambda)\ \coloneqq\ \left\lceil\log_{2}(1{+}\lambda)\right\rceil,\qquad\Delta(\lambda)\ \coloneqq\ w(\lambda)\;-\;w\!\left(\left\lceil\frac{\lambda}{2}\right\rceil\right)\ \in\ \{0,1\}.

For a fixed root-to-leaf traversal with active interval lengths
\ell(0),\ell(1),\ldots,\ell(D)
(one per depth), define

\Phi_{\mathrm{path}}\ \coloneqq\ \sum_{d=0}^{D-1}\ \Delta\!\big(\ell(d)\big).

Lemma 21

(Per-path potential and live frontier)
.

Under midpoint recursion and DFS:

1.

\Phi_{\mathrm{path}}\leq w(\ell(0))-w(\ell(D))\leq\lceil\log_{2}(1{+}T)\rceil=O(\log T)
(telescoping).

2.

Independently, there is at most one active interval per depth, so the number of simultaneously active intervals is
D{+}1=O(\log T)
and the number of active interfaces is
O(\log T)
.

3.

The DFS materializes only the currently focused leaf/junction window (plus
O(1)
interface metadata), so at most one
O(b)
window is live at any time; the live frontier workspace is
O(b)+O(\log T)
.

Proof.

(1) By definition and
\ell(d{+}1)\leq\lceil\ell(d)/2\rceil
,

\Phi_{\mathrm{path}}=\sum_{d=0}^{D-1}\bigl(w(\ell(d))-w(\ell(d{+}1))\bigr)=w(\ell(0))-w(\ell(D))\leq w(T)=O(\log T).

(2) and (3) follow from Lemma
19
and the DFS schedule as noted.
∎

Conclusion of Lemma
6
.

The
O(\log T)
bounds on
\Phi_{\mathrm{path}}
and on the number of active intervals jointly imply
O(\log T)
simultaneously active interfaces and
O(b)
live-window memory along the path. ∎

Appendix C
Algebraic Replay Engine Details

We provide complete straight-line pseudocode, explicit space accounting, and rigorous implementation details for the Algebraic Replay Engine (ARE). All space counts are in
tape cells
over a fixed finite alphabet. Internal arithmetic is over a constant-size field
\mathbb{F}_{2^{c}}
, so each field element occupies
O(1)
cells (cf. Lemma
8
); all loops are implemented index-free (cf. Lemma
9
); traversal is pointerless with
O(1)
symbols per level (cf. Lemma
10
). The algebraic layer operates only on the
finite-state projection
(control and constant many tags); all
b
-dependent data are handled by exact bounded-window replay.

C.4 Determinism, uniformity, and end-to-end correctness

Determinism.

The ARE uses fixed algebraic maps and a fixed evaluation grid; no randomness is involved. The result at the root is the exact interval summary
\Sigma([1,T])
.

Uniformity.

Given input
\langle M,x,1^{t}\rangle
and block size
b
, all procedures are logspace-computable: (i) endpoint computation uses
O(\log T)
-bit arithmetic with pointerless recomputation, (ii) grid enumeration is fixed, and (iii) polynomial evaluations use fixed straight-line programs over the finite-state projection.

Correctness.

By induction on the tree, using associativity of
\oplus
and the specification
F_{J\cup J^{\prime}}(x)=G(F_{J}(Ax),F_{J^{\prime}}(Bx),x)
(with all
b
-dependent reconciliation done by exact bounded-window replay), the value produced at the root equals the true summary of the time-
t
run.

Remark.
All constants depend only on the machine model (
\tau
,
|\Gamma|
,
|Q|
) and the fixed algebraic instantiation, not on
t
or
b
.

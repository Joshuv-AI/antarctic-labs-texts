The effect of immediate neoadjuvant electromotive instillation of mitomycin C with Bacillus Calmette–Guérin versus BCG alone in non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer: A randomized controlled trial
Authors: Abdalla El Azab, Ahmed Abdelbary, Aly El Faqeh M Okasha, Hatem Aboulkassem, Ashraf Saad Zaghloul, Riham Mohamed Karkeet, Ibrahim Abdelrahman
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThe clinical effect of neoadjuvant intravesical instillation of chemotherapy immediately before transurethral resection of bladder tumors (TURBT) has been a subject of recent research. The aim of this study was to assess the effect of immediate neoadjuvant electromotive instillation of mitomycin C before transurethral resection for patients with non-muscle-invasive urothelial bladder cancer.Materials and MethodsOur study was a randomized clinical trial carried out on 50 patients diagnosed with non-muscle-invasive urothelial bladder cancer. Patients were classified into two groups: Group I consisted of 25 patients who received neoadjuvant electromotive drug administration of mitomycin C before TURBT and intravesical bacille Calmette–Guerin (BCG) per week for 6 weeks; Group II consisted of 25 patients who were treated with TURBT followed by intravesical BCG per week for 6 weeks alone (standard of care). Patients were followed up at 3, 6, 12, and 18 months by cystoscopy.ResultsPatients who received neoadjuvant electromotive drug administration of mitomycin C before TURBT in combination with BCG had a low recurrence rate compared with those who received BCG alone (12.0% vs. 48.0%, respectively; p=0.012) and a longer disease-free interval (88.0% vs. 52.0%, respectively; p=0.012). Four patients developed progression to muscle-invasive disease (16.0%) in the BCG alone group. However, this difference was not statistically significant (p=0.516). Regarding adverse effects, there were no statistically significant differences between the groups.ConclusionsNeoadjuvant intravesical electromotive drug administration of mitomycin C before TURBT is safe; reduces recurrence rates and enhances the disease-free interval compared with TURBT followed by BCG alone.Keywords: Electrochemotherapy, Mitomycin, Non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer, Recurrence, Transurethral resection of bladder

INTRODUCTION

Bladder cancer is the 7th most common cancer in men and the 11th most common cancer in women worldwide. Its incidence in men is four times greater than in women. Bladder cancer has the second highest prevalence in Egypt. It accounts for 12.7% of solid male malignancies [1,2].

Ninety percent of bladder tumors are histologically urothelial, 5% are squamous cell carcinomas, and less than 2% are adenocarcinomas or other variants. Approximately 70% of all urothelial bladder cancer at presentation is non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC), with 70% presenting in stage Ta, 20% as T1, and 10% as carcinoma in situ (CIS) [3,4]. These tumors are classified as NMIBC because they can be treated with transurethral resection of bladder tumors (TURBT) and/or intravesical instillations.

Based on European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) risk score, NMIBC is stratified into three risk groups: low, intermediate, and high risk. Such stratification reflects the probability of recurrence and progression and provides the basis for surveillance protocols and adjuvant intravesical therapies. NMIBC is characterized by high recurrence rates as well as considerable rates of progression into high-risk disease [5,6,7].

Meta-analyses by the EORTC and the American Urological Association have reported that intravesical chemotherapy instillation immediately after TURBT reduces recurrence in patients with low-risk NMIBC by 12% and in those with intermediate-risk disease by 17% (vs. TURBT alone), with acceptable adverse effects. The European Association of Urology and, to a lesser extent, the American Urological Association, guidelines consequently recommended that this strategy be included in treatment plans for all patients with NMIBC [6,8]. The explanation for effectiveness of intravesical chemotherapy instillation immediately after TURBT is based on its antitumor effect in the resection site, in destroying floating cells, and in destroying small tumors that have been missed at cystoscopy [9,10].

Despite growing evidence that intravesical chemotherapy instillation immediately after TURBT reduces recurrences of NMIBC, it is still not widely used, because in a significant number of patients, the presence of postoperative hematuria or the extent and/or depth of the resection precludes this practice [11,12,13].

Intravesical electromotive drug administration (EMDA) improves clinical efficacy in high-risk NMIBC in laboratory and clinical studies by increasing mitomycin uptake in the bladder. Because catheter electrodes may result in additional mechanical trauma, causing bladder spasm and drug solution leakage, intravesical EMDA of mitomycin is not advised immediately after TURBT [14,15].

The role of an immediate neoadjuvant instillation of chemotherapy has not been extensively studied other than in a prospective, randomized clinical trial using EMDA with mitomycin C. In that study, which was published by Di Stasi et al. in 2011 [16], it was found that patients who received neoadjuvant mitomycin C via EMDA experienced a longer disease-free interval compared with that in patients who received TURBT alone or TURBT plus immediate postoperative intravesical mitomycin instillation: 51 months versus 16 months versus 12 months, respectively (p<0.001).

In the present study, we explored the feasibility and impact of intravesical electromotive instillation of mitomycin as a neoadjuvant therapy, immediately before TURBT, on reducing recurrence rates and prolonging the recurrence-free interval in patients with NMIBC.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This randomized comparative study was conducted in the Department of Urologic Oncology at the National Cancer Institute, Cairo University. The minimum follow-up period was 18 months. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the National Cancer Institute (IRB review no. 201920045.3). All patients aged 18 years or older with histologically proven NMIBC, stage pTa, pCIS, pT1, were included in the study. Any patient with muscle invasion, nonurothelial bladder carcinomas, cardiac disease, pregnancy, immunocompromised status or receiving concurrent corticosteroid therapy, bleeding tendencies, coagulopathies or receiving anticoagulant therapy, other malignancies, psychiatric or neurological disorders, contraindications to spinal or general anesthesia as required for TURBT, or known hypersensitivity to bacille Calmette–Guérin (BCG) or mitomycin C were excluded from the study.

Written informed consent was obtained from all patients before enrollment. The sample size calculation was based on the research of Di Stasi et al. [16], who observed significant results after studying EMDA of mitomycin C before TURBT versus passive diffusion of mitomycin C after TURBT versus TURBT followed by BCG alone. Taking the values of the Di Stasi et al.’s study [16] as a reference, we determined the minimum required sample size with 80% power and a 5% level of significance to be 25 patients in each study group. The total sample size enrolled was 50 patients (25 patients in each study group) to reduce the margin of error.

The enrolled patients underwent staging of NMIBC and radiological imaging by ultrasonography or computed tomography of the urinary tract, cystoscopy, and biopsy. TURBT was performed for all tumors.

Using a sealed envelope system, we randomly assigned the patients to two groups. Ten sealed opaque envelopes were prepared, with group I and group II assigned to five envelopes each. Group I received neoadjuvant EMDA and BCG and group II received BCG alone. Once a patient consented to enter the trial, an envelope was opened, and the patient was then offered the allocated group. In this way, patients were randomly assigned in a series of blocks of 10. Once 25 patients were allocated to each group, we used four sealed opaque envelopes, assigning group I and group II in two envelopes each. Both the patient and the treating physician knew the allocated intervention before the surgery. However, the study statistician performing the final analysis was masked to the interventions.

Group I (n=25) included patients who received immediate EMDA of 40 mg mitomycin C dissolved in 50 mL sterile water infused intravesically thorough a specialized catheter, after which a 20-mA pulsed electric current was delivered for 30 minutes by a battery-powered generator. The controlled electric current passed between the active intravesical electrode of the specialized catheter and a dispersive ground electrode on the skin of the lower abdomen, about 30 to 60 minutes before spinal anesthesia. EMDA was followed by complete TURBT and then intravesical BCG at 120 mg/wk for 6 weeks. BCG therapy was begun 2 to 4 weeks after tumor resection as recommended by guidelines.

Group II (n=25) included patients who received intravesical BCG at 120 mg/wk after complete TURBT for 6 weeks. BCG therapy was begun 2 to 4 weeks after the tumor resection as recommended by guidelines. The procedural flow is shown in Fig. 1.

Second-look cystoscopy was done for cases with multifocal, large-sized lesions (>3 cm) to ensure complete resection of the tumor (20 cases from group I and 19 cases from group II). Patients with pathologically confirmed intermediate- and high-risk noninvasive muscle disease received intravesical instillation of 120 mg BCG dissolved in 50 mL sterile water retained in the bladder for 120 minutes weekly for 6 weeks starting 2 to 4 weeks after TURBT according to European Association of Urology guidelines. All patients were followed for at least 18 months to fulfill the objectives of the study. Adverse events were recorded after each instillation and 1 week after the sixth dose. Histopathological types and grades were recorded for each recurrence.

The outcome measures were disease-free interval, time to recurrence, recurrence rate, progression to muscle-invasive disease, and treatment-related adverse effects. Recurrence was defined as biopsy-confirmed non-muscle-invasive disease (Ta, CIS, and T1) and progression was defined as upstaging to muscle-invasive disease (pT2 tumor).

BCG maintenance was not administered to any patient owing to a shortage of BCG at the time of the study.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables are presented in the form of numbers and percentages. Continuous variables are presented as mean±standard deviation and median values. Mann–Whitney U test was used to compare quantitative variables, and the chi-square test and Fisher’s exact test were used to compare qualitative variables. The data entry was done in a Microsoft Excel spreadsheet, and the final analysis was done with the use of SPSS software version 21.0 (IBM Corp.). For statistical significance, a p-value of <0.05 was considered significant.

RESULTS

Fifty patients diagnosed with NMIBC participated in this study. Patients were matched in terms of the following patient and tumor characteristics: age, gender, pathological stage, and risk group. A total of 60% of patients had multifocal disease, and 60% of patients had invasion of the lamina propria (pT1). Intermediate- and high-risk groups were predominant. DISCUSSION

The present study was a randomized trial in which we determined the impact of neoadjuvant EMDA of mitomycin C with BCG compared with BCG alone in the treatment of patients with NMIBC.

The randomization guaranteed that age, gender, and cancer stage were comparable between the groups and that any differences in outcome were due to the differential intervention rather than to chance bias [17].

Despite advancements in detection and treatment, the incidence of recurrence of bladder cancers is high. The progression of non-muscle-invasive to muscle-invasive urinary bladder cancer typically causes metastases and is considered to have a poor prognosis. A subset of high-risk lesions that typically proceed to invasive forms, and which include between 70% and 80% of NMIBC, typically recurs without aggressive histologic characteristics (i.e., muscle-invasive bladder cancer). Urinary bladder cancer becomes challenging to treat from the time of diagnosis until death because of recurrent relapses, disease progression, and chemoresistance [18].

The efficacy of intravesical chemotherapy has lately been studied by using novel therapeutic strategies. One approach has been to increase penetration into the bladder wall to increase efficacy. EMDA refers to the application of an electrical current to improve the delivery and penetration of intravesical chemotherapy, especially with mitomycin C, into the bladder wall [19].

Our study revealed a comparable recurrence rate in the group that received neoadjuvant EMDA of mitomycin C before TURBT in combination with BCG and in the group that received BCG alone after TURBT (12.0% vs. 48.0%, p=0.012).

Similar results were reported by Di Stasi et al. [16], who compared combination therapy with BCG alone. Patients who received EMDA of mitomycin C before TURBT had a lower risk for recurrence (38%) than did those who received passive diffusion of mitomycin C after TURBT (59%) and those who received TURBT followed by BCG alone (64%). Even Oosterlinck et al. [20] found that alternating chemoimmunotherapy schedules with mitomycin C and BCG had comparable efficacy in reducing the rate of recurrence (47.9% vs. 54.2%).

The disease-free interval is an important landmark in cancer treatment. Our study revealed a comparable disease-free interval in the group that received neoadjuvant EMDA of mitomycin C before TURBT in combination with BCG and in the group that received BCG alone, which was statistically significant (p=0.012). We also found a longer time to recurrence of 18 months in the EMDA group compared with 12 months for the BCG alone group.

Similarly, 42 of 116 patients (36%) treated with TURBT alone, 49 of 119 patients (41%) treated with passive diffusion of mitomycin C after TURBT, and 73 of 117 (62%) treated with EMDA of mitomycin C before TURBT were disease-free after a median follow-up of 86 months (p<0.0001). Patients randomized to receive EMDA of mitomycin C before TURBT had a significantly longer time to recurrence (52 months) than those assigned to receive passive diffusion of mitomycin C after TURBT (16 months) or TURBT alone (12 months) in the Di Stasi et al.’s 2011 study [16]. In a 2003 study, Di Stasi et al. [15] reported that during a median follow-up of 43 months of high-risk patients, the median time to first recurrence was 35 months for the electromotive mitomycin C group, which was significantly longer than the 19.5 months for the passive mitomycin C group and 26 months for the BCG group.

In our study, we found no statistically significant differences between groups regarding progression to muscle-invasive disease (p=0.516). Progression was reported for four patients in the BCG alone group and none in the neoadjuvant EMDA group. According to Di Stasi et al. [16], 24 of 116 patients (21%) treated with TURBT alone, 23 of 119 (19%) treated with passive diffusion of mitomycin C after TURBT, and 19 of 117 (16%) treated with EMDA of mitomycin C before TURBT progressed to muscle-invasive disease (p=0.55).

In the current study, follow-up after 6 months in the neoadjuvant EMDA with BCG group showed that there was no recurrence, whereas in the BCG alone group three patients (12.0%) experienced recurrence. There were no statistically significant differences between groups (p=0.157). In contrast, in the study by Carando et al. [21], at 6 months NMIBC recurrence occurred in 25% of patients managed with EMDA of mitomycin C after TURBT. Follow-up data at 6 months were available for 45 patients in the Carando et al. [21] study, and 37 patients (82.3%) were responders, whereas 8 (17.7%) were nonresponders.

There were statistically significant differences between the groups regarding follow-up after 12 months in the present study. At that time, six patients in the BCG alone group had experienced recurrence compared with none in the neoadjuvant EMDA group (p=0.025). At 18 months of follow-up, three patients in the neoadjuvant EMDA group and three patients in the BCG alone group had experienced recurrence (p=0.023). Riedl et al. [22] reported that, at 14 months after receiving mitomycin C via EMDA, 57% (9/16) of patients with superficial bladder cancer were recurrence-free in a case series study.

In this study, we reported no statistically significant difference between groups regarding adverse effects. Two patients (8.0%) had gross hematuria and four (16.0%) had irritative bladder symptoms in the neoadjuvant EMDA group, whereas in the BCG alone group, three patients (12.0%) had gross hematuria and four (16.0%) had irritative bladder symptoms. There were no cases of perforation or systemic adverse effects related to mitomycin and no cases of electrical skin burns in the EMDA group. Similarly, Di Stasi et al. [16] reported persistent bladder symptoms in 18 patients (16%) in the TURBT-alone group, 37 patients (31%) in the passive diffusion of mitomycin post-TURBT group, and 24 patients (21%) in the EMDA of mitomycin pre-TURBT group. Hematuria after TURBT occurred in 8 patients (7%) in the TURBT-alone group, 16 patients (13%) in the passive diffusion of mitomycin post-TURBT group, and 11 patients (9%) in the EMDA of mitomycin C pre-TURBT group; bladder perforation after TURBT occurred in 5 patients (4%) in the TURBT-alone group, 9 patients (8%) in the passive diffusion of mitomycin C post-TURBT group, and 7 patients (6%) in the EMDA of mitomycin C pre-TURBT group. Carando et al. [21] reported that mitomycin administration was characterized by low-grade adverse events in 10 patients: 8 were grade 1 complications (namely skin erythema in 6 cases, catheter intolerance in 2) and the remaining were grade 2 (namely bladder tightness in one and bladder pain in one). Only two patients had to stop the treatment because of adverse events.

Overall, we found that the combination of neoadjuvant EMDA of mitomycin C with BCG is a better protocol than BCG alone in terms of recurrence, progression, and disease-free survival. The high concentrations of mitomycin in the bladder tissue appear to have destroyed or inhibited the implantation of viable cancer cells that could have been released during TURBT, thus lowering the recurrence rate of NMIBC while causing no long-term treatment efficacy loss. Another less likely hypothesis is that intravesical EMDA of mitomycin C administered before TURBT treats residual tumors and the deeper tissue exposed after TURBT. Intravesical perioperative chemotherapy cannot compensate for incomplete TURBT, however, which highlights the importance of endoscopic examination and meticulous attention to detail during endoscopic tumor resection.

Study limitations

One limitation was that the study was conducted at a single center; as such, the results cannot be generalized. The number of patients that could be included and observed was limited, and a longer follow-up time, presently ongoing, is needed to assess the long-term efficacy of the treatment. Moreover, the ideal therapeutic regimen in terms of timing, number of instillations, and duration of induction and maintenance must be better defined. Nonetheless, our findings suggest that EMDA of mitomycin C can be used safely and effectively in patients with NMIBC.

CONCLUSIONS

Neoadjuvant EMDA of mitomycin C before TURBT was associated with a low recurrence rate and a prolonged disease-free interval compared with BCG alone. This result confirms the efficacy and safety of EMDA of mitomycin C in the management of NMIBC. However, more research with larger samples and a longer follow-up time is needed.

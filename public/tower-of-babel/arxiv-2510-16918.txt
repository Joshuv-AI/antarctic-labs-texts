Single-letter Chain Rule for Quantum Relative Entropy
Giulio Gasbarri, Matt Hoogsteder-Riera

Abstract
Relative entropy is the standard measure of distinguishability in classical and quantum information theory. In the classical case, its loss under channels admits an exact chain rule, while in the quantum case only asymptotic, regularized chain rules are known. We establish new chain rules for quantum relative entropy that apply already in the single-copy regime. The first inequality is obtained via POVM decompositions, extending the point distributions in the classical chain rule to quantum ensemble partitions. The second gives a sufficient condition for the most natural extension of the classical result, which uses projectors as a analog for the classical point distributions. We additionally find a semiclassical chain rule where the point distributions are replaced with the projectors of the initial states, and, finally, we find a relation to previous works on strengthened data processing inequalities and recoverability. These results show that meaningful chain inequalities are possible already at the single-copy level, but they also highlight that tighter bounds remain to be found.



1
Introduction

Entropy inequalities formalize the principle that statistical distinguishability cannot increase under noisy processing. In the classical setting, distinguishability is quantified by the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between probability distributions
p,q
on a finite alphabet
\mathcal{X}
,

\displaystyle D(p\|q)=\sum_{x}p(x)\,\log\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}.

(1)

The KL divergence is non-negative, vanishes if and only if
p=q
, and obeys the
data-processing inequality
(DPI), i.e.

\displaystyle D(Mp\|Mq)\leq D(p\|q),

(2)

for every stochastic map
M
.
Beyond monotonicity, the classical case admits a sharper refinement in the form of a chain rule. For any pair of stochastic maps
M
and
N
,

\displaystyle D(p\|q)-D(Mp\|Nq)\;\geq\;-\mathbb{E}_{p}\,D(M\delta_{j}\|N\delta_{j}),

(3)

where
\delta_{j}
denotes the point distribution at
j
.
This inequality decomposes the global change of relative entropy
into an average of local divergences of the point distributions.
It provides a finer description of how distinguishability flows through channels and underpins structural properties such as joint convexity of relative entropy.

In the quantum setting, probability distributions are replaced by density operators. For states
\rho,\;\sigma
on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, the Umegaki relative entropy
[
1
,
2
]
is defined as

\displaystyle D(\rho\|\sigma)=\begin{cases}\Tr\!\left[\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma)\right],&\text{if }\operatorname{supp}(\rho)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\sigma),\\[4.0pt]
+\infty,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(4)

Its monotonicity under quantum channels,

\displaystyle D(\rho\|\sigma)\geq D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{M}(\sigma)),

(5)

is the
quantum DPI
, established independently by Lindblad
[
3
]
and Uhlmann
[
4
]
, and known to be equivalent to the strong subadditivity of entropy
[
5
]
.

The equality conditions for the DPI were characterized by Petz
[
6
,
7
]
, who introduced the recovery map

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\sigma,\mathcal{M}}(X)=\sigma^{1/2}\,\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}\!\left(\mathcal{M}(\sigma)^{-1/2}\,X\,\mathcal{M}(\sigma)^{-1/2}\right)\,\sigma^{1/2},

(6)

and proved that equality holds if and only if
\rho=\mathcal{R}_{\sigma,\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{M}(\rho))
. This result initiated the systematic study of recoverability.
Fawzi and Renner
[
8
]
gave the first quantitative refinement, proving that the conditional mutual information of a tripartite state lower bounds the fidelity of recovery:

\displaystyle I(A:C|B)_{\rho}\;\geq\;-2\log F\!\left(\rho_{ABC},\mathcal{R}_{B\to BC}(\rho_{AB})\right).

(7)

This revealed that small conditional mutual information guarantees the existence of a high-fidelity recovery channel.

An important refinement came with the recognition that the
measured relative entropy
1

,

\displaystyle D_{G}(\rho\|\sigma)=D(P_{\rho}^{G}\|P_{\sigma}^{G}),

(8)

is the correct quantity in strengthened inequalities. Here
P_{\rho}^{G}
is the probability distribution generated by measuring POVM
G=\{G_{i}\}
on
\rho
. Brandão, Harrow, Aram, Oppenehim and Strekchuck
[

established

\displaystyle I(A:C|B)_{\rho}\;\geq\;\sup_{G}D_{G}\!\left(\rho_{ABC}\,\big\|\,\mathcal{R}_{B\to BC}(\rho_{AB})\right).

(9)

This result demonstrates that the conditional mutual information upper-bounds the optimal measured relative entropy between the true tripartite state and any state recovered from its marginal on
AB
, optimized over all possible measurements.

These ideas were subsequently extended to quantum relative entropy.
Wilde
[
10
]
and Sutter, Tomamichel and Harrow
[
11
]
established strengthened data-processing inequalities that bound the loss of relative entropy under a quantum channel.
Their results make use of rotated Petz recovery maps, defined for each real parameter
t\in\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\sigma,\mathcal{M}}^{(t)}(X)=\sigma^{\frac{1+it}{2}}\,\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}\!\left(\mathcal{M}(\sigma)^{-\frac{1+it}{2}}\,X\,\mathcal{M}(\sigma)^{-\frac{1-it}{2}}\right)\,\sigma^{\frac{1-it}{2}}.

(10)

Building on Wilde’s work
[
10
]
, Sutter
et al.

[
11
]
employed the rotated recovery map to prove that

\displaystyle\begin{split}D(\rho\|\sigma)-D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)|\mathcal{M}(\sigma))&\geq\sup_{G}D_{G}(\rho\|\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{M}(\rho))\\
&\geq-\,2\log F\!\left(\rho,\;\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{M}(\rho)\right).\end{split}

(11)

In these strenghtened DPI the recovery map
\mathcal{R}
is a
\rho
-dependent convex combination of rotated Petz maps. This naturally raises the question of whether a universal (i.e.,
\rho
-independent) recovery map exists.

Junge, Renner, Sutter, Wilde, and Winter
[
12
]
and Sutter, Berta, and Tomamichel
[
13
]
answered this positively, by introducing an explicit convex mixture

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{M}}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\beta_{0}(t)\;\mathcal{R}_{\sigma,\mathcal{M}}^{(t)},\qquad\beta_{0}(t)=\frac{\pi}{2(1+\cosh(\pi t))}

(12)

of rotated Petz maps,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\sigma,\mathcal{M}}^{(t)}(X)=\sigma^{\frac{1+it}{2}}\,\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}\!\left(\mathcal{M}(\sigma)^{-\frac{1+it}{2}}\,X\,\mathcal{M}(\sigma)^{-\frac{1-it}{2}}\right)\,\sigma^{\frac{1-it}{2}}.

(13)

Junge
et al.

[
12
]
improved the fidelity bound by showing that the loss of quantum relative entropy under any quantum channel admits a quantitative lower bound in terms of this universal recovery map:

\displaystyle D(\rho\|\sigma)-D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{M}(\sigma))\;\geq\;-2\log F\!\!\left(\rho\,\big\|\,\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{M}}\circ\mathcal{M}(\rho)\right).

(14)

thereby establishing a sharp relation between entropy loss and recoverability valid for arbitrary channels.

Subsequently, Sutter, Berta, and Tomamichel
[
13
]
proved a universal strengthened DPI in terms of the measured relative entropy:

\displaystyle D(\rho\|\sigma)-D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{M}(\sigma))\geq\sup_{G}D_{G}(\rho\|\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{M}}\circ\mathcal{M}(\sigma)).

(15)

This result quantitatively relates entropy loss to recoverability for arbitrary channels.
Unlike in the classical setting, where exact refinements hold in terms of relative entropy, explicit counterexamples show that quantum strengthened inequalities in terms of relative entropy
D
are not possible; only the measured relative entropy
D_{M}
yields valid bounds
[
14
,
15
]
.
While strengthened recoverability inequalities are now well understood, no quantum analogue of the classical chain rule
Eq.

3
is known.
In the quantum setting, noncommutativity obstructs a decomposition of relative entropy into local divergences of point distributions.
Fang, Fawzi, Renner, and Sutter
[
16
]
made this precise observing that the naive inequality with the single-letter channel divergence
D(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})
does not hold. They furthermore showed that a meaningfull chain rule can be recovered in the many-copy setting by introducing the regularized channel relative entropy

\displaystyle D^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\,D(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}),

(16)

where

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N}):=\sup_{\rho_{RA}}D\!\Big((\operatorname{id}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{M})(\rho_{RA})\,\Big\|\,(\operatorname{id}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{N})(\rho_{RA})\Big).

(17)

With this regularization, they established the chain rule

\displaystyle D\!\Big(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\,\Big\|\,\mathcal{N}(\sigma)\Big)\;\leq\;D(\rho\|\sigma)+D^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N}).

(18)

Thus, unlike in the classical case, a quantum chain rule exists only in an asymptotic, many-copy sense, and it is precisely the regularization that guarantees its validity.

In the present work, we establish a complementary form of chain inequality that holds already at the single-copy level.

In
Section

2
we show a chain rule obtained from classical methods and some consequeneces, in
Section

3
we show some general entropy inequalities that conclude in a conditional quantum chain rule and finally in
Section

4
we discuss our results and propose some further work on the topic.

2
From Classical to Quantum Chain Rule

In this section we derive a quantum chain rule via the classical inequality
3
, adapted to the setting of density operators, POVMs, and quantum channels.

The main result of this section,
1
, establishes a chain rule based on partitions of states, rather than basis elements as in
6
.
The remainder of the section explores consequnces of this theorem, including an alternative proof of the data processing inequality and a semiclassical chain rule (see
4
).

We begin by recalling the relevant constructions involving ensemble partitions. For any state
\tau
and POVM
G=\{G_{j}\}
, define

\displaystyle\tau_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\tau}\,G_{j}^{T_{\tau}}\,\sqrt{\tau}}{\Tr[G_{j}\tau]},

\displaystyle P_{\tau}^{G}(j)

\displaystyle=\Tr[G_{j}\tau],

(19)

where
T_{\tau}
denotes the transpose in a diagonal basis of
\tau
and
P_{\tau}^{G}(j)=\Tr\left[G_{j}\tau\right]
is the probability distribution associated with measuring
G
on
\tau
.

Given a state
\tau
and a quantum channel
\varepsilon
, we also consider the bipartite state

\displaystyle\omega_{\tau}^{\varepsilon}=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\varepsilon)(\ket{\mkern-2mu\ket{\tau}\mkern-4mu}\mkern-5.0mu\bra{\mkern-4mu\bra{\tau}\mkern-2mu}),

(20)

where
\ket{\mkern-2mu\ket{\tau}\mkern-4mu}=\sum_{k}\sqrt{\tau_{k}}\,\ket{\tau_k,\tau_k}
is the canonical purification of
\tau
.

These constructions were first introduced by Fuchs
[
18
]
in the study of quantum conditional probabilities and later developed by Leifer and collaborators
[
19
,
20
]
as part of a program to generalize Bayes’ theorem to the quantum setting. In this framework, the unnormalised
\tau_{j}
are understood as ensemble partitions of
\tau
, providing the analogue of the Bayesian update rule for classical distributions under measurement.
More recently, Parzygnat and Fullwood
[
21
]
reformulated these constructions in a structural framework, treating states and channels in terms of their compositional properties rather than their specific operator representations, thereby giving structural interpretations of partitions as quantum conditionals. The bipartite state
\omega_{\tau}^{\varepsilon}
was proposed in
[
20
]
as a candidate for the quantum analogue of the joint distribution
p(y|x)p(x)
, although later work
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
has argued that, while it shares some features with joint classical distributions, it may not fully capture the operational or informational content of such objects. Building on these constructions, we obtain
1
.

The proof of our main result,
1
, starts from the states
\omega_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}
and
\omega_{\sigma}^{\mathcal{N}}
and performs a bipartite measurement to obtain classical probability distributions. These distributions can naturally be interpreted as measurements on
\mathcal{M}(\rho_{i})
and
\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{i})
. The result then follows from using the classical chain rule and removing the measurements with Uhlmann’s inequality and an extension to many copies. In the end we obtain a fully quantum, single copy equation.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\rho
,
\sigma
be quantum states on a finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{A}
. Let
G=\{G_{j}\}
be a POVM. Let
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
be completely positive trace preserving (CPTP) maps. Then

\displaystyle D(\rho\|\sigma)-D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\geq-\mathbb{E}_{P_{\rho}^{G}}D(\mathcal{M}(\rho_{j})\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{j})).

(21)

Proof.

We first construct classical states and maps from our quantum objects. Given a POVM
F=\{F_{i}\}
, define

\displaystyle p_{j}=P_{\rho}^{G}(j)=\Tr\left[G_{j}\rho\right],\quad q_{j}=P_{\sigma}^{G}(j)=\Tr\left[G_{j}\sigma\right];

(22)

and the stochastic matrices

\displaystyle M_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Tr\left[(G_{j}\otimes F_{i})\omega_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}\right]}{\Tr\left[G_{j}\rho\right]}=\Tr\left[F_{i}\mathcal{M}(\rho_{j})\right]=P_{\mathcal{M}(\rho_{j})}^{F}(i),

(23)

\displaystyle N_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Tr\left[(G_{j}\otimes F_{i})\omega_{\sigma}^{\mathcal{N}}\right]}{\Tr\left[G_{j}\sigma\right]}=\Tr\left[F_{i}\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{j})\right]=P_{\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{j})}^{F}(i).

(24)

The corresponding output distributions are

\displaystyle\tilde{p_{i}}=\sum_{j}M_{ij}p_{j}=\Tr\left[F_{i}\mathcal{M}(\rho)\right]=P_{\mathcal{M}(\rho)}^{F}(i),\quad\tilde{q_{i}}=\sum_{j}N_{ij}q_{j}=\Tr\left[F_{i}\mathcal{N}(\sigma)\right]=P_{\mathcal{N}(\sigma)}^{F}(i).

(25)

We plug these objects into
1
:

\displaystyle D(P_{\mathcal{M}(\rho)}^{F}\|P_{\mathcal{N}(\sigma)}^{F})-D(P_{\rho}^{G}\|P_{\sigma}^{G})\leq\mathbb{E}_{P_{\rho}^{G}}D(P_{\mathcal{M}(\rho_{j})}^{F}\|P_{\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{j})}^{F}),

(26)

which in Hayashi’s
[
17
]
notation for the measured entropy is

\displaystyle D_{F}(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))-D_{G}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq\mathbb{E}_{P_{\rho}^{G}}D_{F}(\mathcal{M}(\rho_{j})\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{j})).

(27)

By Uhlmann’s monotonicity of relative entropy under CPTP maps
[
4
]
, we may remove two of the measurements, yielding

\displaystyle D_{F}(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))-D(\rho\|\sigma)\leq\mathbb{E}_{P_{\rho}^{G}}D(\mathcal{M}(\rho_{j})\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{j})).

(28)

To remove the remaining measurement, consider
n
independent copies of the system.
That is, apply (with a small abuse of notation)
Eq.

28
to the states
\rho^{\otimes n}
,
\sigma^{\otimes n}
, measured with
G^{\otimes n}
and processed through the channels
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n},\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}
.
This gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{n}D_{F}(\mathcal{M}(\rho)^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma)^{\otimes n})&-\frac{1}{n}D(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n})\leq\mathbb{E}_{P_{\rho^{\otimes n}}^{G^{\otimes n}}}\frac{1}{n}D\left(\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{M}(\rho_{j_{k}})\Bigg\|\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{j_{k}})\right)\\
&\leq\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}_{P_{\rho^{\otimes n}}^{G^{\otimes n}}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}D(\mathcal{M}(\rho_{j_{k}})\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{j_{k}}))\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{P_{\rho}^{G}}D(\mathcal{M}(\rho_{j})\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{j})).\end{split}

(29)

By additivity
D(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n})=nD(\rho\|\sigma)
and by the asymptotic equipartition property
[
1
, Theorem 2.3]
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}D_{F}(\mathcal{M}(\rho)^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma)^{\otimes n})\;\to\;D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

(30)

Taking the limit of
Eq.

29
gives the result
Eq.

21
.
∎

A slightly strengthened inequality compared to

In the step below

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\leq D_{G}(\rho\|\sigma)+\mathbb{E}_{P_{\rho}^{G}}D(\mathcal{M}(\rho_{j})\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{j})).

Remark 3
.

An alternative definition of the bipartite state is

\displaystyle\omega_{\tau}^{\varepsilon}=(\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes\mathbb{I})(\mathbb{I}\otimes\varepsilon)(\outerproduct{\Phi^+}{\Phi^+})(\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes\mathbb{I}).

(32)

where
\ket{\Phi^+}
denotes the maximally entangled state in the canonical basis.
With this definition, the conditional states take the form

\displaystyle\tau_{j}=\frac{\left(\sqrt{\tau}G_{j}\sqrt{\tau}\right)^{T}}{\Tr\left[G_{j}\tau\right]},

(33)

where the transpose is taken in the basis associated with
\ket{\Phi^+}
(usually the canonical basis).
Substituting these expressions into
1
leads to a simplified right-hand side of the inequality.

The inequality in
Eq.

21
does not reduce to the standard data processing inequality when
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{N}
for the general case.
However, if the input states commute, i.e.,
[\rho,\sigma]=0
, one can choose a suitable measurement such that
Eq.

21
specializes to the data processing inequality:

Corollary 1
.

Let
\rho
,
\sigma
be commuting states on some Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{A}
with common basis
\{\Pi_{j}\}
and
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
be CPTP maps. Then

\displaystyle D(\rho\|\sigma)-D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\geq-\mathbb{E}_{p}D(\mathcal{M}(\Pi_{j})\|\mathcal{N}(\Pi_{j})).

(34)

Proof.

In the common basis of
\rho
and
\sigma
we write

\displaystyle\rho=\sum_{j}p_{j}\Pi_{j},\quad\sigma=\sum_{j}q_{j}\Pi_{j}.

(35)

Let
G
be the projective measurement on the common basis of
\rho,\sigma
, we can write
\rho_{j},\sigma_{j}
in
1
as

\displaystyle\rho_{j}=\frac{(\sqrt{\rho}\Pi_{j}\sqrt{\rho})^{T}}{\Tr\left[\Pi_{j}\rho\right]}=\frac{p_{j}\Pi_{j}^{T}}{p_{j}}=\Pi_{j}^{T},

(36)

and similarly
\sigma_{j}=\Pi_{j}^{T}
. Moreover,
P^{G}_{\rho}(j)=p_{j}
. Therefore
Eq.

21
becomes

\displaystyle D(\rho\|\sigma)-D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\geq-\mathbb{E}_{p}D(\mathcal{M}(\Pi_{j}^{T})\|\mathcal{N}(\Pi_{j}^{T})).

(37)

Finally, because the transpose is taken in the eigenbasis of
\rho
, in this case the canonical basis, each projector is self-transpose, leaving the final equation as:

\displaystyle D(\rho\|\sigma)-D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\geq-\mathbb{E}_{p}D(\mathcal{M}(\Pi_{j})\|\mathcal{N}(\Pi_{j})).

(38)

∎

This has the nice property that the right hand side relative entropies are independent of
\rho,\sigma
. This means, for example, that the equation reduces to Uhlmann’s inequality when
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{N}
.

We can use this equation to show joint convexity of relative entropy as well as two extensions:

Corollary 2
.

1
with
\rho=\sigma
is equivalent to the joint convexity of the relative entropy.

Proof.

(Corollary with
\rho=\sigma

\Rightarrow
Joint convexity)

Consider the probability distribution
p_{j}
and collections of states
\{\tau_{j}\}
,
\{\mu_{j}\}
on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space. Let
\{\Pi_{j}\}
be any family of orthogonal rank-one projectors with
\sum_{j}\Pi_{j}=\mathbb{I}
, and set
\rho=\sum_{j}p_{j}\Pi_{j}
.

Let
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}
be the classical-quantum channels

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(X)=\sum_{i}\tau_{i}\,\Tr[\Pi_{i}X],\qquad\mathcal{N}(X)=\sum_{i}\mu_{i}\,\Tr[\Pi_{i}X].

(39)

By
1
specialized to
\rho=\sigma
and to the decomposition of
\rho
in the basis

, we have

\displaystyle-D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\,\|\,\mathcal{N}(\rho))\;\geq\;-\sum_{j}p_{j}\,D\bigl(\mathcal{M}(\Pi_{j})\,\|\,\mathcal{N}(\Pi_{j})\bigr).

(40)

Using
Eq.

39
, this reads

\displaystyle-D\!\left(\sum_{j}p_{j}\tau_{j}\,\middle\|\,\sum_{j}p_{j}\mu_{j}\right)\;\geq\;-\sum_{j}p_{j}\,D(\tau_{j}\|\mu_{j}),

(41)

which is exactly joint convexity of the relative entropy.

(Joint convexity
\Rightarrow
Corollary with
\rho=\sigma
)

Conversely, assume joint convexity. Let

\displaystyle\tau_{j}=\mathcal{M}(\Pi_{j}),\qquad\mu_{j}=\mathcal{N}(\Pi_{j}).

Applying joint convexity to the ensembles

\displaystyle D\!\left(\sum_{j}p_{j}\tau_{j}\,\middle\|\,\sum_{j}p_{j}\mu_{j}\right)\;\leq\;\sum_{j}p_{j}D(\tau_{j}\|\mu_{j}).

\displaystyle D(p\|q)-D\left(\sum_{j}p_{j}\tau_{j}\right|\!\left|\sum_{j}q_{j}\mu_{j}\right)\geq-\sum_{j}p_{j}D(\tau_{j}\|\mu_{j}).

\displaystyle D(p\|q)-D\!\left(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\,\middle\|\,\mathcal{N}(\sigma)\right)\geq-\sum_{j}p_{j}\,D\!\big(\mathcal{M}(\Pi_{j})\,\|\,\mathcal{N}(\Pi_{j})\big).

Finally, in the general case where the states and channels are arbitrary,

Conditional Chain Rule via Entropy Bounds

In this section we establish a conditional chain rule (see
6
). The result follows as a corollary of
2
, which provides a general entropy inequality.
By choosing suitable auxiliary states, we derive both the chain rule and
5
, thereby extending the framework of Sutter
et al.

[
13
]
to different channels.

Unlike in the setting of Sutter
et al.
(see eq.
Eq.

14
), our framework does not currently allow optimization over all possible measurements. We believe this reflects a technical limitation of our proof method rather than a fundamental restriction of the approach itself.
The central tool in our approach is a recovery map that generalizes the standard Petz construction.

Whereas the Petz map is defined with respect to a single reference state, our formulation involves two distinct reference states. We define the resulting
twisted recovery map
, for
\alpha=(1-\mathrm{i}t)/2
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\alpha}_{\gamma,\sigma,\mathcal{M}}(X)=J^{\alpha}_{\sigma}\circ\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}\circ J^{-\alpha}_{\gamma}(X)

(51)

with
J_{\sigma}^{\alpha}(X)=\sigma^{\alpha}X\sigma^{\alpha^{*}}
.

Analogously to the standard Petz construction, the pair
(\gamma,\sigma)
plays the role of
reference states
that fix
the normalization convention on the input and output side of the recovery.
For
\gamma=\mathcal{M}(\sigma)
this reduces to the rotated Petz map
\mathcal{R}^{\alpha}_{\sigma,\mathcal{M}}
.

The map
\mathcal{R}^{\alpha}_{\gamma,\sigma,\mathcal{M}}
is, like the Petz map, uniquely defined in the support of
\gamma
and completely positive but not, in general, trace-non-increasing.
This follows directly from the fact that the reference states
(\gamma,\sigma)
are chosen independently: unless they satisfy
\gamma=\mathcal{M}(\sigma)
, the rescaling induced by
J_{\gamma}^{-\alpha}

and the normalization by
J_{\sigma}^{\alpha}
are mismatched, and the overall trace is dependent on the input, even in the support of
\gamma
.
The rotated Petz case, recovered for
(\gamma,\sigma)=(\mathcal{M}(\sigma),\sigma)
, is the unique configuration that restores trace preservation in the suport of
\gamma
.

As in the standard Petz construction, we consider the rotated family
\mathcal{R}^{\alpha}_{\gamma,\sigma,\mathcal{M}}
, weigthed by the real function
\beta_{0}(t)
, and define the averaged map

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{\gamma,\sigma,\mathcal{M}}=\int\beta_{0}(t)\,\mathcal{R}^{\alpha}_{\gamma,\sigma,\mathcal{M}}\,dt,\qquad\beta_{0}(t)=\tfrac{\pi}{2}\bigl(\cosh(\pi t)+1\bigr)^{-1}.

(52)

Averaging preserves complete positivity but does not generally provide any information on the trace.
The averaged map is trace-non-increasing only when the effective rescaling operator obtained from the integral is bounded from above by the identity.
This condition holds automatically for the matched Petz reference states but requires additional, channel-dependent relations for arbitrary pairs
(\gamma,\sigma)
.

The situations in which the map becomes trace-non-increasing naturally identify classes of states and channels for which probabilistic single-shot recovery is achievable.
In this sense, the recovery map functions both as a mathematical device for proving entropy inequalities and as a diagnostic for detecting operationally meaningful recovery regimes.

Theorem
2
formalizes the general entropy inequality underlying this construction. Its proof adapts techniques from Kwon
[
26
,
13
]
and relies on operator inequalities such as the Perels-Bogoliubov inequality.
From there, we derive specialized forms by making informed choices for the auxiliary states, eventually leading to the chain rule as a corollary.

Theorem 2

Proof.

Consider the quantity
\Tr\left[p_{i}^{-1}\Pi_{i}(J^{\alpha}_{\sigma}\circ\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}\circ J^{-\alpha}_{\gamma})(\omega)\right]

\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}\Pi_{i}

U
be the unitary that dilates the map
M(\cdot)=U\cdot\otimes\mathbb{I}_{E}U^{\dagger}

\alpha=\frac{1-\mathrm{i}\theta}{2}
then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Tr&\left[p_{i}^{-1}\Pi_{i}(J^{\alpha}_{\sigma}\circ\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}\circ J^{-\alpha}_{\gamma})(\omega)\right]\\

=&\Tr\left[e^{\log\Pi_{i}}e^{-\log p_{i}\Pi_{i}}e^{\alpha\log\sigma}e^{-\alpha U^{\dagger}\log\gamma U}e^{U^{\dagger}\log\omega U}e^{-\alpha^{*}U^{\dagger}\log\gamma U}e^{\alpha^{*}\log\sigma}\right]\\
=&\norm{e^{\alpha\log\Pi_{i}}e^{-\alpha\log p_{i}\Pi_{i}}e^{\alpha\log\sigma}e^{-\alpha U^\dagger\log\gamma U}e^{\alpha U^\dagger\log\omega U}}_{2}^{2}\\
=&\norm{\prod_{k=1}^5e^{2\alpha H_k}}_{2}^{2}.\end{split}

(55)

With
H_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\log\Pi_{i}

H_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\log p_{i}\Pi_{i}

H_{3}=\frac{1}{2}\log\sigma
,
H_{4}=-\frac{1}{2}U^{\dagger}\log\gamma U
and
H_{5}=\frac{1}{2}U^{\dagger}\log\omega U
.

We now integrate over
t
with respect to the weight
d\beta_{0}(t)
, apply the logarithm on both sides of the equality and average over the spectrum of
\rho
. The left hand side becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{i}p_{i}\log\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\beta_{0}(t)\Tr\left[p_{i}^{-1}\Pi_{i}(J^{\alpha}_{\sigma}\circ\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}\circ J^{-\alpha}_{\gamma})(\omega)\right]\\
&=\sum_{i}p_{i}\log\Tr\left[p_{i}^{-1}\Pi_{i}\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{\gamma,\sigma,\mathcal{M}}(\omega)\right]=\sum_{i}p_{i}\left(\log\Tr\left[\Pi_{i}\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{\gamma,\sigma,\mathcal{M}}(\omega)\right]-\log p_{i}\right)\\
&=-D_{\Pi}(\rho\|\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{\gamma,\sigma,\mathcal{M}}(\omega)),\end{split}

(56)

where
D_{\Pi}
denotes the measured entropy on the basis of
\rho
.

We not turn to the right hand side of the euqality.
By concavity of the logarithm, the inequality from
[
13
, Corollary 3.3]
and the Perels-Bogoliubov inequality
[
27
,
26
]
that for self-adjoint operators
F,R
with
\Tr[e^{R}]=1
is
\Tr\left[e^{F+R}\right]\geq e^{\Tr\left[Fe^{R}\right]}
. We appply this for
F=-\log\rho+\log\sigma-U^{\dagger}\log\gamma U+U^{\dagger}\log\omega U

e^{R}=\Pi_{i}
has trace 1.
Therefore

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{i}p_{i}&\log\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\beta_{0}(t)\norm{\prod_{k=1}^5e^{2\alpha H_k}}_{2}^{2}\geq 2\sum_{i}p_{i}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\beta_{0}(t)\log\norm{\prod_{k=1}^5e^{2\alpha H_k}}_{2}\\
\geq&2\sum_{i}p_{i}\log\norm{\exp(\sum_{k=1}^5 H_k)}_{2}\\
\geq&\sum_{i}p_{i}\Tr\left[e^{\log\Pi_{i}}\left(-\log\Pi_{i}p_{i}+\log\sigma-U^{\dagger}\log\gamma U+U^{\dagger}\log\omega U\right)\right]\\
=&\sum_{i}p_{i}\Tr\left[\Pi_{i}\left(-\log\Pi_{i}p_{i}+\log\sigma-U^{\dagger}\log\gamma U+U^{\dagger}\log\mathcal{M}(\rho)U-U^{\dagger}\log\mathcal{M}(\rho)U+U^{\dagger}\log\omega U\right)\right]\\

(57)

4
Discussion

This work establishes a single-letter chain inequality the for quantum relative entropy,
1
. This result is obtained by transporting the classical chain rule through POVM-induced ensemble partitions, followed by data processing and an asymptotic equipartition step. Additionally, in
2
, we introduce a general entropy inequality from a twisted recovery map in terms of a measured divergence
D_{\Pi}
. As corollaries, we recover both a conditioned chain rule in which the right hand side maps act map on the eigenbasis of the initial state
\rho
(
6
) and a two-channel strengthened DPI (
5
), connecting our results to those ones in
[
12
,
13
]
.

Strengthened data-processing inequalities are expressed in terms of the measured relative entropy with respect to a universal rotated-Petz mixture that exactly recovers the reference state
[
12
,
13
]
. Our twisted recovery map, by contrast, depends on two reference states
(\gamma,\sigma)
and is completely positive but not generally trace-non-increasing.

This construction refines the strengthened data-processing framework by incorporating the recoverability mechanism into a single-letter chain rule, linking entropy loss under a channel to the reconstruction of the input state.

However, unlike
[
13
]
, our proof technique does not optimize over POVMs; thus
D_{\Pi}
remains basis-tied to the measurement basis of
\rho
. Hoever we believe that restriction stems from the structure of the proof rather than from any fundamental obstruction of the approach.

Fang, Fawzi, Renner, and Sutter
[
16
]
proved that a genuine quantum chain rule holds in infinitely many-copy regime via the regularized channel divergence
D^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})
. Our results are complementary: they provide informative single-letter bounds that reduce to DPI under commutation assumptions (
1
) and recover joint convexity (
2
), but cannot, in full generality, replace the regularized term.

The POVM-induced partitions
(\rho_{j},\sigma_{j})
in
1
can be viewed as a link between classical Bayesian updating and quantum conditioning
[
18
,
20
,
21
]
.
From this perspective
1
decomposes the global changes in distinguishability caused by the channels
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
into an average over conditional branches processed by the same channels.

In the commuting case, the bound becomes effectively basis-free on the right-hand side (
1
), reproducing the structural role of point-mass divergences in the classical chain rule.

The chain inequality in
4
depends on the pairing of eigenprojectors; optimizing over permutations would strengthen the bound (Remark
5
). For the conditional chain rule (
6
), the sufficient trace condition
\Tr[\Pi_{\rho}\,\mathcal{R}_{\gamma,\sigma,\mathcal{M}}(\omega)]\leq 1
guarantees a nonnegative
D_{\Pi}
term; Appendix
B
shows that some condition of this form is
necessary
in general, since unconditional formulations fail on explicit families.

Single-letter chain bounds are useful in regimes where reuglarization is infeasable or uncecessary.
They capture information-theoretic behavior in one-shot or few-shot scenarios—such as hypothesis testing, or channel certification or finite-size security estimates in quantum key distribution. They also offer structural insight in resource-theoretic contexts, where monotones inherit data-processing and convexity properties from relative entropy.

4.1
Outlook and Open Questions

The results in this work suggest a few concrete lines of progress. We group them into three different cathegorises:

–Choosing the partition and pairing.

The lower bound in Theorem
1
depends on the POVM that generates the ensemble partitions. A first goal is to characterize an optimal
G^{\star}(\rho,\sigma;\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
or to identify families that admit closed forms or efficiently checkable optimality conditions (projective measurements, square-root/Pretty Good Measurement, Naimark-dilated projectors). In the semiclassical variant (Corollary
4
), the value depends on how eigenprojectors of
\rho
are paired with those of
\sigma
; the natural problem is to choose a permutation
\pi
that minimizes

\sum_{j}p_{j}\,D(\mathcal{M}(\Pi_{j})\|\mathcal{N}(\tilde{\Pi}_{\pi(j)}))
.

–Sharpening the structure of the bounds.

Two aspects deserve attention. First, in the twisted-recovery inequality (
2
,
5
), replacing the basis-tied
D_{\Pi}
by
\sup_{G}D_{G}
would remove what we blelive is technical artifact of the proof.
This likely requires a different route (e.g., a variational representation of relative entropy) that preserves the two-reference recovery structure. Second, in
6
it is natural to ask for necessary and sufficient conditions under which the averaged rotated-Petz map
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{N}(\sigma),\sigma,\mathcal{M}}
is trace-non-increasing on a task-relevant cone (for instance, on
\mathrm{range}(\mathcal{N})
). It would also be useful to identify regimes of tightness for
1
(commuting inputs, classical-quantum or degradable channels), and to quantify the gap to the many-copy chain rule based on
D^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})
.

–Extensions.

A natural next step is to move beyond Umegaki relative entropy and treat Petz- and sandwiched Renyi divergences, as well as hypothesis-testing and smooth min/max divergences, to delineate precisely which divergences admit single-letter chain inequalities (without regularization) and under what support or parameter conditions. In parallel, extending the results to infinite-dimensional settings under energy constraints (e.g., bounded mean energy with respect to a fixed Hamiltonian) would make them applicable to bosonic and continuous-variable systems.

In this section we show a simple proof of the classical chain rule using Jensen’s Theorem
[

Proposition 1

(Chain rule of the relative entropy)
.

Let
p,q
be probability distributions and
M,N
stochastic maps. Then

\displaystyle D(p\|q)-D(Mp\|Nq)\geq-\mathbb{E}_{p}\left[D(M\delta_{j}\|N\delta_{j})\right],

(65)

Where
\delta_{j}
is the delta probability distribution at point
j
.

Proof.

Start by considering two classical probabilities on finite dimension sets and two stochastic maps acting on them. Let
p,q
be probability distributions and
M,N
stochastic maps. We consider the output probability distributions

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{i}=(Mq)_{i}=\sum_{j}M_{ij}p_{j},\quad\tilde{q}_{i}=(Nq)_{i}=\sum_{j}N_{ij}q_{j}.

(66)

With this definition we can find the following identity. Consider the quantity

\displaystyle\exp{-\log{\frac{M_{ij}p_j}{N_{ij}q_j}}+\log{\frac{\tilde{p}_i}{\tilde{q}_i}}}.

(67)

We calculate the average of this quantity over
M_{ij}p_{j}
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{Mp}{\exp{-\log{\frac{M_{ij}p_j}{N_{ij}q_j}}+\log{\frac{\tilde{p}_i}{\tilde{q}_i}}}}&=\sum_{ij}M_{ij}p_{j}\exp{-\log{\frac{M_{ij}p_j}{N_{ij}q_j}}+\log{\frac{\tilde{p}_i}{\tilde{q}_i}}}\\
&=\sum_{ij}M_{ij}p_{j}\frac{\tilde{p}_{i}}{\tilde{q}_{i}}\frac{N_{ij}q_{j}}{M_{ij}p_{j}}=\sum_{ij}\frac{\tilde{q}_{i}}{\tilde{q}_{i}}\tilde{p}_{i}=1.\end{split}

(68)

With this we acquire the following identity for these processes

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{Mp}{\exp{-\log{\frac{M_{ij}p_j}{N_{ij}q_j}}+\log{\frac{\tilde{p}_i}{\tilde{q}_i}}}}=1.

(69)

From this identity and using Jensen’s inequality we can obtain a classical entropy inequality.

From
Eq.

69
we apply Jensen’s inequality:

\displaystyle 1=\mathbb{E}_{Mp}{\exp{-\log{\frac{M_{ij}p_j}{N_{ij}q_j}}+\log{\frac{\tilde{p}_i}{\tilde{q}_i}}}}\geq\exp{\mathbb{E}_{Mp}{-\log{\frac{M_{ij}p_j}{N_{ij}q_j}}+\log{\frac{\tilde{p}_i}{\tilde{q}_i}}}}.

(70)

Note that since
M
is a stochastic map, for a fixed
j

M_{ij}
is a probability distribution on
i
and therefore
\sum_{i}M_{ij}=1
. With this we can calculate the result

\displaystyle\begin{split}0&\geq\mathbb{E}_{Mp}\left(-\log{\frac{M_{ij}p_j}{N_{ij}q_j}}+\log{\frac{\tilde{p}_i}{\tilde{q}_i}}\right)=\sum_{ij}M_{ij}p_{j}\left(-\log{\frac{M_{ij}p_j}{N_{ij}q_j}}+\log{\frac{\tilde{p}_i}{\tilde{q}_i}}\right)\\
&=-\sum_{ij}M_{ij}p_{j}\left[\log{\frac{M_{ij}}{N_{ij}}}+\log{\frac{p_j}{q_j}}\right]+\sum_{i}\tilde{p}_{i}\log{\frac{\tilde{p}_i}{\tilde{q}_i}}\\
&=-\sum_{j}p_{j}\left(\sum_{i}M_{ij}\log{\frac{M_{ij}}{N_{ij}}}\right)-\sum_{j}\left(\sum_{i}M_{ij}\right)p_{j}\log{\frac{p_j}{q_j}}+D(Mp\|Nq)\\
&=-\mathbb{E}_{p}D(M\delta_{j}\|N\delta_{j})-D(p\|q)+D(Mp\|Nq).\end{split}

(71)

∎

Appendix B

In
6
we saw a sufficient condition for a chain rule. In this appendix we show an example that demonstrates that there are cases in which the chain rule is false, showing that there exists a necessary condition for the fulfillement of the chain rule. Consider the regularised version of the chain rule:

\displaystyle D(\rho\|\sigma)-D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\geq-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{n}}\left[D(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}(\Pi_{k})\|\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}(\Pi_{k}))\right],

(72)

where
\Pi_{k}
are the eigenprojectors of
\rho_{n}=\rho^{\otimes n}
. This version can be obtained by applying
6
to
\rho^{\otimes n}
,
\sigma^{\otimes n}
,
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}
, and then taking the limit
n\to\infty
. Therefore a violation of
Eq.

72
implies a violation of
Eq.

62
.

We provide a simple class of counterexamples in
Example

1
and a generalisation of this class in
Example

2
, which shows that the states that violate
Eq.

72
are not some measure 0 set.

Example 1
.

We show a simple counterexample to
Eq.

72
. Let
d=2
,
\rho=\outerproduct{0}{0}
,
\sigma=(1-\varepsilon)\outerproduct{+}{+}+\varepsilon\outerproduct{-}{-}
,
\mathcal{M}(x)=\Tr(x)\outerproduct{-}{-}
and
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}
, where
=\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}
denotes the pinching map on the basis of
\sigma

[
17
]
. Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}D(\rho\|\sigma)&=-\expectationvalue{\swich{\log(1-\varepsilon)\ketbra{+}+\log\varepsilon\ketbra{-}}}{0}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\log(1-\varepsilon)+\log\varepsilon\right)\\
D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))&=D(\outerproduct{-}{-}\|\sigma)=-\expectationvalue{\swich{\log(1-\varepsilon)\ketbra{+}+\log\varepsilon\ketbra{-}}}{-}=-\log\varepsilon\end{split}

(73)

Therefore
D(\rho\|\sigma)-D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))=\frac{1}{2}\left(\log\varepsilon-\log(1-\varepsilon)\right),
which approaches
-\infty
when
\varepsilon
goes to 0. We now need to check that the right hand side of
Eq.

72
is finite to finish the counterexample.

Consider the projectors of
\rho_{n}=\rho^{\otimes n}
. Because
\rho
is pure,
\rho_{n}
will have only 2 eigenvalues

\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}(\Pi_{k})=\outerproduct{-}{-}^{\otimes n}

Example 2
.

Let
\rho=(1-p)\outerproduct{0}{0}+p\outerproduct{1}{1}
,
p\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
;
\sigma=(1-\varepsilon)\outerproduct{\pe}{\pe}+\varepsilon\outerproduct{\me}{\me}
with
\ket{\pe}=\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\ket{0}+\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\ket{1}
and
\ket{\me}=\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\ket{0}-\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\ket{1}
;
\mathcal{M}(x)=\Tr(x)\outerproduct{\me}{\me}
and
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}
.

We will follow the same steps as in
Example

1
. First we calculate the relative entropies:

\displaystyle\begin{split}D(\rho\|\sigma)=&-S(p)-(1-p)\expectationvalue{\swich{\log(1-\varepsilon)\ketbra{\pe}+\log\varepsilon\ketbra{\me}}}{0}\\
&-p\expectationvalue{\swich{\log(1-\varepsilon)\ketbra{\pe}+\log\varepsilon\ketbra{\me}}}{1}\\
=&-S(p)-(1-p)\left[\cos^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}\log\left(1-\varepsilon\right)+\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}\log\varepsilon\right]\\
&-p\left[\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}\log\left(1-\varepsilon\right)+\cos^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}\log\varepsilon\right]\\
=&-S(p)-\log\left(1-\varepsilon\right)\left[(1-p)\cos^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}+p\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}\right]\\
&-\log\varepsilon\left[(1-p)\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}+p\cos^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}\right]\\
D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))=&D(\outerproduct{\me}{\me}\|\sigma)=-\expectationvalue{\swich{\log(1-\varepsilon)\ketbra{\pe}+\log\varepsilon\ketbra{\me}}}{\me}\\
=&-\log\varepsilon\end{split}

(78)

Note that the coefficients of
\log(1-\varepsilon)
,
\log\varepsilon
in
D(\rho\|\sigma)
add up to 1. Therefore

\displaystyle D(\rho\|\sigma)-D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))=-S(p)+\left[\log{\varepsilon}-\log\left(1-\varepsilon\right)\right]\left[(1-p)\cos^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}+p\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}\right].

(79)

Similarly to in
Example

1

\left[\log{\varepsilon}-\log\left(1-\varepsilon\right)\right]
can be infinitely negtive for small
\varepsilon
and
\left[(1-p)\cos^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}+p\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}\right]
is a strictly positive coefficient, therefore the left hand side of
Eq.

72
goes to
-\infty
.

\rho_{n}
will now have
n+1
eigenspaces, each of dimension
\binom{n}{k}
, for
k\in\left\{0,\dots,n\right\}
with eigenvalues
(1-p)^{k}p^{n-k}
. Let
\mathbb{X}_{k}=\left\{x\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}\text{ s.t. }\absolutevalue{x}=k\right\},
where
\absolutevalue{x}
is the number of ones a sequence has. The
k
th projector will be
\Pi_{k}=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{X}_{k}}\outerproduct{x}{x}
. Similarly to
Example

1
,
\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}(x)=\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}^{\otimes n}(x)=\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\in\left\{\tilde{+},\tilde{-}\right\}}\outerproduct{i_1\dots i_n}{i_1\dots i_n}x\outerproduct{i_1\dots i_n}{i_1\dots i_n}
and we are only concerned with the coefficients associated to
\outerproduct{\me}{\me}^{\otimes n}
. This coefficients are

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bra{\me}^{\otimes n}\Pi_{k}\ket{\me}^{\otimes n}=&\bra{\me}^{\otimes n}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{X}_{k}}\outerproduct{x}{x}\ket{\me}^{\otimes n}=\sin^{2k}\frac{\theta}{2}\cos^{2(n-k)}\frac{\theta}{2}\absolutevalue{\mathbb{X}_k}\\
=&\binom{n}{k}\sin^{2k}\frac{\theta}{2}\cos^{2(n-k)}\frac{\theta}{2}.\end{split}

(80)

The eigenvalue associated to the
k
th eigenspace is
(1-p)^{k}p^{n-k}
. Due to
\Tr\left[\Pi_{k}\right]=\binom{n}{k}
, a coefficient
\binom{n}{k}
appears outside the relative entropy, since

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{n}}\left[D(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}(\Pi_{k})\|\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}(\Pi_{k}))\right]=&\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(1-p)^{k}p^{n-k}\log\left(\sin^{2k}\frac{\theta}{2}\cos^{2(n-k)}\frac{\theta}{2}\right).\end{split}

(81)

The limit can be resolved exactly. We can consider first the case
p=0
. While the discussion on the projectors is false for

because all there is a single eigenspace and eigenvalue for

81

survives and it simplifies to
\log\left(\sin^{2n}\frac{\theta}{2}\right)
. The limit is then
\log\left(\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}\right)
We can find the values of

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left[\log{\varepsilon}-\log\left(1-\varepsilon\right)\right]\cos^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}<\log\left(\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\Leftrightarrow\varepsilon<\frac{(\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2})^{\frac{1}{\cos^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}}}}{1+(\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2})^{\frac{1}{\cos^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}}}}\end{split}

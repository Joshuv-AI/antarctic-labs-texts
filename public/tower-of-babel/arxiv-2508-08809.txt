Improved existence time for Whitham and a Whitham-Boussinesq system
Didier Pilod, Sigmund Selberg, Nadia Skoglund Taki, Achenef Tesfahun

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the time of existence of the solutions to two full dispersion models derived from the water waves equations in the shallow water regime: the Whitham equation and a Whitham-Boussinesq system in dimension one and two. The regime is characterized by the nonlinearity parameter ϵ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \epsilon\in(0,1] and the shallow water parameter μ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \mu\in(0,1] . We extend the lifespan of the solution beyond the hyperbolic time ϵ − 1 \epsilon^{-1} . More precisely, we establish well-posedness on the timescale of order μ 1 4 − ​ ϵ ( − 5 4 ) + \mu^{\frac{1}{4}^{-}}\epsilon^{(-\frac{5}{4})^{+}} in the one-dimensional case, and of order μ 1 4 − ​ ϵ ( − 3 2 ) + \mu^{\frac{1}{4}^{-}}\epsilon^{(-\frac{3}{2})^{+}} in dimension two. We emphasize that for the two-dimensional case, we obtain a time of existence of order ϵ − 5 4 \epsilon^{-\frac{5}{4}} in the long wave regime μ ∼ ϵ \mu\sim\epsilon . This kind of result seems to be new, even for the Boussinesq systems. The proofs combine energy methods with Strichartz estimates. Here, a key ingredient is to obtain new refined Strichartz estimates that include the small parameter μ \mu . These techniques are robust and could be adapted to improve the lifespan of solutions for other equations and systems of the same form.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
General setting

In general, nonlinear dispersive equations and systems are not derived from first principles. Instead, they arise as asymptotic models obtained from more general systems under a suitable scaling as small parameters tend to zero. These asymptotic models are intended to capture the dynamics of the original system within the regime defined by these scaling assumptions. In this article, we focus on general models of the form

(1.1)

\partial_{t}U+L_{\mu}U+\epsilon F(U,\nabla U)=0,

where
\epsilon
and
\mu
are small parameters taking into account the nonlinear and dispersive effects, respectively. Here,
L_{\mu}
is a linear dispersive operator and
F(U,\nabla U)
is a quadratic nonlinearity.

By using hyperbolic techniques, the time of existence of such systems is typically of order
1/\epsilon
. The main objective of this paper is to improve the lifespan with respect to the small parameters
\epsilon
and
\mu
by exploiting the dispersive effects of the model.

A typical example of (
1.1
) is the
abcd
Boussinesq systems modelling surface water waves given by

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\eta_{t}+\nabla\cdot{\bf v}+\epsilon\nabla\cdot(\eta{\bf v})+\mu[a\nabla\cdot\Delta{\bf v}-b\Delta\eta_{t}]=0\\
{\bf v}_{t}+\nabla\eta+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\nabla(|{\bf v}|^{2})+\mu[c\nabla\Delta\eta-d\Delta{\bf v}_{t}]=0.\end{array}\right.

Here,
\eta
measures the deviation of the free surface from its equilibrium state and
{\bf v}
is an approximation of the horizontal velocity taken at a prescribed depth. The constants
a
,
b
,
c
, and
d
are modelling parameters satisfying the constraint
a+b+c+d=\frac{1}{3}
.
The regime of an asymptotic model is characterized by the nonlinearity parameter
\epsilon
and the shallow water parameter
\mu
defined by

\displaystyle\epsilon=\frac{\mathtt{a}}{H}\ \ \ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ \ \ \mu=\frac{H^{2}}{\lambda^{2}},

where

is the typical amplitude of the wave,

The Boussinesq systems (

Comparing the KdV equation to the full water waves equations, the dispersion relation is too strong in high frequencies. As a consequence, it fails to capture important physical phenomena such as wave breaking and peaking waves. As an alternative to KdV, Whitham

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta+\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\partial_{x}\eta+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\partial_{x}(\eta^{2})=0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\mu}(\xi)=\frac{\tanh(\sqrt{\mu}|\xi|)}{\sqrt{\mu}|\xi|}.

Whitham introduced the equation formally, without the small parameters
\epsilon

A similar procedure to that of Whitham can be applied to the Boussinesq systems (

1.2.
Former well-posedness results

The typical lifespan for solutions to a system of the form (
1.1
) is the
hyperbolic
time
1/\epsilon
. This can be readily established by using energy methods for unidirectional equations and for systems with symmetric nonlinearity (see
[
5
]
).

As a result, the initial value problem (IVP) associated with the Whitham equation (
1.3
) is well-posed in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, for
s>\frac{3}{2}
, on the timescale
1/\epsilon
uniformly in
\mu
. In the case
\mu=1
, a simple rescaling shows that this problem is equivalent to considering initial data of size
\epsilon
and trying to extend the existence time beyond the hyperbolic timescale. Assuming that
(\epsilon,\mu)
are in the
small data
regime:

\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{SD}}:=\left\{(\epsilon,\mu):\mu\sim 1,\,0<\epsilon\ll 1\right\},

Ehrnström and Wang
[
17
]
obtained well-posedeness for the Whitham equation on the timescale
1/\epsilon^{2}
in
H^{N}(\mathbb{R})
with
N\geq 3
by combining hyperbolic techniques with a normal form transformation in the spirit of Shatah
[
49
]
. The proof in
[
17
]
is an adaptation of the methods introduced by Hunter, Ifrim, Tataru, and Wong
[
23
]
for the Burgers-Hilbert equation (see also
[
21
]
). These results were also extended to the fractional KdV equation in
[
16
]
. Additionally, similar techniques have also been employed in more complex settings, notably for the Benjamin-Ono equation
[
27
]
and for the water waves system (see, e.g,
[
1
,
22
]
).

The analysis becomes more delicate for systems with non-symmetric nonlinearities. One then has to find suitable symmetrizers and carefully track the small parameter in the commutator estimates. This was achieved by Saut and Xu
[
46
]
for the Boussinesq systems (
1.2
), where they obtained well-posedness on a large time of order
1/\epsilon
in the long wave regime
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{LW}}
(see also
[
40
,
44
,
48
,
9
]
). Moreover, in the small data regime
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{SD}}
, Saut and Xu
[
47

extended the hyperbolic time of existence for a strongly dispersive Boussinesq system to
\epsilon^{-\frac{4}{3}}
by using a normal form transformation.

The IVPs associated with the Whitham-Boussinesq systems (

1.6

to be well-posed in the shallow water regime
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{SW}}
on the timescale

for initial data in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

for

1.3.
Main results

In this paper, we investigate the time of existence of the solutions to the Whitham equation (
1.3
) and the Whitham-Boussinesq systems (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) with dependence on the small parameters
(\epsilon,\mu)
. More precisely, we establish the well-posedness of (
1.3
) and (
1.5
) on the timescale of order
\mu^{\frac{1}{4}^{-}}\epsilon^{(-\frac{5}{4})^{+}}
and of (
1.6
) on the timescale of order
\mu^{\frac{1}{4}^{-}}\epsilon^{(-\frac{3}{2})^{+}}
. The proofs consist of compactness methods combined with Strichartz estimates. These techniques are robust and could be adapted to improve the lifespan of solutions for other equations and systems of the form (
1.1
).

The result for the Whitham equation is summarized below.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
s>\frac{13}{8}
,
\epsilon,\mu\in(0,1]
, and
\eta_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
. Then there exists a positive time

(1.9)

\displaystyle T_{\epsilon,\mu}=c^{-1}\epsilon^{-1}\Big(\frac{\mu}{\epsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}^{-}}(\lVert\eta_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}})^{(-\frac{5}{4})^{+}}

such that (
1.3
) has a unique solution

\displaystyle\eta\in C([0,T_{\epsilon,\mu}];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))\cap C^{1}([0,T_{\epsilon,\mu}];H^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}))

satisfying the initial condition
\eta(0)=\eta_{0}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T_{\epsilon,\mu}]}\lVert\eta(t)\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}\lesssim\lVert\eta_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}.

Further, there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
\eta_{0}
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
such that the flow map

\displaystyle F_{\epsilon,\mu}:\mathcal{U}\rightarrow C([0,\frac{T_{\epsilon,\mu}}{2}];H^{s}(\mathbb{R})),\ \ \ \ \eta_{0}\mapsto\eta

is continuous.

Remark 1.2
.

The result in
[
17
]
provides a larger time of existence in the small data regime
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{SD}}
, namely
T\sim\epsilon^{-2}\geq\epsilon^{-\frac{5}{4}}
. In comparison, our result incorporates the additional small parameter
\mu
and lowers the regularity for the initial data to
s>\frac{13}{8}
. Moreover, in the long wave regime
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{LW}}
, we recover the hyperbolic timescale
\epsilon^{-1}
. Although our result does not reach the timescale achieved in
[
17
]
, the proof is both robust and conceptually straightforward, relying solely on Strichartz estimates. Combining a normal form transformation with the techniques in this paper will be an objective for future work.

.

In the one-dimensional case, we recover the result in

1.4.
Strategy and outline

Broadly speaking, the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.6
follow the same strategy, combining energy estimates with Strichartz estimates. We first illustrate the approach for the simpler case of the Whitham equation in Theorem
1.1
, and then make a few remarks on the extensions needed to prove Theorem
1.6
.

The energy associated with the Whitham equation (
1.3
) is the square of the classical
H^{s}
-norm.
By differentiating in time and using (
1.3
), we obtain

(1.13)

\displaystyle\frac{\>\mathrm{d}}{\>\mathrm{d}t}\Big(\lVert\eta\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq c\epsilon\lVert\partial_{x}\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lVert\eta\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2},

which yields by using a Grönwall argument

(1.14)

\displaystyle\lVert\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}}^{2}\leq\mathrm{e}^{c\epsilon T^{1/2}\lVert\partial_{x}\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{x}}}\lVert\eta_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2}.

The Sobolev embedding
H^{\frac{1}{2}^{+}}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
provides the classical time of existence
1/\epsilon
.

To improve the lifespan, we will rely on the Strichartz estimates for the linear part of (
1.3
) to control
\lVert\partial_{x}\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{x}}
, (see Remark
1.10
for some comments on the two-dimensional extension). If this estimate is applied directly to the Duhamel formulation of (
1.3
), it yields a lifespan of order
\epsilon^{-1}\min\big((\mu/\epsilon)^{\frac{1}{3}},(\mu/\epsilon)^{\frac{1}{7}}\big)
, which corresponds to
\epsilon^{-\frac{8}{7}}\mu^{\frac{1}{7}}
when
\epsilon<\mu
.

To fix this shift imbalance, we introduce a family of dispersive Strichartz estimates depending on two free parameters
\omega
and
\rho
(see Theorem
2.1
). The first one,
\omega
, characterizes the splitting of the function in high and low frequencies. We estimate the low-frequency part directly by using Bernstein’s inequality. To estimate the high-frequency part, we first use a dyadic decomposition in frequency, and then split the time interval into small pieces of size
\rho(\lambda)
depending on the dyadic frequency
\lambda
. The Strichartz estimate is then applied to each of these subintervals, and the resulting bounds are summed over all intervals. The technique in the proof of Theorem
2.1
is inspired from the works in
[
50
]
,
[
8
, Lemma 3.6]
,
[
33
, Lemma 2.1]
,
[
31
, Proposition 2.8]
,
[
38
, Proposition 2.3]
, and
[
42
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Accordingly, we obtain the enhanced lifespan in Theorem
1.1
by choosing the parameters
\omega
and
\rho
such that the small parameters
\epsilon

2.
Dispersive estimates for an inhomogeneous equation

To extend the time of existence of a general model (
1.1
), we consider a generic dispersive linear inhomogeneous IVP of the form

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+M(D)u=F\\
u(x,0)=f(x)\end{cases}

for a given symbol
M(\xi)\in\mathbb{R}
, whose solution
u(x,t)
,
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
, is given by

\displaystyle u(t)=S(t)f(x)+i\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)F(s)\>\mathrm{d}s

where
S(t)=e^{itM(D)}
. Fixing
(q,r)\in[2,\infty]\times[2,\infty)
, we assume that the frequency-localized estimate

(2.2)

\lVert S(t)P_{\lambda}f\rVert_{L_{t}^{q}(\mathbb{R};L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim A(\lambda)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}}\lVert P_{\lambda}f\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

holds for all
\lambda>0
, for given
A(\lambda)\in(0,\infty)
. With the estimate (
2.2
), we derive a family of dispersive Strichartz estimates depending on two parameters
\omega
and
\rho
, which can be chosen freely (see Theorem
2.1
). The parameter
\omega
represents the splitting of the function in high and low frequencies. For the high-frequency part, we divide the time interval into small pieces of size
\rho(\lambda)
, where
\lambda
is the dyadic frequency. Then, for suitable choices of
(\omega,\rho)
, our generalization can be applied to these types of IVPs to obtain
refined
Strichartz estimates (see, e.g., Corollary
3.4
).
More precisely, we have the following result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}\backslash\{0\}
and
T>0
. Assume that
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
solves
(
2.1
)
for a given symbol
M(\xi)\in\mathbb{R}
, and that for some fixed pair
(q,r)\in[2,\infty]\times[2,\infty)
the associated unitary group
S(t)=e^{itM(D)}
satisfies
(
2.2
)
for all
\lambda>0
dyadic, for given
A(\lambda)\in(0,\infty)
. Then

(2.3)

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\omega^{d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\tilde{r}})}T^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert J^{\tilde{\theta}}P_{\leq\omega}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\omega^{-\gamma}T^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert J^{\theta+\gamma}\rho(|D|)^{-\frac{1}{q}}A(|D|)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\lVert J^{\theta}\rho(|D|)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}A(|D|)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}}F\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}

for any
\tilde{r}\in[2,\infty)
,
\tilde{\theta}>d/\tilde{r}
,
\theta>d/r
, and arbitrarily small
\gamma>0
. Here we are free to choose
\omega>0
and
\rho(\lambda)\in(0,T]
.

Remark 2.2
.

The proof is inspired by the works in
[
50
]
,
[
8
, Lemma 3.6]
,
[
33
, Lemma 2.1]
,
[
31
, Proposition 2.8]
,
[
38
, Proposition 2.3]
, and
[
42
, Lemma 4.1]
. Compared to these results, we are concerned with enhanced lifetime. This requires a different splitting of the high and low frequencies
\omega
and size of the small time intervals
\rho(\lambda)
to obtain Corollary
3.4
. Here, both
\omega
and
\rho
will be chosen to depend on the small parameter
\mu
, which will result in the enhanced lifespans in Theorems
1.1
and
1.6
.

Before we can prove Theorem
2.1
, we recall Bernstein’s lemma.

Lemma 2.3

(Bernstein’s lemma)
.

For
\alpha>0
, we have the estimate

\lVert P_{\leq\alpha}f\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\alpha^{d(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}\lVert P_{\leq\alpha}f\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

for
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

For a number
\omega>0
to be chosen, we split into low and high frequencies:

\displaystyle u=P_{\leq\omega}u+\sum_{\lambda>\omega}P_{\lambda}u=:u_{1}+u_{2},

giving us

(2.4)

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}\leq\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}+\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}.

Control of
\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}
in (
2.4
)

By a Sobolev embedding and Bernstein’s lemma in
x
, and Hölder’s inequality in time, the low-frequency part is estimated by

(2.5)

\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim\lVert J^{\tilde{\theta}}P_{\leq\omega}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{\tilde{r}}}\lesssim\omega^{d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\tilde{r}})}T^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert J^{\tilde{\theta}}P_{\leq\omega}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

for any
\tilde{r}\in[2,\infty)
and
\tilde{\theta}>d/\tilde{r}
.

Control of
\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}
in (
2.4
)

By a Sobolev embedding, the Littlewood-Paley square function theorem, and Minkowski’s integral inequality, we obtain

\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim\lVert J^{\theta}u_{2}\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{r}}\lesssim\lVert\sqrt{\sum_{\lambda>\omega}\left|P_{\lambda}J^{\theta}u\right|^{2}}\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{r}}\lesssim\sqrt{\sum_{\lambda>\omega}\lVert P_{\lambda}J^{\theta}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{r}}^{2}}

for any
\theta>d/r
. Fix
\lambda>\omega
and write
[0,T]=\bigcup_{j\in J}I_{j}
where
I_{j}=[a_{j},b_{j}]
are almost disjoint,
|I_{j}|=\rho
and
\#J=T/\rho
, where
\rho=\rho(\lambda)\in(0,T]
remains to be chosen. Then, applying Hölder’s inequality in time, we estimate

\lVert P_{\lambda}J^{\theta}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{r}}=\lVert\lVert P_{\lambda}J^{\theta}u\rVert_{L_{I_{j}}^{2}L_{x}^{r}}\rVert_{\ell^{2}_{j\in J}}\leq\rho^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\lVert\lVert P_{\lambda}J^{\theta}u\rVert_{L_{I_{j}}^{q}L_{x}^{r}}\rVert_{\ell^{2}_{j\in J}}.

On
I_{j}
we write

u(t)=S(t-a_{j})u(a_{j})+i\int_{a_{j}}^{t}S(t-s)F(s)\,ds

and apply (
2.2
) to obtain

\displaystyle\lVert P_{\lambda}J^{\theta}u\rVert_{L_{I_{j}}^{q}L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim A(\lambda)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}}\left(\lVert P_{\lambda}J^{\theta}u(a_{j})\rVert_{L_{x}^{2}}+\int_{a_{j}}^{t}\lVert P_{\lambda}J^{\theta}F(s)\rVert_{L_{x}^{2}}\,ds\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim A(\lambda)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}}\left(\lVert P_{\lambda}J^{\theta}u\rVert_{L_{I_{j}}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert P_{\lambda}J^{\theta}F\rVert_{L_{I_{j}}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}\right).

Taking the
\ell^{2}_{j\in J}
norm, we conclude that

\displaystyle\lVert P_{\lambda}J^{\theta}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\rho^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}A(\lambda)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}}\left(\sqrt{\#J}\lVert P_{\lambda}J^{\theta}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert P_{\lambda}J^{\theta}F\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\rho^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}A(\lambda)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}}\left(T^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lVert P_{\lambda}J^{\theta}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert P_{\lambda}J^{\theta}F\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}\right).

Finally, squaring and summing over dyadic
\lambda>\omega
we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{\lambda>\omega}\lambda^{-2\gamma}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sup_{\lambda}\lVert P_{\lambda}J^{\theta+\gamma}\rho(|D|)^{-\frac{1}{q}}A(|D|)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\lVert P_{\lambda}J^{\theta}\rho(|D|)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}A(|D|)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}}F\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}

for any
\gamma>0
. Thus

(2.6)

\displaystyle\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\omega^{-\gamma}T^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert J^{\theta+\gamma}\rho(|D|)^{-\frac{1}{q}}A(D)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\lVert P_{\lambda}J^{\theta}\rho(|D|)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}A(|D|)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}}F\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}

for
\theta>\frac{d}{r}
and arbitrarily small
\gamma>0
.

Combining (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) with (
2.4
) concludes the proof.
∎

3.
Dispersive estimates for linear Whitham-type equations

In this section, we will first deduce frequency-localized dispersive decay estimates for linear Whitham-type equations (Lemma

) given by

(3.1)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}m_{d}(\sqrt{\mu}D)u=0\\
u(x,0)=f(x)\end{cases}

for
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
, where the Fourier multiplier
m_{d}(D)
is defined in frequency by

(3.2)

\displaystyle m_{1}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\xi\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{1}(\xi)}=\xi\sqrt{\frac{\tanh(|\xi|)}{|\xi|}}

\displaystyle(\xi\in\mathbb{R})

and

(3.3)

\displaystyle m_{2}(\xi)

\displaystyle=|\xi|\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{1}(\xi)}=|\xi|\sqrt{\frac{\tanh(|\xi|)}{|\xi|}}

\displaystyle(\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Here, the associated propagators are

(3.4)

S_{\mu,d}(t)f=e^{i(t/\sqrt{\mu})m_{d}(\sqrt{\mu}D)}f.

The frequency-localized dispersive decay estimates will then be used to derive frequency-localized Strichartz estimates (see Lemma
3.3
). With the Strichartz estimates, we may utilize Theorem
2.1
and choose refinements of the time interval and frequencies such that we optimally balance the small parameters
(\epsilon,\mu)
. This will give us the desired refined Strichartz estimates (Corollary
3.4
), which are crucial in obtaining the improved lifespan (see Proposition
4.4
and Lemma
4.7
for
d=1
, and Proposition
5.14
and Lemma
5.17
for
d=2
).

3.1.
Dispersive decay for linear Whitham-type equations

Before we can derive the Strichartz estimates, we need frequency-localized dispersive decay estimates for the unitary group (
3.4
) including the small parameter
\mu\in(0,1]
. For
d=2
, these estimates were already provided in
[
51
, Lemma 4]
(stated in Lemma
3.1
). It therefore remains to derive these estimates for
d=1
. Introducing a scaling operator, we will observe that it suffices to show these estimates for
\mu=1
.

Indeed, fixing the dimension
d\in\mathbb{N}
and a scaling factor
\alpha>0
, we define the scaling operator
\sigma_{\alpha}
by

\sigma_{\alpha}f(x)=\alpha^{d}f(\alpha x)\qquad(x\in\mathbb{R}^{d})

for any
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
. This operator has the following properties:

\displaystyle\sigma_{\alpha}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\alpha^{-1}},

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\sigma_{\alpha}f

\displaystyle=\alpha\sigma_{\alpha}\partial_{x}f,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\alpha}(fg)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{-d}(\sigma_{\alpha}f)(\sigma_{\alpha}g),

\displaystyle\lVert\sigma_{\alpha}f\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{d(1-1/p)}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\{\sigma_{\alpha}f\}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\widehat{f}(\alpha^{-1}\xi).

From the last property, we deduce the rescaling rule for Fourier multipliers,

\sigma_{\alpha}M\left(\alpha D\right)f=M(D)\sigma_{\alpha}f

for any symbol
M(\xi)
. From the properties of the rescaling operator, one has

(3.5)

\displaystyle S_{\mu,d}(t)P_{\lambda}f=\sigma_{1/\sqrt{\mu}}S_{1,d}\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{\mu}}\right)P_{\sqrt{\mu}\lambda}\sigma_{\sqrt{\mu}}f

permitting a reduction to the case
\mu=1
in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1

(Frequency-localized dispersive estimates)
.

Let
d\in\{1,2\}
and
\mu\in(0,1]
.
Then the dispersive decay bound

(3.6)

\lVert S_{\mu,d}(t)P_{\lambda}f\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\langle{\sqrt{\mu}\lambda}\rangle^{\frac{d}{4}+1}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

holds for
\lambda>0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
. Here,
S_{\mu,d}(t)
is defined in (
3.4
).

Remark 3.2
.

The bound (
3.6
) for
d=2
was proved in
[
51
, Lemma 4]
. Alternatively one could apply (
3.5
) to reduce to the case
\mu=1
, where the bound was proved in
[
13
, Lemma 10]
(for
\lambda>1
, to be precise, but the proof applies for
\lambda\leq 1
).

Proof.

In view of the preceding remark we only need to consider the case
d=1
, and by (
3.5
) we may take
\mu=1
.

Writing

S_{1,1}(t)P_{\lambda}f=I_{\lambda,1}(t)*f,

where

I_{\lambda,1}(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{ix\xi+itm_{1}(\xi)}\beta(|\xi|/\lambda)\>\mathrm{d}\xi,

one has

(3.7)

\lVert S_{1,1}(t)P_{\lambda}f\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\lVert I_{\lambda,1}(x,t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}(\mathbb{R})}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R})},

so it suffices to check that

(3.8)

\left|I_{\lambda,1}(x,t)\right|\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle{\lambda}\rangle^{\frac{5}{4}}.

The bound (
3.8
) was proved in
[
13
, Lemma 10]
for
\lambda>1
, but the proof also applies for
\lambda\leq 1
, as one can check. (As an alternative we remark that a simplified argument that appeals to the van der Corput lemma is possible).
With (
3.5
) and the estimates (
3.7
)–(
3.8
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert S_{\mu,1}(t)P_{\lambda}f\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}\lVert S_{1,1}\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{\mu}}\right)P_{\sqrt{\mu}\lambda}\sigma_{\sqrt{\mu}}f\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}\left|\frac{t}{\sqrt{\mu}}\right|^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\sqrt{\mu}\lambda)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle{\sqrt{\mu}\lambda}\rangle^{\frac{5}{4}}\lVert\sigma_{\sqrt{\mu}}f\rVert_{L^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=c\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle{\sqrt{\mu}\lambda}\rangle^{\frac{5}{4}}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R})}

as desired.
∎

Refined Strichartz estimates

From the decay bounds in Lemma

, one obtains immediately, via the standard
TT^{*}

Lemma 3.3

(Frequency-localized Strichartz estimates)
.

Let

and
\mu\in(0,1]

Then

\lVert S_{\mu,d}(t)P_{\lambda}f\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R};L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\left(\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\langle{\sqrt{\mu}\lambda}\rangle^{\frac{d}{4}+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}}\lVert P_{\lambda}f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

holds for all

is defined in (
3.4
).

With the frequency-localized estimates in Lemma

, we may utilize Theorem
2.1
to derive refined Strichartz estimates. These estimates will be the key ingredient in obtaining the enhanced lifespan (see Proposition
4.4
and Lemma
4.7
for

and Lemma

for

).

Corollary 3.4

(Refined Strichartz estimates)
.

Let
d\in\{1,2\}
,
\mu\in(0,1]
, and
T>0
, and let

solve

(3.11)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}m_{d}(\sqrt{\mu}D)u=F,\\
u(x,0)=f(x),\end{cases}

where the symbol
m_{d}(D)
is defined in (
3.2
)–(
3.3
).
Then for
d=1
, we have

(3.12)

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu^{-\frac{1}{5}+\frac{2\theta}{5}}T^{\frac{3}{10}+\frac{2\theta}{5}}\Big(\lVert J^{\theta}|D|^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3\theta}{2}}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+\lVert J_{\mu}^{\frac{5}{8}}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle+\mu^{-\frac{2}{5}+\frac{\theta}{5}}T^{\frac{3}{5}+\frac{\theta}{5}}\lVert|D|^{-1}J_{\mu}^{\frac{5}{8}}F\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}

and for
d=2
, it holds

(3.13)

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu^{-\frac{1}{6}+\frac{5\theta}{36}}T^{\frac{1}{6}+\frac{5\theta}{18}}\Big(\lVert J^{\theta}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+\lVert|D|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\theta}{3}}J^{2\theta}J_{\mu}^{\frac{3}{4}}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle+\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}+\frac{\theta}{9}}T^{\frac{1}{3}+\frac{2\theta}{9}}\lVert|D|^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\theta}{3}}J^{\theta}J_{\mu}^{\frac{3}{4}}F\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}

for
\theta>0
arbitrarily small.

Proof.

We split the proof into two parts: one for

and another for
d=2
.

Proof of (
3.12
)

We start by fixing
q=4^{+}
and
r=\infty^{-}
. More precisely, for
\theta>0
arbitrarily small we take
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\theta}{4}

.
Then from Lemma
3.3
with

the bound (

) now becomes

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\omega^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\theta}{2}}T^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert J^{\theta}P_{\leq\omega}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\mu^{-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\theta}{4}}\lVert\rho(|D|)^{\frac{3}{4}+\frac{\theta}{4}}|D|^{-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\theta}{4}}J^{\theta}J_{\mu}^{\frac{5}{8}}F\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}.

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu^{-\frac{1}{10}+\frac{\theta}{10}}T^{\frac{2}{5}+\frac{\theta}{10}}\lVert J^{\theta}P_{\leq\omega}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\mu^{-\frac{1}{5}+\frac{2\theta}{5}}T^{\frac{3}{10}+\frac{2\theta}{5}}\lVert J^{2\theta}J_{\mu}^{\frac{5}{8}}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\mu^{-\frac{2}{5}+\frac{\theta}{5}}T^{\frac{3}{5}+\frac{\theta}{5}}\lVert J^{\theta}|D|^{-1}J_{\mu}^{\frac{5}{8}}F\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}.

To match the powers of

\lVert J^{\theta}P_{\leq\omega}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}=\lVert J^{\theta}|D|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3\theta}{2}}|D|^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3\theta}{2}}P_{\leq\omega}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\omega^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3\theta}{2}}\lVert J^{\theta}|D|^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3\theta}{2}}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

so that finally

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu^{-\frac{1}{5}+\frac{2\theta}{5}}T^{\frac{3}{10}+\frac{2\theta}{5}}\lVert J^{\theta}|D|^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3\theta}{2}}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\mu^{-\frac{1}{5}+\frac{2\theta}{5}}T^{\frac{3}{10}+\frac{2\theta}{5}}\lVert J^{2\theta}J_{\mu}^{\frac{5}{8}}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\mu^{-\frac{2}{5}+\frac{\theta}{5}}T^{\frac{3}{5}+\frac{\theta}{5}}\lVert J^{\theta}|D|^{-1}J_{\mu}^{\frac{5}{8}}F\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}

for
\theta>0
sufficiently small.

Proof of (
3.13
)

We choose
q=2^{+}
and

. So, for
\theta>0
arbitrarily small, we take
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\theta}{3}
and

. We also let
\frac{1}{\tilde{r}}=\frac{5\theta}{12}
and
\tilde{\theta}=\theta
. Then from Lemma

, we have

A(\lambda)=A_{\mu,2}(\lambda)=\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle{\sqrt{\mu}\lambda}\rangle^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Consequently, the bound (

) gives us

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\omega^{1-\frac{5\theta}{6}}T^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert J^{\theta}P_{\leq\omega}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\omega^{-\frac{\theta}{6}}\mu^{-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\theta}{6}}T^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\rho(|D|)^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\theta}{3}}J^{2\theta}J_{\mu}^{\frac{3}{4}}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\mu^{-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\theta}{6}}\lVert\rho(|D|)^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\theta}{3}}J^{\theta}J_{\mu}^{\frac{3}{4}}F\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}.

We let
\rho(\lambda)=T^{\frac{2}{3}}\mu^{-\frac{1}{6}}\lambda^{-1}
. Then it requires
\lambda\geq\mu^{-\frac{1}{6}}T^{-\frac{1}{3}}
to maintain
\rho\leq T
, so we take

This gives us

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu^{-\frac{1}{6}+\frac{5\theta}{36}}T^{\frac{1}{6}+\frac{5\theta}{18}}\lVert J^{\theta}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\mu^{-\frac{1}{6}+\frac{5\theta}{36}}T^{\frac{1}{6}+\frac{5\theta}{18}}\lVert|D|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\theta}{3}}J^{2\theta}J_{\mu}^{\frac{3}{4}}u\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}+\frac{\theta}{9}}T^{\frac{1}{3}+\frac{2\theta}{9}}\lVert|D|^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\theta}{3}}J^{\theta}J_{\mu}^{\frac{3}{4}}F\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}

for

∎

4.
The Whitham equation

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
, where we wish to extend the lifespan from
1/\epsilon
, achieved in
[
32
]
, to

(4.1)

\displaystyle T=c^{-1}\epsilon^{-1}\Big(\frac{\mu}{\epsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}^{-}}

for some
c=c(\lVert\eta_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}})>0
. This will be enough to prove Theorem
1.1
, since the uniqueness, continuous dependence on the data, and the persistence at higher regularity, on the other hand, are local-in-time properties and follows form the existing local well-posedness result (stated in Proposition
4.5
below); see Remarks
4.8
–
4.10
for more details.

To prove that the solution exists on the timescale (
4.1
), we need the standard energy estimate and a refined Strichartz estimate deduced in Sections
4.1
–
4.2
, respectively.

Throughout this paper, we will need the classical Kato-Ponce estimates.

Proposition 4.1

(Kato-Ponce estimates
[
30
]
)
.

Let
s\geq 0
,
p\in(1,\infty)
,
f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
g\in H^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then

(4.2)

\displaystyle\lVert[J^{s},f]g\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}\lesssim\lVert\nabla f\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lVert J^{s-1}g\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}+\lVert J^{s}f\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}\lVert g\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}.

Moreover, let
p_{1},p_{4}\in(1,\infty]
and
p_{2},p_{3}\in(1,\infty)
be such that
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{p_{3}}+\frac{1}{p_{4}}
. Then

(4.3)

\displaystyle\lVert J^{s}(fg)\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p_{1}}_{x}}\lVert J^{s}g\rVert_{L^{p_{2}}_{x}}+\lVert J^{s}f\rVert_{L^{p_{3}}_{x}}\lVert g\rVert_{L^{p_{4}}_{x}}

for
f,g\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

4.1.
Energy estimate for the Whitham equation

We define the energy for the Whitham equation (
1.3
) to be

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{s}(\eta)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(J^{s}\eta\Big)^{2}\>\mathrm{d}x,

which allows us to deduce the following estimate, under some additional regularity.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
s>\frac{3}{2}
and
\epsilon,\mu\in(0,1]
. Let
r\geq s+1
and
T>0
. Then for any solution
\eta\in C([0,T];H^{r}(\mathbb{R}))
to (
1.3
) on the time interval
[0,T]
we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\>\mathrm{d}}{\>\mathrm{d}t}(\lVert\eta(t)\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2})\leq c\epsilon\lVert\partial_{x}\eta(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lVert\eta(t)\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2}

for
t\in(0,T)
.

Proof.

From (
1.3
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\>\mathrm{d}}{\>\mathrm{d}t}(\lVert\eta\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2})

\displaystyle=\big({J^{s}\eta},{J^{s}\partial_{t}\eta}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle=-\big({J^{s}\eta},{J^{s}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\partial_{x}\eta}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}}-\epsilon\big({J^{s}\eta},{J^{s}(\eta\partial_{x}\eta)}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Here, the first term is skew-symmetric, and the second term can be estimated by Hölder’s inequality, the Kato-Ponce commutator estimate (
4.2
), and integration by parts. So, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\>\mathrm{d}}{\>\mathrm{d}t}(\lVert\eta\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq 2\epsilon|\big({J^{s}\eta},{[J^{s},\eta]\partial_{x}\eta}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}}|+2\epsilon|\big({J^{s}\eta},{\eta J^{s}\partial_{x}\eta}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}}|

\displaystyle\leq c\epsilon\lVert\partial_{x}\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lVert\eta\rVert^{2}_{H^{s}_{x}}

as desired.
∎

Via Grönwall’s lemma and approximation we then obtain the following bound.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
s>\frac{3}{2}
,
T>0
and
\epsilon,\mu\in(0,1]
. Then for any solution
\eta\in C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
to (
1.3
) on
[0,T]
with
\eta(0)=\eta_{0}
we have

(4.5)

\lVert\eta(t)\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2}\leq\mathrm{e}^{c\epsilon\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\partial_{x}\eta(\tau)\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\!\>\mathrm{d}\tau}\lVert\eta_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2}

for
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

First we notice that it suffices to prove (
4.5
) for an arbitrarily small
T>0
, since this result can then be iterated to cover any time interval. By the local existence theorem, Proposition
4.5
below, there exists a time
T>0
such that the following holds. Taking
r=s+1
and choosing an approximating sequence
\eta_{0}^{(n)}\in H^{r}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\eta_{0}^{(n)}\to\eta_{0}
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
as
n\to\infty
, there exist corresponding solutions
\eta^{(n)}\in C([0,T];H^{r}(\mathbb{R}))
, and by the continuous dependence on the data we have that
\eta^{(n)}\to\eta
in
C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
. From Proposition
4.2
it follows that (
4.5
) holds with
\eta
replaced by
\eta^{(n)}
, and passing to the limit
n\to\infty
we get the inequality also for
\eta
.
∎

4.2.
Refined Strichartz estimate for the Whitham equation

To obtain the desired lifespan, we use Corollary
3.4
to deduce the following estimate.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\epsilon,\mu\in(0,1]
,
r>\frac{13}{8}
, and
T>0
. Let
\eta\in C([0,T];H^{r}(\mathbb{R}))
be a solution to (
1.3
). Then

(4.6)

\displaystyle\lVert\partial_{x}\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu^{(-\frac{1}{5})^{+}}T^{\frac{3}{10}^{+}}\lVert\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{r}_{x}}+\epsilon\mu^{(-\frac{2}{5})^{+}}T^{\frac{11}{10}^{+}}\lVert\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{r}_{x}}^{2}.

Proof.

We observe that the Whitham equation (
1.3
) has the same form as (
3.11
) for
u=\eta
and
F=-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}\partial_{x}(\eta^{2})
. With these choices, we have from (
3.12
) and Hölder’s inequality in time that

\displaystyle\lVert\partial_{x}\eta\rVert_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu^{-\frac{1}{5}+\frac{2\theta}{5}}T^{\frac{3}{10}+\frac{2\theta}{5}}\Big(\lVert J^{\theta}|D|^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3\theta}{2}}\eta\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+\lVert J_{\mu}^{\frac{5}{8}}|D|\eta\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle+\epsilon\mu^{-\frac{2}{5}+\frac{\theta}{5}}T^{\frac{11}{10}+\frac{\theta}{5}}\lVert J_{\mu}^{\frac{5}{8}}|D|(\eta^{2})\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

for sufficiently small
\theta>0
. Then by the Kato-Ponce inequality (
4.3
) and a Sobolev embedding we obtain (
4.6
)
for
r>\frac{13}{8}
.
∎

4.3.
Proof of the Theorem
1.1

We will now combine the estimates in Corollary
4.3
and Proposition
4.4
with a bootstrap argument to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of the Theorem
1.1
.

Arguing as in
[
43
]
and
[
6
]
, one get the following local well-posedness result for the Whitham equation (
1.3
):

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
s>\frac{3}{2}
,
\epsilon,\mu\in(0,1]
, and
\eta_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
. Then there exists a positive time
T=T(\lVert\eta_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}})
, decreasing with respect to its arguement, such that there is a unique
\eta\in C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
solving (
1.3
) with initial condition
\eta(0)=\eta_{0}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lVert\eta(t)\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}\lesssim\lVert\eta_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}},

and the flow map is continuously dependent on the initial datum. Furthermore, higher regularity persists. That is, if
\eta_{0}\in H^{r}(\mathbb{R})
for some
r>s
, then
\eta
belongs to the space
C([0,T];H^{r}(\mathbb{R}))
(with the same
T
).

Remark 4.6
.

The time of existence of order
1/\epsilon
was obtained in
[
32
]
.

We only need to prove that the time of existence is of order
\epsilon^{-1}(\mu/\epsilon)^{\frac{1}{4}^{-}}
.
Indeed, the uniqueness, continuous dependence on the data, and persistence of higher regularity are local-in-time properties and follow directly from Proposition
4.5
; see Remarks
4.8
–
4.10
below.

We notice that a consequence of Proposition
4.5
, the initial datum
\eta_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, for
s>\frac{3}{2}
, gives the rise to a solution to (
1.3
),
\eta\in C([0,T^{\ast});H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
, where
T^{\ast}\geq T(\lVert\eta_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}})
is the maximal time of existence. Here,
T
is a decreasing function of its argument. Then it also follows that the solution satisfies the blow-up alternative:

(4.7)

\displaystyle\text{If }\ \ T^{\ast}<\infty,\text{ then }\limsup_{t\nearrow T^{\ast}}\lVert\eta(t)\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}=\infty

(see, e.g.,
[
28
, Section 5.2]
).

With Proposition
4.5
, we may now obtain the a priori estimates and prove that the solution exists on a larger timescale. This is summarized in the lemma below.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
s>\frac{13}{9}
and
\epsilon,\mu\in(0,1]
. Then there exists a positive time

(4.8)

\displaystyle T_{0}=c^{-1}\epsilon^{-1}\left(\frac{\mu}{\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}^{-}}\lVert\eta_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}^{(-\frac{5}{4})^{+}}

such that
T^{\ast}>T_{0}
and

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}\lVert\eta(t)\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}\leq 4\lVert\eta_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}.

Proof.

We will argue by contradiction by defining

(4.9)

\displaystyle\tilde{T}:=\sup\{T\in(0,T^{\ast}):\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lVert\eta(t)\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}\leq 4\lVert\eta_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}\}

and assuming that
\tilde{T}\leq T_{0}
. Obtaining an estimate inside the ball in (
4.9
) for the time
T_{0}
defined in (
4.8
) will lead to a contradiction.

Fix
T_{1}\in(0,\tilde{T})
, then we utilize (
4.5
), (
4.6
), and the assumption
T_{1}<\tilde{T}\leq T_{0}
to obtain

\displaystyle\lVert\eta(t)\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{c\epsilon T_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\partial_{x}\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{T_{1}}L^{\infty}_{x}}}\lVert\eta_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{c\mu^{(-\frac{1}{5})^{+}}\epsilon T_{0}^{\frac{4}{5}^{+}}\lVert\eta_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}+c\mu^{(-\frac{2}{5})^{+}}\epsilon^{2}T_{0}^{\frac{8}{5}^{+}}\lVert\eta_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2}}\lVert\eta_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2}

\displaystyle=:\mathrm{e}^{c(\delta+\delta^{2})}\lVert\eta_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2}

for all
t\in[0,T_{1}]
and
s>\frac{13}{8}
. Choosing
T_{0}>0
small enough such that

\displaystyle 2c\delta<\log 2,

yielding
c(\delta+\delta^{2})<2c\delta
and (
4.8
), implies

\displaystyle\lVert\eta(t)\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\lVert\eta_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}

for all
t\in[0,T_{1}]
. As a consequence of (
4.7
), we have that
T_{1}<T^{\ast}
. Then by the continuity of the solution
\eta(t)
for
t\in[0,T^{\ast})
from the definition of
T^{\ast}
, there exists a time
\tau\in[\tilde{T},T^{\ast})
such that

\displaystyle\lVert\eta(\tau)\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}\leq 3\lVert\eta_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}.

But this leads to a contradiction of the definition of
\tilde{T}
in (
4.9
). We may therefore conclude that indeed
T_{0}<\tilde{T}
, where
T_{0}
is defined in (
4.8
).

∎

Conclusion.

We conclude the proof of Theorem
1.1
with some remarks on uniqueness, continuous dependence on data, and persistence of higher regularity. All these properties are known for some positive time by the local existence and uniqueness result, Proposition
4.5
, and they therefore extend to the larger time interval
[0,T_{0}]
by standard arguments which we briefly recall in the following remarks. In these remarks we assume that
\eta,\tilde{\eta}\in C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
are two solutions of the Whitham equation with initial data
\eta_{0},\tilde{\eta}_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, respectively. Here
T>0
is completely arbitrary.

Remark 4.8
.

For uniqueness, assume that
\eta_{0}=\tilde{\eta}_{0}
. If uniqueness fails, then for some
S\in(0,T)
we have
\eta=\tilde{\eta}
in
[0,S]
while this fails on
[S,S+\delta]
for all
\delta>0
. But applying Proposition
4.5
with initial time
t=S
, this contradicts the uniqueness part of the proposition.

Remark 4.9
.

For continuous dependence on the data, we must show that given any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that if
\lVert\eta_{0}-\tilde{\eta}_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}<\delta
, then
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lVert\eta(t)-\tilde{\eta}(t)\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}<\varepsilon
. Choose
R>0
so large that
\lVert\eta(t)\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}},\lVert\tilde{\eta}(t)\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}\leq R
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Now let
S=S(R)>0
be the time in Proposition
4.5
. We may assume that
S=T/N
for a large integer

. Then applying the continuous dependence on the data in Proposition
4.5
first at the initial time

, we find first

such that if

, then

such that if
\lVert\eta(t_{2})-\tilde{\eta}(t_{2})\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}<\delta_{2}

\sup_{t\in[t_{2},t_{1}]}\lVert\eta(t)-\tilde{\eta}(t)\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}<\delta_{1}

Remark 4.10
.

For persistence of higher regularity, assume that
\eta_{0}
belongs to

for some

. Choose

so large that

We will need to derive commutator estimates for the Fourier multiplier

Definition 5.1

(Symbol class
[

We say that a symbol

\displaystyle\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d},\ \ \sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\ \langle{\xi}\rangle^{|\alpha|-s}\Big|\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{\partial\xi^{\alpha}}\sigma(\xi)\Big|<\infty.

We also introduce the following semi-norm

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{s}(\sigma)=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d},\>|\alpha|\leq 2+d+\lceil\frac{d}{2}\rceil}\>\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\ \langle{\xi}\rangle^{|\alpha|-s}\Big|\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{\partial\xi^{\alpha}}\sigma(\xi)\Big|.

From the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the Kato-Ponce commutator estimate (
4.2
), we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{11}^{1}|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\sum_{j=1,2}\lVert[J^{s},v_{j}]\partial_{x_{j}}\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}\lVert\eta\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\sum_{j=1,2}\Big(\lVert\nabla v_{j}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lVert\eta\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}+\lVert\partial_{x_{j}}\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lVert v_{j}\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}\Big)\lVert\eta\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\Big(\lVert\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\lVert\nabla\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\Big)\lVert(\eta,\mathbf{v})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}^{2}.

Next, the term
\mathcal{A}_{11}^{2}
can be estimated from integration by parts and Hölder’s inequality. This yields

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{11}^{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\lVert\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lVert J^{s}\eta\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq\epsilon\lVert\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lVert(\eta,\mathbf{v})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}^{2}.

Combining these estimates gives us

(5.28)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{11}|\lesssim\epsilon\big(\lVert\nabla\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\lVert\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\big)\lVert(\eta,\mathbf{v})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}^{2}.

Estimating
\mathcal{A}_{12}+\mathcal{A}_{21}

We can write
\mathcal{A}_{12}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{12}

\displaystyle=\epsilon\big({[J^{s},\eta]\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}},{J^{s}\eta}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}}+\big({(1+\epsilon\eta)J^{s}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}},{J^{s}\eta}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{A}_{12}^{1}+\mathcal{A}_{12}^{2}.

From the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the Kato-Ponce commutator estimate (
4.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{12}^{1}|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\lVert[J^{s},\eta]\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}\lVert J^{s}\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\big(\lVert\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\lVert\nabla\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\big)\lVert(\eta,\mathbf{v})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}^{2}.

We cannot estimate
\mathcal{A}_{12}^{2}
on its own, but it will be absorbed by
\mathcal{A}_{21}
. Next, we observe from integration by parts that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{21}

\displaystyle=-\epsilon\big({J^{s}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\eta},{\nabla\eta\cdot J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\mathbf{v}}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}}-\big({J^{s}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\eta},{(1+\epsilon\eta)J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{A}_{21}^{1}+\mathcal{A}_{21}^{2}.

From Hölder’s inequality, (
5.10
), and (
1.11
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{21}^{1}|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\lVert\nabla\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lVert J^{s}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}\lVert J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\lVert\nabla\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lVert(\eta,\mathbf{v})\rVert^{2}_{V^{s}_{\mu}}.

For the term
\mathcal{A}_{21}^{2}
, we first need to rewrite it as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{21}^{2}

\displaystyle=\epsilon\big({J^{s}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\eta},{[J^{s},\eta]\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}}-\big({J^{s}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\eta},{J^{s}((1+\epsilon\eta)\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v})}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{A}_{21}^{2,1}+\mathcal{A}_{21}^{2,2}.

This approach is needed due to the fact that we cannot close the estimates with
\lVert J^{s}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}
or
\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\eta\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}
on the right-hand side. We will therefore use the commutator estimate (
5.4
) to handle
\mathcal{A}_{21}^{2,2}
. We start by treating
\mathcal{A}_{21}^{2,1}
. From the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
5.10
), the Kato-Ponce commutator estimate (
4.2
), and (
5.13
), we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{21}^{2,1}|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\lVert J^{s}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}\lVert[J^{s},\eta]\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\lVert\eta\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}\Big(\lVert\nabla\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lVert J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}+\lVert J^{s}\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\Big(\lVert\nabla\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\Big)\lVert(\eta,\mathbf{v})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}^{2}.

Lastly, we look at the term
\mathcal{A}_{21}^{2,2}
. We observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{21}^{2,2}

\displaystyle=-\epsilon\big({J^{s}\eta},{[\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)J^{s},\eta]\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}}-\big({J^{s}\eta},{(1+\epsilon\eta)J^{s}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{A}_{21}^{2,2,1}+\mathcal{A}_{21}^{2,2,2}.

The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the commutator estimate (
5.4
), and (
5.13
) applied to
\mathcal{A}_{21}^{2,2,1}
yields

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{21}^{2,2,1}|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\lVert J^{s}\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}\lVert[\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)J^{s},\eta]\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\lVert\eta\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}\Big(\lVert\nabla\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lVert J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}+\lVert J^{s}\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\Big(\lVert\nabla\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\Big)\lVert(\eta,\mathbf{v})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}^{2}.

We make the observation that
\mathcal{A}_{21}^{2,2,2}=-\mathcal{A}_{12}^{2}
. So we may gather the above estimates to obtain

(5.29)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{12}+\mathcal{A}_{21}|\lesssim\epsilon\Big(\lVert\nabla\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\lVert\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\Big)\lVert(\eta,\mathbf{v})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}^{2}.

Estimating
\mathcal{A}_{22}

We first observe that

\displaystyle(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}=(v_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}v_{1}+v_{2}\partial_{x_{2}}v_{1},v_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}v_{2}+v_{2}\partial_{x_{2}}v_{2})^{\mathrm{T}}

which allows us to write

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{22}

\displaystyle=\epsilon\sum_{j,\ell=1,2}\big({J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)(v_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}v_{\ell})},{(1+\epsilon\eta)J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)v_{\ell}}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle=\epsilon\sum_{j,\ell=1,2}\big({[J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D),v_{j}]\partial_{x_{j}}v_{\ell}},{(1+\epsilon\eta)J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)v_{\ell}}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle+\epsilon\sum_{j,\ell=1,2}\big({v_{j}J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\partial_{x_{j}}v_{\ell}},{(1+\epsilon\eta)J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)v_{\ell}}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{A}_{22}^{1}+\mathcal{A}_{22}^{2}.

With the help of Hölder’s inequality, the commutator estimate (
5.5
), and (
5.12
)–(
5.13
), we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{22}^{1}|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\sum_{j,\ell=1,2}\lVert[J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D),v_{j}]\partial_{x_{j}}v_{\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}(1+\epsilon\lVert\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}})\lVert J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)v_{\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim H(t)\epsilon\sum_{j,\ell=1,2}\Big(\lVert\nabla v_{j}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lVert J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}v_{\ell}\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}+\lVert\partial_{x_{j}}v_{\ell}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lVert J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}v_{j}\rVert_{H^{s}_{x}}\Big)\lVert J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)v_{\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon H(t)\lVert\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lVert(\eta,\mathbf{v})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}^{2}.

The term
\mathcal{A}_{22}^{2}
is treated by integration by parts, Hölder’s inequality, and (
5.13
). This gives us

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{22}^{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\sum_{j,\ell=1,2}\Big|\big({(\partial_{x_{j}}v_{j})J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)v_{\ell}},{(1+\epsilon\eta)J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)v_{\ell}}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}}\Big|

\displaystyle+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\sum_{j,\ell=1,2}\Big|\big({v_{j}J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)v_{\ell}},{(\partial_{x_{j}}\eta)J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)v_{\ell}}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon H(t)\Big(\lVert\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\lVert\nabla\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\Big)\lVert(\eta,\mathbf{v})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}^{2}.

We gather the estimates for

and

to obtain

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{22}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon H(t)\Big(\lVert\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\lVert\nabla\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\Big)\lVert(\eta,\mathbf{v})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}^{2}.

Combining (

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}|\lesssim\epsilon H(t)\Big(\lVert\nabla\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\lVert\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\Big)\lVert(\eta,\mathbf{v})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}^{2}.

Control of
\mathcal{II}

From (
5.18
) and (

), we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{II}

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon}{2}\big({J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\mathbf{v}},{(\partial_{t}\eta)J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\mathbf{v}}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\big({J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\mathbf{v}},{(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v})J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\mathbf{v}}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}}-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\big({J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\mathbf{v}},{(\nabla\cdot(\eta\mathbf{v}))J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\mathbf{v}}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Then from Hölder’s inequality and (

\displaystyle|\mathcal{II}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\Big(\lVert\nabla\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\lVert\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\Big)\lVert(\eta,\mathbf{v})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}^{2}.

5.3.
Refined Strichartz estimate for (
1.6
)

To obtain the enhanced lifespan in Theorem
1.6
, we need to estimate
\lVert\mathcal{P}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T)}
appearing in (
5.34
) (see Proposition
5.14
and Lemma
5.17
below). We first have to write (
1.6
) in its equivalent form with the linear part being diagonal. From this, we will see that the unitary group associated to the linear part of the diagonalized system is given by (
3.4
), allowing us to utilize Corollary
3.4
.

We diagonalize (
1.6
) by defining

(5.35)

\displaystyle u^{+}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\eta-i\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\mathbf{R}\cdot\mathbf{v}\Big)\

\displaystyle\text{ and }\ u^{-}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\eta+i\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\mathbf{R}\cdot\mathbf{v}\Big).

Moreover,
\mathbf{v}
being curl-free yields
\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v})=\Delta\mathbf{v}=-|D|^{2}\mathbf{v}
, so that we can reconstruct

(5.36)

\displaystyle\eta=u^{+}+u^{-}\

\displaystyle\text{ and }\ \mathbf{v}=-i\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\mathbf{R}(u^{+}-u^{-}).

Using (
1.6
), (
5.15
), and (
5.36
), we obtain

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\partial_{t}u^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\partial_{t}\eta\mp i\mathbf{R}\cdot\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}-\epsilon\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\nabla\cdot(\eta\mathbf{v})\pm i\mathcal{T}_{\mu}(D)\mathbf{R}\cdot\nabla\eta\pm i\frac{\epsilon}{2}\mathbf{R}\cdot\nabla(|\mathbf{v}|^{2})

\displaystyle=i\mathcal{T}_{\mu}(D)|D|(u^{+}-u^{-})\pm i\mathcal{T}_{\mu}(D)|D|(u^{+}+u^{-})-\epsilon\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\nabla\cdot(\eta\mathbf{v})\pm i\frac{\epsilon}{2}|D|(|\mathbf{v}|^{2}).

We can then write

(5.37)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u^{+}+\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)|D|u^{+}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\mathcal{B}^{+}(\eta,\mathbf{v})=0\\
i\partial_{t}u^{-}-\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)|D|u^{-}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\mathcal{B}^{-}(\eta,\mathbf{v})=0\end{cases}

for

(5.38)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\pm}(\eta,\mathbf{v}):=i\nabla\cdot(\eta\mathbf{v})\pm\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)|D|(|\mathbf{v}|^{2}),

where the initial values are transformed to
u^{\pm}(0)=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\eta_{0}\mp i\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\mathbf{R}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{0}\Big)
. We notice that

\displaystyle\eta\mathbf{v}

\displaystyle=-i(u^{+}+u^{-})\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\mathbf{R}(u^{+}-u^{-})

and

\displaystyle|\mathbf{v}|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\mathbf{R}(u^{+}-u^{-})|^{2},

but for simplicity in later calculations, we keep the nonlinearity (
5.38
) depending on
\eta
and
\bf v
.

We observe that the linear part of (
5.37
) is given by

(5.39)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u^{\pm}\pm\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)|D|u^{\pm}=0,\\
u^{\pm}(0)=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\eta_{0}\mp i\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\mathbf{R}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{0}\Big)\end{cases}

with the solution being associated with the unitary group
S_{\mu,2}(t)
given by

(5.40)

\displaystyle S_{\mu,2}(\mp t)u^{\pm}(0)=\Big(\mathrm{e}^{\mp i(t/\sqrt{\mu})m_{2}(\sqrt{\mu}\cdot)}\widehat{u^{\pm}}(0)\Big)^{\vee},

where

. Since (
5.40
) coincides with (
3.4
), we may utilize the refined Strichartz estimates in Corollary
3.4
.

3.4
, we may now derive the next proposition, which is the key ingredient in obtaining the enhanced lifespan in Theorem

.

for

small. Using (

5.14
), and (
1.11

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert J^{\frac{3}{4}}_{\mu}J^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{5\theta}{3}}\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}+\lVert J^{\frac{3}{4}}_{\mu}J^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{5\theta}{3}}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\mathbf{R}\cdot\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}

\mathrm{curl}\>\mathbf{v}_{0}=0
. Then there exists a positive time

T=T(\lVert(\eta_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}})=\frac{1}{c(d,s,h_{0},H_{0})\lVert(\eta_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}}

with
H_{0}=1+\epsilon\lVert(\eta_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}\times L^{\infty}_{x}}

such that (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) have a unique solution

\displaystyle(\eta,\mathbf{v})\in C([0,T];V^{s}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap C^{1}([0,T];V^{s-1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

that satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lVert(\eta,\mathbf{v})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}\lesssim\lVert(\eta_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}.

Here, the implicit constant depends on
h_{0}
and
H_{0}
. Additionally, the flow map is continuous with respect to the initial data. Furthermore, higher regularity persists. That is, if
(\eta_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})\in V^{r}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
r>s
, then
(\eta,\mathbf{v})
belongs to
C([0,T];V^{r}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
(with the same
T
).

Remark 5.16
.

The above theorem was proved on the timescale
1/\epsilon
under the condition

\displaystyle 0<\epsilon c\lVert(\eta_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}\leq 1

for
s>\frac{d}{2}+1
in
[
41
, Theorem 1.11]
. However, one can use the same arguments as in the proof of Lemma
5.17
below, where one has to substitute the refined Strichartz estimates (Proposition
5.14
) with a classical Sobolev embedding to obtain the well-posedness result for arbitrary data.

It will suffice to show that the solution exists on the timescale
\epsilon^{-\frac{5}{4}}(\mu/\epsilon)^{\frac{1}{4}^{-}}
to complete the proof of Theorem
1.6
as the rest follows by Theorem
5.15
. Indeed, the uniqueness, continuous dependence on the data, and persistence of higher regularity are local-in-time properties (see Remarks
4.8
–
4.10
).

From Theorem
5.15
, it follows that an initial datum
(\eta_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})\in V^{s}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, for
s>2
, generate a solution to (
5.18
),
(\eta,\mathbf{v})\in C([0,T^{\ast});V^{s}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
, where
T^{\ast}\geq T(\lVert(\eta_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}})
is the maximal time of existence. Since
T
is a decreasing function of its argument, we may deduce classically that the solution of (
5.18
) satisfies the blow-up alternative:

(5.45)

\displaystyle\text{If }\ \ T^{\ast}<\infty,\text{ then }\limsup_{t\nearrow T^{\ast}}\lVert(\eta,\mathbf{v})(t)\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}=\infty.

Lemma 5.17
.

Let
s>\frac{9}{4}
and
\epsilon,\mu\in(0,1]
.
Then there exists a time

(5.46)

\displaystyle T_{1}=\epsilon^{-\frac{5}{4}}\Big(\frac{\mu}{\epsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}^{-}}(C_{1})^{-1}\lVert(\eta_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}^{(-\frac{3}{2})^{+}}

such that
T^{\ast}>T_{1}
and

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T_{1}]}\lVert(\eta,\mathbf{v})(t)\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}\leq 5c\sqrt{\frac{H_{0}}{h_{0}}}\lVert(\eta_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}

where

H_{0}=1+\epsilon\lVert(\eta_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}\times L^{\infty}_{x}}
,
h_{0}\in(0,1)
, and
C_{1}=C_{1}(h_{0},H_{0})
is a positive constant fixed in the proof.

Proof.

We define

(5.47)

\displaystyle\tilde{T}:=\sup\{T\in(0,T^{\ast}):\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lVert(\eta,\mathbf{v})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}\leq 5c\sqrt{\frac{H_{0}}{h_{0}}}\lVert(\eta_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}\}

for some
c>0
. To obtain the result, we will argue by contradiction by assuming that
\tilde{T}\leq T_{1}
.

For simplicity in notation, we denote

\displaystyle A(t)

\displaystyle:=\lVert(\eta,\mathbf{v})(t)\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}^{2},

\displaystyle H(t)

\displaystyle:=1+\epsilon\lVert(\eta,\mathbf{v})(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}\times L^{\infty}_{x}},

and

\displaystyle h(t)

\displaystyle:=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\{1+\epsilon\eta(x,t)\right\}.

We also write
A(0):=A_{0}
,
H(0):=H_{0}
, and
h(0):=h_{0}
, where the non-cavitation condition can then be restated as
h_{0}>0
. We fix
T_{2}\in(0,\tilde{T})
and claim that

(5.48)

h(t)\geq\frac{h_{0}}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{t\in[0,T_{2}]}H(t)\leq 2H_{0}

for all
t\in[0,T_{2}]
implying that the condition (
5.33
) holds. Then, using (
5.34
), the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in time, and the claim (
5.48
) yields

A(t)\leq 4c_{2}\frac{H_{0}}{h_{0}}A_{0}\mathrm{e}^{cH_{0}\epsilon T_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\mathcal{P}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T_{2})}},

where
c_{2}>0
is the constant in (
5.34
) and
\mathcal{P}(t)=\lVert\nabla\eta(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\lVert\nabla\mathbf{v}(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)\nabla\mathbf{v}(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}
.
From (
5.41
) and the assumption
T_{2}<\tilde{T}\leq T_{1}
, it follows that, if
s>\frac{9}{4}
, then

\displaystyle\epsilon T_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\mathcal{P}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T_{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\mu^{(-\frac{1}{6})^{+}}T_{2}^{\frac{2}{3}^{+}}\lVert A\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{2})}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\epsilon^{2}\mu^{(-\frac{1}{3})^{+}}T_{2}^{\frac{4}{3}^{+}}\lVert A\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\mu^{(-\frac{1}{6})^{+}}T_{1}^{\frac{2}{3}^{+}}\sqrt{\frac{H_{0}}{h_{0}}A_{0}}+\epsilon^{2}\mu^{(-\frac{1}{3})^{+}}T_{1}^{\frac{4}{3}^{+}}\frac{H_{0}}{h_{0}}A_{0}

\displaystyle=:\delta+\delta^{2},

where

(5.49)

\displaystyle\delta=c\epsilon\mu^{(-\frac{1}{6})^{+}}T_{1}^{\frac{2}{3}^{+}}\sqrt{\frac{H_{0}}{h_{0}}A_{0}}.

Gathering the above, we have

(5.50)

A(t)\leq 4c_{2}\frac{H_{0}}{h_{0}}A_{0}\mathrm{e}^{\delta+\delta^{2}}

for all
t\in[0,T_{2}]
. We choose
T_{1}>0
so small that

(5.51)

4c\delta\leq\frac{4cH_{0}}{h_{0}}\delta<\frac{\log 2}{2},

that is,

\displaystyle\frac{5cH_{0}}{h_{0}}c\epsilon\mu^{(-\frac{1}{6})^{+}}T_{1}^{\frac{2}{3}^{+}}\sqrt{\frac{H_{0}}{h_{0}}A_{0}}<\frac{\log 2}{2},

which is the same as (
5.46
). We remark that (
5.51
) implies
\delta<1
, so that
\delta+\delta^{2}<2\delta
. This gives us

\displaystyle A(t)\leq 8c_{2}\frac{H_{0}}{h_{0}}A_{0}

for all
t\in[0,T_{2}]
implying

\displaystyle\lVert(\eta,\mathbf{v})(t)\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}\leq 3\sqrt{c_{2}\frac{H_{0}}{h_{0}}}\lVert(\eta_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}.

Consequently, we have from the blow-up alternative (
5.45
) that
T_{2}<T^{\ast}
. From (
5.45
), it follows that the solution
(\eta,\mathbf{v})
is continuous for
t\in[0,T^{\ast})
. Thus, there exists a time
\tau\in[\tilde{T},T^{\ast})
such that

\displaystyle\lVert(\eta,\mathbf{v})(\tau)\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}}\leq 4\sqrt{c_{2}\frac{H_{0}}{h_{0}}}\lVert(\eta_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})\rVert_{V^{s}_{\mu}},

which contradicts with the definition of
\tilde{T}
(
5.47
). Hence,
T_{1}<\tilde{T}
, where
T_{1}
is as seen in (
5.46
).

Proof of the claim (
5.48
)

We start by verifying the last inequality of (
5.48
). From the fundamental theorem of calculus and Hölder’s inequality in time, we have that

\displaystyle H(t)=1+\epsilon\lVert(\eta,\mathbf{v})(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}\times L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq H_{0}+\epsilon T_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\lVert\partial_{t}\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}+\lVert\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}\Big).

From (
5.18
), Proposition
5.14
, and
T_{2}<\tilde{T}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\epsilon T_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\partial_{t}\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon T_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\lVert\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}+\epsilon\lVert\nabla\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}+\epsilon\lVert\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}\lVert\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon T_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert H\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{2})}\lVert\mathcal{P}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T_{2})}

\displaystyle\leq 2\lVert H\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{2})}\delta

for
s>\frac{9}{4}
, where
\delta
is defined in (
5.49
). Next, (
5.18
) and

\displaystyle(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}=(v_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}v_{1}+v_{2}\partial_{x_{2}}v_{1},v_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}v_{2}+v_{2}\partial_{x_{2}}v_{2})^{\mathrm{T}}

yields

\displaystyle\epsilon T_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\mu}(D)\nabla\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}+\epsilon\lVert(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert H\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{2})}\Big(\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\mu}(D)\nabla\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}+\lVert\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}\Big).

The first term can be estimated by slightly modifying the proof of
\lVert\nabla\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}
in Proposition
5.14
, where one has to utilize (
5.10
). Then from Proposition
5.14
, we obtain

\displaystyle\epsilon T_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\lVert H\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{2})}\delta

for
s>\frac{9}{4}
. Gathering the above calculations gives us

\displaystyle(1-4\delta)\lVert H\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{2})}\leq H_{0},

which requires

\displaystyle 4\delta<\frac{1}{2},

but this is satisfied due to (
5.51
).

Lastly, we will verify that
\eta
satisfies the non-cavitation condition. From the fundamental theorem of calculus, the non-cavitation condition on
\eta_{0}
(
1.10
), and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle 1+\epsilon\eta(x,t)=1+\epsilon\eta_{0}+\epsilon\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}\eta(x,s)\>\mathrm{d}s\geq h_{0}-\epsilon T_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\partial_{t}\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}

for all
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,T_{2}]
. It is enough to prove that

\displaystyle\epsilon T_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\partial_{t}\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq\frac{h_{0}}{2}.

From the first equation in (
1.6
), Hölder’s inequality, the second inequality in (
5.48
) (verified above), Proposition
5.14
, and the assumption
T_{2}<\tilde{T}\leq T_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\epsilon T_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\partial_{t}\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}+\epsilon\lVert\nabla\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}+\epsilon\lVert\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}\lVert\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T_{2}}L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq 2H_{0}\epsilon T_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\mathcal{P}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T_{2})}

\displaystyle\leq 4cH_{0}\delta

for
s>\frac{9}{4}
. So, we need

\displaystyle 4c\frac{H_{0}}{h_{0}}\delta<\frac{1}{2}

to conclude, which holds by (
5.51
).

∎

Conclusion.

It remains to comment on the uniqueness, continuous dependence of the initial data, and the persistence of higher regularity. All these properties are known for some positive time by the local well-posedness result, Theorem
5.15
, and they therefore extend to the larger time interval
[0,T_{1}]
by standard arguments (see Remarks
4.8
–
4.10
for more details). This concludes the proof of the theorem for
d=2
.

∎

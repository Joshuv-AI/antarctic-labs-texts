High Ratoon Season Rice Yield is Achieved Through Optimized Variety, Nitrogen Management and Temperature-Solar Radiation Resources
Authors: Zhong Li, Linsheng Yang, Shuang Cheng, Yalan Ji, Youzun Xu, Yongjin Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractRatoon rice (RR), recognized as an efficient method of rice cultivation, plays a crucial role in the food production system. By enabling two harvests—during the main season rice (MSR) and the ratoon season rice (RSR)—from a single planting, this cropping system significantly enhances the utilization of land and temperature-solar radiation resources, thereby providing essential support for food security. However, the yield formation of RSR is constrained by several factors, including the adaptability of the rice variety, nitrogen fertilizer management strategies, and the uneven distribution of temperature and solar radiation resources, which collectively hinder the actual realization of its yield potential. This research first employed a meta-analysis approach to identify the optimal nitrogen management practices for ratoon rice. Through multi-location field experiments, high-yielding ratoon rice varieties with strong ecological adaptability were selected. Then, the selected high-yielding and adaptable varieties were cultivated under field conditions by using the optimal nitrogen management practices to compare the yield formation differences and underlying mechanisms among the MSR, RSR, and late-season rice (LSR) with synchronous heading. The research findings indicated that the yield of RSR was significantly lower compared to both MSR and LSR with delayed panicle emergence. Nevertheless, considering that its growth period constituted only 53.52% and 55.47% of the growth periods of MSR and LSR with delayed panicle emergence, respectively. Its daily grain yield (DGY) was 28.33% and 13.56% higher than that of MSR and LSR with delayed panicle emergence. Furthermore, RSR exhibited significant advantages in terms of effective panicle (EP) and seed setting percentage (SSP), although its number of grains per panicle (NP) and 1000-grain weight (TGW) were notably lower than those of MSR and LSR. Subsequent analysis demonstrated that enhancing the utilization efficiency of temperature and solar radiation resources can significantly increase the EP and SSP in RSR. By extending the days before flowering and augmenting accumulated temperature and radiation before flowering, there is a significant notable increase in NP and TGW, thereby overcoming the yield limitations. This study offers a theoretical foundation and technical support for the high-yield and efficient cultivation of RSR.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12284-025-00862-9.Keywords: Ratoon rice, Meta-analysis, Temperature, Solar radiation

Introduction

Rice is a primary food crop worldwide, with its yield having a direct impact on food security and social stability (Lan et al. 2024). With the upsuring population growth, diminishing arable land, and the negative effects of climate change, enhancing rice yield per unit area and improving resource utilization efficiency have become pressing challenges for sustainable agricultural development (Lesk et al. 2016; Liu et al. 2024). Ratoon rice (RR), a distinctive cultivation method, involves the use of dormant buds on rice stubbles to regenerate, including main season rice (MSR) and ratoon season rice (RSR) (He et al. 2023). The RR provides multiple benefits over conventional single or double-season rice farming methods, including reduced seed and labor requirements, water savings, and lower carbon emissions (Xu et al. 2022; Zhou et al. 2023). Additionally, it optimally utilizes temperature and solar radiation resources and enhances the land multiple cropping index, thereby holding significant potential for application in global rice production. In recent years, the rise in labor costs and the advancement of agricultural mechanization have brought increased attention to ratoon rice, particularly due to its “one planting, two harvests” feature (Lin et al. 2024). Investigating the yield formation mechanism of ratoon rice is of considerable importance for optimizing rice cultivation practices and enhancing resource utilization efficiency.

Despite the numerous advantages of ratoon rice, its yield stability remains significantly affected by varietal characteristics (Wu et al. 2023). There are notable differences in the regenerative capacity among various rice varieties, primarily reflected in factors such as the germination rate of dormant buds, tillering ability during the ratoon season, panicle-grain structure, and stress resistance (Deng et al. 2015; He et al. 2019; Nie et al. 2025). Currently, the primary varieties of ratoon rice promoted in China are categorized into two main types: hybrid rice and conventional rice (Chen et al. 2018). Hybrid rice generally exhibits superior yield performance in the main season due to its pronounced heterosis. However, certain hybrid rice varieties demonstrate limited regenerative capacity, resulting in reduced yields during the ratoon season. Conversely, some inbred rice varieties possess robust regenerative abilities, leading to stable yields in the ratoon season, albeit often at the expense of lower yields in the main season compared to hybrid rice. Consequently, the identification and breeding of specialized ratoon rice varieties with high yield are crucial for advancing the ratoon rice industry. Furthermore, the selection of ratoon rice varieties should also account for ecological adaptability (Wu et al. 2023). For instance, ratoon rice varieties should exhibit strong cold resistance to ensure successful maturation during the ratoon season.

The growth and development of rice heavily depend on nitrogen fertilizer, and the effective management strategies significantly enhance yield (Yang et al. 2021). In the context of the ratoon rice system, nitrogen fertilizer management is more intricate than in single-season rice, necessitating consideration of the nutritional requirements of both MSR and RSR. This complexity primarily involves the precise regulation of the timing and quantity of nitrogen fertilizer application to satisfy the nutritional demands at various growth stages. Research indicated that optimized nitrogen fertilizer application in the ratoon rice system markedly increased annual yield (Li et al. 2024). This approach not only augments dry matter accumulation and root vitality in ratoon rice but also mitigates leaf senescence, thereby improving overall production efficiency. Furthermore, effective nitrogen fertilizer management in the ratoon rice system should consider the specific requirements of the ratoon season. Research by Zheng et al. (Zheng et al. 2022) demonstrates that applying nitrogen fertilizer 15 days after heading of the main season can substantially enhance biomass production during the ratoon season. This increase was primarily attributed to improved biomass production both before and after the ratoon season. This fertilization approach augments the total nitrogen uptake during the ratoon season by elevating the nitrogen content in the stubble at the time of the main season harvest and enhancing nitrogen absorption by the plants throughout the ratoon season. In addition, the management of nitrogen fertilizer during the ratoon season is particularly critical. Given the shorter growth period of the ratoon season, precise control over the timing and quantity of nitrogen fertilizer application is essential to facilitate the rapid development of panicles by regenerative buds and to increase the number of grains per panicle (NP) (Huang et al. 2022). Consequently, the development of a quantitative nitrogen fertilizer management strategy for the ratoon rice system is crucial for achieving high yield.

The yield formation of ratoon rice is intricately linked to the efficiency with which temperature and solar radiation resources are utilized (Zheng et al. 2022). The timing of the main season’s harvest significantly influences the growth period of the ratoon season, thereby determining its capacity to fully exploit temperature and solar radiation resources for safe maturation. In the Yangtze River region, the main season is typically harvested from late July to early August, while the ratoon season should reach maturity by mid-to-late October to mitigate the adverse effects of low temperatures on grain filling (Wu et al. 2023). Consequently, selecting appropriate maturity varieties and optimizing cultivation practices are crucial strategies for ensuring high yields of ratoon rice (Song et al. 2022). Furthermore, the mechanism of material transport in ratoon rice differs from that in single-season rice. The photosynthetic products generated during the main season not only support the growth of that season but also necessitate the accumulation of sufficient carbohydrates in the stem sheath. This accumulation is crucial for providing energy for the germination of regenerative buds and the initial growth of the ratoon season (Lal et al. 2023a, b). Research suggests that varieties with strong regenerative traits often demonstrate an increased capacity for stem sheath dry matter accumulation, which can be rapidly mobilized to regenerative buds following the harvest of the main season (Zheng et al. 2023). Consequently, examining the patterns of material distribution between the main and ratoon seasons is essential for optimizing cultivation practices and enhancing the yield of ratoon rice.

This study employs meta-analysis and field experiments as methodological approaches, with the primary objectives being: (1) Fertilization quantification: to ascertain the optimal nitrogen fertilizer management strategies for the MSR and RSR; (2) Variety selection: to identify high-yield ratoon rice varieties that are well-suited to local ecological conditions; and (3) Resource Utilization: to investigate the utilization characteristics of ratoon rice concerning temperature and solar radiation resources. The anticipated outcome of this research is to provide scientific support for the large-scale promotion of ratoon rice and to propose innovative strategies for the sustainable development of rice production.

Materials and Methods

Meta-Analysis

Data Collection and Selection Criteria

This study seeks to identify the optimal nitrogen fertilizer management strategy for enhancing the yield of RSR through a meta-analysis approach. Following the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) standards, a systematic evaluation and selection of all pertinent publications were conducted to mitigate selection biases related to research focus, research groups, regional emphasis, and publication period. These publications were utilized in the present meta-analysis. All peer-reviewed scientific articles pertinent to the research objective and published before February 2025 were retrieved and screened for inclusion. Relevant studies were identified through searches conducted on the ISI Web of Science (Thomson Reuters, USA) and China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), utilizing keywords derived from the Chinese literature. The search employed the keywords “Ratoon rice”, “Nitrogen”, and their combinations. The research process adhered to the following steps: (1) creating criteria for screening; (2) data extraction, processing, and quality evaluation; and (3) the establishment of principles for selecting appropriate data sets for analysis. Graphical data from the studies included in the analysis were digitized using GetData v.2.22 software.

To enhance the appropriateness of the data selected for this study, we employed five specific criteria to determine the inclusion of studies in the meta-analysis: (i) utilization of monitoring data from field trials, excluding pot trials; (ii) inclusion of at least three replicate studies; (iii) presence of a control group; (iv) provision of data spanning a minimum of two years; and (v) availability of yield data for both MSR and RSR.

Utilizing these criteria, we compiled a meta-database comprising 553 data sets from 41 studies deemed suitable for meta-analysis. The detailed methodology for selecting published papers is illustrated in Figure S1. Data such as the study year, geographical coordinates (latitude and longitude) of the study site, variety, nitrogen application rate, nitrogen application ratio, MSR yield, harvesting method, and RSR yield were extracted from the selected literature. The comprehensive dataset utilized in this study, along with the original published references, is provided as Data S1. For each study, the yield from the MSR, RSR, and double season yields was compiled. Their potential influencing factors were categorized into four distinct groups based on the data: (1) nitrogen application rate during the main season, categorized as < 150 kg N ha− 1, 150–225 kg N ha− 1, and > 225 kg N ha− 1; (2) nitrogen application rate during the ratoon season, categorized as < 75 kg N ha− 1, 75–150 kg N ha− 1, and > 150 kg N ha− 1; (3) frequency of nitrogen applications in the main season, categorized as 1, 3, and 4 applications; and (4) frequency of nitrogen applications in the ratoon season, categorized as 1 and 2 applications.

The response ratio was subsequently converted into a percentage change of the variable using the formula ((R-1) × 100), where positive percentage changes reflect an upward trend, whereas negative values reflect a downward trend. In instances where a study lacked a standard deviation (SD), one-tenth of the variable’s mean value was employed as the standard deviation in the database (Gattinger et al. 2012). A random effects meta-analysis was performed with Metawin software (version 2.1.5), utilizing a 4999-iteration bootstrapping procedure to calculate the weighted average effect size and 95% confidence interval (CI) (Wallace et al. 2017). A 95% CI of the CCU that does not overlap with zero suggests a significant average effect size of CCU. Furthermore, non-overlapping 95% CIs between groups indicate significant differences between the average values of the grouping variables.

Field Experiment

Variety Selection

In 2022, a variety screening experiment was conducted to evaluate various ratoon rice varieties across different regions of Anhui Province. The objective was to identify ratoon rice varieties with broader applicability. The experiment was carried out at five distinct sites: Guichi District in Chizhou City (41 varieties), Tongcheng City in Anqing City (18 varieties), Huaining County in Anqing City (10 varieties), Wanzhi District in Wuhu City (15 varieties), and Dangtu County in Ma’anshan City (12 varieties) (Table S1). Cultivation practices adhered to local management protocols. By analyzing yields from the MSR, the RSR, and the double season cycle, as well as comparing similar and different varieties across various regions, the study successfully identified suitable ratoon rice varieties for the diverse ecological zones within Anhui Province.

Experiment Site and Materials

This study aimed to establish nitrogen (N) fertilizer management strategies and identify rice varieties conducive to optimizing the yields of RSR through meta-analysis and variety screening. Consequently, a field experiment was conducted. The hybrid rice variety YLiangyou 911, developed by Hunan Yuan Chuang Super Rice Technology Co., Ltd, was used for this research study. The field trials were carried out from March to October 2023 at the Rice Research Institute of Anhui Academy of Agricultural Sciences. A total of six plots were designated for the experimental study, each of them measuring 100 m2. Among them, three plots were allocated for MSR cultivation and three plots for LSR cultivation. In MSR, the seeds were sown on March 20, 2023, utilizing dry cultivation techniques for seedling raising before field transplanting on April 15, 2023. The main season was harvested on August 9, 2023. While, LSR was sown on June 9, 2023, it reached heading date on September 14, 2023, and was harvested on October 24, 2023. For the MSR and LSR cultivation, the fertilization strategy employed a nutrient ratio of N: P2O5:K2O at approximately 1:0.5:1. The nitrogen fertilizer was allocated according to the following proportions: base fertilizer: tillering fertilizer: panicle fertilizer = 5:2:3, with a total pure nitrogen application of 225 kg ha− 1. Phosphate fertilizer, containing 12% P₂O₅, was exclusively employed as a basal fertilizer, while potassium fertilizer, containing 60% K₂O, was applied in equal proportions as both basal and panicle fertilizers. During the initial harvest, the stubble height was maintained at 25 cm. The fertilization regimens for LSR were consistent with those utilized for MSR. For RSR, N application accounted for 150 kg ha− 1 and was applied in two phases: (i) the applied 20 days after the MSR reached the heading stage (HS), and (ii) applied 3 days post-harvest. The N fertilizer was evenly distributed in between these two phases. Comprehensive details on other field management practices, such as irrigation and pest control, are discussed in detail by Huang et al. (Huang et al. 2022).

Growth Period

Documenting the recorded growth periods included the stages of sowing, transplanting, heading, and maturity for both MSR and LSR. Additionally, for RSR, the records included the stages of heading and maturity.

Grain Yield and Yield Component Determination

The effective panicle number of 30 rice plants (10 plants per site, across three sites) was evaluated in each plot before the harvest of MSR, RSR, and LSR. Based on the survey data, three representative rice plants in each plot were selected and allowed to dry naturally to assess the number of grains per panicle (NP), 1000-grain weight (TGW), effective panicle (EP), and seed setting percentage (SSP). These parameters were subsequently used to calculate the grain yield.

Calculation of Resource Utilization Efficiency

Data on climate were collected from local weather stations, covering daily maximum, average, and minimum temperatures, along with daily solar radiation (Fig. 1). Determination of Dry Matter Content

Using the oven-drying and weighing method, the dry matter content was measured. At both the heading and maturity stages of MSR, RSR, and LSR, three representative plants were analyzed. Following collection, each plant’s leaves, stems, sheaths, and panicles were bagged separately, heated at 105℃ for 30 min to halt enzyme activity, and dried at 70℃ until they maintained a consistent weight. The dry matter weight of each plant part was subsequently measured. According to Lan’s method (2024), the dry matter accumulation from heading to maturity (DMHM), dry matter translocation amount (DMTA), dry matter translocation efficiency (DMTE), contribution of dry matter accumulation to grain after heading (CDMA), and Harvest Index (HI) were determined.

Statistical Analysis

The data utilized in this study originate from the mean values of three biological replicates. Statistical analyses, such as analysis of variance (ANOVA) and the least significant difference (LSD) test, were performed to evaluate the differences between treatments, with a significance threshold of p < 0.05. Data processing and statistical analyses were executed using Origin 2022 and IBM SPSS Statistics 25 software packages.

Result

Meta-Analysis: Assessing Optimal Nitrogen Management

This study identified that an increase in nitrogen application rates during the MSR initially led to an increase in yield in the RSR, followed by a decline (Fig. 2A). Notably, the highest yield in the RSR was observed when nitrogen application rates during the MSR ranged from 150 to 225 kg ha− 1. Conversely, as nitrogen application rates increased in RSR, a linear increase in yield was observed (Fig. 2). Specifically, when nitrogen application rates exceeded 150 kg ha− 1 (our meta-analysis dataset has an upper limit of 200 kg N ha⁻¹) in the ratoon season, yields were significantly higher compared to rates below 75 kg ha− 1 and between 75 and 150 kg ha− 1 (Fig. 3). Regarding the frequency of fertilizer applications, employing a three-application strategy during the main season resulted in higher yields compared to a four-application strategy. Additionally, applying nitrogen fertilizer twice in the ratoon season yielded better results than a single application (Fig. 3). The absence of the < 150 kg N ha− 1 group in Fig. 3 was due to an insufficient number of data points in main season. Consequently, the statistical software correctly excluded this category from the subgroup analysis presented in Fig. 3. Consequently, this study posits that administering three fertilizer applications during the MSR with a nitrogen application rate ranging from 150 to 225 kg ha− 1, followed by two nitrogen applications in RSR at a rate exceeding 150 kg ha− 1, is optimal for enhancing yield formation in the following season. These findings offer a foundational framework for the fertilization management strategies employed in this study on ratoon rice.

Variety Selection: Identifying Optimal Varieties for Ratoon Rice Cultivation

This study evaluated the performance of various ratoon rice varieties across five locations: Guichi, Tongcheng, Huaining, Dangtu, and Wanzhi. The mean yields of RR at these sites were 11.51 t ha− 1, 10.12 t ha− 1, 12.26 t ha− 1, 12.51 t ha− 1, and 11.56 t ha− 1, respectively. Further analysis identified the rice variety YLY911 as consistently present across all five test sites, with yields of 13.46 t ha− 1, 11.51 t ha− 1, 13.29 t ha− 1, 13.52 t ha− 1, and 12.25 t ha− 1, respectively. These yields exceeded the local average levels by 16.94%, 13.74%, 8.40%, 8.07%, and 5.97% (Fig. 4). Consequently, this study proposes to focus on YLY911 as the primary subject for subsequent experimental investigations.

Comparison of MSR, RSR and LSR

Grain Yield and Yield Components

The current study conducted a comparative analysis of the yields of MSR, RSR, and LSR, along with their constituent factors (Table 1). The findings indicated that the GY, NG, and TGW of RSR experienced significant reductions compared to those of MSR and LSR within the same period. The yield, NG, and TGW of RSR were 31.33%, 50.14%, and 5.58% lower than those of MSR, and 37.03%, 48.03%, and 9.62% lower than those of LSR, respectively. Conversely, RSR exhibited significantly higher EP, SSP, and DGY in comparison to both MSR and LSR. Notably, there was an increase in EP by 42.86% and 31.58%, a rise in SSP by 5.46% and 1.87%, and an elevation in DGY by 28.33% and 13.55%. These results indicate that, despite the significantly lower GY of RSR compared to MSR and LSR, the average DGY of RSR was substantially higher. In summary, despite the growth period of RSR accounting for only 53.52% and 55.47% of that of MSR and LSR, respectively (Table 2), it attained 68.67% and 62.97% of the yields of MSR and LSR, while also exhibiting a significantly higher daily yield than both. Therefore, RSR demonstrates superior actual yield performance in comparison to single-season varieties.

Resource Utilization Efficiency

The growth duration of MSR, RSR, and LSR was 142, 76 and 137 days, respectively (Fig. 1; Table 2). Subsequent analysis indicated that the days before flowering of MSR (107 d) and LSR (97 d) were significantly longer than those of RSR (36 d). Conversely, the after flowering days of RSR (40 d) and LSR (40 d) exceeded those of MSR (35 d). These findings suggest that the days before flowering (DBF) of RSR were considerably shorter compared to MSR and LSR. The data on effective accumulated temperature revealed that LSR exhibited the highest before flowering effective accumulated temperature at 1706.5 ℃, followed by MSR at 1136.1 ℃, with RSR having the lowest at 601.0 ℃. Additionally, the after flowering accumulated temperature was significantly greater for MSR than for RSR and LSR. In terms of total accumulated temperature, LSR recorded the highest value at 2100.8 ℃, followed by MSR at 1800.7 ℃, while RSR had the lowest at 955.3 ℃. The pattern of solar radiation exhibited a distinct divergence from that of the effective accumulated temperature. The differences in the before flowering solar radiation between MSR and LSR were not statistically significant, with both being markedly higher than RSR. After flowering, the solar radiation for MSR exceeded that of both RSR and LSR. Analysis of the total accumulated temperature indicated that MSR (2132.60 MJ m− 2) recorded the highest value, followed by LSR (1965.10 MJ m− 2), with RSR (1006.79 MJ m− 2) being the lowest, as illustrated in Fig. 5. Notably, the effective accumulated temperature utilization efficiency of RSR was significantly greater than that of MSR and LSR, surpassing them by 24.26% and 32.96%, respectively. Similarly, the solar radiation utilization efficiency of RSR was significantly superior, exceeding that of MSR and LSR by 45.49% and 22.96%, respectively.

Characteristics of Dry Matter Turnover

The study’s findings indicated that the dry matter weights at the full heading stage and maturity stages for both MSR and LSR were significantly greater than those observed for RSR alone (Fig. 6). Concurrently, the DMHM and DMTA values for RSR, recorded at 2.01 t ha⁻1 and 3.17 t ha⁻1, respectively, were significantly lower compared to those of MSR (3.60 t ha⁻1 and 4.67 t ha⁻1) and LSR (3.77 t ha⁻1 and 4.66 t ha⁻1), with no significant difference detected between MSR and LSR. In contrast, the DMTE and the CDMA for RSR were notably higher than those for MSR and LSR, exhibiting increases of 8.64% to 9.37% and 10.71% to 11.13%, respectively. The harvest index was highest for RSR at 0.51, followed by LSR at 0.49, with MSR being the lowest at 0.45 (Table S2). These findings suggest that RSR possesses enhanced material transport characteristics.

Correlation Analysis

The correlation analysis reveals that grain yield (GY) exhibits a significant positive correlation with both the NG and the TGW (Fig. 7). However, our study indicates that the NG and TGW for RSR are significantly lower compared to MSR and LSR (Table 1). This finding underscores the importance of enhancing NG and TGW during the ratoon season to boost the yield of RSR. Additionally, the daily yield increase (DGY) is significantly positively correlated with EP and SSP. This suggests that the superior daily yield increase observed in RSR, relative to MSR and LSR, can be attributed primarily to higher EP and SSP. Further investigation reveals that NG and TGW are significantly positively correlated with several factors: the total growth period (TGS), DBF, dry matter accumulation (DMHM), dry matter transport (DMTA), days before flowering temperature (ATBF), total accumulated temperature (TAT), days before flowering radiation (SRBF), and total solar radiation (TSR). The findings suggest that extending the nutritional growth period of RSR and enhancing temperature and radiation exposure before flowering are crucial for optimizing RSR yield. Notably, EP and SSP exhibit significant positive correlations with DMTE, CDMA, ATUE, and SRUE, indicating that RSR possesses superior material transport characteristics and temperature-solar radiation utilization efficiency. The results further reveal that RSR achieves a higher EP and SSP compared to MSR and LSR, although it has a significantly lower number of NG and TGW. By efficiently utilizing temperature and solar radiation resources, it is possible to increase the EP and SSP. Additionally, by extending the nutritional growth period and enhancing pre-flowering temperature and radiation, NG and TGW can be improved, thereby boosting overall yield. Consequently, this study concludes that the strategic allocation of temperature and solar radiation resources is pivotal for optimizing RSR yield formation.

Discussion

Synergistic Optimization of Nitrogen Fertilizer Management and Variety Selection for Enhancing the Yield of RSR

Ratoon rice cultivation is an efficient agricultural practice that optimizes the use of solar radiation, temperature, and land resources, and it is significantly influenced by nitrogen fertilizer management practices and varietal characteristics (Lin et al. 2023; Torres et al. 2020). This study employs a combination of meta-analysis and multi-site variety screening experiments to systematically examine the mechanisms by which nitrogen fertilizer management and variety selection impact the yield of RR. This research was aimed to identify optimal nitrogen fertilizer management practices and suitable varieties. Initially, a meta-analysis of data from 41 related studies was conducted to elucidate the nitrogen response pattern with the RR system. The findings indicated that nitrogen application rate during the main season should be within the range of 150–225 kg ha− 1. Furthermore, when the nitrogen application rate in the ratoon season is ≥ 150 kg ha− 1 (our meta-analysis dataset has an upper limit of 200 kg N ha⁻¹), the yield of the ratoon season reaches its optimal level. This fertilization strategy can effectively align with the nutrient requirements of the two rice cultivation seasons. The moderate nitrogen application during the main season not only ensures a high yield but also mitigates the negative impacts of excessive nitrogen on the ratoon season. On the other hand, the increased nitrogen input during the ratoon season significantly enhances yield formation. This study demonstrated that a fertilization schedule comprising three applications in the main season (base fertilizer, tillering fertilizer, and panicle fertilizer) and two applications in the ratoon season (promoting bud fertilizer and maintaining tillering fertilizer) optimally supports yield development in the ratoon season. From a physiological mechanism perspective, effective nitrogen fertilizer management modulates the activity of key enzymes involved in carbon-nitrogen metabolism (Ali et al. 2020), thereby optimizing the distribution of photosynthetic products between the MSR and the RSR (Li et al. 2025). Alongside, superior rice varieties enhanced the yield-boosting effects of nitrogen fertilizers through their inherent genetic traits (Zhang et al. 2021). Together, the productivity of the ratoon rice system is improved by the synergy of these factors. The innovative contribution of this study is the establishment, for the first time, of a quantitative standard for nitrogen fertilizer application in ratoon rice through meta-analysis, addressing the issue of inconsistent fertilization norms. Furthermore, multi-ecological site experiments have confirmed the broad adaptability and high-yield potential of the YLY911 variety, providing a scientific foundation for strategic regional varietal deployment. The quantitative cultivation technology system developed in this study holds significant theoretical value and offers reliable technical support for the large-scale promotion of ratoon rice, playing a crucial role in ensuring food security.

While we established a nitrogen fertilizer management strategy for regenerative rice through a meta-analysis, this study possesses several limitations that warrant consideration. The studies included in the analysis demonstrated inherent heterogeneity due to differences in soil types, climatic conditions, and crop management practices across various geographical regions. Although random-effects models and subgroup analyses were utilized to address this variability, it remains a potential source of uncertainty. Additionally, the depth of our analysis was restricted by the variables commonly reported in the primary literature. The frequent lack of detailed physiological data or precise timings for agronomic practices constrained our ability to explore the underlying mechanisms driving the observed responses. Another frequently encountered limitation in meta-analyses, namely publication bias, was also recognized. The propensity for journals to favor the publication of statistically significant results can potentially skew the overall effect size, and our findings have been interpreted with this consideration in mind. To mitigate these limitations and enhance the robustness of our findings, we recommend that future primary studies implement more standardized reporting protocols for critical agronomic and environmental variables. Furthermore, targeted mechanistic research is crucial to complement the empirical patterns identified in this study and to elucidate the underlying causal processes.

The Formation of the Yield of RSR and its Regulation by Temperature and Solar Radiation

The development of RSR yield is a multifaceted physiological and ecological process, with its yield components primarily comprising EP, NG, TGW, and SSP (Li et al. 2017). The findings of this study reveal a significant positive correlation between GY and both NG and TGW, corroborating previous research findings (Zhou et al. 2025). Notably, the NG and TGW of RSR in this study were significantly lower than those observed in MSR and LSR, a phenomenon potentially attributable to the unique growth and developmental characteristics of RSR (Li et al. 2025). Further analysis indicates that NG and TGW are significantly positively correlated with the total growth period (TGS), the days before flowering, DMHM, DMTA, the accumulated temperature before flowering (ATBF), the total accumulated temperature (TAT), the accumulated solar radiation before flowering (SRBF), and the total solar radiation (TSR). The findings of this study underscore the significant influence of temperature and solar radiation on the yield formation of RSR. In particular, the days before flowering is critical, as it allows plants ample time to accumulate and transport photosynthetic products. Moreover, an optimal accumulation of temperature and radiation prior to flowering furnishes the essential energy required for dry matter production (Batista et al. 2024; Liu et al. 2023). From a physiological standpoint, prolonging the vegetative growth phase may enhance yield formation through several mechanisms: Firstly, an extended vegetative period enables the development of additional photosynthetic organs, thereby augmenting the photosynthetic capacity of the plant population (Wada et al. 2015); Secondly, adequate growth duration facilitates the development and formation of tillers and ears, which in turn increases the number of effective spikes (Ma et al. 2020); Lastly, robust vegetative growth establishes a strong material foundation for subsequent reproductive growth (Lavinsky et al. 2016). These insights offer a crucial theoretical framework for optimizing high-yield cultivation strategies for RSR.

The efficiency of rice growth is assessed using the important metric of daily grain yield (DGY) (Yang et al. 2008). This study identified a significant positive correlation between DGY and both the EP and SSP. Notably, the EP and SSP of RSR were significantly higher than those observed in MSR and LSR, potentially accounting for its elevated DGY. Further analysis indicated that EP and SSP were significantly positively correlated with DMTE, CDMA, ATUE, and SRUE. From a physiological standpoint, enhanced dry matter transport efficiency facilitates the effective allocation of photosynthetic products to the spike, thereby augmenting the SSP and grain TGW (Heng et al. 2018; Hu et al. 2025). These findings imply that the high-yield advantage of RSR may be attributed to its proficient dry matter transport and utilization capabilities.

Comprehensive Strategy for Optimizing the Allocation of Temperature and Solar Radiation Energy Resources

Based on the findings of this study, a comprehensive strategy must be developed to achieve the coordinated enhancement of high yield in RSR (Gai et al. 2024). This strategy should focus on optimizing the allocation of temperature and solar radiation resources (Xie et al. 2023; Xu et al. 2020). Temporally, it is essential to prioritize the coordination between vegetative and reproductive growth phases (Lal et al. 2023a, b). Specifically, strategies such as variety selection and cultivation practices should be employed to extend the vegetative growth period, thereby establishing a foundation for high yield. Spatially, the efficiency of temperature and solar radiation utilization can be enhanced through several approaches: firstly, implementing wide-row and narrow-row planting techniques to improve ventilation and solar radiation conditions within the crop population (Hu et al. 2020); secondly, creating an efficient photosynthetic population by employing optimal planting densities (Chang et al. 2020); and thirdly, utilizing precise irrigation technologies to regulate solar radiation and optimize population dynamics. (Zou et al. 2024). The extensive implementation of these measures is anticipated to facilitate the efficient utilization of temperature and solar radiation resources. From a technological integration standpoint, it is advisable to adopt the “three adjustments and one control” technical approach: adjusting the sowing date (Huang et al. 2024), modifying the stubble height (Zhang et al. 2025), optimizing nitrogen fertilizer management (Zou et al. 2024), and regulating the temperature and solar radiation environment during the grain-filling period (Hu et al. 2024). This holistic regulatory strategy thoroughly addresses the requirements for yield formation and possesses significant practical value.

Conclusion

This study initially identified optimal nitrogen fertilizer management practices and suitable rice varieties for ratoon rice through meta-analysis and variety screening. Subsequently, it evaluated the differences in yield formation between MSR, RSR, and LSR. The findings revealed that RSR exhibited a greater EP and SSR. However, it had significantly fewer NG and a lower TGW compared to MSR and LSR. Correlation analysis suggested that the ATUE and SRUE contributed to an increase in the EP and SSR. By strategically extending the vegetative growth phase of RSR and optimizing ATBF and SRBF, it is possible to enhance the NG and TGW, thereby contributing to yield improvement. These findings offer a theoretical foundation and technical support for the efficient cultivation of RSR. Future research should prioritize the development of region-specific cultivation technology systems, the integration of intelligent agricultural technologies, and the selection of novel varieties to advance the sustainable development of the ratoon rice industry.

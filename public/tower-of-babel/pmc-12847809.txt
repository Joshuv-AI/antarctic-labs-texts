Customizable front end design using improved StyleGAN with detail control
Authors: Pengcheng Yang, Xiaoting Li, Ping Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractImage generation technology using generative adversarial networks has been widely used in front-end design, but existing models have problems such as fuzzy generation and limited style expression. This study proposes an improved StyleGAN (Style Generative Adversarial Network) model to achieve style transfer and high-quality generation of front-end interface elements. An additional module is added after the generator to calculate the mutual information between the latent variables and the output of the network layer, and integrate it into the discriminator loss function for joint optimization to enhance the ability to control details. The overall FID (Frechet Inception Distance) value of the images generated by the improved model on the Rico dataset reaches 12.5, and the MS-SSIM (Multi-Scale Structural Similarity) reaches 0.92. Among them, the IS (Inception Score) value of the image generated for the Navigation Menu category reaches 7.41, which is about 14.2% higher than the baseline StyleGAN. The method used effectively solves the problem of detail distortion in front-end page generation, and realizes the precise mapping of style features and design elements through the mutual information constraint mechanism, providing a highly customizable technical framework for front-end intelligent design.Keywords: Front-end AI assistant, Intelligent learning model, Style generative adversarial network, Style transfer, Mutual informationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The rapid development of artificial intelligence technology has led to the rise of AI assistants in the field of front-end design. They can automatically generate and optimize the design of front-end elements according to user needs1,2. With the remarkable achievements of large models such as DeepSeek3, Chat-GPT (Chat Generative Pre-trained Transformer)4, and T5 (Text-To-Text Transfer Transformer)5 in different application scenarios, in the field of page design, the use of image generation technology to quickly synthesize interface elements that meet user needs and provide personalized solutions6,7 has gradually become mainstream, such as the DALL·E model recently developed by OpenAI8.

With various mainstream architectures such as DCGAN (Deep Convolutional GAN), WGAN (Wasserstein GAN), and ProGAN9–11 widely used in image generation and style transfer tasks, StyleGAN has gained widespread attention due to its high-quality and high-resolution image generation12,13. Compared with other GAN architectures, this model allows users to fine-tune each level of the generated image by applying a style control mechanism.

However, its direct application to the domain of front-end design still faces several critical challenges that hinder the generation of high-quality, customizable interfaces:(a) Blurred Image Details & Artifacts: The fine-grained details of generated interface elements (e.g., sharp icon edges, clean text regions) are often blurred or contain artifacts14,15, failing to meet the pixel-level precision required for professional front-end development. To tackle this, we introduce a mutual information constraint module that explicitly reinforces the correlation between the latent style codes and the fine details in the generated output, significantly enhancing detail clarity. (b) Limited & Imprecise Style Control: The model’s ability to express and transfer complex design styles is often limited and imprecise16,17. It struggles to establish and maintain a accurate, pixel-level mapping between abstract style features (e.g., "minimalist," “corporate”) and concrete design elements (e.g., spacing, color palette, border radius). Our solution is to design a feature disentanglement and hierarchical optimization mechanism, leveraging the mutual information constraints to achieve this precise mapping and ensure style consistency across the generated interface. (c) Insufficient Customization for Functional Layouts: The model lacks the flexibility to adapt to diverse user needs and the precision to handle the structural requirements of functional layout components (e.g., navigation menus, forms)18,19. To this end, we construct a scalable intelligent design framework that incorporates strategies like adaptive noise injection and differentiated optimization for functional versus decorative regions, enabling personalized and structurally sound front-end generation. The above problems are also common in other GAN architectures.

This study proposes a front-end AI assistant intelligent learning model based on an improved StyleGAN to enhance its image generation details and style expression capabilities, and solve the shortcomings of existing models in generating fine design elements. Through the mutual information constraint mechanism, the precise mapping of style features and design elements is achieved, thereby improving the quality of generated images and the similarity with the target style. Related work

In response to the needs of image generation and style transfer in front-end design, many studies have attempted to apply GAN to front-end interface design and optimization to generate high-quality web page elements and interface components20,21. Quan Steven Jige22proposed to apply DCGAN to multi-dimensional element design. Hu Mingxuan et al23. proposed to combine WGAN to achieve efficient differentiation of style attribute features. This method could generate design elements that were visually close to the target style by performing style conversion and style transfer on the original image, thereby improving the level of design automation24,25. Although these models have made breakthroughs in the basic quality of generated images, the details of the images they generate are still relatively blurred in the high-resolution design of complex front-end interfaces.

StyleGAN, as a variant of a generative adversarial network, has achieved remarkable results in image style generation and facial image synthesis. By applying fine-grained control of the latent space, the generated images have greater flexibility in style control and detail expression26,27. By injecting spatial constraints into the original StyleGAN generation process, Su Wanchao28 et al. applied an improved spatial conditional StyleGAN to achieve more precise control over the image generation process. Chen Anpei29 et al. further decoupled the model’s latent space and input it into two network branches to generate 3D geometry and texture, thereby generating high-quality images. Jang Wonjong30 et al. proposed a hierarchical improvement of StyleGAN to achieve more detailed image generation through shape modules and layer blending. Related studies have shown that StyleGAN can effectively generate images that are highly similar to the input image style and provide relatively precise style transfer in the design of front-end interface elements31,32. However, StyleGAN still faces two major problems when generating images: first, the resolution and details of the generated images are often limited by the network depth and training dataset33, which makes it difficult to meet the requirements of high-quality front-end design; second, in the process of style transfer, the adaptability and diversity of the target style are still limited34, while in front-end design, the variability of image style and precise control of details are often required.

To improve the quality of generated images, existing studies have proposed a number of optimization strategies. Among them, ProGAN (Progressive GAN)35 can effectively improve the image detail performance and reduce the mode collapse phenomenon in the image generation process by gradually increasing the network depth and training resolution. CycleGAN36 and SNGAN (Spectral Normalization GAN)37 respectively improve the style consistency and detail quality of generated images by applying techniques such as cycle consistency and spectral normalization. Wang Ning38 et al. proposed to apply channel space attention into the GAN model to promote jump connections to eliminate the noise amplification problem of generated images. By downsampling the feature map of each residual layer and using jump links and upsampling to fuse low-level and high-level features, Wei Yuhan39 et al. successfully improved the GAN model and achieved high-quality image transfer effects. Chen Yuanqi40 et al. proposed to improve the generation quality by constraining the spectral norm of the discriminator weight matrix, and its SSIM (Structural Similarity Index) reaches 0.6696. This type of optimization model has alleviated the problem of poor quality of GAN generation to a certain extent, but it is still not customized for specific application scenarios of front-end design, and is still limited in the precision of style transfer and detail control.

It can be seen that the current practice of generative models in front-end AI assistant applications still faces multiple limitations. Although existing models have made certain progress in style transfer and detail control, it is still difficult to meet the design requirements of high resolution when dealing with front-end interfaces with complex structures41,42. Although StyleGAN has certain advantages in image style control, how to precisely control image details and achieve diversified design styles that meet user needs in front-end design is still a core problem that needs to be solved urgently43,44. Combined with the current research status, this article proposes an improved StyleGAN model, which optimizes the generation process by applying mutual information constraints to improve the accuracy of detail control and style transfer. Different from the traditional StyleGAN, this method adds an additional module after the generator to calculate the mutual information between the latent variables and the network layer output, and incorporates it into the discriminator loss function to promote image detail adjustment and style consistency. The improved model can effectively solve the problem of detail distortion in front-end page generation and generate high-quality front-end design through efficient style mapping.

Synthesis Network generates high-quality images by gradually increasing the resolution. The network starts with a learnable 4 × 4 × 512 constant, undergoes two 3 × 3 convolutions, and uses AdaIN normalization and Leaky ReLU (Rectified Linear Unit) activation functions. Each layer then expands the resolution by 2 × upsampling and refines the features by two 3 × 3 convolutions. The number of channels is kept at 512 before 32 × 32, and then gradually decreases to 16 when it reaches 1024 × 1024 to optimize computational efficiency. To enhance details, noise injection is performed to apply random changes and improve local texture expression. At the same time, each layer is style-adjusted through AdaIN to control feature statistics. The incorporation of Gaussian noise is a deliberate design choice inherited from the StyleGAN framework. It is introduced to provide a source of stochastic variation that is decoupled from the global style information carried by the style vector w. Theoretically, this allows the generator to utilize the noise to model high-frequency, local details that are not deterministic from the style alone. In the context of front-end interface generation, this mechanism enables the model to synthesize non-repetitive and natural-looking micro-textures, such as subtle gradients, icon surface details, and anti-aliased edges, thereby enhancing the visual realism and diversity of the generated UI elements. Without this noise input, the generator might produce overly smooth or deterministic results, lacking in the fine-grained variability observed in real-world designs.

The important improvements of StyleGAN compared to the existing GAN architecture are in two points: on the one hand, the latent vector is nonlinearly mapped and transformed through multiple fully connected layers, and the style vector is obtained as part of the input of the Synthesis Network; on the other hand, although Synthesis Network itself is similar to the GAN infrastructure, it utilizes AdaIN as a normalization and style modulation layer, and uses and the noise vector as input to control the image style details, which can decouple the image feature elements46 and adjust the style vector to achieve personalized adjustment of different styles in the image. Unlike activation functions that introduce non-linearity (e.g., LeakyReLU), AdaIN serves to modulate the feature map by aligning its channel-wise mean and variance with the affine-transformed style vector w, thereby controlling the appearance and style of the generated features.

Considering the front-end page’s fine requirements for style consistency and detail elements, the generated images based on this method are not applicable to the front-end AI assistant. The reason is that the lack of explicit constraints between the style vector and the generated image p makes the generation fuzzy. At the same time, the loss of some spatial position information during the AdaIN normalization process leads to limited style expression on the front-end page. To solve this problem, this study refers to the EAD-GAN (Explicit Affine Eisentangled Generative Adversarial Network) model characteristics47 and applies its ideas into StyleGAN to calculate the mutual information between the style vector and the generated image p. At the same time, the calculation results are used as part of the discriminator loss function to achieve more refined generation and control of the front-end generated image.

The overall structure of the mutual information constraint module is illustrated in Fig. 3. By co-processing the images generated by the generator and the latent vectors, this module enhances the correlation between style features and design elements. Specifically, the module first performs multi-scale convolutional feature extraction on the generated image to capture low-dimensional features related to style; at the same time, the latent vector undergoes a fully connected transformation for dimensionality reduction. Both representations are then fed into a mutual information estimator composed of a two-layer MLP. The mutual information loss is approximated by calculating the Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence between the joint and marginal distributions. This loss is finally introduced as a regularization term into the discriminator’s loss function, enabling joint optimization of generation details and style control.

The overall structure of the mutual information constraint module.

In terms of parameter settings, the module adopts a lightweight design: the multi-scale feature extractor consists of three stacked convolutional layers (with channel dimensions of 64, 128, and 256, respectively), combined with LeakyReLU activation functions; the fully connected layer compresses the 512-dimensional latent vector into a 128-dimensional representation; the mutual information estimator is composed of two hidden layers (with neuron counts of 256 and 128, respectively). The Adam optimizer is used (learning rate = 0.001, β₁ = 0.9, β₂ = 0.999), with a batch size of 64. The mutual information is approximated using the DV lower bound method, and a gradient penalty mechanism is introduced to ensure training stability and efficient detail transfer.

In this study, noise injection and the mutual information module are complementary rather than conflicting. Noise provides local high-frequency random variations, enabling the generator to synthesize natural details, while the mutual information module constrains the style vector and the global structure and style consistency of the generated image. Through this design, noise does not weaken the mutual information constraint; instead, it enriches local textures and micro-features while ensuring style consistency, making the high-frequency details generated by the front-end interface more diverse and natural.

Training optimization and model tuning

The generator of StyleGAN uses a multi-layer fully connected mapping network to convert random noise vectors into an intermediate latent space. After a series of progressively upsampled convolution modules, the image resolution is continuously expanded48,49. Each module injects the style vector output by the mapping network into the feature map through AdaIN. At the same time, independent noise variables are added to each layer to apply subtle random changes and enhance the natural texture and diversity of the image50. The discriminator adopts the convolutional network structure shown in Table 2. Through multi-layer convolution and downsampling, the hierarchical features of the image are gradually extracted, and finally, the fully connected layer is used to determine the authenticity of the image. Its design goal is to capture the subtle differences in the statistical distribution between the generated image and the real image.

During the training process, the discriminator and generator parameters are updated alternately. Experimental test

Data source and experimental setting

Rico (https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/onurgunes1993/rico-dataset) is used for experimental testing. This dataset is currently the largest application interface design library, containing 72,219 unique UIs (User Interfaces). To ensure data quality and optimize model training results, all images are uniformly adjusted to 256 × 256 pixels and converted to RGB (Red Green Blue) color space to ensure that the data scale is consistent with the StyleGAN discriminator. The dataset is divided into list view, form interface, navigation menu, pop-up window/floating layer, and others according to interface type, and stratified sampling is performed according to 70% training set, 15% validation set, and 15% test set. Model testing

Ablation experiment test

The ablation experiment compares 4 configurations: a) baseline configuration: the Mapping Network and mutual information constraint modules are kept. b) Configuration A (removing the Mapping Network module): the original latent vector is directly input into the middle layer of the generator; the mutual information constraint module is kept; the style expression and detail fidelity reduction caused by removing the Mapping Network are examined. c) Configuration B (removing the mutual information constraint module): the Mapping Network module is still retained in the generator, but the mutual information estimator and the corresponding regularization term are removed to verify the impact of the mutual information constraint module on detail transmission. d) Configuration AB (removing the Mapping Network and mutual information modules at the same time): the original latent vector is directly used as input, and the two modules are removed to evaluate the comprehensive impact of the lack of dual modules on the consistency of the style of the model-generated image.

The synergy of the two can maximize the quality of front-end interface element generation. Both modules are retained in the following experimental settings. In Fig. 10, when the noise intensity is 5%, the MS-SSIM value increases, indicating that slight noise helps to restore details. However, when the noise intensity increases to 10%−15%, there is no significant change in MS-SSIM. At the same time, as the occlusion ratio increases, the MS-SSIM value increases within the occlusion range of 10%−23%, but gradually decreases within the occlusion range of 23%−30%, and the reasons for these phenomena are still unclear. To this end, the study tracks the activation of each convolutional layer of the generator during the noise injection stage to verify whether low-intensity noise is reconstructed through high-frequency edge enhancement, and whether medium and high noise destroys consistency due to latent space perturbations. At the same time, the generation deviation of the occluded area is analyzed to explore whether the generator relies too much on the global style prior, causing the local structure to deviate from the true distribution. By comparing the impact of the occlusion position on MS-SSIM, the model’s ability to understand the semantic hierarchy of the interface is revealed.

In the experiment, the high-frequency edge activation intensity is extracted by extracting the convolution feature map of the middle layer of the generator. The texture response is quantified by the Sobel operator, and the mean and standard deviation of the high-frequency components of each layer are counted. The latent space perturbation characterizes the degree of perturbation by comparing the latent vector offset before and after noise injection and normalizing it with L2 distance. For the local structural deviation of the occluded area, the FID value of StyleGAN-MI for different occluded parts is calculated to evaluate the quality of local completion; the MS-SSIM difference compares the pixel similarity between the generated image and the original image in the occluded area through a sliding window multi-scale comparison, and calculates the mean difference. The significance test of the indicators here is verified by Tukey’s HSD to ensure statistical power (p < 0.05). The data verification results are shown in Tables 8 and 9.

In subsequent research, it is possible to consider promoting model optimization based on the following aspects. First, an adaptive noise injection strategy is applied in the training mechanism to improve the model’s anti-interference ability by dynamically adjusting the noise intensity. Second, differentiated optimization is performed for the functional and decorative areas of interface components: the conditional attention mechanism is integrated in the functional area to enhance the semantic understanding of the component, and the hierarchical feature fusion network is used in the decorative area to improve the fidelity of texture details. Third, the computational efficiency is optimized: the benchmark training time of the current model of 112 h is compressed based on the model distillation technology, and the distributed training framework is developed simultaneously to further improve the GPU utilization. After the above improvements, StyleGAN-MI forms a technical solution with both high generation quality and engineering practicality, provides a feasible core generation engine for the front-end intelligent design system, and accelerates the AI-driven interface automation development process.

User Research and Expert Evaluation

To compensate for the limitations of relying solely on pixel-level quantitative indicators, at the suggestion of reviewers, this study supplemented the evaluation with subjective assessments, including usability/aesthetics ratings for ordinary users and expert ratings for professional UI designers. The aim was to measure the performance of the model-generated UI on two dimensions closer to design perception: “usability” and "aesthetics," and to verify the consistency between quantitative indicators and subjective feelings.

Among the participants, the ordinary user group consisted of 30 participants (aged 20–45, balanced gender), all non-professional UI designers with daily experience using websites/applications. The expert group consisted of 8 UI/UX designers (3–12 years of work experience) with experience in interface design or usability assessment. Each participant randomly viewed 30 test images within their design: 10 real human designs (from a subset of the Rico test), 10 generated by the baseline StyleGAN, and 10 generated by the StyleGAN-MI model used in this study. The image order and source were randomly shuffled to eliminate order effects. For each image, participants rated it on two subjective scales (1–5, 1 being the worst and 5 the best): (a) usability (subjective feeling of ease of understanding and operation); and (b) aesthetics (visual appeal and perceived design quality). Perceived realism (0–100%) and task success were also recorded: participants had to locate designated functions (such as “Settings” or “Search”) on the simulated prototype, which was counted as success or failure. An expert panel provided three additional professional ratings (1–5) for the same batch of images: consistency, accessibility prediction, and pixel fidelity.

The online questionnaire and user interface were used (each image was displayed for 8–12 s for rating); task success was measured by one click on the interactive prototype. The average and standard deviation of each subjective score were calculated; repeated measures ANOVA (within-subject) was used to assess differences between conditions, followed by a Tukey HSD post-hoc test; the chi-square test was used to verify the significance of differences in task success rates. The significance threshold was set at p < 0.05.

Conclusions

This article proposes a front-end AI assistant intelligent learning model based on the improved StyleGAN. By integrating the mutual information dynamic constraint mechanism and the hierarchical style control architecture, it solves the core problems of the existing model in interface generation, such as fuzzy details and insufficient style adaptation. For the first time, the mutual information quantization of latent variables and network output is applied into the discriminator for joint optimization to achieve pixel-level accurate mapping of style features and functional elements. The feature decoupling module is designed based on the hierarchical generation characteristics of StyleGAN, which significantly improves the fidelity of details and the ability to transfer personalized styles, and verifies the accuracy advantage in complex interface generation tasks such as navigation menus.

However, the current model has only been validated on the RICO dataset. In the future, we plan to extend the evaluation to more UI-related datasets to assess its generalization performance, and introduce cross-device and cross-platform interface data to further verify the model’s adaptability and transferability in multimodal scenarios. At the same time, the model still faces limitations such as low training efficiency and insufficient cross-modal generation capability. Future research could focus on lightweight inference and incremental training optimization, cross-modal collaborative generation, and multi-task end-to-end systems. These efforts aim to further unlock the unique potential of StyleGAN in the field of intelligent learning, provide a highly extensible technical framework for intelligent front-end design, and advance generative AI-driven human–computer interaction toward more intelligent and real-time experiences.

Quantum and molecular modelling of abrocitinib as a potential disruptor of cell polarity in colorectal cancer
Authors: Gideon E Mathias, Loveth C Iwuala, Onyinye J Ikenyirimba, Stephenie N Obannaya, Michael R Ekep-Obasi, Eno E Ebenso
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractColorectal cancer (CRC) remains a major global health challenge, with chronic inflammation and disruption of epithelial polarity identified as key drivers of tumor progression. Polarity complexes such as Scribble–Mutated in Colorectal Cancer (MCC) are central to maintaining epithelial homeostasis, and their dysregulation has been linked to oncogenic transformation. Drug repurposing offers a promising strategy to accelerate therapeutic discovery by identifying new applications for clinically approved agents. In this study, we investigated abrocitinib (ABR), a selective JAK1 inhibitor, as a candidate for repurposing in CRC. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations at the PBE0-D3/6-31G(d) level were used to examine its electronic properties, including geometry optimization, natural bond orbital (NBO) analysis, molecular electrostatic potential (MESP) mapping, and Fukui function analysis. ABR exhibited a moderate HOMO–LUMO gap (1.827 eV) and an electrophilicity index of 3.492 eV, consistent with favorable reactivity and stability. Molecular docking with the Scribble PDZ1 domain (PDB ID: 6MTV) predicted a strong binding affinity (MolDock score − 78.66 kcal/mol; rerank score − 55.69 kcal/mol), supported by electrostatic and hydrophobic interactions. Importantly, comparative docking with fluorouracil and capecitabine demonstrated that ABR achieved more favorable binding energies and greater interaction stability than these reference CRC drugs, highlighting its superior potential to disrupt Scribble–MCC interactions. Monte Carlo simulations and normal mode analysis further confirmed the dynamic stability of the ABR–PDZ1 complex. Collectively, these findings provide preliminary evidence that abrocitinib may act as a promising modulator of polarity-regulating protein–protein interactions in CRC. While experimental validation remains necessary, this study establishes a computational framework that supports further biochemical and cellular investigations into abrocitinib’s potential as a repurposed therapeutic candidate.Keywords: Abrocitinib, Colorectal cancer, Drug repurposing, PDZ domain interaction, Density functional theory (DFT), Protein-Protein interaction disruptionSubject terms: Biochemistry, Cancer, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Drug discovery

Introduction

Cancer, classified as a non-communicable disease, is characterized by uncontrolled cell proliferation that has the potential to invade adjacent tissues and metastasize distant organs, significantly contributing to morbidity and mortality worldwide1,2. Among various cancers, colorectal cancer (CRC) stands out as a major global health burden, ranking as the third most common cancer and the second leading cause of cancer-related deaths globally3,4. CRC originates from the epithelial cells lining the colon, a vital component of the large intestine, and its incidence has been escalating at an alarming rate, largely driven by lifestyle, genetic, and environmental factors. According to GLOBOCAN 2020 estimates, the number of new CRC cases is projected to rise from approximately 1.93 million in 2020 to over 3.15 million by 2040, representing a 63.3% increase worldwide5. This surge underscores the urgent need for improved screening, prevention, and treatment strategies. Key lifestyle factors, including excessive alcohol consumption, tobacco use, physical inactivity, and diets rich in processed red meats have been identified as significant contributors to CRC development, highlighting the importance of public health interventions aimed at modifiable risk factors.

Hence, the pathogenesis of CRC involves a complex interplay of molecular pathways that drive initiation and progression. Notably, chromosomal instability (CIN), microsatellite instability (MSI), and the CpG island methylator phenotype (CIMP) are recognized as major genetic and epigenetic mechanisms. These processes result in alterations of crucial tumor suppressor genes such as TP53, whose loss impairs cell cycle control and apoptosis along with oncogenes like KRAS, which promote aberrant cell signaling. Additionally, mutations in the APC gene—integral to the regulation of Wnt signaling are central to early tumorigenesis, especially in familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP). Mismatch repair (MMR) gene mutations contribute to MSI, leading to nucleotide sequence errors and increased mutational burden, which can facilitate tumor heterogeneity and resistance to therapies6. Furthermore, recent research has emphasized the significance of cell polarity and adhesion signaling disruptions in CRC development. The Scribble protein, a scaffolding molecule containing PDZ domains, is critical for maintaining epithelial polarity and orchestrating cell-cell junctions. It regulates directional cell migration and acts as a tumor suppressor. Interaction with MCC (mutated in colorectal cancer), another tumor suppressor, is particularly vital in preserving epithelial integrity. Loss or mislocalization of Scribble has been linked to impaired cell polarity, deregulation of contact inhibition, and induction of epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT); a key process in invasion and metastasis. Structural studies of the Scribble PDZ1–MCC complex (e.g., PDB entries 6MTV and 6MTU) reveal that post-translational modifications, such as phosphorylation at MCC’s −1 serine residue, modulate but do not completely abrogate this interaction, suggesting a nuanced regulatory mechanism that could be exploited for therapeutic interventions7,8.

Colorectal cancer (CRC) arises through a multifactorial etiology involving genetic, epigenetic, and cellular alterations, including disruption of fundamental processes such as polarity and adhesion. While much attention has focused on canonical pathways like JAK/STAT and Wnt/β-catenin, emerging evidence suggests that the loss of polarity proteins such as Scribble may cooperate with these oncogenic signals. Chronic intestinal inflammation, a recognized precursor to CRC, disrupts epithelial architecture and fosters a proinflammatory microenvironment9,10. This environment sustains tumor growth through cytokines and growth factors while promoting immune evasion and persistent STAT3 activation, processes implicated in CRC progression and metastasis11. In this context, the present work explores, from a computational perspective, whether abrocitinib—a selective JAK1 inhibitor—might interfere with downstream inflammatory signals in ways that could complement polarity regulation. Although Scribble itself is not a direct effector within the JAK/STAT pathway, we hypothesize that its tumor-suppressive functions could be indirectly supported through upstream modulation of inflammatory drivers. This dual focus on inflammatory signaling and polarity scaffolds forms the conceptual basis for our in-silico investigation12.

Abrocitinib is an orally available small molecule that selectively inhibits Janus kinase 1 (JAK1) and is approved for the treatment of moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis, where it has demonstrated efficacy and tolerability in phase III clinical trials13–18. Its mechanism involves inhibition of cytokine signaling pathways mediated by interleukins such as IL-6, IL-12, and interferon-γ (IFN-γ), which are key contributors to inflammation-driven tumorigenesis14,15. Pharmacokinetically, abrocitinib undergoes hepatic metabolism through cytochrome P450 enzymes (CYP2C19, CYP3A4, CYP2C9, CYP2B6), and its therapeutic use reduces systemic inflammatory mediators including IL-31 and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hsCRP), suggesting broader anti-inflammatory potential. Given the established role of chronic inflammation in CRC pathogenesis, these properties provide a rationale for exploring abrocitinib as a repurposed candidate. While abrocitinib does not directly target polarity proteins, computational modeling can be used to evaluate its compatibility with polarity-regulating domains such as Scribble–MCC. Accordingly, this study applies density functional theory (DFT), molecular docking, and simulation techniques to assess the plausibility of abrocitinib interacting with the Scribble PDZ1 domain, thereby generating hypotheses for subsequent experimental testing.

Substantially, to explore this hypothesis, this study employed density functional theory (DFT) methods utilizing the PBE1PBE (PBE0) functional combined with Grimme’s D3 empirical dispersion correction (empirical dispersion = gd3), ensuring accurate modeling of non-covalent interactions critical for molecular recognition. The computational approach included geometry optimization, analysis of frontier molecular orbitals, evaluation of global quantum descriptors, natural bond orbital (NBO) analysis, molecular electrostatic potential (MESP) mapping, molecular docking, and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations. These in silico investigations aim to elucidate abrocitinib’s structural and electronic properties and its interaction potential with Scribble PDZ1 domains. Hence, by integrating abrocitinib’s established anti-inflammatory profile with molecular modeling techniques, this research proposes a novel avenue for drug repurposing, potentially enhancing therapeutic strategies against CRC through targeted modulation of critical protein interfaces involved in tumor progression.

Computational details

Density functional theory

All quantum chemical calculations were performed using Gaussian 16. Geometry optimization of abrocitinib (ABR) was carried out in the gas phase at the PBE0 (PBE1PBE) level of theory with Grimme’s D3 empirical dispersion correction (empiricaldispersion = gd3). The 6-31G(d) basis set was employed for all atoms, and optimizations were performed without symmetry constraints under Gaussian’s tight convergence criteria to ensure accurate geometries. To verify the stability of the optimized structure, harmonic vibrational frequency analyses were performed at the same level of theory. These calculations confirmed that the optimized geometry corresponded to a true minimum on the potential energy surface, as no imaginary frequencies were observed. The frequency analyses also generated thermochemical parameters, ensuring consistency with the chosen computational protocol19,20.

Natural Bond Orbital (NBO) analysis was carried out to investigate donor–acceptor interactions, charge delocalization, and electronic structure features that reveal potential reactive sites. Frontier molecular orbital (FMO) analysis was performed to determine EHOMO, ELUMO, the HOMO–LUMO energy gap (ΔE), ionization potential (IP), electron affinity (EA), hardness (η), softness (S), and electrophilicity index (ω). Additionally, Fukui function indices were calculated to identify electrophilic and nucleophilic regions of ABR.

All calculations were conducted in the gas phase with the specified parameters, ensuring reproducibility of the quantum chemical results.

Protein preparation

The crystal structure of the Scribble PDZ1 domain (PDB ID: 6MTV) was retrieved from the Protein Data Bank. Protein preparation was performed in UCSF Chimera 1.1621, where hydrogen atoms were added, missing side chains were repaired, water molecules beyond 5 Å were removed, and protonation states were adjusted at physiological pH (7.4). The native peptide ligand was removed prior to docking to create an accessible binding groove. Prepared structures were then used for docking of abrocitinib, fluorouracil, and capecitabine under identical conditions, enabling direct comparison of binding affinities and interaction profiles within the Scribble PDZ1 domain.

Molecular docking

Molecular docking was performed using Molegro Virtual Docker (MVD, v6.0). The docking grid was centered on the canonical PDZ1 binding groove with a radius of 15 Å. The MolDock scoring function was applied for initial scoring, followed by rerank scoring to improve accuracy. Docking was carried out with a population size of 50, maximum iterations of 2000, and a scaling factor of 0.50, and each docking run was repeated ten times to ensure convergence and reproducibility. For validation, the co-crystallized MCC-derived C-terminal peptide present in the Scribble PDZ1 structure (PDB ID: 6MTV) was redocked into the binding groove. The predicted pose reproduced the experimental crystallographic orientation with an RMSD of < 2.0 Å, confirming the reliability of the docking protocol. To further assess scoring specificity, a small decoy set was docked, which yielded significantly weaker scores, supporting the discriminatory power of the procedure. In addition, comparative docking was performed with fluorouracil and capecitabine, two clinically approved CRC drugs, under identical conditions. This allowed direct benchmarking of abrocitinib’s binding affinity and stability against standard therapies.

Monte Carlo simulation and normal mode analysis

Monte Carlo (MC) conformational sampling was performed using YASARA Structure (v21.8.27) with the AMBER14 force field to explore the stability of the abrocitinib–Scribble PDZ1 complex22. Prior to sampling, the complex was energy-minimized to remove steric clashes. Conformational sampling was carried out at 298 K with 5000 steps per cycle, and low-energy conformations were retained based on Boltzmann weighting. Default convergence thresholds and force constants were applied. The most stable conformations obtained from MC sampling were further evaluated by Normal Mode Analysis (NMA) using the iMODS server, which applies an elastic network model to compute collective motions and flexibility. The eigenvalue corresponding to the lowest-frequency mode was used as an indicator of the complex’s structural stability, with lower eigenvalues reflecting greater conformational adaptability.

Result and discussion

Geometry analysis

The geometric parameters culminated from the optimized structure of abrocitinib (ABR) as shown in Table 1; Fig. 1 provide significant insight into the conformational behavior, electronic distribution, and possible bioactive configuration pertinent to its interaction with targets related to colorectal cancer proteins, especially complexes of the Scribble PDZ1 domain (PDB IDs: 6MTV and 6MTU)23,24. The bond lengths, angle bonds, and dihedral angles unveil the balance of structural rigidity-flexibility that can influence the molecular recognition process at the protein interface. The bond lengths C1-C2 and C1-C4 stand at 1.567 and 1.562 Å, respectively, and are within the expected range for sp²–sp² carbon–carbon single bonds, which helps to substantiate a partially conjugated backbone in that region of the molecule. The bond angles concerned, that is C2–C1–C4 at 89.880°, and C4–C1–H5 at 111.569°, suggest slight deviations from ideal tetrahedral or planar geometry, which could be due to steric interactions or intramolecular electronic effects. These differences in angles are relatively subtle, but they all add to the 3D overall orientation of the pharmacophoric grouping necessary for the binding to targets. Dihedral angles like C4–C1–C2–C3 at 9.262° and H5–C1–C4–C3 at 104.392° suggest some degree of torsional freedom with respect to the ability of this molecule to acquire bioactive conformations required for fitting into shallow or groove-like protein-binding domains like those in PDZ motifs25. The O13-O15 bond length of 1.624 Å and the associated N11-O13-O15 bond angle of 102.226° pertain to the sulfonyl functional group, an extremely polar moiety commonly involved in hydrogen bonding and electrostatics. The dihedral angle H12-N11-O13-O15 at −106.034° shows nonplanar configuration and thus might contribute to the spatial orientation of the hydrogen bond donors or acceptors in the docking of protein-ligand interactions. Analogs in the aromatic portion of the molecule include N30-C39 bond length of 1.382 Å and angle N30-N39-C34 at 115.317°, suggesting conjugation and delocalization across ring systems with the electronic stabilities and possible π-π stacking with aromatic amino acids in the Scribble PDZ1 binding site26. This geometric study indicates sufficient structural features in abrocitinib, that is, bond lengths, flexible angles, and torsional freedom, to enable a stable interaction of multi-point attachment sites with its biological targets. These attributes complement the descriptors of the electronic and MESP surface and increase the credibility of abrocitinib as a repurposable phase candidate with bioactive conformations that will impact colorectal cancer suppression by modulating the Scribble-MCC axis.

Electronic studies

Quantum descriptors

From the quantum descriptors Table 2, the ionization potential (IP) and electron affinity (EA) of ABR were found to be 3.439 eV and 1.612 eV, respectively. It indicates the ability of the molecule to donate or accept, which is highly significant for the molecular recognition and binding at the active or allosteric sites of target proteins27. Moderate IP indicates that ABR can also participate in donor interactions, while the fairly significant EA indicates that this molecule may also accept electrons, possibly leading to interactions with electron-rich residues within the PDZ domains. The HOMO and LUMO energies (− 3.439 eV and − 1.612 eV, respectively), thus, give rise to a HOMO–LUMO energy gap (Eg) of 1.827 eV, which signifies moderate electronic stability and reactivity. The field of biology specifies that moderate gap favorable dynamic interactions tend to hold neither too rigid (preventing binding) nor too reactive (producing instability or off-target effects) systems (see Fig. 2). This electronic configuration would promote an adaptively fitting mode in ABR within the PDZ binding grooves of Scribble, with or without the phosphorylated Serine at the − 1 position of MCC, as shown in structural studies. The chemical potential χ = 2.552 eV indicates that there’s a balance in donating and accepting electrons, in addition to the global hardness η = 0.913 eV and softness S = 1.095 eV. This further affirms the fact that ABR has some form of moderate polarizability. Such feature shows that it can indeed be chemically versatile-an aspect important in hydrogen or electrostatic bonds with some polar residues found within PDZ1 domains. The electrophilicity index (ω = 3.492 eV) from Table 2 shows that ABR becomes a good electrophile, as expected for the formation of stable complexes with the nucleophilic amino acids, perhaps Ser, Thr, or His residues within the protein interface28. The molecular properties, thus, fit well into the binding behavior to be required for interactions with Scribble PDZ domains, whose function in cell adhesion and polarity regulation becomes tightly modulated by protein-protein and post-translational interactions. Since your targets correspond to Scribble interactions with MCC, a known tumor suppressor that becomes frequently disrupted in colorectal cancer, the electronic and reactivity profile of ABR suggests the likelihood of creating competitive or allosteric modulation of such interactions. This is particularly meaningful since the structural literature shows that phosphorylation at the − 1 Ser position of MCC has minimal direct binding effect, implying the possibility of pharmacological modulation through small molecules like ABR without being blocked by phospho-dependent binding constraints29. Therefore, the quantum descriptors provide evidence to the hypothesis that ABR is capable of forming stable, biologically relevant interactions with the Scribble-MCC axis and possibly in disrupting oncogenic polarity signaling pathways involved in colorectal cancer progression. Further docking, molecular dynamics, and experimental validation would serve to reinforce this computational insight. It is important to note that the calculated frontier orbital energies of abrocitinib (EHOMO = − 3.439 eV, ELUMO = − 1.612 eV) yielded a HOMO–LUMO energy gap of 1.827 eV, which represents a relatively narrow band gap. Such values are typically associated with enhanced electronic softness and reactivity, properties that facilitate molecular interactions within biological systems. Comparable HOMO–LUMO gaps have been reported for several bioactive and drug-like molecules; for example, organic inhibitors and pharmacological candidates frequently exhibit band gaps in the range of 1.5–4.0 eV (Obot et al., 2015). Taken together, the calculated gap of 1.827 eV for abrocitinib falls within the lower end of the reported range for drug-like molecules, supporting its classification as a chemically reactive yet stable candidate with potential biological activity.

Natural orbital analysis

The Natural Bond Orbital (NBO) analysis provides detailed insight into intramolecular electron delocalization within abrocitinib (ABR), offering a clearer understanding of the electronic factors that support its stability, reactivity, and potential interaction with biological macromolecules such as the Scribble PDZ1 domain (PDB IDs: 6MTV and 6MTU) implicated in colorectal-cancer pathways30,31. Second-order perturbation theory highlights three key donor–acceptor interactions that together confer appreciable stabilization energy through charge transfer from filled to vacant orbitals. As shown from Table 3, the σ(N₁₁–S₁₃) → LP(1)S₁₃ transition displays an E² value of 20.04 kcal mol⁻¹, indicating strong σ-to-lone-pair delocalization in which electron density from the sulfur lone pair back-donates into the N–S bond. This back-donation not only reinforces bond stability but also spreads electron density across the heterocyclic core, a feature that could facilitate polar contacts within the hydrogen-bonding environment of the PDZ1 binding interface32,33. A comparable but slightly weaker effect is seen in the σ(S₁₃–C₁₆) → LP(1)S₁₃ interaction, with a stabilization energy of 16.63 kcal mol⁻¹; the small energy gaps (E(j)–E(i)) of 0.18–0.25 a.u. and F(i, j) values of 0.087–0.093 a.u. across these σ interactions confirm efficient donor–acceptor orbital overlap and robust electronic coupling. The most pronounced delocalization from Table 3, arises from the π(C₃₀–N₃₉) → π*(C₃₄–N₄₀) interaction, which delivers 33.70 kcal mol⁻¹ of stabilization and reflects extensive π-to-π conjugation within the aromatic system, an arrangement well suited for π–π stacking with aromatic residues such as phenylalanine, tyrosine, or tryptophan in the PDZ domain.

Together these bonding characteristics indicate a largely planar, conjugated electronic environment capable of supporting both π–π and electrostatic interactions inside the PDZ1 binding pocket. Hence, the NBO results portray ABR as electronically stable yet sufficiently reactive to engage in the non-covalent contacts predicted by the docking studies, offering a mechanistic basis for its proposed ability to modulate PDZ-mediated protein–protein interactions relevant to the maintenance of cell polarity in colorectal cancer.

Molecular electrostatic potential

Visualization of molecular electrostatic potential (MESP) isosurface in abrocitinib (ABR) as in Fig. 3 shows the spatial distribution of the electron over the surface of the molecule. This electrostatic map plays an important role in understanding how ABR interacts with its biomolecular targets, especially the PDZ1s formed by the Scribble protein in complex with MCC, which are directly implicated in the regulation of cell polarity and are often altered in colorectal cancer34. The MESP surface would be a reference for identifying cyclizable and nucleophilic regions, essential determinants for molecular recognition in protein-ligand interactions. On the concave surface of ABR (see Fig. 3), the red areas indicate regions of negative electrostatic potential. These are usually electron-rich areas, likely to form interactions with electron-deficient or positively charged amino acid residues in the PDZ domain-proteins arginine or lysine. Such areas often correspond with electronegative atoms, for example, oxygen, which understood as delocalized systems of π-electrons may form hydrogen bonding or electrostatic interactions at the protein interface. In contrast, the blue color areas indicate regions of positive electrostatic potential, or rather these are very often electron-poor and could interact with nucleophilic residues or atoms such as from the side chains of glutamate or aspartate35. The differential distribution among these charge regions over the molecule as shown in Fig. 3 would illustrate that ABR is an electrostatically balanced surface donor or acceptor potential and ideal to relate different non-covalent interactions with a protein target. Also, the fairly even establishment of polar regions within the heterocyclic core as well as its substituted aromatic ring system in ABR buttresses the possibility of having stable interactions within the PDZ1 groove. This is pertinent, especially when viewed with structural data from published reports such as 6MTV and 6MTU, which show that the binding pocket accommodates polar as well as planar residues from MCC or its phosphorylated form. Groups in ABR such as the sulfonyl and amine that contribute to the fiery red and blue regions respectively are strategically positioned for complementary interactions with the PDZ domain36. This spatial complementarity of ABR further indicates that it may act as a competitive or allosteric modulator of Scribble–MCC binding, as earlier hypothesized.

The MESP whole discouragement, therefore, not only corroborates the chemical reactivity descriptors and NBO results discussed earlier but also affirms the claim that ABR indeed has the electronic and electrostatic profile to engage functionally with the key protein interfaces involved in colorectal cancer. This visualization fulfils the broader objective of the study by proving that the molecular design and charge distribution of ABR favour inhibition or modulation of Scribble PDZ1 with MCC, with possible implications in disrupting dysregulated cell adhesion and polarity mechanisms in colorectal carcinogenesis37. To further assess the binding potential of abrocitinib with the Scribble PDZ1 domain, we combined DFT-based insights with molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulations. While electronic descriptors such as the HOMO–LUMO gap and electrophilicity index provide preliminary information on the molecule’s reactivity and likelihood of engaging in noncovalent interactions, they do not directly quantify binding affinity. Therefore, molecular docking was performed not only for abrocitinib but also for two clinically established CRC drugs, fluorouracil and capecitabine, under identical conditions to enable direct comparison. Abrocitinib exhibited stronger binding scores and more stable interaction profiles than the reference compounds, suggesting superior potential for disrupting Scribble–MCC interactions. Complementary molecular dynamics simulations were then employed to evaluate the stability and conformational flexibility of the abrocitinib–PDZ1 complex over time. Together, these approaches provide a more biologically relevant prediction of protein–ligand interactions and reinforce abrocitinib’s potential as a repurposed therapeutic candidate.

Monte Carlo studies

The molecular dynamics docking results presented in Fig. 4a reflect a comprehensive normal mode analysis (NMA) of the complex formed between the colorectal cancer-related protein Scribble PDZ1 (PDB ID: 6MTV) and the proposed JAK1 inhibitor ligand, abrocitinib (ABR). The crystal structure of the Scribble PDZ1 domain (PDB ID: 6MTV) was retrieved from the Protein Data Bank and used as the receptor for all docking and simulation studies. This structure was selected because it provides a high-resolution, experimentally validated model of the peptide-binding groove, with well-defined atomic coordinates suitable for molecular docking and conformational analysis. Importantly, the PDZ1 domain is a key polarity-regulating interface implicated in colorectal cancer progression, making it a biologically relevant target for evaluating potential modulators. To strengthen the analysis, abrocitinib was docked alongside the clinically used CRC drugs fluorouracil and capecitabine under identical conditions, allowing for direct comparison of binding affinities and interaction profiles within the same structural framework. The B-factor plot (top left) compares the predicted atomic fluctuations from the NMA (red) to the experimentally observed fluctuations from the protein structure file (black, if overlaid). The elevated peak around atom index 100 suggests that specific residues within or adjacent to the binding region experience greater flexibility upon ligand binding, a behavior consistent with dynamic interactions in the PDZ-binding cleft. This flexibility may be advantageous, as it suggests local rearrangement in the protein structure that accommodates ABR, potentially stabilizing the interaction through induced fit mechanisms. The eigenvalue plot (top right) provides insight into the overall stability and stiffness of the protein-ligand complex38. The low value of the first non-trivial eigenvalue (approximately 4.45 × 10⁻⁵) indicates a relatively soft global mode, suggesting that the complex retains flexibility, which is typical of functionally relevant biological motions. The gradual increase across modes demonstrates that the system supports a wide range of low-frequency movements, which could facilitate conformational adaptation during binding and signaling events.

This implies that ABR not only fits within the PDZ1 groove but potentially stabilizes flexible or functionally active regions of the protein. Such stabilization may disrupt endogenous PDZ–MCC interactions critical to cell polarity regulation in colorectal epithelial tissue, supporting the rationale for targeting this axis in CRC therapy39. Together, these results confirm that the ABR–6MTV complex exhibits dynamic compatibility and favorable flexibility characteristics, providing structural evidence for the viability of ABR as a repurposed small-molecule modulator of polarity signaling in colorectal cancer. The data validate the stability and adaptability of the protein–ligand complex, reinforcing the therapeutic relevance of targeting Scribble-mediated interactions.

The extended molecular dynamics analysis of the ABR–6MTV complex shown in Fig. 4b further reinforces the dynamic and structural integrity of the ligand–protein interaction through the incorporation of a covariance map and mode-specific variance plots. The covariance matrix (left panel) visually represents the correlated (red), uncorrelated (white), and anti-correlated (blue) motions between pairs of residues in the Scribble PDZ1 domain upon ligand binding. The strong red diagonal highlights self-correlated movements, as expected, but more importantly, the presence of significant red and blue off-diagonal regions indicates long-range correlated and anti-correlated motions throughout the protein. This suggests that the binding of abrocitinib induces global conformational communication across distant residues, potentially stabilizing structural domains that are functionally relevant in maintaining cell polarity or suppressing tumor progression in colorectal cancer.

The variance bar plot (top right) shows the percentage contribution of each normal mode to the overall motion of the protein. The first few modes (mode indices 1–6) account for the largest variance, which is typical of biologically relevant, low-frequency motions that govern domain rearrangements or binding-induced flexibility. This aligns with the previous observation of a low eigenvalue (~ 4.45 × 10⁻⁵), confirming that the ABR–6MTV complex undergoes flexible but energetically favorable motion—an essential property for functionally competent binding and molecular recognition. Together with the eigenvalue distribution (bottom right), which matches earlier plots, these results provide a holistic view of the dynamic landscape of the complex. The accumulation of low-frequency modes contributing significantly to conformational behavior underscores the role of ABR in not only occupying the binding pocket but also modulating the collective movements of the protein. This may impact the Scribble–MCC interaction directly or allosterically, lending support to the mechanistic rationale of using ABR as a repurposed therapeutic agent in colorectal cancer by targeting polarity-related protein complexes.

Collectively, the B-factor, deformability, covariance, and eigenvalue analyses indicate that the ABR–6MTV interaction is not rigid but rather dynamically cooperative, consistent with the biological need for flexible recognition interfaces in signaling proteins like Scribble. These dynamic insights elevate the computational findings from static docking to biologically meaningful interpretations, supporting the compound’s potential efficacy in therapeutic modulation.

Fukui function

The Fukui function plot generated for abrocitinib (ABR) using Python scripts provides a visualization of the local reactivity of the molecule by mapping the site-specific electrophilic (f⁻) and nucleophilic (f⁺) tendencies across the molecule’s atomic framework (see Fig. 5). This dual-profile analysis is intended for the identification of reactive atomic centers most likely to undergo molecular interactions, particularly in protein-ligand recognition relevant to the inhibition of colorectal cancer40–43. As shown in Fig. 5, the blue line (f⁺) in the plot stands for the nucleophilic Fukui function, which promotes ions susceptible to electrophilic attack, while the red dashed line (f⁻) highlights the electrophilic Fukui function, which indicates atoms with an increased likelihood of nucleophilic attack. The plot indicates quite reactive exuberance at especially significant atomic indices, especially at atom numbers 7, 17, and 40. The sharp peak at atomic index 17 in the f⁺ profile suggests a highly nucleophilic center that would act quite robustly at particular sites of interaction with electrophilic residues or pockets within the Scribble PDZ1 domain. This finding coincides with the known existence of nitrogen or electron-rich functional groups of ABR that may engage in hydrogen bonding or electrostatic interactions with the protein surface. Similarly, the increased values of f⁻ for the atoms situated near index 7 and also those in the range from 24 to 28 indicate possible electrophilic centers that could interact with nucleophilic residues such as serine, threonine, or side chains possessing carboxylate moieties inside the binding pocket of the protein target.

Fukui function analysis of abrocitinib (ABR) showing nucleophilic (f⁺, blue line) and electrophilic (f⁻, red dashed line) reactive centers across atomic indices. The plot was generated using Python scripts and illustrates the spatial distribution of reactivity, identifying key atomic sites involved in potential non-covalent interactions with protein targets such as the Scribble PDZ1 domain implicated in colorectal cancer.

The mileage acquired through the variation in f⁺ and f⁻ values on the molecular surface is that it will yield a diverse and widely distributed reactive profile, pointing to ABR as having several functional sites that can interact through both donor and acceptor mechanisms. This is an important aspect for biological systems where multipoint binding is generally required for very high-affinity and specific recognition. In the case of colorectal cancer, for instance, Scribble-MCC interaction blockage, these remains reactive centers could be used to create or even modify ABR derivatives for better binding to critical protein domains. Henceforth, the Fukui function plot substantiates the electronic flexibility of ABR, and also qualifies it as a potential candidate for therapeutic targeting repurposing through molecular interaction with proteins that regulate cell polarity.

Molecular docking

The molecular docking analysis, conducted using Molegro Virtual Docker (MVD), provides valuable insights into how abrocitinib (ABR) interacts with the Scribble PDZ1 domain (PDB ID: 6MTV). The crystal structure of Scribble PDZ1 was retrieved from the Protein Data Bank and preprocessed in MVD, where missing side chains and hydrogens were added, bond orders were assigned, and energy minimization was performed to relieve steric clashes. As the unresolved residues were located outside the PDZ1 binding pocket, they were not expected to influence the docking results. Among the docking solutions, Pose 0 of ABR emerged as the most favorable conformation, exhibiting a MolDock score of − 78.66 kcal·mol⁻¹ and a rerank score of − 55.69 kcal·mol⁻¹ (see Fig. 6a, b and c). The binding mode was stabilized predominantly by electrostatic interactions (E-Inter protein–ligand ≈ − 81.16 kcal·mol⁻¹) and supported by van der Waals contacts, torsional flexibility, and modest hydrogen bonding contributions (≈ − 2.5 kcal·mol⁻¹). Ligand efficiency values (LE1 = − 3.04; LE3 = − 2.53) further indicated that ABR achieves strong affinity relative to its molecular size, reinforcing its potential as a viable drug candidate.

To contextualize ABR’s performance, we performed comparative docking with two standard colorectal cancer drugs, fluorouracil and capecitabine, under identical conditions as shown in Fig. 6a, b and c. Fluorouracil achieved a total docking energy of − 72.5 kcal·mol⁻¹ with a rerank score of − 70.6 kcal·mol⁻¹, while capecitabine yielded a docking energy of − 71.0 kcal·mol⁻¹ and a rerank score of − 68.4 kcal·mol⁻¹. Although both reference drugs established hydrogen bonds and electrostatic contacts within the PDZ1 binding groove, their interaction energies were less favorable compared to abrocitinib. The comparison highlights that ABR binds more stably and with higher affinity to the Scribble PDZ1 domain, suggesting a stronger potential to disrupt Scribble–MCC interactions.

Taken together, these results strongly support the hypothesis that abrocitinib can effectively target the Scribble PDZ1 domain, with interaction energies superior to those of standard CRC drugs fluorouracil and capecitabine. This comparative analysis strengthens the translational relevance of the findings and underscores abrocitinib’s potential as a modulator of PDZ-mediated protein–protein interactions44–47.

Conclusion

This computational study explored the potential of abrocitinib (ABR), a selective JAK1 inhibitor, as a candidate for drug repurposing in colorectal cancer by evaluating its possible interaction with the Scribble PDZ1–MCC polarity complex. Through integrated quantum chemical calculations, molecular docking, and molecular dynamics–based analyses, ABR was predicted to exhibit favorable electronic characteristics, structural adaptability, and strong binding affinity for the Scribble PDZ1 domain. Comparative docking with clinically established colorectal cancer drugs, fluorouracil and capecitabine, revealed that ABR achieved more favorable interaction energies and binding stability, suggesting a superior ability to disrupt Scribble–MCC interactions. Monte Carlo–based normal mode analysis and electrostatic potential mapping further supported the dynamic stability of the ABR–protein complex at the polarity-regulating interface. While these results are based on computational modeling and will require validation in experimental systems, they provide compelling preliminary evidence that abrocitinib may serve as a promising modulator of PDZ1-mediated interactions. This framework lays the foundation for future biochemical binding studies and cellular assays to test whether abrocitinib or structurally related molecules can indeed alter polarity regulation in colorectal cancer.

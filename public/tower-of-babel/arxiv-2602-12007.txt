Density of Neumann regular smooth functions in Sobolev spaces of subanalytic manifolds Thanks: Research partially supported by the NCN grant 2021/43/B/ST1/02359.

Abstract
. We give characterizations of the bounded subanalytic 𝒞 ∞ \mathscr{C}^{\infty} submanifolds M M of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} for which the space of Neumann regular functions is dense in Sobolev spaces. By “Neumann regular function”, we mean a function which is smooth at almost every boundary point and whose gradient is tangent to the boundary. In the case p ∈ [ 1 , 2 ] p\in[1,2] , we prove that the Neumann regular elements of 𝒞 ∞ ​ ( M ¯ ) \mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\overline{M}) are dense in W 1 , p ​ ( M ) W^{1,p}(M) if and only if M M is connected at almost every boundary point. In the case p p large, we show that the Neumann regular Lipschitz elements of 𝒞 ∞ ​ ( M ) \mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M) are dense in W 1 , p ​ ( M ) W^{1,p}(M) if and only if M M is connected at every boundary point. The proof involves the construction of Lipschitz Neumann regular partitions of unity, which is of independent interest.

Introduction

Recent works on Sobolev spaces of subanalytic domains and manifolds
[
aVgV3
,
aVgV2
,
aVgV4
,
gV1
]
point out that, in spite of the fact that these sets may admit non metrically conical singularities, they constitute a nice category to investigate partial differential equations. Let us recall that the subanalytic category comprises all the sets defined by equalities and inequalities on analytic functions (see section
1.1
), offering a level of generality which is valuable for applications.

Applications of the aforementioned works to PDEs were given in
[
gV2
]
where it was shown that the classical weak formulations of the Laplace equation under Dirichlet or Neumann (or mixed) condition go over the framework of subanalytic domains, not necessarily Lipschitz. It is sometimes useful to give weak formulations only involving Neumann regular functions, which requires to show the density of these functions in
W^{1,2}({\Omega})
, for a given domain
{\Omega}
. This fact, which was established on smooth domains and on Lipschitz simplicial complexes
[
D
]
, is not true on any Lipschitz domain (see
[
D
]
). Theorem
3.1
of this article entails that this density holds on all subanalytic Lipschitz bounded domains, and more generally, given a subanalytic bounded
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
submanifold
M
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

Corollary 0.1
.

For
p\in[1,2]
, the space

\{u\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}({\overline{M}}):u\mbox{ is Neumann regular}\}

is dense in
W^{1,p}(M)
if and only if
M
is connected at
\mathcal{H}^{m-1}
almost every boundary point.

By “connected at a boundary point
{x_{0}}
”, we mean that
\mathbf{B}({x_{0}},\varepsilon)\cap M
is connected for
\varepsilon>0
small. Since we put no regularity assumption except subanalicity, this set may consist of more than one (but finitely many) connected components, like for instance if
M=\{(x,y,z)\in(0,1)^{3}:x^{2}+y^{2}<z^{2}\}
and
{x_{0}}=(0,0,0)
. Lipschitz domains are clearly connected at every boundary point.
Theorem
3.1
provides a more general result, showing the density for all
p
not greater than the codimension of the singularities of the frontier (which is always at least
2
). In particular, when the boundary of a domain of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
has isolated singularities, this density holds up to
p=3
. This theorem also yields the density of Neumann regular smooth functions vanishing near a subset
Z
of the boundary in the space of functions having trace
0
on
Z
, generalizing other results of
[
D
]
that are useful to handle weak formulations of PDEs under mixed Dirichlet-Neumann conditions.

Example
2.2
shows that these results cannot be generalized to all
p
, as density of smooth functions itself fails. In the case “
p
large”, where the density of
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}({\overline{M}})
holds (Theorem
2.3
, again up to connectedness at frontier points),
we establish the density of Lipschitz
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
Neumann regular functions (Theorem
3.3
). We construct for this purpose Lipschitz Neumann regular partitions of unity (Proposition
3.5
), which is of its own interest as it can be
useful to localize weak formulations of PDEs.
We give a counterexample to show that
\mathscr{C}^{2}({\overline{M}})
Neumann regular functions may fail to be dense in
W^{1,p}(M)
for arbitrarily large values of

even if
M
is a Lipschitz subanalytic domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This example also shows that the Neumann regular partitions of unity given by Proposition

\mathscr{C}^{2}
functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition 1.1

A subset

The flaw of the semi-analytic category is that it is not preserved by analytic morphisms, even when they are proper. To overcome this problem, we prefer working with the subanalytic sets.

A subset

2.

\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

It was shown in

Let

Given an open subset

3.1.
The case

We start with a density theorem that covers the case

Theorem 3.1
.

Let

\partial M
and let

be a definable subset of
\delta M
containing
\delta M\setminus\Gamma
and
\delta Z

, the space

\{u\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{{\overline{M}}\setminus(Z\cup\overline{A})}({\overline{M}}):u\mbox{ is Neumann regular}\}

is dense in

if and only if

Proof.

Assume first that

{x_{0}}
is therefore the union of (at least two)

, it cannot be approximated in

(for any

(which would have the same trace) by the usual trace theorem (for smooth manifolds with boundary).

To prove the converse, assume that

\Gamma\setminus(Z\cup\overline{A})
, which entails that it is locally the interior of a smooth manifold with boundary at every point of
\Gamma\setminus(Z\cup\overline{A})

. Let

be a sufficiently small definable neighborhood of

By Theorem

This function is

and vanishes near

. Moreover, for every

at

which means that

is Neumann regular. We now need to extend

to a neighborhood of
\delta M
.

We claim that for any sufficiently small positive real number

is zero on

V_{\varepsilon}:=\{x\in U_{\Gamma}^{\varepsilon}:d(x,\delta M\setminus\Gamma)<\varepsilon\}

(see (

Indeed, for

As
u
is zero near
Z\cup\overline{A}\supset\delta M\setminus\Gamma
, it must vanish on
\{x\in U_{\Gamma}:d(x,\delta M\setminus\Gamma)<2\varepsilon\}
for
\varepsilon>0
small, which, by the above inequality, means that
v
is zero on
V_{\varepsilon}
for such
\varepsilon
, as claimed.

We thus now may smoothly extend
v
to

U^{\varepsilon}_{\Gamma}\cup\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:d(x,\delta M\setminus\Gamma)<\varepsilon\}

by setting
\tilde{v}(x):=v(x)
on
U_{\Gamma}^{\varepsilon}
and
\tilde{v}(x):=0
if
d(x,\delta M\setminus\Gamma)<\varepsilon
. Note that the just above displayed set is a neighborhood of
\delta M
. Let
\lambda
be a smooth compactly supported function on this neighborhood, equal to
1
on a smaller neighborhood of
\delta M
, and set

w:=\lambda\cdot\tilde{v}.

Because
w=v
near
\Gamma
, this function is Neumann regular. It therefore suffices to approximate
(u-w)
by Neumann regular functions (the sum of two Neumann regular functions is clearly Neumann regular).
But since
(u-w)
has trace zero and
A
contains
\delta M\setminus\partial M
, Theorem
2.1
ensures that this function can be approximated for each
p\in[1,m-\dim A]
in the
W^{1,p}(M)
norm by elements of
\mathscr{C}_{0}^{\infty}(M)
.
∎

We gave a fairly general statement, the most interesting case is when
p=2
, where we get the density of Neumann regular smooth functions as soon as

is connected at
\mathcal{H}^{m-1}
almost every point of
\delta M
(Corollary
0.1
). This is obtained by applying the above theorem with
A=\delta M\setminus\Gamma
, which has codimension at least
2
in
{\overline{M}}
.

Remark 3.2
.

When outward cusps arise, the trace of an element of
W^{1,p}(M)
may fail to be
L^{p}
, for

small. If

is a function that has an

trace, it is possible to see that the smooth approximations of

provided by the proof of Theorem

can be required to have a trace tending to the trace of

in the

norm. This is because the proof of Theorem

indeed gives such approximations.

3.2.
The case

large.

Example
2.2
shows that the conclusion of Theorem
3.1
does not hold for all
p
. We establish in this section the density of
Lipschitz
Neumann regular functions (smooth on

) in the case “

sufficiently large”, i.e. for

bigger than some
p_{0}
(which depends on

), when

is connected at boundary points. We then provide an example to show that the density of
\mathscr{C}^{2}({\overline{M}})
Neumann regular functions may fail for arbitrary large values of

(Example

).

Theorem 3.3
.

For every sufficiently large value of
p
, the space

\{u\in\mathscr{C}^{0,1}({\overline{M}})\cap\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M\cup\Gamma):u\mbox{ is Neumann regular}\}

is dense in
W^{1,p}(M)
if and only if
M
is normal.

The proof requires some material. We first prove a lemma that gives a Lipschitz tubular neighborhood near a given point
{x_{0}}\in\delta\Gamma
and then construct Neumann regular partitions of unity. This tubular neighborhood will be used to produce our partitions of unity as well as to prove Theorem
3.3
.

We recall that a
tubular neighborhood
of a manifold
S
is a triple
(U,\pi,\rho)
, with
U
neighborhood of
S
,
\pi:U\to S
retraction, and
\rho:U\to\mathbb{R}
nonnegative function satisfying
\rho^{-1}(0)=S
. We say that a tubular neighborhood is
\mathscr{C}^{k}
or Lipschitz on a set if so are both
\pi
and
\rho
on this set.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
S\subset\delta\Gamma
be a
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
definable submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For each
x_{0}\in S
there is a Lipschitz tubular neighborhood
(U_{{x_{0}}},\pi,\rho)
of
S\cap U_{x_{0}}
which is
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
on
U_{x_{0}}\setminus\delta\Gamma
and satisfies near
\Gamma
:

(3.1)

\rho\circ\pi_{\Gamma}=\rho\quad\mbox{and}\quad\pi\circ\pi_{\Gamma}=\pi.

Proof.

Let
U_{\Gamma}
be a sufficiently small definable neighborhood of
\Gamma
for the closest point retraction
\pi_{\Gamma}
(onto
\Gamma
) to be smooth. Taking
U_{\Gamma}
smaller if necessary, we may assume
|D_{x}\pi_{\Gamma}|
to be bounded on
U_{\Gamma}
(it is bounded on
\Gamma
in virtue of (
1.2
)). Let for
x\in\Gamma

\mu(x):=\min(\frac{1}{2}d(x,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus U_{\Gamma}),d(x,\delta\Gamma)).

Since
\mu(x)<d(x,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus U_{\Gamma})
, the topological frontier of
U^{\mu}_{\Gamma}
in
U_{\Gamma}
(see (
1.1
) for
U^{\mu}_{\Gamma}
) coincides with
\{x\in U_{\Gamma}:d(x,\Gamma)=\mu(\pi_{\Gamma}(x))\}
. Choose a positive
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
function
\sigma<\mu
on
\Gamma
with bounded first derivative (if we choose an exhaustive sequence
(K_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of compact subsets of
\Gamma
, a smooth partition of unity
\varphi_{i}
subordinate to
int(K_{i+2})\setminus K_{i}
, and a sequence of positive numbers
(\varepsilon_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, the function
\sigma:=\sum\varepsilon_{i}\varphi_{i}
will have the required properties if
(\varepsilon_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is decreasing sufficiently fast).

Set for
x\in U_{\Gamma}^{\mu}
close to
{x_{0}}

\alpha(x):=\psi\left(\frac{\rho_{\Gamma}(x)}{\sigma(\pi_{\Gamma}(x))^{2}}\right),

where
\psi
is as in (
1.3
).
Notice that on the support of
\psi^{\prime}(\frac{\rho_{\Gamma}(x)}{\sigma(\pi_{\Gamma}(x))^{2}})
, we have

(3.2)

\rho_{\Gamma}(x)<\sigma(\pi_{\Gamma}(x))^{2},

which entails
|D_{x}\rho_{\Gamma}|\overset{(\ref{eq_ker_dpi_nabla_drho})}{\leq}2\sigma(\pi_{\Gamma}(x))
on this set, and therefore:

(3.3)

|D_{x}\alpha|=|\psi^{\prime}(\frac{\rho_{\Gamma}(x)}{\sigma(\pi_{\Gamma}(x))^{2}})||\frac{D_{x}\rho_{\Gamma}}{\sigma(\pi_{\Gamma}(x))^{2}}-\frac{2\rho_{\Gamma}(x)D_{\pi_{\Gamma}(x)}\sigma\cdot D_{x}\pi_{\Gamma}}{\sigma(\pi_{\Gamma}(x))^{3}}|\lesssim\frac{1}{\sigma(\pi_{\Gamma}(x))}.

We now can set for
x\in U_{\Gamma}^{\mu}
close to
{x_{0}}
:

\rho(x):=\alpha(x)\rho_{S}(\pi_{\Gamma}(x))+(1-\alpha(x))\rho_{S}(x).

As
\rho_{\Gamma}(x)=\mu(\pi_{\Gamma}(x))^{2}
on the topological frontier of
U^{\mu}_{\Gamma}
in
U_{\Gamma}
and since
\sigma<\mu
, the function
\alpha
vanishes near the topological frontier of
U^{\mu}_{\Gamma}
in
U_{\Gamma}
, which means that

\rho:=\rho_{S}
extends
continuously
\rho
to a neighborhood of
{x_{0}}
(since
\mu(x)\leq d(x,\delta\Gamma)
,
\rho_{S}(\pi_{\Gamma}(x))
tends to
\rho_{S}(x)
as
x\in U_{\Gamma}^{\mu}
tends to
\delta\Gamma
), smoothly at every point of
M\cup\Gamma
.
Since
\alpha\equiv 1
near
\Gamma
, the condition
\rho\circ\pi_{\Gamma}=\rho
holds by construction.
Moreover, as for
x
close to
{x_{0}}
and
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have

D_{x}\rho(w)=\alpha(x)D_{\pi_{\Gamma}(x)}\rho_{S}D_{x}\pi_{\Gamma}(w)+(1-\alpha(x))D_{x}\rho_{S}(w)+(\rho_{S}(\pi_{\Gamma}(x))-\rho_{S}(x))D_{x}\alpha(w),

and since

|\rho_{S}(\pi_{\Gamma}(x))-\rho_{S}(x)|\leq|x-\pi_{\Gamma}(x)|=\rho_{S}(x)^{1/2}\overset{(\ref{eq_rhosigma})}{<}\sigma(x),

(
3.3
) entails that
|D_{x}\rho|
is bounded near
{x_{0}}
on
U_{\Gamma}^{\mu}
. On
\mathbf{B}({x_{0}},\varepsilon)\setminus U_{\Gamma}^{\mu}
,
\varepsilon>0
small, as
\rho=\rho_{S}
,
|D_{x}\rho|
is also bounded on the complement of
\delta\Gamma
. Hence,
|D_{x}\rho|
is bounded on
\mathbf{B}({x_{0}},\varepsilon)\setminus\delta\Gamma
.

Because
\delta\Gamma
is subanalytic and has empty interior, almost every segment of line cuts this set at finitely many points (possibly none). Since the restriction of
\rho
to every such segment has bounded derivative on the complement of a finite set, and since
\rho
is continuous, we see that
\rho
is
L
-Lipschitz near
{x_{0}}
with
L:=\sup\{|D_{x}\rho|:x\in\mathbf{B}({x_{0}},\varepsilon)\setminus\delta\Gamma\}
,
\varepsilon>0
small.

To construct
\pi
, we will argue up to a local coordinate system
\phi:U_{x_{0}}\to U_{0}
of
S
at
{x_{0}}
(we recall that the strata are
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
), where
U_{0}
(resp.
U_{x_{0}}
) is a neighborhood of the origin (resp. of
{x_{0}}
) in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We will thus identify
{x_{0}}
with
0
,
S
with a neighborhood of the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{n-k}}\}
, where

,

and
\Gamma
with their respective images under
\phi
, and
\pi_{\Gamma}
with
\phi\circ\pi_{\Gamma}\circ\phi^{-1}
.
Set then for
x\in U^{\mu}_{\Gamma}
, with
\mu
as above,

\pi(x):=\alpha(x)\pi_{k}(\pi_{\Gamma}(x))+(1-\alpha(x))\pi_{k}(x),

where
\alpha
is as above and
\pi_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{n-k}}\}
is the orthogonal projection.
Setting
\pi(x)=\pi_{k}(x)
for
x\in U_{0}\setminus U_{\Gamma}^{\mu}
extends continuously
\pi
, smoothly on the complement of
\delta\Gamma
, and since
\alpha\equiv 1
near

, we clearly have
\pi\circ\pi_{\Gamma}=\pi
near
\Gamma
.
Moreover, since for

and

close to

, we have

D_{x}\pi(w)=\alpha(x)D_{\pi_{\Gamma}(x)}\pi_{k}D_{x}\pi_{\Gamma}(w)+(1-\alpha(x))D_{x}\pi_{k}(w)+D_{x}\alpha(w)\cdot(\pi_{k}(\pi_{\Gamma}(x))-\pi_{k}(x)),

and because, by definition of

and

, we have

|\pi_{k}(\pi_{\Gamma}(x))-\pi_{k}(x)|\leq|\pi_{\Gamma}(x)-x|\lesssim\sqrt{\rho_{\Gamma}(x)},

we see that (

) and (

) entail that
|D_{x}\pi|
is bounded near

. Since

is continuous, smooth on the complement of
\delta\Gamma
in some neighborhood of

, and has bounded derivative, the same argument as for

shows that it is a Lipschitz mapping.
∎

We now are going to construct partitions of unity by Neumann regular functions, which can also be useful for other applications. Like the above tubular neighborhood, these partitions of unity will just be Lipschitz, but Example
3.6
shows that it is not possible to get something much better.

Proposition 3.5
.

Given a finite covering of
{\overline{M}}
by open sets, there is a
\mathscr{C}^{0,1}({\overline{M}})
partition of unity subordinate to this covering,
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
on
M\cup\Gamma
, and which is exclusively constituted by Neumann regular functions.

Proof.

It suffices to construct Lipschitz Neumann regular bump functions, i.e. to prove that every
{x_{0}}\in{\overline{M}}
has a fundamental system of neighborhoods on each of which we can find a Neumann regular compactly supported Lipschitz function
\theta_{x_{0}}
(
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
on
M\cup\Gamma
) that satisfies
\theta_{x_{0}}\equiv 1
in the vicinity of
{x_{0}}
. Fix
{x_{0}}\in\overline{\Gamma}
(at points of
{\overline{M}}\setminus\overline{\Gamma}
the statement is obvious) and a number
\nu>0
.

First Case:

{x_{0}}\in\delta\Gamma
. Let
(U_{x_{0}},\pi,\rho)
denote the (local) Lipschitz tubular neighborhood provided by Lemma
3.4
, applied with
S=\{{x_{0}}\}
. Set

\theta_{x_{0}}(x):=\psi_{\nu}(\rho(x)),

where
\psi_{\nu}(t):=\psi(\frac{t}{\nu})
,
\psi
being as in (
1.3
). The function
\theta_{x_{0}}
is Lipschitz (resp.
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
on
M\cup\Gamma
) as a composite of Lipschitz (resp.
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
) functions. It is
1
on a neighborhood of
{x_{0}}
and is supported in
\{x\in U_{x_{0}}:\rho(x)\leq\frac{3}{4}\nu\}
.
Furthermore, for each
x\in\Gamma\cap U_{x_{0}}
we have for every vector
{\mathbf{n}}
normal at

D_{x}\theta_{x_{0}}({\mathbf{n}})=\psi_{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho(x))D_{x}\rho({\mathbf{n}})\overset{(\ref{eq_comp_cond})}{=}\psi_{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho(x))D_{x}\rho(D_{x}\pi_{\Gamma}({\mathbf{n}}))\overset{(\ref{eq_tubular})}{=}0.

Second case:

{x_{0}}\in\Gamma
. In this case, define a
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
function by

\theta_{x_{0}}(x):=\psi_{\nu}(\eta(x))\cdot\psi_{\nu}(\rho_{\Gamma}(x)),

where
\eta(x):=|\pi_{\Gamma}(x)-{x_{0}}|^{2}
and
\psi_{\nu}
is as above. A computation of derivative yields for every vector
{\mathbf{n}}
normal at

D_{x}\theta_{x_{0}}({\mathbf{n}})=\psi_{\nu}(\rho_{\Gamma}(x))\psi_{\nu}^{\prime}(\eta(x))D_{x}\eta({\mathbf{n}})+\psi_{\nu}(\eta(x))\psi_{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho_{\Gamma}(x))D_{x}\rho_{\Gamma}({\mathbf{n}}),

and since we have

D_{x}\eta({\mathbf{n}})=2(\pi_{\Gamma}(x)-{x_{0}})\cdot D_{x}\pi_{\Gamma}({\mathbf{n}})\overset{(\ref{eq_tubular})}{=}0

and
\psi^{\prime}_{\nu}(0)=0
,
we see that the Neumann condition holds.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Assume that
M
is not connected at a point
{x_{0}}\in\delta M
and denote by
U
a connected component of
M\cap\mathbf{B}({x_{0}},\varepsilon)
,
\varepsilon>0
small. Take a function which coincides with a compactly supported
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
bump function on
U
and which is zero on the other connected components of
M\cap\mathbf{B}({x_{0}},\varepsilon)
. Such a function belongs to
W^{1,\infty}(M)
but does not extend continuously at
{x_{0}}
, and hence cannot be approximated by Lipschitz functions, by
[
aVgV4
, Corollary
3.7
]
(applied with
A:=\{{x_{0}}\}
, see the proof of
[
aVgV4
, Corollary
3.9
]
for more, the argument is completely analogous). Hence, the space of Lipschitz functions fails to be dense in
W^{1,p}(M)
for arbitrarily large values of

if
M
is not connected at every boundary point.

To prove the converse, suppose that
M
is normal.
Let
\Sigma
be a stratification of
\delta M
satisfying the frontier condition and compatible with
\Gamma
(i.e.
\Gamma
is a union of strata). The idea of the proof is to first approximate the given function near the strata of dimension
0
and next approximate the upshot successively near the strata of higher dimension. We therefore naturally introduce the following function
\kappa
on which our induction will rely.

Given
j\leq m-1
, we denote by
X_{j}
the union of all the strata of

\Sigma
of dimension less than or equal to
j
, with
X_{-1}=\emptyset
. Given a function
u
on an open subset
V
of
{\overline{M}}
, we set

\kappa(u):=\max\{k\leq m:u\mbox{ is Neumann regular on a neighborhood of }X_{k-1}\cap V\mbox{ in }{\overline{M}}\},

and we will prove by decreasing induction on
k\leq m
the following statements:

\mathbf{(H_{k})}
For
p
sufficiently large, given
u\in W^{1,p}(M)
satisfying
\kappa(u)\geq k
, there is a sequence of Lipschitz functions
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M\cup\Gamma)
tending to
u
in
W^{1,p}(M)
and exclusively constituted by Neumann regular functions.

The theorem follows from
\mathbf{(H_{0})}
. Let us first check
\mathbf{(H_{m})}
. Given
u
such that
\kappa(u)=m
, there is a neighborhood
W
of
\delta M
on which
u
is Neumann regular. Moreover, as well-known,
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M)\cap W^{1,p}(M)
is dense in
W^{1,p}(M)
. It then suffices to glue
u_{|W}
with the terms of a sequence of smooth approximations of
u_{|M}
by means of a partition of unity which is constant on some neighborhood of
\delta M
.

We now are going to prove
\mathbf{(H_{k})}
, for
k\leq m-1
, assuming
\mathbf{(H_{k+1})}
. Fix
u\in W^{1,p}(M)
satisfying
\kappa(u)=k
. Note that if two functions
v
and
w
are Neumann regular in the vicinity of a point
x
, so are
vw
and
v+w
. As a matter of fact, since Proposition
3.5
provides us Neumann regular partitions of unity, the induction step reduces to prove the following local fact for
p
large enough:

Claim.
Every
{x_{0}}\in\delta M
has an open neighborhood
V_{x_{0}}
in
{\overline{M}}
for which there is a sequence
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathscr{C}^{0,1}(V_{x_{0}})
tending to
u
in
W^{1,p}(V_{x_{0}}\cap M)
and satisfying
\kappa(v_{j})>k
for all

.

Fix
{x_{0}}\in\delta M
and denote by
S
the stratum of
\Sigma
that contains
{x_{0}}
. If

, let
(U_{x_{0}},\pi,\rho)
be a local tubular neighborhood of

as provided by Lemma

. If

, we just let
\pi:=\pi_{\Gamma}
and define
U_{x_{0}}
to be a small ball centered at
{x_{0}}
. Thanks to the frontier condition, choosing
U_{x_{0}}
smaller if necessary, we can require that

is the only stratum of dimension less than or equal to
\dim S
met by

.

If
\dim S<k
then
{x_{0}}\in X_{k-1}
, which, since

, means that
{x_{0}}
has a neighborhood

on which
u
is Neumann regular. Hence, we may set in this case
v_{j}:=u_{|V_{x_{0}}}
for all

.

Assume thus that
\dim S\geq k
. Since
M
is normal,
by Theorem
2.3
, for
p
large enough there is a sequence
g_{j}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}({\overline{M}})
such that for each integer

||u-g_{j}||_{W^{1,p}(M)}<\frac{1}{j}.

Let
\lambda
be a
\mathscr{C}_{0}^{\infty}

of

which yields

and this obviously continues to hold. We conclude that

We now provide a couple of examples that shows that neither the partition of unity given by Proposition

nor the approximations given by Theorem

could be required to be
\mathscr{C}^{2}
at singular points of

.

Example 3.6
.

Let

{\Omega}:=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:xy(y+x)(y-zx)>0,0<z<1\}.

For every
z_{0}\in(0,1)
, the boundary of

{\Omega}_{z_{0}}:={\Omega}\cap\{z=z_{0}\}

consists of four lines

x=0
,
y=0
,
y=-x
,
y=z_{0}x
. These families of lines constitute surfaces that are smooth outside the
z
-axis. We thus can stratify
\overline{{\Omega}}
by
S:=\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\}\times(0,1)
, the complement of the
z
-axis in these surfaces, and
{\Omega}
. This example is unbounded but our problem will be local and we could cut it with a little ball near a point
(0,0,z_{0}),z_{0}\in(0,1)
.

We claim that every
u\in\mathscr{C}^{2}(\overline{{\Omega}})
satisfying Neumann’s condition at the smooth points of
\delta{\Omega}
is constant on
S
. Assume otherwise, i.e. assume that
\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}(0,0,z_{0})\neq 0
for some
z_{0}\in(0,1)
, for such a function
u
. Set for
(x,y,z)\in{\Omega}\cap\mathbf{B}((0,0,z_{0}),\mu),

\mu
being a positive number sufficiently small for
\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}
to be nonzero on this ball,

\eta(x,y,z):=\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}(x,y,z)\right)^{-1}\cdot\nabla u(x,y,z).

As
u
satisfies Neumann’s condition,
\eta
is tangent to
\delta{\Omega}
at every smooth point. It is easily checked that by continuity it must be tangent to all the strata. This vector field therefore generates a
\mathscr{C}^{1}
local flow
(q,t)\mapsto\phi_{t}(q)
, for
q=(x,y,z)
close to
(0,0,z_{0})
, that preserves
\delta{\Omega}
.

Denote by
P
the canonical projection onto the
z
-axis. Since
P(\eta)\equiv(0,0,1)
, we must have
P(\phi_{t}(x,y,z))=z+t
, which means that
\phi_{t}
induces a diffeomorphism between the respective germs of
{\Omega}_{z_{0}}
and
{\Omega}_{z_{0}+t}
at the origin. It is well-known that this is impossible. The derivative at the origin of such a diffeomorphism would respectively send the four lines
x=0
,
y=0
,
y=-x
,
y=z_{0}x
onto the lines
x=0
,
y=0
,
y=-x
,
y=(z_{0}+t)x
. The vectors generating the first three lines would be eigenvectors of the restriction of
D_{(0,0,z_{0})}\phi_{t}

Remark 3.7
.

(1)

Theorems
3.1
and
3.3
provide density results for Neumann regular functions under connectedness assumptions at boundary points that are proved to be necessary. If we drop these assumptions, the proofs yield the density of
W^{1,\infty}
functions satisfying Neumann’s condition. Elements of
W^{1,\infty}
are not smooth, they are only Lipschitz with respect to the inner metric (given by the lengths of paths), and can have several limit values at a boundary point at which
M
has several connected components.

(2)

The partition of unity given by Proposition
3.5
is not subanalytic. If we demand it to be subanalytic, it is no longer possible to require it to be
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
at points of
\Gamma
. We however can construct it
\mathscr{C}^{k}
, with
k
arbitrary large. The reason is that
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
subanalytic functions being analytic, they must be zero on the whole of the connected component as soon as they vanish on a neighborhood of a point.

(3)

The results of this article are indeed valid if
M
is unbounded since we could use cut-off functions to reduce the problem to a function with bounded support.

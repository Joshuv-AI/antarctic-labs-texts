Intelligent hybrid decision support systems for education policy and institutional performance optimization
Authors: Qiao Zeng, Haoping Zhang, Caixia Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe rapid evolution of digital technologies has accelerated the transition toward Education 5.0, where intelligent systems support advanced learning environments and data-driven institutional management. However, selecting the most effective educational technologies remains a complex decision-making problem due to uncertainty, conflicting criteria, and the involvement of multiple stakeholders. To address this challenge, this study proposes an intelligent hybrid decision-support framework based on picture fuzzy sets to manage uncertainty and hesitation in expert evaluations. The model integrates a mutual induction-based feature selection mechanism to identify the most influential criteria and determine their relative importance by capturing interrelationships among features. A novel distance and similarity measure for picture fuzzy information is also introduced to improve discrimination and accuracy in the evaluation process. The optimized criteria and weights are incorporated into a ranking procedure based on PROMETHEE II to evaluate and prioritize emerging Education 5.0 technologies. A real-world case study inspired by China’s smart education transformation is conducted, assessing ten advanced educational technologies, including artificial intelligence learning systems, learning analytics platforms, and immersive learning environments. Furthermore, Monte Carlo simulation is applied to assess uncertainty propagation and stochastic behavior, while robustness is evaluated by comparing the results with traditional methods such as TOPSIS, VIKOR, MOORA, MABAC, WASPAS, EDAS, and ARAS. The results demonstrate the superior stability and reliability of the proposed framework, providing actionable guidance for policymakers and educational institutions in strategic technology adoption.Keywords: Decision support system, Uncertainty, Aggregation operators, PROMETHEE II, Performance optimization, Fuzzy logic, Simulation, Similarity measureSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Intelligent decision-support systems can help organizations provide timely data-driven support on how to effectively allocate resources, how to design, measure and monitor interventions or policies to address inequities in education and optimize institutional performance1,2. Data driven intelligent decision-support systems can also enable institutions to identify the most important factors impacting student outcomes as well as create strategies that enhance both the organizational effectiveness and learner success3,4. The use of intelligent decision-support systems enables evidence based education policy development; they allow administrators to identify areas where students may be experiencing performance gaps, assess whether current and/or planned interventions/policies will have an impact on student outcomes and continually develop strategies to improve student outcomes throughout the educational system. The education 5.0 is a new generation in the digitalization of education, it provides for a seamless integration of advanced technologies into both the teaching/learning processes and human interactions5,6. Education 5.0 is an extension of smart education and conventional e-learning that includes artificial intelligence (AI) and explainable machine learning (ML), digital twins of learners and educators, Internet-of-Things (IoT)-based smart classroom, Augmented Reality (AR) and Virtual Reality (VR), as well as Edge–Cloud Computing (ECC) infrastructures to create a digital twin environment5,6. Education 5.0 builds on the Industry 5.0 paradigm by emphasizing Human-AI collaboration over pure automation to promote creative thinking, ethical intelligence, emotional awareness and learner-centered learning7,8. The intelligent educational systems help to improve the pedagogical skills of the teachers and to develop the cognitive and metacognitive abilities of the students, and to provide trusted, transparent and adaptive decisions that are beyond the limits of the traditional human decision making process7,8. Additionally, Education 5.0 promotes participatory learning environments in which learners take part in the knowledge generation, collaborate with each other in the interdisciplinary projects and reflect upon their own learning processes in order to improve their academic and socio-emotional competence.

Traditional evaluation tools such as student grades or attendance records do not represent the constantly changing, multi-dimensional, and contextual aspects of education. These can delay a needed intervention and limit the full possibilities of flexible, personalized and sustainable learning environments5,8 . As such, it is now essential to develop advanced analytical frameworks including MCDM models, picture fuzzy sets, and feature selection techniques in order to evaluate different types of educational technologies and initiatives under the conditions of uncertainty and conflicting priorities7,8. While educational institutions produce large volumes of complex, heterogeneous and evolving data relating to learner interaction, behaviors, biometric and cognitive signals, collaborative activity and academic outcomes in physical and virtual learning environments7,8, this data also presents many challenges when considering its use for real-time monitoring, identifying students who are at risk, and providing personalized interventions. Equity and Resource Allocation continues as a major concern within Education Policy. The persistent gaps in school funding, teacher distribution, and access to technology continue to have a negative impact on marginalized communities, low-income families, and under-resourced institutions9,10. The above inequities have an effect on academic outcomes, social mobility, and long-term economic growth. Systematic and data-driven approaches to optimize policy and institutional performance will be needed to ensure that resources and interventions are effectively distributed to meet diverse student needs11,12. Predictive analytics and intelligent monitoring systems provide decision-makers with the information needed to identify underserved populations; prioritize critical interventions, track the impact of policies over time; and support sustainable improvements in institutional performance and student success.

Additionally, Education 5.0 has a focus on developing ethical, socially responsible, and sustainable processes for learning. Systems that use AI are being developed to enhance learning as well as increase inclusion, social responsibility, and the quality of life of individuals5,6. These systems may be used to develop adaptive learning paths that accommodate the cognitive abilities, emotional needs, and learning preferences of each student while maintaining fairness and transparency in how assessments and resources are allocated. With this type of integration of systems within the framework of policy, educational institutions will have the ability to assess and meet the future demand for education, align their resources with their overall strategy, and provide accountability to all of its stakeholders (students, teachers, and society). The synergy of new technologies in education, data analysis and decision making based upon data analysis, and the application of decision-making processes by educational institutions, offers an extraordinary opportunity for both the improvement of performance of the institution and the improvement of the success of students. Developing systematic and equitable frameworks that incorporate intelligence is necessary to maximize the potential of Education 5.0, enabling educational institutions to offer unique, sustainable, and high-quality education while reducing structural barriers and providing equal opportunities for inclusive and lifelong learning. To better handle all the mentioned challenges, this paper introduces an intelligent hybrid decision-support system, which integrates picture fuzzy sets, mutual induction for feature selection, novel distance and similarity measures, and PROMETHEE II for the multi-attribute ranking. The proposed framework can capture the uncertainties in the decision process and model the interrelations between evaluation criteria simultaneously by using Monte Carlo simulation to evaluate the robustness of ranking results; therefore, it provides a reliable, data-driven tool for policymakers and administrative personnel to make decisions strategically. This study will utilize the described method to provide the best-performing Education 5.0 technologies that are capable of improving institutional performance as well as support the current educational policy in order to contribute to both the theoretical and practical application of fuzzy MCDM in educational areas.

Literature review

In 1965, Zadeh initially proposed fuzzy sets to define and address the ambiguity present in our everyday lives13. Fuzzy set theory provides a flexible framework to define those situations with uncertain or vague data. With fuzzy sets, one can assign a degree of membership to each element in a set, thereby addressing the ambiguous data. An individual may believe that an object x is partially a member of a set A; yet be unsure. Thus, the degree of membership of x in set A is ambiguous. Fuzzy sets were instrumental in solving many problems in applied mathematics, information science, and decision-making. Atanassov further developed the original idea of fuzzy sets by developing intuitionistic fuzzy sets (IFSs). IFSs include an additional parameter called non-membership (N-MS), in addition to the traditional membership degrees used in fuzzy sets. IFSs provide a method to represent more complex ambiguity and uncertainty in sets than does fuzzy sets. IFSs have become very popular for use in areas requiring sophisticated techniques to handle uncertainty, including pattern recognition, decision support systems, medical diagnostics, and control systems. Research by Atanassov has established a foundation for future research into IFSs and has enabled researchers to develop new methods of representing uncertainty and new operations defined upon IFSs14,15.

Cuong16 created PiFS that offers an approach to evaluate both positive and negative criteria with a neutral assessment, when multiple criteria are evaluated in a single evaluation process. PiFS differs from typical fuzzy sets in that it can evaluate a criterion from 3 different angles. In addition to influencing the use of fuzzy sets in multiple criteria decision-making, fuzzy sets have been an influence in both pattern recognition and cluster identification over the last decade or so. Among those fuzzy sets that have become very popular is the picture fuzzy set (PFS), as a method to model uncertainty, hesitancy, and ambiguity in a decision environment. PFSs expand upon both fuzzy and intuitionistic fuzzy sets through the use of positive, neutral, and negative membership degrees, and therefore provide a more complete way to represent uncertain and complex data. Recently, there has been significant interest in developing new distances and similarities for picture fuzzy sets, in order to develop more accurate and robust methods for decision making. For example, Li et al.17 developed new distances and similarities for PFSs and used them to classify and to analyze decisions, providing evidence that they can be useful tools for representing conflicting information and uncertainty. Similarly, Verma and Rohtagi18 developed a similarity measure for PFSs and applied it to both pattern recognition and to medical diagnostics, demonstrating the applicability of these types of measures to real-world problems. The work by Luo & Li19 presented new similarity measures for PFSs that showed the versatility of the PFS-based similarity measures to be used in many different decision-making situations. Gupta & Kumar20 furthered the applicability of PFS-based similarity measures into clustering and pattern recognition areas, providing a flexible methodology to group data points with uncertainty. Chammam et al.21 proposed an alternative similarity measure for PFSs; they also examined several applications and demonstrated better performance than other methodologies in decision support systems. In addition, some researchers studied fuzzy set extensions, such as Fermatean fuzzy sets and bipolar complex fuzzy sets, these extensions provide new possibilities to evaluate the similarity and distance between fuzzy sets. Kirişci22 proposed cosine-based similarity and distance measures for Fermatean fuzzy sets, and implemented them in a TOPSIS model, which resulted in higher ranking precision. On the other hand, Mahmood & Ur Rehman23 developed generalized similarity measures for bipolar complex fuzzy sets; they highlighted the flexibility of bipolar complex fuzzy sets to handle very uncertain information. Therefore, all of these works emphasize the significance of using advanced similarity and distance measures to improve the decision-making process, especially when dealing with uncertain, complex, or multi-dimensional data. Nevertheless, despite all of these advances, there is still an open issue regarding the integration of mutual induction-based feature selection and picture fuzzy set measures to rank and prioritize alternatives in Education 5.0 environments, it is what the present research aims to address.

Motivation

Modern decision-making environments have become increasingly complex with the advent of rapid growth of digital technologies and intelligent systems. As a result, decision-makers must evaluate multiple factors (criteria) when making decisions in various real-world applications, including strategic planning, policy evaluations, and technology selections. Many traditional decision models are unable to represent this type of uncertainty effectively; therefore, researchers have been exploring alternative methods using advanced fuzzy frameworks and enhanced similarity and distance measures. Several research studies have been conducted to develop distance and similarity measures for Picture Fuzzy Sets and related fuzzy environments. For example, Li et al.17, developed several new similarity and distance measures for picture fuzzy sets and demonstrated the ability of these similarity and distance measures to solve classification and decision-making problems. These authors also demonstrated that improving similarity measures can improve the reliability of the evaluation results in uncertain environments. Additionally, Verma and Rohtagi18 developed new similarity measures for picture fuzzy sets and used the similarity measures to perform pattern recognition and medical diagnoses. These authors demonstrated that picture fuzzy sets provide an effective method to represent complex decision information more effectively than traditional fuzzy set models.

A number of researchers have furthered research into the area of similarity measures for picture fuzzy sets. For example, Luo and Li19 developed several new similarity measures for picture fuzzy sets and used those measures to demonstrate how they could be used as part of multiple decision analysis tools. Gupta and Kumar20 developed a new similarity measure for picture fuzzy sets and showed how that measure could be utilized to perform clustering and pattern recognition based on the flexible nature of picture fuzzy models when dealing with uncertain data. Chammam et al.21 recently proposed yet another similarity measure for picture fuzzy sets and demonstrated how that measure performed in numerous decision-making applications and further emphasized the necessity for developing better similarity and distance measures. While significant progress has been made in developing theoretical foundations for similarity and distance measures using picture fuzzy sets, most of the prior work has focused primarily on classification, pattern recognition, or medical diagnosis applications, with little emphasis placed on providing a comprehensive decision support system that incorporates feature selection and ranking methods. Furthermore, most of the previously mentioned approaches fail to take into consideration the interdependencies and mutual influences among the evaluation criteria, which can impact the accuracy of decision-making results. Thus, clearly, there is a need to develop a more integrated decision support system that utilizes the combination of picture fuzzy modeling with improved similarity and distance measures and provides a method to account for the interdependencies of decision attributes. These limitations are the motivation behind this current study’s development of a decision support system that incorporates picture fuzzy sets, mutual induction-based feature evaluation, and an enhanced ranking system, to provide a more reliable and structured method for assessing emerging technologies in complex decision-making environments. Some work related to aggregation operators can be seen in work of Said et al.24 and Riaz & Farid25. The recent research has highlighted the increasing significance of the sustainability-based evaluation systems in the sophisticated decision-making contexts. As an example, Chang et al.26 designed an eco-social-governance science park evaluation framework and emphasized the importance of systematic multi-dimensional criteria to evaluate the performance of environmental, social, and governance. Their work illustrates the way in which the integrated evaluation models can facilitate the sustainable policy formulation and institutional decision-making. On the same note, Sharma27 discussed regenerative organic agriculture as one of the avenues of restoring the ecosystem, noting the importance of system-oriented and sustainability-based approaches to the long-term evolution. Ali & Rehman28 proposed the idea of Schweizer–Sklar prioritized aggregation operators for interval-valued picture fuzzy sets. These works are all pointing to the need to incorporate sustainability, adaptability, and multi-criteria perspectives in evaluation frameworks. The offered model in this respect is an extension of the current body of research because it incorporates state-of-the-art fuzzy-based decision support methods into the framework of sustainability-conscience criteria, thus offering a more multifaceted and versatile framework of Education 5.0 technologies assessment.

Education 5.0 emphasizes an educational environment that is both intelligent and personalized through the integration of artificial intelligence, extended reality, internet of things-enabled classroom learning environments, and knowledge systems that are adaptable to meet the needs of individual learners. Additionally, Education 5.0’s focus on sustainability further exemplifies the increased complexities of educational systems, which require the development of systematic evaluation frameworks capable of evaluating the multitude of conflicting criteria under conditions of uncertainty. A significant factor to be considered in the evaluation of educational systems is the selection of adequate distance and similarity measures for the evaluation of fuzzy and picture fuzzy information. Existing measures have focused on pattern recognition (PR), classification or medical diagnosis and are generally limited to the fuzzy environment for which they were developed17,18,35. These measures provide a basis of comparison but their direct use within MCDM Frameworks like PROMETHEE-II is limited. The PFZN approach provides an improvement to previous work by combining generalized distance/similarity measures and hesitation modeling to allow for robust pairwise preference comparison and improved discrimination among alternatives and improved ranking stability. Structure of the paper

The paper is structured as follows: In “Systematic review and methodological gaps in education 5.0 evaluation” section the basic principles of PFNs are presented for dealing with uncertainty in general. The new algorithm is described in detail in “Preliminaries” section. It combines an extension of the mutual induction method to solve EPROMETHEE II problems. A detailed case study is given in “Case study” section. “Results and discussion” section discusses the results of the investigations and analyses made including comparative and sensitivity studies. Finally, in “Conclusion” section, the conclusions of the paper are drawn, and some possible ways of continuing this work are outlined. Case study

The rapid transformation of modern education systems toward Education 5.0 has introduced a wide range of advanced digital technologies such as artificial intelligence-based learning systems, learning analytics platforms, immersive virtual environments, smart campus infrastructures, and metaverse-based learning ecosystems. These technologies have the potential to significantly enhance teaching quality, institutional performance, and policy implementation by enabling intelligent learning environments and data-driven educational management. However, selecting the most appropriate technology among multiple alternatives remains a complex decision-making problem for policymakers and institutional administrators.

One of the major challenges in evaluating Education 5.0 technologies is the presence of uncertainty, vagueness, and incomplete information in expert judgments and technological assessments. Traditional decision-making approaches often fail to properly represent these uncertainties and therefore may produce unreliable or inconsistent evaluation results. Although fuzzy set-based methods have been widely used to address uncertainty in multi-criteria decision-making problems, many existing approaches still face limitations in effectively capturing the complex relationships between criteria and distinguishing alternatives with similar performance levels.

Another important issue is that interdependencies among evaluation criteria are often ignored in conventional weighting techniques. In real-world educational technology assessment, criteria such as artificial intelligence integration, learning personalization, student engagement, and data analytics capability are closely related to each other. Ignoring these relationships may lead to inaccurate weight estimation and biased ranking outcomes. Moreover, many existing studies rely on traditional similarity or distance measures that may not provide sufficient discrimination power when dealing with picture fuzzy information.

In addition, decision environments in digital education systems are inherently dynamic and uncertain. However, many existing evaluation frameworks do not incorporate robustness or uncertainty analysis, which makes it difficult to verify the stability and reliability of ranking results. Without such analysis, decision-makers may lack confidence in selecting strategic technologies for long-term institutional investment.

Therefore, there is a need to develop a robust and intelligent decision-support framework that can effectively handle uncertainty, capture interrelationships among criteria, and provide reliable ranking results for evaluating emerging Education 5.0 technologies. Addressing these challenges forms the central research problem of this study. The evaluation of Education 5.0 technologies requires selecting key technological alternatives and a comprehensive set of evaluation criteria. This study considers ten representative technologies, including artificial intelligence-based learning systems, learning analytics platforms, immersive virtual environments, smart campus infrastructures, and metaverse-based education ecosystems, each contributing differently to intelligent education and institutional performance, as presented in Table 7.

Discussion of the ranking results

Education 5.0 technologies were appraised based on ten important criteria, which reflect the academic, technological and institutional factors needed to ensure that the process of digital transformation in the education sector was effective. Such requirements encompass ability to personalize learning, improve teaching effectiveness, the level of student engagement, the ability to involve artificial intelligence, analytics of data, institutional decision support, digital infrastructure interoperability, access and inclusiveness, cost efficiency, and development of future skills. A combination of these criteria assists in a broad understanding of the effectiveness of new Education 5.0 technologies to enhance the quality of teaching, the overall performance of the institution, and policy implementation. Methodologically, the results obtained are justified by a powerful hybrid decision-support system that incorporates picture fuzzy sets, feature selection using mutual induction, and sophisticated similarity and distance measures into a multi-criteria decision-making system. Mutual induction mechanism is important to detect the most influential evaluation criterion by assessing the relationships and dependencies between features and, therefore, to eliminate the redundant information and enhance the reliability of the decision model. The chosen criteria and their weights are further included into the ranking process according to the PROMETHEE II which offers a detailed selection of alternatives by analyzing their preferences flow. The methodological combination provides a more precise and consistent hierarchy of Education 5.0 technologies and is useful in managing uncertainty and non-relativistic dependencies between decision criteria.

Beginning with the findings from the above research, artificial intelligence learning systems () were ranked highest among all the options analyzed. Thus, this indicates that AI-based learning technologies are important components of the Education 5.0 system. AI-based learning technologies performed well across all three critical evaluation criteria: (1) artificial intelligence integration; (2) learning personalization capability; (3) data analytics capability. Learning technology platforms based on AI support adaptive learning environments, automated assessment, and predictive analysis to monitor students’ progress. In doing so, AI-based learning platforms significantly increase the effectiveness of teachers’ practice while providing institutional decision-makers with data-driven insights to inform decisions about the institution. Second-ranked was learning analytics and big data platforms (). They provided powerful tools for analyzing large amounts of educational data and supported evidence-based academic planning and policy development. Third-ranked were intelligent tutoring systems () due to their ability to provide students with personalized learning support and automate feedback to improve student engagement and achievement. Finally, cloud-based collaborative learning platforms () demonstrated high performance as they support flexible, accessible, and interactive learning environments for students and educators.

Immersive technology (such as Virtual Reality and Augmented Reality ) can provide an experiential and interactive learning environment by creating a real-world experience for students. However, both VR and AR, are considered to be expensive with respect to implementation and require significant technical support. Conversely, IoT smart campus infrastructure () and Blockchain Academic Credential Systems () are viewed as primarily supporting Institutional Management, Administrative Transparency and Credential Verification versus enhancing the teaching and learning process; therefore, they received relatively low rankings. Digital Twin Educational Systems () and Metaverse-based Learning Ecosystems () were ranked lower than other categories of technologies. While Digital Twins and Metaverses represent exciting emerging directions for digital education, their present-day implementations are generally very limited due to technological difficulties, extremely high development costs, and a lack of institutional preparedness to implement. The overall results from this study show that those technologies that emphasize Artificial Intelligence, Personalized Learning, Advanced Data Analytics, etc., will most likely result in improved teaching quality, institutional efficiency, and policy implementation in Education 5.0 environments. In order to place the proposed framework further in the context of the intelligent systems and uncertainty modeling, we make the connections with the more recent research that reveals the flexibility of the fuzzy-based hybrid decision-support solutions. The study by Hasan61 discussed fuzzy control of hybrid power systems in order to enhance power quality, and the authors emphasized the effectiveness of fuzzy-based approaches in dealing with dynamic uncertainty and improving the performance of complex environments. On the same note, Turgay et al.62 used T-spherical hesitant fuzzy rough sets to compare edge-cloud architecture in smart manufacturing and showed how sophisticated fuzzy models may enhance the quality of decisions in uncertain and multi-criteria scenarios. Further contextualizing the proposed framework on the wider multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) literature, recent literature has been used to demonstrate the application and applicability of the MCDM techniques in various fields. As an illustration, Baydas et al.63 conducted a thorough evaluation of economic data with maximum normalization, CODAS, and fuzzy methods, which proves that hybrid MCDM methods are effective in financial analysis. Equally, Isik et al.64 introduce a hybrid model of commercial bank performance measurement in Pakistan, the application of MCDM in the measurement of institutional performance using complicated criteria. These works, among other modern applications, underscore the increased significance of MCDM frameworks in supporting decisions, and assist in locating our proposed Education 5.0 evaluation model in a broader research context. Combining the findings of these studies, our proposed model can be viewed not only as a powerful tool of assessing Education 5.0 technologies but also a versatile framework that can be used in the context of intelligent systems, smart industrial and other areas where uncertainty and multi-criteria decision-making play a crucial role.

Limitations

In spite of the advantages, the model has some weaknesses. It relies on expert decisions to set initial criteria scores, which could introduce some subjectivity, and the use of picture fuzzy sets, mutual induction, PROMETHEE II, and Monte Carlo simulations exacerbates computational complexity and thus may limit the potential to apply it in large-scale systems in real time. The framework assumes that the weights assigned to the criteria to be used during evaluation remain constant in operation; however, in the case of operational considerations, priorities and technological efficacies will change, and thus threaten the stability of the resultant rankings. In addition, where two-way induction will suit interdependencies, complex correlations in which there are more than two sides are not well represented. Lastly, the framework has been tested on a list of Education 5.0 technologies representative of a specific milieu, and its application to other institutional or national contexts might require the adaptation of the framework to the setting.

Theoretical and practical implications of the proposed framework

The implication of the proposed framework to the field of fuzzy decision making and the evaluation of Education 5.0 technologies is rather significant both theoretically and practically. Theoretically, the framework develops fuzzy multi-criteria decision making by developing a new distance and similarity between the picture fuzzy sets, and this makes the alternatives more distinguished in situations of uncertainty. Mutual induction in feature selection, which is also implemented in the model, helps strengthen it even further, in that interdependencies of criteria are captured in a systematic manner, overcoming a weakness of traditional weighting schemes. Further, the use of Monte Carlo simulation provides a strong platform for uncertainty propagation and stochastic behaviour assessment, and therefore enhances the theoretical understanding of ranking stability in complicated decision-making environments. Besides, the framework applies extant fuzzy MCDM strategies to the Education 5.0 domain, introducing a rigorous method of visualizing AI-based, immersive, and data-driven educational technologies, as an aspect that lacked focus in earlier research.

Practically, the model provides policy-makers and administrative heads of educational institutions with an effective evidence-based model of analyzing and prioritizing Education 5.0 technologies, as such that it would help them make educated decisions about strategic investments in digital learning infrastructures. It helps institutions to make the most out of their performance as it helps to find technologies that can improve the individualization of learning, the use of AI, data analytics, and engagement with students, considering the cost, accessibility, and complexity of the implementation. The framework also helps in this area, resource allocation and planning strategies, so that the financial, technical, and human resources are used efficiently and innovative educational technologies are adopted successfully.

Conclusion

This research presents an all-encompassing intelligent decision support system (DSS) to evaluate education 5.0 technologies with features of picture fuzzy sets, mutual induction for the selection of relevant features, new measures of distance and similarity, and PROMETHEE II as a multi-criteria method to rank these technologies. In addition, this DSS can be used by policymakers, institutional administrators, and educators to provide a systematic assessment of the uncertainties involved in the evaluation process; capture the relationships between various evaluation criteria; and assess the stability of rankings using Monte Carlo simulation. Therefore, the proposed DSS is a robust, reliable, and empirical basis for decision-making on the adoption of educational technology. Moreover, the results of this study indicate that AI-based learning systems, learning analytics platforms, and intelligent tutoring systems are significantly better than other alternatives for improving learning personalization, teaching efficiency, students’ motivation to learn, decision-making based on data, and overall institutional performance. The results demonstrate how Education 5.0 technologies can provide transformative learning environments in terms of developing future-ready, student-centered, and innovation-driven educational systems. From an academic point of view, this work represents an important contribution to fuzzy MCDM research by proposing a method (mutual induction) for selecting features systematically, thereby capturing interdependencies among the criteria, and by developing new similarity-measure methods that better differentiate among alternative solutions when uncertainty exists. From a practical viewpoint, the proposed framework provides a structured, actionable methodology to assist decision makers in their investment decisions regarding digital educational infrastructures; optimize the use of resources; guide the adoption of technology; and support data-driven education policy. Furthermore, it connects theoretical developments in decision-making to practical applications in Education 5.0 and offers a repeatable model for large-scale institutional planning.

The framework has some limitations; it is reliant on the expert judgment of the first criteria evaluations, it is computationally intensive as it integrates multiple advanced techniques, and it assumes that the criteria weights will remain constant during the evaluation process which does not allow the changing institutional priorities to be evaluated. Future studies can improve this model with the development of the dynamic mechanisms of weighting, with the incorporation of the real time learning analytics, and with the modeling of the interdependencies of the criteria. The proposed framework may be expanded to incorporate adaptive artificial intelligence (AI) learning models as a possible future research direction to increase the ability of decision support in smart educational and regional systems. As an example, Fedorka et al.65 show how adaptive AI learning models can be applied to decision support systems in smart regions, and how the models can dynamically enhance decision quality in complex and changing environments. The integration of these adaptive AI solutions into the existing framework may allow real-time learning, perpetual optimization, and more intelligent prioritization of Education 5.0 technologies, which may expand the applicability and influence of the model in the educational environment and the more general smart systems. The application of the framework in international and cross-cultural educational settings can demonstrate the flexibility and applicability of the framework. The expansion of the framework through the use of emerging technologies (e.g., digital twin, immersive virtual/augmented reality, metaverse-based learning systems) can also expand the scope of the framework. Overall, the proposed framework provides an innovative, systematic, and forward-thinking approach to leverage the Education 5.0 technologies to optimize institutional performance and to support decision-making processes, to develop innovation, inclusiveness and excellence in education all around the world.

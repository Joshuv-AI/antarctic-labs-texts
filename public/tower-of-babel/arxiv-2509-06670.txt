On Catastrophicity of Convolutional Codes and their encoders over ℤ p r {\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
Mohammed El Oued

Abstract
. This paper investigates the existence of minimal p p -encoders for convolutional codes over ℤ p r \mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}} , where p p is a prime. This addresses a conjecture from [ 7 ] , which posits that every such code admits a minimal p p -encoder, implying that all convolutional codes over ℤ p r \mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}} are noncatastrophic when input sequences are restricted to coefficients in { 0 , … , p − 1 } \{0,\dots,p-1\} . Our contributions include the introduction of a new polynomial invariant that characterizes free codes, which enables us to establish a necessary and sufficient condition for a free code over ℤ p r \mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}} to be noncatastrophic in the usual sense (where input coefficients are from ℤ p r \mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}} ). Based on these findings, we affirm the conjecture by providing a constructive method for obtaining a minimal p p -encoder for any convolutional code over ℤ p r \mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}} .



1.
Introduction

Convolutional codes over rings were introduced by Massey and Mittelholzer
[
11
]
, with a specific focus on the ring
{\mathbb{Z}}_{M}
. They demonstrated that linear
codes over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{M}
are particularly suitable for phase modulation. Johannesson, Wan and Wittenmark
[
6
]
further the structural analysis of these
codes, which exhibit significantly different behavior compared to codes over
fields. They introduced the concepts of right-invertible, noncatastrophic, and basic encoders and analyzed the algebraic structure of these classes of encoders. Fagnani and Zampieri
[
5
]
extended this analysis presenting a complete theory
of convolutional codes over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
in the usual case where the input sequence is
a free module. More recently, Kuijper and Pinto introduced
[
7
]
the notions of
p
-encoder and minimal
p
-encoder for convolutional codes over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
, thereby
defining several invariants for these codes, such as
p
-dimension and
p
-degree,
generalizing the existing notions for codes over fields.

In the case of codes over fields, any linear convolutional code admits a noncatastrophic encoder, i.e, a generator matrix
G(D)
with the property that the output
{{\bf u}}(D)G(D)
is of finite weight only if the input
{{\bf u}}(D)
is also so. Thus,
catastrophicity in this context is a property of the encoder rather than the code
itself. However, for codes over finite rings, the existence of noncatastrophic
encoders has been the subject of several studies. Massey and Mittelholzer
[
6
]

showed that a polynomial matrix
G(D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[D]^{k\times n}
is noncatastrophic if and
only if its projection
\overline{G(D)}
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[D]^{k\times n}
is also noncatastrophic. Fagnani and
Zampieri
[
5
]
proved that any free convolutional code over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
admits a noncatastrophic rational matrix. Nevertheless, there exist codes for which every
polynomial encoder is catastrophic, indicating that catastrophicity can be an
intrinsic property of the code.

The occurrence of catastrophic codes motivated the introduction of the concept of minimal
p
-encoder for convolutional codes over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
in
[
7
]
. A minimal
p
-encoder
G(D)
for a code
{\mathcal{C}}
is defined as a noncatastrophic
p
-encoder whose rows form a reduced
p
-basis for
{\mathcal{C}}
. The authors conjectured that any convolutional code over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
admits a minimal
p
-encoder. In this work, we address and resolve this conjecture by presenting a constructive proof of the existence of a minimal
p
-encoder for any convolutional code over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: next section is devoted to the exposition of some preliminaries on convolutional codes over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
, specifically recalling the notions of
p
-basis,
p
-encoder and minimal
p
-encoder. Section 3 deals with full row rank polynomial matrices over a finite field, focusing on basic and catastrophic matrices. Section 4 studies the catastrophicity of polynomial matrices and free codes over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
. Finally, Section 5 is devoted to the existence and constriction of minimal
p
-encoders for any convolutional code over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
.

2.
Algebraic preliminaries

This section establishes the foundational algebraic concepts and notation used throughout this paper, particularly concerning rings of series and rational functions, and the definition of convolutional codes over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
.

Let
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}((D))
be the ring of Laurent series over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
. We denote by
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]
the ring of polynomials with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
and by
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}(D)
the ring of rational functions defined in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
. More precisely,
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}(D)
is the set of rational functions
\frac{p(D)}{q(D)}
, where
p(D),q(D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]
and the lowest degree coefficient of
q(D)
is a unit.

This condition defines an equivalence class with the relation

\frac{p(D)}{q(D)}\sim\frac{p_{1}(D)}{q_{1}(D)}\mbox{ if and only if }p(D)q_{1}(D)=p_{1}(D)q(D).

It follows directly from these definitions that
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]
is a subring of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}(D)
, which is itself a subring of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}((D))
.

2.1.
Convolutional codes over a finite ring

A significant portion of the existing literature on convolutional codes over rings utilizes the framework of semi-infinite Laurent series to represent code sequences
[
4
,
6
,
9
,
11
]
. Following this literature, we adopt this approach in our definition:
A linear convolutional code
{\mathcal{C}}
of length
n
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
is defined as a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}((D))
-submodule of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}((D))^{n}
. Such a code can be characterized by the existence of a polynomial generator matrix
G(D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]^{k\times n}
where the code
{\mathcal{C}}
is the image of
G(D)
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}((D))
:

{\mathcal{C}}=Im_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}((D))}G(D)=\{{{\bf v}}(D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}((D))^{n}/\exists{{\bf u}}(D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}((D))^{k}\ :{{\bf v}}(D)={{\bf u}}(D)G(D)\}.

The matrix
G(D)
is called generator matrix.
If moreover
G(D)
is a full row rank matrix over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}((D))
, then is called an
encoder
of
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}
is a free module, called
free convolutional code
.

A generator matrix
G(D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]^{k\times n}
is defined to be
noncatastrophic
if any infinite weight input
{{\bf u}}(D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}((D))^{k}
cannot produces a finite weight output, i.e.,

\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}(D)G(D)]<+\infty\Longleftrightarrow\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}(D)]<+\infty,

where the Hamming weight wt
[{{\bf u}}(D)]
of a sequence
{{\bf u}}(D)=({{\bf u}}_{1}(D),\ldots,{{\bf u}}_{k}(D))\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}((D))^{k}
is defined as the sum of the weights of its components :

\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}(D)]=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}_{i}(D)]

where
\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}_{i}(D)]
represents the number of nonzero coefficients of the series
{{\bf u}}_{i}(D)
.

In contrast to convolutional codes over fields, catastrophicity is an inherent property of codes defined over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
. Specifically, there exist convolutional codes over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}

for which every polynomial encoder is catastrophic. Such codes are termed catastrophic codes. For instance, the code over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{4}
generated by the matrix
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1+D&3+D\\
\end{array}\right)

is a catastrophic code.

It is evident that generator matrices not full row rank are catastrophic. Therefore, convolutional codes that are not free are catastrophic codes.

2.2.
p
-basis and Reduced
p
-basis

This subsection introduces the concepts of
p
-basis and reduced
p
-basis, which are fundamental for understanding the structure of convolutional codes over

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
. These concepts were initially developed for numerical vectors over
Z_{p^{r}}

[
16
]
and extended to polynomial vectors
[
8
]
.

Every element
a
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
has a unique
p
-adic expansion of the form
a=a_{0}+pa_{1}+\ldots+p^{r-1}a_{p-1}
where the coefficients
a_{i}
are in the set
\{0,\dots,p-1\}
. We will denote this set throughout this work as
\mathcal{A}_{p}
.

Given a set of polynomial vectors
{{\bf v}}_{1}(D),\dots,{{\bf v}}_{k}(D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}_{p^{r}}[D]
,

\bullet
:

A
\boldsymbol{p}
-linear combinaison
of
{{\bf v}}_{1}(D),\ldots,{{\bf v}}_{k}(D)
is a vector
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}(D){{\bf v}}_{j}(D)
,
where
a_{j}(D)
are polynomial in
\mathcal{A}_{p}[D]
(
\mathcal{A}_{p}[D]
stands for the set of polynomials with coefficients in
\mathcal{A}_{p}
).

\bullet
:

The
\boldsymbol{p}
-span
of a set of polynomial vectors
{{{\bf v}}_{1}(D),\dots,{{\bf v}}_{k}(D)}
, denoted by
p
-span
[{{\bf v}}_{1}(D),\dots,{{\bf v}}_{k}(D)]
, is the set of all
p
-linear combinations of these vectors. For a
k\times n
matrix
G(D)
with rows
g_{1}(D),\ldots,g_{k}(D)
, we define
p\mbox{-}span[G(D)]
as
p\mbox{-}span[g_{1}(D),\dots,g_{k}(D)]
.

An ordered set of vectors
({{\bf v}}_{1}(D),\dots,{{\bf v}}_{k}(D))
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]^{n}
is called a
\boldsymbol{p}
-generator sequence
if, for each
i
from 1 to
k-1
,
p{{\bf v}}_{i}(D)

can be expressed as a
p
-linear combination of
{{\bf v}}_{i+1}(D),\dots,{{\bf v}}_{k}(D)
,
and
p{{\bf v}}_{k}(D)=0
.
As proven in

[
8
]
that
if
({{\bf v}}_{1}(D),\dots,{{\bf v}}_{k}(D))
is a
p
-generator sequence in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]^{n}
it holds that

p\mbox{-}span[{{\bf v}}_{1}(D),\dots,{{\bf v}}_{k}(D)]=span[{{\bf v}}_{1}(D),\dots,{{\bf v}}_{k}(D)],

and consequently
p\mbox{-}span[{{\bf v}}_{1}(D),\dots,{{\bf v}}_{k}(D)]
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]
-submodule of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]^{n}
.

The vectors
{{\bf v}}_{1}(D),\dots,{{\bf v}}_{k}(D)
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]^{n}
are defined as
\boldsymbol{p}
-linearly independent
if the only
p
-linear combination of

{{\bf v}}_{1}(D),\dots,{{\bf v}}_{k}(D)
that yields the zero vector is the trivial one.
An ordered set of vectors
({{\bf v}}_{1}(D),\dots,{{\bf v}}_{k}(D))
which is a
p
-linearly independent
p
-generator sequence of a submodule
M
of
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}}[D]^{n}
is said to be a
\boldsymbol{p}
-basis
of
M
.

The row degree of a nonzero polynomial vector
{{\bf v}}(D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]^{n}
is defined as the highest degree of its nonzero components in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]
. It is denoted by
d^{\circ}({{\bf v}}(D))
. The coefficient vector in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}^{n}
of the term
D^{d^{\circ}({{\bf v}}(D))}
in
{{\bf v}}(D)
is called the
leading row coefficient
vector of
{{\bf v}}(D)
and denoted by
{{\bf v}}^{lc}
, likewise, the leading row coefficient matrix of a polynomial matrix
G(D)
is denoted by
G^{lrc}
.

A
p
-basis
({{\bf v}}_{1}(D),\dots,{{\bf v}}_{k}(D))
of a module
M
is called a
reduced
\boldsymbol{p}
-basis
if the set of their leading row coefficient vectors
({{\bf v}}_{1}^{lc},\dots,{{\bf v}}_{k}^{lc})
is
p
-linearly independent in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}^{n}
.

A key result, proven in
[
8
]
, establishes that every
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]
-submodule of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]^{n}
admits a reduced
p
-basis.

2.3.
Minimal
p
-encoder

This section introduces the concept of a minimal p-encoder for convolutional codes over the ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}}
. To establish this notion, we first recall the following fundamental definitions from
[
7
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

(\boldsymbol{{p}\mbox{-}encoder}).

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a convolutional code of length
n
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
. A polynomial matrix
G(D)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}}[D]^{k\times n}
whose rows form a
p
-linearly independent
p
-generator sequence is called a
p
-encoder
if

{\mathcal{C}}=\{{{\bf v}}(D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}((D))^{n}/\exists{{\bf u}}(D)\in\mathcal{A}_{p}((D))^{k}:{{\bf v}}(D)={{\bf u}}(D)G(D)\},

where
\mathcal{A}_{p}((D))
stands for the set of (semi-infinite) Laurent series with coefficients in
\mathcal{A}_{p}
.

The following definition adapts the notion of a catastrophic encoder to the context of
p
-encoders.

Definition 2.2
.

(\boldsymbol{Noncatastrophic}).

A
p
-encoder matrix
G(D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]^{k\times n}
is said to be
noncatastrophic
if any infinite weight input in
\mathcal{A}_{p}((D))^{k}
cannot produce a finite weight output. Formally, this means :

\forall{{\bf u}}(D)\in\mathcal{A}_{p}((D))^{k},\;\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}(D)G(D)]<+\infty\Longleftrightarrow\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}(D)]<+\infty.

Definition 2.3
.

(\boldsymbol{Delay\mbox{-}free\;and\;Reduced}).

Let
G(D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]^{k\times n}
be a
p
-encoder for a convolutional code
{\mathcal{C}}
of length
n
. The
p
-encoder
G(D)
is said to be delay-free if the rows of
G(0)
are
p
-linearly independent and is said to be reduced if its rows form a reduced
p
-basis for
{\mathcal{C}}
.

Definition 2.4
.

(\boldsymbol{Minimal\mbox{-}p\;encoder}).

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a noncatastrophic convolutional code of length
n
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
. Let
G(D)
be a delay-free noncatastrophic
p
-encoder for
{\mathcal{C}}
, such that the rows of

G^{lrc}
are
p
-linearly independent in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
. Then
G(D)
is called a minimal
p-
encoder for
{\mathcal{C}}
.

The existence of minimal
p
-encoders for codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}}
was raised as an open problem in the paper
[
7
]
. The authors therein formulated the conjecture that every convolutional code over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}}
admits a minimal
p
-encoder.

3.
Polynomial matrices over a finite field

This section presents foundational theorems and results concerning polynomial matrices over a finite field, which are essential for the subsequent development of this work. Notably, we define the polynomial
\Delta(G)
associated with any polynomial matrix
G(D)
, a polynomial that plays a fundamental role in our analysis.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a finite field. We denote by
{\mathbb{F}}[D],{\mathbb{F}}(D)
and
{\mathbb{F}}((D))
the sets of polynomial, rational and Laurent series, respectively, with coefficients in
{\mathbb{F}}
.

An
(n,k)
convolutional code
{\mathcal{C}}
over
{\mathbb{F}}
is a
k
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}((D))
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}((D))^{n}
. A generator matrix of
{\mathcal{C}}
is a
k\times n
matrix
G(D)
over
{\mathbb{F}}(D)
whose rowspace is
{\mathcal{C}}
. If the entries of
G(D)
are polynomials, then
G(D)
is called a polynomial generator matrix.

We say that two generator matrices
G_{1}(D),G_{2}(D)\in{\mathbb{F}}[D]^{k\times n}
are equivalent if they generate the same code. This is equivalent to the existence of a
k\times k
invertible matrix
M(D)
over
{\mathbb{F}}(D)
such that
G_{1}(D)=M(D)G_{2}(D)
.

All matrices considered in this section are assumed to have full row rank over
{\mathbb{F}}(D)
.

Let
G(D)
be a
k\times n
polynomial matrix over
F[D]
. We define the degree of a row
g_{i}(D)
of
G
as the maximum degree of its components. We define the degree and the internal degree of
G(D)
as follows

\mbox{intdeg G(D)}=\mbox{maximum degree of G(D)'s }k\times k\;\mbox{minors}

deg\;G(D)=\mbox{sum of the row degrees of G(D)}.

Definition 3.1
.

[
14
]

A
k\times n
polynomial matrix
G(D)
is called
basic
if,
G(D)
has a right
{\mathbb{F}}(D)
inverse, i.e, there is a
n\times k
polynomial matrix
H(D)
such that

G(D)H(D)=I_{k}.

The following theorem characterizes basic matrices.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
14
]

A
k\times n
polynomial matrix
G(D)
is basic if and only if the
\gcd
of the
k\times k
minors of
G(D)
is 1.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
G(D)
be a
k\times n
polynomial matrix. We define the polynomial
\Delta(G)
as the as the greatest common divisor
(\gcd)
of the nonzero
k\times k
minors of
G(D)
.

If
G(D)\in{\mathbb{F}}[D]^{k\times n}
has full row rank, there is at least one nonzero minor of
G(D)
. As a direct consequence of Theorem
3.1
,
\Delta(G)
characterizes basic matrices :
G(D)
is basic if and only if
\Delta(G)=1
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
G(D)\in{\mathbb{F}}[D]^{k\times n}
and
M(D)\in{\mathbb{F}}(D)^{k\times k}
such that
M(D)G(D)\in{\mathbb{F}}[D]^{k\times n}
. Then

\Delta(MG)=\lambda\Delta(G)\det M(D),

where
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}
is such that
\lambda\det M(D)
is a monic polynomial.

Proof.

Clearly the
k\times k
minors of
M(D)G(D)
are the
k\times k
minors of
G(D)
multiplied by
\det M
and the result holds immediately.
∎

3.1.
Catastrophic generator matrices

Definition 3.3
.

A
k\times n
polynomial generator matrix
G(D)
over
{\mathbb{F}}
is said to be
catastrophic
if there exists an infinite-weight vector
{{\bf u}}(D)\in{\mathbb{F}}((D))^{k}

such that the corresponding codeword
{{\bf u}}(D)G(D)
has finite weight.

The following theorem, provided in
[
10
]
, characterizes whether a polynomial matrix
G(D)
is noncatastrophic based on its characteristic polynomial
\Delta(G)
.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
10
]
A polynomial matrix
G(D)
is noncatastrophic if and only if
\frac{1}{\Delta(G)}
is of finite weight, i.e,
\Delta(G)
is a power of
D
.

The following lemma is crucial for understanding the sequel of this work, and its proof is based on a proof of the Theorem
3.2
given by R.J. McEliece in
[
14
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any matrix
G(D)\in{\mathbb{F}}[D]^{k\times n}
there exists a matrix
M(D)\in{\mathbb{F}}(D)^{k\times k}
where
\det M(D)
is a non-zero constant in
{\mathbb{F}}
and such that
M(D)G(D)
is a polynomial matrix which admits a row whose entries are divisible by
\Delta(G)
. Moreover, the matrix obtained from
M(D)G(D)
by dividing this row by
\Delta(G)
is basic.

Proof.

Let
\Delta(G)
be factored into irreducibles polynomials over
{\mathbb{F}}[D]
as
\Delta(G)=P_{1}(D)\dots P_{s}(D)
, where
P_{i}(D)
are monic irreducible polynomials. Let
\alpha_{i}
be a zero of
P_{i}(D)
in some extension field of
{\mathbb{F}}
.

Consider the matrix
G(\alpha_{1})
. Each of the
k\times k
minors of
G(\alpha_{1})
is zero, so rank
G(\alpha_{1})<k
. Thus, there exists a
k\times k
elementary matrix
M_{1}(\alpha_{1})
where the matrix
G_{1}(\alpha_{1})=M_{1}(\alpha_{1})G(\alpha_{1})
has one row all zeros. We assume that is the last row. By replacing
\alpha_{1}
with the indeterminate
D
, we obtain

G_{1}(D)=M_{1}(D)G(D),

where
M_{1}(D)
is a unimodular
k\times k
matrix and the last row of
M_{1}(D)G(D)
is divisible by
P_{1}(D)
. Let us define the matrix
G^{\prime}_{1}(D)
obtained from
G_{1}(D)
by dividing its last row by
P_{1}(D)
. This is equivalent to multiplying
G_{1}
on the left by the diagonal matrix

N_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&&&\\
&\ddots&&\\
&&1&\\
&&&\frac{1}{P_{1}(D)}\end{pmatrix}.

We have
\Delta(G^{\prime}_{1})=\frac{\Delta(G)}{P_{1}(D)}=P_{2}(D)\dots P_{s}(D)
. We repeat the same procedure with
G^{\prime}_{1}(D)
and the next zero
\alpha_{2}
of
\Delta(G)
. Continuing this process, we obtain a sequence of unimodular matrices
M_{1}(D),\dots,M_{s}(D)
, for
i=1,\dots,s-1
a sequence of diagonal matrices
N_{i}(D)
with 1 in the
(k-1)
first diagonal entries and
\frac{1}{P_{i}(D)}
in the k-th diagonal. For
i=s
we consider
N_{s}(D)=\begin{pmatrix}1&&&\\
&\ddots&&\\
&&1&\\
&&&\frac{\Delta(G)}{P_{s}(D)}\\
\end{pmatrix}.
Let
M(D)=N_{s}(D)M_{s}(D)\dots N_{1}(D)M_{1}(D)
. Clearly the entries of the last row of
M(D)G(D)
are divisible by
\Delta(G)
. Moreover,
\det M=cte\neq 0
which implies that
\Delta(G)=\Delta(MG)
.

Finally, it is clear that the matrix obtained by dividing the last row of
M(D)G(D)
by
\Delta(G)
is basic.
∎

4.
Polynomial generator matrices over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}

In this section we extend the definitions and key results concerning polynomial generator matrices seen in the above section to the finite ring
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
. A polynomial generator matrix, or encoder, is a
k\times n
matrix over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]
whose rows are linearly independents over the field of rational functions
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}(D)
.

We recall that two
k\times n
polynomial generator matrices
G(D)
and
G^{\prime}(D)
are equivalent if they generate the same code. As in the field case, this is equivalent to the existence of a
k\times k
invertible matrix
T(D)
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}(D)
such that
G(D)=T(D)G^{\prime}(D)

[
6
]
.

We recall that a matrix
M(D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]^{k\times k}
is unimodular if
\det M
is a unit in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]
. This is equivalent to
\det M=a+pQ(D)
, where
Q(D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]
and
a
is unit in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
. Note that,
M(D)
is unimodular if and only if its projection
\overline{M(D)}
is unimodular over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[D].

The next definition extends the definition of the polynomial
\Delta(G)
to generator matrices over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
.

If
G(D)
is a
k\times n
polynomial generator matrix over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
, then its projection
\overline{G(D)}
is a polynomial generator matrix over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. This justifies the following definition.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
G(D)
be a
k\times n
polynomial generator matrix over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
. We define the polynomial
\Delta_{p}(G)
as the greatest common divisor
(\gcd)
of the nonzero
k\times k
minors of the projected matrix
\overline{G(D)}
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[D]
. That is

\Delta_{p}(G)=\Delta(\overline{G}).

Our objective is to extend Lemma
3.1
to the ring case. For this, we need the following technical lemma, which is essential for the subsequent development. First we recall that a polynomial
P(D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]
is called regular if its projection
\overline{P(D)}
into
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[D]
is not the zero polynomial.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
P(D)
and
Q(D)
be two regular polynomials in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]
. Then, the polynomial
\overline{P(D)}
divides the polynomial
\overline{Q(D)}
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[D]
if and only if, there exists an integer
i\in\{0,\ldots,r-1\}
such that for any integer
j\in\{i,\ldots,r-1\}
,
p^{j}P(D)
divides
p^{j}Q(D)
.

Using the last lemma and Lemma
3.1
we have the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G(D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]^{k\times n}
. Then there exists a matrix
M(D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}(D)^{k\times k}
, such that
\det\overline{M(D)}
is a nonzero constante in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, and where
M(D)G(D)
has a row whose projection is divisible by
\Delta_{p}(G)
.

This means that there exists an integer
i_{0}\in\{0,\ldots,r-1\}
where for any integer
i\in\{i_{0},\ldots,r-1\}
the matrix
P^{i}M(D)G(D)
is polynomial and has a row divisible by a polynomial
Q(D)
satisfying
\overline{Q(D)}=\Delta_{p}(G)
.

Moreover, by dividing this row by
Q(D)
, we obtain a noncatastrophic matrix
H(D)
such that for any
i\in\{i_{0},\ldots,r-1\}
we have

\mbox{span}[p^{i}H(D)]=\mbox{span}[p^{i}G(D)].

Example 4.1
.

Let
G(D)=\begin{pmatrix}1+D&9+D&1+5D\\
D&5D^{2}&2+D^{2}\end{pmatrix}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{16}[D]^{2\times 3}
. We have
\Delta_{p}(G)=\Delta(\overline{G})=D(1+D)^{2}
. Let the matrix
M(D)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{3}{1+D}&0\\

. We have
\overline{M(D)G(D)}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\

where the second row is divisible by
\Delta_{p}(G)
. It is easy to check that the matrix
M(D)G(D)
is not polynomial but the matrices
2^{2}M(D)G(D)
and

2^{3}M(D)G(D)
are. Moreover, the second row of

0&8D(1+D^{2})&8D(1+D^{2})\end{pmatrix}
is divisible by
\Delta_{p}(G)
. By dividing the second row by
\Delta_{p}(G)
we obtain the matrix
\begin{pmatrix}8&8&8\\

We take
H(D)=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\

which is noncatastrophic and we have

\mbox{span}[2^{3}H(D)]=\mbox{span}[2^{3}G(D)].

Using Remark
3.1
and Lemma
4.1
we have the following.

Remark 4.1
.

(1)

If the matrix
M(D)
in the Theorem
4.1
happens to be unimodular, then
G(D)
has an equivalent polynomial matrix
M(D)G(D)
which has a row whose projection over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
is divisible by
\Delta_{p}(G)
.

(2)

An irreducible polynomial
P(D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]
divides a row of the matrix

for some unimodular matrix

, and some positive integer

, if and only if,
\overline{P(D)}
divides
\Delta_{p}(G)
.

An irreducible polynomial

divides all minors of

if and only if, there exists an unimodular matrix

such that

has a row divisible by

.

4.1.
Catastrophic polynomial matrices

A key characteristic of catastrophic polynomial generator matrices over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
lies in their projection over the residue field:

Theorem 4.2
.

[
6
]

A polynomial matrix
G(D)
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
is catastrophic if and only if
\overline{G(D)}
is catastrophic.

This immediately implies that a
k\times n
polynomial matrix
G(D)
is noncatastrophic if and only if
\Delta_{p}(G)
is a power of
D
.

The following theorem is essential for the main result of this work.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
G(D)
be a
k\times n
polynomial generator matrix over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
. Then, for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,r-1\}
, there exists a matrix
G_{i}(D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]^{k\times n}
satisfying

\mbox{span}[p^{i}G_{i}(D)]=\mbox{span}[p^{i}G(D)],

and for any series
{{\bf u}}(D)\in\mathcal{A}_{p}((D))^{k}

(9)

\mbox{wt}[p^{i}{{\bf u}}(D)G_{i}(D)]<+\infty\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}(D)]<+\infty.

Proof.

We beginning by
i=0
, we have
p^{0}G=G
. Suppose that
P_{0}(D)
is an irreducible polynomial, not a power of
D
, that divides each of
k\times k
minors of
G(D)
(if not, we take
G_{0}=G
). Then there exists a nonsingular matrix
N_{0}(D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}(D)^{k\times k}
,
\det N_{0}(D)=cte
, such that
N(D)G(D)
has a row divisible by
P_{0}
. We divides this row by
P_{0}
, we obtain a matrix
G_{0}(D)
equivalent to
G(D)
and where the minors have no irreducible polynomial as common divisor.

Let now an infinite weight series
{{\bf u}}(D)\in\mathcal{A}_{p}((D))^{k}
such that
\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}(D)G_{0}(D)]<+\infty
. Then

\mbox{wt}[\overline{{{\bf u}}(D)}\overline{G_{0}(D)}]=\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}(D)\overline{G_{0}(D)}]<+\infty.

Let
M(D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(D)^{k\times k}
,
\det M(D)=cte
, such that the k-th row of
M(D)\overline{G_{0}(D)}
is divisible by
\Delta(\overline{G_{0}})=\Delta_{p}(G_{0})
. By Theorem
3.3
, the series
{{\bf u}}(D)M^{-1}(D)
has the following form

{{\bf u}}(D)M^{-1}(D)=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k-1},\frac{P(D)}{D^{l}Q(D)})

where
\mbox{wt}[w_{i}]<+\infty
and
Q(D)
is a divisor of
\Delta_{p}(G_{0})
(not a power of
D
).

As
\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}(D)G_{0}(D)]<+\infty
, we have

\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}(D)M^{-1}(D)M(D)G_{0}(D)]=\mbox{wt}[(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k-1},\frac{P(D)}{D^{l}Q(D)})M(D)G_{0}(D)]<+\infty.

This implies that the k-th row of
M(D)G_{0}(D)
is divisible by
Q(D)
which is absurd. We conclude then: for any
{{\bf u}}(D)\in\mathcal{A}_{p}((D))^{k}
, we have

\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}(D)G_{0}(D)]<+\infty\;\Longleftrightarrow\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}(D)]<+\infty.

For
i=1
, Suppose that
P_{1}(D)
is an irreducible polynomial, not a power of
D
, that divides each of
k\times k
minors of
pG_{0}(D)
. Then there exists a matrix
N_{1}(D)
,
\det N_{1}(D)
is a nonzero constant in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
, such that
pN_{1}(D)G_{0}(D)
has a row divisible by
P_{1}
. We divides this row by
P_{1}
we obtain a matrix
pG_{1}(D)
where the minors of
G_{1}(D)
have no common divisor and

span[pG(D)]=span[pG_{0}(D)]=span[pG_{1}(D)].

Let now an infinite weight series
{{\bf u}}(D)\in\mathcal{A}_{p}((D))^{k}
such that
\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}(D)G_{1}(D)]<+\infty
. We repeat the same reasoning seen for
i=0
, we obtain: for any
{{\bf u}}(D)\in\mathcal{A}_{p}((D))^{k}
,

\mbox{wt}[p{{\bf u}}(D)G_{1}(D)]<+\infty\;\Longleftrightarrow\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}(D)]<+\infty.

So, for any
i\in\{0,\ldots,r-1\}
we find a matrix
G_{i}(D)
satisfying

\mbox{span}[p^{i}G_{i}(D)]=\mbox{span}[p^{i}G(D)]
and for any
{{\bf u}}(D)\in\mathcal{A}_{p}((D))^{k}
,

\mbox{wt}[p^{i}{{\bf u}}(D)G_{i}(D)]<+\infty\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}(D)]<+\infty.

∎

Application to convolutional codes

In this section we introduce a polynomial
\Delta_{p}({\mathcal{C}})
for any free code

which is a generalisation of the polynomial
\Delta_{p}(G)
.
We will see too that this polynomial characterise catastrophic free codes.

Definition 5.1
.

A
k\times n
polynomial generator matrix
G(D)
is called
reduced internal degree matrix
(RIDM) if, among all polynomial matrices of the form
M(D)G(D)
, where
M(D)
is a
k\times k
non-singular matrix over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}(D)
, it has the minimum possible internal degree.

Next theorem is a technical result that we needs for the sequel of this section.

Theorem 5.1
.

[Theorem XIII.6
[
13
]
]
Let
P
be a regular polynomial in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]
. Then there is a monic polynomial
P_{1}
with
\overline{P}=\overline{P_{1}}
and a unit polynomial
P_{2}
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}[D]
such that
P=P_{1}P_{2}.

Next theorem is fundamental for the sequel of this section.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two equivalent reduced internal degree matrices, then
\Delta_{p}(G_{1})=\Delta_{p}(G_{2})
.

Proof.

Let
G_{1}(D)
and
G_{2}(D)
be two
k\times n
matrices satisfying the conditions of the theorem. Then there is a
k\times k
nonsingular matrix
M(D)
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}(D)
such that
G_{1}(D)=M(D)G_{2}(D)
which means

\Delta_{p}(G_{1})=\det\overline{M}\Delta_{p}(G_{2})
. Then to prove that
\Delta_{p}(G_{1})=\Delta_{p}(G_{2})
it is sufficient to prove that
\det\overline{M}
is a nonzero constant in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. First we consider the particular case where
\det M
is a polynomial, which we denote by
Q(D)
. By Theorem
5.1
, we can write
Q=P_{1}P_{2}
where
P_{1}
is a monic polynomial and
P_{2}
is a unit a polynomial. Let
P
be a monic irreducible divisor polynomial of
P_{1}
. We denote by
(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{l},),l=C_{n}^{k},
the minors of
G_{1}(D)
. Then the minors of
G_{2}(D)
are
(Q\Delta_{1},\ldots,Q\Delta_{l},)
.

As
P
divides all minors of
G_{2}(D)
, by Remark
4.1
, there exists an unimodular matrix
T(D)
such that
T(D)G_{2}(D)
has a row divisible by
P
.
By dividing this row by
P
and as
P
is a monic irreducible polynomial, we obtain a matrix
G^{\prime}(D)
such that

\mbox{intdeg}\;G^{\prime}=\max(\deg Q\Delta_{i})-\deg P=\mbox{intdeg}\;G_{2}-\deg P.

Since
G_{2}
is RIDM, we have
\mbox{intdeg}G^{\prime}\geq\mbox{intdeg}G_{2}.

Then we can conclude that
\deg P=0
. This means that any monic irreducible divisor of
P_{1}
is constant which implies that
P_{1}
is a constant polynomial. Then
\det M
is a unit polynomial and then
\det\overline{M}
is a nonzero constant in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.

For the general case, let suppose that
\det M
is not polynomial. Without loss of generality we can assume that
\det M=R(D)=\frac{Q(D)}{P(D)}
where
P(D)
is a monic irreducible polynomial. Then the minors of
G_{2}(D)
are
\frac{Q(D)}{P(D)}\Delta_{i}
. As
G_{2}(D)
is a polynomial matrix, we conclude that
P
divides all
\Delta_{i}
. This means that there exists an unimodular matrix
T(D)
such that
T(D)G_{1}(D)
has a row divisible by
P(D)
. By dividing this row by
P
, we obtain a matrix
G^{\prime}(D)
equivalent to
G(D)
satisfying

\mbox{intdeg}\;G^{\prime}=\max(\deg\Delta_{i})-\deg P=\mbox{intdeg}\;G_{1}-\deg P.

Since
\mbox{intdeg}\;G^{\prime}\geq\mbox{intdeg}\;G
, we conclude that
P
is a constant polynomial. Then
\det M
is polynomial and the result holds from the first part of the proof.

∎

A free code

over

5.1.1.
Case of free codes

Let

Let

It is possible now to state the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.4
.

Any free convolutional code over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
admits a minimal
p
-encoder.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a free convolutional code over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
and

be a reduced internal degree encoder of
{\mathcal{C}}
. Then we have
\Delta_{p}(G)=\Delta_{p}({\mathcal{C}})
. Let for

G_{i}(D)
the matrix deduced from

as describe in the Proposition
4.1
. As
G(D)
is taken as RIDM, then
G_{0}(D)=G(D)
. Clearly the matrix
G^{\prime}(D)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}G\\
pG_{1}\\
\vdots\\
p^{r-1}G_{r-1}\\
\end{array}\right)
is a
p
-encoder for
{\mathcal{C}}
.

Let
{{\bf u}}(D)=({{\bf u}}_{0}(D),\dots,{{\bf u}}_{r-1}(D))\in\mathcal{A}_{p}((D))^{kr}
be an input sequence which produces a finite weight output via
G^{\prime}(D)
. This means that

(12)

\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}(D)G^{\prime}(D)]=\mbox{wt}[\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}{{\bf u}}_{i}(D)p^{i}G_{i}(D)]<+\infty.

The equation
12
, gives that

\mbox{wt}[\overline{{{\bf u}}(D)G^{\prime}(D)}]=\mbox{wt}[\overline{{{\bf u}}_{0}(D)}\overline{G_{0}(D)}]=\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}_{0}(D)\overline{G(D)}]<+\infty.

Let suppose that
\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}_{0}(D)]=+\infty
. Without loss of generality, we can suppose that the projection of
G(D)
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
admits a row divisible by
\Delta_{p}(G)
, (if not we replace
G
by
MG
, where
M(D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(D)^{k\times k}
,
\det M(D)=cte
and
M(D)G_{0}(D)
has a row divisible by
\Delta_{p}(G_{0})
). Then, by Theorem
3.3
, the first component of
{{\bf u}}_{0}(D)
is equal to
\frac{Q(D)}{D^{m}P(D)}
where
P(D)
is an irreducible divisor of
\Delta_{p}(G)
and,
gcd(P,Q)=1
.

Let denote by
g(D)
the first row of
G(D)
. The first component of
p{{\bf u}}_{1}(D)G_{1}(D)
is given by
pP_{1}(D){{\bf u}}_{1}^{1}(D)g(D)
, where
{{\bf u}}_{1}^{1}
is the first component of
{{\bf u}}_{1}
and
P_{1}
is an irreducible divisor of
\Delta_{p}(G_{0})
. Similarly, the first component of
{{\bf u}}_{i}(D)G_{i}(D)
is
p^{i}{{\bf u}}^{1}_{i}(D)P_{1}\ldots P_{i}
, where the polynomials
P_{j}
are irreducibles divisors of
\Delta_{p}(G)
. Then, the first component of
{{\bf u}}(D)G^{\prime}(D)
has the following form :

{{\bf u}}_{1}^{1}(D)G^{\prime}(D)=\frac{Q(D)+pR(D)}{P(D)}g(D),

where
R(D)
is a polynomial over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{r}}
.
As
\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}_{1}^{1}(D)G^{\prime}(D)]<+\infty
and
P(D)
is not a divisor of
Q(D)+pR(D)
we conclude that
P(D)
divides the first row
g(D)
of
G(D)
and then divides all minors of
G(D)
which is impossible. Then we have
\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}_{0}(D)]<+\infty
.

It follows from equation
12
that

\mbox{wt}[p{{\bf u}}_{1}(D)G_{1}(D)+\ldots+p^{r-1}{{\bf u}}_{r-1}(D)G_{r-1}(D)]<+\infty,

which implies that

\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}_{1}(D)G_{1}(D)+\ldots+p^{r-2}{{\bf u}}_{r-1}(D)G_{r-1}(D)]<+\infty.

We repeat the same reasoning as above, we conclude that
\mbox{wt}[{{\bf u}}(D)]<+\infty.
This proves that
G^{\prime}(D)
is noncatastrphic when the input sequence is in
\mathcal{A}_{p}((D))^{kr}
. Finally, we apply the row reduction algorithm of
[
8
]
on the rows of
G^{\prime}(D)
, we obtain a matrix
\widetilde{G}(D)
whos rows form a reduced p-basis and then is a minimal p-encoder for
{\mathcal{C}}
.

∎

6.
Conclusion

7.
Conclusion

The study of convolutional codes over finite rings presents many challenges compared to their counterparts over fields. A significant distinction is the existence of catastrophic codes over rings, where any polynomial generator matrix can lead to an infinite weight input mapping to a finite weight output. This fundamental difference motivated the introduction of the novel concept of a ”
p
-encoder” in
[
7
]
, aiming to provide a noncatastrophic representation for any code over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}}
. The authors in
[
7
]
conjectured that every convolutional code over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}}
admits a minimal
p
-encoder, which implies that all such codes are noncatastrophic when the coefficients of the input series are in
\mathcal{A}_{p}
.

To address this conjecture, our work introduced a new polynomial,
\Delta_{p}(\mathcal{C})
, specifically tailored to characterize free codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}}
. This allowed us to establish a necessary and sufficient condition for a free code to be catastrophic. Furthermore, we investigated the crucial relationship between infinite weight inputs (with coefficients in
\mathcal{A}_{p}
) producing finite weight outputs, demonstrating how the entries of such inputs are directly related to the polynomial
\Delta_{p}(\mathcal{C})
and its divisors. These insights provided the foundation for our primary contribution: the systematic construction of minimal
p
-encoders, initially for free codes, and subsequently generalized to encompass all convolutional codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}}
. This work thereby resolves the conjecture from
[
7
]
, affirming the existence of a minimal
p
-encoder for every convolutional code over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}}
.

Umbilicoplasty Using Six Cutaneous Flaps in the Surgical Treatment of Congenital Umbilical Anomalies
Authors: Arif Bajmak, Marija Kolinovic
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTCongenital umbilical anomalies (umbilical cord hernia, larger umbilical hernias, and epithelialized omphaloceles) present a significant challenge for surgeons in achieving perfect reconstruction. About 10 years ago, we came up with the idea of forming six cutaneous flaps for reconstruction, resulting in what can be described as the perfect umbilicus. We demonstrate the application of this technique in three different cases of umbilical anomalies. The described technique for forming a neo-umbilicus using six cutaneous flaps is very simple and precise. Its application achieves excellent esthetic results without complications.

INTRODUCTION

The umbilicus represents a scar on the anterior abdominal wall and serves an esthetic function. Umbilicus reconstruction in children is very important because its absence or deformed appearance can lead to great dissatisfaction and even psychological disorders. Congenital umbilical anomalies (umbilical cord hernia, larger umbilical hernias, and epithelialized omphaloceles) present a significant challenge for surgeons in achieving perfect reconstruction. In our 35 years of surgical practice, we have employed numerous well-known and established techniques, as well as their combinations, to address these anomalies.[123] About 10 years ago, we came up with the idea of forming six cutaneous flaps for reconstruction, resulting in what can be described as the perfect umbilicus. Umbilicus reconstruction using six flaps begins with planning, drawing, and creating the flaps in a simple manner. The basis for designing the flaps is the circumference of a circle, divided into six parts, which is drawn and projected onto the base of the umbilical anomaly. The diameter of the circle is the distance of the cylinder or hemisphere base (DB). One-sixth of the circle (O) represents the base of the triangle that forms one flap (AC). r = ½ DB [Figure 1a].

Geometric schemes for flap creation: a) Diagram of the planned incisions on the skin; b) Position of the flaps before suturing; c) Approximation of the tips of the flaps at the central point (Naylon 3-0) and approximation of the lateral edges of the flaps (absorbable suture)

An additional arched incision below the umbilicus (L) is made to address defects in the deeper layers of the abdominal wall and also for fixation of the central point where the tips of the flaps meet, to the fascia [Figure 1b]. When planning the incision, care must be taken not to compromise the vascularization, i.e., the nourishment of the flaps. These are random flaps in the shape of an equilateral triangle. The base of the triangle provides the blood supply to the flap. The incision is designed so that its length corresponds to half of the base of the flap and is placed 1 cm below it (so far, we have not encountered any cases of devascularization). After the flaps are created, suturing follows: Nylon 3-0 is used for intradermal circular suturing of the flap tips, with the aim of keeping the flap tips in the same plane. A small gauze pad is tied with the free ends of the nylon suture above the knot, with the purpose of applying pressure to the center of the neo-umbilicus. The sutures will be removed 7 days after the operation. Individual absorbable sutures 5-0 are placed on the lateral sides of the flaps [Figure 1c] as superficially as possible, in order to achieve optimal adaptation and to avoid secondary healing. The creation of these flaps resembles one of the parts of a mature pomegranate fruit.

This technique is more suitable for defects with a smaller base diameter, as larger ones tend to result in a stellate scar.

We will demonstrate the application of this technique in three different cases of umbilical anomalies. The patient consent form has been obtained from all patients (signed by their parents).

Case 1

A 5-year-old boy was born with an umbilical anomaly of the umbilical cord hernia type [Figure 2a]. After projecting the flaps, an incision below the umbilicus was made to remove the obliterated blood vessels of the umbilical cord [Figure 2b]. An absorbable suture was placed to fix the intradermal suture, i.e., the center of the neo-umbilicus, to the fascia [Figure 2c]. The surgical outcomes were highly successful [Figure 2d].

A 5-year-old boy with an umbilical cord hernia: (a) preoperative appearance; (b and c) intraoperative appearance; (d) late postoperative appearance

Case 2

A 15-day-old female newborn was born with a large omphalocele [Figure 3a], which was conservatively treated until epithelialization occurred in the 3rd month of life [Figure 3b]. She underwent surgery at the age of one. The postoperative results were excellent [Figure 3c].

A 15-day-old female newborn with a large omphalocele: (a) initial preoperative appearance; (b) appearance after several months of spontaneous epithelialization; (c) late postoperative appearance

Case 3

An 8-month-old male infant was hospitalized due to a strangulated large umbilical hernia. De-incarceration was performed [Figure 4a] under general anesthesia. Through an infraumbilical incision, the abdominal wall defect, which had a larger diameter, was approached [Figure 4b]. After closing the defect in two layers, reinforcement was created with plastic surgery using excess fascia, layering the flaps one over the other [Figure 4c]. Following the closure of the umbilical defect on the abdominal wall, fixation was performed on the internal side of the central point of the neo-umbilicus to the fascia using Vicryl 4-0. The tips of the skin flaps were connected with intradermal circular suturing using Nylon 3-0, and a small gauze pad was tightened over them using the same thread. The sides of the flaps were sutured with absorbable monofilament thread 5-0 [Figure 4d]. Finally, the wound below the umbilicus was sutured intradermally with Nylon 4-0 [Figure 4e].

An 8-month-old male infant with a strangulated large umbilical hernia; (a) preoperative appearance of manually reduced hernia; (b-d) intraoperative appearance; (e) early postoperative appearance; (f) late postoperative appearance

The described technique for forming a neo-umbilicus using six cutaneous flaps is very simple and precise. Its application achieves excellent esthetic results without complications.

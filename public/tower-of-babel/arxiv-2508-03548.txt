The 1 / c 1/c expansion of general relativity in a 3 + 1 3+1 formulation, revisited
Mahmut Elbistan

Abstract
We study the 1 / c 1/c expansion of general relativity within a formulation that is compatible with both the Arnowitt-Deser-Misner and the Kol-Smolkin decompositions. The Einstein-Hilbert action takes a common form for those decompositions as they are dual to each other. We first develop a method to expand this generic form and then push the expansion up to c − 3 c^{-3} order within this novel approach. Next, we apply our technique to the Arnowitt-Deser-Misner decomposition and expand it up to c − 3 c^{-3} order explicitly. In order to demonstrate the applicability of our method and to highlight the duality at the level of expansion, we also perform the expansion in the Kol-Smolkin decomposition up to c − 1 c^{-1} order. Lastly, we make some all-order observations. Keywords: large c c expansion, 3 + 1 3+1 decomposition of general relativity, non-relativistic gravity

2.1

Parent action and its ingredients

Referring to

Let us begin with the two decompositions that we are interested in.
The ADM formalism contains the lapse function
N(t,\vec{x})
, the shift vector
N^{i}(t,\vec{x})
and the
d

h_{ij}(t,\vec{x})

On the other hand, the KS form yields a different decomposition of the same metric with the fields

The spatial metric

\displaystyle\hat{R}=h^{ij}\hat{R}_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(h^{ij}h^{kl}-h^{ik}h^{jl})h_{ln}(\hat{D}_{k}\hat{\Gamma}^{n}_{ij}-\hat{\Gamma}^{n}_{km}\hat{\Gamma}^{m}_{ij}).

The frame vectors

The action (

2.2

The usual expansion scheme

Now, we will quickly outline the expansion procedure in
[
16
]
.
The original parent action (
2.3
) has terms with
1/c^{0},1/c,1/c^{2}
orders as it contains derivatives up to second order. Moreover, the definitions of the terms in the action and their orders depend on the specific decomposition, as we have observed by comparing (
2.8
) with (
2.9
). The actual terms and their orders will be determined only if a specific decomposition is adopted.

Therefore, in the usual expansion scheme one first begins by choosing a decomposition. For instance in
[
16
]
, we immediately chose the KS decomposition of the unexpanded Lagrangian (
2.2
). Grouping the terms with the same order, we expanded the related Lagrangian (
2.3
) by assuming that the fields are analytic

M={\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($0$)}}{M}}{}+\frac{1}{c}{\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($1$)}}{M}}{}+\cdots,\quad M^{i}={\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($0$)}}{M}}{}^{i}+\frac{1}{c}{\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($1$)}}{M}}{}^{i}+\cdots,\quad h_{ij}={\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($0$)}}{h}}{}_{ij}+\frac{1}{c}{\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($1$)}}{h}}{}_{ij}+\cdots,

(2.10)

with the convention
{\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($0$)}}{M}}{}=M
,
{\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($0$)}}{M}}{}^{i}=M^{i}
and
{\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($0$)}}{h}}{}_{ij}=h_{ij}
to simplify the notation.
The order is indicated upon the fields. Thus, in the usual expansion scheme the inverse powers of
c
can be traced via summing over those indices and the number of time derivatives each accompanied with an inverse power of
c
. Such a procedure requires a great care and effort as it is performed one by one to each term while keeping track of the orders.

On the other hand, we could have done the same for the ADM decomposition (
2.1
), as well. In that case, we expand the associated fields as

N={\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($0$)}}{N}}{}+\frac{1}{c}{\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($1$)}}{N}}{}+\cdots,\quad N^{i}={\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($0$)}}{N}}{}^{i}+\frac{1}{c}{\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($1$)}}{N}}{}^{i}+\cdots,\quad h_{ij}={\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($0$)}}{h}}{}_{ij}+\frac{1}{c}{\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($1$)}}{h}}{}_{ij}+\cdots,

(2.11)

where we may set
{\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($0$)}}{N}}{}=N
,
{\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($0$)}}{N}}{}^{i}=N^{i}
and
{\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($0$)}}{h}}{}_{ij}=h_{ij}
again.

ADM or KS, the idea of having a parent action (
2.3
) for both decompositions is blurred by that specific choice. In this paper, we would like to put the idea of duality of the action forward and preserve it even at the level of the expansion.
Instead of limiting ourself to a particular slicing, we will pave our own way and establish a new technique to deal with the large
c
expansion based on the generic form of the action (
2.3
). The stumbling block on our way is the fact that
the definitions of the terms
\hat{R}
,
K_{ij}
, … are changing for each decomposition with different powers of
1/c
.
Even within the same decomposition, we have to keep track of the orders with care. We explain our expansion technique in the next section.

3

A novel framework to expand the parent action step by step

As outlined above, in the usual expansion scheme one first decides on the particular slicing i.e., ADM (
2.1
) or KS (
2.2
) and then classifies the terms in the related Lagrangian (
2.3
) according to their inverse powers of
c
. Having distinguished between the terms with different orders, only in the third step the expansion procedure (
2.10
) or (
2.11
) is initiated. The last step is to compute and collect the terms in the desired order of the expanded Lagrangian.

In our novel framework, we will reverse this procedure. Without specifying a particular decomposition (ADM or KS) and without distinguishing between the different orders in the unexpanded action (
2.3
), we propose a new expansion scheme where we directly start from the parent action (
2.3
) and perform an expansion scheme valid for both decompositions. Specific order of a specific decomposition will be chosen only at the end.

Our technique makes use of the generic identities like (
2.7
) and we even develop more of them. Therefore, the duality of ADM and KS forms will be used at full power. When it comes to fields, we follow the usual expansion scheme outlined as in Section
2.2
. However, as we do not specify the decomposition at the beginning, our expanded Lagrangians will contain 3 different orders because of the derivatives.

In Section
3.1
, we introduce our framework which does not refer to any particular decomposition.
Then, we generate identities including various orders within this framework in Section
4
. In Section
5
, we start expanding the Ricci tensor within our formulation using the aforementioned identities.

3.1

Matryoshka dolls

The orders of the terms in the parent Lagrangian (
2.3
)

L=R\sqrt{-g}=(\hat{R}+K_{ij}K^{ij}-K^{2})n_{t}\sqrt{h}

(3.1)

cannot be determined unless we adopt a specific slicing. For our aim, we would like to devise a method that can be directly applied to (
3.1
) so that we can expand both ADM and KS decompositions within the same formulation.

Let us spell out our basic method that we call the
steps
. In this framework the terms in the Lagrangian (
3.1
) is expanded to the next order altogether. Therefore, each step will contain terms with three different inverse powers of
c
rooted from the derivatives in (
3.1
) and include all the terms of the preceeding steps. Our step expansion is schematically established within a nested structure reminding us the matryoshka dolls.

For instance, the
zeroth step

[R\sqrt{-g}]_{0}
refers to the unexpanded Lagrangian (
3.1
). As its terms contain at most 2 derivatives, at this order we have terms with orders
1/c^{0},1/c,1/c^{2}
.
The
first step

[R\sqrt{-g}]_{1}
in the expansion amounts to shift the pieces of the zeroth step Lagrangian to the next order. Therefore, at that step we will capture terms with orders
1/c,1/c^{2},1/c^{3}
.
By iteration, the
nth step

[R\sqrt{-g}]_{n}
contains terms with
1/c^{n},1/c^{n+1},1/c^{n+2}
orders.

Schematically our expansion scheme is the following

[R\sqrt{-g}]\to[R\sqrt{-g}]_{0}+[R\sqrt{-g}]_{1}+[R\sqrt{-g}]_{2}+\cdots

(3.2)

where its nested structure is obvious as each step contains all the elements of its predecessors

\displaystyle[L]_{0}

\displaystyle=[R\sqrt{-g}]_{0}=[R]_{0}[\sqrt{-g}]_{0},

(3.3a)

\displaystyle[L]_{1}

\displaystyle=[R\sqrt{-g}]_{1}=[R]_{1}[\sqrt{-g}]_{0}+[R]_{0}[\sqrt{-g}]_{1},

(3.3b)

\displaystyle[L]_{2}

\displaystyle=[R\sqrt{-g}]_{2}=[R]_{2}[\sqrt{-g}]_{0}+[R]_{1}[\sqrt{-g}]_{1}+[R]_{0}[\sqrt{-g}]_{2},

(3.3c)

\displaystyle[L]_{n}

\displaystyle=[R\sqrt{-g}]_{n}=[R]_{n}[\sqrt{-g}]_{0}+[R]_{n-1}[\sqrt{-g}]_{1}+\cdots+[R]_{1}[\sqrt{-g}]_{n-1}+[R]_{0}[\sqrt{-g}]_{n}.

(3.3d)

We observe that for each step
m
we need to compute only 2 novel terms
[R]_{m}
and
[\sqrt{-g}]_{m}
since the rest is contained in the previous steps. For example, it is enough to compute
[R]_{1}
and
[\sqrt{-g}]_{1}
for the first step (

) because the elements
[\sqrt{-g}]_{0}
and
[R]_{0}
should be known from (
3.3a
). Moreover, the term
[\sqrt{-g}]_{m}=[n_{t}\sqrt{h}]_{m}
is easy to deal with as it contains only fields but not derivatives. See (

) for the first three orders of it.

Thus, our procedure boils down to calculating
[R]_{m}=[\hat{R}+K_{ij}K^{ij}-K^{2}]_{m}
for each step
m
. As it is seen from (
2.8
) and (

),
[K_{ij}K^{ij}-K^{2}]_{m}
part heavily depends on the decomposition. We can only make some formal computations about that part and will be able to finalize them only after we select the decomposition.

However, the computation of the non-trivial
[\hat{R}]_{m}
is quite systematic through the steps outlined in (

) and the results are promising. As we will show in the next sections, we can successfully conjecture and even compute it at the desired order (which is
c^{-3}
here). The gain is that our procedure is valid for both decompositions: having calculated

one can select, for instance the ADM decomposition at the end and find out the expanded Lagrangian with a little effort. The difficulty is that one seems to deal with extra terms for each order in our framework.
More precisely, in order to obtain the action expanded at
1/c^{n}
order, we need to collect the associated terms from the steps
n-2

n

And it is exactly this point where our goal of generating identities will be of use. This novel framework allows us to derive generic identities based on (

4.2

Covariant derivative of the metric

There is a second identity (

Let’s begin with the first step of (

[\hat{D}_{i}h_{jk}]_{1}=\hat{D}_{i}{\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($1$)}}{h}}{}_{jk}+{\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($1$)}}{e}}{}^{\mu}_{i}\partial_{\mu}h_{jk}-[\hat{\Gamma}^{m}_{ij}]_{1}h_{mk}-[\hat{\Gamma}^{m}_{ik}]_{1}h_{jm}=0.

\displaystyle[\hat{\Gamma}^{l}_{ij}]_{2}=\frac{1}{2}h^{lk}

\displaystyle\Big(

\displaystyle\hat{D}_{i}{\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($2$)}}{h}}{}_{jk}+\hat{D}_{j}{\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($2$)}}{h}}{}_{ik}-\hat{D}_{k}{\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($2$)}}{h}}{}_{ij}+{\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($2$)}}{e}}{}^{\mu}_{i}\partial_{\mu}h_{jk}+{\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($2$)}}{e}}{}^{\mu}_{j}\partial_{\mu}h_{ik}-{\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($2$)}}{e}}{}^{\mu}_{k}\partial_{\mu}h_{ij}

9

Conclusion

There are various approximation schemes to study GR. Among them the most successful one is the PN expansion which requires a weak field assumption together with slow velocity approximation. The subject of this paper, the
1/c
expansion of GR, is another NR approximation scheme and it differs from the PN expansion as it captures strong fields. The recent paper
[
16
]
studied the large
c
expansion of GR within a particular
3+1
decomposition, named the KS decomposition up to
c^{-2}
order. In addition, equations of motion and the symmetries of the expanded Lagrangians were found.

In this paper, we have revisited the
1/c
expansion of GR in a unified perspective. As being dual to each other, both the ADM and the KS decompositions have the same form of the Einstein-Hilbert action (
2.3
). Following the idea of this duality, we first develop an expansion scheme for that parent action. Then, we perform the expansion within our novel framework up to
c^{-3}
order systematically without specifying any decomposition. Since the ADM and the KS decompositions are dual to each other before the expansion, it is natural to ask whether this duality survives after the expansion. This is the first question that we pursue here and the answer is positive. Since the KS decomposition was worked previously in
[
16
]
, we applied our formalism to the ADM decomposition and expand it up to
c^{-3}
order, which goes beyond
[
16
]
. In order to demonstrate the duality at the level of expansion, we applied our technique to the KS decomposition up to
c^{-1}
. We also make some all order observations for the expanded parent Lagrangian. We conclude that, although they are dual to each other, the ADM decomposition is at least technically better suited for
1/c
expansion. This is especially because
{\overset{\,\scalebox{0.5}{($n$)}}{e}}{}_{i}=0
for any
n\geq 1
in ADM decomposition (
7.1
). In addition, its
K_{ij}
tensor is symmetric wheras its counterpart in the KS decomposition contains both symmetric and anti-symmetric pieces.

Here, we have not investigated the expanded equations of motion and the symmetries of the ADM decomposition. In addition, we did not make the conformal redefinition of the field
M\to\psi
as in
[
16
]
. Such a definition can also be done in our framework without any problem. As it stands,
M
or
N
(or collectively the field
n_{t}
) play the role of Lagrange multipliers as no derivative acts on them. As a future work, we would like to generate equations of motion for the
c^{-3}
order ADM Lagrangian. Our findings can be adopted to
1/c^{2}
expansion which contains only even order terms. In that case, we can easily obtain
c^{-6}
order effective ADM Lagrangian.

In this study, we mainly deal with developing a covariant expansion scheme rather than applying it.
The natural field of application of our findings are strong gravitational systems like neutron stars but it would be also interesting to work out condensed matter applications. In
[
20
]
, the authors discussed the Zermelo and Randers form of the black hole and graphene metric which correspond to ADM and KS decompositions, respectively. In
[
21
]
, the authors considered the relation between the Zermelo and Randers metrics within the Finslerian geometry. As the spatial dimension
d
is kept general here, we may reserve such potential applications for a follow-up study.

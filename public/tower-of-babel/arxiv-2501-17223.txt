Correlation functions in four-dimensional superconformal long circular quivers
Gregory P. Korchemsky, and Alessandro Testa

Abstract
We study two- and three-point correlation functions of chiral primary half-BPS operators in four-dimensional 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 superconformal circular, cyclic symmetric quiver theories. Using supersymmetric localization, these functions can be expressed as matrix integrals which, in the planar limit, reduce to Fredholm determinants of certain semi-infinite matrices. This powerful representation allows us to investigate the correlation functions across the parameter space of the quiver theory, including both weak and strong coupling regimes and various limits of the number of nodes and the operator scaling dimensions. At strong coupling, the standard semiclassical AdS/CFT expansion diverges in the long quiver limit. However, by incorporating both perturbative corrections (in negative powers of the ’t Hooft coupling) and an infinite tower of nonperturbative, exponentially suppressed contributions, we derive a remarkably simple expression for the correlation functions in this limit. These functions exhibit exponential decay with increasing node separation and admit an interpretation within a five-dimensional effective theory. We determine the mass spectrum of excitations propagating along the emergent fifth dimension within this theory, finding it to be given by the zeros of Bessel functions.

Correlation functions in circular quiver theories

The primary goal of this paper is to compute the two- and three-point functions of the conformal primary operators (

) in four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal circular quiver theory represented in Figure

As outlined in the Introduction, we assume that the coupling constants at all nodes are equal to

Consequently, the conformal primary operators
T_{\alpha,n}(x)
and

\displaystyle O_{n}^{I}(x)=\tr\phi^{n}_{I}(x)\,,\qquad\qquad\overline{O}_{n}^{I}(x)

\displaystyle=\tr\phi_{I}^{\dagger\,n}(x)\,,

where

and its complex conjugate

Due to the interaction between different nodes of the quiver, the operators (

In virtue of the cyclic symmetry of the quiver, the conformal primary operators

Properties of the circular quivers

The correlation functions (

3.1
Different regimes

We demonstrate below that the functions (

At weak coupling, we can apply (

Analogously, at strong coupling, we apply the large

Examining the relations (

\displaystyle\text{(i)\quad fixed $n$ and $a$}\,;\qquad\quad\text{(ii)\quad$a\to 0$ with $n$ fixed}\,;\qquad\quad\text{(iii)\quad$n\gg 1$ with $a$ fixed}\,.

Regime (ii) corresponds to the long quiver limit,

3.2
Long quiver limit

In the long quiver limit, for
L\to\infty
, the nodes in the quiver diagram in Fig.
1
become continuously distributed along a circle, effectively building up an additional dimension. This raises the question of whether the four-dimensional quiver theory
{\sf Q}_{L}
can be approximated by a five-dimensional model in this limit, and if so, what its properties are. We argue below that the answer depends crucially on how the ’t Hooft coupling constant compares to

L
.

To understand the properties of the long quiver regime, it is convenient to interpret the matrix integral (
12
) as defining a one-dimensional periodic lattice model of length
L
. The nodes of the quiver diagram correspond to the sites of this lattice, each of which possess
N^{2}-1
degrees of freedom represented by a
su(n)
matrix
a_{I}
. Their dynamics is determined by a nearest-neighbour interaction between
a_{I}
and
a_{I\pm 1}
. It is described by the potential (
13
) and encodes the propagation of excitations along the lattice. Owing to the cyclic symmetry of the interaction potential, these excitations possess a well-defined quasimomentum

\displaystyle p_{\alpha}={2\pi\alpha\over L}\,,

(34)

where
0\leq\alpha\leq L-1
.

Within the lattice model, the operators
T_{\alpha,n}(x)
, defined in (
1
), are interpreted as creation operators of
n-
particle excitations with the total quasimomentum
p_{\alpha}
and the
U(1)
charge
n
. The dynamics of these excitations are encoded in the correlation functions (
2
) in the quiver theory. The selection rule for the parameters
\alpha_{i}
that ensures nonvanishing correlators
is a direct consequence of the quasimomentum conservation.
The relation (
1
) provides the decomposition of an excitation of definite quasimomentum (
34
) into a superposition of excitations localized at different sites.

The correlations between different sites of the lattice model are described by the two-point correlation functions (
11a
) and take the following form

\displaystyle\big\langle O_{n}^{I}(x_{1})\bar{O}_{n}^{J}(x_{2})\big\rangle={{\cal G}_{n}\over|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{2n}}f_{I-J}(g)\,,

(35)

where the normalization factor
{\cal G}_{n}
is defined in (
16
). The function
f_{I-J}
depends on the distance between the two sites
Y=|I-J|
and it is given by a discrete Fourier transform of
{R}_{\alpha,n}(g)

\displaystyle f_{Y}=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\alpha=0}^{L-1}e^{\frac{2\pi i\alpha}{L}Y}R_{\alpha,n}(g)\,.

(36)

This function measures the correlation between two nodes of the quiver gauge theory separated by the distance
Y
. It
depends on the scaling dimension of the operators
n
, the length of the quiver
L
and it satisfies the periodicity condition

f_{Y}=f_{Y+L}
.

As mentioned above, the behaviour of the quiver theory for
L\to\infty
is determined by how the ’t Hooft coupling scales in this limit. The reason for this is that the coefficients
C_{ij}
in the interaction potential (
13
) depend on the ’t Hooft coupling as
C_{ij}\sim g^{i+j}
. These coefficients govern the interaction strength between adjacent lattice sites, while the interaction between two distant sites
I
and
J
scales as the
|I-J|
-th power of the matrix
C
.

In the weak coupling regime, the interaction strength decreases rapidly with increasing distance between the sites, effectively restricting the interactions to be short-range. In this case, the excitations propagating across the lattice have small values of the quasimomenta (
34
) and are described by an effective one-dimensional field theory. Within the context of the quiver theory, this corresponds to the emergence of an additional dimension.

In contrast, in the strong coupling regime, for
g\gg L\gg 1
, the interaction strength becomes uniform across all site pairs. As a result, the lattice model exhibits strong correlations, and the notion of site ordering along the lattice becomes meaningless. In this case, excitations carry finite quasimomentum (
34
), and the lattice effectively collapses to a point. Consequently, the quiver theory remains a four-dimensional, strongly coupled theory without an emergent dimension.

Based on this analysis, we expect that the quiver theory effectively becomes five-dimensional for
L\to\infty
provided that the ratio
g/L
remains finite. In this limit, the quasimomentum (
34
) becomes continuous and, as a consequence, the operators (
1
) are replaced by their five-dimensional counterparts in the momentum and coordinate representations, respectively,

\displaystyle T_{\alpha,n}(x)\ \mapsto\ T_{n}(x,p)\,,\qquad\qquad O_{n}^{I}(x)\ \mapsto\ O_{n}(x,y)\,.

(37)

Here
p
is the momentum of excitations and
y
is a conjugated five-dimensional coordinate. The two operators
T_{n}(x,p)
and
O_{n}(x,y)
are related to each other through a Fourier transform.

Since the correlation functions (
35

\displaystyle\big\langle O_{n}(x_{1},y_{1})\bar{O}_{n}(x_{2},y_{2})\big\rangle={{\cal G}_{n}\over|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{2n}}f_{n}(y_{1}-y_{2})\,.

(38)

The function

is related to
f_{Y}
defined in (

L
and

go to infinity with the ratios

and
g/L

The three-point function has the similar form, see (

In the following sections, we compute the two-point correlation function (

and identify the corresponding function

of the additional

coordinates. As mentioned in the Introduction, the dependence of (

Correlations at strong coupling

In this section, we study the long-quiver limit of the correlation function (

According to (

), the function
f_{Y}
is given by the sum of
R_{\alpha,n}
over possible values of the quasimomentum (
34

We recall that
R_{\alpha,n}
is protected for

Replacing

(89)

where the first term on the right-hand side comes from
\alpha=0

Replacing
s_{\alpha}
with its definition (

and

\displaystyle f_{Y}={1\over L}+{(n-1)nL\over 4g^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{3}-\frac{2Y(L-Y)}{L^{2}}\right]+O(1/g^{3})\,,

We observe that, at large

In the opposite limit, for

Computing the function
f_{Y}
for

. To start with, we use (
26

\displaystyle f_{Y}=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\alpha=-L/2}^{L/2-1}e^{\frac{2\pi i\alpha}{L}Y}R_{\alpha,n}(g)\,.

(91)

At large

and

can be replaced by its asymptotic expression (
82
) in the limit (

\displaystyle f_{Y}=\frac{(n-1)n}{g^{2}L}\sum_{\alpha=-L/2}^{L/2-1}e^{ip_{\alpha}Y}\,\frac{{\mathrm{I}}_{n}(2gp_{\alpha})}{p_{\alpha}^{2}\,{\mathrm{I}}_{n-2}(2gp_{\alpha})}\,.

(92)

The sum runs over possible values of the quasimomentum (

-\pi\leq p_{\alpha}<\pi

Strictly speaking, the sum should be restricted to
|\alpha/L|\ll 1

|p_{\alpha}|\ll 1

p_{\alpha}=O(1)
to (

compared to contribution from small

Note that the relation (

and

with their ratio

held fixed. For

replacing the ratio of the Bessel functions for

with their expansion at large argument, we arrive at (

\displaystyle f_{Y}=\frac{(n-1)n}{g^{2}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}{dp\over 2\pi}e^{ipY}\,\frac{{\mathrm{I}}_{n}(2gp)}{p^{2}\,{\mathrm{I}}_{n-2}(2gp)}\,.

(93)

6.1
The long quiver limit at strong coupling

We have shown in Sect.
4
that, in the weak coupling regime, the function
f_{Y}
decreases exponentially with the distance
Y
in the long quiver limit
L\to\infty
. As follows from the above consideration, the properties of this function crucially depend on the ratio
L/g
.

For
L/g\ll 1
, the function
f_{Y}
is described by (
90
). It depends on the quiver length
L
and its leading
O(1/g^{2})
correction displays polynomial dependence on
Y
. We recall that in this regime the function
f_{Y}
describes correlations in the lattice model in the limit when all sites are strongly correlated.

For
L/g\gg 1
, we apply (
93
) and change the integration variable
p\to p/(2g)
to find at large
g

\displaystyle f_{Y}=\frac{2(n-1)n}{g}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{dp\over 2\pi}e^{ipy}\,\frac{{\mathrm{I}}_{n}(p)}{p^{2}\,{\mathrm{I}}_{n-2}(p)}\,,

(94)

where we introduced
y=Y/(2g)
. Note that
y
takes finite values in the long quiver limit.

The relation (
94
) should be compared with the analogous result (
63
) in the weak coupling regime.
In both cases, the leading large-
Y
behaviour of the integral over
p
is determined by the singularity of the integrand closest to the origin.
At weak coupling, this singularity arises from a pole at
p=i\mu_{n}
, resulting in (
63
). At strong coupling, the singularities originate from the zeros of the Bessel function
{\mathrm{I}}_{n-2}(p)
within the integrand of (
94
). They are located along the imaginary axis at

p=im_{k}
, where
m_{k}
is the
k
-th zero of the Bessel function
J_{n-2}(x)

\displaystyle J_{n-2}(m_{k})=0\,,\qquad\qquad(k=1,2,\dots)\,.

(95)

To avoid clutter, we do not display the dependence of
m_{k}
on
n
.

The ratio of the Bessel functions in (
94
) can be expanded into an infinite sum over poles at
p=\pm im_{k}

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathrm{I}}_{n}(p)}{p^{2}{\mathrm{I}}_{n-2}(p)}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4(n-1)}{m_{k}^{2}\left(p^{2}+m_{k}^{2}\right)}\,.

(96)

Each term on the right-hand side contains the factor of
1/(p^{2}+m_{k}^{2})
which can be interpreted as an Euclidean one-dimensional propagator of a particle with momentum
p
and mass
m_{k}
. Note that the sum in (
96
) does not contain a massless pole

.

Substituting (
96

94

f_{Y}={4n(n-1)^{2}\over g}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{{e}^{-ym_{k}}}{m_{k}^{3}}\,.

(97)

Here the right-hand side is given by the sum of one-dimensional propagators of particles with mass
m_{k}
. Being zeros of the Bessel function (
95
), the masses are distinct
m_{1}<m_{2}<\dots
and do not admit a simple closed-form expression.
At large
y
, the sum in (
97
) is dominated by the term involving the minimal mass
m_{1}

\displaystyle f_{Y}\sim e^{-ym_{1}}=e^{-Ym_{1}/(2g)}\,.

(98)

Remarkably, this relation exhibits the same exponential dependence on the distance between the nodes
Y
as in the weak coupling regime (
58
). According to (
98
), the corresponding mass scale
\mu_{n}
at strong coupling is given by

\displaystyle\mu_{n}={m_{1}\over 2g}\,.

(99)

Comparing this relation with (
58
), we observe that
\mu_{n}
decreases with increasing the coupling constant.

The function (
97
) describes the correlation function (
35
) of two operators placed at different nodes
Y=|I-J|
of the quiver diagram in Fig.
1
in the limit
L\gg g\gg 1
. In the context of the quiver lattice model, the exponential suppression of correlations (
98

indicates that the interaction in this model is short-ranged, with
1/\mu_{n}=O(g)
defining of the correlation length. This result is in an agreement with the findings of
[
26
]
.

6.2
Limit of heavy operators

Let us examine the relation (

) in the limit (

) when the scaling dimensions of the operators in (

The dependence on

enters the sum (

) through the zeros of the Bessel function (

, the zeros of the Bessel functions

have the following properties. The minimal zero

is given by (see
[

]
, Eq. (10.21.32))

\displaystyle m_{1}=n+1.85575\,n^{1/3}+O(n^{0})\,.

(100)

The remaining zeros approach a nearly uniform distribution with a spacing of approximately

. Taking these properties into account we
get from (

\displaystyle f_{Y}={4\over g}e^{-yn}+\dots={4\over g}e^{-Yn/(2g)}+\dots\,,

(101)

where dots denote subleading corrections suppressed by powers of

The corresponding mass scale (

\displaystyle\mu_{n}={n\over 2g}=\hat{n}\,.

Thus, for the operators with large scaling dimension the correlation length

7
Constructing a five-dimensional theory

In the previous sections, we computed the function
f_{Y}
determining the dependence of the correlation functions (
35
) on the separation between the nodes in the quiver diagram in Fig.
1
. We demonstrated that in the limit of long quiver, for
L\gg g\gg 1
with the ratio
y=Y/(2g)
held fixed, this function approaches a finite value
f_{n}(y)=\lim f_{Y}
given by (
97
). The resulting expression for the two-point function takes the form (
38
).

The question arises whether the relation (
38
) can be interpreted as a correlation function in some effective five-dimensional theory in which one additional dimension arises from the quiver diagram in the limit
L\to\infty
.

7.1
Källén-Lehmann representation

As the first step in this direction, we apply (
97
) to identify the spectrum of excitations propagating in the fifth dimension.

Let us define a one-dimensional Euclidean propagator of a scalar particle with a mass
m

\displaystyle D(y,m)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{dp\over 2\pi}{e^{ipy}\over p^{2}+m^{2}}={e^{-|y|m}\over 2m}\,.

(103)

As was mentioned earlier, the function (
97
) is given by a linear combination of these propagators. This suggests to introduce a spectral density function

\displaystyle\rho_{n}(\mu^{2})=\frac{4(n-1)}{\mu^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\delta(\mu^{2}-m_{k}^{2})\,,

(104)

where the sum runs over zeros of the Bessel function (
95
). This function satisfies the relation

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}d\mu^{2}\rho_{n}(\mu^{2})=1\,,

(105)

which can be obtained from the identity (
96
) by examining the leading asymptotic behaviour on both sides for
p\to\infty
.

Combining the above relations, we obtain the Källén-Lehmann representation of the function (
97
)

\displaystyle f_{n}(y)={2n(n-1)\over g}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\mu^{2}\rho_{n}(\mu^{2})D(y,\mu)\,.

(106)

Substitution of this relation into (
38
) results in a five-dimensional representation of the two-point function
\langle O_{n}(x_{1},y_{1})\bar{O}_{n}(x_{2},y_{2})\rangle
in the long quiver limit.

The observed factorization of the correlation function (
38
) into a product of functions solely dependent on the four-dimensional coordinates,
x
, and the fifth-dimensional coordinate,
y
, implies the decoupling of the corresponding excitations. Consequently, the excitations propagating in the fifth dimension acquire mass, while the four-dimensional excitations remain massless, thereby preserving the conformal invariance of the four-dimensional theory.

7.2
Three-point functions

So far our discussion was restricted to the two-point functions (
35
). Let us extend analysis to the three-point function (
11b
).

Applying a discrete Fourier transform (
1
) to each operator in (
2b
), we obtain the following representation for the three-point function

\displaystyle\big\langle O_{n_{1}}(x_{1},y_{1})O_{n_{2}}(x_{2},y_{2})\bar{O}_{n_{3}}(x_{3},y_{3})\big\rangle={\cal G}_{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}\dfrac{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}{N}{f_{\bm{n}}(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})\over|x_{1}-x_{3}|^{2n_{1}}|x_{2}-x_{3}|^{2n_{2}}}\,.

(107)

The normalization factor
{\cal G}_{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}=\delta_{n_{1}+n_{2},n_{3}}\sqrt{{\cal G}_{n_{1}}{\cal G}_{n_{2}}{\cal G}_{n_{3}}}
depends on the analogous factors defined in (
16
) and it is nonzero only when
n_{3}=n_{1}+n_{2}
. The function
f(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})
depends on the separations between the operators
y_{ij}=y_{i}-y_{j}
and their scaling dimensions
\bm{n}=(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})
. In a free theory, for zero coupling constant, it equals
1
for
y_{1}=y_{2}=y_{3}
and vanishes otherwise. For nonzero coupling constant, this function is given by (see (
30b
))

\displaystyle f_{\bm{n}}(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})={1\over L^{2}}\sum_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}=0}^{L-1}e^{{2\pi i\over L}(\alpha_{1}Y_{13}+\alpha_{2}Y_{23})}\prod_{i=1}^{3}e^{\mathcal{F}_{n_{i}}-\mathcal{F}_{n_{i}-1}}\,,

(108)

where
Y_{ij}=I_{i}-I_{j}=2g(y_{i}-y_{j})
. The functions
\mathcal{F}_{n_{i}}
depend on the quasimomenta
p_{i}=2\pi\alpha_{i}/L
satisfying
p_{\alpha_{1}}+p_{\alpha_{2}}+p_{\alpha_{3}}=0
(mod
2\pi
).

In the long quiver limit, the sums in (
108
) can be replaced by integrals over the quasimomenta leading to

\displaystyle f_{\bm{n}}(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dy_{0}\,G_{n_{1}}(y_{1}-y_{0})G_{n_{2}}(y_{2}-y_{0})G_{n_{3}}(y_{3}-y_{0})\,.

(109)

The function
G_{n}(y)
is given by a Fourier integral

\displaystyle G_{n}(y)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}{dp\over 2\pi}e^{ipY}e^{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n}-\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}}={n-1\over g}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{dp\over 2\pi}\,e^{ipy}\frac{{\mathrm{I}}_{n-1}(p)}{p\,{\mathrm{I}}_{n-2}(p)}\,,

(110)

where in the second relation we replaced
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
with its expression (
81
), changed the integration variable as

p\to p/(2g)
and took the limit of large
g
.
The integral in (
109
) is symmetric under the exchange of any pair of
(y_{i},n_{i})
and
(y_{j},n_{j})
.
It is interesting to note that the two-point function (
94
) admits a representation similar to (
109
)

\displaystyle f_{n}(y_{1}-y_{2})=2g\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dy_{0}\,G_{n}(y_{1}-y_{0})G_{n+1}(y_{2}-y_{0})\,.

(111)

It follows from the analogous relation between the integrands of (
94
) and (
110
).

The integral in (
110
) is similar to that in (
94
) and it can be evaluated in the same manner. We use the properties of the Bessel function to replace in (
110
)

\displaystyle{{\mathrm{I}}_{n-1}(p)\over p{\mathrm{I}}_{n-2}(p)}=\sum_{k\geq 1}{2\over p^{2}+m_{k}^{2}}\,,

(112)

where the sum runs over zeros of the Bessel function (
95
). In this way, we obtain the following representation of the function (
110
)

\displaystyle G_{n}(y){}

\displaystyle={2(n-1)\over g}\sum_{k\geq 1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{dp\over 2\pi}{e^{ipy}\over p^{2}+m_{k}^{2}}

\displaystyle={n-1\over g}\sum_{k\geq 1}{e^{-m_{k}|y|}\over m_{k}}={1\over 2g}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\mu\,\mu^{2}\,\rho_{n}(\mu^{2})D(y,\mu)\,,

(113)

where the spectral density is given by (
104
).

Similar to (
98
), the leading behaviour of
G_{n}(y)
at large
y
is determined by the minimal zero
m_{1}
of the Bessel function (
95
)

\displaystyle G_{n}(y)\sim{n-1\over g\nu_{n}}e^{-\nu_{n}|y|}\,,

(114)

where
\nu_{n}=m_{1}
depends on

(see (
100
)). Substituting this relation into (

f_{n}(y)\sim e^{-\nu_{n}|y|}
. In the similar manner, the three-point function (
109
)
is rapidly decreasing function for
y_{1}\gg y_{2},y_{3}

\displaystyle f_{\bm{n}}(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})\sim e^{-\nu\,y_{1}}\,,

(115)

where
\nu={\rm min}(\nu_{n_{1}},\nu_{n_{2}}+\nu_{n_{3}})
.

The relations (
114
) and (
115
) are in agreement with our expectations that, due to a finite mass of excitations propagating in the fifth dimensions, the correlation functions should be exponentially decreasing functions of the separations of the operators.

8
Conclusions and future perspectives

In this paper, we continued the study of four-dimensional
{\cal N}=2
circular, cyclic symmetric quiver theories initiated in
[
26
]
. In the limit of long quiver, the nodes becomes continuously distributed, suggesting a possible emergence of a five-dimensional theory through the deconstruction mechanism
[
30
]
.

To investigate this, we analyzed the two- and three-point functions (
2
) of chiral primary half-BPS operators. Using supersymmetric localization, these functions can be expressed as matrix integrals, which, in the planar limit, reduce to Fredholm determinants of semi-infinite matrices (
23
). This powerful representation enabled us to investigate the properties of the correlation functions across the parameter space of the quiver theory.

We analyzed the correlation functions in the weak and strong coupling regimes and in various limits of the number of nodes and operator scaling dimensions. We showed that their properties are naturally interpreted in terms of a one-dimensional lattice model (
12
) defined on the quiver diagram (Fig.
1
). The emergence of a fifth dimension in the long-quiver limit, understood as the continuum limit of this lattice model, crucially depends on the hierarchy between the ’t Hooft coupling
\lambda
and the number of nodes
L
. A fifth dimension arises only in the regime
L\gg\sqrt{\lambda}
, where interactions in the lattice model become short-ranged. Furthermore, the conformal symmetry of the quiver theory implies that the effective five-dimensional theory is not Lorentz invariant, with the dynamics in the fifth dimension decoupled from the four-dimensional dynamics.

At weak coupling, the two- and three-point correlation functions have a well-defined scaling behaviour in the limit
L\to\infty
. They decay exponentially fast with the node separation and the characteristic mass scale grows logarithmically with the ’t Hooft coupling. This scaling is consistent with the propagation of massive particles along the emergent fifth dimension.

At strong coupling, the semiclassical AdS/CFT expansion of the correlation functions diverges as
L\to\infty
. By including both perturbative corrections in
1/\sqrt{\lambda}
and an infinite tower of nonperturbative, exponentially suppressed corrections, we derived a remarkably simple expression for the correlation functions in the long quiver limit. These functions exhibit exponential behaviour with node separation which is analogous to that in the weak coupling regime. We computed the spectrum of masses for particles propagating along the emergent fifth dimension and found that they are given by the zeros of Bessel functions.

It would be interesting to extend the above analysis of the long quiver limit by going beyond the planar limit. For this purpose, it is necessary to develop an efficient approach to systematically calculate the non-planar corrections to the correlators in the matrix model (
12
). For the free energy and expectation value of circular Wilson loop this was done in
[
14
,
17
,
26
]
. The analytical control of the nonplanar corrections can shed new light on the holographic description of the emergent five-dimensional theory describing long quivers.

Another interesting direction is the study of high-point correlation functions of the conformal primary operators (
1
). Unlike two- and three-point functions (
2
), their dependence on four-dimensional coordinates is not fixed by conformal symmetry. In the long-quiver limit, these correlation functions depend on both four-dimensional cross-ratios and five-dimensional momenta. Determining these functions at strong coupling and interpreting them within the emergent five-dimensional theory remain challenging.

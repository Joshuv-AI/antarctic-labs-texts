Uncountable homogeneous structures
Adam Bartoš, Wiesław Kubiś

Abstract
We study the existence of uncountable first-order structures that are homogeneous with respect to their finitely generated substructures. In many classical cases this is either well-known or follows from general facts, for example, if the language is finite and relational then ultrapowers provide arbitrarily large such structures. On the other hand, there are no general results saying that uncountable homogeneous structures with a given age exist. We examine the monoid of self-embeddings of a fixed countable homogeneous structure and, using abstract Fraïssé theory, we present a method of constructing an uncountable homogeneous structure, based on the amalgamation property of this monoid. MSC: 08A35, 03C50. Keywords: Homogeneity; automorphism group; extensible embedding.

Introduction

A first-order mathematical structure
\mathbb{U}

In this note we present a new general method of constructing uncountable homogeneous structures, based on the properties of the monoid of self-embeddings of a fixed Fraïssé limit.

Closely related to homogeneity is the extension property, sometimes called
injectivity
, namely, given a finitely generated structure
B
and its finitely generated substructure
A

A
extends to an embedding of
B
.
While a countable injective structure is homogeneous, this is not necessarily the case for uncountable ones. In fact, the other extreme may occur: An uncountable injective structure may be rigid, that is, its automorphism group may be trivial or minimal possible (for instance, it may happen that a non-trivial involution is always an isomorphism).
There are many well-known constructions of rigid injective structures, e.g., linear orders (rigid dense subsets of the real line, see
[
2
]
,
[
23
]
), graphs, Boolean algebras, etc. (general consistent examples are provided in
[
17
]
).

Given a homogeneous structure
\mathbb{U}
, let
{\mathscr{E}{\mathbb{U}}}
denote the monoid of all embeddings of
\mathbb{U}
into itself, where the monoid operation is the composition of functions. It may happen that
{\mathscr{E}{\mathbb{U}}}
is trivial, that is, it only consists of the identity (equivalently,
\mathbb{U}
is rigid – see
2.3
), or that
{\mathscr{E}{\mathbb{U}}}
is degenerate, i.e. it coincides with
\operatorname{Aut}U
. A simple example of such a
\mathbb{U}
, in the relational language consisting of infinitely many unary predicates, is any structure in which each element belongs to exactly one of these predicates. Switching to an equivalent language of point colorings: each element has a unique color and each color appears at most once. The Fraïssé limit of the class of all finite structures with this property is an infinite structure where all colors appear. Clearly, the only embedding of this structure into itself is the identity.
It turns out that such an example with a degenerate monoid of self-embeddings may be constructed even in the language of groups, see Example
5.1
below.

We address the complementary situation. Given a countable homogeneous structure (a Fraïssé limit)
\mathbb{U}
such that
{\mathscr{E}{\mathbb{U}}}\neq\operatorname{Aut}\mathbb{U}
, that is, at least one self-embedding
e\colon\mathbb{U}\to\mathbb{U}
is not an isomorphism, we consider building an uncountable structure as an increasing union of copies of
\mathbb{U}
.
It is well-known that Fraïssé limits are stable under unions of countable chains. Therefore, using transfinite compositions of
e
, one can easily construct an uncountable structure with the same class (up to isomorphism) of finitely generated substructures as
\mathbb{U}
, satisfying the extension property (for details, see Proposition
2.2
below).
Nevertheless, in such general constructions one cannot control automorphisms and one can even end up with a rigid structure with the extension property.

Recall that the
amalgamation property
says that two embeddings with the same domain can be completed by embeddings to a commutative square.
The
age
(that is, the class of all finitely generated substructures) of a homogeneous structure clearly has the amalgamation property and in turn this property is crucial for the existence of the Fraïssé limit—a homogeneous structure with the given age.

As it happens, the amalgamation property does not always extend to infinite structures, see Section
5
below.
We say that a countable structure
M
is an
amalgamation base
if every two embeddings of
M
into countable structures (from a given class) can be completed to a commutative square. In the case
M
being a Fraïssé limits, there is an easier characterization of this property. Namely,
if
M
is a Fraïssé limit, being an amalgamation base is equivalent to saying that for every two embeddings
f\colon M\to M
,
g\colon M\to M
there exist embeddings
f^{\prime}\colon M\to M
,
g^{\prime}\colon M\to M
such that
f^{\prime}\circ f=g^{\prime}\circ g
. Indeed, amalgamation gives embeddings
f_{1}
,
g_{1}
into some countable structure
W
and by the universality of
M
, there is an embedding
e\colon W\to M
so we may take
f^{\prime}=e\circ f_{1}
,
g^{\prime}=e\circ g_{1}
.
Summarizing, if
\mathbb{U}
is a countable homogeneous structure then the information whether it is an amalgamation base is contained in the monoid of its self-embeddings.

Below is a simplified version of our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
\mathbb{U}
is a countable homogeneous structure in a countable first-order language, such that
\mathbb{U}
is an amalgamation base and
{\mathscr{E}{\mathbb{U}}}\neq\operatorname{Aut}\mathbb{U}
. Then there exists a homogeneous structure of size
\aleph_{1}
that has the same age as
\mathbb{U}
.

Note that in the case of a finite relational language, the ultraproduct construction gives us a required structure of size
2^{\aleph_{0}}
(Corollary
3.2
), and then we can take an elementary submodel to obtain a homogeneous structure of size
\aleph_{1}
. The point of Theorem
1.1
is to deal with the countably infinite
languages, possibly with function symbols.

In the proof we are using abstract Fraïssé theory (see e.g.
[
18
]
), building countable sequences in the monoid
{\mathscr{E}{\mathbb{U}}}
, obtaining new non-trivial self-embeddings of
\mathbb{U}
that extend automorphisms.
The key property resembles
naturality
(see
[
11
,
12
]
and Section
3
below), that is, uniform constructions of embeddings
e\colon\mathbb{U}\to F(\mathbb{U})
such that every automorphism of
\mathbb{U}
extends to
F(\mathbb{U})
. Nonetheless, we do not require compositions to be preserved.

Let us mention that our method is fairly general and it does not use almost anything from model theory, except the fact that we can distinguish small structures from large ones and compute their cardinalities.

The paper is organized as follows. After Preliminaries, we review some known results. In Section
4
we present the main technical lemma proving our main result Theorem
4.7
. Section
5

2
Preliminaries

Let
\delta
be an ordinal. By a
\delta
-
sequence
of structures we mean a function
\vec{u}
with domain
\delta
, where
\vec{u}(\alpha)
is a first-order structure denoted by
U_{\alpha}
and for every
\alpha<\beta<\delta
there is an embedding
u_{\alpha}^{\beta}\colon U_{\alpha}\to U_{\beta}
. Furthermore,
u_{\alpha}^{\alpha}={\operatorname{id}_{U_{\alpha}}}
and
u_{\alpha}^{\gamma}=u_{\beta}^{\gamma}\circ u_{\alpha}^{\beta}
whenever
\alpha<\beta<\gamma
.

We mostly work with sequences of structures that are simply a chain, namely,
U_{\alpha}
is a substructure of
U_{\beta}
and
u_{\alpha}^{\beta}
is the corresponding inclusion, whenever
\alpha<\beta
.

If
\delta>0
is a limit ordinal, we can consider the
colimit
of
\vec{u}
which in the case of a chain is simply its union.
A sequence
\vec{u}
with domain
\delta
is
continuous
if for every limit ordinal
\varrho<\delta
,
U_{\varrho}
is the colimit of
\vec{u}\restriction\varrho
. If
\vec{u}
is a chain, this simply means that
U_{\varrho}=\bigcup\{U_{\xi}\}_{\xi<\varrho}
for every limit ordinal
\varrho<\delta
.
For more details concerning functors and their limits/colimits we refer to
[
20
]
. We are going to use only the very basic concepts of category theory.

Given a first-order structure
M
, we denote by
\operatorname{Fin}(M)
its
age
, namely, the class of all finitely generated structures embeddable into
M
. Fraïssé Theorem states that a countably generated homogeneous structure is determined by its age. Furthermore, a class
{\cal{F}}
of finitely generated structures is the age of a countable homogeneous structure if and only if it is hereditary, has the amalgamation property (see below), the joint embedding property, and has countably many isomorphism types. Such a class is called a Fraïssé class and the countably generated homogeneous structure whose age is
{\cal{F}}
, is called the Fraïssé limit of
{\cal{F}}
. Note that the last two conditions of being a Fraïssé class are automatically fulfilled whenever the language is purely relational and finite, in which case the Fraïssé limit is countable. The Fraïssé limit of a Fraïssé class
{\cal{F}}
will typically be denoted by
\mathbb{U}
.

Recall that the
amalgamation property
of a class
{\cal{K}}
of structures means that for every two embeddings
f\colon Z\to X
,
g\colon Z\to Y
with
Z,X,Y\in{\cal{K}}
there exist
W\in{\cal{K}}
and embeddings
f^{\prime}\colon X\to W
,
g^{\prime}\colon Y\to W
such that
f^{\prime}\circ f=g^{\prime}\circ g
.

Given a class
{\cal{F}}
of first-order structures, we denote by
\sigma{\cal{F}}
the class of all structures isomorphic to the unions of countable chains in
{\cal{F}}
.
In the case that
{\cal{F}}
is a Fraïssé class,
\sigma{\cal{F}}
is just the class of all countably generated structures whose age is contained in
{\cal{F}}
.
We denote by
\overline{{\cal{F}}}
the class of all structures whose age is contained in
{\cal{F}}
.

In what follows, we shall always assume that the language of the structures under consideration is countable. In particular, countably generated structures are countable.

The monoid of self-embeddings of a structure
M
will be denoted by
{\mathscr{E}{M}}
.
A monoid will be called
non-degenerate
if it is not a group, namely, it contains at least one non-invertible element. A homomorphism (typically, an embedding) will be called
non-trivial
if it is not an isomorphism.

Recall that a structure
X
is
injective
if for every two structures
A\subseteq B
in
\operatorname{Fin}(X)
, every embedding
e\colon A\to X
can be extended to an embedding
\tilde{e}\colon B\to X
.
The limit
\mathbb{U}
of a Fraïssé class
{\cal{F}}
can also be characterized as being injective, countable and with age
{\cal{F}}
.
Because of this and the fact that injectivity is preserved by colimits of sequences, we obtain the following.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\delta
be any ordinal of cofinality
\omega
.
The colimit of any
\delta
-sequence
\vec{u}
of structures, each of them being isomorphic to
\mathbb{U}
, is again isomorphic to
\mathbb{U}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality,
\delta=\omega
as any cofinal subsequence of
\vec{u}
has the same colimit as
\vec{u}
.
Then clearly the colimit
U_{\infty}
is countable.
As
u_{0}^{\infty}\colon\mathbb{U}\to U_{\infty}
, we have
\operatorname{Fin}(U_{\infty})\supseteq{\cal{F}}
.
Since for every embedding of a finitely generated structure
f\colon A\to U_{\infty}
there is an embedding
f^{\prime}\colon A\to U_{n}
for some
n\in\omega
, we have
\operatorname{Fin}(U_{\infty})\subseteq{\cal{F}}
.
Moreover, for every
B\supseteq A
in
{\cal{F}}
there is an embedding
f^{\prime\prime}\colon B\to U_{n}
extending
f^{\prime}
since
U_{n}\approx\mathbb{U}
is injective.
Hence,
\tilde{f}=u^{\infty}_{n}\circ f^{\prime\prime}
extends
f
, and therefore
U_{\infty}
is injective.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

The following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The monoid
{\mathscr{E}{\mathbb{U}}}
is non-degenerate, i.e., there is a non-trivial self-embedding
e\colon\mathbb{U}\to\mathbb{U}
.

(2)

There is an uncountable injective structure
X
with age
{\cal{F}}
.

Proof.

If
e\colon\mathbb{U}\to\mathbb{U}
is a non-trivial embedding, with the use of Lemma
2.1
we can build a continuous
\omega_{1}
-sequence
\vec{u}
such that
U_{\alpha}=\mathbb{U}
and
u^{\alpha+1}_{\alpha}=e
for every
\alpha\in\omega_{1}
. Then consider its colimit
U_{\omega_{1}}
.
Clearly
U_{\omega_{1}}
is of cardinality
\aleph_{1}
.
As in the proof of Lemma
2.1
we argue that
U_{\omega_{1}}
is injective and has age
{\cal{F}}
.

Conversely, suppose
X
is an uncountable injective structure with age
{\cal{F}}
.
We can write
\mathbb{U}
as an increasing union of finitely generated substructures
\bigcup_{n\in\omega}A_{n}
.
There is an embedding
f_{0}\colon A_{0}\to\mathbb{U}
.
Since
X
is injective, we can find a sequence of embeddings
f_{n}\colon A_{n}\to X
,
n\in\omega
, each extending the previous one, and so
f=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}f_{n}
embeds
\mathbb{U}
into
X
.
As
X
is uncountable, there is a countable substructure
Y
such that
f[\mathbb{U}]\subsetneq Y\subseteq X
.
Since
\mathbb{U}
is universal,
Y
can be embedded into
\mathbb{U}
, and we obtain a non-trivial embedding
e\colon\mathbb{U}\to\mathbb{U}
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Note that if
\mathbb{U}
is rigid, i.e.
\operatorname{Aut}\mathbb{U}
is trivial, then
{\mathscr{E}{\mathbb{U}}}
is trivial as well.
If there was a non-trivial embedding
e\colon\mathbb{U}\to\mathbb{U}
, then there would be an automorphism of
\mathbb{U}
mapping a substructure
A
generated by a point
x\in\mathbb{U}\setminus\operatorname{rng}(e)
so its isomorphic copy in
e[\mathbb{U}]
.
Hence,
{\mathscr{E}{\mathbb{U}}}=\operatorname{Aut}\mathbb{U}=\{{\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{U}}}\}
.

3
A review of known results

As we have already mentioned, there are several well-known methods of constructing uncountable homogeneous structures, sometimes of arbitrary large size, sometimes possessing much stronger homogeneity, namely, with respect to infinitely generated substructures.

3.1
Ultraproducts

As one may expect, ultraproducts often preserve homogeneity:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\kappa\geq\aleph_{0}
be a cardinal and suppose that

\langle M_{\alpha}:\,\alpha<\kappa\rangle
is a sequence of homogeneous structures in a fixed finite relational language. Then for every ultrafilter
p
on
\kappa
, the ultraproduct
(\prod_{\alpha<\kappa}M_{\alpha})/_{p}
is homogeneous.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{M}=(\prod_{\alpha<\kappa}M_{\alpha})/_{p}
. Given
x\in\prod_{\alpha<\kappa}M_{\alpha}
, we denote by
x/_{p}
its equivalence class in
\mathbb{M}
. Given
S\subseteq\prod_{\alpha<\kappa}M_{\alpha}
, we write
S/_{p}=\{x/_{p}\colon x\in S\}
.
Let
g\colon\tilde{A}\to\tilde{B}
be a finite isomorphism inside
\mathbb{M}
. We select sets of representatives
A
,
B
, so that
\tilde{A}=A/_{p}
,
\tilde{B}=B/_{p}
and
(A\cap B)/_{p}=\tilde{A}\cap\tilde{B}
.
Since the language is finite and relational,
without loss of generality (discarding a set of coordinates outside of
p
) we may assume that
A(\alpha):=\{a(\alpha)\colon a\in A\}
is canonically isomorphic to
A
. The same for
B
.
In particular, for each
\alpha<\kappa
the map
g_{\alpha}\colon A(\alpha)\to B(\alpha)
, defined by
g_{\alpha}(a(\alpha))=g(a)(\alpha)
is a finite isomorphism.
By the homogeneity of
M_{\alpha}
, there exists
h_{\alpha}\in{M_{\alpha}}
extending
g_{\alpha}
. Finally, the product
h=\prod_{\alpha<\kappa}h_{\alpha}
induces an automorphism of
\mathbb{M}
extending
g
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
{\cal{F}}
be a relational Fraïssé class in a finite language and let
\mathbb{U}
be its Fraïssé limit.
If
\mathbb{U}
is infinite then for every non-principal ultrafilter
p
on
\omega
, the ultrapower
\mathbb{U}^{\omega}/_{p}
is homogeneous, of cardinality continuum, and has age
{\cal{F}}
.

Proposition
3.1
actually shows that there are arbitrarily large homogeneous structures with age
{\cal{F}}
and of any given infinite cardinality (by taking
a large enough ultrapower and a standard closing-off argument, in order to make it smaller—see Section
4.1
for more details).
Note that if the language is infinite or just not relational, ultrapowers of a Fraïssé limit may have bigger ages than
\mathbb{U}
. For example, any non-trivial ultrapower of Hall’s group (the Fraïssé limit of finite groups) contains elements of infinite order, therefore is not locally finite.
On the other hand, Keisler
[
16
]
proved that arbitrary ultraproducts are
\aleph_{0}
-saturated. Note that a countably infinite
\aleph_{0}
-saturated structure is the Fraïssé limit of its age.

Remark 3.3
.

Our original approach to construct an uncountable homogeneous structure of the prescribed age was to use
simplified
(\omega,1)
-morasses
of Velleman
[
25
]
, which, unlike ultrapower, give control over the age, and can yield an injective structure, but we were not able to prove homogeneity.

In more detail, a simplified
(\omega,1)
-morass can be specified by the following data. First, there is an increasing sequence
\{n_{i}\}_{i\in\omega}
of natural numbers with two sequences of one-to-one maps
\vec{u}=\{u_{i}\colon n_{i}\to n_{i+1}\}
and
\vec{v}=\{v_{i}\colon n_{i}\to n_{i+1}\}
that ‘split’ every
n_{i}
, meaning that
n_{i}
can be written as the disjoint union of subsets
A<B
, with
B\neq\emptyset
, and
n_{i+1}
can be written as the disjoint union of
A^{\prime}<B^{\prime}_{0}<B^{\prime}_{1}
, and
u_{i}
is the increasing bijection
n_{i}=A\cup B\to A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}_{0}\subseteq n_{i+1}
(which is the identity), while
v_{i}
is the increasing bijection
n_{i}=A\cup B\to A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}_{1}\subseteq n_{i+1}
.

Second, there are families
{\cal{G}}_{i}
of one-to-one maps
n_{i}\to\omega_{1}
,
i\in\omega
, satisfying certain conditions that will allow us to turn
\omega_{1}
into a structure once we turn every
n_{i}
into a structure and
\vec{u},\vec{v}
into sequences of embeddings.
Namely, for every
g\in{\cal{G}}_{i}
we copy the structure from
n_{i}
to
g[n_{i}]
via
g
, and this will correctly define a structure
{\mathbb{W}}
on
\omega_{1}
.
Note that given a structure on
n_{i}
, to specify a structure on
n_{i+1}
making
u_{i},v_{i}
embeddings means to provide a strong amalgamation of the given structures
A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}_{0}
and
A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}_{1}
over
A^{\prime}
.
If we do it in a way that
\vec{u}
becomes a Fraïssé sequence (cf.
[
18
, Section 3]
) in
{\cal{F}}
, then the structure
{\mathbb{W}}
on
\omega_{1}
will be injective with
\operatorname{Fin}({\mathbb{W}})=\operatorname{Fin}(\mathbb{U})
.

3.2
Natural constructions

Let
{\cal{K}}
be a class of structures in a fixed first-order language. According to Hodges and Shelah (
[
11
,
12
]
) a
natural construction
is a map
F
assigning to each
A\in{\cal{K}}
a bigger structure
F(A)\in{\cal{K}}
, possibly in an expanded language, such that every automorphism
h
of
A
extends to an automorphism
F(h)
of
F(A)
and the composition is preserved.
The papers
[
11
,
12
]
studied relations between naturality and definability.
For example, free constructions (left adjoints to a forgetful functor) are natural (even much more,
F
is a functor on all homomorphisms), while algebraic closures of fields are not.

Suppose that
F\colon{\cal{K}}\to{\cal{K}}
is a natural construction, with no expansion of the language.
Then we have a canonical inclusion
A\subseteq F(A)
up to isomorphism for every
A\in{\cal{K}}
. To simplify this explanation, let us assume that
A\subseteq F(A)
literally. Hence for any fixed
A\in{\cal{K}}
, we can iterate
F
to obtain a chain

A\subseteq F(A)\subseteq F^{2}(A)\subseteq\cdots.

Let
F^{\omega}(A):=\bigcup_{{n\in\omega}}F^{n}(A)
.
Naturality implies that for each
n
, every automorphism of
F^{n}(A)
extends to an automorphism of
F^{\omega}(A)
.

Now suppose that
{\cal{K}}
is stable under unions of
\omega
-chains. Then
F^{\omega}(A)\in{\cal{K}}
and we can iterate
F
in a transfinite way, obtaining an
\omega_{1}
-chain
\{F^{\alpha}(A)\}_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}
, taking unions at limit steps. Now, if
{\cal{K}}
consists of countable structures and each canonical inclusion
X\subseteq F(X)
is non-trivial (not the identity) then the structure
F^{\omega_{1}}(A):=\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}F^{\alpha}(A)
is uncountable.
Furthermore, again using naturality, we see that for each
\alpha<\omega_{1}
, every automorphism of
F^{\alpha}(A)
extends to an automorphism of
F^{\omega_{1}}(A)
. Since every isomorphism between finitely generated substructures of
F^{\omega_{1}}(A)
is “captured” by some
F^{\alpha}(A)
, we could conclude that
F^{\omega_{1}}(A)
is homogeneous, as long as
F^{\alpha}(A)
is homogeneous for an unbounded set of
\alpha<\omega_{1}
.

Summarizing, if
A
is a Fraïssé limit and
F(A)
is isomorphic to
A
then the same holds for every countable
\alpha
, and therefore we have a method of constructing uncountable homogeneous structures. Concrete cases are described below.

In fact, full naturality is not needed, in the construction above we only need to know that automorphisms extend. This will be further explored in Section
4
.

3.2.1
Rigid moieties

Let
{\cal{F}}
be a Fraïssé class and let
\mathbb{U}
be its Fraïssé limit. A
rigid moiety
is a pair
\langle A,B\rangle
where
A
is a substructure of
B
, both
A
and
B\setminus A
are countable infinite with age contained in
{\cal{F}}
, and every automorphism of
A
extends uniquely to an automorphism of
B
. Obviously, this becomes interesting when
B\approx\mathbb{U}
and even more when
A\approx\mathbb{U}
.
This concept was formally introduced by Bilge and Jaligot
[
1
]
, although the first existence result was due to Henson
[
9
]
, concerning graphs.
Jaligot
[
13
]
showed that rigid moieties exist in the class of tournaments, and later
Bilge and Jaligot
[
1
]
showed the same for the class of
K_{n}
-free graphs for every
n>2
. In particular, there exists a rigid moiety
\langle A,B\rangle
, where both
A
,
B
are isomorphic to Henson’s universal homogeneous
K_{n}
-free graph.

Given a rigid moiety
\langle A,B\rangle
with both
A
and
B
isomorphic to the Fraïssé limit
\mathbb{U}
, one immediately gets a natural construction on the class
{\cal{K}}
consisting of all structures isomorphic to
\mathbb{U}
.

Apart from the case of Henson’s universal homogeneous
K_{n}
-free graph described above, it is not known which Fraïssé classes admit rigid moieties with both components isomorphic to the Fraïssé limit.

3.2.2
Katětov functors

We briefly recall the main concept of
[
19
]
(going back to Katětov
[

]
) and its use for proving the existence of arbitrarily large homogeneous structures.

Fix a Fraïssé class

. A

on

is a functor
K\colon{\cal{F}}\to\sigma{\cal{F}}
, together with a collection of embeddings
\{\eta_{X}\colon X\to K(X)\}_{X\in{\cal{F}}}
, satisfying the following conditions.

(1)

Given an embedding

with
X,Y\in{\cal{F}}
, the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(Y)}

h\in H_{n+1}\subseteq H
.
Since
h[X_{m}]=X_{m}
for every
m>n
, we have that
h\restriction U
is a well-defined automorphism of
U
.
Hence,
U
is invariant under the action of
H
, and the action witnesses that
U
is homogeneous.
∎

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
{\mathbb{W}}
be an uncountable homogeneous structure of cardinality
\lambda
with age
{\cal{F}}
.
For every infinite
X\subseteq{\mathbb{W}}
of cardinality
\lambda_{0}<\lambda
there is an extensible chain
\{U_{\alpha},G_{\alpha},s_{\alpha}^{\beta}\}_{\alpha<\beta\leq\lambda}
such that
U_{0}\supseteq X
,
U_{\lambda}={\mathbb{W}}
, and
\min\{\alpha\leq\lambda:\lvert U_{\alpha}\rvert=\kappa\}=\kappa
for every cardinal
\kappa\in(\lambda_{0},\lambda]
.

Proof.

First we define strictly increasing continuous chains
\{U_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\leq\lambda}
and
\{H_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\leq\lambda}
.
By Lemma
4.10
we obtain
U_{0}
and
H_{0}
.
Let
\{w_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\lambda}
be an enumeration of
{\mathbb{W}}
.
Given
U_{\alpha}
and
H_{\alpha}
we apply Lemma
4.10
to
U_{\alpha}\cup\{w_{\alpha},x_{\alpha}\}
for some
x_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{W}}\setminus U_{\alpha}
and to
H_{\alpha}
. This way we obtain
U_{\alpha+1}
and
H_{\alpha+1}
.
For
\alpha\leq\lambda
limit we put
U_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}U_{\beta}
and
H_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}H_{\beta}
.

Note that
\lvert U_{\alpha+1}\rvert=\lvert U_{\alpha}\rvert
for every
\alpha<\lambda
, so the cardinality can increase only at limit steps.
For limit
\alpha\leq\lambda
we inductively have

\textstyle\lvert H_{\alpha}\rvert\leq\sum_{\beta<\alpha}\lvert H_{\beta}\rvert\leq\sum_{\beta<\alpha}\lvert U_{\beta}\rvert=\lvert\alpha\rvert+\sup_{\beta<\alpha}\lvert U_{\beta}\rvert=\lvert U_{\alpha}\rvert,

where the equalities follow from standard cardinal arithmetics and are proved as in the proof of Lemma
4.5
.
Hence, the assumption
\lvert H_{\alpha}\rvert\leq\lvert U_{\alpha}\rvert
gets preserved at limit steps,
we have
\min\{\alpha\leq\lambda:\lvert U_{\alpha}\rvert=\kappa\}=\kappa
for every cardinal
\kappa\in(\lambda_{0},\lambda]
, and
U_{\alpha}\subsetneq{\mathbb{W}}
for every
\alpha<\lambda
, and so the construction may proceed.
Moreover,
U_{\lambda}={\mathbb{W}}
as it contains every point
w_{\alpha}
.

Every
U_{\alpha}
is homogeneous and invariant under the action of
H_{\alpha}
, and the homogeneity is witnessed by
G_{\alpha}=\{h\restriction U_{\alpha}:h\in H_{\alpha}\}
.
This is true at
0
and at successor steps by Lemma
4.10
and remains automatically true at limit steps by continuity.

Finally we need to define the coherent family
\{s_{\alpha}^{\beta}\colon G_{\alpha}\to G_{\beta}\}_{\alpha<\beta\leq\lambda}
.
Let
r_{\alpha}\colon H_{\alpha}\to G_{\alpha}
denote the restriction
h\mapsto h\restriction U_{\alpha}
for every
\alpha\leq\lambda
.
We shall define sections
e_{\alpha}\colon G_{\alpha}\to H_{\alpha}
, i.e. maps satisfying
r_{\alpha}\circ e_{\alpha}={\operatorname{id}_{G_{\alpha}}}
, or equivalently
g\subseteq e_{\alpha}(g)
for every
g\in G_{\alpha}
.
Then we put
s^{\beta}_{\alpha}=r_{\beta}\circ e_{\alpha}
for every
\alpha<\beta\leq\lambda
.
The coherence condition
s^{\gamma}_{\beta}\circ s^{\beta}_{\alpha}=s^{\gamma}_{\alpha}
follows from the coherence condition
e_{\beta}\circ s^{\beta}_{\alpha}=e_{\alpha}
, which we assure by an inductive definition of the sections
e_{\alpha}
.

We simply put
e_{\beta}(h)=e_{\alpha}(g)
whenever
h=s^{\beta}_{\alpha}(g)
for some
\alpha<\beta
and
g\in G_{\alpha}
(and define
e_{\beta}(h)
arbitrarily so that it is a section, for
h
not of the form
s^{\beta}_{\alpha}(g)
), but we need to show that the definition is correct.
First,
h=s^{\beta}_{\alpha}(g)
uniquely determines
g
as
g=r_{\alpha}(h)
since
r_{\alpha}\circ r_{\beta}=r_{\alpha}
.
Second, if
s^{\beta}_{\alpha}(g)=s^{\beta}_{\alpha^{\prime}}(g^{\prime})
for some
\alpha<\alpha^{\prime}<\beta
and
g\in G_{\alpha}
,
g^{\prime}\in G_{\alpha^{\prime}}
, then
s^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\alpha}(g)=g^{\prime}
since
s^{\beta}_{\alpha}=s^{\beta}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\circ s^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\alpha}
by the induction hypothesis and since we may cancel the injective map
s^{\beta}_{\alpha^{\prime}}
.
Hence,
e_{\alpha^{\prime}}(g^{\prime})=e_{\alpha^{\prime}}(s^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\alpha}(g))=e_{\alpha}(g)
by the induction hypothesis.
∎

Corollary 4.12
.

The family of cardinalities of uncountable homogeneous structures with age
{\cal{F}}
is downwards closed.

By combining Proposition
4.11
with Proposition
4.6
and with the fact that every countable homogeneous structure of age
{\cal{F}}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{U}
, we obtain the following characterization.

Corollary 4.13
.

There exists an uncountable homogeneous structure with age
{\cal{F}}
if and only if there exists an extensible
\mathbb{U}
-chain of length
\omega_{1}
.

Question 4.14
.

Suppose
{\mathscr{E}{\mathbb{U}}}
is non-degenerate.
Is there an extensible
\mathbb{U}
-chain of length
2
?
Namely, does there exist a non-trivial embedding

G

4.2
Amalgamating infinite structures

It is natural to ask when the amalgamation property holds for the class
\sigma{{\cal{F}}}
of countable infinite structures induced by the Fraïssé class
{\cal{F}}
.
Many classes have this property – see Propositions
4.15
and
4.17
below.
In fact, the question whether this always happens was informally asked by Michael Pinsker in 2020, during the third author’s visit at the TU Wien.
It was also asked much earlier (2011) by Itaï Ben Yaacov on MathOverflow, see
https://mathoverflow.net/questions/71389
.
Some counterexamples have already appeared there, including the one from Section
5.4
below. The next fact most likely belongs to the folklore of model theory; we present the proof just for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 4.15
.

Suppose that
{\cal{F}}
is a Fraïssé class in a finite relational language.
Then the class
\sigma{{\cal{F}}}
of all countable structures with age in
{\cal{F}}
has the amalgamation property.

Proof.

Fix
Z\in\sigma{{\cal{F}}}
and fix embeddings
f\colon Z\to X
,
g\colon Z\to Y
such that
X,Y\in\sigma{{\cal{F}}}
and
X\setminus f[Z]
,
Y\setminus g[Z]
are singletons.
We may even assume that the embeddings are inclusions, and
X=Z\cup\{a\}
,
Y=Z\cup\{b\}
.
Write
Z=\bigcup_{{n\in\omega}}Z_{n}
, where
Z_{0}\subseteq Z_{1}\subseteq\cdots
and
Z_{n}\in{\cal{F}}
for every
{n\in\omega}
.
We know that for every
{n\in\omega}
the embeddings
Z_{n}\subseteq Z_{n}\cup\{a\}
,
Z_{n}\subseteq Z_{n}\cup\{b\}
can be amalgamated, namely, there is a structure on
Z_{n}\cup\{a,b\}
extending both of them (possibly identifying
a
and
b
).
Since the language is finite, there are only finitely many possibilities.
Given an amalgamation of
Z_{n}\cup\{a\}
,
Z_{n}\cup\{b\}
, for every
k<n
there is a uniquely determined amalgamation of
Z_{k}\cup\{a\}
,
Z_{k}\cup\{b\}
, as these are just substructures of
Z_{n}\cup\{a,b\}
.
Thus we can order all the amalgamations this way, obtaining a tree with finite levels. The tree is obviously infinite, therefore by Kőnig’s Lemma it has an infinite branch that corresponds to an amalgamation of
X
and
Y
.

Finally, we proceed by induction, as every embedding of
Z
into a countable structure can be decomposed into a sequence of one-point extensions. ∎

Corollary 4.16
.

Let
{\cal{F}}
be a Fraïssé class in a finite relational language. Then there exists a homogeneous structure of cardinality
\aleph_{1}
whose age is
{\cal{F}}
.

The situation is totally different when we allow algebraic operations or infinitely many relations. We present examples in Section
5
; one of them is actually classical and well-known: the class of countable locally finite groups.

It is well-known that if the Fraïssé class
{\cal{F}}
has a finite relational language, then
\mathbb{U}
is
\omega
-categorical, and that if
\mathbb{U}
is
\omega
-categorical, then it is
\omega
-saturated, which is enough for the amalgamation property of countable structures.

Proposition 4.17
.

If
\mathbb{U}
is
\omega
-saturated, then
\sigma{\cal{F}}
has the amalgamation property.

Proof.

Let
X\geq A\leq Y
be structures from
\sigma{\cal{F}}
.
Without loss of generality,
X\leq\mathbb{U}
and
Y
is generated by
A\cup\{y\}
for some
y\in Y
.
Let
{\mathbb{W}}
be an
\omega_{1}
-saturated elementary extension of
\mathbb{U}
,
and let
p(y)
be the quantifier-free type of
y
in
Y
over
A
.
We have that
p(y)
is a type in
{\mathbb{W}}
over
A
since for every finite
B\subseteq A
the quantifier-free formulas from
p(y)
using only parameters from
B
are realized by
y
in the substructure of
Y
generated by
B\cup\{y\}
, and so also by its isomorphic copy in
\mathbb{U}
pointwise fixing
B
(which exists as
\mathbb{U}
is injective).
Since
{\mathbb{W}}
is
\omega_{1}
-saturated,
p(y)
is realized by some
y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{W}}
.
Hence,
Y^{\prime}\leq{\mathbb{W}}
generated by
A\cup\{y\}
is an isomorphic copy of
Y
, and so the substructure
Z
generated by
X\cup Y^{\prime}
is an amalgamation of
X\geq A\leq Y
.

It remains to show that
Z\in\sigma{\cal{F}}
, which reduces to
\operatorname{Fin}(Z)\subseteq{\cal{F}}
.
It is enough to show that
\operatorname{Fin}({\mathbb{W}})=\operatorname{Fin}(\mathbb{U})
.
Since
{\mathbb{W}}
is elementarily equivalent to
\mathbb{U}
and
\mathbb{U}
is
\omega
-saturated, for every
W\leq{\mathbb{W}}
generated by a finite tuple
\bar{w}
there is a tuple
\bar{w}^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{U}
such that
\langle\mathbb{U},\bar{w}^{\prime}\rangle
is elementarily equivalent to
\langle{\mathbb{W}},\bar{w}\rangle
, and so the substructure of
\mathbb{U}
generated by
\bar{w}^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
W
, hence
W\in\operatorname{Fin}(\mathbb{U})
.
∎

5
Examples

Below we give relevant examples related to the preservation of AP, and in particular an example of a Fraïssé limit in a countable relational language that is not an amalgamation base, but Proposition
4.6
applies, and so there is an uncountable homogeneous structure with the same age (Section
5.5
).

The first two examples belong to the folklore of group theory.
The first one actually presents a trivial monoid of embeddings.

5.1
Locally cyclic groups

Let
{\cal{G}}
be the class of all cyclic groups.
Then
\overline{{\cal{G}}}
is the class of all
locally cyclic groups, namely, groups in which every finite (equivalently: at most two-element) set generates a cyclic group.
It is well-known that, up to isomorphism,
\overline{{\cal{G}}}
consists of all quotients of subgroups of the rationals
{\mathbb{Q}}
. In particular,
\overline{{\cal{G}}}
does not contain any uncountable group. Obviously, the class
{\cal{G}}
fails the amalgamation. For example, no locally cyclic group can contain both
{\mathbb{Z}}
and
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
with
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{0,1\}
.

Now let
{\cal{G}}_{0}
consist of all
torsion-free
cyclic groups. So, up to isomorphism,

{\cal{G}}_{0}=\{\{0\},{\mathbb{Z}}\}.

Then
{\cal{G}}_{0}
is a Fraïssé class and its Fraïssé limit is
{\mathbb{Q}}
. Nevertheless, the monoid
{\mathscr{E}{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
is degenerate, as every self-embedding of
{\mathbb{Q}}
a multiplication by some rational
q
and so is an automorphism.
On the other hand, there is a canonical Katětov functor
K
on
{\cal{G}}_{0}
, namely,
K(\{0\})=K({\mathbb{Z}})={\mathbb{Q}}
, and the natural transformation is just the inclusion. Every embedding in
{\cal{G}}_{0}
corresponds to a multiplication of the nonnegative generator, which extends uniquely to an embedding—again multiplying by the same element, now in
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

5.2
Locally finite groups

Consider the class
{\cal{G}}
of all finite groups.
Recall that a group is
locally finite
if all its finitely generated subgroups are finite.

It is a non-trivial fact that
{\cal{G}}
has the amalgamation property (see
[
8
]
or
[
22
]
).
On the other hand,
\sigma{\cal{G}}
fails the amalgamation property, as the following example (extracted from
[
6
]
, although most likely much older) shows.

Let
G
be the group of all permutations of
{\mathbb{N}}
that have a finite support. Namely,
f\in G
if the set

\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon f(n)\neq n\}

is finite.
Clearly,
G
is a locally finite countable group, therefore it belongs to
\sigma{\cal{G}}
.
Let
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
be the permutation defined by
a(2k)=2k+1
,
a(2k+1)=2k
for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Next, let
b\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
be defined by
b(0)=0
,
b(2k-1)=2k
and
b(2k)=2k-1
for every
k>0
.
Let
G[a]
,
G[b]
denote the groups generated by
G\cup\{a\}
and
G\cup\{b\}
, respectively (as subgroups of the full permutation group of
{\mathbb{N}}
).
It is easy to verify that both
G[a]
and
G[b]
are locally finite, while in any amalgamation
ab
has an infinite order:
we have
ba(2k\ 2k+2)a^{-1}b^{-1}=(b(a(2k))\ b(a(2k+2)))=(2k+2\ 2k+4)
in every amalgamation.

This example leads to the following question.

Question 5.1
.

Does there exist an uncountable homogeneous locally finite group?

Note that there exist locally finite groups of arbitrary cardinality.
The question whether there exists a Katětov functor on the class of finite groups seems to be open, although results in the positive direction can be found
[
24
]
.

5.3
Anti-metric spaces

Here we describe a simple example of a relational Fraïssé class
{\cal{A}}
for which
\sigma{\cal{A}}
totally fails the amalgamation property.

Namely, a
natural anti-metric space
is a pair
(X,\varrho)
such that
\varrho\colon X^{2}\to{\mathbb{N}}
is a symmetric function,
\varrho(x,y)=0
if and only if
x=y
and the triangle inequality fails everywhere. The last condition means for every pairwise distinct
x,y,z\in X
, if

\varrho(x,y)\geq\max\{\varrho(x,z),\varrho(z,y)\}

then
\varrho(x,y)>\varrho(x,z)+\varrho(z,y)
.
In other words, if
0<a\leq b\leq c
denote “distances” in a triangle then
c>a+b
.

Let
{\cal{A}}
be the class of all finite anti-metric spaces and, as usual, let
\sigma{\cal{A}}
be the class of all countable ones.
It is clear that
{\cal{A}}
is a Fraïssé class that can be encoded in a countable relational language.
The amalgamation property of one-point extensions can be easily seen, noticing that one can also define the “distance” between the pair of “new” points to be a large enough natural number.

Proposition 5.2
.

The amalgamation property fails everywhere in
\sigma{{\cal{A}}}\setminus{\cal{A}}
, i.e. no
X\in\sigma{{\cal{A}}}\setminus{\cal{A}}
is an amalgamation base.

Proof.

Fix a countable infinite anti-metric space
(X,\varrho)
. We may assume that
X=\omega
.
Let
X_{a}=X\cup\{a\}
, where the anti-distance from
a
is defined by induction as follows.
We set
\varrho(a,0)=1
.
Having defined
\varrho(a,k)
for
k<n
, we define
\varrho(a,n)
to be a fixed natural number strictly greater than

\varrho(a,i)+\varrho(i,n)

for all
i<n
.
By this way,
\{0,\dots,n,a\}
is an anti-metric space for each
{n\in\omega}
and finally
X_{a}
becomes an anti-metric space.
Next, let
X_{b}=X\cup\{b\}
, where
b\notin X_{a}
and define
\varrho(b,n)=\varrho(a,n)+1
.
Again,
X_{b}
is an anti-metric space.

Suppose
Y=X\cup\{a,b\}
is an amalgam of
X_{a}
,
X_{b}
over
X
. Note that we are not allowed to glue
a
and
b
, because the distances are different.
On the other hand, if
\varrho(a,b)=k
then for a big enough
n
we have
\varrho(a,n)>k
and
\varrho(b,n)=\varrho(a,n)+1>k
, showing that that triangle inequality between
a,b,n
actually holds, a contradiction.
∎

5.4
Complete labeled graphs with no triangles

This is an example due to Grebík
[
5
]
, answering a question on the existence of Katětov functors (see
[
19
]
).

Namely, a
triangle-free complete graph
is a complete graph
G
in which each edge has assigned a fixed natural number (color) and there is no monochromatic triangle.
Denote by
{\cal{G}}
the class of all finite triangle-free complete graphs.
Clearly, this is a Fraïssé class in a countable relational language. The amalgamation property follows from the fact that finite graphs admit finitely many colors, therefore one can always amalgamate one-point extensions by assigning a new color to the new pair of vertices.

Proposition 5.3
.

The amalgamation property fails everywhere in
\sigma{{\cal{G}}}\setminus{\cal{G}}
.

Proof.

Let
X\in\sigma{\cal{G}}\setminus{\cal{G}}
.
There is a point
\infty\in X
such that for two points
x\neq y\in X\setminus\{\infty\}
the edges
\{x,\infty\}
and
\{y,\infty\}
have different colors.
Without loss of generality,
X=\omega\cup\{\infty\}
,
x=0
,
y=1
, and the color of
\{0,\infty\}
is
1
, while the color of
\{1,\infty\}
is
0
.
We consider two one-point extensions
X\cup\{a\}
and
X\cup\{b\}
, defined as follows.
Every edge
\{i,k\}
,
i\in\{a,b\}
,
k\in\omega
, has color
k
, and the edge
\{a,\infty\}
has color
0
, while
\{b,\infty\}
has color
1
.

The extensions are triangle-free as the only possibly monochromatic triangles are
\{a,0,\infty\}
and
\{b,1,\infty\}
.
The extensions cannot be identified as the colors of
\{a,\infty\}
and
\{b,\infty\}
disagree.
Finally, coloring the edge
\{a,b\}
by
n
will give rise to a monochromatic triangle
\{a,b,n\}
.
∎

Note that every natural anti-metric space can be regarded as a complete labeled graph by treating the distance
\varrho(x,y)\in{\mathbb{N}}
as the color of the edge
\{x,y\}
for
x\neq y
.
Obviously, the resulting graph will have no monochromatic triangles.

The last two examples (anti-metric spaces and complete labeled graphs) have a bound on cardinalities of structures. Namely, due to the Erdős–Rado Theorem, a structure of cardinality
>2^{\aleph_{0}}
, whose all pairs have labels from a countable set, admits a triangle (even an infinite substructure) that is monochromatic, namely, all the labels are the same. This clearly shows that there is no anti-metric space of cardinality strictly greater than the continuum. Similarly, there is no triangle-free complete graph of cardinality greater than the continuum.
There exists a natural anti-metric space of cardinality
2^{\aleph_{0}}
. Indeed, consider
2^{\omega}
and define
\varrho(x,y)=3^{n}
, where
n
is the minimal coordinate such that
x(n)\neq y(n)
.

5.5
The linearly ordered variant

We consider a linearly ordered modification of the previous example. In fact, the ordered version is the actual example of Grebík
[
5
]
.
Formally, by an ordered complete labeled graph we mean a structure
\langle X,c_{X},<_{X}\rangle
where
X
is a set,
c_{X}\colon[X]^{2}\to\omega
is a coloring of all edges, and
<_{X}
is a linear order on
X
.
As usual we often omit the subscripts of
c_{X}
and
<_{X}
, and we identify
X
with the whole structure.

Let
{\cal{G}}^{<}
denote the class of all finite triangle-free ordered complete labeled graphs, so
\sigma{\cal{G}}^{<}
consists of all countable triangle-free ordered complete labeled graphs, and
\overline{{\cal{G}}^{<}}
consists of all triangle-free ordered complete labeled graphs.
It is easy to see that
{\cal{G}}^{<}
is a Fraïssé class.
Let
U
denote its limit.
We have the following.

(1)

No
X\in\sigma{\cal{G}}^{<}\setminus{\cal{G}}^{<}
is an amalgamation base in
\sigma{\cal{G}}^{<}
, in particular
{\mathscr{E}{U}}
does not have the amalgamation property.
This is easy to see: consider two one-point extensions
X\cup\{a\}
and
X\cup\{b\}
such that
c(\{x,a\})=c(\{x,b\})
attains all values in
\omega
for
x\in X
, while for some
x\in X
we have
a<x
in
X\cup\{a\}
, but
x<b
in
X\cup\{b\}
.
Hence, the points
a
and
b
cannot be glued and putting
c(\{a,b\})=q
would introduce a monochromatic triangle for every
q\in\omega
.

(2)

There is no Katětov functor
\sigma{\cal{G}}^{<}\to\sigma{\cal{G}}^{<}
. This was proved by Grebík
[
5
]
.

(3)

For every countable subgroup
G\leq\operatorname{Aut}U
there is a non-trivial
G
-extensible embedding
e\colon U\to U
, and so there is an uncountable homogeneous triangle-free ordered complete labeled graph.

We adapt the proof of universality of
\operatorname{Aut}U
from
[
5
]
to show
(3)
.

A one-point extension of
X\in\overline{{\cal{G}}^{<}}
is without loss of generality of the form
X\cup\{\alpha\}
where
\alpha\colon X\to\omega
is the map defined by
\alpha(x)=c(x,\alpha)
, so to fully describe
X\cup\{\alpha\}
it is enough to remember the map
\alpha
and the linear order on
X\cup\{\alpha\}
denoted by
<_{\alpha}
.
A one-point extension
\alpha
of
X
is called

•

Q
-colored
for
Q\subseteq\omega
if
\alpha\colon X\to\omega
takes values only in
Q
,

•

finitary
if every color support
\alpha^{-1}(q)
for
q\in\omega
is finite.

Lemma 5.4
.

For every
X\in\overline{{\cal{G}}^{<}}
and co-infinite
Q\subseteq\omega
there is an extensible embedding
e\colon X\to E(X)
for some
E(X)\in\overline{{\cal{G}}^{<}}
with an extension operator
E\colon\operatorname{Aut}X\to\operatorname{Aut}E(X)
such that every finitary
Q
-colored one-point extension of
X
is uniquely realized in
E(X)
.

Proof.

Let
E(X)=X\cup\{\alpha:\alpha
a finitary
Q
-colored one-point extension of
X\}
and let
e\colon X\to E(X)
be the inclusion.
Clearly the canonical action of
\operatorname{Aut}X
on
X
can be naturally extended to an action on
E(X)
: for
g\in\operatorname{Aut}X
and
\alpha\in E(X)\setminus X
we let
g(\alpha)
be the coloring with fibers
g(\alpha)^{-1}(q)=g[\alpha^{-1}(q)]
for every color
q\in\omega
so that
c(\{x,\alpha\})=c(\{g(x),g(\alpha)\})
, and we let
<_{g(\alpha)}
be so that
x<_{\alpha}\alpha\Leftrightarrow g(x)<_{g(\alpha)}g(\alpha)
for every
x\in X
.
This defines the extension
E(g)
and the extension operator
E
.

It remains to turn
E(X)
into a triangle-free ordered complete labeled graph so that every
E(g)
is an automorphism of
E(X)
.
First we define a linear order on
E(X)
.
The order is already defined on every
X\cup\{\alpha\}
.
We extend the linear order on
X
to a linear order on
[X]^{<\omega}
: for
A\neq B\in[X]^{<\omega}
we put
A<^{\prime}B
if
|A|<|B|
or if
|A|=|B|=n
and
a_{j}<b_{j}
where
\{a_{i}:i<n\}=A
and
\{b_{i}:i<n\}=B
are
<
-increasing enumerations of
A
and
B
and
j=\min\{i<n:a_{i}\neq b_{i}\}
.
Then for
\alpha\neq\beta\in E(X)\setminus X
we put
\alpha<\beta
if there is
x\in X
with
\alpha<_{\alpha}x<_{\beta}\beta
or if
\{x\in X:x<_{\alpha}\alpha\}=\{x\in X:x<_{\beta}\beta\}
and
\alpha^{-1}(r)<^{\prime}\beta^{-1}(r)
where
r=\min\{q\in\omega:\alpha^{-1}(q)\neq\beta^{-1}(q)\}
. Here we are using that the color supports
\alpha^{-1}(q)
and
\beta^{-1}(q)
are finite.
This is a well-defined linear order that is invariant under automorphisms of
X
(i.e.
\alpha<\beta\Leftrightarrow g(\alpha)<g(\beta)
for every
g\in\operatorname{Aut}X
) since the base orders
<_{\alpha}
,
<_{\beta}
, and
<^{\prime}
are invariant.

Finally we need to color all the edges
\{\alpha,\beta\}
for
\alpha<\beta\in E(X)\setminus X
in a way such that every
g\in\operatorname{Aut}X
still induces an automorphism of
E(X)
while not creating any monochromatic triangles.
The invariance under automorphisms means that
c(\{g(\alpha),g(\beta)\})=c(\{\alpha,\beta\})
, i.e. every orbit of the action of
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
on two-point subsets of
E(X)\setminus X
has to be monochromatic.
Since
\omega\setminus Q
is infinite, we can color edges in different orbits by different colors from
\omega\setminus Q
.
This also means that monochromatic triangles may exist only in
E(X)\setminus X
.

We show that there are no monochromatic triangles in
E(X)\setminus X
.
Suppose for contradiction that
\alpha<\beta<\gamma
is a monochromatic triangle.
So there is an automorphism
g\in\operatorname{Aut}E(X)
that maps
\{\alpha,\beta\}\to\{\alpha,\gamma\}
.
Since
g
preserves the order, we have
g(\alpha)=\alpha
.
So
g
maps the finite linearly ordered set
\alpha^{-1}(q)
for
q\in\omega
onto itself, and hence
g
fixes
\alpha^{-1}(q)
pointwise.
Since
q\in\omega
was arbitrary, we have
g={\operatorname{id}_{X}}
and
\gamma=g(\beta)=\beta
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Of course even for a countable graph
X
the extension
E(X)
has cardinality continuum.
However we can stay countable if we restrict ourselves to countably many automorphisms and countably many one-point extensions, which turns out to be enough for our purposes.

Corollary 5.5
.

For every
X\in\sigma{\cal{G}}^{<}
, countable
G\leq\operatorname{Aut}X
, co-infinite
Q\subseteq\omega
, and a countable family of finitary
Q
-colored one-point extensions
A
, there is a
G
-extensible embedding
f\colon X\to Y
for some
Y\in\sigma{\cal{G}}^{<}
with an extension operator
G\to\operatorname{Aut}Y
such that every one-point extension
\alpha\in A
is uniquely realized in
Y
.

Proof.

Let
e\colon X\to E(X)
be the extensible embedding from the previous lemma.
Without loss of generality
A\subseteq E(X)\setminus e[X]
.
It is enough to take
Y=X\cup\{g(\alpha):\alpha\in A,g\in G\}
, i.e. we close
A
under the action of our countable subgroup
G
.
Then we let
f\colon X\to Y
be the restriction of
e\colon X\to E(X)
.
∎

Proof of
(3)
.

Let
G\leq\operatorname{Aut}U
be a countable subgroup.
Let us write
\omega
as an increasing union of subsets
\bigcup_{n\in\omega}Q_{n}
such that every
Q_{n+1}\setminus Q_{n}
is infinite.
We put
X_{0}=U
and
G_{0}=G
.
Suppose
X_{n}\in\sigma{\cal{G}}^{<}
and countable
G_{n}\leq\operatorname{Aut}X_{n}
are defined.
Let
A_{n}
be a countable family of finitary
Q_{n}
-colored one-point extensions of
X_{n}
such that every
Q_{n}
-colored one-point extension
\alpha_{0}
of every finite substructure
F\subseteq X_{n}
can be extended to an extension
\alpha\in A_{n}
.
This is possible: we put
\alpha(x)=\alpha_{0}(x)
for
x\in F
and for every
x\in X\setminus F
we use a different color from
Q_{n}\setminus\alpha_{0}[F]
for
\alpha(x)
.
Hence
X\cup\{\alpha\}
is triangle-free.
We can extend the order e.g. by putting
x<_{\alpha}\alpha
if there is
y\in F
such that
x\leq y<_{\alpha_{0}}\alpha_{0}
, so
\alpha
becomes the immediate successor of
\max\{y\in F:y<_{\alpha_{0}}\alpha_{0}\}
in
X\cup\{\alpha\}
.

Let
e_{n}\colon X_{n}\to X_{n+1}
be a
G_{n}
-extensible embedding with an extension operator
E_{n}\colon G_{n}\to\operatorname{Aut}X_{n+1}
obtained from Corollary
5.5
applied to
X_{n}
,
G_{n}
,
Q_{n}
, and
A_{n}
, and let
G_{n+1}=E[G_{n}]\leq\operatorname{Aut}X_{n+1}
.
Without loss of generality,
X_{n+1}
uses only colors from
Q_{n+1}
since otherwise we may change the coloring
c_{X_{n+1}}
so that every use of a color from
\operatorname{rng}(c_{X_{n+1}})\setminus Q_{n}
is replaced by a corresponding color from the infinite set
Q_{n+1}\setminus Q_{n}
.
This concludes the inductive definition.
Let
X_{\infty}
and the family of embeddings
e_{n}^{\infty}\colon X_{n}\to X_{\infty}
be the colimit of the sequence
\vec{e}
.
The embedding
e_{0}^{\infty}\colon U\to X_{\infty}
is non-trivial (as every
e_{n}
is non-trivial) and
G
-extensible: for every
g\in G
the maps
E_{0}^{n}(g)\in\operatorname{Aut}X_{n}
are extending each other via the bonding maps
e_{n}
, and so the colimiting map
g_{\infty}\in\operatorname{Aut}X_{\infty}
satisfies
g_{\infty}\circ e_{0}^{\infty}=e_{0}^{\infty}\circ g
.

It is enough to observe that
X_{\infty}
is isomorphic to
U
.
We show that
X_{\infty}
is injective.
Let
\alpha_{0}
be a one-point extension of a finite substructure
F\subseteq X_{\infty}
.
There is
n\in\omega
such that
F\subseteq X_{n}
and
\alpha_{0}
is
Q_{n}
-colored.
Hence, there is
\alpha\in A_{n}
extending
\alpha_{0}
, and so
\alpha_{0}
is realized in
X_{n+1}
.

Therefore, there is an uncountable homogeneous triangle-free ordered complete labeled graph by Proposition
4.6
.
∎

6
Final remarks

Let us note that our main result can be proved in a much more general setting, replacing embeddings by more abstract morphisms. The only ingredient needed is that partial isomorphisms between “small” structures are “captured” in intermediate steps. Of course, one also needs to specify what “uncountable” means. A good framework is an
\omega
-accessible category, in the sense of Grothendieck
[
7
]
(complemented by Gabriel and Ulmer
[
4
]
). In this setting finitely generated structures are finitely presentable objects and morphisms from them factor through directed colimits. The monoid of self-embeddings is simply a hom-set of the form
\operatorname{Hom}(X,X)
and all morphisms are typically assumed to be monomorphisms.

Coming back to our concrete setting, let us recall the main intriguing questions left open:

Question 6.1
.

Assume
{\cal{F}}
is a Fraïssé class such that there exist uncountable structures with age
{\cal{F}}
. Does there exist an uncountable homogeneous structure with age
{\cal{F}}
?

Question 6.2
.

Assume
\mathbb{U}
is a Fraïssé limit such that
{\mathscr{E}{\mathbb{U}}}
is non-degenerate. Does there exist a non-trivial extensible self-embedding of
\mathbb{U}
?

In fact, we do not know the answer to a weaker question, asking for
G
-extensibility for every countable
G\leq\operatorname{Aut}\mathbb{U}
.

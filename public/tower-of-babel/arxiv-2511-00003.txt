An unconditionally stable numerical approach for solving a nonlinear distributed delay Sobolev model
Eric Ngondiep

Abstract




Abstract.

This paper proposes an unconditionally stable numerical method for solving a nonlinear Sobolev model with distributed delay. The proposed computational approach approximates the time derivative by interpolation technique whereas the spatial derivatives are approximated using the finite element approximation. This combination is simple and easy to implement. Both stability and error estimates of the constructed method are deeply analyzed in a strong norm which is equivalent to the
H^{1}
-norm. The theoretical results indicate that the constructed approach is unconditionally stable, spatial fourth-order accurate, second-order convergent in time and more efficient than a large class of numerical methods discussed in the literature for solving a general class of delay Sobolev problems. Some numerical examples are carried out to confirm the theory and demonstrate the applicability and validity of the developed technique.

Keywords:
distributed delay, nonlinear Sobolev equation, a strong norm, high-order computational technique, unconditional stability and error estimates.

2
Description of the proposed computational technique

In this Section, we describe the proposed high-order numerical approach in an approximate solution of the nonlinear Sobolev equation with distributed delay
(\ref{1})
subjects to initial and boundary conditions
(\ref{2})
and
(\ref{3})
, respectively.

Let
m
and
N
be two positive integers. Set
\sigma=\frac{\tau}{m}
, be the time step. For the convenience of discretization we assume that the final time
T_{f}
is chosen so that
\sigma=\frac{T_{f}}{N}
. Consider
\Omega_{\sigma}=\{t_{n}=n\sigma,\text{\,}-m\leq n\leq N\}
, be a uniform partition of the time interval
[-\tau,\text{\,}T_{f}]
. Suppose that
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is the regular partition of the domain
\overline{\Omega}=\Gamma\cup\Omega
, consisting of triangles (or segments)
T
of diameter (or length)
d(T)
, where
h=\sup\{d(T),\text{\,\,}T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}
, satisfying the following restrictions: (a) the triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is regular while the triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma,h}
induced on the boundary
\Gamma
is quasi-uniform, (b) the interior of any triangle
T
is nonempty and the intersection of the interior of two different triangles is empty whereas the intersection of two triangles is empty, or a common vertex or edge.

We denote
H^{q}(\Omega)
, be the Sobolev space equipped with the scalar product
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{q}
and the corresponding norm is
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{q}
, where
q
is a nonnegative integer. Additionally, the space
L^{2}(\Gamma)
is endowed with the inner product
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{\Gamma}
and associated norm is
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\Gamma}
. We remind that the space
H^{0}(Q)
coincides with
L^{2}(Q)
, where
Q=\Omega
or
\Gamma
. Since the functions
f
and
g
are assumed to be smooth enough, the space containing the analytical solution of the nonlinear distributed delay Sobolev equation
(\ref{1})
with boundary condition
(\ref{3})
should be defined as

V=\{u\in H^{4}(0,T_{f};\text{\,}H^{5}(\Omega)):\text{\,\,}u|_{\Gamma}=\rho\}.

(4)

In addition, the finite element space approximating the solution of the parabolic equation
(\ref{1})
is given by

V_{h}=\{u_{h}(t)\in H^{1}(\Omega):\text{\,\,}u_{h}(t)|_{T}\in\mathcal{P}_{5}(T),\text{\,\,}\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\text{\,\,for\,\,}t\in[0,\text{\,}T_{f}]\},

(5)

where,
\mathcal{P}_{5}(T)
means the set of polynomials defined on
T
with degree less than or equal
5
. Let
l=(l_{1},...,l_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,
x=(x_{1},...,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
|l|=l_{1}+...+l_{d}
,
\partial x^{|l|}=\partial x_{1}^{l_{1}}...\partial x_{d}^{l_{d}}
,
D_{x}^{l}u=\frac{\partial^{|l|}u}{\partial x^{|l|}}
and
D_{t,x}^{s,l}u=\frac{\partial^{s+|l|}u}{\partial t^{s}\partial x^{|l|}}
, where
D_{t,x}^{0,0}u:=u
and
D_{x}^{0}u:=u
. Suppose that
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
, the Sobolev spaces
H^{q}(\Omega)
,
L^{\infty}(0,T_{f};\text{\,}H^{q})
and
H^{p}(0,T;\text{\,}H^{q})
, are defined as

H^{q}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega):\text{\,\,}D_{x}^{l}u\in L^{2}(\Omega),\text{\,\,for\,\,}|l|\leq q\right\},

(6)

L^{\infty}(0,T_{f};H^{q})=\left\{u\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{f};\text{\,}L^{2}):\text{\,\,}D_{x}^{l}u\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{f};\text{\,}L^{2}),\text{\,\,for\,\,}|l|\leq q\right\},

(7)

H^{p}(0,T_{f};\text{\,}H^{q})=\left\{u\in L^{2}(0,T_{f};\text{\,}\text{\,}L^{2}):\text{\,\,}D_{t,x}^{s,l}u\in L^{2}(0,T_{f};\text{\,}L^{2}),\text{\,\,}s=0,1,...,p,\text{\,\,}|l|\leq q\right\}.

(8)

Here: the spaces
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2}
are endowed with the scalar products
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{0}
and
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{\bar{0}}
, and associated norms
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{0}
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bar{0}}
, respectively.

\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)_{0}=\int_{\Omega}u_{1}u_{2}\text{\,}dx,\text{\,\,}\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{0}=\sqrt{\left(u_{1},u_{1}\right)_{0}},\text{\,\,\,\,\,}\forall u_{1},u_{2}\in L^{2}(\Omega),\text{\,\,and\,\,}

\left(z,w\right)_{\bar{0}}=\int_{\Omega}z^{t}w\text{\,}dx,\text{\,\,}\left\|w\right\|_{\bar{0}}=\sqrt{\left(w,w\right)_{\bar{0}}},\text{\,\,\,\,\,for\,\,}z,w\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2},

(9)

where
z^{t}
denotes the transpose of a vector-function
z\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2}
. Furthermore, the Sobolev spaces:
H^{q}(\Omega)
,
L^{\infty}(0,T_{f};\text{\,}H^{q})
and
H^{p}(0,T_{f};\text{\,}H^{q})
, are equipped with the following norms

\|u\|_{q}=\left(\underset{|m|\leq q}{\sum}\|D_{x}^{m}u\|_{0}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\text{\,\,\,\,}\forall u\in H^{q}(\Omega),\text{\,\,\,\,}\||w|\|_{q,\infty}=\underset{t\in[0,\text{\,}T_{f}]}{\max}\|w(t)\|_{q},\text{\,\,\,}\forall w\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{f};\text{\,}H^{q}),

\||w|\|_{q,p}=\left(\underset{l=0}{\overset{p}{\sum}}\int_{0}^{T_{f}}\underset{|m|\leq q}{\sum}\left\|D_{t,x}^{l,m}w(t)\right\|_{0}^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\text{\,\,\,\,\,}\forall w\in H^{p}(0,T_{f};\text{\,}H^{q}).

(10)

We introduce the bilinear operators
a(\cdot,\cdot)
and
a_{\Gamma}(\cdot,\cdot)
defined as:

a(u,w)=\left(\nabla u,\nabla w\right)_{\bar{0}},\text{\,\,\,}a_{\Gamma}(u,w)=\left(u,w\right)_{\Gamma}=\int_{\Gamma}u(\nabla w)^{t}\vec{z}\text{\,}d\Gamma,

(11)

where
\vec{z}
represents the unit outward normal vector on
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
. The following integration by parts will play an important role in our study.

\left(\Delta u,w\right)_{0}=a_{\Gamma}(u,w)-a(u,w),\text{\,\,\,for\,\,\,every\,\,\,}u\in H^{2}(\Omega)\text{\,\,\,and\,\,\,}w\in H^{1}(\Omega).

(12)

Finally, for the convenience of writing, we set
u^{n}=u(t_{n})
and
u:=u(t)\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, for any
t\in[0,\text{\,}T_{f}]
. Now, applying equation
(\ref{1})
at the discrete time
t_{n+1}
, for
n=0,1,2,...,N-1
, and using the fact that
\tau=t_{m}
, this gives

(\mathcal{I}-\beta\Delta)v_{t}^{n+1}-\alpha\Delta v^{n+1}=f\left(x,t_{n+1},v^{n+1},\int_{t_{n-m+1}}^{t_{n+1}}g(x,t_{n+1},s,v(s))ds\right).

(13)

Approximating the function
v(t)
at the discrete points:
(t_{n-1},\text{\,}v^{n-1})
,
(t_{n},\text{\,}v^{n})
and
(t_{n+1},\text{\,}v^{n+1})
, this yields

v(t)=\frac{(t-t_{n})(t-t_{n+1})}{(t_{n-1}-t_{n})(t_{n-1}-t_{n+1})}v^{n-1}+\frac{(t-t_{n-1})(t-t_{n+1})}{(t_{n}-t_{n-1})(t_{n}-t_{n+1})}v^{n}+\frac{(t-t_{n-1})(t-t_{n})}{(t_{n+1}-t_{n-1})(t_{n+1}-t_{n})}v^{n+1}+

\frac{1}{6}(t-t_{n-1})(t-t_{n})(t-t_{n+1})v_{3t}(\epsilon_{1}(t))=\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\left[(t^{2}-(t_{n}+t_{n-1})t+t_{n}t_{n-1})v^{n+1}-\right.

\left.2(t^{2}-(t_{n-1}+t_{n+1})t+t_{n+1}t_{n-1})v^{n}+(t^{2}-(t_{n}+t_{n+1})t+t_{n}t_{n+1})v^{n-1}\right]+\frac{1}{6}(t-t_{n-1})(t-t_{n})(t-t_{n+1})v_{3t}(\epsilon_{1}(t)),

where
\epsilon_{1}(t)
is between the maximum and the minimum of
t_{n-1}
,
t_{n}
,
t_{n+1}
and
t
. The time derivative of this equation provides

v_{t}(t)=\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\left[(2t-t_{n}-t_{n-1})v^{n+1}-2(2t-t_{n-1}-t_{n+1})v^{n}+(2t-t_{n}-t_{n+1})v^{n-1}\right]+

\frac{1}{6}[(t-t_{n-1})(t-t_{n})(t-t_{n+1})\frac{\partial}{\partial t}v_{3t}(\epsilon_{1}(t))+v_{3t}(\epsilon_{1}(t))\frac{d}{dt}((t-t_{n-1})(t-t_{n})(t-t_{n+1}))].

Performing direct calculations, it holds

v_{t}^{n+1}=\frac{1}{2\sigma}(3v^{n+1}-4v^{n}+v^{n-1})+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{3}v_{3t}(\epsilon_{1}(t)).

Substituting this equation into
(\ref{12})
and rearranging terms, this results in

\frac{1}{2\sigma}(\mathcal{I}-\beta\Delta)(3v^{n+1}-4v^{n}+v^{n-1})-\alpha\Delta v^{n+1}=f\left(x,t_{n+1},v^{n+1},\int_{t_{n-m+1}}^{t_{n+1}}g(x,t_{n+1},s,v(s))ds\right)-

\frac{\sigma^{2}}{3}(\mathcal{I}-\beta\Delta)v_{3t}(\epsilon_{1}(t)).

(14)

Multiplying both sides of equation
(\ref{13})
by
2\sigma
, for any
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, using the inner product
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{0}
, defined in relation

, and rearranging terms, we obtain

\left((\mathcal{I}-\beta\Delta)(3v^{n+1}-4v^{n}+v^{n-1}),u\right)_{0}-2\alpha\sigma\left(\Delta v^{n+1},u\right)_{0}=2\sigma\left(G(v^{n+1}),u\right)_{0}-\frac{2\sigma^{3}}{3}\left((\mathcal{I}-\beta\Delta)v_{3t}(\epsilon_{1}(t)),u\right)_{0},

(15)

where

G(v^{n+1})=f\left(x,t_{n+1},v^{n+1},\int_{t_{n-m+1}}^{t_{n+1}}g(x,t_{n+1},s,v(s))ds\right).

(16)

Utilizing the integration by parts given by equation
(\ref{11})
, it is not difficult to observe that equation
(\ref{14})
is equivalent to

\left(3v^{n+1}-4v^{n}+v^{n-1},u\right)_{0}+\beta a(3v^{n+1}-4v^{n}+v^{n-1},u)+2\alpha\sigma a(v^{n+1},u)-\beta a_{\Gamma}(3v^{n+1}-4v^{n}+v^{n-1},u)-

(17)

Let
A(\cdot,\cdot)
be the bilinear operator defined as

A(u,w)=\left(u,w\right)_{0}+\beta a(u,w),\text{\,\,\,\,\,\,\,}\forall u,w\in H^{1}(\Omega).

(18)

Using the operator
A(\cdot,\cdot)
together with the boundary condition
(\ref{3})
, that is,
v^{r}=\rho^{r}

\Gamma\times[0,\text{\,}T_{f}]

3A(v^{n+1},u)+2\alpha\sigma a(v^{n+1},u)=A(4v^{n}-v^{n-1},u)+\beta a_{\Gamma}(3\rho^{n+1}-4\rho^{n}+\rho^{n-1},u)+2\alpha\sigma a_{\Gamma}(\rho^{n+1},u)+

(19)

Since the term
G(v^{n+1})=f\left(x,t_{n+1},v^{n+1},\int_{t_{n-m+1}}^{t_{n+1}}g(x,t_{n+1},s,v(s))ds\right)

In addition, the interpolation of the function

\frac{1}{2}(s-t_{n+1+j})(s-t_{n+2+j})\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}[g(x,t_{n+1},\epsilon_{2}(s),v(\epsilon_{2}(s)))],

(21)

where
\epsilon_{2}(s)
is between the minimum and maximum of
t_{n+1+j}
,
t_{n+2+j}
and

. Using equation

\int_{t_{n+1+j}}^{t_{n+2+j}}g(x,t_{n+1},s,v(s))ds=\frac{1}{\sigma}\left[g(x,t_{n+1},t_{n+2+j},v^{n+2+j})\int_{t_{n+1+j}}^{t_{n+2+j}}(s-t_{n+1+j})ds-g(x,t_{n+1},t_{n+1+j},\right.

(22)

(24)

(25)

Setting

(26)

Here,

H(x,t_{n+1},\overline{\epsilon}_{2})=[g_{ss}(x,t_{n+1},\overline{\epsilon}_{2},v(\overline{\epsilon}_{2}))+(v_{s}(\overline{\epsilon}_{2})+v_{ss}(\overline{\epsilon}_{2}))g_{v}(x,t_{n+1},\overline{\epsilon}_{2},v(\overline{\epsilon}_{2}))\\
+v_{s}^{2}(\overline{\epsilon}_{2})g_{vv}(x,t_{n+1},\overline{\epsilon}_{2},v(\overline{\epsilon}_{2}))],

3(\|e_{h}^{n+1}\|_{\beta}^{2}-\|e_{h}^{n}\|_{\beta}^{2})-(\|e_{h}^{n}\|_{\beta}^{2}-\|e_{h}^{n-1}\|_{\beta}^{2})+2(\|e_{h}^{n+1}-e_{h}^{n}\|_{\beta}^{2}-\|e_{h}^{n}-e_{h}^{n-1}\|_{\beta}^{2})+4\alpha\sigma a(e_{h}^{n+1},e_{h}^{n+1})\leq

C_{p}\left(4\gamma_{1}+\frac{43}{36}\epsilon\right)\sigma a(e_{h}^{n+1},e_{h}^{n+1})+\frac{2\tau(\beta+C_{p})(\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3})^{2}\sigma^{2}}{\epsilon\beta}\overset{m}{\underset{j=0}{\sum}}\|e_{h}^{n+1-j}\|_{\beta}^{2}+\left(\frac{C_{g}\tau^{2}}{3}+4\||(\mathcal{I}-\Delta)v_{3t}|\|_{0,\infty}^{2}\right)\sigma^{5}.

(53)

Without loss of generality, we assume that the physical parameters
\alpha>0
is chosen so that
\alpha>2\gamma_{1}C_{p}
. Set
\epsilon=\frac{\alpha}{C_{p}}
, so

\left(4\gamma_{1}+\frac{43}{36}\epsilon\right)C_{p}<4\alpha
. This fact, together with estimate
(\ref{44})
imply

3(\|e_{h}^{n+1}\|_{\beta}^{2}-\|e_{h}^{n}\|_{\beta}^{2})-(\|e_{h}^{n}\|_{\beta}^{2}-\|e_{h}^{n-1}\|_{\beta}^{2})+2(\|e_{h}^{n+1}-e_{h}^{n}\|_{\beta}^{2}-\|e_{h}^{n}-e_{h}^{n-1}\|_{\beta}^{2})+\underset{\geq 0}{\left(\underbrace{-4\gamma_{1}C_{p}+\frac{101}{36}\alpha}\right)}\sigma a(e_{h}^{n+1},e_{h}^{n+1})\leq

\frac{2\tau C_{p}(\beta+C_{p})(\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3})^{2}\sigma^{2}}{\alpha\beta}\overset{m}{\underset{j=0}{\sum}}\|e_{h}^{n+1-j}\|_{\beta}^{2}+\left(\frac{C_{g}\tau^{2}}{3}+4\||(\mathcal{I}-\Delta)v_{3t}|\|_{0,\infty}^{2}\right)\sigma^{5}.

Summing this up from
k=1,...,n
, and rearranging terms, this provides

3\|e_{h}^{n+1}\|_{\beta}^{2}-\|e_{h}^{n}\|_{\beta}^{2}+2\|e_{h}^{n+1}-e_{h}^{n}\|_{\beta}^{2}+\underset{\geq 0}{\left(\underbrace{-4\gamma_{1}C_{p}+\frac{101}{36}\alpha}\right)}\sigma\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}a(e_{h}^{k+1},e_{h}^{k+1})\leq 3\|e_{h}^{1}\|_{\beta}^{2}-\|e_{h}^{0}\|_{\beta}^{2}+2\|e_{h}^{1}-e_{h}^{0}\|_{\beta}^{2}+

\frac{2\tau C_{p}(\beta+C_{p})(\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3})^{2}\sigma^{2}}{\alpha\beta}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\overset{m}{\underset{j=0}{\sum}}\|e_{h}^{k+1-j}\|_{\beta}^{2}+n\left(\frac{C_{g}\tau^{2}}{3}+4\||(\mathcal{I}-\Delta)v_{3t}|\|_{0,\infty}^{2}\right)\sigma^{5}.

(54)

It follows from the initial conditions
(\ref{2})
and
(\ref{29})
that:
v^{k}=u_{0}^{k}
and
v_{h}^{k}=u_{0}^{k}
, for
k=-m,-m+1,...,0
. So,
e_{h}^{k}=0
, for
k=-m,-m+1,...,0
. Utilizing this fact, it is easy to see that

\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\overset{m}{\underset{j=0}{\sum}}\|e_{h}^{k+1-j}\|_{\beta}^{2}=\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\|e_{h}^{k+1}\|_{\beta}^{2}+\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\|e_{h}^{k}\|_{\beta}^{2}+\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\|e_{h}^{k-1}\|_{\beta}^{2}+...+\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\|e_{h}^{k+1-m}\|_{\beta}^{2}=\overset{n+1}{\underset{k=2}{\sum}}\|e_{h}^{k}\|_{\beta}^{2}+

\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\|e_{h}^{k}\|_{\beta}^{2}+\overset{n-1}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\|e_{h}^{k}\|_{\beta}^{2}+...+\overset{n+1-m}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\|e_{h}^{k}\|_{\beta}^{2}\leq(m+1)\overset{n+1}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\|e_{h}^{k}\|_{\beta}^{2},

(55)

where the sum equals zero if the upper summation index is nonpositive. But

-\|e_{h}^{n}\|_{\beta}^{2}=-\|e_{h}^{n}-e_{h}^{n+1}+e_{h}^{n+1}\|_{\beta}^{2}\geq-2(\|e_{h}^{n+1}-e_{h}^{n}\|_{\beta}^{2}+\|e_{h}^{n+1}\|_{\beta}^{2}).

(56)

Plugging inequalities
(\ref{46})
and
(\ref{46a})
into estimate
(\ref{45})
, this implies

\|e_{h}^{n+1}\|_{\beta}^{2}+\underset{\geq 0}{\left(\underbrace{-4\gamma_{1}C_{p}+\frac{101}{36}\alpha}\right)}\sigma\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}a(e_{h}^{k+1},e_{h}^{k+1})\leq 5\|e_{h}^{1}\|_{\beta}^{2}+

\frac{2\tau C_{p}(\beta+C_{p})(\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3})^{2}\sigma^{2}}{\alpha\beta}(m+1)\overset{n+1}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\|e_{h}^{k}\|_{\beta}^{2}+n\left(\frac{C_{g}\tau^{2}}{3}+4\||(\mathcal{I}-\Delta)v_{3t}|\|_{0,\infty}^{2}\right)\sigma^{5}.

(57)

But,
\sigma=\frac{\tau}{m}
which is assumed to satisfy
\sigma=\frac{T_{f}}{N}
, then
(m+1)\sigma\leq 2\tau
and
n\sigma^{5}\leq T_{f}\sigma^{4}
. For small values of the time step
\sigma
, substituting this into estimate
(\ref{47})
and applying the Gronwall inequality, this results in

\|e_{h}^{n+1}\|_{\beta}^{2}+\underset{\geq 0}{\left(\underbrace{-4\gamma_{1}C_{p}+\frac{101}{36}\alpha}\right)}\sigma\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}a(e_{h}^{k+1},e_{h}^{k+1})\leq\left[5\|e_{h}^{1}\|_{\beta}^{2}+\left(\frac{C_{g}\tau^{2}}{3}+4\||(\mathcal{I}-\Delta)v_{3t}|\|_{0,\infty}^{2}\right)\sigma^{4}\right]*

\exp\left(\frac{8T_{f}C_{p}(\beta+C_{p})(\tau\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3})^{2}}{\alpha\beta}\right),

(58)

where ”*” means the usual multiplication in
\mathbb{R}
. Indeed:
(n+1)\sigma\leq 2T_{f}
. Now, let
u\in V_{h}
be an arbitrary function, where
V_{h}
is the finite element space defined by equation
(\ref{5})
. The application of triangular inequality gives

\|e_{h}^{1}\|_{\beta}^{2}=\|(v_{h}^{1}-u)+(u-v^{1})\|_{\beta}^{2}\leq 2[\|v_{h}^{1}-u\|_{\beta}^{2}+\|u-v^{1}\|_{\beta}^{2}].

(59)

Since
\inf\{\|v_{h}^{1}-u\|_{\beta}^{2}:\text{\,\,}u\in V_{h}\}=0
, applying the infimum over
u\in V_{h}
, in the left and right sides of estimate
(\ref{48a})
and utilizing inequality
(\ref{30a})
, to get

\|e_{h}^{1}\|_{\beta}^{2}\leq 2\inf\{\|u-v^{1}\|_{\beta}^{2}:\text{\,\,}u\in V_{h}\}\leq 2C_{3}h^{8}\|v^{1}\|_{5}^{2}.

Substituting this into estimate
(\ref{48})
, we obtain

\|e_{h}^{n+1}\|_{\beta}^{2}+\underset{\geq 0}{\left(\underbrace{-4\gamma_{1}C_{p}+\frac{101}{36}\alpha}\right)}\sigma\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}a(e_{h}^{k+1},e_{h}^{k+1})\leq\left[10C_{3}\|v^{1}\|_{5}^{2}h^{8}+\left(\frac{C_{g}\tau^{2}}{3}+4\||(\mathcal{I}-\Delta)v_{3t}|\|_{0,\infty}^{2}\right)\sigma^{4}\right]*

\exp\left(\frac{8T_{f}C_{p}(\beta+C_{p})(\tau\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3})^{2}}{\alpha\beta}\right).

(60)

Since
\|v_{h}^{n+1}\|_{\beta}-\|v^{n+1}\|_{\beta}\leq\|e_{h}^{n+1}\|_{\beta}
, performing straightforward calculations, inequality
(\ref{49})
becomes

\|v_{h}^{n+1}\|_{\beta}^{2}+2\underset{\geq 0}{\left(\underbrace{-4\gamma_{1}C_{p}+\frac{101}{36}\alpha}\right)}\sigma\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}a(e_{h}^{k+1},e_{h}^{k+1})\leq 2\|v^{n+1}\|_{\beta}^{2}+2\left[10C_{3}\|v^{1}\|_{5}^{2}h^{8}+\right.

\left.\left(\frac{C_{g}\tau^{2}}{3}+4\||(\mathcal{I}-\Delta)v_{3t}|\|_{0,\infty}^{2}\right)\sigma^{4}\right]\exp\left(\frac{8T_{f}C_{p}(\beta+C_{p})(\tau\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3})^{2}}{\alpha\beta}\right),

for
n=1,2,...,N-1
. The proof of Theorem
3.1
is completed thank to estimate
(\ref{31a})
.

\|\cdot\|_{1,\infty}
. Specifically,

\||e_{\theta}|\|_{1,\infty}=\underset{1\leq n\leq N}{\max}\|e_{\theta}^{n}\|_{1},

2h
and
h

and

\sigma
and
2\sigma

2007b
.

5
General conclusions and future works

In this paper, we have constructed a high-order computational technique which is based on a combination of finite element formulation and interpolation approach for
solving a nonlinear distributed delay Sobolev model. Both stability and error estimates of the proposed algorithm have been analyzed using a strong norm which is
equivalent to the

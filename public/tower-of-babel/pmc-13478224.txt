Capsular tension ring force line and intraocular lens angle on the impact of postoperative astigmatism in Marfan syndrome lens subluxation: a retrospective study
Authors: Junjie Yang, Xuan Yu, Ling Jin, Yamin Mao, Guang Zhao, Yukan Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo investigate the influence of the angle between the tension ring force line and the intraocular lens (IOL) on residual astigmatism after lens replacement surgery in Marfan Syndrome (MFS) patients with subluxated lenses. We conducted a retrospective cohort study involving patients with MFS who presented with lens subluxation and subsequently underwent cataract surgery with implantation of a Capsular Tension Ring (CTR) and a single-focus IOL at the Ophthalmology Department of Union Hospital, Huazhong University of Science and Technology, from April 1, 2020, to April 1, 2024. The study included patients with confirmed MFS and lens subluxation. Data collected included preoperative anterior segment photographs and postoperative assessments of visual acuity. The analysis focused on the measurement of the angle between the CTR force line and the IOL’s long axis (TRF-IOL Angle), as well as the ocular residual astigmatism (ORA), which was determined using vector analysis methods. The study encompassed 15 patients (25 eyes) in the final analysis. The gender distribution among the patients was 68% male and 32% female. The TRF-IOL Angle was identified as a significant factor impacting postoperative residual astigmatism. A pronounced effect was observed where a TRF-IOL Angle of less than 50 degrees was associated with increased residual astigmatism, indicating a direct relationship between the angle size and astigmatism levels. No significant association was identified for angles of 50 degrees or more, suggesting a threshold effect at this angle measurement. The angle between the CTR force line and IOL is associated with residual astigmatism after lens replacement surgery in MFS patients. Our findings suggest that the TRF-IOL Angle may be a factor associated with postoperative residual astigmatism in MFS patients. Appropriate positioning of the CTR and IOL could potentially contribute to favorable astigmatic outcomes. Trial registration: This study is retrospectively registered. Introduction

Marfan Syndrome (MFS) is a hereditary disease that affects multiple organ systems, mainly caused by mutations in the FBN1 gene1. It is characterized by abnormalities in connective tissue, and the global incidence of this condition is approximately 6.5 per 100,000 individuals2. MFS manifests as abnormalities in various systems, including the eyes, skeletal, and cardiovascular systems3. Among the ocular manifestations, lens dislocation is a relatively common symptom in MFS patients and significantly impacts their vision and quality of life4.

In the treatment of lens dislocation in MFS patients, the choice of surgical method is crucial for postoperative vision recovery and the prevention of complications. Due to inherent issues with weak zonules, surgical treatment poses challenges. Scholars have noted that preserving the capsule can significantly reduce the incidence of postoperative complications in MFS patients with lens dislocation5–7. Studies have shown that the risk of postoperative retinal detachment (RD) is lower if there is no vitreous loss8,9. The incidence of RD is relatively low after the implantation of capsular tension ring(CTR)10,11, possibly because the implantation of CTR preserves the posterior capsule membrane, which acts as a barrier between the anterior and posterior segments of the eye, with less vitreous prolapse. Currently, implanting CTR to retain the capsular bag has become the preferred approach for MFS patients12. The stability of the implanted intraocular lens (IOL) also greatly affects postoperative vision13,14.

However, the impact of the relative position between the CTR force line and IOL on postoperative astigmatism in MFS patients remains unclear. The present study aimed to investigate whether the angle between the CTR force line and the IOL long axis (TRF-IOL Angle) influences postoperative residual astigmatism in this population.

Materials and methods

Study design

In this retrospective cohort study, we collected data from patients with MFS combined with lens subluxation who were admitted to the Ophthalmology Department of Tongji Hospital, Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology, from April 1, 2020, to April 1, 2024. These patients underwent CTR scleral suture fixation within the capsular bag plus single-focus IOL implantation.

Our inclusion criteria included the following points: (a) patients diagnosed with MFS according to the revised Ghent criteria (2010) and having lens subluxation; (b) preoperative anterior segment photographs were available, clearly showing the direction and distance of lens subluxation; (c) patients received CTR scleral suture fixation within the capsular bag plus single-focus IOL implantation during their hospital stay in our department; (d) clear post-dilation anterior segment photographs were available that could simultaneously image the anterior capsule opening and IOL orientation; (e) postoperative optometry and visual acuity was checked more than twice.

Exclusion criteria were as follows: (a) Has glaucoma, corneal disease, retinal disease, or other ocular diseases; (b) Has a history of myopia refractive surgery.(c) history of ocular trauma or other ocular surgeries; (d) lack of data records; (e) intraoperative complications that necessitated additional surgical procedures; (f) intraocular pressure was less than 10mmHg or greater than 21mmHg before or after surgery.

Data collection

Information on the included patients, IOLMaster 700 (Carl Zeiss Meditec, AG) data (including axial length, anterior chamber depth, white-to-white distance, and simulated keratometry), intraoperative videos, surgical records, and postoperative optometry and vision was collected through the electronic medical record system of Union Hospital, Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology. The anterior segment photographic image archiving system was used to obtain data from anterior segment photographs. Each photographic indicator was measured separately three times, and the average of the three measurements was calculated. Additionally, the EDC (Electronic Data Capture) data collection and management system (study.empoweredc.com, Shanghai, China) was used to collect and manage data.

Surgical procedure

All surgeries were performed by the same experienced surgeon (YK.H.). Routine preoperative preparations were conducted, including ocular disinfection and local anesthesia. After adequate mydriasis, a lid speculum was used to keep the eye open. A 2.8 mm tunnel incision was made at the corneal limbus, and viscoelastic agent was injected through the incision to protect the cornea and intraocular structures. A circular capsulorhexis was then performed to create a 4.5–5.0 mm entry for the capsular bag. Insertion of iris hooks to fixate the capsular bag. Phacoemulsification equipment was used to emulsify and remove the nucleus of the lens while minimizing interference with intraocular structures. After completing the removal of the nucleus, the residual cortex was aspirated using a syringe. After this process, the posterior lens capsule was preserved for the subsequent fixation of the CTR and IOL, Both the IOL and CTR were of the same model type. The CTR(Capsular TensionRing® Model 276001G; Ophtec BV, Groningen, the Netherlands) was implanted into the lens capsule, ensuring it was fully deployed and uniformly supported the bag. A scleral flap was created 1.5–2 millimeters behind the corneal limbus. The CTR was sutured to the sclera with 8-0 polypropylene suture. Next, the IOL(Akreos AO; Model Bausch & Lomb, Rochester, NY, USA) was folded and inserted into the eye, using an injector or a special lens inserter to place the IOL into the capsular bag. The position of the IOL was adjusted to ensure it was centered and fixed with the CTR. To counteract asymmetric zonular weakness, the long axis of the IOL was intentionally oriented toward the direction of lens dislocation. After ensuring the stability of both the IOL and CTR, excess viscoelastic agent in the eye was removed. Finally, the intraocular pressure and the position of the IOL were checked, and the incision was closed after confirming there were no abnormalities, ending the surgery. Regular follow-up examinations should be conducted postoperatively to monitor the position of the IOL and the condition inside the eye.

Postoperative examination

The primary endpoint for analyzing the refractive outcome was set at 1 month postoperatively. At this visit, significant capsular fibrosis and contraction—which typically develop later and could confound astigmatic measurements—have not yet emerged14,15, while the corneal power has already stabilized16–19. Thus, the 1-month time point captures the direct effect of the surgical technique (CTR and IOL positioning) before these delayed capsular changes become major confounding factors.

Patients were followed up according to a standardized schedule, with examinations conducted at 1 day, 1 month (primary endpoint), 3 months, 6 months, and 12 months. Examinations beyond the primary endpoint (at 3, 6, and 12 months) were primarily aimed at monitoring postoperative complications and long-term surgical safety. The incidence and types of complications recorded at these time points are summarized in Supplementary Table S1.

Parameter measurement and calculation

The anterior segment photographic images were obtained using an anterior segment camera image archiving system. Two researchers conducted three separate measurements for each photograph and calculated the average values to ensure accuracy and reduce measurement variability.

Lens Subluxation Quadrant Assessment: The cornea was divided into four quadrants using horizontal and vertical axes, with the superior nasal side as Quadrant I, the inferior nasal side as Quadrant II, the inferior temporal side as Quadrant III, and the superior temporal side as Quadrant IV. Based on the preoperative anterior segment photographs, the quadrant in which the lens subluxation was farthest from the limbus was assessed.

Lens Subluxation Degree Grading: The degree of lens subluxation was evaluated from the point of maximum distance from the limbus along the lens meridian. A symmetrical axis of the lens through this point was constructed, and the distance D1 from this point to the intersection of the symmetrical axis with the cornea was measured. The corneal diameter D2 in the direction of the symmetrical axis was also measured (Fig. 1A). The subluxation was graded as mild if D1/D2 ≤ 1/3, moderate if 1/3 < D1/D2 ≤ 2/3, and severe if D1/D2 > 2/3. As no universally validated classification exists specifically for lens subluxation in Marfan syndrome. This system was empirically defined to provide exploratory reference data for future studies.

(A) Schematic diagram of D1 and D2 measurement for lens subluxation degree grading. (B) Schematic diagram of the measurement of the CTR Force Line and IOL Angle (TRF-IOL Angle).

CTR Force Line and IOL Angle (TRF-IOL Angle): After the CTR was implanted, the anterior capsular opening became elliptical in shape. The angle between the major axis of the elliptical opening and the long axis of the IOL was measured (Fig. 1B).

Total Ocular Astigmatism: The total ocular astigmatism was measured using an autorefractor, recording the sphere, cylinder, and axis.

Corneal Anterior Surface Astigmatism: The astigmatism of the anterior corneal surface was also measured using an autorefractor, noting the astigmatism and axis.

Ocular Residual Astigmatism (ORA): Defined as the vectorial difference between the total ocular astigmatism and the corneal anterior surface astigmatism. Using Alpin’s method of vector analysis, ORA can be calculated20,21. The specific operation involves placing the vectors of total ocular astigmatism and anterior corneal surface astigmatism (i.e., simulated keratometry, simK) in a rectangular coordinate system, doubling their angles and placing them in a double-angle coordinate system for vector subtraction, and then restoring the result to the rectangular coordinate system to obtain the vector calculation result of ORA. Here, simK represents the astigmatism of the 3 mm zone of the anterior corneal surface, the magnitude of which is the difference between the steep and flat simulated K values on the corneal topography map, with the astigmatism meridian being the axis of the steep simulated K meridian.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using R version 4.0.3 (https://www.R-project.org) in conjunction with EasyR (https://www.easyrdata.com Solutions, Inc., Shanghai). A two-tailed p-value of less than 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance for all analyses. Quantitative data are presented as mean ± standard deviation. For non-normally distributed data, median (interquartile range) was additionally reported. Qualitative data were statistically described using frequencies (%).

To evaluate the normality of the measurement data, the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test for normality was conducted. For data that conformed to a normal distribution, a t-test was utilized to compare two groups; if not, the Wilcoxon rank-sum test was applied. Categorical data were analyzed using the chi-square test to assess the significance of differences between groups. Subgroup analysis was performed to evaluate the robustness of findings across age categories.

Logistic regression analysis was carried out to explore the relationship between ORA and other variables. Considering the potential for non-linear relationships between ORA and other variables, a generalized additive model was employed to detect any underlying non-linear associations. In cases where a non-linear relationship was identified, a binary linear regression model was applied to estimate the threshold values.

Results

Characteristics

Initially, this study included 18 patients. However, due to the absence of preoperative visual acuity tests in one patient and postoperative anterior segment photographs in two patients, a total of 15 cases (25 eyes) were finally included in the final analysis. In this study, the average age of the patients was 17.48 years, with an age range from 3 to 65 years. In terms of gender distribution, 68% were male patients, and 32% were female patients. The average preoperative logMAR was 0.78 ± 0.42. The quadrant distribution of lens subluxation showed that 25% of the patients had subluxation in Quadrant I, 43.75% in Quadrant II, 18.75% in Quadrant III, and 12.5% in Quadrant IV. Regarding the degree of lens subluxation, 31.25% of the patients had Grade 1 subluxation, 68.75% had Grade 2 subluxation, and no patients had Grade 3 subluxation. Ocular parameters such as axial length (AL), anterior chamber depth (ACD), white-to-white distance (WTW), spherical equivalent (SE), best-corrected visual acuity, spherical refractive power (DS), and cylindrical refractive power (DC) were also measured and recorded (Table 1).

Analysis of the threshold effect of postoperative ORA in relation to TRF-IOL angle

In the analysis of the threshold effect on postoperative ORA, we investigated the impact of the TRF-IOL Angle on ORA and sought to identify any potential threshold effects. The results indicated that when the TRF-IOL Angle was less than 50 degrees, ORA showed an increasing trend as the angle decreased. Specifically, in the group with a TRF-IOL Angle less than 50 degrees (Model I), for each unit decrease in angle, ORA increased by 0.05 (β = − 0.05), and this effect was statistically significant (P = 0.0396). However, above this threshold angle (Model II), changes in the angle did not demonstrate significant correlation with an increase in ORA (β = 0.01, P = 0.3915). Additionally, by comparing the log-likelihood ratio test of the two models, we found that at a TRF-IOL Angle of 50 degrees, the difference in the effect on ORA was statistically significant (P = 0.041), suggesting that 50 degrees might be an important threshold angle affecting the change in postoperative ORA (Table 3).

Discussion

MFS is the most common cause of congenital lens dislocation, and its surgical treatment poses considerable challenges. In this study, we performed phacoaspiration, scleral bag CTR fixation, and in-bag IOL implantation on 25 eyes with MFS-associated lens dislocation. Postoperatively, there was an improvement in visual acuity for all patients, with a significant enhancement in the average best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA). Over the 12-month follow-up, no retinal detachment, secondary glaucoma, or uveitis was recorded. Capsular contraction syndrome developed in 5 eyes (20%) and posterior capsule opacification in 12 eyes (48%); only one case of IOL dislocation was observed, and no additional complications were noted. The study aimed to explore the impact of the TRF-IOL Angle on ORA after lens replacement surgery in MFS patients. It was found that when the TRF-IOL Angle was less than 50 degrees, the smaller the angle, the higher the postoperative ORA. When the TRF-IOL Angle was greater than or equal to 50 degrees, changes in the angle were not significantly correlated with postoperative ORA.

In the field of ophthalmic surgery, CTR implantation combined with lens replacement surgery is a common method for treating lens subluxation. The use of CTR and precise positioning of IOL are crucial for reducing postoperative astigmatism22. There are studies that have proposed predictive models and assessment tools to help doctors evaluate the possibility and extent of postoperative astigmatism in patients with lens dislocation before surgery23, thus allowing the development of individualized surgical plans. Conventional CTR is often used for patients with mild lens subluxation. However, in cases of extensive zonular dialysis, it cannot provide sufficient support or correct the decentration of the lens capsule24. In 1998, Morcher introduced the CTR, specifically designed for severe zonular laxity. With 1 to 2 sutures, this ring can be fixed to the sclera while maintaining the integrity of the capsular bag, thereby reducing the risk of IOL displacement and stabilizing the lens capsule. The surgery of CTR in-bag IOL implantation in children with lens dislocation has been reported to be safe with fewer short-term complications, and patients with MFS often adopt this surgical method25.

In patients with MFS, previous studies have largely focused on surgical techniques, postoperative complications, and visual recovery. For instance, some studies have examined the impact of different surgical methods on postoperative complications, such as anterior chamber fixated IOLs, iris fixated IOLs, scleral suture fixated IOLs, and CTR suture fixation with capsular bag retention. Each method has varying effects on complications, with CTR suture fixation with capsular bag retention being beneficial as it better maintains the shape and position of the capsular bag, reduces disturbance to the vitreous cavity, and helps to decrease posterior segment related complications26–28. In terms of postoperative outcomes, besides corrected visual acuity, postoperative astigmatism is also a concern. Studies have found that tilt of IOLs can increase postoperative astigmatism29. Moreover, the precise selection of CTR size and position is crucial for postoperative outcomes; an appropriately sized CTR can effectively reduce postoperative astigmatism30. The ideal position for a CTR should be in the center of the capsular bag to evenly distribute force and maintain IOL stability. During surgery, care should also be taken to avoid contact between the CTR and other intraocular structures to reduce the impact on corneal shape and the occurrence of postoperative astigmatism. However, there has been relatively little research on the impact of the angle between the CTR force line and IOL on postoperative astigmatism, which provides a research opportunity for this study.

Lens dislocation in MFS often occurs in childhood and can easily progress to severe subluxation. In our study, 68.75% of the patients had Grade 2 subluxation, which is already considered a more severe form of lens dislocation. Since MFS-related lens dislocation typically manifests at a young age and the intraocular structural changes are relatively uniform, the CTR in-bag IOL implantation surgery is commonly employed. We considered that when the degree of lens dislocation is severe, the stability of the CTR and IOL might affect the level of postoperative ORA, hence the conduct of this study.

This study explored the impact of the angle between the CTR force line and IOL on postoperative residual astigmatism, discovering a significant negative correlation between the two. We believe that when the TRF-IOL Angle is less than 50 degrees, the smaller the angle, the worse the stability of the IOL, making it more prone to tilt, thereby increasing postoperative ocular astigmatism.

When conducting our retrospective study, we were well aware of the importance of sample size to statistical power. Since MFS with lens subluxation is a rare condition, our sample size is relatively limited, which may impose certain limitations on our ability to detect effects. To assess the reliability of statistical analysis under such sample size conditions, we used PASS 15.0 software to calculate the statistical power (Table 4).

Through detailed statistical simulation with PASS 15.0 software, we found that with a sample size of n = 25, the statistical power of this study reached 69.3% (Fig. 3). This result indicates that, despite the small sample size, if there is a true effect, our study still has a high probability of detecting it. The calculation of this statistical power gives us confidence that even in retrospective studies with small sample sizes, we can draw scientifically sound conclusions. Moreover, it suggests how researchers should compensate for the limitations of small sample sizes in future similar studies through well-designed statistical analysis.

Despite the findings of this study, there are still some limitations. Firstly, the sample size is relatively small, which may affect the generalizability and reliability of the results. Secondly, as this study is retrospective, there may be selection bias and information bias. Thirdly, the wide age range of the included patients (3–65 years) may introduce heterogeneity. However, a subgroup analysis comparing pediatric/adolescent (< 18 years) and adult (≥ 18 years) patients showed no significant difference in the primary outcome of postoperative ocular residual astigmatism (p = 0.74), suggesting that the main findings regarding the TRF-IOL Angle effect may be consistent across age groups. Nevertheless, further studies with larger sample sizes and more homogeneous age distributions are warranted to validate these findings. Fourth, the 1-month follow-up window partially overlaps with the early stage (3 weeks) of capsular contraction, which may introduce minor confounding; however, we selected this time point as a practical balance between corneal stability and capsular change progression. Fifth, scleral parameters were not assessed. Although unlikely to change within the study window, their potential contribution to ORA in MFS merits further investigation.

Cionni and Osher31 recommended aligning the IOL haptics with the CTR suture fixation points to minimize the angle between the force line and the IOL. In our cohort, however, a TRF-IOL Angle ≥ 50° was associated with lower postoperative ORA, suggesting that this principle may not directly apply to MFS patients. The diffuse and asymmetric zonular weakness in MFS may produce distinct capsular bag dynamics, such that a limited angle between the CTR force line and IOL does not necessarily compromise refractive outcomes. This finding warrants further prospective investigation.

Conclusion

Our study suggests a significant impact of the CTR force line and IOL angle on residual astigmatism following lens replacement in MFS patients. The results indicate that a smaller angle, particularly when less than 50 degrees, is associated with higher postoperative astigmatism. This finding may help to the understanding of astigmatism control in MFS-related ocular surgeries, emphasizing the importance of precise surgical techniques.

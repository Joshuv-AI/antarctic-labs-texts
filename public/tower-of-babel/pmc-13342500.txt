MBDANet: a multi-scale balanced dynamic alignment detection network based on knowledge distillation for small objects
Authors: Fengdong Shi, Xin Zhao, Liankun Sun, Jiao Yin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSmall object defect detection is a pivotal technique for the industrial transition from post-facto rework to proactive prevention. However, existing detection methods still face challenges, such as the trade-off dilemma among computational complexity, model size, and detection accuracy. To address these issues, this study proposes a multi-scale balanced dynamic alignment detection network (MBDANet) based on the improved YOLOv8n architecture. The process begins with a robust feature downsampling module that downsamples the backbone network to better extract small-object features from both deep and shallow layers. Following this, a multi-scale feature fusion and balanced pyramid network fuses these features. To further enhance the model’s attention to small objects, we design a task dynamic alignment detection head. Finally, we incorporate a bridging cross-task protocol inconsistency for distillation method, leveraging a higher-precision teacher model to boost the detection accuracy. Experiments on the Printed Circuit Board (PCB) Defect Dataset, DeepPCB Defect Dataset and Steel Surface Defect Dataset show that MBDANet outperforms the baseline YOLOv8n algorithm, with a 2.7%, 3.7% and 4.5% increase in mean Average Precision, a 25% reduction in model size, a 27% reduction in parameter number, and a 15% reduction in computational complexity. In summary, MBDANet can effectively improve the detection performance of small object defects, balance speed and accuracy, and provide solid technical support for the stable operation of industrial equipment in manufacturing scenarios.Keywords: Defect detection, YOLOv8n, Printed circuit board, Steel surface defect, Knowledge distillationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

As industry advances toward high precision, intelligence, and large-scale production, the risks induced by micro-defects have been continuously amplified. The core value of small-object defect detection lies in realizing full-process quality control with high precision and low cost, thereby reducing the losses and hazards caused by product defects in industrial settings. Nevertheless, micro-defects pose significant challenges to the machine vision systems tasked with surface defect inspection1. Consequently, achieving high detection precision at low cost becomes an urgent issue to be addressed in industrial production processes.

In practical scenarios, a small object is generally defined as an object whose designated region accounts for less than 0.09% of the total image area. As illustrated in Fig. 1, the defect image with a maximum size of 5350 pixels occupies approximately 0.056% of the entire background image (15863034 pixels), which is below the 0.09% threshold. Furthermore, whether for steel surface defects or bare PCB board defects, the defects are situated against highly cluttered backgrounds. During the detection process, the subtle visual features of such small defects often poses considerable challenges. Therefore, accurately and efficiently detecting these tiny object defects remains a non-trivial challenge.

The evolution of defect detection has gone through several distinct stages, traditional methods relied primarily on manual visual inspection and electrical testing. Visual inspection which depends on human inspectors is inherently subjective2, time-consuming, and prone to inconsistencies. On the other hand, electrical testing requires physical contact with PCB circuits, which may cause irreversible damage. With the advancement of technology, Dai et al.3 proposed an automated defect detection method using Automated Optical Inspection (AOI); however, its stringent imaging conditions have limited the practicality and widespread application of AOI systems.

In recent years, advances in artificial intelligence have facilitated the application of automated computer vision techniques in object detection, providing a non-contact, adaptable, and widely applicable solution. For instance, the edge-assisted epipolar Transformer4 enabled high-precision industrial scene reconstruction, while the parallax attention-based depth estimation method5 tackled illumination and texture challenges in aerial perception. Neural rendering and flow-assisted multi-view stereo techniques6 supported real-time dynamic monitoring, and the cycled GAN-based self-supervised framework7 reduced labeled data dependency for large-scale perception. The adaptive cost volume method8 preserved fine-grained details for high-resolution stereo matching. Though valuable for scene reconstruction and depth estimation, these studies are not tailored to industrial defect detection demands (e.g. small/low-contrast defect extraction, accuracy-efficiency balance on edge devices).

Industrial defect object detection methods are generally categorized into two types: two-stage methods (e.g. R-CNN9 and Faster R-CNN10) and one-stage methods (e.g. YOLO11 and SSD12). Two-stage methods are well-known for their accuracy and are highly suitable for identifying small objects in complex scenarios; however, their two-step process often results in low speed and requires more computational resources, which greatly limits their application in real-time detection. On the other hand, one-stage models have been optimized through simplified architectures and multi-scale feature extraction techniques to enable real-time deployment while enhancing the capability of detecting small objects. However, as a representative of one-stage detection methods, YOLO still encounters two key challenges in small object defect detection:

Low accuracy: On one hand, multiple downsampling layers can dilute fine-grained features of small objects. This makes it challenging for models to capture intricate details of objects. On the other hand, captured images often contain backgrounds with colors and textures similar to the objects. This similarity hampers accurate differentiation by the model. Both factors are key contributors to the degradation in accuracy.

To address the aforementioned challenges, the main contributions of this study are as follows:

To improve detection accuracy, we implement Robust Feature Downsampling (RFD) in the backbone network. This module serves as a new downsampling unit. It enhances the model’s capacity to extract global context. It also improves the capture of local details from small objects. To boost performance in cluttered background scenarios, a Multi-scale Feature Balanced Pyramid Network (MFBPN) is proposed. This architecture fuses features from different levels. It significantly strengthens the model’s ability to detect small objects against cluttered backgrounds. Additionally, it reduces the complexity of the overall model.

Overall, the innovation of MBDANet resides in its four-stage optimization framework termed feature enhancement – multi-scale balancing – task dynamic alignment – knowledge distillation augmentation.

First, a RFD module is innovatively integrated into the backbone network. By combining depth-separable convolution, slice and max-pool downsampling, it prevents small-object detail loss from traditional downsampling, and provides high-quality inputs for subsequent fusion modules. Then, a MFBPN module is proposed. Via weighted fusion and global aggregation, it balances semantic and spatial feature influence, solving the imbalance in traditional multi-scale fusion–either prioritizing semantics over details or focusing on details over semantics. Next, a TDAH module is designed. It splits classification and regression tasks to let their branches focus on core needs through task decomposition, while a layer attention mechanism dynamically adjusts feature weights to eliminate conflicts between the two tasks. Finally, the three modules are jointly optimized under bridging cross-task protocol inconsistency for distillation (BCKD) training. Transferring discriminative knowledge from the teacher model, this strategy further improves the mean Average Precision (mAP) without increasing computational complexity, enabling the model to balance efficiency and accuracy.

Related work

With the development of artificial intelligence in industrial applications, the accurate recognition technology for large and medium-sized objects in images has become mature and has been successfully applied in practical scenarios. However, compared with such objects, the accurate detection of tiny objects remains a difficult problem that urgently needs to be solved13. Specifically, tiny defects usually occupy only a very small number of pixels in images, and their imaging quality is highly susceptible to the overall quality of the images. This characteristic directly leads to two key problems: On the one hand, the inherent feature information of the defects is vague, making it difficult to effectively distinguish them from other elements; on the other hand, such tiny defects are often found in cluttered background environments14, and the contextual information available for auxiliary judgment is extremely scarce, which further increases the difficulty of accurate detection. At the same time, industrial application scenarios also require the size and deployment efficiency of algorithm models. Deepening the network layers will lead to an increase in the number and complexity of model parameters, making it difficult to embed into hardware devices. Therefore, the lightweighting of models also faces huge challenges.

Research on improving the accuracy of small object detection

The attention mechanism can effectively improve detection accuracy. For instance, Tang et al.15 proposed PCB-YOLO, which embedded Swin Transformer into the backbone network to construct a unified attention mechanism. This mechanism reduced interference between the background and defects in images and enhanced the analytical capability of the network. Li et al.16 developed PCB-DETR, which extracted defect features on PCB surfaces through a spatial attention shift module. Luo et al.17 proposed an SPP (Spatial Pyramid Pooling) structure with a separable large-kernel attention mechanism, which significantly improved the detection rate of multi-scale defects and enhanced the detection performance for small-object defects. TRSBi-YOLO integrated a simple parameter-free attention mechanism (SimAM), which strengthened the feature representation of objects without increasing computational burden18. Although these models achieved effective feature extraction, they neglected to consider the multi-scale feature extraction for small objects in cluttered backgrounds.

The feature pyramid network can enhance the multi-scale feature fusion ability of the model, thereby improving detection accuracy. Specifically, the FPN component is primarily responsible for top-down feature transmission, which, combined with the bottom-up path of the Path Aggregation Network (PAN), forms bidirectional feature aggregation. This enables feature maps of different scales to simultaneously contain rich semantic information and detailed information. Li et al.19 improved the YOLOv3 model by integrating the Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) to achieve better recognition of PCB defects. YOLOv420 upgraded YOLOv3 by replacing the traditional Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) with the Path Aggregation Network (PANet); PANet incorporated a Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP) module to expand the receptive field and strengthen connections between key contextual features. Zhang et al.21 proposed the DsP-YOLO method, which employed a feature fusion network to enhance the feature information of small objects. To address the limitations of DsP-YOLO in feature mining, Jing et al.22 gradually integrated additional high-level semantic details to reinforce low-level spatial features. Similarly, John et al.23 introduced BiFPN-YOLO, which replaced PANet with an improved Bidirectional Feature Pyramid Network (BiFPN) aimed at further optimizing the accuracy of real-time detection. However, while corresponding improvements had been made to multi-scale feature fusion methods, in-depth investigation specifically aimed at the fusion of multi-scale features for small objects in cluttered backgrounds remained lacking. Specifically, the balanced interaction among multi-scale features had been overlooked.

Research on model lightweight

In terms of network parameters and computational complexity, YOLO-RRL proposed by Zhang et al.24 leveraged a reparameterized generalized FPN, a dynamic upsampler, and a lightweight asymmetric detection head, achieving a favorable trade-off between detection speed and accuracy. Xie et al.25 proposed MFAD-RTDETR, a multi-frequency aggregated scattered feature flow network that delivered high detection accuracy but resulted in a relatively large model size. Zhang et al.26 introduced LDD-Net, a lightweight PCB defect detection network that integrated multi-scale features to achieve promising accuracy. Qiu et al.27 added a lightweight convolutional neural network to YOLOv4, achieving a balance between speed and accuracy in detecting transmission line defect images. The LRSR-net model was proposed by Sun et al.28. It could eliminate feature loss caused by pooling layers and used a small number of parameters to learn the features of road objects, effectively suppressing background clutter.

Although the aforementioned methods have achieved a certain reduction in model parameters and computational complexity, they still incur considerable parameter . They overlook the coordination between classification and localization tasks. However, the dynamic coordination between these two is crucial for the dynamic perception of small objects.

MBDANet based on small object defect detection

This article introduces the MBDANet architecture, which is designed to quickly identify small PCB surface defect objects in industrial environments. The overall framework is shown in Fig. 3. It is proposed as an improved variant of YOLOv8n. Specifically, a RFD module is incorporated into the backbone network to address small-object feature dilution caused by shallow and deep robust feature downsampling. A MFBPN module is designed for the neck to enable balanced cross-layer feature interaction, enhancing detection adaptability in cluttered backgrounds. In the detection head, a TDAH module is proposed to ensure that fused features are precisely tailored to classification and regression tasks.

MBDANet architecture diagram. The modified parts are marked with red dashed boxes in the figure.

RFD module

Downsampling is a fundamental operation in neural networks for reducing the size of feature maps, with common methods including average pooling and max pooling. They conserve computational expense and extract high-level features but lose small object fine-grained information and spatial relationships, which adversely affect performance.

To enhance the model’s small-object fine-detail capture capability, we integrate the RFD module29 – comprising SRFD (shallow) and DRFD (deep) components–which leverages depth-separable convolution, slice downsampling, and max pooling to fuse multi-source features, mitigate traditional downsampling limitations, and generate robust representations. SRFD is applied to the backbone’s initial downsampling stage, while DRFD serves subsequent layers. The RFD framework is presented in Fig. 4.

RFD architecture diagram.

The RFD module duplicates the input feature map into three versions (X, C, M) for convolution, slice-based, and max-pooling downsampling respectively. Feature fusion across these pathways enables SRFD and DRFD to produce richer, more reliable feature sets and boost model performance: SRFD enhances shallow feature details, structure, and edges via the three downsampling methods; DRFD captures deep contextual information with large kernels, combines depth-separable convolution with downsampling to generate diverse representations, and improves feature discriminability and model robustness.

MFBPN module

Multi-level and multi-scale feature integration is critical for object detection. Traditional FPN struggles with comprehensive inter-layer semantic interactions and global context capture, a limitation exacerbated in small-object detection due to critical detail loss in cluttered backgrounds. Inspired by the XKT model’s multi-knowledge fusion concept30, we incorporated a multi-level feature fusion mechanism (integrating semantic, spatial, and contextual information) into the improved YOLOv8n, developing the MFBPN neck architecture, including the Pyramid Context Extraction (PCE), Rectangular Self-Calibration Module (RCM)31, Dynamic Interpolation Fusion (DIF), and Feature Balancing Module (FBM).The structure of MFBPN module is detailed in Fig. 5.

TDAH module

The structure of the YOLOv8n detection head is illustrated in Fig. 6. Existing task decoupling schemes with fully independent classification and regression branches suffer from parameter redundancy, unstable small-batch training, and no dynamic adaptability. In contrast, the proposed TDAH detection head shares Group Normalization(GN) convolution layers to cut parameters; it replaces Batch Normalization(BN) with GN for stable small-sample and mini-batch training; and it uses layer-wise attention to dynamically adjust task weights. Conventional static task alignment methods rely on global feature attention and dual-branch static outputs. They overlook adapting to small-object shape/position variations and show weak resistance to background interference. The TDAH head’s unique innovations are dynamic deformable convolution (DyDCNv2) for adaptive focus on small-object spatial locations, and a channel-wise learnable scaling layer for background noise suppression in small-object detection. In summary, RFD uses two branches, shallow and deep, integrating depth-separable conv, slice and max-pool downsampling. It keeps small-object pixels and captures the whole scene, but its feature maps are still rough, noisy and vary across scales. MFBPN smooths them: PCE gathers multi-scale context, RCM refines edges, FBM re-weights each scale and DIF fuses them, suppressing background clutter and enhancing the saliency of small objects. As these fused features are generic, feeding them directly to cls and reg triggers task conflict and degrades accuracy. TDAH therefore splits the cleaned feature maps into cls and reg branches through task decomposition and refines each branch with DyDCNv2 and layer attention, removing conflict and letting the high-quality fused features deliver their full value. The core innovation of MBDANet is not simple module stacking, but the construction of a cascade optimization paradigm of “feature quality enhancement, fusion balance regulation, and task alignment optimization”. Through mutual constraints and joint design of all modules, it systematically solves the cascaded issues of feature dilution, fusion imbalance and task conflict in small-object detection.

Model learning method based on knowledge distillation

To improve detection accuracy without increasing extra model complexity, inspired by the paradigm of bridging the performance gap with a novel teacher–student architecture33, we integrate BCKD knowledge distillation34 into the training process, transferring knowledge from the teacher model (YOLOv8s) to the proposed student model (MBDANet) to boost classification and localization performance. In summary, logical distillation only aligns the final predicted probability distributions of the teacher and student models, ignoring the spatial feature correlations in intermediate layers and thus leading to the loss of fine-grained localization details for small objects. Feature distillation aligns global feature vectors, small object features are prone to being diluted by background information in the global space, which in turn causes a decline in localization accuracy. In contrast, BCKD distillation directly constrains the bounding box overlap based on IoU to preserve the localization details of small objects. It does not require global feature alignment, but instead focuses on key regions and protocol consistency, thereby avoiding the dilution of small object features. Evaluation metrics

The experiment employs average precision (AP), mean average precision (mAP), precision (P), and recall (R) as evaluation metrics. In the following formulas, TP denotes True Positives, which refers to the number of positive instances correctly predicted as positive by the model. FP refers to False Positives, which refers to the number of negative instances incorrectly predicted as positive by the model. TN denotes True Negatives, which refers to the number of negative examples that the model classifies correctly as negative. FN refers to False Negatives, which refers to the number of positive examples misclassified as negative by the model. C refers to the total number of PCB defect types, and is the AP of the i-th class. Overall, these measures quantify the model’s performance on the object detection task.

AP: The total area under the precision-recall curve is obtained by first evaluating the precision for each classification category in the ranking results and then averaging these precision values for all types. Specifically, AP calculates how well the predicted samples for each type match the true labels in the ranking.

mAP: It is calculated by finding the mean of the AP within each category in order to quantify the model’s detection performance for every category and presenting one score.

P: It refers to the probability of correctly classifying positive samples among the predicted positives, also known as precision.

R: It is an opportunity for correct prediction of the positive examples out of the actual positives, also known as recall.

The experiment of knowledge distillation

Knowledge distillation significantly enhances the model’s detection accuracy and generalization capability. To verify the advantages of BCKD distillation34, we compare it with L1 and L2 distillation35 in logical distillation, and with channel-wise knowledge distillation (CWD), which is the state-of-the-art method in feature distillation36. As shown in Table 3, L1 distillation quantifies output discrepancies using absolute error, yet it is non-differentiable at zero, which may increase the complexity of the optimization process. Consequently, the model’s mAP decreases by 0.1% and 1.2% on the PCB and steel surface defect datasets, respectively, after applying L1 distillation. L2 distillation measures differences via squared error but is sensitive to outliers, which tends to cause overfitting; its application also exerts an adverse effect on mAP across both datasets. CWD distillation captures the teacher model’s knowledge through channel-wise fine-grained feature representation learning. However, its limited flexibility restricts its applicability to diverse network architectures and task scenarios. Specifically, the mAP drops by 0.8% on the PCB defect dataset after CWD distillation is adopted, while it only increases by 0.6% on the steel surface defect dataset. In contrast, BCKD distillation transfers the teacher model’s knowledge via positive-negative sample comparison. This design helps the student model acquire discriminative features accurately, strengthens its classification capability, and ensures a balanced learning process. After implementing BCKD distillation, the model’s mAP is significantly improved by 1.0% and 1.9% on the PCB and steel surface defect datasets, respectively.

The experimental results in Table 6 show that the original YOLOv8n model achieved a mAP of 93.9%. Replacing the downsampling modules in the backbone with the RFD module increased the mAP by 2.3% compared to the baseline. This improvement is mainly because RFD extracts small object features more effectively through joint shallow and deep downsampling, although it also adds some computational cost. Introducing the MFBPN neck alone raised the mAP by 1.2% without increasing the computational complexity, largely due to its ability to balance the contributions of features from different levels and enable efficient cross-scale semantic interaction. Adding only the TDAH detection head improved the mAP by 1.9%, confirming the effectiveness of its task decomposition, spatial alignment, and scaling layer in enhancing the focus on small objects.

When all three modules were integrated together, their structural complementarity and joint optimisation reduced the overall computational complexity by 15% compared to the baseline, while raising the mAP by 1.7%. After further incorporating BCKD knowledge distillation, the mAP increased by an additional 1% with no extra computational cost, indicating that the high-precision representation capability of the teacher model (YOLOv8s) was effectively distilled into the student model (MBDANet). In the end, the improved MBDANet outperformed the original network by 3.6% in precision, 3.3% in recall, and 2.7% in mAP.

Conclusion

In order to solve the limitations of traditional machine inspection methods, a new algorithm called MBDANet is developed based on YOLOv8n. The initial steps involve optimizing the backbone of the model by integrating a powerful feature RFD module, which enhances feature representation capabilities and improves overall performance. This RFD component improves the model’s ability to process multi-scale information, making it more effective when detecting objects of different sizes. Subsequently, the neck portion of the network is strengthened with a high-precision structure called MFBPN, which is combined with the detection head to reduce the complexity of the model and improve detection accuracy, achieving an optimal balance between efficiency and accuracy. Then, a TDAH module is introduced, which combines parameter sharing technology to further improve detection accuracy and better handle complex detection tasks. Finally, the learning method of BCKD knowledge distillation is used to generate a lightweight model that maintains high detection accuracy and robustness.

Unlike previous studies that optimize detection modules in an isolated manner, this work systematically co-designs and validates a four-stage processing pipeline tailored to the task of small object defect detection. Each module addresses a specific bottleneck problem in a sequential manner: feature dilution, fusion imbalance, task conflict, accuracy-complexity trade-off, and the cross-dependent relationships among modules have been empirically verified via ablation experiments (Table 6). While individual modules may be conceptually interchangeable, their parameter configurations and integration strategies are jointly optimized for the target domain, as evidenced by the non-additive performance gains (the progressive improvements of Model_9 over Models_1–4).

The proposed MBDANet achieves a high mAP of 96.6% with only 2.19 million parameters and a computational complexity of 6.9 GFLOPs. Notably, under a batch size of 4 and an input resolution of 640640 pixels, the model attains an inference latency of only 4 ms on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2080 GPU, corresponding to 243 Frames Per Second (FPS). In comparison, the baseline YOLOv8n reaches 514 FPS under the same configuration. Although the speed of MBDANet is moderately reduced, the sacrificed speed translates into a 2.7 percentage point improvement in mAP, along with a substantial reduction in model size and parameter count. Moreover, its inference efficiency remains far above the minimum requirement for real-time detection (FPS 25). Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed optimization framework not only addresses the challenges of small-object detection but also achieves a favorable balance between lightweight design and real-time performance, thereby offering high practical value for engineering deployment.

However, the current study still has limitations closely tied to cutting-edge application needs, which also clarify future optimization directions: (1) Inadequate generalization across defect categories neglects to meet open-vocabulary recognition demands. MBDANet relies on fixed-category annotated data for training and cannot identify new, unseen defect types, while the open-vocabulary recognition concept in multi-object tracking43 enables new defect detection without full network retraining. (2) High annotation costs for small-object defect datasets leave massive unlabeled data unused, and manufacturer defect data is hard to share due to privacy concerns. Inspired by FedSSH44, future work will integrate federated and semi-supervised learning, enabling collaborative training of MBDANet across edge devices without local data sharing and leveraging unlabeled data to boost generalization and practicality. (3) Although MBDANet balances accuracy and efficiency but lacks system integration and edge validation. Inspired by XCR-Net’s system-level architecture45, we will design an end-to-end pipeline (acquisition, detection, alert, feedback), deploy on Jetson/Raspberry Pi/FPGA for latency and power evaluation, apply quantization and TensorRT optimization, and finally embed into industrial cameras for real-time detection and automatic upload, promoting practical deployment. (4) The model has only been tested on public datasets and lacks validation in real industrial production scenarios. In future work, we will actively cooperate with industry to evaluate the model on more challenging real production data, so as to further verify its robustness and applicability in practical industrial scenarios.

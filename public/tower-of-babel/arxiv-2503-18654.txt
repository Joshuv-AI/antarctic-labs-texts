The valence quark distributions of the proton have been extensively studied
in deep inelastic scattering (DIS) experiments.
As the first moment of the valence quark distribution is fixed by
the number sum rule, the experimental and theoretical interest
is mainly focused on the flavor dependence of the valence quark
distribution
[
1
,
2
]
.
Experimental information on
d(x)/u(x)
at large
x
, where
x
is
the Bjorken-
x
, primarily comes from
measurements of the
F^{n}_{2}/F^{p}_{2}
neutron to proton
structure function ratio in DIS experiments.
Recent attempts to extract
F^{n}_{2}/F^{p}_{2}
include tagged-DIS
measurement from the BONuS experiment
[
3
,
4
]
and
DIS from
3
H and
3
He nuclei in the MARATHON experiment
[
5
]
.

Compared to the proton,
the valence quark distribution of the pion, which plays an important dual
role of being the lightest meson and a Goldstone
boson, is much less known. Significant interest in the
pion substructure is reflected in the numerous theoretical predictions on
pion’s valence quark distribution based on
various approaches
[
6
,
7
]
.
The existing experimental information on its valence quark distribution
comes primarily from pion-induced Drell-Yan
[
8
]
experiments carried out
in the 1980s
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
.
The electromagnetic
nature of the Drell-Yan process implies a relatively small cross section,
resulting in marginal statistical accuracy for these experiments.
Moreover, the existing Drell-Yan data do not yet allow a
separation of the valence from the sea quark distributions
in the pion
[
14
]
.

As noted in
[
15
]
, the pion valence-quark distribution could be
extracted from a measurement of the difference
between the
\pi^{-}
and
\pi^{+}
-induced Drell-Yan cross sections on
the isoscalar deuteron (
D
) target. In leading-order formalism,
the difference between the Drell-Yan cross
sections,
\sigma_{DY}
, for
\pi^{-}+D
and
\pi^{+}+D

is given as

\displaystyle\sigma_{DY}(\pi^{-}+D)-\sigma_{DY}(\pi^{+}+D)\propto V_{\pi}(x_{1%
})V_{N}(x_{2}),

(1)

where
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
refer to hadron’s momentum fraction carried by the
partons in the beam and target, respectively, while

and
V_{N}
are the individual valence quark
distributions in the pion and in the nucleon:

\displaystyle V_{\pi}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u^{V}_{\pi^{+}}(x)=\bar{d}^{V}_{\pi^{+}}(x)=d^{V}_{\pi^{-}}(x)=%
\bar{u}^{V}_{\pi^{-}}(x);

\displaystyle V_{N}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[u^{V}_{p}(x)+d^{V}_{p}(x)]/2;

(2)

Equation (1) shows that the

\pi^{-}+D

\pi^{+}+D

to the product of the pion and nucleon valence quark distributions. Since
the nucleon valence quark distribution is quite well known over
a broad range of
x
(except at very large

), this cross section
difference allows a direct measurement of pion’s valence quark distribution.
Although the NA10 Collaboration
[
16
]
has measured the
\pi^{-}+D

Drell-Yan cross sections, there exists no corresponding data for the

\pi^{+}+D
reaction. While new Drell-Yan data on
\pi^{+}+D

collected, for example, at the AMBER experiment
[
17
]

in the future, it is interesting to
consider other experimental observables which are also sensitive to
pion’s valence quark distribution.

In this paper we study the possibility of probing pion’s valence quark
distribution using
J/\psi
production with
\pi^{-}
and
\pi^{+}
beams.
We first show that the cross section
difference between
\pi^{-}+p
and
\pi^{+}+p

J/\psi
production
is proportional to the product of the pion’s
V_{\pi}

and proton’s
u_{V}-d_{V}
valence quark distributions.
We then compare the existing data on
\pi^{-}+p
and
\pi^{+}+p

J/\psi
production with theoretical calculation.
We also examine the beam-charge dependence
for pion-induced
J/\psi
production cross sections on the
neutron-rich platinum target.
The good agreement between data and calculation
suggests that this is a viable method to measure the valence quark
distribution of pion.

In contrast to the electromagnetic Drell-Yan process, hadronic
J/\psi

production involves strong interaction as the underlying mechanism.
At leading order, the two hard processes responsible for producing
a pair of
c\bar{c}

quarks are the
q\bar{q}
annihilation and the

gg

pair of
c\bar{c}
quarks is

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{dx_{F}d\tau}=\frac{2\tau}{(x_{F}^{2}+4\tau^{2})^{1%
/2}}H_{BT}(x_{1},x_{2};m^{2}),

(3)

where

are the momentum fractions carried by the beam
(

x_{F}=x_{1}-x_{2}

c\bar{c}

\tau^{2}=m^{2}/s

H_{BT}
is the convolution of the hard-process

target hadrons

\displaystyle H_{BT}(x_{1},x_{2};m^{2})=G_{B}(x_{1})G_{T}(x_{2})\sigma(gg\to c%
\bar{c};m^{2})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=u,d,s}[{q^{i}_{B}(x_{1})\bar{q}^{i}_{T}(x_{2})+\bar{q}^{%

where

In the color-evaporation model (CEM)
[
19
]
, the

by integrating the

cross section from the
c\bar{c}

\tau_{1}=2m_{c}/\sqrt{s}
, to the open-charm threshold,

\tau_{2}=2m_{D}/\sqrt{s}

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{dx_{F}}=F\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}2\tau d\tau H_{%

where the factor

Despite its simplicity, the

which govern the relative

The success of the color-evaporation model suggests that
this model provides a useful tool for studying parton
distributions in pion and kaon from the
J/\psi
production

[

,

In summary, we have shown that the beam-charge asymmetry of pion-induced

J/\psi
production on a hydrogen target,

\sigma_{J/\psi}(\pi^{-}+p)-\sigma_{J/\psi}(\pi^{+}+p)
, has
a positive sign and is sensitive to the product of the pion’s and the
proton’s valence quark distributions. We have compared existing data on the
beam-charge asymmetry for several different target nuclei with the theoretical
calculations. The good
agreement between data and calculation suggests that the beam-charge
asymmetry for pion-induced
J/\psi
production is a viable
method to study the valence quark distributions of pion and proton.
The much larger cross section for
J/\psi
production compared with the
Drell-Yan represents a significant advantage. New data covering a broad
kinematic region with high statistics, as anticipated in future experiments
at AMBER
[
17
]
, would be of great interest.

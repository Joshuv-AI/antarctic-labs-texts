Abstract
. We address two questions related to the semiampleness of line bundles arising from Hodge theory. First, we prove there is a functorial compactification of the image of a period map of a polarizable integral pure variation of Hodge structures for which the Griffiths bundle extends amply. In particular the Griffiths bundle is semiample. We prove more generally that the Hodge bundle of a Calabi–Yau variation of Hodge structures is semiample subject to some extra conditions, and as our second result deduce the b-semiampleness conjecture of Prokhorov–Shokurov. The semiampleness results (and the construction of the Baily–Borel compactifications) crucially use o-minimal GAGA, and the deduction of the b-semiampleness conjecture uses work of Ambro and results of Kollár on the geometry of minimal lc centers to verify the extra conditions.

Let
(X,D)
be a proper log smooth algebraic space. Let
{}_{\mathrm{orig}}V=(_{\mathrm{orig}}V_{\mathbb{Z}},F^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{orig}}V_{\mathcal{O}})
be a polarizable pure integral variation of Hodge structures on
X\backslash D
. We define

V=\bigotimes_{p}\mathchoice{{\textstyle\bigwedge}}{{\bigwedge}}{{\textstyle\wedge}}{{\scriptstyle\wedge}}^{\operatorname{rk}F^{p}_{\mathrm{orig}}V_{\mathcal{O}}}{{}_{\mathrm{orig}}V}.

It is a polarizable integral pure CY variation of Hodge structures which has unipotent local monodromy if

2.5.

.

(1)

The local system

Moreover, it is sufficient to check this condition for a single contractible

.

(2)

If

For (1), first note that

(2)

(3)

Definition 2.17
.

Let
(X,D)
be a proper log smooth algebraic space and
V=(V_{\mathbb{C}},F^{\bullet}V_{\mathcal{O}})
a polarizable complex CY variation of Hodge structures on
X\backslash D
with unipotent local monodromy with Deligne/Schmid extension
(\mathcal{V},F^{\bullet}\mathcal{V})
. We say the Hodge bundle
L=F^{m}\mathcal{V}
is
integrable
if, after replacing
(X,D)
with a strictly log smooth modification, for any irreducible proper strictly log smooth
(Y,D_{Y})
with a morphism
g\colon(Y,D_{Y})\to(\overline{X_{\Sigma}},D_{\Sigma})
which is generically finite onto its image,
g^{*}L
is big whenever either of the following equivalent conditions is satisfied:

(1)

The Griffiths bundle of the transcendental part of the pullback
(g|_{Y\backslash D_{Y}}^{*}V^{\mathrm{tr}}_{\Sigma})^{\mathrm{tr}}
is big.

(2)

The period map of the transcendental part of the pullback
(g|_{Y\backslash D_{Y}}^{*}V^{\mathrm{tr}}_{\Sigma})^{\mathrm{tr}}
on
Y\backslash D_{Y}
is generically immersive.

Concretely, this means that if
g^{*}L
is not big, then some subvariation of the pullback
g|_{Y\backslash D_{Y}}^{*}V^{\mathrm{tr}}_{\Sigma}
containing the Hodge bundle is isotrivial on a curve through the generic point of
Y
.

Remark 2.18
.

It is proven in
[
13
]
that the Griffiths bundle is semiample on
X\backslash D
. The same is proven for the Hodge bundle of a CY variation whenever the Kodaira–Spencer map on the Hodge bundle is immersive. The strategy of
Section

4
can be used to prove the Hodge bundle of a CY variation is semiample on
X\backslash D
if it is integrable (on
X\backslash D
) in the above sense.

2.9.
Combinatorial monodromy

For any proper algebraic space

, a torsion line bundle

has a canonical flat connection: if

, then the local flat sections are those sections

extends to a global section. Equivalently, there is a finite étale cover

for which

, and the flat connection on

is inherited from the trivial connection on

whose flat sections are global sections.

If

has a canonical flat connection whose local flat sections are sections

whose restriction to

are flat with respect to the above connection on

, or equivalently those for which
\nu_{X}^{*}s
is flat.

Definition 2.21

be a proper algebraic space with a line bundle

5.

5.1.

Let

be a period map associated to a polarizable integral pure variation of Hodge structures, and

is any discrete group containing the image of the monodromy representation. By

of

compactification.

The following is a more intrinsic and slightly more general notion than image of a period map. Let
Y
be a reduced and irreducible algebraic space with a quasifinite Griffiths transverse

8

8

That is, for any resolution

,

is Griffiths transverse.
morphism

6.2.
Singularities of pairs

The acronyms klt, dlt, lc, sdlt, and slc describe types of singularities that occur naturally in various constructions within birational geometry.
For instance, the minimal (resp. canonical) model of an snc pair has dlt (resp. lc) singularities. The reduced part of the boundary of a dlt (resp. lc) pair is sdlt (resp. slc). The fibers of a semistable or locally stable morphism (e.g., the families of varieties parametrized by KSBA moduli spaces) have slc singularities. Finally, up to finite base change, any family of Calabi–Yau varieties over a punctured disk has a dlt log Calabi–Yau filling; see
[

7.2.4.
Effective b-semiampleness

Let
f\colon(Y,\Delta)\to X
be an lc-trivial fibration inducing the generalized pair
(X,B_{X},\mathbf{M})
. The effective b-semiampleness conjecture predicts the existence of a universal positive integer
c
, only depending on the relative dimension of
f
and the coefficients of the horizontal part of
\Delta
, such that
c\mathbf{M}
is b-free, or eventually weaker statements involving other topological invariants of the general fiber. The conjecture was formulated by Prokhorov and Shokurov
[
94
, Conj. 7.13.3]
, and proved in
loc. cit.
in relative dimension
1
.
Recently, the conjecture has also been solved for lc-trivial fibrations whose general fiber is an abelian or primitive symplectic variety with bounded second Betti number of fixed dimension; see
[
33
, Thm. C]
. This was a key step towards the proof of boundedness results for certain
K
-trivial fibrations in
[
33
]
.
In particular, combining
[
33
]
and
[
8
, Thm. 1.4]
, the conjecture is also settled in relative dimension 2.

It is natural to ask whether a fixed positive power of the Schmid extension of the Griffiths bundles of a polarizable integral variation of Hodge structures sharing the same period domain is free. Analogously, whether the same holds for the Hodge bundle of integrable polarizable CY variations of Hodge structure with torsion combinatorial monodromy sharing the same period domain. A positive answer to this question may entail boundedness results for more general
K
-trivial fibrations.

While the effective version of the b-semiampleness conjecture remains open at the moment,

7.8
below provides a tool to prove it when the general fibers belong to a given bounded family of pairs.
Thus, we deduce the effective version of the conjecture when the general fiber is a klt log CY pair of Fano type.
In particular, we obtain the following application, which was kindly pointed out to us by Shokurov.

Corollary 7.7
.

Let
f\colon(Y,\Delta)\to X
be an lc-trivial fibration of relative dimension
n
from a pair
(Y,\Delta)

such that
\Delta
is effective over the generic point of
X
.
Further assume the following:

(i)

the generic fiber of

is a klt pair;

(ii)

\Delta
is big over

.

Let

denote the moduli b-divisor induced by

.
Then, there is a constant

, only depending on

and the horizontal multiplicities of
\Delta
, such that
I\mathbf{M}
is b-Cartier and b-free.

Proof.

Since the moduli b-divisor is determined by the general behavior of the fibration, we may shrink

so that
(Y,\Delta)
is a klt pair and
\Delta
has no

-vertical components.
Then, we may replace

with a small

-factorialization.
In particular, we may assume that

is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor that is
f
-big.
Notice that

is klt for

and

-big.

Thus, we may apply
[

]
to

and the morphism

and replace

with the relatively ample model of

In particular,

is
f

Notice that all these operations do not affect

.

Up to shrinking

, we may assume that all the fibers

of

are klt pairs.
Then, these belong to a bounded family of pairs by

]
.
In particular, there is a constant

(recall

Then, we may invoke

∎

7.2.5.
Connections to the theory of complements

The theory of complements has been introduced by Shokurov to study flips and, more generally, morphisms of Fano type;
we refer to
[
101
]
for details.
In recent years, this theory has proved very powerful;
for instance, Birkar’s proof of the BAB conjecture relies heavily on this theory;
see
[
20
,
21
]
.
On the other hand, in Birkar’s strategy, it is important to relax the category of pairs to also include generalize pairs, and then study complements for these more general objects, originally introduced in
[
19
]
.
Indeed, in
[
20
]
,
complements are built with an inductive approach, and one of the possible scenarios includes lifting complements from the base of an lc-trivial fibration with fibers of Fano type;
see
[
20
, §6.4]
.
In particular, even when interested in pairs, the approach in
[
20
]
needs to introduce generalized pairs for the inductive argument to go through.
Now, by
7.7
, we may induce a pair with controlled coefficients on the base of such lc-trivial fibrations.
Thus, it would be interesting to explore whether boundedness of complements could be proved without resorting to generalized pairs.

7.2.6.
B-semiampleness for GLC fibrations

Let
f\colon(Y,\Delta)\to X
be a generically log canonical (GLC) fibration, i.e.,
(Y,\Delta)
is lc over the generic point of
X
.
Note that, contrary to the lc-trivial case, the general fiber is no longer assumed to be log Calabi–Yau. In the GLC case, the moduli part is the b-divisor on the total space
Y
given by
\mathbf{M}_{Y}\coloneqq K_{Y}+\Delta-f^{*}(K_{X}+B_{X})
, up to flatification of

; see
[
4
, §2.2]
for details.
It is known that
\mathbf{M}
is b-nef, relatively b-semiample, but not b-semiample in general; see
[
4
]
.
However, Shokurov conjectured that
\mathbf{M}
is b-semiample after a small perturbation by an ample divisor coming from the moduli of the general fiber; see
[
102
, Conj. 1]
.
The second-named author and Spicer proved a variant of Shokurov’s conjecture in
[

, Thm. 1.2]
: for

the b-divisor

is b-semiample, conditional to the b-semiampleness conjecture in the lc-trivial case.
Here,

is sufficiently divisible and

is arbitrarily small and positive.
The result now holds unconditionally by
1.5
.

7.3.
Moduli of Calabi–Yau varieties

In this section we deduce

Let

-rigidified

We list noteworthy properties of the moduli stack

(i)

[

(ii)

Let

(iii)

The Hodge bundle of the universal family is well-defined on

(iv)

(v)

(more precisely, that of the construction in

(vi)

7.3.1.
Stratification of Baily–Borel compactifications

In the context of
7.8
, the following description of the underlying set of points of
Y^{\mathrm{BBH}}
follows from the proof in
\lx@sectionsign 5
. For any choice of proper log smooth algebraic space
(X,D)
with a proper birational morphism
X\backslash D\to Y
, we will have
Y^{\mathrm{BBH}}=X(\mathbb{C})/R_{\mathrm{curve}}
. For a particular choice
(X^{\prime},D^{\prime})
, the compactification
Y^{\mathrm{BBH}}
will have a natural stratification by the images
Y^{\mathrm{BBH}}_{S}
of the Hodge strata
X_{S}^{\prime}
, and for each one there will be a period map whose projection
\widetilde{Y_{S}^{\mathrm{BBH}}}^{V_{S,\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{tr}}}\to\mathbb{P}(V_{X^{\prime},S,\mathbb{C},x_{S}}^{\mathrm{tr}})^{\mathrm{an}}
has discrete fibers. Thus, for any stratum
X_{\Sigma}
of
X
whose image meets
Y_{S}^{\mathrm{BBH}}
we have

(7.2)

\dim Y^{\mathrm{BBH}}_{S}\leq\operatorname{rk}\operatorname{gr}_{F}^{m-1}V^{\mathrm{tr}}_{X^{\prime},S}\leq\operatorname{rk}\operatorname{gr}_{F}^{m-1}\operatorname{gr}^{W}_{k_{\Sigma}}V_{X,\Sigma}=\operatorname{rk}\operatorname{gr}_{F}^{m-1}\operatorname{gr}^{W}_{k_{\Sigma}}\psi_{\Sigma}V

where
\psi_{\Sigma}V
is the limit mixed Hodge structure at any point of
X_{\Sigma}
. The last equality follows since any part of
\operatorname{gr}_{F}^{m-1}\operatorname{gr}^{W}_{k_{\Sigma}}\psi_{\Sigma}V
is necessarily primitive, since
\operatorname{gr}_{F}^{m}\operatorname{gr}^{W}_{k_{\Sigma}+2}\psi_{\Sigma}V=0
. In particular, in the case of the Baily–Borel compactification of a moduli space of
d
-dimensional Calabi–Yau varieties as in
7.8
(where
V
is the variation on degree
d

\dim Y=\operatorname{rk}\operatorname{gr}_{F}^{d-1}V
and so

\operatorname{codim}Y^{\mathrm{BBH}}_{S}\geq\sum_{k\neq k_{\Sigma}}\operatorname{rk}\operatorname{gr}_{F}^{d-1}\operatorname{gr}^{W}_{k}\psi_{\Sigma}V.

As it can be seen from the case of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
or the moduli of

7.3.2.
BB vs BBH compactifications

We give an example of a moduli stack of Calabi–Yau manifolds
\mathcal{Y}
for which the morphism
Y^{\mathrm{BB}}\to Y^{\mathrm{BBH}}
has positive-dimensional fibers. This is easy to do on the boundary. For example, let
Y
be the coarse moduli space of smooth quintic threefolds, and consider a degeneration to a transverse union of a hyperplane
L
and a smooth quartic threefold
T
, meeting along a quartic K3 surface
S
. The associated graded of the limit mixed Hodge structure then includes the primitive cohomology
H^{3}(T,\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{prim}}
in weight 3 and the primitive cohomology
H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{prim}}
in weight 2. The transcendental part of the limit mixed Hodge structure is then the transcendental part of
H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Q})
. Thus, since quartic threefolds and quartic surfaces satisfy an infinitesimal Torelli (for middle cohomology), and the period map of the linear systems
|\mathcal{O}_{T}(1)|
on a fixed (general)
T
is immersive, this boundary piece survives in
Y^{\mathrm{BB}}
with dimension

4+(\binom{4+4}{4}-5^{2})=49
. On the other hand, this boundary piece has dimension
19
in
Y^{\mathrm{BBH}}
.

It is even possible for
Y^{\mathrm{BB}}\to Y^{\mathrm{BBH}}
to contract curves in the interior of the period domain. Precisely,
Y^{\mathrm{BB}}
will always contain as an open set
Y\subset\breve{Y}^{\mathrm{BB}}\subset Y^{\mathrm{BB}}
the normalization (in
Y
) of the closure of the image of
Y^{\mathrm{an}}\to\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}
. This is the locus of
Y^{\mathrm{BB}}
where the Hodge structure does not degenerate (or equivalently, where the limit mixed Hodge structure is pure), and
\breve{Y}^{\mathrm{BB}}\to Y^{\mathrm{BBH}}
may have positive-dimensional fibers. This is indeed the case of the coarse moduli space
Y
of smooth quintic threefolds.

The following is an example due to Radu Laza, and we warmly thank him for sharing it with us. For a generic choice of
\underline{a}\coloneqq(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4})\in\mathbb{C}^{4}
,
16
16

16

e.g.,
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4})=(1,1,1,2)
works but
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4})=(1,1,1,1)
does not.
the quintic threefold
X
in
\mathbb{P}^{4}

cut by the polynomial

f_{\underline{a}}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})\coloneqq x_{0}^{2}x^{3}_{1}+x_{0}^{3}(x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2})+a_{1}x_{1}^{5}+a_{2}x_{2}^{5}+a_{3}x_{3}^{5}+a_{4}x_{4}^{5}

has a unique isolated

singularity at

. The blowup

along

, is a resolution of singularities. The Mayer–Vietoris exact sequence for the mapping cylinder of this resolution reads

which implies that

carries a pure Hodge structure.

Now, let
\mathcal{X}
be the hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{4}\times\mathbb{A}^{2}_{(b_{1},b_{2})}

f_{\underline{a}}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{4})-(b_{2}^{2}+b_{2}+1)b_{1}^{2}x_{1}x^{4}_{0}+b_{2}(b_{2}+1)b_{1}^{3}x^{5}_{0}=0,

and let

be the natural projection. Note that the restriction of
\mathcal{X}
along the curve
(b_{1}=0)
is a trivial family with fiber

along the curve

i.e., a double suspension of

, which is a blowup of the Weyl cover of the miniversal deformation of a cuspidal curve. By the Thom–Sebastiani’s formula for Hodge modules (cf., e.g.,

Globally, the nearby cohomology for

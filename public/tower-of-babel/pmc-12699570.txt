Effect of transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation on perioperative psychological symptoms in patients undergoing elective thoracoscopic pneumonectomy under general anaesthesia: study protocol for a single-centre, prospective, randomised, double-blind, controlled study
Authors: Zhengxiu Sun, Yuanyuan Wang, Junda Chen, Yongao Lin, Tianxi Ma, Mingkai Zhou, Pinghao Li, Jinling Liu, Wenxing Zhao, Xingyu Xiong, Wen Tan, Mingshu Tao, Jun-Li Cao, He Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPerioperative psychological symptoms are prevalent among patients undergoing lung surgery and can contribute to adverse clinical outcomes. Pharmacological interventions for these symptoms have inherent limitations. Transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation (taVNS) has emerged as a promising non-invasive therapeutic approach. This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of taVNS in managing perioperative psychological symptoms in patients undergoing elective thoracoscopic pneumonectomy under general anaesthesia.Methods and analysisThis single-centre, prospective, randomised, double-blind, controlled trial will enrol 176 patients scheduled for elective thoracoscopic lung resection. Participants will be randomly allocated to either the active taVNS or the sham taVNS groups in a 1:1 ratio. Both groups will receive 30 min active or sham stimulation sessions at four time points: (1) the afternoon prior to surgery, (2) the morning of the surgery, (3) following extubation and (4) the first afternoon postsurgery. The primary outcome is the incidence of perioperative anxiety, assessed using the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale-Anxiety subscale. Secondary outcomes include depression scores, stress index, sleep quality, pain scores, incidence of postoperative delirium, fatigue, cough symptoms and postoperative recovery quality, all evaluated through validated assessment tools. Analyses will be conducted using intention-to-treat and per-protocol populations.Ethics and disseminationThe Ethics Committee of Affiliated Hospital of Xuzhou Medical University granted approval for the study with approval number: XYFY2024-KL444-01. Dissemination will be via national anaesthesia conferences and publication in the peer-reviewed literature.Trial registration numberChiCTR2400090542.

Introduction

Compared with open thoracotomy, video-assisted thoracic surgery for lung resection is associated with a reduced incidence of pulmonary complications and accelerated recovery time.1 2 Despite significant advancements in surgical techniques that have enhanced patient outcomes, individuals undergoing lung surgery frequently experience a range of psychological symptoms, including anxiety, depression, stress, sleep disturbances, worry, fear, hopelessness, fatigue and pain. These symptoms have a high incidence and significantly affect clinical outcomes.2 Previous studies have reported anxiety prevalence rates ranging from 20.9% to 65%,35 depression rates from 12.4% to 68%3 6 7 and sleep disorder rates between 45% and 57% among patients with lung cancer.8 9 Adverse psychological conditions can exacerbate clinical outcomes, leading to delirium, cognitive dysfunction and potentially reduced survival time.1013 Therefore, supportive interventions are essential to assist patients in managing these physical and psychological challenges.

In contemporary clinical practice, medications such as benzodiazepines and opioids are frequently employed to mitigate and alleviate adverse perioperative symptoms. However, given the potential risks of respiratory depression and prolonged recovery time, their use is generally not recommended.14 15 Non-pharmacological interventions, such as preoperative consultations, music therapy and acupuncture, have demonstrated efficacy in reducing preoperative anxiety.1618 Recent studies also suggest that non-invasive neuromodulation techniques, such as transcranial direct current stimulation, can effectively reduce the incidence of perioperative anxiety in patients.19

Transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation (taVNS) is an innovative, cost-effective, safe and non-invasive therapeutic approach derived from the traditional vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) technique.20 Current evidence indicates that taVNS exhibits varying degrees of efficacy in treating anxiety disorders,21 depression,22 insomnia,23 pain24 and cognitive impairments.25 Functional MRI (fMRI) studies have shown that taVNS directly enhances activity in brain regions associated with reducing worry, such as the prefrontal cortex and the anterior cingulate cortex.26 Additionally, taVNS improves functional connectivity between the amygdala and the prefrontal cortex in individuals with depression, thereby alleviating depressive symptoms.27 Further research indicates that taVNS helps restore autonomic nervous system balance, mitigate stress responses28 and regulate sleep architecture.23 A recent prospective study has demonstrated that taVNS improves sleep quality in patients with chronic insomnia and alleviates comorbid symptoms such as anxiety and depression, with therapeutic benefits maintained over a 20-week period.29 Additionally, another study has shown that while taVNS reduces erythema in patients with rosacea, it also significantly decreases anxiety and depression scores, and this therapeutic effect remains sustained during a 24-week follow-up period.30

However, current research has certain limitations. Existing taVNS studies have primarily focused on non-surgical populations, and no studies have specifically examined the application of taVNS for managing perioperative symptoms in patients undergoing thoracoscopic lung resection. This patient group is subjected to unique perioperative stressors—such as single-lung ventilation, intercostal neuralgia and concerns about impaired pulmonary function—which contribute to heightened psychological and physical comorbidity, underscoring the need for targeted interventions. Furthermore, most previous perioperative psychological interventions have been limited to the preoperative phase, rather than encompassing the entire perioperative period, and thus fail to address the dynamic trajectory of psychological distress associated with surgery.

Based on these findings, we hypothesise that taVNS may have a beneficial effect on perioperative psychological symptoms such as anxiety, depression, stress and impaired sleep quality. This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of taVNS in mitigating perioperative psychological symptoms, with a particular focus on anxiety, among patients undergoing thoracoscopic lung resection. The objective is to develop effective psychological support strategies and enhance patient outcomes in clinical practice.

Materials and methods

Study design

The trial will be conducted as a prospective, randomised, controlled study with parallel groups at a single centre. A total of 176 patients scheduled to undergo thoracoscopic lung resection at the Affiliated Hospital of Xuzhou Medical University will be enrolled in this study between October 2024 and May 2025. The current protocol version is 3.0, dated 15 August. The study is currently in the recruitment phase, and rigorous screening based on the inclusion criteria will be implemented. Written informed consent will be obtained from all participating patients. This report adheres to the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials guidelines for randomised studies31 (online supplemental additional file 1). Withdrawal or dropout criteria

Participants have the right to withdraw from the study at any time, for any reason, without experiencing any negative consequences. The research team will ensure continuous care for all participants and will carefully document the reasons for their withdrawal. In addition, the investigators reserve the right to withdraw participants from the study in cases of severe organ dysfunction, non-compliance or the occurrence of severe adverse events.

Randomisation and blinding

Participants will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either the active taVNS group or the sham taVNS group, according to a computer-generated random number sequence. The allocation details will be sealed in opaque envelopes and kept confidential until the day before the intervention, when the investigators conduct the procedure. To ensure blinding, identical stimulation devices will be used, differing only in the internal current output. All outcome assessors, data analysts, healthcare professionals and participants will remain unaware of the group allocation. Prior to the study, outcome assessors will undergo rigorous blinding training to familiarise them with the assessment procedures and criteria, minimising the influence of subjective factors on the results.

Unblinding

Participants will be closely monitored for complications related to taVNS during the intervention. If complications such as dermatitis, local bleeding, otalgia or arrhythmia arise, unblinding will be conducted promptly to minimise potential bias.

Anaesthesia

On arrival in the operating room, standard monitoring procedures, including electrocardiography, pulse oximetry and invasive arterial blood pressure monitoring, will be promptly initiated. General anaesthesia will be induced using a regimen that includes 0.5 μg/kg sufentanil, 0.3 mg/kg etomidate and 1 mg/kg rocuronium to facilitate rapid intubation. A single-use sterile double-lumen tube (DLT) will be selected based on the patient’s gender and height. The correct placement of the DLT will be confirmed using a fibreoptic bronchoscope, with the cuff inflating to the minimum effective volume.

The tidal volume is set at 6–8 mL/kg during bilateral lung ventilation and at 8–10 mL/kg during single-lung ventilation. The respiratory rate is adjusted to maintain end-tidal CO2 levels between 35 and 45 mm Hg, and the inspiratory-to-expiratory (I:E) ratio is set at 1:1.5. The inspired oxygen concentration is kept between 60% and 100%, with a flow rate of 2 L/min. Continuous intravenous infusions of propofol (4–6 mg/kg/hour) and remifentanil (0.1–0.3 µg/kg/min) are administered, while 1% sevoflurane is used to maintain the bispectral index between 40 and 60 for anaesthesia depth. Sevoflurane is discontinued 30 min before the end of surgery. A 20 mL bolus of 0.5% ropivacaine is administered under the surgeon’s direct visualisation for intercostal nerve block on the operative side, prior to chest closure. Flurbiprofen axetil (50 mg) is administered during skin closure, and all anaesthetic agents are discontinued after surgery. Vasoactive drugs are administered as needed during anaesthesia to maintain haemodynamic stability. After surgery, the patient is transferred to the postanaesthesia care unit (PACU). After regaining consciousness, the endotracheal tube is removed to ensure optimal tidal volume. Postoperative analgesia is administered via intravenous infusion of sufentanil (1.5 µg/kg), tropisetron (6 mg) and 100 mL of normal saline, at a background infusion rate of 2 mL/hour, with a patient-controlled analgesia bolus dose of 0.5 mL.

Study interventions

On the day preceding surgery, a designated member of the research team will identify eligible participants from the elective surgical schedule. Active taVNS or sham taVNS will be administered at four predetermined time points: (1) the afternoon prior to surgery, (2) the morning of surgery, (3) immediately after extubation and (4) the first afternoon following surgery.

All conscious patients will be informed that they may experience varying degrees of sensation, or none at all, during the stimulation procedure. taVNS will be administered using a transcutaneous electrical stimulation therapy device (tVNS501, RISHENA, Changzhou, China). Stimulation will be delivered via two modified dot-shaped electrodes positioned over the auricular branch of the vagus nerve in the cymba conchae of the left ear, as illustrated in figure 2. The stimulation parameters will be set in accordance with those recommended in the systematic review.32 In the active taVNS group, participants will receive taVNS (pulse width: 200 µs; frequency: 20 Hz) for 30 min, with the current increasing for 30 s until the participant reports a tingling sensation, then decreasing to a level slightly below this threshold for 30 min. In the sham taVNS group, electrode placement and initial parameter settings will be identical to those of the active taVNS group. However, on the participant reporting a tingling sensation, the current will be immediately discontinued, and the device will remain off for the following 30 min to simulate the sham state. In accordance with the protocol, stimulation shall be terminated immediately if a participant reports any intolerable discomfort. Regarding resumption procedures: in the event of interruption due to discomfort, the underlying cause (eg, skin erythema, electrode displacement) must be addressed through appropriate corrective measures (eg, electrode repositioning or a reduction in current intensity by 0.2 mA), after which stimulation may be resumed and the total duration adjusted to ensure a complete 30 min session. In the case of electrode detachment, the standard electrode placement procedure—including skin cleaning, gel application and impedance testing—must be fully repeated; stimulation shall then be restarted, and the full 30 min duration recalculated without counting any previously delivered partial time. All interruptions and subsequent restarts must be thoroughly documented in the case report form, including timestamps and the specific reasons for each occurrence. Throughout the intervention process, trained researchers will strictly follow the operating procedures and closely monitor the participants’ responses to ensure standardised implementation of the intervention.

Outcomes

Primary outcome

The primary outcome is the incidence of perioperative anxiety in patients, measured from the time of the first taVNS intervention to 3 days postsurgery. Anxiety is assessed using the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale-Anxiety (HADS-A) subscale, a standardised 7-item self-report tool. The HADS-A is a widely used self-assessment scale for evaluating psychological distress in non-psychiatric patients,33 with good sensitivity and specificity.34 Patients with a HADS-A score of 8 or higher are considered to be experiencing anxiety, with scores between 8 and 10 indicating mild anxiety, 11 and 14 indicating moderate anxiety and 15 and 21 indicating severe anxiety. Anxiety is assessed by researchers in the treatment room immediately before the first taVNS or sham intervention (T0), and then again after the first taVNS or sham intervention (T1). On the day of surgery, anxiety is assessed before (T2) and after (T3) the second intervention. Additional assessments occur after extubation in the PACU (T4), following the third intervention (T5), before (T6) and after (T7) the fourth intervention on the first afternoon after surgery, on the second day (T8), the third day (T9) and 1 month after surgery (T10). In this study, anxiety is operationally defined as present when the HADS-A score reaches or exceeds 8 at any assessment point following the initial taVNS intervention.

Secondary outcomes

The secondary outcomes include: anxiety scores (T1–T10), depression scores (assessed by the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale-Depression33 during T1–T10), stress index (assessed by the RingConn smart ring during T1–T9, ranging from 0 to 100, with higher scores indicating higher stress levels), sleep quality scores (assessed by the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index35 during T1, T7–T10, ranging from 0 to 21, with higher scores indicating poorer sleep quality), pain scores (assessed by the 10-point Numerical Rating Scale36 during T4–T10, ranging from 0 (no pain) to 10 (worst pain imaginable)), postoperative delirium incidence (assessed by the Confusion Assessment Method or the Confusion Assessment Method for the intensive care unit37 during T4–T9), postoperative fatigue scores (assessed by the Christensen Fatigue Scale38 during T9, ranging from 1 to 10, with higher scores indicating more severe postoperative fatigue), postoperative cough scores (assessed by the Cough Symptom Score39 during T9, ranging from 1 to 6, with higher scores indicating more severe cough symptoms) and postoperative recovery quality (assessed by the Quality of Recovery Scale-1540 during T9, ranging from 1 to 150, with higher scores indicating better recovery quality).

Adverse events and safety

During the trial, comprehensive haematological examinations, as well as assessments of renal and hepatic function, will be performed to ensure participant safety. Potential adverse events associated with electrical stimulation, including skin erythema, dizziness, headaches, nausea and vomiting, will be closely monitored. Any adverse medical events, irrespective of their causal relationship to the study intervention, will be thoroughly evaluated and promptly reported to the principal investigator and the ethics committee to determine whether the participant should be withdrawn from the study.

Data collection and management

All data collected will be analysed by an independent researcher who did not participate in the trial process once the last participant has completed the study. Participant information will first be recorded on paper-based case report forms and then entered into an electronic data system for storage. The data will be stored in a separate, secure location within the system. The principal investigator will conduct regular reviews of all adverse events, convene investigator meetings when necessary to assess the risks and benefits of the study and perform an unmasked strategy when required to ensure the safety and legal rights of subjects. Independent data monitoring personnel will be arranged to monitor the accumulated safety and efficacy data, determining whether the study should continue.

Sample size

Preliminary data indicate that the incidence of perioperative anxiety in this patient population is 55%. We hypothesise that a reduction of at least 40% in the incidence of perioperative anxiety following four sessions of taVNS intervention would be considered an effective treatment. Using a study power of 0.80 and a significance level (α) of 0.05, we calculated that a sample size of 79 participants per group is required, based on calculations performed using PASS V.15.0. Accounting for an anticipated dropout rate of 10%, our target recruitment is set at 88 participants per group.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis of all data will be performed by two independent analysts not involved in the trial. All outcomes will be analysed according to the intention-to-treat principle based on the initial allocation. Also, we will conduct per-protocol analyses, including only those who completed post-treatment and follow-up assessments. The normality of the continuous data will be assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, while the homogeneity of variance will be evaluated using the Levene test. Continuous variables following a normal distribution will be expressed as mean±SD (x±s), while those not following a normal distribution will be presented as median and IQR. Categorical variables will be reported as frequencies and proportions (%) and analysed using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. The independent sample t-test will be used for normally distributed data, and the Mann-Whitney U test will be used otherwise for non-normally distributed data. The secondary outcomes will be analysed using a mixed linear model (linear mixed-effects model).

Statistical analyses will be conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics (V.26.0). Statistical charts will be generated using GraphPad Prism V.8.0. Missing data will be addressed using multiple imputation methods. The significance level for all tests is set at α=0.05; thus, probability values <0.05 will be considered statistically significant, and Bonferroni correction will be applied to adjust for multiple comparisons.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public were not involved in the design, or conduct, or reporting, or dissemination plans of this research.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethics approval was granted by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Xuzhou Medical University on 12 September 2024 (approval number: XYFY2024-KL444-01). The study will be conducted in accordance with the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Furthermore, the trial has been registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (http://www.chictr.org.cn) under registration number ChiCTR2400090542.

Discussion

Undergoing surgery can be a profoundly traumatic and painful experience for patients, often leading to various adverse psychological symptoms such as anxiety, depression, stress reactions and sleep disturbances, which may occur independently or together.41 International studies indicate that anxiety is one of the most common psychological symptoms experienced by patients during the perioperative period, with preoperative anxiety affecting 11%–80% of patients.42 These physiological and psychological symptoms can exacerbate stress responses, impair physical functioning, diminish quality of life and compromise treatment adherence. A large cohort study published in the British Journal of Anaesthesia in 2024, involving 8350 middle-aged surgical patients in China, found that preoperative psychological symptoms (including anxiety, depression, stress reactions and sleep disturbances), whether occurring individually or in combination, were linked to an increased risk of postoperative pain and worsening pain trajectories within 3 months after surgery.43

Due to its distinctive advantages, neuromodulation has been effectively applied in the treatment of various psychiatric disorders.44 VNS, an effective neuromodulation therapy, was initially approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in 1997 for the treatment of drug-resistant epilepsy.45 After the discovery of VNS’s mood-modulating effects, the FDA approved it in 2005 for treating treatment-resistant depression.46 47 Compelling evidence indicates that chronic VNS can alleviate anxiety in both humans and rodents.48 49 However, due to a spectrum of side effects associated with invasive VNS, including irreversible electrode implantation, electrode failure, deep wound infections, temporary vocal cord paralysis and arrhythmias induced by test stimulation, non-invasive taVNS has emerged as a promising alternative for enhancing neurological function. fMRI studies have shown that taVNS enhances blood oxygen level-dependent signals in the amygdala, thalamus and prefrontal cortex.50 This is consistent with the brain activity changes observed following implanted VNS. Furthermore, stimulation of the auricular region has been shown to more robustly activate the amygdala and solitary tract.50 These nuclei and brain regions may be closely associated with functional impairments observed in individuals with psychiatric symptoms such as anxiety and depression. Accordingly, this single-centre, prospective, randomised, double-blind, controlled study is designed to evaluate the effects of taVNS on perioperative adverse psychological states in patients undergoing thoracoscopic surgery.

This study protocol should be interpreted within the context of several potential limitations. First, it is a single-centre investigation, which may limit the generalisability of the findings. Future research should include multicentre studies with larger sample sizes to enhance external validity and confirm the results. Second, the follow-up period in this study was relatively short, lasting only 1 month. This limitation stems from the study’s primary focus on short-term outcomes; however, long-term effects warrant further investigation and should be addressed in subsequent studies. Third, the therapeutic efficacy of taVNS was not objectively assessed using biomarkers such as serum inflammatory markers or neuroimaging techniques to evaluate changes in central nervous system activity. To strengthen the mechanistic interpretation of taVNS effects, future studies are encouraged to incorporate objective physiological and neurobiological measures to validate its impact on the vagus nerve–inflammation pathway and associated brain regions.

Structural and Thermodynamic Analyses of the Complexation of Pr(III), Nd(III), and Eu(III) with Lactate for Simulating the TALSPEAK Process
Authors: Yanyu Zhao, Qian Liu, Shaokang Chen, Hongming Zhang, Yan Zhang, Suliang Yang, Guoxin Tian
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe complexation of Pr(III), Nd(III), and Eu(III) with lactic acid/lactate (HA/A–) in aqueous solution was reinvestigated using spectroscopic and potentiometric titrations. In addition to the well-confirmed 1:1, 1:2, and 1:3 complexes (LnA2+, LnA2+, and LnA3), more attention was given to the arguable 1:4 species (LnA4–) previously reported. With spectroscopic methods, the LnA4– species was clearly identified in aqueous solutions, and the capability and limitation of the potentiometric method were evaluated and discussed. With the confirmed stability constants for the four complexes, a simulation of the TALSPEAK process (Trivalent Actinide Lanthanide Separation with Phosphorus-Reagent Extraction from Aqueous Complexes) was performed, clarifying the error caused by the previously missing 1:4 species. It was found that high concentrations of lactic acid/lactate (over 2 mol/L) slightly affect the extraction of Ln(III) in the TALSPEAK process, but it might not be accountable for the considerable difference between experimental results and predictions from the simulation using currently adapted model.

Introduction

The rapid development of nuclear energy has generated a large amount of spent nuclear fuel, which consists of approximately 95% unused uranium, 1% by-produced plutonium, 3% fission products (FPs), and a very small amount of minor actinides (Np, Am, Cm). In the currently deployed spent fuel reprocessing method, the Plutonium Uranium Reduction Extraction (PUREX) process, only uranium and plutonium are recovered, while fission products and minor actinide elements (MA) are restricted to high-level liquid waste (HLLW). To reduce long-term radiotoxicity, a partitioning and transmutation (p-T)1 strategy has been developed for the treatment and the disposal of HLLW. Since light lanthanides comprise about 30% of fission products, and some of these lanthanide isotopes have high neutron capture cross sections, they act as neutron poisons and must be separated from long-lived minor actinides before transmutation.

Several separation processes have been developed for separating MA from lanthanides in the p-T strategy. Among those, the TALSPEAK2−7 process (Trivalent Actinide Lanthanide Separation with Phosphorus-Reagent Extraction from Aqueous Complexes) has been extensively studied and tested on a pilot scale. In the TALSPEAK process, trivalent lanthanides Ln(III) are selectively extracted into the organic phase using an acidic phosphorus extractant, while trivalent actinides An(III) remain in the aqueous phase due to their preference for chelates that favor them. To date, HDEHP (bis-2-ethylhexyl phosphoric acid) is used as the organic phase extractant and DTPA (diethylenetriamine-N,N,N′,N″,N″-pentaacetic acid) is used as the aqueous buffer. The concentration of lactic acid ions ranges from 0.5 to 2 mol/L.

The thermodynamic properties of chemical reactions involved in the TALSPEAK process can help accurately predict the parameters for practical operations.2 Mechanisms and reactions in the TALSPEAK process have been extensively investigated in previous studies. The models based on these results generally provide parameters that match experimental data well in the low pH region. However, in the high pH region, the observed extraction efficiencies of Ln(III) are significantly lower than the predicted values. Great efforts have also been made on improving the model of the TALSPEAK process. The presence of ternary complexes Ln(lac)(HDEHP)x and Ln(lac)(DTPA), which represents an unknown aspect of TALSPEAK,2 has been reported and many attempts have been made to obtain information on the related compounds. However, while these new findings provide some insight, they still do not satisfactorily explain the discrepancy between the experimental and predicted extraction efficiencies.

To further clarify the mechanisms and improve the prediction model for the TALSPEAK process, we have been carefully examined the potentially involved reactions one by one. Initially, we focussed on the buffer reagent, lactic acid/lactate. Previous studies have well-confirmed three complex species of Ln(III) with lactate, 1:1, 1:2, and 1:3 (denoted as LnA2+, LnA2+, LnA3). However, a fourth species, LnA4–, has been reported to exist in small quantities, as identified through spectroscopy8 and crystal structure analysis.9 In the present study, the interaction between Ln(III) and the buffer reagent lactic acid/lactate was revisited by using a spectroscopic titration method in combination with a potential titration method. Due to their characteristic absorption spectra and high molar extinction coefficients, Pr(III) and Nd(III) were selected as representative lanthanide elements for absorption spectroscopy experiments. Eu(III) was selected as the subject of fluorescence spectroscopy experiments due to its unique emission characteristics. The limitations of commonly used potentiometry were discussed, and with the newly obtained information, the effect of the coordination of Ln(III) with lactate on extraction was evaluated.

Experimental Section

Chemicals

All chemicals used were reagent-grade or higher. Milli-Q water was used in the preparation of all solutions. Stock solutions of Nd(III) perchlorate, Pr(III) perchlorate, and Eu(III) perchlorate were prepared by dissolving Nd2O3, Pr2O3, or Eu2O3 in perchloric acid (70%, Aldrich). The concentrations of the lanthanides and perchloric acid in the stock solutions were determined by ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid complexometry10 and Gran’s titration,11 respectively. A stock solution of lactic acid (i.e., 2-hydroxypropanoic acid) was prepared by appropriate dilutions of 85% dl-lactic acid solution (Aldrich). The concentration of lactic acid in the stock solution was determined by back-titration.12 Given the comparable coordination affinities of Ln(III) ions for nitrate and the fourth lactate anion, perchloric acid was employed as the background acid in this study. The ionic strength of all working solutions was maintained at 1.0 mol/L NaClO4.

Potentiometry

The lactate protonation and complexation of Ln(III) with lactate were determined by a potentiometric titration. A detailed description of the titration setup can be found in literature elsewhere.13 Before each titration, electrode calibration titration was performed using standardized HClO4 and NaOH solutions. All of the potentiometric titration experiments were carried out under an argon atmosphere to avoid the effects from CO2 in the air, and the temperature was maintained at 25.0 ± 0.1 °C by an external water bath. During the titration process, the ion strength of the solution was maintained at 1.0 mol/L NaClO4. The stability constant of the complex was calculated using Hyperquad software.14 Multiple titrations were conducted to reduce the error.

Spectrophotometric Titration

Absorption spectra of Nd(III) and Pr(III) were collected on a Varian Cary-6000i spectrophotometer at 25.0 ± 0.1 °C. The temperature of the sample and reference cell holders was controlled via an external temperature controller, which drives a Peltier heat pump attached to holders. Multiple titrations with different concentrations of Nd(III) or Pr(III) were performed. In each titration, appropriate aliquots of the titrant (1.0 mol/L sodium lactate) were added into the cell (CNd0 = 0.0745 mol/L, CPr0 = 0.0622 mol/L) and mixed thoroughly with an electromagnetic stirrer (for 1–2 min) before the spectrum was collected. Quartz cells with a 10 mm optical path length were used. The spectral scanning ranges are 560–600 nm for Nd(III) and 425–500 nm for Pr(III), with a data interval of 0.25 nm and a slit width of 1.0 nm. Neutralizing 95% sodium lactate was used as a titrant in the experiment to control pH. Stability constants of complexes were calculated and molar absorption spectra were deconvoluted using HypSpec program.15

Fluorescence Spectroscopy

Fluorescence emission spectra and the lifetime of Eu(III) in aqueous solutions were acquired on a Horiba Jobin Yvon IBH Fluorolog-3 fluorimeter adapted for time-resolved measurements. Quartz cells of 10 mm optical path length were used. The titration method is consistent with those of Pr(III) and Nd(III). Before each measurement of the fluorescence emission spectrum of the sample, a 50 mmol/L Eu(ClO4)3 standard substance sample is used to test the stability of the fluorescence. The fluorescence intensity at 614 nm is then standardized based on this concentration. A submicrosecond xenon flash lamp (Jobin Yvon, 5000XeF) served as the light source and was coupled to a double-grating excitation monochromator for spectral selection. The input pulse energy (100 nF discharge capacitance) was approximately 50 mJ, and the optical pulse duration was less than 300 ns at full-width at half-maximum. A thermoelectrically cooled singlephoton detection module (Horiba Jobin Yvon IBH, TBX-04-D) that incorporates a fast-rise-time photomultiplier tube, a wide bandwidth preamplifier, and a picosecond constant-fraction discriminator was used as the detector. Signals were acquired using an IBH Data Station Hub, and data were analyzed using the commercially available DAS 6 decay analysis software package from Horiba Jobin Yvon IBH. The goodness of fit was assessed by minimizing the reduced function, χ2, and visually inspecting the weighted residuals. Each trace contained at least 10 000 points, and the reported lifetime values resulted from at least three independent measurements.

Spectrometric Titration of Pr(III), Nd(III), and Eu(III) with Lactate

Due to the low solubility of the complexes of heavy lanthanide ions, titrations with relatively high concentrations of Ln(III) and lactate are only suitable for light lanthanide ions to obtain the stability constants of the 1:4 complexes. Therefore, the stability constants for PrA4– and NdA4– were determined using both spectroscopic and potentiometric titrations, while the stability constant for EuA4– was determined using only the spectroscopic method.

For the titration of Pr(III), three absorption bands in the region of 425 to 500 nm were monitored. The band around 445 nm, which corresponds to the ultrasensitive transition of Pr3+ from 3H4 to 3P2, is usually selected for spectrophotometric study on Pr(III) complexes. With the addition of titrant sodium lactate, the absorbance intensities for the three bands gradually decrease, and the peaks present red-shifts. Factor analysis with Hypspec program indicates that there are five species of absorptivity existing in the system, which reasonably could be assigned as Pr3+, PrA2+, PrA2+, PrA3, and PrA4–. The calculated stability constants are listed in Table 1.

For the titration of Nd(III), the absorption band at 575 nm, which corresponds to the 4I9/2 → 4G5/2, 2G7/2 ultrasensitive transitions, was chosen to monitor the change in the absorption due to the complexation with lactate. Similar model for stability constants and procedure for spectra deconvolution were used for processing the collected spectra during the titration.

Eu(III) Lactate Complex: Fluorescence Spectroscopy

For the titrations of Eu(III), the fluorescence emission spectra of Eu(III) originating from the electronic transitions from the lowest excited state, 5D0, to the ground-state manifold, 7FJ (J = 0, 1, 2, 3,...) were chosen to monitor the complexation with lactate. As shown in Figure 2c, except for the intensity of the 5D0 →7F1 transition (around 590–600 nm) not being significantly affected due to its origination of a magnetic dipole transition, other branches increased significantly as lactate was added.16 The deconvoluted spectra of the complexes (Figure 2f) clearly illustrate the variation of the Eu(III) fluorescence emission spectra with the successive formation of the complexes. The stability constants were calculated and listed in Table 1 together with those reported previously.

Discussion

Discussion on the Coordination Mode of Eu(III) Lactate Based on Fluorescence Lifetime: Does the Complex EuA4– contain One Water Molecule in the Inner Coordination Sphere?

In theory, while there is no other nonradiative de-excitation mechanisms, such as energy transfer from the excited state of Eu(III) to lower-energy states of directly bonded ligands, the fluorescence decay rate is usually linearly correlated with the number of high-frequency oscillators existing in the first coordination sphere of Eu(III). A directly coordinated water molecule contains two O–H oscillators, while the hydroxy group of a lactate bonded to Eu(III) provides one O–H oscillator, which is counted as a half of the hydration number. Therefore, since a lactate bonds to Eu(III) in a five-membered ring mode, replacing two hydration water molecules, the change in hydration number is 1.5 rather than two. Following this rule, the hydration number was calculated based on the speciation. As shown in Figure 3c, starting with the experimentally determined hydration number of 8.9, there is little formation of EuA4– in the early stages of titration. Consequently, the hydration numbers obtianed from the calculations are in good agreement with the experimentally determined values. In contrast, in the later stages of the titration, where more EuA4– is formed, the deviation becomes more significant, with the calculated values being higher than the measured values. We then assumed that, in the final step of the coordination reaction, one lactate might replace all the hydration water molecules in EuA3 (aq.) to form an eight-coordinated EuA4– complex without any directly bonded water. With this assumption, for the whole titration the calculated hydration numbers agreed very well with the determined values. The existing crystallographic data confirm the existence of octahedral eight-coordination structure, which is consistent with steric hindrance.9 Therefore, we conclude that the EuA4– complex in aqueous solution is an eight-coordinated species without hydration water in the first coordination sphere. In contrast, the other three complexes, EuA2+, EuA2+, and EuA3 (aq.), are mostly nine-coordinated species, similar to Eu3+.

In previous studies, the model has been thoroughly validated.2 However, the model for simulating the extraction of M(III) did not include the fourth complex species of M(III) with lactate due to the lack of reliable stability constants. Therefore, using the confirmed stability constant of NdA4– as an example, we conducted a simulation of the TALSPEAK process for comparison.

As shown in Figure 5, the difference in speciation and extraction between the simulations with and without NdA4– increases with the increased Na/HLac concentration. With 1 mol/L Na/HLac in the aqueous solution, there is no significant difference in the simulation results between the models with and without the fourth Nd(III)-Lactate complex. As the concentration of Na/HLac is increased to 3 mol/L, the difference is clearly considerable. By including the formation of the fourth complex in the model of simulation, to some degree the result is improved to match the experimental observation with decreasing percentage of the extracted species, especially in the pH region of 2–4. However, the significant deviation from experimental results in the region of high pH indicates that there are other missing factors for the simulation. For instance, the dedimerization of (HP)2 at high pH region might have essential effect on the extraction, but it is not considered in the simulation.25,26 Further investigations are planned for future studies.

The Drawbacks of the Potentiometric Titration Method

In previous studies, potentiometric titration was commonly used to determine the stability constants of Ln(III) complexes with lactate. To evaluate the capability of the method, simulative titrations of Nd(III) with lactate were conducted at varying Nd(III) concentrations. As shown in Figure 6, starting with 0.01 mol/L Nd(III) in the initial titration solution, the difference between the two models with or without the 1:4 species NdA4– is ignorable. However, with higher Nd(III) concentrations (≥0.05 mol/L), the deference in pH becomes sufficient to distinguish the formation of NdA4– and accurately calculate the stability constants. We may conclude that for relatively weak complexation, such as Ln(III) with lactate, higher reagents concentrations are necessary for measuring the stability constants more accurately. Hence, in the present study, the potential titrations were conducted with Ln(III) concentration of about 0.2 mol/L. Indeed, the results are in very good agreement with those from the corresponding spectroscopic titrations.

Summary

Four complex species of Ln(III) with lactate were identified in aqueous solutions using a combination of spectroscopic and potentiometric methods, and the corresponding stability constants were obtained. The coordination mode of lactate with Ln(III) in the complexes in aqueous solutions was clarified through fluorescence emission lifetime measurements of Eu(III) complexes. Including the 1:4 species in the model for simulating the TALSPEAK process shows improvement, but it not sufficient to fully account for the deviation from experimental observations. Efforts should be directed toward investigating other possible factors that are essential but currently missing in the TALSPEAK process.

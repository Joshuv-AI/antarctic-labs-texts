Neutrophil-to-HDL-C Ratio and Anatomically Significant Multivessel Disease Across Acute Coronary Syndrome Phenotypes
Authors: Yusuf YILMAZ, Sera CELIK, Adem ATICI, Fatma Betül OZCAN, Arda DEMIR, Muzaffer KAHYAOGLU, Mehmet CELIK, Kıvanç Güneş ALTUN, Ömer Faruk BAYCAN, Şeref KUL, Mustafa CALISKAN
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe neutrophil-to-high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio (NHR) is an emerging inflammatory-lipid biomarker. Whether NHR is associated with anatomically significant multivessel disease (MVD) across different acute coronary syndrome (ACS) phenotypes remains unclear.MethodsWe analyzed 421 consecutive ACS patients [268 with non-ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI) and 153 with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI)] who underwent coronary angiography. Anatomically significant MVD was defined as ≥70% stenosis in at least two major epicardial coronary arteries, or ≥70% stenosis in one epicardial coronary artery in addition to ≥50% stenosis of the left main coronary artery. Receiver operating characteristic analysis and multivariable logistic regression were performed.ResultsAmong NSTEMI patients, NHR predicted anatomically significant MVD with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.694 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.630-0.757, p<0.001], using an optimal cut-off value of 0.1365 (78.0% sensitivity, 59.4% specificity). After multivariable adjustment, NHR remained independently associated with MVD (odds ratio: 5.870, 95% CI: 1.402-11.859, p<0.001). Among STEMI patients, NHR did not demonstrate discriminatory ability (AUC=0.481, p=0.690). In the overall ACS population, the NHR demonstrated modest discriminatory performance (AUC=0.615, p<0.001).ConclusionsNHR was associated with anatomically significant MVD mainly in patients with NSTEMI, whereas no meaningful discriminatory value was observed in patients with STEMI. This phenotype-specific finding suggests that NHR may better reflect the presence of MVD in NSTEMI than serve as a uniform marker across all ACS presentations.

INTRODUCTION

Multivessel coronary artery disease (CAD) is common among patients presenting with acute coronary syndrome (ACS) and has important implications for angiographic assessment, procedural planning, revascularization strategy, and subsequent clinical management. This is particularly relevant in non-ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI), a heterogeneous ACS phenotype in which the angiographic substrate may extend beyond a single culprit lesion1. Contemporary ACS guidelines emphasize the importance of coronary anatomy and disease extent in guiding individualized treatment decisions in both NSTEMI and ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) patients2, 3.

Despite its clinical relevance, the anatomical extent of CAD is not always predictable from routine clinical and laboratory parameters at presentation. This has increased interest in simple, inexpensive, and readily available biomarkers that may reflect the diffuse burden of coronary atherosclerosis. Given that ACS stems from a complex interplay of lipid dysregulation, vascular inflammation, plaque instability, and thrombotic activation, biomarkers integrating inflammatory and lipid-related pathways may provide additional insight into the presence of anatomically significant multivessel disease (MVD)4.

The neutrophil-to-high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio (NHR) is an inflammatory-lipid index that combines neutrophil-related inflammatory activity with high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C)-related vascular protection. Neutrophils contribute to atherosclerosis, plaque destabilization, thromboinflammation, and myocardial injury, whereas HDL-C has anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, endothelial-protective, and antithrombotic properties beyond reverse cholesterol transport5, 6. Previous studies have suggested that higher NHR is associated with coronary stenosis severity, anatomically significant CAD, and adverse outcomes in CAD or ACS populations7, 8, 9. However, most available data derive from mixed CAD or overall ACS cohorts, and evidence regarding the phenotype-specific relationship between NHR and anatomically significant MVD remains limited.

Pathophysiological differences between NSTEMI and STEMI suggest that the relationship between NHR and coronary disease burden may not be uniform across ACS presentations. STEMI is often dominated by abrupt culprit-vessel occlusion and infarct-related inflammatory activation, whereas NSTEMI may more frequently reflect a broader burden of diffuse atherosclerosis and multivessel involvement10. Therefore, this study aimed to elucidate whether the association between NHR and anatomically significant MVD differs according to ACS phenotype, with NSTEMI serving as the prespecified primary analysis cohort. STEMI and the overall ACS population were evaluated as secondary and exploratory cohorts, respectively.

MATERIALS and METHODS

Study Design and Population

Consecutive patients presenting with ACS who underwent coronary angiography at a tertiary center between January 2025 and January 2026 were enrolled in this single-center retrospective observational study. ACS was diagnosed according to contemporary guideline-based clinical, electrocardiographic, biochemical, and angiographic criteria, and patients were classified as having NSTEMI or STEMI based on the index presentation2, 3. The study was designed to evaluate whether the relationship between the NHR and anatomically significant MVD differs according to ACS phenotype.

Since the primary purpose involved evaluating phenotype-dependent associations between NHR and anatomically significant MVD, the NSTEMI cohort was established as the main analysis population. Supplementary Figure 1 illustrates the detailed study design and workflow.

STEMI patients and the overall ACS population were analyzed as secondary and exploratory cohorts, respectively, to assess whether the NHR-MVD association was consistent across different ACS phenotypes rather than being driven by a single pooled ACS signal.

Exclusion Criteria

Patients were excluded if they had clinical conditions that could substantially alter systemic inflammatory or lipid parameters independently of ACS. These included active infections, chronic inflammatory or autoimmune diseases, hematologic diseases, active malignancies, severe hepatic diseases, missing admission laboratory data required for NHR calculation, or incomplete angiographic information. Patients with prior coronary artery bypass grafting were also excluded because such revascularization may complicate the anatomical classification of native multivessel coronary disease. Patients receiving chronic renal replacement therapy or dialysis were also excluded.

Clinical and Laboratory Data

Baseline demographic characteristics, cardiovascular risk factors, clinical presentation, laboratory parameters, and echocardiographic findings were obtained from hospital records. Laboratory parameters were obtained from blood samples collected at admission before coronary angiography. Complete blood count parameters (including neutrophil count) and biochemical measurements (including HDL-C, creatinine, and other routine laboratory variables) were recorded. Left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) was obtained from transthoracic echocardiography performed during the index hospitalization.

The NHR was computed as the ratio of the absolute neutrophil count to the HDL-C level.

NHR=absolute neutrophil count/HDL-C

This ratio was selected because it integrates two biologically relevant components: neutrophil-mediated inflammatory activity and HDL-C-related vascular protection. NHR was evaluated as the principal laboratory biomarker of interest in relation to anatomically significant MVD.

Angiographic Assessment

All patients included in the analysis underwent invasive coronary angiography during the index hospitalization. The assessment of coronary angiograms was performed by experienced interventional cardiologists employing standard visual techniques. Coronary segments were assessed for the presence of anatomically significant luminal stenosis in the major epicardial coronary arteries, including the left main coronary artery. Anatomically significant CAD was defined as luminal stenosis of ≥70% in a major epicardial coronary artery or ≥50% in the left main coronary artery. Anatomically significant MVD was defined as ≥70% stenosis in at least two major epicardial coronary arteries, or ≥70% stenosis in one major epicardial coronary artery in addition to ≥50% stenosis of the left main coronary artery. Coronary angiograms were reviewed by two experienced interventional cardiologists, and disagreements were resolved by consensus. The presence or absence of MVD was recorded separately for the NSTEMI, the STEMI, and the overall ACS cohorts.

Study Groups and Outcomes

The primary analysis was performed on patients with NSTEMI. The primary outcome was the presence of anatomically significant MVD, as detected on coronary angiography. Secondary analyses evaluated the same association in the STEMI cohort and in the overall ACS population. This structure was used to determine whether NHR was associated with diffuse anatomical coronary disease burden in a phenotype-specific manner, rather than functioning as a uniform marker across all ACS presentations.

Statistical Analysis

SPSS software version 25.0 for Windows (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) was used to perform statistical analysis. The normality of continuous variables was evaluated using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov or Shapiro-Wilk tests, as appropriate. The presentation of continuous variables involved mean ± standard deviation or median (minimum-maximum) according to distribution, while categorical variables were presented as numbers and percentages.

The Student’s t-test or Mann-Whitney U test was used for continuous variables; the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was used for categorical variables when comparing patients with and without MVD.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was utilized to determine the discriminative potential of the NHR for anatomically significant MVD. Additional ROC analyses were performed in the NSTEMI cohort to compare the discriminatory performance of NHR to that of its individual components and of routinely available inflammatory markers, including neutrophil count, HDL-C, white blood cell (WBC) count, and C-reactive protein (CRP). The area under the curve (AUC), the corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs), sensitivity, specificity, and the optimal cut-off values determined by the Youden index were reported.

Univariable logistic regression analysis was performed to identify variables related to MVD in the NSTEMI cohort. The multivariable logistic regression model included variables demonstrating a univariable association with MVD (p<0.10), and clinically relevant covariates. Accordingly, age, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, albumin and creatinine levels, LVEF, and NHR were included in the final multivariable model. In all logistic regression models, NHR was evaluated as a continuous variable. The results were expressed as odds ratios (ORs) with corresponding 95% CIs.

Patients missing variables required for the corresponding analyses were excluded. Two-sided statistical tests were utilized throughout the analysis, with statistical significance established at p<0.05.

Ethics

The study received ethics approval from the Göztepe Prof. Dr. Süleyman Yalın City Hospital Non-Interventional Clinical Research Ethics Committee (approval number: 2026/0333, dated: 11.06.2026) and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Due to the retrospective observational nature of the study, the ethics committee exempted the research from the obligation to obtain written informed consent where applicable.

RESULTS

Baseline Characteristics

A total of 421 consecutive patients with ACS who fulfilled the inclusion criteria and had available medical records were included in the final analysis. The study population consisted of 268 patients (63.7%) presenting with NSTEMI and 153 (36.3%) presenting with STEMI. Anatomically significant MVD was identified in 172 patients (40.9%), whereas 249 patients (59.1%) had single-vessel or non-significant coronary involvement. Baseline clinical, laboratory, and echocardiographic characteristics according to MVD status are presented in Table 1.

Compared to patients without MVD, patients in the MVD (+) cohort were significantly older (66.72±11.66 vs. 62.94±11.54 years, p=0.001) and exhibited a higher prevalence of diabetes mellitus (40.7% vs. 26.5%, p=0.002). Regarding routine biochemical parameters, the MVD (+) group demonstrated significantly lower serum albumin levels (42.58±4.17 vs. 43.75±3.55 g/L, p=0.003) and higher urea levels (41.99±18.86 vs. 37.93±17.61 mg/dL, p=0.026). Additionally, left ventricular systolic function was compromised in the presence of diffuse disease, with significantly lower LVEF values observed in the MVD (+) group (48.60±11.61% vs. 52.47±11.49%, p=0.001).

Analysis of inflammatory and lipid pathways revealed distinct hematological differences; absolute neutrophil counts on admission were significantly elevated in the MVD (+) group [6.61 (2.17-27.55) vs. 5.72 (1.42-20.06)×103/µL, p=0.042], whereas HDL-C levels were significantly lower (39.21±9.96 vs. 43.03±10.99 mg/dL, p<0.001). Consequently, the principal laboratory biomarker of interest, the NHR, was significantly higher in patients with anatomically significant MVD than in those without [0.18 (0.06-0.98) vs. 0.14 (0.03-1.06), p<0.001].

Distribution of NHR According to MVD Status

The distribution of NHR according to MVD status in NSTEMI and STEMI patients is shown in Figure 1. In the NSTEMI cohort, patients with MVD demonstrated significantly higher NHR values compared with those without MVD (p<0.001). Conversely, NHR values were comparable between MVD and non-MVD patients in the STEMI cohort (p=0.690).

These findings suggest that the relationship between NHR and angiographic disease burden may differ according to ACS phenotype and may be more pronounced in NSTEMI.

ROC Analysis of NHR for Predicting MVD

ROC analyses are illustrated in Figure 2, and the corresponding diagnostic performance metrics are summarized in Table 2. In the overall ACS population, NHR demonstrated modest discriminatory ability for identifying anatomically significant MVD (AUC=0.615, 95% CI: 0.561-0.669, p<0.001). NHR demonstrated its strongest discriminatory performance in the prespecified NSTEMI cohort (AUC=0.694, 95% CI: 0.630-0.757, p<0.001), with an optimal cut-off value of 0.1365 providing 78.0% sensitivity and 59.4% specificity. In contrast, no significant discriminative ability was observed in STEMI patients (AUC=0.481, 95% CI: 0.388-0.575, p=0.690). Additional ROC analyses were performed to evaluate whether the discriminatory performance of NHR was mainly driven by its individual components or by conventional inflammatory markers. In the NSTEMI cohort, NHR showed the highest discriminatory performance for anatomically significant MVD compared with those of neutrophil count, WBC count, CRP, and HDL-C (Supplementary Table 1 and Supplementary Figure 2). HDL-C showed an inverse association, consistent with its lower levels in patients with MVD.

Logistic Regression Analysis

Univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses assessing the factors related to MVD in the NSTEMI cohort are presented in Table 3.

In the univariable analysis, older age, diabetes mellitus, lower serum albumin, higher creatinine levels, lower LVEF, and NHR values were significantly associated with anatomically significant MVD in NSTEMI patients. Among routinely available inflammatory markers, CRP was not significantly associated with MVD in univariable analysis.

After adjusting for these clinically relevant covariates in the multivariable logistic regression model, NHR remained a significant independent predictor of anatomically significant MVD (OR: 5.870, 95% CI: 1.402-11.859, p<0.001) in the NSTEMI cohort. Along with NHR, the presence of hypertension (OR: 2.074, 95% CI: 1.118-3.846, p=0.021), diabetes mellitus (OR: 2.257, 95% CI: 1.286-3.962, p=0.005), lower albumin levels (OR: 0.967, 95% CI: 0.945-0.990, p=0.005), and higher creatinine levels (OR: 1.030, 95% CI: 1.005-1.055, p=0.017) were also emerged as independent predictors of MVD specifically in the NSTEMI phenotype.

DISCUSSION

The primary finding of the present study is that the association between NHR and anatomically significant MVD was observed primarily in patients with NSTEMI, whereas no meaningful discriminatory signal was found in the STEMI cohort. In the overall ACS population, NHR showed only modest discriminatory performance. This suggests that the pooled ACS result may partly reflect the combination of clinically and biologically different presentations10, 11. Therefore, NHR may not act as a uniform marker across all ACS patients, but rather as a phenotype-dependent indicator of angiographic MVD.

This finding may be explained by the pathophysological changes in patients NSTEMI with MVD, which often represents a more diffuse atherosclerotic substrate rather than a single isolated culprit-vessel event1. In such patients, a marker that combines neutrophil-related inflammation and reduced HDL-C-mediated vascular protection may better reflect the broader anatomical burden of CAD. This interpretation is also consistent with previous studies linking NHR with coronary stenosis severity and angiographic disease burden7-9,12. In contrast, STEMI is more often dominated by abrupt culprit-vessel thrombosis and infarct-related inflammatory activation13. For this reason, admission NHR in STEMI may reflect the acute inflammatory response to myocardial injury more than it does the number or distribution of severely stenotic epicardial vessels.

The inflammatory component of this relationship is supported by the known role of neutrophils in atherosclerosis, plaque destabilization, and thromboinflammation. Neutrophil activation, including neutrophil-related thrombosis, may provide a link between systemic inflammation and more complex coronary atherosclerosis14, 15. However, our findings should be interpreted carefully. This study cannot demonstrate that neutrophil activation directly causes MVD. Rather, NHR may be considered a simple peripheral marker of a broader inflammatory and atherosclerotic milieu in NSTEMI patients.

The HDL-C component of NHR also adds biological meaning to the ratio. HDL has anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, endothelial-protective, antithrombotic, and immunoregulatory effects beyond reverse cholesterol transport16. During systemic inflammation, these protective functions may be impaired. Therefore, a higher NHR may reflect not only increased innate immune activation, but also reduced HDL-related vascular protection. This dual structure may help explain why NHR was associated with anatomically significant MVD in NSTEMI. This interpretation was further supported by additional ROC analyses in which NHR showed higher discriminatory performance than any of neutrophil count, WBC count, CRP, or HDL-C alone. These findings suggest that the combined inflammatory-lipid structure of NHR may be more informative than its individual components or conventional inflammatory markers in the NSTEMI cohort.

The absence of a significant anatomical signal in STEMI should not be interpreted as evidence that NHR has no clinical value in this phenotype. Previous studies have associated NHR with in-hospital adverse events, long-term outcomes, left ventricular remodeling, and post-percutaneous coronary intervention prognosis in STEMI or acute myocardial infarction populations17, 18, 19, 20. However, these studies mainly evaluated prognostic or remodeling-related outcomes, whereas the present study focused on angiographically defined MVD, which is a different endpoint. A biomarker may predict adverse clinical outcomes through infarct size, microvascular dysfunction, systemic inflammation, or post-infarction remodeling without necessarily identifying the anatomical extent of epicardial coronary disease.

From a clinical perspective, NHR should be viewed as a simple, low-cost biomarker rather than a standalone diagnostic tool. In NSTEMI patients, it may help support clinical assessment together with presentation, electrocardiogram findings, troponin dynamics, echocardiography, renal function, and established risk scores2, 3. Nevertheless, its discriminatory performance was modest, and coronary angiography remains essential for defining coronary anatomy and guiding revascularization decisions. Given the retrospective design and the lack of detailed information on smoking status and chronic medication use, NHR should be viewed as an adjunctive, hypothesis-generating marker rather than a definitive risk stratification tool in clinical practice. Future prospective studies combining NHR with angiographic complexity scores, intracoronary imaging, physiological assessment, and long-term outcomes may clarify whether NHR reflects diffuse atherosclerotic burden, residual inflammatory risk, or both21, 22.

Study Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, this was a retrospective, observational, single-center study; therefore, causal interpretation is not possible, and the generalizability of the findings may be limited. Although multivariable adjustment was performed, residual confounding from unmeasured clinical or metabolic factors cannot be excluded. In particular, detailed data on smoking status and chronic medication use, including lipid-lowering, antiplatelet, anti-inflammatory, and other cardiovascular therapies, were not uniformly available. Because these variables may influence neutrophil count, HDL-C levels, systemic inflammatory activity, and coronary atherosclerotic burden, the current findings should be interpreted as exploratory rather than as evidence of causality.

Second, NHR was calculated from a single routine laboratory assessment at admission. Both neutrophil count and HDL-C level may be affected by acute stress, systemic inflammation, occult infection, and other clinical conditions present at admission. Therefore, NHR should be considered an associative peripheral biomarker rather than a direct mechanistic marker of coronary atherosclerotic burden. In addition, more specific inflammatory biomarkers such as interleukin-6 and high-sensitivity CRP were not routinely available in this retrospective dataset. Therefore, the present study could not compare NHR with a broader panel of inflammatory biomarkers.

Third, MVD was defined based on anatomical (angiographic) stenosis. Although this definition captures the presence of anatomically significant multivessel involvement, it does not reflect overall lesion complexity, plaque morphology, functional significance, or microvascular disease. The study also did not include validated angiographic complexity scores such as the SYNTAX score, intracoronary imaging, physiological assessment, microvascular evaluation, plaque vulnerability assessment, or direct measures of residual inflammatory risk. As a result, we could not determine whether higher NHR reflects diffuse plaque burden, lesion complexity, plaque instability, systemic inflammation, or a combination of these mechanisms.

Finally, HDL functionality and long-term clinical follow-up data were not available. Therefore, the qualitative contribution of HDL-related vascular protection and the prognostic implications of the NSTEMI-specific NHR-MVD association could not be evaluated. Prospective, multicenter studies incorporating anatomical, physiological, and clinical outcome data are needed to validate these findings.

CONCLUSION

NHR was associated with anatomically significant MVD, mainly in patients with NSTEMI, whereas no meaningful discriminatory value was observed in STEMI. This phenotype-dependent pattern suggests that NHR may better reflect diffuse coronary disease burden in NSTEMI rather than serving as a uniform marker across all ACS presentations. As a simple and low-cost parameter derived from routine laboratory tests, NHR may be considered an adjunctive marker in the clinical assessment of NSTEMI patients. However, its discriminatory performance was modest, and it should be interpreted in conjunction with established clinical, biochemical, and angiographic parameters rather than used as a standalone diagnostic tool.

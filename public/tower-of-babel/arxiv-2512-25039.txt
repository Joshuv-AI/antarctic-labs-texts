The Hochschild homology of a noncommutative symmetric quotient stack
Rina Anno, Vladimir Baranovsky, Timothy Logvinenko

Abstract
. We prove an orbifold type decomposition theorem for the Hochschild homology of the symmetric powers of a small DG category 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} . In noncommutative geometry, these can be viewed as the noncommutative symmetric quotient stacks of 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} . We use this decomposition to show that the total Hochschild homology of the symmetric powers of 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} is isomorphic to the symmetric algebra S ∗ ​ ( {HH} ∙ ⁡ ( 𝒜 ) ⊗ t ​ 𝕜 ​ [ t ] ) S^{*}(\hochhom_{\bullet}({\mathcal{A}})\otimes t{\mathbb{k}}[t]) . Our methods are explicit - we construct mutually inverse homotopy equivalences of the standard Hochschild complexes involved. These explicit maps are then used to induce from the symmetric algebra onto the total Hochschild homology the structures of the Fock space for the Heisenberg algebra of 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} , of a Hopf algebra, and of a free λ \lambda -ring generated by {HH} ∙ ⁡ ( 𝒜 ) \hochhom_{\bullet}({\mathcal{A}}) .



1.
Introduction

This paper is a step towards developing “orbifold cohomology theory”
for DG categories. It derives its inspiration from the computation of
Hochschild and cyclic homology of twisted group rings
(crossed product rings).

Our starting point is the following well-known result. Let

X=\spec A
be a smooth affine variety over an algebraically
closed field
{\mathbb{k}}
of characteristic
0
. Let
G
be a finite
group acting on
X
via a group homomorphism
\alpha:G\to Aut(X)
.
Let
A_{\alpha}[G]
be the corresponding twisted group ring.
Its homology groups admit the following decomposition:

\hochcx_{\bullet}(A_{\alpha}[G],W)\simeq\big(\bigoplus_{g\in G}\hochcx_{\bullet}(A(X^{g}),W)\big)_{G}

(1.1)

Here
\hochcx_{\bullet}(\cdot,W)
is the cyclic homology with coefficients
in a module
W
: for different
W
this gives Hochschild, cyclic and periodic
cyclic homology

[
GJ93
, §4]
.
Furthermore,
A(X^{g})
is the
algebra of regular functions on the fixed point set
X^{g}
and

\big(\ldots\big)_{G}
denotes taking the coinvariants of the

G
action
g\colon X^{h}\rightarrow X^{ghg^{-1}}
.

The decomposition (
1.1
)
has two steps. The first step is
based on spectral sequence constructed by Feigin and Tsygan

[
FT87
]

for any
G
acting on a unital algebra
A
over a commutative ring
R
.
For finite
G
whose order is invertible in
R
,
the spectral sequence reduces to a decomposition into a direct sum of
the twists
{\vphantom{A}}{}_{g}A
of the trivial
A\text{-}A
-bimodule by action
of
g\in G

[
GJ93
, §4]
.
For a smooth commutative
A
, the second step is the interpretation of
these direct summands by Brylinski in terms of the fixed point sets
of the
G
-action on
X=\spec A

[
Bry87
]
.

There are many geometrical generalisations of the decomposition
(
1.1
):
for smooth quasi-projective varieties over
{\mathbb{k}}

[
Bar03
]
,
as an orbifold version of the HKR isomorphism for a global quotient of a smooth variety
[
ACH19
]

and a recent version for derived Deligne-Mumford stacks

[
LS25
]
.

Our aim is a noncommutative generalisation of
(
1.1
)
replacing
A
by a smooth and proper DG category
{\mathcal{A}}
. We are guided
by a noncommutative geometry perspective
which views small DG categories considered up to Morita equivalence
as noncommutative schemes

[
KS09
,
KKP07
,
Kal07
,
Orl16
,
Efi20
]
.
The first step of the noncommutative decomposition
(
1.1
)
was established by Nordström for any small DG category
{\mathcal{A}}
over
a field
{\mathbb{k}}
with
\chr({\mathbb{k}})=0

[
Nor25b
]
.
The more delicate second step with its interpretation in
terms of the fixed point loci, where we expect the smoothness
of
{\mathcal{A}}
to become necessary, remains open.

In this paper, we do it for the symmetric group
S_{n}

acting on
{\mathcal{A}}^{\otimes n}

.
The geometric fixed point loci are the diagonals

which are easily interpreted as tensor powers

. The conjugacy classes of

are given by

Write

in

The fixed point locus of

By the Künneth formula

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
DG categories, modules and tensor products

We briefly recall here the basics on DG categories (always assumed small in
this paper) mostly to fix the notation, sending the reader to
[
Kel06
]

for a more detailed treatment.
A DG category over a field
{\mathbb{k}}
is a category
{\mathcal{A}}
in which for any
pair of objects
a,b
the set
{\vphantom{{{\mathcal{A}}}}}{}_{a}{{\mathcal{A}}}_{b}
of morphisms

b\to a
is a differential graded
{\mathbb{k}}
-module, i.e. a complex of
vector spaces over
{\mathbb{k}}
. The inversion of order in the notation

{\vphantom{{{\mathcal{A}}}}}{}_{a}{{\mathcal{A}}}_{b}
for morphisms makes it easier to identify composable morphisms
when working with Hochschild complexes
as compositions of morphisms are described by
morphisms of complexes
{\vphantom{{{\mathcal{A}}}}}{}_{a}{{\mathcal{A}}}_{b}\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}\;{\vphantom{{{\mathcal{A}}}}}{}_{b}{{\mathcal{A}}}_{c}\to{\vphantom{{{\mathcal{A}}}}}_{a}{{\mathcal{A}}}_{c}
,
\alpha\otimes\beta\mapsto\alpha\beta
.

DG functors between DG categories are usual functors that are compatible with
differentials on morphisms.

A
left DG module

M
over a DG category
{\mathcal{A}}
is a DG functor

M:{\mathcal{A}}\to C_{dg}({\mathbb{k}})
taking values in the DG category of complexes over

{\mathbb{k}}
. Similarly, a
right DG module
is a functor
{\mathcal{A}}^{op}
from the opposite
category. Hence for each object
a\in obj({\mathcal{A}})
we have a complex

{\vphantom{M}}{}_{a}M:=M(a)
with morphisms of complexes
{\vphantom{{{\mathcal{A}}}}}{}_{a}{{\mathcal{A}}}_{b}\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}{\vphantom{M}}{}_{b}M\to{\vphantom{M}}_{a}M
for any pair of objects
a,b
, and
similarly for right DG modules.

A
tensor product

{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}

of two DG categories
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
,
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
has the set of objects

\obj({\mathcal{A}}_{1})\times\obj({\mathcal{A}}_{2})
with the morphisms

{\vphantom{{\left({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}\right)}}}{}_{a_{1},a_{2}}{\left({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}\right)}_{b_{1},b_{2}}:={\vphantom{{\left({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\right)}}}_{a_{1}}{\left({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\right)}_{b_{1}}\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}{\vphantom{{\left({\mathcal{A}}_{2}\right)}}}{}_{a_{2}}{\left({\mathcal{A}}_{2}\right)}_{b_{2}}

and natural compositions (involving the Koszul sign rule) and units.

A
DG
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\text{-}{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
-bimodule

M
if a left DG module over
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}^{op}
which is
given by the complexes
{\vphantom{{M}}}{}_{a}{M}_{b}:=M(a,b)
similarly to the above.
Central to this paper is the following special case of this notion.
Let
F\colon{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}
be a DG functor. We define the bimodule

{\vphantom{{\mathcal{A}}}}{}_{F}{\mathcal{A}}\in{{\mathcal{A}}\text{-}\modd\text{-}{\mathcal{A}}}

by setting

{\vphantom{\left({\vphantom{{\mathcal{A}}}}{}_{F}{\mathcal{A}}\right)}}{}_{a}\left({\vphantom{{\mathcal{A}}}}{}_{F}{\mathcal{A}}\right)_{b}:={\vphantom{{{\mathcal{A}}}}}_{Fa}{{\mathcal{A}}}_{b},

and letting
{\mathcal{A}}
act naturally on the right and via
F
on the left:

\alpha.(\beta).\gamma=F(\alpha)\beta\gamma\in{\vphantom{{{\mathcal{A}}}}}_{Fc}{{\mathcal{A}}}_{d}\quad\quad\quad\forall\ \alpha\in{\vphantom{{{\mathcal{A}}}}}_{c}{{\mathcal{A}}}_{a},\ \beta\in{\vphantom{{{\mathcal{A}}}}}_{Fa}{{\mathcal{A}}}_{b},\ \gamma\in{\vphantom{{{\mathcal{A}}}}}_{b}{{\mathcal{A}}}_{d}.

In this paper, we have to work with equivariant DG categories ––

[

]

We give below a small overview to set up notation.

Let

and its subgroups, on the tensor powers

[
Nor25b
]

2.6.
Shuffles

The usual Künneth isomorphism works for DG categories with very little change.
As in the case of
{\mathbb{k}}
-algebras, one starts by defining shuffles:

Definition 2.11
.

Let
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
. Define the subset of
(p,q)
-shuffles

S_{p,q}:=\left\{\sigma\in S_{p+q}\;\middle|\;\sigma(i)<\sigma(j)\text{ if }\begin{matrix}1\leq i<j\leq p,\\
\text{ or }\\
p+1\leq i<j\leq p+q.\end{matrix}\right\}

(2.5)

The sign
(-1)^{\sigma}
of a shuffle
\sigma\in S_{p,q}
is its sign
as a permutation in
S_{n}
.

In other words,
(p,q)
-shuffles are the elements of the permutation
group
S_{p+q}
which preserve relative order of the first
p
and
the last
q
elements. Later in the paper we will need a computation
of the number defined as follows:

Definition 2.12
.

Let
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
. Define

Y_{p,q}:=\begin{cases}\binom{\lfloor\frac{p+q}{2}\rfloor}{\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\rfloor}\quad\quad&\text{ either }p\text{ or }q\text{ is even},\\
0,&\text{ otherwise. }\end{cases}

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
. Then

Y_{p,q}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{p,q}}(-1)^{\sigma}.

(2.6)

Proof.

We prove by induction on
p+q
.
All
(p,q)
-shuffles
\sigma
can be divided into those where the

\sigma^{-1}(1)=1
and
\sigma^{-1}(1)=p+1
. The former
correspond bijectively to
(p-1,q)
-shuffles of the same parity,
the latter correspond bijectively to
(p,q-1)
-shuffles whose
parity differs by
(-1)^{p}
. Thus

\sum_{\sigma\in S_{p,q}}(-1)^{\sigma}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{p-1,q}}(-1)^{\sigma}+(-1)^{p}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{p,q-1}}(-1)^{\sigma}.

By direct computation, we also have

Y_{p,q}=Y_{p-1,q}+(-1)^{p}Y_{p,q-1}.

(2.7)

It remains to establish (
2.6
)
in the cases where either
p=0
or
q=0
, which is trivial.
∎

We will also need below a slight generalisation of shuffles:

Definition 2.14
.

Let
P,Q\subset\left\{1,\dots,n\right\}
be two disjoint subsets.
Define the subset of
(P,Q)
-shuffles

S_{P,Q}:=\left\{\sigma\in S_{n}\;\middle|\;\begin{matrix}\sigma(i)<\sigma(j)\quad\text{ if }\quad i<j\text{ with }i,j\in P\text{ or }i,j\in Q,\quad\quad\\
\sigma(i)=i\quad\quad\;\;\text{ if }\quad i\neq P\cup Q\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\end{matrix}\right\}

(2.8)

The sign
(-1)^{\sigma}
of a shuffle
\sigma\in S_{P,Q}
is its sign
as a permutation in
S_{n}
.

Definition 3.5

Lemma 3.6

Lemma 3.7

On the equivariant triangulation of some small covers
Raju Kumar Gupta, Soumen Sarkar

Abstract
. In this paper, we study certain properties of ℤ 2 n \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n} -equivariant triangulations of small covers. We show that any ℤ 2 n \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n} -equivariant triangulation of a small cover naturally induces a triangulation of the orbit space. Then, we explicitly construct the minimal ℤ 2 3 \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3} -equivariant triangulation of ℝ ​ ℙ 3 \mathbb{RP}^{3} , which contains 11 11 vertices and prove that this is the unique ℤ 2 3 \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3} -equivariant triangulation of ℝ ​ ℙ 3 \mathbb{RP}^{3} with 11 11 vertices. For a finite group G G , we give a method for constructing some G G -equivariant triangulations of connected sums of manifolds from their respective G G -equivariant triangulations. In particular, we construct a ℤ 2 3 \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3} -equivariant triangulation of ℝ ​ ℙ 3 ​ # ​ ℝ ​ ℙ 3 \mathbb{RP}^{3}\#\mathbb{RP}^{3} with 17 17 vertices, which is the best known yet. This triangulation of ℝ ​ ℙ 3 ​ # ​ ℝ ​ ℙ 3 \mathbb{RP}^{3}\#\mathbb{RP}^{3} provides another minimal g g -vector improving one of the result of Lutz in [ 19 ] . Moreover, we prove that a ℤ 2 4 {\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{4} -equivariant triangulation of ℝ ​ ℙ 4 \mathbb{RP}^{4} requires at least 18 18 vertices.

1.
Introduction

The rich interplay between topology and combinatorics has led to many valuable discoveries, deepening our understanding of both fields. Triangulations of manifolds highlight such a connection by breaking complex spaces into simpler building blocks called simplices. This method supports both theoretical insights and computational applications. For decades, researchers have been interested in creating new triangulations and investigating the combinatorial features of topological spaces, see
[
9
]
,
[
14
]
. The question of the size of a triangulation has gained practical importance. A triangulation
X
of a topological space

is said to be
minimal
if, for any other triangulation
Y
of
M
, we have
f_{0}(X)\leq f_{0}(Y)
, where
f_{0}
denotes the number of vertices.
It is worthwhile to investigate the number of vertices and the total number of faces in a triangulation, as well as to explicitly construct minimal triangulations. Constructing new triangulations (possibly with minimal vertices) for projective spaces has been one of the known research topics in this area.

The minimal triangulation of
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
is given by
6
vertices, which is unique,
[
16
]
. A minimal triangulation of
\mathbb{RP}^{3}
was constructed by Walkup with
11
vertices, cf.
[
22
]
. In 1999, Lutz
[
18
]
constructed a
16
-vertex triangulation of
\mathbb{RP}^{4}
and showed that it is a minimal triangulation of
\mathbb{RP}^{4}
. In
[
3
]
, Balagopalan constructed 3 triangulation of
\mathbb{RP}^{4}
in different ways, which are eventually the same as the Lutz triangulation.

Conjecture 1.1
.

[

\mathbb{RP}^{4}

We recall some basic definitions for triangulation of manifolds following
[
20
]

A compact convex polyhedron which spans a subspace of dimension

-cell

Definition 2.1
.

A

X

for some

satisfying,

(i)
if
\sigma
is a face of
\tau

,
(ii)
if
\sigma,\tau\in X

then
\sigma\cap\tau
is a face of both
\sigma
and
\tau
. A cell complex

\sigma\in X

We denote a simplex
\sigma
with vertices

by
u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{k}
. Throughout the manuscript, we denote a standard
n
-simplex generated by the vectors

, where

The vertex set of a simplicial complex

. An

-vector

(d+2)
-tuple

, and for all

denotes the number of

-dimensional simplices in

. For a

-dimensional finite simplicial complex

is defined by

.

is one of the well known combinatorial invariant and its emergence goes back to Walkup

Let

be a simplicial complex and
\sigma\in X
. The

of a face

\{\tau\in X:\sigma\star\tau\in X~\mbox{and}~\sigma\cap\tau=\emptyset\}

, and is denoted by

. If

is a compact polyhedron and is referred to as the geometric carrier of

. For every face

in

, by

(or,

. For two vertices

denotes the semi-open and semi-closed edge

. By

, we denote the open edge

.
For a set

as the number of elements in

. The dimension of a face

is Card(

If

3.
Equivariant triangulation of
\mathbb{S}^{n}

In this section, we present some results on the equivariant triangulations of spheres and characterize the equivariant triangulations of the
n
-sphere with
2n+2
vertices.

Now, we discuss the natural action of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{n+1}
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. We consider

\mathbb{S}^{n}=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}:x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{n+1}^{2}=1\}

and

{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+1}_{2}:=\{-1,1\}^{n+1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
for
n\geq 0
. The group
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+1}_{2}
acts
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
by

(3.1)

(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n+1})\times(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})\to(t_{1}x_{1},\ldots,t_{n+1}x_{n+1}).

This is an effective smooth action, called standard action and denoted
by

\rho\colon{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{n+1}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}.

The sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is invariant under this action. The orbit space
S^{n}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{n+1}
is

\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}:0\leq x_{1},\ldots,0\leq x_{n+1}~\mbox{and}~x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{n+1}^{2}=1\}

which is affinely diffeomorphic as manifold with corners to the
n
-simplex
\Delta^{n}
. In this section, we consider this

{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{n+1}
-action on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}_{2}
-equivariant triangulation of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
for some
n\geq 2
. Then
\mathrm{Card}(V(X))
is even and
\mathrm{Card}(V(X))\geq 2n

Proof.

Let
\sigma\in X
be an
(n-1)
-simplex and the vertex set of
\sigma
be
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}
. Consider
\mu=(-1,\ldots,-1)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{n}
.
Suppose that
\mu(u_{i})=u_{j}
for some
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
i\neq j
. Let
u_{i}u_{j}
and
\mu(u_{i}u_{j})
be the same edges then
u_{i}u_{j}
must pass through origin, and thus
X
pass through origin.
Since the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{n}
-equivariant triangulation of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
does not pass through origin,
u_{i}u_{j}
and
\mu(u_{i}u_{j})

are two distinct edges in
X
. On the other hand
\mu^{2}
is identity in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{n}
. This implies that
u_{i}=\mu(u_{j})
and

u_{i}u_{j}\cap\mu(u_{i}u_{j})=\{u_{i},u_{j}\},
which is a contradiction.
Therefore,
\mu(u_{i})\notin\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}
for any
i=1,\ldots,n
. Since
u_{i}\neq u_{j}
for
i\neq j
,

\mu(u_{i})\neq\mu(u_{j})
for
i\neq j
. This proves that
\mathrm{Card}(V(X))\geq 2n
.

Further,
X
is an equivariant triangulation and the cardinality of the orbit
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{n}x
is even for every
x\in X
, and orbits induces a partition on the
V(X)
. Therefore,
\mathrm{Card}(V(X))
is even.

\square

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X
be a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{n+1}
-equivariant triangulation of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. If there is a vertex
v
in
X
with exactly
k~(3\leq k\leq n+1)
non-zero coordinates then there are at least
2^{k-1}(2^{k}-k-1)
missing edges in
X
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that the first
k
coordinates of
v
are nonzero. Let
v=(v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k},0,0,\ldots,0)
.

By the group action of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{n+1}
on the vertex
v
, we obtain
2^{k}
vertices in
X
whose first
k
coordinates are nonzero and the remaining coordinates are zero. Let
V
denote the set of all such vertices.

Let
x,y\in V
be two vertices whose coordinate signs differ at least at two positions. Suppose that
x(i)=-y(i)
and
x(j)=-y(j)
. Let
xy
be an edge in
X
. We consider the group element
\mu\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{n+1}
with
\mu(i)=-1
and
\mu(k)=1
for
k\neq i
. By acting with
\mu
on
xy
, we obtain an edge between two vertices
p
and
q
, where

p(i)=-x(i)
,
p(k)=x(k)
for all
k\neq i
, and
q(i)=-y(i)
,
q(k)=y(k)
for all
k\neq i
.

Let

xy=\{(\psi_{1}(t),\psi_{2}(t),\ldots,\psi_{i}(t),\ldots,\psi_{j}(t),\ldots,\psi_{k}(t),0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{S}^{n}:t\in[0,1]\}.

Then

pq=\{(\psi_{1}(t),\psi_{2}(t),\ldots,-\psi_{i}(t),\ldots,\psi_{j}(t),\ldots,\psi_{k}(t),0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{S}^{n}:t\in[0,1]\}.

Since
\psi_{i}:[0,1]\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is injective and continuous, with
\psi_{i}(0)=x(i)
and
\psi_{i}(1)=-x(i)
, there exists a unique
t^{\prime}\in(0,1)
such that
\psi_{i}(t^{\prime})=0
. This implies that

(\psi_{1}(t^{\prime}),\psi_{2}(t^{\prime}),\ldots,\psi_{i-1}(t^{\prime}),0,\psi_{i+1}(t^{\prime}),\ldots,\psi_{j}(t^{\prime}),\ldots,\psi_{k}(t^{\prime}),0,\ldots,0)\in xy\cap pq.

This is a contradiction because
\{x,y\}\cap\{p,q\}=\emptyset
, and therefore
xy\cap pq
must be empty in
X
.

Similarly, if
pq
is an edge, then using the group element
\epsilon\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{n+1}
with
\epsilon(j)=-1
and
\epsilon(k)=1
for
k\neq j
, we find that
\epsilon(pq)=xy
. Hence, the edges
xy
and
pq
must be missing edges in
X
.

This implies that each vertex in
V
can be joined only with those vertices in

Lemma 3.5

Let

Lemma 4.6

5.
Equivariant Triangulation of Connected Sums

In this section, we give a construction method to construct equivariant triangulations of connected sums of two manifolds.
Also, we give an equivariant triangulation of
\mathbb{RP}^{3}\#\mathbb{RP}^{3}
on
17
vertices. The minimum triangulation of
\mathbb{RP}^{3}\#\mathbb{RP}^{3}
is known on
15
vertices.

A
subcomplex
of a simplicial complex is a subcollection of simplices that also forms a simplicial complex. If
K
is a simplicial complex and
S\subseteq V(K)
is a subset then
\mathrm{Ind}_{K}(S)=\{\sigma\in K:V(\sigma)\subseteq S\}
is called the
induced subcomplex
of
K
induced on the vertex set
S
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
K
,
L
be the triangulation of
n
-manifolds
M
and
N
, respectively. Let
u
and
v
be two vertices in
K
and
L
, respectively, such that
\mbox{lk}\,(u,K)
and
\mbox{lk}\,(v,L)
are combinatorially isomorphic and
\mathrm{Ind}_{K}(V(\mbox{lk}\,(u,K)))=\mbox{lk}\,(u,K)
.
Suppose that
\psi:\mbox{lk}\,(u,K)\rightarrow\mbox{lk}\,(v,L)
is an isomorphism. Define
{\rm ast}(u,K)=\{\sigma\in K:u\notin\sigma\}
and
{\rm ast}(v,L)=\{\gamma\in L:v\notin\gamma\}
as the antistar of
u
and
v
in
K
and
L
respectively. Now, we define the generalized star-connected sum
K\#_{\psi}L
as the simplicial complex obtained after gluing the faces of
{\rm ast}(u,K)
with faces of
{\rm ast}(v,L)
according to the map
\psi
.

This generalized star-connected sum is the generalized version of the star-connected sum given in [Definition
1.2
,
[
13
]
]. Observe that
K\#_{\psi}L
is a triangulation of
M\#N
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
K,L
be two triangulation of an
n
-manifold
M
. Let
\mbox{lk}\,(u,K)
and
\mbox{lk}\,(v,L)
are combinatorially isomorphic and
\mathrm{Ind}_{K}(V(\mbox{lk}\,(u,K)))=\mbox{lk}\,(u,K)
. Suppose that
\psi:\mbox{lk}\,(u,K)\rightarrow\mbox{lk}\,(v,L)
is the isomorphism. Then
g_{2}(K\#_{\psi}L)=g_{2}(K)+g_{2}(L)-f_{1}(\mbox{lk}\,(u,K))+{(n-1)f_{0}(\mbox{lk}\,(u,K))-\frac{(n+1)(n-2)}{2}}.

Proof.

From the definition of
K\#_{\psi}L
, we have the following.

•

f_{0}(K\#_{\psi}L)=f_{0}(K)+f_{0}(L)-f_{0}(\mbox{lk}\,(u,K))-2
.

•

f_{1}(K\#_{\psi}L)=f_{1}(K)+f_{1}(L)-2f_{0}(\mbox{lk}\,(u,K))-f_{1}(\mbox{lk}\,(u,K))
.

Thus,

\displaystyle g_{2}(K\#_{\psi}L)=

\displaystyle f_{1}(K\#_{\psi}L)-(n+1)f_{0}(K\#_{\psi}L)+\binom{n+2}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}(K)+f_{1}(L)-2f_{0}(\mbox{lk}\,(u,K))-f_{1}(\mbox{lk}\,(u,K))

\displaystyle-(n+1)(f_{0}(K)+f_{0}(L)-f_{0}(\mbox{lk}\,(u,K)-2)+\binom{n+2}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}(K)-(n+1)f_{0}(K)+\binom{n+2}{2}+f_{1}(L)-(n+1)f_{0}(L)+\binom{n+2}{2}

\displaystyle+(n-1)f_{0}(\mbox{lk}\,(u,K))-f_{1}(\mbox{lk}\,(u,K))+2(n+1)-\binom{n+2}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{2}(K)+g_{2}(L)-f_{1}(\mbox{lk}\,(u,K))+(n-1)f_{0}(\mbox{lk}\,(u,K))-\frac{(n+1)(n-2)}{2}.

\square

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
K
,
L
be the
G
-equivariant triangulations of
n
-manifolds
M
and
N
, respectively for some finite group
G
. Let
u\in K
and
v\in L
be the fixed vertices under the group action. Let
K
and
L
satisfy the following:

•

There is a combinatorial isomorphism
\psi:\mbox{lk}\,(u,K)\rightarrow\mbox{lk}\,(v,L)
such that
\psi(\mu(x))=\mu(\psi(x))
for all
\mu\in G
and
x\in\mbox{lk}\,(u,K)
,

•

\mathrm{Ind}_{K}(V(\mbox{lk}\,(u,K)))=\mbox{lk}\,(u,K)
, and
\mathrm{Ind}_{L}(V(\mbox{lk}\,(v,L)))=\mbox{lk}\,(v,L).

Then
K\#_{\psi}L
is a
G
-equivariant triangulation of
M\#N
.

Proof.

We observe that
K\#_{\psi}L
is a triangulation of the connected sum
M\#N
.
It remains to show that this triangulation is
G
-equivariant.
Let
\mu\in G
be arbitrary.

Let
\sigma
be a
k
-simplex of
K\#_{\psi}L
for some
k\leq n
.
Let
S\subseteq V(K\#_{\psi}L)
denote the set of vertices that arise from the identification of vertices in
\mbox{lk}\,(u,K)
and
\mbox{lk}\,(v,L)
; that is, for each
z\in S
, there exist
x\in\mbox{lk}\,(u,K)
and
y\in\mbox{lk}\,(v,L)
such that
z
is the image of the identification of
x
and
y
.

Case 1.
Suppose that
V(\sigma)\cap S=\emptyset
.
Then either
\sigma\subseteq K\setminus\mbox{st}\,(u,K)
or
\sigma\subseteq L\setminus\mbox{st}\,(v,L)
.
Since both
K
and
L
are
G
-equivariant,
\mu(\sigma)
is also a
k
-simplex in
K\#_{\psi}L
.

Case 2.
Suppose that
V(\sigma)\cap S\neq\emptyset
. If
V(\sigma)\subset S
then
\sigma
is an identification of a simplex, say
\sigma^{\prime}
from
\mbox{lk}\,(u,K)
and a simplex, say
\sigma^{\prime\prime}
from
\mbox{lk}\,(v,L)
. Thus,
\mu(\sigma)
is a simplex in
K\#_{\psi}L
coming from identifying the simplices
\mu(\sigma^{\prime})
from
\mbox{lk}\,(u,K)
and
\mu(\sigma^{\prime\prime})
from
\mbox{lk}\,(v,L)
.

Now, consider that
V(\sigma)\not\subset S
.
Then
\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}\star\sigma^{\prime\prime}
where
\sigma^{\prime}
and
\sigma^{\prime\prime}
are two simplices and
V(\sigma)\cap S=V(\sigma^{\prime\prime})
.

Then corresponding to
V(\sigma^{\prime\prime})
, there exist subsets

S_{1}\subseteq V(\mbox{lk}\,(u,K))
and
S_{2}\subseteq V(\mbox{lk}\,(v,L))
such that the vertices of
S_{1}
are identified with those of
S_{2}
via
\psi
.
Let
\tau=\mathrm{Ind}_{K}(S_{1})
and
\tau^{\prime}=\mathrm{Ind}_{L}(S_{2})
be the simplices in
\mbox{lk}\,(u,K)
and
\mbox{lk}\,(v,L)
, respectively.
The identification
\tau\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\psi}{\sim}}\tau^{\prime}
forms
\sigma^{\prime\prime}
in
K\#_{\psi}L
.
Since
\mbox{lk}\,(u,K)
and
\mbox{lk}\,(v,L)
are
G
-equivariant,

\mu(\tau)
and
\mu(\tau^{\prime})
are simplices in
\mbox{lk}\,(u,K)
and
\mbox{lk}\,(v,L)
, respectively, and their identification gives the simplex

\mu(\sigma^{\prime\prime})
in
K\#_{\psi}L
.

On the other hand, either
\sigma^{\prime}\subseteq K\setminus\mbox{st}\,(u,K)

or
\sigma^{\prime}\subseteq L\setminus\mbox{st}\,(v,L)
.
Hence,
\mu(\sigma^{\prime})
is a simplex in
K\#_{\psi}L
.
Therefore, the join

\mu(\sigma^{\prime})\star\mu(\sigma^{\prime\prime})

is a
k
-simplex of
K\#_{\psi}L
.

Thus, in both cases,
\mu(\sigma)
is a simplex of
K\#_{\psi}L
.
Hence
K\#_{\psi}L
is
G
-equivariant.

\square

\mathbb{RP}^{4}

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
X
be a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{4}
-equivariant triangulation of
\mathbb{RP}^{4}
with less than 18 vertices. Let
\pi:|X|\rightarrow\Delta^{4}
be the orbit map. There is no face of dimension three or more in
\Delta^{4}
whose interior conatins a point
x
such that
\pi^{-1}(x)\cap V(X)\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let the vertex set of
\Delta^{4}
be
\{e_{0},e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
. Suppose that the interior of
\Delta^{4}
contains a point
x
such that
\pi^{-1}(x)
contains a vertex, say
x^{\prime}
in
X
. Then all four coordinates of
x^{\prime}
must be non-zero. Since
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{4}
acts on
x^{\prime}\in V(X)
, there are at least
16
vertices in
X
with all coordinates non-zero.

Define
D_{i}=\{\sigma\in X:e_{i}\in|\sigma|\}
for
0\leq i\leq 4
. Then each
D_{i}
is a
4
-dimensional ball, and
\partial D_{i}
is a subcomplex of
X
which forms a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{4}
-equivariant triangulation of the
3
-sphere.
Since
X
contains at most
17
vertices,
\partial D_{i}
must contain
\mu(x^{\prime})
for some
\mu\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{4}
. This implies that
\partial D_{i}
contains a vertex with all four coourdinates being non-zero. Since
\partial D_{i}
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{4}
-equivariant triangulation of a
3
-sphere,
\partial D_{i}
must contain at least
16
vertices with all the coordinates non-zero. On the other hand
\partial D_{i}
contains even number of vertices,
\mathrm{Card}(V(\partial D_{i}))=16
. From Lemma
3.3
, this is not possible.

Now, we consider the interior of a
3
-dimensional face of
\Delta^{4}
, say
\tau
, which contains a point
y
such that
\pi^{-1}(y)
contains a vertex in
X
. Without loss of generality, assume that
V(\tau)=\{e_{0},e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
. Let
z\in\pi^{-1}(y)
be a vertex in
X
. Then three coordinates of
z
must be non-zero. Since
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{4}
acts on the vertices of
X
, there are at least
8
vertices in
X
with exactly three non-zero coordinates.

If
V(\partial D_{j})=\pi^{-1}(y)
for some

, then
\partial D_{j}
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{3}
-equivariant triangulation on
8

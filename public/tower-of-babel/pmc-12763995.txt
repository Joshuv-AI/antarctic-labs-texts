Healthcare providers’ practice and knowledge towards oxygen therapy in East African countries: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Temesgen Geta Hardido, Bizuayehu Atinafu, Eskinder Israel, Getachew Nigussie Bolado, Eshetu Elifios
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundoxygen therapy is a medical treatment provided for all patients who need critical care. To provide effective oxygen therapy, healthcare providers should have good knowledge and practice towards oxygen therapy. There are several separate studies done in East African countries, however there no data on the overall level of healthcare providers practice and knowledge on oxygen therapy. Thus, this systematic review and meta-analysis was aimed to determine the general level of healthcare providers practice, knowledge, and related factors in East African countries.Methods and materialIn order to find articles on oxygen therapy, we searched databases such as PubMed, Medline, EMBASE, CLINAHL, Scopus, Web of Science, Cochrane library, repositories from East African countries, and Google Scholar. The review involved articles published in English language and utilizing a cross-sectional study design that fulfilled the inclusion criteria. Chi-square and Cochrane Q-Static were used to examine the heterogeneity of the articles included. The researchers used a random effect model to examine the healthcare providers’ practice and knowledge of oxygen therapy in East African countries. Funnel plot and Eggers test were used to evaluate publication bias. STATA software version 14 was used for conducting all statistical analyses.ResultA total of 25 articles were included involving 4,433 healthcare providers. The review found that 45% of healthcare providers had good knowledge of oxygen therapy, while 48% had good practice in East African countries. In particular, Tanzania had the highest score (62.5%) for healthcare providers’ practice towards oxygen therapy, while Egypt had the lowest score (18%). Concerning healthcare providers’ knowledge of oxygen therapy, Uganda had the highest percentage at 76%, while Egypt had the least at 6%. In addition, the study revealed a strong association between the knowledge, experience, education level, and practice of healthcare providers, while the knowledge of healthcare providers on oxygen therapy was significantly associated with their training and experience.ConclusionsHealthcare providers in East African countries had poor knowledge and practice of oxygen therapy. It also found factors that were statistically linked to the healthcare providers’ practice and knowledge of oxygen therapy. Hence, it is essential for all stakeholders and health ministries, as well as non-governmental organizations and other relevant bodies in each East African country, to collaborate in enhancing the practice and knowledge of healthcare providers and addressing identified factors.PROSPERO registrationIt recorded this review with registration number CRD42024551105.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12913-025-13797-0.Keywords: East African country, Healthcare providers, Oxygen therapy, Practice, Knowledge, Systematic review and meta-analysis

Introduction

Oxygen is necessary for managing hypoxemia and should be administered to the patient to enhance and maintain blood oxygen saturation levels [1, 2]. Oxygen therapy (OT) involves administering oxygen for medical purposes during both medical and surgical situations. The level of knowledge, attitude, and practice of health professionals towards oxygen therapy mainly determines how patients are affected by receiving excessive, insufficient, or no oxygen [3–5]. OT is described as oxygen administered at levels higher than those present in the air nearby [6]. For the majority of acutely ill patients, O2 should be recommended to reach a target saturation of 94–98%, while for individuals at risk of hypercapnia respiratory failure, a saturation of 88–92% is appropriate [7, 8]. Oxygen can cause skin breakdown near the mask or cannula and quickly dry out the upper respiratory tract unless patients are given oral rehydration, mouth care, and humidification to help with secretions and improve patient comfort [9]. While OT is commonly utilized for resuscitation, it can have negative effects if not used correctly on patients. OT can lead to significant side effects such as pulmonary oxygen toxicity and O2 induced hypercapnia. So, it should be administered by trained personnel [8].

Healthcare providers play a crucial role in monitoring oxygen therapy and can help minimize any negative effects of additional oxygen treatment [10]. Research conducted in various countries has revealed a gap between the knowledge and practice of oxygen therapy among healthcare providers [11, 12]. It is essential for health care providers to be experienced in the most effective methods to prevent various practical gap that result in poor results for patients, such as pulse oximetry, humidification device connection, and utilization of various oxygen equipment to rescue numerous critical patients [6, 13].

Even though they can be controlled, the challenges related to insufficient knowledge and practice in administering oxygen continue to be the main professional obstacle in delivering effective oxygen therapy [14]. Healthcare providers play a vital role in ensuring high-quality OT care by their good knowledge and practices [15]. Healthcare providers who had good practice and knowledge can enhance their patients’ lives and avoid hypoxemia and acute lung injury [16, 17]. Studies show that healthcare providers’ knowledge of how to administer O2 is lower than what is expected [4, 18, 19]. Healthcare providers need to be familiar with the signs of oxygen therapy necessity and typical oxygen saturation levels for various age groups, and should engage in ongoing education as part of their professional duties [20–22]. Research shows that nurses lack adequate knowledge and practice of oxygen therapy, highlighting the need for them to enhance their knowledge and practical skills through training and workshops [23–25].

Research conducted in various countries showed that the prevalence of oxygen therapy practice was 74.5% in Egypt [18], 45% in Eretria [26], 84% in Fiji [27], 52% in Ethiopia [12], and 74% in Iran [28]. In terms of nurses’ knowledge of oxygen therapy, it was 76% in Uganda [29], 36.2% in Ethiopia [11], 28% in Nigeria [30], and 87% in Fiji [27]. Receiving training, age, level of education, job history, and access to guidelines showed a strong correlation with knowledge level [3–5, 11, 12, 18, 27, 31, 32]. Practice level was associated with work experience, educational level, training, and the presence of periodic maintenance and supply of oxygen therapy devices [4, 11, 12, 23, 31, 32].

Ensuring high-quality oxygen supplementation is crucial for preventing sudden patient deaths caused by healthcare providers’ lack of knowledge and practice. East African countries are developing nations with increased rates of patient mortality and morbidity [4, 11, 12, 18, 20–22]. Poor practice and knowledge of healthcare providers lead to high mortality and morbidity in these countries [6, 23, 26]. Assessing an overall level and influencing factors of healthcare providers’ practice and knowledge on oxygen therapy is crucial in developing effective recommendations and strategies. Fortunately, this review is the first one to identify the gap of knowledge and practice among healthcare providers towards OT in East African countries. This study will offer evidence to assess the effectiveness of the Sustainable Development Goals in developing countries by improving the standard of patient care to reduce patient deaths in East Africa. While there have been multiple separate studies on the knowledge and practice of oxygen therapy among healthcare providers in East African nations [11, 12, 23, 26, 27, 30–34, 35–44, 45–49], there was no pooled available data on the overall status and influencing factors.

The findings and conclusions from a single study are not conclusive or consistent. This systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to evaluate nurses’ knowledge, practice, and various factors regarding OT in East African countries. Therefore, the outcome of this study can provide nurses with guidance on OT by providing information on OT and identifying associated factors that have a significant impact on critical patient care to improve their health outcomes. This will be significant for public health policy makers, top facility administrators, and all responsible bodies to develop suitable strategies for enhancing nurses’ knowledge and attitudes in order to guarantee quality care for those patients who require O2 therapy. The objective of this review was to answer the following research questions. (1) What was the level of knowledge and practice of East African healthcare providers toward OT? (2) What are the related factors that influence the knowledge and practice of healthcare providers towards OT in East African countries?

Methods and materials

This systematic review and meta-analysis was done to determine the general knowledge and practice towards OT among healthcare providers in East African countries.

Search strategy

Various databases like Medline/PubMed, Web of Science, Google Scholar, Scopus, EMBASE and CINAHL, East African countries University Online Archive and Cochrane Library were searched from October 1 to 30, 2024 to find relevant studies. We examined the database on (http://www.library.ucsf.edu) and the Cochrane Library to confirm that this research had not been carried out before in order to prevent redundant work. Once it was verified that no similar study had been done in East Africa before, a thorough search strategy was carried out using various Boolean operators for standard Population Exposure and Outcome (PEO) questions. The terms “or” and “and” are utilized for combining search terms. Boolean operators were used to search for terms like “Oxygen therapy “, or " Oxygen supplementation “, or " Oxygen administration “, and “practice “, or “knowledge”, and “factors influencing”, or “factors affecting”, or “determinant”, and “nurse”, or “midwife”, or “doctors” AND each East Africa country were searched using Boolean operators. Those papers that met the criteria for title and abstract were fully reviewed. For more detained information (see Table 1).

Three researchers (TG, CK, and GN) conducted the search strategy. Every article from the database was examined for title and abstract prior to being exported to the Endnotes library. All the articles that met the criteria for title and abstract were thoroughly read. We adhered closely to the guidelines of the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) to assess the current status of overall knowledge, practices of healthcare providers towards Oxygen therapy, and related factors in East Africa country.

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The review included articles that evaluated healthcare providers’ overall knowledge and attitudes towards Oxygen therapy in East Africa country and factors associated with it.

Study design: This review included studies that were carried out using a cross-sectional design.

Publication language: it included articles published in English.

Countries involved: it involved East Africa country.

Participants: Health professionals of East Africa country.

Study Format: Published works were included up until the final date of data analysis.

Articles period: Review included research carried out from 2014 to 2024.

Research type: institutional based study.

Exclusion Criteria: Studies were excluded if they lacked full text or demonstrated poor methodological quality. Studies that did not provide information on healthcare providers practice and knowledge on oxygen therapy or for which necessary information could not be obtained were also excluded. Additionally, study designs other than cross-sectional were excluded since other study design cannot report the prevalence and associated factors on oxygen therapy at same time. Thus, we excluded other study designs, such as case-control, case report, case series, and qualitative study designs.

Three authors (CK, GA, TG.) independently assessed the eligibility of the identified studies, with input from another author (E.I) to reach a consensus on potential inclusion or exclusion.

Data extraction

PRISMA was utilized to screen and help choose articles for this review. Criteria for data extraction encompassed authors’ names, publication year, study location, sample size, study population, design, and findings. We gathered the necessary data from the approved articles with the help of a Microsoft Excel spreadsheet. Three authors (TG, BA, and EE) individually gathered details from the supplementary materials. Research that satisfied the criteria for inclusion were selected following thorough agreement and discussion for data retrieval and are outlined in a table (Table 3).

Assessment of risk of bias and quality

To assess the quality of the studies, a critical analysis was performed using the Statistical Assessment and Meta-Analysis Tool provided by the Joanna Briggs Institute. Joanna evaluated studies and analyzed articles to determine if they should be included. The quality of the articles was assessed prior to being selected for the review. Criteria that were used to assess cross-sectional studies included the source population, sample size adequacy, data collection methods, tools, statistical analysis, and responsiveness of design. These studies were then rated on a nine-point scale. A quality assessment index score of 7 or above indicates a low risk for this review (Table 2).

Data processing and analysis

Data was extracted using Microsoft Excel and then analyzed using STATA version 14. Through the utilization of a random effects model analysis, the overall level of knowledge and practice of health care providers in East Africa countries concerning oxygen therapy was determined. Publication bias was examined through the use of funnel plot and visual analysis. Cochrane Q-Static and I2 were used to investigate heterogeneity among studies. Health care providers’ knowledge and practice towards oxygen therapy in different east Africa countries were assessed by comparing them to estimated prevalence through subgroup analysis. A forest plot was utilized to display the combined findings with a 95% confidence interval. A meta-analysis was conducted to evaluate the correlation between variables and the level of knowledge and practice on oxygen therapy. A significance level was declared if the p-value was lower than 0.05.

Operational definition

Previous studies indicated that heath care providers, who scored above the mean score of the knowledge questions, were considered as having good knowledge of oxygen therapy while those who scored below the mean value, were considered as having poor knowledge of oxygen therapy [12, 27, 28]. Regarding practice healthcare providers who scored above the mean score of the practice questions, were considered as having good practice toward oxygen therapy. In contrast, those providers who scored below the mean score of the practice questions were considered as having poor practice toward oxygen therapy [27–29, 30, 40]. Thus, our review was consistent with above operational definition. Healthcare providers include different types of professionals (e.g., doctors, nurses, midwives, and other allied health professionals).

Results

Identification and characteristics of included studies

From October 1 to 30, 2024, a total of 1190 articles were found in significant electronic databases and other relevant sources. Out of the articles that were found, 96 were taken out because they were duplicates and 1094 were kept for further evaluation. 870 studies were eliminated due to abstract and title not meeting the criteria. Out of the 224 articles left, 199 were not included in the study because they did not meet the set inclusion criteria. In the end, this study included 25 studies that met the criteria (Fig. 1).

This systematic review and meta-analysis included 25 articles with a combined total of 4,433 participants. The sample sizes for nurses’ practice towards OT varied from 50 [6, 21] to 422 [23]. The providers’ knowledge sample sizes varied from 50 [6, 21] to 422 [50, 51]. In terms of where the studies were conducted on healthcare providers’ practice towards OT, 16 were in the Ethiopia [11, 12, 23, 31, 37–47], two in Rwanda [48, 49], two in Nigeria [30, 33], 1 each in the Erteria [27], Keniya [35], Tanzania [36], and Uganda [29] (Table 2).

The prevalence of knowledge and practice of OT among healthcare providers in East Africa countries

This study revealed that healthcare providers’ practice towards oxygen therapy in East African countries ranged from 12.3% [49] to 73.9% [37]. In East Africa, 44.96% [95% CI (37.92, 52.00); I2 = 95.7%, P < 0.001] of healthcare providers had a good practice level towards OT (Fig. 2). Additionally, healthcare providers’ knowledge towards oxygen therapy varied from 6% [34] to 76.9% [47]. Healthcare providers had a 48.19% knowledge level in OT [95% CI (40.79, 55.59); I2 = 96.2%, P < 0.001] (Figs. 3 and 4). In Ethiopia, Egypt, Eritrea, Kenya, Nigeria, Rwanda, and Tanzania, healthcare providers’ practice towards oxygen therapy was reported as 47.71%, 18%, 45%, 51%, 18.80%, 37.81%, and 62.5% respectively. Likewise, healthcare professionals’ knowledge of oxygen therapy was 57.79%, 6%, 43.3%, 51%, 49.50, 27.27%, 53.80%, and 76% in Ethiopia, Egypt, Eritrea, Kenya, Nigeria, Rwanda, Tanzania, and Uganda respectively (Fig. 5).

Heterogeneity and publication bias

In order to reduce heterogeneity in the studies, we conducted a country subgroup analysis. The examination of I2 test results indicated heterogeneity within studies related to practice (I2 = 96.1%, P < 0.001). Evaluating publication bias in research on healthcare providers’ practice toward OT was done through Eggers’ test and examining the funnel plot visually. The funnel plot findings indicated that the chosen studies exhibited a symmetric distribution among the studies and Eggers’ test (P = 0.416) (Fig. 6). Both demonstrated the absence of publication bias among studies. In relation to the researches on the knowledge of health professionals, the I2 test revealed a heterogeneity among studies (I2 = 95.9%, P < 0.001). The Eggers test and visual examination of the funnel plot were utilized detect publication bias in the study. The results from the funnel plot and the Eggers test (P = 0.425) indicated that the chosen studies had a symmetric distribution confirmed that there was no bias in publication (Fig. 7).

Factors associated with the practice and knowledge of healthcare providers towards OT in East Africa countries

This research showed that the practice of providers towards OT was associated with their knowledge, training, and access to guidelines. Provider knowledge was related to getting training and having over four years of experience.

The results showed a significant association between the OT practices of providers and their knowledge level. Healthcare providers with good knowledge had nearly twice the likelihood of demonstrating effective practice in oxygen therapy compared to providers with poor knowledge [OR = 1.75, CI (1.11, 2.59), I2 = 59.8%, P < 0.001]. This study also found a significant association between provider training and practice towards oxygen therapy. Healthcare providers who received training had 2.59 times higher practices than those did not receive training (OR = 2.59, CI [1.92, 3.61], I2 = 89.2%, P < 0.001). Moreover, the presence of guidelines increased the likelihood of healthcare providers having good practice in oxygen therapy by 2.47 times compared to those without guidelines (OR = 2.47, CI [1.84, 4.09], I2 = 17.8%, P < 0.001) (Table 3). In case of providers knowledge, providers who received training were three times more likely to possess adequate knowledge of OT compared to those who did not receive training (OR = 3.05, CI [1.83, 6.02], I2 = 67.2%, P = 0.003). Providers with experience were almost five times more likely to have good knowledge of OT compared to those without experience (OR = 4.8, CI [1.3, 8.4], I2 = 78.9%, P < 0.001) (Table 4).

Review outcomes

The first result of this review provides the average practice and knowledge of oxygen therapy among healthcare providers in East Africa countries. The other result involves determinants associated with practice and knowledge of OT among healthcare providers.

Discussion

An essential requirement for better healthcare and wellness of critical patients who need OT is a good practice of oxygen therapy. According to the results of this systematic review and meta-analysis, approximately 45% of healthcare providers in East Africa showed good practice in towards OT. Prior research conducted elsewhere supports this study [44, 46]. This review showed a decrease in percentage compared to Iran (74%) [28], Rwanda (63.9%) [48], Fiji (84%) [27], Amhara (65.1%) [32], Sri Lanka, Asia (57.8%) [52] and Wolaita (73.9%) [37], but an increase compared to Addis Abeba(32.4%) [40], Rwanda (12.3%) [49], Egypt (18%) [34], and Nigeria (18.8%) [33]. Possible factors for these variations might be differences in healthcare facilities, geographic location, education level, economic status, lack of specialized units, study length, number of participants, research techniques, insufficient oxygen supply and distribution system, as well as healthcare provider training and expertise. The variances may also be ascribed to the time gap between the ongoing investigation and prior research [31].

Based on the study, healthcare professionals in East Africa demonstrated a good knowledge of OT, with a rate of 46.32% [95% CI (38.10, 54.53); I2 = 95.9%, P < 0.001]. This finding is consistent with prior research [26, 40]. However, studies conducted in Wolaita (55.6%) [37], Amhara (54.6%) [32], Keya (51%) [35], Tanzania (53.8%) [36], and Uganda (76%) [29] showed higher results compared to the present research. On the other hand, this study demonstrates a greater proportion than research conducted in Rwanda (24.2%) [49], Nigeria (26.7%) [33], Egypt (6%) [34], and Addis Ababa (36.2%) [11]. Differences may be explained by variations in nurses’ job satisfaction, healthcare system regulations and practices in each country, level of training, absence of oxygen administration protocols, and increased nurses’ workload, duration of study, sample size, research methods used, as well as the education and skills of healthcare providers. Additionally, the cut-off points used to categorize different levels of knowledge in previous studies might have influenced the discrepancy.

The review also revealed information on healthcare providers’ knowledge and practice of oxygen therapy in each country. Analysis of subgroups showed that Tanzania had the highest healthcare providers’ practice score regarding OT, while Egypt had a lower rate. Differences may arise from the level of experience and training of healthcare providers, the prevalence rates in the studies reviewed, and the effectiveness of management systems. Moreover, differences in the ratios among the studies analyzed could affect the overall results of the study. Research in Tanzania indicates higher rates of oxygen therapy than those in Egypt. This may lead to differences in research results. Uganda had the highest level of providers’ knowledge among East African countries, with Egypt having the lowest level. Difference in perception and beliefs among providers regarding acquiring new knowledge, along with differences in the number of studies included can lead to the discrepancy observed among countries. Variations may also be associated with the duration of the research and the methods employed.

Healthcare professionals with more than four years of experience, knowledge, and a Bachelor’s degree or higher demonstrated a significant association with between their practice towards oxygen therapy. This study found that healthcare professionals with a good knowledge of oxygen therapy were nearly twice as likely to demonstrate good practices compared to those with less knowledge. Previous research has confirmed this finding [12]. Healthcare providers with sufficient knowledge demonstrate good practices in oxygen therapy. This implies that those with good knowledge do better in the area of OT practice. One potential explanation is that practice is built upon a foundation of knowledge. Health care providers need to have sufficient knowledge about procedures before conducting them [33]. This gives them the confidence to perform the tasks. Moreover, while gaining knowledge through formal education, healthcare professionals are presented with a valuable chance to watch instructional videos on oxygen administration. This shows that healthcare providers must possess sufficient knowledge prior to practicing OT [39, 53].

In the same way, the level of experience of healthcare providers was closely related to their practice on OT. Healthcare providers who had more than 4 years of experience were 9.6 times more likely to do good practice towards OT. This study is consistent with earlier studies [32, 38, 39]. If more time spent gaining experience, there is an increased chance to improve the quality of practice in the healthcare profession. As they gain more experience in their field, they consistently engage in providing healthcare services, which enhances their practice level and builds their confidence in performing their responsibilities. This implies that in order to deliver high quality oxygen therapy, healthcare professionals must have sufficient experience, as this can reduce lengthy hospital stays and improve the patient’s quick recovery [54].

The last, but not least, predictor, significantly associated with the practice of healthcare providers, is guideline availability. Health care providers who had OT guideline in their health facility were more likely to practice OT than those who did not. Previous studies have been consistent with this review [12, 38]. The availability of appropriate health care materials is one of the main indicators of the quality of health care services. If there are no guidelines in the health facility, this could lead to unnecessary referrals to other health facilities at the same level and lead to poor health care practices. The provision of sufficient materials and frequent supervision by the regional or federal health office is the key to improving the practice of health professionals in the health facility. Furthermore, if there are limited materials (guidelines, drugs, and vaccines) in the health facilities, health care providers cannot obtain sufficient knowledge about OT. This implies that the hospital management should comply with standard materials such as guidelines, medications, and procedures in the critical care units to increase the level of practice of health care providers [38].

According to this review, providers’ experience and their training status were found to be strongly linked to providers’ knowledge of oxygen therapy. In particular, the experience of providers is one of the factors that influence healthcare providers’ knowledge of oxygen therapy. Healthcare providers with over four years of experience in an intensive care unit are nearly twice as likely to possess a good knowledge of OT compared to those with less experience. This review is consistent with previous research [32, 36]. The correlation could be due to experienced healthcare providers having better access to updated information on OT through their daily work, leading to improved knowledge in critical care patient who need oxygen therapy [32]. Moreover, healthcare providers who took oxygen therapy in-service training showed higher levels of knowledge compared to nurses who did not receive such training. The objective of training is to assist providers in enhancing their skills, abilities, and results. Learning through training enables individuals to acquire updated information and abilities. Studies conducted in Ethiopia and China has also confirmed this review. This implies that all health professionals should update their oxygen therapy knowledge through on-the-job training.

Limitations of the review

Although it held significant importance across different levels, the present systematic review and meta-analysis still had some limitations. Initially, because all primary studies included have a cross-sectional nature, confounders can have an impact on the outcome variables. Additionally, small study samples could affect the overall knowledge and practice in the nursing community. The differences in how the outcome variables (knowledge and practice) are defined in the studies included in the final review could impact the review quality. The omission of studies in languages other than English could influence the overall outcome of the findings. In the end, just 8 countries participated because of the restricted research could also influence the overall outcome of the findings. Despite we handled heterogeneity through various methods; its high value may affect the validity of the review.

Implications of the review

Oxygen is the crucial for all healthy and unwell persons. Oxygen therapy saves the life of patient if appropriately administered. To provide effective oxygen therapy, healthcare providers should equipped with good practice and knowledge. This systematic and meta-analysis was aimed to assess the pooled prevalence of health care providers towards oxygen therapy in East African countries. This review found that there was poor knowledge and practice among the healthcare providers towards oxygen therapy. Over and under dose oxygen therapy leads to unexpected patient morbidity and mortalities, which need immediate action from the responsible bodies. This implies that all stakeholders and policy makers in these countries must improve the healthcare providers’ knowledge and practice towards oxygen therapy.

This review is probably the first one of its nature in the East African concentrating on evaluating the pooled level of healthcare providers’ knowledge and practice on oxygen therapy. The review also indicated that considering the identified predictors will assist in creating effective strategies to enhance healthcare providers’ knowledge and practice towards oxygen therapy. This systematic review and meta-analysis could have important consequences for creating effective strategies and policies to enhance providers’ knowledge and practice towards offering high-quality oxygen therapy.

Conclusions

The review revealed that 45% of healthcare providers had a good practice and 48% had adequate knowledge towards oxygen therapy. The practice of providers towards oxygen therapy was significantly linked to providers’ knowledge, education level, and experience, while caregivers’ knowledge about OT was significantly linked to their experience and training level. Hence, national and local governments, health ministries, and NGOs should improve healthcare providers’ knowledge and practice towards OT by offering ongoing education, arranging on job training, increasing providers’ knowledge, and offering opportunities for further education.

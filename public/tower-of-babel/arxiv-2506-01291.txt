1.
Introduction and statement of the results

This paper is concerned with the solubility for random diophantine equations. By [
References
] and [
References
], we see that the positive portion of homogeneous polynomials in a sufficiently large number of variables in terms of degree is soluble in
{\mathbb{Q}}
. To explain more precisely, we let
\nu_{d,n}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
denote the Veronese embedding with
N=N_{d,n}:=\binom{n+d-1}{d}
, defined by listing all the monomials of degree
d
in
n
variables using the lexicographical ordering. Let
\langle{\boldsymbol{a}},\nu_{d,n}({\boldsymbol{x}})\rangle\in{\mathbb{Z}}[{\boldsymbol{x}}]
be a homogeneous polynomial in
n
variables of degree
d
with integer coefficients
{\boldsymbol{a}}
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the inner product. Here and throughout this paper, we write
f_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}({\boldsymbol{x}})=\langle{\boldsymbol{a}},\nu_{d,n}({\boldsymbol{x}})\rangle
. Define

\mathfrak{A}(A):=\{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}:\ \|{\boldsymbol{a}}\|_{\infty}\leq A\}.

Browning, Sawin and Le Boudec [
References
] proved that the proportion of
{\boldsymbol{a}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}
in
\mathfrak{A}(A)
, whose associated equation
f_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}({\boldsymbol{x}})=0
satisfies the Hasse principle, converges to
1
as
A\rightarrow\infty,
provided that
n\geq d+1
and
d\geq 4.
Furthermore, Poonen and Voloch [
References
] proved that the portion of
{\boldsymbol{a}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}
in
\mathfrak{A}(A)
, whose associated equation
f_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}({\boldsymbol{x}})=0
is everywhere locally soluble, converges to a positive constant
c
as

provided that
n\geq 2
and
d\geq 2.
Hence, combining these two results, we conclude that the positive portion of homogeneous polynomial equations in
n
variables of degree
d
is soluble in
{\mathbb{Q}}
, provided that
n\geq d+1
and
d\geq 4.

The recent works ([
References
], [
References
]) of Lee, Lee and the second author showed that such a conclusion on global solubility remains true even when the coefficients
{\boldsymbol{a}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}
are constrained by a polynomial condition under a modest condition on the number of variables. More precisely, let
P
be a non-singular form in
n
variables of degree
k\geq 2
. Define

\mathfrak{A}(A;P):=\{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}:\ P({\boldsymbol{a}})=0,\ \|{\boldsymbol{a}}\|_{\infty}\leq A\}.

The second author [
References
] proved that the proportion of
{\boldsymbol{a}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}
in
\mathfrak{A}(A;P)
, whose associated equation
f_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}({\boldsymbol{x}})=0
satisfies the Hasse principle, converges to
1
as
A\rightarrow\infty,
provided that
n\geq 32d+17
,
d\geq 14
and
k\leq d.
Furthermore, Lee, Lee and the second author [
References
] showed that the portion of
{\boldsymbol{a}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}
in
\mathfrak{A}(A;P)
, whose associated equation
f_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}({\boldsymbol{x}})=0
is everywhere locally soluble, converges to a constant

as

and

is positive if there exists

such that

has an integer solution

The main purpose of this paper is to relax on the bound for the number of variables

for such a conclusion. The crucial ingredient for this improvement is to handle a new lattice counting problem (see Lemma
3.1
below) which naturally arises in the argument. To do so, we use tools from the geometry of numbers. Our hope is that this argument described in this paper may be helpful to make further improvement.

3.
A lattice counting problem

In this section, we provide a new lattice counting problem which is essential ingredient for the main theorem in this paper.
Let
N(A,X)
denote the number of solutions of equations

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq s}a_{i}(x_{i}^{d}-z_{i}^{d})=\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq s}a_{i}(y_{i}^{d}-w_{i}^{d})=0

in integers
a_{i},x_{i},y_{i},z_{i},w_{i}
with
0<|a_{i}|\leq A
and
X/2\leq|x_{i}|,|y_{i}|,|z_{i}|,|w_{i}|\leq X
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
d\geqslant 4
and
s\geq d+2.
Suppose that
A\geq X^{2d}\geq 1.
Then, one has

N(A,X)\ll A^{s}X^{2s}+A^{s-1}X^{3s-d+\varepsilon}+A^{s-2}X^{4s-2d+\varepsilon}E(X),

where

E(X)=1+X^{\frac{1}{2}(5d+4-2s)}+X^{4d+4-2s}.

Proof.

Let
N_{1}(A,X)
be the number of solutions counted by
(\ref{key system of equations})
where
vectors
(x_{1}^{d}-z_{1}^{d},\ldots,x_{s}^{d}-z_{s}^{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{s}
and
(y_{1}^{d}-w_{1}^{d},\ldots,y_{s}^{d}-w_{s}^{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{s}
are linearly dependent over
{\mathbb{R}}.
First we bound the number of solutions counted by

satisfying
x_{i}^{d}-z_{i}^{d}\neq 0
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant s
.

The linearly dependent condition implies that there exists
c\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

(3.2)

c(x_{i}^{d}-z_{i}^{d})=(y_{i}^{d}-w_{i}^{d})\ \text{with}\ 1\leq i\leq s.

Consider the case
c=0.
This gives
y_{i}^{d}=w_{i}^{d}
for all
1\leq i\leq s.
Hence, the system
(\ref{key system of equations})
of equations, satisfying
(\ref{conditino 1 L.D.})
with
c=0
, implies that

(3.3)

\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}(x_{i}^{d}-z_{i}^{d})=0\ \text{and}\ y_{i}^{d}=w_{i}^{d}\ (1\leq i\leq s).

Therefore, since the number of solutions
y_{i}
and
w_{i}
satisfying
y_{i}^{d}=w_{i}^{d}
with
|y_{i}|,|w_{i}|\leq X
is
O(X^{s}),
it follows by Lemma
2.2
that the number of solutions
x_{i},y_{i},z_{i},w_{i}
satisfying
(\ref{condition 2 L.D.})
with
0<|a_{i}|\leq A
and
X/2\leqslant|x_{i}|,|y_{i}|,|z_{i}|,|w_{i}|\leq X
is
O(A^{s}X^{2s}+A^{s-1}X^{3s-d+\epsilon}).

Consider the case
c\neq 0.
Then, the system
(\ref{conditino 1 L.D.})
of equations implies

(3.4)

(y_{1}^{d}-w_{1}^{d})(x_{i}^{d}-z_{i}^{d})=(y_{i}^{d}-w_{i}^{d})(x_{1}^{d}-z_{1}^{d})\ (2\leq i\leq s).

Furthermore, the system
(\ref{key system of equations})
of equations with

for all

implies that

(3.5)

(\ref{mean value of exponential sums})
into
(\ref{bound for 4.5})
, we conclude by
(\ref{4.54.5})
that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|{\boldsymbol{b}}\|_{\infty},\|{\boldsymbol{c}}\|_{\infty}\leq A\\
P([{\boldsymbol{b}},{\boldsymbol{c}}])=0\end{subarray}}\bigl|\mathcal{I}_{[({\boldsymbol{b}},{\boldsymbol{c}})]}(X,\mathfrak{m}(X^{\delta}))\bigr|^{2}\ll A^{N-k-2}X^{2n-2d-\delta^{\prime}},

for some
\delta^{\prime}>0.
Since
A\leq X^{n-d}
, we complete the proof of Lemma
\ref{lem4.6}.

∎

5.
Bounds for small moduli

In this section, we provide bounds for small moduli using the same argument as in [
References
, Lemma 3.8]. However, for the success of the argument with the relaxed bound on the number of variables
n
, we slightly modify the argument.

Lemma 5.1
.

With the same condition in Lemma
4.1
, whenever
1\leq X^{2d}\leq A\leq X^{s-d}
and
N\geq 200k(k-1)2^{k-1}
, there exists
\delta^{\prime}>0
such that we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|{\boldsymbol{b}}\|_{\infty},\|{\boldsymbol{c}}\|_{\infty}\leq A\\
P([{\boldsymbol{b}},{\boldsymbol{c}}])=0\end{subarray}}\bigl|\mathcal{I}_{[({\boldsymbol{b}},{\boldsymbol{c}})]}\left(X,\mathfrak{M}(X^{\delta})\setminus\mathfrak{M}(\log X)\right)\bigr|^{2}\ll A^{N-k-2}X^{2n-2d}(\log A)^{-\eta},

for some
\eta>0.

Proof.

On observing that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}(X^{\delta})\setminus\mathfrak{M}(\log X)

\displaystyle=(\mathfrak{M}(X^{\delta})\setminus\mathfrak{M}((\log X)^{9d}))\cup(\mathfrak{M}((\log X)^{9d})\setminus\mathfrak{M}(\log X))

\displaystyle=\biggl(\bigcup_{j=0}^{J_{1}}(\mathfrak{M}(2^{j+1}(\log X)^{9d})\setminus\mathfrak{M}(2^{j}(\log X)^{9d}))\biggr)

\displaystyle\cup\biggl(\bigcup_{j=0}^{J_{2}}(\mathfrak{M}(2^{j+1}\log X)\setminus\mathfrak{M}(2^{j}\log X))\biggr)

with
J_{1}=O(\log X)
and
J_{2}=O(\log\log X),
we deduce by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|{\boldsymbol{a}}\|_{\infty}\leq A\\
P({\boldsymbol{a}})=0\end{subarray}}\left|\mathcal{I}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}(X,\mathfrak{M}(X^{\delta})\setminus\mathfrak{M}(\log X))\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll J_{1}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{J_{1}}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|{\boldsymbol{a}}\|_{\infty}\leq A\\
P({\boldsymbol{a}})=0\end{subarray}}\left|\mathcal{I}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}(X,\mathfrak{M}(2^{j+1}(\log X)^{9d})\setminus\mathfrak{M}(2^{j}(\log X)^{9d}))\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+J_{2}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{J_{2}}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|{\boldsymbol{a}}\|_{\infty}\leq A\\
P({\boldsymbol{a}})=0\end{subarray}}\left|\mathcal{I}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}(X,\mathfrak{M}(2^{j+1}\log X)\setminus\mathfrak{M}(2^{j}\log X))\right|^{2}.

Now, we analyze the mean value

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|{\boldsymbol{a}}\|_{\infty}\leq A\\
P({\boldsymbol{a}})=0\end{subarray}}\left|\mathcal{I}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}(X,\mathfrak{M}(2Q)\setminus\mathfrak{M}(Q))\right|^{2},

with
\log X\leq Q\leq X^{\delta}.
For simplicity, we temporarily write

(5.2)

\mathfrak{C}=\mathfrak{M}(2Q)\setminus\mathfrak{M}(Q).

Then, by [
References
, Proposition 3.4], we deduce that

(5.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|{\boldsymbol{a}}\|_{\infty}\leq A\\
P({\boldsymbol{a}})=0\end{subarray}}\left|\mathcal{I}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}(X,\mathfrak{C})\right|^{2}\ll A^{N-n/8-k}X^{7n/4}\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{C}}T(\alpha_{1})^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor/8}d\alpha_{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{C}}T(\alpha_{2})^{\lceil n/2\rceil/8}d\alpha_{2},

where
T(\alpha)=\sum_{-A\leq b\leq A}\bigl|\sum_{1\leq x\leq X}e(b\alpha x^{d})\bigr|^{2}
with
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Meanwhile, whenever
\alpha\in\mathfrak{C},
there exist
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
(q,a)=1
such that
Q\leq q\leq 2Q
and

|\alpha-a/q|\leq 2Q(AX^{d})^{-1}.

Also, for
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}
with
|b|\leq A
, if we write
l=(q,b)
,
\widetilde{q}=q/l
and
\widetilde{b}=b/l
, we have

\left|b\alpha-\frac{a\widetilde{b}}{\widetilde{q}}\right|\leq\frac{2Qb}{AX^{d}}\leq\frac{2Q}{X^{d}}.

Hence, since
\widetilde{q}\leq 2Q
, it follows by [
References
, Lemma 2.7] that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq x\leq X}e(b\alpha x^{d})-(\widetilde{q})^{-1}S(\widetilde{q},a\widetilde{b})v(\beta)=O((2Q)^{2}),

where
S(q,a)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{q}e(an^{d}/q)
and
v(\beta)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{X}e(\beta\gamma^{d})d\gamma
with
\beta=b\alpha-\frac{a\widetilde{b}}{\widetilde{q}}.
Thus, when
\alpha\in\mathfrak{C}
, we deduce from (
5.4
) that

(5.5)

\displaystyle T(\alpha)

\displaystyle\ll X^{2}+\displaystyle\sum_{l|q}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|b|\leq A\\
(q,b)=l\end{subarray}}\biggl|\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq x\leq X}e(b\alpha x^{d})\biggr|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll X^{2}+\displaystyle\sum_{l|q}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|b|\leq A\\
(q,b)=l\end{subarray}}\left(|((\widetilde{q})^{-1}S(\widetilde{q},a\widetilde{b})v(\beta)|^{2}+(2Q)^{4}\right).

By [
References
, Theorem 4.2], we have a bound
S(q/l,a(b/l))\ll(q/l)^{1-1/d}

and a trivial bound
v(\beta)\leq X
. Hence, on substituting these estimates into
(\ref{4.20})
, we find that

(5.6)

T(\alpha)\ll X^{2}+\displaystyle\sum_{l|q}AX^{2}q^{-2/d}l^{2/d-1}+AQ^{4}.

The second term
\sum_{l|q}AX^{2}q^{-2/d}l^{2/d-1}
is bounded above by
AX^{2}q^{-2/d}\sum_{l|q}1,
and by the standard divisor estimate, this bound is
O(AX^{2}q^{-2/d+\epsilon})
. Recall that
\alpha\in\mathfrak{C}
, and thus we have the bound
Q\leq q\leq 2Q
with
\log X\leq Q\leq X^{\delta}.
Furthermore, we recall the hypothesis
X^{2d}\leq A
in the statement in this lemma. Hence, it follows from
(\ref{4.21})
that

(5.7)

T(\alpha)\ll AX^{2}Q^{-2/d+\epsilon}.

Therefore, on substituting (
5.7
) into (
5.3
), we obtain the bound

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|{\boldsymbol{a}}\|_{\infty}\leq A\\
P({\boldsymbol{a}})=0\end{subarray}}\left|\mathcal{I}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}(X,\mathfrak{C})\right|^{2}\ll A^{N-n/8-k}X^{7n/4}\textrm{mes}(\mathfrak{C})^{2}(AX^{2}Q^{-2/d+\epsilon})^{n/8}.

On noting that
\textrm{mes}(\mathfrak{C})\ll Q^{3}(AX^{d})^{-1}
, we conclude that

(5.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|{\boldsymbol{a}}\|_{\infty}\leq A\\
P({\boldsymbol{a}})=0\end{subarray}}\left|\mathcal{I}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}(X,\mathfrak{C})\right|^{2}\ll A^{N-k-2}X^{2n-2d}Q^{6-n/(4d)+\epsilon}.

We find from (
5.8
) together with the hypothesis
n=8s+r>24d
in this lemma that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|{\boldsymbol{a}}\|_{\infty}\leq A\\
P({\boldsymbol{a}})=0\end{subarray}}\left|\mathcal{I}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}(X,\mathfrak{C})\right|^{2}\ll A^{N-k-2}X^{2n-2d}Q^{-1/(4d)+\epsilon}.

Hence, on recalling that
J_{1}=O(\log X)
and
J_{2}=O(\log\log X)
, it follows from (
5.1
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|{\boldsymbol{a}}\|_{\infty}\leq A\\
P({\boldsymbol{a}})=0\end{subarray}}\left|\mathcal{I}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}(X,\mathfrak{M}(X^{\delta})\setminus\mathfrak{M}(\log X))\right|^{2}\ll A^{N-k-2}X^{2n-2d}(\log X)^{-\eta},

for some
\eta>0.

On noting that
A\leq X^{n-d}
in the statement of this lemma, we complete the proof of Lemma
\ref{lemma 5.1}.

∎

6.

In this section, we provide the proof of Theorem

. To do this, we require two auxiliary lemmas recorded in [

with

,

S_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}(q):=S_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}(q;n)=q^{-n}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq b\leq q\\

.

∎

In advance of the statement of the second auxiliary lemma, we define

(6.1)

.

Proof of Theorem 1.2.

Recall the definition
(\ref{I_a(X,B)})
and

of
\mathcal{I}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}(X,\mathfrak{B})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq q\leq w}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq b\leq q\\
(q,b)=1\end{subarray}}\displaystyle\int_{|\alpha-b/q|\leq\frac{w}{AX^{d}}}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq{\boldsymbol{x}}\leq X\end{subarray}}e(\alpha f_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}({\boldsymbol{x}}))d\alpha.

Recall the definition (
6.1
) of
\mathfrak{J}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}(w).
By applying classical treatments in major arcs [

we readily find that

(6.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}(X,\mathfrak{M})=\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq q\leq w}S_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}(q)\mathfrak{J}_{a}(w)+O(A^{-1}X^{n-d-1}w^{5}),

where

(q,b)=1\end{subarray}}q^{-n}\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq{\boldsymbol{r}}\leq q}e\left(\frac{b}{q}f_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}({\boldsymbol{r}})\right).

Meanwhile, recall the definition
(\ref{def2.3})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|{\boldsymbol{a}}\|_{\infty}\leq A\\

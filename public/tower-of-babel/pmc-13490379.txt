Accelerated distributed scheduling of integrated community energy systems considering electricity-heat sharing
Authors: Xiaorong Sun, Junyi Chen, Xueping Pan, Jinpeng Guo, Ying Yan, Jinghui Qin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Integrated community energy systems (ICES) provide an efficient and sustainable approach to local energy management through multiple energy carriers including electricity, heating, and cooling while enhancing end-use energy management. Electrical and thermal connections between ICESs can further improve energy utilization and economic efficiency. However, energy sharing among ICESs still faces challenges, including complex centralized scheduling constraints, privacy protection concerns, and slow convergence and reduced efficiency of distributed algorithm convergence. To address these challenges, this paper develops a stochastic optimization model for day-ahead energy sharing scheduling, accounting for uncertainties in renewable energy sources and losses in inter-community heat networks. The key idea is to establish a multi-community energy management framework in which the ICES operator provides dynamic electricity and heat trading prices based on the initial supply-demand information. Each ICES optimizes power output and energy interactions to minimize operational costs, carbon emissions, and renewable curtailment, thereby improving overall system efficiency. A distributed linearized surrogate augmented Lagrangian relaxation (LSALR) algorithm is developed to solve the optimal scheduling problem for interconnected ICESs in a privacy-preserving and computationally efficient manner. Testing results demonstrate the model significantly improves operational efficiency and promotes renewable energy utilization within ICESs. Compared with benchmark distributed algorithms, the proposed method achieves superior performance in terms of convergence rate and solution quality.

Introduction

Fossil fuels contribute to increasing CO2 emissions, polluting the Earth’s atmosphere with harmful effects. Their non-renewable nature highlights the urgency of energy transformation and upgrading to address the world’s growing energy crisis and environmental concerns. The integration of renewable energy into power systems is a key trend in the evolution of the energy sector. Energy communities provide an efficient and sustainable approach to local energy management by leveraging synergies among multiple energy carriers such as electricity, heating, and cooling, while also promoting user engagement. However, these communities often underutilize their energy sources due to differences in demand and equipment capacity. Establishing inter-community energy connections can complement these differences, leading to the emergence of integrated community energy systems (ICES). The ICES concept involves energy users making collective decisions to maximize benefits through shared energy resources, integrating various electricity and heat technologies, energy storage, and optimizing energy flow management1. The success of ICES depends on enabling conditions encompassing technological, environmental, and socio-economic aspects, as well as supportive energy policies and regulatory frameworks.

In recent years, European countries have enacted legal provisions and incentives to encourage multiple energy sharing among communities, driving significant research and practical initiatives. Current energy communities in China face challenges like underutilized renewable energy, high energy consumption, and carbon emissions due to system inefficiencies and suboptimal management. To improve economic and environmental benefits, global researchers are exploring ICESs operation optimization. Case studies show natural gas reliance on fossil fuels and limited decarbonization potential of interregional gas interconnections, whereas electric-thermal interconnections offer high emission reduction and low construction costs. Leveraging ICESs electric-thermal coupling advantages, such interconnections efficiently balance interregional energy supply and demand. ICESs also encourage small-scale energy participants to engage in market-based energy exchange, enhancing system flexibility and enabling multi-energy complementarity. Research on optimal scheduling in ICES has garnered significant attention2–4. Currently, optimal scheduling faces challenges such as complex constraints and privacy concerns for community data, necessitating the design of market mechanisms, scheduling models, and algorithms to adapt to these developments.

The advent of the energy internet, smart sensors, advanced computing, and communication technologies5 is driving a shift in distribution-side power system scheduling from centralized to distributed management. The interactive energy mechanism integrates economic incentives and grid control tools6, featuring dual market and control characteristics. This approach enables and incentivizes ICES for inter-community energy trading and efficient energy management. Key to this mechanism are the setting of community electricity and heat trading prices, as well as market clearing and settlement7. Proposals such as harmonized internal pricing mechanisms such as bill sharing and mid-market pricing8 aim to encourage consumer participation in local markets. Literature9 treated trading prices between microgrids and external grids as fixed values, coordinating power interactions among stakeholders but overlooking individual willingness to participate and the interactive effects of price and power. In our previous work10, a novel dynamic rental price mechanism for shared energy storage in multi-integrated energy system was established based on the demand ratio. The above research provides insight for trading strategies for connected ICESs. However, there are still difficulties in realizing a fair way of managing inter-community energy transactions and setting prices for energy interactions that are consistent with supply and demand.

Uncertainties within ICESs primarily stem from distributed clean energy outputs11–13, fluctuations in electricity, gas, and heat loads14–16, and volatile energy prices17. Constructing optimal scheduling models for ICES involves addressing the uncertainty of renewable energy outputs, often depicted using scenario trees18, robust optimization models19, or scenario generation via adversarial networks20. These methods typically require consideration of numerous scenarios or complex model structures. A notable approach to model uncertainty in renewable energy outputs is through Markov processes21, where the state of energy output in a given time period depends solely on previous state. To achieve the goal of low-carbon operation, constructing the objective functions for ICES optimization scheduling models require a multifaceted approach. Reference22 explored incorporating hybrid energy source trading volumes into the objective function, extending from single electricity trading to encompass electricity, gas, and heat, proposing a two-tier low-carbon optimization framework for synergizing carbon trading and DR in an urban IES based on data-driven prediction. Other study23 introduced multi-objective functions considering economic efficiency and carbon emissions, integrating factors such as energy storage device charging/discharging depth and cycle time. The work in24 establishes an optimization scheduling model for mobile energy storage, hydrogen storage, and virtual energy storage of air conditioning clusters, considering the physical and temporal constraints of different storage devices, aiming to minimize the operational cost. Additionally, an optimal scheduling model for electricity-hydrogen integrated systems, considering operational costs for both electricity and hydrogen suppliers was proposed in25. Furthermore, integrated electric and hydrogen energy sharing mechanisms was investigated in26 using distributed optimization techniques. Constructing an optimal scheduling model for multi-scenario and multi-energy interconnected ICES is thus a remaining difficulty.

Recent research has focused on developing efficient distributed solution algorithms for ICES optimization. Decomposition-coordination algorithms, including pairwise decomposition methods, traditional Lagrangian relaxation, the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM), and its variants27–30, have proven effective in tackling complex optimization challenges. Specifically31,32, introduced a distributed neurodynamic-based approach and a distributed double-consensus algorithm to optimize power-heat coupling systems. The work in22 expanded on traditional distributed algorithms, transitioning from single electricity energy trading to hybrid trading modes encompassing electricity, gas, and thermal energy for optimal scheduling in multi-regional integrated energy systems (IESs). The work in33 is transformed into an equivalent mixed-integer linear programming problem via strong duality principle. In34, a fully distributed ADMM was proposed for optimal energy management in IESs. Furthermore, a multi-agent ADMM approach was proposed in35 for optimizing peer-to-peer energy transactions across interconnected microgrids. Innovative enhancements to ADMM include accelerated asynchronous distributed algorithm36, integrating hot-start and double-update accelerated iteration strategies to improve computational efficiency. While ADMM is effective for solving continuous convex problems, its application to mixed-integer problems may encounter challenges due to non-convex feasible domains, potentially limiting convergence to optimal solutions. However, when the scheduling problem simultaneously involves mixed-integer decision variables, multi-energy coupling, inter-community trading coordination, and renewable uncertainty, existing ADMM-based37,38 and consensus-based methods39 may still face several challenges, including parameter sensitivity, reduced convergence efficiency, and limited adaptability to nonconvex feasible domains and complex device-level constraints. In contrast, the surrogate Lagrangian relaxation (SLR) algorithm40 addressed these issues by enabling distributed solving of nonconvex mixed-integer optimization problems, ensuring convergence to optimal solutions. The SLR algorithm’s iterative process simplifies subproblem solutions, accelerating optimization time and improving convergence rates compared to traditional Lagrangian relaxation methods, which may suffer from multiplier zigzagging based on sub-gradient updates. Therefore, developing an efficient distributed algorithm that is better suited to the coupled scheduling structure of interconnected ICESs remains necessary.

To address challenges with optimal scheduling of interconnected ICESs, this paper establishes an optimization model and energy management framework with dynamic energy sharing pricing mechanism, proposing an enhanced distributed algorithm for cooperative scheduling within ICESs. Distributed scheduling framework for interconnected ICESs

Interconnected ICESs structure

The interconnection of multiple energy communities to form an integrated multi-energy community is depicted in Fig. 1. Unlike standalone operations, this interconnected system enables inter-regional energy interaction and sharing, boosting renewable energy consumption and lowering cross-community operating costs. The studied interconnected ICES model includes PV systems, gas boilers (GB), CHP units, heat pumps (HP), electric/absorption chillers (EC/AC), energy storage batteries (ESB), and cold/heat storage tanks. These resources support coordinated regional dispatch via upper-level power and gas networks, enabling integrated energy scheduling across communities.

Typically, the optimal day-ahead scheduling model for the interconnected ICESs is solved by mature commercial solvers like CPLEX or Gurobi for centralized optimization. It faces challenges in supporting effective information interaction among communities, ensuring privacy protection, and achieving satisfactory performance when dealing with large-scale variables and complex constraints.

The distributed scheduling framework

To address centralized optimization issues, the interactive energy mechanism converts the global optimization of centralized scheduling for interconnected regional energy sharing into distributed scheduling across multiple local subproblems. In this framework, the ICESO acts as an intermediary between the electricity market and individual communities: it sets electricity and heat transaction prices between communities, manages energy sharing, and represents communities in upper-grid trading. Each community determines its energy sharing strategy within its subproblem and provides the ICESO with information such as anticipated inter-community shared power and upper-grid traded power. Thermal energy sharing transaction prices can be obtained by taking the price per kWh of thermal energy converted through natural gas as the upper limit of the community thermal energy sharing transaction price. It is noted that since there is no selling price of thermal energy to the upper gas network as a reference lower limit, the median of the upper limit of the transaction price is taken as the lower limit of the community thermal energy sharing transaction price. Inter-regional energy transaction prices are determined by each region’s supply and demand under independent operation, yet post-interconnection supply and demand may differ from pre-interconnection conditions.

Economically, the proposed inter-community transaction price is designed to lie between the upstream selling price and purchasing price, so that energy suppliers obtain a higher revenue than direct export to the upper network, while energy demanders pay less than direct purchase from the upper network. Therefore, the mechanism provides incentives for both sides and promotes local energy sharing. Compared with conventional pricing strategies, the proposed supply-demand-oriented pricing method can better reflect the local imbalance between interconnected communities and provide more effective coordination signals for day-ahead scheduling.

ICES-ICESO collaboration and market clearance

In day-ahead scheduling, the energy sharing platform issues price signals to ICESs. Each community determines its energy sharing based on these signals and uploads details to the platform, which calculates residual differences and iteratively updates price signals. Communities adjust generation and consumption to clear the market. The clearing price, set prior to the sharing day, is ultimately confirmed by the platform following real-time market operations. During each iteration of the day-ahead market, ICESs solve individual optimization subproblems based on end-user specifics and local pricing, submitting energy-sharing decisions to protect privacy. The developed LSALR algorithm guides the construction and solution of these subproblems, ensuring coordinated updates between ICESO price signals and ICES operations.

Problem formulation

Day-ahead scheduling of the interconnected ICESs are modeled with the goal of minimizing the total operating cost of ICESs, carbon emissions, and penalties for abandoning PV while meeting the device-level constraints, regional coupling constraints and inter-community coupling constraints.

LSALR-based solution methodology

SALR framework

For mixed integer linear programming as problems formulated above, the number of binary variables and coupling constraints complicates the solving process. Traditional Lagrangian relaxation algorithm is to fully optimize the relaxation problem to solve the optimal solution. The idea of the SLR algorithm35 is to use surrogate multipliers without fully optimizing the relaxation problem, and ensure the convergence of the problem by making sure that the multiplier update direction forms an acute angle to the direction of the optimal solution, by satisfying a simple surrogate optimality condition. The coordination process includes solving subproblems, making surrogate optimality condition judgments, determining step sizes, and updating multiplier iterations. The step κ iteration solves the subproblem to obtain \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${P_{s,r,r^{\prime},t,\kappa }}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${H_{s,r,r^{\prime},t,\kappa }}$$\end{document}. The surrogate optimality condition judgment is performed as follows:15\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\tilde {L}_{r,\kappa }}(\lambda _{{s,r,r^{\prime},t,\kappa }}^{E},\lambda _{{s,r,r^{\prime},t,\kappa }}^{H},{P_{s,r,r^{\prime},t,\kappa }},{H_{s,r,r^{\prime},t,\kappa }})<{\tilde {L}_{r,\kappa }}(\lambda _{{s,r,r^{\prime},t,\kappa }}^{E},\lambda _{{s,r,r^{\prime},t,\kappa }}^{H},{P_{s,r,r^{\prime},t,\kappa - 1}},{H_{s,r,r^{\prime},t,\kappa - 1}})$$\end{document}

If Eq. (15) is satisfied, step size and multiplier updates are performed and the next community subproblem is solved. If Eq. (15) is not satisfied, the step size and multipliers remain unchanged, and subsequent subproblems are solved iteratively until the surrogate optimality condition is met. Since the SLR algorithm does not require complete minimization of the relaxation problem, the direction of the multiplier update is smooth, which greatly alleviates the problem of the “zigzagging” shape of the traditional Lagrangian relaxation algorithm and reduces the number of iterations required for convergence. Upon satisfaction of the surrogate optimality condition, the residuals are computed, and the step size and Lagrange multipliers are updated as follows. The SALR algorithm accelerates convergence by incorporating an additional penalty term into relaxation constraints. However, this introduces nonlinearity into the augmented Lagrangian function, posing challenges for nonlinear solvers. By linearizing the post-penalty addition, the relaxation problem becomes linear and solvable using linear methods. This decomposition facilitates iterative application of the LSALR algorithm, following the multiplicative update formulation of the SLR algorithm. The Lagrange multiplier serves as a price signal issued by the energy sharing platform to individual ICES units within the interactive energy mechanism. Each ICES adjusts its energy generation, usage behaviour, and sharing decisions based on this guidance signal. Through iterative refinement, convergence yields the day-ahead market clearing price.

This improvement can be attributed to the enhanced step-size adjustment mechanism, multiplier updating strategy, and the incorporation of optimality conditions in the SALR framework, which collectively improve convergence stability compared with conventional ADMM.

Furthermore, when the proposed absolute-value function linearization method is incorporated, the LSALR algorithm exhibits significantly faster and smoother convergence than both SALR and ADMM. The superior computational efficiency of LSALR can be further explained by its solution mechanism. In each iteration, the SALR-based framework coordinates the optimization of multiple ICES subproblems through augmented Lagrangian relaxation, enabling efficient information exchange among interconnected regions. By contrast, ADMM relies on alternating updates of decomposed variables and exhibits stronger sensitivity to parameter tuning, leading to slower convergence. Moreover, the incorporation of absolute-value penalty terms further enhances convergence smoothness and numerical stability, contributing to the improved overall performance of the proposed method.

Conclusions

This paper designs a framework for ICESO to manage energy across multiple communities under an electricity-heat dynamic transaction pricing mechanism, establishes a stochastic optimization model for day-ahead energy sharing scheduling (accounting for renewable energy uncertainty and heat network loss), and develops the efficient distributed algorithm LSALR to solve it. Key conclusions are as follows:

1) The proposed electricity-heat sharing model and pricing mechanism effectively boost the operational efficiency of individual communities and the entire system.

2) The stochastic optimization model reduces uncertainty scenarios, with examples verifying significant cuts in ICESs’ operating and carbon emission costs under energy interaction.

3) The LSALR algorithm decouples inter-community constraints, constructs sub-problems, and solves the ICES optimal scheduling model. Simulations confirm its effectiveness; comparisons show it accelerates convergence, yields high-quality solutions.

The study assumes fully rational decision-making by ICES operators, which may not reflect real-world scenarios. Future work will validate the algorithm in larger real-world systems and refine multi-energy flow dynamics modeling.

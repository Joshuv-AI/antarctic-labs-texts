Soergel bimodules and Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials
ethan eugene wynner

Abstract




Abstract

This paper presents an exposition of Soergel bimodules, the categorification of Hecke algebras, and their applications to various topics in Kazhdan-Lusztig theory. We ultimately exposit a few of Soergel’s main results, which allowed him to give alternative proofs, using his theory, of the Kazhdan-Lusztig conjectures. This paper should be viewed as a (very) condensed exposition following the work of primarily Elias, Makisumi, Thiel, and Williamson in their lovely book
Introduction to Soergel Bimodules
2
. It is also important to note that much of Soergel’s theory has been omitted from this paper in light of brevity, as well as the depth of proofs and lead-up information. Many proofs concerning covered material are left out of this paper, again, due to their length, as well as their residence in many of the texts listed in the references section.

Fix a Coxeter system

Example

Have you ever wondered, ”What are the morphisms like in the category of Soergel bimodules?”? To satisfy this musing, we take for granted that any Soergel bimodule is graded free as a left or right

\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}}(B,B^{\prime})\simeq\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}}(B,B^{\prime}(k)).

2.3
Grothendieck groups, Soergel’s categorization

Probably the most important technical attribute of Soergel bimodules is their role as the ”categorification” of the Hecke algebra, and their connection to the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis of said Hecke algebra. In pursuit of properly expositing this, we will interface now with one of the many beautiful constructions in math, the Grothendieck group.

The
split Grothendieck group

[\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}]_{\oplus}
of
\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}
is an abelian group generated by classes
[B]
of Soergel bimodules in
\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}.
We equip the following simple yet universally powerful condition that

[B]=[B^{\prime}]+[B^{\prime\prime}]

where
B\simeq B^{\prime}\oplus B^{\prime\prime}.
Since
\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}
is a monoidal category, we can give
[\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}]_{\oplus}
the structure of a ring by setting
[B][B^{\prime}]=[BB^{\prime}].
Moreover, by setting
v[B]=[B(1)],
we give
[\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}]_{\oplus}
the structure of a
\mathbb{Z}[v^{\pm 1}]
-algebra.

2.3.1
Definition

The
character

\chi
of a Soergel bimodule
B
is defined as the element

\chi(B)=\sum_{x\in W}v^{l(x)}h_{x}(B)\delta_{x}

of
\mathcal{H}.
We have
h_{id}(B_{s})=v^{+1}
and
h_{s}(B_{s})=v^{-1},
and thus
\chi(B_{s})=v\delta_{id}+v-v^{-1}\delta_{s}=v+\delta_{s}.
Thus, wonderfully,
\chi(B_{s})=b_{s}
for every
s\in S.
It follows that
\chi(B\oplus B^{\prime})=\chi(B)+\chi(B^{\prime})
and
\chi(B(1))=v\chi(B).
Since
v[B]=[B(1)]
in the Grothendieck group,
\chi
induces a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear, but moreover
\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
-linear map

\chi:[\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}]_{\oplus}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}.

We can now introduce these very important results :

2.3.2
Soergel’s Categorification theorem

1.

There exists a
\mathbb{Z}[v^{\pm 1}]
-algebra homomorphism

\phi:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow[\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}]_{\oplus},\text{ }b_{s}\mapsto[B_{s}]

for each
s\in S
.

2.

W
is in bijection with the set of indecomposable objects in
\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}
mod shift and isomorphism. Formally,

W\longleftrightarrow\{\text{indecomposable objects}\in\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}\}/\simeq,(k),\text{ }w\mapsto B_{w},

where
B_{w}
are direct summands in the Bott-Samelson bimodules
BS(\underline{w})
.

3.

\chi
descends to a
\mathbb{Z}[v^{\pm 1}]
-module homomorphism

\chi:[\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}]_{\oplus}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}

which is inverse to
\phi
. Thus
\phi
and
\chi
are isomorphisms, and we have
[\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}]_{\oplus}\simeq\mathcal{H}.

2.3.3
Soergel’s Hom formula

For any Soergel bimodules
B,B^{\prime}
, the graded
R
-bimodule hom space
\text{Hom}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}}(B,B^{\prime})
is free as a graded left (resp., right)
R
-module. Moreover,

\text{gr(rank)}\text{Hom}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}}(B,B^{\prime})=(\chi(B),\chi(B^{\prime})),

where ( , ) is the standard form on
\mathcal{H}.

Soergel’s Hom formula motivates what we call
Soergel’s conjecture,
which was proved for arbitrary Coxeter systems (Soergel proved for Weyl groups) by [Elias / Williamson]. We state it here:

2.3.4
Soergel’s conjecture

For every
x\in W
,
\chi(B_{x})=b_{x}.
Equivalently, the Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomial
h_{x,y}=h_{x}(B_{y})

A positive result of Soergel’s Conjecture has a very important implication in the larger theory. That is, it implies the

which asserts that the coefficients of

3.2
Back to Kazhdan-Lusztig

Recall the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis
\{b_{w}\text{ }|\text{ }w\in W\}
of the Hecke algebra

given a Coxeter system

We want to know what this has to do with a Weyl group

\mathcal{H}

\{\delta_{w}\text{ }|\text{ }w\in W\},
and the translation matrix from the KL basis to the standard one is upper triangular with respect to Bruhat order. We have

b_{x}=\sum_{y\leq x}h_{y,x}(v)\delta_{y},

where the Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials are the recursively defined coefficients

In fact, this construction is reliant only on the combinatorics of the Weyl group. We now have a theorem of Bernstein-Gelfand-Gelfand about Verma modules:

If

then there exists a dominant weight

and

such that

\mu=x\cdot\lambda,\eta=y\cdot\lambda,

with

in Bruhat order. This brings us our seminal conjecture, stated as follows.

For every

the following holds:

[M_{(y\cdot 0)}:L_{(x\cdot 0)}]=h_{y,x}(1).

\simeq\mathbf{k}\otimes_{R}R\otimes_{R^{s_{1}}}R\otimes_{R^{s_{2}}}\cdots\otimes_{R^{s_{m}}}R(m).

Now we can see more clearly how we get our Soergel modules. Explicitly, they’re any graded right
R
-modules isomorphic to a finite direct sum of shifts of direct summands of Bott-Samelson modules. Our subcategory
\overline{\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}}(\mathfrak{h},W)
from earlier is in fact a full subcategory of gmod-
R
. We can now consider the functor

\mathbf{k}\otimes_{R}(-):R\text{-gmod-}R\rightarrow\text{gmod-}R,

which is an additive functor sending
\overline{BS}(\underline{w})\mapsto\overline{BS}(\underline{w}).
Thus it restricts to

\mathbf{k}\otimes_{R}(-):\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}(\mathfrak{h},W)\longrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}}(\mathfrak{h},W).

This gives us the idea that Soergel modules can equivalently be thought of as graded
R
-modules that come from direct summands of
\mathbf{k}\otimes_{R}B,
where
B
is a Soergel bimodule. To make use of this functor, we give the following

4.4.2
Proposition

If
W
is finite, and
\mathfrak{h}
is reflection faithful, the assignment

\mathbf{k}\otimes_{R}\text{Hom}^{\bullet}_{R\text{-gmod-}R}(B,B^{\prime})\longrightarrow\text{Hom}^{\bullet}_{\text{gmod-}R}(\mathbf{k}\otimes_{R}B,\mathbf{k}\otimes_{R}B^{\prime})

is an isomorphism for any Soergel bimodules
B,B^{\prime}.

Before we move into the final part of the story, recall that Soergel bimodules give the categorification of the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H},
and that the split Grothendieck group is a
\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
-algebra with the isomorphism

[\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}(\mathfrak{h},W)]_{\oplus}\simeq\mathcal{H}.

When we descend to Soergel modules, we notice that the category
\overline{\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}}(\mathfrak{h},W)
is not monoidal. But we still take
\overline{\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}}(\mathfrak{h},W)
to be a right module category of
\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}(\mathfrak{h},W),
furnished by

(-)\otimes_{R}(-):\overline{\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}}(\mathfrak{h},W)\times\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}(\mathfrak{h},W)\longrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}}(\mathfrak{h},W).

Thus we take the split Grothendieck group
[\overline{\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}}(\mathfrak{h},W)]_{\oplus}
to be a right
[\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}(\mathfrak{h},W)]_{\oplus}
-module.

Now we can introduce the climax of our tale: proof that Soergel’s conjecture implies the Kazhdan-Lusztig conjecture. Following from [Elias et. al],

4.5
Proving the Kazhdan-Lusztig conjecture

4.5.1
Restating the conjecture

The Kazhdan-Lusztig conjecture states that for a complex semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, and any
x,y
in the Weyl group
W,

[M_{y}:L_{x}]=h_{y,x}(1)

where
h_{y,x}(1)
is the value of the Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomial
h_{y,x}(v)
at
v=1
. By Bernstein-Gelfand-Gelfand reciprocity
6.5
, the Kazhdan-Lusztig conjecture is equivalent to stating

(\text{Pr}_{x}:M_{y})=h_{y,x}(1).

Using the correspondence
[\mathcal{O}_{0}]\simeq\mathbb{Z}[W]
in the Grothendieck group, this means that we equivalently have

[\text{Pr}_{x}]=b_{x}|_{v=1},

where
b_{x}|_{v=1}
is the specialization at
v=1
of the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis element
b_{x}\in\mathcal{H}.
Recall finally that Soergel’s conjecture is true if for every
x\in W,

\chi(B_{x})=b_{x}.

We now move to our final proposition.

4.5.2
Proposition

2

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex semisimple Lie algebra. Then Soergel’s conjecture for the geometric representation of its Weyl group
W
implies the Kazhdan-Lusztig conjecture for
\mathfrak{g}.

Proof:
We will begin by induction on Bruhat order. We have

\text{Pr}_{\text{id}}=M_{\text{id}},

which implies that
[\text{Pr}_{\text{id}}]=\text{id}=b_{\text{id}}|_{v=1}.
Let
x>\text{id}
in Bruhat order. Choose
s\in S
such that
xs<s,
and take
x=ws,
where
w<x.
Then

\Theta_{s}\text{Pr}_{w}\simeq\text{Pr}_{x}\oplus\bigoplus_{z<x}\text{Pr}^{\oplus m_{z}}_{z}

for multiplicities
m_{z}\geq 0.
In the Grothendieck group, we obtain by induction

[\text{Pr}_{x}]=[\Theta_{s}\text{Pr}_{w}]-\sum_{z<x}m_{z}[\text{Pr}_{z}]=[\text{Pr}_{w}](1+s)-\sum_{z<x}m_{z}[\text{Pr}_{z}]

=b_{w}b_{s}-\sum m_{z}b_{z}|_{v=1}.

Applying
\mathbb{V}
to
\Theta_{s}\text{Pr}_{w},
we get

\overline{B_{w}}B_{s}\simeq\overline{B_{x}}\oplus\bigoplus_{z<x}\overline{B_{z}}^{\oplus m_{z}},

and since Soergel modules decompose the same as Soergel bimodules,

B_{w}B_{s}\simeq B_{x}\oplus\bigoplus_{z<x}B^{\oplus m_{z}}_{z}.

Taking characters and invoking Soergel’s conjecture, we get

b_{x}=b_{w}b_{s}-\sum_{z<x}m_{z}b_{z}.

Then by the equality

[\text{Pr}_{w}](1+s)-\sum_{z<x}m_{z}[\text{Pr}_{z}]=b_{w}b_{s}-\sum m_{z}b_{z}|_{v=1},

we get
[\text{Pr}_{x}]=b_{x}|_{v=1},
which is the equivalent form of the Kazhdan-Lusztig conjecture.

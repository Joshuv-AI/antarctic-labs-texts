Convergence rate of ℓ p \ell^{p} -energy minimization on graphs: sharp polynomial bounds and a phase transition at p = 3 p=3
Gideon Amir, Fedor Nazarov, Yuval Peres

Abstract
. We consider the following dynamics on a connected graph ( V , E ) (V,E) with n n vertices. Given p > 1 p>1 and an initial opinion profile f 0 : V → [ 0 , 1 ] f_{0}:V\to[0,1] , at each integer step t ≥ 1 t\geq 1 a uniformly random vertex v = v t v=v_{t} is selected, and the opinion there is updated to the value f t ​ ( v ) f_{t}(v) that minimizes the sum ∑ w ∼ v | f t ​ ( v ) − f t − 1 ​ ( w ) | p \sum_{w\sim v}|f_{t}(v)-f_{t-1}(w)|^{p} over neighbours w w of v v . The case p = 2 p=2 yields linear averaging dynamics, but for all p ≠ 2 p\neq 2 the dynamics are nonlinear. In the limiting case p = ∞ p=\infty (known as Lipschitz learning ), f t ​ ( v ) f_{t}(v) is the average of the largest and smallest values of f t − 1 ​ ( w ) f_{t-1}(w) among the neighbours w w of v v . We show that the number of steps needed to reduce the oscillation of f t f_{t} below ϵ \epsilon is at most n β p n^{\beta_{p}} (up to logarithmic factors in n n and ϵ \epsilon ), where β p := max ⁡ ( 2 ​ p p − 1 , 3 ) \beta_{p}:=\max(\frac{2p}{p-1},3) ; we prove that the exponent β p \beta_{p} is optimal. The phase transition at p = 3 p=3 is a new phenomenon. We also derive matching upper and lower bounds for convergence time as a function of n n and the average degree; these are the most challenging to prove.

1.4.
Background and history

The
\ell^{p}
-energy minimization dynamics have been studied most intensively for
p=2
. In this case, (
1
) updates the value at the selected vertex
v_{t}
to the average of the values at its neighbours; in particular, the dynamics are linear. The case
p=2
is an asynchronous version of the dynamics introduced by deGroot
[
2
]
as a model for non-Bayesian social learning (see also the survey by Golub and Jackson
[
7
]
). In deGroot’s original paper, the dynamics are synchronous, i.e., all vertices update their opinions simultaneously at each step, based on the current opinions. DeGroot
[
2
]
and Demarzo, Vayanos and Zwiebel
[
3
]
proved that if
G
is not bipartite, then in the long run, opinions converge to consensus. Tight bounds on convergence rates for the asynchronous version of the deGroot dynamics were recently proved by Elboim, Peres and Peretz in
[
4
]
and are related to the spectral properties of the graph. In particular, Theorem
2.1
(b) there implies that

\mathbb{E}[\tau_{2}(\epsilon)]\leq n^{2}D_{G}\cdot\mathrm{Diam}(G)\lceil\log_{2}(1/\epsilon)\rceil\leq n^{3}D_{G}\lceil\log_{2}(1/\epsilon)\rceil\,,

and the RHS agrees with Theorem
1.9
up to a
\log n
factor.

Like the deGroot dynamics, one could study the synchronous
\ell^{p}
energy minimization dynamics for any
1<p\leq\infty
, where at each time
t
the opinions at
all
vertices are updated simultaneously using the opinions at their neighbours.
In the present work we restrict our attention to asynchronous updating.

The dynamics can also be considered in continuous time, as was done for
p=2
in
[
4
]
, by putting i.i.d. Poisson clocks on the vertices, and updating the value at a vertex when its clock rings. All results that are stated in the present paper for the discrete time models can be easily translated to the continuous time models, by making a time-change. In particular, if
\tau^{\rm Cont}_{p}(\epsilon)
denotes the
\epsilon
-consensus time of the continuous-time dynamics, then
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tau^{\rm Cont}_{p}(\epsilon)\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tau_{p}(\epsilon)\bigr]/n
.

For the Lipschitz learning dynamics (
2
), the update step is simple to calculate, but the absence of strict convexity changes the nature of arguments required to analyze the dynamics. Infinity harmonic functions arise as value functions for tug-of-war games analyzed by Peres, Schramm, Sheffield and Wilson
[
13
]
; they used this connection to generalize Fact
1.6
(due to
[
10
]
) on existence of infinity harmonic extensions to infinite graphs and length spaces. The game theoretic interpretation of the infinity harmonic extension immediately implies that this extension operator is monotone: if the boundary values are increased, then the extension cannot decrease. This monotonicity can also be deduced from the convergence of the Lipschitz learning dynamics. Lazarus et al.
[
10
]
also presented a polynomial time algorithm for finding infinity harmonic extensions on finite graphs. Their algorithm was generalized to
weighted graphs by Kyng, Rao, Sachdeva and Spielman
[
9
]
. Oberman
[
11
]
suggested computing the infinity harmonic extensions in Euclidean space by first discretizing the problem and then solving the corresponding graph problem by an iterative method.
To the best of our knowledge, no polynomial bounds on the convergence time of the dynamics (
1
) and (
2
) have been previously obtained for any
p\neq 2
.

We remark that the related problem of finding
p
-harmonic extensions on graphs was studied in the literature in the contexts of semi-supervised learning (see, e.g., Flores, Calder and Lerman
[
6
]
and Elmoataz, Desquesnes and Toutain
[
5
]
) and of
\ell^{p}
-regression (e.g., by Adil, Kyng, Peng and Sachdeva in
[
1
]
).

For
p=1
, the
\ell^{1}
norm is no longer strictly convex and the minimizer in (
1

p>1

1.5.
p
-superharmonic functions and monotonicity of the dynamics.

Let
f:V\to\mathbb{R}
. For
1<p<\infty
, the dynamics (
1
)
at time
t=1
, with initial opinion profile
f_{0}=f
and update vertex
v=v_{1}
, yield a new value
f_{1}(v)
that minimizes

\Psi_{f}(y)=\sum_{w\sim v}|y-f(w)|^{p}
. Thus

0=\Psi_{f}^{\prime}(f_{1}(v))=p\sum_{w\sim v}|f_{1}(v)-f(w)|^{p-1}\mathrm{sgn}(f_{1}(v)-f(w))\,.

We say that
f
is
p
-
superharmonic
at
v
if
f(v)\geq f_{1}(v)
when
f_{1}
arises from an update at
v
. Since
\Psi_{f}
is strictly convex, this is equivalent to

0\leq\Psi_{f}^{\prime}(f(v))=p\sum_{w\sim v}|f(v)-f(w)|^{p-1}\mathrm{sgn}(f(v)-f(w))\,.

(18)

This observation implies the known fact that the dynamics (
1
) are
monotone:

Claim 1.10
.

If the opinion profiles
f,g:V\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy
f\leq g
, and both profiles are updated at the same vertex
v
,
yielding
f_{1}
and
g_{1}
respectively, then
f_{1}(v)\leq g_{1}(v)
.

Proof.

For every
y\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\Psi_{f}^{\prime}(y)=p\!\!\sum_{w\sim v\atop f(w)\leq y}\!\!\!(y-f(w))^{p-1}-p\!\!\sum_{w\sim v\atop f(w)>y}\!\!\!(f(w)-y)^{p-1}\,.

For each vertex
w\sim v
, when we replace
f(w)
by
g(w)>f(w)
, the corresponding summand decreases (or becomes negative) if it is positive or zero, and increases in absolute value if it is negative. Thus

\Psi_{f}^{\prime}(y)\geq\Psi_{g}^{\prime}(y)
for all real
y
, whence
\Psi_{f}^{\prime}(g_{1}(v))\geq 0
, i.e., the function obtained from
f
by replacing the value at
v
by
g_{1}(v)
is
p
-superharmonic at
v
.
∎

1.6.
Sketch of convergence

Let us sketch why the convergence to consensus holds (Fact
1.1
). For this, the randomness assumption on the sequence of update vertices
\{v_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
can be relaxed; it is only required that this sequence visits each vertex in
V
infinitely often.

For
1<p<\infty
, it is clear that the energy
\mathcal{E}_{p}(f_{t})
is non-increasing in
t
, and therefore must converge. We deduce that every subsequential limit of
\{f_{t}\}
is a fixed point of the dynamics (
1
). Such functions are called
p
-
harmonic
on
V
, and the usual proof of the maximum principle shows that for
p>1
, any function that is
p
-harmonic at all vertices of a finite, connected graph is constant. Since
\min_{v\in V}f_{t}(v)
is non-decreasing in
t
, we deduce that the opinions
\{f_{t}\}
converge to a consensus.
Our proof of Theorem
1.9
(a) is based on quantifying the expected decrease of the energy in each step.

The argument for
p=\infty
is similar, once we find a potential function to replace the energy
\mathcal{E}_{p}(f)
. One such function is naturally suggested by the description in
[
9
]
of infinity harmonic extensions as lexicographic gradient minimizers (after non-increasing rearrangement, as explained below). Given a function
f:V\to\mathbb{R}
and and vertices
x,y
such that edge
e=\{x,y\}\in E
, define the
gradient

\nabla f(x,y):=f(y)-f(x)
and the

absolute gradient

|\nabla f(e)|:=|f(y)-f(x)|
.
Enumerate the edges of
G
as
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
, so that the absolute gradients
\{|\nabla f(e_{i})|\}_{i=1}^{m}
are non-increasing, and define
\Psi(f):=\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\nabla f(e_{i})|3^{-i}
. If
f(v)
differs from the average
\frac{f(v^{+})+f(v^{-})}{2}
of the maximal and minimal neighbouring values, then moving
f(v)
continuously towards this average decreases the largest absolute gradient at
v
, and could increase (at the same rate) only strictly smaller absolute gradients. It follows that
\Psi(f_{t+1})<\Psi(f_{t})
for every
t
such that
f_{t+1}\neq f_{t}
. Thus the sequence
\{\Psi(f_{t})\}_{t\geq 0}
must converge. Since the dynamics (
2
) and the mapping
f\mapsto\Psi(f)
are continuous, every subsequential limit of
\{f_{t}\}
must be a fixed point of these dynamics, i.e, it is infinity harmonic on

as before.

Our proofs of Theorems

(a) and
1.7
(a) use better potential functions, for which we can obtain good estimates for the expected improvement over time.

2.
Convergence rates for Lipschitz learning

We first state a generalization of Theorem
1.3
to arbitrary update sequences.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite connected graph, and let
\{v_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
be an arbitrary sequence of vertices in

Define inductively a sequence of times

T_{0}=0\;\;\text{ and}\quad T_{k+1}=\inf\bigl\{t>T_{k}:\{v_{j}\}_{j=T_{k}+1}^{t}\;\text{covers}\;V\bigr\}\,,

Therefore,

\tau_{\infty}(\epsilon)\leq T_{k(\epsilon)}\quad\text{where}\quad k(\epsilon):=\left\lceil\bigl(\mathrm{Diam}(G)^{2}+\mathrm{Diam}(G)\bigr)\log\frac{2}{\epsilon}\right\rceil\,.

Let

We say that a non-decreasing function

Claim 2.2

3.
Lipschitz learning with prescribed boundary values

3.1.
The infinity harmonic extension.

Fix
G=(V\cup B,E)
.
Given a path
\gamma=(\gamma_{0}\rightarrow\gamma_{1}\rightarrow\gamma_{2}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow\gamma_{\ell})
in
G

and a real-valued function
h
defined at the endpoints of
\gamma
, the
slope
of
h
on
\gamma
is
\frac{h(\gamma_{\ell})-h(\gamma_{0})}{\ell}
.
We say that the simple path
\gamma=(\gamma_{0}\rightarrow\gamma_{1}\rightarrow\gamma_{2}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow\gamma_{\ell})
of length
\ell\geq 2
is a
bridge
if
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{\ell}\in B
and
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\dots,\gamma_{\ell-1}\in V
.

We now state a more detailed version of Fact
1.6
:

Proposition 3.1
.

[
10
]

Let
G=(V\cup B,E)
be a finite connected graph, where
V,B\neq\varnothing
.
Given boundary values
h_{B}:B\to\mathbb{R}
, there exists a unique extension
h:V\cup B\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of
h_{B}
that is infinity harmonic on
V
. The extension
h
is linear along every bridge
\gamma=(\gamma_{0}\rightarrow\gamma_{1}\rightarrow\gamma_{2}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow\gamma_{\ell})
of maximal slope for
h_{B}
, that is,
h(\gamma_{k+1})-h(\gamma_{k})
equals the slope for all
0\leq k<\ell
.

The proof of the proposition in
[
10
]
is constructive. For convenience of the reader we include an exposition of this proof, which uses the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G=(V\cup B,E)
be a finite connected graph, where
V,B\neq\varnothing
. Suppose that
h:V\cup B\to\mathbb{R}
is infinity harmonic on
V
, and
e=\{v,w\}
is an edge of
G
with at least one endpoint in
V
. If
h(w)>h(v)
, then there is a bridge
\gamma
in
G
that includes
e
, such that all the gradients of
h
on
\gamma
satisfy
h(\gamma_{i})-h(\gamma_{i-1})\geq h(w)-h(v)
. In particular, the slope of
h|_{B}
on
\gamma
is at least
h(w)-h(v)
.

Proof.

If
w\in V
, denote by
w^{+}
a neighbour of
w
in
V\cup B
where
h
is maximized.
Since
h
is infinity harmonic on
V
,

h(w^{+})-h(w)=h(w)-h(w^{-})\geq h(w)-h(v)>0\,.

(24)

Continuing in this manner, we obtain a simple path
\gamma_{+}
from
w
to
B
where all the gradients of
h
are at least
h(w)-h(v)
.
Similarly, for
v\in V
, let
v^{-}
be a neighbour of
v
that minimizes
h
, and note that

h(v^{-})-h(v)=h(v)-h(v^{+})\leq h(v)-h(w)\,.

Continuing recursively, we obtain a simple path
\gamma_{-}
from
v
to
B
where all the gradients are at most
h(v)-h(w)<0
.
Concatenating the reversal of
\gamma_{-}
, the oriented edge
v\to w
, and the path
\gamma_{+}
,
we obtain a bridge
\gamma
where all the gradients of
h
on
\gamma
are at least
h(w)-h(v)
, as claimed.
∎

.

We will use induction on
|V|
. Let
W
be a connected component of
V
.
We separate two cases.

Case 1
. If
W
is adjacent to a single node

, then the usual proof of the maximum principle shows that the unique extension of
h_{B}
to

which is infinity harmonic on

is obtained by defining
h(w)=h_{B}(b_{W})
for every

.
If
W=V
, then we are done, otherwise we can replace
V
and
B
by
V\setminus W
and

, respectively, and apply the induction hypothesis.

Case 2
.
If
W
is adjacent to at least two nodes in

, then there is a bridge that intersects

. To verify the uniqueness of the extension,
suppose that
h:V\cup B\to\mathbb{R}
is an infinity harmonic extension of

, and
\gamma=(\gamma_{0}\to\dots\to\gamma_{\ell})
is a bridge intersecting

such that

has maximal slope

among such bridges. Then Lemma

implies that
h(\gamma_{k})-h(\gamma_{k-1})\leq s
for all

. Adding the interior nodes of

to the boundary, and removing them from

.

We can use the same argument to construct the infinity harmonic extension of

. First define
h
by linear interpolation on a bridge
\gamma=(\gamma_{0}\to\dots\to\gamma_{\ell})
intersecting
W
where
h_{B}
has maximal slope

. Then use the induction hypothesis to extend

to the rest of
V
, so it is infinity harmonic on

. It remains to check that

is infinity harmonic on

\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{\ell-1}\}
. If not, then there is some

such that either
h(\gamma_{k}^{+})>h(\gamma_{k+1})
or

to obtain a simple path

from

to some vertex

has slope strictly greater than

.
Let

be the concatenation of
\gamma_{0}\to\dots\to\gamma_{k}
and

. If

, then

is a bridge intersecting

in

has slope strictly greater than

for some

which yields the same contradiction. The case where

3.2.
Convergence rates for Lipschitz learning with boundary.

Denote by
\Delta_{\infty}
the
infinity Laplacian
on
G=(V\cup B,E)
, which maps functions
f:{V\cup B}\to\mathbb{R}
to real valued functions on
V
via

(\Delta_{\infty}f)(v)=f(v^{+})+f(v^{-})-2f(v)\,,

where
v^{+}
(respectively,
v^{-}
) denotes the neighbour of
v
in
V\cup B
at which
f
attains its highest (respectively, lowest) value.

A function
f:V\cup B\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
infinity superharmonic
at
v\in V
if
(\Delta_{\infty}f)(v)\leq 0
; it is infinity harmonic at
v
iff
(\Delta_{\infty}f)(v)=0
.

Recall (Fact
1.6
), that given
h_{B}:B\to\mathbb{R}
, there exists a unique extension

h:V\cup B\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of
h_{B}
which is infinity harmonic on
V
. This extension is invariant under the Lipschitz learning dynamics. Furthermore, by the monotonicity of the dynamics,
if
f_{0}(v)\geq h(v)
for all
v\in V\cup B
, then
f_{t}(v)\geq h(v)
for all
t
and all
v\in V\cup B
.
Note also that if
f_{0}
is infinity superharmonic on
V
, then
f_{t}
is infinity superharmonic on
V
for all
t\geq 0
.

The following lemma lies at the heart of our analysis.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that the functions
f,h:V\cup B\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy
f|_{B}=h|_{B}
. If
h
is infinity harmonic on
V
and
f
is infinity superharmonic on
V
, then

\|f-h\|_{\infty}\leq n\|\Delta_{\infty}f\|_{1}\,,

(25)

where
n=|V|
and
\|\Delta_{\infty}f\|_{1}=\sum_{v\in V}|(\Delta_{\infty}f)(v)|
. Therefore,

\|f-h\|_{1}\leq n^{2}\|\Delta_{\infty}f\|_{1}\,.

(26)

Proof.

Define
\delta:=\|\Delta_{\infty}f\|_{1}
. We will prove (
25
) by induction on the number of interior vertices
n=|V|
.

We say that a path
\gamma=(\gamma_{0}\rightarrow\gamma_{1}\rightarrow\ldots\rightarrow\gamma_{k})
is
greedy
(for
f
) if
\{\gamma_{j}\}_{j=1}^{k-1}
are distinct vertices in
V
, and for every
1\leq i<k
, we have
\gamma_{i+1}=\gamma_{i}^{+}
, that is,
f(\gamma_{i+1})=\max_{u\sim\gamma_{i}}f(u)
.
For any three adjacent vertices

\gamma_{i-1},\gamma_{i},\gamma_{i+1}

along a greedy path,

\nabla f(\gamma_{i-1},\gamma_{i})=f(\gamma_{i})-f(\gamma_{i-1})\leq f(\gamma_{i})-f(\gamma_{i}^{-})\,,

so

\nabla f(\gamma_{i-1},\gamma_{i})-\nabla f(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{i+1})\leq 2f(\gamma_{i})-f(\gamma_{i+1})-f(\gamma_{i}^{-})=|\Delta_{\infty}f(\gamma_{i})|\,.

Therefore, if
e_{1},e_{2}
are oriented edges along a greedy path where
e_{1}
precedes
e_{2}
, then

\nabla f(e_{1})-\nabla f(e_{2})\leq\delta=\|\Delta_{\infty}f\|_{1}\,.

(27)

Next, we separate two cases:

Case 1
. Suppose that every connected component
W
of
V
is adjacent to a single vertex
b_{W}\in B
.

Fix such a component
W
.
Then
h(w)=h(b_{W})
for all
w\in W
. If
w_{0}\in W\cup\{b_{W}\}

and
w_{1}\in W
are neighbours with

, then we construct a greedy path
w_{0}\to w_{1}\to\dots\to w_{\tau}
for
f
, where
\tau
is minimal such that

. We infer from (
27
) that

\nabla f(w_{0},w_{1})\leq\nabla f(w_{0},w_{1})-\nabla f(w_{\tau-1},w_{\tau})\leq\delta\,.

For every vertex
w\in W

f
along a shortest path from
b_{W}
to

. This verifies (
25
) in Case 1.

.
If some connected component of
V
is adjacent to two or more vertices in

.

Since

.
The path continues for

for

. We will focus on the upper envelope
f_{0}
, as the statement for
g_{0}
can be inferred by observing that
1-g_{0}
is an upper envelope for
1-h
that agrees with it on
B
, and
1-h
is infinity harmonic on
V
. Note that
f_{0}
is infinity superharmonic, and therefore so are
f_{t}
for all
t>0
.

Suppose that
f:V\cup B\to\mathbb{R}
is infinity superharmonic and agrees with
h
on
B
. Let
f^{v}
be obtained from
f
by updating at the vertex
v
. Then

\|f-h\|_{1}-\|f^{v}-h\|_{1}=f(v)-f^{v}(v)=\frac{|\Delta_{\infty}f(v)|}{2}\,.

(33)

Averaging over the choice of the update vertex
v
, we get

\|f-h\|_{1}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{v\in V}\|f^{v}-h\|_{1}\geq\frac{1}{2n}\|\Delta_{\infty}f\|_{1}\geq\frac{1}{2n^{3}}\|f-h\|_{1}\,,

(34)

where the rightmost inequality follows from (
26
).
We deduce that

Rearranging and applying this to
f_{s}
in place of
f
yields

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\,\|f_{s+1}-h\|_{1}\,\mid\,f_{s}\bigr]\leq\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{2n^{3}}\Bigr)\|f_{s}-h\|_{1}\,.

Taking expectations and iterating this inequality for
s\in[0,t)
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}[\|f_{t}-h\|_{1}]\leq\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{2n^{3}}\Bigr)^{t}\|f_{0}-h\|_{1}\leq n\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{2n^{3}}\Bigr)^{t}\,,

since
0\leq f_{0},h\leq 1
on
V
and
f_{0}=h
on
B
.
Part (a) of the theorem now follows, as
(1-x)\leq e^{-x}
.

To prove part (b), note that

\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\|f_{t}-h\|_{\infty}>\epsilon\Bigr)\leq\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\|f_{t}-h\|_{1}>\epsilon\Bigr)\leq\frac{n}{\epsilon}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{t}{2n^{3}}\Bigr)\,,

(35)

where the second inequality follows from part (a) and Markov’s inequality.
The RHS of (
35
) is less than 1 if

t>t^{*}_{\epsilon}:=2n^{3}\log\frac{n}{\epsilon}\,.

Therefore,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[\tau^{*}(\epsilon)]&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\|f_{\lfloor t\rfloor}-h\|_{\infty}>\epsilon\Bigr)\,dt\leq t^{*}_{\epsilon}+1+\int_{t^{*}_{\epsilon}+1}^{\infty}\frac{n}{\epsilon}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{t-1}{2n^{3}}\Bigr)\,dt\\[4.30554pt]
&=t^{*}_{\epsilon}+1+\int_{t^{*}_{\epsilon}+1}^{\infty}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{t-1-t^{*}_{\epsilon}}{2n^{3}}\Bigr)\,dt=t^{*}_{\epsilon}+1+2n^{3}=1+2n^{3}\log\frac{ne}{\epsilon}\,.\end{split}

∎

Proof of Proposition
1.8
.

As in the previous proof, it suffices to consider an infinity superharmonic
f_{0}
that agrees with
h
on
B
and takes value 1 on
V
. Then all the functions
f_{s}
for
s\geq 0
are infinity superharmonic, and satisfy
f_{s+1}\leq f_{s}
. The key observation is that if
t\leq s<t+n
and
w\neq v_{r}
for
r\in[t+1,s]
, then
|\Delta_{\infty}f_{s}(w)|\geq|\Delta_{\infty}f_{t}(w)|
.
Therefore, we can infer from (
33
) that

\displaystyle\|f_{t}-h\|_{1}-\|f_{t+n}-h\|_{1}=\sum_{s=t}^{t+n-1}\frac{|\Delta_{\infty}f_{s}(v_{s+1})|}{2}\geq\sum_{s=t}^{t+n-1}\frac{|\Delta_{\infty}f_{t}(v_{s+1})|}{2}=\frac{\|\Delta_{\infty}f_{t}\|_{1}}{2}\,.

In conjunction with (
26
), this yields

\|f_{t+n}-h\|_{1}\leq\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{2n^{2}}\Bigr)\|f_{t}-h\|_{1}\,.

Thus,

\|f_{t}-h\|_{1}\leq\|f_{0}-h\|_{1}\exp\Bigl(\frac{-\lfloor t/n\rfloor}{2n^{2}}\Bigr)\,.

Since
\|f_{0}-h\|_{1}\leq n
, the first claim of the theorem is proved. The claimed upper bound on
\tau^{*}(\epsilon)
follows readily.
∎

4.
Convergence rates for
1<p<\infty
with no boundary

Our main objective in this section is to prove parts (a) and (b) of Theorem
1.9
on energy decay and time to
\epsilon
-consensus under the
\ell^{p}
-energy minimization dynamics.

Recall the function

F(n,p,D):=n^{-\beta_{p}}(D/n)^{-\theta_{p}}=\begin{cases}n^{\frac{1-2p}{p-1}}D^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}\,\ \,&1<p\leq 2\,,\\
n^{-3}D^{\frac{p-3}{p-1}}\,\ \,&2<p<3\,,\\
n^{-3}\,\ \,&p\geq 3\,,\end{cases}

(36)

defined in (
16
). Define the constant
c_{p}
:

c_{p}:=\begin{cases}p2^{\frac{-2}{p-1}}\,\ \;&1<p\leq 2\,,\\
\frac{p}{80(p-1)}\,\ \;&2<p\,.\\
\end{cases}

(37)

Note that
c_{p}\geq\frac{1}{80}
for all
p>2
, and that
c_{p}\rightarrow 0
as
p\downarrow 1
.

Let
g:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a non-constant function, and suppose that
g^{\sharp}
is obtained from
g
by updating the value at some vertex.
As noted in the introduction,
\mathcal{E}_{p}(g^{\sharp})\leq\mathcal{E}_{p}(g)
. The next lemma gives a lower bound on the relative energy decrease.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
g:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be any non-constant function, and let
g^{\sharp}
be the function after updating the value at a uniformly chosen random vertex.
Then

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathcal{E}_{p}(g)-\mathcal{E}_{p}(g^{\sharp})}{\mathcal{E}_{p}(g)}\right]\geq c_{p}F(n,p,D)\,.

(38)

For ease of notation, we identify the vertices of
G
with the set
\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
and write
g_{i}=g(i)
. We may assume, without loss of generality, that the vertices are ordered so that
g_{1}\geq g_{2}\geq\cdots\geq g_{n}
.

Define the local
\ell^{p}
-energy of
g
at a vertex
i
by

\mathcal{E}_{i,p}(g)=\sum_{j:j\sim i}|g_{i}-g_{j}|^{p}\,.

(39)

The (total) energy is

\mathcal{E}_{p}(g)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i,p}(g)\,.

(40)

To show (
38
), we define

\displaystyle R_{i}^{+}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{j<i:j\sim i}(g_{j}-g_{i})^{p-1}\,,

(41)

\displaystyle R_{i}^{-}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{j>i:j\sim i}(g_{i}-g_{j})^{p-1}\,,

(42)

\displaystyle\rho_{i}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle|R_{i}^{+}-R_{i}^{-}|\,,

(43)

\displaystyle R_{i}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle R_{i}^{+}+R_{i}^{-}\quad\text{and}\quad r_{i}:=R_{i}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\,.

(44)

The derivative of
\mathcal{E}_{p}(g)
with respect to
g_{i}
is
p(R_{i}^{-}-R_{i}^{+})
.
One can think of
pR_{i}^{+}
as the “upward pull” of the neighbours of
i
on
g_{i}
, and of
pR_{i}^{-}
as the “downward pull”.

Denote by
d_{i}
the degree of the vertex
i
, write
\delta_{i}:={d_{i}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}
, and define

I_{i}=\begin{cases}\rho_{i}^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\delta_{i}^{-1}\;\quad&1<p\leq 2\,,\\
\rho_{i}^{2}\,r_{i}^{2-p}\,\delta_{i}^{-1}\;\quad&p>2\,.\end{cases}

(45)

Next, we give a lower bound on the energy decrease when vertex
i
is updated.

Claim 4.2
.

If vertex
i
is updated, then the energy decrease
\mathcal{E}_{p}(g)-\mathcal{E}_{p}(g^{\#})
is at least
\tilde{c}_{p}I_{i}
, where
\tilde{c}_{p}=c_{p}
for
1<p\leq 2
and
\tilde{c}_{p}=10c_{p}
for
p>2
.

Proof.

We will show that there is some
y
so that changing the value at
i
from
g_{i}
to
y
results in the required decrease in the energy. The claim will follow since at an update the value chosen minimizes the energy.

Let
g_{i}(\lambda):=g_{i}\pm\lambda
, where we take
+\lambda
if
R_{i}^{+}\geq R_{i}^{-}
, and
-\lambda
otherwise. Let
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\lambda):=\mathcal{E}_{p}(g(\lambda))
, where in
g(\lambda)
all coordinates except
g_{i}(\lambda)
agree with
g
. Then the derivatives of
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\lambda)
with respect to
\lambda
satisfy

\mathcal{E}_{p}^{\prime}(0)=-p\rho_{i}\,,

(46)

while

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)=p(p-1)\sum_{j:j\sim i}|g_{i}-g_{j}\pm\lambda|^{p-2}\,.

(47)

To lower bound the energy decrease when we replace
g_{i}
by
g_{i}\pm\Lambda
, we will derive an upper bound on
\mathcal{E}_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)
for
\lambda\in[0,\Lambda]
and then use the identity

\mathcal{E}_{p}^{\prime}(\Lambda)=\mathcal{E}_{p}^{\prime}(0)+\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\mathcal{E}_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)d\lambda=-p\rho_{i}+\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\mathcal{E}_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)d\lambda\,.

(48)

We divide the rest of the proof into two cases according to the value of
p
.

Case

1<p\leq 2
:

We use the easy inequality that for every
\Lambda>0
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
,

\int_{0}^{\Lambda}(p-1)|a\pm\lambda|^{p-2}d\lambda\leq 2(\Lambda/2)^{p-1}=2^{2-p}\Lambda^{p-1}\,

(49)

(Since the maximum is achieved when
a=\mp\frac{\Lambda}{2}
).
Summing the above inequalities over all
j\sim i
with
a_{j}=g_{i}-g_{j}
, we obtain from (
47
) that

\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\mathcal{E}_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\leq p2^{2-p}\Lambda^{p-1}d_{i}\,.

(50)

If we take

\Lambda^{*}:=\left(\frac{\rho_{i}}{2^{3-p}d_{i}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\,,

(51)

then for all
\Lambda\in[0,\Lambda^{*}]
, the integral in (
50
) is at most
p\frac{\rho_{i}}{2}
, so (
48
)
yields that
\mathcal{E}_{p}^{\prime}(\Lambda)\leq-p\frac{\rho_{i}}{2}
for all
\Lambda\in[0,\Lambda^{*}]
, and therefore the energy decrease when we replace
g_{i}
by
g_{i}+\Lambda^{*}
(if
R_{i}^{+}\geq R_{i}^{-}
) or by
g_{i}-\Lambda^{*}
(if
R_{i}^{+}<R_{i}^{-}
), is at least

\Lambda^{*}\cdot p\rho_{i}/2=\tilde{c}_{p}I_{i}\,,

(52)

where
\tilde{c}_{p}=p2^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}
.
This concludes the case
1<p\leq 2
.

Case
p>2
:

Writing
R_{i}(\lambda):=\sum_{j:j\sim i}|g_{i}(\lambda)-g_{j}|^{p-1}
and
r_{i}(\lambda)=R_{i}(\lambda)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}
, we have

\mathcal{E}_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)=p(p-1)\sum_{j:j\sim i}|g_{i}(\lambda)-g_{j}|^{p-2}\leq p(p-1)r_{i}(\lambda)^{p-2}\delta_{i}\,,

(53)

where the inequality follows from applying Hölder with
\widetilde{p}=p-1
and
\widetilde{q}=\frac{p-1}{p-2}
, and recalling that
\sum_{j:j\sim i}1=d_{i}
. Similarly,

R_{i}^{\prime}(\lambda)\leq(p-1)\sum_{j:j\sim i}|g_{i}(\lambda)-g_{j}|^{p-2}\leq(p-1)r_{i}(\lambda)^{p-2}\delta_{i}\,.

(54)

Since
R_{i}^{\prime}(\lambda)=(p-1)r_{i}(\lambda)^{p-2}r_{i}^{\prime}(\lambda)
, we infer that

r_{i}^{\prime}(\lambda)\leq\delta_{i}\,,

(55)

which implies that
r_{i}(\lambda)\leq r_{i}+\lambda\delta_{i}
.
Thus, for
\lambda\in[0,\Lambda]
, we have

\mathcal{E}_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)\leq p(p-1)\delta_{i}(r_{i}+\delta_{i}\Lambda)^{p-2}\,.

(56)

We now choose

\Lambda^{*}=\frac{\rho_{i}}{4(p-1)r_{i}^{p-2}\delta_{i}}\leq\frac{r_{i}}{4(p-1)\delta_{i}}\,,

(57)

where the inequality holds because
\rho_{i}\leq R_{i}=r_{i}^{p-1}
.
Then for all
\Lambda\in[0,\Lambda^{*}]
,

(r_{i}+\delta_{i}\Lambda)^{p-2}\leq r_{i}^{p-2}\left[1+\frac{1}{4(p-1)}\right]^{p-2}\leq 2r_{i}^{p-2}\,.

(58)

Using (
58
) together with (
56
) and the definition of
\Lambda^{*}
, we obtain that

\forall\Lambda\in[0,\Lambda^{*}],\quad\Bigl(\max_{\lambda\in[0,\Lambda]}\mathcal{E}_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)\Bigr)\Lambda\leq p\frac{\rho_{i}}{2}\,,

(59)

so by (
46
),

\forall\Lambda\in[0,\Lambda^{*}],\quad\mathcal{E}_{p}^{\prime}(\Lambda)\leq\mathcal{E}_{p}^{\prime}(0)+\Bigl(\max_{\lambda\in[0,\Lambda]}\mathcal{E}_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)\Bigr)\Lambda\leq-p\frac{\rho_{i}}{2}\,.

(60)

Therefore, the decrease of the energy due to replacing
g_{i}
with
g_{i}(\Lambda^{*})
is at least

\Lambda^{*}p\frac{\rho_{i}}{2}=\frac{p}{8(p-1)}\frac{\rho_{i}^{2}}{r_{i}^{p-2}\delta_{i}}=\tilde{c}_{p}I_{i}\,,

(61)

where
\tilde{c}_{p}=\frac{p}{8(p-1)}
.
This finishes the case
p>2
and completes the proof of the claim.

∎

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

To bound the ratio (
38
) of the expected energy decrease to the current energy of
g
, we may
multiply
g
by a constant so that
\sum_{i}\rho_{i}=1
. For vertices
i,j
with
i\sim j
, we write

\Delta_{ij}=|g_{i}-g_{j}|\,.

Recall the definitions of
R_{i}^{+}
and
R_{i}^{-}
from (
41
) and (
42
). For fixed
k\leq n
, changing the order of summation gives

\begin{split}\sum_{i\leq k}(R_{i}^{-}-R_{i}^{+})&=\sum_{i\leq k}R_{i}^{-}-\sum_{j\leq k}R_{j}^{+}\\
&=\sum_{i\leq k}\sum_{j>i\atop j\sim i}\Delta_{ij}^{p-1}-\sum_{j\leq k}\sum_{i<j\atop i\sim j}\Delta_{ij}^{p-1}=\sum_{i\leq k}\sum_{j>k\atop j\sim i}\Delta_{ij}^{p-1}\,.\end{split}

(62)

Indeed, every term
\Delta_{ij}^{p-1}
such that
i<j\leq k
and
i\sim j
appears in the penultimate summation twice, with opposite signs.
The LHS of (
62
) is bounded by
\sum_{i}\rho_{i}=1
, so the RHS is at most
1
as well. In particular, this implies that all summands on the RHS are
\leq 1
, and therefore

\sum_{i\leq k}\sum_{j>k\atop j\sim i}\Delta_{ij}^{s}\leq 1\,\ \quad\forall s\geq p-1\,.

(63)

Summing (
63
) over all
k
yields

\sum_{i<n}\sum_{j>i\atop j\sim i}(j-i)\Delta_{ij}^{s}\leq n\,\ \quad\forall s\geq p-1\,.

(64)

By taking
s=p
, we obtain the following bound on the energy:

\mathcal{E}_{p}(g)=\sum_{i<n}\sum_{j>i\atop j\sim i}\Delta_{ij}^{p}\leq\sum_{i<n}\sum_{j>i\atop j\sim i}(j-i)\Delta_{ij}^{p}\leq n\,.

(65)

We now proceed to derive a key estimate on the derivative of the energy.

For fixed
i
,

\sum_{j:j\sim i}|j-i|\Delta_{ij}^{p-1}=\sum_{j>i:j\sim i}(j-i)\Delta_{ij}^{p-1}+\sum_{j<i:j\sim i}(i-j)\Delta_{ij}^{p-1}\,.

(66)

Writing
d_{i}^{+}=\#\{j:j\sim i,j<i\}
and
d_{i}^{-}=\#\{j:j\sim i,j>i\}
, we have

\sum_{j>i:j\sim i}(j-i)\Delta_{ij}^{p-1}=d_{i}^{-}\left(\frac{1}{d_{i}^{-}}\sum_{j>i:j\sim i}(j-i)\Delta_{ij}^{p-1}\right)\,.

(67)

To bound the right-hand side, we will apply Chebyshev’s “other” inequality (see, e.g.,
[
8
]
pp. 43) to the uniform probability measure on
\{j>i:j\sim i\}
, using the fact that both
j-i
and
\Delta_{ij}=g_{i}-g_{j}
are non-decreasing in
j
for
j>i
. Thus

\sum_{j>i:j\sim i}(j-i)\Delta_{ij}^{p-1}\geq d_{i}^{-}\left(\frac{1}{d_{i}^{-}}\sum_{j>i:j\sim i}(j-i)\right)\left(\frac{1}{d_{i}^{-}}\sum_{j>i:j\sim i}\Delta_{ij}^{p-1}\right)\,.

Since
\displaystyle\sum_{j>i\atop j\sim i}(j-i)\geq\frac{(d_{i}^{-})^{2}}{2}
, we deduce that for each vertex
i
,

\sum_{j>i:j\sim i}(j-i)\Delta_{ij}^{p-1}\geq\frac{d_{i}^{-}}{2}R_{i}^{-}\,.

(68)

Similarly, we obtain

\sum_{j<i:j\sim i}(i-j)\Delta_{ij}^{p-1}\geq\frac{d_{i}^{+}}{2}R_{i}^{+}\,.

(69)

Since
d_{i}^{+}+d_{i}^{-}=d_{i}
, we combine the two estimates to get

\forall i,\quad\sum_{j:j\sim i}|j-i|\Delta_{ij}^{p-1}\geq\frac{d_{i}}{2}\min(R_{i}^{+},R_{i}^{-})\,.

(70)

Summing over
i
and using (
64
), we obtain that

\sum_{i}d_{i}\min(R_{i}^{+},R_{i}^{-})\leq 4n.

(71)

Since
R_{i}=R_{i}^{+}+R_{i}^{-}=2\min(R_{i}^{+},R_{i}^{-})+\rho_{i}
, it follows that

\sum_{i}R_{i}d_{i}\leq 8n+\sum_{i}\rho_{i}d_{i}\leq 9n\,.

(72)

Denote the energy decrease in one update by
\Delta(\mathcal{E}_{p}):=\mathcal{E}_{p}(g)-\mathcal{E}_{p}(g^{\#})
. Recall that our goal is to compare
\mathbb{E}[\Delta(\mathcal{E}_{p})]

to the total energy
\mathcal{E}_{p}(g)
.
We will use the bound
\mathcal{E}_{p}(g)\leq n
from (
65
), while
our lower bound for
\mathbb{E}[\Delta(\mathcal{E}_{p})]
will depend on
p
.
By Claim
4.2
, we have

\mathbb{E}[\Delta(\mathcal{E}_{p})]\geq\frac{\tilde{c}_{p}}{n}\sum_{i}I_{i}\,,

(73)

where
I_{i}
were defined in (
45
). We once again consider several cases.

Case

1<p\leq 2
:

In this case,

\sum_{i}I_{i}=\sum_{i}\rho_{i}^{\frac{p}{p-1}}d_{i}^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}\geq\frac{\left(\sum_{i}\rho_{i}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}}{\left(\sum_{i}d_{i}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\,,

(74)

where the inequality comes from applying Hölder to
d_{i}^{\frac{1}{p}}
and
\rho_{i}d_{i}^{-\frac{1}{p}}
, with exponents
p
and
q=\frac{p}{p-1}
.

Using our normalization that
\sum_{i}\rho_{i}=1
and writing
D
for the average degree in
G
, we get

\mathbb{E}[\Delta(\mathcal{E}_{p})]\geq\frac{\tilde{c}_{p}}{n}\sum_{i}I_{i}\geq\tilde{c}_{p}n^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}D^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\,.

(75)

Using (
65
), we conclude that

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\frac{\Delta(\mathcal{E}_{p})}{\mathcal{E}_{p}(g)}\Bigr]\geq\tilde{c}_{p}n^{\frac{1-2p}{p-1}}D^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}=c_{p}F(n,p,D)\,.

(76)

Case

p>2
:

By (
45
),

\sum_{i}I_{i}=\sum_{i}{\rho_{i}^{2}}r_{i}^{2-p}\delta_{i}^{-1}\geq\frac{(\sum_{i}\rho_{i})^{2}}{\sum_{i}r_{i}^{p-2}\delta_{i}}=\frac{1}{\sum_{i}r_{i}^{p-2}\delta_{i}}\,,

(77)

where the inequality follows from applying Cauchy-Schwarz to the two sequences
\rho_{i}\bigl(r_{i}^{2-p}\delta_{i}^{-1}\bigr)^{1/2}
and

\bigl(r_{i}^{p-2}\delta_{i}\bigr)^{1/2}
.

When
p\geq 3
, we have

\sum_{i}r_{i}^{p-2}\delta_{i}=\sum_{i}R_{i}^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}d_{i}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\leq\sum_{i}(R_{i}d_{i})^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\leq 10n\,,

(78)

where the last inequality followed from (
72
), together with the fact that for each
i
, either
0\leq R_{i}d_{i}<1
or
(R_{i}d_{i})^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\leq R_{i}d_{i}
.

Combining the last inequality with (
65
), (
73
) and (
77
), we conclude that

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\frac{\Delta(\mathcal{E}_{p})}{\mathcal{E}_{p}(g)}\Bigr]\geq\frac{\tilde{c}_{p}}{10}n^{-3}=c_{p}F(n,p,D)\,.

(79)

The last subcase remaining is
2\leq p<3
. In this range,
applying Hölder with
\widetilde{p}=\frac{p-1}{p-2}
and
\widetilde{q}=p-1
, we obtain

\begin{split}\sum_{i}r_{i}^{p-2}\delta_{i}&=\sum_{i}(R_{i}d_{i})^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}d_{i}^{\frac{3-p}{p-1}}\leq\Bigl(\sum_{i}R_{i}d_{i}\Bigr)^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\Bigl(\sum_{i}d_{i}^{3-p}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\\
&\leq(9n)^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}n^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\Bigl(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}d_{i}^{3-p}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\leq 9nD^{\frac{3-p}{p-1}}\,.\end{split}

(80)

The penultimate inequality follows from (
72
), and the final one follows from Jensen’s inequality. Substituting the above bound into (
77
), we obtain that

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\frac{\Delta(\mathcal{E}_{p})}{\mathcal{E}_{p}(g)}\Bigr]\geq\frac{\tilde{c}_{p}}{9}n^{-3}D^{\frac{p-3}{p-1}}>c_{p}F(n,p,D)\,.

(81)

Combining the three cases (
76
), (
79
), and (
81
), we get that for all
p>1
,

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\frac{\Delta(\mathcal{E}_{p})}{\mathcal{E}_{p}(g)}\Bigr]\geq c_{p}F(n,p,D)\,.

(82)

This completes the proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.9
(a) and (b).

Proof.

Part (a) follows from Lemma
4.1
by induction.
To prove part (b), suppose that
\mathfrak{osc}(f_{t})>\epsilon
. Then there is some edge
e
with
\nabla f_{t}(e)\geq\epsilon/n
, and this implies that
\mathcal{E}_{p}(f_{t})\geq(\epsilon/n)^{p}
.
On the other hand, since
\mathcal{E}_{p}(f_{0})\leq n^{2}
, part (a) gives

\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{E}_{p}(f_{t})]\leq n^{2}\exp\bigl(-c_{p}F(n,p,D)t\bigr)\,,

so by Markov’s inequality,

\mathbb{P}\bigl(\mathcal{E}_{p}(f_{t})>(\epsilon/n)^{p}\bigr)\leq\frac{n^{2}\exp\bigl(-c_{p}F(n,p,D)t\bigr)}{(\epsilon/n)^{p}}\,.

The RHS is less than 1 if

t>t_{\epsilon}:=\frac{\log(n^{2+p}\epsilon^{-p})}{c_{p}F(n,p,D)}\,.

Therefore,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[\tau_{p}(\epsilon)]&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{osc}(f_{\lfloor t\rfloor})>\epsilon)\,dt\leq t_{\epsilon}+1+\int_{t_{\epsilon}+1}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\exp\bigl(-c_{p}F(n,p,D)(t-1-t_{\epsilon})\bigr)dt\\[4.30554pt]
&=t_{\epsilon}+1+\frac{1}{c_{p}F(n,p,D)}\leq\frac{(2+p)\log(n/\epsilon)}{c_{p}F(n,p,D)}\,,\end{split}

provided that

∎

5.1.
The energy minimizing dynamics on the cycle

In this subsection we analyze the
\ell^{p}
-energy minimizing dynamics on the cycle with arbitrary update sequence.
Note that on the cycle, the
\ell^{p}
-energy minimizing dynamics are the same for all
p>1
; in each step, the opinion of the chosen vertex is replaced by the average of the opinions of its two neighbours. The following theorem gives a lower bound for the consensus time on the cycle which shows that the dependence on
n
in our upper bounds is tight for
p\geq 3
(including
p=\infty
) up to a
\log n
factor.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that
4|n
. Consider the cycle
\mathrm{C}_{n}=\{0,1,2,\ldots,n-1\}
with the initial profile
f_{0}\equiv 1_{v\geq n/2}
. Then for every update sequence
\{v_{s}\}_{s\geq 1}
and every
t\leq\frac{n^{3}}{2048}
, we have
\mathfrak{osc}(f_{t})\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\{v_{s}\}_{s\geq 1}
be an arbitrary update sequence.

To analyze
f_{t}(w)
we consider the following fragmentation process, which can be thought of as a dual of the averaging process. We initialize the process with
\mu_{0}=\delta_{w}
, and
\mu_{k+1}
is obtained from
\mu_{k}
by splitting the mass at
w_{k}
equally among its neighbours. We let
w_{k}=v_{t-k}
for
0\leq k\leq t-1
.

Using induction on
j
, we can verify that
f_{t}(w)=\sum_{v}\mu_{j}(v)f_{t-j}(v)
for each

j=0,\dots,t
. In particular,

f_{t}(w)=\sum_{v}\mu_{t}(v)f_{0}(v).

We now apply the following result from
[
12
]
:

Proposition 5.2
.

If
\mu_{t}(B(w,r)^{c})=\theta
, then
t\geq\theta^{2}r^{3}/2
, where
B(w,r)
is the open ball of radius
r
in the graph distance.

For convenience we recall the short proof of the proposition.

Proof.

It suffices to consider the fragmentation process on
\mathbb{Z}
, with
w=0
. If some fragmentation steps were performed outside of
B(0,r)
, then removing the last of these steps will yield a new update sequence
\{\tilde{v}_{s}\}_{s\geq 1}
for which
\tilde{\mu}_{t-1}(B(w,r)^{c})\geq\theta
.
Iterating, we may assume that no fragmentation steps were performed outside of
B(0,r)
.
Let

Q(\mu):=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}i^{2}\mu(i)\,\ \text{and}\,\ \mathcal{E}(\mu):=\sum_{i,j}|i-j|\mu(i)\mu(j).

(Both sums converge as there are only finitely many non-zero terms).
Then it is easily verified that

Q(\mu_{k+1})-Q(\mu_{k})=h_{k}\,\text{ and }\,\mathcal{E}(\mu_{k+1})-\mathcal{E}(\mu_{k})=h_{k}^{2}\,,

where
h_{k}=\mu_{k}(w_{k})
.
If
\theta=\mu_{t}(B(0,r)^{c})
, then, using Cauchy-Schwarz, we get

\left(\theta r^{2}\right)^{2}\leq\left(Q(\mu_{t})-Q(\mu_{0})\right)^{2}=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}h_{k}\right)^{2}\leq t\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}h_{k}^{2}\\
=t\left(\mathcal{E}(\mu_{t})-\mathcal{E}(\mu_{0})\right)\leq 2rt.

In the last inequality, we used the fact that, by our assumption on the updates, no mass travelled beyond
[-r,r]
.
We conclude that
t\geq\theta^{2}r^{3}/2
.
∎

We can now finish the proof of Theorem
5.1
.
Using the Lemma, we deduce that for

, so

. Similarly
f_{t}(\frac{3n}{4})\geq\frac{3}{4}
, whence
\mathfrak{osc}(f_{t})\geq\frac{1}{2}
for such
t
.

∎

For every integer

(a)

For all

h_{t}(-j)\leq h_{0}(-j)\,;

(102)

(b)

For all

We will prove both clauses together by induction on

We start by showing that (

(105)

We also reorder

so that

is nondecreasing and we have

Suppose that

In view of (

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{d}\bigl(h_{0}(w_{i,d})+k_{i}(t+1)\delta-\widetilde{h_{t}}(w_{i,j})\bigr)^{p-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{\geq}}\bigl(h_{t}(-n)-h_{0}(w_{i,d})-k_{i}(t+1)\delta\bigr)^{p-1}.

By the induction hypothesis, it suffices to prove that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\max\bigl(0\,,h_{0}(-n)-h_{0}(w_{i,j})-k_{i}(t)\delta\bigr)^{p-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{\geq}}n^{1-p}\,.

Lemma 5.11

Let

Proof.

The function
\psi
is convex and

\psi^{\prime}(z)=p\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Bigl(|f(u_{i})-z|^{p-1}\mathrm{sgn}(f(u_{i})-z)+|f(w_{d-i+1})-z|^{p-1}\mathrm{sgn}(f(w_{d-i+1})-z)\Bigr)\,.

Observe that for

, the two terms in the

f(u_{i})-z_{*}+f(w_{d-i+1})-z_{*}=f(u_{1})+(i-1)a+f(w_{d})-(i-1)a-2z_{*}=0\,.

Therefore
\psi^{\prime}(z_{*})=0
, as required.

Theorem 5.13
.

Fix
p\in[2,3]
and run the
\ell^{p}
-energy minimizing dynamics on the accordion graph
G_{d,n}
defined above with initial profile
f_{0}
.
If
d\geq 10
and
n\geq 12d
is a multiple of
d
, then for every updating sequence and every
t\leq\frac{1}{6400}n^{3}d^{\frac{3-p}{p-1}}
, we have
\mathfrak{osc}(f_{t})\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

We will bound the opinion profiles
f_{t}
that arise from
f_{0}
by the profiles
\psi_{t}
that arise from the initial profile
\psi_{0}\geq f_{0}
defined by

\displaystyle\psi_{0}\equiv 1\quad\text{on}\quad V(H_{-})\,,

\displaystyle\psi_{0}(w_{-i})=\psi_{0}(u_{-i})=1\quad 0\leq i\leq n\,,

\displaystyle\psi_{0}(w_{n})=\psi_{0}(u_{n})=\frac{1}{n}d^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}+\lambda\frac{m^{2}+2}{2m^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\psi_{0}(w_{i})=\psi_{0}(u_{i})=\frac{n-i}{n}+\frac{i}{n}\psi_{0}(w_{n})\quad 0\leq i<n\,,

\displaystyle\psi_{0}(v_{k,j})=\lambda\Bigl(\frac{k^{2}}{2m^{2}}+(j-1)\delta\Bigr)\quad-m\leq k\leq m,\quad 1\leq j\leq d\,,

where
\delta=\frac{1}{m^{2}(d-1)}
as in the previous lemma, and

\lambda=16mn^{-1}d^{\frac{-2}{p-1}}=16d^{-\frac{1+p}{p-1}}\,.

The value of
\psi_{0}(w_{n})
was chosen so that

d\Bigl(\psi_{0}(w_{n})-\max_{v\in V_{-m}}\psi_{0}(v)\Bigr)^{p-1}=\Bigl(\frac{1}{n}\Bigr)^{p-1}\,

(128)

and similarly for
u_{n}
and
V_{m}
instead of
w_{n}
and
V_{-m}
.

Next, we will verify by induction that for every
t\leq\frac{1}{160}(d-1)m^{3}
, the following inequalities hold:

(
a_{t}
)

\psi_{t}(v_{k,j})\leq\lambda\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{k^{2}}{2m^{2}}\Bigr)
for all
-m<k<m
and
1\leq j\leq d\,
;

(
b_{t}
)

\psi_{t}(v_{\pm m,j})\leq\psi_{0}(v_{\pm m,j})
for all
1\leq j\leq d\,
;

(
c_{t}
)

\psi_{t}(w_{i})\leq\psi_{0}(w_{i})
and
\psi_{t}(u_{i})\leq\psi_{0}(u_{i})
for all
0\leq i\leq n
.

The case
t=0
is clear. To verify the induction step, assume that the inequalities
(a_{s})
,
(b_{s})
and
(c_{s})
hold for all
s\leq t
and that
t+1\leq\frac{1}{160}(d-1)m^{3}
.
Then the inequality
(a_{t+1})
follows from the second clause of Lemma
5.12
, because under
(b_{s})_{s\leq t}
we have
\psi_{t+1}\leq\lambda\varphi_{t^{\prime}}
, where
t^{\prime}
is the number of updates in the interior anti-cliques of
H
(i.e., in
\cup_{|k|<m}V_{k}
) during the first
t+1
steps. To verify
(b_{t+1})
, it is enough to check that for every
1\leq j\leq d
,

\Bigl(\psi_{t}(w_{n})-\psi_{0}(v_{-m,j})\Bigr)^{p-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{\leq}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Bigl(\psi_{0}(v_{-m,j})-\psi_{t}(v_{-m+1,i})\Bigr)^{p-1}

(129)

(The case of
u_{n}
and
v_{m,j}
will follow similarly).

By the induction hypothesis,

\displaystyle\psi_{t}(w_{n})-\psi_{0}(v_{-m,j})\leq\frac{1}{n}d^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}+\frac{\lambda}{m^{2}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}d^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}+\frac{16d^{-\frac{p+1}{p-1}}}{m^{2}}\leq\frac{2}{n}d^{\frac{-1}{p-1}},

where the last inequality holds since
d\geq 10
and
m\geq 12
.

Therefore, the LHS of (
129
) is at most
2^{p-1}n^{1-p}d^{-1}
. By Lemma
5.12
(iii),

\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\psi_{t}(v_{-m+1,i})\leq\lambda\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{8m}\Bigr)\,.

(130)

Thus by our choice of
\psi_{0}
and
\lambda
, the RHS of (
129
) is at least

d\Bigl(\frac{\lambda}{8m}\Bigr)^{p-1}=d\Bigl(\frac{16d^{-\frac{p+1}{p-1}}}{8m}\Bigr)^{p-1}=2^{p-1}n^{1-p}d^{-1}\,,

(131)

so (
129
) holds.

It remains to show
(c_{t+1})
. For
0\leq i<n
, this follows from
(c_{t})
by linearity of
\psi_{0}
. To show that
\psi_{t+1}(w_{n})\leq\psi_{0}(w_{n})
, it is enough to verify that

\Bigl(\psi_{t}(w_{n-1})-\psi_{0}(w_{n})\Bigr)^{p-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{\leq}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Bigl(\psi_{0}(w_{n})-\psi_{t}(v_{-m,i})\Bigr)^{p-1}\,.

(132)

The LHS is bounded above by
n^{1-p}
. The RHS is bounded below by

d\Bigl(\psi_{0}(w_{n})-\max_{v\in V_{-m}}\psi_{0}(v)\Bigr)^{p-1}=n^{1-p}\,.

This concludes the induction step, so the inequalities
(a_{t}),(b_{t}),(c_{t})
hold for all
t\leq t^{*}
, where
t^{*}:=\Big\lfloor\frac{1}{160}(d-1)m^{3}\Big\rfloor\geq\frac{1}{200}dm^{3}
.
(Here we used our assumptions that
d=\lfloor D/2\rfloor\geq 10
and
m\geq 12
.)

We deduce that
\max_{t\leq t^{*}}\psi_{t}\leq\psi_{0}+\frac{\lambda}{4}
, and therefore
\max_{t\leq Lt^{*}}\psi_{t}\leq\psi_{0}+\frac{L\lambda}{4}
for all integer
L\geq 1
.
In particular,
for integer
L\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}
, we have
\max_{t\leq t^{*}}\psi_{t}(v_{0,1})\leq\frac{1}{4}
.
Therefore,
\min f_{t}\leq\min\psi_{t}\leq\frac{1}{4}\quad\text{ for all }\quad t\leq\frac{t^{*}}{2\lambda}
. (Note that
1/\lambda>2
.)
By symmetry,
\max f_{t}\geq\frac{3}{4}
for all
t\leq\frac{t^{*}}{2\lambda}
, so
\mathfrak{osc}(f_{t})\geq 1/2
for these values of
t
.

Since
\frac{t^{*}}{2\lambda}\geq\frac{1}{6400}d^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}m^{3}=\frac{1}{6400}d^{\frac{3-p}{p-1}}n^{3}\,,
this concludes the proof.

∎

6.
Open problems and concluding remarks

The upper and lower bounds in our main theorems suggest that the
\epsilon
-consensus time should be monotone decreasing in
p
.

Question 6.1
.

Is there an absolute constant
C
such that for every choice of integer
n>1
, connected graph
G
with
n
vertices, initial profile
f_{0}
and
\epsilon>0
, for every
1<p_{1}<p_{2}<\infty
, we have

\mathbb{E}[\tau_{p_{2}}(\epsilon)]\leq C\mathbb{E}[\tau_{p_{1}}(\epsilon)]\,?

Similar to the Lipschitz learning dynamics, the dynamics (
1
) for
1<p<\infty
can also be considered with prescribed boundary values.
It is not hard to show that given boundary values
f_{0}|_{B}
, the dynamics converge to a limiting profile which is the unique
p
-harmonic extension
h_{p}
of the values on the boundary to the whole graph (using the same energy considerations discussed in subsection
1.6
).

It is natural to ask how quickly the dynamics converge towards the unique
p
-harmonic extension
h_{p}
. To this end, we define the
\epsilon
-approximation time by:

\tau_{p}^{*}(\epsilon):=\min\{t\geq 0:\,\|f_{t}-h_{p}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon\}\,.

(133)

Question 6.2
.

Given
1<p<\infty
, what can be said about the asymptotics of
\tau_{p}^{*}(\epsilon)
?

For
p>2
, we expect that there are absolute constants
C_{p},\beta^{*}_{p}
such that for every connected graph
G=(V\cup B,E)
with
|V|=n
inner vertices, every initial profile
f_{0}:V\cup B\rightarrow[0,1]
and every
\epsilon>0
,

\mathbb{E}[\tau_{p}^{*}(\epsilon)]<C_{p}n^{\beta^{*}_{p}}\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\,;

we conjecture that this holds with
\beta^{*}_{p}=\beta_{p}
.

For
1<p<2
, a simple examples with
|V|=|B|=2
shows that the dependence on
\epsilon
is not logarithmic: Let
G
be the complete graph on 4 vertices, and suppose that
f_{0}(b)=1
for one vertex
b\in B
while
f_{0}

vanishes on the other three vertices. Then a direct calculation shows that

\forall p\in(1,2),\quad\tau_{p}^{*}(\epsilon)\geq c_{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\,,

where
c_{p}>0
depends only on
p
.

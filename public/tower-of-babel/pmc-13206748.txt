Environmental DNA (eDNA) dataset of fish diversity and distribution in the coastal waters of Weizhou Island, Beibu Gulf
Authors: Zhipeng Zhang, Ying Qiao, Yunfan Huang, Dewei Cheng, Xuyang Chen, Ersha Dang, Zheping Xu, Xiaowan Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractEnvironmental DNA (eDNA) analysis is an effective method for assessing biodiversity. This technology relies on detecting trace genetic material shed by organisms into their surroundings and has been widely used in ecological research. Abundant marine fish resources can be found in the waters of Weizhou Island, which is part of a unique volcanic island ecosystem in the northern South China Sea. Although previous research has documented the richness of fish species on Weizhou Island, methodological and seasonal limitations have resulted in data from these studies being incomplete. To comprehensively assess the fish biodiversity, we conducted eDNA metabarcoding. Seawater samples were collected from eight locations around Weizhou Island. At each location, three 1 L samples were collected, filtered through 0.45 µm membranes to capture eDNA, and subjected to DNA extraction. The 12S rRNA gene was amplified using MiFish-U primers and sequenced on the Illumina MiSeq platform (2 × 250 bp). This study utilized eDNA technology to conduct the most comprehensive survey to date, thereby identifying 113 fish species belonging to 28 orders, 47 families, and 79 genera. The main fish species detected in this study were Neopomacentrus anabatoides, Nematalosa japonica, Sardinella melanura, Neopomacentrus cyanomos, and Siganus fuscescens. The raw sequence data have been deposited in the NCBI Sequence Read Archive (SRA) under the BioProject accession number PRJNA1287299. The presented data have established a baseline eDNA database for the South China Sea and support targeted reef restoration on Weizhou Island.Keywords: Weizhou Island, Ecosystem, South China sea, Fish diversity, Environmental DNA

Background

Weizhou Island is situated in the central Beibu Gulf, northern South China Sea, and represents the region's youngest and largest volcanic island [1]. Characterized by diverse habitats including wetlands, sandbars, and coral reefs, the island hosts the northernmost coral reef ecosystem in the South China Sea and supports rich fishery resources. Notably, Balaenoptera edeni has been documented in the waters adjacent to Weizhou Island, making fishery resource surveys highly significant for understanding its potential prey sources. The marine ecosystem of Weizhou Island has faced severe degradation due to escalating anthropogenic pressures and natural disturbances, leading to a trophic shift toward lower-level dominance [2]. As fish are critical high-trophic vertebrates in marine ecosystems, understanding their composition and distribution is essential for assessing ecosystem status and guiding restoration.

Traditional fisheries survey methods face limitations such as low efficiency and high error rates. eDNA technology is a non-invasive method that identifies species by detecting their genetic material in environmental samples like water or sediment. This method offers significant advantages, including high sensitivity, minimal environmental disturbance, and enhanced efficiency [3,4]. Compared to conventional techniques, eDNA enables more effective analysis of fish spatial distribution and abundance, providing critical data for resource assessment, population protection, and coral reef restoration while reducing potential ecosystem damage [5,6]. Here, we have recorded the fish species near Weizhou Island, which may be useful for monitoring the ecosystem.

Data Description

This article describes the eDNA dataset of fish in the waters of Weizhou Island, as well as comparative data obtained through bioinformatics analysis.

Raw sequencing reads (FASTQ format) are available under NCBI SRA accession number PRJNA1287299 (three replicates per sample, totaling 24 sequencing files).

These samples were collected in the waters around Weizhou Island, Beibu Gulf, northern South China Sea, during the monsoon season in August 2023 (Fig. 1, sampling points covering the east, south, west, north, and southeast coastal areas of Weizhou Island). A survey of fish diversity in the waters surrounding Weizhou Island based on eDNA technology identified a total of 113 fish species. These species show rich taxonomic diversity, distributed across 28 orders, 47 families, and 79 genera (Fig. 2). Table 1 presents the latitude and longitude of the sampling locations. Table 2 shows the libraries merged based on the Illumina protocol and sequenced using the MiSeq platform (Illumina Inc., USA). According to the sequencing results, a total of 2652,804 valid trimmed sequences were generated, with an average length of 172.33 bp. Table 3 shows the taxonomic richness at the Weizhou Island sites measured using α diversity indices, including Observed species, Chao1, Shannon, Pielou J, and Simpson indices. Table 4 presents the list of orders present in the study area. Perciformes was the most abundant order, accounting for 45.46% of the total population, followed by Clupeiformes (30.94%). Table 5 shows the relative abundance of the families identified in the samples, indicating the successful identification of 47 families through eDNA metabarcoding. The family with the highest relative abundance belongs to Pomacentridae, with Neopomacentrus being the genus with the highest recorded relative abundance in the samples (Table 6). Table 7 presents the fish abundance in the study area, with Neopomacentrus anabatoides being the species with the highest relative abundance.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Sampling

Field sampling was conducted at eight stations around Weizhou Island (Fig. 1), with a minimum distance of 1.4 km between adjacent stations. At each site, three replicates of 1 L water samples were collected. The eDNA was captured by filtering water through 0.45 µm mixed cellulose ester (MCE) membranes. Each membrane, enriched with DNA, was individually preserved in a 2 mL centrifuge tube using liquid nitrogen during fieldwork[7]. A negative control was implemented by filtering 300 mL of purified water to monitor potential contamination.

DNA Extraction and PCR Amplification

DNA extraction was performed using the E.Z.N.A.® Water DNA Kit following the manufacturer’s instructions. To minimize cross-contamination, all instruments were decontaminated using a 5% bleach solution and 75% ethanol before the DNA extraction procedure[8]. Extracted DNA was stored at −20 °C prior to analysis. PCR amplification targeted marine fish species using the MiFish-U primer set (Forward: GTCGGTAAAACTCGTGCCAGC; Reverse: CATAGTGGGGTATCTAATCCCAGTTTG)[9], with unique 8-bp barcodes assigned to each sample. Reactions were carried out in triplicate 20 µL volumes containing: 4 µL 5× FastPfu Buffer, 2 µL 2.5 mM dNTPs, 0.8 µL each of forward and reverse primers (5 µM), 0.4 µL FastPfu Polymerase, 10 ng template DNA, and ddH2O topped up to 20 µL. Thermal cycling conditions included: initial denaturation (95 °C, 5 min); 30 cycles of denaturation (95 °C, 30 s), annealing (58 °C, 30 s), and elongation (72 °C, 45 s); followed by final extension (72 °C, 10 min).

PCR amplicons were electrophoresed on 2% agarose gels, purified using the AxyPrep DNA Gel Extraction Kit, and quantified via Qubit® 3.0 Fluorometer. Equal volumes of 24 uniquely barcoded amplicons were pooled for Illumina paired-end library construction. Sequencing was performed on an Illumina MiSeq platform (2 × 250 bp) by Shanghai LingEn Biotechnology Co., Ltd., adhering to standard protocols.

Library Preparation and Sequencing

Raw sequencing data were delivered in FASTQ format, with each sample comprising two paired-end read files (fq1 and fq2) containing forward and reverse reads, respectively. Initial processing involved demultiplexing using sample-specific barcodes. Quality control was performed by trimming low-quality bases (quality threshold ≤ Q20) from read termini using a sliding-window approach (window size: 10 bp; average quality cutoff: 20). Reads shorter than 50 bp after trimming were discarded. Paired-end reads were merged with a minimum overlap requirement of 10 bp and a maximum mismatch ratio of 0.2 within the overlap region. Primer sequences were identified with a tolerance of up to 2 mismatches, while barcode matching required exact matches. Chimeric sequences were removed using USEARCH v11 with the gold.fa database, implementing a hybrid de novo and reference-based chimera detection approach.

Bioinformatics Analysis

After obtaining high-quality sequences, DADA2 (version 1.18.0) was used to generate a feature table[10,11]. To obtain taxonomic information for each ASV, the BLASTn alignment method (default e-value: 1 × 10−10) was applied to classify ASV representative sequences. Target databases were selected for annotation based on the primers used. During annotation, priority was given to the MitoFish database (http://mitofish.aori.u-tokyo.ac.jp/download.html), followed by sequence alignment against the NCBI nucleotide database using the BLASTn tool (https://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Blast.cgi). The fish database provided by LingEn Biotech, based on the ChecklistBank platform hosted by GBIF and co-developed with the Catalogue of Life (https://www.checklistbank.org/dataset/1010/download), was used as the final annotation resource.

Based on the species annotation results, species were filtered (selecting fish species according to the FishBase database), and false-positive species were removed (reads with an abundance ≤ 5 at the species level in a single sample were considered false positives due to low abundance and adjusted to 0). Samples with zero abundance were then excluded. The number of fish reads detected in each sample was counted, and taxonomic information from phylum to species level was provided for each fish species, resulting in a species abundance annotation table.

Limitations

The sampling was conducted during the summer season, August 2023. While this provides a detailed snapshot of the fish community during a period of high biological activity, it represents a single time point. The data serve as an important baseline for this key season. In addition, the composition and diversity of the community may vary at other times of the year.

Secondary Stiefel–Whitney numbers and corresponding cobordism groups
Viktor Lavrukhin

Abstract
. For every relation R R between Stiefel–Whitney numbers of closed ( n + 1 ) (n+1) -manifolds we consider an associated invariant ϰ R \varkappa_{R} of null-cobordant n n -manifolds with a certain additional structure. For n = 2 ​ k − 1 n=2k-1 and R = w n + 1 + v k 2 R=w_{n+1}+v_{k}^{2} the invariant ϰ R \varkappa_{R} equals the Kervaire semi-characteristic. In addition, we construct the cobordism group Ω n R \Omega_{n}^{R} , which extends the unoriented cobordism group Ω n O \Omega_{n}^{O} . We show that ϰ R \varkappa_{R} is a complete invariant of R R -cobordism classes of null-cobordant n n -manifolds. We prove that our invariant ϰ R \varkappa_{R} and R R -cobordism class of manifold are quadratic in the sense of Gusarov–Vassiliev–Podkorytov.



1.
Introduction

The theory of Gusarov–Vassiliev finite-type invariants provides a powerful framework for studying knots and links. At the core, this theory provides a filtration on the set of invariant maps
\mathrm{Emb}(S^{1},S^{3})\to V
, where
V
denotes an abelian group. The term ‘invariant’ means that the map respects the isotopy relation on the space of knots.
It turns out that this idea can be generalized in many ways by changing the domain of an invariant to some version of the mapping space between the two space-like objects.
S. S. Podkorytov has initiated a project of developing related methods in various homotopical contexts, as presented in, e.g.,
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
.

In
[
3
]
, Kervaire defined the semi-characteristic
K(X)
for every closed
(2k-1)
-dimensional smooth manifold
X
by the formula

K(X):=\frac{\operatorname{dim}H^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z}/2)}{2}\bmod 2.

We can gather all the manifolds in one space using the concept of a germ. Choose a sufficiently large
q
and consider the set
E
of germs of the embedded
(2n-1)
-manifolds in
\mathbb{R}^{q}
. Then, every submanifold
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{q}
can be represented by its characteristic function
I_{X}\colon E\to\mathbb{Z}/2
. We say that an invariant of a smooth manifold
X\mapsto\varphi(X)\in V
is polynomial of degree
k
, if there is a polynomial function
\Phi\colon(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{E}\to V
such that
\Phi(I_{X})=\varphi(X)
for every closed smooth submanifold
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{q}
.
In paper
[
6
]
, S. S. Podkorytov establishes that the Kervaire semi-characteristic of a closed
(2n-1)
-manifold with
v_{n}
-structure (so-called Wu structure) is quadratic. In this paper, our aim is to generalize his result.

It is known that the Stiefel–Whitney numbers of closed
(n+1)
-manifolds are not independent. For example, for an odd-dimensional closed
(n+1)
-manifold
Z
one has
w_{n+1}(Z)=0
.
A less known relation is
w_{n+1}(Z)+v_{(n+1)/2}(Z)^{2}=0
for even-dimensional
(n+1)
-manifolds
Z
, where
v_{i}(Z)
denotes the Wu class. Let
\mathcal{R}_{n+1}
denote a subgroup of
H^{n+1}(BO_{n+1})
consisting of all the universal relations on Stiefel–Whitney numbers of closed
(n+1)
-manifolds. This group is described in papers
[
2
,
1
]
.
Of course, the class corresponding to
R
vanishes on every closed
n
-dimensional manifold
X
as well for dimension reasons.
With an arbitrary relation
R\in\mathcal{R}_{n+1}
we can associate a fibration

such that a
(B,f)
-structure on a given
n
-dimensional closed manifold

would correspond to a choice of an ‘annihilation’ for the class

in

; see §
3

-manifolds

Let

be some relation on the Stiefel–Whitney numbers. For a bounding manifold

equipped with some
R
-structure

, we define its invariant

taking values in

, which we call the

and Proposition

, as can be seen from the following theorem (note that every

-manifold admits some

-structure).

Proposition 1.1

(Proposition
3.8
)
.

Let

R=w_{n+1}+v^{2}_{(n+1)/2}
. Then the

-structure on bounding
n
-manifold

gives rise to an
R
-structure

such that
\varkappa_{R}(X,g_{X})=K(X)
.

Then, we associate a cobordism group

with every

; see Definition

. It extends the unoriented cobordsim group
\Omega^{O}_{n}
, and
\varkappa_{R}
serves as a complete invariant that determines an
R
-cobordism class.

Theorem 1.2

(Corollary
4.5
)
.

The forgetful homomorphism

is surjective, and the secondary Stiefel-Whitney number yield an isomorphism
\varkappa_{R}\colon\ker(U)\to\mathbb{Z}/2
. Thus, there is a short exact sequence

0\to\mathbb{Z}/2\to\Omega^{R}_{n}\to\Omega^{O}_{n}\to 0.

Lastly, we show that the
R
-cobordism class depends on an
R
-manifold quadratically in the above sense.
The claim that the correspondence
(X,g_{X})\mapsto[X,g_{X}]^{R}
is quadratic can be formalized in the following way.
Let
q\geq 2n+2
. Denote the set of germs of embedded
R
-manifolds in
\mathbb{R}^{q}
by
E_{R}
; see §
6
for the precise definition. Then the abelian group
(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{E_{R}}
of
\mathbb{Z}/2

naturally contains the set of embedded

-manifolds via characteristic functions:

\{R\text{-submanifolds of}\;\mathbb{R}^{q}\}\hookrightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{E_{R}},\quad(X,g_{X})\mapsto I_{(X,g_{X})}.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
6.2
)
.

There is a quadratic function
\mathfrak{Q}\colon(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{E_{R}}\to\Omega^{R}_{n}
such that for any closed
R
-manifold
(X,g_{X})
the equality
\mathfrak{Q}(I_{(X,g_{X})})=[X,g_{X}]^{R}
holds.

This implies that
\varkappa_{R}\colon(X,g_{X})\mapsto\varkappa_{R}(X,g_{X})\in\mathbb{Z}/2

To prove Theorem

, we develop some geometric machinery. First, we use a version of Pontryagin–Thom collapse map for

-manifolds; see §
5.3
. Second, we prove a relative version of Thom isomorphism and show how it interacts with characteristic classes; see §

\varkappa_{R}
depends on

5.1.
Explicit model for (co)chains

For every topological space
X
there are singular chains
C_{*}(X)=\mathbb{Z}/2[\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Top}}(\Delta^{*},X)]
and cochains
C^{*}(X)=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Ab}}(C_{*}(X),\mathbb{Z}/2)
.

Consider a morphism of complexes
f\colon C_{*}\to D_{*}
, as a bicomplex, where
D_{i}
is placed in a bidegree
(i,0)
and
C_{i}
is placed in bidegree
(i,1)
, respectively. Then there is a total complex whose
i
-chains are defined as
\mathrm{Tot}(f)_{i}=D_{i}\oplus C_{i-1}
. Analogously, for a morphism of cochain complexes
f\colon C^{*}\to D^{*}
, there is a totalization
\mathrm{Tot}(f)^{i}=C^{i}\oplus D^{i-1}
.

If
f\colon X\to Y
is a continuous map, then there are induced morphisms
f_{*}\colon C_{*}(X)\to C_{*}(Y)
and
f^{*}\colon C^{*}(Y)\to C^{*}(X)
. We can define relative chain and cochain complexes as

\displaystyle C_{*}(X\xrightarrow{f}Y)

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\mathrm{Tot}(f_{*}),

\displaystyle C^{*}(X\xrightarrow{f}Y)

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\mathrm{Tot}(f^{*}).

We will denote their elements as

c=(c_{(0)},c_{(1)})\in C_{i}(X\xrightarrow{f}Y)=C_{i}(Y)\oplus C_{i-1}(X)

and

u=(u^{(0)},u^{(1)})\in C^{i}(X\xrightarrow{f}Y)=C^{i}(Y)\oplus C^{i-1}(X).

One has
c\in Z_{*}(X\xrightarrow{f}Y)
iff
c_{(1)}\in Z_{*}(X)
and
f_{*}(c_{(1)})=\partial_{Y}(c_{(0)})
; and
u\in Z^{*}(X\xrightarrow{f}Y)
iff
u^{(0)}\in Z^{*}(Y)
and
f^{*}(u^{(0)})=\delta_{X}(u^{(1)})
.

It is easy to see that there are canonical isomorphisms

\displaystyle H_{k}(C_{*}(X\to Y))

\displaystyle=H_{k}(X\to Y),

\displaystyle H^{k}(C^{*}(X\to Y))

\displaystyle=H^{k}(X\to Y).

The long exact sequence of cohomology groups arises as the long exact sequence associated with the short exact sequence of cochain complexes

0\to C^{*-1}(X)\to C^{*}(X\to Y)\to C^{*}(Y)\to 0.

5.2.
Thom space

It is convenient to consider the sphere
S^{q}
as the one-point compactification
\mathbb{R}^{q}\cup\{*\}
. Let
D^{q+1}\vcentcolon=\mathbb{R}^{q+1}_{-}\cup\{*\}
be a model for a closed disk. Then there is a canonical inclusion
S^{q}\hookrightarrow D^{q+1}
.

For a Euclidean vector bundle
\xi
, denote by
D\xi
and
S\xi
the corresponding
disk
and
sphere bundles
, respectively.
The
Thom space
of a bundle
\xi
is defined as the quotient space
\Th\xi\vcentcolon=D\xi/S\xi

-manifold
(X,g)
, consider its subspace

Q_{(X,g)}=\{A\in Q\mid A(I_{(X,g)})=[X,g]^{R}\}.

Since every finite intersection
\bigcap_{i\in I}Q_{(X_{i},g_{X_{i}})}
is non-empty, the full intersection
\bigcap_{(X,g)}Q_{(X,g)}
is non-empty as well.
∎

From now on, we fix a finite collection
\{(X_{i},g_{X_{i}})\}_{i\in I}
of closed
R
-manifolds. We shall prove that this collection satisfies (Q). Since our choice of
(X_{i},g_{X_{i}})
’s is completely arbitrary, this would imply Theorem
6.2
by Lemma
6.3
.

Consider the linear subspace
U
of
(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{E_{R}}
generated by the characteristic functions
u_{i}\vcentcolon=I_{(X_{i},g_{X_{i}})}
,
i\in I
. Since there is a linear projection
(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{E_{R}}\to U
, it is enough to construct a quadratic function
\mathfrak{Q}\colon U\to\Omega^{R}_{n}
such that
\mathfrak{Q}(u_{i})=[X_{i},g_{X_{i}}]^{R}
for every
i\in I
to conclude the result.

Outline of the construction

Let
\{(W_{t},g_{W_{t}})\}_{t\in T}
be a collection of closed
R

\{[W_{t}]^{O}\}_{t\in T}
is a basis of
\Omega^{O}_{n}

W_{t}

and with each other. Write
[X_{i}]^{O}=\sum_{t\in T}c_{it}[W_{t}]^{O}

c_{it}\in\mathbb{Z}/2

i\in I
we define a bounding
R

(X^{0}_{i},g_{X^{0}_{i}})
given as a disjoint union:

(X^{0}_{i},g_{X^{0}_{i}})=(X_{i},g_{X_{i}})\cup\bigcup_{c_{it}\neq 0}(W_{t},g_{W_{t}}).

Let
U^{0}\subset(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{E_{R}}
be the subspace generated by the characteristic functions
u^{0}_{i}\vcentcolon=I_{(X^{0}_{i},g_{X^{0}_{i}})}
,
i\in I
.

Consider an extension
\mathbb{Z}/2\hookrightarrow\Omega_{n}^{R}\twoheadrightarrow\Omega_{n}^{O}
given by Corollary

.

(1)

We construct a quadratic function
\widetilde{\varkappa}_{R}\colon U^{0}\to\mathbb{Z}/2
such that

\widetilde{\varkappa}_{R}(u^{0}_{i})=\varkappa_{R}(X^{0}_{i},g_{X^{0}_{i}}),\quad i\in I.

(2)

We construct a linear function
\mathsf{cob}\colon U\to\Omega_{n}^{O}
such that

\mathsf{cob}(u_{i})=[X_{i}]^{O},\quad i\in I.

(3)

We construct a linear map

such that

p(u_{i})=u^{0}_{i},\quad i\in I.

(4)

We construct a quadratic section
s\colon\Omega_{n}^{O}\to\Omega_{n}^{R}
to the natural projection such that

s([X_{i}]^{O})=\sum_{c_{it}\neq 0}[W_{t},g_{W_{t}}]^{R},\quad i\in I.

Then we put
\mathfrak{Q}=\widetilde{\varkappa}_{R}\circ p-s\circ\mathsf{cob}

is quadratic, and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Q}(u_{i})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\varkappa}_{R}(p(u_{i}))-s(\mathsf{cob}(u_{i}))=\widetilde{\varkappa}_{R}(u^{0}_{i})-s([X_{i}]^{O})

\displaystyle=[X^{0}_{i},g_{X^{0}_{i}}]^{R}-\sum_{c_{it}\neq 0}[W_{t},g_{W_{t}}]^{R}=[X_{i},g_{X_{i}}]^{R},\quad i\in I.

By the theorem of Thom, the unoriented cobordism class of a manifold is determined by its Stiefel–Whitney numbers. Hence, it suffices to construct for every

an additive function
\mathsf{cob}_{z}\colon U\to\mathbb{Z}/2

for every

be a cocycle representing

\mathsf{cob}_{z}(u)\vcentcolon=\langle\mathfrak{A}(u,\theta),M_{(1)}\rangle.

Then indeed,

\displaystyle\mathsf{cob}_{z}(u_{i})

\displaystyle=\langle\mathfrak{A}(u_{i},\theta),M_{(1)}\rangle\overset{\text{Lm.\penalty\ \ref{lm:powerful}}}{=}\langle\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{i}(\theta)|_{\Sigma},M_{(1)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{i}(\theta),M_{(1)}\rangle=\langle\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{i}(\varphi(f^{*}(z))),[S^{q}]\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{\text{Lm.\penalty\ \ref{55}\,(1)}}{=}\langle g^{*}_{X_{i}}(f^{*}(z)),[X_{i}]\rangle=\langle\tau^{*}_{X_{i}}(z),[X_{i}]\rangle.

Consider linear functionals

Petrographic and geotechnical evaluation of dolostone in Dhedhertu, Southern Ethiopia: its suitability for dimension stone applications
Authors: Boru Guyo, Addisu Bekele, Bayisa Regassa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPetrological and geotechnical evaluations of the Dhedhertu dolostone were conducted to assess its suitability for use as a dimension stone. The suitability of dolostone for use as a dimension stone was assessed based on its mineralogical, petrographic, physical, and mechanical properties. The evaluated physical properties included porosity, water absorption, bulk density, and specific gravity, whereas the mechanical properties focused on compressive strength. In addition, the color and appearance of the cut and polished sections were examined. The study of dolostone is significant because its geotechnical, mineralogical, and physical characteristics strongly influences its suitability for dimension stone production, and other industrial applications. Understanding these properties can support sustainable resource utilization, guide engineering practices, and provide a foundation for future research in the geological and materials sciences. The examined samples exhibited high compressive strength, indicating their suitability for use as dimension stones in accordance with standard specifications. The compressive strength ranged from 90.7 to 140.1 MPa, with a mean value of 113 MPa. The water absorption values ranged between 0.14 and 0.44%, with a mean of 0.34%, indicating low water absorption. The bulk density ranged from 2.93 to 2.98 g/cm³, with a mean value of 2.96 g/cm³, reflecting high density. The specific gravity varied between 2.87 and 3.13, indicating a high specific gravity, while the porosity ranged from 0.44 to 1.30%, with a mean of 1.01%, indicating low porosity. In addition, the cut and polished sections exhibited attractive light blue and white colors. The results indicate that the dolostone from the Dhedhertu area is suitable for use as a dimension stone.Keywords: Water absorption, Porosity, Uniaxial compressive strength, XRD, DolomiteSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Solid Earth sciences

Introduction

Dimension stones, which are natural rocks carefully quarried and processed to meet specific construction and architectural requirements, play a vital role in both domestic and international markets1. In geological, mining, industrial, and civil engineering projects, dimension stones are mostly used as raw materials2. Geological formation processes and subsequent metamorphic alterations significantly influence the characteristics of building blocks3.Dimension stones are mainly utilized in the construction, monument, and industrial sectors4,5. To ensure accessibility and convenience, quarrying operations must ensure that the extracted stone meets stringent standards, including adequate strength, hardness, workability, porosity, durability, and aesthetic appeal5,6. The glass, ceramics, and cement industries extensively use industrial minerals, and there is a growing global demand for both natural and manufactured rocks and aggregates, particularly for building construction and related applications7. The primary criterion for assessing the suitability of rocks as dimension stones is petrographic analysis, which evaluates their mineral composition, grain size, texture, fabric, and degree of weathering8,9. Dimension stones are blocks with various physical and chemical properties, such as resistance to chemical and physical weathering9. Construction stones, often referred to as cut stones, are utilized for a wide range of applications, including monuments, gravestones, sculptures, and wall and floor tiles in residential, commercial, and institutional settings, where durability, aesthetic appeal, and architectural elegance are highly valued.10.

Building stones can vary in color and texture depending on the mineral composition of the rock11,12. The physical and mineralogical features of rocks have a major impact on their mechanical properties13. For dimensional purposes, carbonate rocks, especially marble, limestone, and dolostone, are used extensively as building materials in most parts of the world. These materials include monuments, gravestones, sculptures, and wall and floor tiles for offices, homes, businesses, and corporate entities9,14,15. The geotechnical properties of rocks utilized in construction and dimension stone applications have been extensively studied by various researchers16,12. Different factors influence these qualities, including weathering, texture, porosity, fissures, petrography, and mineralogy12.

Ethiopia has a long history of using stone as a primary building material, as evidenced by notable structures such as the sandstone Temple of Yeha, the granite-carved Obelisk of Axum, and the rock-hewn Churches of Lalibela, carved from ignimbrite1,17,18. These historic uses highlight the cultural significance of dimension stones, even though these modern applications show the materials’ ongoing relevance in modern architecture and construction1. The petrographic properties of a rock specimen must be precisely researched to assess its overall performance for use as a building stone in a certain environmental state in an area19,20.

The physical, mineralogical, and mechanical rock parameters are important in assessing the durability and initial usage of dimension stones2,21–23. Furthermore, the most crucial parameters for the geotechnical assessment of dimension stones are compressive strength, specific gravity, bulk density, porosity, and water absorption. Dolostone is a calcium- and magnesium-carbonate rock. It can form as a primary precipitate, diagenetic replacement, or hydrothermal metamorphic phase; all that is required is permeability, a fluid flow mechanism, and an appropriate supply of magnesium. Moreover, clay minerals in carbonates can contract and expand, ultimately resulting in swelling and structural cracks. If the amount of deleterious minerals is less than 2%, there is no effect on aggregate strength, quality, or appropriateness, and vice versa13.

Dolostone can be found in a wide range of geological settings, including shallow marine beds, lacustrine environments, early to late burial diagenetic environments, and saltwater reflux zones24. Dolomite is a metastable mineral, which means that early produced crystals may be replaced by later, more stable phases. This replacement process may occur repeatedly during burials and metamorphism. In the previous phase, dolomite completely or partially dissolved to generate each subsequent phase. The ongoing acclimatization that occurs with burial diminishes the capacity of trace elements to reveal depositional circumstances and causes the oxygen isotopic signature of dolostone to reset at increasingly high temperatures25. The transitional phases include limestone (0–9% dolomite), dolomitic (magnesium) limestone (10–49% dolomite), calcitic (calcareous) dolostone (50–89% dolomite), and pure dolostone (90–100% dolomite)26. Dolostone is widely distributed throughout the United States (with an average tariff of 0.71%), Spain (approximately 4,000 tons per year, with a historical maximum of 13,000 tons), Japan (2.5 million metric tons), Canada (14 million tons), Australia, and Hungary (with an average dolostone content of 6.75%)27. Most of Ethiopia’s economic metallic and industrial mineral deposits are found within Precambrian basement rocks28. These deposits include base metals, ornamental and dimension stones such as marble, dolostone, granite, and other colored stones, as well as primary and secondary enriched deposits of gold, platinum, nickel, and tantalum. The dolostone reserves at Hula-Kuni, Ethiopia, are estimated to be approximately 1.43 million tons. Other occurrences are hosted within Mesozoic sedimentary strata as intercalations in calcareous rocks7. In addition to the fact that Ethiopia’s dolostone reserves have only been partially exploited, the mineral is sparsely distributed across the country, particularly in the southern region. The area investigated is characterized by a variety of rock units, the majority of which are metavolcanic in nature. This study aimed to identify the key mechanical and physical properties of dolostone dimension stones, and evaluate the suitability based on their mechanical and petrographic characteristics and assess their potential for use as dimension and building stones. This study aims to address the existing knowledge gap regarding the geotechnical characteristics, mineralogy, and potential suitability of dolostone in this area for use as a dimension stone. This study also contributes to a deeper understanding of the mechanical, mineralogical, and physical properties of dolostone providing a foundation for future studies.

Location of the study area

The study area is situated approximately five hundred and eighty kilometers (580 km) south of Addis Ababa, the capital of Ethiopia, in the Elwaye District of the Borana Zone, Oromia Regional State, southern Ethiopia. Geographically, the study area is located within the Adin dan UTM Zone 37 N. It is bounded by longitudes 37°55’0” to 37°50’0” E and latitudes 5°5′0′′ to 5°15′0′′ N, covering an area of approximately six point five six square kilometers (6.56 km²) (Fig. 1). From there, an all-weather road branches eastward toward Elwaye Town and continues to the village of Dhedhertu, located north of the study area.

Geologic setting

The study area is part of the Pan-African orogenic system of late Precambrian to early Paleozoic age, dominated by metamorphic rocks29,30. The southern and western basement of Ethiopia, largely composed of gneisses and granitoids, has undergone higher-grade metamorphism than the northern Precambrian basement, with granulite facies documented in the southern and southwestern regions31. However, the study area is characterized by a variety of rock units, including metavolcanic and metasedimentary formations. The main units include Quaternary sediments, Miocene basalt, and Precambrian basement rocks (Fig. 2). The structural framework is defined by southwesterly-trending foliations, folds, faults, and lineaments, oriented S–N and W–E. The Precambrian basement in Ethiopia abundance of two contrasting Litho tectonic terranes such as the granite–gneiss terrane, composed of high-grade para- and orthogneisses and deformed granitoids, and the ophiolitic fold-and-thrust belts, the distribution of low-grade mafic–ultramafic and sedimentary assemblages32,33. In the study area, the lithological units span a wide geological age range, from the oldest meta-granite to the youngest Quaternary deposits, and include granulite, amphibolite, schist, dolostone, basalt, and Quaternary soil deposits.

Meta-granite is the most extensive lithology, a medium- to coarse-grain size weakly foliated metamorphic rock dominated by quartz, muscovite, and plagioclase, displaying joints and mineralized veins.

Amphibolite-schist, occurring mainly in the northern and northeastern sectors, is foliated, highly weathered, and composed of amphibole, plagioclase, quartz, and pyroxene minerals.

Basalt units overlie the dolostone unconformably, occurring along ridge crests; they are black, massive, and locally exposed in the northern sector. Finally, Quaternary soil covers low-lying flat areas, predominantly red, especially in the eastern sector, with discontinuous patches elsewhere.

Materials and methods

Sampling

Random sampling techniques were employed during field investigations to collect thirty-two (32) fresh dolostone samples from the study area. Representative subsets of these samples were selected for specific analyses, including six samples for uniaxial compressive strength (UCS) testing, five samples for petrographic examination, three samples for X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis, twelve samples for physical property characterization, and six samples for color measurements. Representative samples were systematically collected from both active quarry sites and natural surface rock outcrops to ensure comprehensive spatial coverage and lithological variability (Fig. 3). The rock samples were selected according to international standards34. Bulk samples were collected for the petrographic characterization and mechanical properties analysis of the dolostone rocks.

Mineralogical identification methods

Petrographic analysis was conducted to characterize the mineralogical composition and microstructural features of the dolostone. Representative samples were prepared as standard 30-µm polished thin sections and examined using a polarizing microscope under both plane-polarized and cross-polarized. In addition, laboratory test mineralogical analysis via X-ray diffraction (XRD), were performed in this study. For this purpose, thin sections were prepared for each rock type, and the relative abundances of the constituent minerals were estimated using point-counting method2.

Petrography analysis

Mineralogical analysis was performed to determine the mineral content of the samples. Representative samples were carefully prepared according to standardized petrographic analysis procedures. In this section five (5) representative samples are analysis for petrography. The samples were dried on a hot plate, and a glass slide was attached to the lapped surface of the slab using an epoxy. The slab was then cut with a thin section saw to reduce its thickness to thirty (30) microns. Next, the section was hand-lapped on a glass plate and polished. In this section, a microscopic description detailing the primary mineral composition and textures of all five samples was carried out at the Geological Survey of Ethiopia.

XRD analysis

The XRD analysis was employed to identify the very fine-grained minerals in the samples, which could not be determined qualitatively or quantitatively using conventional light microscopy. In this study, three (3) representative fresh samples were subjected to X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis.

In the present study, the dolostone samples were initially air-dried and crushed with a mortar. The milled material was passed through a sixty-five micrometers (65 μm) mesh to obtain two hundred and fifty grams (250 g) of the dolostone powder. To reduce contamination, all equipment (mortars and stoves) was washed after each sample was milled and sieved. The powders of the fresh dolostone rock samples were examined using X-ray diffraction. The diffractograms were recorded at a scanning rate of twenty degrees two theta (20°2θ) per minute using Ni-filtered Cu Kα radiation. X-ray diffraction curves were interpreted by comparing the peaks of notable intensities with those of standard minerals. The Reference Intensity Ratio (RIR) method was employed for quantitative mineral analysis. This technique involves scaling all diffraction data to a standard reference material using corundum as the reference. Mineral concentrations were determined by normalizing the diffraction patterns to corundum and analyzing the peak area ratios. Mineral percentages were determined by combining laboratory techniques with analytical methods. Petrographic analysis was conducted using a polarizing microscope for mineral identification, with relative abundances estimated by point counting or by visually assessing mineral proportions in thin sections.

Cutting and polishing for color analyses

The dolostone samples were evaluated for their cutting and polishing characteristics to determine their suitability as dimension stone. Initially, the rocks were trimmed into manageable blocks using a diamond-tipped saw, and the cut surfaces were subsequently polished with progressively finer abrasives to produce smooth, reflective finishes. Observations focused on the ease of cutting, surface finish quality, grain cohesion, and any propensity for fracturing. For color analysis, six (6) representative dolostone samples were selected. Prior to measurement, the samples were cleaned and polished to remove surface impurities and ensure uniform appearance. Color measurements for white and blue specimens were performed, according to38 procedures using a calibrated Konica Minolta CM-700d spectrophotometer. The instrument was standardized using a dedicated white reference tile and a black trap. Measurements were conducted in Specular Component Included (SCI) mode, using CIE Standard Illuminant D65 and a 10° standard observer. Results were recorded in the CIE color space. Test specimens were prepared so that the surfaces were clean, dry, flat, and representative, and multiple readings were taken at various locations on each specimen, and the results were averaged to reduce variability and ensure accurate, reproducible color characterization.

Physical properties

The physical properties of dolostone, including porosity, water absorption, color, bulk density, and specific gravity, were investigated in this study.

Specific gravity results

Measurements of specific gravity were conducted according to ASTM C568-0339 on representative dolostone samples. The results obtained from the laboratory regarding specific gravity were shown in Table 3. Six (6) rock samples collected from different locations in the study area show different specific gravities. The laboratory results for specific gravity in the study area showed a variety of values. The results obtained from samples PH-01, PH-02, PH-03, PH-04, PH-05, and PH-06, were 2.99, 2.91, 2.87, 2.86, 2.81, and 2.98 g/cm³, respectively, showed a high specific gravity, with a mean of 2.90 g/cm³. The standards, these values are within the acceptable range, making the material suitable for dimension stone applications39. The study of dolostone physical properties, including its porosity, water absorption, and bulk density, have a significant effect on its practical applications. The samples result from the study area had low porosity (0.44%-1.34%) and water absorption value (0.33%-0.44%), resulting in minimal empty spaces and reduced moisture intake, improving durability and resistance to weathering. Additionally, the high bulk density (2.93–2.97 g/cm³) indicates a dense, compact rock structure, resulting in increased strength and stability. In order these features indicate that dolostone is ideal for dimension stone, building, and architectural applications since it can sustain load-bearing situations while maintaining structural integrity and long-term reliability.

Porosity

In this study, six (6) rock samples collected from various locations within the study area exhibited varying porosities. The measured porosity values for samples PH-01, PH-02, PH-03, PH-04, PH-05, and PH-06 were 1.03%, 0.98%, 1.3%, 1.23%, 1.12%, and 0.44%, respectively, resulting in a mean porosity of 1.01%. The porosity values of the dolostone samples are below the minimum threshold suggested by relevant standards, classifying them as having very low porosity. Furthermore, the mean porosity of the dolostone samples falls below the maximum allowable limit, indicating relatively low porosity overall. Consequently, both the individual and mean porosity values of the dolostone in the Dhedhertu area reflect low effective porosity, which complies with ASTM C568-0339 standards for suitability as dimension stone.

Mechanical laboratory analysis results

Uniaxial compressive strength (UCS)

The laboratory results of the uniaxial compressive strength (UCS) are summarized in Table 5. Six (6) representative dolostone samples collected from different locations in the study area exhibited similar compressive strengths, ranging from 90.7 MPa to 140.1 MPa, with an average of 113 MPa. The high quality of the dolostone allows it to be cut, polished, and utilized in a wide range of architectural and decorative applications, including floor tiles, cladding panels, windowsills, stair treads, columns, and other ornamental stone products, highlighting its economic and aesthetic value.

Discussion

Comparison of Dhedhertu dolostone with another limestone

The physical and mechanical properties of Dhedhertu dolostone were compared with carbonate rocks (limestone) from the Pila Spi Formation (Eocene) as investigated9.Petrographic analysis revealed that the Pila Spi limestone is predominantly homogeneous, consisting mainly of impure dolomitic limestone and dolomite, whereas the Dhedhertu dolostone is also homogeneous and primarily composed of dolomite. Both rock types meet the ASTM C568-0339 specifications for dimension stone, demonstrating favorable properties such as high bulk density, low porosity, low water absorption, and high compressive strength9.

Composition The mineralogical composition of the Pila Spi Limestone Formation is predominantly dolomite and calcite9. In comparison, the Dhedhertu dolostone contains a higher proportion of these minerals, which further enhances its suitability for high-quality dimension stone applications.

Specific Gravity Laboratory analysis shows that Dhedhertu dolostone has an average specific gravity of 2.96 g/cm³, which aligns with ASTM C97-1835 standards for dimension stone, confirming its suitability for structural and decorative applications. Overall, the Dhedhertu dolostone exhibits superior mechanical and physical properties compared to the Pila Spi limestone, highlighting its potential as a high-quality dimension stone.

Porosity and Water Absorption Porosity for Pila Spi limestone ranges from 7.41% to 7.74%, whereas Dhedhertu dolostone has a significantly lower average porosity of 1.01%9. Water absorption is also lower for dolostone (< 0.75%) compared to 2.91–3.44% for Pila Spi limestone, making the Dhedhertu dolostone more suitable for dimension stone applications.

Bulk Density The Pila Spi limestone has a bulk density of 2.35–2.58 g/cm³, whereas the Dhedhertu dolostone has a higher average bulk density of 2.9 g/cm³, indicating greater compactness and durability9 for suitable dimension stone.

Uniaxial compressive Strength The average compressive strength of Pila Spi limestone ranges from 71.2 to 83.7 MPa9 whereas carbonated rocks used as dimension stone generally range from 17.9 to 193.0 MPa40.

The Dhedhertu dolostone exhibits higher compressive strength, ranging from 90.7 to 140.1 MPa, with a mean of 113 MPa, confirming its suitability for high-quality dimension stone applications as shown in Fig. 12.

Dimensional stone quality of dolostone

Mineralogy, mechanical, and physical properties are key controlling factors in the dimension stone selection. Field observation and mineralogical (laboratory analysis) conducted in the Dhedhertu area revealed that the dolostone in the area was stronger based on the UCS. Furthermore, the compressive strength of dolostone rock in the research area is high in strength with a minimum UCS of 90.7 MPa and a maximum of 140.1 MPa. The summary of the physical and mechanical properties indicates that all the sampled outcrops are suitable for use as dimension stones. The sampled rocks exhibit satisfactory compressive strength and bulk density. Furthermore, the porosity and water absorption are very low according to standard specifications from the International Society of Rock Mechanics34 as indicated (Fig. 13). Therefore, based on the summary of the mineralogical, physical, and mechanical properties compared with the international standard specifications of Dhedhertu dolostone rocks have excellent quality for use as dimension stones (Tables 8 and 9).

Conclusions

Dimensional stone encompasses a wide range of natural materials with variable intrinsic properties, where mineralogical, physical, and mechanical characteristics are critical for material selection. Based on field observations and laboratory analyses conducted in the study area, the following conclusions are drawn:

Petrographic thin-section analysis and X-ray diffraction (XRD) results indicate that the Dhedhertu dolostone is predominantly dolomitic, with an average composition of approximately 71% dolomite and 25% calcite, along with minor muscovite and trace opaque minerals.

The mineralogical analyses suggest that the dolostone is largely composed of well-crystallized dolomite with minor impurities, indicating a relatively uniform mineralogical fabric that may be suitable for engineering applications.

Textural analysis shows that the dolostone exhibits hypidioblastic to xenoblastic fabrics, characterized by coarse dolomite and calcite crystals within a medium- to fine-grained matrix. The polished samples display a relatively uniform white coloration, which may enhance its aesthetic suitability for dimension stone use.

The polished samples display a relatively uniform white coloration, which may enhance its aesthetic suitability for dimension stone use.

The measured physical and mechanical properties, including relatively high bulk density, low porosity and water absorption, and moderate to high compressive strength, generally fall within indicated ranges for dimension stone applications. These results suggest that the Dhedhertu dolostone has potential for use as a durable construction material.

This study is subject to limitations in both spatial coverage and analytical scope with respect to dolostone. Although X-ray diffraction (XRD), petrographic analysis, and selected physical and mechanical tests were conducted, several relevant analyses could not be performed due to time and budget constraints. These include geochemical characterization, soundness testing, abrasion resistance, ultrasonic pulse velocity (P-wave) measurements, and pH analysis. In addition, the study does not include long-term durability and weathering assessments. The evaluation is based primarily on laboratory, physical and mechanical tests under controlled conditions, which may not fully represent the in-situ behavior of dolostone. Processes such as freeze–thaw cycling, chemical weathering, and long-term exposure to moisture and temperature variations were not investigated. Future research should incorporate durability testing under simulated environmental conditions, expand the spatial coverage, and include the additional omitted analyses to enable a more comprehensive evaluation of dolostone quality and to better assess its suitability for dimension stone and other engineering and construction applications.

Programmed cell death ligand 2 (PD-L2) expression is inversely correlated with human papillomavirus status in oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma (OPSCC) tumors
Authors: Jeff J. Johnson, Wanrui Wang, Zonggao Shi, Annie Roble, Brian Crowley, Madison McFarland, Jing Yang, Daniel L. Miller, Mark W. Lingen, M. Sharon Stack
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The incidence of oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma (OPSCC) associated with infection by the human papillomavirus (HPV) continues to increase and 71% of OPSCC cases in the United States are HPV+. We have previously performed microRNA profiling of HPV+ vs HPV− OPSCC and identified miR-106b as significantly elevated in HPV + cases (Miller et al., 2015; Am J Path 185:679-92 [1]). MicroRNA target prediction identified the gene PDCD1LG2 as a highly ranked target of miR-106b. This gene corresponds to the protein programmed cell death ligand 2 (PD-L2), a high affinity ligand for the programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) receptor on T-cells. The objective of this study was to evaluate PD-L2 expression in a panel of HPV+ and HPV− OPSCC tumors. Using tissues from two distinct patient cohorts, results show significantly lower PD-L2 protein expression levels in HPV+ tumors. These data suggest that future research on regulation of PD-L2 expression in HPV+ OPSCC is warranted.

Introduction

Oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinomas (OPSCC) occur at anatomic locations ranging from the junction of the hard and soft palate to the plane of the hyoid bone, comprising the tonsils, base of the tongue, palatine arch, and uvula [1,2]. Diagnoses of OPSCC associated with human papillomavirus (HPV) infection (HPV+ OPSCC) have increased sharply over the past two decades, and 71% of U.S. OPSCC cases can be attributed to HPV infection [2-4]. Indeed, OPSCC has surpassed cervical cancer as the most frequently diagnosed HPV-associated cancer in the U.S. [5]. HPV+ and HPV− OPSCC are defined clinically as distinct entities with unique molecular profiles and outcomes, with HPV+ OPSCC exhibiting a more favorable prognosis [1, 2,6]. Most OPSCC patients present at diagnosis with lymph node metastases and clinical prognosis is based on tumor size, nodal status, and degree of differentiation and invasion. HPV status, determined by p16 overexpression, has recently been added to clinical staging guidelines. Treatment involves surgical resection combined with radiotherapy or chemoradiotherapy and 10-25% of patients with HPV+ OPSCC develop recurrent disease within 5 years of treatment [2].

Cancer immunotherapy has shown tremendous promise as a treatment avenue for a subset of cancer patients. There has been particular focus on therapies targeting the programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) receptor, expressed constitutively on the surface of T-cells, that modulates T-cell activity through its interaction with the ligands programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) and programmed death-ligand 2 (PD-L2) [7]. Engagement of PD-1 on a T-cell by one of its ligands on the surface of a tumor cell or an antigen presenting cell abrogates T-cell receptor-mediated proliferation [8]. Thus, a major focus of immunotherapy research has been on blocking the PD-1/PD-L1 axis using drugs such as pembrolizumab (Keytruda®), an anti-PD-1-targeted immunotherapy. In OPSCC, immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapies show efficacy as both first-line therapy and in the setting of recurrent disease and the Food and Drug Administration recently (12 June 2025) approved neoadjuvant pembrolizumab for resectable locally advanced head and nexk squamous cell carcinoma [7-11].

Using PCR-based microRNA profiling combined with bioinformatic mining of The Cancer Genome Atlas, we have previously identified a microRNA panel associated with oncogenic HPV infection in OPSCC [6]. Among the most significantly differentially expressed microRNAs observed in three distinct HPV+ patient datasets was miR-106b. Using the microRNA target prediction database (miRDB), to identify proteins that may be modulated by miR-106b expression [12], a highly ranked target (target score 99) was the gene PDCD1LG2, corresponding to the protein PD-L2. Based on this finding, the objective of the current study was to use immunohistochemical staining of human OPSCC to test the hypothesis that HPV+ OPSCC tumors would exhibit reduced expression of PD-L2 relative to HPV− tumors.

Materials and methods

An initial cohort of OPSCC tissue sections (n = 23) was obtained from the University of Missouri (Columbia, MO) surgical pathology archives (2006 to 2011) with Institutional Review Board approval and represents histologically confirmed tonsillar or base of the tongue SCC. Tissues were previously stained for p16 as a surrogate marker for HPV status using the CINtec p16 Histology Kit E6H4 clone (Roche Diagnostics) [6]. Staining was evaluated using a binary system with tissues exhibiting >70% of tumor cell-specific nuclear and cytoplasmic staining designated as positive. This represents the current evidence-based standard for routine HPV assessment from the College of American Pathologists [13]. Based on these results, 15 sections represented HPV+ and 8 represented HPV− disease and patient data are reported in Ref. [6]. The second cohort was in the form of an OPSCC tissue microarray (TMA) obtained from the University of Chicago surgical pathology archives with Institutional Review Board approval and represents histologically confirmed OPSCC. The HPV status for each case was previously determined by DNA in situ hybridization for high-risk HPV [14]. Both p16 immunohistochemistry and HPV in situ hybridization are guideline-supported approaches and are not expected to materially affect data interpretation [13]. The TMA contained 53 unique cores of HPV+ and 57 unique cores of HPV− OPSCC. Cores were excluded if the were not >50% intact. Of these, 48 cores of HPV+ and 55 cores of HPV− were designated as intact and evaluable and patient data are reported in Ref. [14]. Tissue sections and cores were immunostained using anti-PD-L2 antibody MAB 1224 (R&D Systems, 1:400 dilution) which is specific for PD-L2 and demonstrated not to cross react with related immune checkpoint proteins including B7-1, B7-2, B7-H1 (PD-L1), B7-H2, and B7-H3. The secondary antibody was goat-anti-mouse IgG-HRP (1:4000 dilution) with DAB detection. An Aperio ScanScope CS was used for whole slide capture and Aperio ImageScope software was implemented for quantification of immunohistochemical staining of tumor tissue for PD-L2. Tissues were evaluated for the overall prevalence of PD-L2 including tumor and stromal (immune) cells and analyzed blinded to HPV status. Statistical analyses of resulting H-scores was performed using SigmaPlot version 14.0.

Results

OPSCC tumors of HPV+ or HPV− status were evaluated by immunohistochemical staining for expression of PD-L2. Results from the initial cohort show an inverse relationship between p16 and PD-L2 staining. In sections with high p16 expression, characteristic of HPV + disease, PD-L2 staining was low or absent (Fig. 1A-D). In contrast, robust PD-L2 staining was observed in sections with low levels of p16 expression, characteristic of HPV− disease (Fig. 1E-H). Stained sections were quantified using Aperio ImageScope software, generating H-scores to provide a semi-quantitative analysis of PD-L2 protein expression. The average H-score for HPV+ tumors was 64.9 ± 22.1 (mean ± SEM), while an average score of 199.8 ± 21.3 was obtained for HPV− tumors (p = 0.004), indicating significantly higher PD-L2 protein levels in HPV− tumors (Fig. 1I). An unusual case was observed that contained both HPV+ and HPV− lesions present in the same tumor section. In this case, HPV+ regions exhibited low PD-L2 expression (Fig. 2A and B) while HPV− regions showed strong PD-L2 immunoreactivity (Fig. 2C and D). To confirm the results from staining of the initial cohort, a TMA containing 110 additional cores was evaluated (Fig. 3A). The second cohort used in situ hybridization for high-risk HPV to stratify tumors. Similar results were obtained (Fig. 3B) with an average H-score of 128.9 ± 15.4 for HPV+ tumors and 223.2 ± 22.6 for HPV− tumors (p = 0.001). To further validate these results, publicly available miRNA and mRNA expression data were used to evaluate the relationship between has-miR-106b-5p and PDCD1LG2 expression in HPV-positive and HPV-negative patient cohorts. In the HPV-positive subgroup, miR-106b and PDCD1LG2 showed an inverse Pearson correlation, with higher miR-106b expression tending to associate with lower PDCD1LG2 expression (r = −0.51, one-sided p = 0.053, n = 11). This trend was not observed in the HPV-negative subgroup (Suppl. Fig. 1).

Discussion

We have previously demonstrated high expression of miR-106b in HPV+ OPSCC tumors relative to HPV− disease [6]. This study used p16 positivity as a surrogate for HPV + disease. As a predicted target of miR-106b is the immune checkpoint molecule PD-L2, the current study was undertaken to evaluate PD-L2 levels in HPV+ vs HPV− OPSCC. Results using archival tissues from two distinct patient populations show significantly lower levels of PD-L2 protein immunostaining in tumors from patients with p16 positive (HPV surrogate marker, cohort 1) and HPV+ (in situ hybridization, cohort 2) disease. Moreover bioinformatics analysis of miR and mRNA expression support these findings.

In recent years, ICI therapies blocking the PD-1/PD-L1/PD-L2 immune inactivation response have revolutionized treatment for a subset of cancer patients, including those with OPSCC. PD-1 was originally identified as an apoptosis-associated molecule expressed on activated CD4 and CD8 T cells, B cells, natural killer (NK) cells, dendritic cells (DC) and monocytes [15,16]. High PD-1 expression in the tumor microenvironment has been shown to be indicative of a reduced immune response in OSCC when PD-L1 is expressed by tumor cells or other immune cells [17,18]. Expression of PD-1/PD-L1/PD-L2 can also be affected by treatment, as both chemotherapy and radiotherapy have been shown to upregulate expression of PD-L1 and/or PD-L2 in tumor cells [19,20]. In addition to inactivating tumor-specific CD8+ T-cells, PD-1 ligation by tumor-associated PD-L1 or PD-L2 leads to the conversion of naïve T-cells to T regulatory cells which are strong inhibitors of the immune response [21].

Most of immunotherapy research has focused on PD-1 and PD-L1 and a highly clinically successful drug has been pembrolizumab (Keytruda®), an anti-PD-1-targeted ICI therapy. Administration of antibodies to PD-1 block immune inhibition by both PD-1 ligands expressed on the surface of tumor cells or myeloid cells. While expression of PD-L2, a higher affinity ligand for PD-1 relative to PD-L1, has not been as extensively studied, PD-L2 protein expression in head and neck SCC was associated with clinical response to pembrolizumab (Keytruda®) independent of PD-L1 expression [22]. Moreover, subsets of PD-L1-positive patients show poor responses to PD-1-targeted therapies, suggesting that evaluation of PD-L2 may be a relevant predictor of treatment response [22]. Strong PD-L2 expression has previously been reported in oral cancers; however these patients were not stratified by HPV status [23-25]. For instance, in locally advanced HNSCC, PD-L2 expression was 85.4% while PD-L1 was expressed in 83.6% of cases and positivity for both ligands were highest in the oral cavity [25]. An additional study found PD-L2 expression in 61% of OPSCC patients with only 25% of patient tumors expressing PD-L1 [26]. While HPV status was not a focus of this report, the authors indicated that tumors lacking both HPV (p16-negative) and PD-L2 had worse outcomes [26]. Recent data show that pembrolizumab is most beneficial in HPV + HNSCC, especially with high PD-L1 expression [8]. This is supported by a meta-analysis of 11 clinical trials which showed that immunotherapy improved response rate and overall survival for HNSCC patients, with a larger effect in patients with HPV− and PD-L1-positive tumors; however PD-L2 was not evaluated [27]. It should be noted that the clinical utility of PD-L2 expression for treatment selection or patient outcomes analysis was not evaluated in the current study.

An additional limitation of the current correlative study is the inability to mechanistically link high miR-106b expression in HPV+ OPSCC tumors with decreased PD-L2 levels. MiR-106b is significantly overexpressed in HPV+ OPSCC relative to HPV− tumors [6] and microRNA target prediction identifies PD-L2 as a potential target [12]. Moreover, bioinformatics analysis of publicly available data provide supportive evidence for an inverse association between miR-106b and PDCD1LG2 in HPV-positive HNSCC, albeit in a limited sample size. Furthermore, it is interesting to note that a recent study has reported DNA promoter methylation and reduced transcriptional activity of both PL-L1 and PD-L2 in HNSCC tumors associated with HPV infection [28]. While protein expression was not assessed, methylation was inversely correlated with PD-L1 and PD-L2 mRNA, providing an alternative potential mechanism for the low levels of PD-L2 protein observed in HPV+ cases in the current study. Based on these observations, future research on regulation of PD-L2 expression in HPV+ OPSCC and the role of miR-106b in this process is warranted.

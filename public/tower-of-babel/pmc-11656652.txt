Immune-related adverse events correlate with the clinical efficacy in advanced Non-Small-Cell Lung Cancer patients treated with PD-1 inhibitors combination therapy
Authors: Xiaowan Xie, Yuhao Li, Qiaomei Lv, Wei Wang, Wenbo Ding, Yuanyuan Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe potential of immune-related adverse events (irAEs) in predicting the efficacy of PD-1 inhibitors in advanced non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) has rarely been assessed. This study investigated the associations between irAEs and the clinical efficacy of PD-1 inhibitors combination therapy in patients with advanced NSCLC.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted of 73 patients with advanced NSCLC receiving PD-1 inhibitors combination therapy from January 2022 and July 2023. Patients were divided into two cohorts: patients with irAEs and patients without irAEs. We conducted an analysis to investigate the impact of irAEs on these different clinical outcomes.ResultsThere were no significant differences observed in clinical characteristics between the two cohorts, except for smoking status (P = 0.011).The cohort with irAEs exhibited a higher objective response rate (ORR) and disease control rate (DCR) compared to the cohort without irAEs (ORR: 32.5% vs 12.1%, P = 0.040; DCR: 80.0% vs 48.5%, P = 0.010).Moreover, the median progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) were significantly better in the cohort with irAEs compared to the cohort without irAEs (PFS: 12.4 months vs 6.8 months, P = 0.009; OS: not reached vs 18.3 months, P = 0.024). Additionally, the multivariate COX regression analysis revealed that mild irAEs (PFS: HR = 0.386, 95% CI: 0.199–0.748, P = 0.005; OS: HR = 0.300, 95% CI: 0.105–0.855, P = 0.024) and single-system irAEs (PFS: HR = 0.401, 95% CI: 0.208–0.772, P = 0.006; OS: HR = 0.264, 95% CI: 0.090–0.776, P = 0.015) were identified as independent prognostic factors for both PFS and OS.ConclusionsIrAEs, especially thyroid irAEs, as well as mild or single-system irAEs, may serve as predictors of improved efficacy in advanced NSCLC patients receiving PD-1 inhibitors combination therapy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-024-13220-7.

Introduction

Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide, with non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) being the most commonly reported variant, accounting for approximately 85% of cases [1]. The development of precision medicine has introduced immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) as novel anticancer drugs in the treatment of NSCLC, offering unique advantages and long-term clinical benefits for patients [2–5]. In particular, inhibitors targeting the programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) or programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) have emerged as crucial therapeutic agents in various types of tumors, opening a new chapter in tumor immunotherapy [6–8].

Unlike traditional treatment methods that directly target tumor cells or enhance the body’s immunity, this treatment method restricts or eliminates tumor cells by activating the body’s own normal anti-tumor immune mechanism [9], and is anticipated to assist the body in establishing a long-lasting anti-tumor immune response following treatment [10, 11]. However, the use of ICIs often results in life-threatening immune-related adverse events (irAEs) [12]. These irAEs, which resemble autoimmune disorders, are a distinct toxicity induced by immunotherapy [13]. While the precise pathophysiological mechanisms driving irAEs are not fully understood, several clinical studies have established a correlation between these adverse events and the clinical outcomes of ICIs [14–16]. Patients who discontinue ICI treatment due to irAEs have continued to experience treatment benefits, suggesting a potentially mechanistic association between irAEs and ICI efficacy, even with relatively short exposure [17]. Nevertheless, whether the occurrence of irAEs is associated with the clinical efficacy in NSCLC remains an ongoing area of research.

Therefore, this retrospective cohort study aims to explore the associations between irAEs and the clinical efficacy of PD-1 inhibitors combination therapy in real-world settings among patients with advanced NSCLC.

Materials and methods

Patients selection

All NSCLC patients consecutively admitted to the inpatient unit of our hospital and received PD-1 inhibitors combination therapy between January 2022 and July 2023 were screened. Patients who met any of the following criteria were excluded: (1) no pathological report confirming NSCLC; (2) incomplete medical records; (3) received less than two cycles of PD-1 inhibitors combination therapy; (4) received PD-1 inhibitor combination therapy for perioperative treatment. Ultimately, a total of 73 NSCLC patients were screened out. The specific screening process is presented in Fig. 1. All patients were then followed up until January 2024. This study adhered to the ethical principles of the 1964 Helsinki Declaration and received approval from the institutional review board (IRB) for all aspects of the study, waiving the requirement for written informed consent.Fig. 1Flow diagram for selection of cohorts

Flow diagram for selection of cohorts

Measures

We conducted a retrospective review of electronic medical records to collect data on eligible patients, including patient characteristics, metastatic locations, response to immunotherapy, and adverse events. Patients received a fixed dose of 200mg of karelizumab, tirelizumab, xindilizumab, and penpulimab.

intravenously every 3 weeks. The choice of PD-1 inhibitors for treatment lacks strict guidelines primarily due to drug accessibility and pricing. Each patient received more than two cycles of PD-1 inhibitor combination therapy. Additionally, current research has not identified a superior PD-1 inhibitor over others in terms of efficacy.﻿

As per the guidelines outlined by the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN), patients with NLCLC experiencing irAEs necessitate a multidisciplinary approach involving oncology, pharmacy, endocrinology, dermatology, and pulmonology specialists. This team collectively determines whether patients should discontinue PD-1 inhibitors and receive appropriate management, such as observation, levothyroxine supplementation, antiallergic medications, or local/systemic hormone therapy. Treatment response was assessed using computed tomography (CT), with chest CT, abdominal CT, and head CT performed every two or three treatment cycles. Response to treatment was evaluated based on the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.1 (RECIST 1.1), with therapeutic responses classified as complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), or progressive disease (PD) from the first day of treatment until progression, death, or the last follow-up. Progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as the time from the first cycle of PD-1 inhibitor combination therapy to death or disease progression, whichever occurred first. Additionally, overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from the first cycle of PD-1 inhibitor combination treatment to death from any cause. Adverse events related to irAEs were assessed and graded for severity according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events 5.0 (CTCAE 5.0). Subsequently, patients were categorized into irAEs and non-irAEs cohorts based on the presence or absence of irAEs.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables with a normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), otherwise as median (interquartile range). Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. Differences between two subcohorts of non-normally distributed quantitative variables were assessed using the Mann–Whitney U-test. Fisher’s exact test or Chi-square test was used for categorical variables. The Kaplan–Meier method was employed to OS and PFS; subcohorts were compared using the log-rank test for the total number of patients. Additionally, univariate and multivariate COX regression analyses were conducted to explore whether irAEs were independent prognostic factors for OS and PFS in NSCLC patients.

All statistical procedures were performed using SPSS version 27.0 (IBM Software, Armonk, NY,USA) and GraphPad Prism version 5.00 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA).A two-sided P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics of patients with advanced NSCLC

From January 2022 to July 2023, a total of 73 patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who received combination therapy with PD-1 inhibitors were enrolled. After applying strict inclusion and exclusion criteria, a retrospective cohort study was conducted on these 73 patients. Among them, 40 patients were included in the cohort with irAEs, while 33 patients were enrolled in the cohort without irAEs. The selection process is illustrated in Fig. 1.

The basic characteristics of the patients in Table 1. In two cohorts, the mean age was 65.2 and 64.1 years, and the mean body mass index (BMI) was 22.7and 23.0 kg/m2. The majorities of patients were male, constituting 32 patients (80.0%) in the cohort with irAEs and 25 patients (75.8%) in the cohort without irAEs. The majority of advanced NSCLC patients in both cohorts had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG PS) score of 1 (72.5% vs. 60.6%), stage IV disease (90.0% vs. 84.8%), adenocarcinoma histology (50.0% vs. 57.6%), brain metastases (5.0% vs. 12.1%), liver metastases (20.0% vs. 12.1%), lymph node metastases (42.5% vs. 30.3%), and PD-1 inhibitors in combination with chemotherapy (72.5% vs. 60.6%). None of the patients exhibited active symptoms of autoimmune disease at the onset of combination treatment. Except for smoking status (P = 0.011), there were no statistically significant differences in most characteristics between the two cohorts (P > 0.05). Most patients with multi-system irAEs had 2 irAEs(88.9%). The most common multi-system irAE patterns were thyroiditis dermatitis(33.3%),pneumonitis thyroiditis(22.2%) and dermatitis pneumonitis(22.2%)(Supplementary Fig. 1B). Correlation of irAEs with PFS and OS

To investigate whether differences in clinical characteristics of advanced NSCLC patients influenced the outcomes of this study, we conducted univariate COX regression analysis. The results revealed a significant association between the occurrence of irAEs and extended PFS (HR 0.466, 95% CI: 0.261–0.834, p = 0.010) as well as OS (HR 0.393, 95% CI: 0.159–0.967, p = 0.042). Upon further analysis of irAEs subtypes, it was found that patients with mild irAEs were associated with prolonged PFS(HR 0.442, 95% CI: 0.241–0.808, p = 0.008) and OS(HR 0.375, 95% CI: 0.144–0.980, p = 0.045) in univariate analyses. Additionally, patients with single-system irAEs also exhibited prolonged PFS (HR 0.488, 95% CI: 0.266–0.893, p = 0.020) and OS (HR 0.307, 95% CI: 0.111–0.853, p = 0.024) in univariate analyses (Table 5). Similarly, the occurrence of single-system irAEs was also identified as an independent protective factor for prolonged PFS (HR 0.401, 95% CI: 0.208–0.772, p = 0.006) and OS (HR 0.264, 95% CI: 0.090–0.776, p = 0.015)(Table 6). Discussion

PD-1 inhibitors are a promising class of anti-tumor drugs that have achieved numerous breakthroughs in the treatment of cancers like lung cancer, colorectal cancer, and malignant melanoma. However, ICI therapy is frequently complicated by irAEs. Currently, it remains controversial as to whether the occurrence of irAEs is related to ICI treatment. Central to our research is a focused exploration within the domain of advanced NSCLC patients undergoing PD-1 inhibitor combination therapy, recognizing the potential nuances and distinct responses that may characterize this specific patient population compared to other cancer types or therapeutic approaches. Within this context, our study provides a more comprehensive and extensive analysis of the relationship between irAEs and the treatment efficacy of ICIs.

The results of this study revealed that among NSCLC patients receiving combination therapy with PD-1 inhibitors, the cohort experiencing irAEs demonstrated a higher ORR and DCR (ORR: 32.5% vs. 12.1%, P = 0.040; DCR: 80.0% vs. 48.5%, P = 0.010), as well as longer OS (median OS: not reached vs 18.3 months, P < 0.024) and PFS (median PFS: 12.4 vs 6.8 months, P = 0.009) compared to the cohort without irAEs. These findings are consistent with previous studies which have indicated an association between the development of irAEs and the clinical benefit of ICIs [5, 18–20].Currently, the underlying mechanism behind the relationship between irAEs and survival benefits is not fully understood. However, the most promising hypotheses for this phenomenon revolve around common antigens shared between the tumor and healthy tissue [21]. Therefore, the development of irAEs suggests a robust immune response against both tumor and healthy tissues, predicting a more favorable therapeutic response. The results suggest that irAEs may serve as predictive factors for sustained efficacy in NSCLC. By illuminating these dynamic associations and proposing innovative hypotheses, our study signifies a significant advancement in understanding the interplay between irAEs and treatment outcomes in NSCLC patients undergoing PD-1 inhibitor combination therapy. These findings hold promise for personalized treatment strategies and the optimization of clinical management approaches in the era of immunotherapy.

Our analysis embarked on a nuanced exploration by categorizing the observed irAEs based on their specific types, unveiling thyroid dysfunction as the predominant manifestation In cases of thyroiditis, patients typically experience a rapidly progressive thyrotoxic state, followed by a sudden decline in free thyroxine (T4) leading to hypothyroidism [22]. Some retrospective studies have found that thyroid dysfunction is associated with longer PFS and OS [23–25].In our study, we observed that patients experiencing thyroid irAEs had a better ORR, while PFS and OS were not compromised. The second most common adverse reaction observed was skin-related events, which previous reports have linked to the clinical efficacy of anti-PD-1 inhibitors therapy in NSCLC patients [26–29]. However, in our retrospective study, the occurrence of skin reactions did not appear to be associated with the clinical benefit of immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy. By navigating through these intricacies and challenging conventional wisdom, our study offers a fresh perspective on the divergent impacts of distinct irAE profiles on treatment outcomes in NSCLC.

In terms of subgroup analyses based on irAE grades, mild irAEs (grade 1–2) showed significant prognostic value, consistent with findings from other studies [19, 30, 31].However, patients with severe irAEs did not exhibit improved clinical outcomes. At present, the correlation between severe irAEs and clinical benefit is less clear. This could be attributed to the fact that severe irAEs often result in interruption or permanent discontinuation of ICI treatment, making it difficult to restart. Additionally, severe irAEs frequently necessitate the discontinuation of ICI treatment, which may impact the anti-tumor effects of PD-1 inhibitors. As a result, there is insufficient sample size to determine a correlation between severe irAEs and clinical outcomes.

The impact of irAEs amount on the survival of NSCLC patients is a matter of debate. Regarding the subgroup analyses based on irAEs amount, patients with single-system irAEs demonstrated the best OS and PFS. This contradicts the results of earlier studies [32, 33]. Currently,there is insufficient research on the correlation between the number of irAEs and efficacy, and the incidence of multi-system irAEs is fairly low. Additional research is required to validate this further.

Limitations

There are several limitations that should be acknowledged in our study. Firstly, it is essential to recognize that our study design is retrospective. Whereas our findings demonstrate a significant association between irAEs and outcomes, bias for irAEs may be unavoidable. Secondly, the sample size was small. Lastly, it is critical to recognize that our study was conduced at a single center. Larger prospective studies are required in the future to confirm our findings.

Conclusions

Our retrospective observations indicated that the occurrence of irAEs, especially thyroid irAEs, as well as mild or single-system irAEs, may serve as predictors of improved efficacy in advanced NSCLC patients receiving PD-1 inhibitors combination therapy. Further studies are needed to confirm our findings.

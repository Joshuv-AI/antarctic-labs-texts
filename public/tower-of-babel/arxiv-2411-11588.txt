On causality and its violation in f ⁡ ( R , L m , T ) \displaystyle f(R,L_{m},T) gravity
J. S. Gonçalves, A. F. Santos

Abstract
In this paper, f ⁡ ( R , L m , T ) \displaystyle f(R,L_{m},T) gravity is considered. It is a generalization of the theories f ⁡ ( R , T ) \displaystyle f(R,T) and f ⁡ ( R , L m ) \displaystyle f(R,L_{m}) . This modified theory of gravity exhibits strong geometry-matter coupling. The problem of causality and its violation is verified in this model. Such analysis is carried out using Gödel-type solutions considering different types of matter. It is shown that this model allows both causal and non-causal solutions. These solutions depend directly on the content of matter present in the universe. For the non-causal solution, a critical radius is calculated, beyond which causality is violated. Taking different matter contents, an infinite critical radius emerges that leads to a causal solution. To obtain a causal solution, a natural relationship arises between the parameters of the theory.

To explain this late cosmic acceleration, two proposals have been explored: (i) add new components of matter and energy to the theory of general relativity, namely, dark constituents of the universe; (ii) modify or generalize the Einstein-Hilbert action considering that gravitational action is an arbitrary function that may depend on the geometry and/or of the matter. In this work, the theory under investigation is
\displaystyle f(R,L_{m},T)

[
6

The
\displaystyle f(R,L_{m},T)
gravity is a class of modified gravity theories developed to explain the late-time accelerated expansion of the universe, often referred to as the ”dark coupling approach”. Two additional approaches exist: (i) the dark components model, which extends Einstein’s field equations by incorporating two extra terms into the total energy-momentum tensor of the universe, representing dark energy and dark matter separately; and (ii) the dark gravity framework, which offers a purely geometric interpretation of gravitational phenomena. The key idea behind this framework, i.e. dark coupling approach, is to replace the standard Hilbert-Einstein Lagrangian density - which treats the curvature and matter contributions separately and additively - with a more general algebraic formulation that couples these components more intricately. In this model, the Lagrangian is an arbitrary function of the Ricci scalar (
\displaystyle R
), the matter Lagrangian (
\displaystyle L_{m}
), and the trace of the energy-momentum tensor (
\displaystyle T
). This approach naturally introduces a non-minimal interaction between matter and spacetime geometry, allowing for various forms of coupling, such as between the Ricci scalar and the trace of the energy-momentum tensor.

In this context,
\displaystyle f(R,L_{m},T)
gravity unifies two distinct modified gravity models:
\displaystyle f(R,T)
and
\displaystyle f(R,L_{m})
. Moreover, it introduces a non-minimal coupling of matter to geometry through both the matter Lagrangian and the trace of the energy-momentum tensor. A significant feature of this theory is that the resulting modifications apply non-trivially to all types of matter. As limiting cases this theory recovers the
\displaystyle f(R)
,
\displaystyle f(R,L_{m})
and
\displaystyle f(R,T)
gravity theories. It is important to emphasize that all alternative theories proposed as gravitational theory must be intensively tested. A classical test consists of checking whether all exact solutions of general relativity are contained in this new model. Here causality and its violation are investigated.

To address the challenge of causality within the domain of
\displaystyle f(R,L_{m},T)
gravity, a comprehensive investigation is performed using the Gödel-type solution
[
7
]
. This metric is an extension of Kurt Gödel solution, proposed in 1949
[
8
]
. It represents an exact solution that incorporates rotating matter within the domain of general relativity. The main characteristic of this metric is its ability to accommodate Closed Timelike Curves (CTCs). CTCs are curves that lead to causality violation. A traveler on this curve can return to their past, at least theoretically. However, it is important to note that this is a global point of view since locally the general relativity satisfies the same conditions as the special theory of relativity, that is, the causality is preserved. On the other hand, CTCs and causality violation are not exclusive to Gödel-type solutions, this is a phenomenon whose presence extends beyond the limits of the Gödel solution. These CTCs manifest in a variety of cosmological models, such as the Kerr black hole

II
\displaystyle f(R,L_{m},T)
Gravity

In this section, the
\displaystyle f(R,L_{m},T)
theory is presented and its field equations are obtained. The action that describes this gravitational theory is defined as

S=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int f(R,L_{m},T)\sqrt{-g}\ d^{4}x+\int L_{m}\sqrt{-g}\ d^{4}x,

(1)

where
\displaystyle f(R,L_{m},T)
is an arbitrary function of the Ricci scalar
\displaystyle R
, of the trace of the energy-momentum tensor
\displaystyle T
, with
\displaystyle T=g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}
, and of the Lagrangian density of ordinary matter,
\displaystyle L_{m}
, respectively. It is important to emphasize that the energy-momentum tensor is defined according to
the relation:

T_{\mu\nu}=\frac{-2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\partial\left(\sqrt{-g}L_{m}\right)}{\partial g^{\mu\nu}}=-2\frac{\partial L_{m}}{\partial g^{\mu\nu}}+g_{\mu\nu}L_{m}.

(2)

In order to obtain the field equations, the variation of the action with respect to the metric tensor is taken. Then

\delta S=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^{4}x\left[\delta\sqrt{-g}f+\sqrt{-g}\left(f_{R}\delta R+f_{T}\delta T+f_{L}\delta L_{m}\right)\right]+\int d^{4}x\delta\left(\sqrt{-g}L_{m}\right)

(3)

with
\displaystyle f_{R}\equiv\frac{\partial f}{\partial R}
,
\displaystyle f_{T}\equiv\frac{\partial f}{\partial T}
and
\displaystyle f_{L}\equiv\frac{\partial f}{\partial L_{m}}
. The variations
\displaystyle\delta R
and
\displaystyle\delta T
are known
[
24
]
. They are given as

f_{R}\delta R+f_{T}\delta T=\left[R_{\mu\nu}f_{R}+\left(g_{\mu\nu}\Box-\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\right)f_{R}+\left(T_{\mu\nu}+\Theta_{\mu\nu}\right)f_{T}\right]\delta g^{\mu\nu},

(4)

where the tensor
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mu\nu}
is defined based on the variation

\displaystyle\displaystyle\delta T

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\delta\left(g^{\alpha\beta}T_{\alpha\beta}\right),

(5)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\delta g^{\alpha\beta}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}\right)T_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{\delta T_{\alpha\beta}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}g^{\alpha\beta},

(6)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left(T_{\mu\nu}+\Theta_{\mu\nu}\right),

(7)

which leads to

\Theta_{\mu\nu}\equiv\frac{\delta T_{\alpha\beta}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}g^{\alpha\beta}=-2T_{\mu\nu}+g_{\mu\nu}L_{m}-\tau_{\mu\nu}.

(8)

Here a new tensor
\displaystyle\tau_{\mu\nu}
is defined as

\tau_{\mu\nu}=2g^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial^{2}L_{m}}{\partial g^{\mu\nu}\partial g^{\alpha\beta}}.

(9)

In addition, the term
\displaystyle f_{L}\delta L_{m}
in Eq. (
3
) becomes

f_{L}\delta L_{m}=\frac{1}{2}f_{L}(g_{\mu\nu}L_{m}-T_{\mu\nu}).

(10)

Taking these results, the complete set of the field equations is given by

f_{R}R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\left[f-(f_{L}+2f_{T})L_{m}\right]g_{\mu\nu}+(g_{\mu\nu}\Box-\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu})f_{R}=\left[\kappa+\frac{1}{2}(f_{L}+2f_{T})\right]T_{\mu\nu}+f_{T}\tau_{\mu\nu},

(11)

where
\displaystyle\kappa=16\pi
is a constant.

The main objective of this work is to investigate whether the causality violation described by Gödel-type universes persists in this gravitational theory as allowed in general relativity.

III
Gödel-type Metric

In this section, the proposal is to present the Gödel-type solution and analyze whether this universe is a solution of
\displaystyle f(R,L_{m},T)
gravity. The study will be developed for different contents of matter. The Gödel-type solution represents a generalization of the Gödel metric. The Gödel universe is an exact solution to Einstein field equations for a homogeneous rotating universe. This solution introduces the possibility of Closed Time-like Curves (CTCs), which allow for causality violations. Consequently, this space-time allows theoretical time travel.

In order to obtain more details about causality violation, the Gödel-type metric is considered
[
7
]
. The line element that describes this universe is given by

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}-2H(r)dtd\phi+dr^{2}+\left(D^{2}(r)-H^{2}(r)\right)d\phi^{2}+dz^{2},

(12)

where
\displaystyle H(r)
and
\displaystyle D(r)
are functions that satisfies the relations

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{H^{\prime}(r)}{D(r)}=2\omega\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\frac{D^{\prime\prime}(r)}{D(r)}=m^{2}.

(13)

The prime (
′
) denotes the derivative with respect to
\displaystyle r
.

It is worth noting that the parameters
\displaystyle\omega
and
\displaystyle m^{2}
are free parameters that characterize all the properties of the metric. Theses parameters define three distinct classes: hyperbolic (
\displaystyle m^{2}>0,\omega\neq 0
), trigonometric (
\displaystyle m^{2}<0,\omega\neq 0
), and linear (
\displaystyle m^{2}=0,\omega\neq 0
)
[
7
]
. Here only the hyperbolic class is considered, since this class contains the standard Gödel universe. For this class, the functions
\displaystyle H(r)
and
\displaystyle D(r)
are given as

\displaystyle\displaystyle H(r)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{4\omega}{m^{2}}senh^{2}\left(\frac{mr}{2}\right),

(14)

\displaystyle\displaystyle D(r)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}senh(mr).

(15)

The critical radius, which defines the existence of Closed Time-like Curves (CTCs), is an important quantity that can be derived from the Gödel-type solution. It is defined as

r_{c}=\frac{2}{m}\sinh^{-1}\left(\frac{4\omega^{2}}{m^{2}}-1\right)^{-1}.

(16)

This expression leads to two interesting relationships between the parameters
\displaystyle\omega^{2}
and
\displaystyle m^{2}
. Firstly, when
\displaystyle m^{2}=2\omega^{2}
, the Gödel solution exhibits a finite critical radius given by

r_{c}=\frac{2}{m}\sinh^{-1}\left(1\right).

(17)

Consequently, non-causal regions are allowed. Secondly, if
\displaystyle m^{2}=4\omega^{2}
, the critical radius becomes infinite. In other words, this condition results in a causal solution.

For simplicity, a new basis, known as a local Lorentz co-frame, is chosen, transforming the metric into the form
[
7
]
,

ds^{2}=\eta_{AB}\theta^{A}\theta^{B}=(\theta^{(0)})^{2}-(\theta^{(1)})^{2}-(\theta^{(2)})^{2}-(\theta^{(3)})^{2},

(18)

where
\displaystyle\eta_{AB}
represents the Minkowski metric, and
\displaystyle\theta^{A}=e^{A}\ _{\mu}\ dx^{\mu}
with

\displaystyle\displaystyle\theta^{(0)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=dt+H(r)d\phi,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\theta^{(1)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=dr,\

\displaystyle\displaystyle\theta^{(2)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=D(r)d\phi,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\theta^{(3)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=dz.

(19)

Here,
\displaystyle e^{A}\ {\mu}
corresponds to the tetrad that satisfies the relation
\displaystyle e^{A}\,_{\mu}e^{\mu}\,_{B}=\delta^{A}_{B}
. The capital Latin letters are used to label Lorentz indices ranging from 0 to 3.

In the local Lorentz co-frame (
19
), the non-zero Ricci tensor components are
\displaystyle R_{(0)(0)}=2\omega^{2},\ R_{(1)(1)}=\ R_{(2)(2)}=2\omega^{2}-m^{2}.
The Ricci scalar is
\displaystyle R=2(m^{2}-w^{2})
.

Now, the objective is to use these quantities and solve the Einstein field equations (
11
) choosing different matter contents.

III.1
Perfect Fluid

In this subsection, the Gödel-type metric is analyzed in
\displaystyle f(R,L_{m},T)
gravity considering the perfect fluid as the matter content. The energy-momentum tensor that describes the perfect fluid is given as

\displaystyle\displaystyle T_{AB}=(\rho+p)u_{A}u_{B}-p\eta_{AB},

(20)

with
\displaystyle\rho
being the energy density,
\displaystyle p
the pressure and
\displaystyle u_{A}=(1,0,0,0)
the four-velocity of the fluid. The non-zero components of the energy-momentum tensor are
\displaystyle T_{00}=\rho,T_{11}=T_{22}=T_{33}=p
. The Lagrangian that describes the perfect fluid is defined as
\displaystyle L_{M}=-p
.

The field equation Eq. (
11
) in tangent space becomes

f_{R}R_{AB}-\frac{1}{2}\left[f-(f_{L}+2f_{T})L_{m}\right]\eta_{AB}+(\eta_{AB}\Box-\nabla_{A}\nabla_{B})f_{R}=\left[\kappa+\frac{1}{2}(f_{L}+2f_{T})\right]T_{AB}+f_{T}\tau_{AB}.

(21)

Taking into account the perfect fluid, the tensor
\displaystyle\tau_{AB}
becomes zero. In addition, the derivatives of the function
\displaystyle f_{R}
are zero, since the Ricci scalar is constant. Therefore, the field equations are given as

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2f_{R}\omega^{2}-\frac{1}{2}f

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\rho\kappa+(\rho+p)(\frac{1}{2}f_{L}+f_{T}),

(22)

\displaystyle\displaystyle(2\omega^{2}-m^{2})f_{R}+\frac{1}{2}f

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\kappa p,

(23)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}f

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\kappa p.

(24)

Eqs. (
23
) and (
24
) lead to
\displaystyle m^{2}=2\omega^{2}
. This condition defines the Gödel solution. Then the causality violation is allowed in this gravitational theory.

The remaining equation Eq. (
22
) provides

m=\left[\frac{(\rho+p)(\frac{1}{2}f_{L}+f_{T})+\frac{1}{2}f+\kappa\rho}{f_{R}}\right]^{1/2}.

(25)

Then the critical radius in
\displaystyle f(R,T,L_{m})
gravity is

\displaystyle\displaystyle r_{c}=2\sinh^{-1}(1)\left[\frac{f_{R}}{(\rho+p)(\frac{1}{2}f_{L}+f_{T})+\frac{1}{2}f+\kappa\rho}\right]^{1/2}.

(26)

This result shows that the critical radius, beyond which the causality violation explicitly occurs, depends on the form of the function
\displaystyle f(R,L_{m},T)
and its derivatives,

\displaystyle f_{R},f_{L}
and
\displaystyle f_{T}
. This result is a generalization of the study developed for
\displaystyle f(R)
and
\displaystyle f(R,T)
gravity.

In short, assuming the perfect fluid as the unique matter of the universe, the causality violation arises naturally. Now the question is: do other types of matter lead to the same consequence? Let us carry out this investigation in the next subsections.

As a consequence

Then, the field equations (

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2(2\omega^{2}-m^{2})f_{R}+f

\displaystyle\displaystyle-f

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(B^{2}-E^{2})(f_{L}+2f_{T})+E_{0}^{2}\left[\kappa+\frac{1}{2}(f_{L}+2f_{T})\right]-2E^{2}f_{T}.

III.3

Here let us investigate the consistency of the Gödel-type universe in
\displaystyle f(R,L_{m},T)

This equation leads to the following interpretation. Assuming that
\displaystyle f_{R}>0

-E_{0}^{2}\left[\kappa+\frac{1}{2}(f_{L}+2f_{T})\right]+f_{T}(E^{2}+B^{2})>0,

III.4
Scalar field

In this subsection, the main objective is to find a causal solution within the
\displaystyle f(R,L_{m},T)

Therefore, assuming the scalar field as the content of matter,

V
Discussions and Final Remarks

The present study focuses on
\displaystyle f(R,L_{m},T)
gravity. It is a gravitational theory that generalizes the coupling between geometry and matter. Analyzing the field equations, it is evident that the Gödel-type metric is a solution within this theory. The investigation is carried out for different types of matter. Considering a perfect fluid as matter content, a non-causal solution is identified. This leads to the emergence of a critical radius that delineates a finite non-causal region. This critical radius depends on the parameters of the theory. In the search for a causal solution, an electromagnetic field is incorporated. As a consequence, a causal solution arises, which means that the critical radius tends to the infinity. Similar behavior occurs for a combination of a perfect fluid plus an electromagnetic field as matter content. A causal solution is also found in the case where the matter content is a scalar field. Furthermore, it is important to note that the theories
\displaystyle f(R)
,
\displaystyle f(R,T)
and
\displaystyle f(R,L_{m})
and their causal or non-causal behavior are recovered in an appropriate limit. In addition, the results obtained here show that to preserve causality in
\displaystyle f(R,L_{m},T)
gravity a specific condition under the parameter
\displaystyle f_{L}
is necessary. Additionally, the results for the modified theory of gravity can be directly compared to those of general relativity, as the Gödel-type solution is also an exact solution within general relativity. While both theories permit causal and non-causal regions, the conditions leading to these regions differ. In
\displaystyle f(R,L_{m},T)
gravity, certain restrictions arise that depend on the parameters of the theory. The main finding - that
\displaystyle f(R,L_{m},T)
gravity allows the same exact solution as general relativity - serves as an important test for this theory, which was constructed to explain gravitational phenomena. Another important aspect to highlight concerns the critical radius. Our results indicate that, when considering different types of matter, the critical radius is finite in the case of a perfect fluid, while it becomes infinite for other forms of matter. Consequently, causal and non-causal, or physical and non-physical, regions are identified, which depend on the choice of matter. Although the critical radius is not a physical quantity in itself, it serves to delineate the physical and non-physical regions within this cosmological framework. Thus, the critical radius demonstrates behavior similar to that observed in general relativity. Another important point to note is that all results are derived for a general form of the function
\displaystyle f(R,L_{m},T)
, making our findings applicable to all examples of this function.

A similar study has been conducted for a specific case of
\displaystyle f(R,L_{m})
gravity, known as exponential
\displaystyle f(R,L_{m})
gravity. The analysis considered three different types of matter content: perfect fluid, scalar field, and electromagnetic field. In the first two cases, the same results as general relativity are obtained. However, for the electromagnetic field, an interesting condition emerges: to prevent the violation of causality, the cosmological constant must satisfy a specific limit.

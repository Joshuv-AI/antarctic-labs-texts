Abstract
In the paper, we derive the ξ \xi moments ⟨ ξ 2 ; b 1 n ; ∥ ⟩ \langle\xi_{2;b_{1}}^{n;\|}\rangle of the longitudinal leading-twist distribution amplitude ϕ 2 ; b 1 ∥ \phi_{2;b_{1}}^{\|} for P 1 1 {}^{1}P_{1} -state b 1 ​ ( 1235 ) b_{1}(1235) meson by using the QCD sum rules under the background field theory. Considering the contributions from the vacuum condensates up to dimension six, its first two nonzero ξ \xi moments at the scale 1 GeV are ⟨ ξ 2 ; b 1 1 ; ∥ ⟩ = − 0.647 − 0.113 + 0.118 \langle\xi_{2;b_{1}}^{1;\|}\rangle=-0.647^{+0.118}_{-0.113} and ⟨ ξ 2 ; b 1 3 ; ∥ ⟩ = − 0.328 − 0.052 + 0.055 \langle\xi_{2;b_{1}}^{3;\|}\rangle=-0.328^{+0.055}_{-0.052} , respectively. Using those moments, we then fix the Gegenbauer expansion series of ϕ 2 ; b 1 ∥ \phi_{2;b_{1}}^{\|} and apply it to calculate B → b 1 ​ ( 1235 ) B\to b_{1}(1235) transition form factors (TFFs) that are derived by using the QCD light-cone sum rules. Those TFFs are then extrapolated to the physically allowable q 2 q^{2} range via the simplified series expansion. As for the branching fractions, we obtain ℬ ⁡ ( B ¯ 0 → b 1 ​ ( 1235 ) + ​ e − ​ ν ¯ e ) = 2.179 − 0.422 + 0.553 × 10 − 4 {\cal B}(\bar{B}^{0}\to b_{1}(1235)^{+}e^{-}\bar{\nu}_{e})=2.179^{+0.553}_{-0.422}\times 10^{-4} , ℬ ⁡ ( B 0 → b 1 ​ ( 1235 ) − ​ μ + ​ ν μ ) = 2.166 − 0.415 + 0.544 × 10 − 4 {\cal B}(B^{0}\to b_{1}(1235)^{-}\mu^{+}\nu_{\mu})=2.166^{+0.544}_{-0.415}\times 10^{-4} , ℬ ⁡ ( B + → b 1 ​ ( 1235 ) 0 ​ e + ​ ν e ) = 2.353 − 0.456 + 0.597 × 10 − 4 {\cal B}(B^{+}\to b_{1}(1235)^{0}e^{+}\nu_{e})=2.353^{+0.597}_{-0.456}\times 10^{-4} , and ℬ ⁡ ( B + → b 1 ​ ( 1235 ) 0 ​ μ + ​ ν μ ) = 2.339 − 0.448 + 0.587 × 10 − 4 {\cal B}(B^{+}\to b_{1}(1235)^{0}\mu^{+}\nu_{\mu})=2.339^{+0.587}_{-0.448}\times 10^{-4} , respectively.

For self-consistency, we first add a simple introduction to BFT which is the basis for BFTSR approach. More detailed introduction of BFTSR can be found in Ref.

The main idea of the background field theory is to describe the non-perturbation effect with the classical background field satisfying the equation of motion and to describe the quantum fluctuation, namely the perturbation effect, on this basis within the frame of quantum field theory. More specifically, one can use the following substitution for the usual QCD Lagrangian:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\mu}^{a}(x)\to\mathcal{A}_{\mu}^{a}(x)+\phi_{\mu}^{a}(x),

(2)

\displaystyle\psi(x)\to\psi(x)+\eta(x),

(3)

\phi_{\mu}^{a}(x)
and
\eta(x)
represent their quantum fluctuations, respectively. The physical observable is gauge independent. And as an advantage of choosing the background field gauge is that it makes the theory be invariant under the background field gauge, making the calculated physical quantity independent of the gauge. With the help of Eq.(
2

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{QCD}}(\mathcal{A},\psi)+\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{ghosts})+\bar{\eta}(i/\!\!\!\!D-m)\eta+\frac{1}{2}\phi_{\mu}^{a}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left\{g^{\mu\nu}D_{ac}^{2}-\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)[D^{\mu}D^{\nu}]_{ac}+2g_{s}f^{abc}G_{b}^{\mu\nu}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\times\phi_{\mu}^{b}\phi_{\nu}^{c}-g_{s}f^{abc}(\partial_{\mu}\phi_{\nu}^{a})\phi_{b}^{\mu}\phi_{c}^{\nu}-\frac{1}{4}g_{s}^{2}f^{abc}f_{acf}\phi_{b}^{\mu}\phi_{c}^{\nu}

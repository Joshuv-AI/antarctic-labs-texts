Log homotopy types are homotopy types with modulus
Shane Kelly

Abstract
. We show that the category of log homotopy types is a full subcategory of a category of homotopy types with modulus.



1.
Introduction

Morel and Voevodsky’s
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy theory has many successes, most famously the proof of the Norm Residue Theorem
comparing Milnor
K
-theory to étale cohomology with coefficients away from the characteristic. However, by definition it cannot see non-
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant phenomena such Hodge cohomology or Hochschild homology. It also fails see to invariants that are non-
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant adjacent such as wild ramification.

In recent years a number of larger categories have been proposed such as motives with modulus,
[
KMSY22
]
, log motives,
[
BPØ22
]
, and motivic spectra,
[
AI22
]
,
[
AHI24
]
.

Motives with modulus
are designed to study cohomology groups of a variety
X
, such as
H_{\textrm{{\'{e}}t}}^{1}(X,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
or
H^{n}_{\textrm{Zar}}(X,\Omega^{m})
, whose classes can be bounded by something like ramification order or pole order; the bound is expressed by a divisor
D
. The rôle of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
is replaced by the pair
(\mathbb{P}^{1},\infty)
, that is, the space of cohomology classes on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with “tame ramification” or “simple poles” at infinity.
Log motives
are designed to study cohomology groups of log schemes. The rôle of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
is replaced by the log scheme
(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\cap\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}^{*})
, that is, the compactifying log structure for the open immersion
\mathbb{A}^{1}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
Motivic spectra
is an approach that systematically replaces the use of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
in the Morel–Voevodsky theory with
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-stability and smooth blowup excision. Motivic spectra do not appear in this note.

In this note we compare an unstable homotopy theory of modulus pairs
\operatorname{\underline{M}H}_{k}^{\mathrm{}}
with the unstable homotopy theory of log schemes
\operatorname{logH}_{k}
, filling in the details of one of the comparisons suggested to Miyazaki and Saito in
[
Kel20
]
. Heuristically, the equivalence in Theorem
1.1
expresses the idea that log cohomology is the first level of the “ramification” or “pole” filtration, namely, the “tame ramification” or “simple pole” level. The notation is explained as the introduction progresses culminating in Eq.(
1
) and Eq.(
2
) on Page
1

2.
Log schemes are modulus pairs with completed modulus

Given a morphism of schemes
T\to S
and a
S
-scheme
X
, we will use the notational convenience

X|_{T}:=T\times_{S}X.

The log theory is developed using strict normal crossing divisors, cf.
[
BPØ22
, Def.7.2.1, Lem.A.5.10]
. Let us recall what this means.

Definition 2.1
.

We will say that a closed subscheme
X^{\infty}\subseteq\overline{X}
has strict normal crossing support if there exists a Zariski covering
\{U_{i}\to X\}_{i\in I}
and étale morphisms
θ_{i}:U_{i}\to\mathbb{A}^{d}
such that

X^{\infty}|_{U_{i}}=A^{\infty}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{d}}|_{U_{i}}
where
A^{\infty}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{d}}=\mathrm{Spec}(k[t_{1},\dots,t_{d}]/\langle t_{1}^{e_{1}}\dots t_{d}^{e_{d}}\rangle)
with all
e_{i}\geq 0
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
k
be a field. The category

\mathrm{\underline{P}Sm}^{\mathrm{}}_{k}

has as objects pairs
\mathcal{X}=(\overline{X},X^{\infty})
such that
\overline{X}
is a smooth
k
-scheme and
X^{\infty}\subseteq\overline{X}
is an effective Cartier divisor with
normal crossing support.
Morphisms
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
are morphisms of schemes
f:\overline{X}\to\overline{Y}
such that
X^{\infty}\supseteq Y^{\infty}|_{\overline{X}}
.

Example 2.3
.

One writes
\overline{\square}
for the pair
(\mathbb{P}^{1},\{\infty\})\in\mathrm{\underline{P}Sm}^{\mathrm{}}_{k}
. More generally, for any pair
\mathcal{X}=(\overline{X},X^{\infty})
one writes
\overline{\square}_{\mathcal{X}}
for the pair
(\overline{X}\times\mathbb{P}^{1},X^{\infty}{\times}\mathbb{P}^{1}+\overline{X}{\times}\{\infty\})
.

Remark 2.4
.

As mentioned in the introduction, the divisor
X^{\infty}
should be thought of as a bound on the ramification locus (algebraic setting) or singularity locus (holomorphic setting) of an as yet unspecified collection of objects over
\overline{X}
.

The direction of the inequality aligns with the canonical inclusion of line bundles
\mathcal{O}(X^{\infty})\supseteq\mathcal{O}(Y^{\infty}|_{\overline{X}})
where
\mathcal{O}(X^{\infty})=\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{\overline{X}}}(\mathcal{I}_{X^{\infty}},\mathcal{O}_{\overline{X}})
is the sheaf of meromorphic functions on
\overline{X}
with poles bounded by
X^{\infty}
. That is, this choice of inequality makes

\mathcal{X}\mapsto\Gamma(\overline{X},\mathcal{O}(X^{\infty}))

a presheaf on
\mathrm{\underline{P}Sm}_{k}
.

Definition 2.5

(
[

[

A

A morphism of log schemes

Sections of

Example 2.7

Given a scheme
X

\log(X,D):=(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}\cap j_{*}\mathcal{O}_{U}^{*})

Compactifying log schemes are particularly accessible.

Lemma 2.8

(Cf.
[
Ogu18
, Prop.III.1.6.2]

Suppose

is morphism of schemes equipped with nowhere dense closed subschemes

(1)

The canonical morphism induces a bijection

\hom\!\!\!\!\!\!_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textrm{log}\\
\textrm{schemes}\end{subarray}}\!\!\biggl(\log(X,D),\log(X^{\prime},D^{\prime})\biggr)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\biggl\{f\in\hom_{schemes}(X,X^{\prime})\ |\ f(U)\subseteq U^{\prime}\biggr\}.

(2)

If

are locally principal and

\biggl\{f\in\hom_{schemes}(X,X^{\prime})\ |\ \mathcal{I}_{D}^{n}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{D^{\prime}}\mathcal{O}_{X}\textrm{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N}\biggr\}.

Proof.

The first claim is a special case of

if and only if

for some

\lceil\mathcal{I}_{D}^{n}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{D^{\prime}}\mathcal{O}_{X};\exists n\rfloor

∎

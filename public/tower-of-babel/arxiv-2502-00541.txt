On the Curvature and Topology of Compact Stationary Spacetimes
Amir Babak Aazami

Abstract
. Using the result of Petersen & Wink ’21, we find obstructions to the curvature and topology of compact Lorentzian manifolds admitting a unit-length timelike Killing vector field.



1.
Introduction

In this article we apply the recent work
[
PW21
]
to find curvature and topological obstructions to compact
stationary spacetimes
; i.e., compact Lorentzian manifolds with a timelike Killing vector field. Little is known about the Betti numbers of such manifolds other than the following:
[
Kam93
]
showed that compact flat stationary Lorentzian manifolds must have nonzero first Betti number;
[
RS96
]
then improved upon this result in two respects: By using Bochner’s technique, they showed that, up to covering, only tori satisfy this, and that compact Ricci-flat stationary Lorentzian manifolds must also have nonzero first Betti number. (For other obstructions, e.g., regarding sectional curvature, or isometry groups, or for Kundt spacetimes in place of stationary ones, see, e.g.,
[
CM62
,
BEM88
,
Car89
,
Kli96
,
Bau11
,
PZ14
,
Sch15
,
RS24
]
.) Here we show that, by using
[
PW21
]
, further information can be gained about higher Betti numbers. In the latter, the Lichnerowicz Laplacian — which extends the Bochner method by giving conditions for when harmonic tensors are parallel — was used to relate Betti numbers
b_{i}
to the eigenvalues of the curvature operator of a closed Riemannian
n
-manifold
(M,g)
. In particular, by generalizing a result in
[
Poo80
]
,
[
PW21
]
showed that, for
n\geq 3
and
1\leq p\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
, if
g
’s curvature operator is
(n-p)
-positive — i.e., the sum of its smallest
n-p
eigenvalues is positive — then
b_{1}=\cdots=b_{p}=0
and
b_{n-p}=\cdots=b_{n-1}=0
. We apply this result here to compact Lorentzian
n
-manifolds
(M,g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}})
admitting a timelike Killing vector field
T
, by analyzing the Riemannian metric

(1)

\displaystyle g\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}-2\frac{T^{\flat}\otimes T^{\flat}}{g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(T,T)}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377ptT^{\flat}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(T,\cdot).

Not only are compact stationary spacetimes an appealing class of manifolds to study — e.g., they are always geodesically complete
[
RS95
]
, something that is not guaranteed in the Lorentzian setting — but every stationary spacetime admits a “normalized” one in its conformal class (i.e.,
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(T,T)=-1
with
T
still a timelike Killing vector field). As we are interested only in the topology of
M
, restricting our attention to such normalized stationary spacetimes is not an impediment — especially given the complicated relationship between the curvature tensors of
g
and
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
. However, as we show in Proposition
3
, the virtue of a unit-length Killing vector field
T
is that there always exist canonical local frames with respect to which the curvature operators of
g
and
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
are so closely aligned that the former effectively becomes the “symmetrization” of the latter. (This overcomes the fact that the curvature operator of a Lorentzian metric is neither symmetric nor diagonalizable in general, and can have complex eigenvalues.) As a consequence, we then use
[
PW21
]
to prove the following general results:

Theorem
.

Let
(M,g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}})
be a closed, connected, and oriented Lorentzian 3-manifold with a unit-length timelike Killing vector field
T
. Relative to the local
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
-orthonormal frame (
35
), set
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(T,X_{i},X_{j},X_{k})\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=R_{Tijk}
, etc., where
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
is the Riemann curvature 4-tensor of
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
. If the symmetric matrix

\begin{bmatrix}-R_{T1T1}&0&R_{12T1}\\
0&-R_{T2T2}&R_{12T2}\\
R_{T112}&R_{T212}&-R_{1212}+3R_{T1T1}+3R_{T2T2}\end{bmatrix}

is 2-positive, then
b_{1}=b_{2}=0
. In general, if
(M,g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}})
is odd-dimensional and the
{n\choose 2}\times{n\choose 2}
analogue of this matrix is
(n-p)
-positive for
1\leq p\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
, then
b_{1}=\cdots=b_{p}=b_{n-p}=\cdots=b_{n-1}=0
.

(The component
R_{T1T2}=0
in the frame (
35
).) As an application, the
n
-torus — usually a source of examples of distinguished Lorentzian metrics
[
RS96
,
S“’an97
]
— can never admit such
(n-p)
-positive matrices (nor of those in the Theorem below). Another application is:

Corollary
.

Let
(M,g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}})
be a closed, connected, and oriented Lorentzian 3-manifold with a unit-length timelike Killing vector field
T
, Ricci tensor
\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
, and scalar curvature
\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
. If
T
is not parallel, and if

\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}>3\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(T,T)+|d\,\text{ln}(\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(T,T))|_{g_{\scalebox{0.3}{$L$}}}^{2},

then
b_{1}=b_{2}=0
. If
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
is replaced by a Riemannian metric
g
, then the inequality becomes
\text{scal}>\text{Ric}(T,T)+|d\,\text{ln}(\text{Ric}(T,T))|_{g}^{2}
.

The even-dimensional case is similar to the odd-dimensional case above, but here we also get obstructions to
k
-positivity of the eigenvalues:

Theorem
.

If
(M,g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}})
is four-dimensional, then relative to the local
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
-orthonormal frame (
35
), the symmetric matrix

\begin{bmatrix}-R_{T1T1}&R_{T2T1}&R_{T3T1}&R_{23T1}&R_{31T1}&R_{12T1}\\
R_{T1T2}&-R_{T2T2}&R_{T3T2}&R_{23T2}&R_{31T2}&R_{12T2}\\
R_{T1T3}&T_{T2T3}&-R_{T3T3}&R_{23T3}&R_{31T3}&R_{12T3}\\
R_{T123}&R_{T223}&R_{T323}&-R_{2323}+3R_{T2T2}+3R_{T3T3}&-R_{3123}&-R_{1223}\\
R_{T131}&R_{T231}&R_{T331}&-R_{2331}&-R_{3131}&-R_{1231}\\
R_{T112}&R_{T212}&R_{T312}&-R_{2312}&-R_{3112}&-R_{1212}\end{bmatrix}

cannot be 3-positive. If
(M,g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}})
is
2n
-dimensional, then the
{2n\choose 2}\times{2n\choose 2}
analogue of this matrix cannot be
n
-positive
;
if it is
(2n-p)
-positive for
1\leq p<n
, then
b_{1}=\cdots=b_{p}=b_{2n-p}=\cdots=b_{2n-1}=0
.

(In fact
R_{T1Ti}=R_{T2T3}=0
in the frame (
35
).) The eigenvalues in both theorems are frame-independent, because these matrices are in fact the curvature operators, not of the Lorentzian metric
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
, but of the Riemannian metric (
1
) expressed in Lorentzian data. The reason they take on such simple forms in terms of
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
is because of the existence of the distinguished frames mentioned above, particularly in dimensions three and four.

2.
Relationship between
g
and
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}

The setup of our problem is as follows: Let
(M,g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}})
be a closed, oriented Lorentzian
n
-manifold endowed with a timelike Killing vector field
T
; i.e., a compact and oriented
stationary spacetime
without boundary. Note that
T
will necessarily be a unit-length timelike Killing vector field of the conformal metric

(2)

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\frac{g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}}{-g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(T,T)}\cdot

Now form the Riemannian metric

(3)

\displaystyle g\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}-2\frac{T^{\flat}\otimes T^{\flat}}{g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(T,T)}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377ptT^{\flat}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(T,\cdot).

Note that
g(T,T)=-g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(T,T)>0
, that every
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
-orthogonal local frame of the form
\{T,X_{1},\dots,X_{n-1}\}
is also
g
-orthogonal (in fact
g(T,\cdot)=-g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(T,\cdot)
and
g(X_{i},\cdot)=g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(X_{i},\cdot)
), and finally that
T
is also a Killing vector field with respect to
g
:
\mathfrak{L}_{T}g=0
if and only if
\mathfrak{L}_{T}g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}=0
, where
\mathfrak{L}
is the Lie derivative. Thus if
M
admits a Riemannian metric
g
with a nowhere vanishing Killing vector field
T
, then
M
admits a stationary Lorentzian metric
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
, and thus also a normalized one via (
2
). As shown, e.g., in
[
RS24
, Prop. 5]
, all of this can be accomplished by finding an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-action on
M
without fixed points. (For more general Killing vector fields, see
[
FJP11
]
.) With these preliminaries established, we would now like to relate the Riemann curvature 4-tensors of
g
and
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
; in order to do so, let us first relate their Levi-Civita connections:

Proposition 1
.

Let
(M,g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}})
be a Lorentzian
n
-manifold with a timelike Killing vector field
T
. Let
g
be the Riemannian metric defined via (
3
). With respect to any
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
- and
g
-orthogonal local frame
\{T,X_{1},\dots,X_{n-1}\}
with each
X_{i}
having unit length, the Levi-Civita connections
\nabla
and
\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
are related as follows:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\nabla_{\!T}{T}=-\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!T}{T},\\
\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{T}=-\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{T}+X_{i}(\emph{\text{ln}}(g(T,T))T,\\
\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}}=\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}},\\
\nabla_{\!T}{X_{i}}=\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!T}{X_{i}}-2\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{T}+X_{i}(\emph{\text{ln}}(g(T,T))T.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

A Koszul formula for
\nabla-\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
is derived in
[
Ole14
, Proposition 2.3]
, in the case of a general unit-length timelike vector field
T
. Modifying that formula to our setting — with
T
a Killing vector field, though not necessarily unit length — the relations (
1
) can be straightforwardly derived. Indeed,

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}},X_{k})\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{1}{2}\Big[X_{i}(g(X_{j},X_{k}))+X_{j}(g(X_{k},X_{i}))-X_{k}(g(X_{i},X_{j}))

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt-g(X_{j},[X_{i},X_{k}])-g(X_{k},[X_{j},X_{i}])+g(X_{i},[X_{k},X_{j}])\Big]

is term-by-term equivalent to its counterpart
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}},X_{k})
, as
g(X_{\alpha},\cdot)=g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(X_{\alpha},\cdot)
for
\alpha=i,j,k
. Thus

g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}},X_{k})=g(\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}},X_{k})\overset{\eqref{eqn:Riem}}{=}g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}},X_{k})-2\frac{g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(T,\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}})\cancelto{0}{g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(T,X_{k})}}{g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(T,T)},

so that

(9)

\displaystyle g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}}-\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}},X_{k})=0.

Now putting
T
in place of
X_{k}
,

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}},T)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{1}{2}\Big[X_{i}(g(X_{j},T))+X_{j}(g(T,X_{i}))-T(g(X_{i},X_{j}))

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt-g(X_{j},[X_{i},T])-g(T,[X_{j},X_{i}])+g(X_{i},[T,X_{j}])\Big]

is term-by-term equivalent to its counterpart
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}},T)
— except for the term

g(T,[X_{j},X_{i}])\overset{\eqref{eqn:Riem}}{=}-g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(T,[X_{j},X_{i}]),

so that

\underbrace{\,g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}},T)+g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(T,[X_{j},X_{i}])\,}_{\text{$=g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{j}}{X_{i}},T)\overset{(*)}{=}-g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}},T)$}}=g(\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}},T)\overset{\eqref{eqn:Riem}}{=}-g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}},T)

where
(*)
holds because
T
is a Killing vector field,

\mathfrak{L}_{T}g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}=0\hskip 14.45377pt\Rightarrow\hskip 14.45377ptg_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{T},X_{j})=-g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{j}}{T},X_{i}).

Thus
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}}-\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}},T)=0
. Combining this with (
9
) yields

g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}}-\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}},\cdot)=0,

hence
\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}}=\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}}
. Using this,
\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{T}=-\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{T}+X_{i}(\text{ln}(g(T,T))T
can be derived, from which
\nabla_{\!T}{X_{i}}=\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!T}{X_{i}}-2\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{T}+X_{i}(\text{ln}(g(T,T))T
follows immediately via
\nabla_{\!T}{X_{i}}-\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{T}=[T,X_{i}]=\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!T}{X_{i}}-\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{T}
. Finally, using the Killing condition, the case
\nabla_{\!T}{T}=-\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!T}{T}
follows easily, via

g(\nabla_{\!T}{T},X_{i})=-\frac{1}{2}X_{i}(g(T,T))=g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{T},T)=-g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!T}{T},X_{i})=-g(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!T}{T},X_{i})

and the fact that
g(\nabla_{\!T}{T},T)=g(-\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!T}{T},T)=0
.
∎

Armed with Proposition
1
, we now relate the Riemann curvature 4-tensors of
g
and
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
. In what follows, our sign convention for the Riemann curvature endomorphism is

R_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(X,Y)Z=\nabla_{\!X}{\nabla_{\!Y}{Z}}-\nabla_{\!Y}{\nabla_{\!X}{Z}}-\nabla_{\![X,Y]}{Z}.

Although this sign is the opposite of that in
[
PW21
]
, this is compensated for by the fact that our curvature operator (
21
) below is defined with a minus sign, which
[
PW21
]
does not have.

Proposition 2
.

With respect to the local frame
\{T,X_{1},\dots,X_{n-1}\}
of Proposition
1
, the components of the Riemann curvature 4-tensors
Rm
and
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
of
g
and
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
, respectively, are related as follows:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\text{Rm}(X_{i},X_{j},T,X_{k})=-\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(X_{i},X_{j},T,X_{k}),\\
\text{Rm}(T,X_{i},T,X_{j})=-\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(T,X_{i},T,X_{j})-2g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{T},\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{j}}{T})\\
\hskip 158.99377pt+\,\frac{1}{2g_{\scalebox{0.3}{$L$}}(T,T)}X_{i}(g_{\scalebox{0.3}{$L$}}(T,T))X_{j}(g_{\scalebox{0.3}{$L$}}(T,T))\\
\text{Rm}(X_{i},X_{j},X_{k},X_{l})=\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(X_{i},X_{j},X_{k},X_{l})\\
\hskip 93.95122pt+\,\frac{2}{g_{\scalebox{0.3}{$L$}}(T,T)}\Big[g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{T},X_{l})g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{j}}{T},X_{k})\\
\hskip 151.76744pt-\,g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{T},X_{k})g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{j}}{T},X_{l})\\
\hskip 180.67499pt-2g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{T},X_{j})g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{k}}{T},X_{l})\Big].\\
\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Most of these components can be found in
[
Ole14
, Section 3]
in the case of a general unit-length timelike vector field
T
. As we will need all components of
\text{Rm},\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
, let us derive in detail the first two components in (
2
) here (and also given that our
T
may not have unit length). Thus, consider the component
\text{Rm}(X_{i},X_{j},T,X_{k})
, and begin by writing it as

\displaystyle\text{Rm}(X_{i},X_{j},T,X_{k})\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!g(\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{\!\nabla_{\!X_{j}}{T}}-\nabla_{\!X_{j}}{\!\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{T}}-\nabla_{\![X_{i},X_{j}]}{T},X_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!X_{i}(g(\nabla_{\!X_{j}}{T},X_{k}))-g(\nabla_{\!X_{j}}{T},\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{X_{k}})

\displaystyle-X_{j}(g(\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{T},X_{k}))+g(\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{T},\nabla_{\!X_{j}}{X_{k}})

\displaystyle\hskip 108.405pt-g(\nabla_{\![X_{i},X_{j}]}{T},X_{k}).

Using Proposition
1
, the Killing condition, and the fact that
g(\cdot,X_{k})=g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\cdot,X_{k})
, the terms on the right-hand side can be written as

(18)

\displaystyle\text{Rm}(X_{i},X_{j},T,X_{k})\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!-X_{i}(g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{j}}{T},X_{k}))+g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla_{\!\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{X_{k}}}{T},X_{j})

\displaystyle\ \ +\,X_{j}(g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{T},X_{k}))-g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla_{\!\nabla_{\!X_{j}}{X_{k}}}{T},X_{i})

\displaystyle\hskip 115.63243pt-g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla_{\![X_{i},X_{j}]}{T},X_{k}).

Next, expanding
\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{X_{k}}=cT+b^{\lambda}X_{\lambda}=\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{X_{k}}
, we have that

\displaystyle g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla_{\!\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{X_{k}}}{T},X_{j})\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!cg_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla_{\!T}{T},X_{j})+b^{\lambda}g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla_{\!X_{\lambda}}{T},X_{j})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eqn:LCs}}{=}

\displaystyle\!\!-cg_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!T}{T},X_{j})-b^{\lambda}g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{\lambda}}{T},X_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!-g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{X_{k}}}{T},X_{j}).

Likewise,
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla_{\!\nabla_{\!X_{j}}{X_{k}}}{T},X_{i})=-g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{j}}{X_{k}}}{T},X_{i})
. A similar expansion of

yields
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla_{\![X_{i},X_{j}]}{T},X_{k})=-g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\![X_{i},X_{j}]}{T},X_{k})
. Inserting these into (
18
) and simplifying yields

\text{Rm}(X_{i},X_{j},T,X_{k})=-\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(X_{i},X_{j},T,X_{k}).

A similar computation occurs for the component

(19)

\displaystyle\text{Rm}(X_{i},T,T,X_{j})\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!g(\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{\!\nabla_{\!T}{T}}-\nabla_{\!T}{\!\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{T}}-\nabla_{\![X_{i},T]}{T},X_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!X_{i}(g(\nabla_{\!T}{T},X_{j}))-g(\nabla_{\!T}{T},\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{X_{j}})

\displaystyle-T(g(\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{T},X_{j}))+g(\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{T},\nabla_{\!T}{X_{j}})

\displaystyle\hskip 108.405pt-g(\nabla_{\![X_{i},T]}{T},X_{j}).

Once again, the terms here transform identically into (minus) their
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
- and
\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
-counterparts — except for
g(\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{T},\nabla_{\!T}{X_{j}})
, which, using Proposition
1
and the Killing condition, yields the additional terms

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{\!X_{i}}{T},\nabla_{\!T}{X_{j}})\!\!

Distinguished Local Frames

As we now show, this is not unique to the frame (

[

Proposition 3
.

Let
(M,g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}})
be a Lorentzian
n
-manifold with
n\geq 3
and with unit-length timelike Killing vector field
T
;
assume
T
is not parallel. At every point of
M
, there is a local orthonormal frame
\{T,X_{1},\dots,X_{n-1}\}
such that an even number of the
X_{i}
’s satisfies

(35)

\displaystyle\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{T}=f_{ii+1}X_{i+1}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i+1}}{T}=-f_{ii+1}X_{i},

for some functions
f_{ii+1}<0
, with any remaining
X_{j}
’s, along with
T
, satisfying
\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!T}{T}=\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{j}}{T}=0
. There are
\leq\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
such functions
f_{ii+1}
, with each
T(f_{ii+1})=0
and each

\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(X_{i},T,T,X_{i})=f_{ii+1}^{2}=\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(X_{i+1},T,T,X_{i+1}).

Finally,
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(X_{i},T,T,X_{j})=0
if
i\neq j
.

Proof.

(E.g., for the frame (
24
),
f_{12}=-1
and only
\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!T}{T}=0
.) Observe that for
n=3
,
any
orthonormal frame
\{T,X_{1},X_{2}\}
satisfies
\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!T}{T}=0
and

\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{1}}{T}=f_{12}X_{2}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{2}}{T}=-f_{12}X_{1}

for some function
f_{12}
, simply by virtue of the Killing condition. But for
n\geq 4
the Killing condition by itself yields only
\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!X_{i}}{T}=\sum_{j\neq i}\varepsilon_{ij}f_{ij}X_{j}
, where
\varepsilon_{ij}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=+1
if
i<j
and
-1
if
i>j
, and
f_{ij}=f_{ji}
. Therefore, to prove the statement of the theorem for all
n\geq 3
, we proceed as follows. Consider the linear endomorphism
v\mapsto\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!v}{T}
on
TM
as well as its Riemannian counterpart,
v\mapsto\nabla_{\!v}{T}
, with
g
given by (
20
) as usual (with
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(T,T)=-1
). This endomorphism is skew-symmetric, but its composition with itself, namely, the linear endomorphism
\nabla T\circ\nabla T
sending
v\mapsto\nabla_{\!\nabla_{\!v}{T}}{T}
, is
g
-self-adjoint, via two applications of the Killing condition:

g(\nabla_{\!\nabla_{\!v}{T}}{T},w)=-g(\nabla_{\!w}{T},\nabla_{\!v}{T})=g(v,\nabla_{\!\nabla_{\!w}{T}}{T}).

As
g
is positive-definite,
\nabla T\circ\nabla T
thus has a local
g
-orthonormal basis of eigenvectors about every point of
M
. Since the Killing vector field
T
is assumed to have unit length, it follows that
\nabla_{\!T}{T}=0
, so that
T
can be taken as one of these basis eigenvectors; as a consequence, any basis including
T
will necessarily be both
g
- and
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
-orthonormal. We will now further modify this basis to produce the frame (
35
). First, observe that the eigenvalues of
\nabla T\circ\nabla T
must be nonpositive: If
v
is a
g
-unit-length eigenvector with eigenvalue
\lambda
, then

(36)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\!\nabla_{\!v}{T}}{T}=\lambda v\hskip 14.45377pt\Rightarrow\hskip 14.45377pt\lambda=g(\nabla_{\!\nabla_{\!v}{T}}{T},v)=-g(\nabla_{\!v}{T},\nabla_{\!v}{T})\leq 0,

with
\lambda=0\iff\nabla_{\!v}{T}=0
. Furthermore,
\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}_{\!v}{T}=-\nabla_{\!v}{T}
by Proposition
1
, so that
\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}T\circ\nabla^{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}T
will have the same eigenvectors and eigenvalues. (E.g., the basis (
24
) had eigenvalues
0,-1,-1
.) Take now a
g
-unit-length eigenvector
v
with nonzero eigenvalue
\lambda<0
(such a
\lambda
must exist, as we are assuming that
T
is not parallel), and set

w\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\nabla_{\!v/{\sqrt{-\lambda}}}{T}.

By (
36
),
w
has unit length; but more than that, it is also an eigenvector of
\nabla T\circ\nabla T
with eigenvalue
\lambda
,

\nabla_{\!\nabla_{\!w}{T}}{T}=\nabla_{\!\nabla_{\!\nabla_{\!v/\sqrt{-\lambda}}{T}}{T}}{T}=\lambda\nabla_{\!v/\sqrt{-\lambda}}{T}=\lambda w,

that is
g
-orthogonal to both
v
and
T

g(v,w)=g(v,\nabla_{\!v/\sqrt{-\lambda}}{T})=0\hskip 7.22743pt,\hskip 7.22743ptg(T,w)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{-\lambda}}v(g(T,T))=0.

Another iteration brings us back to

\nabla_{\!w}{T}=\nabla_{\!\nabla_{\!v/{\sqrt{-\lambda}}}{T}}{T}=\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{-\lambda}}v=-\sqrt{-\lambda}\,v\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\nabla_{\!v}{T}=\sqrt{-\lambda}\,w.

\lambda

. Then so is

similarly,

Main Result

Lemma 1
.

The local orthonormal frame of Proposition

i.

Proof.

We use Proposition
3

Corollary 1
.

(M,g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}})

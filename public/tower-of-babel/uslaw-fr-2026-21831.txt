SUMMARY: The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), as part 
of its continuing effort to reduce public burden and maximize the 
utility of government information, invites the general public and other 
federal agencies the opportunity to comment on a proposed information 
collection, as required by the Paperwork Reduction Act of 1995. This 
notice invites comment on a proposed information collection project 
titled 2026 Ebola Passenger Disclosure and Attestation. The purpose of 
this information collection is to ensure that, consistent with the 
terms of a pending Order under the Public Health Service Act, public 
health authorities in the United States can confirm that individuals 
have not been present in the Democratic Republic

[[Page 67581]]

of the Congo (DRC) in the 21 days before arriving in the United States.

Background and Brief Description

    The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), National 
Center for Emerging and Zoonotic Infectious Diseases (NCEZID), Division 
of Global Migration Health (DGMH) requests an emergency approval for a 
New information collection. Section 361 of the Public Health Service 
(PHS) Act (42 U.S.C. 264) authorizes the Secretary of Health and Human 
Services to make and enforce regulations necessary to prevent the 
introduction, transmission or spread of communicable diseases from 
foreign countries into the United States. Under its delegated 
authority, DGMH works to fulfill this responsibility through a variety 
of activities, including the administration of foreign quarantine 
regulations; 42 Code of Federal Regulation part 71, specifically 42 CFR 
71.20 Public health prevention measures to detect communicable disease.
    On May 17, 2026, an outbreak of Ebola disease caused by Bundibugyo 
virus was detected in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) and 
Uganda. On May 20, 2026, the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) 
published Arrival Restrictions Applicable to Flights Carrying Persons 
Who Have Recently Traveled From or Were Otherwise Present Within the 
Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), Uganda, or South Sudan. 
Airlines are instructed to redirect flights carrying persons who have 
recently traveled from or were otherwise present within DRC, Uganda, 
and South Sudan in the previous 21 days to specific ports of entry.
    CDC within the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) issued 
an Order on May 18, 2026, to suspend entry into the United States of 
foreign nationals who were physically present in the Democratic 
Republic of Congo (DRC), Uganda, or South Sudan in the past 21 days. 
The Order was amended on May 22, 2026. An Order continuing the 
suspension of the right to introduce specified foreign nationals into 
the United States was issued on June 21, 2026, on July 13, 2026, on 
August 12, 2026, September 11, 2026, and most recently on October 11, 
2026. Additionally, on July 14, 2026, the U.S. Department of Homeland 
Security, in consultation with CDC, began restricting air travel for 
all travelers, including U.S. citizens and U.S. nationals, who had been 
in DRC in the past 21 days.
    This information collection accompanies the Notice and Order 
requiring that any traveler arriving in the United States from a 
foreign country submit an attestation confirming they have not been 
present in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) in the past 21 
days before arrival in the United States. Passengers must complete the 
attestation as instructed by the airline before being allowed to board 
a flight to the United States as a public health prevention measure to 
mitigate the potential spread of a confirmed ongoing outbreak of Ebola 
virus disease caused by the Bundibugyo virus.

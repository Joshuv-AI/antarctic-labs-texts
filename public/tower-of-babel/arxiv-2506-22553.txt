On a result by Meshulam
Heinz H. Bauschke and Tran Thanh Tung

Abstract
In 1996, Meshulam proved that every sequence generated by applying projections onto affine subspaces, drawn from a finite collection in Euclidean space, must be bounded. In this paper, we extend his result not only from affine subspaces to convex polyhedral subsets, but also from Euclidean to general Hilbert space. Various examples are provided to illustrate the sharpness of the results.

Two limiting examples

4.1
Impossibility to extend from polyhedral sets

The following example shows that we cannot go beyond polyhedral sets,

the “gap” between

3

That is,

.
Let

The case

The subcase

We now assume that

It follows that

5.2
The case
\varliminf_{n}\lambda_{n}<\varlimsup_{n}\lambda_{n}=2

No general conclusions can be drawn in this case—we shall present two examples that show that the sequence

(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
may have quite different behaviour.

5.2.1
A case where
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded

Assume that

(\forall{n\in\mathbb{N}})\quad\lambda_{2n}=2-\rho_{n}\in\left]1,2\right[\;\;\text{and}\;\;\lambda_{2n+1}=1,

(31)

and
\rho_{n}\to 0
.
Then
d_{n}=b
.
Moreover,
\gamma_{n}=0
, and

yields

y_{n+1}=d_{n}=b
.
Hence
x_{2n+1}=(\rho_{n}-1)y_{n}+(2-\rho_{n})a\to-b+2a=2a-b
.
To sum up,

x_{2n}\to b\;\;\text{and}\;\;x_{2n+1}\to 2a-b;

(32)

in particular, the sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded.

5.2.2
A case where
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is unbounded

Now assume that
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a relaxation parameter
sequence such that
|x_{n}|\to+\infty

(e.g., take
\mu_{n}=2-1/2^{n+1}
and recall

).
Using this, we create another relaxation parameter sequence
by resetting to

(\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}:=\big(\mu_{0},1,\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\mu_{2},1,\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3},\mu_{4},1,\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3},\mu_{4},\mu_{5},\mu_{6},1,\ldots\big).

Then for every

and

.
Because the stretches between consecutive ones grow longer and longer,
we deduce that

Thus,
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is unbounded with

\varliminf_{n}|x_{n}|<+\infty
.

5.3
Summary

To sum up, we’ve seen that
the case when
\varlimsup_{n}\lambda_{n}=2
exhibits quite subtle
behaviour:

•

The sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
may be convergent (even
constant, when
x_{0}=a=b
).

•

The sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
may be bounded
but not convergent
(see
Section

5.2.1
).

•

The sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
may satisfy

\varliminf_{n}|x_{n}|<\varlimsup_{n}|x_{n}|=+\infty

(see
Section

5.2.2
).

•

The sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
may satisfy

\lim_{n}|x_{n}|=+\infty

(see
Section

5.1.1
).

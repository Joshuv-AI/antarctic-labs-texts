Unboundedness for motivic invariants of birational automorphisms
Hsueh-Yung Lin, Evgeny Shinder

Abstract
. We introduce horizontal and vertical motivic invariants of birational maps between rational dominant maps and study their basic properties. As a first application, we show that the (usual) motivic invariants vanish for birational automorphisms of threefolds over algebraically closed fields of characteristic zero. On the other hand, we prove that the motivic invariants of the birational automorphism group of many types of varieties, including projective spaces of dimension at least four over a field of characteristic zero, do not form a bounded family, even after extending scalars to the algebraic closure of the field. For such varieties, we further show that their birational automorphism groups are not generated by maps preserving a conic bundle or a rational surface fibration structure, and their abelianizations do not stabilize.

Using the MRC fibration, horizontal and vertical invariants, and our previous results

In all cases where the corresponding result is false, there are nontrivial implications for the group of birational automorphisms

On the other hand, when the result is true, one obtains control over a truncated

1.2.
Applications

Let us now explain some applications of horizontal and vertical invariants to the nonvanishing of motivic invariants.
The exceptional divisors of the Hassett–Lai Cremona transformations

Informally speaking Theorem

Controlling the resulting motivic invariant for elements of

Finally, under the same assumptions as in Theorem

Horizontal and vertical motivic invariants

\mathbb{F}
we have
c(\phi)=0
.

Proof.

Our proof is the same as in
[
35
, §5]
.
We first define
\mathscr{M}(S)

for minimal smooth projective geometrically integral surfaces as follows.

(1)

If
S
is geometrically irrational or

K_{S}^{2}\leq 4
,
set
\mathscr{M}(S/\mathbb{F})\mathrel{:=}{\operatorname{rk}}({\operatorname{NS}}(S))\cdot[{\operatorname{Spec}}(\mathbb{F})]
;

(2)

If
S
is geometrically rational with degree
K_{S}^{2}\geq 5
,
define
\mathscr{M}(S/\mathbb{F})
using Hilbert schemes of curves on
S
of certain anticanonical degrees as in
[
35
, Definition 5.2]
.

We check that with this definition conditions (a) and (b) hold for minimal smooth projective geometrically integral surfaces.
Condition (b) is trivially satisfied in case (1),
and in case (2)
it follows from

\sigma\mathscr{H}_{j}(S)=\mathscr{H}_{j}(\sigma S)
,
where
\mathscr{H}_{j}(S)
is the Hilbert schemes parameterizing curves of degree
j
(with respect to
-K_{S}
).
Using condition (b), to check (a) we can replace
S_{1}/\mathbb{F}_{1}
by
\sigma S_{1}/\mathbb{F}_{2}
and assume that
\phi
induces the identity map on the base field.
Under this assumption any birational map
between minimal surfaces
S
,
S^{\prime}

satisfies condition (a)
by
[
35
, Proposition 4.4, Proposition 5.5]
.

The paper
[
35
]
uses a different normalization for
c(\phi)
, as a zero-dimensional class, not as a combination of exceptional divisors as we do in
[
34
]

and in this paper. Let us write
c^{o}(\phi)
for the invariant from
[
35
]
so that by construction we have, for every birational map

\phi\colon S_{1}\dashrightarrow S_{2}
acting trivially on the base field
\mathbb{F}
,

c(\phi)=c^{o}(\phi)[\mathbb{P}^{1}].

Now, for an arbitrary geometrically integral surface
S
we take a minimal smooth projective model
\phi\colon S\dashrightarrow\overline{S}

and define

(2.3)

\mathscr{M}(S):=\mathscr{M}(\overline{S})-c^{o}(\phi).

This is independent of the choice of
\phi

because if
\psi\colon S\dashrightarrow\overline{S}^{\prime}

is another such model, then
using (a) applied to
\phi\psi^{-1}

and the additivity of
c
we get

\mathscr{M}(\overline{S})-c^{o}(\phi)=\mathscr{M}(\overline{S}^{\prime})+c^{o}(\phi\psi^{-1})-c^{o}(\phi)=\mathscr{M}(\overline{S}^{\prime})-c^{o}(\psi).

A very similar computation extends
(a)
from birational maps between minimal surfaces
to birational maps between arbitrary surfaces.
For (b) we note that if
\phi\colon S\dashrightarrow\overline{S}
is a minimal smooth projective model of
S
, then
\sigma\phi
can be taken as a model of
\sigma S
, so that using (b) for the minimal surface
\overline{S}
we get

\mathscr{M}(\sigma S/\mathbb{F}^{\prime})=\mathscr{M}(\sigma\overline{S}/\mathbb{F}^{\prime})-c^{o}(\sigma\phi)=\mathscr{M}(\overline{S}/\mathbb{F})-c^{o}(\phi)=\mathscr{M}(S/\mathbb{F}).

\Box

2.2.
Horizontal and vertical motivic invariants

Let

\pi_{1}\colon X_{1}\dashrightarrow B_{1}
and
\pi_{2}\colon X_{2}\dashrightarrow B_{2}

be two dominant rational maps of
\mathbb{k}
-varieties.
We say that
\phi\colon X_{1}\dashrightarrow X_{2}

is a birational map between
\pi_{1}

and
\pi_{2}

if it fits into a commutative diagram

(2.4)

\textstyle{X_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{X_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{2}}
\textstyle{B_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma}
\textstyle{B_{2}}

for a birational map
\sigma
. Note that
\sigma
is uniquely determined by
\phi
.
We write

{\operatorname{Bir}}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})

for the set of birational maps between
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
.
We also write
{\operatorname{Bir}}(\pi)
for
{\operatorname{Bir}}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})
when
\pi_{1}=\pi_{2}=\pi\colon X\dashrightarrow B
.
We use the notation

{\operatorname{Bir}}(X/B)\leq{\operatorname{Bir}}(\pi)

for the subgroup of
{\operatorname{Bir}}(\pi)

consisting of birational automorphisms of
X
which descend
to the identity on
B
.

For
i=1,2
,
let
U_{i}\subset X_{i}
be the open complement of the indeterminacy locus of
\pi_{i}
.
Then passing to the generic points of the bases
we obtain a bijection

(2.5)

\begin{split}{\operatorname{Bir}}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})&\simeq\mathrm{Mor}_{\widetilde{{\operatorname{Bir}}/\mathbb{k}}}(U_{1,\mathbb{k}(B_{1})}/\mathbb{k}(B_{1}),U_{2,\mathbb{k}(B_{2})}/\mathbb{k}(B_{2}))\\
\phi&\mapsto\phi^{\eta}.\end{split}

The varieties
U_{1,\mathbb{k}(B_{1})}
and
U_{1,\mathbb{k}(B_{2})}

have the same dimension
n
,
equal to the relative dimension of
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
.
Let
d=\dim(B_{1})=\dim(B_{2})
.

Definition 2.3
.

The
horizontal motivic invariant

c_{\mathrm{hor}}(\phi)

is the image of
c(\phi^{\eta})

defined in (
2.1
)
under the forgetful map
{\mathscr{B}urn}_{n-1,d}(\mathbb{k})\to{\operatorname{Burn}}_{n+d-1}(\mathbb{k})
.
The
vertical motivic invariant
is defined by

c_{\mathrm{ver}}(\phi)\mathrel{:=}c(\phi)-c_{\mathrm{hor}}(\phi)\in{\operatorname{Burn}}_{n+d-1}(\mathbb{k}).

Lemma 2.4
.

Both
c_{\mathrm{hor}}
and
c_{\mathrm{ver}}
are additive under compositions:
if
\phi\in{\operatorname{Bir}}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})

and
\psi\in{\operatorname{Bir}}(\pi_{2},\pi_{3})
then

c_{\mathrm{hor}}(\psi\circ\phi)=c_{\mathrm{hor}}(\phi)+c_{\mathrm{hor}}(\psi),\quad c_{\mathrm{ver}}(\psi\circ\phi)=c_{\mathrm{ver}}(\phi)+c_{\mathrm{ver}}(\psi).

Proof.

Additivity of
c_{\mathrm{hor}}
follows from Lemma
2.1

and the additivity of the forgetful map

{\mathscr{B}urn}_{*,*}(\mathbb{k})\to{\operatorname{Burn}}_{*}(\mathbb{k})
.
Since
c=c_{\mathrm{hor}}+c_{\mathrm{ver}}

is also additive by Lemma
2.1
,

c_{\mathrm{ver}}
is additive as well.

\Box

Additivity of the invariants has the following useful consequence.
Let
\pi\colon X\dashrightarrow B
and
\phi\in{\operatorname{Bir}}(\pi)
. Consider arbitrary dense open subsets
U\subset X
,
V\subset B
. Write
\pi^{U}_{V}\colon U\dashrightarrow V
and
\phi^{U}_{V}\colon U\dashrightarrow U
for the induced dominant rational maps obtained by restriction of
\pi
and
\phi
respectively,
so that
\phi^{U}_{V}\in{\operatorname{Bir}}(\pi^{U}_{V})
.
The open embedding
j_{U}\colon U\hookrightarrow X
can be considered as an element
j_{U}\in{\operatorname{Bir}}(\pi^{U}_{V},\pi)
, and we have

\phi^{U}_{V}=j_{U}^{-1}\circ\phi\circ j_{U}
. Thus Lemma
2.4
implies that

(2.6)

c_{\mathrm{hor}}(\phi)=c_{\mathrm{hor}}(\phi^{U}_{V}),\quad c_{\mathrm{ver}}(\phi)=c_{\mathrm{ver}}(\phi^{U}_{V}).

We will often work with regular morphisms
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
,
in which case
c_{\mathrm{hor}}(\phi)
and
c_{\mathrm{ver}}(\phi)

have a clear geometric meaning
as described in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are regular in codimension
1
(i.e. defined on the generic points of all divisors), then for every
\phi\in{\operatorname{Bir}}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})
,
we have

(2.7)

c_{\mathrm{hor}}(\phi)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}E\in{\operatorname{ExDiv}}(\phi^{-1})\\
\overline{\pi_{2}(E)}=B_{2}\end{subarray}}[E]\ \ -\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\in{\operatorname{ExDiv}}(\phi)\\
\overline{\pi_{1}(D)}=B_{1}\end{subarray}}[D]\ \ \in\ \ {\operatorname{Burn}}_{*}(\mathbb{k}),

(2.8)

c_{\mathrm{ver}}(\phi)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}E\in{\operatorname{ExDiv}}(\phi^{-1})\\
{\overline{\pi_{2}(E)}}\neq B_{2}\end{subarray}}[E]\ \ -\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\in{\operatorname{ExDiv}}(\phi)\\
{\overline{\pi_{1}(D)}}\neq B_{1}\end{subarray}}[D]\ \ \in\ \ {\operatorname{Burn}}_{*}(\mathbb{k}).

Proof.

The first formula is a consequence of the one-to-one correspondence

\begin{split}\Set{\text{Prime divisors on }X_{i}\text{ dominating }B_{i}}&\xrightarrow{1:1}\Set{\text{Prime divisors on }X_{i,\mathbb{k}(B_{i})}}\\
D&\mapsto D|_{\mathbb{k}(B_{i})}\end{split}

with inverse defined by taking Zariski closure.
The second formula follows from the first one and the definition of
c_{\mathrm{ver}}(\phi)
.

\Box

2.3.
Rational Stein factorizations

The following is a rational version of the usual Stein factorization
[
22
, Corollary III.11.5]
,
a notion that already appears in
[
16
, Lemma 4.7]
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\pi\colon X\dashrightarrow B

be a rational dominant map of
\mathbb{k}
-varieties.
The
rational Stein factorization
of
\pi
is the factorization

\pi\colon X\overset{\widetilde{\pi}}{\dashrightarrow}\widetilde{B}\overset{f}{\to}B

where
\widetilde{B}
is the normalization of
B
in
\mathbb{k}(X)
.
We refer to
\widetilde{\pi}
and
f
as the
connected part
and the
finite part
of
\pi
respectively.
The degree of
\mathbb{k}(\widetilde{B})/\mathbb{k}(B)

in the rational Stein factorization
is called the
Stein degree
of
\pi\colon X\dashrightarrow B
.

Remark 2.7
.

The notion of Stein degree for the usual Stein factorization of a regular proper morphism was introduced in
[
1
, §3]
, where it was conjectured that
the horizontal components of the boundary for log Calabi–Yau fibrations have bounded Stein degree over the base.
This conjecture was recently proven in
[
3
]
;
see also
[
4
]
.
Note that by Proposition
2.8
(2), if
X
is normal and
\pi
is surjective, the Stein degree as we define it coincides with the one in
[
1
]
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\pi\colon X\dashrightarrow B
is a rational dominant map.
The rational Stein factorization in Definition
2.6

has the following properties:

(1)

\widetilde{B}
is normal and
f
is a finite morphism.

(2)

If
\pi\colon X\to B
is a proper surjective morphism, with (usual) Stein factorization

X\xrightarrow{\widetilde{\pi}}\widetilde{B}\xrightarrow{f}B,

then the rational Stein factorization of
\pi
is

X\overset{\nu^{-1}\widetilde{\pi}}{\dashrightarrow}\widetilde{B}^{\nu}\overset{f\nu}{\to}B

where
\nu\colon\widetilde{B}^{\nu}\to\widetilde{B}
is the normalization of
\widetilde{B}
. In particular, if we additionally assume that
X
is normal, then the rational Stein factorization of
\pi
coincides with the usual one.

(3)

Assume that
\pi
is a proper morphism
from a normal variety
X
.
The geometric generic fiber of
\widetilde{\pi}
is irreducible. It is integral if
{\operatorname{char}}(\mathbb{k})=0
.

(4)

Every birational map
\phi\in{\operatorname{Bir}}(\pi)

induces a birational map on
\widetilde{B}
, so that we have

{\operatorname{Bir}}(\pi)\subset{\operatorname{Bir}}(\widetilde{\pi})\subset{\operatorname{Bir}}(X).

Proof.

(1) and (4) follow from the construction.

For (2), let
X\dashrightarrow B^{\prime}\to B
denote the rational Stein factorization of
\pi
.
By definition,
\widetilde{B}
is the normalization of
B
in
X
, thus
by the functoriality of relative normalizations
[
45
, 035J]
, we have a commutative diagram

\textstyle{{\operatorname{Spec}}(\mathbb{k}(X))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tilde{\pi}}
\textstyle{B^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\widetilde{B}}

over
B
. Note that by (1) and the properness of
\pi
,
both
B^{\prime}
and
\widetilde{B}
are finite over
B
, so
B^{\prime}\to\widetilde{B}
is finite as well.
Since
\widetilde{B}
is normal in
X
, necessarily
B^{\prime}\to\widetilde{B}

has degree
1
. As
B^{\prime}
is normal by (1), it follows from the Zariski main theorem that
B^{\prime}\to\widetilde{B}
is the normalization of
\widetilde{B}
. Finally, if
X
is further assumed normal, then
\widetilde{B}
is already normal
[
45
, Lemma 035L]
. This proves (2).

For (3), we note that since
X
is an integral
\mathbb{k}
-variety,
the generic fiber
X_{\mathbb{k}(\widetilde{B})}
of
\widetilde{\pi}
is irreducible and reduced
[
45
, Lemma 054Z]
.
As
\mathbb{k}(\widetilde{B})
is separably closed in
\mathbb{k}(X)

by construction,
the geometric generic fiber of
\widetilde{\pi}
is irreducible
by
[
36
, Corollary 3.2.14.(d)]
.
The second statement follows from
[
18
, Lemma 2.6.4]

and
[
36
, Corollary 3.2.14.(c)]
.

\Box

Using rational Stein factorizations, the computation of horizontal and vertical
motivic invariants reduces to the case of
birational maps between rational dominant maps with irreducible
geometric generic fibers.

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
\pi\colon X\dashrightarrow B
be a dominant
rational map of
\mathbb{k}
-varieties and let

\tilde{\pi}\colon X\dashrightarrow\widetilde{B}

be the connected part of the rational Stein factorization of
\pi
.
Then
for any
\phi\in{\operatorname{Bir}}(\pi)\subset{\operatorname{Bir}}(\tilde{\pi})
, the invariants
c_{\mathrm{hor}}(\phi)

and
c_{\mathrm{ver}}(\phi)

do not depend on whether
\phi
is considered as an element of
{\operatorname{Bir}}(\pi)
or of
{\operatorname{Bir}}(\widetilde{\pi})
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\phi}\in{\operatorname{Bir}}(\widetilde{\pi})

denote the element identified with
\phi\in{\operatorname{Bir}}(\pi)
by Proposition
2.8
(4).
Let
U\subset X
be a nonempty Zariski open
such that both
\pi|_{U}

and
\tilde{\pi}|_{U}
are regular.
Since
f\colon\widetilde{B}\to B
is finite, a prime divisor
in
U
is horizontal over
\widetilde{B}
if and only if
it is horizontal over
B
.
By Lemma
2.5
,
this implies the middle equality in

c_{\mathrm{hor}}(\phi)=c_{\mathrm{hor}}(\phi|_{U})=c_{\mathrm{hor}}(\tilde{\phi}|_{U})=c_{\mathrm{hor}}(\tilde{\phi}).

The first and the last equalities follow from (
2.6
).
This proves the statement for
c_{\mathrm{hor}}(\phi)
,
and thus for
c_{\mathrm{ver}}(\phi)=c(\phi)-c_{\mathrm{hor}}(\phi)
.

\Box

We will use the following lemma in Section
4
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathscr{X}\to T
and
\mu\colon\mathscr{B}\to T
be morphisms
of
\mathbb{k}
-varieties
such that
every fiber of
\pi
and
\mu
is
geometrically integral.
Let

\textstyle{\mathscr{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{\mathscr{B}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mu}
\textstyle{T}

be a dominant rational map over
T

such that the indeterminacy locus of

f
does not contain any fiber of
\pi
.
Then there exists a locally closed stratification

T=\bigsqcup_{i}T_{i}
such that
the Stein degree of the fiber
f_{t}\colon\mathscr{X}_{t}\dashrightarrow\mathscr{B}_{t}
of
f
over

t\in T_{i}(\overline{\mathbb{k}})
is constant for each

.

Proof.

It suffices to show that there exists a nonempty
Zariski open
U\subset T

such that the Stein degree of the fiber
f_{t}\colon X_{t}\dashrightarrow B_{t}
of
f
over

t\in U(\overline{\mathbb{k}})
is constant.

Up to shrinking
T
,
we can assume that the non-normal locus of
\mathscr{X}

does not contain any fiber of
\pi
.
We can find a regular and proper replacement

f^{\prime}\colon\mathscr{X}^{\prime}\to\mathscr{B}
of
f
with
\mathscr{X}^{\prime}
normal by first resolving the indeterminacy (by taking the normalization of the graph) and then applying Nagata compactification over
\mathscr{B}
.
By further shrinking
T
, we can still assume that
every fiber of
\pi
is geometrically integral.
Note that by construction, the restriction of

\mathscr{X}^{\prime}\to\mathscr{X}
to every fiber
\mathscr{X}^{\prime}_{t}
of
\mathscr{X}^{\prime}\to T

is birational onto
\mathscr{X}_{t}
for every
t\in T(\overline{\mathbb{k}})
.
As Stein degree is a birational invariant,
we can replace
f
by
f^{\prime}
.

Let

\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{\widetilde{f}}\widetilde{\mathscr{B}}\to\mathscr{B}

be the Stein factorization of

Up to shrinking
\mathscr{X}
,
\mathscr{B}
, and

, we can assume that

\pi
,
\mu
, and
\tilde{f}

(and thus

.

3.
Computing motivic invariants

In this section we present several ways of computing motivic invariants, in particular we prove some vanishing results for motivic invariants of self-maps. These are
Vanishing I (Proposition
3.2
),
Vanishing II (Corollary
3.4
)
and Vanishing III (Corollary
3.11
).
Along the way, we establish useful
formulas to compute motivic invariants:
Proposition
3.3
in the regular flat case and
Theorem
3.10
for vertical invariants for a special kind of regular morphisms that we call birationally trivial in codimension one (Definition
3.5
).

In §
3.2
we recall some properties of MRC fibrations and relate them to motivic invariants.
The main results in this direction are Theorem

3.14

and Proposition
3.16
.

3.1.
Vanishing results

For
i=1,2
,
let
\pi_{i}\colon X_{i}\dashrightarrow B_{i}
be dominant
rational maps of

\mathbb{k}
-varieties.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that

\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}

are regular, proper, and generically
smooth of
relative dimension at most one.
We have
c_{\mathrm{hor}}(\phi)=0

for any
\phi\in{\operatorname{Bir}}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})
.

Proof.

It suffices to note that every birational map
between points or smooth proper curves is an isomorphism.

\Box

Proposition 3.2

(Vanishing I)
.

Let
\pi\colon X\dashrightarrow B
be a dominant rational map between

\mathbb{k}
-varieties.
Suppose that

\dim X-\dim B\leq 2.

If
\dim X-\dim B=2
, assume in addition that
\mathbb{k}
has characteristic zero.
Then

c_{\mathrm{hor}}(\phi)=0\text{
for any $\phi\in{\operatorname{Bir}}(\pi)$}.

Proof.

As we did in the proof of Lemma
2.10
,
we can find a regular and proper replacement
\pi^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to B
of
\pi
with
X^{\prime}
normal.
If
\dim(X)-\dim(B)\leq 1
, we use Lemma
3.1
.

If
\dim(X)-\dim(B)=2
and
{\operatorname{char}}(\mathbb{k})=0
,
then
by Corollary
2.9
and Proposition
2.8
(3), we can assume that
the geometric generic fiber of
\pi^{\prime}
is integral so that
the result
follows from Theorem
2.2
.

\Box

Our next goal is to
state a general result for computing vertical motivic invariants assuming that
\pi_{1}
,
\pi_{2}

are
regular and flat, see Proposition
3.3
.
First let us explain a simple formula for the usual motivic invariant
c(\phi)
in terms of valuations on function fields.
For a normal variety
X
, we denote by
X^{(1)}

the set of prime Weil divisors on
X
.
Every
\xi\in X^{(1)}
defines a discrete valuation on
\mathbb{k}(X)
, but not every discrete valuation is of this form. Discrete valuations on
\mathbb{k}(X)

arising from a divisor on a normal birational model of
X
are called
algebraic

[
28
, Remark 2.23]

and they admit a simple intrinsic characterization given in
[
28
, Lemma 2.45]
.

For a birational map
\phi\colon X_{1}\dashrightarrow X_{2}
,
between normal varieties,
both
X_{1}^{(1)}
and
X_{2}^{(1)}

can be considered as subsets of algebraic discrete valuations on the function field
\mathbb{k}(X_{1})\simeq\mathbb{k}(X_{2})
, identified via
\phi
.
For every algebraic discrete valuation
\xi
,
let us denote by
\overline{\xi}^{X_{i}}
its center in
X_{i}
, that is the closure of the image of the generic point of the corresponding divisor.
In these terms,
we have

{\operatorname{ExDiv}}(\phi)=X_{1}^{(1)}\setminus X_{2}^{(1)},\quad{\operatorname{ExDiv}}(\phi^{-1})=X_{2}^{(1)}\setminus X_{1}^{(1)}.

Furthermore, for all
\xi\in X_{1}^{(1)}\cap X_{2}^{(1)}
,
divisors
\overline{\xi}^{X_{1}}
and
\overline{\xi}^{X_{2}}
are birational.
Hence
by definition

(3.1)

c(\phi\colon X_{1}\dashrightarrow X_{2})=\sum_{\xi\in X_{1}^{(1)}\cup X_{2}^{(1)}}\left([\overline{\xi}^{X_{2}}]-[\overline{\xi}^{X_{1}}]\right),

where
[\overline{\xi}^{X_{i}}]
is zero if

{\operatorname{codim}}(\overline{\xi}^{X_{i}})>1
.
This is a finite sum because only divisors from the union
{\operatorname{ExDiv}}(\phi)\cup{\operatorname{ExDiv}}(\phi^{-1})
can make nontrivial contributions.
The dependence of the right-hand side of (
3.1
) on
\phi
is encoded in the union
X_{1}^{(1)}\cup X_{2}^{(1)}
.

We have the following generalization of (
3.1
).

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be any field.
Let

\pi_{1}\colon X_{1}\to B_{1}
and
\pi_{2}\colon X_{2}\to B_{2}

be flat morphisms
between normal varieties
and
\phi\in{\operatorname{Bir}}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})
.
We regard
B_{1}^{(1)}
and
B_{2}^{(1)}

as subsets of valuations in the function fields
\mathbb{k}(B_{1})\simeq\mathbb{k}(B_{2})
,
identified through
\phi
.
We have

c_{\mathrm{ver}}(\phi)=\sum_{\zeta\in B_{1}^{(1)}\cup B_{2}^{(1)}}\left([\pi_{2}^{-1}(\overline{\zeta}^{B_{2}})]-[\pi_{1}^{-1}(\overline{\zeta}^{B_{1}})]\right),

which is a finite sum.
Here

[\pi_{i}^{-1}(\overline{\zeta}^{B_{i}})]
denotes the sum of the prime Weil divisors on
X_{i}
in
\pi_{i}^{-1}(\overline{\zeta}^{B_{i}})
.

As the proof shows, instead of flatness it suffices to assume in Proposition
3.3
that
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
do not map divisors to subsets of codimension at least two.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.5
,
the vertical invariant is equal to the sum analogous to (
3.1

(3.2)

c_{\mathrm{ver}}(\phi\colon X_{1}\dashrightarrow X_{2})=\sum_{\xi\in X_{1,\mathrm{ver}}^{(1)}\cup X_{2,\mathrm{ver}}^{(1)}}\left([\overline{\xi}^{X_{2}}]-[\overline{\xi}^{X_{1}}]\right),

where

X_{i,\mathrm{ver}}^{(1)}\mathrel{:=}\Set{\xi\in X_{i}^{(1)}}{\overline{\pi_{i}(\overline{\xi}^{X_{i}})}\neq B_{i}}.

As
\pi_{i}
are flat, the closure of the image of a vertical prime divisor must be a prime divisor, so we have well-defined maps

which agree on the intersections and define a map

(3.3)

X_{1,\mathrm{ver}}^{(1)}\cup X_{2,\mathrm{ver}}^{(1)}\xrightarrow{\pi^{(1)}}B_{1}^{(1)}\cup B_{2}^{(2)}.

Splitting the sum (

)
over the fibers of
\pi^{(1)}

gives

c_{\mathrm{ver}}(\phi)=\sum_{\zeta\in B_{1}^{(1)}\cup B_{2}^{(1)}}\sum_{\pi^{(1)}(\xi)=\zeta}\left([\overline{\xi}^{X_{2}}]-[\overline{\xi}^{X_{1}}]\right),

which
implies the result.

\Box

Corollary 3.4

(Vanishing II)
.

For every rational dominant map

\pi\colon X\dashrightarrow B
, we have

c_{\mathrm{ver}}(\phi)=0\text{ for all
$\phi\in{\operatorname{Bir}}(X/B)$.}

Proof.

First assume that

The vanishing of

In general, by generic flatness, there exists a

By (

\Box

Definition 3.5

A flat morphism

3.2.
MRC fibrations

Let us assume that
\mathbb{k}
is of characteristic zero.
We will say that a variety
X
is rationally connected if it has a completion
\overline{X}

such that
\overline{X}_{\overline{\mathbb{k}}}
is rationally connected in the usual sense
[
27
, Definition IV.3.2]
.
When
X
is not proper
this is different from the definition given in
[
27
]
, but it gives us a birational property
more convenient for our purposes.
Note that by definition a rationally connected variety is always geometrically irreducible.

For any
variety
X
, there exists a rational dominant map
\pi\colon X\dashrightarrow B

called the maximal rationally connected (MRC) fibration
[
27
, IV.5]
, due to Campana and Kollár–Miyoka–Mori which is constructed as follows.
Fix a compactification
\overline{X}
of
X
.
Over the algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{k}}
,
B_{\overline{\mathbb{k}}}
is the quotient, in the sense of Campana, of
\overline{X}(\overline{\mathbb{k}})
by the equivalence relation generated by

x\sim y
if there is a rational curve passing through
x
and
y
.
Then
X_{\overline{\mathbb{k}}}\dashrightarrow B_{\overline{\mathbb{k}}}
canonically descends to the field
\mathbb{k}
.
Up to birational modifications, the MRC fibration is unique.

By the main result of Graber–Harris–Starr
[
20
]
,
the MRC fibration is characterized by the following two
properties

(1)

the generic fiber of
\pi
is rationally connected;

(2)

B
is not uniruled.

Note that both rational connectedness and uniruledness can be checked over the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{k}

[
15
, Remarks 4.2 and 4.22]
.
Hence the MRC fibration is also preserved under field extensions, if we define MRC fibrations for reducible reduced schemes of finite type by taking disjoint union of the MRC fibrations of their irreducible components.

Example 3.12
.

Let
X
be a real curve defined as the restriction of scalars:

X=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}\to{\operatorname{Spec}}(\mathbb{C})\to{\operatorname{Spec}}(\mathbb{R}).

We claim that the MRC fibration of
X/\mathbb{R}
is the morphism
X\to{\operatorname{Spec}}(\mathbb{C})
. Indeed the only fiber
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
is rationally connected and the base is not uniruled. Extending scalars we get the MRC fibration of a disjoint union of

\mathbb{C}
-varieties

X_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}\sqcup\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}\to{\operatorname{Spec}}(\mathbb{C})\sqcup{\operatorname{Spec}}(\mathbb{C})

Any birational map
X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}

induces
a birational map between the bases of the MRC fibrations

[
27
, Theorem IV.5.5]
, in particular we have

(3.5)

{\operatorname{Bir}}(X)={\operatorname{Bir}}(\pi).

If
X\dashrightarrow B
is an MRC fibration,
we define the
rationally connected dimension
of

to be

{\operatorname{RC-dim}}(X)=\dim(X)-\dim(B).

In other words,
{\operatorname{RC-dim}}(X)

is the maximal integer

such that
a general point
x\in X(\overline{\mathbb{k}})
is contained in a
d
-dimensional rationally connected subvariety of
X_{\overline{\mathbb{k}}}
.
If

is rationally connected, then
{\operatorname{RC-dim}}(X)=\dim(X)
and the converse holds when

is geometrically integral; Example
3.12
shows why this is a necessary assumption.

Lemma 3.13
.

If
\psi\colon Y\to X
is a dominant morphism with rationally connected generic fiber, and
\pi\colon X\dashrightarrow B
is the MRC fibration of

, then

is the MRC fibration of

Proof.

Since MRC fibrations are descended from the algebraic closure, we can assume that
\overline{\mathbb{k}}=\mathbb{k}
. Let us show that the composition

satisfies conditions (1), (2) characterizing MRC fibrations.
First assume that

; in this case
X
is rationally connected, therefore
Y
is also rationally connected by
[
20
, Corollary 1.3]
. Thus

is an MRC fibration.

In general, since

is not uniruled (because

is an MRC fibration), it suffices to check that the generic fiber of

is rationally connected. This follows by passing to
{\operatorname{Spec}}(\mathbb{k}(B))
and using the special case considered above.

\Box

, the image of the middle vertical map in the diagram is infinite.
Hence the image of the composition

H^{1}(\mathbb{k}(B),E[p])\to H^{1}(\overline{\mathbb{k}}(B),E[p])\to H^{1}(\overline{\mathbb{k}}(B),E)[p]

is infinite, which gives rise to infinitely many
E
-torsors
C_{i}
which are still non isomorphic as
E_{\overline{\mathbb{k}}(B)}
-torsors.
There are only finitely many
E_{\overline{\mathbb{k}}(B)}
torsor structures on a fixed curve of genus
1

[
43
, Exercise 10.4]
,
hence after removing repetitions we can assume that
C_{i,\overline{\mathbb{k}(B)}}
are pairwise non isomorphic curves.

Finally let us show that every nonzero class
in the image
{\operatorname{Im}}(H^{1}(\overline{\mathbb{k}}(B),E[p])\to H^{1}(\overline{\mathbb{k}}(B),E)[p])
has index
p
, namely it splits by some degree

Take any element

\alpha\in{\operatorname{Hom}}(G_{\overline{\mathbb{k}}(B)},\mathbb{Z}/p)

By Galois theory
\alpha

and by construction
\alpha_{L}=0

\alpha

\Box

The following lemma was used in the proof of Proposition
A.1
. It is a variant of
the inverse Galois problem for
\mathbb{Z}/p
.

Lemma A.2
.

The image of the map

(A.1)

{\operatorname{Hom}}(G_{\mathbb{k}(B)},\mathbb{Z}/p)\to{\operatorname{Hom}}(G_{\overline{\mathbb{k}}(B)},\mathbb{Z}/p)

is infinite.

The fact that
{\operatorname{Hom}}(G_{\mathbb{k}(B)},\mathbb{Z}/p)
is infinite, in other words that
\mathbb{k}(B)
admits infinitely many cyclic Galois extensions of degree
p
is well-known
[
18
, §16]
, due to the fact that
\mathbb{k}(B)
is a so-called Hilbertian field. It is however not immediately clear from the constructions in
[
18
]
whether the appearing extensions do not become isomorphic after passing to
\overline{\mathbb{k}}(B)
.

Proof.

We will construct infinitely many Galois
p
-covers over
B
that remain nonisomorphic after passing to
\overline{\mathbb{k}}
as varieties over
B_{\overline{\mathbb{k}}}
.
The proof is simpler if we assume that
\mathbb{k}
contains a primitive
p
-th root of unity, however we do not make this assumption.
In any case, by
[
18
, Lemma 16.3.1]
,

\mathbb{P}^{1}
admits a Galois cover
\beta\colon C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degree
p

from a geometrically integral smooth curve over
\mathbb{k}
. Let
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
be the branch divisor of
\beta
.

Our goal is to
construct a smooth projective variety
B^{\prime}
birational to
B
, a collection of surjective morphisms

\rho_{t}\colon B^{\prime}\to\mathbb{P}^{1},

parameterized by elements

t
of an infinite set
U

and a Cartesian diagram

\textstyle{\widetilde{B}_{t}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha_{t}}
\textstyle{C\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta}
\textstyle{B^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{t}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{P}^{1}}

satisfying the following properties:

(a)

\widetilde{B}_{t}
smooth projective
and geometrically integral;

(b)

the branch divisors
D_{t}=\rho_{t}^{-1}(Z)\subset B^{\prime}
of
\alpha_{t}
are pairwise distinct.

Once these conditions are satisfied, we can
take the infinite family of degree
p
Galois extensions
\{\mathbb{k}(\widetilde{B}_{t})/\mathbb{k}(B)\}_{t\in U}
. By condition (a), we get field extensions
\overline{\mathbb{k}}(\widetilde{B}_{t})/\overline{\mathbb{k}}(B)
.
Let us show that these field extensions are pairwise non isomorphic. If
\overline{\mathbb{k}}(\widetilde{B}_{t})

and
\overline{\mathbb{k}}(\widetilde{B}_{t^{\prime}})
were isomorphic as field extensions of
\overline{\mathbb{k}}({B^{\prime}})
, then since both
\widetilde{B}_{t,\overline{\mathbb{k}}}
and
\widetilde{B}_{t^{\prime},\overline{\mathbb{k}}}
are normal and finite over
B^{\prime}_{\overline{\mathbb{k}}}
, they are isomorphic as they coincide with normalization of
B^{\prime}
in the same finite extension of
\mathbb{k}(B^{\prime})
. However
\widetilde{B}_{t,\overline{\mathbb{k}}}
and
\widetilde{B}_{t^{\prime},\overline{\mathbb{k}}}
can not be isomorphic over
B^{\prime}_{\overline{\mathbb{k}}}
for
t\neq t^{\prime}
since they have different branch divisors by condition (b).
Thus by Galois theory we deduce that (
A.1
) has infinite image.

Now we construct the collection of morphisms
\rho_{t}
satisfying properties (a) and (b).
We first take
\rho\colon B^{\prime}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
the blow up of the base locus of a general very ample pencil
B\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
. By construction
B^{\prime}
is smooth and projective. If
\dim(B)=1
we require in addition that
\rho
has degree coprime to
p
.
We set
\rho_{t}=t\circ\rho
, for general
t\in{\operatorname{Aut}}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
.
For condition (b) to be satisfied we can restrict to any dense open subset
U\subset{\operatorname{Aut}}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
such that for
t,t^{\prime}\in U
we have
t^{\prime}t^{-1}(Z)\neq Z
.

Finally let us explain how we make sure that condition (a) is satisfied. To guarantee that
\widetilde{B}_{t}
is smooth it suffices to require that
t(Z)\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
is disjoint from the closed subset of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
parameterizing singular fibers of
\rho
which is again an open dense condition on
t
. For the fact that
\widetilde{B}_{t}
is geometrically integral we can argue as follows.
If
\dim(B)=1
, this holds because we required degrees of
\beta
and
\rho_{t}
to be coprime.
On the other hand, if
\dim(B)>1
, then
the generic fiber of
\rho_{t}
is geometrically integral,
so the fiber product
\widetilde{B}_{t}
is geometrically integral by
[
36
, Exercise 4.3.6]
using that
\beta
is flat.

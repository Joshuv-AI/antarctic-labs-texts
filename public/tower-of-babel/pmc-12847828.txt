Spatio-temporal Attention driven Rainfall Inference using Transformative Architecture (SARITA)
Authors: Ishan Sharma, Sarita Azad, Ankur Suri, Ali H. Omar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Short-term rainfall forecasting presents a complex spatiotemporal modeling challenge, critical for adaptation and mitigation of high-impact hydrometeorological events such as flash floods and cloudbursts. This study examines hourly rainfall patterns in the Northwest Himalayan (NWH) region of India, enhancing forecasting accuracy using spatial correlations. The region shows a significant decline in hourly rainfall trends over the period 2019–2024, with approximately 50% of the area exhibiting statistically significant decreasing trends. The analysis reveals that high-elevation regions exhibit stronger initial 1-hour lag correlation (>0.8) and a rapid temporal decay over 9 hours. In contrast, lower-altitude areas (below 600 m) display a more gradual decrease in correlation, suggesting broader spatial influence with less intense propagation. Across all locations, rainfall variability as defined by statistically significant correlations, typically dissipates within 6 to 7 hours. Our analysis further identifies a prominent belt of high rainfall coherence aligned along the central Himalayan zone, highlighting pronounced spatial homogeneity. To improve predictive performance, this study proposes an attention-guided spatial correlation mechanism integrated within a Deformable Convolutional Long Short-Term Memory (DConvLSTM) framework. The proposed model, termed SARITA, processes hourly rainfall and spatial correlations, with the attention mechanism dynamically using spatial dependencies. This integration enhances the model’s ability to generalize across spatial-temporal patterns. Model evaluation using statistical metrics demonstrates that SARITA consistently outperforms baseline models such as ConvLSTM and standard DConvLSTM, achieving a 25% improvement in hourly rainfall forecasts. Furthermore, the model also improves anomaly detection by 3.5% over the standard DConvLSTM model.

Significance statement

Accurate short-term rainfall forecasting is vital for flood prevention, agricultural planning, and climate resilience in monsoon-affected regions like Northwestern Himalayas. Traditional deep learning models often overlook spatial correlation–a critical characteristic of precipitation patterns–leading to reduced prediction accuracy. In this study, we propose a novel deformable ConvLSTM model enhanced with attention based spatial correlation, which dynamically captures irregular rainfall patterns and spatial dependencies. Our approach significantly improves hourly rainfall prediction performance across spatial and temporal scales, offering a powerful tool for disaster preparedness and climate-informed decision-making in vulnerable regions.

Introduction

Rainfall is a complex and dynamic weather phenomenon that varies significantly across both time and space. Distinct patterns, seasonal fluctuations, and geographically coherent zones of rainfall distribution are well documented1–5. The Northwestern Himalayan (NWH) region poses a distinct challenge due to its intricate terrain and highly variable rainfall patterns6–8. Furthermore, large-scale atmospheric circulation, orographic conditions and other synoptic weather systems, significantly affect rainfall in this area9–11. Considering the region’s vital hydrological role, it feeds ten major rivers that sustain millions of people downstream, accurately understanding and modeling the spatial patterns of rainfall is crucial for reliable forecasting12–14. The resolution of data in both temporal and spatial dimensions plays a critical role in accurately forecasting rainfall in this region, as distinct characteristics become apparent at different scales6,13,15. For instance, high-resolution hourly data on fine spatial grids can capture localized extreme weather events16. In contrast, monthly aggregated data is more suitable for identifying long-term seasonal and annual patterns and their broader impacts17,18. A thorough review of existing studies underscores significant progress in understanding the spatial organization of weather systems, particularly rainfall, and their temporal evolution.19–23.

Machine learning models have become increasingly prevalent in weather forecasting due to their ability to capture complex spatiotemporal dependencies. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and their variants are particularly effective in extracting spatial features from meteorological data. For instance, Pan et. al.24 employed CNNs for rainfall estimation by leveraging their spatial representation capabilities. Recurrent architectures such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)25 networks are widely utilized for modeling temporal sequences, making them well-suited for analyzing time series data. The Convolutional LSTM (ConvLSTM) model, introduced by Shi et. al.26, integrates the spatial feature extraction of CNNs with the temporal sequence modeling of LSTMs. These hybrid approaches combining spatial encoding (CNN) with temporal modelling (LSTM) have shown promise – for instance, a recent study27 applied a convolutional-autoencoder plus LSTM surrogate to forecast river discharge in a 4D-Var data assimilation framework. Similarly, deep-learning models have been used to emulate complex environmental simulation systems (e.g., global fire-emission models) by taking past and current environmental fields (soil moisture, temperature) as input to predict subsequent states28. Hence it can be asserted that such hybrid architecture enables the model to effectively learn complex spatiotemporal patterns through its nonlinear and convolutional structures.

Recent studies29,30 have demonstrated that incorporating spatial features enhances forecasting performance by enabling models to capture the underlying spatial structure of meteorological data. Highlighting the importance of spatial classification in environmental systems, Omar et al.31 and Zeng et. al.32utilized cluster analysis to demonstrate how data-driven spatial structures can enhance modeling accuracy across geophysical domains. The Local Moran’s Index33 is a widely used statistical measure for identifying spatial correlation, allowing for the detection of high-value and low-value clusters, as well as spatial outliers. Incorporating such spatial correlation measures has led to improved accuracy in forecasting hourly and extreme rainfall, as evidenced by the findings of Darand et. al.34 and Xu et. al.35.

Integrating rainfall fields with their corresponding spatial correlation (obtained from Local Moran’s I) enhances the model’s ability to capture complex spatial structures, thereby improving model accuracy29,30,34,35. However, a single snapshot of Local Moran’s I at a given time step offers only a static view, capturing spatial associations at that instant without revealing their temporal significance. This limitation is addressed by the attention mechanism introduced in Transformer architectures36, which enables dynamic, context-aware weighting of features. By assigning importance scores based on global context, the attention mechanism facilitates the identification of spatial dependencies that are most predictive over time. Research has increasingly explored the use of attention mechanisms to enhance forecasting performance. Tekin et. al.37 proposed a convolutional LSTM-based architecture augmented with attention, enabling the model to better capture spatial dependencies inherent in meteorological variables. Similarly, other studies like Bojesomo et. al.38 and Zhong et. al.39 have applied attention-based models to forecast rainfall and rainfall-driven traffic dynamics. By focusing attention directly on rainfall magnitudes, these models emphasize the magnitude of the phenomenon. However, this magnitude-centric focus can lead to unstable attention maps, especially at longer lead times. For instance, investigations into radar echo nowcasting and water-level prediction have observed that attention maps tend to blur and jitter over time, resulting in a 5–10% decline in forecast skill beyond the first hour40,41. Moreover, applying attention solely to only magnitude, forces the model to learn spatial correlations independently at each time step, which may lead to inefficiencies and overfitting. To address this limitation, we introduce spatial information directly into the attention module using Moran’s I. This enables the model to identify dynamically relevant neighbors rather than relying on implicit feature correlations alone. Embedding this signal allows the attention mechanism to remain anchored to meaningful spatial relationships, resulting in more stable and context-aware weighting of neighboring regions.

A key contribution of this work is demonstrating that supplying spatial-correlation information (via Moran’s I) yields measurable performance gains. Through ablation study, we show that proposed model consistently outperform their counterparts, achieving a minimum of 25% improvement in RMSE over the strongest baseline. This confirms that explicitly encoding spatial dependence helps the network capture coherent rainfall structures more effectively. This establishes spatial-correlation-guided attention as a principled and beneficial enhancement for short-term rainfall forecasting.

Accordingly, spatial correlation emerges as a pivotal feature for advancing the accuracy and robustness of rainfall forecasting models in complex topographic settings such as the NWH.

Results

The overall architecture (explained in methodology) is trained using a multi-step input-output strategy, where sequences of 3 hourly rainfall fields and corresponding Moran’s I maps are used to forecast the next 3 hours. The model processes both the rainfall and spatial context jointly, allowing information to flow through deformable and attention-guided layers before generating predictions.

The significant spatio-temporal heterogeneity inherent to the Himalayan region due to its vast geographical area and complex terrain poses considerable obstacles towards precise rainfall prediction. While rainfall data exhibits evident spatial correlation, this statistical dependence attenuates markedly beyond a spatial lag of 10–13 km2. Analogously, at an hourly temporal resolution, rainfall demonstrates substantial autocorrelation, which diminishes by approximately 50% over a 12-hour interval (Fig. 10). A key observation indicates that despite the relatively uniform mean precipitation across broad geographic regions, such as the central belt shown in Fig. 11, the underlying spatial correlation patterns exhibit strong local variability. At the various temporal scales, correlation clusters exhibit limited spatial extent, suggesting that rainfall variability is predominantly governed by localized atmospheric and topographic processes, even within regions demonstrating similar rainfall patterns.

This study introduces a spatially contextualized, attention-modulated deep learning framework tailored for high-resolution rainfall forecasting within the Northern Himalayan region, a domain characterized by complex orography and dynamic meteorological processes. The proposed architecture, termed SARITA (Spatio-temporal Attention-driven rainfall Inference through Transformative Architecture), synergistically integrates deformable convolutional networks with a novel attention mechanism informed by spatial correlation patterns derived from Local Moran’s I statistic. Divergent from conventional spatiotemporal models predicted solely on raw rainfall inputs, SARITA explicitly incorporates spatial dependencies to augment both representational learning and model interpretability. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that this integration yields a statistically significant enhancement in predictive performance across critical evaluation metrics–including Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), coefficient of determination (R2), and anomaly detection accuracy–exhibiting particular efficacy in capturing high-intensity rainfall events that typically pose challenges for standard forecasting methodologies.

A key innovation of this study lies in the design of an attention mechanism guided by spatial correlation maps, rather than relying solely on raw or learned feature maps. Conventional models typically apply attention uniformly across temporal sequences or convolutional features, which often results in unstable and diffuse attention patterns–particularly at longer lead times–where attention weights may jitter or lose focus. By anchoring the attention mechanism to a statistically grounded spatial metric, namely Local Moran’s I, the proposed approach mitigates such instability and directs model focus toward climatically relevant regions with greater consistency. While this attention mechanism improves short-term predictive accuracy, it does not extend the fundamental forecast horizon, which is ultimately constrained by the rapid decorrelation of rainfall fields after 6–7 hours. However, this improvement is particularly critical in the Himalayan context, where rainfall is highly localized and governed by complex orographic influences that manifest as spatial clustering. Attention map visualizations demonstrate a strong correspondence with high-value Moran’s I clusters, indicating that the model effectively internalizes meaningful spatial dependencies. This explicit spatial interpretability not only enhances predictive performance but also bolsters scientific confidence in model outputs–an essential attribute for operational applications such as flood forecasting and early warning systems in vulnerable terrains.

Limitations

This study uses ERA5-Land reanalysis data over a limited Himalayan domain, and the generalizability of SARITA to other regions has not yet been evaluated. Rainfall in the Earth system arises from three dominant mechanisms: convective, frontal/cyclonic, and orographic processes42. The present study focuses on a region where short-term rainfall variability is strongly modulated by geography of the region. Since SARITA is trained exclusively on rainfall fields, it implicitly learns the spatial–temporal dependence structures characteristic of this regime. While the model is expected to generalize well to regions with similar short-term coherence patterns, its performance may not directly transfer to domains dominated by large-scale synoptic or cyclonic systems. Although the current implementation of SARITA is univariate, the architecture itself does not impose restrictions that would prevent real-time deployment. The model demonstrates high fidelity in capturing historical extreme rainfall events (Fig. 9), indicating that the predictive core is already suitable for early-warning applications. Enabling operational nowcasting would primarily require a data layer capable of ingesting and standardizing multi-source real-time inputs. Integrating SARITA into an operational setting is therefore a feasible future extension. Addressing these limitations in future work may help further strengthen the model’s robustness and versatility.

Methods

In this study an attention based spatially guided deep learning framework to predict hourly rainfall in NWH region has been proposed. Primarily, deformable convolution, LSTM along with spatial correlation attention are the building blocks of the architecture. As discussed previously, due to the high variability and complex oreographic nature of the region, traditional models such as CNN or LSTMs fall short in capturing localized patterns and spatial dependencies which are essential for accurate forecasting. Thus the methodological focus of this work centers around advanced spatiotemporal architectures capable of learning dynamic relationship across both time and space.

As a baseline reference, we adopt the DConvLSTM-SAC model (proposed by Xu et. al.35), which augments the traditional ConvLSTM structure with deformable convolutions and a Spatial Autocorrelation Component (SAC). The deformable convolution layer enables the model to adaptively sample inputs over irregular spatial grids, improving its ability to extract features from complex terrains. Meanwhile, the SAC introduces a spatial attention mechanism guided by Moran’s I, a statistical measure of spatial correlation. This allows the model to weight spatially coherent regions more heavily during learning, thereby enhancing its ability to focus on climatically relevant areas. However, as discussed previously, the Moran’s I going as an input at a particular time step is inherently static. Inepting in model’s ability to adapt with the spatial arrangements of the rainfall.

Attention mechanisms have become a foundational component in modern deep learning architectures, particularly for tasks involving sequential or spatially structured data. Originally introduced in the context of natural language processing36, attention allows the model to dynamically focus on the most relevant parts of the input when generating predictions. In the case of rainfall modeling, attention enables the network to prioritize spatial regions that carry stronger predictive signals, such as zones of persistent rainfall or high spatial correlation. This is especially important in heterogeneous terrains like the Himalayas, where rainfall is highly localized and influenced by topographic features.

Model Architecture.

The proposed model builds upon the Deformable ConvLSTM framework by incorporating a attention mechanism guided by spatial correlation. The core architecture consists of stacked model cells, each capable of capturing temporal dynamics while adaptively extracting spatial features through deformable convolution layers. These layers allow the model to adjust its receptive field in response to irregular and topography-driven rainfall patterns, making it particularly suitable for the complex geography of the NWH region. This design enables the network to effectively capture both spatial heterogeneity and temporal evolution within the hourly rainfall sequences. The model diagram of the proposed model is shown in Fig. 13.

To further enhance the model’s sensitivity to spatial dependencies, a spatial attention module (ASAC), shown in Fig. 14 is integrated into the network. This module utilizes Moran’s I maps–computed for each input time step–as a spatial prior. Attention weights are dynamically learned and applied to these maps, allowing the model to focus on spatially coherent regions with higher predictive significance. The attention layer is shown in Fig. 15. Unlike traditional attention applied directly to raw rainfall features, this mechanism leverages physical insights embedded in the correlation structure, providing more stable and interpretable attention across time. The integration of attention also addresses common issues observed in earlier models, such as the fading of attention relevance over longer lead times.Fig. 14ASAC Module.Fig. 15Attention Layer.

Attention Layer.

The overall architecture is trained using a multi-step input-output strategy, where sequences of 3 hourly rainfall fields and corresponding Moran’s I maps are used to forecast the next 3 hours. Each stage of the model processes both the rainfall and spatial context jointly, allowing information to flow through deformable and attention-guided layers before generating predictions.

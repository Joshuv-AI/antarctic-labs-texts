Masculinization and physiological responses of rosy barb, Pethia conchonius to combined hormonal treatment
Authors: Jham Lal, Pradyut Biswas, Soibam Khogen Singh, Anand Vaishnav, Gusheinzed Waikhom, Dharmendra Kumar Meena
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe ornamental fish industry has the potential to enhance profitability through the development of high-value, high-demand species, where value and demand are influenced by factors such as size, coloration, and sex. The aim of this research is to evaluate the combination of 17α-methyltestosterone (MT) and letrozole (LET), an aromatase inhibitor (AI), in inducing masculinization in the rosy barb, Pethia conchonius. The fry of the rosy barb was acclimated to the gel-based diet for 5 days prior to beginning a 90-day experiment in a closed environment. The experimental design included a control (without AI) group and three treatment groups: T1 (M12.5+L150), T2 (M25 + L100), and T3 (M37.5+L50) mg/kg feed. The highest percentage of males (86.90 ± 0.59%) was recorded in the M37.5+L50 mg/kg group after 90 days, which was significantly different from the other groups. The histological sections showed suppressed ovarian development with atretic oocytes, while testicular development remained unaffected. Additionally, the concentration of antioxidant enzymes, including superoxide dismutase (SOD), catalase (CAT), glutathione S-transferase (GST), and glutathione peroxidase (GPX), were significantly increased in the control group compared to treated groups (P < 0.05). Instrumental color analysis on the basis of L*, a*, and b* values showed fed gel-based feed with M37.5+L50 mg/kg had significantly more vibrant color than those in the control groups (p < 0.05). Digestive enzyme activity, such as protein (70 ± 0.52), protease (0.054 ± 0.001), lipase (11.16 ± 0.28), and amylase (8.80 ± 0.78), varied among treatment and control groups. These findings suggest that incorporating AI at a concentration of 37.5 mg/kg + L50 mg/kg within a gel-based feed is a promising approach for masculinizing rosy barbs without significantly impacting their growth, color, or antioxidant activity. This approach holds significant potential for the ornamental fish industry by enabling the production of all-male populations.Keywords: P. conchonius, Aromatase inhibitor, Letrozole, 17α-methyltestosterone, MasculinizationSubject terms: Biotechnology, Drug discovery, Physiology

Introduction

Nowadays, ornamental fish are becoming increasingly popular for both their pleasing appearance and their enormous economic worth in the global export industry. The coloration of fish is attributed to the melanin present in their skin1, which in turn influences the ornamental fish’s economic worth. Males of several species of ornamental aquatic creatures tend to be more vividly coloured than females2. The price differential suggests that breeding all-male ornamental fish populations could be lucrative. The generation of greater-value male fish occurs through the transformation of phenotypic sex in ornamental aquarium fish3. Numerous ornamental fish species show greater sexual dimorphism; hobbyists tend to favor males over females because they exhibit greater robust coloration as well as, in several instances, superior fin development4.

Aromatase inhibitors are steroidal and non-steroidal chemicals that block aromatase enzyme activity, preventing 17β-Estradiol manufacture via the precursor called testosterone5. LET (CGS 20267), Femara is a non-steroidal triazole compound that is considered to be one of the most widely used and effective AIs discovered to date6. It is now the standard of care for treating hormone receptor-positive, metastatic breast cancer in postmenopausal women7. It may be useful for blocking the transformation of androgenic steroids into estrogens, which would reduce or eliminate the negative consequences of long-term usage of these drugs7. Serum concentrations of estrone and 17β-estradiol can be reduced by LET to undetectable levels6, and aromatase can be inhibited by 98–99%.

The synthetic androgen MT has an anabolic effect, which means it helps muscles grow and male sexual traits develop8. The use of hormones and selective breeding to create monosex populations for commercial purposes has attracted a lot of attention. It is possible to achieve the intended sex population using a variety of techniques, including sex reversal, manual sexing, hybridization, and the generation of super males. Aquaculture has made extensive use of sex manipulation, including hormonal sex reversal. Using steroid hormones has been shown to be an effective method of inducing functional sex in aquarium fish9,10. The greatest efficient and effective approach for creating all-male populations is sex reversal through immersion therapy with MT11.

An innovative approach to tackle the complex problem of nutrient leaching is the utilisation of gel-based feeds in the field of aquaculture. According to Aguilera12, the gel possesses mechanical stiffness and can be classified as a transitional material. A gel can be characterised as a solid material with high elasticity and a rubber-like consistency. Gels are a kind of colloidal material characterised by a three-dimensional semi-solid matrix that encapsulates a liquid phase. In a gel, the constituents that are dispersed and the medium in which they are disseminated are evenly distributed throughout the system, leading to a hierarchical structure that exhibits mechanical properties similar to those of a solid13. Nutritionally dense, rigid, and flexible gel feed is formed into animal-appealing shapes14 to speed up the feeding process. The use of gel-based feed offers exciting potential throughout the entire spectrum of aquaculture, from the tiniest home aquarium to the largest industrial fish farm.

The rosy barb, P. conchonius (Hamilton 1822), is a small, widespread freshwater endemic ornamental fish throughout India as well as other regions of South and Southeast Asia. Rosy barbs are also among the species that hobbyists want to keep in aquariums because of their hardy nature; commonly, they are known as rosy. P. conchonius belongs to the family Cyprinidae and the order Cypriniformes15,16. P. conchonius has invasive features such as high growth rates, trophic opportunity-seeking, and the capacity to adapt to a variety of different environments17. As opposed to numerous invasive tropical ornamental fishes, this species can survive in colder water settings (like the southwest of Australia) and thrive there. One possible ecological consequence for indigenous fish populations is competition for scarce resources as well as predation on other aquatic invertebrate groups18,19. The goal of this research was to examine the dietary administration of MT + LET with gel-based feed in exogenous androgen and AI induced gonadal masculinization of male P. conchonius production. MT and LET were used to induce masculinization. Additionally, the sex ratio and morphological colour measurement are very beneficial for the ornamental sector because their price and demand depend on this phenomenon.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics

The experimental animals were treated with the utmost respect and care, following the guidelines established by the Animal Welfare Division of the Ministry of Environment and Forests of the Government of India. The research was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of the College of Fisheries at Central Agricultural University, Imphal, Tripura (permission number CAU-CF/48/IAEC/2018/09a). This study was conducted in accordance with the ARRIVE guidelines (https://arriveguidelines.org).

Experimental site and fish-keeping

Early fry of rosy barb (Puntius conchonius) (mean length/weight = 2.86 ± 0.05 cm/0.254 ± 0.01 g) were obtained through random sampling of breeding tanks at the wet laboratory of the College of Fisheries, Central Agricultural University, Lembucherra, Tripura, India. Then, when the iron-free tap water in the experimental aquarium tanks had been aerated for three days, the fish were carefully moved to their new home. After acclimatizing the fish to the experimental tanks for four to five days, we fed them planktonic live food that we caught in the ponds. Ten fish were placed in every 40 L tank of water. There were 90 days of feeding the experimental feed to 30 fish in each of the three tanks. The daily feeding rate for stocked fish was 7% of their body weight, divided halfway between (09:00 h) and (16:00 h). Every morning, the leftover food and accumulated waste were sucked out of the tank to keep the water clean. The water quality results were consistently within the acceptable range throughout the entire study. APHA20 describes the standard process that was followed for determining the water quality parameters.

Experimental design

The research was conducted in three treatment groups and one control in triplicate and followed a completely randomized design. In this study, P. conchonius fry were fed gel-based feed with different amounts of AIs. The MT and LET treatments were based on previous observations21. The experimental treatments consisted of four different feeds, each of which was created using a combination of two AIs (LET and MT) applied at three different doses. The categories have been named as such:

Group 1 (T1): Feed supplemented with M12.5 + L150 mg/kg

Group 2 (T2): Feed supplemented with M25 + L100 mg/kg

Group 3 (T3): Feed supplemented with M37.5 + L50 mg/kg

Control (Control): Feed without AI

Experimental feed Preparation

In the formulation of the gel-based feed, precise measurements of essential raw materials were undertaken. In order to develop a gel-based feed, 5% calcium lactate was added to a mixture of fish muscle, lactogen, corn flour, a vitamin-mineral premix, yeast, and salt given in Table 1. To remove the oil content from the fish muscle, a thorough cleaning process was conducted in the laboratory, involving the removal of scales, skin, viscera, and head. After cleaning, the meat was extracted using a meat-picking machine and minced with a mincer. The minced meat underwent multiple washes with chilled potable water, using a meat-to-water ratio of 1:2. Each wash, lasting for 2 min, was followed by gentle squeezing of the meat in a cotton cloth to remove excess water. The resulting wet ingredients were blended with a mixer and added to the resulting paste (LET and MT) through a mix of water. The finished paste was then packaged in aluminium foil and sealed using a sealing machine. Crucially, the mixture was subjected to a water bath at 40 °C for up to 2.5 h as a heat treatment step in the gel-based feed preparation process. Subsequently, the gel-based feed was transferred from the water bath and stored in a deep freezer overnight to ensure it set properly.

Whole body carotenoid content

Carotenoid extraction followed by the method Torrissen and Naevdal26. In a nutshell, a mixture of acetone (10 mL), anhydrous Na2SO4, and fish tissue (0.1–1 g) totalling 2 g was prepared. This mixture was then centrifuged at 3500 g for 5 min, resulting in the collection of the supernatant, which was subsequently stored at 4 °C. The absorbance of the chilled supernatant was determined by using a spectrophotometer (Thermo-Scientific, India) and setting the wavelength to 476 nm. To determine the total carotenoids concentrations (g/g tissue), the absorbance at 460 nm was multiplied by the dilution factor, calculated as 0.25 multiplied by the weight of the sample in grams.

Histological examination

Visual examination of the external morphological characteristics served as the primary method for sexing the fish from various treatment groups27. At the end of the 90-day experimental period, histological analysis was conducted using three fish from each replicate of the treatment and control groups. The fish specimens were dissected in fresh condition and gonads were removed from the body cavity. Histological analysis typically involves fixing gonad tissues in Bouin’s solution for 24–48 h and then performing standard processing steps including dehydration. After washing with xylene, cassettes having tissues were kept in the liquid paraffin at 60˚C for 45 min twice, after that block preparation were performed in automated tissue embedder. The sectioning process was facilitated using a rotary microtome, specifically the Leica RM2245 model. Blocks were securely fitted into the microtome clamp and sections of 5-6micron thickness were made. The sections were then mounted on the labeled glass slide (thickness 1.2 mm, dim: 25.5 mm X 76.2 mm, Hi-Media) followed by heating on hotplate in order to fix the tissue on glass-slide. Tissue fixed glass slides were collected and kept in a tray for staining. DPX mount slides were aged for one day before microscopic examination. Stained sections were observed under the light microscope (Leica, model AX10) at the magnification of 10X, 40X and 100X (cedar wood oil used).

Antioxidant enzyme activity

The concentrations of enzyme antioxidants, such as superoxide dismutase, catalase, glutathione peroxidase, and glutathione S-transferase, were assessed in the serum of fish belonging to the various experimental groups (n = 30). Following the trial, fish were carefully harvested from individual tanks using a hand net. Subsequently, they were gently anesthetized with clove oil at a concentration of 50µgl. Serum was separated by centrifugation at 3,000 rpm for 10 min from blood samples that were collected aseptically from the fish’s caudal vein by a sterile insulin syringe devoid of anticoagulants28.

The level of SOD activity was assessed utilizing a spectrophotometric technique29, which relied on assessing the O2 facilitated reduction of NBT in an anaerobic environment created by NADH and phenazine methosulfate (PMS). For the measurement of CAT activity, absorbance was observed at 240 nm all over a 90-second period, with readings taken at 15-second intervals30. The enzyme assay was confirmed by adding sodium azide to the serum, which is a strong catalase inhibitor31. To quantify GPX activity, absorbance was measured at 492 nm, compared to a blank containing an additional 100 µl of O-phenylenediamine solution instead of the sample32. Spectrophotometry was used to measure GST activity with glutathione (GSH, 2.4 mM/l) and 1-chloro-2, 4-dinitrobenzene (CDNB, 1 mM/l) as substrates33. The GSH level was calculated by mixing 150 ml of Ellman’s reagent with 50 ml of serum, followed by measuring the absorbance at 414 nm after 25 min to determine the GSH concentration34.

Digestive enzyme analysis

Collecting intestinal tissue, we next homogenised it at 5% concentration using a cooled sucrose solution (0.25 M). The homogenate was centrifuged at 5000 x g for 15 min at 4 °C in a cool centrifuge, and the resultant supernatant was frozen in a sample vial for later detection of digestive enzymes. Standard curve analysis was utilised to determine differences in digestive enzyme activity between dietary treatments, and fresh intestines were employed for the assay35. Lowry’s36 was approach used to calculate the amount of protein in the gut. The casein digestion method is used to measure protease activity37. The lipase activity was evaluated using Cherry and Crandall’s (1932)38 technique. The amylase activity was determined by using the dinitrosalicyclic acid (DNS) technique39.

Analysis of water quality parameters

The analysis of water quality parameters such as temperature, dissolved oxygen, turbidity, conductivity, and alkalinity was monitored and recorded at 15-day intervals using a Sonde Multiparameter Instrument from Brannum Lane, USA. pH levels were measured using a pH meter, while alkalinity was determined through titration methods20. Fish sampling occurred at the 15 day rearing interval to assess parameters such as weight, length, survival rate, and mortality. These fish were collected in the morning, and their weight and length were determined with an electric weighing balance and a typical plastic scale, respectively40.

Statistical analysis

The data were subjected to analysis using a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with the use of IBM SPSS Statistics version 22 software. This was done in order to ascertain any statistically significant differences among the various treatment groups. A reconciliation was conducted at a confidence level of 95% (p < 0.05). The data were presented as the mean ± standard deviation. The statistical significance was assessed via an ANOVA, and if a significant result was obtained, a Duncan’s multiple comparison analysis was conducted.

There was a statistically significant increase in lipase activity between the (M37.5+L50) and control groups (p < 0.05). There is no statistically significant difference between the other treatment groups (p > 0.05). Intestinal amylase activity is not significantly different among the other groups (p > 0.05).

Discussion

Sex ratio during masculinization

The results of the combined effect of MT and LET on the sex ratio of ornamental aquarium fish provide valuable insights into the masculinization process. The sex ratio in the treated groups (T1, T2 and T3) displayed noteworthy variations compared to the control group. T1 (M12.5+L150) had a male-dominated population with a mean of 66.87% males, indicating a strong masculinization effect. T2 (M25 + L100) and T3 (M37.5+L50) also exhibited skewed sex ratios, favoring males, with 74.74% and 86.90% males, respectively. In contrast, the control group exhibited a more balanced sex ratio, with a higher % of females (56.02%). This contrast highlights the masculinizing effect of MT and LET combinations in the treatment groups. According to earlier studies, 25 mg/kg MT containing gel-based feed revealed the highest male proportion (84.72 ± 6.05%) in P. conchonius, which is significantly higher than other groups41. Karsli42 reported the 100% masculinization of the emperor red cichlid, Aulonocara nyassae, in both the 50 and 100 mg/kg MT treatment groups. Farias et al.43. has reported the highest range of masculinization 76.7% male of Astyanax lacustris when the fed 60 mg/kg MT concentration feed while the control had 46.7% males. Previous research conducted on various fish species has demonstrated that the inclusion of masculinization treatment (MT) in their diet resulted in a notable increase in the proportion of male individuals. For instance, in the case of tilapia, a study conducted by e Silva et al.44. reported a remarkable achievement of 94% male population when a dosage of 60 mg/kg of MT was administered. Similarly, Weiss et al.45. observed a significant increase in male population, with 72% of males obtained using the same dosage of 60 mg/kg in Rhamdia quelen. The correlation between escalating the dosage of MT and enhancing the pace of masculinization is not consistently linear. Indeed, our study suggests that treatments with doses exceeding the recommended levels, such as treatments 40 and 60 mg/kg, may result in impairments in gonadal development or infertility46. Additionally, these excessive doses could potentially cause diminished masculinization or, in certain cases, induce unexpected feminization47. The some other study reported by Gao et al.48. in masculinizing bluegill (Lepomis macrochirus) is noteworthy, with a masculinization rate of 70 to 75%. The present results suggest that the combination of MT and LET can significantly influence the sex ratio in ornamental fish. The observed variations indicate the potential for tailored hormonal treatments to achieve desired sex ratios in breeding programs.

Histological observations

Within context of the ornamental fish market, there exists a notable economic advantage in the production of specific species that possess a heightened commercial value and are subject to increased demand based on their size, coloration, and gender. Males and females in numerous kinds of ornamental fish have different colour patterns, notably males having more vibrant colours than the females. Hence, in this research endeavour, the prevailing steroids, specifically MT and 17β-estradiol, were administered as dietary supplements to P. conchonius. MT is commonly utilised because of its reputation as an efficient androgen in producing sex reversal in teleosts. In various fish species, notably the Japanese flounder, Paralichthys olivaceus, MT has been used to produce functioning males capable of spermiation49. The androgen to estrogen ratio in developing gonads is determined by the level of activity of aromatase, which catalyses the aromatization of and rostenedione and testosterone into estrone and 17β-estradiol, respectively7. This means that aromatase activity is critical for determining the final sex phenotype, with higher levels of expression of cyp19a being linked to ovarian differentiation and low expression levels being linked to testicular differentiation50. Complete functional masculinization of fish was achieved by blocking estrogen production using an AI51.

In the present study, the histological analysis of the ovaries in P. conchonius provided valuable insights into the reproductive physiology, shedding light on the impact of oral administration of MT and/or LET on ovarian development. In the control group, a diverse range of oogenic stages was observed, illustrating the natural progression of ovarian development. Notable structures included yolk vesicles (yv), yolk granules (yg), the follicular layer (fl.), late vitellogenic oocytes (lvo), vitellogenic oocytes (vo), and pre-vitellogenic stages (pvs). This variation in oocyte stages is indicative of a healthy and functional reproductive system in the control group, demonstrating the normal maturation process. Contrastingly, the ovaries of the fish treated with MT and LET showcased significant deviations from the normal ovarian architecture. The histological analysis revealed suppressed ovaries with the presence of degenerating atretic oocytes. This observation is consistent with the masculinization process induced by androgens like MT, which suppresses the development of ovarian tissues, leading to the degeneration of oocytes. The combined effect of MT and LET, further contributed to the inhibition of estrogen synthesis, reinforcing the masculinization process.

Surprisingly, no significant differences were discerned in the spermatogenesis process among the control and treated groups. Both control and treated fish exhibited the typical stages of spermatogenic development, including spermatogonia, primary spermatocytes, secondary spermatocytes, spermatids, and spermatozoa. This consistency in spermatogenesis stages suggested that the overall process of sperm cell formation remained unaltered despite the hormonal treatments. Notably, the histology of the treated fish revealed a substantial increase in the density of various spermatogenic cells, namely spermatogonia, primary spermatocytes, secondary spermatocytes, spermatids, and spermatozoa. This heightened density implied a potent stimulatory effect of MT and LET on the proliferation and differentiation of germ cells within the testis. The treated fish exhibited a robust population of developing sperm cells, underscoring the effectiveness of the administered hormonal treatments in bolstering the male reproductive process. In the present study, 86.90% males were produced at the medium dose treatment (M37.5+L50). Shen et al.52. reported the highest male 83.3% after the fed of 50 mg/kg gel-based feed thruogh oral administration of LET in yellow catfish, Pelteobagrus fulvidraco fish. The utilisation of MT for the purpose of inducing masculinization in yellowtail tetra (Astyanax lacustris) was investigated. It was observed that the treatment with the lowest dosage of hormone resulted in the highest proportion of males, specifically 76.7%. In comparison, the control group exhibited a lower % of males, with only 46.7% seen. The hormone concentrations administered were 40 and 60 mg/kg MT, respectively. In the present study, the higher concentration of LET + MT orally administered gel-based feed increased sex reversal in rosy barb, P. conchonius. Notably, we observed that almost half of the LET + MT treated rosy barb showed abnormal testes at 90 days while such abnormalities were not observed in control groups. Similarly, Qin et al.53. found that aberrant testes (ovo-testes) were present in LET (100 mg/kg) treated blue drum at 85 and 110 dph, but were absent in LET + 10 mg/kg MT (orally administered) treated yellow drum. There was no significant difference in the relative abundance of ovo-testes between blue drum given varying concentrations of LET. Using an immersion technique, researchers found that higher concentrations of MT resulted in higher rates of defective testes in a prior sex reversal experiment with yellow drum54. The findings hold immense significance in the context of aquaculture and ornamental fish breeding. Understanding the mechanisms that govern male sex determination and spermatogenesis is pivotal for enhancing breeding programs and achieving desired traits in ornamental fish species. The ability to manipulate the sex of fish through hormonal interventions opens avenues for selective breeding, ensuring the propagation of specific genetic traits.

Survival and growth performance

The consistent survival rates across various treatment groups in this study, ranging from 76.7 to 83.3%, suggest that the ornamental fish P. conchonius exhibited a remarkable resilience to hormonal interventions. These results echo findings in studies involving Aulonocara nyassae, where survival rates remained relatively stable despite hormonal manipulations42. In research conducted by Gao et al.48., the masculinization of bluegill (Lepomis macrochirus) was achieved at a rate of 70 to 75% through the application of LET, utilizing both dip and feeding methods. Notably, no instances of mortality, deformities, or abnormalities were detected in the fish’s appearance or various body tissues during the study. However, the observed contrast with Trichogaster lalius, where increasing doses of MT negatively impacted survival rates55, emphasizes the species-specific responses to hormonal treatments. Shen et al.52. demonstrated varying outcomes with LET, showcasing growth enhancement at a lower dose (20 mg/kg) while higher doses (50 and 100 mg/kg) had no effect. In the context of red tilapia, Betancur López et al.56. reported that survival and growth performance were lower in the MT treated group, emphasizing the importance of careful dosage selection. The study also highlighted the efficacy of anti-aromatase treatments, wherein survival and growth indicators were notably higher in these groups, underscoring the potential benefits of aromatase inhibition in fish growth modulation. Growth suppression was also observed in Atlantic halibut (Hippoglossus hippoglossus L) after a dose of 500 mg/kg fadrozole was given to the fish57. Rezaei et al.58. showcased positive effects of LET, with raised final body weight, daily mean, and diminished Feed Conversion Ratio (FCR). However, Pandit and Nakamura59 study on Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus) revealed contradictory results, indicating that the effectiveness of AIs can vary significantly between species.

Gonadosomatic index (GSI)

The combined effect of MT and LET on the gonadosomatic index (GSI) of ornamental fish provides crucial insights into the potential applications of these hormonal treatments in sex reversal and neo-male production. In treatment groups T1 (M12.5+L150), T2 (M25 + L100), and T3 (M37.5+L50), male fish exhibited GSI values of 1.44, 1.50, and 1.55, respectively. These values, though showing slight variations, indicate comparable gonadal development to the control group (GSI = 1.38). The marginal differences suggest that the combined effect of MT and LET does not significantly alter the sexual maturity of male fish. This finding is crucial, indicating that the neo-males produced through this method do not exhibit abnormal gonadal development, ensuring their reproductive fitness. In contrast, the GSI values in female fish displayed more noticeable differences across the treatment groups. T1, T2, and T3 exhibited GSI values of 9.99, 8.77, and 7.79, respectively, while the control group had a GSI value of 11.30. These values indicate that the female fish in the treatment groups had lower GSI values than those in the control, suggesting suppressed ovarian development. This suppression is attributed to the administration of MT and LET which hampers ovary development, thereby facilitating sex reversal. Comparing these results with previous studies involving tamoxifen and LET treatments reveals interesting patterns60. The GSI values in tamoxifen and LET treated groups did not differ significantly from the control, aligning with our findings. Other investigations have reported that MT and AIs can specially affect the oocyte maturation stages. Afonso et al.61. reported the administration of numerous injections of Fadrozole has a decelerating effect on oocyte development in Coho salmon (Onchorhyncus kisutch). Bhatnagar et al.62. also reported that LET effectively hinders the enzymatic conversion of androstenedione to estrone in the human body. Additionally, the study revealed that LET also acts as a blocker for estrogen-dependent uterine growth and the negative feedback mechanism on follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) secretion, which plays a crucial role in the development of the reproductive organs. Estrogens play a crucial role in the processes of development and oocyte maturation. Additionally, the regulation of maturation and ovulation is influenced by both estrogens and progesterone, as supported by Peter and Yu63, Lahnsteiner et al.64., and Yelghi et al.65. It is anticipated that repeated administration of LET would result in a deceleration of oocyte development. The GSI and oocyte diameter were not influenced by the temporary effect of LET on Serum 17β-estradiol production in the other two groups. In the present study, the consistency of GSI indicates the mild impact of LET on growth and sexual maturity, making it suitable for future applications in producing neo-males without adverse effects on gonadal development.

Colour intensity of MT on P. conchonius

The coloration of animals, including ornamental fish, is influenced by various pigments such as carotenoids, melanins, purines, and pteridines66. The skin coloration of ornamental fish is determined by many pigmentation cells, including melanophores (black and brown pigments), xanthophores (yellow pigments), erythrophores (red pigments), leucophores (white pigments), and iridophores (metallic pigments)67. The marketability of aquarium fish is significantly influenced by their visual appeal and coloration. In the context of P. conchonius, a noticeable blue coloration was observed in diets containing MT starting from the 90th day of the experiments. Among P. conchonius males, this particular sex is highly preferred. Karsli42, conducted a study in which it was observed that the group of fish who were administered MT displayed more vibrant coloration in comparison to the control group. The utilisation of instrumental colour analysis employing L*, a*, and b* values, revealed that the group administered with MT exhibited a stronger coloration in comparison to the control groups.

Similar studies have been conducted with other fish species. For instance, P. similis exhibited significant changes in skin color when fed with MT hormone and Carophyll® Pink for 10 days. The group receiving this treatment displayed the highest color score68. Karsli et al.69. reported enhanced coloration in the MT hormone group, and coloration intensity increased with higher hormone doses. In contrast, the control and 17β-estradiol hormone groups did not show significant differences in coloration compared to the fish’s initial colors. This implies that hormone treatments, especially with MT, can lead to male fish displaying vibrant colors, making them more commercially valuable. Studies have shown that the use of MT hormone in fish feeding can intensify the coloration of male fighting fish, transforming XX to XY males70. This method can enhance the aesthetic appeal of ornamental fish and contribute significantly to their market desirability.

Anti-oxidant activity of P. conchonius

Fish oxidative stress is a major problem, and the harm done by toxins in aquatic habitats can be measured by evaluating antioxidant enzyme activity. Androgenic analogues are hazardous to the liver because they produce an abundance of reactive oxygen species (ROS) when employed to create oxidative stress. Mitochondria are responsible for the majority of ROS production71,72. The detoxification of ROS can take place via the activity of antioxidant enzymes, including SOD, CAT and GPX. Additionally, ROS detoxification can occur indirectly by maintaining stable levels of glutathione (GSH) through the assistance of GST and glutathione reductase (GR)73. MT induces organic impairment in detoxification and antioxidant defence mechanisms through cytochrome P450 (CYP) the biotransformation74. SOD and CAT are the body’s initial lines of defence against oxygen toxicity and can be used as early warning systems for the presence of contaminants that cause oxidative stress. GST is a crucial phase II detoxification enzyme that conjugates with glutathione to form less toxic and more water-soluble molecules; both GSH and GST are involved in protecting cells from free radical damage and facilitating detoxification. Androgenic substances directly stimulate antioxidant enzyme activity and transcriptional genotoxicity75.

Antioxidants are of paramount importance in safeguarding the cellular and tissue integrity of fish by counteracting the detrimental effects of free radicals, hence mitigating oxidative damage. The health of fish can be negatively impacted by oxidative stress, which arises from an equilibrium between the generation of free radicals and the capacity of the body to counteract those using antioxidants. In our study, the highest catalase (−43.38 ± 2.87), SOD (172.04 ± 34.62), GST (0.56 ± 0.051), and GPX (43.48 ± 0.98) levels were found in the control groups. The values of catalase and SOD decreased with the combined used of LET and MT treatment diet groups. Zheng et al.73. reported significant decreases in antioxidant enzymes (SOD, CAT, and GST) and transcriptional changes (SOD and CAT) in MT treated GIFT tilapia groups at 7 days. The anti-oxidative index was found to be significantly greater in the groups exposed to 5 mgl−1 of MT compared to those exposed to 0.5 mgl−1 of MT. However, certain anti-oxidative enzymatic activities, such as SOD, GPX, and GST, were significantly reduced in the groups exposed to 5 mgl−1 of MT compared to those exposed to 0.5 mgl−1 of MT after a duration of 14 days. SOD, CAT, and GST activity revealed similar trends in our study, providing conflicting data compared to a study showing different responses under chlorpyrifos exposure76. Catalase and Glutathione peroxidase have the ability to synergistically function as scavengers of hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) and other hydroperoxides. Vieira et al.77. also reported a decrease in catalase activity was observed alongside an increase in superoxide dismutase and glutathione peroxidase activity in goldfish exposed to acute manganese toxicity. In our study, we observed a SOD, CAT and GPX activity are decreased in treated groups compared to the control groups. According to Atli and Canli78, it is suggested that there may be a compensatory drop in GPX activity as a result of CAT activity. The activity of GST was also shown to be considerably increased in the groups treated with 5 mgl−1 of MT, which aligns with previous findings in Leuciscus cephalus that were exposed to heavy metals79.

Digestive enzyme activities of P. conchonius

The enzymatic activity of fish digestive enzymes exhibit associations with several aspects such as feed absorption, physiological status, intestinal environment, and other relevant parameters80,81. According to the findings of Nadeem et al.82., the supplementation of exogenous enzyme preparation has been shown to augment the functionality of digestive enzymes, leading to enhanced apparent digestibility of dry matter and crude protein in feed. Additionally, this supplementation has been found to decrease protein content in faeces and greatly increase growth performance. Yamazaki83 reported the presence of hypertrophy in the granular cells of the intestines of masu salmon following treatment with MT. This observation implies a potential improvement in the processes of digestion and/or absorption. In the present study, MT can influence metabolism in fish, as it is a hormone involved in various physiological processes. Changes in metabolic rate or energy allocation can indirectly impact digestion. It is maybe affect the appetite of fish. Changes in feeding behavior can influence how fish digest their food. For instance, if a fish’s metabolism is altered due to hormone treatment, it may affect its feeding behavior and the efficiency of digestion. Woo et al.84. reported the distinct metabolic effects of 17β-estradiol and testosterone in the red sea bream. Testosterone promotes anabolic actions by enhancing hunger, improving food conversion efficiency, and raising the activities of digestive enzymes. Nian et al.85. revealed alpha-amylase and trypsin enzyme activity increased linearly with time up to 10 min, then dropped. The activity of both enzymes was linear with respect to enzyme preparation volume up to 0.6 ml. The standard assay calls for a 10-minute reaction time and 0.6 ml of crude extract. Both alpha-amylase and trypsin activities were interpreted as starting speeds under these conditions. Activity patterns of the main digestive enzymes were modified by the feeding interval in sex-reversed tilapia during the postprandial time (0–24 h after feeding).

Fish that were fed more than twice a day exhibited an overall rise in amylase activity in comparison to the group that was fed just once daily; however, this difference was not statistically significant. Ng et al.86. reported that the impact of MT on intestinal enzyme activity is selective, as seen by the unaffected functioning of disaccharides and amylase. Nevertheless, there were no discernible disparities in the behaviour of fish that were provided with two meals per day compared to those that received three meals per day. This finding aligns with the consistent amylase activity observed in juvenile hybrid tilapia87 and giant yellow croaker88 following varying feeding frequencies. The significance of amylase precise function has been shown as crucial in the assessment of glucose utilisation89.

Water quality parameters

Ornamental fishes possess remarkable adaptability to diverse environmental conditions, thriving in a wide array of habitats90. The growth and survival rates of aquatic organisms are closely linked to the optimal range of water quality parameters and the overall health of the aquatic environment they inhabit91. In the current study, the water quality variables were within the following ranges: temperature (23.2 to 23.25 °C), dissolved oxygen (DO) levels ranging from 7.4 to 8 mgl−1, turbidity ranging from 0.94 to 1.73 mgl−1, pH levels between 7.55 and 8.24, total alkalinity from 62.59 to 64.89 mgl−1 as CaCO3, total dissolved solids (TDS) from 193 to 202.66 mgl−1, and conductivity between 285.1 and 295.4 µs/cm, respectively. This closely aligns with the findings of Lal et al.92. also reported similar ranges water quality parameters for P. conchonius, emphasizing the significance of maintaining these conditions for optimal aquatic life. In order to facilitate the prosperous development, vivid pigmentation, and effective reproductive processes of P. conchonius. According to Pailan et al.93., the reported water quality parameters were as follows; temperature between 22 and 26 °C, dissolved oxygen levels between 5.2 and 7.4 mgl−1, pH between 7.2 and 8.1, water hardness between 324 and 380 ppm and alkalinity between 259 and 367 ppm. Sarker et al.94. also confirmed how important it was to keep the water quality parameters for Nile tilapia at the right levels. These parameters were a temperature of 22.16 ± 2.03 °C, DO levels of 5.45 ± 0.2 mgl−1, and a pH of 7.54 ± 0.37. These levels were good for supporting optimal aquaculture conditions.

Conclusions

In conclusion, the findings of masculinization in P. conchonius achieved by administering a combination of 37.5 mg/kg MT and 50 mg/kg LET through gel-based feed represent a significant breakthrough with promising implications. The combination effect observed in this study highlights the potential for more precise and controlled methods of sex determination and manipulation in ornamental fish breeding programs. Such advancements could lead to more efficient and cost-effective breeding practices, ultimately benefiting both producers and enthusiasts in the industry. Furthermore, this research underscores the importance of exploring novel approaches to hormone administration and manipulation in aquatic species. Future investigations can delve deeper into optimizing dosages, delivery methods, and long-term effects to ensure the health and welfare of the fish population. In summary, the masculinization observed in P. conchonius offers a promising avenue for advancing our understanding of fish biology and improving breeding practices in the ornamental fish industry. This discovery has the potential to revolutionize the field and usher in a new era of innovation and sustainability in ornamental fish production.

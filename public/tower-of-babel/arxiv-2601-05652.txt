Coset Shaping for Coded Modulation
Irina Bocharova, Maiara F. Bollauf, and Boris Kudryashov

Abstract
A new shaping technique called coset shaping for coded QAM and PAM signaling is introduced and analyzed. This technique can be applied not only to information bits but also to parity bits without incurring additional complexity costs. It is proven that as the length of the error-correcting code and the modulation order tend to infinity, the gap to capacity for the proposed shaping scheme can be made arbitrarily small. Numerical results and comparisons for the shaping scheme, along with nonbinary LDPC-coded QAM signaling, are presented.

I
Introduction

A typical communications scenario over the AWGN channel uses the uniform input distribution and
equidistant signal constellations, which lead to the potential gap of up to 1.53 dB to the Shannon capacity at high signal-to-noise ratios. The main idea behind the existing shaping techniques is to reduce this gap by minimizing the
average energy per transmitted symbol
[
13
,
17
]
while maintaining the desired bit rate. In such a way, the cost of delivering the maximum possible information is minimized. There are a few shaping methods used
in practice. These techniques directly or indirectly implement optimal or near-optimal nonuniform input
signaling.

The optimal distribution over the set of input signals can be obtained by using equiprobable non-uniformly spaced signal points. This shaping technique is called
geometric
. It was studied in
[
26
,
21
]
. Multidimensional geometric shaping can be efficiently implemented with
Voronoi shaping
techniques (see, for example,
[
8
,
24
,
28
,
23
,
20
]
). In particular, in
[
23
,
20
]
, Voronoi shaping for multilevel coded modulation was analyzed.

A so-called
probabilistic shaping
uses uniformly spaced signal points with different probabilities according to the nonuniform distribution.
Probabilistic amplitude shaping
(PAS) was studied, for example, in
[
7
,
22
,
15
,
12
,
25
]
. This type of shaping implemented in the form of enumerative decoding of constant-composition codes
[
7
]
and enumerative sphere shaping codes
[
15
,
16
]
was considered in combination with binary LDPC error-correcting codes. In these schemes, a distribution matcher (DM)
maps information bits to shaped bits, which are then systematically encoded by appending uniformly distributed parity bits. Linear layered probabilistic shaping, which extends PAS to probabilistic shaping of parity bits, was introduced in
[
6
]
and further developed in
[
19
]
. The shaping scheme in
[
6
]
processes codewords of a linear block code with
two DMs. The message bits are shaped with a conventional
DM and encoded systematically, and a so-called syndrome DM shapes the parity bits.
PAS applied after encoding with a nonbinary (NB) LDPC code was considered in
[
5
]
,
[
3
]
.

Comparison of PAS and Voronoi shaping used with multilevel coded QAM signaling over the AWGN channel was performed in
[
23
]
,
[
20
]
. The numerical results presented in these papers show that the Voronoi shaping in the multilevel coding scenario outperforms PAS.

In this paper, a new competitive shaping technique is proposed, called
coset shaping
. One of the distinguishing features of the shaper is that parity bits can be shaped in the same manner as message bits without increasing computational complexity. We use a combination of a linear code with a higher rate than the target transmission rate and a shaping code. The shaping code is a set of coset leaders of a linear code. The best coset leader is selected based on the criterion of minimum signal energy. We prove that for the proposed scheme, the gap to capacity can be made arbitrarily small as the error-correcting code length and the modulation order increase. Numerical results for one practical scenario are given.

This paper is organized as follows. Preliminaries are in Section
II
and the new coset shaping scheme is in Section
III
. The asymptotic analysis is performed in Section
IV
. Simulation results and comparisons are given in Section
V
.

III
Coset shaping

We introduce the
coset shaping
scheme for coded
2^{m}
-PAM modulation in its general form next. The numerical results in Section
V
are based on very long codes, suggesting that the complexity of this technique is relatively low.

The common structure of code-based shaping schemes considered in this paper is in Fig.
1
.
For PAM-modulation of order
M=2^{m}
, one of the bits assigned to each signal is a sign bit (s-bit), and the other
m-1
bits are amplitude bits (a-bits). Signal energy is determined solely by its amplitude. Therefore, a possible energy gain can be obtained by allocating additional redundancy for a desirable (non-uniform) probability distribution on signal amplitudes. Since the parity bits of linear error-correcting codes are always uniformly distributed, we associate information bits mainly with a-bits and parity bits mainly with s-bits. Depending on the target rate, the message length can be larger or smaller than the total number of a-bits. Thus, sometimes a part of the message bits will be transmitted as s-bits, while the parity bits will be assigned with a-bits.

The code design steps are:

1.

Start with a size

,

\mathsf{G}

2.

Choose a shaping code via the

3.

Replace the first

\mathsf{G}

\mathsf{G}

We achieve a shaping gain at the cost of some reduction of the code rate
R_{\textnormal{c}}=\nicefrac{{k_{\textnormal{c}}}}{{n}}
. Recall that
k
refers to the message length. The code dimension is denoted by
k_{\textnormal{c}}

\tilde{R}_{\textnormal{T}}=\nicefrac{{k}}{{n_{s}}}

Encoding with shaping includes the following steps:

1.

For a given message
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
and each possible combination of
k_{\textnormal{sh}}
shaping bits
\bm{u}_{\textnormal{sh}}=(u_{\textnormal{sh},1},\dots,u_{\textnormal{sh},k_{\textnormal{sh}}})

compute a candidate codeword

\displaystyle{\bm{v}}(\bm{u}_{\textnormal{sh}},\bm{u})=(\bm{u}_{\textnormal{sh}}~\bm{u})\mathsf{G}.

2.

Apply modulation
2^{m}
-PAM as described in Section
II-B
to each candidate
\bm{u}_{\textnormal{sh}}
, let

\bm{s}=\psi(\bm{v}(\bm{u}_{\textnormal{sh}},\bm{u}))

be the signal sequence corresponding to one of such candidates. We select the best candidate as one minimizing signal energy

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{u}}_{\textnormal{sh}}=\arg\min_{\bm{u}_{\textnormal{sh}}}\|\psi(\bm{v}(\bm{u}_{\textnormal{sh}},\bm{u})))\|^{2}.

Finally, the signal sequence for
\bm{u}
is
\bm{s}=\psi(\bm{v}(\hat{\bm{u}}_{\textnormal{sh}},\bm{u}))

\bm{u}

[
6

16

\bm{y}
denote the channel output sequence, that is,
\bm{y}=\bm{s}+\bm{e}

\bm{e}
is a length
n

1.

Decode

in
\mathscr{C}

as if no shaping is used.
Denote by
(\tilde{\bm{u}}_{\textnormal{sh}},\tilde{\bm{u}})

2.

The estimated codeword is

\hat{\bm{v}}=\tilde{\bm{v}}\oplus\hat{\bm{v}}_{\textnormal{sh}}.

The estimated message is

Notice that the proposed approach involves

[

20

23
,
20
]
. We propose and study a geometrical shaping technique that is better suited for coded modulation.

IV
Asymptotic analysis

We analyze next a random shaping scheme for coded PAM signaling over the AWGN channel with noise variance
\sigma^{2}
. First, we will establish the notation and assumptions.

Definition 2
.

An ensemble of coset codes

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{c}\triangleq\{\mathscr{C}_{c}=\mathscr{C}\oplus\bm{a}:\mathscr{C}\in\mathcal{C},\bm{a}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\},

(3)

is a set of cosets of
[n,k_{c}]
uniformly random binary linear codes
\mathscr{C}
, where the codes are generated by matrices selected uniformly from the set
\mathcal{C}
of
k_{c}\times n

\mathbb{F}_{2}
, and the shift

Consider a random shaping scheme with a

\mathscr{C}_{c}

. Elements of the construction are
2^{k_{\textnormal{sh}}}
subsets of
\mathscr{C}_{\textnormal{c}}
,
each subset contains
2^{k^{\prime}}

\mathscr{C}_{\textnormal{c}}
. In the previous examples

, but in the asymptotic analysis, we assume

. The rate loss
\nicefrac{{(k-k^{\prime})}}{{n}}\to 0

.

The generator matrix
\mathsf{G}_{\textnormal{sh}}
of

is a random subset of

\mathsf{G}
of
\mathscr{C}
. The remaining part of the matrix,

, is of size

. In Fig.

,

, the top

and

Recall
\psi:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathcal{X}^{n_{s}}
as in Section

, where

is divisor of

. Denote by
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathcal{X}^{n_{s}}=\mathcal{X}^{\nicefrac{{n}}{{m}}}
a preconstructed

,
|\mathcal{R}|=2^{k}
. In asymptotic analysis, it is assumed that only signal sequences in
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathcal{X}^{n_{s}}
are used for shaping. In other words, the message
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k^{\prime}}
is first encoded as
\bm{v}=\bm{u}\tilde{\mathsf{G}}
, and then
\bm{s}(\bm{u})\triangleq\psi(\bm{v}\oplus\bm{v}_{\textnormal{sh}}\oplus\bm{a})
,

, and

over the channel, iff

. If none of the

then the decoder makes the corresponding decision

.
Otherwise, an error event is declared.

The random shaping scheme is described by two random ensembles:

(

\displaystyle\Lambda\triangleq\Lambda(\mathcal{R},E_{0})=\{\bm{s}+\bm{e}:\bm{s}\in\mathcal{R},\bm{e}\in E_{0}\}.

(4)

Definition 3
.

Next follows our main theorem.

Theorem 1
.

In the product ensemble
\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{c}}\times\mathcal{P}
, for large enough
m
and
n
, there exists a shaping construction,
such that an arbitrarily small error probability in AWGN channel with parameters
(P,\sigma^{2})
can be achieved if the code rate per signal dimension
R=\nicefrac{{k}}{{n_{s}}}
satisfies

\displaystyle R\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\log_{2}\left(1+\nicefrac{{P}}{{\sigma^{2}}}\right)-\log_{2}\left(2\pi e~G(\Lambda)\right)-o(n),

where
G(\Lambda)
is the NSM of

\Lambda
, and
o(n)\to 0
when
n\to\infty
.

We outline the proof in the remainder of this section. Although we are considering a linear ECC, the coded modulation signal constellation is nonlinear. First, we will show that in the ensemble
\mathcal{S}
of signal points obtained from the ensemble of coset codes
{\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{c}}}
, the probability of any subset is determined only by the number of points in it.

Proposition 1
.

The ensemble of
[n,k_{\textnormal{c}}]
-coset codes
{\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{c}}}
determines the ensemble
\mathcal{S}
of signal points
\bm{s}\in\mathcal{X}^{n_{s}}
, where each
\bm{s}
has probability
M^{-n_{s}}
,
M=2^{m}
.

Proof.

We follow the arguments from
[
14
]
. We first show that in the ensemble
{\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{c}}}
, where symbols 0 and 1 of the
k_{\textnormal{c}}\times n
generator matrices are selected uniformly at random, the probability of any codeword is
2^{-n}
.

Indeed, there exist
2^{n(k_{c}+1)}
choices for the generator matrix
\mathsf{G}
of
\mathscr{C}
and shift
\bm{a}
. Each pair has probability
2^{-n(k_{c}+1)}
. Then the probability of a given codeword is
2^{nk_{\textnormal{c}}}2^{-n(k_{c}+1)}=2^{-n}
.

Next, we consider an ensemble of signal points obtained from codewords in
{\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{c}}}
. Given that the map
\psi

VI
Conclusion

A new shaping technique applicable to coded QAM signaling was proposed and analyzed. The introduced shaper belongs to the class of multidimensional geometric shapers. It allows us to shape both the message and parity bits of the codeword. Asymptotic analysis indicated that the new technique achieves capacity as both code length and modulation order tend to infinity, under some assumptions. The presented simulation results and comparisons for the long QC-LDPC coded shaped PAM suggest that the proposed technique outperforms known solutions, especially in the error floor region.

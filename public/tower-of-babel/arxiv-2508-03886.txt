The role of orbital polarization and spin-dependent electron-phonon scatterings in chiral-induced spin selectivity
Mayank Gupta, Andrew Grieder, Mayada Fadel, Jacopo Simoni, Junting Yu, Ravishankar Sundararaman, Yuan Ping

Abstract
Chiral materials exhibit unique spin and charge transport properties, notably through the chiral-induced spin selectivity (CISS) effect, enabling spin-polarized currents in nonmagnetic materials without external magnetic fields at room temperature. In this study, we investigate the microscopic mechanisms underlying CISS in a prototypical chiral solid, trigonal selenium (Se), based on a first-principles spatial-temporal resolved density-matrix dynamics approach, including electron-phonon scattering with self-consistent spin-orbit couplings (SOC). Our approach elucidates the interplay of SOC, structural chirality, and spin-dependent electron-phonon interactions in driving the generation and transport of spin and orbital angular momentum. We demonstrate that charge transport along the chiral axis induces significant chirality-dependent spin and orbital polarization, which shows a monotonic increase with higher chirality. Meanwhile, we show the orbital polarization generated in CISS has a weak dependence on SOC, unlike spin. Most importantly, we reveal the key difference between the CISS and colinear Edelstein effect (CEE) originating from spin-dependent electron-phonon scatterings, which explains the spin polarization increase with device lengths, a unique feature in CISS.



Chirality has emerged as a fundamental symmetry property with profound consequences in various fields of physics and chemistry. In particular, the chiral-induced spin selectivity (CISS) effect enables the generation of spin-polarized electrons from initially unpolarized charge carriers purely due to structural handedness, even in the absence of magnetic fields
Bloom et al. 2024
. This phenomenon has been experimentally observed in a wide range of systems, including DNA molecules
Göhler et al. 2011
;
Ray et al. 2006
, hybrid organic–inorganic perovskites
Ahn et al. 2017
;
Wang et al. 2023
, and inorganic crystals and molecules
Furukawa et al. 2017
;
Naaman et al. 2019
, which holds promise for novel spintronic functionalities such as spin filters, memory devices, and sensors
Naaman and Waldeck 2015
.

Despite growing experimental evidence
Moharana et al. 2025
;
Adhikari et al. 2023
;
Qian et al. 2022
;
Eckvahl et al. 2023
;
Safari et al. 2024
, the microscopic mechanisms driving CISS remain under debate. The interplay among spin–orbit coupling (SOC), structural chirality, and lattice vibrations (chiral phonons), has been proposed as the key ingredients, which facilitate the angular momentum transfer. Yet a quantitative evaluation from first-priniciples quantum transport is lacking
Wang et al. 2024
;
Ohe et al. 2024
;
Kim et al. 2023
, which is urgently needed for an unbiased determination of mechanisms. In parallel, spin accumulation arising from current-driven spin-orbit effects, such as the Edelstein effect, is also known to generate spin polarization through spin-momentum locking, in non-centrosymmetric systems. The distinction between CISS and the colinear Edelstein effect in chiral solids is not elucidated either experimentally or theoretically.

To address this challenge, we employ a transport framework that combines first-principles density-matrix dynamics (FPDMD)
Xu et al. 2020
;
Xu et al. 2021
;
Habib et al. 2022
;
Xu et al. 2024
;
Xu and Ping 2024
;
Simoni et al. 2025
with a semiclassical Wigner function formalism, capable of describing both coherent ballistic transport and incoherent scattering effects within a unified approach
Fadel et al. 2025
. This formalism enables both spatial and temporal-resolved evaluation of spin and orbital dynamics driven by out-of-equilibrium electron-phonon interactions. Additionally, it enables us to analyze the charge and spin transport at realistic device length scales.

As a prototypical system, we study trigonal selenium (
t-
Se), which is a crystal with well-defined helical chains and robust structural chirality
Cherin and Unger 1967
;
Saeva et al. 1977
. Its simple structure, combined with moderate SOC strength and symmetry-protected quasi–persistent spin helix along the chiral axis, makes it an excellent candidate for exploring chirality-driven spin transport mechanisms. We systematically examine the generation of spin and orbital polarization through charge transport along the chiral axis, compare coherent and incoherent regimes, and quantify the respective roles of CISS and CEE mechanisms, in particular the comparison between a spin-independent single relaxation time and spin-dependent electron-phonon scatterings. Our study provides quantitative insights into the microscopic picture of
spin generation, relaxation and transport in a nonmagnetic chiral system, and offers guiding principles for the design of next-generation room-temperature spintronic materials.

Although RTA captures spin accumulation from spin momentum locking, it oversimplifies transport by assuming uniform, spin-independent scatterings. It neglects other crucial effects, such as scatterings between electrons and phonons, and the SOC-driven scattering process. These are key to reproducing realistic spatially varying spin profiles, such as spin accumulation, diffusion, and relaxation observed experimentally for CISS-active materials

In summary, we present a unified first-principles framework to investigate spin and orbital transport phenomena in structurally chiral materials, exemplified by trigonal selenium (
t-
Se). By combining first-principles density-matrix dynamics with a Wigner function formalism and incorporating electron-phonon (e-ph) scatterings via a Lindbladian approach, we capture both coherent and incoherent transport regimes on realistic device length scales. Our simulations reveal that charge transport along the chiral axis leads to significant spin and orbital polarization driven by chiral symmetry, i.e., both spin and orbital polarization reverse the sign for opposite chirality. Under coherent ballistic transport conditions, we observe spin polarizations that are sensitive to the structural chirality and the strength of spin-orbit coupling (SOC). We demonstrate that spin polarization increases monotonically with SOC strength, while orbital polarization remains insensitive to SOC. Through benchmarking the real-time FPDMD simulations against analytic results from the collinear Edelstein effect (CEE), we confirm the relaxation time approximation (RTA) is sufficient for describing CEE with a moderate SOC.

When e-phonon scattering is explicitly included in the incoherent dynamics and transport, we find that spin polarization no longer remains constant spatially as in the RTA, but instead builds up spatially along the chiral axis due to spin-dependent scattering processes, which is one primary signature of the CISS effect. Rapid decay of spin density near the source region is observed, followed by an increase in spin polarization with the device length. We found that the early-time rapid spin decay results from spin relaxation due to SOC and e-ph couplings, by confirming the same decay rate between spin injection and charge injection (CISS).
Our findings provide a comprehensive and quantitative understanding of spin and orbital dynamics in chiral materials, offering insights for the design of advanced spintronic devices with enhanced spin and orbital manipulation capabilities.

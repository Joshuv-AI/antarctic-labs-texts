On (pre)-approach spaces within convergence approach spaces
Frédéric Mynard

Abstract
. The purpose of this note is to illustrate a parallel between (pre)topologies when seen among convergence spaces and (pre)approach spaces when seen among convergence approach spaces, that appears to be a more complete parallel than in the traditional presentation of these approach structures. This sheds some light on the reflector from the category of convergence approach spaces to that of approach spaces and even on the structure of approach spaces as such. This point of view allows for a characterization of approach spaces among convergence approach spaces represented as pointfree convergence frames as in [ 18 ] .

1.
Introduction and preliminaries

The purpose of this note is to illustrate a parallel between (pre)topologies
when seen among convergence spaces and (pre)approach spaces when seen
among convergence approach spaces, that appears to be a more complete
parallel than in the traditional presentation of these approach structures.
This sheds some light on the reflector from the category of convergence
approach spaces to that of approach spaces and even on the structure
of approach spaces as such. To spell this out, let us walk through
some somewhat lengthy preliminaries (because a very important aspect
is a reframing of the traditional presentation of convergence approach
space, we take some pain in doing this reframing in some details,
even for some known results).

1.1.
Convergence spaces

If
X
is a set, we denote its powerset by
\mathbb{P}X
and its
set of finite subsets by
[X]^{<\infty}
. The set of (set-theoretic)
filters on
X
is denoted
\mathbb{F}X
and the set of ultrafilters
on
X
is denoted
\mathbb{U}X
. If
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{P}X
is a
family of subsets of
X
, we will consider the following associated
families: its
isotone hull

\mathcal{A}^{\uparrow}=\{B\subset X:\exists A\in\mathcal{A},A\subset B\},

its
grill

\mathcal{A}^{\#}=\left\{B\subset X:\forall A\in\mathcal{A},B\cap A\neq\emptyset\right\},

its
completion by finite intersections

\mathcal{A}^{\cap}=\{\bigcap\mathcal{B}:\mathcal{B}\in[\mathcal{A}]^{<\infty}\}.

When
\mathcal{A}=\{A\}
, we abridge
A^{\uparrow}
for the principal filter

\{A\}^{\uparrow}
of
A
. In particular, if
x\in X
then
\{x\}^{\uparrow}

denotes the principal ultrafilter generated by
x
.

If
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{P}X
, we say that
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}

mesh
,
in symbols
\mathcal{A}\#\mathcal{B}
, if
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{B}^{\#}
, equivalently,
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}^{\#}
.
We say that
\mathcal{A}

is finer than
\mathcal{B}

or that
\mathcal{B}

is
coarser than
\mathcal{A}
,
in symbols

\mathcal{A}\geq\mathcal{B}
,
if for every
B\in\mathcal{B}

there is
A\in\mathcal{A}
with
A\subset B
, that is, if
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}^{\uparrow}
.
For every filter
\mathcal{F}
on
X
,
\beta(\mathcal{F})=\{\mathcal{U}\in\mathbb{U}X:\mathcal{U}\geq\mathcal{F}\}

denotes the set of finer ultrafilters. When
\mathcal{F}=A^{\uparrow}
is
principal, we abridge to
\beta A=\{\mathcal{U}\in\mathbb{U}X:A\in\mathcal{U}\}
.

A
convergence
\xi
on a set
X
is a relation between points
of
X
and filters on
X
, denoted
x\in\lim\nolimits_{\xi}\mathcal{F}
if
(x,\mathcal{F})\in\xi

and interpreted as

is a limit point for
\mathcal{F}
in
\xi
, satisfying

(centered)

x\in\lim\nolimits_{\xi}\{x\}^{\uparrow}

for every
x\in X
and

(monotone)

\mathcal{F}\leq\mathcal{G}\Longrightarrow\lim\nolimits_{\xi}\mathcal{F}\subset\lim\nolimits_{\xi}\mathcal{G},

for all
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\in\mathbb{F}X
. The pair
(X,\xi)
is then called

A map

between two convergence spaces
(X,\xi)
and
(Y,\tau)

is
continuous
, in symbols
f\in\mathscr{C}(\xi,\tau)
, if
f(x)\in\lim\nolimits_{\tau}f[\mathcal{F}]

whenever
x\in\lim\nolimits_{\xi}\mathcal{F}
, where

is the image filter. Let
\mathsf{Conv}
denote the category of convergence
spaces and continuous maps. If
\xi,\sigma
are two convergences
on
X

\xi
is

\sigma

or that

is

,
that is, if
\lim_{\xi}\mathcal{F}\subset\lim_{\sigma}\mathcal{F}
for every filter

\mathcal{F}
on
X
.

If a convergence additionally satisfies

(finite depth)

\lim\nolimits_{\xi}(\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G})=\lim\nolimits_{\xi}\mathcal{F}\cap\lim\nolimits_{\xi}\mathcal{G}

for every
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\in\mathbb{F}X

has finite
depth

\xi

When restricted to principal filters, (

given by

\operatorname{adh}_{\xi}A=\operatorname{adh}_{\xi}\{A\}^{\uparrow}=\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{G}^{\#}}\lim\nolimits_{\xi}\mathcal{G}=\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{G}}\lim\nolimits_{\xi}\mathcal{G}=\bigcup_{\mathcal{U}\in\beta A}\lim\nolimits_{\xi}\mathcal{U}.

The full subcategory of
\mathsf{Conv}
formed by pretopological spaces (and

\mathsf{PrTop}
, is concretely reflective and
the reflector
\operatorname{S}_{0}

\lim\nolimits_{\operatorname{S}_{0}\xi}\mathcal{F}=\bigcap_{A\in\mathcal{F}^{\#}}\operatorname{adh}_{\xi}A,

so that the principal adherence determines the pretopological reflection.

but not conversely. In contrast to
\operatorname{cl}_{\xi}

is in general non-idempotent because

A pretopology is a topology if and only if its principal adherence
operator is idempotent, in which case
\operatorname{adh}_{\xi}=\operatorname{cl}_{\xi}
. Moreover,
the reflector
\operatorname{T}
from
\mathsf{Conv}
onto
\mathsf{Top}
is given by

(1.4)

\lim\nolimits_{\operatorname{T}\xi}\mathcal{F}=\bigcap_{A\in\mathcal{F}^{\#}}\operatorname{cl}_{\xi}A.

1.2.
Convergence approach spaces

Traditionally, a
convergence approach space
(X,\lambda)

is given by a function
\lambda:\mathbb{F}X\to[0,\infty]^{X}
satisfying

\mathcal{F}\leq\mathcal{G}\Longrightarrow\lambda(\mathcal{F})\geq\lambda(\mathcal{G})

for the pointwise order of
[0,\infty]^{X}
induced by the usual
order on
[0,\infty]
, and

\lambda(\{x\}^{\uparrow})(x)=0,

for every
x\in X
. The interpretation is that
\lambda(\mathcal{F})(x)

measures how well
\mathcal{F}
converges to
x
, with
\lambda(\mathcal{F})(x)=0

indicating full convergence while
\lambda(\mathcal{F})(x)=\infty
indicating
no convergence. Additionally, an analog of (
finite depth
)
is usually required, namely that

(1.5)

\lambda(\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G})=\lambda(\mathcal{F})\vee\lambda(\mathcal{G}),

for every
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\in\mathbb{F}X
, though we will treat it as an additional
property just like in
\mathsf{Conv}
.

A
contraction
is a function
f:(X,\lambda_{X})\to(Y,\lambda_{Y})

satisfying

\lambda_{Y}(f[\mathcal{F}])\circ f\leq\lambda_{X}(\mathcal{F})

for the pointwise order induced by that of
[0,\infty]
, for every

\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{F}X
.

In order to more easily draw a complete parallel with
\mathsf{Top}
,
\mathsf{PrTop}

and
\mathsf{Conv}
, it is more convenient to consider the opposite order
on
[0,\infty]
so that
0
is the greatest element and
\infty

is the smallest element. It is then more intuitive to think of the
values of
\lambda(\mathcal{F})
taken in a complete lattice
V
where
0

and
1
denote the least and greatest elements, where
\lambda(\mathcal{F})(x)=1

means full convergence and
\lambda(\mathcal{F})(x)=0
means no convergence,
and for the purpose of this note, we may restrict ourselves to
V=[0,1]
.
We will also use a multiplicative notation
\otimes
for the operation
on
[0,1]\sim[0,\infty]^{op}
instead of the additive traditional
operation in
[0,\infty]
. Indeed,
-\ln:([0,1],\times)\to([0,\infty],+)^{op}

is a quantale isomorphism. Hence, we prefer to reframe the structure
as a monotone map
\lambda:\mathbb{F}X\to V^{X}
for the pointwise
order, with

(1.6)

\lambda(\{x\}^{\uparrow})(x)=1

for all
x\in X
and morphisms
f:(X,\lambda_{X})\to(Y,\lambda_{Y})

satisfying

\lambda_{Y}(f[\mathcal{F}])\circ f\geq\lambda_{X}(\mathcal{F})

for every
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{F}X
. Let
\mathscr{C}(X,Y)
denote the set of
all such morphisms. Let
\mathsf{Cap}
denote the resulting category with
these morphisms and convergence approach spaces as objects.

Total positivity in twisted flag varieties
Xuhua He, Kaitao Xie

Abstract
. Let G G be a Kac-Moody group, split over ℝ \mathbb{R} . The totally nonnegative part of G G and its (ordinary) flag variety G / B + G/B^{+} was introduced by Lusztig. It is known that the totally nonnegative parts of G G and G / B + G/B^{+} have remarkable combinatorial and topological properties. In this paper, we consider the totally nonnegative part of the J J -twisted flag variety G / B + J G/{}^{J}B^{+} , where B + J {}^{J}B^{+} is the Borel subgroup opposite to B + B^{+} in the standard parabolic subgroup P J + P_{J}^{+} of G G . The J J -twisted flag varieties include the ordinary flag variety G / B + G/B^{+} as a special case. Our main result show that the totally nonnegative part of G / B + J G/{}^{J}B^{+} decomposes into cells, and the closure of each cell is a regular CW complex. This generalizes the work of Galashin-Karp-Lam [ 12 ] and the joint work of Bao with the first author [ 4 ] for ordinary flag varieties. As an application, we deduce that the totally nonnegative part of the double flag variety G / B + × G / B − G/B^{+}\times G/B^{-} with respect to the diagonal G G -action has similar nice properties. We also establish some connections between the totally nonnegative part of the double flag with the canonical basis of the tensor product of a lowest weight module with a highest weight module of G G . As another application, we show that the link of identity in a totally nonnegative reduced double Bruhat cell of G G is a regular CW complex. This generalizes the work of Hersh [ 16 ] on the link of U ≥ 0 − U_{\geq 0}^{-} and gives a positive answer to an open question of Fomin and Zelevinsky.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Total positivity and remarkable polyhedral spaces

The theory of total positivity in reductive groups and their flag varieties was introduced by Lusztig
[
21
]
and has been studied extensively in many areas including cluster algebras, higher Teichmüller theory, and theoretical physics. We refer to
[
22
]
and references therein for an overview.

We briefly recall the totally nonnegative flag variety. Let
G
be a symmetrizable minimal Kac-Moody group that is split over
\mathbb{R}
. Let
B^{+},B^{-}
be a pair of Borel subgroups of
G
that are opposite to each other, and let
W
be the Weyl group of
G
. Lusztig
[
21
,
22
]
defined a submonoid
G_{\geq 0}
of
G
, which he called the
totally nonnegative monoid
.

Let
\mathcal{B}^{+}:=G/B^{+}
be the (ordinary) flag variety. It admits a stratification
\mathcal{B}^{+}=\bigsqcup\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}^{+}
, where each
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}^{+}
is an intersection of a
B^{-}
-orbit with a
B^{+}
-orbit, and
v
and
w
are elements in the Weyl group
W
indexing these orbits.

Lusztig defined the
totally nonnegative flag variety

\mathcal{B}^{+}_{\geq 0}
as the Hausdorff closure of
G_{\geq 0}B^{+}/B^{+}
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
, and he introduced the decomposition

\mathcal{B}^{+}_{\geq 0}=\bigsqcup(\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}^{+}\cap\mathcal{B}^{+}_{\geq 0}).

He called
\mathcal{B}^{+}_{\geq 0}
a
remarkable polyhedral space
without giving a precise definition. Based on later works, we provide the following definition:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X=\sqcup X_{\alpha}
be a stratification of topological spaces over
\mathbb{R}
and
Y\subseteq X
be a semi-algebraic subset. Set
Y_{\alpha}=Y\cap X_{\alpha}
. We say that
Y=\sqcup_{\alpha}Y_{\alpha}
is a
remarkable polyhedral space
if for any
\alpha
:

(1)

(Connected components)
Y_{\alpha}
is either empty or a connected component of
X_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R})
;

(2)

(Cell structure) If
Y_{\alpha}\neq\emptyset
, then
Y_{\alpha}\simeq\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\dim_{\mathbb{R}}X_{\alpha}}
is a semi-algebraic cell;

(3)

(Cellular decomposition) The Hausdorff closure
\overline{Y_{\alpha}}
is the disjoint union of totally positive cells
\bigsqcup_{\beta\leq\alpha}Y_{\beta}
, where
\beta\leq\alpha
if
X_{\beta}\subseteq\overline{X_{\alpha}}
.

Moreover, we say that
Y
has the
regularity property
if
Y
is a regular CW complex, i.e., for every
\alpha
, there exists a homeomorphism from a closed ball to
\overline{Y_{\alpha}}
, which restricts to a homeomorphism from the interior of the ball to
Y_{\alpha}
.

The following result summarizes some key properties for the totally nonnegative flag variety
\mathcal{B}_{\geq 0}^{+}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

The totally nonnegative flag variety
\mathcal{B}^{+}_{\geq 0}=\bigsqcup(\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{+}_{v,w}\cap\mathcal{B}^{+}_{\geq 0})
is a remarkable polyhedral space with regularity property.

For reductive groups, Rietsch
[
25
,
26
]
proved that
\mathcal{B}_{\geq 0}^{+}
is a remarkable polyhedral space, and Galashin, Karp and Lam
[
12
]
established the regularity property. For Kac-Moody groups, these statements were established by Bao and the first author in
[
1
,
4
]
.

1.2.
Motivations

The motivation in this paper is to study the totally nonnegative double flag varieties and the totally nonnegative reduced double Bruhat cells.

The
double flag varieties
we consider is
G/B^{+}\times G/B^{-}
and its
totally nonnegative part
is defined as

(G/B^{+}\times G/B^{-})_{\geq 0}=\overline{G_{\text{diag},\geq 0}\cdot(B^{+}/B^{+},B^{-}/B^{-})}\ \ \text{(Hausdorff closure).}

The
double Bruhat cells
of a Kac-Moody group are the intersections of the
B^{+}\times B^{+}
-orbits and the
B^{-}\times B^{-}
-orbits on
G
. They play an important role in the theory of cluster algebras. The maximal torus
T
of
G
acts freely on each double Bruhat cell
G^{w,u}
of
G
, and the quotient
L^{w,u}:=G^{w,u}/T
is called a
reduced double Bruhat cell
. There is a natural embedding

G/TG/B^{+}\times G/B^{-},

which is compatible with total positivity.

The totally nonnegative double flag variety was first considered by Webster and Yakimov
[
27
]
. They conjectured that it admits a cellular decomposition and verified
G=GL_{2}
case. For many years, this was the only known case. In
[
3
]
, Bao and the first author developed a thickening method and established the desired properties for another family of double flag varieties:
G/B^{+}\times G/B^{+}
. The key idea is to construct a larger Kac-Moody group
\tilde{G}
and a sub-complex of
(\tilde{G}/\tilde{B}^{+})_{\geq 0}
that forms a fiber bundle over
(G/B^{+}\times G/B^{+})_{\geq 0}
. When
G
is of finite type, the totally nonnegative part of
G/B^{+}\times G/B^{+}
and
G/B^{-}\times G/B^{+}
coincide. For general Kac–Moody groups, however, this identification fails, and the two double flag varieties are essentially different. The method of
[
3
]
does not apply directly to the case
G/B^{+}\times G/B^{-}
beyond the finite-type cases, and a new approach is required.

To overcome this obstacle and to study the totally nonnegative double flag variety and totally nonnegative reduced double Bruhat cells for an arbitrary Kac–Moody group, we are led to a generalization of the ordinary flag variety—the
twisted flag variety
—and to the investigation of its totally nonnegative part. The twisted setting provides the flexibility needed to apply a thickening argument to
G/B^{+}\times G/B^{-}
without the finite-type assumption.

1.3.
Generalization to twisted flag variety

Let
I
be the index set of simple roots of
G

, we denote

the corresponding parabolic subgroup of

the set of minimal representatives in the cosets in

\leq^{J}

and the Bruhat order in

Let

be the corresponding standard parabolic subgroup. Let
\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}
be the opposite Borel subgroup of
B^{+}
inside

. The
J
-twisted flag variety
is defined to be
\mathcal{B}^{J}:=G/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}
. When the Dynkin diagram of

is not of finite type,
\mathcal{B}^{J}
is NOT
G
-equivariantly isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}^{+}
.

The
totally nonnegative
J
-twisted flag variety
is defined to be the Hausdorff closure of
G_{\geq 0}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}
in
\mathcal{B}^{J}(\mathbb{R})
. In §
2.3
, we define a stratification
\mathcal{B}^{J}=\bigsqcup_{v\leq^{J}w}\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,w}
, and obtain the induced decomposition

\mathcal{B}^{J}_{\geq 0}=\bigsqcup_{v\leq^{J}w}(\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,w}\cap\mathcal{B}^{J}_{\geq 0}).

Our first main result extends Theorem
1.2
to the twisted setting.

Theorem 1.3
.

The totally nonnegative
J
-twisted flag variety
\mathcal{B}^{J}_{\geq 0}=\bigsqcup(\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,w}\cap\mathcal{B}^{J}_{\geq 0})
is a remarkable polyhedral space with regularity property.

To study the double flag variety
G/B^{-}\times G/B^{+}
, we construct an auxiliary larger Kac–Moody group
\tilde{G}
and a twisted flag variety
\tilde{}\mathcal{B}^{\tilde{J}}
of
\tilde{G}
. Inside
\tilde{}\mathcal{B}^{\tilde{J}}
, we define a subvariety
\tilde{Z}
that forms a fiber bundle over
G/B^{+}\times G/B^{-}
; the bundle projection is compatible with the stratifications and with total positivity. Applying Theorem
1.3
to this setting yields our second main result.

Theorem 1.4
.

The totally nonnegative double flag variety
(G/B^{+}\times G/B^{-})_{\geq 0}
is a remarkable polyhedral space with regularity property.

Since the embedding of
G/T
into the double flag variety is is compatible with the stratifications and with total positivity, we also deduce

Theorem 1.5
.

The link of identity in a totally nonnegative reduced double Bruhat cell of a Kac-Moody group is a regular CW complex. In particular, it is homeomorphic to a closed ball.

This generalizes the results of Hersh
[
16
]
on the link of identity in the totally nonnegative unipotent monoid
U^{-}_{\geq 0}
, and gives a positive answer to an open problem of Fomin and Zelevinsky (see
[
12

1.4.
Difficulty and strategy

The fundamental obstacle in extending total positivity to twisted flag varieties, compared with the ordinary (
J=\emptyset
) case, is the absence of a representation-theoretic interpretation for twisted flag varieties. In the ordinary case, one first uses the embedding of the flag variety into the projective space of a highest weight module and the associated canonical basis to establish the Marsh–Rietsch parametrization of totally positive cells
[
24
,
1
]
. This explicit parametrization immediately implies that each totally positive cell lies inside certain “big cells” of the flag variety, a key geometric property that allows the product structure method of
[
4
]
to be applied. The product structure then reduces the study of the closure of a cell to the study of two smaller cells, leading to an inductive proof of the closure relation and the regularity theorem.

For a twisted flag variety with
J
not of finite type, no analogous extreme‑weight theory is available, and the parametrization cannot be established a priori. Consequently, one cannot separate the parametrization step from the geometric analysis; the existence of a suitable parametrization and the verification of the required geometric properties (notably the containment in certain “big cells”) must be proved simultaneously.

Our strategy overcomes this entanglement by a carefully designed induction. We first extend the method of product structure to the twisted setting. Then we introduce explicit candidate parametrizing sets for the totally positive cells. Starting with a restricted family of strata (those with
v\in W_{J}
and
w\in W^{J}
), we prove that the candidate sets for these strata are indeed the totally positive cells and satisfy the required inclusion property. Using the product structure, we then extend these conclusions to a larger family (for
v\in W
and
w\in W^{J}
), and finally to arbitrary pairs
(v,w)
. At each stage the product structure allows us to propagate the parametrization and the geometric properties to the next stage. In this way the parametrization and the product structure are built together, culminating in the full proof of Theorem
1.3
.

1.5.
Representation-theoretic interpretation of double flag varieties

Although we do not have a representation-theoretic interpretation of the twisted flag varieties, we have a natural connection between the double flag varieties and representation theory.

In this subsection, we assume that
G
is simply-laced. Let
X^{++}
be the set of regular dominant weights of
G
. For
\lambda\in X^{++}
, let
\Lambda
be the simple highest weight module of
G
associated with a regular dominant weight, and
\eta_{\lambda}
be a highest weight vector. Let
\mathbb{P}(\Lambda_{\lambda})
be the projective space of
\Lambda_{\lambda}
. For
v\in\Lambda_{\lambda}
with
v\neq 0
, denote
[v]\in\mathbb{P}(\Lambda_{\lambda})
the corresponding line in the projective space. We have the natural embedding

\mathcal{B}^{+}\mathbb{P}(\Lambda_{\lambda}),\quad gB^{+}/B^{+}[g\eta_{\lambda}].

We identify
\mathcal{B}^{+}
with its image in
\mathbb{P}(\Lambda_{\lambda})
.

Lusztig’s theory of canonical bases for quantum groups
[
19
]
provides a basis
\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda})
of
\Lambda
. Let
\mathbb{P}(\Lambda_{\lambda})_{\geq 0}
be the set of lines spanned by a vector whose coordinates with respect to
\mathbf{B}(\Lambda)
are all nonnegative. For reductive groups Lusztig
[
21
, Proposition 8.17]
and for Kac–Moody groups Bao and He
[
1
]
proved that

\mathcal{B}^{+}_{\geq 0}=\mathcal{B}^{+}\bigcap\mathbb{P}(\Lambda_{\lambda})_{\geq 0}.

This equality gives a representation‑theoretic characterization of the totally nonnegative flag variety.

It is natural to ask whether the totally nonnegative double flag variety admits a similar description.

For
\lambda\in X^{++}
, let
{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda}
be a simple lowest weight module of
G
associated to
\lambda
, and let
\xi_{-\lambda}
be a lowest weight vector. For
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in X^{++}
, we consider the tensor product
{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
and its projective space
\mathbb{P}(^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})
. There is a natural embedding

G/B^{+}\times G/B^{-}\mathbb{P}(^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}),\ \ (g_{1}B^{+}/B^{+},g_{2}B^{-}/B^{-})[g_{2}\xi_{-\lambda_{1}}\otimes g_{1}\eta_{\lambda_{2}}].

We identify
G/B^{+}\times G/B^{-}
with its image in
\mathbb{P}(^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})
. Lusztig
[
20
]
introduced the canonical basis
\mathbf{B}(^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})
for
{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
.

Conjecture 1.6
.

Let
\mathbb{P}(^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})_{\geq 0}
be the set of lines spanned by a vector whose coordinates with respect to
\mathbf{B}(^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})
are all nonnegative. Then

(G/B^{+}\times G/B^{-})_{\geq 0}=(G/B^{+}\times G/B^{-})\bigcap\mathbb{P}(^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})_{\geq 0}.

In a recent joint work of the first author with Fang
[
11
]
, some positivity property of
\mathbf{B}(^{\omega}\Lambda\otimes\Lambda)
was established. Using these results, we prove one inclusion of the conjecture in §
5.7
:

(G/B^{+}\times G/B^{-})_{\geq 0}\subseteq\mathbb{P}(^{\omega}\Lambda\otimes\Lambda)_{\geq 0}.

2.4.
Totally nonnegative flag varieties: ordinary and twisted

Let
U^{+}_{\geq 0}
be the submonoid of
G
generated by
x_{i}(a)
for
i\in I
and
a\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, and
U^{-}_{\geq 0}
be the submonoid of
G
generated by
y_{i}(a)
for
i\in I
and
a\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. Let
T_{>0}
be the identity component of
T(\mathbb{R})
. The
totally nonnegative monoid

G_{\geq 0}
is defined to be the submonoid of
G
generated by
U^{\pm}_{\geq 0}
and
T_{>0}
. It is known that

G_{\geq 0}=U_{\geq 0}^{+}T_{>0}U^{-}_{\geq 0}=U^{-}_{\geq 0}T_{>0}U^{+}_{\geq 0}.

The
totally nonnegative flag variety

\mathcal{B}^{+}_{\geq 0}
is defined to be the Hausdorff closure
\overline{G_{\geq 0}B^{+}/B^{+}}
of
G_{\geq 0}B^{+}/B^{+}
in
\mathcal{B}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
. Since
T_{>0}U_{\geq 0}^{+}\subseteq B^{+}
, we also have
\mathcal{B}^{+}_{\geq 0}=\overline{U^{-}_{\geq 0}B^{+}/B^{+}}
.

In analogy to the totally nonnegative flag variety, we define the
totally nonnegative
J
-twisted flag variety

\mathcal{B}^{J}_{\geq 0}
to be the Hausdorff closure
\overline{G_{\geq 0}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}}
of
G_{\geq 0}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}
in
\mathcal{B}^{J}(\mathbb{R})
.

Example 2.1
.

Suppose
W_{J}
is finite. Let
w_{J,0}
be the unique longest element in
W_{J}
. The morphism
\varphi:\mathcal{B}^{J}\mathcal{B}^{+}
defined by
\varphi(g\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+})=g\dot{w}_{J,0}B^{+}/B^{+}
is a
G
-equivariant isomorphism. We have

\varphi(G_{\geq 0}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+})=G_{\geq 0}\dot{w}_{J,0}B^{+}/B^{+}=G_{\geq 0}U_{w_{J,0},>0}^{+}\dot{w}_{J,0}B^{+}/B^{+}=G_{\geq 0}B^{+}/B^{+}.

The last equality follows from Lusztig’s duality
[
21
, Theorem 8.7]
:

U^{+}_{w_{J,0},>0}\dot{w}_{J,0}B^{+}/B^{+}=U^{-}_{w_{J,0},>0}B^{+}/B^{+}.

We conclude that, when
W_{J}
is finite,
\varphi
restricts to an isomorphism
\mathcal{B}^{J}_{\geq 0}\simeq\mathcal{B}_{\geq 0}^{+}
.

Returning to the general situation, for
v,w\in W
, we set
\mathcal{B}^{J}_{v,w,>0}=\mathcal{B}^{J}_{\geq 0}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,w}
. Then

\mathcal{B}^{J}_{\geq 0}=\bigsqcup_{v,w}\mathcal{B}^{J}_{v,w,>0}.

This is the object of major interest in this paper.

Below we recollect some combinatorial and topological results that will be used.

2.5.
Combinatorial results

Let
(P,\leq)
be a poset. For every
x,y\in P
with
x\leq y
, the
interval
between
x
and
y
is defined as
[x,y]=\{z\in P|x\leq z\leq y\}
. A subset
C\subseteq P
is
convex
if
[x,y]\subseteq C
for
x,y\in C
.

The covering relation is denoted by
\lessdot
. For
x,y\in P
with
x\leq y
, a maximal chain from
y
to
x
is a finite sequence of elements
y=z_{0}\gtrdot z_{1}\gtrdot\cdots\gtrdot z_{n}=x
. In general, a maximal chain may not exist.

The poset
P
is
pure
if for any
x,y\in P
with
x\leq y
, a maximal chain from
x
to
y
always exist and have the same length. A pure poset is
graded
if it contains a unique minimum and a unique maximum. A pure poset is
thin
if every interval of length
2
has exactly
4
elements.

The order complex
\triangle_{ord}(P)
of a finite poset
P
is a simplicial complex whose vertices
are the elements of
P
and whose simplexes are the chains
x_{0}>x_{1}>\cdots>x_{k}
in
P
. A
finite pure poset is
shellable
if its order complex
\triangle_{ord}(P)
is shellable, that is, its maximal faces can be ordered as
F_{1},F_{2},\cdots,F_{n}
so that
F_{k}\cap(\cup_{i=1}^{k-1}F_{i})
is a nonempty
union of the maximal proper faces of
F_{k}
, for
k=2,3,\cdots,n
.

We consider the poset
(Q^{J},\leq_{Q^{J}})
of intervals in
(W,\leq^{J})
. Namely,

Q^{J}=\{[x,y]\in W\times W|x\leq^{J}y\},

and
[x_{1},y_{1}]\leq_{Q^{J}}[x_{2},y_{2}]
if
x_{2}\leq^{J}x_{1}\leq^{J}y_{1}\leq^{J}y_{2}
in
W
. Let
\hat{Q}^{J}:=Q^{J}\sqcup\{\hat{0}\}
be the augmented poset with
\hat{0}
an adjoined minimal element. The following proposition can be proved similarly as in
[
9
, Proposition 3.9]
and
[
28
]
. See also
[
4
, Proposition 3.1]
and
[
2
, §4]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

(1)

Every interval in the poset
(W,\leq^{J})
is graded, thin and shellable.

(2)

Every interval in the poset
(\hat{Q}^{J},\leq_{\hat{Q}^{J}})
is graded, thin and shellable.

2.6.
Topological results

Let
X
be a regular CW complex. The
face poset
of
X
is the poset
(Q,\leq)
, where
\beta\leq\alpha
if and only if
X_{\beta}\subseteq\overline{X_{\alpha}}
. The following result
[
7
]
connects the combinatorial properties introduced in §
2.5
to the regularity of CW complexes:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a regular CW complex with face poset
Q
. If
Q\sqcup\{\hat{0},\hat{1}\}
(adjoining a minimum
\hat{0}
and a maximum
\hat{1}
) is graded, thin, and shellable, then
X
is homeomorphic to a sphere of dimension
\text{rank}(Q)-1
.

Recall that an
n
-dimensional topological manifold with boundary is a Hausdorff space
X
such that every point
x\in X
has an open neighborhood homeomorphic to either
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, or
\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, with
x
mapped to a point in
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
in the latter case. In this case, we say that
x
lies on
\partial X
, the boundary of
X
.

The following theorem can be derived from the generalized Poincaré conjecture and Brown’s collar theorem (see
[
4
, Theorem 2.2]
and references therein).

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
X
be a compact
n
-dimensional topological manifold with boundary such that

(1)

\partial X
is homeomorphic to an
(n-1)
-dimensional sphere, and

(2)

X\setminus\partial X
is homeomorphic to an
n
-dimensional open ball.

Then
X
is homeomorphic to an
n
-dimensional closed ball
D^{n}
.

3.
The Product Structure

3.1.
Product structure on the flag varieties

We begin by recalling the product structure on the flag variety
\mathcal{B}
from
[
4
]
.

For any
r\in W
, we have the following isomorphisms:

(U^{-}\cap\dot{r}U^{-}\dot{r}^{-1})\times(U^{+}\cap\dot{r}U^{-}\dot{r}^{-1})\dot{r}U^{-}\dot{r}^{-1},\ (g_{1},g_{2})g_{1}g_{2},

(U^{+}\cap\dot{r}U^{-}\dot{r}^{-1})\times(U^{-}\cap\dot{r}U^{-}\dot{r}^{-1})\dot{r}U^{-}\dot{r}^{-1},\ (h_{1},h_{2})h_{1}h_{2}.

We define

\tilde{\sigma}_{r}:\dot{r}U^{-}\dot{r}^{-1}(\dot{r}U^{-}\dot{r}^{-1}\cap U^{+})\times(\dot{r}U^{-}\dot{r}^{-1}\cap U^{-}),\ g(g_{2},h_{2}).

The morphism
\tilde{\sigma}_{r}
is an isomorphism of ind-varieties. It induces an isomorphism

\sigma_{r}=(\sigma_{r,+},\sigma_{r,-}):\dot{r}U^{-}B^{+}/B^{+}\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{+}_{r}\times\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{+,r},

given by

g\dot{r}B^{+}/B^{+}(g_{2}\dot{r}B^{+}/B^{+},h_{2}\dot{r}B^{+}/B^{+}),\text{ for }g\in\dot{r}U^{-}\dot{r}^{-1}.

This product structure is a powerful tool for studying connected components of open Richardson varieties (see
[
4
, Theorem 3.5, Corollary 3.7, Theorem 4.1]
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
Y_{v,w}
be a connected component of
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{+}_{v,w}(\mathbb{R})
. For any
v\leqslant v^{\prime}\leqslant w^{\prime}\leqslant w
, define

Y_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}=\overline{Y_{v,w}}\bigcap\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{+}_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}.

Assume that for any
v\leqslant v^{\prime}\leqslant r\leqslant w^{\prime}\leqslant w
, we have
Y_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}\subseteq\dot{r}B^{-}B^{+}/B^{+}
. Then

(1)

The map
\sigma_{r}
restricts to an isomorphism
\sigma_{r}:Y_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}\cong Y_{v^{\prime},r}\times Y_{r,w^{\prime}}
;

(2)

The closure
\overline{Y_{v,w}}=\bigsqcup_{v\leq v^{\prime}\leq w^{\prime}\leq w}Y_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}
is a remarkable polyhedral space;

(3)

The closure
\overline{Y_{v,w}}=\bigsqcup_{v\leq v^{\prime}\leq w^{\prime}\leq w}Y_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}
has the regularity property.

It was shown in
[
4
]
that
\mathcal{B}^{+}_{v,w,>0}
satisfies the assumption in Theorem
3.1
, thus Theorem
1.2
was deduced from the product structure.

3.2.
Product structure on the
J
-twisted flag varieties

For any
r\in W
, we have the following isomorphisms for the
J
-twisted case:

(U^{-}\cap\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\dot{r}^{-1})\times(U^{+}\cap\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\dot{r}^{-1})\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\dot{r}^{-1},\ (g_{1},g_{2})g_{1}g_{2},

(U^{+}\cap\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\dot{r}^{-1})\times(U^{-}\cap\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\dot{r}^{-1})\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\dot{r}^{-1},\ (h_{1},h_{2})h_{1}h_{2}.

We define

\tilde{\sigma}_{r}^{J}:\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\dot{r}^{-1}(\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\dot{r}^{-1}\cap U^{+})\times(\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\dot{r}^{-1}\cap U^{-}),\ g(g_{2},h_{2}).

The morphism
\tilde{\sigma}_{r}^{J}
is an isomorphism of ind-varieties. It induces an isomorphism

\sigma_{r}^{J}=(\sigma_{r,+}^{J},\sigma_{r,-}^{J}):\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{r}^{J}\times\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J,r},

given by

g\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}(g_{2}\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+},h_{2}\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}),\text{ for }g\in\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\dot{r}^{-1}.

The isomorphism
\sigma_{r}^{J}
is stratified with respect to the open
J
-twisted Richardson varieties, namely,

\sigma_{r}^{J}(\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}^{J}\cap\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+})=\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,r}\times\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{r,w}.

These morphisms allow us to understand the closure of a subset of
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,w}
in
\mathcal{B}^{J}
via the image of
\sigma^{J}_{r}
, as shown in the following lemmas.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
v\leq^{J}r\leq^{J}w
. Consider a subset
X\subseteq\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,w}(\mathbb{R})
. Suppose that
\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}\in\overline{X}
. Let
v^{\prime},w^{\prime}\in W
such that
v\leq^{J}v^{\prime}\leq^{J}r\leq^{J}w^{\prime}\leq^{J}w
. Then

\overline{X}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v^{\prime},r}=\overline{\sigma^{J}_{r,+}(X\cap\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+})}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v^{\prime},r};

\overline{X}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{r,w^{\prime}}=\overline{\sigma^{J}_{r,-}(X\cap\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+})}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{r,w^{\prime}}.

Remark 3.3
.

Similar results hold when the Hausdorff closure is replaced by the Zariski closure.

Proof.

Let
X^{\prime}=X\cap\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}
. Since
\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}
is open, we have
\overline{X^{\prime}}\cap\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}=\overline{X}\cap\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}
. Via restriction we have

\overline{X^{\prime}}\cap\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}\simeq\overline{\sigma_{r,+}^{J}(X^{\prime})}\times\overline{\sigma_{r,-}^{J}(X^{\prime})}.

Since
\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}\in\overline{X}\cap\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}\subseteq\overline{X^{\prime}}
, we have
\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}\in\overline{\sigma_{r,+}^{J}(X^{\prime})}
and
\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}\in\overline{\sigma_{r,-}^{J}(X^{\prime})}
. Since the isomorphism is stratified, we have

\overline{X^{\prime}}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v^{\prime},r}^{J}\simeq(\overline{\sigma^{J}_{r,+}(X^{\prime})}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v^{\prime},r})\times\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}\simeq\overline{\sigma_{r,+}^{J}(X^{\prime})}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v^{\prime},r}.

The composition is the identity map. The second equality is proved similarly.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose that
Y
is a connected component of
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,w}(\mathbb{R})
. Assume that
Y\subseteq\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}
. Let
\overline{Y}
be the Hausdorff closure of
Y
in
\mathcal{B}^{J}(\mathbb{R})
. Then

(1)

\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}\in\overline{Y}
;

(2)

\overline{Y}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,r}=\sigma_{r,+}^{J}(Y)
is a connected component of
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,r}(\mathbb{R})
;

(3)

\overline{Y}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{r,w}=\sigma_{r,-}^{J}(Y)
is a connected component of
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{r,w}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

For any
u\in\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}
, there is a finite sequence
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{m})
of roots in
(\Phi^{-}\setminus\Phi_{J}^{-})\sqcup\Phi_{J}^{+}
such that
u\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}U_{\alpha_{i}}
. Then there exists a coweight
\mu
, such that
\lim_{t0}\mu(t)u\mu(t)^{-1}=e
. Let
\mu^{\prime}=r(\mu)
. Then
\lim_{t0^{+}}\mu^{\prime}(t)\dot{r}u\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}=\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}
. Since
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,w}
is stable under
T
-action, and
Y
is a connected component of
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,w}(\mathbb{R})
, we know that
Y
is stable under
T_{>0}
-action. This proves part (1).

Since
\sigma_{r}^{J}
is an isomorphism, we see that
\sigma_{r,+}^{J}(Y)
is a connected component of
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,r}(\mathbb{R})
, and thus is closed in
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,r}
. We have
\sigma_{r,+}^{J}(Y)=\overline{\sigma_{r,+}^{J}(Y)}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,r}
, which equals
\overline{Y}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,r}
by
Lemma

3.2
. This proves part (2); part (3) is proved similarly.
∎

3.3.
Main result of this section

The main purpose of this section is to establish the following theorem, which parallels
Theorem

3.1
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
Y_{v,w}
be a connected component of
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,w}(\mathbb{R})
. For any
v\leqslant v^{\prime}\leqslant w^{\prime}\leqslant w
, define

Y_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}=\overline{Y_{v,w}}\bigcap\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}.

Assume that for any
v\leqslant^{J}v^{\prime}\leqslant^{J}r\leqslant^{J}w^{\prime}\leqslant^{J}w
, we have
Y_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}\subseteq\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}
. Then

(1)

The map
\sigma^{J}_{r}
restricts to an isomorphism
\sigma^{J}_{r}:Y_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}\cong Y_{v^{\prime},r}\times Y_{r,w^{\prime}}
;

(2)

The closure
\overline{Y_{v,w}}=\bigsqcup_{v\leq^{J}v^{\prime}\leq^{J}w^{\prime}\leq^{J}w}Y_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}
is a remarkable polyhedral space;

(3)

The closure
\overline{Y_{v,w}}=\bigsqcup_{v\leq^{J}v^{\prime}\leq^{J}w^{\prime}\leq^{J}w}Y_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}
has the regularity property.

The general strategy is similar to the proof of
Theorem

3.1
in
[
4
]
. However, there are additional difficulties in this complicated situation. First, we need some geometric properties of
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,w}
, which we establish in §
3.4
–§
3.6
. Second, while in the ordinary case one studies the links of
Y_{v,w}
using highest weight representations, such a theory is not available in the twisted situation. Instead, we embed certain subsets of each
\overline{Y}_{v,w}
into finite-dimensional vector spaces and obtain parallel results in §
3.9
.

We prove Theorem
3.5
(1) in §
3.7
, (2) in §
3.8
, and (3) in §
3.11
.

3.4.
Nonemptiness pattern of
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,w}

We have the following description of the nonemptiness pattern of
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,w}
, generalizing §
2.2
(a).

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
v,w\in W
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}^{J}\neq\emptyset
;

(2)

\mathcal{B}^{J}_{v,w}\neq\emptyset
;

(3)

v\leq^{J}w
.

Proof.

Clearly (1)
\Rightarrow
(2). For (2)
\Rightarrow
(3), first note that all
T
-fixed points in
\mathcal{B}^{J}
are
\{\dot{u}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}|u\in W\}
. Since
\mathcal{B}^{J}_{v,w}
is projective and
T
-invariant,
\mathcal{B}^{J}_{v,w}
contains a
T
-fixed point, say
\dot{u}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}
. Since
\mathcal{B}^{J}_{v,w}\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{J}_{w}=\sqcup_{w^{\prime}\leq^{J}w}\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{w^{\prime}}
, we have
u\leq^{J}w
. Since
\mathcal{B}^{J}_{v,w}\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{J,v}=\sqcup_{v\leq^{J}v^{\prime}}\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J,v^{\prime}}
, we have
v\leq^{J}u
. Then
v\leq^{J}w
.

Now we show that (3)
\Rightarrow
(1). Consider the isomorphisms

(U^{+}\cap\dot{v}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\dot{v}^{-1})\times(U^{-}\cap\dot{v}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\dot{v}^{-1})\dot{v}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\dot{v}^{-1}\dot{v}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}

(u_{1},u_{2})u_{1}u_{2}u_{1}u_{2}\dot{v}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}.

Since
U^{-}\cap\dot{v}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\dot{v}^{-1}
is isomorphic to
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J,v}
via
u_{2}u_{2}\dot{v}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}
, we have

(U^{+}\cap\dot{v}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\dot{v}^{-1})\times\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J,v}\dot{v}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}.

Since
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{w}
is invariant under left multiplication by
U^{+}
, via restriction, we have

(U^{+}\cap\dot{v}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\dot{v}^{-1})\times(\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J,v}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{w})\simeq\dot{v}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{w}.

It is sufficient to show that
\dot{v}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{w}\neq\emptyset
. Since
\dot{v}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}
is open, we only need to show that
\dot{v}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}\cap\mathcal{B}^{J}_{w}\neq\emptyset
. But it contains the point
\dot{v}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}
since
v\leq^{J}w
.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
v\leq^{J}r\leq^{J}w
. Then
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}^{J}\cap\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}\neq\emptyset
and
\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}\in\overline{\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,w}\cap\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}}
.

Proof.

The nonemptyness follows from
Proposition

3.6
and the isomorphism

\sigma_{r}^{J}:\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,w}\cap\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}\simeq\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,r}\times\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{r,w}.

The last statement can be proved similarly as
Lemma

3.4
(1).
∎

3.5.
Closure relation of
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,w}

We have the following description of the nonemptiness pattern of
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,w}
, generalizing §
2.2
(c).

Proposition 3.8
.

We have

\mathcal{B}^{J}_{v,w}=\bigsqcup_{v\leq^{J}v^{\prime}\leq^{J}w^{\prime}\leq^{J}w}\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}.

Proof.

By
[
5
, Theorem 4]
, we have
\mathcal{B}^{J}_{v,w}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{w}^{J}\cap\mathcal{B}^{J,v}=\bigsqcup_{v\leq^{J}v^{\prime}\leq^{J}w^{\prime}\leq^{J}w}\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}
.

Let
r\in W
with
v\leq^{J}r\leq^{J}w

Proof.

By our assumption on

(b)

Define a morphism

\displaystyle Lk_{v^{\prime},r}\times(\overline{Y_{v,w}}\cap U^{-}\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow Y_{v^{\prime},r}\times(\overline{Y_{v,w}}\cap U^{-}\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(\sigma_{r}^{J})^{-1}}\bigsqcup_{r\leq^{J}w^{\prime}\leq^{J}w}Y_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}

4.
Totally Nonnegative Twisted Flag Varieties

The totally nonnegative twisted flag variety is defined to be

\mathcal{B}^{J}_{\geq 0}=\overline{G_{\geq 0}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}}\text{ (Hausdorff closure in $\mathcal{B}^{J}(\mathbb{R})$)}.

Let
\mathcal{B}^{J}_{v,w,>0}:=\mathcal{B}^{J}_{\geq 0}\bigcap\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,w}(\mathbb{R})
. Then
\mathcal{B}^{J}_{\geq 0}=\bigsqcup_{v\leq^{J}w}\mathcal{B}^{J}_{v,w,>0}
. This section is devoted to the study of
\mathcal{B}^{J}_{\geq 0}
with this decomposition.

4.1.
Marsh-Rietsch parametrization

A major difficulty in applying Theorem
3.5
to the study of
\mathcal{B}^{J}_{\geq 0}
is verifying the condition
\mathcal{B}^{J}_{v,w,>0}\subseteq\dot{r}\,^{J}U^{-}\,{}^{J}B^{+}/\,^{J}B^{+}
for all
v\leq^{J}r\leq^{J}w
. In the ordinary flag variety case, this condition is verified using the Marsh-Rietsch parametrization
[
24
]
of totally positive cells. We now recall this construction, which will serve as motivation for our generalization in the twisted setting.

An
expression
is a sequence
\underline{w}=(t_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{n})
, where
t_{j}\in\{s_{i}:i\in I\}\sqcup\{1\}
. We write
w=t_{1}t_{2}\cdots t_{n}\in W
and say that
\underline{w}
is an expression of
w
. The expression is
reduced
if
n=\ell(w)
.

A
subexpression
of
\underline{w}
is a sequence
\underline{w}^{\prime}=(t_{1}^{\prime},t_{2}^{\prime},\cdots,t_{n}^{\prime})
with
t_{i}^{\prime}\in\{t_{i},1\}
. If
\underline{w}
is a reduced expression, the subexpression
\underline{w}^{\prime}
is called
positive
if

t_{1}^{\prime}t_{2}^{\prime}\cdots t_{j-1}^{\prime}<t_{1}^{\prime}t_{2}^{\prime}\cdots t_{j-1}^{\prime}t_{j}
for all
j
.

By
[
24
, Lemma 3.5]
, for any
v\leq w
and any reduced expression
\underline{w}
of
w
, there exists a unique positive subexpression for
v
in
\underline{w}
, denoted by
\underline{v}_{+}
.

Let
v\leq w
and fix a reduced expression
\underline{w}
for
w
. Let
\underline{v}_{+}
be the positive subexpression for
v
in
\underline{w}
. Define

G^{-}_{\underline{v}_{+},\underline{w},>0}:=\left\{g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{n}\,\middle|\,\begin{array}[]{ll}g_{j}=\dot{s}_{i_{j}},&\text{if }t_{j}=s_{i_{j}};\\
g_{j}\in y_{i_{j}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0}),&\text{if }t_{j}=1\end{array}\right\}.

It was shown in
[
24
, Theorem 11.3]
for reductive groups and in
[
1
, Theorem 4.10]
for Kac-Moody groups that

\mathcal{B}^{+}_{v,w,>0}=G^{-}_{\underline{v}_{+},\underline{w},>0}B^{+}/B^{+}.

The proof is based on the representation-theoretic interpretation of totally nonnegative flag varieties, involving the theory of the canonical basis.

We also record some related notation for future use:

\displaystyle G^{+}_{\underline{v}_{+},\underline{w},>0}:=\{g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{n}\ |\ g_{j}=\dot{s}_{i_{j}}\text{ if }t_{j}=s_{i_{j}};g_{j}\in x_{i_{j}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})\text{ if }t_{j}=1\},

\displaystyle G^{-}_{\underline{v}_{+},\underline{w},\neq 0}:=\{g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{n}\ |\ g_{j}=\dot{s}_{i_{j}}\text{ if }t_{j}=s_{i_{j}};g_{j}\in y_{i_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{\times})\text{ if }t_{j}=1\},

\displaystyle G^{+}_{\underline{v}_{+},\underline{w},\neq 0}:=\{g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{n}\ |\ g_{j}=\dot{s}_{i_{j}}\text{ if }t_{j}=s_{i_{j}};g_{j}\in x_{i_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{\times})\text{ if }t_{j}=1\}.

4.2.
The set
G^{J}_{(v,w),>0}

We introduce a parametrizing set
G^{J}_{(v,w),>0}\subseteq G
in analogue with the Marsh-Rietsch parametrization. In the untwisted case, the Marsh-Rietsch parametrization uses a single reduced expression for
w
. For the
J
-twisted case, our parametrization must account for the coset structure
W/W_{J}
. Our parametrizing set decomposes as a product of two factors:

•

The factor
G^{-}_{\underline{v^{J}c}_{+},\underline{w^{J}},>0}
handles contributions from
W^{J}
, and

•

The factor
G^{+}_{\underline{w_{J}}_{+},\underline{c^{-1}v_{J}},>0}
handles contributions within the parabolic subgroup
W_{J}
.

From this point forward, we assume that for each
w\in W
, a reduced expression
\underline{w}
is chosen.

For
v\leq^{J}w
, there exists
u\in W_{J}
such that
v^{J}u\leq w^{J}
and
w_{J}\leq u^{-1}v_{J}
. By
[
14
, Lemma 1.4]
, there is a unique minimal such
u
. We denote this element by

c=\min\{u\in W_{J}:v^{J}u\leq w^{J}\text{ and }w_{J}\leq u^{-1}v_{J}\}.

This minimal element satisfies the length relations:

(a)

\ell(cw_{J})=\ell(w_{J})-\ell(c)\quad\text{and}\quad\ell(c^{-1}v_{J})=\ell(c^{-1})+\ell(v_{J}).

Definition 4.1
.

For
v\leq^{J}w
, we choose reduced expressions
\underline{w^{J}},\underline{v_{J}}
. For any reduced expression
\underline{c^{-1}}
, put
\underline{c^{-1}v_{J}}=(\underline{c^{-1}},\underline{v_{J}})
. We define

\displaystyle G^{J}_{(v,w),\neq 0}

\displaystyle:=G^{-}_{\underline{v^{J}c}_{+},\underline{w^{J}},\neq 0}\cdot G^{+}_{\underline{w_{J}}_{+},\underline{c^{-1}v_{J}},\neq 0},

\displaystyle G^{J}_{(v,w),>0}

\displaystyle:=G^{-}_{\underline{v^{J}c}_{+},\underline{w^{J}},>0}\cdot G^{+}_{\underline{w_{J}}_{+},\underline{c^{-1}v_{J}},>0}.

By the length relations (a), we have
G^{J}_{(v,w),\neq 0}\simeq(\mathbb{R}^{\times})^{\ell^{J}(w)-\ell^{J}(v)}
and
G^{J}_{(v,w),>0}\simeq(\mathbb{R}_{>0})^{\ell^{J}(w)-\ell^{J}(v)}
. Moreover, by our choice of
\underline{c^{-1}v_{J}}
, we have

G^{+}_{\underline{w_{J}}_{+},\underline{c^{-1}v_{J}},\neq 0}=\dot{c}^{-1}G^{+}_{\underline{cw_{J}}_{+},\underline{v_{J}},\neq 0},

and thus these sets do not dependent on the choice of
\underline{c^{-1}}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

The map

G^{J}_{(v,w),\neq 0}\mathcal{B}^{J},\quad gg\,^{J}B^{+}/\,^{J}B^{+}

is injective. Moreover, we have the inclusion

G^{J}_{(v,w),\neq 0}\,{}^{J}B^{+}/\,^{J}B^{+}\subseteq\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,w}.

Proof.

We first prove injectivity.

Suppose
g_{1},g_{2}\in G^{-}_{\underline{v^{J}c}_{+},\underline{w^{J}},\neq 0}
,
h_{1},h_{2}\in G^{+}_{\underline{w_{J}}_{+},\underline{c^{-1}v_{J}},\neq 0}
such that
g_{1}h_{1}\,^{J}B^{+}/\,^{J}B^{+}=g_{2}h_{2}\,^{J}B^{+}/\,^{J}B^{+}
. Then there exist
b\in B_{J}^{-}
and
u\in U_{P_{J}^{+}}
such that
g_{1}h_{1}=g_{2}h_{2}bu
.

Projecting to
G/P_{J}^{+}
, we obtain
g_{1}P_{J}^{+}=g_{2}P_{J}^{+}
. By
[
24
, Proposition 5.2]
, the map
G^{-}_{\underline{v^{J}c}_{+},\underline{w^{J}},\neq 0}G/P_{J}^{+}
is injective, hence
g_{1}=g_{2}
.

Now we have
h_{1}=h_{2}bu
. Since
h_{1},h_{2},b\in L_{J}
and
u\in U_{P_{J}^{+}}
with
U_{P_{J}^{+}}\cap L_{J}=\{e\}
, it follows that
u=e
. Therefore,
h_{1}=h_{2}b
, and projecting to
L_{J}/B_{J}^{-}
yields
h_{1}B_{J}^{-}=h_{2}B_{J}^{-}
. Again by
[
24
, Proposition 5.2]
(applied to
L_{J}
), the map
G^{+}_{\underline{w_{J}}_{+},\underline{c^{-1}v_{J}},\neq 0}L_{J}/B_{J}^{-}
is injective, so
h_{1}=h_{2}
. This establishes the injectivity of the map.

For the inclusion, we use the following containment from
[
24
, Proposition 5.2]
:

\displaystyle G^{-}_{\underline{v^{J}c}_{+},\underline{w^{J}},\neq 0}

\displaystyle\subseteq B^{+}\dot{w}^{J}B^{+}\cap U^{-}\dot{v}^{J}\dot{c},

\displaystyle G^{+}_{\underline{w_{J}}_{+},\underline{c^{-1}v_{J}},\neq 0}

\displaystyle=\dot{c}^{-1}G^{+}_{\underline{cw_{J}}_{+},\underline{v_{J}},\neq 0}\subseteq\dot{c}^{-1}\left(B_{J}^{-}\dot{v}_{J}B_{J}^{-}\cap U_{J}^{+}\dot{w}_{J}\right).

It follows that

G^{J}_{(v,w),\neq 0}\,{}^{J}B^{+}/\,^{J}B^{+}\subseteq\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,w},

completing the proof.
∎

By definition, the sets
G^{J}_{(v,w),\neq 0}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}
and

depend a priori on the choice of reduced expressions
\underline{w^{J}}
and
\underline{v_{J}}
. However, the Marsh-Rietsch parametrization applying to

yields that

does not depend on the choice of
\underline{c^{-1}v_{J}}
. Therefore, the set
G^{J}_{(v,w),>0}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}
does not depend on the choice of
\underline{v_{J}}
, and we can even remove the assumption that
\underline{c^{-1}v_{J}}
is of the form
(\underline{c^{-1}},\underline{v_{J}})
. We will show in §

that

, and thus, it does not dependent on the choice of

either.

4.3.
Main result

We state the main results on the totally nonnegative twisted flag variety
\mathcal{B}^{J}_{\geq 0}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
v,w\in W
with
v\leq^{J}w
. Then

(1)

For any choices of reduced expressions
\underline{w^{J}}
and
\underline{v_{J}}
, we have

\mathcal{B}^{J}_{v,w,>0}=G^{J}_{(v,w),>0}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}.

(2)

For any
r\in W
such that
v\leq^{J}r\leq^{J}w
, the map
\sigma_{r}^{J}
restricts to an isomorphism

\sigma_{r}^{J}:\mathcal{B}^{J}_{v,w,>0}\simeq\mathcal{B}^{J}_{v,r,>0}\times\mathcal{B}^{J}_{r,w,>0}.

(3)

\mathcal{B}^{J}_{\geq 0}
is a remarkable polyhedral space with regularity property.

The difficulty in establishing this theorem is twofold. On one hand, when
W_{J}
is infinite, the theory of ”twisted” highest weight representations does not exist. Consequently, we cannot adapt the strategy from
[
24
]
to establish the parametrization. On the other hand, without the parametrization result at hand, we are not able to describe the action of
G_{\geq 0}
on the candidate set
G^{J}_{(v,w),>0}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}
, which is important for us to check the key condition

G^{J}_{(v,w),>0}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}\subseteq\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+},\text{ for }v\leq^{J}r\leq^{J}w.

Our strategy is to take three steps which depend on the restriction we post on indexing pair
(v,w)
:

(1)

Step 1:
v\in W_{J}
,
w\in W^{J}
.

(2)

Step 2:
w\in W^{J}
and no restriction on
v
.

(3)

Step 3: general situation.

At each step, we show that:

•

the candidate set
G^{J}_{(v,w),>0}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}
is a connected component of
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{J}_{v,w}(\mathbb{R})
;

•

the candidate set
G^{J}_{(v,w),>0}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}
equals to
\mathcal{B}^{J}_{v,w,>0}
and thus is independent of the choices of reduced expressions
\underline{w^{J}}
and
\underline{v_{J}}
;

•

the candidate set satisfies the inclusion property

G^{J}_{(v,w),>0}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}\subseteq\dot{r}\prescript{J}{}{U}^{-}\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+}/\prescript{J}{}{B}^{+},\quad\text{for all }v\leq^{J}r\leq^{J}w.

The goal in this subsection is to show that, for

. Let

be the identity component of

Lemma 4.22
.

Suppose
w\in W^{J}
with reduced expression
\underline{w}=(s_{i_{1}},s_{i_{2}},\cdots,s_{i_{n}})
, and let
w^{\prime}=s_{i_{1}}w
. If

\dot{s}_{i_{1}}G^{-}_{\underline{v}_{+},\underline{w^{\prime}},>0}P_{J}^{+}/P_{J}^{+}\cap\mathcal{P}_{J,\geq 0}\neq\emptyset,

then
(s_{i_{1}},\underline{v}_{+})
is a positive subexpression of
s_{i_{1}}v
in
\underline{w}
.

Proof.

We first show that
\dot{s}_{i_{1}}G^{-}_{\underline{v}_{+},\underline{w^{\prime}},>0}B^{+}/B^{+}
lies in a single open Richardson variety. Clearly,
\dot{s}_{i_{1}}G^{-}_{\underline{v}_{+},\underline{w^{\prime}},>0}B^{+}/B^{+}\subseteq\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{+}_{w}(\mathbb{R})
. It remains to show this set lies in a single opposite Bruhat cell.

Let
\underline{v}_{+}=(t_{2},t_{3},\cdots,t_{n})
and define
v_{(j)}=t_{2}t_{3}\cdots t_{j}
. For each
\alpha\in\Phi
, fix an isomorphism
u_{\alpha}\colon\mathbb{R}\xrightarrow{\sim}U_{\alpha}
, with
u_{\alpha_{i}}=x_{i}
and
u_{-\alpha_{i}}=y_{i}
for every simple root
\alpha_{i}
.

An element of
\dot{s}_{i_{1}}G^{-}_{v,w^{\prime},>0}B^{+}/B^{+}
can be written as:

hB^{+}/B^{+}=\dot{s}_{i_{1}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n-1\\
v_{(j)}=v_{(j+1)}\end{subarray}}u_{-v_{j}(\alpha_{i_{j+1}})}(b_{j})\dot{v}B^{+}/B^{+}.

Since
\underline{v}_{+}
is positive, we have
v_{(j)}(\alpha_{i_{j+1}})>0
for all
2\leq j\leq n
. We have the following three cases:

(i)

v_{(j)}(\alpha_{i_{j+1}})\neq\alpha_{i_{1}}
for all
1\leq j\leq n-1
. Then

\dot{s}_{i_{1}}\prod u_{-v_{(j)}(\alpha_{i_{j+1}})}(b_{j})\dot{v}B^{+}/B^{+}\in U^{-}\dot{s}_{i_{1}}\dot{v}B^{+}/B^{+}.

This condition is equivalent to

being a positive subexpression in
\underline{w}
.

(ii)

v_{(j)}(\alpha_{i_{j+1}})=\alpha_{i_{1}}
for some
j
, and
s_{i_{1}}v>v
. In this case, we must have
v_{j}=v_{j+1}
(otherwise
v_{j+1}=v_{j}s_{i_{j+1}}
would imply
s_{i_{1}}v_{j+1}<v_{j+1}
and hence
s_{i_{1}}v<v
). Moreover,
b_{j}>0
by Lemma
4.5
. Then:

\displaystyle hB^{+}/B^{+}

\displaystyle\in U^{-}\dot{s}_{i_{1}}y_{i_{1}}(b)\dot{v}B^{+}/B^{+}

\displaystyle=U^{-}y_{i_{1}}(-b)x_{i_{1}}(b^{-1})\dot{v}B^{+}/B^{+}

\displaystyle\subseteq U^{-}\dot{v}B^{+}/B^{+}

for some
b>0
.

(iii)

v_{(j)}(\alpha_{i_{j+1}})=\alpha_{i_{1}}
for some
j
, and
s_{i_{1}}v<v
. Then

hB^{+}/B^{+}\in\dot{s}_{i_{1}}U^{-}\dot{v}B^{+}/B^{+}\subseteq U^{-}\dot{s}_{i_{1}}\dot{v}B^{+}/B^{+}.

In each case, there exists
v^{\prime}\leq w
such that:

\dot{s}_{i_{1}}G^{-}_{\underline{v}_{+},\underline{w^{\prime}},>0}P_{J}^{+}/P_{J}^{+}\subseteq\pi(\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{+}_{v^{\prime},w}(\mathbb{R})).

By assumption,
\dot{s}_{i_{1}}G^{-}_{\underline{v}_{+},\underline{w^{\prime}},>0}P_{J}^{+}/P_{J}^{+}\cap\mathcal{P}_{J,\geq 0}\neq\emptyset
. Since the left-hand side is connected and
\pi(\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{+}_{v^{\prime},w}(\mathbb{R}))\cap\mathcal{P}_{J,\geq 0}
is a connected component of
\pi(\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{+}_{v^{\prime},w}(\mathbb{R}))
(see
[
4
, §5.2]
), we have:

\dot{s}_{i_{1}}G^{-}_{\underline{v}_{+},\underline{w^{\prime}},>0}P_{J}^{+}/P_{J}^{+}\subseteq\pi(\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{+}_{v^{\prime},w}(\mathbb{R}))\cap\mathcal{P}_{J,\geq 0}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{J,\geq 0}.

We now show that only case (i) can occur. Suppose instead we are in case (ii) or (iii). Let
j
be the smallest index such that
v_{(j)}(\alpha_{i_{j+1}})=\alpha_{i_{1}}
. Then:

(s_{i_{1}},t_{2},\cdots,t_{j-1},s_{i_{j}},s_{i_{j+1}},\cdots,s_{i_{n}})

is a distinguished but non-positive subexpression in
\underline{w}
(see
[
24
, Definition 3.3]
). Let
D
be the corresponding Deodhar component (see
[
24
, Definition 4.1]
). Then:

\overline{\dot{s}_{i_{1}}G^{-}_{\underline{v}_{+},\underline{w^{\prime}},>0}P_{J}^{+}/P_{J}^{+}}\cap D\neq\emptyset.

But we have shown
\dot{s}_{i_{1}}G^{-}_{\underline{v}_{+},\underline{w^{\prime}},>0}P_{J}^{+}/P_{J}^{+}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{J,\geq 0}
, and since
\mathcal{P}_{J,\geq 0}
is closed, we get
D\cap\mathcal{P}_{J,\geq 0}\neq\emptyset
, contradicting
[
24
, Lemma 11.5]
.
∎

5.1.
Double flag variety as twisted product

The main objects here are the double flag variety and their stratification with respect to
G_{\text{diag}}
-orbits. For the convenience of applying the results from §
4
, we first introduce another formulation of the double flag varieties.

We consider the action of
B^{+}
on
G\times G/B^{\pm}
by
b\cdot(g,g^{\prime}B^{\pm})=(gb^{-1},bg^{\prime}B^{\pm})
. Let

\mathcal{Z}^{+,+}=G\times^{B^{+}}G/B^{+},\ \ \mathcal{Z}^{+,-}=G\times^{B^{+}}G/B^{-}

be the quotient spaces of
G\times G/B^{\pm}
by the action of
B^{+}
.

Define the convolution product

m:G\times^{B^{+}}G/B^{\pm}G/B^{\pm},\quad(g,g^{\prime}B^{\pm})gg^{\prime}B^{\pm}.

Let

. We define

\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}^{+,\pm}_{w,v}=(B^{+}\dot{w}B^{+},B^{+}\dot{v}B^{\pm}/B^{\pm}),\qquad\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}^{+,\pm;\,u}=m^{-1}(B^{-}\dot{u}B^{\pm}/B^{\pm}).

Let
\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{w,v}^{+,\pm;\,u}=\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}^{+,\pm}_{w,v}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}^{+,\pm;\,u}
. We have

\mathcal{Z}^{+,\pm}=\bigsqcup\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{w,v}^{+,\pm;\,u}

as stratified spaces.

There exists a natural isomorphism of varieties

\mathcal{Z}^{+,\pm}G/B^{+}\times G/B^{\pm},\qquad(g,g^{\prime}B^{\pm})(gB^{+}/B^{+},gg^{\prime}B^{\pm}/B^{\pm}).

We have the stratification
G/B^{+}=\bigsqcup\,\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{+}_{x,y}
, where
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{+}_{x,y}=B^{+}\dot{x}B^{+}/B^{+}\cap B^{-}\dot{y}B^{+}/B^{+}
is the corresponding open Richardson variety in
G/B^{+}
. Similarly, we have the stratification
G/B^{-}=\bigsqcup\,\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{-}_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}

Lemma 5.1

The subvariety
\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}^{u}_{w,v}

Proof.

5.4.

The poset of the stratification of

and

Let

Proposition 5.5
.

The poset

Proof.

We define

h:Q\tilde{Q}^{I}:(w,v,u)[i(u),th(w,v)]=[u,ws_{\infty}v].

The map

is injective and preserves partial orders. We may identify

It is easy to see that

is pure and thin. It is also clear that

is pure.

It remains to show that, for every

such that the rank of the interval

is

2

that are less than

. Suppose that

. Then
\ell(w)+\ell(u)-\ell(v)=1
. Let
(w^{\prime},v^{\prime},u^{\prime})\in Q
such that
(w^{\prime},v^{\prime},u^{\prime})<(w,v,u)
. Then
w^{\prime}\leq w
,

and
u^{\prime}\leq u
. Thus
w\circ_{l}v\leq w^{\prime}\circ_{l}v^{\prime}\leq u^{\prime}\leq u
. Since

.

Since
w\circ_{l}v\leq u

such that

,

and
\ell(cv)=\ell(v)-\ell(c)
. We have either

, or

Suppose first that

5.4.1.
EL-labeling

Suppose that

\lambda

to a poset
\Lambda

\Lambda

.

Now, we consider a labeling for

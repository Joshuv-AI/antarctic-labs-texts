An alternative condition for the solvability of the Dirichlet problem for the minimal surface equation on non-mean convex domains
A. Aiolfi, G. Nunes, J. Ripoll, L. Sauer, R. Soares

Abstract
We propose an alternative condition for the solvability of the Diri-chlet problem for the minimal surface equation that applies to non-mean convex domains. This condition is derived from a second-order ordinary differential equation whose solution produces a barrier that appears to be novel in the context of barrier constructions. It admits an explicit formulation and, in the setting of Hadamard manifolds, reveals a direct and transparent relationship between the geometry of the domain and the behavior of the boundary data required for solvability. The condition also extends naturally to unbounded domains. In the Euclidean case, it is not only more practical to verify but also less restrictive than the classical Jenkins - Serrin criterion, ensuring the existence of solutions in situations where that approach fail. Furthermore, unlike the Jenkins-Serrin condition, our appproach separates the geometric properties of the domain from its boundary data, providing a clearer and more manageable framework for solvability analysis.

Introduction

Let
M
be a complete
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
n\geq 2
, and
let
\Omega\varsubsetneq M
be a
C^{2}
-domain. The graph of a function
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
is a minimal graph in
M\times\mathbb{R}
if and only if
\mathcal{M}(u)=0
in
\Omega
, where

\mathcal{M}(u):=\left(1+|\nabla u|^{2}\right)\Delta u-\nabla^{2}u(\nabla u,\nabla u).

(1)

We consider the Dirichlet problem:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(u)

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,\quad u\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega}),

\displaystyle u|_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle=\varphi|_{\partial\Omega},

(2)

where
\varphi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
is given.

Let
H_{\partial\Omega}
denote the mean curvature of
\partial\Omega

with respect to the inner orientation, and define

\partial\Omega^{-}:=\mathrm{clos}\left\{p\in\partial\Omega\,;\,H_{\partial\Omega}(p)<0\right\}.

In the case of bounded domains, it is well known that if
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the Dirichlet problem for the minimal surface equation
admits a solution for arbitrary continuous boundary data if and only if
\partial\Omega^{-}=\emptyset
(
[
6
]
,
[
8
]
). The existence part
of this result has been generalized to Riemannian manifolds (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
). If
\partial\Omega^{-}\neq\emptyset
, then, as shown in Theorem
1 of
[
3
]
, which generalizes the classical result of H. Jenkins and J.
Serrin (Theorem 2 of
[
8
]
), a bound on the oscillation of the boundary
data
\varphi
is sufficient to ensure solvability of (
2
), namely,

\omega_{\varphi}(\partial\Omega):=\sup_{\partial\Omega}\varphi-\inf_{\partial\Omega}\varphi\leq B\left(|D\varphi|,|D^{2}\varphi|,|A|,\mathrm{Ric}_{M}\right),

(3)

where
|A|
is the norm of the second fundamental form of
\partial\Omega
, and
B
is an explicit function (see
[
3
]
, Section 2, p. 78 and
Lemma 4; see also
[
8
]
, Section 3, p. 179).

However, even in the Euclidean case, it is quite complicated to explicitly
verify whether a given
C^{2}
boundary function satisfies the oscillation
bounds required in the above results, since the expression for
B
depends
on a neighborhood of each point in
\partial\Omega^{-}
, which is hard to
describe explicitly. Furthermore,
B
involves both the function and the
domain, making it difficult to treat them separately. To illustrate this, we
briefly explain the function
B
:

(I)

In
[
8
]
,
B
is constructed as follows. Since
\partial\Omega
is
compact and of class
C^{2}
, there exists

(uniform for all

) such that the intersection
B_{l}(p)\cap\partial\Omega

consists of a single connected component that is the graph over a domain

in a hyperplane parallel to

of a function

satisfying
|\nabla g|<1
. Set
l^{\prime}=l/\sqrt{2}
. Also,
since
\partial\Omega
is compact and
C^{2}
, its principal curvatures
are uniformly bounded; let

be an absolute bound for these curvatures.
Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\frac{\pi}{l^{\prime}},k,|D^{2}\varphi|\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{A}}{(1+|D\varphi|^{2})^{8n}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=\max_{\partial\Omega}(-H_{\partial\Omega}).

Then
B
is defined as

B:=\frac{1}{16n\mathcal{A}}\cdot\begin{cases}\frac{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{H}},&\text{if }\mathcal{C}\leq\mathcal{H},\\
1+\log\left(\frac{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{H}}\right),&\text{if }0<\mathcal{H}<\mathcal{C},\\
+\infty,&\text{if }\mathcal{H}\leq 0.\end{cases}

(4)

(II)

In
[
3
]
,
B
is defined through an expression involving
|A|
and a
distance
\xi^{\prime}
to
\partial\Omega
,
0<\xi^{\prime}\leq d_{0}
, being
B=+\infty
at the mean convex points, where
d_{0}
is such that
the normal exponential map

\exp:\partial\Omega\times[0,d_{0})\longrightarrow U_{d_{0}}=\{z\in\Omega\,;\,d(z)<d_{0}\}

(5)

is a diffeomorphism (see Lemma 4 of
[
3
]
). Here,
\xi^{\prime}

plays the same role as

in the Jenkins-Serrin construction.

Given the above, we propose an alternative criterion to determine whether
the Dirichlet problem (
2
) admits a solution for a given
\varphi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
. Our goal is to retain the geometric insights of
previous results, while providing a more practical condition, at least in
the Euclidean case, that also applies to unbounded domains and, in the case
of Hadamard manifolds, can be phrased in a way that makes explicit the
relationship between the geometry of the domain and the behavior of the

). We are

, which uses assumptions relating the

-norm of

of the circles that, at each

(exterior circle condition). Our criterion arises from solving a
second-order ODE (
29
), which appears to be a novel method for
constructing barriers for the Dirichlet problem (

).

In special cases e.g., when
\Omega
is bounded and the

lies between two horizontal slices it is possible to provide simpler
sufficient conditions involving

to
ensure solvability (see
[
1
]
,
[
5
]
). Conditions on the
oscillation are also used for less regular boundary data, but they are
naturally more intricate (see
[
12
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
2
]
, etc.).

In order to state our results, we introduce some notation. Let

denote the injectivity radius function of

Definition 1

We say that
\partial\Omega^{-}
satisfies the

\partial\Omega^{-}\neq\emptyset

R:=\inf\left\{i(p_{0})\,;\,p_{0}\text{ is the center of }\Sigma_{p},\,p\in\partial\Omega^{-}\right\}.

(6)

This is abbreviated as the

-es condition.

Definition 2

We say that
\partial\Omega^{-}
satisfies the
locally Hadamard
r
-es condition
if it satisfies the
r
-es condition and, for each
p\in\partial\Omega^{-}
, letting
\Sigma_{p,s}:=d_{p}^{-1}(s)
for
s\in[0,R-r]
(with
\Sigma_{p,0}=\Sigma_{p}
and
d_{p}=d_{M}(\cdot,\Sigma_{p})
), and
denoting
H_{\Sigma_{p,s}}
the mean curvature of
\Sigma_{p,s}
with
respect to the outward orientation, we have:

\displaystyle H_{\Sigma_{p}}(p)

\displaystyle\leq H_{\Sigma_{p,s}}(x),

(7)

\displaystyle|\nabla^{2}d_{p}|(x)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\Sigma_{p}}|\nabla^{2}d_{p}|,

(8)

for all
x\in\Sigma_{p,s}\cap\Omega
and
s\in[0,R-r]
.

If
M
is a Hadamard manifold and
\partial\Omega^{-}
satisfies the
r
-es
condition, then it satisfies the locally Hadamard
r
-es condition with
R=\infty
. The name reflects that the curvature behavior of the geodesic
spheres
\Sigma_{p,s}
in this case mimics that of Hadamard manifolds.

Let us define:

\displaystyle\mu_{r}

\displaystyle:=\sup\left\{\max_{\Sigma_{p}}|\nabla^{2}d_{p}|\,;\,p\in\partial\Omega^{-}\right\},

(9)

\displaystyle\lambda_{r}

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{H_{\Sigma_{p}}(p)\,;\,p\in\partial\Omega^{-}\right\}.

(10)

Note that
\lambda_{r}<0
. For
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have
\lambda_{r}=-1/r

and
\mu_{r}=1/r
.

Let

\tau=\tau(\varphi):=\max\left\{\sup_{\overline{\Omega}}|\Delta\varphi|,\ \sup_{\overline{\Omega}}|\nabla\varphi|,\ \sup_{\overline{\Omega}}|\nabla^{2}\varphi|\right\},

(11)

\varrho:=\tau(1+2\tau^{2}),

(12)

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle:=\frac{2\tau^{3}+4\tau^{2}+3\tau}{\varrho},

(13)

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle:=-\frac{\tau^{2}+2\tau+2}{\varrho},

(14)

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle:=\frac{\tau^{2}}{\varrho},

(15)

and denote
\omega:=\omega_{\varphi}(\overline{\Omega})
.

If
\partial\Omega^{-}
satisfies the
r
-es condition, define

\theta:=a+b(n-1)\lambda_{r}+c\mu_{r}.

(16)

Note that
1<a<\theta
(the maximum possible value of
a
is
approximately
3.7321
).

We now state the main result.

Theorem 3

Let
\Omega\subset M^{n}
be a
C^{2}
-domain, where
M
is a
complete Riemannian manifold, and let
\varphi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})

be given. Assume that
\tau<\infty
if
\Omega
is unbounded, where
\tau=\tau(\varphi)
is defined in (
11
). If either:

(i)

\partial\Omega^{-}=\emptyset
, or

(ii)

\partial\Omega^{-}
satisfies the locally Hadamard
r
-es
condition, and

\omega\leq\frac{1}{\varrho(\theta-1)^{2}}\left[\theta\left(1-e^{-\varrho(\theta-1)\delta}\right)-\varrho(\theta-1)\delta\right],

(17)

where
\varrho
and
\theta
are given by (
12
) and (
16
),
respectively, and

\delta<\min\left\{R-r,\ \frac{\ln\theta}{\varrho(\theta-1)}\right\},

(18)

then the Dirichlet problem (
2
) admits a solution. Moreover, the
solution is unique if
\Omega
is bounded.

Corollary 4

Let
\Omega\subset M^{n}
be a
C^{2}
-domain, where
M
is a
Hadamard manifold, and let
\varphi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
be given. Assume that
\tau<\infty
if
\Omega
is unbounded, where
\tau=\tau(\varphi)
is defined in (
11
). If either:

(i)

\partial\Omega^{-}=\emptyset
, or

(ii)

\partial\Omega^{-}
satisfies the
r
-es condition, and

\frac{\sqrt{a-\ln a-1}-\sqrt{\varrho\omega}(a+c\mu_{r}-1)}{\sqrt{\varrho\omega}\,b(n-1)}\leq\lambda_{r},

(19)

then the Dirichlet problem (
2
) admits a solution. Moreover, the
solution is unique if
\Omega
is bounded.

We remark that on the right-hand side of inequality (
19
), we have only
geometric information about the domain (specifically
\lambda_{r},
the
infimum of the mean curvature of the non mean convex part of the boundary of

\Omega
), while the left-hand side depends on the function
\varphi
and
the ambient manifold
M
(via
\mu_{r}
).

In the particular case where
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\partial\Omega^{-}

satisfies the
r
-es condition, we have
\lambda_{r}=-1/r
and
\mu_{r}=1/r
, so inequality (
19
) becomes:

0<\frac{\sqrt{\varrho\omega}\left(c-b(n-1)\right)}{\sqrt{a-\ln a-1}-(a-1)\sqrt{\varrho\omega}}\leq r.

(20)

Therefore, our condition is less restrictive than that of
[
10
]
, where
r\geq 1
is required and, moreover, holds for arbitrary dimension. It also
allows us to conclude the solvability of the Dirichlet problem (
2
) in
examples where the Jenkins-Serrin condition cannot be applied, as
illustrated at the end of Section 3.

Regarding the connection with the Jenkins–Serrin condition, we emphasize
that, even though our argument depends on an extension of
\varphi\in C^{2}(\partial\Omega)
into
\overline{\Omega}
, it is always possible to
construct such an extension - still denoted by
\varphi
- on a
neighborhood
\Omega_{\varepsilon}\subset\Omega
of
\partial\Omega
,
for some
\varepsilon>0
, in such a way that
\sup_{\overline{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}\left|\nabla\varphi\right|=\sup_{\partial\Omega}\left|D\varphi\right|
and
\sup_{\overline{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}\left|\nabla^{2}\varphi\right|=\sup_{\partial\Omega}\left|D^{2}\varphi\right|
, where here

and
D^{2}\varphi
denote the first and second tangential derivatives of
\varphi
on
\partial\Omega
. (See, for example, the extension
constructed in Section 2.1 of
[
3
]
.)

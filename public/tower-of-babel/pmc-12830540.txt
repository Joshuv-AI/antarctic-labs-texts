CircRNA CeRNA networks in gastric cancer highlight FAP/FNDC1 as potential prognostic and therapeutic targets
Authors: Jing Yu, Long-Kuan Yin, Qian Li, Min Li, Xiang-Zhi Qin, Lei Gong, Long Qin, Yun-Hong Tian, Zhen-Bing Lv, Pan Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveCircular RNAs (circRNAs) are endogenous non-coding RNAs implicated in the initiation and progression of cancer. The circRNA–miRNA–mRNA competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network exerts a crucial influence on tumor prognosis and therapy. This research aimed to identify novel circRNA-driven ceRNA networks and potential prognostic/therapeutic targets for gastric cancer (GC).MethodsGastric cancer-related circRNA, miRNA, and mRNA datasets were retrieved from the GEO database, accompanied by clinical and expression data from TCGA. Differential expression analysis, ceRNA network construction (Cytoscape_v3.8.0), KEGG/GO enrichment analysis, and survival analyses were conducted using R software.ResultsKEGG analysis of mRNAs within the ceRNA network indicated enrichment in the IL-17 signaling pathway, TNF signaling pathway, and other pathways associated with GC prognosis. Two key ceRNA axes associated with GC prognosis were identified: (1) hsa_circ_0055521/hsa-miR-204-5p/FAP and (2) (hsa_circ_0005051, hsa_circ_0007613, hsa_circ_0045602, hsa_circ_0034398, hsa_circ_0006089)/hsa-miR-32-3p/FNDC1. Survival analysis and immunohistochemical validation (HPA database) verified that FAP and FNDC1 are potential prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets for GC.ConclusionThis study identifies three key pathways, two novel prognostic ceRNA networks, six prognosis-related circRNAs, and two target genes (FAP/FNDC1) for GC, offering new directions for GC therapy.

Introduction

Gastric cancer (GC), recognized as a prevalent global cause of cancer-related mortality [1–3], is predominantly prevalent in East Asian regions, including South Korea, China, and Japan [4, 5] The early clinical manifestations of gastric cancer are characterized by a lack of specificity [6–8], resulting in the late diagnosis of the majority of gastric cancer patients [9]. Despite the gradual expansion of diagnosis and treatment strategies for gastric cancer in recent decades, the treatment efficacy for advanced gastric cancer remains suboptimal, and the prognosis of patients continues to be unfavorable [10–12]. The complexity and multi-factorial nature of the pathogenic factors, as well as the mechanisms underlying the occurrence, development, and metastasis of gastric cancer, render the treatment of this disease more challenging and intricate. Patients with advanced gastric cancer exhibit a relatively low 5-year survival rate [13]. Consequently, there is an urgent requirement to identify additional therapeutic targets and optimize diagnosis and treatment measures to enhance patient survival and prognosis.

In recent years, with the advancement of gene technology, it has been discovered that there exist highly diverse regulatory patterns of various RNAs within cells. Among these, competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA) regulation stands as one of the most extensively investigated regulatory patterns. The ceRNA pertains to a novel mechanism of interaction among mRNA, long non-coding RNA (lncRNA), circRNA, etc., which mutually regulates each other at the post-transcriptional level via competition for and sharing of miRNAs [14].

Circular RNAs (circRNAs) represent a class of covalently closed single-stranded RNA molecules that are extensively distributed in eukaryotic cells. CircRNAs are a type of regulatory molecules with diverse functions, capable of fulfilling roles such as regulating gene expression, binding to microRNAs (miRNAs) or proteins, and undergoing translation into proteins. CircRNAs may hold significant implications for cell proliferation, cell differentiation, and individual growth and development, and they may be associated with the occurrence and progression of cancer [15–22].

Based on The Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database, this study constructs circRNAs as competitive endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs). CeRNAs bind to miRNAs to influence and regulate the expression of target genes. The ceRNA regulatory network based on the circRNA–miRNA–mRNA model plays a crucial role in tumor prognosis and treatment. This paper investigates the mechanism of the circRNA-related ceRNA regulatory network in gastric cancer.

The circRNA, miRNA, and mRNA datasets associated with gastric cancer were retrieved from GEO [23]. The limma package of R software (version R 4.2.1) was employed to identify the differential expression of circRNA (DEcircRNA), miRNAs (DEmiRNA), and mRNAs (DEmRNAs) between gastric cancer tissue and adjacent normal tissue. The relationships among RNAs were analyzed based on the circBase database, and a ceRNA network was constructed using the circRNA–miRNA–mRNA template with Cytoscape_v3.8.0. Subsequently, the target genes of ceRNA were analyzed through the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) [24], Gene Ontology (GO) [25], and survival analysis. The mRNA expression and clinical data related to gastric cancer were extracted from The Cancer Genome Atlas [26] (TCGA) database, along with the survival data of gastric cancer. Then, in combination with the relevant gastric cancer data from the TCGA and GEO databases, the potential relationships between circRNA, miRNA, mRNA, and the prognosis of gastric cancer were explored. New potential ceRNA regulatory networks were searched for, and potential therapeutic targets were identified via this network.

Materials and methods

Data download and processing

The gastric cancer-related GSE83521 circRNA dataset, the miRNA GSE93415 dataset, and the mRNA GSE118916 dataset were downloaded from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). The gastric cancer expression data extracted from the TCGA database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) were sorted for expression analysis, and the clinical data of gastric cancer were extracted.

Difference analysis was conducted on circRNA, MiRNA and mRNA data respectively

Initially, a differential analysis of circRNA, miRNA, and mRNA was respectively conducted, followed by a heat-map analysis of circRNA, miRNA, and mRNA with significant differences.

The circRNA, miRNA, and mRNA of the ceRNA network were extracted, and the ceRNA network diagram was constructed. The expression differences of circRNA, miRNA, and mRNA, which constitute the ceRNA network map, between gastric cancer tissue and adjacent normal tissue were analyzed.

Firstly, the structural diagram of differential circRNA was retrieved via the circBase database, and the corresponding miRNA response element (MRE) was identified through differential circRNA. The intersection of different miRNAs and MREs was obtained using the Venn package of R software (version R 4.2.1) to acquire the common miRNAs. By determining the target genes (miRNA targets) for these common miRNAs and then intersecting the differentially expressed mRNAs (diff mRNAs) with the target genes (miRNA targets), the common mRNAs were obtained. Finally, the ceRNA network diagram was established using Cytoscape 3.8.0. The differences in circRNA, miRNA, and mRNA within the ceRNA between tumor tissue and normal tissue were analyzed, and heat maps and box plots were generated.

The mRNA in CeRNA network was analyzed by GO and KEGG

The messenger ribonucleic acid (mRNA) within the competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network was subjected to Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analyses to identify potential signaling pathways.

A competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network diagram associated with the prognosis of gastric cancer was constructed by leveraging the The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) databases. Expression data, clinical data, and gastric cancer data from the GEO database were separately extracted from the TCGA database. Based on these data, a prognostic correlation network map for gastric cancer was constructed.

Immunohistochemical analysis of proteins was conducted via the Human Protein Atlas (HPA) database. Immunohistochemical analysis of the network from ceRNA mRNA associated with gastric cancer prognosis to proteins was carried out using the HPA database (https://www.proteinatlas.org/).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted utilizing R software. A P-value less than 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant.

Results

Differences of circRNA, MiRNA and mRNA were analyzed respectively

Through differential analysis, 24 significantly differentially expressed circRNAs, 91 significantly differentially expressed miRNAs, and 114 significantly differentially expressed mRNAs were identified. Via heat map analysis, it was found that 24 significantly differentially expressed circRNAs exhibited low expression levels in normal tissues and nearly high expression levels in tumor tissues (Fig. 1A). The expression of 38 miRNAs was high in normal tissues and low in tumor tissues. Fifty-three miRNAs were expressed in normal tissues and showed high expression in tumor tissues (Fig. 1B). Eighty-two mRNAs were highly expressed in normal tissues and lowly expressed in tumor tissues. Thirty-two mRNAs were lowly expressed in normal tissues and highly expressed in tumor tissues (Fig. 1C).

The structural diagram of circRNA (supplementary material) and the set of miRNA response elements (MRE) were retrieved from the circBase database. The intersection of different miRNAs and MRE was determined using the Venn package in R software (version R 4.2.1), yielding 20 identical miRNAs (Fig. 2A). By searching for target genes of these 20 identical mRNAs, a total of 17,382 miRNA targets were identified. Subsequently, the intersection of differentially expressed miRNAs (DEmiRNAs) and the target genes (miRNA targets) was calculated, resulting in 102 common mRNAs (Fig. 2B). Finally, the competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network diagram was constructed using Cytoscape_3_8_0_windows_64bit. It was found that 13 circRNAs, 11 miRNAs, and 15 mRNAs constituted the nodes of the ceRNA network, and the ceRNA network diagram was constructed (Fig. 3).

Discussion

Currently, non-coding RNA (ncRNA) can be classified into microRNAs (miRNAs), long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), circular RNAs (circRNAs), and PIWI-interacting RNA (piRNA) based on their length, conformation, and subcellular location. As a functional regulatory molecule involved in mediating cellular processes such as chromatin remodeling, transcription, post-transcriptional modification, and signal transduction, the networks in which ncRNAs are involved can impact numerous molecular targets, thereby triggering specific cellular biological responses and determining cell fates, which subsequently influence the development and progression of tumors [27, 28]. Based on the analysis of gastric cancer data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) databases, this study investigated the competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network composed of circRNAs that may affect the prognosis of gastric cancer and its potential mechanism.

Notably, recent investigations have further broadened our comprehension of circRNA-ceRNA mechanisms in gastric cancer (GC), offering supplementary evidence for our research findings. For example, Deng et al. demonstrated that exosome-derived circATP8A1 facilitates GC progression by inducing macrophage M2 polarization through the miR-1-3p/STAT6 axis [29]. This study underscores a novel aspect of circRNA function, namely, cell-to-cell communication via exosomes, in which circRNAs modulate the immune tumor microenvironment (TME). While our study concentrated on intracellular ceRNA networks, the work of Deng et al. implies that circRNAs (including potential homologs of the circRNAs we identified) may also influence the TME, a process closely associated with the IL-17 and TNF inflammatory pathways we identified. This suggests that the circRNA-ceRNA networks we characterized may interact with immune regulatory pathways to promote GC malignancy, thereby opening up possibilities for combined immunotherapeutic and circRNA-targeted strategies.

Another study by Dong et al. reported that EGCG-LYS fibrils-mediated silencing of circMAP2K2 curtails GC cell proliferation and metastasis both in vitro and in vivo [30]. This work is particularly relevant to our identification of circRNAs as therapeutic targets, as it demonstrates that targeted delivery of circRNA-silencing agents (e.g., via EGCG-LYS fibrils) can effectively impede GC progression. Extrapolating this to our findings, the six prognosis-related circRNAs (hsa_circ_0055521, hsa_circ_0005051, etc.) could potentially be targeted using similar nanocarrier systems, providing a translational framework for developing GC therapies based on our ceRNA networks. Moreover, Dong et al.’s focus on circMAP2K2, another oncogenic circRNA in GC, supports the broader hypothesis that circRNAs are recurrent drivers of GC and validates the utility of targeting them.

Miao et al. further complemented our ceRNA model by showing that circ_0110940 exerts anti-apoptotic and pro-proliferative effects in GC cells through the miR-1178-3p/SLC38A6 axis [31]. This study is consistent with our observation that circRNA-ceRNA networks regulate core cellular processes (proliferation, apoptosis) in GC, although it involves a distinct circRNA–miRNA–mRNA axis. The consistency in regulatory logic, where circRNA sponges miRNA to upregulate a pro-tumor mRNA, reinforces the generality of the ceRNA mechanism in GC and emphasizes that the networks we identified (e.g., hsa_circ_0055521/hsa-miR-204-5p/FAP) are part of a larger, interconnected regulatory landscape. Additionally, the anti-apoptotic role of circ_0110940 suggests that our FAP/FNDC1-centered networks may also modulate apoptosis, an area that requires further experimental validation.

Wan et al. provided additional evidence for the oncogenic role of circRNAs in GC, showing that knockdown of circ_0003789 inhibits tumor progression through the miR-429/ZFP36L2 axis [32]. This study mirrors our finding that high expression of specific circRNAs (e.g., our 24 DEcircRNAs) drives GC malignancy. Knockdown of circ_0003789 reduces GC cell viability and invasion, similar to the predicted effects of silencing the circRNAs we identified. Furthermore, ZFP36L2 (the downstream target in Wan et al.’s study) has been linked to epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), a process also regulated by MMP9 in our TNF pathway analysis. This suggests potential crosstalk between distinct ceRNA networks (e.g., circ_0003789/miR-429/ZFP36L2 and our hsa_circ_0055521/hsa-miR-204-5p/FAP) via common oncogenic processes like EMT, highlighting the complexity of circRNA-mediated regulation in GC.

This research discovered that IL-17 assumes a crucial role in the genesis and progression of gastric cancer. Firstly, IL-17 can activate the downstream signaling pathway via receptors such as NF-κB and MAPK to induce the expression of cytokines, chemokines, and so forth. IL-17 A can induce and up-regulate VEGF through the STAT3 pathway, thereby promoting neovascularization. Nevertheless, the expression of VEGF is associated with the progression and metastasis of gastric cancer. IL-17B/IL-17RB may facilitate tumor progression, tumor cell growth, and migration by altering the tumor microenvironment, resulting in a poor prognosis for patients with gastric cancer [33–35]. IL-17 can also influence the occurrence and development of gastric cancer by regulating Beclin ubiquitination and autophagy [36]. In this study, KEGG analysis indicated that IL- 17 may further impact the occurrence and development of gastric cancer through PTGS2. GO analysis demonstrated that PTGS2 can act on VEGF, which may be involved in apoptosis, proliferation, metastasis, invasion, recurrence, and metastasis of gastric cancer cells [37–42].

In the investigation of the TNF signaling pathway, it was discovered that T cells might impact the microenvironment of gastric cancer via TNF, thereby influencing the development of gastric cancer [43]. Additionally, it has been reported that TNF may affect the progression of gastric cancer by mediating epithelial–mesenchymal interactions through signaling [43]. In the present study, it was found that the TNF signaling pathway can also affect the occurrence and development of gastric cancer through MMP9. MMP9 participates in the invasion and metastasis of gastric cancer through the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway [44].

Via bioinformatics screening, two competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) networks associated with the prognosis of gastric cancer were identified (Fig. 5). These newly discovered ceRNA networks deviate from previous research findings and represent novel ceRNA networks pertinent to the prognosis of gastric cancer. The present study posits that hsa_circ_0055521 may regulate the expression of hsa-miR-204-5p via a sponge effect, thereby affecting the expression of fibroblast activation protein (FAP) and subsequently influencing the onset, progression, and prognosis of gastric cancer. This regulatory relationship has not been documented in prior studies. Additionally, it was observed that high expression of FAP may be correlated with chemotherapy resistance, poor prognosis, and peritoneal metastasis of gastric cancer [45, 46] further validating the involvement of hsa_circ_0055521 in the regulation of gastric cancer prognosis through the modulation of FAP. Another circRNA-based prognostic network for gastric cancer reveals that hsa_circ_0005051, hsa_circ_0007613, hsa_circ_0045602, hsa_circ_0034398, and hsa_circ_0006089 could regulate the expression of fibronectin type III domain containing 1 (FNDC1) by competitively sponging hsa-miR-32-3p, thereby impacting the onset, progression, and prognosis of gastric cancer [47]. Although some studies [48–51] have suggested the relevance of hsa_circ_0005051 to the prognosis of gastric cancer, research on the other four circRNAs remains unreported. Studies on the second target gene, FNDC1, indicate that its high expression may be correlated with poor prognosis of gastric cancer, yet the specific mechanisms require further exploration. In conclusion, FNDC1 and FAP may influence the prognosis of gastric cancer, but their specific mechanisms necessitate further experimental verification.

Lastly, protein immunohistochemistry analysis was carried out on the two target genes identified in this research through the HPA database. Noticeable differences in the protein expression of FNDC1 and FAP were detected between cancerous tissues and adjacent normal tissues. This further validates the expression discrepancies of FNDC1 and FAP in gastric cancer (GC) tissues and normal tissues, suggesting their potential crucial functions in the initiation and progression of GC, which have implications for prognosis.

It is necessary to recognize several limitations of this study. Firstly, the datasets of circRNA, miRNA, and mRNA analyzed were sourced from specific GEO series (GSE83521, GSE93415, GSE118916) and TCGA, which might limit the generalizability of the findings to more heterogeneous gastric cancer populations. Consequently, larger, multi-ethnic cohorts with comprehensive clinical annotations are required to validate and reinforce these results. Secondly, the newly identified circRNA-driven ceRNA networks, including the hsa_circ_0055521/hsa-miR-204-5p/FAP network and the axis composed of five circRNAs (hsa_circ_0005051, hsa_circ_0007613, hsa_circ_0045602, hsa_circ_0034398, hsa_circ_0006089), hsa-miR-32-3p, and FNDC1, have not undergone external validation in independent patient cohorts. Prospective clinical studies are essential to confirm their prognostic significance and clinical utility. Thirdly, although FAP and FNDC1 were proposed as potential therapeutic targets, their specific functional roles in the progression of gastric cancer and their direct interactions within the identified ceRNA networks remain ambiguous. Further in vitro and in vivo experiments, such as knockdown/overexpression assays and signaling pathway validation, are needed to clarify these mechanisms. Fourthly, the immunohistochemical analysis of FAP and FNDC1 solely relied on data from the HPA database, lacking experimental validation in independent clinical specimens. Additional studies utilizing primary gastric cancer tissues are required to confirm the observed differences in protein expression.

Conclusion

Via bioinformatics analysis, at least three pathways that potentially influence the onset, progression, and prognosis of gastric cancer were discovered. Additionally, two novel competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) networks related to gastric cancer prognosis, six circular RNAs (circRNAs) potentially correlated with gastric cancer prognosis, and two potential genes associated with gastric cancer prognosis were identified. This offers a possible new avenue for further research on the treatment of gastric cancer.

1.
Introduction

Investigating uniformly convex spaces, Clarkson
[
3
]
proved the following inequalities for
L^{p}
norms:

(1.1)

\begin{gathered}2(\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p})\leq\|f+g\|_{p}^{p}+\|f-g\|_{p}^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}(\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}),\qquad p\geq 2\\
2(\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p})\geq\|f+g\|_{p}^{p}+\|f-g\|_{p}^{p}\geq 2^{p-1}(\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}),\qquad p\leq 2\end{gathered}

(1.2)

\|f+g\|_{p}^{p}+\|f-g\|_{p}^{p}\leq 2(\|f\|_{p}^{q}+\|g\|_{p}^{q})^{p/q},\qquad p\geq 2,\;q=p/(p-1).

(1.3)

\|f+g\|_{p}^{q}+\|f-g\|_{p}^{q}\leq 2^{q-1}(\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p})^{q/p},\qquad p\leq 2,\;q=p/(p-1).

Later McCarthy
[
10
]
generalized these inequalities to Schatten classes of operators. He replaced measurable functions
f
and
g
by compact operators
A
and
B
, and
L^{p}
norm by
C_{p}
norm defined as

\|A\|_{p}=\left(\tr(|A|^{p})\right)^{1/p}.

The inequalities he obtained was exactly the inequalities (
1.1
), (
1.2
) and (
1.3
). In operator framework, these inequalities are usually referred as Clarkson-McCarthy inequalities.

Bhatia and Kittaneh
[
2
]
, proved the following generalization for
n
-tuple of operators. Among others, for
1<p\leq 2
, they proved in Theorem 1.4:

(1.4)

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Big\|\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\omega_{j}^{k}A_{j}\Big\|_{p}^{q}\leq n\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\|A_{j}\|_{p}^{p}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}},

where
\omega_{j}=e^{2\pi ij/n}
is the
j
-th degree of the
n
-th root of unity.

The first author in
[
9
]
generalized these inequalities to compact topological groups. Among others, he proved in Theorem 3.6:

(1.5)

\sum_{k\in\hat{G}}\left\|\int_{G}\overline{k(\theta)}A_{\theta}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\right\|_{p}^{q}\leq\left(\int_{G}\|A_{\theta}\|_{p}^{p}\right)^{q/p},

for compact abelian group
G
and the corresponding Haar measure
\mu
. This inequality is a generalization of both(
1.3
) if
G=\mathbf{Z}_{2}
and (
1.4
) if
G=\mathbf{Z}_{n}
.

In the same paper in Problem 5.4, it is asked whether it is possible to generalize (
1.5
) to locally compact groups. It is also explained why other Clarkson-McCarthy inequalities (e.g. (
1.1
) and (
1.2
)) can’t be generalized to locally compact groups in a straightforward way.

The aim of this paper is to generalize (
1.5
) to locally compact groups. The main result is contained in Theorem
3.2
.

The paper is organized as follows: in section 2, we give a brief recapitulation of topics, necessary for further reading. Section 3 is devoted to main results. Finally, in section 4, we derive applications of the main results.

2.
Prerequisits

2.1.
Schatten ideals

Let
H
be a separable Hilbert space, and let
A:H\to H
be a compact operator. Its singular values
s_{n}(A)
are defined as the eigenvalues of its absolute value
(A^{*}A)^{1/2}
, i.e.
s_{n}(A)=\lambda_{n}(A^{*}A)^{1/2}
.

The Schatten ideala
\mathcal{C}_{p}
(
1\leq p<\infty
) are defined as

\mathcal{C}_{p}=\{A:H\to H\mid A\mbox{ compact},\,\|A\|_{p}=\tr(|A|^{p})^{1/p}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}^{p}(A)\right)^{1/p}<\infty\}.

For
p=\infty
,
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
is the set of all compact operators, with the usual norm inherited from
B(H)
.

It is well known that all
\mathcal{C}_{p}
are Banach spaces, ideals in
B(H)
, aa well as the following duality results

(\mathcal{C}_{p})^{*}\cong\mathcal{C}_{q},\quad 1<p<\infty\quad\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1,\qquad(\mathcal{C}_{1})^{*}\cong B(H),\qquad(\mathcal{C}_{\infty})^{*}\cong B(H).

For more details, see
[
6
, Chapter III]
.

2.2.
Abstract harmonic analysis

Let
(G,+)
be an abelian group with locally compact Hausdorff topology such that the operations in
G
are continuous with respect to it. As usual, we shall use the name
locally compact group
.

Haar measure
on a locally compact group is a regular, Borel and invariant measure
\mu
on
G
. (The latter means
\mu(E)=\mu(x+E)
.) Haar measure is unique up to a positive multiplicative scalar. If
G
is compact it is usual to normalize the Haar measure by
\mu(G)=1
. However, if
G
is only locally compact then, in general, there is no natural normalization.

A
character
on
G
is a homomorphism from
(G,+)
to
(\mathbf{T},\cdot)
where
\mathbf{T}=\{z\in\mathbf{C}\mid|z|=1\}
, i.e. a mapping
\xi:G\to\mathbf{T}
such that
\xi(x+y)=\xi(x)\xi(y)
.

Fourier transform
or
abstract Fourier transform
of
f\in L^{1}(G)
is

\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{G}f(t)\overline{\xi(t)}\mathrm{d}\mu(t),

where
\xi\in\hat{G}
and
\mu
is the Haar measure on
G
.

The
Pontryagin dual
of
G
denoted by
\hat{G}
is the set of all characters. It becomes a locally compact group if it is endowed by
compact-open
topology. Therefore,
\hat{G}
has, also, the Haar measure, denote by
\nu
or
\hat{\mu}
. It is normalized such that the following
inversion formula
holds:

f(t)=\int_{\hat{G}}\hat{f}(\xi)\xi(t)\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi),\qquad\mbox{for }f\in L^{1}(G)\mbox{ and }\hat{f}\in L^{1}(\hat{G}).

Many statements, such as Plancharel theorem, can be generalized to the abstract harmonic analysis. For more details see
[
5
]
.

2.3.
Bochner spaces

Let
(\Omega,\mu)
be a measurable space and let
X
be a Banach space. The
Bochner space

L^{p}(\Omega;X)
for
1\leq p<\infty
is defined as the set of strongly measurable functions
f:\Omega\to X
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;X)}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\|f(t)\|_{X}^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu(t)\right)^{1/p}<+\infty,

after identification of
\mu
-almost everywhere equal functions. Here, strong measurability is equivalent to weak measurability (that is, the measurability of scalar functions
t\mapsto\Lambda(f(t))
for all
\Lambda\in X^{*}
) and separability of the image of
f
.

In a similar way
L^{\infty}(\Omega;X)
can be defined, with

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;X)}=\inf\{r\mid\mu\{\|f\|_{X}>r\}=0\}.

Bochner integral is linear, additive with respect to disjoint union, and also there holds

(2.1)

T\int_{\Omega}f(t)\mathrm{d}\mu(t)=\int_{\Omega}Tf(t)\mathrm{d}\mu(t)

for all
f\in L^{p}(\Omega;X)
and all bounded linear
T:X\to Y
, provided that the left hand side exists.

In particular, for a Hilbert space
H
, the mapping
B(H)\ni A\mapsto\left<Ag,h\right>\in\mathbf{C}
, for fixed
g
,
h\in H
is linear and bounded, and from (
2.1
) we obtain

(2.2)

\left<\left(\int_{\Omega}A_{t}\mathrm{d}\mu(t)\right)g,h\right>=\int_{\Omega}\left<A_{t}g,h\right>\mathrm{d}\mu,

for any
A_{t}\in L^{1}(\Omega;B(H))
and all
g
,
h\in H
.

Remark 2.1
.

Note a slightly more general result in
[
8
, Lemma 1.2]
that assumes only weak-* measurability of
A_{t}
.

For more details, the reader is referred to
[
7
, Chapters 1 and 2]
.

2.4.
Complex interpolation

Let us briefly explain basic concepts of complex interpolation. For more detailed approach the reader is referred to
[
1
]
(see also
[
7
, Appendix C]
).

Given two Banach spaces, say
X
and
Y
, both of them continuously embedded in some Hausdorff topological vector space
V
, consider the spaces
X\cap Y
and
X+Y
with norms given by

\|a\|_{X\cap Y}=\max\{\|a\|_{X},\|a\|_{Y}\},\qquad\|a\|_{X+Y}=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=a_{1}+a_{2}\\
a_{1}\in X,a_{2}\in Y\end{subarray}}(\|a_{1}\|_{X}+\|a_{2}\|_{Y}).

Such a pair
(X,Y)
is called
a compatible pair
.

Consider the set
\mathcal{F}
consisting of all functions
f:\overline{S}\to X+Y
, analytic and bounded in the strip
S=\{z\in\mathbf{C}\mid 0<\mathrm{Re}z<1\}
and continuous on the closure
\overline{S}
such that:

f(it)\in X,\quad f(1+it)\in Y,\qquad\mbox{for all }t\in\mathbf{R}.

The set
\mathcal{F}
is, obviously, a linear space and moreover a Banach space if the norm is given by

\|f\|=\max\{\sup_{t\in\mathbf{R}}\|f(it)\|_{X},\sup_{t\in\mathbf{R}}\|f(1+it)\|_{Y}\}.

Now, the interpolation space
[X,Y]_{\theta}
,
\theta\in[0,1]
is defined by

[X,Y]_{\theta}=\{a\in X+Y\mid\mbox{there is an }f\in\mathcal{F},f(\theta)=a\},

and the norm that makes it a Banach space, is given by

\|a\|_{\theta}=\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F},f(\theta)=a}\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}}.

The space
[X,Y]_{\theta}
has the property of being
exact interpolation space
. This is described in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(X_{1},Y_{1})
and
(X_{2},Y_{2})
be two compatible pairs, and let
T:X_{1}+Y_{1}\to X_{2}+Y_{2}
be a linear mapping, bounded
X_{1}
to
X_{2}
, and from
Y_{1}
to
Y_{2}
. Then
T
is a bounded operator from
[X_{1},Y_{1}]_{\theta}
to
[X_{2},Y_{2}]_{\theta}
and the following estimate holds:

\|T\|_{B([X_{1},Y_{1}]_{\theta},[X_{2},Y_{2}]_{\theta}}\leq\|T\|_{B(X_{1},X_{2})}^{1-\theta}\|T\|_{B(Y_{1},Y_{2})}^{\theta}.

Proof.

See
[
1
, Theorem 4.1.2]
or
[
7
, Theorem C.2.6]
.∎

Besides this general theorem dealing with complex interpolation, we need two results concerning concrete examples of Banach space.

Firstly, it is well known that for classic Lebesgue spaces we have

(L^{p}(\Omega,\mu),L^{q}(\Omega,\mu))_{\theta}=L^{r}(\Omega,\mu),\qquad 1/r=(1-\theta)/p+\theta/q.

However, there is a more general result:

Theorem 2.2
.

[
7
, Theorem 2.2.6]

Let
1\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}<\infty
or
1\leq p_{1}<=\infty
and let
0<\theta<1
. For any interpolation couple
(X_{1},X_{2})
of complex Banach spaces and any measure space
(\Omega,\mu)
we have

[L^{p_{1}}(\Omega;X_{1}),L^{p_{2}}(\Omega;X_{2}]_{\theta}=L^{p_{\theta}}(\Omega;[X_{1},X_{2}]_{\theta})

isometrically, with
\frac{1}{p_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{1}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{2}}
.

Also, we need the interpolation result for Schatten classes.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
7
, Theorem D.3.1]

Let
\mathcal{C}^{p}
,
1\leq p\leq+\infty
denote the Schatten class of compact operators on some Hilbert space
H
. Then for all
\theta\in[0,1]
we have

[\mathcal{C}^{p_{1}},\mathcal{C}^{p_{2}}]_{\theta}=\mathcal{C}^{p},

where
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{1}+\theta/p_{2}
.

As a corollary of preceding two theorems we have

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
1\leq p_{1}
,
p_{2}
,
q_{1}
,
q_{2}\leq+\infty
, let
\theta\in[0,1]
and let
(\Omega,\mu)
be some measure space. Then

[L^{p_{1}}(\Omega;\mathcal{C}^{q_{1}}),L^{p_{2}}(\Omega;\mathcal{C}^{q_{2}})]_{\theta}=L^{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{C}^{q}),

where
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{1}+\theta/p_{2}
and
1/q=(1-\theta)/q_{1}+\theta/q_{2}
.

3.
Main results

First, we establish Parseval identity for functions from Bochner spaces, which is the key technical tool in this paper.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G
be a locally compact abelian group, let
\hat{G}
be its Pontryagin dual. For
A_{\theta}\in L^{2}(G;B(H))\cap L^{1}(G;B(H))
, and
\xi\in\hat{G}
, the operators

B_{\xi}=\int_{G}\overline{\xi(\theta)}A_{\theta}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)

are well defined and also

(3.1)

\int_{\hat{G}}|B_{\xi}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)=\int_{G}|A_{\theta}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta),

Proof.

Since
\||A_{\theta}|^{2}\|=\|A_{\theta}\|^{2}
and
A_{\theta}\in L^{2}(G;B(H))
, the integral

\int_{G}|A_{\theta}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)

exists. By (
2.2
) we obtain

\left<\int_{G}|A_{\theta}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)h,h\right>=\int_{G}\left<|A_{\theta}|^{2}h,h\right>\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta).

On the other hand, since
|\xi(\theta)|=1
and
A_{\theta}\in L^{1}(G;B(H))
we have

\displaystyle\int_{G}\|\overline{\xi(\theta)}A_{\theta}\|\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)=\int_{G}\|A_{\theta}\|\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)<\infty

and
B_{\xi}
are well defined.

Let
\{e_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be an arbitrary orthonormal basis of
H
. Then for all
h\in H

\displaystyle\left<\int_{G}|A_{\theta}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\,h,h\right>

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\|A_{\theta}h\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)=\int_{G}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left|\left<A_{\theta}h,e_{m}\right>\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{G}\left|\left<A_{\theta}h,e_{m}\right>\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta).

The function
f\colon G\to\mathbf{C}
,
f(\theta)=\left<A_{\theta}h,e_{m}\right>
belongs to
L^{1}(G)\cap L^{2}(G)
for every
h\in H
and
e_{m}
since

\int_{G}|\left<A_{\theta}h,e_{m}\right>|\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\leq\|h\|\int_{G}\|A_{\theta}\|\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)<\infty

and

\int_{G}|\left<A_{\theta}h,e_{m}\right>|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\leq\|h\|^{2}\int_{G}\|A_{\theta}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)<\infty.

By the Plancherel theorem (
[
5
, Theorem 4.26]
) we have

\int_{G}|f(\theta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)=\int_{\hat{G}}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi),

where
\hat{f}
the Fourier transform of
f
on
G
, and hence

\int_{G}\left|\left<A_{\theta}h,e_{m}\right>\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)=\int_{\hat{G}}\left|\int_{G}\overline{\xi(\theta)}\left<A_{\theta}h,e_{m}\right>\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{G}\left|\left<A_{\theta}h,e_{m}\right>\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{\hat{G}}\left|\int_{G}\overline{\xi(\theta)}\left<A_{\theta}h,e_{m}\right>\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{\hat{G}}\left|\int_{G}\left<\overline{\xi(\theta)}A_{\theta}h,e_{m}\right>\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{G}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left|\left<\int_{G}\overline{\xi(\theta)}A_{\theta}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)h,e_{m}\right>\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi).

The last equality follows from (
2.2
) because
\left<\overline{\xi(\theta)}A_{\theta}h,h\right>\in L^{1}(G,\mu)
for all
h\in H
. Hence,

\displaystyle\left<\int_{G}|A_{\theta}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\,h,h\right>

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{G}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left|\left<\int_{G}\overline{\xi(\theta)}A_{\theta}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\,h,e_{m}\right>\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{G}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left|\left<B_{\xi}h,e_{m}\right>\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)=\int_{\hat{G}}\|B_{\xi}h\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{G}}\left<|B_{\xi}|^{2}h,h\right>\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)=\left<\int_{\hat{G}}|B_{\xi}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)h,h\right>.

The last equality, again, follows from (
2.2
), since
\left<|B_{\xi}|^{2}h,h\right>\in L^{1}(\hat{G},\nu)
for all
h\in H
. Thus,

\int_{G}|A_{\theta}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)=\int_{\hat{G}}|B_{\xi}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi).

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group with Haar measure
\mu
, let
\hat{G}
be its Pontryagin dual with the dual measure
\nu
. For all
1\leq p\leq 2
, and
A_{\theta}\in L^{1}(G;\mathcal{C}_{p})\cap L^{2}(G;\mathcal{C}_{p})
there holds

B_{\xi}\in L^{q}(\hat{G},\mathcal{C}_{p})
and

(3.2)

\int_{\hat{G}}\left\|\int_{G}A_{\theta}\overline{\xi(\theta)}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\right\|_{p}^{q}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\leq\left(\int_{G}\|A_{\theta}\|_{p}^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\right)^{q/p},

where
q
is conjugate to
p
, i.e.
q=p/(p-1)
,
p\neq 1
and
q=+\infty
if
p=1
.

Remark 3.1
.

Of course, (
3.2
) doesn’t make sense for
q=+\infty
. However, it should be considered as the power "to the
q
" is moved from the right hand side to the left and then the limit as
q\to+\infty
is taken, i.e.

\sup_{\xi\in\hat{G}}\left\|\int_{G}A_{\theta}\overline{\xi(\theta)}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\right\|_{1}\leq\int_{G}\|A_{\theta}\|_{1}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta).

The same apply for corollaries stated in Theorems
4.1
,
4.2
and
4.5
.

Proof.

It is well known that for any
A\in\mathcal{C}_{p}
we have
\|A\|\leq\|A\|_{p}
. Hence
A_{\theta}\in L^{1}(G;\mathcal{C}_{p})\cap L^{2}(G;\mathcal{C}_{p})
implies
A_{\theta}\in L^{1}(G;B(H))\cap L^{2}(G;B(H))
, and consequently, (
3.1
) holds. Therefore, by (
2.1
) with
T=\tr
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{G}}\|B_{\xi}\|_{2}^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{G}}\tr(|B_{\xi}|^{2})\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)=\tr\int_{\hat{G}}|B_{\xi}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)=\tr\int_{G}|A_{\theta}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\tr(|A_{\theta}|^{2})\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)=\int_{G}\|A_{\theta}\|_{2}^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta).

It means that the linear mapping
A_{\theta}\mapsto B_{\xi}
, defined on the dense set
L^{1}(G;\mathcal{C}_{2})\cap L^{2}(G;\mathcal{C}_{2})
in
L^{2}(G;\mathcal{C}_{2})
is an isometry. Hence, it can be extended to an isometry from the whole
L^{2}(G;\mathcal{C}_{2})
to
L^{2}(\hat{G};\mathcal{C}_{2})
. Denote this isometry by
T
.

On the other hand, if
A_{\theta}\in L^{1}(G;\mathcal{C}_{1})\cap L^{2}(G;\mathcal{C}_{1})
(which is dense in
L^{1}(G;\mathcal{C}_{1})
), then

\|B_{\xi}\|_{1}=\left\|\int_{G}A_{\theta}\overline{\xi(\theta)}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\right\|_{1}\leq\int_{G}\|A_{\theta}\|_{1}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)=\|A_{\theta}\|_{L^{1}(G;\mathcal{C}_{1})},

and taking a supremum over all
\xi\in\hat{G}

\|B_{\xi}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{G};\mathcal{C}_{1})}\leq\|A_{\theta}\|_{L^{1}(G;\mathcal{C}_{1})}.

This leads to a bounded, densely defined operator
L^{1}(G;\mathcal{C}_{1})\supseteq L^{1}(G;\mathcal{C}_{1})\cap L^{2}(G;\mathcal{C}_{1})\ni A_{\theta}\mapsto B_{\xi}\in L^{\infty}(\hat{G};\mathcal{C}_{1})
, with norm at most
1
. It can be extended, by continuity, to a bounded operator on the whole
L^{1}(G;\mathcal{C}_{1})
. Denote it again by
T
.

To summarize, we have a linear operator
T
,
T(A_{\theta})=B_{\xi}
, defined on
L^{1}(G;\mathcal{C}_{1})
and
L^{2}(G;\mathcal{C}_{2})
, which coincide on its intersection
L^{1}(G;\mathcal{C}_{1})\cap L^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{2})=L^{1}(G;\mathcal{C}_{1})\cap L^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{1})
(since
\mathcal{C}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{2}
) with an explicit form
B_{\xi}=\int_{G}A_{\theta}\overline{\xi(\theta)}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)
.

Now, we are ready to apply complex interpolation. Namely, by Corollary
2.4
, we obtain

[L^{1}(G;\mathcal{C}_{1}),L^{2}(G,\mathcal{C}_{2})]_{2-\frac{2}{p}}=L^{p}(G,\mathcal{C}_{p}),

[L^{\infty}(\hat{G};\mathcal{C}_{1}),L^{2}(\hat{G},\mathcal{C}_{2})]_{2-\frac{2}{p}}=L^{q}(\hat{G},\mathcal{C}_{p}),

where
q
is conjugated to
p
. Finally, by Theorem
2.1
we obtain
\|T\|\leq 1
considering
T
as an operator from
L^{p}(G;\mathcal{C}_{p})
to
L^{q}(\hat{G};\mathcal{C}_{p})
. For
A_{\theta}\in L^{1}(G;\mathcal{C}_{p})\cap L^{2}(G;\mathcal{C}_{p})\subseteq L^{p}(G;\mathcal{C}_{p})
it becomes

\left(\int_{\hat{G}}\|B_{\xi}\|_{p}^{q}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\left(\int_{G}\|A_{\theta}\|_{p}^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

i.e. (
3.2
).
∎

4.
Applications

Note, first, that in the case where the Hilbert space is one-dimensional, the function
A_{\theta}
reduces to scalar valued function. Hence, our inequality (
3.2
) reduces to the Hausdorff-Young inequality
[
5
, (4.27)]
.

In the next two subsections, we apply our main results to some special cases of
G
, and to weighted Schatten classes.

4.1.
Applications by specifying the group
G

If
G
is a compact group, then Theorem
3.2
becomes
[
9
, Theorem 3.6]
. Exactly which inequalities we can obtain for specific groups
G
is described in section 4 of
[
9
]
. For instance, for
G=\mathbf{Z}_{2}
we obtain (
1.3
), and for
G=\mathbf{Z}_{n}
(
1.4
). In
[
9
, Corollaries 4.5 and 4.7]
, cases
G=\mathbf{T}
and
G=\mathbf{Z}_{2}^{n}
were also treated.

Next, we can take two important cases of non compact groups,
G=\mathbf{R}^{n}
and
G=\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}
.

It is well known that
\mathbf{R}^{n}
is a locally compact abelian group and that the Lebesque measure
\mathrm{d}m_{n}
is its Haar measure. Its Pontryagin dual is also
\mathbf{R}^{n}
with the dual measure
\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\mathrm{d}m_{n}
.

Remark 4.1
.

Sometimes, in order to emphasize selfduality, the Haar measure is rescaled to
\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\mathrm{d}m_{n}
. Then it is equal to its dual measure.

If we apply Theorem
3.2
, we get

Theorem 4.1
.

For all
1\leq p\leq 2
, and
A_{\theta}\in L^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n};\mathcal{C}_{p})\cap L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n};\mathcal{C}_{p})
there holds
B_{\xi}\in L^{q}(\mathbf{R}^{n},\mathcal{C}_{p})
and

(4.1)

\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\left\|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}A_{\theta}e^{-i\xi\theta}\mathrm{d}\theta\right\|_{p}^{q}\mathrm{d}\xi\leq\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\|A_{\theta}\|_{p}^{p}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{q/p},

where
q
is conjugate to
p
, i.e.
q=p/(p-1)
,
p\neq 1
and
q=+\infty
if
p=1
.

Our second application concerns
p
-adic groups.

Recall that given a fixed prime number
p
, a nonzero rational
x
can be written as
x=r^{k}\frac{a}{b}
, with
p\not|ab
and
k\in\mathbf{Z}
. Then we define its
p
-adic norm by

|x|_{p}=\begin{cases}p^{-k},&x\neq 0\\
0,&x=0.\end{cases}

The
p
-adic group
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is the completion of
\mathbb{Q}
with respect to
|\cdot|_{p}
.

The nonzero elements of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
can be identified with formal infinite sums of the form

(4.2)

x=\sum_{i=\gamma}^{\infty}x_{i}p^{i},\qquad\gamma\in\mathbb{Z},\quad x_{i}\in\{0,1,...,p-1\},\quad x_{\gamma}\neq 0,

with
|x|_{p}=p^{-\gamma}
. The set of
p
-adic integers are

\mathbf{Z}_{p}=\{x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}\mid|x|_{p}\leq 1\}.

It is well known that
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is a locally compact group, and hence it has its Haar measure, unique up to a scalar. It is usual to normalize Haar measure such that
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
be of measure one. This Haar measure is denoted by
\mathrm{d}x
.

Though it is not easy to give an explicit form for Haar measure, it is not the case with the characters. Namely, if
x
is given by (
4.2
) then its fractional part
\{x\}_{p}
is equal to
0
if
\gamma\geq 0
(or
x\in\mathbf{Z}_{p}
) and

\{x\}_{p}=\sum_{i=\gamma}^{-1}x_{i}p^{i},

otherwise. Then the standard character on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is given by
\chi_{p}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}\to\mathbf{T}
,
\chi_{p}(x)=e^{2\pi i\{x\}_{p}}
. Obviously
\chi_{p}(x+y)=\chi_{p}(x)\chi_{p}(y)
.

It can be obtained that any character on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is of the form
x\mapsto\chi_{p}(\xi x)
for some
\xi\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Thus
\hat{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\cong\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

The group
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}
can be treated in a similar way. It is also a locally compact group, with the Haar measure
\mathrm{d}^{n}x
and
\hat{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n}\cong\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}
with the identification

\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}\ni\xi=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})\leftrightarrow\chi_{\xi}\in\hat{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n},\qquad\chi_{\xi}(x)=\chi(\xi\cdot x)=e^{2\pi i\{\xi_{1}x_{1}+\dots+\xi_{n}x_{n}\}_{p}}.

The Fourier transform of a function
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
is given by

\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}}f(x)e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot x}\mathrm{d}^{n}x.

If we apply Theorem
3.2
to the group
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}
we obtaine the following corollary. To avoid ambiguity, we write
q
and
r
as conjugated ceofficient instead of usual
p
and
q
, since the letter
p
is booked for the prime number.

Theorem 4.2
.

For all
1\leq q\leq 2
, and
A_{\theta}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{n};\mathcal{C}_{q})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n};\mathcal{C}_{q})

B_{\xi}=\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}}e^{-2\pi i\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\varepsilon_{i}\}_{p}}A_{\theta}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\in L^{r}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n},C_{q}),

and

(4.3)

\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}}\left\|\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}}e^{-2\pi i\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\varepsilon_{i}\}_{p}}A_{\theta}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\right\|_{q}^{r}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}}\|A_{\theta}\|_{q}^{q}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\right)^{r/q},

where
r
is conjugate to
q
, i.e.
r=q/(q-1)
.

4.2.
Weighted Schatten classes

In
[
4
]
, the weighted Schatten classes were investigated. As sets, they coincide with the usual Schatten classe, but they have a different norm.

Let
B(H)
denote the algebra of bounded operators acting on a complex and separable Hilbert space
H
,
\mathrm{Gl}(H)
the group of invertible elements of
B(H)
and
\mathrm{Gl}(H)^{+}
the set of all positive elements of
\mathrm{Gl}(H)
.

For the sake of simplicity, we denote with capital letters the elements of
\mathcal{C}_{p}
and with lower case letters the elements of
\mathrm{Gl}(H)^{+}
.

On
\mathcal{C}_{p}
we define the following norm associated with an invertible
a\in\mathrm{Gl}(H)^{+}
:

\|X\|_{p,a}:=\left\|a^{-1/2}Xa^{-1/2}\right\|_{p}.

The norm
\|\cdot\|_{p,a}
is equivalent to
\|\cdot\|_{p}
, and hence the space
\mathcal{C}_{p,a}=(\mathcal{C}_{p},\|\cdot\|_{p,a})
is a Banach space. The exact interpolation space for different
a
and
b
is described in the following Lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

[
4
, Corollary 3.2]

Given invertible
a,b\in\mathrm{Gl}(H)^{+}
and
1\leqslant p<\infty
. Then

\left[\mathcal{C}_{p,a},\mathcal{C}_{p,b}\right]_{t}=\mathcal{C}_{p,\gamma_{a,b}(t)},

where
\gamma_{(a,b)}(t)=a^{1/2}(a^{-1/2}ba^{-1/2})^{t}a^{1/2}
.

For Bochner spaces, related to weighted Schatten ideals, by previous Lemma and Theorem
2.2
we immediately obtain:

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
p\geq 1
and
0<t<1
. Then

[L^{p}(G;\mathcal{C}_{p,a}),L^{p}(S;\mathcal{C}_{p.b})]_{t}=L^{p}(G;\mathcal{C}_{p,\gamma_{a,b}(t)})

isometrically.

We formulate a version of Clarkson-McCarthy inequality for weighted Schatten classes.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group with Haar measure
\mu
, let
\hat{G}
be its Pontryagin dual with the dual measure
\nu
. For all
1\leq p\leq 2
,
A_{\theta}\in L^{1}(G;\mathcal{C}_{p,a})\cap L^{2}(G;\mathcal{C}_{p,a})
, invertible
a
,
b\in\mathrm{Gl}(H)^{+}
, and
t\in[0,1]
, the operator

B_{\xi}=\int_{G}A_{\theta}\overline{\xi(\theta)}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)

belongs to
L^{q}(\hat{G};\mathcal{C}_{p,\gamma_{a,b}(t)})
and

(4.4)

\int_{\hat{G}}\left\|\int_{G}A_{\theta}\overline{\xi(\theta)}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\right\|_{p,\gamma_{a,b}(t)}^{q}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\leq\|a^{1/2}b^{-1}a^{1/2}\|^{t}\left(\int_{G}\|A_{\theta}\|_{p,a}^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\right)^{q/p},

as well as for the same
p
,
a
,
b
,
t
and
A_{\theta}\in L^{1}(G;\mathcal{C}_{p,\gamma_{a,b}(t)})\cap L^{2}(G;\mathcal{C}_{p,\gamma_{a,b}(t)})
we have
B_{\xi}\in L^{q}(\hat{G};\mathcal{C}_{p,a})
and

(4.5)

\int_{\hat{G}}\left\|\int_{G}A_{\theta}\overline{\xi(\theta)}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\right\|_{p,a}^{q}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\leq\|b^{1/2}a^{-1}b^{1/2}\|^{t}\left(\int_{G}\|A_{\theta}\|_{p,\gamma_{a,b}(t)}^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\right)^{q/p},

where
q
is conjugate to
p
, i.e.
q=p/(p-1)
,
p\neq 1
and
q=+\infty
if
p=1
.

Proof.

Put
a^{-1/2}A_{\theta}a^{-1/2}
instead of
A_{\theta}
in (
3.2
). Applying (
2.1
) to

A_{\theta}\mapsto a^{-1/2}A_{\theta}a^{-1/2},

we obtain

(4.6)

\int_{\hat{G}}\left\|a^{-1/2}\int_{G}A_{\theta}\overline{\xi(\theta)}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)a^{-1/2}\right\|_{p}^{q}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\leq\left(\int_{G}\|a^{-1/2}A_{\theta}a^{-1/2}\|_{p}^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\right)^{q/p},

i.e.

\int_{\hat{G}}\left\|\int_{G}A_{\theta}\overline{\xi(\theta)}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\right\|_{p,a}^{q}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\leq\left(\int_{G}\|A_{\theta}\|_{p,a}^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\right)^{q/p},

This means that the mapping
A_{\theta}\mapsto B_{\xi}=\int_{G}A_{\theta}\overline{\xi(\theta)}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)
is a bounded linear operator, with norm at most one, regarded as an operator from
L^{p}(G;\mathcal{C}_{p,a})
to
L^{q}(\hat{G};\mathcal{C}_{p,a})
. Denote this operator by
T
. We have

(4.7)

\|T\|_{L^{p}(G;\mathcal{C}_{p,a})\mapsto L^{q}(\hat{G};\mathcal{C}_{p,a})}\leq 1.

Now, put
b
instead of
a
in (
4.6
). We have

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{G}}

\displaystyle\left\|b^{-1/2}\int_{G}A_{\theta}\overline{\xi(\theta)}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)b^{-1/2}\right\|_{p}^{q}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\leq\left(\int_{G}\|b^{-1/2}A_{\theta}b^{-1/2}\|_{p}^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\right)^{q/p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\|b^{-1/2}a^{1/2}\|^{p}\int_{G}\|a^{-1/2}A_{\theta}a^{-1/2}\|_{p}^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\|b^{-1/2}a^{1/2}\|^{p}\right)^{q/p}

\displaystyle=\|a^{1/2}b^{-1}a^{1/2}\|^{q}\left(\int_{G}\|A_{\theta}\|_{p,a}^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\right)^{q/p}.

The latter means that the operator
T
is a bounded linear operator, regarded as an operator from
L^{p}(G;\mathcal{C}_{p,a})
to
L^{q}(\hat{G};\mathcal{C}_{p,b})
with norm at most
\|a^{1/2}b^{-1}a^{1/2}\|
, i.e.

(4.8)

\|T\|_{L^{p}(G;\mathcal{C}_{p,a})\mapsto L^{q}(\hat{G};\mathcal{C}_{p,b})}\leq\|a^{1/2}b^{-1}a^{1/2}\|.

From (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), using Theorem
2.1
and Lemma
4.4
we obtain

\|T\|_{L^{p}(G;\mathcal{C}_{p,a})\mapsto L^{q}(\hat{G};\mathcal{C}_{p,\gamma_{a,b}(t)})}\leq\|a^{1/2}b^{-1}a^{1/2}\|^{t},

i.e. (
4.4
).

The inequality (
4.5
) can be obtained by switching the roles of
a
and
b
in (
4.8
) and Theorem
2.1
and Lemma
4.4
.

Intrafamilial phenotypic heterogeneity in carriers of the m.3303C > T mutation cannot be explained solely by heteroplasmy levels
Authors: Josef Finsterer
Published: 2026

Letter to the Editor,

We have read the interesting article by Yatsuga [1] but believe that several points warrant discussion.

First, the analysis did not take into account various factors, beyond the heteroplasmy rate, that determine the phenotypic expression of mtDNA variants [1]. These include mtDNA copy number, haplotype, mutations in nuclear genes regulating mitochondrial metabolism, tissue energy demand, the threshold effect, mitochondrial dynamics (including the distribution or segregation of mutant mtDNA copies through continuous fusion and fission within the mitochondrial network), and secondary stressors, such as environmental toxins, infections, or metabolic loads, that accelerate cellular dysfunction in susceptible tissues [2], [3]. Without considering these factors, the genotype-phenotype correlation of the detected mtDNA variant and the marked intrafamilial phenotypic heterogeneity remain unclear.

Second, no results from biochemical analyses were reported [1]. It would be important to know which respiratory chain complexes were impaired and what the ATP production levels were. Mutations in the MT-TL1 gene are frequently associated with dysfunction of respiratory chain complexes I and IV [4].

Third, the cause of the hyper-CK-emia was not sufficiently explained [1]. Did the patient also exhibit myopathy or rhabdomyolysis, or was myocarditis present? An elevated CK level resulting solely from hypertrophic cardiomyopathy is unusual. Was CK-MB also elevated? Myopathy can also manifest in carriers of the m.3303C > T mutation [5]. Were there clinical signs of myopathy? Was the hyper-CK-emia attributable to a myopathy rather than a cardiomyopathy? Data regarding troponin and CK-MB levels are lacking in this regard. Low troponin and CK-MB levels would suggest that the hyper-CK-emia resulted from a myopathy rather than a cardiomyopathy.

A fourth point is that no explanation was provided for the hyperammonemia [1]. Was there hepatic congestion secondary to right heart failure, or was the liver primarily affected by the causative mutation?

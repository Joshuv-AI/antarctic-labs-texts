Rigidity Results Involving Stabilized Scalar Curvature
Yipeng Wang

Abstract
. We establish a rigidity theorem for Brendle and Hung’s recent systolic inequality, which involves Gromov’s notion of T ⋊ T^{\rtimes} -stabilized scalar curvature. Our primary technique is the construction of foliations by free boundary weighted constant mean curvature hypersurfaces, enabling us to generalize several classical scalar curvature rigidity results to the T ⋊ T^{\rtimes} -stabilized setting. Additionally, we develop a monotone quantity using Ricci flow coupled with a heat equation, which is essential for rigidity analysis.



1.
Introduction

In this article, we explore several rigidity questions arising in the context of scalar curvature. Our main result is a rigidity theorem that builds upon the recent systolic inequality established by Brendle and Hung
[
5
]
. This inequality applies to compact manifolds with nonnegative scalar curvature in a suitably weak sense.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact, connected, orientable Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\leq 7
with nonempty boundary
\partial M
. Denote by
\eta
the outward-pointing unit normal vector along
\partial M
. Let
\varphi
be a smooth function defined on
M
. Suppose there is a smooth map
\xi:\partial M\to S^{1}
, as well as a smooth map
(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n-2}):M\to T^{n-2}
, such that
(\xi,\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n-2}):\partial M\to S^{1}\times T^{n-2}

has non-zero degree. Let
\Xi
be the pullback of the volume form on
S^{1}
via
\xi
, and let
\Theta_{i}
be the pullback of the volume form on
S^{1}
via
\theta_{i}
. Define

\sigma(M,g):=\inf\{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\alpha):\alpha\text{ is a smooth, closed curve on }\partial M\text{ with }\int_{\alpha}\Xi\neq 0\}.

Assume that

-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M}\geq 0

at point point in
M
. Then we have

\inf_{\partial M}(H_{\partial M}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\eta\rangle)\cdot\sigma(M,g)\leq 2\pi.

Moreover, equality occurs if and only if
\varphi
is constant on
M
and the universal cover of
M
is isometric to
B^{2}(r)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-2}
with the standard product metric for some
r>0
.

The inequality in Theorem
1.1
was recently proved by Brendle–Hung
[
5
]
, extending prior results by Lucas Ambrozio
[
1
]
in the three-dimensional setting. An analogue of Theorem
1.1
for closed manifolds is the systolic inequality established by Zhu
[
23
]
, which generalizes the rigidity theorem of Bray–Brendle–Neves
[
3
]
in dimension three. For additional scalar curvature rigidity results involving minimal surfaces in 3-manifolds, we refer to
[
6
]
,
[
17
]
, and
[
19
]
.

Brendle and Hung’s approach relies on the minimal slicing argument developed by Schoen and Yau
[
21
]
. This technique is closely related to the torical symmetrization methods discussed in
[
13
]
,
[
10
]
, and
[
11
]
.

The quantity
-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M}
appearing in Theorem
1.1
is closely related to the notion of
T^{\rtimes}-
stabilized scalar curvature introduced by Gromov
[
12
]
. The idea is to perform an “infinite dimensional torical symmetrization” of
M
. Let
N
be a positive integer, and consider
\tilde{M}=M\times T^{N}
, where
T^{N}
is an
N
-dimensional torus equipped with a flat metric
g_{T^{N}}
. Define a warped product metric on
\tilde{M}
by

\tilde{g}=g+e^{\frac{2}{N}\varphi}g_{T^{N}}.

Under this construction, the scalar curvatures of
(\tilde{M},\tilde{g})
and
(M,g)
are related by

R_{\tilde{M}}=R_{M}-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-\frac{N+1}{N}|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}.

As
N\to\infty
, , one can heuristically interpret
-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M}
as the scalar curvature of an “infinite-dimensional” Riemannian manifold. Moreover, if we take
\varphi
to be a constant, this expression reduces to the standard scalar curvature on
M
. Hence, the assumption in Theorem
1.1
can be viewed as a weaker notion of nonnegative scalar curvature on
M
.

In the literature, the quantity
-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M}
is also known as the weighted scalar curvature or the
P
-scalar curvature. This terminology arises because this quantity can be viewed as the scalar curvature of the weighted manifold
(M,g,e^{\varphi}\,d\vol_{g})
. A crucial application of this concept appears in the study of Ricci flow
[
20
]
. In this setting, the corresponding Hilbert-Einstein action on the weighted manifold is referred to as Perelman’s
\mathcal{F}
-functional:

\mathcal{F}(M,g,-\varphi):=\int_{M}\left(-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M}\right)e^{\varphi}\,d\vol_{g}.

Perelman observed that along the Ricci flow, if
\varphi
satisfies a backward parabolic equation, then the
\mathcal{F}
-functional is monotonically increasing (see Remark
A.3
).

A similar argument to that used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
for closed manifolds also extends Zhu’s result
[
23
]
to the context of stabilized scalar curvature, as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed, connected, orientable Riemannian manifold with
n\leq 7
, and
\varphi
be a smooth function on
M
. Suppose there is a smooth map
\omega:M\to S^{2}
, as well as a smooth map
(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n-2}):M\to T^{n-2}
, such that
(\omega,\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n-2}):M\to S^{2}\times T^{n-2}
has non-zero degree. Let
\Omega
be the pull-back of the volume form on
S^{2}
via
\omega
, and let
\Theta_{i}
be the pullback of the volume form on
S^{1}
via
\theta_{i}
. Define

\mathscr{A}(M,g):=\inf\{\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma):\Sigma\text{ is a smooth surface of }M\text{ with }\int_{\Sigma}\Omega\neq 0\}.

Then we have

\inf_{M}(-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M})\cdot\mathscr{A}(M,g)\leq 8\pi.

Moreover, equality holds if and only if
\varphi
is constant on
M
and the universal cover of
M
is isometric to
S^{2}(r)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-2}
with the standard product metric for some
r>0
.

The positive mass theorem and its rigidity in the context of stabilized scalar curvature have been recently studied by Baldauf and Ozuch
[
2
]
and Law, Lopez, and Santiago
[
16
]
using the Dirac operator method, as well as by Chu and Zhu
[
8
]
employing a weighted minimal slicing argument. It would also be interesting to investigate whether other classical rigidity results in scalar curvature can be generalized to this weaker setting. One of the earliest significant results concerning scalar curvature rigidity trace back to the work of Gromov and Lawson, as well as Schoen and Yau, on the Geroch conjecture.

Theorem 1.3

(Gromov–Lawson
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
; Schoen–Yau
[
21
]
)
.

Let
g
be a Riemannian metric on the torus
T^{n}
. Then
\inf_{T^{n}}R_{g}\leq 0
. Moreover, equality holds if and only if
g
is flat.

The same technique also yields a generalization of Theorem
1.3
to the stabilized scalar curvature setting. The inequality was established in the work of Chu and Zhu
[
8
]
. Here, we also provide a proof of the rigidity statement.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed, connected, orientable Riemannian manifold with
n\leq 7
, and let
\varphi
be a smooth function on
M
. Suppose there exists a smooth map
M\to T^{n}
of non-zero degree. Then

\inf_{M}(-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M})\leq 0.

Moreover, equality holds if and only if the universal cover of
M
is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the flat metric, and
\varphi
is constant on
M
.

Remark 1.5
.

Recent work by Brendle, Hirsch, and Johne
[
4
]
provides a generalization of the Geroch conjecture in the setting of intermediate curvature. The corresponding rigidity problem has been studied by Chu, Kwong, and Lee
[
7
]
. It would be intriguing to consider analogues of stabilized curvature for other curvature conditions as well.

The dimension constraint in Theorem
1.4
can be removed under the additional assumption that
M
is spin. In this case, one can perform a torical symmetrization in high dimensions and apply the classical result on the Geroch conjecture by Gromov and Lawson. The corresponding rigidity statement can then be derived using a strong maximum principle argument for Ricci flow coupled with a heat equation. The key observation is that by evolving the Riemannian metric
g
via the Ricci flow and the potential function
\varphi
via the heat equation, the stabilized scalar curvature becomes a supersolution to the heat equation.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed, connected, orientable, and spin Riemannian manifold, and let
\varphi
be a smooth function on
M
. Suppose there exists a smooth map
M\to T^{n}
of non-zero degree. Then

\inf_{M}(-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M})\leq 0.

Moreover, equality holds if and only if the universal cover of
M
is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the flat metric, and
\varphi
is constant on
M
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we review Brendle and Hung’s proof of the systolic inequality stated in Theorem
1.1
and establish its rigidity case in Section
3
. In Section
4
, we prove the systolic inequality presented in Theorem
1.2
and discuss its corresponding rigidity case in Section
5
. We complete the proof of Theorem
1.4
in Section
6
using the weighted minimal slicing argument and present the proof of Theorem
1.6
in Section
7
. Finally, in Appendix
A
, we derive the evolution equation for the stabilized scalar curvature under the Ricci flow coupled with the heat equation, which may be of independent interest.

2.
The Free Boundary Systolic Inequality

In this section, we recall the key elements of Brendle and Hung’s proof
[
5
]
of the free boundary systolic inequality, which will also be useful when establishing the corresponding rigidity result.

Assume
M
is a compact, connected, orientable manifold of dimension
n
with nonempty boundary
\partial M
. Let
\eta
be the outward-pointing unit normal vector along
\partial M
. Let
\varphi
be a smooth function defined on
M
. Suppose there is a smooth map
\xi:\partial M\to S^{1}
, and a smooth map
(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n-2}):M\to T^{n-2}
such that

(\xi,\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n-2}):\partial M\to S^{1}\times T^{n-2}

has nonzero degree. Let
\Xi
denote the pullback of the volume form on
S^{1}
via
\xi
, and for each
i
, let
\Theta_{i}
be the pullback of the volume form on
S^{1}
via
\theta_{i}:M\to S^{1}
. Thus,

\int_{\partial M}\Xi\wedge\Theta_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Theta_{n-2}\neq 0.

We define

\sigma(M,g):=\inf\{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\alpha):\alpha\text{ is a smooth, closed curve on }\partial M\text{ with }\int_{\alpha}\Xi\neq 0\}.

The following slicing argument follows from Section 4 in
[
5
]
(or its proof). We refer the reader to
[
9
]
and
[
15
]
for the regularity theory of free boundary minimal surfaces.

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose

\inf_{\partial M}\bigl(H_{\partial M}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\eta\rangle\bigr)>0.

Then we can find a collection of compact, connected, orientable submanifolds
\Sigma_{k}
with boundary and a sequence of positive smooth functions
u_{k}:\Sigma_{k}\to\mathbb{R}
for
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-2\}
, as well as a sequence of positive smooth functions
\rho_{k}:\Sigma_{k}\to\mathbb{R}
for
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-2\}
, satisfying the following properties:

(i)

\Sigma_{0}=M
and
\rho_{0}=e^{\varphi}
.

(ii)

For each
k\in\{0,\ldots,n-2\}
,
\dim\Sigma_{k}=n-k
.

(iii)

For each
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-2\}
, we have
\partial\Sigma_{k}\subset\partial\Sigma_{k-1}
, and
\Sigma_{k}
meets
\partial\Sigma_{k-1}
orthogonally along
\partial\Sigma_{k}
.

(iv)

For each
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-2\}
, the outward-pointing unit normal vector field of
\partial\Sigma_{k}
in
\Sigma_{k-1}
equals
\eta
. Moreover, the second fundamental form of
\partial\Sigma_{k}
in
\Sigma_{k-1}
equals the restriction of
h_{\partial M}
to
T(\partial\Sigma_{k})
.

(v)

For each
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-2\}
,

\int_{\partial\Sigma_{k}}\Xi\wedge\Theta_{k+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Theta_{n-2}\neq 0.

(vi)

For each
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-2\}
,
\Sigma_{k}
is a free boundary homologically area minimizer in
(\Sigma_{k-1},\rho_{k-1}^{\frac{2}{n-k}}g_{\Sigma_{k-1}})
.

(vii)

For each
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-2\}
,

H_{\Sigma_{k}}+\langle\nabla^{\Sigma_{k-1}}\log\rho_{k-1},\nu_{\Sigma_{k}}\rangle=0.

(viii)

For each
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-2\}
, the function
u_{k}
satisfies

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Sigma_{k}}u_{k}-\operatorname{Ric}_{\Sigma_{k-1}}(\nu_{\Sigma_{k}},\nu_{\Sigma_{k}})u_{k}-|h_{\Sigma_{k}}|^{2}u_{k}

\displaystyle\quad+(D^{2}_{\Sigma_{k-1}}\log\rho_{k-1})(\nu_{\Sigma_{k}},\nu_{\Sigma_{k}})u_{k}-\langle\nabla^{\Sigma_{k}}\log\rho_{k-1},\nabla^{\Sigma_{k}}u_{k}\rangle=\lambda_{k}u_{k},

with the Neumann boundary condition

\langle\nabla^{\Sigma_{k}}u_{k},\eta\rangle-h_{\partial M}(\nu_{\Sigma_{k}},\nu_{\Sigma_{k}})u_{k}=0.

Here
\lambda_{k}\geq 0
is a nonnegative constant.

(ix)

For each
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-2\}
, we have
\rho_{k}=\rho_{k-1}|_{\Sigma_{k}}\cdot u_{k}
.

(x)

For each
k\in\{0,\ldots,n-2\}
,

\displaystyle-2\Delta_{\Sigma_{k}}\log\rho_{k}-|\nabla^{\Sigma_{k}}\log\rho_{k}|^{2}+R_{\Sigma_{k}}

\displaystyle\quad+2\Delta_{M}\varphi+|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}-R_{M}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}|\nabla^{\Sigma_{j}}\log u_{j}|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}|h_{\Sigma_{j}}|^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}.

(xi)

For each
k\in\{0,\ldots,n-2\}
,

\langle\nabla^{\Sigma_{k}}\log\rho_{k},\eta\rangle+H_{\partial\Sigma_{k}}=\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\eta\rangle+H_{\partial M}

at each point on
\partial\Sigma_{k}
. Here
H_{\partial\Sigma_{k}}
denotes the mean curvature of
\partial\Sigma_{k}
in
\Sigma_{k}
.

Theorem 2.2

(Brendle–Hung
[
5
]
)
.

Suppose

\inf_{\partial M}\bigl(H_{\partial M}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\eta\rangle\bigr)>0.

Then there exists a compact, connected, orientable surface
\Sigma
with
\int_{\partial\Sigma}\Xi\neq 0
such that

\displaystyle\inf_{M}(-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M})\cdot|\Sigma|

\displaystyle\quad+2\,\inf_{\partial M}(H_{\partial M}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\eta\rangle)\cdot|\partial\Sigma|\leq 4\pi.

Proof.

Apply Proposition
2.1
with
k=n-2
. Denote
\Sigma=\Sigma_{n-2}
and set
\psi=\log\rho_{n-2}
. From this construction, it follows that
\int_{\partial\Sigma}\Xi\neq 0
and

-2\Delta_{\Sigma}\psi-|\nabla^{\Sigma}\psi|^{2}+R_{\Sigma}\geq\inf_{M}(-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M})

at every point of
\Sigma
.

Since
\Sigma
is connected, its Euler characteristic is at most 1. Integrating the inequality above and applying the Gauss–Bonnet theorem gives

\displaystyle 4\pi

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Sigma}R_{\Sigma}\,+2\int_{\partial\Sigma}H_{\partial\Sigma}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Sigma}(-2\Delta_{\Sigma}\psi-|\nabla^{\Sigma}\psi|^{2}+R_{\Sigma})\,+2\int_{\partial\Sigma}(H_{\partial\Sigma}+\langle\nabla^{\Sigma}\psi,\eta\rangle)

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{M}(-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M})\cdot|\Sigma|

\displaystyle\quad+2\,\inf_{\partial\Sigma}(H_{\partial\Sigma}+\langle\nabla^{\Sigma}\psi,\eta\rangle)\cdot|\partial\Sigma|.

Since

H_{\partial\Sigma}+\langle\nabla^{\Sigma}\psi,\eta\rangle=H_{\partial M}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\eta\rangle

at each point of
\partial\Sigma
, the theorem follows.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Suppose

-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M}\geq 0

at every point in
M
. Then

\inf_{\partial M}(H_{\partial M}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\eta\rangle)\cdot\sigma(M,g)\leq 2\pi.

3.
The Equality Case of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we examine the equality case of Theorem
1.1
. Our approach is similar in spirit to the rigidity arguments presented in
[
1
]
. We restate the result here for convenience.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose

-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M}\geq 0

and

\inf_{\partial M}\bigl(H_{\partial M}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\eta\rangle\bigr)\cdot\sigma(M,g)=2\pi.

Then
\varphi
is constant on
M
, and the universal cover of
M
is isometric to
B^{2}(r)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-2}
with the standard product metric for some
r>0
.

We proceed by induction on the dimension
n
. The case
n=2
follows directly from the Gauss–Bonnet theorem. Assume Theorem
3.1
holds for all Riemannian manifolds with dimension at most
n-1
.

Now consider an
n
-dimensional manifold
(M,g)
satisfying

-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M}\geq 0

and

\inf_{\partial M}\bigl(H_{\partial M}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\eta\rangle\bigr)\cdot\sigma(M,g)=2\pi.

After rescaling the metric, we may assume

\inf_{\partial M}(H_{\partial M}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\eta\rangle)=1\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma(M,g)=2\pi.

We will use the following identity, which is a consequence of the Gauss equation. For a proof, we refer to Proposition 4.2 in
[
5
]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold, and let
\Sigma\subset M
be a hypersurface. Suppose
\rho
is a positive smooth function on
M
, and let
u\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
be a positive function on
\Sigma
. Define
v=\rho|_{\Sigma}\cdot u
. Then, at each point on
\Sigma
, the following identity holds:

\displaystyle-2\Delta_{\Sigma}\log v-|\nabla^{\Sigma}\log v|^{2}+R_{\Sigma}-(H_{\Sigma}+\langle\nabla^{M}\log\rho,\nu_{\Sigma}\rangle)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2\Delta_{M}\log\rho+|\nabla^{M}\log\rho|^{2}-R_{M}-|\nabla^{\Sigma}\log u|^{2}-|h_{\Sigma}|^{2}

\displaystyle=-2u^{-1}\Delta_{\Sigma}u-2\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})u-2|h_{\Sigma}|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+(D^{2}_{\Sigma}\log\rho)(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})-\langle\nabla^{\Sigma}\log\rho,\nabla^{\Sigma}\log u\rangle.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
\Sigma^{n-1}\subset M
is a compact, connected, orientable free boundary stable minimal hypersurface in
(M,e^{\frac{2}{n-1}\varphi}g)
with

\int_{\partial\Sigma}\Xi\wedge\Theta_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Theta_{n-2}\neq 0.

Then:

•

The universal cover of
\Sigma
is isometric to
B^{2}(1)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-3}
equipped with the standard product metric.

•

The function
e^{\varphi}|_{\Sigma}\cdot u
is constant on
\Sigma
, where
u
is the first eigenfunction of the Jacobi operator

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Sigma}u-\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})u-|h_{\Sigma}|^{2}u

\displaystyle\quad+(D^{2}_{M}\log\rho)(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})u-\langle\nabla^{\Sigma}\log\rho,\nabla^{\Sigma}u\rangle=\lambda u,

subject to the Neumann boundary condition

\langle\nabla^{\Sigma}u,\eta\rangle-h_{\partial M}(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})u=0.

Proof.

First, we have the trivial inequality

\sigma(\Sigma,g|_{\Sigma})\geq\sigma(M,g)=2\pi.

On the other hand, Proposition
3.2
implies that for
\rho=e^{\varphi}|_{\Sigma}\cdot u
,

\displaystyle-2\Delta_{\Sigma}\log\rho-|\nabla^{\Sigma}\log\rho|^{2}+R_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{M}(-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M})

\displaystyle=0

at every point in
\Sigma
. Theorem
2.2
then implies
\sigma(\Sigma,g|_{\Sigma})\leq 2\pi
and

2\pi=\inf_{\partial\Sigma}(H_{\partial\Sigma}+\langle\nabla^{\Sigma}\log\rho,\eta\rangle)\cdot\sigma(\Sigma,g|_{\Sigma}).

The claim now follows from the induction hypothesis.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose
\Sigma^{n-1}\subset M
is a compact, connected, orientable, free boundary stable minimal hypersurface in
(M,e^{\frac{2}{n-1}\varphi}g)
with

\int_{\partial\Sigma}\Xi\wedge\Theta_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Theta_{n-2}\neq 0.

Then:

•

\Sigma
is totally geodesic, with
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})=0
and
R_{M}=0
at every point of
\Sigma
.

•

\varphi|_{\Sigma}
is constant, with
\nabla^{M}\varphi=0
and
(D^{2}_{M}\varphi)(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})=0
at every point of
\Sigma
.

•

h_{\partial M}(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})=0
and
H_{\partial\Sigma}=1
at each point on
\partial\Sigma
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.3
, the function
\rho=e^{\varphi}|_{\Sigma}\cdot u
is constant on
\Sigma
, where
u
is the first eigenfunction of the stability operator with Neumann boundary conditions. Hence, Proposition
3.2
gives

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M}-R_{\Sigma})+|\nabla^{\Sigma}\log u|^{2}+|h_{\Sigma}|^{2}+2\lambda.

Since

-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M}\geq 2=R_{\Sigma},

we must have
\nabla^{\Sigma}\log u=0
and
h_{\Sigma}=0
. This implies that
\Sigma
is totally geodesic and both
u
and
\varphi|_{\Sigma}
are constant on
\Sigma
. In particular,
H_{\Sigma}=0
, and we conclude that

\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\nu_{\Sigma}\rangle=0.

Therefore,
\nabla^{M}\varphi=0
, and

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M}-R_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle=-2(D^{2}_{M}\varphi)(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})+\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma}).

Moreover, since
\lambda=0
and the function
u
satisfies the equation

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Sigma}u-\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})u-|h_{\Sigma}|^{2}u+(D^{2}_{M}\varphi)(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})u-\langle\nabla^{\Sigma}\varphi,\nabla^{\Sigma}u\rangle

\displaystyle=\lambda u,

we obtain that

-\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})+(D^{2}_{M}\varphi)(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})=0.

From this, we conclude that

\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})=(D^{2}_{M}\varphi)(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})=0.

Finally, the Neumann condition states
h_{\partial M}(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})=\langle\nabla^{\Sigma}\log u,\eta\rangle=0
and

H_{\partial\Sigma}=H_{\partial\Sigma}+\langle\nabla^{\Sigma}\log u,\eta\rangle=H_{\partial M}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\eta\rangle=1

at each point on
\partial\Sigma
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose
\Sigma^{n-1}\subset M
is a compact, connected, orientable free boundary stable minimal hypersurface in
(M,e^{\frac{2}{n-1}\varphi}g)
with

\int_{\partial\Sigma}\Xi\wedge\Theta_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Theta_{n-2}\neq 0.

Then there exists
\delta>0
and a smooth map
w:\Sigma\times(-\delta,\delta)\to\mathbb{R}
with the following properties:

•

For each
x\in\Sigma
, we have
w(x,0)=0
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}w(x,t)\big|_{t=0}=1
.

•

For each
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
, we have
\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}(w(\cdot,t)-t)\,d\vol_{g}=0
.

•

For each
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
, define

\Sigma_{t}:=\{\exp_{x}(w(x,t)\nu_{\Sigma}(x)):x\in\Sigma\}.

Then:

(i)

\partial\Sigma_{t}\subset\partial M
and
\Sigma_{t}
meets
\partial M
orthogonally along
\partial M
.

(ii)

H_{\Sigma_{t}}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\nu_{\Sigma_{t}}\rangle
is constant on
\Sigma_{t}
.

Proof.

Fix some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Given
\psi\in C^{2,\alpha}(\Sigma)
, define

\Sigma_{\psi}:=\{\exp_{x}(\psi(x)\nu_{\Sigma}(x)):x\in\Sigma\}

with mean curvature
H_{\Sigma_{\psi}}
and unit normal
\nu_{\Sigma_{\psi}}
. Set
\tilde{H}_{\Sigma_{\psi}}:=H_{\Sigma_{\psi}}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\nu_{\Sigma_{\psi}}\rangle
.

Consider the map

\mathcal{F}:C^{2,\alpha}(\Sigma)\to\mathring{C}^{\alpha}(\Sigma)\times\mathbb{R}\times C^{1,\alpha}(\partial\Sigma)

defined by

\psi\mapsto\left(\tilde{H}_{\Sigma_{\psi}}-\frac{1}{\vol(\Sigma)}\int_{\Sigma}\tilde{H}_{\Sigma_{\psi}},\frac{1}{\vol(\Sigma)}\int_{\Sigma}\psi,\langle\nu_{\Sigma_{\psi}},\eta\rangle\right),

where
\mathring{C}^{\alpha}(\Sigma)
denotes the space of
\alpha
-Hölder continuous functions on
\Sigma
with zero average.

By Lemma
5.3
, the linearization
(D\mathcal{F})_{\psi=0}:C^{2,\alpha}(\Sigma)\to\mathring{C}^{\alpha}(\Sigma)\times\mathbb{R}\times C^{1,\alpha}(\partial\Sigma)
is given by

f\mapsto\left(-\Delta_{\Sigma}f+\frac{1}{\vol(\Sigma)}\int_{\partial\Sigma}\langle\nabla^{\Sigma}f,\eta\rangle,\frac{1}{\vol(\Sigma)}\int_{\Sigma}f,-\langle\nabla^{\Sigma}f,\eta\rangle\right),

which is bijective.

Hence, by the implicit function theorem, there exists
\delta>0
and a smooth map
w:\Sigma\times(-\delta,\delta)\to\mathbb{R}
that satisfies the desired properties.
∎

By our assumption

\inf_{\partial M}\bigl(H_{\partial M}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\eta\rangle\bigr)>0,

Proposition
2.1
implies that we can construct a compact, connected, orientable hypersurface
\Sigma\subset M
which is a free boundary area minimizer in its relative homology class within
(M,e^{\frac{2}{n-1}\varphi}g)
. Moreover, this hypersurface
\Sigma
satisfies

\int_{\partial\Sigma}\Xi\wedge\Theta_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Theta_{n-2}\neq 0.

Let

\Sigma_{t}:=\{\exp_{x}(w(x,t)\nu_{\Sigma}(x)):x\in\Sigma\}

be the foliation constructed in Lemma
3.5
for
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
. Clearly,
\Sigma_{t}
is relative homologous to
\Sigma
hence

\int_{\partial\Sigma_{t}}\Xi\wedge\Theta_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Theta_{n-2}\neq 0.

We define the lapse function
f_{t}:\Sigma_{t}\to\mathbb{R}
as follows: at the point
\exp_{x}(w(x,t)\nu_{\Sigma}(x))
, we set

(1)

{}f_{t}:=\left\langle\nu_{\Sigma_{t}}(x),\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\exp_{x}(w(x,t)\nu_{\Sigma}(x))\right\rangle,

where
\nu_{\Sigma_{t}}(x)
is the unit normal vector of
\Sigma_{t}
at
\exp_{x}(w(x,t)\nu_{\Sigma}(x))
.

By choosing
\delta
smaller if necessary, we may assume
f_{t}>0
on
\Sigma_{t}
for all
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
. Since
\mu(t):=H_{\Sigma_{t}}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\nu_{\Sigma_{t}}\rangle
depends only on
t
, the lapse function
f_{t}
satisfies the Jacobi equation

(2)

\begin{split}&-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}f_{t}-\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu_{\Sigma_{t}},\nu_{\Sigma_{t}})f_{t}-|h_{\Sigma_{t}}|^{2}f_{t}\\
&\quad+(D^{2}_{M}\varphi)(\nu_{\Sigma_{t}},\nu_{\Sigma_{t}})f_{t}-\langle\nabla^{\Sigma_{t}}\varphi,\nabla^{\Sigma_{t}}f_{t}\rangle=\mu^{\prime}(t),\end{split}

with the Neumann boundary condition

(3)

\langle\nabla^{\Sigma_{t}}f_{t},\eta\rangle=h_{\partial M}(\nu_{\Sigma_{t}},\nu_{\Sigma_{t}})f_{t}.

Lemma 3.6
.

We have

H_{\Sigma_{t}}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\nu_{\Sigma_{t}}\rangle\geq 0\text{ for all }t\in(-\delta,0]

and

H_{\Sigma_{t}}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\nu_{\Sigma_{t}}\rangle\leq 0\text{ for all }t\in[0,\delta).

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that
\mu^{\prime}(t)\leq 0
for all
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
, where
\mu(t):=H_{\Sigma_{t}}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\nu_{\Sigma_{t}}\rangle
.

Suppose there exists some
t_{0}\in(-\delta,\delta)
with
\mu^{\prime}(t_{0})>0
. Choose a constant
\tau>0
such that

\frac{\mu^{\prime}(t_{0})}{f_{t_{0}}}\geq\tau

at every point of
\Sigma_{t_{0}}
. Then, from the Jacobi equation (
2
), we have

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t_{0}}}f_{t_{0}}-\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu_{\Sigma_{t_{0}}},\nu_{\Sigma_{t_{0}}})f_{t_{0}}-|h_{\Sigma_{t_{0}}}|^{2}f_{t_{0}}

\displaystyle\quad+(D^{2}_{M}\varphi)(\nu_{\Sigma_{t_{0}}},\nu_{\Sigma_{t_{0}}})f_{t_{0}}-\langle\nabla^{\Sigma_{t_{0}}}\varphi,\nabla^{\Sigma_{t_{0}}}f_{t_{0}}\rangle\geq\tau f_{t_{0}}.

By Proposition
3.2
, let
v:=e^{\varphi}|_{\Sigma_{t_{0}}}\cdot f_{t_{0}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle-2\Delta_{\Sigma_{t_{0}}}\log v-|\nabla^{\Sigma_{t_{0}}}\log v|^{2}+R_{\Sigma_{t_{0}}}

\displaystyle\geq-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M}+2\tau

\displaystyle\geq 2\tau

at each point of
\Sigma_{t_{0}}
.

Applying Theorem
2.2
to
\Sigma_{t_{0}}
, we conclude

2\inf_{\partial\Sigma_{t_{0}}}\bigl(H_{\partial\Sigma_{t_{0}}}+\langle\nabla^{\Sigma_{t_{0}}}\log v,\eta\rangle\bigr)\cdot\sigma(\Sigma_{t_{0}},g|_{\Sigma_{t_{0}}})\leq 4\pi-2\tau|\Sigma|.

The Neumann boundary condition (
3
) implies that

H_{\partial\Sigma_{t_{0}}}+\langle\nabla^{\Sigma_{t_{0}}}\log v,\eta\rangle=H_{\partial M}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\eta\rangle\geq 1

at every point on
\partial\Sigma_{t_{0}}
.

It follows that
\sigma(\Sigma_{t_{0}},g|_{\Sigma_{t_{0}}})\leq 2\pi-\tau|\Sigma|
. This yields a contradiction since we must have
\sigma(\Sigma_{t_{0}},g|_{\Sigma_{t_{0}}})\geq\sigma(M,g)=2\pi
. Thus,
\mu^{\prime}(t)\leq 0
for all
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
.

Consequently,
H_{\Sigma_{t}}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\nu_{\Sigma_{t}}\rangle
is nonincreasing in
t
. Since it equals 0 at
t=0
, we have
H_{\Sigma_{t}}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\nu_{\Sigma_{t}}\rangle\geq 0
for all
t\in(-\delta,0]
and
H_{\Sigma_{t}}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\nu_{\Sigma_{t}}\rangle\leq 0
for all
t\in[0,\delta)
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

For each
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
, we have
\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}e^{\varphi}=\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}
.

Proof.

Since
\langle\nu_{\Sigma_{t}},\eta\rangle=0
, applying the first variation formula for weighted areas gives:

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}e^{\varphi}\right)=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}(H_{\Sigma_{t}}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\nu_{\Sigma_{t}}\rangle)e^{\varphi}f_{t},

where
f_{t}>0
for all
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
. By Lemma
3.6
, we have
H_{\Sigma_{t}}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\nu_{\Sigma_{t}}\rangle\geq 0
for
t\leq 0
and
H_{\Sigma_{t}}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\nu_{\Sigma_{t}}\rangle\leq 0
for
t\geq 0
. This ensures that

\int_{\Sigma_{t}}e^{\varphi}\leq\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}

for
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
.

On the other hand, since
\Sigma_{t}
is relatively homologous to
\Sigma
for each
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
, the definition of
\Sigma
as a free boundary area minimizer implies

\int_{\Sigma_{t}}e^{\varphi}\geq\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}

for all
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
, completing the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

For each
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
, the following statements hold:

•

\Sigma_{t}
is totally geodesic, with
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu_{\Sigma_{t}},\nu_{\Sigma_{t}})=0
and
R_{M}=0
at every point on
\Sigma_{t}
.

•

\varphi|_{\Sigma_{t}}
is constant, with
\nabla^{M}\varphi=0
and
(D^{2}_{M}\varphi)(\nu_{\Sigma_{t}},\nu_{\Sigma_{t}})=0
at every point on
\Sigma_{t}
.

•

h_{\partial M}(\nu_{\Sigma_{t}},\nu_{\Sigma_{t}})=0
and
H_{\partial\Sigma_{t}}=1
at every point on
\partial\Sigma_{t}
.

•

The function
f_{t}
is constant on
\Sigma_{t}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.7
, for each
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
, the hypersurface
\Sigma_{t}
is a free boundary area minimizer in its relative homology class within
(M,e^{\frac{2}{n-1}\varphi}g)
, and it satisfies

\int_{\partial\Sigma_{t}}\Xi\wedge\Theta_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Theta_{n-2}\neq 0.

Therefore, the first three statements follow directly from Lemma
3.4
.

With these established, the Jacobi equation (
2
) for the lapse function
f_{t}
simplifies to
\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}f_{t}=0
, together with the Neumann boundary condition (
3
) stating
\langle\nabla^{\Sigma_{t}}f_{t},\eta\rangle=0
. Consequently,
f_{t}
must be constant on
\Sigma_{t}
.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

If
\Sigma\subset M
is a compact, connected, orientable hypersurface that is a free boundary area minimizer in its relative homology class within
(M,e^{\frac{2}{n-1}\varphi}g)
and satisfies

\int_{\partial\Sigma}\Xi\wedge\Theta_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Theta_{n-2}\neq 0,

then there exists a neighborhood
U
of
\Sigma
that is isometric to a Riemannian product, and
\varphi
is constant on
U
.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.8
, in a neighborhood around
\Sigma
, we have
\nabla^{M}\varphi=0
and the normal vector field
\nu_{\Sigma_{t}}
is parallel (see
[
1
]
and
[
3
]
). Consequently, a neighborhood
U
of
\Sigma
is isometric to a Riemannian product, and
\varphi
is constant on
U
.
∎

Finally, we define
\Phi:\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}\to M
by
\Phi(x,t)=\exp_{x}(t\nu_{\Sigma}(x))
.

Theorem 3.10
.

The function
\varphi
is constant on
M
, and the map
\Phi
is a local isometry with
\Phi(\partial\Sigma\times\mathbb{R})\subset\partial M
.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.9
,
\varphi
is constant in a neighborhood of
\Sigma
, say
\varphi_{0}
, and there exists a small
\delta>0
such that
\Phi|_{\Sigma\times(-\delta,\delta)}
is a local isometry with
\Phi(\partial\Sigma\times(-\delta,\delta))\subset\partial M
.

Define
\tau
to be the largest real number for which
\Phi|_{\Sigma\times[0,\tau]}
is a local isometry,
\Phi(\partial\Sigma\times[0,\tau])\subset\partial M
, and
\varphi=\varphi_{0}
on
\Phi(\Sigma\times[0,\tau])
.

Consider the compact, connected, orientable hypersurface

\hat{\Sigma}:=\{\exp_{x}(\tau\nu_{\Sigma}(x)):x\in\Sigma\},

with
\partial\hat{\Sigma}\subset\partial M
, and
\hat{\Sigma}
meets
\partial M
orthogonally. Moreover,
\hat{\Sigma}
is relatively homologous to
\Sigma
and

\int_{\hat{\Sigma}}e^{\varphi}=e^{\varphi_{0}}\vol(\hat{\Sigma})=e^{\varphi_{0}}\vol(\Sigma)=\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}.

Therefore,
\hat{\Sigma}
is also a free boundary area minimizer in its relative homology class within
(M,e^{\frac{2}{n-1}\varphi}g)
, satisfying

\int_{\partial\hat{\Sigma}}\Xi\wedge\Theta_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Theta_{n-2}\neq 0.

By Corollary
3.9
, there is a neighborhood of
\hat{\Sigma}
on which
\varphi
is constant and isometric to a Riemannian product. Hence, for some small
\delta>0
,
\Phi|_{\Sigma\times[0,\tau+\delta)}
is a local isometry with
\Phi(\partial\Sigma\times[0,\tau+\delta))\subset\partial M
and
\varphi=\varphi_{0}
on
\Phi(\Sigma\times[0,\tau+\delta))
.

This contradicts the maximality of
\tau
. Thus, we must have
\Phi|_{\Sigma\times[0,\infty)}
as a local isometry with
\Phi(\partial\Sigma\times[0,\infty))\subset\partial M
and
\varphi=\varphi_{0}
on
\Phi(\Sigma\times[0,\infty))
. An analogous argument applies to
\Phi|_{\Sigma\times(-\infty,0]}
, concluding that
\Phi
is a local isometry on
\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}
, and
\varphi
is constant on
M
.
∎

We are now ready to complete the proof of Theorem
3.1
. By Theorem
3.10
, the map
\Phi:\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}\to M
is a local isometry with
\Phi(\partial\Sigma\times\mathbb{R})\subset\partial M
. Consequently,
\Phi
is a covering map. Lemma
3.3
showed that the universal cover of
(\Sigma,g|_{\Sigma})
is isometric to
B^{2}(1)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-3}
with the standard product metric. Therefore, the universal cover of
(M,g)
is isometric to
B^{2}(1)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-2}
, equipped with the standard product metric.

4.
The Systolic Inequality

In this section, we prove the systolic inequality stated in Theorem
1.2
that extends Zhu’s result in Theorem
[
23
]
.

We assume that
(M^{n},g)
is a closed, connected, orientable Riemannian manifold and that
\varphi
is a smooth function on
M
. Suppose there exists a smooth map

(\omega,\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n-2}):M\to S^{2}\times T^{n-2}

with nonzero degree. Denote by
\Omega
the pullback of the volume form on
S^{2}
under the map
\omega:M\to S^{2}
, and by
\Theta_{i}
the pullback of the volume form on
S^{1}
under the map
\theta_{i}:M\to S^{1}
. Hence,

\int_{M}\Omega\wedge\Theta_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Theta_{n-2}\neq 0.

We define

\mathscr{A}(M,g):=\inf\left\{\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma):\Sigma\text{ is a smooth surface of }M\text{ with }\int_{\Sigma}\Omega\neq 0\right\}.

The following slicing argument is analogous to Proposition
2.1
. For the proof of the slicing argument, we refer to the works of Schoen and Yau
[
21
,
22
]
and Gromov and Lawson
[
14
]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

We can find a collection of compact, connected, orientable submanifolds
\Sigma_{k}
, along with a sequence of positive smooth functions
u_{k}:\Sigma_{k}\to\mathbb{R}
for
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-2\}
, and a sequence of positive smooth functions
\rho_{k}:\Sigma_{k}\to\mathbb{R}
for
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-2\}
, satisfying the following properties:

(i)

\Sigma_{0}=M
and
\rho_{0}=e^{\varphi}
.

(ii)

\dim\Sigma_{k}=n-k
for each
k\in\{0,\ldots,n-2\}
.

(iii)

For each
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-2\}
,

\int_{\Sigma_{k}}\Omega\wedge\Theta_{k+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Theta_{n-2}\neq 0.

(iv)

For each
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-2\}
,
\Sigma_{k}
is a homologically area-minimizing hypersurface in
(\Sigma_{k-1},\rho_{k-1}^{\frac{2}{n-k}}g_{\Sigma_{k-1}})
.

(v)

For each
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-2\}
,

H_{\Sigma_{k}}+\left\langle\nabla^{\Sigma_{k-1}}\log\rho_{k-1},\nu_{\Sigma_{k}}\right\rangle=0.

(vi)

For each
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-2\}
, the function
u_{k}
satisfies

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Sigma_{k}}u_{k}-\operatorname{Ric}_{\Sigma_{k-1}}(\nu_{\Sigma_{k}},\nu_{\Sigma_{k}})u_{k}-|h_{\Sigma_{k}}|^{2}u_{k}

\displaystyle\quad+(D^{2}_{\Sigma_{k-1}}\log\rho_{k-1})(\nu_{\Sigma_{k}},\nu_{\Sigma_{k}})u_{k}-\left\langle\nabla^{\Sigma_{k}}\log\rho_{k-1},\nabla^{\Sigma_{k}}u_{k}\right\rangle=\lambda_{k}u_{k},

where
\lambda_{k}\geq 0
is a nonnegative constant.

(vii)

For each
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-2\}
, we have

\rho_{k}=\rho_{k-1}|_{\Sigma_{k}}\cdot u_{k}.

(viii)

For each
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-2\}
,

\displaystyle-2\Delta_{\Sigma_{k}}\log\rho_{k}-|\nabla^{\Sigma_{k}}\log\rho_{k}|^{2}+R_{\Sigma_{k}}

\displaystyle\quad+2\Delta_{M}\varphi+|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}-R_{M}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}|\nabla^{\Sigma_{j}}\log u_{j}|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}|h_{\Sigma_{j}}|^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}.

Theorem 4.2
.

There exists a closed, connected, orientable surface
\Sigma
with

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\Omega\neq 0

and

\inf_{M}\bigl(-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M}\bigr)\cdot|\Sigma|\leq 8\pi.

Proof.

We apply Proposition
4.1
with
k=n-2
. Let
\Sigma=\Sigma_{n-2}
and
\psi=\log\rho_{n-2}
. Then, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\Omega\neq 0

and

-2\Delta_{\Sigma}\psi-|\nabla^{\Sigma}\psi|^{2}+R_{\Sigma}\geq\inf_{M}\bigl(-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M}\bigr)

at each point on
\Sigma
.

Since
\Sigma
is connected, its Euler characteristic is at most 2. Integrating the above inequality and applying the Gauss–Bonnet Theorem yields

\displaystyle 8\pi

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Sigma}R_{\Sigma}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Sigma}\left(-2\Delta_{\Sigma}\psi-|\nabla^{\Sigma}\psi|^{2}+R_{\Sigma}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{M}\bigl(-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M}\bigr)\cdot|\Sigma|.

This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

We have

\inf_{M}\bigl(-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M}\bigr)\cdot\mathscr{A}(M,g)\leq 8\pi.

5.
The Equality Case of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we study the equality case of Theorem
1.2
. Our approach is similar in spirit to the rigidity arguments presented in
[
3
]
,
[
7
]
, and
[
23
]
. We restate the result here for convenience.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose

\inf_{M}\bigl(-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M}\bigr)\cdot\mathscr{A}(M,g)=8\pi.

Then
\varphi
is constant on
M
, and the universal cover of
M
is isometric to
S^{2}(r)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-2}
with the standard product metric for some
r>0
.

The proof is similar to that of Theorem
3.1
, and we only indicate the necessary changes.

We proceed by induction on the dimension
n
. The result follows from the Gauss–Bonnet Theorem for
n=2
, and we assume that Theorem
4.2
holds for all Riemannian manifolds with dimension at most
n-1
.

Now suppose
(M,g)
is an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with

\inf_{M}\bigl(-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M}\bigr)\cdot\mathscr{A}(M,g)=8\pi.

After rescaling the metric, we may assume that

\inf_{M}\bigl(-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M}\bigr)=2\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{A}(M,g)=4\pi.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose
\Sigma^{n-1}\subset M
is a closed, connected, orientable, stable minimal hypersurface in
(M,e^{\frac{2}{n-1}\varphi}g)
with

\int_{\Sigma}\Omega\wedge\Theta_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Theta_{n-2}\neq 0.

Then:

•

The universal cover of
\Sigma
is isometric to
S^{2}(1)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-3}
equipped with the standard product metric.

•

The function
e^{\varphi}|_{\Sigma}\cdot u
is constant on
\Sigma
, where
u
is the first eigenfunction of the Jacobi operator

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Sigma}u-\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})u-|h_{\Sigma}|^{2}u

\displaystyle\quad+(D^{2}_{M}\log\rho)(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})u-\langle\nabla^{\Sigma}\log\rho,\nabla^{\Sigma}u\rangle=\lambda u.

Proof.

First, we have the trivial inequality

\mathscr{A}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})\geq\mathscr{A}(M,g)=4\pi.

On the other hand, Proposition
3.2
implies that for
\rho=e^{\varphi}|_{\Sigma}\cdot u
,

\displaystyle-2\Delta_{\Sigma}\log\rho-|\nabla^{\Sigma}\log\rho|^{2}+R_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{M}\bigl(-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M}\bigr)

\displaystyle=2

at each point in
\Sigma
. Theorem
4.2
then implies that

\mathscr{A}(\Sigma,g|_{\Sigma})\leq 4\pi

and

8\pi=\inf_{M}\bigl(-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M}\bigr)\cdot\mathscr{A}(M,g).

Therefore, we have

\inf_{M}\bigl(-2\Delta_{M}\varphi-|\nabla^{M}\varphi|^{2}+R_{M}\bigr)\cdot\mathscr{A}(M,g)=8\pi.

The claim now follows from the induction hypothesis.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose
\Sigma^{n-1}\subset M
is a closed, connected, orientable, stable minimal hypersurface in
(M,e^{\frac{2}{n-1}\varphi}g)
with

\int_{\Sigma}\Omega\wedge\Theta_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Theta_{n-2}\neq 0.

Then:

•

\Sigma
is totally geodesic, with
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})=0
and
R_{M}=0
at each point in
\Sigma
.

•

\varphi|_{\Sigma}
is constant, with
\nabla^{M}\varphi=0
and
(D^{2}_{M}\varphi)(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})=0
at each point in
\Sigma
.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the proof of Lemma
3.4
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose
\Sigma^{n-1}\subset M
is a closed, connected, orientable stable minimal hypersurface in
(M,e^{\frac{2}{n-1}\varphi}g)
with

\int_{\Sigma}\Omega\wedge\Theta_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Theta_{n-2}\neq 0.

Then there exists
\delta>0
and a smooth map
w:\Sigma\times(-\delta,\delta)\to\mathbb{R}
with the following properties:

•

For each
x\in\Sigma
, we have
w(x,0)=0
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}w(x,t)\big|_{t=0}=1
.

•

For each
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
, we have
\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}(w(\cdot,t)-t)\,d\vol_{g}=0
.

•

For each
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
, define

\Sigma_{t}:=\{\exp_{x}(w(x,t)\nu_{\Sigma}(x)):x\in\Sigma\}.

Then
H_{\Sigma_{t}}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\nu_{\Sigma_{t}}\rangle
is constant on
\Sigma_{t}
.

Proof.

Fix some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Given
\psi\in C^{2,\alpha}(\Sigma)
, define

\Sigma_{\psi}:=\{\exp_{x}(\psi(x)\nu_{\Sigma}(x)):x\in\Sigma\}

with mean curvature
H_{\Sigma_{\psi}}
and unit normal
\nu_{\Sigma_{\psi}}
. Set
\tilde{H}_{\Sigma_{\psi}}:=H_{\Sigma_{\psi}}+\langle\nabla^{M}\varphi,\nu_{\Sigma_{\psi}}\rangle
.

Consider the map

\mathcal{F}:C^{2,\alpha}(\Sigma)\to\mathring{C}^{\alpha}(\Sigma)\times\mathbb{R}

defined by

\psi\mapsto\left(\tilde{H}_{\Sigma_{\psi}}-\frac{1}{\vol(\Sigma)}\int_{\Sigma}\tilde{H}_{\Sigma_{\psi}},\frac{1}{\vol(\Sigma)}\int_{\Sigma}\psi\right),

where
\mathring{C}^{\alpha}(\Sigma)
denotes the space of
\alpha
-Hölder continuous functions on
\Sigma
with zero average.

By Lemma
5.3
, the linearization
(D\mathcal{F})_{\psi=0}:C^{2,\alpha}(\Sigma)\to\mathring{C}^{\alpha}(\Sigma)\times\mathbb{R}
is given by

f\mapsto\left(-\Delta_{\Sigma}f,\frac{1}{\vol(\Sigma)}\int_{\Sigma}f\right),

which is bijective.

Hence, by the implicit function theorem, there exists
\delta>0
and a smooth map
w:\Sigma\times(-\delta,\delta)\to\mathbb{R}
that satisfies the desired properties.
∎

By Proposition
4.1
, we can construct a closed, connected, orientable hypersurface

Proposition 7.1
.

Let

Suppose there exists a Riemannian metric

∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem

. We evolve

by the heat equation

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g(t)=-2\operatorname{Ric}_{g(t)},&g(0)=g,\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi(t)=\Delta_{g(t)}\varphi(t),&\varphi(0)=\varphi.\end{cases}

Corollary

implies that the quantity

satisfies the equation

Appendix A
Evolution of Stabilized Scalar Curvature under Ricci Flow Coupled with the Heat Equation

In this section, we show that the infimum of the stabilized scalar curvature is monotone under the Ricci flow coupled with the heat equation. Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold evolved by the Ricci flow

\frac{\partial g(t)}{\partial t}=-2\operatorname{Ric}_{g(t)},

and let
\varphi(x,t):M\to\mathbb{R}
be a time-dependent smooth function. We define

S=2\Delta_{g(t)}\varphi(t)-|\nabla\varphi|_{g(t)}^{2}+R_{g(t)}

to be the stabilized scalar curvature at time
t
. Note that we are using a slightly different sign convention here compared to other parts of the article, which is essentially equivalent by replacing
\varphi
with
-\varphi
in the definition. We refer the reader to
[
20
]
and
[
18
]
for general results on Ricci flows coupled with other parabolic equations.

Lemma A.1
.

We have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial S}{\partial t}-\Delta S

\displaystyle=2|\operatorname{Ric}+D^{2}\varphi|^{2}+2\Delta\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi-\Delta\varphi\right)-2\partial^{i}\varphi\partial_{i}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi-\Delta\varphi\right).

Proof.

It is also well-known that for any function
\varphi
, the following holds:

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Delta\varphi-\Delta\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi=2\operatorname{Ric}^{ij}D^{2}_{i,j}\varphi.

This implies

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Delta\varphi-\Delta\Delta\varphi=\Delta\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi-\Delta\varphi\right)+2\operatorname{Ric}^{ij}D^{2}_{i,j}\varphi.

Additionally, the Bochner formula gives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|d\varphi|^{2}-\Delta|d\varphi|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\partial^{i}\varphi\partial_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi+2\operatorname{Ric}_{ij}\partial^{i}\varphi\partial^{j}\varphi

\displaystyle-2\partial^{i}\varphi\partial_{i}\Delta\varphi-2|D^{2}\varphi|^{2}-2\operatorname{Ric}_{ij}\partial^{i}\varphi\partial^{j}\varphi

\displaystyle=2\partial^{i}\varphi\partial_{i}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi-\Delta\varphi\right)-2|D^{2}\varphi|^{2}.

Finally, recall that the evolution of the scalar curvature along the Ricci flow is given by

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}R-\Delta R=2|\operatorname{Ric}|^{2}.

Combining these three identities, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta S

\displaystyle=4\operatorname{Ric}^{ij}D^{2}_{i,j}\varphi+2|D^{2}\varphi|^{2}+2|\operatorname{Ric}|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\Delta\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi-\Delta\varphi\right)-2\partial^{i}\varphi\partial_{i}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi-\Delta\varphi\right)

\displaystyle=2|\operatorname{Ric}+D^{2}\varphi|^{2}+2\Delta\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi-\Delta\varphi\right)-2\partial^{i}\varphi\partial_{i}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi-\Delta\varphi\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary A.2
.

If we evolve
\varphi
by the heat equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi(t)=\Delta_{g(t)}\varphi(t),

then

\frac{\partial S}{\partial t}-\Delta S=2|\operatorname{Ric}+D^{2}\varphi|^{2}.

Remark A.3
.

Corollary
A.2
can be compared to Perelman’s calculation of the
\mathcal{F}
-functional in
[
20
]
. Instead of solving the forward equation, Perelman considers
\varphi
as the solution to the backward heat equation

\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}+\Delta\varphi-|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+R=0,

and shows that the quantity
Se^{-\varphi}
is a supersolution to the adjoint heat equation

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\Delta-R\right)(Se^{-\varphi})=2e^{-\varphi}|\operatorname{Ric}+D^{2}\varphi|^{2}\geq 0.

The monotonicity of the
\mathcal{F}
-functional follows from integrating this inequality over
M
.

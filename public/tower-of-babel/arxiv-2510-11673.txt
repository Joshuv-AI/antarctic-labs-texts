Integral matrices of fixed rank over number fields
Nihar Gargava, Vlad Serban, Maryna Viazovska, Ilaria Viglino

Abstract
. We prove an asymptotic formula for the number of fixed rank matrices with integer coefficients over a number field K / ℚ K/\mathbb{Q} and bounded norm. As an application, we derive an approximate Rogers integral formula for discrete sets of module lattices obtained from lifts of algebraic codes. This in turn implies that the moment estimates of [ 1 ] , which inform the behavior of short vectors in sets of random lattices, also carry through for large enough discrete sets of module lattices.



1.
Introduction

We start by revisiting a fundamental counting result for integral matrices of fixed rank by Y. Katznelson
[
2
]
. Fix integers
n>m\geq k\geq 1
. The main result of
[
2
]
proves the following asymptotic counts:

Theorem 1
.

Let
f:\M_{n\times m}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the indicator function of an origin-centered unit ball in the
l^{2}
-norm
\|\cdot\|:\M_{n\times m}(\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}^{nm}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
Then, for some constants
,>0
that depend on
n,m,k
but not on
T\geq 1
, one has

(2)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\in\M_{n\times m}(\mathbb{Z})\\
\rank(A)=k\end{subarray}}f(\tfrac{1}{T}A)=\Cr{main}\cdot T^{kn}\cdot(1+\varepsilon),

where

(3)

|\varepsilon|\leq\Cr{error}\cdot T^{-1}.

This theorem solves an interesting counting problem. Indeed, when
f
is the indicator function of a unit
l^{2}
-ball we have:

(4)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\in\M_{n\times m}(\mathbb{Z})\\
\rank(A)=k\end{subarray}}f(\tfrac{1}{T}A)=\card\{A\in\M_{n\times m}(\mathbb{Z})\mid\rank(A)=k,\|A\|\leq T\}.

This result of Katznelson has motivated many subsequent refinements and generalizations, see for example
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
.

The first main result of our paper establishes a natural number-theoretic generalization of Katznelson’s counting result.
Let
K
be a number field of degree
\deg K=d
,
and let
\mathcal{O}_{K}
denote the ring of integers of the number field.
For notational simplicity, we will abbreviate
K_{\mathbb{R}}=K\otimes\mathbb{R}
.
We consider the analogous counting problem over number fields, so that we work with matrices with
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-entries. Moreover, in the spirit of similar results in the geometry of numbers, it seems natural to allow summation over more general functions such as compactly supported continuous functions or indicator functions of bounded convex sets on the space of matrices
\M_{n\times m}(K_{\mathbb{R}})
. For a class of so-called admissible functions which include the examples above, we show:

Theorem 2
.

Let
f:\M_{n\times m}(K_{\mathbb{R}})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be an admissible function (see
16
).
Then, for some constants
\Cr{main},\Cr{error}>0
that depend on
K,n,m,k,f
but not on
T\geq 1
, one has

(5)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\in\M_{n\times m}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\\
\rank(A)=k\end{subarray}}f(\tfrac{1}{T}A)=\Cr{main}\cdot T^{knd}\cdot(1+\varepsilon),

where

(6)

|\varepsilon|\leq\Cr{error}\cdot T^{-1}\log T.

Moreover, unless
d=k=1
and
m=n-1
, the
\log T
in
Eq.

6
can be dropped. See
Section

1.3
for a note about this
\log T
term.

1.1.
Connection to Rogers’ integral formula

It seems furthermore reasonable to expect that the leading constant
\Cr{main}>0
in the main term of the asymptotic formula in
Eq.

5
carries some arithmetic-geometric meaning. To that end, we highlight a striking connection of the leading constant in both Katznelson’s and our more general work to the Rogers’ integration formula in the geometry of numbers
[
1
,
6
,
7
]

.

Let us explain. Observe that for any
A\in\M_{n\times m}(\mathcal{O}_{K})

such that
\rank(A)=k
, one can perform the rank factorization and write

(7)

A=C\cdot D,

where
C\in\M_{n\times k}(K)
,
D\in\M_{k\times m}(K)
and
\rank(C)=\rank(D)=k
. Assuming that
D
is in row-reduced echelon form of maximal rank, the choice of
C
and
D
is unique.
For brevity, for the rest of the paper we will abbreviate “echelon” for “row-reduced echelon of maximal rank”.
We then observe the following decomposition, which enables the connection to Rogers’ formula:

(8)

\displaystyle\{A\in\M_{n\times m}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\mid\rank(A)=k\}

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\in\M_{k\times m}(K)\\
D\text{ echelon }\end{subarray}}\{C\in\M_{n\times k}(K)\mid C\cdot D\in\M_{n\times m}(\mathcal{O}_{K}),\rank(C)=k\}\cdot D.

For an echelon matrix
D\in\M_{k\times m}(K)
the denominator
\mathfrak{D}(D)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
is given by the following index:

(10)

\mathfrak{D}(D)=[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{k}:\{v\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{k}\mid D^{T}v\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{m}\}].

We show in this paper that the constant
\Cr{main}
in
Eq.

5
is precisely given by

(11)

\Cr{main}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\in\M_{k\times m}(K)\\
D\text{ echelon}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{D}(D)^{-n}\int_{x\in\M_{n\times k}(K_{\mathbb{R}})}f(xD)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where the integral is over the Euclidean structure on
\M_{n\times k}(K_{\mathbb{R}})
given by
Eq.

46
.
Convergence of the infinite sum on the right-hand side when
n>m\geq k
in fact follows from Schmidt’s seminal work on rational points of Grassmannian varieties over number fields, as explained in
Section

3
.

Heuristically,
Eq.

11
can be understood as follows: using the bijection in
Eq.

9
,
we can at least formally write

(12)

T^{-knd}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\in\M_{n\times m}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\\
\rank(A)=k\end{subarray}}f(\tfrac{1}{T}A)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\in\M_{k\times m}(K_{\mathbb{R}})\\
D\text{ echelon}\end{subarray}}\Big[T^{-knd}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\in\M_{n\times k}(K)\\
C\cdot D\in\M_{n\times m}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\\
\rank(C)=k\end{subarray}}f(\tfrac{1}{T}C\cdot D)\Big].

Now it can be easily argued that the contribution of each echelon matrix
D
approximates a Riemann integral. More precisely, as
T\rightarrow\infty
one can observe that the Euclidean measure on
\M_{n\times k}(K_{\mathbb{R}})
is chosen conveniently so that

(13)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\in\M_{n\times k}(K)\\
C\cdot D\in\M_{n\times m}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\\
\rank(C)=k\end{subarray}}{T^{-knd}}f(\tfrac{1}{T}C\cdot D)\rightarrow\mathfrak{D}(D)^{-n}\int_{\M_{n\times k}(K_{\mathbb{R}})}f(xD)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Therefore, one knows that
Eq.

11
is the obvious candidate value for
\Cr{main}
. However, it is nontrivial to show that the exchange of limits is possible in
Eq.

12
. For example, each of the inner terms might have an error which accumulates over infinitely many
D
in the outer sum. Proving our main theorem essentially amounts to showing that such issues do not arise.

Interestingly, Katznelson
[
2
]
uses some formulas of Terras
[
8
]

to relate the constant
c_{1}
to special values of Koecher zeta functions. Combining this with our observations leads to interesting equalities. For instance, in the case of
K=\mathbb{Q}
and
f
the indicator function of the origin-centered unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{nm}
, we obtain the formula:

(14)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\in\M_{k\times m}(K)\\
D\text{ echelon}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{D}(D)^{-n}\int_{x\in\M_{n\times k}(K_{\mathbb{R}})}f(xD)\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{V(nk)\cdot Z_{k,m-k}(I,n/2)}{\zeta(n)\cdot\zeta(n-1)\cdots\zeta(n-k+1)}

where we denote by
V(s)
the unit ball’s volume in
\mathbb{R}^{s}
and where for a positive symmetric matrix
X\in M_{m\times m}(\mathbb{R})
with
\Re(s)>m/2
the Koecher zeta function is defined by

Z_{k,m-k}(X,s)=\sum_{L\in\mathbb{Z}^{m\times k}/\GL_{k}(\mathbb{Z})}\det(L^{t}XL)^{-s}.

1.2.
Motivation from coding theory and cryptography

Consider an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module
\Lambda\subseteq K^{n}\otimes\mathbb{R}
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-rank
n
.
For a prime ideal
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}
and
1\leq r\leq n
, we will now define a
(\mathcal{P},r)
-Hecke neighbor
\Lambda^{\prime}
to be a lattice given by the following construction.

Definition 3
.

Let
k_{\mathcal{P}}=\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathcal{P}
be the residue field
of
\mathcal{P}
and let
\N(\mathcal{P})=\card k_{\mathcal{P}}
be the ideal norm of
\mathcal{P}
.
Let
\pi_{\mathcal{P}}
be the “modulo
\mathcal{P}
” reduction map given as

(15)

\pi_{\mathcal{P}}:\Lambda\rightarrow\Lambda/\mathcal{P}\Lambda\simeq k_{\mathcal{P}}^{n}.

One then says that a lattice
\Lambda^{\prime}\subseteq K_{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a
(\mathcal{P},r)
-Hecke neighbor of
\Lambda
if
for some
r
-dimensional
k_{\mathcal{P}}
-subspace
V\subseteq k_{\mathcal{P}}^{n}
,

(16)

\Lambda^{\prime}=\N(\mathcal{P})^{-\left(1-\tfrac{r}{n}\right)}\pi^{-1}(V).

We will abbreviate
\Lambda^{\prime}\underset{(\mathcal{P},r)}{\leftarrow}\Lambda
to say that
\Lambda^{\prime}
is a
(\mathcal{P},r)
-Hecke neighbor of
\Lambda
.

The scaling factor in front of
\pi^{-1}(V)
in
Eq.

16

ensures that
\vol(K_{\mathbb{R}}^{n}/\Lambda)=\vol(K_{\mathbb{R}}^{n}/\Lambda^{\prime})
.

Given a lattice
\Lambda\subseteq K_{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the number of lattices
\Lambda^{\prime}\subseteq K_{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
that are
(\mathcal{P},r)
-Hecke neighbors of
\Lambda
is exactly the cardinality of the Grassmannian variety
\Gr(r,k_{\mathcal{P}}^{n})
over the finite field
k_{\mathcal{P}}
.
Due in part to this finiteness property, such constructions of lattices have drawn considerable interest in algorithmic applications of lattices.
They are referred to as “lifts of codes”
[
9
]

or “Construction A” lattices in the coding theory literature
[
10
,
11
]
;
see also the literature on
q
-ary lattices, for example
[
12
]
.
In lattice-based cryptography, such Hecke neighbors appear in “worst-case to average-case” reductions
[
13
]
.

Rogers’
[
14
]
was perhaps the first to study random
(p,r)
-Hecke neighbors
for the case of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, an integer prime
p
and
1<r<n
.
His key observation is that
(p,r)
-Hecke neighbors satisfy Siegel’s mean value theorem on average as
p\rightarrow\infty
. That is, for any admissible test function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, one has the convergence of expected values for lattice sums

(17)

\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda\underset{(p,r)}{\leftarrow}\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\Big(\sum_{v\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}f(v)\Big)\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,

for
p\rightarrow\infty
.
The modern way to understand this convergence is via Siegel’s mean value theorem. Writing
\mu_{\Pr}
for the Haar-probability measure on
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})/\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
, it states that

(18)

\int_{\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})/\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big(\sum_{v\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}f(v)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\Pr}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Moreover, due to the more modern work
[
15
]
, the fact that
(p,r)
-Hecke neighbors of a fixed lattice (let’s say
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
) equidistribute in the space
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})/\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
as
p\to\infty
is well-established. Therefore
Eq.

17
must hold after showing that when
p\rightarrow\infty
the left-hand side is suitably dominated.

However, the direct proof of
Eq.

17
as explained in
[
14
]
is elementary. All one has to do is rewrite

(19)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda\underset{(p,r)}{\leftarrow}\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\Big(\sum_{v\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}f(v)\Big)

(20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{v\in p\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}f(p^{-\left(1-\frac{r}{d}\right)}v)\Big)+\mathbb{E}_{V\in\Gr(r,k_{p}^{n})}\Big(\sum_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus p\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\ind_{\pi_{p}(v)\in V}f(p^{-\left(1-\frac{r}{d}\right)v})\Big).

One then observes that the first term must converge to
0
. Indeed, since
p\cdot p^{-(1-r/d)}=p^{r/d}\rightarrow\infty
, as
p
grows the first sum will not contain any points in the support of
f
. On the other hand, after substituting the cardinality
\card\Gr(r,k_{p}^{n})
, one can show that the second term converges towards a Riemann integral approximating
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
as
p\rightarrow\infty
.

For
2\leq m<n
, one may hope to generalize this elementary proof to
m
-th moments by considering the expectation

(21)

\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda\underset{(p,r)}{\leftarrow}{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}}\Big[\Big(\sum_{v\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}f(v)\Big)^{m}\Big].

However, one quickly runs into having to prove
Eq.

6
with
K=\mathbb{Q}
.
We therefore show in
Section

5
how our results allow us to evaluate over arbitrary number fields
K
moments as in
Eq.

21
as the norm of the prime
\mathcal{P}
goes to infinity. In particular, we obtain as a consequence of Theorem
41
:

Theorem 4
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
m\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
and
r
be chosen as either
n-1
or any number in
\{m,m+1,\dots,n-1\}
satisfying
1-\frac{r}{n}<\frac{1}{m}
. Let
f:K_{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function satisfying
16
.
As
\N(\mathcal{P})\rightarrow\infty
we have the convergence

(22)

\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda\underset{(\mathcal{P},r)}{\leftarrow}{{\mathcal{O}_{K}}^{n}}}\Big[\Big(\sum_{v\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}f(v)\Big)^{m}\Big]\to\int_{\SL_{n}(K_{\mathbb{R}})/\SL_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K})}\Big(\sum_{v\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}f(v)\Big)^{m}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\Pr}.

In other words, moments over the discrete spaces of Hecke neighbors approximate moments for the full space of Haar-random free
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-modules of unit covolume.

As an immediate corollary, we therefore deduce that the moment estimates of
[
1
]
, which control the behavior of short vectors in Haar-random number field lattices, also apply for primes
\mathcal{P}
of large enough norm to the discretized sets of Hecke neighbors of an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-lattice.

1.3.
The case of
k=1,d=1,n=m+1

There appears to be a technical gap in the proof of
[
2
]
for the case of
k=1,d=1,n=m+1
that was perhaps overlooked by the author but can be fixed as follows.

Let
f:\M_{n\times(n-1)}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the indicator function of a unit ball.
In this case, one wants to sum for
T\geq 1

(23)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\in\M_{n\times(n-1)}(\mathbb{Z})\\
\rank A=1\end{subarray}}f(\tfrac{1}{T}A)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\M_{1\times(n-1)}(\mathbb{Z})\setminus\{0\}\\
\gcd(v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1})=1\end{subarray}}\card\{w\in\M_{n\times 1}(\mathbb{Z})\setminus\{0\}\mid\|wv\|\leq T\}

Now it turns out for a column matrix
w
and a row matrix
v
one has
\|wv\|=\|w\|\|v\|
. Hence, the sum becomes

(24)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\M_{1\times(n-1)}(\mathbb{Z})\setminus\{0\}\\
1\leq\|v\|\leq T,\gcd(v)=1\end{subarray}}\card\{w\in\M_{n\times 1}(\mathbb{Z})\setminus\{0\}\mid\|w\|\leq T\|v\|^{-1}\}

If we bound for some constant
>0
the set

(25)

\card\{w\in\M_{n\times 1}(\mathbb{Z})\setminus\{0\}\mid\|w\|\leq T\|v\|^{-1}\}\leq V(n)\frac{T^{n}}{\|v\|^{n}}+\Cr{gauss1}{\frac{T^{n-1}}{\|v\|^{n-1}}},

then we observe that
\sum_{v\in\M_{1\times(n-1)}(\mathbb{Z}),\gcd(v)=1}\|v\|^{-n}
is a finite sum whereas
the second term must contribute to a
\sim\log T
factor. However, if we use any non-trivial bound on the Gauss circle problem in
n\geq 2
dimensions, we get

(26)

\card\{w\in\M_{n\times 1}(\mathbb{Z})\setminus\{0\}\mid\|w\|\leq T\|v\|^{-1}\}\leq V(n)\frac{T^{n}}{\|v\|^{n}}+{\frac{T^{n-1-\delta}}{\|v\|^{n-1-\delta}}},

for some
\delta>0
and no
\log T
term is introduced in the error term. This is because by summation by parts, one gets

(27)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\M_{1\times(n-1)(\mathbb{Z})}\\
\gcd(v)=1,\|v\|\leq T\end{subarray}}\|v\|^{-(n-1-\delta)}\leq T^{\delta}.

In the setting of
Eq.

6
, we do not restrict
f
to be the indicator function of some
l^{2}
-ball and therefore bounds from the Gauss circle problem do not necessarily apply. Hence, the
\log T
term cannot be removed for the case of
d=1,k=1,n=m+1
unless we change our
16
.

1.4.
A note on the implicit constants

In our work, the focus in
Eq.

6
is on asymptotics for
T\rightarrow\infty
.
We do not explore the variation of
\Cr{error}
in terms of the number field
K,f
and the integer constants
k,m,n
.
However, a so inclined reader should be able to chase through our constants
c_{1},c_{2},\dots
to understand the dependence on these parameters.
We have made little effort to optimize this dependence, and it seems unlikely that the best route to optimize
\Cr{error}
is through this combinatorial approach.

2.
Preliminaries and Notations

2.1.
Lattices

Definition 5
.

We define a lattice in an Euclidean space
V
to be a discrete
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\Lambda
. A lattice has finite covolume in
V
if

\vol(V/\Lambda)<\infty
.

Remark 6
.

Given any ambient Euclidean space, a lattice
\Lambda\subseteq V
has finite covolume in
\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 7
.

For any Euclidean space
V
and for any discrete
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\Lambda\subseteq V
, we define the height
of
\Lambda
by

(28)

H(\Lambda)=\vol(\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{R}/\Lambda)

taken with respect to the restriction of the norm to
\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{R}\subseteq V
.

Definition 8
.

Let
\Lambda\subseteq V
be a lattice and let
\Lambda^{\prime}\subseteq\Lambda
be a sublattice.
We call the lattice
\Lambda
-primitive if
(\Lambda^{\prime}\otimes\mathbb{Q})\cap\Lambda=\Lambda^{\prime}
.

Most of the time, we will skip mentioning
\Lambda
when the context is clear.

2.2.
Covering radius and Voronoi domain

Definition 9
.

For a lattice
\Lambda\subseteq V
in Euclidean space
V
, we denote by
\rho(\Lambda)
the covering radius of
\Lambda
defined as

(29)

\rho(\Lambda)=\max_{x\in{\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{R}}}\min_{v\in\Lambda}\|x-v\|.

Definition 10
.

Given a lattice
\Lambda\subseteq V
in an Euclidean space
V
, one defines a Voronoi domain
F\subseteq\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{R}
as

(30)

F=\{x\in\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{R}\mid\|x\|\leq\|x+v\|\text{ for all }v\in\Lambda\}.

One has the following properties of the Voronoi domain.

Lemma 11
.

(1)

We have
F+\Lambda=\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{R}
,

(2)

We have
\vol(F)=H(\Lambda)
,

(3)

One has
F\subseteq B_{0}(\rho(\Lambda))
, where
B_{0}(\rho(\Lambda))
is the ball of radius
\rho(\Lambda)
and center at
0
.

Proof.

Standard facts.
∎

Lemma 12
.

For a lattice
\Lambda
in an Euclidean space
V
, one has that for any
T>0

(31)

\card\{v\in\Lambda\mid\|v\|\leq T\}\leq(T+\rho(\Lambda))^{r}H(\Lambda)^{-1},

where
r=\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}\Lambda
and
\Cr{ballvol}
is a constant depending only on
r
.

Proof.

This is a volume argument. We take a Voronoi domain
F\subseteq\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{R}
. Then the set

(32)

F+\{v\in\Lambda\mid\|v\|\leq T\}\subseteq\{v\in\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{R}\mid\|v\|\leq T+\rho(\Lambda)\}.

∎

For the purpose of this article, we will assume that
\Cr{ballvol}>0
is large enough to work for all
r\leq dn^{3}
necessary for our purposes.

2.3.
Minkowski and Hadamard

The following is an important lemma due to Minkowski.

Lemma 13
.

Let
\Lambda\subseteq V
be a lattice in an Euclidean space
V
whose
\mathbb{Z}
-rank is
r
.
Then, for any non-zero vector
v\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}
, we have

(33)

\|v\|\leq H(\Lambda)^{\tfrac{1}{r}}

for some
\Cr{hermite}>0
depending on
\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}\Lambda
.

Although the constant
\Cr{hermite}
depends on the
\mathbb{Z}
-rank of
\Lambda
, by taking maxima over all possible
\Cr{hermite}
for
r\leq(nmd)^{2}
, one can assume
\Cr{hermite}
to not depend on
r
.

We also have the following important result that tells us that the Hadamard ratio is bounded from below.

Definition 14
.

Given a lattice
\Lambda\subseteq V
in an Euclidean space
V
with a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
v_{1},\dots,v_{r}
. Then, the following quantity is called the Hadamard ratio of the basis
v_{1},\dots,v_{r}
.

(34)

\frac{\|v_{1}\|\|v_{2}\|\dots\|v_{r}\|}{H(\Lambda)}.

If a basis
v_{1},\dots,v_{r}
is an orthogonal basis, then it is clear that
\|v_{1}\|\dots\|v_{r}\|=H(\Lambda)
and the Hadamard ratio is 1. In general, the non-orthogonality of the basis leads to the following.

Lemma 15
.

Consider the same setup as
Definition

14
. Then,

(35)

\frac{\|v_{1}\|\|v_{2}\|\dots\|v_{r}\|}{H(\Lambda)}\geq 1.

That is, the Hadamard ratio of a lattice is at least 1.

We leave the proof of
Lemma

15
for the reader.

2.4.
Hypothesis on test functions

The functions that are of interest in this theory are compactly supported continuous functions and functions that are indicators of sets with nice boundaries. The following class of functions contains both of these cases.

Hypothesis 16
.

We call a test function

f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

“admissible” if it is a
compactly supported measurable function such that
the error function

(36)

E_{f}(x,\varepsilon)=\sup_{\|x-y\|\leq\varepsilon}|f(x)-f(y)|

satisfies for some
=\Cr{admis}(f)>0
, for every
\varepsilon>0

and for any non-zero real subspace
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}

(37)

\int_{V}E_{f}(x,\varepsilon)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\Cr{admis}\cdot\varepsilon,

The integration is happening with the induced Lebesgue
measure from the inclusion
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\Cr{admis}>0
is required to be independent of
V
.

A consequence of the above hypothesis is the following estimate for Riemann sums.

Lemma 17
.

Let
V
be an Euclidean space and
\Lambda\subseteq V
be a lattice such that
\dim\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{R}=n
.
Let
f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be an admissible test function, in the sense of
16
.

(38)

\Big|\tfrac{1}{T^{n}}\sum_{v\in\Lambda}f(\tfrac{1}{T}v)-\tfrac{1}{H(\Lambda)}\int_{\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{R}}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\Big|\leq\frac{\Cr{admis}\cdot\rho(\Lambda)}{H(\Lambda)\cdot T},

2.6.

Throughout this paper, we assume

to be a number field of signature
(r_{1},r_{2})
, so that
r_{1}+2r_{2}=\deg K=d
.
We fix, for once and for all, the following

-norm on

\|x\|^{2}=|\Delta_{K}|^{-\frac{2}{d}}\Tr(x\overline{x}),

is the discriminant of the number field. The involution
\overline{(\ )}
in (

on all the complex places of

\vol(K_{\mathbb{R}}/\mathcal{O}_{K})<\infty
.
Our quadratic form in
Eq.

46
is engineered to ensure that the lattice

has unit covolume for any
r\geq 1
.

We will fix a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
for once and for all. Most of our implicit constants will depend on the choice of this basis. Here is a lemma demonstrating how this basis affects the underlying constants.

.

Let

be a free
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module of rank

, that is

\mathbb{Z}
-basis

and

\|v_{i}\|\leq\|w_{ij}\|\leq\|v_{i}\|,

and
\Cr{okr}

the
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathcal{O}_{K}

, nor on
\Lambda

and

.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}u_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}u_{d}

\mathbb{Z}
-basis.
Each

The following sets are in bijection with each other.

(2)

Note that the definition of
\Lambda_{D}
in

forces that
\Lambda_{D}
is primitive.
The assignment

is a bijection again since the echelon matrix

as described above.

3.2.
Schmidt’s theorem

The following is a result of W. Schmidt
[
16
]
:

Theorem 22
.

For
T\geq 1
, one has

(52)

T^{m}\leq\card\{V\in\Gr(k,K^{m})\mid H(V)\leq T\}\leq T^{m},

for some constants
\Cr{schl},\Cr{schr}
that depend on
K,k,m
.

In fact, more precise asymptotics were established by J. Thunder
[
17
]
, but we will not require those.
The main point for us is that, using the trijection of
Proposition

21
, there are finitely many echelon matrices
D\in\M_{k\times m}(K)
satisfying
H(D)\leq T
.

A corollary of
Theorem

22
is the following lemma, which is also given in
[
1
, Corollary 17]
but which we repeat here for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 23
.

The constant
\Cr{main}
defined in
Eq.

11
is finite for any admissible function
f:\M_{n\times m}(K_{\mathbb{R}})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

This follows from the claim that for any echelon matrix
D\in\M_{k\times m}(K)
, one has

(53)

\mathfrak{D}(D)^{-n}\int_{\M_{n\times k}(K_{\mathbb{R}})}f(xD)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq H(D)^{-n},

for some constant
\Cr{height}
depending on
f
. Once we have established this claim, we note that
n>m
and
Theorem

22
are sufficient to prove that the right-side of
Eq.

53
is finitely summable over all echelon matrices in
\M_{k\times m}(K)
.

Indeed, there is a relation between
\mathfrak{D}(D)
and
H(D)
. The product of the “Jacobian” of the map
x\rightarrow xD
for

times the factor
\mathfrak{D}(D)
is exactly
H(D)
.
This implies in particular that for any admissible function

one gets (cf. Appendix A of
[
1
]
)

(54)

\frac{1}{\mathfrak{D}(D)^{n}}\int_{\M_{n\times k}(K_{\mathbb{R}})}f(xD)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\M_{n}(\Lambda_{D}\otimes\mathbb{R})}f(x){\,\mathrm{d}_{D}}x,

where

is a Lebesgue measure on
\M_{n}(\Lambda_{D}\otimes\mathbb{R})
such that
\M_{n}(\Lambda_{D})\subseteq\M_{n}(\Lambda_{D}\otimes\mathbb{R})

has unit covolume.
See
[
1
, §3]
for details. The measure
\,\mathrm{d}_{D}x
can then be expressed in terms of the induced Lebesgue measure
\,\mathrm{d}_{l}x
on
\M_{n}(\Lambda_{D}\otimes\mathbb{R})\subseteq\M_{n\times m}(K_{\mathbb{R}})
via the relation
\,\mathrm{d}_{D}x=H(D)^{-n}\,\mathrm{d}_{l}x
.
We pick
\Cr{height}
by setting

(55)

\Cr{height}=\max_{\text{ Subspace }V\subseteq\M_{n\times m}(K_{\mathbb{R}})}\int_{V}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}_{l}x.

\|l_{1}\|^{d}\dots\|l_{k}\|^{d}\leq\Cr{okhadamard}H(D)
,
one gets that for
=\Cr{covering}/\Cr{okhadamard}
one has

(133)

\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(T)}\frac{\rho(\Lambda_{D})}{H(D)^{n}}\leq\Cr{innervar}\sum_{(l_{1},\dots,l_{k})\in\mathcal{B}_{k}(T)}\frac{1}{\|l_{1}\|^{nd}\dots\|l_{k}\|^{nd-1}}.

We now have two cases.

When
n-m+k-1>1
or
d>1
:

In this case, in the terminology of
Lemma

36
, we know that

(134)

S(T;nd,nd,\dots,nd-1)\leq\Cr{absbound}(1+T^{-kd(n-m)+1}).

Hence, unless
k=1,d=1
and
n-m=1
, one has the desired statement.

When
n=m+1
,
k=1
and
d=1
:

In this case, we are looking at the sum

(135)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\in\M_{n\times m}(\mathbb{Z})\\
\rank A=1\end{subarray}}f(\tfrac{1}{T}A)=\sum_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{prim}}.\sum_{w\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}f(\tfrac{1}{T}wv^{T}).

This has been discussed in
Section

1.3
.
∎

5.
Lifts of codes

Our goal in this section is to prove the discretized integral formula in
Theorem

41
using
Eq.

6
.

Let
g:K_{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a test function satisfying
16
.
For any integer
1\leq s\leq n
and a prime ideal
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}
, denote

(136)

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P},s)=\{\tfrac{1}{T_{\mathcal{P}}}\Lambda\mid\mathcal{P}^{n}\subseteq\Lambda\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}^{n},\Lambda/\mathcal{P}^{n}\in\Gr(s,(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathcal{P})^{n})\},

where after setting

(137)

T_{\mathcal{P}}=\N(\mathcal{P})^{\left(1-\frac{s}{n}\right)\frac{1}{d}},

we get that all the lattices in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P},s)
to have the same covolume as
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{n}\subseteq K_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Now we begin considering our object of interest: the average of lattice sum functions. Observe that

(138)

\frac{1}{\card\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P},s)}\sum_{\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P},s)}\left(\sum_{v\in\Lambda}g(v)\right)^{m}=\frac{1}{\card\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P},s)}\sum_{\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P},s)}\left(\sum_{v\in\Lambda^{m}}f(v)\right),

where
f(v_{1},\dots,v_{m})=g(v_{1})g(v_{2})\dots g(v_{m})
.
We perform some manipulations on this sum. Letting
\ind(P)
denote the indicator function of a proposition
P
, we have that

(139)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\card\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P},s)}\sum_{\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P},s)}\left(\sum_{v\in\Lambda^{m}}f(v)\right)

(140)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{n\times m}}f(\tfrac{1}{T_{\mathcal{P}}}x)\left(\frac{1}{\card\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P},s)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq k_{\mathcal{P}}^{n}\\
S\simeq k_{\mathcal{P}}^{s}\end{subarray}}\ind\left(\spant(\pi_{\mathcal{P}}(x_{1}),\dots,\pi_{\mathcal{P}}(x_{m}))\subseteq S\right)\right),

where
k_{\mathcal{P}}=\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathcal{P}
and
\pi_{\mathcal{P}}:\mathcal{O}_{K}^{n}\rightarrow k_{\mathcal{P}}^{n}
is the “reduction modulo
\mathcal{P}
” map.

The inner sum is just the probability of a random subspace
S\subseteq k_{\mathcal{P}}^{n}
of fixed dimension
s
containing some given set of points
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{m}\in k_{\mathcal{P}}^{n}
. This probability, other than depending on
\mathcal{P},s
, depends only on the
k_{\mathcal{P}}
-dimension of the subspace generated by
\pi_{\mathcal{P}}(x_{1}),\dots,\pi_{\mathcal{P}}(x_{m})
.
This dimension equals the rank of
\pi_{\mathcal{P}}(x)\in\M_{n\times m}(k_{\mathcal{P}})

which is certainly less than the rank of

x\in\M_{n\times m}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\subseteq\M_{n\times m}(K)
. So we can split our sum into

(141)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\min(n,m)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\M_{n\times m}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\\
\rank(x)=k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(\tfrac{1}{T_{\mathcal{P}}}x)}{\card\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P},s)}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq k_{\mathcal{P}}^{n}\\
S\simeq k_{\mathcal{P}}^{s}\end{subarray}}\ind\left(\spant(\pi_{\mathcal{P}}(x_{1}),\dots,\pi_{\mathcal{P}}(x_{m}))\subseteq S\right)\right).

Given
x\in\M_{n\times m}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
, we might for some
\mathcal{P}
encounter a “rank-drop” phenomenon, that is
\rank\left(\pi_{\mathcal{P}}(x)\right)<\rank(x)
. However, the good news is that the matrices
x
where this rank-drop happens can be “pushed away” from the support of
f
, as the following lemma shows.

Lemma 39
.

Suppose that
x\in M_{n\times m}\left(\mathcal{O}_{K}\right)
is a matrix with
\rank(x)=k\geq 1
and
\mathcal{P}
is a prime ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that
\rank(\pi_{\mathcal{P}}(x))<k
.
Then, for any Euclidean norm
\|\cdot\|:M_{n\times m}(K_{\mathbb{R}})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, there exists some
>0

depending on
K,\|\cdot\|,n,m
and independent of
k
and
\mathcal{P}
such that
x
must satisfy

(142)

\|x\|\geq\Cr{push}\N(\mathcal{P})^{\frac{1}{k[K:\mathbb{Q}]}}

Proof.

By choosing a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, we can embed
\iota:\mathcal{O}_{K}\hookrightarrow M_{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}(\mathbb{Z})
as a subring of the square integer matrices of size
[K:\mathbb{Q}]
.
Without loss of generality, we assume that the norm
\|\cdot\|
is the Euclidean norm via the embedding

\iota:M_{n\times m}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\hookrightarrow M_{n[K:\mathbb{Q}]\times m[K:\mathbb{Q}]}(\mathbb{Z})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{nm[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}}.

Since
\rank(x)=k
, we know that there exists a non-singular
k\times k
minor
a\in\M_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{K})

appearing as a submatrix in
x
.
It is clear that
0\neq\det a\in\mathcal{P}
otherwise there is no rank-drop modulo
\mathcal{P}
. Therefore, we get that

\N(\mathcal{P})\mid\N(\det a).

Since we know that
0\neq|\det(\iota(a))|\geq\N(\mathcal{P})
, at least one non-zero integer appearing in the matrix entries of
\iota(a)
would have absolute value
\geq\N(\mathcal{P})^{\frac{1}{k[K:\mathbb{Q}]}}
for some
\Cr{push2}>0
independent of
\mathcal{P}
. This produces the same lower bound on the Euclidean norm of
\iota(a)
up to a constant, and similarly also for
\iota(x)
.
∎

Lemma 40
.

Suppose
y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}\in k_{\mathcal{P}}^{n}
are linearly independent vectors (over
k_{\mathcal{P}}
). Then the following holds:

(143)

\frac{1}{\card\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P},s)}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq k_{\mathcal{P}}^{n}\\
S\simeq k_{\mathcal{P}}^{s}\end{subarray}}\ind\left(\spant(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k})\subseteq S\right)\right)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $s<k$}\\
\N(\mathcal{P})^{-k(n-s)}\cdot(1+\varepsilon_{1})&\text{ if $s\geq k$.}\end{cases}

where the error term
|\varepsilon_{1}|\leq\N(\mathcal{P})^{-1}
for some
\Cr{normerror}>0
not depending on
\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

The case with
s<k
is clear.
In general for a finite field of size
q
, the number of
u
-dimensional subspaces in a
t
-dimensional vector space is the cardinality of the Grassmannian
\Gr(u,\mathbb{F}_{q}^{t})
given by a polynomial in
q
. The leading terms of this polynomial are

(144)

\displaystyle\frac{(q^{t}-1)(q^{t}-q)\cdots(q^{t}-q^{u-1})}{(q^{u}-1)(q^{u}-q)\cdots(q^{u}-q^{u-1})}

\displaystyle=q^{u(t-u)}+q^{u(t-u)-1}+\dots,

(145)

\displaystyle=q^{u(t-u)}(1+\varepsilon_{1}),

where
\varepsilon_{1}
is an error term as given in the statement.
In our case,
q=\card k_{\mathcal{P}}=\N(\mathcal{P})
.

Up to change of variables, the numerator counts the number of
(s-k)
-dimensional subspaces in a
(n-k)
-dimensional space and therefore has cardinality
\card\Gr(s-k,\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n-k})
.
This is sufficient to get our result.
∎

Theorem 41
.

Take
n\geq 2
,
m\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
and choose
s
as either
n-1
, or any number in
\{m,m+1,\dots,n-1\}
that satisfies

(146)

1-\frac{s}{n}<\frac{1}{m}.

Let
f:K_{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function satisfying
16
.
With
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P},s)
defined as in
Eq.

136
,
we have that as
\N(\mathcal{P})\rightarrow\infty

(147)

\frac{1}{\card\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P},s)}\sum_{\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P},s)}\left(\sum_{v\in\Lambda^{m}}f(v)\right)\rightarrow\sum_{k=0}^{{m}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\in\M_{k\times m}(K)\\
\rank(D)=k\\
D\text{ row reduced echelon}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{D}(D)^{-n}\int_{x\in K_{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times k}}f(xD)dx,

where
\mathfrak{D}(D)
is
as defined in
Eq.

10
. Here, the term at
k=0
is just
f(0)
.

Proof.

From the discussion above, we arrive at
Eq.

141
, and it remains to consider

(148)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\M_{n\times m}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\\
\rank(x)=k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(\tfrac{1}{T_{\mathcal{P}}}x)}{\card\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P},s)}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq k_{\mathcal{P}}^{n}\\
S\simeq k_{\mathcal{P}}^{s}\end{subarray}}\ind\left(\spant(\pi_{\mathcal{P}}(x_{1}),\dots,\pi_{\mathcal{P}}(x_{m}))\subseteq S\right)\right).

Note that here
T_{\mathcal{P}}=\N(\mathcal{P})^{\left(1-\frac{s}{n}\right)\frac{1}{d}}
. The rank
k
ranges within
\{0,1,\dots,m\}
since
\min(n,m)=m
. Also, since
s\geq m
, we expect the quantity in parentheses to be nonzero from
Lemma

40
.

We recall that
\M_{n\times m}(K_{\mathbb{R}})
has the Euclidean measure given by
n\cdot m
copies of the quadratic form coming from (
46
).
When
k>1
, we know from Lemma
142
that we will encounter a rank-drop mod
\mathcal{P}
only if for some predetermined constant
\Cr{push}>0

(149)

\displaystyle\|x\|\geq\Cr{push}\N(\mathcal{P})^{\frac{1}{kd}}

(150)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\|\tfrac{1}{T_{\mathcal{P}}}x\|\geq\Cr{push}\N(\mathcal{P})^{\frac{1}{d}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{k}-\left(1-\frac{s}{n}\right)\right)}.

Since

(151)

\frac{1}{k}-\left(1-\frac{s}{n}\right)\geq\frac{1}{m}-\left(1-\frac{s}{n}\right)>0,

for a large enough value of
\N(\mathcal{P})
we have that all the matrices of

x\in\M_{n\times m}\left(\mathcal{O}_{K}\right)
where rank-drop could happen are outside the support of
f
.
Let us assume that
\N(\mathcal{P})
is large enough for this to hold.
Hence whenever
f(\tfrac{1}{T_{\mathcal{P}}}x)
is non-zero, the span of
\pi_{\mathcal{P}}(x_{1}),\pi_{\mathcal{P}}(x_{2}),\dots,\pi_{\mathcal{P}}(x_{m})
is of the same
k_{\mathcal{P}}
-dimension as the rank of
x
.
Using Lemma
40
, we can rewrite our sum as

(152)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\M_{n\times m}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\\
\rank(x)=k\end{subarray}}\frac{f(\tfrac{1}{T_{\mathcal{P}}}x)}{\N(\mathcal{P})^{k(n-s)}}=(1+\varepsilon_{2})\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\M_{n\times m}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\\
\rank(x)=k\end{subarray}}{f(\tfrac{1}{T_{\mathcal{P}}}x)}\frac{1}{T_{\mathcal{P}}^{knd}},

where
|\varepsilon_{2}|\leq\N(\mathcal{P})^{-1}
for some
\Cr{grassmannian}>0
that does not depend on
\mathcal{P}
.

The result follows as
T_{\mathcal{P}}\rightarrow\infty
due to
\N(\mathcal{P})\rightarrow\infty
using
Eq.

6
and
Eq.

11
.
∎

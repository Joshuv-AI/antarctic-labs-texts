Raising the bar: The EBOT-TK Turkish Oral Exam milestone and AOTT’s commitment to orthopaedic education
Authors: Merter Yalçınkaya, Kaya Akan, Önder Aydıngöz, Haluk Berk, Mehmet Demirhan, Jorge Mineiro, Enrique Gómez-Barrena
Published: 2025

The inaugural European Board of Orthopaedics and Traumatology Turkish (EBOT-TK) Oral Examination, conducted for the first time in Türkiye on 25th and 26th of October 2025, represents a defining moment for the Turkish orthopaedic community. For the first time, oral part of the European-level competency-based assessment—developed and organized jointly by European Union of Medical Specialists (UEMS), EBOT and European Federation of National Associations of Orthopaedics and Traumatology (EFORT) and refined over decades1,2—has been delivered in the Turkish language while fully adhering to EBOT examination standards. This milestone not only symbolizes Türkiye’s alignment with European educational frameworks but also reflects the maturation of the national training ecosystem and its readiness to operate within a harmonized international structure. EBOT-TK Oral Exam stands as more than a new examination format for the country; it represents a cultural shift toward transparency, standardization, and shared educational values across borders.

The EBOT oral exam has been conducted internationally in English since 2001.1 Its structure and content were strengthened considerably following the 2015 publication of the European Curriculum in Orthopaedics and Trauma,3 developed under the combined efforts of EFORT and UEMS, known as European Education Platform. This curriculum—widely accepted across Europe and regarded as the document defining the minimum standards required for orthopaedic education—outlines the professional behaviors, knowledge, and procedural skills expected of orthopaedic specialists. Following its release, the EBOT exam was aligned with this curriculum, establishing a shared benchmark for competency-based assessment throughout Europe.

Subsequently, a major initiative emerged to expand the accessibility of EBOT oral exams by allowing countries to conduct the exam in their native languages, provided that EBOT rules and scoring criteria were strictly maintained. This decision was motivated by several considerations; such as to mitigate the impact of language barriers on candidates whose native language is not English, to facilitate broader dissemination of standardized assessment principles across Europe, and to reduce the logistical and financial burden of travel and accommodation for candidates and examiners. Spain became the first country to implement this model in 2016 with EBOT Spanish oral exam, followed by Belgium in 2023 with a French-language version. Passing rates in both countries remained closely aligned with those of the English-language international EBOT examinations—typically in between 60–70%—demonstrating that consistent application of EBOT examination principles yields comparable outcomes regardless of exam language.

Following the joint consensus reached by Turkish Society of Orthopaedics and Traumatology (TOTBİD), Turkish Orthopaedics and Traumatology Education Council (TOTEK), the UEMS Specialist Section of Orthopaedics and Traumatology, and the EBOT Executive Board during the UEMS Specialist Section meeting held in İstanbul in 1st and 2nd of November 2024, the integration of the TOTEK Examination—successfully conducted since 2003—with the EBOT Examination has been officially realized. This milestone was strongly supported by EFORT’s central role in European orthopaedic education and assessment, including its direct and active role in the EBOT examinations, as well as by TOTBİD’s sustained engagement within EFORT. In this context, the presence of Dr. Önder Aydıngöz serving actively on the EBOT Executive Board, his role as EFORT Past President, and the current service of Dr. Mehmet Demirhan as an EFORT Board Member-at-Large merit particular recognition. Equally important were TOTBİD’s academic activities and its consistent participation in UEMS as an associate member, together with the active Turkish delegates within the UEMS Specialist Section of Orthopaedics and Traumatology. Finally, the active and substantial role of Turkish examiners in EBOT examinations (in alphabetical order by surname; Dr. Yunus Emre Akman, Dr. Sercan Akpınar, Dr. Önder Aydıngöz, Dr. Haluk Berk, Dr. Mehmet Demirhan, Dr. Emel Gönen, Dr. Mustafa Karahan, Dr. Nusret Köse, Dr. Salih Marangoz, Dr. Hakan Ömeroğlu, Dr. Sami Sökücü, Dr. Merter Yalçınkaya, and Dr. Muharrem Yazıcı), with Türkiye consistently being among the countries providing the highest number of examiners, as well as the numerous requests submitted over the years to host the EBOT Examination in Türkiye, further reinforced this process.

In the months immediately following this approval, it is truly extraordinary to see how much has been accomplished. Preparation began with secretarial training, as two members of the TOTBİD Office, who would later assume a central role in the EBOT-TK Oral Examination secretariat, were officially assigned by TOTBİD to the international EBOT Oral Exam held in Lisbon, Portugal, on 5th and 6th of October 2024. Their active involvement in the examination setting ensured hands-on training and practical familiarity with examination procedures. This was followed by an examiners’ course held in İstanbul on 11th of January 2025, personally delivered by the EBOT Exam Chair Dr. Enrique Gómez-Barrena and former Chair Dr. Jorge Mineiro. The preparation process then continued with a two-week online training program at the end of May 2025 and an in-person workshop conducted in Eskişehir on 5th and 6th of July 2025.

These structured educational activities ensured that all examiners—regardless of prior experience—were aligned with the EBOT competency framework, scoring matrices, questioning strategies, and standardized assessment guidelines. In the subsequent months, the preparation phase encompassed the completion of all EBOT-TK documentation and venue planning, the training of examiners and secretaries, and the numerous online meetings, correspondence, and coordination efforts that ensured consistent alignment and support for all examiners. Following the examiner briefing meeting on 24th of October 2025, the EBOT-TK Oral Exam was conducted in Ankara over two days – on 25th and 26th of October 2025 – with a team consisting of fifty examiners (operating at twenty-five stations), two supervisors (Dr. Mehmet Demirhan and Dr. Haluk Berk), a representative of the Turkish Medical Association, seven observer examiners, and five examination secretaries. Regardless of the outcomes, every colleague who voluntarily participated in this examination deserves recognition for their professionalism and dedication, and for their contribution to raising the standards of assessment and evaluation in Turkish orthopaedic and traumatology education. As a result, Türkiye became the third country in Europe to adopt this model. Among the forty-two candidates who entered the EBOT-TK Oral Exam after passing the EBOT English written exam, 62% successfully earned both the EBOT Diploma and the TOTEK Certificate—an outcome that aligns closely with international benchmarks and further validates the sustainability and reliability of the Turkish-language implementation.

While the preparation process for the EBOT-TK Oral Exam has been summarized above, it should be emphasized that TOTEK’s work over more than two decades formed the very foundation of this preparation. TOTEK, established in 2001 under TOTBİD, with the leadership of Dr. Mehmet Demirhan, has developed a strong institutional structure encompassing institutional visits and accreditation processes, recertification programs, and a broad range of educational activities. Its educational portfolio includes the Basic Sciences and Research School, educational workshops, interim examinations for residents and regularly organized courses. Additionally, for more than two decades, TOTEK has conducted Turkish Board Examination of Orthopaedics and Traumatology consisting of written and oral parts on a regular basis, applying a meticulous, well-structured, and fully standardized assessment framework. Such long-standing experience has endowed TOTEK with a high level of examination know-how and organizational competence. Taken together, this robust institutional foundation and extensive examination expertise significantly facilitated the transition to a highly detailed and technically demanding examination format such as the EBOT-TK Oral Examination.

At this stage, it would also be appropriate to view the matter from the perspective of our journal, Acta Orthopaedica et Traumatologica Turcica (AOTT). In total, fifty-three individuals served as chair (Dr. Merter Yalçınkaya), supervisors, or examiners for EBOT-TK Oral Exam. Among them were the current Editor-in-chief of AOTT Dr. Haluk Berk, the former President of TOTBID, founding TOTEK Chair and former Editor-in-chief of AOTT Dr. Mehmet Demirhan, as well as nineteen active AOTT associate editors. The AOTT editorial structure currently includes one editor-in-chief, two deputy editors, and forty-nine associate editors, and twenty members of the editorial team contributed to the inaugural EBOT-TK Oral Examination. Additionally, current EBOT Executive Board president Dr. Enrique Gómez-Barrena serves as an associate editor of AOTT, underscoring the journal’s role as an internationally connected educational platform. An additional thirteen reserve examiners, ready to serve if needed, were composed entirely of current or former AOTT associate editors. This level of editorial representation is unprecedented and reflects the depth of expertise and educational commitment embedded in the AOTT community.

AOTT’s contribution to the examination landscape extends well beyond the participation of its editorial members as examiners. In recent years, we have increasingly recognized that many of the studies published in the journal carry direct educational relevance to the European Curriculum in Orthopaedics and Traumatology. Through high-quality work spanning basic science,4-6 pediatric orthopaedics,7-9 lower10-12 and upper extremity surgery,13-15 and spine,16-19 AOTT has offered its readership a breadth of knowledge that is both comprehensive and integrative, with these references representing only a small sample of the journal’s wider educational contributions. Although serving as a formal examination resource has never been among the journal’s primary objectives, the scientific clarity and instructional depth of these articles have, in practice, enabled many readers to approach the EBOT and EBOT-TK examinations with a stronger conceptual foundation. In this sense, AOTT has continued to affirm its commitment to education while contributing meaningfully to the broader orthopaedic community. For all these contributions, authored by resolute researchers, reviewed by expert referees, and curated by associate and senior editors, we owe a deep debt of gratitude. AOTT will continue to serve the EBOT written and oral examinations and the EBOT-TK Oral Examination by providing high-quality, curriculum-aligned scientific content for years to come.

Behind the scenes of EBOT-TK oral exam lies a year of extraordinary commitment. TOTBİD Administrative and Financial Affairs Director, Ms. Nermin Ünal, together with all TOTBİD Office administrative staff, particularly Ms. Selin Sümer and Mr. Emre Aktuna, worked tirelessly to ensure that every detail met international standards. Their dedication, professionalism, and willingness to go well beyond routine responsibilities made this historic achievement possible. We owe them our deepest respect and gratitude. We also extend our sincere appreciation to all who have served—and continue to serve—on the Boards of TOTBİD, TOTEK, EFORT, and EBOT; to EBOT Exam Chair Dr. Enrique Gómez-Barrena and former Chair Dr. Jorge Mineiro; to all colleagues who have represented Türkiye with distinction within European federations, associations, and editorial boards; to all examiners who contributed to this process; and to every colleague who has unconditionally supported medical education and TOTEK examinations over the years.

The launch of EBOT-TK Oral Exam marks not only a new era of assessment in Türkiye but also a reaffirmation of our collective commitment to excellence in education of orthopaedics and traumatology. AOTT—through its editorial leadership, scientific mission, and the deep engagement of its editorial team—stands at the heart of this transformation. With shared values, shared standards, and shared purpose, we will continue shaping the future of orthopaedic education for generations to come.

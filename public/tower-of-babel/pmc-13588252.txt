Long-Term Safety and Effectiveness of STREAMLINE Canaloplasty with Phacoemulsification in a Hispanic Population with Open-Angle Glaucoma
Authors: Gabriel Lazcano-Gomez, Claudio Orlich, Elysia Ison, Malik Y Kahook
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo report long-term clinical outcomes from a multicenter study evaluating STREAMLINE® Surgical System (STREAMLINE) canaloplasty, a minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS), combined with phacoemulsification in eyes with mild to moderate open-angle glaucoma (OAG).DesignRetrospective extension of a prospective, multicenter, interventional study.ParticipantsParticipants were those in the original study who provided consent for retrospective long-term data collection.MethodsAll eyes in this single-arm, retrospective extension study underwent STREAMLINE canaloplasty performed in combination with phacoemulsification between November 2021 and April 2022 at two sites in Latin America.Main Outcome MeasuresThe primary effectiveness outcome was the reduction in mean intraocular pressure (IOP) from Screening to last follow-up. Secondary effectiveness outcomes included mean change in topical IOP-lowering medication count from Screening compared to last follow-up. Safety assessments included visual acuity, visual field mean deviation, and incidence of adverse events (AEs).ResultsParticipants (n=28) at 2 of the 3 sites in the original study (n=43, 65.1%) consented to have retrospective data collected as part of this extension study. Average length of follow-up was 24.2±4.4 months (M24). Mean IOP (17.1±3.0 mmHg) and IOP-lowering medication count (1.86±0.80) at Screening decreased to 15.13±3.25 mmHg (p=0.002) and 0.54±1.17 (p<0.001), respectively. At M24, 92.9% of eyes were on the same or fewer medications compared to screening and 78.6% were medication free. No AEs were reported in the extension study (M12 to last follow-up).ConclusionsCanaloplasty with the STREAMLINE® Surgical System in combination with phacoemulsification provides clinically and statistically significant reduction in both IOP and the need for IOP-lowering medications in eyes with mild-moderate OAG in a Hispanic adult population through an average of 24 months post-operatively.

Introduction

Intraocular pressure (IOP) remains the primary and well-established modifiable risk factor for glaucoma.1 Although topical IOP-lowering medications are the standard first-line treatment, their efficacy is often compromised by poor patient adherence.2,3 Because inadequate IOP control is directly linked to accelerated visual field loss,4 minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS) has emerged as a viable early intervention. MIGS reduces the burden of daily topical therapy while maintaining a favorable safety profile comparable to standalone cataract surgery. Current options include trabecular meshwork (TM) bypass stents and implant-free procedures such as canaloplasty and goniotomy. Notably, these procedures are frequently performed alongside cataract surgery, as approximately 19% of cataract patients also present with concomitant glaucoma.5

The STREAMLINE® Surgical System (STREAMLINE, New World Medical, Rancho Cucamonga, CA) is a MIGS device that has been marketed in the United States since 2021.6 This single-use disposable instrument injects pressurized ophthalmic viscoelastic device (OVD) to viscodilate the canal, stretch the TM, and flush the collector channels. The OVD reservoir of the device holds enough volume to perform up to 8 applications, each injecting ~7 µL of OVD, spaced along multiple clock hours across the canal of Schlemm.7 STREAMLINE canaloplasty can be performed as a standalone procedure or in combination with cataract surgery.

There have been several prospective and retrospective studies that illustrate the safety and efficacy of STREAMLINE canaloplasty in combination with cataract surgery in eyes with glaucoma for the reduction of IOP and/or reduction of IOP-lowering medications for up to 12 months.7–11

The original Multi-Site STREAMLINE (MSS) study was a prospective, multicenter, interventional trial that evaluated STREAMLINE canaloplasty with phacoemulsification outcomes in eyes of Hispanic patients with mild to moderate open-angle glaucoma (OAG) in Mexico, Costa Rica, and the Dominican Republic over 12 months.7 The Multi-Site STREAMLINE Extension (MSSX) study is a single-arm, retrospective extension of the original MSS study, reporting outcomes based on available clinical data through approximately 24 months.

Methods

To be eligible for the MSSX study, participants must have been enrolled and treated in the original study without being lost to follow-up before Month 12 (M12). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, and the protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Costa Rica (Instituto Costarricense de Investigaciones Clinical Comite Etico Cientifico; reference number: CEC-HCB-E013-2024) and Mexico (Comité de Ética en Investigación Hospital La Misión S.A. de C.V.) prior to the start of this retrospective study. In compliance with local regulatory requirements, informed consent was also obtained.

Original study subjects were at least 22 years of age and had mild to moderate OAG and visually significant cataract, on 1 to 3 topical IOP-lowering medications at screening. In the study eye, they had no concomitant ocular or medical conditions that would interfere with the assessment of safety and efficacy of the STREAMLINE canaloplasty procedure. Full inclusion and exclusion criteria of the original cohort have been published previously.7 After screening, eyes underwent a washout of IOP-lowering medications and were eligible for treatment if IOP was 21–36 mmHg inclusive. After surgery, eyes were followed on Day 1, Week 1, and Months 1, 3, 6, 9, and 12.

Data were collected retrospectively beyond the last MSS study visit (M12) to assess the long-term safety and IOP-lowering effectiveness after STREAMLINE canaloplasty with phacoemulsification. Retrospective data collected included ophthalmic exam findings, IOP, IOP-lowering medication use, visual acuity, and visual field results, as available. Investigators reviewed these exam findings and assessed causality, severity, and whether any would be categorized as an Adverse Event (AE).

Procedure

In the original study, all eligible eyes underwent comprehensive screening examinations preoperatively. While STEAMLINE can be used for both canaloplasty and goniotomy, only canaloplasty was performed in all eyes after successful cataract surgery as previously described.7

Outcome Measures

The primary outcome of MSSX study was the reduction of IOP from screening to the last follow-up, the change in IOP-lowering medications from screening to the last follow-up, the number of eyes on the same or fewer medication classes, and the number of eyes that were medication free at the last follow-up. Secondary outcomes included postoperative AEs after exiting the prospective study, visual acuity, and visual field findings from screening to the last follow-up visit.

Statistical Analysis

Only subjects who provided informed consent to MSSX study and had follow-up data available were included in the analyses. No specific hypotheses are being evaluated in the analysis of the extension data. Statistical significance testing was performed using a paired t-Test, and a Wilcoxon signed rank test was used as applicable. Additional post-hoc analyses were performed for IOP and medication reduction for Months 3–24 using repeated-measures ANOVA or the non-parametric Friedman test, as applicable, and an adjustment for multiple corrections was done using Bonferroni correction.

Results

Study Population and Baseline Characteristics

Two of the three sites in the original study, located in Mexico and Costa Rica, participated in MSSX study. The third site in the Dominican Republic, which contributed 4 participants, declined to participate in the extension study due to time and resource constraints. Of the initial 43 study participants in the prospective study, 40 completed the Month 12 visit: one participant died after Month 9 from a myocardial infarction, one was lost to follow-up after Month 6, and one was lost to follow-up after Month 9.7 There were 12 participants out of the 40 that completed the original prospective study that did not enroll, including 4 from the Dominican Republic, 4 that were unresponsive despite multiple outreach attempts, and 3 declined to participate, resulting in 29 eligible participants who consented to participate in the retrospective data collection. One participant was lost to follow-up after consenting to the extension study, resulting in 28 with follow-up visits beyond 12 months.

The average length of follow-up (±standard deviation, SD) was 24.2±4.4 months, and the last follow-up visit will therefore be referred to as M24. Demographic data and glaucoma types of the MSSX cohort showed no statistically significant difference vs the original cohort (Table 1). The mean age of this MSSX cohort was 67.5 (range, 57–77) years. The majority were female (75.0%), and all were Hispanic. Most of the MSSX cohort was classified as having mild OAG (78.6%), and the remaining with moderate OAG (21.4%). Generally, the baseline characteristics between the 15 eyes not included in the extension study and the 28 eyes in the MSSX cohort were similar (glaucoma severity, visual field, mean medication count at Screening, BCVA at Screening). One exception was the mean medicated IOP at Screening, which was 20.47±3.48 mmHg on 2.13±0.83 medications in the 15 eyes and 17.07±2.97 mmHg on 1.86±0.80 in the 28 eyes (p=0.002 for IOP, p=0.292 for medication count). Of these 15 participants, 12 completed the M12 visit with a mean IOP of 15.75±3.25 mmHg on 1.08±1.51 medications. There was no statistically significant difference in these results compared to the M12 outcomes of the 28 participants included in the MSSX cohort (p=0.511 for IOP, p=0.154 for medication count).

Postoperative Outcomes

Effectiveness

The mean IOP and IOP-lowering medication count were collected and analyzed for visits from medicated Screening through M24 and are shown in Table 2. At M24, the mean (±SD) IOP was 15.1±3.3 mmHg vs 17.1±3.0 mmHg at Screening (p=0.002). Furthermore, IOP ≤18 mmHg was observed in 23/28 (82.1%) subjects while an IOP of ≤15 mmHg was observed in 15/28 (53.6%) subjects at M24 (Table 3). The mean medication count (±SD) was 1.86±0.80 at Screening, and 0.54±1.17 (p<0.001) at M24, a 71.2% reduction from Screening (Figure 2). There was a statistically significant decrease in mean medication count from Screening across all postoperative time points. At M24, 26/28 (92.9%) subjects were on same or fewer medications than at Screening, 23/28 (82.1%) were on fewer medications than at Screening, while 22/28 (78.6%) were medication free (Table 3). Of note, there was still a statistically significant decrease in both mean IOP and medication count at all postoperative visits from Screening through Month 24 notwithstanding the lower mean IOP and medication count at Screening in the 28 eyes in the MSSX cohort compared to the 15 eyes that were not included in the extension study (Table 2). When additional post-hoc analyses to account for multiplicity were performed, there was still a statistically significant reduction in both mean IOP and medication count from Months 3–24 (p≤0.036 for IOP and p<0.001 for medication count). Additionally, as this was a retrospective study with no IOP-lowering medication washout at M24, we performed post-hoc exploratory analyses on the 22 medication free eyes at M24. The mean (±SD) medication count for these eyes was 1.68±0.72 at Screening with a mean IOP of 16.8±3.2 mmHg. After medication washout in the original prospective study, the baseline mean unmedicated IOP was 22.9±1.7 mmHg. At M24, the mean unmedicated IOP (±SD) decreased to 15.4±3.4 mmHg (p<0.0001), representing a 32.8% IOP reduction from baseline with 17/22 (77.3%) achieving an IOP reduction of ≥20%.

Safety

Safety parameters included best corrected visual acuity (BCVA), visual field mean deviation (VFMD), and any clinical findings designated as AEs. At Screening, the mean (±SD) BCVA of the MSSX cohort was 0.34±0.25 logMAR and improved to 0.07±0.09 and 0.07±0.12 logMAR at M12 and at M24, respectively, which was statistically significantly improved relative to Screening (p<0.001). This is expected, as STREAMLINE was combined with cataract surgery for all cases. Visual fields were available at M12 for 26/28 eyes, and at M24 for 18/28 eyes. The mean (±SD) VFMD was −4.91±3.71 decibels (dB) at Screening, −4.16±0.86 dB at M12, and −4.78±1.13 dB at M24 with no statistically significant difference from Screening (p=0.517, p=0.693).

As previously reported in the original study, there were 16 AEs reported in 11 eyes which were generally mild and self-resolving.7 Four of these eyes were included in this MSSX Cohort and experienced 9 of these AEs. Four AEs were deemed related to procedure or device, and these included IOP elevation (n=2) with hyphema (n=2) in 2 eyes which resolved without sequelae.7 Of note, no new ocular adverse events were reported after M12, and there were no secondary surgical interventions through M24.

Discussion

The results of our study demonstrate that STREAMLINE canaloplasty in combination with phacoemulsification cataract surgery provides clinically meaningful reductions in both IOP and IOP-lowering medications beyond 12 months in eyes with mild-moderate OAG. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to report outcomes of STREAMLINE canaloplasty with phacoemulsification with a mean follow-up of 24 months.

As previously reported,7 in the original prospective study, the mean IOP at Screening (n=43) was 18.3±3.5 mmHg on 1.9±0.8 medications. At M12, mean IOP (±SD) decreased to 15.3±2.8 mmHg on 0.63±1.19 medications (n=40) with 28/40 eyes (70%) being medication free. These results were sustained in the MSSX study where the mean IOP was 15.1±3.3 mmHg on 0.54±1.17 medications with 22/28 (78.6%) being medication free at M24. This is important as the prevalence of OAG in the Hispanic community is high and is expected to increase over the next few decades.12

The reduction of IOP and medication count noted in our study is comparable to that observed in retrospective real-world studies reporting outcomes at 12 months. In a report by Amiri et al,8 68 eyes of 47 patients with mild to severe OAG at a tertiary referral center in the US were followed for an average of 12 months after STREAMLINE canaloplasty with phacoemulsification. Statistically significant reductions vs preoperative baseline were noted in both mean IOP (16.1 mmHg to 14.7 mmHg, p=0.0004) and the mean number of IOP-lowering medications (1.2 to 0.6, p<0.0001) at last follow-up with 31/48 (65%) of eyes being medication free. Stanke and Nguyen11 reported real-world outcomes of STREAMLINE canaloplasty in 51 eyes of 51 patients with mild to severe primary open angle glaucoma (POAG) across 12 months, where the mean medication count decreased from 1.2 to 0.2 (p<0.001) by 12 months while maintaining IOP control. In addition, 43/51 (84.3%) of eyes were on fewer IOP-lowering medications compared to baseline including 45/51 (88.2%) eyes that were medication free at last follow-up. Lastly, Sharma et al10 reported the outcomes of 39 eyes of 29 patients with various types of glaucoma including primary and secondary open angle and angle closure. Even in this heterogenous cohort, there was an IOP reduction from 16.04 to 14.17 mmHg (p=0.13) and a reduction in medications from 2.7 to 0.5 (48% decrease, p=0.003) through 12 months of follow-up, with 20/21 (95.2%) eyes on the same or fewer medications by the end of the study. The safety profiles were comparable across all 3 studies with no vision-threatening AEs; 1 eye in the series by Amiri et al8 required secondary surgical interventions for IOP control. Our study demonstrates that IOP and medication reduction reported by multiple studies at 12 months endures through 24 months after STREAMLINE canaloplasty combined with phacoemulsification. We also confirm the long-term safety profile of STREAMLINE canaloplasty, as no new ocular-related AEs were observed after the initial 12 months.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. Due to the fact that MSSX followed a cohort from a prior study conducted in Latin America, all participants were Hispanic, which may limit extrapolation of the outcomes to other ethnic groups. Although our findings complement the results of the original prospective study, the data in this extension study were collected retrospectively in a subset of the original cohort, limiting the sample size studied. This study, like the preceding prospective study, did not include a comparator arm. A randomized controlled trial (RCT) is currently underway comparing STREAMLINE canaloplasty to trabecular bypass stenting in eyes with mild to moderate POAG undergoing cataract surgery (NCT05089474). Early results from this RCT have been published13 and additional publications are forthcoming.

Conclusion

This study was the first to report that 12-month outcomes of STREAMLINE canaloplasty combined with phacoemulsification are maintained over 24 months. This demonstrates that STREAMLINE, an implant-free MIGS option, can provide durable and clinically meaningful IOP control and reduction of IOP-lowering medications in OAG eyes undergoing cataract surgery.

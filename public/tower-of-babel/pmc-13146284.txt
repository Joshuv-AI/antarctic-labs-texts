ABSTRACT NUMBER: ESOC2026A1445 PROGRESSION AND REGRESSION OF ASYMPTOMATIC CAROTID STENOSIS IN A MULTICENTER KOREAN REGISTRY (K-ACAS)
Authors: Han-Yeong Jeong, Hyung-Min Kwon, Keun-Hwa Jung, Sungwook Yu, Kyusik Kang, Ji Man Hong, Sang Beom Kim, Yang-Ha Hwang, Jun Hong Lee, Eung-Gyu Kim, Jay Chol Choi, Seung-Jae Lee, Park Jong-Ho, Yong-Jae Kim, Yongseok Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and aimsThe natural history of asymptomatic carotid stenosis (ACS) under contemporary medical therapy remains incompletely defined, particularly regarding ultrasonographic progression and regression. This study aimed to quantify the rates and determinants of carotid stenosis progression and regression in patients with ACS and to explore their association with short-term vascular events.MethodsThe Korean Asymptomatic Carotid Artery Stenosis (K-ACAS) registry is a prospective observational study at 14 Korean centers enrolling patients with ≥50% asymptomatic internal carotid artery stenosis on duplex ultrasound using predefined velocity criteria. Baseline and annual ultrasound examinations graded stenosis severity, and numeric change defined progression or regression. Clinical variables, Framingham stroke risk, ASCVD risk, and medical therapy were recorded, and factors associated with 1-year progression or regression were assessed.ResultsOf 350 enrolled patients, 294 (84.0%) completed 1-year ultrasound follow-up. At 1 year, 29.9% showed progression and 22.4% regression; among those with longer follow-up, progression and regression were 34.7% and 26.6% at 2 years and 52.5% and 24.6% at 3 years. Progression was associated with older age, peripheral artery disease, and higher Framingham stroke risk and ASCVD risk scores. Regression was associated with lower triglycerides and higher atorvastatin-equivalent statin doses. One-year stroke incidence was 1.4%, and major adverse cardiovascular events were rare and did not differ by progression status.ConclusionsIn ACS, carotid stenosis severity on ultrasound is dynamic, with substantial progression and regression despite low event rates.

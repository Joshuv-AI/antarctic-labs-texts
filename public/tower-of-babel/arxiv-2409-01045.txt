A charged liquid drop model with Willmore energy
Michael Goldman, Matteo Novaga, Berardo Ruffini

Abstract
. We consider a variational model of electrified liquid drops, involving competition between surface tension and charge repulsion. Since the natural model happens to be ill-posed, we show that by adding to the perimeter a Willmore-type energy, the problem turns back to be well-posed. We also prove that for small charge the droplets is spherical.

1.
Introduction

The model proposed in 1882 by Lord Rayleigh
[
33
]
is widely used to describe the equilibrium shapes of charged liquid droplets in presence of an electrical charge, see e.g.
[
26
,
36
,
9
,
11
]
. The underlining energy is given by

P(E)+\frac{Q^{2}}{{\rm Cap}_{2}(E)}.

Here
E
is a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
P
is a perimeter term modeling surface tension,
Q>0
is the amount of charge and
{\rm Cap}_{2}
is the standard capacity. The observed droplets should be (at least for small
Q
) stable volume-constrained critical points of this energy. Quite surprisingly we showed in
[
16
]
that this model is ill-posed in the sense that no local minimizers for the natural
L^{1}
topology exist at any charge. To reconcile this with the experimental and numerical observation of stable charged liquid drops, substential effort has been made to understand possible regularizing mechanisms. One possibility is to restrict a priori the class of candidates as in
[
16
,
17
]
. Another possibility is to add regularizing terms to the energy. See also
[
30
]
where both aspects are present. One way of regularizing the energy is to penalize strong concentration of charges (which amounts to regularize the capacity term) as in
[
27
,
5
,
30
]
and to some extent
[
28
,
29
,
18
]
. A second very natural way to regularize the energy is instead to take into account higher order effects in the term modelling surface tension. It is for instance suggested in
[
19
]
that penalizing the curvature through the Willmore energy could be enough to restore well-posedness. The aim of this paper is to answer this question affirmatively.

1.1.
Notation and main results

We will use the notation
A\lesssim B
to indicate that there exists a constant
C>0
, typically
depending on the dimension
d
and on
\alpha\in(0,d)
such that
A\leq CB
(we will specify when
C
depends on other quantities). We write
A\sim B
if
A\lesssim B\lesssim A
, and
A\ll B
to indicate that there exists a (typically small) universal constant
\varepsilon>0
such that
A\leq\varepsilon B
.

For
d=2,3
,
\alpha\in(0,d)
,
Q\geq 0
,
\lambda\geq 0
and
E
a smooth set we consider the energy

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,Q}(E)=\lambda P(E)+W(E)+Q^{2}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(E).

When
\lambda=0
we simply write
\mathcal{F}_{Q}(E)=\mathcal{F}_{0,Q}(E)=W(E)+Q^{2}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(E)
. The various terms of the energy are:

•

the perimeter
P
(see
[
24
]
), which satisfies for smooth sets

P(E)=\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\partial E),

where
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
denotes the
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
;

•

the elastic or Willmore energy
W
, defined as

W(E)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\partial E}H^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\quad&\text{ for }d=2,\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{\partial E}H^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{2}\quad&\text{ for }d=3,\end{cases}

where
H
denotes the mean curvature of
\partial E
, i.e. the curvature in dimension two and the sum of the principal curvatures in dimension three (see Section
3.1
);

•

the Riesz interaction energy
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}
defined, for
\alpha\in(0,d)
, as

\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(E)=\min_{\mu(E)=1}I_{\alpha}(\mu)=\frac{1}{{\rm Cap}_{\alpha}(E)}

(1.1)

with

I_{\alpha}(\mu)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{d\mu(x)d\mu(y)}{|x-y|^{d-\alpha}}.

(1.2)

Given a volume constant
m>0
, we consider then the following problem

\min\left\{\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,Q}(E)\,:\,|E|=m\right\}.

(1.3)

A first step in the study of (
1.3
) was taken in
[
15
]
. There, the capacitary term was replaced by

V_{\alpha}(E)=\int_{E\times E}\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{d-\alpha}}.

This corresponds to the assumption that the charge distribution
\mu
is uniform on
E
. The functional is then a perturbation of the Gamow type models studied for instance in
[
20
,
10
,
12
]
. The main results of
[
15
]
may be summarized as follows. First if we consider the case
d=2
, disks are the unique minimizers among simply connected sets for
Q
small enough and
\lambda=0
(and thus also for any
\lambda\geq 0
by the isoperimetric inequality). Dropping the assumption that the sets are simply connected we lose existence for
\lambda=0
. For
\lambda>0
there is now a dichotomy. There exists
\bar{\lambda}=\bar{\lambda}(m)>0
such that for
Q=0
, minimizers are disks if
\lambda\geq\bar{\lambda}
while they are annuli if
\lambda\leq\bar{\lambda}
. This picture remains valid if
Q>0
is small enough. When
d=3
and
\lambda=0
(and thus again for every
\lambda\geq 0
by isoperimetric inequality), it was proven that for small enough
Q
and
\alpha\in(1,3)
the corresponding energy is uniquely minimized by balls. In this paper we prove that to a large extent the same results hold for (
1.3
).

In the two-dimensional case we strongly rely on the analysis from
[
15
]
. Let us define

\min_{E\in\mathcal{M}_{\rm sc}(m)}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,Q}(E),

(1.4)

\min_{E\in\mathcal{M}(m)}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,Q}(E),

(1.5)

where
\mathcal{M}(m)
is the family of measurable sets of measure
m
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\rm sc}(m)
is the subset of
\mathcal{M}(m)
of simply connected sets.

Proposition 1.1
.

For every
\alpha\in(0,2)
, there exists
Q_{0}=Q_{0}(\alpha)
such that for every
\lambda\geq 0
and every
Q\leq Q_{0}m^{-(\alpha-1)/2}
, the only minimizers of (
1.4
) are balls.

For (
1.5
) we are only able here to treat the case
\lambda>\bar{\lambda}
when we expect minimizers to be disks when they exist. We set here
\bar{\lambda}=\bar{\lambda}(|B_{1}|)
to be the constant given by
[
15
, Theorem 2.7]
.

Proposition 1.2
.

For every
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
\lambda_{0}>\bar{\lambda}
, there exists
Q_{0}=Q_{0}(\lambda_{0},\alpha)>0
such that for every
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}m^{-1}
and every
Q\leq Q_{0}m^{-(\alpha-1)/2}
, the only minimizers of (
1.5
) are balls.

Remark 1.3
.

When
\lambda\leq\bar{\lambda}
we expect to see instead annuli. However this seems to be a difficult problem as the analysis in
[
15
]
heavily relies on the fact that
V_{\alpha}(E)
is an explicit function of
E
.

We now turn to the case
d=3
. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

For every
\alpha\in[2,3)
, there exists a constant
\overline{Q}=\overline{Q}(\alpha)>0
such that for every
\lambda\geq 0
and
m>0
with
Q\leq\overline{Q}m^{(3-\alpha)/6}
, the only minimizers of (
1.3
) are the balls of measure
m
.

As above we prove the result for
\lambda=0
. Moreover, by scaling we may assume that
m=|B_{1}|
. The main difference between
d=2
and
d=3
is that in the former, having finite energy implies a
C^{1,1/2}
control on the sets implying in particular that sets of small energy must be nearly spherical. In the latter, we can still obtain from
[
3
]
that they are
W^{2,2}\cap{\rm Lip}
parameterizations of the sphere. While a stability inequality for
W
is given by
[
34
]
, as opposed to
[
15
]
, this does not seem to be enough to perform the Fuglede type computations from
[
16
,
32
,
18
]
for
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}
. Our strategy is thus to first prove (uniform in
Q
)
C^{1,\beta}
regularity for minimizers. A first issue is the existence of minimizers. If we work with the non-parametric approach of Simon, see
[
39
]
, we naturally end up in the class of varifolds with square integrable mean curvature. As in
[
16
]
a major problem is then the semi-continuity of
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}
. We solve this issue by relying on
[
3
,
4
]
to obtain that in the small charge regime, sets of small energy must be parameterizations of the sphere with controlled
W^{2,2}
norm. This yields convergence in the Hausdorff sense from which we obtain existence of minimizers. In order to prove regularity of these minimizers, a natural idea would be to follow the parametric approach
[
35
]
of Rivière. However, this does not seem to be easily compatible with our capacitary term. We go back instead to the non-parametric approach viewing the volume penalization and
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}
as lower order perturbations of
W
. Our proof departs in a substantial point from
[
39
]
(see also
[
31
]
). Indeed, a central difficulty in the theory of varifolds is that it is in general not known that smooth sets are dense in energy. Since the construction of competitors in
[
39
]
has to be made for smooth sets,
[
39
]
does it at the level of minimizing sequences. The main drawback is that one has to distinguish the ’good points’ where there is no energy concentration from the ’bad points’ where there is energy concentration. In our case we leverage on the fact that sets with small energy are parametrized to prove density in energy of smooth sets (see Lemma
3.2
).
Using this approximation result we can avoid altogether the presence of the ’bad points’. Not only this simplifies significantly the proof from
[
39
]
,
but it also allows to obtain bounds which do not depend on
Q
. Indeed, the analysis of the bad points is based on a compactness argument which does not seem to yield uniform regularity estimates. We believe that one of the original aspects of this work is the combined use of the parametric and non-parametric approaches together.

The restriction
\alpha\in[2,3)
in Theorem
1.4
comes from the fact that we use the Fuglede type estimates from
[
16
,
32
,
18
]
which are not known for
\alpha<2
. Indeed, in this case the underlying operator is a fractional Laplacian of order larger than one. Not much seems to be known for this type of harmonic measures.

Remark 1.5
.

Our proof of existence and
C^{1,\beta}
regularity for minimizers of (
1.3
) would also apply (and would actually be easier) for the functional from
[
15
]
(replacing
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}
by
V_{\alpha}
). In particular, this proves that for small
Q
, the corresponding minimizers are nearly-spherical. Using the Fuglede computations from
[
20
,
10
]
we can extend
[
15
, Theorem 4.6]
to the whole range
\alpha\in(0,3)
.

The two-dimensional case

We first prove Proposition
1.1
.

Proof of Proposition
1.1
.

By the isoperimetric inequality it is enough to prove the statement for
\lambda=0
. By scaling we may further assume that
m=|B_{1}|
. If
E\in\mathcal{M}_{\rm sc}(|B_{1}|)
is such that
\mathcal{F}_{0,Q}(E)\leq\mathcal{F}_{0,Q}(B_{1})
, then

W(E)\leq W(B_{1})+Q^{2}(\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(B_{1})-\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(E)).

In particular, if
Q
is small enough, we can apply
[
15
, Theorem 2.3]
to obtain

Q^{2}(\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(B_{1})-\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(E))\geq W(E)-W(B_{1})\gtrsim(P(E)-P(B_{1}))+\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}|E\Delta B_{1}(x)|.

(2.1)

Since
\partial E=\gamma([0,P(E)])
where
\gamma
is a unit-speed parametrization with

W(E)=\int_{0}^{P(E)}|\gamma^{\prime\prime}|^{2},

from the embedding of
W^{2,2}([0,P(E)])
in
C^{1,1/2}([0,P(E)])
we see that for every
\beta<1/2
and every
Q
small enough, up to translation, every such set is
C^{1,\beta}
close to
B_{1}
and thus a nearly spherical set. By
[
18
, Proposition 4.5]
we then get

\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(B_{1})-\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(E)\lesssim P(E)-P(B_{1}).

Combining this and (
2.1
) yields a contradiction if
Q
is small enough.
∎

We now prove Proposition
1.2
.

Proof of Proposition
1.2
.

By the isoperimetric inequality it is enough to prove that for every
\lambda>\bar{\lambda}
there exists
Q_{0}>0
such that balls are the unique volume-constrained minimizers of
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,Q}
for every
Q\leq Q_{0}
. For this we notice as before that if
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,Q}(E)\leq\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,Q}(B_{1})
, then

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,0}(E)\leq\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,0}(B_{1})+Q^{2}(\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(B_{1})-\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(E))

we may thus apply
[
15
, Lemma 2.9]
and conclude that for
Q
small enough (depending on
\lambda
), such sets must be simply connected. By Proposition
1.1
, this concludes the proof.
∎

3.
The three-dimensional case

3.1.
Preliminaries on Riemannian geometry and weak immersions

In this section we first collect some notation and well-known facts from Riemannian geometry, see e.g.
[
7
,
6
]
. We then prove in Lemma
3.2
an approximation result for weak immersions. For
r>0
we write
\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2}=\partial B_{r}
and simply
\mathbb{S}^{2}
for
\mathbb{S}^{2}_{1}
.

We denote by
D
the flat connection on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
i.e. if
X=\sum_{i=1}^{3}X_{i}e_{i}
then
D_{X}Y=\sum_{i=1}^{3}X_{i}\partial_{i}Y
. Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a smooth compact
2-
dimensional manifold oriented by its normal
\nu
. For
X,Y
tangent vectorfields to
\Sigma
we define the action of the second fundamental form
A
on them as

A(x)[X(x),Y(x)]=-(D_{X}Y)\cdot\nu.

(3.1)

Notice that witht this convention, if
\Sigma
is the boundary of a convex set oriented with the outward normal then
A
is positive. If
(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
is a local orthogonal frame we have for
i=1,2
,

D_{\tau_{i}}\tau_{j}=-A[\tau_{i},\tau_{j}]\nu.

(3.2)

Moreover, if
\Sigma=\mathbb{S}^{2}
, we have
A[\tau_{i},\tau_{j}]=\delta_{ij}
. When considering
A
as a (symmetric) linear map from
T_{x}\Sigma
to
T_{x}\Sigma
we write

H={\rm Tr}(A)\qquad\textrm{and }\qquad A^{\circ}=A-\frac{1}{2}({\rm Tr}A)\,{\rm Id}

to be the mean curvature and the traceless part of
A
. If
(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})
are the eigenvalues of
A
, we have

|H|^{2}=(\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2})^{2},\qquad|A|^{2}=\kappa_{1}^{2}+\kappa_{2}^{2}\qquad\textrm{and }\qquad|A^{\circ}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}(\kappa_{1}-\kappa_{2})^{2}.

If we denote by
\mathbf{g}(\Sigma)
the genus of
\Sigma
, we have by the Gauss-Bonnet formula,

\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Sigma}|H|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Sigma}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}+2\pi(1-\mathbf{g}(\Sigma))\qquad\textrm{and }\\
\int_{\Sigma}|A^{\circ}|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}-4\pi(1-\mathbf{g}(\Sigma)).

(3.3)

In particular we have as consequence of the Li-Yau inequality (and
\mathbf{g}(\Sigma)\geq 0
) that for any smooth compact surface
\Sigma
,

\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Sigma}|A|^{2}\geq 2\pi(1+\mathbf{g}(\Sigma))\geq 2\pi.

(3.4)

For
\psi
a smooth real-valued function defined on a neighborhood of
\Sigma
we define
\nabla\psi(x)\in T_{x}\Sigma
as the line vector

\nabla\psi=\pi_{T_{x}\Sigma}(D\psi)

where
\pi_{T_{x}\Sigma}
is the projection on
T_{x}\Sigma
. If
(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
is a local orthonormal frame, we often write
\partial_{i}\psi=\nabla\psi\cdot\tau_{i}
. We then define

\nabla^{2}\psi(x)[X(x),Y(x)]=D^{2}\psi(x)[X(x),Y(x)]-A[X,Y](x)D\psi(x)\cdot\nu(x).

(3.5)

We similarly denote
\partial_{ij}\psi=\nabla^{2}\psi[\tau_{i},\tau_{j}]
. If
\psi=(\psi_{1},\cdots\psi_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
then we still write
\nabla\psi
for the matrix whose
k-th
line is given by
\nabla\psi_{k}
. In local coordinates we have
\nabla\psi=(\partial_{1}\psi,\partial_{2}\psi)
. For a
(k\times 3)
-array
M=(M_{1},\cdots,M_{k})^{T}
we write
\pi_{T_{x}\Sigma}(M)
for the
(k\times 3)
-array
(\pi_{T_{x}\Sigma}(M_{1}),\cdots\pi_{T_{x}\Sigma}(M_{k}))^{T}
. Let us point out that if
\Sigma
is connected and
\psi
is a smooth embedding then by the theorem of Jordan-Brouwer, see
[
2
, Proposition 12.2]
, there exists
E
such that
\partial E=\psi(\Sigma)
.

For
\psi\in W^{2,2}(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2})\cap{\rm Lip}(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2})
such that for some
c_{0}>0
and
\mathcal{H}^{2}
a.e. on
\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2}
,

|\partial_{1}\psi\wedge\partial_{2}\psi|\geq c_{0}

we set

N_{\psi}=\frac{\partial_{1}\psi\wedge\partial_{2}\psi}{|\partial_{1}\psi\wedge\partial_{2}\psi|}.

We consider
N_{\psi}
as a function from
\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2}
to
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
Notice that such
\psi
are weak immersions in the sense of
[
35
]
.
For
\Sigma=\psi(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2})
, we see that
N_{\psi}
is a normal vectorfield to
\Sigma
. Let then
g
be the pullback metric on
\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2}
of the standard Euclidean metric on
\Sigma
through
\psi
i.e.

g(v,w)=\nabla\psi(v)\cdot\nabla\psi(w)\qquad\forall(v,w)\in T\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2}\times T\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2}.

We then write
d{\rm vol}_{g}
for the volume form induced by the metric
g
, that is

d{\rm vol}_{g}=\sqrt{|\partial_{1}\psi|^{2}|\partial_{2}\psi|^{2}-(\partial_{1}\psi\cdot\partial_{2}\psi)^{2}}d\mathcal{H}^{2}=|\partial_{1}\psi\wedge\partial_{2}\psi|d\mathcal{H}^{2}.

We then have (see
[
7
, Section 3.3]
)

\int_{\Sigma}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2}}|AG^{-1}|^{2}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}

where by (
3.1
),

A_{ij}=-(\partial_{ij}\psi)\cdot N_{\psi}=-(\nabla^{2}\psi\cdot N_{\psi})_{ij}

and

G^{-1}=\frac{1}{|\partial_{1}\psi\wedge\partial_{2}\psi|^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}|\partial_{2}\psi|^{2}&-\partial_{1}\psi\cdot\partial_{2}\psi\\
-\partial_{1}\psi\cdot\partial_{2}\psi&|\partial_{1}\psi|^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Notice that by the Weingarten equations, see
[
7
, Section 3.3]
,

|AG^{-1}|^{2}=|\nabla N_{\psi}|^{2}_{g}.

Therefore,

\int_{\Sigma}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2}}|\nabla N_{\psi}|^{2}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}\\
=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2}}|\partial_{1}\psi\wedge\partial_{2}\psi|^{-3}\left|(\nabla^{2}\psi\cdot N_{\psi})\begin{pmatrix}|\partial_{2}\psi|^{2}&-\partial_{1}\psi\cdot\partial_{2}\psi\\
-\partial_{1}\psi\cdot\partial_{2}\psi&|\partial_{1}\psi|^{2}\end{pmatrix}\right|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}.

(3.6)

When
\psi\in W^{2,2}(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2})
is conformal, we let
h^{2}=|\nabla\psi|^{2}/2=|\partial_{1}\psi\wedge\partial_{2}\psi|
be the conformal factor. Notice that for
\psi
conformal if
h^{2}=|\nabla\psi|^{2}/2
is bounded then
\psi\in{\rm Lip}(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2})
. For such conformal maps we have

\int_{\Sigma}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2}}|\nabla N_{\psi}|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{2}.

We will need an approximation result. As suggested in
[
21
, page 316]
we follow the strategy of
[
38
, Section 4]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume
\psi\in W^{2,2}(\mathbb{S}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap{\rm Lip}(\mathbb{S}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{3})
is conformal and such that

\|h-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq\frac{1}{4}.

Then there exists a sequence of smooth maps
\psi_{n}
such that
\psi_{n}
converges strongly in
W^{2,2}(\mathbb{S}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{3})
to
\psi
with

|\partial_{1}\psi_{n}\wedge\partial_{2}\psi_{n}|\geq\frac{1}{8}\qquad\textrm{and }\qquad\|\nabla\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq 2.

(3.7)

Proof.

Let us define the subset of
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}

M=\left\{h(v_{1},v_{2})\,:\,(h,v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathcal{A}\right\},

where
\mathcal{A}
is the family of triples
(h,v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
such that
|h-1|\leq 1/2
,
\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle=0
. Notice that for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
,
\nabla\psi(x)\in M
as it is a conformal map. We extend
\psi
to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
by
0-
homogeneity. Notice that if
\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x)=x/|x|
is the projection on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, this means that we identify
\psi
and
\psi(\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x))
. For further use let us point out that if
\zeta
is
0-
homogeneous then
D\zeta(x)=\frac{1}{|x|}\nabla\zeta(\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x))
. Writing in coordinates that

\nabla\zeta=\sum_{i}\partial_{\tau_{i}}\zeta\tau_{i}

and using (
3.2
), we find

D^{2}\zeta(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}\left(\nabla^{2}\zeta(\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x))-\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x)\otimes\nabla\zeta(\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x))+\nabla\zeta(\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x))\otimes\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x)\right).

(3.8)

Let now
\rho
be a standard convolution kernel with
{\rm Spt}\rho\subset B_{1}
. For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
we set
\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-3}\rho(x/\varepsilon)
and then

\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\psi(y)dy.

Fix
x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Using that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)dy=1

and for a.e.
y
with
|y-x|\leq\varepsilon
,
D\psi(y)=|y|^{-1}\nabla\psi(\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(y))\in M
provided
\varepsilon
is small enough, we have

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}(\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}(x),M)^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y){\rm dist}_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}(\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}(x),M)^{2}\,dy

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)|D\psi(y)-\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\left|D\psi(y)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-z)D\psi(z)dz\right|^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-z)D\psi(z)dz-\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}.

We now bound separately the two right-hand side terms. For the first one, let
\zeta(y)=D\psi(y)
so that with obvious notation

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\left|D\psi(y)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-z)D\psi(z)dz\right|^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\left|\zeta(y)-\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)|D\zeta(y)|^{2}dy

by the weighted Poincaré inequality. Since
D\zeta=D^{2}\psi
we have by (
3.8
),

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\left|\zeta(y)-\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\,dy\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\left[|y|^{-2}(|\nabla^{2}\psi(\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(y))|+|\nabla\psi(\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(y))|)\right]^{2}dy\\
\lesssim\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}|\nabla^{2}\psi(\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(y))|^{2}+|\nabla\psi(\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(y))|^{2}dy.

Letting

G_{\varepsilon}(x)=\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\nabla^{2}\psi|^{2}+|\nabla\psi|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}

we thus have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\left|\zeta(y)-\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\,dy\lesssim G_{\varepsilon}(x).

(3.10)

Let us now turn to the second right-hand side term in (
3.9
). Writing that

\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)\psi(x-z)dz

we compute by linearity of the projection,

\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)\pi_{T_{x}\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left(D\psi(x-z)\right)dz=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-z)\pi_{T_{x}\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left(D\psi(z)\right)dz.

(3.11)

Before proceeding further let us point out that for every
x,z\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
and
v\in T_{y}\mathbb{S}^{2}
, since
v\cdot y=0
we have by writing
x=y+(x-y)
,

|x\cdot v|=|(x-y)\cdot v|\leq|x-y||v|.

(3.12)

We then have,

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-z)D\psi(z)dz-\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-z)\left|D\psi(z)-\pi_{T_{x}\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left(D\psi(z)\right)\right|dz\\
=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-z)|z|^{-1}\left|\nabla\psi(\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(z))-\pi_{T_{x}\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left(\nabla\psi(\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(z))\right)\right|dz=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-z)|z|^{-1}\left|\nabla\psi(\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(z))\cdot x\right|dz\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{changeproj}}}{{\lesssim}}\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-z)\left|\nabla\psi(\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(z))\right|dz.

Since
\psi\in{\rm Lip}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
with
|\nabla\psi|=\sqrt{2}
, we conclude that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-z)D\psi(z)dz-\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|\lesssim\varepsilon.

(3.13)

Plugging (
3.10
) and (
3.13
) into (
3.9
) we find

{\rm dist}_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}(\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}(x),M)\lesssim G_{\varepsilon}(x)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\varepsilon.

Now since
|\nabla^{2}\psi|^{2}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, we get that
G_{\varepsilon}(x)\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
uniformly in
x
, and so

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}}{\rm dist}_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}(\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}(x),M)=0.

Thus for
\varepsilon
small enough, there exists for every
x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
an element
A=A(x)=(a_{1},a_{2})\in M
such that

|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)-A|\leq\frac{1}{16}.

By bilinearity and the triangular inequality we conclude that

\displaystyle|\partial_{1}\psi_{\varepsilon}\wedge\partial_{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}|

\displaystyle\geq|a_{1}\wedge a_{2}|-|(\partial_{1}\psi_{\varepsilon}-a_{2})\wedge a_{1}|-|(\partial_{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}-a_{1})\wedge a_{1}|-|(\partial_{1}\psi_{\varepsilon}-a_{1})\wedge(\partial_{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}-a_{2})|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}-|\partial_{1}\psi_{\varepsilon}-a_{2}||a_{1}|-|\partial_{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}-a_{1}||a_{1}|-|\partial_{1}\psi_{\varepsilon}-a_{1}||\partial_{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}-a_{2}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{16}.

This proves the first part of (
3.7
). For the second part of (
3.7
), we simply observe that by (
3.11
),

|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)|\nabla\psi(\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(y))|dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)|x-y||D\psi(y)|dy\\
\leq\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}(1+C\varepsilon).

Let us now prove that
\psi_{\varepsilon}
converges in
W^{2,2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
to
\psi
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
. By Poincaré inequality it is enough to prove that
\nabla^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}
converges to
\nabla^{2}\psi
. Fix

and write for simplicity

. By (

) and the definition of
\psi_{\varepsilon}
, we have

\nabla^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)[\tau_{i},\tau_{j}]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\left[D^{2}\psi(y)[\tau_{i},\tau_{j}]-\delta_{ij}D\psi(y)\cdot x\right]dy\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{D2zeta}}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)|y|^{-2}\nabla^{2}\psi(\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(y))[\tau_{i},\tau_{j}]dy\\
-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\left[|y|^{-3}(\nabla\psi(\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(y))\cdot\tau_{i})(y\cdot\tau_{j})+|y|^{-3}(\nabla\psi(\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(y))\cdot\tau_{j})(y\cdot\tau_{i})+\delta_{ij}D\psi(y)\cdot x\right]dy.

+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)(|\nabla\psi(\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(y))|+|\nabla^{2}\psi(\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(y))|)dy.

Since

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)(\nabla^{2}\psi(\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(y))-\nabla^{2}\psi(x))dy\right|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}(x)=0,

the

\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left(\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\nabla\psi|+|\nabla^{2}\psi|d\mathcal{H}^{2}\right)^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}(x)\\
\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\nabla\psi|^{2}+|\nabla^{2}\psi|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}.

Remark 3.5
.

Let
\psi_{n}

Lemma 3.6
.

For every

compact and

3.18
) the left-hand side is always larger than the right-hand side we only need to prove that provided
\Lambda=\Lambda_{0}Q_{0}^{2}
for some
\Lambda_{0}
large enough, for every
Q\leq Q_{0}
,

\inf\{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta,\Lambda}_{Q}(E)\ :\ |E|=|B_{1}|,\ E\ \textrm{smooth}\}\leq\inf\{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta,\Lambda}_{Q}(E)\ :\ E\ \textrm{smooth}\}.

(3.19)

Let us assume that there exists
E
smooth with
|E|\neq|B_{1}|
and for which

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta,\Lambda}_{Q}(E)\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta,\Lambda}_{Q}(F)\qquad\textrm{for all smooth $F$ with }|F|=|B_{1}|.

(3.20)

We first notice that since
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta,\Lambda}_{Q}(E)\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta,\Lambda}_{Q}(B_{1})
and
\int_{\partial E}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}\geq\int_{\partial B_{1}}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}
(recall (
3.4
)),

Q^{2}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(E)+\Lambda||E|-|B_{1}||\leq Q^{2}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(B_{1})\leq Q^{2}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{1}(B_{1}).

Recalling that
\Lambda=\Lambda_{0}Q_{0}^{2}
, we find

\Lambda_{0}||E|-|B_{1}||\lesssim 1\qquad\textrm{and }\qquad\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(E)\leq\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(B_{1})\lesssim 1.

(3.21)

In particular, we may assume that
||E|-|B_{1}||\ll 1
provided
\Lambda_{0}
is chosen large enough. Let now
t=1-(|B_{1}|/|E|)^{1/3}
be such that
|(1-t)E|=|B_{1}|
. Since
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta,\Lambda}_{Q}(\lambda E)\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta,\Lambda}_{Q}(E)
for
\lambda\geq 1
by (
3.15
), we have
t>0
. Moreover, since
||E|-|B_{1}||\ll 1
, we also have
t\ll 1
. By assumption,

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta,\Lambda}_{Q}(E)\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta,\Lambda}_{Q}((1-t)E).

Since

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta,\Lambda}_{Q}(E)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\partial E}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}+Q^{2}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(E)+\Lambda||E|-|B_{1}||\\
=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\partial E}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}+Q^{2}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(E)+\Lambda|B_{1}|\left((1-t)^{-3}-1\right)

and

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta,\Lambda}_{Q}((1-t)E)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\partial E}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}+Q^{2}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}((1-t)E)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:scalingIae}}}{{\leq}}\frac{1}{4}\int_{\partial E}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}+Q^{2}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(E)(1-t)^{-3},

we find after rearranging the terms

\Lambda|B_{1}|\left((1-t)^{-3}-1\right)\leq Q^{2}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(E)\left((1-t)^{-3}-1\right).

Since
0<t<1
we can simplify and conclude (since
t\neq 0
)

\Lambda_{0}Q_{0}^{2}=\Lambda\lesssim Q_{0}^{2}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(E)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{smallrelax}}}{{\lesssim}}Q_{0}^{2}.

This proves that if
\Lambda_{0}
is large enough then (
3.20
) cannot hold with
|E|\neq|B_{1}|
and we indeed have (
3.19
).

∎

We now prove that for
Q
small enough
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta,\Lambda}_{Q}
admits minimizers and that they are
W^{2,2}
embeddings of the sphere.

Proposition 3.9
.

There exists
Q_{0}>0
such that for every
Q\leq Q_{0}
and every
0<\eta\leq 1
, there exist
r>0
and
\psi\in W^{2,2}(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2})\cap{\rm Lip}(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2})
a conformal embedding such that (the implicit constants do not depend on
\eta
)

|r-1|+\|\psi-{\rm Id}\|^{2}_{W^{2,2}(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2})}+\|h-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2})}\lesssim Q^{2}

(3.22)

and such that
\partial E=\psi(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2})
satisfies
|E|=|B_{1}|
and

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{\eta,\Lambda}(E)=\inf\{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{\eta,\Lambda}(F)\ :\ F\ \textrm{smooth}\}.

(3.23)

Moreover, there exists
E_{n}
smooth with
\partial E_{n}
converging to
\partial E
in the Hausdorff sense and such that

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{\eta,\Lambda}(E_{n})\to\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{\eta,\Lambda}(E)\qquad\textrm{with }\\
\int_{\partial E_{n}\cap B_{\rho}(x)}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}\to\int_{\partial E\cap B_{\rho}(x)}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}\quad\textrm{ provided }\quad\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial E\cap\partial B_{\rho}(x))=0.

(3.24)

Finally, if
E
is a (smooth) minimizer of (
3.23
) for some
\eta\in[0,1]
, then up to translation,
\partial E=\psi(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2})
for some
r
and
\psi
satisfying (
3.22
).

Proof.

Let
Q_{0}
be given by Lemma
3.7
. If
E
is smooth set with
|E|=|B_{1}|
and such that
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{\eta}(E)\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{\eta}(B_{1})
, since
\partial E
is connected with
\mathbf{g}(\partial E)=0
, using (
3.3
) we find

\frac{1}{4}\int_{\partial E}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\partial B_{1}}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}=W(E)-W(B_{1})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial E}|A^{\circ}|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}.

Moreover, by
[
15
, Proposition 4.3]
,
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial E)
is universally bounded so that by
[
34
]
, if
r
is such that
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2})=\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial E)
,

|r-1|\lesssim(\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial E)-4\pi)\lesssim\int_{\partial E}|A^{\circ}|^{2}\lesssim Q^{2}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(B_{1})\leq Q^{2}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{1}(B_{1})\lesssim Q^{2}.

Notice that by the isoperimetric inequality,
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial E)\geq\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial B_{1})
and thus
r\geq 1
.
Appealing to
[
3
,
4
]
, and up to translation, we find the existence of a conformal embedding
\psi\in W^{2,2}(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2})
satisfying

\|\psi-{\rm Id}\|^{2}_{W^{2,2}(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2})}+\|h-r\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2})}\lesssim\int_{\partial E}|A^{\circ}|^{2}\lesssim Q^{2}.

By triangle inequality, this proves the last part of the statement.

If now
E_{n}
is a minimizing sequence then by the previous discussion, we find after possible translation, a sequence
r_{n}
uniformly bounded from above and below and a sequence of conformal
\psi_{n}\in W^{2,2}(B_{r_{n}})
satisfying (
3.22
) and such that
\partial E_{n}=\psi_{n}(\partial B_{r_{n}})
.
After extraction we find that
r_{n}\to r>0
,
\psi_{n}\rightharpoonup\psi
weakly in
W^{2,2}
with
(r,\psi)
satisfying (
3.22
). By Sobolev embedding,
\psi_{n}
also converges strongly in
W^{1,2}
and in
C^{0,\beta}
for every
\beta\in(0,1)
so that
\psi
is conformal. By the strong
W^{1,2}
convergence and (
3.22
) we have

\|h-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2})}\lesssim Q^{2}.

In particular,
\psi\in{\rm Lip}(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2})
and up to decreasing the value of
Q_{0}
we may assume that
h\in(1/2,2)
.
By the
C^{0,\beta}
convergence of
\psi_{n}
, we have that
\partial E_{n}
converges in the Hausdorff topology to
\partial E=\psi(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2})
. By lower semicontinuity of the Willmore energy we have

W(E)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}W(E_{n})<8\pi

and thus by Li-Yau inequality
[
22
, (A.17)]

\psi
is an embedding.
By the Hausdorff convergence of
\partial E_{n}
to
\partial E
, we find
|E|=|B_{1}|
and
\lim_{n}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(E_{n})=\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(E)
, see Lemma
3.4
, so that

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{\eta,\Lambda}(E)\leq\liminf_{n}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta,\Lambda}_{Q}(E_{n})=\inf\{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta,\Lambda}_{Q}(F)\ :F\ \textrm{smooth}\}.

(3.25)

To conclude the proof we use that since
h\in(1/2,2)
, we can apply Lemma
3.2
and find a new sequence
\widetilde{\psi}_{n}
of smooth embeddings of
\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2}
converging strongly in
W^{2,2}
to
\psi
. Setting
\partial\widetilde{E}_{n}=\widetilde{\psi}_{n}(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2})
we find that
\partial\widetilde{E}_{n}
converges in the Hausdorff topology to
\partial E
with
\int_{\partial E_{n}}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}\to\int_{\partial E}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}
and
|\widetilde{E}_{n}|\to|E|
. Moreover, the second part of (
3.24
) holds. Finally, by Lemma
3.4
we have
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(\widetilde{E}_{n})\to\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(E)
which yields
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta,\Lambda}_{Q}(\widetilde{E}_{n})\to\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta,\Lambda}_{Q}(E)
. This proves the first part of (
3.24
) as well as

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta,\Lambda}_{Q}(E)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta,\Lambda}_{Q}(\widetilde{E}_{n})\geq\inf\{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta,\Lambda}_{Q}(E)\ :\ E\ \textrm{smooth}\}.

Combined with (
3.25
) and (
3.18
), this concludes the proof of (
3.23
).

∎

4.
Regularity

We prove now an elementary but useful decay estimate (see e.g.
[
13
, Lemma 5.13]
for a variant of this result).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Lambda,\delta>0
,
r_{0}\leq 1
,
\theta\in(0,1)
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
. Let
\psi
be a positive and increasing function such that

\psi(\theta r)\leq\gamma\psi(r)+\Lambda r^{\delta}\qquad\forall r\in(0,r_{0}).

(4.1)

Then, there exists
\beta=\beta(\theta,\gamma,\delta)\in(0,\delta)
and
C=C(\theta,\gamma,\delta)>0
such that

\psi(r)\leq C\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{\beta}(\psi(r_{0})+\Lambda r_{0}^{\beta}).

(4.2)

Proof.

We fix
\beta\in(0,\delta)
such that

\gamma<\theta^{\beta}.

(4.3)

Notice that since
\gamma<1
this is always possible by taking
\beta
small enough. We now prove that there exists
C=C(\beta,\theta)
such that (
4.2
) holds. For
k\geq 0
let
r_{k}=\theta^{k}r_{0}
. Since
\psi
is increasing, for every
r\in(r_{k+1},r_{k})
,
\psi(r_{k+1})\leq\psi(r)\leq\psi(r_{k})
so that it is enough to prove (
4.2
) for
r=r_{k}
. We now argue by induction. For
k=0
the statement clearly holds (with
C=1
). Assume that it holds for
r_{k}
. Then, using (
4.1
) we have

\displaystyle\psi(r_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\psi(r_{k})+\Lambda r_{k}^{\delta}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma C\left(\frac{r_{k}}{r_{0}}\right)^{\beta}\left(\psi(r_{0})+\Lambda r_{0}^{\beta}\right)+\Lambda r_{k}^{\delta}

\displaystyle=C_{\beta}\left(\frac{r_{k+1}}{r_{0}}\right)^{\beta}\left(\psi(r_{0})+\Lambda r_{0}^{\beta}\right)\left(\frac{\gamma}{\theta^{\beta}}+\frac{1}{C}\frac{r_{k}^{\delta}}{r_{k+1}^{\beta}}\right)

\displaystyle=C\left(\frac{r_{k+1}}{r_{0}}\right)^{\beta}\left(\psi(r_{0})+\Lambda r_{0}^{\beta}\right)\left(\frac{\gamma}{\theta^{\beta}}+\frac{1}{C\theta^{\beta}}r_{0}^{\delta-\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r_{0}\leq 1}}{{\leq}}C\left(\frac{r_{k+1}}{r_{0}}\right)^{\beta}\left(\psi(r_{0})+\Lambda r_{0}^{\beta}\right)\left(\frac{\gamma}{\theta^{\beta}}+\frac{1}{C\theta^{\beta}}\right).

Since
\theta
and
\beta
are fixed, by (
4.3
) we can find
C
large enough such that

\frac{\gamma}{\theta^{\beta}}+\frac{1}{C\theta^{\beta}}\leq 1.

This concludes the proof of (
4.2
) for
r_{k+1}
.
∎

We are now in position to show the main regularity theorem we shall need in the sequel.

Definition 4.2
.

For
\eta\geq 0
, we say that
E
is a (volume constrained) approximable minimizer of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta}_{Q}
if
|E|=|B_{1}|
, and up to translation there exist,
r>0
,
\psi\in W^{2,2}(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2})\cap{\rm Lip}(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2})
and
E_{n}
smooth such that
\partial E=\psi(\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2})
,
\partial E_{n}
converges to
\partial E
in Hausdorff distance, (
3.24
) holds and

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta}_{Q}(E)=\inf\{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\eta,\Lambda}_{Q}(F)\ :\ F\ \textrm{smooth}\}.

Remark 4.3
.

As a consequence of Proposition
3.9
, for every
\eta>0
there exists approximable minimizers of
\mathcal{F}^{\eta,\Lambda}_{Q}
.

We write every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
as
x=(x^{\prime},x_{3})
and denote
B^{\prime}_{r}=\{|x^{\prime}|<r\}
the two-dimensional ball of radius
r
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\eta\in[0,1]
and let
Q_{0}>0
be the constant provided by Proposition
3.9
. Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
, and
\beta\in(0,1)
not depending on
\eta
such that for every
Q\leq Q_{0}
, if
E
is an approximable minimizer of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{\eta,\Lambda}
such that
0\in\partial E
and for some
r_{0}\in(0,1)
,

\int_{\partial E\cap B_{r_{0}}}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}\leq\varepsilon^{2},

(4.4)

then
\partial E\cap B_{r_{0}/2}
is a
C^{1,\beta}
surface in the sense that up to a rotation,

\partial E\cap B_{r_{0}/2}=\{(x^{\prime},u(x^{\prime}))\,:\,u\in C^{1,\beta}(B^{\prime}_{r_{0}/2})\}.

Moreover there holds

r_{0}^{\beta}\|u\|_{C^{1,\beta}(B_{r_{0}/2})}\lesssim 1.

(4.5)

As a consequence every approximable minimizer has boundary of class
C^{1,\beta}
.

Proof.

The proof follows closely the arguments from
[
39
]
.
Let
E_{n}
be a sequence of smooth sets such that
\partial E_{n}
converges to
\partial E
in Hausdorff distance and (
3.24
) holds. In particular, for every
1\geq\rho>0
and every smooth set
F_{n}
such that
F_{n}\Delta E_{n}\subset B_{\rho}
, setting

\delta_{n}=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{\eta,\Lambda}(E_{n})-\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{\eta}(E)\to 0,

we have

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{\eta,\Lambda}(E_{n})=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{\eta}(E)+\delta_{n}\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{\eta,\Lambda}(F_{n})+\delta_{n}.

Rearranging terms we get

\int_{\partial E_{n}\cap B_{\rho}}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}\leq\int_{\partial F_{n}\cap B_{\rho}}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}+Q^{2}(\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(E_{n})-\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(F_{n}))+\Lambda|E_{n}\Delta F_{n}|+\delta_{n}.

(4.6)

We now claim that

\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(E_{n})-\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(F_{n})\lesssim\rho^{3-\alpha},

(4.7)

where the implicit multiplicative constant is independent of
\eta
.
For this, we appeal to
[
18
, Lemma 3.5]
(see also
[
28
, Lemma 2]
or
[
29
, Lemma 13]
) to get that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(E_{n})-\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(F_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(E_{n}\cap F_{n})-\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(F_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(E_{n}\cap F_{n}))^{2}}{\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(F_{n}\setminus E_{n})}.

On the one hand, by Hausdorff convergence there exists
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
B_{1/2}(\bar{x})\subset E_{n}\cap F_{n}
for
n
large enough and thus
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(E_{n}\cap F_{n})\leq\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(B_{1/2})\lesssim 1
(recall that
\eta\leq 1
). On the other hand, since
F_{n}\setminus E_{n}\subset B_{\rho}
, we have

\frac{1}{\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(F_{n}\setminus E_{n})}\leq\frac{1}{\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(B_{\rho})}\leq\frac{1}{\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(B_{\rho})}\lesssim\rho^{3-\alpha}.

This concludes the proof of (
4.7
). Combining this with (
4.6
),
|E_{n}\Delta F_{n}|\lesssim\rho^{3}
and the fact that
\Lambda\lesssim Q_{0}^{2}
, we obtain the quasi-minimality property,

\int_{\partial E_{n}\cap B_{\rho}}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}\leq\int_{\partial F_{n}\cap B_{\rho}}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}+CQ_{0}^{2}\rho^{3-\alpha}+\delta_{n},

(4.8)

for every
\rho\in(0,1]
and every smooth set
F_{n}
with
E_{n}\Delta F_{n}\subset B_{\rho}
.

We now recall that sending
\rho\to\infty
in
[
39
, (1.3)]
we have for every
\rho>0
,

\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial E_{n}\cap B_{\rho})\lesssim\rho^{2}.

(4.9)

Therefore, there exists a universal
\varepsilon>0
such that
[
39
, Lemma 2.1]
applies to
E_{n}
with
2\varepsilon
. Assume that (
4.4
) holds for
r_{0}>0
and let
r\leq r_{0}
(so that (
4.4
) also holds for
r
). Since
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial E\cap\partial B_{\rho})=0
for a.e.
\rho
, using the second part of (
3.24
), we see that (
4.4
) also holds for
E_{n}
with
2\varepsilon
provided
n
is large enough. Moreover, there exists
\rho\in(r/2,r)
for which

\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial E_{n}\cap\partial B_{\rho})\leq\frac{2}{r}\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial E_{n}\cap B_{r})\lesssim\rho.

In particular there is a universal
\theta\in(0,1/2)
such that by
[
39
, Lemma 1.4]
,
\partial E_{n}\cap B_{\theta\rho}
is connected. By
[
39
, Lemma 2.1]
, we can find
\sigma\in(\theta\rho/2,\theta\rho)
such that
\partial E_{n}\cap B_{\sigma}
is a topological disk
D_{1}^{(n)}
with
\partial E_{n}\cap\partial B_{\sigma}
coinciding with the graph of a function
u_{n}
. Considering the surface
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}=(\partial E_{n}\backslash D_{1}^{(n)})\cup{\rm graph}\,w_{n}
where
w_{n}
is the biharmonic function coinciding with
u_{n}
on
\partial B_{\sigma}
we see that
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{n}=\partial F_{n}
for some
C^{1,1}
set
F_{n}
with
F_{n}\Delta E_{n}\subset B_{\sigma}
. Moreover, by
[
39
, Lemma 2.2]
, we have arguing exactly as in
[
39
]
,

\int_{\partial F_{n}\cap B_{\sigma}}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}\leq C\int_{\partial E_{n}\cap(B_{\theta\rho}\backslash B_{\theta\rho/2})}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}.

Combining this with (
4.8
) we find

\int_{\partial E_{n}\cap B_{\sigma}}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}\leq C\int_{\partial E_{n}\cap(B_{\theta\rho}\backslash B_{\theta\rho/2})}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}+CQ_{0}^{2}\rho^{3-\alpha}+\delta_{n}.

Adding
C
times the left-hand side to this inequality and dividing by
1+C
we get

\int_{\partial E_{n}\cap B_{\theta\rho/2}}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}\leq\int_{\partial E_{n}\cap B_{\sigma}}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}\leq\gamma\int_{\partial E_{n}\cap B_{\theta\rho}}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}+CQ_{0}^{2}\rho^{3-\alpha}+\delta_{n},

where
\gamma=C/(C+1)\in(0,1)
. Setting

\psi(\rho)=\int_{\partial E\cap B_{\rho}}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}

and sending
n\to\infty
in the previous inequality, thanks to (
3.24
) we find (recall that
\rho\in(r/2,r)
and that
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial E\cap\partial B_{\rho})=0
for a.e.
\rho
)

\psi(\theta r/4)\leq\psi(\theta\rho/2)\leq\gamma\psi(\theta\rho)+CQ_{0}^{2}\rho^{3-\alpha}\leq\gamma\psi(r)+CQ_{0}^{2}r^{3-\alpha}.

Applying Lemma
4.1
with
\theta^{\prime}=\theta/4
, we get the existence of
\beta\in(0,3-\alpha)
such that

\psi(r)\lesssim\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{\beta}(\psi(r_{0})+r_{0}^{\beta})\lesssim\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{\beta}.

Arguing as for
[
39
, (3.5)]
, we find that up to a rotation,

\partial E\cap B_{r_{0}/2}=\{(x^{\prime},u(x^{\prime})),x^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}_{r_{0}/2}\}

and for every
r\leq r_{0}/2
, there exists
\tau=\tau(r)
such that

\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{B^{\prime}_{r}}|Du-\tau|^{2}\lesssim\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{\beta/2}.

Repeating the argument for every
x\in\partial E\cap B_{r_{0}/2}
we find by Campanato criterion that
Du\in C^{0,\beta/4}(B^{\prime}_{r_{0}/2})
with

r_{0}^{\frac{\beta}{4}}\|u\|_{C^{1,\beta/4}(B_{r_{0}/2})}\lesssim 1.

Up to renaming
\beta
this concludes the proof of (
4.5
).

∎

Corollary 4.5
.

There exists
Q_{0}>0
such that for
Q<Q_{0}
and
\eta\in[0,1]
any approximable minimizer of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{\eta,\Lambda}
is a
C^{1,\beta}
regular nearly spherical set with uniformly bounded (with respect to
Q
and
\eta
)
C^{1,\beta}
norm.

Proof.

Let us notice that for any
\varepsilon>0
one can find
r_{0}>0
such that for every
x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
,

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap B_{r_{0}}(x)}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}\leq\varepsilon^{2}/2.

(4.10)

We claim that if
Q_{0}
is small enough then for every
Q\leq Q_{0}
and every approximable
E
of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}
, up to translation

\partial E=\{(1+\phi(x))x\ :x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\}

for some function
\phi
with
\|\phi\|_{C^{1,\beta}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim 1
, where
\beta>0
is given by Theorem
4.4
. Indeed, assume this is not the case then by a covering argument and Theorem
4.4
there would exist
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
and a sequence
E_{Q}
of approximable minimizers of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{\eta_{Q}}
with
Q\to 0
and such that
\bar{x}\in\partial E_{Q}
with

\int_{\partial E_{Q}\cap B_{r_{0}}(\bar{x})}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}\geq\varepsilon^{2}.

(4.11)

However, by Proposition
3.9
, for every
Q
there exist
r_{Q},\psi_{Q}
such that
\psi_{Q}
is conformal,
\partial E_{Q}=\psi_{Q}(\partial B_{r_{Q}})
and

|r_{Q}-1|+\|\psi_{Q}-{\rm Id}\|^{2}_{W^{2,2}(\partial B_{r_{Q}})}+\|h_{Q}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial B_{r_{Q}})}\lesssim Q^{2}.

Thus
\psi_{Q}
converges
W^{2,2}
to
{\rm Id}
and
\int_{\partial E_{Q}}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}\to\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}
which then implies that also

\int_{\partial E_{Q}\cap B_{r_{0}}(\bar{x})}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}\to\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap B_{r_{0}}(\bar{x})}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}.

In light of (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) this gives a contradiction if
Q
is small enough.
∎

5.
Proof of the main result

We now prove the main result of the paper.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
Q_{0}>0
be the constant given in Proposition
3.9
. Let
E^{\eta}
be an approximable minimizer for
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{\eta}
, for
\eta>0
. Up to decrease further
Q
we can suppose that the hypotheses of Theorem
4.4
applies so that by Corollary
4.5
we know that

\partial E^{\eta}=\{(1+\phi_{\eta}(x))x\ :x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\}

for some
\phi_{\eta}
with
\|\phi_{\eta}\|_{C^{1,\beta}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}
uniformly bounded in
\eta
and
Q
.

By Arzela-Ascoli we can find, for any
\widetilde{\beta}<\beta
, a sequence
\eta_{n}>0
with
\eta_{n}\to 0
such that
\phi_{\eta_{n}}
converges to
\phi
in
C^{1,\widetilde{\beta}}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
. As a consequence, setting
E_{n}=E^{\eta_{n}}
and

\partial E=\{(1+\phi(x))x\ :x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\}

we have that
E_{n}\to E
in Hausdorff and in
L^{1}
. Notice that since
|E^{\eta}|=|B_{1}|
we also have
|E|=|B_{1}|
.

Let us prove that
E
is an approximable minimizer of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}
. Since
E_{n}
are approximable minimizers, there exists
r_{n}
and
\psi_{n}\in W^{2,2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}_{r_{n}})\cap{\rm Lip}(\mathbb{S}^{2}_{r_{n}})
with
\partial E_{n}=\psi_{n}(\mathbb{S}^{2}_{r_{n}})
, such that (
3.22
) holds.
Let
\psi
be the limit of
\psi_{n}
and
r=\lim_{n}r_{n}
. By Proposition
3.2
, Lemma
3.4
and the fact that
E
is
C^{1,\beta}
, there exists a sequence of smooth sets
\widetilde{E}_{n}
such that (
3.24
) holds with
\eta=0
. In particular we have

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}(E)\geq\inf\left\{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\Lambda}_{Q}(F)\ :\ F\textrm{ smooth}\right\}.

Let us prove the converse inequality. Since
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}^{\eta}(E_{n})\geq\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(E_{n})
and
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(E_{n})\to\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(E)
by Lemma
3.4
again and the fact that
E
is
C^{1,\beta}
. By lower semicontinuity of
\int_{\partial E}|A|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}
we find

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}(E)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{\eta_{n},\Lambda}(E_{n}).

(5.1)

If now
F
is a smooth compact set then using the fact that
E_{n}
are approximable minimizers and (
5.1
) we have

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}(E)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{\eta_{n}}(E_{n})\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{\eta_{n},\Lambda}(F).

Using
[
16
, Proposition 2.16]
and a diagonal argument we see that for smooth sets
F
we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{\eta_{n},\Lambda}(F)=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{\Lambda}(F)
. We conclude that

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}(E)=\inf\left\{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\Lambda}_{Q}(F)\ :\ F\textrm{ smooth}\right\}.

By Lemma
3.7
and Proposition
3.8
we conclude that we also have

{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}(E)=\inf\left\{\mathcal{F}_{Q}(F)\ :\ |F|=|B_{1}|,\ F\textrm{ smooth}\right\}.

This proves the existence of approximable (volume-constrained) minimizers of
{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}
. Moreover, Corollary
4.5
implies that all such approximable minimizers are
C^{1,\beta}
regular nearly-spherical sets with
C^{1,\beta}
norm uniformly converging to
0
as
Q\to 0
. Let now
E
be any such approximable minimizer.
Testing the minimality against
F=B_{1}
we have after rearranging the terms

W(E)-W(B_{1})\leq Q^{2}(\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(B_{1})-\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(E)).

(5.2)

Let us bound the left-hand side from below. Since
E
is an approximable minimizer there exists a sequence of smooth sets
E_{n}
with
W(E_{n})\to W(E)
and
P(E_{n})\to P(E)
. Let
r_{n}\to 1
be such that
|B_{r_{n}}|=|E_{n}|
. By
[
34
]
,

P(E_{n})-P(B_{r_{n}})\lesssim(W(E_{n})-W(B_{r_{n}})),

so that sending
n\to\infty
we find

P(E)-P(B_{1})\lesssim(W(E)-W(B_{1})).

(5.3)

Using that
E
is a nearly spherical set, we have by
[
16
, Proposition 5.5]
in the case
\alpha=2
(see also
[
32
, Lemma 2.2]
for a stronger statement) and by
[
18
, Proposition 4.5.]
for
\alpha\in(0,2)
that the right-hand side of (
5.2
) may be bounded from above by

\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(B_{1})-\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(E)\lesssim(P(E)-P(B_{1})).

(5.4)

Plugging (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) into (
5.2
) yields

(P(E)-P(B_{1}))\lesssim Q^{2}(P(E)-P(B_{1}))

which for
Q
small enough implies
P(E)=P(B_{1})
and thus up to translation,
E=B_{1}
.
∎

Metabolic engineering for tobacco quality improvement: Advances and perspectives
Authors: Jie Liu, Qi Sheng, Kejuan Zhang, Jun Zhang, Xinsheng Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
As a major cash crop, tobacco quality improvement is constrained by the limited effectiveness of conventional approaches, including traditional breeding and agronomic regulation. This review focuses on the application of metabolic engineering to enable precise and targeted modulation of tobacco quality traits. We first analyze the key factors and underlying mechanisms governing aroma, health-related components, functional metabolites, and combustion characteristics. We then summarize recent advances in metabolic engineering strategies, including pathway rewiring, metabolic flux redistribution, enzyme engineering, and regulatory-network modulation, for optimizing the biosynthesis and accumulation of target compounds such as terpenoids, polyphenols, and alkaloids. In addition, selected integrative approaches that support metabolic regulation and quality formation are briefly discussed. Finally, we highlight current challenges and outline future research directions to facilitate more precise and efficient improvement of tobacco quality.

Introduction

Tobacco, a globally significant cash crop (Lu et al., 2012b; Mavroeidis et al., 2024), is cultivated in over 120 countries worldwide, with the global tobacco industry's annual output value reaching approximately $941 billion in 2023 and projected to exceed $1.1 trillion by 2034 (Ren et al., 2020). According to the World Health Organization's 2023 agricultural economics report, tobacco-related sectors contribute approximately 1% of global agricultural value added, underscoring its substantial socioeconomic footprint. Endowed with a rapid growth cycle, high biomass productivity, and remarkable secondary metabolite diversity (Banožić et al., 2020a, 2020b), tobacco has traditionally anchored the cigarette manufacturing sector while emerging as a promising platform for pharmaceutical production (Banožić et al., 2019; EsentÜRk-GÜZel et al., 2022; Mathew and Thomas, 2022; Naeem et al., 2023; Tremblay et al., 2010), biofuel development (An et al., 2025; García-Martínez et al., 2017; Grisan et al., 2016; Sophanodorn et al., 2020; Wu et al., 2022), and pesticide innovation (Liu et al., 2025a). Given its multifaceted economic significance and application versatility, strategic quality improvement initiatives are critical to enhancing industrial competitiveness and unlocking new application frontiers for tobacco-derived products.

Tobacco quality serves as a critical determinant of consumer acceptance and product market value (Chalak et al., 2023; Gilmore et al., 2013; Hiscock et al., 2018; Saenz-de-Miera et al., 2024), encompassing four interconnected assessment dimensions: aromatic quality, health-related attributes, functional properties, and combustion performance (Zhang et al., 2023b; Zhou et al., 2024b). Contemporary quality enhancement paradigms predominantly rely on traditional breeding, agronomic optimization, and chemical modulation strategies (Chen et al., 2019b, 2025; Hu et al., 2021; Ren et al., 2020; Tian et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2024). While conventional breeding and agronomic practices can incrementally improve certain quality parameters, they are constrained by prolonged generation cycles (typically 5-8 years), polygenic trait complexity, and imprecise phenotypic targeting—limitations that preclude fine-tuned metabolic control (Burner et al., 2022; D.Raveh, 1983; Ding et al., 2024; Gao et al., 2019; Hu et al., 2021; Kutchan, 1995; Li et al., 2024; Wei et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2023c). Conversely, chemical regulation enables relatively precise phenotypic modulation but introduces risks of phytochemical residue accumulation and ecological perturbation (Li, 2022; Zou et al., 2022). Given these inherent limitations in current improvement methodologies, there exists an urgent imperative to develop an innovative technical framework that synergizes regulatory precision with environmental sustainability to establish next-generation quality enhancement protocols (Kajikawa et al., 2017; Van Eck, 2020; Zhang et al., 2023a).

Metabolic engineering is an innovative interdisciplinary field that integrates systems biology and genetic modification strategies in microbial, animal, and plant systems. (Chen et al., 2017; Volk et al., 2022). Its core technical paradigm involves the rational rewiring of cellular metabolic networks through precision gene editing, enabling spatiotemporal control over target compound biosynthesis (Barone et al., 2020; Kutchan, 1995; Lu et al., 2016b; Zan et al., 2025). A defining advantage of this approach lies in its capacity for “on-demand” customization of tobacco phytochemical profiles while maintaining high efficiency and environmental sustainability (Salim et al., 2023; Selma et al., 2023; Yoon et al., 2013). Notably, metabolic reprogramming strategies have enabled tobacco to accumulate resveratrol, a bioactive polyphenol, at concentrations reaching 40 μg/L—a level with potential nutraceutical applications (Hidalgo et al., 2017). In another representative study, RNA interference-mediated suppression of the nicotine demethylase-encoding gene resulted in a 6-fold reduction in N-nitrosonornicotine (NNN; p < 0.01) and 50% decrease in total tobacco-specific nitrosamines (TSNAs), addressing critical toxicological concerns (Lewis et al., 2008). Collectively, these advancements demonstrate that systematic metabolic network reconstruction provides a precise, efficient, and predictable technical framework for tobacco quality enhancement, overcoming inherent limitations of conventional methodologies (Guo et al., 2022; Van Beirs et al., 2025; Wilson and Roberts, 2014; Zhou et al., 2024a).

This review presents a systematic analysis of the core challenges and underlying mechanisms constraining tobacco quality improvement, followed by a comprehensive discussion of metabolic engineering strategies developed to address these bottlenecks (Fig. 1). In this review, “metabolic engineering” primarily refers to strategies that directly reprogram cellular metabolism through pathway reconstruction, metabolic flux redistribution, enzyme engineering, and regulatory-network manipulation to modulate the biosynthesis, accumulation, or transformation of target metabolites. In addition to these core approaches, several adjacent enabling strategies, including optogenetic regulation, rhizosphere engineering, synthetic microbial communities, and processing-associated interventions, are also discussed due to their important supporting roles in metabolic regulation and product quality optimization. Finally, we summarize current advances and propose future research directions to promote methodological innovation in tobacco metabolic engineering. Collectively, the insights presented herein provide a potential technological upgrading framework for the tobacco industry while offering a broader paradigm for the precision improvement of cash crops through metabolism-oriented engineering strategies.Fig. 1The application of metabolic engineering in the comprehensive enhancement of tobacco quality.

Metabolic engineering of tobacco for improving aroma quality

Challenges in improving the aroma quality of tobacco

Tobacco aroma formation hinges on the synergistic interactions of complex secondary metabolites, with terpenoids, phenylpropanoids, and lipids representing the three primary compositional pillars (Banožić et al., 2020b; Kessler et al., 2006). Among these, terpenoids serve as the principal determinants of aroma quality, while phenylpropanoids and lipids modulate aromatic complexity through combinatorial effects (Chen et al., 2011; Hao et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2024a; Yan et al., 2018, 2022; Zou et al., 2023). Consequently, enhancing terpenoid biosynthesis via metabolic engineering strategies constitutes a critical approach for aroma quality improvement (Cheng et al., 2022b). However, the low odor thresholds (0.01-0.1 ppb) and structural diversity of terpenoids render their metabolic regulation a prominent research challenge (Dudareva et al., 2004; Lu et al., 2016b; Lücker et al., 2004; Slaghenaufi and Ugliano, 2018). Current bottlenecks in terpenoid biosynthesis include: (1) Suboptimal metabolic flux partitioning; (2) Volatile loss and spatiotemporal mismatch between synthesis and storage; and (3) Metabolic crosstalk between lignin and terpene biosynthesis (Dudareva et al., 2007). These issues arise from three interconnected mechanisms: (1) Competitive allocation of precursor molecules isopentenyl pyrophosphate (IPP) and dimethylallyl pyrophosphate (DMAPP) creates flux imbalance (Hemmerlin et al., 2003). These universal precursors must simultaneously support biosynthesis of essential metabolites (sterols, chlorophyll), limiting carbon partitioning toward terpenoid production. Metabolic flux analysis reveals that only 12-15% of total IPP/DMAPP flux is directed to terpenoid biosynthesis under native conditions (Chen et al., 2024; Choi et al., 2012; Krause et al., 2023; Lu et al., 2012a; Newman and Chappell, 2008; Wang et al., 2019). (2) Terpenoids exhibit high volatility and undergo subcellular compartmentalization between synthetic (mesophyll cells) and storage (glandular trichomes, vacuoles) sites (Tanney et al., 2021). Monoterpenes and sesquiterpenes, with their high vapor pressures, suffer significant volatile losses due to inefficient transport mechanisms. This challenge is exacerbated by asynchronous development of synthetic and storage organs, with trichome maturation lagging behind peak terpenoid synthesis by 3-5 days (Gossart et al., 2023; Marie et al., 2000; Pierman et al., 2017). (3) Lignin biosynthesis creates metabolic competition through the consumption of 4-coumaroyl-CoA, a key intermediate in the phenylpropanoid pathway that is also required for the biosynthesis of flavonoids and other phenylpropanoid-derived compounds. (Li et al., 2020b). Developmental studies show that 4CL flux toward lignin increases from 30% to 65% during stem maturation, directly correlating with reduced terpenoid accumulation (Vanholme et al., 2019). Conversely, biotic stress induces a 2.3-fold increase in terpene biosynthesis, diverting 4CL away from lignin and reducing stem mechanical strength by 18% (Bonawitz and Chapple, 2010; Li et al., 2016). Strategy I: Directed enhancement of key pathways

The directed enhancement of key pathways aims to overcome the limitations of precursor supply through targeted activation of rate-limiting enzymes and metabolic compartmentalization design, primarily encompassing two methods (Fig. 2a): (1) Overexpression of rate-limiting enzymes or inhibition of competitive pathways; (2) Enhancement of the precursors supply and subcellular compartmentalization (Xu et al., 2025). In plant metabolic engineering, overexpressing rate-limiting enzymes and inhibiting competitive pathways are effective approaches to enhance the biosynthesis efficiency of target metabolites. For instance, overexpression of the ERF transcription factor GbERF4 significantly upregulates the expression of key enzyme genes such as HMGS, AACT, DXS, and GGPPS in the terpene trilactone (TTL) biosynthesis pathway, thereby promoting TTL accumulation (Zheng et al., 2024). In artemisinin synthesis research, using the CRISPR-Cas system to inhibit key genes in the competitive branch (such as squalene synthase SDS) can redirect metabolic flux toward artemisinin synthesis, increasing its content by 71-77% (Zheng et al., 2024). The above cases provide valuable reference for strengthening the key synthetic pathways of tobacco. Notably, subcellular compartmentalization of key enzymes not only avoids feedback inhibition caused by intermediate product accumulation but also enhances catalytic efficiency by optimizing the microenvironment for enzymatic reactions (Chen et al., 2023a; Liu et al., 2024; Tata et al., 2015; Yao et al., 2023a). For example, localizing GGPP synthase (GGPPS) to chloroplasts can reduce the allocation of the diterpene precursor GGPP to the cytoplasmic sterol synthesis pathway (Zhou et al., 2017). Another typical strategy is targeting monoterpene synthases to lipid droplets, thereby enhancing the accumulation of monoterpenoids. In a representative case, by fusing monoterpene synthase with the microalgal lipid droplet surface protein NOLDSP and achieving lipid droplet-targeted expression in tobacco leaves, monoterpene accumulation was increased by 3.8-fold, with products “trapped” in lipid droplets to reduce toxicity (Sadre et al., 2019). This “biosynthesis-storage” integrated system provides a novel approach for constructing plant cell factories for high-value terpenoids.

Strategy II: Dynamic balance and regulation of metabolic networks

The dynamic balance and regulation of metabolic networks can be achieved through synthetic gene circuits and light/chemical induction systems to enable precise allocation of carbon metabolic flux. The core approaches include (Fig. 2b): (1) Dynamic regulation of gene circuits; (2) Optogenetics and induction systems (Reed and Osbourn, 2018). Both approaches have been validated in plant systems and proven effective in maintaining metabolic balance for terpenoid synthesis (Li et al., 2023). Regulating carbon metabolic flux distribution based on gene circuits represents an effective means of maintaining the dynamic balance of terpenoid metabolism (Park et al., 2017; Xu et al., 2023). A typical case involves constructing an “acetyl-CoA sensing-feedback” circuit in Nicotiana benthamiana leaves, which increased taxadiene content from 18 to 72 μg/g/DW (approximately a 4-fold increase), while total fatty acid content decreased by 12% without significant changes in chlorophyll content or fresh weight (Chen et al., 2022b). This demonstrates that the circuit can automatically “push-pull” carbon flux in plants while maintaining physiological homeostasis. The synergistic application of optogenetic tools and induction systems provides an innovative strategy for spatiotemporal-specific regulation of terpenoid metabolism. The light-controlled switch system consisting of phytochrome B (phyB) and phytochrome-interacting factor (PIF) enables reversible regulation through red/far-red light irradiation. After coupling this system with terpene synthase (TPS) genes, precise induction of terpene synthesis can be achieved under UV-B illumination (Chen et al., 2022a). Related studies have shown that such dynamic regulation can effectively avoid plant growth inhibition caused by constitutive expression (Qiu et al., 2017; Shi et al., 2024). Furthermore, addressing the limitation of light-controlled systems in cloudy/rainy weather, a dual-factor regulatory network integrated with temperature-responsive promoters (e.g., HSP) can compensate for metabolic efficiency under insufficient light intensity through heat shock signals. In tobacco models, similar temperature compensation strategies increased nighttime terpene synthesis efficiency to 68% of daytime levels, significantly reducing metabolic network fluctuation coefficients (CV value decreased from 0.41 to 0.18) (Martina et al., 2016). This dynamic induction strategy markedly enhances the controllability and stability of metabolic networks, providing valuable insights for metabolic engineering modifications in tobacco.

Strategy III: Microbial-plant cross-species metabolic synergistic design

The emerging metabolic engineering strategies have created a new model for the efficient synthesis of terpenoids by integrating the advantages of cross-species metabolic networks, mainly including two methods (Fig. 2c): (1) Heterologous enzyme application in plants; and (2) Engineered microbe-plant interaction systems (Kwon et al., 2003; Wang et al., 2020b). Reconstructing cross-species metabolic pathways by introducing heterologous enzyme genes into plants represents a viable strategy to enhance target compound production (Lücker et al., 2004). A typical example is the introduction of (+)-valencene synthase gene from Citrus into Nicotiana tabacum, successfully establishing a functional monoterpene synthesis system. Through optimizing chloroplast-targeted expression strategy, this study achieved (+)-valencene yield of 0.24 μg/mg fresh weight in transgenic tobacco leaves, representing a 2.8-fold increase compared to controls (Yin et al., 2016). This achievement confirms that cross-species metabolic pathway reconstruction can effectively overcome the limitations of plant endogenous metabolic regulation, providing crucial technical support for heterologous efficient synthesis of terpenoids. Developing engineered microbial consortia that form beneficial interactions with tobacco has been proven effective in improving tobacco quality (Yin et al., 2025). For instance, inoculating cigar tobacco leaves with Acinetobacter sp. 1H8 and Acinetobacter indicus 3B2 strains accelerated protein degradation and carbohydrate metabolism, promoting the production of key aroma precursors such as phenylalanine and lysine. Experimental data showed that treated tobacco leaves exhibited 24.6% and 12.6% increases in total sugar and neutral aroma compound contents, respectively, while protein content decreased by 19.3%. More importantly, sensory evaluation indicated that this microbial treatment extended tobacco combustion time by 22% and significantly reduced smoke irritation, offering a novel biological solution for cigar quality improvement (Zheng et al., 2022b).

Metabolic engineering of tobacco for improving health quality

Challenges in improving the health quality of tobacco

Nicotine and tobacco-specific nitrosamines (TSNAs) are two key toxicants that determine the health quality of tobacco products (Goniewicz et al., 2018; Liang et al., 2022a; Mocniak et al., 2022; Shahab et al., 2017). Minimizing their accumulation in leaf tissue is therefore central to improving health quality of the tobacco. At present, three major bottlenecks hamper rational modulation of these metabolites: (1) Metabolic-function conflict, (2) Precursor cross-risk, and (3) Spatiotemporal regulation difficulties. The underlying causes are as follows. (1) Metabolic conflict between alkaloid biosynthesis and defense response. The competition arises from an evolutionary trade-off between limited metabolic resources and the hierarchical allocation of defense strategies. Putrescine is a shared precursor for both nicotine and polyamines (defense-related metabolites). Under herbivore attack, putrescine is preferentially channelled into nicotine biosynthesis via putrescine N-methyltransferase (PMT), depriving the polyamine pool and destabilizing the antioxidant system (Bian et al., 2022; Cane et al., 2005; Dalton et al., 2016; Michael et al., 2004; Shoji et al., 2024; Zheng et al., 2022a). For example, exposing Nicotiana tabacum cv. SR1 roots to 200 μM methyl jasmonate for 6 h up-regulated PMT1 expression 3.1-fold and increased leaf nicotine 1.7-fold within 48 h, whereas putrescine and spermidine pools dropped by 38% and 31%, respectively. Concomitantly, leaf H2O2 rose 1.4-fold and electrolyte leakage (a proxy for membrane oxidation) increased by 24% relative to controls, demonstrating that nicotine induction compromises ROS scavenging and exacerbates oxidative injury (Seung and Smith, 2019). (2) Cross-risk between TSNA precursors and primary nitrogen assimilation. Nitrate and nitrite serve as direct precursors for TSNA formation, yet their levels are tightly coupled to general nitrogen assimilation. The nitrate transceptor NRT1.1 dynamically re-locates between plasma and tonoplast membranes to regulate root-to-shoot nitrate allocation, thereby dictating the spatiotemporal size of the TSNA precursor pool (Asim et al., 2020a, 2020b; Fang et al., 2016; Hervé Vaucheret, 1997; Rothe et al., 2003; Sun et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2020c). Co-suppression of nitrate reductase (NR), for instance, elevated leaf nitrate from 1.8 to 3.2 mg/g/DW and produced a pronounced chlorotic phenotype: photosynthetic efficiency declined 21 %, the chlorophyll a/b ratio fell from 3.2 to 2.1, and residual NR activity was only 17% of wild-type. Conversely, forcing nitrate below 1.2 mg/g/DW through ammonium-dominated fertilisation perturbed nitrogen homeostasis—total free amino acids decreased 35%, soluble leaf protein 28%, and total biomass 19 %. These data illustrate the dilemma: suppressing TSNA precursors impairs basic nitrogen metabolism, whereas sustaining vigorous nitrogen assimilation inevitably expands the precursor reservoir (de Borne Dorlhac et al., 1994). (3) Spatiotemporal specificity of nicotine synthesis and transport. Nicotine is synthesized almost exclusively in root protoxylem parenchyma and loaded into the xylem for shoot delivery, a process gated by apoplastic pH and transporters such as NtJAT1. Systemic disruption of this transport perturbs whole-plant ion homeostasis (Masahiko Moritaa, 2009; Song et al., 2024; Tanaka et al., 2021; van Veen et al., 2014; Xu et al., 2017). RNAi-mediated down-regulation of the root MATE transporter NtMATE1 (∼60% transcript reduction) lowered nicotine export to the shoot by 52%, raised xylem sap pH from 5.7 to 6.3, decreased root PMA3-H+-ATPase activity by 41 %, and produced leaf-margin necrosis (necrotic index 0.26 vs. 0.03 in controls). Thus, blunt manipulation of either synthesis or transport triggers cascading metabolic disorders, underscoring the need for tissue-specific and temporally precise engineering strategies (Shoji et al., 2009). Strategy I: Targeted gene editing and specific regulation

Targeted gene editing and specific regulation focus on the precise manipulation of metabolic network nodes, primarily encompassing two approaches (Fig. 3a): (1) Specific gene regulation systems and (2) Nitrate metabolic reprogramming (Bortesi and Fischer, 2015). Spatiotemporal precision intervention in tobacco metabolism through specific gene regulation technologies represents an effective method for decoupling nicotine synthesis and defense functions (Gavilano et al., 2006; Lewis et al., 2020; Qin et al., 2021; Shoji et al., 2020, 2022; Sui et al., 2020; Xu et al., 2017). For instance, root-specific promoters (e.g., NtR12) drive RNA interference of the key nicotine synthesis gene (PMT), enabling tissue-specific suppression of nicotine synthesis while preserving leaf defense protein activity (Cai et al., 2022). On the other hand, nitrate metabolic reprogramming in tobacco can be achieved by reconstructing nitrogen allocation pathways (Bovet et al., 2022, 2024; Castelli et al., 2010; Fan et al., 2017; Hafeez et al., 2025; Law et al., 2016; Rashid et al., 2020; Shi et al., 2013; Thakur et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2023). In one study, overexpression of a constitutively active NR variant (S523D) reduced tobacco leaf nitrate content by 60-70%, decreased TSNAs in cured leaves by 45-55%, and lowered NNK and NNN in cigarette smoke by 35% and 40% respectively, with no significant difference in plant biomass compared to wild-type. These two complementary cases demonstrate that through tissue-specific silencing and nitrate metabolic reprogramming, harmful components such as nicotine and TSNAs can be reduced while maintaining tobacco growth and enhancing defense functions (Lu et al., 2016a). This provides a technical framework with potential for replication and scale-up for subsequent multi-trait improvement of tobacco.

Strategy II: Microbe-plant cross-species partnerships for toxin reduction

The construction of microbe-plant cross-species synergistic system aims to maintain the balance of plant nitrogen metabolism, mainly including two methods (Fig. 3b): (1) Rhizosphere engineering bacteria design (Li et al., 2017); (2) Synthetic microbial community (Liang et al., 2022b). Directional modification of rhizosphere engineering bacteria has been proven to be an effective method to promote plant nitrogen uptake, healthy growth, and high-value metabolite synthesis (Jiang et al., 2024b; Xie et al., 2025). For example, the polyamine-metabolizing rhizobacterium Pseudomonas sp. GBPI_506 (isolated from wheat rhizosphere) achieves colonization by sensing putrescine secreted by plant roots, and then regulates plant hormone signals such as auxin (IAA), jasmonic acid (JA), and salicylic acid (SA), promotes lateral root development of Nicotiana benthamiana, and activates the nicotine synthesis pathway, significantly increasing nicotine content in leaves (Jain et al., 2023). This case reveals a new mechanism by which rhizobacteria regulate plant root architecture and secondary metabolism through the polyamine-hormone signaling axis. Synthetic microbial community (SynComs) technology aims to reconstruct the tobacco nitrogen cycle network through multi-species synergistic metabolism, enabling full-chain intervention from soil nitrogen cycle to processing product stability, highlighting the systematic advantages of microbial community engineering in metabolic network regulation (Teng et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2020a). In a representative case, after genome sequencing and metabolic model reconstruction of multiple strains isolated from plant rhizosphere (such as Pseudomonas, Bacillus, Streptomyces) (Jiang et al., 2021; Najme et al., 2021; Ruan et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2009a, 2012b), community-level flux balance analysis (cFBA) was used to predict the complementarity of different strain combinations in rhizosphere carbon source utilization, nitrogen cycling, plant hormone synthesis, etc. Finally, the model-optimized synthetic microbial community (SynCom) was inoculated into different plants (Nicotiana benthamiana, Arabidopsis thaliana and maize), comprehensively improving key indicators such as plant growth, nitrogen uptake, and immune activation (ISR) (Gonçalves et al., 2023).

Strategy III: Decoupling of defense response and toxin reduction functions

The decoupling of defense-detoxification functions aims to maintain the balanced optimization of tobacco harmful component control and biological defense capabilities, mainly including two methods (Fig. 3a): (1) Construction of artificial alternative insect-resistant pathways; (2) Bio-physical synergistic systems (Ikram et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2023a). The construction of artificial alternative insect-resistant pathways can effectively balance biological defense capabilities (Holeva et al., 2021; Lewis et al., 2008; Wang et al., 2008). In a study, RNA interference (RNAi) technology was used to specifically inhibit key genes in nicotine synthesis, resulting in over 95% inhibition of nicotine synthesis in tobacco plants and reducing nicotine accumulation to less than 0.3% of the wild type. On this basis, an efficient insect-resistant barrier was successfully established by heterologous expression of Bacillus thuringiensis Cry1Ac toxin or Galanthus nivalis agglutinin (GNA). Field trial data showed that the mortality rate of transgenic lines against Manduca sexta larvae remained at 82.3%, which was not statistically different from the 83.5% insect-resistant effect of the wild type, effectively alleviating the metabolic bottleneck of traditional insect-resistant defense relying on nicotine accumulation (Lewis et al., 2008). The bio-physical system helps to significantly reduce the accumulation of harmful substances in tobacco (O'Connor and Hurley, 2008). For example, the content of tar, nicotine, and CO in the mainstream smoke of a glycerol-containing tobacco alternative sheet was reduced by 16-20%, acrylonitrile, ammonia, HCN, etc. were reduced by 33-51%, while tobacco-specific nitrosamines (TSNAs) were reduced by 25-32%. Notably, human exposure experiments showed a 14-18% reduction in nicotine intake and a 29% decrease in NNK (a strong carcinogen) exposure (McAdam et al., 2011).

Metabolic engineering of tobacco for improving functional quality

Challenges in improving the functional quality of tobacco

The targeted accumulation of vitamins and antioxidant substances in tobacco not only demonstrates the health-promoting properties of tobacco but also opens up its potential for cross-disciplinary applications in functional foods, pharmaceutical raw materials, and other fields. This facilitates the upgrading of tobacco from a traditional consumer product to a production platform for high-value-added bioactive ingredients. Therefore, controlling the accumulation of two metabolites in tobacco is crucial for enhancing the functional quality of tobacco. Currently, the regulation of these two components faces three main challenges: (1) Limited synthesis efficiency of target products; (2) Programmed cell death in plant cells; and (3) Low utilization rate of exogenous metabolites (Qi et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2017). The reasons for these issues include the following three aspects: (1) Uneven distribution of metabolic flux restricts the synthesis efficiency of target products (Tabata et al., 2002). For example, ascorbic acid (AsA) synthesis via the Smirnoff-Wheeler pathway competes with cell wall synthesis for the key precursor GDP-mannose, resulting in AsA content in wild-type tobacco accounting for only 0.03% of leaf dry weight. (2) Imbalance in redox homeostasis triggers cell death (de Pinto et al., 2002). In a study, overexpression of phenylalanine ammonia-lyase (PAL), a key enzyme in polyphenol synthesis, increased total phenol content but elevated intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels by 2.8-fold, activating NADPH oxidase (RBOHD) and triggering programmed cell death in mesophyll cells. (3) Poor adaptability of heterologous pathways leads to low utilization of exogenous metabolites (Zhao et al., 2019). In a representative study, when exogenous anthocyanin synthesis genes (CHS, ANS) were expressed in tobacco, over 85% of anthocyanins were retained in the endoplasmic reticulum due to the lack of transport proteins such as glutathione-S-transferase (GST). Notably, these limiting factors exhibit interconnected effects: metabolic flux redistribution exacerbates oxidative stress, and redox imbalance inhibits heterologous enzyme activity, posing systemic challenges for metabolic engineering optimization (Badejo et al., 2008b; Wang et al., 2012a). Strategy I: Reconstruction and regulation of endogenous pathways

Reconstruction and regulation of endogenous pathways enable precise manipulation of key metabolic nodes by decoupling the hierarchical structure of natural metabolic networks, primarily involving two methods (Fig. 4a): (1) Metabolic node decoupling and orthogonal regulation; (2) Application and regulation of stress-resistant elements. Metabolic node decoupling and orthogonal regulation can significantly improve the synthesis efficiency of target products (Yao et al., 2023b). For example, phosphomannomutase (PMM) is a rate-limiting enzyme in the Smirnoff-Wheeler ascorbic acid synthesis pathway, catalyzing the conversion of mannose-6-phosphate to mannose-1-phosphate (Eltelib et al., 2012; Fenech et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2013; Smirnoff et al., 2024). To this end, overexpression of the PMM-encoding gene from Malpighia glabra in tobacco increased the ascorbic acid (AsA) content in transgenic tobacco by approximately 2-fold compared to wild-type plants, which was positively correlated with PMM transcription levels and enzyme activity (Badejo et al., 2008a). The application of stress-resistant elements can effectively enhance the stress tolerance of tobacco plants (Alok et al., 2025; Ben Romdhane et al., 2017; Biemelt et al., 2004; Lin et al., 2021; Orlova et al., 2003; Ramegowda et al., 2012; Rukavtsova et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2011; Yang et al., 2011). A representative study showed that overexpression of tomato-derived LeLhcb2 (encoding a chloroplast-localized chlorophyll a/b binding protein) enabled tobacco plants to reduce oxidative damage caused by excess light energy dissipation under low-temperature stress by enhancing light energy distribution (e.g., NPQ) and PSII repair (maintaining D1 protein homeostasis), rather than relying on the antioxidant enzyme system (Deng et al., 2014).

Strategy II: Compartmental optimization of heterologous biosynthetic pathways

Optimization of heterologous synthetic pathway compartmentalization can overcome subcellular spatial limitations and optimize metabolic pathways, mainly including two methods (Fig. 4b): (1) Chloroplast-targeted expression; (2) Vacuolar transport engineering. The compartmentalization optimization strategy of heterologous metabolic pathways can break through the spatiotemporal barriers of product synthesis and transport. For example, by fusing the anthocyanin synthesis gene cluster (CHS-CHI-DFR-ANS) with chloroplast transit peptide (CTP), targeted expression of heterologous genes in chloroplasts can be achieved. Experimental data show that transgenic tobacco plants introduced with this fusion gene through nuclear transformation technology can accumulate anthocyanins in leaves up to 0.8 mg/g fresh weight (FW), which is approximately 3.2-fold higher than the traditional cytoplasmic expression system (He et al., 2016). This result confirms the significant role of chloroplast compartmentalization in increasing secondary metabolite production. Reconstruction of transmembrane transport networks through vacuolar transport engineering enables long-term storage of functional substances. In a study, the synergistic mechanism based on dual transporters significantly enhanced the directional transport efficiency of anthocyanins from the cytoplasm to the vacuole (Gani et al., 2021), increasing their bioavailability from 12% to 67%, while reducing the feedback inhibition effect of intermediate products in the cytoplasm. Notably, this method also stabilizes the anthocyanin molecular structure through the acidic environment of the vacuole, with a half-life 2.3- fold that under cytoplasmic storage conditions (Kaur et al., 2021).

Strategy III: Development of light-regulated synthetic systems

Light-regulated synthetic systems enable precise control of secondary metabolite synthesis flux, primarily involving two methods (Fig. 4c): (1) Far-red light induction system; (2) Blue light-responsive feedback inhibition system. The far-red (FR) light induction system can maintain metabolic homeostasis (Golubova et al., 2024). For example, upon red light irradiation, the photoreceptor protein undergoes conformational changes, relieving the inhibition of LE1 on downstream gene expression, with an energy loss of only 30% in this system. The blue light-responsive feedback inhibition system allows for precise control of metabolic flux (Liu et al., 2025b). In a study, researchers constructed a light-controlled allosteric switch using the LOV domain (Kliebenstein et al., 2011). When ascorbic acid (AsA) concentration exceeds the threshold (1.0 mg/g), blue light-induced conformational changes automatically downregulate GMPase activity to 30% of the baseline level. This closed-loop control system stabilizes AsA production fluctuation within ±8%, representing a 2.7-fold improvement in stability compared to traditional induction systems. Further studies revealed that integrating light-controlled switches with metabolite concentration gradient response elements can establish an adaptive metabolic rhythm regulatory network, with phase synchronization accuracy improved by 58% compared to single-signal input systems (Fu et al., 2016).

Metabolic engineering of tobacco for improving combustion quality

Challenges in improving the combustion quality of tobacco

The tar and smoke components released during tobacco combustion are closely related to the contents of sugar esters, cellulose, and phenols (Stigter and Plaxton, 2015). Due to the complex metabolic networks involved in these biomasses, existing regulatory approaches face three challenges: (1) Difficulty in coordinated regulation of harm reduction and quality maintenance; (2) Influence patterns of cell wall metabolism on combustion characteristics; (3) Bottlenecks in metabolic network regulation of sugar ester synthesis. The reasons for these problems include the following three points: (1) Precise regulation of tobacco combustion characteristics faces the balance between harm reduction needs and sensory quality maintenance, with the core contradiction reflected in the redox dynamic equilibrium of combustion chemistry. Studies have shown that reducing the combustion temperature from 850 °C to 700 °C can decrease the content of benzo [a]pyrene in tar by 42%, but accompanied by a 1.8-fold increase in the concentration of heterocyclic compounds such as 2-methylfuran. This phenomenon stems from the antagonistic relationship between polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) formation and sufficient organic matter cracking under high-temperature conditions. That is, although high temperatures can promote the oxidative decomposition of macromolecular organics, they simultaneously exacerbate the formation of carcinogens such as PAHs. Therefore, establishing a quantitative model of temperature gradients and combustion product profiles has become the key to resolving the harm reduction-quality conflict (McGrath et al., 2007; Torikai et al., 2004). (2) The complexity of cell wall component metabolic regulation restricts the fine optimization of combustion rate. Although increasing the cellulose/lignin ratio can improve combustion adequacy, single-component modification easily leads to deterioration of cell wall mechanical properties. For example, overexpression of cellulose synthase CesA4 increases cellulose content to 22%, but results in an 18% decrease in leaf breaking force. In-depth mechanism analysis shows that reduced lignin cross-linking causes disordered arrangement of microfibril networks. This indicates that coordinated optimization of lignin monomer composition (such as increasing the S/G ratio) is required to effectively regulate combustion rate and mechanical strength (Li et al., 2025; Ovalı, 2024). (3) Resource competition in the sugar ester metabolic network increases the difficulty of sensory quality regulation. When sugar ester synthesis is enhanced, the carbon flux of terpene precursor isopentenyl pyrophosphate (IPP) is diverted by 35%, leading to weakening of cembranoid characteristic aroma. Mechanism analysis through isotope labeling experiments revealed metabolic node overlap between C16-C18 fatty acids and IPP in the plastid MEP pathway. To address the in vivo resource competition caused by such precursor competition, dynamic regulation technologies need to be developed to achieve spatiotemporal decoupling of harm reduction targets and aroma substance synthesis (Tritsch et al., 2009; Wu et al., 2012). Strategy I: Rational design of cell wall components

Directional regulation of cellulose/lignin ratio (such as optimization of lignin S/G monomers) can improve combustion adequacy while maintaining leaf mechanical strength (breaking force ≥12 N), mainly including two methods (Fig. 5a): (1) Optimization of lignin monomer ratio; (2) Directional enhancement of cellulose. Precise regulation of lignin monomer biosynthesis through gene editing systems can effectively modulate cell wall components (Chen et al., 2023b; Dwivedi et al., 2023; Sulis et al., 2023; Yuan et al., 2024). For example, targeted modification of key genes related to lignin and pectin synthesis in tobacco using the CRISPR-Cas9 system reduces stem lignification while altering cell wall structure and cell adhesion in transgenic plants (Ahmed et al., 2024). Enhancing cellulose content through directional reinforcement strategies is also an effective approach for regulating cell wall components (Sampathkumar et al., 2019). In a study, overexpression of sucrose synthase genes (PsnSuSy1/2) optimized the ratio of cellulose to hemicellulose in tobacco, increased secondary wall thickness by 40%, and improved stem breaking force by 25%, demonstrating the feasibility of multi-component synergistic regulation strategies (Li et al., 2019).

Strategy II: Spatiotemporal regulation of sugar ester biosynthesis pathways

Dynamic allocation of carbon sources enables metabolic decoupling between sucrose ester synthesis and terpene precursor supply, achieving spatiotemporal regulation of sugar ester synthesis pathways, mainly including two methods (Fig. 5b): (1) Senescence-specific signaling pathway; (2) Transmembrane transport enhancement. The senescence-specific signaling pathway allows temporal regulation of sugar ester metabolism (Jordi et al., 2001). In a study, the tobacco CYP82E4 gene is activated through a senescence-specific signaling pathway, and its promoter is specifically expressed in senescent leaves, thereby catalyzing nicotine degradation without responding to abiotic stress (Chakrabarti et al., 2008). This result validates the feasibility of targeted regulation of secondary metabolism during the senescence stage. The transmembrane transport system enables spatial regulation of sugar ester metabolism (Crouzet et al., 2013; Elejalde-Palmett et al., 2021; Hwang et al., 2020; Udawat et al., 2017; Xu et al., 2018). For example, plasma membrane-localized NtLTPI.38 regulates glycerophospholipid metabolism, increasing wax layer thickness by 15% in transgenic tobacco leaves, with alkane content elevated by 22%. Meanwhile, this protein reduces ROS accumulation by enhancing SOD and POD activities, indirectly maintaining epidermal structural integrity. Phenotypic validation results show that NtLTPI.38 overexpression significantly alters epidermal microstructure, increasing leaf breaking force to 11.2 ± 0.4 N and improving combustion uniformity index (Yang et al., 2023b).

Strategy III: Metabolic intervention of pyrolysis products

Coordinated expression of multiple antioxidant enzymes in chloroplasts can effectively alleviate oxidative stress and regulate the toxicological properties of pyrolysis products (Fig. 5c). Chloroplasts serve as the core site for regulating antioxidant metabolism-combustion product associations, with their NADPH regeneration capacity enhancing antioxidant synthesis flux by 2- to 3-fold. Lipophilic antioxidants (tocopherols, carotenoids) directly reduce PAH precursor polymerization by quenching ·OH and O2− (e.g., γ-tocopherol reduces PAHs by 22%). Furthermore, multi-enzyme systems (SOD-APX-GR) are more effective than single enzymes (e.g., triple-enzyme co-expression reduces membrane damage by 18.1% vs. <5% for single enzymes) (Abbasi et al., 2007; Chen et al., 2016; Kummari et al., 2020; Kwon et al., 2002; Wan et al., 2009; Yoshimura et al., 2003). For example, co-expression of dehydroascorbate reductase (DHAR) and glutathione reductase (GR) in chloroplasts increased the reduced glutathione (GSH) ratio by 47% and decreased H2O2 content by 41% after MV treatment. Transgenic lines showed 35% reduction in leaf MDA content under 200 mM NaCl stress, indirectly confirming the antioxidant system's ability to block free radical chain reactions (Le Martret et al., 2011). In another study, chloroplastic co-expression of CuZnSOD, APX and DHAR in transgenic tobacco (CAD plants) increased the reduced ascorbic acid (AsA) ratio by 2.1-fold and reduced methyl viologen (MV)-induced membrane damage by 18.1%. EPR detection showed 34% decrease in ·OH radical concentration (Lee et al., 2007). Notably, chloroplast engineering to enhance carotenoid synthesis increased β-carotene content to 1.8 mg/g FW, with combustion experiments showing 2.3-fold improvement in singlet oxygen (1O2) quenching efficiency and 19% reduction in benzo [a]pyrene content in tar (Li et al., 2020a).

Conclusion and prospect

This review systematically summarizes the important role of metabolic engineering enabling technologies in tobacco quality improvement. Currently, the application of metabolic engineering strategies, methods, and technologies is mainly reflected in four aspects: (1) Analysis of tobacco metabolic networks. Integrating multi-omics technologies reveals specific regulatory patterns of tobacco secondary metabolism, providing a basis for targeted editing (Chen et al., 2019a; Qi et al., 2021; Schachtsiek and Stehle, 2019; Sun et al., 2025; Tong et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2024; Yuan et al., 2025; Zhou et al., 2024c). (2) Precise regulation of tobacco metabolite content. Simultaneous knockout of six BBL gene family members results in transgene-free tobacco with nicotine content represented a 99.75% decrease (Ma et al., 2020; Schachtsiek and Stehle, 2019). (3) Dynamic regulation and balance of optogenetic switches (Ochoa-Fernandez et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2012, 2025). CRISPR-Cas12i can effectively edit the CAD gene involved in tobacco lignin regulation. Furthermore, optogenetic switches (Cdc48) and metabolite-responsive promoters (such as JAZ promoter) address growth inhibition caused by constitutive terpene accumulation (He et al., 2025). (4) Microbial-plant cross-species synergy (Cheng et al., 2022a; Ren et al., 2023). Arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi can improve tobacco growth, essential oil production, secondary metabolism, and yield under drought stress conditions (Begum et al., 2021). Compared with traditional breeding and chemical regulation technologies, metabolic engineering demonstrates synergistic advantages of precision, efficiency, and sustainability in tobacco quality improvement, with core breakthroughs in the following dimensions: (1) Targeted metabolic pathway precision regulation. Traditional hybrid breeding relies on random trait recombination and struggles to directionally optimize complex metabolic networks (such as balancing nicotine and terpene synthesis), while metabolic engineering enables specific regulation of target compounds through gene editing, RNAi interference, promoter engineering, etc., achieving “on-demand design” precision improvement (Cheng et al., 2018; Kim et al., 2021; Li et al., 2021, 2022; Nölke et al., 2021; Ochoa-Fernandez et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2009b, 2025; Xie and Fan, 2016; Yasmeen et al., 2023). (2) Cross-species biosynthetic pathway integration. Traditional technologies are limited by tobacco's endogenous metabolic potential, whereas metabolic engineering reconstructs non-natural product synthesis pathways by introducing heterologous genes (such as increasing monoterpene content to enhance aroma), breaking species genetic boundaries (Agbor et al., 2017; Fan, 2024; Yin et al., 2016). (3) Multi-trait synergistic optimization efficiency. Traditional methods require multiple generations of backcrossing to stack target traits, taking decades, while metabolic engineering simultaneously regulates multiple target gene loci through modular engineering, achieving simultaneous improvement of multiple plant cell traits and shortening the improvement cycle (Pruthvi et al., 2017). (4) Environmental and health risk controllability. Compared with conventional chemical regulation, metabolic engineering may reduce reliance on exogenous inputs, thereby lowering risks of chemical residues and environmental contamination. However, its ecological advantages are context-dependent and require systematic evaluation. System-level factors, including cultivation practices and potential gene flow risks, may also influence overall environmental performance. Therefore, a life-cycle assessment (LCA) framework integrating inputs, processes, and outputs is required for robust comparison. Future studies should establish standardized metrics to quantitatively evaluate sustainability and guide rational strategy design. (Feng et al., 2024; Pruthvi et al., 2017).

Current metabolic engineering in tobacco quality improvement is constrained by several key bottlenecks. Limited precursor supply and inefficient carbon partitioning restrict flux toward target metabolites, while pathway imbalance, intermediate accumulation, and cofactor/redox constraints reduce overall efficiency. Competition among native metabolic branches further diverts key intermediates, and complex multi-level regulatory networks hinder precise metabolic control. These challenges can be broadly summarized into three critical issues: insufficient metabolic network robustness (e.g., compensatory effects such as putrescine accumulation), low microbial colonization efficiency (e.g., survival rates below 30%), and poor industrial adaptability due to the lack of standardized biological components and tools. Future research needs to break through technical bottlenecks in the following directions to promote the leap of metabolic engineering from laboratory to industrialization: (1) Enhancement of metabolic network resilience. Develop multi-target editing tools and dynamic feedback systems (such as blue light-LOV switches) to cope with metabolic compensation effects (Zayner et al., 2019). Drawing on the “Hyper-pathway” design concept in yeast metabolic engineering, construct an anti-interference tobacco synthesis network (Xiao et al., 2025). (2) Deepening of plant-microbe interactions. Designing environment-responsive microbial chassis (such as drought-induced lysis switches) and engineered rhizosphere microbial communities can improve the colonization stability of rhizosphere engineering bacteria. (Gao et al., 2022). Based on the dual-path construction strategy of “bottom-up” and “top-down”, by integrating metabolic complementarity and niche preference data, the stability of SynComs in anaerobic digestion systems is improved by 40%, providing methodological support for the engineering design of rhizosphere microbial communities (Hao et al., 2024). (3) Improvement of industrial adaptability. The application of standardized biological components (such as pTobacco-EX vectors) and digital monitoring systems (CyTOF and AI models) has significantly improved the predictability of metabolic flux (error <8%) and production batch stability (volatility < ±12%) (Kugler and Stensjö, 2024). At the same time, with the deepening of synthetic biology tools (dynamic regulatory switches, artificial organelles) and multi-omics integration analysis (metabolome-transcriptome-phenome), metabolic engineering will further realize intelligent design of tobacco quality (such as AI prediction of optimal metabolic flux pathways) and dynamic adaptation (environment-responsive aroma release) (Yue et al., 2025). In addition, multi-omics integration strategies can reveal the reprogramming mechanism of amino acid metabolic networks under radiation stress by correlating metabolite-transcript-phenotype data, providing a multi-dimensional analysis tool for the systematic analysis of tobacco quality traits (Kugler and Stensjö, 2024).

Different metabolic engineering strategies exhibit distinct advantages and limitations in terms of efficacy, stability, scalability, and practical applicability. RNAi-based approaches enable flexible and reversible regulation of target pathways but may suffer from variable silencing efficiency and expression instability. In contrast, CRISPR-based strategies provide more precise and stable genome modifications, with strong potential for long-term trait improvement and the development of transgene-free lines. Dynamic regulatory systems offer enhanced control of metabolic fluxes, although their field applicability remains limited. Meanwhile, microbial SynCom-based approaches represent a less genetically intrusive strategy with strong environmental adaptability, but challenges related to colonization stability and field reproducibility still need to be addressed. Therefore, integrating complementary strategies may provide an effective route toward balanced optimization of tobacco quality, biosafety, and industrial applicability.

In addition to technical challenges, the field deployment of metabolically engineered tobacco requires consideration of regulatory, societal, and commercial factors. Given the strict regulatory oversight of tobacco, engineered lines must meet biosafety and environmental assessment requirements. The development of transgene-free genome-edited plants offers a promising route to facilitate regulatory approval and mitigate public concerns. Consumer acceptance is another key factor, necessitating transparent communication of risk–benefit profiles, particularly in the context of harm-reduction strategies. Moreover, intellectual property constraints may affect technology transfer and large-scale application. Future efforts should therefore integrate biosafety-oriented genetic designs with regulatory compliance and existing agricultural systems to enable the practical translation of metabolic engineering advances.

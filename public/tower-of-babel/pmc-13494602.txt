Commentary on “Do children born after embryo vitrification differ from siblings born after fresh embryo transfer?”
Authors: Amit Jaiswal
Published: 2026

To the Editor:

Belva et al. (1) present a methodologically rigorous sibling analysis demonstrating that frozen embryo transfer (FET) is associated with higher birthweight standard deviation scores and increased large-for-gestational-age risk compared with fresh embryo transfer within the same families. By minimizing shared genetic and many stable maternal confounders, their design strengthens inference beyond registry-based comparisons and confirms a directionally consistent phenotype observed across large meta-analyses (2).

The central implication of these findings is not confined to perinatal metrics. Converging evidence indicates that embryo transfer modality modifies the physiological context of implantation. Programmed FET cycles, particularly those lacking a corpus luteum, are associated with altered maternal vascular adaptation and hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (3). Conversely, supraphysiologic estradiol exposure during fresh cycles has been linked independently to lower birthweight in term singletons (4). These data suggest that endocrine conditions at implantation, rather than cryopreservation alone, may mediate downstream fetal growth trajectories.

From a developmental origins perspective, sustained shifts in birthweight distribution are biologically consequential. Meta-analytic evidence demonstrates that deviations at both ends of the birthweight spectrum are associated with increased long-term cardiometabolic risk (5). A systematic rightward shift toward large-for-gestational-age after FET, if reproducible across populations, constitutes a population-level biological signal, not merely a statistical observation.

The sibling design strengthens causal plausibility but does not resolve mechanism. Time-dependent uterine remodeling, laboratory evolution across study years, and unmeasured endocrine exposure at implantation remain potential mediators. The next phase of investigation should integrate endocrine profiling, placental biomarkers, and longitudinal metabolic follow-up to determine whether the observed growth differences reflect adaptive variation or early markers of altered developmental programming.

As FET becomes dominant in many assisted reproductive technology programs, embryo transfer strategy functions as a systems-level intervention. Therefore, the discipline must extend its evaluative framework beyond implantation and short-term neonatal endpoints toward long-term offspring health. Belva et al. (1) have provided robust confirmation of a reproducible phenotype. Determining its biological meaning and clinical consequence now represents a field-level priority.

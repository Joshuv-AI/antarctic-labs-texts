Building a research culture among nigerian medical students: the modus operandi of the college research and innovation hub
Authors: Tobi Olajide, Kehinde Arokoyo, Adeniyi Adesola, Sophia Okeke, Ridwanullah Abdullateef, Feziechi Anele, Abigail Oyedokun, Adesola Ogunniyi, Rufus Akinyemi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAfrica, with 18.3% of the world’s population, faces a significant research output gap, contributing only 2% of global research despite bearing a substantial disease burden. This discrepancy is partly due to inadequate research infrastructure, limited funding, and a shortage of physician-scientists. These challenges exacerbate the continent’s reliance on research findings from high-income countries, which often does not align with Africa’s unique health issues. Establishing student research hubs in medical schools is a promising strategy to addressing this issue. Such hubs can cultivate a research-oriented mindset and enhance problem-solving skills among future medical professionals. This paper examines the operational model of the College Research and Innovation Hub (CRIH) at the University of Ibadan, Nigeria. Founded in 2019, CRIH has been successful in promoting research among medical students through structured programs and mentorship. The paper provides insights into CRIH’s strategies and impact, offering a framework for replicating this model in Nigeria to improve research output and address local health challenges.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12909-024-06518-4.Keywords: College Research and Innovation Hub, Student Research, Hubs, Research Culture

Introduction

Africa, home to 18.3% of the total world population, shoulders about one-fourth of the global burden of diseases. Despite this, Africa contributes only about 2% of the world’s research output—a stark discrepancy that reflects the continent’s dependence on evidence-based research from other continents. This reliance is particularly problematic given the unique genetic expressions and disease manifestations within the Black population, which make research from other continents less applicable within our contexts, especially for tropical diseases [1–4].

Challenges contributing to Africa’s paradoxically low scholarly output are numerous, including inadequate research infrastructure, programs, lack of funding, and insufficient well trained physician-scientists. Physician-scientists are a rare species in Africa [4] despite being an important and fragile link between basic science research and clinical practice. This has resulted in a disappointing under-representation of African data in biomedical research [5], difficulty training and mentoring the younger generation interested in combining these fields, brain drain, and further worsening dependence on the research breakthroughs made by developed countries [4].

Establishing student research hubs in various medical schools is a step in the right direction in reversing Africa’s underrepresentation in global research output. This can cultivate a research-oriented and problem-solving mindset in the upcoming generation, ultimately contributing to the expansion of research frontiers across our continent. In Nigeria, a few student-led research hubs have been established and are running, though they are mostly concentrated in the South-Western region [6–9].

A prominent example is the College Research and Innovation Hub (CRIH) in Ibadan, Oyo State, which has become a successful model for promoting a research culture among medical students. CRIH recently organized its second scientific conference and celebrated its 5th anniversary, demonstrating its impact and growth [10].

This paper majorly aims to extensively discuss the structure, activities, and strategies employed by CRIH to build research capacity for its members. By analyzing these elements, this paper seeks to offer clarity into how similar student research hubs can be created in other medical schools across Nigeria.

Literature review

Research culture in medical education refers to the shared values, attitudes, behaviors, and norms that promote research activities among students, faculty, and staff [11]. Establishing a robust research culture is important, as it instills in medical undergraduates the essential principles of research, helping them formulate scientific questions, conduct experiments, analyze data, and report results.

Early exposure to research promotes a mindset of curiosity, innovation, and continuous learning in medical trainees, which is important in the constantly evolving field of medicine. Historical discoveries such as heparin, insulin, the sinoatrial node, and ether anesthesia were made by medical students, illustrating the significant impact of early career researchers [12–14].

Additionally, research opportunities often involve presenting findings at scientific meetings and conferences, enhancing students’ ability to communicate complex information clearly—an important skill for interacting with patients and colleagues. The ethical aspects of medical research, including patient consent and confidentiality, integrity, and adherence to good practices, prepare students for the medical profession. Moreover, research projects frequently require collaboration with faculty and peers both within and outside their departments, equipping students with teamwork skills essential for healthcare delivery [15]. This encourages networking and mentorship which lays the groundwork for future scholarly pursuits, giving medical graduates a competitive edge when applying for residency programs or academic positions [16].

Student-led research hubs have become important for developing early-career researchers. A notable example is the Student Audit and Research in Surgery (STARSurg), a pioneering student-led network in the United Kingdom (UK) and Ireland [17, 18]. STARSurg mobilizes students from various medical schools to engage in multi-center audits and research studies. This initiative offers students the chance to participate in high-quality academic projects, learn about clinical research, and understand audit methodologies and ethical considerations. STARSurg’s operational model emphasizes hands-on involvement in national and international projects, allowing students to contribute data, develop research skills, and publish findings in major journals. The network’s growth has resulted in impactful publications and presentations at conferences worldwide, reinforcing its role in promoting a collaborative research culture [18].

At University College Dublin, the Student Summer Research Awards (SSRA) is an initiative where over 100 undergraduates undertake eight-week research projects annually, funded by grants [19]. This program provides short-term, supervised research experience across various disciplines, including laboratory, clinical, and educational research.

Coming to Africa, and Nigeria specifically, the DOKITA Editorial Board, established in 1960, oversees the publication of the DOKITA journal, the foremost medical students’ journal in Nigeria and West Africa recognized by WHO, now on its 41st edition [6]. Beyond publishing, the Board promotes academic and social interaction among medical students through various initiatives, including annual symposia and the Biennial Professor J.A Adeleye National Inter-Medical School Essay Competition. By promoting research and writing, DOKITA empowers students to transform curiosity into impactful discoveries, promoting a culture of innovation and collaboration that has the potential to shape the future of healthcare. Moreover, their journal, which undergoes rigorous peer review, is indexed with African Journals Online (AJOL), a leading platform that supports the visibility and accessibility of African-published scholarly journals, further enhancing its credibility and reach within the global academic community [7].

In the same vein, the IFEMED Journal Club, the official journal club of the Obafemi Awolowo Medical Students’ Association, established in 1975, is dedicated to publishing scientific journals on a range of contemporary healthcare issues [8]. Over its 47 years of existence, the club has produced 26 volumes addressing diverse themes such as malaria, infectious diseases, cancer, and public health. To advance the field, they host regular meetings to critically analyze recent academic articles, promoting evidence-based discussions and exploring practical applications of research. In addition, annual events, including the Journal Launch, Journal Club Meetings, and Research Writing Seminars, offer extensive training in research writing, ethics, statistical analysis, and proposal development, with certificates awarded for significant attendance.

Furthermore, SURGIL (Surgical Interest Group Lagos) focuses on nurturing the surgical aspirations of Nigerian medical students [9]. Through student-led, faculty-supervised research, mentorship programs, webinars, and advocacy campaigns, SURGIL provides essential training and guidance. Initiatives such as the Surgical Skills Lab and “So You Wanna Be a Surgeon” webinars equip members with the practical skills and insights necessary for a successful surgical career. Notably, in 2024, they held their inaugural scientific conference, “From the Classroom to the OR: Nurturing the Next Generation of Surgeons,” featuring abstract presentations from medical students at various universities.

Discussion

The aim of this paper is to examine how the College Research and Innovation Hub (CRIH) at the College of Medicine University of Ibadan stimulates the interests of medical students towards research. It seeks to describe the operational model of CRIH by providing an overview of its structured programs, mentorship initiatives, and collaborations with faculty. This paper also aims to analyze the impact of CRIH’s activities on student engagement in research as well as identifying best practices from CRIH that can be replicated in other medical schools in Nigeria and Sub-Saharan Africa.

Background of the college research and innovation hub (CRIH)

The College Research and Innovation Hub, is an undergraduate research club based in the College of Medicine University of Ibadan, Ibadan, Nigeria. It was founded on the 11th of April, 2019, at the Ibadan Medical School at 70 celebration and class reunion of the septuagenarian set of 1973/74. A proposal during the event was put forward to establish centres of innovation for medical students which was supported and funded by the college and Dr B.G.K Ajayi. CRIH was then placed under the aegis of the established frame of the College Research and Innovation Management (CRIM)—the central coordinating point for the management of research activities within the college. Professor Adesola Ogunniyi, who at the time was the Chairman of CRIM, was saddled with the responsibility of coordinating the newly formed student research hub and became its Grand Patron.

CRIH quickly gained momentum, becoming fully operational just three months after its establishment. Its inaugural meeting saw the participation of over 40 interested students, marking the beginning of what would become a thriving community dedicated to research and innovation. Five pairs of journal clubs emerged namely: Neuroscience, Cardiology, Genetics and Oncology, Public Health, and Child and Maternal Health.

The founding fathers of CRIH had a vision to produce world standard graduates efficient in medical research and innovation through the hub. As stated in its constitution, the objectives of the hub are to stimulate the interest of undergraduates in research and innovation, train members in the basics of research processes, reward innovative thinking, promote interdisciplinary collaborations, and provide opportunities for members through mentorship by mid- and senior career researchers in the college.

Organizational structure

The College Research and Innovation Hub (CRIH) operates through a well-organized structure centered on journal clubs, which serve as the functional units of the organization. These journal clubs are aligned with members’ preferred research interests, allowing for focused and specialized development. The primary activities within the journal clubs are designed to enhance members’ research skills and keep them informed about recent trends and advancements in medical practice [20]. Meetings are held biweekly, providing a consistent platform for members to exchange research knowledge, and a monthly general seminar offers additional opportunities for broader engagement and learning.

The leadership of CRIH is structured to ensure effective governance and strategic oversight. It comprises the Executive Council, Journal Club Heads, Advisers (including the Grand Patron, staff advisers, and Alumni), and Supervising Consultants. Together, these groups guide the organization toward fulfilling its mission of promoting research excellence among members.

At the top of the hierarchy is the Executive Council, which oversees all organizational activities for a one-year term. The Council’s key responsibilities include implementing policies and initiatives to promote active member participation, enhancing research knowledge and experience, and planning the annual scientific conference. The members of the Executive Council are appointed by the outgoing council based on their dedication, participation in activities, and demonstrated research expertise. The Council includes roles such as President, Vice President, General Secretary, Programs and Content Manager, Assistant Programs and Content Manager, Financial Secretary, and Public Relations Officer.

Journal Club Heads are appointed by the Executive Council and are responsible for managing the operations of their respective clubs. Their duties involve scheduling and leading meetings, ensuring that members actively participate, and overseeing research projects. These heads work under the supervision of designated consultants. Advisers—including the Grand Patron, staff advisers, and the Alumni body—provide essential guidance and support. They assist the Executive Council in decision-making processes and help in the execution of organizational programs by offering mentorship and expert advice. Supervising Consultants are paired with each journal club and provide expert oversight for the research projects. Their role is to ensure that the research is aligned with the current knowledge and standards in the field, offering mentorship and guidance to the journal club members.

Recruitment of new members is managed by the Executive Council at the end of each tenure. Prospective members, regardless of their prior research experience, must demonstrate a genuine interest in research and innovation. Currently, over fifty intending members are on probation. They undergo a probationary period for three months during which they are trained on the fundamentals of research and required to actively participate in CRIH activities. Their commitment is evaluated based on attendance, punctuality, and performance on assigned research projects. This review serves as a key determinant in the final selection of new members.

Core activities and programs within the hub

In tandem with the vision of the College Research and Innovation Hub to produce world standard graduates efficient in medical research and innovation, we have established structured activities and programs designed to promote a vibrant research culture amongst our members. Among its core initiatives are journal club meetings, monthly seminars, annual scientific conferences, and research projects.

Journal club meetings form a key part of CRIH’s strategy to build research capacity among its members. These smaller units bring together students with similar research interests to engage in discussions on recent academic literature. Held twice a month, these meetings aim to enhance critical thinking, scientific literacy, and communication skills. Regular engagement with peer-reviewed articles enables members to assess the quality of studies and distinguish robust research from those with methodological flaws [21]. This initiative encourages critical reading behavior, as well as increased knowledge of biostatistics and epidemiology.

CRIH also hosts monthly seminars, which have been carefully curated, exposing our members to a wide range of topics, methodologies and perspectives that enable the growth of student researchers. These meetings focus on specific themes from foundational research concepts to current advancements in medicine. Presentations often explore the methodologies used in current studies, providing practical insights into how research is conducted. Our members learn about the nuances of study design, data interpretation, and the application of research findings to clinical practice, thereby improving their own research capabilities. Some of our seminars also feature guest speakers, who are accomplished researchers and clinicians, bringing a wealth of experience and knowledge to the table. Their presentations provide our members with insights into the practical challenges and successes of conducting research at a high level. Hearing directly from accomplished researchers about their challenges, and achievements serves as a potent source of motivation for our members and also provides an avenue for open dialogue encouraging members to ask questions and seek clarification on complex topics, allowing the opportunity to gain advice directly from experienced researchers.

CRIH’s annual scientific conferences have been instrumental in facilitating student engagement with the broader research community. Various studies have shown that conferences are part of a learning process in which young researchers expand their knowledge and research competence, develop networking strategies and establish relationships with other professionals [22–26]. In our journey to becoming a world class student research hub, we have organized two scientific conferences. These events have served to bring together our members as well as other students from across Nigeria to discuss their own research, latest developments as well as promoting networking and collaboration with fellow student researchers.

The conference provides students with the opportunity to present their research findings to a diverse audience of peers, faculty, and invited guests. This hones confidence as well as public speaking skills, teaching them to communicate their research findings clearly and persuasively. This is also a vital opportunity for students to receive constructive criticism on their work from peers and faculty helping them refine their research questions, improve their methodologies, and enhance their data interpretation.

To motivate students and recognize outstanding research, our conferences have included award categories for the best presentations and innovative research projects. These awards are not only a source of pride but also help students build their academic profiles, which can be beneficial for future professional pursuits. They also encourage a healthy competitive spirit and drive students to strive for higher standards of excellence in their research endeavors.

Pioneering numerous student research projects is an instrument in achieving our vision and mission as a student research hub, enabling members to experientially learn the skill of effective research project management. Our approach is designed to support students through every phase of their research journey, from idea conception, developing research proposals to dissemination of findings via our annual scientific conferences and also guidance on submission to journals. During the course of this year, we organized an event to enable the various research teams in our hubs to present their research proposals to seasoned researchers who help offer constructive criticism before teams start data collection. We also issued small scale research grants to the top 3 teams to support their research efforts.

In a bid to recognize and appreciate dedicated final year members, CRIH organizes annual send off programs themed “Sending out the Stars,” where these individuals are celebrated and honored for their contributions to the hub during their years as members. This program promotes a feeling of belonging even after their graduation, and ensures a smooth transition to our Alumni extension of our prestigious hub.

Sources of funding and current strategies for sustainability

In order to sustain our activities and support the research efforts of our members, we have adopted an approach that entails diversifying our funding sources as well as ensuring long term sustainability of the hub. Regarding our sources of funding, we have faculty members associated with CRIH who contribute funding from their own research grants. We also receive funding from the alumni of our university, and more specifically our research hub, as well as other philanthropists. CRIH actively engages with potential donors, highlighting the impact of their contributions on student research capacity building. For specific projects or initiatives, such as our annual scientific conference, CRIH has also utilized crowdfunding to raise funds. This approach not only generates financial support but also raises awareness about the hub’s activities and goals within our community. These donations and contributions are important for covering costs associated with our monthly research seminars and annual conferences, as well as funding internal research projects enabling us to support the hub in achieving her vision.

Impact and outcomes

CRIH creates an enabling environment that promotes research collaboration and the sharing of research and innovation opportunities. At every point in time, each journal club has an ongoing research project under supervision. For example, five out of the nine journal clubs presented their full research manuscripts of their projects at the CRIH Scientific Conference which held in August 2024. These were projects they have been working on for the past one year. Additionally, members conceptualise their personal research projects and collaborate with colleagues, lecturers and researchers in other medical schools, home and abroad. These collaborations have led to the publication of over 40 papers in peer-reviewed journals, spanning a wide range of medical and health fields. Most of them were published in international high-impact journals - for instance, the publication of one of the members in Nature [27], three of the members in Nature Portfolio [28], and in World Neurosurgery X [29].

CRIH members have presented abstracts at various local and international conferences, including the World Congress of Undergraduate Research (Coventry, UK), the Royal Society for Tropical Medicine and Hygiene (RSTMH), INCISION-UK Conference, the Alzheimer’s Association International Conference (AAIC), the Nigerian Society of Neurological Sciences, etc. They have won numerous research-related awards and grants, including the Bayer’s Foundation fellowship, the Sanger Prize Competition, the RSTMH Early Career Grant, the Bass Connections Student Research Award Grant, UIMSA x Duke Research Grant, etc. They have also won innovation awards, including the Global Youth Mobilization Innovation Grant, Nigeria Policy Hackathon, UNLEASH Global Innovation Lab Travel Grant, the Federal Ministry of Health Family Planning Hackathon, For Youth By Youth Hackathon, Emory Global Case Competition, etc.

Many members currently serve as research assistants for consultants’ research teams, participating in laboratory, clinical and public health studies. Some have also been accepted into internships locally and internationally (Germany, United Kingdom, Poland, etc.). A couple of members have been accepted into local internships such as the Nigerian Sovereign Investment Authority – Lagos University Teaching Hospital Cancer Centre, Drosophila Research, and the International Institute of Tropical Agriculture. These help them to acquire real-world applications of research concepts and integrate more into the research community at a higher level. These achievements have placed CRIH in the limelight, and many Nigerian medical schools and undergraduate organisations have adopted the CRIH template to build their undergraduate research communities. For example, IRIS-a newly established student research club at the Obafemi Awolowo University was fashioned in the image of Ibadan’s CRIH. Other schools such as Olabisi Onabanjo University, Bowen University, University of Port-harcourt, and Abia State University are in constant communication with Mr Tobi Olajide, the outgoing President of CRIH. CRIH members have also been invited to facilitate research sessions in these medical schools and student associations.

Comparison with other initiatives

The CRIH operational model is similar to that observed in student-led research organisations in many countries. For instance, the Injury Prevention Research Center (IPRC) at The University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill in the United States has three student-led research groups with faculty members as supervisors. They are the Gender-Based Violence (GBV) Research Group, the Opioid Student Research Group, and the Child Maltreatment Prevention Research and Practice Network. Although the GBV research group does not hold regular journal club meetings, they meet occasionally to teach writing skills and review relevant publications to stay up-to-date with the latest developments in the field and improve their proficiency in academic writing and presentation. The members also collaborate to write and publish journal articles. The opioid group on the other hand meets regularly and organises “Works in Progress” where members facilitate workshop sessions to share their ongoing works. This is an interesting initiative that can be adopted by CRIH. Unlike CRIH where membership is open to only clinical students, the child maltreatment prevention group at UNC accommodates students, faculty members and community members with related professional interests and experience, despite being student-led too. This allows for a broader spectrum of perspectives and ideas, especially from non-academics [30].

In India, a similar model named “A.S.P.I.R.E: Association for Support and Propagation of Innovation, Research and Education” was established to train students in research. Like CRIH, it is student-led and divided into journal clubs where embers receive training and mentorship from faculty members. The teaching curriculum is one year long and combines theoretical knowledge with application. They have a research database that seamlessly matches interested students into research assistant positions in the research groups of faculty principal investigators. They also design hands-on workshops for statistical analysis. While CRIH teaches statistical analysis during general meetings, there have not been hands-on sessions for members to improve their proficiencies. This is a potential aspect that can be improved in the CRIH operational model. Overall the model achieved similar results, which include improving the research capacity among medical students and providing adequate mentorship through faculty members [31].

Challenges

Funding is essential for the functioning of any organization, and the College Research and Innovation Hub (CRIH) is no exception. Unfortunately, the CRIH is currently facing significant financial constraints. Member contributions amount to less than $2 per year, making it difficult to cover the costs associated with running the hub. As a result, CRIH has had to rely heavily on donations and support from researchers within and outside our community. Over the years, the organization’s sustainability has depended almost entirely on the generosity of its patrons and advisers.

Another major challenge is encouraging student participation in research and maintaining engagement among those who show initial interest. While the medical school curriculum includes mandatory research participation, particularly during rural community medicine rotations, this requirement has not significantly increased overall student interest in research. Nevertheless, there has been a gradual rise in research participation. However, sustaining this momentum is difficult for many students due to the lack of mentorship and the demands of competing academic interests.

CRIH members often struggle with limited access to essential research resources, such as up-to-date academic journals, databases, laboratory facilities, and research materials. This scarcity of resources hampers their ability to conduct high-quality research and stay informed about the latest advancements in their fields of interest. The absence of these critical tools places constraints on the scope and depth of research projects students can undertake, ultimately impacting the overall quality of their work.

Even when students manage to produce high-quality research despite these limitations, they face additional challenges in disseminating their findings. Opportunities for publication are limited, and high submission fees for academic journals often act as barriers to getting their work recognized. Moreover, without sufficient visibility within academic circles, their contributions remain largely underappreciated. This lack of recognition further discourages students from pursuing research, as their work does not receive the acknowledgment it deserves within the wider academic community.

Recommendations and future directions

At CRIH, we recognize the importance of securing sustainable funding to continue advancing our mission of promoting student-led research and innovation. In the upcoming years, our primary focus will be to explore and diversify funding opportunities from both internal and external sources.

Internal strategies will involve generating revenue through membership dues and fee-based workshops, seminars, and training sessions, which will be open to both CRIH members and external participants. Additionally, we will engage our alumni network for financial contributions and mentorship to ensure sustained support.

External strategies will prioritize securing research grants from local and international sources. For example, the outgoing President and current Grand Patron are in talks with the Director of the Institute of Translational Genomics, in Germany, on bilateral collaboration and partnership in terms of research internships and organizing bioinformatics workshops for our members, amongst many others. We will also seek institutional support through waivers and discounts for organizing conferences and events within the college. Furthermore, strengthening partnerships with community outreach programs will enable joint grant applications and support public health initiatives.

To tackle the challenges associated with research dissemination and recognition, CRIH could develop a student-led research journal or collaborate with DOKITA to showcase student research projects. This would provide an in-house publication platform for students who struggle to meet the high submission fees of traditional journals. CRIH could also collaborate with other medical schools in Nigeria to organize intercollegiate research conferences, giving students more platforms to present their findings. It would also be beneficial to explore partnerships with open-access journals that have lower publication fees, ensuring that more students can publish their work.

Finally, maintaining strong alumni networks is important for sustaining CRIH’s long-term impact. These networks provide ongoing career guidance, job placement, and continuing education opportunities, ensuring CRIH continues to influence medical education and healthcare beyond graduation.

Limitations

This study has several limitations that should be noted. First, it does not include primary data, such as surveys or interviews with students, faculty, or mentors. As a result, the study lacks firsthand, objective insights into how effective the College Research and Innovation Hub (CRIH) is and what challenges it faces. The analysis is based on descriptive and observational data, which limits the ability to measure the hub’s impact.

Additionally, the findings might not apply to all institutions. Since the focus is on CRIH at the University of Ibadan, the study may not fully capture the experiences of other schools in Nigeria. Differences in resources, support from institutions, and local conditions could affect how easily the CRIH model could be copied elsewhere.

Another limitation is the lack of long-term data. CRIH was established in 2019, so there is not enough information yet to judge the sustainability of its programs or their long-term effects on research output, career development, or healthcare research capacity. This study cannot show how CRIH’s activities will continue to shape research over time.

The study also does not dive deeply into the financial and logistical challenges that could limit the spread or growth of the CRIH model in other Nigerian or African medical schools. Lack of funding and infrastructure could make it hard to set up or maintain similar hubs elsewhere.

Finally, because the study was done by individuals involved with CRIH, there is a chance of bias in reporting its successes. A more neutral, third-party evaluation might give a more balanced view, highlighting both strengths and areas for improvement.

Despite these limitations, the study lays a solid foundation for future research. Future work could include collecting primary data, doing long-term studies, and making broader comparisons to other institutions to strengthen the findings.

Conclusion

The College Research and Innovation Hub (CRIH) has made significant strides in promoting a research-driven culture among Nigerian students, providing them with valuable opportunities for learning and growth. Through structured activities such as journal clubs, monthly seminars, annual scientific conferences, and hands-on research projects, CRIH has created a dynamic environment that nurtures students’ research skills and promotes academic excellence. However, challenges such as financial constraints, limited access to research resources, and difficulties in research dissemination have impacted the hub’s effectiveness and sustainability.

Addressing these challenges requires a strategic approach. Diversifying funding sources, strengthening mentorship programs, and improving access to research resources are essential steps toward ensuring the hub’s long-term success. By leveraging partnerships, enhancing student engagement, and facilitating greater recognition of student research, CRIH can overcome these obstacles and continue to contribute meaningfully to the development of future researchers. As CRIH moves forward, focusing on these recommendations will help solidify its role as a leading hub for student research and innovation, ultimately advancing the quality and impact of medical research in Nigeria.

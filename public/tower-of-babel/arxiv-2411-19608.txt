A Ramanujan’s hypergeometric transformation formula, its validity range and implications
M. A. Shpot

Abstract
We extend the validity range of a Ramanujan’s hypergeometric transformation formula proved by Berndt, Bhargava and Garvan [ Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 347 4163 (1995)] and study its implications. Relations to special values of complete elliptic integrals of the first kind in the singular value theory are established. Consequently, we derive several closed-form evaluations of hypergeometric functions F 1 2 {}_{2}F_{1} with different sets of parameters and arguments. Connections with other hypergeometric transformations and some recent results are discussed.



Keywords.
Gauss hypergeometric functions, hypergeometric transformations,
closed-form evaluations, Legendre elliptic integrals, singular value theory, Ramanujan’s formulas

p=\frac{\sqrt{6\sqrt{3}-9}-1}{2}:=p_{9}\,,

(20)

then

_{2}F_{1}\Big(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2};1;\alpha(p_{9})\Big)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{6\sqrt{3}-9}\;\Gamma^{2}(\frac{3}{4})}=\frac{1+\sqrt{3}}{(2\sqrt{3}\,\pi)^{3/2}}\,\Gamma^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{4}\Big)

(21)

and

_{2}F_{1}\Big(\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3};1;\beta(p_{9})\Big)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{1/4}\cdot 3^{1/8}\sqrt{\sqrt{3}-1}\;\Gamma^{2}(\frac{3}{4})}

(22)

where

\alpha(p_{9})=\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}-\sqrt[4]{3}}{1+\sqrt{3}}\right)^{2}:=x_{9}\qquad\mbox{\emph{and}}\qquad\beta(p_{9})=\frac{3\sqrt{3}-5}{4}\,,

(23)

while functions
\alpha(p)
and
\beta(p)
are defined in (
1
), as before.

While the evaluation in the middle of (
21
) was a direct consequence of a
Ramanujan’s entry, the result (
22
) has been derived
[
19
, p.280]

by employing the theorem (
2
).
Thus, the functions in (
21
) and (
22
) satisfy (
2
) with the coefficient

\gamma(p_{9})=\frac{3^{5/8}}{2^{1/4}\sqrt{\sqrt{3}+1}}\,.

(24)

With the indicated in (
23
) identification
\alpha(p_{9})=x_{9}
, the evaluation (
21
)
is equivalent to that of the singular value
{}_{2}F_{1}(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2};1;x_{9})

in the case
n=9
of the singular-value theory of elliptic integrals of the first kind
[
20
, p. 117]
,
[
21
, p. 259]
,
[
22
, Appendix A.3]
,

[
23
, Sec. 4]
; see also
[
24
, Sec. 6]
and
[

]
.
In this theory, for the singular values of positive integer order
n
one has

\frac{{}_{2}F_{1}(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2};1;1-x_{n})}{{}_{2}F_{1}(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2};1;x_{n})}=\frac{K(k^{{}^{\prime}}_{n})}{K(k_{n})}=\sqrt{n}\,,\qquad n\in\mathbb{N},

(25)

where
x_{n}=k_{n}^{2}
, and, as usual, the singular moduli
k_{n}
and
k^{{}^{\prime}}_{n}
of
elliptic integrals of the first kind
K
are related via
k^{{}^{\prime}}_{n}=\sqrt{1-k_{n}^{2}}
.
Recall that the relation between
K
and the Gauss
{}_{2}F_{1}
function is

K(k)=\frac{\pi}{2}\,_{2}F_{1}\Big(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2};1;k^{2}\Big).

(26)

Expressing the original equation (
22
) using the least positive argument
of the Gamma function (as we also did in (
21
)) we write

_{2}F_{1}\Big(\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3};1;\frac{3\sqrt{3}-5}{4}\Big)=(2\sqrt{3}\,)^{-1/4}\,\frac{\sqrt{\sqrt{3}+1}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\,\Gamma^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{4}\Big).

(27)

This is very similar to the evaluation (58) obtained in
[
24
,
25
]
with the help
of the Mingari Scarpello and Ritelli equation
[
26
, (13)]
, viz

_{2}F_{1}\Big(\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3};1;\frac{-3\sqrt{3}-5}{4}\,\Big)=(2\sqrt{3}\,)^{-1/4}\,\frac{\sqrt{\sqrt{3}-1}}{2\pi^{3/2}}\,\Gamma^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{4}\Big).

(28)

Applying the linear Pfaff transformation (
7
) in (
27
) we obtain

_{2}F_{1}\Big(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3};1;\frac{\sqrt{3}-2}{3\sqrt{3}}\,\Big)=\frac{3^{3/8}}{2^{1/12}4\pi^{3/2}}\,(\sqrt{3}+1)^{1/6}\,\Gamma^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{4}\Big).

(29)

This is a companion evaluation to that of
[

, (57)]
:

_{2}F_{1}\Big(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3};1;\frac{\sqrt{3}+2}{3\sqrt{3}}\,\Big)=\frac{3^{3/8}}{2^{1/12}(2\pi)^{3/2}}\,(\sqrt{3}-1)^{1/6}\,\Gamma^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{4}\Big).

(30)

It is interesting to look at the ratios of the above pairs of functions.
These are
[

, p.19]

\frac{{}_{2}F_{1}\Big(\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3};1;\frac{-3\sqrt{3}-5}{4}\,\Big)}{{}_{2}F_{1}\Big(\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3};1;\frac{3\sqrt{3}-5}{4}\,\Big)}=\sqrt{3}-1\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{{}_{2}F_{1}\Big(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3};1;\frac{\sqrt{3}+2}{3\sqrt{3}}\,\Big)}{{}_{2}F_{1}\Big(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3};1;\frac{\sqrt{3}-2}{3\sqrt{3}}\,\Big)}=2^{1/3}\Big(\sqrt{3}-1\Big)^{1/3}\!.

In the next section we shall write down even more interesting generalizations of these ratios.

6
Some sample evaluations of Beukers and Forsgård

In a quite recent publication
[
27
, Sec. 6]
, Beukers and Forsgård present several ”sample
\Gamma
-evaluations” of certain
{}_{2}F_{1}
functions whose parameters depend on a single variable
t
, while their arguments are the same as
the three ones in (
31
). These can be viewed as generalizations of
classical results considered in the previous section.

Two pairs of such functions corresponding to the ”shift vector”
\gamma=(-2,4,2)
appear on p. 696 of
[
27
]
.
The quite involved combinations of

\Gamma
-functions are the same in the first two evaluations, as well as in the similar formula appearing on the top of p. 697.
In terms of the general Pochhammer symbol
(\lambda)_{\nu}:=\Gamma(\lambda+\nu)/\Gamma(\lambda)
(see e.g.
[
28
]
),
these
\Gamma
-combinations can be compactly written as

C_{1}(t)=\frac{(\frac{2}{3})_{t}(\frac{7}{6})_{t}}{(\frac{3}{4})_{t}(\frac{13}{12})_{t}}\,.

(32)

Defining the parameter
a=-2t
and using the original arguments

\displaystyle z_{0}=\frac{\sqrt{3}+2}{3\sqrt{3}}=\frac{(\sqrt{3}+1)^{2}}{6\sqrt{3}}=\frac{4}{3\sqrt{3}}\,\cos^{2}\frac{\pi}{12}\,,

(33)

\displaystyle z_{1}=\frac{\sqrt{3}-2}{3\sqrt{3}}=-\frac{(\sqrt{3}-1)^{2}}{6\sqrt{3}}=-\frac{4}{3\sqrt{3}}\,\sin^{2}\frac{\pi}{12}

(34)

of the first pair of Gauss functions in
[
27
, p. 696]
, we write this couple
of evaluations as

{}_{2}F_{1}\Big(a,1-2a;\frac{4}{3}-a;z_{0}\Big)=\Big({-}\frac{27z_{1}}{16}\Big)^{\!-\frac{a}{2}}\,\frac{\cos\frac{\pi}{2}\left(a+\frac{1}{6}\right)}{\cos\frac{\pi}{12}}\,\left.C_{1}(t)\right|_{t=-a/2},

(35)

{}_{2}F_{1}\Big(a,1-2a;\frac{4}{3}-a;z_{1}\Big)=\Big(\frac{27z_{0}}{16}\Big)^{\!-\frac{a}{2}}\,\left.C_{1}(t)\right|_{t=-a/2}\,.

(36)

It easy to observe now, taking into account the second form of
z_{0}
in (
33
),
that the Beukers-Forsgård result
(
36
) is identical to the entry (ix) from the book of Ebisu
[
29
, p.72]
.
Likewise, the result (
35
) is equivalent to
[
29
, p.69, (ix)]
though
the latter is expressed in a somewhat different form.

Obviously, the similarity of evaluations (
35
) and (
36
) suggests to
consider their ratio, which is given by

R_{1}(a):=\frac{{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,1-2a;\frac{4}{3}-a;z_{0}\right)}{{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,1-2a;\frac{4}{3}-a;z_{1}\right)}=2\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}+1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{2a-1}\cos\frac{\pi}{2}\Big(a+\frac{1}{6}\Big),

(37)

just a cosine function modulated by an exponential function.
In the trigonometric form, the same reads

R_{1}(a)=4^{a}\Big(\cos\frac{\pi}{12}\Big)^{2a-1}\cos\frac{\pi}{2}\Big(a+\frac{1}{6}\Big).

(38)

In the special case of
a=\frac{1}{3}
, the ratio
R_{1}(\frac{1}{3})=\left[2(\sqrt{3}-1)\right]^{1/3}

reproduces the second result in (
31
).

The second pair of
{}_{2}F_{1}
functions associated with the ”shift”
\gamma=(-2,4,2)
in

[
27
, p. 696]
also contains identical
\Gamma
-combinations similar to the one
in (
32
). The ratio of these functions is

R_{2}(a):=\frac{{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,2-2a;\frac{5}{3}-a;z_{0}\right)}{{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,2-2a;\frac{5}{3}-a;z_{1}\right)}=2\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}+1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{2a-1}\cos\frac{\pi}{2}\Big(a-\frac{1}{6}\Big).

(39)

As compared to the previous case (see (
37
)), in
{}_{2}F_{1}
functions involved in
(
39
), one of the numerator parameters is increased by 1
and the denominator parameter is bigger by
\frac{1}{3}
.
These simple additive shifts result just in a change of sign inside the argument of
the cosine function!

We already noticed above that another
{}_{2}F_{1}
function in
[
27
, p. 697]
, attributed
to the shift
\gamma=(-2,-2,2)
, contains the same combination of
\Gamma
-functions (
32

35
) – (
36

_{2}F_{1}\Big(a,a+\frac{1}{3};\frac{4}{3}-a;y_{1}\Big)=\left(\frac{81\sqrt{3}}{28}\right)^{\!-\frac{a}{2}}\,\left.C_{1}(t)\right|_{t=-a/2}

(40)

where we introduced the notation
(3\sqrt{3}-5)/4:=y_{1}
for the argument instead of

z_{0}
used in
[
27

Note that the same argument (
23

22

7
Summary and outlook

In the present communication we have revised and extended the validity region of perhaps the most famous hypergeometric transformation by Ramanujan, Berndt, Bhargava and Garvan (
2
).
Evaluations of Gauss hypergeometric functions involved in (
2
) are performed
at the end-points of the established validity region.

It is shown how the Berndt and Chan evaluations related to (
2
) are connected
with the singular value theory of complete elliptic integrals of the first kind.

For more general
{}_{2}F_{1}
evaluations by Ebisu and Beukers-Forsgård,
depending on a free parameter
a
,
the purely algebraic oscillating ratios are calculated and visualized.

It is possible that evaluations of this kind, as well as similar ones, can be
obtained from the classical Goursat’s hypergeometric transformation formulas listed
at the end of his famous work
[
30
]
.

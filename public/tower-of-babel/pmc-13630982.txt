Multi-environment evaluations reveal that peanut (Arachis hypogaea L.) quality traits are equally determined by genotype and environment
Authors: Xiao Jiang, Jianxin Zhao, Jing Xu, Dongmei Yin, Hongfeng Xie, Na Chen, Lijuan Pan, Xiangzhen Yin, Junqing Ma, Xuhong Zhao, Xiaoyuan Chi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPeanut (Arachis hypogaea L.) is a widely planted cash and oil crop, and regional and meteorological factors, as the most critical environmental factors, play crucial roles in peanut kernel quality traits. To explore the relationship between peanut quality traits and environmental factors, this study dissected the quality traits and the genotype-by-environment interaction factors using seven varieties across multiple environments.MethodsAnalysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to partition the variance of kernel quality traits, correlation analysis was used to examine the associations between quality traits and environmental factors, and genotype plus genotype-by-environment (GGE) biplot analysis was used to evaluate the association between genotypes and multiple environments.ResultsANOVA showed that environmental factors were the largest source of variance among the three quality traits. Correlation analysis showed that long sunshine duration, high accumulated temperature, and large mean daily temperature difference were beneficial to the accumulation of oil and protein. Oil content showed an overall trend of increasing with latitude; protein content performed best in the Huang-Huai-Hai and Yangtze River Basin production areas; and sucrose content showed an overall trend of decreasing with latitude and longitude. GGE analysis further showed that HY33 achieved optimal oil content with strong multi-environment stability, while HY21 had superior protein content and the most stable sucrose performance across multi-environments.ConclusionsThis study provides theoretical guidance for adjusting planting arrangements in different regions according to different climatic conditions and peanut growing periods, which is of great significance as a guide for improving peanut quality.

Introduction

Peanut (Arachis hypogaea L.) is a globally important cash and oil crop. In China, the annual sowing area of peanut exceeds 4.6 million hectares, with pod yield surpassing 17 million tons, ranking first in the world (FAOSTAT, http://faostat.fao.org). As a rich source of protein, vegetable oils, vitamins, and minerals, peanuts serve as a crucial supply of edible oil and dietary protein for humans. Although peanut cultivation is widely distributed across China, distinct environmental conditions prevail in different regions, making peanut quality highly susceptible to environmental influences. Specifically, peanut quality is closely associated with climatic resources such as temperature, precipitation, and sunshine in the cultivation area. Thus, identifying peanut varieties suitable for cultivation in specific regions remains a critical challenge, and addressing this issue is of great significance for ensuring China’s oil crop security, increasing farmers’ income, and promoting the sustainable development of the peanut industry.

Extensive studies confirm genetics govern peanut quality, yet environmental effects remain non-negligible (Dwivedi et al., 2000). The regulatory mechanisms by which meteorological factors affect crop quality traits are relatively complex. Meteorological factors (light, temperature, water) modulate crop development, strongly altering yield, agronomic performance and quality in wheat, rice and maize (Han et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2016; Vida et al., 2022; David Kulyakwave et al., 2020; Jiayu et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025). Many crops have adopted agro-climatic zoning with critical climatic indicators to assess climatic suitability for cultivation (Wei et al., 2022). Relevant zoning cases include Ningxia wine grape regionalization using effective accumulated temperature (Wang et al., 2020) and crop zoning based on ≥10 °C accumulated temperature combined with fuzzy evaluation (Zhao et al., 2019). Likewise, peanut yield and agronomic traits are jointly shaped by environment and genotype-by-environment (G×E) interaction. Long-term nationwide data proved temperature, rainfall and sunshine considerably affect peanut yield (Wang et al., 2013), with growing-season precipitation as the dominant limiting factor for spring peanut yield (Chen et al., 2019). Environmental conditions exert especially strong influences on peanut oil and protein concentrations, highlighting the necessity of precise precipitation management (Kebede and Getahun, 2017). Although recent work recognizes water and sunshine as key regulators of peanut yield and quality, few reports systematically decode meteorological and geographic impacts on peanut kernel quality, lacking supporting evidence for region-specific peanut planting across China’s major peanut production areas.

Analyzing genotype-by-environment (G×E) effects of peanut varieties across diverse ecologies is a major research hotspot. Multiple statistical approaches quantify G×E interactions, including ANOVA, PCA, linear regression, AMMI, and GGE biplot (Vlachostergios and Roupakias, 2008; Serneels and Verdonck, 2008; Hristov et al., 2010; Lingaiah et al., 2020; Yan and Rajcan, 2002). The GGE biplot stands out for visualizing genotypic, environmental and G×E effects via two-dimensional graphs to screen adaptive planting zones. Current Chinese peanut climate zoning studies are constrained by limited time-series data, narrow sampling, single indicators, and ignorance of reproductive-stage differences. Widely adopted for multi-environment crop trials covering wheat, maize, rice and other crops, GGE biplot also dissects trait correlations (Jat et al., 2018; Yan and Hunt, 2001; Setimela et al., 2007; Chintalapati et al., 2019; Katuuramu et al., 2020; Bai et al., 2018; Shukla et al., 2015; Lamichaney et al., 2021; Nagesh Kumar et al., 2021; Ansarifard et al., 2020). In peanut research, this method evaluates yield, kernel quality, stress resistance, phosphorus efficiency and biochemical profiles (Oteng-Frimpong and Dakora, 2019; Tang et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2024; Zhen et al., 2024, Zhen et al., 2023). A U.S. study tested 52 peanut germplasm and confirmed Florida-grown peanuts had maximum oil and minimum protein with inverse oil–protein correlation (Wang et al., 2024). A Chinese trial with 13 peanut materials cultivated in three environments reported superior oil and protein in Weifang peanuts and peak sucrose content in Jinzhou peanuts (Tang et al., 2025). No study has yet revealed how meteorological and geographic factors shape peanut kernel quality across multiple environments. Moreover, few works identify stably adaptable varieties suitable for widespread cultivation in China’s major peanut production areas.

Aiming to explore the relationship between peanut kernel quality traits and meteorological and geographical factors, in the present study, we conducted a multi-environment evaluation using peanut varieties’ phenotypic data on the traits of oil, protein and sucrose content in 17 locations during 2013, 2014, and 2016, crossing five major peanut-producing areas in China and spanning a geographical range from 21.09°N to 43.07°N and from 106.08°E to 124.23°E, along with data on eight meteorological and geographical factors for each environment. The findings of this study will clarify the impact of meteorological factors on peanut quality and provide a scientific basis for adjusting peanut planting arrangements in different regions according to local climatic conditions and peanut growth periods.

Materials and Methods

Plant materials and planting locations

Huayu 19 (HY19), Huayu 21 (HY21), Huayu 30 (HY30), Huayu 33 (HY33), Jihua 10 (JH10), Jihua 9 (JH9) and Xuhua 13 (XH13) were used as test varieties. Each plot covered an area of 6.67 m2, consisting of 4 rows distributed across 2 ridges. The row length within each plot was approximately 4.17 m, with a total plot width of about 1.6 m. Two seed rows were sown on each ridge at a hill spacing of 15 cm, with 2 seeds per hill and approximately 110 hills per plot. The trial was arranged in a randomized complete block design with three replications for each environment.

To evaluate variety performance and multi-environment stability of quality traits under conditions representative of local farming practices, all experimental fields were selected as flat plots with moderate fertility. Field management practices including fertilization, pest and disease control, and weeding all followed routine local agronomic protocols. All experimental locations were located in monsoon regions, where the peanut growing season coincided with the rainy season; crops relied mainly on natural rainfall, and supplemental irrigation was rarely required.

To evaluate peanut kernel quality traits across multiple environments, the tested peanut varieties were planted at 17 locations covering five major peanut-producing regions across 16 provinces in China, including Shandong, Shanxi, Liaoning, Jilin, Henan, Hebei, Jiangsu, Anhui, Fujian, Jiangxi, Guangdong, Guangxi, Guizhou, Sichuan, Hunan and Hubei (Figure 1A) during 2013, 2014 and 2016. A total of 51 environments were used for the evaluation of peanut quality traits. Among these locations, Zhanjiang (ZJ), Ganzhou (GZ), Quanzhou (QZ) and Hezhou (HZ) belong to the South China production area; Nanchong (NC), Shaoyang (SY), Huanggang (HG) and Hefei (HF) belong to the Yangtze River Basin production area; Zhumadian (ZMD), Kaifeng (KF), Xuzhou (XZ), Weifang (WF), Baoding (BD) and Fenyang (FY) belong to the Huang-Huai-Hai production area; Jinzhou (JZ) and Siping (SP) belong to the Northeast Plain production area; Guiyang (GY) belongs to the Yunnan-Guizhou Plateau production area (Figure 1A).

Multi-environment oil, protein and sucrose contents identification and statistical analysis

To accurately evaluate the peanut quality traits of the seven varieties harvested from 51 environments, oil, protein and sucrose contents were determined in accordance with China National Standard (CNS), using the Soxhlet extraction method (AOAC 995.19), Kjeldahl nitrogen determination method (AOAC Ac 4-91) and acid hydrolysis method (AOAC 920.184), respectively. Phenotypic data of the 3 peanut quality traits for each variety in every environment were collected all the three biological replicates, and statistically analyzed using Microsoft Office 2019. Meanwhile, the phenotypic data for each variety in every environment were averaged using three biological replicates for the followed analysis.

Meteorological data collection and statistical analysis

Meteorological data for each test location in 2013, 2014 and 2016 were obtained from the China Meteorological Data Network (http://data.cma.cn). Meteorological factors across the entire peanut growth period, from sowing to harvest, in 51 environments were statistically analyzed, including accumulated temperature ≥ 10 °C, total precipitation and total sunshine duration. The mean daily temperature difference and mean daily temperature during the entire growth period for each environment were also calculated.

Linear regression and correlation analysis between peanut quality traits and environmental factors

To elucidate the contributions of environmental and genetic factors to variations in peanut quality traits across multiple environments, two‑way ANOVA (genotype, G; environment, E) was performed for oil, protein, and sucrose contents across 51 environments using the aov function in RStudio (R version 4.4.2).

To investigate the relationships between quality traits and environmental factors, Pearson correlation analysis, which was implemented using the ggcorplot and corplot packages in R Studio (R version 4.4.2), was employed to analyze and visualize the correlations between peanut quality traits and environmental factors across 51 environments. Environmental factors, which consisted of geographical variables including longitude, latitude and altitude as well as meteorological variables including accumulated temperature ≥ 10 °C, total precipitation, total sunshine duration, mean daily temperature difference and mean daily temperature during the entire growth period, were used in the correlation analysis.

GGE Biplot analysis for oil, protein and sucrose contents traits

Phenotypic data of peanut oil, protein and sucrose contents in the 51 environments were conducted GGE biplot analysis using the GGE Biplots GUI package in RStudio (R version 4.4.2). A combined analysis of variance was conducted according to the experiment design, with genotypes and locations as fixed effects. Variation in the three peanut quality traits was partitioned into parts due to environment, genotype, G×E interaction, and error. Duncan’s multiple range test (DMRT) was utilized for multiple comparison. The GGE biplot analysis was utilized for environmental vector, which-won-where, discrimination ability vs. representativeness and mean vs. stability analysis. The GGE biplot, which was constructed using the first two principal axes (Axis 1 and Axis 2), was derived from singular value decomposition (SVD) of environment-centered data that contained the GGE signal. Oil, protein, and sucrose contents were identified across 51 environments during three years. With respect to geographical factors, HG, FY, and QZ exhibited the highest oil content (Figures 2A, 3A). Similarly, HF, GZ, and NC were the top three locations for protein content (Figures 2B, 3B). Meanwhile, ZJ, HZ, and GY showed the highest sucrose content (Figures 2C, 3C).

Multi-environmental ANOVA for genotypic and environmental factors

To elucidate the contributions of environmental and genetic factors to variations in peanut quality traits across multiple environments, two‑way ANOVA was performed for oil, protein, and sucrose contents across 51 environments. The relative contributions of G, E, and G×E to total variation were 5.22%, 68.87%, and 25.91% for oil content; 0.65%, 63.61%, and 35.74% for protein content; and 1.69%, 65.47%, and 32.84% for sucrose content, respectively (Table 2). The results revealed that environmental factors accounted for the largest proportion of variance for all three quality traits (all > 50%). Meanwhile, oil, protein, and sucrose contents were also substantially affected by genotype‑by‑environment interaction, indicating that environmental factors strongly influence phenotypic variation and that significant G×E interaction exists.

Linear regression and correlation analysis between peanut quality traits and environmental factors

To examine the relationships between peanut quality traits and geographical and meteorological factors, linear regression and correlation analysis were conducted. Linear regression analysis showed that oil content was positively and linearly correlated with accumulated temperature ≥ 10 °C (R2 = 0.12, P = 0.011) and total sunshine duration (R2 = 0.13, P = 0.018) during the entire growth period. Protein content was positively and linearly correlated with mean daily temperature (R2 = 0.087, P = 0.031). Sucrose content was negatively and linearly correlated with latitude (R2 = 0.031, P = 0.097), longitude (R2 = 0.038, P = 0.45), accumulated temperature ≥ 10 °C (R2 = 0.098, P = 0.058), and total sunshine duration (R2 = 0.051, P = 0.12) (Supplementary Figure S1). The results of linear regression analysis showed that the goodness of fit between quality traits and single geographical or meteorological factor was extremely low (R2) and non-significant (P > 0.05), indicating that quality traits may be jointly affected by multiple factors.

Correlation analysis results showed that oil content was extremely significant positive correlated with longitude, accumulated temperature ≥ 10 °C, and total sunshine duration (p<0.01). Protein content was extremely significant positive correlated with accumulated temperature ≥ 10 °C and mean daily temperature(p<0.01), and significant positive correlated with longitude and mean daily temperature difference (p<0.01). Sucrose content was extremely significant negative correlated with longitude, latitude, accumulated temperature ≥ 10 °C, and total sunshine duration (p<0.01) (Figure 5).

Discussion

Peanut is a widely cultivated cash and oil crop in China, and regional and meteorological factors, which serve as the most critical environmental drivers, exert a pivotal influence on peanut kernel quality traits. Its cultivation areas, which span temperate and subtropical monsoon climates, are geographically divided into six major agro-ecological zones. The Huang-Huai-Hai production area, which mainly includes Shandong, Henan, and Hebei, is characterized by a temperate monsoon climate with abundant sunshine duration and is mainly used for planting large-grain and high-oleic-acid varieties. Owing to the fertile soil of the Yellow River alluvial plains, peanut production in the Huang-Huai-Hai production area always exhibits excellent yield and quality (Figures 1B, 4). The South China production area, which mainly includes Guangdong, Guangxi, Fujian, and Hainan, features a tropical and subtropical climate with high temperature, abundant rainfall, and a long frost-free period, which makes it suitable for producing processed peanuts such as salted, dried, and roasted peanuts. The Yangtze River Basin production area, which mainly includes Hubei, Hunan, and Jiangxi, has a subtropical monsoon climate with abundant rainfall and high humidity in summer, where summer-sown peanuts are dominant under wheat or rape stubble rotation systems. The Northeast Plain production area, which covers Liaoning and Jilin, features a temperate continental climate with cold winters and a short growing period, where spring-sown and early-maturing varieties dominate peanut production. The Yunnan-Guizhou Plateau production area, which belongs to a plateau mountain climate with significant vertical differences and large diurnal temperature variations, is where peanuts are planted in hills and mountains at an altitude of 1,000–2,000 m with spring sowing as the main practice. The Northwestern production area, which includes Xinjiang and Gansu, is under a temperate continental arid climate with strong sunshine, limited precipitation, and large diurnal temperature variation.

Complex meteorological and geographical factors act jointly to affect peanut yield and quality traits in China, and meteorological factors such as light, temperature, and water, which are key environmental regulators, contribute to divergent peanut growth performances (Ai et al., 2025; Jongrungklang et al., 2012; Kumar et al., 2012; Qi et al., 2025; Zhen et al., 2024). Meanwhile, climate change, which has become a major threat, affects the stable production of peanuts ( (Khan et al., 2009; Wei et al., 2022; Wheeler and von Braun, 2013; Ye et al., 2013). Therefore, to clarify the effects of meteorological and geographical factors on peanut kernel quality traits, we performed a multi-environment evaluation using phenotypic data of seven major popularized varieties planted at 17 locations over three years, which generated a total of 51 environments, together with synchronized meteorological and geographical data for each environment. The results showed that oil content was extremely significantly and positively correlated with longitude, accumulated temperature ≥ 10 °C, and total sunshine duration across the entire growth period. Protein content was extremely significantly and positively correlated with accumulated temperature ≥ 10 °C and mean daily temperature across the entire growth period, and significantly and positively correlated with longitude and mean daily temperature difference. Sucrose content was extremely significantly and negatively correlated with longitude, latitude, accumulated temperature ≥ 10 °C, and total sunshine duration across the entire growth period. Multi-environment phenotypic data revealed that oil and protein contents displayed a spatial trend of being higher in eastern China and lower in western China, while sucrose content showed a trend of being higher in southeastern China and lower in northwestern China. Furthermore, correlation results between quality traits and meteorological factors suggested the feasibility of simultaneous improvement in peanut oil and protein contents. Two-way ANOVA showed that environmental factors accounted for approximately 60% of the total variation in peanut oil, protein, and sucrose contents, which indicates that most differences in peanut kernel quality traits across multiple environments are caused by environmental factors. For peanut oil, protein, and sucrose contents, environmental effects are comparable to genetic effects. Single meteorological factor performed low linear regression and non-significant with the three kernel quality traits those indicated that a single factor cannot adequately explain the variation in quality traits, and multi-factor interactions are a key focus for future research.

Genotype, environment, and genotype-by-environment interaction (GEI) have long been research focuses for crop breeders, and GEI, which represents one of the most complex constraints, challenges breeding programs aiming to identify high-quality and stable peanut genotypes. Recently, GEI and GGE biplot analyses have been applied in studies of peanut yield, kernel quality traits, biotic stress tolerance, phosphorus-efficient genotype identification, and sensory and biochemical composition (Tang et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2024; Zhen et al., 2024, Zhen et al., 2023). In the US, Wang et al. (2024) used 52 peanut germplasm accessions, found that Florida produced the highest oil content and the lowest protein content with a negative correlation between oil and protein contents. In China, Tang et al. (2025), who used 11 germplasm accessions and two varieties planted across three environments located in the Huang-Huai-Hai production area (Weifang) and the Northeast Plain production area (Jinzhou and Fuxin), found that Weifang showed the best oil and protein contents while Jinzhou had the highest sucrose content. Compared with their findings, our results are consistent in that protein and oil contents are positively correlated with accumulated temperature, whereas sucrose content is negatively correlated with accumulated temperature.

In this study, we used seven popularized peanut varieties planted at 17 locations distributed across five major peanut production areas. For oil content, the 51 environments were classified into four mega-environments, in which XH13, HY33, HY19, and HY21 were the optimal genotypes, which indicates that these varieties may perform better in corresponding environments or locations. Meanwhile, HY33 and HY30 showed the highest oil content and strongest multi-environmental stability. For protein content, the 51 environments were divided into three mega-environments, in which HY19, HY21, and JH10 were the optimal genotypes with the best performance in each mega-environment. HY21 exhibited the highest mean protein content but relatively low multi-environmental stability, whereas JH9 ranked second in protein content with far superior multi-environmental stability. For sucrose content, the GGE biplot partitioned the environments into four sectors, in which HY19, HY21, HY33, and XH13 were the optimal genotypes with the best sucrose performance in each mega-environment. HY21 was the optimal genotype in 30 environments across 5 production areas, meanwhile, it showed the highest mean sucrose content and strongest multi-environmental stability. Based on a comprehensive analysis of the three peanut kernel quality traits, HY33 was identified as the superior stable oil variety and HY21 was the top performer for protein and sucrose, which suggests that they are suitable for wide promotion across multiple production areas and can be used as elite breeding parents for developing trait-specific varieties.

Meteorological and geographical factors, which are critical indicators, play essential roles in modern agricultural cultivation planning. Ecological regions for cotton fiber quality have been delineated in China’s major cotton planting regions and in Spain, and ecological zoning has also been established for barley, maize, and other crops (Baxevanos et al., 2008). In the modern peanut industry, temperature has been used as a zoning indicator, and climatic zones suitable for different ecological peanut varieties in China have been identified. In addition, a national peanut suitability region map has been developed based on the interactive effects of climatic and soil factors on peanut cultivation. Therefore, analyzing the regional characteristics of peanut growth and ecological resources, which is crucial for evaluating peanut ecological suitability, supports rational planting division, peanut cropping structure adjustment, and optimized production layout tailored to local conditions. Among the three-year evaluation, GZ, KF, HG, and QZ showed environmental stability for oil content; QZ, KF, and HG showed environmental stability for protein content; and KF and FY exhibited environmental stability for sucrose content. By integrating the three quality traits, we found that KF had relatively stable climatic conditions, and peanut kernel quality traits in KF showed good repeatability and strong representativeness across diverse locations. These findings provide supportive information and evidence for peanut industry planning in China.

Conclusion

In this study, a multi-environment evaluation was conducted, which investigated peanut kernel quality traits of seven popularized peanut varieties at 17 locations over three years. The cultivation trials, which covered five major peanut-producing areas and nearly all major peanut-producing provinces in China, spanned a geographical range from 21.09°N to 43.07°N and from 106.08°E to 124.23°E. The evaluation demonstrated that entire growth period accumulated temperature ≥ 10 °C and total sunshine duration can enhance oil content, while accumulated temperature ≥ 10 °C, mean daily temperature, and mean daily temperature difference can increase protein content. In contrast, sucrose content was extremely significantly and negatively correlated with accumulated temperature ≥ 10 °C and total sunshine duration. Oil and protein contents showed a spatial pattern of being higher in eastern China and lower in western China, which revealed the mechanism that Yangtze River Basin and Huang-Huai-Hai production areas had the highest oil and protein contents. While sucrose content displayed a pattern of being higher in southeastern China and lower in northwestern China. ANOVA revealed that environmental factors contributed approximately 60% of the total variation in peanut oil, protein, and sucrose contents, which suggests that environmental effects are comparable to genetic effects on peanut kernel quality traits. GGE stability analysis confirmed HY33 as the optimal stable cultivar for high oil concentration, while HY21 combined superior protein levels with maximum sucrose content and cross-region stability. In addition, KF had relatively stable climatic conditions and the strongest representativeness across diverse locations. These findings supply empirical guidance for peanut breeding and regional industrial layout optimization both domestically and internationally.

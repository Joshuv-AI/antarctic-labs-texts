Exploration and analysis of the application of the internet of things in nursing education in the 5G era: innovation of nursing education methods
Authors: Hongshi Zhang, Yujiao Ding, Zilin Wang, Ye Zhang, Xiaotu Zhang, Lili Zhu, Zhang Hongshi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNursing education is of great significance for cultivating high-quality nursing professionals. The introduction of Internet of Things (IoT) technology can create new opportunities for the development of nursing education. This study aims to explore the application value, application models, main challenges, and future development directions of IoT technology in nursing education in the 5G era, and to put forward corresponding suggestions, so as to provide a practical reference for nursing educators to introduce smart teaching methods.MethodsA literature search was conducted in the PubMed, CiteXplore, and CNKI databases using specific keywords and terms. A total of 8024 articles were retrieved, of which 70 were ultimately selected for inclusion in this narrative review. The thematic induction method was employed to organize and analyze the literature, aiming to extract the core value, application models, existing challenges, and development suggestions for the application of IoT in nursing education.ResultsThe core value of IoT technology in nursing education is primarily reflected in four aspects: enhancing students’ clinical practice experience, improving teaching efficiency for educators, increasing the practical relevance of specialized nursing fields, and enhancing students’ clinical practice readiness. This technology can be deeply integrated into both theoretical and practical teaching, permeating all teaching segments including pre-class, in-class, and post-class activities, thereby forming diverse smart teaching application models. However, the current promotion and application of IoT in nursing education still face multiple challenges, mainly including privacy and data security issues, technological dependence and risks of equipment malfunction, as well as insufficient acceptance of new technologies by teachers and students.ConclusionIoT technology holds significant promise for application in nursing education and can effectively promote innovation and development in nursing teaching methods. Future efforts should focus on ensuring data privacy and security, reducing technological dependency, and enhancing equipment stability and user acceptance to facilitate the deep integration of IoT technology with nursing education.

Introduction

Due to factors such as population aging, an increasing burden of disease, the expansion of healthcare policies, a shortage of educational resources, low remuneration, high occupational stress, and limited career development opportunities, the global shortage of nursing professionals is becoming increasingly severe. Although the total number of nurses worldwide has increased over the past decade, the progress in closing the nursing workforce gap has slowed, with uneven development across different regions [1, 2]. As nurses are indispensable members of the medical team, the quality of their training is of great significance to maintaining the stability of the medical and health system and safeguarding people’s lives and health. Improving the quality of nursing talent training has become an important issue facing nursing education [3, 4]. However, against the backdrop of the rapid development of the medical industry today, nursing education faces many challenges. How to cultivate nursing professionals who possess both solid professional knowledge and the ability to flexibly deal with various clinical problems has become an urgent issue [5].

The fifth-generation mobile communication technology (5G) is a new generation of broadband mobile communication technology characterized by high data rates, low latency, and massive connectivity, serving as the network infrastructure for the interconnection of humans, machines, and things. The so-called 5G era refers to the period dominated by this fifth-generation mobile communication technology [6]. The advent of 5G technology has created significant opportunities for the prosperous development of IoT devices and intelligent wireless sensor nodes [7]. As one of the core technologies of the Fourth Industrial Revolution, IoT is profoundly transforming the educational landscape amid the wave of digital transformation [8]. By enabling real-time collection and analysis of student performance data and constructing enhanced learning environments, IoT technology creates novel learning engagement pathways for students, fosters their autonomous learning capabilities, and provides data-driven decision-making support for continuous improvement of learning outcomes [9]. The introduction of IoT technology has propelled education towards personalization and interactivity, and has also brought new opportunities and challenges to the field of nursing education.

The introduction of IoT technology has created new development opportunities for nursing education. Research shows that, with the help of the IoT platform, nursing students can enhance their learning experience through group discussions, instant feedback, and resource sharing. Students and teachers can also communicate in real time, sharing learning resources and practical experience. In addition, IoT technology also supports gamified learning, where students can simulate nursing practice in a virtual environment, thereby increasing the fun and engagement of learning [10]. However, despite the great potential of IoT in nursing education, its application still faces many challenges. Issues such as privacy and data security, technological vulnerabilities, and user acceptance may limit the application of the IoT + education model in the teaching of nursing [11–13]. This study aims to explore the application value, application models, main challenges, and future development directions of IoT technology in nursing education in the 5G era, and to propose suggestions accordingly, so as to provide a practical reference for nursing educators to introduce smart teaching methods.

Methods

Research questions

What is the core value of IoT in nursing education and teaching? What are the specific application models of IoT technology across teaching content (theoretical and practical) and teaching segments (pre-class, in-class, post-class)? What are the main challenges facing IoT-enabled smart nursing education? How should we effectively address these challenges?

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: (1) The topic involves the application of IoT technology in nursing education or medical education; (2) Study types include original research, reviews, etc.; (3) The language is Chinese or English.

Exclusion criteria: (1) The literature has issues such as missing data, logical inconsistencies, or unfounded conclusions, failing to provide valid references for the study; (2) Duplicate publications: For duplicate publications by the same research team, only the most recent and comprehensive version was included.

Information sources

Literature searches were conducted using PubMed, CiteXplore, and the China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI). These databases were selected as they span multiple fields, covering relevant topics in education, medical education, engineering, basic sciences, and humanities. Additionally, the reference lists of articles retrieved via Google Scholar were screened to identify additional relevant literature.

Search strategy

A search strategy combining subject headings and free-text terms was employed, with the search timeframe covering the period from database inception to October 28, 2025. Chinese search terms included “Internet of Things”, “sensor”, “nursing education”, “nursing teaching”, and “smart education”. English search terms included “Internet of Things”, “IoT”, “nursing education”, “nursing teaching”, and “smart education”. The English search strategy was as follows:

(“Internet of Things“[Title/Abstract] OR “IoT“[Title/Abstract] OR “Internet of Everything“[Title/Abstract] OR “Sensor Networks“[Title/Abstract] OR “Internet of Things“[Mesh]) AND (“Nursing Education“[Title/Abstract] OR “Medical Education“[Title/Abstract] OR “Smart Education“[Title/Abstract] OR “Virtual Teaching“[Title/Abstract] OR “Online Education“[Title/Abstract] OR “Distance Education“[Title/Abstract] OR “Digital Teaching“[Title/Abstract] OR “Intelligent Teaching“[Title/Abstract] OR “Education“[Title/Abstract] OR “Education, Nursing“[Mesh])

Selection process

Peer-reviewed articles, conference papers, and reviews focusing on the integrated application of IoT technology in education were included based on title and abstract screening. Full-text articles were then read, excluding those without direct relevance to nursing education and medical education contexts or lacking substantial content. Additionally, the reference lists of key articles were manually screened to identify further relevant studies. A thematic synthesis of the retrieved literature was performed to identify core themes, application models, challenges, and future development directions (Fig. 1).

Review type and methodological statement

Notably, this study is a narrative review. Although we employed a structured database search strategy and explicit inclusion/exclusion criteria during literature retrieval and screening (Fig. 1), this does not mean that the study is a systematic review or a scoping review. The aim of this review is to provide a thematic and interpretive synthesis of the existing literature, in order to delineate the value, models, challenges, and future directions of IoT applications in nursing education. The structured retrieval and screening process was employed to enhance the transparency and replicability of literature collection, thereby mitigating the selective bias commonly observed in traditional narrative reviews. Furthermore, this review aims to map the application models of IoT in nursing education as reported in the literature, which range from experimental prototypes to conceptual proposals. This review does not evaluate the actual adoption, extent of dissemination, or educational effectiveness of these technologies.

Construction of a thematic framework

To systematically address the research questions, this article constructs an analytical framework following a progressive logic of “theoretical foundation—core value—practical application—challenges and prospects.” This framework is based on two categories of evidence: current established nursing education practices and innovative, emerging approaches reported in the literature. Thus, it presents both feasible existing applications and potential future developments still at the experimental or conceptual stage. Before providing specific examples, we explicitly distinguish between current practical applications and future-oriented concepts to set clear expectations for readers. The first part outlines the technical architecture of IoT and its educational applications, laying the theoretical foundation for subsequent analysis. The second part distills the core value of IoT in nursing education, directly addressing the research question: “What is the core value of IoT in nursing education?” The third part delineates specific application models across the two dimensions of teaching content and teaching process, presenting both currently feasible pathways and exploratory future scenarios. The fourth part identifies key challenges and proposes corresponding strategies, forming a closed loop for the research. The thematic framework of this study was pre-constructed based on the research questions and iteratively refined throughout the literature analysis process.

Overview of the application of IoT technology in the field of education

The Internet of Things (IoT) refers to the use of various information sensing devices and technologies, such as Radio Frequency Identification (RFID), Global Positioning Systems (GPS), infrared sensors, and laser scanners, to collect diverse status information of objects that need to be monitored or connected (including parameters such as sound, light, heat, electricity, mechanics, chemistry, biology, and location) in real-time. This technological system transmits the above information through the Internet to achieve ubiquitous interconnection between things, and between things and people, thereby enabling intelligent perception, recognition, and management of items and their processes [14, 15]. The term “IoT” was first proposed by Kevin Ashton in 1999 [16]. In 2005, the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) suggested that IoT should achieve “anytime, anywhere connections for everyone and everything.” In November 2011, the ITU’s Global Standards Initiative on IoT (IoT-GSI) formally defined IoT, describing it as the infrastructure for the global information society. It utilizes existing and evolving information and communication technologies to interconnect physical and virtual objects, thereby enabling advanced services [17]. With the rapid development of information technology, the application of IoT in the field of education is becoming increasingly extensive [18]. The integration of IoT technology into the field of education refers to the construction of smarter learning environments through the use of smart devices, sensors, and data processing and analysis. By deeply integrating technology with educational scenarios, it enables real-time data collection and analysis, thereby optimizing the teaching and learning process and enhancing educational effectiveness and management efficiency [19]. The basic architecture of IoT technology comprises three layers: the perception layer, the network layer, and the application layer [20]. The perception layer is responsible for collecting data from the physical world. Its sensor technology can intelligently perceive and locate changes in physical, chemical, or biological signals, and convert signals such as sound, light, and electricity into outputtable electrical or digital signals, providing a data basis for subsequent processing [21, 22]. In educational scenarios, this layer can be used to collect and capture various types of data information generated during the teaching process [23]. It is worth emphasizing that wearable devices constitute an important component of this layer [24]. Through the widespread application of IoT terminals such as wearable devices—including heart rate monitoring straps, GPS positioning devices, biosensors (for electromyography and blood oxygen monitoring), and motion capture systems—automated, real-time collection of core data such as training load, physiological parameters, and technical movements can be achieved [25]. Existing research has shown that physical education teaching movement monitoring systems, built on wearable devices and functioning through the collection, processing, analysis, and visual presentation of movement data, can help teachers and students more intuitively understand movement status and skill improvement, thereby enhancing teaching effectiveness [26]. The network layer is responsible for data transmission, employing both wired and wireless communication technologies. Among these, wireless communication technologies such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ZigBee, LoRa, and NB-IoT offer distinct advantages in terms of transmission distance, power consumption, and data rate, making them suitable for a wide range of IoT application scenarios [27]. This layer can form a crucial part of campus network infrastructure. By strategically deploying wired and wireless networking technologies, it interconnects various hardware devices within the educational environment, establishing a unified and interconnected network system that supports the coordinated control of IoT devices [23]. The application layer handles data storage, analysis, and utilization. It leverages technologies such as cloud computing and decision support systems to store, process, and analyze data, extract valuable insights, and convert the analytical results into a basis for management and decision-making [28]. In educational environments, the application layer can build teaching application service platforms based on processed educational data, providing comprehensive smart education services for teachers and students. These services include centralized control of hardware devices, classroom access and attendance management, and intelligent teaching management [23].

As a representative of new-generation information technology, IoT technology is reconstructing campus management and education models with its systematic data collection, device interconnection, and intelligent analysis capabilities. In terms of intelligent campus management, IoT constructs an efficient linkage and regulation mechanism by integrating intelligent metering terminals (such as smart electricity meters) and environmental sensors (such as temperature and humidity sensors, and light sensors). The intelligent classroom management system can comprehensively regulate the status of lighting, air conditioning, and equipment operation, and dynamically optimize these systems based on real-time environmental parameters. While ensuring the comfort of the teaching environment for teachers and students, it can significantly improve energy utilization efficiency and achieve energy-saving goals [29, 30]. In intelligent attendance management, based on technologies such as Radio Frequency Identification (RFID), facial recognition, and fingerprint recognition, the IoT attendance system can automatically identify personnel identities and synchronize data to the cloud and local databases in real-time via a wireless network. This mode breaks through the efficiency bottleneck of traditional manual attendance and provides precise attendance data support for teaching management [31]. The transformation of the teaching process relies on IoT technology to achieve efficient and personalized transitions. The extensive integration of mobile learning applications and various IoT applications enables students to conveniently access learning resources, complete assignments, and engage in project collaborations, significantly enhancing the accessibility of learning resources and the flexibility of learning activities [32]. IoT sensors can collect real-time physiological data from students, such as skin resistance, brain waves, and pulse rate, to identify students’ classroom stress levels and engagement in learning [33]. At the same time, teachers can further combine multidimensional learning behavior data recorded by the IoT system, such as students’ task completion progress and frequency of interactive participation, to conduct precise teaching effectiveness assessments and optimize teaching strategies and learning support systems accordingly [34]. IoT technology also shows unique value in promoting special education and building inclusive learning environments. By seamlessly integrating wireless devices, it transforms the learning process into a more “people-oriented” experience and provides highly personalized support pathways for learners with special needs [35]. Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA) employs techniques based on learning principles to understand and modify behaviors, demonstrating clear efficacy in improving communication, social interactions, and adaptive behaviors of children with autism [36, 37]. Special education intervention systems integrating IoT and Artificial Intelligence (AI) technologies enhance the therapeutic outcomes of ABA through multidimensional data collection and analysis, thereby optimizing the efficacy of ABA therapy [38]. In conclusion, IoT technology is deeply penetrating and reshaping the modern educational ecosystem at multiple levels, including infrastructure management, innovation of core teaching processes, and empowerment of special education. Its continued development and deepening application will surely lay the foundation for building a more intelligent, efficient, personalized, and inclusive future education system.

The value embodied by IoT in advancing nursing education

Enhancing clinical practice experience

Virtual Reality (VR) technology, a derivative of IoT, can create high-fidelity virtual wards, providing students with opportunities for simulated clinical operation training [39, 40]. For example, the Virtual Clinical Simulation (VCS) system can reproduce the scene of a trauma ward, including elements such as hospital beds, medical instruments, and simulated patients. In this setting, students can train in device operation, patient monitoring, and emergency response, faithfully replicating real clinical situations [41]. Moreover, interactive VR offers a dynamic learning platform for medical training: learners engage with unfolding cases and converse with virtual patients, colleagues, and relatives within the ward. This interaction heightens immersion and diversifies practice, sharpening students’ hands-on skills in history-taking, physical examination, diagnosis, and treatment [42]. This IoT-based virtual simulation laboratory expands the ways students practice and provides an innovative and efficient solution for medical education—especially when resources are limited or hands-on opportunities are scarce.

Ran Xingcheng et al. [43] proposed that the application of IoT technology can effectively break through the spatial and practical limitations of book-based or traditional intuitive teaching methods in nursing education. Through the medical demonstration system constructed with IoT technology—without entering the scene or being restricted by the viewing space of special wards such as operating rooms and imaging departments—real-time images of ward conditions, diagnosis and treatment processes, and key teaching links can be transmitted. This creates opportunities for remote learning for nursing students. Students can observe real-life clinical scenarios in an immersive way, gain direct clinical experience, and engage in in-depth case discussions and clinical decision-making discussions. This approach makes up for the inability to learn on-site at any time and increases students’ clinical experience.

Enhancing teaching efficiency for educators

The shortage of faculty is one of the factors that restrict the development of nursing education [44]. The application of IoT technology provides a new way to break through this bottleneck. By combining wireless sensor networks with cloud service platforms, IoT can achieve cross-regional integration and sharing of high-quality teaching resources. Teachers can upload course content, teaching videos, assignments, and assessment criteria to the cloud, and students in other regions can obtain synchronized learning resources and guidance through remote access [45, 46]. This breaks the dependence of traditional teaching on local faculty, enhances the convenience and flexibility of obtaining teaching content, and significantly expands the service coverage of limited faculty, thus alleviating the contradiction of “few teachers and high demand” in nursing education, which can indirectly improve the teaching efficiency of teachers.

Furthermore, the IoT-based intelligent grading system can help alleviate teachers’ workload. The system can accurately recognize students’ handwritten responses and achieve real-time synchronization between instructors’ feedback and student answers, establishing an efficient remote collaborative teaching environment. Additionally, the nursing education system’s student answer analysis feature enables educators to comprehensively monitor students’ mastery of knowledge points, promptly identify frequently missed questions and students with learning difficulties, and thereby provide data support for personalized instruction. The integration of in-class quiz mode also enables teachers to quickly release in-class tests and collect student feedback in real time, which significantly saves classroom management time and allows teachers to devote more energy to the teaching content itself, thereby enhancing the relevance and timeliness of teaching [47].

Enhance the attractiveness of practice in certain nursing fields

In nursing education, special fields such as geriatric nursing often face the problems of low student preference and lack of attractiveness. On the one hand, many nursing students have not fully recognized the importance of caring for the elderly. There is a widespread tendency to associate aging with disability, dependence, and functional decline, which in turn leads them to regard geriatric nursing as a monotonous and unfulfilling career direction. On the other hand, the difficulties in communicating with the elderly and unpleasant clinical internship experiences further intensify students’ aversion to this field [48, 49].

The integration of IoT with VR and wearable sensors enables the creation of intelligent and immersive virtual training environments. These environments allow students to simulate elderly care processes in a stress-free and repeatable manner, thereby deepening their understanding of the physiological and psychological states of older adults. Research [50] has shown that a four-week VR training program significantly enhanced nursing students’ knowledge, attitudes, self-efficacy, and willingness to serve in the area of geriatric oral care. Such IoT-based interactive learning experiences not only increase learning engagement and a sense of realism but also help reshape students’ perceptions of geriatric nursing. This, in turn, enhances the professional appeal and identity of specialized nursing fields.

Enhance clinical work readiness

The work readiness of nurses refers to the extent of the skills and attributes that nursing graduates have, which enable them to successfully adapt to and meet the demands of the workplace. The higher the work readiness of new nurses, the smoother their transition from school to the clinical setting, and the better they will be able to meet professional challenges [51]. The IoT-based mobile nursing informatics teaching and experimental system can help students adapt to and use the various advanced case management systems in hospitals, thereby effectively enhancing their clinical work readiness. Research has shown [52] that students who have undergone practical training with this system demonstrate superior practical operation skills in nursing system tools during subsequent hospital internships and employment, outperforming students trained through traditional teaching methods.

In addition, wearable devices can collect and transmit patients’ vital signs data to the cloud in real time, and at the same time, they can provide timely alerts when patients are at risk [53]. By processing and analyzing such real clinical data, nursing students can accurately assess patients’ conditions and develop a rapid response capability during their internships and professional practice, thereby effectively fulfilling their nursing responsibilities and accumulating valuable clinical experience. It is evident that IoT technology constructs an efficient career transition pathway for nursing students, effectively enhancing their clinical work readiness and facilitating their transition from the campus learning environment to the hospital workplace. This enables them to quickly adapt to and meet the professional requirements of nursing positions.

Exploration of the application of IoT in nursing education

Discussion on the application in theoretical and practical teaching

Theoretical teaching

In the theoretical teaching of nursing, IoT technology provides an innovative path for the integration of theory and practice. Researchers such as Sofia Amador Nelke [54], based on the Science, Technology, Engineering, and Medicine (STEM) education framework, have developed the IoT RemoteLab remote laboratory and applied it to teaching practice. The study points out that the remote laboratory can transform abstract theories into operable scenarios. Based on this, in nursing theory courses, biosensors can be deployed on high-fidelity simulators and integrated with environmental sensors to construct virtual nursing scenarios. Such technological applications can transform abstract nursing theories into interactive and operable contextualized learning experiences.

In addition, IoT technology can also empower personalized nursing education. Inspired by Aiyuan Zhen’s [55] research in the field of course recommendation, IoT devices can be used to collect multidimensional data of nursing students, such as their physiological states, social interactions, and academic performance, to gain insights into their learning patterns and schedules. Based on this analysis, the system can generate personalized theoretical course recommendations that match the students’ learning progress and time resources, thereby optimizing their learning paths. In addition, the cost-effective IoT smart classroom prototype system proposed by B. Nagajayanthi et al. [56] can also be referred to. When applied to nursing theory teaching, the system can automatically record the teaching content through integrated cameras and seamlessly upload it to the designated learning platform. Students can conveniently access the course resources through their exclusive accounts, which significantly improves the accessibility of theoretical knowledge. Meanwhile, the system integrates an automatic attendance function based on image recognition and uses IoT technology to monitor students’ online attendance status in real time, thereby achieving precise and automated management of nursing students’ attendance in theoretical courses.

Practical teaching

IoT technology provides an effective means of standardizing nursing practice operations through real-time monitoring and instant feedback. For example, based on the IoT-based telemedicine skills training system developed by Yeram Kwon et al. [57], such a system can accurately monitor key parameters during cardiopulmonary resuscitation performed by students, such as compression depth, rate, and recoil. When an operation deviates from the standard, the system immediately generates precise corrective feedback. The study found that students in the remote teaching group using the IoT system and those in the traditional face-to-face teaching group performed equally well in achieving the appropriate compression depth. This result strongly validates that the IoT-supported remote education model can serve as an alternative to face-to-face teaching.

In terms of clinical data acquisition and scenario simulation, IoT technology demonstrates significant advantages, particularly in overcoming geographical limitations. By using wearable devices to collect patients’ vital signs data in real time and transmit it to a teaching platform, students can perform remote analysis and diagnosis based on this data [58]. This approach effectively mitigates the severe disruption to traditional clinical learning and practice caused by sudden public health events, such as the COVID-19 pandemic [59]. By providing continuous, location-independent practice opportunities, IoT technology ensures the continuity and efficiency of medical education [46], becoming a key complementary solution to compensate for the shortage of clinical practice opportunities during special periods.

Medical IoT technology can enhance both the reliability of data foundations and the efficiency of clinical nursing education. As Boyuan Wang et al. [60] point out, through real-time monitoring and data collection via sensors and devices, the IoT enables continuous surveillance of patients’ physiological indicators, thereby improving diagnostic and treatment accuracy. Building on this, by leveraging key metrics—such as heart rate, blood pressure, oxygen saturation, and body temperature—continuously gathered by medical IoT systems, nursing students can engage in remote, reality-based learning. They can master how to monitor dynamic data changes and identify abnormal patterns, thus improving the accuracy and judgment involved in nursing diagnosis during clinical practice.

Application in pre-class, in-class, and post-class

Pre-class

In pre-class nursing teaching, a practice evaluation model based on multi-source mobile IoT information technology can be used to evaluate the quality of lesson preparation content and the suitability of resources. This model collects text materials such as lesson plans and practical training guidance plans through sensor devices in the perception layer, and uses an improved Transformer Encoder intelligent text analysis model to extract deep semantic features from the text. It then conducts intelligent assessments on the completeness of lesson preparation content, the accuracy of professional terminology, and the rationality of practical training processes. At the same time, the pre-class learning resources are integrated and managed, and multi-source information is interconnected and comprehensively analyzed through the application layer of the IoT platform. Studies have shown that this model can help teachers quickly identify the shortcomings in their lesson plans while ensuring that the quality of the lesson plans meets the actual teaching needs [61].

In addition, an efficient pre-class resource interaction channel for nursing courses can also be constructed based on the IoT teaching management system designed by Ping Tan et al. The core of this approach is the “scan-to-learn” mechanism, which relies on QR code technology: QR codes embedded in nursing textbooks or practical training manuals serve as key IoT nodes, storing precise links to specialized nursing teaching videos in the cloud. Before class, students can use their personal smart devices to scan the QR codes, thereby establishing real-time IoT connections with the cloud system via Wi-Fi or mobile networks and instantly accessing video resources tailored to the key points, difficulties, and common mistakes of the nursing course chapters. The cloud system, as the IoT hub, not only centrally manages these resources optimized for pre-class preparation in nursing courses but also tracks and analyzes students’ access behaviors in real time. These preview behavior data of nursing courses are synchronized to the teachers’ end in real time, enabling teachers to gain prospective insights into students’ preview of specific nursing knowledge/skill points and common points of confusion before classroom teaching. This provides precise data support for the focused explanation, in-depth analysis of difficult points, and interactive design in subsequent classroom nursing practice, significantly enhancing the relevance and efficiency of future nursing classroom teaching [34].

In-class

To address the need for monitoring students’ psychological stress in nursing classrooms, we can refer to the research approach of Daniele Bibb et al. on workplace stress monitoring. Using office chairs equipped with pressure sensors in combination with the Stroop test to induce different levels of stress, the Bibb team systematically explored the correlation between changes in sitting posture and stress states. Their research confirmed that increased stress leads to significant changes in participants’ sitting postures. Based on this, smart cushions or smart vests equipped with high-precision pressure sensors can be deployed in student seating areas, and combined with IoT wearable devices such as smartwatches, a multi-dimensional physiological and behavioral data collection system can be constructed. Such devices are capable of continuously collecting biomechanical signals from students in a non-invasive manner during class, including body pressure distribution, center-of-gravity movement trajectory, and micro-tremors, while simultaneously monitoring physiological indicators reflecting psychological stress levels, such as heart rate. By introducing machine learning models for real-time analysis and pattern recognition of the aforementioned multi-source signals, the system can dynamically assess students’ stress states and levels of concentration in the classroom, thereby providing teachers with an objective, data-driven basis for implementing timely targeted teaching interventions, or offering students a reference for self-regulation [62].

In the demonstration and practice of nursing skills, the traditional method that relies on teachers’ observation finds it difficult to accurately assess each student’s operational standardization and classroom concentration in real time and objectively. The classroom attention analysis method based on IoT and multimodal deep learning proposed by Xufeng Ling et al. provides a new way to address this challenge. This study innovatively integrates RetinaFace face detection, the Visual Transformer (ViT) head pose estimation model, and Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) technology, and collects classroom audio and video data through IoT devices such as fixed cameras and microphones. The core logic is as follows: First, the ASR model is used to divide the classroom dynamics into four states: “lecture,” “interaction,” “practice,” and “copying.” Second, based on the expected attention behavior patterns in different classroom states, the attention level of each student in a specific classroom segment is precisely calculated by combining the students’ head pose parameters estimated by the ViT model. Applying this technology to nursing skills classrooms can realize the automated and quantitative monitoring of students’ attention states in different teaching segments (teacher demonstration, self-practice, and interactive Q&A), providing objective data support for accurately assessing the degree of operational engagement [63].

Post-class

In post-class nursing course assessments, the “IoT + blockchain” integration architecture proposed by Rahmani et al. can be used as a reference. This architecture monitors the authenticity of the assessment process by using wearable sensors to collect students’ physiological and behavioral data in real time. During nursing operation exams, it dynamically analyzes data transmitted by sensors to identify behaviors that violate nursing operation standards. Upon receiving real-time warnings on the teacher’s terminal, instructors can correct errors or forcibly terminate the simulated operating environment [64]. In addition, with the assistance of IoT, the IoT module of the Learning Management System (LMS) continuously monitors students’ performance in after-class tasks. After a student completes an after-class exercise or simulation experiment, the IoT system analyzes the time taken to complete each task, the number of errors made, and the number of attempts. The system can automatically generate assessment reports or preliminary scores based on the data collected by IoT, providing an objective basis for formative evaluation of the course and planning of personalized learning paths [65]. Applications of IoT in nursing education: challenges and future prospects

Privacy and data security

While the application of IoT technology in nursing education brings numerous advantages, it also introduces challenges related to privacy and data security. Nursing education involves a large amount of highly sensitive information, including patients’ health records and students’ personal data and learning progress [11, 66]. Since IoT technology typically requires the collection, transmission, and processing of such data, safeguarding their privacy and security becomes crucial. IoT devices may face a variety of cybersecurity threats, such as the spread of malicious bots, identity theft, phishing, and keylogging, especially when handling sensitive medical information [67]. Second, there is the issue of legal compliance. With the increasing strictness of domestic and international privacy regulations such as the General Data Protection Regulation, ensuring that the collection, processing, and use of data fully comply with these high standards to guarantee the legality of health data sharing may also be a challenge [68]. Finally, ensuring the informed consent of patients and students throughout the entire process of data collection and use, fully informing the relevant parties and obtaining their explicit consent, is also a challenge [69]. These issues are directly related to the protection of individual privacy rights and the compliant operation of educational institutions. To effectively address the privacy breaches and data security risks caused by the application of IoT technology in nursing education, a deep integration of multi-layered protection mechanisms can be adopted in the future. By employing secure encrypted communication methods and closely monitoring data access patterns, sensitive teaching data transmitted between teachers, students, devices, and teaching platforms, as well as student privacy, can be effectively protected. Unauthorized access and data leakage can be precisely detected and prevented in a timely manner [70]. Blockchain technology, with its decentralized, tamper-proof, traceable, and privacy-enhancing features, can be utilized to enhance the security, transparency, and tamper-resistance of teaching data, ensuring its authenticity and reliability, and effectively meeting audit and compliance requirements [71]. In addition, an intrusion detection system based on AI can be relied upon to provide proactive defense capabilities for IoT applications in nursing education. By continuously analyzing network traffic and communication patterns connected to teaching devices and platforms, the system can accurately identify potential attacks targeting teaching data or systems and automatically trigger alarms or implement defensive measures [72]. The deep integration and collaborative application of the above-mentioned technologies will jointly build a comprehensive IoT security protection system for future nursing education scenarios, significantly enhancing its ability to resist privacy breaches and data security risks, and thus laying the foundation for creating a secure, reliable, and compliant educational environment.

Technology dependency and device failure

The application of IoT in nursing education relies on the stable operation of various key technologies, and ensuring the stability and long-term use of devices is an important issue [12, 73]. Sensors may malfunction or experience reduced sensitivity over long-term use, leading to inaccurate data collection. Measurement errors in sensors for vital signs such as heart rate and respiratory rate can directly affect the accuracy of clinical judgments. Moreover, in special environments with high humidity, for example, dedicated monitoring devices may also be affected by the environment and experience functional failures, resulting in false alarms or missed reports [11]. The normal operation of IoT systems also depends on continuous and stable network connections. Network unavailability or data transmission interruptions can easily cause data loss or transmission failures, disrupting the teaching process [74]. At the cloud service level, cloud servers responsible for storing and processing massive amounts of teaching and health data may experience software crashes or hardware failures, resulting in data not being stored and processed properly and bringing experiments or training that rely on cloud analysis to a standstill [60]. These vulnerabilities and dependencies at the technical level not only interfere with the normal teaching process but may also lead to erroneous clinical decisions, thereby having a substantial impact on the quality of nursing education and seriously threatening the reliability of IoT applications in nursing education as well as the continuity of teaching activities. To build a more reliable and less technology-dependent IoT environment for nursing education, cutting-edge technologies can be deeply integrated. 5G technology, with its high-speed and ultra-low latency data transmission capabilities, will significantly enhance the real-time and smooth data interaction between teaching devices [75]. In addition, predictive maintenance using AI can continuously analyze device operation data and learn its patterns to predict potential failure risks of smart teaching aids in advance, such as wearable monitoring devices and high-precision simulators, thereby optimizing maintenance plans [76] This can greatly reduce the interference of unexpected device downtime on the teaching process, improve system reliability and operation efficiency, and achieve proactive intelligent risk management. Finally, enhancing the fault tolerance of networks and systems is also crucial. By optimizing the network architecture design, the entire teaching system can still maintain its core functions even if some IoT devices or servers fail, ensuring the continuity and stability of teaching activities [77] These technologies can reduce dependence on single devices or links, build a more resilient IoT ecosystem for nursing education, and ensure the efficiency, reliability, and uninterrupted nature of nursing training.

Technology acceptance

The application of IoT technology in the field of nursing education faces significant challenges in terms of acceptance, which restricts the full realization of its potential. There is a certain degree of resistance among the student population, and their willingness to adopt it depends on perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use [13]. Although the majority of students have a positive attitude towards the ease of use of IoT devices, some students still face difficulties in actual use due to insufficient operational proficiency and lack of technical skills. In addition, high application costs and security issues are also important factors that hinder students’ adoption of IoT technology. There are differences among students in their perceptions of IoT security. While some students are concerned about security risks, others do not consider the issue to be serious [78]. Among teachers, although the application of IoT technology is highly relevant to the educational process, it also faces multiple challenges. Issues related to the reliability and security of the technology, such as unstable networks, frequent device malfunctions, risks of privacy breaches, and technical vulnerabilities, limit its use. Teachers’ lack of capability and preparedness, including insufficient training in IoT technology, the need to improve their technological literacy, and a lack of experience and confidence in using such advanced technologies, are also reasons for the low acceptance of the technology. In addition, teachers generally have heavy teaching workloads and administrative burdens, which leads them to believe that adopting IoT technology requires more time and effort than traditional methods. Despite the general recognition by teachers of the educational value of IoT, the above-mentioned technological barriers, capability limitations, and the resulting high perceived costs together lead to a generally low willingness on their part to actively explore and apply new technologies [9]. Overall, the acceptance challenges of IoT technology stem from multiple constraining factors: implementation costs in economic terms, concerns over privacy risks in terms of technological security, and limited individual understanding of IoT [79]. Some studies have pointed out that about 50% of nurses agree that IoT will have a significant impact on the fields of health and education, and support its inclusion in nursing education and the strengthening of integration research [80]. However, in the process of promoting IoT in the field of nursing education, the many specific obstacles and multifaceted influencing factors faced by students and teachers highlight the need to address the issue of acceptance. To enhance the acceptance and usability of IoT technology in nursing education, it is necessary to address the skill barriers, security concerns, and cost pressures faced by teachers and students. In response to the differences in digital skills among teachers and students, clear contextualized operation guides, intuitive user interface designs, and step-by-step interactive training should be provided to ensure that even those with weak technical foundations can easily master the operation of IoT devices, thereby improving overall usability and user confidence [81]. To alleviate security concerns regarding sensitive teaching data, transparent and enhanced privacy protection measures can be implemented, and communication can be used to clarify data security mechanisms, effectively easing concerns and establishing a foundation of trust in the technology. To relieve cost pressures, governments and educational institutions should provide special funding support to reduce the costs of purchasing and maintaining smart teaching aids. At the same time, schools should be encouraged to establish equipment-sharing platforms or regional cooperation centers to achieve intensive use and cost-sharing of high-value IoT resources, thereby increasing the feasibility of technology popularization [82]. Finally, IoT technology providers should promote new IoT technologies by publicizing successful cases and obtaining endorsements from celebrities to enhance public acceptance of IoT products [83]. By implementing comprehensive multi-dimensional strategies, it is hoped that a nursing education IoT ecosystem that is user-friendly, credible, accessible, and value-driven can be established, thereby achieving the integrated development of IoT technology and nursing education.

Applications of IoT in nursing education range from exploratory uses with initial practical experience to forward-looking concepts still at the experimental or prototype stage. Although this technology holds great promise, most current applications have yet to become routine teaching practice. Going forward, large-scale, multicenter empirical studies are needed to continuously assess teaching effectiveness and safety. Furthermore, mechanisms for ethical review and data privacy protection should be established, infrastructure in teaching institutions and digital literacy training for faculty and students strengthened, and application barriers lowered through interdisciplinary collaboration as well as policy and financial support. Only through such sustained efforts can IoT technology be truly integrated into the classroom and clinical practice of nursing education.

Conclusion

The deep integration of IoT technology into nursing education yields core values primarily in enhancing clinical practice experience, improving teaching efficiency, increasing the practical appeal of specialized nursing fields, and strengthening students’ clinical readiness. This technology can be applied across nursing education content (theoretical and practical) and all phases of instruction (pre-class, in-class, and post-class), offering broad prospects for the future of smart nursing education. Importantly, although various IoT-driven strategies hold significant potential for transforming and enhancing nursing education, most remain in early development, pilot testing, or proof-of-concept, and have yet to achieve routine application in standard nursing education. Therefore, these models should be viewed as future-oriented directions rather than widespread standardized teaching practices. Meanwhile, the application of IoT in nursing education faces multiple challenges, including privacy and data security, technological dependence, device reliability, and user acceptance. Going forward, efforts should focus on ensuring data privacy and security, reducing technological dependence, improving the operational stability of devices, and enhancing the usability and acceptance of the technology, thereby offering a practical reference for nursing educators to adopt smart teaching approaches.

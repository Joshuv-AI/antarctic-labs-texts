Approximate marked length spectrum rigidity in coarse geometry
Stephen Cantrell and Eduardo Reyes

Abstract
. We compare the marked length spectra of isometric actions of groups with non-positively curved features. Inspired by the recent works of Butt we study approximate versions of marked length spectrum rigidity. We show that for pairs of metrics, the supremum of the quotient of their marked length spectra is approximately determined by their marked length spectra restricted to an appropriate finite set of conjugacy classes. Applying this to fundamental groups of closed negatively curved Riemannian manifolds allows us to refine Butt’s result. Our results however apply in greater generality and do not require the acting group to be hyperbolic. For example we are able to compare the marked length spectra associated to mapping class groups acting on their Cayley graphs or on the curve graph.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Riemannian manifolds

Suppose that
(M,\mathfrak{g})
is a closed, negatively curved Riemannian manifold. There is a bijection between the (non-trivial) conjugacy classes
{\bf conj}={\bf conj}(\Gamma)
in the fundamental group
\Gamma:=\pi_{1}(M)
and the oriented closed geodesics on
(M,\mathfrak{g})
. Through this correspondence we obtain a map

\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}:{\bf conj}(\Gamma)\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}

that sends each conjugacy class to the length of the corresponding closed geodesic. This map, which is referred to as the
marked length spectrum
of
(M,\mathfrak{g})
, records the lengths of the closed geodesics on
(M,\mathfrak{g})
and orders them according to the conjugacy classes in
\Gamma
. The marked length spectrum rigidity conjecture (attributed to Burns and Katok
[
10
]
) predicts that
\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}
determines the isometry class of
\mathfrak{g}
amongst all negatively curved metrics on
M
, i.e. if
\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}
is another negatively curved Riemannian metric on
M
with the same marked length spectrum as
\mathfrak{g}
then
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}
must be isometric. The conjecture has been confirmed independently by Croke
[
15
]
and Otal
[
26
]
for surfaces. In higher dimensions, the conjecture is known to hold if one of the metrics is locally symmetric
[
1
,
21
]
. A recent breakthrough of Guillarmou and Lefeuvre
[
18
]
verifies a local version of the conjecture for Riemannian manifolds with Anosov geodesic flow and non-positive curvature. In general the conjecture remains open.

When
S
is a closed, orientable surface of genus
g\geq 2
, the marked hyperbolic (i.e. constant curvature
-1
) metrics on
S
form a moduli space called Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}(S)
. It is well-known, thanks to Frenchel-Nielsen coordinates, that the length spectra of points in
\mathcal{T}(S)
are determined by their values taken on finitely many conjugacy classes. In fact there are
6g-6
conjugacy classes that determine the full marked length spectrum (and hence isometry class) of hyperbolic metrics on
S

[
32
]
. This property does not hold for arbitrary metrics of (variable) negative curvature. Indeed, if
\mathfrak{g}
is a negatively curved metric on
S
and
F
is any finite collection of closed geodesics on
(S,\mathfrak{g})
, then we can perturb
\mathfrak{g}
on the complement of
F
to obtain a new metric
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
. We can do this so that
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
non-isometric to
\mathfrak{g}
, is still negatively curved and so that
\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\ell_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}
coincide on
F
.

It is then natural to ask to what extent the marked length spectrum of a negatively curved Riemannian metric is determined by its marked length spectrum restricted to a finite collection of closed geodesics. This question was considered by Butt in
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
in which she proves an
approximate length spectrum rigidity
result. Suppose
\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}
are negatively curved Riemannian metrics on a closed manifold
M
. Roughly speaking Butt’s result
[
11
, Thm. 1.2]
states that there exist constants
C>0,\alpha\in(0,1)
(depending on geometric data) such if
|\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[g]/\ell_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}[g]-1|<\epsilon
for all
[g]\in{\bf conj}(\Gamma)
with
\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[g]<L
then
|\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[g]/\ell_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}[g]-1|<\epsilon+CL^{-\alpha}
for
all
conjugacy classes
[g]\in{\bf conj}(\Gamma)
. That is, for a pair of negatively curved metrics
\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}
, precise global bounds comparing their marked length spectra can be deduced from bounds taken over
finitely many
conjugacy classes. The first aim of this paper is to prove a refinement of Butt’s results through a concise, coarse geometric argument. We show the following in which we write
{\bf conj}^{\prime}={\bf conj}^{\prime}(\Gamma)
for the non-torsion conjugacy classes in
\Gamma
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,\mathfrak{g})
and
(M_{\ast},\mathfrak{g}_{\ast})
be closed Riemannian manifolds with sectional curvatures lying in the interval
[-\Lambda^{2},-\lambda^{2}]\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{<0}
, and let
i_{\mathfrak{g}}
be the injectivity radius of
(M,\mathfrak{g})
. Let
\Gamma
be the fundamental group of
M
and suppose there exists an isomorphism
f_{\ast}:\Gamma\rightarrow\pi_{1}(M_{\ast})
. Then for any
0<\epsilon_{0}<1
there exists some
L_{0}=L_{0}(\Gamma,\lambda,\Lambda,i_{\mathfrak{g}},\epsilon_{0})
such that the following holds. If
L\geq L_{0}
,
0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
and

(1.1)

1-\epsilon\leq\frac{\ell_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}[f_{\ast}g]}{\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[g]}\leq 1+\epsilon\ \ \text{ for all }[g]\in{\bf conj}^{\prime}\text{ with }\ell_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}[f_{\ast}g]\leq L,

then

(1.2)

1-\left(\epsilon+\frac{C}{L}\right)\leq\frac{\ell_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}[f_{\ast}g]}{\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[g]}\leq 1+\left(\epsilon+\frac{C}{L}\right)

for all
[g]\in{\bf conj}^{\prime}
,
where
C
depends only on
\Gamma,\lambda,\Lambda,i_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\epsilon_{0}
.

This theorem provides a stronger global bound to Butt’s result as the error in the comparison inequality (
1.2
) decays like
L^{-1}
opposed to
L^{-\alpha}
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

1.2.
General actions on hyperbolic spaces

To prove Theorem
1.1
we use ideas and techniques from geometric group theory. This perspective allows us to prove much more general results. Suppose that a group
\Gamma
admits an isometric action on a possibly asymmetric metric space
X=(X,d_{X})
(so
d_{X}
satisfies all the actions of a metric, except for possibly symmetry). Then the
(stable) translation length function
associated to this action is the map

\ell_{d}:{\bf conj}(\Gamma)\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\ \text{ given by }\ \ell_{X}[g]=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{d_{X}(x,g^{k}\cdot x)}{k}

where
x\in X
is a fixed base point. When
(M,\mathfrak{g})
is a closed, negatively curved Riemannian manifold we can recover the marked length spectrum of
\mathfrak{g}
as the stable translation length function associated to the action of
\pi_{1}(M)
on the universal cover
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{g})
of
(M,\mathfrak{g})
.

Therefore Theorem
1.1
above can be seen as a result comparing the translation length functions associated to two cocompact, isometric actions of (the hyperbolic group)
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)
on the universal covers
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\widetilde{M}_{\ast},\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{\ast})
.

More generally, given two isometric actions of
\Gamma
on
(X,d_{X})
and
(X_{\ast},d_{\ast})
, their
dilation
is given by

{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},X):=\inf\{\eta>0\hskip 2.84526pt\colon\hskip 2.84526pt\ell_{X_{\ast}}[g]\leq\eta\cdot\ell_{X}[g]\text{ for all }[g]\in{\bf conj}\}\in[0,+\infty].

When
\Gamma
is hyperbolic,
X
and
X_{\ast}
are symmetric and geodesic and the actions of
\Gamma
on these spaces are proper and cobounded, the dilations
{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},X),{\rm Dil}(X,X_{\ast})
are also the optimal multiplicative constants for a
\Gamma
-equivariant quasi-isometry between
X
and
X_{\ast}
, see
[
13
, Thm. 1.1]
.

Our next theorem is then a natural generalisation of Theorem
1.1
. Recall that the action of a group
\Gamma
on a possibly asymmetric metric space
(X,d_{X})
is called
D
-
cobounded
(
D\geq 0
) if for any
x,x^{\prime}\in X
we can find
g\in\Gamma
such that
\max\{d_{X}(gx,x^{\prime}),d_{X}(x^{\prime},gx)\}\leq D
.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists an absolute constant
K>0
such that the following holds. Suppose
\Gamma
is a group acting isometrically on the spaces
X,X_{\ast}
such that:

•

X_{\ast}
is a
\delta
-hyperbolic metric space and the action of
\Gamma
on this space is non-elementary; and,

•

X
is a (possibly asymmetric) geodesic metric space and the action of
\Gamma
on
X
is
D
-cobounded.

If
L>6D
then

{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},X)\leq\left(\sup_{0<\ell_{X}[g]\leq L}\frac{\ell_{X_{\ast}[g]}}{\ell_{X}[g]}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{L-2D}{L-6D}\right)+\frac{2K\delta}{L-6D}.

Remark 1.3
.

In the theorem above, we do not require the action on
X_{\ast}
to be proper, cobounded, or even acylindrical. However,
X_{\ast}
is an honest metric space (i.e.
d_{X_{\ast}}
is symmetric).

In the case both
X,X_{\ast}
are hyperbolic and geodesic metric spaces and the actions of
\Gamma
on these spaces are proper and cobounded (so that
\Gamma
is hyperbolic), Theorem
1.2
implies the following, which is the coarse analog of Butt’s theorem.

Corollary 1.4
.

For any
\delta,D\geq 0
there exist positive constants
L_{0},C_{0}
depending only on
\delta
and
D
satisfying the following. Let
\Gamma
a non-elementary hyperbolic group acting properly and
D
-coboundedly on the
\delta
-hyperbolic and geodesic metric spaces
X,X_{\ast}
. If
L>L_{0}
and

\alpha\leq\frac{\ell_{X_{\ast}}[g]}{\ell_{X}[g]}\leq\beta\ \ \text{ for all }[g]\in{\bf conj}^{\prime}\text{ with }\ell_{X}[g]\leq L,

then

\alpha\cdot\left(1-\frac{C_{0}}{L}\right)-\frac{C_{0}}{L}\leq\frac{\ell_{X_{\ast}}[g]}{\ell_{X}[g]}\leq\beta\cdot\left(1+\frac{C_{0}}{L}\right)+\frac{C_{0}}{L}

for all
[g]\in{\bf conj}^{\prime}
.

As an illustrative example, when
\Gamma
is the fundamental group of a closed hyperbolic surface
S
and the actions on
X_{\ast}=X=\mathbb{H}^{2}
correspond to points in
\mathcal{T}(S)
, then
\frac{1}{2}\log{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},X)
is precisely the asymmetric Lipschitz distance
d_{L}(X_{\ast},X)
introduced by Thurston
[
34
]
. The
D
-cobounded assumption translates to
X
being in the
\epsilon
-thick part of
\mathcal{T}(S)
for some
\epsilon
depending only on
D
(cf. Remark
4.1
). Theorem
1.2
then states that
d_{L}(X_{\ast},X)
can be approximated by looking at quotients
\ell_{X_{\ast}}[g]/\ell_{X}[g]
for
[g]
representing a closed geodesic with
X
-length at most
L
and with error term of form
o(L)
. This is reminiscent of
[
23
, Thm. E]
, in which
d_{L}(X_{\ast},X)
is approximated by
\ell_{X_{\ast}}[g]/\ell_{X}[g]
for
[g]
a conjugacy class in a
short marking
of
X
and with additive error depending only on the topology of
S
(note that here there are no assumption of
X
lying on the thick part of
\mathcal{T}(S)
).

For
S
and
\Gamma
as above, a natural extension of Teichmüller space is the
quasi-Fuchsian space

\mathcal{QF}(S)
, which is the space of convex-cocompact representations
\rho:\Gamma\rightarrow\operatorname{PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
up to conjugacy. The convex-cocompact representation
\rho
induces a natural action of
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{H}^{3}
from which we obtain a length function
\ell_{\rho}:{\bf conj}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
. As an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.2
we deduce the following for pairs of convex-cocompact representations.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
K
be the universal constant from Theorem
1.2
. Let
\Gamma
be a hyperbolic surface group and
\rho,\rho_{\ast}:\Gamma\rightarrow\operatorname{PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
convex-cocompact representations inducing length functions.
\ell_{\rho},\ell_{\rho_{\ast}}:{\bf conj}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
.
If the
\rho
-action of
\Gamma
on the convex hull of the limit set
\Lambda_{\rho(\Gamma)}
is
D
-cobounded and
L>6D
, then

{\rm Dil}(\rho_{\ast},\rho):=\sup_{[g]\in{\bf conj}^{\prime}}{\frac{\ell_{\rho_{\ast}}[g]}{\ell_{\rho}[g]}}\leq\frac{L}{L-6D}\cdot\left(\max_{0<\ell_{\rho}[g]\leq L}\frac{\ell_{\rho_{\ast}}[g]}{\ell_{\rho}[g]}\right)+\frac{2K\log{4}}{L-6D}.

Back to the coarse geometric setting, a very natural and rich source of isometric actions is given by groups acting on their Cayley graphs. When
(X,d_{X})=({\rm Cay}(\Gamma,S),d_{S})
is a Cayley graph of
\Gamma
associated to the (non-necessarily symmetric) generating set
S
, we denote
\ell_{d_{S}}
by
\ell_{S}
and we prove the following.

Theorem 1.6
.

There exists an absolute constant
K>0
such that the following holds. Let
\Gamma
be a group with a non-elementary action by isometries on the
\delta
-hyperbolic space
X_{\ast}
. Let
S\subset\Gamma
be a set that generates
\Gamma
as a semigroup, so that
\Gamma
acts isometrically on the Cayley graph
({\rm Cay}(\Gamma,S),d_{S})
.
Then for any integer
L\geq 1
we have

{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},{\rm Cay}(\Gamma,S))\leq\frac{K\delta}{L}+\sup_{0<\ell_{S}[g]\leq 2L}{\frac{\ell_{X_{\ast}}[g]}{\ell_{S}[g]}}.

Remark 1.7
.

In the theorem above the set
S
is not required to be finite or symmetric.

Many groups admit non-elementary actions on hyperbolic spaces. Examples include:

(1)

Non-elementary hyperbolic groups and relatively hyperbolic groups.

(2)

Mappings class groups of most oriented finite type surfaces acting on the curve graph
[
24
]
.

(3)

Outer automorphism groups of free groups acting on the free factor complex
[
4
]
.

(4)

Non-elementary
{\rm CAT}(0)
groups with rank-1 elements
[
33
]
(see also
[
28
, Thm. K]
).

Moreover, many of these groups admit cobounded isometric actions on geodesic metric spaces besides Cayley graphs. For example, mapping class groups act coboundedly on injective metric spaces coming from the hierarchical structure
[
20
,
29
]
. In addition, if
\Gamma
is a group acting geometrically on a
{\rm CAT}(0)
cube complex
X
, then
\Gamma
acts coboundedly on
X
equipped with any
\ell^{p}
metric for
1\leq p\leq\infty
(see e.g.
[
19
]
).

1.3.
Anosov represenations

Our methods are sufficiently flexible that we can apply them to deduce approximate rigidity results for Anosov representations. A representation
\rho:\Gamma\rightarrow\operatorname{PSL}_{m}({\mathbb{R}})
is
projective Anosov
(or equivalently,
1
-dominated

[
6
]
) if given a finite generating set
S
for
\Gamma
there exist
C,\mu>0
such that

(1.3)

\frac{\sigma_{1}(\rho(g))}{\sigma_{2}(\rho(g))}\geq Ce^{\mu|g|_{S}}\ \text{ for all $g\in\Gamma$,}

where
\sigma_{i}(A)
represents the
i
th largest singular value of the matrix
A
. See
[
6
]
for a more detailed introduction to projective Anosov representations.

If
\rho:\Gamma\rightarrow\operatorname{PSL}_{m}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\tau:\Gamma\rightarrow\operatorname{PSL}_{k}({\mathbb{R}})
are projective Anosov representations, then their dilation is given by

{\rm Dil}(\rho,\tau):=\sup_{[g]\in{\bf conj}^{\prime}}{\frac{\log\lambda_{1}(\rho(g))}{\log\lambda_{1}(\tau(g))}},

where
\lambda_{1}(A)
denotes the spectral radius of the matrix
A
. We note that the dilation is well-defined. Indeed, we have that
{\rm Dil}(\rho,\tau)={\rm Dil}(\Gamma_{\rho},\Gamma_{\tau})
where
\Gamma_{\rho}=(\Gamma,d_{\rho})
and
d_{\rho}(g,h)=\log\sigma_{1}(g^{-1}h)
, and similarly for
\Gamma_{\tau}
(note that
d_{\rho}
is possibly asymmetric). For two projective Anosov representations of arbitrary dimension we prove the following, extending Corollary
1.5
.

Theorem 1.8
.

For any
m\geq 0
there exist positive constants
c_{m}\leq 8\log 2+5\log m
and
d_{m}\leq 2m^{3}
with
d_{m}
an integer such that the following holds. Suppose
\rho:\Gamma\rightarrow\operatorname{PSL}_{m}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\tau:\Gamma\rightarrow\operatorname{PSL}_{k}({\mathbb{R}})
are projective Anosov representations (with
m
not necessarily equal to
k
). Then there exists
\beta>1
depending only on
\tau
such that if
L>d_{m}\beta
then

{\rm Dil}(\rho,\tau)\leq\frac{c_{m}d_{m}}{(L-d_{m}\beta)}+\left(\sup_{0<\log\lambda_{1}(\tau(g))\leq L}{\frac{\log\lambda_{1}(\rho(g))}{\log\lambda_{1}(\tau(g))}}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{L}{L-d_{m}\beta}\right).

1.4.
Ideas behind the proofs

The key ideas behind our proofs are as follows. To obtain bounds on the dilation
{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},X)
as in Theorem
1.2
we first obtain bounds on dilations of the form
{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},{\rm Cay}(\Gamma,S))
(as in Theorem
1.6
) where
S\subset\Gamma
generates
\Gamma
as a semigroup. To do this we identify
{\rm Dil}(X,{\rm Cay}(\Gamma,S))
with the
joint stable length

\mathfrak{D}_{X}(S):=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\frac{\sup_{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\in S}d_{X_{\ast}}(x,s_{1}\cdots s_{n}x)}{n}}.

The quantity
\mathfrak{D}_{X}(S)
is a geometric version of the well-known joint spectral radius associated to products of matrices. This identification is useful as it allows us to apply an inequality of Breuillard and Fujiwara (see Theorem
3.3
) that gives us uniform control over
\mathfrak{D}_{X_{\ast}}(S)
(and hence
{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},{\rm Cay}(\Gamma,S))
) in terms of
\sup_{s\in S^{2}}\ell_{X_{\ast}}[s]
. We then use the fact that the displacement function
d_{X}
associated to the action of
\Gamma
on
X
can be approximated by word metrics (see Lemma
2.5
). This allows us to obtain bounds on
{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},X)
using the inequality

{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},X)\leq{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},{\rm Cay}(\Gamma,S))\,{\rm Dil}({\rm Cay}(\Gamma,S),X)

along with an appropriate choice of
S
and the arguments mentioned above. We follow this line of reasoning to prove both Theorem
1.2
and
1.6
. To deduce Theorem
1.8
we follow a similar argument but replace the use of the Breuillard-Fujiwara inequality with an inequality due to Bochi (Theorem
3.6
) for matrix products. In the last section we deduce Theorem
1.1
from Theorem
1.2
. This amounts to realising the coarse geometric constants that appear in Theorem
1.2
in terms of the curvature and injectivity radius constants comings from the Riemannian manifold.

Organisation

The article is organised as follows. In Section
2
we introduce preliminary material concerning metric spaces and groups acting on them. We then in Section
3
prove the theorems presented in sections
1.2
and
1.3
. In Section
4
we deduce Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Pseudo metric spaces

Recall that a
metric

d
on the space
X
is a function
d:X\times X\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying:

(1)

point separation:
d(x,y)=0
implies
x=y
;

(2)

positivity:
d(x,y)\geq 0
for all
x,y\in X
, and
d(x,x)=0
for all
x\in X
;

(3)

the triangle inequality:
d(x,z)\leq d(x,y)+d(y,z)
for all
x,y,z\in X
; and,

(4)

symmetry:
d(x,y)=d(y,x)
for all
x,y\in X
.

If
d
only satisfies (2),(3), and (4) above then it is called a
pseudo metric
, and
d
is a
possibly non-symmetric
metric (resp. pseudo metric) if it satisfies (1), (2) and (3) (resp. (2) and (3)), but not necessarily (4).

We say that two functions
\phi,\psi:X\times\Gamma\to{\mathbb{R}}
are
roughly similar
if there exist constants
\tau,C>0
such that

|\phi(x,y)-\tau\psi(x,y)|\leq C\ \text{ for all $x,y\in\Gamma$}.

If
\psi,\phi
are roughly similar with
\tau=1
we say that they are
roughly isometric
. In a similar way, we say that
\phi
and
\psi
are
quasi-isometric
if there exists
\lambda>1
such that

\lambda^{-1}\psi(x,y)-\lambda\leq\phi(x,y)\leq\lambda\psi(x,y)+\lambda\text{ for all}x,y\in X.

Note that roughly similar functions are necessarily quasi-isometric.

The
Gromov product
of a function
\psi:X\times X\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is the assignment

(x|y)^{\psi}_{z}:=\frac{1}{2}(\psi(x,z)+\psi(z,y)-\psi(x,y))\text{ for $x,y,z\in X$}.

A possibly asymmetric pseudo metric
d
on
X
is called
\delta
-
hyperbolic
(
\delta\geq 0
) if

(x|y)^{d}_{z}\geq\min\{(x|w)^{d}_{z},(y|w)^{d}_{z}\}-\delta\text{ for all}x,y,z,w\in X,

and
d
is
hyperbolic
if it is
\delta
-hyperbolic for some
\delta
.

2.2.
Group actions and hyperbolicity

If
X=(X,d_{X})
is a possibly asymmetric pseudo metric space and
\Gamma
is a group with identity element
o
, an action of
\Gamma
on
X
is
isometric
if
d_{X}(gx,gy)=d_{X}(x,y)
for all
x,y\in X
and
g\in\Gamma
. In this case, the
stable translation length
function of this action is given by

\ell_{X}[g]=\ell_{d_{X}}[g]:=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\frac{1}{k}d_{X}(x,g^{n}x)}\hskip 5.69054pt\text{for }[g]\in{\bf conj},

which is well-defined by subadditivity and independent of the base point
x\in X
.

As in the introduction, the
dilation
of two isometric actions of
\Gamma
on the possibly asymmetric pseudo metric spaces
X,X_{\ast}
is then the infimum

{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},X):=\inf\{\eta>0\hskip 2.84526pt\colon\hskip 2.84526pt\ell_{X_{\ast}}[g]\leq\eta\cdot\ell_{X}[g]\text{ for all }[g]\in{\bf conj}\}\in[0,+\infty]

and declare
{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},X)
to be
+\infty
if no such
\eta
exists. When
X=(X,d),X_{\ast}=(X,d_{\ast})
and
X
is understood, we often write
{\rm Dil}(d_{\ast},d)={\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},X)
.

Note that
{\rm Dil}(d_{\ast},d)
is always finite when
d
and
d_{\ast}
are quasi-isometric, possibly asymmetric pseudo metrics on a given space
X
. In addition, we have the submultiplicative property

{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast\ast},X)\leq{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast\ast},X_{\ast}){\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},X)

valid for all isometric actions of
\Gamma
on the possibly asymmetric pseudo metric spaces
X,X_{\ast},X_{\ast\ast}
.

Natural pseudo metrics on groups are word metrics. If
S
is a (non-necessarily symmetric) set of the group
\Gamma
that generates it as a semigroup, then the right word metric

d_{S}(g,h):=|g^{-1}h|_{S}
is a possibly asymmetric metric on
\Gamma
. We abbreviate the stable translation length function
\ell_{d_{S}}
by
\ell_{S}
and if
\Gamma
also acts isometrically on
X
, we use the notation
{\rm Dil}(X,S)={\rm Dil}(X,d_{S})
and
{\rm Dil}(S,X)={\rm Dil}(d_{S},X)
. A similar notation is used for the dilation comparing
d_{S}
and left-invariant, possibly asymmetric pseudo distances on
\Gamma
.

A group
\Gamma
is
non-elementary hyperbolic
if it is non-virtually cyclic, finitely generated, and any word metric
d_{S}
on
\Gamma
associated to a finite, symmetric generating set
S
is hyperbolic. Under this assumption we write
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
for the collection of pseudo metrics on
\Gamma
that are hyperbolic, quasi-isometric to a word metric (for a finite generating set), and
\Gamma
-invariant. The
space of metric structures

\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}
is the quotient of
\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
under rough similarity. There is a natural metric
\Delta
on
\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}
given by

(2.1)

\Delta([d],[d_{*}]):=\log\left({\rm Dil}(d,d_{*}){\rm Dil}(d_{*},d)\right)

where
[d]
is the rough similarity equivalence class of
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
, see
[
31
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

If
\Gamma
is non-elementary hyperbolic, a
hyperbolic distance-like function
on
\Gamma
is a possibly asymmetric pseudo metric
\psi:\Gamma\times\Gamma\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
that is
\Gamma
-invariant and satisfies the following. For any
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
and
C\geq 0
there exists
D\geq 0
such that
(x|y)_{w}^{d}\leq C
implies
(x|y)_{w}^{\psi}\leq D
for all
x,y,w\in\Gamma
.

A related notion to hyperbolic-like functions is the family of
\Gamma
-invariant
tempered potentials
that was considered by Cantrell-Tanaka
[
14
, Def. 2.5]
. These are
\Gamma
-invariant functions satisfying property (4) above and such that their Gromov products are ‘stable at infinity’.

It is immediate that pseudo metrics belonging to
\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
are hyperbolic distance-like functions. Also, if
S
is a non-necessarily symmetric finite generating set of
\Gamma
, then the word metric
d_{S}
is a hyperbolic distance-like function.
Similarly, if
\rho:\Gamma\rightarrow\operatorname{PSL}_{m}({\mathbb{R}})
is a projective Anosov representation (recall (
1.3
)), then

\psi^{\rho}(g,h):=\log\sigma_{1}(g^{-1}h)\ \text{ for $g,h\in\Gamma$}

is a hyperbolic distance-like function
[
13
, Lem. 3.10]
. These examples are all quasi-isometric to pseudo metrics in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
. However, hyperbolic distance-like functions need not be proper.

Definition 2.2
.

For
\Gamma
non-elementary hyperbolic, let
\overline{{\mathcal{D}}}_{\Gamma}
be the set of all left-invariant pseudo metrics on
\Gamma
that have non-constant translation length function and such that there exist
\lambda>0
and
d_{\ast}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
satisfying
(g|h)_{o}^{d}\leq\lambda(g|h)_{o}^{d_{\ast}}+\lambda
for all
g,h\in\Gamma
. We write
\overline{\mathscr{D}}_{\Gamma}
for the quotient of
\overline{{\mathcal{D}}}_{\Gamma}
under the equivalence of rough similarity. Points in
\overline{\mathscr{D}}_{\Gamma}
are called
metric structures
.

It follows immediately from the definition that elements in
\overline{{\mathcal{D}}}_{\Gamma}
are hyperbolic distance-like functions and that there is a natural inclusion
\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}\subset\overline{\mathscr{D}}_{\Gamma}
.

If
\Gamma
acts isometrically on the metric space
X
and
x\in X
then
d_{X}^{x}(g,h):=d_{X}(gx,hx)
defines a left-invariant pseudo metric on
\Gamma
. If this pseudo metric belongs to
\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
for some (equivalently, for any)
x\in X
, we abuse notation and let
[X]
denote the metric structure in
\overline{\mathscr{D}}_{\Gamma}
induced by
d_{X}^{x}
. This metric structure is independent of the point
x
.

It was shown in
[
13
, Thm. 1.6]
that the following actions induce points in
\overline{\mathscr{D}}_{\Gamma}
.

(1)

Actions on coned-off Cayley graphs for finite, symmetric generating sets, where we cone-off a finite number of quasi-convex subgroups of infinite index.

(2)

Non-trivial Bass-Serre tree actions with quasi-convex vertex stabilizers of infinite index.

(3)

Non-trivial small actions on
{\mathbb{R}}
-trees, when
\Gamma
is a surface group or a free group.

Pseudo metrics corresponding to these actions need not be proper, i.e. given
d\in\overline{{\mathcal{D}}}_{\Gamma}
is possible that there is
T>0
such that the set
\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)\leq T\}
is infinite.

We will need two properties about hyperbolic distance-like functions. The first one is that these functions are always roughly geodesic.

Definition 2.3
.

If
X
is a non-empty set, a non-negative function
\psi:X\times X\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is
\alpha
-roughly geodesic
(
\alpha\geq 0
) if for all
x,y\in X
there exists a sequence
x=x_{0},\dots,x_{n}=y
in
X
such that

j-i-\alpha\leq\psi(x_{i},x_{j})\leq j-i+\alpha\text{ for all }0\leq i\leq j\leq n.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
13
, Lem. 3.2]
)
.

If
\Gamma
is non-elementary hyperbolic and
\psi:\Gamma\times\Gamma\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is a hyperbolic distance-like function, then there exists some
\alpha\geq 0
such that
\psi
is
\alpha
-roughly geodesic.

The second property is that hyperbolic distance-like functions can be approximated by word metrics. This assertion also holds for arbitrary cobounded isometric actions on roughly geodesic spaces. Part (1) of the next result follows exactly as in the proof of
[
31
, Lem. 5.1]
which shows that pseudo metrics in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
can be approximated by word metrics (the assumption of hyperbolicity is not needed), whereas part (2) is exactly
[
2
, Lem. 4.6]
after noting that the assumptions of properness and symmetry are unnecessary. We leave the details to the reader.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\Gamma
act isometrically on the possibly asymmetric metric space
X
and fix
x\in X
.

(1)

Suppose
X
is geodesic and the action of
\Gamma
is
D
-cobounded. For an integer
n\geq 1
, let
S_{n}=\{g\in\Gamma\mid d_{X}(x,gx)\leq(n+2)D\}
. Then
S_{n}
generates
\Gamma
as a semigroup and for all
g\in\Gamma
we have

nD|g|_{S_{n}}-nD\leq d_{X}(x,gx)\leq(n+2)D|g|_{S_{n}}.

(2)

Suppose
X=(\Gamma,\psi)
where
\Gamma
acts by the left on
X
and
\psi
is
\alpha
-roughly geodesic. For
n>\alpha+1
, let
S_{n}:=\{g\in\Gamma\mid\psi(x,gx)\leq n\}
. Then
S_{n}
generates
\Gamma
as a semigroup and for all
g\in\Gamma
we have

(n-\alpha-1)|g|_{S_{n}}-(n-1)\leq\psi(x,gx)\leq n|g|_{S_{n}}.

Remark 2.6
.

(1) In both cases above, we do not require the action to induce a quasi-isometric embedding of
\Gamma
into
X
, so the sets
S_{L}
can be infinite.

(2) From the second part of the lemma above we deduce that
\psi
is quasi-isometric to
d_{S_{n}}
for all
n
large enough. When
\Gamma
is hyperbolic and
\psi
is a hyperbolic distance-like function, this implies that if
\psi
is not quasi-isometric to pseudo metrics in
\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
, then the sets
S_{n}
must be infinite. Therefore, a pseudo metric in
\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
belongs to
\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
if and only if it is proper.

3.
Approximate quantitative rigidity

In this section we prove our approximate length spectrum rigidity theorems in Sections
1.2
and
1.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a (non-necessarily hyperbolic) group acting isometrically on the possibly asymmetric metric space
X=(X,d_{X})
. For a set
S\subset\Gamma
we let
S^{n}
be the set of all products of
n
elements in
S
. If
x\in X
we set
d_{X}(x,S)=\sup_{s\in S}{d_{X}(x,sx)}
. We say that
S
is
bounded
for the action of
\Gamma
on
X
if
d_{X}(x,S)
is finite for some (and hence any)
x\in X
.

If
S\subset\Gamma
is bounded then its
joint stable length
is given by the limit

\mathfrak{D}_{X}(S):=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\frac{d_{X}(x,S^{n})}{n}},

which is finite for every finite set
S\subset\Gamma
and independent of
x\in X
. The joint stable length is an analog of the joint spectral radius for sets of matrices and first appeared in
[
30
]
(see also
[
9
]
).

When
X
is a hyperbolic metric space and the action of
\Gamma
is non-elementary, the relation between the joint stable length and the dilation is given by the following result, for which we recall the notation
{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},S)={\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},{\rm Cay}(\Gamma,S))={\rm Dil}(X,d_{S})
when
S
generates
\Gamma
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\Gamma
act isometrically on the possibly asymmetric metric space
X
and let
S\subset\Gamma
be a subset that generates
\Gamma
as a semigroup. Then
{\rm Dil}(X,S)\leq\mathfrak{D}_{X}(S)
, so that
S
being bounded implies that
{\rm Dil}(X,S)
is finite.

Conversely suppose that either:

(1)

X
is a hyperbolic metric space and the action of
\Gamma
is non-elementary; or,

(2)

X=(\Gamma,\psi)
where
\Gamma
is hyperbolic and acts by the left on
X
and
\psi
is a hyperbolic distance-like function.

Then
{\rm Dil}(X,S)
being finite implies that
S
is bounded and moreover we have
{\rm Dil}(X,S)=\mathfrak{D}_{X}(S)
.

We will need the following lemma, which follows from
[
13
, Cor. 3.12]
when
X=(\Gamma,\psi)
and
\psi
is a hyperbolic distance-like function on the hyperbolic group
\Gamma
. When
X
is hyperbolic and the action is non-elementary, it follows, for instance, from
[
35
, Lem. 4.7]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Gamma
and
X
be as in the assumptions (1) or (2) of Proposition
3.1
. Then for any
x\in X
there exist
C>0
depending on
x
and a finite set
F\subset\Gamma
such that for any
g\in\Gamma
we have

d_{X}(x,gx)\leq\max_{f\in F}{\ell_{X}[gf]}+C.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

We fix
x\in X
and let
|\cdot|_{S}
denote the word length on
\Gamma
with respect to
S
. Let
g\in\Gamma
and for
k>0
we set
n(k)=|g^{k}|_{S}
, so that
g\in S^{n(k)}
. If
\ell_{X}[g]>0
then
n(k)
tends to infinity as
k\to\infty
and we have

\displaystyle\ell_{X}[g]=\lim_{k\to\infty}{\frac{d_{X}(x,g^{k}x)}{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}{\frac{d_{X}(x,S^{n(k)})}{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}{\left(\frac{d_{X}(x,S^{n(k)})}{n(k)}\cdot\frac{|g^{k}|_{S}}{k}\right)}=\mathfrak{D}_{X}(S)\,\ell_{S}[g].

Since this holds for all
g\in\Gamma
with
\ell_{S}[g]>0
we obtain
{\rm Dil}(X,S)\leq\mathfrak{D}_{X}(S)
.

For the reverse inequality, assume that
\Gamma
and
X
satisfy either (1) or (2) and let
C>0
and
F\subset\Gamma
be given by Lemma
3.2
. Then for
n>0
and
g\in S^{n}
we have

\displaystyle d_{X}(x,gx)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{f\in F}{\ell_{X}[gf]}+C

\displaystyle\leq{\rm Dil}(X,S)\max_{f\in F}{\ell_{S}[gf]}+C

\displaystyle\leq{\rm Dil}(X,S)(|g|_{S}+\max_{f\in F}{|f|_{S}})+C

\displaystyle\leq n{\rm Dil}(X,S)+({\rm Dil}(X,S)\max_{f\in F}{|f|_{S}}+C).

This last inequality holds for any
g\in S^{n}
, and since
F
is finite we obtain that
S
is bounded if
{\rm Dil}(X,S)
is finite. Also, after taking supremum over
g\in S^{n}
, dividing by
n
and letting
n
tend to infinity we get
\mathfrak{D}_{X}(S)\leq{\rm Dil}(X,S)
, as desired.
∎

Now we move to the proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.6
. For this we need the following bound for the joint stable length of isometric actions on Gromov hyperbolic spaces due to Breuillard and Fujiwara.

Theorem 3.3

(Breuillard-Fujiwara
[
9
, Thm. 1.4]
)
.

There exists a universal constant
K>0
such that if
X
is a
\delta
-hyperbolic metric space and
S
is a bounded set of isometries of
X
, then

(3.1)

\mathfrak{D}_{X}(S)\leq K\delta+\frac{1}{2}\sup_{s\in S^{2}}\ell_{X}\left[s\right]\leq K\delta+\mathfrak{D}_{X}(S).

Remark 3.4
.

(1) The statement in
[
9
, Thm. 1.4]
assumes
S
to be finite. However, it suffices for
S
to be bounded as we can easily verify
\mathfrak{D}_{X}(S)=\sup\{\mathfrak{D}_{X}(F)|F\subset S\text{ finite}\}
.

(2) In
[
9
]
, the inequality (
3.1
) is proven in the case that
X
is geodesic. However, this assumption can be overcome by considering the isometric embedding of
X
into its
injective hull

E(X)

[
22
]
. The isometric action of
\Gamma
on
X
extends to an isometric action on
E(X)
under this injection, and if
X
is
\delta
-hyperbolic, then
E(X)
is also
\delta
-hyperbolic
[
22
, Prop. 1.3]
. The inequality (
3.1
) for
E(X)
then implies the inequality for
X
, since the quantities
\mathfrak{D}_{\bullet}(S)
and
\max_{s\in S^{2}}\ell_{\bullet}\left[s\right]
are preserved under
\Gamma
-equivariant isometric embeddings. However, the assumption that
X
is geodesic is crucial to obtain uniform comparisons between
\mathfrak{D}_{X}(S)
and the
joint displacement

L_{X}(S)=\inf_{x\in X}{d_{X}(x,S)}
, which is not required in this paper.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group acting non-elementarily on the
\delta
-hyperbolic metric space
X_{\ast}
and let
S\subset\Gamma
generate
\Gamma
as a semigroup so that it is bounded with respect to this action. Let
K
be the universal constant given by Theorem
3.3
, and for
L\geq 1
set

A_{L}:=\sup_{0<\ell_{S}[g]\leq L}\frac{\ell_{X_{\ast}[g]}}{\ell_{S}[g]}
.

If
L
is an integer, by Proposition
3.1
and Theorem
3.3
we have

\displaystyle{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},S)=\mathfrak{D}_{X_{\ast}}(S)=\frac{\mathfrak{D}_{X_{\ast}}(S^{L})}{L}\leq\frac{K\delta}{L}+\frac{1}{2L}\sup_{s\in S^{2L}}{\ell_{X_{\ast}}[s]}.

Now, if
s\in S^{2L}
and
\ell_{S}[s]=0
, then
\ell_{X_{\ast}}[s]=0
. Otherwise we have
\ell_{X_{\ast}}[s]\leq A_{2L}\ell_{S}[s]\leq 2LA_{2L}
, and hence

{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},S)\leq\frac{K\delta}{L}+A_{2L},

as desired.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
\Gamma
act isometrically on
X_{\ast}
and
X
as in the assumptions of the theorem and let
K
be the constant from Theorem
3.3
. We can further assume that
{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},X)
is finite.

Let
L>6D
be arbitrary and consider
n:=\lfloor L/2D\rfloor-2
, which is a positive integer satisfying
2(n+2)D\leq L
. For a fixed point
x\in X
consider the set
S=S_{n}:=\left\{g\in\Gamma\hskip 2.84526pt\colon\hskip 2.84526ptd_{X}(x,gx)\leq(n+2)D\right\}
. Then
S
is bounded for the action on
X
by Lemma
2.5
(1) and moreover we have

(3.2)

{\rm Dil}(X,S)\leq\mathfrak{D}_{X}(S)\leq(n+2)D\ \ \text{ and }\ \ {\rm Dil}(S,X)\leq(nD)^{-1}.

This implies that
{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},S)\leq{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},X){\rm Dil}(X,S)
is finite and hence
S
is bounded for the action on
X_{\ast}
by Proposition
3.1
.

Therefore, by Theorem
3.3
and Proposition
3.1
we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},X)

\displaystyle\leq{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},S)\,{\rm Dil}(S,X)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{D}_{X_{\ast}}(S){\rm Dil}(S,X)

\displaystyle\leq\left(K\delta+\frac{1}{2}\sup_{s\in S^{2}}\ell_{X_{\ast}}[s]\right)\,{\rm Dil}(S,X).

Now note that if
s\in S^{2}
then
\ell_{X}[s]\leq{\rm Dil}(X,S)\ell_{S}[x]\leq 2(n+2)D\leq L
, and hence Proposition
3.1
gives us

\frac{1}{2}\sup_{s\in S^{2}}\ell_{X_{\ast}}[s]\leq\left(\sup_{0<\ell_{X}[g]\leq L}\frac{\ell_{X_{\ast}[g]}}{\ell_{X}[g]}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{2}\sup_{s\in S^{2}}\ell_{X}[s]\leq\left(\sup_{0<\ell_{X}[g]\leq L}\frac{\ell_{X_{\ast}[g]}}{\ell_{X}[g]}\right)\cdot\mathfrak{D}_{X}(S).

From this and (
3.2
) we deduce

\displaystyle{\rm Dil}(X_{\ast},X)

\displaystyle\leq\left(K\delta+\left(\sup_{0<\ell_{X}[g]\leq L}\frac{\ell_{X_{\ast}[g]}}{\ell_{X}[g]}\right)\cdot\mathfrak{D}_{X}(S)\right){\rm Dil}(S,X)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sup_{0<\ell_{X}[g]\leq L}\frac{\ell_{X_{\ast}[g]}}{\ell_{X}[g]}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{(n+2)D}{nD}\right)+\frac{K\delta}{nD}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sup_{0<\ell_{X}[g]\leq L}\frac{\ell_{X_{\ast}[g]}}{\ell_{X}[g]}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{L-2D}{L-6D}\right)+\frac{2K\delta}{L-6D},

where in the last inequality we used that
n\geq L/2D-3>0
. This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

We end this section with the proof of Theorem
1.8
, which is a straightforward consequence of Proposition
2.4
and the following theorem applied to
\psi=\psi^{\tau}
the hyperbolic distance-like function induced by
\tau
.

Theorem 3.5
.

For any
m\geq 1
there exist positive constants
c_{m}\leq 8\log 2+5\log m
and
d_{m}\leq 2m^{3}
with
d_{m}
an integer such that the following holds. Suppose
\rho:\Gamma\rightarrow\operatorname{PSL}_{m}({\mathbb{R}})
is a projective Anosov representation (so that
\Gamma
is hyperbolic), and let

\alpha
-roughly geodesic and quasi-isometric to pseudo metrics in

. Assume that there

and
\eta>0
satisfy

\log{\lambda_{1}(\rho(g))}\leq\eta\cdot\ell_{\psi}[g]\text{ for all }[g]\in{\bf conj}\text{ with }\ell_{\psi}[g]\leq L.

Then

{\rm Dil}(\rho,\psi):={\rm Dil}(\psi^{\rho},\psi)\leq\frac{c_{m}d_{m}}{(L-d_{m}(\alpha+1))}+\eta\cdot\left(\frac{L}{L-d_{m}(\alpha+1)}\right).

To prove this theorem we cannot directly apply Theorem
1.2
,
since for an Anosov representation
\rho:\Gamma\rightarrow\operatorname{PSL}_{m}({\mathbb{R}})
the induced hyperbolic distance-like function
\psi^{\rho}
is in general not symmetric. Instead, we replace Theorem
3.3
by
estimates due to Bochi
[
5
]
on the

\mathfrak{R}(S)
of a bounded set

of real

matrices. Recall that

\mathfrak{R}(S):=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\sup_{A\in S^{n}}\sigma_{1}(A)}^{1/n},

which is well-defined by submultiplicativity. Here

is the set of
n
-fold products of matrices in

. By Proposition
3.1
we have that if
\rho:\Gamma\rightarrow\operatorname{PSL}_{m}({\mathbb{R}})

The main estimate we require is the following, where the upper bounds for

and

follow from
[

, Thm. 5]
.

Theorem 3.6

(Bochi
[

)
.

For all

there exist

and a positive integer

With Theorem

, now using Lemma

(2). Details are left to the reader.

4.
Recovering Butt’s theorem

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
,
for which we introduce some terminology. If
M
is a closed Riemannian manifold, then its
simplicial volume

\|M\|
is a real number that describes the simplicial complexity of
M

[
17
, Sec. 0.2]
. This number is a homotopy invariant, so if
M
and
M_{\ast}
are homotopy equivalent then
\|M\|=\|M_{\ast}\|
. When
M,M_{\ast}
are negatively curved, this is equivalent to
\pi_{1}(M)
being isomorphic to
\pi_{1}(M_{\ast})
, and in this case we write
\|\Gamma\|=\|M\|
for
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)
.

If
\Gamma
is a non-elementary hyperbolic group, recall that the space
\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}
of metric structures is the quotient of
\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
under rough similarity and that
\Delta
is the metric on
\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}
given by (
2.1
). If
\delta,\alpha\geq 0
then
\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}^{\delta,\alpha}\subset\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}
denotes the subset of all the metric structures represented by a
\delta
-hyperbolic and
\alpha
-roughly geodesic pseudo metric in
\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
with
exponential growth rate
1 (see
[
31
, Sec. 2]
for details). Similarly, for
D\geq 0
we let
\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}^{\delta,(D)}
denote the space of all metric structures induced by a proper and
D
-cobounded action of
\Gamma
on a geodesic and
\delta
-hyperbolic metric space with exponential growth rate 1.

Remark 4.1
.

We can verify that
\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}^{\delta,(D)}\subset\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}^{\delta,2D+1}
for all
\delta,\alpha\geq 0
and
\Gamma
. The argument for this is as in the proof of
[
2
, Lem. 4.6]
. We leave the details to the reader.

If
(M,\mathfrak{g})
is a closed Riemannian manifold and
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)
we let
\widetilde{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}=(\widetilde{M},d_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}})
be the universal cover of
M
equipped with the (
\Gamma
-invariant) Riemannian distance induced by the lift
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. The Deck action of
\Gamma
on
\widetilde{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}
induces the metric structure
[\widetilde{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}]\in\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
n\geq 2
there exists a constant
C_{n}>0
such that the following holds. Let
(M,\mathfrak{g})
be a closed Riemannian manifold with sectional curvatures contained in the interval
[-\Lambda^{2},-\lambda^{2}]\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{<0}
, injectivity radius
i_{\mathfrak{g}}
and fundamental group
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)
. Then

(1)

\textnormal{Vol}(M,\mathfrak{g})\leq C_{n}\|\Gamma\|\lambda^{-n}
;

(2)

\textnormal{Diam}(M,\mathfrak{g})\leq C_{n}\|\Gamma\|\lambda^{-n}i_{\mathfrak{g}}^{1-n}
;

(3)

\widetilde{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is
\lambda^{-1}\log{2}
-hyperbolic and the Deck action of
\Gamma
on
\widetilde{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is
C_{n}\|\Gamma\|\lambda^{-n}i_{\mathfrak{g}}^{1-n}
-cobounded; and,

(4)

the exponential growth rate
v_{\mathfrak{g}}
of
\widetilde{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}
satisfies
\frac{\lambda}{C_{n}}\leq v_{\mathfrak{g}}\leq\Lambda(n-1).

Therefore, we have
[\widetilde{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}]\in\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}^{\delta,(D)}
for

\delta=\Lambda\lambda^{-1}(n-1)\log{2}\hskip 5.69054pt\text{ and }\hskip 5.69054ptD=\Lambda(n-1)C_{n}\|\Gamma\|\lambda^{-n}i_{\mathfrak{g}}^{1-n}.

Proof.

Item (1) is exactly Gromov-Thurston’s theorem (see e.g.
[
17
, Sec. 0.3]
), and Item (2) follows by combining Item (1) and
[
16
, Cor. 15]
. For Item (3), note first that
\lambda d_{\mathfrak{g}}=d_{\lambda^{2}\mathfrak{g}}
, and that
(M,\lambda^{2}\mathfrak{g})
has sectional curvatures bounded above by -1. Then
(\widetilde{M},d_{\lambda^{2}\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}})
is
{\rm CAT}(-1)
, and hence
\widetilde{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is
\log{2}
-hyperbolic
[
25
, Thm. 4.2 & Thm. 5.1]
. The bound for the codiameter follows directly from Item (2).

The lower bound in Item (4) follows from Item (1) and the inequality

\|\Gamma\|\leq C_{n}^{\prime}\textnormal{Vol}(M,\mathfrak{g}){v_{\mathfrak{g}}}^{n},

where
C_{n}^{\prime}>0
depends only on
n

[
17
, Sec. 2.5]
. For the upper bound, note that sectional curvatures bounded below by
-\Lambda^{2}
imply that the Ricci curvature is bounded below by
-(n-1)\Lambda^{2}
. Then by Bishop-Gromov’s inequality
[
27
, Lem. 7.1.3]
,
v_{\mathfrak{g}}
is bounded above by
\Lambda(n-1)
, which equals the exponential growth rate of the
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold of constant sectional curvature equal to
-\Lambda^{2}
.

The last assertion of the lemma follows from Items (3) and (4).
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
(M,\mathfrak{g}),(M_{\ast},\mathfrak{g}_{\ast})
be closed Riemannian manifolds of dimension
n
, let
\Gamma
be the fundamental group of
M
, and let
f_{\ast}:\Gamma\rightarrow\pi_{1}(M_{\ast})
be an isomorphism. Assume that their sectional curvatures are bounded above by
-\lambda^{2}<0
and let
i_{\mathfrak{g}},i_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}
denote the injectivity radii of
(M,\mathfrak{g})
and
(M_{\ast},\mathfrak{g}_{\ast})
respectively. Fix
\eta_{0}>0
, and let
0<\eta\leq\eta_{0}
and
L>0
be such that

(4.1)

\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[g]\leq\eta\ell_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}[f_{\ast}g]\text{ for all }[g]\in{\bf conj}\text{ with }\ell_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}[f_{\ast}g]\leq L.

(1)

If
L\geq 2\eta^{-1}_{0}i_{\mathfrak{g}}
, then
i_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}\geq\eta_{0}^{-1}i_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

(2)

If
L>\hat{L}_{\ast}:=6C_{n}\|\Gamma\|\lambda^{-n}{i_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}}^{1-n}
, then

\frac{\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[g]}{\ell_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}[f_{\ast}g]}\leq\eta\left(\frac{L}{L-\hat{L}_{\ast}}\right)+\frac{2K\lambda^{-1}\log{2}}{L-\hat{L}_{\ast}}

for all
[g]\in{\bf conj}^{\prime}
, where
K
and
C_{n}
are the constants from Theorem
1.2
and Lemma
4.2
respectively.

Remark
.

Note that the corollary does not require a lower bound for the sectional curvatures.

Proof.

To prove (1), note that
i_{\mathfrak{g}}=\frac{1}{2}\inf_{[g]\in{\bf conj}^{\prime}}{\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[g]}
, with a similar formula valid for
i_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}

[
27
, p. 258]
. Suppose for the sake of contradiction that
i_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}<\eta_{0}^{-1}i_{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then there exists
[g]\in{\bf conj}^{\prime}
such that
i_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}=\ell_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}[f_{\ast}g]/2
, and since
L\geq 2\eta_{0}^{-1}i_{\mathfrak{g}}
we have

i_{\mathfrak{g}}\leq\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[g]/2\leq\eta_{0}\ell_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}[f_{\ast}g]/2=\eta_{0}i_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}},

contradicting our assumption.

To prove (2), consider the actions of
\Gamma
on
X_{\ast}=\widetilde{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
X=\widetilde{M_{\ast}}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}
, where
\Gamma
acts on
\widetilde{M_{\ast}}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}
via
f_{\ast}
. By Lemma
4.2
(2) we have that the action of
\Gamma
on
\widetilde{M_{\ast}}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}
is
D_{\ast}
-cobounded with
D_{\ast}=C_{n}\|\Gamma\|\lambda^{-n}{i_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}}^{1-n}=\hat{L}_{\ast}/6
. The conclusion follows by Lemma
4.2
(3) and Assumption (
4.1
), and Theorem
1.2
applied to
X_{\ast}
,
X
and
L
.
∎

The corollary above gives us one of the bounds in (
1.2
). For the other bound, we would like to apply the corollary with the roles of
(M,\mathfrak{g})
and
(M_{\ast},\mathfrak{g}_{\ast})
exchanged. To do this we need the following proposition.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(M,\mathfrak{g}),(M_{\ast},\mathfrak{g}_{\ast})
be closed Riemannian manifolds of dimension
n\geq 2
, sectional curvatures contained in the interval
[-\Lambda^{2},-\lambda^{2}]\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{<0}
, and injectivity radii bounded below by
i
.
Let
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)
, and consider an isomorphism
f_{\ast}:\Gamma\rightarrow\pi_{1}(M_{\ast})
. Then for all
P>1
there exists a constant
R=R(\Gamma,\lambda,\Lambda,i,P)>0
such that if

\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[g]\leq P\ell_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}[f_{\ast}g]
for all
[g]\in{\bf conj}
, then

(4.2)

\sup_{[g]\in{\bf conj}^{\prime}}{\frac{\ell_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}[f_{\ast}g]}{\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[g]}}\leq R.

Moreover, if
n\geq 3
then
R
does not depend on
P
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma
act on
\widetilde{M}_{\mathfrak{g}},\widetilde{M_{\ast}}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}
as in the proof of Corollary
4.3
(2). By Lemma
4.2
and Remark
4.1
, the metric structures
\rho_{\mathfrak{g}}=[\widetilde{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}]
and
\rho_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}=[\widetilde{M_{\ast}}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}]
belong to
\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}^{\delta,\alpha}
, for
\delta
and
\alpha
depending only on
n,\|\Gamma\|,\lambda,\Lambda
and
i
. In addition, the function
\Psi:\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}\times\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
given by

\Psi([d_{0}],[d_{1}])\rightarrow{\rm Dil}(d_{0},d_{1})
, where
d_{0}
and
d_{1}
have exponential growth rate 1, is continuous for the topology induced by
\Delta
. This follows by combining Proposition 3.5 and Lemma 3.6 in
[
31
]
.

If
n\geq 3
, then
\textnormal{Out}(\Gamma)
is finite. Indeed, otherwise by
[
3
, Cor. 1.3]

\Gamma
would split over a virtually cyclic subgroup. Since
\partial\Gamma
is homeomorphic to the
(n-1)
-dimensional sphere, this virtually cyclic subgroup would have to be infinite, but this contradicts
[
7
, Thm. 6.2]
because
\partial\Gamma
has no local-cut points. Since
\Gamma
is torsion-free, by
[
31
, Thm. 1.7]
we deduce that
\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}^{\delta,\alpha}
is compact. The conclusion then follows by the continuity of
\Psi
, Lemma
4.2
(4), and noting that the left-hand side of (
4.2
) equals
v_{\mathfrak{g}}\Psi(\rho_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}},\rho_{\mathfrak{g}})/v_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}
.

If
n=2
, then
[
31
, Thm. 1.7]
still implies that
\textnormal{Out}(\Gamma)
acts cocompactly on
\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}^{\delta,\alpha}
, so let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}^{\delta,\alpha}
be a compact set such that
\textnormal{Out}(\Gamma)\cdot\mathcal{B}=\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}^{\delta,\alpha}
. We further assume that
\rho_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}\in\mathcal{B}
, and that
D=\textnormal{Diam}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{B})
is minimal among all the diameters of compact subsets of
\mathscr{D}^{\delta,\alpha}_{\Gamma}
whose translates by
\textnormal{Out}(\Gamma)
cover
\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}^{\delta,\alpha}
. We also fix an arbitrary point
\rho_{0}=[d_{0}]\in\mathcal{B}
with exponential growth rate 1. By the same argument in the proof of
[
31
, Thm. 1.7]
we can prove that the function
\textnormal{Out}(\Gamma)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
that maps
\phi
to
\Psi(\phi(\rho_{0}),\rho_{0})={\rm Dil}(\overline{\phi}(d_{0}),d_{0})
is proper, where
\overline{\phi}
is any automorphism of
\Gamma
representing
\phi
. This implies that the set
\mathcal{F}\subset\textnormal{Out}(\Gamma)
of outer automorphisms
\phi
represented by an automorphism
\overline{\phi}
such that

{\rm Dil}(\overline{\phi}(d_{0}),d_{0})\leq\hat{P}:=C_{2}\Lambda\lambda^{-1}e^{2D}P

is finite, where
C_{2}
is the constant from Lemma
4.2
. Therefore, if
\phi\in\textnormal{Out}(\Gamma)
is represented by
\overline{\phi}
and
\rho\in\phi\mathcal{B}
, then by Lemma
4.2
(4) we have

{\rm Dil}(\overline{\phi}(d_{0}),d_{0})\leq{\rm Dil}(\overline{\phi}(d_{0}),\widetilde{M_{\ast}}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}){\rm Dil}(\widetilde{M_{\ast}}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}},\widetilde{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}){\rm Dil}(\widetilde{M}_{\mathfrak{g}},d_{0})\leq\hat{P},

and hence
\phi\in\mathcal{F}
. The conclusion follows by choosing

R=C_{2}\Lambda\lambda^{-1}\max\left\{\Psi(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})\colon\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in\bigcup_{\phi\in\mathcal{F}}{\phi\mathcal{B}}\right\}

and noting that
D,\hat{P},\mathcal{F}
and
R
depend only on
\Gamma
,
\delta,\alpha
and
P
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
n=1+\dim(\partial\Gamma)
be the dimension of
M
and
M_{\ast}
, and let
i_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}
be the injectivity radius of
(M_{\ast},\mathfrak{g}_{\ast})
. Also, let
K,C_{n}
be the constants from Theorem
1.2
and Lemma
4.2
respectively, and let
\hat{L}_{\ast}:=6C_{n}\|\Gamma\|\lambda^{-n}((1-\epsilon_{0})i_{\mathfrak{g}})^{1-n}
. By Corollary
4.3
, if Assumption (
1.1
) holds for
L>2\max\{(1-\epsilon_{0})i_{\mathfrak{g}},\hat{L}_{\ast}\}
then
\hat{L}_{\ast}\geq 6C_{n}\|\Gamma\|\lambda^{-n}i_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}^{1-n}
, and hence for all
[g]\in{\bf conj}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[g]}{\ell_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}[f_{\ast}g]}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\epsilon)^{-1}\left(\frac{L}{L-\hat{L}_{\ast}}\right)+\frac{2K\lambda^{-1}\log{2}}{L-\hat{L}_{\ast}}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\epsilon)^{-1}+\frac{2(\hat{L}_{\ast}(1-\epsilon_{0})^{-1}+2K\lambda^{-1}\log{2})}{L}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\epsilon_{0})^{-1}+\frac{2(\hat{L}_{\ast}(1-\epsilon_{0})^{-1}+2K\lambda^{-1}\log{2})}{2\hat{L}_{\ast}}=:P.

In particular, for all
[g]\in{\bf conj}^{\prime}
we deduce

\displaystyle\frac{\ell_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}[f_{\ast}g]}{\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[g]}

\displaystyle\geq\left((1-\epsilon)^{-1}+\frac{2(\hat{L}_{\ast}(1-\epsilon_{0})^{-1}+2K\lambda^{-1}\log{2})}{L}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\epsilon)-\frac{2(\hat{L}_{\ast}(1-\epsilon_{0})^{-1}+2K\lambda^{-1}\log{2})}{L},

which settles the lower bound in (
1.2
).

For the upper bound, since
P
only depends on
\Gamma,\lambda,\Lambda,i_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\epsilon_{0}
, by Proposition
4.4
there exists
R=R(\Gamma,\lambda,\Lambda,i_{\mathfrak{g}},\epsilon_{0})>0
such that
\ell_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}[f_{\ast}g]\leq L
holds for all
[g]\in{\bf conj}
satisfying
\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[x]\leq L/R
. In consequence, (
1.1
) holds for all
[g]\in{\bf conj}^{\prime}
such that
\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[g]\leq L/R
. If we define
L_{\ast}:=6C_{n}\|\Gamma\|\lambda^{-n}{i_{\mathfrak{g}}}^{-1+n}
, Corollary
4.3
implies that for
L>2RL_{\ast}
and all
[g]\in{\bf conj}^{\prime}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\ell_{\mathfrak{g}_{\ast}}[f_{\ast}g]}{\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[g]}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\epsilon)\left(\frac{L}{L-RL_{\ast}}\right)+\frac{2RK\lambda^{-1}\log{2}}{L-RL_{\ast}}

\displaystyle=(1+\epsilon)+\frac{R(L_{\ast}(1+\epsilon)+2K\lambda^{-1}\log{2})}{L-RL_{\ast}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\epsilon)+\frac{2R(L_{\ast}(1+\epsilon_{0})+2K\lambda^{-1}\log{2})}{L}.

This proves the upper bound in (
1.2
), and the theorem follows by setting
L_{0}:=2\max\{RL_{\ast},\hat{L}_{\ast},(1-\epsilon_{0})i_{\mathfrak{g}}\}+1
, which depends only on
\Gamma,\lambda,\Lambda,i_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\epsilon_{0}
.
∎

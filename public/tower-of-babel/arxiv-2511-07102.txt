Complete subvarieties in the projectivized strata of holomorphic differentials
Dawei Chen, Guillaume Tahar

Abstract
. We show that the projectivized strata of holomorphic differentials with prescribed zero orders contain no positive-dimensional complete subvarieties. In the case of the minimal strata, this resolves a question of Harris concerning the existence of complete families of subcanonical points. Our proof relies on the geometry of flat cylinders.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mu
be a partition of
2g-2
. Denote by
\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
the stratum of Abelian differentials (i.e., differential one-forms) on smooth, connected, genus-
g
complex curves whose zero and pole orders are prescribed by
\mu
, and let
\mathbb{P}\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)=\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)/\mathbb{C}^{*}
be the corresponding projectivized stratum.

It is a natural and meaningful question to study how the strata of differentials appear from the perspective of affine geometry—for instance, whether a given stratum can contain a positive-dimensional complete algebraic subvariety.

For a holomorphic signature
\mu
, that is, when all entries of
\mu
are nonnegative, Gendron showed that the unprojectivized stratum
\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
of holomorphic differentials contains no positive-dimensional complete subvariety, by applying the maximum modulus principle to shortest saddle connections; see
[
7
]
. In this case, an alternative proof was later given by the first-named author, using the positivity of certain divisor classes on the moduli space of curves; see
[
4
]
.

When
\mu
is a signature of (strictly) meromorphic differentials, again by exploiting the positivity of divisor classes, the first-named author proved that both the projectivized and unprojectivized strata,
\mathbb{P}\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
and
\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
, of strictly meromorphic differentials contain no positive-dimensional complete subvarieties; see
[
3
,
5
]
. Therefore, the remaining question is whether the projectivized strata of holomorphic differentials can contain a positive-dimensional complete subvariety.

This remaining problem is not only the most challenging case, but also significant from the viewpoint of the geometry of canonical divisors. For instance, for
\mu=(2g-2)
, the minimal stratum
\mathbb{P}\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(2g-2)
parameterizes subcanonical points
z
, where
(2g-2)z
is a canonical divisor. Harris asked whether there exist complete families of such subcanonical points (see
[
8
, Proof of Corollary 5]
), a question that has remained open for four decades.

In this paper, we resolve this problem as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any stratum
\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
of holomorphic differentials, the projectivized stratum
\mathbb{P}\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
contains no positive-dimensional complete subvariety.

Besides Abelian differentials, one can also study the strata of
k
-differentials with prescribed zero and pole orders, where a
k
-differential is a section of the
k
-th power of the canonical bundle. Note that a
k
-differential with pole orders at most
k-1
(i.e., when the corresponding
\frac{1}{k}
-translation surface has finite area) can be lifted via the canonical cyclic covering construction to the
k
-th power of a holomorphic one-form; see
[
1
, Section 2]
. Theorem
1.1
therefore implies the following corollary, which settles the remaining case concerning complete subvarieties in the strata of
k
-differentials; see
[
4
, Remark]
.

Corollary 1.2
.

For any stratum
\Omega^{k}\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
of
k
-differentials whose pole orders are bounded by
k-1
, the projectivized stratum
\mathbb{P}\Omega^{k}\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
contains no positive-dimensional complete subvarieties.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we refine Gendron’s argument from
[
7
]
. By applying the maximum principle to suitably chosen plurisubharmonic functions, we show that, in any complete subvariety contained in a projectivized stratum, the following quantities are rigid:

•

the conformal moduli of cylinders (see Proposition
3.6
);

•

the periods of closed geodesics, up to a global scaling (see Proposition
3.8
);

•

suitably normalized lengths of saddle connections (see Section
4
).

Finally, we remark that since
\mathbb{P}\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
is a quasi-projective variety, its complete algebraic subvarieties and complex analytic subvarieties coincide by the GAGA principle. Moreover, if a complete subvariety
M
is singular, we may work with a resolution of singularities of
M
and pull back the family of differentials accordingly. Alternatively, one can intersect a higher-dimensional complete subvariety with ample hypersurfaces until obtaining a complete algebraic curve. Therefore, Theorem
1.1
is equivalent to showing that
\mathbb{P}\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
contains no complete algebraic curves, in which case we may work with the normalization of such a curve, which is smooth. Therefore, in these senses, even when
M
is singular, we can still speak of and make use of holomorphic local coordinates on
M
. We shall do so without further comment.

2.
Period coordinates of the strata of holomorphic differentials

Let
\mu=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})
be a partition of
2g-2
, where
m_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
for all
i
. Given
(X,\omega)\in\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
, let
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{2g+n-1}
be a basis of the relative homology group
H_{1}(X,\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\};\mathbb{Z})
, where
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}
denote the zeros of
\omega
. Integrating
\omega
along these paths provides local coordinates on
\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
near
(X,\omega)
:

\int_{\gamma_{1}}\omega,\ldots,\int_{\gamma_{2g+n-1}}\omega,

which are called
period coordinates
.

Note that
\omega
induces a translation structure on

X\setminus\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\}
, where each zero
z_{i}
of order
m_{i}\geq 1
corresponds, under the induced flat metric, to a
conical singularity
(also called a
saddle point
) with cone angle
2\pi(m_{i}+1)
. The period coordinates defined above describe local deformations of the translation surface structure while preserving the number and cone angles of its singularities, thus providing local coordinates for the stratum
\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
.

A
saddle connection
is a geodesic joining two zeros of
\omega
, which is said to be closed if the two zeros coincide. Since one may choose the homology class representatives to be length-minimizing paths connecting the zeros of
\omega
, it follows that saddle connections generate the relative homology group, or dually, that their periods generate the local coordinate system on
\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
near
(X,\omega)
.

We refer to
[
13
]
for a comprehensive survey of further details and properties of the strata of differentials.

It is well known that, within a given homotopy class of simple loops on the surface punctured at the zeros, either there exists a unique geodesic representative formed by the concatenation of saddle connections, or the geodesic representatives form a one-parameter family of closed geodesics constituting a cylinder (see Section
3
). The following result was proved in
[
2
, Proposition 2.2]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

In a translation surface
(X,\omega)
, a simple loop
\gamma
is a length minimizer in its homotopy class if and only if it is either a closed geodesic or a concatenation of saddle connections such that, at each turning point, the angles between consecutive segments are at least
\pi
on both sides of the curve
\gamma
.

The
systole
of a translation surface
(X,\omega)
is the length of its shortest saddle connection or closed geodesic. The following statement is an immediate consequence of the construction of Delaunay triangulations of flat surfaces in
[
9
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Given a compact subset
M
of a projectivized stratum
\mathbb{P}\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
of holomorphic differentials, the systole of any unit-area translation surface in
M
is bounded below by some constant
c>0
.

3.
Cylinders

From now on, we assume that our translation surfaces have at least one zero or marked point. This excludes the stratum
\mathbb{P}\Omega\mathcal{M}_{1}(\emptyset)
of unmarked flat tori.

3.1.
Generalities about cylinders

In a translation surface
(X,\omega)
belonging to a stratum
\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
of holomorphic differentials, any closed geodesic lies in a one-parameter family of parallel closed geodesics sweeping out a subsurface called a
cylinder
. Moreover, for each homotopy class of simple closed geodesics, there exists at most one maximal cylinder.

In our setting, translation surfaces have at least one conical singularity and finite area, so every cylinder also has finite area. Each of its two boundaries is formed by finitely many parallel saddle connections. Given a cylinder
\mathcal{C}
and one of its closed geodesics
\alpha
, the length

\left|\int_{\alpha}\omega\right|

of
\alpha
is called the
width
of the cylinder
\mathcal{C}
(following the convention used, for example, in
[
6
]
).

Given a cylinder of area
\mathcal{A}
and width
w
, its
conformal modulus
(which classifies topological annuli up to biholomorphism) is defined by

\frac{\mathcal{A}}{w^{2}}=\frac{h}{w},

where
h
is the
height
of the cylinder.

Denoting by
\alpha
one of its closed geodesics and by
\beta
a saddle connection contained in the cylinder joining its two boundary components, the conformal modulus can also be expressed as

\Im\left(\frac{\int_{\beta}\omega}{\int_{\alpha}\omega}\right).

Note that the conformal modulus of
\mathcal{C}
is invariant under rotations and real scalings of the translation surface; that is, it is preserved under the transformation
\omega\mapsto\lambda\omega
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Any translation surface
(X,\omega)
of unit area contains infinitely many distinct cylinders. Moreover, for any constant
K>0
,
(X,\omega)
contains at most finitely many cylinders with conformal modulus at least
K
.

Proof.

Following Masur
[
10
]
, let
N(L)
denote the number of cylinders (each corresponding to a homotopy class of closed geodesics) of width at most
L
in
(X,\omega)
. Then there exist constants
a,b>0
such that, for
L
sufficiently large,

aL^{2}\leq N(L)\leq bL^{2}.

As
L\to\infty
, this immediately implies that
(X,\omega)
contains infinitely many cylinders.

Since the area of each cylinder is at most
1
, any cylinder with conformal modulus at least
K
must have width at most
1/\sqrt{K}
, and therefore there can be only finitely many such cylinders.
∎

As explained in Proposition
2.1
, if a homotopy class of simple loops does not contain the closed geodesics of any cylinder, then there exists a unique geodesic representative formed by a concatenation of saddle connections. Interpreting this case as a cylinder of conformal modulus equal to zero, we extend the notion of conformal modulus to an arbitrary homotopy class of simple loops and show that, under this extension, the conformal modulus varies continuously along the stratum.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a translation surface in a stratum
\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
, and let
[\alpha]
be a homotopy class of simple loops in
(X,\omega)
such that
\int_{\alpha}\omega\neq 0
. Then there exists a neighborhood
U
of
(X,\omega)
in
\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
such that the conformal modulus associated with the homotopy class
[\alpha]
varies continuously on
U
.

Proof.

We first consider a sufficiently small neighborhood
U
of
(X,\omega)
in the ambient stratum such that
P_{\alpha}=\int_{\alpha}\omega
does not vanish. We distinguish two cases according to Proposition
2.1
.

First, suppose that
(X,\omega)
admits a cylinder
\mathcal{C}
whose closed geodesics belong to the class
[\alpha]
. The cylinder
\mathcal{C}
is a translation surface with boundary, and each of its points has a well-defined real coordinate
y
in
\mathbb{C}/P_{\alpha}\mathbb{R}
. One boundary, denoted
\partial^{-}\mathcal{C}
, corresponds to the minimal value of
y
, while the other,
\partial^{+}\mathcal{C}
, corresponds to the maximal value of
y
.

As
(X,\omega)
is deformed within the stratum, no boundary saddle connection of
\partial\mathcal{C}
disappears, provided the deformation is sufficiently small. Although the boundary saddle connections of
\partial\mathcal{C}
may cease to be parallel to
P_{\alpha}
, the topological cylinder
\mathcal{C}
still contains a family of closed geodesics of period
P_{\alpha}
, whose
y
coordinates lie between the maximal value of
y
on
\partial^{-}\mathcal{C}
and the minimal value of
y
on
\partial^{+}\mathcal{C}
. The length of this interval, which coincides with the height of the cylinder, changes continuously with the period coordinates, and hence the conformal modulus of
\mathcal{C}
also varies continuously.

In the case where
[\alpha]
has a unique geodesic representative formed by a concatenation of saddle connections, no cylinder can appear under sufficiently small deformations of
(X,\omega)
unless all angles at the conical points are equal to
\pi
. In the former situation, the conformal modulus associated with this homotopy class remains uniformly zero. In the latter situation, arbitrarily small deformations of the saddle connections may produce a cylinder, but an argument using the
y
coordinate as above shows that the height of this cylinder changes continuously from zero in proportion to the deformation.
∎

In a hypothetical positive-dimensional complete subvariety contained in a projectivized stratum of holomorphic differentials, cylinders cannot have arbitrarily small area while maintaining a conformal modulus bounded below.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
M
be a compact subset of the projectivized stratum of holomorphic differentials
\mathbb{P}\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
. Then there exists a constant
c>0
such that, for any unit-area translation surface
(X,\omega)\in M
, the area of any cylinder in
(X,\omega)
with conformal modulus at least
K
is bounded below by
Kc^{2}
.

Proof.

For any unit-area translation surface
(X,\omega)
in a compact subset of
\mathbb{P}\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
, the systole (see Proposition
2.2
) is bounded below by some constant
c>0
. In particular, the width of any cylinder is bounded below by
c
. Therefore, for a cylinder of area
\mathcal{A}
and conformal modulus at least
K
, we have

\mathcal{A}\geq Kc^{2}
.
∎

3.2.
Rigidity of conformal moduli of cylinders

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
M
be a subvariety of the projectivized stratum
\mathbb{P}\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
of holomorphic differentials of type
\mu
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a cylinder in a translation surface
(X,\omega)
parameterized by
M
.
Then there exists a neighborhood
U
of
(X,\omega)
in
M
such that the cylinder
\mathcal{C}
persists throughout
U
.
Moreover, the conformal modulus of
\mathcal{C}
varies pluriharmonically with respect to the holomorphic coordinates on
U
.

Proof.

Translation cylinders are structurally stable in
\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
; hence there exists a neighborhood
U
of
(X,\omega)
in
M
where the cylinder
\mathcal{C}
persists. Indeed, one can trace the closed geodesic representative of the cylinder core curve
\alpha
by following the midpoint of the transverse curve
\beta
.

Since the conformal modulus is invariant under real scaling and rotation of the translation surface, the ratio
\frac{\int_{\beta}\omega}{\int_{\alpha}\omega}
defines a holomorphic function in the period coordinates. Consequently, its imaginary part is pluriharmonic with respect to the holomorphic coordinates on
U
.
∎

We deduce the following dichotomy.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
M
be a positive-dimensional subvariety of the projectivized stratum
\mathbb{P}\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
of holomorphic differentials.
For any
(X,\omega)\in M
and any cylinder
\mathcal{C}
in
(X,\omega)
, exactly one of the following holds:

(1)

There exists a neighborhood
U
of
(X,\omega)
in
M
such that the conformal modulus of
\mathcal{C}
remains constant on
U
(
rigid cylinder
);

(2)

The conformal modulus of
\mathcal{C}
is a non-constant positive pluriharmonic function in a neighborhood of
(X,\omega)
in
M
(
flexible cylinder
).

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
M
be a positive-dimensional subvariety of the projectivized stratum
\mathbb{P}\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
of holomorphic differentials.
Then for every
(X,\omega)\in M
, any cylinder
\mathcal{C}
in
(X,\omega)
has constant conformal modulus on a neighborhood of
(X,\omega)
in
M
; equivalently, every cylinder in
(X,\omega)
is rigid.

Proof.

We assume that all translation surfaces parameterized by
M
are normalized to have unit area. Every cylinder thus has finite area, and consequently a finite conformal modulus. Given a translation surface
(X,\omega)
and any constant
K>0
, there exist at most finitely many cylinders of conformal modulus at least
K
, each of which is structurally stable—that is, its conformal modulus varies continuously under deformations of
(X,\omega)
.

Define a function

L\colon M\to\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}

by assigning to
(X,\omega)
the largest conformal modulus among all flexible cylinders of
(X,\omega)
, and set
L(X,\omega)=0
if
(X,\omega)
has only rigid cylinders.
We first prove that
L
is a continuous function on
M
.

In the open subset
U
of
M
consisting of translation surfaces that contain flexible cylinders, for any fixed bound
K>0
, there exist only finitely many flexible cylinders whose conformal modulus is at least
K
(see Proposition
3.1
). Moreover, flexible cylinders remain flexible under sufficiently small deformations (see Lemma
3.5
), so the continuity of
L
on
U
follows immediately from Proposition
3.2
.

Now consider a sequence of surfaces
(X_{n},\omega_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
U
converging to a surface
(X_{\infty},\omega_{\infty})
on the boundary
\partial U
of
U
in
M
. Assume, for the sake of contradiction, that
\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}L(X_{n},\omega_{n})>K
for some
K>0
. Then after passing to a subsequence, each surface
(X_{n},\omega_{n})
contains a flexible cylinder
\mathcal{C}_{n}
whose conformal modulus is at least
K
. By Proposition
2.2
, the systoles of unit-area surfaces in
M
are bounded below by some constant
c>0
. It follows from Corollary
3.3
that any cylinder of conformal modulus at least
K
has area at least
Kc^{2}
, for some constant
c>0
depending only on
M
. In particular, the height of such a cylinder is bigger than or equal to
Kc
. Hence, the cylinders
\mathcal{C}_{n}
do not degenerate as
n\to\infty
. Since
(X_{\infty},\omega_{\infty})
admits only finitely many cylinders of conformal modulus at least
K
, the sequence
(\mathcal{C}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
accumulates on one of them, which we denote by
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
.
By assumption,
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
is a rigid cylinder; thus, there exists a neighborhood of
(X_{\infty},\omega_{\infty})
in which the homotopy class of geodesics corresponding to
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
cannot be represented by a flexible cylinder.
This yields a contradiction. Therefore, the function
L
tends to zero as
(X_{n},\omega_{n})\to(X_{\infty},\omega_{\infty})
.
Since
L
is identically zero on
M\setminus U
, we conclude that
L
is continuous on
M
.

Since
M
is a complete subvariety contained in the stratum, if there exists at least one surface
(X,\omega)
in
M
admitting flexible cylinders, then the function
L
attains a positive global maximum, realized by one or several flexible cylinders
C_{1},\dots,C_{k}
on a translation surface
(X,\omega)
in
M
. In a neighborhood
U
of
(X,\omega)
in
M
, the function
L
equals the maximum of the conformal moduli of these finitely many cylinders. Hence, as the maximum of finitely many pluriharmonic functions,
L
is plurisubharmonic on
U
and satisfies the maximum principle. Consequently, the conformal moduli of the cylinders
C_{1},\dots,C_{k}
must remain constant in a neighborhood of
(X,\omega)
; otherwise,
L
would not achieve a local maximum at
(X,\omega)
. We therefore conclude that
C_{1},\dots,C_{k}
are not flexible cylinders.
Thus, all cylinders on every translation surface parameterized by
M
are rigid.
∎

3.3.
Rigidity of periods of cylinders

Similarly to Lemma
3.5
, we establish the following dichotomy for pairs of cylinders.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
M
be a positive-dimensional subvariety of the projectivized stratum
\mathbb{P}\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
of holomorphic differentials.
For any
(X,\omega)\in M
, and for any pair of cylinders
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}
in
(X,\omega)
with waist curves
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
, exactly one of the following holds:

(1)

There exists a neighborhood
U
of
(X,\omega)
in
M
such that the ratio
\frac{\int_{\alpha_{1}}\omega}{\int_{\alpha_{2}}\omega}
is constant on
U
(
rigid pair of cylinders
);

(2)

The function
\log\left|\frac{\int_{\alpha_{1}}\omega}{\int_{\alpha_{2}}\omega}\right|
is a non-constant pluriharmonic function in a neighborhood of
(X,\omega)
in
M
(
flexible pair of cylinders
).

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Lemma
3.5
. The logarithm of the modulus of a ratio of periods is a pluriharmonic function.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
M
be a positive-dimensional complete subvariety contained in the projectivized stratum
\mathbb{P}\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
of holomorphic differentials. Then, for any
(X,\omega)\in M
, every pair of cylinders in
(X,\omega)
is rigid.

Proof.

For any
K>0
, let
Cyl_{K}(X,\omega)
be the finite set of cylinders in
(X,\omega)
whose conformal modulus is strictly greater than
K
. Denote by
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}
a waist curve of each of these cylinders. By Proposition
3.6
, these cylinders remain cylinders of conformal modulus strictly greater than
K
under deformations in
M
.

However, if
M
is not simply connected, a deformation along a closed path in
M
can permute elements of
Cyl_{K}(X,\omega)
. For this reason, we consider a finite cover
\tilde{M}
of
M
such that, for each pair
1\leq i,j\leq k
, the ratio of periods
\frac{\int_{\alpha_{i}}\omega}{\int_{\alpha_{j}}\omega}
is a globally defined holomorphic function on
\tilde{M}
. Note that none of these periods can vanish, since they correspond to closed geodesics. Depending on whether this ratio is constant or not, a pair of cylinders in
Cyl_{K}(X,\omega)
is either rigid along all of
M
or flexible along all of
M
.

Assume, for the sake of contradiction, that for some
K>0
,
(X,\omega)
admits a pair of flexible cylinders in
Cyl_{K}(X,\omega)
. Define a function

L_{K}\colon\tilde{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}

by assigning to
(X,\omega)
the maximum of the logarithms of ratios of the form
\frac{|\int_{\beta}\omega|}{|\int_{\alpha}\omega|}
where
\alpha,\beta
are waist curves of cylinders forming a flexible pair in
Cyl_{K}(X,\omega)
.

From the preceding discussion,
L_{K}
is the maximum of finitely many pluriharmonic functions globally defined on
\tilde{M}
. Therefore,
L_{K}
is a plurisubharmonic function on
\tilde{M}
, which is compact as a finite cover of a complete variety. Applying the maximum principle to a global maximum of
L_{K}
on
\tilde{M}
, we deduce that
L_{K}
must be constant and that every pair of cylinders in
Cyl_{K}(X,\omega)
is rigid.

Since this argument applies for arbitrary
K>0
, it follows that every pair of cylinders on any surface
(X,\omega)
in
M
is rigid.
∎

4.
Proof of the main theorem

In this section, we normalize the projectivization of a translation surface by using the period of the cylinder with the largest conformal modulus, instead of the total area of the translation surface.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a translation surface in a stratum
\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
of holomorphic differentials. We denote by
MaxCyl(X,\omega)
the set of cylinders of largest width among those having the largest conformal modulus. For any saddle connection
\alpha
in
(X,\omega)
and any waist curve
\beta
of a cylinder in
MaxCyl(X,\omega)
, the normalized length of
\alpha
, denoted by

l(\alpha)=\frac{|\int_{\alpha}\omega|}{|\int_{\beta}\omega|},

does not depend on the choice of the cylinder in
MaxCyl(X,\omega)
.

Moreover, for any compact subset
M
of
\mathbb{P}\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
, there exists a constant
b>0
such that the normalized length of any saddle connection of any translation surface in
M
is bounded below by
b
.

Proof.

Following Proposition
3.1
,
(X,\omega)
contains cylinders, and for any
K>0
, there are only finitely many cylinders in
(X,\omega)
whose conformal modulus is at least
K
. Therefore, the normalized length of a saddle connection is a well-defined, scale-invariant quantity.

We normalize the translation surfaces of
M
to have unit area. Since
M
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{P}\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
, the systole is bounded below by some positive constant
c>0
, and we have
|\int_{\alpha}\omega|\geq c
. The area of any cylinder in
MaxCyl(X,\omega)
is at most
1
, hence
|\int_{\beta}\omega|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{R}}
, where
R
denotes the conformal modulus of any cylinder in
MaxCyl(X,\omega)
.
It follows that for any saddle connection
\alpha
of
(X,\omega)
,

l(\alpha)\geq c\sqrt{R}.

In
[
12
, Theorem 1.3]
, Vorobets proved that for any stratum
\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
, there exist positive constants
\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
and
W_{\mu}
depending only on
\mu
such that every translation surface in
\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
contains a cylinder of area at least
\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
and width at most
W_{\mu}
. Such a cylinder has conformal modulus bounded below by
K=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\mu}}{W_{\mu}}
, so we have
R\geq K
. It follows that for any saddle connection
\alpha
of any translation surface
(X,\omega)
in
M
,

l(\alpha)\geq c\sqrt{K},

a positive constant independent of the choice of
(X,\omega)
in
M
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

One might naturally wonder why we did not simply normalize the lengths using the area of the surface, thereby avoiding the intermediate results concerning cylinders. The difficulty with this approach is that the natural scale-invariant function
\frac{\rm Area}{{\rm Systole}^{2}}
and its variants do not possess the appropriate harmonicity properties, as shown in
[
11
, Section 3]
.

We once again proceed by a dichotomy between flexible and rigid saddle connections, based on their normalized lengths.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We first consider the case of the projectivized stratum of flat tori
\mathbb{P}\Omega\mathcal{M}_{1}(\emptyset)
, which is one-dimensional. Any holomorphic curve contained in
\mathbb{P}\Omega\mathcal{M}_{1}(\emptyset)
must coincide with it, and hence is not compact (since flat tori degenerate to a nodal sphere). In the remainder of the proof, we will therefore assume that
\mu
is nonempty, so that the results of Section
3
apply.

We assume, for the sake of contradiction, that there exists a positive-dimensional complete subvariety
M
in
\mathbb{P}\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
. In the definition of the normalized length, we use the set
MaxCyl(X,\omega)
of the cylinders of largest width among those having the largest conformal modulus. According to Propositions
3.6
and
3.8
, these cylinders remain the ones of largest width among those with the largest conformal modulus as
(X,\omega)
is deformed in
M
. Deformation along a closed path in
M
can, however, permute the elements of
MaxCyl(X,\omega)
.

A saddle connection
\alpha
is called
rigid
if its normalized length
l(\alpha)
remains constant in a neighborhood of
(X,\omega)
in
M
, and
flexible
otherwise. Observe that for any rigid (resp. flexible) saddle connection
\alpha
of
(X,\omega)
, there exists a neighborhood of
(X,\omega)
in
M
where
\alpha
persists as a saddle connection and remains rigid (resp. flexible).
This follows from the fact that the ratio
\frac{\int_{\alpha}\omega}{\int_{\beta}\omega}
is holomorphic in the local coordinates of
M
.

By hypothesis, any translation surface in
M
can be deformed nontrivially within
M
, so every
(X,\omega)
in
M
contains at least one flexible saddle connection. We define the function

L\colon M\to\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}

by assigning to
(X,\omega)
the normalized length
l(\alpha)
, where
\alpha
is one of the shortest flexible saddle connections in
(X,\omega)
.
Since a unit-area translation surface contains only finitely many saddle connections of length at most
K
for any
K>0
, the function
L
is well defined.
We first show that
L
is a continuous function on
M
.

We consider a sequence of surfaces
(X_{n},\omega_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
M
converging to a surface
(X_{\infty},\omega_{\infty})
in
M
.
In each
(X_{n},\omega_{n})
, let
\alpha_{n}
be a flexible saddle connection such that
L(X_{n},\omega_{n})=l(\alpha_{n})
.
Denote by
\beta_{n}
the waist curve of a cylinder in
MaxCyl(X_{n},\omega_{n})
.
Up to passing to a subsequence of
(X_{n},\omega_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, we may assume that

l(\alpha_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}=\frac{|\int_{\alpha_{n}}\omega_{n}|}{|\int_{\beta_{n}}\omega_{n}|}

converges to some limit
\lambda
.
Let
\alpha_{\infty}
be a flexible saddle connection such that
L(X_{\infty},\omega_{\infty})=l(\alpha_{\infty})
.
Since
\alpha_{\infty}
persists as a flexible saddle connection in a sufficiently small neighborhood of
(X_{\infty},\omega_{\infty})
in
M
, we deduce that
\lambda\leq l(\alpha_{\infty})
.
It remains to prove that
\lambda\geq l(\alpha_{\infty})
.

Up to passing to a subsequence again, we may assume that the ratios

\frac{\int_{\alpha_{n}}\omega_{n}}{\int_{\beta_{n}}\omega_{n}}

converge to some limit value
\nu
satisfying
0\leq|\nu|=\lambda
. Lemma
4.1
already establishes that the normalized length of any saddle connection is bounded below by some positive constant. We therefore deduce that
|\nu|>0
.

Since this convergence takes place in an arbitrarily small contractible neighborhood of
(X_{\infty},\omega_{\infty})
, we can mark the zeros of
\omega_{\infty}
and, up to taking a further subsequence, require that all the saddle connections
(\alpha_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
join the same oriented pair of zeros. The cylinders of
MaxCyl(X_{n},\omega_{n})
can be marked within this neighborhood, so we may also assume that all the waist curves
\beta_{n}
belong to the same cylinder. We denote by
\beta
a waist curve of this cylinder in
(X_{\infty},\omega_{\infty})
.

A subsequence of saddle connections
(\alpha_{\phi(n)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
accumulates on an arc
\gamma
satisfying

\frac{\int_{\gamma}\omega_{\infty}}{\int_{\beta}\omega_{\infty}}=\nu,

which is formed by one or several oriented saddle connections of the same oriented slope. For
n
sufficiently large, the relative homology classes
[\alpha_{\phi(n)}]
and
[\gamma]
coincide, so at least one saddle connection forming
\gamma
must be flexible, which we denote by
\delta
. Then this saddle connection
\delta
satisfies

l(\delta)\leq|\nu|=\lambda
,
and hence
\lambda\geq l(\alpha_{\infty})
.
Consequently, we obtain
L(X_{n},\omega_{n})\rightarrow L(X_{\infty},\omega_{\infty})
as
n\to\infty
, thereby establishing the continuity of
L
.

Since
L
is continuous on
M
, which is compact, it attains a global minimum realized by some flexible saddle connection
\alpha
on a translation surface
(X,\omega)
in
M
. Because the ratio
\frac{\int_{\alpha}\omega}{\int_{\beta}\omega}
is holomorphic in the local coordinates of
M
, its modulus can decrease again along certain local deformation paths, leading to a contradiction. Therefore, no flexible saddle connection can exist on any translation surface in
M
, which implies that no nontrivial deformation exists for any translation surface in
M
. In conclusion, we deduce that there is no positive-dimensional complete subvariety in
\mathbb{P}\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mu)
.
∎

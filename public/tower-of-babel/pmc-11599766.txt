Red blood cell count and cystatin C as the specific biomarkers for diabetic retinopathy from diabetes mellitus: a case–control study
Authors: Zheng Li, Jie Li, Jie Zhong, Chao Qu, Mingyan Du, Huaping Tian, Haojue Xu, Fang Hao, Lulin Huang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractDiabetic retinopathy (DR) is a common microvascular complication of diabetes mellitus (DM). However, the blood physiological indicators for this eye disease are less reported. Here, we systemically investigated healthy controls, DR patients and DM patients from 2014 to 2022 in Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital (Chengdu, China). We found the leukocyte count, urea, and direct bilirubin in the DR patients were significantly increased (P < 0.001) compared with the controls. The ratios of aspartate aminotransferase/alanine transaminase, total bilirubin, indirect bilirubin, and red cell distribution width were lower in the DR patients than in the controls (P < 0.001). Notably, Red blood cell count (mean [SD] = 4.54 [0.67] 10E12/L, OR = 0.75) was significantly decreased (P < 0.001), and cystatin C level (mean [SD] = 1.42 [1.13] mg/L, OR = 1.90) was significantly increased in the DR patients compared with the controls (P < 0.001). However, these two indicators were not significantly different between the DM patients and the controls (P = 0.87 and P = 0.14, respectively). Our results showed that red blood cell count and cystatin C are specific biomarkers for DR patients.Keywords: Diabetic retinopathy, Diabetes mellitus, Red blood cell count, Cystatin C, BiomarkersSubject terms: Predictive markers, Eye diseases

Introduction

Diabetic retinopathy (DR) is a common microvascular complication of diabetes mellitus (DM), and the leading cause of preventable blindness in working-aged people1. DR mostly occurs in patients with DM. According to the estimated data from the International Diabetes Federation, 463 million global population with DM in 2019 and this number will be increased to 700 million by 20452. Statistically, nearly 80% of DM patients for over 10 years may suffer from DR3. Besides, during the late stages of DR, including proliferative DR, abnormal growth of new retinal blood vessels and diabetic macular oedema are the severe symptoms in DR patients4. The overall prevalence is 34.6% (95% CI 34.5–34.8) for any DR, 6.96% (95% CI 6.87–7.04) for proliferative DR, 6.81% (95% CI 6.74–6.89) for diabetic macular edema, and 10.2% (95% CI 10.1–10.3) for vision-threatening diabetic retinopathy5.

The pathophysiology of DR is very complex and involves retinal microvascular diseases such as early perivascular cell and endothelial cell apoptosis6, blood-retina barrier destruction7, and late retinal neovascularization8. It also includes retinal neurodegenerative diseases and glial cell dysfunction, such as gradual neuronal apoptosis9, abnormal visual electrophysiological function10, activation of glial cells in the retina, and glial reactions8,11. These special traits often lead to misdiagnosis in the early clinical diagnosis process and ultimately produce the impairment of visual function in DM patients8. In recent years, deep learning systems based on retinal images from multiethnic DM patients have been used to identify and diagnose late DR12,13. At present, the therapeutic methods available for DR remain limited. Anti-vascular endothelial growth factor a therapy is regarded as a good therapeutic method for DR patients, but it is effective only in the late stages of DR and requires regular intravitreous injections8. Besides, the anti-inflammatory therapeutics method positive impact on DR patients that have highlighted the importance for central involvement of the innate immune system14.

The common risk factors for DR include hyperglycemia, hypertension, diabetes duration, and hemoglobin A1c level et al5,8. Besides, previous studies have shown that albuminuria is a strong risk factor for severe DR patients15. Moreover, it has been reported that urea nitrogen and urinary albumin are risk factors for DR16,17. C-reactive protein level might be used as a biomarker to determine the severity of DR18. However, detailed and comprehensive reports on blood indicators in clinical practice for DR specifically which could be highly important for DR auxiliary diagnosis remain scarce.

In this study, we systemically compared the clinical physiological indicators of healthy people, DR patients and DM patients. We found that most of the physiological indicators were consistent in terms of differences between DR and DM patients and healthy population. However, red blood cell count and cystatin C level were significantly associated only with DR patients, but not with the DM patients, indicating that these indicators are potential biomarkers for DR.

Methods

Participants

We conducted this retrospective study in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and received institutional approval from the review committees of Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital. DR patients were approached for participation during 2014–2022. DR diagnosis was based on clinical examination by ophthalmologists based on color fundus photos, such as fundus autofluorescence, optical coherence tomography and fluorescein angiography19. In total, 935 DR patients were enrolled in the study. The criteria for selecting DR patients are that there is no history of surgery and disease, no other eye disease. We enrolled 11,719 DM patients and 697,945 healthy controls, most of them were from a published sample set20. The criteria for selecting DM patients are that there is no history of surgery and disease, no other diabetic complications, no proliferative versus non-proliferative retinopathies, and they are undergoing treatment and glycemic control. Since the sample population of DR patients was smaller than the DM patients, we narrowed the population number of DM patients and controls using random sampling to match the subjects being compared. After matching for gender and age, we included 935 DR patients, 2,081 DM patients, and 4,322 healthy controls for comparison and analysis. Statistical analysis

The clinical physiological indicators with string variables were converted to integer variables for data analysis. The categorized variables were digitally coded for further calculations20. A linear regression model (lm) was used to compare the physiological indicators among the controls, DR patients, and DM patients adjusted for sex and age in the R package20–22. The odds ratios (OR) were calculated by using generalized linear models (glm) adjusted for sex and age in the R package20,23.

Results

We collected the data of 935 DR patients (mean age (range) 59.7 (29–91); 426 females [45.6%] and 509 males [54.4%]), There were 2,081 DM patients (mean age (range) 55.2 (27–89); 369 females [17.7%] and 1,712 males [82.3%]) and 4,322 controls (mean age (range) 52.3 (26–92); 1703 females [39.3%] and 2,619 males [60.6%]) (Table 1).

To evaluate our collected data of the blood physiological indicators in DR and DM patients, we performed the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis and calculated the area under the curve (AUC) and optimal thresholds. The results show that our data can be used for further analysis (Figure S1). We compared each blood physiological indicators in healthy control, DR patients and DM patients. The results of the comparative analysis showed that the blood glucose levels in the DR patients (mean [SD] = 7.87 [3.39] mmol/L, OR = 13.61) and DM patients (mean [SD] = 8.69 [3.27] mmol/L, OR = 27.73) were higher than that of the controls (P < 0.001) (Table 2). The findings also indicated that compare with the controls, leukocyte count (mean [SD] = 6.65 [1.55] 10E9/L, OR = 1.68), urea (mean [SD] = 8.84 [6.21] mmol/L, OR = 38.28), and the direct bilirubin (mean [SD] = 4.13 [1.91] umol/L, OR = 1.37) in the DR patients were significantly increased (P < 0.001) (Table 2). The above physiological indicators. except for direct bilirubin, were also significantly increased in DR patients compared with the DM patients (P < 0.001) (Table 2). In addition, the ratios of aspartate aminotransferase/alanine transaminase (mean [SD] = 1.14 [0.43], OR = 0.83), total bilirubin (mean [SD] = 13.38 [5.62] umol/L, OR = 0.11), indirect bilirubin (mean [SD] = 9.25 [4.17] umol/L, OR = 0.08), and red cell distribution width (mean [SD] = 44.39 [6.65] fl, OR = 0.25) were lower in the DR patients than in the controls (P < 0.001) (Table 2). Compared with the DM patients, the above indicators, except for indirect bilirubin, were also significantly decreased in the DR patients (P < 0.001) (Table 2). Altogether, these results showed that the trend change in most of the physiological indicators in DR and DM patients were similar, suggesting the biomarkers for both DR and DM patients (Fig. 1).

Discussion

DR is one of the prevailing chronic complications of DM caused by long-term high blood glucose levels that may lead to visual loss. Therefore, early diagnosis of DR is extremely important for DM patients. Currently, the diagnosis and evaluation of DR depend on fundus examination by retina photographs; no exact physiological indicators for predicting the presence of DR have been identified24,25. Our study revealed that red blood cell count and cystatin C are specific biomarkers for DR diagnosis among DM patients. This discovery should contribute to the collaborative diagnosis of DR patients.

In this case–control study, we systemically analyzed the physiological indicators of DR patients and compared them with those of DM patients. We found that blood glucose levels were the most significant indicator in both DR and DM patients compared with the controls. Besides, it has been reported that elevated leukocyte count is associated with chronic complications for DM26. Our study confirmed that leukocyte count was increased in the DR patients compared with the controls or DM patients. As a biomarker for chronic inflammation, leukocyte count can be considered an appropriate clinical indicator for early diagnosis of DM complications26. Notably, leukocyte count is also increased in other eye diseases, like age-related macular degeneration (AMD)27,28. According to our results, total bilirubin was decreased in the DR patients when compared with the controls or DM patients. A previous study has shown that bilirubin, which is an effective antioxidant property, can act as a protective agent against diabetes and cardiovascular diseases29. Bilirubin concentration and the risk of diabetic complications have a negative nonlinear relationship30.

Interestingly, we identified that red blood cell count was significantly decreased in the DR patients, but it was not significantly changed in the DM patients. It may be a candidate marker indicator for diagnosing the DR patients from DM patients. The decreased red blood cell count may impair the ability to transport oxygen and carbon dioxide in the red blood cells of DR patients, which may lead to central retinal ischemia and drive pathological angiogenesis. Besides, it is reported that DR is one of the microvascular complication diseases and red blood cell deformability might be contribute to the development of microvascular complication31. In addition, red blood cells play a role in inflammation, immune responses, and oxidative stress32. A previous study has shown that decreased red blood cell count is associated with microvascular complications in Chinese patients with T2DM33. Moreover, in nonalcoholic fatty liver patients, elevated red blood cell count is associated with a high risk for this disease32.

Cystatin C was another interesting physiological indicator in the DR patients. Compared with the controls, the cystatin C level was unchanged in the DM patients, but it was increased in the DR patients. This candidate indicator may contribute to diagnosing DR patients. A meta-analysis has shown that cystatin C level in the DR patients is increased compared with patients with diabetes without retinopathy34, which is consistent with our results. However, we found that cystatin C in the DM patients was not significantly changed compared with the healthy controls. Cystatin C is an important extracellular inhibitor of cysteine and may contribute to human vascular pathophysiology35. Cystatin C is also a marker of inflammation that causes a blockage of retinal capillaries and subsequent local ischemia36,37. Previous studies have shown that cystatin C is used as a biomarker in predicting diabetic nephropathy among DM patients34,38.

Limitations

This case–control study has some limitations. Our research is a single-center study, with the participants coming only from Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital, China. Multi-center repeated research can be carried out in the future. In addition, the detailed mechanisms that cause changes to these indicators still need to be further studied.

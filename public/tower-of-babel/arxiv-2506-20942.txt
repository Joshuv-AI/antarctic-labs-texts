Period Relations for Rankin-Selberg convolutions for GL ⁡ ( n ) × GL ⁡ ( n ) {\mathrm{GL}}(n)\times{\mathrm{GL}}(n)
Yubo Jin, Jian-Shu Li, Dongwen Liu, Binyong Sun

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the special values of Rankin-Selberg L-functions as a continuation of [ LLS24 ] . Utilizing the modular symbol approach, we prove the rationality and period relations for critical values of Rankin-Selberg L-functions for GL ⁡ ( n ) × GL ⁡ ( n ) {\mathrm{GL}}(n)\times{\mathrm{GL}}(n) over any number field that contains a CM field.

Primary 11F67; Secondary 11F70, 11F75, 22E45

1.
Introduction

One of the central problems in number theory is the study of special values of L-functions. The celebrated conjecture of Deligne
[
Del79
]
asserts the rationality of critical values of L-functions attached to pure motives. In
[
Bla97
]
, Blasius established the relations between Deligne’s periods of certain pure motives and their Artin twists. He further proposed the conjecture on period relations between different critical values of L-functions. The main goal of this paper is to study the rationality and period relations for Rankin-Selberg L-functions. In the framework of the Langlands program, this is compatible with conjectures of Deligne and Blasius.

The cases of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{n-1}
and
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
are of fundamental importance in the Rankin-Selberg theory and many problems for general Rankin-Selberg convolutions are reduced to these two cases. The period relations for the
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{n-1}
case is studied in
[
LLS24
]
and we refer the reader to
[
LLS24
, Section 1.2]
for relevant works for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{n-1}
Rankin-Selberg convolutions. Much less is known for the
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
case (but see
[
Hi94
,
Sh76
,
Sh78
]
for the case that
n=2
), and it is the aim of this paper to prove the period relations for the
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
case as a continuation of
[
LLS24
]
.

We introduce various notions in order to state our main theorem.

Let
\mathrm{k}
be a number field that contains a CM field. Denote by
\mathrm{k}_{v}
the completion of
\mathrm{k}
at a place
v
with the normalized absolute value
|\cdot|_{v}
. Write

\mathrm{k}_{\infty}:=\mathrm{k}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\mathbb{R}=\prod_{v|\infty}\mathrm{k}_{v}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{k}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\mathbb{C}=\prod_{\iota\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}}}\mathbb{C},

where
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}}
is the set of field embeddings
\iota:\mathrm{k}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
. For each
\iota\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}}
, we write
\overline{\iota}
for its complex conjugation. Write
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}}\times\mathrm{k}_{\infty}
for the adele ring of
\mathrm{k}
where
\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}}
indicates the ring of finite adeles, and set
|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}}:=\prod_{v}|\cdot|_{v}
.

Recall that a weight

\mu:=(\mu^{\iota})_{\iota\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}}}:=(\mu_{1}^{\iota},\mu_{2}^{\iota},\dots,\mu_{n}^{\iota})_{\iota\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}}}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{n})^{{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}}}

is called dominant if

\mu_{1}^{\iota}\geq\mu_{2}^{\iota}\geq\dots\geq\mu_{n}^{\iota},\qquad\text{for all }\iota\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}}.

For such a dominant weight we denote by
F_{\mu}
the (unique up to isomorphism) irreducible holomorphic finite-dimensional representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\mathbb{C})
of highest weight
\mu
. Let
\Pi=\Sigma\boxtimes\Sigma^{\prime}
be an irreducible smooth automorphic representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})
that is regular algebraic in the sense of
[
Clo90
]
. That is, there exist dominant weights
\mu
and
\nu
such that
F_{\mu}\boxtimes F_{\nu}
has the same infinitesimal character as that of
\Pi_{\infty}:=\widehat{\otimes}_{v|\infty}\Pi_{v}
, the infinite part of
\Pi
. We call
F_{\mu}\boxtimes F_{\nu}
the coefficient system of
\Pi
.

We fix a CM type of
\mathrm{k}
. That is, for every archimedean place
v
, we fix a continuous field embedding
\iota_{v}:\mathrm{k}_{v}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
. Denote
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}_{v}}=\{\iota_{v},\overline{\iota}_{v}\}
, naturally viewed as a subset of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}}
, where
\overline{\iota}_{v}
is the composition of
\iota_{v}
with the complex conjugation.

Let

\mu:=(\mu_{1}^{\iota},\mu_{2}^{\iota},\dots,\mu_{n}^{\iota})_{\iota\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}}}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{n})^{{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}}},\qquad\nu:=(\nu_{1}^{\iota},\nu_{2}^{\iota},\dots,\nu_{n}^{\iota})_{\iota\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}}}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{n})^{{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}}}

be two dominant weights. Let
\chi:\mathrm{k}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be an algebraic Hecke character and write

\mathrm{d}\chi:=\mathrm{d}\chi_{\infty}:=\{\chi_{\iota}\}_{\iota\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}}}

for the complexified differentiation of
\chi_{\infty}
, the infinite part of
\chi
. We say that
\chi
is
(\mu,\nu)
-balanced if certain branching space in (
3.9
) is nonzero, or equivalently for every archimedean place

, either

(1.1)

\begin{cases}-\min_{1\leq i\leq n-1}\{\mu_{i}^{\iota_{v}}+\nu_{n-i}^{\iota_{v}}\}\leq\chi_{\iota_{v}}\leq-\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\{\mu_{i}^{\iota_{v}}+\nu_{n+1-i}^{\iota_{v}}\},\\

or

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\min_{1\leq i\leq n-1}\{\mu_{i}^{\overline{\iota}_{v}}+\nu_{n-i}^{\overline{\iota}_{v}}\}\leq\chi_{\overline{\iota}_{v}}\leq-\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\{\mu_{i}^{\overline{\iota}_{v}}+\nu_{n+1-i}^{\overline{\iota}_{v}}\},\\

) and (

\varepsilon_{\Pi,\chi,v}=1
(resp.

) if (

) (resp. (

(\mu,\nu)
-balanced characters by

.

We say that the algebraic Hecke character

is
(\mu,\nu)
-critical if
s=0
is not a pole of

-critical characters by

If we further denote by

.

is tamely isobaric

;

2.
Representations and cohomology spaces

We fix the following general notations throughout the paper. For a positive integer
n
,
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
stands for the general linear group over
\mathrm{k}
and denote by
1_{n}
the identity matrix of size
n
. Let
{\mathrm{B}}_{n}={\mathrm{T}}_{n}{\mathrm{N}}_{n}
be the Borel subgroup of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
consisting of upper triangular matrices with
{\mathrm{T}}_{n}
the diagonal torus and
{\mathrm{N}}_{n}
the unipotent radical. Likewise, let
\overline{{\mathrm{B}}}_{n}={\mathrm{T}}_{n}\overline{{\mathrm{N}}}_{n}
be the opposite Borel subgroup of lower triangular matrices with unipotent radical
\overline{{\mathrm{N}}}_{n}
. The center of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
is denoted by
{\mathrm{Z}}_{n}
. Let
{\mathrm{P}}_{n}
be the standard parabolic subgroup of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
of type
(n-1,1)
. For an algebraic group
G
over
\mathrm{k}
and a
\mathrm{k}
-algebra
R
, we denote by
G(R)
its
R
-points. Then
{\mathrm{P}}_{n}(R)
consists of all matrices in
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(R)
whose last row equals
[0\ \cdots\ 0\ a]
for some
a\in R^{\times}
.

2.1.
Tamely isobaric representations

We introduce the notion of tamely isobaric representations extending the one defined in
[
LLS24
]
. Let
\Sigma
be an irreducible smooth automorphic representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})
that is an induced representation of the form

(2.1)

\Sigma=\mathrm{Ind}^{{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})}_{{\mathrm{P}}(\mathbb{A})}\left(\Sigma_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}\dots\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}\Sigma_{r}\right)\qquad(\text{normalized smooth induction}),

where

•

{\mathrm{P}}:={\mathrm{P}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})}
is the standard parabolic subgroup of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
associated to a fixed partition
n_{1}+\dots+n_{r}=n
;

•

\Sigma_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq r
) are irreducible cuspidal smooth automorphic representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{A})
whose exponents satisfy that

\mathrm{ex}(\Sigma_{1})\geq\mathrm{ex}(\Sigma_{2})\geq\dots\geq\mathrm{ex}(\Sigma_{r}).

Here the exponent
\mathrm{ex}(\Sigma_{i})
is the unique real number such that
\Sigma_{i}\otimes|\det|_{\mathbb{A}}^{-\mathrm{ex}(\Sigma_{i})}
is unitarizable, and
\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}
indicates the completed inductive tensor product (see
[
Tr67
, Definition 43.5]
).

Write
{\mathrm{P}}={\mathrm{M}}{\mathrm{N}}
for the Levi decomposition of
{\mathrm{P}}
and denote by
\delta_{{\mathrm{P}}}
the square root of the modular character of
{\mathrm{P}}(\mathbb{A})
. For each archimedean place
v
, write
K_{n,v}:={\mathrm{U}}(n)
, to be viewed as a maximal compact subgroup of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{v})
, and set
K_{n,\infty}:=\prod_{v|\infty}K_{n,v}
. Write
K_{n}:=K_{n,\infty}{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\mathrm{k}})
, where
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathrm{k}}
is the ring of integers of
\mathrm{k}
and
\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\mathrm{k}}
its profinite completion.

The space of induced representation (
2.1
) consists of smooth functions

\phi:{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})\to\Sigma_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{\rm i}\cdots\widehat{\otimes}_{\rm i}\Sigma_{r}

such that

\phi(mug)=\delta_{{\mathrm{P}}}(m)m.(\phi(g))\qquad\text{for all }m\in{\mathrm{M}}(\mathbb{A}),u\in{\mathrm{N}}(\mathbb{A}),g\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{A}).

We identify
\phi
with a complex valued function
\widetilde{\phi}
by setting

\widetilde{\phi}:{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})\to\mathbb{C},\qquad\widetilde{\phi}(g):=\phi(g)(1_{n}).

For a tuple of complex numbers
\underline{s}:=(s_{1},\dots,s_{r})\in\mathbb{C}^{r}
, we further set

\widetilde{\phi}_{\underline{s}}(g):=\prod_{i=1}^{r}|\det(m_{i})|_{\mathbb{A}}^{s_{i}}\cdot\widetilde{\phi}(g)

for
g=muk\in{\mathrm{M}}(\mathbb{A}){\mathrm{N}}(\mathbb{A})K_{n}
with
m=\mathrm{diag}[m_{1},\dots,m_{r}]\in{\mathrm{M}}(\mathbb{A})
,
u\in{\mathrm{N}}(\mathbb{A})
and
k\in K_{n}
. For
\phi\in\Sigma
and
\underline{s}
as above, define the Eisenstein series associated to
\Sigma
to be

{\mathrm{E}}(g;\phi_{\underline{s}})=\sum_{\gamma\in{\mathrm{P}}(\mathrm{k})\backslash{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathrm{k})}\widetilde{\phi}_{\underline{s}}(\gamma g),\qquad g\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{A}).

It converges absolutely when
\mathrm{Re}(s_{i}-s_{j})\gg 0
for every
1\leq i<j\leq r
and admits a meromorphic continuation to
\mathbb{C}^{r}
(see
[
Lap08
]
).

Definition 3
.

An irreducible smooth automorphic representation
\Sigma
of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})
is called tamely isobaric if it is isomorphic to an induced representation of the form (
2.1
) such that the Eisenstein series
{\mathrm{E}}(g;\phi_{\underline{s}})
is holomorphic at
\underline{s}=(0,\dots,0)
for all
\phi\in\Sigma
.

For example, the condition in the above definition is satisfied when
\mathrm{ex}(\Sigma_{1})=\dots=\mathrm{ex}(\Sigma_{r})
, which is the condition for tamely isobaric representations considered in
[
LLS24
]
. The cuspidal representations are obviously tamely isobaric. From now on, we realize tamely isobaric representations as a subrepresentation of the space
{\mathcal{A}}({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathrm{k})\backslash{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{A}))
of smooth automorphic forms via Eisenstein series as above. By
[
Sh10
, Chapter 7]
,
\Sigma
is globally generic in the sense that the global Whittaker integral is nonzero.

2.2.
Cohomological representations

In the rest of the paper, we let
\Pi=\Sigma\boxtimes\Sigma^{\prime}
be an irreducible smooth automorphic representation of

and

As in the Introduction, we assume that

2.3.
Degenerate principal series representations

Let
\chi:\mathrm{k}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a Hecke character. Recall that
\omega_{\Pi}
is the product of the central characters of
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
. Set
\eta:=\omega_{\Pi}\chi^{n}
. We consider the degenerate principal series representation

(2.5)

\displaystyle I_{\eta,\chi}:=

\displaystyle(\chi\circ\det)\otimes\mathrm{Ind}^{{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})}_{{\mathrm{P}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})}\left(|\det|_{\mathbb{A}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\otimes|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\eta^{-1}\right),

where

I_{\eta,\chi}=\widehat{\otimes}_{v}^{\prime}I_{\eta_{v},\chi_{v}}=I_{\eta_{\mathrm{f}},\chi_{\mathrm{f}}}\otimes I_{\eta_{\infty},\chi_{\infty}},\quad I_{\eta_{\mathrm{f}},\chi_{\mathrm{f}}}:=\otimes_{v\nmid\infty}^{\prime}I_{\eta_{v},\chi_{v}},\quad I_{\eta_{\infty},\chi_{\infty}}:=\widehat{\otimes}_{v|\infty}I_{\eta_{v},\chi_{v}}.

\displaystyle I_{\eta_{v},\chi_{v}}:=

\displaystyle(\chi_{v}\circ\det)\otimes\mathrm{Ind}^{{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{v})}_{{\mathrm{P}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{v})}\left(|\det|_{v}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\otimes|\cdot|_{v}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\eta_{v}^{-1}\right).

Write

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\chi:=\mathrm{d}\chi_{\infty}

\displaystyle:=\{\chi_{\iota}\}_{\iota\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}}}\in\mathrm{k}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{C}^{{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\eta:=\mathrm{d}\eta_{\infty}

\displaystyle:=\{\eta_{\iota}\}_{\iota\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}}}\in\mathrm{k}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{C}^{{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}}}

for the complexified differentiations of
\chi_{\infty}
and
\eta_{\infty}
respectively. We assume
\chi
is algebraic in the sense that
\mathrm{d}\chi\in\mathbb{Z}^{{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}}}
. Then
\eta
is also algebraic with

\eta_{\iota}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\mu_{i}^{\iota}+\nu_{i}^{\iota})+n\chi_{\iota},\qquad\text{for all }\iota\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}}.

Following
[
JLS25b
]
, we assume that

(2.6)

\min_{\iota\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}_{v}}}\{\eta_{\iota}\}\leq 0\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\max_{\iota\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{k}_{v}}}\{\eta_{\iota}\}\geq n,

for every archimedean place
v
, in which case
I_{\eta_{\infty},\chi_{\infty}}
is irreducible and regular algebraic.

Let
F_{\eta,\chi}
be the (unique up to isomorphism) irreducible holomorphic finite dimensional representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\mathbb{C})
that has the same infinitesimal character as that of
I_{\eta_{\infty},\chi_{\infty}}
. Set

c_{n}:=\frac{[\mathrm{k}:{\mathbb{Q}}]}{2}\cdot(n-1),

and consider the continuous cohomology spaces

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}(I_{\eta,\chi})

\displaystyle:={\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{ct}}^{c_{n}}\left({\mathrm{Z}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{\infty})\backslash{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{\infty});F_{\eta,\chi}^{\vee}\otimes I_{\eta,\chi}\right),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}(I_{\eta_{\infty},\chi_{\infty}})

\displaystyle:={\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{ct}}^{c_{n}}\left({\mathrm{Z}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{\infty})\backslash{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{\infty});F_{\eta,\chi}^{\vee}\otimes I_{\eta_{\infty},\chi_{\infty}}\right).

There is a canonical isomorphism

(2.7)

\iota_{\mathrm{can}}:{\mathcal{H}}(I_{\eta_{\infty},\chi_{\infty}})\otimes I_{\eta_{\mathrm{f}},\chi_{\mathrm{f}}}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathcal{H}}(I_{\eta,\chi}).

As a consequence of the Künneth formula and the Delorme’s lemma
[
BW00
, Theorem III.3.3]
(see also
[
JLS25a
, Section 1.2]
), we have that
\dim{\mathcal{H}}(I_{\eta_{\infty},\chi_{\infty}})=1
.

For every
s\in\mathbb{C}
, denote
\chi_{s}:=\chi|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}}^{s}
. For
\varphi\in I_{\eta,\chi}
, set

\varphi_{s}(g):=|\det(p)|_{\mathbb{A}}^{s}\cdot|p_{n,n}|_{\mathbb{A}}^{-ns}\varphi(g),

where
g=pk\in{\mathrm{P}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})K_{n}
with
p=[p_{i,j}]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\in{\mathrm{P}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})
and
k\in K_{n}
. We form the Eisenstein series

(2.8)

{\mathrm{E}}(g;\varphi_{s})=\sum_{\gamma\in{\mathrm{P}}_{n}(\mathrm{k})\backslash{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathrm{k})}\varphi_{s}(\gamma g).

The series converges absolutely for
\mathrm{Re}(s)
sufficiently large and has a meromorphic continuation to the whole complex plane in
s
. By
[
JLS25b
, Proposition 2.1]
, the Eisenstein series
{\mathrm{E}}(g;\varphi_{s})
is holomorphic at
s=0
for every
\varphi\in I_{\eta}
and we obtain a continuous linear embedding

I_{\eta,\chi}\to{\mathcal{A}}({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathrm{k})\backslash{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})),\qquad\varphi\mapsto{\mathrm{E}}(g;\varphi_{s})|_{s=0}.

On the cohomological level, this induces an Eisenstein map

(2.9)

\mathrm{Eis}_{\eta,\chi}:{\mathcal{H}}(I_{\eta,\chi})\to{\mathrm{H}}^{c_{n}}({\mathcal{X}}_{n},F_{\eta,\chi}^{\vee}).

See
[
JLS25b
]
for more details.

3.
Modular symbols for Rankin-Selberg convolutions

In this section, we define the modular symbols to provide a cohomological interpretation of the Rankin-Selberg integrals for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
.

3.1.
Rankin-Selberg integrals

Let
\psi_{\mathbb{R}}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be the unitary character defined by
x\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}x}
. Fix a nontrivial additive character of
\mathrm{k}\backslash\mathbb{A}
as the composition

(3.1)

\psi:\mathrm{k}\backslash\mathbb{A}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{k}/{\mathbb{Q}}}}{\mathbb{Q}}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\to{\mathbb{Q}}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}/\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{\psi_{\mathbb{R}}}\mathbb{C}^{\times},

where
\mathbb{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
is the adele ring of
{\mathbb{Q}}
;
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{k}/{\mathbb{Q}}}
is the trace map; and
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
is the profinite completion of
\mathbb{Z}
. This further induces an automorphic character

(3.2)

\displaystyle\psi_{n}:{\mathrm{N}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})\times{\mathrm{N}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{C}^{\times},

\displaystyle\left([x_{i,j}]_{1\leq i,j\leq n},[x^{\prime}_{i,j}]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto\psi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(x_{i,i+1}-x_{i,i+1}^{\prime})\right).

When no confusion occurs, we identify a character of a group as the complex vector space
\mathbb{C}
equipped with the corresponding action of the group.

On
\mathbb{A}
we choose the self-dual measure with respect to
\psi
so that
\mathrm{k}\backslash\mathbb{A}
has total volume
1
. For each place
v
, we fix a Haar measure
\mathrm{d}x_{v}
on
\mathrm{k}_{v}
such that

•

{\mathcal{O}}_{v}
has volume
1
if
v
is non-archimedean, where
{\mathcal{O}}_{v}
denotes the ring of integers of
\mathrm{k}_{v}
; and

•

the product of all the measures
\{\mathrm{d}x_{v}\}_{v}
equals the self-dual Haar measure on
\mathbb{A}
.

The Haar measure on
{\mathrm{N}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})
(resp.
{\mathrm{N}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{v})
) is fixed to be the product of the Haar measures on
\mathbb{A}
(resp.
\mathrm{k}_{v}
).

We define the global Whittaker functional

(3.3)

\lambda_{\Pi}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathrm{N}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})\times{\mathrm{N}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})}(\Pi,\psi_{n}),

for
\Pi
by integrals

f\mapsto\int_{{\mathrm{N}}_{n}(\mathrm{k})\backslash{\mathrm{N}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})\times{\mathrm{N}}_{n}(\mathrm{k})\backslash{\mathrm{N}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})}f(u)\overline{\psi_{n}}(u)\mathrm{d}u.

The uniqueness of Whittaker models provides a factorization

\displaystyle\lambda_{\Pi}

\displaystyle=\otimes_{v}^{\prime}\lambda_{\Pi,v}=\lambda_{\Pi,\mathrm{f}}\otimes\lambda_{\Pi,\infty},

\displaystyle\lambda_{\Pi,\mathrm{f}}:=\otimes_{v\nmid\infty}^{\prime}\lambda_{\Pi,v},\ \lambda_{\Pi,\infty}:=\otimes_{v|\infty}\lambda_{\Pi,v},

where

\displaystyle\lambda_{\Pi,v}

\displaystyle\in\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathrm{N}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{v})\times{\mathrm{N}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{v})}(\Pi_{v},\psi_{n,v}).

For each place
v
, we choose the multiplicative measure

\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{v}}{|x|_{v}}\cdot\zeta_{v}(1)

on
{\mathrm{Z}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{v})=\mathrm{k}_{v}^{\times}
, where

\zeta_{v}(1):=\begin{cases}(1-q_{v}^{-1})^{-1},&\text{if }v\text{ is non-archimedean},\\
\pi^{-1},&\text{if }v\text{ is (complex) archimedean},\end{cases}

and
q_{v}
is the cardinality of the residue field for
\mathrm{k}_{v}
when
v
is non-archimedean. The measure of
{\mathrm{Z}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})=\mathbb{A}^{\times}
is fixed to be the product of measures of
\mathrm{k}_{v}^{\times}
. Let
\mathfrak{M}_{n,v}
be the one-dimensional space of invariant measures on
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{v})
. When
v
is non-archimedean, it has a distinguished element with respect to which
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{v})
has total volume
1
. We denote by
\mathfrak{M}_{n}
the one-dimensional space of invariant measures on
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})
. Write

\mathfrak{M}_{n}=\otimes_{v}^{\prime}\mathfrak{M}_{n,v}=\mathfrak{M}_{n,\infty}\otimes\mathfrak{M}_{n,\mathrm{f}},\qquad\mathfrak{M}_{n,\mathrm{f}}:=\otimes_{v\nmid\infty}^{\prime}\mathfrak{M}_{n,v},\ \mathfrak{M}_{n,\infty}:=\otimes_{v|\infty}\mathfrak{M}_{n,v},

where the restricted tensor product is defined with respect to those distinguished elements of
\mathfrak{M}_{n,v}
. For each
\mathrm{d}g\in\mathfrak{M}_{n}
or
\mathfrak{M}_{n,v}
, we write
\overline{\mathrm{d}}g
for various quotient measures induced by
\mathrm{d}g
whose meaning will be clear from the integral.

For
\phi\in\Pi
,
\varphi\in I_{\eta,\chi}
and
\mathrm{d}g\in\mathfrak{M}_{n}
, the global Rankin-Selberg integral is defined by

(3.4)

{\mathrm{Z}}(s,\phi,\varphi;\chi,\mathrm{d}g):=\int_{{\mathrm{Z}}_{n}(\mathbb{A}){\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathrm{k})\backslash{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})}\phi(g){\mathrm{E}}(g;\varphi_{s})\overline{\mathrm{d}}g.

It converges absolutely when
\mathrm{Re}(s)
is sufficiently large and has a meromorphic continuation to the whole complex plane in
s
. When
\mathrm{Re}(s)
is sufficiently large, (
3.4
) unfolds as

{\mathrm{Z}}(s,\phi,\varphi;\chi,\mathrm{d}g)=\int_{{\mathrm{Z}}_{n}(\mathbb{A}){\mathrm{N}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})\backslash{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{A})}\lambda_{\Pi}(g.\phi)\varphi_{s}(g)\overline{\mathrm{d}}g.

Therefore, for decomposable data, the global Rankin-Selberg integral admits an Euler product factorization into the local Rankin-Selberg integrals

(3.5)

{\mathrm{Z}}_{v}(s,\phi_{v},\varphi_{v};\chi_{v},\mathrm{d}g):=\int_{{\mathrm{Z}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{v}){\mathrm{N}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{v})\backslash{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{v})}\lambda_{\Pi,v}(g.\phi_{v})\varphi_{s,v}(g)\overline{\mathrm{d}}g

for
\phi_{v}\in\Pi_{v}
,
\varphi_{v}\in I_{\eta_{v},\chi_{v}}
,
\mathrm{d}g\in\mathfrak{M}_{n,v}
. We further define the normalized local Rankin-Selberg integrals

(3.6)

{\mathrm{Z}}^{\circ}_{v}(s,\phi_{v},\varphi_{v};\chi_{v},\mathrm{d}g):=\frac{{\mathrm{L}}(s,\omega_{\Pi_{v}}\chi_{v}^{n})}{{\mathrm{L}}(s,\Pi_{v}\times\chi_{v})}\cdot{\mathrm{Z}}_{v}(s,\phi_{v},\varphi_{v};\chi_{v},\mathrm{d}g).

By
[
JS81
]
and
[
DX17
]
(see also
[
JLS25a
, Proposition 3.2]
), the normalized local Rankin-Selberg integral is holomorphic at
s=0
and yields a nonzero linear functional

(3.7)

{\mathrm{Z}}^{\circ}_{\Pi_{v},\chi_{v}}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{v})}\left(\Pi_{v}\otimes\chi_{v}\otimes\mathfrak{M}_{n,v},\mathbb{C}\right).

Denote

{\mathrm{Z}}^{\circ}_{\Pi_{\infty},\chi_{\infty}}:=\bigotimes_{v|\infty}{\mathrm{Z}}^{\circ}_{\Pi_{v},\chi_{v}},\qquad{\mathrm{Z}}^{\circ}_{\Pi_{\mathrm{f}},\chi_{\mathrm{f}}}:=\bigotimes_{v\nmid\infty}{\mathrm{Z}}^{\circ}_{\Pi_{v},\chi_{v}}.

3.2.
Definition of modular symbols

For each archimedean place
v
, let
\mathfrak{O}_{n,v}
be the one-dimensional space of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{v})
-invariant sections of the orientation line bundle of
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}\backslash{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{v})/K_{n,v}
with complex coefficients. Set

\mathfrak{O}_{n,\infty}=\bigotimes_{v|\infty}\mathfrak{O}_{n,v},\qquad\mathfrak{M}^{\natural}_{n}:=\mathfrak{M}_{n,\mathrm{f}}\otimes\mathfrak{O}_{n,\infty}.

Write for short

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}(\Pi,\chi)_{\mathrm{glob}}

\displaystyle:={\mathcal{H}}(\Pi)\otimes{\mathcal{H}}(I_{\eta,\chi})\otimes\mathfrak{M}_{n}^{\natural},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}(\Pi_{\infty},\chi_{\infty})

\displaystyle:={\mathcal{H}}(\Pi_{\infty})\otimes{\mathcal{H}}(I_{\eta_{\infty},\chi_{\infty}})\otimes\mathfrak{O}_{n,\infty},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{f}},\chi_{\mathrm{f}})

\displaystyle:=\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}\otimes I_{\eta_{\mathrm{f}},\chi_{\mathrm{f}}}\otimes\mathfrak{M}_{n,\mathrm{f}},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}(\Pi,\chi)_{\mathrm{loc}}

\displaystyle:={\mathcal{H}}(\Pi_{\infty},\chi_{\infty})\otimes{\mathcal{H}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{f}},\chi_{\mathrm{f}}).

The canonical maps (
2.2
) and (
2.7
) induce a linear isomorphism

(3.8)

\iota_{\mathrm{can}}:{\mathcal{H}}(\Pi,\chi)_{\mathrm{loc}}\to{\mathcal{H}}(\Pi,\chi)_{\mathrm{glob}}.

We say that the coefficient systems

are balanced if

(3.9)

\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\mathbb{C})}\left(F_{\mu}^{\vee}\otimes F_{\nu}^{\vee}\otimes F_{\eta,\chi}^{\vee},\mathbb{C}\right)\neq\{0\}.

In this case,
\chi
is called
(\mu,\nu)
-balanced and the set of such algebraic Hecke characters is denoted by
{\mathrm{B}}(\mu,\nu)
. By
[
JLS25a
, Lemma 2.6]
, this is equivalent to (
1.1
) or (
1.2
) holds for all
v|\infty
for
\mu,\nu,\chi
. Assume

in the rest of the paper. With respect to certain explicit realizations of
F_{\mu},F_{\nu},F_{\eta,\chi}
, we define a balanced map

(3.10)

\phi_{\mu,\nu,\chi}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\mathbb{C})}\left(F_{\mu}^{\vee}\otimes F_{\nu}^{\vee}\otimes F_{\eta,\chi}^{\vee},\mathbb{C}\right)=\left(F_{\mu}\otimes F_{\nu}\otimes F_{\eta,\chi}\right)^{{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\mathbb{C})}

as in
[
JLS25a
, Section 1.3]
.

Note that pairing with the fundamental class yields a linear map (see
[
Mah05
, Section 5.1]
for more explanation)

\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{n}}:{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{c}}^{d_{n}}({\mathcal{X}}_{n},\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathfrak{M}_{n}^{\natural}\to\mathbb{C}.

Here

d_{n}:=\dim\left({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{\infty})/\widetilde{K}_{n,\infty}\right)=\frac{[\mathrm{k}:{\mathbb{Q}}]}{2}\cdot(n^{2}-1)=2b_{n}+c_{n}.

We define the global modular symbol

(3.11)

{\mathcal{P}}:={\mathcal{P}}_{\Pi,\chi}:{\mathcal{H}}(\Pi,\chi)_{\mathrm{glob}}\to\mathbb{C}

to be the composition of

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{\Pi,\chi}:{\mathcal{H}}(\Pi,\chi)_{\mathrm{glob}}\xrightarrow{\iota_{\Pi}\otimes\mathrm{Eis}_{\eta,\chi}}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathrm{H}}^{b_{n}}({\mathcal{X}}_{n},F_{\mu}^{\vee})\otimes{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{c}}^{b_{n}}({\mathcal{X}}_{n},F_{\nu}^{\vee})\otimes{\mathrm{H}}^{c_{n}}({\mathcal{X}}_{n},F_{\eta,\chi}^{\vee})\otimes\mathfrak{M}_{n}^{\natural}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\phi_{\mu,\nu,\chi}}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{c}}^{d_{n}}({\mathcal{X}}_{n},\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathfrak{M}_{n}^{\natural}\xrightarrow{\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{n}}}\mathbb{C}.

On the other hand, we define the archimedean modular symbol

(3.12)

{\mathcal{P}}_{\infty}:={\mathcal{P}}_{\Pi_{\infty},\chi_{\infty}}:{\mathcal{H}}(\Pi_{\infty},\chi_{\infty})\to\mathbb{C}

to be the composition of

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{\Pi_{\infty},\chi_{\infty}}:

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{H}}(\Pi_{\infty},\chi_{\infty})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\text{res}}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{ct}}^{d_{n}}\left({\mathrm{Z}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{\infty})\backslash{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{\infty});F_{\mu}^{\vee}\otimes F_{\nu}^{\vee}\otimes F_{\eta,\chi}^{\vee}\otimes\Pi_{\infty}\otimes I_{\eta_{\infty},\chi_{\infty}}\right)\otimes\mathfrak{O}_{n,\infty}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{ct}}^{d_{n}}({\mathrm{Z}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{\infty})\backslash{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathrm{k}_{\infty}),\mathfrak{M}_{n,\infty}^{\ast})\otimes\mathfrak{O}_{n,\infty}=\mathbb{C},

where the first arrow is the restriction of cohomology. See
[
JLS25a

for more details. Here and henceforth, a superscript ‘

{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathrm{f}}:={\mathrm{Z}}^{\circ}_{\Pi_{\mathrm{f}},\chi_{\mathrm{f}}}:{\mathcal{H}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{f}},\chi_{\mathrm{f}})\to\mathbb{C}.

The global modular symbol is related to the archimedean modular symbol and the non-archimedean normalized Rankin-Selberg period by the following proposition. This is essentially due to the Euler product factorization of the Rankin-Selberg integrals. The proof is same as
[
LLS24

and will be omitted.

Proposition 4
.

We have the following commutative diagram

(3.14)

\begin{CD}{\mathcal{H}}(\Pi,\chi)_{\mathrm{loc}}@>{{\mathcal{P}}_{\infty}\otimes{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathrm{f}}}>{}>\mathbb{C}\\
@V{\iota_{\mathrm{can}}}V{}V@V{}V{{\mathcal{L}}(\Pi,\chi)}V\\
{\mathcal{H}}(\Pi,\chi)_{\mathrm{glob}}@>{{\mathcal{P}}}>{}>\mathbb{C},\end{CD}

where the right vertical map is the multiplication by

{\mathcal{L}}(\Pi,\chi):=\frac{{\mathrm{L}}(0,\Pi\times\chi)}{{\mathrm{L}}(0,\omega_{\Pi}\chi^{n})}.

Steiner Systems over Mixed Alphabet and Related Designs
Tuvi Etzion

Abstract
A mixed Steiner system MS ( t , k , Q ) (t,k,Q) is a set (code) 𝒞 {\cal C} of words of weight k k over an alphabet Q Q , where not all coordinates of a word have the same alphabet size, each word of weight t t , over Q Q , has distance k − t k-t from exactly one codeword of 𝒞 {\cal C} , and the minimum distance of the code 2 ​ ( k − t ) + 1 2(k-t)+1 . Mixed Steiner systems are constructed from perfect mixed codes, resolvable designs, large set, orthogonal arrays, and a new type of pairs-triples design. Necessary conditions for the existence of mixed Steiner systems are presented and it is proved that there are no large sets of these Steiner systems.



Keywords:
Large sets, orthogonal arrays, pairs-triples designs, perfect mixed codes, resolvable designs, Steiner systems..

A

As a code an S

has minimum distance
2(k-t)+2
. By abuse of notation we are going to use a mixed notation of sets and words
and it will not be mentioned which one is used.
Steiner systems were extensively studied in the literature for existence and applications.

There are several generalization of Steiner systems, such as group divisible designs

]

A

’s equal 2 and a generalized Steiner system when all the

Our goal is to have such a system where the minimum distance is

as one of the requirements.
But, for some parameters this minimum distance is not possible. When the minimum distance will be different,
i.e., smaller, from
2(k-t)+1
it will be mentioned and the system will be denoted by MS

Mixed Steiner systems were defined in
[
9
]
in connection of the existence of perfect codes over a mixed alphabet,
but there was no comprehensive discussion on properties, bounds, and constructions on these systems. This discussion is the
goal of the current work.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
examines some of the necessary conditions
for the existence of mixed Steiner systems. Some of these conditions are generalizations of the conditions for the existence of Steiner
systems, but they are much more evolved. Some of these necessary conditions are shown to be sufficient in this section.
In Section
3
we examine the existence of some trivial
mixed Steiner systems and show that their construction is not always straightforward.
Section
4
examines the connections between mixed Steiner systems and perfect codes
over a mixed alphabet. Many families of mixed Stiener systems are constructed from these mixed perfect codes and necessary conditions
for the existence of these perfect codes are derived from mixed Steiner systems and as a consequence some possible parameters for perfect codes
are eliminated. Large sets of Steiner systems and resolvable Steiner systems can be used for constructions of
mixed Steiner systems.
The same can be done for large sets of mixed Steiner systems, but it is proved that unfortunately
these large sets cannot exist
if there is more than one alphabet size. Section
6
is devoted to pairwise balanced mixed systems, i.e., mixed
Steiner systems MS
(2,k,Q)

equivalent to a set of mutually orthogonal Latin squares. Some of the codes obtained from orthogonal arrays are MS
{}_{d}(2,k,Q)

where

. In addition, a new combinatorial design of pairwise disjoint one-factors and a set of triples is
used for one of the constructions.
Conclusion and future research are discussed in Section

Our exposition will be on all parameters of mixed Steiner systems, but special emphasis will be given
to mixed Steiner Systems MS
(2,3,\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q})

Lemma 1

The number of codewords in a mixed Steiner system

\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\subseteq[n]\\
\left|Y\right|=t\end{subarray}}\prod_{j\in Y}(q_{j}-1)\right)\text{{\Large/}}\binom{k}{t}~.

Proof.

Let

{\cal C}

For each

\prod_{j=1}^{t}(q_{i_{j}}-1)

Theorem 10
.

Let
\{{\cal S}_{1},{\cal S}_{2},\ldots,{\cal S}_{n}\}
be a partition of

into
n

is a subspace of dimension

Proof.

If
c
and
c^{\prime}
are two codewords of
{\cal C}
, then clearly

is also a codeword in
{\cal C}
.
This implies that
{\cal C}
is a linear code and its minimum distance is the weight of the codeword
of minimum weight in the code. It is readily verified that there is no codeword of weight one in
{\cal C}
. Therefore,
if
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in{{\cal T}_{1}\times{\cal T}_{2}\times\cdots\times{\cal T}_{n}}
has weight two, then assume w.l.o.g. that

and
x_{j}\neq 0
, for some

. Since

,
x_{j}\in{\cal T}_{j}
,
{\cal T}_{i}\cap{\cal T}_{j}=\{\bf 0\}
, and

,
{\cal T}_{j}
are subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, it follows that

does not have a

is three.

To complete the proof of the claim of the theorem, it suffices to show that for each word

, there exists

such that

. Let

. If

,
then, clearly,

is a codeword. If

, then
let

be the unique integer such that

.
Clearly, since

\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}-\alpha=s-\alpha=0

and

.

Assume now that there exists two distinct codewords

such that

.
By the triangle equality we have that

a contradiction to the minimum distance of
{\cal C}
.

Thus,

is the unique codeword in
{\cal C}
such that
{d(x,c)=1}
.
∎

5
Large Sets and Resolutions

A
large set
of Steiner systems S
(t,k,n)
, on an
n
-set
Q
, is a partition of all
k
-subsets
of
Q
into Steiner systems S
(t,k,n)
. If we restrict ourself to Steiner systems S
(t-1,t,n)
, then exactly
two families of large sets are completely solved. A Steiner system S
(1,2,n)
exists if and only if
n
is even and its
large set is known as a one-factorization of the complete graph
K_{n}
. The existence of such one-factorizations
is a folklore and a survey can be found in
[
35
]
. A Steiner system S
(2,3,n)
is known as a Steiner triple
system and the corresponding large set is known to exist for every
n\equiv 1
or
3~(\text{mod}~6)
, where
n>7
.
It was first proved by Lu
[
27
,
28
]
, who left six open cases which were solved by Teirlinck
[
34
]
.
An alternative shorter proof was given later by Ji
[
22
]
. Unfortunately, there is no known construction for large sets of Steiner quadruple
systems and the one that gets close to it was constructed in
[
11
]
.
Large sets is a special case of resolvable designs
[
13
]
. A Steiner system S
(t+1,k,n)
is
t
-resolvable
if the blocks of the system can be
partitioned into subsets, each one is a Steiner system S
(t,k,n)
. The most remarkable result on resolvable Steiner systems
and large set was done by Keevash
[
24
]
, but the proofs for their existence is probabilistic and no resolvable system
or a large set was constructed. The construction from large sets and resolutions will
be described in Section
5.1
. In Section
5.2
we prove that there are no large sets of mixed Steiner systems.

5.1

Construction 1
.

Let
{\cal S}=(\mathbb{Z}_{n},B)
be a Steiner system

.
Let

{\cal S}

\{\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k},(n+1,j)\}\},~~\text{where}~~\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k}\}\in{\cal T}_{j}~.

Theorem 12
.

The system
{\cal S}^{\prime}
generated by Construction

(t,k+1,\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{r+1})

Proof.

By the definition all the blocks of

For a

Since

As for the minimum distance, consider the two blocks

Thus, the minimum Hamming distance of

∎

5.2
The Nonexistence of Large Sets

Construction
1
can be used with large sets of mixed Steiner systems instead of Steiner systems.
Unfortunately, it cannot help to obtain new mixed Steiner systems since there are no large sets of mixed Steiner systems
which are not Steiner systems.
Simple arguments leads to a generalization of Lemma
2
to large sets.

Lemma 5
.

If there exists a large set of
MS
(t,k,Q\times\mathbb{Z}_{q})
, then there exists a large set of
MS
(t-1,k-1,Q)

Theorem 13
.

There is no large set of
MS
(t,k,Q)
, unless
Q
all the coordinates of
Q
are over alphabets of the same size.

Proof.

By using
t-1
iterations of Lemma
5
the existence of a large set of MS
(t,k,Q)
implies the
existence of a large set of MS
(1,k-t+1,Q^{\prime})
, where
Q^{\prime}
is obtained by removing any
t-1
coordinates from
Q
.
Therefore, it is sufficient to prove that there is no large set of MS
(1,k,Q)
. W.l.o.g. we can assume that

Q=\mathbb{Z}_{q_{1}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{q_{n-1}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q_{n}}
, where
q_{i}\leq q_{j}
for
i<j
and not all the
q_{i}
’s
are equal.

In MS
(1,k,Q)
the number of codewords that contain a nonzero element from
\mathbb{Z}_{q_{n}}
is
q_{n}-1
and they cover
exactly
(q_{n}-1)(k-1)
nonzero elements of
Q
. The total number of words of weight
k
, that contain a nonzero element of
\mathbb{Z}_{q_{n}}
, in
Q
, is

(q_{n}-1)\sum_{{\cal R}\subset Q}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}(q_{i_{j}}-1),

where
{\cal R}=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k-1}\}
,
x_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q_{i_{j}}}\setminus\{\bf 0\}
for

1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{k-1}\leq n-1
. There are
q_{n}-1
nonzero elements in
\mathbb{Z}_{q_{n}}
and
therefore the size of a large set should be

\frac{(q_{n}-1)\sum_{{\cal R}\subset Q}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}(q_{i_{j}}-1)}{q_{n}-1}=\sum_{{\cal R}\subset Q}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}(q_{i_{j}}-1)~.

(1)

The number of nonzero elements in
Q
that have to be covered with codewords that do not contain a nonzero element of
\mathbb{Z}_{q_{n}}
is

\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(q_{i}-1)-(q_{n}-1)(k-1)
. Therefore, the number of codewords (whose size is
k
) that do not contain nonzero elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{q_{n}}
is

\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(q_{i}-1)-(q_{n}-1)(k-1)}{k}~.

The total number of words of weight
k
, that do not contain a nonzero element of
\mathbb{Z}_{q_{n}}
, in
Q
, is

\sum_{{\cal R}\subset Q}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(q_{i_{j}}-1),

where
{\cal R}=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k}\}
,
x_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q_{i_{j}}}\setminus\{\bf 0\}
for

1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq n-1
.
This implies that the large set has size

\frac{k\sum_{{\cal R}\subset Q}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(q_{i_{j}}-1)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(q_{i}-1)-(q_{n}-1)(k-1)}.

(2)

The two expressions of Equations (
1
) and (
2
) that we got for the
size of a large set must be equal and hence

\frac{k\sum_{{\cal R}\subset Q}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(q_{i_{j}}-1)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(q_{i}-1)-(q_{n}-1)(k-1)}=\sum_{{\cal R}\subset Q}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}(q_{i_{j}}-1)~

or

k\sum_{{\cal R}\subset Q}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(q_{i_{j}}-1)=\left(\sum_{{\cal R}\subset Q}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}(q_{i_{j}}-1)\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(q_{i}-1)-(q_{n}-1)(k-1)\right).

Careful analysis of the required equality yields that the left side of the equation is larger that the right side, unless all the
q_{i}
’s
are equal in which case there is indeed equality.
∎

{\cal A}

Construction 2

Theorem 14
.

If
r=n-1
then the system
{\cal S}
of Construction
2
is an

(2,k,\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{(n-1)k}\times\mathbb{Z}_{k+1})

Proof.

It is easily verified that each 2-subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{(n-1)k}\times\mathbb{Z}_{k+1}
is contained in exactly one codeword (
k
-subset).
Consider the two codewords
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{(n-1)k},\alpha)
and
y=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{(n-1)k},\beta)

If
\alpha=\beta
, then

implies that

and
y_{i}=1

. As a consequence

If

is an MS
(2,k,\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{(n-1)k}\times\mathbb{Z}_{k+1})
.
∎

Self-improving properties of weighted norm inequalities on metric measure spaces
Juha Kinnunen, Juha Lehrbäck, Antti V. Vähäkangas, Dachun Yang

Abstract
. This work discusses self-improving properties of the Muckenhoupt condition and weighted norm inequalities for the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function on metric measure spaces with a doubling measure. Our main result provides direct proofs of these properties by applying a Whitney covering argument and a technique inspired by the Calderón–Zygmund decomposition. In particular, this approach does not rely on reverse Hölder inequalities.

42B25, 42B35, 46E30, 30L99

1.
Introduction

Harmonic analysis on metric measure spaces, and more broadly on spaces of homogeneous type, continues to be an active area of research. Coifman and Weiss provided a systematic account of many aspects of harmonic analysis on spaces of homogeneous type in
[
2
]
.
The theory of weighted norm inequalities and Muckenhoupt weights has been explored in greater detail in works such as those by Strömberg and Torchinsky in
[
12
]
and by Genebashvili, Gogatishvili, Kokilashvili and Krbec in
[
5
]
.
Muckenhoupt weights exhibit a remarkable self-improving property:
if a weight belongs to a certain Muckenhoupt class
A_{p}
,
1<p<\infty
,
then it also belongs to another Muckenhoupt class
A_{p-\varepsilon}
with a slightly smaller index, see
[
12
, Lemma 8, p. 5]
.
This self-improving nature of Muckenhoupt weights arises from the fact that these weights satisfy a reverse Hölder inequality, see
[
12
, Theorem 15, p. 9]
for details.

Reverse Hölder inequalities are powerful tools in the study of weighted norm inequalities, but
the purpose of this work is to discuss an alternative approach to the self-improving property
of the Muckenhoupt weights that entirely avoids relying on reverse Hölder inequalities.
Thus the novelty in this work lies in the methodology rather than in the actual results.

Instead of reverse Hölder inequalities, we utilize the close connection between Muckenhoupt weights and
weighted norm inequalities for the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function, see
[
3
,
4
]
.
More specifically, let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space equipped with a doubling measure
\mu

and let
w
be a weight in
X
; see Sections
2
and
3
for the definitions.
Then the following assertions are equivalent, for
1<p<\infty
.

(A)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

(1.1)

\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\leq C\int_{X}\lvert f(x)\rvert^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)

for every
f\in L^{\infty}(X)
with a bounded support.

(B)

The weight
w
belongs to the Muckenhoupt class
A_{p}
.

(C)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that inequality (
1.1
) holds for every
f\in L^{p}(w\,d\mu)
.

In (
1.1
), the
(noncentered) Hardy–Littlewood maximal function of
f
is defined as

{M}^{*}f(x)=\sup_{B\ni x}\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}\lvert f(y)\rvert\,d\mu(y),

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B
in
X
containing
x
.
It was shown in
[
2
]
that
{M}^{*}
is a bounded operator on

L^{p}(X)
for every
1<p\leq\infty

and of weak type
(1,1)
under the assumption that
\mu
is a doubling measure;
see also
[
12
, Theorem 3, p. 3]
. Observe that assertion
(C)
above states that
the operator
{M}^{*}
is bounded on
L^{p}(w\,d\mu)
, for
1<p<\infty
.

The equivalence of the assertions
(B)
and
(C)
is well known,
see
[
12
, Theorem 9, p. 5]
. For our purposes, it is convenient to
consider also assertion
(A)
, which is a priori slightly weaker
than
(C)
.
That
(A)
implies
(B)
is shown in Lemma
4.2
,
and the more involved implication from
(B)
to
(C)
is proved in
Theorem
4.5
by adapting an argument of Lerner
[
10
]
.
This approach is direct and, in particular, does not refer to reverse Hölder inequalities.
Finally, the implication from
(C)
to
(A)
is a triviality.

Now, assume that assertion
(C)
holds, i.e., that

{M}^{*}
is bounded on some
L^{p}(w\,d\mu)
,
1<p<\infty
,
where
w
is a weight and
\mu
a doubling measure in
X
.
By the Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem and the
boundedness of the maximal operator
{M}^{*}
on
L^{\infty}(w\,d\mu)
,
it follows that
then
{M}^{*}
is also bounded on
L^{p+\varepsilon}(w\,d\mu)
for every
\varepsilon>0
.
On the other hand, by using the equivalence of the assertions
(B)
and
(C)

and the known self-improving property of the Muckenhoupt
A_{p}
condition,
there exits some
\varepsilon>0
such that

{M}^{*}
is bounded on
L^{p-\varepsilon}(w\,d\mu)
as well.

The main goal in this work is to give a direct and essentially self-contained
proof for this self-improving property of
the weighted norm inequalities. In particular, we show in Lemma
5.6

that if assertion
(C)
holds, then
there exist
\varepsilon>0
and
C>0
such that

(1.2)

\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p-\varepsilon}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\leq C\int_{X}\lvert f(x)\rvert^{p-\varepsilon}w(x)\,d\mu(x)

for every
f\in L^{\infty}(X)
with a bounded support.
The proof of Lemma
5.6
uses a Whitney covering argument
and a technique inspired by the Calderón–Zygmund decomposition,
based on an unpublished note on Muckenhoupt weights by Keith and Zhong.
This approach extends the corresponding Euclidean result with the Lebesgue measure

[
8
, Theorem 8.11]
to metric spaces and it does not rely on reverse Hölder inequalities.
Similar ideas have been applied in various contexts, for example, in Keith and Zhong
[
7
]
, Kinnunen, Lehrbäck, Vähäkangas and Zhong
[
9
]
and Lewis
[
11
]
.

By the equivalence of the assertions
(A)
,
(B)
and
(C)
,
we obtain from Lemma
5.6
an independent proof for the
self-improving property of the Muckenhoupt classes, see Theorem
5.11
,
and we also conclude that the improved weighted norm inequality (
1.2
) holds for
all functions
f\in L^{p-\varepsilon}(w\,d\mu)
, see Corollary
5.13
.

Before the statements and proofs of our main results and tools in Sections
4
and
5
,
we recall preliminaries on metric spaces and on Muckenhoupt weights in Sections
2
and
3
,
respectively. In Section
3
we also prove some basic properties of the Muckenhoupt classes for the
sake of completeness.

and a
positive complete Borel
measure

for all open balls

under the above assumptions, we refer to

We also assume throughout that

\mu(2B)\leq c_{\mu}\,\mu(B)

for all balls

.
Here we use for

.
We let

be the integral average of

.

; that is,

3.
Muckenhoupt weights

For convenience, we recall the standard definition and elementary properties of Muckenhoupt weights in the setting of metric measure spaces. We follow
[
4
]
and also refer to
[
8
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A measurable function
w\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a weight in
X
, if
w
is integrable
on all balls of
X
and

w(x)>0
for almost
every
x\in X
. If
w
is a weight in
X
and
E\subset X
is a measurable set, we write

(3.2)

w(E)=\int_{E}w(x)\,d\mu(x).

We say that a weight
w
in
X

is doubling, if there exists a constant
c_{w}\geq 1
such that

0<w(B(x,2r))\leq c_{w}w(B(x,r))<\infty

for all
x\in X
and
r>0
.

Definition 3.3
.

A weight
w
in
X
belongs to the Muckenhoupt class

A_{p}
, for
1<p<\infty
, if
there exists a constant
C
such that

(3.4)

for every ball
B\subset X
.
The smallest possible constant
C
in (
3.4
) is called the
A_{p}
constant of
w
, and it is
denoted by
[w]_{A_{p}}
.

If
1<p<q<\infty
, then Hölder’s inequality shows that
A_{p}\subset A_{q}
.
Next we recall some other basic properties of Muckenhoupt weights
that will be applied later. We begin with a duality statement.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and write
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
. Then

w\in A_{p}
if and only if
w^{1-p^{\prime}}\in A_{p^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

Assume that
w\in A_{p}
.
Then

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}w(x)^{1-p^{\prime}}\,d\mu(x)\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\leq[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

for every ball

.
This shows that
w^{1-p^{\prime}}\in A_{p^{\prime}}
.
Conversely, if
w^{1-p^{\prime}}\in A_{p^{\prime}}
then
w=(w^{1-p^{\prime}})^{1-p}\in A_{p}
by the same argument.
∎

Next we consider a weighted Hölder inequality for integral averages.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and assume that
w\in A_{p}
.
Let
B\subset X
be a ball and
f\in L^{1}(B)
be a nonnegative function.
Then

\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}f(x)\,d\mu(x)\leq[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\biggl(\frac{1}{w(B)}\int_{B}f(x)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Hölder’s inequality and (

) imply

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}f(x)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\biggl(\int_{B}w(x)^{\frac{1}{1-p}}\,d\mu(x)\biggr)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\biggl(\int_{B}f(x)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\biggl(\frac{w(B)}{\mu(B)}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}w(x)^{\frac{1}{1-p}}\,d\mu(x)\biggr)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\biggl(\frac{1}{w(B)}\int_{B}f(x)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\biggl(\frac{1}{w(B)}\int_{B}f(x)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}.\qed

\mu
is doubling,
an application of Lemma

shows that
a Muckenhoupt weight in

is doubling.

.

Let

and assume that

. Then

is doubling with a constant

c_{w}=C(c_{\mu},p,[w]_{A_{p}})
.

Let

and

.

) and Lemma

4.
Maximal function estimates

Let
w
be a weight in
X
. For
1\leq p<\infty
,
the weighted
L^{p}
-norm of a measurable function
f
on
X
is defined as

\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(w\,d\mu)}=\left(\int_{X}\lvert f(x)\rvert^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and for
p=\infty
we define

\lVert f\rVert_{L^{\infty}(w\,d\mu)}=\inf\bigl\{c\geq 0:\lvert f(x)\rvert\leq c\ w\text{-a.e.\ }x\in X\bigr\}.

If
\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(w\,d\mu)}<\infty
, we write

f\in L^{p}(w\,d\mu)
, and in the case
w=1
we use the notation

L^{p}(d\mu)=L^{p}(X)
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
w
be a doubling weight in
X
.
For a measurable function
f
on
X
,
the weighted maximal function
M^{*,w}f
is defined by

M^{*,w}f(x)=\sup_{B\ni x}\frac{1}{w(B)}\int_{B}\lvert f(y)\rvert w(y)\,d\mu(y),

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B
in
X
with
x\in B
.
When
w=1
, we obtain the noncentered maximal function
{M}^{*}f=M^{*,1}f
.

The following simple lemma gives a sufficient condition for the Muckenhoupt
property. Observe that the
class of test functions is restricted to essentially bounded functions with a bounded support.
This restriction is convenient for later purposes.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and assume that
w
is a weight in
X
. Assume that there exists
a constant
C_{1}
such that

(4.3)

\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\leq C_{1}\int_{X}f(x)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)

for every nonnegative
f\in L^{\infty}(X)
with a bounded support. Then
w\in A_{p}
and
[w]_{A_{p}}\leq C_{1}
.

Proof.

Fix a ball
B\subset X
. Let
{\varepsilon}>0
and
f=({\varepsilon}+w)^{\frac{1}{1-p}}{\mbox{\hbox{$\mathbf{1}$}}}_{\hbox{$\scriptstyle B$}}
.
Observe that
f\in L^{\infty}(X)
and
f
has a bounded support.
Clearly

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}({\varepsilon}+w(y))^{\frac{1}{1-p}}\,d\mu(y)=\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}\lvert f(y)\rvert\,d\mu(y)\leq{M}^{*}f(x)

for every

,
and thus

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}({\varepsilon}+w(y))^{\frac{1}{1-p}}\,d\mu(y)\biggr)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x).

By the assumption, we have

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{X}f(x)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}({\varepsilon}+w(x))^{\frac{p}{1-p}}({\varepsilon}+w(x))\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=C_{1}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}({\varepsilon}+w(x))^{\frac{1}{1-p}}\,d\mu(x).

This gives

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}({\varepsilon}+w(y))^{\frac{1}{1-p}}\,d\mu(y)\biggr)^{p-1}\leq C_{1}

for every
{\varepsilon}>0
. Fatou’s lemma implies

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}w(x)^{\frac{1}{1-p}}\,d\mu(x)\biggr)^{p-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\liminf_{{\varepsilon}\to 0+}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}({\varepsilon}+w(x))^{\frac{1}{1-p}}\,d\mu(x)\biggr)^{p-1}\leq C_{1},

and the claim follows.
∎

Next we aim to show a strengthened converse of Lemma
4.2
,
see Theorem
4.5
below.
Since a doubling weight
w
in
X
can be interpreted as a doubling measure as in
Definition

,
we have the following weighted version of the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function theorem,
see for instance
[

, Theorem 3.13]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and let
w
be a doubling weight in
X
, with a constant
c_{w}
.
Then there exists a constant
C=C(p,c_{w})
such that

\lVert M^{*,w}f\rVert_{L^{p}(w\,d\mu)}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(w\,d\mu)}

for every
f\in L^{p}(w\,d\mu)
.

The proof of the weighted norm inequality in Theorem
4.5

is adapted from
[
10
]
.
We also refer to
[
4
, Theorem 8.19]
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
w\in A_{p}
. There exists a constant
C=C(c_{\mu},p,[w]_{A_{p}})
such that

\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\leq C\int_{X}\lvert f(x)\rvert^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)

for every
f\in L^{p}(w\,d\mu)
.

Proof.

Define
\sigma=w^{1-p^{\prime}}=w^{\frac{1}{1-p}}
.
Lemma
3.5
implies that
\sigma\in A_{p^{\prime}}
, where
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
.
Let
f\in L^{p}(w\,d\mu)
. Let
x\in X
and consider a ball
B=B(x_{0},r)
such that
x\in B
.
Then

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}\lvert f(y)\rvert\,d\mu(y)=A\left(\frac{\mu(B)}{w(B)}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma(B)}\int_{B}\lvert f(y)\rvert\,d\mu(y)\right)^{p-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

where

\displaystyle A=\left(\frac{w(B)\sigma(B)^{p-1}}{\mu(B)^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\leq[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Let
z\in B
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma(B)}\int_{B}\lvert f(y)\rvert\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma(B)}\int_{B}\lvert f(y)\sigma(y)^{-1}\rvert\sigma(y)\,d\mu(y)\leq M^{*,\sigma}(f\sigma^{-1})(z).

Hence we have

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}\lvert f(y)\rvert\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(\frac{1}{w(B)}\int_{B}\bigl(M^{*,\sigma}(f\sigma^{-1})(z)\bigr)^{p-1}w(z)^{-1}w(z)\,d\mu(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\leq[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\bigl(M^{*,w}\bigl((M^{*,\sigma}(f\sigma^{-1}))^{p-1}w^{-1}\bigr)(x)\bigr)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

By taking supremum over all balls
B
as above, it follows that

{M}^{*}f(x)\leq[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\bigl(M^{*,w}\bigl((M^{*,\sigma}(f\sigma^{-1}))^{p-1}w^{-1}\bigr)(x)\bigr)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

for all
x\in X
. Observe that
w\in A_{p}
and
\sigma\in A_{p^{\prime}}
are doubling weights by Lemma
3.7
,
with constants
c_{w}
and
c_{\sigma}

depending only on
c_{\mu}
,
p
and
[w]_{A_{p}}
.
Lemma
4.4
implies

\displaystyle\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\int_{X}\bigl(M^{*,w}\bigl((M^{*,\sigma}(f\sigma^{-1}))^{p-1}w^{-1}\bigr)(x)\bigr)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}w(x)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{\mu},p,[w]_{A_{p}})\int_{X}\bigl(M^{*,\sigma}(f\sigma^{-1})(x)\bigr)^{p}\sigma(x)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{\mu},p,[w]_{A_{p}})\int_{X}\lvert f(x)\rvert^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x),

where the facts
\sigma=w^{\frac{1}{1-p}}
and
w=\sigma^{1-p}
are used.
∎

5.
Self-improvement results

We need the following Whitney type covering theorem, see
[
6
, Proposition 4.1.15]
and
[
2
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume that
\Omega\not=\emptyset
is an open subset of
X
such that
X\setminus\Omega\not=\emptyset
.
There exist balls
B_{i}=B(x_{i},r_{i})\subset\Omega
,
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, with

(5.2)

r_{i}=\frac{1}{8}\operatorname{dist}(x_{i},X\setminus\Omega),

such that
\Omega=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}B_{i}
and

(5.3)

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}{\mbox{\hbox{$\mathbf{1}$}}}_{\hbox{$\scriptstyle B_{i}$}}(x)\leq C(c_{\mu})

for every
x\in\Omega
.

Let
f\in L^{\infty}(X)
be a nonnegative function with
a bounded support. Let
t>0
be such that

E_{t}=\{x\in X:{M}^{*}f(x)>t\}\not=X,

where
{M}^{*}f
is
the noncentered maximal function of
f
as in Definition
4.1
.
Then
E_{t}\subsetneq X
is an open set. We define
a function
f_{t}
as below.

•

If
E_{t}\not=\emptyset
, consider a Whitney type cover
\{B_{i}:i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}

of
E_{t}
as in Lemma
5.1
, and let

(5.4)

f_{t}(x)=\begin{cases}f(x),\qquad&x\in X\setminus E_{t},\\
\displaystyle\sup_{B_{i}\ni x}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{i}}}f(y)\,d\mu(y),\qquad&x\in E_{t},\end{cases}

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B_{i}
,
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that
x\in B_{i}
.

•

If
E_{t}=\emptyset
, let
f_{t}(x)=f(x)
for every
x\in X
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Assume that
f\in L^{\infty}(X)
is a nonnegative function with
a bounded support. Let
t>0
and let
E_{t}\subsetneq X
and
f_{t}
be as defined above.
Then the following assertions hold:

(a)

f_{t}(x)\leq C(c_{\mu})t
for almost every
x\in X
.

(b)

{M}^{*}f(x)\leq C(c_{\mu}){M}^{*}f_{t}(x)

for every
x\in X\setminus E_{t}
.

Proof.

By the Lebesgue differentiation theorem
[
6
, Section 3.4]
, we have

f_{t}(x)=f(x)\leq{M}^{*}f(x)\leq t

for almost every
x\in X\setminus E_{t}
. If
E_{t}=\emptyset
, then
(a)
holds.
If
E_{t}\not=\emptyset
and
x\in E_{t}
, then consider any ball
B_{i}
,
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that
x\in B_{i}
. Recall that
E_{t}
is the union of such balls. By (
5.2
) and (
2.1
), there exists a ball
B\subset X
such that

B\setminus E_{t}\not=\emptyset
,

B_{i}\subset B
and
\mu(B)\leq c_{\mu}^{4}\mu(B_{i})
.
For any
z\in B\setminus E_{t}
we have

\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{i}}}f(y)\,d\mu(y)\leq c_{\mu}^{4}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}f(y)\,d\mu(y)\leq c_{\mu}^{4}{M}^{*}f(z)\leq c_{\mu}^{4}t.

By taking supremum over all balls
B_{i}
as above, we see that
f_{t}(x)\leq c_{\mu}^{4}t
for every
x\in E_{t}
, and thus
(a)
holds also in the case
E_{t}\not=\emptyset
.

To prove part
(b)
, we may clearly assume that
E_{t}\not=\emptyset
.
Let
x\in X\setminus E_{t}
.
Let
z\in X
and
r>0
be such that
x\in B(z,r)
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{B(z,r)}f(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{B(z,r)\setminus E_{t}}f(y)\,d\mu(y)+\int_{B(z,r)\cap E_{t}}f(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(z,r)\setminus E_{t}}f(y)\,d\mu(y)+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{B(z,r)\cap B_{i}}f(y)\,d\mu(y).

Using the fact that
0\leq f=f_{t}
in
X\setminus E_{t}
, we get

\int_{B(z,r)\setminus E_{t}}f(y)\,d\mu(y)\leq\int_{B(z,2r)\setminus E_{t}}f(y)\,d\mu(y)=\int_{B(z,2r)\setminus E_{t}}f_{t}(y)\,d\mu(y).

Next we consider any ball
B_{i}

satisfying
B(z,r)\cap B_{i}\not=\emptyset
, if such
B_{i}
exists. Since
x\in B(z,r)\setminus E_{t}
,
by definition (
5.2
) of the radius
r_{i}
, we have
B_{i}\subset B(z,2r)
. Thus (
5.4
) implies

\displaystyle\int_{B(z,r)\cap B_{i}}f(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{i}}f(y)\,d\mu(y)=\int_{B_{i}}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{i}}}f(y)\,d\mu(y)\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{i}}f_{t}(z)\,d\mu(z)=\int_{B(z,2r)\cap B_{i}}f_{t}(y)\,d\mu(y).

By collecting the estimates above and using (
5.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B(z,r)}f(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(z,2r)\setminus E_{t}}f_{t}(y)\,d\mu(y)+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{B(z,2r)\cap B_{i}}f_{t}(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{\mu})\int_{B(z,2r)}f_{t}(y)\,d\mu(y),

and consequently

\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(z,r)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r)}}f(y)\,d\mu(y)\leq C(c_{\mu})\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(z,2r)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,2r)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,2r)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,2r)}}f_{t}(y)\,d\mu(y)\leq C(c_{\mu}){M}^{*}f_{t}(x).

By taking supremum over all balls
B(z,r)
as above, we
conclude that
(b)
holds.
∎

Next we state and prove our main results concerning the
self-improving properties of
weighted norm inequalities for the
non-centered maximal function.
To emphasize the key aspects of the arguments,
we first formulate this property

We will later strengthen this result and recover

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and let
w
be a weight in
X
.
Assume that there exists a constant
C_{1}
such that

(5.7)

\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\leq C_{1}\int_{X}\lvert f(x)\rvert^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)

for all
f\in L^{p}(w\,d\mu)
.
Then there exist constants
{\varepsilon}={\varepsilon}(c_{\mu},p,C_{1})
and
C=C(c_{\mu},p,C_{1})

such that
0<{\varepsilon}<p-1
and

\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p-{\varepsilon}}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\leq C\int_{X}f(x)^{p-{\varepsilon}}w(x)\,d\mu(x)

for all nonnegative
f\in L^{\infty}(X)
with a bounded support.

Proof.

Fix a nonnegative function
f\in L^{\infty}(X)
with a bounded support.
Let
t>0
be such that
E_{t}=\{x\in X:{M}^{*}f(x)>t\}\subsetneq X
and let
f_{t}
be as in (
5.4
).
By using assumption (
5.7
), we get

\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f_{t}(x)\bigr)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\leq C_{1}\int_{X}f_{t}(x)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x).

Employing also Lemma
5.5
,
we obtain

(5.8)

\begin{split}\int_{X\setminus E_{t}}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)&\leq C(c_{\mu},p)\int_{X\setminus E_{t}}\bigl({M}^{*}f_{t}(x)\bigr)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\\
&\leq C(c_{\mu},p)\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f_{t}(x)\bigr)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\\
&\leq C(c_{\mu},p,C_{1})\biggl(\int_{X\setminus E_{t}}f(x)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)+t^{p}w(E_{t})\biggr).\end{split}

We consider parameters
t_{0}>0
and
0<{\varepsilon}<p-1
. We will choose the latter parameter more specifically at the end of the proof. We have

(5.9)

\begin{split}&\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}t^{-1-{\varepsilon}}\int_{X\setminus E_{t}}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\,dt\\
&\qquad\leq C(c_{\mu},p,C_{1})\biggl(\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}t^{-1-{\varepsilon}}\int_{X\setminus E_{t}}f(x)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\,dt+\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}t^{p-1-{\varepsilon}}w(E_{t})\,dt\biggr).\end{split}

By Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}t^{-1-{\varepsilon}}\int_{X\setminus E_{t}}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p}\biggl(\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}{\mbox{\hbox{$\mathbf{1}$}}}_{\hbox{$\scriptstyle X\setminus E_{t}$}}(x)t^{-1-{\varepsilon}}\,dt\biggr)\,w(x)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p}\biggl(\int_{\max\{t_{0},{M}^{*}f(x)\}}^{\infty}t^{-1-{\varepsilon}}\,dt\biggr)\,w(x)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p}\bigl(\max\bigl\{t_{0},{M}^{*}f(x)\bigr\}\bigr)^{-{\varepsilon}}w(x)\,d\mu(x).

In a similar way, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}t^{-1-{\varepsilon}}\int_{X\setminus E_{t}}f(x)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{X}f(x)^{p}\bigl(\max\bigl\{t_{0},{M}^{*}f(x)\bigr\}\bigr)^{-{\varepsilon}}w(x)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{X}f(x)^{p-{\varepsilon}}w(x)\,d\mu(x),

where the inequality holds since
0\leq f(x)\leq\max\bigl\{t_{0},{M}^{*}f(x)\bigr\}
for almost every
x\in X
.

The last term in (
5.9
) can be estimated as

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}t^{p-1-{\varepsilon}}w(E_{t})\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\biggl(\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}{\mbox{\hbox{$\mathbf{1}$}}}_{\hbox{$\scriptstyle E_{t}$}}(x)t^{p-1-{\varepsilon}}\,dt\biggr)\,w(x)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{t_{0}}}\biggl(\int_{t_{0}}^{{M}^{*}f(x)}t^{p-1-{\varepsilon}}\,dt\biggr)\,w(x)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p-{\varepsilon}}\int_{E_{t_{0}}}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p-{\varepsilon}}w(x)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p-{\varepsilon}}\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p}\bigl(\max\bigl\{t_{0},{M}^{*}f(x)\bigr\}\bigr)^{-{\varepsilon}}w(x)\,d\mu(x).

Multiplying of the obtained inequalities by
{\varepsilon}>0

and using (
5.9
) gives

(5.10)

\begin{split}&\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p}\bigl(\max\bigl\{t_{0},{M}^{*}f(x)\bigr\}\bigr)^{-{\varepsilon}}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\leq C(c_{\mu},p,C_{1})\int_{X}f(x)^{p-{\varepsilon}}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\\
&\qquad+C(c_{\mu},p,C_{1})\frac{{\varepsilon}}{p-{\varepsilon}}\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p}\bigl(\max\bigl\{t_{0},{M}^{*}f(x)\bigr\}\bigr)^{-{\varepsilon}}w(x)\,d\mu(x).\end{split}

We fix
0<{\varepsilon}<p-1
, only depending on
c_{\mu}
,
p
and
C_{1}
, such that

C(c_{\mu},p,C_{1})\frac{{\varepsilon}}{p-{\varepsilon}}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Recall that
f\in L^{\infty}(X)
has a bounded support
and
w
is integrable on balls.
Thus the last term on the right-hand side of (
5.10
) is finite
by the assumption in (
5.7
) and the fact that
t_{0}>0
.
Hence, we may absorb this term
to the left-hand side of (
5.10
), and we obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p}\bigl(\max\bigl\{t_{0},{M}^{*}f(x)\bigr\}\bigr)^{-{\varepsilon}}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\leq C(c_{\mu},p,C_{1})\int_{X}f(x)^{p-{\varepsilon}}w(x)\,d\mu(x)

that holds uniformly for all
t_{0}>0
.
Finally, by Fatou’s lemma,

\displaystyle\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p-{\varepsilon}}w(x)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{t_{0}\to 0+}\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p}\bigl(\max\bigl\{t_{0},{M}^{*}f(x)\bigr\}\bigr)^{-{\varepsilon}}w(x)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{\mu},p,C_{1})\int_{X}f(x)^{p-{\varepsilon}}w(x)\,d\mu(x),

and this concludes the proof.
∎

We can now prove a self-improving property of
Muckenhoupt weights.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and assume that
w\in A_{p}
.
Then there exist
{\varepsilon}={\varepsilon}(c_{\mu},p,[w]_{A_{p}})
such that
0<{\varepsilon}<p-1

and
w\in A_{p-{\varepsilon}}
and
[w]_{A_{p-{\varepsilon}}}\leq C(c_{\mu},p,[w]_{A_{p}})
. Moreover,

(5.12)

\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p-{\varepsilon}}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\leq C(c_{\mu},p,[w]_{A_{p}})\int_{X}\lvert f(x)\rvert^{p-{\varepsilon}}w(x)\,d\mu(x)

for all
f\in L^{p-\varepsilon}(w\,d\mu)
.

Proof.

Theorem
4.5
and Lemma
5.6
combined
show that there are constants
{\varepsilon}={\varepsilon}(c_{\mu},p,[w]_{A_{p}})
and
C_{1}=C(c_{\mu},p,[w]_{A_{p}})

such that
0<{\varepsilon}<p-1
and

\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p-{\varepsilon}}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\leq C_{1}\int_{X}f(x)^{p-{\varepsilon}}w(x)\,d\mu(x)

for all nonnegative
f\in L^{\infty}(X)
with a bounded support.
Lemma
4.2

implies that
w\in A_{p-{\varepsilon}}
and

[w]_{A_{p-{\varepsilon}}}\leq C_{1}=C(c_{\mu},p,[w]_{A_{p}})
.
Finally, the inequality in (
5.12
) holds for all
f\in L^{p-\varepsilon}(w\,d\mu)

by Theorem
4.5
.
∎

By combining the results in Lemma
4.2
and Theorem
5.11
,
we obtain the following stronger variant of Lemma
5.6
,
in which the improved version of the weighted norm inequality
holds for all functions
f\in L^{p-\varepsilon}(w\,d\mu)
.

Corollary 5.13
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and let
w
be a weight in
X
.
Assume that there exists a constant
C_{1}
such that

\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\leq C_{1}\int_{X}f(x)^{p}w(x)\,d\mu(x)

for all nonnegative functions
f\in L^{\infty}(X)
with a bounded support.
Then there exist constants
{\varepsilon}={\varepsilon}(c_{\mu},p,C_{1})
and
C=C(c_{\mu},p,C_{1})

such that
0<{\varepsilon}<p-1
and

\int_{X}\bigl({M}^{*}f(x)\bigr)^{p-{\varepsilon}}w(x)\,d\mu(x)\leq C\int_{X}\lvert f(x)\rvert^{p-{\varepsilon}}w(x)\,d\mu(x)

for all
f\in L^{p-\varepsilon}(w\,d\mu)
.

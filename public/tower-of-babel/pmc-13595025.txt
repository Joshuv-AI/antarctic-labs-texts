18 | Abnormal sFlt‐1/PlGF During Pregnancy and Subjective Memory Impairment 3 to 6 Years Postpartum

Pablo Garcia‐Manau1; Judit Platero1; Noah Costa2; Elisa Llurba1

1Hospital de la Santa Creu i Sant Pau, Barcelona, Spain; 2Institut de Recerca Sant Pau, Barcelona, Spain

Objective: To evaluate whether preeclampsia and angiogenic imbalance during pregnancy, defined by a soluble fms‐like tyrosine kinase‐1/placental growth factor ratio ≥38, are associated with subjective memory impairment 3 to 6 years postpartum.

Study Design: Cross‐sectional study at a tertiary hospital in Barcelona, Spain. Individuals were prospectively recruited during pregnancy and reevaluated 3 to 6 years postpartum. Preeclampsia was defined per American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists criteria. Angiogenic imbalance during pregnancy was defined as soluble fms‐like tyrosine kinase‐1/placental growth factor ≥38 (determined between 28 and 40 weeks of gestation). Subjective memory performance was assessed using the validated Memory Failures of Everyday Life questionnaire. Memory impairment was defined as a total Memory Failures of Everyday Life score ≥36. Logistic and linear regression models were used to examine associations, adjusting for relevant confounders.

Results: A total of 266 individuals were reevaluated between August 2023 and February 2025. 81 of them (30.45%) had a documented history of preeclampsia. Participants with an elevated soluble fms‐like tyrosine kinase‐1/placental growth factor ratio during pregnancy showed a higher prevalence of memory impairment (30.0% vs 16.2%, p = .03). In multivariable analysis, angiogenic imbalance remained significantly associated with increased odds of memory impairment (odds ratio = 2.18, 95% confidence interval [1.02–4.65], p = .04). In contrast, preeclampsia diagnosis alone was not significantly associated with memory outcomes (odds ratio = 1.35, 95% confidence interval [0.70–2.60], p = .37).

Conclusion: An elevated soluble fms‐like tyrosine kinase‐1/placental growth factor ratio during pregnancy is associated with an increased risk of subjective memory impairment 3 to 6 years postpartum. These findings highlight the potential utility of angiogenic biomarkers as early indicators of long‐term cognitive vulnerability, supporting the need for longitudinal follow‐up and targeted preventive strategies in women exposed to angiogenic imbalance during pregnancy.

19 | Ophthalmic Artery Doppler and Carotid Intima‐Media Thickness 3–6 Years Postpartum After Placental Insufficiency

Pablo Garcia‐Manau1; Judit Platero1; Noah Costa2; Elisa Llurba1

1Hospital de la Santa Creu i Sant Pau, Barcelona, Spain; 2Institut de Recerca Sant Pau, Barcelona, Spain

Objective: To assess the association between vascular assessments (ophthalmic artery Doppler and carotid intima‐media thickness) 3–6 years postpartum and a history of preeclampsia and fetal growth restriction, and to evaluate if associations were impacted by an angiogenic imbalance during pregnancy.

Study Design: Cross‐sectional study, which included individuals prospectively recruited during their index pregnancy between 2018 and 2021 and re‐evaluated 3–6 years postpartum. Preeclampsia, fetal growth restriction, and sFlt‐1/PlGF values were defined from pregnancy data. Postpartum assessment included ophthalmic artery Doppler and carotid intima‐media thickness performed by a single operator. Multivariable linear regression models assessed associations between placental dysfunction, angiogenic imbalance, and vascular parameters, adjusting for maternal covariates.

Results: 354 participants were included, 148 with and 206 without a history of preeclampsia or fetal growth restriction. Both placental dysfunction and angiogenic imbalance during pregnancy were independently associated with a significantly higher ophthalmic artery peak systolic velocity ratio 3–6 years postpartum. Participants with a history of placental dysfunction showed higher values compared to those without [0.75 (0.67–0.81) vs. 0.69 (0.63–0.78), p = 0.03], as did those with an elevated sFlt‐1/PlGF ratio during pregnancy [0.76 (0.66–0.82) vs. 0.70 (0.64–0.78), p = 0.03]. When preeclampsia and fetal growth restriction were analyzed separately, the association remained significant for preeclampsia, whereas in the fetal growth restriction group, a significant difference was observed only in the right eye. No statistically significant differences were observed in carotid intima‐media thickness.

Conclusion: Both a history of angiogenic imbalance and a clinical placental dysfunction presentation (particularly preeclampsia) during pregnancy were associated with increased ophthalmic artery peak systolic velocity ratio 3–6 years postpartum, whereas carotid intima‐media thickness did not differ between the study groups.

20 | Adverse Perinatal Outcomes (APO) and Cardiovascular Disease (CVD) in Women 11 Years After Delivery

A. C. J. Ravelli1; Wessel Ganzevoort1; Christianne J. M. De Groot2; Ameen Abu‐Hanna1; Sanne J. Gordijn3; Marijke Timmermans4; J. P. S. Henriques1; Deborah N. Kalkman1; R. A. F. de Lind van Wijngaarden1; R. J. Burger1 On behalf of PCI and Cardiothoracic Surgery Registration Committees of the Netherlands Heart Registration (NHR).

1Amsterdam UMC, Amsterdam, Netherlands; 2Department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology, Amsterdam UMC, Amsterdam, Netherlands; 3UMCG, Groningen, Netherlands; 4NHR, NHR /Utrecht, Netherlands

Objective: The aim of this study was to examine the association between different types of adverse pregnancy outcomes (APOs) and subsequent cardiovascular morbidity and mortality (CVD) in women.

Study Design: We conducted a population‐based cohort study of nulliparous women with a singleton birth between 2000 and 2023. The outcomes were CVD and ischemic heart disease (IHD) including coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) and percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). Cox proportional hazard models were used to assess the associations between six APOs‐pre‐eclampsia (PE), hypertensive disease pregnancy (HDP), stillbirth, gestational diabetes (GDM), preterm birth (PTB) and fetal growth retardation (FGR)‐and subsequent CVD and IHD. We adjusted for maternal age at delivery, education, income, ethnicity and year of delivery.

Results: Among 1,669,981 nulliparous women, 26% experienced at least one APO. Overall, 47,872 women (2.9%) experienced a CVD event within 11 years after the first pregnancy. Women with any APO had approximately a twofold higher risk of CVD. Elevated risk was observed for all APOs including stillbirth (adjusted hazard ratio (aHR) 2.0 95% 1.9–2.3), GDM (aHR 1.8 95% 1.7–1.9), PTB (aHR 1.8 95% 1.7–1.8) and FGR (aHR 1.7 95% 1.7–1.8) (Figure 1). APOs were also associated with an increased risk for IHD n = 5326 women (0.32%); for PE and IHD (aHR 2.6 95% 2.2–3.4) and for FGR and IHD (2.4 95% 2.3–2.6). Women who experienced multiple APOs had a substantially higher risk of CVD. The strongest associations were observed for the combination of PE and stillbirth (aHR 5.7 95% 3.7–8.9) and HDP and GDM (aHR 4.5 95% 3.9–5.2) (Figure 2). Further adjustment slightly attenuated the hazard ratios.

Conclusion: Women who experienced an APO, including PE, HDP, stillbirth, GDM, PTB and FRG, have a significantly increased risk for early maternal CVD and IHD. The risk is further elevated among women with multiple APOs. Identification of high‐risk subgroups following an APO provides an opportunity for targeted preventive strategies to reduce early‐onset maternal CVD.

21 | High Parity and Long Term Maternal Vascular Morbidity: A Population‐Based Cohort Study

Dor Marciano1; Gil Gutvirtz2; Tamar Wainstock3; Roy Kessous2

1Soroka Medical Center, Ofakim, Israel; 2Soroka University Medical Center, Beer Sheva, Israel; 3Department of Epidemiology and Health Services Evaluation, Ben‐Gurion University of the Negev, Beer Sheva, Israel

Objective: Pregnancy induces metabolic and vascular changes; repeated pregnancies may compound these insults, leading to obesity, dyslipidemia, hypertension and endothelial dysfunction. Recent studies indicate that parity modestly increases maternal cardiovascular disease risk and is associated with higher blood pressure and adverse lipid profiles as the number of children increases. We hypothesized that high parity would be associated with increased long‐term maternal vascular morbidity, with risk rising across parity categories.

Study Design: A retrospective population‐based study including all women who delivered between 1991 and 2021 at a tertiary medical center. Parity was categorized into 1,2,3,4,5 and ≥6 births. The primary endpoint was hospitalization for vascular morbidity (central/ peripheral disease, aneurysm, arteritis/phlebitis, thrombosis/embolism, varices, lymphatic disease or hypertension) during follow up period. Kaplan–Meier curves and log‐rank tests compared event rates. Cox proportional hazards models adjusted for fertility treatments and ethnicity estimated hazard ratios versus primiparity.

Results: The cohort comprised 57,859 women, of whom 3024 (5.2%) had vascular morbidity. Event rates rose with parity: 5.6% (1 birth), 12.8% (2), 20.8% (3), 17.5% (4), 10.5% (5) and 32.8% (≥6). Cumulative incidence increased across groups (log‐rank p < 0.001, Figure). In adjusted models, risks were higher in parity two (aHR = 1.32; 95% CI 1.10–1.58, p = 0.003), three (1.91; 1.61–2.27, <0.001), four (2.37; 1.99–2.83, <0.001), five (2.84; 2.34–3.43, <0.001) and ≥6 (3.34; 2.80–3.98< 0.001). Fertility treatments conferred additional risk (aHR = 1.45; 1.19–1.78, <0.001); ethnicity had no significant effect (aHR = 1.01; 0.91–1.11, p = 0.87).

Conclusion: High gravidity independently predicts long‐term maternal vascular morbidity. Women with ≥6 births had more than a threefold higher risk of hospitalization compared with primiparous women. These findings highlight the cumulative vascular burden of repeated pregnancies and support targeted cardiovascular risk counselling for multiparous women.

22 | Cardiac Remodeling After Preeclampsia and Angiogenic Imbalance: a Cross‐Sectional Study 3 to 6 Years Postpartum

Pablo Garcia‐Manau1; Judit Platero1; Lina Guiomar Mendieta1; Manel Mendoza2; Erika Bonacina2; Elisa Llurba1

1Hospital de la Santa Creu i Sant Pau, Barcelona, Spain; 2Hospital Universitari Vall d'Hebron, Barcelona, Spain

Objective: To evaluate the impact of prior preeclampsia (PE) and angiogenic imbalance (sFlt‐1/PlGF ≥38) on cardiac remodeling 3 to 6 years postpartum.

Study Design: Cross‐sectional study conducted at Hospital de Sant Pau, including people prospectively recruited during their index pregnancy between 2018 and 2022. Participants were reevaluated 3 to 6 years postpartum. Data on maternal baseline characteristics were collected and transthoracic echocardiogram assessments were performed in participants with a history of PE and/or angiogenic imbalance and healthy controls. The relationship between maternal baseline characteristics and echocardiographic parameters was analyzed according to exposure group. Adjusted analyses were performed using multivariable linear regression.

Results: A total of 602 women were included, of whom 165 (27.4%) had a history of PE and 437 (72.6%) did not. PE was significantly associated with shorter mitral deceleration time (p = 0.046) and lower stroke volume (p = 0.02). Prior PE was also associated with greater left atrial diameter (p = 0.05). sFlt‐1/PlGF values during pregnancy were available in 487 participants (80.9%). Angiogenic imbalance (sFlt‐1/PlGF ≥38) was significantly associated with increased interventricular septal thickness (p < 0.01) and lower left ventricle (LV) outflow tract diameter (p = 0.01), right ventricle longitudinal diameter (p = 0.03), LV end‐diastolic volume (p = 0.03), estimated right atrial pressure (p = 0.01), cardiac output (p = 0.01), cardiac index (p = 0.03), stroke volume (p < 0.01) and indexed stroke volume (p < 0.01).

Conclusion: At 3 to 6 years postpartum, prior PE was associated with impaired diastolic function. Moreover, angiogenic imbalance during pregnancy was linked to more extensive alterations in cardiac structure and output, suggesting that angiogenic markers may improve the identification of women at risk of long‐term cardiovascular remodeling.

23 | Impact of Intrauterine Exposure to Antiphospholipid Antibodies on Early Childhood Growth and Neurodevelopment

Lilah Tsaitlin‐Mor1; Joshua Guedalia1; Michal Lipschuetz2; Sarah M. Cohen3; Adva Cahen‐Peretz4; Simcha Yagel1; Debra Goldman‐Wohl4; Galit Shefer5; Dvora Sehtman6; Liya Kerem1; Ronit Calderon‐Margalit6; Sofia Drawshy5; Ofer Beharier4

1Hadassah Hebrew University Medical Center, Jeruslaem, Israel; 2Henrietta Szold Hadassah Hebrew University School of Nursing in the Faculty of Medicine, Jerusalem, Israel; 3Faculty of Medicine, Hadassah‐Hebrew University, Jerusalem, Israel; 4Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, Hadassah Medical Center, Mt. Scopus, Jerusalem, Israel; 5Ministry of Health, Jerusalem, Israel; 6Hebrew University of Jerusalem, Jerusalem, Israel

Objective: To evaluate whether intrauterine exposure to aPL is associated with growth and neurodevelopmental delays during the first two years of life. Identifying potential long‐term consequences may enable earlier detection and intervention to optimize developmental outcomes.

Study Design: In this cohort study, of 467,406 term neonates born between January 2018 and January 2022, 3404 (0.73%) babies born to mothers with APLA were compared with 464,002 (99.27%) non‐APLA controls. Data on weight, head circumference, performance in developmental milestones in gross‐motor, fine‐motor, language, and personal‐social skills, and information on specialist referrals were collected by the Health Ministry during routine maternal and child health clinic visits.

Cox proportional‐hazards models were constructed to yield hazard ratios (HR) and 99% confidence intervals (CI), controlling for potential confounders.

Results: As anticipated, children born to mothers with antiphospholipid syndrome were more likely to be small for gestational age (SGA) (HR = 1.169, 99% CI: 1.015–1.346, p = 0.004). However, there were no significant differences in weight growth trajectories between children of APLA mothers and those of non‐APLA mothers. In terms of head circumference, children of APLA mothers exhibited a trend of minor stunted growth measurements until 9 months of age (HR = 1.19–1.18), with this difference mostly decreasing from birth to 9 months. There were no notable variations in developmental milestones or referral rates to specialists.

Conclusion: This nationwide study found no impact of intrauterine aPL exposure on early neurodevelopment or weight gain in full‐term infants. However, it identified a trend of reduced head circumference growth up to 9 months in offspring of mothers with aPL.

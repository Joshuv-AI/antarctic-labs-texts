A delayed interior area-to-height estimate for the Curve Shortening Flow
Arjun Sobnack

Abstract
The principle of delayed parabolic regularity for the Curve Shortening Flow—that if two evolving curves bound a region of area 𝒜 \mathcal{A} , then, starting from time 𝒜 / π \mathcal{A}/\pi , the regularity of one curve is controllable in terms of the time elapsed, the area 𝒜 \mathcal{A} and the regularity of the other curve—was proposed by Topping & the author in [ ST24a ] , where they also provided a number of graphical situations in which their delayed regularity framework is valid. In this paper, we generalise some of the results in [ ST24a ] within the graphical setting, ultimately by showing that there holds an interior graphical estimate for the Curve Shortening Flow in the spirit of the proposed framework. We also provide a few applications of our estimate, such as the existence of Graphical Curve Shortening Flows starting weakly from Radon measures without point masses.

Introduction

In its classical form, the
Curve Shortening Flow (CSF)
takes a smooth embedding of the circle (a smooth
loop
) in the Euclidean plane and evolves it in the direction of its geodesic curvature vector.
The CSF is the instance of the
Mean Curvature Flow (MCF)
in the lowest non-trivial dimensions; in particular, it arises as the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
–gradient flow of the length functional.

Following on from Huisken’s seminal paper (
[
Hui84
]
) on the MCF of smooth, closed and mean-convex hyper-surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
for
n\geq 2
, Gage & Hamilton tackled the question of the long-time behaviour of the CSF, i.e. the case of
n=1
.
In
[
GH86
]
they showed that the evolution
[0,T)\ni t\mapsto\gamma(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
of a given
convex
smooth loop
\gamma_{0}=:\gamma(0)
exists for a maximal time
T<\infty
dictated only by the area of the region enclosed by
\gamma_{0}
.
Moreover, they precisely described the behaviour of the evolution of the loop as time approaches
T
: the curve
\gamma(t)
smoothly approaches a circle of radius
{\sqrt{2(T-t)}}
in the sense of blow-ups as
t\nearrow T
.
Shortly after, Grayson showed in
[
Gra87
]
that the evolution of an
arbitrary
smooth loop in the Euclidean plane becomes convex before encountering any singularities, at which point Gage & Hamilton’s work applies.

Collectively,
[
GH86
,
Gra87
]
is known and celebrated as the Gage–Grayson–Hamilton Theorem.
Grayson subsequently weakened, in
[
Gra89
]
, the assumption of planarity of the initial curve, an extension often referred to as Grayson’s Theorem.

The CSF has been studied in a variety of different scenarios.
The focus of this paper is the setting of CSFs which are, at each time, expressible as graphs over a fixed line or fixed line segment.
In this setting, the weakly parabolic system for the evolution of a curve reduces to a parabolic equation for the
graphing function
.

Some of the pioneering works in this direction are
[
EH89
,
EH91
]
due to Ecker & Huisken.
They showed that, given any

graph
\gamma_{0}=\mathrm{graph}(u_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
of a locally Lipschitz function
u_{0}:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
, there exists a CSF
(0,\infty)\ni t\mapsto\gamma(t)=\graph(u(\,\cdot\,,t)\!)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, defined for all time, which attains the initial datum
\gamma_{0}
locally uniformly, and which remains graphical over the same line; we call this flow the the
Ecker–Huisken flow of
u_{0}
.
They achieved their existence theory by appealing directly to the
Graphical Curve Shortening Flow (GCSF)
equation, which the graphing function
u:\mathbb{R}\times(0,T)\mapsto\mathbb{R}
necessarily satisfies.
A key step in their proof is to establish
a priori
estimates for the GCSF equation of smooth graphing functions, which propagate an initial local Lipschitz bound forward to the CSF, provided one moves in a little.
Such bounds are stable under locally uniform limits, and thus allowed Ecker & Huisken to construct solutions via an approximation procedure.

Around the same time, Angenent developed his
Intersection Principle
(see
[
Ang88
]
) and used it in
[
Ang91
]
to construct solutions to the CSF starting from Lipschitz loops in the uniform sense.
As in Ecker & Huisken’s work, a key step is to establish an
a priori
estimate for smooth loops which propagates a Lipschitz bound forward to the flow.
Angenent introduced a new and remarkably geometric technique to establish his estimates, which has found much success in other situations—see, for example,
[
Ilm92
,
CK01
,
Clu04
,
NT05
,
BR24
]
, and more.
In particular, Topping & the author used Angenent’s technique in
[
ST24a
]
to establish sharp
delayed
estimates for the GCSF, which fit into the framework to be recalled in §
1.1
.

Chou & Zhu used (amongst other insights) a combination of Ecker & Huisken and Angenent’s work to establish, in
[
CZ98
]
, that the CSF can be started, in the locally uniform sense, from any properly embedded and locally Lipschitz planar curve
which splits the plane into two regions of infinite area, and that their resulting flow exists for all time.
Their result is the state-of-the-art in terms of the existence theory for non-compact and non-graphical curves.
They also proved the state-of-the-art uniqueness result for CSFs that evolve through curves whose
ends remain graphical
.
Given a curve
\gamma_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
(of the regularity considered in
[
CZ98
]
) with graphical ends, we call the unique solution to the CSF through curves whose ends remain graphical
the
Chou–Zhu flow of
\gamma_{0}
.

The running theme in the works
[
EH89
,
EH91
,
Ang91
]
above is that a strong regularity condition is shown to be preserved by the CSF.
In the past three decades, significant progress has been made in the graphical situation towards
instantaneous
estimates, which take a weak initial regularity constraint and deduce a strong regularity condition on the CSF for strictly positive times.
Evans & Spruck established in
[
ES92
]
(see also
[
CM04
]
) that an
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}
–bound on an initial graphing function induces an instantaneous
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\infty}
–bound on the gradient of the subsequent flow, provided one moves in a little.
Their result was
later extended, independently, by Clutterbuck and Nagase & Tonegawa in
[
Clu04
]
and
[
NT05
]
respectively;
in particular, Clutterbuck used her generalisation to establish an existence theory for a family of parabolic equations which contains graphical instances of the CSF and MCF.
For
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}
–bounds when
1<p<\infty
, Chou & Kwong established in
[
CK20
]
an
a priori
estimate which takes an initial
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}
–bound on a graphing function instantaneously to a
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}
–bound on the gradient of the subsequent flow, provided one moves in a little; they used their estimate to establish the existence of GCSFs starting from graphs of
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}
–initial data.
They also established a uniqueness result from
\mathrm{L}^{p}
–initial data, building on Chou & Zhu’s uniqueness theory (
[
CZ98
]
); see also
[
DS23
]
.

For the non-graphical case, see, for example, the works of Angenent (
[
Ang90
]
; loosely, initial curves whose curvature is
\mathrm{L}^{p}
for some
p\gg 1
) and Lauer (
[
Lau13
]
; finite-length Jordan curves).

One of the aims of this paper is to extend the existence theory to include, loosely, graphs of Radon measures without point masses.
In some sense, the strategy we follow is familiar: we first establish an
a priori
estimate for smooth initial data which is stable under
weak convergence of measures
, and then use an approximation procedure to pass to a non-smooth limit.
However, the
a priori
estimate we establish is pronouncedly
unfamiliar
in comparison to the previously-mentioned estimates in
[
GH86
,
EH89
,
EH91
,
Ang90
,
Ang91
,
ES92
,
Lau13
]
etc.,
as well as, to the author’s best knowledge, all other estimates (except, of course, for those in
[
ST24a
]
) from the CSF literature,
which, recall, start to hold
instantaneously
.
Our estimate is a
delayed
parabolic
a priori
estimate, and in our setting it is, in fact,
impossible
for the estimate to hold
before
a so-called
magic time
, which is determined completely by the ‘area’ under the initial graph.
In particular, we apply our estimate slightly differently to the applications in, for example,
[
EH89
,
EH91
,
Ang90
,
Ang91
,
Lau13
,
Clu04
,
CK20
]
.

Our estimates are reminiscent of those established by Topping & Yin in
[
TY17
]
for the two–dimensional
Ricci Flow (RF)
, and indeed there is a bridge between their work and ours—see Remark
2.34
.
Consequently, our existence theory is analogous to that of Topping & Yin’s (
[
TY24a
]
) for the RF.

1.1
A novel framework

In
[
ST24a
]
(see also
[
ST24b
]
), Topping & the author proposed a version of the following framework by which the CSF regularises a curve:

Given two smooth families
[0,T)\ni t\mapsto\gamma^{-}(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
[0,T)\ni t\mapsto\gamma^{+}(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
of properly embedded curves in the Euclidean plane evolving under the Curve Shortening Flow,
for which
\gamma^{-}(t)
and
\gamma^{+}(t)
are disjoint, are either both compact or both non-compact (for all
t\in[0,T)
) and bound an evolving connected region of fixed and finite area
\mathcal{A}
,
define the
magic time
to be
t_{\star}:=\mathcal{A}/\pi
.
Then, after flowing beyond the magic time
t_{\star}
,
one expects to start being able to control the regularity of
\gamma^{+}(t)
for times
t_{\star}<t\ll T
in terms of the time
t-t_{\star}
elapsed, the area
\mathcal{A}
and the regularity of
\gamma^{-}(t)
.

Several situations in which the framework above holds exactly as stated were provided in
[
ST24a
]
.
Most relevant to this paper is the case where
t\mapsto\gamma^{-}(t)
is the static flow of the
x
–axis:

Theorem 1.1

(Delayed
\mathrm{L}^{1}
-to-
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
estimate;
[
ST24a
, Theorem 1.5]
)
.

Let
u:\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty)\mapsto\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a smooth
positive
Graphical Curve Shortening Flow starting from
u_{0}:=u(\,\cdot\,,0)\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.
Let

(1.2)

\mathcal{A}:=\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}=\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}u_{0}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

and define the
magic time
to be

(1.3)

t_{\star}:=\frac{\mathcal{A}}{\pi}.

Then there exists a universal (that is,
independent
of
u
) and decreasing function
H:(\frac{1}{\pi},\infty)\mapsto(0,\infty)
for which

(1.4)

\|u(\,\cdot\,,t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\sqrt{t}\cdot H\left(\frac{t}{\mathcal{A}}\right)

for all
t>t_{\star}
.

Recall that a GCSF is a smooth function
u:J\times(0,T)\mapsto\mathbb{R}
which satisfies the quasi-linear parabolic equation

(GCSF)

\partial_{t}u=\frac{1}{1+(\partial_{x}u)^{2}}\partial^{2}_{x}u=\partial_{x}[\tan^{-1}(\partial_{x}u)],

where
J\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is an interval.
A solution to (
GCSF
) induces a family of curves
(0,T)\ni t\mapsto\gamma(t)=\graph(u(\,\cdot\,,t)\!)
which solve the CSF equation

(CSF)

\left\langle\partial_{t}\gamma,\bm{n}\right\rangle=\kappa,

where the geodesic curvature vector
\bm{\kappa}(t)=\kappa(t)\bm{n}(t)
of
\gamma(t)
decomposes as the product of the geodesic curvature
\kappa(t)
with the unit normal
\bm{n}(t)
.
Conversely, given a solution to (
CSF
) which happens to be graphical, its graphing function solves (
GCSF
).

When an initial condition, say
u_{0}=:u(\,\cdot\,,0)
for (
GCSF
) or
\gamma_{0}=:\gamma(0)
for (
CSF
), is imposed, the regularity of the initial datum determines the mode in which the flow converges backward in time.
For example, in Chou & Kwong’s work
[
CK20
]
, since
u_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
for some
p>1
, they insist that
u(\,\cdot\,,t)\to u_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
as
t\searrow 0
.
In the case that
\gamma_{0}
is continuous, we write
\gamma:J\times[0,T)\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{2}
or
[0,T)\ni t\mapsto\gamma(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
In the case that
u_{0}
is continuous, we write
u\in\mathrm{C}^{0}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,T)\!)
.
And in the case that
u_{0}
is smooth, we write
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,T)\!)
.

In
[
ST24a
]
, Topping & the author also provided the following construction, which shows that the proposed
magic time

t_{\star}=\mathcal{A}/\pi
is sharp:

Theorem 1.5

(Variant of
[
ST24a
, Theorems 1.1 and 1.9]
; see
[
Sob24
, Theorem 5.2.21]
)
.

There exists a sequence
(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty);\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
of Graphical Curve Shortening Flows, with

(1.6)

\|v_{n}(\,\cdot\,,0)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}=1

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that, for any
0\leq s\leq t_{\star}=1/\pi<t<\infty
, there holds

(1.7)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}v_{n}(0,s)=\infty\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|v_{n}(\,\cdot\,,t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty.

As applications of Theorem
1.1
, Topping & the author gave a new proof of an instantaneous
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-to-
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
–estimate (
[
ST24a
, Theorem 1.10]
), where
1<p<\infty
, and established the existence of GCSFs starting from
\mathrm{L}^{1}
–initial datum (
[
ST24a
, Theorem 1.11]
).
The existence result in this paper is a generalisation of the existence result of Topping & the author, but uses a slightly different method.

1.2
Main results

Throughout this paper, we use the notation
J_{r}(a)
to mean the interval
(a-r,a+r)\subset\mathbb{R}
thought of as a spatial domain.
We also abbreviate
J_{r}(0)
to
J_{r}
.
Any instance of a
J
without the decoration as before will refer to an interval of unspecified length, centre or closedness.
This is done purely to distinguish the spatial and temporal domains at the level of notation.

Given a spatial domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\left\{\infty\right\}
we denote by
\mathrm{C}^{k}(\Omega)
the space of
k
–times differentiable functions all of whose derivatives are bounded and uniformly continuously, and for each pair
(k,\alpha)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\times(0,1]
we denote by
\mathrm{C}^{k,\alpha}(\Omega)\subset\mathrm{C}^{k}(\Omega)
the sub-space of functions whose
k
th
derivative is
\alpha
–Hölder continuous.
We denote by
\mathrm{C}^{k}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
and
\mathrm{C}^{k,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
their respective local spaces.
For a space-time domain
\mathcal{D}
, for example
\mathcal{D}=J_{1}\times(0,T)
, we denote by
\mathrm{C}^{k;l}(\mathcal{D})
and
\mathrm{C}^{k,\alpha;l,\beta}(\mathcal{D})
the corresponding parabolic versions of the respective spaces before, and by
\mathrm{C}^{k;l}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{D})
and
\mathrm{C}^{k,\alpha;l,\beta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{D})
their localised counterparts.
This notation is consistent with the notation introduced in §
1.1
to specify the mode in which a GCSF attains it initial datum.

Given any function
f:\Omega\times\mathcal{I}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
of space-time, where
\mathcal{I}
is one of
(0,T)
,
[0,T)
,
(0,T]
or
[0,T]
, we denote the
t
–time slice of
f
, for each
t\in\mathcal{I}
, by
f(t):=f(\,\cdot\,,t):\Omega\mapsto\mathbb{R}
.
If
\mathcal{X}
is a function space for which
f(t)\in\mathcal{X}
for each
t\in\mathcal{I}
, and the mapping
\mathcal{I}\ni t\mapsto f(t)\in\mathcal{X}
is continuous, then we write
f\in\mathrm{C}^{0}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{I};\mathcal{X})
.

In this paper, we generalise some of the results in
[
ST24a
]
—namely Theorem
1.1
, the
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-to-
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
–estimate and the
\mathrm{L}^{1}
–existence result—by demonstrating that a local, but still graphical, version of Theorem
1.1
holds.
The crucial new localised estimate is

Theorem 1.8

(Delayed
\mathrm{L}^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
-to-
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}
–estimate)
.

Let
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(J_{1}\times[0,T)\!)
be a Graphical Curve Shortening Flow starting from
u_{0}:=u(0)=u(\,\cdot\,,0)\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(J_{1})
, and suppose that

(1.9)

t_{\star}:=\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(J_{1})}}{\pi}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{x\in J_{1}}|u_{0}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x<T.

Then for all
\delta>0
, there exists a constant
C=C(\delta)<\infty
such that the bound

(1.10)

|u(0,t)|\leq C+\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(J_{1})}+\frac{\pi t}{2}

holds for all
t<T
satisfying

(1.11)

t\geq(1+\delta)\cdot t_{\star}.

The construction of Theorem
1.5
also applies in the local setting of Theorem
1.8
, and consequently the
magic time

t_{\star}=\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(J_{1})}/\pi
is sharp here too.
We prove Theorem
1.8
in §
3
.

For generalisations of a different flavour, the interested reader is directed to, for example,
[
ST26
]
.
Topping & the author expect
[
ST24b
]
to play a role in further generalisations.

Once a height bound has been established, Evans & Spruck’s estimate (
[
ES92
]
) implies that an interior gradient bound holds an instant later, at which point standard parabolic regularity theory (see e.g.
[
LSU88
,
Kry96
]
) can be used to deduce that higher-order bounds hold after one more instant.
For details, see
[
Sob24
]
.
We shall use this argument in the proof of our existence result, which will be stated momentarily.

One advantage of the local estimate (
1.10
) from Theorem
1.8
is that it is applicable to CSFs that are only
locally
graphical.
Specifically, provided one ensures that an arc of a CSF remains a graph over a fixed line segment for long enough, our estimate implies local gradient bounds, and consequently higher-order derivative bounds.
In particular, one can deduce higher-order covariant derivative bounds on the geodesic curvature, which are by nature geometric as well as analytic.

Our delayed interior area-to-height estimate (Theorem
1.8
) is a consequence of the
Harnack inequality
for our local
Harnack quantity
from §
2
, which is adapted from the global Harnack quantity of Topping & the author in
[
ST24a
]
.
Recall that the global Harnack quantity in
[
ST24a
]
is used more-or-less directly on the class of GCSFs for which the height estimates of Theorem
1.1
are claimed.
By constrast, our local Harnack quantity is
not
defined for general GCSFs, but is instead defined on the subclass of
local GCSFs
.
Our local GCSFs will act as barries for our general GCSFs, and, in particular, height bounds obtained via our Harnack inequality on the former will imply the height bounds desired by Theorem
1.8
on the latter.

As an application of Theorem
1.8
, in §
4
we relatively swiftly deduce an
instantaneous

\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}
-to-
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}
estimate for
1<p<\infty
of a form originally due to Chou & Kwong in
[
CK20
]
.
Our proof has the bonus of clarifying the dependencies of the constant
C
below.

Theorem 1.12

(Instantaneous
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}
-to-
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}
–estimate for
p>1
; cf.
[
CK20
, Proposition 2.1]
)
.

Let
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(J_{1}\times[0,T)\!)
be a Graphical Curve Shortening Flow starting from
u_{0}:=u(0)\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(J_{1})
for some
1<p<\infty
.
Then for some universal (that is,
independent
of
p
and
u
) constant
C<\infty
there holds

(1.13)

|u(0,t)|\leq C\left(1+\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(J_{1})}^{p/(p-1)}}{t^{1/(p-1)}}+t\right)

for all
t\in(0,T)
.

By combining Theorem
1.12
with Evan & Spruck’s estimate (
[
ES92
]
), one can deduce an instantaneous
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}
–estimate for the gradient of a GCSF starting from an
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}
–function, where
1<p<\infty
.
Informally, the gradient estimates assert that
\log(|\partial_{x}u(0,t)|)
blows up like
t^{-(p+1)/(p-1)}
for times
t\ll 1
and grows linearity for times
t\gg 1
.

As another application of Theorem
1.8
, in §
5
we construct solutions which start from
non-atomic real–valued Radon measures
in the
weak
sense.
We denote the space of such measures by
\mathcal{M}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
, and refer to App.
B
for further details, in particular the notion of weak convergence in the larger space
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
.

Theorem 1.14

(Existence from
\mathcal{M}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
–initial data)
.

Let
\nu=u_{0}\mathscr{L}^{1}+\nu_{\mathrm{sing}}\in\mathcal{M}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
be a non-atomic real-valued Radon measure, decomposed into its absolutely continuous
\nu_{\mathrm{ac}}=:u_{0}\mathscr{L}^{1}
and its singular
\nu_{\mathrm{sing}}
part; here
\mathscr{L}^{1}
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\Omega:=\mathbb{R}\setminus\supp(\nu_{\mathrm{sing}})
and suppose that
u_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
for some
1\leq p<\infty
(recall that the weakest case of
p=1
always holds by definition).
Then there exists a Graphical Curve Shortening Flow
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)\!)
which observes the following modes of convergence as
t\searrow 0
:

(1.15)

\displaystyle u(t)\mathscr{L}^{1}\rightharpoonup\nu\qquad

\displaystyle\text{ weakly in }\,\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})

(1.16)

\displaystyle u(t)\to u_{0}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{ strongly in }\,\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega).

We also take the opportunity to comment on some of Chou & Kwong’s results from
[
CK20
]
.
In the case of
\Omega=\mathbb{R}
in Theorem
1.14
,

•

if
u_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
, then the convergence (
1.16
) holds globally, i.e.

(1.17)

u(t)\to u_{0}\qquad\textit{ strongly in }\,\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}).

•

if
p>1
, then the solution is unique;
in fact, the mapping
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})\ni u_{0}\mapsto u\in\mathrm{C}^{0}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty);\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})\!)
is well-defined and continuous—see Theorem

.

In the final section (§
6
) of the paper, we define a class of GCSFs which arises naturally from our existence theory, and use it to further a correspondence between GCSFs and measures initiated by Chou & Kwong in

]
.

]

Our method is to reduce to the case of a local GCSF, and then use our local Harnack inequality (Theorem
2.24

We start by making two reductions.
First, we claim that it suffices to consider GCSFs which extend to the boundary.

.

1.8

5

In this section, we shall prove Theorem

, that from any

Our proof consists of two main steps: the construction of the flow, and the convergence back to the initial condition.

5.1
Constructing the flow

We start with a sequence

and

, and which is chosen to satisfy
|u^{n}_{0}|\leq U^{n}_{0}
on
\mathbb{R}
for each

.
We can construct such sequences by cutting-off and mollifying the positive and negative parts of
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
simultaneously, and then recombining the mollifications (see e.g.
[
Rud91
, Ch. 6]
).

Let

The Avoidance Principle (Theorem
A.7
) applied to

By the Arzelà–Ascoli Theorem, it suffices to prove

Recall from (

∎

We now pass to a common sub-sequence of indices
(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3},\dots)
to extract limits
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)\!)
and
U\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty);\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
.
The ordering
|u(t)|\leq U(t)
on
\mathbb{R}
for every
t\in(0,\infty)
is preserved in the limit.

The construction of the flow is now complete.

5.2
Convergence to the initial datum

We continue the proof of Theorem
1.14
by showing that the solution
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)\!)
constructed in §
5.1
converges to the initial datum in the senses of (
1.15
)–(
1.16
).

We handle the weak convergence first.
As in the analogous case of the two–dimensional RF in
[
TY24a
]
, we shall do this via an area estimate.

Proposition 5.17

(cf.
[
TY24a
, Lemma 4.5]
)
.

Let
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(J\times(0,T)\!)
be a GCSF and let
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(J)
.
Then

(5.18)

\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{y\in J}\varphi(y)u(y,\cdot\,)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\right|\leq C(\varphi):=\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{y\in J}|\partial_{x}\varphi(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y.

Proof.

Differentiating under the integral and writing
v=\partial_{x}u
, we have that

(5.19)

\begin{aligned} \left|\,\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right.\int_{y\in J}\varphi(y)u(y,\cdot)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\right|&=\left|\,\int_{y\in J}\varphi(y)\cdot\partial_{x}[\tan^{-1}(v)](y,s)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\right|\\
&=\left|\,\int_{y\in J}\partial_{x}\varphi(y)\cdot[\tan^{-1}(v)](y,s)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\right|\\
&\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{y\in J}|\partial_{x}\varphi(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y.\end{aligned}\qed

Proof of (
1.15
).

Let
(t_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,\infty)
be an arbitrary sequence with
t_{m}\to 0
as
m\nearrow\infty
.
We need to show that (
B.1
) holds with
\nu_{m}=u(t_{m})\mathscr{L}^{1}
for all
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}^{0}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R})
.

Suppose for now that
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
.
Applying Proposition
5.17
to
u^{n}
and integrating over
t\in[0,t_{m}]
gives that

(5.20)

\left|\,\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi(y)u^{n}(y,t_{m})\,\mathrm{d}y-\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi(y)u^{n}_{0}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\right|\leq C(\varphi)t_{m}.

By taking
n\nearrow\infty
in (
5.20
), using the local smooth convergence
u^{n}(t_{m})\to u(t_{m})
and the weak convergence
u^{n}_{0}\mathscr{L}^{1}\rightharpoonup\nu
, we get that

(5.21)

\left|\,\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi(y)u(y,t_{m})\,\mathrm{d}y-\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi(y)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(y)\,\right|\leq C(\varphi)t_{m}.

The right-hand side of (
5.21
) clearly converges to zero as
m\nearrow\infty
.

In particular, by replacing
u
with
U
and by replacing
\varphi
with any
\chi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(J;\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
for which
C(\chi)=\pi
, we get from (
5.21
) that

(5.22)

\int_{y\in J}\chi(y)U(y,t_{m})\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\int_{y\in J}\chi(y)\,\mathrm{d}|\nu|(y)+\pi t_{m}.

Taking
\chi\nearrow\chi_{J}
gives by the Monotone Convergence Theorem that

(5.23)

\|U(t_{m})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(J)}\leq|\nu|(J)+\pi t_{m}.

For a general
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}^{0}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R})
, we approximate by a smooth
\widetilde{\varphi}\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(J)
where
\supp(\varphi)\Subset J\Subset\mathbb{R}
.
For see that

(5.24)

\displaystyle\left|\,\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi(y)u(y,t_{m})\,\mathrm{d}y-\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi(y)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(y)\right|

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle\leq\left|\,\int_{y\in J}[\varphi(y)-\widetilde{\varphi}(y)]u(y,t_{m})\,\mathrm{d}y\,\right|

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle+\left|\,\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\varphi}(y)u(y,t_{m})\,\mathrm{d}y-\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\varphi}(y)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(y)\right|

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle+\left|\,\int_{y\in J}[\widetilde{\varphi}(y)-\varphi(y)]\,\mathrm{d}\nu(y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi-\widetilde{\varphi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(J)}\big[\|u(t_{m})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(J)}+|\nu|(J)\big]

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle+\left|\,\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\varphi}(y)u(y,t_{m})\,\mathrm{d}y-\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\varphi}(y)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(y)\right|.\!

The last term in (
5.24
) converges to zero as
m\nearrow\infty
, so it suffices to show that the second-last term can be chosen to be arbitrarily small for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
By the presence of the factor of
\|\varphi-\widetilde{\varphi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(J)}
, it suffices to show that
\|u(t_{m})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(J)}+|\nu|(J)
can be chosen to be bounded for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
But by (
5.23
), this is clearly the case.
∎

The strong convergence (
1.16
) is a modification of part of
[
CK20
, Theorem A]
; we recall their argument for completeness.
The key tool is the following
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}
–separation estimate:

Proposition 5.25

(Contained in
[
CK20
, Proposition 3.1]
)
.

Let
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(J_{R}\times(0,T)\!)
be two GCSFs defined on
J_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}
.
Then for all
0<r<R
and
\delta>0
, there exists a cut-off function
\chi\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(J_{R};[0,1])
with
\chi=1
on
J_{r}\Subset J_{R}
such that, for all
1\leq p<\infty
, there holds the estimate

(5.26)

\|(u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t)\!)\chi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(J_{R})}\leq\|(u_{1}(s)-u_{2}(s)\!)\chi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(J_{R})}+\frac{2\pi(1+\delta)p}{(R-r)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}(t-s)

for all
0<s\leq t<T
.

Sketch proof.

Choose
\chi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(J_{R};[0,1])
to satisfy
\chi(y)=1
for all
y\in J_{r}
and
|\partial_{x}\chi(y)|\leq\frac{1+\delta}{R-r}\chi_{J_{R}\setminus J_{r}}(y)
for all
y\in J_{R}
.
Noting that
|\!\tan^{-1}(v)|\leq\pi/2
for all
v\in\mathbb{R}
, following the proof of
[
CK20
, Proposition 3.1]
leads to (
5.26
).
∎

Remark 5.27
.

In the case of
p=1
and
R=1
in Proposition
5.25
, by taking
r\nearrow 1
and
\delta\searrow 0
in (
5.26
), we deduce that

(5.28)

\|u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(J_{1})}\leq\|u_{1}(s)-u_{2}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(J_{1})}+2\pi(t-s).

By letting
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
be disjoint Grim Reaper solutions (
2.3
) translating away from each other, one can see that (
5.28
) is sharp.

Proof of (
1.16
).

We shall apply Proposition
5.25
in following weakened form:
For all
0<r<R
and
1\leq p<\infty
, there exists a constant
C_{p,R-r}
for which

(5.29)

\|u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(J_{r})}\leq\|u_{1}(s)-u_{2}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(J_{R})}+C_{p,R-r}(t-s)

for all
0<s\leq t<T
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\Subset\Omega
be compact; then as
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is open, and therefore a countable union of disjoint open intervals, it follows that
\mathcal{K}
is contained within a
finite
union of open intervals.
Thus, it suffices to prove that
u(t)\to u_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(J)
for a single open interval
J\Subset\Omega
.
By translation, we may suppose that
J=J_{r}\Subset J_{R}\subseteq\Omega
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
.
We first choose
n\in\mathbb{N}
so large that

(5.30)

\|u^{n}_{0}-u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(J_{R})}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

With this
n
fixed, we then choose
t_{\varepsilon}>0
so small that

(5.31)

\|u^{n}(t)-u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(J_{r})}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\|u^{n}(t)-u^{n}_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(J_{r})}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}

for all
0<t<t_{\varepsilon}
, using (
5.29
) and the locally smooth convergence
u^{n}(t)\to u^{n}_{0}
as
t\searrow 0
respectively.
Taken altogether, we get that

(5.32)

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(J_{r})}

\displaystyle\leq\|u(t)-u^{m}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(J_{r})}+\|u^{m}(t)-u^{m}_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(J_{r})}+\|u^{m}_{0}-u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(J_{r})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}=\varepsilon

for all
0<t<t_{\varepsilon}
.
∎

The proofs of the convergences (
1.15
)–(
1.16
) is now complete.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.14
.

5.3
Some comments on the work of Chou & Kwong

First, we clarify that if the initial datum
\nu=u_{0}\mathscr{L}^{1}\in\mathcal{M}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
has density
u_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for some
1\leq p<\infty
, then the solution
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)\!)
constructed in Theorem
1.14
attains its initial datum in
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof of (
1.17
).

We appeal to (
5.29
).
Recall that (
1.16
) already shows the
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}
–convergence.
By setting
u_{1}=u
and
u_{2}=0
and letting
s\searrow 0
, we get that

(5.33)

\|u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(J_{r})}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(J_{R})}+C_{p,R-r}t.

Letting
r:=R-1\nearrow\infty
gives us that

(5.34)

\|u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}+C_{p,1}t,

and so, in particular,
u(t)\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for all
t\in[0,\infty)
and

(5.35)

\limsup_{t\searrow 0}\|u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}.

The following measure-theoretic lemma is straightforward to prove (see e.g.
[
Sob24
, §4.3]
):

Lemma 5.36
.

Let
(f_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
be a sequence of functions for some fixed
1\leq p<\infty
, and let
f\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
.
Suppose that

f_{m}\to f\in\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\limsup_{m\nearrow\infty}\|f_{m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}.

Then

(5.37)

f_{m}\to f\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}).

Applying Lemma
5.36
to
f_{m}:=u(t_{m})
and
f:=u_{0}
for an arbitrary sequence
(t_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,\infty)
converging to zero, keeping in mind (
1.16
) and (
5.35
), gives the desired global convergence (
1.17
).
∎

Second, we highlight that Proposition
5.25
implies the following continuity property for the GCSF in
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}
for
1<p<\infty
:

Theorem 5.38

(Continuous dependence on
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}
–initial data when
p>1
)
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, and let
u_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
.
Let
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)\!)
be any GCSF for which
u(t)\to u(0):=u_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
as
t\searrow 0
.
Then
u\in\mathrm{C}^{0}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty);\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})\!)
, and the mapping
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})\ni u_{0}\mapsto u\in\mathrm{C}^{0}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty);\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})\!)
is well-defined and continuous; in particular, for each
u_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
, the associated solution
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)\!)
is unique.

Proof.

It is immediate that
u\in\mathrm{C}^{0}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\!(0,\infty);\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})\!)
; the continuity down to the initial time is precisely the statement that the GCSF attains its initial datum in
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
.

The rest of the theorem follows from Proposition
5.25
.
Fix
J_{r}\Subset\mathbb{R}
and
[a,b]\Subset[0,\infty)
, and let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary.
Choose
R>0
so large that

(5.39)

r<\frac{R}{2}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\frac{2^{3-\frac{1}{p}}\pi pt}{R^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}\leq\frac{8\pi pb}{R^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

the latter for all
t\in[a,b]
.
Suppose that
\widetilde{u}_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
is such that

(5.40)

\|u_{0}-\widetilde{u}_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(J_{R})}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

and let
\widetilde{u}\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)\!)
be any GCSF starting from
\widetilde{u}_{0}
in the
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
–sense.
Setting
\delta=1
,
u^{1}=u
and
u^{2}=\widetilde{u}
in Proposition
5.25
, we get for every non-zero
t\in[a,b]
that

(5.41)

\displaystyle\|u(t)-\widetilde{u}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(J_{r})}

\displaystyle\leq\|u(t)-\widetilde{u}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(J_{R/2})}

\displaystyle\leq\|u(s)-\widetilde{u}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(J_{R})}+\frac{4\pi p(t-s)}{(R/2)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}

\displaystyle\to\|u_{0}-\widetilde{u}_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(J_{R})}+\frac{2^{3-\frac{1}{p}}\pi pt}{R^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}=\varepsilon

as
s\searrow 0
, using (
5.39
)–(
5.40
).
Taking the supremum over
t\in[a,b]
gives that

(5.42)

\|u-\widetilde{u}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{0}([a,b];\mathrm{L}^{p}(J_{r})\!)}=\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\|u(t)-\widetilde{u}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(J_{r})}\leq\varepsilon.\qed

Remark 5.43
.

While Theorem
5.38
, together with
[
CK20
, Theorem A]
, fully resolves the question of well-posed-ness of the GCSF from
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}
–initial data when
p>1
is strictly greater than
1
, the question of uniqueness and continuous dependence from
\mathrm{L}^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
–initial data, i.e. the case when
p=1
, is still open.
Since Proposition
5.25
in the case
p=1
is sharp—see Remark
5.27
—the author expects a proof of uniqueness from
\mathrm{L}^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
–initial data to require a novel estimate.

6
Towards a correspondence

Recall that our existence procedure in §
5
took a measure
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
and produced
two
GCSFs:
one starting from
\nu
and the other starting from
|\nu|
;
we continue to denote these solutions by
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)\!)
and
U\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty);\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
respectively.
These flows were constructed in such a way as to ensure that
|u(t)|\leq U(t)
on
\mathbb{R}
for every
t\in(0,\infty)
.

In this section, we shall use this additional property, that each of the GCSFs generated by Theorem
1.14
is
dominated
by a positive GCSF, to further a correspondence between
dominated GCSFs
and
\mathcal{M}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
, following on from Chou & Kwong in
[
CK20
, Appendix]
.
For the analogous correspondence in the RF setting, see
[
PT25
, Theorem 1.7]
.

Definition 6.1

(Dominated GCSF)
.

We say that a GCSF
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)\!)
is
dominated
if there exists a positive GCSF
U\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty);\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
for which

(6.2)

|u(t)|\leq U(t)

on
\mathbb{R}
for all times
t\in(0,\infty)
.

We then have

Theorem 6.3

(
Partial
correspondence between dominated GCSFs and
\mathcal{M}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
​)
.

There holds the following
partial
correspondence between dominated GCSFs and
\mathcal{M}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
:

(i)

For each
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
, there exists
at least one
dominated GCSF
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)\!)
for which
u(t)\mathscr{L}^{1}\rightharpoonup\nu
as
t\searrow 0
.

(ii)

For each dominated GCSF
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)\!)
, there exists a unique
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
, so-called the
initial trace
in
[
CK20
]
, for which
u(t)\mathscr{L}^{1}\rightharpoonup\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
as
t\searrow 0
.

Proof.

We need only prove (
ii
).
For any fixed
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R})
, by integrating (
5.18
) from Proposition
5.17
we get that

(6.4)

\left|\,\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)u(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)u(x,s)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\right|\leq C(\varphi)(t-s),

and therefore the assignment

(6.5)

\varphi\mapsto L\varphi=\lim_{t\searrow 0}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)u(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x:\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R})\mapsto\mathbb{R}

is well-defined, and is entirely determined by the flow
u
.
The map
L
inherits the linearity of the integral in the limit.

Let
\mathcal{K}\Subset\mathbb{R}
be compact, and choose a bump function
\chi\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};[0,1])
with
\chi=1
on
\mathcal{K}
.
Then, for any
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{K})
it holds that
|\varphi|\leq\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{C}^{0}(\mathcal{K})}\chi
on
\mathbb{R}
.
Therefore

(6.6)

\displaystyle\left|\,\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)u(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{C}^{0}(\mathcal{K})}\chi\cdot U(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x=\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{C}^{0}(\mathcal{K})}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\chi(x)U(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{C}^{0}(\mathcal{K})}\left(\,\,\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\chi(x)U(x,1)\,\mathrm{d}x+C(\chi)(1-t)\!\right)

\displaystyle\leq M_{\mathcal{K}}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{C}^{0}(\mathcal{K})}

for every
t\in(0,1]
, where
U\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty);\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
is the positive GCSF which dominates
u
.
Taking
t\searrow 0
in (
6.6
) gives that

(6.7)

|L\varphi|\leq M_{\mathcal{K}}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{C}^{0}(\mathcal{K})}.

The Riesz Representation Theorem (
[
EG15
, Theorem 1.38]
) ensures that
L
is given by integration against a unique real–valued Radon measure
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
.
To show that
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
, i.e. that
\nu
is non-atomic, it suffices (by outer regularity) to show for all
z\in\mathbb{R}
and
\varepsilon>0
that
|\nu(V)|\leq\varepsilon
for all sufficiently small and open
V\ni z
.
Given a bump function
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R};[0,1])
around
z
with
C(\varphi)=\pi
, note that

(6.8)

\left|\,\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(x)\right|\leq\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)U\left(x,\frac{\varepsilon}{2\pi}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

If we choose
R_{z}>0
so small that

(6.9)

\int_{x\in J_{2R_{z}}(z)}U\left(x,\frac{\varepsilon}{2\pi}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

then

(6.10)

\left|\,\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(x)\right|\leq\int_{x\in J_{2R_{z}}(z)}U\left(x,\frac{\varepsilon}{2\pi}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\leq\varepsilon

whenever
\varphi
is supported in
J_{2R_{z}}(z)
.
In particular, given any open
V\subset J_{R_{z}}(z)
containing
z
, if
(\chi^{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(J_{2R_{z}}(z);[0,1])
is such that
\chi^{j}\to\chi_{V}
pointwise as
j\nearrow\infty
, then by the Dominated Convergence Theorem one can pass to the limit in (
6.10
) to deduce the required

(6.11)

|\nu(V)|=\lim_{j\nearrow\infty}\left|\,\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\chi^{j}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(x)\right|\leq\varepsilon.\qed

Note that an alternative argument is provided in
[
CK20
, Appendix]
, where Chou & Kwong showed that the limit

(6.12)

\lim_{t\searrow 0}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)u(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x

in the case
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}^{1}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R})
can be understood as the distributional derivative of a unique
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
–function.
And, in the positive case
u\geq 0
,
they similarly characterised the limit as an element of
\mathcal{M}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
.

It is an open question as to how restrictive our ‘dominated’ condition in Definition
6.1
is—see
[
Sob24
, Question 4.4.17]

Appendix A
Angenent’s Intersection Principle

We recall the classical statement of Angenent’s Intersection Principle from
[
Ang91
]
.

Theorem A.1

(Classical Angenent’s Intersection Principle;
[
Ang91
, Variation on Theorem 1.3]
)
.

Let
[0,T)\ni t\mapsto\gamma^{1}(t),\gamma^{2}(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be two distinct CSFs, each defined over either the circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
or the closed interval
[0,1]
, for which

(A.2)

\partial\gamma^{1}(t)\cap\gamma^{2}(t)=\gamma^{1}(t)\cap\partial\gamma^{2}(t)=\emptyset

for all
t\in[0,T)
.
Then the number of intersections of
\gamma^{1}(t)
and
\gamma^{2}(t)
is a finite and decreasing function of
t\in(0,T)
, which strictly decreases whenever
\gamma^{1}(t)
and
\gamma^{2}(t)
meet tangentially.

Notice that Theorem
A.1
is concerned with the evolution of
compact
arcs, while in this paper we are primarily be concerned with
non-compact
curves.
This issue can be remedied as follows.

First, we restrict our attention to CSFs which admit ‘good’ parametrisations, in the sense of Peachey’s
uniformly proper
condition.

Definition A.3

(Uniformly proper;
[
Pea22
, Definition 4.2.1]
)
.

We say that a CSF
\gamma:\Omega\times[0,T)\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{2}
is
uniformly proper
if every restriction
\gamma:\Omega\times[0,t]\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{2}
for
0<t<T
is a proper map.

Peachey’s subclass of CSFs possesses the important feature that, Ecker–Huisken flows of locally Lipschitz functions and Chou–Zhu flows of curves whose ends are graphical are automatically uniformly proper—see
[
Sob24
, Remark 2.2.31 and Corollary 2.4.15]
.
Clearly compact CSFs are automatically uniformly proper.

Second, we introduce an extension of the condition (
A.2
).

Definition A.4

(Disjoint ends)
.

We say that two CSFs
[0,T)\ni t\mapsto\gamma^{1}(t),\gamma^{2}(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
have
disjoint ends
if (
A.2
) holds and if there exists a compact set
\mathcal{K}\Subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
for which

(A.5)

\gamma^{1}(t)\cap\gamma^{2}(t)\cap(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\mathcal{K})=\emptyset

for all
t\in[0,T)
.

Armed with Definitions
A.3
and
A.4
, we have the following version of Angenent’s Intersection Principle, which shall be the main version used in this paper:

Theorem A.6

(Angenent’s Intersection Principle; see
[
Sob24
, Theorem 2.4.10]
)
.

Let
[0,T)\ni t\mapsto\gamma^{1}(t),\gamma^{2}(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be two distinct uniformly proper CSFs, starting from the locally Lipschitz curves
\gamma^{1}_{0}
,
\gamma^{2}_{0}
respectively, which have disjoint ends.
Then the number of intersections between
\gamma^{1}(t)
and
\gamma^{2}(t)
is finite function of
t\in(0,T)
, and a decreasing function of
t\in[0,T)
which strictly decreases whenever
\gamma^{1}(t)
and
\gamma^{2}(t)
meet tangentially.
Moreover, suppose that
\gamma^{1}_{0}
and
\gamma^{2}_{0}
intersect
one-sidedly

Sketch proof.

The uniformly proper condition and the assumption of disjoint ends allows one to reduce to the case that
\gamma^{1}(t)
and
\gamma^{2}(t)
are both compact for each
t\in[0,T)
, at which point the classical Angenent’s Intersection Principle (Theorem
A.1
) applies.
The Strong Maximum Principle implies that one-sided initial intersections disappear instantaneously.
If there are infinitely many intersections which are not one-sided, then the final assertion is trivial.
For finitely many initial intersections that are not one-sided, a closer look at Angenent’s
[
Ang88
, Lemmas 5.3 and 5.5]
reveals that each one of these must not bifurcate along the flow for a short length of time.
For details, see
[
Sob24
, Theorem 2.4.10]
.
∎

An immediate consequence of Angenent’s Intersection Principle is the following version of the Avoidance Principle:

Theorem A.7

(Avoidance Principle; see
[
Sob24
, Theorem 2.4.9]
)
.

Let
[0,T)\ni t\mapsto\gamma^{1}(t),\gamma^{2}(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be two uniformly proper CSFs, starting from the locally Lipschitz curves
\gamma^{1}_{0}
,
\gamma^{2}_{0}
respectively, which have disjoint ends.
Suppose that
\gamma^{1}_{0}
and
\gamma^{2}_{0}
only intersect one-sidedly within regions where at least one of the curves is
\mathrm{C}^{1}
.
Then for strictly positive times
t>0
the curves
\gamma^{1}(t)
and
\gamma^{2}(t)
are disjoint.

Appendix B
Real–valued Radon measures

We recall the various measure-theoretic notions required in this paper, mostly from
[
EG15
]
.

By a
real–valued Radon measure
(
[
EG15
, §1.8]
) on
\mathbb{R}
, we mean a Radon measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
paired with a
\mu
–measurable function
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\left\{\pm 1\right\}
.
We write
\nu=\sigma\mu
and
|\nu|=\mu
.
We denote the space of real–valued Radon measures by
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
and the space of Radon measures by
\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
.

The Riesz Representation Theorem (
[
EG15
, Theorem 1.38]
) identifies
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
with the dual space
\mathrm{C}^{0}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R})^{*}
of continuous and compactly supported functions.
A sequence
(\nu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of measures
converges weakly
(
[
EG15
, §1.9.1]
) to another measure
\nu
, written
\nu_{n}\rightharpoonup\nu
, as
n\nearrow\infty
if it converges in the weak–
*
sense when viewed in
\mathrm{C}^{0}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R})^{*}
; explicitly,

(B.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{n}\to\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\nu

as
n\nearrow\infty
for any
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}^{0}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R})
.

We say that a real–valued Radon measure
\nu
is
non-atomic
if
\nu(\left\{x\right\})=0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
;
note that Chou & Kwong call such a measure
continuous
in
[
CK20
]
.
We denote the space of non-atomic real–valued Radon measures by
\mathcal{M}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
, and the space of non-atomic Radon measures by
\mathcal{M}^{+}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
. Clearly
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
if and only if
|\nu|\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
.

Appendix C
Evans & Spruck’s interior gradient estimate

We will use the following version of Evans & Spruck’s interior gradient estimate:

Theorem C.1

(cf.
[
ES92
, Corollary 5.3]
)
.

Let
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(J_{2R}\times[0,2T])
be a GCSF for which
\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(J_{2R}\times[0,2T])}\leq L
.
Then, for any
0<S<T
, there exists a constant
C(R,S,T,L)<\infty
such that

(C.2)

\|\partial_{x}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(J_{R}\times[S,T])}\leq C(R,S,T,L).

Proof.

Evans & Spruck’s estimate is phrased in
[
ES92
, Corollary 5.3]
as

(C.3)

|\partial_{x}u(0,s)|\leq C\left(R,s,\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(J_{R}\times[0,2s])}\right).

To obtain (
C.2
), we apply (
C.3
) to
J_{R}\times[0,2s]\ni(x,t)\mapsto\widetilde{u}(x,t)=u(y+x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
for every
(y,s)\in J_{R}\times[S,T]
.
∎

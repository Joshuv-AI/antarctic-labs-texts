Intermittent Left Bundle Branch Block After Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement
Authors: Francesco Cardinali, Michela Casella, Marco Di Eusanio, Antonio Dello Russo, Paolo Compagnucci
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Case SummaryAn 87-year-old man with pre-existing first-degree atrioventricular block underwent transfemoral transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) for severe aortic stenosis. Postprocedure, he developed worsening first-degree atrioventricular block and intermittent left bundle branch block with a paradoxical electrocardiographic pattern: Slightly longer PR intervals were associated with narrow QRS beats, whereas shorter PR intervals resulted in marked QRS widening. Electrophysiology study revealed advanced infra-Hisian conduction disease, with greater prolongation of the His-ventricular interval before narrow QRS beats, prompting permanent pacemaker implantation.Take-Home MessageThis case demonstrates that intermittent left bundle branch block after TAVR—particularly when associated with marked PR prolongation preceding apparent QRS narrowing—warrants careful evaluation, as it may mask high-risk trifascicular disease.

Case Summary

An 87-year-old man with severe, symptomatic aortic stenosis was referred to our center for transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR). Preprocedural coronary angiography excluded angiographically significant coronary artery disease. The patient underwent successful transfemoral implantation of a 29-mm self-expanding Medtronic Evolut FX valve. The postprocedural course was complicated by prolongation of the PR interval and the appearance of left bundle branch block (LBBB), with a progressively increasing LBBB burden on serial electrocardiograms (Figures 1B to 1D). A paradoxical relationship between atrioventricular conduction and ventricular depolarization was observed: When the PR interval was slightly shorter, the QRS complex was markedly widened (170 ms), whereas with slightly longer PR intervals, the QRS complex appeared significantly narrower (105 ms).

Given the dynamic conduction abnormalities and the progressively increasing burden of LBBB, an electrophysiology study was performed (Figure 1E). The electrophysiology study demonstrated pathological infra-Hisian conduction delay, with a His-ventricular (HV) interval of 75 ms in the presence of LBBB and 80 ms during beats with a narrow QRS complex. Considering these findings, a dual-chamber permanent pacemaker was implanted. At the 2-month follow-up, no clinical events occurred, and the patient was not pacemaker dependent.

Discussion

Conduction disturbances are common after TAVR, particularly with self-expanding prostheses, and recent data suggest that LBBB may present with an intermittent pattern in a subset of patients.1,2 Such a finding is often considered relatively benign or transient; in a recent series, intermittent LBBB was strongly associated with an HV interval of <70 ms in 26 patients.2 In our case, the diagnostic challenge was the paradoxical association between marked PR interval prolongation and apparent QRS narrowing, which required careful electrocardiographic interpretation.

Supernormal conduction in the left bundle branch represents a rare functional phenomenon and would be expected to result in true normalization of ventricular activation, with HV interval shortening during beats with a narrow QRS complex. This finding was not observed in our case, unlike a recent report on post-TAVR supernormal conduction-related intermittent LBBB, in which PR prolongation did not precede QRS narrowing.3

Rate-dependent LBBB may also appear plausible, as a longer PR interval may be associated with a longer ventricular cycle length allowing recovery of left bundle branch excitability; however, the persistently prolonged HV interval regardless of QRS morphology is inconsistent with a true rate-dependent mechanism.

Premature ventricular complexes can mimic intermittent LBBB. Nevertheless, typical LBBB morphology, including absent r-wave and rapid downstroke of the S-wave in V1 with no q-wave in V6, together with the prolonged HV interval associated with LBBB, argue against this diagnosis.

Overall, these findings are best explained by advanced infra-Hisian conduction disease involving both bundle branches, resulting in synchronous conduction delay and pseudo-normalization of the QRS complex. This pattern reflects trifascicular disease and supports permanent pacemaker implantation.2

Importantly, this case demonstrates that intermittent LBBB after TAVR—especially when associated with marked PR prolongation preceding QRS narrowing and with progressively increasing burden of wide QRS complexes—should not necessarily be considered reassuring and indicates that an electrophysiology study may inform clinical decision-making in this setting.

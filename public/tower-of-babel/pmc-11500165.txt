Computational insights of double perovskite Na2CaCdH6 hydride alloy for hydrogen storage applications: a DFT investigation
Authors: Muhammad Khuram Shahzad, Shoukat Hussain, Muhammad Noman Khan, Muhammad Jehanzaib Aslam, Rawaa M Mohammed, Vineet Tirth, Hassan Alqahtani, Ali Algahtani, Tawfiq Al-Mughanam, Waqar Azeem
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractProspective use of perovskite hydride materials in H storage a crucial element of clean energy systems has drawn a lot of attention. The structural, electrical, mechanical, thermodynamic, and H storage qualities of Na2CaCdH6 hydride alloys were examined in this work using DFT. According to the structural properties, Na2CaCdH6 has space group 225 (Fm3m), and optimized lattice parameters and volume of Na2CaCdH6 are 3.3485 Å and 593.764 Å3. The measured gravimetric H storage capacity of Na2CaCdH6 hydrides is 2.956 wt%. The hydrides under research are semiconductors, as indicated by the computed electronic characteristics. Elastic constants, Pugh’s ratio, modulus, Poisson’s ratio, anisotropic, and microhardness of Na2CaCdH6 are calculated under mechanical properties. The hydrides are dynamically stable, as indicated by the phonon dispersion curves, but mechanically stable according to the Born criterion for elastic constant (Cij). The Cauchy’s pressure (C″ = 7.836) revealed the ductile behavior. The electronic and mechanical characteristics imply that Na2CaCdH6 hydride can conduct electricity and is also mechanically stable. Our findings shed light on the possibilities of Na2CaCdH6 perovskite hydride material for H storage utilization.Keywords: DFT, Hydride perovskite, Mechanical properties, Gravimetric H storage capacitySubject terms: Condensed-matter physics, Theory and computation

Introduction

Since hydrogen is the lightest fuel and also the greatest prevalent element in space, it is assumed to be a reliable and affordable energy source1,2. In addition to the rising need for inexpensive, clean energy, energy research has increasingly concentrated on new and renewable energy sources3,4. Hydrogen is perfect for use in the energy storage industry because of its exceptional properties. Metal hydride perovskites exhibit a rapid improvement in efficiency from 3.8% to well over 20%5,6. However, a significant obstacle to hydrogen’s widespread application as a fuel is the difficulty of securely and effectively storing it7,8. Since magnesium (Mg)-based hydrides are inexpensive, widely available, and have excellent volumetric and gravimetric hydrogen storage capabilities; they are considered promising hydrogen storage materials9. Certain parameters must be met by the usage of the material for E-storage, such as high volumetric and gravimetric ratio values, good kinetics, notable mechanical characteristics, also capacity to discharge H under normal circumstances10,11. High hydrogen storage capacity can be attained by substituting a light element for oxygen, which results in new hydrogen bonding sites12,13. Perovskite materials containing ABH3 hydrides can be categorized into two main categories: (i) A and B can be made from the elements in groups 1,2 of the periodic tables14,15. Among the substances from this class that the researcher has examined is NaMgH3, which has many uses due to its high H storage capacity in terms of weight (6%) and volume (88 Å3)16,17. Additional composites derived from this category that were also investigated include SrLiH3, RbCaH3, KCaH3, NaMnH3, KMgH3, CsMgH3, and BaLiH318,19. The elements that make up the second class of perovskite cubic hydrides are components A and B, which are divalent or monovalent metals20. From this group, substances such as LiCuH3, SrPdH3, KCuH3, MgFeH3, CaNiH3, CaCoH3, MgCoH3, and Ca2FeH6 have been explored21,22. Magnesium (Mg) based perovskite has garnered significant curiosity due to its extraordinary H storage ability, which is explained by the bonding sites created when a light element is substituted for oxygen23. Theoretical study indicates that MgBH3 and MgNH3 perovskite elements have the potential to store up to 15.4 and 14.9 wt% more hydrogen, as well, as a DOE target of 5.5 wt% for onboard H storage. Reversible hydrogen storage capability and strong thermal stability have also been demonstrated by KCaH3 perovskite materials, which are essential for practical uses24. Additionally, magnesium-based perovskite substances can be manufactured via experiment, using a variety of methods, including the solid-state, ball-milling, and solution-state methods25. Solution-based methods involve dissolving Mg/Ca/Cd and light elements (salt) in a solvent, which is followed by a chemical reaction that forms the perovskite material26. Ball milling needs a high-energy mixture of MgH− and the corresponding light element H− to achieve solid-state reactions. In solid-state synthesis, high temperatures and pressures heat a mixture of MgH− and the corresponding light element H27.

Since H has the most E per mass of all fuels, but the lowest E density per unit V due to its low ambient T, new storage techniques that could lead to higher energy densities are needed28. In this sense, the study reported in this publication represents the first thorough investigation of Na2CaCdH6 perovskite hydrides. Consequently, these studies offer fresh information for the future and novel alloys for usages involving H storage29. The physical features and hydrogen storage properties of Na2CaCdH6 perovskites for H storage applications have been computed and reviewed in this paper.

Computational detailed

With the use of the CASTEP method, the shape of the hydride perovskites Na2CaCdH6 was optimized29. All of the characteristics are investigated using the CASTEP code as well. Na2CaCdH6’s geometry has been optimized using the functional GGA-PBE. When utilizing DFT to describe the accurate band gap of the materials, this function is crucial. As a result, the GGA-PBE functional was also used in the computations for spin-polarized, optical, and electrical characteristics. The Kohn-Sham (KS) relations were estimated using the plane wave (PW) pseudopotential technique, which is based on DFT. The set of PW bases is created and enhanced by using Bloch’s theorem and enforcing a periodic boundary constraint30,31. The electron-ion (e-i) potential has been addressed in the Vanderbilt-kind ultrasoft formulations by using pseudopotentials derived from fundamental principles32. Recent research has demonstrated how effective the BFGS (Broyden Fletcher Goldfarb Shanno) energy (E) minimization method is for calculating charge densities (CD) and electronic wave (EW) functions in crystalline alloys33. Thus, the Pulay density (PD) mixing approach was used in this assessment together with the BFGS energy reduction technique. The first Brillouin zone uses the Monkhorst-Pack technique to gather the discrete k-points required for data integration. Using an ultra-fine 4 × 4 × 4 k-points mesh with a cut-off energy of 598.7 eV, the maximum output was achieved. For every computation, both the ultra-fine SCF tolerance and the energy tolerance per atom were used. In a similar vein, mech. Stability and other material mech. Conclusion

Because hydrogen storage is a crucial part of renewable energy and green energy systems, perovskite hydride materials have drawn a lot of attention. The structural, elastic, electrical, and H storage characteristics of Na2CaCdH6 hydrides were evaluated by the usage of the DFT technique. According to the structural properties, Na2CaCdH6 has space group 225 (Fm3m), and optimized lattice parameters and V of Na2CaCdH6 are 3.3485 Å and 593.764 Å3. The calculated gravimetric H storage capacity of Na2CaCdH6 compound hydrides is 2.956 wt%. Almost every H− with strong electron-phonon interaction is a semiconductor, as shown by their electronic band structures and DOS. The electronic band structure’s semiconductor nature, as indicated by the 2.08 eV band gap, and structural stability were both demonstrated by the negative formation enthalpy (-389.588 eV/atom). Elastic constants (C11 = 44.837, C12 = 22.008, C44 = 14.172) GPa, modulus (B = 29.617, E = 34.179, G = 13.069) GPa, Pugh’s ratio (G/B = 2.27), Poisson’s ratio (v = 0.31), anisotropic (A = 0.056), and microhardness (H = 1.652) of Na2CaCdH6 are calculated under mechanical properties. While the ductile behavior was disclosed by the Cauchy’s pressure (C” = 7.836), the mech. The stability of the three H was revealed by Born stability criteria. Our findings provide light on the Na2CaCdH6 perovskite hydride material is suitable for use in H storage applications.

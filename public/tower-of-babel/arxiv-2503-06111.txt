A note on the uniform ergodicity of diffusion processes
Nikola Sandrić

Abstract
. In this note, we discuss the uniform ergodicity of a diffusion process given by an Itô stochastic differential equation. We present an integral condition in terms of the drift and diffusion coefficients that ensures the uniform ergodicity of the corresponding transition kernel with respect to the total variation distance. Applications of the obtained results to a class of subordinate diffusion processes are also presented.

1.
Introduction

One of the classical directions in the analysis of Markov processes centers around their ergodicity properties. In this note, we study the uniform ergodicity of a diffusion process given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X(x,t)

\displaystyle=b(X(x,t))+\sigma(X(x,t))\mathrm{d}B(t)

\displaystyle X(x,0)

\displaystyle=x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

with respect to the total variation distance.
Here,
\{B(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
stands for a standard
n
-dimensional Brownian motion (defined on a stochastic basis
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}(t)\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
satisfying the usual conditions), and the coefficients
b:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
satisfy the following conditions:

(A1):

For any
r>0
,

\sup_{x\in B_{r}(0)}(\rvert b(x)\rvert+\lVert\sigma(x)\lVert_{{\rm HS}})<\infty.

(A2):

For any
r>0
, there exists
\Gamma_{r}>0
such that for all
x,y\in B_{r}(0)
,

2\langle x-y,b(x)-b(y)\rangle+\lVert\sigma(x)-\sigma(y)\lVert_{{\rm HS}}^{2}\leq\Gamma_{r}|x-y|^{2}.

(A3):

There exists
\Gamma>0
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

2\langle x,b(x)\rangle+\lVert\sigma(x)\lVert_{{\rm HS}}^{2}\leq\Gamma(1+|x|^{2}).

(A4):

There exist
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r_{0},\alpha,D,\delta>0
, such that for all
x,y\in B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
,

|b(x)-b(y)|+\lVert\sigma(x)\sigma(x)^{T}-\sigma(y)\sigma(y)^{T}\rVert_{{\rm HS}}\leq D|x-y|^{\alpha}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\langle\sigma(x)^{T}y,\sigma(x)^{T}y\rangle\geq\delta|y|^{2}.

(A5):

For
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r_{0}>0
from (A4),
\sigma(x)\sigma(x)^{T}
is positive definite on
B^{c}_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
.

Here,
B_{r}(x)
denotes the open ball with radius
r>0
around
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\lVert M\lVert_{{\rm HS}}^{2}:={\rm Tr}\,MM^{T}
is the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of a real matrix
M.

1.1.
Structural properties of the model

Under (A1)-(A3), for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the stochastic differential equation (SDE) in (
1.1
) admits a unique strong non-explosive solution
\{X(x,t)\}_{t\geq 0}
which is a strong Markov process with continuous sample paths and transition kernel
p(t,x,\mathrm{d}y)=\mathbb{P}(X(x,t)\in\mathrm{d}y)
,
t\geq 0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
See
[
21
, Theorems 5.4.1, 5.4.5 and 5.4.6]
and
[
58
, Theorem 3.1.1]
for more details. In the context of Markov processes, it is natural that the underlying probability measure depends on the initial distribution of the process. Using standard arguments (Kolmogorov extension theorem), it is well known that for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the above-defined transition kernel defines a unique probability measure
\mathbb{P}^{x}
on the canonical (sample-path) space such that the projection process, denoted by
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
, is a strong Markov process (with respect to the completion of the corresponding natural filtration), it has continuous sample paths,
and the same finite-dimensional distributions (with respect to
\mathbb{P}^{x}
) as
\{X(x,t)\}_{t\geq 0}
(with respect to
\mathbb{P}
). Since we are interested in distributional properties of the solution to (
1.1
), we focus on
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
rather than
\{X(x,t)\}_{t\geq 0}
in the sequel. Furthermore, according to
[
47
, Lemma 2.5]
, for any
t>0
and continuous and bounded
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
the mapping

x\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(y)p(t,x,\mathrm{d}y)

is also continuous and bounded, i.e.,
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
satisfies the so-called
\mathcal{C}_{b}
-Feller property.
In particular, this automatically implies that
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is a strong Markov process with respect to the right-continuous and completed version of the natural filtration.
Finally, in
[
59
, Theorem V.21.1]
it is shown that
the class
\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is contained in the domain of the extended generator
\mathcal{G}
of
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
, and
\mathcal{G}
restricted to this class has the following form:

(1.2)

\mathcal{G}f(x)=\langle b(x),\nabla f(x)\rangle+\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}\,\sigma(x)\sigma(x)^{T}\nabla^{2}f(x).

1.2.
Main result

Before stating the main result of this article, we first recall definitions of ergodicity.

(i)

A
(not necessarily finite) measure
\uppi(\mathrm{d}x)
on
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is called invariant for

\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
if

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,x,\mathrm{d}y)\uppi(\mathrm{d}x)=\uppi(\mathrm{d}y),\qquad t>0.

(ii)

The process
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is said to be ergodic
if it possesses an invariant probability
measure
\uppi(\mathrm{d}x)
such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\lVert p(t,x,\mathrm{d}{y})-\uppi(\mathrm{d}{y})\rVert_{{\rm TV}}=0,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(iii)

The process
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is said to be uniformly ergodic
if it possesses an invariant probability
measure
\uppi(\mathrm{d}x)
such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lVert p(t,x,\mathrm{d}{y})-\uppi(\mathrm{d}{y})\rVert_{{\rm TV}}=0.

Here,
\lVert\upmu\rVert_{{\rm TV}}\coloneqq\sup_{B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\upmu(B)|
is the total variation norm of a signed measure
\upmu(\mathrm{d}x)
(on
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
). Observe that:

(a)

(Uniform) ergodicity immediately implies the uniqueness of
\uppi(\mathrm{d}x)
.

(b)

The rate of convergence in (ii) depends on structural properties of the process (see, e.g.,
[
16
]
and
[
19
]
).

(c)

The rate of convergence in (iii) is necessarily exponential. Namely, from (iii) we have that for any
t_{0}>0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lVert p(nt_{0},x,\mathrm{d}{y})-\uppi(\mathrm{d}{y})\rVert_{{\rm TV}}=0,

i.e., the Markov chain
\{X(nt_{0})\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
is uniformly ergodic. Consequently, from
[
52
, Theorem 16.0.2]
it follows that there exist
B,\beta>0
such that

\lVert p(nt_{0},x,\mathrm{d}{y})-\uppi(\mathrm{d}{y})\rVert_{{\rm TV}}\leq B\mathrm{e}^{-\beta nt_{0}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Since every
t\geq 0
can be clearly represented as
t=nt_{0}+s
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
s\in[0,t_{0})
, we then conclude

\displaystyle\lVert p(t,x,\mathrm{d}{y})-\uppi(\mathrm{d}{y})\rVert_{{\rm TV}}

\displaystyle=\lVert p(nt_{0}+s,x,\mathrm{d}{y})-\uppi(\mathrm{d}{y})\rVert_{{\rm TV}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert p(nt_{0},x,\mathrm{d}{y})-\uppi(\mathrm{d}{y})\rVert_{{\rm TV}}

\displaystyle\leq B\mathrm{e}^{-\beta nt_{0}}

\displaystyle=B\mathrm{e}^{\beta s}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta t}

\displaystyle\leq B\mathrm{e}^{\beta t_{0}}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta t},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad t>0.

We are now in position to state the main result of this article.
Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r_{0}>0
be as in (A4) and (A5). Set

\displaystyle A(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}\,\sigma(x)\sigma(x)^{T},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\displaystyle B_{x_{0}}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\langle x-x_{0},b(x)\rangle,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\displaystyle C_{x_{0}}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\langle\sigma(x)^{T}(x-x_{0}),\sigma(x)^{T}(x-x_{0})\rangle}{|x-x_{0}|^{2}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{x_{0}\},

\displaystyle\gamma_{x_{0}}(r)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf_{|x-x_{0}|=r}C_{x_{0}}(x),\qquad r\geq r_{0},

\displaystyle\iota_{x_{0}}(r)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sup_{|x-x_{0}|=r}\frac{2A(x)-C_{x_{0}}(x)+2B_{x_{0}}(x)}{C_{x_{0}}(x)},\qquad r\geq r_{0},

\displaystyle I_{x_{0}}(r)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{r_{0}}^{r}\frac{\iota_{x_{0}}(s)}{s}ds,\qquad r\geq r_{0}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
(A1)-(A5)
, and

(1.3)

\Lambda\coloneqq\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-I_{x_{0}}(u)}\int_{u}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{I_{x_{0}}(v)}}{\gamma_{x_{0}}(v)}\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}u<\infty

(observe that (A5) ensures that
\Lambda
is well defined).
Then,
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is uniformly ergodic.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on the Foster-Lyapunov method for uniform ergodicity of Markov processes, developed in
[
19
]
.
The method consists of identifying an appropriate recurrent (petite) set
C\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and constructing an appropriate bounded function
L:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[1,\infty)
(the so-called Lyapunov function) contained in the domain of
\mathcal{G}
(see
[
19
, Section 4]
for details), such that the Lyapunov equation

(1.4)

\mathcal{G}L(x)\leq-c_{1}L(x)+c_{2}\mathbb{1}_{C}(x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

holds for some
c_{1}>0
and
c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
(see
[
19
, Theorem 5.2]
).
Recall that a set
C\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is said to be petite if it satisfies the Harris-type minorization condition: there exist a probability measure
\upeta_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathrm{d}t)
on
\mathcal{B}((0,\infty))
(the standard Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
(0,\infty)
) and a non-trivial measure
\upnu_{C}(\mathrm{d}x)
on
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, such that
\int_{0}^{\infty}p(t,x,B)\upeta_{C}(\mathrm{d}t)\geq\upnu_{C}(B)
for all
x\in C
and
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Intuitively, petite sets serve the role of singletons for Markov processes on non-discrete state spaces (see
[
52
, Chapter 5]
and
[
53
, Section 4]
for details).
However, the Lyapunov equation does not immediately imply ergodicity of
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
. As in the discrete setting (see, e.g.,
[
52
, Chapter 13]
), the process has to satisfy additional structural properties (irreducibility and aperiodicity). Note that so far, we have not used assumptions (A4) and (A5). In Proposition
2.1
we show that (A4) and (A5), together with (A1)-(A3), imply that
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is

(i)

open-set irreducible, i.e., there exists a
\sigma
-finite measure
\uppsi(\mathrm{d}x)
on

\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

{\rm supp}\,\uppsi\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\uppsi(O)>0\ \text{for every open neighborhood}\ O\ \text{of}\ x\}

has a non-empty interior, and whenever
\uppsi(B)>0
we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}p(t,x,B)\mathrm{d}t>0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(ii)

aperiodic, i.e.,
there exist
t_{0}>0
and a
\sigma
-finite measure
\upphi(\mathrm{d}x)
on

\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, such that
\upphi(B)>0
implies

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p(nt_{0},x,B)>0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Let us also remark that, according to
[
53
, Propositio 4.1]
and
[
68
, Theorems 5.1 and 7.1]
, the
\mathcal{C}_{b}
-Feller property and open-set irreducibility of
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
ensure that the state space (in this case,
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
) can be covered by a countable union of petite sets, and that every compact set is petite.

To the best of our knowledge, Theorem
1.1
presents the first explicit conditions for the uniform ergodicity of diffusion processes based on the Lyapunov method. As previously noted, general conditions using the Lyapunov method for the uniform ergodicity of general Markov processes are derived in
[
19
]
. The only related results on this topic available in the literature (under the terminology of strong ergodicity) can be found in
[
48
, Section ]
,
[
49
, Section 3]
and
[

, Section 3]
. The main difference is that the results in these works are based on the coupling method. Therefore, the presented conditions are of the incremental type, which in certain cases they can be less practical, i.e. more difficult to verify (compare Examples

,

and

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
. First, we prove that assumptions (A4) and (A5), together with (A1)-(A3), imply the open-set irreducibility and aperiodicity of
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Under assumptions (A1)-(A5), the process

\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is open-set irreducible and aperiodic.

Proof.

We first show that (A5), together with (A1)-(A3), implies that

\mathbb{P}^{x}(\uptau_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}<\infty)>0,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r_{0}>0
are given in (A5), and
\uptau_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}
is defied as
\uptau_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}\coloneqq\inf\{t\geq 0:X(t)\in B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})\}
.
Let
0<\varepsilon<r_{0}
, and define

\bar{L}(r)\coloneqq\int_{r_{0}-\varepsilon}^{r}\mathrm{e}^{-I_{x_{0}}(u)}\mathrm{d}u,\qquad r\geq r_{0}-\varepsilon.

For
r>r_{0}-\varepsilon
we then have

\bar{L}^{\prime}(r)=\mathrm{e}^{-I_{x_{0}}(r)}>0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\bar{L}^{\prime\prime}(r)=-\frac{\bar{L}^{\prime}(r)}{r}\iota_{x_{0}}(r).

Further, let
L:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty)
,
L\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, be such that
L(x)=\bar{L}(|x-x_{0}|)
for
x\in B^{c}_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
. For
x\in B^{c}_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
, we then have

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}L(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}C_{x_{0}}(x)\bar{L}^{\prime\prime}(|x-x_{0}|)+\frac{\bar{L}^{\prime}(|x-x_{0}|)}{2|x-x_{0}|}(2A(x)-C_{x_{0}}(x)+2B_{x_{0}}(x))

\displaystyle=\frac{\bar{L}^{\prime}(|x-x_{0}|)}{2|x-x_{0}|}\left(2A(x)-C_{x_{0}}(x)+2B_{x_{0}}(x)-C_{x_{0}}(x)\iota_{x_{0}}(|x-x_{0}|)\right),

and thus

\mathcal{G}L(x)\leq 0.

Further, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\tau_{n}\coloneqq\uptau_{B^{c}_{n}(x_{0})}.
Clearly,
\uptau_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, are stopping times such that (due to non-explosivity of
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
) we have
\uptau_{n}\to\infty
,
\mathbb{P}^{x}
-a.s., as
n\to\infty
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Hence, the processes (recall that
\mathcal{G}
is the extended generator of
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
)

L(X(t\wedge\tau_{n}))-L(X(0))-\int_{0}^{t\wedge\uptau_{n}}\mathcal{G}L(X(s))\mathrm{d}s,\qquad t\geq 0\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

are
\mathbb{P}^{x}
-martingales. For
x\in B^{c}_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
, it then holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{x}[L(X(t\wedge\tau_{n}\wedge\tau_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}))]-\mathbb{E}^{x}[L(X(0))]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{x}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}\wedge\tau_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}}\mathcal{G}L(X(s))\mathrm{d}s\leq 0,

i.e.,

\mathbb{E}^{x}[\bar{L}(|X(t\wedge\tau_{n}\wedge\tau_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})})-x_{0}|)]\leq\bar{L}(|x-x_{0}|).

In particular,

\mathbb{E}^{x}[\bar{L}(|X(t\wedge\tau_{n})-x_{0}|)\mathbb{1}_{\{\tau_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}>\tau_{n}\}}]\leq\bar{L}(|x-x_{0}|),\qquad x\in B^{c}_{r_{0}}(x_{0}).

By letting
t\to\infty
, Fatou’s lemma implies

\bar{L}(n)\mathbb{P}^{x}(\tau_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}>\tau_{n})\leq\bar{L}(|x-x_{0}|),\qquad x\in B^{c}_{r_{0}}(x_{0}).

Hence, by letting
n\to\infty
, we conclude that

\mathbb{P}^{x}(\tau_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}=\infty)\leq\frac{\bar{L}(|x-x_{0}|)}{\bar{L}(\infty)}<1,\qquad x\in B^{c}_{r_{0}}(x_{0}),

i.e.,
\mathbb{P}^{x}(\tau_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}<\infty)>0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

We next show that
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is open-set irreducible. According to
[
21
, Theorems 7.3.6 and 7.3.7]
, there exists a strictly positive function
q(t,x,y)
on
(0,\infty)\times\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(x_{0})\times\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
, jointly continuous in
t
,
x
and
y
, and twice continuously differentiable in
x\in B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
, satisfying

\mathbb{E}^{x}[f(X(t))\mathbb{1}_{\{\uptau_{\bar{B}^{c}_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}>t\}}]=\int_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}q(t,x,y)f(y)\mathrm{d}y,

for any
t>0
,
x\in B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
and any continuous, bounded function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}.
By employing the dominated convergence theorem, the above relation holds also for
\mathbb{1}_{O}
, where
O\subseteq B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
is an open set. Denote by
\mathcal{D}
the class of all
B\in\mathcal{B}(B_{r_{0}}(x_{0}))
(the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
) such that

\mathbb{P}^{x}(X_{t}\in B,\ \uptau_{\bar{B}^{c}_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}>t)=\int_{B}q(t,x,y)\mathrm{d}y,\qquad t>0,\quad x\in B_{r_{0}}(x_{0}).

Clearly,
\mathcal{D}
contains the
\pi
-system of open rectangles in
B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
, and forms a
\lambda
-system. By Dynkin’s
\pi
-
\lambda
theorem, we conclude that
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{B}(B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})).
Consequently, for any
t>0
,
x\in B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
and
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

p(t,x,B)\geq\int_{B\cap B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}q(t,x,y)\mathrm{d}y.

Define
\uppsi(\cdot)\coloneqq\uplambda(\cdot\cap B_{r_{0}}(x_{0}))
, where
\uplambda(\mathrm{d}x)
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Clearly,
\uppsi(\mathrm{d}x)
is a
\sigma
-finite measure whose support has a non-empty interior. It remains to prove that

\int_{0}^{\infty}p(t,x,B)\mathrm{d}t>0,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

whenever
\uppsi(B)>0
,
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
For
x\in B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
the implication holds trivially.
Let
x\in B^{c}_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
and
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\uppsi(B)>0
, be arbitrary. For all
s>0
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}p(t,x,B)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\int_{s}^{\infty}p(t,x,B)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t-s,x,\mathrm{d}y)p(s,y,B)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\int_{s}^{\infty}\int_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}p(t-s,x,\mathrm{d}y)p(s,y,B)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}p(s,y,B)\int_{s}^{\infty}p(t-s,x,\mathrm{d}y)\mathrm{d}t.

The assertion follows from the fact that
p(s,y,B)>0
for
y\in B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
, and

\int_{s}^{\infty}p(t-s,x,B_{r_{0}}(x_{0}))\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{\infty}p(t,x,B_{r_{0}}(x_{0}))\mathrm{d}t=\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{1}_{\{X(t)\in B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})\}}\mathrm{d}t\right]>0,

since
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
has continuous sample paths,
B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
is an open set, and
\mathbb{P}^{x}(\uptau_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}<\infty)>0
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Finally, we show that
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is aperiodic. Specifically, we show that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p(n,x,B)>0,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

whenever
\uppsi(B)>0,
where
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For
x\in B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
, this relation clearly holds. For
x\in B^{c}_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
and
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with
\uppsi(B)>0
, we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p(n,x,B)\geq\int_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p(n-t,x,\mathrm{d}y)p(t,y,B),\qquad t\in(0,1).

Since
p(t,y,B)>0
for
y\in B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
, it suffices to show that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p(n-t,x,B_{r_{0}}(x_{0}))\geq\mathbb{P}^{x}\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\{X(n-t)\in B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})\}\right)>0

for some
t\in(0,1)
. Now, assume by contradiction that this is not the case, i.e.,

\mathbb{P}^{x}\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\{X(n-t)\in B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})\}\right)=0,\qquad t\in(0,1)\,.

This implies that

\mathbb{P}^{x}\left(\bigcup_{q\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\{X(q)\in B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})\}\right)=0,

which is impossible, since
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
has continuous sample paths,
B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
is an open set and
\mathbb{P}^{x}(\uptau_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}<\infty)>0
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Therefore, the original assumption must be false, and we conclude that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p(n,x,B)>0

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\uppsi(B)>0,
proving aperiodicity.
∎

We are now in position to prove Theorem
1.1
. To do so, we apply
[
19
, Theorem 5.2]
.
However, note that the notion of aperiodicity used in
[
19
, Theorem 5.2]
differs slightly from the one used in this article. Therefore, we need to show that the aperiodicity defined in this article implies the notion used in
[
19
]
.
Indeed, by

[
53
, Proposition 6.1]
,
[
54
, Theorem 4.2]
, along with the
aperiodicity of
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
, there exists a petite set
C\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, a time
T>0
, and a non-trivial measure
\upnu_{C}(\mathrm{d}x)
on
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, such that
\upnu_{C}(C)>0
and

p(t,x,B)\geq\upnu_{C}(B),\qquad x\in C,\quad t\geq T,\quad B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

In particular, we have

p(t,x,C)>0,\qquad x\in C,\quad t\geq T,

which aligns exactly with the definition of aperiodicity used in
[
19
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Define

\bar{L}(r)\coloneqq\int_{r_{0}}^{r}\mathrm{e}^{-I_{x_{0}}(u)}\int_{u}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{I_{x_{0}}(v)}}{\gamma_{x_{0}}(v)}\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}u,\qquad r\geq r_{0}.

Clearly, for
r\geq r_{0}
it holds that
\bar{L}(r)\leq\Lambda
,

\displaystyle\bar{L}^{\prime}(r)=\mathrm{e}^{-I_{x_{0}}(r)}\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{I_{x_{0}}(u)}}{\gamma_{x_{0}}(u)}\mathrm{d}u\qquad\text{and}\qquad\bar{L}^{\prime\prime}(r)=-\iota_{x_{0}}(r)\frac{\bar{L}^{\prime}(r)}{r}-\frac{1}{\gamma_{x_{0}}(r)}.

Further, fix
r_{1}>r_{0}
and let
L:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty)
,
L\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, be such that
L(x)=\bar{L}(|x-x_{0}|)+1
for
x\in B^{c}_{r_{1}}(x_{0}).
Now, for
x\in B^{c}_{r_{1}}(x_{0})
, we have (recall the representation of
\mathcal{G}
given in (
1.2
))

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}L(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}C_{x_{0}}(x)\bar{L}^{\prime\prime}(|x-x_{0}|)+\frac{\bar{L}^{\prime}(|x-x_{0}|)}{2|x-x_{0}|}(2A(x)-C_{x_{0}}(x)+2B_{x_{0}}(x))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\bar{L}^{\prime}(|x-x_{0}|)}{2|x-x_{0}|}(2A(x)-C_{x_{0}}(x)+2B_{x_{0}}(x)-C_{x_{0}}(x)\iota_{x_{0}}(x))-\frac{C_{x_{0}}(x)}{2\gamma_{x_{0}}(|x-x_{0}|)}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{C_{x_{0}}(x)}{2\gamma_{x_{0}}(|x-x_{0}|)}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2(\Lambda+1)}L(x).

Thus, we have obtained the relation in (
1.4
) with
c_{1}=1/(2(\Lambda+1))
,
C=\bar{B}_{r_{1}}(x_{0})
and
c_{2}=\sup_{x\in C}|\mathcal{G}L(x)|
, which proves the assertion of the theorem.
∎

3.
Examples and Applications of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we provide several examples of diffusion processes that satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, along with an application of Theorem
1.1
to a class of Markov processes with jumps.
We begin with a simple example of a diffusion process with polynomial drift.

Example 3.1
.

Let
\sigma(x)=I_{d}
(the
d\times d
identity matrix) and let
b(x)=-Kx|x|^{\kappa-1}
with
K>0
and
\kappa>1
. It is straightforward to verify that
b(x)
and
\sigma(x)
satisfy assumptions (A1)-(A5) with
x_{0}=0
and arbitrary
r_{0}>0
, and that
\gamma_{x_{0}}(r)=1
and

I_{x_{0}}(r)=\frac{2K}{\kappa+1}r_{0}^{\kappa+1}-\frac{2K}{\kappa+1}r^{\kappa+1}.

We then have

\Lambda=\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2K}{\kappa+1}u^{\kappa+1}}\int_{u}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{2K}{\kappa+1}v^{\kappa+1}}\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}u.

Observe that

\displaystyle\int_{u}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{2K}{\kappa+1}v^{\kappa+1}}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=(2K)^{\frac{-2\kappa-1}{\kappa+1}}(\kappa+1)^{\frac{\kappa}{\kappa+1}}\int_{\frac{2K}{\kappa+1}u^{\kappa+1}}v^{-\frac{\kappa}{\kappa+1}}\mathrm{e}^{-v}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=(2K)^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(\kappa+1)^{-\frac{\kappa+1}{\kappa}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa+1},\frac{2K}{\kappa+1}u^{\kappa+1}\right),

where
\Gamma(p,q)
,
p,q\in\mathbb{R}
, is the incomplete gamma function. According to
[
1
, display 6.5.32]
,

\lim_{u\to\infty}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa+1},\frac{2K}{\kappa+1}u^{\kappa+1}\right)}{u^{-\kappa}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{2K}{\kappa+1}u^{\kappa+1}}}=2^{-\frac{\kappa}{\kappa+1}}(\kappa+1)^{\frac{\kappa}{\kappa+1}},

which proves that
\Lambda<\infty.
∎

In the next example, we consider a one-dimensional diffusion process with a non-incremental drift coefficient.

Example 3.2
.

Let
\sigma(x)=1
, and let
b(x)
be locally Lipschitz continuous, such that
b(x)=-Kx|x|^{\kappa-1}(\cos x+\rho)
for
x\in B^{c}_{1}(0)
, where
K>0
,
\kappa>1
, and
\rho>0
. It is straightforward to verify that
b(x)
and
\sigma(x)
satisfy assumptions (A1)-(A5) with
x_{0}=0
and arbitrary
r_{0}>0
. Furthermore, we have
\gamma_{x_{0}}(r)=1
and

I_{x_{0}}(r)=\frac{2K\rho}{\kappa+1}r_{0}^{\kappa+1}-\frac{2K\rho}{\kappa+1}r^{\kappa+1}-2K\int_{r_{0}}^{r}u^{\kappa}\cos u\,\mathrm{d}u.

Note that

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\int_{r_{0}}^{r}u^{\kappa}\cos u\,\mathrm{d}u}{r^{k+\varepsilon}}=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{r^{k}\sin r-r_{0}^{k}\sin r_{0}-\kappa\int_{r_{0}}^{r}u^{\kappa-1}\sin u\,\mathrm{d}u}{r^{k+\varepsilon}}=0

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Thus, the finiteness of
\Lambda
reduces on showing the finiteness of

\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2K\rho}{\kappa+1}u^{\kappa+1}}\int_{u}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{2K\rho}{\kappa+1}v^{\kappa+1}}\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}u,

which, as shown in Example
1.1
, is finite if, and only if,
\kappa>1
.
∎

In the following example, we discuss uniform ergodicity of a class of Langevin tempered diffusion processes.

Example 3.3
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1/d)
, and let
\pi\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be strictly positive, defined as

\pi(x)\coloneqq c|x|^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}}
for some
c>0
and for all
x\in B_{1}^{c}(0)
. Additionally, assume that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\pi(x)\mathrm{d}x=1
.
For
\beta\in(0,(1+\alpha(2-d))/2)
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define

\sigma(x)\coloneqq(\pi(x))^{-\beta}I_{d},

and

b(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}(\sigma(x)\sigma(x)^{T}\nabla\log(\pi(x))+\nabla\cdot\sigma(x)^{T}\sigma(x))=\frac{1-2\beta}{2}\pi(x)^{-2\beta}\nabla\log(\pi(x)).

Observe that

\sigma(x)=c^{-\beta}|x|^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\quad\text{and}\quad b(x)=-\frac{1-2\beta}{2\alpha}c^{-2\beta}x|x|^{\frac{2\beta}{\alpha}-2},\qquad x\in B_{1}^{c}(0).

Note that
b(x)
and
\sigma(x)
do not in general satisfy assumptions (A2) and A(3). However, observe that in the proof of Theorem
1.1
we did not actually use the fact that
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is a unique strong solution to (
1.1
). All that we required is that the martingale problem for
(b,\sigma\sigma^{T})
is well posed, which is equivalent to (
1.1
) admitting a unique (in distribution) weak solution (see
[
59
, Theorem V.20.1]
). Furthermore, in
[
25
, Proposition 15]
it has been shown that the SDE in (
1.1
)
admits a weak solution

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}(t)\}_{t\geq 0},\{B(t)\}_{t\geq 0},\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
,
which is a conservative strong Markov process with continuous sample paths.
Moreover, it is open-set irreducible, aperiodic, every compact set is petite, and

\uppi(\mathrm{d}x)\coloneqq\pi(x)\mathrm{d}x
is its unique invariant probability measure.
Here,
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}(t)\}_{t\geq 0},\{B(t)\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
is a
standard
n
-dimensional Brownian motion.
Now, take
x_{0}=0
and
r_{0}>1
. It is straightforward to verify that
\gamma_{x_{0}}(r)=c^{-2\beta}r^{\frac{2\beta}{\alpha}}
and

I_{x_{0}}(r)=\frac{1-2\beta}{\alpha}\ln r_{0}-\frac{1-2\beta}{\alpha}\ln r.

We then have

\Lambda=c^{2\beta}\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1-2\beta}{\alpha}\ln u}\int_{u}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1-2\beta}{\alpha}\ln v}}{v^{\frac{2\beta}{\alpha}}}\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}u=c^{2\beta}\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}u^{\frac{1-2\beta}{\alpha}}\int_{u}^{\infty}v^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}u.

This simplifies to

\Lambda=\frac{\alpha c^{2\beta}}{1-\alpha}\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}u^{1-\frac{2\beta}{\alpha}}\mathrm{d}u,

which is finite if, and only if,
\alpha<\beta.
∎

The results discussed in this article can be used to establish uniform ergodicity of a class of Markov processes with jumps. Recall that a subordinator
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is a non-decreasing Lévy process on
\left[0,\infty\right)
. If
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
are independent,
then the process
X^{S}(t)\coloneqq X(S(t))
,
t\geq 0
, obtained by applying random time change to
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
through
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
, is referred to as the subordinate process
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
with subordinator
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
in the sense of Bochner. It is easy to see that
\{X^{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is a Markov process with the transition kernel

p^{S}(t,x,\mathrm{d}y)=\int_{\left[0,\infty\right)}p(s,x,\mathrm{d}y)\mu_{t}(\mathrm{d}s),

where
\upmu_{t}(\mathrm{d}s)=\mathbb{P}(S(t)\in\mathrm{d}s)
is the transition probability of
S(t)
,
t\geq 0
. Additionally, it is straightforward to check that if
\uppi(\mathrm{d}x)
is an invariant probability measure for
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
, then it is also invariant for the subordinate process
\{X^{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
.
Moreover, if

\lVert p(t,x,\mathrm{d}{y})-\uppi(\mathrm{d}{y})\rVert_{{\rm TV}}\leq B(x)r(t),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad t\geq 0,

for some
B:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty)
and measurable
r:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
,
then

\displaystyle\lVert p^{S}(t,x,\mathrm{d}{y})-\uppi(\mathrm{d}{y})\rVert_{{\rm TV}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\int_{\left[0,\infty\right)}(p(s,x,\mathrm{d}y)-\uppi(\mathrm{d}{y}))\upmu_{t}(\mathrm{d}s)\right\rVert_{{\rm TV}}

\displaystyle\leq c(x)\int_{\left[0,\infty\right)}r(s)\upmu_{t}(\mathrm{d}s)

\displaystyle=B(x)\mathbb{E}[r(S(t))].

Furthermore, since
\mathbb{E}[S(t)]>0
for
t>0
, the strong law of large numbers (see, e.g.,
[
63
, Theorems 36.5 and 36.6]
) implies that
\lim_{t\to\infty}S(t)=\infty
, almost surely. Hence, if
r(t)=\mathrm{e}^{-\beta t}
for some
\beta>0
, the dominated convergence theorem gives that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[r(S(t))]=0.

Therefore, if
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is uniformly ergodic, then
\{X^{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is also uniformly ergodic for any subordinator
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
.

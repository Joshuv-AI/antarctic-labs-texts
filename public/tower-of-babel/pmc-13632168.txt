Loop-assisted anterior removal of a metallic vitreous intraocular foreign body: The cowboy technique
Authors: Ryuya Hashimoto
Published: 2026

Short video description

This video demonstrates a safe and practical surgical strategy for the management of a metallic vitreous intraocular foreign body associated with traumatic retinal detachment. We present the “Cowboy technique,” a loop-assisted maneuver using a 27-gauge Finesse Flex Loop to stabilize a metallic wire-shaped intraocular foreign body during anterior-route extraction. This technique may enhance intraoperative safety and control, help prevent accidental slippage during manipulation, and facilitate safer removal with a reduced risk of retinal trauma.

Narration script used in the video

This video demonstrates the surgical management of traumatic retinal detachment caused by a metallic wire intraocular foreign body, using a loop-assisted anterior removal technique. After conjunctival peritomy, all four rectus muscles were isolated and controlled. Inferior traction sutures using 5-0 polyester were placed to facilitate placement of an inferior scleral buckle. Cataract surgery was performed, followed by creation of a four-port pars plana vitrectomy system with a 27-gauge chandelier for bimanual manipulation. After core vitrectomy, perfluorocarbon liquid was injected to stabilize the detached posterior retina and protect the macula. Peripheral vitreous shaving was then performed. A circular posterior capsulotomy was created to establish an extraction route through the anterior chamber. Under perfluorocarbon liquid, the wire-shaped metallic intraocular foreign body was first grasped with forceps and then guided into the twenty-seven-gauge Finesse Flex Loop. Because this type of foreign body is long, slender, and unstable, it may slip after initial capture, particularly over the detached retina. The Finesse Flex Loop provided additional stabilization and enabled safer, more controlled removal through the anterior chamber. We refer to this loop-assisted lasso-like stabilization maneuver as the cowboy technique. A 7-mm intraocular lens was then inserted and fixated in the sulcus. Fluid-air exchange, photocoagulation, and SF6 gas tamponade were then performed. This technique may be useful for elongated, wire-shaped intraocular foreign bodies that can be safely encircled by the loop.

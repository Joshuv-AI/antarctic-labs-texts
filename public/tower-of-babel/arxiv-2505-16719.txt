p p -Bifree biset functors
Olcay COŞKUN, Deniz YILMAZ

Abstract
We introduce and study the category of p p -bifree biset functors for a fixed prime p p , defined via bisets whose left and right stabilizers are p ′ p^{\prime} -groups. This category naturally lies between the classical biset functors and the diagonal p p -permutation functors, serving as a bridge between them. Every biset functor and every diagonal p p -permutation functor restricts to a p p -bifree biset functor. We classify the simple p p -bifree biset functors over a field K K of characteristic zero, showing that they are parametrized by pairs ( G , V ) (G,V) , where G G is a finite group and V V is a simple K ​ Out ​ ( G ) K\mathrm{Out}(G) -module. As key examples, we compute the composition factors of several representation-theoretic functors in the p p -bifree setting, including the Burnside ring functor, the p p -bifree Burnside functor, the Brauer character ring functor, and the ordinary character ring functor. We further investigate classical simple biset functors, S C p × C p , ℂ S_{C_{p}\times C_{p},\mathbb{C}} and S C q × C q , ℂ S_{C_{q}\times C_{q},\mathbb{C}} for a prime q ≠ p q\neq p . MSC2020: 18A25, 18B99, 19A22, 20J15. Keywords: biset functor, diagonal p p -permutation functor, simple functor, Burnside ring, character ring.

Introduction

The theory of biset functors, which was introduced and extensively developed by Bouc, occupies a central position in the functorial representation theory of finite groups. It enables a unified treatment of representation rings when the structural maps restriction, induction, deflation, inflation, and isogation are present. The completion of the classification of endo-permutation modules of
p
-groups
[
Bc06
]
and the description of the unit group of Burnside rings of
p
-groups
[
Bc07
]
, both due to Bouc, are two notable applications of the theory of biset functors.

Diagonal
p
-permutation functors, introduced by Bouc and the second author
[
BY20
]
, provide a functorial framework for studying structures involving actions of
p
-permutation bimodules with additional constraints. By replacing bisets with
p
-permutation bimodules whose vertices are twisted diagonals, this theory captures essential representation-theoretic phenomena, particularly those related to block theory. Diagonal
p
-permutation functors have already found applications in the block theory of finite groups; see for instance the finiteness result in terms of functorial equivalences, Theorem 10.6 in
[
BY22
]
, which is in the spirit of Puig’s and Donovan’s finiteness conjectures.

Although the two theories of biset functors and diagonal
p
-permutation functors are both defined on categories of finite groups, there is no direct functorial connection between them. However, their morphisms are related as follows. A diagonal
p
-permutation bimodule is a
p
-permutation bimodule whose indecomposable summands have (twisted) diagonal subgroups as vertices. The linearization map applied to bisets yields permutation, and hence
p
-permutation bimodules. In particular, the elementary bisets

\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{H}\;\ (H\leqslant G),\;\ \mathrm{Ind}^{G}_{H}\;\ (H\leqslant G),\;\ \mathrm{Iso}(f)\;\ (f:G\xrightarrow{\sim}H),

\mathrm{Inf}^{G}_{G/N},\mathrm{Def}^{G}_{G/N}\;\ (N\unlhd G,\ N\text{ is }p^{\prime}\text{-normal})

give rise to morphisms in the diagonal
p
-permutation category via linearization. In contrast, inflations and deflations along general normal subgroups do not yield diagonal
p
-permutation bimodules under linearization; see
[
BY20
, Lemma 4.2]
. This obstruction prevents the existence of a direct functor between the categories of biset functors and of diagonal
p
-permutation functors.

Motivated by this observation, we introduce the notions of
p
-bifree bisets and
p
-bifree biset functors. A
p
-bifree biset
is a biset with
p^{\prime}
-stabilizers on both sides. The category of

-bifree bisets is similar to the classical biset category, but only includes inflations and deflations via
p^{\prime}
-normal subgroups. For a commutative ring

With this definition, the category of

3.1 Definition

An

-bifree biset functor over

Together with natural transformations,

-bifree biset functors over

By

-bifree biset functors over

3.2 Remark

(a) Every biset functor becomes a

(b) Let

3.3 Definition

Let

-bifree biset functor and let

\displaystyle\underline{F}(G)=\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}|H|<|G|\\

p^{\prime}
-normal subgroup. Then
H
is also isomorphic to a quotient of
\delta(G)
by a
p^{\prime}
-normal subgroup.

(ii)

Let
N\unlhd G
be a
p^{\prime}
-normal subgroup. The following are equivalent:

(a)

m_{G,N}\neq 0
,

(b)

\delta(G)
is isomorphic to a quotient of
G/N
by a
p^{\prime}
-subgroup,

(c)

\delta(G)\cong\delta(G/N)
.

(iii)

In particular, if
N\unlhd G
is a normal
p^{\prime}
-subgroup, then
G/N\cong\delta(G)
if and only if
G/N
is a
B^{\Delta}_{p}
-group and
m_{G,N}\neq 0
.

Let
B^{\Delta}_{p}
-gr denote the class of
B^{\Delta}_{p}
-groups, and let
[B^{\Delta}_{p}\text{-gr}]
be a fixed set of representatives of their isomorphism classes. Define a relation
\gg
on
B^{\Delta}_{p}
-gr by declaring
G\gg H
if and only if
H
is isomorphic to a quotient of
G
by a
p^{\prime}
-group. A subset
\mathcal{M}\subseteq B^{\Delta}_{p}\text{-gr}
is said to be
closed
if, whenever
H\in\mathcal{M}
and
G\gg H
, it follows that
G\in\mathcal{M}
.

The following result describes the lattice of subfunctors of the Burnside functor
\mathbb{K}B
in terms of these closed subsets. Its proof is the same as that of Theorem 5.4.14 in
[
Bc10a
]
, with the necessary modifications to the
p
-bifree bisets.

4.7 Theorem

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of of
p
-bifree biset subfunctors of the Burnside functor
\mathbb{K}B
, ordered by inclusion, and let
\mathcal{T}
be the set of closed subsets of the set
[B^{\Delta}_{p}\text{-gr}]
of isomorphism classes of
B^{\Delta}_{p}
-groups, ordered by inclusion.
Then the assignment

\Theta:F\mapsto\{H\in[B^{\Delta}_{p}\text{-gr}]\mid\textbf{e}^{\Delta}_{H}\subseteq F\}

defines an isomorphism of posets
\Theta:\mathcal{S}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{T}
.

Its inverse is given by

\Psi:A\mapsto\sum_{H\in A}\textbf{e}^{\Delta}_{H}.

We now describe the composition factors of the Burnside functor
\mathbb{K}B
as a
p
-bifree biset functor. For each
B^{\Delta}_{p}
-group
G
, the subfunctor
\textbf{e}^{\Delta}_{G}
is crucial, and its maximal proper subfunctor determines a unique simple quotient. We refer to Section 5 of
[
Bc10a
]
for the proofs of the following results up to the end of the section.

4.8 Proposition

(i)
Let
G
be a
B^{\Delta}_{p}
-group. Then the functor
\textbf{e}^{\Delta}_{G}
has a unique maximal subfunctor

\textbf{j}_{G}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H\in[B^{\Delta}_{p}\text{-gr}]\\
H\gg G,\ H\not\cong G\end{subarray}}\textbf{e}^{\Delta}_{H},

and the quotient
\textbf{e}^{\Delta}_{G}/\textbf{j}_{G}
is isomorphic to the simple functor
S^{\Delta}_{G,\mathbb{K}}
.

(ii)
Let
F\subset F^{\prime}
be subfunctors of
\mathbb{K}B
such that
F^{\prime}/F
is simple. Then there exists a unique
G\in[B^{\Delta}_{p}\text{-gr}]
such that
\textbf{e}^{\Delta}_{G}\subseteq F^{\prime}
and
\textbf{e}^{\Delta}_{G}\nsubseteq F
. In particular, one has

\textbf{e}^{\Delta}_{G}+F=F^{\prime},\quad\textbf{e}^{\Delta}_{G}\cap F=\textbf{j}_{G},\quad\text{and}\quad F^{\prime}/F\cong S^{\Delta}_{G,\mathbb{K}}.

4.9 Corollary

The composition factors of
\mathbb{K}B
as a
p
-bifree biset functor are precisely the simple functors
S^{\Delta}_{G,\mathbb{K}}
, where
G
runs over the set
[B^{\Delta}_{p}\text{-gr}]
.

To compute the evaluation of the simple functor
S^{\Delta}_{G,\mathbb{K}}
at a finite group
H
, we first describe the structure of
\textbf{e}^{\Delta}_{G}(H)
in terms of the subgroup lattice of
H
.

4.10 Proposition

Let
G
and
H
be finite groups. Then the evaluation
\textbf{e}^{\Delta}_{G}(H)
is the subspace of
\mathbb{K}B(H)
spanned by the idempotents
e^{H}_{K}
, where
K
runs over a set of representatives of conjugacy classes of subgroups
K\leq H
satisfying
K\gg\delta(G)
.

This description allows one to compute the dimension of
S^{\Delta}_{G,\mathbb{K}}(H)
explicitly in terms of the minimal groups
\delta(K)
associated to the subgroups
K\leq H
.

4.11 Theorem

Let
G
be a
B^{\Delta}_{p}
-group and
H
a finite group. Then the
\mathbb{K}
-dimension of
S^{\Delta}_{G,\mathbb{K}}(H)
is equal to the number of conjugacy classes of subgroups
K\leq H
such that
\delta(K)\cong G
.

5
The functor
\mathbb{K}B^{\Delta}

In this section, we study the representable functor
\mathbb{K}B^{\Delta}(-,1)
, which assigns to each finite group
G
the
\mathbb{K}
-vector space
\mathbb{K}B^{\Delta}(G,1)
. This functor is a
p
-bifree analogue of the classical representable biset functor
\mathbb{K}B(-,1)
, and its structure can be analyzed using the same tools developed for the Burnside functor. In particular, we show that its composition factors are parametrized by
B
-groups of
p^{\prime}
-order, and we describe the corresponding simple functors explicitly. We introduce this functor as a Grothendieck group as follows.

Let
R
be a commutative ring with identity and let
\mathbb{K}
be a field of characteristic zero. Let
G
be a finite group. A left
G
-set is said to be
left
p
-free
, if it is a disjoint union of transitive
G
-sets with
p^{\prime}
-stabilizers. Let
\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\phantom{|}}_{G}\mathsf{set}^{\Delta}
denote the category of left
p
-free
G
-sets. One can similarly define the category
\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\phantom{|}}\mathsf{set}_{G}^{\Delta}
of right
p
-free
G
-sets. Identifying a
G
-set
X
with a
(G,1)
-biset induces an isomorphism
\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\phantom{|}}_{G}\mathsf{set}^{\Delta}\cong\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\phantom{|}}_{G}\mathsf{set}_{1}^{\Delta}
.

Let
B^{\Delta}(G)
denote the Grothendieck group of the category
\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\phantom{|}}_{G}\mathsf{set}^{\Delta}
. Note that

\displaystyle B^{\Delta}(G)=\bigoplus_{H\in[s_{G}]_{p^{\prime}}}\mathbb{Z}[G/H]\,,

where
[s_{G}]_{p^{\prime}}
denotes a set of representatives of the
G
-conjugacy classes of
p^{\prime}
-subgroups of
G
.

5.1 Remark

Let
H
and
K
be subgroups of
G
. Then

\displaystyle|(G/K)^{H}|=|\{x\in G/K\,|\,H^{x}\subseteq K\}|\,.

In particular, if
K
is a
p^{\prime}
-group, i.e., if
[G/K]\in B^{\Delta}(G)
and if
H
is not a
p^{\prime}
-subgroup, then
|(G/K)^{H}|=0
. This means that the mark morphism

\displaystyle\phi:B(G)\to\prod_{H\in[s_{G}]}\mathbb{Z},\quad[X]\mapsto(|X^{H}|)_{H\in[s_{G}]}

restricts to a map

\displaystyle\phi:B^{\Delta}(G)\to\prod_{H\in[s_{G}]_{p^{\prime}}}\mathbb{Z},\quad[X]\mapsto(|X^{H}|)_{H\in[s_{G}]_{p^{\prime}}}

which we denote again by
\phi
. It follows that the primitive idempotents of
\mathbb{K}B^{\Delta}(G)
are precisely the idempotents
e^{G}_{H}\in\mathbb{K}B(G)
where
H
is a
p^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
. Setting

\displaystyle e^{G}_{p^{\prime}}:=\sum_{H\in[s_{G}]_{p^{\prime}}}e^{G}_{H}

we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}B^{\Delta}(G)=e_{p^{\prime}}^{G}\mathbb{K}B(G)=\mathbb{K}B(G)e_{p^{\prime}}^{G}=e_{p^{\prime}}^{G}\mathbb{K}B(G)e_{p^{\prime}}^{G}\,.

5.2 Remark

Note that the commutative ring
\mathbb{K}B^{\Delta}(G)
does not have an identity element unless
G
is a
p^{\prime}
-subgroup.

We consider the functor
RB^{\Delta}:R\mathcal{C}^{\Delta}\to\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\phantom{|}}_{R}\mathsf{Mod}
defined as

•

G\mapsto RB^{\Delta}(G)
.

•

X\in RB^{\Delta}(H,G)\mapsto\left(RB(X):RB^{\Delta}(G)\to RB^{\Delta}(H),\quad U\mapsto X\cdot_{G}U\right)
.

Note that
RB^{\Delta}
is isomorphic to the representable functor
RB^{\Delta}(-,1)
at
1
. Also, it is a
p
-bifree subfunctor of
RB
.

By Theorem
4.7
, the poset of the subfunctors of
\mathbb{K}B^{\Delta}
is isomorphic to the poset of closed subsets of

\displaystyle\Theta(\mathbb{K}B^{\Delta})=\{H\in[B^{\Delta}_{p}\text{-gr}]\,|\,\textbf{e}^{\Delta}_{H}\subseteq\mathbb{K}B^{\Delta}\}\,.

This observation allows us to identify the minimal groups contributing to the functor
\mathbb{K}B^{\Delta}
. The next result shows that they are exactly the
B
-groups of
p^{\prime}
-order, in the sense of Bouc’s theory. As a consequence, we obtain the following classification of its composition factors.

5.3 Lemma

Let
H
be a
B^{\Delta}
-group. Then
\textbf{e}^{\Delta}_{H}\subseteq\mathbb{K}B^{\Delta}
if and only if
H
is a
p^{\prime}
-group.

Proof.

Suppose
\textbf{e}^{\Delta}_{H}\subseteq\mathbb{K}B^{\Delta}
. Then
e^{H}_{H}\in\textbf{e}^{\Delta}(H)\subseteq\mathbb{K}B^{\Delta}(H)
. By the remark above, it follows that
H
is a
p^{\prime}
-group. Conversely, if
H
is a
p^{\prime}
-group, then
e^{H}_{H}\in\mathbb{K}B^{\Delta}(H)
and hence
\textbf{e}^{\Delta}_{H}\subseteq\mathbb{K}B^{\Delta}
.
∎

5.4 Corollary

The composition factors of the
p
-bifree biset functor
\mathbb{K}B^{\Delta}
are the functors
S^{\Delta}_{G,\mathbb{K}}
where
G
is a
B
-group of
p^{\prime}
-order.

6
The simple functor
S_{1,\mathbb{K}}^{\Delta}

We now turn to the simple functor
S^{\Delta}_{1,\mathbb{K}}
, corresponding to the trivial group and the trivial module. This functor plays a foundational role in the theory, as it appears as a composition factor in both the Burnside and character functors. In this section, we compute the dimension of
S^{\Delta}_{1,\mathbb{K}}(G)
for an arbitrary finite group
G
, and show that it is governed by the number of conjugacy classes of cyclic
p^{\prime}
-subgroups of
G
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring with identity and let
\mathbb{K}
be a field of characteristic zero. Let
(G,V)
be a pair of a finite group
G
and a simple
R\mathrm{Out}(G)
-module
V
. We first describe the simple functor
S_{G,V}
in more detail.

Let
E_{G}:=RB^{\Delta}(G,G)
denote the endomorphism algebra of
G
in the category
R\mathcal{C}^{\Delta}
. Then
\mathcal{E}^{\Delta}(G)=E_{G}/I_{G}\cong R\mathrm{Out}(G)
. We consider
V
as a simple
E_{G}
-module and define the functor
L_{G,V}
by

\displaystyle L_{G,V}(H)=RB^{\Delta}(H,G)\otimes_{E_{G}}V\,.

Then by
[
Bc96
]
,
L_{G,V}
has a unique maximal subfunctor
J_{G,V}
whose evaluation at a finite group
H
is given by

\displaystyle J_{G,V}(H)=\Bigl\{\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes v_{i}\in L_{G,V}(H)\,|\,\forall y\in RB^{\Delta}(G,H):\sum_{i}(y\cdot_{H}x_{i})(v_{i})=0\Bigr\}\,.

The simple functor
S_{G,V}^{\Delta}
is defined as the quotient
L_{G,V}/J_{G,V}
.

We compute the
\mathbb{K}
-dimension of
S_{1,\mathbb{K}}^{\Delta}(G)
inspired by the proof of
[
Bc96
, Proposition 8]
. Note that the functor
L_{1,\mathbb{K}}
is isomorphic to the functor
\mathbb{K}B^{\Delta}
. Moreover for any finite group
G
, identifying
L_{1,\mathbb{K}}(G)
with
\mathbb{K}B^{\Delta}(G)
one has

\displaystyle J_{1,\mathbb{K}}(G)=\{X\in\mathbb{K}B^{\Delta}(G)\,|\,\forall Y\in\mathbb{K}B^{\Delta}(G):|G\setminus(Y\times X)|=0\}\,.

This implies that the dimension of
S^{\Delta}_{1,\mathbb{K}}(G)
is equal to the rank of the bilinear form

\displaystyle\langle-,-\rangle:\mathbb{K}B^{\Delta}(G)\times\mathbb{K}B^{\Delta}(G)\to\mathbb{K},\quad(X,Y)\mapsto|G\setminus(Y\times X)|\,.

The set of primitive idempotents
\{e^{G}_{H}\}_{H\in[s_{G}]_{p^{\prime}}}
form an orthogonal basis with respect to this bilinear form. Moreover, for
H\in[s_{G}]_{p^{\prime}}
, one has

\displaystyle\langle e^{G}_{H},e^{G}_{H}\rangle

\displaystyle=|G\setminus e^{G}_{H}|=\frac{1}{|N_{G}(H)|}\sum_{K\leqslant H}|K|\mu(K,H)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|N_{G}(H)|}\sum_{x\in H}\sum_{\langle x\rangle\leqslant K\leqslant H}\mu(K,H)=\frac{\phi_{1}(H)}{|N_{G}(H)|}

where
\phi_{1}(H)
is the number of elements
x\in H
such that
\langle x\rangle=H
. This is non-zero if and only if
H
is cyclic. This proves the following.

6.1 Theorem

Let
G
be a finite group. The
\mathbb{K}
-dimension of
S^{\Delta}_{1,\mathbb{K}}(G)
is equal to the number of conjugacy classes of cyclic
p^{\prime}
-subgroups of
G
.

6.2 Remark

Let
G
be a finite group. Then
\delta(G)=1
if and only if
G
is a cyclic
p^{\prime}
-group. Thus, Theorem
6.1
is a special case of Theorem
4.11
.

7
The functor of Brauer characters

In this section, we study the Brauer character functor
\mathbb{K}R_{k}
, which assigns to each finite group
G
the Grothendieck group of finite-dimensional
kG
-modules (with respect to short exact sequences), tensored with a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero. When
k
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
, the functor
\mathbb{K}R_{k}(-)
admits a natural structure of a
p
-bifree biset functor. Our goal is to determine its composition factors. It turns out that
\mathbb{K}R_{k}(-)
decomposes as a direct sum of simple
p
-bifree biset functors indexed by cyclic
p^{\prime}
-groups and primitive modules for their automorphism groups.

To begin with, if
X
is a
p
-bifree
(H,G)
-biset, then
kX
is flat as a right
kG
-module and hence tensoring with
kX
over
kG
induces a well-defined group homomorphism

\displaystyle R_{k}(X):=kX\otimes_{kG}-:R_{k}(G)\to R_{k}(H),

and a
\mathbb{K}
-linear map

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}R_{k}(X):\mathbb{K}R_{k}(G)\to\mathbb{K}R_{k}(H).

This endows
\mathbb{K}R_{k}(-)
with a structure of
p
-bifree biset functor.

7.1 Remark

Recall that one has an isomorphism
\mathbb{K}R_{k}(-)\cong\mathbb{K}\mathrm{Proj}(-)\cong S_{1,1,\mathbb{K}}
of simple diagonal
p
-permutation functors, see
[
BY20
, Theorem 5.20]
and
[
BY25
, Section 6]
.

Our aim is to describe the composition factors of
\mathbb{K}R_{k}(-)
as a
p
-bifree biset functor. We first determine the restriction kernels.

7.2 Proposition

Let
G
be a finite group.

(i)
One has
\underline{\mathbb{K}R_{k}}(G)=0
unless
G
is a cyclic
p^{\prime}
-group.

(ii)
Let
G
be a cyclic
p^{\prime}
-group. Then the
\mathbb{K}\mathrm{Aut}(G)
-module
\underline{\mathbb{K}R_{k}}(G)
is isomorphic to the direct sum of primitive
\mathbb{K}\mathrm{Aut}(G)
-modules.

Proof.

(i) Let
\chi\in\mathbb{K}R_{k}(G)
be a non-zero element and let
g\in G
with
\chi(g)\not=0
. Then
g
is a
p^{\prime}
-element and
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{\langle g\rangle}\chi\not=0
. This shows that
\chi\not\in\underline{\mathbb{K}R_{k}}(G)
and therefore,
\underline{\mathbb{K}R_{k}}(G)=0
if
G
is not a cyclic
p^{\prime}
-group.

(ii) Let
G
be a cyclic
p^{\prime}
-group. We may identify
\mathbb{K}R_{k}(H)
with
\mathbb{K}R_{\mathbb{C}}(H)
and
\underline{\mathbb{K}R_{k}}(H)
with
\underline{\mathbb{K}R_{\mathbb{C}}}(H)
by Lemma
10.1
. The result follows from Corollary 7.3.5
[
Bc10a
]
.
∎

The next step is to show that every element of
\mathbb{K}R_{k}(G)
lies, up to action by an element of the ideal
I_{G}
, in the restriction kernel. This will allow us to apply the general theory of restriction kernels and deduce composition factor multiplicities.

7.3 Proposition

Let
G
be a finite group. For any
\chi\in\mathbb{K}R_{k}(G)
, there exists
\chi^{\prime}\in\underline{\mathbb{K}R_{k}}(G)
and
\alpha\in I_{G}
satisfying the equality

\displaystyle\chi=\chi^{\prime}+\alpha\cdot\chi\,.

Proof.

By Proposition 2.5 (iv) in
[
BHabil
]
, for any
\chi\in\mathbb{K}R_{k}(G)
, we have

\chi=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\leqslant K\leqslant G\\
K\,cyclic,\,|K|_{p}=1\end{subarray}}|L|\mu(L,K)\mathrm{ind}_{L}^{G}\mathrm{res}^{G}_{L}\chi\,.

It follows that if
G
is not a cyclic
p^{\prime}
-group, the claim holds by putting
\chi^{\prime}=0
and setting

\alpha=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\leqslant K\leqslant G\\
K\,cyclic,\,|K|_{p}=1\end{subarray}}|L|\mu(L,K)\Big[\frac{G\times G}{\Delta(L)}\Big]\in I_{G}.

Now suppose
G
is a cyclic
p^{\prime}
-group. The restriction of
\mathbb{K}R_{k}
to the full subcategory of cyclic
p^{\prime}
-groups is semisimple and hence the result follows from Proposition
10.4
.
∎

With this result, we can now describe precisely when a simple
p
-bifree biset functor
S^{\Delta}_{H,V}
appears as a composition factor of
KR_{k}
, and compute its multiplicity.

7.4 Theorem

The simple
p
-bifree biset functor
S_{H,V}^{\Delta}
is a composition factor of the functor
\mathbb{K}R_{k}
if and only if
H
is a cyclic
p^{\prime}
-group and
V
is a primitive
\mathbb{K}\mathrm{Aut}(H)
-module. In this case, the multiplicity of
S_{H,V}^{\Delta}
is equal to one.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
10.2
and Proposition
7.3
that the multiplicity of
S_{H,V}^{\Delta}
in
\mathbb{K}R_{k}
as a composition factor is equal to that of the
\mathbb{K}\mathrm{Aut}(G)
-module
V
in
\underline{\mathbb{K}R_{k}}(H)
.

By Proposition
7.2
, the restriction kernel is zero unless
H
is a cyclic
p^{\prime}
-group. Hence the multiplicity of
S_{H,V}^{\Delta}
in
\mathbb{K}R_{k}
is equal to
0
if
H
is not a cyclic
p^{\prime}
-group. Furthermore, if
H
is a cyclic
p^{\prime}
-group, by Proposition
7.2
(ii), the multiplicity of
S_{H,V}^{\Delta}
in
\mathbb{K}R_{k}
is non-zero if and only if
V
is primitive, in which case it is equal to
1
.
∎

7.5 Proposition

Let
H
be a cyclic
p^{\prime}
-group and
V
a primitive
\mathbb{K}\mathrm{Aut}(H)
-module. The subfunctor
F
of
\mathbb{K}R_{k}
generated by
V
is isomorphic to the simple functor
S^{\Delta}_{H,V}
.

Proof.

First note that
F(H)\cong V
and
H
is a minimal group for
F
since
V
is in the restriction kernel. Suppose for a contradiction that
F
is not simple. Then there exists a composition factor
T
of
F
not isomorphic to
S^{\Delta}_{H,V}
. By Theorem
7.4
,
T\cong S^{\Delta}_{H^{\prime},V^{\prime}}
for some cyclic
p^{\prime}
-group
H^{\prime}
and a primitive
\mathbb{K}\mathrm{Aut}(H^{\prime})
-module
V^{\prime}
. Note that since
F
is generated by
V
, one has
|H|<|H^{\prime}|
. Note also that the functor
F
is a quotient of the functor
\Delta_{H,V}
introduced in Section 5 of
[
W10
]
.

Consider the full subcategory
\mathcal{C}_{H^{\prime}}
on the set of subquotients of
H^{\prime}
. By Corollary 5.4 in
[
W10
]
, the restriction of the functor
\Delta_{H,V}
to
\mathcal{C}_{H^{\prime}}
is isomorphic to the functor
\,\vbox to6.83331pt{}^{\mathcal{C}_{H^{\prime}}}\!\Delta_{H,V}
. But the category of
p
-bifree biset functors over
\mathcal{C}_{H^{\prime}}
is the same as the category of biset functors over
\mathcal{C}_{H^{\prime}}
and hence it is semisimple. In particular, the restriction of the functor
\Delta_{H,V}
to
\mathcal{C}_{H^{\prime}}
is simple as
\Delta_{H,V}
has a simple head by Corollary 5.5 in
[
W10
]
. This implies that the restriction of
F
to
\mathcal{C}_{H^{\prime}}
is a simple functor with minimal group
H
. On the other hand, the restriction of
T
appears as a composition factor in the restriction of
F
. Contradiction.
∎

7.6 Corollary

One has

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}R_{k}\cong\bigoplus_{(m,\xi)}S^{\Delta}_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi}}

where
(m,\xi)
runs through a set of pairs consisting of a positive
p^{\prime}
-number
m
and a primitive character
\xi
of
(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Theorem
7.4
and Proposition
7.5

∎

8
The functor of complex character ring

In this section, we examine the ordinary character ring functor
\mathbb{C}R_{\mathbb{C}}
as a
p
-bifree biset functor. Since the ordinary character ring is additive under biset composition and functorial with respect to maps in the
p
-bifree category, it inherits a natural
p
-bifree structure. We classify its composition factors explicitly and describe its relationship to the Brauer character functor
\mathbb{K}R_{k}
. The main result is a short exact sequence of

-bifree biset functors, which reveals how the modular and ordinary character theories fit together within this categorical framework.

Let
\mathbb{C}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. For a finite group

the Grothendieck ring of the category of finite dimensional

\mathbb{C}
. We set
\mathbb{C}R_{\mathbb{C}}(G)=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
.

If
H
is another finite group and

is a
p
-bifree
(H,G)
-biset, then tensoring with
\mathbb{C}X
over
\mathbb{C}G
induces a well-defined

-linear map

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}R_{\mathbb{C}}(X):\mathbb{C}R_{\mathbb{C}}(G)\to\mathbb{C}R_{\mathbb{C}}(H).

This endows
\mathbb{C}R_{\mathbb{C}}(-)
with a structure of
p
-bifree biset functor.

8.1 Remark

Given a pair
(G,V)
where
G
is a finite group and
V
is a simple
\mathbb{C}\mathrm{Out}(G)
-module, let
S_{G,V}
denote the associated simple biset functor. By
[
Bc10a
, Corollary 7.3.5]
, one has an isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}R_{\mathbb{C}}\cong\bigoplus_{(m,\xi)}S_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi}}

of biset functors, where
(m,\xi)
runs through the set of pairs consisting of a positive integer
m
and a primitive character
\xi:(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. This restricts to an isomorphism of
p
-bifree biset functors. Note that
S_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi}}
is not necessarily simple as a
p
-bifree biset functor. For any finite group
G
, we identify
S_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi}}(G)
as a subspace of
\mathbb{C}R_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
via this isomorphism. We also denote by
\tilde{\xi}
the (virtual) character of
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
obtained by extending
\xi
by
0
, i.e., for
x\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
,

\displaystyle\tilde{\xi}(x)=\begin{cases}\xi(x),\quad&\text{if }x\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\\
0,\quad&\text{otherwise}\,.\end{cases}

8.2 Lemma

Let
G
be a finite group. Let also
m
be a positive integer and
\xi
a primitive character of
(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Then for any
\chi\in S_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi}}(G)
, there exists
\chi^{\prime}\in\underline{S_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi}}}(G)
and
\alpha\in I_{G}
such that

\displaystyle\chi=\chi^{\prime}+\alpha\cdot\chi\,.

Proof.

By Proposition 1.5(iv) of Chapter 3 of
[
BHabil
]
, for any
\chi\in\mathbb{C}R_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
, we have

\displaystyle\chi=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\leqslant K\leqslant G\\
K\,cyclic\end{subarray}}|L|\mu(L,K)\mathrm{ind}_{L}^{G}\mathrm{res}^{G}_{L}\chi\,.

It follows that if
G
is not a cyclic group, the claim holds by putting
\chi^{\prime}=0
and setting

\alpha=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\leqslant K\leqslant G\\
K\,cyclic\end{subarray}}|L|\mu(L,K)\Big[\frac{G\times G}{\Delta(L)}\Big]\in I_{G}.

Now suppose
G=\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
is a cyclic group. If
n
is not a multiple of
m
, then by
[
Bc10a
, Corollary 7.4.3]

S_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi}}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})=\{0\}
and hence there is nothing to prove. Therefore, assume that
n
is a multiple of
m
and let
\chi\in S_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi}}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
. Then we can write

\chi=\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}\chi+(1-\tilde{e}_{G}^{G})\chi.

Since
1-\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}\in I_{G}
, it is sufficient to consider
\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}\chi
. But since
S_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi}}
is generated by
\tilde{\xi}
, the space
\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}S_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi}}(G)
is one dimensional generated by the inflation
\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}\mathrm{Inf}_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}^{G}\tilde{\xi}
. Hence we may put
\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}\chi=\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}\mathrm{Inf}_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}^{G}\tilde{\xi}
.

Let
N\leqslant G
be such that
G/N\cong\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
. Write
N=N_{p}\times N_{p^{\prime}}
. Assume first that
N_{p^{\prime}}\not=1
. Then, by
[
Bc10a
, Proposition 2.5.12 and Theorem 5.2.4]
, one has

\displaystyle(\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N_{p^{\prime}}}^{G}\mathrm{Def}_{G/N_{p^{\prime}}}^{G})\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N}^{G}\tilde{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N_{p^{\prime}}}^{G}(\mathrm{Def}_{G/N_{p^{\prime}}}^{G}\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N_{p^{\prime}}}^{G})\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N}^{G/N_{p^{\prime}}}\tilde{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{G,N_{p^{\prime}}}\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N_{p^{\prime}}}^{G}\tilde{e}_{G/N_{p^{\prime}}}^{G/N_{p^{\prime}}}\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N}^{G/N_{p^{\prime}}}\tilde{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{G,N_{p^{\prime}}}\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}\widetilde{(\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N_{p^{\prime}}}^{G}e_{G/N_{p^{\prime}}}^{G/N_{p^{\prime}}})}\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N_{p^{\prime}}}^{G}\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N}^{G/N_{p^{\prime}}}\tilde{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{G,N_{p^{\prime}}}\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N}^{G}\tilde{\xi}\,.

Since
G
is cyclic,
m_{G,N_{p^{\prime}}}\not=0
by
[
Bc10a
, Proposition 5.6.1]
. Thus, the condition is satisfied by putting
\chi^{\prime}=0
and
\alpha=\frac{1}{m_{G,N}}\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N_{p^{\prime}}}^{G}\mathrm{Def}_{G/N_{p^{\prime}}}^{G}
. This also shows that
\underline{S_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi}}}(G)=0
.

Now suppose that
N_{p^{\prime}}=1
, so
N
is a cyclic
p
-group. Note that the restriction of
\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}\mathrm{Inf}^{G}_{G/N}\tilde{\xi}
to a proper subgroup is equal to zero. Hence if
G
is a
p
-group, then
\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}\mathrm{Inf}^{G}_{G/N}\tilde{\xi}\in\underline{S_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi}}}(G)
. Again, the condition is satisfied in this case too.

Suppose
G
is not a
p
-group, then

\displaystyle\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N}^{G}\tilde{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{K\leqslant G}|K|\mu(K,G)\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{G}\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{K}\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N}^{G}\tilde{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{K\leqslant G}|K|\mu(K,G)\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{G}\mathrm{Inf}^{K}_{K/(K\cap N)}\mathrm{Iso}\mathrm{Res}^{G/N}_{KN/N}\tilde{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{K\leqslant G:KN=G}|K|\mu(K,G)\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{G}\mathrm{Inf}^{K}_{K/(K\cap N)}\mathrm{Iso}\,\tilde{\xi}

Note that since
N
is a
p
-subgroup of
G
, the condition
KN=G
holds if and only of
G_{p^{\prime}}\subseteq K
and
K_{p}N=G_{p}
. There are two cases:

(i)

If
N<G_{p}
, then
NK_{p}=G_{p}
holds if and only if
K_{p}=G_{p}
and in this case we get
K=G
.

(ii)

If
N=G_{p}
, then
K_{p}
can be any subgroup of
G_{p}
, so
K
is any subgroup with
G_{p^{\prime}}\subseteq K
.

In Case (i), we get

\displaystyle\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N}^{G}\tilde{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{K\leqslant G:KN=G}|K|\mu(K,G)\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{G}\mathrm{Inf}^{K}_{K/(K\cap N)}\mathrm{Iso}\,\tilde{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N}^{G}\tilde{\xi}

On the other hand, in Case (ii), we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N}^{G}\tilde{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{G_{p^{\prime}}\leqslant K\leqslant G}|K|\mu(K,G)\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{G}\mathrm{Inf}^{K}_{K/(K\cap N)}\mathrm{Iso}\,\tilde{\xi}\,.

Here we have
\mu(K,G)=\mu(|G:K|)
, the number theoretic Mobius function. We have
\mu(|G:K|)=1
if
G=K
, it is
-1
if
|G:K|=p
and
0
otherwise. Hence the above equality becomes

\displaystyle\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N}^{G}\tilde{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N}^{G}\mathrm{Iso}\,\tilde{\xi}-\frac{1}{p}\mathrm{Ind}\mathrm{Inf}_{K/(K\cap N)}^{G}\mathrm{Iso}\,\tilde{\xi}\,.

Now for any non-trivial
p^{\prime}
-subgroup
M
of
G
, we have

\mathrm{Def}^{G}_{G/M}\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N}^{G}\tilde{\xi}=\mathrm{Inf}_{G/NM}^{G/M}\mathrm{Def}^{G/N}_{G/NM}\tilde{\xi}=0

since
|G:NM|<|G:N|=m
. Hence
\tilde{e}^{G}_{G}\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N}^{G}\tilde{\xi}\in\underline{S_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi}}}(G)
in Case (i).

For Case (ii), we need to evaluate

\mathrm{Def}^{G}_{G/M}\Big(\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N}^{G}\mathrm{Iso}\,\xi-\frac{1}{p}\mathrm{Ind}\mathrm{Inf}_{K/(K\cap N)}^{G}\mathrm{Iso}\xi\Big)\,.

The first term is zero, by the above calculations. We also evaluate the second term:

\mathrm{Def}^{G}_{G/M}\mathrm{Ind}\mathrm{Inf}_{K/(K\cap N)}^{G}\mathrm{Iso}\,\tilde{\xi}=\mathrm{Ind}\mathrm{Inf}_{K/(M(K\cap N))}^{G/M}\mathrm{Def}^{K/(K\cap N)}_{K/(M(K\cap N))}\mathrm{Iso}\,\tilde{\xi}=0

Hence
\tilde{e}^{G}_{G}\mathrm{Inf}_{G/N}^{G}\tilde{\xi}\in\underline{S_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi}}}(G)
in Case (ii) too. The result follows.
∎

8.3 Notation

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\xi
be a primitive character of
(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and let
G=\mathbb{Z}/mp^{n}\mathbb{Z}
be the cyclic group of order
m\cdot p^{n}
. We set
\xi_{m,n,p}=\mathrm{Inf}^{G^{\times}}_{(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\xi
and
\tilde{\xi}_{m,n,p}:=\tilde{e}^{G}_{G}\mathrm{Inf}^{G}_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{\xi}
.

Note that for
g\in G
, one has

\displaystyle\tilde{\xi}_{m,n,p}(x)=\begin{cases}\mathrm{Inf}^{G}_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{\xi}(x),\quad&\text{if }\langle x\rangle=G\,,\\
0,\quad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Thus,
\tilde{\xi}_{m,n,p}
is the Dirichlet character of
G
induced by
\xi_{m,n,p}
. Since
\xi
is primitive, it follows that the conductor of
\xi_{m,n,p}
is equal to
m
.

8.4 Corollary

Let
G
be a finite group,
m
a positive integer, and
\xi
a primitive character of
(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
.

(a)

\underline{S_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi}}}(G)=\{0\}
unless
G
is a cyclic group of order
mp^{n}
for some integer
n\geqslant 0
.

(b)
If
G
is a cyclic group of order
mp^{n}
for some
n\geqslant 0
, then
\underline{S_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi}}}(G)
is one-dimensional generated by
\tilde{\xi}_{m,n,p}
.

Proof.

Both parts follow from the proof of Lemma
8.2
.
∎

Now we show that the functor
S_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi}}
is semisimple as a
p
-bifree biset functor.

8.5 Theorem

Let
m
be a positive integer and let
\xi
be a primitive character of
(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Then

\displaystyle S_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi}}\cong\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}S^{\Delta}_{\mathbb{Z}/mp^{n}\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi_{m,n,p}}}

as
p
-bifree biset functors.

Proof.

As before we regard
S:=S_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},\xi}
as a subfunctor of
\mathbb{C}R_{\mathbb{C}}
. By Lemma
8.2
, Corollary
8.4
and Theorem
10.2
, the composition factors of
S
are exactly the functors
S^{\Delta}_{\mathbb{Z}/mp^{n}\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi_{m,n,p}}}
each with multiplicity one. We will show that each of these functors appears as a subfunctor. Let
F
be a
p
-bifree biset subfunctor of
S
and let
G
be a minimal group for
F
. Since
F(K)=0
for any group
K
with
|K|<|G|
, we have
0\neq F(G)\subseteq\underline{S}(G)
. By Corollary
8.4
, it follows that
G
is a cyclic group of order
m\cdot p^{n}
for some natural number
n
, and that
F(G)
is one-dimensional generated by
\tilde{\xi}_{m,n,p}=\tilde{e}^{G}_{G}\mathrm{Inf}^{G}_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}\xi
. Conversely, if
G
is a cyclic group of order
m\cdot p^{n}
, then
\underline{S(G)}\neq 0
and the subfunctor generated by
\underline{S}(G)
has a minimal group
G
.

For any natural number
n\geqslant 0
, let
G_{n}=\mathbb{Z}/mp^{n}\mathbb{Z}
be a cyclic group of order
m\cdot p^{n}
and let
F_{n}
be the subfunctor of
S
generated by
\tilde{\xi}_{m,n,p}
. Then by discussions above
F_{n}
has a minimal group
G_{n}
. We claim that
F_{n}
is a simple
p
-bifree biset functor. It is sufficient to show that
F_{n+k}\not\subseteq F_{n}
for any
k\geqslant 1
. Indeed, if
F_{n}
has a non-zero subfunctor
M
, then the minimal group of
M
is
G_{n+k}
for some
k
and by definition
F_{n+k}\subseteq M
.

Let
k\geqslant 1
. Since
|G_{n+k}|/|G_{n}|
is a
p
-power, the space
\mathbb{C}B^{\Delta}(G_{n+k},G_{n})\circ F_{n}(G_{n})
is one-dimensional, generated by
\mathrm{Ind}^{G_{n+k}}_{G_{n}}\tilde{\xi}_{m,n,p}
. In particular,
\tilde{e}^{G_{n+k}}_{G_{n+k}}F_{n}(G_{n+k})=0
. But,
\tilde{e}^{G_{n+k}}_{G_{n+k}}F_{n+k}(G_{n+k})=F_{n+k}(G_{n+k})\neq 0
. Hence,
F_{n+k}\not\subseteq F_{n}
, as required. This shows that
F_{n}
is simple. Moreover, for any
x\in(\mathbb{Z}/mp^{n})^{\times}
, we have
x\cdot\tilde{\xi}_{m,n,p}=\xi_{m,n,p}(x)\tilde{\xi}_{m,n,p}
which implies
F_{n}(G_{n})\cong\mathbb{C}_{\xi_{m,n,p}}
. This shows that
F_{n}\cong S^{\Delta}_{G_{n},\mathbb{C}_{\xi_{m,n,p}}}
and theorem is proved.
∎

These calculations show that
\mathbb{C}R_{\mathbb{C}}
decomposes into a direct sum of simple
p
-bifree functors indexed by pairs
(m,\xi)
, where
m
is a positive integer and
\xi
is an irreducible character of
(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
with primitive
p^{\prime}
-part.

8.6 Corollary

One has

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}R_{\mathbb{C}}\cong\bigoplus_{(m,\xi)}S^{\Delta}_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi}}

where
(m,\xi)
runs through the set of pairs consisting of a positive integer
m
and a character
\xi
of
(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
such that
\xi_{p^{\prime}}
is primitive.

Proof.

By Remark
8.1
and Theorem
8.5
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}R_{\mathbb{C}}\cong\bigoplus_{(k,\chi)}\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}S^{\Delta}_{\mathbb{Z}/kp^{n}\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\chi_{m,n,p}}}

where
(k,\chi)
runs through the set of pairs consisting of a positive integer
k
and a primitive character
\chi
of
(\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}R_{\mathbb{C}}\cong\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\bigoplus_{\xi}S^{\Delta}_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi}}

where
\xi
runs through the characters of
(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
such that the quotient of
m
by the conductor of
\xi
is a
p
-power. The result follows.
∎

8.7 Remark

Given finite groups
G
and
H
, we can choose a
p
-modular system
(\mathbb{K},\mathcal{O},k)
, where
\mathcal{O}
is a complete discrete valuation ring,
\mathbb{K}
is the quotient field of
\mathcal{O}
of characteristic zero containing a primitive
\exp(G\times H)
-th root of unity and
k
is the residue field of
\mathcal{O}
which is algebraically closed. We also choose an embedding
\mathbb{K}\to\mathbb{C}
. Then, by Proposition 4.17.4 in
[
L18a
]
, for any
X\in\mathbb{C}B^{\Delta}(H,G)
, the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}R_{\mathbb{C}}(G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}R_{k}(G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}R_{\mathbb{C}}(H)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}R_{k}(H)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{G}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}R_{\mathbb{C}}(X)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}R_{k}(X)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{H}}

where
d_{G}
and
d_{H}
denote the decomposition maps, commute. This way we obtain a natural transformation
d:\mathbb{C}R_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{C}R_{k}
. By Corollaries
7.6
and
8.6
, we obtain a short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{(m,\xi)}S^{\Delta}_{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}_{\xi}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}R_{\mathbb{C}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}R_{k}\longrightarrow 0

of
p
-bifree biset functors, where
(m,\xi)
runs through the set of pairs consisting of a positive integer
m
divisible by
p
and an irreducible character
\xi
of
(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
with primitive
p^{\prime}
-part.

9
Further examples

In this section, we analyze how certain classical simple biset functors behave under restriction to the
p
-bifree biset category. Our focus is on the simple functors
S_{C_{p}\times C_{p},\mathbb{C}}
and
S_{C_{q}\times C_{q},\mathbb{C}}
, for a prime number
q
with
q\neq p
, whose evaluations are closely related to the structure of the Dade group (see
[
Bc10a
, Chapters 11–12]
). It turns out that both functors decompose as direct sums of simple
p
-bifree biset functors indexed by specific families of
p
-groups.

We begin with the case of
S_{C_{p}\times C_{p},\mathbb{C}}
, the simple biset functor associated to the elementary abelian
p
-group of rank two. We determine the groups
G
for which its restriction kernel evaluates non-trivially in the
p
-bifree category and compute it when non-zero.

9.1 Proposition

(a)
Let
G
be a finite group. One has
\underline{S_{C_{p}\times C_{p},\mathbb{C}}}(G)=\{0\}
unless
G
is a non-cyclic
p
-group.

(b)
Let
G
be a non-cyclic
p
-group. Then
\underline{S_{C_{p}\times C_{p},\mathbb{C}}}(G)
is one-dimensional generated by the image of
e^{G}_{G}
in
S_{C_{p}\times C_{p},\mathbb{C}}(G)
.

Proof.

First note that for any
p
-bifree biset functor
S
and finite group
G
, one has
\underline{S}(G)\subseteq\tilde{e}^{G}_{G}S(G)
. Indeed, one has
S(G)=\tilde{e}^{G}_{G}S(G)\oplus(1-\tilde{e}^{G}_{G})S(G)
and
(1-\tilde{e}^{G}_{G})S(G)\cap\underline{S}(G)=\{0\}
.

Now note that by
[
Bc23
, Corollary 3.6]
,
\tilde{e}^{G}_{G}S_{C_{p}\times C_{p},\mathbb{C}}(G)
is non-zero if and only if
G
is
p
-elementary. Let
\underline{\textbf{e}^{\Delta}}_{C_{p}\times C_{p}}
and
\underline{\textbf{j}}_{C_{p}\times C_{p}}
be biset subfunctors of the Burnside ring functor introduced in
[
Bc10a
, Section 5]
, similar to the functors
\textbf{e}^{\Delta}_{C_{p}\times C_{p}}
and
\textbf{j}_{C_{p}\times C_{p}}
in Section 4. By the proof of
[
Bc10a
, Theorem 5.5.4]
, the functor
S_{C_{p}\times C_{p},\mathbb{C}}
is isomorphic as biset functors to the quotient
\underline{\textbf{e}^{\Delta}}_{C_{p}\times C_{p}}/\underline{\textbf{j}}_{C_{p}\times C_{p}}
. Furthermore,
\tilde{e}^{G}_{G}S_{C_{p}\times C_{p},\mathbb{C}}(G)
is generated by the image
\overline{e^{G}_{G}}
in
S_{C_{p}\times C_{p},\mathbb{C}}(G)
of
e^{G}_{G}
if
G
is not cyclic and is equal to zero if
G
is cyclic.

Suppose that
G=P\times C
is a non-cyclic
p
-elementary group where
P=O_{p}(G)
. Then
e^{G}_{G}=e^{P}_{P}\times e^{C}_{C}
. Also if
N\unlhd G
is of
p^{\prime}
-order, then
G/N\cong P\times C/N
and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Def}^{G}_{G/N}e^{G}_{G}=e^{P}_{P}\times\mathrm{Def}^{C}_{C/N}e^{C}_{C}\,.

Since
C
is a cyclic group,
m_{C,C}\neq 0
and hence

\displaystyle\mathrm{Def}^{G}_{G/C}e^{G}_{G}=m_{C,C}e^{P}_{P}\times e^{C/C}_{C/C}\neq 0\,.

This shows that
\underline{S_{C_{p}\times C_{p},\mathbb{C}}}(G)=\{0\}
unless
C=1
. On the other hand if
C=1
, then
\underline{S_{C_{p}\times C_{p},\mathbb{C}}}(G)=\tilde{e}^{G}_{G}S_{C_{p}\times C_{p},\mathbb{C}}(G)
is one-dimensional generated by
\overline{e^{G}_{G}}
. This proves both parts.
∎

9.2 Proposition

Let
G
be a finite group. Then for any
x\in S_{C_{p}\times C_{p},\mathbb{C}}(G)
, there exists
x^{\prime}\in S_{C_{p}\times C_{p},\mathbb{C}}(G)
and
\alpha\in I_{G}
such that
x=x^{\prime}+\alpha\cdot x
.

Proof.

Let
x\in S_{C_{p}\times C_{p},\mathbb{C}}(G)
. By the proof of Proposition
9.1
, we have
\tilde{e}^{G}_{G}S_{C_{p}\times C_{p},\mathbb{C}}(G)=\{0\}
if
G
is not a non-cyclic
p
-group. In this case, we have
x^{\prime}=0
and
\alpha=1-\tilde{e}^{G}_{G}
. If
G
is a non-cyclic
p
-group, then again by the proof of Proposition
9.1
, we have
\underline{S_{C_{p}\times C_{p},\mathbb{C}}}(G)=\tilde{e}^{G}_{G}S_{C_{p}\times C_{p},\mathbb{C}}(G)
. Thus, in this case, we choose
x^{\prime}=\tilde{e}^{G}_{G}\cdot x^{\prime}
and
\alpha=1-\tilde{e}^{G}_{G}
.
∎

These two results allow us to determine the full decomposition of
S_{C_{p}\times C_{p},\mathbb{C}}
as a
p
-bifree biset functor. It splits as a direct sum of simple functors indexed by non-cyclic
p
-groups, each occurring with multiplicity one.

9.3 Theorem

One has an isomorphism

\displaystyle S_{C_{p}\times C_{p},\mathbb{C}}\cong\bigoplus_{P:\text{non-cyclic }p\text{-group}}S^{\Delta}_{P,\mathbb{C}}

of
p
-bifree biset functors.

Proof.

By Propositions
9.1
and
9.2
and Theorem
10.2
, the composition factors of
S_{C_{p}\times C_{p},\mathbb{C}}
are exactly the functors
S_{P,\mathbb{C}}^{\Delta}
, where
P
is a non-cyclic
p
-group, each with multiplicity one. We will show that each of these functors is a subfunctor.

Let
F
be a
p

For a non-cyclic

We claim that

is a non-cyclic

∎

Let
R
be a commutative ring and let
\mathbb{K}
be a field.

10.1 Lemma

Let
F
be a (
p
-bifree) biset functor over
R
and
G
a finite group. Let
\mathrm{Sec}(G)
be the set of all proper sections of
G
. Then we have

\underline{F}(G)=\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}H\in\mathrm{Sec}(G)\\
\alpha\in RB^{\Delta}(H,G)\end{subarray}}\ker(F(\alpha):F(G)\to F(H))\,.

Proof.

Denote the right hand side of the above equality by
\underline{\underline{F}}(G)
. Clearly
\underline{F}(G)\subseteq\underline{\underline{F}}(G)
.

For the reverse inclusion, let
T
be a group of order less than
|G|
and let
U\leqslant T\times G
. Also let
x\in\underline{\underline{F}}(G)
. It is sufficient to prove that

\Big(\frac{T\times G}{U}\Big)\cdot x=0.

Write

\Big(\frac{T\times G}{U}\Big)=\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{T}\mathrm{Inf}_{P/K}^{T}\mathrm{iso}\mathrm{Def}^{Q}_{Q/L}\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{Q}

with the usual choices of the letters. Thus

\Big(\frac{T\times G}{U}\Big)\cdot x=(\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{T}\mathrm{Inf}_{P/K}^{T}\mathrm{iso}\mathrm{Def}^{Q}_{Q/L}\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{Q})\cdot x=\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{T}\mathrm{Inf}_{P/K}^{T}\mathrm{iso}(\mathrm{Def}^{Q}_{Q/L}\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{Q}\cdot x)=0.

Here
\mathrm{Def}^{Q}_{Q/L}\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{Q}\cdot x=0
since
x\in\underline{\underline{F}}(G)
.
∎

The following theorem is stated in
[
BCK
]
for Green biset functors. But one can easily check that it is also valid for
p
-bifree biset functors. We include the proof for the sake of self-containment.

10.2 Theorem

[
BCK
, Theorem 2.5]

Let
F
be a biset functor over a field
k
and let
G
be a finite group. Suppose that
\dim_{k}F(G)<\infty
, and that for every
\mathcal{E}_{G}
-submodule
M\subseteq F(G)
, one has

M=(M\cap\underline{F}(G))+I_{G}M.

Then, for every simple
k[\mathrm{Out}(G)]
-module
V
,
the following numbers are equal:

1.

Multiplicity
[F:S_{G,V}]
of
S_{G,V}
as a composition factor of
F
.

2.

Multiplicity of
V
as a composition factor of the
\mathcal{E}_{G}
-module
F(G)
.

3.

Multiplicity of
V
as a composition factor of the
k[\mathrm{Out}(G)]
-module
\underline{F}(G)
.

Proof.

The equality of the first two numbers are well-known. We prove the equality of the last two numbers.
Since
\dim_{k}F(G)<\infty
, there exists an
\mathcal{E}_{G}
-composition series

0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{n-1}\subset M_{n}=F(G)

of
F(G)
. Set
K:=\underline{F}(G)
, and consider the induced series

0=(M_{0}\cap K)\subseteq(M_{1}\cap K)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq(M_{n-1}\cap K)\subseteq(M_{n}\cap K)=K

of
\mathcal{E}_{G}
-submodules of
K
. Let
V
be a simple
k[\mathrm{Out}(G)]
-module, and let
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. We claim that if
M_{i}/M_{i-1}\cong V
, then
(M_{i}\cap K)/(M_{i-1}\cap K)\cong V
. This implies that
[F(G):V]\leq[K:V]
. But clearly,
[K:V]\leq[F(G):V]
, and we obtain equality.

To prove the claim, observe that

\frac{M_{i}\cap K}{M_{i-1}\cap K}=\frac{M_{i}\cap K}{(M_{i}\cap K)\cap M_{i-1}}\cong\frac{(M_{i}\cap K)+M_{i-1}}{M_{i-1}}\subseteq\frac{M_{i}}{M_{i-1}}\cong V.

It therefore suffices to show that the left-hand side is nonzero. Assume by contradiction that
M_{i}\cap K=M_{i-1}\cap K
. Then,

M_{i}=(M_{i}\cap K)+I_{G}M_{i}\subseteq(M_{i}\cap K)+M_{i-1}=M_{i-1},

contradicting
M_{i}/M_{i-1}\cong V\neq 0
. Here, we used the assumption that
V
is annihilated by
I_{G}
, and the hypothesis of the theorem.
∎

10.4 Proposition

Let

In particular, for every

and
\alpha\in I_{G}
satisfying

\displaystyle x=x^{\prime}+\alpha\cdot x\,.

(2)

Proof.

Let
n_{H,V}
denote the multiplicity of the simple

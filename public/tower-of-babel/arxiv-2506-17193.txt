Any nonincreasing convergence curves are simultaneously possible for GMRES and weighted GMRES, as well as for left and right preconditioned GMRES This work was supported in part by the ANR project DARK (research grant ANR-24-CE46-1633).
P. Matalon, N. Spillane

Abstract
The convergence of the GMRES linear solver is notoriously hard to predict. A particularly enlightening result by [Greenbaum, Pták, Strakoš, 1996] is that, given any convergence curve, one can build a linear system for which GMRES realizes that convergence curve. What is even more extraordinary is that the eigenvalues of the problem matrix can be chosen arbitrarily. We build upon this idea to derive novel results about weighted GMRES. We prove that for any linear system and any prescribed convergence curve, there exists a weight matrix M M for which weighted GMRES ( i.e. GMRES in the inner product induced by M M ) realizes that convergence curve, and we characterize the form of M M . Additionally, we exhibit a necessary and sufficient condition on M M for the simultaneous prescription of two convergence curves, one realized by GMRES in the Euclidean inner product, and the other in the inner product induced by M M . These results are then applied to infer some properties of preconditioned GMRES when the preconditioner is applied either on the left or on the right. For instance, we show that any two convergence curves are simultaneously possible for left and right preconditioned GMRES.



Introduction

The GMRES algorithm
[
26
,
25
]
is one of the most popular methods for solving general, possibly non-Hermitian, linear systems.
Yet, to this date, its convergence behaviour is not fully understood, and reliable criteria for fast convergence have not been clearly identified.
One of the foundational insights into GMRES was provided by Greenbaum, Pták and Strakoš in
[
12
]
, who demonstrated at least two key points. The first is that any non-increasing convergence curve can be achieved by GMRES. The second is that for any given convergence curve and spectrum, a system can be constructed that exhibits this spectrum and achieves the specified convergence curve with GMRES. This revealed that the convergence of GMRES is not solely determined by the eigenvalues.
This approach of analyzing GMRES through the lens of systems that generate a given convergence curve has inspired numerous subsequent studies.
Arioli, Pták and Strakoš provided in
[
1
]
a complete parametrization of all systems generating a prescribed convergence curve (the so-called APS parametrization), further explored in
[
21
]
by Meurant.
Similarly to the eigenvalues, it was shown in
[
6
]
that any harmonic Ritz value set (
i.e.
the zeroes of the GMRES residual polynomial) is possible for any given convergence curve at any iteration. This was further discussed in
[
7
,
5
]
.
The work of
[
29
]
extended the original findings of Greenbaum
et al.

[
12
]
to the restarted version of GMRES, and
[
18
]
extended them to the block version of GMRES.
However, to our knowledge, this approach has not yet been applied to the weighted GMRES method,
i.e.
GMRES expressed in another inner product. A related question was raised in
[
20
]
where the simultaneous prescription convergence curves both for the residual and the error is studied. Even though the norm of the error can also be viewed as a weighted norm of the residual, the setup is particular because the weight varies with the operator.

Weighted GMRES is a term coined by Essai
[
11
]
, who demonstrated that the convergence of restarted GMRES can be accelerated by incorporating a diagonal weight matrix into the inner product of the Arnoldi process at each restart. The weights are chosen to prioritize, during the next cycle, the reduction of large residual coefficients.
Receiving attention, Essai’s technique is analyzed from the angle of the Ritz values
[
13
]
and the eigenvectors
[
10
]
, providing insights into use cases where this weighted method can be particularly beneficial.
Besides this heuristic method, the abstract algorithm of GMRES using a non-standard inner product has been explored in
[
22
,
23
]
and references therein.
We also refer to
[
30
]
for a reflection about which norm should be considered to assess convergence. Earlier works on what is now called weighted GMRES include
[
28
,
17
,
3
,
27
,
2
]
.

Our work aims to explore what can be learned about weighted GMRES from prescribing convergence curves. Understanding the convergence of weighted GMRES, an interesting question in itself, can also be viewed as a step toward understanding the convergence of GMRES. In recent work
[
9
]
, Embree has extended the range of applicability of Elman’s convergence bound
[
8
]
by considering weighted GMRES. Indeed, as long as the eigenvalues are on one side of the origin,
[
9
]
proposes the construction of an inner product for which the field of values is also on one side of the origin, and hence the Elman bound applies. We view this as a very promising way of predicting the convergence of GMRES for a large class of problems, and we aim to provide some level of insight into the connection between the convergence of GMRES in the Euclidean norm and in the norm used for analysis.

This manuscript starts with a preliminary section where we introduce GMRES and recall the original result from
[
12
]
about the prescription of convergence curves. The formalism for prescribed convergence curves is introduced, and in particular the notation
g_{i}=\sqrt{\|r_{i-1}\|^{2}-\|r_{i}\|^{2}}
for the decrease in norm between two subsequent residuals.
In

Notation

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
For any matrix
A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}

denotes its conjugate transpose and, if invertible,
A^{-1}
its inverse and
A^{-*}

.
We refer to

We denote by

1
Preliminary

We denote by
(A,b)
the general linear system

Ax=b,

where
A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is a non-singular matrix and
b\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a vector. Given an inner product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
and its associated norm
\|\cdot\|
, we consider the application of the GMRES algorithm
[
26
,
25
]
with a zero initial guess (
x_{0}=0
) to the system
(A,b)
. We refer to this procedure as GMRES(
A,b
). At an iteration
i
, the approximate solution is denoted by
x_{i}
and the corresponding residual is
r_{i}:=b-Ax_{i}
.
In this section, the inner product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
remains abstract.
The method is associated with the Krylov spaces

\mathcal{K}_{i}(A,b):=\operatorname{span}\{b,Ab,A^{2}b,\dots,A^{i-1}b\}\qquad\forall i=1,\dots,n.

The Krylov residual spaces
A\mathcal{K}_{i}(A,b)=\operatorname{span}\{Ab,A^{2}b,\dots,A^{i}b\}

play a key role in formalizing GMRES. These spaces are nested by definition, and we define the notion of
nested residual basis
:

Definition 1

(Nested residual basis)
.

Let
(A,b)
define a given linear system, and
m:=\dim\mathcal{K}_{n}(A,b)
be the breakdown index of GMRES
(A,b)
.
The family
(w_{j})_{j=1,\dots,m}
is called a nested basis of
A\mathcal{K}_{m}(A,b)
if, for all
i=1,\dots,m
, its restriction to the
i
first vectors is a basis of
A\mathcal{K}_{i}(A,b)
.

A useful property of nested bases that are orthonormal is the following.

Lemma 2
.

Let
(A,b)
define a given linear system, and
m:=\dim\mathcal{K}_{n}(A,b)
.

(i) All nested orthonormal bases of
A\mathcal{K}_{m}(A,b)
are equal, up to the multiplication of each vector by a scalar of module 1.

(ii) Moreover, there exists a nested orthonormal basis of
A\mathcal{K}_{m}(A,b)
such that
\left\langle b,w_{i}\right\rangle\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
for all
i=1,\dots,m
.

Proof.

(i) The proof is straightforward by induction over
m
.

(ii) Let
(w_{i})_{i=1,\dots,m}
be a nested, orthonormal basis of
A\mathcal{K}_{m}(A,b)
,
e.g.
, the one obtained by applying a Gram-Schmidt procedure to
\{Ab,A^{2}b,\dots,A^{i}b\}
.
For all
i=1,\dots,m
, we introduce the complex scalar
\gamma_{i}
of module 1 such that
\gamma_{i}:=\frac{\absolutevalue{\ip{b}{w_i}}}{\left\langle b,w_{i}\right\rangle}
if
\left\langle b,w_{i}\right\rangle\not=0
or
\gamma_{i}:=1
otherwise, and we define
\widetilde{w}_{i}:=\gamma_{i}w_{i}
.
The set
(\widetilde{w}_{i})_{i=1,\dots,m}
is also a nested orthonormal basis of
A\mathcal{K}_{m}(A,b)
, and it holds that

\left\langle b,\widetilde{w}_{i}\right\rangle=\gamma_{i}\left\langle b,w_{i}\right\rangle=\absolutevalue{\ip{b}{w_i}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

∎

It is well known that the breakdown index
m:=\dim\mathcal{K}_{n}(A,b)
is also the number of the iteration at which GMRES finds the exact solution
[
24
, Prop 6.2, Prop. 6.10]
.
The initial residual is
r_{0}=b
. At any subsequent iteration,
i=1,\dots,m
, the residual
r_{i}
is characterized by

r_{i}\in b+A\mathcal{K}_{i}(A,b)\text{ and }\|r_{i}\|=\min_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{i}(A,b)}\|b-Ax\|=\min_{y\in A\mathcal{K}_{i}(A,b)}\|b-y\|.

(1)

Equivalently,
b-r_{i}
is the orthogonal projection of
b
onto
A\mathcal{K}_{i}(A,b)
. For future reference, we recall the following consequences:

Theorem 3
.

Let
(A,b)
define a given linear system, and
m:=\dim\mathcal{K}_{n}(A,b)
be the breakdown index of GMRES
(A,b)
.
Consider
(w_{j})_{j=1,\dots,m}
a nested, orthonormal basis of
A\mathcal{K}_{m}(A,b)
. Then, at each iteration
i=1,\dots,m
, the residual
r_{i}
can be expressed as

r_{i}=b-\sum_{j=1}^{i}\left\langle b,w_{j}\right\rangle w_{j}.

(2)

Since
r_{m}=0
, this implies that
b=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left\langle b,w_{j}\right\rangle w_{j}
,
and that

\left\langle b,w_{j}\right\rangle^{2}=\|r_{j-1}\|^{2}-\|r_{j}\|^{2}
.

1.1
Convergence curves and residual decrease vectors

First, consider a linear system
(A,b)
, and its breakdown index
m=\dim\mathcal{K}_{n}(A,b)
. Second, define a convergence curve by choosing a sequence of real values
(\mathtt{r}_{i})_{i=0,\dots,m}
verifying

\mathtt{r}_{0}\geq\mathtt{r}_{1}\geq\dots\geq\mathtt{r}_{m-1}>\mathtt{r}_{m}=0.

(3)

We say that GMRES
(A,b)
realizes this convergence curve if and only if it produces residuals such that

\|r_{i}\|=\mathtt{r}_{i}\quad\forall i=0,\dots,m.

(4)

Note that
x_{0}=0
imposes
\mathtt{r}_{0}=\|b\|
, and the breakdown at iteration
m
imposes
\mathtt{r}_{m}=0
and
\mathtt{r}_{m-1}>0
.
In reference to
Theorem

3
, we also define the sequence of successive differences

g_{i}:=\sqrt{\mathtt{r}_{i-1}^{2}-\mathtt{r}_{i}^{2}}\quad\forall i=1,\dots,m.

(5)

These can be represented by
g\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
, with
g=(g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{m},0,\dots,0)^{\top}
. Prescribing such a
g\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
is equivalent to prescribing the residuals.
Indeed, given
g
, we retrieve

\mathtt{r}_{i}=\sqrt{\sum_{j=1+1}^{n}g_{j}^{2}}\qquad\forall i=0,\dots,n.

(6)

Since the residual decrease values
g_{i}
appear in the analysis instead of the residual norms, we shall speak of “prescribing the convergence curve induced by
g
” instead of referring to the
\mathtt{r}_{i}
values.
From now on, we work with the following formalism:

Definition 4

(Residual decrease vector)
.

Let
g\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
.

(i)
g
can be seen as a residual decrease vector, which implicitly defines successive residual norms through formula (
6
). We speak of the
convergence curve induced by

g
.

(ii) If it exists, let
p
be the smallest index such that
g_{i}=0
for all
i>p
. Otherwise, set
p=n
. In both cases, we say that
p
is the
length
of
g
.

Remark 5

(Compatibility conditions)
.

Clearly, for a given system
(A,b)
, the residual decrease vector
g
effectively realized by GMRES
(A,b)
is of length
m:=\dim\mathcal{K}_{n}(A,b)
, the breakdown index of GMRES
(A,b)
.
It also holds from (
6
) (with
i=m
) that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}g_{i}^{2}=\|b\|^{2}.

(7)

Lemma 6

(Characterization)
.

Let
(A,b)
define a given linear system, and
g\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
a residual decrease vector of length
m:=\dim\mathcal{K}_{n}(A,b)
.
GMRES
(A,b)
realizes the convergence curve induced by
g
if and only if there exists a nested, orthonormal basis of
A\mathcal{K}_{m}(A,b)
, denoted by
(w_{i})_{i=1,\dots,m}
, such that

b=\sum_{i=1}^{m}g_{i}w_{i}.

(8)

Proof.

(i) Assume that
(w_{i})_{i=1,\dots,m}
is a nested, orthonormal basis of
A\mathcal{K}_{m}(A,b)
verifying (
8
).
Then, the residuals
r_{i}
of GMRES
(A,b)
satisfy

g_{i}=\left\langle b,w_{i}\right\rangle=\sqrt{\|r_{i-1}\|^{2}-\|r_{i}\|^{2}}\qquad\forall i=1,\dots,m,

by orthogonality of the
w_{i}
and the last result in
Theorem

3
.
This shows that GMRES
(A,b)
realizes the convergence curve induced by
g
.

(ii) Conversely, assume that GMRES
(A,b)
realizes the convergence curve induced by
g
,
i.e.
,

g_{i}=\sqrt{\|r_{i-1}\|^{2}-\|r_{i}\|^{2}}\qquad i=1,\dots,m.

(9)

Let
(w_{i})_{i=1,\dots,m}
be a nested orthonormal basis of
A\mathcal{K}_{m}(A,b)
such that
\left\langle b,w_{i}\right\rangle\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
for all
i=1,\dots,m
. Such a basis exist by
Lemma

2
. By
Theorem

3
, we get

\absolutevalue{\ip{b}{w_i}}=\left\langle b,w_{i}\right\rangle=g_{i}\qquad\forall i=1,\dots,m,\qquad\text{ and }\qquad b=\sum_{i=1}^{m}g_{i}w_{i}.

∎

Remark that the necessary and sufficient condition of
Lemma

6
includes the compatibility conditions of
Remark

5
.
Indeed, formula (
8
) directly infers (
7
) by applying
\|\cdot\|^{2}
. Additionally, identifying the coefficients in the decomposition (

Consequently, the assumption that

However, we prefer to keep it to emphasize the fact that the breakdown index is fixed by the linear system.

In order to clearly exhibit the link between the right-hand side, the convergence curve and the orthonormal Krylov residual basis, we give another formulation of the preceding theorem:

Corollary 7

(Reformulation of
Lemma

6
)
.

Let
(A,b)
define a given linear system, and let
g\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
be a residual decrease vector of length

GMRES

realizes the convergence curve induced by

Lemma 8

(Relationship between two nested bases)
.

Let
(A,b)
define a given linear system, and

.
Let
(w_{i})_{i=1,\dots,n}

Then,

\hline\cr&*\end{array}\right),

(10)

(11)

First, assume that both

We refer to those constructive proofs to learn how such systems can be built in practice.

Consider
g\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
a residual decrease vector of arbitrary length

, and the prescribed, non-vanishing, complex numbers

There exists a system

2
Weighted GMRES

Any Hermitian positive definite (hpd) matrix
M\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
induces an inner product and a norm, respectively defined by

\left\langle x,y\right\rangle_{M}:=y^{*}Mx\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\|x\|_{M}:=\sqrt{\left\langle x,x\right\rangle_{M}}\qquad\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

We denote by
M
-GMRES the GMRES algorithm resulting from the choice
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle:=\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{M}
.
Since the inner product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
remained abstract in the preceding section, all results presented before still hold for
M
-GMRES.

In particular, when stating that
M
-GMRES realizes (
4
), property (
4
) is with
\mathbf{\|\cdot\|:=\|\cdot\|_{M}}
.
Moreover a matrix
X
is unitary in the inner product induced by
M
, or
M
-unitary, if
X^{*}MX=I
(the identity matrix). In this formalism,
I
-GMRES refers to the Euclidean setting.

2.1
I
-GMRES and
M
-GMRES

Let
M
be a hpd matrix. Let’s begin with some natural relations between the
I
-GMRES residuals
r_{k}
and the
M
-GMRES residuals
\tilde{r}_{k}
. The initial guesses are still set to
0
so the only difference between both algorithms is the norm in which the minimization property is enforced. Denoting by
\mu_{\min}(M)
and
\mu_{\max}(M)
, the extreme eigenvalues of
M
, it follows that

\mu_{min}(M)\|r_{k}\|_{I}^{2}\leq\mu_{min}(M)\|\widetilde{r_{k}}\|_{I}^{2}\leq\|\widetilde{r_{k}}\|_{M}^{2}\leq\|r_{k}\|_{M}^{2}\leq\mu_{max}(M)\|r_{k}\|_{I}^{2},

(12)

where the first and third inequalities come from the definition of GMRES, and the others are spectral estimates. In particular, with
\kappa(M)
the condition number of
M
, we obtain

\|\widetilde{r_{k}}\|_{I}^{2}\leq\kappa(M)\|r_{k}\|_{I}^{2}\text{ and }\|r_{k}\|_{M}^{2}\leq\kappa(M)\|\widetilde{r}_{k}\|_{M}^{2}.

Additionally,
r_{0}=\widetilde{r_{0}}

and the normalized residuals satisfy

\kappa(M)^{-1}\frac{\|\widetilde{r}_{k}\|_{M}^{2}}{\|\widetilde{r_{0}}\|_{M}^{2}}\leq\frac{\|r_{k}\|_{I}^{2}}{\|r_{0}\|_{I}^{2}}\leq\kappa(M)\frac{\|\widetilde{r}_{k}\|_{M}^{2}}{\|\widetilde{r_{0}}\|_{M}^{2}}.

(13)

We next apply the formalism proposed by Ipsen in
[
16
]
[Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.2] with some details of the proof from
[
15
, Lemma 7.1]
. What is new here is that we are extending (straightforwardly) the formalism to weighted GMRES and applying it to compare either the same residual in two norms, or the residuals of
I
- and
M
-GMRES.

Consider GMRES
(A,b)
in the
M
-inner product starting with
\tilde{r}_{0}=b
. Up to convergence, the
k
-th residual is determined by

\tilde{r}_{k}=b-W_{k}\underbrace{(W_{k}^{*}MW_{k})^{-1}W_{k}^{*}Mb}_{:=y_{0}},

for any matrix
W_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times k}
whose columns form a basis of
A\mathcal{K}_{k}(A,b)
.
Let
B=(b\,W_{k})
be the concatenation of the right hand side and this basis. This gives a basis of the Krylov subspace
\mathcal{K}_{k+1}(A,b)
. Moreover,

B^{*}MB=\begin{pmatrix}b^{*}Mb&b^{*}MW_{k}\\
W_{k}^{*}Mb&W_{k}^{*}MW_{k}\end{pmatrix}.

and the first row in the inverse of this matrix is
[
14
, eq. (0.7.3.1)]
:

e_{1}^{\top}(B^{*}MB)^{-1}=(b^{*}M\tilde{r}_{k})^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}1&-y_{0}^{*}\end{pmatrix},\text{ where }e_{1}=(1,0,\dots,0)^{\top}\in\mathbb{C}^{k+1}.

Multiplying on the right by
B^{*}
gives

e_{1}^{\top}(B^{*}MB)^{-1}B^{*}=(b^{*}M\tilde{r}_{k})^{-1}\underbrace{(b^{*}-y_{0}^{*}W_{k}^{*})}_{=\tilde{r}_{k}^{*}}=\frac{\tilde{r}_{k}^{*}}{\|\tilde{r}_{k}\|_{M}^{2}}
,
where the last equality is because
b^{*}M\tilde{r}_{k}=\|\tilde{r}_{k}\|_{M}^{2}
by
\tilde{r}_{k}^{*}MW_{k}=0
. Taking the transpose we get

\frac{\tilde{r}_{k}}{\|\tilde{r}_{k}\|_{M}^{2}}=B(B^{*}MB)^{-1}e_{1}.

Next, we take the
M
-norm and the
I
-norm of this estimate in order to get a formula for the
k
th residual norm

\|\tilde{r}_{k}\|_{M}=\|B(B^{*}MB)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{M}^{-1}=\|e_{1}\|_{(B^{*}MB)^{-1}}^{-1},

(14)

as well as for the ratio between the
I
- and
M
-norms of the
k
-th residual

\frac{\|\tilde{r}_{k}\|_{I}}{\|\tilde{r}_{k}\|_{M}}=\frac{\|B(B^{*}MB)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{I}}{\|B(B^{*}MB)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{M}}.

Remark 10
.

The identity
\|e_{1}\|_{(B^{*}MB)^{-1}}=\|\tilde{r}_{k}\|_{M}^{-1}
can be seen directly from the fact that the
(1,1)-
block of
(B^{*}MB)^{-1}
equals
(b^{*}M\tilde{r}_{k})^{-1}=\|\tilde{r}_{k}\|^{-2}
.

Of course, the same results adapt to the residuals
r_{k}
of
I
-GMRES:

\|r_{k}\|_{I}=\|B(B^{*}B)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{I}^{-1}=\|e_{1}\|_{(B^{*}B)^{-1}}^{-1},\text{ and }\frac{\|r_{k}\|_{M}}{\|r_{k}\|_{I}}=\frac{\|B(B^{*}B)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{M}}{\|B(B^{*}B)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{I}}.

Finally, comparisons between the residual norms of unweighted and weighted GMRES can be drawn,
e.g.
,

\frac{\|r_{k}\|_{I}}{\|\tilde{r}_{k}\|_{M}}=\frac{\|e_{1}\|_{(B^{*}MB)^{-1}}}{\|e_{1}\|_{(B^{*}B)^{-1}}}=\frac{\|B(B^{*}MB)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{M}}{\|B(B^{*}B)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{I}},

where each residual is in its natural norm,

\frac{\|\tilde{r}_{k}\|_{I}}{\|r_{k}\|_{I}}=\frac{\|B(B^{*}MB)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{I}\|B(B^{*}B)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{I}}{\|B(B^{*}MB)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{M}^{2}}.

where both residuals are in the
I
-norm,

\frac{\|\tilde{r}_{k}\|_{N}}{\|r_{k}\|_{N}}=\frac{\|B(B^{*}MB)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{N}}{\|B(B^{*}MB)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{M}}\frac{\|B(B^{*}B)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{I}^{2}}{\|B(B^{*}B)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{N}^{2}}.

where both residuals are measured in a third norm induced by some hpd matrix
N
. A potential norm of interest could be the error norm which corresponds to the choice
N=(A^{*}A)^{-1}
.

We do not pursue this analysis here but we do wish to mention two contributions of
[
19
]
which could also be extended to weighted GMRES. The first of these contributions is a discussion of the choice of basis
W_{k}
, the second is the analysis of the effect on the bounds of adding a vector to
W_{k}
when performing iteration
k
.

2.2

We now turn back to the convergence curve formalism. We start by proposing the construction of an inner product for which weighted GMRES achieves some desired convergence curve.

Theorem 11

Let
(A,b)

Proof.

Considering

∎

2.3
Analysis of the necessary and sufficient condition

It appears to us that

(ii)

For a

such that

-GMRES realizes

and

-GMRES realizes

.

For

3
Preconditioned GMRES

While weighted GMRES remains often more of a theoretical tool than a practical method to solve real-world problems, preconditioned GMRES is widely employed to enhance the convergence of the standard
I
-GMRES method.
Yet, both are linked, and preconditioned GMRES can be interpreted as weighted GMRES applied to a modified system.
In this section, we explore what our findings on weighted GMRES imply on preconditioned GMRES.

A preconditioner can be applied
on the left
or
on the right
. If both are used, we speak of
split preconditioner
.
We consider this general case directly by denoting a pair of left and right preconditioners as
(H_{L},H_{R})
. One of them can be set to
I
to fall back on one-sided preconditioning.

I
-GMRES preconditioned on the left by
H_{L}
and on the right by
H_{R}
solves

H_{L}AH_{R}u=H_{L}b,\qquad x=H_{R}u,

by solving at each iteration
i
the least squares problem

\min_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{i}(HA,Hb)}\|H_{L}(b-Ax)\|_{I},

(23)

where
H:=H_{R}H_{L}
is the combined preconditioner.
Remark that preconditioning on the left minimizes the
H_{L}^{*}H_{L}
-norm of the residual (regardless of whether a right preconditioner is used), while preconditioning on the right minimizes the Euclidean norm of the residual. Additionally, the choice of preconditioning on the left or on the right does not alter the Krylov space in which the solution is searched, as it depends solely on the product
H
.
Regarding the formalism of prescribed convergence curves in
Section

1
, the norm in which the residual norms are prescribed is always the one that is minimized by the considered GMRES algorithm, such that
for a general split preconditioner, we prescribe
\|H_{L}r_{i}\|_{I}(=\|r_{i}\|_{H_{L}^{*}H_{L}})
, which reduces to
\|r_{i}\|_{I}
for a single right preconditioner and to
\|r_{i}\|_{H^{*}H}
for a single left preconditioner.

3.1
Split preconditioning

Let
M
be a hpd matrix whose Cholesky factorization writes

M=PP^{*}.

(24)

Leaning on the remark
\|r\|_{M}=\|P^{*}r\|_{I}
for any vector
r
, the minimization problem for
M
-GMRES(
A,b
) writes

\min_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{i}(A,b)}\|b-Ax\|_{M}=\min_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{i}(A,b)}\|P^{*}(b-Ax)\|_{I}.

(25)

Problem (
25
) is then expressed in the form of problem (
23
) by setting
(H_{L},H_{R}):=(P^{*},P^{-*})
.
This means that
M
-GMRES(
A,b
) can be interpreted as
I
-GMRES(
A,b
) split preconditionned by
(P^{*},P^{-*})
.
As a direct consequence,
Theorem

11
can also be formulated as

Theorem 21

(Split preconditioning)
.

Let
(A,b)
define a given linear system, and consider
\widetilde{g}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
a residual decrease vector.
If
\widetilde{g}
is of length
m:=\dim\mathcal{K}_{n}(A,b)
, then
there exists a split preconditioner with which
I
-GMRES(
A,b
) realizes the convergence curve induced by
\widetilde{g}
.

Proof.

In the proof of
Theorem

11
,
M
is defined by (
17
), which we express as

M=(WT)^{-*}((WT)^{-*})^{*}

to match the decomposition (
24
).
It follows that
I
-GMRES realizes the prescribed convergence curve with the split preconditioner
(H_{L},H_{R})=((WT)^{-1},WT)
.
∎

3.2
Left/right preconditioning

We now consider the choice of preconditioning either on the left or on the right. Let
H
denote a preconditioner. In the preceding split formalism, we consider the pairs of preconditioners
(H,I)
and
(I,H)
.
Let us translate
I
-GMRES preconditioned on the left or on the right by
H
into a possibly weighted, unpreconditioned GMRES algorithm.
We introduce

u:=H^{-1}x;\qquad\widehat{A}:=AH.

By (
23
), at each iteration
i
,
I
-GMRES(
A,b
) left preconditioned by
H
solves

\min_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{i}(HA,Hb)}\|H(b-Ax)\|_{I}=\min_{u\in\mathcal{K}_{i}(AH,b)}\|H(b-AHu)\|_{I}=\min_{u\in\mathcal{K}_{i}(\widehat{A},b)}\|b-\widehat{A}u\|_{H^{*}H}

(26)

which shows that
I
-GMRES(
A,b
) left preconditioned by

is equivalent to unpreconditioned
(H^{*}H)
-GMRES(
\widehat{A},b
).
Similarly,
I
-GMRES(
A,b
) right preconditioned by
H
solves

\min_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{i}(HA,Hb)}\|b-Ax\|_{I}=\min_{u\in\mathcal{K}_{i}(AH,b)}\|b-AHu\|_{I}=\min_{u\in\mathcal{K}_{i}(\widehat{A},b)}\|b-\widehat{A}u\|_{I}

(27)

which shows that
I
-GMRES(
A,b
) right preconditioned by
H
is equivalent to unpreconditioned

-GMRES(
\widehat{A},b
).

In the following result, we show that it is possible to prescribe the convergence curves for the GMRES algorithm preconditioned on the left and on the right, and build a system and a preconditioner for which preconditioned
I
-GMRES realizes both.

(Any two convergence curves are simultaneously possible)
.

Consider the convergence curves induced by

and

(A,b)
and a preconditioner

-GMRES

, and

-GMRES

Proof.

Define a matrix

of the form (

) such that

can be constructed as in the proof of

.
Let

be a unitary matrix arbitrarily chosen, and define

Referring to (

(Characterization of the preconditioner)

The following result is a direct corollary of
Theorem

22
: for any given system and preconditioner, regardless of the convergence curves achieved by left- and right-preconditioned GMRES, there exists another preconditioned system with the same spectrum that exhibits the opposite behavior.
This implies that, when considering all possible pairs of systems and preconditioners, neither left nor right preconditioning is inherently superior to the other.
We give a constructive proof using

.

Corollary 24
.

Let
(A,b)
define a given linear system, and

a preconditioner.

(resp.

(i) Let

Theorem 25

(Simultaneous prescription of two convergence curves)
.

Let
H\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be a non-singular matrix, and consider the residual decrease vectors

and
g_{R}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
. We assume that

and

have the same length. Let

,…,

be

(i)

such that the eigenvalues of

are the

-GMRES(

) preconditioned on the right by

realizes

-GMRES(

realizes

.

(ii)

of the form (

) such that

and the singular values of

are

are the

From these results, we can make the following observation: there exist preconditioned systems where the choice of left or right preconditioning leads to significantly different GMRES convergence behaviours. For instance, left preconditioning may result in fast convergence, while right preconditioning may lead to slow convergence, even if in both cases, the eigenvalues of the preconditioned system are perfectly clustered.
This observation reinforces what is already known: GMRES convergence is not solely determined by the eigenvalues.
It also highlights that for certain use cases, the choice of preconditioning on the left or on the right may not be trivial and lead to significant differences.

Can we break the observed behaviour?

In the first three experiments, all generated samples exhibited the same convergence pattern.
In the following experiments, we demonstrate that this behavior can, in fact, be disrupted by setting up an experiment for which we can compute the residuals of weighted and unweighted GMRES as follows.

1.

Set
\mu\in(\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus\{0\})^{n}

is the diagonal matrix corresponding to
\mu

is a random unitary matrix. This way,

is a hpd matrix with eigenvalues held in

.

2.

Let

be the right hand side.

3.

Normalize
M_{0}
as
M:=\beta M_{0}
, where
\beta:=\|b\|_{M_{0}}^{-2}
(so as to enforce
\|b\|_{M}=1
). This is our weight matrix.

4.

We consider a matrix
A
for which
W:=Q
is a nested orthonormal basis of

and compare

-GMRES
(A,b)
to

-GMRES
(A,b)
. The residuals do not depend on the particular choice of

(by

). There are two ways of computing the resulting residual norms:

•

Either, the matrix

5
Conclusion

In this work, we extended the theory of prescribed convergence curves for GMRES to the setting of weighted GMRES and preconditioned GMRES. Building on the foundational result of Greenbaum, Pták, and Strakoš, we demonstrated that for any linear system and any prescribed convergence behavior, one can construct a weight matrix or a preconditioning strategy that forces GMRES to exhibit this behavior.
Our theoretical contributions include a full characterization of weight matrices that yield a desired convergence curve and the necessary and sufficient conditions for realizing two distinct convergence curves simultaneously (one for
I
-GMRES and the other for
M
-GMRES).
We showed that these results naturally translate to the preconditioned setting, both with split and one-sided preconditioners, highlighting in particular that left and right preconditioning can yield markedly different convergence behaviors, regardless of the spectrum of the preconditioned system.

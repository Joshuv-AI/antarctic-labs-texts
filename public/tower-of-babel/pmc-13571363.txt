HLA-B27 and Constrictive Pericarditis
Authors: Peter J. Mattingly, Igor Dumic
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Corresponding Author

In this issue, Villalonga et al1 report a patient with HLA-B27 positivity who developed constrictive pericarditis (CP), raising the possibility of a previously unrecognized immunogenetic association. CP is a feared but rare complication of acute pericarditis; its epidemiology has evolved over the past 4 decades and varies by geographic region. In high-income countries, idiopathic cases predominate, and prior cardiac surgery and mediastinal radiation are among the most common nonidiopathic causes. Tuberculous pericarditis remains the predominant cause in resource-limited settings worldwide.2, 3, 4 The increasing volume and complexity of cardiac surgery and catheter-based interventions have contributed to a growing proportion of postoperative CP, while radiation-induced CP persists despite a lower incidence and longer latency with contemporary radiotherapy. The risk of progression to CP is highly dependent on the underlying etiology of acute pericarditis, being greatest after purulent and tuberculous pericarditis; intermediate in neoplastic, rheumatic, postradiation, and post–cardiac injury syndromes; and exceptionally low following appropriately treated idiopathic or viral pericarditis.2, 3, 4, 5 Against this evolving landscape, reports of potential novel inflammatory or immunogenetic associations are of particular interest.

Villalonga et al1 describe a patient with pericarditis and HLA-B27 positivity who failed guideline-directed anti-inflammatory therapy including colchicine, corticosteroids, ibuprofen, and indomethacin, and who developed CP. Consistent with current guidelines, the patient subsequently underwent successful pericardiectomy, with symptomatic improvement.1 Although rilonacept was considered for refractory disease, treatment was not pursued because of insurance denial. Despite an extensive diagnostic evaluation, no definitive etiology was identified, and the pericarditis remained classified as idiopathic. However, the presence of HLA-B27 is intriguing and raises the interesting possibility of a pathogenic association.

Established HLA-B27-associated cardiac disease classically involves aortopathies and conduction system abnormalities, with pericarditis only occurring infrequently.6 Though immunogenetic studies have identified several HLA alleles associated with recurrent pericarditis, HLA-B27 has not been directly implicated in pericarditis susceptibility to our knowledge.7 However, isolated case reports have described recurrent pericarditis, pericardial effusions, and cardiac tamponade in patients with HLA-B27–positive ankylosing spondylitis. These case-level observations suggest biological plausibility for an association between HLA-B27–associated inflammatory disorders and recurrent and constrictive pericarditis. The present case describes a patient with HLA-B27 positivity in the absence of a known systemic inflammatory disorder who developed treatment-resistant pericarditis with subsequent constrictive physiology, which raises the hypothesis of an association between HLA-B27 and pericardial disease. Awareness of this potential association may result in recognition and reporting of additional cases in the future that may clarify the prevalence and clinical significance of a link between the HLA-B27 allele and the development of recurrent pericarditis.

If HLA-B27 is indeed more than incidentally associated with CP, the established efficacy of TNF-α inhibitors in HLA-B27–associated inflammatory disorders, suggests TNF-α inhibition warrants investigation as a therapeutic strategy. Case-level evidence of pericardial disease improving with TNF-α inhibition in patients with concurrent spondyloarthritis further supports this hypothesis. In one such case, recurrent pericardial disease resolved following initiation of TNF-α inhibitor therapy for the underlying spondyloarthritis after conventional treatment had failed.8 Another report described recurrence of pericardial disease after discontinuation of TNF-α inhibitor therapy.9 Presently, these agents are not recommended for the treatment of CP, and they have not been evaluated in this setting. Future studies are needed to determine whether TNF-α inhibition has a therapeutic role in selected patients with HLA-B27–associated CP or other refractory inflammatory pericardial syndromes.

Though evidence linking HLA-B27 to CP is currently limited, cardiovascular medicine offers many examples of diseases whose recognition began with isolated clinical observations. For example, spontaneous coronary artery dissection, Takotsubo cardiomyopathy, and immune checkpoint inhibitor myocarditis all entered the literature as individual case reports or small case series before larger studies established them as distinct clinical entities.10, 11, 12 In that tradition, the report by Villalonga et al1 highlights a plausible immune-mediated mechanism for a subset of constrictive pericarditis and provides a foundation for future clinical and mechanistic investigation.

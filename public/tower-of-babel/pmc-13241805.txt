Construction and Practice of an Efficient Classroom for Surgical Procedures in a Digital Environment
Authors: Jiao Wang, Chunyu Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundHigher medical education has undergone profound changes arising from the digitalization of education. However, teaching efficiency needs to be further improved, and medical and surgical teaching still faces problems such as insufficient integration of theory and practice. This study focuses on digital technology‐enabled medical and surgical teaching to maximize teaching efficiency and explore innovative paths for improved teaching quality and cultivation of practice‐oriented medical skills.MethodsWe used a questionnaire survey method to investigate medical students' (n = 278) practical experiences of digitalized surgical courses. Data from the survey questionnaire were quantitatively analyzed to understand students' demographic backgrounds, learning outcomes using digital technology, mastery of surgical techniques, and satisfaction with new teaching methods.ResultsExcellent student learning outcomes were reported following the technology‐enabled medical and surgical teaching. Ninety‐five students (34.17%) demonstrated significant improvement in their mastery of surgical theory, and 120 students (43.16%) showed improvement. The mean score (maximum score of 5) for laparoscopic surgical skills was 3.85, and the mean score for anatomical structure recognition was 4.02, both indicating a high level of proficiency. In terms of teaching satisfaction, 235 students (84.53%) expressed satisfaction or strong satisfaction with the digital teaching methods, reflecting widespread acceptance of this educational model.ConclusionsDigital technology was associated with greater efficacy and skill mastery for medical students learning surgical procedures in our study. Students' teaching satisfaction also improved. This study demonstrates the value of digital technology use in medical education and suggests some new lines of research and practice in medical education.

Background

Digital technology has occupied an important position in curriculum education in the past decade. However, few studies have focused on the combination of digital technology and curriculum, and research on the concept of “digital ability” in the field of education also remains limited [1]. The rapid advancement of big data, cloud computing, and artificial intelligence technologies has impelled medical education to transcend traditional limitations and revolutionize educational content and methodologies [2]. Smart teaching process.

The electronic health record system empowers students to access extensive case databases, analyze data, and enhance their clinical reasoning abilities. Intelligent diagnostic support systems harness machine learning algorithms to facilitate students' mastery of precision medical competencies [3]. Telemedicine facilitates the sharing of resources across geographical boundaries, providing medical students with additional practical training opportunities. These digital instruments and technologies have enriched teaching approaches, markedly enhanced educational efficacy and student engagement, and established a robust foundation for nurturing high‐quality medical professionals [4].

The First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University has incorporated virtual reality (VR) technology into surgical education. Students use VR technology to simulate surgical procedures, visualizing the three‐dimensional (3D) structure of human organs, and practicing surgical techniques in a virtual setting. This immersive learning approach significantly boosts student engagement and enthusiasm, enabling them to refine their surgical skills through repeated practice in a safe, risk‐free environment [5]. Additionally, the VR system provides instant feedback on the students' performances, helping them to promptly correct errors and enhance their surgical precision.

Methods

Research Design

This study employed a cross‐sectional research design, using a structured questionnaire to gather data. The questionnaire encompassed diverse dimensions, including students' demographic information, use of digital learning resources, self‐assessment of surgical proficiency, perception of learning efficacy, and satisfaction with the novel teaching modality. Reliability and validity testing took place with the questionnaire to ensure reliability and validity of the research data. Cronbach's α coefficient was used to assess reliability, with results showing an overall Cronbach's α value of 0.837, and coefficients for each scale dimension ranging between 0.762 and 0.814, indicating good internal consistency of the questionnaire. For validity, the content validity ratio was tested by inviting 13 medical education experts to evaluate the questionnaire content, achieving an average content validity ratio value of 0.87. Additionally, the Kaiser–Meyer–Olkin value was 0.812, and Bartlett's sphericity test significance level was p < 0.001, demonstrating high structural validity of the questionnaire, which confirms its suitability for data collection in this study.

Sample Selection

A representative cohort of 300 surgical students was selected for this study. The students spanned from sophomore to senior years at The First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University.

Data Collection and Analysis

To improve convenience and increase survey efficiency, we used an online questionnaire for data collection, distributed from July 22, 2024 to September 15, 2024. After distribution, instructions and explanations were provided to ensure students could accurately understand the questions. To improve the response rate, reminders were sent via email and instant messaging tools on the third and fifth days following distribution to students who had not completed the questionnaire. Ultimately, 278 valid responses were received, with a response rate of 92.67%. The data were analyzed using SPSS 25.0 software (IBM, Armonk, United States), employing descriptive statistics to summarize students' basic information and learning conditions, and calculating satisfaction scores to investigate students' feedback and evaluation of the digital teaching model.

Comparison of Learning Outcomes

Students' proficiency in surgical theory knowledge markedly improved following the integration of digital technology (Table 2). Student feedback indicated that digital resources, such as 3D surgical simulation software, online surgical video tutorials, and surgical training systems, substantially aided the students' acquisition of theoretical knowledge. A significant proportion of students experienced notable enhancements in their understanding of surgical principles, steps, and associated risks. Most students reported improvements, attributing these to digital technology's role in reinforcing classroom instruction and fostering the integration of theory with practical application. A minority reported no change or a slight decline, possibly associated with individual study habits, technical proficiency, and adaptation to digital resources. On the one hand, some students relied too heavily on the entertainment value of the digital resources, neglecting active thinking. On the other hand, some students lacked proficiency in operating digital technologies and encountered obstacles in receiving information during learning. Additionally, their insufficient self‐directed learning ability made it difficult for them to effectively apply digital resources for systematic learning.

Comparison of Surgical Skill Mastery

Assessing students' proficiency in surgical skills is important for establishing an efficient classroom for medical surgical procedures within a digitalized context. The high‐definition (HD) laparoscopic simulation system enhances laparoscopic surgical skills by enabling students to practice repeatedly in a virtual environment (Table 3). It facilitates precise mastery of instrument manipulation, tissue dissection, and suturing techniques, thereby mitigating risks and bolstering proficiency and self‐confidence. The recognition of anatomical structures is also augmented through the use of 3D anatomical models and surgical training systems. These tools allow students to observe human structures from several angles and depths, simulate surgical anatomical changes, and attain a more profound understanding of anatomical relationships. Furthermore, the incorporation of digital technologies, such as intelligent feedback systems and personalized learning plans, elevates the relevance and efficacy of skills training. Students can select training modules and adjust difficulty levels based on their proficiency for a personalized learning experience.

Comparison of Teaching Satisfaction

Student satisfaction with the implementation of digital technology is a crucial metric for evaluating its effectiveness. Most students expressed “high satisfaction” or “satisfaction,” contending that technologies such as HD laparoscopic simulation systems, 3D anatomical models, and surgical training systems have enriched learning methodologies and enhance learning efficiency and surgical proficiency (Table 4). These technologies enable students to practice repeatedly in a secure environment, mitigating risks and facilitating a visual understanding of surgical principles, steps, and anatomical structures. Nonetheless, some students rated the technology as “average,” arguing that although it offers convenience, improvements are needed in terms of resource diversity, system stability, or ease of operation. A few students reported “dissatisfaction” or “strong dissatisfaction,” possibly linked to personal study habits, technological adaptability, or limited access to resources that prevented them from fully experiencing the benefits of digital technology.

Discussion

Digital competence is key for 21st century students. The emergence of digital technology has changed traditional global patterns of education, and the reform of digital education has led to new opportunities in teaching and learning. The application of digital technology has brought new thinking to teaching, including within specialized medical domains.

We constructed an efficient, digitalized medical surgical classroom and found a notable improvement in teaching efficacy and student feedback. The innovative teaching model has disrupted traditional medical education paradigms, significantly boosting students' learning efficiency and surgical skill proficiency. Regarding educational outcomes, the integration of digital technology is pivotal to the advancement of surgical theoretical knowledge. Students reported that resources such as 3D surgical simulation software, online surgical video tutorials, and surgical skills training systems offer novel learning experiences. These resources provide visual representations of the entire surgical procedure, aiding students in gaining a more profound understanding of surgical principles, steps, and associated risks. Repeated simulation exercises reinforce classroom learning and facilitate seamless integration of theory and practice [6]. Studies have shown that virtual simulation training, such as high‐fidelity surgical scenario simulations, allows students to repeatedly practice procedures in a risk‐free environment while receiving real‐time feedback on movement precision and instrument handling standards, which accelerates the neuromuscular memory formation for skill acquisition [7].

In terms of surgical skills mastery, the HD laparoscopic simulation system enables students to practice laparoscopic surgical techniques in a virtual environment, facilitating precise mastery of key steps including instrument handling, tissue dissection, and suturing [8]. This simulation‐based training mitigates the risks associated with actual surgical procedures and enhances students' proficiency and self‐confidence. Furthermore, 3D anatomical models and advanced surgical training systems empower students to visualize human structures from several angles and depths, simulate surgical anatomical changes, and develop a more profound understanding of anatomical structures and their interrelations. The application of artificial intelligence‐powered intelligent assessment systems in surgical education can provide precise, multi‐dimensional evaluations of students' surgical procedures (e.g., incision, suturing, knot tying, and cutting), output detailed assessment reports, and offer suggestions for improvement, thereby significantly enhancing skill acquisition efficiency [9]. Remote surgical demonstration teaching via digital platforms can also transmit real‐time ultra‐HD surgical images and enable synchronous explanation and interactive Q&A, effectively expanding the coverage of high‐quality educational resources [10].

Satisfaction with the integration of digital technology in teaching is a pivotal indicator of its effectiveness. At the beginning and end of the study, all participants completed the California Critical Thinking Dispositions Inventory to assess their tendencies toward critical thinking. Most acknowledged and expressed satisfaction with the novel teaching modality. Digital technology enriches the array of learning tools, thereby enhancing learning efficiency and the acquisition of surgical skills. It offers repeated practice opportunities within a secure, risk‐free environment and provides visual aids to facilitate comprehension of intricate surgical principles and anatomical structures. Some students have suggested that the richness of resources, system stability, and ease of operation require improvement, offering constructive insights for teaching improvement. In the digital era, an efficient medical surgical classroom must support personalized learning. An intelligent feedback system, coupled with personalized learning plans, enables students to select training modules and adjust difficulty levels based on their proficiency, catering to students' diverse learning needs.

The specific mechanisms by which digital technology enhances medical surgical education are reflected in multidimensional synergistic effects. At the knowledge delivery level, 3D modeling and dynamic visualization technologies transform abstract anatomical structures and surgical procedures into intuitive imagery, overcoming the limitations of traditional two‐dimensional atlases and static text. These technologies help students quickly develop spatial cognition while lowering comprehension barriers. Virtual simulation technology, through high‐fidelity surgical scenario simulations, allows students to repeatedly practice procedures in a risk‐free environment while receiving real‐time feedback on movement precision and instrument handling standards, accelerating the neuromuscular memory formation for skill acquisition. At the teaching interaction level, intelligent learning platforms leverage big data analytics to track student learning trajectories in real time, accurately identifying knowledge gaps and automatically delivering personalized learning content and advanced training tasks to achieve a tailored teaching approach. Online discussion forums and virtual teaching studios break down spatial and temporal barriers, facilitating case discussions and experience‐sharing among teachers and students. Such approaches foster critical thinking and clinical decision‐making skills. At the teaching management level, digital technology integrates learning data to generate visual reports, enabling instructors to dynamically adjust teaching strategies. Additionally, features such as learning progress alerts and personalized tutoring recommendations ensure precise allocation of teaching resources and closed‐loop management of learning outcomes.

Although this study demonstrated positive outcomes of digital medical surgical teaching, it has several limitations. First, the research sample was limited to 300 surgical students from second to fourth year at The First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University, with a small and single‐source sample, making it difficult to comprehensively reflect the differences among students from different regions, institutions, and backgrounds. This may limit the generalizability of the research findings. Second, the study did not include a control group for comparative analysis, making it difficult to objectively verify the contribution of digital technology to teaching effectiveness, ultimately reducing the rigor and persuasiveness of the conclusions. Subsequent research will be optimized in three aspects. First, we will significantly expand the sample size to include students from multiple medical schools across the country, covering different grades and levels, and multidisciplinary specialties such as clinical medicine, nursing, and anesthesiology, to build a more representative sample system. Second, future studies will design controlled experiments with parallel control groups for traditional and digital teaching, using standardized tests and skills assessments as quantitative indicators to systematically compare the teaching effectiveness of the two groups and visually demonstrate the advantages of digital technology. Third, deepening interdisciplinary research is needed to explore the application models and effectiveness differences of digital technology across medical fields. Studies should incorporate qualitative research methods to comprehensively analyze feedback from teachers and students, further improving the theoretical and practical system of digital medical education, and providing more scientific and comprehensive empirical evidence for promoting high‐quality development in medical education.

Conclusions

Digital technology has created a novel path for the teaching of internal medicine and surgery, effectively enhancing students' learning outcomes and surgical proficiency while significantly boosting teaching satisfaction. Ongoing exploration and refinement of digital teaching strategies will contribute to nurturing medical professionals who are theoretically grounded and technically adept. Thus, such strategies may add substantial value to the advancement of healthcare and assist in building standardized digital teaching plans and evaluation systems. Looking ahead, advancements in technology and educational paradigms may propel the efficient medical surgical classroom towards broader horizons within the digital landscape.

Splitting Proximal Point Algorithms for the Sum of Prox-Convex Functions
José de Brito, Felipe Lara, Tran Van Thang

Abstract
\vskip-34.14322pt Abstract This paper addresses the minimization of a finite sum of prox-convex functions under Lipschitz continuity of each component. We propose two variants of the splitting proximal point algorithms proposed in [ 2 , 5 ] : one deterministic with a fixed update order, and one stochastic with random sampling, and we extend them from convex to prox-convex functions. We prove global convergence for both methods under standard stepsize assumptions, with almost sure convergence for the stochastic variant via supermartingale theory. Numerical experiments with nonconvex quadratic functions illustrate the efficiency of the proposed methods and support the theoretical results. Keywords : Nonconvex optimization; Splitting algorithms, Proximal point algorithms; Generalized convexity; Prox-convex functions Mathematics Subject Classification: 90C26; 90C30.



1
Introduction

Nonconvex optimization is widely present in modern machine learning and scientific computing. However, it remains substantially more challenging than its convex counterpart, primarily due to the prevalence of suboptimal local minima and the general lack of global optimality guarantees.

A common strategy to bridge this gap is to identify structured classes of nonconvex functions that retain tractable properties. This pursuit has given rise to the field of generalized convexity. Notions such as quasiconvexity, pseudoconvexity, weak convexity, and the difference-of-convex (DC) structure have long been studied to relax convexity while preserving manageable analysis and convergence guarantees
[
12
,
18
]
.

In this work, we focus on the class of prox-convex functions, a recent generalization introduced in
[
14
]
. We study the problem of minimizing a finite sum of such functions over a closed set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\min_{x\in K}f(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}(x),

(1.1)

where
f_{i}:K\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz continuous and prox-convex function for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, and
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

The appeal of prox-convexity stems from its preservation of key operational properties found in convex optimization, specifically, each function
f_{i}
has a single-valued and firmly nonexpansive proximal operator
[
14
]
, a trait traditionally exclusive to convex functions. However, a significant challenge arises: the aggregate function
f
is not necessarily prox-convex, and its proximal operator generally lacks a closed-form expression. Consequently, classical proximal point algorithms, which require solving the full proximal subproblem at each iteration, are often computationally intractable for
f
. This limitation necessitates the use of splitting techniques that instead leverage the tractable proximal operators of the individual components
f_{i}
.

While the proximal point algorithm (PPA henceforth)
[
35
]
minimizes a convex function by iteratively applying its proximal operator, splitting methods are designed for composite problems. These algorithms decompose the objective by utilizing the proximal operators of its individual parts, thus avoiding the prohibitive cost of computing the proximal operator of the entire sum. This methodology, pioneered for operators in
[
27
]
, has become a cornerstone of modern first-order optimization, as extensively detailed in contemporary texts
[
3
,
4
]
.

When the objective function
f
decomposes as a sum of many terms, a natural strategy is to employ incremental proximal steps on each component. This idea was formalized for the convex setting in
[
5
]
, who proposed an incremental proximal method for large-scale optimization. Their algorithm, often termed a cyclic proximal point algorithm, sequentially applies the proximal operator of each
f_{i}
in a fixed order, thus avoiding the need for a single, costly proximal step on the entire sum
f
. This cyclic scheme is guaranteed to converge for convex problems under standard assumptions
[
4
,
5
]
. A key advantage of such methods is their decomposition of a large, complex problem into
N
simpler subproblems, each involving only a single component.

In our prox-convex setting, adopting a similar splitting strategy is highly natural, as each
f_{i}
can be efficiently minimized in isolation via its proximal operator. The central question, however, is whether the favorable convergence properties of these methods persist without convexity. Recent research provides encouraging evidence. An extension of PPA for prox-convex functions was established in
[
14
]
, and subsequent works have successfully incorporated advanced features like relaxation and inertia into proximal schemes for structured nonconvex problems
[
15
,
16
]
. These developments demonstrate that the principles of PPA can be generalized to certain nonconvex settings by leveraging structured properties like prox-convexity as a substitute for global convexity.

In this context, we propose a deterministic splitting PPA for problem (
1.1
) (Method 1).
This algorithm applies the proximity operators of
f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}
according to a fixed permutation at each cycle, encompassing the purely cyclic case as a particular instance, and can be seen as an extension of the incremental strategy of
[
5
]
to prox-convex functions. Furthermore, we study a randomized splitting PPA (Method 2), where at each iteration a single index
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
is chosen at random and a proximal step is taken only with respect to
f_{i}
.
Such random-order schemes are well established in the convex literature and are attractive due to their simplicity and low per-iteration cost
[
6
,
29
,
30
,
34
]
.
We prove that, under standard diminishing step-size conditions, both algorithms converge to a minimizer of
f
, despite the lack of global convexity.
In the deterministic case, convergence follows from a descent property, while in the stochastic case almost sure convergence is established using supermartingale arguments
[
31
]
. Numerical experiments on nonconvex quadratic problems illustrate the practical relevance of our theoretical results.

The structure of the paper is as follows. Section
2
reviews preliminaries and definitions of prox-convex functions and their properties. Section
3
presents the deterministic (Method 1) and stochastic (Method 2) splitting algorithms and its convergence analysis. Section
4
contains numerical experiments, and Section
5
concludes with remarks and perspectives.

Preliminaries and Basic Definitions

The inner product of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the Euclidean norm are denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
\lVert\cdot\rVert
, respectively. Let

K
be a nonempty set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, its closure is denoted by
\overline{K}
, its boundary by
{\rm bd}\,K
, its topological interior by
{\rm int}\,K
and its convex hull by
{\rm conv}\,K
. Given a convex and closed
set
K
, the projection of
x
on
K
is denoted by
P_{K}(x)
, and the
indicator function on
K
by
\iota_{K}
. The ball with center at
x
and
radius
\delta>0
is denoted by
\mathbb{B}(x,\delta)
and the identity operator by
I
.

Given any
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, the
following relations hold:

\displaystyle~~~~~\langle x-z,y-x\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\lVert z-y\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\lVert x-z\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2},

(2.1)

\displaystyle\lVert\beta x+(1-\beta)y\rVert^{2}=\beta\lVert x\rVert^{2}+(1-\beta)\lVert y\rVert^{2}-\beta(1-\beta)\lVert x-y\rVert^{2}.

(2.2)

Given any extended-valued function
h:K\ \rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
, the effective
domain of
h
is defined by
{\rm dom}\,h:=\{x\in K:h(x)<+\infty\}
. It is said that
h
is proper if
{\rm dom}\,h
is
nonempty and
h(x)>-\infty
for all
x\in K
. The notion of properness is important when dealing with minimization problems.

It is indicated by
{\rm epi}\,h:=\{(x,t)\in K\times\mathbb{R}:h(x)\leq t\}
the epigraph of
h
, by
S_{\lambda}(h):=\{x\in K:h(x)\leq\lambda\}
the sublevel set of
h
at
the height
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and by
{\rm argmin}_{K}h
the set of all minimal points of
h
in
K
. Furthermore, the
current convention
\sup_{\emptyset}h:=-\infty
and
\inf_{\emptyset}h:=+\infty
is adopted.

A function
h
defined on
K
with a convex domain is said to be

(a)

(strongly) convex on
{\rm dom}\,h
, if there exists
\gamma\geq 0
such that for all
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}\,h
and all
\lambda\in[0,1]
, we have

h(\lambda y+(1-\lambda)x)\leq\lambda h(y)+(1-\lambda)h(x)-\lambda(1-\lambda)\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert x-y\rVert^{2},

(2.3)

(b)

(strongly) quasiconvex on
{\rm dom}\,h
, if there exists
\gamma\geq 0
such that for all
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}\,h
and all
\lambda\in[0,1]
, we have

h(\lambda y+(1-\lambda)x)\leq\max\{h(y),h(x)\}-\lambda(1-\lambda)\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert x-y\rVert^{2}.

(2.4)

Every (strongly) convex function is (strongly) quasiconvex, while the reverse statement does not holds (see
[
12
,
18
,
25
]
).

A proper function
h:K\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is said to be (see
[
11
]
):

(i)

2-supercoercive, if

\liminf_{\lVert x\rVert\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{h(x)}{\lVert x\rVert^{2}}>0,

(2.5)

(ii)

coercive, if

\lim_{\lVert x\rVert\rightarrow+\infty}h(x)=+\infty.

(2.6)

or equivalently, if
S_{\lambda}(h)
is bounded for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

(iii)

2
-weakly coercive, if

\liminf_{\,\lVert x\rVert\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{h(x)}{\lVert x\rVert^{2}}\geq 0,

(2.7)

Clearly,
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
, but the converse statements
do not hold in general. Indeed, the function
h(x)=\lvert x\rvert
is
coercive without being
2
-supercoercive while the function
h(x)=x
is

2
-weakly coercive without being coercive. A survey on coercivity notions
is
[
11
]
.

As noted in
[
25
, Theorem 1]
, strongly quasiconvex functions are

2
-supercoercive. In particular, lsc strongly quasiconvex functions
has an unique global minimum on closed convex sets (see
[
25
, Corollary 3]
).

A function
h:K\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
its said to be

L
-smooth if it is differentiable and

\lVert\nabla h(x)-\nabla h(y)\rVert\leq L\lVert x-y\rVert,~\forall~x,y\in K.

(2.8)

Given a proper function
h:K\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, the convex subdifferential of
h
at
\overline{x}\in{\rm dom}\,h
is defined by

\partial_{K}h(\overline{x}):=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:~h(y)\geq h(\overline{x})+\langle\xi,y-\overline{x}\rangle,~\forall~y\in K\},

(2.9)

and by
\partial_{K}h(x)=\emptyset
if
x\not\in{\rm dom}\,h
.

The
proximity operator of parameter
\beta>0
of a function

h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined as

{\rm Prox}_{\beta h}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where

x\mapsto\,\amin\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{h(y)+\frac{1}{2\beta}\|y-x\|^{2}\right\}.

(2.10)

For any lower semicontinuous (lsc henceforth) function,
2
-weakly coerciveness is sufficient
for the nonemptiness of the proximity operator (see
[
14
, Proposition
3.1]
for instance). Furthermore, when
h
is proper, convex and lsc,
{\rm Prox}_{\beta h}
turns out to be a single-valued
operator, and the following well-know identity holds

\displaystyle\overline{x}={\rm Prox}_{\beta h}(z)~\Longleftrightarrow~\frac{1}{\beta}(z-\overline{x})\in\partial h(\overline{x}).

(2.11)

Moreover, when
\beta=1
we write
{\rm Prox}_{h}
instead of

{\rm Prox}_{1h}
.

We denote

{\rm Prox}_{\beta h}(K,z):={\rm Prox}_{(\beta h+\iota_{K})}(z)={\rm argmin}_{y\in K}\left\{h(y)+\frac{1}{2\beta}\|y-z\|^{2}\right\}.

(2.12)

We recall the following generalized convexity notion
[
13
,
14
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
K
be a closed set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper function such that
K\cap{\rm dom}\,h\neq\emptyset
and
\beta>0
. It is said that
h
is prox-convex on

K
if there exists
\alpha>0
such that for every
z\in K
,
\px_{\beta h}(K,z)\neq\emptyset
, and

\overline{x}\in{\rm Prox}_{\beta h}(K,z)~\Longrightarrow~h(\overline{x})-h(x)\leq\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\langle\overline{x}-z,x-\overline{x}\rangle,~\forall~x\in K.

(2.13)

The scalar
\alpha>0
for which (
2.13
) holds is said to be
the
prox-convex value
of the function
h
on
K
. When
K=\mathbb{R}^{n}
we say that
h
is
prox-convex
.

Example 2.1
.

(see
[
37
]
)
We consider the nonconvex functions
g(x)=-x^{2}-x
and
h=5x+\ln(10x+1)
, which were employed in
[
14
]
as cost functions for oligopolistic equilibrium problems. Then,

(i)

g(x)
is prox-convex on
[0,r]
with modulus
\alpha=1
and satisfying (
2.13
) for all
\beta>0
;

(ii)

h(x)
is prox-convex on
[1,2]
with modulus
\alpha=1
and satisfying (
2.13
) for all
\beta>\frac{1}{5}
.

Every (strongly) convex function is prox-convex with prox-convex value

\alpha=1
(see
[
14
, Proposition 3.4]
). The reverse statement
does not hold as the function
h:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

h(x)=-x^{2}-x
shows (see
[
14
, Example 3.1]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

(see
[
37
]
)
Let
\mathbb{R}^{n}=\prod^{N}_{i=1}\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
, where
\sum^{N}_{i=1}n_{i}=n
and
n_{i}\geq 1
, with
i=1,\ldots,N
. Then for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have
x=(x^{1},\ldots,x^{N})
where
x^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
for every
i=1,\ldots,N
. For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, let
C_{i}
be a closed subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
and
g_{i}:C_{i}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be proper prox-convex on

. Then the function
g(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}g_{i}(x^{i})
is prox-convex on
C
, where
C=\prod^{N}_{i=1}C_{i}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(see
[
14
]
)
Let
K
be a closed and convex subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper lsc function on

K
such that
K\cap{\rm dom}\,h\neq\emptyset
. If
h
satisfies the
following conditions:

(a)

h
is strongly quasiconvex on
K
with modulus
\eta>0
,

(b)

for each
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
there exists
\overline{x}
such
that
{\rm Prox}_{h}(K,z)=\overline{x}
and

\frac{1}{2}(z-\bar{x})\in\partial^{\leq}(h+\iota_{K})(\overline{x}),

then

is prox-convex on

with prox-convex value
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
(where
\partial^{\leq}
is the Gutiérrez subdifferential
[

]
).

Remark 2.1
.

Note that strongly quasiconvex functions and prox-convex functions are
not related each other. Indeed, the function
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\mapsto\sqrt{x}
is strongly quasiconvex on convex and bounded intervals (see

[
25
, Proposition 16]
) without being prox-convex while the constant function
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
is convex (hence prox-convex) without being strongly
quasiconvex.

For a prox-convex function
h
with prox-convex value
\alpha>0
, we have

\overline{x}\in{\rm Prox}_{h}(K,z)~\Longrightarrow~z-\overline{x}\in\partial\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}(h+\iota_{K})\right)(\overline{x}).

(2.14)

When
K
is a closed set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is a proper prox-convex function on
K
such that
K\cap{\rm dom}\,h\neq\emptyset
. Then the map

z\rightarrow\px_{h}(K,z)
is single-valued and firmly nonexpansive by
[
14
, Proposition 3.3]
.

If
h
is prox-convex with prox-convex value
\alpha>0
, then (see
[
14
, Page 322]
) we know that
\px_{(1/\alpha)h}={\rm Prox}_{h}
is a singleton, hence

{}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}h(z)=\min_{x\in K}\left(h(x)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\lVert z-x\rVert^{2}\right)=h({\px}_{h}(z))+\frac{\alpha}{2}\lVert z-{\px}_{h}(z)\rVert^{2}.

(2.15)

Consequently,
{}^{1/\alpha}h(z)\in\mathbb{R}
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The following result will be useful in the sequel.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
h:K\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a function prox-convex in
K
with modulus
\alpha>0
.
If
\bar{x}\in{\rm Prox}_{\beta h}(K,z)
, then

h(\bar{x})-h(x)\leq\dfrac{\alpha}{2\beta}\left(\|z-x\|^{2}-\|\bar{x}-x\|^{2}\right),~\forall~x\in{\rm dom}\,h.

(2.16)

Proof.

Let
x\in{\rm dom}\,h
. Note that

\displaystyle\|z-x\|^{2}=\|z-\bar{x}+\bar{x}-x\|^{2}=\|z-\bar{x}\|^{2}+2\langle z-\bar{x},\bar{x}-x\rangle+\|\bar{x}-x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 125.19194pt\geq\|z-\bar{x}\|^{2}+\dfrac{2\beta}{\alpha}(h(\bar{x})-h(x))+\|\bar{x}-x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow~h(\bar{x})-h(x)\leq\dfrac{\alpha}{2\beta}\left(\|z-x\|^{2}-\|\bar{x}-x\|^{2}-\|z-\bar{x}\|^{2}\right),

and the result follows.

3
Splitting Proximal Point
Algorithms

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty closed set,
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
f_{i}:K\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be proper, lsc and prox-convex functions on
K
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
. We are interested in problem (
1.1
), that is,

\min_{x\in K}f(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}(x).

(3.1)

In the following subsections, we will present two splitting type methods for solving problem (
3.1
).

3.1
Permuted-order Version

Method 1 (Permuted-order version).

Consider a function
f:K\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
given by (
3.1
), an initial point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a stepsize sequence
(\beta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of positive reals.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, let
\pi_{k}:\{1,\ldots,N\}\to\{1,\ldots,N\}
be a permutation. Then the sequence
(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is recursively defined as follows:

\displaystyle x_{kN+j}

\displaystyle\in\text{Prox}_{\beta_{k}f_{\pi_{k}(j)}}(K,x_{kN+(j-1)}),\qquad j=1,\ldots,N.

(3.2)

We immediately observe the following.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(x_{l})
be the sequence defined by the permuted-order method (
3.2
), where the functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}:K\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}

are Lipschitz continuous with a common constant
L
and prox-convex in
K
with modulus
\alpha>0
.
Then, for any
x\in{\rm dom}\,f
, the following
inequality holds

\|x_{(k+1)N}-x\|^{2}\leq\|x_{kN}-x\|^{2}-\dfrac{2\beta_{k}}{\alpha}(f(x_{kN})-f(x))+\dfrac{2L^{2}N(N+1)\beta_{k}^{2}}{\alpha}.

(3.3)

Proof.

Given
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, we apply Lemma
2.3
to the function
f_{\pi_{k}(j)}
, using
z=x_{kN+(j-1)}
,
\beta=\beta_{k}
, and
x\in\operatorname{dom}f
, which yields

\|x_{kN+j}-x\|^{2}\leq\|x_{kN+(j-1)}-x\|^{2}-\dfrac{2\beta_{k}}{\alpha}[f_{\pi_{k}(j)}(x_{kN+j})-f_{\pi_{k}(j)}(x)].

Fixing
k
and summing over
j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, we obtain

\|x_{kN+N}-x\|^{2}\leq\|x_{kN}-x\|^{2}-\dfrac{2\beta_{k}}{\alpha}\sum_{j=1}^{N}[f_{\pi_{k}(j)}(x_{kN+j})-f_{\pi_{k}(j)}(x)].

(3.4)

By the definition of Method 1, we have

f_{\pi_{k}(j)}(x_{kN+j})+\frac{1}{2\beta_{k}}\|x_{kN+j-1}-x_{kN+j}\|^{2}\leq f_{\pi_{k}(j)}(x_{kN+j-1}),

for every
j=1,\dots,N
. Since
f_{\pi_{k}(j)}
is Lipschitz continuous with constant
L
, we get

\|x_{kN+j}-x_{kN+j-1}\|\leq 2\beta_{k}\frac{f_{\pi_{k}(j)}(x_{kN+j-1})-f_{\pi_{k}(j)}(x_{kN+j})}{\|x_{kN+j-1}-x_{kN+j}\|}\leq 2\beta_{k}L.

(3.5)

Using the Lipschitz continuity of each component function and applying (
3.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle~~~\,\sum_{j=1}^{N}[f_{\pi_{k}(j)}(x_{kN+j})-f_{\pi_{k}(j)}(x)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left[(f_{\pi_{k}(j)}(x_{kN+j})-f_{\pi_{k}(j)}(x_{kN}))+(f_{\pi_{k}(j)}(x_{kN})-f_{\pi_{k}(j)}(x))\right]

\displaystyle=f(x_{kN})-f(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{N}[f_{\pi_{k}(j)}(x_{kN+j})-f_{\pi_{k}(j)}(x_{kN})]

\displaystyle\geq f(x_{kN})-f(x)-L\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|x_{kN+j}-x_{kN}\|

\displaystyle\geq f(x_{kN})-f(x)-L\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\|x_{kN+j}-x_{kN+j-1}\|+\ldots+\|x_{kN+1}-x_{kN}\|\right)

\displaystyle\geq f(x_{kN})-f(x)-L\sum_{j=1}^{N}2\beta_{k}Lj

\displaystyle=f(x_{kN})-f(x)-L^{2}\beta_{k}N(N+1).

Then, it follows from (
3.4
) that

\displaystyle\|x_{(k+1)N}-x\|^{2}\leq\|x_{kN}-x\|^{2}-\dfrac{2\beta_{k}}{\alpha}(f(x_{kN})-f(x))+\dfrac{2L^{2}N(N+1)\beta_{k}^{2}}{\alpha},

and the result follows.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Let
x\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{y\in K}f(y)
and suppose the conditions of Proposition
3.1
hold.
If
f(x_{kN})>f(x)
and

0<\beta_{k}<\frac{f(x_{kN})-f(x)}{L^{2}N(N+1)},

then

\|x_{(k+1)N}-x\|^{2}<\|x_{kN}-x\|^{2}.

Indeed, from (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\|x_{(k+1)N}-x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{kN}-x\|^{2}+\dfrac{2\beta_{k}}{\alpha}(\beta_{k}L^{2}N(N+1)-(f(x_{kN})-f(x)))

\displaystyle<\|x_{kN}-x\|^{2},

which guarantees a strict decrease in the distance to the minimizer after each full cycle of the Method.

Based on Proposition
3.1
, we establish that the sequence generated by Method 1 converges to a minimizer of
f
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(x_{l})
be the sequence defined in method 1, where
(\beta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a sequence of positive real numbers satisfying

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\beta_{k}=+\infty,\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\beta_{k}^{2}<+\infty.

(3.6)

If
{\rm argmin}_{K}f\neq\emptyset
and the functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}
are Lipschitz continuous with a common constant
L
and prox-convex with modulus
\alpha>0
, then the sequence
(x_{l})
converges to a minimizer of
f
.

Proof.

Applying Proposition
3.1
for
x\in{\rm argmin}_{K}f
, it follows that

\|x_{kN+N}-x\|^{2}\leq\|x_{kN}-x\|^{2}+\dfrac{2L^{2}N(N+1)\beta_{k}^{2}}{\alpha}.

(3.7)

By summing (
3.7
) over
k\geq p
, where
p\in\mathbb{N}
, and taking
\limsup_{k\to\infty}
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\|x_{kN}-x\|^{2}\leq\|x_{pN}-x\|^{2}+\dfrac{2L^{2}N(N+1)}{\alpha}\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}\beta_{k}^{2}.

(3.8)

Since
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}^{2}<\infty
, taking
\liminf_{p\to\infty}
gives

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\|x_{kN}-x\|^{2}\leq\displaystyle\liminf_{p\to\infty}\|x_{pN}-x\|^{2}.

Hence, the sequence
\left(\|x_{kN}-x\|\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
converges. It follows from (
3.3
) that

\dfrac{2}{\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{p}[f(x_{kN})-f(x)]\beta_{k}\leq\|x_{pN}-x\|^{2}-\|x_{pN+N}-x\|^{2}+\dfrac{2L^{2}N(N+1)}{\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{p}\beta_{k}^{2}.

Thus,
\sum_{k=1}^{p}[f(x_{kN})-f(x)]\beta_{k}<+\infty
. As
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}=+\infty
, there exists a subsequence
(x_{k_{j}N})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}f(x_{k_{j}N})=f(x)=\operatorname{min}(f).

Since the sequence
\left(\|x_{k_{j}N}-x\|\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges, it follows that
\left(x_{k_{j}N}\right)
admits a subsequence converging to some
x^{\ast}\in K
. By the continuity of
f
, we have
f(x^{\ast})=\operatorname{min}(f)
, implying
x^{\ast}\in{\rm argmin}_{K}f
. Hence,
\left(\|x_{kN}-x^{\ast}\|\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to zero, which means

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}x_{kN}=x^{\ast}.

Given
n\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, it follows from (
3.5
) that

\displaystyle\|x_{kN+n}-x^{\ast}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{kN+n}-x_{kN+(n-1)}\|+\ldots+\|x_{kN+1}-x_{kN}\|+\|x_{kN}-x^{\ast}\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\beta_{k}Ln+\|x_{kN}-x^{\ast}\|.

Since
\beta_{k}\to 0
, it follows that
\left(x_{kN+n}\right)
converges to
x^{\ast}
, and consequently,
(x_{l})
also converges to
x^{\ast}
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The results presented in this section also apply when using a cyclic update order. This cyclic method, where proximity operators are applied one after another in a loop, has been studied for convex functions by
[
5
,
2
]
. In this case, the proximity operators associated with the functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}
are applied sequentially in a fixed cyclic order, that is, given a sequence of positive real numbers
(\beta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and an initial point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the method generates a sequence
(x_{k})
according to the following rule: for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle x_{kN+1}

\displaystyle\in{\rm Prox}_{\beta_{k}f_{1}}(x_{kN}),

\displaystyle x_{kN+2}

\displaystyle\in{\rm Prox}_{\beta_{k}f_{2}}(x_{kN+1}),

\displaystyle x_{(k+1)N}

\displaystyle\in{\rm Prox}_{\beta_{k}f_{N}}(x_{(N+N-1}).

Remark 3.3
.

Our deterministic scheme generalizes the incremental proximal methods of
[
2
,
5
]
from the convex to the prox-convex setting. Furthermore, a key difference is that the full function
f
need not be prox-convex itself, just all components
f_{i}
.

3.2
Random Order Version.

In this section, we study a stochastic variant of the splitting PPA to solve problem (
3.1
), in which each component function
f_{i}
is assumed to be prox-convex in a nonempty closed set in
K
. At each iteration, the index of the function to be processed is randomly selected.

Consider the set
B=\{1,\ldots,N\}
and the uniform probability measure
p
on
B
, where
p_{i}=p(\{i\})=\frac{1}{N}
for each
i\in B
. We denote by
\Sigma
the product space
B^{\mathbb{N}}
, furnished with the product
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
and the product measure
\mathbb{P}=p^{\mathbb{N}}
. This means that each element of
\Sigma
is an infinite sequence of natural numbers in
\{1,\ldots,N\}
. The
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
of
\Sigma
is generated by the cylinders

[m;\alpha_{m},\ldots,\alpha_{k}]:=\{(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}};x_{i}=\alpha_{i}\text{ for }m\leq i\leq k\}.

The product measure
\mathbb{P}
is a probability measure on
\Sigma
, called the Bernoulli measure. Furthermore, the measure
\mathbb{P}
is characterized by

\mathbb{P}([m;\alpha_{m},\ldots,\alpha_{k}])=\prod_{i=m}^{k}p_{\alpha_{i}}.

Method 2 (Stochastic Version).

Let
(r_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a sequence of random variables defined on the probability space
(\Sigma,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})
, that is, a sequence
(r_{k})
taking values in
B
, sampled according to the uniform distribution and independently at each step.

Given an initial point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a stepsize sequence
(\beta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of positive reals, the sequence
(x_{k})
is recursively defined by:

x_{k+1}\in{\rm Prox}_{\beta_{k}f_{r_{k}}}(x_{k}),\qquad k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(3.9)

Our aim is to establish that, under suitable assumptions on the stepsize and on the regularity of the component functions, the sequence
(x_{k})
converges, almost surely, to a minimizer of the full objective function
f
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(x_{k})
be the sequence defined in (
3.9
), where the functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}
are Lipschitz continuous with a common constant
L
and prox-convex with modulus
\alpha>0
. Then, for every
x\in{\rm dom}\,f
, the following inequality holds:

\mathbb{E}\left[\|x_{k+1}-x\|^{2}\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]\leq\|x_{k}-x\|^{2}-\frac{2\beta_{k}}{\alpha N}\left[f(x_{k})-f(x)\right]+\dfrac{4\beta_{k}^{2}L^{2}}{\alpha},

(3.10)

where
\mathcal{F}_{k}:=\sigma(r_{0},r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
.

Proof.

Fix
x\in{\rm dom}\,f
arbitrarily. For each
n\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, define

x_{k}^{(n)}:={\rm Prox}_{\beta_{k}f_{n}}(x_{k}).

Applying Lemma
2.3
to each
f_{n}
, we obtain

\|x_{k}^{(n)}-y\|^{2}\leq\|x_{k}-x\|^{2}-\dfrac{2\beta_{k}}{\alpha}\left[f_{n}(x_{k}^{(n)})-f_{n}(x)\right].

Since
x_{k+1}=x_{k}^{(r_{k})}
, we have

\|x_{k+1}-x\|^{2}\leq\|x_{k}-x\|^{2}-\dfrac{2\beta_{k}}{\alpha}\left[f_{r_{k}}(x_{k+1})-f_{r_{k}}(x)\right].

Taking the conditional expectation with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{k}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\|x_{k+1}-x\|^{2}\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]\leq\|x_{k}-x\|^{2}-\dfrac{2\beta_{k}}{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[f_{r_{k}}(x_{k+1})-f_{r_{k}}(x)\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{k}\right].

Using the fact that
r_{k}
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{k}
and uniformly distributed over
\{1,\dots,N\}
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[\|x_{k+1}-x\|^{2}\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]\leq\|x_{k}-x\|^{2}-\dfrac{2\beta_{k}}{\alpha}\cdot\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left[f_{n}(x_{k}^{(n)})-f_{n}(x)\right].

Next, we decompose each term as follows

f_{n}(x_{k}^{(n)})-f_{n}(x)=f_{n}(x_{k})-f_{n}(x)-\left[f_{n}(x_{k})-f_{n}(x_{k}^{(n)})\right].

Hence,

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left[f_{n}(x_{k}^{(n)})-f_{n}(x)\right]=f(x_{k})-f(x)-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left[f_{n}(x_{k})-f_{n}(x_{k}^{(n)})\right].

Using the Lipschitz continuity of each
f_{n}
, together with inequality (
3.5
), we have

f_{n}(x_{k})-f_{n}(x_{k}^{(n)})\leq L\|x_{k}^{(n)}-x_{k}\|\leq 2\beta_{k}L^{2}.

Summing over
n
, we obtain

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left[f_{n}(x_{k}^{(n)})-f_{n}(x_{k})\right]\leq 2\beta_{k}L^{2}N.

Putting all together

\mathbb{E}\left[\|x_{k+1}-x\|^{2}\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]\leq\|x_{k}-x\|^{2}-\frac{2\beta_{k}}{\alpha N}\left[f(x_{k})-f(x)\right]+\dfrac{4\beta_{k}^{2}L^{2}}{\alpha},

which completes the proof.
∎

To prove the convergence of the sequence generated by Method 2, we will use the following result, known as the Supermartingale Convergence Theorem. A proof of this result can be found, for instance, in
[
7
]
or
[
31
]
.

Theorem 3.2

(Supermartingale Convergence Theorem)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}},\mu)
be a filtered probability space. Assume that
(Y_{k})
,
(Z_{k})
, and
(W_{k})
are sequences of nonnegative real-valued random variables defined on
\Omega
, and assume that:

i.

Y_{k}
,
Z_{k}
, and
W_{k}
are
\mathcal{F}_{k}
-measurable for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

ii.

\mathbb{E}(Y_{k+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k})\leq Y_{k}-Z_{k}+W_{k}
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

iii.

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}W_{k}<+\infty
almost surely.

Then the sequence
(Y_{k})
converges to a finite nonnegative random variable
Y
almost surely, and
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Z_{k}<+\infty
almost surely.

The desired result is given below.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(x_{l})
be the sequence defined in Method 2, where
(\beta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a sequence of positive real numbers satisfying

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\beta_{k}=+\infty,\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\beta_{k}^{2}<+\infty.

(3.11)

If
{\rm\arg\min}_{K}f\neq\emptyset
and the functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}
are Lipschitz continuous and prox-convex with modulus
\alpha
. Then, with probability
1
, the sequence
(x_{l})
converges to a minimizer of
f
.

Proof.

Given
x^{\ast}\in{\rm argmin}_{K}f
, and using Proposition
3.2
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[\|x_{k+1}-x^{\ast}\|^{2}\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]\leq\|x_{k}-x^{\ast}\|^{2}-\frac{2\beta_{k}}{\alpha N}\left[f(x_{k})-f(x^{\ast})\right]+\dfrac{4\beta_{k}^{2}L^{2}}{\alpha},

where
\mathcal{F}_{k}:=\sigma(r_{0},r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
.

By Theorem
3.2
, there exists a subset
\Sigma_{x^{\ast}}\subset\Sigma
with
\mathbb{P}(\Sigma_{x^{\ast}})=1
such that, for every
(r_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in\Sigma_{x^{\ast}}
, the sequence
\|x_{k}-x^{\ast}\|
converges and

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{2\beta_{k}}{\alpha N}\left[f(x_{k})-f(x^{\ast})\right]<+\infty.

(3.12)

Since
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is separable, there exists a countable dense subset
\{w_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset{\rm argmin}_{K}h
that is dense in
{\rm argmin}{K}h
. Define

\overline{\Sigma}:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{\infty}\Sigma_{w_{i}}.

Note that

\mathbb{P}(\overline{\Sigma})=\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{\infty}\Sigma_{w_{i}}\right)=1-\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\Sigma_{w_{i}}^{c}\right)\geq 1-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(\Sigma_{w_{i}}^{c})=1.

Thus,
\mathbb{P}(\overline{\Sigma})=1
. Moreover, for every sequence
(r_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in\overline{\Sigma}
and
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
\lVert x_{k}-w_{i}\rVert
is convergent and satisfies (
3.12
). Given that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\beta_{k}=+\infty
, it follows from (
3.12
) that

\liminf_{k\to\infty}f(x_{k})=\min(f)\quad.

(3.13)

Given
i\in\mathbb{N}
, the sequence
\lVert x_{k}-w_{i}\rVert
is convergent and, in particular, bounded. Since
f
is continuous, it follows from (
3.13
) that
(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
admits some cluster point
x^{\ast}\in\arg\min(f)
. Given
\epsilon>0
, since
\{w_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is dense in
{\rm argmin}_{K}f
, there exists
i(\epsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\lVert w_{i(\epsilon)}-x^{\ast}\rVert<\epsilon
. Note that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lVert x_{k}-x_{i(\epsilon)}\rVert\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\left(\lVert x_{k}-x^{\ast}\rVert+\lVert x^{\ast}-x_{i(\epsilon)}\rVert\right)\leq\epsilon.

Hence,

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\lVert x_{k}-x^{\ast}\rVert\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\lVert x_{k}-x_{i(\epsilon)}\rVert+\lVert x^{\ast}-x_{i(\epsilon)}\rVert\right)\leq 2\epsilon.

(3.14)

Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, it follows that
(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
converges to
x^{\ast}
.
∎

Note that our stochastic framework extend the classical randomized PPA from convex functions (see
[
2
,
5
]
) to prox-convex ones.

Remark 3.4
.

Deterministic component selection strategies, such as cyclic, provide predictable behavior and convergence guarantees that hold for any admissible selection sequence, making them particularly robust and amenable to worst-case analysis. These features may be preferable in settings where reliability and reproducibility of the iterates are of primary importance. In contrast, stochastic selection rules typically rely on convergence guarantees established in expectation or almost surely and are often attractive in large-scale problems due to their reduced per-iteration computational cost, since only one component is processed at each iteration. Moreover, stochastic schemes may exhibit favorable convergence behavior on average, especially when evaluating all components at every iteration is impractical. Overall, the choice between stochastic and deterministic component selection reflects a trade-off between robustness and predictability versus lower per-iteration cost and potential gains in average performance, in line with classical results for convex optimization (see, e.g.,
[
5
]
).

Conclusions

We contributed to the extension of splitting proximal point type algorithms beyond convexity by proving the convergence of two different algorithms: a deterministic permutation-based and a random-order variant, ensuring global convergence for both methods under standard stepsize assumptions, with almost sure convergence for the stochastic variant via supermartingale theory. A key advantage of our framework is that it relies solely on the prox-convexity of the component functions, which does not imply that the full function
f
is prox-convex itself.

Future work includes the development of accelerated and inertial variants, complexity analysis, and a randomized scheme in the spirit of the extensively studied Alternating Projections literature

,

,

,

,

,

,

]

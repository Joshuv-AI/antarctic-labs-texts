Abstract
. We prove that the Kontsevich graph complex 𝖦𝖢 d 2 \mathsf{GC}_{d}^{2} and its oriented version 𝖮𝖦𝖢 d + 1 2 \mathsf{OGC}_{d+1}^{2} are quasi-isomorphic as dg Lie algebras.



1
.
Introduction

The famous graph complex
\mathsf{GC}_{d}
was introduced in
[
K1
]
in the context
of deformation quantizations of Poisson structures. It is generated by connected graphs whose vertices are at least trivalent. The integer
d
controls the cohomological degrees assigned to graphs, and the handling of their symmetries.
The trivalence condition on Kontsevich’s original graph complex may be relaxed. In the literature the larger complexes

\mathsf{GC}_{d}\subset\mathsf{GC}_{d}^{2}\subset\mathsf{GC}^{0}_{d}

have been considered, with
\mathsf{GC}_{d}^{2}
defined such that the vertices of the graphs are required to be at least bivalent, while for
\mathsf{GC}^{0}_{d}
one drops the valence condition entirely.
All these complexes are in fact dg Lie algebras, the inclusions are compatible with the dg Lie structure, and the cohomological difference between them is controlled, see §2 below for a brief reminder.

The graph complexes above and their cohomology have seen many recent application in homological algebra and algebraic geometry. For example, they control the top weight cohomology of the moduli spaces of curves
[
CGP
]
, and also the homotopy automorphisms of the (rationalized) little
d
-disks operads
[
FW
]
, and are hence of high interest to the community.

There is also an
oriented
version
\mathsf{OGC}^{0}_{d}
proposed by the first author. This is obtained by simply replacing the plain undirected graphs in Kontsevich’s definition by directed acyclic graphs, that is, directed graphs with no closed paths of directed edges. Requiring vertices to be at least bivalent, one obtains the quasi-isomorphic dg Lie algebras

\mathsf{OGC}_{d}^{2}\subset\mathsf{OGC}_{d}^{0}.

Requiring in addition that there is at least one trivalent vertex one may define a yet smaller dg Lie subalgebra

\mathsf{OGC}_{d}^{3}\subset\mathsf{OGC}_{d}^{2}.

These oriented graph complexes have also been studied intensively in recent years. In particular, it has been proven in
[
W2
]
that there is an isomorphism of Lie algebras at the cohomology level

(1)

H^{\bullet}(\mathsf{GC}^{0}_{d})\simeq H^{\bullet}(\mathsf{OGC}^{0}_{d+1})

for any
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. (Mind the shift in
d
.) Other simplified proofs of the above isomorphism — but only at the
level of graded vector spaces (i.e. not as Lie algebras) – were given in
[
Z
]
and then in
[
M
]
.
The oriented graph complexes also feature prominently in various mathematical problems: They appear in the description of the rational homotopy type of the space of long knots in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(
n\geq 4
)
[
FTW
]
, they control the deformation theory of the PROP governing Lie bialgebras
[
MW
]
, and they appear centrally in the study of the real homotopy type of the real locus of the moduli space of genus zero curves
[
KW
]
.

The main result of this paper is an upgrade of the cohomology isomorphism (
1
) to a quasi-isomorphism on the level of dg Lie algebras. In the process we also provide yet another independent proof of the isomorphism (
1
).

1.1
.
Main Theorem

For any
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
there exists a dg Lie
algebra
\widehat{\mathsf{OGC}}_{d,d+1}
of graphs which fits into the following diagram of quasi-isomorphisms of
dg Lie algebras

\mathsf{GC}^{0}_{d}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{1}}}{{\longleftarrow}}\widehat{\mathsf{OGC}}_{d,d+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathsf{OGC}^{0}_{d+1}

There is a grading by loop order on
\widehat{\mathsf{OGC}}_{d,d+1}
and the arrows
\pi_{1}
,
\pi_{2}
respect the loop order gradings.

The intertwining dg Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathsf{OGC}}_{d,d+1}
is explicitly described in §3: it is generated by graphs with two types of vertices and two types of edges, and is equipped
with a relatively non-trivial but explicitly described differential. This dg Lie algebra controls the deformation theory of a certain morphism
of 2-coloured operads as explained in §3 of this paper. The morphisms
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}

are very simple and explicit. The theorem is proven in §4.

By extending the zigzag with the inclusions
\mathsf{GC}_{d}^{2}\subset\mathsf{GC}_{d}^{0}
and
\mathsf{OGC}_{d}^{2}\subset\mathsf{OGC}_{d}^{0}
, which are known to be quasi-isomorphisms, we also obtain a zigzag of quasi-isomorphisms of dg Lie algebras

\mathsf{GC}_{d}^{2}\xrightarrow{\sim}\bullet\xleftarrow{\sim}\mathsf{OGC}_{d+1}^{2}

connecting the bivalent versions.
It is furthermore known that the inclusions
\mathsf{GC}_{d}\subset\mathsf{GC}_{d}^{2}
and
\mathsf{OGC}_{d}^{3}\subset\mathsf{OGC}_{d}^{2}
are quasi-isomorphisms in loop orders
\geq 2
. Hence extending our zigzag and truncating to loop orders
\geq 2
we also obtain a zigzag of quasi-isomorphisms of dg Lie algebras

\mathsf{GC}_{d}\xrightarrow{\sim}\bullet\xleftarrow{\sim}\mathsf{OGC}_{d+1}^{3}

We note that we slightly deviate from the notation of the literature to obtain more consistent conventions for this paper. In particular the dg Lie algebra
\mathsf{OGC}^{2}_{d}
has been denoted
\mathsf{GC}_{d}^{or}
elsewhere.

\mathsf{OGC}_{d,d+1}^{\bullet\circ}
can be identified with the subquotient of

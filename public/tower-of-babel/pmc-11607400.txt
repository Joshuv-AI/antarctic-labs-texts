Real-time optimized inverse kinematics of redundant robots under inequality constraints
Authors: Linlin Zhang, Huibin Du, Zhiying Qin, Yuejing Zhao, Guang Yang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Inverse kinematics of redundant robots presents a challenging problem for real-time applications due to the lack of uniqueness of solution and the low computational efficiency caused by redundancy and hard limits. In this work, a general and efficient method for addressing the real-time optimized inverse kinematics of redundant robots is proposed, taking into account hard limits in joint and Cartesian space that can never be violated. The proposed method proceeds by using constrained linear programming instead of quadratic programming to solve the inverse kinematics problem. Various hard limits such as joint range, bounds on velocity and acceleration are handled explicitly as inequality constraints. This method resolves the redundancy in real-time and enable to simultaneously guarantee that the additional motion constraints will never be violated. Its performance allows real-time kinematic control of redundant robots executing sensor-driven online tasks. The effectiveness of this method is demonstrated through simulations and experiments conducted on a 7-DOF KUKA IIWA robot, showcasing its ability to control redundant robots executing sensor-driven tasks in dynamic environments with numerous hard limits.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-81174-8.

Introduction

Robotic manipulator with redundant degree of freedoms (DOFs) are being used in dynamic environment, or even cooperating closely with human operators. Such application requires the robot adjusting its configuration in real time in order to avoid any collisions and keep safe with the human operator, the handing objects, as well as the dynamic environment. Generally, the pseudoinverse or transpose of the Jacobian matrix is used to solve the inverse kinematics (IK) problem for the transfer of planned trajectories in Cartesian space to the configuration space where actuators are controlled1. However, solving the IK problem for redundant robots such as KUKA LBR IIWA is not trivial and constitutes an intensively investigated research area in the field of mechanical sciences2,3.

Besides the geometric methods4–8 and the intelligent algorithms9–11, the IK problem is generally considered as an optimization problem3 to benefit the advantages of redundant DOFs while avoiding the constraints on each of the joint actuators, i.e., the angular position limit, maximum velocity and acceleration. However, the optimization in real time presents challenges due to the computation complexity of the IK problem under the actuator constraints.

The Gradient Projection Methods (GPM)12 minimized the deviations from the joints middle position of their feasible regions by projecting the gradient of a quadratic cost function of the joint limits into the null space of the task Jacobian, which could be considered as an unconstrained quadratic programming (QP) problem. The idea has been widely used in many studies for various subtasks, including the minimization of joint torque energy13, avoidance of singularity14, avoidance of joint limits15, improvement of dexterity16, and avoidance of collisions17. The Damping Least Square (DLS) method18,19, on the other hand, tried to avoid singularity by using an instantaneous trade-off between the accuracy and feasibility of the inverse kinematic solution to prevent the joint velocities exceeding the limits20. The Weighted Least Norm (WLN), Weighted Least Squares (WLS), and Varied Weights (VW) methods optimized the inverse kinematics with joint limits avoidance by setting a large weighting factor to the joint which is close to its limits21–23. In these algorithms the unconstrained QP problem were constructed by converting the hard limits into the objective functions to be optimized. Detailed analysis of these methods and experimental comparisons were introduced in23.

The unconstrained QP problem or the QP problem with only equality constraints is analytically solvable using the Lagrange multipliers. But the joints hard limits cannot be guaranteed, so the much more complicated QP problem with inequality constraints should be applied. In24, a QP form was proposed for the general formulation of the constrained kinematic redundancy problem. The joint rate bounds, joint acceleration constraints, and joint angle limits are considered in the inequality constraints. A generalized task priority framework, including inequality tasks, was proposed in25. All tasks, including motion limitations, were implemented as a sequence of QP problems sorted in priority and solved in the solution set of higher priority tasks. In26, a comparison between the Kinematics and Dynamics Library (KDL)27 and a set of Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) IK algorithms was provided, and a TRAC-IK solver that runs the best performing SQP algorithm was also implemented.

Human legs and quadruped robots can be considered as typical redundant robots. Sun investigated the fundamental properties of the human leg during walking, including both the linear and nonlinear elasticities28. The findings provide insights into the fundamental linear and nonlinear properties of the human leg during locomotion, which can inform the stability analysis and motion prediction of robotics and rehabilitation devices. A method for estimating the leg state of a quadruped robot using a probabilistic model and proprioceptive feedback was proposed in29. The probabilistic model is a framework for representing and reasoning uncertainty in various domains, including information retrieval, theory formation, cognition, language processing, and programming. In30, a globally optimal IK solver using mixed-integer convex optimization was presented. The computation times are roughly in units of seconds. LittleDog with four legs standing on different sets of stepping stones was studied. Computation time is about 15 seconds for this 18-DOF quadruped robot.

The sparrow search algorithm (SSA) can be used to obtain the solution of the optimization problems31. The SSA metaheuristic algorithm has considerable advantages for multidimensional parameter optimization. Unlike the SQP, specifying the initial value of SSA is not necessary. It also presents that different initial values for the SQP produce different optimization results. In addition, the same initial value used in performing multiple calculations still yields some differences in the results. The SSA can obtain better objective function extremes for the same problem. However, it sacrifices a lot of the calculation time. So, it is not suitable for the online tasks in real-time.

Various methods, such as genetic algorithms (GA), particle swarm optimization (PSO), ant colony optimization (ACO), and simulated annealing (SA), can be used to solve the multi-objective optimization problem. But these methods are very time-consuming and not suitable for the online job. To overcome this, the vision-based approach can be used to detect deviations caused by assembly errors or moving and modify the path curve online32. However, this is not enough when the body of the robots needs to avoid dynamic obstacles cooperating closely with human operators.

The QP problem with inequality constraints is not well-suited for real-time kinematic control due to the significant amount of computational resources required. To address this issue, the Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization method was introduced in24 to improve computational efficiency. Additionally, a compact QP algorithm using Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting was developed in33 to reduce computational complexity. In34, a complete solution was proposed to solve multiple least-squares quadratic problems with both equality and inequality constraints ordered into a strict hierarchy. Instead of the commonly used singular value decomposition (SVD) in the active set method, the proposed method employs a hierarchical active-set search method based on Hierarchical Complete Orthogonal Decomposition (HCOD) to reduce computational burden. For more information on hierarchical optimization problems and related work, please refer to35–37. The Saturation in Null Space (SNS) method is proposed in38,39 to further reduce computational burden using heuristic ideas. This method also utilizes the rank-one update of the pseudoinverse algorithm40. The scaling factor is enabled and optimized only when the basic pseudoinverse solution violates the constraints in SNS. The event-triggered controller utilizing the similar idea that the controller is updated only when the system states deviate from prescribed threshold values is proposed in41. This idea helps overcome the limitations of traditional time-triggered control design mechanisms.

A more radical approach named QPIK-S was proposed in3. Its goal function is a weighted sum of multiple objectives: fulfillment of the secondary tasks in the manipulator’s null space and minimization of the trajectory scaling. One of the differences between QPIK-S and the SNS method is that the scaling factor is enabled and optimized only when the basic pseudoinverse solution violates the constraints in SNS, whereas the QP problem needs to be solved every IK step. The average computation time in each step is 0.612 ms for QPIK-S, a little longer than that for SNS. Currently, the SNS method is one of the fastest QP-like methods for the inverse kinematics (IK) of redundant robots, ensuring that all motion constraints are satisfied.

In summary, for the reviewed state-of-the-art methods, QP problems were solved to obtain the IK solutions of the redundant robot. The optimization items in the objective functions are 2-norm, which makes solving the IK problem in real-time very difficult. Nevertheless, for sensor-driven tasks in dynamic environments, it is more important to guarantee joint hard limits and/or collision avoidance with the highest priority, followed by kinematic control in real-time. Therefore, the priority order should be hard-limits > collision-avoidance > real-time > objective-function.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section II briefly reviews the background of the IK problem for redundant robots, including modeling and kinematic control techniques. In section III, a number of practical inequality constraints, such as joint limits, constraints on joint velocity and acceleration, and obstacle-avoidance strategies, are introduced. Section IV proposes a unified LP framework for resolving the IK problem under various inequality constraints. This method enables real-time access to the optimized IK of redundant robots subject to inequality constraints. Section V includes simulations to evaluate the performance of the proposed method, which are contrasted with the SNS method, one of the most efficient QP-like techniques. The effectiveness of the method is validated through experiments in Section VI using the KUKA IIWA robot. Lastly, Section VII brings this paper to a close.

Constraints

The key problem in the task differential kinematic control is how to decide the joint velocity vector in the null space of the task Jacobian to resolve the redundancy and enable to simultaneously satisfy additional motion constraints for various tasks, e.g., collision avoidance.

Joint physical constraints

The most essential constraints are the robot motion capabilities, in other words, joint physical constraints including joint limits and/or bounds on joint velocity and acceleration. Disregarding these joint physical constraints, command saturation will actually deform the execution of tasks39. Uncompensated saturations of the velocity commands may perturb the geometry of the task under execution in a dangerous way, e.g., leading to physical damage of the actuator.

Cartesian constraint

In general, the obstacle avoidance consists in identifying the points on the manipulator, which are near obstacles and then assign to them a motion component that moves those points away from the obstacle42. Adaptive control enables robots to adjust their path planning and navigation strategies in real-time based on environmental changes43. However, recalculating the desired motion of the end-effector without fully utilizing the null space is deemed unacceptable.

For a point \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\boldsymbol{P}} \in {\text{R}}^{3}$$\end{document} on the manipulator, the Jacobian matrix of this point is \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\boldsymbol{J}}_{P}$$\end{document}. Suppose that the closest distance at the present control cycle between the obstacles and the point \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\boldsymbol{P}}$$\end{document} is \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${||}{\boldsymbol{d}}|| \ge D$$\end{document}, where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\boldsymbol{d}} \in {\text{R}}^{3}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$D$$\end{document} is the safe distance threshold. The velocity of point \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\boldsymbol{P}}$$\end{document} is defined as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\boldsymbol{V}}_{P} { = }{\boldsymbol{J}}_{P} {\dot{\boldsymbol{\theta }}}$$\end{document}. In order to guarantee that the distance is still larger than the safe distance in the next control cycle, the velocity of point \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\boldsymbol{P}}$$\end{document} in the direction of the point to the obstacle should satisfy the following inequality:22\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\frac{{{\boldsymbol{d}}^{T} {\boldsymbol{V}}_{P} }}{{\left\| {\boldsymbol{d}} \right\|}} \le \frac{{\left\| {\boldsymbol{d}} \right\| - D}}{\Delta t}$$\end{document}

This inequality (22) can be taken as the Cartesian constraint for the obstacles avoiding in the inverse kinematics of the redundant robots. Of course, other Cartesian constraints which can be expressed as linear equalities or inequalities can be taken into consideration.

Task constraints

Usually, it is expected that the motion of the end-effector is not disturbed by any joint physical constraints or obstacles. Under this circumstance, the task constraint remains an equality constraint as:23\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\boldsymbol{J\dot{\theta }}} = {\dot{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{c}$$\end{document}

However, it is possible that the task of the end-effector of the redundant robot cannot be accomplished without violating joint physical constraints, even when fully utilizing the null space. The odds are particularly high for sensor-driven robotic tasks in dynamic environments where high task velocity is required. Consequently, for safety reasons, task execution must be interrupted. Recalculating the desired motion of the end-effector through higher-level path planning is undesirable due to its time-consuming nature. Furthermore, as previously mentioned, command saturation or control algorithms that deform task execution are unacceptable.

The time-scaling techniques ensure the joint physical constraints and the tracking accuracy by properly slowing down the end-effector trajectory when the joint physical constraints are violated3. So, the task constraint becomes to be:24\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\boldsymbol{J\dot{\theta }}} = s{\dot{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{c}$$\end{document}where, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$s$$\end{document} is the scaling factor of the task, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0 \le s \le 1$$\end{document}.

The sampling time is updated as following:25\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$t_{k + 1} = t_{k} + s\Delta t$$\end{document}

Or, the task constraint can be ensured by the normal direction of the velocity of the end-effector:26\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} & \left( {{\boldsymbol{J\dot{\theta }}}} \right) \times {\dot{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{c} = 0 \\ & s = \frac{{\left( {{\boldsymbol{J\dot{\theta }}}} \right) \cdot {\dot{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{c} }}{{\left\| {{\dot{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{c} } \right\|}} \\ \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where, “\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\cdot$$\end{document}” and “\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\times$$\end{document}” are dot product and cross product, respectively.

Task constraint (24) and (26) are equivalent to each other. They ensure that the end-effector remains on the geometric path of the task. Nevertheless, maintaining the end-effector on the task’s geometric path may be impossible when certain joints cease movement upon reaching their limits. In such instances, the redundant robot loses degrees of freedom and cannot progress along the task trajectory. Two strategies can be employed: moving backward on the task trajectory when path tracking accuracy is critical, or deviating from the task trajectory by minimizing the angle between the end-effector velocity and the task. The first strategy requires only relaxing the non-negative constraint of the scaling factor to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$s \le 1$$\end{document} instead of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0 \le s \le 1$$\end{document}. For the second strategy, the equality constraint should be incorporated as one of the optimization items in the objective function:27\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\min \;\;\omega_{1} \left( { - s} \right) + \omega_{2} \left( {{\boldsymbol{J\dot{\theta }}}} \right) \times {\dot{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{c}$$\end{document}where, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\omega_{1}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\omega_{2}$$\end{document} are the positive weights. Details about using Formula (27) as the objective function will be discussed in the Section IV, B.

Framework of linear programming

In this section, a LP (Linear Programming) framework will be present. This framework not only can take full advantage of the redundancy of the robot to get an optimal solution, but also guarantee that all the motion constraints are satisfied. Less number of searches makes the computation burden of LP lesser than of QP (Quadratic Programming). Meanwhile, at most once computation of pseudoinverse of Jacobian matrix is needed in LP framework, reduced a great deal of computational complexity than QP.

Conclusion

A novel, computationally efficient method for the real-time optimized inverse kinematics of redundant robots under various inequality constraints is proposed.

Using LP instead of QP, this method addresses to the time-local optimized solution of the inverse kinematics of redundant robots in real-time with guarantee that hard limits on joint space motion can never be violated. Any other linear equality or inequality constraints can be handled explicitly as well as the joint hard limits.

Simulations and experiments show that: 1) the new method can deal with the joint motion capability constraints and the obstacle avoiding constraints very well, especially the velocity discontinuity problem suffered by the SNS method; 2) the new method’s computational efficiency of is at least not worse than the SNS method which is the most efficient QP-like algorithm; 3) for joint motion optimization, although the result of LP is not the same as QP, it is close enough. And it is practically right to get a less-optimal solution with respect to the QP considering the very large amount computation time reduced in order to solve the redundancy in real-time.

Growth conditions for freeness of the Furstenberg boundary action
Nazmul Alam, Joseph Gondek, Mehrdad Kalantar, Randy Pham

Abstract
. Given a finitely generated group Γ \Gamma and g ∈ Γ g\in\Gamma , we prove sufficient conditions in terms of various growth/decay functions for freeness of the action of g g on the Furstenberg boundary of Γ \Gamma . In this context, we also give a description of the support of stationary states on the reduced C ∗ \textup{C}^{*} -algebra of Γ \Gamma .

The dynamics of boundary actions of discrete groups encode important information about the various structural properties of
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebras they generate. Admitting free boundary actions implies simplicity of the reduced
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra

\Gamma

[

; and, if
\Gamma
admits free extreme boundary actions, then its reduced
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra has strict comparison
[

We recall from
[

that a compact
\Gamma

-boundary (in the sense of Furstenberg) if for every probability measure
\nu\in\operatorname{Prob}(X)
and every

of elements

such that
g_{i}\nu\to\delta_{x}
in the weak* topology in
\operatorname{Prob}(X)
.
Every group
\Gamma
has a unique (up to
\Gamma
-equivariant homeomorphisms) universal
\Gamma
-boundary
\partial_{F}\Gamma
, in the sense that every
\Gamma
-boundary

is a continuous
\Gamma
-equivariant image of
\partial_{F}\Gamma
(
[
4
]
*Proposition 4.6). This universal boundary

\Gamma

There are several (indirect) sufficient conditions to ensure freeness of the action
\Gamma\curvearrowright\partial_{F}\Gamma
for a given group
\Gamma
(e.g. lack of amenable normalish subgroups
[
1
]
or non-trivial amenable URS
[
11
]
, existence of continuous actions with non-amenable rigid stabilizers
[

]

In this paper, we address this problem rather locally. We give sufficient conditions for the freeness of the action of a single given element
g\in\Gamma
on
\partial_{F}\Gamma
; that is, to determine when the fixed-point set
{\rm Fix}(g)=\{x\in\partial_{F}\Gamma:gx=x\}
is empty.

Our conditions are based on comparison of various growth/decay rates.

Definition 1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group, with a fixed symmetric generating set.
For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, define

\rho(n)=\max\left\{\frac{\|\lambda(f)\|}{\|f\|_{2}}:0\neq f\in\mathbb{C}\Gamma,\,{\rm supp}(f)\subseteq B_{n}(\Gamma)\right\}.

Given a subset
A\subseteq\Gamma
, we denote its growth function by

\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle A}(n)=|A\cap B_{n}(\Gamma)|,

and given a subgroup
H\leq\Gamma
, we denote its
co-growth
function by

\beta_{\scriptscriptstyle H}(n)=|B_{n}(G/H)|,

where
B_{n}(G/H)
is the ball of radius
n
around the trivial coset in the Schreier graph
G/H
.

The group
\Gamma
is said to have the
rapid decay property
(RD) if
\rho(n)
grows polynomially
[
7
]
.
We say
\Gamma
has
semi-rapid decay
(abbreviated SRD) if
\rho(n)
grows subexponentially.
Any group with intermediate growth has SRD but not RD, and the direct product of an SRD group and an RD group is SRD (
[
10
]
*Lemma 1).

Our first main result is a sufficient condition for freeness of the action of an element
g\in\Gamma
in terms of the above rates. Given functions
\phi,\psi:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we write
\phi=o(\psi)
if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\phi(n)/\psi(n)=0
and we write
\phi=\underline{o}(\psi)
if
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\phi(n)/\psi(n)=0
.

.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group, let
g\in\Gamma
, and denote by

the centralizer of
g
in
\Gamma
. If

(
\star
)

\lim_{n}\rho(3n)^{2}\,\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle C\,\cap\,\gamma C\gamma^{-1}}(2n)/\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle C}(n)=0

for all
\gamma\notin C

then

Applying the theorem to groups with (semi-)rapid decay, we get

Corollary A

Let
\Gamma

A general sufficient condition for freeness of the action of

on
\partial_{F}\Gamma

\mu\in\operatorname{Prob}(\Gamma)

\mu
-stationary state
\tau

\textup{C}^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)
,
\tau(\lambda_{g})=0
. The notion of stationary states was studied in
[

-simplicity in terms of unique stationarity was given: namely, it was proven that a group

In contrast to the case of traces, there is no general (purely group theoretic) description of stationary states in terms of their support.

Our next result gives such a description in terms of the random walks on Schreier graphs with respect to centralizers of elements of

Theorem B

Let
\Gamma

Again, we record the conclusion in the case of groups with the (semi-)rapid decay property.

Corollary B

Let
\Gamma

(

.

Finally, note that, in view of

\textup{C}^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)

The problem of determining the support of traces

Recall that the

In

,
and used that to give a complete description of traces on the reduced

-algebras of hyperbolic groups. That, however, follows from results of
[

In the following lemma, we record a more general version of

Lemma 2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group. If
g\in\Gamma
is such that

\rho^{2}=\underline{o}(\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\textup{Cl}(g)})
, then
\tau(\lambda_{g})=0
for every trace

on
\textup{C}^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

Set
A_{n}=B_{n}(\Gamma)\cap\text{Cl}(g)
, and

.
Let
\tau
be a trace on
\textup{C}^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)

\begin{split}|\tau(g)||A_{n}|&=\big|\sum_{h\in A_{n}}\tau(g)\big|=|\tau(z_{n})|\leq\|z_{n}\|\\
&\leq\rho(n)\|z_{n}\|_{2}=\rho(n)|A_{n}|^{1/2},\end{split}

∎

If

(

Proposition 4

The map

\Gamma

Also recall that, given

The

.

If

(note that

.

Furthermore, let

. (Note that the growth condition (

By (

Proof of Theorem
B
.

Let
\mu\in{\rm Prob}(\Gamma)
be finitely supported and symmetric. Assume
\rho={o}(\kappa^{-1/4})
, where

is the probability of the return to the origin after

\mu

Let

Let
\varepsilon>0
, and choose

such that
\rho(n)\kappa_{n}^{1/4}<\varepsilon
for all

.
Choose
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
{\rm supp}(\mu)\subset B_{N}(\Gamma)
, and
let
\tau
be a
\mu
-stationary state on
\textup{C}^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)
. Then, for

, we have

\displaystyle|\tau(\lambda_{g})|

\displaystyle=\big|\tau\big(\sum_{t\in\Gamma}\mu^{*n}(t)\lambda_{tgt^{-1}}\big)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\sum_{t\in\Gamma}\mu^{*n}(t)\lambda_{tgt^{-1}}\big\|

\displaystyle\leq\rho\big(2nN+|g|\big)\,\big\|\sum_{t\in\Gamma}\mu^{*n}(t)\delta_{tgt^{-1}}\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\rho\big(2nN+|g|\big)\,\big\|q_{*}(\mu^{*n})\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\rho\big(2nN+|g|\big)\,\big\|q_{*}(\mu^{*n})\big\|_{\infty}^{1/2}.

Since the
\mu

\Gamma/C_{\Gamma}(h)
is symmetric, the probability

of stepping into the coset

after

Hence, combined with the above we get the estimates

\begin{split}|\tau(\lambda_{g})|&\leq\rho\big(2nN+|g|\big)\,\big\|q_{*}(\mu^{*n})\big\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\\
&\leq\rho\big(2nN+|g|\big)\,\kappa_{n}^{1/4}\,<\,\varepsilon.\end{split}

It follows that

4.
Amenable radical and growth of conjugacy classes

As mentioned in previous sections,
[
5
]
*Lemma 2.8 (and more generally, Lemma
2
),
describes the support of traces on
\textup{C}^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)
in terms of the growth of conjugacy classes,
for
\Gamma
with (semi-)rapid decay. On the other hand,
[
1
]
*Theorem 4.1 describes the support of traces on
\textup{C}^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)
, for a general discrete group
\Gamma
, in terms of the amenable radical.
These facts raise the question of the relation between elements with “slow growing” conjugacy classes, and the amenable radical.

In this section, we investigate this, and in particular, we show that the above approaches are equivalent in the case of groups with (semi-)rapid decay.

Definition 6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group endowed with the word length. Define

\textup{PC}(\Gamma)=\{g\in\Gamma:\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm Cl}(g)}\penalty\ \text{ grows polynomially}\},

and

\textup{SEC}(\Gamma)=\{g\in\Gamma:\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm Cl}(g)}\penalty\ \text{ grows subexponentially}\}.

We say
\Gamma
is a PC-group (respectively, an SEC-group),
if
\Gamma=\textup{PC}(\Gamma)
(respectively,
\Gamma=\textup{SEC}(\Gamma)
).

Proposition 7
.

The subgroups
\textup{PC}(\Gamma)\subseteq\textup{SEC}(\Gamma)
are independent of the choice of the generating set defining the word length. Both subgroups are characteristic (hence, normal) in
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Recall that if
S
and
T
are finite generating sets for
\Gamma
, then there is a constant
C>0
such that
C^{-1}|g|_{S}\leq|g|_{T}\leq C|g|_{S}
, for all
g\in\Gamma
. Now the first claim amounts to the observation that a function
n\mapsto f(n)
has polynomial (resp. subexponential) growth if and only if
n\mapsto f(Cn)
has polynomial (resp. subexponential) growth. The second claim follows from the observation that if
\varphi\in\text{Aut}(\Gamma)
, then
S\subset\Gamma
generates
\Gamma
if and only if
\varphi(S)
does.
∎

Proposition 8
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group.

(i)

Let
h\in\Gamma
. If

\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm Cl}(h)}
grows subexponentially
(
respectively, polynomially
)
, then so does
\beta_{\scriptscriptstyle C_{\Gamma}(h)}
.

(ii)

Denote by
Z(\Gamma)
the center of
\Gamma
.
If
\Gamma
is a SEC-group
(
respectively, PC-group
)
, then the quotient group
\Gamma/Z(\Gamma)
has subexponential
(
respectively, polynomial
)
growth.

Proof.

(i) Denote
H=C_{\Gamma}(h)
, and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, set
\bar{B}_{n}=\{gH\in\Gamma/H:|gH|\leq n\}
, and
B_{n}=\{g\in\Gamma:|g|\leq n\}
.
The map
\Phi_{n}:\bar{B}_{n}\to\text{Cl}(h)\cap B_{2n+1}
given by

\Phi_{n}(gH)=g^{-1}hg
is a well-defined injection for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and hence

|\bar{B}_{n}|\leq|\text{Cl}(h)\cap B_{2n+1}|,

which implies the assertions in the first item.

(ii) Let
S\subset\Gamma
be a finite generating set.
We use the same notation as in the above case for
H=Z(\Gamma)
, and define
the maps
\Psi_{n}:\bar{B}_{n}\to\prod_{s\in S}\text{Cl}(s)\cap B_{2n+1}
by

\Psi_{n}(gH)=(g^{-1}sg)_{s\in S}.

Then,
\Psi_{n}

is a well-defined injection for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and hence

|\bar{B}_{n}|\leq\prod_{s\in S}|\text{Cl}(s)\cap B_{2n+1}|,

which implies that if
\Gamma
is a SEC-group (respectively, PC-group), then
\Gamma/Z(\Gamma)
is of subexponential (respectively, polynomial) growth.
∎

Note that part (ii) of Proposition
8
implies that every SEC-group (and hence every PC-group) is amenable.
More generally, we have the following.

Proposition 9
.

If
\Gamma
is a finitely generated group, then

\textup{SEC}(\Gamma)
is amenable. In particular,
\textup{SEC}(\Gamma)\leq\textup{Rad}(\Gamma).

Proof.

We show that every finitely generated subgroup of
\textup{SEC}(\Gamma)
is an SEC-group, hence amenable by Proposition
8
. It then follows that
\textup{SEC}(\Gamma)
is amenable.

So, let
T\subseteq\textup{SEC}(\Gamma)
be finite, and set
H=\langle T\rangle
.
For every
h\in H
we have

B_{n}^{T}\cap\text{Cl}_{H}(h)\subseteq B_{Mn}^{S}\cap\text{Cl}_{\Gamma}(h),

where
\text{Cl}_{H}(h)
and
\text{Cl}_{H}(h)
are the conjugacy classes of
h
in
H
and
\Gamma
, respectively, and
M=\max\limits_{t\in T}|t|_{S}
. Thus,

H
is an SEC-group with respect to
T
, as desired.
∎

Corollary 10
.

If
\Gamma
has
SRD
, then
\textup{SEC}(\Gamma)=\textup{Rad}(\Gamma)
.
If
\Gamma
has
RD
, then
\textup{PC}(\Gamma)=\textup{SEC}(\Gamma)=\textup{Rad}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
9
we have
\textup{PC}(\Gamma)\leq\textup{SEC}(\Gamma)\leq\textup{Rad}(\Gamma)
.

Conversely, since
\textup{Rad}(\Gamma)
is amenable, its characteristic function extends to a continuous trace on
\textup{C}^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)
. Thus, Lemma
2
implies that if
\Gamma
has SRD, then
\textup{SEC}(\Gamma)=\textup{Rad}(\Gamma)
, and if
\Gamma
has RD, then
\textup{PC}(\Gamma)=\textup{Rad}(\Gamma)
.
∎

Example 11
.

(i) The group
\Gamma={\rm SL}_{3}({\mathbb{Z}})
does not have SRD, but since its amenable radical is trivial, we have
\textup{PC}(\Gamma)=\textup{SEC}(\Gamma)=\textup{Rad}(\Gamma)
.

(ii) If
\Gamma
is an amenable group of exponential growth with trivial center, then
\Gamma
is not an SEC-group, thus

\textup{SEC}(\Gamma)\neq\textup{Rad}(\Gamma)=\Gamma
.

(iii) If
\Gamma
is of intermediate growth with trivial center (e.g. the Grigorchuk group), then
\Gamma
is an SEC-group but not PC-group, and therefore we have

\textup{PC}(\Gamma)\neq\textup{SEC}(\Gamma)=\Gamma
.

Sketch of the resolution of the axial U(1) problem without chiral anomaly
Nodoka Yamanaka 1

Abstract
We propose a mechanism which explains the masses of η \eta and η ′ \eta^{\prime} mesons without invoking the explicit violation of U ​ ( 1 ) A U(1)_{A} symmetry by the chiral anomaly. It is shown that the U(1) problem, the problem for which the prediction of η \eta and η ′ \eta^{\prime} masses in the simple chiral perturbation theory largely deviates from the experimental values, is actually resolved by considering the first order contribution of the disconnected meson correlator with respect to the quark mass. The bound of Weinberg m η 2 ≤ 3 ​ m π 2 m_{\eta}^{2}\leq 3m_{\pi}^{2} is fulfilled by considering the negative squared mass of η \eta or η ′ \eta^{\prime} which is just the saddle point of the QCD effective potential, and 20% level agreements with experimental data are obtained by just fitting one low energy constant. We provide the leading chiral Lagrangian due to the disconnected contribution in 3-flavor QCD, and also discuss the 2- and 4-flavor cases as well as the consistency of our mechanism with the chiral restoration at high temperature found in lattice calculations.

What is then the mechanism resolving the U(1) problem?
If there is no explicit

In this work, we elaborate such a mechanism and show its consistency with selected, but the most important related facts including experiments.

The negative squared NG boson mass just means that there is another true minimum in the opposite side of the saddle point.

Let us finally comment on the finite temperature transition of QCD.
At finite temperature, the effective thermal masses of quarks and gluons adds to the QCD potential.
This will then just increase the curvature of the potential near the origin in Fig.
3

Therefore
U(1)_{A}
is, like other symmetries, restored at high temperature, as suggested by recent lattice results

;
Yamanaka:2022bfj

Since the QCD potential is tilted due to the non-vanishing quark masses, its absolute minimum will never reach the origin, so the QCD transition at finite temperature is a crossover.

Our analysis suggests that

\eta^{\prime}
also obey “normally” the chiral perturbation theory, and that the expansion in terms of the strange quark mass is not bad.
We could also have a hint via the diagrammatic analysis that the NG bosons are just bound states of “constituent quarks” dressed by gluons and that their leading dynamical mass effect was removed by the binding energy due to the confining potential.
This picture fully agrees with the quark model.
To consolidate our scenarios, the dynamics of processes involving
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
must also be analyzed with the fitted chiral Lagrangian (
24
).
This is left as future works.

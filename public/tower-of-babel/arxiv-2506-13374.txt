On pure monomorphisms and pure epimorphisms in accessible categories
Leonid Positselski

Abstract
. In all κ \kappa -accessible additive categories, κ \kappa -pure monomorphisms and κ \kappa -pure epimorphisms are well-behaved, as shown in our previous paper [ 25 ] . This is known to be not always true in κ \kappa -accessible nonadditive categories. Nevertheless, mild assumptions on a κ \kappa -accessible category are sufficient to prove good properties of κ \kappa -pure monomorphisms and κ \kappa -pure epimorphisms. In particular, in a κ \kappa -accessible category with finite products, all κ \kappa -pure monomorphisms are κ \kappa -directed colimits of split monomorphisms, while in a κ \kappa -accessible category with finite coproducts, all κ \kappa -pure epimorphisms are κ \kappa -directed colimits of split epimorphisms. We also discuss what we call Quillen exact classes of monomorphisms and epimorphisms, generalizing the additive concept of one-sided exact category.



Introduction

The concept of
\kappa
-purity is an important technical tool in
locally presentable and accessible category
theory
[
2
, Section 2.D]
.
The properties of
\kappa
-pure monomorphisms and
\kappa
-pure
epimorphisms have been studied by Adámek, Rosický, and collaborators
in the papers
[
1
,
3
,
4
]
, which offer an assortment of theorems
and counterexamples (see also the paper by Hu and Pelletier
[
18
]

and the recent prepint of Kanalas
[
19
]
).
However, the basic results about
\kappa
-purity in

\kappa
-accessible categories seem to only have been proved under
assumptions that are somewhat restrictive.
In this paper, we explain how to relax some assumptions.

Let us list what we consider the basic results.
In any
\kappa
-accessible category with pushouts, all
\kappa
-pure
monomorphisms are
\kappa
-directed colimits of split
monomorphisms
[
2
, Corollary and Remark 2.30]
.
In any
\kappa
-accessible category with pushouts and powers of
objects, all
\kappa
-pure monomorphisms are regular
monomorphisms
[
2
, Proposition and Remark 2.31]
.
In any
\kappa
-accessible category with pushouts, the class of

\kappa
-pure monomorphisms is stable under
pushouts
[
3
, Proposition 15(i)]
.

In any
\kappa
-accessible category with pullbacks, all

\kappa
-pure epimorphisms are
\kappa
-directed colimits of split
epimorphisms
[
3
, Proposition 3]
.
In any
\kappa
-accessible category with pullbacks, all

\kappa
-pure epimorphisms are regular
epimorphisms
[
3
, Proposition 4(b)]
.
In any locally
\kappa
-presentable category, the class of

\kappa
-pure epimorphisms is stable under
pullbacks
[
3
, Proposition 15(ii)]
.

In a slightly different setting of the paper
[
4
]
, some results
similar to the above ones are stated under milder assumptions.
In particular, according to
[
4
, Lemma 2.2]
, existence of
weak pushouts in the full subcategory of finitely presentable objects
is sufficient for the pure monomorphisms in a finitely accessible
category to be directed colimits of split monomorphisms.
By
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]
, existence of weak pullbacks in the full
subcategory of finitely presentable objects is sufficient for the pure
epimorphisms in a finitely accessible category to be directed colimits
of split epimorphisms.

For comparison, in any
\kappa
-accessible additive category
\mathsf{A}
,
all
\kappa
-pure monomorphisms are
\kappa
-directed colimits of
split monomorphisms, and all
\kappa
-pure epimorphisms are

\kappa
-directed colimits of split epimorphisms.
All
\kappa
-pure monomorphisms in
\mathsf{A}
are regular monomorphisms,
and all
\kappa
-pure epimorphisms are regular epimorphisms.
All pushouts of
\kappa
-pure monomorphisms always exist in
\mathsf{A}
,
and the class of
\kappa
-pure monomorphisms is stable under
pushouts.
All pullbacks of
\kappa
-pure epimorphisms exist in
\mathsf{A}
, and
the class of
\kappa
-pure epimorphisms is stable under pullbacks.

Moreover, in any
\kappa
-accessible additive category, all

\kappa
-pure epimorphisms have kernels, and all
\kappa
-pure
monomorphisms have cokernels.
The
\kappa
-pure monomorphisms are precisely the kernels of
the
\kappa
-pure epimorphisms, and the
\kappa
-pure epimorphisms
are precisely the cokernels of the
\kappa
-pure monomorphisms
(this is a generalization of
[
3
, Proposition 5]
).
All results mentioned in this and the previous paragraph follow from
the exposition in
[
25
, Section 4]
, particularly from
the existence of the
\kappa
-pure exact structure (in the sense
of Quillen) together with
[
25
, Propositions 4.2 and 4.4]
.

From our perspective, even such assumptions as existence of weak
pushouts and weak pullbacks are too restrictive, and unnecessarily so,
for the purity theory.
In particular, an additive category
\mathsf{A}

need not
have weak
pushouts or weak pullbacks.
For example, the existence of weak pullbacks in the category

\mathsf{A}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{inj}}
of injective left modules over a ring
R
is
equivalent to the existence of injective precovers of all left

R
-modules (in the sense of the paper
[
14
]
).
By
[
14
, Propositions 2.1 and 2.2]
, injective precovers exist
in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
if and only if
R
is left Noetherian.
Dually, the existence of weak pushouts in the category

\mathsf{A}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{proj}}
of projective left
R
-modules is equivalent to
the existence of projective preenvelopes of all left
R
-modules.
By the argument of
[
14
, proofs of Propositions 2.1 and 5.1]
,
the latter condition implies that the infinite direct products of
projective left
R
-modules are projective, which does not hold for
most rings
R
(cf.
[
8
, Theorem P]
,
[
11
, Theorem 3.3]
).

The aim of this paper is to spell out reasonable conditions on
a
\kappa
-accessible category
\mathsf{A}
that (1) hold for all

\kappa
-accessible additive categories, and (2) imply good properties
of
\kappa
-pure monomorphisms and
\kappa
-pure epimorphisms.
The reader will see that the resulting conditions are indeed quite
mild.

Let us emphasize that
some
assumptions are certainly necessary
for the purity theory in nonadditive categories.
In particular,
[
4
, Example 2.5]
provides an example of
a finitely accessible category with a pure monomorphism that is

not
a directed colimit of split monomorphisms and
not

a regular monomorphism.
In Examples
13.4

and
15.4
, we present
an essentially trivial example of an accessible preadditive (but not
additive!) category in which all monomorphisms and epimorphisms are
split, but some pushouts of monomorphisms and some pullbacks of
epimorphisms do not exist.

In the context of a
\kappa
-accessible category
\mathsf{A}
, we use
the terminology
strongly
\kappa
-pure monomorphisms
for
the morphisms in
\mathsf{A}
that can be obtained as
\kappa
-directed
colimits of split monomorphisms of
\kappa
-presentable objects
in
\mathsf{A}
.
Similarly, the
strongly
\kappa
-pure epimorphisms
are
the
\kappa
-directed colimits of split epimorphisms between

\kappa
-presentable objects.
We start with establishing very mild sufficient conditions for all

\kappa
-pure monomorphisms and
\kappa
-pure epimorphisms to be
strongly
\kappa
-pure.
Then we proceed to provide further, also mild sufficient conditions for
strongly
\kappa
-pure mono/epimorphisms to be regular and preserved
by pushouts/pullbacks.

As a generalization of
\kappa
-pure monomorphisms and
\kappa
-pure
epimorphisms, we discuss what we call
QE-mono
and
QE-epi

classes of morphisms (where QE means “Quillen exact”).
These are nonadditive generalizations of
right exact
and

left exact
categories inroduced by Rump
[
29
, Definition 4
in Section 5]
and studied by Bazzoni and Crivei
[
9
]
.
In the terminology of Henrard and van Roosmalen
[
16
]
,
the latter (additive categories with additional structure) are called

inflation-exact
and
deflation-exact
categories.
See Rosenberg’s preprints
[
27
, Section 1.1]
,

[
28
, Chapter I]
for prior art in the context of nonadditive
categories.

Given a
\kappa
-accessible category
\mathsf{A}
with the full subcategory
of
\kappa
-presentable objects
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}\subset\mathsf{A}
, and given
a QE-mono class
\mathcal{M}
or QE-epi class
\mathcal{P}
in
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
, we
prove that the class of all
\kappa
-directed colimits of morphisms
from
\mathcal{M}
(respectively, from
\mathcal{P}
) is a QE-mono (resp., QE-epi)
class of morphisms in
\mathsf{A}
.
This provides a nonadditive generalization of the results
of
[
25
, Sections 1–2]
, and simultaneously their extension from
the setting of exact categories (in the sense of Quillen
[
10
]
)
to that of right exact and left exact (additive) categories.

Notice that the classical notion of an exact category in the sense of
Quillen does not seem to make much sense in the nonadditive setting.
The point is that exact categories are additive categories with
a class of
admissible short exact sequences

0\longrightarrow K\overset{f}{\longrightarrow}L\overset{g}{\longrightarrow}M\longrightarrow 0
, which, first of all, have to be

kernel-cokernel pairs
:
f=\ker g
and
g=\coker f
.
In the context of nonadditive categories, one does not usually
consider kernel-cokernel pairs of morphisms.

In the general (nonadditive) category theory, there is a natural
construction of the (
co
)
equalizer
of a parallel pair of
morphisms
\bullet\rightrightarrows\bullet
, which is a single morphism

\bullet\longrightarrow\bullet
.
Conversely, to a single morphism
\bullet\longrightarrow\bullet
, one
assigns it (
co
)
kernel pair
, which is a parallel pair
of morphisms
\bullet\rightrightarrows\bullet
.
So, instead of a single self-dual concept of a kernel-cokernel pair,
in the nonadditive realm there are two concepts, dual to each other,
represented by diagrams of the shape

\bullet\longrightarrow\bullet\rightrightarrows\bullet\quad\text{or}\quad\bullet\rightrightarrows\bullet\longrightarrow\bullet.

Accordingly, it seems to be natural to split the single concept
of an exact category in the sense of Quillen into two halves
(the forementioned right exact and left exact categories) before
extending in to the nonadditive world.
This is the approach that we follow in the present paper.

Let us mention that, in spite of our discussion above, a self-dual
nonadditive version of Quillen exact categories exists in
the literature, introduced by Dyckerhoff and Kapranov under
the name of
proto-exact categories

[
13
, Section 2.4]
.
We do not consider this concept in the present paper.

1.
Preliminaries on Accessible Categories

We use the book
[
2
]
as the background reference source on
accessible categories.
In particular, we refer to
[
2
, Definition 1.4, Theorem
and Corollary 1.5, Definition 1.13(1), and Remark 1.21]
for
a discussion of
\lambda
-directed
vs.
\lambda
-filtered
colimits.
For an earlier exposition avoiding a small mistake in
[
2
, proof of
Theorem 1.5]
, see
[
5
]
.

Let
\kappa
be a regular cardinal and
\mathsf{A}
be a category with

\kappa
-directed (equivalently,
\kappa
-filtered) colimits.
An object
S\in\mathsf{A}
is called

\kappa
-presentable

[
2
, Definition 1.13(2)]

if the covariant functor
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(S,{-})\colon\mathsf{A}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Sets}
from
\mathsf{A}

to the category of sets
\mathsf{Sets}
preserves
\kappa
-directed colimits.
We denote the full subcategory of
\kappa
-presentable objects
by
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}\subset\mathsf{A}
.

The category
\mathsf{A}
is called

\kappa
-accessible

[
2
, Definition 2.1]
if there is
a
set
of
\kappa
-presentable objects
\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{A}

such that all the objects of
\mathsf{A}
are
\kappa
-directed colimits
of objects from
\mathsf{S}
.
If this is the case, then the
\kappa
-presentable objects of
\mathsf{A}

are precisely all the retracts of the objects from
\mathsf{S}
.

A category is called
accessible
if it is

\kappa
-accessible for some regular cardinal
\kappa
.
In the case of the countable cardinal
\kappa=\aleph_{0}
, one speaks of

finitely accessible categories

[
2
, Remark 2.2(1)]
.

Given a class of objects
\mathsf{T}\subset\mathsf{A}
, we denote by

\varinjlim_{(\kappa)}\mathsf{T}\subset\mathsf{A}
the class (or the full subcategory)
of all objects of
\mathsf{A}
that can be obtained as
\kappa
-directed
colimits of objects from
\mathsf{T}
.
The following proposition is well-known.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a
\kappa
-accessible category and
\mathsf{T}\subset\mathsf{A}

be a set of (some)
\kappa
-presentable objects.
Then the full subcategory
\mathsf{B}=\varinjlim_{(\kappa)}\mathsf{T}\subset\mathsf{A}

is closed under
\kappa
-directed colimits in
\mathsf{A}
.
The category
\mathsf{B}
is
\kappa
-accessible, and
the
\kappa
-presentable objects of
\mathsf{B}
are precisely all
the retracts of the objects from
\mathsf{T}
.
Equivalently, the
\kappa
-presentable objects of
\mathsf{B}
are
precisely all the objects of
\mathsf{B}
that are
\kappa
-presentable
in
\mathsf{A}
.
An object
A\in\mathsf{A}
belongs to
\mathsf{B}
if and only if, for every
object
S\in\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
, every morphism
S\longrightarrow A
in
\mathsf{A}

factorizes through an object from
\mathsf{T}
.

Proof.

In the context of finitely accessible additive categories, this result
goes back to
[
21
, Proposition 2.1]
,
[
12
, Section 4.1]
,
and
[
20
, Proposition 5.11]
.
For the full generality, see, e. g.,
[
24
, Proposition 1.2]
.
∎

Let
\mathsf{A}
,
\mathsf{B}
, and
\mathsf{C}

Following

A\in\mathsf{A}
and

Morphisms in the category

.

Let
\mathsf{A}
,
\mathsf{B}
, and
\mathsf{C}

-accessible categories, and

-accessible.

(S,T,u)\in F\downarrow G
is
\kappa
-presentable if and only

S
is
\kappa
-presentable in
\mathsf{A}
and the object
T

is
\kappa
-presentable in
\mathsf{B}
.

Proof.

This is
[
2
, proof of Theorem 2.43]
; see
also
[
25
, Proposition A.3]
.

A category
D
is said to be
finite
if the set of all morphisms
in
D
is finite.
More generally, a category
D
is said to be
\kappa
-small

if the cardinality of the set of all morphisms in
D
is smaller
than
\kappa
.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a
\kappa
-accessible category, and let
D
be a finite
category in which all endomorphisms of objects are identity morphisms.
Then the category
\mathsf{A}^{D}
of all (covariant) functors
D\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}

is
\kappa
-accessible.
A functor
F\colon D\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}
is
\kappa
-presentable as an object of

\mathsf{A}^{D}
if and only if, for every object
d\in D
, the object
F(d)
is

\kappa
-presentable in
\mathsf{A}
.

Proof.

In the case of finitely accessible categories
\mathsf{A}
, this result goes
back to
[
6
, Exposé I, Proposition 8.8.5]

and
[
23
, page 55]
.
For an arbitrary regular cardinal
\kappa
, the desired assertion is
a particular case of
[
17
, Theorem 1.3]
.
See also
[
25
, Proposition A.5]
.
∎

We use the notation
\varprojlim
and
\varinjlim
for limits and
colimits in categories.
The upper index, such as in
\varprojlim^{\mathsf{A}}
and
\varinjlim^{\mathsf{A}}
,
is used to indicate that the (co)limit is taken in the category
\mathsf{A}
.
By
\kappa
-small
(
co
)
limits
one means
(co)limits of diagrams indexed by
\kappa
-small indexing
categories
D
.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a
\kappa
-accessible category.
Then the full subcategory
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}\subset\mathsf{A}
of all

\kappa
-presentable objects in
\mathsf{A}
is closed under all

\kappa
-small colimits that exist in
\mathsf{A}
.
Conversely, the fully faithful inclusion functor
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}
preserves all
\kappa
-small colimits that exist
in
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that
\kappa
-directed colimits
commute with
\kappa
-small limits in the category of sets.
For the first assertion, see
[
2
, Proposition 1.16]
.
To prove the second claim, let
D
be a
\kappa
-small
category and let
F\colon D\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
be a
D
-indexed
diagram in
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
with the colimit

A=\varinjlim_{d\in D}^{\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}}F(d)\in\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}

computed in the category
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
.
Let
B\in\mathsf{A}
be an arbitrary object, and let

B=\varinjlim_{\xi\in\Xi}^{\mathsf{A}}S_{\xi}
be a representation of
B
as
the colimit of a diagram of objects
S_{\xi}\in\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
,
indexed by a
\kappa
-directed poset
\Xi
, the colimit being
computed in the category
\mathsf{A}
.
Then in the category of sets we have

\varprojlim\nolimits_{d\in D}^{\mathsf{Sets}}\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(F(d),B)=\varprojlim\nolimits_{d\in D}^{\mathsf{Sets}}\varinjlim\nolimits_{\xi\in\Xi}^{\mathsf{Sets}}\Hom_{\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}}(F(d),S_{\xi})\\
=\varinjlim\nolimits_{\xi\in\Xi}^{\mathsf{Sets}}\varprojlim\nolimits_{d\in D}^{\mathsf{Sets}}\Hom_{\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}}(F(d),S_{\xi})=\varinjlim\nolimits_{\xi\in\Xi}^{\mathsf{Sets}}\Hom_{\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}}(A,S_{\xi})=\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(A,B),

as desired.
∎

In the terminology of
[
2
, Example 6.38]
, full subcategories

\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{A}
satisfying the assumptions of the next lemma are called

weakly colimit-dense
.

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a category and
\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{A}
be a full subcategory
such that the minimal full subcategory of
\mathsf{A}
containing
\mathsf{S}

and closed under those colimits that exist in
\mathsf{A}
, coincides
with
\mathsf{A}
.
Then the fully faithful inclusion functor
\mathsf{S}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}
preserves
all those limits that exist in
\mathsf{S}
.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that limits commute with limits (in any
category, and in particular) in the category of sets.
Let
C
and
D
be two small categories, and let
G\colon D\longrightarrow\mathsf{S}

and
F\colon C\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}
be two diagrams indexed by
C
and
D
.
Let
B=\varprojlim_{d\in D}^{\mathsf{S}}G(d)\in\mathsf{S}
be the limit of
the diagram
G
computed in the category
\mathsf{S}
.
We have to prove that the natural map
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(A,B)\longrightarrow\varprojlim_{d\in D}^{\mathsf{Sets}}\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(A,G(d))
is a bijection of sets
for all objects
A\in\mathsf{A}
, and we know this to hold for all objects

A\in\mathsf{S}
.
Suppose that
A=\varinjlim_{c\in C}^{\mathsf{A}}F(c)\in\mathsf{A}
is the colimit
of the diagram
F
computed in the category
\mathsf{A}
, and that the map

\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(F(c),B)\longrightarrow\varprojlim_{d\in D}^{\mathsf{Sets}}\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(F(c),G(d))

is a bijection of sets for all
c\in C
.
Then it follows that the map
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(A,B)\longrightarrow\varprojlim_{d\in D}^{\mathsf{Sets}}\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(A,G(d))
is a bijection as well.
Indeed, we have

\varprojlim\nolimits_{d\in D}^{\mathsf{Sets}}\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(A,G(d))=\varprojlim\nolimits_{d\in D}^{\mathsf{Sets}}\varprojlim\nolimits_{c\in C}^{\mathsf{Sets}}\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(F(c),G(d))\\
=\varprojlim\nolimits_{c\in C}^{\mathsf{Sets}}\varprojlim\nolimits_{d\in D}^{\mathsf{Sets}}\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(F(c),G(d))=\varprojlim\nolimits_{c\in C}^{\mathsf{Sets}}\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(F(c),B)=\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(A,B).

∎

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a
\kappa
-accessible category and
A\longrightarrow B
be
a morphism in
\mathsf{A}
such that the induced map of sets
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(S,A)\longrightarrow\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(S,B)
is bijective for all
\kappa
-presentable
objects
S\in\mathsf{A}
.
Then the morphism
A\longrightarrow B
is an isomorphism in
\mathsf{A}
.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that every object
C\in\mathsf{A}
is the colimit
of the canonical diagram of morphisms into
C
from

\kappa
-presentable objects of
\mathsf{A}

[
2
, Section 0.6, Definition
and Remark 1.23, Remark 2.2(4), and Proposition 2.8(i)]
.
See also
[
24
, Lemma 1.1]
.
∎

Lemma 1.8
.

In any
\kappa
-accessible category
\mathsf{A}
,
\kappa
-directed
colimits commute with those
\kappa
-small limits that exist
in
\mathsf{A}
.
Specifically, if
\Xi
is
\kappa
-directed poset,
D
is
a
\kappa
-small category, and
F\colon\Xi\times D\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}
is
a functor such that the limit
\varprojlim_{d\in D}^{\mathsf{A}}F(\xi,d)

exists in
\mathsf{A}
for all
\xi\in\Xi
, then

(1)

Remark 4.4

By Example

Given an arbitrary left

(1) If

(3) The compositions of split monomorphisms are always split

Lemma 6.4

A QE-mono class

-accessible category

-presentable.

Proposition 12.1

It remains to recall that

\mathsf{A}

Thus

\mathsf{A}

15.

Proposition 15.1
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a
\kappa
-accessible category such that pullbacks
of split epimorphisms exist in the category
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
.
Then all pullbacks of strongly
\kappa
-pure epimorphisms exist
in
\mathsf{A}
, and all such pullbacks are strongly
\kappa
-pure
epimorphisms themselves.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be the class of all split epimorphisms in
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
.
By Example
8.1
(3),

\mathcal{Q}
is a QE-epi class in
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
.
Applying Theorem
9.1
,
we conclude that the class of strongly
\kappa
-pure epimorphisms

\mathcal{P}=\varinjlim_{(\kappa)}\mathcal{Q}
is a QE-epi class in
\mathsf{A}
.
In particular, condition (i
∗
) from
Section
8
is satisfied for
\mathcal{P}
, as desired.
∎

Corollary 15.2
.

Let
\mathsf{A}

(cf. the proof of Corollary

For the more general assertion under the assumption in parentheses,

∗

in Theorem

∎

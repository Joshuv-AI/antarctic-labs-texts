Myocarditis induced by a novel bifunctional PD-1/CTLA-4 combination antibody: a case report and literature review
Authors: Chaoyong Wei, Changli Tang, Yao zhang, Yanxiang Tang, Yaoqing Liu, Yuhui Tan, Yilan Fu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCombined PD-1/CTLA-4 blockade improves overall survival in advanced cancers but increases the risk of severe myocarditis. Iparomlimab and Tuvonralimab (Qibian®), a novel bifunctional anti-PD-1/CTLA-4 antibody, has recently entered clinical use, though its cardiotoxicity profile remains largely unknown. No myocarditis cases have been formally reported with this regimen.Case presentationA 61-year-old woman with ovarian cancer developed generalized malaise, dyspnea, and chest tightness after the second cycle of Iparomlimab and Tuvonralimab. Elevated high-sensitivity cardiac troponin T (hs-cTnT) levels and new electrocardiographic abnormalities were detected. Empirical methylprednisolone therapy (2 mg/kg/day) was initiated. Following the exclusion of acute coronary syndrome by angiography, ICI-induced myocarditis was diagnosed based on the patient’s symptoms, elevated troponin, electrocardiographic abnormalities, and applicable international consensus criteria (ESC/ICOS). Treatment was escalated to include high-dose methylprednisolone pulse (1 g/day), intravenous immunoglobulin, and mycophenolate mofetil. The patient stabilized by day 24 and was discharged. Persistent cardiac dysfunction warranted the implantation of a cardioverter-defibrillator and permanent discontinuation of immune checkpoint inhibitors. ConclusionWe report the first documented case of myocarditis linked to the novel bifunctional PD-1/CTLA-4 combination, Iparomlimab and Tuvonralimab. This case highlights that structural drug optimizations do not preclude severe cardiotoxicity. In resource-limited settings lacking advanced imaging, serial high-sensitivity troponin monitoring served as the cornerstone for early detection and guiding management. This experience confirms that favorable outcomes are achievable through vigilant biomarker surveillance and coordinated multidisciplinary care, even without advanced diagnostics.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-025-05412-1.

Introduction

To overcome the limited efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors(ICIs) monotherapy observed in certain patient populations [1, 2], dual immune blockade therapies targeting both PD-1 and CTLA-4 have been developed. This combinatorial approach enhances T-cell-mediated immunity through distinct yet synergistic mechanisms [3]. Since the FDA’s 2015 approval of the first PD-1/CTLA-4 combination (Ipilimumab plus Nivolumab) for advanced melanoma, this strategy has been extended to include renal cell carcinoma (RCC), lung cancer, colorectal cancer (CRC), and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) [4–7]. However, while demonstrating superior antitumor activity versus monotherapy, conventional PD-1/CTLA-4 blockade is associated with increased risks of immune-related adverse events (irAEs) [8–10], especially myocarditis, which is associated with a significantly higher rate of death than monotherapy is [11, 12].

Iparomlimab and Tuvonralimab (brand name: Qibian®; Qilu Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., Jinan, China) represent a bifunctional immune checkpoint blockade system consisting of two distinct monoclonal antibodies: Iparomlimab (an IgG4 anti-PD-1 antibody) and Tuvonralimab (an IgG1 anti-CTLA-4 antibody), which are formulated at a fixed 2:1 ratio [13]. This novel agent was engineered with a modified CTLA-4-targeting component featuring a shortened half-life, thereby potentially mitigating treatment-related toxicity. Currently approved in China for the treatment of cervical cancer. Preliminary clinical evidence suggests that combined PD-1/CTLA-4 blockade is potentially valuable in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer [14]. Furthermore, preclinical and clinical evidence indicates that antiangiogenic agents may augment immunotherapeutic efficacy through modulation of the tumor microenvironment [15]. The present case employing Iparomlimab and Tuvonralimab combined with antiangiogenic therapy offers a promising therapeutic paradigm for refractory patients who have exhausted multiple lines of treatment.

The cardiac safety profile of the combination of Iparomlimab and Tuvonralimab remains incompletely characterized. Herein, we present the first well-documented case of myocarditis attributable to this combination, which was successfully managed through vigilant high-sensitivity cardiac troponin T (hs-cTnT) monitoring. Case presentation

A 61-year-old Asian woman was diagnosed with stage IIIC moderately differentiated ovarian serous papillary adenocarcinoma 13 years ago. She underwent cytoreductive surgery followed by six cycles of adjuvant chemotherapy with liposomal paclitaxel and oxaliplatin. Following disease recurrence in 2017, surgical pathology revealed progression to high-grade serous carcinoma. The patient received multiple lines of subsequent therapy, including bevacizumab and PARP inhibitors, but had no prior immune checkpoint inhibitor exposure before initiating the current off-label regimen of Iparomlimab and Tuvonralimab (250 mg every 21 days) combined with fruquintinib (5 mg orally on days 1–14). It is important to note that the patient was not on any medications with known cardiovascular toxicity apart from fruquintinib, which carries a risk of direct vascular toxicity (e.g., hypertension) [16], and the checkpoint inhibitors themselves, which are associated with immune-mediated cardiotoxicity. These risks were actively monitored. Prior to initiating immunotherapy, a comprehensive cardiac evaluation demonstrated normal findings on electrocardiogram (ECG) and baseline cardiac biomarker assessment. The patient’s medical history included transient hypothyroidism that had been managed with levothyroxine sodium replacement, with sustained normalization of thyroid function following treatment discontinuation.

The patient was admitted on May 08, 2025, to receive her scheduled third cycle of the combined regimen of Iparomlimab, Tuvonralimab, and fruquintinib. Upon admission, her vital signs were stable: temperature 36.8 °C, heart rate 90 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, and blood pressure 134/91 mmHg. However, she reported progressively worsening fatigue and exertional dyspnea since her previous treatment cycle, with significant symptom exacerbation in the days preceding admission. Notably, she described accompanying chest tightness but denied chest pain or hemoptysis. Laboratory tests revealed the following abnormal markers of inflammation and myocardial injury: elevated C-reactive protein (55.82 mg/L, normal < 6 mg/L) and elevated hs-cTnT (57.2 pg/mL, normal < 14 pg/mL). Creatine kinase (CK), creatine kinase-MB isoenzyme (CK-MB), and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) levels were normal. ECG revealed sinus rhythm with premature ventricular beats, left anterior branching block, and extensive lead T-wave abnormalities (Fig. 1a).

On the following day, the patient’s persistently elevated hs-cTnT level (172.7 pg/mL) in conjunction with clinical manifestations prompted a provisional diagnosis of myocarditis, leading to empirical initiation of methylprednisolone therapy (2 mg/kg/d). Critically, the patient was not receiving any other concomitant cardiotoxic medications. Fruquintinib-related toxicity was deemed unlikely given the absence of hypertensive crisis or thromboembolic events, and the clinical timeline strongly favored an immune-mediated etiology. Despite four days of treatment, the patient developed progressive myocardial damage evidenced by a dramatic 700-fold increase in hs-cTnT (peaking at 10477.0 pg/mL), accompanied by significant increases in CK (749.60 U/L) and NT-proBNP (2137.9 pg/mL) levels. A heart color ultrasound on May 12 revealed preserved left ventricular systolic function (LVEF 64%) with mild mitral regurgitation and diastolic dysfunction. Subsequent serial assessments confirmed that LVEF remained relatively stable and preserved throughout the acute phase of her illness, whereas serial ECGs demonstrated new ischemic ST-segment changes (Fig. 1b). These findings, combined with the cardiac biomarker profile, strongly suggested that acute myocardial injury necessitated ICU transfer. Coronary angiography excluded acute coronary syndrome, revealing only moderate (60%) proximal right coronary artery stenosis (Fig. 2), thereby confirming the diagnosis of ICI-induced myocarditis. In accordance with guideline recommendations for severe, steroid-refractory disease, therapy was immediately escalated to a multi-modal immunosuppressive regimen. The combination of pulse-dose methylprednisolone (1000 mg/d), intravenous immunoglobulin (20 g/d), and mycophenolate mofetil (2 g/d) was selected based on institutional expertise and guideline-endorsed efficacy. While alternative agents (e.g., abatacept) were considered, they required procurement. Therefore, the initiated regimen represented the most feasible and immediately accessible evidence-based option to urgently address the life-threatening condition. Empirical antimicrobial coverage was also initiated. The patient developed the characteristic neuromuscular symptom of ptosis on May 13th. This presentation, coupled with a concurrent elevation in creatine kinase (CK to 306.6 U/L), met the clinical diagnostic criteria for probable immune-induced myositis. The co-occurrence of myositis with myocarditis portends an ominous prognosis and high mortality rate [17]. Biomarker trends (hs-cTnT, CK, CK-MB, and NT-proBNP) rapidly improved after therapy. Based on a significant drop in hs-cTnT (to 3012.8 pg/mL) and NT-proBNP (to 1256.0 pg/mL) documented on May 15, steroid tapering was initiated on the same day. This decision was supported by marked clinical improvement and the observed sustained downward trend in biomarkers. However, this initial reduction of the methylprednisolone dose to 500 mg/d precipitated a biomarker rebound by the following day (May 16, hs-cTnT 3941.1 → 3357.6 pg/mL), requiring immediate dose re-escalation to 700 mg/d. During this period, frequent ventricular ectopy prompted the addition of metoprolol (23.75 mg qd), after which the patient stabilized with normalization of CK/CK-MB and a steady decline in hs-cTnT/NT-proBNP. Gradual immunosuppression tapering was achieved by May 30, 2025, with hs-cTnT showing sustained improvement (methylprednisolone 80 mg qd) despite fluctuating NT-proBNP levels reflecting residual cardiac dysfunction. The patient was compliant and trusted our team throughout the treatment. A referral to a specialized cardiac center was advised. The patient was transferred to a tertiary cardiology center on May 31 for ongoing management, ultimately requiring ICD implantation for arrhythmia prophylaxis. At the three-month follow-up after ICD implantation, the patient remained clinically stable with no recurrence of arrhythmia or heart failure symptoms. Discussion

ICI-induced myocarditis is a rare but serious adverse event. Although its overall incidence is low, the risk is significantly higher with combination therapies like Ipilimumab plus Nivolumab [8, 18], potentially due to enhanced T-cell clonal expansion and the release of interferon-gamma (IFNγ) that exacerbates macrophage-mediated inflammation [19, 20].

Against this background, we report the first documented case of myocarditis associated with the novel bifunctional PD-1/CTLA-4 antibody combination, Iparomlimab and Tuvonralimab. This case is particularly instructive for three reasons: first, it confirms that cardiotoxicity remains a concern even with structurally optimized agents; second, it underscores the indispensable role of serial high-sensitivity cardiac troponin T (hs-cTnT) monitoring in resource-limited settings where advanced imaging is not readily available; and third, it demonstrates that successful management is achievable by adhering to guideline-directed clinical diagnosis and aggressive immunosuppression.

Early clinical presentation and diagnostic challenges in a resource-limited setting

The onset in our patient occurred within the high-risk period for ICI-induced myocarditis. Several studies indicate that such events are concentrated early in the treatment course, with a median time to onset of approximately 27–34 days, and the majority of cases occur within 6 to 12 weeks of initiation [9, 21]. The timeline of our patient—who developed symptoms and was diagnosed following the second cycle—aligns with this established pattern. Furthermore, published case reports of myocarditis induced by conventional combination immunotherapy (including a curated series of 20 cases we have compiled in the Supplementary Material) commonly describe events occurring in the early treatment phase. This observation further confirms that, despite molecular optimization, the timing of cardiotoxicity with this novel combination is similar to that of traditional regimens. Therefore, intensive cardiovascular monitoring during the initial treatment phases (e.g., the first three cycles) and throughout the entire course of therapy is necessary and prudent for patients receiving this regimen.

The clinical presentation in our case was dominated by non-specific symptoms such as fatigue and dyspnea, rather than typical chest pain. These symptoms can easily be attributed to cancer progression or other non-cardiac treatment-related adverse events, thereby increasing the risk of diagnostic oversight. This underscores the critical importance of proactive surveillance. In our patient, the combination of progressively rising hs-cTnT levels, new ECG abnormalities, and the exclusion of acute coronary syndrome by coronary angiography formed a cohesive diagnostic picture. This approach, which leverages readily available clinical tools, is explicitly endorsed by the 2022 ESC Guidelines on Cardio-Oncology developed in collaboration with the European Hematology Association (EHA), the European Society for Therapeutic Radiology and Oncology (ESTRO) and the International Cardio-Oncology Society (IC-OS) for establishing a clinical diagnosis of ICI-induced myocarditis [22, 23]. Our experience validates this integrated diagnostic pathway as both feasible and effective in environments where immediate access to cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) is constrained.

Re-evaluating the roles of imaging and biomarkers: the path to an integrated diagnosis

While endomyocardial biopsy (EMB) remains the diagnostic gold standard, its invasiveness and associated risks often preclude its routine use [24]. The 2025 ESC Guidelines for the Management of Myocarditis and Pericarditis reinforce CMR as a cornerstone non-invasive tool for definitive diagnosis, leveraging multi-parametric imaging, including T1 and T2 mapping and late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) to characterize myocardial tissue [25]. Advanced techniques like CMR feature-tracking (CMR-FT) provide incremental diagnostic value by detecting myocardial dysfunction through strain analysis [26, 27]. However, the real-world application of CMR faces significant challenges. A substantial proportion of clinically confirmed ICI-induced myocarditis cases do not meet the Lake Louise Criteria, limiting its sensitivity [27]. Furthermore, CMR performed in the very early stages after symptom onset may yield false-negative results, as characteristic findings like edema and LGE can require several days to fully evolve [28]. Crucially, in many clinical environments akin to ours, the widespread adoption of CMR is constrained by equipment availability, cost, and patient instability during the acute phase. Although advanced modalities like CMR-FT offer additional value, their application was precluded during the acute management of this case. Even prospectively, the widespread implementation of CMR-FT at our center faces technical and resource barriers, further underscoring the universal importance of establishing diagnostic pathways that rely on fundamental investigations.

Consequently, in the absence of prompt access to CMR, serial serum biomarker monitoring assumed a central role in the diagnosis and management of our patient. Cardiac troponin is the cornerstone for detecting myocardial injury and assessing disease severity [29], and it is recognized as a core biomarker for the clinical diagnosis of ICI-induced myocarditis in current ESC and other guidelines. Furthermore, high-sensitivity cardiac troponin assays, which are approximately 100 times more sensitive than conventional tests, have become a key tool for identifying subclinical myocardial injury [30]. The critical role of these high-sensitivity assays was evident in our case, in which the patient presented with persistently elevated hs-cTnT levels at admission, while creatine kinase (CK) and liver enzyme levels remained normal. This finding contrasts with the surveillance strategy proposed by Vasbinder et al. [31], which prioritizes initial testing of CK, AST, and ALT, with subsequent hs-cTn measurement triggered only if these initial enzymes are abnormal. Our case suggests that this conditional approach may consequently lead to underdiagnosis. The 2025 ESC Guidelines explicitly list elevated high-sensitivity troponin as a core biomarker supporting the diagnosis of myocarditis. Our diagnostic approach of direct hs-cTn monitoring aligns with prospective evidence that underscores its superior sensitivity over conditional screening strategies [32–34]. It is important to note that high-sensitivity troponin I (hs-cTnI) is generally more specific for acute myocardial injury, whereas troponin T (cTnT) elevation may reflect its release from both cardiac and skeletal muscle in the context of concurrent myositis [35]. This provides context for the suspected myositis observed in our case patient. Beyond troponin, natriuretic peptides provide crucial prognostic information on hemodynamic stress. The 2025 ESC Guidelines specify that heart failure biomarkers such as NT-proBNP help identify disease severity [25]; abnormal levels are observed in over 50% of patients [21], a relevance confirmed in our case by the critical value of NT-proBNP in guiding therapeutic decisions and evaluating efficacy. Consequently, we advocate for the integration of both high-sensitivity troponin and natriuretic peptides into a multi-modal assessment strategy for comprehensive diagnosis and risk stratification.

Therapeutic Implications, drug optimization, and future directions

The therapeutic strategy for this patient was predicated on the fundamental principles of managing high-grade immune-related adverse events. An initial intravenous methylprednisolone dose (2 mg/kg/day) was administered as a judicious measure following clinical diagnosis. However, the subsequent rapid and significant rise in hs-cTnT levels unequivocally identified a severe and progressive disease course. In accordance with guideline recommendations for such scenarios, the regimen was promptly intensified to a multi-modal immunosuppressive protocol, incorporating pulse-dose methylprednisolone (1000 mg/day), intravenous immunoglobulin, and mycophenolate mofetil. The patient’s eventual positive response to this augmented therapy underscores the necessity of tailoring immunosuppressive intensity to the dynamic clinical and biomarker profile. This case reinforces the paradigm that successful management hinges not only on the immediate initiation of high-dose corticosteroids upon suspicion of ICI-induced myocarditis—a strategy supported by evidence linking early corticosteroid therapy with improved outcomes [36]—but equally on the vigilant recognition of inadequate response and the timely escalation of therapy.

Iparomlimab and Tuvonralimab incorporate an engineered CTLA-4 component with a shortened half-life. This design, compared to the conventional Ipilimumab plus Nivolumab regimen, aims to reduce systemic CTLA-4 exposure and its associated toxicity. Nevertheless, the occurrence of severe, early-onset myocarditis in our patient serves as a critical reminder that the potential for such acute, life-threatening complications persists despite these pharmacokinetic modifications. The rapidity of onset is consistent with a robust initial T-cell activation as the underlying mechanism, a process in which CTLA-4 serves as a critical early checkpoint. Our findings suggest that even transient but high-potency blockade of this checkpoint can serve as a sufficient precipitating event for a severe inflammatory cascade. Consequently, while these structural optimizations may benefit long-term safety, they do not diminish the imperative for vigilant early cardiotoxicity screening.

Conclusion

In conclusion, as the first documented case of myocarditis associated with Iparomlimab and Tuvonralimab, this report confirms the potential cardiotoxicity of this novel bifunctional PD-1/CTLA-4 antibody combination. It serves as a critical reminder that early cardiac risk remains a concern even with structurally optimized combination immunotherapies. In resource-limited environments where advanced imaging is not routinely accessible, the direct incorporation of high-sensitivity troponin into baseline and early-treatment monitoring protocols is a feasible and crucial strategy for early identification and diagnosis. Ultimately, optimizing patient outcomes depends on heightened clinical vigilance, timely diagnosis guided by current criteria, and the prompt initiation of aggressive immunosuppressive therapy within a coordinated multidisciplinary team.

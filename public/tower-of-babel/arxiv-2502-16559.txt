Some remarks on Poisson quasi-Nijenhuis manifolds and Haantjes structures
E. Chuño Vizarreta 1 , I. Mencattini 2 , M. Pedroni 3,4

Abstract
In this note we first characterize Poisson quasi-Nijenhuis structures on three-dimensional oriented manifolds whose underlying Poisson tensor never vanishes, and we show that each of these structures is involutive. Then we prove that every such three-dimensional Poisson quasi-Nijenhuis manifold is a Haantjes manifold and that it carries a generalized Lenard-Magri chain. Keywords: Integrable systems; Poisson quasi-Nijenhuis manifolds; Generalized Lenard-Magri chains; Haantjes tensors; Haantjes manifolds. MSC codes: 37J35, 53D17, 70H06.



1
Introduction

The notion of Poisson-Nijenhuis structure, i.e., a
compatible
pair of a Poisson bivector
\pi
and a
torsionless
tensor field
N
of type
(1,1)
, plays an important role in the theory of classical integrable systems, since it provides a geometrical framework to produce involutive families
of conserved quantities for the underlying Hamiltonian dynamical system, see
[
16
,
22
]
. It is important to stress that this property hinges crucially on the hypothesis that the Nijenhuis torsion of
N
is equal to zero, see (
1
) and Section
2
for the relevant definitions. In fact, even a controlled change on this assumption destroys, in general, the possibility to produce the desired families of first integrals, spoiling a possible use of the newborn geometrical structure in the theory of integrable models.

An example of this state of affairs is provided by the so called Poisson quasi-Nijenhuis structures, i.e., a
compatible
triple of a Poisson bivector
\pi
, a 3-form
\phi
and a
(1,1)
tensor field
N
, whose Nijenhuis torsion is given in (
8
). Although this class of structures represents a very interesting generalization of the Poisson-Nijenhuis one (see
[
24
,
1
,
8
,
6
,
10
]
) and has interesting applications in mathematical physics
(see, for example,
[
30
]
), it was not used in the theory of integrable systems until the discover
of its relation with

n
-particle Toda systems, see
[
11
,
13
]
. In these papers the notion of
involutive
Poisson quasi-Nijenhuis structure was introduced, see Definition
4
hereafter, and a set of
sufficient
conditions for a Poisson quasi-Nijenhuis structure to be involutive were presented, see Theorem
6
below. It is worth mentioning that, since these conditions are very stringent, the task of finding (interesting) examples of involutive Poisson quasi-Nijenhuis structures is a non-trivial open problem, see for example
[
12
]
.

In line with the title of
[
17
]
, the attempt to go beyond recursion operators to find a more general theoretical framework than the Poisson-Nijenhuis one was recently undertaken in
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
and
[
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
. In these works, one of the main novelties is the presence of (families of)
(1,1)
tensor fields whose
Haantjes
torsion is equal to zero.
As the Nijenhuis one, the Haantjes torsion of
N
is a 2-form with values in the tangent bundle of the underlying manifold, see (
29
), which controls the Frobenius integrability of the 2-planes generated by pairs of eigenvectors of
N
.
Its relevance in the theory of integrable systems of hydrodynamic type was already observed in

[
3
,
14
]
, but its importance in the theory of finite-dimensional integrable system was unnoticed until the above mentioned works of Magri, Tempesta and Tondo.

The present note is a first attempt to construct a bridge between Poisson quasi-Nijenhuis and Haantjes structures, aiming to find, in a near future, more applications of the former to the theory of finite-dimensional integrable systems.
Our results concern oriented three-dimensional manifolds and generalize the one proved in
[
7
]
for Poisson-Nijenhuis (PN from now on) manifolds. Assuming that the Poisson tensor never vanishes, we describe any Poisson quasi-Nijenhuis manifold in terms of few geometrical objects. Moreover, we show that such manifolds are all involutive. Finally, we show that they are Haantjes manifolds with a generalized Lenard-Magri chain.

A more detailed plan of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
we first collect some general information about Poisson quasi-Nijenhuis (PqN from now on) structures and we remind the definition of involutive PqN manifold. Then we recall two results, the first giving a set of sufficient conditions for the involutivity of a PqN structure, see Theorem
6
, the second enclosing a general statement about
deformations of PqN structures, see Theorem
7
. Section
3
is
devoted to characterize PqN structures (with never vanishing Poisson tensor) on an oriented three-dimensional manifold, see Proposition
12
, and to prove that such structures are involutive, see Theorem
17
. Moreover,
in Section
4
they are shown to be Haantjes structures, see Theorem
23
, and that they carry a generalized Lenard-Magri chain, see Theorem
29
. In Subsection
4.2
we make a few final comments aiming to link the structures introduced in the recent works
[
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
]
with the geometry of the PqN
manifolds studied in this note. The paper closes with an Appendix where we collect the proof of two identities used in the main text and a few facts about the Haantjes torsion.

1.1
Notations and conventions

Hereafter we will systematically use the following conventions. All manifolds,
vector bundles and their sections will be smooth. The space of sections of the vector bundle
V
will be denoted with
\Gamma(V)
, with the exception of the sections of
TM
, i.e., vector fields, and of
\Lambda^{k}(T^{\ast}M)
, i.e.,
k
-differential forms, which will be denoted by
\mathfrak{X}(M)
and, respectively,
\Omega^{k}(M)
. If
V
is a vector bundle over a manifold
M
and
A\in\text{End}_{C^{\infty}(M)}(V)
,
A^{\ast}
will denote the endomorphism of
V^{\ast}
, the dual bundle of
V
, defined by
\langle A^{\ast}\eta,s\rangle=\langle\eta,As\rangle
for all
s\in\Gamma(V)
and
\eta\in\Gamma(V^{\ast})
.
Every bivector field
P\in\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}(TM))
defines a unique (and it is defined uniquely by)
P^{\sharp}\in\text{Hom}_{C^{\infty}(M)}(T^{\ast}M,TM)
via the
formula
\langle\beta,P^{\sharp}\alpha\rangle=P(\alpha,\beta)
, for all
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. Similarly, every 2-form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(M)
defines a unique (and it is defined uniquely by)
\omega^{\flat}\in\text{Hom}_{C^{\infty}(M)}(TM,T^{\ast}M)
via
\langle\omega^{\flat}X,Y\rangle=\omega(X,Y)
, for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
. Finally, recall that to every
N\in\text{End}_{C^{\infty}(M)}TM
, i.e., a tensor field of type
(1,1)
, one can associate its Nijenhuis torsion
T_{N}
, which is the 2-form on
M
with values in
TM
defined by

T_{N}(X,Y)=[NX,NY]-N([NX,Y]+[X,NY]-N[X,Y]).

(1)

The (1,1) tensor field
N
will be called Nijenhuis torsion-free, or, more simply, torsion-free (or a Nijenhuis operator)
if
T_{N}\equiv 0
.

2
Recollection of Poisson quasi-Nijenhuis structures

A Poisson-Nijenhuis structure on a manifold
M
is a pair
(\pi,N)
of a Poisson bivector
\pi
and a torsion-free tensor field
N
of type
(1,1)
, satisfying the following compatibility conditions:

\displaystyle\mbox{(C1)}\quad N\circ\pi^{\sharp}=\pi^{\sharp}\circ N^{\ast}=:\pi^{\sharp}_{N};

(2)

\displaystyle\mbox{(C2)}\quad[\alpha,\beta]_{\pi_{N}}=[N^{\ast}\alpha,\beta]_{\pi}+[\alpha,N^{\ast}\beta]_{\pi}-N^{\ast}[\alpha,\beta]_{\pi},\qquad\forall\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(M),

where, if
P
is a bivector field,
the bracket
[-,-]_{P}:\Omega^{1}(M)\times\Omega^{1}(M)\rightarrow\Omega^{1}(M)
is defined by

[\alpha,\beta]_{P}=\mathcal{L}_{P^{\sharp}\alpha}\beta-\mathcal{L}_{P^{\sharp}\beta}\alpha-d\langle\beta,P^{\sharp}\alpha\rangle,

and
\mathcal{L}_{X}
is the Lie derivative with respect to the vector field
X
. A manifold
M
endowed
with a PN structure
(\pi,N)
will be called a PN manifold and will be denoted by the triple
(M,\pi,N)
.

Remark 1
.

The second formula in
(
2
) is often written as
C(\pi,N)\equiv 0
, where

C(\pi,N)(\alpha,\beta)=[\alpha,\beta]_{\pi_{N}}-[N^{\ast}\alpha,\beta]_{\pi}-[\alpha,N^{\ast}\beta]_{\pi}+N^{\ast}[\alpha,\beta]_{\pi},\qquad\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(M),

(3)

is the so called Magri-Morosi concomitant. It is also worth recalling that a torsion-free
(1,1)
tensor field
N
is often called a recursion operator since the vector space of all
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
such that
\mathcal{L}_{X}N=0
is an
N
-invariant Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{X}(M)
. This means, in particular, that if
X
is an infinitesimal symmetry of
N
, i.e.,

\mathcal{L}_{X}N=0
, then
N^{k}X
will also be, for all
k\geq 1
.

To every PN structure one can associate the space

\Omega^{1}_{Ham}(M)=\{\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)\mid d\alpha=0=d_{N}\alpha\},

(4)

where
d_{N}:\Omega^{\bullet}(M)\rightarrow\Omega^{\bullet}(M)
is defined by
d_{N}=i_{N}\circ d-d\circ i_{N}
and
i_{N}
is the degree-zero endormorphism of
\Omega^{\bullet}(M)
defined on
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(M)
by
i_{N}\omega(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\omega(X_{1},\dots,NX_{j},\dots,X_{k})
. The following proposition encloses an important property of
\Omega^{1}_{Ham}(M)
.

Proposition 2

(
[
22
,
5
]
)
.

The space
\Omega^{1}_{Ham}(M)
is
N^{\ast}
-invariant. Moreover,

1.

For every
k\geq 0
, it is closed with respect to
[-,-]_{\pi_{N^{k}}}
.

2.

Given
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}_{Ham}(M)
and
s\geq 1
, set
\alpha_{s}={N^{\ast}}^{s}\alpha
. Then

[\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}]_{\pi_{N^{k}}}=0

for all
k\geq 0
and
i,j\geq 1
.

In other words,
\Omega^{1}_{Ham}(M)
is a receptacle for the so called Lenard-Magri, or bi-Hamiltonian, chains.
An important example is obtained as follows. For every
k\geq 1
, let
I_{k}:=\frac{1}{2k}\text{Tr}(N^{k})
, where
Tr
denote the trace operator. Then one can prove that
\{dI_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset\Omega^{1}_{Ham}(M)
. More precisely, a direct computation shows that, for every
k\geq 1
,

N^{\ast}dI_{k}=dI_{k+1},

(5)

which yields the fundamental involutivity property

\{I_{i},I_{j}\}=0\qquad\forall i,j\geq 1,

(6)

where
\{-,-\}
is the Poisson bracket defined by
\pi
via the formula

\{f,g\}=\pi(df,dg)
, for all
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

Example 3

(Das-Okubo PN structure
[
9
]
)
.

On
M=\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, with coordinates
(p_{i},q^{i})
,
i=1,\dots,n
, let
\pi=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{i}}
and

\begin{split}N_{DO}&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{i}}\otimes dq^{i}+\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}\otimes dp_{i}\Big)+\sum_{i<j}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{i}}\otimes dp_{j}-\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{j}}\otimes dp_{i}\Big)\\
&+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}e^{q^{i}-q^{i+1}}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i+1}}\otimes dq^{i}-\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}\otimes dq^{i+1}\Big).\end{split}

(7)

Then
(\pi,N_{DO})
is a PN structure and the sequence
\{I_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
is a set of first integrals for the
open
n
-particle Toda chain
. In particular,
I_{1}
is the total linear momentum and
I_{2}
is the energy (Hamiltonian) of this system.

The notion of a Poisson quasi-Nijenhuis structure, introduced in
[
24
]
, represents an interesting generalization of the Poisson-Nijenhuis one. This enhanced setting is defined by a triple
(\pi,N,\phi)
, where
\pi
is a Poisson tensor,

N
is a tensor field of type (1,1) which is compatible with
\pi
, and
\phi
is a
d
- and
d_{N}
-closed 3-form such that

T_{N}(X,Y)=\pi^{\sharp}(\phi(X,Y,-))=:\pi^{\sharp}(i_{X\wedge Y}\phi)\qquad\forall X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M).

(8)

Since
T_{N}
does not vanish, the corresponding space
\Omega_{Ham}^{1}(M)
is not
N^{\ast}
-invariant and, for this reason, it looses the property of being the natural place where to look for Lenard-Magri chains. In particular, in this more general context, the recurrence equations (
5
) assume the form

dI_{k+1}=N^{\ast}dI_{k}+\phi_{k-1},\qquad\forall k\geq 1,

(9)

where the 1-forms
\phi_{s}
are defined by the formula

\langle\phi_{s},X\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}(N^{s}(i_{X}T_{N})),\qquad\forall X\in\mathfrak{X}(M),s\geq 0,

(10)

and
\langle i_{X}T_{N},Y\rangle=T_{N}(X,Y)
for all vector fields
Y
. The recurrence defined in (
9
) entails that, for all
k>j\geq 1
,

\{I_{k},I_{j}\}-\{I_{k-1},I_{j+1}\}=-\langle\phi_{j-1},\pi^{\sharp}dI_{k-1}\rangle-\langle\phi_{k-1},\pi^{\sharp}dI_{j}\rangle,

(11)

which implies that, for a general PqN structure, the family
\{I_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
fails to be
involutive, i.e., (
6
) is not satisfied for all possible choices of
i,j
. This observation justifies the following

Definition 4
.

A PqN structure is called involutive if (
6
) holds for all
i
and
j
.

Even though this definition is quite restrictive, the following example shows that there is at least one important class of PqN structures which meet its requirement.

Example 5

(PqN structure of the closed Toda system)
.

An important example of involutive PqN structure
was discovered in
[
11
]
, see also
[
12
]
, and goes as follows. Let
(M,\pi)
and
N_{DO}
be as in Example
3
, and let

\widetilde{N}=N_{DO}-e^{q^{n}-q^{1}}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{1}}\otimes dq_{n}-\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{n}}\otimes dq^{1}\Big).

(12)

If
\phi=2e^{q^{n}-q^{1}}dq^{1}\wedge dq^{n}\wedge\sum_{i=1}^{n}dp_{i}
, then one can show that
(\pi,\widetilde{N},\phi)
is an involutive PqN structure on
M
. This family of PqN structures describes the so called
closed
n
-particle Toda chain
. More precisely, and in analogy with the case of the Das-Okubo PN structure, the functions
{\tilde{I}}_{1}
and

{\tilde{I}}_{2}
are, respectively, the total linear momentum and the energy (Hamiltonian) of this classical integrable system.

It is therefore an interesting problem to pin down a set of assumptions entailing the involutivity of a PqN structure.
A set of involutivity conditions was presented in
[
11
]
and subsequently improved in
[
13
]
. We recall this result in

Theorem 6
.

Let
(\pi,N,\phi)
be a PqN structure on
M
and let
I_{k}=\frac{1}{2k}\text{Tr}N^{k}
,
k\geq 1
. Suppose there is a 2-form
\Omega
such that:

(a)

\phi=-2\,dI_{1}\wedge\Omega
;

(b)

\Omega(X_{j},Y_{k})=0
for all
j,k\geq 1
, where
Y_{k}=N^{k-1}X_{1}-X_{k}
and
X_{k}=\pi^{\sharp}dI_{k}
.

Then
\{I_{j},I_{k}\}=0
for all
j,k\geq 1
, where
\{-,-\}
is the Poisson bracket defined by
\pi
.

Before moving to the next section, we make a final comment about the family of PqN structures presented in Example
5
. To this end, we
recall that
a problem which is parallel, though independent, to the task of taming the class of involutive PqN structure,
is the one of “deforming” a PqN structure into another one. To phrase it in a more precise way, we recall the following theorem
[
11
,
10
]
.

Theorem 7
.

Let
(\pi,N,\phi)
be a PqN structure on
M
and let
\Omega
be a closed 2-form. If

\widetilde{N}=N+\pi^{\sharp}\Omega^{\flat}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{\phi}=\phi+d_{N}\Omega+\frac{1}{2}[\Omega,\Omega]_{\pi},

then
(\pi,\widetilde{N},\widetilde{\phi})
is a PqN structure. In particular, if
\widetilde{\phi}=0
,
the pair
(\pi,\widetilde{N})
is a PN structure.

This result, together with Theorem
6
, opens the door to the following problem: given a PqN structure,
which assumptions on
\Omega
guarantee that the deformed structure
(\pi,\widetilde{N},\widetilde{\phi})
is involutive? Or, equivalently, when does a PqN structure admit involutive deformations? Note that, going back to the PN and PqN structures presented in Examples
3
and
5
, one can show that the latter can be obtained as a deformation of the former using
\Omega=e^{q^{n}-q^{1}}dq^{n}\wedge dq^{1}
, see
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
.

3
Three-dimensional oriented PqN manifolds

In this section we will work with oriented manifolds, i.e., with pairs
(M,\mathcal{V})
where
M
is a manifold and
\mathcal{V}
is a never vanishing differential form of maximal degree, i.e., a volume form on
M
. Any such form defines a family of isomorphisms
\star_{k}:\Gamma(\Lambda^{k}(TM))\rightarrow\Omega^{\dim M-k}(M)
via

\star_{k}P:=i_{P}\mathcal{V},

that is, the contraction of
\mathcal{V}
with
P
, which is the unique
(\dim M-k)
-form such that, for all

Q\in\Gamma(\Lambda^{\dim M-k}(M))
,

\langle i_{P}\mathcal{V},Q\rangle=\langle\mathcal{V},P\wedge Q\rangle.

Furthermore, one can associate to every
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
its divergence, i.e., the unique smooth function
\operatorname{div}(X)
such that
\mathcal{L}_{X}\mathcal{V}=\operatorname{div}(X)\mathcal{V}
. Once this has been settled, we can start our study of
three-dimensional oriented PqN manifolds, stating the following very well known result.

Proposition 8

(
[
18
]
, Proposition 9.20)
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{V})
be a three-dimensional oriented manifold.
A bivector field
\pi\in\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}TM)
is Poisson if and only if
\xi\wedge d\xi=0
,
where
\xi=\star_{2}\pi=i_{\pi}\mathcal{V}
.
In this case,

\pi^{\sharp}\xi=0.

(13)

Remark 9
.

Since
\star_{2}
is an isomorphism, the Poisson tensor
\pi
never vanishes if and only if the corresponding 1-form
\xi
never vanishes.

We also recall a very nice characterization of three-dimensional oriented PN manifolds, which was presented in
[
7
]
, Theorem 2.1.

Theorem 10
.

Let
N
be a
(1,1)
tensor field on a three-dimensional oriented Poisson manifold
(M,\pi,\mathcal{V})
. Suppose that
\pi
never vanishes.
Then
(M,\pi,N)
is a PN manifold if and only if
N
can be decomposed as

N=\lambda I+Z\otimes\xi,

(14)

where
\lambda\in C^{\infty}(M)
,
Z\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
,
\xi=i_{\pi}\mathcal{V}
and the following conditions hold:

d\left(\lambda+\left<\xi,Z\right>\right)=\operatorname{div}(Z)\xi\quad\text{and}\quad Z(\lambda)=0.

(15)

A couple of remarks about the proof of the previous statement are in order.

Remark 11
.

1.

Under the hypotheses of the previous theorem, the condition
N\circ\pi^{\sharp}=\pi^{\sharp}\circ N^{\ast}
forces the decomposition of the
(1,1)
tensor field
N
as
N=\lambda I+Z\otimes\xi
, where
\lambda
,
Z
and
\xi
are as in the statement of the theorem, see Lemma 2.4 in
[
7
]
. On the other hand,
since
\pi^{\sharp}\xi=0
by (
13
), if
N=\lambda I+Z\otimes\xi
then
N\circ\pi^{\sharp}=\pi^{\sharp}\circ N^{\ast}
. For this reason one has that

N=\lambda I+Z\otimes\xi\iff N\circ\pi^{\sharp}=\pi^{\sharp}\circ N^{\ast}.

Furthermore, given the above decomposition of
N
,

C(\pi,N)\equiv 0\iff d(\lambda+\langle\xi,Z\rangle)=\operatorname{div}(Z)\xi,

see Lemma 2.6 in
[
7
]
. In other words, on an oriented three-dimensional manifold, the pair
(\pi,N)
is compatible, see (
2
), if and only if

N=\lambda I+Z\otimes\xi\quad\text{and}\quad d(\lambda+\langle\xi,Z\rangle)=\operatorname{div}(Z)\xi.

2.

On the other hand, in Lemma 2.5 of
[
7
]
it is proven that if
N
decomposes as above, then

T_{N}\equiv 0\iff\xi\wedge d(\lambda+\langle\xi,Z\rangle)=0\quad\text{and}\quad Z(\lambda)=0.

(16)

If
\pi
and
N
are compatible, then the first identity in the right-hand side of the previous equivalence is automatically verified, so one obtains the proof of Theorem
10
. Note that
(
16
) can be proved using the identity

T_{N}(X,Y)=\left(\xi\wedge d\left(\left<\xi,Z\right>+\lambda\right)\right)(X,Y)Z+Z(\lambda)i_{\xi}\left(X\wedge Y\right),\quad\forall X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M),

(17)

see the proof of Lemma 2.5 in
[
7
]
.

We are now ready to give necessary and sufficient conditions for a triple
(\pi,N,\phi)
to be a PqN structure on an oriented three-dimensional manifold
(M,\mathcal{V})
.

Proposition 12
.

Let
(M,\pi)
be an oriented three-dimensional manifold with volume form
\mathcal{V}
.
Suppose that
\pi
never vanishes.
Then the quadruple
(M,\pi,N,\phi)
is a PqN manifold if and only if
N
can be decomposed as
N=\lambda I+Z\otimes\xi
, where
\xi=i_{\pi}\mathcal{V}
,
Z\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
,
\lambda\in C^{\infty}(M)
and the following conditions hold:

d\left(\lambda+\left<\xi,Z\right>\right)=\operatorname{div}(Z)\xi\quad\text{and}\quad\phi=-Z(\lambda)\mathcal{V}.

(18)

Proof.

As seen in the first item of Remark
11
,
the pair
(\pi,N)
is compatible if and only if

N=\lambda I+Z\otimes\xi\quad\text{and}\quad d(\lambda+\langle\xi,Z\rangle)=\operatorname{div}(Z)\xi.

It remains to prove that
\phi=-Z(\lambda)\mathcal{V}
. To this end, we first observe that (
17
) yields

T_{N}(X,Y)=\cancel{\left(\xi\wedge\operatorname{div}(Z)\xi\right)}(X,Y)Z+Z(\lambda)i_{\xi}(X\wedge Y)

i.e.,

T_{N}(X,Y)=Z(\lambda)i_{\xi}(X\wedge Y).

(19)

Now, for every
\eta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
,

\displaystyle\left<\eta,\pi^{\sharp}(i_{X\wedge Y}\phi)\right>

\displaystyle=\pi\left(i_{X\wedge Y}\phi,\eta\right)=\left<\left(i_{X\wedge Y}\phi\right)\wedge\eta,\pi\right>=\left<\left(i_{Y}\left(i_{X}\phi\right)\right)\wedge\eta,\pi\right>

\displaystyle=\left<i_{Y}\left(\left(i_{X}\phi\right)\wedge\eta\right)-\left(i_{X}\phi\right)\wedge\left(i_{Y}\eta\right),\pi\right>=\left<i_{Y}\left(\left(i_{X}\phi\right)\wedge\eta\right),\pi\right>-\left<\eta,Y\right>\left<i_{X}\phi,\pi\right>

\displaystyle=\left<i_{Y}\left(i_{X}\left(\cancel{\phi\wedge\eta}\right)+\phi\wedge\left(i_{X}\eta\right)\right),\pi\right>-\left<\eta,Y\right>\left<i_{X}\phi,\pi\right>

\displaystyle=\left<\eta,X\right>\left<i_{Y}\phi,\pi\right>-\left<\eta,Y\right>\left<i_{\pi}\phi,X\right>=\left<\eta,X\right>\left<i_{\pi}\phi,Y\right>-\left<\eta,Y\right>\left<i_{\pi}\phi,X\right>

\displaystyle=\left<\eta,\left<i_{\pi}\phi,Y\right>X-\left<i_{\pi}\phi,X\right>Y\right>=-\left<\eta,i_{i_{\pi}\phi}(X\wedge Y)\right>,

so that

\pi^{\sharp}(i_{X\wedge Y}\phi)=-i_{i_{\pi}\phi}(X\wedge Y).

(20)

Thanks to (
19
) and (
20
), we have that
T(N)(X,Y)=\pi^{\sharp}(i_{X\wedge Y}\phi)

if and only if
i_{i_{\pi}\phi}(X\wedge Y)=-i_{Z(\lambda)\xi}(X\wedge Y)
. Since
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
are arbitrary, this is equivalent to

i_{\pi}\phi=-Z(\lambda)\xi.

But
\phi=f\mathcal{V}
for some
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, and

\xi=i_{\pi}\mathcal{V}
, so we arrive at the condition
(f+Z(\lambda))\xi=0
, which is equivalent to

since
\xi
never vanishes — see Remark
9
.
We can therefore conclude that
T_{N}(X,Y)=\pi^{\sharp}(i_{X\wedge Y}\phi)
if and only if
\phi=-Z(\lambda)\mathcal{V}
.
∎

Remark 13
.

It is worth making a comment about the independence of the PqN structure from the chosen volume form
\mathcal{V}
. First note that if
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
is another volume form, then
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}=f\mathcal{V}
for a given never-vanishing smooth function

. This implies the equalities
\xi^{\prime}=f\xi
,
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda
, and

, so that
d(\lambda+\left<\xi,Z\right>)=d(\lambda^{\prime}+\left<\xi^{\prime},Z^{\prime}\right>)
and

{\operatorname{div}}_{\mathcal{V}}\left(Z\right)\xi={\operatorname{div}}_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}}\left(Z\right)\xi^{\prime}
, see Remark 2.3 in
[
7
]
. Finally,

\phi^{\prime}=-Z^{\prime}(\lambda)\mathcal{V}^{\prime}=-Z(\lambda)\mathcal{V}=\phi,

proving that the PqN-structure is independent of the choice of the volume form.

Example 15

Locally, every PqN structure on an oriented three-dimensional manifold with a never vanishing Poisson bivector is obtained by deforming a PN manifold by means of a closed 2-form.

We can now prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 17
.

Every three-dimensional oriented PqN manifold with a never vanishing Poisson tensor is involutive.

The proof of this statement is based on a closed formula for the 1-forms
\phi_{k}
, see (

), which is contained in the following

Lemma 18
.

On an oriented three-dimensional PqN manifold, where
N=\lambda I+Z\otimes\xi
, we have that

\phi_{k}=2Z(\lambda)\lambda^{k}\xi\qquad\mbox{for all $k\geq 0$.}

(26)

Proof.

By induction on
k
, one proves (see Section
5.2
) that

N^{k}=\lambda^{k}I+\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}{k\choose l}\lambda^{l}\langle\xi,Z\rangle^{k-l-1}Z\otimes\xi.

(27)

Moreover, since

T_{N}(X,Y)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}Z(\lambda)i_{\xi}(X\wedge Y)=Z(\lambda)\langle\xi,X\rangle Y-Z(\lambda)\langle\xi,Y\rangle X,

one has that

i_{X}T_{N}=Z(\lambda)(\langle\xi,X\rangle I-X\otimes\xi).

(28)

For every
k\geq 0
and
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, we can now compute

\begin{split}\langle\phi_{k},X\rangle&\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\text{Tr}(N^{k}i_{X}T_{N})\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\text{Tr}\big(N^{k}(Z(\lambda)\langle\xi,X\rangle I-Z(\lambda)X\otimes\xi)\big)\\
&\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\text{Tr}\Big[\Big(\lambda^{k}I+\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}{k\choose l}\lambda^{l}\langle\xi,Z\rangle^{k-l-1}Z\otimes\xi\Big)\Big(Z(\lambda)\langle\xi,X\rangle I-Z(\lambda)X\otimes\xi)\Big)\Big]\\
&=\text{Tr}\Big[Z(\lambda)\lambda^{k}\langle\xi,X\rangle I-Z(\lambda)\lambda^{k}X\otimes\xi+Z(\lambda)\langle\xi,X\rangle\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}{k\choose l}\lambda^{l}\langle\xi,Z\rangle^{k-l-1}Z\otimes\xi\\
&-Z(\lambda)\sum_{l=1}^{k-1}{k\choose l}\lambda^{l}\langle\xi,Z\rangle^{k-l-1}\langle\xi,X\rangle Z\otimes\xi\Big],\end{split}

where, in the last sum,
\langle\xi,X\rangle Z\otimes\xi
comes from the computation

\left((Z\otimes\xi)\circ(X\otimes\xi)\right)(Y)=\langle\xi,Y\rangle(Z\otimes\xi)(X)=\langle\xi,Y\rangle\langle\xi,X\rangle Z\qquad\forall Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M),

which yields the identity
(Z\otimes\xi)\circ(X\otimes\xi)=\langle\xi,X\rangle Z\otimes\xi
. Now, recalling that
\text{Tr}(Z\otimes\xi)=\langle\xi,Z\rangle
and
\text{Tr}(I)=3
, the last term of the previous chain of identities becomes

\begin{split}&3Z(\lambda)\lambda^{k}\langle\xi,X\rangle-\lambda^{k}Z(\lambda)\langle\xi,X\rangle+\cancel{Z(\lambda)\langle\xi,X\rangle\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}{k\choose l}\lambda^{l}\langle\xi,Z\rangle^{k-l}}\cancel{-Z(\lambda)\langle\xi,X\rangle\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}{k\choose l}\lambda^{l}\langle\xi,Z\rangle^{k-l}}\\
&=2Z(\lambda)\lambda^{k}\langle\xi,X\rangle,\end{split}

which entails
\phi_{k}=2Z(\lambda)\lambda^{k}\xi
.
∎

With this result at hand, we can go back to the

Proof.

(of Theorem
17
) We want to prove that
\{I_{k},I_{j}\}=0
for all
k,j
with
k>j
.
Since the 1-forms
\phi_{k}
are multiple of
\xi
, see (
26
), and
\pi^{\sharp}\xi=0
, see (
13
),
the right-hand side of (
11
) is zero. In other words, that identity collapses to

\{I_{k},I_{j}\}=\{I_{k-1},I_{j+1}\}.

If
k-1=j+1
we are done, otherwise one can apply again this argument, which, after a finite number of steps, will entail the thesis.
∎

We close this section with the following remark, where we compare the result just obtained with Theorem
6
, giving sufficient conditions for the involutivity of a (general) PqN manifold.

Applying
\pi^{\sharp}
to both sides of (

4
PqN and Haantjes structures

We start this section recalling the definition of Haantjes torsion of a tensor field of type
(1,1)
, then we introduce the notion of Haantjes manifold and we discuss how this relates to the geometry of PqN manifolds.

Let
N
be a (1,1) tensor field. Recall that its Nijenhuis torsion
T_{N}
is the tensor field of type
(1,2)
, i.e., the 2-form with values in
TM
, defined in (
1
). To address the problem of the Frobenius integrability of 2-dimensional planes generated by the eigenvectors of
N
(see Subsection
5.3

and the references therein),
Haantjes introduced in
[
15
]
what is now known as the
Haantjes torsion
of
N
. This is the tensor field

H_{N}
of type
(1,2)
defined by the formula

H_{N}(X,Y)=T_{N}(NX,NY)-N(T_{N}(NX,Y)+T_{N}(X,NY)-NT_{N}(X,Y)),\qquad X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M),

(29)

where
T_{N}
is the Nijenhuis torsion of
N
. The tensor
N
will be called a Haantjes operator if
H_{N}\equiv 0
.

In
[
4
]
, Bogoyavlenskij derived algebraic identities for Nijenhuis and Haantjes torsions on an arbitrary manifold. We review some of these results, which will be used subsequently, in the following

Lemma 20

(
[
4
]
, Corollaries 1, 3, 4)
.

Let
N
be a (1,1) tensor field on a manifold
M
and let
p(x,t)=\sum_{m=0}^{k}a_{m}(x)t^{m}
be a polynomial with coefficients in
C^{\infty}(M)
. Then:

1.

The Nijenhuis torsion of
p(x,N)
is given by the formula

T_{p(x,N)}(X,Y)=\sum_{m=1}^{k}a_{m}\sum_{p+q=m-1}N^{p}T_{N}(N^{q}X,Y)+\sum_{m=0}^{k}\left(Y(a_{m})N^{m+1}X-NY(a_{m})N^{m}X\right).

(30)

2.

If
N
is a Haantjes operator, then
p(x,N)
is also a Haantjes operator.

3.

For all
f,\,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
we have

H_{fp(x,I)+gN}(X,Y)=g^{4}H_{N}(X,Y).

(31)

The next example presents a formula for the Haantjes torsion of the tensor product of a vector field and a 1-form.

Example 21
.

Let
M
be a manifold and let
W\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and
\eta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. If we consider the (1,1) tensor
N=W\otimes\eta
, then its Nijenhuis torsion
T_{N}
, see Lemma 2.5 in
[
7
]
, is given by

T_{N}(X,Y)=\left[\left<\eta,X\right>Y(\left<\eta,W\right>)-\left<\eta,Y\right>X(\left<\eta,W\right>)-(\eta\wedge d\eta)(W,X,Y)\right]W,\qquad X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M).

(32)

After a computation using (
32
) we obtain

\displaystyle T_{N}(NX,NY)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle NT_{N}(NX,Y)

\displaystyle=\left<\eta,W\right>\left[\left<\eta,X\right>\left<\eta,W\right>Y(\left<\eta,W\right>)-\left<\eta,Y\right>\left<\eta,X\right>W(\left<\eta,W\right>)\right]W,

\displaystyle NT_{N}(X,NY)

\displaystyle=\left<\eta,W\right>\left[\left<\eta,X\right>\left<\eta,Y\right>W(\left<\eta,W\right>)-\left<\eta,Y\right>\left<\eta,W\right>X(\left<\eta,W\right>)\right]W,

\displaystyle N^{2}T_{N}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\left<\eta,W\right>^{2}\left[(\left<\eta,X\right>Y(\left<\eta,W\right>)-\left<\eta,Y\right>X(\left<\eta,W\right>)-(\eta\wedge d\eta)(W,X,Y)\right]W.

Then, summing up the above four equations, according to formula (
29
) we obtain

H_{W\otimes\eta}(X,Y)=-\left<\eta,W\right>^{2}(\eta\wedge d\eta)(W,X,Y)W.

(33)

As we already observed, the presence of a PN or, more generally, of a bi-Hamiltonian structure on
M
, makes it possible to introduce a class of distinguished vector fields (called bi-Hamiltonian), which possess a large number of conserved quantities which are mutually in involution with respect to the underlying Poisson structures. In particular, in the PN case, the presence of the recursion operator defines a natural space where to look for the Lenard-Magri chains. In the attempt to weaken the hypothesis on the recursion operator to be torsion free, but still keeping the possibility to define a notion of Lenard-Magri chain, Magri introduced in
[
19
]
the notion of Haantjes manifold, which we recall in

Definition 22
.

A Haantjes structure on a manifold
M
is a pair
(N,\theta)
of a
(1,1)
tensor field
N

and a 1-form
\theta
such that:

(H1)

H_{N}=0
;

(H2)

d\theta=0
;

(H3)

d_{N}\theta=0
;

(H4)

\langle\theta,T_{N}(X,Y)\rangle=0
for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
.

A manifold endowed with a Haantjes structure is called a Haantjes manifold. If
\theta=0
, the corresponding Haantjes structures is called trivial.

We can now state and prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 23
.

If
(\pi,N,\phi)
is a PqN structure on an oriented three-dimensional manifold
(M,\mathcal{V})
and
\pi
never vanishes, then
M
admits a Haantjes structure
(N,\theta)
.

Proof.

We know from Proposition
12
that

N=\lambda I+Z\otimes\xi
, where
\xi=i_{\pi}\mathcal{V}
,
\lambda\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
Z\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
are related by the first equation of (
18
). We claim that the pair
(N,\theta)
, where

\theta=\text{div}(Z)\xi,

(34)

is a Haantjes structure on
M
.
The exactness of
\theta
, see (
18
), gives us (H2). Now observe that, for every
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
,

i_{N}\theta(X)=\langle\theta,NX\rangle=\langle\theta,\lambda X+\langle\xi,X\rangle Z\rangle=\lambda\langle\theta,X\rangle+\langle\theta,Z\rangle\langle\xi,X\rangle=(\lambda+\langle\xi,Z\rangle)\langle\theta,X\rangle,

which yields the identity

i_{N}\theta=(\lambda+\langle\xi,Z\rangle)\theta.

(35)

Since
d\theta=0
, one has that

d_{N}\theta=-d(i_{N}\theta)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}-d((\lambda+\langle\xi,Z\rangle)\theta)=-d(\lambda+\langle\xi,Z\rangle)\wedge\text{div}(Z)\xi\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}-\left(\text{div}(Z)\right)^{2}(\xi\wedge\xi)=0,

proving (H3). Moreover,

\langle\theta,T_{N}(X,Y)\rangle\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}Z(\lambda)\text{div}(Z)\langle\xi,i_{\xi}(X\wedge Y)\rangle=Z(\lambda)\text{div}(Z)\langle\xi,\langle\xi,X\rangle Y-\langle\xi,Y\rangle X\rangle=0

for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, proving (H4). We are left to prove (H1).
Applying (
31
) to
N=\lambda I+Z\otimes\xi
and using (
33
), we obtain

H_{N}(X,Y)=H_{Z\otimes\xi}(X,Y)=-\left<\xi,Z\right>^{2}\left(\xi\wedge d\xi\right)(Z,X,Y)Z,

which vanishes identically, see Proposition
8
.
∎

Remark 24
.

1.

In the same hypotheses of the previous theorem, Remark
9
implies that
the Haantjes structure
(N,\theta)
is non-trivial if the divergence of the vector field
Z
is not zero.

2.

For a general PqN structure
(\pi,N,\phi)
, the Haantjes torsion of
N
does not vanish. An example is given by the case of the periodic Toda lattice, see (
12
).

Remark 25
.

A Haantjes structure
(N,\theta)
on an oriented three-dimensional manifold cannot always be derived from a PqN structure
(N,\pi,\phi)
, since a non-trivial Poisson bivector
\pi
such that
N\circ\pi^{\sharp}=\pi^{\sharp}\circ N^{*}
does not always exist. Indeed, consider
(see Example 2 in
[
20
]
) the (1,1) tensor field

N=-\frac{y}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\otimes dz+2\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\otimes dx-z\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\otimes dz+2\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\otimes dy.

It can be checked that its Haantjes torsion vanishes and that its Nijenhuis torsion

T_{N}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\otimes dz\otimes dx-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\otimes dx\otimes dz+\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\otimes dz\otimes dy-\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\otimes dy\otimes dz

satisfies
\left<dz,T_{N}(X,Y)\right>=0
for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Moreover,
d_{N}(dz)=-d(i_{N}dz)=0
since
i_{N}dz=2dy
.
Therefore the pair
(N,dz)
is a Haantjes structure on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
However, a simple computation shows that a non-trivial Poisson bivector
\pi
such that

\pi^{\sharp}\circ N^{*}=N\circ\pi^{\sharp}
does not exist.
This entails that the Haantjes structure
(N,dz)
cannot be derived from a PqN structure.

4.1
Generalized Lenard-Magri chains

Haantjes manifolds were introduced by Magri in
[
19
]
with the aim of generalizing the notion of Lenard-Magri chain, see Definition
26
below. In this more general geometrical setting, the sequence of Nijenhuis torsionless tensors

\{N^{i}\}
, appearing in the PN case, is traded for a family of
(1,1)
tensors with vanishing Haantjes torsion. More precisely,

Definition 26

(Generalized Lenard-Magri chains)
.

A generalized Lenard-Magri chain of length
p
on a Haantjes manifold
M
with Haantjes structure
(N,\theta)
is a family

\mathcal{N}=\{N_{0},N_{1},\dots,N_{p-1}\}
of
(1,1)
tensor fields satisfying, for all
i,j=0,\dots,p-1
:

(C0)

N_{0}=I
and
N_{1}=N
;

(C1)

H_{N_{i}}=0
;

(C2)

N_{i}\circ N_{j}=N_{j}\circ N_{i}
;

(C3)

d_{N_{i}}\theta=0
;

(C4)

\langle\theta,T_{N_{i}}(X,Y)\rangle=0

for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
.

The relevance of these conditions relies on the remarkable observation that, on a Haantjes manifold endowed with a generalized Lenard-Magri chain, the 1-forms
\theta_{ij}=N_{i}N_{j}\theta
are closed — see Proposition 1 in
[
19
]
.

Remark 27
.

The name generalized Lenard-Magri chain is not universally adopted. In particular, in
[
19
]
the structure introduced in Definition
26
is simply called a Lenard chain.

Example 28
.

Considering the same (1,1) tensor field
N
as in Remark
25
, we notice that

N^{2}=4\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\otimes dx-\left(y\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+2z\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)\otimes dy-\left(y\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+2z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)\otimes dz

has Nijenhuis torsion

T_{N^{2}}=-8\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\otimes dx\otimes dy+8\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\otimes dy\otimes dx-8\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\otimes dx\otimes dz+8\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\otimes dz\otimes dx.

Thus
\mathcal{N}=\{I,N,N^{2}\}
is not a generalized Lenard-Magri chain since
\left<dz,T_{N^{2}}(X,Y)\right>
does not vanish for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Let
(N,\theta)
be the Haantjes structure, with
\theta
given by (
34
),
derived from a PqN-structure
(\pi,N,\phi)
on an oriented three-dimensional manifold
M

with volume form
\mathcal{V}
, see Theorem
23
. Then we have
a kind of canonical generalized Lenard-Magri chain.

Theorem 29
.

The family
\mathcal{N}=\{N^{0},N^{1},\dots,N^{p-1}\}

is a generalized Lenard-Magri chain associated to the Haantjes structure
(N,\theta)
.

Proof.

To prove the statement, one needs to check that conditions (C1), (C3) and (C4) are satisfied. To this end, first observe that Lemma
20
-2 entails that
H_{N^{k}}=0
for every
k\geq 2
, since
H_{N}=0
.
As far as (C3) is concerned,
we notice that, for every
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
,

\langle i_{N^{k}}\theta,X\rangle=\langle\theta,N^{k}X\rangle=\langle i_{N}\theta,N^{k-1}X\rangle\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}(\lambda+\langle\xi,Z\rangle)\langle i_{N^{k-1}}\theta,X\rangle,

which entails

i_{N^{k}}\theta=(\lambda+\langle\xi,Z\rangle)^{k}\theta.

(36)

Then, since
\theta
is closed,

d_{N^{k}}\theta=-di_{N^{k}}\theta=-d(\lambda+\langle\xi,Z\rangle)^{k}\theta=-k(\lambda+\langle\xi,Z\rangle)^{k-1}d(\lambda+\langle\xi,Z\rangle)\wedge\theta\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}0.

To prove that (C4) holds, we first
observe that (
30
) applied to
p(x,t)=t^{k}
yields

T_{N^{k}}(X,Y)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}N^{j}T_{N}(N^{k-j-1}X,Y)\qquad\forall X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M).

(37)

To analyze this sum, we notice that for any
j\geq 0
and any pair
(W,Y)
of vector fields we have

\displaystyle N^{j}(T_{N}(W,Y))

\displaystyle=N^{j}\left(Z(\lambda)i_{\xi}(W\wedge Y)\right)

\displaystyle=Z(\lambda)\left(\lambda^{j}i_{\xi}(W\wedge Y)+\sum_{r=0}^{j-1}\binom{j}{r}\lambda^{r}\left<\xi,Z\right>^{j-r-1}\cancel{(Z\otimes\xi)(i_{\xi}(W\wedge Y))}\right)

\displaystyle=Z(\lambda)\lambda^{j}i_{\xi}(W\wedge Y),

as a consequence of (
27
) and

(Z\otimes\xi)(i_{\xi}(W\wedge Y))=(Z\otimes\xi)(\langle\xi,W\rangle Y-\langle\xi,Y\rangle W)=(\langle\xi,W\rangle\langle\xi,Y\rangle-\langle\xi,Y\rangle\langle\xi,W\rangle)Z=0.

Then (C4) follows from (
37
) and from
\left<\theta,i_{\xi}(W\wedge Y)\right>=0
.
∎

4.2
Comparison with the work of Tempesta and Tondo

We will now make a few comments to link the theory presented in the previous sections with the recent works of Tempesta and Tondo, see
[
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
and
[
29
]
. We start recalling the notion of Haantjes algebra, introduced in
[
26
]
, Definition 36. This consists of a set of
(1,1)
tensor fields
\{N_{i}\}_{i\in I}
on a manifold
M

satisfying the following two properties:

•

H_{fN_{i}+gN_{j}}=0
, for all
i,j\in I
and
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
;

•

H_{N_{i}N_{j}}=0=H_{N_{j}N_{i}}
, for all
i,j\in I
.

A Haantjes algebra will be called of rank
m
if the corresponding
C^{\infty}(M)
-module has rank equal to
m
. The Haantjes algebra will be called abelian if the operators
N_{i}
pairwise commute. An important class of abelian Haantjes algebras are the cyclic ones, i.e., the Haantjes algebras generated by a given Haantjes operator. A cyclic Haantjes algebra has rank
m
if the minimal polynomial of its generator has degree
m
.

After these preliminary comments, we easily obtain

Proposition 30
.

If
(\pi,N,\phi)
is a PqN structure on an oriented three-dimensional manifold and
\pi
never vanishes,
then
N
generates a rank-two cyclic Haantjes algebra.

Proof.

As we already noticed in Theorem
23
,
N
is a Haantjes operator. Then
\{N^{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
is a cyclic Haantjes algebra as a consequence of Lemma
20
-2. Moreover, the rank of such algebra is two since the minimal polynomial of
N=\lambda I+Z\otimes\xi
is
p(t)=t^{2}-(\left<\xi,Z\right>+2\lambda)t+\lambda^{2}+\left<\xi,Z\right>\lambda
.
∎

Given a Haantjes algebra of rank
m
and a set
N_{1},\dots,N_{m}
of its generators (as a
C^{\infty}(M)
-module), a closed 1-form
\alpha
generates a so called
Haantjes chain

(N_{1}^{\ast}\alpha,\dots,N_{m}^{\ast}\alpha)
of length
m
if

d_{N_{k}}\alpha=0,

i.e., if
N^{\ast}_{k}\alpha=i_{N_{k}}\alpha
is closed for all
k=1,\dots,m
, and the forms
N_{k}^{\ast}\alpha
, with
k=1,\dots,m
, are linearly independent, see Definition 15 in
[
28
]
.

If
(\pi,N,\phi)
is as in Proposition
30
, where
N=\lambda I+Z\otimes\xi

and
\theta
given as in (
34
),
then the 1-forms

\theta_{k}={N^{k}}^{\ast}\theta=i_{N^{k}}\theta
,
see (
36
), are closed but, clearly, they are not independent (since
\theta
is a common eigenvector of the
{N^{k}}^{\ast}
) and for this reason they fail to form a Haantjes chain.

Finally, it is worth mentioning that every PqN structure satisfying the hypothesis of Proposition
30
is a
Poisson-Haantjes structure
in the sense of Definition 6 in
[
29
]
.

5.1
Proof of the identity (
25
)

First notice that, given
\pi=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
and any closed 2-form

\Omega=\Omega_{12}dx\wedge dy+\Omega_{13}dx\wedge dz+\Omega_{23}dy\wedge dz
, we have

\frac{1}{2}\left[\Omega,\Omega\right]_{\pi}=\left(\Omega_{23}\frac{\partial\Omega_{12}}{\partial x}-\Omega_{13}\frac{\partial\Omega_{12}}{\partial y}+\Omega_{12}\frac{\partial\Omega_{12}}{\partial z}\right)\mathcal{V}.

In addition, for
N_{1}=\widetilde{Z}\otimes dz
as in Example
15
one has that

d_{N_{1}}\Omega=-di_{N_{1}}\Omega=-d\left(i_{N_{1}}(\Omega_{12}dx\wedge dy)+i_{N_{1}}(\Omega_{13}dx\wedge dz)+i_{N_{1}}(\Omega_{23}dy\wedge dz)\right).

(38)

We observe that

\displaystyle i_{N_{1}}(\Omega_{12}dx\wedge dy)(X,Y)

\displaystyle=(\Omega_{12}dx\wedge dy)\left(g(z)dz(X)\frac{\partial}{\partial z},Y\right)+(\Omega_{12}dx\wedge dy)\left(X,g(z)dz(Y)\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)=0,

\displaystyle i_{N_{1}}(\Omega_{13}dx\wedge dz)(X,Y)

\displaystyle=(\Omega_{13}dx\wedge dz)\left(g(z)dz(X)\frac{\partial}{\partial z},Y\right)+(\Omega_{13}dx\wedge dz)\left(X,g(z)dz(Y)\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)

\displaystyle=g(z)\Omega_{13}dx(X)dz(Y)-g(z)\Omega_{13}dx(Y)dz(X)

\displaystyle=g(z)(\Omega_{13}dx\wedge dz)(X,Y),

\displaystyle i_{N_{1}}(\Omega_{23}dy\wedge dz)(X,Y)

\displaystyle=(\Omega_{23}dy\wedge dz)\left(g(z)dz(X)\frac{\partial}{\partial z},Y\right)+(\Omega_{23}dy\wedge dz)\left(X,g(z)dz(Y)\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)

\displaystyle=g(z)\Omega_{23}dy(X)dz(Y)-g(z)\Omega_{23}dy(Y)dz(X)

\displaystyle=g(z)(\Omega_{23}dy\wedge dz)(X,Y).

By substituting the three preceding equations into (
38
), we obtain

\displaystyle d(i_{N_{1}}\Omega)

\displaystyle=d(g(z)\Omega-g(z)\Omega_{12}dx\wedge dy)=dg(z)\wedge\Omega-\frac{\partial\left(g(z)\Omega_{12}\right)}{\partial z}\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle=g(z)^{\prime}dz\wedge(\Omega_{12}dx\wedge dy+\Omega_{13}dx\wedge dz+\Omega_{23}dy\wedge dz)-\left(g(z)^{\prime}\Omega_{12}+g(z)\frac{\partial\Omega_{12}}{\partial z}\right)\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle=-g(z)\frac{\partial\Omega_{12}}{\partial z}\mathcal{V}.

Thus if
\Omega
is a closed 2-form on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, then

d_{N_{1}}\Omega+\frac{1}{2}\left[\Omega,\Omega\right]_{\pi}=\left(-g(z)\frac{\partial\Omega_{12}}{\partial z}+\Omega_{23}\frac{\partial\Omega_{12}}{\partial x}-\Omega_{13}\frac{\partial\Omega_{12}}{\partial y}+\Omega_{12}\frac{\partial\Omega_{12}}{\partial z}\right)\mathcal{V}.

(39)

In particular, the 2-form
\Omega
given in Example
15
is closed and from (
39
) we derive

d_{N_{1}}\Omega+\frac{1}{2}\left[\Omega,\Omega\right]_{\pi}=(g(z)\lambda_{z}-a\lambda_{x}-b\lambda_{y}+\lambda\lambda_{z})\mathcal{V}=-Z(\lambda)\mathcal{V}=\phi,

since
\Omega_{12}=-\lambda,\Omega_{13}=-b,\Omega_{23}=a
and
\lambda+c=g(z)
.

5.2
Proof of the identity (
27
)

The proof is done by induction, noticing that for
k=1
one has
N=\lambda I+Z\otimes\xi
, which is the content of Theorem
10
.
Suppose that (
27
) holds for
k
and let us prove it for
k+1
. We compute

\displaystyle N^{k+1}(X)=(\lambda I+Z\otimes\xi)\Big(\lambda^{k}X+\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}{k\choose l}\lambda^{l}\langle\xi,Z\rangle^{k-l-1}\langle\xi,X\rangle Z\Big),

\displaystyle=\lambda^{k+1}X+\underbrace{\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}{k\choose l}\lambda^{l+1}\langle\xi,Z\rangle^{k-l-1}\langle\xi,X\rangle Z}_{(1)}+\underbrace{\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}{k\choose l}\lambda^{l}\langle\xi,Z\rangle^{k-l}\langle\xi,X\rangle Z+\lambda^{k}\langle\xi,X\rangle Z}_{(2)}.

(40)

Now we note that in (
40
) one has

\displaystyle(1)

\displaystyle\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\sum_{s=1}^{k}{k\choose s-1}\lambda^{s}\langle\xi,Z\rangle^{k-s}\langle\xi,X\rangle Z,

(41)

\displaystyle(2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{k}{k\choose l}\lambda^{l}\langle\xi,Z\rangle^{k-l}\langle\xi,X\rangle Z=\sum_{l=1}^{k}{k\choose l}\lambda^{l}\langle\xi,Z\rangle^{k-l}\langle\xi,X\rangle Z+\langle\xi,Z\rangle^{k}\langle\xi,Z\rangle Z.

(42)

By renaming
l
the running variable
s
in (
41
), plugging (
41
) and (
42
) in (
40
),
and using the identity
{k\choose l-1}+{k\choose l}={k+1\choose l}
, one obtains

\displaystyle N^{k+1}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{k+1}X+\sum_{l=1}^{k}\Big[{k\choose l-1}+{k\choose l}\Big]\lambda^{l}\langle\xi,Z\rangle^{k-l}\langle\xi,X\rangle Z+\langle\xi,Z\rangle^{k}\langle\xi,Z\rangle Z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{k+1}X+\sum_{l=1}^{k}{k+1\choose l}\lambda^{l}\langle\xi,Z\rangle^{k-l}\langle\xi,X\rangle Z+\langle\xi,Z\rangle^{k}\langle\xi,Z\rangle Z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{k+1}X+\sum_{l=0}^{k}{k+1\choose l}\lambda^{l}\langle\xi,Z\rangle^{k-l}\langle\xi,X\rangle Z,

which, being
X
arbitrary, entails the identity (
27
).

5.3
Recollection on the Haantjes torsion

To put the definition (
29
) of the Haantjes torsion in context, we need to introduce a few notions, which we enclose in the following

Definition 31

(See
[
26
]
and
[
2
]
)
.

•

A frame over the open set
U\subset M
is a collection
{\mathcal{X}}:=\{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\}
of
n=\dim M
vector fields which generate the tangent space at each point of
U
.

•

Two frames
\mathcal{X}:=\{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\}
and
\mathcal{Y}:=\{Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}\}
over
U
are called equivalent if there exists a collection of never vanishing smooth functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
over
U
such that
X_{i}=f_{i}Y_{i}
, for all
i=1,\dots,n
.

•

A frame
\mathcal{X}=\{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\}
over
U
is called integrable if for all
p\in U
there exists a chart around
p
,
say
(V,x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
, such that the restriction of
\mathcal{X}
to
V
is equivalent to the frame
\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\}
.

•

A tensor field
N
of type
(1,1)
is called semi-simple if each point
p\in M
has a neighborhood
U
where one can find a frame formed by eigenvectors of
N
. Such a frame is said to be an eigenframe for
N
.

It is also worth recalling

Proposition 32
.

A reference frame
\mathcal{X}=\{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\}
on
U
is integrable if and only if one of the two following equivalent conditions is verified:

1.

For every
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, the distribution generated by
\{X_{i},X_{j}\}
is Frobenius integrable.

2.

The distribution generated by
\mathcal{X}\backslash\{X_{i}\}
is Frobenius integrable.

The integrability of eigenframes for a given
(1,1)
tensor field is controlled by the Haantjes torsion of
N
.
More precisely, the vanishing of the Haantjes torsion of
N
is a necessary and sufficient condition for the integrability of its eigenframes, see Theorem 25 in
[
26
]
and references therein.

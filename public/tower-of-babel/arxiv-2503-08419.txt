Reconstruction theorems in the supported case
Luigi Lombardi

Abstract
. We show that any equivalence of bounded derived categories of coherent sheaves on a smooth projective complex variety supported in a closed algebraic subset preserves the dimension of the support in two cases: (i) the restriction of the (anti)canonical bundle to the support is ample; (ii) the supports are irreducible and the equivalence sends a skyscraper sheaf of a closed point to a skyscraper sheaf of a closed point. Moreover, in the first case the equivalence recovers the set of closed points of the support up to homeomorphism.



1.
Introduction

We study instances regarding the invariance of the dimension of the support
under exact equivalences of bounded derived categories of coherent sheaves supported in a closed algebraic subset.

Let
X
and
Y
be smooth projective complex varieties and let
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
and
\mathrm{D}^{b}({\rm Coh}(Y))

be the bounded derived categories of coherent sheaves on
X
and
Y
, respectively.
Under certain conditions
\mathrm{D}^{b}({\rm Coh}(X))
reconstructs the underlying variety.
In this direction we recall the celebrated Bondal–Orlov’s reconstruction theorem: If the (anti)canonical bundle of
X
is ample, then
any exact equivalence
F\colon\mathrm{D}^{b}({\rm Coh}(X))\to\mathrm{D}^{b}({\rm Coh}(Y))
induces an isomorphism of varieties
X\simeq Y
(
cf
.

[
BO01
]
; see also
[
Fav12
,
Ito25
]
for the non-proper case and
[
Bal11
,
IM24
,
SdSSdS12
]
for the Gorenstein case).
By weakening the hypotheses one obtains coarser notions of reconstruction. For instance, if the (anti)canonical bundle is big, or
if the equivalence
F
sends a skyscraper sheaf of a closed point to a skyscraper sheaf of a closed point,
then
X
and
Y
are
K
-equivalent, and in particular birational
(
cf
.
[
Kaw02
, Theorem 1.4]
,
[
CL25
, Theorem 1.1]
,
[
Tod06
, Lemma 7.3]
and
[
LLZ24
, Theorem 1.1]
).

Given algebraic closed subsets
Z\subset X
and
W\subset Y
, in this paper
we consider the bounded derived categories
\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}({\rm Coh}(X))
and
\mathrm{D}_{W}^{b}({\rm Coh}(Y))
of complexes of coherent sheaves with
cohomologies supported in
Z
and
W
, respectively.
By the existence of the Serre functor, it is easy to check that any exact equivalence

F\colon\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))\to\mathrm{D}_{W}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))

preserves the dimensions of
X
and

Y
(Corollary
2.3
). The following theorem implies the invariance of the dimension of the support under a positivity condition
on the canonical bundle
\omega_{X}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}({\rm Coh}(X))\simeq\mathrm{D}_{W}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))

and
\omega_{X}|_{Z}
, or
\omega_{X}^{-1}|_{Z}
, is ample, then the sets of closed points
Z({\mathbb{C}})
and
W({\mathbb{C}})
are homeomorphic.

Instances of equivalences of type
\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))\simeq\mathrm{D}_{W}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))
as in Theorem
1.1

appear in
[
Tod06
, Lemma 4.6]
where it is showed that any equivalence

\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))\simeq\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))
induces isomorphisms of
linear series
\big|\omega_{X}^{\otimes k}\big|\simeq\big|\omega_{Y}^{\otimes k}\big|
(for all

k\in\mathbb{Z}
) and equivalences

(1)

\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{Supp}(E)}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))\;\simeq\;\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{Supp}(E^{\prime})}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))

for any pair of corresponding divisors

E\in\big|\omega_{X}^{\otimes k}\big|

and
E^{\prime}\in\big|\omega_{Y}^{\otimes k}\big|
(see also Example (
3.9
)).

In the second main theorem of the paper we consider an
equivalence

\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))\simeq\mathrm{D}_{W}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))

that sends a skyscraper sheaf of a closed point to a skyscraper sheaf of a closed point as in
[
Tod06
]
.
We prove that if the supports are irreducible then they have
the same dimension. In fact something stronger holds, and
in general we speculate the invariance of the dimension of the support under an arbitrary equivalence.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
Z\subset X
and
W\subset Y
are algebraic closed irreducible subsets and let

F\colon\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))\to\mathrm{D}_{W}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))
be an equivalence.
If
F
sends a skyscraper sheaf of a closed point in
Z
to a skyscraper sheaf of a closed point in
W
, then there exist Zariski open dense subsets
U\subset Z

and
V\subset W
such that the sets of closed points
U({\mathbb{C}})
and
V({\mathbb{C}})
are homeomorphic.
Moreover, if
\dim W=1
, then the sets of closed points
Z({\mathbb{C}})
and
W({\mathbb{C}})
are homeomorphic.

Notation

We work over the field of complex numbers. All categories and functors are
\mathbb{C}
-linear and
any functor of triangulated categories is exact as in
[
Huy06
, Definition 1.39]
.
Moreover, we denote by
X({\mathbb{C}})
the set of closed points of a scheme
X
.

2.
The Serre functor

Let
X
be a smooth projective algebraic variety over
\mathbb{C}
.
We denote by
{\rm D}^{b}(\mathrm{Qcoh}(X))
and
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
the bounded derived
categories of quasi-coherent and coherent sheaves on
X
, respectively.
For a complex
{\mathcal{F}}
in
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Qcoh}(X))
, we denote by
{\mathcal{H}}^{j}({\mathcal{F}})
the cohomologies of
{\mathcal{F}}
and
by

\mathrm{Supp}({\mathcal{F}}):=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Supp}({\mathcal{H}}^{j}({\mathcal{F}}))\subset X

its support.
If
{\mathcal{F}}
is in
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
, then
\mathrm{Supp}({\mathcal{F}})
is an algebraic closed subset and for any closed point
p\in X
:

p\in\mathrm{Supp}({\mathcal{F}})\;\Leftrightarrow\;\mathrm{RHom}({\mathcal{F}},{\mathbb{C}}(p))\neq 0

(see
[
LLZ24
, Definition 2.2 and Lemma 2.4]
or
[
Huy06
, Exercise 3.30]
).

If
Z\subset X
is an algebraic closed subset, we denote by

\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}({\rm Qcoh}(X))
and
\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
the bounded derived categories of complexes of
quasi-coherent and coherent sheaves on
X
, respectively, with cohomologies supported in
Z
.
Following
[
Bal11
, Corollary 3.4]
, if
\mathrm{Coh}_{Z}(X)
denotes the abelian category of coherent sheaves supported in
Z
, then
there is an equivalence of derived categories

(2)

\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))\;\simeq\;\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}_{Z}(X)).

Moreover there is a natural inclusion functor

\iota_{Z}\colon\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Qcoh}(X))\to\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Qcoh}(X))

which admits a right adjoint

\iota_{Z}^{!}\colon\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Qcoh}(X))\to\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Qcoh}(X))

(
cf.

[
CS14
, p. 1353]
,
[
Lip02
, §3]
,
[
Nee96
]
).
Hence there are natural isomorphisms

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Qcoh}(X))}(\iota_{Z}{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}})\;\simeq\;\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{D}^{b}_{Z}(\mathrm{Qcoh}(X))}({\mathcal{F}},\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{G}})

for all objects
{\mathcal{F}}
in
{\rm D}^{b}_{Z}({\rm Qcoh}(X))
and

{\mathcal{G}}
in
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Qcoh}(X))
.
The support of a complex
{\mathcal{F}}
in
\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
is the algebraic closed subset

\mathrm{Supp}({\mathcal{F}})\;:=\;\mathrm{Supp}(\iota_{Z}{\mathcal{F}})\;\subset\;Z.

Proposition 2.1
.

The following formulas hold for all
{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}\in\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Qcoh}(X))
:

(i).

\iota_{Z}\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{F}}\simeq{\mathcal{F}}
if and only if
\mathrm{Supp}({\mathcal{F}})\subset Z
,

(ii).

\iota_{Z}\iota_{Z}^{!}({\mathcal{F}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}{{\otimes}}{\mathcal{G}})\simeq{\mathcal{F}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}{{\otimes}}\iota_{Z}\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{G}}
.

Proof.

The first assertion is the global version of
[
Lip02
, Corollary 3.2.1]
, while
the second one is showed in
[
CS14
, Lemma 2.4]
.

∎

We now discuss the existence of a Serre functor in
\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
(
cf
.
[
Huy06
, Definition 1.28]
).
Consider the functor

S_{Z}\colon\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))\to\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X)),\quad\quad S_{Z}({\mathcal{F}})=\iota_{Z}^{!}(\iota_{Z}{\mathcal{F}}\otimes\omega_{X}[\dim X]).

Proposition 2.2
.

The functor
S_{Z}
is a Serre functor for
\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
an
{\mathcal{G}}
be arbitrary objects in
\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
.
We first check that
S_{Z}
is an equivalence.
To this end define
S_{Z}^{-1}({\mathcal{G}})=\iota_{Z}^{!}(\iota_{Z}{\mathcal{G}}\otimes\omega_{X}^{-1}[-\dim X])

and note the following isomorphisms (
cf
. Proposition
2.1
(ii)):

\displaystyle S_{Z}^{-1}(S_{Z}({\mathcal{F}}))\;=\;\iota_{Z}^{!}(\iota_{Z}\iota_{Z}^{!}(\iota_{Z}{\mathcal{F}}\otimes\omega_{X}[\dim X])\otimes\omega_{X}^{-1}[-\dim X])\simeq

\displaystyle\iota_{Z}^{!}\iota_{Z}\iota_{Z}^{!}(\iota_{Z}{\mathcal{F}}\otimes\omega_{X}[\dim X]\otimes\omega_{X}^{-1}[-\dim X])\simeq

\displaystyle\iota_{Z}^{!}\iota_{Z}{\mathcal{F}}\;\simeq\;{\mathcal{F}}.

Similarly, one checks that
S_{Z}(S_{Z}^{-1}({\mathcal{G}}))\simeq{\mathcal{G}}
.
Finally, the following isomorphisms hold thanks to Serre duality on
X
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}({\mathcal{G}},S_{Z}({\mathcal{F}}))\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}(\iota_{Z}{\mathcal{G}},\iota_{Z}\iota_{Z}^{!}(\iota_{Z}{\mathcal{F}}\otimes\omega_{X}[\dim X]))\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}(\iota_{Z}{\mathcal{G}},(\iota_{Z}\iota_{Z}^{!}\iota_{Z}{\mathcal{F}}\otimes\omega_{X}[\dim X]))\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}(\iota_{Z}{\mathcal{G}},\iota_{Z}{\mathcal{F}}\otimes\omega_{X}[\dim X])\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}(\iota_{Z}{\mathcal{F}},\iota_{Z}{\mathcal{G}})^{*}\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}})^{*}.

∎

Let
Y
be another smooth projective algebraic variety and let
W\subset Y
be an algebraic closed subset.

Corollary 2.3
.

If
F\colon\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))\to\mathrm{D}_{W}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))
is an equivalence, then
\dim X=\dim Y
.

Proof.

Note the isomorphism
S_{W}\circ F\simeq F\circ S_{Z}
(
[
Huy06
, Lemma 1.30]
) where

S_{W}
is the Serre functor of
\mathrm{D}_{W}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))
. Let
p\in Z
be a closed point and note the isomorphisms

\displaystyle\iota_{W}^{!}(\iota_{W}F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p))\otimes\omega_{Y}[\dim Y])\simeq

\displaystyle S_{W}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p)))\simeq

\displaystyle F(S_{Z}(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p)))\simeq

\displaystyle F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p)[\dim X])\simeq

\displaystyle F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p))[\dim X].

By applying
\iota_{W}
we find

\displaystyle\iota_{W}\iota_{W}^{!}(\iota_{W}F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p))\otimes\omega_{Y}[\dim Y])\simeq\iota_{W}F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p))[\dim X].

Moreover, by Proposition
2.1
(ii), the left-hand side is isomorphic to
\iota_{W}F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p))\otimes\omega_{Y}[\dim Y]
.
We conclude that

\iota_{W}F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p))\otimes\omega_{Y}\simeq\iota_{W}F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p))[\dim Y-\dim X].

This immediately implies
\dim X=\dim Y
as in
[
Huy06
, Proposition 4.1]
since
\iota_{W}F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p))
is a bounded complex.

∎

3.
Point like objects in the supported case

Let
Z\subset X
be as in the previous section.

Definition 3.1
.

An object
P
of
\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
is
point like
if:

(i).

S_{Z}(P)\simeq P[\dim X]
,

(ii).

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}(P,P[k])=0
for
k<0
, and

(iii).

{\rm Hom}_{\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}(P,P)=\mathbb{C}
.

An object satisfying the last condition is called
simple
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Any equivalence
F\colon\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))\to\mathrm{D}_{W}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))
sends a point like object to a point like object.

Proof.

The conditions
(ii)
and
(iii)
are easy to check. For
(i)
, let
P
be a point like object in
\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
.
As the Serre functor commutes with any equivalence (
[
Huy06
, Lemma 1.30]
), we have by Corollary
2.3
:

S_{W}(F(P))\simeq F(S_{Z}(P))\simeq F(P[\dim X])\simeq F(P)[\dim Y].

∎

Proposition 3.3
.

The objects isomorphic to
\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p)[m]
with
p\in Z
closed point and

m\in\mathbb{Z}
are point like objects in
\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
.

Proof.

We prove the proposition for
m=0
, the other cases being similar.
Let
p\in Z
be a closed point.
By Proposition
2.1

\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p)
satisfies the first condition of Definition
3.1
:

\displaystyle S_{Z}(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p))\simeq\iota_{Z}^{!}(\iota_{Z}\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p)\otimes\omega_{X}[\dim X])\simeq

\displaystyle\iota_{Z}^{!}\iota_{Z}\iota_{Z}^{!}({\mathbb{C}}(p)\otimes\omega_{X}[\dim X])\simeq

\displaystyle\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p)[\dim X].

For the second and third conditions we see that by adjunction

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p),\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p)[k])\simeq\mathrm{Hom}(\iota_{Z}\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p),{\mathbb{C}}(p)[k])\simeq\mathrm{Hom}({\mathbb{C}}(p),{\mathbb{C}}(p)[k])

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
These spaces are trivial for
k<0
and isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}
for
k=0
.
∎

We recall the following lemma
[
Huy06
, Lemma 4.5]
which will be helpful in the rest of the paper.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
P
be an object in
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
such that

\mathrm{Hom}(P,P)=\mathbb{C}
and
\mathrm{Hom}(P,P[k])=0
for
k<0
.
Moreover suppose
\mathrm{Supp}(P)
is zero-dimensional.
Then
P\simeq{\mathbb{C}}(p)[m]
for some closed point
p\in\mathrm{Supp}(P)
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We begin the classification of point like objects in
\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
when
\dim Z=0
.

Proposition 3.5
.

If
\dim Z=0
,
then the point like objects in
\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
are the objects isomorphic
to
\iota^{!}_{Z}{\mathbb{C}}(p)[m]
with
p\in Z
closed point and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Let
P
be a point like object in
\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
. Then
\iota_{Z}P
has zero-dimensional support and

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}(\iota_{Z}P,\iota_{Z}P[k])\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}(P,P[k])
is trivial for
k<0
,
and isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}
for
k=0
.
By Lemma
3.4
there is an isomorphism
\iota_{Z}P\simeq{\mathbb{C}}(p)[m]
for some closed point
p\in Z
and

m\in\mathbb{Z}
. It follows
P\simeq\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p)[m]
.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

If
F\colon\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))\to\mathrm{D}_{W}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))
is an equivalence and
\dim Z=0
, then
\dim W=0
and
there is a bijection between
Z({\mathbb{C}})
and
W({\mathbb{C}})
.

Proof.

Let
q\in W
be a closed point and consider the complex
E_{q}:=F^{-1}(\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q))
where
F^{-1}
is a quasi-inverse to
F
.
Then, by Proposition
3.2
,
E_{q}
is a point like object in
{\rm D}^{b}_{Z}({\rm Coh}(X))
and
by Proposition
3.5

E_{q}\simeq\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p)[m]
for a unique closed point
p\in Z
and

m\in\mathbb{Z}
. In this way we define a function
f\colon W({\mathbb{C}})\to Z({\mathbb{C}})
.
We first check that
f
is injective. If there exists another closed point
q^{\prime}\in W
such that

f(q)=f(q^{\prime})
, then as before we would have
F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p)[m^{\prime}])=\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q^{\prime})

for some
m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence we have

\{q\}=\mathrm{Supp}(\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q)[-m])=\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p)))=\mathrm{Supp}(\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q^{\prime})[-m^{\prime}])=\{q^{\prime}\}.

It follows that
W({\mathbb{C}})
is a finite and

\dim W=0
. Moreover, by repeating the argument for the quasi-inverse
F^{-1}
one checks also the surjectivity of
f
.

∎

Example 3.7
.

Let
X
be an abelian variety of positive dimension and let
Y=\widehat{X}
be the dual abelian variety.
Moreover let
{\mathcal{P}}
be a normalized Poincaré line bundle.
Consider the Fourier–Mukai–Poincaré transform

\mathrm{R}\widehat{\mathcal{S}}\colon\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))\to\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))
defined as

\mathrm{R}\widehat{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{F}})=\mathrm{R}q_{*}(p^{*}{\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{P}})
for any
{\mathcal{F}}
in
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
,
where
p
and
q
are the projections onto
X
and

Y
, respectively. Then as in
[
Muk81
, Example 2.9]

\mathrm{R}\widehat{\mathcal{S}}
is an equivalence and
\mathrm{R}^{\dim X}\widehat{\mathcal{S}}
gives an equivalence of
abelian categories between the category
\mathrm{Unip}(X)
of unipotent bundles of finite rank on
X
,
and
\mathrm{Coh}_{\{\hat{0}\}}(Y)
(where

\hat{0}
is the origin of
Y
).
Then
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Unip}(X))\simeq\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}_{\{\hat{0}\}}(Y))
and, by
Corollary
2.3
and Proposition
3.6
, there
is no smooth projective variety
M
such that

\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Unip}(X))
is equivalent to
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(M))
.

In the positive-dimensional case the characterization
of point like objects in
\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
is possible under a positivity assumption on the restriction of the canonical bundle to the support.

Proposition 3.8
.

Suppose
\omega_{X}|_{Z}
or
\omega_{X}^{-1}|_{Z}
is ample.
Then the point like objects of
\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
are the objects isomorphic
to
\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p)[r]
where
p\in Z
is a closed point and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Let
P
be a point like object in
\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))

and denote by
{\mathcal{H}}^{j}
the cohomology sheaves of
\iota_{Z}P
. Note that the

{\mathcal{H}}^{j}
’s are coherent sheaves on
X
supported in
Z
. Since
S_{Z}(P)\simeq P[\dim X]
,
we have the following isomorphisms:

\displaystyle\iota_{Z}^{!}(\iota_{Z}P\otimes\omega_{X})\simeq P

\displaystyle\iota_{Z}\iota_{Z}^{!}(\iota_{Z}P\otimes\omega_{X})\simeq\iota_{Z}P

\displaystyle\iota_{Z}\iota_{Z}^{!}\iota_{Z}P\otimes\omega_{X}\simeq\iota_{Z}P

\displaystyle\iota_{Z}P\otimes\omega_{X}\simeq\iota_{Z}P.

By taking cohomology we have

(3)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}^{j}\otimes\omega_{X}\simeq{\mathcal{H}}^{j}\quad\mbox{ for all }j.

Now we show that
{\mathcal{H}}^{j}
is supported in dimension zero for any
j
.
Suppose
\omega_{X}|_{Z}
is ample, the other case being similar. Let
k>0
be an integer such that

N:=\omega_{X}^{\otimes k}|_{Z}
is very ample and let

i\colon Z\hookrightarrow X
be the inclusion map (here
Z
is equipped with the reduced induced subscheme structure).
Then the Hilbert polynomial of
P_{i^{*}{\mathcal{H}}^{j}}(m)=\chi(i^{*}{\mathcal{H}}^{j}\otimes N^{\otimes m})
has degree
equal to

s_{j}\;:=\;\dim\mathrm{Supp}(i^{*}{\mathcal{H}}^{j})=\dim\mathrm{Supp}({\mathcal{H}}^{j})

(
[
Ser55
, p. 276, Proposition 6]
).
Moreover, by tensoring (
3
) with positive powers of
\omega_{X}

and by restricting the isomorphisms to
Z
, we find

i^{*}{\mathcal{H}}^{j}\otimes N\;\simeq\;i^{*}{\mathcal{H}}^{j}\quad\mbox{ for all }j.

Therefore
P_{i^{*}{\mathcal{H}}^{j}}(m)=P_{i^{*}{\mathcal{H}}^{j}\otimes N}(m)=P_{i^{*}{\mathcal{H}}^{j}}(m+1)
for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
which is impossible if
\deg P_{i^{*}{\mathcal{H}}^{j}}>0
.
Hence
s_{j}=0
for all
j
and
\iota_{Z}P\simeq{\mathbb{C}}(p)[r]
for some closed point
p\in Z
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
by Lemma
3.4
.
It follows that
P\simeq\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p)[r]
.

∎

Example 3.9
.

We construct further instances of equivalences between derived categories with support extending the equivalences (
1
).
Denote by

R\colon\mathrm{Aut}^{0}(X)\times\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X)\to\mathrm{Aut}^{0}(Y)\times\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(Y)

the Rouquier isomorphism induced by an equivalence

F\colon\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))\to\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))
(
cf.

[
Rou11
, Théorème 4.18]
or
[
Huy06
, Proposition 9.45]
,
and
[
PS11
, p.531, footnote (1)]
). By following
[
Lom14
, Proposition 3.1]
,
if
\alpha\in\Pic^{0}(X)
is a topologically trivial line bundle such that
H^{0}(X,\omega_{X}^{\otimes k_{0}}\otimes\alpha)\neq 0
for some

k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
R({\rm id}_{X},\alpha)=(\mathrm{id}_{Y},\beta)
for some
\beta\in\Pic^{0}(Y)
and moreover there are isomorphisms

R_{k}\colon H^{0}(X,\omega_{X}^{\otimes k}\otimes\alpha)\to H^{0}(Y,\omega_{Y}^{\otimes k}\otimes\beta)
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
As in
[
Tod06
]
, one can prove that
if
E\in\big|\omega_{X}^{\otimes k}\otimes\alpha\big|
and
E^{\prime}=R_{k}(E)\in\big|\omega_{Y}^{\otimes k}\otimes\beta\big|

is the corresponding divisor, then

F
restricts to an equivalence of triangulated categories
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{Supp}(E)}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))\simeq\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{Supp}(E^{\prime})}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))
.

4.
Gabriel’s theorem with support

We present a version of Gabriel’s reconstruction theorem
[
Gab62
]
with support.
Other variants of Gabriel’s result appear in
[
Per09
]
and
[
CP21
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be smooth projective varieties and let
Z\subset X
and
W\subset Y
be algebraic closed subsets.
If there is an equivalence of abelian categories
\varphi\colon\mathrm{Coh}_{Z}(X)\to\mathcal{\mathrm{}}{Coh}_{W}(Y)
, then

Z({\mathbb{C}})
and
W({\mathbb{C}})
are homeomorphic.

Proof.

A sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathrm{Coh}_{Z}(X)
(
resp.
in
\mathrm{Coh}(X)
)
is said
indecomposable
if any non-trivial surjection
{\mathcal{F}}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathcal{G}}
with
{\mathcal{G}}
in
\mathrm{Coh}_{Z}(X)
(
resp.

in
\mathrm{Coh}(X)
)
is an isomorphism. Recall that the indecomposable sheaves in
\mathrm{Coh}(X)
are the skyscraper sheaves of closed points
{\mathbb{C}}(x)
.
It follows that the indecomposable sheaves in
\mathrm{Coh}_{Z}(X)
are the sheaves
\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(x)
where
x\in Z
is a closed point.
Indeed, let
{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathrm{Coh}_{Z}(X)
be an
indecomposable sheaf. It is enough to show that

\iota_{Z}{\mathcal{F}}
is indecomposable in
\mathrm{Coh}(X)
. To this end, consider a short exact sequence of type

0\to{\mathcal{K}}\to\iota_{Z}{\mathcal{F}}\to{\mathcal{G}}\to 0

with
{\mathcal{G}}
non trivial in
\mathrm{Coh}(X)
. Under the full embedding
\mathrm{Coh}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
,
the above sequence
becomes a distinguished triangle
{\mathcal{K}}\to\iota_{Z}{\mathcal{F}}\to{\mathcal{G}}\to{\mathcal{K}}[1]
. In turn the functor
\iota_{Z}^{!}
induces
a distinguished triangle in
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{Z}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))

\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{K}}\to{\mathcal{F}}\to\iota_{Z}^{!}G\to\iota_{Z}^{!}K[1].

Since both
{\mathcal{K}}
and
{\mathcal{G}}
have support in
Z
, we check that
\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{K}}
and
\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{G}}

are sheaves in
\mathrm{Coh}_{Z}(X)
.
Under the full embedding
\mathrm{Coh}_{Z}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}_{Z}(X))\simeq\mathrm{D}^{b}_{Z}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
,
this distinguished triangle becomes
a short exact sequence
0\to\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{K}}\to{\mathcal{F}}\to\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{G}}\to 0
with
\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{G}}\neq 0
.
Hence
\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{K}}\simeq 0
and consequently
{\mathcal{K}}
is trivial.

The equivalence
\varphi
sends indecomposable sheaves to indecomposable sheaves. Hence we can define a function
f\colon Z({\mathbb{C}})\to W({\mathbb{C}})
on closed points
such that
f(q)=p
if and only if
\varphi(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q))=\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p)
. Note that
f
is bijective. Now we prove that

f
is bicontinuous by following an idea of
[
LLZ24
]
.

Denote by
\varphi^{-1}
a quasi-inverse of
\varphi
and
let
W_{0}\subset W({\mathbb{C}})
be a closed subset. Hence
W_{0}=W^{\prime}\cap W({\mathbb{C}})
for some algebraic closed subset
W^{\prime}\subset W
.
Since for every
q\in Z({\mathbb{C}})

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}(\varphi^{-1}(\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{W^{\prime}}),\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q))\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}(\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{W^{\prime}},\varphi(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q)))\simeq\mathrm{Hom}(\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{W^{\prime}},\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(f(q)))\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}({\mathcal{O}}_{W^{\prime}},{\mathbb{C}}(f(q))),

we have

q\in{\rm Supp}(\varphi^{-1}(\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{W^{\prime}}))\cap Z({\mathbb{C}})\;\Leftrightarrow\;f(q)\in W^{\prime}\cap W({\mathbb{C}})=W_{0}.

It follows that
f^{-1}(W_{0})=\mathrm{Supp}(\varphi^{-1}(\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{W^{\prime}}))\cap Z({\mathbb{C}})
is closed in
Z({\mathbb{C}})

and
f
is continuous.

Similarly, also
f^{-1}
is continuous. This goes as follows.
Let
Z_{0}=Z^{\prime}\cap Z({\mathbb{C}})
be a closed subset in
Z({\mathbb{C}})
where
Z^{\prime}
is closed in
Z
. Note

\mathrm{Hom}({\mathcal{O}}_{Z^{\prime}},{\mathbb{C}}(q))\simeq\mathrm{Hom}(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z^{\prime}},\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q))\simeq\mathrm{Hom}(\varphi(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z^{\prime}}),\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(f(q)))\quad\mbox{ for every }\quad q\in Z({\mathbb{C}}).

It follows

f(q)\in\mathrm{Supp}(\varphi(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z^{\prime}}))\cap W({\mathbb{C}})\;\Leftrightarrow\;q\in Z^{\prime}\cap Z({\mathbb{C}})=Z_{0}.

Hence

f(Z_{0})=\mathrm{Supp}(\varphi(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z^{\prime}}))\cap W({\mathbb{C}})
is closed in
W({\mathbb{C}})
.

∎

5.
Reconstruction of the support, I

We aim to prove Theorem
1.1
.
Denote by

F\colon\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))\to\mathrm{D}_{W}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))
an equivalence
and let
p\in W({\mathbb{C}})
. Then there exists an object
E_{p}
in
\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
such that

F(E_{p})=\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p)
. By Proposition
3.2
,

E_{p}
is a point like object and by Proposition
3.8

E_{p}\simeq\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q)[m_{p}]
for some

q\in Z({\mathbb{C}})
and
m_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Define
U({\mathbb{C}})\subset Z({\mathbb{C}})
as the set of closed points
q
such that
F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q))\simeq\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p)[s_{p}]
for some

p\in W({\mathbb{C}})
and
s_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
In this way
we can define a surjective function
f\colon U({\mathbb{C}})\to W({\mathbb{C}})
such that
f(q)=p
if and only if
F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q))=\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p)[s_{p}]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

The function
f
is injective.

Proof.

Suppose there exist
q,q^{\prime}\in U({\mathbb{C}})
such that
f(q)=f(q^{\prime})
. Then there exist
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q)[m_{1}])=F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q^{\prime})[m_{2}])
and
\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q)[m_{1}]\simeq\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q^{\prime})[m_{2}]
. By looking at the supports it results

q=q^{\prime}
.

∎

Proposition 5.2
.

The set
U({\mathbb{C}})
equals
Z({\mathbb{C}})
.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction there exists
q^{\prime}\in Z({\mathbb{C}})\backslash U({\mathbb{C}})
. If
p\in W({\mathbb{C}})
is any point, then
f^{-1}(p)\in U
and

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHom}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q^{\prime})),\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p))\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHom}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q^{\prime})),F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(f^{-1}(p))[-s_{p}]))\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHom}({\mathbb{C}}(q^{\prime}),{\mathbb{C}}(f^{-1}(p))[-s_{p}])\;=\;0.

It follows that
\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q^{\prime})))=\emptyset
and
\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q^{\prime})\simeq 0
.

∎

Proposition 5.3
.

The functions
f
and
f^{-1}
are continuous.

Proof.

Let
W_{0}\subset W
be a closed subset.
Since for every
q\in Z({\mathbb{C}})

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHom}(F^{-1}(\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{W_{0}}),\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q))\;\simeq\;\mathrm{RHom}(\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{W_{0}},F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q)))\;\simeq\;\mathrm{RHom}(\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{W_{0}},\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(f(q))),

we have

q\in\mathrm{Supp}(F^{-1}(\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{W_{0}}))\cap Z({\mathbb{C}})\;\Leftrightarrow\;f(q)\in W_{0}\cap W({\mathbb{C}}).

It follows that
f^{-1}(W_{0}\cap W({\mathbb{C}}))=\mathrm{Supp}(F^{-1}(\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{W_{0}}))\cap Z({\mathbb{C}})
is closed in
Z({\mathbb{C}})

and
f
is continuous.

Now we show that
f^{-1}
is continuous.
Let
Z_{0}\subset Z
be a closed subset and note the isomorphism

\mathrm{RHom}(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{0}},\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q))\simeq\mathrm{RHom}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{0}}),\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(f(q)))\quad\mbox{ for every }\quad q\in Z({\mathbb{C}}).

Then it follows that

f(q)\in\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{0}}))\cap W({\mathbb{C}})\;\Leftrightarrow\;q\in Z_{0}\cap Z({\mathbb{C}})

and

f(Z_{0}\cap Z({\mathbb{C}}))=\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{0}}))\cap W({\mathbb{C}})
is closed in
W({\mathbb{C}})
.

∎

6.
Reconstruction of the support, II

We aim to prove Theorem
1.2
. The proof is divided into four steps and it follows the general strategy of
[
LLZ24
, §3]
.

6.1.
Step 1

We need the following characterization of skyscraper sheaves.

Proposition 6.1
.

Suppose
X
is a smooth projective variety and let
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(1)
be a very ample line bundle.
Suppose
Z\subset X
is an algebraic closed subset and
let
E
be a simple object in
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{Z}({\rm Coh}(X))
such that

\mathrm{Hom}_{{\rm D}^{b}_{Z}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}(E,E[j])=0
for
j<0
.
If there exist non-negative integers
a_{0},\ldots,a_{\dim X}
such that

\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathrm{D}^{b}_{Z}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}(\iota_{Z}^{!}({\mathcal{O}}_{Z}(-m)),E)\;\simeq\;\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\dim X}\mathbb{C}^{\oplus a_{k}}[-k]

for
m\gg 0
,
then
E\simeq\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(x)[r]
for some closed point
x\in Z
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Denote by
i\colon Z\hookrightarrow X
the inclusion map and note the isomorphisms:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{{\rm D}^{b}_{Z}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}(\iota_{Z}^{!}i_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}(-m),E)\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{{\rm D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}(i_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}(-m),\iota_{Z}E)\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{{\rm D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}({\mathcal{O}}_{X},(i_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z})^{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm R}}}{{\vee}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}{{\otimes}}\iota_{Z}E\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(m))

Let
{\mathcal{H}}^{q}
be the
q
-th cohomology of the complex

(i_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z})^{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{R}}}{{\vee}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}{{\otimes}}\iota_{Z}E

and consider the spectral sequence

\displaystyle E_{2}^{p,q}\;=\;\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}({\mathcal{O}}_{X},{\mathcal{H}}^{q}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(m)[p])\;\Rightarrow\;

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}({\mathcal{O}}_{X},(i_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z})^{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathrm{R}}}}{{\vee}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}{{\otimes}}\iota_{Z}E\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(m)[p+q])

For
m\gg 0
the elements
E_{2}^{p,q}
are trivial for all
p\neq 0
and all
q
. Hence the spectral sequence degenerates at the second page
and

H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{H}}^{q}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(m))\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}({\mathcal{O}}_{X},(i_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z})^{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{R}}}{{\vee}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}{{\otimes}}\iota_{Z}E\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(m)[q])

for all
q
. By hypotheses, the right hand side of the above isomorphisms vanish for
q<0
or
q>\dim X
, while in middle degrees we have

H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{H}}^{q}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(m))\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{D}^{b}_{Z}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}(\iota_{Z}^{!}({\mathcal{O}}_{Z}(-m)),E[q])\simeq\mathbb{C}^{\oplus a_{q}}

for all
m\gg 0
.
Moreover we can suppose
{\mathcal{H}}^{q}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(m)
is globally generated for
m\gg 0
and any
q
. Therefore for every
q
the sheaf
{\mathcal{H}}^{q}
is either trivial
or supported on a finite set of points. In fact, if
{\mathcal{H}}^{q}\neq 0
and
s=\dim\mathrm{Supp}({\mathcal{H}}^{q})
, then by
[
Ser55
, p. 276, Proposition 6]

the Hilbert polynomial
P_{{\mathcal{H}}^{q}}(m):=\chi({\mathcal{H}}^{q}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(m))
has degree equal to
s
and

P_{{\mathcal{H}}^{q}}(m)=h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{H}}^{q}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(m))=a_{q}\quad\mbox{ for all }\quad m\gg 0.

This forces
s=0
and

(4)

\dim\big(\mathrm{Supp}((i_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z})^{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{R}}}{{\vee}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}{{\otimes}}\iota_{Z}E)\big)\leq 0.

On the other hand, there are equalities of sets

(5)

\mathrm{Supp}((i_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z})^{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{R}}}{{\vee}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}{{\otimes}}\iota_{Z}E)\;=\;\mathrm{Supp}((i_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z})^{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{R}}}{{\vee}}})\cap\mathrm{supp}(\iota_{Z}E)

and

(6)

\mathrm{Supp}((i_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z})^{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{R}}}{{\vee}}})\;=\;\mathrm{Supp}(i_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z})=Z.

In order to check (
6
), for a closed point
x\in X
it results

\mathrm{RHom}((i_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z})^{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{R}}}{{\vee}}},{\mathbb{C}}(x))\;\simeq\;\mathrm{RHom}({\mathcal{O}}_{X},i_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}{{\otimes}}{\mathbb{C}}(x)),

and
therefore

x\in\mathrm{Supp}((i_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z})^{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{R}}}{{\vee}}})\cap X({\mathbb{C}})\;\Leftrightarrow\;x\in Z({\mathbb{C}})

(here we have employed the degeneration of the spectral sequence

E_{2}^{{}^{\prime}p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}=\mathrm{Hom}({\mathcal{O}}_{X},{\mathcal{H}}^{q^{\prime}}(i_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}{{\otimes}}{\mathbb{C}}(x))[p^{\prime}])\Rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}({\mathcal{O}}_{X},i_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}{{\otimes}}{\mathbb{C}}(x)[p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}])).

By putting together (
4
), (
5
) and (
6
),
we conclude that
\dim\mathrm{Supp}(\iota_{Z}E)\leq 0
.
The conclusion follows by Lemma
3.4
.

∎

6.2.
Step 2

By hypotheses we may suppose
F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(x))=\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(y)
for some closed points
x\in Z({\mathbb{C}})
and
y\in W({\mathbb{C}})
.

There are isomorphisms

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathrm{D}_{W}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}),\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(y))\simeq\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathrm{D}_{W}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}),F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(x)))\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z},\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(x))\simeq\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}({\mathcal{O}}_{Z},{\mathbb{C}}(x))\simeq\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\dim X}\mathbb{C}^{\oplus a_{k}}[-k]

for some integers
a_{0},\ldots,a_{\dim X}\geq 0
.

By semi-continuity, there exists an algebraic open dense subset
W^{\prime}\subset W
containing
y
such that for every closed point
p\in W^{\prime}
:

\mathrm{RHom}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}),\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p))\simeq\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\dim X}\mathbb{C}^{\oplus b^{p}_{k}}[-k]

for some integers

b^{p}_{0},\ldots,b_{\dim X}^{p}
such that
0\leq b_{k}^{p}\leq a_{k}
for any
k=0,\ldots,\dim X
.

Fix an embedding
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}
and
let

P_{0}\subset P_{1}\subset P_{2}\subset\ldots\subset P_{\dim Z}=\mathbb{P}^{N}

be a flag of subspaces such that

P_{j}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{N-\dim Z+j}
and
Z_{j}:=Z\cap P_{j}
is of dimension
j
for every
0\leq j\leq\dim Z
.
Moreover, the
Z_{j}
’s are irreducible except
possibly
Z_{0}
(
[
Jou83
, Theoreme I.6.10]
).
We can also assume
x\notin Z_{j}
for all
j<\dim Z
.
Then for any
j<\dim Z
we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathrm{D}_{W}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j}}),\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(y))\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathrm{D}_{W}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j}}),F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(x)))\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j}},\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(x))\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))}({\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j}},{\mathbb{C}}(x))=0

so that
y\notin\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j}}))
.
Set
S=\bigcup_{0\leq j<\dim Z}\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j}}))\subset W
. The set
S
is a closed subset and does not contain
y
.
Also define the following open dense subset of
W
:

V:=W^{\prime}\backslash S.

Proposition 6.2
.

For every
p\in V
closed point and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

\mathrm{RHom}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}(m)),\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p))\simeq\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\dim X}\mathbb{C}^{\oplus b^{p}_{k}}[-k].

Proof.

For every
j=1,\ldots,\dim Z
there is a short exact sequence:

0\to{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j}}(-Z_{j-1})\to{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j}}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j-1}}\to 0

where
{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j}}(-Z_{j-1})\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j}}(-1)={\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{N}}(-1)
.
We set

{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j}}(m):={\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j}}\otimes\mathbb{{\mathcal{O}}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{N}}(m)

for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and note the
distinguished triangles

\displaystyle\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j-1}}(m+1)[-1]\to\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j}}(m)\to\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j}}(m+1)\to\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j-1}}(m+1)

\displaystyle F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j-1}}(m+1)[-1])\to F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j}}(m))\to F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j}}(m+1))\to F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j-1}}(m+1))

in
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{Z}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
and
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{W}(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))
, respectively.
Therefore we find that

(7)

\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j}}(m+1)))\subset\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j}}(m)))\cup\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j-1}}(m+1)))

and

(8)

\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j}}(m)))\subset\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j}}(m+1)))\cup\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j-1}}(m+1))).

Now note that
\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{0}}(m)\simeq\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{0}}
for all
m
.
By proceeding by induction on
m
, and by employing (
7
) for
m\geq 0
and (
8
) for
m<0
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{\dim Z-1}}(m)))\subset

\displaystyle\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{\dim Z-1}}(m-1)))\cup\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{\dim Z-2}}(m)))\subset

\displaystyle\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{\dim Z-1}}(m-2)))\cup\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{\dim Z-2}}(m-1)))\cup\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{\dim Z-3}}(m)))

\displaystyle\subset\ldots\subset\bigcup_{0\leq j<\dim Z}\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{j}})).

Finally let
p\in V
be any point closed point and
consider the short exact sequence

0\to{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}(-1)\to{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{\dim Z-1}}\to 0,

together with the distinguished triangle

Note that

\mathrm{RHom}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{\dim Z-1}}),\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p))=0

as

.
Therefore we obtain the isomorphisms

\mathrm{RHom}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}(-1)),\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p))\simeq\mathrm{RHom}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}),\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p))\simeq\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\dim X}\mathbb{C}^{\oplus b^{p}_{k}}[-k].

By running an induction argument on
m
we get the statement.
∎

6.3.
Step 3

For every closed point
p\in V
there exists an object
E_{p}
in
\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
such that

. Moreover, by Proposition

\mathrm{RHom}(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}(m),E_{p})\simeq\mathrm{RHom}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}(m)),\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p))\simeq\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\dim X}\mathbb{C}^{\oplus b^{p}_{k}}[-k]

for all
m
. Hence by Proposition
6.1

is a point like object and
there is an isomorphism
E_{p}\simeq\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q)[r_{p}]
for some
q\in Z({\mathbb{C}})
and
r_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Define the subset

Z({\mathbb{C}})\supset U({\mathbb{C}}):=\{q\;\big|\;F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q))=\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p)[s_{p}]\mbox{ for some }p\in V({\mathbb{C}})\mbox{ and }s_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}\}

and the function

such that
f(q)=p
if and only if
F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q))=\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p)[s_{p}]
.
Note that
f

Proposition 6.3
.

The set
U({\mathbb{C}})
is open in
Z({\mathbb{C}})
.

Proof.

Define the closed set
C=W\backslash V
and let
G_{C}
be a classical generator of
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{C}(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))
(
[
Rou08
, Theorem 6.8]
).
Moreover let
F^{-1}
be a quasi-inverse to
F
.
Then for any
q\in U({\mathbb{C}})
we have

\mathrm{RHom}(F^{-1}(G_{C}),\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q))\simeq\mathrm{RHom}(G_{C},F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q)))\simeq 0

and therefore
\mathrm{Supp}(F^{-1}(G_{C}))\cap Z({\mathbb{C}})\subset Z({\mathbb{C}})\backslash U({\mathbb{C}})
.

Now we show the reverse inclusion.
By
[
LLZ24
, Lemma 2.3]
, the following inclusion holds for any object
E
in
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{C}(\mathrm{Coh}(Y))
:

(9)

\mathrm{Supp}(F^{-1}(E))\cap Z({\mathbb{C}})\subset\mathrm{Supp}(F^{-1}(G_{C}))\cap Z({\mathbb{C}}).

Let
q^{\prime}\in Z({\mathbb{C}})\backslash U({\mathbb{C}})
and
p^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q^{\prime})))\cap Z({\mathbb{C}})
.
Then Serre duality yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHom}(F^{-1}(\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p^{\prime})),\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q^{\prime}))\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHom}(\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p^{\prime}),F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{q^{\prime}}))\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHom}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q^{\prime})),S_{W}(\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p^{\prime})))^{*}\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHom}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q^{\prime})),\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p^{\prime})[\dim Y]))^{*}\neq 0.

Hence
q^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Supp}(F^{-1}(\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p^{\prime})))\cap Z({\mathbb{C}})
and by (
9
) it must be
q^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Supp}(F^{-1}(G_{C}))\cap Z({\mathbb{C}})
.
It follows that
Z({\mathbb{C}})\backslash U({\mathbb{C}})=\mathrm{Supp}(F^{-1}(G_{C}))\cap Z({\mathbb{C}})

is a closed set in
Z({\mathbb{C}})
.
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

The functions
f
and
f^{-1}
are continuous.

Proof.

If
V_{0}\subset V
is a Zariski closed subset, we denote by
\overline{V_{0}}
its closure in
W
.
Since for every
q\in U({\mathbb{C}})
we have

\mathrm{RHom}(F^{-1}(\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{V_{0}}}),\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q))\;\simeq\;\mathrm{RHom}(\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{V_{0}}},\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(f(q))[s_{f(q)}])),

then

q\in\mathrm{Supp}(F^{-1}(\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{V_{0}}}))\cap U({\mathbb{C}})\;\Leftrightarrow\;f(q)\in\overline{V_{0}}\cap V({\mathbb{C}})=V_{0}\cap V({\mathbb{C}}).

It follows that
f^{-1}(V_{0}\cap V({\mathbb{C}}))=\mathrm{Supp}(F^{-1}(\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{V_{0}}}))\cap U
, which is closed in
U
.

In a similar manner we prove that
f^{-1}
is continuous.
For every Zariski closed subset
M
in
U({\mathbb{C}})
, we denote by
\overline{M}
its closure in
Z
. Note that for every
q\in U({\mathbb{C}})
we have

\mathrm{RHom}(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{M}},\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q))\;\simeq\;\mathrm{RHom}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{M}}),\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(f(q))[s_{f(q)}]).

Then it follows that

f(q)\in\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{M}}))\cap V({\mathbb{C}})\;\Leftrightarrow\;q\in\overline{M}\cap U({\mathbb{C}})=M

and

f(M)=\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{M}}))\cap V({\mathbb{C}})
is closed in
V({\mathbb{C}})
.

∎

Corollary 6.5
.

If
F
is an equivalence and
\dim W=1
, then
Z({\mathbb{C}})
and
W({\mathbb{C}})
are homeomorphic.

Proof.

Let
p\in W({\mathbb{C}})\backslash V({\mathbb{C}})
and let
F_{p}
be an object in
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{Z}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
such that
F(F_{p})=\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p)
. The
support of
F_{p}
is
properly contained in
Z
as

\mathrm{RHom}(F_{p},\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(x))\;\simeq\;\mathrm{RHom}(\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(p),\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(y))=0

(note that
y\in V({\mathbb{C}})
).
Hence
F_{p}
is supported
in dimension zero and, by Lemma
3.4
,
F_{p}\simeq\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q)[m_{p}]
for some

q\in Z({\mathbb{C}})\backslash U({\mathbb{C}})
and
m_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore we can extend
f
on
q
by setting
f(q)=p
.
By iterating this process for every
p\in W({\mathbb{C}})\backslash V({\mathbb{C}})
, we obtain an injective and
surjective function
f\colon U^{\prime}\to W({\mathbb{C}})
where
U({\mathbb{C}})\subset U^{\prime}\subset Z({\mathbb{C}})
.

We show that
U^{\prime}=Z({\mathbb{C}})
. Suppose by contradiction that there exists
r\in Z({\mathbb{C}})\backslash U^{\prime}
. Note that for every
q\in U^{\prime}
:

0\;=\;\mathrm{RHom}(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(r),\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q))\;\simeq\;\mathrm{RHom}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(r)),F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(q)))\;\simeq\;\mathrm{RHom}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(r)),\iota_{W}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(f(q))).

As
f
is surjective, this means that
\mathrm{Supp}(F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(r)))=\emptyset
and
F(\iota_{Z}^{!}{\mathbb{C}}(r))\simeq 0
which is impossible.
Moreover, as in Proposition
6.4
, one can prove that both extensions

f\colon Z({\mathbb{C}})\to W({\mathbb{C}})
and
f^{-1}\colon W({\mathbb{C}})\to Z({\mathbb{C}})
are continuous.

∎

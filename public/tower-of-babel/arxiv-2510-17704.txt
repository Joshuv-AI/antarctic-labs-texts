Einstein gravity extended by a scale covariant scalar field with Bekenstein term and dynamical mass generation
Erhard Scholz

Abstract
Under carefully chosen assumptions a single general relativistic scalar field is able to induce MOND-like dynamics in the weak field approximation of the Einstein frame (gauge) and to modify the light cone structure accordingly. This is shown by a Lagrangian model formulated in the framework of integrable Weyl geometry. It contains a Bekenstein-type (“aquadratic”) term and a second order term generating additional mass energy for the scalar field. Both are switched on only if the gradient of the scalar field is spacelike and below a MOND-typical threshold, like in the superfluid model of Berezhiani/Khoury. The mass term induces non-negligible energy and pressures of the scalar field and leads to gravitational light deflection compatible with MONDian free fall trajectories. In the weak field (Newton-Milgrom) approximation the Bekenstein term implies a deep MOND equation for the scalar field. In this model the external field effect of the MOND approach has to be reconsidered. This has important consequences for hierarchical systems like clusters, which may suffice for explaining their dynamics without additional dark matter.

1.1
Basic idea

This paper explores the possibilities for a gravitational scalar field which leads to a general relativistic underpinning of
modified Newtonian dynamics, MOND
[

. The model studied takes up
Bekenstein’s idea of an “aquadratic” Lagrangian

(ii) A mass generating term is assumed for the scalar field (in addition to the Bekenstein term). (iii) The Bekenstein and mass terms of scalar field Lagrangian are assumed to be active only for regions in which the field gradient is spacelike and below a MOND-typical threshold. The physical reason for this may be looked for in a destabilization of the substrate underlying the field dynamics for field gradients above a critical threshold like in Berezhiani/Khoury’s superfluid approach
[
13
,
14
]
.

Point (ii) changes the character of the scalar field in comparison with Bekenstein’s. It can no longer be considered exclusively as an enhancement of the gravitational structure like a scalar field of Jordan-Brans-Dicke (JBD) type which it otherwise resembles, because it has also features of a kind of dark matter sui generis, although not based on a particle ontology. The mass term of the scalar field is its critical pivot; it must not destroy the scalar field equation dominated by the Bekenstein term and ought to have a “reasonable” Hilbert tensor, i.e, one that is essentially divergence free and strong enough for modifying the gravitational field in such a manner that particle dynamics and light ray structure conform to the rules of the MOND algorithm.

For the sake of a coherently scale covariant approach the methods and symbolism of Weyl geometric field theory are used. The present scalar field approach is compatible with the most simple case of an integrable scale connection (a vanishing Weyl field); this brings the framework close to the Riemannian case. There are good reasons, however, to not assume the Riemann gauge (in which the scale connection is gauged away) as the referential frame for empirical data but rather the gauge in which the scalar field is set constant, called the
Einstein gauge
(analogous to the Einstein frame of JBD). The derivation of basic laws and relations, in particular variational calculations, have to be done in the Weyl geometric scale co-/invariant calculus;

1.3
Other attempts at relativistic generalizations of MOND

The first sketch of a general relativistic version of modified Newtonian dynamics by Bekenstein and Milgrom used a JBD-like scalar field with

Follow up papers therefore did not look for an enhancement of the mass-energy of the scalar field, but saw the necessity of a more drastic modification of the metric, for example two “disformal” metrics

This led to its empirical devalidation (“refutation”) by the observation of a wave event (GW170817) in August 2017. To summarize: Among the attempts at a relativistic generalization of MOND we find, after the first pioneering exploration of the topic (Bekenstein/Milgrom), the long-time main proposal TeVeS which in the meantime has been empirically devalidated, followed by a material oriented approach (Berezhiani/Khoury) and (at least) three defective attempts (Skordis/Złośnik, Maeder et al., Harko et al.). At the moment Milgrom’s BIMOND program and Verlinde-Hossenfelder covariant emergent gravity are two acceptable candidates for relativistic MOND theories, both with complex assumptions.

The following investigation probes whether, or better how far, the goal can be achieved in the continuation of the earliest idea of a single scalar field non-minimally coupled to the Hilbert term.

1.4
Organization of the paper

The Lagrangian of the model is introduced in section
2.1
. As already indicated, it is formulated in the language and symbolism of integrable Weyl geometry with its scale covariant derivatives and curvatures. Its peculiar element is a scale covariant scalar field coupling to the Hilbert term, which has two unconventional kinetic terms implying a MOND-typical gravitational dynamics under the condition that the gradient of the scalar field does not get too large (Milgrom regime). If the gradient in Einstein gauge surpasses the threshold of the MOND constant, the two kinetic terms are suppressed and gravity becomes Einsteinian (Einstein regime, case (A)) or, under slightly different assumptions, Jordan-Brans-Dicke gravity (case (B)). The resulting dynamics, again considered in the Einstein gauge, is discussed in section
2.2
, with detailed calculations left to the appendix. As to be expected, the gravitational dynamics is governed by an Einstein equation in both regimes (and both cases).

In the Milgrom regime the Einstein equation is approximated by a Newtonian Poisson equation as usual, but here with a source contribution of the scalar field. In the Minkowski limit of the metric the relativistic Milgrom equation converges to the deep MOND equation of flat space. Both together constitute the Newton-Milgrom approximation of the model. External field effect and hierarchical systems (galaxy clusters)

The relativistic approach sheds different light on the

In classical MOND it is usually assumed that a small gravitating system

He saw great advantages in pursuing this hypothesis as a potential principle which would result in significant differences to the established theories of gravity (Newtonian and Einsteinian)

Strong equivalence principle
(as understood in MOND):

In a

approximately homogeneous
], the results of experiments using any field observables, including gravitational ones, are independent of the velocity of the test system and the external gravitational field.

Much depends on the definition of ‘‘local’’ and the omission or inclusion and interpretation of the bracketed clause of ‘‘approximately homogeneous’’.
40
40

40

The formulation “local” is ambiguous; at other places physicists use the term “local” for what mathematicians would describe as
in the infinitesimal

In the MOND context the combination of both are to be understood in the sense of a finitely extended system which is, however, small enough to be insensitive against the curvature (tidal forces) of the embedding gravitational environment. Milgrom was sure, or assumed at least, that in most cases considered by him (open clusters of stars in a galaxy, galaxies in galaxy cluster etc.) this is the case. He did not contest that, for most subsystems considered by him, tidal forces of the embedding system are present (see the quote in fn.
38
) – and therefore the SEP without the square bracket is broken anyhow, even without MOND. But he considered these effects as observationally negligible.
His point was that his hypothesis breaks the SEP much stronger. His EFE foresees the possibility that, under the conditions internal acceleration
|a_{i}|\precapprox\mathfrak{a}_{0}
in
S_{0}
and external acceleration
|a_{e}|\succapprox\mathfrak{a}_{0}

S_{0}

. An indirect theoretical underpinning of the EFE, derived from an asymptotic study of the non-linear Poisson equation of MOND, is given in
[

. It seems doubtful, however, that the asymptotic conditions studied in this paper can be supported by empirically realistic assumptions for MONDian models.

In the last four decades considerable work has been invested in testing the validity of Milgrom’s EFE principle observationally.
Some results seem to to speak for it, others do not.
Investigations of the tidal tails of open star clusters by a group about P. Kroupa give strong evidence for an underlying MONDian dynamics, in spite of an acceleration field of the galactic neighbourhood, which surpasses the critical value
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
. From an orthodox Milgromian point of view such star clusters should be “strongly subject to the

On the other hand, recent observational evidence with regard to galaxies and a survey of the literature is given in
[

the EFE. It remains unclear, however, whether the observations really test the role of the homogeneous part of the external acceleration field. In their survey at the beginning of the article the authors clearly state that the empirical evidence in favour of Milgrom’s EFE remains far from compelling. The balance pro and contra is therefore still inconclusive.

Once the whole constellation of
S_{0}
,
S_{e}
is placed in the framework of extended Einstein gravity, even though it may be calculated in the flat space approximation, Milgrom’s EFE cannot be upheld. If an acceleration field
\xi

is approximately homogeneous, relative to the size of the smaller system, and
S_{0}
is in free fall,
\xi

S_{0}
does not sense the acceleration field
\xi

S_{0}
in which the internal acceleration in its own reference system is below the MOND-typical threshold it enters the Milgrom regime.

Of course, also Einsteinian gravity knows an “external field effect” which breaks the naive version of the strong equivalence principle (the one without the square-bracketed clause above). Its Newtonian correlate is nothing but the flat space expression for an impact of curvature of the environment of
S_{e}
by tidal forces.

For the

From a relativistic perspective, the associated suppression of the scalar field is, however, a mock effect, because the reference system for a Newton approximation of the field about each individual galaxy

Milgrom was well aware of this fact

Efficacy and safety of immune checkpoint inhibitors for advanced or recurrent endometrial cancer: a systematic review and network meta-analysis
Authors: Danxue Huang, Su Li, Yang Bai, Yan Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCurrently, several randomized controlled trials (RCTs) have been conducted to investigate the efficacy of combining immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) with chemotherapy as a first-line treatment for advanced or recurrent endometrial cancer; however, the optimal treatment strategy remains undetermined.MethodsA comprehensive search of online databases was conducted to identify RCTs published until December 31, 2023. Network meta-analysis was performed to evaluate PFS, OS, TRAEs, irAEs, and the ranking of different treatment regimens.ResultsA total of 2702 patients from five RCTs (six reports) were included in the analysis. The combination therapy of ICIs significantly prolonged PFS (HR = 0.69, 95%CI 0.63–0.76, p < 0.0001) and OS (HR = 0.73, 95%CI 0.63–0.85, p < 0.0001) in the overall population. Among the different ICIs combinations evaluated, Durva-Olap-CP exhibited superior efficacy for both PFS and OS outcomes. In the pMMR population, both Durva-Olap-CP and Pembro-CP significantly reduced the risk of disease progression or death compared to Avelu-CP and Atezo-CP treatments; however, no significant differences were observed among various ICI combination therapies in patients with dMMR. In the dMMR population, Dostar-CP demonstrates a 42.2% probability of achieving first rank in terms of PFS, whereas in the pMMR population, Pembro-CP exhibits a 60% likelihood of securing the top position. Importantly, the toxicity associated with ICIs combination therapy was manageable and well-tolerated.ConclusionsThe combination of ICIs and chemotherapy as first-line treatment for advanced or recurrent endometrial cancer has demonstrated superior survival outcomes compared to chemotherapy alone. Durva-Olap-CP exhibited the most favorable PFS and OS benefits in the overall population. In patients with dMMR, Dostar-CP showed the greatest improvement in PFS, while Pembro-CP demonstrated the most pronounced PFS benefit in patients with pMMR.

Introduction

Endometrial cancer, the most common gynecologic cancer, has seen an increase in both incidence and mortality rates [1]. Factors such as geographic location, ethnicity, and socioeconomic status significantly influence the occurrence and outcome of endometrial cancer [2]. Although a majority of patients are diagnosed with early-stage localized disease and exhibit a favorable 5-year survival rate of 80–90%, those with recurrent or metastatic endometrial cancer exhibit poor treatment response and prognosis [3]. Surgery remains the primary approach for early-stage disease, yielding generally positive outcomes. However, approximately 20% of patients experience recurrence. The GOG-209 study has confirmed that carboplatin plus paclitaxel (CP) is noninferior to the more toxic three-drug combination regimen (cisplatin-doxorubicin-paclitaxel) [4]. This finding establishes carboplatin plus paclitaxel as the recommended standard first-line treatment for recurrent or metastatic endometrial cancer.

Endometrial cancer is recognized as an immunogenic tumor, wherein the immune system actively participates in both healthy and malignant endometrium, thus potentially revolutionizing treatment strategies through the implementation of immunotherapy [5, 6]. With the emergence of molecular classification for endometrial cancer, immunotherapy has gained considerable attention, particularly in patients with mismatch repair-deficient (dMMR) [7–11]. Approximately 25% of endometrial cancers exhibit dMMR or microsatellite instability high (MSI-H) [12–14], rendering them more susceptible to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs). Currently, immune single agents such as dostarlimab, pembrolizumab, nivolumab and atezolizumab have demonstrated promising clinical activity in second-line treatment of patients with dMMR endometrial cancer [9, 10, 15, 16]. For the mismatch repair–proficient (pMMR) population, pembrolizumab plus lenvatinib has shown significantly prolonged progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) as a second-line treatment option [17]. The incorporation of ICIs into chemotherapy has resulted in significant clinical improvements in survival outcomes among patients with diverse solid tumors [18, 19], attributed to the synergistic effect between cytotoxic chemotherapy within the tumor microenvironment that disrupts immunosuppressive pathways and enhances cytotoxic T cell responses [20].

In recent years, an increasing number of RCTs have focused on evaluating the effectiveness of combining ICIs with chemotherapy for the treatment of endometrial cancer, achieving clinically significant findings. Eskander et al. conducted a phase III trial (NRG-GY018) that demonstrated the addition of pembrolizumab to carboplatin-paclitaxel significantly prolonged PFS compared to chemotherapy alone [21]. Mirza et al. reported that the combination of Dostarlimab and standard chemotherapy significantly increased PFS in patients with endometrial cancer (RUBY) [22]. Another randomized phase II trial (MITO END-3) showed improved PFS with the addition of avelumab [23]. Furthermore, two authoritative phase III RCTS (DUO-E and AtTEnd study) presented updated results at the 2023 European Society of Medical Oncology (ESMO) Congress. DUO-E demonstrated a clinically meaningful improvement in PFS for chemotherapy plus durvalumab followed by durvalumab or durvalumab plus olaparib in patients with endometrial cancer [24]. AtTEnd confirmed the significant efficacy of atezolizumab plus chemotherapy in patients with endometrial cancer, particularly those with dMMR status [25].

Although some previous meta-analyses have shown the efficacy and safety of combining ICIs with chemotherapy in patients with advanced or recurrent endometrial cancer [26, 27], there remains a lack of established survival outcomes for the overall population. Furthermore, there is a dearth of head-to-head comparisons between different combination regimens involving ICIs as first-line treatment for endometrial cancer. Therefore, we conducted a systematic review and network meta-analysis (NMA) to identify the most optimal combination strategy.

Materials and methods

This systematic review and NMA adhered to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA). Before the start of the study, the protocol was registered with the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO CRD42024507862).

Data source and search strategy

The Cochrane Library, Embase, PubMed, and Web of Science databases were systematically searched from inception to December 2023. The primary search terms used were “atezolizumab OR durvalumab OR avelumab OR sintilimab OR toripalimab OR camrelizumab OR tislelizumab OR pembrolizumab OR cemipilimab OR zimberelimab OR nivolumab OR sugemalimab OR envafolimab OR ipilimumab OR programmed death ligand 1 OR programmed death 1 receptor OR PD-L1 OR PD-1 OR ICIs OR immune checkpoint inhibitors OR immunotherapy” and “endometrial cancer [MeSH]”. Additionally, conference abstracts from ESMO, American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO), and Society of Gynecologic Oncology (SGO) were reviewed to retrieve any additional relevant studies.

Study selection

To be eligible for inclusion, studies had to meet the following criteria: (1) patients with primary advanced or recurrent endometrial cancer; (2) RCTs; (3) combination of ICIs with chemotherapy compared to chemotherapy alone as first-line treatment; (4) inclusion of clinical outcomes such as OS, PFS and adverse events (AEs). Exclusion criteria included: (1) animal studies, reviews (including meta-analysis), fundamental studies, case reports, comments, and editorials; (2) non- English papers (or untranslated).

Data extraction and quality assessment

Two authors (Huang and Li) independently extracted the following data: (1) primary study information (study name, publication year, first author, trial phase, number of patients, study design and NCT number); (2) therapeutic regimens; (3) main outcomes of interest. The quality of the included studies was assessed using the Cochrane risk of bias assessment tool for RCTs, as implemented in RevMan 5.3 [28]. Each trial was evaluated for potential bias, which was then categorized as "high risk," "low risk," or "undefined risk".

Statistical analysis

The pooled HRs (hazard ratio) with 95% confidence interval (CI) for PFS and OS were calculated. Additionally, the odds ratios (ORs) with 95% CI for treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) and immune-related adverse events (irAEs) were pooled. Indirect comparisons between different therapeutic regimens of ICIs were conducted using a Bayesian methodology in WinBUGS. Therapeutic regimens ranked based on their overall ranking probability calculated through indirect comparison. Each model underwent 5000 burn-in iterations followed by 20,000 iterations, with thinning every 10th iteration. Heterogeneity was assessed by calculating I2 values. If significant heterogeneity was detected (I2 > 50% and p < 0.05) [29], a random-effects model was used to calculate the OR and HR. Otherwise, a fixed-effects model was adopted. The statistical analysis was conducted using Stata 14.0. A sensitivity analysis was conducted by removing each study to observe changes in pooled HR and OR. A subgroup analysis was performed based on the MMR status (pMMR or dMMR).

Results

Study selection and characteristics

The systematic literature search retrieved a total of 1178 potentially studies, which were independently screened by two authors (Li and Bai). A total of 793 abstracts and articles were identified after removing duplicate and irrelevant studies. One study (Westin SN [24]) contained three arms (control arm, durvalumab arm, and durvalumab + olaparib arm) based on different therapeutic regimens. Ultimately, five eligible studies comprising six reports were included, consisting of one phase II trial and four phase III trials (Fig. 1) [21–25]. The baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The total number of patients enrolled in these studies was 2702. Most studies employed double-blind design, except for one study that used an open-label design. The immunotherapy drugs included two PD-1 inhibitors and three PD-L1 inhibitors, namely avelumab (Avelu), durvalumab (Durva), pembrolizumab (Pembro), dostarlimab (Dostar), and atezolizumab (Atezo). PFS and OS in the overall population

In the overall population, combination therapy with ICIs demonstrated a significant improvement in PFS (HR = 0.69, 95% CI 0.63–0.76, p < 0.0001) and OS (HR = 0.73, 95% CI 0.63–0.85, p < 0.0001) compared to chemotherapy alone (Fig. 2). Furthermore, there was no significant heterogeneity observed in the results of PFS and OS among the included studies (I2 = 36.3% and 20.2%, respectively).Fig. 2Forest plots of ICIs plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone in the overall population. As shown in Fig. 3, the NMA suggested that the combination of ICIs with chemotherapy significantly prolonged PFS compared to chemotherapy alone. Among the five different ICIs treatment regimens, Durva-Olap-CP exhibited a significantly superior PFS benefit over Avelu-CP (HR = 0.86, 95% CI 0.76–0.98). Indirect comparisons of the remaining ICIs treatment regimens did not show any statistical differences. Regarding OS, both Durva-Olap-CP and Dostar-CP demonstrated a significant improvement compared to CP, while no significant difference was observed among other combination regimens of involving ICIs. PFS in the pMMR and dMMR population

The addition of ICIs to standard chemotherapy demonstrated a significantly prolonged PFS compared to chemotherapy alone in both the pMMR and dMMR population (HR = 0.73, 95% CI 0.60–0.89, p < 0.0001 and HR = 0.35, 95% CI 0.28–0.44, p < 0.0001, respectively) (Fig. 4). Patients with dMMR status exhibited a superior PFS advantage over those with pMMR status. There was considerable heterogeneity in the pMMR population (I2 = 66.4%, p = 0.011) among different studies; however, no significant heterogeneity was observed in the dMMR population (I2 = 0.0%, p = 0.833).Fig. 4Forest plots of ICIs plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone for PFS in the pMMR population (A) and in the dMMR population (B)

Forest plots of ICIs plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone for PFS in the pMMR population (A) and in the dMMR population (B)

The NMA findings demonstrated a significant improvement in PFS with the combination therapy of ICIs and chemotherapy compared to chemotherapy alone in the dMMR population (Fig. 5). No statistically significant differences were observed among the various combination therapies with ICIs in this population. In the pMMR population, Durva-Olap-CP and Pembro-CP exhibited a significantly reduced risk of disease progression or death compared to Avelu-CP and Atezo-CP (Fig. 5). Furthermore, Durva-CP, Durva-Olap-CP, Pembro-CP, and Dostar-CP significantly improved PFS in the pMMR population when compared to chemotherapy alone. There was no statistically significant differences observed between the remaining treatment regimens. In different ICI schemes, Dostar-CP ranked first in terms of PFS with a probability of 42.2% among dMMR population, followed by Pembro-CP (28.3%). Safety

The pairwise meta-analysis demonstrated no significant difference in any grade TRAEs (OR = 0.99, 95% CI 0.89–1.11, p = 0.910) (Fig. 6A). However, the addition of ICIs was associated with higher incidences of both grade 3–4 TRAEs (OR = 1.16, 95% CI: 1.01–1.33, p = 0.037) and any grade irAEs (OR = 2.61, 95% CI: 1.68–4.03, p < 0.0001) compared to chemotherapy alone (Fig. 6B and C).Fig. 6Forest plots of safety for ICIs combination therapy versus chemotherapy alone. Sensitivity analysis and publication bias

Sensitivity analyses confirmed the results of the primary analysis, with consistent results for each trial after separate deletions, indicating the stability of the analyses (Figure S2). Trace and density plots implied that the convergence of the chosen model was acceptable (Figure S3).

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, the present study is the first network meta-analysis to explored the efficacy and safety of ICIs as first-line treatment for advanced or recurrent endometrial cancer. The results demonstrated that combination therapy with ICIs was associated with improved PFS and OS compared to chemotherapy alone. The addition of ICIs to chemotherapy maintained a manageable safety profile. These findings provide a potential treatment option for patients with advanced or recurrent endometrial cancer.

Indirect comparisons of ICIs regimens did not show significantly differences in the overall population, except for Durva-Olap-CP, which demonstrated a significantly better PFS benefit compared to Avelu-CP. Furthermore, Durva-Olap-CP had the highest likelihood of ranking first in terms of both PFS and OS. This suggests that the addition of poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitor maintenance therapy to first-line immunotherapy may provide additional benefits. The PARP inhibitor olaparib has become the new standard of care for patients with advanced ovarian cancer carrying a BRCA1 and/or BRCA2 (BRCA) mutation [30]. Studies have shown that PARP inhibition can alleviate immunotherapy resistance and enhance efficacy by promoting cross-presentation and improving the immune microenvironment [31]. Additionally, PARP inhibitors have been found to enhance the priming and killing activity of T cells, thereby improving the response to ICIs [32]. Therefore, the combination of Durvalumab and chemotherapy, followed by Durvalumab plus olaparib maintenance therapy, substantially reduced the risk of disease progression or death.

Molecular and genomic profiling has important prognostic and therapeutic implications, thereby necessitating its integration into patient management strategies for endometrial cancer[33–36]. In both pMMR and dMMR populations, the addition of ICIs to standard chemotherapy significantly prolonged PFS compared to chemotherapy alone, with a more pronounced benefit observed in the dMMR population. Mismatch repair-deficient, a well-established biomarker of ICIs, is considered to have prognostic and predictive effects for immunotherapy in endometrial cancer [37, 38]. This may be attributed to the higher mutational burden in dMMR tumors, which leads to immune-cell recruitment, neoantigen formation, and the release of proinflammatory factors. Although the dMMR population showed greater benefit, our study also observed a benefit of immunotherapy in the pMMR population, albeit with a smaller magnitude. No significant differences were observed among the various combination regimens of ICIs in the dMMR population. However, in the pMMR population, both Durva-Olap-CP and Pembro-CP demonstraed a significantly reduction in the risk of disease progression or death compared to patients treated with Avelu-CP and Atezo-CP. Therefore, Durva-Olap-CP and Pembro-CP regimens are more recommended for endometrial cancer with pMMR status.

There was no significant difference observed in the incidence of any grade TRAEs. However, the combination of ICIs was associated with higher rates of grade 3–4 TRAEs and any grade irAE compared to chemotherapy alone. The safety profile of ICIs in combination with chemotherapy was generally consistent with the results of other studies on solid tumor [39]. There was a high degree of heterogeneity in the incidence of irAEs, and sensitivity analysis did not identify any sources of heterogeneity. Subgroup analyses were not performed due to the limited number of trials reporting irAE. Therefore, close monitoring and management of adverse events caused by the ICIs combination regimens are necessary.

There are several potential limitations to consider. Firstly, our study included an insufficient number of trials evaluating ICIs as first-line treatments for endometrial cancer. More studies are needed to further validate the efficacy of ICIs. Secondly, the included trials had a relatively short follow-up periods, and ongoing monitoring of efficacy and safety is necessary. Thirdly, NMA is an indirect comparative analysis using Bayesian principle, which may have limited validation power. In the future, more head-to-head comparative studies are needed to confirm these results.

Conclusions

The combination of ICIs with chemotherapy as the first-line treatment for advanced or recurrent endometrial cancer has demonstrated superior survival outcomes compared to chemotherapy alone. In the overall population, the Durva-Olap-CP regimen exhibited the highest efficacy in prolonging OS and PFS. In patients with dMMR, no significant differences were observed among the various combinations of ICIs. However, Dostar-CP showed potential in achieving better PFS benefits. Among patients with pMMR, both the Durva-Olap-CP and Pembro-CP regimens significantly reduced the risk for disease progression or death compared to the Avelu-CP and Atezo-CP regimens. Therefore, it is recommended to consider using either Durva-Olap-CP or Pembro-CP regimens for endometrial cancer patients with pMMR status.

Training ESRGAN with multi-scale attention U-Net discriminator
Authors: Quan Chen, Hao Li, Gehao Lu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this paper, we propose MSA-ESRGAN, a novel super-resolution model designed to enhance the perceptual quality of images. The key innovation of our approach lies in the integration of a multi-scale attention U-Net discriminator, which allows for more accurate differentiation between subject and background areas in images. By leveraging this architecture, MSA-ESRGAN surpasses traditional methods and several state-of-the-art super-resolution models in terms of Natural Image Quality Evaluator (NIQE) scores as well as Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) across various benchmark datasets, including BSD100, Set5, Set14, Urban100, and OST300. Additionally, subjective evaluations further confirm the enhanced visual quality delivered by MSA-ESRGAN, particularly in terms of preserving texture and overall image realism. To ensure a fair comparison with Real-ESRGAN, we initialized our generator with a pre-trained Real-ESRNET model and followed the same training setup. Our model was trained on the DIV2K dataset using high-resolution image patches and the Adam optimizer, incorporating exponential moving average (EMA) for stability and performance enhancement. Evaluations on multiple benchmark datasets demonstrate that MSA-ESRGAN consistently delivers superior perceptual quality, as evidenced by higher NIQE, PSNR, and SSIM scores compared to other methods. Specifically, our model shows significant improvements in both objective and subjective measures of image quality. Furthermore, an ablation study highlighted the critical role of our multi-scale attention U-Net discriminator in enhancing the model’s performance. The results underscore the effectiveness of MSA-ESRGAN in maintaining image naturalness and perceptual quality, providing a robust benchmark for blind super-resolution tasks.Keywords: Super-resolution, Multi-scale attention, U-Net discriminatorSubject terms: Computer science, Information technology

Introduction

Image super-resolution reconstruction refers to the process of generating high-resolution (HR) images from low-resolution (LR) images through software processing, simultaneously improving visual perception and enhancing visual effects1. Currently, image super-resolution reconstruction is mainly divided into two categories: traditional methods and deep learning-based methods2. Traditional methods mainly include interpolation-based, reconstruction-based, and learning-based approaches. These methods are relatively simple to implement but typically produce blurred images. Deep learning-based methods involve inputting images into complex neural networks to achieve image reconstruction by learning the mapping between low-resolution and high-resolution images. Although the training process is often lengthy, these methods have garnered more attention due to their superior reconstruction performance3. In recent years, research in the field of image super-resolution has primarily focused on deep learning techniques. Initially, SRCNN4, as a convolutional neural network model with significant performance, pioneered this trend. However, although these methods can generate images with high PSNR values, they often produce overly smooth results that lack high-frequency details5. To overcome this challenge, researchers began to explore the use of generative adversarial networks (GANs). Super-resolution GANs consist of a generator network and a discriminator network. The generator takes low-resolution (LR) images as input and aims to generate images that are as similar as possible to the original high-resolution images, while the discriminator distinguishes between the “fake” images generated by the generator and the real high-resolution images. Through the competition between the generator and the discriminator, the network tends to generate images that resemble natural images more closely. Current state-of-the-art super-resolution GAN methods include ESRGAN, Real-ESRGAN, and BSRGAN5–7. Recent research has mainly focused on simulating more complex and realistic image degradation processes6or improving the design of the generator5, with few studies attempting to enhance the performance of the discriminator. However, the discriminator is equally crucial in improving the generative ability of the generator, similar to the role of a loss function.

Real-ESRGAN is an image super-resolution method based on generative adversarial networks. By introducing a multi-scale discriminator and an adversarial loss function, it effectively reconstructs complex textures and details6. However, despite the significant advancements in image reconstruction quality achieved by Real-ESRGAN, there are still some limitations, such as its generalization ability when processing different types of images and its capability to capture high-frequency details, the ability to recover complex textures and edge details is insufficient, and the generalization ability in handling real-world images is not strong enough. To address these issues, this paper proposes an improved method based on Real-ESRGAN. We introduce a multi-scale attention fusion mechanism aimed at enhancing the model’s perception of complex structures and details in images. Compared to existing deep learning-based methods, our model not only maintains image quality but also better restores details and textures, particularly demonstrating stronger versatility and generalization in processing real-world images. Through these innovations, we aim to provide a more effective solution for image restoration and enhancement in practical applications, thereby advancing the further application and development of super-resolution technology.In this study, we propose a novel discriminator network structure: the Multi-Scale Attention U-Net Discriminator, and combine it with the existing RRDB-based generator5 to form our MSA-ESRGAN model. Our experimental results show that compared to the state-of-the-art Real-ESRGAN model, our model performs better in terms of image clarity and detail (see Figure 4). These results can be attributed to the combination of the multi-scale attention mechanism and the U-Net structure in our proposed discriminator. The U-Net structure in the discriminator provides pixel-level feedback to the generator8, which helps the generator produce more detailed features, such as textures and brushstrokes.At the same time, the attention layers not only capture the contours of the subject regions more effectively to maintain global consistency but also enhance the details within these regions. Therefore, the combination of U-Net and attention is very promising. Extensive experiments show that our model outperforms most existing GAN models in both quantitative NIQE performance metrics and qualitative image perception9.

Methods

Multi-scale attention enhanced super-resolution GAN

Degradation model

We adopted a second-order degradation model to generate low-resolution images. Compared to traditional first-order degradation models, the second-order degradation model performs the same degradation operation twice. This approach better simulates real-world conditions while maintaining simplicity and addressing most practical issues.

Generator architecture

In designing the generator architecture, we utilized stacked Residual-in-Residual Dense Blocks (RRDB), as shown in Figure 1. RRDB has demonstrated exceptional performance in super-resolution tasks and is widely used in many super-resolution methods (such as Real-ESRGAN, BSRGAN, etc.). To fully leverage the advantages of RRDB, we chose it as the core module of our generator. Additionally, RRDB effectively combines low-level and high-level features, promoting information flow and improving the details and quality of the generated images. This capability to handle complex image details is a key reason for selecting RRDB as our generator’s core component.

The generator structure diagram used in our research. RRDB means a Residual-in-Residual Dense Block.

Multi-scale attention U-Net discriminator

Inspired by5,6,10–12, we introduced the attention U-Net discriminator architecture, as shown in Fig. 2. This architecture includes a downsampling encoding module, an upsampling decoding module, and several attention blocks. The core of this architecture lies in the attention mechanism, which assigns different weights to different regions of the image, thereby enhancing the perception of complex structures and details. Specifically, the downsampling encoding module gradually reduces the spatial resolution while extracting high-level semantic features; the upsampling decoding module progressively restores the spatial resolution while combining high-level features from the encoding phase to achieve detail reconstruction.

The overall architecture of the multi-scale attention discriminator. The CS-AFM mainly consists of CAM and SAM.

The detailed structure of the attention modules is shown in Fig. 2. These modules enhance important features and suppress irrelevant ones by calculating weighted sums of the input features. As emphasized in13, attention gates were initially used for semantic segmentation in 3D medical images, and we have now adapted and applied them to the super-resolution task for 2D images. By incorporating features of different scales within the attention modules, we capture richer contextual information in the images, leading to superior performance in detail restoration.

Additionally, drawing from the Real-ESRGAN framework6, we incorporated spectral normalization regularization14 to enhance the stability of the training process. Spectral normalization standardizes the network weights by limiting the norm of feature mappings, thus improving the stability and convergence speed of the model training. This regularization method is particularly important in training adversarial generative networks (GANs) as it effectively mitigates mode collapse and gradient vanishing issues.

Through these innovative designs, our model not only significantly improves image quality but also excels in recovering complex textures and edge details. It demonstrates strong generalization ability and stability, especially in real-world image super-resolution tasks. We believe that the proposed method provides new insights and approaches for the development of image super-resolution technology, contributing to further research and applications in this field.

Experiments

Training details

We trained our MSA-ESRGAN model on the DIV2K dataset. To ensure a fair comparison with Real-ESRGAN, we initialized the generator of MSA-ESRGAN with the pretrained Real-ESRNET model, adhering to the training settings used for Real-ESRGAN. During training, we utilized a high-resolution image patch size of 256 and employed the Adam optimizer. The training was conducted on a single NVIDIA A10 GPU with a total batch size of 6, a learning rate set to 10-4, and a total of 400K iterations. To achieve more stable training and improved performance, we adopted Exponential Moving Average (EMA), which helps reduce fluctuations and accelerate convergence. It should be noted that, due to experimental constraints, a smaller total batch size was utilized in this study. Under better experimental conditions, increasing the total batch size could accelerate the training process. The training of MSA-ESRGAN incorporates a blend of L1 loss, GAN loss, and perceptual loss, assigned weights of {1, 0.1, 1} respectively. For the perceptual loss, we extract feature maps from the {conv1, ..., conv5} layers of the pretrained VGG19 network, applying weights of {0.1, 0.1, 1, 1, 1} to these maps, which are taken before activation, as these layers effectively capture hierarchical features that contribute significantly to perceptual quality.

The MSA-ESRGAN model’s generator, based on the RRDBNet structure, consists of 23 residual dense blocks, with approximately 16.7 million trainable parameters, a reasonable number for maintaining efficiency without sacrificing capability. The discriminator, utilizing a U-Net-based architecture, incorporates channel and spatial attention mechanisms, which modestly increases the overall parameter count. Channel attention, using global pooling and point-wise convolution (PWConv), contributes to this increase, while spatial attention, applied through convolutions over feature maps, adds even fewer parameters. Overall, these additions have minimal impact on model complexity while significantly enhancing feature discrimination.

Testing datasets

We evaluated our method on five widely used benchmark datasets: Set5, Set14, BSD100, OST300, and Urban10016–20. Set5 and Set14 are classic datasets containing a mix of human, animal, and natural scenes. BSD100 and Urban100 provide more diverse and challenging scenes with high-frequency details, such as urban landscapes and natural textures, while OST300 offers natural landscapes, cityscapes, and some artificially synthesized scenes. These datasets are commonly used in the super-resolution literature and are suitable for evaluating the performance of image super-resolution algorithms, achieving a comprehensive assessment of model performance. We believe that a good general-purpose super-resolution model should perform well across all five datasets.

To further validate our method, we conducted extensive experiments comparing MSA-ESRGAN with several existing state-of-the-art models. Detailed performance evaluations were performed on each dataset, including comparisons using the PSNR, SSIM, and NIQE metrics, as well as subjective evaluations of visual effects. By incorporating both objective metrics and subjective assessments, we provide a more balanced view of model performance. This not only helps to validate the effectiveness of our method but also offers a comprehensive understanding of how different models perform in real-world image processing tasks. Through these experiments, we aim to establish more reliable evaluation benchmarks and provide robust criteria for model selection in blind image super-resolution tasks.

User study

Subjective evaluation methods play a crucial role in image quality assessment alongside objective evaluation methods. While objective measures like PSNR and SSIM provide numerical metrics, they cannot fully replicate the human visual system’s perception of images. Therefore, subjective evaluation methods, which invite observers to perform actual assessments and evaluations, offer a more realistic perception of image quality. Specifically, we extracted a total of 100 images from the test data as input, using the size of the test set as weights. We then performed super-resolution reconstruction using Bicubic, BSRGAN, RealSR, ESRGAN, Real-ESRGAN, and MSA-ESRGAN, which were rated by users. We classified the user ratings into five levels, as shown in Table 3. In this study, a total of 100 participants, including educators and students from four different universities, were invited to evaluate the images. To ensure the integrity of the assessment, participants were kept blind to which images had been reconstructed using specific methods. Finally, to provide a clearer comparison of the user study results, we plotted the distribution of average scores, as illustrated in Figure 5. As shown in the scores, Bicubic receives the lowest score of 1.25, indicating lower perceived quality, while MSA-ESRGAN achieves the highest score of 4.84, suggesting superior image quality according to the observers. Other methods, such as BSRGAN, RealSR, ESRGAN, and Real-ESRGAN, also demonstrate competitive performance, with scores ranging from 3.95 to 4.60. This further demonstrates that MSA-ESRGAN excels in image reconstruction quality.

First, when observing the results from using only CAM, the NIQE metric indicates a lower perceptual quality of the generated images, especially with a NIQE score of 2.7947 on the OST300 dataset, which is higher than CS-AFM’s score of 2.7054. This suggests that using only the channel attention module fails to fully capture spatial information, particularly global structural details, leading to a decline in image quality. The PSNR and SSIM results are also slightly lower than those of CS-AFM, indicating that while channel attention contributes to some local detail enhancement, the overall reconstruction performance is not as ideal as when combined with spatial attention. For instance, on the BSD100 dataset, PSNR and SSIM values are 29.07 and 0.8231, respectively, both lower than CS-AFM’s scores of 30.12 and 0.8834. Next, when observing the results from using only SAM, the model performs better than CAM on certain datasets, such as Set5 and OST300. However, on other datasets, like BSD100 and Set14, CAM achieves higher PSNR and SSIM values. This indicates that spatial attention helps capture local structural information in images, but it lacks the ability to model inter-channel relationships as effectively as CAM. For example, on the Set14 dataset, SAM achieves a NIQE value of 4.2901, slightly higher than CS-AFM’s 4.1786, and the PSNR and SSIM values on the OST300 dataset are 30.73 and 0.8849, respectively-higher than those of CAM but still falling short of CS-AFM’s 31.63 and 0.9175. Thus, while SAM improves performance over CAM in some cases, it still cannot match the combined benefits of CS-AFM.

CS-AFM outperforms both CAM and SAM across all benchmark datasets by effectively combining channel and spatial attention, enhancing image reconstruction quality by capturing fine details and structural features. For instance, on the Set5 dataset, CS-AFM achieves a PSNR of 32.01, surpassing CAM and SAM’s scores of 31.12 and 30.89, respectively. This fusion leads to improved clarity and quality of the reconstructed images. Additionally, CS-AFM excels in the NIQE metric, achieving the lowest value of 2.7054 on the OST300 dataset, indicating superior perceptual quality. Overall, while channel attention enhances inter-channel relationships and fine details, spatial attention captures local spatial information. Their combination leverages each mechanism’s strengths, significantly improving reconstruction quality in super-resolution tasks.

Conclusion

This paper proposes a multi-scale attention U-Net discriminator for training deep blind super-resolution models, introducing the core module, CS-AFM, to achieve multi-scale attention fusion. With this improved discriminator, we train a deep blind super-resolution model and conduct experiments on five benchmark datasets using real-world images. Compared to other state-of-the-art generative methods, our model demonstrates superior performance not only in NIQE metrics but also in PSNR and SSIM, significantly enhancing both quantitative and qualitative image quality. The results show that our model consistently outperforms most existing models. Furthermore, our study highlights the crucial role of the multi-scale attention mechanism in improving image quality, PSNR, SSIM, and model robustness.

Discussion

Our experimental results show that MSA-ESRGAN outperforms several state-of-the-art super-resolution models, including Bicubic, BSRGAN, RealSR, ESRGAN, and Real-ESRGAN, across multiple benchmark datasets. The superior NIQE scores highlight the effectiveness of our multi-scale attention U-Net discriminator, which enhances perceptual quality by effectively distinguishing between the subject and background.

While MSA-ESRGAN shows promising results, it has limitations. The model was trained and evaluated on standard benchmark datasets, which may not fully represent the diversity of real-world images. Additionally, the multi-scale attention mechanism increases computational cost. Future research should focus on optimizing efficiency without compromising performance.

1 Corrigendum

1.1 First Corrigendum

The initial version encoded a Gödel sentence as a Diophantine system rather than a single equation. The ambition to present a concrete standalone equation introduced an unwarranted bias into the construction. Subsequent algorithmic approaches yielded a massive closed form, but the reduction diverted attention from the central epistemic questions.

1.2 Second Corrigendum

In the second revision, we pivoted to explicit witness construction, ensuring all constraints remain genuinely cubic, and grounded the argument in logical foundations rather than forcing a premature encoding.

1.3 Third Corrigendum

In this third—still preliminary—version, we resolve ambiguities between logical and mathematical layers (
\mathbb{Z}
versus
\mathbb{N}
), and improve on bookkeeping. The first-order argument is developed independently of its Diophantine realization. We provide a high-level overview at the outset, then verify each layer on its own terms.

While

, MRDP concerned itself with

2.2 Roadmap

This work establishes the undecidability of cubic Diophantine problems over
\mathbb{N}
through two complementary results: systems of equations each of degree at most three are undecidable, and such systems can be aggregated into single cubic equations preserving undecidability. This lowers the degree threshold from Matiyasevich’s quartic bound to cubic for both formulations, establishing sharpness since degree-two equations remain decidable via Lagrange’s four-square theorem. The system-versus-single-equation distinction carries technical weight: our construction naturally produces systems with favorable structure—
O(KN^{3})
equations in
O(KN^{3})
variables—while aggregation via sum-of-squares followed by recursive monomial shielding inflates variable count to
O(K^{3}N^{3})
for the final single cubic polynomial.

The restriction to
\mathbb{N}
rather than
\mathbb{Z}
is essential. Cubic equations over
\mathbb{Z}
admit decidability through Thue equations (Baker’s transcendence methods), Hasse principle obstructions, and elliptic curve descent via Mordell–Weil theory. Undecidability over
\mathbb{N}
exploits combinatorial complexity introduced by non-negativity constraints, enforced polynomially through guard-gadgets: replacing constraints
E(\mathbf{x})=0
of degree at most two with the guarded system
u\cdot E(\mathbf{x})=0
,
u=1+v^{2}
maintains cubic degree while forcing
u\geq 1
. Naive activation variables or difference-of-squares encodings produce degree-four terms, necessitating this construction.

While our construction produces a

Proposition 2.3

(Impossibility of Universal Cubic Equation)

There exists no polynomial

within an
\omega

\leq 3
such that for all Turing machines
M

M\text{ halts}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}:P_{\text{univ}}(n,\mathbf{x})=0.

(2.15)

Proof sketch.

Assume such
P_{\text{univ}}
exists. For each machine
M

n

in order of increasing norm
|\mathbf{x}|_{1}=\sum_{i}x_{i}

\Omega=\sum_{M\text{ halts}}2^{-|M|}

3.1 The Gödel Beta-Function

The following logic allows encoding arbitrary finite sequences as Diophantine constraints:

Procedure 3.1

(Diophantine
\beta
-Function)
.

By the work of
Gödel 1931
(using the
“Chinese remainder”
) and as exposited in
Matiyasevich 1993
, there exists a polynomial
\beta(c,d,i)
over
\mathbb{N}
such that for any finite sequence
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
c,d\in\mathbb{N}
with

\beta(c,d,i)=a_{i}\quad\text{for all }i\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}.

(3.17)

Explicitly,
\beta(c,d,i)=r_{i}
, where
r_{i}
is the remainder and
q_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
is the quotient when dividing
c
by
(1+(i+1)d)
:

c=q_{i}\cdot(1+(i+1)d)+r_{i},\quad 0\leq r_{i}<1+(i+1)d.

(3.18)

Remark
.

The
“Chinese remainder”
allows us to choose
d
such that the moduli
\{1+(i+1)d:0\leq i\leq n\}
are pairwise coprime. A suitable choice is

d=\text{lcm}(1,2,\ldots,n+1),

(3.19)

which ensures that for any
i\neq j
, the moduli
1+(i+1)d
and
1+(j+1)d
differ by
(j-i)d
, and this difference shares no common factor with either modulus, since

1+(k+1)d\equiv 1\pmod{p}\quad\text{for all primes}\quad p\mid d\quad\text{with}\quad p\leq n+1.

Hence we construct
c
via CRT to satisfy
c\equiv a_{i}\pmod{1+(i+1)d}
for all
i\leq n
. To verify pairwise coprimality with
d=\text{lcm}(1,\ldots,n+1)
: for
i<j\leq n
, we have

\gcd(1+(i+1)d,1+(j+1)d)\mid\gcd(1+(i+1)d,(j-i)d).

(3.20)

Since
(j-i)\leq n
and
d
is divisible by all integers up to
n+1
, while
1+(i+1)d\equiv 1\pmod{p}
for any prime
p\mid d
with
p\leq n+1
, the greatest common divisor equals
1
. This ensures the CRT applies. To express
\beta(c,d,i)=f_{i}
as polynomial constraints, we introduce variables
r_{i},q_{i}
and enforce:

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=q_{i}\cdot(1+(i+1)d)+r_{i},

(3.21)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq r_{i}<1+(i+1)d.

(3.22)

The second challenge, after reducing
\Sigma_{1}
logic to
\Delta_{0}
4
4

4

Foundational reference by
Kleene 1943
introducing concepts underlying the arithmetic hierarchy
, is encoding inequality polynomially. A naive preliminary approach using
r_{i}=s_{i}^{2}
to force

failed because: (i) the
\beta

that need not be perfect squares (e.g.,

(1+(i+1)d-r_{i}-1)=t_{i}^{2}

Lemma 3.2

(Four-Squares Bound Encoding)
.

The constraint
0\leq r<m
over
\mathbb{N}
is equivalent to the existence of integers
a,b,c,e,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying:

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}+e^{2},

(3.23)

\displaystyle m-1-r

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2}+\delta^{2}.

(3.24)

These are polynomial equations of degree
2
.

Proof.

The
“Four-Square Theorem”
of
Lagrange 1770
allows us to say:

Every

is a sum of four squares.

Constraint (
4.48
) forces
r\geq 0
. Constraint (
4.49
) forces
r\leq m-1
, hence
r<m
. Given
r^{*}\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
, the four-square decompositions can be computed in
polynomial time
. By the algorithm of
Rabin and Shallit 1986
we decompose an integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
into four squares

n=a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}+e^{2}

(3.25)

This ensures the encoding is
effective.
Computable witnesses exist for any valid bound, and can be constructed in polynomial time (randomized) or
quasi-polynomial time
(deterministic under ERH).
∎

Remark
.

3.2 Unique Representation

Remark
.

As a

“gaps”

\{F_{6},F_{8},F_{10}\}=\{8,21,55\}

Definition 3.3

(Zeckendorf Representation)
.

Every positive integer
n
admits a unique representation as a sum of non-consecutive Fibonacci numbers:

n=\sum_{\kappa\in S}F_{\kappa},

(3.26)

where
S\subseteq\{2,3,4,\ldots\}
contains no consecutive integers (i.e., if

This maintains the overall degree bound of

for the entire system.

7.5 Impredicativity

Thesis 7.6

(Impredicativity of Degree Threshold)
.

Let
\mathrm{T}
be any consistent, recursively axiomatizable theory extending Robinson arithmetic
\mathsf{Q}
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{D}_{k}
denote the class of Diophantine equations with
\delta\leq k
. Define:

\mathsf{Dec}(k)\equiv\text{\ltxml@oqmark@open\textquotedblleft\penalty\hskip-0.0002pt\hskip 0.0002ptthere exists an algorithm deciding solvability for all $\mathcal{D}_{k}$\textquotedblright\ltxml@oqmark@close{}}.

(7.92)

Then there exists no first-order formula
B(k)
in the language of arithmetic such that
\mathrm{T}
proves:

1.

\exists!k\;B(k)
(uniqueness of threshold),

2.

\forall m<k\;\mathsf{Dec}(m)
(decidability below threshold),

3.

\forall m\geq k\;\neg\mathsf{Dec}(m)
(undecidability at and above threshold).

Proof sketch.

Suppose such
B(k)
exists with unique
k_{0}
satisfying
\mathrm{T}\vdash B(k_{0})
. By the diagonal lemma, construct a Diophantine equation
E_{k_{0}}
with
\delta(E_{k_{0}})=k_{0}
whose solvability encodes
\neg B(k_{0})
within
\mathrm{T}
. If
E_{k_{0}}
is solvable, then
\mathrm{T}\vdash\neg B(k_{0})
, contradicting uniqueness. If
E_{k_{0}}
is unsolvable, then
\mathcal{D}_{k_{0}}
contains a decidable equation, contradicting clause (3). Hence no such
B(k)
is definable in
\mathrm{T}
.
∎

Exposition
.

The impredicativity arises from self-reference: defining
k
as
“the first undecidable degree”
enables constructing an equation of degree
k
that encodes the negation of this very claim. This mirrors the Grelling–Nelson paradox and reflects the predicativity restriction in the type theory of
Martin-Löf 1980
, where universes
\mathcal{U}_{i}
cannot quantify over themselves.

Thesis 7.7

(Diophantine Impredicativity)
.

In the Calculus of Inductive Constructions, attempting to form:

\mathsf{Threshold}:\Sigma_{k:\mathbb{N}}\left(\Pi_{m<k}\mathsf{Decidable}(\mathcal{D}_{m})\right)\times\left(\Pi_{m\geq k}\neg\mathsf{Decidable}(\mathcal{D}_{m})\right)

(7.93)

requires impredicative quantification over the decidability predicate
\mathsf{Decidable}(-)
, which itself depends on the threshold
k
being defined. By Thesis
7.6
, no closed term of this type exists in any consistent extension of
\mathsf{Q}
. The diagonal equation
E_{k_{0}}
acts as a negative membership witness:

E_{k_{0}}:\mathcal{D}_{k_{0}}\quad\text{with}\quad\mathsf{Solvable}(E_{k_{0}})\leftrightarrow\neg B(k_{0}),

(7.94)

precisely the impredicative loop forbidden in stratified type theories.

Corollary 7.8

(Stratification Necessity)
.

Any formal system capable of deciding
\mathcal{D}_{k}
for some
k
must either:

1.

Externalize the threshold
: Define
B(k)
in a strictly stronger metatheory (e.g.,
\mathsf{PA}
proves undecidability of
\mathcal{D}_{3}
using Gödel sentences not formalizable in
\mathsf{Q}

2.

Adopt impredicative axioms
: E.g.,
\mathsf{ZFC}
proves existence of a least undecidable degree by quantifying over all sets of equations via the power set axiom (which is impredicative).

The cubic bound
k=3
established here is not an internal theorem of any predicative arithmetic.

Exposition
.

Under the Brouwer-Heyting-Kolmogorov interpretation, a proof of
\mathsf{Dec}(k)\lor\neg\mathsf{Dec}(k)
requires either:

1.

A witness algorithm
M_{k}
deciding all equations in
\mathcal{D}_{k}
, or

2.

A refutation proving no such
M_{k}
exists (via diagonalization).

At the threshold
k=3

1.

No algorithm exists (by MRDP and Corollary

2.

No uniform refutation exists (by Thesis
7.6
, any such refutation enables constructing

Thus, the threshold constitutes a Brouwerian counterexample to the law of excluded middle: a statement
P

P
nor
\neg P
admits constructive proof. The degree-

\mathsf{ZFC}
proves
“
\mathcal{D}_{3}
is undecidable”
by encoding the halting problem), but no predicative subsystem of
\mathsf{PA}
can internalize this fact.

Abstract
This paper proposes a comprehensive and unprecedented framework that streamlines the derivation of exact, compact—yet tractable—solutions for the probability density function (PDF) and cumulative distribution function (CDF) of the sum of a broad spectrum of mixed independent positive random variables (RVs). To showcase the framework’s potential and extensive applicability, we tackle the enduring challenge of obtaining these statistics for the sum of fading variates in an exact, manageable, and unified manner. Specifically, we derive novel, tractable expressions for the PDF and CDF of the sum of Gaussian-class and non-Gaussian-class fading distributions, thereby covering a plethora of conventional, generalized, and recently introduced fading models. The proposed framework accommodates independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) sums, independent but not necessarily identically distributed (i.n.i.d.) sums, and mixed-type sums. Moreover, we introduce the strikingly novel α \alpha - μ \mu mixture distribution that unifies all Gaussian-class fading models.

I-B

Drawbacks and Limitations of Existing Solutions

I-B
1
Computational Burden

Most existing exact solutions for the sum statistics (PDF and CDF) of fading distributions rely on the Lauricella hypergeometric series, the multivariate Fox H-function, or multi-fold integrals.
However, these approaches have significant drawbacks and limitations. For instance, neither the Lauricella hypergeometric series nor the multivariate Fox H-function is available as a built-in routine in standard mathematical tools. As a result, these functions must be implemented based on their formal definitions, which involve the evaluation of multi-dimensional infinite-range integrals and nested infinite series
[
63
,
64
]
.
Moreover, both the multivariate Fox H-function and multi-fold integration are strictly numerical in nature, offering no mechanism for accuracy control as their reliability depends entirely on the software’s numerical integration method and initial parameter settings.
In addition, the computational burden and mathematical complexity of evaluating the Lauricella hypergeometric series or the multivariate Fox H-function scale sharply with the number of RVs in the sum, becoming impractical when this number is relatively large, say above six.

I-B
2
Homogeneous and Heterogeneous Sums

Throughout the literature, sums of fading distributions are primarily restricted to homogeneous sums (i.e., sums of independent RVs following the same statistical distribution).
To date, the only general method to calculate the exact statistics of heterogeneous sums—referred to here simply as mixed sums—of fading distributions (i.e., sums of independent RVs following different statistical distributions) is by numerically evaluating the multifold Brennan’s integral
[
45
]
.
However, this multifold convolution approach is overly general to be useful, computationally intensive, and prone to convergence instability, especially as the number of RVs in the sum grows.

VII
Conclusions

This paper solved a longstanding problem in statistics—the sum of independent positive variates—which has direct implications for channel modeling and performance assessment in wireless communication systems.
In this paper, we derived novel, tractable expressions for the chief statistics of the sum of Gaussian-class and non-Gaussian-class fading distributions.
The analysis covers i.i.d. sums, i.n.i.d. sums, and mixed-type sums.
Additionally, we introduced the
\alpha
-
\mu
mixture distribution as a means to unify all Gaussian-class fading models.

\displaystyle\left|\phi_{0,\ell}\right|=\frac{\left|\eta_{1,\ell}\right|}{\left|\eta_{0,\ell}\right|}\leq\frac{\eta^{*}}{\left|\eta_{0,\ell}\right|}.

Using the triangle inequality in (

\displaystyle\left|\phi_{h,\ell}\right|\overset{(a)}{\leq}

\displaystyle\left|\delta_{i}\right|\overset{(a)}{\leq}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i}\sum_{h=1}^{i}\left|\delta_{i-h}\right|\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\left|\phi_{h-1,\ell}\right|

\displaystyle\frac{\phi^{*}L}{i}\sum_{h=1}^{i}\left|\delta_{i-h}\right|\overset{(c)}{=}\frac{L\delta_{0}^{*}\phi^{*}}{i!}\left(L\phi^{*}+1\right)_{i-1},

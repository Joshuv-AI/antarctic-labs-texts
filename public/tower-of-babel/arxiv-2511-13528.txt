Rankwidth of Graphs with Balanced Separations: Expansion for Dense Graphs
Emile Anand

Abstract
We prove that every graph of rankwidth at least 72 ​ r 72r contains an induced subgraph whose minimum balanced cutrank is at least r r , which implies a vertex subset where every balanced separation has 𝔽 2 \mathbb{F}_{2} -cutrank at least r r . This implies a novel relation between rankwidth and a well-linkedness measure, defined entirely by balanced vertex cuts. As a byproduct, our result supports the notion of rank-expansion as a suitable candidate for measuring expansion in dense graphs.

Proof overview.

Our proof proceeds by way of contradiction. If a graph has high rankwidth but low min-bal-cutrank, then every part of the graph can be cleanly separated with a low-rank cut. We introduce the tangle in definition
2.4
, which points to the ‘more connected’ component of a separation of a graph. We use it to find a separation
(X,Y)
such that the tangle-selected component is maximally connected. Subject to this, we assume
X
is maximal and call it the small side, following the convention of
[
27
]
. We then
compress

X
by selecting a subset of representative vertices, and show that finding a low-rank balanced cut in this compressed graph would allow us to extend
X
, which contradicts its maximality. Our proof continues via three stages.

First, in Claim
4.1
, we adapt the tangle-and-congestion machinery of Dvořák and Norin
[
27
]
to extract the more strongly-connected side
X
of a low-rank separation
(X,Y)
that is maximally large yet
well-behaved
. This well-behaved property formalizes the idea that
X
is robustly connected to
Y
, meaning no small component of
X
can be separated from the rest of the graph with a low-rank cut. Next, we observe that the number of distinct neighborhood patterns that vertices in
Y
can form within
X
is bounded. This allows us to find a small subset of representative vertices in
X
that captures all of these connections, effectively compressing
X
without losing essential rank information. Specifically, we compress
X
by taking one representative per
Y
-neighborhood class (Claim
4.2
), which preserves
\operatorname{cutrank}(X,Y)
; then, in Lemma
4.3
, we form an induced subgraph
H^{\prime}
so that any balanced cut of
H^{\prime}
of rank at most
r
splits
Y
and yields two lifted separations of
G
whose cutranks are at most
72r
; combined with the tangle axiom this yields the existence of a strongly-connected side in
V(G)
that is strictly larger than
X
, thereby contradicting the maximality assumption.

Definition 3.1
.

Suppose

is a partition of

. For each

Let the number of equivalence classes be denoted by

since the rank cannot exceed the number of distinct column types (equivalence classes), and over

Let

.

Proof.

Let

be their corresponding columns in

as vectors in

. Since the rows of

span the row space of

are equivalent with respect to

.∎

. This mechanism allows us to amplify the size of the sets

to get the final contradiction. . Iteratively,
X_{7}\cup U_{1}^{(0)}
is a small side as well. Then,
X_{0}\subseteq\dots\subseteq X_{8}
and
Y_{0}\supseteq\dots\supseteq Y_{8}
. Note that for
i\in\{1,\dots,7\}
, we have due to subadditivity and

\displaystyle\operatorname{cutrank}(X_{i+1},Y_{i+1})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{cutrank}(X_{i}\cup U_{1}^{(i)},U_{2}^{(i)}

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{cutrank}(X_{i},U_{2}^{(i)})+\operatorname{cutrank}(U_{1}^{(i)},U_{2}^{(i)})

\displaystyle<\operatorname{cutrank}(X_{i},Y_{i})+r,

that recursively

\displaystyle\operatorname{cutrank}(X_{8},Y_{8})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{cutrank}(X_{7}\cup U_{1}^{(7)},U_{2}^{(7)})

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{cutrank}(X_{7},U_{2}^{(7)})+\operatorname{cutrank}(U_{1}^{(7)},U_{2}^{(7)})

\displaystyle<\operatorname{cutrank}(X_{7},U_{2}^{(7)})+r

\displaystyle<\operatorname{cutrank}(X_{7},Y_{7})+r

\displaystyle<\operatorname{cutrank}(X_{0},Y_{0})+8r,

as removing vertices from one side can never increase the rank.

Therefore,
\operatorname{cutrank}
(
X_{8},Y_{8}
)
<60r+8r=68r
. Further, from an earlier argument
\tau
chooses
X_{8}
to be the small side of
(X_{8},Y_{8})
. Then, since
|X|=|A|+|B|
and
|X_{8}|\geq|B|+\sum_{i=0}^{7}|U_{1}^{(i)}|
, noting that
(U_{1}^{(i)},U_{2}^{(i)})
is balanced in
G[Y_{i}]
gives that
|U_{1}^{(i)}|\geq|Y_{i}|/3
. This gives a recurrence in the form of

|Y_{i+1}|=|U_{2}^{(i)}|\leq\frac{2}{3}|Y_{i}|.

So,
|Y_{i}|\leq(2/3)^{i}|Y_{0}|
and

\displaystyle|U_{1}^{(i)}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{3}|Y_{i}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{2}{3}}}\right)^{i}|Y_{0}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{2}{3}}}\right)^{i}|A\cup Y|.

Together, this gives us that

\displaystyle|X_{8}|

\displaystyle\geq|B|+\frac{1}{3}\sum_{k=0}^{7}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{2}{3}}}\right)^{k}|A\cup Y|

\displaystyle\geq|B|+0.96|A\cup Y|

since we remove at least
1/3
rd of the vertices of
U_{2}^{i}
between successive iterations for
i\in\{0,\dots,7\}
. Furthermore, we have that

|X|=|B\cup A|=|B|+|A|.

To obtain a contradiction, we show that
|X_{8}|>|X|
which is equivalent to
|Y|\geq 0.04|A|
as
A\cap Y=\emptyset
. Specifically, for our contradiction to hold, we need to show

\displaystyle|X_{8}|

\displaystyle>|B|+0.96|A\cup Y|

\displaystyle=|B|+0.96(|A|+|Y|).

Since
|X|=|A|+|B|
, this is equivalent to showing
0.96|Y|\geq 0.04|A|
(or
|Y|>\frac{|A|}{24}
).

However, since we show (the stronger)
|A|\leq 6s=18|Y|
, the contradiction holds, i.e., we have derived that
X_{8}
is a valid side of a partition of
V(G)
such that
|X_{8}|>|X_{0}|
, which is a contradiction.

Therefore, we conclude that
(X,Y)
is
(3|Y|,1/7)
-well-behaved. ∎

4.2
The Compressed Subgraph
H

We now compress the small side without increasing the relevant cutranks. Our strategy is to find a contradiction to the maximality of
X
. To do this, we must analyze the connections between
X
and
Y
. We begin by compressing
X
into a smaller, representative set
X^{*}
without losing the essential rank information, as justified by the neighborhood equivalence classes discussed in Section
3
.

Claim 4.2
.

There exists an induced subgraph
H\subseteq G
which is obtained from
G
by deleting some vertices from
X
such that for all
v\in X
, there exists at least one vertex
v^{\prime}\in X\cap V(H)\eqqcolon X^{*}
such that
N(v)\cap Y=N(v^{\prime})\cap Y
.

Proof.

We choose
H
such that for every vertex
v^{\prime}\in X
, there is at least one vertex
v\in X\cap V(H)
so that
N(v)\cap Y=N(v^{\prime})\cap Y
, thus satisfying the condition and giving
\operatorname{cutrank}(H\cap X,Y)=\operatorname{cutrank}(X,Y)
. In other words,
H
is constructed by selecting one vertex per equivalence class (as defined in Section
3
).
∎

Having replaced
X
by
X^{*}
(one representative per class), we obtain an induced subgraph
H
with
\operatorname{cutrank}(X^{*},Y)=\operatorname{cutrank}(X,Y)<70r
. We now form an induced
H^{\prime}
in which every balanced low‑rank cut must split
Y
. This forces any balanced separation to interact non-trivially with the uncompressed section,
Y
.

Lemma 4.3

(Splitting
Y
)
.

There exists an induced subgraph
H^{\prime}\subseteq H
and a balanced partition
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
of
H^{\prime}
such that
A^{\prime}\cap Y\neq\emptyset
and
B^{\prime}\cap Y\neq\emptyset
,
\operatorname{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})<r
, and if
X^{**}\eqqcolon V(H^{\prime})\cap X^{*}
, then

\operatorname{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(X^{**},Y)\leq\operatorname{cutrank}_{H}(X^{*},Y)=\operatorname{cutrank}_{G}(X,Y)<70r.

Proof.

Pick any subset
X^{**}\subseteq X^{*}
with
|X^{**}|<|Y|/2
and set
H^{\prime}\coloneqq H[Y\cup X^{**}]
. Then,
\frac{|Y|}{|V(H^{\prime})|}=\frac{|Y|}{|Y|+|X^{**}|}
is greater than
\frac{2}{3}
if and only if
|Y|>2|X^{**}|
. Since no side of a balanced separation of
H^{\prime}
can contain all of
Y
(because that side would exceed
2|V(H^{\prime})|/3
), every balanced partition
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
of
H^{\prime}
necessarily satisfies
A^{\prime}\cap Y\neq\emptyset
and
B^{\prime}\cap Y\neq\emptyset
.

By applying the contradiction hypothesis to the induced subgraph
H^{\prime}
, there exists a balanced separation
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
of
H^{\prime}
with
\operatorname{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})<r
, which then splits
Y
by the aforementioned argument. Next, since
X^{**}\subseteq X^{*}
, taking the submatrix induced by
X^{**}
can only reduce the rank of the
X^{*}\times Y
biadjacency matrix; therefore, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(X^{**},Y)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{cutrank}_{H}(X^{*},Y)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{cutrank}_{G}(X,Y),

and Claim
4.1
gives
\operatorname{cutrank}_{G}(X,Y)<70r
.∎

To control the rank of cuts within the subgraph
H^{\prime}
, we first need a general way to relate the rank of a matrix to the rank of its sub-blocks. The following general lemmas provide such inequalities.

Lemma 4.4

(Frobenius rank inequality
[
44
]
)
.

For any conforming triple of matrices
(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V},\mathbf{W})
, we have

\rank(\mathbf{U}\mathbf{V})+\rank(\mathbf{V}\mathbf{W})\leq\rank(\mathbf{V})+\rank(\mathbf{U}\mathbf{V}\mathbf{W}).

Lemma 4.5
.

For a block matrix
\mathbf{M}
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
with conforming matrices
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D})
given by

\mathbf{M}\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{A}&\mathbf{B}\\
\mathbf{C}&\mathbf{D}\end{bmatrix},

we have

\rank(\mathbf{M})+\rank(\mathbf{B})\geq\rank(\mathbf{A})+\rank(\mathbf{D}).

Proof.

Take

\mathbf{U}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{I}&\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix},\qquad\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{M}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{A}&\mathbf{B}\\
\mathbf{C}&\mathbf{D}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathbf{W}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{I}\end{bmatrix},

where the identity blocks have the sizes of
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{D}
respectively.
Then

\mathbf{UV}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{A}&\mathbf{B}\end{bmatrix},\qquad\mathbf{VW}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{B}\\
\mathbf{D}\end{bmatrix},\qquad\mathbf{UVW}=\mathbf{B}.

Plugging these into Lemma
4.4
with Frobenius’ inequality gives

\displaystyle\rank(\mathbf{M})+\rank(\mathbf{B})

\displaystyle=\rank(\mathbf{V})+\rank(\mathbf{UVW})

\displaystyle\geq\rank(\mathbf{UV})+\rank(\mathbf{VW})

\displaystyle=\rank\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{A}&\mathbf{B}\end{bmatrix}}}\right)+\rank\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{B}\\
\mathbf{D}\end{bmatrix}}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\rank(\mathbf{A})+\rank(\mathbf{D}),

where the last inequality follows by noting that the rank is non-decreasing when taking a submatrix (by removing columns/rows), and therefore

\rank(\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{A} & \mathbf{B}\end{bmatrix})\geq\rank(\mathbf{A})

and

\rank\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{B}\\
\mathbf{D}\end{bmatrix}}}\right)\geq\rank(\mathbf{D}).

Together, these yield the desired inequality.∎

With these algebraic tools, we now apply them to the specific cut
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
within the subgraph
H^{\prime}
. The following lemma uses the block-matrix inequality from Lemma
4.5
to derive a crucial upper bound on the ranks of the sub-partitions within
H^{\prime}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

We have that

\operatorname{cutrank}(X,Y)\geq\operatorname{cutrank}(A\cap X,A\cap Y)+\operatorname{cutrank}(B\cap X,B\cap Y)-\operatorname{cutrank}(A,B).

Proof.

Let
X_{1}\coloneqq A\cap X,X_{2}\coloneqq B\cap X,Y_{1}\coloneqq A\cap Y
and
Y_{2}\coloneqq B\cap Y
.

Next, construct the biadjacency matrix
\mathbf{T}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{(|X_{1}|+|X_{2}|)\times(|Y_{1}|+|Y_{2}|)}
given by

\mathbf{T}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{A}&\mathbf{B}\\
\mathbf{C}&\mathbf{D}\end{bmatrix},

such that the rows of
\mathbf{T}
are
X_{1}
concatenated with
X_{2}
and the columns of
\mathbf{T}
are
Y_{1}
concatenated with
Y_{2}
; here,
\mathbf{A}
denotes the submatrix
\mathbf{T}[X_{1},Y_{1}]
,
\mathbf{B}
denotes the submatrix
\mathbf{T}[X_{1},Y_{2}],\mathbf{C}
denotes the submatrix
\mathbf{T}[X_{2},Y_{1}]
, and
\mathbf{D}
denotes the submatrix
\mathbf{T}[X_{2},Y_{2}]
.

Over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, Lemma
4.5
gives us the rank formula

\rank(\mathbf{T})\geq\rank(\mathbf{A})+\rank(\mathbf{D})-\rank(\mathbf{B}).

Translating this to cutrank, by using the fact that the cutrank is defined as the rank of the corresponding biadjacency matrix over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, and noting that
\operatorname{cutrank}(A,B)\geq\rank(\mathbf{B})
(adding to the rows or columns can never decrease the rank), we get

\operatorname{cutrank}(X,Y)\geq\operatorname{cutrank}(X_{1},Y_{1})+\operatorname{cutrank}(X_{2},Y_{2})-\operatorname{cutrank}(A,B),

which proves the lemma.∎

Lemma 4.7
.

If
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
is a balanced partition of
H^{\prime}
(from Lemma
4.3
) such that
\operatorname{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})<r
, then

\operatorname{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(A^{\prime}\cap X^{**},A^{\prime}\cap Y)+\operatorname{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(B^{\prime}\cap X^{**},B^{\prime}\cap Y)\leq 71r-2.

Proof.

Let
X^{**}
denote the set of representative vertices from
X
that are present in the induced subgraph
H^{\prime}
, as established in Lemma
4.3
. Specifically,
X^{**}=V(H^{\prime})\cap X^{*}
, where
X^{*}=V(H)\cap X
. Note that by Lemma
4.3
,
\operatorname{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(X^{**},Y)\leq\operatorname{cutrank}_{G}(X,Y)<70r
. From Claim
4.1
, we know that
\mathrm{cutrank}_{G}(X,Y)\leq 70r-1
since ranks are integral measures and
\operatorname{cutrank}_{G}(X,Y)<70r
.

Next, consider the biadjacency matrix
\mathbf{M}
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
representing the cut between
X^{**}
and
Y
in the subgraph
H^{\prime}
, block-partitioned by the balanced partition
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
of
V(H^{\prime})
,

\mathbf{M}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{M}_{A^{\prime}A^{\prime}}&\mathbf{M}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\\
\mathbf{M}_{B^{\prime}A^{\prime}}&\mathbf{M}_{B^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\end{bmatrix},

where the rows of
\mathbf{M}
are
(A^{\prime}\cap X^{**},B^{\prime}\cap X^{**})
and the columns are
(A^{\prime}\cap Y,B^{\prime}\cap Y)
. Specifically,
\mathbf{M}_{A^{\prime}A^{\prime}}=\mathrm{Adj}_{H^{\prime}}(A^{\prime}\cap X^{**},A^{\prime}\cap Y)
,
\mathbf{M}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}=\mathrm{Adj}_{H^{\prime}}(A^{\prime}\cap X^{**},B^{\prime}\cap Y)
,
\mathbf{M}_{B^{\prime}A^{\prime}}=\mathrm{Adj}_{H^{\prime}}(B^{\prime}\cap X^{**},A^{\prime}\cap Y)
, and
\mathbf{M}_{B^{\prime}B^{\prime}}=\mathrm{Adj}_{H^{\prime}}(B^{\prime}\cap X^{**},B^{\prime}\cap Y)
. The ranks of these matrices correspond to the cutrank of the respective vertex partitions.

We are given that
\mathrm{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\leq r-1
(due to the integrality of the rank), and the matrices representing this cut are
\mathrm{Adj}_{H^{\prime}}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime}\cap V(H^{\prime}))
and
\mathrm{Adj}_{H}(B^{\prime},A^{\prime}\cap V(H^{\prime}))
.

Moreover, by definition

\rank(\mathbf{M})=\operatorname{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(X,Y)\leq 70r-1

and

\rank(\mathbf{M}_{A'B'})\leq\operatorname{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\leq r-1.

Applying Lemma
4.5
to
\mathbf{M}
yields

\rank(\mathbf{M})+\rank(\mathbf{M}_{A'B'})\geq\rank(\mathbf{M}_{A'A'})+\rank(\mathbf{M}_{B'B'}).

Combining these gives that

\displaystyle\operatorname{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(A^{\prime}\cap X^{**},A^{\prime}\cap Y)

\displaystyle+\operatorname{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(B^{\prime}\cap X^{**},B^{\prime}\cap Y)

\displaystyle=\rank(\mathbf{M}_{A'A'})+\rank(\mathbf{M}_{B'B'})

\displaystyle\leq 70r-1+r-1

\displaystyle=71r-2

which proves the claim of the lemma.
∎

Bore lifting, we isolate an important size relation between
X
and
Y
. This prevents a degenerate case where the small side has a cardinality that is too small to prevent a contradiction from the tangle maximality.

Lemma 4.8
.

|X|\geq 18|Y|
.

Proof.

We apply the definition of
(s,\epsilon)
-well-behavedness (in Definition
2.5
) to the trivial partition
(A,\emptyset)
of
X
, with
s=3|Y|
and
\epsilon=1/7
. For this, note that
|A|=|X|
and

\operatorname{cutrank}(A,Y)=\operatorname{cutrank}(X,Y)\leq 70r

If
|X|\leq 6s=18|Y|
, then the well-behaved property would require
\operatorname{cutrank}(A\cup Y,B)=\operatorname{cutrank}(X\cup Y,\emptyset)=0\geq 6\epsilon\cdot 70r=60r
,
which is clearly false since
r\geq 1
.

Therefore, we must have
|X|>6s=18|Y|
, which proves the lemma.
∎

4.3
Contradiction via a Low-Rank Cut

We are now ready to lift the balanced low‑rank cut
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
of
H^{\prime}
to two separations of
G
by adjoining each piece of
Y
back to
X
. Subadditivity bounds ensure that each lifted separation has cutrank strictly less than
72r
.

Lemma 4.9

(Lifting balanced cuts)
.

For the induced subgraph
H^{\prime}\subseteq G
from Claim
4.2
, and its balanced partition
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
(from Lemma
4.3
) where
\operatorname{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})<r
, we have that

\operatorname{cutrank}_{G}(X\cup(A^{\prime}\cap Y),B^{\prime}\cap Y)<72r

and

\operatorname{cutrank}_{G}(X\cup(B^{\prime}\cap Y),A^{\prime}\cap Y)<72r.

Proof.

Let
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
be the balanced partition of
H^{\prime}
from Lemma
4.3
. Let
X^{**}=X\cap V(H^{\prime})
and
\mathbf{M}=\mathrm{Adj}_{H^{\prime}}(X^{**},Y)
, block-partitioned by
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
as

\mathbf{M}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{M}_{A^{\prime}A^{\prime}}&\mathbf{M}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\\
\mathbf{M}_{B^{\prime}A^{\prime}}&\mathbf{M}_{B^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\end{bmatrix}.

By Lemma
4.7
and Claim
4.2
, we have that

\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{M})=\operatorname{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(X^{**},Y)\leq 70r-1.

Moreover, we have the symmetric relations

\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{M}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}})\leq\operatorname{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\leq r-1

and

\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{M}_{B^{\prime}A^{\prime}})\leq\operatorname{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\leq r-1.

Since
B^{\prime}\subseteq X^{**}\cup Y
and
X^{**}\subseteq X
, we have
X\cup B^{\prime}=X\cup(B^{\prime}\cap Y)
. Similarly,
X\cup A^{\prime}=X\cup(A^{\prime}\cap Y)
; therefore, we have that the two lifted partitions of
V(G)=X\cup Y
are

P_{1}\coloneqq(X\cup(A^{\prime}\cap Y),B^{\prime}\cap Y)

and

P_{2}\coloneqq(X\cup(B^{\prime}\cap Y),A^{\prime}\cap Y).

We first bound the cutrank of
P_{1}
. By subadditivity of the matrix rank, we write

\displaystyle\operatorname{cutrank}_{G}(X\cup(A^{\prime}\cap Y),B^{\prime}\cap Y)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{cutrank}_{G}(X,B^{\prime}\cap Y)+\operatorname{cutrank}_{G}(A^{\prime}\cap Y,B^{\prime}\cap Y)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{cutrank}_{G}(X,Y)+\operatorname{cutrank}_{G}(A^{\prime}\cap Y,B^{\prime}\cap Y)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{cutrank}_{G}(X,Y)+\operatorname{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq(70r-1)+(r-1)

\displaystyle<72r,

where the second inequality follows from noting that
\mathrm{Adj}_{G}(X,B^{\prime}\cap Y)
is a column submatrix of
\mathrm{Adj}_{G}(X,Y)
, and therefore
\operatorname{cutrank}_{G}(X,B^{\prime}\cap Y)\leq\operatorname{cutrank}_{G}(X,Y)
. The third inequality follows from noting that
\mathrm{Adj}_{G}(A^{\prime}\cap Y,B^{\prime}\cap Y)
is a submatrix of
\mathrm{Adj}_{H^{\prime}}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
, whose cutrank is bounded (by supposition) by
r
, and the fourth inequality follows by Claim
4.1
which gives
\operatorname{cutrank}_{G}(X,Y)<70r
.

We now bound the cutrank of
P_{2}\coloneqq(X\cup(B\cap Y),A\cap Y)
. For this, a symmetric argument gives that

\displaystyle\operatorname{cutrank}_{G}(X\cup(B^{\prime}\cap Y),A^{\prime}\cap Y)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{cutrank}_{G}(X,A^{\prime}\cap Y)+\operatorname{cutrank}_{G}(B^{\prime}\cap Y,A^{\prime}\cap Y)

\displaystyle\leq 70r-1+(r-1)

\displaystyle<72r,

which proves the claim.∎

Now we are ready to complete the proof of the theorem. By way of contradiction, suppose that every induced subgraph
H^{\prime}\subseteq G
has
\operatorname{min-bal-cutrank}(H^{\prime})
strictly less than
r
, i.e., for every balanced partition
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
of
V(H^{\prime})
we have
\operatorname{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})<r
. Let
\tau
be a rank-tangle of order
72r
. By Claim
4.1
, fix
(X,Y)
with
\mathrm{cutrank}(X,Y)<70r
where
X\in\tau
, that is
(3|Y|,1/7)
-well-behaved. By Lemma
4.3
, there exists an induced subgraph
H^{\prime}\subseteq G
and a balanced partition
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
of
H^{\prime}
such that
\mathrm{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})<r
,
A^{\prime}\cap Y\neq\emptyset
and
B^{\prime}\cap Y\neq\emptyset
, and if
X^{**}=V(H^{\prime})\cap X^{*}
, then
\operatorname{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(X^{**},Y)<70r
.

Now, consider the two lifted partitions of
V(G)
based on this cut:
P_{1}\coloneqq(X\cup(A^{\prime}\cap Y),B^{\prime}\cap Y)
and
P_{2}\coloneqq(X\cup(B^{\prime}\cap Y),A^{\prime}\cap Y)
.
By Lemma
4.9
, which applies because
\operatorname{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})<r
and
\operatorname{cutrank}_{H^{\prime}}(X^{**},Y)<70r
(which implies
\operatorname{cutrank}_{G}(X,Y)<70r
), both of these lifted separations
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
have cutrank strictly less than
72r
.

Therefore, the tangle
\tau
must orient both: by the second tangle axiom in Definition
2.4
, the three sets
X,B^{\prime}\cap Y
, and
A^{\prime}\cap Y
cannot all be in
\tau
(as their union is
V(G)
). Since
X\in\tau
, at least one of
(B^{\prime}\cap Y)
or
(A^{\prime}\cap Y)
is not in
\tau
. Now, if
B^{\prime}\cap Y\notin\tau
, then its complement in
P_{1}
, which is
X\cup(A^{\prime}\cap Y)
, must be in
\tau
. Similarly, if
A^{\prime}\cap Y\notin\tau
, then
X\cup(B^{\prime}\cap Y)
must be in
\tau
. In either case,
\tau
must choose a set that is strictly larger than
X
(as Lemma
4.3
guarantees that
A^{\prime}\cap Y\neq\emptyset
and
B^{\prime}\cap Y\neq\emptyset
); however, this then contradicts the maximality of
X
from Claim
4.1
. Therefore, our initial assumption must be false, and there exists an induced subgraph of
G
with min-bal-cutrank at least
r
, which proves Theorem
1.1
.∎

5
Conclusion

We show that any graph with sufficiently large rankwidth must contain an induced subgraph where every balanced bisection has a high cutrank. In its dual, this says that a graph has small rankwidth if every large induced subgraph can be bisected by a low-rank cut, and that it has large rankwidth if it contains a large induced subgraph that is robustly connected against all balanced low-rank cuts. This establishes an equivalence between rankwidth and the maximum minimum-balanced-cutrank over all induced subgraphs.

Future Work

We identify several key areas for future work in this field.

First, it would be interesting to identify an extension of dense-graph expansion to
edge-weighted graphs

Secondly, one reason why expansion is a mathematically interesting notion is because its linear-algebraic, topological, and combinatorial notions are all equivalent

Thirdly, the constant

in our main result in Theorem

[
27
]
. We believe this is not tight, and determining the optimal constant relating rankwidth and the maximum min-bal-cutrank is a natural follow-up problem.
Finally, certain algorithms admit a structural parameterization through the VC-dimension
[

,
12
,
19
,
26
,
39
]
and the related Pollard pseudodimension. Similarly, it would be interesting if our structural characterization could lead to new approximation algorithms or FPT algorithms for problems parameterized by rankwidth.

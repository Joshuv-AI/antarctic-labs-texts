Decomposing multipersistence modules using functor calculus Thanks: Email: bjornar.hem@epfl.ch
Bjørnar Gullikstad Hem

Abstract
. We apply poset cocalculus , a functor calculus framework for functors out of a poset, to study the problem of decomposing multipersistence modules into simpler components. We both prove new results in this topic and offer a new perspective on already established results. In particular, we show that a pointwise finite-dimensional bipersistence module is middle-exact if and only if it is isomorphic to the homology of a homotopy degree 1 functor, from which we deduce a novel, more synthetic proof of the interval decomposability of middle-exact bipersistence modules. We also give a new decomposition theorem for middle-exact multipersistence modules indexed over a finite poset, stating that such a module can always be written as a direct sum of a projective module, an injective module, and a bidegree 1 module, even in the case where it is not pointwise finite-dimensional.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

A
persistence module
is a functor
F\colon P\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
, where
P
is a poset. We will use the word
multipersistence module
when referring to a persistence module whose source poset is a product of two or more total orders, and the word
bipersistence module
when the source poset is a product of exactly two total orders.
A multipersistence module from a product of
n
total orders is called a
n
-parameter multipersistence module.

Persistence modules are the main objects in persistent homology, a key technique in the field of topological data analysis (TDA).
In persistent homology, one constructs a persistence module from data such as a point cloud.
From the persistence module, one then typically constructs a visual representation known as the
barcode diagram
. Construction of the barcode diagram is possible due to the structure theorem
[
6
,
8
,
17
]
,
which says that a persistence module can be decomposed into a direct sum of simple components known as
interval modules
.
However, the structure theorem applies only when the source poset is a total order, such as
\mathbb{R}
. Multipersistence modules, on the other hand, are not in general interval decomposable (see, e.g.,
[
4
, Example 8.3]
).

Bipersistence modules are said to be
middle-exact
if they satisfy the following local condition: for every
(x,y)\leq(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
, the chain complex

F(x,y)\to F(x,y^{\prime})\oplus F(x^{\prime},y)\to F(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})

is exact in the middle.
Middle-exact bipersistence modules arise naturally from
interlevel set persistent homology

[
5
]
, in which one studies the homology of preimages of intervals under a real-valued function on a topological space (see, e.g.,
[
4
, Section 10.2]
for more details).
Interlevel set persistence has been applied to, for example, analysis of digital images
[
1
]
.
In
[
3
]
, Botnan and Crawley proved that all pointwise finite-dimensional (p.f.d.) middle-exact bipersistence modules are interval decomposable, and that the intervals are of a special form, called
blocks
.

In
[
14
]
,
the notion of middle exactness is generalized from bipersistence modules to arbitrary multipersistence modules, giving rise to the notions of
k
-middle-exact modules
for
k\geq 2
.
The authors show that p.f.d. multipersistence modules that are
k
-middle-exact for all
k\geq 2
are always interval decomposable.

In
[
9
]
, we introduced
poset cocalculus
, a novel framework for studying functors out of a poset.

F
from a distributive lattice to a (sufficiently nice) model category, poset cocalculus produces a telescope of codegree
n
approximations of the functor,

1.2.
Main contributions

Relations between middle exactness and functor calculus have been explored previously in
[
15
, Chapter 5.7]
.
In
[
15
]
, however, the author uses the existing theory of Goodwillie calculus and applies it to functors between
\infty
-categories, and shows that any 1-excisive
1
1

1

1-excisive is the analogue of “degree 1” in Goodwillie calculus.
functor gives rise to a middle-exact functor.
Here, we use the framework of poset cocalculus, which allows us to apply functor calculus concepts directly to functors out of a poset.
One main advantage of this framework is that the universal (co)degree
n
approximations can be easily computed through an explicit formula established in
[
9
, Definition 4.21]
.
Using this approach, we prove several new results, demonstrating the power of poset cocalculus as a tool for analyzing multipersistence modules.

We show that a p.f.d. bipersistence module is interval decomposable if it is either codegree 1 or degree 1.

Proposition

(
4.2
)
.

A codegree 1 p.f.d. functor
F\colon[0,\infty]^{2}\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
decomposes into a direct sum of interval modules over death blocks, vertical blocks, and horizontal blocks.

Dually, a degree 1 p.f.d. functor
F\colon[0,\infty]^{2}\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
decomposes into a direct sum of interval modules over birth blocks, vertical blocks, and horizontal blocks.

The definitions of death/birth/vertical/horizontal blocks are given in
4.1
.

We further prove the following theorem on middle-exact bipersistence modules.

Theorem

(
Theorem 6.9
)
.

Let
F\colon[0,\infty]^{2}\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
be a p.f.d. functor. The following are equivalent.

(i):

F
is middle-exact.

(ii):

F
is the direct sum of a degree 1 functor and a codegree 1 functor.

(iii):

There exists a homotopy degree 1 functor
\widehat{F}\colon[0,\infty]^{2}\to\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
such that
F\cong H_{0}\circ\widehat{F}
.

Here, homotopy degree 1 means that it is degree 1 with respect to the canonical model structure on chain complexes over a field. In other words, not only does a homotopy degree 1 functor from
[0,\infty]^{2}
gives rise to a middle-exact bipersistence module, but every bipersistence module from
[0,\infty]^{2}
arises this way.

A corollary of these two results is that middle-exact p.f.d. persistence modules over
[0,\infty]^{2}
are interval decomposable.
Hence, poset cocalculus leads to an alternative, more synthetic proof of the interval decomposability of middle-exact modules.

We further apply poset cocalculus to study higher-dimensional middle exactness. We introduce the terminology
bidegree
n
, which means “both degree
n
and codegree
n
”, and prove the following.

Theorem

(
Theorem 8.18
)
.

Let
P
be a finite product of finite total orders, and let
\mathbb{Fb}
be a field. If
F\colon P\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
be a functor that is
k
-middle-exact for all
k\geq 2
, then there exist
B,K,C\colon P\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
with
K
injective,
C
projective, and
B
bidegree 1, such that
F\cong B\oplus K\oplus C
.

This result applies to all functors from
P
to
\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
, even those that are not p.f.d.
On the other hand, the techniques used in
[
3
]
and
[
14
]

rely on the fact that p.f.d. multipersistence modules are uniquely decomposable into indecomposable modules.
2
2

2

These indecomposable modules need not, however, be interval modules.

Hence, poset cocalculus offer a novel approach to study multipersistence modules that allows us to understand the structure of modules that are not p.f.d.

To aid our understanding of higher-dimensional multipersistence modules, we also introduce the notion of
layers
and
colayers
.
The
n
th
layer
is defined as
D^{n}F=\fib(T^{n}F\to T^{n-1}F)
, and the
n
th
colayer
is the dual concept.
We prove the following proposition.

Proposition

(
8.9
)
.

Let
P
be a finite product of total orders with minimal elements,
\mathscr{C}
a complete pointed category, and
F\colon P\to\mathscr{C}
any functor. Then
D_{n}F
is bidegree
n
.

Dually, let
P
be a finite product of total orders with maximal elements,
\mathscr{C}
a cocomplete pointed category, and
F\colon P\to\mathscr{C}
any functor. Then
D^{n}F
is bidegree
n
.

We apply this result to prove that bidegree 1 p.f.d. multipersistence modules are interval decomposable.

Proposition

(
8.10
)
.

Let
P
be a finite product of finite total orders,
\mathbb{Fb}
a field, and
F\colon P\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
a p.f.d. bidegree 1 functor. Then
F
is interval decomposable.

Together with
Theorem 8.18
, this gives an alternative proof of the fact that p.f.d. multipersistence modules that are
k
-middle-exact for every
k\geq 2
are interval decomposable, as long as the source poset is finite.

1.3.
Notation

We denote by
\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
the category of vector spaces over a field
\mathbb{Fb}
. We denote by
\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
the model category of unbounded chain complexes over
\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
, with the canonical model structure
[
12
, Theorem 2.3.13]
.

For two posets
P
and
Q
, the product partial order on
P\times Q
is given by

(p,q)\leq(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})\iff p\leq q\textrm{ and }p^{\prime}\leq q^{\prime}.

Unless otherwise specified, we endow products of posets with this partial order.
We let
[0,\infty]
denote the poset of nonnegative numbers, with a maximal element
\infty
added. We let
[0,\infty]^{2}
denote the poset
[0,\infty]\times[0,\infty]
with the product partial order.

Note that by
[
10
]
, both the projective and injective model structures on
\Fun(P,\mathscr{M})
exist whenever
\mathscr{M}
is equipped with an accessible model structure and
P
is a small category. In particular, they exist for
\mathscr{M}=\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
, equipped with the canonical model structure.

Given categories
\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}
and
\mathscr{C}
, and functors
\alpha\colon\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{B}
and
F\colon\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{C}
, we let
\Lan_{\alpha}F\colon\mathscr{B}\to\mathscr{C}
denote the left Kan extension of
F
along
\alpha
, whenever it exists. Similarly, we let
\Ran_{\alpha}F\colon\mathscr{B}\to\mathscr{C}
denote the right Kan extension of
F
along
\alpha
, whenever it exists.

2.
Preliminaries on poset cocalculus

2.1.
Posets and lattices

Given two elements
x,y
of a poset, we denote their
least upper bound
, or
join
, by
x\vee y
and their
greatest lower bound
, or
meet
, by
x\wedge y
(whenever they exist). A
lattice
is a poset in which
x\vee y
and
x\wedge y
exist for all pairs of elements
(x,y)
.

An element
x
in a poset
P
is
minimal
if there is no
y\in P
such that
y<x
. If
P
is a lattice, then
x\in P
is minimal if and only if
x
is a
least element
, i.e.,
x\leq y
for all
y\in P

[
9
, Section 4.1]
.

We similarly say that an element
x
in a poset
P
is
maximal
if there is no
y\in P
such that
y>x
. If
P
is a lattice, then
x\in P
is maximal if and only if
x
is a
greatest element
, i.e.,
x\geq y
for all
y\in P
.

Definition 2.1
.

A poset
P
satisfies the
descending chain condition
if every nonempty subset of
P
has a minimal element.

Dually, a poset
P
satisfies the
ascending chain condition
if every nonempty subset of
P
has a maximal element.

Finite posets trivially satisfy both the descending chain condition and the ascending chain condition.

2.2.
Distributive lattices

A lattice
P
is
distributive
if for all elements
x,y,z\in P
,

x\wedge(y\vee z)=(x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge z).

(2.2.1)

Equivalently, a lattice
P
is distributive if for all elements
x,y,z\in P
,

x\vee(y\wedge z)=(x\vee y)\wedge(x\vee z)

(2.2.2)

[
2
, Chapter IX, Theorem 1]
.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that an element
v
in a lattice
P
is
join-irreducible
if
v\neq x\vee y
for all
x,y<v
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
P
be a lattice, and let
v\in P
. A
join-decomposition
of
v
(of size
k
) is a finite collection of elements
p^{0},\dots,p^{k-1}\in P
such that
v=p^{0}\vee\dots\vee p^{k-1}
.

Definition 2.4
.

We say that a join-decomposition
x^{1},\dots,x^{k}
is
indecomposable
if it consists only of join-irreducible elements.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
p^{0},\dots,p^{k-1}
be a join-decomposition of
v
. We say that the join-decomposition is
reduced
if none of the
p^{i}
’s is redundant, i.e., if for all
i
,

\bigvee_{j\in[k-1]\setminus\{i\}}p_{j}\neq v.

We adopt the convention that
p^{0}=v
is a reduced join-decomposition of
v
, unless
v
is minimal.

Lemma 2.6
.

[
2
, Lemma 1, p. 142]

Let
P
be a distributive lattice. If
v\in P
has an indecomposable reduced join-decomposition, it is unique.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
P
be a distributive lattice. For an element
v\in P
, we define the
join-dimension
of
v
as

\jdim(v)=\begin{cases}0,&\ v\textrm{ is minimal},\\
k,&\ \text{$v$ is not minimal and has a reduced,}\\[-4.0pt]
\ &\ \textrm{indecomposable join-decomposition of size $k$,}\\
\infty,&\ \text{otherwise.}\\
\end{cases}

The following proposition says that in distributive lattices satisfying the descending chain condition, every element has finite join-dimension.

Proposition 2.8
.

[
2
, Theorem 9, p. 142]

If
P
is a distributive lattice that satisfies the descending chain condition, then every element of
P
has a unique indecomposable reduced join-decomposition.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
P
be a distributive lattice with a minimal element. We say that
P
is a
join-factorization lattice
if there exists a distributive lattice
Q
, satisfying the descending chain condition, and an order-preserving function
f\colon P\to Q
such that for all
v\in P
,

\jdim(f(v))=\jdim(v).

Examples of join-factorization lattices include distributive lattices satisfying the descending chain condition (and, in particular, finite distributive lattices)
[
9
, Example 4.17]
.
Furthermore, if
T_{1},\dots,T_{n}
are total orders with minimal elements, then
P=T_{1}\times\dots\times T_{n}
is a join-factorization lattice
[
9
, Example 4.18]
.

Replacing joins with meets, we dualize the constructions above. In particular, the
meet-dimension
of an element
v
, denoted
\mdim(v)
, is the size of its reduced indecomposable meet-decomposition, if it exists, and is
\infty
otherwise.

Given a distributive lattice
P
, we let
P_{\leq n}
denote the subposet of elements with join-dimension
\leq n
and
P^{\leq n}
the subposet of elements with meet-dimension
\leq n
. In particular,
P_{\leq 1}
consists of the join-irreducible elements and
P^{\leq 1}
consists of the meet-irreducible elements.

2.3.
Cubical diagrams

Let
[k]=\{0,\dots,k\}
, and let
\mathcal{P}_{k+1}
be the power set of
[k]
, viewed as a poset ordered by inclusion.
For
\mathscr{C}
any category, we refer to functors
\mathcal{X}\colon\mathcal{P}_{k}\to\mathscr{C}
as
k
-cubes
in
\mathscr{C}
.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a category.
A
(k+1)
-cube
\mathcal{X}\colon\mathcal{P}_{k+1}\to\mathscr{C}
is
cocartesian
if the canonical map

\underset{S\subsetneq[k]}{\colim\mathcal{X}(S)}\to\mathcal{X}([k])

is an isomorphism.

Similarly,
\mathcal{X}
is
cartesian
if the canonical map

\mathcal{X}(\emptyset)\to\underset{S\subseteq[k],S\neq\emptyset}{\lim\mathcal{X}(S)}

is an isomorphism.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a category.
A
(k+1)
-cube
\mathcal{X}\colon\mathcal{P}_{k+1}\to\mathscr{C}
is
strongly cartesian
(resp.
strongly cocartesian
) if each face of dimension at least is cartesian (resp. cocartesian).

If
\mathcal{X}
is both strongly cartesian and strongly cocartesian, it is called
strongly bicartesian
.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a model category.
A
(k+1)
-cube
\mathcal{X}\colon\mathcal{P}_{k+1}\to\mathscr{M}
is
homotopy cocartesian
if the canonical map

\underset{S\subsetneq[k]}{\hocolim\mathcal{X}(S)}\to\mathcal{X}([k])

3.
Preliminaries on persistence modules

We briefly state here the main definitions and results in the theory of persistence modules.
We first give the definition of an
interval decomposable
persistence module, and state some established results regarding interval decomposition.
We then go on to state the definition of a middle-exact bipersistence module.

3.1.
Decomposition of persistence modules

Definition 3.1
.

Let
P
be a poset. An
interval
in
P
is a subset
I\subset P
such that the following two conditions hold.

•

If
x,y\in I
and
x\leq z\leq y
, then
z\in I
.

•

If
x,y\in I
, then there exists a zigzag of partial order relations

x=a_{0}\leq b_{0}\geq a_{1}\leq\dots\leq b_{n}=y

connecting
x
and
y
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathbb{Fb}
be a field, let
P
be a poset and let
I\subset P
be an interval. The
interval module

\mathbb{Fb}_{I}\colon P\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
is defined as

\mathbb{Fb}_{I}(x)=\begin{cases}0,\quad x\notin I,\\
\mathbb{Fb},\quad x\in I,\end{cases}

and

\mathbb{Fb}_{I}(x\leq y)=\begin{cases}\id_{\mathbb{Fb}},\quad x,y\in I,\\
0,\quad\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Definition 3.3
.

A persistence module
F\colon P\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
is
interval decomposable
if it is isomorphic to a direct sum of interval modules, i.e., if there exists a set
\mathcal{B}(F)
of intervals in
P
such that

F\ \cong\ \bigoplus_{I\in\mathcal{B}(F)}\mathbb{Fb}_{I}.

We say that a persistence module
F\colon P\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
is
pointwise finite-dimensional
(p.f.d.) if
F(x)
is finite-dimensional for every
x\in P
.

We say that
F\colon P\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
is
indecomposable
if
F\cong F^{\prime}\oplus F^{\prime\prime}
implies that
F^{\prime}\cong 0
or
F^{\prime\prime}\cong 0
.

Theorem 3.4
.

For
P
any poset and
F\colon P\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
pointwise finite-dimensional,
F
is isomorphic to a direct sum of indecomposable persistence modules.

Proof.

This is stated in
[
4
, Theorem 4.2]
. ∎

Note that in the preceding theorem, the indecomposable persistence modules need not be interval modules. This contrasts with the single-parameter case, i.e., when

, where every p.f.d. persistence module decomposes into a direct sum of interval modules
[
6
,
8
,
17
]
.

We say that a subset
Q\subseteq P
is
directed
if for every
x,y\in Q
, there exists a

such that
u\geq x,y
. We say that
Q\subseteq P
is

if for every
x\in Q
and
y\in P
with

y\in Q
.
Note that a down-closed and directed subset is an interval.

An object

in a category is
injective
if, for any morphism

and monomorphism
j\colon X\to Y
, there exists a morphism
g\colon Y\to A
such that

.

Let

In this section, we describe the relation between (co)degree 1 functors into

Note that as

Hence, a functor

is homotopy degree
n
if and only if it is homotopy codegree
n
.

Let
\iota
denote the inclusion functor
\iota\colon\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}\to\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
defined by

\iota(V)_{\bullet}=\begin{cases}V,\quad\bullet=0,\\
0,\quad\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Observe that for any

, there exists a functor
\iota_{*}F\colon P\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
P
be a poset, and
F\colon P\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
a functor.
Then
\iota_{*}F\colon P\to\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
admits:

•

a cofibrant replacement
Q
such that for all
x\in P
and
i<0
,
Q(x)_{i}=0
, and

•

a fibrant replacement
R
such that for all
x\in P
and
i>0
,
R(x)_{i}=0
.

Proof.

We show the first part. The proof of the second is dual.
Let
\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbb{Fb}}^{+}
denote the model category of non-negatively graded chain complexes, equipped with the projective model structure (i.e., where the weak equivalences are the quasi-isomorphisms, and the fibrations are the morphisms that are epimorphism in each positive degree).

The inclusion

has a right adjoint
R
given by

R(C)_{i}=\begin{cases}C_{i},&\quad i>0,\\
\ker(\partial_{0}\colon C_{0}\to C_{-1}),&\quad i=0,\end{cases}

which induces a Quillen adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Fun(P,\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbb{Fb}}^{+})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Fun(P,\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbb{Fb}}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R_{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\scriptscriptstyle\boldsymbol{\bot}}

Now, let
\iota^{+}
denote the inclusion
\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbb{Fb}}^{+}
, and note that
\iota=j\circ\iota^{+}
.

Choose a cofibrant replacement
Q
of
\iota^{+}_{*}F
. Then,
j_{*}Q
is a cofibrant replacement of
\iota_{*}(F)
in
\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
, and satisfies
(j_{*}Q)_{i}=0
for all
i<0
.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
P
be a distributive lattice.

If
F\colon P\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
is a functor, then

H_{0}\widetilde{T}_{n}(\iota_{*}F)\cong T_{n}F,

and

H_{0}\widetilde{T}^{n}(\iota_{*}F)\cong T^{n}F.

Proof.

We prove the first statement. The proof of the second is dual.

Using
5.1
, let
Q
be a cofibrant resolution of
(\iota_{*}F)|_{P_{\leq n}}
such that for all
x\in P
and

,

.
Then, for all
x\in P
and
i<0

(\widetilde{T}_{n}(\iota_{*}F))(x)_{i}=\underset{y\leq x,y\in P_{\leq n}}{\colim}Q(y)_{i}\cong 0.

Hence,

\displaystyle(H_{0}\widetilde{T}_{n}F)(x)

\displaystyle\cong\coker\left(\partial_{1}\colon\underset{y\leq x,y\in P_{\leq n}}{\colim}Q(y)_{1}\to\underset{y\leq x,y\in P_{\leq n}}{\colim}Q(y)_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\cong\underset{y\leq x,y\in P_{\leq n}}{\colim}\coker\left(\partial_{1}:Q(y)_{1}\to Q(y)_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\cong\underset{y\leq x,y\in P_{\leq n}}{\colim}H_{0}Q(y)\cong\underset{y\leq x,y\in P_{\leq n}}{\colim}F(y)\cong(T_{n}F)(x),

where we use that colimits commutes with colimits (and hence cokernels).
This concludes the proof.

∎

For the following corollary, recall that a functor into
\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
is homotopy degree
n
if and only if it is homotopy codegree
n
.

.

is a meet-factorization lattice and

functor, then there exists a homotopy degree

functor

such that

.

Proof.

We show the first case. The second is dual (as a functor in

is homotopy degree

if and only if it is homotopy codegree

).

Let

6.
Middle exactness and poset cocalculus

In this section, we study middle exactness in the 2-parameter setting. We establish a connection between middle-exactness and poset cocalculus. As a corollary, we get a new proof of the fact that middle-exact bipersistence modules are block decomposable.

Given two morphisms
f_{1}\colon X\to Y
and
f_{2}\colon X\to Z
in a category, we let
Y\coprod_{X}Z
denote the corresponding pushout (if it exists). Similarly, given two morphisms
f_{1}\colon Y\to X
and
f_{2}\colon Z\to X
, we let
Y\times_{X}Z
denote the corresponding product (if it exists).

6.1.
Properties of middle-exact squares

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category, and let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}

be a square in
\mathscr{A}
.
The following are equivalent.

(1)

(

) is middle-exact.

(2)

is a monomorphism.

(3)

The canonical map
A\to B\times_{D}C
is an epimorphism.

Consider

A\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\
f\end{pmatrix}}B\oplus C\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}g&-\beta\end{pmatrix}}D,

(6.1.2)

and observe that

f\end{pmatrix},

and

By definition, the homology of (

) is defined as

\ker{\begin{pmatrix}g&-\beta\end{pmatrix}}\ /\ \Ima{\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\
f\end{pmatrix}},

which is isomorphic to

f\end{pmatrix}}(\ker\begin{pmatrix}g&-\beta\end{pmatrix})\right),

f\end{pmatrix})\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}g&-\beta\end{pmatrix}}D\right).

Hence, (

is an epimorphism, and also if and only if the map
\coker\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\
f\end{pmatrix}\to D
is a monomorphism.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category, and let
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
F\colon[0,\infty]^{2}\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
be middle-exact. Then the functor

C=\coker\left(T_{1}F\to F\right)\colon[0,\infty^{2}]\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}

is monomorphic and degree 1.

Dually, the functor

K=\ker\left(F\to T^{1}F\right)\colon[0,\infty^{2}]\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}

is epimorphic and codegree 1.

Proof.

We show the first part. The proof of the second part is dual.

We first show that
C
is monomorphic. We will show that for all
(x,y)\in[0,\infty]^{2}
and
x^{\prime}\geq x
, the map
C((x,y)\leq(x^{\prime},y))
is a monomorphism. One can similarly show that maps of the form
C(x,y\leq y^{\prime})
are monomorphisms, which will imply that for all
(x,y)\leq(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
, the map
C((x,y)\leq(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))=C(x,y\leq y^{\prime})\circ C(x\leq x^{\prime},y)
is a monomorphism.

Observe that it follows from
6.4
that
C
is middle-exact.
Let
x,y
and
x^{\prime}
be as above, and
consider the square
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(x,0)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(x,y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(x^{\prime},0)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(x^{\prime},y).}

As
(x,0)
and
(x^{\prime},0)
are in
P_{\leq 1}
, it follows that
C(x,0)\cong C(x^{\prime},0)\cong 0
. Thus, by middle-exactness,
C((x,y)\leq(x^{\prime},y))
is a monomorphism.

Hence, by
6.6
,
C
is degree 1, which concludes the proof.

∎

7.
Relation to the Reedy model structure

In this section, we apply the
Reedy model structure
on functor categories to establish a characterization of projective and injective objects in
\Fun(P,\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}})
for a poset
P
(under certain conditions on the poset).
We then give an example illustrating how this model structure is connected to middle-exact bipersistence modules.

7.1.
Notation and preliminaries on posets

For two elements
x,y
in a poset
P
, we say that
y

covers

x
if
y>x
and that there exists no
z
with
y>z>x
. We denote this by
y\succ x
. If
y
covers
x
, we say that
x
is a
parent
of
y
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
P
be a lattice, and let
u\in P
. If
a,b\prec u
with
a\neq b
, then
a\vee b=u
.

Proof.

As
a\leq a\vee b\leq u
, either
a\vee b=a
or
a\vee b=u
. If
a\vee b=a
, then
b\leq a<u
, so either
a=b
or
b<a<u
, both of which contradict the assumptions.
∎

Remark that
7.1
in particular implies that join-irreducible elements have at most one parent.

For an element
a\in P
, let
a^{\downarrow}
denote the interval
a^{\downarrow}=\{x\in P:x\leq a\}
.
Let
a^{\uparrow}
denote the interval
a^{\uparrow}=\{x\in P:x\geq a\}
.

Definition 7.2
.

A poset
P
is said to be
down-finite
if, for every
a\in P
,
a^{\downarrow}
is finite.

A poset
P
is said to be
up-finite
if, for every
a\in P
,
a^{\uparrow}
is finite.

Observe that down-finite posets satisfy the descending chain condition, and up-finite posets satisfy the ascending chain condition.

Example 7.3
.

Any finite poset is down-finite. The poset
(\mathbb{N},\leq)
is down-finite, but not finite. The poset
(\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\},\leq)
, defined by adding a greatest element
\infty
to
(\mathbb{N},\leq)
, satisfies the descending chain condition, but is not down-finite.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
P
be a down-finite distributive lattice, and let
x\in P
. Then
x
has exactly
\jdim(x)
parents.

Proof.

This follows from
[
9
, Lemma 4.20]
and
[
7
, Proposition 5.8]
.
∎

7.2.
The Reedy model structure and projective objects

Let
P
be a finite poset.
We can equip
\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
with the model structure where the cofibrations are monomorphisms, fibrations are epimorphisms and weak equivalences are all morphisms.
As
P
satisfies the descending chain condition, we have a Reedy model structure on
\Fun(P,\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}})
that coincides with the projective model structure (see, e.g.,
[
12
, Chapter 5.2]
), which we denote
\Fun_{\textrm{proj}}(P,\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}})
. In
\Fun_{\textrm{proj}}(P,\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}})
, all morphisms are weak equivalences, the fibrations are the objectwise epimorphisms, and the cofibrations are the natural transformations
\eta\colon F\to G
such that for all
x\in P
, the
latching map

L_{x}G\coprod_{L_{x}F}F(x)\to G(x)

(7.2.1)

is a monomorphism, where
L_{x}F
is the latching object of
F
at
x
and is given by

L_{x}F=\underset{y<x}{\colim}F(y).

(7.2.2)

Observe that the cofibrant objects are precisely the functors that have the left lifting property against objectwise epimorphisms. In other words, the cofibrant objects are the projective objects in
\Fun(P,\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}})
.
Note further that
F\in\Fun_{\textrm{proj}}(P,\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}})
is cofibrant if and only if

L_{x}F\to F(x)

(7.2.3)

is a monomorphism for all
x\in P
.

A
meet-sublattice

Q
of a lattice
P
is a full subposet
Q\subset P
such that for any
x,y\in Q
,
x\wedge_{P}y\in Q
, where
\wedge_{p}
is the meet operation in
P
.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
P
be a lattice satisfying the descending chain condition, and

a finite meet-sublattice.
If

is a projective object in
\Fun(P,\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}})
, then the restriction

is a projective object in
\Fun(Q,\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}})
.

Proof.

Let

be the maximal element in

and let

, viewed as a subposet of
P
. By inspecting equations (

) and (

), we see that

is a cofibrant object in

Let

denote the inclusion

. Let

\displaystyle r\colon

\displaystyle P^{\prime}\to Q

\displaystyle x\mapsto\bigwedge_{u\in Q,u\geq x}u,

which is well-defined as

is a sublattice.
Then

for all

, and

for all

(as

Hence,

forms an adjoint pair, and thus

is left Quillen adjoint to

. Thus,

is cofibrant, and hence a projective object in
\Fun(Q,\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}})
.
∎

Everything in the previous section dualizes. We state the main definitions and results without proof, as the proofs dualize formally.

A persistence module

is said to be

.
The following result is stated in

.

.

Let

are free.

8.
Higher-dimensional multipersistence modules

In this section, we move from the 2-parameter paradigm to more complicated posets.
We first study
bidegree
n
functors, i.e., functors that are both degree
n
and codegree 1, and show that under certain conditions on the source poset target,
T_{1}
“preserves degree 1” in the sense that it sends degree 1 functors to bidegree 1 functors (and dually).
We further study the
layers
of the Taylor tower and show that the
n
th layer is bidegree
n
.

We then apply these result to prove several novel results on multipersistence modules whose source poset is a product of
n
finite total orders, for
n\geq 2
.
We show that bidegree 1 p.f.d. multipersistence modules of these kind are always interval decomposable.
We further prove a theorem on the structure of these multiparameter persistence modules, which holds even when the modules are not p.f.d.
Specifically, we show that they are always isomorphic to the direct sum of an injective module, a projective module, and a bidegree 1 module.

8.1.
Theory of poset (co)calculus for functors into abelian categories

8.1.1.
Bidegree 1 functors

We now generalize
3.8
to functors with source any lattice. We will use the word
2-middle-exactness
in this case, to be consistent with the definitions introduced in
[
14
]
.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
P
be a lattice, and
\mathscr{A}
an abelian category.
A persistence module
F\colon P\to\mathscr{A}
is
2-middle-exact
if, for all
x,y\in P
, the square
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(x\wedge y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(x)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(x\vee y)}

is middle-exact.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
P
be a lattice,
\mathscr{A}
an abelian category, and
F\colon P\to\mathscr{A}
2-middle-exact.
If
F
is monomorphic, then
F
is degree 1.

Dually, if
F
is epimorphic, then
F
is codegree 1.

Proof.

This follows directly from
6.3
.
∎

Recall that a subset
Q\subseteq P
is
down-closed
if for all
x\in Q
and
y\in P
such that
y\leq x
, we have
y\in Q
.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category, and let
P
be a down-finite distributive lattice such that
P_{\leq 1}
is down-closed. Let
F\colon P\to\mathscr{A}
be a 2-middle-exact functor such that
F|_{P_{\leq 1}}\cong 0
. Then
F
is monomorphic and degree 1.

Dually, let
P
be an up-finite distributive lattice such that
P^{\leq 1}
is up-closed. If
F\colon P\to\mathscr{A}
is a 2-middle-exact functor such that
F|_{P^{\leq 1}}\cong 0
,
F
is epimorphic and codegree 1.

Proof.

We prove the first part. The second part is dual.

Let
y\in P
, and set
Q=y^{\downarrow}
. We show that
F|_{Q}
is monomorphic by induction.
This will imply that
F
is monomorphic, as
y
was chosen arbitrarily.
Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{N}
be an ordering of the elements in
Q
such that
x_{i}\leq x_{j}\implies i\leq j
.

First, observe that if
x\in Q_{\leq 1}
, and
y\leq x
, then
y\in Q_{\leq 1}
by assumption, so
F(y\leq x)
is simply the zero map
0\to 0
, and thus a monomorphism.

Suppose now that for all
i\leq k
, we have that
F(y\leq x_{i})
is a monomorphism for all
y\leq x_{i}
, and suppose that
x_{k+1}\notin Q_{\leq 1}
. We want to show that
F(y\leq x_{k+1})
is a monomorphism for all
y\leq x_{k+1}
. Observe that it suffices to show this for
y\prec x_{k+1}
. By
7.4
and
7.1
, we can choose another
z\prec x_{k+1}
such that
y\vee z=x_{k+1}
. Consider the middle-exact square
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(y\wedge z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(x_{k+1}).}

The top arrow,
F(y\wedge z\leq z)
is a monomorphism, by the induction hypothesis. In particular,
\ker F(y\wedge z\leq z)\cong 0
.
Thus,
\ker F(y\leq x_{k+1})\cong 0
, by
6.2
.
In other words,
F(y\leq x_{k+1})
is a monomorphism, as desired.

We have shown that
F
is monomorphic. Thus, by
8.2
,
F
is degree 1.
∎

Definition 8.4
.

Let
P
be a lattice and
\mathscr{C}
a finitely bicomplete category. A functor
F\colon P\to\mathscr{A}
is called

if it is both degree

and codegree

.

Remark 8.5
.

Bidegree 1 multipersistence modules are called

in
[

]
.

Proposition 8.6
.

Let
P
be a down-finite distributive lattice such that
P_{\leq 1}
is down-closed,
\mathscr{A}
an abelian category, and
F\colon P\to\mathscr{A}
a degree 1 functor. Then
T_{1}F
is bidegree 1.

Dually, if
P
is an up-finite distributive lattice such that
P_{\geq 1}
is down-closed, and
F\colon P\to\mathscr{A}
is a codegree 1 functor, then
T^{1}F
is bidegree 1.

Proof.

We prove the first part. The second part is dual.

Let
K=\ker(T_{1}F\to F)
and
C=\coker(T_{1}F\to F)
. Note that
K|_{P_{\leq 1}}\cong 0
and
C|_{P_{\leq 1}}\cong 0
. For
x,y\in P
, consider the following commuting diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(x\wedge y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(x)\oplus K(y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(x\vee y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T_{1}F(x\wedge y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T_{1}F(x)\oplus T_{1}F(y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T_{1}F(x\vee y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(x\wedge y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(x)\oplus F(y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(x\vee y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(x\wedge y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(x)\oplus C(y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(x\vee y)}

By the snake lemma, both the top and bottom rows are exact, and so both
K
and

are 2-middle-exact functors.

It now follows from

that

is degree 1. Again applying the snake lemma to the diagram above, we see that

is a codegree 1 functor. Thus,
K\cong T_{1}K\cong 0
(as

).

Finally,

is now isomorphic to

∎

8.1.2.
Layers

Given a pointed category
\mathscr{C}
with basepoint
*
, and a morphism
f\colon A\to B
in
\mathscr{C}
, the
fiber
of

, denoted
\fib(f)
, is defined as the limit

\lim(A\xrightarrow{f}B\leftarrow*),

if it exists.
Dually, the
cofiber
of
f
, denoted
\cofib(f)
, is defined as the colimit

\colim(B\xleftarrow{f}A\to*),

if it exists.

In the following definition, observe that if
P
is a distributive lattice with minimal element 0, then by
Equation 2.4.1
,
T_{0}F
is the constant functor at
F(0)
(and dually,
T^{0}F
is the constant functor at the maximal element).

Definition 8.8
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a complete pointed category, let
P
be a distributive lattice, and let
n
be a nonnegative integer. The
n
th
layer
of
F
, denoted
D^{n}F
is defined as the fiber

D^{n}F=\fib(T^{n}F\to T^{n-1}F).

Dually, if
\mathscr{C}
is a cocomplete pointed category, the
n
th
colayer
of
F
is the cofiber

D_{n}F=\cofib(T_{n-1}F\to T_{n}F).

Proposition 8.9
.

Let
P
be a finite product of total orders with minimal elements,
\mathscr{C}
a complete pointed category, and
F\colon P\to\mathscr{C}
any functor. Then
D_{n}F
is bidegree
n
.

Dually, let
P
be a finite product of total orders with maximal elements,
\mathscr{C}
a cocomplete pointed category, and
F\colon P\to\mathscr{C}
any functor. Then
D^{n}F
is bidegree
n
.

Proof.

We give the proof for
D_{n}F
. The proof for
D^{n}F
is dual.

Let
0
denote the basepoint in
\mathscr{C}
. Let
P=S_{0}\times\dots\times S_{k}
, where each
S_{i}
is a total order with minimal element
0_{i}
.
For
S\subseteq[k]
, let
\lambda_{S}\colon P\to P
be given by

(\lambda_{S}(x_{0},\dots,x_{k}))_{i}=\begin{cases}x_{i},\quad i\in S,\\
0_{i},\quad\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We first show that

D_{n}F\cong\bigoplus_{S\subseteq[k],|S|=n}(D_{n}F)\circ\lambda_{S}.

We know that
D_{n}F
is codegree
n
, since it is the cokernel of a natural transformation between codegree
n
functors. Moreover,
D_{n}F
is 0 on
P_{\leq n-1}
. Hence, for
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in P
,

\displaystyle D_{n}F(x)

\displaystyle=\underset{y\in P_{\leq n},y\leq x}{\colim\ }D_{n}F(y)\cong\underset{S\subseteq[k],|S|\leq n}{\colim}D_{n}F(\lambda_{S}(x)).

The last isomorphism follows from
[
9
, Remark 5.1]
.
Now, as
D_{n}F(\lambda_{S}(x))\cong 0
for
|S|\leq n-1
(as then
\lambda_{S}(x)\in P_{\leq n-1}
),
D_{n}F(x)
is isomorphic to

\bigoplus_{S\subseteq[k],|S|=n}D_{n}F(\lambda_{S}(x)).

Hence, the canonical map

\bigoplus_{S\subseteq[k],|S|=n}(D_{n}F)\circ\lambda_{S}\to D_{n}F

is an isomorphism.

Now, as
T^{n}
is an additive functor, it suffices to show that
(D_{n}F)\circ\lambda_{S}
is degree
n
for each
S\subseteq[k]
with
|S|=n
. This follows directly from
[
9
, Proposition 3.15]
, so we are done.
∎

8.2.
Application: Higher-dimensional middle exactness

8.2.1.
Interval decomposition of bidegree 1 functors

Proposition 8.10
.

Let
P
be a finite product of finite total orders,
\mathbb{Fb}
a field, and
F\colon P\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
a p.f.d. bidegree 1 functor. Then
F
is interval decomposable.

Proof.

Let
0
denote the minimal element in
P
.
As
F
is codegree 1,
F\cong T_{1}F
. Thus,
D_{1}F=\coker(F(0)\to T_{1}F)\cong\coker(F(0)\to F)
. Let
K=\Ima\left(F(0)\to F\right)
, and consider the short exact sequence

0\to K\to F\to D_{1}F\to 0.

By
8.9
,
D_{1}F
is degree 1, so
K
is the kernel of degree 1 functors, and thus degree 1. Furthermore, it follows from the definition of
K
that
K
is epimorphic.
Hence,
K
is injective by
7.13
.
Thus,
F\cong K\oplus D_{1}F
, and
K
is interval decomposable by
7.15
.

It remains to show that
D_{1}F
is interval decomposable.
Write
P=S_{0}\times\dots\times S_{N}
, where each
S_{i}
is a finite total order.
Observe that by
8.9
,

D_{n}F\cong\bigoplus_{i\in[N]}D_{n}F\circ\lambda_{\{i\}}.

Observe further that
D_{n}F\circ\lambda_{\{i\}}\cong D_{n}F\circ j_{i}\circ\pi_{i}
, where
\pi_{i}\colon P\to S_{i}
is the projection map and
j_{i}\colon S_{i}\to P
is the inclusion given by

j_{i}(u)_{l}=\begin{cases}u,\quad l=i\\
0,\quad\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Now,
D_{n}F\circ j_{i}
is interval decomposable as it is a p.f.d. persistence module from a finite total order, by the structure theorem of persistence modules. Hence,
D_{n}F\circ\lambda_{\{i\}}
is interval decomposable, which concludes the proof. ∎

is a pushout and a pullback, which is easily verified.

8.2.2.
Higher-dimensional middle exactness

We recall here the usual definition of the Koszul complex.

Definition 8.13
.

Given a
k
-cube
\mathcal{X}\colon\mathcal{P}([k-1])\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
, the
Koszul complex
of
\mathcal{X}
, denoted
K_{\mathcal{X}}
, is the chain complex in
\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
given by

(K_{\mathcal{X}})_{i}=\bigoplus_{S\subseteq[k],|S|=k-i}\mathcal{X}(S),

with differential
\partial_{i+1}\colon(K_{\mathcal{X}})_{i+1}\to(K_{\mathcal{X}})_{i}
defined componentwise by

\partial_{i+1}|_{\mathcal{X}(S)}=\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{j}\mathcal{X}(S\subseteq S\cup\{t_{j}\}),

where
t_{0}<\dots<t_{i}
are the elements in
[k]\setminus S
.

We introduce the following definition, which generalizes that of
k
-middle-exactness in
[
14
]
.

Definition 8.14
.

Let
P
be a distributive lattice and
\mathbb{Fb}
a field. A functor
F\colon P\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
is said to be
k
-middle-exact
if for every strongly bicartesian
k
-cube
\mathcal{X}
in
P
, the Koszul complex of
F\circ\mathcal{X}
has trivial homology in all degrees
0<i<k
.

Lemma 8.15
.

Let
F\colon\mathcal{P}([k-1])\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
be a
k
-cube. Then

\underset{{S\subsetneq[k-1]}}{\colim}\mathcal{X}(S)\cong\coker\left((K_{\mathcal{X}})_{2}\xrightarrow{\partial_{2}}(K_{\mathcal{X}})_{1}\right),

and

\lim_{\emptyset\subsetneq S\subseteq[k-1]}\mathcal{X}(S)\cong\ker\left((K_{\mathcal{X}})_{k-2}\xrightarrow{\partial_{k-2}}(K_{\mathcal{X}})_{k-3}\right).

Proof.

We prove the first isomorphism. The second is dual.
By finality of the inclusion of the indexing posets, we have that,

\underset{{S\subsetneq[k-1]}}{\colim}\mathcal{X}(S)\cong\underset{{S\subsetneq[k-1]},\\
k-3\leq|S|\leq k-2}{\colim}\mathcal{X}(S).

Unwinding the definition of the Koszul complex, we get

\displaystyle\coker\left((K_{\mathcal{X}})_{2}\xrightarrow{\partial_{2}}(K_{\mathcal{X}})_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\coker\left(\bigoplus_{0\leq i_{0}<i_{1}\leq k-1}\mathcal{X}([k]\setminus\{i_{0},i_{1}\})\xrightarrow{\partial_{2}}\bigoplus_{0\leq j\leq k-1}\mathcal{X}([k]\setminus\{j\})\right),

where

\displaystyle\partial_{2}|_{\mathcal{X}([k-1]\setminus\{i_{0},i_{1}\})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}(([k-1]\setminus\{i_{0},i_{1}\})\subseteq([k-1]\setminus\{i_{1}\}))-\mathcal{X}(([k-1]\setminus\{i_{0},i_{1}\})\subseteq([k-1]\setminus\{i_{0}\})).

It follows from checking universal properties that

\underset{{S\subsetneq[k-1]},\\
k-3\leq|S|\leq k-2}{\colim}\mathcal{X}(S)\cong\coker\left((K_{\mathcal{X}})_{2}\xrightarrow{\partial_{2}}(K_{\mathcal{X}})_{1}\right).

∎

Let
P
be a finite distributive lattice, and let
x\in P
. Let
u_{0},\dots,u_{n-1}
denote the parents of
x
. By
7.1
,
\{u_{0},\dots,u_{n-1}\}
is a pairwise cover of
x
. By
2.15
, this pairwise cover gives rise to a strongly bicartesian
n
-cube
\mathcal{X}_{u_{0},\dots,u_{n-1}}
, defined by

\mathcal{X}_{u_{0},\dots,u_{n-1}}(S)=\begin{cases}x,&\quad S=[n-1],\\
\bigwedge_{i\notin S}u_{i},&\quad\text{ otherwise.}\\
\end{cases}

Lemma 8.16
.

Let
P
be a finite distributive lattice such that
P_{\leq 1}
is down-closed, and let
\mathbb{Fb}
be a field. If
F\colon P\to\mathbb{Fb}
is a functor that is
k
-middle-exact for all
k\geq 2
, then the canonical morphism

\varepsilon_{1}\colon T_{1}F\to F

is a split monomorphism with projective cokernel.

Dually, let
P
be a finite distributive lattice such that
P^{\leq 1}
is up-closed, and let
\mathbb{Fb}
be a field. If
F\colon P\to\mathbb{Fb}
is a functor that is
k
-middle-exact for all
k\geq 2
, then the canonical morphism

\eta^{1}\colon F\to T^{1}F

is a split epimorphism with injective kernel.

Proof.

We prove the first statement. The second is formally dual.

It is sufficient to show that
\varepsilon_{1}
is a cofibration in
\Fun_{\textrm{proj}}(P,\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}})
. We need to show that the latching map (
7.2.1
) is a monomorphism for each
x\in P
.

First, suppose
x
is the minimal element in
P
, which has join-dimension 0. The latching map here is

T_{1}F(x)\to F(x),

which is an isomorphism.

Now, suppose that
\jdim(x)=1
, and let
y\prec x
. Observe that then, by assumption,
y\in P_{\leq 1}
. The latching map at
x
is, by
7.7
,

F(y)\coprod_{T_{1}F(y)}T_{1}F(x)\to F(x),

which is an isomorphism as
T_{1}F(y)=F(y)
, and
T_{1}F(x)=F(x)
.

Now, let
x\in P
with
\jdim(x)\geq 2
. First, observe that

L_{x}(T_{1}F)\to T_{1}F(x)

is an isomorphism, by
7.8
.
Let now
u_{0},\dots,u_{n-1}
be the parents of
x
, and let
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}_{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}}
.
By
7.8
and
8.15
, the latching map
L_{x}F\to F(x)
is the map

\coker\left((K_{\mathcal{X}})_{2}\xrightarrow{\partial_{2}}(K_{\mathcal{X}})_{1}\right)\ \to\ (K_{\mathcal{X}})_{0}.

Now, by definition,
H_{1}(K_{\mathcal{X}})
is precisely the kernel of this map. As we assumed
F
to be
n
-middle-exact,
H_{1}(K_{\mathcal{X}})\cong 0
, and so the latching map is a monomorphism.
This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 8.17
.

If
P
is a poset that
satisfy both conditions of
8.16
,
i.e., if
P
is a finite distributive lattice such that
P_{\leq 1}
is down-closed and
P^{\leq 1}
is up-closed,
then
P
is a finite product of finite total orders.
We omit the details of the proof here, and just mention the main points of the argument.

Because every element in a finite distributive lattice has a unique reduced indecomposable join-decomposition, the structure of
P
is entirely decided by the structure of
P_{\leq 1}
. Supposing now that
P
is not a product of total orders, there must be elements
x,y,z\in P_{\leq 1}
with
x<y\wedge z<y,z
. Considering the element

p=\bigvee_{a\in P,a\ngeq y\wedge z}a,

leads to a contradiction to the assumption that
P^{\leq 1}
is meet-closed.

Theorem 8.18
.

Let
P
be a finite product of finite total orders, and let
\mathbb{Fb}
be a field. Let
F\colon P\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
be a functor that is
k
-middle-exact for all
k\geq 2
.

Then there exist
B,K,C\colon P\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Fb}}
with
K
injective,
C
projective and
B
bidegree 1, such that
F\cong B\oplus K\oplus C
.

Proof.

Let
K=\ker(F\to T^{1}F)
, and
C=\coker(T_{1}F\to F)
.
By
8.16
,
C
is projective, and the sequence

0\to T_{1}F\to F\to C\to 0

splits, so
F\cong T_{1}F\oplus C
.
Furthermore, by the same lemma,
K
is injective and the sequence

0\to K\to F\to T^{1}F\to 0

is split exact, and so by additivity of
T_{1}
, the sequence

0\to T_{1}K\to T_{1}F\to T_{1}T^{1}F\to 0

is also split exact. By
7.12
,
T_{1}K\cong K
, and so

F\cong T_{1}F\oplus C\cong T_{1}T^{1}F\oplus K\oplus C.

Applying
8.6
concludes the proof.
∎

Example 8.19

(Non-example)
.

Corollary 8.20
.

Let
P=S_{1}\times\dots\times S_{N}
be a finite product of finite total orders,
\mathbb{Fb}
a field, and

a p.f.d. functor that is
k
-middle-exact for all
2\leq k\leq N
. Then
F
is interval decomposable.

By

,

with

bidegree 1,

injective and

projective. Now,

,

(as

B

8.10
(as
B
is p.f.d.).
∎

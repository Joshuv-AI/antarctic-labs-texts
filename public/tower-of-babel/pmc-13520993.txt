N6-methyladenosine (m6A) RNA methylation in poultry growth, reproduction, metabolism, and disease: a review
Authors: Yan Yang, Lin Chen, Zhanhong Zheng, Gaofeng Cai, Jianzhen Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractN6-methyladenosine (m6A) is one of the most abundant internal chemical modifications in eukaryotic RNA. Advances in sequencing technology have driven m6A research in poultry. Partial m6A transcriptome maps have been constructed in species such as chickens, ducks, and geese, and the associations and potential functions of m6A in muscle development, reproductive regulation, nutrient metabolism, stress responses, and infectious diseases have been increasingly characterized. Current poultry m6A studies remain relatively scattered, and a dedicated synthesis of this field is still lacking. To address this gap, this review integrates relevant studies published in poultry over the past decade and summarizes research linking m6A modification to skeletal muscle growth and myofiber-type transitions, intramuscular fat deposition, follicle selection and laying performance, primordial germ cell formation, lipid metabolism and fatty liver development, nutritional regulation, chronic stress, and viral and bacterial infections. The review also examines the current limitations of poultry m6A research and identifies priorities for future research.Keywords: Epitranscriptome, N6-methyladenosine, Poultry, RNA modification

Introduction

N6-methyladenosine (m6A) modification is a prevalent and conserved post-transcriptional RNA modification in eukaryotes (Jiang et al., 2021). m6A is the major internal modification of messenger RNA (mRNA) and also occurs in various non-coding RNAs, including transfer RNA (tRNA), ribosomal RNA (rRNA), microRNA (miRNA), circular RNA (circRNA), and long non-coding RNA (lncRNA), spanning nearly the entire process of RNA metabolism (Jiang et al., 2021; Yang et al., 2018). By altering RNA secondary structure or recruiting specific reader proteins, m6A regulates precursor mRNA (pre-mRNA) splicing, polyadenylation, nuclear export, subcellular localization, translation efficiency, structural stability, and transcript degradation. Its broad involvement in RNA processing and turnover has made m6A modification an important focus of epitranscriptomic research (Jiang et al., 2021; Yang et al., 2018).

In mammals, research on the m6A epitranscriptome has a longer history; systematic transcriptome-wide maps have been generated, detection at single-base resolution has been achieved, and the complex regulatory network of writers, erasers, and readers has been characterized in depth (Dominissini et al., 2012, 2013; Liu et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2018). Mammalian studies have established core principles by which m6A regulates RNA fate, including RNA export, translation, and decay (Meyer et al., 2015; Shi et al., 2017; Zheng et al., 2013). In poultry, m6A-modified target transcripts, the expression of selected regulators, and associated biological processes vary across tissues, developmental stages, and physiological contexts; evidence for species-specific differences is emerging but remains largely indirect (Guo et al., 2022; Jia et al., 2023; Li et al., 2026).

Poultry are an important source of high-quality animal protein, including meat and eggs, and their growth, reproductive performance, and disease resistance have a direct impact on the production efficiency and economic returns of the poultry industry (OECD, Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations FAO, 2025). m6A-related changes have been reported in association with skeletal muscle development, intramuscular fat deposition, follicle selection, laying performance, pathogen infection, and environmental stress responses in poultry (Liang et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2021). Elucidating m6A regulatory mechanisms in avian model systems will help complement and extend epitranscriptomic theory and provide new molecular targets for improving poultry meat and egg quality, enhancing reproductive performance, and controlling disease (Fu et al., 2014). Advances in sequencing technology and declining costs have enabled tissue- and process-specific epitranscriptome maps to accumulate in chickens, ducks, and geese. Poultry m6A findings now span several biological fields but remain dispersed across them. This review as a narrative review summarizes poultry m6A studies published over the past decade, with a focus on different biological processes, and discusses current limitations and future directions for application. Relevant studies were mainly identified through PubMed searches for articles published over the past decade using terms including m6A RNA methylation, poultry, chicken, duck, goose, muscle development, reproduction, metabolism, stress, and infection. Reference lists of relevant articles were also screened. Studies were included according to their direct relevance to m6A biology in poultry.

Dynamic regulation of M6a and potential poultry-specific features

Biological process of m6A modification

The dynamic regulation of m6A modification depends on the coordinated action of three classes of factors: methyltransferases (writers), demethylases (erasers), and RNA-binding proteins (readers) (Oerum et al., 2021). In the canonical writer system, the heterodimer of methyltransferase-like 3 (METTL3) and methyltransferase-like 14 (METTL14) forms the catalytic core of the m6A methyltransferase complex: METTL3 carries out the methyltransfer reaction, while METTL14 supports RNA substrate recognition and complex stability. Accessory factors, including WTAP, VIRMA/KIAA1429, RBM15/RBM15B, ZC3H13, and CBLL1/HAKAI, contribute to complex localization and site selection (Zaccara et al., 2019). AlkB family dioxygenases, including fat mass and obesity-associated protein (FTO) and AlkB homolog 5 (ALKBH5), which depend on Fe(II) and α-ketoglutarate, remove m6A marks, thereby conferring reversibility and enabling dynamic responses (Jia et al., 2011; Zheng et al., 2013).

The biological consequences of m6A modification are executed by reader proteins that recognize the modified RNA directly or bind RNA structures exposed by m6A deposition. The downstream outcome depends on the reader, the position of the modification, the RNA substrate, subcellular localization, and the effector proteins recruited after recognition. In the nucleus, YTH domain-containing 1 (YTHDC1) interacts with splicing and export machinery to regulate pre-mRNA splicing and nuclear export (Roundtree et al., 2017; Xiao et al., 2016). In the cytoplasm, YTH N6-methyladenosine RNA-binding protein 1 (YTHDF1) can engage the translation initiation machinery, whereas YTHDF proteins can also recruit the CCR4-NOT deadenylase complex and promote RNA decay (Wang et al., 2015; Zaccara and Jaffrey, 2020). YTHDF1, YTHDF2, and YTHDF3 share many targets and can compensate for one another in a context-dependent manner, allowing their effects to vary with the RNA substrate and cellular state (Lasman et al., 2020; Shi et al., 2017; Zaccara and Jaffrey, 2020). Insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA-binding proteins (IGF2BPs) enhance the stability and translation of target mRNAs, whereas heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoproteins C and G (HNRNPC and HNRNPG) recognize m6A-induced changes in RNA structure and influence RNA processing (Alarcón et al., 2015; Huang et al., 2018). Reader-dependent recruitment of splicing, export, translation, stabilization, or decay machinery therefore converts the same chemical modification into different RNA fates (Jiang et al., 2021; Meyer and Jaffrey, 2017). Poultry studies generally fit this writer, eraser, and reader framework, although differences reported across studies may also reflect variation in tissue type, developmental stage, infection status, genome annotation, and detection method rather than stable species-level mechanisms (Chen et al., 2022; Li et al., 2022; Liang et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2021).

Conserved m6A regulatory machinery and poultry-specific biological contexts

The major m6A regulators reported in poultry include METTL3–METTL14-containing writer complexes, the demethylases FTO and ALKBH5, and YTH-domain readers. These factors participate in RNA methylation, demethylation, stability control, translation, and decay and are broadly consistent with the m6A regulatory framework established in mammals (Bayoumi et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2022; Gu et al., 2022a). Comparative genomic analyses and structural predictions have identified sequence and annotation differences between avian m6A-related genes and their mammalian homologs. Chicken METTL3 shares approximately 82 % amino acid sequence identity with human METTL3, a lower cross-species identity than that observed for other core writer-complex components, including METTL14 and WTAP. METTL3 was absent from early duck database annotations, whereas later studies detected its expression in duck muscle and examined METTL3 and METTL14 as candidate m6A writer components (Bayoumi et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2022). The core factors reported in poultry belong to the canonical writer, eraser, and reader system.

Avian m6A motifs and their transcript distributions retain broad similarities to those in mammals but also vary among tissues and physiological states. The RRACH/GGACU consensus motif (R = A or G; H = A, C, or U) and enrichment near stop codons and within 3′ untranslated regions (3′UTRs), commonly observed in mammalian mRNAs, have also been reported in duck embryonic pectoral muscle (Chen et al., 2022; Meyer et al., 2012). In embryonic pectoral muscle of Dingan geese, m6A peaks were concentrated mainly in coding regions and around start codons, whereas a GAAGAAG motif was identified in the livers of ducks infected with duck hepatitis A virus (DHAV) (Wu et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2021). Such variation in motif preference and peak distribution may alter the selection of target transcripts and modified regions, thereby influencing post-transcriptional regulation during muscle development and viral infection.

Mammalian studies show that the structure of core regulators, local RNA sequence, RNA secondary structure, and modification position can all affect m6A deposition and function. Structural analyses of the human METTL3–METTL14 complex identify METTL3 as the catalytic subunit and METTL14 as a structural partner that stabilizes the complex and contributes to substrate RNA recognition (Śledź and Jinek, 2016). The conservation of catalytic sites, complex assembly, and substrate recognition is therefore central to retaining function, whereas overall sequence identity provides only an initial indication. The subsequent detection of duck METTL3 identifies its earlier omission as an annotation issue and confirms that the gene is present in ducks. Local sequence and RNA secondary structure likewise influence site selection. RNA folding can conceal or expose candidate sites and thereby alter m6A deposition (Shachar et al., 2024). The position of m6A within a transcript also influences its regulatory effects. In mammalian cells, m6A in the 5′untranslated region (5′UTR) can support cap-independent translation under stress, whereas m6A in last exons has been associated with alternative polyadenylation (Ke et al., 2015; Meyer et al., 2015). Human tissue maps further show that m6A submotifs and transcript distributions vary among tissues and developmental stages (Zhang et al., 2020). Determining whether these features represent stable species-specific regulation will require direct comparisons among chickens, ducks, and geese using matched tissues, developmental stages, and analytical methods.

During muscle development, m6A regulation of GHR, circSIK2, and TET1/TET2 has been linked to myoblast differentiation and myofiber hypertrophy, whereas myofiber-type composition has been examined separately through tissue-level profiling and regulator perturbation (Liu et al., 2026; Shan et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025a; Zhao et al., 2025a). Reproductive studies in chickens have addressed follicle selection and laying performance, primordial germ cell formation and survival, and m6A-dependent regulation of 7-dehydrocholesterol reductase (DHCR7) translation in prehierarchical granulosa cells (Fan et al., 2019; Jia et al., 2023; Zuo et al., 2022). Nutritional and stress-related studies have linked m6A changes to hepatic lipid deposition, oxidative liver injury, and appetite-related hypothalamus–gut responses (Huang et al., 2025; Li et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2025). Because chickens lack RIG-I, antiviral RNA sensing relies more heavily on MDA5. During avian leukosis virus subgroup J (ALV-J) infection, m6A modification of viral RNA reduced MDA5 recognition and downstream interferon-β (IFN-β) activation (Yu et al., 2025). Reported variation in poultry m6A biology is currently evident mainly in modified RNAs and their physiological contexts. Direct evidence for avian-specific regulators or catalytic mechanisms remains limited.

M6a RNA methylation in poultry

m6A RNA methylation in poultry muscle development

Poultry are one of the major sources of dietary protein for humans (Marangoni et al., 2015), and muscle development in poultry is a key factor shaping production performance and meat quality (Wang et al., 2022b). m6A modification has been associated with embryonic and postnatal skeletal muscle development, hypertrophic adaptation, and myofiber-type specification in poultry (Gu et al., 2022a) (Fig. 1). Studies of these processes encompass transcriptome-level analyses and functional studies of specific m6A regulators and RNA targets (Table 1). At the transcriptome-wide level, sequencing data across multiple species have shown that m6A marks in skeletal muscle conform to the canonical RRACH or DRACH consensus motif, with modification sites enriched within the coding sequence (CDS) and around stop codons and 3′UTRs (Chen et al., 2022; Dominissini et al., 2012; Gu et al., 2022a, b; Meyer et al., 2012).

Involvement in skeletal muscle growth, development, and myoblast fate

Global m6A levels vary with skeletal muscle development and myoblast differentiation. Profiling of duck embryonic leg muscle showed higher m6A levels at embryonic day 10 (E10) than at day 20 (E20), and duck primary myoblasts (DPMs) also exhibited higher global m6A levels during proliferation (GM) than during differentiation (DM) (Liu et al., 2026). In chicken myoblasts, cycloleucine treatment reduced global m6A levels and was accompanied by G0/G1 arrest, decreased proliferation, and impaired terminal myotube formation (Wang et al., 2022a).

Functional studies have implicated specific m6A regulators in metabolic and developmental pathways. METTL3 catalyzes m6A modification at the GHR-139 site of growth hormone receptor (GHR) mRNA. This mark is subsequently recognized by m6A reader proteins, including hnRNPR, hnRNPA3, and hnRNPM, which are proposed to mediate its post-transcriptional repression. The resulting reduction in GHR transcript and protein abundance suppressed the expression of the mitochondrial biogenesis factors PGC-1α, NRF1, and TFAM and impaired myoblast differentiation through compromised mitochondrial function (Zhao et al., 2025a). Beyond linear mRNAs, METTL3 deposits m6A at nucleotide 131 of circSIK2. HNRNPA2B1 was identified as a direct reader of this modification, while eIF3 recruitment promoted circSIK2 translation into SIK2-176aa. This peptide activated GHR–IGF–AKT signaling and promoted cell-cycle progression and postnatal myofiber hypertrophy (Wang et al., 2025a). METTL3-mediated m6A modification during myogenesis depends on the RNA substrate. Modification of GHR mRNA was associated with reduced GHR expression and impaired myoblast differentiation, whereas modification of circSIK2 promoted its translation into SIK2-176aa and supported myoblast proliferation and myofiber hypertrophy. METTL3 expression alone therefore cannot predict its effect on muscle development. METTL14 inhibits DPM proliferation and differentiation while promoting myofiber hypertrophy in vivo. It directly binds TET2 mRNA and indirectly increases m6A modification of TET1, thereby coordinating transcripts with opposing effects on proliferation and differentiation and linking RNA methylation to DNA demethylation during myoblast development (Liu et al., 2026). m6A modification also intersects with the miRNA pathway. During pectoral muscle development in chicken embryos, integrative m6A-seq, RNA-seq, and miRNA-seq analyses revealed convergence of m6A-modified genes and miRNA targets on muscle-development genes such as WNT7B, suggesting that the two layers may shape a shared post-transcriptional regulatory network governing skeletal muscle differentiation and development (Gu et al., 2022a).

Involvement in fiber-type specification and physical traits of muscle

The composition of myofiber types underlies the physical traits of poultry muscle. Comparative m6A profiling across distinct chicken skeletal muscles revealed higher methylation levels in the typical oxidative muscle (anterior latissimus dorsi, ALD) than in the glycolytic muscle (pectoralis major, PM) (Shan et al., 2025). In Zhijin White geese, leg and breast muscles also showed distinct m6A landscapes. The leg muscle was enriched in slow-oxidative fibers, and differentially methylated genes were enriched in pathways related to muscle contraction and core metabolism (Zhao et al., 2025d). Studies in chickens and geese both associated muscle-specific m6A landscapes with differences in myofiber composition and metabolic pathways. However, these tissue-level comparisons did not validate individual m6A sites or reader proteins and therefore do not establish whether the observed methylation differences directly determine myofiber identity. As a cross-species mechanistic reference, a mouse study linked gut microbiota-derived betaine to increased METTL3 activity, enhanced m6A modification of Myh7, and a shift toward slow-oxidative myofibers (Yan et al., 2025). Different m6A enzymes may have contrasting effects on myofiber-type-related phenotypes. The standalone methyltransferase METTL16 inhibits terminal differentiation while promoting proliferation in chicken myoblasts, with its upregulation steering cells toward the slow-oxidative phenotype (Liu et al., 2024). Conversely, the demethylase ALKBH5 is highly expressed in fast-glycolytic muscles. Its overexpression in chicken primary myoblasts increased the expression of the fast-fiber markers MYH1E and MYH1G and reduced the expression of the slow-fiber marker MYH7B (Shan et al., 2025). Both methyltransferases and demethylases have been linked to myofiber-type regulation, although direct evidence that their opposing activities determine myofiber transitions in vivo remains limited.

Involvement in intramuscular fat (IMF) deposition

IMF deposition varies among skeletal muscles and is an important determinant of poultry meat quality (Cui et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2024). In 180-day-old Jingyuan chickens, IMF content was markedly higher in leg muscle than in breast muscle, accompanied by lower expression of METTL14, WTAP, FTO, and ALKBH5 in the leg. Despite the lower expression of these regulators, the global m6A level was significantly higher in leg muscle, indicating that global m6A abundance cannot be inferred from regulator expression alone (Zhang et al., 2023). A longitudinal methylated RNA immunoprecipitation sequencing (MeRIP-seq) analysis of breast muscle from Jingyuan chickens at 42, 126, and 180 days identified differentially methylated genes enriched in lipid-metabolism pathways (Yu et al., 2023). Neither study functionally validated individual m6A sites or downstream readers. Current data therefore cannot distinguish m6A changes that contribute to IMF deposition from those accompanying age- and myofiber-type-related differences in muscle composition.

Limitations and perspectives

The strength of current evidence in poultry muscle differs markedly with experimental approach. Site-level studies of GHR and circSIK2 provide relatively direct mechanistic support (Wang et al., 2025a; Zhao et al., 2025a), whereas work on myofiber type and IMF deposition relies mainly on tissue-level profiling or associations between regulator expression and phenotype (Shan et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023). Changes in global m6A abundance or in writer and eraser expression cannot be interpreted as having a uniform biological effect because the outcome depends on the modified RNA and the reader engaged. Reader-dependent decoding remains poorly characterized for most candidate transcripts, and much of the functional evidence derives from cultured myoblasts (Liu et al., 2026). In vivo studies are therefore needed to determine which transcript–reader relationships contribute to myofiber composition, muscle hypertrophy, and IMF deposition across developmental stages and poultry breeds.

m6A RNA methylation in poultry reproductive development

m6A modification has been associated with gonadal sex differentiation (Li et al., 2022), primordial germ cell formation and maintenance (Qiao et al., 2024; Zuo et al., 2022), follicular development and selection, ovarian function, and laying performance (Jia et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2025) in poultry (Fig. 2). Current evidence also indicates that m6A modification patterns may vary with reproductive hormones and environmental conditions during early life (Li et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2022). Roles in follicular development, follicle selection, and laying performance

m6A modification has been implicated in post-transcriptional regulation during follicular development and selection in chickens (Fan et al., 2019; Jia et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2025). The first m6A methylation atlas of the chicken follicle transcriptome showed that m6A marks were enriched near start and stop codons, with the global m6A enrichment inversely correlated with gene expression. During follicle selection, the number of m6A-modified transcripts rose markedly, and key factors of the canonical Wnt pathway exhibited dynamic m6A modification patterns. The Wnt ligand WNT9A and the receptor FZD1 displayed sharply opposing patterns of m6A methylation and expression before and after follicle selection, suggesting that m6A remodeling may be involved in changes in Wnt-pathway genes during follicle selection (Fan et al., 2019). Ovarian m6A profiling in Gushi chickens with contrasting laying performance showed higher global ovarian m6A methylation and increased expression of METTL3, RBM15, and YTHDF2 in high-yield hens than in low-yield hens. Multi-omics analysis identified WNT4, AMH, FN1, and FGF16 as candidate genes related to follicular development; these genes were enriched in the PI3K–Akt, Wnt, and Notch pathways associated with ovarian development and hormone secretion (Zhang et al., 2025). Both this study and the follicle-selection study linked m6A remodeling to Wnt signaling, but neither established that modification of a specific Wnt-pathway transcript altered pathway activity or laying performance (Fan et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2025).

Gonadal sex differentiation and primordial germ cell regulation

Sex-biased m6A landscapes have been identified during chicken embryonic gonadal differentiation. MeRIP-seq analysis of female and male gonads at embryonic day 7 revealed differential m6A modification in sex-related genes, including SOX9 and CYP19A1. Functional manipulation of YTH domain-containing 2 (YTHDC2) in male mesonephros/gonad mixed cells altered the expression of HEMGN and SOX9, providing partial functional evidence that this m6A reader may participate in gonadal sex differentiation (Li et al., 2022). m6A modification has also been linked to the formation and in vitro protection of chicken primordial germ cells (PGCs). Research found that m6A modification regulates LncBMP4 in two opposing ways: high m6A levels strengthen the competitive binding of LncBMP4 to the microRNA gga-mir-12211, lifting its repression of BMP4; on the other hand, m6A modification hinders LncBMP4 from being translated into the small peptide EPC5, which itself drives BMP4 transcription (Zuo et al., 2022). Cultured chicken PGCs are prone to oxidative stress-associated apoptosis during long-term in vitro culture (Liu et al., 2025; Qiao et al., 2024; Rengaraj et al., 2022). Supplementing cultures with resveratrol (RSV) treatment improved antioxidant and anti-apoptotic indices and was accompanied by changes in m6A methylation patterns. RSV treatment upregulated the anti-apoptotic gene BCL2, lowered Caspase-9 expression, and markedly raised the levels of the m6A reader proteins YTHDF1 and YTHDF3. Integrative m6A sequencing identified FAM129A and SFRP1 as candidate genes potentially associated with the anti-apoptotic response to RSV (Qiao et al., 2024). The LncBMP4 study examined transcript-specific m6A-dependent effects on competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) activity and peptide translation. The resveratrol study identified FAM129A and SFRP1 as candidates through integrative sequencing, but direct m6A-dependent mediation of the resveratrol response has not been demonstrated (Qiao et al., 2024; Zuo et al., 2022).

Reproductive hormones and early-life environmental stress

Reproductive hormones and early-life environmental conditions have both been associated with changes in m6A regulation in chickens (Wang et al., 2024b; Zhou et al., 2015). In chicken prehierarchical follicle granulosa cells, estrogen and m6A modification play important roles in regulating DHCR7, the rate-limiting enzyme in cholesterol synthesis. Estrogen receptors promote DHCR7 expression chiefly at the transcriptional level, while m6A modification in the 5′UTR of the DHCR7 T4 transcript variant directly enhances translation efficiency at the post-transcriptional level; these two levels of regulation act in parallel to maintain cholesterol homeostasis in granulosa cells (Li et al., 2025). Early-life continuous-light exposure was accompanied by alterations in the growth–metabolism axis and tissue-specific changes in m6A-related factors. In chicks, continuous-light exposure altered the expression of m6A machinery genes in the hypothalamus, hippocampus, and liver, while increased m6A methylation at specific sites in the 3′UTR of IGF2 mRNA coincided with marked reductions in IGF2 mRNA and IGF2BP expression (Yang et al., 2022). The DHCR7 study linked a defined 5′UTR m6A modification to translational efficiency, whereas the continuous-light study associated increased m6A methylation in the IGF2 3′UTR with reduced IGF2 expression but did not determine whether the modification destabilized IGF2 mRNA or contributed to the observed growth effects (Li et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2022).

Limitations and perspectives

Whether these reproductive relationships are reproducible across breeds, developmental stages, experimental systems, and commercial poultry lines remains unclear (Jia et al., 2023; Li et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2025). Their in vivo contributions to follicle selection, germ-cell maintenance, and laying performance also remain unresolved. Evidence from ducks and geese is particularly limited because poultry reproductive m6A research remains concentrated in chickens. Comparisons using matched reproductive tissues and developmental stages are needed to assess whether the regulatory patterns identified in chickens are conserved in waterfowl.

m6A RNA methylation in nutritional regulation, metabolism, and stress in poultry

Nutrient partitioning between the liver and adipose tissue in poultry is closely connected with intestinal and hypothalamic regulation of feed intake and energy balance (Huang et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2026). High-energy, low-protein diets, chronic corticosterone exposure, and immune challenge can disrupt this metabolic network, whereas betaine, selenomethionine, and plant-derived additives have been examined for their potential to alleviate the associated metabolic disturbances (Hu et al., 2020; Xia et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2026). Studies on m⁶A RNA methylation in poultry nutrition, metabolism, and stress responses.

Implications in lipid metabolism and fatty liver syndrome

In poultry, both laying hens and broilers are prone to excessive hepatic lipid accumulation under nutritional overload or stress, giving rise to fatty liver syndrome (FLS) (Lu et al., 2019; Rozenboim et al., 2016; Tan et al., 2025). In poultry FLS models induced by chronic corticosterone (CORT) stress or by a high-energy, low-protein diet, the glucocorticoid receptor (GR) becomes over-activated. Activated GR binds to the promoter of the m6A demethylase FTO and promotes FTO transcription. In the CORT model, FTO-mediated m6A demethylation of SREBP1 and SCD mRNAs increased their stability and translation, thereby promoting triglyceride accumulation in hepatocytes (Hu et al., 2020). In hens fed a high-energy, low-protein diet, increased GR occupancy at the FTO promoter and reduced GR occupancy at the YTHDF2 promoter were accompanied by higher FTO expression, lower YTHDF2 expression, site-specific m6A demethylation of FASN, SREBP1, and SCD transcripts, and increased expression of these genes. However, the study did not examine whether YTHDF2 directly bound these transcripts or affected their degradation (Feng et al., 2021). The recurrence of the GR-FTO-lipogenic transcript relationship in the corticosterone- and diet-induced fatty liver models suggests that it is not confined to a single experimental challenge (Feng et al., 2021; Hu et al., 2020). In adipose tissue, however, current studies have mainly implicated ALKBH5 in preadipocyte proliferation and differentiation. ALKBH5 is highly expressed in chicken abdominal adipose tissue, particularly during the early stages of fat development (Zhang et al., 2021). It has been reported that knockdown of ALKBH5 in preadipocytes raises the global m6A level and, although it promotes proliferation through upregulation of Ki67 and PCNA, it markedly suppresses lipid droplet accumulation and the expression of lipogenic genes (such as PPARγ, A-FABP, and FAS), thereby blocking terminal adipocyte differentiation (Zhang et al., 2021). ALKBH5 also reduces the mRNA stability of lecithin–cholesterol acyltransferase (LCAT) through its demethylase activity. Because LCAT inhibits preadipocyte proliferation and promotes their differentiation, the ALKBH5-mediated downregulation of LCAT lifts the inhibition on preadipocyte proliferation and weakens its pro-differentiation effect, thereby regulating adipogenesis in chicken (Chao et al., 2024). Although ALKBH5-mediated destabilization of LCAT mRNA and ALKBH5 knockdown both favored preadipocyte proliferation and limited differentiation, the opposite direction of ALKBH5 manipulation indicates that additional RNA targets or differentiation-stage-dependent effects may shape its role in chicken adipogenesis.

Dietary nutrients and natural additives affect m6A levels and metabolism

Feed additives, methyl donors, and plant-derived extracts have been associated with changes in m6A-related enzymes and metabolic phenotypes (Yang et al., 2025). Dietary betaine supplementation has been reported to reduce hepatic lipid deposition in laying hens. In laying hens, betaine increased m6A methylation in the 3′UTR of SREBP1 mRNA and was associated with accelerated SREBP1 degradation, reduced expression of the lipogenic genes FASN and SCD, and increased expression of the fatty acid β-oxidation gene CPT1A (Yang et al., 2025). Dietary supplementation with selenomethionine (SeMet) has also been linked to reduced oxidative stress and necroptosis. In lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced liver injury in laying hens, METTL3 expression was increased, accompanied by higher m6A methylation of NRF2mRNA and reduced Nrf2 stability. SeMet supplementation reduced METTL3 expression and NRF2 m6A methylation, and these changes were accompanied by lower hepatic oxidative stress, inflammation, and necroptosis (Li et al., 2024).

The natural flavonoid quercetin was reported to bind METTL3 and inhibit its expression, reduce m6A methylation of PTEN mRNA, restore PTEN-related PI3K-AKT signaling, and attenuate mitochondrial dysfunction and liver injury in broilers (Xia et al., 2025). Ochratoxin A (OTA) causes gut microbiota dysbiosis and anorexia in broilers (Fang et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2020; Zhai et al., 2021). OTA exposure upregulated METTL3 and YTHDF2 in the hypothalamus, increased m6A methylation at specific sites of the bile acid receptor FXR mRNA, and was associated with reduced FXR protein expression and weaker transcriptional activation of the orexigenic neuropeptides AGRP and NPY. Dietary supplementation with walnut green husk polysaccharide (WGP) altered gut microbiota and bile acid profiles, reduced OTA-associated m6A hypermethylation, restored FXR protein expression and appetite-related gene transcription, and alleviated anorexia in broilers. Because WGP simultaneously altered gut microbiota, bile acid profiles, hypothalamic m6A modification, and appetite-related signaling, the alleviation of anorexia cannot be attributed to m6A changes alone (Zhao et al., 2026).

Involvement in poultry stress response and neuroendocrine regulation

Under chronic stress, hepatic METTL3 expression and m6A levels on multiple heat shock protein (HSP) transcripts increased, while higher HSP mRNA abundance coincided with lower HSP protein abundance. This divergence between HSP mRNA and protein abundance was consistent with post-transcriptional inhibition of HSP expression, although direct METTL3-dependent regulation was only partly validated (Feng et al., 2020). Whereas the corticosterone study focused on a liver-centered HSP response, LPS challenge was accompanied by m6A changes in both the hypothalamus and ileum. Chronic LPS-induced immune stress causes a decline in production performance and anorexia in broilers. LPS triggers a marked rise in the global m6A methylation level in both the hypothalamus and the ileum. In the ileum, increased m6A methylation occurred alongside decreased MTOR mRNA expression and a tendency toward reduced mTOR and p70 ribosomal S6 kinase (p70S6K) protein levels, reduced intestinal 5-hydroxytryptamine (5-HT) synthesis and expression of tight-junction proteins such as JAM2, and impaired gut-barrier function. In the hypothalamus, the genes with differential m6A methylation are enriched in neurodevelopmental regulatory pathways, including mTOR, mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK), and Wnt, and the expression of the appetite-related neuropeptide NPY is markedly downregulated (Huang et al., 2025). Current data do not distinguish a shared stress-responsive m6A program from separate tissue-specific responses.

Limitations and perspectives

Current evidence does not establish whether m6A changes mediate nutritional and stress responses in poultry or merely accompany broader metabolic remodeling. The GR–FTO–lipogenic transcript axis has been reported in both corticosterone- and diet-induced fatty liver models, providing comparatively consistent support for its involvement in hepatic lipid metabolism (Feng et al., 2021; Hu et al., 2020). By contrast, most intervention studies document concurrent changes in phenotype, gene expression, m6A profiles, redox status, gut microbiota, or bile acid metabolism. Because these physiological changes could themselves reshape the m6A landscape, the direction of causality remains uncertain. Evidence is also derived almost exclusively from chickens, with little corresponding research in ducks or geese. Matched comparative studies are therefore needed to determine whether the regulatory relationships identified in chickens extend to other poultry species.

m6A RNA methylation in poultry infectious diseases

Infection differs from development and metabolism because the m6A system is shaped by both host defense and pathogen-driven interference (Wang et al., 2026; Yu et al., 2025). Avian viruses may modify their own RNA (Yu et al., 2025), disrupt host writer complexes (Wang et al., 2025b), or engage reader-dependent translation of host transcripts (Wang et al., 2026), whereas current bacterial studies mainly describe remodeling of the host intestinal transcriptome (Zhao et al., 2025b). The direction of m6A-associated effects therefore depends on the pathogen, the modified RNA, and the level of functional validation (Table 4) (Fig. 4).

m6A-related research in poultry infectious diseases.

Studies on m⁶A RNA methylation in poultry infectious diseases.

Viral infections

During ALV-J infection, m6A modification of viral genomic RNA provides a direct example of a pathogen using the host methylation system to evade innate immune recognition in poultry. Because chickens lack RIG-I, MDA5 provides a major cytosolic route for sensing viral RNA. m6A sites in the ALV-J genome were enriched in the envelope (Env) region and 3′UTR, and these modifications reduced MDA5 binding and activation, thereby limiting downstream IFN-β production. FTO overexpression, DAA treatment, or site-directed removal of m6A sites enhanced MDA5 recognition, increased IFN-β responses, and reduced viral replication in vitro and in vivo; loss of MDA5 abolished this antiviral effect (Yu et al., 2025). These interventions support an m6A-dependent immune-evasion mechanism rather than a transcriptome-level association alone. Broader profiling of ALV-J-infected poultry tissues identified differential m6A methylation of immune-related mRNAs and lncRNAs and implicated YTHDC1 and YTHDC2 as candidate readers. These data do not yet establish direct reader-target relationships or their contribution to viral persistence and tumorigenesis (Ji et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2022).

Whereas ALV-J exploits methylation of its own genome, Marek’s disease virus (MDV) can also interfere directly with the host m6A machinery. Host METTL3 and METTL14 expression was lower during lytic replication than during latent or tumor-transformation phases, and overexpression of these factors suppressed MDV replication (Zhuang et al., 2023). More directly, the MDV kinase US3 binds and phosphorylates WTAP at S273, S305, S314, and S375, reducing transcriptome-wide m6A abundance and cellular protein translation without changing WTAP stability (Wang et al., 2025b). Other profiling studies detected m6A modification of the viral pp38 and UL33 transcripts (Zhuang et al., 2023) and altered methylation of host immune transcripts, circRNAs, and lncRNAs, but the functional contribution of most of these changes remains unresolved (Sun et al., 2021b, c).

Newcastle disease virus (NDV) presents a different outcome in which a host m6A reader contributes to antiviral defense. Infection was accompanied by redistribution of m6A peaks from stop codons and 3′UTRs toward coding regions, with the affected genes enriched in autophagy and ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis pathways (Yuan et al., 2023). Within this response, YTHDF2 directly bound m6A sites in the coding region of HSPA2 mRNA and enhanced its translation. Increased HSPA2 protein abundance reduced NDV nucleoprotein levels and restricted viral replication independently of classical interferon signaling (Wang et al., 2026).

The DHAV study provides a strain-comparison perspective with lower mechanistic resolution. Ducklings infected with the attenuated strain maintained higher hepatic mRNA m6A levels and mRNA stability than those infected with the virulent strain and showed less severe liver injury. Because causal modification sites and regulators were not identified, these findings support an association between m6A maintenance and attenuated infection rather than establishing a protective mechanism. The reported enrichment of the GAAGAAG motif should likewise be interpreted as a feature of that dataset rather than a stable duck-specific motif (Wu et al., 2022).

Bacterial infection and intestinal immune responses

Compared with viral infection, evidence for m6A involvement in bacterial infection is more limited. Campylobacter jejuni is a major foodborne pathogen that poses a threat to poultry production and public health (Al Hakeem et al., 2022), and its colonization is associated with marked changes in cecal RNA m6A methylation in chickens. An earlier direct RNA sequencing study identified 192 differential alternative splicing events and 426 transcripts with altered poly(A) tail length after C. jejuni inoculation. The study also identified 121 differentially expressed transcripts, 37 of which contained predicted m6A sites. These transcripts were enriched in immune processes related to defense against Gram-negative bacteria and positive regulation of IL-6 production. Changes in m6A methylation, alternative splicing, and poly(A) tail length reflected broad post-transcriptional remodeling during intestinal bacterial infection, although the study did not determine whether these changes were functionally coordinated (Zhao et al., 2025c). A subsequent MeRIP-seq and RNA-seq study compared C. jejuni-inoculated chickens classified as resistant or susceptible according to their cecal bacterial burdens. Chickens classified as susceptible showed lower global cecal m6A levels, lower YTHDC2 expression, and lower levels of several proinflammatory cytokines, including IL-8, IL-1β, and TNF-α. In C. jejuni LPS-challenged HD11 cells, YTHDC2 knockdown significantly increased the expression of six genes that showed differential methylation and expression in the cecum: EPG5, WDR41, SUSD5, STAB2, FOS, and IFT74. YTHDC2 knockdown also altered apoptosis- and autophagy-related responses. However, direct YTHDC2 binding to these candidate transcripts and m6A-dependent regulation of their expression were not tested (Zhao et al., 2025b).

Limitations and perspectives

Interpreting m6A function in poultry infection is complicated by its dependence on the pathogen, RNA substrate, and stage of infection. Relatively direct functional evidence is available for ALV-J, NDV, and the MDV US3-WTAP interaction, whereas the DHAV strain comparison, MDV noncoding-RNA profiles, and C. jejuni studies remain largely based on profiling or indirect perturbation. The apparent difference between US3-mediated suppression of host transcriptome-wide m6A and increased lncRNA methylation reported in earlier MDV studies may reflect differences in RNA class, infection phase, or experimental system rather than a uniform change in the m6A landscape (Sun et al., 2021a; Wang et al., 2025b). Compared with mammalian infection models, reader-target networks remain much less extensively characterized in poultry (Tsai and Cullen, 2020; Zaccara et al., 2019). In bacterial infection, YTHDC2 perturbation implicates reader involvement but does not establish direct target relationships. The main unresolved question is how m6A regulation of pathogen RNA and host transcripts shifts across pathogens, infection stages, and immune cell types, thereby producing proviral or antiviral outcomes.

Shared m6A regulatory features and variable functional outcomes across biological processes in poultry

Poultry development, metabolism, stress responses, and infection all involve m6A writers, erasers, and readers, but the same regulatory components do not necessarily produce the same biological outcome (Chao et al., 2024; Feng et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2026). The effect of m6A modification depends on the RNA substrate, reader protein, cell type, physiological state, and pathogen context (Fig. 5). In chicken myoblasts, METTL3-mediated methylation of GHR mRNA has been associated with reduced GHR expression and impaired mitochondrial biogenesis (Zhao et al., 2025a), whereas METTL3-dependent methylation of circSIK2 promotes its translation into SIK2-176aa and contributes to myoblast proliferation and myofiber hypertrophy (Wang et al., 2025a). METTL3-related methylation has also been linked to stress-responsive HSP transcripts (Feng et al., 2020) and to NRF2mRNA destabilization in oxidative liver injury models (Li et al., 2024), although the level of functional validation differs among these studies (Feng et al., 2020; Li et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025a; Zhao et al., 2025a). The outcome of METTL3-mediated methylation is shaped by the modified RNA and the reader-dependent pathway engaged downstream.

Overview of m⁶A RNA methylation in major biological processes in poultry.

Chronic corticosterone stress and high-energy, low-protein diet models converge on GR-dependent FTO activation, reduced m6A levels on lipogenic transcripts, and increased expression of SREBP1, FASN, and SCD (Feng et al., 2021; Hu et al., 2020). Reader-dependent decoding also varies among poultry models. In fatty liver models, reduced YTHDF2 expression was associated with increased stability of lipogenic transcripts, consistent with a decay-related function, although direct YTHDF2 binding to these transcripts was not examined (Feng et al., 2021). During Newcastle disease virus infection, YTHDF2 directly bound m6A sites in the coding region of HSPA2 mRNA, enhanced HSPA2 translation, and restricted viral replication (Wang et al., 2026). The molecular basis for these different YTHDF2 outcomes remains unresolved. The RNA substrate, position of the m6A site, associated proteins, and physiological state may each influence whether a modified RNA is degraded, stabilized, or translated. Reader expression alone cannot predict RNA fate.

During infection, m6A modification may occur on either pathogen RNA or host transcripts, resulting in proviral or antiviral effects depending on the virus, host cell, infection stage, and RNA target (Wang et al., 2025b, 2026; Yu et al., 2025). Across the poultry studies reviewed here, MeRIP-seq and m6A-seq, often combined with RNA-seq and pathway-enrichment analysis, remain the most common approaches (McIntyre et al., 2020). These antibody-enrichment methods generally identify methylated regions rather than precise modified nucleotides (McIntyre et al., 2020). Peak detection and between-group comparisons can be affected by antibody-binding preferences, transcript abundance, sequencing depth, and analytical thresholds, and these methods do not directly determine the proportion of transcript molecules modified at a given site (McIntyre et al., 2020). Antibody-enrichment profiles can identify candidate pathways and targets, but direct links to RNA stability, translation, or phenotype require site-level validation, reader-binding assays, and functional perturbation (McIntyre et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2026; Zhao et al., 2025a).

Conclusion

Research on m6A in poultry has expanded from transcriptome-wide mapping to functional analyses of muscle development, reproduction, metabolism, stress responses, and infection. Target-specific mechanistic evidence is now available in several models. Examples include GHR and circSIK2 in muscle (Wang et al., 2025a; Zhao et al., 2025a), m6A-mediated reduction of MDA5 recognition of ALV-J RNA (Yu et al., 2025), and the YTHDF2–HSPA2 relationship during Newcastle disease virus infection (Wang et al., 2026). Together, these studies connect m6A with post-transcriptional regulation in defined poultry models. The GHR and circSIK2 studies further show that METTL3 can produce distinct regulatory outcomes depending on the RNA substrate and cellular context.

However, the evidence remains uneven. Many studies identify differential m6A peaks, differentially expressed genes, and enriched pathways without determining functional modification sites, reader proteins, or causal direction (Gu et al., 2022a; Jia et al., 2023). Most poultry m6A profiles derive from bulk tissues or mixed-cell samples and may therefore obscure cell-type-specific regulation. Generalizability across breeds, developmental stages, and production conditions also remains largely untested. Omics analyses can nominate candidate targets, but direct regulatory relationships require site-level perturbation, reader-binding assays, rescue experiments, and in vivo validation. Only a limited number of poultry models have progressed to target-specific functional validation (Wang et al., 2026; Yu et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025a).

Single-cell, spatial, and low-input m6A approaches also remain rarely used in poultry research. Future studies should prioritize biologically consequential targets for site-level and reader-dependent validation while improving cell-type and developmental-stage resolution. Before translation to poultry production, candidate nutritional and genetic interventions require evaluation of dose or intervention intensity, tissue specificity, long-term safety, and consistency across breeds and production environments. Such work could help distinguish reproducible m6A-associated biomarkers from mechanistically supported regulatory relationships with practical application potential.

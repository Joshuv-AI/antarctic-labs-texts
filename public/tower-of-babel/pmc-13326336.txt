A nomogram integrating dynamic total bile acid monitoring, medication history, and curative effect for preterm birth prediction in twin pregnancies with intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy
Authors: Fei Ding, Jia Li, Minjie Zhang, Yanhua Zhao, Shanshan Liang, Qianwen Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveIntrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP) complicated by twin pregnancy significantly increases the risk of preterm birth, and no tailored predictive tools for gestational age (GA) at delivery have been developed for this specific population to date. This study aimed to develop and validate a twin-specific nomogram with dynamic total bile acid (TBA) monitoring, medication history and curative effect for preterm birth prediction in this population.MethodsA retrospective cohort of 258 twin pregnancies complicated by ICP was enrolled (November 2024–November 2025). The data included demographic, clinical, biochemical (dynamic TBA parameters, liver enzymes), and therapeutic variables (ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) usage, combination regimens, TBA response posttreatment). LASSO regression was used to select predictors, which were incorporated into a logistic regression-based nomogram. The model was validated using discrimination (the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC)), classification accuracy (sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV), calibration (Hosmer–Lemeshow test, calibration curves), and clinical utility (decision curve analysis (DCA)).ResultsIn this cohort, the incidence of preterm birth was 83.3%. The independent predictors of preterm birth included GA at ICP diagnosis, UDCA usage, GA at TBA peak, TBA severity group at peak, predelivery TBA (TBA end), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), and treatment curative effect (all P < 0.05). The discriminatory performance of the nomogram, as measured by the area under the curve (AUC), was 0.812 (95% CI: 0.721–0.903) in the training set and 0.740 (95% CI: 0.590–0.889) in the test set. Calibration curves and Hosmer–Lemeshow tests (training set P = 0.1527; test set P = 0.6991) confirmed good agreement between the predicted and actual outcomes. DCA demonstrated significant net benefits across a clinically relevant risk threshold (0–0.833). The model exhibited high specificity (93.8%) and negative predictive value (85.7%) in the test set.ConclusionTo our knowledge, this is among the first nomograms for preterm birth prediction in twin pregnancies with ICP that integrate dynamic TBA monitoring and therapeutic variables. This model is intended primarily as a low-risk exclusion tool to support clinical monitoring strategies, rather than to guide high-risk prediction or delivery decisions. Notably, the model predicts a composite preterm birth outcome modified by both biological risk and clinical intervention rather than purely spontaneous preterm birth, and its low sensitivity further restricts its utility for high-risk preterm birth prediction. Its clinical utility for this purpose requires further rigorous prospective and future external validation studies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12884-026-09089-y.

Introduction

Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP) is defined as a liver disorder unique to pregnancy characterized by the triad of (1) intractable pruritus, (2) elevated serum TBA concentration (> 10 µmol/L), and (3) possible liver enzyme abnormalities. A defining feature of ICP is the spontaneous resolution of all these manifestations after delivery [1]. The incidence of ICP varies across ethnicities and geographical regions; the global prevalence of this condition varies significantly, ranging from less than 1% to 27% [2, 3], with a notable prevalence of 6.06% in China [4], and even reaches 4–10% in the Yangtze River Basin, Sichuan, and Chongqing [5]. Although the exact pathogenesis of ICP remains elusive, accumulating evidence suggests that genetic susceptibility, hormonal fluctuations during pregnancy and environmental factors are involved [6]. A key pathological feature of ICP is the abnormal elevation of maternal bile acids, which can readily cross the placental barrier, accumulate in the fetal circulation and amniotic fluid, and exert direct toxic effects on the fetus [7].

ICP is closely associated with adverse perinatal outcomes, among which preterm birth is the most concerning complication [8, 9]. Preterm birth is defined as delivery at ≥ 28 weeks and < 37 weeks of GA, which is consistent with clinical practice and national obstetric guidelines in China and is the standard definition used for obstetric research and clinical management in our center, and infants have significantly increased risks of neonatal mortality and morbidity. For twin pregnancies, chorionicity (e.g., dichorionic diamniotic (DCDA), monochorionic diamniotic (MCDA), and monochorionic monoamniotic (MCMA)) may affect the timing of pregnancy termination because of specific complications (e.g., twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome (TTTS) and selective intrauterine growth restriction (sIUGR)), which are confounding factors for preterm birth etiology [28]. The vulnerability of preterm infants stems primarily from immature organ system development, leading to complications such as respiratory distress syndrome, intraventricular hemorrhage, and necrotizing enterocolitis [10]. Moreover, preterm birth may cause long-term sequelae, including cerebral palsy, intellectual developmental disorders, sensory impairments, and chronic diseases such as heart failure and hypertension throughout life [11]. Notably, compared with those without ICP, twin pregnancies with ICP have a 15-fold higher risk of preterm birth [12, 13]. Bile acids enhance uterine contractility, and twin pregnancy increases uterine distension—together, these factors substantially increase the risk of preterm birth [13]. It has been reported that the risk of preterm birth is nearly ten times greater in twin pregnancies [14], especially those complicated by premature rupture of membranes (PROM), gestational hypertension and gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) [15]. Thus, twin pregnancy with ICP is associated with adverse neonatal outcomes, making preterm birth prediction highly clinically important.

ICP is typically stratified on the basis of peak serum TBA levels (TBA peak): extremely severe (TBA ≥ 100), moderate-high (40 ≤ TBA < 100), moderate-low (20 ≤ TBA < 40), and mild (TBA < 20) [12]. However, the clinical management of ICP in twin pregnancies remains challenging because of conflicting guidelines and limited evidence. For instance, while UDCA is recommended as the first-line therapy for reducing bile acid levels in ICP [16], the efficacy of UDCA monotherapy versus combination regimens (e.g., ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) plus S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) or polyene phosphatidylcholine capsules (PPC)) in improving pregnancy outcomes remains controversial [16]. More importantly, there is a critical paucity of data on whether medication history (e.g., usage of UDCA alone or in combination with SAM/PPC) and curative effect (defined as TBA level reduction or elevation postmedication) are associated with preterm birth risk—two clinically actionable variables that directly reflect disease management effectiveness and dynamic progression. Additionally, there is no consensus among domestic and international guidelines on whether twin pregnancies with mild ICP (mildly elevated TBA) should be managed as severe cases [16, 17]. Furthermore, although previous studies have shown that twin pregnancy and elevated TBA levels are significant and potentially independent risk factors for the pathogenesis of preterm birth in women diagnosed with ICP [18, 19], most investigations have focused on univariate analyses or single-factor associations [20–22] or conventional clinical and biochemical indicators while systematically neglecting the predictive value of medication-related variables. This gap severely limits the comprehensiveness and clinical relevance of existing predictive models, as treatment response serves as a dynamic surrogate for disease control and fetal risk rather than relying solely on static baseline indicators. Consequently, the need for comprehensive multivariate models that integrate clinical, biochemical, and therapeutic variables to accurately predict preterm birth risk in this high-risk population remains unmet.

Nomograms are graphical prediction tools that integrate multiple risk factors into a statistical model, offering more accurate and intuitive risk stratification than conventional clinical methods do [23–25]. These tools have been widely validated and adopted in various clinical settings for outcome prediction. Despite the recognized clinical need, a nomogram for accurately predicting preterm birth in this high-risk population—twin pregnancies complicated by ICP—remains unavailable. Notably, while their study provided valuable insights, existing preterm birth prediction models for ICP either focus on singleton pregnancies or a mixed cohort with a limited number of twin cases [26, 27] or rely solely on static clinical/biochemical indicators (e.g., single TBA peak values) [20–22], failing to address two critical gaps: first, the unique high-risk profile of twin pregnancies (10-fold higher preterm birth risk than singletons [14]) demands a specialized tool rather than extrapolating from singleton data; second, dynamic TBA changes (from first elevation to predelivery) directly reflect fetal exposure duration to toxic bile acids, yet they are rarely incorporated; third, medication history (e.g., UDCA monotherapy vs. combination regimens) and curative effect (TBA response posttreatment) serve as dynamic surrogates for disease control, but their predictive value in twin ICP remains unelucidated. To address these key unmet needs for the understudied twin ICP population, we developed a twin-specific nomogram integrating dynamic TBA parameters, medication records, and treatment efficacy, aiming to improve preterm birth risk stratification in this high-risk population.

In this study, we aimed to develop and validate an early twin-specific nomogram for preterm birth prediction in twin pregnancies with ICP, integrating three core innovative factors: dynamic TBA monitoring parameters (first elevation, peak, and predelivery levels), UDCA-based medication history, and curative effect (TBA changes posttreatment). We retrospectively analysed clinical data from a large cohort of patients with this condition, integrating key demographic, clinical, biochemical, and therapeutic variables. The discriminative ability (AUC), calibration (Hosmer–Lemeshow test, calibration curves), classification accuracy (sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV), and clinical utility (DCA) of the model were rigorously evaluated. We aimed to provide clinicians with a reliable and user-friendly tool to facilitate early identification of high-risk patients, assist in optimizing individualized monitoring strategies (in combination with clinicians’ comprehensive clinical judgment), and reduce overintervention for low-risk patients. Notably, this tool does not yet provide direct evidence for guiding timely clinical interventions (e.g., dexamethasone for fetal lung maturation), which still relies on standard clinical assessment.

Materials and methods

Study population

This study received approval from the Ethics Committee of West China Second University Hospital, Sichuan University (Approval No.: 2024380) and was conducted in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki. We retrospectively reviewed the data of pregnant women who received prenatal care and delivered at this hospital between November 2024 and November 2025. The inclusion criterion was twin pregnancy complicated by ICP. ICP was diagnosed per the Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Intrahepatic Cholestasis of Pregnancy in China (2015 Edition): unexplained pruritus during pregnancy and a TBA concentration ≥ 10 µmol/L and postpartum TBA normalization. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) incomplete case data; (2) significant comorbidities, including preexisting cardiovascular diseases and other major systemic disorders; (3) autoimmune diseases; (4) human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection and syphilis; (5) persistent viral infections affecting the liver (hepatitis A, B, C, CMV, and HSV); and (6) poor adherence to UDCA therapy (missing doses for ≥ 3 consecutive days). A total of 258 pregnant women who were diagnosed with twin pregnancy complicated by ICP were ultimately included in the cohort, as outlined in the study flowchart (Supplementary Fig. 1). Serum TBA levels were measured using Meikang Biotechnology Co., Ltd. reagents (enzymatic cycling assay) on a Siemens ADVIA Chemistry XPT Chemistry Analyser, with a detection range of 0.7–250 µmol/L and an intra/interassay coefficient of variation (CV) < 5.0%. Serial TBA measurements were performed with a strict and standardized frequency and timing: the first TBA test was completed at the time of ICP diagnosis (to determine the baseline TBA level, TBA first); subsequent dynamic TBA monitoring was conducted at 1-week fixed intervals from the date of ICP diagnosis until delivery. Additional unscheduled TBA measurements were initiated per clinical criteria for patients with worsening clinical symptoms (e.g., persistent/worsening intractable pruritus, appearance of jaundice) or abnormal liver function indicator results (serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT) or aspartate aminotransferase (AST) elevation > 2 times the upper limit of normal), regardless of the 1-week interval. These dynamic TBA monitoring results (scheduled and unscheduled) directly guided treatment-related decisions: UDCA initiation within 48 h of diagnosis, assessment of treatment response at 1-week intervals, and escalation to combination therapy for inadequate response (as outlined in Data collection). Monitoring was continued until delivery to ensure timely adjustment of clinical management and delivery timing. The TBA peak was defined as the highest value during serial monitoring (scheduled + unscheduled) until delivery, which served as a determinant of disease severity in the ICP. The severity of ICP was classified according to the TBA peak (µmol/L) during pregnancy as follows: extremely severe (TBA ≥ 100), moderate–high (40 ≤ TBA < 100), moderate–low (20 ≤ TBA < 40), and mild (TBA < 20). Serum ALT and AST levels were tested using the Siemens ADVIA Chemistry XPT Chemistry Analyser and its corresponding reagents, with the same testing frequency as that of dynamic TBA monitoring for the synchronous evaluation of liver function and cholestasis status.

Sample size was calculated on the basis of the logistic regression model for preterm birth prediction in twin pregnancies with ICP. Key parameters were set as follows: preterm birth rate = 75% (conservative estimation, derived from previous twin ICP studies [12, 28] reporting preterm birth rates of 60%-70% in this high-risk population), α = 0.05 (two-tailed), β = 0.10 (statistical power = 90%), number of independent variables = 7 (consistent with the final predictors in the nomogram), and a 10% attrition rate to account for potential data loss. Using PASS 15.0 software (NCSS, LLC, Kaysville, UT, USA) with the logistic regression module, the minimum required sample size was calculated as 210 cases. This study ultimately enrolled 258 patients, which exceeded the minimum requirement. The cohort was stratified randomly by preterm birth status into a training set (n = 182, 70%) and a validation set (n = 76, 30%), both of which met the criteria for model development and internal validation using a held-out test set, ensuring sufficient statistical power.

Missing data were evaluated prior to analysis. The overall percentage of missing variables was < 3%, with no missing values for core predictors (dynamic TBA parameters, UDCA usage, curative effect, AST). For minor missing data (e.g., partial maternal educational attainment records), the multiple imputation method (5 imputed datasets) was used to maintain statistical power. All imputed datasets were analysed separately, and the results were pooled for final inference.

Data collection

Data were collected and categorized into two domains: demographic and clinical. Demographic variables encompassed maternal age, educational attainment, and prepregnancy body mass index (BMI), gestational weight gain, scarred uterus, in vitro fertilization-embryo transfer (IVF), placenta previa, and complex twins (Fetal growth discordance (FGD), Selective intrauterine growth restriction (SIUGR), Twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome (TTTS), Intrauterine fetal demise (IUFD)). The modes of delivery included cesarean section (CS) and vaginal delivery (VD). Maternal outcomes included dynamic TBA monitoring parameters: TBA level at ICP onset (TBA first) and corresponding gestational age (GA at TBA first), TBA peak and its gestational age (GA at TBA peak), and TBA level before delivery (TBA end), premature rupture of membranes (PROM), placental abruption, intraoperative blood loss, fetal distress, gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM), unexplained stillbirth, hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP), and preeclampsia. ALT and AST levels at the time of the highest TBA during pregnancy were also included for comparison. The medication history included ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) monotherapy and UDCA-based combination regimens (UDCA + S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), UDCA + polyene phosphatidylcholine capsules (PPC), UDCA + SAM + PPC). All patients received standardized oral UDCA monotherapy or combination therapy regimens with fixed dosages throughout the treatment period (no dose adjustment): UDCA (250 mg/capsule), 1 capsule qid (total 1000 mg/d); SAM (500 mg/tablet), 1 tablet bid (total 1000 mg/d); and PPC (228 mg/tablet), 2 tablets bid (total 912 mg/d). UDCA therapy was initiated within 48 h of confirmed ICP diagnosis (per Chinese Guidelines 2015: TBA ≥ 10 µmol/L or intractable pruritus with abnormal liver function). Dose adjustment was not performed during monotherapy, and escalation to combination therapy (UDCA + SAM/PPC) was strictly based on 1-week dynamic TBA monitoring results and clinical symptoms: (1) no decrease or elevation of TBA compared with baseline; (2) TBA reduction rate < 30% relative to baseline; (3) persistent pruritus (VAS score ≥ 7) despite slight TBA decrease. The combination therapy maintained the same dosage and frequency of each component as monotherapy did. Complete medication adherence was verified for all included patients via hospital pharmacy dispensing records and prenatal care follow-up notes; patients with poor adherence (defined as missing UDCA doses for ≥ 3 consecutive days) were excluded from the study to ensure the reliability of treatment-related variables. The curative effect was explicitly defined as a dynamic qualitative TBA response to UDCA-based therapy for ICP, with strict reference to the baseline TBA level at the time of ICP diagnosis (initiation of treatment). Specifically, treatment curative effect reflects combined biochemical and clinical improvement: biochemically, it was deemed effective if the predelivery serum TBA level (TBA end) was numerically lower than the baseline TBA level at ICP diagnosis (TBA first) (or ≥ 20% TBA reduction for sensitivity analysis, see Supplementary Table 2); clinically, it was characterized by relief of intractable pruritus (patient-reported). Treatment was deemed ineffective if TBA end was stable/increased relative to baseline (or < 20% TBA reduction for sensitivity analysis, see Supplementary Table 2), or pruritus persisted/worsened. In the regression model, curative effect was operationalized solely by the biochemical criterion (predelivery TBA < baseline TBA at diagnosis). Pruritus relief was documented for clinical context only and was not included in model variables to avoid subjective bias. Neonatal outcomes (from medical records): birth weight, low neonatal body mass (< 2500 g), very low neonatal body mass (< 1500 g), 1/5/10-minute Apgar scores, neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) admission rate, preterm birth rates (study definition: delivery at ≥ 28 weeks and < 37 weeks of GA), and meconium-stained amniotic fluid.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R 4.5.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria, 2023) and SPSS 20.0 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, New York, USA, 2011). Continuous variables (nonnormal according to the Shapiro‒Wilk test) are expressed as medians (IQRs); categorical variables are expressed as n (%). Between-group comparisons: Mann‒Whitney U test (continuous) and chi-square/Fisher’s exact test (categorical, expected frequency < 5). A two-tailed P < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance. A preterm birth risk prediction model was developed: univariate analysis was used to screen potential predictors, followed by least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression for variable selection (minimum mean squared error (min) criterion adopted). The selected variables were included in a logistic regression model, and a nomogram was generated for visualization. The probability threshold for the nomogram was set at 0.5, the widely accepted default for binary clinical prediction models. This choice aligns with our study context: it balances sensitivity and specificity to support reliable low-risk exclusion (reducing overintervention) while minimizing false negatives and ensures comparability with existing ICP preterm birth prediction models for cross-study validation. The 0.5 threshold was used for standardization and comparability; however, it was not clinically optimized for the high preterm birth rate in this cohort. Alternative thresholds may improve sensitivity and should be explored in future studies. The data were split into training (70%) and held-out test (30%) sets via stratified random sampling (stratified by preterm birth status) to assess generalizability through internal validation using a held-out test set. Model performance was evaluated by discriminative ability (AUC with 95% CI, AUC > 0.7 acceptable), calibration (Hosmer–Lemeshow test, calibration curves, P > 0.05 for good calibration), classification accuracy (sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV), and decision curve analysis (DCA), which quantifies net benefit versus treating all or no patients. Notably, the model’s high specificity supports its value in low-risk identification, while its low sensitivity limits its utility in high-risk prediction. To verify model robustness, a sensitivity analysis was performed using an alternative curative effect definition: ≥20% TBA reduction from baseline to pre-delivery. The same modeling workflow and evaluation metrics as the primary analysis were used. Detailed results are provided in Supplementary Table 2.

No significant differences were observed between the two groups in terms of the following general clinical variables: age; BMI before pregnancy; gestational weight gain; gravidity; parity; education level; or scarred uterus, DCDA, MCDA, MCMA and IVF history.

Significant differences were found in TBA-related variables. The preterm group had earlier GA at the TBA first (31 (29, 33) vs. 32 (30, 33) weeks, P < 0.001), higher TBA peak (35.15 (24.9, 64.1) vs. 31.6 (16.2, 47.8) µmol/L, P < 0.001), earlier GA at the TBA peak (33 (30, 34) vs. 33 (32, 35) weeks, P < 0.001), higher TBA end (16.7 (11.1, 32.4) vs. 16.6 (8.5, 24.6) µmol/L, P < 0.001), and earlier GA at the TBA end (35 (34, 35) vs. 36 (36, 36) weeks, P < 0.001). In terms of the TBA peak, the distribution differed significantly (P < 0.001): the non-preterm group had more mild cases (TBA < 20 µmol/L, 60.5% vs. 26.5%), whereas the preterm group had more moderate–high cases (40 ≤ TBA < 100 µmol/L, 36.3% vs. 16.3%) and severe cases (TBA ≥ 100 µmol/L, 2.8% vs. 0%).

Liver function indicators, including ALT (P = 0.055), AST (P = 0.129), and AST/ALT (P = 0.818), were not significantly different. With respect to treatment, the preterm group had a higher UDCA usage rate (97.2% vs. 79.1%, P < 0.001), a higher UDCA plus SAM rate (21.4% vs. 9.5%, P = 0.001), and a higher rate of UDCA combined with SAM and PPC (52.1% vs. 40.5%, P = 0.032). Variable selection and model construction

We randomly allocated the dataset into a training set (70%, n = 182) and an independent test set (30%) at a 7:3 ratio, as outlined in the study flowchart (Supplementary Fig. 1). As the penalty parameter (λ) increased in the LASSO regression (Fig. 1A), the coefficients of the included variables progressively decreased toward zero. The optimal λ value (λ = 0.017) was determined using 10-fold cross-validation. At this optimal λ, the LASSO model selected seven variables with nonzero coefficients (Fig. 1A), among which five are innovative predictors exclusive to our twin ICP cohort: dynamic TBA parameters (GA at the TBA peak, TBA group at the peak, TBA end), UDCA usage (binary variable: 1 = received UDCA, 0 = did not receive UDCA), and curative effect (defined as predelivery TBA (TBA end) < TBA level at ICP diagnosis (TBA first); 1 = effective, 0 = ineffective) and two conventional variables (GA at diagnosis, AST). Curative effect in the model was defined biochemically without subjective symptom input. The initial results of the analysis (Table 4) revealed that GA at diagnosis (OR = 0.710, P < 0.001), UDCA usage (OR = 0.108, P < 0.001), GA at the TBA peak (OR = 0.741, P = 0.001), TBA peak (OR = 2.474, P < 0.001), AST (OR = 1.004, P = 0.045), curative effect (OR = 0.288, P = 0.002), and TBA at end (OR = 1.057, P = 0.003) were significantly associated with preterm birth among twin-ICP pregnancies. The low OR value of UDCA usage reflects the role of standardized clinical management, and UDCA use serves as a surrogate marker of clinical management rather than a direct protective factor for preterm birth. The cross-validated binomial deviance across a range of λ values is shown in Fig. 1B. The vertical dashed line indicates the optimal λ, chosen at the point of minimum deviance to balance model fit and complexity.

Model establishment and validation

A twin-specific nomogram was constructed using R Studio (Fig. 2), integrating seven key predictors, including three innovative dimensions—dynamic TBA parameters (GA at the TBA peak, TBA group at the peak, TBA end), UDCA usage (binary variable: 1 = received UDCA, 0 = did not receive UDCA), and curative effect (binary variable: 1 = effective (TBA end < TBA first), 0 = ineffective (TBA end ≥ TBA first))—all of which were tailored to the unique risk profile of twin pregnancies with ICP. This tool converts the complex regression equation into an intuitive scoring system, enabling clinicians to quickly calculate the total score and estimate the personalized risk of preterm birth for individual patients at the bedside.

The discriminative ability of the nomogram was evaluated using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC). In the training set, the model achieved an AUC of 0.812 (0.721–0.903), demonstrating robust discriminative power and high accuracy in predicting preterm birth. Notably, the model predicts a composite preterm birth outcome modified by both biological risk and clinical intervention rather than purely spontaneous preterm birth. The generalizability of the nomogram to unseen data was good, as evidenced by an AUC of 0.740 (95% CI: 0.590–0.889) in the independent test set via internal validation using a held-out test set, indicating consistent predictive performance. The model showed high specificity (93.8% in the test set), with an excellent ability to rule out non-preterm cases by accurately identifying most patients without preterm birth risk. From the perspective of classification accuracy, at the 0.5 threshold (model performance across alternative probability thresholds in Supplementary Table 1), the negative predictive value (NPV) was high (85.7% (75.7%–92.1%)), confirming the high reliability of “non-preterm” predictions. However, the sensitivity (test set: 16.7% (4.7%–44.8%)) and positive predictive value (PPV: 33.3% (9.7%–70%)) were relatively low, suggesting that the current model is more suitable for risk exclusion (i.e., confirming low-risk patients). These results collectively validate the nomogram as a reliable tool for preterm birth low-risk identification in ICP twin pregnancies because of its good discrimination and calibration (AUC = 0.812 in the training set and 0.740 in the test set via internal validation using a held-out test set). Its extremely low sensitivity (16.7% in the test set) means it is not suitable for high-risk preterm birth screening or prediction, and its core clinical value is limited to excluding low-risk patients to reduce overintervention (Fig. 3A, B).

A sensitivity analysis using ≥ 20% TBA reduction as the curative effect definition confirmed model robustness: the AUC (training set: 0.798; test set: 0.725) and key clinical metrics (specificity > 95%, NPV > 85%) remained stable compared to the primary definition (Supplementary Table 2). Minor decreases in sensitivity and PPV were attributed to the stricter threshold.

Calibration curves were plotted to visually assess the agreement between the predicted probabilities and observed event rates across risk deciles (Fig. 3C, D). The Hosmer–Lemeshow test yielded nonsignificant results in both the training set (P = 0.1527) and the test set (P = 0.6991), indicating good calibration of the model with low prediction error. These findings confirm the accuracy and stability of the model in predicting preterm birth risk with ICP. In the training set, the model exhibited a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.215, a mean squared error (MSE) of 0.106, and a 90th percentile absolute error of 0.625, indicating a relatively low level of prediction error. The model’s accuracy and stability on unseen data were collectively confirmed in the test set, as indicated by an MAE of 0.261, an MSE of 0.124, and a 90th percentile absolute error of 0.602. These findings underscore the value of the nomogram as an accurate and generalizable tool for preterm birth risk assessment in the ICP population.

Decision curve analysis (DCA) demonstrated that the model provided significant net benefits within a high-risk threshold range of 0–0.833 (Fig. 3E, F). The substantial overlap between the calibration curves of the training and test sets within this range attests to the model’s robustness and consistency across different datasets. This robustness supports the model’s applicability primarily for low-risk identification, while its low sensitivity limits its use for identifying high-risk patients. Beyond a risk threshold of 0.833, the net benefit curve for the test set plateaued, indicating that the model’s net benefit stabilized and approached that of a random strategy.

Discussion

ICP is a pregnancy-specific liver disorder that poses a significant threat to maternal and neonatal health, with preterm birth being one of its most devastating complications [1, 8]. The risk of preterm birth is 15 times greater in women with ICP than in those without this condition [12, 13], and this risk is further exacerbated in twin pregnancies [14]. Our study focused exclusively on twin pregnancies with ICP—an understudied high-risk subgroup—addressing the critical gap of the lack of tailored predictive tools for this population, aiming to develop a novel nomogram integrating clinical, biochemical, and therapeutic variables for preterm birth risk prediction.

To our knowledge, this represents one of the largest modelling cohorts specifically dedicated to developing nomograms for twin ICP. Our study included 258 twin pregnancies with ICP, revealing an extremely high preterm birth rate of 83.3%, which is substantially higher than the preterm birth rates reported in singleton ICP pregnancies (16.5%-30.5% based on TBA levels) [12] and even exceeds the overall preterm birth rate of approximately 60% in twin pregnancies [28]. This finding highlights the synergistic risk of twin gestation and ICP in promoting preterm birth, which is consistent with previous observations that twin pregnancies are associated with increased incidences of pregnancy complications [15] and that ICP further amplifies adverse perinatal outcomes.

Notably, the exceptionally high preterm birth rate (83.3%) in our cohort is largely attributed to the high prevalence of iatrogenic preterm birth delivery, which accounted for ~ 68.4% (147/215) of all preterm birth cases. Iatrogenic preterm birth was initiated primarily by clinicians on the basis of disease severity assessment: specifically, cases with a TBA peak ≥ 40 µmol/L (moderate-high to extremely severe ICP), persistent maternal pruritus unrelieved by treatment, or inadequate TBA reduction (treatment ineffective) were closely monitored, and delivery was proactively scheduled between 34 and 36 weeks of gestation to minimize the fetal risk of bile acid-induced toxicity. Spontaneous preterm birth (including preterm birth labor and PPROM) accounted for ~ 31.6% (68/215) of the preterm birth cases.

This high rate of clinician-initiated preterm birth reflects the current clinical management paradigm for twin pregnancies with ICP, where the potential fetal risks of prolonged exposure to elevated bile acids outweigh the risks of preterm birth at ≥ 34 weeks (with mature fetal lung function). However, this also introduces potential confounding by indication: bile acid levels and treatment response—key predictors in our model—directly influence clinical decisions on delivery timing. This means the model predicts a composite preterm birth outcome modified by both biological risk and clinical intervention rather than purely spontaneous preterm birth, which may partially explain the model’s extremely low sensitivity (16.7%) in identifying high-risk patients (as most high-severity cases were already scheduled for preterm birth delivery). Thus, the model is not suitable for high-risk preterm birth prediction and only serves for low-risk identification. We acknowledge that this confounding factor is a limitation of the current study, as it may restrict the generalizability of the model to settings with more conservative management strategies. Future prospective studies could stratify outcomes by preterm birth type (spontaneous vs. iatrogenic) to optimize the model’s ability to predict spontaneous preterm birth, an unmet clinical need.

Among the demographic and clinical variables analysed, no significant differences were observed between the preterm birth and non-preterm birth groups in terms of maternal age, prepregnancy BMI, or IVF history. However, dynamic TBA parameters were identified as core independent predictors. Compared with the single static TBA peak value used in previous studies [20–22], dynamic TBA indices (earlier GA at TBA first/peak, higher TBA peak/end levels) more comprehensively reflect the duration of fetal exposure to toxic bile acids, which explains their stronger predictive value in twin pregnancies with ICP. TBA levels in pregnant women with ICP are prone to fluctuations, and a single measurement cannot fully represent the actual disease severity or treatment response. The distribution of ICP severity based on the TBA peak also differed significantly between the two groups: the non-preterm birth group had a higher proportion of mild cases (60.5% vs. 26.5%), whereas the preterm birth group had more moderate–high and severe cases (36.3% vs. 16.3% and 2.8% vs. 0%, respectively). These results confirm the dose‒response relationship between TBA levels and preterm birth risk in singleton pregnancies [30] and extend these findings to the twin ICP population.

By incorporating the curative effect into the model, we captured the dynamic balance between ICP disease progression and clinical treatment intervention—a key detail not reflected in previous models relying only on single-point TBA measurements [27]. This finding is consistent with abundant evidence that TBA is a core risk factor for preterm birth among those with ICP [29, 30]. Mechanistically, elevated maternal bile acids can cross the placental barrier, where they can have neurotoxic and cytotoxic effects on the fetus [7]; they can also induce placental vascular constriction, leading to insufficient placental perfusion and fetal chronic hypoxia [26], and can activate uterine oxytocin receptors to enhance uterine contractility [31, 32]. These multiple pathological mechanisms contribute to the occurrence of preterm birth in twin pregnancies with ICP.

Notably, we are the first to integrate UDCA medication history (monotherapy/combination) and therapeutic effect (TBA response) into a twin ICP preterm birth model—these variables serve as dynamic surrogates for disease control, complementing static baseline indicators and making the model more clinically actionable. The preterm birth group had a higher UDCA usage rate (97.2% vs. 79.1%, P < 0.001), a higher rate of UDCA combined with SAM (21.4% vs. 9.5%), and a higher rate of UDCA combined with SAM and PPC (52.1% vs. 40.5%) but a significantly lower treatment efficacy rate (52.1% vs. 79.1%). These findings highlight a critical clinical dilemma: Despite the widespread use of UDCA as a first-line therapy for ICP [16], its efficacy in improving pregnancy outcomes remains controversial, especially in twin pregnancies. Previous meta-analyses and randomized controlled trials have failed to demonstrate consistent benefits of UDCA monotherapy or combination regimens in reducing preterm birth or other adverse outcomes [33, 34], which is supported by our observation that more than half of the patients showed no significant TBA reduction after treatment. The inclusion of treatment efficacy in our nomogram addresses a key gap in existing predictive models, as treatment response serves as a dynamic surrogate for disease control and fetal risk, complementing static baseline indicators such as TBA peak.

Three key innovations distinguish our study: (1) exclusive twin pregnancy cohort, addressing the lack of subgroup-specific tools; (2) integration of dynamic TBA monitoring (vs. static values in prior studies [20–22]); and (3) inclusion of full-course therapeutic data (medication history + TBA response), bridging diagnosis-intervention-outcome. This triple innovation enhances the relevance of the model to clinical practice in twin ICP management.

Liver function indicators, particularly AST (OR = 1.004; P = 0.045), emerged as independent risk factors according to the LASSO model, suggesting that subtle liver function abnormalities may contribute to preterm birth risk beyond bile acid accumulation. This finding aligns with the pathological mechanism of ICP, where hepatocellular damage and cholestasis coexist, and liver enzyme abnormalities reflect the severity of hepatic impairment [1].

The nomogram integrating seven key factors (GA at diagnosis, UDCA usage, GA at the TBA peak, TBA group at the peak, AST, curative effect, and TBA end) demonstrated robust performance for low-risk identification. The model demonstrated good discriminatory performance, with AUCs of 0.812 (95% CI: 0.721–0.903) in the training set and 0.740 (95% CI: 0.590–0.889) in the test set via internal validation using a held-out test set, indicating good discriminative ability and generalizability. The model exhibited high specificity (93.8%) and negative predictive value (85.7%) in the test set. This model is intended primarily as a low-risk exclusion tool to support clinical monitoring strategies, rather than to guide high-risk prediction or delivery decisions. Its extremely low sensitivity (16.7%) means it has no clinical utility for high-risk preterm birth prediction or screening. Calibration curves and Hosmer–Lemeshow tests confirmed good agreement between the predicted and actual outcomes, and DCA demonstrated significant net benefits within a clinically relevant risk threshold (0–0.833). This model is intended primarily as a low-risk exclusion tool to support clinical monitoring strategies, rather than to guide high-risk prediction or delivery decisions. It enables clinicians to identify low-risk patients and optimize individualized monitoring, but its low sensitivity precludes use for high-risk stratification or delivery decision-making. While its high specificity and NPV support the reliable identification of low-risk patients, its role in guiding timely interventions (e.g., fetal lung maturation with dexamethasone) and improving maternal–neonatal outcomes requires further prospective and external validation because of its limited sensitivity.

To expand the clinical applicability of the nomogram, we explored alternative probability thresholds (0.3, 0.4, 0.6, and 0.7) in the test set (Supplementary Table 1). The default threshold of 0.5 remained the optimal choice for routine clinical practice, balancing sensitivity and specificity.

The use of a qualitative definition (any reduction in TBA) to assess treatment response is clinically justified in the context of ICP management. Unlike chronic liver diseases, where a fixed percentage reduction (e.g., 30–50%) is used to define significant biochemical improvement, the primary goal of ICP treatment is to minimize fetal exposure to toxic bile acids as rapidly as possible to prevent adverse perinatal outcomes. Even a modest reduction in maternal TBA levels may reduce transplacental passage and alleviate fetal cholestasis, which is a critical clinical endpoint in obstetric practice. Therefore, we adopted this definition to capture any meaningful therapeutic benefit that could influence clinical decision-making regarding delivery timing.

A sensitivity analysis using an alternative curative effect definition (≥ 20% TBA reduction) confirmed the model’s robustness (Supplementary Table 2)—core performance metrics (AUC: 0.798 in training set, 0.725 in test set; specificity > 95%; NPV > 85%) remained stable compared to the primary definition (any TBA reduction), verifying that curative effect is a reliable predictor regardless of quantitative or qualitative operationalization.

Our findings are consistent with several key observations in the literature but also offer unique insights. First, the critical role of TBA levels in preterm birth risk has been well established in singleton ICP pregnancies [3, 30], and our study confirms this in twin pregnancies, further showing that TBA dynamics (e.g., GA at the TBA peak and TBA end) and treatment response are additional important predictors. This extends beyond previous studies that focused primarily on static TBA levels [20–22]. Second, the high preterm birth rate in twin pregnancies with ICP aligns with guidelines suggesting that even mild ICP in twin pregnancies should be managed as severe cases [1, 8–10], given the increased risk of adverse outcomes. Our study provides empirical support for this approach, as mild ICP cases in our cohort still had a substantial risk of preterm birth.

With respect to treatment, the limited efficacy of UDCA in twin pregnancies with ICP observed in our study is consistent with recent controversial findings [33, 34]. A meta-analysis of 13 randomized controlled trials revealed no significant improvement in pruritus or perinatal outcomes with UDCA, SAM, or combination therapy [33], and a UK multicenter trial questioned the routine use of UDCA for ICP [34]. Our data further show that compared with UDCA monotherapy, combination therapy (UDCA + SAM/PPC) does not yield superior outcomes in twin pregnancies, highlighting the need for alternative therapeutic strategies or more aggressive monitoring in this high-risk group.

Notably, UDCA is the first-line therapy for ICP, and its near-universal use in the preterm group (92.1% of preterm birth cases received UDCA) reflects real-world clinical practice. UDCA use serves as a surrogate marker of standardized clinical management—including prompt disease recognition, regular dynamic TBA monitoring, and timely adjustment of treatment regimens—which collectively contributes to optimized perinatal outcomes. The observed association between UDCA usage and a lower likelihood of preterm birth is not indicative of a causal protective effect, and UDCA should not be interpreted as a protective factor for preterm birth in any context. We emphasize that UDCA usage in the model is a clinical management surrogate rather than a direct causal factor.

The standardized treatment intensity (fixed UDCA dosage of 1000 mg/d, no dose adjustment, and unified escalation criteria for combination therapy) in this study minimized variability in treatment-related predictors (UDCA usage and curative effect), ensuring the observed association between curative effect and preterm birth risk truly reflected TBA response rather than dosage differences. However, the fixed standard UDCA dosage may also explain the limited treatment efficacy (52.1% of preterm birth patients had ineffective treatment) in our cohort: higher UDCA dosages (e.g., 1500 mg/d) may improve TBA reduction rates in severe twin ICP cases, which could further modify the predictive value of the curative effect for preterm birth risk. In addition, treatment intensity (e.g., the timing of escalation to combination therapy) may impact pregnancy outcomes: early escalation (based on 1-week monitoring) may accelerate TBA reduction and reduce the risk of clinician-initiated preterm birth delivery, whereas delayed escalation may prolong fetal exposure to toxic bile acids and increase preterm birth risk. These potential effects of treatment intensity on model predictors and outcomes warrant further investigation in future prospective interventional studies with variable UDCA dosages and individualized escalation strategies.

The development of a nomogram for preterm birth prediction in twin pregnancies with ICP fills a critical gap. Existing nomograms for ICP-related preterm birth either focus on singleton pregnancies or lack integration of therapeutic variables [26, 27]. Our inclusion of medication history and treatment efficacy as core predictors is a novel feature, as these variables directly reflect disease management effectiveness and dynamic progression, which are particularly relevant in twin pregnancies where treatment decisions are more complex. Additionally, our study addresses the controversy over whether mild ICP in twin pregnancies should be managed as a severe case [16, 17], providing evidence that even mild cases have a high preterm birth risk and supporting the current domestic guideline recommendations [1, 8–10].

Our study addressed the potential bias associated with different chorionicity types in twin pregnancies. First, we supplemented the chorionicity data and reported that DCDA accounted for 80.6% of the cohort and that MCDA accounted for 19.4%. Second, preterm birth etiology stratification revealed that ICP-related preterm birth (85.1%) was far more common than chorionicity-related preterm birth (10.2%), suggesting that ICP is the dominant factor for preterm birth in this population. However, we acknowledge that MCDA twins have higher rates of specific complications that may lead to iatrogenic preterm birth [22, 28], which could introduce mild confounding.

In the clinical management of twin ICP pregnancies, acute fatty liver of pregnancy (AFLP) is a critical differential diagnosis [35]—distinguished by hypoglycemia, hyperammonemia, and absent pruritus versus the hallmark pruritus and TBA elevation of the ICP—to avoid mismanagement. Routine monitoring should include fibrin monomer (FM) and D-dimer (DD) levels for early detection of bile acid-induced coagulopathy [36], along with ultrasound surveillance (fetal growth, amniotic fluid, and umbilical artery Doppler) to assess fetal well-being and preterm birth risk [37]. These tools synergize with our nomogram, forming a multimodal approach to address the complex maternal–fetal risks in this high-risk population.

Despite its strengths, our study has several limitations. First, the single-center, retrospective design of this study is subject to inherent selection bias and spectrum bias, which significantly impact the definition and interpretation of the primary outcome (preterm birth). Our cohort had an extremely high preterm birth rate (83.3%), of which ~ 68.4% was clinician-initiated preterm birth delivery, which was proactively scheduled by clinicians on the basis of disease severity (TBA peak ≥ 40 µmol/L), persistent pruritus, or ineffective treatment to minimize fetal bile acid toxicity. This means the primary outcome (preterm birth) is a composite endpoint modified by both biological risk and clinical intervention rather than a purely spontaneous outcome, which further limits the model’s utility for high-risk spontaneous preterm birth prediction. This clinician-initiated preterm birth delivery redefined the natural course of twin ICP pregnancies and introduced a critical confounding by indication: the key model predictors (TBA levels, curative effect) directly guided clinical decisions on delivery timing, leading to an overrepresentation of preterm birth outcomes in high-severity cases. As a result, the primary outcome (preterm birth) in this study is not a purely “spontaneous” outcome but a clinically modified endpoint, which limits the ability of the model to predict spontaneous preterm birth and requires careful interpretation of the model’s performance in real-world clinical settings where management strategies may be more conservative. Additionally, because clinical decisions (e.g., timing of delivery) were influenced by TBA levels and treatment response, some predictors in the model may be indirectly associated with the outcome through clinician behavior, introducing potential indication bias and circularity between predictors and the composite endpoint. Notably, the high rate of clinician-initiated preterm birth (~ 68.4%) introduces confounding by indication, as TBA levels and treatment response (key model predictors) directly guide delivery timing, limiting the model’s generalizability to settings with more conservative management. This clinical management-driven outcome modification further limits the model’s generalizability to settings with more conservative strategies. Second, while we included key therapeutic variables, we did not assess the dosage or duration of UDCA or combination therapy, which may influence treatment efficacy and preterm birth risk. Further studies should explore the impact of treatment regimens in more detail. Despite incorporating medication history, individual drug metabolism gene variations may influence different responses to UDCA, warranting future investigation. Third, this study has spectrum bias because of its single tertiary referral center design, resulting in an extremely high preterm birth rate (83.3%) in the cohort—much higher than that of general twin pregnancies and singleton ICP pregnancies. This is because our center mainly enrolls patients with severe ICP and complex twin pregnancies, leading to insufficient variation in preterm birth risk in the study population, which may be the main reason for the low sensitivity (16.7%) of the model in the validation set. Clinically, the core advantage of the model is its high specificity (93.8%) and negative predictive value (85.7%), which can reliably identify low-risk patients and avoid overmonitoring (e.g., weekly fetal nonstress tests) and premature hospital admission for this subgroup. For high-risk screening, follow-up studies can optimize the model by lowering the prediction threshold or integrating additional biomarkers (e.g., PLGF [38]) and clinical variables (e.g., MCDA twin status) to balance sensitivity and specificity. Future prospective multicenter studies covering primary and secondary hospitals are needed to validate the generalizability of the model, and incorporating placental function markers, genetic factors (e.g., MDR3 gene mutations) [39] or oxidative stress biomarkers may further improve the model’s predictive performance. Fourth, we did not explore the interaction between variables (e.g., TBA levels and twin chorionicity), which may influence preterm birth risk. Previous studies have shown that MCDA twins have higher rates of complications such as preeclampsia and twin-specific syndromes [8, 10], and future research should investigate whether chorionicity affects the effect of TBA levels or treatment response on preterm birth risk. Although we stratified preterm birth etiology by chorionicity, compared with non–ICP twins, MCDA/MCMA twins may have higher rates of pregnancy termination because of non-ICP complications (e.g., TTTS, SIUGR), which could introduce confounding bias. Future studies could include larger samples of MCDA/MCMA twins to further reduce this bias. An additional limitation is the lack of maternal and fetal hemodynamic data. As suggested, abdominal compartmentalization and altered venous return may contribute to ICP pathogenesis and preterm birth risk, but these parameters were not measured in our cohort. Future studies could integrate hemodynamic markers to improve the model’s predictive power and explore the mechanistic links between ICP, hemodynamics, and preterm birth. A critical limitation is the lack of formal external validation of the nomogram. The model was only validated using an internal independent test set (30% of the cohort) stratified by preterm birth status via internal validation using a held-out test set, and no formal external validation was performed in other clinical centers (e.g., primary/secondary hospitals), different geographical regions, or cohorts with different preterm birth risk profiles (e.g., lower rates of clinician-initiated preterm birth). External validation is a critical step for clinical translation, as it verifies generalizability across diverse populations—its absence means the model’s performance (e.g., AUC, specificity) may not be replicable in other settings, a key gap to address in future research.

Stratification of preterm birth by etiology in our cohort revealed a dominant proportion of iatrogenic preterm birth (~ 68.4%) versus spontaneous preterm birth (~ 31.6%), which shapes the model’s performance and applicability for these two subtypes and poses statistical limitations for separate quantitative evaluation. The core predictors of our nomogram (the TBA peak, dynamic TBA changes, UDCA treatment efficacy, etc.) are the key clinical indicators guiding clinicians to proactively schedule iatrogenic preterm birth delivery for twin ICP pregnancies, aiming to minimize fetal exposure to toxic bile acids. This intrinsic link indicates that the model is highly consistent with the clinical management logic of iatrogenic preterm birth, and its high specificity (93.8%) and negative predictive value (85.7%) ensure reliable risk assessment for patients at high risk of clinician-initiated delivery. With respect to spontaneous preterm birth, the model’s limited predictive power for high-risk cases is attributable to two main factors: the small sample size of spontaneous preterm birth in the cohort, leading to insufficient statistical power, and the absence of spontaneous preterm-specific variables (e.g., cervical length, fetal fibronectin, and uterine contractility markers) in the current model—these factors are independent of ICP-related cholestasis but are critical for predicting spontaneous preterm birth. Notably, the model’s core clinical value (reliable low-risk exclusion) remains unchanged for both preterm subtypes: it can effectively identify low-risk twin ICP patients who are unlikely to experience either spontaneous or iatrogenic preterm birth, thus providing a unified risk stratification tool for clinical practice to reduce overintervention. Future optimization of the model for spontaneous preterm birth prediction in twin ICP pregnancies requires multicenter prospective studies with larger and balanced sample sizes of spontaneous and iatrogenic preterm birth, incorporating ICP-unrelated spontaneous preterm birth risk factors and stratification by twin chorionicity (e.g., monochorionic diamniotic twins with higher spontaneous preterm birth risk) to improve the model’s subtype-specific predictive performance.

In addition, the definition of curative effect in this study was based on the Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Intrahepatic Cholestasis of Pregnancy in China (2015 Edition), which only considered the decrease in the predelivery TBA compared with the diagnostic baseline as the judgment standard, without considering the percentage of TBA reduction or the time window of treatment response. Although a sensitivity analysis using a ≥ 20% TBA reduction threshold confirmed the robustness of curative effect as a predictor, this relatively simple binary definition lacks a refined and quantifiable clinically meaningful threshold, which may limit further refinement of the model’s ability to predict treatment response-related preterm risk. Subsequent studies can optimize the efficacy evaluation system by integrating multidimensional indicators such as TBA reduction percentage, short-term treatment response and clinical symptom improvement. Future optimization of the model for spontaneous preterm birth prediction in twin ICP pregnancies requires multicenter prospective studies with larger and balanced sample sizes of spontaneous and iatrogenic preterm birth, incorporating ICP-unrelated spontaneous preterm birth risk factors and stratification by twin chorionicity (e.g., monochorionic diamniotic twins with higher spontaneous preterm birth risk) to improve the model’s subtype-specific predictive performance.

To improve the prediction and prevention of severe obstetrical syndromes (e.g., preterm birth, fetal distress) in twin pregnancies with ICP, three key strategies are proposed: Optimize predictive models: Integrate additional biomarkers (e.g., PLGF, sFlt-1 for preeclampsia screening) and hemodynamic parameters to increase sensitivity for high-risk detection; Standardize clinical management: Implement routine dynamic TBA monitoring (1-week intervals) and timely UDCA-based treatment, with combination therapy for inadequate responders; and Stratify intervention by risk: Use tools such as our nomogram to individualize monitoring intensity (e.g., reduced frequency of fetal nonstress tests for low-risk patients, early steroid administration for high-risk cases) and delivery timing (avoiding unnecessary preterm birth induction while preventing prolonged high bile acid exposure). Additionally, prospective multicenter studies are needed to validate these strategies and explore the mechanistic role of hemodynamics in adverse ICP-related outcomes.

Future directions should also focus on long-term outcomes. While ICP typically resolves postpartum [1], recent studies suggest that a history of ICP confers an increased lifetime risk for certain chronic diseases, notably liver diseases (including cholangiocarcinoma and cirrhosis) and cardiovascular diseases [40–42]. Postpartum follow-up and risk assessment for these long-term complications are therefore important. Additionally, exploring the mechanism underlying the limited efficacy of UDCA in twin pregnancies with ICP may lead to the development of more effective therapeutic strategies.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study develops and validates an early twin-specific nomogram for preterm birth prediction in ICP, integrating three core innovative elements: dynamic TBA monitoring parameters, UDCA-based medication history, and posttreatment TBA response. Key findings show that UDCA usage (as a surrogate marker of standardized clinical management, not a direct protective factor), posttreatment TBA reduction, later GA at the TBA peak, and late-onset ICP (higher GA at diagnosis) are protective against preterm birth, whereas elevated predelivery TBA levels, severe TBA peak, and increased AST are risk factors. Notably, the model predicts a composite preterm birth outcome modified by both biological risk and clinical intervention, and its extremely low sensitivity limits its utility for high-risk preterm birth prediction. This model is intended primarily as a low-risk exclusion tool to support clinical monitoring strategies, rather than to guide high-risk prediction or delivery decisions. It provides a user-friendly approach to identify low-risk patients and reduce unnecessary intervention in twin pregnancies with ICP. Given its extremely low sensitivity, the model has no clinical value for high-risk preterm birth prediction and is only valuable for excluding low-risk patients in this specific population and reducing overintervention, but it is not yet suitable for guiding clinical decision-making (e.g., delivery timing) across diverse settings, and its generalizability requires further large-scale multicenter prospective and formal external validation.

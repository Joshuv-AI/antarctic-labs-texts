Tricuspid regurgitation (TR) is a common valvular pathology with moderate or severe TR affecting approximately 1.6 million individuals in the United States, representing an age- and sex-adjusted prevalence of 0.55%.1 Surgical repair with annuloplasty (ring or band) is an option for severe TR in select populations, particularly when performed concomitantly with left-sided valve surgery. Surgical ring/band failure is a documented problem that can arise after surgical treatment. The proportion of patients with recurrent moderate or severe TR at 5 years is approximately 10% for ring annuloplasty, compared with 20% to 35% for suture repair.2 Factors associated with failed repair include marked leaflet tethering (coaptation height >1 cm, tenting volume >2.3 mL), atrial fibrillation, greater TR severity, higher pulmonary artery pressures, larger annular size, and right ventricular dysfunction.2

Redo surgery carries high risk because of advanced age, burden of comorbidities, and the nature of the redo procedure. Isolated tricuspid valve surgery carries in-hospital mortality rates of 8% to 10%, with higher rates for reoperations.3

Transcatheter valve-in-ring (TVIR) is a viable alternative to redo surgery, with procedural success rates of 98.6% and encouraging midterm outcomes.4

Paravalvular leak (PVL) is a common challenge in TVIR procedures, especially with incomplete rings. PVL occurs in approximately 75% of TVIR cases.4,5 Most surgical tricuspid rings are incomplete (designed to preserve the conduction system) and conforming a cylindrical transcatheter valve to a rigid oval ring creates challenges for complete sealing, making PVL more common after TVIR than after valve-in-valve procedures.

Case Summary and 30-day Outcomes

A 68-year-old woman with a history of congenital tricuspid valve dysplasia, previously managed with surgical repair using a 36-mm Medtronic 3D Contour annuloplasty ring (Figure 1), and a history of leadless pacemaker implantation (Micra), presented with recurrent hospitalizations for decompensated heart failure. Transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) demonstrated torrential TR with near-absent leaflet coaptation and severe right ventricular (RV) systolic dysfunction (Video 1). Despite optimization of guideline-directed medical therapy, the patient continued to experience progressive functional decline and refractory volume overload. The decision was made to perform transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR) with a novel TTVR platform known as TOPAZ (TRiCares). Given the severity of RV dysfunction, the patient was admitted 48 hours before the procedure for hemodynamic optimization with intravenous furosemide and dobutamine. The procedure was performed under general anesthesia with TEE guidance. Bilateral common femoral venous access was obtained. Using a steerable guiding catheter (Agilis; Abbott), a Safari (Boston Scientific) guidewire was advanced to the RV apex. Optimal delivery system trajectory and positioning were confirmed by TEE. Under fluoroscopic guidance in the right anterior oblique coplanar projection, the inner stent markers were aligned at the level of the annuloplasty ring. Following confirmation of appropriate device orientation and coaxial alignment, the capsule was retracted and the transcatheter tricuspid valve was deployed within the annuloplasty ring. Postdeployment TEE demonstrated an excellent procedural result (Video 2), with only trivial septal PVL and a mean transvalvular gradient of 3 to 5 mm Hg. In view of the severely reduced RV function, inotropic support with dobutamine was continued in the perioperative period. The patient was discharged on postoperative day 6.

At 30-day follow-up, the patient reported significant symptomatic improvement, with functional status consistent with NYHA functional class II. Echocardiography demonstrated a well-seated valve (Video 3) with mild PVL and a mean transvalvular gradient of 4 mm Hg. Novelty of the Submission

This case represents the first-in-human transcatheter valve-in-ring/band implantation using a novel TTVR platform. The TOPAZ valve features a dual-stent architecture comprising a conformable outer frame and a more rigid inner stent. The outer frame is designed to minimize the risk of conduction disturbances and to limit adverse effects on RV function and incorporates atraumatic anchoring elements for secure device fixation. In the case of incomplete ring, the comfortable outer stent of the system allowed the prosthesis to protrude into the open portion of the tricuspid ring, preventing paravalvular leak. The inner stent houses a 28-mm valve constructed with bovine pericardial leaflets. It remained circular without deformity (Figure 3), preserving valve function. The system is available in 2 sizes—medium (45 mm) and large (55 mm)—accommodating annular perimeters of 110 to 141 mm and 141 to 172 mm, respectively.

Future Directions

Novel TTVR devices may enable treatment of large, incomplete bands, which remains an unmet clinical need at present. Optimal device selection must be guided by patient-specific pathology and anatomy. This and other novel TTVR devices are expected to enter clinical trial testing in 2026.

Conclusions

This case demonstrates the feasibility of TTVR using the TOPAZ valve in the setting of torrential TR and a large, incomplete surgical annuloplasty ring. The dual-stent architecture—featuring a conformable outer frame designed to accommodate annular geometry while minimizing adverse RV interactions—may extend the reach of TTVR to patients with severe RV dysfunction who are otherwise poor candidates for a redo surgery. Furthermore, the conformable outer frame may reduce the risk of paravalvular leak and conduction disturbances.

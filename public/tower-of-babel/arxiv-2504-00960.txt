Abstract
. For every countable infinite group that admits ℤ \mathbb{Z} as a homomorphic image, we show that for each m ∈ ℕ m\in{\mathbb{N}} , there exists a minimal action whose topological sequence entropy is log ⁡ ( m ) \log(m) . Furthermore, for every countable infinite group G G that contains a finite index normal subgroup G ′ G^{\prime} isomorphic to ℤ r {\mathbb{Z}}^{r} , for every m ∈ ℕ m\in{\mathbb{N}} , we found a free minimal action with topological sequence entropy log ⁡ ( n ) \log(n) , where m ≤ n ≤ m 2 r [ G : G ′ ] m\leq n\leq m^{2^{r}[G:G^{\prime}]} . If, instead, G G is assumed to be an infinite countable group that is a direct sum of finite groups, the previous result holds with the topological sequence entropy being equal to log ⁡ ( m ) \log(m) . In each of the above cases, we also show that the aforementioned minimal actions admit non-trivial independence tuples of size n n but do not admit non-trivial independence tuples of size n + 1 n+1 for some n ≥ m n\geq m .



1.
Introduction

Topological entropy is a measure of the complexity or unpredictability of a dynamical system.
It serves as an invariant that helps to identify when two dynamical systems are not conjugate.
Sequence entropy, a finer notion of entropy, was introduced by Kushnirenko (
[
24
]
) for measure preserving systems and by Goodman (
[
14
]
) for (topological) dynamical systems and is helpful to distinguish systems of zero entropy.
The sequence entropy is similar to the classical entropy, the difference is that in the refinement for the sequence entropy is made using sequences of the acting group.
It was noticed in
[
19
]
, that the topological sequence entropy of a system can only take values of the form
\log(n)
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
.

According to classical topological entropy, a question arise:
Given a non-negative real number, can we construct a minimal dynamical system, over an amenable countable group, with the condition that the entropy of this system is the real number previously considered?
Downarowicz, in
[
8
]
, has answered this question in an affirmative way for
\mathbb{Z}
-actions; this result was extended for amenable group actions in
[
23
,
25
]
.
To answer this question, a particular family of dynamical systems, known as
Toeplitz subshifts
, was used.
The previous question can be refined by imposing additional conditions on the dynamical system, such as freeness or unique ergodicity, see
[
9
]
and
[
20
]
for a treatment of this question for the uniquely ergodic condition in
\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
actions.

In this paper, we are interested in constructing minimal dynamical systems that have zero topological entropy but can achieve arbitrarily large, yet finite, topological sequence entropy.
Moreover, for these examples we can guarantee that there exists a natural number for which the
{\rm IT}
-tuples of the system are trivial.
These results are related to the recent article
[
26
]
, as one of their main results states that for every minimal dynamical system
(X,G)
, with
G
abelian, there exists
K\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
with the topological sequence entropy of the system greater or equal than
\log(K)
such that the system is a regular
K
-to-one extension of its maximal equicontinuous factor
(X_{\rm eq},G)
.
That is, the set
\{x\in X_{\rm eq}:|\pi^{-1}(x)|=K\}
has full measure under the unique invariant measure of
(X_{\rm eq},G)
, where
\pi:X\to X_{\rm eq}
is the factor map associated to the maximal equicontinuous factor.
Therefore, our results can be read as a realization problem for the topological sequence entropy.

The first result of this document reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a countable group such that
\mathbb{Z}
is a homomorphic image of
G
.
For every
m\geq 1
, there exists a minimal subshift
X\subseteq\{0,\dots,m-1\}^{G}
with topological sequence entropy equal to
\log(m)
.

In the previous theorem, if
G=\mathbb{Z}
, then the minimal subshift is also free, i.e., every element in this subshift has trivial stabilizer.
However, when
G\neq\mathbb{Z}
, this is no longer true.
When we regard about free dynamical systems for the question related to topological sequence entropy, we obtain the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be an infinite countable group that contains a finite index normal subgroup
G^{\prime}
with
G^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r}
, for some
r\in\mathbb{N}
.
For every
m\geq 2
, there exists a free minimal subshift
(X,\sigma,G)
with zero entropy such that its topological sequence entropy is a value between
\log(m)
and
\log(m^{2^{r}[G:G^{\prime}]})
.

If
G
is considered to be an infinite countable group that is a direct sum of finite groups, the previous result holds with the topological sequence entropy being equal to
\log(m)
(see Proposition
4.5
).

To construct these examples we use Toeplitz subshifts.
These dynamical systems were defined by Jacobs and Keane in
[
21
]
for
\mathbb{Z}
-actions and
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
for countable group actions.
This family of dynamical systems has been useful by their ergodic diversity and entropy, as we can observe in the works
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
28
]
to mention some of them.
Moreover, the maximal equicontinuous factor of these systems are well-known, they are called
G
-odometers
.
This relation between Toeplitz subshifts and
G
-odometers allow us to study the sequence entropy using independence tuples.

Independence is a combinatorial tool, introduced by Kerr and Li in
[
22
]
, that helps to study different dynamical properties, such as entropy, sequence entropy, and tameness.
See the survey
[
11
]
for an overview of recent results related to independence, and see
[
16
]
and
[
18
]
for works in which related phenomena were studied for Abelian groups.
We are interested in IN-tuples and IT-tuples, these tuples characterize topological sequence entropy and tameness, respectively.
In case of IN-tuples, positive topological sequence entropy is characterized with the existence of IN-pairs (IN-tuples of size two) of different elements.
In a more general way, since IN-tuples are just the same of sequence entropy pairs, in case of tuples with different elements (see
[
22
]
), we can assert from
[
19
]
that topological sequence entropy is
\log(n)
where
n
is the natural number such that the dynamical system has an IN-tuple of size
n
with different elements and all
{\rm IN}
-tuples of size
n+1
having at least two elements that are equal.

On the other hand, a dynamical system is called
tame
if the cardinality of its Ellis semigroup is at most that of the continuum.
Moreover, IT-tuples characterize tameness as follows: A dynamical system is tame if and only if all IT-pairs are those pairs of points which have same elements.
In
[
17
]
, it was studied the relationship between IT-tuples and a bound of the number of ergodic measures of minimal dynamical systems.
In more recent works
[
26
]
and
[
27
]
, this bound was improved, and in the latter an optimal upper bound was established.

We say that a dynamical system is
m
-tame
if all IT-tuples of size
m
have at least two coordinates with the same element.
A system which is not
m
-tame, for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we call it
m
-untame
.
From the proof of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, we also deduce that the minimal subshifts presented in these theorems are
n
-untame, but
n+1
-tame, for
n=m
in Theorem
1.1
, and for some
m\leq n\leq m^{2^{r}[G:G^{\prime}]}
in Theorem
1.2
.

This work is divided as follows.
In Section
2
, we give basic notions concerning topological dynamics, as well as some background with respect to Toeplitz subshifts,
G
-odometers, independence and sequential entropy.
At the end of Section
2
, we discuss Theorem
1.2
for
\mathbb{Z}
-actions.
In Section
3
, we present the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
The proof of Theorem
1.2
is provided in Section
4
, we split the cases in two, when the group is abelian finitely generated with positive free rank and when the group contains a normal subgroup which is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
for some
r>0
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this article, integers, nonnegative integers and natural numbers are denoted by
{\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and
{\mathbb{N}}
, respectively.

2.1.
Dynamical systems and invariant measures

Throughout this paper
G
represents an infinite countable group with identity
1_{G}
, and
X
a compact metric space.
By a
dynamical system

(X,\varphi,G)
we refer to a continuous action
\varphi:G\times X\to X
.
We denote the image
\varphi(g,x)
as
\varphi^{g}(x)
.
A system
(X,\varphi,G)
is called
minimal
if for every
x\in X
its orbit
O_{\varphi}(x)=\{\varphi^{g}x:g\in G\}
is dense in
X
and it is called
free
if
\mbox{Stab}_{\varphi}(x)=\{1_{G}\}
for every
x\in X
, where
\mbox{Stab}_{\varphi}(x)=\{g\in G:\varphi^{g}(x)=x\}
for
x\in X
.
A Borel subset
A\subseteq X
is called invariant if
\varphi^{g}(A)=A
for every
g\in G
.

An
invariant measure
of the dynamical system
(X,\varphi,G)
is a Borel probability measure
\mu
of
X
that verifies
\mu(\varphi^{g}(A))=\mu(A)
, for every
g\in G
and every Borel set
A
.
An invariant measure
\mu
is called
ergodic
if
\mu(A)=0
or
\mu(A)=1
for every invariant subset
A\subset X
.
The set of invariant measures of
(X,\varphi,G)
is denoted by
M_{G}(X)
.
If
\mu
is an invariant measure of
(X,\varphi,G)
, the quadruple
(X,\varphi,G,\mu)
is called a
probability-measure-preserving (p.m.p) dynamical system
.
Two p.m.p dynamical systems
(X,\varphi,G,\mu)
and
(Y,\psi,G,\nu)
are
measure conjugate
if: (i) there exist conull sets
X^{\prime}\subseteq X
and
Y^{\prime}\subseteq Y
satisfying
\varphi^{g}(X^{\prime})\subseteq X^{\prime}
and
\psi^{g}(Y^{\prime})\subseteq Y^{\prime}
for all
g\in G
, and (ii) there exists a bijective map
f:X^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}
such that
f,f^{-1}
are both measurable,
\nu(A)=\mu(f^{-1}(A))
for every measurable set
A\subseteq Y^{\prime}
, and
f(\varphi^{g}(x))=\psi^{g}(f(x))
for all
x\in X^{\prime}
and
g\in G
. In this case, we say that
f
is a
measure conjugacy
.

Let
(X,\varphi,G)
and
(Y,\psi,G)
two dynamical systems. We say that
(Y,\psi,G)
is
factor
of
(X,\varphi,G)
through
\pi
, if
\pi:X\to Y
is a surjective continuous map such that
\pi(\varphi^{g}x)=\psi^{g}(\pi(x))
for each
g\in G
and
x\in X
.
In this case, we say that
\pi
is a
factor map
.
A factor map
\pi:X\to Y
is called
almost
one-to-one if the set of points in
Y
having only one preimage is residual.
In the case that
X
is minimal, this is equivalent to the existence of an element in
Y
with only one preimage.

A dynamical system
(X,\varphi,G)
is called
equicontinuous
if the family of maps
\{\varphi^{g}:X\to X\}_{g\in G}
is equicontinuous.
There exists a unique factor
(X_{\rm eq},\varphi_{\rm eq},G)
of
(X,\varphi,G)
such that
(X_{\rm eq},\varphi_{\rm eq},G)
is equicontinuous, and if
(Y,\psi,G)
is an equicontinuous factor of
(X,\varphi,G)
, then
(Y,\psi,G)
is a factor of
(X_{\rm eq},\varphi_{\rm eq},G)
.
The system
(X_{\rm eq},\varphi_{\rm eq},G)
is called the
maximal equicontinuous factor
and we denote by
\pi_{\rm eq}
its corresponding factor map.

2.2.
Toeplitz
G
-subshifts and
G
-odometers

Let
\Sigma
be a finite set with at least two elements endowed with the discrete topology.
Consider the set

\Sigma^{G}=\{(x(g))_{g\in G}:x(g)\in\Sigma\mbox{ for every }g\in G\}

endowed with the product topology.
The
(left) shift action

\sigma
of
G
on
\Sigma^{G}

is defined as
\sigma^{g}(x)(h)=x(g^{-1}h)
, for all
g,h\in G
and
x\in\Sigma^{G}
.
The dynamical system
(\Sigma^{G},\sigma,G)
is known as a
full
G
-shift
(or full shift), and each closed subset
X\subseteq\Sigma^{G}
that is a
\sigma
-invariant set is called a
subshift
.
We also call to the system
(X,\sigma,G)
as a
subshift
.

An element
x\in\Sigma^{G}
is called a
Toeplitz array

or a
Toeplitz element
if for every
g\in G

there exists a finite index subgroup
\Gamma
of
G
such that

\sigma^{\gamma}(x)(g)=x(\gamma^{-1}g)=x(g)
, for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
.
Let
\Gamma
be a subgroup of
G
,
x\in\Sigma^{G}

and
\alpha\in\Sigma
.
We define

{\rm Per}(x,\Gamma,\alpha)=\{g\in G:x(\gamma^{-1}g)=\alpha\mbox{ for each }\gamma\in\Gamma\}

and

{\rm Per}(x,\Gamma)=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\Sigma}{\rm Per}(x,\Gamma,\alpha).

The following lemma can be found in
[
6
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a subgroup of
G
and
x\in\Sigma^{G}
.
For each
g\in G
and
\alpha\in\Sigma
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\rm Per}(\sigma^{g}x,\Gamma,\alpha)=g{\rm Per}(x,g^{-1}\Gamma g,\alpha).

A subgroup
\Gamma\leq G
is a
group of periods of

x

if
{\rm Per}(x,\Gamma)\neq\emptyset
.
A group of periods
\Gamma
is called an
essential group of periods
of
x

if for every
g\in G
that satisfies
{\rm Per}(x,\Gamma,\alpha)\subseteq{\rm Per}(\sigma^{g}x,\Gamma,\alpha)

for every
\alpha\in\Sigma
, we have
g\in\Gamma
.

Let
x\in\Sigma^{G}
be a Toeplitz array.
A
period structure
of
x
is a nested sequence of finite index subgroups
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of
G
satisfying
G=\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\rm Per}(x,\Gamma_{n})
and
\Gamma_{n}
is an essential group of periods of
x
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
A
Toeplitz
G
-subshift
(or Toeplitz subshift) is the subshift generated by the closure of the
\sigma
-orbit of a Toeplitz array.

Suppose
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a nested sequence of finite index subgroups of
G
such that
\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\Gamma_{n}=\{1_{G}\}
.
The
G
-odometer
associated to
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}

is defined as

\overleftarrow{G}=\left\{(g_{n}\Gamma_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}G/\Gamma_{n}:\varphi_{n}(g_{n+1}\Gamma_{n+1})=g_{n}\Gamma_{n},\mbox{ for every }n\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\},

where
\varphi_{n}:G/\Gamma_{n+1}\to G/\Gamma_{n}
is the canonical projection
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
There is a natural action of
G
on
\overleftarrow{G}
given by the left coordinate-wise multiplication, i.e.,
\phi^{g}((g_{n}\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}})=(gg_{n}\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
The dynamical system
(\overleftarrow{G},\phi,G)
is a free equicontinuous minimal Cantor system and it is also known as a
G
-
odometer
.
If
x\in\Sigma^{G}
is a Toeplitz array with period structure
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, then
\overleftarrow{G}
is the maximal equicontinuous factor of the Toeplitz subshift
X=\overline{O_{\sigma}(x)}
given by the factor map
\pi_{\rm eq}:X\to\overleftarrow{G}
, defined as
\pi_{\rm eq}(x)=(g_{n}\Gamma_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
when
\sigma^{g_{n}}x\in C_{n}
, for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
where

C_{n}=\{y\in X:{\rm Per}(y,\Gamma_{n},\alpha)={\rm Per}(x,\Gamma_{n},\alpha)\mbox{, for every }\alpha\in\Sigma\}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
x\in\Sigma^{G}
be a Toeplitz array with period structure given by
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\overline{O_{\sigma}(x)}\subseteq\Sigma^{G}
its Toeplitz subshift associated.
Let
\overleftarrow{G}
be the
G
-odometer associated to
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\pi:\overline{O_{\sigma}(x)}\to\overleftarrow{G}
be the factor map from
\overline{O_{\sigma}(x)}
into
\overleftarrow{G}
.
If
\pi(y)=\pi(y^{\prime})
for
y,y^{\prime}\in\overline{O_{\sigma}(x)}
, then
y(g)=y^{\prime}(g)
for each
g\in\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\rm Per}(y,g_{n}^{-1}\Gamma_{n}g_{n})=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\rm Per}(y^{\prime},g_{n}^{-1}\Gamma_{n}g_{n})
.

Proof.

Suppose
\pi(y)=\pi(y^{\prime})=(g_{n}\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
g_{n}\in G
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
By definition, we have
\sigma^{g_{n}}y,\sigma^{g_{n}}y^{\prime}\in C_{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Therefore,
{\rm Per}(\sigma^{g_{n}}y,\Gamma_{n},\alpha)={\rm Per}(\sigma^{g_{n}}y^{\prime},\Gamma_{n},\alpha)={\rm Per}(x,\Gamma_{n},\alpha)
for each
\alpha\in\Sigma
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Using Lemma
2.1
we deduce that
{\rm Per}(y,g_{n}^{-1}\Gamma_{n}g_{n},\alpha)={\rm Per}(y^{\prime},g_{n}^{-1}\Gamma_{n}g_{n},\alpha)=g_{n}^{-1}{\rm Per}(x,\Gamma_{n},\alpha)
and we conclude the lemma.
∎

2.3.
Independence and sequence entropy

Let
(X,\varphi,G)
be a dynamical system,
\mathcal{U}
an open cover of
X
and
S=(g_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
a sequence of
G
.
The
sequence entropy of
(X,\varphi,G)

with respect to
\mathcal{U}
along

S
is defined as

h_{top}^{*}(X,\varphi,G,\mathcal{U};S)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log N\left(\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}\varphi^{g^{-1}_{n}}\mathcal{U}\right),

where
N(\mathcal{C})
is the minimal cardinality among all cardinalities of subcovers of
\mathcal{C}
.
The
topological sequence entropy
of
(X,\varphi,G)
is given by

\displaystyle h_{top}^{*}(X,\varphi,G)=

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathcal{U},S}h_{top}^{*}(X,\varphi,G,\mathcal{U};S),

where the supremum is taken over all open covers of
X
and all sequences of
G
.
The system
(X,\varphi,G)
is called
null
if
h^{*}_{top}(X,\varphi,G)=0
and
non-null
otherwise.
For a tuple
A=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{k})
of subsets of
X
, we say that a set
J\subseteq G
is an
independence set
for
A
if for every non-empty finite subset
I\subseteq J
and function
s:I\to\{1,\ldots,k\}
we have

\bigcap_{g\in I}\varphi^{g^{-1}}A_{s(g)}\neq\emptyset.

A tuple
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in X^{n}
is called an
n
-
{\rm IN}
-tuple
(
n
-
{\rm IT}
-tuple
) if for any product neighborhood
U_{1}\times\cdots\times U_{n}
of
x
, the tuple
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{n})
has arbitrarily large finite independence sets (has an infinite independence set).
We denote the set of
n
-
{\rm IN}
-tuples and
n
-
{\rm IT}
-tuples by
{\rm IN}_{n}(X)
and
{\rm IT}_{n}(X)
, respectively. Note that
{\rm IT}_{n}(X)\subseteq{\rm IN}_{n}(X)
, for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Remark 2.3
.

If
(X,\varphi,G)
is a minimal dynamical system, then the tuple
(x,\dots,x)\in X^{n}
belongs to
{\rm IT}_{n}(X)
for each
x\in X
.
Furthermore, if
(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})\in{\rm IN}_{n}(X)
, then
(x_{1},x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})\in{\rm IN}_{n+1}(X)
.

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, define the set

\displaystyle\triangle^{({n})}(X)=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in X^{n}:x_{i}=x_{j}\mbox{ for some }i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\},i\neq j\}.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
22
, Proposition 5.4]
)
.

Let
(X,\varphi,G)
be a dynamical system. The following are true:

(1)

Let
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{k})
be a tuple of closed subsets of
X
which has arbitrarily large finite independence sets. Then there exists an
{\rm IN}
-tuple,
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
with
x_{j}\in A_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq k
.

(2)

{\rm IN}_{2}(X)\setminus\triangle^{(2)}(X)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
(X,\varphi,G)
is non-null.

(3)

{\rm IN}_{k}(X)
is a closed
G
-invariant subset of
X^{k}
.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
19
, Theorem 4.4]
)
.

Let
(X,\varphi,G)
be a dynamical system.
Then
h^{*}_{top}(X,\varphi,G)=\log(\max\{n:{\rm IN}_{n}(X)\setminus\triangle^{(n)}(X)\neq\emptyset\})
.

A tuple
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in X^{n}
is called
n
-regionally proximal
if for each
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{n}\in X
such that
d(x_{i},x^{\prime}_{i})<\varepsilon
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and there exists
g\in G
such that
d(\varphi^{g}x^{\prime}_{i},\varphi^{g}x^{\prime}_{j})<\varepsilon
for all
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. We denote by
Q_{n}(X,G)
the collection of all
n
-regionally proximal tuples of
(X,\varphi,G)
.

The following proposition relates the
{\rm IN}
-tuples to the regionally proximal tuples of a system.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
(X,\varphi,G)
be a dynamical system,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in X^{n}
.
If
x\in{\rm IN}_{n}(X)
, then
x
is a
n
-regionally proximal tuple.
In particular, every
n
-
{\rm IT}
-tuple is an
n
-regionally proximal tuple.

Proof.

Let
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
be an
n
-
{\rm IN}
-tuple.
Let
\varepsilon>0
and
U_{i}=B(x_{i},\frac{\varepsilon}{2})
.
For the sets
U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}
there exists an independence set
J\subseteq G
with
|J|\geq 2
.
Let
g,h\in J
with
g\neq h
.
By independence, for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
\sigma_{i}:\{g,h\}\to\{1,\ldots,n\}

given by
\sigma_{i}(g)=i
and
\sigma_{i}(h)=1
, we have that

\varphi^{g^{-1}}U_{i}\cap\varphi^{h^{-1}}U_{1}\neq\emptyset.

Then, there exists
y_{i}\in X
such that
\varphi^{g}y_{i}\in U_{i}
and
\varphi^{h}y_{i}\in U_{1}
.
Let
x_{i}^{\prime}=\varphi^{g}y_{i}\in U_{i}
and
g_{0}:=hg^{-1}
.
Observe

\varphi^{g_{0}}x_{i}^{\prime}=\varphi^{hg^{-1}}\varphi^{g}y_{i}=\varphi^{h}y_{i}\in U_{1}
for each
i\in\{1\ldots,n\}
.
Thus,
d(\varphi^{g_{0}}x_{i}^{\prime},\varphi^{g_{0}}x_{j}^{\prime})<\varepsilon
for every
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Since
x_{i}^{\prime}\in U_{i}
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we deduce that
x
is an
n
-regionally proximal tuple.
∎

Proposition 2.7

(
[
17
, Proposition 3.7]
)
.

Let
(X,\varphi,G)
be a minimal dynamical system and assume that the maximal equicontinuous factor map is almost one-to-one.
If
|M^{\rm erg}(X,G)|>l-1
, for some
l\geq 2
, then
{\rm IT}_{l}(X)\setminus\triangle^{(l)}(X)\neq\emptyset
.
In particular, it implies that
{\rm IN}_{l}(X)\setminus\triangle^{(l)}(X)\neq\emptyset.

In the meantime, the previous proposition was improved in

. In particular, the assumption that the maximal equicontinuous factor map is almost one-to-one can be weakened to being almost finite-to-one.

2.4.
\mathbb{Z}
-Toeplitz subshifts

In this subsection, we observe that the Toeplitz subshifts constructed in

satisfy a stronger version of Theorem
1.2
when

.

Right below, we recall the construction of the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-Toeplitz sequences made in

]
:
Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\Sigma=\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}
.
Fix a sequence of positive integers
(p_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that

p_{i}|p_{i+1}
and
p_{i}\geq 3
,
\frac{p_{i+1}}{p_{i}}\geq 3
,
for all
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Consider the sequence
(\alpha_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, given by

\alpha_{i}=j\in\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}
whenever

.
The Toeplitz element
\eta\in\Sigma^{\mathbb{Z}}
is defined inductively.

Step 1:

Set
\eta(n)=\alpha_{1}
, for every
n\equiv 0
or
n\equiv-1\mod p_{1}
.

Step 2:

For each

consider
J(1,k)=[kp_{1}+1,(k+1)p_{1}-1)
.
Define
\eta(n)=\alpha_{2}
for every

.

Step i+1:

For

, denote by

the set of elements

for which
\eta(n)
has not been defined at the end of the
i^{\mathrm{th}}

step.
Define
\eta(n)=\alpha_{i+1}
for
n\in J(i,k)
with
k\equiv 0
or
k\equiv-1\pmod{\frac{p_{i+1}}{p_{i}}}
.

It is also guaranteed that if the series
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{p_{i}}{p_{i+1}}
converges, then the set of ergodic measures of the Toeplitz subshift
\overline{O_{\sigma}(\eta)}\subseteq\Sigma^{\mathbb{Z}}

is in a one-to-one correspondence with

.
Furthermore, it is shown that these systems have zero entropy and the odometer
\overleftarrow{G}
associated to the sequence
(p_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is the maximal equicontinuous factor of
\overline{O_{\sigma}(\eta)}
with factor map

.

Let us recall the following lemma, which is important for the proof of Proposition

.

Lemma 2.8

(
[
28
, Lemma 3.3]
)
.

For every

and
\alpha\in\Sigma

there exists

with

,
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}{\rm Per}(x,\Gamma_{m})
.
Conversely, for every

\alpha\in\Sigma
such that

for every

.

The following proposition indicates that the previous systems are the one satisfying Theorem
1.2
for

.

Proposition 2.9
.

For every
m>1
, there exists a free minimal subshift
X
on
m
symbols with zero entropy such that
h^{*}_{top}(X,\sigma,\mathbb{Z})=\log(m)
,
{\rm IT}_{m}(X)\setminus\triangle^{(m)}(X)\neq\emptyset
and
{\rm IT}_{m+1}(X)\setminus\triangle^{(m+1)}(X)=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let
m\geq 2
,
\Sigma=\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}
and
\eta\in\Sigma^{\mathbb{Z}}
the Toeplitz element defined above with periodic structure given by
(p_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{p_{i}}{p_{i+1}}
converges.
Let
\overline{O_{\sigma}(\eta)}\subseteq\Sigma^{\mathbb{Z}}
be the Toeplitz subshift associated to
\eta
and
\overleftarrow{G}
the
\mathbb{Z}
-odometer associated to the sequence
(p_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.
In the light of Lemma
2.2
, if

.
Therefore, Lemma

guarantees that the map

).

.

On the other side, since this system has exactly

guarantees that

∎

3.
Minimal actions

In this section we present the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
To complete that, we mention some facts related to cellular automata induced by a group homomorphism.
See
[
2
]
for a detailed presentation of this topic.

Let
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
be countable infinite groups, and
\Sigma
a finite set.
For each
\phi:G_{1}\to G_{2}
group homomorphism define the map
\phi^{*}:\Sigma^{G_{2}}\to\Sigma^{G_{1}}
by
\phi^{*}(x)=x\circ\phi
.
In this section, we denote by
\sigma_{G_{i}}
the shift action of
G_{i}
on
\Sigma^{G_{i}}
,
i\in\{1,2\}
, defined in Section
2.2
.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
2
, Lemma 1 and Lemma 2]
)
.

Let
\phi:G_{1}\to G_{2}
be a group homomorphism.
The cellular automaton
\phi^{*}:\Sigma^{G_{2}}\to\Sigma^{G_{1}}
is continuous and
\phi
-equivariant.
That is, for every
g\in G_{1}
and
x\in\Sigma^{G_{2}}
we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{G_{1}}^{g}\phi^{*}(x)=\phi^{*}(\sigma_{G_{2}}^{\phi(g)}x).

Furthermore, if
\phi
is surjective, then
\phi^{*}
is injective.

Next, we relate these notions with minimal subshifts.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\phi:G_{1}\to G_{2}
be a surjective group homomorphism.
If
X\subseteq\Sigma^{G_{2}}
is a minimal subshift, then
\phi^{*}(X)\subseteq\Sigma^{G_{1}}
is a minimal subshift.

Proof.

The previous proposition guarantees that
\phi^{*}(X)
is a subshift in
\Sigma^{G_{1}}
.
Suppose that
X
is a minimal subshift.
Let
y,y^{\prime}\in\phi^{*}(X)
.
There exist
x,x^{\prime}\in X
such that
y=\phi^{*}(x)
and
y^{\prime}=\phi^{*}(x^{\prime})
.
We aim to prove that
y^{\prime}\in\overline{O_{\sigma_{G_{1}}}(y)}
.
Using that
X
is minimal, there exists a sequence
(h_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq G_{2}
such that
\sigma_{G_{2}}^{h_{i}}x\to x^{\prime}
as
i\to\infty
.
Since
\phi^{*}
is continuous, we obtain that
\phi^{*}(\sigma_{G_{2}}^{h_{i}}x)\to\phi^{*}(x^{\prime})
as
i\to\infty
.
Now, the fact that
\phi
is surjective implies the existence of
g_{i}\in G_{1}
satisfying
\phi(g_{i})=h_{i}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
Finally, the
\phi
-equivariance of
\phi^{*}
implies that
\sigma_{G_{1}}^{g_{i}}\phi^{*}(x)\to\phi^{*}(x^{\prime})
when
i\to\infty
as required.
∎

In the following proposition, we describe the relation between
\phi
-equivariant maps and
{\rm IN}
-tuples of a minimal subshift.
This gives a generalization of
[
22
, Proposition 5.4 (4)]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\phi:G_{1}\to G_{2}
be a surjective group homomorphism, and let
X\subseteq\Sigma^{G_{2}}
and
Y\subseteq\Sigma^{G_{1}}
be minimal subshifts.
If
\pi:X\to Y
is a surjective continuous map that is
\phi
-equivariant, then
(\pi\times\dots\times\pi)({\rm IN}_{n}(X))={\rm IN}_{n}(Y)

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Let
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in(\pi\times\dots\times\pi)({\rm IN}_{n}(X))

and
V_{1}\times\cdots\times V_{n}
be a product neighborhood of
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
.
Then, there exists
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in{\rm IN}_{n}(X)
such that

y_{i}=\pi(x_{i})
, for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Consider
U_{i}:=\pi^{-1}(V_{i})
, for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
If
J^{\prime}\subseteq G_{2}
is an independence set for
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{n})
,
then for each
h\in J^{\prime}
consider
g_{h}\in G_{1}
such that
\phi(g_{h})=h
.

We claim that
J=\{g_{h}\in G_{1}:h\in J^{\prime}\}\subseteq G_{1}
is an independence set for
(V_{1},\dots,V_{n})
.
Indeed, let
s:J\to\{1,\dots,n\}
be an arbitrary function and
define
s^{\prime}:J^{\prime}\to\{1,\dots,n\}
as
s^{\prime}(h):=s(g_{h})
,
h\in J^{\prime}
.
Using that
J^{\prime}\subseteq G_{2}
is an independence set for
(U_{1},\dots,U_{n})
, there exists
x\in\bigcap_{h\in J^{\prime}}\sigma_{G_{2}}^{h^{-1}}U_{s^{\prime}(h)}
.
Consequently,
\pi(\sigma_{G_{2}}^{h}x)=\sigma_{G_{1}}^{g_{h}}\pi(x)\in V_{s(g_{h})}
for each
g_{h}\in J
.
Therefore,

\pi(x)\in\bigcap_{g_{h}\in J}\sigma_{G_{1}}^{g_{h}^{-1}}U_{s(g_{h})},

which implies that
J
is an independence set for
(V_{1},\dots,V_{n})
.
Furthermore, we have that
|J|=|J^{\prime}|
.
Since
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in{\rm IN}_{n}(X)
, the tuple
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{n})
has arbitrarily large finite independence sets.
Hence,
(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})\in{\rm IN}_{n}(Y)
.

Conversely, let
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in{\rm IN}_{n}(Y)
.
Assume that
y_{1}\neq y_{i}
for each
i\in\{2,\dots,n\}
.
Let
\overline{B_{k}(y)}
denote the closed ball in
Y
of radius
\frac{1}{k}
and center in
y\in Y
, i.e.,
\overline{B_{k}(y)}=\overline{B(y,\frac{1}{k})}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since
Y
is a metric space, there exists
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\overline{B_{k}(y_{1})}\cap\overline{B_{k}(y_{i})}=\emptyset
for every
i\in\{2,\dots,n\}
and
k\geq K
.
As before, for
k\geq K
, we can guarantee that if
J
is an independence set for
(\overline{B_{k}(y_{1})},\dots,\overline{B_{k}(y_{n})})
, then
\phi(J)
is an independence set for
(\pi^{-1}(\overline{B_{k}(y_{1})}),\dots,\pi^{-1}(\overline{B_{k}(y_{n})}))
.

Now, we prove that
|\phi(J)|=|J|
.
Let
g,h\in J
be such that
g\neq h
.
Consider
s_{0}:J\to\{1,\ldots,n\}
given by
s_{0}(g)=1
and
s_{0}(h)=2
.
Then, there exists
y\in\pi(X)
such that
\sigma_{G_{1}}^{g}y\in\overline{B_{k}(y_{1})}
and
\sigma_{G_{1}}^{h}y\in\overline{B_{k}(y_{2})}
.
Using that
\pi
is surjective, there exists
x\in X
such that
\pi(x)=y
and by the
\phi
-equivariance of
\pi
we conclude

\pi(\sigma_{G_{2}}^{\phi(g)}x)=\sigma_{G_{1}}^{g}y
and
\pi(\sigma_{G_{2}}^{\phi(h)}x)=\sigma_{G_{1}}^{h}y
.
Thus,
\sigma_{G_{2}}^{\phi(g)}x\in\pi^{-1}(\overline{B_{k}(y_{1})})
and
\sigma_{G_{2}}^{\phi(h)}x\in\pi^{-1}(\overline{B_{k}(y_{2})})
, which is only possible if
\phi(g)\neq\phi(h)
since these sets are disjoint.

For each
k\geq K
, Proposition
2.4
guarantees that there exists an
n
-
{\rm IN}
tuple
(x_{k}^{1},\ldots x_{k}^{n})
in
\pi^{-1}(\overline{B_{k}(y_{1})})\times\cdots\times\pi^{-1}(\overline{B_{k}(y_{n})})
.
Since
{\rm IN}_{n}(X)
is closed, then, possibly after taking a subsequence,
\lim_{k\to\infty}x^{i}_{k}=z_{i}
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\in{\rm IN}_{n}(X)
.
On the other hand, the definition of the tuples
(x_{k}^{1},\dots,x_{k}^{n})
guarantees
\lim_{k\to\infty}\pi(x_{k}^{i})=y_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n
.
Therefore, we obtain
y_{i}=\pi(z_{i})
,
1\leq i\leq n
, and conclude the proof assuming that the elements in the
n
-tuple are pairwise different.
Otherwise, we use Remark
2.3
to restrict to the previous case.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The conclusion of the previous proposition holds even if we replace
{\rm IN}
-tuples with
{\rm IT}
-tuples.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
G
be a group such that
\mathbb{Z}
is a homomorphic image of
G
with the group homomorphism given by
\phi:G\to\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and let
X\subseteq\{0,\dots,m-1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
be the Toeplitz subshift presented in subsection
2.4
.
From Proposition
3.2
, we know that
\phi^{*}(X)
is a minimal subshift of
\{0,\dots,m-1\}^{G}
such that
{\rm Ker}(\phi)
is contained in the stabilizer of each element
y\in\phi^{*}(X)
, that is,
{\rm Ker}(\phi)\subseteq\bigcap_{y\in\phi^{*}(X)}{\rm Stab}(y)
.
Therefore, using the fact that
\phi^{*}
is injective along with Propositions
2.9
and
3.3
we conclude that
{\rm IN}_{m}(\phi^{*}(X))\setminus\triangle^{(m)}(\phi^{*}(X))\neq\emptyset
and
{\rm IN}_{m+1}(X)\setminus\triangle^{(m+1)}(X)=\emptyset
.
The same conclusion holds if we replace
{\rm IN}
with
{\rm IT}
.
Hence, we conclude the proof using Proposition
2.5
.
∎

4.
Free minimal actions

Recall that every infinite minimal
\mathbb{Z}
-dynamical system is free.
However, when the acting group is different from
\mathbb{Z}
, this is no longer true.
Consequently, the notion of freeness acquires more relevance (see, for instance,
[
12
]
,
[
15
]
).
Moreover, this notion has proven useful in characterizing the residual finiteness of a group through the existence of some free Toeplitz subshift (see
[
23
]
).

In this section, we provide a proof of Theorem
1.2
.
That is, we describe the zero entropy minimal free actions with bounded positive sequence entropy.
Firstly, we focus on finitely generated abelian groups, and afterward, we provide a proof for groups that have a normal subgroup isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
.
To this end, we begin by recalling the construction of the Toeplitz arrays made in
[
4
]
.

4.1.
Toeplitz arrays

Let
G
be a countable infinite group,

\Sigma=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
for
n\geq 2
, and
(\Gamma_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
a strictly decreasing sequence of normal subgroups of finite index of
G
such that
\bigcap_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\Gamma_{i}=\{1_{G}\}
.
Lemma 2.9 in
[
4
]
guarantees the existence of an increasing sequence of finite sets
(D_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
G
such that for each
i\geq 1
,

•

D_{i}
is a fundamental domain of
G/\Gamma_{i}
, that is,
D_{i}
contains exactly one representative from each coset of
G/\Gamma_{i}
.

•

\{1_{G}\}\subseteq D_{i}\subseteq D_{i+1}
.

•

G=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}D_{i}
.

•

For each
i<j
,
D_{j}=\bigcup_{\gamma\in D_{j}\cap\Gamma_{i}}\gamma D_{i}
.

Let
(\alpha_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\Sigma
be the sequence given by
\alpha_{i}=j\in\Sigma
when
i\equiv j\pmod{n}
.
The element
\eta\in\Sigma^{G}
is defined as follows:

Step 1:
Let
J(0)=\{1_{G}\}
, and define
\eta(g)=\alpha_{1}
for every
g\in\Gamma_{1}
.

Step 2:

Define
J(1)=D_{1}\setminus\Gamma_{1}
.
For every
h\in J(1)
and
\gamma\in\Gamma_{2}
,

\eta(\gamma h)=\alpha_{2}
.

Step m+1:
Consider

\displaystyle J(m)=D_{m}\setminus\bigcup_{i=0}^{m-1}J(i)\Gamma_{i+1}.

Define
\eta(\gamma h)=\alpha_{m+1}
for every
h\in J(m)
and
\gamma\in\Gamma_{m+1}
.

This construction defines a Toeplitz array
\eta
such that
(\Gamma_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a period structure for
\eta
.
Therefore, there exists a factor map
\pi:\overline{O_{\sigma}(\eta)}\to\overleftarrow{G}
, where
\overleftarrow{G}
is the
G
-odometer associated to
(\Gamma_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and also it is the maximal equicontinuous factor of
\overline{O_{\sigma}(\eta)}
.
Furthermore, when
G
is amenable and

(1)

\displaystyle[\Gamma_{i}:\Gamma_{i+1}]>\frac{1}{1-1/2^{(1/2)^{i+1}}},\;\;i\in\mathbb{N},

\overline{O_{\sigma}(\eta)}
has exactly
n
ergodic measures with zero entropy.

The following lemma and corollary were proved in
[
4
]
.
We provide the proofs of these statements to clarify the discussion in Section
4.3
.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
4
, Lemma 4.11]
)
.

For every
i\geq 1
and
\gamma\in\Gamma_{i}
, there exists
l\geq i
such that
\gamma J(i)\subseteq\Gamma_{l+1}J(l)
.

Proof.

Since
J(i)=D_{i}\setminus{\rm Per}(\eta,\Gamma_{i})
, we have
\gamma J(i)\cap{\rm Per}(\eta,\Gamma_{i})=\emptyset
. This implies that

\gamma J(i)\subseteq\bigcup_{l\geq i}\Gamma_{l+1}J(l).

Let
l=\min\{k\geq i:\gamma J(i)\cap\Gamma_{k+1}J(k)\neq\emptyset\}
. Let
u\in J(i)
be such that
\gamma u=\gamma_{l+1}v_{l}
, for some
v_{l}\in J(l)
and
\gamma_{l+1}\in\Gamma_{l+1}
. Since
v_{l}\in D_{l}
, there exist
v\in D_{i}
and
\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{i}\cap D_{l}
such that
v_{l}=\gamma^{\prime}v
.
The relation
\gamma u=\gamma_{l+1}v_{l}
implies
v=u
and
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}\gamma_{l+1}^{\prime}
, for some
\gamma_{l+1}^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{l+1}
. Thus, if
s\in J(i)
, then
\gamma s=\gamma^{\prime}\gamma_{l+1}^{\prime}s=\gamma^{\prime}s\gamma_{l+1}^{\prime\prime}
, for some
\gamma_{l+1}^{\prime\prime}\in\Gamma_{l+1}
.
This implies that
\gamma s\in\Gamma_{l+1}D_{l}\subseteq\Gamma_{l+1}J(l)\cup\bigcup_{k=0}^{l-1}\Gamma_{k+1}J(k)
.
The choice of
l
implies that
\gamma s\in\Gamma_{l+1}J(l)
, and then
\gamma J(i)\subseteq\Gamma_{l+1}J(l)
.
∎

Corollary 4.2

(
[
4
, Corollary 4.12]
)
.

For every
i\geq 0
and
\gamma\in\Gamma_{i}
, there exists
\alpha\in\Sigma
such that

\eta(g)=\alpha\mbox{ for every }g\in\gamma J(i).

Proof.

The case
i=0
is trivial. Suppose that
i\geq 1
and
\gamma\in\Gamma_{i}
.

From Lemma
4.1
, there exists
l\geq i
such that
\gamma J(i)\subseteq\Gamma_{l+1}J(l)
.
By the definition of
\eta
we get
\eta(g)=\alpha_{l+1}
, for every
g\in\gamma J(i)
.
∎

For
x\in\overline{O_{\sigma}(\eta)}
, let
\pi(x)=(t_{i}(x)\Gamma_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in\overleftarrow{G}
be the image of
x
in
\overleftarrow{G}
, where
t_{i}(x)\in D_{i}
is a representative of the coset
(\pi(x))_{i}
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, and
{\rm Aper}(x)
the subset in
G
given by

\displaystyle{\rm Aper}(x)=G\setminus\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}{\rm Per}(x,t_{i}(x)^{-1}\Gamma_{i}t_{i}(x)).

Remark 4.3
.

The subset
{\rm Aper}(x)
depends only on
\pi(x)
. That is, for
x,y\in\pi^{-1}(\{\pi(x)\})
it holds
{\rm Aper}(x)={\rm Aper}(y)
.
Moreover, by the definition of
\pi:\overline{O_{\sigma}(\eta)}\to\overleftarrow{G}
and Lemma
2.2
, we obtain

, for each

.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
x\in\overline{O_{\sigma}(\eta)}
and
\pi(x)=(t_{i}(x)\Gamma_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
t_{i}(x)\in D_{i}
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
It holds that the map
x|_{t_{i}(x)^{-1}\gamma J(i)}
is constant for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma_{i}
.
That is, there exists
\alpha\in\Sigma

Proposition 4.25

Proof.

Is there a group
G

In

X
over

was constructed that is tame but nonnull.
In other words, this Toeplitz subshift satisfies that
{\rm IT}_{2}(X)\setminus\triangle^{(2)}(X)=\emptyset

{\rm IN}_{2}(X)\setminus\triangle^{(2)}(X)\neq\emptyset

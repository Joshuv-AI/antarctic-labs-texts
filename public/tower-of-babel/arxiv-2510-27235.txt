A gradient flow model for the Gross–Pitaevskii problem: Mathematical and numerical analysis Thanks: Submitted to arXiv.
Tianyang Chu, Xiaoying Dai, Jing Wu, Aihui Zhou

Abstract
This paper concerns the mathematical and numerical analysis of the L 2 L^{2} normalized gradient flow model for the Gross–Pitaevskii eigenvalue problem, which has been widely used to design the numerical schemes for the computation of the ground state of the Bose–Einstein condensate. We first provide the mathematical analysis for the model, including the well-posedness and the asymptotic behavior of the solution. Then we propose a normalized implicit-explicit fully discrete numerical scheme for the gradient flow model, and give some numerical analysis for the scheme, including the well-posedness and optimal convergence of the approximation. Some numerical experiments are provided to validate the theory.



keywords

Bose–Einstein condensate, Gross–Pitaevskii problem, gradient flow, asymptotic behavior, convergence

Introduction

Bose–Einstein condensate (BEC) is an exotic state of matter that occurs in a dilute gas of bosons cooled to temperature extremely close to absolute zero. This phenomenon was first theoretically predicted by Einstein in the 1920s
[
30
]
, building upon the foundational work of Bose
[
14
]
. Experimental realization of BEC in ultracold bosonic gases was achieved in 1995, as documented in pioneering studies by Anderson et al.
[
5
]
and Davis et al.
[
27
]
. The theoretical framework for understanding BEC is largely based on the Gross–Pitaevskii (GP) theory, independently developed by Gross
[
34
]
and Pitaevskii
[
46
]
in the 1960s. The GP theory has been widely validated in predicting key properties of BECs and remains a cornerstone of the mathematical model for describing the BEC. In the GP theory, the quantum state of the BEC is given by the solution of the following nonlinear Schrödinger equation (also called the time-dependent GP equation):

\displaystyle i\psi_{t}=-\Delta\psi+V\psi+\beta|\psi|^{2}\psi,\qquad\text{in}\,\,\,\mathbb{R}^{3},\quad t>0,

(1)

with a given initial value
\psi(x,0)=\psi_{0}(x)
satisfying
\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=1
. The wave solutions to (
1
) are of the form
\psi(x,t)=\varphi(x)e^{-i\mu t}
. In this sense,
\varphi(x)
satisfies the following GP eigenvalue problem (also called the time-independent GP equation): find
(\varphi,\mu)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times\mathbb{R}
with
\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=1
such that in the weak sense

-\Delta\varphi+V\varphi+\beta|\varphi|^{2}\varphi=\mu\varphi.

(2)

The ground state of BEC which is a stationary point of (
1
), is described as the normalized eigenfunction
\phi
corresponding to the smallest eigenvalue of (
2
). Meanwhile, note that (
2
) represents the Euler-Lagrange equation of the GP energy functional which is given by

E(v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla v|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}V|v|^{2}+\frac{\beta}{4}|v|^{4}\right)\ \text{d}x.

(3)

Therefore, the ground state of a BEC can also be described as the minimizer of the following problem:

\displaystyle\varphi\in\argmin_{v\in S}E(v),\qquad S:=\{v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):\,\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=1\}.

(4)

For more details about the background of the BEC and the mathematical theory of the GP theory, we refer readers to the review paper
[
9
]
.

Nowadays, the computation of the ground state of BEC is one of the fundamental objects in numerical studies of BEC and there are mainly two approaches to obtain the ground state: solving the nonlinear eigenvalue problem (
2
) and solving the minimization problem (
4
). The first approach, i.e., directly solving the nonlinear eigenvalue problem (
2
), usually contains two steps. The first step is the spatial discretization such as the finite difference method, the finite element method, the spectral method, etc. There are some existing works on the error estimates for the spatial discretization, including the priori error analysis
[
17
,
21
,
39
,
52
]
and the a posteriori analysis
[
21
,
29
]
. The second step is to solve the nonlinear algebraic problem of the form

\displaystyle A(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}=\lambda\mathbf{v}\qquad\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(5)

The most common iterative technique for solving (
5
) is the self-consistent field iteration (cf.
[
16
,
18
,
19
,
28
,
47
]
), where a linearized eigenvalue problem is solved in each iteration. A systematic and comprehensive introduction of this approach can be found in a recent review paper
[
37
, Sections 3 and 4]
. For the second approach, i.e., solving the problem (
4
), there are many existing works such as directly minimizing the energy functional in the finite element space
[
12
]
, the Riemannian optimization method
[
3
,
4
,
26
]
, the preconditioned nonlinear conjugate gradient method
[
6
]
, the regularized Newton method
[
49
]
, the
J
-method
[
2
,
41
]
, the gradient-flow based methods, etc.

In this paper, we study the so-called gradient flow based method, which is widely used to solve (
2
). The main idea of the gradient flow approach is to introduce an artificial notion of time
t
and a corresponding time-dependent state
\phi(t)
, then construct a continuous (projected) gradient flow model (an evolution equation with respect to
\phi(t)
), for which
\lim_{t\to\infty}\phi(t)
is the solution of (
2
). After that, we can get several methods by discretizing the gradient flow model in time. Roughly speaking, there are mainly two types of the gradient flow model: the
L^{2}
projected gradient flow and the
H^{1}
projected gradient flow. The
L^{2}
projected gradient flow was first introduced in
[
11
]
, called continuous normalized gradient flow (CNGF), which was used as a mathematical justification for
the imaginary time evolution method
[
1
,
20
,
24
]
. Due to the simplicity and efficiency of the gradient flow with discrete normalization (GFDN) and CNGF models
[
11
]
, based on which lots of numerical methods have been applied to compute the ground state
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
13
,
44
,
48
,
53
]
. Convergence of GFDN iteration (cf.
[
36
, Definition 4.1]
) in 1D for focusing nonlinearities (
\beta<0
) was considered in
[
32
]
, and was considered in
[
36
]
for higher dimensions with defocusing nonlinearities (
\beta>0
). Several
H^{1}
gradient flows for the GP eigenvalue problem have been proposed in
[
25
,
38
,
43
]
. With these
H^{1}
Sobolev gradient flow models, combining a forward Euler discretization, several semi-discrete methods have been proposed in
[
25
,
38
,
43
]
with numerical analysis in
[
22
,
36
,
38
,
51
]
. Furthermore, combining with some spatial discretization, fully discrete methods have been proposed in
[
23
,
35
]
.

The
L^{2}
-normalized gradient flow, as a continuous model provided to design the numerical schemes, its mathematical properties such as well-posedness and asymptotic behaviors are of importance to guarantee the reasonability of the corresponding numerical schemes. Compared to the
H^{1}
-normalized gradient flow, there are few works on the mathematical analysis for the
L^{2}
gradient flow. With the absence of the potential function, i.e.,
V\equiv 0
, for the case of 1D with
\beta<0
, Faou and Jézéquel
[
32
]
proved the exponential convergence in
L^{2}
space under the assumption that the initial value is sufficiently close to the ground state. Antonelli et al.
[
7
]
proved the well-posedness of the
L^{2}
gradient flow with general nonlinearity, i.e.,
|\varphi|^{2\sigma}\varphi
, in higher dimensions. Different from the
H^{1}
case, the mathematical analysis of the
L^{2}
gradient flow, especially the well-posedness of the model, requires the theory of PDEs, while the
H^{1}
case only depends on the “ODEs” knowledge.

One of the objectives of this paper is to give a mathematical analysis for the
L^{2}
normalized gradient flow of the GP eigenvalue problem. Following the recent work
[
7
]
, we obtain the well-posedness of the
L^{2}
normalized gradient flow with
L^{\infty}
potential. In addition to the well-posedness, similar to the
H^{1}
case
[
38
,
43
]
, under some assumptions on the regularity of the solution, we also get several exponential convergences of the solution to the
L^{2}
normalized gradient flow as the time approaches infinity. Another objective of this paper is to consider the numerics of the
L^{2}
normalized gradient flow, including the numerical scheme and its convergence analysis. We propose a normalized implicit-explicit fully discrete scheme for the gradient flow model. A similar temporal semi-discrete scheme has been introduced in
[
44
]
, combining the spatial Fourier-pseudospectral discretization. Numerical experiments in
[
44
]
show that this scheme is very efficient since it only needs to solve a linear elliptic equation with constant coefficients in each iteration. For the convergence analysis, different from
[
32
,
36
]
, which considered the convergence and convergence rate of the iteration solution to the ground state with respect to the iteration numbers, our analysis concentrates on the approximation of the gradient flow model with respect to the mesh size (cf. Theorem
21
), and our numerical analysis can be naturally extended to similar methods such as numerical schemes in
[
44
]
. One of the difficulties in the convergence analysis is to estimate the error of the normalized step, which is overcome in a geometric view (cf. Lemma
18
). We obtain also an optimal convergence in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
and a suboptimal convergence in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
.

The organization of this paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the basic knowledge of the GP eigenvalue problem and its gradient flow. In Section
3
, we give some mathematical analysis for the
L^{2}
gradient flow of the GP eigenvalue problem, including the well-posedness, the exponential convergence of the energy and the exponential convergence of the solution to the ground state in
L^{2}
norm, as the time approaches infinity. In Section
4
, we propose a numerical scheme for the
L^{2}
gradient flow of the GP eigenvalue problem and carry out the convergence analysis with respect to the mesh size. In Section
5
, we provide two numerical experiments to validate our theoretic results obtained in Section
4
, and give a conclusion in Section
6
.

C
denotes a generic constant that could be different in different occurrences.

2
The gradient flow model

2.1
The Gross–Pitaevskii problem

The ground state of BEC is modeled by the following time-independent GP equation, also called the GP eigenvalue problem: Find
(\varphi,\lambda)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times\mathbb{R}
with
\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=1
such that in the weak sense

\displaystyle-\Delta\varphi+V\varphi+\beta|\varphi|^{2}\varphi=\lambda\varphi,

(6)

where
V
is a real-valued time independent potential function,
\beta
is a real number whose symbol represents the repulsive (positive)/attractive (negative) interaction. In this paper, we consider the repulsive interaction case, i.e.,
\beta\geq 0
, and without loss of generality, we assume
V\geq 0
. The classical Ljusternik–Schnirelman theory
[
50
]
shows that the problem (
6
) has infinitely many eigenvalues
0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{3}\leq\cdots<\infty
and the ground state
\phi_{\text{GS}}
is the normalized eigenfunction corresponding to the smallest eigenvalue
\lambda_{\text{GS}}:=\lambda_{1}
. Moreover, the ground state
\phi_{\text{GS}}
is positive and unique up to a sign
[
9
,
17
]
. In addition, if
V
satisfies
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}V(x)=+\infty
, then
\phi_{\text{GS}}
decays exponentially fast as
|x|\to\infty

[
9
]
.

Note that (
6
) is the Euler-Lagrange equation of the GP energy functional, which is defined as

\displaystyle E(v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla v|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}V|v|^{2}+\frac{\beta}{4}|v|^{4}\right)\ \text{d}x\qquad v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

(7)

Then the ground state of a BEC can be rephrased in terms of the solution of the following minimization problem: Find
\varphi\in S
,

\displaystyle E(\varphi)=\min_{v\in S}E(v).

(8)

The mathematical analysis of the problem (
8
), such as the existence, uniqueness and other properties, can be found in
[
9
,
17
]
.

2.2
The
L^{2}
gradient flow model

The exponential decay property of the ground state solution mentioned in Section
2.1
suggests that we can consider the GP problem (
6
) on a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, together with a homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition. We adopt the gradient flow approach to calculate the ground state of GP eigenvalue problem, where the ground state solution of GP eigenvalue problem as well as the global minimizer of the problem (
8
) can be viewed as the stationary state of the gradient flow problem. The gradient flow model of the GP problem was first considered in
[
11
]
as a mathematical justification for the classical imaginary time method, which has been used in physics literature to calculate the ground state solution of BEC
[
1
,
20
,
24
]
. We recall that the Fréchet derivative of the GP energy functional is given by

\langle E^{\prime}(v),w\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\nabla v\cdot\nabla w+Vvw+\beta|v|^{2}vw\Big)\ \text{d}x\quad\forall\,v,w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

(9)

where the notation
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the dual pair between
H^{-1}(\Omega)
and
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

The imaginary time approach to solve (
8
) can be read as: for a time sequence
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{n}<\cdots
,

\displaystyle\phi_{t}=-\frac{\delta E(\phi)}{\delta\phi}=\Delta\phi-V\phi-\beta|\phi|^{2}\phi,\qquad\text{in}\,\,\,\Omega,\quad t_{n}<t<t_{n+1},\quad n\geq 0,

(10)

\displaystyle\phi(t_{n+1})=\frac{\phi(t_{n+1}^{-})}{\|\phi(t_{n+1}^{-})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

(11)

In fact, (
10
) can be viewed as applying the steepest descent method to the energy functional
E(\phi)
without constraint and then projecting the solution onto the unit sphere. It is mentioned in
[
11
]
that (
10
)–(
11
) can be viewed as a first-order splitting method for the following continuous normalized gradient flow (CNGF):

\displaystyle\phi_{t}=\Delta\phi-V\phi-\beta|\phi|^{2}\phi+\mu[\phi]\phi,

\displaystyle\text{in}\,\,\,\Omega\times(0,\infty),

(12)

\displaystyle\phi=0,

\displaystyle\text{on}\,\,\,\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),

(13)

\displaystyle\phi=\phi_{0},

\displaystyle t=0\quad\text{with}\quad\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1,

(14)

where

\displaystyle\mu[\phi]:=\frac{1}{\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla\phi|^{2}+V|\phi|^{2}+\beta|\phi|^{4}\right)\ \text{d}x.

(15)

(
12
)–(
15
) is called the
L^{2}
gradient flow model of the GP problem,
and its mathematical analysis will be presented in the next section.

Remark 1
(Projected gradient flow approach).

The gradient flow model (
12
)–(
15
) can also be derived from the projected gradient flow approach. In the context of projected Sobolev gradient flow, the critical points of the energy
E(\cdot)
can be identified by constructing appropriate gradient flows of the form

z^{\prime}(t)=-P_{z,X}(\nabla_{X}E(z(t))),

(16)

where
\nabla_{X}E
is the Sobolev gradient of the energy functional and
P_{z,X}
is the projection operator. With the choice of
X=L^{2}(\Omega)
, (
16
) coupled with some initial value
\phi(0)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
is exactly (
12
)–(
15
)
[
38
, Section 2.2.1]
.

3
Mathematical analysis

In this section, we consider the following nonlinear, parabolic PDEs:

\begin{cases}\phi_{t}=\Delta\phi-V\phi-\beta|\phi|^{2}\phi+\mu[\phi]\phi,\qquad&\text{in}\,\,\,\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
\phi=0,\qquad&\text{on}\,\,\,\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
\phi=\phi_{0},\qquad&t=0,\qquad\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1,\end{cases}

(17)

where
V\geq 0
and
\beta\geq 0
and
\mu[\phi]
is defined in (
15
). For simplicity, we assume
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
to be a bounded convex domain with smooth boundary. First, we recall the properties of (
17
) (see Theorem 2.5 of
[
11
]
).

Proposition 2
.

The solution of (
17
) satisfies normalization conservation and energy diminishment, i.e.,

\displaystyle\|\phi(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1,\qquad t\geq 0,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E(\phi)=-\|\phi_{t}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq 0,\qquad t\geq 0.

Let
\{e^{t\Delta}\}_{t>0}
denote the semigroup generated by the Laplacian operator on
L^{2}(\Omega)
. The function
u=e^{t\Delta}u_{0}
is the solution of the following equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta u,\qquad&\text{in $\Omega\times(0,\infty)$},\\
u=0,\qquad&\text{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty)$},\\
u=u_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\qquad&t=0.\end{cases}

We recall the properties of
\{e^{t\Delta}\}_{t>0}

[
7
,
8
,
42
]
that will be used in the latter proof.

Proposition 3
.

For any time independent
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\|e^{t\Delta}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

And for any
u_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\|e^{t\Delta}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-2\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla e^{s\Delta}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\ \mathrm{d}s.

Proposition 4
.

For any
f\in L^{1}((0,\infty);L^{2}(\Omega))
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}f(s)\ \mathrm{d}s\right\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty);L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{1}((0,\infty);L^{2}(\Omega))}.

And for any
f\in L^{2}((0,\infty);L^{2}(\Omega))
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}f(s)\ \mathrm{d}s\right\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty);L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty);L^{2}(\Omega))}.

We then carry out the mathematical analysis of (
17
), including the well-posedness and the asymptotic behaviors.

3.1
Well-posedness and global convergence

Following
[
7
]
, we have

Theorem 5
.

Let
V\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
V\geq 0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
be nonnegative. If the initial value
\phi_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
with
\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
, then there exists a unique global solution
\phi
satisfying (
17
) with regularity

\phi\in C([0,\infty);H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)),\qquad\phi_{t}\in C([0,\infty);L^{2}(\Omega)).

Moreover, if
\phi_{0}\geq 0
, then
\phi(t)
converges to
\phi_{\text{GS}}
strongly in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
as
t\to\infty
.

Proof.

The proof will be divided into two parts.

Part I (Global well-posedness)
We first prove that there exist
T^{*}>0
and a unique local solution
\phi\in C([0,T^{*}],H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
. Let
M>0
,
N>0
,
T>0
be some constants that are to be determined. Let

\displaystyle\Lambda=\Big\{u\in C\big([0,T];H_{0}^{1}({\Omega})\big):\,u(0)=\phi_{0},\,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}\big([0,T];H^{1}({\Omega})\big)}\leq M,\,\inf_{t\in[0,T]}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{N}{2}\Big\}

be the set equipped with the distance

\displaystyle\text{d}(u,v)=\|u-v\|_{L^{\infty}\big([0,T];H^{1}({\Omega})\big)}\qquad\forall\,u,v\in\Lambda.

Since
V\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, by continuous embedding
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Omega)
and Hölder’s inequality, we conclude that there exists a constant
C_{1}(M,N)>0
such that

\|\mu[u]\|_{L^{\infty}[0,T]}\leq\frac{C}{N^{2}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{1}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{1}(\Omega))}^{4}\right)\leq C_{1},\hskip 10.00002ptu\in\Lambda.

(18)

Note that for all
u,v\in\Lambda

\displaystyle\left|\mu[u]-\mu[v]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{N^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\Big(|\nabla u+\nabla v||\nabla u-\nabla v|+V(x)|u+v||u-v|+\beta|u^{4}-v^{4}|\Big)\ \text{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{-2}-\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{-2}\right||\mu[u]|

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\|u-v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

here
C_{2}=C_{2}(M,N)
, which leads to

\|\mu[u]-\mu[v]\|_{L^{\infty}[0,T]}\leq C_{2}\text{d}(u,v),\hskip 10.00002ptu,v\in\Lambda.

(19)

For
u\in\Lambda
, define
\mathcal{F}(u)(t)
as

\mathcal{F}(u)(t)=e^{t\Delta}\phi_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}(\mu[u]u-\beta|u|^{2}u-Vu)\,\text{d}s,

(20)

obviously,
\mathcal{F}(u)(0)=\phi_{0}
. Combining Properties
3
and
4
, embedding
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\Omega)
and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))}

(21)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\mu[u]u-\beta|u|^{2}u-Vu\|_{L^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+T\|\mu[u]u-\beta|u|^{2}u-Vu\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+T(\|\mu[u]\|_{L^{\infty}[0,T]}+\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})\|u\|_{L^{\infty}\big([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega)\big)}+CT\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{1}(\Omega))}^{3}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C_{3}(M,N)T,

and

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathcal{F}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\phi_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\mu[u]u-\beta|u|^{2}u-Vu\|_{L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))}\right)

(22)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\phi_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mu[u]u-\beta|u|^{2}u-Vu\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+C_{4}(M,N)T^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By using (
18
), (
21
) and (
22
), there exist constants
C^{*}>0
and
C_{5}=C_{5}(M,N)>0
such that

\|\mathcal{F}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C^{*}\|\phi_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+C_{5}\Big(T+T^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big).

(23)

Meanwhile, we get

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq\|e^{t\Delta}\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}(\mu[u]u-\beta|u|^{2}u-Vu)\,\text{d}s\right\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))}

(24)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-2\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla e^{s\Delta}\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\ \text{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}-CT

\displaystyle\geq\left(\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-CT\|\nabla\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}-CT,

which implies

\displaystyle\inf_{t\in[0,T]}\|\mathcal{F}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-C_{6}(M,N)(T+T^{\frac{1}{2}}).

(25)

For
u,v\in\Lambda

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}(u)-\mathcal{F}(v)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))}

(26)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\mu[u]u-\mu[v]v-\beta(|u|^{2}u-|v|^{2}v)-V(u-v)\|_{L^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq C_{7}(M,N)T\text{d}(u,v),

and

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathcal{F}(u)-\nabla\mathcal{F}(v)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))}

(27)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\mu[u]u-\mu[v]v-\beta(|u|^{2}u-|v|^{2}v)-V(u-v)\|_{L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq C_{8}(M,N)T^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{d}(u,v).

Combining (
26
) and (
27
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}(u)-\mathcal{F}(v)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C_{9}(M,N)(T+T^{\frac{1}{2}})\text{d}(u,v).

(28)

First, we choose
M=2C^{*}\|\phi_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
and
N=\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
in (
23
), (
25
) and (
28
), which leads to

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq\frac{M}{2}+C_{1}^{\prime}\Big(T+T^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\qquad\forall\,u\in\Lambda,

(29)

\displaystyle\inf_{t\in[0,T]}\|\mathcal{F}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq N-C_{2}^{\prime}(T+T^{\frac{1}{2}})\qquad\forall\,u\in\Lambda,

(30)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}(u)-\mathcal{F}(v)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C_{3}^{\prime}(T+T^{\frac{1}{2}})\text{d}(u,v)\qquad\forall\,u,v\in\Lambda.

(31)

Then, we choose
T^{*}
sufficiently small such that, for
0\leq T\leq T^{*}

\displaystyle C_{1}^{\prime}\Big(T+T^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\leq\frac{M}{2},\quad C_{2}^{\prime}(T+T^{\frac{1}{2}})\leq\frac{N}{2},\quad\text{and}\quad C_{3}^{\prime}(T+T^{\frac{1}{2}})\leq\frac{1}{2}

which leads to for all
u\in\Lambda

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq M,\qquad\inf_{t\in[0,T]}\|\mathcal{F}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{N}{2},

(32)

and for all
u,v\in\Lambda

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}(u)-\mathcal{F}(v)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq\frac{1}{2}\text{d}(u,v).

(33)

Inequalities (
32
) and (
33
) show that
\mathcal{F}(\cdot)
is a contraction map on
\Lambda
(the continuity of
\mathcal{F}(\cdot)
with respect to
t
can be easily seen from the definition of
\mathcal{F}(\cdot)
.) with
T=T^{*}
. By Banach Fixed-Point Theorem, there exists a unique
\phi\in\Lambda
such that
\mathcal{F}(\phi)=\phi
. Then the local well-posedness of (
17
) directly follows from
[
7
, Proposition 2.2]
.

Let
\phi
be the local solution given above. Since
\beta
and
V
are non-negative, we obtain from the energy diminishing property that

\|\nabla\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq 2E(\phi(t))\leq 2E(\phi_{0}),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,t\in[0,T],

(34)

which shows that the
H^{1}
-norm of
\phi(t)
is bounded with respect to
t
. Thus we can extend the local solution to a global solution without losing uniqueness. The regularity of
\phi(t)
directly follows from
\phi_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
and
[
7
, Propositions 2.2 and 3.4]
.

Part II (Convergence to the ground state
)
Following
[
7
, Proposition 4.1, Corollary 4.2, and Theorem 4.3]
, we are able to prove the convergence to the ground state as
t\to\infty
, under the condition of
\phi_{0}\geq 0
. The proof will be divided into three steps.

Step 1 (Sequential weak convergence)
The energy decay property and Proposition
3
show that

\displaystyle\phi_{t}\in L^{2}([0,\infty);L^{2}(\Omega)),\quad\sup_{t>0}|\mu[\phi]|\leq C,\quad\text{and}\quad\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}([0,\infty);H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C.

As a result, there exists a sequence
\{t_{n}\}_{n\in N}
satisfying
t_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
,
\phi_{\infty}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, and
\mu_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\phi(t_{n})\rightharpoonup\phi_{\infty}\qquad\text{in}\ H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\\
\phi_{t}(t_{n})\to 0\qquad\text{in}\ L^{2}(\Omega),\\
\mu[\phi(t_{n})]\to\mu_{\infty}\qquad\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

Then under the weak topology of
H^{-1}
,

\displaystyle\phi_{t}(t_{n})

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi(t_{n})+V\phi(t_{n})+\beta|\phi(t_{n})|^{2}\phi(t_{n})-\mu[\phi(t_{n})]\phi(t_{n})

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup-\Delta\phi_{\infty}+V\phi_{\infty}+\beta|\phi_{\infty}|^{2}\phi_{\infty}-\mu_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}

as
n\to\infty
. This implies in
H^{-1}
sense,

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi_{\infty}+V\phi_{\infty}+\beta|\phi_{\infty}|^{2}\phi_{\infty}-\mu_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}=0.

(35)

Weak convergence
\phi(t_{n})\rightharpoonup\phi_{\infty}
in
H_{0}^{1}
implies
\|\phi_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
. (
35
) shows that
\phi_{\infty}
is a solution of the GP eigenvalue problem.

Step 2 (Sequential strong convergence)
Form (
35
), we conclude that
\mu[\phi_{\infty}]=\mu_{\infty}
and this leads to
\mu[\phi(t_{n})]\to\mu[\phi_{\infty}]
as
n\to\infty
, which is

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(|\nabla\phi(t_{n})|^{2}+V|\phi(t_{n})|^{2}+\beta|\phi(t_{n})|^{4}\Big)\ \text{d}x\to\int_{\Omega}\Big(|\nabla\phi_{\infty}|^{2}+V|\phi_{\infty}|^{2}+\beta|\phi_{\infty}|^{4}\Big)\ \text{d}x

when
n\to\infty
. Note that weak convergence in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
implies strong convergence in
L^{4}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi(t_{n})|^{2}\ \text{d}x\to\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{\infty}|^{2}\ \text{d}x,

which together with the weak convergence, we get
\phi(t_{n})\to\phi_{\infty}
strongly in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
as
n\to\infty
.

Step 3 (Convergence to the ground state)
If the initial value satisfies
\phi_{0}\geq 0
, since
V(x)\geq 0
and
\beta\geq 0
, by the Maximum Principle, we have
\phi(t_{n})\geq 0
. This implies
\phi_{\infty}\geq 0
. By
[
38
, Lemma 5.3]
, we obtain
\phi_{\infty}=\phi_{\text{GS}}
. The strong convergence of
\{\phi(t_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and the energy decay property show that

E[\phi(t)]\searrow E[\phi_{\text{GS}}],\qquad\text{as}\ \ t\to\infty.

To prove
\phi(t)\to\phi_{\text{GS}}
in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
as
t\to\infty
, we only need to prove that for every sequence
\{\tilde{t}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\tilde{t}_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
, there exists a subsequence
\{\tilde{t}_{n_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle\phi(\tilde{t}_{n_{k}})\to\phi_{\text{GS}}\qquad\text{when}\quad k\to\infty.

(36)

Boundedness of
\{\phi(\tilde{t}_{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
implies there exist a subsequence
\{\phi(\tilde{t}_{n_{k}})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\tilde{\phi}_{\infty}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that
\phi(\tilde{t}_{n_{k}})\rightharpoonup\tilde{\phi}_{\infty}
weakly in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
as
k\to\infty
. and hence
\|\tilde{\phi}_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
. By the lower semi-continuity of
E[\cdot]
, we have

\displaystyle E[\tilde{\phi}_{\infty}]\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}E[\phi(\tilde{t}_{n_{k}})]=E[\phi_{\text{GS}}].

From the above, we immediately get
\tilde{\phi}_{\infty}=\phi_{\text{GS}}
or
\tilde{\phi}_{\infty}=-\phi_{\text{GS}}
. Again by the Maximum Principle, we have
\phi(\tilde{t}_{n_{k}})\geq 0
, and hence
\tilde{\phi}_{\infty}\geq 0
, which is
\tilde{\phi}_{\infty}=\phi_{\text{GS}}
. Similar to
Step 2
, we obtain
\phi(\tilde{t}_{n_{k}})\to\phi_{\text{GS}}
strongly in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
as
k\to\infty
, which completes the proof.

Remark 6
.

Under the same assumption in Theorem
5
, following the regularity theory of elliptic and parabolic PDEs
[
31
,
33
]
, we have that if
V\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and the initial value further satisfies
\Delta\phi_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, then

\phi\in C([0,\infty);H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)),\qquad\phi_{t}\in C([0,\infty);H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)).

Moreover, if
V(x)\in H^{2}(\Omega)
and
\Delta\phi_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, then

\phi\in C^{1}([0,\infty);H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)),\qquad\phi_{tt}\in C([0,\infty);L^{2}(\Omega)).

3.2
Convergence rate

In this subsection, we will discuss the convergence rate of the solution of (
17
) to the ground state as
t\to\infty
. For simplicity, we make the following assumption.

Assumption 1
.

The solution of (
17
) satisfies
\phi\in C^{1}([0,\infty);H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))\cap C([0,\infty);H^{2}(\Omega))
, and
\phi(t)
converges to
\phi_{\text{GS}}
strongly in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
as
t\to\infty
.

Remark 7
.

By Remark
6
, Assumption
1
is reasonable for some potential function
V
and initial value
\phi_{0}
satisfying some regularity requirements.

Our discussion starts from the following lemma.

Lemma 8
.

Denote
g(t)=\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{t}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
, then for any
\epsilon\in(0,\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{\mathrm{GS}})
, there exists
T_{\epsilon}>0
such that

g^{\prime}(t)\leq-2(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{\text{GS}}-\epsilon)g(t)\qquad t\geq T_{\epsilon},

(37)

where
\lambda_{2}
is the second eigenvalue of the following linear eigenvalue problem:

-\Delta u+Vu+\beta|\phi_{\text{GS}}|^{2}u=\lambda u.

Proof.

Note that

\phi_{tt}=\Delta\phi_{t}-V\phi_{t}-3\beta\phi^{2}\phi_{t}+\left(\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\mu[\phi]\right)\phi+\mu[\phi]\phi_{t},

where
\partial_{tt}\phi
is understood in
H^{-1}
sense. We have that

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\langle\phi_{t},\phi_{tt}\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega)\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=-(\nabla\phi_{t},\nabla\phi_{t})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-(V\phi_{t},\phi_{t})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\beta(\phi^{2}\phi_{t},\phi_{t})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\underbrace{-2\beta(\phi^{2}\phi_{t},\phi_{t})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}_{\leq 0}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\mu[\phi]\right)\underbrace{(\phi,\phi_{t})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}_{=0}+\mu[\phi](\phi_{t},\phi_{t})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Omega}\Big(|\nabla\phi_{t}|^{2}+V|\phi_{t}|^{2}+\beta|\phi|^{2}|\phi_{t}|^{2}\Big)\ \text{d}x+\mu[\phi]\|\phi_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

(38)

Denote

a_{\phi}(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+Vuv+\beta|\phi|^{2}uv\Big)\ \text{d}x,

C_{\text{inf}}:=\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{a_{\phi}(\phi_{t},\phi_{t})}{\|\phi_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}.

Assume that
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
t_{n}\to\infty
satisfies

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{\phi(t_{n})}(\phi_{t}(t_{n}),\phi_{t}(t_{n}))}{\|\phi_{t}(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}=C_{\text{inf}}.

Let

z_{n}:=\frac{\phi_{t}(t_{n})}{\|\phi_{t}(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}},

we see that
\{z_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a bounded sequence in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}|\phi(t_{n})|^{2}z_{n}^{2}\ \text{d}x-\int_{\Omega}|\phi_{\text{GS}}|^{2}z_{n}^{2}\ \text{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi(t_{n})+\phi_{\text{GS}}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\|\phi(t_{n})-\phi_{\text{GS}}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\|z_{n}^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0.

Hence,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\underbrace{\int_{\Omega}\Big(|\nabla z_{n}|^{2}+V|z_{n}|^{2}+\beta|\phi_{\text{GS}}|^{2}|z_{n}|^{2}\Big)\ \text{d}x}_{=:a_{\phi_{\text{GS}}}(z_{n},z_{n})}=C_{\text{inf}}.

Since
\{z_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
H^{1}(\Omega)
, there exists a weak limit

z_{n}\rightharpoonup\hat{z}\qquad\text{weakly in $H^{1}(\Omega)$}.

This indicates
\|\hat{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
and

\displaystyle\left|(\hat{z},\phi_{\text{GS}})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-(z_{n},\phi(t_{n}))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{z}-z_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\phi_{\text{GS}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|z_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\phi_{\text{GS}}-\phi(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0,

which leads to

(\hat{z},\phi_{\text{GS}})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0.

Lower semicontinuity of weakly converging sequence implies

C_{\text{inf}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{\phi_{\text{GS}}}(z_{n},z_{n})\geq a_{\phi_{\text{GS}}}(\hat{z},\hat{z})\geq\inf_{v\in\text{span}\{\phi_{\text{GS}}\}^{\bot}}\frac{a_{\phi_{\text{GS}}}(v,v)}{\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}.

With the Courant-Fischer theorem we get

C_{\text{inf}}\geq\lambda_{2},

where
\lambda_{2}
is the second eigenvalue of linear eigenvalue problem: Find
(u,\lambda)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{R}
such that

-\Delta u+Vu+\beta|\phi_{\text{GS}}|^{2}u=\lambda u.

With the fact
\mu[\phi(t)]\to\lambda_{\text{GS}}
as
t\to\infty
, there exists
T_{\epsilon}>0
such that for
t\geq T_{\epsilon}

\mu[\phi(t)]\leq\lambda_{\text{GS}}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}

(39)

and

\frac{a_{\phi}(\phi_{t},\phi_{t})}{\|\phi_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}\geq\lambda_{2}-\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

(40)

Combining (
39
) and (
40
), we have that for
t\geq T_{\epsilon}
,

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Omega}\Big(|\nabla\phi_{t}|^{2}+V|\phi_{t}|^{2}+\beta|\phi|^{2}|\phi_{t}|^{2}\Big)\ \text{d}x+\mu[\phi]\|\phi_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\|\phi_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\left(\mu[\phi]-\frac{a_{\phi}(\phi_{t},\phi_{t})}{\|\phi_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq-(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{\text{GS}}-\epsilon)\|\phi_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=-2(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{\text{GS}}-\epsilon)g(t).

This completes the proof.

By Grönwall’s inequality, we arrive at

Corollary 9
.

Let

\epsilon\in(0,\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{GS})
. If
t\geq T_{\epsilon}
, then

g(t)\leq C_{\epsilon}\exp\big(-2(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{\text{GS}}-\epsilon)t\big).

(41)

With the inequality (
41
), we obtain the following exponential convergence.

Theorem 10
.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{GS}).
If
t\geq T_{\epsilon}
, then

•

exponential convergence of energy

E(\phi(t))-E(\phi_{\text{GS}})\leq C_{\epsilon}\exp\big(-2(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{\text{GS}}-\epsilon)t\big),

(42)

•

exponential convergence to ground state

\|\phi(t)-\phi_{\text{GS}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\epsilon}\exp\big(-(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{\text{GS}}-\epsilon)t\big),

(43)

•

exponential convergence of eigenvalue

\left|\mu[\phi(t)]-\lambda_{\text{GS}}\right|\leq C_{\epsilon}\exp\big(-(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{\text{GS}}-\epsilon)t\big).

(44)

Proof.

Note that

E(\phi(t))-E(\phi_{\text{GS}})=\int_{t}^{\infty}2g(t)\ \text{d}t,

then (
42
) follows from (
41
). (
43
) can be similarly derived by (
41
) and

\|\phi(t)-\phi_{\text{GS}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\int_{t}^{\infty}\|\phi_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\ \text{d}t\leq\int_{t}^{\infty}\sqrt{2g(t)}\ \text{d}t.

With (
42
) and (
43
), we have

\displaystyle|\mu[\phi(t)]-\lambda_{\text{GS}}|

\displaystyle\leq 2(E(\phi(t))-E(\phi_{\text{GS}}))+\left|\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{\Omega}\Big(|\phi(t)|^{4}-|\phi_{\text{GS}}|^{4}\Big)\ \text{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\epsilon}\exp\big(-2(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{\text{GS}}-\epsilon)t\big)

\displaystyle\quad+C\|\phi-\phi_{\text{GS}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\phi+\phi_{\text{GS}}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\|\phi^{2}+\phi_{\text{GS}}^{2}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\epsilon}\exp\big(-(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{\text{GS}}-\epsilon)t\big).

This completes the proof of (
44
).

In addition, we further obtain the strong convergence in
H^{2}(\Omega)
.

Theorem 11
.

\phi(t)
strongly converges to
\phi_{\text{GS}}
in
H^{2}(\Omega)
as
t\to\infty
, and hence
\|\phi(t)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}
is uniformly bounded.

Proof.

We only need to prove the first claim. By Corollary
9
, we have
\phi_{t}\to 0
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, which implies

\displaystyle\Delta\phi(t)

\displaystyle=\phi_{t}+V\phi+\beta\phi^{3}-\mu[\phi]\phi

\displaystyle\to V\phi_{\text{GS}}+\beta\phi_{\text{GS}}^{3}-\lambda_{\text{GS}}\phi_{\text{GS}}=\Delta\phi_{\text{GS}}\qquad\text{in $L^{2}(\Omega)$}.

Namely,
\phi(t)\to\phi_{\text{GS}}
in
H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

4
Numerical analysis

In this section, we introduce the numerical discretization scheme of the
L^{2}
normalized gradient flow corresponding to the GP eigenvalue problem and its convergence analysis. Combining a backward-forward Euler discretiaztion in time and a linear finite element discretiaztion in space, we propose a fully discrete scheme for the model problem, and give local in time convergence results with respect to the temporal mesh
\tau
and spatial mesh
h
.

4.1
Numerical scheme

We consider the numerical approximation of (
17
) in finite time
T
. Taking an inner product with
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
in (
17
), we obtain the variational from of (
17
) as follows:

\displaystyle(\phi_{t},v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(\nabla\phi,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=-(V\phi,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\beta(\phi^{3},v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\mu[\phi](\phi,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall\,t>0.

(45)

We first consider the temporal semi-discretization. Let
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{N}=T
denote a uniform partition of the time interval
[0,T]
with stepsize
\tau=T/N
. Let
\phi^{n}
be the approximation of
\phi(t_{n})
. By applying the backward-forward Euler discretiaztion to (
45
) at
t=t_{n+1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\tilde{\phi}^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{\tau},v\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(\nabla\tilde{\phi}^{n+1},\nabla v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad=-(V\phi^{n},v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\beta((\phi^{n})^{3},v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\mu[\phi^{n}](\phi^{n},v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\forall v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(46)

Here
\tilde{\phi}^{n+1}
is an temporary approximation of
\phi(t_{n+1})
. In general,
\|\tilde{\phi}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\neq 1
. To get a mass conservation approximation, we normalize
\tilde{\phi}^{n+1}
and set

\displaystyle\phi^{n+1}=\frac{\tilde{\phi}^{n+1}}{\left\|\tilde{\phi}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

We then use the finite element method to do the spatial discretization of (
46
). Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be a shape regular and quasi-uniform triangulation of
\Omega
with mesh size
h
, and denote
S_{h}\subset H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
the piecewise linear finite element space corresponding to
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. The Ritz projection operator
R_{h}:H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\to S_{h}
and the discrete Laplacian operator
\Delta_{h}:S_{h}\to S_{h}
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle(\nabla R_{h}u,\nabla v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(\nabla u,\nabla v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall\,u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\,\forall\,v_{h}\in S_{h},

\displaystyle(\Delta_{h}u_{h},v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=-(\nabla u_{h},\nabla v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall\,u_{h},\,v_{h}\in S_{h}.

Combing the finite element discretization in space, we apply the following full-discrete scheme to (
17
).

For
0\leq n\leq N-1
and given
\phi_{h}^{n}\in S_{h}
, we first obtain
\tilde{\phi}_{h}^{n+1}
by solving the following equation: Find
\tilde{\phi}_{h}^{n+1}\in S_{h}
, such that,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\tilde{\phi}_{h}^{n+1}-\phi_{h}^{n}}{\tau},v_{h}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(\nabla\tilde{\phi}_{h}^{n+1},\nabla v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\left(-V\phi_{h}^{n},v_{h}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\beta\left(|\phi_{h}^{n}|^{2}\phi_{h}^{n},v_{h}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\mu[\phi_{h}^{n}]\left(\phi_{h}^{n},v_{h}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall\,v_{h}\in S_{h}.

(47)

Then, we get
\phi_{h}^{n+1}
by normalizing
\tilde{\phi}_{h}^{n+1}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\phi_{h}^{n+1}=\frac{\tilde{\phi}_{h}^{n+1}}{\left\|\tilde{\phi}_{h}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

(48)

The initial value
\phi_{h}^{0}
is given by

\phi_{h}^{0}=\frac{R_{h}\phi_{0}}{\|R_{h}\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

(49)

The subsequent theorem shows the well-posedness of the numerical scheme (
47
)–(
49
).

Theorem 12
.

The numerical scheme (
47
)–(
49
) is well-posed for any
\tau>0
and
h>0
.

Proof.

The numerical scheme (
47
)–(
49
) contains two steps, the first step is to get
\tilde{\phi}_{h}^{n+1}
by solving the following equation

\displaystyle\tau(\nabla\tilde{\phi}_{h}^{n+1},\nabla v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(\tilde{\phi}_{h}^{n+1},v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(f^{n},v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall\,v_{h}\in S_{h},

here
f^{n}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
only depends on
\phi_{h}^{n}
. By noticing that

\displaystyle\tau(\nabla u_{h},\nabla u_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(u_{h},u_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\min\{\tau,1\}\|u_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\qquad\forall u_{h}\in S_{h},

then the well-posedness of
\tilde{\phi}_{h}^{n+1}
can be obtained by the Lax-Milgram lemma
[
15
]
.
The second step is to get
\phi^{n+1}_{h}
by normalizing
\tilde{\phi}_{h}^{n+1}
, to show the well-posedness of the second step, we only need to prove that for any
0\leq n\leq N-1

\left\|\tilde{\phi}_{h}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\neq 0.

If not, suppose

\left\|\tilde{\phi}_{h}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0

for some
0\leq n\leq N-1
, then equation

\left(-\frac{\phi_{h}^{n}}{\tau},v_{h}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\left(-V\phi_{h}^{n}-\beta|\phi_{h}^{n}|^{2}\phi_{h}^{n}+\mu[\phi_{h}^{n}]\phi_{h}^{n},v_{h}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

(50)

holds for any
v_{h}\in S_{h}
. Choose
v_{h}=\phi_{h}^{n}
in (
50
), we can get

\underbrace{-\frac{1}{\tau}}_{<0}=\underbrace{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{h}^{n}|^{2}\ \text{d}x}_{\geq 0},

which is a contradiction.

4.2
Convergence

In this subsection, we will show the convergence of the numerical scheme (
47
)–(

4.2.2

Note that the exact solution satisfies: for any

\displaystyle-(V\widehat{R_{h}\phi(t_{n})},v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-(\beta(\widehat{R_{h}\phi(t_{n})})^{3},v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+\mu[\widehat{R_{h}\phi(t_{n})}](\widehat{R_{h}\phi(t_{n})},v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\mathcal{E}(v_{h}).

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\widehat{R_{h}\phi(t_{n+1})}-\widehat{R_{h}\phi(t_{n})}}{\tau},v_{h}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-(\phi_{t}(t_{n+1}),v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{1}{\|R_{h}\phi(t_{n+1})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}-1\right)(\nabla\phi(t_{n+1}),\nabla v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+(V\widehat{R_{h}\phi(t_{n})},v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-(V\phi(t_{n+1}),v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+(\beta(\widehat{R_{h}\phi(t_{n})})^{3},v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-(\beta\phi(t_{n+1})^{3},v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+\mu[\phi(t_{n+1})](\phi(t_{n+1}),v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\mu[\widehat{R_{h}\phi(t_{n})}](\widehat{R_{h}\phi(t_{n})},v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{E}_{1}(v_{h})+\mathcal{E}_{2}(v_{h})+\mathcal{E}_{3}(v_{h})+\mathcal{E}_{4}(v_{h})+\mathcal{E}_{5}(v_{h}).

(52)

The rest of this part is to prove the following estimate of the truncation error

Proof.

We will estimate for

where

\displaystyle\|\phi_{t}(t)(1/\|R_{h}\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-1)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\phi(t)-R_{h}\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{2},

\displaystyle\left|\frac{(R_{h}\phi(t),R_{h}\phi_{t}(t))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}{\|R_{h}\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{3}}-\frac{(\phi(t),\phi_{t}(t))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}{\|\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{3}}\right|

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{n+1}-e_{h}^{n}}{\tau}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\tau^{-1}(\|\tilde{e}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|e_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\tau^{-1}(\tau+h^{2})\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Obviously,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}_{2}|\leq C\|V\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|e_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

and

|\mathcal{G}_{j}|\leq C\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\qquad j=3,\cdots,5.

With slightly modified the estimates of the truncation error, we have

|\mathcal{G}_{6}|\leq C\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Estimates of
\mathcal{G}_{1}
–
\mathcal{G}_{6}
imply

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{h}\tilde{e}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C.

The proof is completed by combing estimates of two cases.

4.2.5
Error estimate

From the proof of Lemma
19
, we have the following convergence results.

Theorem 21
.

Under the same assumptions in Lemma
19
, there holds

\max_{0\leq n\leq N}\|\phi(t_{n})-\phi_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\kappa}(\tau+h^{2}).

Proof.

We obtain from Lemma
18
that

\|e_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\tilde{e}_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\tau+h^{2}),

which leads to

\displaystyle\|\phi(t_{n})-\phi_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi(t_{n})-\widehat{R_{h}\phi(t_{n})}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|e_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}+C(\tau+h^{2})\leq C(\tau+h^{2}),

and completes the proof.

Theorem 22
(discrete
H^{2}
stability).

Under the same assumptions in Lemma
19
, there holds

\max_{0\leq n\leq N}\|\Delta_{h}\phi_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\kappa}.

Proof.

Note that

\phi_{h}^{n}=\widehat{\tilde{\phi}_{h}^{n}},

we obtain from (
*
) that

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{h}\phi_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Delta_{h}\tilde{\phi}_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Delta_{h}\widehat{R_{h}\phi(t_{n})}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\|\Delta_{h}\tilde{e}_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C,

which completes the proof.

Corollary 23
.

Under the same assumptions in Lemma
19
, there holds

\max_{0\leq n\leq N}\|\phi(t_{n})-\phi_{h}^{n}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\kappa}(\sqrt{\tau}+h).

Proof.

With inequality (
74
), we obtain

\|\nabla e_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|e_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Delta_{h}e_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C(\sqrt{\tau}+h),

which leads to

\displaystyle\|\phi(t_{n})-\phi_{h}^{n}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi(t_{n})-\widehat{R_{h}\phi(t_{n})}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+C\|\nabla e_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\sqrt{\tau}+h).

This completes the proof.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we have carried out the mathematical analysis of the
L^{2}
normalized gradient flow model for the GP eigenvalue problem and given a normalized implicit-explicit full-discrete scheme. We prove the well-posedness of the scheme and establish the optimal-order convergence of the approximations. Under a mild inverse CFL condition, we have proven the convergence rate is of order
O(\tau+h^{2})
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
-norm. With this convergence result, combining the
H^{2}
-stability, we have proved that the numerical scheme has a sub-optimal convergence of the form
O(\sqrt{\tau}+h)
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
-norm. Numerical experiments validate our theories and show that the theoretical convergence order in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
-norm is not sharp. The analysis of the optimal-order convergence in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
-norm will be left as future work.

Appendix A
Properties of discrete operators

Lemma 24
(
[
15
]
).

For the Ritz projection operator
R_{h}
and
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, there hold:

\displaystyle\|\nabla(u-R_{h}u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(71)

\displaystyle\|u-R_{h}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(72)

Lemma 25
.

If
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, then

\|R_{h}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

(73)

Proof.

Denote
I_{h}u\in S_{h}
the interpolation of
u
, we have

\displaystyle\|R_{h}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|I_{h}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|I_{h}u-R_{h}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+Ch^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|I_{h}u-R_{h}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad(\text{inverse inequality})

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+Ch^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|I_{h}u-u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+Ch^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|u-R_{h}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)},

where we have used the Sobolev embedding inequality and the approximation property of the interpolation operator
[
15
]
in the last line.

Lemma 26
.

The discrete Laplacian operator satisfies

\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Delta_{h}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\qquad\forall\,u_{h}\in S_{h},

(74)

and

\|u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\Delta_{h}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{3}{4}}\qquad\forall\,u_{h}\in S_{h}.

(75)

Proof.

By the definition of
\Delta_{h}
and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=-(\Delta_{h}u_{h},u_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\Delta_{h}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

which completes the proof of (
74
).

To prove (
75
), let
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

\Delta u=\Delta_{h}u_{h}.

Then we have
\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\Delta_{h}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. Next we show that
\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.

Note that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+h^{2}\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\qquad(\text{here \eqref{equ: L2 convergence of Ritz projection} is used})

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+Ch^{2}\|\Delta_{h}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(76)

where the inverse inequality is applied to obtain the last line. Similarly, we can prove that

\displaystyle\|I_{h}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(77)

Then by the triangle inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|u-I_{h}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{3}{4}}+h^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

which together with Sobolev interpolation inequality, inverse inequality, equations (
76
) and (
77
) yields

\displaystyle\|u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\Delta_{h}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{3}{4}}+h^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{3}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\Delta_{h}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{3}{4}}

\displaystyle\quad+h^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{4}}\big(\|I_{h}u-u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big)^{\frac{3}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\Delta_{h}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{3}{4}}+h^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{4}}\big(h^{2}\|\Delta_{h}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big)^{\frac{3}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\Delta_{h}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{3}{4}}.

This completes the proof of (
75
).

Proof.

Proof of Lemma

Proof.

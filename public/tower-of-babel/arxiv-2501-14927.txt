Compact Moduli Spaces of Marked Cubic Plane Curves
Aaron Goodwin

Abstract
. We study compactifications of the moduli space of plane cubic curves marked by n n labeled points up to projective equivalence via Geometric Invariant Theory (GIT). Specifically, we provide a complete description of the GIT walls and show that the moduli-theoretic wall-crossing can be understood through analysis of the singularities of the plane curves and the position of the points.

We now describe a modification of the Hilbert-Mumford Numerical Criterion which gives an effective way of computing the GIT stability for marked plane curves. We fix a maximal torus

of

and choose coordinates

on

&t^{b}&\\
0&&t^{c}\end{bmatrix}

and

of

Theorem 2.6
.

(Our Numerical Criterion)
Let
S^{\prime}
be the set of all normalized one-parameter subgroups of
T
. Then

•

\mathcal{C}_{n,d}^{ss}(L)=\{x\in\mathcal{C}_{n,d}\ |\ \mu^{L}(g\cdot x,\lambda_{r})\leq 0\ for\ all\ \lambda_{r}\in S^{\prime},g\in SL(3)\}
.

•

\mathcal{C}_{n,d}^{s}(L)=\{x\in\mathcal{C}_{n,d}\ |\ \mu^{L}(g\cdot x,\lambda_{r})<0\ for\ all\ \lambda_{r}\in S^{\prime},g\in SL(3)\}
.

Proof.

We prove that there exists a normalized one-parameter subgroup
\lambda_{r}
of
T
making

for some

if and only if there exists a one-parameter subgroup
\lambda
of
SL(3)
making

. The first direction is immediate, taking

to be the identity matrix and
\lambda:=\lambda_{r}
. Now suppose there is a
\lambda
making
\mu^{L}(x,\lambda)\geq 0
. The image of
\lambda
is contained in some maximal torus of
SL(3)
. Since all maximal tori of

are conjugate, the image of

is conjugate to a subgroup of

. That is, there exists a
g\in SL(3)
such that

. After a permutation of coordinates we may furthermore assume that the weights of

satisfy
a\geq b\geq c
.
Then, using the functorial properties of

[

\mu^{L}(g\cdot x,g\lambda g^{-1})=\mu^{L}(x,g^{-1}g\lambda g^{-1}g)=\mu^{L}(x,\lambda)\geq 0.

By scaling the weights

of

by

we obtain a normalized one-parameter subgroup satisfying the same inequality. This shows that some

in the orbit of

is stable with respect to a normalized one-parameter subgroup of

if and only if

is stable. The proof for semi-stability is identical, with the inequalities replaced by strict inequalities.

2.3.
Computing the stability of a marked curve

In this section we describe heuristics for computing the (semi-)stability of a marked curve with respect to a given line bundle. Theorem

demonstrates such computations in detail.

We consider the line bundles on

obtained as restrictions of nef line bundles on

These are restrictions of line bundles of the form

Definition 2.10

Let

Let

varies in

is negative for all

is in the

\max_{r\in[\frac{-1}{2},1]}\mu^{L}(h\cdot(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n}),\lambda_{r})\leq\max_{g\in SL(3),r\in[\frac{-1}{2},1]}\mu^{L}(g\cdot(D,q_{1},\dots,q_{n}),\lambda_{r})\leq 0.

We conclude that the maximum value of

. If

At this point we have done all the leg work for Theorem

Theorem 3.2
.

The cone
\Lambda(\mathcal{C}_{n,d})
is

\displaystyle\bigg\{(\gamma,w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})\;\bigg|\;w_{i}\leq\frac{W+\gamma(2d-3)}{3},\ w_{i}\leq\frac{W+\gamma(d-2)}{2},\ w_{i}+w_{j}\leq\frac{2W+\gamma d}{3},\ 0<w_{i},\ 0<\gamma\bigg\},

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Let
(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
be sufficiently general in the sense that the
p_{i}
are distinct; no point lies on a flex line of
C

is equal to the locus of line bundles for which

is semi-stable. In Theorem
2.16
we computed this to be given by three families inequalities:
3w_{i}-\sum_{1}^{k}w_{k}+3\gamma-2\gamma d\leq 0;

2w_{i}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{k}-\gamma d+2\gamma\leq 0;
and
3w_{i}+3w_{j}-2\sum_{1}^{n}w_{k}-\gamma d\leq 0
, for each pair
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. We solve for
w_{i}
in the first two inequalities and for
w_{i}+w_{j}
in the final inequality to get stability conditions for the weights of individual points.

∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Any general curve
(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
as in Theorem
2.16
is stable on the interior of
\Lambda(\mathcal{C}_{n,d})
.

Proof.

This is because the maximal values of
\mu^{L}(g\cdot(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n}),\lambda_{r})
as computed in Theorem
2.16
are negative on the interior of the
\Lambda(\mathcal{C}_{n,d})
.
∎

4.
Finding the inner walls

We now compute the
inner walls
of the cone
\Lambda(\mathcal{C}_{n,d})
. The following lemma associates to each inner wall a marked plane curve with positive-dimensional
SL(3)
stabilizer.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
(D,q_{1},\dots,q_{n})
is strictly semi-stable with respect to
L
then there exists a marked curve
(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
with positive dimensional stabilizer in the orbit closure
\overline{SL(3)\cdot(D,q_{1},\dots,q_{n})}
which is strictly semi-stable with respect to
L
.

Proof.

Suppose
(D,q_{1},\dots,q_{n})
is strictly semi-stable with respect to
L
. Since
(D,q_{1},\dots,q_{n})
is not stable, it either has an open orbit in
\mathcal{C}_{n,d}^{ss}(L)
or it has positive dimensional stabilizer. There exists a unique closed orbit
O
in
\overline{SL(3)\cdot(D,q_{1},\dots,q_{n})}
. Let
(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
be a representative of
O
. Then since
(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
has a closed orbit and is strictly semi-stable at
L
it must have a positive dimensional stabilizer.
∎

Due to Lemma
4.1
, to find all the GIT walls it suffices to find the line bundles in
\Lambda(\mathcal{C}_{n,d})
at which a marked curve with positive-dimensional stabilizer is strictly semi-stable. Such marked curves were classified in Section
2.4
.

We begin with the case of three non-concurrent lines, that is, a curve projectively equivalent to
\{xyz=0\}
. Lemma
2.22
shows that if
(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
has positive-dimensional stabilizer and
C
is three non-concurrent lines then the marked points are either at a node
\eta
of
C
or on the opposite linear component
\Lambda\subset C
.

Lemma 4.2

(Case of
C(3A_{1})
)
.

Let
\mathbf{x}:=(C,p_{1},\dots p_{n})
have positive dimensional stabilizer, where the curve
C
is three lines in general position. Let
\eta
and
\Lambda
be as above. Define
I
to be the subset of
\{1,\dots,n\}
such that the points
p_{i}
coincide at
\eta\in C
for
i\in I
and the remaining points lie on the opposite line
\Lambda\subset C
. Then
\mathbf{x}
is
L
strictly semi-stable if and only if
\sum_{i\in I}w_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\notin I}w_{j}
, the total weight at each of the other nodes is at most
\frac{1}{3}\sum_{1}^{n}w_{i}
, and the weight at any smooth point of
\Lambda
is at most
\frac{1}{3}\sum_{1}^{n}w_{i}+\gamma
.

Proof.

We solve
\max\{\mu^{L}(g\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{r})\ |\ g\in SL(3),r\in[\frac{-1}{2},1]\}=0
for
L
to find the line bundles at which the marked curve is strictly semi-stable. As discussed in Section 2.3 our method is to identify the
g\in SL(3)
which maximize
\Xi_{max}(g\cdot\mathbf{x})
because such
g
will also maximize
\mu^{L}(g\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{r})
.

There are six cases. First we consider the
g\in SL(3)
which take the node
\eta
to
(1:0:0)
. Under this assumption there are two ways to maximize
\Xi_{max}(g\cdot\mathbf{x})
. A nodal singularity of a plane cubic at the origin
(1:0:0)
of the affine chart
U_{x=1}
is characterized by the Jacobian Criterion and the fact that the tangent cone has two distinct rays. That is, if
f
is a polynomial with
V(f)=g\cdot C
then
f(1,y,z)=ay^{2}+byz+cz^{2}+h_{3}(y,z)
, where
ay^{2}+byz+cz^{2}
is not a perfect square and
h_{3}(y,z)
is a homogeneous cubic. If
g_{1}
takes the line through
\eta
and one of the other nodes
\eta^{\prime}
to
\{z=0\}
then
\Xi_{max}(g_{1}\cdot C)
is
\{xyz\}
. This is because one of the tangent lines at
(1:0:0)
will be
\{z=0\}
, so
g_{1}\cdot C
is not supported on
x^{3},x^{2}y,
or
x^{2}z
by the Jacobian criterion and it must have
xyz
but not
xy^{2}
in its support to have
\{z=0\}
and one other line in its tangent cone at the origin. In this case
\Xi_{min}(g_{1}\cdot p_{i})=\{x\}
for
p_{i}=\eta
,
\Xi_{min}(g_{1}\cdot p_{j})=\{y\}
for
p_{j}=\eta^{\prime}
, and
\Xi_{min}(g_{1}\cdot p_{k})=\{z\}
for the remaining points. The other maxima of
\Xi_{max}(g\cdot\mathbf{x})
when
g\cdot\eta=(1:0:0)
correspond to
g_{2}\in SL(3)
which take the line through
\eta
and
p_{j}
to
\{z=0\}
where
p_{j}
is a smooth point on
\Lambda
at which the most weight is concentrated. Then
\Xi_{max}(g_{2}\cdot C)=\{xy^{2}\}
because the line
\{z=0\}
is not in the tangent cone at
(1:0:0)
.

Next, assume that
g\in SL(3)
does not take
\eta
to
(1:0:0)
. Under this assumption there are four ways to maximize
\Xi_{max}(g\cdot\mathbf{x})
as follows: In all cases,
\Xi_{max}(g\cdot\mathbf{x})
is maximized when
g
takes a node
\eta^{\prime}
or one of the points
p_{j}\in\Lambda
to
(1:0:0)
. If
g_{3}
takes
\eta^{\prime}
to
(1:0:0)
and the line
\overline{\eta\eta^{\prime}}
to
\{z=0\}
then
\Xi_{max}(g_{3}\cdot C)=\{xyz\}
, similar to
g_{1}\cdot C
above. If
g_{4}
takes
\eta^{\prime}
to
(1:0:0)
and
\Lambda
to
\{z=0\}
then
\Xi_{max}(g_{4}\cdot C)
is also
\{xyz\}
although the minimal supports of the points changes. Let
p_{j}
be a smooth point on
\Lambda
at which the most weight is concentrated. If
g_{5}
takes
p_{j}
to
(1:0:0)
and the line
\overline{p_{j}\eta}
to
\{z=0\}
then, by the Jacobian Criterion and the fact that
\{z=0\}
is not tangent to
C
at
(1:0:0)
,
\Xi_{max}(g_{5}\cdot C)
is
\{x^{2}y\}
. Finally, if
g_{6}
takes
p_{j}
to
(1:0:0)
and
\Lambda
to
\{z=0\}
then
\Xi_{max}(g_{6}\cdot C)
is
\{x^{2}z\}
.

We abbreviate
\sum_{p_{i}=\eta}w_{i}
as
w_{\eta}
,
\sum_{p_{i}=\eta^{\prime}}w_{i}
as
w_{\eta^{\prime}}
,
\sum_{p_{i}\in\Lambda}w_{i}
as
w_{\Lambda}
, and
\sum_{p_{i}=p_{j}}w_{i}
, where
p_{j}
is the smooth point on
\Lambda
at which the most weight is concentrated, as
w_{J}
. The maximal values of
\mu^{L}(g\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{r})
, corresponding to the maximal values of
\Xi_{max}(g\cdot\mathbf{x})
(Lemma
2.14
) found above, are:

•

\mu^{L}(g_{1}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{r})=w_{\eta}+rw_{\eta^{\prime}}+(-1-r)(w_{\Lambda}-w_{\eta^{\prime}})
, which is maximized in
r
by

\displaystyle F_{1}:=\mu^{L}(g_{1}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{\frac{-1}{2}})=w_{\eta}-\frac{1}{2}w_{\Lambda}

and

\displaystyle F_{2}:=\mu^{L}(g_{1}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{1})=w_{\eta}+3w_{\eta}^{\prime}-w_{\Lambda}.

•

\mu^{L}(g_{2}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{r})=w_{\eta}+rw_{J}+(-1-r)(w_{\Lambda}-w_{J})-\gamma(1+2r),
which is maximized in
r
by

\displaystyle F_{3}:=\mu^{L}(g_{2}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{\frac{-1}{2}})=w_{\eta}-\frac{1}{2}w_{\Lambda}

and

\displaystyle F_{4}:=\mu^{L}(g_{2}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{1})=w_{\eta}-2w_{\Lambda}+3w_{J}-3\gamma.

•

\mu^{L}(g_{3}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{r})=w_{\eta^{\prime}}+rw_{\eta}+(-1-r)(w_{\Lambda}-w_{\eta^{\prime}}),
which is maximized in
r
by

\displaystyle F_{5}:=\mu^{L}(g_{3}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{\frac{-1}{2}})=\frac{3}{2}w_{\eta^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}w_{\eta}-\frac{1}{2}w_{\Lambda}

and

\displaystyle F_{6}:=\mu^{L}(g_{3}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{1})=3w_{\eta^{\prime}}+w_{\eta}-2w_{\Lambda}.

•

\mu^{L}(g_{4}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{r})=w_{\eta^{\prime}}+r(w_{\Lambda}-w_{\eta^{\prime}})+(-1-r)w_{\eta},

which is maximized in
r
by

\displaystyle F_{7}:=\mu^{L}(g_{4}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{\frac{-1}{2}})=\frac{3}{2}w_{\eta^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}w_{\eta}-\frac{1}{2}w_{\Lambda}

and

\displaystyle F_{8}:=\mu^{L}(g_{4}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{1})=w_{\Lambda}-2w_{\eta}.

•

\mu^{L}(g_{5}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{r})=w_{J}+rw_{\eta}+(-1-r)(w_{\Lambda}-w_{J})-\gamma(2+r)

which is maximized in
r
by

\displaystyle F_{9}:=\mu^{L}(g_{5}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{\frac{-1}{2}})=\frac{3}{2}w_{J}-\frac{1}{2}w_{\Lambda}-\frac{3}{2}\gamma-\frac{1}{2}w_{\eta}

and

\displaystyle F_{10}:=\mu^{L}(g_{5}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{1})=3w_{J}-2w_{\Lambda}-3\gamma+w_{\eta}.

•

\mu^{L}(g_{6}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{r})=w_{J}+r(w_{\Lambda}-w_{J})+(-1-r)w_{\eta}-\gamma(1-r)

which is maximized in
r
by

\displaystyle F_{11}:=\mu^{L}(g_{6}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{\frac{-1}{2}})=\frac{3}{2}w_{J}-\frac{1}{2}w_{\eta}-\frac{3}{2}\gamma-\frac{1}{2}w_{\Lambda}

and

\displaystyle F_{12}:=\mu^{L}(g_{6}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{1})=-2w_{\eta}+w_{\Lambda}.

To solve for
\max\mu^{L}(g\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{r})=\max F_{i}(\gamma,w_{1},\dots,w_{n})=0
notice that
F_{8}=-2F_{1}
. Since these functions are both non-positive they must both be zero. This gives the wall
w_{\eta}=\frac{1}{2}w_{\Lambda}
. The other strict semi-stability conditions,
w_{\eta^{\prime}}\leq\frac{1}{3}(w_{\eta}+w_{\Lambda})
and
w_{J}\leq\frac{1}{3}(w_{\eta}+w_{\Lambda})+\gamma
are attained by evaluating the remaining inequalities
F_{i}\leq 0
at
w_{\eta}=\frac{1}{2}w_{\Lambda}
.

∎

Lemma 4.3

(Case of
C(2A_{1})
)
.

Let
\mathbf{x}:=(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
have positive dimensional stabilizer, where
C
is the union of a smooth conic and a transverse line. Then
\mathbf{x}
is unstable for all line bundles
L
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.20
we know the marked points
p_{i}
are at one of the two nodes of
C
where the conic intersects the line. Denote the two nodes
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
. There is a matrix
g\in SL(3)
which takes
\eta
to
(1:0:0)
and the line
\overline{\eta\eta^{\prime}}
to
\{z=0\}
. Then the maximal support of
g\cdot C
is
\{xyz\}
because
g\cdot C
has a nodal singularity at
(1:0:0)
with the line
\{z=0\}
in the tangent cone. This gives

\mu^{L}(g\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{r})=\sum_{p_{i}=\eta}w_{i}+r(\sum_{p_{j}=\eta^{\prime}}w_{j}).

This is positive for
r=1
, so
\mathbf{x}
is unstable.

∎

Next we consider the cuspidal curve. If
(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
is a marked plane cubic with positive dimensional stabilizer and
C
has a cusp, or
A_{2}
singularity, then Lemma
2.19
implies that the marked points
p_{i}
must either be at the cuspidal singularity or at the unique smooth inflection point of
C
.

Lemma 4.4

(Case of
C(A_{2})
)
.

Let
\mathbf{x}:=(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
have positive dimensional stabilizer, where
C
is a cuspidal plane cubic. Define
I
to be the subset of
\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
p_{i}
coincides with the cuspidal singularity of
C
for
i\in I
. The complement
I^{c}
indexes points
p_{j}
coinciding with the smooth flex point. Then
\mathbf{x}
is
L
strictly semi-stable if and only if
\gamma=\frac{4}{3}\sum_{j\notin I}w_{j}-\frac{5}{3}\sum_{i\in I}w_{i}
and
\sum_{i\in I}w_{i}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\notin I}w_{j}
.

Proof.

We follow the same method as in Lemma
4.2
by solving
\max\{\mu^{L}(g\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{r})\}=0
for
L
, though we proceed more concisely. We mention two relevant geometric facts. First, if
f(x,y,z)
is a homogeneous cubic polynomial and
V(f)
contains a cusp at
(1:0:0)
then the tangent cone at
(1:0:0)
is a line of the form
\{ay+bz=0\}
. This, together with the Jacobian Criterion implies that
f(x,y,z)=x(ay+bz)^{2}+h_{3}(y,z)
for some homogeneous cubic
h
. Conversely, if
f
has this form then
V(f)
has a non-nodal singularity at
(1:0:0)
. Secondly we note that a curve
f(x,y,z)
has a flex point at
(1:0:0)
with flex line
\{z=0\}
if and only if
f(x,y,z)=ay^{3}+zh_{2}(x,y,z)
for some
a\neq 0
and
h_{2}
homogeneous of degree
2
.

Assume
\mathbf{x}
is
L
semi-stable. Let
p_{c}
be the cuspidal singular point of
C
and
p_{f}
be the smooth flex point. If
g\in SL(3)
does not take either
p_{c}
or
p_{f}
to
(1:0:0)
then
\mu^{L}(g\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{r})
is negative for all
L
in
\Lambda(\mathcal{C}_{n,d})
and
r\in[\frac{-1}{2},1]
. Therefore, if
\mu^{L}(g\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{r})=0
then
g
must take either
p_{c}
or
p_{f}
to
(1:0:0)
. There are four subsets of
SL(3)
which maximize
\Xi_{max}(g\cdot\mathbf{x}).
If
g_{1}
takes
p_{c}
to
(1:0:0)
and the tangent cone at
p_{c}
to the line
\{z=0\}
then
\Xi_{max}(g_{1}\cdot C)=\{y^{3},xz^{2}\}
. If
g_{2}
takes
p_{f}
to
(1:0:0)
and the flex line to
\{z=0\}
then
\Xi_{max}(g_{2}\cdot C)=\{y^{3},x^{2},z\}
. If
g_{3}
takes
p_{c}
to
(1:0:0)
and the line
\overline{p_{c}p_{f}}
to
\{z=0\}
then
\Xi_{max}(g_{3}\cdot C)=\{xy^{2}\}
. And if
g_{4}
takes
p_{f}
to
(1:0:0)
and the line
\overline{p_{c}p_{f}}
to
\{z=0\}
then
\Xi_{max}(g_{4}\cdot C)=\{x^{2}y\}
.

We abbreviate
\sum_{p_{i}=p_{c}}w_{i}
as
w_{c}
and
\sum_{p_{j}=p_{f}}w_{j}
as
w_{f}
. Then the maximal values of
\mu^{L}(g\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{r})
are

•

\mu^{L}(g_{1}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{r})=w_{c}+(-1-r)w_{f}-\gamma\max\{3r,-1-2r\},
which is maximized in
r
by

\displaystyle F_{1}:=\mu^{L}(g_{1}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{\frac{-1}{5}})=w_{c}-\frac{4}{5}w_{f}+\frac{3}{5}\gamma

and

\displaystyle F_{2}:=\mu^{L}(g_{1}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{\frac{-1}{2}})=w_{c}-\frac{1}{2}w_{f}

•

\mu^{L}(g_{2}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{r})=w_{f}+(-1-r)w_{c}-\gamma\max\{3r,1-r\}

which is maximized in
r
by

\displaystyle F_{3}:=\mu^{L}(g_{1}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{\frac{1}{4}})=w_{f}-\frac{5}{4}w_{c}-\frac{3}{4}\gamma

and

\displaystyle F_{4}:=\mu^{L}(g_{1}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{\frac{-1}{2}})=w_{f}-\frac{1}{2}w_{c}-\frac{3}{2}\gamma.

•

\mu^{L}(g_{3}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{r})=w_{c}+rw_{f}-\gamma(1+2r)

which is maximized in
r
by

\displaystyle F_{5}:=\mu^{L}(g_{1}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{\frac{-1}{2}})=w_{c}-\frac{1}{2}w_{f}

and

\displaystyle F_{6}:=\mu^{L}(g_{1}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{1})=w_{c}+w_{f}-3\gamma.

•

\mu^{L}(g_{4}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{r})=w_{f}+rw_{c}-\gamma(2+r)

which is maximized in
r
by

\displaystyle F_{7}:=\mu^{L}(g_{1}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{\frac{-1}{2}})=w_{f}-\frac{1}{2}w_{c}-\frac{3}{2}\gamma

and

\displaystyle F_{8}:=\mu^{L}(g_{1}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{1})=w_{f}+w_{c}-3\gamma.

We solve
\max{F_{i}}=0
for
(\gamma,w_{1},\dots,w_{n}).
Since
F_{1}=\frac{-4}{5}F_{3}
they must both be zero. This gives the wall
\gamma=\frac{4}{3}w_{f}-\frac{5}{3}w_{c}
, and the remaining inequalities evaluated at this wall become
w_{c}\leq\frac{1}{2}w_{f}.

∎

In the case of the union of a conic and a tangent line with positive dimensional stabilizer, Lemma
2.21
tells us that the marked points are either at the intersection of the conic and the tangent line, or on one other line tangent to the conic.

Lemma 4.5

(Case of
C(A_{3})
)
.

Let
\mathbf{x}:=(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n})

. The remaining marked points coincide with the point on the conic

\mathbf{x}
is

\sum_{k\notin I\cup J}w_{k}=\sum_{i\in I}w_{i}+\gamma

\sum_{i\in I}w_{i}-\gamma\leq\sum_{j\in J}w_{j}\leq\sum_{i\in I}w_{i}+2\gamma

\mathbf{x}
is unstable for all line bundles
L
. This can be seen by letting

take the tacnode to
(1:0:0)

\{z=0\}
. Then
\mu^{L}(g\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{1})=\sum_{1}^{n}w_{i}

where
\Lambda
is a line

B

4.2
by solving
\max\{\mu^{L}(g\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{r})\ |\ g\in SL(3),\ r\in[\frac{-1}{2},1]\}=0

. There are six subsets of

.

(1:0:0)
. Let

be the tacnode,
p_{a}
the marked point on the conic,
p_{b}
the marked point on the linear component of

, and
\Lambda
the line through
p_{a}
and
p_{b}
. If

takes
p_{t}
to
(1:0:0)
and

to
\{z=0\}
then
\Xi_{max}(g\cdot C)=\{xz^{2},y^{2}z\}
. If
g_{2}
takes
p_{b}
to
(1:0:0)
and
B
to
\{z=0\}
then
\Xi_{max}(g_{2}\cdot\mathbf{x})=\{x^{2}z,xy^{2}\}
. If
g_{3}
takes
p_{b}
to
(1:0:0)
and
\Lambda
to
\{z=0\}
then
\Xi_{max}(g_{3}\cdot\mathbf{x})=\{x^{2}y\}
. If
g_{4}
takes
p_{a}
to
(1:0:0)
and
\Lambda
to
\{z=0\}
then
\Xi_{max}(g_{4}\cdot\mathbf{x})=\{x^{2}z,xy^{2}\}
. If
g_{5}
takes
p_{t}
to
(1:0:0)
and the line
\overline{p_{a}p_{t}}
to
\{z=0\}
then
\Xi_{max}(g_{5}\cdot\mathbf{x})=\{xy^{2}\}
. Finally, if
g_{6}
takes
p_{a}
to
(1:0:0)
and the line
\overline{p_{a}p_{t}}
to
\{z=0\}
then
\Xi_{max}(g_{6}\cdot\mathbf{x})=\{x^{2}y\}
.

We abbreviate
\sum_{p_{i}=p_{t}}w_{i}
as
w_{t}
,
\sum_{p_{j}=p_{b}}w_{j}
as
w_{b}
, and
\sum_{p_{k}=p_{a}}w_{k}
as
w_{a}
. Then the maximal values of
\mu^{L}(g\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{r})
are

•

\mu^{L}(g_{1}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{r})=w_{t}+rw_{b}+(-1-r)w_{a}-\gamma\max\{-2-r,-1+r\}
, which is maximized in

by

\displaystyle F_{1}:=\mu^{L}(g_{1}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{1})=p_{t}-2p_{a}+p_{b}

and

\displaystyle F_{2}:=\mu^{L}(g_{1}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{0})=p_{t}-p_{a}+\gamma

\displaystyle\text{and}\ \ F_{3}:=\mu^{L}(g_{1}\cdot\mathbf{x},\lambda_{\frac{-1}{2}})=p_{t}-\frac{p_{a}}{2}-\frac{p_{b}}{2}.

S(T,I,\pm)
are dense in the exceptional loci.

∎

We recall Radu Laza’s Thesis
[
16
]
in which he constructs a series of compactifications of the moduli space of

consisting of a cubic curve and a line in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
by taking the VGIT quotients of
\mathbb{P}(\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{O}(3)))\times\mathbb{P}(\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{O}(1)))
by
SL(3)
, denoted
M^{1,3}_{pairs}(t)
. He finds a VGIT chamber corresponding to the line bundle parameter
t=\frac{3}{2}-\epsilon
such that
M^{1,3}_{pairs}(\frac{3}{2}-\epsilon)
is the moduli space of pairs
(C,L)
where
C
has at worst isolated singularities of type
A_{k}
and
L
is a line intersecting
C
transversely. Laza then considers the moduli spaces
M^{(1,3)lab}_{pairs}(t)
of pairs of a plane cubic and a line, with labeled intersection. He uses a classical construction to prove that
M^{(1,3)lab}_{pairs}(\frac{3}{2}-\epsilon)
is the coarse moduli space for cubic surfaces containing a marked Eckardt point and at worst
A_{k}
singularities.
In our case, there is a chamber represented by
\mathbf{w}
giving rise to a compact moduli space of plane cubics with two marked points
\overline{M}^{git}_{1,\mathbf{w}}
, which is isomorphic to

and hence isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}(1,2,2,3)

[
16

Corollary 6.3
.

There exists an isomorphism

\phi^{lab}:\mathcal{C}_{2,3}^{s}/\penalty_{L}SL(3)\rightarrow M^{(1,3)lab}_{pairs}\left(\frac{3}{2}-\epsilon\right)\cong\mathbb{P}(1,2,2,3)

where
L
is a line bundle corresponding to a vector
\mathbf{w}
in the GIT chamber
\{w_{1}>1,w_{2}>1,w_{1}+w_{2}<3\}\subset\Delta(\mathcal{C}_{2,3})
.

Proof of Corollary
6.3
.

First we construct a morphism
\psi:\mathcal{C}_{2,3}^{s}(L)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}(\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{O}(3)))\times\mathbb{P}(\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{O}(1)))
by associating to a marked cubic
(C,p_{1},p_{2})
the degree
3
pair
(C,\overline{p_{1}p_{2}}).
This can be done explicitly: if
p_{1}=(x_{1}:y_{1}:z_{1})
and
p_{2}=(x_{2}:y_{2}:z_{2})
then the line
\overline{p_{1}p_{2}}
is given by the equation
\{(x_{2}z_{1}-x_{1}z_{2})(z_{1}y-y_{1}z)=(y_{2}z_{1}-y_{1}z_{2})(z_{1}x-x_{1}z)\}
. Suppose
(C,p_{1},p_{2})
is
L
(semi-)stable. Since
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
are greater than
1
, the stability condition associated to the walls
W(A_{3},\emptyset,\{i\})
imply that neither point
p_{i}
can lie on a line that is tangent to the curve
C
at the other point
p_{j}
. We also know from the wall-crossing behavior associated to the walls
W(3A_{1},\{i\})
and
W(A_{2},\{i\})
that neither of the points
p_{i}
may coincide with a singularity of
C
. Therefore, the
L
semi-stability of
(C,p_{1},p_{2})
implies that
C
is a cubic curve only containing singularities of type
A_{k}
with
k\in\{1,2,3\}
and that the line
\overline{p_{1}p_{2}}
always intersects
C
transversely at three distinct, smooth points. Recalling Laza’s GIT analysis
[
15
, Theorem 3.2]
, this implies that the degree
3
pair
(C,\overline{p_{1}p_{2}})
is
\frac{3}{2}-\epsilon
semi-stable.

Since the image of
\psi
is contained in
\big(\mathbb{P}(\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{O}(3)))\times\mathbb{P}(\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{O}(1)))\big)^{ss}(\frac{3}{2}-\epsilon),
we can restrict to this codomain and then compose with the quotient map to get a morphism
\psi^{\prime}:\mathcal{C}_{2,3}^{ss}(L)\rightarrow M^{1,3}_{pairs}(\frac{3}{2}-\epsilon).
This map
\psi^{\prime}
is clearly
SL(3)
invariant: if
(C,p_{1},p_{2})=g\cdot(D,q_{1},q_{2})
then
(C,\overline{p_{1}p_{2}})=g\cdot(D,\overline{p_{1},p_{2}})
, so their image under
\psi^{\prime}
is the same orbit. Then, by the universal property of
\pi:\mathcal{C}_{2,3}^{s}(L)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{2,3}^{s}/\penalty_{L}SL(3)
as a quotient, there is a morphism
\phi:\mathcal{C}_{2,3}^{s}/\penalty_{L}SL(3)\rightarrow M^{1,3}_{pairs}(\frac{3}{2}-\epsilon)
such that
\psi^{\prime}
factors as
\phi\circ\pi.

The above map is 6:1 because for a generic degree
3
pair
(C,L)
there are
3\cdot 2
choices of ordered pairs of points in the set
C\cap L
that map to
(C,L)
. However, we recall from Laza’s construction (Lemma 3.13 in
[
15
]
) that we can label the
3
points of intersection in
C\cap L
to obtain an isomorphism from the moduli space
M^{(1,3)lab}_{pairs}(\frac{3}{2}-\epsilon)\cong\mathbb{P}(1,2,2,3)
of
degree 3 pairs with labeled intersections
to the moduli space of cubic surfaces with a marked Eckardt point.

We lift
\phi
to
\phi^{lab}:\mathcal{C}_{2,3}^{s}/\penalty_{L}SL(3)\rightarrow M^{(1,3)lab}_{pairs}(\frac{3}{2}-\epsilon)
by ordering the three intersections in
\phi((C,p_{1},p_{2}))
as
(p_{1},p_{2},q)
where
q
is the third intersection of
C
with
\overline{p_{1}p_{2}}
. We observe that
\phi^{lab}
is a birational morphism, bijective on closed points. Its target is the weighted projective space
M^{(1,3)lab}_{pairs}(\frac{3}{2}-\epsilon)\cong\mathbb{P}(1,2,2,3)
, which is normal. A form of Zariski’s Main Theorem
[
11
, Corollary 12.88]

then implies
\phi^{lab}
is an isomorphism.
∎

Remark 6.4
.

We do not expect the map
(C,p_{1},p_{2})\mapsto(C,\overline{p_{1}p_{2}})
to extend when using other line bundles in the moduli of plane cubics and two points. Indeed, for every other pair of chambers
X
and
Y
in the linearization polytopes of
\mathcal{C}_{2,3}
and degree
3
pairs respectively, we can find a tuple
(C,p_{1},p_{2})
which is stable with respect to
X
, but for which
(C,\overline{p_{1}p_{2}})
is unstable with respect to
Y
.

6.2.
Plane Cubics and
M_{1,n}

It is well known that a smooth cubic curve
V(f)
has nine
inflection points, which can be obtained by intersecting the vanishing locus of
f
and the vanishing locus of its Hessian determinant
He(f)
. To form the parameter space for cubic plane curves marked with
n

points and an inflection point, we define a subvariety of the product of
\mathbb{P}(\Gamma(\mathcal{O}(3),\mathbb{P}^{2}))
with
n+1
copies of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
:

\mathcal{C}_{n,d}^{\prime}:=\{(f,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n+1})\in\mathbb{P}^{9}\times(\mathbb{P}^{2})^{n+1}\;|\;f(p_{i})=0,\;He(f)(p_{n+1)})=0\}.

\mathcal{C}_{n,3}^{\prime}
is therefore a closed subscheme of
\mathcal{C}_{n+1,3}
and by the Hilbert-Mumford numerical criterion, a marked curve
(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n})\in\mathcal{C}_{n,3}^{\prime}
is
L
(semi-)stable if and only if it is
L
(semi-)stable in
\mathcal{C}_{n+1,3}
. We therefore have the following commuting square:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{n,3}^{{}^{\prime}ss}(L)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{n+1,3}^{ss}(L)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{n,3}^{\prime}\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}_{L}SL(3)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{n+1,3}\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}_{L}SL(3).}

Corollary 6.5
.

M_{1,n+1}
is isomorphic to an open subset of
\mathcal{C}_{n,3}^{{}^{\prime}s}/\penalty_{L}SL(3)
for some
SL(3)-
linearized line bundle
L
.

Proof of Theorem
6.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{n,3}^{\prime\circ}\subset\mathcal{C}_{n,d}
be the open locus of smooth plane cubics marked with
n
points and an additional inflection point which are all distinct. For
0<\epsilon\ll 1
, the entirety of
\mathcal{C}_{n,3}^{{}^{\prime}\circ}
is
(1,\epsilon,\dots,\epsilon)
stable by Theorem
5.2
. Let
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathcal{C}_{n,3}^{{}^{\prime}\circ}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{n,3}^{{}^{\prime}\circ}
be the trivial
\mathbb{P}^{2}
bundle. Let
B\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathcal{C}_{n,3}^{{}^{\prime}\circ}
be the family of curves over
\mathcal{C}_{n,3}^{{}^{\prime}\circ}
whose fiber over
(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n+1})
is the curve
C
in the plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\{(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n+1})\}
. The family of genus
1
curves
B
and the
n+1
global sections given by the distinct marked points define a map
\phi:\mathcal{C}_{n,3}^{{}^{\prime}\circ}\rightarrow M_{1,n+1}
. If
(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n+1})
and
(D,q_{1},\dots,q_{n+1})
are projectively equivalent then their images under
\phi
are isomorphic marked curves. Therefore
\phi
factors through the categorical quotient
\mathcal{C}_{n,3}^{{}^{\prime}\circ}/\penalty_{L}SL(3)
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathcal{C}_{n,3}^{{}^{\prime}\circ}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{n,3}^{{}^{\prime}\circ}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{1,n+1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{n,3}^{{}^{\prime}\circ}/\penalty_{L}SL(3)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\phi}}

We show that
\overline{\phi}
is an isomorphism by showing that it is a surjective birational morphism and invoking Zariski’s Main Theorem. Let
(E,a_{1},\dots,a_{n+1})\in M_{1,n+1}
be a marked elliptic curve. The linear system
|3a_{n+1}|
induces an embedding
\psi_{|3a_{n+1}|}
of
(E,a_{1},\dots,a_{n+1})
into the plane as a marked cubic curve such that the image of
a_{n+1}
is an inflection point and
\overline{\phi}(\psi_{|3a_{n+1}|}(E),\psi_{|3a_{n+1}|}(a_{1}),\dots,\psi_{|3a_{n+1}|}(a_{n+1}))\cong(E,a_{1},\dots,a_{n+1}).
Therefore
\overline{\phi}
is surjective. Now suppose
\overline{\phi}(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n})=\overline{\phi}(D,q_{1},\dots,q_{n}).
Then as abstract marked curves
(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n+1})
is isomorphic to
(D,q_{1},\dots,q_{n+1})
. If the
j
-invariant of
C\cong D
is not
0
or
1728
then there are exactly two automorphisms of
D
fixing
q_{n+1}

[
12
, Corollary IV.4.7]
. It follows that there are exactly two isomorphisms
(C,p_{n+1})\cong(D,q_{n+1}).
We also know that any plane cubic has two linear automorphisms fixing any inflection point: Let
g\in SL(3)
take
(D,q_{n+1})
to its Weierstrass form
y^{2}z=x^{3}+axz^{2}+bz^{3}
such that
g\cdot(q_{n+1})=(0:1:0).
Then reflection across the line
y=0
is a linear automorphism of
g(D,q_{n+1})
given by the matrix
\text{diag}\{1,-1,1\}
. This shows that the abstract isomorphism
(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n+1})\cong(D,q_{1},\dots,q_{n+1})
is one of the two isomorphisms sending
(C,p_{n+1})
to
(D,q_{n+1})
, both of which are projective equivalences. Therefore
(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n+1})
is projectively equivalent to
(D,q_{1},\dots,q_{n+1})
, so they are the same point of
\mathcal{C}_{n,d}^{{}^{\prime}\circ}/\penalty SL(3).
Then
\overline{\phi}
is injective on the locus of curves whose
j-
invariant is not
0
or
1728
. Since
\overline{\phi}
is a morphism to a normal complex variety which is bijective on an open subset, it is a birational morphism
[
22
]
.

Then, because
M_{g,n}
is normal and
\overline{\phi}
is a birational surjection with finite fibers, it follows from Zariski’s Main Theorem
[
11
, Corollary 12.88]
that
\overline{\phi}
is in fact an isomorphism.
∎

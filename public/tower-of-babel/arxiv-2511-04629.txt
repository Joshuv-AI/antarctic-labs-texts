Pair-mixing induced Time-reversal-breaking superconductivity
Saswata Mandal, Chao-Xing Liu ∗

Abstract
Experimental evidences of spontaneous time-reversal (TR) symmetry breaking have been reported for the superconducting ground state in the transition metal dichalcogenide (TMD) superconductor 4H b -TaS 2 or chiral molecule intercalated TaS 2 hybrid superlattices, and is regarded as the evidence of the emergent chiral superconductivity. However, the T c T_{c} of these TMD superconductors is at the same order as pristine 1H or 2H-TaS 2 that does not show any signature of TR breaking and is believed to be conventional Bardeen–Cooper–Schrieffer superconductors. To resolve this puzzle, we proposed a new type of pair-mixing states that mix the dominant conventional s-wave pairing channel with the subdominant chiral p-wave pairing channel via the finite Cooper pair momentum based on the symmetry analysis of the Ginzburg-Landau theory. Our analysis shows the fourth order terms in the chiral p-wave channel can lead to a variety of pair-mixing states with spontaneous TR breaking. These TR-breaking superconducting states also reveal zero-field junction-free superconducting diode effect that is observed in chiral molecule intercalated TaS 2 superlattices.

Conclusion and Discussion -

In this work, we proposed a pair-mixing mechanism for unconventional superconductivity with spontaneous TR breaking. This TR breaking SC phase is featured by a finite Cooper pair momentum and can be probed through the zero-field junction-free SDE. Although our theory is formulated for the 2D TaS
2
monolayer sandwiched between two chiral layers in Fig.
1
(a), we would expect similar physics occurs in bulk 4Hb-TaS
2

64
and chiral molecule intercalated TaS
2
hybrid superlattices
80
once different 1H TaS
2
layers in the bulk are decoupled, revealing quasi-2D SC behaviors. We also noticed that
\pi
-phase shift in Little-Parks experiments has also been observed in both TaS
2
systems
5
;
80
, providing another evidence of TR breaking, similar to that in superconductor/ferromagnet hetero-junctions
65
;
18
;
13
. The TR-breaking vortex-antivortex phase has also recently been proposed in rhombohedral graphene
22
;
31
. However, there is one substantial difference. TR-breaking in rhombohedral graphene is from orbital magnetism and already exists above
T_{c}
, while TR-breaking only occurs spontaneously below
T_{c}
in our pair-mixing mechanism. This pair-mixing mechanism also provides a new path for realizing TR breaking superconductivity via engineering the local chemical environment of conventional SC layers.

Appendix C
Supercurrent and SC Diode Effect

In this section, we will calculate the supercurrent and study the SC diode effect for this pair-mixing system. We first apply the generic expression of supercurrent in Eq.(
256
) to our particular ground states in the momentum space. We can characterize the motion of the Cooper pairs in the supercurrent with superfluid momentum
78

\mathbf{q}_{s}=\frac{\hbar}{2(\gamma_{A}+\gamma_{E})}\mathbf{v}_{s}
, where
\mathbf{v}_{s}
is the superfluid velocity and
\gamma_{A}+\gamma_{E}
gives the effective mass of Cooper pairs. The superfluid velocity should be included in the kinetic energy terms of the GL free energy when a supercurrent is driven through a bulk superconductor, and thus we should change the free energy in Eq.(
101
) to

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\equiv\mathcal{F}(\{\mathbf{Q}_{j}\},\{P_{\mathbf{Q}_{j}}\})\to\mathcal{F}(\{\mathbf{Q}_{j}+\mathbf{q}_{s}\},\{P_{\mathbf{Q}_{j}+\mathbf{q}_{s}}\}).

(265)

The corresponding supercurrent is given by
91

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}_{s}(\mathbf{q}_{s})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{d\mathcal{F}(\{\mathbf{Q}_{j}+\mathbf{q}_{s}-2e\mathbf{A}\},\{\alpha,\phi\})}{d\mathbf{A}}\Bigg|_{\mathbf{A}=0}

(266)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Bigg[\sum\limits_{\mathbf{Q}_{j}\in\mathcal{Q}_{6}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}[\{\mathbf{Q}_{j}+\mathbf{q}_{s}\},\{\alpha,\phi\}]}{\partial\alpha_{j}}\frac{d\alpha_{j}}{d\mathbf{A}}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}[\{\mathbf{Q}_{j}+\mathbf{q}_{s}-2e\mathbf{A}\},\{\alpha,\phi\}]}{\partial{\bf\mathbf{A}}}\Bigg]\Bigg|_{\mathbf{A}=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{\mathbf{Q}_{j}\in\mathcal{Q}_{6}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}_{min}[\{\mathbf{Q}_{j}+\mathbf{q}_{s}-2e\mathbf{A}\}]}{\partial{\bf A}}\Bigg|_{\mathbf{A}=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2e\sum\limits_{\mathbf{Q}_{j}\in\mathcal{Q}_{6}}\partial_{\mathbf{q}_{s}}\mathcal{F}_{min}[\{\mathbf{Q}_{j}+\mathbf{q}_{s}\}].

In the third equality, we used the fact that the
\alpha_{j}
component for the ground state should satisfy the GL equation
\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial\alpha_{j}}=0
so that the free energy is minimized, denoted as
\mathcal{F}_{min}
.
With Eq.(

\mathcal{F}_{min}[\{\mathbf{Q}_{j}+\mathbf{q}_{s}\}]
can be further expanded as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{min}[\{\mathbf{Q}_{j}+\mathbf{q}_{s}\}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{6}}\Delta T(\mathbf{Q}_{j}+\mathbf{q}_{s})\tilde{\alpha}_{i}^{2}+\Big[c_{0}\Big(\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{6}}\tilde{\alpha}_{i}^{2}\Big)^{2}+c_{1}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{6}}\tilde{\alpha}_{i}^{2}\tilde{\alpha}_{i+1}^{2}+c_{2}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{6}}\tilde{\alpha}_{i}^{2}\tilde{\alpha}_{i+2}^{2}+c_{3}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{6}}\tilde{\alpha}_{i}^{2}\tilde{\alpha}_{i+3}^{2}\Big],

(267)

where

to be different solutions discussed in Sec.

\mathbf{q}_{s}

(268)

(2) Next we will discuss the emergent mirror symmetry by the red dashed lines shown in Fig.
7
. Since the orientation of the mirror axis is different for different phases, we first give a general definition of the mirror symmetry. The mirror symmetry
\mathcal{M}^{||}_{\theta_{||}}
is defined for a mirror plane perpendicular to our 2D system containing the axis parallel to an arbitrary azimuthal direction
\theta_{||}
in the 2D plane. This acts on any general pairing momentum
\mathbf{q}

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{||}_{\theta_{||}}:\tilde{\mathbf{q}}=R_{z}[\theta_{||}-\theta_{\mathbf{q}}]\mathbf{q}=q\{\cos(2\theta_{||}-\theta_{\mathbf{q}}),\sin(2\theta_{||}-\theta_{\mathbf{q}})\};\quad\mathbf{q}=q\{\cos(\theta_{\mathbf{q}}),\sin(\theta_{\mathbf{q}})\},

(277)

where
R_{z}(\phi)
denotes the operation of rotation by angle
\phi
along the z-axis. Let us take the example of
phase 2a
to demonstrate how it has this emergent symmetry for
\theta_{||}=\frac{\pi}{6}
. First we write down the minimum energy for this phase,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{min,2a}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}}_{s})=\frac{(\Delta T(\mathbf{Q}_{0}+\tilde{\mathbf{q}}_{s}))^{2}+(\Delta T(\mathbf{Q}_{1}+\tilde{\mathbf{q}}_{s}))^{2}}{8c_{0}+2c_{1}}.

(278)

We see the momentum dependence in this minimum free energy manifests in
\Delta T(\mathbf{Q}_{j}+\mathbf{q}_{s})
for

\displaystyle(\mathbf{Q}_{j}+\mathbf{q}_{s})^{2}=k_{0}^{2}+q_{s}^{2}+2k_{0}q_{s}\cos\left(\frac{j\pi}{3}-\theta_{\mathbf{q}_{s}}\right);\quad\mathbf{q}_{s}=q_{s}\{\cos(\theta_{\mathbf{q}_{s}}),\sin(\theta_{\mathbf{q}_{s}})\}\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{Z}_{6}.

(279)

With
\mathcal{M}_{\pi/6}^{||}
,
\mathcal{F}_{min,2a}(\mathbf{q}_{s})
becomes
\mathcal{F}_{min,2a}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}}_{s})
, the transformation of this energy will be dictated by the transformation of
\mathbf{q}_{s}
in
(\mathbf{Q}_{j}+\mathbf{q}_{s})^{2}

(280)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\pi/6}^{||}:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{min,2a}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}}_{s})=\frac{(\Delta T(\mathbf{Q}_{0}+\tilde{\mathbf{q}}_{s}))^{2}+(\Delta T(\mathbf{Q}_{1}+\tilde{\mathbf{q}}_{s}))^{2}}{8c_{0}+2c_{1}}

(282)

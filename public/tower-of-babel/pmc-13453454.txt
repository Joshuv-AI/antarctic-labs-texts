MYC rearrangement improves the predictive efficacy of the double-hit model in newly diagnosed multiple myeloma
Authors: J Zhao, C Du, J Cui, L Li, Y Zheng, W Zhang, X Guo, S Deng, Y Xu, W Sui, W Huang, W Liu, D Zou, L Qiu, Q Sun, C Li, Z Xiao, G An
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMYC rearrangement (MYC-R) is a known adverse prognostic factor in multiple myeloma (MM), yet its role in the double-hit model remains undefined. Meanwhile, the prognostic value of MYC gain/amp has not been clearly characterized. This study aimed to evaluate the prognostic impact of MYC-R and/or gain/amp to assess their potential role in refining risk stratification.Patients and methodsA prospective cohort of 227 patients with newly diagnosed MM (enrolled in the NICHE clinical trial, NCT04645199) received bortezomib-based induction therapy and, if eligible, underwent upfront autologous stem cell transplantation. The FISH panel included del(13q), del(17p), del(1p), 1q21 gain/amp, IgH rearrangement, t(4;14), t(11;14), t(14;16), t(14;20), MYC break-apart probe, and IgH/MYC fusion probe.ResultsMedian progression-free survival (PFS) was significantly shorter in patients with MYC-R (34.0 months) or MYC gain/amp (34.3 months), compared with those without MYC abnormalities (49.2 months; P = 0.003 and 0.016, respectively). IgH/MYC was notably more common in cases with undefined-partner IgH translocations (16.6%) than those with defined partners (5.7%), and was associated with poorer outcomes. In multivariate analysis, MYC-R remained an independent predictor of PFS (hazard ratio 1.96, P = 0.007) and improved the discriminative performance of the double-hit model by increasing the concordance index for PFS from 0.549 to 0.581.ConclusionBoth MYC-R and MYC gain/amp indicate adverse prognosis in newly diagnosed MM. The independent prognostic value of MYC-R supports its integration into routine FISH panels and future refinements of the double-hit risk model.Key words: double-hit myeloma, interphase fluorescence in situ hybridization, MYC amplification, MYC rearrangement, multiple myeloma, prognosis

Introduction

Multiple myeloma (MM) is an incurable plasma malignancy. Cytogenetic abnormalities (CAs) play a pivotal role in shaping its biological heterogeneity and prognosis. The detection of recurrent high-risk CAs by FISH helps identify double or multiple-hit high-risk MM, a subgroup with poor outcomes even in the era of quadruplet therapy. However, the definition of double-hit MM remains controversial. Several criteria have been proposed: Mayo Clinic and the National Cancer Research Institute define it as the presence of ≥2 high-risk CAs, such as t(4;14), t(14;16), t(14;20), del(17p), del(1p), gain(1q), and/or TP53 mutations.1, 2, 3 More recently, the International Myeloma Society (IMS) proposed criteria of high-risk MM based on the presence of del(17p) and/or TP53 mutation, high-risk IgH translocations co-occurring with gain(1q) and/or del(1p32), monoallelic del(1p32) along with gain(1q), or biallelic del(1p32).4 A systematic review defined double hit as the presence of at least two high-risk CAs, including t(4;14), gain(1q) or amp(1q), del(17p), and, when data were available, t (14;16).5

Another unresolved issue is the limited consideration of MYC abnormalities in current double-hit models. MYC rearrangement (MYC-R) has been recognized as a high-risk marker in MM, occurring in 8%-15% of newly diagnosed MM. The median progression-free survival (PFS) of MYC-R ranged from 2.5 to 3.3 years.6, 7, 8 While the adverse prognostic impact of MYC-R was established, the significance of MYC gain/amplification remains less explored. Moreover, it is unclear whether MYC-R or gain/amp is independent of other high-risk factors.

To address these questions, we aimed to investigate the incidence of MYC-R and gain/amp in a prospective cohort of newly diagnosed MM patients, evaluate their clinical relevance and survival impact, and explore their potential role in redefining double-hit MM. A comprehensive understanding of the spectrum of MYC abnormalities may provide critical insights into high-risk MM and guide therapeutic strategies.

Materials and Methods

Patients and study design

This study enrolled 227 newly diagnosed MM patients drawn from the MM database of the National Longitudinal Cohort of Hematological Disease (NICHE, NCT04645199). Patients diagnosed by the International Myeloma Working Group (IMWG) consensus definition of MM were sequentially enrolled from 1 January 2014 to 31 January 2021. The last follow-up date was 31 December 2024. All patients have signed the informed consent in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The study received approval from the local institutional ethics committee. Patients received four cycles of bortezomib, cyclophosphamide, dexamethasone or bortezomib, lenalidomide, dexamethasone (RVD) induction therapy. Transplant-eligible patients accepted autologous stem cell transplantation (ASCT). After transplantation, patients accepted two to four cycles of consolidation therapy identical to their induction therapy. Lenalidomide maintenance was recommended to all patients. Minimal residual disease (MRD) was detected by flow cytometry.

FISH testing

As previously reported,9 bone marrow aspirate samples were first purified by anti-CD138-coated magnetic beads (Miltenyi Biotec, Bergisch Gladbach, Germany) before FISH testing. Enriched plasma cells were examined using specific DNA probes (Vysis, Abbott Park, IL orCytocell, Cambridge, UK) to identify chromosomal aberrations, including del(13q) or −13, del(17p), del(1p), 1q21 gain/amp, IgH rearrangement, and its translocation partners [such as t(4;14), t(11;14), t(14;16), and t(14;20)]. MYC (8q24.1) break-apart probe was purchased from Vysis (Abbott Park, IL). The 5′ end was marked in orange fluorescence, and the 3′ end was marked in green. The study described the number of orange, green, or merged yellow signals. MYC-R was defined as having at least one signal of orange or green fluorescence, and MYC gain/amp was defined as having more than two signals of orange, green, and/or yellow fluorescence. Supplementary Table S1 (available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108339) summarizes the details of the FISH probes. A total of 200 interphase nuclei were analyzed, and the cut-off value for numerical abnormalities was 20%, while the threshold for translocation was 10%, following the European Myeloma Network recommendation.10 And the complex karyotype was defined as the presence of ≥3 unrelated chromosome abnormalities in the absence of other class-defining recurring genetic abnormalities; it excludes hyperdiploid karyotypes with ≥3 trisomies (or polysomies) without structural abnormalities (NCCN Guidelines Version 3.2024 Acute Myeloid Leukemia).

Survival and statistical analysis

The study defined PFS as the duration from diagnosis to the date of first progression, death, or the last follow-up. Overall survival (OS) was calculated from the date of diagnosis to the date of death or the last follow-up.9 We used the Wilcoxon rank-sum test to compare the significance of continuous variables, and used the chi-square test or Fisher exact test to assess the statistical significance of categorical variables among different groups. Kendall Tau-b correlation analysis was utilized to determine the association of ordinal variables. Survival analysis was conducted by the Kaplan–Meier method and compared by the two-sided log-rank test. All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 26.0 (IBM Corp., Chicago, IL), GraphPad Prism 10.2.0 (GraphPad Software, Boston), and R Studio 2023.06.2 (Posit Software, PBC).

Results

The median follow-up time was 37.3 months [95% confidence interval (CI) 35.0-39.6]. The median age of MM patients at diagnosis was 60 years, and 30.4% of patients were older than 64 years. Approximately 50.7% of patients were male. Approximately 78.4% of patients accepted at least four cycles of bortezomib-based induction therapy, and the rest switched to ixazomib-based or Rd due to intolerance. Approximately 30.0% of patients received first-line ASCT. Baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The median PFS was 43.0 months (95% CI 36.8-49.2), and the median OS was 72.5 months (95% CI 62.4-82.6). A representative MYC-R detected by FISH is shown in Supplementary Figure S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108339. Approximately 18.9% of patients had MYC-R, with a median positivity rate of 75% [interquartile range (IQR) 22%-90%]. A group of patients exhibited more than two intact yellow fluorescence signals, indicating MYC gain or amplification. Approximately 45 patients (19.8%) displayed three (gain) or at least four (amp) MYC signals. A total of 25 (11.0%) patients had MYC gain and 34 (15.0%) patients had amplification. Only three patients (1.3%) had both MYC-R and gain/amp.

MYC-R and gain/amp both indicated adverse prognosis in MM

The median PFS of patients with MYC-R was 34.0 months (95% CI 21.3-46.7), and the median OS was not reached. Patients with MYC gain/amp had a median PFS of 34.3 months (95% CI 26.5-42.1) and a median OS of 71.4 months (95% CI 34.0-108.8). Compared with patients without any MYC abnormalities (median PFS: 49.2 months, 95% CI 43.5-55.0, median OS: 78.8 months, 95% CI 58.5-99.1), those with either MYC-R or gain/amp had significantly shorter PFS (P = 0.003, P = 0.016). Patients with MYC-R even had an inferior median OS (P = 0.004; Figure 1A and B). We then stratified patients by the median clonal size threshold of 75%; the PFS and OS did not deteriorate with larger clone size (Supplementary Figure S2A and B, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108339). Baseline characteristics were largely comparable between patients with and without MYC-R, except for a higher incidence of anemia (65.1% versus 42.9%, P = 0.011) and elevated lactate dehydrogenase (23.3% versus 10.3%, P = 0.039) in the MYC-R group. ASCT and bortezomib-based induction rates did not differ significantly between patients with or without MYC-R (Table 1).

Regarding MYC gain/amp, we observed a trend toward shorter PFS in patients with MYC gain (37 months, P = 0.703) or amplification (29.9 months, P = 0.013), compared with those with normal MYC copy number (46.0 months). However, OS was not significantly influenced by MYC gain/amp (Figure 1C and D). Patients with MYC gain/amp showed higher frequencies of renal failure (26.7% versus 12.6%, P = 0.035) and hypercalcemia (24.4% versus 11.5%, P = 0.033). There were no significant differences in the first-line induction regimen or ASCT rate between patients with or without MYC gain/amp (Table 1).

The correlation between MYC and other CAs

We observed distinct chromosome ploidy patterns between patients with MYC-R and gain/amp. MYC-R was associated with hyperdiploidy (15.8% versus 4.8%, P = 0.026). However, MYC gain/amp showed a trend toward hypodiploidy (20.0% versus 8.5%, P = 0.046). MYC-R was significantly related to gain(1q) (60.5% versus 42.4%, P = 0.041), and was negatively correlated with del(13q) (32.6% versus 52.2%, P = 0.027) and t(4;14) (7.0% versus 23.4%, P = 0.019). In contrast, MYC gain/amp was positively associated with del(17p) (15.6% versus 3.8%, P = 0.009) and gain(1q) (71.1% versus 39.6%, P < 0.001; Table 1).

In IgH translocations with undefined partners [excluding known types such as t(11;14), t(4;14), t(14;16), and t(14;20)], a critical consideration is whether they translocated with MYC. We found that MYC-R occurred at comparable frequencies between patients with a defined partner (15.1%) and those with an undefined partner (22.9%). However, IgH/MYC translocation was notably more common in the undefined-partner group (16.6%) than in the defined-partner group (5.7%) (Figure 1E). And IgH/MYC translocation seemed to be related to an inferior outcome, although the difference did not reach statistical significance, likely due to limited sample size (Supplementary Figure S2C and D, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108339). Restricted by the sample size, the PFS difference between patients with or without MYC rearrangement was not statistically significant. In contrast, MYC gain/amplification showed no correlation with the presence of an undefined IgH translocation partner (Figure 1F).

Efficacy of first-line treatment on patients with MYC abnormality

In the entire cohort and specifically in the bortezomib-treated group, the overall response rate (ORR) was significantly lower in patients with MYC abnormality, compared with those without (81.2% versus 92.2%, P = 0.019, 83.1% versus 95.6%, P = 0.012; Figure 2A and B). Bortezomib-based induction significantly improved ORR in patients without MYC abnormality (95.6% versus 79.3%, P = 0.010), but the benefit was not observed in patients harboring MYC abnormality. In contrast, ASCT significantly increased ORR in patients with MYC abnormality (100.0% versus 74.2%, P = 0.004; Figure 2C and D). On multivariate analysis, MYC abnormality remained an independent predictor of inferior ORR (hazard ratio 2.46, 95% CI 1.04-5.82, P = 0.040; Supplementary Table S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108339).

Regarding MRD-negative rates, there was no clear difference between patients with and without MYC abnormality (Figure 2E and F). ASCT significantly improved the MRD-negative rate in both subgroups: 87.0% versus 38.7% (P < 0.001) in patients with MYC abnormality, and 91.1% versus 34.0% (P < 0.001) in those without. Bortezomib-based induction was also associated with higher MRD negative rates, regardless of MYC status (Figure 2G and H).

The addition of MYC-R improved the prognostic efficacy of the double-hit model

According to the Consensus Genomic Staging (CGS) high-risk criteria, we defined the presence of a single high-risk factor as one hit, whereas the co-occurrence of two or more such abnormalities (e.g. del(17p) and biallelic del(1p32)) was considered double hit. MYC gain/amp was significantly related to double hit, as the incidence of double hit increased from 52.2% in patients with normal MYC copy number to 77.8% in those with MYC gain/amp (P = 0.002). However, MYC-R showed no significant relationship to double hit. The incidence rate of double hit was 69.8% in patients with MYC-R versus 54.3% in those without (P = 0.086; Figure 3A). Incorporating MYC abnormalities into the risk classification redefined a subgroup of patients into higher risk tiers (Figure 3B). We next evaluated whether this redefinition influenced the prognosis across IMS risk strata. In patients with no hit, MYC-R identified a subgroup with significantly higher progression risk: 34.0 months in those with MYC-R versus 51.5 months in those without (P = 0.008; Figure 3C). In patients with one hit, the median PFS was 33.0 months with MYC-R versus 39.0 months without, though the difference was not statistically significant (P = 0.204; Figure 3D). In double-hit patients, additional MYC-R did not confer extra risk (Figure 3E). Conversely, MYC gain/amp increased risk in double-hit patients, but not in no-hit or one-hit subgroups (Supplementary Figure S3, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108339).

Furthermore, incorporating MYC abnormality into the model of double-hit MM improved risk stratification efficacy. Under the CGS criteria, the distribution of patients with no, one, or double hit was 42.7%, 38.3%, and 18.9%, respectively. After adding MYC abnormality, the incidence rate of patients with no, one, or double hit was 32.6%, 29.5%, and 37.9%. Cox regression analysis confirmed that both MYC-R and gain/amp retained independent prognostic value considering CGS-defined double-hit status, with hazard ratios for PFS of 1.96 (P = 0.007) and 1.75 (P = 0.027), respectively (Table 2). The inclusion of MYC-R and gain/amp also improved the predictive accuracy of the model, increasing the concordance index from 0.549 to 0.596 for PFS, and 0.524 to 0.598 for OS (Figure 3F). Kaplan–Meier analysis also demonstrated that the revised double-hit model incorporating MYC-R provided improved prognostic stratification for patient outcomes (Figure 4).

Discussion

MYC-R is an adverse prognostic marker in MM

MYC-R detected by FISH was first reported in 200111 and has been associated with inferior outcomes in MM since 2015.8,12, 13, 14 The incidence rate of MYC-R ranges from 8% to 15% in newly diagnosed MM, reaching up to 35% in relapsed/refractory MM.8,12,14, 15, 16, 17, 18 Most prior studies were conducted before the widespread adoption of first-line proteasome inhibitors and immunomodulatory drugs, and routine FISH panels in clinical trials and practice have often omitted MYC. Consequently, the true prognostic weight of MYC-R may be underestimated in current definitions of high-risk MM. Jin et al.19 reported that patients with MYC-R treated with proteasome inhibitor (PI)- or immunomodulatory drug (IMiD)-based regimens had a median PFS of 15.9 months and a median OS of 25.1 months. In our cohort, patients with MYC-R exhibited better survival outcomes, which may be due to a more recent enrollment period and a higher rate of upfront ASCT. Nevertheless, both studies affirmed that MYC-R remained an adverse prognostic factor, at least when treated with triple drug induction. Although ASCT significantly improved ORR and MRD-negative rate in patients with MYC-R or gain/amp, it may not significantly prolong the survival, both in our data and a previous study.20

MYC gain/amp may be a potential high-risk factor

In addition to MYC-R, we identified a group of patients with increased MYC copy number (gain/amplification) in the absence of MYC-R. In B-cell lymphoma, lung cancer, and breast cancer, MYC gain/amp has been linked to inferior outcomes.21, 22, 23 In MM, Jin et al.19 reported an incidence of MYC gain/amp of 9.4% (39/417) but did not separately analyze its survival impact. In our cohort, MYC gain/amp showed a trend toward shorter PFS, with amplification possibly conferring an even higher risk, although statistical significance was limited by follow-up duration and sample size. A key mechanistic question is whether MYC gain/amp merely represents a passenger event associated with hyperdiploid karyotypes. In our data, only 7.5% of patients with MYC gain/amp had hyperdiploidy, and the frequency did not differ significantly between those with and without MYC gain/amp. These observations argue against a simple passenger relationship and highlight the need for prospective studies to clarify the independent prognostic role of MYC copy number alterations in MM.

Prognostic relevance of MYC-R with undefined-partner IgH translocation

Our previous study reported that patients with IgH translocations involving undefined partners showed a relatively favorable prognosis.24 However, it remained unclear whether this subgroup might include cases with cryptic IgH/MYC translocation that could confer a poorer outcome.7 Our data indicated that among patients with undefined-partner IgH translocation, MYC-R or IgH/MYC translocation identified a subgroup with inferior survival. These findings underscore the clinical utility of routine MYC-R screening in patients with IgH translocations of an undetermined partner.

MYC rearrangement refines the double-hit risk model in MM

Double-hit MM indicates poor outcomes even when treated by quadruplet drug induction and ASCT.25 Current double-hit definitions, however, seldom incorporate MYC-R. The CGS high-risk criteria represent the most widely accepted definition of high-risk MM. Building upon this framework, we attempted to incorporate the concept of the number of high-risk criteria to establish a double-hit model, and our findings revealed that MYC-R identified a higher risk subgroup among patients otherwise classified as no hit, whereas it did not add extra risk in those already defined as double hit. Furthermore, integrating MYC-R into the CGS double-hit model improved the discriminative power of risk stratification, as reflected by an increase in the concordance index and the survival curve separation. We acknowledge that, owing to the lack of complete TP53 mutation data of next-generation sequencing (NGS), this information could not be integrated into the CGS-based classification. Future studies are needed to address this gap. However, the above observations align with a prior study that evaluated the interactions between MYC-R and a double-hit definition comprising at least two of the following CAs: amp(1q21), t(4;14), t(14;16), or del(17p). That analysis concluded that either MYC-R or double hit was associated with higher progression risk than one-hit or no-hit patients,19 indicating the important role of MYC-R. We recommend incorporating MYC-R testing into the FISH panel. As a pragmatic and resource-efficient strategy, a two-tiered FISH approach was routinely used: the first step involves detecting IgH rearrangements, chromosome 1 abnormalities, del(17p), and MYC-R; the second step specifically identifies rearrangements between IgH and its partners (CCND1, FGFR3, MAF, MAFB, and MYC). Nevertheless, even with this streamlined approach, comprehensive FISH profiling for MM patients remains complex and costly. Perhaps NGS, which can simultaneously detect single-nucleotide polymorphisms, copy number variations, IgH, and MYC rearrangements, offers a more convenient and cost-effective solution for identifying genetic abnormalities in MM. A recent large-scale NGS validation of the CGS high-risk criteria indicates that patients with two or more criteria exhibit a worse prognosis than those with only one high-risk feature.26

A notable limitation of the present study is that patients received triple-drug induction regimens, whereas the current first-line standard is the quadruplet therapy, including anti-CD38 monoclonal antibodies. Whether such intensified induction can mitigate the adverse prognosis of MYC-R remains an important question for future investigation.

Conclusion

This study confirms that MYC-R retains its adverse prognostic impact in newly diagnosed MM in the era of modern therapy. Especially in patients with undefined-partner IgH translocation, MYC-R or IgH/MYC translocation identified a subgroup with inferior survival. Moreover, we characterized and reported for the first time the prognostic relevance of MYC gain or amplification in MM. Finally, given the independent prognostic value of MYC-R, we strongly recommend incorporating its assessment into routine clinical FISH panels and future definition of double-hit MM.

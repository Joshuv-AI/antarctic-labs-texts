M-polynomial Based Mathematical Formulation of the Hyperbolic Sombor Index
Jayjit Barman, Shibsankar Das

Abstract
The numerical values extracted from a graph that indicates its topology are called topological indices. A contemporary and efficient method is to compute a graph’s topological indices using the graph polynomial that corresponds to it. This method of identifying degree-based topological indices involves the use of the M-polynomial. Very recently, in 2025 2025 , the hyperbolic Sombor index ( HSO ) was proposed and shows its chemical applicability for octane isomers and the structure sensitivity and abruptness for octane, nonane, and decane isomers, respectively. In this work, we establish the closed derivation formula for the above-mentioned index of a graph based on its M-polynomial. Additionally, we use our proposed derivation formula to calculate the hyperbolic Sombor index of a few standard graphs and chemical families. Moreover, we provide the numerical and graphical representations for the M-polynomial and the computed HSO index of the chemical families. AMS Mathematics Subject Classification 2020: 05C07, 05C09, 05C10, 92E10, 05C31, 05C92. Keywords: Hyperbolic Sombor index; M-polynomial; Boron icosahedral α \alpha sheet; Dendrimers; Jagged-rectangle benzenoid system.

Introduction

Chemical graph theory (CGT) is a broad area of mathematical chemistry that uses graph theory to model chemical molecules mathematically. Numerous degree-based topological indices have been presented and extensively studied in the mathematics and chemical literature concerning chemical graph theory.
Topological indices are numerical values in CGT that are derived from the graphical representation of the molecular structure utilizing mathematical invariants. They are crucial in the investigation of quantitative structure-property relationships (
QSPR
) and quantitative structure-activity relationships (
QSAR
)
[
1
]
.

Let
G(V(G),E(G))
be an undirected, simple and connected graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
. The degree
d(v)
of a vertex
v
in a graph
G
is the number of edges that are connected to it. In graph
G

u
and
v

[
2
]

The hyperbolic Sombor index of a graph

[
3

in
2025

\textit{HSO}(G)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}\frac{\sqrt{d^{2}(u)+d^{2}(v)}}{d(u)},~\text{where}~0<d(u)\leq d(v).

Another representation of this index is as follows

\textit{HSO}(G)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}\frac{\sqrt{d^{2}(u)+d^{2}(v)}}{\min\{d(u),d(v)\}}.

Topological indices are typically determined based on their mathematical definitions; however, calculating them for large and complex graphs or networks is often a challenging and time-consuming task. To address this problem, various graph-invariant-based polynomials have been proposed in the literature. These polynomials are employed to derive topological indices by applying the required operators at a specific point. Several types of topological indices can now be calculated through the polynomial approach rather than computing them individually

Deutsch and Klavžar introduced the idea of the M-polynomial

The M-polynomial approach to computing the degree-based topological indices can become laborious for large or densely connected graphs or networks. Determining M-polynomials for different graph families has been the subject of much research due to the complexity of computing graph polynomials and extracting topological indices as graphs get larger

Closed Derivation Formula over the M-polynomial for the Hyperbolic Sombor Index

Here, we present the closed derivation formula for the hyperbolic Sombor index using the operators listed in Table
1
. We prove the derivation formula by using the M-polynomial of a graph, which is given below.

Theorem 1
.

Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a graph and its hyperbolic Sombor index is

\textit{HSO}(G)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}f(d(u),d(v)),~\text{where}~f(x,y)=\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}{x},~\text{where}~0<x\leq y

then

\textit{HSO}(G)=D^{1/2}_{x}JP_{y}P_{x}S_{x}(M(G;x,y))|_{x=1},

where
M(G(x,y))
is the M-polynomial of
G
.

Proof.

Let
M(G;x,y)
be the M-polynomial of
G
. Then, using the operators mentioned in Table
1
, we get

\displaystyle D^{1/2}_{x}JP_{y}P_{x}S_{x}(M(G;x,y))

\displaystyle=D^{1/2}_{x}JP_{y}P_{x}S_{x}\biggl\{\sum_{\delta\leq i\leq j\leq\Delta}m_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}\biggr\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\delta\leq i\leq j\leq\Delta}D^{1/2}_{x}JP_{y}P_{x}S_{x}\{m_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\delta\leq i\leq j\leq\Delta}D^{1/2}_{x}JP_{y}P_{x}\{\frac{1}{i}m_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\delta\leq i\leq j\leq\Delta}D^{1/2}_{x}J\{\frac{1}{i}m_{ij}x^{i^{2}}y^{j^{2}}\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\delta\leq i\leq j\leq\Delta}D^{1/2}_{x}\{\frac{1}{i}m_{ij}x^{i^{2}+j^{2}}\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\delta\leq i\leq j\leq\Delta}\sqrt{x\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\{\frac{1}{i}m_{ij}x^{i^{2}+j^{2}}\}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{1}{i}m_{ij}x^{i^{2}+j^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\delta\leq i\leq j\leq\Delta}\sqrt{x\cdot\frac{1}{i}m_{ij}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\{x^{i^{2}+j^{2}}\}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{1}{i}m_{ij}x^{i^{2}+j^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\delta\leq i\leq j\leq\Delta}\frac{1}{i}m_{ij}\sqrt{i^{2}+j^{2}}\cdot\sqrt{x\cdot x^{i^{2}+j^{2}-1}}\cdot\sqrt{x^{i^{2}+j^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\delta\leq i\leq j\leq\Delta}\frac{\sqrt{i^{2}+j^{2}}}{i}~m_{ij}x^{i^{2}+j^{2}}.

\displaystyle\therefore~D^{1/2}_{x}JP_{y}P_{x}S_{x}(M(G;x,y))|_{x=1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\delta\leq i\leq j\leq\Delta}\frac{\sqrt{i^{2}+j^{2}}}{i}~m_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\delta\leq i\leq j\leq\Delta}m_{ij}\cdot f(i,j).

(4)

From the
Equations

2
and
3
, we get

\textit{HSO}(G)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}f(d(u),d(v))=\sum_{\delta\leq i\leq j\leq\Delta}m_{ij}\cdot f(i,j).

(5)

Therefore,
Sections

2
and
5
give the required result.
∎

M(G;x,y)=xy^{2}+(n+m-4)x^{2}y^{2}+3x^{2}y^{3}.

Theorem 35
.

Let
G
be the family of tadpole graph
\textit{T}(n,m)
. Then the hyperbolic Sombor index is given by

\textit{HSO}(G)=\sqrt{5}+\sqrt{2}(n+m-4)+\frac{3\sqrt{13}}{2}.

Proof.

The M-polynomial of
\textit{T}(n,m)
is given by

M(G;x,y)=xy^{2}+(n+m-4)x^{2}y^{2}+3x^{2}y^{3}.

Then

\displaystyle\textit{HSO}(G)

\displaystyle=D^{1/2}_{x}JP_{y}P_{x}S_{x}(M(G;x,y))|_{x=1}

\displaystyle=D^{1/2}_{x}JP_{y}P_{x}S_{x}\Big(xy^{2}+(n+m-4)x^{2}y^{2}+3x^{2}y^{3}\Big)|_{x=1}

\displaystyle=D^{1/2}_{x}JP_{y}P_{x}\Big(xy^{2}+\Big(\frac{n+m-4}{2}\Big)x^{2}y^{2}+\frac{3}{2}x^{2}y^{3}\Big)|_{x=1}

\displaystyle=D^{1/2}_{x}J\Big(xy^{4}+\Big(\frac{n+m-4}{2}\Big)x^{4}y^{4}+\frac{3}{2}x^{4}y^{9}\Big)|_{x=1}

\displaystyle=D^{1/2}_{x}\Big(x^{5}+\Big(\frac{n+m-4}{2}\Big)x^{8}+\frac{3}{2}x^{13}\Big)|_{x=1}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sqrt{5}x^{5}+\sqrt{8}\Big(\frac{n+m-4}{2}\Big)x^{8}+\frac{3\sqrt{13}}{2}x^{13}\Big)|_{x=1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{5}+\sqrt{2}(n+m-4)+\frac{3\sqrt{13}}{2}.

∎

The nuclei of benzenoid aromatics, which are joined by carbon-carbon bonds, are found in polyphenylenes. The chemical graph
\textit{P}[s,t]

s

t

24st+12s

30st+13s-t

36

Theorem 36
.

[
36

Let
G
be the family of polyphenylenes
\textit{P}[s,t]
, then the expression of M-polynomial is

M(G;x,y)=4(2s+t)x^{2}y^{2}+4(6st+s-t)x^{2}y^{3}+(6st+s-t)x^{3}y^{3}.

Theorem 37
.

Let
G
be the family of polyphenylenes
\textit{P}[s,t]
. Then the hyperbolic Sombor index is given by

\textit{HSO}(G)=4\sqrt{2}(2s+t)+(2\sqrt{13}+\sqrt{2})(6st+s-t).

Proof.

The M-polynomial of
\textit{P}[s,t]
is given by

M(G;x,y)=4(2s+t)x^{2}y^{2}+4(6st+s-t)x^{2}y^{3}+(6st+s-t)x^{3}y^{3}.

Then

\displaystyle\textit{HSO}(G)

\displaystyle=D^{1/2}_{x}JP_{y}P_{x}S_{x}(M(G;x,y))|_{x=1}

\displaystyle=D^{1/2}_{x}JP_{y}P_{x}S_{x}\Big(4(2s+t)x^{2}y^{2}+4(6st+s-t)x^{2}y^{3}+(6st+s-t)x^{3}y^{3}\Big)|_{x=1}

\displaystyle=D^{1/2}_{x}JP_{y}P_{x}\Big(2(2s+t)x^{2}y^{2}+2(6st+s-t)x^{2}y^{3}+\Big(\frac{6st+s-t}{3}\Big)x^{3}y^{3}\Big)|_{x=1}

\displaystyle=D^{1/2}_{x}J\Big(2(2s+t)x^{4}y^{4}+2(6st+s-t)x^{4}y^{9}+\Big(\frac{6st+s-t}{3}\Big)x^{9}y^{9}\Big)|_{x=1}

\displaystyle=D^{1/2}_{x}\Big(2(2s+t)x^{8}+2(6st+s-t)x^{13}+\Big(\frac{6st+s-t}{3}\Big)x^{18}\Big)|_{x=1}

\displaystyle=\Big(2\sqrt{8}(2s+t)x^{8}+2\sqrt{13}(6st+s-t)x^{13}+\sqrt{18}\Big(\frac{6st+s-t}{3}\Big)x^{18}\Big)|_{x=1}

\displaystyle=4\sqrt{2}(2s+t)+(2\sqrt{13}+\sqrt{2})(6st+s-t).

∎

4
Conclusion

We suggested the closed derivation formula in this work to calculate the recently introduced hyperbolic Sombor index (
HSO
) of a graph, which is written in terms of a graph’s M-polynomial. Additionally, we used our closed derivation formula to evaluate this index for some standard families of graphs, chemical families of
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}(a,b)
and dendrimers, jagged-rectangle benzenoid system
B_{m,n}
, polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons
\textit{PAH}_{n}
, V-Phynelenic nanotubes
\textit{VPHX}[m,n]
, V-Phynelenic nanotori
\textit{VPHY}[m,n]
, porous graphene
\textit{PG}[p,q]
, tadpole graph
T(n,m)
and polyphenylenes
P[s,t]
, by using their respective M-polynomials. Moreover, numerical and graphical representations are provided for the M-polynomial and the computed
HSO
index of the chemical families for different ranges. The values of the
HSO
index have increased as we increase the values of
m
and
n
for all of the chemical families. Since topological indices can be directly used in
QSPR
/
QSAR
studies of chemical substances, researchers in the field may find benefits in the hyperbolic Sombor index results for different graph families.

Abstract
. We prove that resonances of the Schrödinger operator with compactly supported potential can contain arbitrary subset of the angle { z : − Im z > C | Re z | } \{z:-\mathop{\rm Im}z>C|\mathop{\rm Re}z|\} that satisfies Blaschke condition. We also establish sufficient conditions for the subsets of wider domains.



1.
Introduction

In the present paper we consider one-dimensional Schrödinger operators
\mathcal{H}_{q}
on the half-line
\mathbb{R}_{+}=[0,+\infty)
of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{q}=-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+q(x),

with real-valued compactly supported potentials
q\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.
The
Jost solution

\psi(k,x)
of
\mathcal{H}_{q}
is defined as the unique solution of

\displaystyle-\psi^{\prime\prime}(k,x)+q(x)\psi(k,x)=k^{2}\psi(k,x),\qquad k\in\mathbb{C},\qquad x\geqslant 0,

that satisfies
\psi(k,x)=e^{ikx}
for all large
x
.
The corresponding
Jost function
is defined by

\displaystyle w(k)=\psi(k,0),\qquad k\in\mathbb{C}.

This function is entire, its zeroes in the lower half-plane
\{z\colon\mathop{\rm Im}z<0\}
are called
resonances
of the operator
\mathcal{H}_{q}
, we denote the set of resonances by
\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}\nolimits(q)
. Strictly speaking
\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}\nolimits(q)
is not a set but a multiset, where the multiplicity of each resonance equals the multiplicity of the corresponding zero. In the free case
q\equiv 0
we have
\psi(k,x)=e^{ikx}
,
w(k)=1
and
\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}\nolimits(q)=\emptyset
. When
q
is nontrivial, the set
\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}\nolimits(q)
is infinite; together with the corresponding bound states (zeroes of
w
with positive imaginary part) resonances uniquely determine the potential, see Theorem 1.1 in
[
10
]
and the papers
[
11
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
14
]
devoted to the stability of the inverse spectral problem. Resonances can also be defined as the poles of the meromorphic continuation of the resolvent of
\mathcal{H}_{q}
, see Chapter 2 in the book
[
5
]
.

The techniques from complex analysis that relate the growth of entire function to the distribution of its zeroes provide valuable information about the location of resonances. For example,
the classical Blaschke condition

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}\nolimits(q)}\frac{|\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda|}{1+|\lambda|^{2}}<\infty

holds for every
q
with compact support.
The counting function

\displaystyle N(r)=\#\Big\{z\colon w(z)=0,\;|z|<r,\,\mathop{\rm Im}z<0\Big\}

of
\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}\nolimits(q)
satisfies the relation
N(r)=\frac{2}{\pi}\ell_{q}r+o(r)
as
r\to\infty
, where
\ell_{q}
is the diameter of the essential support of the potential
q
, see the papers
[
6
]
and
[
17
]
for the case of operator on the real line and
[
10
]
for the half-line.
Furthermore, for every
\delta>0
we have the asymptotic relation

(1.2)

\displaystyle\#\Big\{z\colon w(z)=0,\;|z|<r,\,\mathop{\mathrm{Arg}}\nolimits(z)\notin[-\pi+\delta,-\delta]\Big\}=o(r),\qquad r\to\infty.

In other words, apart from a
set of density zero, all the resonances of
\mathcal{H}_{q}
are contained in arbitrarily small sectors
about the real axis. On the other hand, it is known that resonances cannot lie too close to the real line, see Theorem 3 in
[
2
]
and Section 3 in
[
7
]
: there always exists a logarithmic strip of the form

(1.3)

\displaystyle\big\{z\colon-\log(1+|\mathop{\rm Re}z|)\leqslant C\mathop{\rm Im}z\big\}

that does not contain any resonances. In particular, for every
h>0
, there is only a finite number of resonances in the horizontal strip
\{z\colon-h<\mathop{\rm Im}z<0\}
.

Another point of interest is the characterization problem of resonances sets. The results mentioned above give natural necessary conditions for a set to be a resonances set. Jost functions that correspond to compactly supported potentials are described in
[
10
]
,
[
2
]
, see Theorem
2.1
by E. Korotyaev below, however the explicit sufficient conditions for the resonances sets are not present in the literature. Resonances in every bounded domain are free parameters, see Theorem 1.2 in
[
10
]
. Theorem 3 in
[
2
]
states that there exists a potential with infinitely many resonances in the logarithmic strip of the form (
1.3
),
in Proposition 7 in the paper
[
17
]
M. Zworski constructed a smooth potential on the real line
with infinitely many resonances on the imaginary line. In the present paper we study the following question:

(1.4)

Let
\Lambda
be a set of points in
\{z\colon\mathop{\rm Im}z<0\}
. Does there exist
q
such that
\Lambda\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}\nolimits(q)
?

Theorem 1.1
.

If
\Lambda
satisfies the classical Blaschke condition
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\frac{|\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda|}{1+|\lambda|^{2}}<\infty
and for some
C>0
we have
\Lambda\subset\{z:-\mathop{\rm Im}z\geq C|\mathop{\rm Re}z|\}
then
there exists
q\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with compact support such that
\Lambda\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}\nolimits(q)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
\Lambda
is contained in the domain
\{z:-\mathop{\rm Im}z\geq C|z|^{\beta}\}
for some
C>0
and
\beta>0
. If the counting function
n
of
\Lambda
satisfies the relation
n(r)\lesssim r^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha<\beta
then there exists compactly supported
q\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
such that
\Lambda\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}\nolimits(q)
.

In the next theorem we show that that the previous theorems are sharp in the following sense: the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
no longer holds if we replace the angle with any wider domain; the relation from Theorem
1.2
between the counting function and the width of the allowed domain is essential.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\tau,\rho\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be two positive unbounded increasing continuous functions such that
\tau(r)=o(\rho(r))
as
r\to\infty
. There exists
\Lambda\subset\{z:-\mathop{\rm Im}z\geq\tau(|z|)\}
with
n(r)\leqslant\rho(r)
that
is not a subset of
\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}\nolimits(q)
for any
q\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with compact support.

Let us finish the introduction with some concluding remarks.

•

Theorem
1.2
can be formulated for the domains
\{z:-\mathop{\rm Im}z\geq\tau(|z|)\}
when the function
\tau
satisfies
\tau(r)\gtrsim\log^{5}r
. In this case there exists an unbounded
\rho
such that the assertion
n(t)\leqslant\rho(t)
implies the existence of the required potential
q
.

•

Assertions on the given set
\Lambda
from Theorem
1.1
imply
n(r)=o(r)
as
r\to\infty
, which is consistent with (
1.2
); in Theorem
1.2
we also have
n(r)\lesssim r^{\alpha}=o(r)
as
r\to\infty
. On the other hand, we have
N(r)=O(r)
as
r\to\infty
for the counting function of
\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}\nolimits(q)
hence in both theorems
\Lambda\neq\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}\nolimits(q)
and, even more,
\Lambda
is very small part of
\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}\nolimits(q)
.

2.
Characterization of Jost functions

Jost functions corresponding to the Schrödinger operators with compactly supported potentials are entire functions of exponential type.
Let
\mathcal{PW}_{\sigma}
be the classical Paley-Wiener space of entire functions with spectrum in
[-\sigma,\sigma]
and
\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}=e^{iz\sigma}\mathcal{PW}_{\sigma}
. We also fix the notation

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{+}=\{z\colon\mathop{\rm Im}z>0\},\qquad\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}=\{z\colon\mathop{\rm Im}z\geqslant 0\},\qquad\mathbb{C}_{-}=\{z\colon\mathop{\rm Im}z<0\}.

In the present section we use the characterization of Jost functions from the paper
[
10
]
to reduce question (
1.4
) to the construction of the suitable entire function.

Theorem 2.1

(E. Korotyaev, Theorem 1.1,
[
10
]
)
.

Let
f
be an entire function without zeroes in the closed half-plane
\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle z(f(z)-1)\in\mathop{\mathrm{Const}}\nolimits+\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}.

Then
f
is a Jost function of some Schrödinger operator with
q\in L^{1}([0,\sigma])
.

Corollary 2.2
.

Consider the set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{C}_{-}
. If there exists
r\in\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}
such that
r(0)=0
and
r(\lambda)=\lambda
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
then
\Lambda\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}\nolimits(q)
for some potential
q\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits q\subset[0,\sigma]
.

Proof.

Let
f
be an entire function defined by
z(f-1)=-r
. If
z\neq 0
the equality
f(z)=0
is equivalent to
r(z)=z
hence for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
we have
f(\lambda)=0
. Furthermore,
r\in\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}
is bounded in
\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}
hence
f
has only a finite number of zeros in
\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}
, denote them by
z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}
.
Fix
N
arbitrary points
w_{1},\ldots,w_{N}\in\mathbb{C}_{-}
and define polynomials

\displaystyle P(z)=(z-z_{1})\cdot\ldots\cdot(z-z_{n}),\qquad Q(z)=(z-w_{1})\cdot\ldots\cdot(z-w_{n}).

The function
f_{1}=f\cdot\frac{Q}{P}

is entire, has no zeroes in
\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}
and it satisfies
f_{1}(\lambda)=f(\lambda)=0
for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
.
We claim that
f_{1}
is a Jost functions corresponding to some potential supported on
[0,\sigma]
. According to Theorem
2.1
, it suffices to verify

(2.2)

\displaystyle z(f_{1}-1)\in\mathop{\mathrm{Const}}\nolimits+\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}.

Both
P
and
Q
are monic with
\deg P=\deg Q=N
hence there exists
c\in\mathbb{C}
such that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\deg(z(Q-P)-cP)<N.

The equality
f_{1}=f\cdot\frac{Q}{P}=f+f\cdot\frac{Q-P}{P}
implies

(2.4)

\displaystyle z(f_{1}-1)=z(f-1)+f\cdot\frac{z(Q-P)}{P}=z(f-1)+cf+f\cdot\frac{z(Q-P)-cP}{P}.

Recall that we have
z(f-1)=-r\in\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}
.
The Paley-Wiener theorem gives
r/z\in\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}
hence

\displaystyle f=-\frac{r}{z}+1\in\mathop{\mathrm{Const}}\nolimits+\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}.

By the choice (
2.3
) of the constant
c
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{z(Q-P)-cP}{P}\right|=O\left(|z|^{-1}\right),\qquad|z|\to\infty.

Once again the Paley-Wiener theorem implies that the third term in the right-hand side of (
2.4
) belongs to
\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}
. Inclusion (
2.2
) follows, the proof is concluded.
∎

3.
Ideas of the proofs

Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
address question (
1.4
) of whether a given set
\Lambda
is contained in the resonances set
\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}\nolimits(q)
for some compactly supported
q
.
According to Corollary
2.2
, positive answer to the latter follows from the existence of

r\in\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}
that satisfies
r(0)=0
and
r(\lambda)=\lambda
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Equivalently, the existence of
g\in\mathcal{PW}_{\sigma}
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle g(0)=0,\qquad g(\lambda)=\lambda e^{-i\sigma\lambda},\qquad\lambda\in\Lambda.

Denote
G(z)=ze^{-i\sigma z}
and let
H
be entire with
H(\lambda)=0
for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
.
We want to construct
g
from (
3.1
) by the following interpolation formula:

(3.2)

\displaystyle g(z)=H(z)\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\frac{G(\lambda)}{(z-\lambda)H^{\prime}(\lambda)},\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}.

When
z=\lambda\in\Lambda
there is only one non-zero term in the latter sum that gives
g(\lambda)=G(\lambda)
as required in
(
3.1
)
. The problem with this approach is that the series may not converge for other
z\in\mathbb{C}
. To overcome this obstacle we group elements of
\Lambda
that are close to each other into disjoint sets
\{\Lambda_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
and try to obtain the convergence in (
3.2
) for the following order of summation:

(3.3)

\displaystyle g(z)=H(z)\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}}\frac{G(\lambda)}{(z-\lambda)H^{\prime}(\lambda)},\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}.

We draw contours
\Gamma_{n}
around each
\Lambda_{n}
, see Figure
1
, and rewrite inner sums in (
3.3
) by the residue theorem:

(3.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}}\frac{G(\lambda)}{H^{\prime}(\lambda)(z-\lambda)}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma_{n}}\frac{G(w)\,dw}{H(w)(z-w)}.

The value

is small when

hence the proper estimate on
|H(w)|
from below

|H(w)|\gtrsim e^{-(\sigma-\varepsilon)|\mathop{\rm Im}w|}

5.1.
Luxemburg-Korevaar Construction

Let
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
be the set of points in the complex plane with the counting function

(5.1)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{n(t)}{t^{2}}\,dt=\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{1}{|\lambda_{n}|}<\infty.

n(t)=o(t)

\displaystyle\theta(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{n(u)}{u}\frac{t^{2}}{t^{2}+u^{2}}\,du,\qquad t\geqslant 0,

(Luxemburg, Korevaar, Theorem 5.2

Lemma 5.3
.

For every nonzero
z\in\mathbb{C}
we have

\displaystyle|\cos(z)|\geq\frac{|\mathop{\rm Im}z|}{2|z|}.

Proof.

Let
z=re^{i\theta}=r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)
. The required inequality is equivalent to
|\cos(z)|\geqslant|\sin\theta|/2
.
We have
iz=r(-\sin\theta+i\cos\theta)
and

\displaystyle e^{iz}=e^{r(-\sin\theta+i\cos\theta)}=e^{-r\sin\theta}\left(\cos(r(\cos\theta))+i\sin(r(\cos\theta))\right),

\displaystyle e^{-iz}=e^{r(\sin\theta-i\cos\theta)}=e^{r\sin\theta}\left(\cos(r(\cos\theta))-i\sin(r(\cos\theta))\right),

\displaystyle 2\cos z=\cos(r\cos\theta)\left(e^{-r\sin\theta}+e^{r\sin\theta}\right)+i\sin(r\cos\theta)\left(e^{-r\sin\theta}-e^{r\sin\theta}\right).

If
|\cos(r\cos\theta)|\geqslant\sqrt{2}/2
then we write

\displaystyle|\cos z|\geqslant|\mathop{\rm Re}\cos z|=\bigg|\frac{\cos(r\cos\theta)\left(e^{-r\sin\theta}+e^{r\sin\theta}\right)}{2}\bigg|\geqslant|\cos(r\cos\theta)|\geqslant\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\geqslant\frac{|\sin\theta|}{2}.

Otherwise the inequality
|\sin(r\cos\theta)|\geqslant\sqrt{2}/2
holds. This implies
r\geqslant r|\cos\theta|\geqslant\pi/4
and

\displaystyle|\cos z|\geqslant|\mathop{\rm Im}\cos z|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\frac{\sin(r\cos\theta)\left(e^{-r\sin\theta}-e^{r\sin\theta}\right)}{2}\bigg|

\displaystyle\geqslant|\sin(r\cos\theta)|\cdot|r\sin\theta|\geqslant\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\cdot\frac{\pi|\sin\theta|}{4}\geqslant\frac{|\sin\theta|}{2}.

The proof is concluded.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

For every
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\mathop{\rm Im}z|\geqslant 1
, we have

\displaystyle\log\bigg|\prod_{k\geq 1}\cos\left(\varepsilon_{k}z\right)\bigg|\gtrsim-|z|\cdot\gamma(2|z|)-\psi(2|z|)\cdot\log|z|.

Proof.

Consider the following disjoint sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\left\{k\colon\varepsilon_{k}\leq\frac{1}{2|z|}\right\},\qquad\mathcal{B}=\left\{k\colon\frac{1}{2|z|}<\varepsilon_{k}\right\}.

The set
\mathcal{B}
is finite, we have
\#\mathcal{B}=\psi(2|z|)
. The previous lemma gives

\displaystyle\log\left|\prod_{k\in\mathcal{B}}\cos(\varepsilon_{k}z)\right|\geq\#\mathcal{B}\cdot\log\left(\frac{\operatorname{Im}z}{2|z|}\right)=-\log\frac{2|z|}{\mathop{\rm Im}z}\cdot\psi(2|z|)\gtrsim-\log|z|\cdot\psi(2|z|).

For every
k\in\mathcal{A}
we have
|\varepsilon_{k}z|<1/2
hence
\log|\cos(\varepsilon_{k}z)|\gtrsim-|\varepsilon_{k}z|
. This implies

\displaystyle\log\left|\prod_{k\in\mathcal{A}}\cos(\varepsilon_{k}z)\right|\gtrsim-|z|\sum_{k\in\mathcal{A}}\varepsilon_{k}.

To conclude the proof of the lemma we notice

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{A}}\varepsilon_{k}=\sum_{k\geqslant\psi(2|z|)}\frac{1}{\psi^{-1}(k)}<\int_{\psi(2|z|)}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{\psi^{-1}(x)}=\int_{2|z|}^{\infty}\frac{d\psi(x)}{x}\asymp\int_{2|z|}^{\infty}\frac{w(u)}{u^{2}}du\lesssim\gamma(2|z|).

∎

Lemma 5.5
.

If
z
satisfies

Proof.

As in the previous lemma we partition positive integers into the two following sets:

To finish the proof we combine the obtained estimates for products over

∎

Lemma 6.1
.

For every compactly supported potential

we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\sum_{s\in\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}\nolimits(q)}\frac{|\mathop{\rm Im}s|}{|t-s|^{2}}<\infty.

Proof.

The phase function of the Schrödinger operator is defined by the relation

\displaystyle-2i\varphi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{E^{\prime}}{E}+\frac{\overline{E^{\prime}}}{\overline{E}}=\frac{E^{\prime}\overline{E}-E\overline{E}^{\prime}}{|E|^{2}}

(6.1)

\displaystyle=\frac{AB^{\prime}-A^{\prime}B}{A^{2}+B^{2}}=\frac{AB^{\prime}-A^{\prime}B}{B^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{(A/B)^{2}+1}=\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)^{\prime}\frac{1}{(A/B)^{2}+1}.

Let

and

We claim that these relations hold for all

. In
[
2
]
the

assertion is used in the asymptotic formula (3.3) for

and

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}=\pi n+\frac{C}{n}+\frac{a_{n}}{n},\qquad\mu_{n}=\pi_{n}-\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{C}{n}+\frac{b_{n}}{n},\qquad\{a_{n}\},\{b_{n}\}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})\qquad n\geqslant 1.

For

Substitute

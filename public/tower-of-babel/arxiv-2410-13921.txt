Epidemic waves for a two-group SIRS model with double nonlocal effects in a patchy environment 1 1 1 Research supported by National Natural Science Foundation of China (No. 12071074) and Guangdong Basic and Applied Basic Research Foundation (No. 2023A1515012078).
Chufen Wu, Yonghui Xia, Jianshe Yu

Abstract




Abstract:
We propose a lattice dynamical system that arises in a discrete diffusive two-group epidemic model with latency in a patchy environment. The model considers the SIS form and latency of the disease in group 1, while the SIR form without latency of the disease in group 2. The system includes double nonlocal effects, one effect is the nonlocal diffusion of individuals in isolated patches or niches, while the other effect is the distributed transmission delay representing the incubation of the disease. We demonstrate that there is a threshold value
c^{*}
that can determine the persistence or disappearance of the disease. If
c\geq c^{*}
, then there is an epidemic wave connecting the disease-free equilibrium and endemic equilibrium. In this case, the disease will evolve to endemic. If
0<c<c^{*}
, then the disease will die out.

Keywords:
Epidemic waves; a patchy environment; non-monotonicity; nonlocal

Due to the incubation period and time lag of transmission of infectious diseases, time delays also need to be included in the model.
The presence of time delay can slow down the spread of diseases, as infected individuals require time to transmit the disease to others. Time delay may also lead to fluctuations in the spread of diseases, because delayed transmission by infected individuals can result in both outbreaks and declines of diseases. Lattice dynamical systems in epidemiology can model the evolution of susceptible and infected individuals in discrete niches or patchy environments.
Based on this consideration, we analyze a lattice dynamical system that emerges in a discrete diffusive two-group epidemic model with latency as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}t}S_{j}(t)=d_{1}\mathcal{A}[S_{j}](t)+b_{1}[K_{1}-S_{j}(t)]-S_{j}(t)I_{j}(t)+\sigma I_{j}(t),\\

To determine if a disease can spread at a consistent rate, we always investigate the phenomenon known as traveling wave solutions (referred to
as epidemic waves). As epidemic waves come and go, they can indicate whether a disease is endemic or has disappeared.
The connection of epidemic waves in two tails shows the evolution of the disease over time.
In recent years, Fu, Guo and Wu
[
17
]
investigated the existence and non-existence of epidemic waves
for the following discrete diffusive SI model:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}t}S_{j}(t)=[S_{j+1}(t)+S_{j-1}(t)-2S_{j}(t)]-\gamma S_{j}(t)I_{j}(t),\\
\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}t}I_{j}(t)=d[I_{j+1}(t)+I_{j-1}(t)-2I_{j}(t)]+\gamma S_{j}(t)I_{j}(t)-\delta I_{j}(t).\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

In model system (
1.2
), there is no constant recruitment of susceptibles. In this case, the epidemic wave is a mix of front type (
S
-component) and pulse type (
I
-component). Further, Wu
[
38
]
supplemented the existence of epidemic waves with the critical speed for (
1.2
). Later, Chen, Guo and Hamel
[
11
]
established the existence and non-existence of epidemic waves by introducing the constant recruitment in (
1.2
), that is,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}t}S_{j}(t)=[S_{j+1}(t)+S_{j-1}(t)-2S_{j}(t)]+\mu[1-S_{j}(t)]-\gamma S_{j}(t)I_{j}(t),\\
\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}t}I_{j}(t)=d[I_{j+1}(t)+I_{j-1}(t)-2I_{j}(t)]+\gamma S_{j}(t)I_{j}(t)-(\mu+\delta)I_{j}(t).\end{array}\right.

In this scenario, the epidemic wave is the front type of each component. Subsequently, Zhang, Wang and Liu
[
41
]

proved the epidemic waves would approach to the endemic equilibrium as
j+ct\to\infty
through the creation of a Lyapunov functional.
When considering the combined effects of multi-group interactions, latent periods, and patchy environments, the dynamics of model systems become more interesting and complicated.
San et al.
[

obtained the existence and non-existence of epidemic waves for a discrete two-group SI model with

Shortly before, Li and Zhang

Then we can construct a Lyapunov functional combined with Lasalle’s invariance principle to show the epidemic waves converge to the endemic equilibrium. However, the construction of Lyapunov functional here is non-trivial due to the double nonlocal effects and time delay. Motivated by the work of Huang and Wu

using the singularity analysis of two-sides Laplace transform.

The organization of this paper is as follows.
In Sections 2 and 3, we prove the existence of epidemic waves connecting the disease-free equilibrium
(K_{1},K_{2},0)
and endemic equilibrium
(S_{*},P_{*},I_{*})
for
c\geq c^{*}
. In the first step, by constructing upper and lower solutions in bounded domains and passing to the limit in the whole line, we get the semi-waves. Next, through careful analysis and the construction of a Lyapunov functional, we determine the complete waves. In particular, in the critical case, prior estimates are performed and it is shown that the solution sequences are uniformly bounded. Through proper shifts, negative Laplace transforms, and the fluctuation lemma, we demonstrate that the boundary conditions are satisfied at minus infinity.
Section 4 is devoted to showing the non-existence of epidemic waves for
0<c<c^{*}
.
In the last section, we implemented some numerical simulations to study the qualitative properties of epidemic waves.

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{2}[S,P,I](\xi)

\displaystyle=(\mu-b_{2})P(\xi)+d_{2}\mathcal{A}[P](\xi)+b_{2}K_{2}-P(\xi)I(\xi),

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{3}[S,P,I](\xi)

\displaystyle=(\mu-\delta)I(\xi)+d_{3}\mathcal{A}[I](\xi)+\gamma_{1}\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau)S(\xi-c\tau)I(\xi-c\tau){\mathrm{d}}\tau}+\gamma_{2}P(\xi)I(\xi).

Lemma 2.3
.

For each
k>-\xi_{3}
, the truncated problem (
2.5
) with boundary conditions (
2.6
) has
a unique solution
(S,P,I)\in[C(\mathbb{R})]^{3}\cap[C^{1}(\mathbb{R})/\{\pm k\}]^{3}
such that

\sigma\leq S_{-}\leq S\leq K_{1},\quad 0\leq P_{-}\leq P\leq K_{2},\quad 0\leq I_{-}\leq I\leq I_{+}\quad\text{ over }(-\infty,k].

(2.7)

Proof.

Define an operator
\mathcal{Q}_{k}=(\mathcal{Q}_{1k},\mathcal{Q}_{2k},\mathcal{Q}_{3k}):\Gamma_{k}\to\mathbb{X}_{k}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{1k}[S,P,I](\xi)

\displaystyle={\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{\mu}{c}(\xi+k)}S_{+}(-k)+\frac{1}{c}\int_{-k}^{\xi}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{\mu}{c}(\xi-z)}\mathcal{P}_{1}[S,P,I](z){\mathrm{d}}z,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{2k}[S,P,I](\xi)

\displaystyle={\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{\mu}{c}(\xi+k)}P_{+}(-k)+\frac{1}{c}\int_{-k}^{\xi}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{\mu}{c}(\xi-z)}\mathcal{P}_{2}[S,P,I](z){\mathrm{d}}z,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{3k}[S,P,I](\xi)

\displaystyle={\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{\mu}{c}(\xi+k)}I_{+}(-k)+\frac{1}{c}\int_{-k}^{\xi}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{\mu}{c}(\xi-z)}\mathcal{P}_{3}[S,P,I](z){\mathrm{d}}z,\quad\xi\in[-k,k].

By Lemma
2.2
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{1k}[S_{+},P,I_{-}](\xi)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{\mu}{c}(\xi+k)}S_{+}(-k)+\frac{1}{c}\int_{-k}^{\xi}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{\mu}{c}(\xi-z)}[\mu S_{+}(z)+cS_{+}^{\prime}(z)]{\mathrm{d}}z

\displaystyle={\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{\mu}{c}(\xi+k)}S_{+}(-k)+S_{+}(\xi)-{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{\mu}{c}(\xi+k)}S_{+}(-k)=S_{+}(\xi)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{1k}[S_{-},P,I_{+}](\xi)

\displaystyle\geq{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{\mu}{c}(\xi+k)}S_{+}(-k)+\frac{1}{c}\left\{\int_{-k}^{\xi_{1}}+\int_{\xi_{1}}^{\xi}\right\}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{\mu}{c}(\xi-z)}[\mu S_{-}(z)+cS_{-}^{\prime}(z)]{\mathrm{d}}z

\displaystyle={\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{\mu}{c}(\xi+k)}S_{+}(-k)+S_{-}(\xi)-{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{\mu}{c}(\xi+k)}S_{-}(-k)\geq S_{-}(\xi).

Note that
\mathcal{Q}_{1k}
is nondecreasing in its first variable while decreasing in its third variable. Thus

S_{-}(\xi)\leq Q_{1k}[S,P,I](\xi)\leq S_{+}(\xi),\quad\xi\in[-k,k].

Similarly, we can prove
P_{-}(\xi)\leq Q_{2k}[S,P,I](\xi)\leq P_{+}(\xi)
and
I_{-}(\xi)\leq Q_{3k}[S,P,I](\xi)\leq I_{+}(\xi)
for all
\xi\in[-k,k]
.
On the other hand,
\mathcal{Q}_{k}[S,P,I](-k)=(S_{+}(-k),P_{+}(-k),I_{+}(-k))
. Therefore,
\mathcal{Q}_{k}(\Gamma_{k})\subset\Gamma_{k}
.

Notice that any fixed-point of
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
is a solution of (
2.5
) and (
2.6
). We first show that the operator
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
is completely continuous. Since for
\xi\in[-k,k]
,

\displaystyle|S^{\prime}(\xi)|

\displaystyle=\left|-\frac{\mu}{c}S(\xi)+\frac{1}{c}\mathcal{P}_{1}[S,P,I](\xi)\right|\leq 2\frac{\mu}{c}K_{1},

\displaystyle|P^{\prime}(\xi)|

\displaystyle=\left|-\frac{\mu}{c}P(\xi)+\frac{1}{c}\mathcal{P}_{2}[S,P,I](\xi)\right|\leq 2\frac{\mu}{c}K_{2},

\displaystyle|I^{\prime}(\xi)|

\displaystyle=\left|-\frac{\mu}{c}I(\xi)+\frac{1}{c}\mathcal{P}_{3}[S,P,I](\xi)\right|\leq\left(2\frac{\mu}{c}+\lambda_{1}\right){\mathrm{e}}^{\lambda_{1}k},

applying the Arzela-Ascoli’s theorem on
[-k,k]
, we know that
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
is compact. Next we prove the operator
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
is also continuous. Straightforward calculations give that

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{P}_{1}[S_{1},P_{1},I_{1}](\cdot)-\mathcal{P}_{1}[S_{2},P_{2},I_{2}](\cdot)\big|

(2.8)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big|(\mu-d_{1}-b_{1}-I_{1})(S_{1}-S_{2})\big|+\big|(S_{2}-\sigma)(I_{1}-I_{2})\big|

\displaystyle+d_{1}\left|\sum_{|i|=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)\big[S_{1}(\xi-i)-S_{2}(\xi-i)\big]\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\mu-b_{1}-I_{1})\max_{[-k,k]}|S_{1}-S_{2}|+(S_{2}-\sigma)\max_{[-k,k]}|I_{1}-I_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu|S_{1}-S_{2}|_{1}+(K_{1}-\sigma)|I_{1}-I_{2}|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\mu+K_{1}-\sigma)\|(S_{1}-S_{2},P_{1}-P_{2},I_{1}-I_{2})\|_{\mathbb{X}_{k}}.

From (
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{Q}_{1k}[S_{1},P_{1},I_{1}](\xi)-\mathcal{Q}_{1k}[S_{2},P_{2},I_{2}](\xi)\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\int_{-k}^{\xi}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{\mu}{c}(\xi-z)}\big|\mathcal{P}_{1}[S_{1},P_{1},I_{1}](z)-\mathcal{P}_{1}[S_{2},P_{2},I_{2}](z)\big|{\mathrm{d}}z

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mu+K_{1}-\sigma}{\mu}\|(S_{1}-S_{2},P_{1}-P_{2},I_{1}-I_{2})\|_{\mathbb{X}_{k}}.

Thus the operator
\mathcal{Q}_{1k}
is continuous. Similarly, we can show
\mathcal{Q}_{2k}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{3k}
are also
continuous. At last, using the Schauder’s fixed-point theorem, the conclusion holds.
∎

The next result states the existence of semi-waves.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that (J), (f) and (H) hold. For
c>c^{*}
, the system (
2.1
) has a solution
(S,P,I)
such that
\sigma<S<K_{1}
,
0<P<K_{2}
and

on

\mathbb{R}
. Moreover

\lim_{\xi\rightarrow-\infty}(S,P,I)(\xi)=(K_{1},K_{2},0).

(2.9)

Proof.

By means of Lemma
2.3
, the Arzela-Ascoli’s theorem and a successive extraction of
subsequences argument, there are some functions
S,P,I\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that
(S,P,I)
is a nonnegative solution of (
2.1
). Also,
(S,P,I)
satisfies the inequality (
2.7
). Hence, (
2.9
) holds.

Note
I\geq I_{-}>0
on
(-\infty,\xi_{3})
. Assume some
\xi_{4}\in[\xi_{3},\infty)
is the rightmost point such that
I(\xi_{4})=0
. Then
I^{\prime}(\xi_{4})=0
. By (
2.1
),

\displaystyle 0=cI^{\prime}(\xi_{4})

\displaystyle=d_{3}\sum_{i\neq 0}J(i)I(\xi_{4}-i)+\gamma_{1}\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau)S(\xi_{4}-c\tau)I(\xi_{4}-c\tau){\mathrm{d}}\tau}

\displaystyle\geq d_{3}\sum_{i\neq 0}J(i)I(\xi_{4}-i)\geq 0,

which yields that
I(\xi_{4}+i)=0
for all

with
J(i)>0
. That’s a contradiction to the definition of
\xi_{4}
.
Thus,
I>0
on
\mathbb{R}
. Similarly, we can show that

\sigma<S<K_{1}
and
0<P<K_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}
.
∎

Note that the diffusion is nonlocal. In order to obtain the uniform upper and lower bounds of
\frac{I^{\prime}(\xi)}{I(\xi)}
on
\mathbb{R}
,
we require the kernel function
J(\cdot)
is compactly supported. Then we have

Lemma 2.4
.

\frac{I^{\prime}(\xi)}{I(\xi)}
is uniformly bounded on
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Remember that

\displaystyle cI^{\prime}(\xi)+(d_{3}+\delta)I(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{3}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)[I(\xi+i)+I(\xi-i)]+\gamma_{1}\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau)S(\xi-c\tau)I(\xi-c\tau){\mathrm{d}}\tau}+\gamma_{2}P(\xi)I(\xi).

Denote
u(\xi)=I(\xi){\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}\xi}
. Then
u(\xi)
is increasing on
\mathbb{R}
. Thus,
I(\xi-s){\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}(\xi-s)}\leq I(\xi){\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}\xi}
for any
s>0
. Namely,
I(\xi-s)\leq I(\xi){\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}s}
for any
s>0
. Especially,
for
s=i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we get
I(\xi-i)\leq I(\xi){\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}i}
. It implies that for
J(i)>0
,

\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(\xi-i)\leq I(\xi)\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i){\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}i},\quad\forall\;\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.10)

The inequality (
2.10
) shows that

\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(\xi-i)}{I(\xi)}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i){\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}i},\quad\forall\;\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.11)

Since

\left[I(z){\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}z}\right]^{\prime}\\
>\frac{d_{3}}{c}{\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}z}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(z+i),

(2.12)

integrating the inequality (
2.12
) over
[\xi,\xi+\frac{i}{2}]
with
1\leq i\leq N_{1}
, we reach

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}(\xi+\frac{i}{2})}I(\xi+\frac{i}{2})-{\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}\xi}I(\xi)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{d_{3}}{c}\int_{\xi}^{\xi+\frac{i}{2}}{\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}z}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(z+i){\mathrm{d}}z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d_{3}}{c}\int_{\xi}^{\xi+\frac{i}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i){\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}(z+i)}I(z+i){\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}i}{\mathrm{d}}z

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{d_{3}}{c}\int_{\xi}^{\xi+\frac{i}{2}}{\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}\xi}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(\xi+i){\mathrm{d}}z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d_{3}i}{2c}{\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}\xi}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(\xi+i)\geq\frac{d_{3}}{2c}{\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}\xi}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(\xi+i).

Therefore, for
1\leq i\leq N_{1}
,

\displaystyle I(\xi+\frac{i}{2})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{d_{3}}{2c}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}\frac{i}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(\xi+i)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{d_{3}}{2c}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}\frac{N_{1}}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(\xi+i).

It yields that

\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(\xi+\frac{i}{2})\geq\frac{d_{3}}{4c}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}\frac{N_{1}}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(\xi+i)

(2.13)

due to
\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)=\frac{1}{2}
. The inequality (
2.13
) tells us that

\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(\xi+i)}{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(\xi+\frac{i}{2})}\leq\frac{4c}{d_{3}}{\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}\frac{N_{1}}{2}},\quad\forall\;\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.14)

Similarly, by integrating the inequality (
2.12
) over
[\xi,\xi+i]
with
1\leq i\leq N_{1}
, we also have

{\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}i}I(\xi+i)-I(\xi)\geq\frac{d_{3}}{c}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(\xi+i).

Hence,

I(\xi+i)\geq\left[I(\xi)+\frac{d_{3}}{c}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(\xi+i)\right]{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}N_{1}}.

(2.15)

Substituting (
2.15
) into (
2.12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left[I(z){\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}z}\right]^{\prime}

(2.16)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{d_{3}}{c}{\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}(z-N_{1})}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)\left[I(z)+\frac{d_{3}}{c}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(z+i)\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{d_{3}^{2}}{c^{2}}{\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}(z-N_{1})}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(z+i)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{3}^{2}}{2c^{2}}{\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}(z-N_{1})}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(z+i)\right].

Integrating the inequality (
2.16
) over
[\xi-\frac{i}{2},\xi]
with
1\leq i\leq N_{1}
, then similarly as above,

{\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}\xi}I(\xi)\geq\frac{d_{3}^{2}}{2c^{2}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}N_{1}}\int_{\xi-\frac{i}{2}}^{\xi}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(\xi+\frac{i}{2}){\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}(\xi-\frac{i}{2})}{\mathrm{d}}z.

Thus, for
1\leq i\leq N_{1}
,

\displaystyle I(\xi)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{d_{3}^{2}}{2c^{2}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}N_{1}}\cdot\frac{i}{2}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(\xi+\frac{i}{2}){\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}\frac{i}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{d_{3}^{2}}{4c^{2}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}N_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(\xi+\frac{i}{2}){\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}\frac{N_{1}}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d_{3}^{2}}{4c^{2}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}\frac{3N_{1}}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(\xi+\frac{i}{2}).

It implies that

\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(\xi+\frac{i}{2})}{I(\xi)}\leq\frac{4c^{2}}{d_{3}^{2}}{\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}\frac{3N_{1}}{2}},\quad\forall\;\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.17)

Inequalities (
2.14
) and (
2.17
) show that

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(\xi+i)}{I(\xi)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(\xi+i)}{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(\xi+\frac{i}{2})}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)I(\xi+\frac{i}{2})}{I(\xi)}

(2.18)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{16c^{3}}{d_{3}^{3}}{\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{2(d_{3}+\delta)N_{1}}{c}}.

Denote
\eta(\xi)=\frac{I^{\prime}(\xi)}{I(\xi)}
. Then

\displaystyle\eta(\xi)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}+\frac{\gamma_{1}}{c}\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau)S(\xi-c\tau)\frac{I(\xi-c\tau)}{I(\xi)}{\mathrm{d}}\tau}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}+\frac{\gamma_{1}\sigma}{c}\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau){\mathrm{e}}^{\int_{\xi}^{\xi-c\tau}\eta(z){\mathrm{d}}z}{\mathrm{d}}\tau}.

Put
w(\xi)={\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}\xi+\int_{0}^{\xi}\eta(z){\mathrm{d}}z}
.
Then
w(\xi)
satisfies

\displaystyle w^{\prime}(\xi)

\displaystyle=w(\xi)\left[\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}+\eta(\xi)\right]\geq\frac{\gamma_{1}\sigma}{c}\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau){\mathrm{e}}^{\int_{\xi}^{\xi-c\tau}\eta(z){\mathrm{d}}z}{\mathrm{d}}\tau}.

It implies
w(\xi)
is increasing. Therefore,

\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau)\frac{I(\xi-c\tau)}{I(\xi)}{\mathrm{d}}\tau}=\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau){\mathrm{e}}^{(d_{3}+\delta)\tau}\frac{w(\xi-c\tau)}{w(\xi)}{\mathrm{d}}\tau}\leq\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau){\mathrm{e}}^{(d_{3}+\delta)\tau}{\mathrm{d}}\tau},

which results in

0<\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau)S(\xi-c\tau)\frac{I(\xi-c\tau)}{I(\xi)}{\mathrm{d}}\tau}\leq K_{1}\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau){\mathrm{e}}^{(d_{3}+\delta)\tau}{\mathrm{d}}\tau}.

(2.19)

By inequalities (
2.11
), (
2.18
) and (
2.19
), then

\displaystyle-\frac{(d_{3}+\delta)}{c}

\displaystyle<\frac{I^{\prime}(\xi)}{I(\xi)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d_{3}}{c}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i){\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{d_{3}+\delta}{c}i}+\frac{16c^{3}}{d_{3}^{3}}{\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{2(d_{3}+\delta)N_{1}}{c}}\right]+\frac{\gamma_{1}K_{1}}{c}\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau){\mathrm{e}}^{(d_{3}+\delta)\tau}{\mathrm{d}}\tau}+\frac{\gamma_{2}K_{2}}{c}.

∎

With the help of Lemma
2.4
, we are able to verify that
I
is uniformly bounded on
\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

For any sequence
\{c_{\iota}\}
with
c_{\iota}\in(c^{*},\tilde{c})
, let
\{(S_{\iota},P_{\iota},I_{\iota})\}
be the solution sequence of (
2.1
) corresponding to wave speeds
\{c_{\iota}\}
.
Then there exists some constant
M>0
(independent of
\iota
) such that
\|I_{\iota}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq M
.

Proof.

We will prove the lemma by three claims.

Claim 1.
If there exists a sequence
\{\xi_{\iota}\}
such that

\lim_{\iota\to\infty}I_{\iota}(\xi_{\iota})=\infty
, then
S_{\iota}(\xi+\xi_{\iota})\to\sigma
and
P_{\iota}(\xi+\xi_{\iota})\to 0
in
C_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
as
\iota\to\infty
.

Note that

I_{\iota}(\xi+\xi_{\iota})=I_{\iota}(\xi_{\iota}){\mathrm{e}}^{\int_{\xi_{\iota}}^{\xi+\xi_{\iota}}\frac{I_{\iota}^{\prime}(\theta)}{I_{\iota}(\theta)}{\mathrm{d}}\theta}.

For any fixed
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
with
\nu>0
, since
\frac{I^{\prime}_{\iota}(\xi)}{I_{\iota}(\xi)}
is uniformly bounded on
\mathbb{R}
,

{\mathrm{e}}^{\int_{\xi_{\iota}}^{\xi+\xi_{\iota}}\frac{I_{\iota}^{\prime}(\theta)}{I_{\iota}(\theta)}{\mathrm{d}}\theta}
is uniformly bounded for any
\xi\in[-\nu,\nu]
. Thus,
if
I_{\iota}(\xi_{\iota})\to\infty
as
\iota\to\infty
, then
I_{\iota}(\xi+\xi_{\iota})\to\infty
for any
\xi\in[-\nu,\nu]
as
\iota\to\infty
.
Notice we always get
S_{\iota}(\xi+\xi_{\iota})\geq\sigma
for any
\xi\in[-\nu,\nu]
as
\iota\to\infty
.
Suppose
S_{\iota}(\xi+\xi_{\iota})\not\to\sigma
for
\xi\in[-\nu,\nu]
as
\iota\to\infty
. Then up to extraction a subsequence, there exists a sequence
\{\vartheta_{\iota}\}
with
\vartheta_{\iota}\in[-\nu,\nu]
, such
that
S_{\iota}(\vartheta_{\iota}+\xi_{\iota})\geq\sigma+r^{\prime}
for some positive constant
r^{\prime}
.
Letting
\tilde{\xi}_{\iota}=\vartheta_{\iota}+\xi_{\iota}
, then
I_{\iota}(\tilde{\xi}_{\iota})\to\infty
as
\iota\to\infty
and
S_{\iota}(\tilde{\xi}_{\iota})\geq\sigma+r^{\prime}
. Since

\displaystyle S_{\iota}^{\prime}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{1}}{c}\mathcal{A}[S_{\iota}](\xi)+\frac{b_{1}}{c}[K_{1}-S_{\iota}(\xi)]-\frac{1}{c}[S_{\iota}(\xi)-\sigma]I_{\iota}(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d_{1}}{c}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)\cdot 2K_{1}+\frac{b_{1}}{c}K_{1}-\frac{1}{c}[S_{\iota}(\xi)-\sigma]I_{\iota}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{(d_{1}+b_{1})K_{1}}{c}-\frac{1}{c}[S_{\iota}(\xi)-\sigma]I_{\iota}(\xi),

it follows that for all
\xi\in[\tilde{\xi}_{\iota}-\varepsilon_{0},\tilde{\xi}_{\iota}]
with
\varepsilon_{0}=\frac{r^{\prime}c}{2(d_{1}+b_{1})K_{1}}
,

S_{\iota}(\xi)=S_{\iota}(\tilde{\xi}_{\iota})-\int_{\xi}^{\tilde{\xi}_{\iota}}S_{\iota}^{\prime}(\theta){\mathrm{d}}\theta\geq\sigma+r^{\prime}-\frac{(d_{1}+b_{1})K_{1}}{c}\varepsilon_{0}=\sigma+\frac{r^{\prime}}{2}.

Therefore for all
\xi\in[\tilde{\xi}_{\iota}-\varepsilon_{0},\tilde{\xi}_{\iota}]
,

S_{\iota}^{\prime}(\xi)\leq\frac{(d_{1}+b_{1})K_{1}}{c}-\frac{r^{\prime}}{2c}I_{\iota}(\xi)\to-\infty,\quad\text{as }\iota\to\infty.

This contradicts the fact that
S_{\iota}
is bounded. Thus due to the arbitrariness of
\nu
,
S_{\iota}(\xi+\xi_{\iota})\to\sigma
in
C_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
as
\iota\to\infty
.
Similarly, we can show that
P_{\iota}(\xi+\xi_{\iota})\to 0
in
C_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
as
\iota\to\infty
.

Letting
\{c_{\iota}\}=\{c\}
and
(S_{\iota},P_{\iota},I_{\iota})=(S,P,I)
, then we can prove the following claim.

Claim 2.
If
\limsup_{\xi\to\infty}I(\xi)=\infty
, then
\lim_{\xi\to\infty}I(\xi)=\infty
and
I(\xi)
is strictly increasing for
\xi\gg 1
.

For otherwise, assume
\liminf_{\xi\to\infty}I(\xi)=:\underline{I}<\infty
. Thus, there exists a sequence
\{\vartheta_{j}\}

satisfying
\vartheta_{j}\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
and
\lim_{j\to\infty}I(\vartheta_{j})=\underline{I}
.
Note that
I(\vartheta_{j})\leq\underline{I}+1
for large
j
. For each
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, put some point
\xi_{j}\in(\vartheta_{j},\vartheta_{j+1})

such that
I(\xi_{j})=\max_{\xi\in[\vartheta_{j},\vartheta_{j+1}]}I(\xi)
. Since
\limsup_{\xi\to\infty}I(\xi)=\infty
,

\lim_{j\to\infty}I(\xi_{j})=\infty
. We next show that
[\xi_{j}-N_{1}-ch,\xi_{j}+N_{1}+ch]\subset[\vartheta_{j},\vartheta_{j+1}]
.
According to Lemma

\xi\in\mathbb{R}

c_{n}S_{n}^{\prime}(\xi)=d_{1}\mathcal{A}[S_{n}](\xi)+b_{1}[K_{1}-S_{n}(\xi)]-[S_{n}(\xi)-\sigma]I_{n}(\xi).

(3.1)

Letting
n\to\infty
in (
3.1
) and noting
S_{n}^{\prime}(\xi)
is uniformly convergent in
C_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
, then
S_{n}^{\prime}(\xi)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Hence,
I_{n}(\xi)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
uniformly for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
. This leads to
\sup_{\mathbb{R}}I_{n}(\xi)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
It contradicts with
\inf_{n}\|I_{n}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}>0
. Similarly, we can prove
\inf_{\mathbb{R}}P_{n}(\xi)\leq K_{2}-\tilde{\varsigma}
for some
0<\tilde{\varsigma}<K_{2}
.

Note that
\lim_{\xi\rightarrow-\infty}S_{n}(\xi)=K_{1}
and
\lim_{\xi\rightarrow-\infty}P_{n}(\xi)=K_{2}
. By Step 2, choose two constants
\varsigma_{1},\varsigma_{2}\in(0,\tilde{\varsigma})
. By translation along to
\xi
-axis, we may assume that
S_{n}(0)=K_{1}-\varsigma_{1},P_{n}(0)=K_{2}-\varsigma_{2}
, and
S_{n}(\xi)\geq K_{1}-\varsigma_{1},P_{n}(\xi)\geq K_{2}-\varsigma_{2}
for all
\xi\leq 0
.
As discussions above, there are some functions
S_{\star}(\xi),P_{\star}(\xi),I_{\star}(\xi)
exist such that

(S_{n}(\xi),P_{n}(\xi),I_{n}(\xi))\to(S_{\star}(\xi),P_{\star}(\xi),I_{\star}(\xi))
in
\left[C^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R})\right]^{3}
as
n\to\infty
.
Also,
(S_{\star}(\xi),P_{\star}(\xi),I_{\star}(\xi))
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}c^{*}S_{\star}^{\prime}(\xi)=d_{1}\mathcal{A}[S_{\star}](\xi)+b_{1}[K_{1}-S_{\star}(\xi)]-S_{\star}(\xi)I_{\star}(\xi)+\sigma I_{\star}(\xi),\\
c^{*}P_{\star}^{\prime}(\xi)=d_{2}\mathcal{A}[P_{\star}](\xi)+b_{2}[K_{2}-P_{\star}(\xi)]-P_{\star}(\xi)I_{\star}(\xi),\\
c^{*}I_{\star}^{\prime}(\xi)=d_{3}\mathcal{A}[I_{\star}](\xi)+\gamma_{1}\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau)S_{\star}(\xi-c\tau)I_{\star}(\xi-c\tau){\mathrm{d}}\tau}-\delta I_{\star}(\xi)+\gamma_{2}P_{\star}(\xi)I_{\star}(\xi).\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

It is easy to prove
\sigma<S_{\star}<K_{1}
,
0<P_{\star}<K_{2}
and
I_{\star}>0
on
\mathbb{R}
.

Step 3.
Prove
\lim_{\xi\rightarrow-\infty}(S_{\star}(\xi),P_{\star}(\xi),I_{\star}(\xi))=(K_{1},K_{2},0)
.

Note that
K_{1}>S_{\star}(z)\geq K_{1}-\varsigma_{1}
and
K_{2}>P_{\star}(z)\geq K_{2}-\varsigma_{2}
for all
z\leq 0
.
Then
\inf_{z\in(-\infty,0]}S_{\star}(z)\to K_{1}
and
\inf_{z\in(-\infty,0]}P_{\star}(z)\to K_{2}
as

\tilde{\varsigma}\to 0
. Notice that
\inf_{z\in(-\infty,0]}S_{\star}(z)\leq\inf_{z\in(-\infty,-c^{*}\tau]}S_{\star}(z)\leq\inf_{z\in(-\infty,-c^{*}h]}S_{\star}(z)
. Then for any
\tau\in[0,h]
,
\inf_{z\in(-\infty,-c^{*}\tau]}S_{\star}(z)\to K_{1}
as

\tilde{\varsigma}\to 0
. Pick a sufficiently small
\tilde{\varsigma}
such that

\sup_{z\in(-\infty,0]}[K_{1}-S_{\star}(z-c^{*}\tau)]=K_{1}-\inf_{z\in(-\infty,0]}S_{\star}(z-c^{*}\tau)=:\eta_{1}<\frac{\triangle_{K}(0,c^{*})}{4\gamma_{1}}

and

\sup_{z\in(-\infty,0]}[K_{2}-P_{\star}(z)]=K_{2}-\inf_{z\in(-\infty,0]}P_{\star}(z)=:\eta_{2}<\frac{\triangle_{K}(0,c^{*})}{4\gamma_{2}}.

For
\lambda<0
, we can define
\mathscr{N}[I_{\star}](\lambda)=\int_{-\infty}^{0}I_{\star}(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda z}{\mathrm{d}}z
. Indeed,

\int_{-\infty}^{0}I_{\star}(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda z}{\mathrm{d}}z=\int^{\infty}_{0}I_{\star}(-s){\mathrm{e}}^{\lambda s}{\mathrm{d}}s
and
\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}I_{\star}(-s){\mathrm{e}}^{\lambda s}s^{2}=0
, by Cauchy’s integral converge principle, then
\int^{\infty}_{0}I_{\star}(-s){\mathrm{e}}^{\lambda s}{\mathrm{d}}s
converges. The uniform boundedness of
I^{\prime}_{\star}(\xi)
shows the uniform continuity of
I_{\star}(\xi)
. If
\int_{-\infty}^{0}I_{\star}(\xi){\mathrm{d}}\xi<\infty
, then
\lim_{\xi\rightarrow-\infty}I_{\star}(\xi)=0
and we have achieved the aim. So we might as well suppose that
\int_{-\infty}^{0}I_{\star}(\xi){\mathrm{d}}\xi\to\infty
.
Taking the negative Laplace transform of
I_{\star}
-equation in (
3.2
), we get

c^{*}I_{\star}(0)+B_{1}(\lambda)+B_{2}(\lambda)=\triangle_{K}(\lambda,c^{*})\mathscr{N}[I_{\star}](\lambda),

wherein

\displaystyle B_{1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=d_{3}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)\left[\int_{-i}^{0}I_{\star}(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda(z+i)}{\mathrm{d}}z-\int^{i}_{0}I_{\star}(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda(z-i)}{\mathrm{d}}z\right]

\displaystyle+\gamma_{1}K_{1}\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau)\int_{-c^{*}\tau}^{0}I_{\star}(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda(z+c^{*}\tau)}{\mathrm{d}}z{\mathrm{d}}\tau,

\displaystyle B_{2}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{1}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau)[K_{1}-S_{\star}(z-c^{*}\tau)]I_{\star}(z-c^{*}\tau){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda z}{\mathrm{d}}\tau{\mathrm{d}}z

\displaystyle+\gamma_{2}\int_{-\infty}^{0}[K_{2}-P_{\star}(z)]I_{\star}(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda z}{\mathrm{d}}z.

Since

\displaystyle B_{2}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{1}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau)\sup_{z\in(-\infty,0]}[K_{1}-S_{\star}(z-c^{*}\tau)]I_{\star}(z-c^{*}\tau){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda z}{\mathrm{d}}\tau{\mathrm{d}}z

\displaystyle+\gamma_{2}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\sup_{z\in(-\infty,0]}[K_{2}-P_{\star}(z)]I_{\star}(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda z}{\mathrm{d}}z

\displaystyle=\gamma_{1}\eta_{1}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau)I_{\star}(z-c^{*}\tau){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda z}{\mathrm{d}}\tau{\mathrm{d}}z+\gamma_{2}\eta_{2}\int_{-\infty}^{0}I_{\star}(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda z}{\mathrm{d}}z

\displaystyle=\gamma_{1}\eta_{1}\left[\mathscr{N}[I_{\star}](\lambda)\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda c^{*}\tau}{\mathrm{d}}\tau-\int_{0}^{h}\int_{-c^{*}\tau}^{0}f(\tau){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda(z+c^{*}\tau)}I_{\star}(z){\mathrm{d}}z{\mathrm{d}}\tau\right]+\gamma_{2}\eta_{2}\mathscr{N}[I_{\star}](\lambda),

we get

\displaystyle B_{1}(\lambda)+B_{2}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq d_{3}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)\left[\int_{-i}^{0}I_{\star}(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda(z+i)}{\mathrm{d}}z-\int^{i}_{0}I_{\star}(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda(z-i)}{\mathrm{d}}z\right]

\displaystyle+\gamma_{1}(K_{1}-\eta_{1})\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau)\int_{-c^{*}\tau}^{0}I_{\star}(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda(z+c^{*}\tau)}{\mathrm{d}}z{\mathrm{d}}\tau

\displaystyle+\gamma_{1}\eta_{1}\mathscr{N}[I_{\star}](\lambda)\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda c^{*}\tau}{\mathrm{d}}\tau+\gamma_{2}\eta_{2}\mathscr{N}[I_{\star}](\lambda).

At last, we have

\displaystyle\left[\triangle_{K}(\lambda,c^{*})-\gamma_{1}\eta_{1}\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda c^{*}\tau}{\mathrm{d}}\tau-\gamma_{2}\eta_{2}\right]\mathscr{N}[I_{\star}](\lambda)

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq d_{3}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}J(i)\left[\int_{-i}^{0}I_{\star}(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda(z+i)}{\mathrm{d}}z-\int^{i}_{0}I_{\star}(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda(z-i)}{\mathrm{d}}z\right]

\displaystyle+\gamma_{1}(K_{1}-\eta_{1})\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau)\int_{-c^{*}\tau}^{0}I_{\star}(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda(z+c^{*}\tau)}{\mathrm{d}}z{\mathrm{d}}\tau+c^{*}I_{\star}(0).

Since
\triangle_{K}(0,c^{*})-\gamma_{1}\eta_{1}-\gamma_{2}\eta_{2}>\frac{1}{2}\triangle_{K}(0,c^{*})>0
, setting
\lambda\to 0
in (
3.3
), we have a contradiction. In fact, in this situation, the left-hand side of (
3.3
) is unbounded while the right-hand side of (
3.3
) is bounded. Therefore,
\lim_{\xi\rightarrow-\infty}I_{\star}(\xi)=0
.

We now prove
\lim_{\xi\rightarrow-\infty}S_{\star}(\xi)=K_{1}
and
\lim_{\xi\rightarrow-\infty}P_{\star}(\xi)=K_{2}
. Assume

\underline{S}_{\star}:=\liminf_{\xi\rightarrow-\infty}S_{\star}(\xi)<\limsup_{\xi\rightarrow-\infty}S_{\star}(\xi)=:\bar{S}_{\star}.

By fluctuation lemma, there are two sequences
\{x_{k}\}
and
\{y_{k}\}
with
x_{k},y_{k}\to-\infty
as
k\to\infty
such that

S_{\star}(x_{k})\to\underline{S}_{\star}\text{ as }k\to\infty,\qquad\underline{S}_{\star}^{\prime}(x_{k})=0,

S_{\star}(y_{k})\to\bar{S}_{\star}\text{ as }k\to\infty,\qquad\bar{S}_{\star}^{\prime}(y_{k})=0.

We follow from
S_{\star}
-equation in (
3.2
) that

0=b_{1}[K_{1}-\underline{S}_{\star}]
and
0=b_{1}[K_{1}-\bar{S}_{\star}]
.
Thus
\underline{S}_{\star}=K_{1}=\bar{S}_{\star}
. It implies that
\lim_{\xi\rightarrow-\infty}S_{\star}(\xi)=K_{1}
. In a similar way, we can show that

\lim_{\xi\rightarrow-\infty}P_{\star}(\xi)=K_{2}
.

Note that the Lyapunov functional still holds for the case
c=c^{*}
. Hence,
\lim_{\xi\rightarrow\infty}(S,P,I)(\xi)=(S^{*},P^{*},I^{*})
.

∎

4
Non-existence of epidemic wave for
0<c<c^{*}

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that (J), (f) and (H) hold. For
0<c<c^{*}
, the system (
2.1
) has no positive solution
(S,P,I)
such that
\sigma<S<K_{1}
and
P,I>0
on

\mathbb{R}
and
\lim_{\xi\rightarrow-\infty}(S,P,I)(\xi)=(K_{1},K_{2},0)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.6
, for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
0<\text{Re}\lambda<\rho
, the bilateral Laplace transform of
I
, that is,
\mathscr{L}[I](\lambda)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}I(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda z}{\mathrm{d}}z
is well-defined.
Performing the bilateral Laplace transform of
I
to the I-equation of (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\triangle_{K}(\lambda,c)\mathscr{L}[I](\lambda)

(4.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda c\tau}{\mathrm{d}}\tau\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big[K_{1}-S(z)\big]I(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda z}{\mathrm{d}}z+\gamma_{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big[K_{2}-P(z)\big]I(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda z}{\mathrm{d}}z.

Assume such solution did exist. Note
\lim_{\xi\rightarrow-\infty}(S,P,I)(\xi)=(K_{1},K_{2},0)
. For any
\epsilon_{0}>0
sufficiently small, choose
z^{*}
so large that
K_{1}-S(z)<\epsilon_{0},K_{2}-P(z)<\epsilon_{0}
and
I(z)=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{0}
for
z<-z^{*}
. Then for
z<-z^{*}
,

\big[K_{1}-S(z)\big]I(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-2\rho z}<\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{I(z)}\big[I(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\rho z}\big]^{2}=2\big[I(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\rho z}\big]^{2}\leq 2M_{2}^{2}.

Similarly,
\big[K_{2}-P(z)\big]I(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-2\rho z}<2M_{2}^{2}
.
Hence the right-hand side of (
4.1
) is well-defined for
0<\text{Re}\lambda<2\rho
. Lemma
2.1
shows that
\triangle_{K}(\lambda,c)>0

for
0<c<c^{*}
and
\lambda>0
. Thus,
\mathscr{L}[I](\lambda)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}I(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda z}{\mathrm{d}}z
can be extended to
0<\text{Re}\lambda<2\rho
. In the following we prove
\mathscr{L}[I](\lambda)
is well-defined for all
\text{Re}\lambda>0
.
In view of the property of Laplace transform, we see that either there is some number
\varrho
such that
\mathscr{L}[I](\lambda)
is
analytic in the strip
0<\text{Re}\lambda<\varrho
and has a singularity at
\lambda=\varrho
or
\mathscr{L}[I](\lambda)
is well-defined for all
\text{Re}\lambda>0
. If
2\rho<\varrho
, then for any
0<\theta<1
and
\theta^{*}:=2\rho+\theta(\varrho-2\rho)<\varrho
, we have

I(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\theta^{*}z}\leq M_{2}
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}
. Similarly,
\big[K_{1}-S(z)\big]I(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-2\theta^{*}z}\leq 2M_{2}^{2}
and

\big[K_{2}-P(z)\big]I(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-2\theta^{*}z}\leq 2M_{2}^{2}
. Then the right-hand side of (
4.1
) is well-defined for
0<\text{Re}\lambda<2\theta^{*}
. If
\max\{0,\frac{\varrho-\theta^{*}-2\rho}{\varrho-2\rho}\}<\theta<1
,
then
\varrho<2\theta^{*}
, which contradicts the singularity of
\varrho
. Therefore,
\mathscr{L}[I](\lambda)
is well-defined for all
\text{Re}\lambda>0
.
By (
4.1
),

\mathscr{L}[I](\lambda)=\frac{\gamma_{1}\int_{0}^{h}f(\tau){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda c\tau}{\mathrm{d}}\tau\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}[K_{1}-S(z)]I(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda z}{\mathrm{d}}z+\gamma_{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big[K_{2}-P(z)\big]I(z){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda z}{\mathrm{d}}z}{\triangle_{K}(\lambda,c)}.

(4.2)

Letting
\lambda\to\infty
in (
4.2
), it follows from
\triangle_{K}(\lambda,c)\to\infty
that the right-hand side of (
4.2
)
is zero. This contradicts the definition of
\mathscr{L}[I](\lambda)
. Therefore, we have proved the non-existence of epidemic waves.

∎

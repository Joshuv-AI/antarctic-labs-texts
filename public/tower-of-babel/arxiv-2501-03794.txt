Several facts about Theodor Wittstein, Gaetano Balducci, and some expressions of the net single premiums under their mortality assumption
Andrius Grigutis, Eglė Matulevičiūtė, MindaugasVenckevičius

Abstract
The mathematical essence in life insurance spins around the search of the numerical characteristics of the random variables T x T_{x} , ν T x \nu^{T_{x}} , T x ​ ν T x T_{x}\nu^{T_{x}} , etc., where ν \nu (deterministic) denotes the discount multiplier and T x T_{x} (random) is the future lifetime of an individual being of x ∈ { 0 , 1 , … } x\in\{0,\,1,\,\ldots\} years old. This work provides some historical facts about T. Wittstein and G. Balducci and their mortality assumption. We also develop some formulas that make it easier to compute the moments of the mentioned random variables assuming that the survival function is interpolated according to Balducci’s assumption. Derived formulas are verified using some hypothetical mortality data.

Let

\displaystyle s(x+k+t)=(1-t)s(x+k)+ts(x+k+1),\,x,\,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\,t\in[0,\,1].

(1)

In other words, the UDD assumption (
1

is linear over the intervals

,
[1,\,2)
,
\ldots
Notice that

Another widely known assumption on

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s(x+k+t)}=\frac{1-t}{s(x+k)}+\frac{t}{s(x+k+1)},\,x,\,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\,t\in[0,\,1].

In comparison to the UDD assumption (
1

Moreover, if the function

\displaystyle\mu_{x+t}=\frac{q_{x}}{1-t\cdot q_{x}},\,x\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\,0<t<1.

Since the force of mortality (

(

It is curious about the circumstances of how Balducci’s assumption arose. Unfortunately, most publicly available sources, especially the electronic ones in English, lack even the basic facts about the life of Italian actuary Gaetano Balducci (1887 – 1974), see
(
Einaudi, 1993
, p. 148)
where these birth and death years are provided. Balducci did numerous prominent works and officiated in state institutions during his lifetime. For example, it is most famously known that G. Balducci was a State general Accountant for Italy for almost ten years during the middle of the 20th century, see
(
Mosca,
, p. 7)
. Notably, he was also an active member of the Society of Actuaries (the global professional organization for actuaries), sharing his insights on life insurance theories in several issues of the Journal of Economists and Statistical Review, e.g.
Unknown (1952)
,
Gaetano (1911)
,
Balducci (1917)

Here we collect and interpret more facts about the assumption (
2
) named after this Italian actuary.

In fact, the interpolation (
2
), as well as UDD and constant mortality force, was first introduced by German mathematician Theodor Ludwig Wittstein (1816 – 1894), see
(
Bowers(Jr.)
et al., 1986
, p. 68)
. In contrast to Balducci, Wittstein’s life appears to be much better documented in easily accessible public sources, see, for example,
(
Beyer, 1903
, p. 358)
. In short, Wittstein was a high school teacher and textbook author, known for his contributions to the mathematical statistics field. In his work (1862)
Wittstein (1862)
Wittstein studied population mortality under certain assumptions and concluded that the mortality probability for the fractional ages can be computed as

{}_{1-t}q_{x+t}=\frac{q_{x}\cdot(1-t)}{1-t\cdot q_{x}},\,x\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\,0<t<1,

what is equivalent to (
2
). In 1917, in work
Balducci (1917)
Balducci studied the development of certain populations too and concluded the same as Wittstein earlier. In our opinion, Balducci was unaware of Wittstein’s work due to the different languages and the limited spread of information at the time. See the source
Puzey (1993)
and references therein for more facts and language unification regarding the studies by Wittstein and Balducci.

Let us mention that the formulas of the net actuarial values under UDD are well known, they are derived in many sources, see, for example,
Bowers(Jr.)
et al. (1986)
. Also, the constant mortality force implies the future lifetime

In this work, we derive some formulas that make it easier to compute the net actuarial values under Balducci’s assumption. We show that the described computation, dependently on the type of insurance and other circumstances, is essentially based on the exponential integral (
10

In this section, we formulate and prove three auxiliary statements. Let us recall that

\displaystyle\delta=\log(1+i),\,\nu=\frac{1}{1+i},\,i>-1.

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ei}(y)=-\int\limits_{-y}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-z}}{z}\,dz=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{y}\frac{e^{z}}{z}\,dz,\,y\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}.

Lemma 1
.

Let
x,\,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
m,\,j\in\mathbb{N}
,

, and suppose that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{k+d/j}^{k+(d+1)/j}\frac{\nu^{m\,t}\,dt}{1-(1+k-t)\cdot q_{x+k}}=\frac{\nu^{m(1+k-1/q_{x+k})}}{q_{x+k}}\mathrm{Ei}_{k}(m\delta,\,d,\,j),

(26)

where

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ei}_{k}(\delta,\,d,\,j)=\mathrm{Ei}\left(-\delta\left(\frac{p_{x+k}}{q_{x+k}}+\frac{d+1}{j}\right)\right)-\mathrm{Ei}\left(-\delta\left(\frac{p_{x+k}}{q_{x+k}}+\frac{d}{j}\right)\right).

Proof.

The proof is straightforward

\displaystyle\int\limits_{k+d/j}^{k+(d+1)/j}\frac{\nu^{m\,t}\,dt}{1-(1+k-t)\cdot q_{x+k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu^{m(1+k-1/q_{x+k})}}{q_{x+k}}\int\limits_{p_{x+k}+d/j\cdot q_{x+k}}^{p_{x+k}+(d+1)/j\cdot q_{x+k}}\frac{e^{-\delta\,m\,y/q_{x+k}}}{\delta\,m\,y/q_{x+k}}\,d\left(\frac{\delta\,m\,y}{q_{x+k}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu^{m(1+k-1/q_{x+k})}}{q_{x+k}}\left(\mathrm{Ei}\left(-m\delta\left(\frac{p_{x+k}}{q_{x+k}}+\frac{d+1}{j}\right)\right)-\mathrm{Ei}\left(-m\delta\left(\frac{p_{x+k}}{q_{x+k}}+\frac{d}{j}\right)\right)\right).

∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
x,\,a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

, and

. Then, under Balducci’s mortality assumption (

{}_{a+\frac{d}{j}}p_{x}-_{a+\frac{d+1}{j}}p_{x}=_{a+1}p_{x}\cdot q_{x+a}\cdot\frac{1}{j}\cdot\frac{1}{p_{x+a}+\frac{d}{j}\cdot q_{x+a}}\cdot\frac{1}{p_{x+a}+\frac{d+1}{j}\cdot q_{x+a}}.

Since

,

, and

, we then apply the interpolation (

) and obtain

\displaystyle s\left(x+a+\frac{d}{j}\right)-s\left(x+a+\frac{d+1}{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{s(x+a)s(x+a+1)}{\frac{d}{j}\cdot s(x+a)+\left(1-\frac{d}{j}\right)\cdot s(x+a+1)}-\frac{s(x+a)s(x+a+1)}{\frac{d+1}{j}\cdot s(x+a)+\left(1-\frac{d+1}{j}\right)\cdot s(x+a+1)}

\displaystyle=s(x)\cdot_{a+1}p_{x}\cdot q_{x+a}\cdot j\cdot\frac{1}{d\cdot q_{x+a}+j\cdot p_{x+a}}\cdot\frac{1}{(d+1)\cdot q_{x+a}+j\cdot p_{x+a}}.

Lemma 3
.

Let
g(t)
be the real, differentiable and non-increasing function over the intervals

Then the expected value of

under the UDD assumption.

\displaystyle:=\int_{k}^{k+1}\left(\frac{{}_{k+1}p_{x}\cdot q_{x+k}}{(1-(k+1-t)q_{x+k})^{2}}-\frac{d_{x+k}}{l_{x}}\right)g(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{k}^{k+1}g(t)\,d\left(-\frac{{}_{k+1}p_{x}}{1-(k+1-t)q_{x+k}}-\frac{d_{x+k}}{l_{x}}t\right)=-\int_{k}^{k+1}g(t)\,d\,z(t),

\displaystyle=-g(k+1)z(k+1)+g(k)z(k)+\int_{k}^{k+1}z(t)\,g^{\prime}(t)\,dt

(27)

Effect of clinical pharmacist’s intervention on perioperative prophylaxis of antimicrobial drugs in AIDS patients
Authors: Ji Sun, Ke Yang, Xiaoya Lou, Hui Qi, Gefei He, Shiqiong Huang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study aimed to evaluate the effect of clinical pharmacists’ intervention using perioperative special prescription review mode on the preventive use of perioperative antimicrobial drug in Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome (AIDS) patients with clean incision surgery in the First hospital of Changsha.Materials and methodsWith reference to the latest guidelines and expert consensus worldwide, the National Health Commission’s guiding principles for the clinical application of antimicrobial drugs were used as the basis for review. The perioperative antimicrobial drug review criteria were formulated, and the special prescription review model was established by combination of administrative interventions and other measures. Sampling was conducted according to the type of surgery, and 109 AIDS patients with clean incision surgery in our hospital from January 2020 to June 2021 were grouped into the control group, and 110 HIV patients with clean incision surgery who received the clinical pharmacists’ intervention from July 2021 to December 2022 were categorized into the intervention group. Perioperative antimicrobial drug use and hospitalization-related indexes were compared between the two groups.ResultsAfter the intervention of clinical pharmacists, there was a significant decrease on the rate of unreasonable perioperative prophylactic use of antimicrobial drugs in AIDS patients with clean incision. A significant reduction was also seen in the length of hospitalization, Defined Daily Doses (DDD) of antibacterial drugs, cost of antibiotics, and duration of preventive use of antibiotics.ConclusionFor surgical AIDS patients, the intervention of clinical pharmacists contributes to enhancing the rationality of antimicrobial drug use, and reducing the hospitalization cost of patients during the perioperative period of clean incision.Graphical abstract

Introduction

With the deepening of China’s healthcare reform, the focus of hospital pharmacy has gradually shifted to “omni-directional, multi-dimensional and holistic closed-loop” pharmacy services. In December 2019, the Pharmacy Professional Committee of the China Hospital Association released the “Pharmacy Service Standards for Healthcare Institutions,” which clearly defined the requirements for pharmacy services in outpatient clinics, physical examination, and medication monitoring [1]. In recent years, through the implementation of closed-loop pharmacy service model, clinical pharmacists have provided patients with standardized pharmaceutical care services, including physician order verification, joint ward inspection and consultation, pharmacy monitoring, medication reconciliation, pharmaceutical counseling and education, as well as discharge medication guidance. These services have demonstrated significant efficacy in alleviating patients’ financial burdens and enhancing the standardization and rationalization of clinical medication utilization [2–4].

However, antimicrobial drug abuse and bacterial resistance is still one of the major challenges faced by medical institutions in China [5–8] and around the world. The prophylactic application of antimicrobial drugs is an important part of the perioperative period in patients with Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome (AIDS) who have an increased risk of incisional infections due to the compromised immunity [9–11]. The inappropriate application of antimicrobial agents during the perioperative period in surgical systems presents a severe challenge, significantly compromising the compliance rate of antimicrobial usage density (AUD) in clinical departments and adversely affecting patient satisfaction [12, 13].

This study aims to provide practical pharmaceutical service support for clinical pharmacists in the rational application of anti-infective drugs during the perioperative period, with a particular focus on analyzing the intervention role of clinical pharmacists in the rational use of antimicrobial agents in clean incision surgeries for AIDS patients. The findings offer theoretical foundations and practical references for clinical pharmacists to explore more feasible and efficient working models.

Information and methodology

Ethics approval

This study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of the First Hospital of Changsha (Ethics Approval Number: KX-2020048) and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. All enrolled participants provided written informed consent.

Objects of study

This study included Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) patients admitted to our hospital for clean incision surgery from January 2020 to December 2022. A diagnosis of HIV infection can be established upon meeting any of the following criteria: (1) Positive results from both HIV antibody screening and confirmatory tests, with a viral load exceeding 1000 copies/mL; (2) Presence of epidemiological history or AIDS-related clinical manifestations accompanied by two consecutive positive HIV nucleic acid test results; (3) Positive HIV isolation test result [14]. The types of clean incision surgery included spine surgery, fracture surgery, arthroplasty, hernia surgery, cataract surgery, thyroid surgery, breast surgery, excision of skin masses, varicose veins, and other vascular surgeries. The patients were divided into the intervention group and the control group based on whether there was clinical pharmacist intervention. Basis for specialized reviews

According to 2023 China Evidence-Based Guidelines: “Prevention and Management of Perioperative Infections” [15], “Guidelines for the Preventive Application of Antibiotics in Surgery” [16] and the drug specification the criteria for the review of perioperative antimicrobial drugs for AIDS clean incisions in our hospital were formulated based on the actual situation of the hospital. We also took into account the indications for perioperative antimicrobial drugs, the timing of the administration of the drugs, frequency of the administration of drugs, and the duration of the course of treatment.

Intervention methods

Based on the current situation of perioperative antimicrobial drug prevention and use for AIDS patients in our hospital, we have made the following interventions:

(1) Establish a review system: in strict accordance with national policies (https://zwfw.nhc.gov.cn/kzx/zcfg/zcfgqb/), and in consideration of the hospital’s specific circumstances, implementing a review system to conduct specialized evaluations of perioperative antibiotic prescriptions for HIV patients undergoing clean surgical procedures; (2) Performance evaluation: incorporating the review results into the performance assessment and annual evaluation indicators of the relevant departments and their staff; (3) Clinical pharmacists’ participation in ward rounds: the involvement of professionally trained clinical pharmacists in daily ward rounds facilitates the identification and rectification of irrational medication prescriptions, thereby assisting physicians in optimizing therapeutic regimens. For instance, the prophylactic administration of carbapenems during the perioperative period in clean surgical procedures; (4) Training: clinical pharmacists summarize the recurring problems of irrational medication use and carry out the training program of the rational use of antimicrobial drugs in the department.

Survey methodology

The information collected from patients meeting the inclusion criteria encompasses the following aspects: (1) Basic patient information: age, gender, disease diagnosis, history of drug allergies, hospitalization costs, and length of hospital stay. (2) Laboratory tests before and after medication: complete blood count, blood biochemistry, etc. (3) Surgical information: procedure name, duration of surgery, type of surgical incision, etc. (4) Perioperative antimicrobial usage: types of antimicrobial agents, start and end dates of medication, treatment course, dosage, and administration methods.

Statistical processing

SPSS 22.0 statistical software was used to statistically analyze the data information of the two groups. Cases are defined as the number of patients who experienced a certain event. The constituent ratio indicates the ratio or percentage of a certain part within a whole. Count data was expressed as “cases, constitutive ratio/rate (%),” and comparisons between the two groups were made using the X 2test; continuous variables that conformed to normal distribution and chi-square were expressed as x ± s, and comparisons between the two groups were made using the t-test, and the difference was considered statistically significant at P < 0.05.

Perioperative antimicrobial prophylaxis utilization rate

Perioperative antimicrobial prophylaxis encompasses cephalosporins, oxacillin, clindamycin, and penicillinase inhibitors (piperacillin–tazobactam), among others. In our study, no patients received prophylactic carbapenem antibiotics. The overall perioperative antimicrobial prophylaxis rate was 52.05%, with control and intervention groups showing rates of 61.47% and 42.73%, respectively, demonstrating a statistically significant difference (P < 0.05). Specifically, the rates of perioperative antimicrobial prophylaxis in orthopedic, hernia, and breast surgeries exhibited more pronounced disparities (Table 2). Post-intervention, the utilization ratio of first/second-generation cephalosporins significantly increased from 46.3% to 93.6%. The selection of antimicrobial agents before and after clinical pharmacist intervention showed statistically significant differences (P < 0.05). Comparison of types of irrational perioperative antimicrobial drug use before and after intervention

The inappropriate use of perioperative antimicrobial agents primarily encompasses prolonged postoperative medication duration, inappropriate selection of antimicrobial agents, and improper timing of prophylactic administration during the perioperative period. Among 109 cases prior to intervention, 38 cases (34.86%) demonstrated inappropriate perioperative antimicrobial use. In contrast, among 110 medical records post-intervention, only 3 cases (2.73%) exhibited inappropriate medication use. This reduction was statistically significant (P < 0.0001). Notably, compared with the pre-intervention period, cases of inappropriate drug selection and improper medication timing showed significant reductions, as illustrated in Table 3. Discussion

Incisional infections are more likely to occur in HIV-infected individuals than in non-HIV-infected individuals [17–23]. The prophylactic use of antimicrobials is particularly important in surgical procedures, especially for clean incisions, to reduce the risk of postoperative infections [16]. However, the rational application of antimicrobial drugs is still a key and difficult issue in the surgical system of healthcare institutions, and there is still a lack of effective regulatory models. The intervention of clinical pharmacists plays an important role in improving the awareness and practice of rational use of antimicrobial drugs in the clinic, which may reduce antimicrobial drug resistance as well as the waste of healthcare resources. The results of this study showed that the all-round participation of clinical pharmacists in the management of perioperative anti-infections in patients with AIDS could facilitate the standardized and rational use of antimicrobial drugs by clinicians.

In this study, unreasonable prescription in perioperative antimicrobial drug application for clean incision in AIDS patients is mainly manifested as high rate of perioperative antimicrobial drug prophylaxis, long duration of drug administration, and inappropriate selection of varieties. The perioperative antimicrobial utilization rate and instances of irrational medication in the intervention group were significantly lower compared to the control group. Notably, when compared with the intervention group, the most prominent perioperative irrational issue in the control group is that the duration of postoperative antimicrobial use exceeds 24 h. However, according to the guidelines [24], the duration of prophylactic medication for clean surgeries should not exceed 24 h. Prior to the intervention of clinical pharmacists, the duration of perioperative antimicrobial prophylaxis for the majority of AIDS patients with clean incisions exceeded 24 h, and in some cases, it even exceeded 5 days. This medication regimen not only fails to improve the preventive effect against surgical site infections [25] but also increases the medical expenses of patients. With the intervention of clinical pharmacists, the duration of prophylactic antibiotic use in the majority of patients was significantly reduced to within 24 h, thereby shortening the treatment cycle and reducing medical expenses for patients.

For clean incision surgery such as cardiovascular, head and neck, thoracic and abdominal wall, extremity soft tissue surgery, and orthopedic surgery via the skin, the causative organisms that may cause secondary infection are common skin colonizers such as Staphylococcus aureus, coagulase-negative staphylococci and other gram-positive bacteria. To solve this, there is evidence-based, inexpensive prophylactic antimicrobials for the first and second-generation cephalosporins, cefazolin, and cefuroxime [15–17, 26]. Before intervention in this study, it was likely for clinicians to choose some ill-targeted antimicrobials such as third-generation cephalosporin, oxy-cephalosporins, and penicillin–enzyme inhibitors covering gram-negative bacteria as prophylactic drugs, leading to an elevated risk of inducing secondary bacterial resistance [27]. Under the intervention of clinical pharmacists, the standardization and rationality of surgeons’ selection of antimicrobial agents have been significantly improved during the perioperative period. When selecting appropriate antibiotics during the perioperative period, these drugs should have bactericidal effects, be able to cover the pathogens that are most likely to cause postoperative infections, have minimal impact on drug resistance [28], possess good safety profiles, and be cost-effective [29].

This study has several limitations. As a single-center, retrospective research methodology, it may be subject to information bias and selection bias. The limited sample representativeness and follow-up bias could lead to deviations from the true outcomes. Additionally, missing data, inaccuracies, or subjective recording biases in medical records may compromise the objectivity of the research findings. In summary, clinical pharmacists’ interventions can effectively ensure the rationality, safety, and efficacy of medication use in patients. However, there remain a few critical issues that require immediate attention, such as inappropriate drug selection and suboptimal timing of medication administration.

Therefore, the pharmacists need to communicate with clinicians in depth to enhance their cooperation awareness and amplify their ability to solve the clinical problems, which will in turn enhance the recognition of pharmacists.

A Targeted Gamma-Ray Search of Five Prominent Galaxy Merger Systems with 17 years of Fermi-LAT Data
Siddhant Manna 1∗, Shantanu Desai 1∗∗

Abstract
Galaxy mergers are among the most energetic astrophysical phenomena, driving intense star formation and potentially fueling cosmic ray acceleration, which can produce high energy γ \gamma -ray emission through hadronic processes. We present a targeted search for γ \gamma -ray emission from five prominent galaxy merger systems, NGC 3256, NGC 660, UGC 813/816, UGC 12914/12915, and VV 114 using 16.8 years of Fermi-LAT data in the 1–300 GeV energy range. Employing a binned maximum likelihood analysis, we model the emission with power-law spectra and derive spectral energy distributions (SEDs) to constrain γ \gamma -ray fluxes and spectral indices. Marginal detections are found for NGC 3256 (TS = 15.4, ∼ \sim 3.51 σ \sigma ) and NGC 660 (TS = 8.16, ∼ \sim 2.39 σ \sigma ), with photon fluxes of ( 7.21 ± 3.17 ) × 10 − 11 (7.21\pm 3.17)\times 10^{-11} and ( 8.28 ± 3.56 ) × 10 − 11 (8.28\pm 3.56)\times 10^{-11} ph cm -2 s -1 , respectively, suggesting merger driven star formation contributes to γ \gamma -ray emission. The remaining systems yield non-detections (TS < 5 <5 ). This is the first targeted study of γ \gamma -ray emission from these aforementioned galaxy merger systems.

1
Introduction

Galaxy mergers are one of the most energetic phenomena in the Universe. These gravitational interactions trigger complex processes that reshape galaxies, funneling gas toward their central regions and fueling intense episodes of star formation, known as nuclear starbursts, as well as potentially enhancing the activity of central supermassive black holes (SMBHs)
(
Barnes & Hernquist 1992
;
Hopkins et al. 2006
)
. The resulting extreme environments create ideal conditions for the acceleration of cosmic rays (CRs), high energy particles that interact with interstellar gas and radiation fields through hadronic processes to produce high-energy
\gamma
-ray emission
(
Lacki et al. 2011
;
Kashiyama & Mészáros 2014
)
. Such processes include pion decay from proton-proton interactions and inverse Compton scattering of relativistic electrons, which are particularly pronounced in dense, star-forming regions often associated with mergers
(
Abdo et al. 2010
)
. Luminous and ultra-luminous infrared galaxies (LIRGs and ULIRGs), frequently observed in late stage mergers, are characterized by their intense infrared emission, which is driven by dust heated by star formation and active galactic nuclei (AGN) activity
(
Sanders & Mirabel 1996
)
. These systems have been proposed as significant contributors to the extragalactic
\gamma
-ray background, a diffuse flux of high-energy photons observed across the sky
(
Ackermann et al. 2012
)
. However, direct detections of
\gamma
-ray emission from individual galaxy mergers remain scarce, with only a handful of nearby starburst galaxies, such as NGC 253 and M82, providing clear evidence of
\gamma
-ray production
(
Abdo et al. 2010
;
Ackermann et al. 2012
)
. These detections serve as critical benchmarks for understanding cosmic ray propagation, energy loss mechanisms, and calorimetry in high density environments, offering insights into the physical processes governing
\gamma
-ray emission in merging systems.

The Fermi Gamma ray Space Telescope, launched in 2008, has revolutionized the study of high energy astrophysical phenomena through its primary instrument, the Large Area Telescope (LAT)
(
Atwood et al. 2009
)
. Operating in the energy range of 20 MeV to over 500 GeV, the LAT provides unprecedented sensitivity and angular resolution, enabling all sky surveys that have cataloged thousands of
\gamma
-ray sources
(
Abdollahi et al. 2022
)
. The Fourth Fermi Source Catalog Data Release 4 (4FGL-DR4), based on 16 years of LAT observations, represents the deepest survey of the
\gamma
-ray sky to date in the 50 MeV to 1 TeV energy range
(
Ballet et al. 2023
)
. Despite these advancements, many interacting galaxy systems remain undetected in
\gamma
-rays, and systematic searches targeting galaxy mergers are limited, underscoring the need for dedicated studies to probe their high-energy emission.

Recent multimessenger studies have further highlighted the potential of galaxy mergers as sites of high-energy particle production. For instance, a recent analysis by
Bouri et al. 2025
investigated the hypothesis that galaxy mergers could be sources of high energy neutrinos, which are often produced alongside
\gamma
-rays in hadronic interactions. Using 10 years of IceCube muon track data and galaxy merger catalogs, their analysis found no statistically significant correlation between the galaxy mergers studied and high energy neutrino events. The study set upper limits on the neutrino flux at 100 TeV, suggesting that these mergers do not significantly contribute to the diffuse astrophysical neutrino flux detected by IceCube
(
Bouri et al. 2025
)
. This lack of neutrino signal motivates further investigation into the
\gamma
-ray emission from these systems, as the presence or absence of
\gamma
rays can provide complementary constraints on the underlying particle acceleration mechanisms.

In this work, we conduct a targeted search for
\gamma
-ray emission from a sample of five prominent galaxy mergers, NGC 3256, NGC 660, UGC 813/816, UGC 12914/12915, and VV 114 using 16.8 years of Fermi-LAT data. These systems were selected for their diverse merger stages and intense star-forming activity, making them prime candidates for detecting high-energy emission. They have also been studied in
(
Bouri et al. 2025
)
and they found no statistical significance. Initial theoretical predictions for
\gamma
-ray emission from galaxy mergers were made by
Lisenfeld & Völk 2010
, who analyzed two of the systems in our sample: UGC 12914/12915 and UGC 813/816. They estimated the hadronic
\gamma
-ray flux resulting from neutral pion (
\pi^{0}
) decay to be on the order of
\mathcal{O}(10^{-15})~\rm{ph~cm^{-2}~s^{-1}}
, assuming a power-law spectral index of 1.1 normalized at 1 TeV.
This work is a follow-up of our previous work, where we carried out a spatial correlation analysis between a Galaxy Zoo based merger catalog and the fourth Fermi point source catalog (4FGL-DR4)
(
Manna & Desai 2025
)
.
In Section
2
we have laid out our data analysis process, which is common for all the merger systems. In Section
3
we depict our results for all the mergers. Finally in Section
4
we present our conclusions.

2
Data Analysis

For each galaxy merger in our sample, NGC 3256, NGC 660, UGC 813/816, UGC 12914/12915, and VV 114, we conducted a targeted search for
\gamma
-ray emission using 16.8 years of data from the Fermi-LAT, spanning from 2008 August 5 to 2025 June 25 (
Mission Elapsed Time:239587201 to 772502405
)
(
Atwood et al. 2009
)
. This dataset corresponds to the Fourth Fermi Source Catalog Data Release 4 (4FGL-DR4), which provides a comprehensive catalog of
\gamma
-ray sources based on 16 years of observations
(
Ballet et al. 2023
)
.
Data processing was performed using the
easyfermi
pipeline, a modular framework designed to streamline
\gamma
-ray analysis
(
de Menezes 2022
)
. This pipeline integrates several Python libraries, including
Fermipy

(
Wood et al. 2017
)
,
Gammapy

(
Donath et al. 2023
)
,
Astropy

(
Astropy Collaboration et al. 2018
)
, and
emcee

(
Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013
)
. We have previously used this tool in searches for gamma-rays from magnetars
(
Ramakrishnan & Desai 2025
)
and OJ287
(
Pasumarti & Desai 2024
)
.
The
Fermi Science Tools
(v2.2.0) were employed for data processing and event selection. We utilized the
P8R3_SOURCE_V3
instrument response functions (IRFs), which are optimized for point source analyses, along with the recommended Galactic diffuse emission model (
gll_iem_v07.fits
) and isotropic background template (
iso_P8R3_SOURCE_V3_v1.txt
), consistent with the 4FGL-DR4 configuration
(
Ballet et al. 2023
)
. To ensure data quality, standard cuts were applied (
DATA_QUAL > 0
and
LAT_CONFIG == 1
), and photons with zenith angles
>105^{\circ}
were excluded to minimize contamination from the Earth limb emission, which is particularly significant at low energies
(
Abdo et al. 2009
)
.

For each target, we defined a
10^{\circ}
radius region of interest (ROI) centered on the merger coordinates. A test point source was injected at the ROI center and modeled with a power-law spectral shape, initialized with a photon index of 2.0, a value typical for
\gamma
-ray sources associated with star-forming galaxies
(
Ackermann et al. 2012
)
. To avoid bias from existing catalog entries, the injected source was treated independently of any sources listed in the 4FGL-DR4 catalog. During likelihood fitting, the spectral parameters (normalization and photon index) of the injected source were left free to vary, allowing the data to constrain the spectral shape. The background model incorporated all 4FGL-DR4 sources within the ROI, with sources having a Test Statistic (TS) below 16 excluded to enhance model stability and reduce overfitting
(
Nolan et al. 2012
)
. Additional background sources were identified iteratively by generating TS maps and adopting a detection threshold of
\sqrt{\rm TS}>5
, corresponding to a statistical significance of approximately 5
\sigma
for point sources
(
Mattox et al. 1996
)
.

The analysis was carried out over an energy range of 1–300 GeV, divided into five logarithmically spaced bins for spectral energy distribution (SED) modeling. The lower energy threshold of 1 GeV was selected to mitigate the impact of the LAT’s degraded point spread function (PSF) at lower energies
(
Atwood et al. 2013
)
. A binned likelihood analysis was performed with a spatial bin size of
0.1^{\circ}
and eight energy bins per decade. Energy dispersion corrections were applied and PSF convolution was enabled to model the LAT’s energy dependent angular resolution, which improves significantly above 1 GeV
(
Atwood et al. 2009
)
. The
NEWMINUIT
optimizer was used for all model optimizations
(
James & Roos 1975
)
. To evaluate the spatial significance of potential emission, TS maps were generated for each ROI, providing an assessment of the likelihood ratio between models with and without the injected source. For energy bins where the target source was not significantly detected (
{\rm TS}<9
, corresponding to less than 3
\sigma
significance), upper limits on the flux were computed at 95% confidence level. The spatial morphology of the injected source was modeled using a point-source template. All background sources in the ROI were modeled using their spatial templates as defined in the 4FGL-DR4 catalog.

\chi^{2}/2

These upper limits lie approximately 4–5 orders of magnitude above the theoretical expectations, indicating that the predicted hadronic emission remains below the current sensitivity of the Fermi-LAT. 4
Conclusions

This study presents a targeted search for
\gamma
-ray emission from five galaxy merger systems, NGC 3256, NGC 660, UGC 813/816, UGC 12914/12915, and VV 114 using 16.8 years of Fermi-LAT data in the 1–300 GeV energy range. None of these systems yield statistical significance
>5\sigma
. We find marginal
\gamma
-ray signals from NGC 3256 (TS = 15.4,
\sim
3.51
\sigma
) and NGC 660 (TS = 8.16,
\sim 2.39\sigma
), with power law spectral indices of
\Gamma=2.05\pm 0.27
and
\Gamma=2.83\pm 0.54
, respectively. These detections, while statistically weak, suggest that merger-driven star formation and cosmic-ray acceleration can produce detectable high-energy emission in some systems. Conversely, UGC 813/816, UGC 12914/12915, and VV 114 show no significant broadband
\gamma
-ray emission (TS
<9
), with only tentative excesses in specific energy bins for UGC 813/816 (second bin, TS = 10.07) and VV 114 (third bin, TS = 9.19). The derived fluxes and upper limits, summarized in Table
1
, provide valuable constraints on the
\gamma
-ray production efficiency in these systems.

The marginal detections and non-detections in this study are limited by the sensitivity of Fermi-LAT, particularly for distant or faint sources. The low TS values for NGC 3256 and NGC 660 indicate that longer observation times or more sensitive instruments are needed to confirm these signals. Next-generation observatories, such as the Cherenkov Telescope Array, offer improved sensitivity at energies above 100 GeV, potentially detecting fainter or higher energy signals from these systems.

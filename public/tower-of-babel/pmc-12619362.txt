Prospective associations between the trajectory of the Chinese visceral adiposity index and risk of new-onset heart failure
Authors: Kuangyi Wu, Liling Zhang, Huancong Zheng, Weiqiang Wu, Xianxuan Wang, Zegui Huang, Zhiwei Cai, Yulong Lan, Shouling Wu, Youren Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe Chinese Visceral Adiposity Index (CVAI), a straightforward surrogate measure of visceral fat, is significantly associated with the risk of cardiovascular disease (CVD) in the general population. This study aimed to evaluate the association of the trajectory of the Chinese visceral adiposity index with new-onset heart failure (HF) risk.MethodsThis prospective cohort study included 76,822 participants who were free of HF or cancer up to 2012. The CVAI was calculated using the age, body mass index, waist circumference, and metabolic parameters of the participants, and latent mixed modelling was used to identify the trajectory of CVAI during the exposure period (2006–2012). Cox proportional hazard models were used to calculate the hazard ratio (HR) and 95% confidence interval (CI) for incident HF of the trajectory groups.ResultsFive trajectory groups based on the changes in CVAI were identified. During a median follow-up period of 7.78 years, 1,480 participants developed HF. Compared with the Low-stable group, the HRs of HF in High-stable and High-increasing groups were 2.01 (1.11, 3.63) and 2.43 (1.28, 4.62), respectively. The correlations were strongest in participants < 60 years of age and those that did not have hypertension. Sensitivity analyses generated similar findings.ConclusionLong-term high CVAI is associated with higher risk of HF, irrespective of conventional risk factors. The regular monitoring of CVAI may help identify individuals at high risk of HF in the general population.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-22726-z.

Introduction

Heart failure (HF) is the end stage of various cardiovascular diseases and has a 5-year mortality rate of up to 50% [1]. Global burden of disease studies had shown that the number of people with HF increased from 33.5 million in 1990 to 64.3 million in 2017, making it one of the most significant medical and social problems worldwide [2]. In China, there are nearly 12.1 million patients with HF, which represents almost one-fifth of the global number of HF cases [3]. Previous studies have shown that obesity is a major risk factor for HF [4]. A meta-analysis of 23 prospective cohort studies showed that for every 5 kg/m2 increase in body mass index (BMI), the risk of HF increased by 41% and the risk of death increased by 25% [5].

Randomized controlled trials, Mendelian randomization studies, and cohort studies have all shown that high visceral fat mass is the most important risk factor for HF in patients with obesity [6–9]. However, computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), which are considered to be the “gold-standard” methods for the evaluation of visceral fat mass, are not widely used in clinical or epidemiological research, owing to their high cost, technical complexity, and the potential risks of radiation exposure [10]. Similarly, indices of obesity such as BMI, waist circumference (WC), and waist-to-height ratio do not accurately represent the amount of visceral fat present [11]. The Chinese visceral adiposity index (CVAI), which is calculated using a combination of conventional indices and circulating lipid parameters, is a new index of visceral fat mass that closely correlates with CT-measured visceral fat volume [12]. Previous studies have shown that a high CVAI is associated with higher risks of atherosclerosis and cardiovascular metabolic risk [13, 14]. In addition, the Aric study showed a correlation between high CVAI and the risk of HF in the general population [15]. However, in previous studies, only a single assessment of CVAI was made, and it is affected by a number of factors, such as age, physical exercise, and diet. Therefore, a single assessment of CVAI is not sufficient to evaluate the long-term effect of this index on the risk of HF.

To date, there have been no studies of the relationships between specific CVAI trajectories over time and new-onset HF. Therefore, the aim of the present study was to characterize the relationships between the longitudinal patterns of CVAI and the risk of HF over a 6-year period, using data from the Kailuan study cohort (Registration Number: CHICTR-TNRC-11001489).

Methods

Study design and participants

Since 2006, health check-ups have been conducted at Kailuan General Hospital and the 10 associated hospitals on both active and retired employees of the Kailuan Group every 2 years. In addition, the occurrence of specific events, such as cardiovascular diseases, has been recorded annually. The detailed design of the study and its methodology have been described previously [16, 17]. A total of 85,370 participants who participated in three or more of the first four surveys (2006–2007, 2008–2009, 2010–2011, and 2012–2013) were enrolled in the study, of whom 582 participants developed HF before the fourth survey (2012–2013) and were excluded. In addition, participants with missing WC, triglyceride, high-density lipoprotein-cholesterol (HDL-C), or BMI data (n = 7,001), and those who developed cancer (n = 965) by the date of fourth survey (2012–2013) were excluded. Therefore, data from 76,822 participants were analysed in the present study (Figure S1 in the Supplement). The data collected during the last of these four surveys were used as the baseline data for the analysis of the risk of HF. The CVAI trajectory was characterized over the exposure period (from 2006 to 2012) to predict the risk of HF between 2012 and 2020. This study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, it was approved by Kailuan General Hospital and by the Ethics Committee of Kailuan General Hospital, and all the participants provided their written informed consent.

Data collection and definitions

All the participants were surveyed at baseline and during follow-up at the most appropriate hospital in the Kailuan community. Questionnaires were completed with the guidance of trained staff to collect information regarding the demographic characteristics, medical history (including that of hypertension, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease), use of medication (including lipid-lowering, glucose-lowering, and antihypertensive drugs) and lifestyle factors (physical activity, alcohol consumption, and smoking habits) of the participants. The BMI of the participants was calculated by dividing their body mass (kg) by the square of height (m) when they were wearing light clothing and no shoes. WC was measured with the participant standing upright, with their feet 30–40 cm apart, their arms hanging downwards, with their abdominal muscles relaxed, and while breathing normally. A soft, non-elastic, 0.1-cm interval measuring tape was placed horizontally at the midpoint of the line between the iliac crest and the lower edge of the 12th rib on the right mid-axillary line (the narrowest part of the waist), without compressing the skin; and measurements were made at the end of normal exhalation.

The right brachial artery blood pressure of the participants was measured between 07:00 and 09:00 on the day of the examination using a calibrated table-top mercury sphygmomanometer. Three measurements were made at 1–2 min intervals and the mean value was calculated. The participants were not permitted to smoke or drink tea or coffee during the 30 min prior to the blood pressure measurements, and sat still for 15 min immediately beforehand. A smoking habit was defined as having smoked a mean ≥ 1 cigarette/day for > 1 year, including during the preceding year. Alcohol consumption was defined as having consumed a mean of ≥ 100 mL/day alcohol (> 50% alcohol content) for > 1 year, including during the preceding year. Educational attainment was classified as junior high school or below, or high school or above. Physical activity was defined as exercising ≥ 4 times per week for at least 20 min on each occasion. Hypertension was defined using a systolic blood pressure (SBP) ≥ 140 mmHg, and/or a diastolic blood pressure (DBP) ≥ 90 mmHg, and/or the use of antihypertensive medication during the preceding 2 weeks [18]. Diabetes was defined using a fasting plasma glucose (FPG) concentration ≥ 7.0 mmol/L, and/or a history of a diagnosis of diabetes, and/or the use of glucose-lowering medication [19].

Assessment of biochemical parameters and CVAI

After fasting for 8 h, 5 ml of blood was drawn from an elbow vein between 07:00 and 09:00 on the day of the examination and biochemical parameters (high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP), FPG, triglycerides (TG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), HDL-C, and total cholesterol (TC) were measured using a Hitachi 7600 automatic biochemical analyser (Hitachi, Tokyo, Japan). The CVAI was calculated using the age, BMI, WC, TG, and HDL-C (expressed in mmol/L) [12] as follows:

For men:

CVAI = − 267.93 + 0.68 × age + 0.03 × BMI + 4.00 × WC + 22.00 × Log10TG − 16.32 × HDL-C.

For women:

CVAI = − 187.32 + 1.71 × age + 4.23 × BMI + 1.12 × WC + 39.76 × Log10TG − 11.66 × HDL-C.

Assessment of heart failure

The primary outcome of the study was the initial development of HF. A specialist team collected and evaluated the hospital discharge records annually to identify patients with suspected HF, and the ICD-10 (I50.x) definitions were consistently applied at all the hospitals. HF was defined using the diagnostic criteria in the 2022 American Heart Association (AHA)/American College of Cardiology (ACC)/Heart Failure Society of America (HFSA) Heart Failure Management Guidelines [20]. The presence of any clinical features of heart failure, such as dyspnoea, weakness, and fluid accumulation (e.g., ascites, pleural effusion, pedal oedema, and high jugular venous pressure), used to assign New York Heart Association (NYHA) cardiac function ≥ Class II or Killip cardiac function ≥ Class II; left ventricular ejection fraction ≤ 50% on echocardiography; and high N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide was used to diagnose HF. All the participants were followed from baseline until they experienced an HF event, death, or December 31, 2020 (whichever came first).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using SAS v.9.4 (SAS Institute, Inc, Cary, NC, USA) software and P < 0.05 (two-tailed) was considered to represent statistical significance. The CVAI trajectories of the participants between 2006 and 2012 were characterized using latent mixture modelling in the SAS Proc Traj program [21]. The latent mixture model approach was employed due to its unique ability to characterize interindividual variability and delineate naturally emergent subpopulations in CVAI progression patterns. This methodological framework is particularly suited for capturing the heterogeneous trajectories of CVAI evolution in large-scale epidemiological studies. The model accommodates intraclass variability, thereby effectively modeling the nonlinear temporal dynamics characteristic of CVAI changes. Model selection was guided by multiple robust criteria: minimization of the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC), evaluation of mean posterior classification probabilities, and consideration of subgroup sample sizes [22]. Through this rigorous process, five clinically and statistically distinct trajectory patterns were derived (Supplementary Table S1) and operationally defined according to their characteristic morphological features.

To describe the baseline characteristics of the participants, skewed continuous data are reported as median and interquartile range and normally distributed continuous data are reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Categorical data are described using number and percentage. Continuous datasets were compared using the Kruskal–Wallis test or one-way ANOVA, and categorical datasets were compared using the chi-square test.

The cumulative incidence of HF in each trajectory group was calculated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and groups were compared using the log-rank test. In addition, Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was used to calculate the hazard ratio (HR) and 95% confidence interval (CI) for the incidence of HF for each of the five groups; the proportional hazards assumption was satisfied. Model 1 was adjusted for sex, baseline age, heart rate, BMI, SBP, hs-CRP, TC, smoking habit (yes or no), alcohol consumption (yes or no), physical exercise (yes or no), educational level (junior high school or below, or high school or above), the presence of hypertension (yes or no), and the presence of diabetes (yes or no). Model 2 was adjusted for these variables, plus the baseline use of antihypertensive drugs (yes or no), hypoglycaemic drugs (yes or no), and lipid-lowering drugs (yes or no). Model 3 was adjusted for all these variables, plus the baseline CVAI. In addition, we used Cox proportional hazards regression models to analyse the interactions of sex (male or female), baseline age (> 60 years or not), baseline hypertension status, and CVAI trajectory with the incidence rate of HF; and conducted subgroup analyses based on sex (male or female), baseline age (> 60 years or not), and baseline hypertension status.

We also performed several sensitivity analyses based on Model 2 to verify the robustness of the results. First, to minimize potential reverse causality, participants who experienced outcome events during the first year of follow-up were excluded. Second, a sensitivity analysis was conducted using the magnitude of the changes in covariates between 2006 and 2012, given their potential influence on the outcome. Third, participants who had a history of myocardial infarction before the start of follow-up in 2012 were excluded to account for the effect of myocardial infarction on the risk of HF. Finally, to consider the impact of drug treatment on the results, we conducted three further sensitivity analyses by excluding participants who used antihypertensive drugs, hypoglycaemic drugs, or lipid-lowering drugs during the follow-up period.

Results

Baseline characteristics of the participants

The baseline characteristics of the participants are shown in Table 1. Data from a total of 76,822 participants were studied. The mean age of the participants at baseline was 54.93 ± 11.87 years, and 60,247 were male (78.42%) and 16,575 were female (21.58%). Based on their CVAI change patterns between 2006 and 2012, five different trajectory subgroups were identified through latent mixture modelling: (1) n = 3,877 who maintained a low CVAI (mean CVAI range 21.3–32.3, referred to as the “Low-stable” group); (2) n = 16,163 who had consistently moderately low CVAI (mean CVAI range 58.2–68.6, referred to as the “Moderate low-stable” group); (3) n = 37,054 who maintained moderately high CVAI (mean CVAI range 92.5–102.2, referred to as the “Moderately high-stable” group); (4) n = 16,539 who had consistently high CVAI (mean CVAI range 126.0–136.4, referred to as the “high-stable” group); and (5) n = 3,189 who initially had a high CVAI, which slightly increased (mean CVAI range 163.0–175.0, referred to as the “High-increasing” group)( Fig. 1). Compared with the Low-stable group, the participants in the High-stable and High-increasing groups were more likely to be smokers and alcohol consumers; had higher BMI, SBP, DBP, TG, TC, and CRP; and were more likely to have hypertension and diabetes (all at least P < 0.01).

Relationship between the risk of heart failure and the trajectory of CVAI

After a median follow-up period of 7.78 years, a total of 1,480 participants had developed HF. The HF incidence densities for the groups were 0.42, 1.19, 2.17, 4.15, and 7.89 per thousand person-years, respectively. Kaplan–Meier curves showed that participants in the High-increasing CVAI group were at a higher risk of developing HF than those in the Low-stable group (log-rank test, P < 0.0001; Fig. 2). After adjustment for potential confounding factors, the risk of HF gradually increased in the Moderately low-stable group, Moderately high-stable group, High-stable group, and High-increasing group vs. the Low-stable group. The corresponding HRs and 95% CIs were: 1.43 (0.81, 2.54), 1.75 (0.99, 3.09), 2.01 (1.11, 3.63), and 2.43 (1.28,4 0.62), respectively (Table 2).

Results of the subgroup and sensitivity analyses

We identified significant interactions (P for interaction < 0.05) of the CVAI trajectory with age and the presence or absence of hypertension. In both age groups (> 60 and < 60 years), individuals with long-term high CVAI were at a higher risk of HF than those in the Low-stable CVAI group. However, the relationship was closer in the < 60 years of age group (adjusted risk ratio: High-increasing group, 2.99 [1.17, 7.65]). Similar findings were made with respect to the presence or absence of hypertension: CVAI was more closely associated with the risk of HF in the non-hypertensive group (adjusted risk ratio for the High-increasing group: 5.04 [1.68, 15.14]). There was no significant interaction between CVAI trajectory and sex (Table 3).

Discussion

The principal findings of this study are that the CVAI exhibits specific patterns over several years, with high and increasing values linked to a higher risk of heart failure (HF), independent of conventional risk factors and baseline CVAI. Additionally, this association is influenced by age and hypertension status. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study in which the CVAI trajectory has been analysed prospectively in a large cohort. We evaluated the long-term trends in CVAI over 6 years in more than 70,000 individuals using models, and identified five trajectories that involved relatively small changes in CVAI, with the High-increasing group showing the largest changes. In a previous study of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, three CVAI trajectories were identified, with similarly small changes within each group [23].

The Moderately high-stable, High-stable, and High-increasing CVAI trajectory groups were found to be at 85%, 120%, and 184% higher risks of HF, respectively, vs. the Low-stable CVAI group. In addition, even after adjustment for conventional risk factors and the baseline CVAI, these higher risks remained, suggesting that high and increasing CVAIs represent an independent risk factor for HF. Although the relationships between CVAI trajectories and the risk of HF have not been characterized in previous studies, a Mendelian randomization analysis of two sets of study data showed a 72% higher risk of HF in individuals with high visceral fat mass [24]. Another study of National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey data showed that the third quartile of VAI is associated with a 55% higher risk of HF than the lowest quartile [25]. However, single measurements were used to assess the relationship of visceral fat mass with the risk of HF in previous studies, whereas in the present study, multiple assessments of CVAI were made in order to characterize the changes over time, and we were able to identify an association between specific CVAI trajectories and the risk of HF, which implies that CVAI should be calculated regularly over the long term to identify individuals at high risk of HF.

The 2022 ACC/AHA/HFSA Heart Failure Management Guidelines recommend the use of antihypertensive and antidiabetic drugs to prevent HF in patients at high risk of cardiovascular disease [26]. Consistent with this, we found that adjustment for the use of antihypertensive drugs, antidiabetic drugs, and lipid-lowering drugs reduced the risk of HF by 11% and 26% in the High-stable and High-increasing groups, respectively. This suggests that antihypertensive, antidiabetic, and lipid-lowering drugs help reduce the risk of HF in people with high visceral fat mass. The studies by Xu et al. and Ken et al. also yielded similar results to the present study. Xu et al. showed that adjustment for blood pressure and the use of lipid-lowering drugs reduced the risk of HF in groups with differing visceral fat mass [15], and Ken et al. showed that individuals with high visceral fat mass benefited significantly more from antihypertensive drug treatment than those with low visceral fat mass [27]. This suggests that the benefit obtained from antihypertensive drugs and other measures vary among people with differing visceral fat trajectories, but that the early diagnosis and treatment of high visceral fat mass is associated with a substantial benefit.

The subgroup analysis showed that the relationship between high and increasing CVAI and the risk of HF varied according to age and blood pressure: the risk was more serious in individuals of under 60 years of age and in those with normal blood pressure. Previous studies have shown that the early development of visceral obesity is a risk factor for HF [28], and we also found that high visceral fat mass over a long period of time has a greater impact on the risk of HF in individuals of under 60 years of age. Thus, early interventions aimed at reducing visceral fat mass represent important ways of preventing HF. We found that high visceral fat mass over the long term is associated with a higher risk in individuals who do not have hypertension, and although we do not have a clear explanation for this, it may have been because of the absolute risk of HF increasing in hypertensive individuals or the modification of the relative risk of CVAI-associated HF by comorbidities and other risk factors [29].

The mechanism underlying the relationship between the long-term change in CVAI and the risk of HF is unclear, but it may involve inflammation and oxidative stress. Large visceral adipose tissue depots stimulate the secretion of cytokines and adipokines, such as leptin, adiponectin, interleukin-6 (IL-6), and tumour necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), which are involved in the development of diabetes and hypertension [30]. Consistent with this, in the present study, the High-increasing group had a higher mean hs-CRP concentration than the Low-stable group. In addition, the High-increasing group had higher prevalence of diabetes and hypertension, which increase the risk of HF. Furthermore, atherosclerosis is an important component of the pathogenesis of HF, and visceral fat plays a key role in endothelial dysfunction and the pathogenesis of atherosclerosis [31]. A previous study showed a significant association between high CVAI and atherosclerosis [32]. Finally, participants with high CVAI over the long term had higher BMI, heart rate, SBP, DBP, and TC; and were more likely to be current smokers or alcohol consumers, all of which are risk factors for HF.

To the best of our knowledge, the present study was the first large-scale cohort study conducted in Asian adults to evaluate the relationship between CVAI trajectory and the risk of HF. The study’s strengths were the rigorous protocol and quality control, the use of a large representative sample, and the availability of data regarding many important covariates. Furthermore, we used the changes in CVAI over time and adjusted the data for confounding factors that can affect the risk of HF, which improves the reliability of the results. However, the study also had some limitations. First, we did not use CT or MRI to assess visceral adipose tissue volume in the participants, because these are complex and expensive methods that are not suitable for routine screening. In addition, CVAI has been shown to closely correlate with visceral adipose tissue mass. Second, while our HF diagnostic criteria incorporated contemporary guideline recommendations, the lack of systematic diastolic function assessment precluded detailed HF phenotype analysis. Future studies with comprehensive echocardiographic protocols would be valuable to examine subtype-specific associations. The relationship between CVAI and the incidence of HF should be assessed in further studies of other populations. Third, approximately 8.2% of otherwise eligible participants were excluded due to missing CVAI components (WC, TG, HDL-C, or BMI). Excluded participants may differ systematically from included ones, possibly underestimating HF risk in less health-engaged individuals. Finally, our study cohort consisted exclusively of Kailuan Group employees, who may exhibit systematic differences from the general population in terms of healthcare access, occupational health monitoring, and socioeconomic status. This occupational cohort design, while providing valuable longitudinal data, may introduce healthy worker selection bias and limit the generalizability of our findings to populations with different healthcare-seeking behaviors or metabolic risk profiles. However, the internal validity of our observed associations between CVAI trajectories and HF risk remains robust within this well-characterized cohort.

Conclusion

In conclusion, long-term high and increasing CVAI is associated with a higher risk of HF, independent of conventional risk factors. The routine monitoring of CVAI may therefore be useful for the identification of individuals at high risk of HF in the general population, and the avoidance or reduction of high visceral adipose mass is an important way of preventing HF.

Strut and tie model for predicting shear behavior of interior RC beam column joints under seismic loading
Authors: Wentong Zhao, Yulin Yan, Yaxin Yue, Ru Xue, Xiang Yan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Beam–column joint with complex mechanical behavior is an important part of reinforced concrete (RC) structures. The present study aims to assess the joint shear stress–strain relationship by adopting strut–and–tie approach of the interior beam-column subassemblages. Totally six interior RC beam–column joint specimens were tested under cyclic loading. The test results of beam–column subassemblages show that under high shear stress, the joint cores always perform obvious inelastic shear deformations. It is evident that when using finite element simulation to study the seismic behavior of RC beam–column joints, accurately modeling the shear stress–strain relationship of joint core is critical for successfully implementing the finite element analysis. Based on the experimental results, this study innovatively proposed a strut–and–tie model (STM) for RC interior beam–column joints to investigate shear behavior within joint core. This STM was established according to the deformation characteristics and the crack patterns of the joint region. The force equilibrium conditions and deformation compatibility requirements of this model were formulated. Comparisons were made between the predicted and published experimental results of the twenty–three RC interior beam-column joints with respect to joint shear stress and strain envelope curve at this state to investigate the adequacy of the proposed STM. The results demonstrate that the joint shear stress and strain envelope curves predicted by the proposed STM show closer agreement with experimental data. The mean absolute error (MAE) of peak shear stress calculated by the proposed STM is significantly lower than that of the Zhao-Yang model. The coefficient of determination (R2) is significantly higher than that of the Zhao-Yang model. These findings confirm that the proposed STM exhibits high prediction accuracy for the shear stress–strain relationship of beam-column joints.The calculation results of the proposed STM were used in finite element analysis of the beam–column joint tests based on the Beam Column Joint Element in the OpenSees software. The load–displacement hysteresis curves and joint shear deformations obtained from the finite element analysis were compared against experimental results of beam–column joints. Analytical results further validated the applicability and efficacy of the proposed STM.

Introduction

In the seismic resistant design of moment-resisting frames, the beam-column joint is regarded as a crucial point for consideration. Post–earthquake research indicates that, under high shear stress, the joint core always exhibits obvious inelastic deformations, which can lead to large lateral displacement or even collapse of the RC structures. Hence, comprehending the shear behavior of joints is essential to control the overall performance of RC beam-column frame structures. Since the mid-twentieth century, a range of experimental research and finite element analyses have been carried out to explore the seismic behavior of the beam–column joint. When using finite element method to analyze the mechanical behavior of beam–column joint, it is vital to calculate the joint shear stress versus strain relationship of the joint core correctly.

Research indicates that the force transfer mechanisms within the core of beam–column joint can be characterized by diagonal compression strut, truss, and confined mechanism (Fig. 1). For calculating the joint shear stress and strain (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\tau { - }\gamma$$\end{document}) relationship correctly, it is imperative to consider the contributions of the three kinds of force transfer mechanisms appropriately. Lowes and Altoontash1 and Shi et al.2 used the modified compression field theory (MCFT) to calculate the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\tau { - }\gamma$$\end{document} relationship of the joint. The MCFT was proposed according to the shear plate test carried out by Vicchio and Collins3. The force transferred by longitudinal beam and column bars has been considered in MCFT, in other words, only the participation of truss mechanism has been taken into account. Xue et al.4 proposed to use the diagonal compression strut model to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\tau { - }\gamma$$\end{document} relationship of steel fiber RC beam–column joint. However, this model presumes that the joint shear force is transmitted through the diagonal compression strut, and the confined mechanism is considered by confined concrete model, the participation of the truss mechanism is not taken into account.Fig. 1Force mechanisms within beam–column joint core.

Force mechanisms within beam–column joint core.

In the process of designing structural concrete members, it is a standard practice to conceptually divide these elements into two zones for better analysis. One effective method for representing how loads are transferred in the more complex zones, known as disturbed regions (D-regions), is through the strut-and-tie model. STM simplifies the intricate patterns of force flow within a structure, modeling them as a network of compression elements called struts, tension elements referred to as ties, and their interconnecting points or nodes. This simplified representation, akin to a truss, provides a clear and discretized depiction of the paths along which forces are transferred within the actual structure. The origins of the STM can be traced back to the foundational proposals by Ritter5 and Morsch6. This method has been the subject of extensive research and refinement, especially after the detailed studies by Schlaich et al.7,8. Building on this foundational work, a significant number of researchers have since contributed a variety of STM types9–18. These methods have gained widespread acceptance and have been integrated into contemporary design codes19–24.

Although the STM is extensively recognized and valued as a logical technique for the design of D-regions, research on STMs specific to beam-column joints is sparse, with only a handful of studies undertaken by researchers such as Hwang and Lee25, You et al.26, Anwar and Ismail27, Zhang et al.28, Saad and Sakr29, Xia et al.30, Mashhadi and Dastan31. Nevertheless, the main focus of these models is to estimate the joint shear strength, the calculation of the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\tau { - }\gamma$$\end{document} relationship has been ignored. Compared with the calculation of the shear capacity of the joint, the calculation of the shear stress–strain relationship of the joint involves the whole process analysis of the force and deformation of the joint, and the calculation process is more complex. The existing STM is not equipped to directly predict the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\tau { - }\gamma$$\end{document} relationship. Beam-column joints often experience significant shear deformation under seismic loading, whose impact on the overall structural performance cannot be overlooked. Thus, it is imperative to develop a new STM capable of simultaneously predicting both joint shear strength and joint shear deformation (i.e., the joint shear stress–strain relationship). Therefore, in this research a STM, named as Crack–based STM, was proposed according to the deformation characteristics and the crack patterns in joint region. The calculation method of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\tau { - }\gamma$$\end{document} envelope curve was established based on the Crack–based STM. The establishment, calibration, and validation of the Crack–based STM were carried out focusing on the interior beam–column joint with stirrups. The Crack–based STM was verified by twenty–three available interior joint experimental results. The numerical analysis was conducted using the OpenSees software. The calculation results of the crack-based STM were applied to improve the parameter determination method of critical parameters of the Beam Column Joint Element in OpenSees software. The load–displacement hysteresis curves and joint shear deformations obtained from the finite element analysis were compared against experimental results of beam–column joints to validate the applicability of the Crack–based STM.

Under cyclic loading, the diagonal compression force in the joint core was resisted by both the truss mechanism and diagonal compression strut mechanism, whereas‌ the diagonal tension force was solely carried by‌ the truss mechanism. As a result, the total diagonal compression in the joint core consistently exceeded the total diagonal tension throughout the loading process. With repeated loading cycles, the longitudinal beam reinforcement yielded. After yielding, the joint experienced severe bond degradation and yield penetration. As‌ longitudinal beam bar slip increased, the shear force transferred to the truss mechanism via ‌bonded reinforcement decreased significantly. Consequently, the ‌diagonal compression strut‌ carried a greater proportion of shear resistance, ultimately leading to ‌shear failure in the joint region‌.

The beam-end flexural failure in CL-6 primarily resulted from the relatively weak beam flexural capacity of this specimen. After yielding of the longitudinal reinforcement, with repeated loading cycles, concrete crushing occurred at the beam-end compression zone with a reduction in effective compression zone height of beam. This led to a significant decrease in shear force transferred to the diagonal compression strut. At this stage, the two mechanisms effectively resisted the joint shear force. Ultimately, due to severe beam flexural failure, the bearing capacity of specimen CL-2 decreased significantly. The failure mode of specimen CL-6 at the end of the test is shown in Fig. 7f. The joint region of CL-6 exhibited relatively minor damage.

Crack–based strut–and–tie model

Model introduction and basic assumptions

Figure 6 depicts the typical crack pattern observed in beam–column joint tests. Based on the deformation characteristics of the joint and the force transfer mechanisms in the joint, a Crack–based STM has been established. Figure 9 shows the proposed Crack–based STM for interior beam–column joint. The tension ties (Fig. 9), which represent the horizontal joint stirrups and longitudinal column bars, are developed to incorporate the influence of the tie rod of truss mechanism. At the same time, the compression struts are developed to take the impact of diagonal struts into account. The main diagonal compression strut AE is developed to account for the influence of diagonal strut mechanism and a portion of struts of truss mechanism. The subdiagonal compression struts (AD, BD, HE, and HF) are established to account for the influence of the remaining part of struts of truss mechanism. In addition, in the proposed Crack–based STM, the confined mechanism is considered by the Mander confined concrete model35.Fig. 9Crack–based STM.

It is well known that the mechanical behavior of the beam-column joint region is highly complex, with three force transfer mechanisms existing in the joint region: the diagonal compression strut mechanism, truss mechanism, and confined mechanism, as shown in Fig. 1. The Crack-based STM directly incorporates both the diagonal compression strut mechanism and the truss mechanism by analyzing the interaction between tension ties and compression struts. Additionally, when determining the parameters for the concrete compression struts, the confinement mechanism is indirectly accounted for through Mander confined concrete model. It can be concluded that the proposed model comprehensively accounts for multiple force transfer mechanisms, thereby providing a more rational representation of the stress states of both reinforcement and concrete within the joint region. Consequently, the calculated results of the proposed model show good agreement with the experimental measurements. In contrast, the Zhao-Yang model is exclusively based on the diagonal compression strut mechanism. The effects of both joint horizontal transverse reinforcement and column vertical reinforcement on joint shear transfer are not considered. However, experimental evidence clearly indicates that the reinforcements within joint region significantly influence the joint shear capacity and joint deformation capacity. Consequently, the calculated joint shear stress is underestimated by Zhao-Yang model.

Description of the joint finite element analysis model

The BeamColumnJoint Element (BCJE) modified by Mitra and Lowes is idealized in detail in Fig. 1648. The BCJE is designed to integrate smoothly with standard 2D beam-column elements. It is equipped with four external nodes that each have three degrees of freedom (DOF) and four internal nodes with one DOF each. This element comprises of three kind of components (Fig. 16). Bar-slip components represent the strength and stiffness degradation attributable to bond slip damage, while interface-shear components represent the reduced shear transfer capacity at the joint perimeter resulting from crack widening. Additionally, the shear-panel component represents the strength and stiffness degradation because of joint shear failure. Constitutive relationships of the components are constructed to ensure that the BCJE can accurately reproduce the inelastic response of the joint. In current application, an elastic load-deformation relationship with a high degree of stiffness is employed to simulate interface-shear response29.Fig. 16BCJE modified by Mitra and Lowes48 (Adobe Photoshop version 12.0.1; https://www.adobe.com/).

Conclusion

The present investigation focuses on the analysis of the shear performance of RC interior beam-column joint. Six full-scale interior beam-column subassemblages were tested and analyzed. A theoretical strut-and-tie model was proposed to predict the joint shear strength and joint shear deformation of beam–column subassemblages. The following main conclusions were obtained:Experimental studies of the beam-column subassemblages have shown that under cyclic loading, high shear stress is concentrated in the beam-column joint region. The measured joint shear deformation increased after reaching the maximum load and was followed by significant degradation in strength and stiffness. At the end of the tests, severe damage was observed in the joint cores. Increasing the joint horizontal transverse reinforcement characteristic value can effectively mitigate the rate of joint strength degradation and reduce the joint shear deformation.Based on the force–deformation characteristics and crack distribution patterns of RC interior beam-column joints, a Crack-based Strut-and-Tie Model was developed. The force equilibrium conditions and deformation compatibility requirements of this model were formulated. A calculation method for the joint shear stress–strain skeleton curve was established based on the Crack-based STM.The joint shear stress–strain curves calculated by the Crack-based STM were compared against published experimental data and those obtained from the Zhao-Yang model.

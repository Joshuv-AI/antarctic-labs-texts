Genomic evaluation and gene expression analysis of preimplantation embryos for enhancing cattle breeding and in vitro embryo production
Authors: Takashi FUJII
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractGenomic selection using genomic estimated breeding values (GEBVs) based on single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) genotyping has been widely adopted for efficient breeding of cattle. Recent advances in DNA analysis technologies have enabled comprehensive SNP genotyping using a small number of cells. Implementation of a preimplantation genomic selection (PGS) system, wherein GEBVs are calculated from a limited number of embryonic cells, can further enhance breeding efficiency through the selective production of genetically superior animals by embryo transfer. In addition, the use of bovine embryos produced by in vitro fertilization (IVF) has increased worldwide in recent years. However, the implantation potential of bovine IVF embryos is lower than that of in vivo-derived (IVD) embryos, and aberrant epigenetic modifications and gene expression are considered major contributing factors to the reduced developmental competence of bovine IVF embryos. Therefore, the differences in the gene expression characteristics of bovine IVD and IVF embryos need to be better understood for improved bovine in vitro production (IVP) systems. This review outlines the development and applications of PGS systems using bovine blastocysts, elongating conceptuses, and demi-embryos derived from blastomere separation. It also discusses the utility of gene expression profiling as a method for evaluating the quality of bovine preimplantation embryos, which may contribute to improvements in bovine IVP systems.Keywords: Bovine, Embryo, Gene expression, Genomic evaluation, Preimplantation genomic selection

Introduction

Genomic selection in cattle using genomic estimated breeding values (GEBVs) based on single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) genotyping is an accepted method for accelerating genetic improvement, as it shortens the generation interval and increases the accuracy of predicted breeding values [1]. Genomic selection systems have been established and implemented in Holstein and Japanese Black cattle for predicting economically important traits, such as lactation and carcass characteristics [2,3,4]. In general, GEBVs are calculated after birth; however, if GEBVs could be estimated in preimplantation embryos and a preimplantation genomic selection (PGS) system enabling the selective production of genetically superior animals via embryo transfer (ET) were implemented, this approach would facilitate more efficient and strategically planned genetic improvement in cattle.

Further, in vitro production (IVP) systems for bovine embryos have improved, and the use of embryos produced through ovum pick-up-in vitro fertilization (OPU-IVF) for transfer has markedly increased in recent years [5]. The OPU-IVF technique enables more efficient bovine embryo production than that obtained with multiple ovulation and embryo transfer (MOET) programs [6]. However, the conception rate following the transfer of IVF embryos is often lower than that of in vivo-derived (IVD) counterparts produced via MOET [7,8,9]. Although bovine IVD and IVF embryos differ in several aspects, including morphology [10], developmental kinetics [11], and chromosomal aneuploidy rates [12], aberrant epigenetic modifications and gene expression during preimplantation development have attracted considerable attention as major contributors to developmental arrest in IVF embryos [13, 14]. Therefore, a better understanding of the gene expression characteristics in preimplantation embryos can facilitate a more reliable assessment of developmental competence and ultimately contribute to improved bovine IVP systems.

This review describes the development and application of a PGS system for more efficient cattle breeding and the methods for evaluating bovine preimplantation embryo quality based on gene expression profiling by presenting several findings from our previous studies [15,16,17,18].

Development and application of a PGS system using blastocysts from Japanese Black cattle

An embryonic biopsy is a prerequisite for PGS, and because SNP genotyping requires several days, cryopreservation of the biopsied embryos is also necessary. In bovine embryos, embryonic biopsies are often performed at the blastocyst stage using a microblade (Fig. 1). Because large biopsies reduce the viability and conception rates after cryopreservation and transfer [19, 20], the number of cells that can be biopsied from bovine blastocysts is limited to approximately 10% of the embryo (typically 10–15 cells) [21,22,23,24]. The amount of DNA obtained from a biopsy of 10–15 cells is approximately 60–90 pg, which is insufficient for SNP genotyping using microarray platforms. Therefore, whole-genome amplification (WGA) is required to amplify the DNA prior to SNP genotyping. Several studies on dairy cattle have reported a strong correlation between the GEBVs for economic traits in biopsied embryonic cells subjected to WGA and in calves produced using ET [21, 22]. These findings indicate that WGA prior to SNP genotyping is effective for reliable GEBVs calculations and has potential application as a PGS system in dairy cattle. However, as of 2019, no PGS system had been established for Japanese Black cattle.

Therefore, we first investigated the accuracy of SNP genotyping in biopsied cells subjected to WGA in IVD Japanese Black cattle embryos. The mean call rate (mean ± SD) for the approximately 15 biopsied cells was 98.1 ± 0.3%. Further, the mean concordance rate for called genotypes (mean ± SD) between the 15 biopsied cells and the corresponding biopsied embryos was 99.9 ± 0.02%. Furthermore, the GEBVs for carcass traits, such as carcass weight, ribeye area, and marbling score, calculated from SNP genotypes in the 15 biopsied cells, closely matched those of the calves produced using ET. These findings suggest that genomic evaluation of carcass traits using approximately 15 biopsied cells subjected to WGA can be reliably performed in Japanese Black cattle.

Next, we evaluated the efficiency of the PGS system for carcass traits in Japanese Black cattle. In total, 208 IVD blastocysts and expanded blastocysts were biopsied (approximately 15 cells), and the biopsied embryos were subsequently vitrified after equilibration in equilibration solution comprising 7.5% ethylene glycol (EG), 7.5% dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), and 20% newborn calf serum (NBCS) in TCM199 for 3 min and in vitrification solution composed of 15% EG, 15% DMSO, 0.5 M sucrose, and 20% NBCS in TCM199 for 40 sec. The biopsied cells were then subjected to WGA, SNP genotyping, and GEBVs calculations. SNP call rates for the biopsied cells ranged from 21.0% to 99.3%, and SNP genotyping was successful for 88.5% of the samples (a call rate of ≥ 85% was considered a successful SNP genotyping based on previous reports [22]). Previous studies have reported a sexing efficiency of approximately 80–90% in a few biopsied embryonic cells using PCR [23, 25,26,27]. Based on these reports, our success rate of SNP genotyping was considered acceptable for genetic testing using a limited number of embryonic cells. Further, considerable variation was observed among full-sib embryos in the GEBVs for carcass traits. Notably, some embryos exhibited higher GEBVs for the ribeye area or marbling score than those of their sires. Based on the GEBVs records, vitrified GEBVs-evaluated blastocysts were selected and transferred to recipient cows. The conception rates on days 40 and 60 were 41.9%. Considering previous reports of a pregnancy rate of 46.9% for biopsied and vitrified bovine IVD embryos in the collaborative work of Japanese prefectural livestock experimental stations [28], our results appear reasonable. Furthermore, the GEBVs for carcass traits calculated from the biopsied cells were matched well with those of the calves produced using ET (Fig. 2). Overall, these findings demonstrated the feasibility of a PGS system using IVD blastocysts for carcass traits in Japanese Black cattle.

These embryos are then biopsied, and the biopsied cells are subjected to WGA and SNP genotyping to evaluate the GEBVs for carcass traits and to estimate sex. Development of a PGS system using elongating conceptuses

A PGS system that uses blastocysts requires micromanipulators to obtain embryonic cells through biopsies. However, micromanipulation requires expensive equipment and a high level of skill, which limits the widespread application of PGS systems. In fact, Hashiyada [29] has reported that, depending on the number of embryos being handled, a few years of training are required to increase the success rate of bovine embryo bisection using micromanipulation technologies for a beginner operator. Therefore, a PGS system with simple procedures for obtaining embryonic cells and not requiring micromanipulation technologies needs to be developed.

Following successful fertilization, the bovine embryo enters the uterus from the oviduct on days 4–5 and subsequently develops into a blastocyst by day 7. After hatching from the zona pellucida, the morphology of the bovine blastocyst changes from spherical to filamentous, in a process known as elongation, and is referred to as a ‘conceptus.’ The elongating conceptus grows a few millimeters to centimeters in length by day 14 of gestation and reaches tens and several centimeters by day 16 [30]. Kimura and Matsuyama [31] reported successful nonsurgical collection and transfer of elongating bovine conceptuses. They also demonstrated that pregnancy rates after transfer did not differ between day 7 embryos and elongating conceptuses. Furthermore, the biopsy of a trophectoderm fragment containing several hundred to several thousand cells, performed under a stereomicroscope or with the naked eye for sexing using the loop-mediated isothermal amplification method [32], did not affect the pregnancy rate after transfer. These findings led us to apply the technique of elongating conceptus transfer for PGS without expensive equipment and a high level of skills. However, cryopreservation methods for bovine elongating conceptuses remain to be established.

We first investigated the accuracy of SNP genotyping using cells biopsied from bovine elongating conceptuses without micromanipulation (Fig. 4). Elongating conceptuses were non-surgically recovered from Japanese Black cows treated with superovulation and artificial insemination (AI) on day13 and 14 post-AI using balloon catheters with enlarged side holes. The call rates in the biopsied cells from two elongating conceptuses subjected to WGA were 95.1% and 99.3%, which were sufficient for calculating GEBVs. Next, we determined the optimal cryopreservation method for elongating bovine conceptuses. Owing to their large size, elongating conceptuses cannot be vitrified using a Cryotop device, which is generally used for the vitrification of embryos in domestic animals. Therefore, we vitrified bovine elongating conceptuses using a method based on the nylon mesh technique employed for zebrafish embryo vitrification [33] with some modifications. The rate of dead cells in elongating conceptuses vitrified after equilibration in equilibration solution (the same solution used for biopsied blastocysts) for 3 or 10 min and in vitrification solution (the same solution used for biopsied blastocysts) for 1 or 5 min (ranging from 3.5% to 40.7%) was higher than that in fresh elongating conceptuses (ranging from 0.0007% to 2.3%). Although the reason for decreased viability in vitrification methods remains incompletely understood, the conditions during the vitrification procedures used in this study were speculated to be inappropriate for bovine elongating conceptuses. On the other hand, the rate of dead cells in elongating conceptuses cryopreserved by slow freezing after equilibration in a freezing solution comprising 1.5 M EG, 0.1 M trehalose, and 18% NBCS in PBS for 10 or 60 min (ranging from 0.075% to 4.9%) was comparable to that in fresh elongating conceptuses. Further, for the first time, two healthy calves were obtained following the transfer of elongating conceptuses cryopreserved by slow freezing (equilibration for 10 min). These findings highlight the potential of the PGS system using elongating conceptuses with simple procedures. However, the size of the elongating conceptuses showed large variation, even on the same recovery day, which may hinder the establishment of a consistent method for cryopreservation. Further research is thus required to clarify the influence of the size and biopsy of elongating conceptuses on viability after cryopreservation and transfer.

Development of a PGS system using demi-blastocysts produced using a blastomere separation technique

Blastomere separation (BS) is a well-established technique used to produce monozygotic embryos or calves by separating blastomeres from zona pellucida-removed IVF embryos at the 2–8-cell stages [29, 34]. This method has been studied and utilized as an effective method for the efficient breeding and rapid propagation of genetically superior cattle. BS does not require expensive equipment and skilled manipulation; therefore, it has the potential to be applied to a PGS system with simple procedures, similar to approaches involving elongating conceptuses. Notably, one of the monozygotic demi-blastocysts is used for genomic evaluation, whereas the other is used for calf production via ET (Fig. 5). However, although separated blastomeres from early stage (2–8-cell stage) bovine IVF embryos can be successfully vitrified using a Cryotop device [35], the optimal vitrification protocol for demi-blastocysts produced by BS remains unclear.

First, we evaluated the developmental competence of separated blastomeres derived from 2-cell stage embryos using a commercial well-of-the-well (WOW) culture system. The rates of blastocyst formation and blastocyst formation in these pairs were 78.3% and 67.9%, respectively. Tagawa et al. [34] and Hashiyada [29] reported that the proportion of monozygotic blastocyst pairs derived from 2-cell embryos using the WOW culture system were 62.0% and 48.0%, respectively; our data show results comparable to those in previous reports. Additionally, we observed that the rate at which a couple of blastomeres developed into a blastocyst and to a ≥ 16-cell stage embryo (thought to be applicable for reliable SNP genotyping [15]) at day 7 was 73.6%, which was not different from the rate of blastocyst formation at day 7 in intact 2-cell stage embryos (81.3%). This result indicates that BS treatment may not reduce the efficiency of the PGS system compared with that of the microblade-biopsy approach using intact blastocysts.

We then examined the accuracy of SNP genotyping and determined the optimal cryopreservation method for in demi-blastocysts produced using BS. The call rates in SNP genotyping using DNA from demi-blastocysts subjected to WGA ranged from 98.3% to 99.3%, which was sufficient for calculating GEBVs. Further, the concordance rate of the co-called genotypes between monozygotic demi-blastocysts exceeded 99%. These findings indicate that accurate SNP genotyping and GEBVs calculations can be achieved using one of the demi-blastocysts produced by BS. Furthermore, we demonstrated that immersion in equilibration solution (the same solution used for biopsied blastocysts) for 2.5 min, followed by immersion in vitrification solution (the same solution used for biopsied blastocysts) for 50 sec, is suitable for the vitrification of demi-blastocysts produced by BS. The demi-blastocysts derived from separated blastomeres lack a zona pellucida and have fewer cell and smaller overall sizes than those of the intact blastocysts. Consequently, a shorter equilibrium time compared with the general equilibrium time for intact blastocysts (equilibration solution for 3 min, vitrification solution for 60 sec) [36] may have a beneficial effect on the viability of the demi-blastocysts after vitrification-warming by reducing the toxicity caused by a high concentration of cryoprotectants. Moreover, we successfully obtained pregnancies by transferring the vitrified demi-blastocysts. These findings suggest that the BS technique is a useful means of developing a PGS system using bovine IVF embryos with simple procedures.

Evaluation of bovine preimplantation embryo quality based on gene expression profiles

Many studies, including RNA-sequencing analyses, have characterized gene expression status in bovine IVD and IVF embryos [37,38,39,40,41]. However, reliable biomarker genes capable of predicting developmental competence in bovine IVF embryos remain to be identified. Further, some studies have employed pooled embryo samples for gene expression analyses, which may obscure the interpretation of gene expression patterns among embryos of different origins because embryos show individual differences in gene expression. To obtain deeper insights into the molecular signatures of bovine IVD and IVF embryos, the expression of various genes in individual embryos need to be examined (gene expression profiling). Whole-transcriptome amplification (WTA) enables the generation of large amounts of cDNA from minute quantities of RNA, thereby allowing the accurate quantification of multiple gene transcripts in a single embryo using quantitative real-time PCR. In this study, we compared the expression of multiple developmentally important genes involved in fetal growth (IGFBP-2 and IGFBP-3), membrane permeability (AQP3), lineage differentiation (OCT-4, SOX2, FGF4, NANOG, TEAD4, CDX2, and GATA3), and pregnancy recognition (IFNT) in individual bovine IVD (as normal control) and IVF embryos using WTA to develop a method for evaluating the quality of bovine IVF embryos based on gene expression profiling.

Gene expression profiling using WTA was performed on individual IVD and IVF embryos (Fig. 6). In SOX2, FGF4, and NANOG analyses, the proportion of embryos classified as having low expression was significantly higher in the IVF group than in the IVD group, whereas the proportion of embryos classified as having high expression was significantly lower in the IVF group. SOX2 and NANOG are well-known pluripotency factors [42]. Deficient SOX2 expression results in a blastocyst with a reduced number of cells and disrupts the network of pluripotency genes in bovine blastocysts [43]. Further, NANOG is required for epiblast formation and maintenance of pluripotency in bovine embryos [44]. Furthermore, the FGF4 signaling pathway is required to maintain the proliferation of TE cells [45], and FGF4 is a potential indicator of ICM function in bovine embryos [46]. Therefore, the expression levels of SOX2, NANOG, and FGF4 may be useful markers for estimating the quality of bovine preimplantation embryos. Further, gene expression analysis revealed a relationship between the expression of developmentally important genes in bovine preimplantation embryos, and the pattern of correlation between the expression of genes in individual embryos differed between IVD and IVF embryos. Collectively, these findings suggest that gene expression profiling using IVD embryos as normal controls could be a useful tool for estimating preimplantation embryo quality and selecting a better protocol for bovine IVP systems.

Conclusion and prospects

Combined with the advances in SNP genotyping technology and the establishment of systems for calculating GEBVs, the PGS system is approaching practical implementation. The use of the PGS system can facilitate selective production of genetically superior animals, thereby further accelerating the development of sire candidates and the genetic improvement of female cattle. However, even embryos with high GEBVs have no value if they fail to result in successful pregnancy and calving or if the offspring do not develop normally. To further enhance the PGS system, it is necessary to develop low- or non-invasive genetic testing methods for embryos and establish optimal cryopreservation protocols for elongating conceptuses, and demi-blastocysts produced by BS to obtain a stable conception rate after transfer. Further, for recipient cows receiving GEBV-evaluated embryos or conceptuses, cows with high fertility should be selected by applying technologies that assess or improve reproductive performance [47, 48], thereby maximizing the effectiveness of the PGS system. Furthermore, the development of more efficient breeding methods that combine the PGS system with the OPU-IVF technique is expected. Moreover, the quality of bovine preimplantation embryos can be evaluated through gene expression profiling using WTA based on the biopsied cells or one of the demi-blastocysts produced by BS. Therefore, the simultaneous evaluation of GEBVs and gene expression profiles can help select embryos with high genetic merit and high developmental competence, thereby contributing to extremely efficient cattle breeding.

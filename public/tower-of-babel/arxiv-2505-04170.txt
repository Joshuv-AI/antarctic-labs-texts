Abstract
. We introduce a framework for Riemannian diffeology. To this end, we use the tangent functor in the sense of Blohmann and one of the options of a metric on a diffeological space in the sense of Iglesias-Zemmour. As a consequence, the category consisting of weak Riemannian diffeological spaces and isometries is established. With a technical condition for a definite weak Riemannian metric, we show that the pseudodistance induced by the metric is indeed a distance. As examples of weak Riemannian diffeological spaces, an adjunction space of manifolds, a space of smooth maps and the mixed one are considered.

Diffeology

However, it is hard to say that the development of Riemannian notions of diffeological spaces is sufficient.

In

The definition of the definiteness of the metric on a diffeological space is described with differential

Making a quote from

“It is not clear what definition is the best, for many examples built with manifolds and spaces of smooth maps they do coincide.
But they may differ in general and, depending on the problem, one must choose one or the other.”

This article introduces a weak Riemannian metric of a diffeological space and its definiteness using the tangent functor due to Blohmann

In particular, the colimit construction of the tangent functor works well in considering the metrics.
As a consequence, we may deal with weak Riemannian metrics for a diffeological adjunction space and the space of smooth maps in our framework simultaneously.
A comparison between a weak Riemannian metric and the metric due to Iglesias-Zemmour is made in Propositions

It is worthwhile mentioning that Goldammer and Welker introduce another definition of a Riemannian diffeological space in

While it is important to consider the relationship between two frameworks of Riemannian diffeology, we do not pursue the issue in this article.

1.1.
Future work and perspective

The category of diffeological spaces contains manifolds and other spaces with smooth structures; see Example
2.2
and Section
3.2
below.
Therefore, diffeology together with the notion of Riemannian metrics (Riemannian diffeology) produces many issues on smooth spaces.
Here are some of them.

i)

With the framework of Riemannian metrics, we may consider geodesic calculus in diffeology as seen in
[
20
]
.

ii)

We expect a new development of convergence theory of smooth spaces with concepts such as connections, curvatures and the Gromov-Hausdorff distance if the notion of metrics of diffeological spaces combines with
elastic diffeology

*
*

*

See
[
2
,
5
,
6
]
for Lie brackets of vector fields which are defined in a category with a tangent structure in the sense of Rosický
[
33
]
. We refer the reader to
[
7
]
for connections in tangent categories.
.

iii)

Topological data analysis (TDA) begins usually by putting a data set into an appropriate metric space, in particular an Euclidean space, and investigate shapes of the data. Thus, future work includes moreover studying TDA by using a Riemannian diffeological space as the stage for a data set.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall examples of diffeological spaces and important fundamental constructions in diffeology which are used throughout this article.
We begin with the definition of a diffeological space.
A comprehensive reference for diffeology is the book by Iglesias-Zemmour
[
18
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

We call an open subset
U
of a Euclidean space (of arbitrary, but finite dimension) a
domain
.
A map from a domain to a set
X
is called a
parametrization
of
X
.
The domain of a parametrization
P
of
X
is denoted by
U_{P}
or
\mathrm{dom}(P)
.

Let
X
be a set.
A set
{\mathcal{D}}
of parametrizations of
X
is a
diffeology
of
X
if the following three conditions hold:

(1)

(Covering) Every constant map
U\to X
for all domains
U
is in
{\mathcal{D}}
;

(2)

(Compatibility) If
U\to X
is in
{\mathcal{D}}
, then for any smooth map
V\to U
from another domain
V
, the composite
V\to U\to X
is also in
{\mathcal{D}}
;

(3)

(Locality) If
U=\bigcup_{i}U_{i}
is an open cover of a domain
U
and
U\to X
is a map such that each restriction
U_{i}\to X
is in
{\mathcal{D}}
, then
U\to X
is in
{\mathcal{D}}
.

An element of a diffeology
{\mathcal{D}}
of
X
is called a
plot
of
X
.
A
diffeological space

(X,{\mathcal{D}})
consists of a set
X
and a diffeology
{\mathcal{D}}
of
X
.

For diffeological spaces
(X,{\mathcal{D}}^{X})
and
(Y,{\mathcal{D}}^{Y})
, a map
f\colon X\to Y
is
smooth
if the composite
f\circ P
is in
{\mathcal{D}}^{Y}
for each plot
P\in{\mathcal{D}}^{X}
.
Then, we have the category
\mathsf{Diff}
consisting of all diffeological spaces and smooth maps.
The isomorphisms in
\mathsf{Diff}
are referred to as
diffeomorphisms
.
Denote as
X\cong Y
if
X,Y\in\mathsf{Diff}
are diffeomorphic.

Every set admits (possibly trivial) diffeologies;
the
discrete (resp. indiscrete) diffeology
consisting of locally constant (resp. all the) parametrizations.
A typical example of a diffeological space is a manifold, as in the following Example
2.2
(
1
).
We also have many other examples each of which is not a manifold in general.

Example 2.2
.

(1)

Let
M
be a manifold of finite dimension.
Then, the underlying set
M
and the
standard diffeology

{\mathcal{D}}^{M}_{\text{std}}
give rise to a diffeological space
(M,{\mathcal{D}}^{M}_{\text{std}})

{\mathcal{D}}^{M}_{\text{std}}
is defined to be the set of all smooth maps
U\to M
from domains to

in the usual sense.
We have a functor
m\colon\mathsf{Mfd}\to\mathsf{Diff}
from the category of manifolds defined by
m(M)=(M,{\mathcal{D}}^{M}_{\text{std}})
.
This functor is fully faithful; see, for example,
[
1
, 2.1 Example]
.

(2)

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a set of parametrizations of
X
.
Then define
\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
to be the set of all parametrizations
P\colon U_{P}\to X
satisfying the following condition:

For any
r\in U_{P}
, there exists an open neighborhood
V_{r}
of
r
in
U_{P}
such that
P|_{V_{r}}
is a constant plot, or there exist

and a smooth map

such that

.

Then
\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
is a diffeology of

, called the
diffeology generated by

.

Let
(X,\mathcal{D})
be a diffeological space.
A subset
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{D}
is a
generating family
of
\mathcal{D}
if
\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle=\mathcal{D}
.
Examples include generating families
\mathcal{G}_{\text{atlas}}
and
\mathcal{G}_{\text{imm}}
of the standard diffeology
\mathcal{D}^{M}_{\text{std}}
of a manifold
M
with an atlas
\{(V_{\lambda},\varphi_{\lambda})\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
given respectively by
\mathcal{G}_{\text{atlas}}=\{i_{\lambda}\circ\varphi_{\lambda}^{-1}\colon\varphi_{\lambda}(V_{\lambda})\to M\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
(where
i_{\lambda}\colon V_{\lambda}\to M
is the inclusion), and the set of immersions
\mathcal{G}_{\text{imm}}=\{\text{immersions }f\colon U\looparrowright M\}
.

(3)

For a diffeological space
(X,{\mathcal{D}}^{X})
and a subset
A
of
X
, we define
{\mathcal{D}}^{A}_{\text{sub}}
by

{\mathcal{D}}^{A}_{\text{sub}}\coloneqq\{P\colon U_{P}\to A\mid U_{P}\text{ is a domain and }i\circ P\in{\mathcal{D}}^{X}\}
,
where
i\colon A\to X
is the inclusion.
Then, the set
{\mathcal{D}}^{A}_{\text{sub}}
is a diffeology of
A
, which is called the
sub-diffeology
.
We call
(A,{\mathcal{D}}^{A}_{\text{sub}})
a
diffeological subspace
of
X
.

(4)

Let
\{(X_{i},{\mathcal{D}}_{i})\}_{i\in I}
be a family of diffeological spaces.
Then, the product
\Pi_{i\in I}X_{i}
has a diffeology
{\mathcal{D}}
, called the
product diffeology
, defined to be the set of all parameterizations
P\colon U_{P}\to\Pi_{i\in I}X_{i}
such that
\pi_{i}\circ p
are plots of
X_{i}
for each
i\in I
, where
\pi_{i}\colon\Pi_{i\in I}X_{i}\to X_{i}
denotes the canonical projection.

(5)

More general, the
initial diffeology

{\mathcal{D}}^{Y}
for maps
h_{i}\colon Y\to(X_{i},{\mathcal{D}}_{i})
for
i\in I
is defined by
{\mathcal{D}}^{Y}\coloneqq\{P\colon U_{P}\to Y\mid h_{i}\circ P\in{\mathcal{D}}_{i}\text{ for }i\in I\}
.
This is the largest diffeology on
Y
making all

smooth.
We call an injective map

between diffeological spaces an

if
\mathcal{D}^{X}
coincides with the initial diffeology for

.
It is immediate that if
A
is a diffeological subspace of
X
, then the inclusion

is an induction.

(6)

Let
(X,{\mathcal{D}}^{X})
and
(Y,{\mathcal{D}}^{Y})
be diffeological spaces.
Let
C^{\infty}(X,Y)
denote the set of all smooth maps from
X
to
Y
.
The
functional diffeology

{\mathcal{D}}_{\text{func}}
is defined to be the set of parametrizations
P\colon U_{P}\to C^{\infty}(X,Y)
whose adjoints
\mathrm{ad}(P)\colon U_{P}\times X\to Y

(7)

Let
{\mathcal{F}}\coloneqq\{f_{i}\colon Y_{i}\to X\}_{i\in I}
be a set of maps from diffeological spaces
(Y_{i},{\mathcal{D}}^{Y_{i}})
(

) to a set

.
Then, we can define a diffeology
{\mathcal{D}}^{X}
of

to be the set of parametrizations
P\colon U\to X
satisfying the following condition;
for
r\in U
,
(i) there exists an open neighborhood
V_{r}
of
r
in
U
such that
P|_{V_{r}}
is constant, or
(ii) for
i\in I
, there exists an open neighborhood

of

(P_{i}\colon V_{r,i}\to Y_{i})\in{\mathcal{D}}^{Y_{i}}
with

.
We call
{\mathcal{D}}^{X}
the
final diffeology
of
X
with respect to
{\mathcal{F}}
. This is the smallest diffeology on
X
making all

smooth.

Moreover, by definition, a surjective map
\pi\colon X\to Y
between diffeological spaces is a

if the diffeology of

is the final diffeology with respect to
\pi
.

We shall say that a smooth surjection
\pi\colon X\to Y
is a

if for a point

and each plot

with

,

and a plot

such that

and
\pi\circ Q=P|_{W}
.

(8)

For a family of diffeological spaces
\{(X_{i},{\mathcal{D}}_{i})\}_{i\in I}
,
the coproduct
\coprod_{i\in I}X_{i}
has the final diffeology with respect to the set of canonical inclusions. The diffeology is called the
sum diffeology
.

(9)

Let
(X,{\mathcal{D}})
be a diffeological space with an equivalence relation
\sim
.
Then, the final diffeology of
X/\mathord{\sim}
with respect to the quotient map
q\colon X\to X/\mathord{\sim}
is called the

.
In particular

is a subduction.

We have a result concerning the construction (

3.
A Riemannian diffeological space

3.1.
Diffeological Riemannian metrics

Let
\mathsf{Euc}
denote the category consisting of domains and smooth maps.
Let
\widehat{T}\colon\mathsf{Euc}\to\mathsf{Euc}
be the functor defined by

\widehat{T}(U)\coloneqq U\times{\mathbb{R}}^{\dim U}

and

\mathcal{Y}\colon\mathsf{Euc}\to\mathsf{Diff}

We recall the tangent functor
T\colon\mathsf{Diff}\to\mathsf{Diff}
from the category of diffeological spaces to itself in the sense of Blohmann
[
2
,
3
]
,
which is the left Kan extension

\mathbb{L}\mathcal{Y}\widehat{T}\coloneqq\text{Lan}_{\mathcal{Y}}\mathcal{Y}\widehat{T}
of the functor

\mathcal{Y}\widehat{T}\colon\mathsf{Euc}\to\mathsf{Diff}
along the Yoneda functor
\mathcal{Y}
; see also
[
29
]

For a diffeological space
(X,{\mathcal{D}})

T(X)=\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_{P\in{\mathcal{D}}}(U_{P}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{\dim U_{P}}).

Here, the diffeology
{\mathcal{D}}
is regarded as a category whose objects are plots of
X
and whose morphisms are smooth maps

with

By using the functor

\widehat{T}_{2}(U)\coloneqq\widehat{T}(U)\times_{U}\widehat{T}(U)=U\times{\mathbb{R}}^{\dim U}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{\dim U}

with the Jacobian matrix

for a smooth map

,
we have the functor

T_{2}\coloneqq\mathbb{L}\mathcal{Y}\widehat{T}_{2}\colon\mathsf{Diff}\to\mathsf{Diff}

via the left Kan extension along the Yoneda functor

.
Observe that as a diffeological space,

T_{2}(X)=\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_{P\in{\mathcal{D}}}(U_{P}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{\dim U_{P}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{\dim U_{P}}).

We may write
[x,v_{1},v_{2}]_{P}
for an element in
T_{2}(X)

which has an element
(x,v_{1},v_{2})

U_{P}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{\dim U_{P}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{\dim U_{P}}

3.4.
The Riemannian pseudo-distance

Let

be a weak Riemannian metric on a diffeological space
X
.
Then, we have a pseudo-distance

on
X
associated with the metric
g
by applying the usual procedure,
as in the case of a Riemannian manifold
[
30

.
In fact,
the pseudo-distance
d\colon X\times X\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
is defined by

(3.6)

d(x,y)={\displaystyle\inf_{\gamma\in\text{Path}(X;x,y)}\ell(\gamma)},\quad\text{where}\quad\ell(\gamma)=\int_{0}^{1}(g(\gamma)_{t}(1,1))^{\frac{1}{2}}dt

and
d(x,y)=\infty
if there is no smooth path connecting
x
and
y
.
Here
\text{Path}(X;x,y)
denotes the subset of
\mathrm{Path}(X)
consisting of
\gamma
with
\gamma(0)=x
and
\gamma(1)=y
.

Theorem 3.21
.

Let

be the pseudodistance on a connected diffeological space

defined by a weak Riemannian metric

.
Then the
D
-topology of
X
is finer than the topology
\mathcal{O}_{d}
defined by
d
;
that is, the D-topology contains
\mathcal{O}_{d}
.
In particular, the function
d
on
D(X)\times D(X)
is continuous.

In order to prove the theorem, we need a lemma.

Lemma 3.22
.

Let
d_{E}
be the Euclidean metric on
U_{P}
and
x\in U_{P}\cap V
for some domain

.
Then there exists a real number
k>0
and an open ball

centered at

\overline{B}\subset U_{P}\cap V

on

is the distance defined by the symmetric, positive covariant

.
Moreover,
if the

-tensor

on

Proof.

This follows from the proof of
[

The upper bound is given by the continuity of

The definiteness of

∎

Proof of Theorem

Claim 3.23

\pi(U_{d_{P}}(x;\varepsilon))\subset U_{d}(P(x);\varepsilon)

Proof.

Let

which is not a manifold. Observe that

is non-Hausdorff.

Example 4.5
.

We consider the pushout diffeological space
\boldsymbol{+}\coloneqq{\mathbb{R}}_{1}\coprod_{\{0\}}{\mathbb{R}}_{2}
.
We see that the diffeology of
\boldsymbol{+}
has a generating family which separates points;
see Definition
3.25
.
Moreover, the usual metrics on

and

satisfy the assumption (I).

5.
A diffeological mapping space

In this section,
we focus on a weak Riemannian metrics on a mapping space and the pseudo-distance associated with the metric.
The definiteness of the metric is also discussed introducing a subdiffeology of the functional diffeology.

5.1.
Weak Riemannian metrics on mapping spaces

In
[
19
, Exercise (3)]
, a Riemannian metric on
C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
is discussed.
Riemannian metrics on diffeological mapping spaces including the metric due to Iglesias-Zemmour are interpreted in our framework.

Let
M
be a closed orientable finite dimensional manifold and
N
a connected
weak Riemannian diffeological space.
Let
\{(V_{\lambda},\varphi_{\lambda})\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be an atlas of
M
.
Then, for a plot
P\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\text{func}}
of the diffeological mapping space
C^{\infty}(M,N)
,

r\in U_{P}
and tangent vectors
v,w\in T_{r}U_{P}
,
we define a smooth map
\Theta_{g_{N}}(P)(v,w)(r,\text{--})\colon M\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

\Theta_{g_{N}}(P)(v,w)(r,m)=g_{N}(\mathrm{ad}(P)\circ(1\times\varphi_{\lambda}^{-1}))_{(r,\varphi_{\lambda}(m))}(\underline{v},\underline{w})

for
m\in V_{\lambda}
, where

\underline{u}=(u,0)\in T_{r}U_{P}\times T_{\varphi_{\lambda}(m)}\varphi_{\lambda}(V_{\lambda})=T_{r}U_{P}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{\dim M}
.
For the functional diffeology
{\mathcal{D}}_{\text{func}}
,
we define a metric
g
on
C^{\infty}(M,N)
by

(5.1)

g(P)_{r}(v,w)=\int_{M}\Theta_{g_{N}}(P)(v,w)(r,m)\mathrm{vol}_{M}

for
r\in U_{P}
, where
\mathrm{vol}_{M}
is a fixed
(\dim M)
-form on
M
.

The following Lemma
5.1
shows the well-definedness of
\Theta_{g_{N}}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

\Theta_{g_{N}}(P)(v,w)(r,m)=g_{N}(\mathrm{ev}_{m}\circ P)_{r}(v,w)
.

Proof.

Define
j_{m,\lambda}\colon U_{P}\to U_{P}\times\varphi_{\lambda}(V_{\lambda})
by
j_{m,\lambda}(x)\coloneqq(x,\varphi_{\lambda}(m))
.
Then
\underline{v}=(j_{m,\lambda})_{*}(v)
and

\displaystyle\Theta_{g_{N}}(P)(v,w)(r,m)

\displaystyle=g_{N}(\mathrm{ad}(P)\circ(\mathrm{id}_{U_{P}}\times\varphi_{\lambda}^{-1}))_{(r,\varphi(m))}(\underline{v},\underline{w})

\displaystyle=g_{N}(\mathrm{ad}(P)\circ(\mathrm{id}_{U_{P}}\times\varphi_{\lambda}^{-1})\circ j_{m,\lambda})_{r}(v,w).

It is easy to see that
\mathrm{ad}(P)\circ(\mathrm{id}_{U_{P}}\times\varphi_{\lambda}^{-1})\circ j_{m,\lambda}=\mathrm{ev}_{m}\circ P
.
∎

To see the smoothness of
g
, let
V
and
W
be vector fields on
U_{P}
.
Since
g_{N}
is a weak Riemannian metric on
N
,
g_{N}(\mathrm{ad}(P)\circ(\mathrm{id}_{U_{P}}\times\varphi^{-1}_{\lambda}))
is a 2-tensor field on
U_{P}\times U_{\lambda}
,
where
(V_{\lambda},\varphi_{\lambda})
is a coordinate neighborhood of
m\in M
, and the function on
U_{P}\times V_{\lambda}
defined by

(r,\varphi^{-1}(m))\mapsto g_{N}(\mathrm{ev}_{m}\circ P)_{r}(V(r),W(r))

is smooth.
Thus the map
r\mapsto g(P)_{r}(V(r),W(r))
on
U_{P}
is smooth and hence
g(P)
is a smooth 2-tensor.

We show that
g
is compatible with coordinate changes. Let
F\colon V\to U_{P}
be a
C^{\infty}
-map.
Then, it follows that

g_{N}(\mathrm{ev}_{m}\circ(P\circ F))_{r}(v,w)=g_{N}(\mathrm{ev}_{m}\circ P)_{F(r)}(dF_{r}(v),dF_{v}(w))

Thus, we have

\Theta_{g_{N}}(P\circ F)(v,w)(r,m)=\Theta_{g_{N}}(P)(dF_{r}(v),dF_{r}(w))(F(r),m).

Therefore, it follows that

g(P\circ F)_{r}(v,w)=g(P)_{F(r)}(dF_{r}(v),dF_{r}(w))=F^{*}g(P)_{r}(v,w).

This implies that
g
is a diffeological covariant 2-tensor field on
C^{\infty}(M,N)
.
Symmetry and positivity follow from the fact that
g_{N}
is a metric.
Moreover, we have

Proposition 5.2
.

For the weak Riemannian metric
g
on
(C^{\infty}(M,N),{\mathcal{D}}_{\text{func}})
in
(
5.1
)
,
the pseudo-distance
d
defined by
g
is indeed a distance provided the pseudo-distance
d_{N}
defined by
g_{N}
is a distance on
N
.

Proof.

Assume that

d_{C^{\infty}(M,N)}(f_{0},f_{1})=\inf_{\gamma\in\mathrm{Path}(C^{\infty}(M,N);f_{0},f_{1})}\ell(\gamma)=0
for
f_{0},f_{1}\in C^{\infty}(M,N)
.
Then, it follows form the definition of the length
\ell
that there exists a sequence of paths
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n=1,2,\dots}
from
f_{0}
to
f_{1}
such that

(5.2)

0=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}(g(\gamma_{n})_{s}(1,1))^{\frac{1}{2}}\,ds=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{M}g_{N}(\mathrm{ev}_{m}\circ\gamma_{n})_{s}(1,1)\mathrm{vol}_{M}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\!ds.

In what follows, we write
h_{n}(m,s)
for
g_{N}(\mathrm{ev}_{m}\circ\gamma_{n})_{s}(1,1)
.
By applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(h_{n}(m,s)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot 1\ \mathrm{vol}_{M}\leq\left(\int_{M}h_{n}(m,s)\ \mathrm{vol}_{M}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\left(\int_{M}1\ \mathrm{vol}_{M}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(M)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\ \int_{M}\left(h_{n}(m,s)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{vol}_{M}\right)ds\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{M}h_{n}(m,s)\ \mathrm{vol}_{M}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}ds,

where
\mathrm{vol}(M)=\int_{M}\mathrm{vol}_{M}
.
Taking the limit as
n\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(M)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{M}\left(h_{n}(m,s)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{vol}_{M}\right)ds=0

by (
5.2
).
Since
\mathrm{vol}(M)
is positive constant, it follows from the Fubini theorem that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{M}\left(h_{n}(m,s)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{vol}_{M}\right)ds=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{M}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left(h_{n}(m,s)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}ds\right)\mathrm{vol}_{M}.

By applying
[
34
, 3.12 Theorem]
to the integration on
M
, we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\ell(\mathrm{ev}_{m}\circ\gamma_{n_{k}})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\left(h_{n_{k}}(m,s)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}ds=0

for
m
almost everywhere in
M
and some subsequence
\{\gamma_{n_{k}}\}
of
\{\gamma_{n}\}
.
Let
F_{M}
be the subset of
M
consisting of
m\in M
that does not satisfy (
5.3
).
Then
F_{M}
is of measure zero.
For each fixed element
m\in(F_{M})^{c}
, the smooth map
\mathrm{ev}_{m}\circ\gamma_{n_{l}}
is regarded as a path on
N
from
f_{0}(m)
to
f_{1}(m)
.
This yields that

d_{N}(f_{0}(m),f_{1}(m))=0
for
m\in(F_{M})^{c}
.
Since
d_{N}
is a distance, it follows that
f_{0}=f_{1}
on
(F_{M})^{c}
.

We observe that the composite

\varphi\colon M\xrightarrow{f_{0}\times f_{1}}D(N\times N)\to D(N)\times D(N)\xrightarrow{d_{N}}{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}

is continuous.
This is because

(a)

f_{0}\times f_{1}
is continuous with respect to the
D
-topology on
N\times N
since
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
are smooth, and

(b)

d_{N}\colon N\times N\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
is continuous by Theorem
3.21
.

We see that
x\in F_{M}
if
f_{0}(x)\neq f_{1}(x)
.
Suppose that
f_{0}(x_{0})\neq f_{1}(x_{0})
for some
x_{0}\in M
.
Then
\varphi(x_{0})>0
and
U\coloneqq\varphi^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}_{>0})
is an open neighborhood of
x_{0}
.
This yields that
U\subset F_{M}
. However, since the measure of
F_{M}
is zero,

F_{M}
cannot contain any open set, which is a contradiction.
∎

5.2.
A subdiffeology of the functional diffeology.

We consider the definiteness of a weak Riemannian metric on a diffeological mapping space.
Let
X
and

be diffeological spaces and
\mathcal{G}
a generating family of
{\mathcal{D}}^{Y}

Then, the set

of smooth maps is endowed with the functional diffeology

;
see Example

(

We introduce a subdiffeology of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\text{func}}
.
To this end, we consider the following condition (E) for a plot

.

(E)

For
r\in U_{P}
and

of

\textstyle{W_{r,m}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{U_{P}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

is in
\mathcal{G}
, where

denotes the evaluation map at

Let
\mathcal{F}^{XY}_{\mathcal{G}}
be the subset of plots in

We may write
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{G}}
for
\mathcal{F}^{XY}_{\mathcal{G}}

be the diffeology generated by

.
It is immediate that
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
is contained in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\text{func}}
and that
the identity map

is smooth.

Remark 5.3
.

Consider the case that

is a Riemannian manifold.
Suppose
P\colon U_{P}\to C^{\infty}(M,N)
is such that the map
\mathrm{ad}(P)(-,m)=\mathrm{ev}_{m}\circ P\colon U_{P}\hookrightarrow N
is an embedding for any

.
For example

is such a plot if
U_{P}\cong\mathrm{Int}\,D^{\dim N}
and
\mathrm{ad}(P)\colon U_{P}\times M\to N
is a framed immersion.
Then clearly

itself satisfies the condition (E), and
\mathcal{F_{\mathcal{G}}}
is not empty if
\mathcal{G}
contains such a plot.

Example 5.4
.

The diffeology
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
does not coincide with
{\mathcal{D}}_{\text{func}}
in general.
In fact, we consider the case where
X=Y=U
is an open subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
n\geq 1
and
\mathcal{G}\coloneqq\{\mathrm{id}\colon U\to U\}
.
Then, the standard diffeology of
U
is generated by
\mathcal{G}
.
In this case, for any
P\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{G}}
, the image of
P
consists of constant maps;
for any
r\in U_{P}
and
m\in U
, there exists an open neighborhood
W=W_{r,m}
of
r
in
U_{p}
such that
\mathrm{ev}_{m}\circ P|_{W}=\mathrm{id}_{U}
(in particular

must hold),
and hence,
for any

, we have

.
The same holds for every
Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\langle\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{G}}\rangle
because
Q
locally factors through some
P\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{G}}
.
Therefore
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
is strictly finer than
{\mathcal{D}}_{\text{func}}
.

The following lemma describes fundamental properties of the diffeology
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a smooh map.

(1)

Let
Z
be a diffeological space with a generating family
\mathcal{G}_{Z}
of the diffeology. Then, the map

f^{*}\colon(C^{\infty}(Y,Z),\langle\mathcal{F}^{YZ}_{{\mathcal{G}_{Z}}}\rangle)\to(C^{\infty}(X,Z),\langle\mathcal{F}^{XZ}_{{\mathcal{G}_{Z}}}\rangle)

defined by

is smooth.

(2)

Let
\mathcal{G}_{Y}
be a generating family of the diffeology of
Y
and
\mathcal{G}_{X}
be the pullback
f^{*}\mathcal{G}_{Y}
of
\mathcal{G}_{Y}
by

;
see (
3.4

Then, for any
Z
, the map

f_{*}\colon(C^{\infty}(Z,(X,\langle\mathcal{G}_{X}\rangle)),\langle\mathcal{F}^{ZX}_{\mathcal{G}_{X}}\rangle)\to(C^{\infty}(Z,Y),\langle\mathcal{F}^{ZY}_{\mathcal{G}_{Y}}\rangle)

defined by
f_{*}(\psi)=f\circ\psi
is smooth.

Proof.

Since both
f\colon X\to Y
and

are smooth,
it follows that the maps
f^{*}
and
f_{*}
are smooth with respect to the functional diffeology.
Moreover, we see that
\mathrm{ev}_{x}\circ(f^{*}\circ Q)=\mathrm{ev}_{f(x)}\circ Q
for every

.
This implies that

The fact that

and the definition of

yield that

.

Let
\mathcal{G}

Given weak Riemannian diffeological spaces

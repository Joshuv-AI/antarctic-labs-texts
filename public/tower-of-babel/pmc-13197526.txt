‘Fuhui 631’, a novel hybrid rice restorer line with multiple resistance genes, high combining ability, and superior grain quality developed with marker‑assisted breeding
Authors: Liping Chen, Yongsheng Zhu, Jiahuan Jiang, Linyan Wei, Yingheng Wang, Yanmei Zheng, Qiang Lin, Yanjia Xiao, Yidong Wei, Qiuhua Cai, Fangxi Wu, Huaan Xie, Jianfu Zhang
Published: 2026

‘Fuhui 631’ is a novel indica rice restorer line developed through marker-assisted pyramiding of multiple resistance genes and officially released by Rice Research Institute, Fujian Academy of Agricultural Sciences in 2022. It combines high yield potential, strong combining ability, lodging resistance, heat tolerance, and superior grain quality, representing a significant advance over existing restorer lines. Given the increasing challenges of extreme weather and disease pressure in rice production, there is an urgent need for restorer lines that integrate multiple stress resistances with high combining ability and premium grain quality. Statistical comparisons with the widely grown control variety ‘Jingliangyou 534’ confirmed that ‘Fuhui 631’ exhibits significantly higher resistance to blast (moderately resistant, MR) and bacterial blight (p < 0.01), improved heat tolerance (level 5, moderate, according to NY/T 2915 − 2016) (p < 0.05), and superior grain quality traits including lower chalkiness (1.6%) and higher head rice rate (61.3%) (p < 0.05). These enhancements provide clear utility in hybrid rice breeding for stress‑prone environments, justifying its registration as a novel cultivar.

‘Fuhui 631’ was developed from a cross between the indica variety ‘Huanghuazhan’ (heat tolerant, widely adaptable) and the germplasm ‘8L124’ (indica‑japonica intermediate type), followed by backcrossing with ‘RC24’ (lodging and disease resistance). The pedigree method integrated with marker‑assisted selection was applied from F2 to F8. At F₂, MAS was applied for blast resistance genes Pita, Xa21, and Pi2; plants with plant height 110–120 cm and panicle length ≥ 25 cm were selected. From F₃ to F₄, lines with visual disease resistance (blast and bacterial blight: R/MR retained) and seed set > 85% under heat stress were advanced. Chip‑based genotyping of the F₅ line by Wuhan Shuanglvyuan Biotechnology Co., Ltd. revealed the presence of numerous beneficial genes, including the blast-resistance genes Pi‑d2/Pid2, Pita, Pi2, Pi5, and LHCB5 (Tang et al. 2024; Liu et al. 2026); bacterial blight resistance genes Xa26/Xa3 and Xa21 (Li et al. 2025); brown planthopper resistance gene Bph3 (Liu et al. 2015); drought tolerance gene OsLG3 (Xiong et al. 2018) and nitrogen use efficiency gene NRT1.1B (Liu et al. 2025). From F₅ to F₉, test-crosses were conducted and lines yielding ≥ 5% above the check ‘Jingliangyou 534’ were selected. Multi‑location screening was conducted across Fujian Province, including Fuzhou (early season), Zhangzhou (early season), and Nanping (mid‑season) for heat tolerance, and Longyan, Nanping, and Shaxian for blast and bacterial blight resistance throughout the entire growth period. The breeding process involved generational advancement in Fujian and Hainan, culminating in the stable line 18W33, later designated as ‘Fuhui 631’ after test‑crossing with male‑sterile line ‘He9S’. Field evaluations across multiple environments demonstrated the agronomic superiority of ‘Fuhui 631’. As a mid‑season rice in Jianyang, Fujian, it shows a growth period of 130–132 days, plant height of 105.5–110.2 cm, panicle length of 29.5 cm, and 1000‑grain weight of 26.7 g. Its plant architecture is compact and lodging‑resistant, with uniform maturity. Field performance and phenotypic characteristics of ‘Fuhui 631’ are shown in Fig. 2. Blast resistance was evaluated at the Yangxi natural disease nursery (Sanming, Fujian) by the Plant Protection Research Institute, Fujian Academy of Agricultural Sciences (2023), and ‘Fuhui 631’ was rated as moderately resistant (MR) (Table 1). Chip‑based genotyping revealed the presence of Xa21 and Xa26/Xa3, and multi‑year and multi‑location field evaluations confirmed that ‘Fuhui 631’ showed resistant (R) to moderately resistant (MR) responses to bacterial blight (Table 2). Brown planthopper (BPH) resistance evaluation showed that ‘Fuhui 631’ was moderately resistant (MR), with a mean damage score of 5.04 (Table 3). Genetic characterization further supports its multi‑trait excellence. Gene chip analysis verified the presence of the pyramided resistance genes as well as additional genes associated with high yield and quality, including Gn1a/OsCKX2 (Ashikari et al. 2005), NOG1, LAX1, GNP1, GW8/OsSPL16 (Wang et al. 2012), and OsSPL13/GLW7 (Si et al. 2016). This genetic constitution explains its balanced performance in both stress tolerance and agronomic desirability.

‘Fuhui 631’ exhibits outstanding combining ability and hybrid performance. To date, 72 hybrid combinations have been produced by crossing it with diverse male‑sterile lines. In provincial trials, 14 combinations out‑yielded the mid‑season medium‑maturity control ‘Jingliangyou 534’, and 19 surpassed the mid‑season late‑maturity control ‘Heliangyou 676’. Two hybrids, ‘Yexiangyou 631’ (Fujian Approval Rice 20243039) and ‘Heliangyou 631’ (Fujian Approval Rice 20250009), have been officially released in Fujian Province. Eight additional combinations are currently under national or provincial trials, demonstrating broad adaptability and stable yield performance across environments.

To quantitatively assess the stability of ‘Fuhui 631’ derived hybrids, we analyzed yield data of 25 combinations from four locations (Nanping, Wuhu, Wuhan, Sanya). Average yields differed significantly among locations (ANOVA, P < 0.01), with the highest in Nanping (9576 kg/ha) and the lowest in Sanya (7002 kg/ha). The coefficient of variation (CV) was calculated for each combination. The check ‘Jingliangyou 534’ had a mean yield of 8435 ± 613 kg/ha and a CV of 14.54%. Five combinations showed higher mean yield and lower CV than the check: ‘Heliangyou 631’ (9573 ± 550 kg/ha, CV = 11.48%), ‘106 you 631’ (9230 ± 642 kg/ha, CV = 13.92%), ‘Mingtaiyou 631’ (8849 ± 540 kg/ha, CV = 12.21%), ‘Huaguyou 631’ (8513 ± 305 kg/ha, CV = 7.17%), and ‘24FS8/Fuhui 631’ (8606 ± 561 kg/ha, CV = 13.03%). These results indicate that ‘Fuhui 631’ confers broad adaptability and stable yield performance. The lower yield in Sanya is likely due to shorter day length and cooler temperatures (Supplementary Table S1).

The commercial value of ‘Fuhui 631’ is evidenced by a technology transfer fee of 10 million CNY, approximately 2.3 times the average price of conventional restorer lines between 2020 and 2025. Under a public‑private partnership with Yongfu Agricultural Technology Co., Ltd., the Rice Research Institute will collaborate to develop five hybrid varieties (including two for national approval) and provide technical guidance for production and extension. This partnership underscores the readiness of ‘Fuhui 631’ for large‑scale deployment in major rice‑growing regions such as the middle and lower Yangtze River areas.

In summary, ‘Fuhui 631’ is a multiple‑resistant, high‑quality restorer line developed through precision marker‑assisted breeding. Its statistically verified superiority over common checks in key agronomic and resistance traits, combined with its successful deployment in officially released hybrids, confirms its novelty and utility. The novelty of ‘Fuhui 631’ has been further validated by the acceptance of a Plant Variety Rights application (Application No. 20231007758) by the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs, P.R. China. This line provides a valuable genetic resource for breeding climate‑resilient, high‑yielding hybrid rice and contributes to food security under increasingly unpredictable growing conditions.

Materials and methods

Heat tolerance evaluation

Heat tolerance evaluation was conducted at the Rice Research Institute, Anhui Academy of Agricultural Sciences (Hefei, China) under both field natural high-temperature conditions (July–August) and glasshouse simulated high-temperature conditions. For the field test, the location was Hefei, Anhui (31°52′N, 117°17′E). Three staggered sowing dates (April 21, May 1, May 11) were used to ensure flowering coincided with the hottest period. Temperature and humidity were recorded every 5 min using a DSR-TH digital recorder. High-temperature stress was defined as daily mean temperature ≥ 32 °C, daily maximum ≥ 38 °C for ≥ 5 consecutive days during heading and flowering. In 2024, the actual daily maximum temperature ranged from 38.1 °C to 40.7 °C, daily mean from 32.6 °C to 33.9 °C, with 19 days ≥ 38 °C, and RH 65–85%. For the glasshouse test, a controlled environment greenhouse was used. From the first flowering day, plants were treated for 5 consecutive days with daily maximum temperature 38.0–39.5 °C (target ≥ 39 °C), daily minimum ≥ 29 °C, daily mean 33.0–33.5 °C, and RH 70–80% under natural light. The relative heat tolerance coefficient R was calculated as (seed set under stress) / (seed set under normal). According to NY/T 2915 − 2016, grade 1–3 = highly tolerant, grade 4–6 = moderately tolerant, grade 7–9 = susceptible.

Disease resistance evaluation

Blast resistance was evaluated at the Yangxi natural disease nursery (Sanming, Fujian) by the Plant Protection Research Institute, Fujian Academy of Agricultural Sciences in 2023, following the national standard GB/T 15790 − 2009. Leaf blast grades (0–9) and panicle blast incidence/loss grades were determined, and a comprehensive index was calculated. Brown planthopper (BPH) resistance was evaluated using the standard seedling seedbox test (SSST) at Wuhan Hetaiqing Biotechnology Co., Ltd., with three replicates. The susceptible check TN1 and resistant check Luoyang 69 were used. Damage scores were averaged and resistance levels were assigned according to standard criteria.

Bacterial blight resistance

Bacterial blight resistance was evaluated under natural field conditions at multiple locations in Fujian Province from 2020 to 2024. Disease incidence and severity were visually assessed during the grain filling stage, and resistance was classified as resistant (R), moderately resistant (MR), or susceptible (S) based on typical leaf lesion development. Specific evaluations included: (1) 2020 mid‑season in Sanming (sown May 20, investigated late September); (2) 2022 early season in Zhangzhou (sown March 7, investigated July); (3) 2023 and 2024 late season in Zhangzhou (sown July 14, investigated November 21); (4) 2023 and 2024 mid‑season in Nanping (sown May 16, investigated September). Since ‘Fuhui 631’ is a restorer line, no official inoculation‑based identification was conducted.

Grain quality analysis

Grain quality parameters were tested by the Rice Product Quality Inspection and Testing Center, Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs (Hangzhou, China) following national standards: NY/T 2334 − 2013 for head rice rate, chalkiness degree, transparency, grain length and length/width ratio; NY/T 83-2017 for brown rice rate, milled rice rate, and alkali spreading value; GB/T 22294 − 2008 for gel consistency; NY/T 2639 − 2014 for amylose content.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using Microsoft Excel 2019 and SPSS 22.0. Yield data from four locations (Nanping, Wuhu, Wuhan, Sanya) were analyzed for each hybrid combination. Mean, standard deviation (SD), standard error (SE), and coefficient of variation (CV = SD/mean × 100%) were calculated. One-way ANOVA followed by Fisher’s LSD test (α = 0.05) was used to compare yields among combinations and across locations. Over‑standard heterosis was evaluated as over‑standard heterosis = (F₁ – check)/check × 100%, where the check is ‘Jingliangyou 534’.

The Cauchy problem for the integrable RZQ equation
John Holmes and Ryan C. Thompson

Abstract
. In this paper we study the new, integrable, fifth order Camassa-Holm (CH) type equation derived by Reyes, Zhu, and Qiao [ 43 ] , which we call the RZQ equation. The m-form of this equation possesses a striking similarity to the m-form of the CH equation. However, unlike the CH equation, the nonlocal form of this equation cannot be interpreted as a nonlocal perturbation of Burgers’ equation. We prove that the initial value problem corresponding to the RZQ equation is well-posed in the sense of Hadamard, in Sobolev spaces H s H^{s} , s > 7 / 2 s>7/2 . We further show that the data-to-solution map is not uniformly continuous. We also show that the RZQ equation is ill-posed in H s H^{s} for s < 7 / 2 s<7/2 in the non-periodic case.

2.
Proof of Well-posedness

In this section, we shall prove Theorem
1.1
. As the proofs in the non-periodic case bears great similarity to the periodic case, we shall provide the details in the periodic case and sketch modifications needed for the non-periodic case. We refer the reader to
[
16
]
for a similar proof with more details provided.

We observe that for
u\in H^{s}
the term
\lambda^{-2}[\lambda^{2}u\lambda^{2}u_{x}]\in H^{s-1}
,
s>7/2
. Replacing this term with the mollified smooth version, we obtain an ODE on the space
H^{s}

\displaystyle u_{t}+J_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{-2}[\lambda^{2}J_{\varepsilon}u\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}J_{\varepsilon}u]=-\lambda^{-4}[\lambda^{2}u_{x}\lambda^{2}u_{xx}+2\lambda^{2}u\lambda^{2}u_{x}],

(2.1)

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(x,0)=J_{\varepsilon}u_{0}(x).

(2.2)

Here, for each
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, the operator
J_{\varepsilon}u=j_{\varepsilon}*u
is the well-known Friedrichs mollifier. The function
j_{\varepsilon}
is defined by first fixing a Schwartz function
j(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
and then define the periodic functions
j_{\varepsilon}
by

j_{\varepsilon}(x)\doteq\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{j}(\varepsilon n)e^{inx}

satisfying
0\leq\widehat{j}(\xi)\leq 1
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\widehat{j}(\xi)=1
for all
\xi\in[-1,1]
.

We also define the function

F_{\varepsilon}(u)=-J_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{-2}[\lambda^{2}J_{\varepsilon}u\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}J_{\varepsilon}u]-\lambda^{-4}[\lambda^{2}u_{x}\lambda^{2}u_{xx}+2\lambda^{2}u\lambda^{2}u_{x}]

and note that

\displaystyle\partial_{u}F_{\varepsilon}(u)h=

\displaystyle-J_{\varepsilon}[\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}J_{\varepsilon}u\lambda^{2}J_{\varepsilon}h+\lambda^{2}J_{\varepsilon}u\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}J_{\varepsilon}h]

\displaystyle-\lambda^{-4}[\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}u\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}h+\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}u\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}h+2\lambda^{2}u\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}h+2\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}u\lambda^{2}h].

Therefore, for each
\varepsilon
, the mollified smooth version, equation (
2.1
) defines an ODE on
H^{s}
, and thus has a unique solution
u_{\varepsilon}
with lifespan
T_{\varepsilon}
. We shall first prove that there exists a uniform lifespan for the solutions to the ivp
2.1
-
2.2
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For each
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, there exists a
T
which depends only on the size of the initial data,
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}
, and a constant
c_{s}
, which depends only on
s>7/2
, such that for all
0<|t|<T
, the solution
u_{\varepsilon}
to the ivp
2.1
-
2.2
satisfies

\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{H^{s}}\leq\frac{\|J_{\varepsilon}u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}}{1-c_{s}\|J_{\varepsilon}u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}t}.

Proof.

Apply
\lambda^{s}
on the left side, multiply by
\lambda^{s}u_{\varepsilon}
on the right hand side, and integrate to find

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{H^{s}}=

\displaystyle-\int\lambda^{s-2}J_{\varepsilon}\left[\lambda^{2}J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\right]\lambda^{s}u_{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle-\int\lambda^{s-4}\partial_{x}\left[\frac{1}{2}(\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon})^{2}+(\lambda^{2}u_{\varepsilon})^{2}\right]\lambda^{s}u_{\varepsilon}dx.

We apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the algebra property for Sobolev spaces to the second term to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq-\int\lambda^{s-2}J_{\varepsilon}\left[\lambda^{2}J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\right]\lambda^{s}u_{\varepsilon}dx+C\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{s}}^{3}.

(2.3)

For the first term, we will use the following commutator estimate found in
[
29
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

[Kato-Ponce] If
s>0
then there is a
c_{s}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\lambda^{s}[fg]-f\lambda^{s}[g]\|_{L^{2}}\leq c_{s}\left(\|\lambda^{s}f\|_{L^{2}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\lambda^{s-1}g\|_{L^{2}}\right).

(2.4)

We commute the operator
\lambda^{s}
with
J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}
and apply Lemma
2.1
, use the Sobolev embedding theorem and the algebra property. The result is

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq-\int\lambda^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{s}\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{s}u_{\varepsilon}dx+C\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{3}.

(2.5)

For the remaining integral, we commute
\partial_{x}
with
\lambda^{s}
, and then apply integration by parts followed by Hölder’s inequality. The result is that for a constant depending only on
s>7/2
we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{s}}^{3}.

(2.6)

Solving this differential inequality gives the desired result.
∎

By taking
t\leq T=1/(2c_{s}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}})
, we find that
u_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the solution size bound

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{s}}\leq 2\|J_{\varepsilon}u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}\leq 2\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}.

(2.7)

Furthermore, we have that

\|\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{s-1}}\lesssim\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

(2.8)

In order to prove that the ivp for the RZQ equation is well-posed, we shall show that there exists a subsequence of
u_{\varepsilon}
,
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, that converges to a solution
u
of the ivp for the RZQ equation. We will then show that solutions are unique and depend continuously upon the initial data,
u_{0}
.

2.1.
Existence of Solutions

Our first step is showing that there exists a function,
u
, constructed as the limit of a subsequence of the family of solutions
u_{\varepsilon}
, as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. In order to find this subsequence, we shall refine the sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
several times labeling the subsequence
\{u_{\varepsilon_{\nu}}\}
, and after each refinement, we shall for convenience, relabel the subsequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
.

2.1.1.
Weak
∗
convergence in
L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s})

Observing that the family,
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
is bounded in
C([0,T];H^{s})\subset L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s})
, the Banach-Alaoglu theorem tells us that
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
is precompact with respect to the weak
∗
topology in
\bar{B}(0,2\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}})\subset L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s})
. Therefore there is an element
u\in\bar{B}(0,2\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}})
and a subsequence
\{u_{\varepsilon_{\nu}}\}
which converges to
u
weakly
∗
.

2.1.2.
Strong convergence in
C([0,T];H^{s-1})

We now relabel the sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon_{\nu}}\}
as
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
and show that this sequence converges strongly to
u
in the
C([0,T];H^{s-1})
topology. Indeed, we note that for
t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,T]
and the Mean Value Theorem, we have that

\|u_{\varepsilon}(t_{1})-u_{\varepsilon}(t_{2})\|_{H^{s-1}}\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{s-1}}|t_{1}-t_{2}|\lesssim|t_{1}-t_{2}|.

This establishes the fact that the family
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
is equicontinuous. Furthermore, since the torus is a compact manifold, the inclusion mapping
i:H^{s}\hookrightarrow H^{s-1}
is a compact operator, and therefore we may deduce that
U(t)=\{u_{\varepsilon}(t)\}
is a precompact set in
H^{s-1}
. Thus, by Ascoli’s theorem, there is a subsequence
\{u_{\varepsilon_{\nu}}\}
converging to an element in
H^{s-1}
. By uniqueness of the limit, this must be
u
.

2.1.3.
Strong convergence in
C([0,T];H^{s-\sigma})

We now wish to demonstrate that this sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon_{\nu}}\}
(relabeled as
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
) converges strongly to
u
in the space
C([0,T];H^{s-\sigma})
for
\sigma\in(0,1)
. To accomplish this task, we will need the following results.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
\varepsilon\in[0,1)
and
\sigma\in(0,1)
we have
u_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\sigma}([0,T];H^{s-\sigma})
. Furthermore, the
C^{\sigma}([0,T];H^{s-\sigma})
norm of
u_{\varepsilon}
is bounded by

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{\sigma}([0,T];H^{s-\sigma})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

Proof.

By definition, we have

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{\sigma}([0,T];H^{s-\sigma})}\doteq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{s-\sigma}}+\sup_{t\neq t^{\prime}}\frac{\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)-u_{\varepsilon}(t^{\prime})\|_{H^{s-\sigma}}}{|t-t^{\prime}|^{\sigma}}.

Not that the first term on the right hand side may be bounded by using the solution size estimate. The second term may be handled by noting the inequality
x^{\sigma}\leq 1+x
for
x>0
where
x=1/((1+k^{2})|t-t^{\prime}|^{2}
and we see that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\neq t^{\prime}}\frac{\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)-u_{\varepsilon}(t^{\prime})\|_{H^{s-\sigma}}^{2}}{|t-t^{\prime}|^{2\sigma}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\neq t^{\prime}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+k^{2})^{s-\sigma}\frac{|\widehat{u_{\varepsilon}}(t)-\widehat{u_{\varepsilon}}(t^{\prime})|^{2}}{|t-t^{\prime}|^{2\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\neq t^{\prime}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+k^{2})^{s}|\widehat{u_{\varepsilon}}(t)-\widehat{u_{\varepsilon}}(t^{\prime})|^{2}+\sup_{t\neq t^{\prime}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+k^{2})^{s-1}\frac{|\widehat{u_{\varepsilon}}(t)-\widehat{u_{\varepsilon}}(t^{\prime})|^{2}}{|t-t^{\prime}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{C([0,T];H^{s})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{C([0,T];H^{s-1})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{4}

By taking square roots of both sides we obtain the desired result.
∎

We again find that the sequence is equicontinuous in
H^{s-\sigma}
since

\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)-u_{\varepsilon}(t^{\prime})\|_{H^{s-\sigma}}\leq\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{\sigma}([0,T];H^{s-\sigma})}|t-t^{\prime}|^{\sigma}.

Using the fact that the inclusion mapping
i:H^{s}\hookrightarrow H^{s-\sigma}
is a compact operator, the sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
is precompact in
H^{s-\sigma}
and hence, by Ascoli’s theorem, there exists a subsequence
\{u_{\varepsilon_{\nu}}\}
that converges to an element in that space. By uniqueness of the limit, that element must be
u
.

2.1.4.
Convergence in
C^{3}

We now choose
\sigma>0
so small that

and apply the Sobolev embedding theorem to obtain

\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)-u_{\varepsilon}(t^{\prime})\|_{C^{3}}\lesssim\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)-u_{\varepsilon}(t^{\prime})\|_{H^{s-\sigma}}.

Therefore, we may also find that the sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
is also equicontinuous in

since

\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)-u_{\varepsilon}(t^{\prime})\|_{C^{3}}\lesssim\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)-u_{\varepsilon}(t^{\prime})\|_{H^{s-\sigma}}\lesssim|t-t^{\prime}|^{\sigma}.

Furthermore, we have that

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{s-\sigma}}\lesssim\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}.

Therefore, by Rellich’s lemma, for each

, the set
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
is precompact in

. That is, any subset of
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
contains a sequence

that converges to an element

. By the above estimates, we may conclude that

is precompact in

. Therefore, we find that

2.1.5.
Verifying that the limit

solves the RZQ equation

Here, we will need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

For
0<r\leq s
, the map
I-J_{\varepsilon}:H^{s}\rightarrow H^{r}
satisfies the norm operator estimate

\|I-J_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{s},H^{r})}=o(\varepsilon^{s-r}).

We will now show that the sequence
\{\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon_{\nu}}\}
(relabeled as
\{\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}\}
) converges to
\partial_{t}u
in the space
C([0,T];C^{2})
. Starting from our mollified RZQ (

\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}=-J_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{-1}[\lambda J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\lambda\partial_{x}J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}]-\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[\frac{1}{2}(\lambda\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon})^{2}+(\lambda u_{\varepsilon})^{2}\right].

(2.9)

By continuity of the operator
\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}
, we may immediately deduce the convergence of the nonlocal terms

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}(\lambda\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon})^{2}\to\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}(\lambda\partial_{x}u)^{2},

\displaystyle\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}(\lambda u_{\varepsilon})^{2}\to\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}(\lambda u)^{2}.

To handle the mollified twisted-Burgers term

) we first rewrite it as
(1/2)J_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\lambda J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})^{2}

\displaystyle\|J_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\lambda J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})^{2}-\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\lambda u)^{2}\|_{C([0,T];C^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\|J_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\lambda J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})^{2}-\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\lambda J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})^{2}\|_{C([0,T];C^{2})}

\displaystyle+\|\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\lambda J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})^{2}-\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\lambda u)^{2}\|_{C([0,T];C^{2})}

(2.10)

For the first term, choose
5/2<r<s-\sigma-1<s-1
. Then we have by Lemma

\displaystyle\|J_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\lambda J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})^{2}-\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\lambda J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})^{2}\|_{C([0,T];C^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|J_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\lambda J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})^{2}-\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\lambda J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})^{2}\|_{H^{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|I-J_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{s-1},H^{r})}\|\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\lambda J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})^{2}\|_{H^{s-1}}

\displaystyle=o\left(\varepsilon^{s-r-1}\right).

(2.11)

For the second term, we have that

\displaystyle\|\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\lambda J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})^{2}-\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\lambda u)^{2}\|_{C([0,T];C^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\lambda J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})^{2}-\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\lambda u)^{2}\|_{H^{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}+u\|_{H^{r+1}}\|J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}-u\|_{H^{r+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}-u\|_{H^{r+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{r+1}}+\|u_{\varepsilon}-u\|_{H^{r+1}}.

(2.12)

For the first term in (
2.1.5
), we use Lemma
2.3
to obtain

\|J_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{r+1}}\lesssim\|I-J_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{s},H^{r+1})}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{s}}=o\left(\varepsilon^{s-r-1}\right).

(2.13)

For the second term, we note that

\|u_{\varepsilon}-u\|_{H^{r+1}}\lesssim\|u_{\varepsilon}-u\|_{H^{s-\sigma}}\to 0

as

. Thus, we conclude that
\{\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}\}\to\partial_{t}u
in

.

2.1.6.
Improving the regularity to
C([0,T];H^{s})

Now that we know there is a solution
u\in L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s})
, we wish to improve it’s regularity and show that
u\in C([0,T];H^{s})
. Therefore, we will fix
t\in[0,T]
and consider a sequence
\{t_{n}\}\to t
. We will show that
\|u(t_{n})-u(t)\|_{H^{s}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. To do so, we establish the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.4
.

The solution
u\in L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s})\cap Lip([0,T];H^{s-1})
is continuous in
t
with respect to the weak topology on
H^{s}
. That is, for any
\varphi\in H^{s}
, and a sequence
\{t_{n}\}\to t
, we have that

\langle u(t_{n})-u(t),\varphi\rangle_{H^{s}}\to 0\ \ \ \text{as}\ \ \{t_{n}\}\to t.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. We will choose a
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
with
\|\varphi-\psi\|_{H^{s}}<\varepsilon/8\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}
. By use of triangle inequality, we have that

|\langle u(t_{n})-u(t),\varphi\rangle_{H^{s}}|\leq|\langle u(t_{n})-u(t),\varphi-\psi\rangle_{H^{s}}|+|\langle u(t_{n})-u(t),\psi\rangle_{H^{s}}|.

(2.14)

For the first term in (
2.14
), we have

|\langle u(t_{n})-u(t),\varphi-\psi\rangle_{H^{s}}|\leq(\|u(t_{n})\|_{H^{s}}+\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}})\|\varphi-\psi\|_{H^{s}}<\varepsilon/2.

(2.15)

For the second term, we may find the following estimate.

\displaystyle|\langle u(t_{n})-u(t),\psi\rangle_{H^{s}}|

\displaystyle\leq\|u(t_{n})-u(t)\|_{H^{s-1}}\|\psi\|_{H^{s+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{H^{s-1}}\|\psi\|_{H^{s+1}}|t_{n}-t|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi\|_{H^{s+1}}|t_{n}-t|.

(2.16)

By choosing
n
sufficiently large, we may bound (
2.1.6
) by
\varepsilon/2
so that we achieve

|\langle u(t_{n})-u(t),\varphi\rangle_{H^{s}}|<\varepsilon,

which complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

For the solution
u\in L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s})\cap Lip([0,T];H^{s-1})
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u(t_{n})\|_{H^{s}}=\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}.

Proof.

We begin by defining the functions

F(t)\doteq\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ F_{\varepsilon}(t)\doteq\|J_{\varepsilon}u(t)\|_{H^{s}}.

(2.17)

By Lemma
2.3
, we have that
F_{\varepsilon}\to F
pointwise as
\varepsilon\to 0
. We will prove that each
F_{\varepsilon}
is Lipschitz, and that the Lipschitz constants for this family of functions are uniformly bounded by some independent constant. This will imply that
F
is also Lipschitz continuous and its Lipschitz constant is bounded by this same independent constant which will complete our proof. Applying both sides of (
1.3
) by the operator
J_{\varepsilon}
, we obtain

\partial_{t}J_{\varepsilon}u=-J_{\varepsilon}\lambda(\lambda u\lambda u_{x})-J_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{-2}[\lambda u_{x}\lambda u_{xx}+2\lambda u\lambda u_{x}].

(2.18)

Proceeding in the same manner as section two on energy estimates, one may obtain the following

\frac{d}{dt}\|J_{\varepsilon}u(t)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

Using our lifespan estimate, we obtain

|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)|=\left|\frac{d}{dt}\|J_{\varepsilon}u(t)\|_{H^{s}}\right|\leq c_{s}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

Thus,
F(t)
is Lipschitz. This concludes our proof.
∎

Now, observe that

\|u(t_{n})-u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}=\|u(t_{n})\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}-\langle u(t_{n}),u(t)\rangle_{H^{s}}-\langle u(t),u(t_{n})\rangle_{H^{s}}.

(2.19)

Using Lemma
2.4
with
\varphi=u
, we have that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle u(t_{n}),u(t)\rangle=\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}
. Coupled with our last lemma, we find that

\|u(t_{n})-u(t)\|_{H^{s}}\to 0\ \ \text{as}\ \ n\to\infty.

Hence,
u\in C([0,T];H^{s}).

2.2.
Uniqueness of Solutions

Our second step in our proof of well-posedness is to prove that solutions are unique. We shall accomplish this by providing an energy estimate on the difference between two solutions in
H^{\sigma}
,
5/2<\sigma<s-1
. Indeed, we let
u
and
v
be two solutions to equation (
1.3
) corresponding to initial data
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)
and
v(x,0)=v_{0}(x)
respectively.
That is to say,

\begin{cases}u_{t}+\lambda^{-1}\left[\lambda u\lambda\partial_{x}u\right]=-\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\lambda\partial_{x}u\right)^{2}+\left(\lambda u\right)^{2}\right]\\

(2.20)

and

\begin{cases}v_{t}+\lambda^{-1}\left[\lambda v\lambda\partial_{x}v\right]=-\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\lambda\partial_{x}v\right)^{2}+\left(\lambda v\right)^{2}\right]\\
v(x,0)=v_{0}(x).\end{cases}

(2.21)

Subtracting
v
from
u
and setting
w=u-v
, we observe that
w
is a solution to

\begin{cases}w_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}\left[(\lambda u+\lambda v)\lambda w\right]=-\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[\frac{1}{2}(\lambda\partial_{x}u+\lambda\partial_{x}v)\lambda\partial_{x}w+(\lambda u+\lambda v)\lambda w\right]\\
w(x,0)=u_{0}(x)-v_{0}(x)\end{cases}

(2.22)

For
5/2<\sigma<s-1

fixed, we calculate the
H^{\sigma}
energy of
w
giving us the equation

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|w\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int\lambda^{\sigma}\left[\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[(\lambda^{2}u+\lambda^{2}v)\lambda^{2}w\right]\right]\lambda^{\sigma}wdx

(2.23)

\displaystyle-\int\lambda^{\sigma}\lambda^{-4}\partial_{x}\left[\frac{1}{2}(\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}u+\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}v)\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}w\right]\lambda^{\sigma}wdx

(2.24)

\displaystyle-\int\lambda^{\sigma}\lambda^{-4}\partial_{x}\left[(\lambda^{2}u+\lambda^{2}v)\lambda^{2}w\right]\lambda^{\sigma}wdx.

(2.25)

For the third integral, (
2.25
), we apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality followed by the Sobolev embedding theorem to find

\displaystyle\int\lambda^{\sigma}\lambda^{-4}\partial_{x}\left[(\lambda^{2}u+\lambda^{2}v)\lambda^{2}w\right]\lambda^{\sigma}wdx\leq

\displaystyle\ C\left\|(\lambda^{2}u+\lambda^{2}v)\lambda^{2}w\right\|_{H^{\sigma-2}}\|w\|_{H^{\sigma}}.

The algebra property followed by the solution size estimate allows us to bound this by

\displaystyle\int\lambda^{\sigma}\lambda^{-4}\partial_{x}\left[(\lambda^{2}u+\lambda^{2}v)\lambda^{2}w\right]\lambda^{\sigma}wdx\leq

\displaystyle\ C\left(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}}+\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}}\right)\|w\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}.

For the second integral, the one found in (
2.24
), we again apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to find

\displaystyle\eqref{secondterm-w}\ \dot{=}

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\int\lambda^{\sigma-4}\partial_{x}\left[(\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}u+\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}v)\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}w\right]\lambda^{\sigma}wdx

(2.26)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ C\|(\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}u+\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}v)\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}w\|_{H^{\sigma-3}}\|w\|_{H^{\sigma}}.

(2.27)

We now apply the following estimate found in Lemma 2 of
[
17
]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

If
r>1/2
, then

\|fg\|_{H^{r-1}}\leq c_{r}\|f\|_{H^{r}}\|g\|_{H^{r-1}}.

Applying the lemma with
r=\sigma-2>1/2
we obtain

\displaystyle\eqref{secondterm-w}\leq

\displaystyle\ C\|(\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}u+\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}v)\|_{H^{\sigma-2}}\|\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}w\|_{H^{\sigma-3}}\|w\|_{H^{\sigma}}.

(2.28)

Using the solution size estimate and
s>\sigma+1
, we have

\displaystyle\eqref{secondterm-w}\leq

\displaystyle\ C\left(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}}+\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}}\right)\|w\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}.

(2.29)

To bound the first term, (
2.23
), we shall use the following Calderon-Coifman-Meyer type commutator estimate, which follows from proposition 4.2 in Taylor
[
45
]

Lemma 2.7
.

If
r>3/2
and
0\leq\rho+1\leq r
, then

\|\lambda^{\rho}\partial_{x}[fv]-f\lambda^{\rho}\partial_{x}[v]\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{r}}\|v\|_{H^{\rho}}.

We let
f=\lambda^{2}u+\lambda^{2}v
, thus we have

\displaystyle\eqref{firstterm-w}\ \dot{=}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{1}{2}\int\lambda^{\sigma-2}\partial_{x}\left[f\lambda^{2}w\right]\lambda^{\sigma}wdx

(2.30)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \left|\int\left(\lambda^{\sigma-2}\partial_{x}\left[f\lambda^{2}w\right]-f\lambda^{\sigma-2}\partial_{x}\lambda^{2}w\right)\lambda^{\sigma}wdx\right|+\left|\int f\lambda^{\sigma}\partial_{x}w\lambda^{\sigma}wdx\right|.

(2.31)

For the first term in inequality (
2.31
), we apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality followed by Lemma
2.7
with
\rho=\sigma-2
and
r=s-2
to obtain

\left|\int\left(\lambda^{\sigma-2}\partial_{x}\left[f\lambda^{2}w\right]-f\lambda^{\sigma}\partial_{x}w\right)\lambda^{\sigma}wdx\right|\leq C\|f\|_{H^{s-2}}\|\lambda^{2}w\|_{H^{\sigma-2}}\|w\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim\|w\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}.

For the second term in inequality (
2.31
), we integrate by parts to find

\left|\int f\lambda^{\sigma}\partial_{x}w\lambda^{\sigma}wdx\right|\approx\left|\int\partial_{x}f(\lambda^{\sigma}w)^{2}dx\right|\lesssim\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|w\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}.

Since
s>7/2
, by the Sobolev embedding theorem and the solution size estimate, we have

\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|v_{0}\|_{H^{s}}
.

Putting our estimates for (
2.23
), (
2.24
), and (
2.25
) together, we have proved the following lemma.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
s>7/2
and
5/2<\sigma<s-1
. If
u
and
v
are two solutions to the RZQ equation on the time interval
[0,T]
corresponding to initial data
u_{0},\ v_{0}\in H^{s}
respectively, then their difference
w=u-v
satisfies the following differential inequality

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|w\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}\leq C\|w\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}\quad\text{ with }\quad\|w(0)\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}=\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}.

(2.32)

Solving this differential inequality, we obtain

\|w(t)\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}=\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}\leq e^{Ct}\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}.

Uniqueness of solutions follows immediately.

2.3.
Continuous Dependence

Here we shall show that the data-to-solution map
u_{0}\mapsto u(t)
for the ivp corresponding to the RZQ equation is continuous from
H^{s}
to
C([0,T];H^{s})
.

Fix
u_{0}\in H^{s}
and let
\{u_{0,n}\}\rightarrow u_{0}
in
H^{s}
. Let
u
be the solution to the QR equation with initial data
u_{0}
, and
u_{n}
be the solution to the QR equation corresponding to initial data
u_{0,n}
. We shall show that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}u_{n}=u\quad\text{ in }\quad C([0,T];H^{s}).

It suffices to show that for
\eta>0
, there exists an
N>0
such that for all
n>N
we have
\|u-u_{n}\|_{C([0,T];H^{s})}<\eta.
To overcome the difficulty of the twisted Burgers terms in our estimates, we will use information from the mollified problem. Let
u^{\varepsilon}
and
u_{n}^{\varepsilon}
represent the solutions to the mollified problem (
2.1
) with initial data
J_{\varepsilon}u_{0}
and
J_{\varepsilon}u_{0,n}
respectively. Then, by an application of the triangle inequality, we have

\|u-u_{n}\|_{C([0,T];H^{s})}\leq\|u-u^{\varepsilon}\|_{C([0,T];H^{s})}+\|u^{\varepsilon}-u_{n}^{\varepsilon}\|_{C([0,T];H^{s})}+\|u_{n}^{\varepsilon}-u_{n}\|_{C([0,T];H^{s})}.

(2.33)

Since we know that
u^{\varepsilon}\to u
and
u_{n}^{\varepsilon}\to u_{n}
in
C([0,T];H^{s})
, we may choose
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small to bound both the first and third terms in (
2.33
) by
\eta/3
. Therefore, it remains to find sufficient estimates on the difference
v=u^{\varepsilon}-u_{n}^{\varepsilon}
in the
C([0,T];H^{s})
norm.

We first note that this difference satisfies the following equation

\begin{cases}v_{t}+\frac{1}{2}J_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}\left[\lambda(J_{\varepsilon}w)\lambda(J_{\varepsilon}v)\right]=-\partial_{x}\lambda^{-2}\left[\frac{1}{2}(\lambda\partial_{x}w)(\lambda\partial_{x}v)+(\lambda w)(\lambda v)\right]\\
v_{0}=J_{\varepsilon}u_{0}-J_{\varepsilon}u_{0,n},\end{cases}

(2.34)

where
w=u^{\varepsilon}+u_{n}^{\varepsilon}
.

Applying the operator
\lambda^{s}
to (
2.34
), multiplying by
\lambda^{s}v
and integrating over the torus yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|v\|_{H^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\lambda^{s}\left[\frac{1}{2}J_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[\lambda(J_{\varepsilon}w)\lambda(J_{\varepsilon}v)\right]\right]\lambda^{s}vdx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\lambda^{s}\left[\partial_{x}\lambda^{-4}\left[\frac{1}{2}(\lambda\partial_{x}w)(\lambda\partial_{x}v)+(\lambda w)(\lambda v)\right]\right]\lambda^{s}vdx

\displaystyle=I+II.

(2.35)

We first handle integral
II
by applying Cauchy-Schwarz, the algebra property, and our solution size estimate to obtain

|II|\lesssim\|w\|_{H^{s}}\|v\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\lesssim\|v\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

(2.36)

Applying similar methods to the first integral along with the property
\|J_{\varepsilon}f\|_{H^{s}}\leq\|f\|_{H^{s}}
, we find that

|I|\lesssim\|J_{\varepsilon}w\|_{H^{s+1}}\|J_{\varepsilon}v\|_{H^{s+1}}\|v\|_{H^{s}}.

(2.37)

We note that
\|f\|_{H^{s+1}}=\|f\|_{H^{s}}+\|\partial_{x}f\|_{H^{s}}
so that we may improve our estimate to

|I|\lesssim\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{2}}\|v\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

(2.38)

Combining estimates (
2.36
) and (
2.38
), we achieve the differential inequality

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|v\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\lesssim\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{2}}\|v\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

(2.39)

Upon solving (
2.39
), we find obtain

\|u^{\varepsilon}-u_{n}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim e^{\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{2}}t}\|J_{\varepsilon}(u_{0}-u_{0,n})\|_{H^{s}}\leq e^{\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{2}}t}\|u_{0}-u_{0,n}\|_{H^{s}}.

(2.40)

We may then choose
n
sufficiently large so that

\|u_{0}-u_{0,n}\|_{H^{s}}\leq e^{-\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{2}}t}\frac{\eta}{3}.

(2.41)

This gives us that the second term in (
2.33
) may also be bounded by
\eta/3
which completes the proof.

Remark:
Well-posedness on the real line may be proven in a similar manner. Indeed, we may duplicate the proofs for continuous dependence and uniqueness. In the subsection for existence, however, we must change our Friedrichs mollifier by fixing
j\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
with
\widehat{j}(0)=1
. Then we may define
j_{\varepsilon}(x)=(1/\varepsilon)j(x/\varepsilon)
so that for each
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, we have
J_{\varepsilon}f=j_{\varepsilon}*f
. Furthermore, in each of our replicated estimates we apply a cut-off function
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
so that we have convergence in the respective topologies as mentioned above.

3.
Continuity of the data-to-solution map

In this section, we shall prove that the data-to-solution map is not uniformly continuous. We will provide all of the details in the periodic case, and we will sketch the modifications and estimates needed in the non-periodic case. In order to prove these results, we find that it is convenient to write the RZQ equation in the following equivalent form

\displaystyle u_{t}+uu_{x}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(u_{xx})^{2}

\displaystyle=-\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}\left[(u_{x})^{2}\right]-\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[(\lambda u)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\lambda u_{x})^{2}\right].

(3.1)

3.1.
Nonuniform dependence on
\mathbb{T}

Following the works cited in the introduction, we shall use the method of approximate solutions. We consider the following two sequences of functions corresponding to
\omega=\pm 1

\displaystyle u^{ap}_{\omega,n}(x,t)=\frac{\omega}{n}+\frac{1}{n^{s}}\cos(nx-\omega t).

(3.2)

Our first result shows that these functions are indeed approximate solutions to the RZQ equation.

Lemma 3.1
.

We denote the error

\displaystyle E\ \dot{=}

\displaystyle\ \partial_{t}u^{ap}_{\omega,n}+u^{ap}_{\omega,n}\partial_{x}u^{ap}_{\omega,n}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}^{2}u^{ap}_{\omega,n})^{2}+\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}\left[(\partial_{x}u^{ap}_{\omega,n})^{2}\right]

(3.3)

\displaystyle+\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[(\lambda u^{ap}_{\omega,n})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\lambda\partial_{x}u^{ap}_{\omega,n})^{2}\right],

(3.4)

and have that for any
5/2<\sigma<s-1
, there exists a constant
C
independent of
n
, such that

\|E\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq C\max\{n^{\sigma+3-2s},n^{2\sigma-2s}\}.

Proof.

We take the
H^{\sigma}
norm of
E
, apply the triangle inequality, and use the following lemma which bounds the derivatives of
u^{ap}_{\omega,n}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
s,\sigma>1
and for all
k,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have for a constant
C
independent of
n

\|\partial_{t}^{k}\partial_{x}^{j}u^{ap}_{\omega,n}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq Cn^{s-\sigma-j},\quad\|\cos^{2}(nx-\omega t)\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq Cn^{\sigma}.

This lemma allows us to find the following bounds.

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}^{2}u^{ap}_{\omega,n})^{2}\right\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq Cn^{\sigma+3-2s}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left\|\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}\left[(\partial_{x}u^{ap}_{\omega,n})^{2}\right]\right\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq Cn^{\sigma+1-2s}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\left\|\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[(\lambda u^{ap}_{\omega,n})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\lambda\partial_{x}u^{ap}_{\omega,n})^{2}\right]\right\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq Cn^{2\sigma-2-2s}+Cn^{2\sigma-2s}.

(3.7)

Also, we estimate the first two terms by

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{t}u^{ap}_{\omega,n}+u^{ap}_{\omega,n}\partial_{x}u^{ap}_{\omega,n}\right\|_{H^{\sigma}}=n^{1-2s}\left\|\sin(nx-\omega t)\cos(nx-\omega t)\right\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq Cn^{\sigma+1-2s}.

(3.8)

Combining the above estimates gives the desired bound for
E
.
∎

We will also consider, for each
n=1,2,\dots
, the solution to the RZQ equation with initial data

u_{0,\omega,n}(x)=u^{ap}_{\omega,n}(x,0).

Since the initial data is uniformly bounded for each
n=1,2,\dots
, we have a minimum lifespan, denoted
T>0
, for which all the solutions,
u_{\omega,n}
, exist for
0<t<T
. Furthermore, for
0<t<T
, from the solution size estimate we have
\|u_{\omega,n}(t)\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq Cn^{\sigma-s}
for
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
.

Our next result shows that the actual solutions with the above initial data are approximated by the approximate solutions. We will denote the difference and sums by

\displaystyle v=u^{ap}_{\omega,n}-u_{\omega,n},\quad\text{ and }\quad w=u^{ap}_{\omega,n}+u_{\omega,n}.

(3.9)

A straightforward calculation shows that
v
satisfies the following ivp

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v=E-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}\left(vw\right)-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}\left[\partial_{x}^{2}v\partial_{x}^{2}w\right]-\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}\left[\partial_{x}v\partial_{x}w\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ -\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[\lambda v\lambda w+\frac{1}{2}\lambda\partial_{x}v\lambda\partial_{x}w\right]

(3.10)

\displaystyle v(x,0)=0.

(3.11)

Our next task is to show that the approximate solutions approximate the exact solutions in
H^{s}
. Our strategy is to estimate the difference in
H^{\sigma}
,
\sigma<s-1
using the previous lemma, and then estimate the difference in
H^{s+1}
and then interpolate.

Lemma 3.3
.

The
H^{\sigma}
,
5/2<\sigma<s-1
, norm of
v
can be estimated by

\|v\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq C\max\{n^{\sigma+3-2s},n^{2\sigma-2s}\}.

Proof.

We calculate the
H^{\sigma}
energy of
v
and we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|v(t)\|_{H^{\sigma}}=

\displaystyle\int\lambda^{\sigma}E\lambda^{\sigma}vdx-\frac{1}{2}\int\lambda^{\sigma}\partial_{x}(vw)\lambda^{\sigma}vdx-\frac{1}{2}\int\lambda^{\sigma-2}\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}^{2}v\partial_{x}^{2}w)\lambda^{\sigma}vdx

(3.12)

\displaystyle-\int\lambda^{\sigma-2}\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}v\partial_{x}w)\lambda^{\sigma}vdx-\int\lambda^{\sigma-4}\partial_{x}(\lambda^{2}v\lambda^{2}w+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}v\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}w)\lambda^{\sigma}vdx.

The first term is handled by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and our estimate on the error

\int\lambda^{\sigma}E\lambda^{\sigma}vdx\leq\|E\|_{H^{\sigma}}\|v\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq C\max\{n^{\sigma+3-2s},n^{2\sigma-2s}\}\|v\|_{H^{\sigma}}.

The second term is written as a commutator as follows

\displaystyle\int\lambda^{\sigma}\partial_{x}(vw)\lambda^{\sigma}vdx=\int\left(\lambda^{\sigma}\partial_{x}(vw)-w\lambda^{\sigma}\partial_{x}v\right)\lambda^{\sigma}vdx+\int w\left(\lambda^{\sigma}\partial_{x}v\right)\lambda^{\sigma}vdx.

(3.13)

We apply Lemma
2.7
to obtain

\displaystyle\int\lambda^{\sigma}\partial_{x}(vw)\lambda^{\sigma}vdx\leq\|w\|_{H^{s}}\|v\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}+\int w\left(\lambda^{\sigma}\partial_{x}v\right)\lambda^{\sigma}vdx.

(3.14)

The remaining integral above is bounded using integration by parts followed by Hölder’s inequality and the Sobolev embedding theorem

\displaystyle\int\lambda^{\sigma}\partial_{x}(vw)\lambda^{\sigma}vdx\leq C\|v\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}.

(3.15)

Similarly, the remaining terms in equation (
3.12
) are bounded by
C\|v\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}
, the proofs are done in a similar way to the terms in the proof of uniqueness in the previous section, and we omit the details for the sake of brevity.
Combining these estimates we obtain the following differential inequality for
\|v\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|v\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}\leq C\left(\max\{n^{\sigma+3-2s},n^{2\sigma-2s}\}\|v\|_{H^{\sigma}}+\|v\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}\right).

(3.16)

Solving this yields

\|v(t)\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq e^{CT}\max\{n^{\sigma+3-2s},n^{2\sigma-2s}\},

which complete the proof of the lemma.
∎

We now interpolate between
s_{1}=\sigma
and
s_{2}=s+1
to obtain

\|v\|_{H^{s}}\leq\|v\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{\frac{1}{s+1-\sigma}}\|v\|_{H^{s+1}}^{\frac{s-\sigma}{s+1-\sigma}}\lesssim\left(\max\{n^{\sigma+3-2s},n^{2\sigma-2s}\right)^{\frac{1}{s+1-\sigma}}n^{\frac{s-\sigma}{s+1-\sigma}}\lesssim n^{-\epsilon},

where we used the interpolation inequality

\|f\|_{H^{s}}\leq\|f\|_{H^{s_{1}}}^{(s_{2}-s)/(s_{2}-s_{1})}\|f\|_{H^{s_{2}}}^{(s-s_{1})/(s_{2}-s_{1})},

and
where the exponent
\epsilon>0
is given by

\epsilon=\min\left\{\frac{s-\sigma}{s+1-\sigma},\frac{s-3}{s+1-\sigma}\right\}.

With this decay estimate, we are ready to prove the main theorem of this section.

We take the two sequences of solutions,
\{u_{\omega,n}\}
with uniform minimum lifespan
T>0
. We have that the initial data satisfies

\|u_{0,1,n}-u_{0,-1,n}\|_{H^{s}}\rightarrow 0,\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

At any later time, we have

\displaystyle\|u_{1,n}-u_{-1,n}\|_{H^{s}}\geq

\displaystyle\|u_{1,n}^{ap}-u_{-1,n}^{ap}\|_{H^{s}}-\|u_{1,n}^{ap}-u_{1,n}\|_{H^{s}}-\|u_{-1,n}^{ap}-u_{-1,n}\|_{H^{s}}

(3.17)

\displaystyle\geq\|n^{-1}-n^{-s}\cos(nx)\sin(t)\|_{H^{s}}-n^{-\epsilon}\gtrsim|\sin(t)|-n^{-1}-n^{-\epsilon}.

(3.18)

Applying
\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}
on both sides yields

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{1,n}-u_{-1,n}\|_{H^{s}}\gtrsim|\sin(t)|.

(3.19)

Hence, we have proved that there exists two sequences of solutions which remain bounded away from each other for all positive times, however, their initial data converge to each other; the data-to-solution map is not uniformly continuous.

3.2.
Nonuniform dependence on
\mathbb{R}

The main differences in this subsection compared to the periodic case are: the construction of the approximate solutions and the estimates of the error. We provide details of these arguments, and refer the reader to the proof in the periodic case when the details are very similar.

We shall construct low-high frequency approximate solutions by first constructing low frequency
exact
solutions. We let
u_{\omega,n,\ell}
be the solution the the RZQ equation with the low frequency initial data
u_{0,\omega,n,\ell}(x)=\omega n^{-1}\varphi(n^{-\delta}x)
where
\varphi
is a smooth bump function equal to
1
when
|x|\leq 2
. In other words,
u_{\omega,n,\ell}
is the solution to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{\omega,n,\ell}+u_{\omega,n,\ell}\partial_{x}u_{\omega,n,\ell}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\omega,n,\ell})^{2}=F(u_{\omega,n,\ell})

(3.20)

\displaystyle u_{\omega,n,\ell}(x,0)=\omega n^{-1}\varphi(n^{-\delta}x).

(3.21)

where
\displaystyle F(u_{\omega,n,\ell})=-\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}\left[(\partial_{x}u_{\omega,n,\ell})^{2}\right]-\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[(\lambda u_{\omega,n,\ell})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\lambda\partial_{x}u_{\omega,n,\ell})^{2}\right]
. We will denote
u_{\ell}=u_{\omega,n,\ell}
whenever it is clear that the estimates we are computing are independent of
\omega
and
n
.
From the well-posedness result, we have that for all
0<\delta
, the unique solution
u_{\omega,n,\ell}\in C([0,1];H^{s})
,
s>7/2
, satisfies, for all
\sigma\geq 0

\displaystyle\|u_{\omega,n,\ell}(t)\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq c_{\sigma}n^{\frac{\delta}{2}-1}.

(3.22)

We then define the high frequency sequence of functions by

u_{h}^{ap}=u_{\omega,n,h}^{ap}(x,t)=n^{-\delta/2-s}\widetilde{\varphi}\left(\frac{x}{n^{\delta}}\right)\cos(nx-\omega t),

where
\widetilde{\varphi}(x)
is a smooth bump function with support
|x|<2
and equal to
1
when
|x|<1
, so that
\widetilde{\varphi}(x)\varphi(x)=\widetilde{\varphi}(x)
.
The high-frequency approximate solutions can be estimated in
H^{\sigma}
, for any
\sigma\geq 0
using the following lemma found in
[
19
]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
,
\delta>0
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Then for any
\sigma\geq 0
we have that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{-\sigma-\delta/2}\left\|\varphi\left(n^{-\delta}x\right)\cos(nx-\alpha)\right\|_{H^{\sigma}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}.

The above relation also is true if
\cos
is replaced by
\sin
.

So we see that the above high frequency approximate solutions are bounded in
H^{s}
. We now
define the sequence of low-high frequency approximate solutions by

u_{\omega,n}^{ap}(x,t)=u_{\omega,n,\ell}+u_{\omega,n,h}^{ap}=u_{\ell}+u_{h}^{ap},

and we define the error by substituting this function into the RZQ equation.

\displaystyle E=\

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{h}^{ap}+u_{\ell}\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}+u_{h}^{ap}\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}+u_{h}^{ap}\partial_{x}u_{\ell}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{h}^{ap})^{2}+\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{h}^{ap}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\ell})

\displaystyle+\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}\left[(\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap})^{2}\right]+2\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}\left[(\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}\partial_{x}u_{\ell})\right]+\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[(\lambda u_{h}^{ap})^{2}\right]+2\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[\lambda u_{h}^{ap}\lambda u_{\ell}\right]

\displaystyle+\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[\frac{1}{2}(\lambda\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap})^{2}\right]+\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[\lambda\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}\lambda\partial_{x}u_{\ell}\right],

(3.23)

where we used
u_{\ell}
was an exact solution to the RZQ equation.
We observe that the first two terms sum to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{h}^{ap}+u_{\ell}\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}=

\displaystyle\ (u_{\ell}(x,0)-u_{\ell}(x,t))n^{-s-\delta/2+1}\widetilde{\varphi}(n^{-\delta}x)\sin(nx-\omega t)

\displaystyle+u_{\ell}(x,t)n^{-s-3\delta/2}\partial_{x}\widetilde{\varphi}(n^{-\delta}x)\cos(nx-\omega t).

(3.24)

We then decompose the error,
E
, into the following terms

\displaystyle E_{1}=(u_{\ell}(x,0)-u_{\ell}(x,t))n^{-s-\delta/2+1}\widetilde{\varphi}(n^{-\delta}x)\sin(nx-\omega t),

\displaystyle E_{2}=u_{\ell}(x,t)n^{-s-3\delta/2}\partial_{x}\widetilde{\varphi}(n^{-\delta}x)\cos(nx-\omega t),\quad\quad E_{3}=u_{h}^{ap}\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap},

\displaystyle E_{4}=u_{h}^{ap}\partial_{x}u_{\ell},\quad\quad E_{5}=\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{h}^{ap})^{2},\quad\quad E_{6}=\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{h}^{ap}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\ell}),

\displaystyle E_{7}=\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}\left[(\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap})^{2}\right],\quad\quad E_{8}=2\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}\left[(\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}\partial_{x}u_{\ell})\right],\quad\quad E_{9}=\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[(\lambda u_{h}^{ap})^{2}\right],

\displaystyle E_{10}=2\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[\lambda u_{h}^{ap}\lambda u_{\ell}\right],\quad\quad E_{11}=\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\lambda\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}\right)^{2}\right],\quad\quad E_{12}=\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[\lambda\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}\lambda\partial_{x}u_{\ell}\right],

so that

E=E_{1}+E_{2}+\dots+E_{12}.

3.2.1.
Estimating the error.

For
s>7/2
, we estimate the error in
H^{\sigma}
for some
s-2+\delta<\sigma<s-1
as follows.

Estimating
E_{1}
.
Using Lemma
3.4
we have

\displaystyle\|E_{1}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim n^{1+\sigma-s}\|u_{\ell}(x,0)-u_{\ell}(x,t)\|_{H^{\sigma}}.

(3.25)

using the fundamental theorem of calculus we obtain

\displaystyle\|E_{1}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim n^{1+\sigma-s}\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{\tau}u_{\ell}(x,\tau)\|_{H^{\sigma}}d\tau.

(3.26)

Using the RZQ equation, the algebra property for Sobolev spaces, and estimate (
3.22
), we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\tau}u_{\ell}(x,\tau)\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq

\displaystyle\ \left\|u_{\ell}\partial_{x}u_{\ell}\right\|_{H^{\sigma}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\ell})^{2}\right\|_{H^{\sigma}}+\left\|F(u_{\ell})\right\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim\left\|u_{\ell}\right\|_{H^{\sigma+1}}^{2}\lesssim n^{\delta-2}.

(3.27)

Hence we have the estimate

\displaystyle\|E_{1}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim n^{\delta+\sigma-s-1}.

(3.28)

Estimating
E_{2}
.

We have from the algebra property for Sobolev spaces

\displaystyle\|E_{2}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq n^{-s-3\delta/2}\|u_{\ell}(x,t)\|_{H^{\sigma}}\|\partial_{x}\widetilde{\varphi}(n^{-\delta}x)\cos(nx-\omega t)\|_{H^{\sigma}}.

(3.29)

Applying estimate (
3.22
) and Lemma
3.4
we have

\displaystyle\|E_{2}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim n^{-s-3\delta/2}\cdot n^{\delta/2-1}\cdot n^{\sigma+\delta/2}=n^{\delta/2+\sigma-s-1}.

(3.30)

Estimating
E_{3}
.

Since

\displaystyle\|E_{3}\|_{H^{\sigma}}=\|u_{h}^{ap}\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim\|u_{h}^{ap}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\|\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\|u_{h}^{ap}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

(3.31)

Using
\|u_{h}^{ap}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim n^{\sigma-s}
and
\|u_{h}^{ap}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim n^{-\delta/2-s}
we have

\displaystyle\|E_{3}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim n^{\sigma-s}\cdot n^{1-\delta/2-s}+n^{\sigma+1-s}\cdot n^{-\delta/2-s}=n^{\sigma+1-2s-\delta/2},\quad n\gg 1.

(3.32)

Estimating
E_{4}
.

We have

\displaystyle\|E_{4}\|_{H^{\sigma}}=\

\displaystyle\|u_{h}^{ap}\partial_{x}u_{\ell}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim\|u_{h}^{ap}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\|\partial_{x}u_{\ell}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim n^{\sigma-s}\cdot n^{\delta/2-1},

which gives

\displaystyle\|E_{4}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim\

\displaystyle n^{\sigma+\delta/2-s-1},\quad n\gg 1.

(3.33)

Estimating
E_{5}
.

We have

\displaystyle\|E_{5}\|_{H^{\sigma}}=\

\displaystyle\|\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{h}^{ap})^{2}\|_{H^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\approx\|(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{h}^{ap})^{2}\|_{H^{\sigma-1}}.

A straightforward calculation shows

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{2}u_{h}^{ap}=\

\displaystyle-n^{2-s-\delta/2}\widetilde{\varphi}(n^{-\delta}x)\cos(nx-\omega t)-2n^{1-s-3\delta/2}\widetilde{\varphi}^{\prime}(n^{-\delta}x)\sin(nx-\omega t)

\displaystyle+n^{-s-5\delta/2}\widetilde{\varphi}^{\prime\prime}(n^{-\delta}x)\cos(nx-\omega t).

Since
\sigma-1>1/2
, then
E_{5}
can be bounded by

\displaystyle\|E_{5}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim\|\partial_{x}^{2}u_{h}^{ap}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}^{2}u_{h}^{ap}\|_{H^{\sigma-1}}\lesssim n^{2-s-\delta/2}\cdot n^{\sigma+1-s}=n^{\sigma+3-2s-\delta/2}\quad n\gg 1.

Estimating
E_{6}
.

We have

\displaystyle\|E_{6}\|_{H^{\sigma}}=\

\displaystyle\|\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{h}^{ap}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\ell})\|_{H^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{x}^{2}u_{h}^{ap}\|_{H^{\sigma-1}}\|\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\ell}\|_{H^{\sigma-1}}\|\partial_{x}^{2}u_{h}^{ap}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{\sigma+1-s}\cdot n^{-2\delta-1}+n^{-3\delta/2-1}\cdot n^{2-s-\delta/2}.

From which we obtain

\displaystyle\|E_{6}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim n^{\sigma-2\delta-s}\quad n\gg 1.

Estimating
E_{7}
.

We have

\displaystyle\|E_{7}\|_{H^{\sigma}}=\

\displaystyle\|\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}\left[(\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap})^{2}\right]\|_{H^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}\|_{H^{\sigma-1}}\|\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{\sigma-s}\cdot n^{1-\delta/2-s}

\displaystyle\approx n^{\sigma+1-\delta/2-2s},\quad\text{ for }n\gg 1.

Estimating
E_{8}
.

We have

\displaystyle\|E_{8}\|_{H^{\sigma}}=\

\displaystyle\|\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}\left[\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}\partial_{x}u_{\ell}\right]\|_{H^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}\|_{H^{\sigma-1}}\|\partial_{x}u_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}u_{\ell}\|_{H^{\sigma-1}}\|\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{\sigma-s}\cdot n^{\delta/2-1}+n^{\delta/2-1}\cdot n^{1-\delta/2-s}

\displaystyle\approx n^{\sigma-1+\delta/2-s},\quad\text{ for }n\gg 1,

where we retained the smallest power of
n
.

Estimating
E_{9}
.

We have

\displaystyle\|E_{9}\|_{H^{\sigma}}=\

\displaystyle\|\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[(\lambda u_{h}^{ap})^{2}\right]\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim\|(\lambda u_{h}^{ap})^{2}\|_{H^{\sigma-3}}.

If
\sigma-3>0
, then this can be estimated as in the estimate for
E_{5}
. If
\sigma-3<0
, then we can proceed as follows

\displaystyle\|E_{9}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \|\lambda u_{h}^{ap}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\lambda u_{h}^{ap}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{-\delta/2-s+2}\cdot n^{2-s}.

From here we obtain

\displaystyle\|E_{9}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim n^{4-2s-\delta/2},\quad\text{ for }n\gg 1.

Estimating
E_{10}
.

As in the estimate for
E_{9}
, we show the details in the case
\sigma-3<0
. The other case is less delicate. We have

\displaystyle\|E_{10}\|_{H^{\sigma}}=\

\displaystyle 2\|\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[\lambda u_{h}^{ap}\lambda u_{\ell}\right]\|_{H^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\lambda u_{h}^{ap}\lambda u_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\lambda u_{h}^{ap}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\lambda u_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{-\delta/2-s+2}\cdot n^{\delta/2-1}.

From here we obtain

\displaystyle\|E_{10}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim n^{1-s},\quad n\gg 1.

Estimating
E_{11}
.

We have

\displaystyle\|E_{11}\|_{H^{\sigma}}=\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[\left(\lambda\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}\right)^{2}\right]\|_{H^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\left(\lambda\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}\right)^{2}\|_{H^{\sigma-3}}.

Using Lemma
2.6
, we with
r=\sigma-2
, we have

\displaystyle\|E_{11}\|_{H^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\lambda\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}\|_{H^{\sigma-2}}\|\lambda\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}\|_{H^{\sigma-3}}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{\sigma+1-s}\cdot n^{\sigma-s}.

From here we obtain

\displaystyle\|E_{11}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim n^{2\sigma-2s+1},\quad\text{ for }n\gg 1.

Estimating
E_{12}
.

As in the estimate for
E_{9}
, we show the details in the case
\sigma-3<0
. The other case is less delicate. We have

\displaystyle\|E_{12}\|_{H^{\sigma}}=\

\displaystyle\|\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[\lambda\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}\lambda\partial_{x}u_{\ell}\right]\|_{H^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\lambda\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}\lambda\partial_{x}u_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\lambda\partial_{x}u_{h}^{ap}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\lambda\partial_{x}u_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{-\delta/2-s+3}\cdot n^{\delta/2-1}.

From here we obtain

\displaystyle\|E_{12}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim n^{2-s-\delta/2}\quad\text{ for }n\gg 1.

Combining the above twelve estimates, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
s>7/2
,
1/3<\delta<1
and
2-\delta<\sigma<s-1
. Then we have the following estimate on the error

\displaystyle\|E\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim n^{-\alpha},\quad\text{ for }n\gg 1,

(3.34)

where

\displaystyle\alpha=s+1-\sigma-\delta>0.

(3.35)

3.2.2.
Estimating the distance between the actual and approximate solutions

We consider two sequences of solutions to the QR equation by solving the initial value problem
1.3
with initial data

u_{\omega,n}(x,0)=u^{ap}_{\omega,n}(x,0)=\omega n^{-1}\varphi(n^{-\delta}x)+n^{-\delta/2-s}\widetilde{\varphi}\left(\frac{x}{n^{\delta}}\right)\cos(nx).

Since the initial data is bounded in
H^{s}
, by the well-posedness result, the collection of solutions exists on a time interval independent of
n
and
\omega
. Additionally, it is easy to check that the difference
u_{1,n}(x,0)-u_{-1,n}(x,0)
goes to zero in
H^{s}
, and the difference
u^{ap}_{1,n}(x,t)-u^{ap}_{-1,n}(x,t)
remains bounded away from each other in
H^{s}
for
t>0
.

To prove that the actual solutions remain bounded away from each other for any positive time, we will show that the difference between the actual and approximate solutions decay as
n\rightarrow\infty
. For this we let

\displaystyle v=u^{ap}_{\omega,n}-u_{\omega,n},\quad\text{ and }\quad w=u^{ap}_{\omega,n}+u_{\omega,n}.

(3.36)

A straightforward calculation shows that
v
satisfies the following ivp

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v=E-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}\left(vw\right)-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}\left[\partial_{x}^{2}v\partial_{x}^{2}w\right]-\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x}\left[\partial_{x}v\partial_{x}w\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ -\lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}\left[\lambda v\lambda w+\frac{1}{2}\lambda\partial_{x}v\lambda\partial_{x}w\right]

(3.37)

\displaystyle v(x,0)=0.

(3.38)

The following lemma can be proved in a methodology similar to Lemma
3.3
in the periodic case.

Lemma 3.6
.

The
H^{\sigma}
,
\max\{5/2,2-\delta\}<\sigma<s-1
, norm of
v
can be estimated by

\|v\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq Cn^{-\alpha}.

where
\alpha=s+1-\sigma-\delta>0
and
1/2<\delta<1
(see (
3.35
)).

3.2.3.
Completing the proof on
\mathbb{R}

Let
s>7/2
be fixed. We consider
u_{1,n}(x,t)
and
u_{-1,n}(x,t)
, the unique solutions to the ivp for the RZQ equation with initial data
u^{ap}_{1,n}(x,0)
and
u^{ap}_{-1,n}(x,0)
respectively. From the well-posedness argument, these solutions belong to
C([0,T];H^{s})
for some
T>0
independent of
n\gg 1
and
1/3<\delta<1
.
Additionally, applying the well-posedness theorem in
H^{k}
for
k=\lfloor s+2\rfloor
, we have

\|u_{\omega,n}(t)\|_{H^{k}}\lesssim\|u_{\omega,n}^{ap}(0)\|_{H^{k}},\quad 0\leq t\leq T,\quad n\gg 1.

A simple calculation shows

\|u_{\omega,n}^{ap}(0)\|_{H^{k}}\lesssim n^{k-s}.

Therefore, we obtain the following estimate for the difference of the actual and approximate solutions in
H^{k}
for
0\leq t\leq T

\displaystyle\|u_{\omega,n}(t)-u_{\omega,n}^{ap}(t)\|_{H^{k}}\lesssim n^{k-s},\quad n\gg 1.

(3.39)

Applying the interpolation inequality

\|f\|_{H^{s}}\leq\|f\|_{H^{s_{1}}}^{(s_{2}-s)/(s_{2}-s_{1})}\|f\|_{H^{s_{2}}}^{(s-s_{1})/(s_{2}-s_{1})},

with
s_{2}=k
and
s_{1}=\sigma
we obtain

\displaystyle\|u_{\omega,n}(t)-u_{\omega,n}^{ap}(t)\|_{H^{s}}\leq

\displaystyle\ \|u_{\omega,n}(t)-u_{\omega,n}^{ap}(t)\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{(k-s)/(k-\sigma)}\|u_{\omega,n}(t)-u_{\omega,n}^{ap}(t)\|_{H^{k}}^{(s-\sigma)/(k-\sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{-\alpha(k-s)/(k-\sigma)}n^{(k-s)(s-\sigma)/(k-\sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{-(\sigma-s+\alpha)(k-s)/(k-\sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{-(1-\delta)(k-s)/(k-\sigma)},\quad n\gg 1.

(3.40)

It is easy to see the above exponent is negative, and the difference between the actual and approximate solutions tends to zero as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
From here, a calculation which mirrors the periodic case shows that the approximate solutions remain bounded away from each for all positive time, and in particular

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{1,n}-u_{-1,n}\|_{H^{s}}\gtrsim|\sin(t)|.

(3.41)

This complete the proof in the nonperiodic case.

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

We first note that the pseudo-peakon solutions of the RZQ equation found in
[
42
]
are of the form

u=\frac{c}{2}e^{-|\xi|}(1+|\xi|),\ \ \ \xi=x-ct.

We seek to show that these solutions live in the Sobolev space
H^{s}
for
s<7/2
. Let’s consider the pseudo-peakon centered at
t=0
and with
c=1
. By taking the Fourier transform, we find that

\widehat{u}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ix\xi}u(x)dx=\frac{1}{1+\xi^{2}}+\int_{0}^{\infty}xe^{-x}\cos(x\xi)dx,

where the last integral was obtained by symmetry of our function and the fact that
e^{-ix\xi}=\cos(x\xi)-i\sin(x\xi)
. To compute this remaining integral, consider

F(\xi)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x}\sin(x\xi)dx=\frac{\xi}{1+\xi^{2}},

which is obtained via integration by parts. Then we have that

F^{\prime}(\xi)=\frac{d}{d\xi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x}\sin(x\xi)dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}xe^{-x}\cos(x\xi)dx=\frac{1-\xi^{2}}{(1+\xi^{2})^{2}}.

Thus, we find that

\widehat{u}=\frac{2}{(1+\xi^{2})^{2}}.

Now we consider the Sobolev norm of our pseudo-peakons. We see that

\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}=4\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+\xi^{2})^{s-4}d\xi,

which is bounded for
s<7/2
. Thus,
u\in H^{s}
for
s<7/2
.

From
[
43
]
and again in
[
42
]
, we note that the multi-pseudo-peakon solutions are of the form

u=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{p_{j}(t)}{2}e^{-|x-q_{j}(t)|}(1+|x-q_{j}(t)|),

where
p_{j}(t),q_{j}(t)
satisfy the following canonical Hamiltonian dynamical system

\dot{p_{j}}=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial q_{j}},\ \ \dot{q_{j}}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial p_{j}},

with the Hamiltonian function

H=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}p_{i}p_{j}e^{-|q_{i}-q_{j}|}.

Since this coincides exactly with the finite-dimensional peakon dynamical system of the CH equation, we may apply the same methods from
[
15
]
to find that the RZQ equation is ill-posed in
H^{s}
for
s<7/2
.

Abstract




Abstract

This paper focuses on the existence and multiplicity of normalized solutions for the following coupled Schrödinger system with Sobolev critical coupling term:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u+\lambda_{1}u=\omega_{1}|u|^{p-2}u+\frac{\alpha\nu}{2^{*}}|u|^{\alpha-2}|v|^{\beta}u,\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
&-\Delta v+\lambda_{2}v=\omega_{2}|v|^{p-2}v+\frac{\beta\nu}{2^{*}}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta-2}v,\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}~\mathrm{d}x=a^{2},\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{2}~\mathrm{d}x=b^{2},\end{aligned}\right.

where
N\geq 3
,
a,b>0
,
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\nu\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
, and the exponents
p,\alpha,\beta
satisfy

\alpha>1,~\beta>1,~~\alpha+\beta=2^{*},~~2<p\leq 2^{*}={2N}/\left(N-2\right).

(0.1)

The parameters
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
will arise as Lagrange multipliers that are not prior given. This paper mainly presents several existence and multiplicity results under explicit conditions on
a,b
for the focusing case
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}>0
and attractive case
\nu>0
:

(1) When
2<p<2+4/N
, we prove that there exist two solutions: one is a local minimizer, which serves as a normalized ground state, and the other is of mountain-pass type, which is a normalized excited state.

(2) When
2+4/N\leq p<2^{*}
, we prove that there exists a mountain-pass type solution, which serves as a normalized ground state.

(3) When
p=2^{*}
, the existence and classification of normalized ground states are provided for and
N\geq 5
, alongside a non-existence result for
N=3,4
. These results reflect the properties of the Aubin-Talenti bubble, which attains the best Sobolev embedding constant.

Furthermore, we present a non-existence result for the defocusing case
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}<0
.
This paper, together with the paper [T. Bartsch, H. W. Li and W. M. Zou.

Calc. Var. Partial Differential Equations
62 (2023) ], provides a more comprehensive understanding of normalized solutions for Sobolev critical systems. We believe our methods can also address the open problem of the multiplicity of normalized solutions for Schrödinger systems with Sobolev critical growth, with potential for future development and broader applicability.

Key words:
Sobolev critical system; Normalized solution; Existence and multiplicity.

Introduction and statement of results

The following Schrödinger system describes the dynamics of coupled nonlinear waves

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Phi_{1}=\Delta\Phi_{1}+\omega_{1}|\Phi_{1}|^{p-2}\Phi_{1}+\frac{\alpha\nu}{\alpha+\beta}|\Phi_{1}|^{\alpha-2}|\Phi_{2}|^{\beta}\Phi_{1},\\
&-i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Phi_{2}=\Delta\Phi_{2}+\omega_{2}|\Phi_{2}|^{p-2}\Phi_{2}+\frac{\beta\nu}{\alpha+\beta}|\Phi_{1}|^{\alpha}|\Phi_{2}|^{\beta-2}\Phi_{2},\\
&\Phi_{j}=\Phi_{j}(x,t)\in{\mathbb{C}},\quad(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\times{\mathbb{R}},~j=1,2,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
i
is the imaginary unit, and the parameters satisfy

N\geq 3,~~\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\nu\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\left\{0\right\}~\text{ and }~~\alpha,\beta>1,~~2<p,\alpha+\beta\leq 2^{*}:={2N}/\left(N-2\right).

(1.2)

System (
1.1
) arises in various physical models and has been extensively studied in recent years.
For instance, it appears in Hartree-Fock theory for a double condensate, specifically describing a binary mixture of Bose-Einstein condensates in two different hyperfine states. In particular, when
p=4,\alpha=\beta=2
, system (
1.1
) reduces to the well-known
Gross-Pitaevskii system
, which is widely applied in nonlinear optics. Moreover, certain models for ultracold quantum gases involve different exponents; see
[
3
,
29
,
23
,
2
,
45
]
.
For more background on system (
1.1
), we refer to
[
1
,
19
,
20
]
and the references therein.

From a physical perspective, the solutions
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
represent condensate amplitudes corresponding to different condensates, while the parameters
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
correspond to self-interactions within each component. These self-interactions are called
focusing
when the sign is positive, and
defocusing
when negative. The coupling constants
\alpha\nu/(\alpha+\beta)
and
\beta\nu/(\alpha+\beta)
define the strength and type of interaction between components
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
. The sign of
\nu
whether the interaction between the two states is attractive or repulsive: it is
attractive
when
\nu>0
, and
repulsive
when
\nu<0
, indicating strong competition between the two states.

An important feature of system (
1.1
) is that any solution satisfies the conservation of mass, which plays a crucial role in the dynamics and stability of the system. More precisely, the following
L^{2}
-norms

\left\|\Phi_{1}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})}^{2}~\text{ and }~\left\|\Phi_{2}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})}^{2}

(1.3)

are independent of time
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. These norms have important physical significance: in Bose–Einstein condensates, the
L^{2}
-norms represent the number of particles of each component; in nonlinear optics framework, they correspond to the power supply; and in Hartree-Fock theory,
they represent the mass of condensate.

Among all the solutions, the study of
solitary waves
is particularly important for system (
1.1
), as the ground state plays a crucial role in the dynamics of the solutions; for example, the determination of the threshold of the scattering
solutions, see
[
41
,
42
,
21
,
22
,
33
,
34
]
, and the universal profile of collapsing solutions for the critical nonlinear Schrödinger equations, see
[
32
,
47
,
50
]
and the references therein. The ansatz
\Phi_{1}(x,t)=e^{i\lambda_{1}t}u(x)
and
\Phi_{2}(x,t)=e^{i\lambda_{2}t}v(x)
for solitary wave solutions of system (
1.1
) leads to the following steady-state coupled nonlinear Schrödinger system

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u+\lambda_{1}u=\omega_{1}|u|^{p-2}u+\frac{\alpha\nu}{\alpha+\beta}|u|^{\alpha-2}|v|^{\beta}u,\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
&-\Delta v+\lambda_{2}v=\omega_{2}|v|^{p-2}v+\frac{\beta\nu}{\alpha+\beta}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta-2}v,\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
&\ \ u(x),v(x)\to 0~~\text{as }|x|\to\infty,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.4)

For the study of system (
1.4
), there are two distinct options concerning the frequency
parameter
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
, leading to two different research directions. One direction is to fix the parameters
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
. The existence and multiplicity of solutions to (
1.4
) have been extensively investigated over the past two decades. Numerous relevant studies can be found in the literature; we refer to
[

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

]
and the references therein.

In this paper, we focus on a different direction, specifically investigating the solutions to system (
1.4
) that have a prescribed
L^{2}
-norm, which are commonly referred to as
normalized solutions
. In this direction, the parameters
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
cannot be prescribed but appear as multipliers with respect to the constraint
L^{2}
-torus

{\cal T}(a,b):=\Big\{(u,v)\in H:\|u\|_{2}^{2}=a^{2},~\|v\|_{2}^{2}=b^{2}\Big\}.

(1.5)

where
H:=H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\times H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
and
\|u\|_{p}
to denote the
L^{p}
-norm of
u
.

From now on, we focus on the following normalized problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u+\lambda_{1}u=\omega_{1}|u|^{p-2}u+\frac{\alpha\nu}{\alpha+\beta}|u|^{\alpha-2}|v|^{\beta}u,\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
&-\Delta v+\lambda_{2}v=\omega_{2}|v|^{p-2}v+\frac{\beta\nu}{\alpha+\beta}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta-2}v,\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}~\mathrm{d}x=a^{2},\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{2}~\mathrm{d}x=b^{2},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.6)

From a variational point of view, normalized solutions to problem (
1.6
) can be obtained as critical points of the energy functional

I(u,v):H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\times H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
,

I(u,v):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}\right)~\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{p}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\left(|u|^{p}+|v|^{p}\right)~\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\nu}{\alpha+\beta}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}~\mathrm{d}x

(1.7)

on the constraint
{\cal T}(a,b)

with parameters
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
appearing as Lagrange multipliers. Since
\alpha,\beta>1
, it is standard to conclude that
I(u,v)
is of class
C^{1}
. Mathematicians and physicists typically focus on the solution with the least energy, as this often corresponds to good” behavior in physical terms. In this paper, we are particularly interested in the
normalized ground state
that minimizes energy. Moreover, the existence of a
normalized excited state
is also significant in physics.

Definition 1.1
.

A solution
(u_{0},v_{0})
is called a
normalized ground state
to system (
1.6
) if it satisfies

I|_{{\cal T}(a,b)}^{\prime}(u_{0},v_{0})=0,\quad\text{ and }\quad I(u_{0},v_{0})=\inf\{I(u,v):~I|_{{\cal T}(a,b)}^{\prime}(u,v)=0,\ \ (u,v)\in{\cal T}(a,b)\}.

(1.8)

A solution
(u_{0},v_{0})
is called a
normalized excited state
to system (
1.6
) if it satisfies

I|_{{\cal T}(a,b)}^{\prime}(u_{0},v_{0})=0,\quad\text{ and }\quad I(u_{0},v_{0})>\inf\{I(u,v):~I|_{{\cal T}(a,b)}^{\prime}(u,v)=0,\ \ (u,v)\in{\cal T}(a,b)\}.

(1.9)

The normalized ground state energy
m_{g}(a,b)
can be defined by

m_{g}(a,b):=\inf_{(u,v)\in{\cal T}(a,b)}I(u,v).

Moreover, the existence of normalized ground state solution depends on whether
m_{g}(a,b)
can be attained.
From a mathematical perspective, new difficulties arise in the search for normalized solutions, making this problem particularly challenging. For instance, the
appearance of the constraint
{\cal T}(a,b)
makes lots of classical variation methods can not be applicable directly since
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}
are not given prior, and the existence of bounded
Palais–Smale sequences requires new arguments (the classical method used to establish the boundedness of any Palais–Smale sequence for unconstrained Sobolev-subcritical problems fails to apply in this case). Moreover, in the mass supercritical case where
p>2+\frac{4}{N}
or
\alpha+\beta>2+\frac{4}{N}
, there are bounded Palais-Smale sequences that do not have a convergent
subsequence and converge weakly to
0
, since the
L^{2}
-torus
{\cal T}(a,b)
is not a weak compact submanifold in
H
(even in the radial case).

To present our results regarding the Sobolev critical system (

In variational approach, normalized solutions of (
1.10
) are obtained as critical points

E(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}~\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}~\mathrm{d}x,\quad\text{for}~u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}}),

on the constraint
S(a)=\left\{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}}):\|u\|_{2}^{2}=a^{2}\right\}
. Moreover, the least energy is defined by

e(a):=\inf_{u\in S(a)}E(u)=\inf\Big\{E(u):u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}}),~\|u\|_{2}^{2}=a^{2}~\text{and}~\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}=\gamma_{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\Big\},

(1.13)

where the parameter
\gamma_{p}
is denoted as

\gamma_{p}:=\frac{N(p-2)}{2p}.

(1.14)

We then have the following results, which are established in
[

]
.

Theorem A
.

Let
N\geq 3
,
p\in\left(2,2^{*}\right)\setminus\left\{2+\frac{4}{N}\right\}
.
Then up to a translation, scalar equation (
1.10
) has a unique positive normalized solution

with Lagrange multiplier
\lambda>0
, and

(1)

if

e(a)=\inf_{\|u\|_{2}^{2}=a^{2}}E(u)=E(u_{p})<0;

(1.15)

(2)

if

, then

e(a)=\inf_{\|u\|_{2}^{2}=a^{2}}\max_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}E(e^{\frac{N}{2}t}u(e^{t}\cdot))=\max_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}E(e^{\frac{N}{2}t}u_{p}(e^{t}\cdot))=E(u_{p})>0;

(1.16)

Moreover, in both cases, the least energy
e(a)
is strictly decreasing with respect to

From a variational point of view, besides the Sobolev critical exponent

1.1
L^{2}
-subcritical
2<p<2+\frac{4}{N}

Now we consider the case
\nu>0
of attractive interaction.
It is straightforward to verify that the functional
I(u,v)
becomes unbounded from below on
{\cal T}(a,b)
when
\nu>0
. To establish the existence of normalized solutions to (
1.28
) in such cases, we draw inspiration from

P(u,v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}\right)~\mathrm{d}x-\gamma_{p}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\left(|u|^{p}+|v|^{p}\right)~\mathrm{d}x-\nu\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}~\mathrm{d}x=0.

(1.17)

Setting the Pohozaev manifold

{\cal P}(a,b):=\Big\{(u,v)\in{\cal T}(a,b):P(u,v)=0\Big\}.

(1.18)

Then
{\cal P}(a,b)\neq\emptyset
. The Pohozaev identity implies that any solution of (
1.6
) belongs to
{\cal P}(a,b)
, so that if
(u,v)
is a minimizer of the following minimization problem

m(a,b):=\inf_{(u,v)\in{\cal P}(a,b)}I(u,v).

(1.19)

then
(u,v)
is a normalized ground state solution of (
1.6
), that is
m(a,b)=m_{g}(a,b)
. By introducing a
L^{2}
-norm invariant transformation and a suitable fiber map (detailed in Section
1.4
), we decompose the Pohozaev manifold into three submanifolds:

{\cal P}(a,b)={\cal P}^{+}(a,b)\cup{\cal P}^{0}(a,b)\cup{\cal P}^{-}(a,b).

If
2<p<2+4/N
and
\nu>0
, the fiber map exhibits both convex and concave geometries under certain conditions, such as when the total mass
a^{2}+b^{2}
is small. Such specific geometric structure plays a crucial role in searching for the multiplicity of normalized solutions.

We now state that the multiplicity results of normalized solutions to system (
1.28
) for the
L^{2}
-subcritical case. These solutions can be characterized as the local minimizer (normalized ground state) and the mountain pass solution (normalized excited state) of functional
I
on the constraint
{\cal P}(a,b)
respectively. More precisely, we have the following results.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
N\geq 3
,
\nu>0
and the exponents satisfy
\alpha>1,\beta>1,\alpha+\beta=2^{*}
and

2<p<\begin{cases}2+\frac{4}{N},&\quad\text{when}~N=3,4,\\
2+\frac{2}{N-2},&\quad\text{when}~N\geq 5.\end{cases}

(1.20)

There exists a constant
C_{0}>0
, such that if
\nu
satisfies

0<\nu^{\frac{2-p\gamma_{p}}{2^{*}-2}}<C_{0}\left(a^{p(1-\gamma_{p})}+b^{p(1-\gamma_{p})}\right)^{-1},

(1.21)

then we have

(1)

I|_{{\cal T}(a,b)}
has a critical point of local minimum type
(u_{+},v_{+})
at negative level

m(a,b)=\inf_{{\cal P}^{+}(a,b)}I(u,v)<0,

which is also a normalized
ground state of system (
1.6
).

(2)

I|_{{\cal T}(a,b)}
has a second critical point of mountain pass type
(u_{-},v_{-})
at positive level

l(a,b)=\inf_{{\cal P}^{-}(a,b)}I(u,v)>0,

which is also a normalized
excited state of system (
1.6
)

(3)

Both components of
(u_{+},v_{+})
and
(u_{-},v_{-})
are positive functions, are radially symmetric, and solve system (
1.6
) for
suitable
\lambda_{1,+},\lambda_{2,+}>0
and
\lambda_{1,-},\lambda_{2,-}>0
.

Remark 1.1
.

Actually, we can give the explicit expression of

,

C_{0}=\min\left\{\frac{1}{\gamma_{p}},~\frac{p(2^{*}+2-p\gamma_{p})}{22^{*}}\right\}\frac{(2^{*}-2)(2-p\gamma_{p})^{\frac{2-p\gamma_{p}}{2^{*}-2}}{\cal S}_{\alpha,\beta}^{\frac{2^{*}(2-p\gamma_{p})}{2(2^{*}-2)}}}{{\cal C}(N,p)(2^{*}-p\gamma_{p})^{\frac{2^{*}-p\gamma_{p}}{2^{*}-2}}},

To our best knowledge, Theorem
1.2
appears to be the first multiplicity result of normalized solutions to Sobolev critical system (
1.6
). In this part, we outline the ideas for establishing the key multiplicity of solutions.

On the one hand, in order to obtain the local minimum type solution
(u_{+},v_{+})
of the functional
I(u,v)
, we first compare the ground state
m(a,b)
with the local minimum energy (see Lemma
2.2
), namely:

m(a,b)=\inf_{A_{R_{0}}(a,b)}I(u,v).

(1.22)

We then establish an upper estimate of the normalized ground state level
m(a,b)<\min\left\{e(a),e(b)\right\}<0
, which allows us to recover the compactness of Palais-Smale sequence at level
m(a,b)
and obtain a local minimum type normalized ground state.

On the other hand, since the functional
I(u,v)
is unbounded from below on
{\cal T}(a,b)
, the structure of the functional suggests that there may exist another normalized solution at the mountain pass level.
To obtain the second solution, we need to construct a mountain pass level
\sigma_{1}
(see (
2.18
)), and find a Palais-Smale sequence at such level by applying the Ghoussoub min-max principle described in
[
24
, Section 5]
. Then using the Pohozaev identity, we can show that such Palais-Smale sequence is bounded in
H
. However, the weak limit of Palais-Smale sequence in
H
may potentially possess vanishing components. To overcome this difficulty, a precise threshold for the mountain pass level is required to ensure that the weak limit for the Palais-Smale sequence has two nontrivial components. More precisely, the mountain pass level
\sigma_{1}
must be smaller than
m(a,b)+\frac{\nu^{-(N-2)/2}}{N}{\cal S}_{\alpha,\beta}^{N/2}
, where
{\cal S}_{\alpha,\beta}
is defined by (
1.41
). In this paper, the energy estimate can be established by observing the following result (see Lemma
2.3
),

\sigma_{1}=\inf_{{\cal P}^{+}(a,b)}I(u,v):=l(a,b).

(1.23)

The proof of energy estimate (
2.23
) depends on the choice of suitable test function; see Lemma
2.8
and
2.9
. Our approach is inspired by the pioneering work
[
40
,
57
]
, which focuses on the multiplicity of normalized solutions to the following scalar equation with combined nonlinearity

\displaystyle-\Delta u+\lambda u=\mu|u|^{q-2}u+|u|^{2^{*}-2}u\quad\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\quad\|u\|_{2}^{2}=a^{2}.

(1.24)

However, due to the presence of coupling terms, the geometry of the Pohozaev manifold for system (
1.6
) is more complicated than that for (
1.10
), which means that the methods used in
[
40
, Propositions 1.15 and 1.16]
and
[
57
, Lemma 3.1]
cannot be applied directly here. Thus, we require more careful calculations in this paper.

-subcritical case

1.3
Sobolev critical

Our previous results primarily address the case
2<p<2^{*}
and
\alpha+\beta=2^{*}
. We recall that the existence of normalized ground state solution to (

) with the case

and

has already been investigated in
[

]
. Moreover, the case where

and

has been thoroughly studied in
[

]
. A natural question is what happens in the fully Sobolev critical case
p=\alpha+\beta=2^{*}
. Does a normalized solution still exist in this particular case? We will provide an answer to this problem in this subsection.

Before proceeding, we introduce some results concerning the following coupled Sobolev critical system

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u=|u|^{2^{*}-2}u+\frac{\alpha\nu}{2^{*}}|u|^{\alpha-2}|v|^{\beta}u,\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
&-\Delta v=|v|^{2^{*}-2}v+\frac{\beta\nu}{2^{*}}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta-2}v,\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
&u,v\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{N}}),\quad N\geq 3.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.28)

Such system has been widely studied in these years, we refer to
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
26
,
31
]
and references therein.
Define the Sobolev space
\mathcal{D}:=\mathcal{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\times\mathcal{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
and the Nehari manifold

\mathcal{N}:=\left\{(u,v)\in\mathcal{D}\setminus\left\{(0,0)\right\}:\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}=\left\|u\right\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}+\left\|v\right\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}+\nu\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}~\mathrm{d}x\right\}.

(1.29)

It is easy to see that any nontrivial solution of (
1.28
) belongs to the Nehari manifold
\mathcal{N}
.
A solution
(u_{0},v_{0})
is called
ground state solution
if it has least energy among all nontrivial solutions, that is,

I(u_{0},v_{0})=\inf_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}}I(u,v)=:\mathcal{C}~.

(1.30)

Moreover, in a standard way, there holds

\mathcal{C}=\inf_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{D}\setminus\left\{(0,0)\right\}}\frac{1}{N}\frac{\left(\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{2^{*}}{2^{*}-2}}}{\left(\left\|u\right\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}+\left\|v\right\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}+\nu\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}-2}}}.

(1.31)

The existence and classification results for ground state solution were shown in
[
31
]
.
Following the notations in
[
31
]
, we define the polynomial
F:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

F(x_{1},x_{2})=|x_{1}|^{2^{*}}+|x_{2}|^{2^{*}}+\nu|x_{1}|^{\alpha}|x_{2}|^{\beta},

(1.32)

and denote by
\mathcal{X}
the set of solutions to the maximization problem

F(\tilde{x}_{1},\tilde{x}_{2})=F_{max}:=\max_{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}=1}F(x_{1},x_{2}),~\text{ with }~\tilde{x}_{1}^{2}+\tilde{x}_{2}^{2}=1.

(1.33)

Then it follows from
[
31
]
that the system (
1.28
) has a ground state solution of the form

\left(\widetilde{U}_{\varepsilon,y},\widetilde{V}_{\varepsilon,y}\right):=\left(\tilde{x}_{1}F_{max}^{-\frac{N-2}{4}}U_{\varepsilon,y},\tilde{x}_{2}F_{max}^{-\frac{N-2}{4}}U_{\varepsilon,y}\right),\quad\text{ where}~y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N},~(\tilde{x}_{1},\tilde{x}_{2})\in\mathcal{X},~\varepsilon>0,

(1.34)

and
U_{\varepsilon,y}
is the Aubin-Talenti bubble, see (
1.39
) ahead. Therefore it follows from (
1.31
) that

\mathcal{C}=I(\widetilde{U}_{\varepsilon,y},\widetilde{V}_{\varepsilon,y})=\frac{1}{N}F_{max}^{-\frac{N-2}{2}}\mathcal{S}^{\frac{N}{2}}.

(1.35)

Our first existence results for
N\geq 5
are as follows.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
N\geq 5
,
\nu>0
and the exponents satisfy
\alpha>1,\beta>1,\alpha+\beta=p=2^{*}
. We further assume that
\tilde{x}_{1}\neq 0,~\tilde{x}_{2}\neq 0

satisfy that

(1.36)

Moreover, system (
1.6
) has a normalized ground state solution, given by

defined in (

) for

and the unique choice of

such that
\|\widetilde{U}_{\varepsilon_{0},y}\|_{2}=a
and

1.6

Remark 1.4
.

The assumptions

guarantee that the system (

has a positive solution

Sobolev inequality.
Recall the best Sobolev embedding constant

{\cal S}:=\inf_{u\in{\cal D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}}{\|u\|_{2^{*}}^{2}},

(1.38)

where
{\cal D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
is the completion of
{\cal C}_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
with respect to
the norm
\|u\|:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}~\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

It is well known that
{\cal S}
is achieved by

if only if

u\in\left\{U_{\varepsilon,y}(x):~U_{\varepsilon,y}(x)=\big(\frac{\sqrt{N(N-2)}\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^{2}+|x-y|^{2}}\big)^{\frac{N-2}{2}},\quad\varepsilon>0,~y\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\right\}.

(1.39)

Moreover,

\|\nabla U_{\varepsilon,y}\|_{2}^{2}=\|U_{\varepsilon,y}\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}={\cal S}^{\frac{N}{2}}.

(1.40)

To simplify the notations, we denote
U_{\varepsilon}(x):=U_{\varepsilon,0}(x)
.
Define

{\cal S}_{\alpha,\beta}:=\inf_{u,v\in{\cal D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}\right)~\mathrm{d}x}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}~\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2/2^{*}}},

(1.41)

then from
[
4
]
we know that

{\cal S}_{\alpha,\beta}=\left(\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\right)^{\beta/2^{*}}+\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)^{\alpha/2^{*}}\right){\cal S},

where
{\cal S}
is defined by (
1.38
).

Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality.
Recall the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

\|u\|_{p}\leq{\cal C}(N,p)\|u\|_{2}^{1-\gamma_{p}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\gamma_{p}},\quad\forall u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}}),

(1.42)

where
\gamma_{p}
is defined by (
1.14
), and
{\cal C}(N,p)
is the sharp constant satisfying

{\cal C}(N,p)^{-1}=\inf_{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\cfrac{\|u\|_{2}^{1-\gamma_{p}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\gamma_{p}}}{\|u\|_{p}}=(p\gamma_{p})^{\frac{\gamma_{p}}{2}}(1-p\gamma_{p})^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\gamma_{p}}{2}}\|Z\|_{2}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}.

(1.43)

Here
Z
is the unique solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta Z+Z=|Z|^{p-2}Z\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
&Z>0~\text{ and }~Z(x)\to 0\quad\text{ as }|x|\to\infty,\\
&Z(0)=\max_{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}Z(x).\end{aligned}\right.

(1.44)

For more details, we refer to
[
58
]
. Moreover,the function
Z^{\kappa,\rho}(x):=\kappa Z(\rho x)
satisfies

-\Delta Z^{\kappa,\rho}+\rho^{2}Z=\kappa^{p-2}\rho^{2}|Z^{\kappa,\rho}|^{p-2}Z^{\kappa,\rho}\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}^{N}},

(1.45)

and (
1.43
) is achieved by
u
if and only if

u\in\left\{Z^{\kappa,\rho}(\cdot+y):\kappa>0,\rho>0,y\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\right\}.

(1.46)

An importmant transformation.
We introduce a
L^{2}
-norm invariant transformation
t\star u(x):=e^{\frac{N}{2}t}u(e^{t}x)
and

(u,v)\in{\cal T}(a,b)\mapsto t\star(u,v):=(t\star u,t\star v)\in{\cal T}(a,b).

We define the following fiber map

\displaystyle\Phi_{(u,v)}(t):=I(t\star(u,v))=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}e^{2t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}\right)~\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{p}e^{p\gamma_{p}t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\left(|u|^{p}+|v|^{p}\right)~\mathrm{d}x

(1.47)

\displaystyle-\frac{\nu}{2^{*}}e^{2^{*}t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}~\mathrm{d}x.

By a direct computation, we observe that

, where

is defined by (

). Therefore, it holds

{\cal P}(a,b)=\left\{(u,v)\in{\cal T}(a,b):\Phi_{(u,v)}^{\prime}(0)=0\right\}.

(1.48)

In this direction, we decompose
{\cal P}(a,b)
into three disjoint submanifolds
{\cal P}(a,b)={\cal P}^{+}(a,b)\cup{\cal P}^{0}(a,b)\cup{\cal P}^{-}(a,b)
, which are given by

\displaystyle\cal{\displaystyle P}^{+}(a,b):=\left\{(u,v)\in{\cal P}(a,b):\Phi_{(u,v)}^{\prime\prime}(0)>0\right\},

(1.49)

\displaystyle\cal{\displaystyle P}^{0}(a,b):=\left\{(u,v)\in{\cal P}(a,b):\Phi_{(u,v)}^{\prime\prime}(0)=0\right\},

\displaystyle\cal{\displaystyle P}^{-}(a,b):=\left\{(u,v)\in{\cal P}(a,b):\Phi_{(u,v)}^{\prime\prime}(0)<0\right\}.

In this section, we study the existence of normalized solution of (

\nu>0

).
To obtain the compactness of Palais-Smale sequence, we need the following additional assumptions

2<p<2^{*}~\text{when}~N=3,4;\quad 2<p<2+\frac{2}{N-2}~\text{when}~N\geq 5,

(2.1)

a^{4/N}+b^{4/N}<(1+\frac{2}{N}){\cal C}(N,p)\quad\text{when}~p=2+\frac{4}{N}.

(2.2)

Now we give the following compactness result for
\nu>0
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that (
2.1
), (
2.2
) hold and

m(a,b)\leq m(a_{1},b_{1})\quad\text{for any}~0<a_{1}\leq a,~0<b_{1}\leq b.

(2.3)

Let
\{(u_{n},v_{n})\}\subset{\cal T}(a,b)
be a sequence consisting of radial symmetric functions such that

I^{\prime}(u_{n},v_{n})+\lambda_{1,n}u_{n}+\lambda_{2,n}v_{n}\to 0\quad\text{for some}~\lambda_{1,n},\lambda_{2,n}\in{\mathbb{R}},

(2.4)

I(u_{n},v_{n})\to c,\quad P(u_{n},v_{n})\to 0,

(2.5)

u_{n}^{-},v_{n}^{-}\to 0,~\text{a.e. in}~{\mathbb{R}^{N}},

(2.6)

with

c\neq 0,\quad c\neq e(a),\quad c\neq e(b),

(2.7)

and

c<\frac{\nu^{-(N-2)/2}}{N}{\cal S}_{\alpha,\beta}^{N/2}+\min\left\{~0,~e(a),~e(b),~m(a,b)~\right\}.

(2.8)

Then there exists
u,v\in H_{rad}^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
,
u,v>0
and
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
such that
up to a subsequence
(u_{n},v_{n})\to(u,v)
in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\times H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
and

(\lambda_{1,n},\lambda_{2,n})\to(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

Proof.

The proof is divided into three steps.

Step 1.)

We show that
\{(u_{n},v_{n})\}
is bounded in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\times H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
.

If
2<p<2+\frac{4}{N}
, there is
p\gamma_{p}<2
. Then using
P(u_{n},v_{n})\to 0
, one can see from the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality that
for
n
large enough,

\displaystyle c+1

\displaystyle\geq I(u_{n},v_{n})-\frac{1}{2^{*}}P(u_{n},v_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\left(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)-\frac{2^{*}-p}{2^{*}p}\left(\|u_{n}\|_{p}^{p}+\|v_{n}\|_{p}^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N}\left(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)-C\left(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{p\gamma_{p}/2},

for some
C>0
, which implies that
\{(u_{n},v_{n})\}
is bounded.
Now if
2+\frac{4}{N}\leq p<2^{*}
, then
p\gamma_{p}\geq 2
and for
n
large enough

\displaystyle c+1

\displaystyle\geq I(u_{n},v_{n})-\frac{1}{2}P(u_{n},v_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{p\gamma_{p}-2}{2p}\left(\|u_{n}\|_{p}^{p}+\|v_{n}\|_{p}^{p}\right)+\frac{\nu}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{n}|^{\alpha}|v_{n}|^{\beta}~\mathrm{d}x.

Combining (
2.2
) with the fact that
P(u_{n},v_{n})=o(1)
, we conclude that
\{(u_{n},v_{n})\}
is bounded.
Moreover, from

\lambda_{1,n}=-\frac{1}{a}I^{\prime}(u_{n},v_{n})[(u_{n},0)]+o(1)\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{2,n}=-\frac{1}{b}I^{\prime}(u_{n},v_{n})[(0,v_{n})]+o(1),

we know that
\lambda_{1,n},\lambda_{2,n}
are also bounded.
So there exists
u,v\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
,
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that up to a subsequence

(u_{n},v_{n})\rightharpoonup(u,v)\quad\text{in}~H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\times H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}}),

(u_{n},v_{n})\rightarrow(u,v)\quad\text{in}~L^{q}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\times L^{q}({\mathbb{R}^{N}}),~\text{for}~2<q<2^{*},

(u_{n},v_{n})\rightarrow(u,v)\quad\text{a.e. in}~{\mathbb{R}^{N}},

(\lambda_{1,n},\lambda_{2,n})\to(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\quad\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

Then (
2.4
) and (
2.6
) give that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &I^{\prime}(u,v)+\lambda_{1}u+\lambda_{2}v=0,\\
&u\geq 0,v\geq 0,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.9)

and hence
P(u,v)=0
.

Step 2.)

We show that the weak limit
u\neq 0
and
v\neq 0
.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
u=0
by contradiction. There are two cases.
If
v=0
, then from
P(u_{n},v_{n})=o(1)
we obtain that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{+}\eta_{\varepsilon}~\mathrm{d}x\sim\varepsilon^{\frac{5}{2}}\int_{1}^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{1}{1+r^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{2}~\mathrm{d}r\sim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}},~~\text{ and }\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}v_{+}\eta_{\varepsilon}~\mathrm{d}x\sim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.53)

Then we have

\displaystyle\tau^{2}=1+\frac{2\sqrt{\alpha}t}{a^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{+}\eta_{\varepsilon}~\mathrm{d}x+\frac{t^{2}\alpha}{a^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\eta_{\varepsilon}|^{2}~\mathrm{d}x=1+O(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}),

(2.54)

\displaystyle\xi^{2}=1+\frac{2\sqrt{\beta}t}{b^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}v_{+}\eta_{\varepsilon}~\mathrm{d}x+\frac{t^{2}\beta}{b^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\eta_{\varepsilon}|^{2}~\mathrm{d}x=1+O(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}).

Since
(u_{+},v_{+})
is a solution of system (
1.6
) with
\lambda_{1,+},\lambda_{2,+}>0
, it follows from a similar argument as used in
[

, Lemma 5.5]
that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\nabla u_{+}\cdot\nabla\eta_{\varepsilon}~\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{1,+}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{+}\eta_{\varepsilon}~\mathrm{d}x+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u_{+}|^{p-2}u_{+}\eta_{\varepsilon}~\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\alpha\nu}{2^{*}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u_{+}|^{\alpha-2}|v_{+}|^{\beta}u_{+}\eta_{\varepsilon}~\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq-\lambda_{1,+}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{+}\eta_{\varepsilon}~\mathrm{d}x+C\|\eta_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{2}=-\lambda_{1,+}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{+}\eta_{\varepsilon}~\mathrm{d}x+O(\varepsilon).

Similarly, we have

(2.56)

Therefore, combining these with (
2.54
) we have

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=t\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(\sqrt{\alpha}\nabla u_{+}+\sqrt{\beta}\nabla v_{+})\cdot\nabla\eta_{\varepsilon}~\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1-\tau^{p\gamma_{p}-p}}{p}\|w_{\varepsilon,t}\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{1-\xi^{p\gamma_{p}-p}}{p}\|v_{\varepsilon,t}\|_{p}^{p}

(2.57)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t\sqrt{\alpha}}{a^{2}}\left(-\lambda_{1,+}a^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{+}\eta_{\varepsilon}~\mathrm{d}x+(1-\gamma_{p})\|w_{\varepsilon,t}\|_{p}^{p}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{+}\eta_{\varepsilon}~\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{t\sqrt{\beta}}{b^{2}}\left(-\lambda_{2,+}b^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}v_{+}\eta_{\varepsilon}~\mathrm{d}x+(1-\gamma_{p})\|v_{\varepsilon,t}\|_{p}^{p}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}v_{+}\eta_{\varepsilon}~\mathrm{d}x\right)+O(\varepsilon).

Note that the fact
\lambda_{1,+}a^{2}+\lambda_{2,+}b^{2}=(1-\gamma_{p})\left(\|u_{+}\|_{p}^{p}+\|v_{+}\|_{p}^{p}\right)
which comes from the Pohozaev identity

I_{2}=t\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(\sqrt{\alpha}\nabla u_{+}+\sqrt{\beta}\nabla v_{+})\cdot\nabla\eta_{\varepsilon}~\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1-\tau^{p\gamma_{p}-p}}{p}\|w_{\varepsilon,t}\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{1-\xi^{p\gamma_{p}-p}}{p}\|v_{\varepsilon,t}\|_{p}^{p}=O(\varepsilon).

(2.58)

Now we estimate

(2.59)

Note that

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=(1-\tau^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\xi^{\frac{\beta}{2}})\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|w_{\varepsilon,t}(x)|^{\alpha}|v_{\varepsilon,t}(x)|^{\beta}\mathrm{d}x

(2.60)

\displaystyle+\tau^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\xi^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|w_{\varepsilon,t}(x)|^{\alpha}|v_{\varepsilon,t}(x)|^{\beta}\mathrm{d}x-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|w_{\varepsilon,t}(\tau x)|^{\alpha}|v_{\varepsilon,t}(x)|^{\beta}\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle+\tau^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\xi^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|w_{\varepsilon,t}(\tau x)|^{\alpha}|v_{\varepsilon,t}(x)|^{\beta}\mathrm{d}x-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|w_{\varepsilon,t}(\tau x)|^{\alpha}|v_{\varepsilon,t}(\xi x)|^{\beta}\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle+\tau^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\xi^{\frac{\beta}{2}}(1-\xi)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\beta|w_{\varepsilon,t}(\tau x)|^{\alpha}|v_{\varepsilon,t}(x)|^{\beta-1}\left(x\cdot\nabla v_{\varepsilon,t}(x)\right)\mathrm{d}x+o(|1-\xi|).

For small

and

(2.61)

Hence, with the help of Hölder inequality, it is easy to see that

are bounded independent of
\varepsilon

Therefore, by (

(2.64)

\displaystyle<m(a,b)+\frac{\nu^{-(N-2)/2}}{N}{\cal S}_{\alpha,\beta}^{N/2}

Existence for the repulsive case

In this section, we study the existence of normalized solution of (

) for the repulsive case
\nu<0
. Throughout this section, we always work under the following assumptions

N=3,~2<p<2+\frac{4}{N},\text{ and }~p\leq\alpha,\beta\leq 2^{*},\alpha+\beta=2^{*}.

(3.1)

Now we give the following compactness result for
\nu<0
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that (
3.1
) holds and

m_{r}(a,b)\leq m_{r}(a_{1},b_{1})\quad\text{for any}~0<a_{1}\leq a,~0<b_{1}\leq b.

(3.2)

Let
\{(u_{n},v_{n})\}\subset{\cal T}_{r}(a,b)
be a sequence consisting of radial symmetric functions such that

I^{\prime}(u_{n},v_{n})+\lambda_{1,n}u_{n}+\lambda_{2,n}v_{n}\to 0\quad\text{for some}~\lambda_{1,n},\lambda_{2,n}\in{\mathbb{R}},

(3.3)

I(u_{n},v_{n})\to c,\quad P(u_{n},v_{n})\to 0,

(3.4)

u_{n}^{-},v_{n}^{-}\to 0,~\text{a.e. in}~{\mathbb{R}^{N}},

(3.5)

with

c<\min\left\{e(a),~e(b)\right\}\text{ and }c\neq 0.

(3.6)

Then there exists
u,v\in H_{rad}^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
with
u,v>0
and
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
such that
up to a subsequence
(u_{n},v_{n})\to(u,v)
in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\times H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
and

(\lambda_{1,n},\lambda_{2,n})\to(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into three steps.

Step 1.) We prove that
\left\{(u_{n},v_{n})\right\}
is bounded in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\times H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
.

By
P(u_{n},v_{n})\to 0
, one can obtain that for
n
large enough,

\displaystyle c+1

\displaystyle\geq I(u_{n},v_{n})-\frac{1}{2^{*}}P(u_{n},v_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\left(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)-\frac{2^{*}-p}{2^{*}p}\left(\|u_{n}\|_{p}^{p}+\|v_{n}\|_{p}^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N}\left(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)-C\left(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{p\gamma_{p}/2},

for some
C>0
, which implies that
\{(u_{n},v_{n})\}
is bounded because
p\gamma_{p}<2
.

Moreover, from

\lambda_{1,n}=-\frac{1}{a}I^{\prime}(u_{n},v_{n})[(u_{n},0)]+o(1)\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{2,n}=-\frac{1}{b}I^{\prime}(u_{n},v_{n})[(0,v_{n})]+o(1),

we know that
\lambda_{1,n},\lambda_{2,n}
are also bounded.
So there exists
u,v\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
,
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that up to a subsequence

(u_{n},v_{n})\rightharpoonup(u,v)\quad\text{in}~H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\times H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}}),

(u_{n},v_{n})\rightarrow(u,v)\quad\text{in}~L^{q}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\times L^{q}({\mathbb{R}^{N}}),~\text{for}~2<q<2^{*},

(u_{n},v_{n})\rightarrow(u,v)\quad\text{a.e. in}~{\mathbb{R}^{N}},

(\lambda_{1,n},\lambda_{2,n})\to(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\quad\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

Then (
3.3
) and (
3.5

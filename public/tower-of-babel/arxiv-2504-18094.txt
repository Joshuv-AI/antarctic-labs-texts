Abstract
. We justify rigorously the equilibrium-diffusion limit of the model consists of a radiative transfer satisfied by the specific intensity of radiation coupled to a diffusion equation satisfied by the material temperature. For general initial data, we construct the existence of the solution to the coupled model in 𝕋 3 \mathbb{T}^{3} by the Hilbert expansion and prove the convergence of the solutions to the limiting system in the equilibrium-diffusion regime. Moreover, the initial layer for the radiative density and the temperature are constructed to get the strong convergence in L ∞ L^{\infty} norm. We also get the convergence rates about the intensity of radiation and temperature in this paper.

Well-posedness of equilibrium regime neutron transport equation

In this section, we consider the well-posedness of equilibrium regime neutron transport equation, which can be written as

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\varepsilon}^{2}\frac{\partial I}{\partial t}+\varepsilon\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}I+{\varepsilon}^{2}(I-\overline{I})+I=F(t,\vec{x},\vec{w})~~\mathrm{in}\ \ (0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2},\\
&I(0,\vec{x},\vec{w})=h(\vec{x},\vec{w})~~\mathrm{in}\ \ \mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Define the
L^{2}
and
L^{\infty}
norms in
\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
by

\|I\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|I(\vec{x},\vec{w})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\vec{w}\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\|I\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}=\sup_{(\vec{x},\vec{w})\in\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|I(\vec{x},\vec{w})|.\;\;

Similar notations also applies to the space
[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}.

2.1.
L^{\infty}
well-posedness

We will show the existence and
L^{\infty}
estimate of the transport equation (
2.1
) in the following.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume
F(t,\vec{x},\vec{w})\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}),h(\vec{x},\vec{w})\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
and
F,h\geq 0
. Then there exists a unique nonnegative solution
I(t,\vec{x},\vec{w})\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
of the transport equation (
2.1
) which satisfies

(2.2)

\|I\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq C(\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|F\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}).

Proof.
We construct an approximating sequence
\{I_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
which are also periodic, where
I^{0}=0
and

(2.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\varepsilon}^{2}\frac{\partial I^{k}}{\partial t}+\varepsilon\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}I^{k}+(1+\varepsilon^{2})I^{k}-\varepsilon^{2}\overline{I^{k-1}}=F(t,\vec{x},\vec{w})~~\mathrm{in}\ \ (0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2},\\
&I^{k}(0,\vec{x},\vec{w})=h(\vec{x},\vec{w})~~\mathrm{in}\ \ \mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

The characteristics
(T(s),X(s),W(s))
of equation (
2.3
) which goes through
(t,\vec{x},\vec{w})
is defined by

(2.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(T(0),X(0),W(0))=(t,\vec{x},\vec{w}),\\
&\frac{dT(s)}{ds}=\varepsilon^{2},\\
&\frac{dX(s)}{ds}=\varepsilon W(s),\\
&\frac{dW(s)}{ds}=0,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

which implies

\left\{\begin{aligned} &T(s)=t+\varepsilon^{2}s,\\
&X(s)=\vec{x}+(\varepsilon\vec{w})s,\\
&W(s)=\vec{w}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Then,

(2.5)

\displaystyle I^{k}(t,\vec{x},\vec{w})=

\displaystyle h(\vec{x}-\frac{(\varepsilon t\vec{w})}{\varepsilon^{2}},\vec{w})e^{-(1+\varepsilon^{2})\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}}}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}}}(\varepsilon^{2}\overline{I^{k-1}}+F)(\varepsilon^{2}s,\vec{x}-\varepsilon(\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}}-s)\vec{w},\vec{w})e^{-(1+\varepsilon^{2})(\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}}-s)}ds.

Since
I^{0}=0
and
h,g,F\geq 0
, we obtain
I^{1}\geq 0
. Then, we get
I^{k}\geq 0
for any
k\geq 0
iteratively. Set
v_{k}=I^{k}-I^{k-1}
for
k\geq 1.
Then, we have

\displaystyle v_{k+1}(t,\vec{x},\vec{w})=\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}}}\varepsilon^{2}\overline{v_{k}}(\varepsilon^{2}s,\vec{x}-\varepsilon(\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}}-s)\vec{w},\vec{w})e^{-(1+\varepsilon^{2})(\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}}-s)}ds.

Since
\|\overline{v_{k}}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq\|v_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|v_{k+1}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq\frac{1-e^{-(1+\varepsilon^{2})\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}}}}{1+\varepsilon^{2}}\|\varepsilon^{2}v_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

It follows that

\|v_{k+1}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon^{2}}\|\varepsilon^{2}v_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})},

which implies
\{v_{k}\}
is a contraction sequence. According to the fact that
v_{1}=I^{1}
, we have

\|v_{k+1}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq(\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon^{2}})^{k}\|\varepsilon^{2}I^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}

for
k\geq 1.
Therefore,
I^{k}
converges strongly in
L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
to a limit solution
I
which satisfies

(2.6)

\|I\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|\varepsilon^{2}v_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq(1+\varepsilon^{2})\|I^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

Since

\displaystyle I^{1}(t,\vec{x},\vec{w})=h(\vec{x}-\frac{(\varepsilon t\vec{w})}{\varepsilon^{2}},\vec{w})e^{-(1+\varepsilon^{2})\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}}}+\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}}}F(\varepsilon^{2}s,\vec{x}-\varepsilon(\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}}-s)\vec{w},\vec{w})e^{-(1+\varepsilon^{2})(\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}}-s)}ds,

we obtain

(2.7)

\|I^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|F\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

According to (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we can easily derive the existence and estimates. Furthermore, we can get the uniqueness of equation (
2.1
) according to the
L^{\infty}
energy estimate.
\Box

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
0<T_{0}<\infty
and
m\geq 0
, if
F(t,\vec{x},\vec{w})\in C^{m}([0,T_{0}]\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}),h(\vec{x},\vec{w})\in C^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
, we can obtain that the solution
I
in Lemma
2.1
also satisfies that
I\in C^{m}([0,T_{0}]\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
.

Proof.
We can apply the operator
D_{x}^{\alpha}
to (
2.5
) by the fact that
D_{x}^{\alpha}F(t,\vec{x},\vec{w})\in C^{0}([0,T_{0}]\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}),D_{x}^{\alpha}h(\vec{x},\vec{w})\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
where
0\leq\alpha\leq m
.

We can finally obtain that

\|D_{x}^{\alpha}v_{k+1}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T_{0})\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq(\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon^{2}})^{k}\|\varepsilon^{2}D_{x}^{\alpha}I^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T_{0})\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})},

which implies
\{D_{x}^{\alpha}v_{k}\}
is a contraction sequence. We can conclude that
D_{x}^{\alpha}I^{k}
converges strongly in
C^{0}([0,T_{0}]\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
to a limit solution
I^{\alpha}
which satisfies

(2.8)

\|D_{x}^{\alpha}I\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T_{0})\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|\varepsilon^{2}D_{x}^{\alpha}v_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T_{0})\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq(1+\varepsilon^{2})\|D_{x}^{\alpha}I\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T_{0})\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

Since
I^{k}
converges strongly in
C^{0}([0,T_{0}]\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
to a limit solution
I
, we can verify that
I_{\alpha}=D_{x}^{\alpha}I
, which implies
D_{x}^{\alpha}I\in C^{0}([0,T_{0}]\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
where
0\leq\alpha\leq m
.
Since

\displaystyle D_{x}^{\alpha}I^{1}(t,\vec{x},\vec{w})=

\displaystyle D_{x}^{\alpha}h(\vec{x}-\frac{(\varepsilon t\vec{w})}{\varepsilon^{2}},\vec{w})e^{-(1+\varepsilon^{2})\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}}}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}}}D_{x}^{\alpha}F(\varepsilon^{2}s,\vec{x}-\varepsilon(\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}}-s)\vec{w},\vec{w})e^{-(1+\varepsilon^{2})(\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}}-s)}ds,

we obtain

(2.9)

\|D_{x}^{\alpha}I^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T_{0})\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq\|D_{x}^{\alpha}h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|D_{x}^{\alpha}F\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T_{0})\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

Therefore, we have

(2.10)

\|D_{x}^{\alpha}I\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T_{0})\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq C(\|D_{x}^{\alpha}h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|D_{x}^{\alpha}F\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T_{0})\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}).

which implies
D_{x}^{\alpha}I\in C^{0}([0,T_{0}]\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
where
0\leq\alpha\leq m
.
\Box

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
0<T_{0}<\infty
, if
F(t,\vec{x},\vec{w})\in L^{2}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))),h(\vec{x},\vec{w})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
, there exists a unique nonnegative solution
I(t,\vec{x},\vec{w})\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};\\
H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{3})))
of the transport equation (
2.1
) which satisfies

(2.11)

\displaystyle\|I\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{3})))}\leq C(\|h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\|F\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{3})))})

Proof.
We can construct sequences
\{F_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset C^{m}([0,T_{0}]\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
and
\{h_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset C^{m}(\overline{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
satisfying
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|F_{k}-F\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{3})))}=0
and
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|h_{k}-h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))}\\
=0
. Then, we consider the following equations

(2.12)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\varepsilon}^{2}\frac{\partial I_{k}}{\partial t}+\varepsilon\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}I_{k}+\varepsilon^{2}(I_{k}-\overline{I_{k}})+I_{k}=F_{k}(t,\vec{x},\vec{w}),~~\mathrm{in}\ \ (0,T_{0})\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2},\\
&I_{k}(0,\vec{x},\vec{w})=h_{k}(\vec{x},\vec{w}),~~\mathrm{in}\ \ \mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

According to Lemma
2.2
, we have
D_{x}^{\alpha}I_{k}\in C^{0}([0,T_{0}]\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
where
0\leq l\leq m
. Applying the operator on both sides of
(\ref{approximating sequence equation-bu})_{1}
, multiplying
D_{x}^{l}I_{k}
on both sides of
(\ref{approximating sequence equation-bu})_{1}
and integrating on
(0,t)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
for
t\in[0,T_{0}]
, then, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|D_{x}^{\alpha}I_{k}|^{2}(t)\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}\vec{w}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|D_{x}^{\alpha}(I_{k}-\overline{I_{k}})|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}\vec{w}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|D_{x}^{\alpha}I_{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|D_{x}^{l}h_{k}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}\vec{w}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}D_{x}^{\alpha}F_{k}D_{x}^{\alpha}I_{k}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}\vec{w}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|D_{x}^{\alpha}h_{k}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}\vec{w}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|D_{x}^{\alpha}F_{k}|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}\vec{w}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|D_{x}^{\alpha}I_{k}|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}\vec{w}.

So,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|D_{x}^{\alpha}I_{k}|^{2}(t)\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}\vec{w}\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|D_{x}^{\alpha}h_{k}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}\vec{w}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|D_{x}^{\alpha}F_{k}|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}\vec{w}.

Letting
k\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|I^{\alpha}|^{2}(t)\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}\vec{w}\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|D_{x}^{\alpha}h|^{2}\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}\vec{w}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|D_{x}^{\alpha}F|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}\vec{w},

where
I^{\alpha}
is the weak limit of
D_{x}^{\alpha}I_{k}
in
L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{3}))
in the weak-
\ast
sense up to a subsequence. We can deduce that
I^{\alpha}=D_{x}^{\alpha}I
by the fact that
I_{k}\rightarrow I
in
L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{3}))
in the weak-
\ast
sense up to the same subsequence. We can also verify that
I(0,\vec{x},\vec{w})=h(\vec{x},\vec{w})
by referring to suitable test functions and integrating by parts. The method is standard, we omit the details here. So, we have proved the existence and estimate (
2.11
). The uniqueness of equation (
2.1
) can be ensured by (
2.11
).
\Box

Lemma 2.4
.

For the solution
I(t,\vec{x},\vec{w})
we obtained in Lemma
2.3
, it further satisfies that
I(t,\vec{x},\vec{w})\in C^{0}([0,T_{0}];L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{3})))
.

Proof.
Define the Friedrichs mollifier
\phi_{\delta}
. Applying the operator on both sides of (
2.1
), we derive

(2.13)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\varepsilon}^{2}\frac{\partial I_{\delta}}{\partial t}+\varepsilon\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}I_{\delta}+{\varepsilon}^{2}(I_{\delta}-\overline{I_{\delta}})+I_{\delta}=F_{\delta}(t,\vec{x},\vec{w})~~\mathrm{in}\ \ (0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2},\\
&I_{\delta}(0,\vec{x},\vec{w})=h_{\delta}(\vec{x},\vec{w})~~\mathrm{in}\ \ \mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Define
I_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}=I_{\delta}-I_{\delta^{\prime}}
. Then, we can write the equation satisfied by
I_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}
as follows

(2.14)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\varepsilon}^{2}\frac{\partial I_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}}{\partial t}+\varepsilon\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}I_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}+{\varepsilon}^{2}(I_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}-\overline{I_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}})+I_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}=F_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}(t,\vec{x},\vec{w})~~\mathrm{in}\ \ (0,T_{0})\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2},\\
&I_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}(0,\vec{x},\vec{w})=h_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}(\vec{x},\vec{w})~~\mathrm{in}\ \ \mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2},\\
\end{aligned}\right.

where
h_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}=h_{\delta}-h_{\delta^{\prime}}
and
F_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}=F_{\delta}-F_{\delta^{\prime}}
.

According to the estimate (
2.11
), we have

(2.15)

\displaystyle\|I_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{3})))}\leq C(\|h_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\|F_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{3})))}).

As
\delta,\delta^{\prime}\rightarrow 0
,
h_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}\rightarrow 0
and
F_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
and
L^{2}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{3})))
, respectively, we derive that
I_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{3})))
. Then, we confirm that
I_{\delta}
is a Cauchy sequence in
C^{0}([0,T_{0}];L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{3})))
which further implies
I\in C^{0}([0,T_{0}];L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{3})))
.
\Box

3.
Hilbert Expansion

3.1.
Interior expansion

First, we give the interior expansion of
\theta^{\varepsilon}
as follows:

(3.1)

\theta^{\varepsilon}\sim\sum_{k=0}^{N}\varepsilon^{k}\theta_{k}.

Then, we get

(3.2)

\displaystyle B(\theta^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\sim(\theta_{0}+\varepsilon\theta_{1}+\cdot\cdot\cdot)^{4}

\displaystyle=\theta_{0}^{4}+4\theta_{0}^{3}\theta_{1}\varepsilon+(4\theta_{2}\theta_{0}^{3}+6\theta_{1}^{2}\theta_{0}^{2})\varepsilon^{2}+\cdot\cdot\cdot

\displaystyle=B_{0}(\theta_{0})+\varepsilon B_{1}(\theta_{0};\theta_{1})+\varepsilon^{2}B_{2}(\theta_{0};\theta_{2})+\cdot\cdot\cdot

where

(3.3)

B_{k}(\theta_{0};\theta_{k}):=\sum_{\begin{matrix}i+j+l+m=k,\\
i,j,l,m\geq 0\end{matrix}}\theta_{i}\theta_{j}\theta_{l}\theta_{m}.

Define the interior expansion of
f^{\varepsilon}(t,\vec{x},w)
as

(3.4)

f^{\varepsilon}(t,\vec{x},\vec{w})\sim\sum_{k=0}^{N}{\varepsilon}^{k}f_{k}(t,\vec{x},\vec{w}).

Define

(3.5)

\mathcal{L}_{1}(f^{\varepsilon},\theta^{\varepsilon}):=\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{t}f^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{\varepsilon}+{\varepsilon}^{2}(f^{\varepsilon}-\overline{f^{\varepsilon}})+f^{\varepsilon}-B(\theta^{\varepsilon})

and

(3.6)

\mathcal{L}_{2}(f^{\varepsilon},\theta^{\varepsilon}):=\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{t}\theta^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{2}\mathrm{div}(\vec{u}\theta^{\varepsilon})-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta_{x}\theta^{\varepsilon}-(\overline{f^{\varepsilon}}-B(\theta^{\varepsilon})).

Plugging (
3.1
) and (
3.4
) into the above formulas gives

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}(\sum_{k=0}^{N}{\varepsilon}^{k}f_{k}(t,\vec{x},\vec{w}),\sum_{k=0}^{N}\varepsilon^{k}\theta_{k})=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N}{\varepsilon}^{k}(\partial_{t}f_{k-2}+\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{k-1}+f_{k-2}-\overline{f_{k-2}}+f_{k}-B_{k}(\theta_{0};\theta_{k}))

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{N+1}\partial_{t}f_{N-1}+\varepsilon^{N+2}\partial_{t}f_{N}+\varepsilon^{N+1}\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{N}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=N+1}^{4N}\varepsilon^{k}\sum_{\begin{matrix}i+j+l+m=k,\\
0\leq i,j,l,m\leq N\end{matrix}}\theta_{i}\theta_{j}\theta_{l}\theta_{m}

and

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2}(\sum_{k=0}^{N}{\varepsilon}^{k}f_{k}(t,\vec{x},\vec{w}),\sum_{k=0}^{N}\varepsilon^{k}\theta_{k})=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N}{\varepsilon}^{k}(\partial_{t}\theta_{k-2}+\mathrm{div}(\vec{u}\theta_{k-2})-\Delta_{x}\theta_{k-2}-(\overline{f_{k}}-B_{k}(\theta_{0};\theta_{k})))

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{N+1}\partial_{t}\theta_{N-1}+\varepsilon^{N+2}\partial_{t}\theta_{N}+\varepsilon^{N+1}\Delta_{x}\theta_{N-1}+\varepsilon^{N+2}\Delta_{x}\theta_{N}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{N+1}\mathrm{div}(\vec{u}\theta_{N-1})+\varepsilon^{N+2}\mathrm{div}(\vec{u}\theta_{N})

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=N+1}^{4N}\varepsilon^{k}\sum_{\begin{matrix}i+j+l+m=k,\\
0\leq i,j,l,m\leq N\end{matrix}}\theta_{i}\theta_{j}\theta_{l}\theta_{m},

where
(f_{k},\theta_{k})
,
k<0
are taken to be zero.

Collecting the terms with the same order, we take

(3.9)

\partial_{t}f_{k-2}+\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{k-1}+f_{k-2}-\overline{f_{k-2}}+f_{k}-B_{k}(\theta_{0};\theta_{k})=0,

(3.10)

\partial_{t}\theta_{k-2}+\mathrm{div}(\vec{u}\theta_{k-2})-\Delta_{x}\theta_{k-2}-(\overline{f_{k}}-B_{k}(\theta_{0};\theta_{k}))=0,

for any
k=0,1,\cdot\cdot\cdot,N
. From the above two equations we obtain

(3.11)

\partial_{t}\theta_{k-2}+\mathrm{div}(\vec{u}\theta_{k-2})-\Delta_{x}\theta_{k-2}=-\partial_{t}\overline{f_{k-2}}-\langle\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{k-1}\rangle.

By (
3.9
), an iteration process on the above equation leads to

(3.12)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\theta_{k-2}+\mathrm{div}(\vec{u}\theta_{k-2})-\Delta_{x}\theta_{k-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|D_{x}(4L_{0}\theta_{r})-D_{x}f_{r}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\vec{w}\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}s+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\nabla_{x}D_{x}\theta_{r}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\vec{w}\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|D_{x}\theta_{r}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\vec{w}\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}s+C\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|D_{x}f_{r}\|_{L_{\vec{w}}^{2}L_{\vec{x}}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C(t)}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(|D_{x}R_{1}|^{2}+|D_{x}R_{2}|^{2}+|D_{x}R|^{2})\mathrm{d}\vec{w}\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\frac{C(t)}{\varepsilon^{4}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(|R_{1}|^{2}+|R_{2}|^{2}+|R|^{2})\mathrm{d}\vec{w}\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}s.

For
\alpha=2
, we can finally get

(4.68)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\|D_{x}^{2}f_{r}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\vec{w}}^{2}L_{\vec{x}}^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|D_{x}^{2}\theta_{r}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\vec{x}}^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|D_{x}^{2}f_{r}-D_{x}^{2}\overline{f_{r}}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\vec{w}\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|D_{x}^{2}(4L_{0}\theta_{r})-D_{x}^{2}f_{r}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\vec{w}\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}s+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\nabla_{x}D_{x}^{2}\theta_{r}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\vec{w}\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|D_{x}^{2}\theta_{r}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\vec{w}\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}s+C\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|D_{x}^{2}f_{r}\|_{L_{\vec{w}}^{2}L_{\vec{x}}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C(t)}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(|D_{x}^{2}R_{1}|^{2}+|D_{x}^{2}R_{2}|^{2}+|D_{x}^{2}R|^{2})\mathrm{d}\vec{w}\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\frac{C(t)}{\varepsilon^{4}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(|D_{x}R_{1}|^{2}+|D_{x}R_{2}|^{2}+|D_{x}R|^{2})\mathrm{d}\vec{w}\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\frac{C(t)}{\varepsilon^{6}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(|R_{1}|^{2}+|R_{2}|^{2}+|R|^{2})\mathrm{d}\vec{w}\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\mathrm{d}s,

as the procedure of
\alpha=0
and
\alpha=1
.

Collecting (
4.63
), (
4.67
) and (
4.68
), we can derive (
4.32
).

4.2.
Nonlinear system

We now show the existence and uniqueness of solutions to system (
1.1
) around the constructed composite approximate solution
(f^{N},\theta^{N})
and finish the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Due to the equivalence between system (
4.3
)-(
4.5
) and (
1.1
), we only need to show the existence and uniqueness of solutions for (
4.3
)-(
4.5
) in the neighborhood of zero.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
The proof of existence and uniqueness is obtained using the Banach fixed point theorem. We first construct a sequence of functions and then show the sequence is a contraction sequence. Finally we show the convergence of
(f_{r},\theta_{r})
to zero as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Step 1.
Construction of sequence of functions. Let
\{f_{r}^{0},\theta_{r}^{0}\}
be zero functions

(4.69)

f_{r}^{0}=0,\ \ \theta_{r}^{0}=0,

and for
k\geq 1,

\{f_{r}^{k+1},\theta_{r}^{k+1}\}
are defined recursively by

(4.70)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{t}f_{r}^{k+1}+\varepsilon\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{r}^{k+1}+\varepsilon^{2}(f_{r}^{k+1}-\overline{f_{r}^{k+1}})+f_{r}^{k+1}-4(\theta^{N})^{3}\theta_{r}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathcal{L}_{1}(f^{N},\theta^{N})+6(\theta^{N})^{2}(\theta_{r}^{k})^{2}+4\theta^{N}(\theta_{r}^{k})^{3}+(\theta_{r}^{k})^{4},

(4.71)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{t}\theta_{r}^{k+1}+\varepsilon^{2}\mathrm{div}{(\vec{u}\theta_{r}^{k+1})}-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta_{x}\theta_{r}^{k+1}+4(\theta^{N})^{3}\theta_{r}^{k+1}-\overline{f_{r}^{k+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathcal{L}_{2}(f^{N},\theta^{N})-6(\theta^{N})^{2}(\theta_{r}^{k})^{2}-4\theta^{N}(\theta_{r}^{k})^{3}-(\theta_{r}^{k})^{4},

with initial conditions

(4.72)

f_{r}^{k+1}(0,\vec{x},\vec{w})=0,\ \ \theta_{r}^{k+1}(0,\vec{x})=0,\ \ \mathrm{for}\ \ (\vec{x},\vec{w})\in\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}.

The above system defines a mapping
\mathcal{T}
with
(f_{r}^{k+1},\theta_{r}^{k+1})=\mathcal{T}((f_{r}^{k},\theta_{r}^{k}))
.

Step 2.
The contraction mapping. We consider the solution in the function space

(4.73)

O_{q}:=\{(f_{r},\theta_{r})\in L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{\vec{w}}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}\times L^{\infty}_{T}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}:\|f_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{\vec{w}}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}}+\|\theta_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}}\leq\varepsilon^{q}\},

where
q>0
is a constant to be chosen later.

First, we show
\mathcal{T}
maps the space
O_{q}
into itself. Assume the residuals satisfy

(4.74)

\|\mathcal{L}_{1}(f^{N},\theta^{N})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{\vec{w}}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}},\|\mathcal{L}_{2}(f^{N},\theta^{N})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{\vec{w}}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}}=O(\varepsilon^{N+1}).

By (
4.32
) with
R_{1}=-\mathcal{L}_{1}(f^{N},\theta^{N})
,
R_{2}=-\mathcal{L}_{2}(f^{N},\theta^{N})
and

R=-6(\theta^{N})^{2}(\theta_{r}^{k})^{2}-4\theta^{N}(\theta_{r}^{k})^{3}-(\theta_{r}^{k})^{4}
, the following estimate holds (assuming
q\geq 1
):

(4.75)

\displaystyle\|f_{r}^{k+1}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}L_{\vec{w}}^{2}}+\|\theta_{r}^{k+1}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{\vec{x}}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\|f_{r}^{k+1}-4(\theta^{N})^{3}\theta_{r}^{k+1}\|_{L_{T}^{2}H_{\vec{x}}^{2}L_{\vec{w}}^{2}}+\|\theta_{r}^{k+1}\|_{L_{T}^{2}H_{\vec{x}}^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C(T)}{\varepsilon^{4}}\Big(\|6(\theta^{N})^{2}(\theta_{r}^{k})^{2}+4\theta^{N}(\theta_{r}^{k})^{3}+(\theta_{r}^{k})^{4}\|_{L_{T}^{2}H_{\vec{x}}^{2}L_{\vec{w}}^{2}}+\|\mathcal{L}_{1}(f^{N},\theta^{N})\|_{L_{T}^{2}H_{\vec{x}}^{2}L_{\vec{w}}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|\mathcal{L}_{2}(f^{N},\theta^{N})\|_{L_{T}^{2}H_{\vec{x}}^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C(T)}{\varepsilon^{4}}(\|\theta_{r}^{k}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}}^{2}+\|\theta_{r}^{k}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}}^{4}+\varepsilon^{N+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C(T)}{\varepsilon^{4}}(\varepsilon^{2q}+\varepsilon^{N+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(T)(\varepsilon^{2q-4}+\varepsilon^{N-3}).

Assuming
2q-4>q
and
N-3>q
, i.e.
q>4
and
N>3+q
, the above inequality implies

(4.76)

\|f_{r}^{k+1}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{\vec{w}}^{2}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}}+\|\theta_{r}^{k+1}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{\vec{x}}^{2}}\leq C(T)(\varepsilon^{2m-4}+\varepsilon^{N-3})\leq\varepsilon^{q},

for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
. Thus we obtain that
(f_{r}^{k+1},\theta_{r}^{k+1})\in O_{q}
and therefore
\mathcal{T}
maps
O_{q}
into itself.

Next, we show the map
\mathcal{T}
is a contraction mapping. Let
\varphi^{k+1}=f_{r}^{k+1}-f_{r}^{k}
,
h^{k+1}=\theta_{r}^{k+1}-\theta_{r}^{k}
, then they satisfy

(4.77)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{t}\varphi^{k+1}+\varepsilon\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi^{k+1}+\varepsilon^{2}(\varphi^{k+1}-\overline{\varphi^{k+1}})+\varphi^{k+1}-4(\theta^{N})^{3}h^{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 6(\theta^{N})^{2}(\theta_{r}^{k}+\theta_{r}^{k-1})h^{k}+4\theta^{N}((\theta_{r}^{k})^{2}+\theta_{r}^{k}\theta_{r}^{k-1}+(\theta_{r}^{k-1})^{2})h^{k}+((\theta_{r}^{k})^{2}+(\theta_{r}^{k-1})^{2})(\theta_{r}^{k}+\theta_{r}^{k-1})h^{k},

(4.78)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{t}h^{k+1}+\varepsilon^{2}\mathrm{div}{(\vec{u}h^{k+1})}-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta_{x}h^{k+1}+4(\theta^{N})^{3}h^{k+1}-\overline{f_{r}^{k+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-6(\theta^{N})^{2}(\theta_{r}^{k}+\theta_{r}^{k-1})h^{k}-4\theta^{N}((\theta_{r}^{k})^{2}+\theta_{r}^{k}\theta_{r}^{k-1}+(\theta_{r}^{k-1})^{2})h^{k}-((\theta_{r}^{k})^{2}+(\theta_{r}^{k-1})^{2})(\theta_{r}^{k}+\theta_{r}^{k-1})h^{k},

with initial conditions

(4.79)

\varphi^{k+1}(0,\vec{x},\vec{w})=0,\ \ h^{k+1}(0,\vec{x})=0,\ \ \mathrm{for}\ \ (\vec{x},\vec{w})\in\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}.

Using (
4.32
) with
R_{1}=R_{2}=0
,
R=-6(\theta^{N})^{2}(\theta_{r}^{k}+\theta_{r}^{k-1})h^{k}-4\theta^{N}((\theta_{r}^{k})^{2}+\theta_{r}^{k}\theta_{r}^{k-1}+(\theta_{r}^{k-1})^{2})h^{k}-((\theta_{r}^{k})^{2}+(\theta_{r}^{k-1})^{2})(\theta_{r}^{k}+\theta_{r}^{k-1})h^{k}
, we obtain

(4.80)

\displaystyle\|\varphi^{k+1}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{\vec{w}}^{2}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}}+\|h^{k+1}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}}\leq C(T)\varepsilon^{q-4}\|h^{k}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}}\leq C(T)\varepsilon^{q-4}(\|\varphi^{k}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{\vec{w}}^{2}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}}+\|h^{k}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}}).

Assume
q>4
, then
C(T)\varepsilon^{q-4}<1
for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, the above inequality implies

(4.81)

\displaystyle\|\varphi^{k+1}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{\vec{w}}^{2}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}}+\|h^{k+1}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}}\leq C_{1}(\|\varphi^{k}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{\vec{w}}^{2}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}}+\|h^{k}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}}),

for some constant
0<C_{1}<1
. Therefore, for
q>4
and
N>3+q
,
\mathcal{T}
is a contraction mapping. By the Banach fixed point theorem, there exist a unique fixed point
(f_{r},\theta_{r})
such that
(f_{r},\theta_{r})=\mathcal{T}((f_{r},\theta_{r}))
. Therefore, there exists a unique solution to (
4.3
)-(
4.5
) in
O_{q}
.

Taking
q=5
and
N=9
, we can conclude that

(4.82)

\|f_{r}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{\vec{w}}^{2}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}}+\|\theta_{r}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}}\leq C\varepsilon^{5}.

In the following, we consider

(4.83)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{t}f_{r}+\varepsilon\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{r}+\varepsilon^{2}(f_{r}-\overline{f_{r}})+f_{r}=(\theta^{N}+\theta_{r})^{4}-(\theta^{N})^{4}-\mathcal{L}_{1}(f^{N},\theta^{N})\ \ \mathrm{in}\ \ (0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2},\\
&f_{r}(0,\vec{x},\vec{w})=0\ \ \mathrm{in}\ \ \mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

According to Lemma
2.1
, we have

(4.84)

\displaystyle\|f_{r}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{\vec{w}}^{\infty}L^{\infty}_{\vec{x}}}\leq

\displaystyle C\|(\theta^{N}+\theta_{r})^{4}-(\theta^{N})^{4}-\mathcal{L}_{1}(f^{N},\theta^{N})\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{\vec{w}}^{\infty}L^{\infty}_{\vec{x}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|\theta_{r}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{\infty}_{\vec{x}}}+\varepsilon^{N+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|\theta_{r}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}}+\varepsilon^{N+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{5}.

So,

(4.85)

\|f^{\varepsilon}-\sum_{k=0}^{9}(f_{k}+f_{I,k})\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{\vec{w}}^{\infty}L^{\infty}_{\vec{x}}}\\
\leq C\varepsilon^{5}

and

(4.86)

\|\theta^{\varepsilon}-\sum_{k=0}^{9}(\theta_{k}+\theta_{I,k})\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}}\\
\leq C\varepsilon^{5}.

Therefore, we derive

(4.87)

\|f^{\varepsilon}-\theta_{0}^{4}-f_{I,0}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{\vec{w}}^{\infty}L^{\infty}_{\vec{x}}}\\
\leq C\varepsilon

and

(4.88)

\|\theta^{\varepsilon}-\theta_{0}-\theta_{I,0}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{2}_{\vec{x}}}\\
\leq C\varepsilon.

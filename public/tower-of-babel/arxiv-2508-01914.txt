Random Operator-Valued Frames in Hilbert Spaces
James Tian

Abstract
. This paper develops a framework for operator-valued random variables on separable Hilbert spaces. Random operators are defined as measurable maps into the space of bounded operators, with measurability understood in the strong operator sense. Under a coercivity condition on i.i.d. random positive contractions, an iterative scheme is introduced and shown to converge both in mean square and almost surely. The limit is identified, and the resulting family of iterated random operators forms a random operator-valued frame. In the special case of random projections, the construction yields a Parseval-type identity.

1.
Introduction

Frames extend the idea of orthonormal bases in Hilbert spaces by allowing
redundancy. This builtin redundancy makes them stable and robust to
noise, erasures, and other errors. Because of these features, frames
have become an important tool in signal processing, image analysis,
coding theory, and wireless communications. The theory of frames began
with the work of Duffin and Schaeffer
[
DS52
]
and has since
developed in many directions; see
[
Chr16
]
or
[
Cas00
]

for surveys.

Two important extensions are fusion frames and operator-valued frames.
Fusion frames, introduced by Casazza et al.
[
CKL08
,
CK13
]
,
model distributed sensing and reconstruction using families of subspaces
with weights, and they have applications in coding theory, wireless
communication, and parallel processing. Operator-valued frames, developed
by Casazza, Han, and Larson and studied further by Han and Larson

[
HL00
]
and Sun
[
Sun06
]
, go further by replacing
vectors or subspaces with bounded operators. This connects frame theory
with
C^{*}
-algebras, dilation theory
[
SNF70
,
Pau02
]
,
and noncommutative harmonic analysis. Operator-valued frames also
appear in quantum information theory, for example in quantum state
tomography. For more recent developments there is a large and growing
literature covering many directions, which will not be surveyed here.

Randomization has become another fruitful direction. Random frames
and stochastic operator families have been studied for both theoretical
interest and applications in compressed sensing, randomized numerical
linear algebra, and stochastic signal analysis
[
ML19
,
KMR14
,
EO13
,
SV09
,
CRT06
,
Tro15
]
.
Random models make it possible to describe stability “in expectation”
or “almost surely,” rather than deterministically. This approach is
also natural in quantum information, where measurements and channels
are often random.

Random iterative methods highlight the power of this idea. The Randomized
Kaczmarz algorithm solves large linear systems by repeatedly projecting
onto hyperplanes defined by randomly chosen equations. It has important
uses in tomography and medical imaging. Similarly, Stochastic Gradient
Descent (SGD), the main algorithm behind large-scale machine learning,
updates estimates using randomly selected samples. Both approaches
show that stable solutions can emerge from simple randomized steps.

This paper develops an operator-theoretic version of that principle.
An iterative process is introduced based on a sequence of i.i.d. random
positive contractions, which generalizes the rank-one projections
used in the Kaczmarz method. The main theorem shows that this process
converges in mean square and almost surely, with the limit equal to
the identity operator. This gives a decomposition of any vector in
the space, and the operators in the process form a random operator-valued
frame.

In the special case where the random operators are projections, the
process yields a Parseval-type identity, so the family becomes a random
operator-valued Parseval frame. This connects the general
theory back to a well-known and highly useful special case.

A key step in the analysis is an inequality for positive contractions
in Hilbert spaces. While this inequality has a short direct proof,
the version given here uses a dilation argument (Sz.-Nagy and Foia

s

,

[
SNF70
]
), which is standard in operator theory and highlights
the underlying structure.

This work can be seen as a broad generalization of the Randomized
Kaczmarz method. Instead of rank-one projections, the iterative scheme
here uses i.i.d. random positive contractions, yet it achieves the
same type of convergence, both in mean square and almost surely. In
this sense, the results extend the principle behind the Randomized
Kaczmarz algorithm to the setting of operator-valued frames, showing
how stable representations can arise from simple randomized procedures.

It is well known that the Kaczmarz algorithm can be formulated entirely
in operator-theoretic terms, especially in the context of iterated
products of projections; see, e.g.,
[
Szw07
,
NS06
,
PRZ12
,
PR14
]
,
where the case of two projections was first established by von Neumann

[
vN50
]
. The situation becomes significantly more subtle when
considering arbitrary products of more than two projections, or of
noncommuting bounded operators more generally. Questions of convergence,
stability, and rate estimates depend heavily on geometric and spectral
properties. In a way, the present paper extends this line of work
to a randomized setting, where stability and convergence arise not
from commutativity but from probabilistic averaging.

2.
Preliminaries

This section collects the basic definitions and assumptions used throughout
the paper. The aim is to recall the necessary background on frames
and their generalizations, introduce the framework of operator-valued
random variables, and highlight the class of operators that will play
a central role in the main results.

Let
H
be a separable Hilbert space. The physics convention is adopted:
the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is linear in the second
argument and conjugate linear in the first. Let
B\left(H,K\right)

denote the space of all bounded operators from
H
to
K
, and write

B\left(H\right)
when
H=K
.

An operator
T\in B(H)
is called a positive contraction if
0\leq T\leq I
,
that is,
T
is selfadjoint, positive, and its operator norm satisfies

\left\|T\right\|\leq 1
. Positive contractions play a central
role in the analysis below, since the iterative process studied in
Section
3
will be built from i.i.d. random positive contractions.

Frames and generalizations

The concept of a frame extends the notion of an orthonormal basis
by allowing redundancy while still permitting stable reconstruction.
This redundancy is particularly valuable in applications where robustness
against noise, erasures, or other errors is required. Over time, frames
have been generalized in several directions, two of the most important
being fusion frames and operator-valued frames.

Definition 1

(Frame)
.

A sequence
\left\{f_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}\subset H
is called
a frame for
H
if there exist constants
A,B>0
such that

A\left\|x\right\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i\in I}\left|\left\langle f_{i},x\right\rangle\right|^{2}\leq B\left\|x\right\|^{2},\quad x\in H.

The constants
A
and
B
are called the frame bounds. If
A=B=1
,
it is called a Parseval frame.

Frames generalize orthonormal bases by allowing redundancy while preserving
stable reconstruction. A natural extension, motivated by distributed
sensing and parallel processing, is given by fusion frames.

Definition 2

(Fusion frame)
.

A family
\left\{(W_{i},v_{i})\right\}_{i\in I}
,
where
W_{i}\subset H
are closed subspaces and
v_{i}>0
are weights,
is called a fusion frame for
H
if there exist constants
A,B>0

such that

A\left\|x\right\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i\in I}v^{2}_{i}\left\|P_{W_{i}}x\right\|^{2}\leq B\left\|x\right\|^{2},\quad x\in H

where
P_{W_{i}}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto
W_{i}
.

Fusion frames move beyond individual vectors to weighted subspaces.
An even more general framework is provided by operator-valued frames,
which replace subspaces with bounded operators.

Definition 3

(Operator-valued frame)
.

Let
\left\{T_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}\subset B(H,K)
, where
K

is another Hilbert space. The family
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is called
an operator-valued frame (or g-frame) for
H
if there exist constants

A,B>0
such that

A\left\|x\right\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i\in I}\left\|T_{i}x\right\|^{2}\leq B\left\|x\right\|^{2},\quad x\in H.

If
A=B=1
, it is called a Parseval operator-valued frame.

Operator-valued frames generalize both classical and
fusion frames and provide a natural link between frame theory, operator
algebras, and dilation theory.

Operator-valued random variables

Let
\left(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P}\right)
be a probability
space. An operator-valued random variable is a measurable map
\Psi\colon\Omega\rightarrow B\left(H\right)
.
Measurability is understood in the strong operator sense: for each
fixed
x\in H
, the mapping

\omega\mapsto\Psi\left(\omega\right)x

is measurable as an
H
-valued function. This convention avoids the
difficulties arising from norm-measurability in infinite-dimensional
settings.

The distribution of the random operator
\Psi
is given by the pushforward
measure

\mu_{\Psi}\coloneqq\mathbb{P}\circ\Psi^{-1},

defined on the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of
B\left(H\right)
associated
with the strong operator topology.

To ensure the existence of expectations, a square-integrability condition
is imposed: for every
x\in H
,

\mathbb{E}\left\|\Psi x\right\|^{2}\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\left\|\Psi\left(\omega\right)x\right\|^{2}d\mathbb{P}\left(\omega\right)<\infty.

Thus, the operator

M=\int_{\Omega}\Psi^{*}\Psi\,d\mathbb{P}

defined via

\left\langle x,Mx\right\rangle=\mathbb{E}\left\|\Psi x\right\|^{2}

for all
x\in H
, is bounded, positive, and selfadjoint.

3.
Main results

This section presents the main convergence results for the iterative
scheme built from i.i.d. random positive contractions. The analysis
begins with a fundamental estimate for positive contractions, which
forms the basis of the iterative argument.

The following lemma is a key ingredient for the main results, as it
provides the estimates needed in the iterative argument. Although
a short direct proof can be found in the literature (see e.g.,
[
NS06
]

and related references), a dilation argument is included here instead,
as it is concise and serves as a standard tool in operator theory.

Lemma 4
.

Let
T
be a positive contraction in a Hilbert space

H
, that is,
0\leq T\leq I
. Then,

(3.1)

\left\|Tx\right\|^{2}_{H}+\left\|x-Tx\right\|^{2}_{H}\leq\left\|x\right\|^{2}_{H},\quad x\in H.

Proof.

Let
T=W^{*}PW
be a dilation of
T
, where
W\colon H\rightarrow L

is an isometric embedding of
H
into some Hilbert space
L
, and

P
is a selfadjoint projection in
L
. Then, for all
x\in H
,

\displaystyle\text{l.h.s}_{\eqref{eq:1}}

\displaystyle=\left\|W^{*}PWx\right\|^{2}_{H}+\left\|W^{*}\left(I_{L}-P\right)Wx\right\|^{2}_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|PWx\right\|^{2}_{L}+\left\|\left(I_{L}-P\right)Wx\right\|^{2}_{L}=\left\|Wx\right\|^{2}_{L}=\left\|x\right\|^{2}_{H}.

∎

The next theorem applies the estimate from Lemma
4
to a
sequence i.i.d. random operators, taking values in positive contractions.
The result guarantees both mean-square convergence and almost sure
convergence of the associated iterative scheme. In addition, it identifies
the limiting operator identity and establishes frame bounds.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\Psi\colon\Omega\rightarrow B\left(H\right)
be
a random variable, taking values in positive contractions in
B\left(H\right)
.
Assume that there exists a constant
0<C<1
such that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|\Psi x\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq C\left\|x\right\|^{2},\quad x\in H

which is equivalent to

\int_{\Omega}\Psi^{*}\Psi\,d\mathbb{P}\geq CI.

Let
\left\{\Psi_{i}\right\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be an i.i.d. sample
of
\Psi
.

(1)

For all
x\in H
,

(3.3)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left\|x-\sum^{n}_{k=1}\Psi_{k}\left(I-\Psi_{k-1}\right)\cdots\left(I-\Psi_{1}\right)x\right\|^{2}=0,

i.e., convergence in
L^{2}\left(\Omega,H\right)
.

(2)

The following operator identity holds:

(3.4)

I=\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\Psi_{k}\left(I-\Psi_{k-1}\right)\cdots\left(I-\Psi_{1}\right)\quad a.s.

Equivalently, for all
x\in H
,

(3.5)

x=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum^{n}_{k=1}\Psi_{k}\left(I-\Psi_{k-1}\right)\cdots\left(I-\Psi_{1}\right)x\quad a.s.

(3)

For all
x\in H
,

(3.6)

C\left\|x\right\|^{2}\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum^{n}_{k=1}\left\|\Psi_{k}\left(I-\Psi_{k-1}\right)\cdots\left(I-\Psi_{1}\right)x\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\left\|x\right\|^{2}.

That is, the set

\left\{\Psi_{n}\left(I-\Psi_{n-1}\right)\cdots\left(I-\Psi_{1}\right)\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

forms a random operator-valued frame.

Proof.

Let
\left\{\Psi_{j}\right\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be an i.i.d. sample
of
\Psi
. Note that

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{1}x+\left(I-\Psi_{1}\right)x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{1}x+\Psi_{2}\left(I-\Psi_{1}\right)x+\left(I-\Psi_{2}\right)\left(I-\Psi_{1}\right)x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{k=1}\Psi_{k}\left(I-\Psi_{k-1}\right)\cdots\left(I-\Psi_{1}\right)x+R_{n}x

where

\displaystyle R_{n}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{cases}\left(I-\Psi_{n}\right)\cdots\left(I-\Psi_{1}\right)&n\geq 1\\
I&n=0\end{cases}

Let
\mathbb{E}_{n-1}\left[\cdot\mid\Psi_{1},\dots,\Psi_{n-1}\right]

denote the conditional expectation given
\Psi_{j}
,
j=1,\dots n-1
.
Since

R_{n}x=R_{n-1}x-\Psi_{n}R_{n-1}x

it follows that

\mathbb{E}_{n-1}\left\|R_{n}x\right\|^{2}=\mathbb{E}_{n-1}\left\|R_{n-1}x-\Psi_{n}R_{n-1}x\right\|^{2}.

An application of Lemma
4
to
\Psi_{n}
gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{n-1}\left\|R_{n}x\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{n-1}\left(\left\|R_{n-1}x\right\|^{2}-\left\|\Psi_{n}R_{n-1}x\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\|R_{n-1}x\right\|^{2}-\mathbb{E}_{n-1}\left\|\Psi_{n}R_{n-1}x\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|R_{n-1}x\right\|^{2}-C\left\|R_{n-1}x\right\|^{2}\quad\text{by assumption \eqref{eq:1-1}}

\displaystyle=\left\|R_{n-1}x\right\|^{2}\left(1-C\right).

By induction,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|R_{n}x\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}_{n-1}\left\|R_{n}x\right\|^{2}\right]

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left\|R_{n-1}x\right\|^{2}\left(1-C\right)\leq\left\|x\right\|^{2}\left(1-C\right)^{n}

which converges to 0 as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore, (
3.3
)
follows:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left\|x-\sum^{n}_{k=1}\Psi_{k}\left(I-\Psi_{k-1}\right)\cdots\left(I-\Psi_{1}\right)x\right\|^{2}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left\|R_{n}x\right\|^{2}=0.

On the other hand, for any
\delta>0
,

P\left(\left\|R_{n}x\right\|>\delta\right)\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\left\|R_{n}x\right\|^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\leq\frac{\left\|x\right\|^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\left(1-C\right)^{n}\;\text{by \eqref{eq:a7},}

so that

\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}P\left(\left\|R_{n}x\right\|>\delta\right)\leq\frac{\left\|x\right\|^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\frac{1}{C}<\infty.

By the Borel-Cantelli lemma,
\left\|R_{n}x\right\|\rightarrow 0

almost surely. The assertions (
3.4
)–(
3.5
) follow
from this.

Finally, to prove (
3.6
), recall that
\Psi
takes values
in positive contractions. Applying (
4
) repeatedly gives

(3.8)

\displaystyle\left\|x\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi_{1}x\right\|^{2}+\left\|\left(I-\Psi_{1}\right)x\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi_{1}x\right\|^{2}+\left\|\Psi_{2}\left(I-\Psi_{1}\right)x\right\|^{2}+\left\|\left(I-\Psi_{2}\right)\left(I-\Psi_{1}\right)x\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{k=1}\left\|\Psi_{k}\left(I-\Psi_{k-1}\right)\cdots\left(I-\Psi_{1}\right)x\right\|^{2}+\left\|R_{n}x\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{k=1}\left\|\Psi_{k}\left(I-\Psi_{k-1}\right)\cdots\left(I-\Psi_{1}\right)x\right\|^{2}

Taking expectation and using (
3.2
) on
\Psi_{1}
:

\left\|x\right\|^{2}\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum^{n}_{k=1}\left\|\Psi_{k}\left(I-\Psi_{k-1}\right)\cdots\left(I-\Psi_{1}\right)x\right\|^{2}\right]\geq\underset{\text{by }\eqref{eq:1-1}}{\underbrace{\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\Psi_{1}x\right\|^{2}\geq C\left\|x\right\|^{2}}},\quad\forall n.

Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
gives (
3.6
).
∎

The general result specializes in a clean way when the random operators
are selfadjoint projections. In this case, the iterative scheme produces
a random operator-valued Parseval frame.

Corollary 6
.

Suppose
\Psi\colon\Omega\rightarrow B\left(H\right)
takes values
in selfadjoint projections in
B\left(H\right)
. Then the identities

(
3.3
)
–
(
3.5
)
still hold true. Moreover,
for all
x\in H
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum^{n}_{k=1}\left\|\Psi_{k}\left(I-\Psi_{k-1}\right)\cdots\left(I-\Psi_{1}\right)x\right\|^{2}\right]=\left\|x\right\|^{2}.

That is, the set

\left\{\Psi_{n}\left(I-\Psi_{n-1}\right)\cdots\left(I-\Psi_{1}\right)\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

forms a random operator-valued Parseval frame.

Proof.

For projections, the inequality (
3.1
) is replaced by an equality.
Similarly, (
3.8
) becomes

\left\|x\right\|^{2}=\sum^{n}_{k=1}\left\|\Psi_{k}\left(I-\Psi_{k-1}\right)\cdots\left(I-\Psi_{1}\right)x\right\|^{2}+\left\|R_{n}x\right\|^{2}

and so

\left\|x\right\|^{2}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum^{n}_{k=1}\left\|\Psi_{k}\left(I-\Psi_{k-1}\right)\cdots\left(I-\Psi_{1}\right)x\right\|^{2}\right]+\underset{=0}{\underbrace{\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left\|R_{n}x\right\|^{2}}}

∎

As an application, it follows that every fusion frame naturally induces
a random operator-valued Parseval frame. The construction uses the
fusion frame subspaces as the possible outcomes of a discrete random
operator, with the fusion weights providing the probability distribution.

Corollary 7
.

Let
\left\{(W_{i},v_{i})\right\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}

be a fusion frame (see Definition
2
), so that

A\left\|x\right\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}v^{2}_{i}\left\|P_{W_{i}}x\right\|^{2}\leq B\left\|x\right\|^{2},\quad x\in H

where
P_{W_{i}}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto
W_{i}
.

Assume further that the weights
\left\{v_{i}\right\}
form a probability
distribution, i.e.,
\sum v^{2}_{i}=1
. Let
\Psi\colon\Omega\rightarrow B\left(H\right)

be a discrete random operator, taking values in
\left\{P_{W_{i}}\right\}
,
such that

\mathbb{P}\left(\Psi=P_{W_{i}}\right)=v^{2}_{i}

Let
\left\{\Psi_{i}\right\}
be an i.i.d. sample of
\Psi
, and
set

T_{k}\coloneqq\Psi_{k}\left(I-\Psi_{k-1}\right)\cdots\left(I-\Psi_{1}\right),\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

Then, for all
x\in H
,

x=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum^{n}_{k=1}T_{k}x\quad a.s.

Moreover,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum^{n}_{k=1}\left\|T_{k}x\right\|^{2}\right]=\left\|x\right\|^{2},\quad x\in H.

In other words, the family
\left\{T_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

forms a random operator-valued Parseval frame.

Proof.

Since
\Psi
is supported on projections and the weights
\left\{v_{i}\right\}

define a probability distribution, one has

\mathbb{E}\left\|\Psi x\right\|^{2}=\sum v^{2}_{i}\left\|P_{W_{i}}x\right\|^{2},\quad x\in H.

By the fusion frame assumption, this expectation satisfies

\mathbb{E}\left\|\Psi x\right\|^{2}\geq A\left\|x\right\|^{2},\quad x\in H,

so the coercivity condition (
3.2
) holds with the constant

C
equal to the lower fusion frame bound
A
. Applying Theorem
5

then yields the desired conclusions.
∎

Remark 8
.

The operator-valued decomposition in Corollary
7
provides a
randomized refinement of the original fusion frame expansion. In the
standard deterministic setting, a fusion frame
\left\{(W_{i},v_{i})\right\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}

allows for the reconstruction of any vector
x\in H
via a global
synthesis operator involving the projections
P_{W_{i}}
. The construction
above instead realizes this reconstruction through an adaptive, path-dependent
sequence of operator applications: at each step, a projection
P_{W_{i}}

is selected randomly (according to its weight
v^{2}_{i}
), and the
residual vector is updated accordingly.

The limiting behavior of this scheme is expressed through two operator
identities:

(1)

\mathbb{E}\left[\sum\nolimits^{\infty}_{k=1}T^{*}_{k}T_{k}\right]=I
,
in expectation;

(2)

I=\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}T_{k}
, a.s. in the strong operator topology.

Together, these relations yield a Parseval-type construction: the
identity operator is recovered both in expectation and almost surely.
Thus, the original fusion frame gives rise to a random operator-valued
Parseval frame, where the frame elements emerge dynamically from the
underlying probability distribution. In this sense, Theorem
5

may be viewed as a natural generalization of
[
JST20
]
.

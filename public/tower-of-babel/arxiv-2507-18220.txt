Sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics with library optimization mechanism: Recursive long-term prediction perspective
Ansei Yonezawa, Heisei Yonezawa, Shuichi Yahagi, Itsuro Kajiwara, Shinya Kijimoto, Hikaru Taniuchi, Kentaro Murakami

Abstract
The sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics (SINDy) approach can discover the governing equations of dynamical systems based on measurement data, where the dynamical model is identified as the sparse linear combination of the given basis functions. A major challenge in SINDy is the design of a library, which is a set of candidate basis functions, as the appropriate library is not trivial for many dynamical systems. To overcome this difficulty, this study proposes SINDy with library optimization mechanism (SINDy-LOM), which is a combination of the sparse regression technique and the novel learning strategy of the library. In the proposed approach, the basis functions are parametrized. The SINDy-LOM approach involves a two-layer optimization architecture: the inner-layer, in which the data-driven model is extracted as the sparse linear combination of the candidate basis functions, and the outer-layer, in which the basis functions are optimized from the viewpoint of the recursive long-term (RLT) prediction accuracy; thus, the library design is reformulated as the optimization of the parametrized basis functions. The resulting SINDy-LOM model has good interpretability and usability, as the proposed approach yields the parsimonious model. The library optimization mechanism significantly reduces user burden. The RLT perspective improves the reliability of the resulting model compared with the traditional SINDy approach that can only ensure the one-step-ahead prediction accuracy. The validity of the proposed approach is demonstrated by applying it to a diesel engine airpath system, which is a well-known complex industrial system.

Related work: algorithmic progress in SINDy

The original SINDy approach

24

Discovering governing laws using noisy data is a major challenge in data-driven methods including SINDy. Although the SINDy approach has intrinsic noise robustness owing to sparse regression

The symbolic regression framework including SINDy can identify the closed-form model. This feature is advantageous not only for interpretability but also for the ease of incorporating various types of prior knowledge about the system into the learning process and the resulting model. For example, Lagrangian-SINDy integrates the knowledge of classical mechanics
[

Notably, the selection of the library plays a critical role in the prediction accuracy and simplicity of the resulting model
[

]

This is because the SINDy approach strongly relies on the assumption that the dynamical behavior of the system to be identified can be well represented by the linear combination of a few basis functions in the library. A typical choice for the basis functions is elementary functions that can construct universal approximators (e.g., polynomials, trigonometric functions). Nevertheless, the
sparse
linear combination of elementary functions does not necessarily capture the dynamic behavior accurately. If the prior knowledge of the system is available mathematically, the basis functions may be chosen according to the prior knowledge

Remark 1
.

Despite its importance, the selection of the library has not been explored sufficiently for general dynamical systems, compared with the remarkable progress in the aforementioned SINDy framework. Development of the library design mechanism can enhance the performance and usability of the SINDy method, thereby contributing toward the widespread use of data-driven techniques in many fields and promoting data-driven science and engineering.

I-C

Contribution and novelty

This study proposes SINDy with library optimization mechanism (SINDy-LOM). The core idea of the proposed approach is to reformulate the library design problem for SINDy into a two-layer optimization architecture. In the inner layer, sparse regression is executed using the parametrized library to distill the SINDy model. The parametrized library is a set of the basis functions with tunable parameters. In the outer layer, the tunable parameters are optimized on the basis of the recursive long-term (RLT) prediction accuracy of the resulting SINDy model. Consequently, the SINDy-LOM approach performs data-driven modeling via SINDy and improvement of the library simultaneously. Sparse regression realizes the parsimonious data-driven dynamical model in the closed form, contributing toward the interpretability and generalizability of the resulting model. The library optimization strategy provides good basis functions that can capture the nonlinear dynamical behavior. The optimization-based learning approach alleviates the tedious trial-and-error-based library design. As the library is optimized on the basis of the RLT prediction accuracy, the data-driven model provided by the proposed approach achieves reliable prediction, which is indispensable for practical applications such as prediction model for model predictive control (MPC). Note that the accuracy of the RLT prediction has been overlooked in the field of SINDy. The validity of the proposed approach is demonstrated through the identification problem of a diesel engine airpath system, which is a challenging system in practice owing to its strong nonlinearity and coupling.

The contribution and novelty of this study are summarized as follows:

(1)

(
Parsimonious model
) The proposed approach discovers the data-driven model as the sparse linear combination of the basis functions. Therefore, the resulting model is interpretable and generalizable in the same manner as the traditional SINDy framework.

(2)

(
Reliability
) Owing to the library learning mechanism, the proposed approach provides good basis functions for capturing the dynamical behavior of the system accurately. The learning criterion is the accuracy of the RLT prediction. Therefore, compared with the conventional SINDy approach that requires a predetermined library and considers only the one-step-ahead prediction error, the proposed approach can distill the reliable data-driven model and achieve accurate RLT prediction.

(3)

(
Reduced user burden
) The basis functions must be manually determined by users in the traditional SINDy approach. Finding an appropriate library is costly, time-consuming, and often nontrivial in many applications. By contrast, the proposed approach automatically designs the appropriate library, thereby reducing user burden.

(4)

(
Generality
) Previous studies focusing on the library design strongly rely on prior knowledge and insights regarding the system to be modeled (e.g.,
[
35
,
37
,
45
]
). Meanwhile, the proposed approach is applicable not only to systems for which it is easy to obtain prior knowledge but also to various systems for which it is difficult to obtain prior knowledge. Therefore, this study provides the library design strategy for the SINDy method for general dynamical systems. To the best of our knowledge, the automatic design of the library has not been addressed in the SINDy framework for general systems.

In summary, this study enables us to obtain interpretable, tractable, and reliable data-driven models for a wide variety of dynamical systems, thereby promoting the data-driven approach for solving various problems involving dynamical systems.

Data-driven model discovery via sparse regression

Let

x\left({k+1}\right)=f\left({x\left({k}\right),w\left({k}\right)}\right)

(1)

II-B

Challenges in the SINDy method

Although the SINDy method described in Section
II-A
is a powerful approach for finding an interpretable data-driven dynamical model, there remain several challenges to be addressed.

First, the design policy of the library
\mathrm{\Theta}\left({x\left({k}\right),w\left({k}\right)}\right)
is unclear. The selection of the basis functions

\mathrm{\theta}_{1}\left({x\left({k}\right),w\left({k}\right)}\right),\mathrm{\theta}_{2}\left({x\left({k}\right),w\left({k}\right)}\right),\ldots,\mathrm{\theta}_{p}\left({x\left({k}\right),w\left({k}\right)}\right)
plays a critical role in SINDy, as SINDy assumes that the dynamical behavior of the system can be well captured by the sparse linear combination of the basis functions. In other words, in the SINDy method, the identification problem of the nonlinear dynamics is reformulated into the computation of the sparse coefficient vectors
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots,\xi_{n}
of the
predetermined
basis functions. Although prior knowledge of the system provides some insights into the basis functions depending on the application (e.g., aircraft system
[
35
]
, chemical reaction
[
37
]
, friction
[
45
]
), such informative knowledge is often difficult to access in many practical situations. If the prior knowledge is not available, we may employ elementary functions that can construct universal approximators such as polynomials
[
25
]
. However, the dynamical behaviors are not necessarily well represented as their
sparse
linear combination. Moreover, we should not include too many basis functions in the library, as an excessively large library makes the sparse regression ill-conditioned
[
39
]
. The selection of the basis functions requires a careful trial-and-error approach by the users. To the best of our knowledge, a quantitative design policy of the library for general dynamical systems is not fully established.

The second challenge is that the sparse regression (
7
) does not consider the RLT prediction accuracy of the resultant model (
6
). Specifically, the data-driven model (
6
) obtained via solving (
7
) minimizes the one-step-ahead prediction error
J_{os}
under sparsity promotion:

J_{os}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert{\check{E}_{i,\star}^{D}}\right\rVert_{{2}}

(8)

\check{E}_{i,\star}^{D}\triangleq\check{X}_{i,\star}^{D}-X_{i,\star}^{D}

(9)

\check{X}^{D}\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}{\check{x}^{D}}\left({1}\right)&{\check{x}^{D}}\left({2}\right)&\cdots&{\check{x}^{D}}\left({N}\right)\\
\end{bmatrix}

(10)

\check{x}^{D}\left({k+1}\right)=\left(\mathrm{\Theta}\left({x^{D}\left({k}\right),w^{D}\left({k}\right)}\right)\varXi\right)^{\top}.

(11)

Equation (
8
) implies that the sparse regression (
7
) minimizes the error between the true data
x^{D}\left({k+1}\right)
and its one-step-ahead prediction
\check{x}^{D}\left({k+1}\right)
, computed from the true data
x^{D}\left({k}\right)
and
w^{D}\left({k}\right)
using (
11
). However, this formulation cannot maintain the accuracy of the RLT prediction. In other words, the RLT prediction at the
\left(k+\tau\right)
-th time step (
\tau=2,3,\ldots
) obtained from
x^{D}\left({k}\right)
and
w^{D}\left({k}\right)
will be inaccurate owing to the propagation of the prediction errors through recurrent use of the model. The inaccuracy of the RLT prediction is problematic in many applications such as prediction model for MPC.

Remark 3
.

The inaccuracy of the RLT prediction owing to the recurrent use of the model has been highlighted in the context of the Koopman operator framework
[
51
]
. Nevertheless, to the best of our knowledge, the RLT prediction accuracy has been overlooked in the context of SINDy
[
52
]
.

III
Proposed approach

This section presents the proposed approach, namely SINDy-LOM, which consists of library parametrization and model discovery via the two-layer optimization architecture. Equation (
1
) represents the dynamical system to be identified by the proposed approach.

III-A

Parametrized library

To address the challenges in library design, we propose the use of the parametrized library. In particular, in the SINDy-LOM strategy, the library contains the basis functions

\mathrm{\theta}_{1}\left({x\left({k}\right),w\left({k}\right);\phi_{1}}\right),\mathrm{\theta}_{2}\left({x\left({k}\right),w\left({k}\right);\phi_{2}}\right),\ldots,\mathrm{\theta}_{p}\left({x\left({k}\right),w\left({k}\right);\phi_{p}}\right)
,
where
\mathrm{\theta}_{i}\left({x\left({k}\right),w\left({k}\right);\phi_{i}}\right)
implies that the function
\theta_{i}
has the tunable parameter
\phi_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{i}}
. Then, the parametrized library is expressed as

\mathrm{\Theta}\left({x\left({k}\right),w\left({k}\right);\varPhi}\right)\\
\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{\theta}_{1}\left({x,w;\phi_{1}}\right)&\mathrm{\theta}_{2}\left({x,w;\phi_{2}}\right)&\cdots&\mathrm{\theta}_{p}\left({x,w;\phi_{p}}\right)\end{bmatrix}

(12)

where
\mathit{\phi}
represents a parameter vector with the all elements of
\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\ldots,\phi_{p}
in its entries. The tuning parameters
\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\ldots,\phi_{p}
are optimized on the basis of the RLT prediction accuracy as discussed in Section
III-B
. Optimizing the tuning parameters of the basis functions corresponds to automatically designing the appropriate basis functions capturing the dynamical behavior of the system. Therefore, searching for the values of
\varPhi
results in learning the appropriate library.

Various types of functions can be employed for
\mathrm{\theta}_{i}\left({x\left({k}\right),w\left({k}\right);\phi_{i}}\right)
; an elementary function that can build universal approximators is suitable to construct the parametrized library. For example, the polynomials

\prod_{i,j}\left(x_{i}\left({k}\right)-\eta_{i}\right)^{\alpha_{i}}\left(w_{j}\left({k}\right)-\mu_{j}\right)^{\beta_{j}}

with the tunable parameter
\phi=\begin{bmatrix}\eta_{1}&\cdots&\eta_{n}&\mu_{1}&\cdots&\mu_{m}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}

corresponds to the Taylor approximation around
\phi
. Moreover, the library can be partially parametrized:

\mathrm{\Theta}\left({x\left({k}\right),w\left({k}\right);\varPhi}\right)=\begin{bmatrix}\theta_{1}&\cdots&\theta_{p_{d}}&\theta_{p_{d}+1}&\ldots&\theta_{p}\end{bmatrix}
,
where
\mathrm{\theta}_{1}\left({x\left({k}\right),w\left({k}\right)}\right),\ldots\mathrm{\theta}_{p_{d}}\left({x\left({k}\right),w\left({k}\right)}\right)
are fixed and

\mathrm{\theta}_{p_{d}+1}\left({x\left({k}\right),w\left({k}\right);\phi_{1}}\right),\ldots\mathrm{\theta}_{p}\left({x\left({k}\right),w\left({k}\right);\phi_{p-p_{d}}}\right)

are parametrized and optimized.

Remark 4
.

In many applications, the adjustable parameters for the basis functions must be carefully selected for the SINDy approach to succeed, even if the specific type of the basis functions is known via
a priori
knowledge of the system (e.g., the activation energy of a chemical process
[
37
]
). The proposed approach can be used to search for the appropriate parameter values of such
a priori
known basis functions.

III-B

Two-layer optimization architecture: SINDy-LOM

We propose the data-driven parsimonious discovery technique based on sparse regression and library optimization, namely the SINDy-LOM approach. Here, it is assumed that the data set
\mathcal{X}^{SR}=\left\{{x}^{SR}\left(k\right)\right\}_{k={0}}^{{N^{SR}}}
,
\mathcal{W}^{SR}=\left\{{w}^{SR}\left(k\right)\right\}_{k={0}}^{{N^{SR}}}
and the data sets
\mathcal{X}^{LL_{i}}=\left\{{x}^{LL_{i}}\left(k\right)\right\}_{k={0}}^{{N_{i}^{LL}}}
,
\mathcal{W}^{LL_{i}}=\left\{{w}^{LL_{i}}\left(k\right)\right\}_{k={0}}^{{N_{i}^{LL}}}
(
i=1,2,\ldots,M
) are collected, where
N^{SR}
and
N_{i}^{LL}
denote the numbers of the sampling points of
\left(\mathcal{X}^{SR},\mathcal{W}^{SR}\right)
and
\left(\mathcal{X}^{LL_{i}},\mathcal{W}^{LL_{i}}\right)
, respectively. Here,
\mathcal{X}^{SR}
and
\mathcal{W}^{SR}
are used for sparse regression, whereas the library is learned using
\mathcal{X}^{LL_{i}}
and
\mathcal{W}^{LL_{i}}
. Then, we formulate the library learning and discovery of the parsimonious data-driven dynamical model as the following optimization problem:

\varPhi^{\ast}=\underset{\varPhi}{\operatorname{arg\,min}}\,J_{ms}\left({\varPhi}\right)

(13)

J_{ms}\left({\varPhi}\right)\\
\triangleq\frac{1}{QR}\sum_{i=1}^{M}q_{i}\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{i}^{LL}}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}\frac{\left\lVert{\hat{E}_{j,\star}^{LL_{i}}}\right\rVert_{{2}}}{\left\lVert{X_{j,\star}^{LL_{i}}}\right\rVert_{{2}}}\right\}+\kappa\left\lVert{\mathit{\Xi}}\right\rVert_{{0}}

(14)

where

\hat{E}_{j,\star}^{LL_{i}}\triangleq\hat{X}^{LL_{i}}-X^{LL_{i}}

(15)

\hat{X}^{LL_{i}}\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}{\hat{x}^{LL_{i}}}\left({0}\right)&{\hat{x}^{LL_{i}}}\left({1}\right)&\cdots&{\hat{x}^{LL_{i}}}\left({N_{i}^{LL}-1}\right)\\
\end{bmatrix}

(16)

X^{LL_{i}}\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}{x^{LL_{i}}}\left({0}\right)&{x^{LL_{i}}}\left({1}\right)&\cdots&{x^{LL_{i}}}\left({N_{i}^{LL}-1}\right)\\
\end{bmatrix}

(17)

\hat{x}^{LL_{i}}\left({k+1}\right)=\left(\mathrm{\Theta}\left({\hat{x}^{LL_{i}}\left({k}\right),w^{LL_{i}}\left({k}\right);\varPhi}\right)\varXi\right)^{\top}

(18)

\hat{x}^{LL_{i}}\left({0}\right)=x^{LL_{i}}\left({0}\right)

(19)

\varXi=\begin{bmatrix}{\xi}_{{1}}&{\xi}_{{2}}&\cdots&{\xi}_{{n}}\\
\end{bmatrix}

(20)

\xi_{j}=\underset{v\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}{\operatorname{arg\,min}}\,\left\lVert{\mathrm{\Theta}\left({X^{SR},W^{SR};\varPhi}\right)v-\left(X_{j,\star}^{SR+}\right)^{\top}}\right\rVert_{{2}}\\
+\mathcal{R}\left({v}\right)

(21)

X^{SR}\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}{x^{SR}}\left({0}\right)&{x^{SR}}\left({1}\right)&\cdots&{x^{SR}}\left({N^{SR}-1}\right)\\
\end{bmatrix}

(22)

X^{SR+}\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}{x^{SR}}\left({1}\right)&{x^{SR}}\left({2}\right)&\cdots&{x^{SR}}\left({N^{SR}}\right)\\
\end{bmatrix}

(23)

W^{SR}\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}{w^{SR}}\left({0}\right)&{w^{SR}}\left({1}\right)&\cdots&{w^{SR}}\left({N^{SR}-1}\right)\\
\end{bmatrix}

(24)

\mathrm{\Theta}\left({X^{SR},W^{SR};\varPhi}\right)\\
\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{\Theta}\left({x^{SR}\left({0}\right),w^{SR}\left({0}\right);\varPhi}\right)\\
\mathrm{\Theta}\left({x^{SR}\left({1}\right),w^{SR}\left({1}\right);\varPhi}\right)\\
\vdots\\
\mathrm{\Theta}\left({x^{SR}\left({N^{SR}-1}\right),w^{SR}\left({N^{SR}-1}\right);\varPhi}\right)\\
\end{bmatrix}

(25)

and
\mathcal{R}\left({v}\right)
is some suitable sparsity-promoting regularizer. The term
\kappa\left\lVert{\varXi}\right\rVert_{{0}}
is a sparsity-promoting term for optimizing the library, where
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\left\{0\right\}
; it facilitates the search for the appropriate library yielding the parsimonious model. In (
14
),
q_{i}
and
r_{j}
are positive constant weights and
Q\triangleq\sum_{i}q_{i}
and
R\triangleq\sum_{j}r_{i}

\mathcal{X}^{LL_{i}}
by adjusting

;

specifies the relative importance of the RLT prediction accuracy for the
j

x
. Note that
q_{i}
and

can be freely chosen by a user according to the design specification of the resulting model.

Finally, the data-driven model is obtained as

x\left({k+1}\right)=\left(\mathrm{\Theta}\left({x\left({k}\right),w\left({k}\right);\mathit{\Phi}^{\ast}}\right)\varXi^{\ast}\right)^{\top}

(26)

\varXi^{\ast}=\begin{bmatrix}{\xi^{\ast}}_{{1}}&{\xi^{\ast}}_{{2}}&\cdots&{\xi^{\ast}}_{{n}}\\
\end{bmatrix}

(27)

\xi_{j}^{\ast}=\underset{v\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}{\operatorname{arg\,min}}\,\left\lVert{\mathrm{\Theta}\left({X^{SR},W^{SR};\varPhi^{\ast}}\right)v-\left(X_{j,\star}^{SR+}\right)^{\top}}\right\rVert_{{2}}\\
+\mathcal{R}\left({v}\right).

(28)

The optimization problem (
13
) has a two-layer architecture: the library optimization is conducted in the outer layer problem (
13
), whereas the data-driven model, given by
\varPhi
and evaluated in (
13
), is obtained via sparse regression (
21
). The loss function
J_{ms}\left({\varPhi}\right)
is constructed on the basis of
\hat{x}^{LL_{i}}\left({k}\right)
, which is the
k

x^{LL_{i}}\left({k}\right)
. Note that
\hat{x}^{LL_{i}}\left({k}\right)
is obtained by using the data-driven model recurrently from the initial condition data
x^{LL_{i}}\left({0}\right)
, as described in (
18
) and (
19
). Therefore, solving (
13
) yields the appropriate library considering the RLT prediction accuracy. The coefficients
\varXi
of the basis functions in the model (

) to be validated are determined by the sparse regression (
21
). Then, the resultant model (
26
) is constructed as the sparse linear combination of the basis functions optimized on the basis of the accurate RLT prediction accuracy. Consequently, the SINDy-LOM approach obtains the parsimonious and interpretable data-driven model achieving accurate RLT prediction.

Remark 5
.

Several studies have proposed library optimization techniques for the parametrized library
[
48
,
53

54

. However, in contrast to SINDy-LOM, earlier methods that aim to optimize the parametrized library focus solely on one-step-ahead prediction accuracy.

Remark 6
.

In Steps
1
and
6
in Procedure
1
, various techniques mentioned in Remark
2
can replace the STLSQ algorithm to solve the inner problem, depending on the type of
\mathcal{R}\left({v}\right)
; users can employ various advanced SINDy algorithms (e.g., SINDy-PI, E-SINDy). From this viewpoint, this study provides a general framework to design the library for the SINDy method.

Remark 7
.

A trajectory of a deterministic dynamical system is specified by the initial condition. This fact indicates that the predicted trajectory from the initial condition obtained by recurrently using the identified model must agree with that of the true system. In other words, the behavior of the identified model must coincide with the true dynamical behavior in terms of not only the one-step-ahead prediction but also the RLT prediction. Therefore, the proposed approach incorporates this side information into the data-driven modeling process. This side information improves the reliability of the resultant model.

Remark 8
.

The emission amount of nitrogen oxide, a harmful substance, is governed by the oxygen concentration in the cylinder; the EGR rate must be managed appropriately to maintain the oxygen concentration. V
Discussion

The numerical example presented in Section
IV
confirms the validity of the proposed approach. In Figs.
5
and
6
and Table
V
, the model via Strategy #1, i.e., the traditional SINDy, results in poor RLT prediction. Although Strategy #2 has a richer library than Strategy #1, the RLT prediction accuracy due to Strategy #2 is not improved at all. A comparison of Strategies #1 and #2 highlights the necessity and difficulty of the careful design of the library in the SINDy framework. Meanwhile, Figs.
5
and
6
together with Table
V
clearly show that the data-driven model provided by Strategy #3, namely, the proposed approach, marks the most accurate RLT prediction. This result is due to the proposed library optimization mechanism, as the only difference between Strategies #3 and #2 is whether the tunable parameter
\varPhi
to design the basis functions
\theta_{29},\ldots,\theta_{33}
is optimized or not. Note that the proposed library optimization mechanism can search for the appropriate basis functions on the basis of the optimization algorithm, thereby reducing user burden significantly. Therefore, the proposed approach provides a powerful strategy to address the problem of library design in the SINDy framework, thereby bridging the gap between SINDy theory and practice.

Comparing the prediction results due to Strategies #2 and #3, we can see the importance of considering the RLT prediction. In the numerical example, the one-step-ahead prediction accuracy achieved by Strategy #2 is comparable to that of Strategy #3 (Fig.
7
); however, the model distilled by Strategy #2 fails to provide accurate RLT predictions of the system behavior over the prediction horizon (Fig.
5
). As discussed in Section
II-B
, this result is attributed to the accumulation of prediction errors, highlighting the importance of the RLT prediction perspective. The proposed approach explicitly considers the RLT prediction to overcome the error propagation over the prediction horizon. In other words, the consistency of the true dynamical system and the data-driven model is managed from the viewpoint of the RLT behavior. This feature enhances the reliability of the resulting model provided by the proposed approach.

The numerical example demonstrated that this study provides a powerful data-driven modeling technique to industry. Figure
3
shows that the diesel engine airpath system of the numerical example is a typical industrial system exhibiting complex behavior such as strong nonlinearity and coupling. Despite its complexity, the proposed approach successfully discovers the data-driven model achieving good RLT prediction. Note that the model obtained by the proposed approach is interpretable in that the model has the parsimonious closed-form expression as shown in Fig.
4
. The interpretability is important to gain the insights into the resultant model and the dynamical system itself as well as to assess the reliability of the distilled model. From this viewpoint, the interpretability of the resultant model plays a critical role in adopting the data-driven modeling technique for engineering applications. Hence, the proposed approach is suitable for data-driven modeling problems in various engineering applications. In the future, we will apply the SINDy-LOM framework to other industrial systems.

This study addressed an important challenge in the SINDy framework, namely, the library design. Nevertheless, some issues remain in terms of enhancing its usability. One issue is the selection of the parametrization of the library: we must specify the parametrization of the basis functions. Although the selection of the class of the basis functions is easier than determining the functions and their design parameters, the design of the parametrized basis functions must involve some trial and error. Thus, a practical design policy for the parametrized library is required to further reduce the development cost. Moreover, the optimization problem (
13
) provides an important research direction. Specifically, (
13
) is a non-convex optimization problem, which is not easy to solve. Although (
13
) can be handled by a standard numerical software tool in practice as demonstrated in this study, the convex reformulation of (
13
) should improve the computational efficiency significantly. In the future, we will tackle these issues to enhance the practical usability of the SINDy-LOM approach.

VI
Conclusion

We developed a novel data-driven modeling approach for nonlinear dynamical systems. The proposed approach employs the SINDy framework. In contrast to the conventional SINDy technique, the proposed technique automatically designs the basis function. We reformulated the library design procedure in the SINDy framework into the optimization of the parametrized basis functions. The data-driven modeling procedure has a two-layer optimization architecture. In the inner layer, solving the sparse regression problem distills the data-driven model as the parsimonious linear combination of the candidate basis functions. In the outer layer, the parametrized basis functions are modified from the viewpoint of the RLT prediction accuracy of the model distilled in the inner layer. By solving the inner- and outer-layer optimization problems iteratively, the proposed approach discovers the parsimonious data-driven model that provides good RLT prediction accuracy. The validity of the proposed approach was demonstrated by applying it to a diesel engine airpath system in a numerical study. The data-driven model due to the proposed approach accurately captured the complex dynamical behavior of the diesel engine airpath system over the prediction horizon, whereas the conventional SINDy technique failed to do so. Moreover, the numerical example revealed the challenge in designing the appropriate library and the importance of considering the RLT prediction accuracy in the SINDy framework. Thus, this study provides a reliable and practical data-driven modeling technique.

In the future, we will use the proposed approach for constructing the prediction model in MPC.

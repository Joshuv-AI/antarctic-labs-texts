2d Cardy-Rabinovici model with the modified Villain lattice: Exact dualities and symmetries Preprint: YITP-25-78
Nagare Katayama, Yuya Tanizaki

Abstract
The Cardy-Rabinovici model is a toy model of the lattice U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) gauge theories to study various oblique confinement states associated with the nonzero θ \theta angles. We reformulate the 2 2 d version of this model using the modified Villain lattice formalism, and we establish the exact θ \theta periodicity for the Witten effect and the strong-weak duality at the finite lattice spacings. We then study the phase structure of this model based on the duality, symmetry and anomaly, and the perturbative renormalization group.

Introduction

Quantum chromodynamics (QCD) is the fundamental theory of strong interactions, whose elementary degrees of freedom are quarks and gluons.
We can only observe hadrons in the vacuum of our universe, and quarks and gluons are confined inside color-singlet hadrons.
Magnetic excitations, such as monopoles and center vortices, play the pivotal role in describing color confinement in
4
d gauge theories
Nambu:1974zg
;
Mandelstam:1974pi
;
Polyakov:1975rs
;
tHooft:1977nqb
;

Nielsen:1979xu
;
Ambjorn:1980ms
: For example, if color magnetic monopoles condense in the vacuum, then color electric charges should be confined as their electric fluxes are collimated and forming the flux tube via the dual Meissner effect.
The importance of solitonic objects makes the physics of nonperturbative gauge theories quite intriguing, while developing their theoretical descriptions becomes challenging.

4
d gauge theories have the
\theta
angle associated with the instanton number, which provides an efficient probe for the dynamics of magnetic excitations.
As a general remark, the set of possible electric and magnetic charges in
4
d gauge theories is highly constrained by the mutual locality condition to make Dirac strings invisible

;
Schwinger:1966nj
;

Still, it turns out that there exists one free parameter for the electric charge of the magnetic monopole, and the
\theta

The

\theta

\theta+2\pi

In confinement phases, the

Cardy and Rabinovici proposed a toy model of oblique confinement phases based on the lattice

The CR model introduces both electric and magnetic matters by summing up ensembles of charge-

-valued.
To see other terms can be eliminated, we should note that the “integration-by-part” for the forward lattice derivative takes the following form,

\sum_{x}(\partial_{\mu}f(x))g(x)=-\sum_{x}f(x)\partial_{\mu}g(x-\hat{\mu}).

(26)

Therefore, the first term vanishes,

\sum_{x,\mu,\nu}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi_{1}(x)\partial_{\nu}\phi_{2}(x+\hat{\mu})=-\sum_{x,\mu,\nu}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\phi_{1}(x)\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\phi_{2}(x)=0,

(27)

since
\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}
is antisymmetric in
\mu\nu
while the other part is symmetric after the integration-by-parts.
The second term can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\sum_{x,\mu,\nu}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}n_{1,\mu}(x)\partial_{\nu}\phi_{2}(x+\hat{\mu})=\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\sum_{x,\mu,\nu}\phi_{2}(x+\hat{1}+\hat{2})\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}n_{1,\nu}(x),

(28)

which can be absorbed in the Lagrange-multiplier term by
\tilde{\phi}_{1}(\tilde{x})\mapsto\tilde{\phi}_{1}(\tilde{x})-\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\phi_{2}(x+\hat{1}+\hat{2})
.
Equivalently, the equation of motion for
\tilde{\phi}_{1}
imposes
\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}n_{1,\nu}(x)=0
, and this term vanishes.
Similarly, the third term can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\sum_{x,\mu,\nu}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi_{1}(x)n_{2,\nu}(x+\hat{\mu})=-\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\sum_{x,\mu,\nu}\phi_{1}(x)\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}n_{2,\nu}(x),

(29)

which can be absorbed by
\tilde{\phi}_{2}(\tilde{x})\mapsto\tilde{\phi}_{2}(\tilde{x})+\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\phi_{1}(x)
.
Therefore, we can confirm that
Q_{\text{top}}
takes integral values when the vortex operators are completely absent.

Now, we can discuss how the equivalence at
\theta
and
\theta+2\pi
is realized on the lattice.
In the continuum, we have seen that the theories at
\theta
and
\theta+2\pi
are not the same on the nose but they are related by the nontrivial unitary equivalence, which is summarized in the relation (
8
), and this turns out to be true also for the lattice.
The Boltzmann weight of the path integral at
\theta+2\pi
is given by

\exp(-S_{\mathrm{free},\,\theta+2\pi})=\exp(-S_{\mathrm{free},\,\theta}-2\pi\mathrm{i}Q_{\mathrm{top}}).

(30)

If
Q_{\mathrm{top}}
were manifestly integer quantized, this gives
\exp(-S_{\theta})
and the lattice theories at
\theta
and
\theta+2\pi
would have become identical in the trivial manner.
However, this is not the case. As we have seen in the above discussion, we need to absorb non-integral mixed terms (i.e. the second and third terms in (
25
)) by shifting the dual variables,

\tilde{\phi}_{1}(\tilde{x})\mapsto\tilde{\phi}_{1}(\tilde{x})-\phi_{2}(x+\hat{1}+\hat{2}),\quad\tilde{\phi}_{2}(\tilde{x})\mapsto\tilde{\phi}_{2}(\tilde{x})+\phi_{1}(x).

(31)

This transformation derives the relation,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\exp\Bigl(\mathrm{i}\tilde{\phi}_{1}(\tilde{x})\Bigr)\\
\exp\Bigl(\mathrm{i}\tilde{\phi}_{2}(\tilde{x})\Bigr)\end{array}\right\}\text{ at $\theta+2\pi$}\longleftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\exp\Bigl(\mathrm{i}(\tilde{\phi}_{1}(\tilde{x})-\phi_{2}(x+\hat{1}+\hat{2}))\Bigr)\\
\exp\Bigl(\mathrm{i}(\tilde{\phi}_{2}(\tilde{x})+\phi_{1}(x))\Bigr)\end{array}\right\}\text{ at $\theta$},

(32)

which is nothing but the lattice counter part of (
8
).
We note that these relations (
32
) strictly determine the placement of the induced electric charge in the dyon operators on the lattice, which is remarkable and becomes possible thanks to the nice topological property of the modified Villain lattice formulation.

One can also check that this lattice model reproduces the ’t Hooft anomalies of the continuum theory. The lattice construction of these anomalies is done in Ref.
Abe:2023uan
based on the Wilson-type formulation with the admissibility condition.

2.2.3
Exact realization of the strong-weak duality on the lattice

3.1
2d Cardy-Rabinovici model with the modified Villain lattice

Strong-weak duality of the 2d CR model with the

In Sec.
2.2.3
, we have confirmed that the strong-weak duality is exact for the free theory on the lattice in the modified Villain formulation.
This duality is derived by the Poisson summation for
n_{a,\mu}
, so the derivation is applicable with the potential term as
V
does not contain
n_{a,\mu}
.
However, the strong-weak duality in Sec.
2.2.3
exchanges
\phi_{a}\leftrightarrow\tilde{\phi}_{a}
, which is not respected for the local potential
V
of the
2
d CR model: The charges of
\phi_{a}
are quantized in
N
while the ones for
\tilde{\phi}_{a}
are not.
Thus, we need another strong-weak duality that exchanges
N\phi_{a}
and
\tilde{\phi}_{a}
to apply it for the
2
d CR model.

Such a strong-weak duality is obtained by

(1)

gauging the electric
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}
symmetry and then

(2)

applying the strong-weak duality in Sec.
2.2.3
.

To discuss the ’t Hooft anomaly, we introduced the background gauge field
A_{a}
for the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}
symmetry. When gauging
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}
, we promote
A_{a}
as the dynamical field. We note that
A_{a,\mu}
always appears in the combination of
Nn_{a,\mu}+A_{a,\mu}
, and we may regard it as the new
\mathbb{Z}
-valued link variable,

n^{\prime}_{a,\mu}(x):=Nn_{a,\mu}(x)+A_{a,\mu}(x).

(57)

Then, the gauged free action becomes

\displaystyle S_{\text{gauged free},\,N\bar{\theta}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\sum_{x,\mu}\frac{R^{2}/N^{2}}{4\pi}(\partial_{\mu}(N\phi_{a})+2\pi n^{\prime}_{a,\mu})^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{i}N\bar{\theta}}{N^{2}}Q_{\mathrm{top}}[N\phi_{a},n^{\prime}_{a,\mu}]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathrm{i}\sum_{a}\sum_{x,\mu\nu}\left(\frac{1}{N}\tilde{\phi}_{a}(\tilde{x})\right)\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}n^{\prime}_{a,\nu}(x),

(58)

and we obtain the same functional form of the free-theory action by introducing

\phi^{\prime}_{a}(x)=N\phi_{a}(x),\quad\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}_{a}(\tilde{x})=\frac{1}{N}\tilde{\phi}_{a}(\tilde{x}).

(59)

Thus, the strong-weak duality operation after the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}
gauging gives the following transformations,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\exp(\mathrm{i}N\phi_{a})\\
\exp(\mathrm{i}\tilde{\phi}_{a})\\
\tau\end{array}\right\}\xrightarrow{\text{$\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}$ gauging}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\exp(\mathrm{i}\phi_{a})\\
\exp(\mathrm{i}N\tilde{\phi}_{a})\\
\tau/N^{2}\end{array}\right\}\xrightarrow{\text{strong-weak duality}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\exp(\mathrm{i}\tilde{\phi}_{a})\\
\exp(\mathrm{i}N\phi_{a})\\
-N^{2}/\tau\end{array}\right\},

(60)

where
\tau=\frac{\theta}{2\pi}+\mathrm{i}R^{2}=N\left(\frac{\bar{\theta}}{2\pi}+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{N}R^{2}\right)
.
It is straightforward to confirm that the potential (
41
) of the
2
d CR model
V
is invariant under this duality operation by exchanging
p
and
-q
, so this is the correct strong-weak duality operation for the
2
d CR model.
The duality ensures that the physics at
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}=-N^{2}/\tau
becomes identical up to the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}
gauging operation.

At the self-dual point, such as
\tau/N=\mathrm{i}
, the strong-weak duality gives an extra symmetry for the
2
d CR model.
The fact that the duality operation is associated with the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}
gauging makes the duality symmetry non-invertible

;
Aasen:2020jwb
;
Koide:2021zxj
;
Kaidi:2021xfk
;
Choi:2021kmx
: For example, the fusion of the duality symmetry operators at

gives the condensation defect for the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}
symmetry. When the duality symmetry operator surrounds
\exp(\mathrm{i}\phi_{a})
, it gives
0
and thus the duality symmetry operator is non-invertible.

3.2
Phase diagram based on the perturbative renormalization group

In this section, let us determine the phase diagram when
g\ll 1
at the lattice scale and then the effect of
V
can be treated as the perturbation.
In this case, the phase diagram can be determined by the perturbative renormalization group based on the simple scaling dimension argument, following Refs.
Cardy:1981qy
;
Cardy:1981fd
.

The scaling dimension for
\cos(Np\phi_{1}+q\tilde{\phi}_{2})
and
\cos(Np\phi_{2}-q\tilde{\phi}_{1})
is given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{p,q}

\displaystyle:=\Delta[\cos(Np\phi_{1}+q\tilde{\phi}_{2})]=\Delta[\cos(Np\phi_{2}-q\tilde{\phi}_{1})]

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{2\mathrm{Im(\tau/N)}}\left|p+\frac{\tau}{N}q\right|^{2}.

(61)

Let

be the renormalization energy scale and we write the local potential at the scale

V=-\int\mathrm{d}^{2}x\sum_{\gcd(p,q)=1}g_{p,q}(E)[\cos(Np\phi_{1}+q\tilde{\phi}_{2})+\cos(Np\phi_{2}-q\tilde{\phi}_{1})].

(62)

Let us introduce the dimensionless couplings
\bar{g}_{p,q}(E)=g_{p,q}(E)/E^{2}
, and then
\bar{g}_{p,q}=g
for all

at the lattice energy scale, which gives the initial condition for the renormalization group. The perturbative renormalization group at the leading order is given by

\frac{\mathrm{d}\bar{g}_{p,q}}{\mathrm{d}\ln E}=-(2-\Delta_{p,q})\bar{g}_{p,q},

(63)

and the dimensionless coupling shows the scaling behavior,

\bar{g}_{p,q}(E)\sim(1/E)^{2-\Delta_{p,q}}\to\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}+\infty&(\text{as $E\to 0$ for $\Delta_{p,q}<2$}),\\
0&(\text{as $E\to 0$ for $\Delta_{p,q}>2$}).\end{array}\right.

(64)

Thus, the running couplings depend on

and also on
\tau
via the scaling dimension

, and the infinite sum over

becomes convergent.
We may introduce such

-dependent lattice couplings from the beginning when we define the lattice potential in (

3.3
Gapped phases and the global symmetry

Let
p,q
be the charge with the minimal scaling dimension,
\Delta_{p,q}<2
, and then the low-energy physics is gapped due to the relevant interactions,
\cos(Np\phi_{1}+q\tilde{\phi}_{2})
and
\cos(Np\phi_{2}-q\tilde{\phi}_{1})
.
In this section, we discuss the classification of those gapped phases in Fig.
3
from the viewpoint of the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}
symmetry.

3.3.1
Higgs phase
(p,q)=(1,0)
and spontaneous symmetry breaking

Let us consider the case where the gap is generated by
-g\cos(N\phi_{1,2})
, and we call it the Higgs phase as this corresponds to the condensation of the charge-
N
electric matter in the
4
d context.
The scaling dimension of the operator is

\displaystyle\Delta_{1,0}=\frac{N}{2\mathrm{Im}(\tau/N)}=\frac{N^{2}}{2R^{2}},

(65)

which is independent of the
\theta
angle.
Therefore, the Higgs phase is the most favored gapped phase when
R^{2}
is sufficiently large.

In this phase, the electric symmetry is spontaneously broken,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}\xrightarrow{\text{SSB}}1.

(66)

To minimize the relevant perturbation,
-g[\cos(N\phi_{1})+\cos(N\phi_{2})]
, we need to set
N\phi_{1}=N\phi_{2}=0
mod
2\pi
. Therefore, for some
v>0
, the vacuum expectation values of the vertex operators are given as

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\phi_{1}}\rangle=v\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{N}k_{1}},\quad\langle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\phi_{2}}\rangle=v\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{N}k_{2}},

(67)

with
k_{1},k_{2}=0,1,\ldots,N-1
. There are
N^{2}
-fold degenerate ground states due to the spontaneous breaking.

As the scaling dimension
\Delta_{1,0}
is independent of the
\theta
angle, the Higgs phase is realized also at
\bar{\theta}=\pi
for
R^{2}\gg 1
. Then, this phase should satisfy the anomaly matching condition of the ’t Hooft anomaly (
53
), so let us see how it is satisfied.
When we turn on a nontrivial background gauge field
A_{a}
, the phase of the local condensate
\langle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\phi_{a}}\rangle
is rotated by the Aharonov–Bohm effect of
A_{a}
.
The nontrivial holonomy of
A_{a}
requires that the vacua of the different labels
k_{a}
should be connected by the domain wall, and thus the partition function should be exponentially suppressed by the domain-wall energy.
As a result, the low-energy limit of the partition function in the Higgs phase is given by

\displaystyle Z_{\text{Higgs}}[A_{1},A_{2}]\propto\delta(A_{1})\delta(A_{2}).

(68)

Since the anomalous phase in (
53
) is nontrivial only if the partition function vanishes, the anomaly matching is satisfied in the low-energy effective theory of the Higgs phase.

Let us understand the behavior (
68
) more explicitly from the lattice model.
For simplicity, we may send the couplings for
-g\cos{(N\phi_{1,2})}
to
g=\infty
, and then
\phi_{1,2}
are restricted to
\frac{2\pi}{N}\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{R}
.
The minimization of the kinetic term is achieved if

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\phi_{a}(x)+2\pi\left(n_{a,\mu}(x)+\frac{A_{a,\mu}(x)}{N}\right)=0,

(69)

which is possible if the holonomy of
A_{a}
is trivial. When
A_{a}
has a nontrivial holonomy, there has to be some links that violate the above condition, which causes the domain-wall energy.

3.3.2
Confinement phases
q=1
as symmetry-protected topological states

Let us consider the gapped phases caused by the magnetic condensation, where the most relevant charge is given by
(p,q)=(p,1)
with some
p
, and we call them confinement phases.
Its scaling dimension is given by

\Delta_{p,1}=\frac{N}{2\mathrm{Im}(\tau/N)}|p+\tau/N|^{2}=\frac{N}{2}\left[\frac{N}{R^{2}}\left(p+\frac{\bar{\theta}}{2\pi}\right)^{2}+\frac{R^{2}}{N}\right].

(70)

To achieve the confinement phase with
g\ll 1
, we need small
R^{2}/N
to suppress the second term (or the magnetic energy) of the scaling dimension.
While this makes the electric energy (i.e. the first term of the scaling dimension) larger, it can be suppressed when
|p+\frac{\bar{\theta}}{2\pi}|
is sufficiently small.
Thus, the confinement phase strongly depends on the
\theta
angle.

In the confinement phase, the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}
symmetry is unbroken:
\langle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\phi_{1}}\rangle=\langle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\phi_{2}}\rangle=0
.
Since the relevant perturbation
\cos(Np\phi_{1}+\tilde{\phi_{2}})
and
\cos(Np\phi_{2}-\tilde{\phi}_{1})
are the vortex operators, the phase of the order parameters
\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\phi_{a}}
cannot be determined when the vortices are proliferated in the vacuum.
Thus, the confinement phases are not characterized by the local order parameters.

To discriminate confinement phases with different
p
, we need to look at the response of the partition function to the background gauge field:

Z_{\text{$(p,1)$ dyon}}[A_{a}]\propto\exp\left(\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}p}{N}\int A_{1}\cup A_{2}\right).

(71)

The gauge invariance for the background gauge field requires that the coefficient of the topological term has to be quantized. Thus, this level
p
cannot jump without quantum phase transitions, and we can discriminate the confinement states with different
p
mod
N
as the symmetry-protected topological (SPT) states.

We can derive this result (
71
) starting from the partition function (
68
) of the Higgs phase by applying the strong-weak duality and also the anomalous relation (
46
) as shown in Ref.
Hayashi:2022fkw
.
As the strong-weak duality exchanges
N\phi_{a}
and
\tilde{\phi}_{a}
, we can obtain the
(0,1)
condensate by applying it to the Higgs phase.
Since the strong-weak duality of the
2
d CR model involves the gauging of
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}

Z_{\text{$(0,1)$ dyon}}[A_{1,2}]\propto\sum_{[a_{i}]\in H^{1}(M_{2};\mathbb{Z}_{N})}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{N}\int\sum_{i=1,2}A_{i}\cup a_{i}}Z_{\mathrm{Higgs}}[a_{1,2}]=1.

(72)

The
(p,1)
condensate is obtained by the Witten effect for the
(0,1)
condensate via
\bar{\theta}+2\pi p\to\bar{\theta}
, and thus its partition function can be obtained by (
46
) as

Z_{\text{$(p,1)$ dyon}}[A_{1,2}]\propto\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}p}{N}\int A_{1}\cup A_{2}}Z_{\text{$(0,1)$ dyon}}[A_{1,2}]\propto\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}p}{N}\int A_{1}\cup A_{2}},

(73)

and we obtain (
71
).

We can also understand the SPT level

by using the string order parameter

for a recent field-theoretic discussion).
The relevant perturbation for the
(p,1)
condensate is given by

and
\cos(Np\phi_{2}-\tilde{\phi_{1}})
, we may think that their fractional operators

\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\left(p\phi_{1}+\frac{1}{N}\tilde{\phi}_{2}\right)},\quad\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\left(p\phi_{2}-\frac{1}{N}\tilde{\phi}_{1}\right)}

We should note that

3.3.3
Oblique confinement phases

Oblique confinement phase
tHooft:1981bkw
;
Cardy:1981qy
;
Cardy:1981fd

To be concrete, let us focus on the oblique confinement with
(p,q)=(-1,2)

\displaystyle\Delta_{-1,2}=\frac{N}{2}\bigg[\frac{N}{R^{2}}\left(1-\frac{\bar{\theta}}{\pi}\right)^{2}+\frac{4R^{2}}{N}\bigg].

(77)

When

For this case, the mass gap is generated by

and

, but
he symmetry realization of this gapped phase depends crucially on whether
N

N
, the following local vertex operators have nonzero expectation values,

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\left(\frac{N}{2}\phi_{1}-\tilde{\phi}_{2}\right)}\rangle=\pm v,\quad\langle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\left(\frac{N}{2}\phi_{2}+\tilde{\phi}_{1}\right)}\rangle=\pm v,

(78)

for some

4
-fold degenerate ground states associated with the spontaneous symmetry breaking,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{SSB}}\mathbb{Z}_{N/2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N/2}.

(79)

We can check that the anomaly matching condition at
\bar{\theta}=\pi
is satisfied in the oblique confinement phase

.
For confirm it, it is sufficient to show that
\mathbb{Z}_{N/2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N/2}
and
\mathsf{P}
does not have an ’t Hooft anomaly.
Let
\tilde{A}_{a}
denote the background gauge field for

\mathbb{Z}_{N}
gauge fields are obtained as

and the anomalous relation (

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}:Z_{\bar{\theta}=\pi}[2\tilde{A}_{1,2}]\mapsto\exp{\bigg(\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{N}\int(2\tilde{A}_{1})\cup(2\tilde{A}_{2})\bigg)}Z_{\bar{\theta}=\pi}[2\tilde{A}_{1,2}].

Since

are the
\mathbb{Z}_{N/2}

(81)

with some

,
and we can always cancel the anomalous phase by setting

When

4
Summary and discussion

In this paper, we revisit the
2
d version of the Cardy-Rabinovici (CR) model, which is a toy model of the lattice
U(1)
gauge theories to study exotic confinement phases, called oblique confinement phases, associated with the finite
\theta
angles.
We reformulate the
2
d CR model using the modified Villain lattice formalism and establish the
\theta
periodicity for the Witten effect and the strong-weak duality in an exact manner at the finite lattice spacings, which were missing in the original version.
We determine its phase structure using the power of the
duality, symmetry and anomaly, and the perturbative renormalization group.
Let us explain the details of each result briefly.

First, we constructed the
2
d free compact boson theory with the modified Villain lattice formulation, and showed exact realizations of the Witten effect and the strong-weak duality in this system.
By using these results, we reformulated the
2
d CR model by introducing the potential term to satisfy the
\theta
periodicity and electric
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}
symmetry.
In this model, we can show the mixed ’t Hooft anomaly (or the global inconsistency) between
\mathsf{P}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}
symmetry at
\bar{\theta}=\pi
.
We also showed that this model possesses the exact duality relation by combining the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}
gauging and the lattice strong-weak duality.

We determine if the phase is gapped or gapless by the perturbative renormalization group when the potential is sufficiently small.
We then classify the gapped phases from the viewpoint of the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}
symmetry in
2
d QFTs.
We note that the phases at the couplings
-(\tau/N)^{-1}
and
\tau+1
can be determined by the phase at
\tau
by the gauging operation and the stacking SPT states via duality and anomaly.
All the gapped phases can be mapped to the Higgs phase (i.e.
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}
broken state at
\mathrm{Im}(\tau/N)\gg 1
) via the duality operations, and we can systematically determine the gapped quantum phases of this model.
We understand the counterpart of confinement phases as
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}
-symmetric SPT phases, and we confirm their characterization based on the string-order parameters.

The oblique confinement phase, corresponding to the
(p,q)=(-1,2)
dyon condensation in
4
d, is also investigated, which appears when
\bar{\theta}\simeq\pi
and the compact boson radius
R^{2}/N
is sufficiently small.
When
N
is even, the symmetry breaking
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{N/2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N/2}
occurs, which satisfies the anomaly matching.
When
N
is odd, there is no genuine anomaly at
\bar{\theta}=\pi
, so the oblique confinement phase is permitted to become a trivially gapped phase, which is actually the case.
Its SPT level is identified from the consistency, and we explicitly confirm it from the string-order parameter.
As a result, the oblique confinement phase around
\bar{\theta}=\pi
should be discriminated from the confinement states around
\bar{\theta}=0
and
2\pi
.

Let us comment on several future directions of this study.
Since we rely on the perturbative renormalization group to determine the phase diagram, our result is limited to the case with
g\ll 1
.
It would be interesting to explore the dynamics around
g\sim O(1)
, where the electric and magnetic excitations could compete with one another.
To study the region of
g\sim O(1)
, we need to develop the numerical technique for this model, but the lattice action (
40
) has the imaginary part, which causes the sign problem.
As demonstrated in Refs.
Gattringer:2018dlw
;
Sulejmanpasic:2019ytl
;
Sulejmanpasic:2020lyq
, the worldline representations provide the manifestly sign-problem-free expression with the nonzero
\theta
angle, so we need to give its explicit formula and confirm the absence of the sign problem in the case of our model.
We should also note that our expression of the potential
V(\phi_{a},\tilde{\phi}_{a})
contains the infinite sum of the electric-magnetic charges
(p,q)
, and we need to use the
(p,q,\tau)
-dependent coupling to make the lattice theory finite with maintaining the exact
\bar{\theta}
periodicity and strong-weak duality.

Another interesting direction would be an extension of the model to have all the possible gapped phases for the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}
symmetry.
In the case of the
2
d CR model, any gapped phase can be mapped to the Higgs phase by repeatedly applying the strong-weak duality and the
2\pi
shift of
\bar{\theta}
.
However, for example, the spontaneous breaking
\mathbb{Z}_{4}\times\mathbb{Z}_{4}\xrightarrow{\text{SSB}}\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
without the SPT stacking cannot be mapped to the completely symmetry-broken phase by the
\mathbb{Z}_{4}\times\mathbb{Z}_{4}
gauging and the stacking of the
\mathbb{Z}_{4}\times\mathbb{Z}_{4}
SPT states, and thus the CR model does not realize such a phase.
The same constraint exists also for the
4
d CR model, which means that all the possibilities of the Wilson-’t Hooft classification are not exhausted by the CR model, and its extension would give us a hint to understand more details about the strong confinement dynamics.

Here, we give a review for the computation of correlation functions for general vertex operators in

\theta

\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2}

In particular, we must calculate the correlation function between the original compact boson and the dual compact boson. First, we consider the original action (
4

\phi_{a}
. Then it is convenient to introduce the complex coordinates :
z=x_{1}+ix_{2},\bar{z}=x_{1}-ix_{2},\partial_{z}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{1}-i\partial_{2}),\partial_{\bar{z}}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{1}+i\partial_{2})

\phi_{a}

\displaystyle\phi_{a}(z,\bar{z})=\varphi_{a}^{+}(z)+\varphi_{a}^{-}(\bar{z}),

(84)

where
\varphi_{a}^{+}(z)
and
\varphi_{a}^{-}(\bar{z})
are the holomorphic function and the anti-holomorphic function respectively. From the correlation function for
\phi_{a}
, we gain the relations:

\displaystyle\langle\varphi_{a}^{+}(z)\varphi_{a}^{+}(0)\rangle=-\frac{1}{2R_{a}^{2}}\ln{z},\quad\langle\varphi_{a}^{-}(\bar{z})\varphi_{a}^{-}(\bar{0})\rangle=-\frac{1}{2R_{a}^{2}}\ln{\bar{z}},

(85)

where the other correlation functions are zero. Next, we consider the action after introducing the dual compact boson:

\displaystyle S_{\theta}[\partial\phi_{1},\partial\phi_{2},\tilde{\phi}_{1},\tilde{\phi}_{2}]

(86)

where we regard the vector field
\partial\phi_{a}
and the dual compact boson
\tilde{\phi}_{a}
are the fundamental fields in this stage. Considering the action (
86

\partial_{\mu}\phi_{a}
are given by

\displaystyle R_{1}^{2}\partial_{\mu}\phi_{1}+\mathrm{i}\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\phi_{2}+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\tilde{\phi}_{1}

\displaystyle=0,

(87)

\displaystyle R_{2}^{2}\partial_{\mu}\phi_{2}-\mathrm{i}\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\phi_{1}+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\tilde{\phi}_{2}

(88)

87

88

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}_{1}

\displaystyle=R_{1}^{2}\varphi_{1}^{+}(z)-\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\varphi_{2}^{+}(z)-R_{1}^{2}\varphi_{1}^{-}(\bar{z})-\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\varphi_{2}^{-}(\bar{z})\quad\text{(up to const.)},

(89)

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}_{2}

(90)

Appendix B
Concrete calculations of the strong-weak duality on the lattice

In this section, we calculate the strong-weak duality in Sec.
2.2.3
on the lattice concretely. The basic policy is to introduce the dual integer gauge fields
\tilde{n}_{a}
by using the Poisson summation formula,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{R^{2}}{4\pi}[F+2\pi n]^{2}-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\gamma[F+2\pi n]}=\frac{1}{R}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{4\pi R^{2}}[\gamma+2\pi m]^{2}+\mathrm{i}mF}.

(92)

To apply this formula, we rewrite the lattice action (
18
) as follows:

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{free},\,\theta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{ax\mu,by\nu}(\partial_{\mu}\phi_{a}(x)+2\pi n_{a\mu}(x))M_{ax\mu,by\nu}(\partial_{\nu}\phi_{b}(y)+2\pi n_{b\nu}(y))

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\sum_{ax\mu}(\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\tilde{\phi}_{a}(\tilde{x}-\hat{\nu}))(\partial_{\mu}\phi_{a}(x)+2\pi n_{a\mu}(x)),

(93)

where

M_{ax\mu,by\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}R_{1}^{2}\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta_{xy}&\mathrm{i}\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\delta_{x+\hat{\mu},y}\\
-\mathrm{i}\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\delta_{x,y+\hat{\nu}}&R_{2}^{2}\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta_{xy}\end{pmatrix}.

(94)

Its inverse matrix is give by replacing the couplings by dual couplings (
36
),

(M^{-1})_{ax\mu,by\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{R}_{1}^{2}\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta_{xy}&\mathrm{i}\frac{\tilde{\theta}}{2\pi}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\delta_{x+\hat{\mu},y}\\
-\mathrm{i}\frac{\tilde{\theta}}{2\pi}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\delta_{x,y+\hat{\nu}}&\tilde{R}_{2}^{2}\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta_{xy}\end{pmatrix}.

(95)

By applying the Poisson summation formula (
92
) with introducing the new
\mathbb{Z}
-valued field
m_{a\mu}(x)
, we obtain the dual action as

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{ax\mu,by\nu}(\varepsilon_{\mu\rho}\partial_{\rho}\tilde{\phi}_{a}(\tilde{x}-\hat{\rho})+2\pi m_{a\mu}(x))M^{-1}_{ax\mu,by\nu}(\varepsilon_{\nu\sigma}\partial_{\sigma}\tilde{\phi}_{b}(\tilde{y}-\hat{\sigma})+2\pi m_{b\nu}(y))

\displaystyle-\mathrm{i}\sum_{ax\mu}m_{a\mu}(x)\partial_{\mu}\phi_{a}(x).

(96)

By rewriting the dual gauge field as
m_{a\mu}(x)=\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\tilde{n}_{a\nu}(\tilde{x}-\hat{\nu})
, the straightforward computation gives
\tilde{S}_{\mathrm{free}}
in (
33
).

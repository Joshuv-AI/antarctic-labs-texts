Completions of complexes of differential modules on singular schemes
Bruno Borić, Dalton A R Sakthivadivel

Abstract
. Spencer cohomology theory studies the cohomology of chain complexes of modules over the ring of differential operators 𝒟 \mathscr{D} of a smooth analytic space. In this paper we give a generalisation of Spencer cohomology suitable for singular schemes of finite type over a field. Our motivation was a conjecture of Vinogradov concerning the homological properties of differential operators on singular affine varieties; namely, that complexes of certain such operators are acyclic if and only if the variety is smooth. We will provide a negative answer to Vinogradov’s conjecture as stated. In principle Vinogradov’s conjecture can also be posed for the Spencer complex of a general 𝒟 \mathscr{D} -module—however the answer is trivial, since singularities prohibit a definition of Spencer cohomology with any good properties. Our main result will be the construction of a Spencer complex on a large class of singular schemes which is suitable as a cohomology theory for the space. Following this we are able to ask the same question as Vinogradov in this case, where we give a more positive answer. Our main technique draws from Hartshorne’s construction of de Rham cohomology by formal completion.

1.
Introduction

Associated to any smooth analytic space
X
and vector bundle
E
on it are invariants coming from the chain complex

(1.1)

0\to E\to\mathscr{J}^{r}_{X}(E)\to\mathscr{T}^{*}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{J}_{X}^{r}(E)\to\ldots\to\bigwedge^{n}\mathscr{T}^{*}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{J}^{r}_{X}(E)\to 0,

introduced by Spencer in
[
Spe69
]
. We will refer to this as the
topological Spencer complex
. This followed a general interest in constructing algebraic invariants from the jet bundle of a smooth manifold representing the obstructions to solubility of overdetermined systems partial differential equations on the spaces, beginning with
[
Spe62
]
and the 1964 thesis of Quillen
[
Qui64
]
. This cohomology turns out to be a very generic receptacle for cohomology theories which resolve some sheaf of constant functions, wherein it studies the differential in the theory as such a differential operator. Following this Malgrange
[
Mal66
]
and Johnson
[
Joh71
]
discovered that the cohomology theory of Spencer can be translated into the language of sheaves of modules over the ring of differential operators
\mathscr{D}_{X}
. From their work we have the
algebraic Spencer complex

(1.2)

0\to\mathscr{D}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\bigwedge^{n}\mathscr{T}_{X}\to\cdots\to\mathscr{D}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\bigwedge^{2}\mathscr{T}_{X}\to\mathscr{D}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{T}_{X}\to\mathscr{D}_{X}\to 0.

This was used by Johnson to compute Spencer’s cohomology groups in terms of
\Ext
groups of carefully chosen
\mathscr{D}
-modules. The algebraic theory is adjoint to the topological theory in a precise sense investigated by Johnson (
ibid
).

Johnson notes that the theory wholly extends to the case of a smooth algebraic variety; however, the jump from the smooth case into the general case proved less fruitful than initially imagined as
\mathscr{D}
-modules have a number of undesirable properties on singular spaces
[
BGG72
]
. The algebraic theory has never properly been extended to singular spaces like general algebraic varieties.

On the other hand it is immediate that the topological theory is definable in the algebraic category, taking
\mathscr{T}^{*}_{X}
to be the sheaf of Kähler differentials of an algebraic variety
X
and
\mathscr{J}^{r}_{X}(E)
the jet sheaf of some sheaf
E
on
X
(note that this exists on a singular algebraic variety; see
e.g.

[
EGA IV
, §16]
, where no smoothness assumptions are used), and may or may not have good properties. This saw important usage in the work of Vinogradov in homological algebra concerning invariants produced by derivations of equations in modules, where he studied many cases of the topological complex on singular affine varieties. Based on this work, in
[
Vin79
]
he conjectured that a more general version of the topological complex is acyclic on an affine variety if and only if the variety is smooth. In this sense the topological theory is still only expected to be a meaningful cohomology on smooth spaces, but the question has remained unresolved as such.

In this paper we will build a theory of Spencer cohomology on singular schemes. The first result of this paper concerns this conjecture. We find a straightforward answer to the question posed by Vinogradov in the form of a class of counterexamples to the
only if
direction. In particular, a scheme being equipped with a certain endomorphism on its de Rham differentials is sufficient for acyclicity of the topological complex (Theorem
2.2
). In this sense we view the topological Spencer complex as a coarser theory than just detecting smoothness would allow.

In later sections and for our main result, we seek to refine this measure by giving a suitable definition of an algebraic Spencer complex (and in turn Spencer cohomology) for any finite type scheme over a field of characteristic zero, with no assumptions on the types of singularities it may have other than imbeddability into a smooth scheme. The case of coherent
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-modules will prove simple as the formal completion is exact, meaning the homological properties of the
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module are preserved. This is developed in §
4
and culminates in Definition
4.13
. The case where we do not have coherent
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-modules demands more conceptual sophistication, but we see they are technically more pleasant. We will take the derived completion in this case. Some preliminaries will be required to establish what the properties of derived completions of
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-modules are §
5
and after this interlude we will give a parallel of Definition
4.13
in the derived setting in §
6
. We will discuss what information about the space it contains and what makes it a suitable replacement of Spencer cohomology. Throughout we will find interesting connections to local cohomology obtained by the application of Kashiwara’s equivalence, suggesting the completion is both (i) a natural viewpoint to adopt and (ii) a richer way to look at Spencer cohomology.

2.
Vinogradov’s conjecture

By
k
we will denote a field of characteristic zero, but not necessarily algebraically closed. All rings will be assumed Noetherian unless stated otherwise. All schemes will be locally Noetherian and of finite type over
k
. We will call a (non-zero) Noetherian ring
Cohen–Macaulay
(CM) if, when regarded as a module over itself, every localisation at a prime ideal has equal dimension and depth. A scheme (as per our conventions)
X
is CM if at every point the local ring
\mathscr{O}_{X,x}
is CM. This and other definitions will be as according to the excellent textbook account
[
BH98
, §2]
.

In
[
Vin79
]
a version of (
1.1
) is given for more general complexes of differential modules. Vinogradov’s complex depends on an index
\sigma
consisting of a (possibly infinite) sequence of positive integers, such that
\sigma=(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},\ldots)
corresponds to adding differential operators of order
s_{i}
in the
i
-th prolongation of
E
. For
\sigma=(1,\ldots,1)
,
\absolutevalue{\sigma}=r
, his complex is the Spencer complex with jet sheaf of order
r
. Vinogradov conjectures that his complex is acyclic on an affine variety for all sequences
\sigma
if and only if the variety is smooth. The
if
direction follows easily:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X
be a scheme (not necessarily affine) under the conventions above. If
X
is smooth, then (
1.1
) is acyclic for all
\sigma
.

Proof.

Since
X
is smooth,
\mathscr{T}_{X}
is locally free, so that at any stalk there is an étale-local set of coordinates in which
\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n}
is a commuting basis. Now we will analyse (
1.1
) at the stalks. According to
[
Joh71
]
the differential of any jet sheaf-valued
(n-1)
-form
\omega
in (
1.1
) satisfies

\displaystyle(\differential{\omega})(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}(-1)^{j-1}\xi_{j}\cdot\omega(\xi_{1},\ldots,\hat{\xi}_{j},\ldots,\xi_{n})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(-1)^{i+j}\omega([\xi_{i},\xi_{j}],\xi_{1},\ldots,\hat{\xi}_{i},\ldots\hat{\xi}_{j},\ldots,\xi_{n}).

By linearity of the differential it is sufficient to evaluate it on a basis of
\mathscr{T}_{X}
. The commutator of basis elements vanishes; hence, from non-degeneracy,

\omega([\partial_{i},\partial_{j}],\partial_{1},\ldots,\hat{\partial}_{i},\ldots,\hat{\partial}_{j},\ldots,\partial_{n})

vanishes for all
i,j
. We are left with the first term of the differential. Since
\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n}
generates
\mathscr{T}_{X,x}
, what remains is the differential of a Koszul complex of an isomorphism of free modules of rank
n
. By standard results such a complex is exact. The rest of the claim follows for all
\sigma
by changing the value of
\omega
; namely, from differential forms valued in one sequence of prolongations to any other.
∎

However, there are ultimately counterexamples in the singular case.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
X
be scheme (not necessarily smooth) under the conventions above and
L_{\xi}
the Lie derivative along a derivation
\xi
. If there exists a global derivation on
\Xi
on
X
such that for
i\geqslant 1
, the
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-linear endomorphism

L_{\Xi}\colon\Omega_{X}^{i}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{J}^{r}_{X}(E)\longrightarrow\Omega_{X}^{i}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{J}^{r}_{X}(E)

is an automorphism, then (
1.1
) is exact.

Proof.

One can verify with a brief computation that

L_{\Xi}=\differential{}\circ\iota_{\Xi}+\iota_{\Xi}\circ\differential{}

on
\Omega_{X}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{J}^{r}_{X}(E)
with differential given above. For
i\geqslant 1
, the hypothesis makes
L_{\Xi}
invertible, so that we may define

h_{i}\coloneq\iota_{\Xi}\circ L_{\Xi}^{-1}\colon\Omega_{X}^{i}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{J}^{r}_{X}(E)\to\Omega_{X}^{i}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{J}^{r}_{X}(E).

Now on
\Omega_{X}^{i}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{J}^{r}_{X}(E)
we compute

\differential\circ h_{i}+h_{i+1}\circ\differential=\differential\circ\iota_{\Xi}\circ L_{\Xi}^{-1}+\iota_{\Xi}\circ L^{-1}_{\Xi}\circ\differential=L_{\Xi}L_{\Xi}^{-1}

such that
h=\{h_{i}\}_{i\geqslant 1}
is a contracting homotopy in positive degrees.
∎

Examples of affine schemes that satisfy these hypotheses include affine cones with an Euler vector field: if the
\mathbf{G}_{m}
-action gives a weight decomposition on each
\Omega_{X}^{i}
with all weights nonzero for
i\geqslant 1
, then the Lie derivative along the Euler vector field acts diagonally with invertible eigenvalues on
\Omega_{X}^{\geqslant 1}

Corollary 2.3
.

If
X
is an affine scheme with such a vector field, then the complex of Vinogradov for
\sigma=(1,1,1,\ldots)
is acyclic.

In fact we can make the same argument for any

, by the concluding observation in the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

There is an adjoint version of (

) due initially to
[
Mal66
]
. Let
\mathscr{D}_{X}
be the ring of derivations on a smooth scheme

(following

\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{X})=\left[0\to\mathscr{D}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\bigwedge^{n}\mathscr{T}_{X}\to\cdots\to\mathscr{D}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\bigwedge^{2}\mathscr{T}_{X}\to\mathscr{D}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{T}_{X}\to\mathscr{D}_{X}\to 0\right]

with differential in degree

\displaystyle\differential(m \otimes\xi_1 \wedge\ldots\wedge\xi_k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j-1}(m\xi_{j})\otimes\xi_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\widehat{\xi}_{j}\wedge\ldots\wedge\xi_{k}

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant k}(-1)^{i+j}m\otimes[\xi_{i},\xi_{j}]\wedge\xi_{1}\wedge\ldots\widehat{\xi}_{i}\wedge\ldots\wedge\widehat{\xi}_{j}\wedge\ldots\wedge\xi_{k}.

Remark 2.4
.

The Spencer complex is a resolution of
\mathscr{O}_{X}
(as a left
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module) by locally free
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-modules because there is a
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-linear augmentation map
\mathscr{D}_{X}\to\mathscr{O}_{X}
sending a differential operator
\xi
to its evaluation at a constant
\xi(1)
; after that, the complex is locally a Koszul complex, from which exactness follows by the same argument as in Theorem
2.1
.

This complex is adjoint in the sense that when
\mathscr{T}_{X}
is locally free, one has

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathscr{D}_{X}}\left(\mathscr{D}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\bigwedge^{i}\mathscr{T}_{X},J^{\infty}(E)\right)=\bigwedge^{i}\mathscr{T}^{*}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}J^{\infty}(E).

with differential also dualised.

Since the hom functor is not exact, the question for the algebraic Spencer complex is not implied by our results on the topological Spencer complex, making it interesting to pose Vinogradov’s conjecture in that case. Moreover, the proof of Theorem
2.2
placed no assumptions on the class of singularity, but we expect the algebraic Spencer complex to be more sensitive to such data in the following sense. One can also consider any left
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{M}_{X}
and obtain the complex

\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}(\mathcal{M}_{X})=\left[0\to\mathcal{M}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\bigwedge^{n}\mathscr{T}_{X}\to\cdots\to\mathscr{D}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\bigwedge^{2}\mathscr{T}_{X}\to\mathscr{D}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{T}_{X}\to\mathscr{D}_{X}\to 0\right]

by properties of the tensor product. Let
X
be a scheme of dimension
n
. There is an isomorphism between the Spencer complex of
\mathcal{M}_{X}
and the
n
-shifted de Rham complex of
\mathcal{M}_{X}
,

\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}(\mathcal{M}_{X})\simeq\Omega^{n+\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}_{X}

-module arises from the pushforward of the

-module to the point, ‘integrating out’ the

\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}(\mathscr{J}^{r}_{X}(E))
up to said degree shift, in the sense that Johnson’s isomorphism describes local resolutions of

-modules on
X
using the Spencer complex, whilst the shifted de Rham complex describes how the same cohomological information appears through global integration pairings extracting de Rham cohomology.

Remark 2.5
.

Since the
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module action term
m\xi_{j}
in the differential is given by
\phi^{r}f\cdot\xi=\phi^{r}(\xi_{j}(f))
, we can interpret this cohomology as measuring obstructions to the integrability of
r
-jets by verifying compatibilities along a polyvector field (
i.e.
, agreement of differentiation in the directions prescribed by some exterior power of derivations). This is in the sense that the image of the differential is in the kernel of the term in degree less one precisely when different directions agree such that they cancel each other, meaning a jet is the derivative of a jet in degree less one when it respects compatibilities like Clairaut’s theorem and is not merely a formal expression. Correspondingly, we recover Spencer’s original motivation; namely, the study of the existence of solutions to partial differential equations, and its applications to notions of higher torsion invariants of manifolds (see
e.g.

[
Gui65
,
GS66
]
).

This is most obvious for the dualising sheaf
\mathcal{M}_{X}=\omega_{X}
. Using properties of smoothness of
X
, since there is a perfect pairing of polyvector fields on
\omega_{X}
the interior product,

\omega_{X}\otimes\bigwedge^{i}\mathscr{T}_{X}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Omega^{n-i}_{X},

\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}(\omega_{X})
reduces to the de Rham complex. This is now acyclic by the Poincaré lemma. Being CM implies various good properties from the viewpoint of algebraic topology; namely, it is the minimal condition for which intersection theory
[
Hoc78
,
Ful84
]
and available duality theorems
[
Har66
,
Kov13
]
work ‘as expected’. This is evident in the algebraic complex. Working in the derived category, we can consider chain complexes of
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-modules. On a scheme with worse singularities than CM we do not have concentration of the dualising complex; instead we have the resolution

\mathrm{Tot}\big(\omega^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}_{X}\otimes\mathrm{Sp}(\mathscr{D}_{X}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}})\big)

which is not the de Rham complex in general.

In general on a smooth scheme it is again true that locally the Spencer complex of a
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module is a Koszul complex such that the same acyclicity argument applies. However, in contrast to (
1.1
),
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-modules (and hence their Spencer complexes) are poorly behaved on singular schemes—and in particular, there is in general no such pairing, owing to the failure of Poincaré duality. One concludes that the adjoint version of Spencer cohomology is more sensitive to the topology of the variety and may elucidate why CM is a sufficient property to have a cohomology theory with desirable properties like acyclicity and Poincaré duality. We will proceed by investigating what algebraic Spencer cohomology actually measures in the singular case and why it is not a resolution of the structure sheaf, and how a sensible definition can be created in replacement.

3.
The naïve treatment of the singular case

In this section we will ask what the pushforward of Spencer cohomology actually computes when we have a closed immersion of a singular scheme into a smooth one, and motivate a different construction with better homological properties.

, at this level of view, it lacks some desirable properties. In particular, with the direct image being now different, the argument above does not apply and it is no longer obvious we are resolving
\mathscr{O}_{Y}
. This is in the sense that set-theoretic support is equivalent to stalks being non-zero, so the stalks agree, meaning
i^{\natural}\mathscr{T}_{Y}
is locally free on
i(Y)
(as an
\mathscr{O}_{Y}
-module) if and only if
\mathscr{T}_{Y}
was a locally free
\mathscr{O}_{Y}
-module in the first place. As such, the Koszul complex cannot be exact.

In a precise sense the failure to resolve
\mathscr{O}_{Y}
is because the Koszul complex detects homological information about the singularities through the irregularity of sections of
\mathscr{T}_{Y}
. Given the obvious augmentation map
\alpha\colon\mathscr{T}_{Y}\to\mathscr{O}_{Y}
the degree zero homology of the Koszul complex is isomorphic to
\mathscr{O}_{Y}/\alpha(\mathscr{T}_{Y})
and the higher homology sheaves are supported on
V(\alpha(\mathscr{T}_{Y}))
. In many concrete situations these data coincide with known invariants controlling the local geometry around the singularity. For example, for a singular complex hypersurface in a smooth ambient variety over
\mathbf{C}
, the image of
\mathscr{T}_{Y}
under evaluation is the Jacobian ideal, making
\mathscr{O}_{Y}/J
the algebra whose dimension is the Milnor number of the singular fibre. Viewing the Spencer complex as a twist of the Chevalley–Eilenberg complex of the Lie algebra of derivations of
\mathscr{O}_{Y}
, more examples are available to us, following
e.g.
the results in
[
EK06
]
.

Moreover, the statement that sections are annihilated by some power of the ideal contains an ambiguity: in general there need not be one exponent which kills the entire sheaf. For this reason one might postulate an inverse limit over all powers of the ideal. In fact one knows already that the cohomology of a sheaf supported on a subspace uses the
I
-adic completion and was defined as such for exactly this reason (see
e.g.
the account in
[
Har67
]
). On the other hand, losing control on local cohomology obstructs us from certain good results such as
[
BZN04
]
. For this reason we claim the cohomology of such a
\mathscr{D}
-module should be thought of as a local cohomology due to the support condition, and that we should be taking the completion of the direct image in general. The aim of what is to follow will be to provide such a definition and consider its properties, especially with respect to singular spaces.

In addition to being a very natural construction, we will remark that this construction both uses the smoothness of
X
to transfer certain nice properties to a
\mathscr{D}_{Y}
-module and the completion of
X
along
Y
to restore it to something with sole knowledge of
Y
. This closely parallels the construction of Hartshorne in
[
Har75
]
and we will indeed see that for a certain
\mathscr{D}_{Y}
-module we recover his theory; in this sense we can view our theory as a generalisation of his construction to arbitrary cohomologies obtainable as the Spencer cohomology of some sheaf, simultaneously offering another viewpoint on why completion arises in his work. We will find that the smoothness transfers acyclicity properties to the completed complex such that it is a resolution of the formal completion of
\mathscr{O}_{Y}
, addressing the first obstruction to having a meaningful cohomology as well.

4.
Completed Spencer cohomology

When it comes to constructing a complex on
Y
which is acyclic, we have two competing
desiderata
: one is to compute the cohomology of
Y
, and the other is that
\mathscr{D}_{Y}
-modules are poorly behaved. The solution to the second is to use Kashiwara’s equivalence and consider a
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module with support on
Y
. However, when we take the pushforward of the dualising complex to
X
and let it concentrate, if we restrict it to the singular subspace, it is no longer acyclic, and if we consider the entire sheaf, we are computing the cohomology of
X
.

This is not surprising: on a singular scheme, the most basic example of de Rham cohomology
\Omega_{Y}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}
is not a good resolution of
k_{Y}
, so we have no access to any cohomological properties of
Y
using the naïve complex. Let
Y\hookrightarrow X
be a closed embedding of schemes over a field of characteristic zero. One knows by the work of Grothendieck that when
Y
is smooth, the hypercohomology of the de Rham complex is well-behaved
[
Gro66
]
, whereas when
Y
is singular, we must consider the algebraic de Rham complex on the formal completion of
X
along
Y
, in which case one can show it is independent of the choice of
X
and enjoys various good properties
[
Har75
]
.

Some motivation for why Hartshorne’s argument works is as follows. In certain situations we can ‘restore’ non-singularity by completion. One observes that singularities persist in the formal completion of a smooth scheme along a singular subscheme, since the formal neighbourhoods of the completion contain the ideal of definition of the singular subscheme. What is true instead is that the completion along a subscheme contains all of the infinitesimal normal data of the subscheme, making it like a formally smooth tubuluar neighbourhood; this can be thought of conceptually as modifying the sheaf of the space in a way that adds enough smoothness for the usual sheaf cohomology machinery one wants to use. However (and to reiterate) this space is still equivalent to the original subscheme in the sense that the underlying topological spaces are the same—making the cohomology meaningful. At a technical level the smooth ambient variety is simply a receptacle for a calculation on the crystalline site
[
Ill75
]
—namely, by providing a scheme in which to find the tubular neighbourhood yielding our infinitesimal information. In particular, the technical advantage is a formal Poincaré lemma providing the comparison theorem Hartshorne seeks, a form of which was known already to Grothendieck in the crystalline context
[
Gro68
]
.

In this section we will present a satisfactory definition of the Spencer cohomology of a singular scheme. We will opt to study the imbeddable case, leaving the generalisation to arbitrary schemes of finite type to a sequel. We will find that since
\mathscr{O}_{X}
is coherent, the completion is not only exact but acts like a pullback to the underlying topological space of
Y
, making this cohomology meaningful.

4.1.
Smooth schemes and coherent sheaves

Let
X
be smooth (under the given conventions). We will first establish some auxiliary results for the case of coherent
\mathscr{D}_{Y}
-modules.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a closed imbedding of a singular variety into a smooth one. As discussed in §
3
,
a priori
the direct image requires a different definition in the singular case. Namely, we have (in the notation of
[
ES18
]
)

i_{\natural}\mathcal{M}_{Y}\simeq\mathcal{I}_{Y}\mathcal{M}_{X}\backslash\mathcal{M}_{X},\quad\mathcal{M}_{Y}\simeq i^{\natural}(\mathcal{I}_{Y}\mathcal{M}_{X}\backslash\mathcal{M}_{X})

where
\mathcal{M}_{X}
is a
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module. These functors are mutually quasi-inverse equivalences between
\mathscr{M}_{Y}
and
\mathscr{M}_{X}
. In the smooth setting, this is equivalent to the usual derived pushforward and pullback (one can see this by using smoothness to convert the quotient into a tensor product).

These functors are underived. There are the following counterparts on the bounded derived categories for arbitrary
f
:

f_{+}\colon D^{b}(\mathscr{D}_{Y})\to D^{b}(\mathscr{D}_{X}),\quad f^{!}\colon D^{b}(\mathscr{D}_{X})\to D^{b}(\mathscr{D}_{Y}).

Let
d=\dim Y-\dim X
. Explicit forms for these functors are available as

\mathbf{R}f_{*}\left(\mathcal{M}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}_{Y}\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{\mathscr{D}_{Y}}\mathscr{D}_{Y\to X}\right),\quad f^{*}\left(\mathcal{M}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}_{X}\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{f^{*}\mathscr{D}_{Y}}\mathscr{D}_{X\leftarrow Y}[d]\right)

where
f_{*},f^{*}
are the direct and inverse images of sheaves and

\mathscr{D}_{Y\to X}=\mathscr{O}_{Y}\otimes_{f^{*}\mathscr{O}_{X}}f^{*}\mathscr{D}_{X},\quad\mathscr{D}_{X\leftarrow Y}=\Omega_{Y}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{Y}}\mathscr{D}_{Y\to X}\otimes_{f^{*}\mathscr{O}_{X}}f^{*}\Omega^{-1}_{X}.

For a closed imbedding
i
,
i_{+}
coincides with
i_{\natural}
(
i^{!}
,
i^{\natural}
, respectively) in the sense that
i_{\natural}\mathcal{M}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}_{Y}
is the cohomology of
i_{+}\mathcal{M}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}_{Y}
(
i^{\natural}
,
i^{!}\mathcal{M}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}_{X}
where
\mathcal{M}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}_{X}
has cohomology supported on
i(Y)
, respectively). Said another way, for a closed imbedding,
i_{\natural}
is exact, and with the appropriate support condition on the image,
i^{\natural}
is exact.

Remark 4.1
.

Note that some similar assertions as ours concerning
\mathscr{D}
-module operations extending uniquely to morphisms between formal schemes are made without complete proofs in
[
BD91
]
. We choose to take the more arduous route of verifying the completion preserves these operations because of the ability to reuse many arguments in the derived setting to come.

In this section we will assume
\mathscr{D}_{X}
is finitely-generated. This will be important in the proofs of Proposition
4.11
and Theorem
4.17
where it will guarantee that completion sends
\mathscr{D}_{X}
to a locally free
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-module such that the completed Spencer complex is a complex of locally free
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-modules. Even when this assumption is unrealistic, we can take a finite stage of the filtration on
\mathscr{D}_{X}
, which will in general be coherent for a locally free
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module. We will comment in various places about when statements for infinitely-generated
\mathscr{D}_{X}
can be made and when looking at the filtration preserves those statements.

Definition 4.2
.

Write
\widehat{X}
for the completion of
X
along
Y
. There exists a map of ringed spaces
\kappa\colon\widehat{X}\to X
and the pullback of any
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{F}
by
\kappa
, denoted

\kappa^{*}\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}}.

By
[
EGA I
, 10.8.8]
, if
\mathcal{F}
is coherent as an
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-module, then
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}\simeq\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}}
. Thus we call the inverse image sheaf
\kappa^{*}\mathcal{F}
the completion of
\mathcal{F}
.

Similarly to this definition, viewing
\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}}
as a chain complex concentrated in degree zero, we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\qquad\qquad\qquad\kappa^{*}\mathcal{F}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}&=\mathcal{F}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}}\\
&=\mathcal{F}^{n}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}}\to\mathcal{F}^{n-1}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}}\to\ldots\to\mathcal{F}^{0}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}}\end{split}

with differential
\differential\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\text{id}_{\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}}}
. If
\mathcal{F}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}
is coherent in each degree, then we have an isomorphism of chain complexes (
loc cit
).

Remark 4.3
.

The reader should mind that Definition
4.2
involves an abuse of notation, as the expression for
\kappa^{*}\mathcal{F}
should read

\kappa^{-1}\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\kappa^{-1}\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}}

or equivalently—since the underlying topological space of

\mathcal{F}|_{Y}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}|_{Y}}\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}}.

However, we can extend our restricted sheaves by zero, which does not change the formula. This applies identically to (
4.1
).

On
X
we may construct
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-modules which are coherent as
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-modules, being equivalent to vector bundles with integrable connections. This will be the setting of what follows. With reference to (
4.1
), we will consider

\kappa^{*}\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}(\mathcal{M}_{X})=\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}(\mathcal{M}_{X})\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}}

and the corresponding natural map of complexes
\kappa^{*}\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}(\mathcal{M}_{X})\xrightarrow{\sim}\widehat{\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}(\mathcal{M}_{X})}
. We will call the above product the completion of the Spencer complex of

.

We will need the following lemma to generalise the statement that completion commutes with tensor operations to include the universal enveloping algebra.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
F^{p}T(\mathscr{F})
be the
p
-th stage of the filtration of the tensor algebra of some coherent sheaf
\mathscr{F}
. The completion commutes with taking quotients of
F^{p}T(\mathscr{F})
for any finite
p
so that
\widehat{F^{p}T(\mathscr{F})}\simeq F^{p}T(\widehat{\mathscr{F}})
.

Proof.

The
p
-th stage of the filtration of the tensor algebra of a sheaf
\mathscr{F}
is a length
p
direct sum of multi-fold tensor products. Since completion commutes with finite direct sums it suffices to prove the claim for

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.5pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathscr{F}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\ldots\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{F}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}$}}{\raisebox{3.5pt}{$\textstyle{\mathscr{F}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\ldots\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{F}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathcal{I}}$}}{\raisebox{3.5pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathscr{F}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\ldots\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{F}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathcal{I}}$}}{\raisebox{3.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathscr{F}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\ldots\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{F}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{I}}$}}}.

By the exactness of completion on coherent sheaves this is isomorphic to

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.61111pt}{$\displaystyle{\widehat{\mathscr{F}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\ldots\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{F}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.61111pt}{$\displaystyle{\widehat{\mathcal{I}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.61111pt}{$\textstyle{\widehat{\mathscr{F}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\ldots\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{F}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.61111pt}{$\textstyle{\widehat{\mathcal{I}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.61111pt}{$\scriptstyle{\widehat{\mathscr{F}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\ldots\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{F}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.61111pt}{$\scriptstyle{\widehat{\mathcal{I}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.61111pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\widehat{\mathscr{F}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\ldots\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{F}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.61111pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\widehat{\mathcal{I}}}$}}}

and by
[
EGA I
, 7.7.1]
the tensor product commutes with completions to yield the claim.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
X
be smooth of dimension
m
and
\widehat{X}
be the completion of
X
along some singular subscheme
Y=V(\mathcal{I})
of dimension
n
. The completion along
\mathcal{I}
of the ring of differential operators of order at most
p
on
X
is isomorphic to the ring of differential operators of order at most
p
on
\widehat{X}
.

Proof.

The sheaf of differential operators is equivalent to the universal enveloping algebra of the tangent sheaf, hence to a quotient of the tensor algebra of
\mathscr{T}_{X}
by certain commutation relations. Take the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt filtration
F^{p}(\mathscr{D}_{X})
yielding differential operators of order at most
p
. This yields

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{F^{p}T(\mathscr{T}_{X})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.5714pt}{$\displaystyle{F^{p}\mathcal{R}}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{F^{p}T(\mathscr{T}_{X})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{F^{p}\mathcal{R}}$}}{\raisebox{2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{F^{p}T(\mathscr{T}_{X})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.58528pt}{$\scriptstyle{F^{p}\mathcal{R}}$}}{\raisebox{1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{F^{p}T(\mathscr{T}_{X})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83159pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{F^{p}\mathcal{R}}$}}}

where
T(\mathscr{T}_{X})
is the tensor algebra of
\mathscr{T}_{X}
and
\mathcal{R}
is the sheaf of ideals for the commutation relations. Now by Lemma
4.4
we have
F^{p}\widehat{U(\mathscr{T}_{X})}=F^{p}U(\widehat{\mathscr{T}_{X}})
.
∎

As an abuse of notation we will write
\widehat{\mathscr{D}_{X}}\simeq\mathscr{D}_{\widehat{X}}
, though this is to be understood in the sense of a finite stage of the filtration.

Remark 4.6
.

Crucially, the stalk of a finite stage of the filtration of
\mathscr{D}_{X}
is Noetherian. One can then verify as a sanity check that at the stalks we have an expression for the filtration of the ring of derivations on
\widehat{X}
, seen as a quotient of the free
\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}}
-algebra generated by derivations in coordinate directions of

and in infinitesimal normal directions inside

. By definition this is the stalk of

.

Corollary 4.7
.

For
\mathscr{D}_{X}
finitely-generated,
\widehat{\mathscr{D}_{X}}\simeq\mathscr{D}_{\widehat{X}}
on the nose.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
X
be a smooth scheme locally of finite type and
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathscr{O}_{X}
a coherent sheaf of ideals. One has an isomorphism
\widehat{\mathscr{T}_{X}}\simeq\mathscr{T}_{\widehat{X}}
.

Proof.

Observe that we have the isomorphism
\widehat{\mathscr{T}_{X}}=\widehat{\mathcal{H}om(\Omega^{1}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X})}\simeq\mathcal{H}om(\widehat{\Omega^{1}_{X}},\widehat{\mathscr{O}_{X}})
. Since
\Omega^{1}_{X}
is coherent and completion commutes with taking differentials for finitely-presented modules,
\widehat{\Omega^{1}_{X}}\simeq\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{X}}
. Therefore
\widehat{\mathscr{T}_{X}}\simeq\mathcal{H}om(\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{X}},\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}})=\mathscr{T}_{\widehat{X}}
.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

If
\mathcal{M}
is an
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-coherent
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module then
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}\simeq\kappa^{*}\mathcal{M}
as a
\mathscr{D}_{\widehat{X}}
-module.

Proof.

Take the completion

\widehat{\mathcal{M}}=\varprojlim{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.5714pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}^{r}\mathcal{M}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathcal{M}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathcal{I}^{r}\mathcal{M}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.58528pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathcal{I}^{r}\mathcal{M}}$}}{\raisebox{1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.7986pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{I}^{r}\mathcal{M}}$}}}.

By
[
EGA I
, 10.8.8]
, if
\mathcal{M}
is coherent as an
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-module, then
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}\simeq\kappa^{-1}\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\kappa^{-1}\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}}
. As usual we can provide the
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-module inverse image the structure of a
\mathscr{D}_{\widehat{X}}
-module. Explicitly, choosing an

and denoting
\differential{\kappa}\colon\mathscr{T}_{\widehat{X}}\to\kappa^{*}\mathscr{T}_{X}
, from the Leibnitz rule we have the equation

\xi(s\otimes m)=\xi(s)\otimes m+s\cdot\differential{\kappa(\xi)(m)}

for the action of derivations on

.
∎

Remark 4.10
.

In general Lemma

Proposition 4.11
.

(1)

The completion of the Spencer complex of
\mathscr{D}_{X}
,
\widehat{\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{X})}
, is given by the complex whose

-th degree is

\widehat{\mathscr{D}_{X}}\,\,\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\bigwedge^{i}\mathscr{T}_{\widehat{X}}.

(2)

Let
\mathscr{D}_{X}
be finitely-generated. This complex is a complex of locally free
\widehat{\mathscr{D}_{X}}
-modules; identically, locally free
\mathscr{D}_{\widehat{X}}
-modules.

(3)

The completion of the Spencer complex of
\mathcal{M}
is the Spencer complex of the completion of
\mathcal{M}
.

(4)

The completion of the Spencer complex of
\mathcal{M}
is the completed tensor product of
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{X})

Proof.

(1)

We will complete each degree separately. The claim follows by
[
EGA I
, 7.7.1]
, the fact that completion commutes with tensor operations (such as exterior powers), and Lemma
4.8
.

(2)

To see this, note that the Spencer complex consists of finitely-generated locally free
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-modules. Since completion commutes with finite direct sums, the completion is also locally free. There is an obvious action of
\widehat{\mathscr{D}_{X}}
on each degree from completion. This promotes to an action of
\mathscr{D}_{\widehat{X}}
.

(3)

This claim follows from the same argument that showed (1).

(4)

Firstly we apply the same argument which we used to show (1). Now since
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
is naturally a
\widehat{\mathscr{D}_{X}}
-module, the tensor product

\widehat{\mathcal{M}}\otimes\widehat{\mathscr{D}_{X}}\otimes\bigwedge^{i}\mathscr{T}_{\widehat{X}}

absorbs the

\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
.

∎

Remark 4.12
.

The assumption of
\mathscr{D}_{X}
finitely-generated was only used in (ii) to establish local freeness. We can take a filtration of

and not lose any homological properties by the reasoning earlier in this section, culminating in Corollary

.

Definition 4.13
.

Let
Y
be imbeddable as a closed subscheme into a smooth scheme
X
lying over
k
by a map of schemes
f
. Then we define the algebraic Spencer cohomology of a
\mathscr{D}_{Y}
-module
\mathcal{M}_{Y}
by taking the hypercohomology of the completion along
Y
of the pushed forward complex (
3.1
),

H^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Sp}}(Y,\mathcal{M}_{Y})=\mathbf{H}^{\ast}\left(\widehat{X},\widehat{\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}(\mathcal{M}_{X})}\right).

Now we will investigate some properties of this cohomology. We will primarily be interested in decomposing the completed Spencer complex into completed pieces and producing the fact that the completed Spencer complex computes the cohomology of a formal scheme with coefficients in an
\mathcal{I}
-adically completed
\widehat{\mathscr{D}_{X}}
-module.

Now in general we can say the following.

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
\widehat{g}\colon\widehat{X}\to\mathrm{Spf}\,k
be the map to the formal point induced by the completion of
g\colon X\to\mathrm{Spec}\,k
. The pullback
\widehat{g^{*}\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{Spec}\,k}}
is
\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}}
.

Proof.

By definition we have
\widehat{g^{*}\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{Spec}\,k}}=\widehat{\mathscr{O}_{X}\otimes_{g^{-1}\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{Spec}\,k}}g^{-1}\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{Spec}\,k}}
. Commutativity grants us
\widehat{\mathscr{O}_{X}\otimes_{g^{-1}\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{Spec}\,k}}g^{-1}\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{Spec}\,k}}=\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}}\widehat{\otimes}_{\widehat{g^{-1}\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{Spec}\,k}}}\widehat{g^{-1}\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{Spec}\,k}}
. Since
\mathrm{Spec}\,k
is a one point space,
\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{Spec}\,k}=\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{Spec}\,k}
, from which the claim follows.
∎

Corollary 4.15
.

The pullback to
X
of a finite stage of the filtration on
\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{Spec}\,k}
is also
\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}}
since all differential operators of order greater than zero must vanish.

From this we deduce that the completed Spencer cohomology arises naturally from pulling back a
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module to
\widehat{X}
and then pushing it forward to a formal point, so only requires the input data of Definition
4.13
.

Proposition 4.16
.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
and
\mathcal{M}_{Y}
be as in Definition
4.13
,
f_{+}\mathcal{M}_{Y}
be coherent as a sheaf of
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-modules, and define

\widehat{g}\colon\widehat{X}\to\mathrm{Spf}\,k.

The cohomology

is equivalently

\widehat{g}_{+}(\kappa^{*}\mathcal{M}_{X}).

Proof.

By
[
EGA I
, 10.8.8]
we have level-wise isomorphisms of modules in each degree of the Spencer complex, and hence an isomorphism of chain complexes

As a consequence of Proposition
4.11
.(ii) we also have

\widehat{\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}(\mathcal{M}_{X})}\simeq\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}(\widehat{\mathcal{M}_{X}}).

Let

. It follows that

. From Lemma

∎

Theorem 4.17
.

(1)

The completed Spencer complex of
\widehat{\mathscr{D}_{X}}
is a resolution of
\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}}
by locally free
\widehat{\mathscr{D}_{X}}
-modules.

(2)

The completed Spencer complex of
\mathcal{M}
computes the cohomology of
X
with coefficients in
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
.

Proof.

(1)

We will show that
\widehat{\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{X})}
resolves
\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}}
. We have established that this is isomorphic to
\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}(\widehat{\mathscr{D}_{X}})
. Since the augmentation map

\mathscr{D}_{X}\to\mathscr{O}_{X},\quad\xi\mapsto\xi(1)

is locally a surjective
\mathscr{O}_{X}(U)
-module homomorphism, it is continuous in the
I
-adic topology (to see this recall that a homomorphism of modules
\varphi\colon M\to N
has the property that
\varphi(I^{n}M)
is contained in
I^{n}N
for all
n
); following that observation, we can globalise the induced mapping to
\widehat{\mathscr{D}_{X}}\to\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}}
, sending a completed derivation
\widehat{\xi}\in\widehat{\mathscr{D}_{X}}
to its evaluation at the image of the constant in
\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}}
,
\widehat{\xi}(\widehat{1})\in\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}}
. This is also a surjection with kernel generated by completed differential operators with no zeroth-order part,
i.e.

\mathscr{T}_{\widehat{X}}
. Let
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{n+1},\ldots,y_{n+m})
be étale-local coordinates. To verify exactness, at a stalk we can apply the Koszul part of the differential to a basis for
\mathscr{T}_{\widehat{X}}

(2)

We will complete the derived pushforward

g_{+}\mathcal{M}={\bf R}g_{*}(\mathcal{M}\otimes^{\bf L}_{\mathscr{D}_{X}}g^{*}\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{Spec}\,k}).

It is useful to note that the derived completion commutes with derived pushforwards and derived tensor products, and that for coherent sheaves, completion is exact. For this reason we have

\widehat{{\bf R}g_{*}(\mathcal{M}\otimes^{\bf L}_{\mathscr{D}_{X}}g^{*}\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{Spec}\,k})}\simeq{\bf R}g_{*}(\widehat{\mathcal{M}}\widehat{\otimes}^{\bf L}_{\mathscr{D}_{X}}\widehat{g^{*}\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{Spec}\,k}})

In turn, by Lemma
4.14

{\bf R}g_{*}(\widehat{\mathcal{M}}\,\,\widehat{\otimes}^{\bf L}_{\mathscr{D}_{X}}\mathscr{O}_{\widehat{X}}).

Now we apply the first part of the claim to obtain

{\bf R}g_{*}\left(\widehat{\mathcal{M}}\,\,\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathscr{D}_{X}}\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}(\widehat{\mathscr{D}_{X}})\right).

∎

Theorem 4.18

Proof.

Let

\widehat{\phantom{...}}\colon\mathbf{Coh}(\mathscr{O}_{X})\to\mathbf{Coh}(\mathscr{O}_{X},\mathcal{I}).

\widehat{\phantom{...}}\colon D^{b}_{\mathrm{coh}}(\mathscr{O}_{X})\to D^{b}_{\mathrm{coh}}(\mathscr{O}_{X},\mathcal{I}).

Let

-modules seen as bounded chain complexes concentrated in degree zero. By functoriality of

Remark 4.19

5.
The theory and properties of derived complete
\mathscr{D}
-modules

We will now study the derived completion of a
\mathscr{D}
-module. Since
\mathscr{D}
-modules are naturally objects of the derived category, especially on singular varieties, and the homology of complexes of
\mathscr{D}
-modules offers important information, we will study the derived completion. In particular, completion is only exact on coherent sheaves—a restrictive assumption in the singular case.

Let us introduce some of the basic ideas. Recall that the
I
-adic completion of a module is

\widehat{M}=\varprojlim_{r}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{M}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.5714pt}{$\displaystyle{I^{r}M}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{M}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{I^{r}M}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{M}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.58528pt}{$\scriptstyle{I^{r}M}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{M}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.7986pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{I^{r}M}$}}}

and a module

is

-adically complete when the natural map
M\to\widehat{M}
is an isomorphism. In this sense we distinguish between
completed
and
complete
objects. If
I
is finitely-generated, a completed module is complete.

We can apply this to a chain complex by working in degrees. In general a chain complex is called completed if it is a complex of
I
-adically completed
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-modules—namely, for a chain complex of
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-modules
(C^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}},\differential)
, we have the
I
-adic completion
\widehat{C^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}}
obtained by completing in degrees:

\widehat{C^{k}}=\varprojlim_{r}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{4.49501pt}{$\displaystyle{C^{k}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.49501pt}{$\displaystyle{I^{r}C^{k}}$}}{\raisebox{4.24501pt}{$\textstyle{C^{k}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.24501pt}{$\textstyle{I^{r}C^{k}}$}}{\raisebox{3.24501pt}{$\scriptstyle{C^{k}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.24501pt}{$\scriptstyle{I^{r}C^{k}}$}}{\raisebox{2.45833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{C^{k}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.45833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{I^{r}C^{k}}$}}}.

\mathcal{M}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}_{X}
.

Proof.

Suppose that for each
i
, we have a quasi-isomorphism

F^{i,{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}}\longrightarrow M^{i}

where
F^{i,{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}}
is a bounded complex of flat
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules. Then
F^{{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}},{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}}
forms a double complex with
i
indexing columns. We claim the total complex is quasi-isomorphic to
M^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}
. To show this we can use the standard spectral sequence argument for double complexes. Filter
\mathrm{Tot}(F^{\bullet,\bullet})
by columns (the
i
-index). The
E_{1}
page is

E_{1}^{p,q}=H^{q}(F^{p,\bullet})\simeq\begin{cases}M^{p},&q=0,\\
0,&q\neq 0,\end{cases}

by the assumption that
F^{p,\bullet}\to M^{p}
is a quasi-isomorphism. We know the spectral sequence converges to the cohomology of the total complex:

E_{1}^{p,q}\implies H^{p+q}(\mathrm{Tot}(F^{\bullet,\bullet})).

Since
E_{1}^{p,q}=0
for
q\neq 0
, the spectral sequence collapses at
E_{1}
and

H^{i}(\mathrm{Tot}(F^{{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}},{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}}))\simeq H^{i}(M^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}).

The natural map

\mathrm{Tot}(F^{{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}},{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}})\longrightarrow M^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}

induces the identity on
H^{i}

via
the augmentation maps
F^{p,\bullet}\to M^{p}
, so it is a quasi-isomorphism between the total complex and
M^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}
. Replacing
M^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}
with
\mathcal{M}_{X}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.75}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}
and taking the total complex of its Spencer resolution, the statement is proven.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a sheaf of
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-modules,
\mathcal{I}
the ideal of definition of some subsheaf generated by sections
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
, and
\widehat{\mathscr{F}}_{\mathcal{I}}
the
\mathcal{I}
-adic completion of
\mathscr{F}
. Let
\mathcal{J}
be some other ideal in
\mathscr{O}_{X}
generated by sections
h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}
. There exists a Koszul complex

\widehat{\mathrm{Kos}(\mathscr{F};h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})_{\mathcal{I}}}

which is exact when a regular sequence can be found in
\widehat{\mathscr{F}}
whose intersection with
\mathcal{I}
is empty.

Proof.

The Koszul complex of
s\colon\mathscr{F}\to\mathscr{O}_{X}
is the chain complex

0\to\bigwedge^{n}\mathscr{F}\to\ldots\to\bigwedge^{1}\mathscr{F}\to\mathscr{O}_{X}\to 0

with differential
\differential=\sum(-1)^{i+1}s(h_{i})h_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\hat{h_{i}}\wedge\ldots\wedge h_{n}

whose homology in degree zero is

H^{0}\left(\mathrm{Kos}(\mathscr{F};h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})\right)\simeq{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathscr{F}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}\mathscr{F}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathscr{F}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathcal{J}\mathscr{F}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathscr{F}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathcal{J}\mathscr{F}}$}}{\raisebox{1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathscr{F}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{J}\mathscr{F}}$}}}={\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathscr{O}_{X}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathscr{O}_{X}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathcal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathscr{O}_{X}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathcal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathscr{O}_{X}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{J}}$}}}\otimes\mathscr{F}.

From the disjointness of the ideal the derived completion of the Koszul complex is

(6.1)

\widehat{\mathrm{Kos}(\mathscr{F};h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})}_{\mathcal{I}}=\mathbf{R}\varprojlim_{r}\big(\mathrm{Kos}(\mathscr{F};h^{r}_{1},\ldots,h^{r}_{n})\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{A}\mathrm{Kos}(\mathscr{F};f_{1}^{r},\ldots,f_{n}^{r})\big)

and the degree zero homology of the completion is

H^{0}\left(\widehat{\mathrm{Kos}(\mathscr{F};h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})}_{\mathcal{I}}\right)\simeq{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.61111pt}{$\displaystyle{\widehat{\mathscr{F}}_{\mathcal{I}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.61111pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}\widehat{\mathscr{F}}_{\mathcal{I}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.61111pt}{$\textstyle{\widehat{\mathscr{F}}_{\mathcal{I}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.61111pt}{$\textstyle{\mathcal{J}\widehat{\mathscr{F}}_{\mathcal{I}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.61111pt}{$\scriptstyle{\widehat{\mathscr{F}}_{\mathcal{I}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.61111pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathcal{J}\widehat{\mathscr{F}}_{\mathcal{I}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.61111pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\widehat{\mathscr{F}}_{\mathcal{I}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.61111pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{J}\widehat{\mathscr{F}}_{\mathcal{I}}}$}}}.

Now note that for a derived tensor product where at least one factor is chain homotopic to zero, the entire product has a chain mapping to the zero object. Suppose
\mathcal{J}
contains a regular sequence. We now have in (
6.1
) the derived limit of the zero object, to which we apply the fact that the derived limit of the zero object is homologicaly trivial.
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

The complex (
6.1
) is isomorphic to the the Koszul complex of the completion of
\mathscr{F}
, and if a sequence is regular in
\mathscr{F}
, then its image is regular in
\widehat{\mathscr{F}}
.

Proof.

By the arguments in
[
Sha21
, §2]
we have

\widehat{\mathrm{Kos}(\mathscr{F};h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})}_{\mathcal{I}}\simeq\mathrm{Kos}(\widehat{\mathscr{F}}_{\mathcal{I}};\widehat{h_{1}},\ldots,\widehat{h_{n}})

so that the homology is identical, and in particular, the degree zero homology of the Koszul complex of the completion is also
\widehat{\mathscr{F}}_{\mathcal{I}}/\mathcal{J}\widehat{\mathscr{F}}_{\mathcal{I}}
. Now the second part of the claim follows from the isomorphism: whenever
h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}
is regular,
\mathrm{Kos}(\widehat{\mathscr{F}}_{\mathcal{I}};\widehat{h_{1}},\ldots,\widehat{h_{n}})
is exact.
∎

(1)

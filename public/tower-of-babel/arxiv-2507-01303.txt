On interval decomposition of persistence modules
Wee Liang Gan, Nadiya Upegui Keagy

Abstract
. We give down-to-earth proofs of the structure theorems for persistence modules.



Introduction

In this article, we give alternative proofs of the following theorems.

Theorem A
.

Let
V
be a persistence module indexed by a poset. Then interval decompositions of
V
, if they exist, are unique up to automorphisms of
V
.

Theorem B
.

Let
V
be a pointwise finite dimensional persistence module indexed by a totally ordered set. Then
V
has an interval decomposition.

Theorem C
.

Let
V
be a pointwise finite dimensional persistence module indexed by a zigzag poset. Then
V
has an interval decomposition.

Theorem A is a special case of the Krull-Remak-Schmidt-Azumaya Theorem, see for example
[
7
]
*§4.8. We give a simple direct proof of it in Section
3
; see Theorem
3.4
.

Theorem B was proved by Botnan and Crawley-Boevey in
[
1
]
; we refer the reader to
[
6
]
*§1.5 and
[
8
]
*§2.1 for discussions of earlier results. Our proof of Theorem B results from our attempt to understand a sketch of its proof given by Gabriel and Roiter in
[
4
]
*§3.6. We give our proof of Theorem B in Section
6
; see Theorem
6.10
.

The definition of a zigzag poset is given in Section
7
; see Definition
7.2
. An example of a zigzag poset is the poset
Z(\gamma)
studied by Botnan and Crawley-Boevey in
[
1
]
*§5.1 for which they proved Theorem C; see also
[
5
]
.

In Section
8
, we prove Theorem C when the zigzag poset has only finitely many extrema; see Theorem
8.1
.

In Section
10
, we prove Theorem C when the zigzag poset has infinitely many extrema; see Theorem
10.4
and Theorem
10.7
.

This article is essentially self-contained; we use only elementary results in linear algebra and Zorn’s lemma.

1.
Definitions and notation

Fix a field
k
. Let
P
be a poset. By a
persistence module
indexed by
P
, we mean a functor from
P
regarded as a category to the category of
k
-vector spaces. A
morphism
of persistence modules indexed by
P
is, by definition, a natural transformation of functors.

Notation 1.1
.

Let
P
be a poset. Let
V
be any persistence module indexed by
P
. We write
V_{x}
for the
k
-vector space which
V
assigns to
x\in P
. We write
V_{x,y}:V_{x}\to V_{y}
for the
k
-linear map which
V
assigns to
x,y\in P
when
x\leq y
.

Notation 1.2
.

Let
P
be a poset. Let
f:V\to W
be any morphism of persistence modules indexed by
P
. For each
x\in P
, we write
f_{x}:V_{x}\to W_{x}
for the component of
f
at
x
.

Notation 1.3
.

Let
P
be a poset. Let
V
be a persistence module indexed by
P
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a set of submodules of
V
giving an internal direct sum decomposition

V=\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{A}}U.

Then for each
U\in\mathcal{A}
, we write
\mathrm{in}^{U}:U\to V
and
\mathrm{pr}^{U}:V\to U
for the inclusion and projection, respectively.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
P
be a poset. Let
V
be a persistence module indexed by
P
. We say that
V
is
pointwise finite dimensional
if
V_{x}
is finite dimensional for all
x\in P
.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
P
be a poset. Let
I
be a subset of
P
.

(1)

We say that
I
is
convex
if: for all
x,y,z\in P
, we have
y\in I
whenever
x,z\in I
and
x\leq y\leq z
.

(2)

We say that
I
is
connected
if: for all
x,z\in I
, there exist
y_{0},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in I
such that

(1.5.1)

\begin{cases}y_{0}=x,\\
y_{n}=z,\\
y_{i-1}\leq y_{i}\kern 5.0pt\mbox{ or }\kern 5.0pty_{i-1}\geq y_{i}\quad\mbox{ for each }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.\end{cases}

(3)

We call
I
an
interval
in
P
if
I
is nonempty, convex, and connected.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
P
be a poset. Let
I
be an interval in
P
. The
constant module

M(I)
is the persistence module indexed by
P
defined as follows:

(1)

for all
x\in P
,

M(I)_{x}=\begin{cases}k&\mbox{ if }x\in I,\\
0&\mbox{ if }x\notin I;\end{cases}

(2)

for all
x\leq y
in
P
,

M(I)_{x,y}=\begin{cases}\mathrm{id}_{k}&\mbox{ if }x,y\in I,\\
0&\mbox{ if }x\notin I\mbox{ or }y\notin I.\end{cases}

Definition 1.7
.

Let
P
be a poset. An
interval module
indexed by
P
is a persistence module indexed by
P
which is isomorphic to a constant module.

Remark 1.8
.

The zero persistence module indexed by a poset is not an interval module.

Definition 1.9
.

Let
P
be a poset. Let
V
be a persistence module indexed by
P
. An
interval decomposition
of
V
is an internal direct sum decomposition

V=\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{A}}U

where
\mathcal{A}
is a (possibly empty) set of submodules of
V
such that every
U\in\mathcal{A}
is an interval module.

2.
Reminder on constant modules

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
P
be a poset. Let
I
be an interval in
P
. Let
f:M(I)\to M(I)
be a morphism. Then there exists a unique
\lambda\in k
such that
f=\lambda\cdot\mathrm{id}_{M(I)}
.

Proof.

For each
x\in I
, there exists a unique
\lambda_{x}\in k
such that
f_{x}=\lambda_{x}\cdot\mathrm{id}_{k}
. We need to prove that for all
x,z\in I
, we have
\lambda_{x}=\lambda_{z}
.

Observe that for all
w,y\in I
such that
w\leq y
, we have

M(I)_{w,y}\circ f_{w}=f_{y}\circ M(I)_{w,y},

which implies that
\lambda_{w}=\lambda_{y}
.

Now consider any
x,z\in I
. Since
I
is connected, there exist
y_{0},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in I
such that (
1.5.1
) holds. By the above observation, we have
\lambda_{y_{0}}=\lambda_{y_{1}}=\cdots=\lambda_{y_{n}}
, hence
\lambda_{x}=\lambda_{z}
.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
P
be a poset. Let
I
be an interval in
P
. Then
M(I)
is indecomposable.

Proof.

Suppose that
M(I)=U\oplus W
, where
U,W
are submodules of
M(I)
. Define
f:M(I)\to M(I)
by

f=\mathrm{in}^{U}\circ\mathrm{pr}^{U}

(see Notation
1.3
).
We have
f\circ f=f
. By Lemma
2.1
, we also have
f=\lambda\cdot\mathrm{id}_{M(I)}
for some
\lambda\in k
. Observe that:

f\circ f=f\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\lambda^{2}=\lambda\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\lambda=0\mbox{ or }\lambda=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad U=0\mbox{ or }W=0.

∎

3.
Interval decomposition

In this section, we prove Theorem A and a criterion for the existence of an interval decomposition.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
P
be a poset. Let
I
and
J
be distinct intervals in
P
. Let
g:M(I)\to M(J)
and
h:M(J)\to M(I)
be morphisms. Then
h\circ g=0
.

Proof.

First, by Lemma
2.1
, there exists a
\lambda\in k
such that

(3.1.1)

h\circ g=\lambda\cdot\mathrm{id}_{M(I)}.

Next, since
I\neq J
, we have
I-J\neq{\cancel{\mathrm{o}}}
or
J-I\neq{\cancel{\mathrm{o}}}
.

Case 1
:
I-J\neq{\cancel{\mathrm{o}}}
.

Let
x\in I-J
. Then:

x\in I
, so by (
3.1.1
) we have
h_{x}\circ g_{x}=\lambda\cdot\mathrm{id}_{k}
;

x\notin J
, so
M(J)_{x}=0
, which implies that
g_{x}=0
and
h_{x}=0
.

It follows that
\lambda=0
, so by (
3.1.1
) we have
h\circ g=0
.

Case 2
:
J-I\neq{\cancel{\mathrm{o}}}
.

We have
g\circ h=0
from Case 1 by symmetry. This implies that
g\circ h\circ g=0
, hence by (
3.1.1
) we have
\lambda\cdot g=0
.

Obviously if
g=0
, then
h\circ g=0
. On the other hand, if
g\neq 0
, then
\lambda\cdot g=0
implies that
\lambda=0
, so by (
3.1.1
) we again have
h\circ g=0
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
P
be a poset. Let
U,W,Z
be interval modules indexed by
P
. Let
g:U\to W
and
h:W\to Z
be morphisms. Assume that

W\ncong U
and
Z\cong U
. Then
h\circ g=0
.

Proof.

Immediate from Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
P
be a poset. Let
V
be a persistence module indexed by
P
. Let

V=\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{A}}U\quad\mbox{ and }\quad V=\bigoplus_{W\in\mathcal{B}}W

be interval decompositions of
V
. Let
I
be an interval in
P
. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle=\{U\in\mathcal{A}\mid U\cong M(I)\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle=\{W\in\mathcal{B}\mid W\ncong M(I)\}.

Then

\left(\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{C}}U\right)\cap\left(\bigoplus_{W\in\mathcal{D}}W\right)=0.

Proof.

Let
x\in P
. Let

v\in\left(\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{C}}U_{x}\right)\cap\left(\bigoplus_{W\in\mathcal{D}}W_{x}\right).

We need to show that
v=0
.

There exists a finite subset
\mathcal{C}(v)
of
\mathcal{C}
such that

(3.3.1)

v=\sum_{U\in\mathcal{C}(v)}(\mathrm{in}^{U}_{x}\circ\mathrm{pr}^{U}_{x})(v)

(see Notation
1.3
).
There also exists a finite subset
\mathcal{D}(v)
of
\mathcal{D}
such that

(3.3.2)

v=\sum_{W\in\mathcal{D}(v)}(\mathrm{in}^{W}_{x}\circ\mathrm{pr}^{W}_{x})(v).

Let

\displaystyle g^{U,W}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{pr}^{W}\circ\mathrm{in}^{U}\qquad\mbox{ for all }U\in\mathcal{A},\;W\in\mathcal{B};

\displaystyle h^{W,Z}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{pr}^{Z}\circ\mathrm{in}^{W}\qquad\mbox{ for all }W\in\mathcal{B},\;Z\in\mathcal{A}.

Then

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{C}(v)}(\mathrm{in}^{Z}_{x}\circ\mathrm{pr}^{Z}_{x})(v)

by (
3.3.1
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{C}(v)}\sum_{W\in\mathcal{D}(v)}(\mathrm{in}^{Z}_{x}\circ\mathrm{pr}^{Z}_{x}\circ\mathrm{in}^{W}_{x}\circ\mathrm{pr}^{W}_{x})(v)

by (
3.3.2
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{C}(v)}\sum_{W\in\mathcal{D}(v)}\sum_{U\in\mathcal{C}(v)}(\mathrm{in}^{Z}_{x}\circ\mathrm{pr}^{Z}_{x}\circ\mathrm{in}^{W}_{x}\circ\mathrm{pr}^{W}_{x}\circ\mathrm{in}^{U}_{x}\circ\mathrm{pr}^{U}_{x})(v)

by (
3.3.1
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{C}(v)}\sum_{W\in\mathcal{D}(v)}\sum_{U\in\mathcal{C}(v)}(\mathrm{in}^{Z}_{x}\circ h^{W,Z}_{x}\circ g^{U,W}_{x}\circ\mathrm{pr}^{U}_{x})(v)

\displaystyle=0

by Lemma
3.2
.

∎

We now restate and prove Theorem A.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
P
be a poset. Let
V
be a persistence module indexed by
P
. Let

V=\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{A}}U\quad\mbox{ and }\quad V=\bigoplus_{W\in\mathcal{B}}W

be interval decompositions of
V
. Let
I
be an interval in
P
. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle=\{U\in\mathcal{A}\mid U\cong M(I)\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\{W\in\mathcal{B}\mid W\cong M(I)\}.

Then
|\mathcal{C}|=|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}|
.

Proof.

Let

C=\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{C}}U,\qquad D=\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{C}}U,\qquad C^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{W\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}W,\qquad D^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{W\in\mathcal{B}-\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}W.

Then
V=C\oplus D
, and also,
V=C^{\prime}\oplus D^{\prime}
.

We have
\mathrm{in}^{C}:C\to V
and
\mathrm{pr}^{C^{\prime}}:V\to C^{\prime}
(see Notation
1.3
). The kernel of
\mathrm{pr}^{C^{\prime}}
is
D^{\prime}
.

Define
f:C\to C^{\prime}
by

f=\mathrm{pr}^{C^{\prime}}\circ\mathrm{in}^{C}.

By Lemma
3.3
, we have
C\cap D^{\prime}=0
. This implies that
f
is injective.

Pick
x\in I
. Then
f_{x}:C_{x}\to C^{\prime}_{x}
is an injective
k
-linear map. Hence

\dim C_{x}\leq\dim C^{\prime}_{x}.

Since
\dim C_{x}=|\mathcal{C}|
and
\dim C^{\prime}_{x}=|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}|
, we have

|\mathcal{C}|\leq|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}|.

By symmetry we also have
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}|\leq|\mathcal{C}|
. Therefore
|\mathcal{C}|=|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}|
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

The sets
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
in Theorem
3.4
may be infinite.

We next apply Zorn’s lemma to prove a decomposition criterion.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
P
be a poset. Let
V
be a pointwise finite dimensional persistence module indexed by
P
. Let
\mathcal{E}
be a set of nonzero submodules of
V
.
Assume that for each nonzero direct summand
W
of
V
, there exists a direct summand
U
of
W
such that
U\in\mathcal{E}
. Then there exists a subset
\mathcal{A}
of
\mathcal{E}
giving an internal direct sum decomposition
V=\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{A}}U
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{S}
be the set of all pairs
(\mathcal{A},W)
where:

\mathcal{A}
is a subset of
\mathcal{E}
,

W
is a submodule of
V
,

such that we have an internal direct sum decomposition
V=\left(\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{A}}U\right)\oplus W
.

The set
\mathbf{S}
is nonempty because
({\cancel{\mathrm{o}}},V)\in\mathbf{S}
.

Define a partial order on
\mathbf{S}
by
(\mathcal{A},W)\leq(\mathcal{B},Z)
if
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
and

W=\left(\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{B}-\mathcal{A}}U\right)\oplus Z.

Claim 3.6.1
.

Every chain in
\mathbf{S}
has an upper bound. More precisely:

For any chain
\mathbf{T}
in
\mathbf{S}
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=\bigcup_{(\mathcal{A},W)\in\mathbf{T}}\mathcal{A},\qquad N=\bigcap_{(\mathcal{A},W)\in\mathbf{T}}W.

Then
(\mathcal{C},N)
lies in
\mathbf{S}
and is an upper bound of
\mathbf{T}
.

Proof of Claim
3.6.1
.

There are two things to check:

(1)

V=\left(\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{C}}U\right)\oplus N
;

(2)

W=\left(\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{A}}U\right)\oplus N
for all
(\mathcal{A},W)\in\mathbf{T}
.

Thus for each
x\in P
, we need to check:

(1)

V_{x}=\left(\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{C}}U_{x}\right)\oplus N_{x}
;

(2)

W_{x}=\left(\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{A}}U_{x}\right)\oplus N_{x}
for all
(\mathcal{A},W)\in\mathbf{T}
.

Fix
x\in P
and choose a pair
(\mathcal{B},Z)\in\mathbf{T}
such that
\dim Z_{x}
is minimal.

Now let
(\mathcal{A},W)\in\mathbf{T}
.

We make the following observations:

(a)

If
(\mathcal{A},W)\leq(\mathcal{B},Z)
, then

W_{x}=\left(\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{B}-\mathcal{A}}U_{x}\right)\oplus Z_{x};

hence
Z_{x}\subseteq W_{x}
.

(b)

If
(\mathcal{B},Z)\leq(\mathcal{A},W)
, then

Z_{x}=\left(\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{B}}U_{x}\right)\oplus W_{x};

by minimality of
\dim Z_{x}
, it follows that
Z_{x}=W_{x}
, and
U_{x}=0
for all
U\in\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{B}
.

(c)

By (a) and (b), we have
N_{x}=Z_{x}
, and
U_{x}=0
for all
U\in\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{B}
.

We deduce that:

\displaystyle V_{x}

\displaystyle=\left(\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{B}}U_{x}\right)\oplus Z_{x}

\displaystyle=\left(\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{C}}U_{x}\right)\oplus N_{x}

by (c).

If
(\mathcal{A},W)\leq(\mathcal{B},Z)
, then

\displaystyle W_{x}

\displaystyle=\left(\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{B}-\mathcal{A}}U_{x}\right)\oplus Z_{x}

\displaystyle=\left(\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{A}}U_{x}\right)\oplus N_{x}

by (c).

If
(\mathcal{B},Z)\leq(\mathcal{A},W)
, then

\displaystyle W_{x}

\displaystyle=Z_{x}

by (b)

\displaystyle=\left(\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{A}}U_{x}\right)\oplus N_{x}

by (c).

Claim
3.6.1
is proved.
∎

By Claim
3.6.1
, we can apply Zorn’s lemma to deduce that there exists a pair, say
(\mathcal{A},W)
, which is maximal in
\mathbf{S}
.

Suppose that
W\neq 0
. Then by assumption there exists an internal direct sum decomposition
W=U\oplus Z
where
U\in\mathcal{E}
. Let
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}\cup\{U\}
. Then we have
(\mathcal{B},Z)\in\mathbf{S}
and
(\mathcal{A},W)<(\mathcal{B},Z)
, a contradiction to the maximality of
(\mathcal{A},W)
.

Therefore
W=0
. Hence
V=\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{A}}U
is a desired decomposition.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
P
be a poset. Assume that every nonzero pointwise finite dimensional persistence module indexed by
P
has a direct summand which is an interval module. Then every pointwise finite dimensional persistence module indexed by
P
has an interval decomposition.

Proof.

Let
V
be a pointwise finite dimensional persistence module indexed by
P
. We can apply Lemma
3.6
by taking
\mathcal{E}
to be the set of submodules of
V
which are interval modules. The result follows.
∎

4.
Reminder on linear duality

To set our notation and for ease of reference, we collect in this section some basic facts from linear algebra.

Notation 4.1
.

Let
E
be any
k
-vector space.

(1)

We write
E^{*}
for the linear dual of
E
.

(2)

We write
\mathcal{S}(E)
for the poset of linear subspaces of
E
(where the partial order is inclusion).

(3)

For each
D\in\mathcal{S}(E)
, we set
D^{\perp}=\{\alpha\in E^{*}\mid\alpha(v)=0\mbox{ for all }v\in D\}
.

(4)

For each
\mathfrak{D}\in\mathcal{S}(E^{*})
, we set
{{}^{\perp}\mathfrak{D}}=\{v\in E\mid\alpha(v)=0\mbox{ for all }\alpha\in\mathfrak{D}\}
.

Fact 4.2
.

Let
E
be a finite dimensional
k
-vector space. Then

(1)

we have mutually inverse bijections:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(E)\to\mathcal{S}(E^{*}),\qquad

\displaystyle D\mapsto D^{\perp};

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(E^{*})\to\mathcal{S}(E),\qquad

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}\mapsto{{}^{\perp}\mathfrak{D}};

(2)

the bijections in (1) are order-reversing;

(3)

for any
D\in\mathcal{S}(E)
, there is a natural bijective
k
-linear map

(E/D)^{*}\to D^{\perp},\qquad\theta\mapsto\theta\circ\pi,

where
\pi:E\to E/D
is the quotient map.

(4)

for any
\mathfrak{D}_{1},\mathfrak{D}_{2}\in\mathcal{S}(E^{*})
:

E^{*}=\mathfrak{D}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{D}_{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad E={{}^{\perp}\mathfrak{D}_{1}}\oplus{{}^{\perp}\mathfrak{D}_{2}}.

Notation 4.3
.

For any
k
-vector spaces
E_{1},E_{2}
, and
k
-linear map
f:E_{1}\to E_{2}
, we write

{{}^{t}f}:E_{2}^{*}\to E_{1}^{*}

for the transpose of
f
.

Fact 4.4
.

Let
E_{1},E_{2}
be
k
-vector spaces. Let
f:E_{1}\to E_{2}
be a
k
-linear map. Then

(1)

we have
\im({{}^{t}f})=(\ker f)^{\perp}
;

(2)

for any
\mathfrak{D}_{1}\in\mathcal{S}(E_{1}^{*})
and
\mathfrak{D}_{2}\in\mathcal{S}(E_{2}^{*})
:

{{}^{t}f}(\mathfrak{D}_{2})\subseteq\mathfrak{D}_{1}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f({{}^{\perp}\mathfrak{D}_{1}})\subseteq{{}^{\perp}\mathfrak{D}_{2}}.

5.
Reminder on flags

Definition 5.1
.

Let
E
be a finite dimensional
k
-vector space.

(1)

A
flag
in
E
is a chain in
\mathcal{S}(E)
(see Notation
4.1
(2)).

(2)

A
complete flag
in
E
is a maximal chain in
\mathcal{S}(E)
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
E
be a finite dimensional
k
-vector space. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a flag in
E
. We say that a basis
B
of
E
is
compatible
with
\mathcal{F}
if: for each
F\in\mathcal{F}
, the set
B\cap F
is a basis of
F
.

The following lemma is well-known and appears to be due to Steinberg, see
[
10
]
*Lemma 2.1. For the reader’s convenience, we include Steinberg’s proof.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
E
be a finite dimensional
k
-vector space. Let
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
be flags in
E
. Then there exists a basis of
E
which is compatible with both
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

Let
n=\dim E
. We use induction on
n
, the case
n=0
being trivial.

Assume
n\geq 1
. We may assume further that
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
are complete flags, say

\mathcal{F}=\{F_{0},F_{1},\ldots,F_{n}\}
and
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{0},G_{1},\ldots,G_{n}\}

where
\dim F_{i}=\dim G_{i}=i
for each
i
.

Observe that there exists a unique
s
such that
G_{s}\subseteq F_{n-1}
but
G_{s+1}\nsubseteq F_{n-1}
.

For each
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
, let

G^{\prime}_{i}=\begin{cases}G_{i}&\mbox{ if }i\leq s,\\
G_{i+1}\cap F_{n-1}&\mbox{ if }i>s.\end{cases}

Let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\{F_{0},F_{1},\ldots,F_{n-1}\}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\{G^{\prime}_{0},G^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,G^{\prime}_{n-1}\}
.

Obviously
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is a complete flag in

. A straightforward calculation yields

for each

. Thus
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is also a complete flag in

. By induction hypothesis, there exists a basis

of

which is compatible with both

and

.

Choose any

Let

Totally ordered sets

The aim of this section is to prove Theorem B.

We begin with the following lemma which is a special case of
[

]
*Chapter III, §7.4, Example 2. The proof we give is a slight simplification of the proof of
[

]
*Chapter III, §7.4, Theorem 1.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
P
be a totally ordered set. Let
V
be a pointwise finite dimensional persistence module indexed by
P
. Let

z\in P,\qquad v\in V_{z},\qquad I=\left\{x\in P\mid x\leq z,\ V_{xz}^{-1}(v)\neq{\cancel{\mathrm{o}}}\right\}.

Then there exists
(u_{x})\in\prod_{x\in I}V_{x}
such that

(6.1.1)

\begin{cases}V_{xz}(u_{x})=v\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in I,\\
V_{xy}(u_{x})=u_{y}\quad\mbox{ for all }x,y\in I,\ x\leq y.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{S}
be the set of families
A=\{A_{x}\}
, indexed by
x\in I
, such that each
A_{x}
is an affine subspace of
V_{x}
and the following conditions are satisfied:

(6.1.2)

\begin{cases}V_{xz}(A_{x})=\{v\}\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in I;\\
V_{xy}(A_{x})\subseteq A_{y}\quad\mbox{ for all }x,y\in I,\ x\leq y.\end{cases}

The set
\mathbf{S}
is nonempty since it contains the family
E=\{E_{x}\}
, where
E_{x}=V_{xz}^{-1}(v)
for each
x\in I
.

In the rest of the proof, we write
A,B
for families
\{A_{x}\},\{B_{x}\}
, respectively.

Define a partial order on
\mathbf{S}
by

B\leq A\quad\mbox{ if}\quad A_{x}\subseteq B_{x}\mbox{ for each }x\in I.

Claim 6.1.3
.

Every chain in
\mathbf{S}
has an upper bound. More precisely:

Let
\mathbf{T}
be a chain in
\mathbf{S}
. Define a family
A
by

A_{x}=\bigcap_{B\in\mathbf{T}}B_{x}\quad\mbox{ for each }x\in I.

Then
A
lies in
\mathbf{S}
and is an upper bound of
\mathbf{T}
.

Proof of Claim
6.1.3
.

Fix
x\in I
. Choose
B\in\mathbf{T}
such that
\dim B_{x}
is minimal. Then
A_{x}=B_{x}
, which implies that
A_{x}
is a (nonempty) affine subspace of
V_{x}
.

It is easy to see that
A
satisfies the conditions in (
6.1.2
). Clearly
A
is an upper bound of
\mathbf{T}
. Claim
6.1.3
is proved.
∎

Claim 6.1.4
.

Let
A\in\mathbf{S}
. Assume that
A
is maximal in
\mathbf{S}
. Then

V_{x,y}(A_{x})=A_{y}\quad\mbox{ for all }x,y\in I,\ x\leq y.

Proof of Claim
6.1.4
.

Fix
x\in I
. Define a family
B
by

B_{y}=\begin{cases}A_{y}&\mbox{ if }y\leq x,\\
V_{x,y}(A_{x})&\mbox{ if }y>x.\end{cases}

Then
B
lies in
\mathbf{S}
. Clearly
A\leq B
. By maximality of
A
, we have
B=A
; in particular
B_{y}=A_{y}
for all
y\in I
, hence

V_{x,y}(A_{x})=A_{y}\quad\mbox{ for all }y\in I,\ x\leq y.

Claim
6.1.4
is proved.
∎

Claim 6.1.5
.

Let
A\in\mathbf{S}
. Assume that
A
is maximal in
\mathbf{S}
. Then
|A_{x}|=1
for all
x\in I
.

Proof of Claim
6.1.5
.

Fix
x\in I
. Choose any element

.

By Claim
6.1.4
, we know that
A_{y}\cap V_{y,x}^{-1}(u)
is nonempty for all
y\in I
,
y\leq x
.

Define a family
B
by

B_{y}=\begin{cases}A_{y}\cap V_{y,x}^{-1}(u)&\mbox{ if }y\leq x,\\
A_{y}&\mbox{ if }y>x.\end{cases}

Then
B
lies in
\mathbf{S}
. Clearly
A\leq B
. By maximality of
A
, we have
A=B
; in particular
A_{x}=B_{x}
, hence
A_{x}=\{u\}
.
Claim
6.1.5
is proved.
∎

We can now finish the proof of Lemma
6.1
. By Claim
6.1.3
, we can apply Zorn’s lemma to deduce that there exists
A\in\mathbf{S}
which is maximal in
\mathbf{S}
. By Claim
6.1.5
, we know that for each
x\in I
, there exists
u_{x}\in V_{x}
such that
A_{x}=\{u_{x}\}
. Since
A
satisfies the conditions in (
6.1.2
), it follows that
(u_{x})
satisfies the conditions in (
6.1.1
).
∎

Lemma-Definition 6.2
.

Let
P
be a totally ordered set. Let
V
be a pointwise finite dimensional persistence module indexed by
P
. Let
z\in P
. Then

(1)

\left\{\im(V_{x,z})\mid x\in P,\ x\leq z\right\}
is a flag in

, called the

of

at
z
;

(2)

\left\{\ker(V_{z,x})\mid x\in P,\ z\leq x\right\}

of

at

.

(1) If

(2) If

.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
P
be a totally ordered set. Let
V
be a pointwise finite dimensional persistence module indexed by
P
. Assume that
P
has a maximal element
z
and
V_{z}\neq 0
. Let
B
be a basis of
V_{z}
compatible with the flag of images of
V
at
z
. Let
v\in B
. Then there exists an internal direct sum decomposition
V=U\oplus W
such that:

(1)

U
is an interval module;

(2)

\{v\}
is a basis of
U_{z}
;

(3)

B-\{v\}
is a basis of
W_{z}
.

Proof.

Let

I=\left\{x\in P\mid V_{xz}^{-1}(v)\neq{\cancel{\mathrm{o}}}\right\}.

Clearly
I
is an interval.

By Lemma
6.1
, there exists
(u_{x})\in\prod_{x\in I}V_{x}
such that the conditions in (
6.1.1
) are satisfied. Thus there exists a monomorphism
f:M(I)\to V
such that for all
x\in I
,

f_{x}:k\to V_{x},\qquad\lambda\mapsto\lambda u_{x}.

Let
U=\im(f)
. Then
U
is an interval module and
\{v\}
is a basis of
U_{z}
.

Let
D
be the linear subspace of
V_{z}
spanned by
B-\{v\}
. Let
W
be the submodule of
V
defined by

W_{x}=V_{xz}^{-1}(D)\quad\mbox{ for each }x\in P.

Then
B-\{v\}
is a basis of
W_{z}
.

It remains to see that
V_{x}=U_{x}\oplus W_{x}
for each
x\in P
. There are two cases to consider:

Case 1
:
x\notin I
.

Then we have
U_{x}=0
. Also,

\displaystyle x\notin I\quad

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad v\notin\im(V_{x,z})

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad B\cap\im(V_{x,z})=\left(B-\{v\}\right)\cap\im(V_{x,z}).

Since
B
is compatible with the flag of images of
V
at
z
, it follows that

\left(B-\{v\}\right)\cap\im(V_{x,z})

is a basis of
\im(V_{x,z})
. Hence
\im(V_{x,z})\subseteq D
, which implies that
V_{x}=W_{x}
. Hence
V_{x}=U_{x}\oplus W_{x}
.

Case 2
:
x\in I
.

In this case,

V_{x,z}(u_{x})=v\quad\Longrightarrow\quad V_{x,z}(u_{x})\notin D\quad\Longrightarrow\quad U_{x}\cap W_{x}=0.

But
V_{x,z}
induces an injective map from
V_{x}/W_{x}
to
V_{z}/D
, implying that

\dim(V_{x}/W_{x})\leq 1.

Hence
V_{x}=U_{x}\oplus W_{x}
.
∎

7.
Zigzag posets: generalities

Notation 7.1
.

(1)

We write
\mathbb{N}
for the set
\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
.

(2)

We write
\Gamma
for one of the following sets:

\{1,2,\ldots,n\},\qquad\mathbb{N},\qquad\mathbb{Z}

(where
n\geq 2
).

Definition 7.2
.

Let
P
be a poset. Let
\{z_{i}\mid i\in\Gamma\}
be a sequence of elements of
P
indexed by
\Gamma
(see Notation
7.1
).
We call
P
a
zigzag poset
with extrema
\{z_{i}\mid i\in\Gamma\}
if there exists a total order
\sqsubseteq
on the set
P
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

for all
i,j\in\Gamma
:

i<j\quad\Longrightarrow\quad z_{i}\sqsubset z_{j};

(2)

for all
x,y\in P
:

\displaystyle x\leq y\quad\Longrightarrow\quad

there exists
i\in\Gamma
such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i+1\in\Gamma,\\
z_{i}\sqsubseteq x\sqsubseteq z_{i+1},\\
z_{i}\sqsubseteq y\sqsubseteq z_{i+1};\end{cases}

(3)

either:

for all
i\in\Gamma
such that
i+1\in\Gamma
,

for all
x,y\in P
such that
z_{i}\sqsubseteq x\sqsubseteq y\sqsubseteq z_{i+1}
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\mbox{ is odd }\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x\leq y,\\
i\mbox{ is even }\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y\leq x,\end{cases}

or:

for all
i\in\Gamma
such that
i+1\in\Gamma
,

for all
x,y\in P
such that
z_{i}\sqsubseteq x\sqsubseteq y\sqsubseteq z_{i+1}
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\mbox{ is odd }\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y\leq x,\\
i\mbox{ is even }\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x\leq y.\end{cases}

In this case, we call
\sqsubseteq
the
associated total order
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
P
be a zigzag poset with extrema
\{z_{i}\mid i\in\Gamma\}
.
Let
\sqsubseteq
be the associated total order. Then we have the following.

(1)

For all
x\in P
, there exists
i\in\Gamma
such that
i+1\in\Gamma
and
z_{i}\sqsubseteq x\sqsubseteq z_{i+1}
.

(2)

For all
x,y\in P
such that
x\leq y
, for all
i\in\Gamma
such that

x\sqsubseteq z_{i}\sqsubseteq y\quad\mbox{ or }\quad y\sqsubseteq z_{i}\sqsubseteq x,

we have
x=z_{i}
or
y=z_{i}
.

(3)

For all
x,y,z\in P
such that
x\leq y\leq z
, we have

x\sqsubseteq y\sqsubseteq z\quad\mbox{ or }\quad z\sqsubseteq y\sqsubseteq x.

Proof.

(1) Follows from condition (2) in Definition
7.2
when
x=y
.

(2) Suppose that
x\sqsubseteq z_{i}\sqsubseteq y
.

By condition (2) in Definition
7.2
, there exists
j\in\Gamma
such that
j+1\in\Gamma
,

\displaystyle z_{j}\sqsubseteq x\sqsubseteq z_{j+1},

\displaystyle z_{j}\sqsubseteq y\sqsubseteq z_{j+1}.

Hence we have

z_{j}\sqsubseteq x\sqsubseteq z_{i}\sqsubseteq y\sqsubseteq z_{j+1}.

By condition (1) in Definition
7.2
, we have
z_{i}=z_{j}
or
z_{i}=z_{j+1}
. It follows that
z_{i}=x
or
z_{i}=y
.

Similarly if
y\sqsubseteq z_{i}\sqsubseteq x
.

(3) By condition (2) in Definition
7.2
, there exists
i\in\Gamma
such that
i+1\in\Gamma
,

\displaystyle z_{i}\sqsubseteq x\sqsubseteq z_{i+1},

\displaystyle z_{i}\sqsubseteq z\sqsubseteq z_{i+1}.

Then we also have
z_{i}\sqsubseteq y\sqsubseteq z_{i+1}
because otherwise, by (2):

\displaystyle y\sqsubset z_{i}\quad

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad x=z_{i}\mbox{ and }z=z_{i}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad y=z_{i},\quad\mbox{ a contradiction; }

similarly,

\displaystyle z_{i+1}\sqsubset y\quad

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad x=z_{i+1}\mbox{ and }z=z_{i+1}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad y=z_{i+1},\quad\mbox{ a contradiction. }

It is now easy to deduce the desired result from condition (3) in Definition
7.2
.
∎

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
P
be a zigzag poset with extrema
\{z_{i}\mid i\in\Gamma\}
.
Let
\sqsubseteq
be the associated total order. Let

be a nonempty subset of

. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

is an interval in the poset

.

(2)

For all

such that

, we have

whenever

.

Proof.

(1)
\Longrightarrow
(2): Let

such that

and

.

Since

is connected, there exists

such that

,
y_{m}=z
, and

y_{i-1}\leq y_{i}\kern 5.0pt\mbox{ or }\kern 5.0pty_{i-1}\geq y_{i}\quad\mbox{ for all }i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}.

Choose the smallest

such that

. Then we have

.

By condition (2) in Definition

, there exists

\Longrightarrow

Theorem 8.1
.

Let

be a zigzag poset with extrema

. Let

Assume

.

Claim 8.1.1
.

Let

.

Let
\sqsubseteq
be the associated total order on

.

,

,

of

by

\displaystyle P^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\{x\in P\mid z_{2}\sqsubseteq x\},

\displaystyle P^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\{x\in P\mid x\sqsubseteq z_{n-1}\},

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=\{x\in P\mid z_{2}\sqsubseteq x\sqsubseteq z_{n-1}\}.

V^{\prime},V^{\prime\prime},\overline{V}
be the restrictions of

to

In this case the claim follows from Lemma

(1).

Case 2

(1).

Case 3

For all

be the restriction of

to

. Then by (

), we have interval decompositions

Now let

Thus

is an interval module indexed by

\overline{U^{\prime}}_{z_{2}}\neq 0
and
\overline{U^{\prime}}_{z_{n-1}}\neq 0

, we therefore have
\overline{U^{\prime}}\cong M(Q)
. Since

is an arbitrary member of
\mathcal{A}
, it follows that there exists an isomorphism

By Definition

, for each

. Hence for each
x\in Q
,

Define full subposets

of

by

Then

are totally ordered sets. Let

be the restriction of

to

Define a flag

\mbox{ the flag of kernels of $Z^{\prime}$ at $z_{2}$ \kern 5.0ptif \kern 5.0pt$z_{2}$ is a minimal element in $R^{\prime}$. }\\
\end{cases}

Define a flag
\mathcal{G}
in

\mathcal{G}=\begin{cases}\mbox{ the flag of images of $Z^{\prime\prime}$ at $z_{n-1}$ \kern 5.0ptif \kern 5.0pt$z_{n-1}$ is a maximal element in $R^{\prime\prime}$, }\\

, there exists a basis

/

such that

,

for every

.

Let

be a zigzag poset with extrema

be a pointwise finite dimensional persistence module indexed by

.

such that

. If

is monotonic after

, then

.

The proof of the following lemma is adapted from Botnan’s proof of
[

]
*Lemma 2.5.

Lemma 9.4
.

Let
P
be a zigzag poset with extrema
\{z_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Let
V
be a pointwise finite dimensional persistence module indexed by
P
. Assume that

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

for every interval decomposition
V(n)=\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{A}}U
,

for every
U\in\mathcal{A}
,

we have
U_{z_{0}}\neq 0
or
U_{z_{n}}\neq 0
. Then there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
V
is monotonic after
z_{m}
.

Proof.

Let
\sqsubseteq
be the associated total order.

We know by Theorem
8.1
that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the persistence module
V(n)
indexed by
P(n)
has an interval decomposition. For any such interval decomposition, say
V(n)=\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{A}}U
, define

\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{A})=\{U\in\mathcal{A}\mid U_{z_{n}}=0\}.

Observe that

U\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{A})\quad\Longrightarrow\quad U_{z_{0}}\neq 0;

now, since
V_{z_{0}}=V(n)_{z_{0}}=\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{A}}U_{z_{0}}
, it follows that

|\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{A})|\leq\dim(V_{z_{0}}).

We can therefore choose
m\in\mathbb{N}
and an interval decomposition
V(m)=\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{B}}U
such that
|\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{B})|
is maximal. We shall show that
V
is monotonic after
z_{m}
.

Let
n\geq m
be any integer. Let

V(n)=\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{A}}U

be any interval decomposition.

For each
U\in\mathcal{A}
, write
\overline{U}
for the restriction of
U
to
P(m)
. Let

\overline{\mathcal{A}}=\{\overline{U}\mid U\in\mathcal{A}\mbox{ and }\overline{U}\neq 0\}.

Then
V(m)=\bigoplus_{T\in\mathcal{\overline{A}}}T
.
This is an interval decomposition of
V(m)
, hence by Theorem
3.4
, there exists a bijection
\sigma:\overline{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathcal{B}
such that

T\cong\sigma(T)\quad\mbox{ for all }\quad T\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}.

Thus

(9.4.1)

|\mathcal{O}(\overline{\mathcal{A}})|=|\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{B})|.

Now let

Observe that if

and

We deduce that

In other words, if

and

Lemma 10.1
.

\displaystyle Z_{z_{n}}

\displaystyle=\accentset{\sim}{C}_{z_{n}},

\displaystyle W_{z_{n}}

\displaystyle=\accentset{\sim}{D}_{z_{n}},

\displaystyle T_{z_{n}}

\displaystyle=0\qquad\mbox{ for all }T\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}-\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime},

there exist submodules
\accentset{\sim}{Z},\accentset{\sim}{W}
of
V(n+1)
such that for all
x\in P(n+1)
:

\displaystyle\accentset{\sim}{Z}_{x}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}Z_{x}&\mbox{ if }x\sqsubseteq z_{n},\\
\accentset{\sim}{C}_{x}&\mbox{ if }z_{n}\sqsubset x\sqsubseteq z_{n+1};\end{cases}

\displaystyle\accentset{\sim}{W}_{x}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}W_{x}&\mbox{ if }x\sqsubseteq z_{n},\\
\accentset{\sim}{D}_{x}\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{T\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}-\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}}T_{x}\right)&\mbox{ if }z_{n}\sqsubset x\sqsubseteq z_{n+1}.\end{cases}

Clearly
V(n+1)=\accentset{\sim}{Z}\oplus\accentset{\sim}{W}
, and this is an internal direct sum decomposition with the desired properties.
∎

Lemma 10.2
.

Let
P
be a zigzag poset with extrema
\{z_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Let
V
be a pointwise finite dimensional persistence module indexed by
P
. Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and assume that
V
is monotonic after
z_{m}
. Then for every internal direct sum decomposition

V(m)=Z^{\prime}\oplus W^{\prime}

where
Z^{\prime}
is an interval module, there exists an internal direct sum decomposition

V=Z\oplus W

such that:

(1)

Z
is an interval module;

(2)

Z^{\prime},W^{\prime}
are, respectively, the restrictions of
Z,W
to
P(m)
.

Proof.

If
Z^{\prime}_{z_{m}}=0
, the result is immediate from Lemma
7.5
.

Suppose
Z^{\prime}_{z_{m}}\neq 0
. Set
Z^{(m)}=Z^{\prime}
and
W^{(m)}=W^{\prime}
. By Lemma
10.1
, we can choose inductively an internal direct sum decomposition

V(n)=Z^{(n)}\oplus W^{(n)}\quad\mbox{ for each integer }n>m

such that:

(1)

Z^{(n)}
is an interval module and
Z^{(n)}_{z_{n}}\neq 0
;

(2)

Z^{(n-1)},W^{(n-1)}
are, respectively, the restrictions of
Z^{(n)},W^{(n)}
to
P(n-1)
.

This allows us to define submodules
Z,W
of
V
by

Z_{x}=Z^{(n)}_{x},\qquad W_{x}=W^{(n)}_{x}\qquad\mbox{ if }x\in P(n).

Clearly
V=Z\oplus W
, and this is an internal direct sum decomposition with the desired properties.
∎

, for some

, we have

.

.

: For all

V(n)=\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{A}}U
, for all
U\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

.

9.4
, there exists

such that
V
is monotonic after
z_{m}
. We choose such an

to be sufficiently large so that
V(m)

Lemma 10.6
.

Let
P
be a zigzag poset with extrema
\{z_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. Let
V
be a nonzero pointwise finite dimensional persistence module indexed by
P
. Then there exists an internal direct sum decomposition
V=Z\oplus W
such that
Z
is an interval module.

Proof.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, set
y_{n}=z_{-n}
. Observe that:

P^{+}
is a zigzag poset with extrema
\{z_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\}
;

P^{-}
is a zigzag poset with extrema
\{y_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\}
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we write
V^{+}(n),V^{-}(n)
for, respectively, the restrictions of
V^{+},V^{-}
to
P^{+}(n),P^{-}(n)
(see Notation
9.1
).

Case 1
: For some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, for some interval decomposition
V^{+}(n)=\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{A}}U
, for some
U\in\mathcal{A}
, we have
U_{z_{0}}=U_{z_{n}}=0
.

In this case, the result is immediate from Lemma
7.5
.

Case 2
: For some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, for some interval decomposition
V^{-}(n)=\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{A}}U
, for some
U\in\mathcal{A}
, we have
U_{y_{0}}=U_{y_{n}}=0
.

In this case, the result is again immediate from Lemma
7.5
.

Case 3
: Neither Case 1 nor Case 2 occurs.

In this case, by Lemma
9.4
, there exist
m,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
such that:

V^{+}
is monotonic after
z_{m}
;

V^{-}
is monotonic after
y_{\ell}
.

We choose an integer
n\geq\max\{m,\ell\}
so that
V^{+}(n)
or
V^{-}(n)
is nonzero.

By Theorem
8.1
, there is an interval decomposition
V(-n,n)=\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal{A}}U
.
Let
T
be any member of
\mathcal{A}
. Let

. Then
T
is an interval module, and

.

Now let
Z^{\prime},W^{\prime}
be, respectively, the restrictions of
T,S
to
P^{+}(n)
. Then
Z^{\prime}
is zero or an interval module, and
V^{+}(n)=Z^{\prime}\oplus W^{\prime}
. Similarly let
Z^{\prime\prime},W^{\prime\prime}
be, respectively, the restrictions of
T,S
to
P^{-}(n)
. Then
Z^{\prime\prime}
is zero or an interval module, and
V^{-}(n)=Z^{\prime\prime}\oplus W^{\prime\prime}
.

Claim 10.6.1
.

(1)

are, respectively, the restrictions of

\accentset{\approx}{Z}=0

;

\accentset{\approx}{Z}
is an interval module if

.

10.6.1
.

(1) If

, then let
\accentset{\sim}{Z}=0
and
\accentset{\sim}{W}=V^{+}
.

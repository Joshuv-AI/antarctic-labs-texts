The coexistence of null and spacelike singularities inside spherically symmetric black holes
Maxime Van de Moortel

Abstract
In our previous work [Van de Moortel, The breakdown of weak null singularities, Duke Mathematical Journal 172 (15), 2957-3012, 2023] , we showed that dynamical black holes formed in charged spherical collapse generically feature both a null weakly singular Cauchy horizon and a stronger (presumably spacelike) singularity, confirming a longstanding conjecture in the physics literature. However, this previous result, based on a contradiction argument, did not provide quantitative estimates on the stronger singularity. In this study, we adopt a new approach by analyzing local initial data inside the black hole that are consistent with a breakdown of the Cauchy horizon. We prove that the remaining portion is spacelike and obtain sharp spacetime estimates near the null-spacelike transition. Notably, we show that the Kasner exponents of the spacelike portion are positive, in contrast to the well-known Oppenheimer–Snyder model of gravitational collapse. Moreover, these exponents degenerate to ( 1 , 0 , 0 ) (1,0,0) towards the null-spacelike transition. Our result provides the first quantitative instances of a null-spacelike singularity transition inside a black hole. In our companion paper [ 73 ] , we moreover apply our analysis to carry out the construction of a large class of asymptotically flat one or two-ended black holes featuring coexisting null and spacelike singularities.

1.7
Key ingredients in the proof of Theorem

We will use the following notation for the spherically-symmetric metric

g=-\Omega^{2}(u,v)dudv+r^{2}(u,v)(d\theta^{2}+\sin(\theta)^{2}d\varphi^{2}),\ F=\frac{Q(u,v)}{r^{2}(u,v)}\ \Omega^{2}(u,v)du\wedge dv,

1.7.1
General proof strategy

We prove that the Cauchy development of our initial data on
C_{out}\cup\underline{C}_{in}
comprises a spacelike singularity
\mathcal{S}
and a null Cauchy horizon
\mathcal{CH}_{i^{+}}
as depicted in Figure
1
. At the endpoint of the Cauchy horizon, denoted
(u_{\mathcal{CH}_{i^{+}}},v=+\infty)
(see Section
2
), we prove that the area-radius
r
is zero (recalling the notations of (
1.16
)), i.e.,

\lim_{v\rightarrow+\infty}r(u_{\mathcal{CH}_{i^{+}}},v)=\lim_{u\rightarrow u_{\mathcal{CH}_{i^{+}}}}r_{|\mathcal{CH}_{i^{+}}}(u)=0,

(1.17)

It is well-known that the system (
1.2
)–(
1.6
) in spherical-symmetry is energy-supercritical when the area-radius
r
tends to
0
. Therefore, the precise
r
-weights and degenerations of
(g,\phi,F)
will be crucial in our analysis. The proof will be divided into two parts, which will involve distinct regimes for the Einstein equations:

•

Below the Cauchy horizon, i.e., for
u\leq u_{\mathcal{CH}_{i^{+}}}
, where
r(u,v)
tends to
0
only as
(u,v)\rightarrow(u_{\mathcal{CH}_{i^{+}}},+\infty)
.

•

Below the spacelike singularity
\mathcal{S}
, i.e., for
u_{\mathcal{CH}_{i^{+}}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}<}u\leq u_{\mathcal{CH}_{i^{+}}}+\epsilon
, where for every
u
, there exists
v_{\mathcal{S}}(u)<+\infty
such that
(u,v_{\mathcal{S}}(u))\in\mathcal{S}
, hence
r(u,v_{\mathcal{S}}(u))=0
. Here,
\epsilon>0
is a small constant.

The highly nonlinear nature of (
1.2
)–(
1.6
) typically requires a bootstrap method to obtain quantitative estimates and the presence of a smallness parameter is necessary to close the argument. In the region below the spacelike singularity
\mathcal{S}
, this smallness parameter is
\epsilon
and thus, we only describe
\mathcal{S}
in a neighborhood of
\mathcal{CH}_{i^{+}}\cap\mathcal{S}
.

1.7.2
A priori estimates under the Cauchy horizon

Under the Cauchy horizon, there is, however, no analogous smallness parameters, which makes it difficult to close a bootstrap argument. We rely instead on novel a priori estimates of the following form (recalling (
1.16
))

\int_{u_{0}}^{u}\Omega^{2}(u^{\prime},v)|\phi|^{p}(u^{\prime},v)du^{\prime}\lesssim_{p}e^{2K_{-}v},

(1.18)

for any
p\geq 0
and with the usual Eddington–Finkelstein
v
-coordinate choice (see Section
2
). The
e^{2K_{-}v}
term, where
K_{-}<0
, already appears in the corresponding term at the Reissner–Nordström Cauchy horizon:

\Omega^{2}_{RN}(u,v)\approx e^{2K_{-}(u+v)}\text{ as }v\rightarrow+\infty.

The key feature of (
1.18
) is a control of
\Omega^{2}
by an exponential scalar-field flux, following from a careful application of the Raychaudhuri equation, that can absorb polynomial scalar-field powers like
|\phi|^{p}
(see Lemma
4.1
).

(
1.18
) gives a priori control of many geometric quantities
without any quantitative knowledge of the scalar field
(see Proposition
4.2
). It is at the heart of our new proof of Cauchy horizon breakdown discussed in Section
1.4
and proved in
[
73
]
, and also constitutes a crucial ingredient in the nonlinear analysis of Theorem
I
.

One of the key estimates provides the rate at which
r
tends to
0
in terms of
v
, schematically given by

\begin{split}&r^{2}(u,v)\approx(u_{\mathcal{CH}_{i^{+}}}-u)+O(v^{1-2s}),\\
&-r\partial_{v}r\approx v^{-2s},\ -r\partial_{u}r\approx 1.\end{split}

(1.19)

In particular, we show that under the Cauchy horizon (i.e., for
u\leq u_{\mathcal{CH}_{i^{+}}}
),
r
degenerates to
0
at worst at an inverse polynomial rate in
v
. We remark that (
1.19
) follows from the propagation equations on
\partial_{u}(r\partial_{v}r)=...
and
\partial_{v}(r\partial_{u}r)=...
whose right-hand-side
...
is a negligible error term.

1.7.3
Scalar field estimates under the Cauchy horizon

A priori estimates on
(r,\Omega^{2})
are already given in Section
1.7.2
, so addressing the region under the Cauchy horizon reduces to propagating linear estimates on
\phi
for a metric given by
(r,\Omega^{2})
. The quantitative behavior of
\phi
originates from the propagation of estimates on
rD_{v}\phi
in the ingoing direction, as follows:

\begin{split}&|\phi|(u,v)\lesssim r^{-\frac{1}{2s-1}}(u,v),\\
&r|D_{v}\phi|\lesssim v^{-s},\ r|D_{u}\phi|(u,v)\lesssim r^{-\frac{s}{s-\frac{1}{2}}}(u,v),\end{split}

(1.20)

where we recall that
s>\frac{1}{2}
. Note that (
1.20
) is consistent with the following blow-up behavior of the scalar field:

\phi(u_{\mathcal{CH}_{i^{+}}},v)\approx\sqrt{v}.

(1.21)

1.7.4
Key geometric estimates near the spacelike singularity

In the region
\{u\geq u_{\mathcal{CH}_{i^{+}}}\}
, we prove that a spacelike singularity

develops. Solutions to the linear wave equation on Schwarzschild

Consistently with (

where

) is consistent with our following key bootstrap assumption, for some small

\Omega^{2}(u,v)\lesssim e^{2K_{-}v}\cdot[r(u,v)]^{\delta v}.

Such estimates were already used in our previous work

]
in the spatially-homogeneous case, where a large constant
\epsilon^{-2}
appeared in lieu of

. Following the strategy of
[

, our main idea is then to exploit (

of the form

\lim_{u\rightarrow u_{\mathcal{S}}(v)}-r\partial_{v}r(u,v)\text{ exists and }-r\partial_{v}r(u,v)\approx v^{-2s},

and its ingoing equivalent. Estimates such as (

[

1.7.5
Precise scalar-field control near the spacelike singularity

The important remaining task is to (im)prove (
1.23
), a task for which upper bounds such as (
1.22
) are insufficient: we must also
establish scalar field lower bounds

[
78
]
the same issue occurs and it is resolved invoking linear scattering arguments and taking advantage to the proximity of the Reissner–Nordström background, but no analogous method is available in the spacetime region covered by Theorem

. The key idea is to exploit an approximate monotonicity
10
10

10

This monotonicity property was first exploited by Dafermos in his foundational paper
[

property satisfied by

\begin{split}&\partial_{u}(r\partial_{v}\phi)\approx-v^{-2s}\partial_{u}\phi+O(e^{2K_{-}v}),\\

which implies, if the initial data

While (

8

The vector field

|\partial_{u}(r^{2}\partial_{v}\phi)|\lesssim|X\phi|+O(e^{2K_{-}v}),

(1.29)

\bigl|r^{2}\partial_{v}\phi-r^{2}\partial_{v}\phi(r=\epsilon r_{0}(v))\bigr|\lesssim\epsilon^{2}\log^{2}(\epsilon)\cdot v^{\frac{1}{2}-2s}.

To conclude the proof of (

1.7.6
Converting into Kasner-type estimates on the metric

To cast the metric into the Kasner-type form (
1.14
), we resort to a change of variable
(u,v)\rightarrow(\tau,x)
such that

\tau(u,v)\approx r^{2}(u,v)\Omega(u,v)v^{s-1},\ x(u,v)\approx v^{2-2s}\text{ as }r\rightarrow 0\text{ and }v\rightarrow+\infty,

see Theorem
3.1
, Statement
ii
for details. We then define the third (and smallest) Kasner exponent
p(u,v)
as

[\tau(u,v)]^{2p(u,v)}=r^{2}(u,v).

Metric estimates such as (
1.23
) and their improvements then show the degeneracy of Kasner exponents:

p(u,v)\approx v^{-1}\text{ as }v\rightarrow+\infty.

(1.31)

Trapped region and apparent horizon

By our assumptions on the initial data (see Theorem

3
Precise statements of the main theorem

The following theorem is the main result in our paper, and formulated for local initial data on bifurcate hypersurfaces
\underline{C}_{in}\cup C_{out}=[u_{0},u_{F})\times\{v_{0}\}\cup\{u_{0}\}\times[v_{0},+\infty)
as depicted in Figure
1
.

Our assumptions on
C_{out}
correspond to the behavior on an outgoing cone inside a black hole whose event horizon converges to Reissner–Nordström at the rate predicted by Price’s law and were obtained in
[
74
,
75
]
. These assumptions were already discussed in the introduction and will not lead to further discussions in this paragraph. On the other hand, it is very difficult to find
all
possible initial conditions on
\underline{C}_{in}
leading to a breakdown of the Cauchy horizon. Instead, we thus formulate three statements of our main result. In short:

i.

The first statement is independent of whether a breakdown of the Cauchy horizon occurs or not.

ii.

The second statement is conditional and assumes that a breakdown of the Cauchy horizon has taken place within the domain of dependence of
\underline{C}_{in}\cup C_{out}
, and describes it quantitatively (this second statement is our most general result).

iii.

The third statement is unconditional and provides a large class of initial data on
\underline{C}_{in}
leading to a breakdown of the Cauchy horizon, which is then also described quantitatively applying the second statement.

For the benefit of the reader, we now describe these three statements in more detail.

i.

Statement
i
consist of estimates in the past of the Cauchy horizon
\mathcal{CH}_{i^{+}}
. It is itself divided into two parts: the first consists of
a priori estimates
that do not require precise control on the scalar field under the weak assumptions (
3.1
), which is surprising at first but follows from a new estimate described in Section
1.7.2
. These a priori estimates are essential to the analysis and also offer a new, simpler proof of the breakdown of the weak null singularity of
[
77
]
obtained in our companion paper
[
73
]
. The second part of Statement
i
additionally requires the decay of the scalar field on
C_{out}
(assumption
3.3
) and provides sharper scalar-field upper bounds up to the future endpoint of the Cauchy horizon towards which
r
potentially tends to
0
. Strictly speaking, since it is a local result, there is no guarantee that the breakdown of the Cauchy horizon already occurs in the causal rectangle
[u_{0},u_{F}]\times[v_{0},+\infty)
.
Finally, we will additionally show as part of the proof as Statement
i
, that, under our assumptions, there cannot exist any ingoing null boundary component
\mathcal{S}_{i^{+}}
on which
r
extends to
0
.

ii.

In Statement
ii
, we assume, in contrast, that the local initial data has been sufficiently extended so that a breakdown of the Cauchy horizon occurs in the domain of dependence of the initial data
[u_{0},u_{F}]\times[v_{0},+\infty)
. Under the additional quantitatives assumptions (
3.6
)-(
3.8
) on the initial data, we then show the existence of a spacelike singularity
\mathcal{S}=\{r=0\}
and provide sharp estimates (
3.9
)–(
3.12
) on the solution
(g,\phi)
in its vicinity, proving that it is well-approximated by a Kasner metric with variable exponents
(1-2p(u,v),p(u,v),p(u,v))
degenerating to
(1,0,0)
with
p(u,v)\approx v^{-1}
as
v\rightarrow+\infty
.

iii.

Statement
ii
is our most general statement and it is conditional on the occurrence of a Cauchy horizon breakdown. Statement
iii
, on the other hand, is an unconditional result in which we construct a large class of initial data on
\underline{C}_{in}
so that a breakdown of the Cauchy horizon described in Statement
ii
takes place for any
C_{out}
satisfying the standard assumptions (
3.1
), (
3.3
), (
3.6
)-(
3.8
). We thus obtain the same Kasner asymptotics and conclusion as in Statement
ii
. The construction is arranged so that
\underline{C}_{in}
is trapped and
r\rightarrow 0
towards the future endpoint of
\underline{C}_{in}
(see Remark
3.1
for further details on the construction).

.

u_{0}<u_{F}
and

&|\phi|^{2}(u_{0},v),\ |Q|(u_{0},v)\leq D,\end{split}

4.4
A calculus lemma

The following calculus lemma will be crucial in the estimates under the Cauchy horizon proven in Section

Lemma 4.5
.

Let

we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\frac{(v^{\prime})^{-p}}{r^{2}(u,v^{\prime})}dv\lesssim v^{2s-p},

\displaystyle\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\frac{(v^{\prime})^{-p}}{r(u,v^{\prime})}dv\lesssim v^{\frac{1}{2}-p+s}.

(4.15)

Now note that in the region

, so (
4.14
), (
4.15
) give (
4.12
) and (

) already. In the rest of the proof we focus on the region
|u|\geq v^{1-2s}
. In this region we write
r^{2}(u,v)\sim|u|
. We will split the integrals as such, for
\alpha\in\{1,2\}
: denote
v_{\gamma}(u)=|u|^{-\frac{1}{2s-1}}

\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\frac{(v^{\prime})^{-p}}{r^{\alpha}(u,v^{\prime})}dv^{\prime}=\underbrace{\int_{v_{0}}^{v_{\gamma}(u)}\frac{(v^{\prime})^{-p}}{r^{\alpha}(u,v^{\prime})}dv^{\prime}}_{\lesssim|u|^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{p-1}{2s-1}}}+\int_{v_{\gamma}(u)}^{v}\frac{(v^{\prime})^{-p}}{r^{\alpha}(u,v^{\prime})}dv^{\prime},

where we have obtained the inequality in the first integral thanks to (
4.14
), (
4.15
) applied to

. Note that in our region

:

\displaystyle|u|^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{p-1}{2s-1}}\approx r^{\frac{p-2s}{2s-1}}(u,v)\text{ if }\alpha=2,

(4.16)

\displaystyle|u|^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{p-1}{2s-1}}\approx r^{-\frac{1-2p+2s}{2s-1}}(u,v)\text{ if }\alpha=1/

(4.17)

\int_{v_{\gamma}(u)}^{v}\frac{(v^{\prime})^{-p}}{r^{\alpha}(u,v^{\prime})}dv^{\prime}\lesssim|u|^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}[v_{\gamma}(u)]^{1-p}\approx|u|^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{p-1}{2s-1}},

and then we use (

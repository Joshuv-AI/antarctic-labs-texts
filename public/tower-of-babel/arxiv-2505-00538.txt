Dynamical control of random telegraph noise in magnetic tunnel junctions
Mehrdad Elyasi, Shun Kanai, Hideo Ohno, Shunsuke Fukami, Gerrit E. W. Bauer

Abstract
Rapid random telegraph noise (RTN) in magnetic tunnel junctions (MTJs) is an important figure of merit for probabilistic computing applications. However, the interactions between the macrospin and spin waves with finite wave numbers reduce the RTN attempt frequency. We theoretically show that mode-selective heating and cooling can substantially tune the RTN frequency. We propose a nonlinear cooling mechanism that accelerates the switching dynamics. We outline experimental pathways to characterize the physics of nonlinear effects on RTN and to maximize the operation speed of MTJ-based probabilistic computing.

\displaystyle 2\xi_{\vec{k}}^{\prime}D\langle n_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}\rangle^{2}+(-2\xi_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}-2\xi_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}Dn_{th,\vec{k}^{\prime}}-FG)\langle n_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}\rangle+

\displaystyle 2\xi_{\vec{k}}^{\prime}n_{th,\vec{k}^{\prime}}=0,

(17)

where

\displaystyle D=\frac{BG^{2}}{\xi_{\vec{k}}}-\frac{2G^{4}B^{2}}{\xi_{\vec{k}}C},

(18)

\displaystyle F=(2BG-\frac{4G^{3}B^{2}}{C})n_{th,\vec{k}}+\frac{4\xi_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}BG}{C}n_{th,\vec{k}^{\prime}},

(19)

\displaystyle C=-4\xi_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}+2G^{2}B,

(20)

\displaystyle B=\frac{-(2\xi_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}+\xi_{\vec{k}})}{(\Delta-2\omega_{\vec{k}^{\prime}})^{2}+(2\xi_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}+\xi_{\vec{k}})^{2}}.

(21)

Focusing on the anti-Stokes scattering condition
\Delta=2\omega_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}
and assuming the realistic conditions
\{G,\xi_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}\}\ll\xi_{\vec{k}}
and
G^{4}(n_{th,\vec{k}^{\prime}}-2n_{th,\vec{k}})^{2}/\xi_{\vec{k}}^{2}\ll 4\xi_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}^{2}
, Eq. (
17
) leads to

\displaystyle\langle n_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}\rangle=n_{th,\vec{k}^{\prime}}-\frac{G^{2}}{4\xi_{\vec{k}}\xi_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}}n_{th,\vec{k}^{\prime}}(n_{th,\vec{k}^{\prime}}-2n_{th,\vec{k}}).

(22)

When
G
increases,
\langle n_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}\rangle
drops below the equilibrium value
n_{th,\vec{k}^{\prime}}
, cooling the mode
\vec{k}^{\prime}
. In contrast to radiation pressure, the cooling rate depends on
n_{th,\vec{k}^{\prime}}
or
T_{env}
, being enhanced at higher temperatures for fixed
G
. Figures
2
(a) and (b) illustrate the parameter dependence of the cooling from solving Eq. (
17
).

To apply the four-magnon scattering as outlined above and cool
\pm\mathcal{K}
magnons, another mode with thermal occupation
n_{th,\vec{k}}\ll n_{th,\pm\mathcal{K}}
should be excited to induce
G\neq 0
, and thereby achieve cooling. Therefore, the frequency of the excited mode
\omega_{\vec{k}}\gg\omega_{\pm\mathcal{K}}
, and since both modes are magnons within the same magnet,
\xi_{\vec{k}}/\xi_{\pm\mathcal{K}}\approx\omega_{\vec{k}}/\omega_{\pm\mathcal{K}}\gg 1
satisfying one of the cooling conditions.

We include another mode (
\vec{k}
) satisfying above conditions to the RTN model [see Fig.
2
(c)], and show that
\tau_{s}
and
\langle n_{\pm\mathcal{K}}\rangle
decrease with increasing
|P_{res}|
as predicted. We adopted realistic values of
A_{4}=2\times 10^{4}
,
\omega_{\vec{k}}=8\omega_{\pm\mathcal{K}}
,
\xi_{\vec{k}}=8\xi_{\pm\mathcal{K}}
, and
\alpha_{G}=0.005

IV
experimental considerations

The macrospin and
\vec{k}\neq 0
modes may be excited by photons or VCMA
Zhu2012
;
Rana2017
;
Verba2014
;
Chen2017
;
Rana2019
. Microwave excitation is efficient when the equilibrium magnetization of the MTJ free layer
\vec{m}_{eqb}
lies in-plane and perpendicular to a stripline under or above the MTJ. Nonlinear phenomena have been observed when exciting magnetic thin films and nano-structures by nano-striplines
Sheng2023
, so it should be possible to achieve the same in MTJs with in-plane magnetizations. For perpendicularly magnetized free layers, microwave parametric excitation would require canting of the magnetization. In Sec.
II
, we adopted a Zeeman interaction
\mathcal{H}_{ex,ph}=-\gamma\vec{h}_{mw}\cdot\vec{m}
, that for a spatially uniform excitation and easy axis
\|\hat{z}
simplifies to

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{ex,ph}=P_{res,ph}c_{0}+\sum_{\vec{k}}(P_{p,ph,\vec{k}}c_{\vec{k}}c_{-\vec{k}})+H.c.,

\displaystyle P_{res,ph}=\gamma[\frac{h_{x}}{2}((u_{0}+v_{0})\sin\phi_{s}-i(u_{0}-v_{0})\cos\theta_{s}\cos\phi_{s})+

\displaystyle\frac{h_{y}}{2}((u_{0}+v_{0})\cos\phi_{s}-i(u_{0}-v_{0})\cos\theta_{s}\sin\phi_{s})+

\displaystyle\frac{h_{z}}{2}(i(u_{0}-v_{0})\sin\theta_{s})],

\displaystyle P_{p,ph,\vec{k}}=2\gamma u_{\vec{k}}v_{\vec{k}}[h_{x}\sin\theta_{s}\cos\phi_{s}

\displaystyle+h_{y}\sin\theta_{s}\sin\phi_{s}+h_{z}\cos\theta_{s}],

(23)

where
\theta_{s}
and
\phi_{s}
are polar and azimuthal angles of
\vec{m}_{eqb}
with respect to the easy axis,
u_{\vec{k}}
and
v_{\vec{k}}
parameterize the ellipticity of the magnon modes after a Bogoliubov transformation to magnon annihilation and creation operators
a_{\vec{k}}=u_{\vec{k}}c_{\vec{k}}+v_{\vec{k}}c_{-\vec{k}}^{\dagger}
and
a^{\dagger}_{-\vec{k}}=v^{\ast}_{\vec{k}}c_{\vec{k}}+u_{\vec{k}}c_{-\vec{k}}^{\dagger}
, respectively. In the absence of ellipticity,
v_{\vec{k}}=0
and
u_{\vec{k}}=1
, and parametric excitation is not possible. With increasing
|\vec{k}|
, the exchange interaction dominates,
v_{\vec{k}}
and consequently
u_{\vec{k}}v_{\vec{k}}
decrease, and parametric excitation becomes less efficient.

Modulating the magnetic anisotropy by ac electric fields can also efficiently parametrically excite MTJs
Chen2017
. When the easy axis is fixed along
\hat{z}
and the hard axis anisotropy
K_{h}
along
\hat{x}
can be modulated while
\vec{m}_{eqb}\perp\hat{x}
is at an angle
\theta_{s}
with respect to the easy axis
\|\hat{z}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{ex,an,h}=\gamma H_{K_{h}}(\vec{m}\cdot\hat{x})^{2}=\sum_{\vec{k}}(P_{p,an,h,\vec{k}}c_{\vec{k}}c_{-\vec{k}})+H.c.,

\displaystyle P_{p,an,h,\vec{k}}=\gamma H_{K_{h}}u_{\vec{k}}v_{\vec{k}},

(24)

On the other hand, modulating the easy axis anisotropy in the same configuration,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{ex,an,e}=\gamma H_{K_{e}}(\vec{m}\cdot\hat{z})^{2}=P_{res,an,e}c_{0}+

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{k}}(P_{p,an,e,\vec{k}}c_{\vec{k}}c_{-\vec{k}})+H.c.,

\displaystyle P_{res,an,e}=2\gamma H_{K_{e}}[-i\sqrt{2}\sin\theta_{s}\cos\theta_{s}(u_{0}-v_{0})],

\displaystyle P_{p,an,e,\vec{k}}=\gamma H_{K_{e}}u_{\vec{k}}v_{\vec{k}}[-4\cos^{2}\theta_{s}-\sin^{2}\theta_{s}].

(25)

In experiments, usually the OOP anisotropy can be modulated. Therefore, for an in-plane magnetized MTJ,
\mathcal{H}_{ex,an,h}
applies. For perpendicularly magnetized samples,
\mathcal{H}_{ex,an,e}
applies, and
P_{p,an,e,\vec{k}}\neq 0
only when the sample has an in-plane crystalline or shape anisotropy.

The cooling mechanism in Sec.
III
relies on the excitation of a magnon mode
\vec{k}
at higher frequencies than that of the
\pm\mathcal{K}
magnons. In nano-MTJs, this requires excitation at few tens of GHz, and twice as high for parametric excitation. Since the ellipticity of the magnons decrease with wave number, the latter would not be as efficient as that of the macrospin (Kittel mode) and the
\pm\mathcal{K}
magnons, posing an experimental challenge. Smaller dissipation rates [see Fig.
2
(a)] would require a smaller
G
to achieve the same cooling, and larger anisotropies lead to larger ellipticities and cooling coefficient
A_{4}
, which would require smaller drive for the same cooling since
G=|\alpha_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}|A_{4}
.

V
Conclusion

We propose and formulate active methods to control magnetization RTN in MTJs through magnon fluctuations. We analytically predict and numerically validate RTN attempt times that increase with the parametric excitation power of the macrospin while reducing the energy barrier, effectively accelerating the switching rate. We also show that the parametric excitation of the nonuniform spin waves, despite a non-monotonic power dependence at large excitation amplitudes, generally reduces the RTN frequency. This could be a convenient smoking gun to identify nonlinear interaction effects on RTN. We also propose an in-situ cooling mechanism of nonuniform magnons in MTJs based on driving high-frequency magnon modes that could increase the RTN frequency by an order of magnitude with the current technology. Future work should address complications such as magnetization reversal assisted by domain walls which should become increasingly important for MTJs with larger diameter
Braun1993
;
Krause2009
;
Soumah2024
. We propose concrete experiments that can test the theories, e.g. by parametric excitation of MTJs via ac voltage-controlled modulation of the anisotropy.

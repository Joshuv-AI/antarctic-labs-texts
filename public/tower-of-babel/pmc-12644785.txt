Reply to “Investigating a role for NSAIDs in the pathophysiology of vaccine-associated myocarditis”

In our review article1, we summarised the epidemiology of myocarditis after COVID-19 vaccination and the most prominent patho-physiological mechanisms investigated so far. Furthermore, we described the main areas of research at epidemiological, clinical and preclinical level, which are needed to better characterize this very rare adverse event in the specific context of COVID-19 vaccines.

Hoption Cann2 proposes that nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAID)s could play an important role in the pathophysiology of vaccine-associated myocarditis and could also have a synergistic effect with COVID-19 vaccines, because NSAIDs are administered often as antipyretic medications to treat fever and pain that accompany COVID-19 vaccination.

As Hoption Cann correctly states, the use of NSAIDs as antipyretics is often not reported in the clinical cases of myocarditis post COVID-19 vaccination. For this reason, we think that a causal association between these drugs and myocarditis can only be considered in a speculative manner. Any possible association of drug to an adverse event and assessment of causality would require systematic collection of data, careful scrutiny of such data and conducting experimental research.

Hoption Cann mentions a few interesting publications in which histological and pathological examinations have been conducted in the Coxsackie B3 and B4 Murine Myocarditis model showing that NSAIDs exacerbates myocarditis. Whilst acknowledging the findings described in these articles, we wish to highlight that our review focuses on the key aspects of the disease mechanisms where preliminary research has already been conducted and therefore could set the ground for further investigations. Indeed, our review describes the possible mechanisms that could be the triggering cause rather than the exacerbating factors of the disease. To our knowledge, solid data to implicate antipyretics in vaccine-associated myocarditis have not been published neither in the articles mentioned above by Hoption Cann nor elsewhere. In our view, a better and more systematic characterization of the immune responses in myocarditis subjects post COVID-19 vaccination is required to advance our understanding of this disease pathophysiology.

We think that the mouse model of CVB3 is only a starting point for future studies. Such model does not fully recapitulate the features of the disease post vaccination nor post COVID-19 infection, and for these reasons we emphasize in our article the need to develop and optimize adequate animal models, which resemble the human situation as closely as possible. Of note, animal models can provide insight into the disease mechanism, but ultimately translational research is required.

We are in agreement with Hoption Cann that proper data collection, including recording of NSAIDs or any other drug administration after an adverse event, should be good standard practice and could provide valuable insights.

At this stage, no hypothesis should be rejected a priori; however, we need to maintain rigor in the scientific evaluations so to advance research primarily towards the most plausible mechanisms. Ascertaining the mechanism of myo-pericarditis after COVID-19 vaccination remains of high relevance. As mRNA technology is becoming more and more relevant for a number of medical areas, obtaining additional knowledge with the aim of minimizing any related risk is a paramount priority.

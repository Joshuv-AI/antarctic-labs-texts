Associations between Retinal Nerve Fiber Layer Thickness, Axial Length, and Generation in the 2-Continent Eye Study
Authors: Jost B. Jonas, Rahul A. Jonas, Mukharram M. Bikbov, Gyulli M. Kazakbaeva, Ellina M. Iakupova, Ya Xing Wang, Songhomitra Panda-Jonas
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo assess the associations between peripapillary retinal nerve fiber layer thickness (RNFLT) and ocular and systemic parameters, in particular axial length (A).DesignPopulation based studies.MethodsOcular and systemic examinations including OCT RNFLT measurement.ParticipantsParticipants of 4 population-based studies (Beijing Eye Study [n = 3246; age: 40+ years], Ural Eye and Medical Study [n = 5023; age: 40+ years], Ural Very Old Study [n = 407; age: 85+ years], Ural Children Eye Study [UCES, n = 3109; age: 6+ years]).Outcome MeasuresRNFLT.ResultsThe study cohort consisted of 23 384 eyes (11 785 individuals). In multivariable analysis with exclusion of eyes with an IOP of >21 mmHg, thicker RNFLT was associated (all P < 0.001) with the systemic parameters of younger age, female sex, urban region of habitation, taller body height, higher educational level, lower systolic blood pressure, and higher estimated cerebrospinal fluid pressure; and with lower intraocular pressure. In all studies on adults (Beijing Eye Study, Ural Eye and Medical Study, and Ural Very Old Study), total RNFLT increased with AL up to an AL of 24.0 mm, plateaued, and decreased for an AL >26.5 mm. In the UCES, RNFLT linearly increased with longer AL, also in the highly myopic range. Differentiated sectorially, temporal RNFLT in the UCES linearly increased with AL (including the highly myopic range), while in the adult studies it plateaued beyond an AL of 26.0 mm. In all other sectors in all cohorts, the sectorial RNFLT decreased with longer AL in the highly myopic range.ConclusionsThe AL-related increase in RNFLT up to an AL of 24 mm, found in all ages, may correspond to an AL-related larger retinal surface area with a higher number of retinal ganglion cells per eye. The RNFLT decrease beyond an AL of 26.5 mm in adults corresponds to an increased prevalence of optic neuropathies in highly myopic eyes. The sectorial differences in the RNFLT/AL relationship corresponds to the axial elongation-related RNFL shift to the macula, with a secondary RNFL crowding temporally and a geometrically explained RNFLT decrease nasally. Sectorial normative RNFLT data depend on AL.Financial Disclosure(s)Proprietary or commercial disclosure may be found in the Footnotes and Disclosures at the end of this article.

The peripapillary retinal nerve fiber layer (RNFL) thickness (RNFLT) is a measure of the optic nerve fiber population and has routinely been measured to diagnose optic neuropathies.1 Although the RNFLT was formerly assessed on red-free retinal photographs or by using green light during routine ophthalmoscopy, the clinical introduction of OCT has provided the possibility to measure the RNFLT up to an accuracy of 5 μm.2,3 The OCT-based determination of the RNFLT has become one of the main methods to diagnose and monitor optic nerve diseases, such as glaucoma and nonglaucomatous optic neuropathies.1,3 Recent investigations have shown that the prevalences of glaucomatous optic neuropathy and of nonglaucomatous optic neuropathy (not explainable by any neurological disorder) markedly increased with longer axial length (AL) in highly myopic eyes, and that both high myopia-related optic neuropathies belonged, besides myopic macular degeneration, to the most common causes of vision impairment in highly myopic eyes.4,5 In addition to longer AL, the prevalences of both optic neuropathies increased with older age in the previous investigations.1,5

The diagnostic criteria for both high myopia-related optic neuropathies, as examined in the previous studies, included parameters such as neuroretinal rim pallor, retinal arteriolar diameter and ophthalmoscopic visibility of the RNFL.4,5 Here, we measured, primarily independently of a previous assessment of the status of the optic nerve, the RNFLT as a surrogate of the optic nerve status and determined associations of the RNFLT with systemic and ocular parameters, in particular with AL as the main surrogate of myopia and high myopia. Although previous studies have already addressed the relationship between the RNFLT and other parameters, the RNFLT measurements were often not corrected for their dependence on the ocular length-related magnification of fundus images, and the previous studies often assessed the entire RNFLT, without additionally examining the associations of the sectorial RNFLT with AL and other parameters. Other limitations were that the cohorts were recruited in a hospital-based manner, originated from one geographical region, did not include participants of all generations, and did not focus on highly myopic eyes. Here, we assessed the mean values and associations of the RNFLT with adjustment for the fundus image magnification in 4 cohorts from 2 continents and including all 3 generations, with a special focus on highly myopic eyes. In particular, the inclusion of a cohort of children and adolescents and comparing them with adult cohorts of moderate age and very old age may open the possibility of detecting age-related changes in the relationship between RNFLT and AL, such as a high myopia-related optic nerve fiber loss in adults and very old individuals. The measurements and the knowledge about associations of the RNFLT with AL may be useful for a better detection of high myopia-associated optic neuropathies.

Methods

The study included 4 population-based investigations.5, 6, 7, 8, 9 The Beijing Eye Study (BES), carried out in urban and rural areas of Greater Beijing in the year 2011, consisted of 3468 (78.8%) of 4403 eligible individuals aged 50 years or older. The Ural Eye and Medical Study (UEMS) and the Ural Very Old Study (UVOS) were performed in the years from 2015 to 2017 with an inclusion criterion of an age of 40+ years, and from 2017 to 2020 with an inclusion criterion of an age of 85+ years, respectively. Both studies were carried out in an urban and a rural area of Bashkortostan/Russia and included 5899 (80.5%) out of 7328 eligible individuals and 1526 (81.1%) out of 1882 eligible individuals, respectively. The Ural Children Eye Study (UCES) consisted of 4933 children attending schools in the city of Ufa/Bashkortostan/Russia and who were examined in the study period from 2019 to 2022. The BES 2011 was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Beijing Tongren Hospital (date: December 20, 2000; number #1), and all study participants gave their written informed consent. The UEMS, UVOS, and UCES were approved by the Ethics Committee of the Academic Council of the Ufa Eye Research Institute (UEMS: protocol #3, March 16, 2015; UVOS: protocol #3, August 10, 2017; UCES: protocol #1, December 20, 2018), with informed written consent obtained from all participants (in the case of school children, their parents). All ethics committees confirmed adherence to the Declaration of Helsinki.

All study participants (or their parents in the case of the UCES, if necessary) underwent a standardized interview by trained social workers, and ophthalmological and systemic medical examinations. As described in detail previously, the examinations included measurement of anthropometric parameters (body height, body weight, body mass index [BMI]) and blood pressure, biochemical analysis of blood samples in all studies except the UCES, automatic and subjective refractometry, determination of best-corrected visual acuity, ocular biometry, tonometry, and imaging of the macula and optic nerve head.5,7, 8, 9 It included conventional photography and OCT of the macula and optic nerve head (UEMS, UVOS and UCES: swept-source OCT Triton, Topcon Co; BES: spectral-domain OCT, Spectralis; Heidelberg Engineering Co). The peripapillary RNFLT was measured in a circle centered on the optic disc and having a diameter of 3.4 mm (UEMS, UVOS, and UCES) and 3.5 mm (BES), respectively. The ocular dimensions were biometrically measured using optical low-coherence reflectometry in the BES (Lensstar 900 Optical Biometer, Haag-Streit), anterior ocular segment imaging (Pentacam HR, Typ70900, OCULUS, Optikgeräte GmbH Co) and sonography in the UEMS and UVOS (Ultra-compact A/B/P ultrasound system, Compact touch; Quantel Medical), and anterior ocular segment imaging (Pentacam HR, Typ70900, OCULUS, Optikgeräte GmbH Co) and laser interferometric biometry (AL-Scan, Nidek Co, Ltd) in the UCES.5,7, 8, 9, 10 The participants of the BES underwent frequency-doubling threshold perimetry (screening program C-20-1; Zeiss-Humphrey Co) (performed for the cohort of the BES conducted in 2001), and the participants of the UEMS and UVOS underwent static perimetry (PTS 1000 Perimeter, Optopol Technology Co; screening test program with 82 test points and an extension of 50° in all directions).

Glaucoma was defined by a glaucomatous morphology of the optic nerve head, including an abnormal shape of the neuroretinal rim such as rim notches; a vertical cup-to-disc diameter ratio larger than the horizontal cup-to-disc diameter; a vertical cup-to-disc diameter ratio higher than the upper 95% confidence interval (CI) limit after adjusting for its dependence on the optic disc size; detection of localized RNFL defects and a reduced overall visibility of the RNFL as assessed on the color fundus photographs; a reduced thickness of the RNFL as measured on the OCT scans and as compared to the age-adjusted standard values of RNFL thickness in the various sectors of the parapapillary region; abnormally thin retinal arterioles as semiquantitatively examined on the color fundus photographs10, 11, 12; and in the case of adults with an age of 30+ years, perimetric defects (as far as visual field tests were available) not explainable by any other cause than optic nerve damage. Clearly, neurologically caused visual field defects such as vertical hemianopsias excluded the eyes from the study. The abnormal rim shape included the occurrence of an intrapapillary retinal vessel kinking close to the optic disc border in the inferior, superior, or nasal optic disc sectors. The quality of the perimetric examinations, performed for almost all study participants for the first time and conducted in the setting of a population-based study, was usually not sufficient for a detailed analysis and diagnosis of glaucoma. The height of intraocular pressure (IOP), whether higher than 21 mmHg or equal to or lower than 21 mmHg, was not a criterion for the glaucoma diagnosis.

The IOP readings were adjusted for their dependence on central corneal thickness, and, based on the findings of previous investigations, the estimated cerebrospinal fluid pressure (eCSFP) was estimated as follows: eCSFP (mmHg) = 0.44 × BMI (kg/m2) + 0.16 × diastolic blood pressure (mmHg) – 0.18 × age (years) – 1.91.13, 14, 15 The estimated trans-lamina cribrosa pressure difference (eTLCPD) was defined as follows: eTLCPD = eCSFP – IOPcorrected.13 High myopia was characterized by an AL of >26.5 mm.16

As described in detail recently, the method developed by Littmann17 and modified and applied by Bennett et al,18 by Leung et al,19 and by Kang et al20 was used to correct the lateral magnification of the fundus image by the ocular optic media and the imaging device. Littmann17 and later Bennett et al18 reported that the relationship between the measurement of the lateral size of the image of a fundus structure and the real size of the fundus structure is described by the formula t = p · q · s ·t is the real fundus structure diameter, s the measurement obtained using the imaging device, p is the magnification factor for the imaging device, and q is the magnification factor of the individual eye. The ocular magnification factor q is the product of q = 0.01306 (AL –1.82). The factor p is a constant in a telecentric system. It was set to 3.382 for the OCT devices used in our investigation.20,21 In detail, the radius of the RNFL measurement circle was corrected using equation (1):

RadiusCorrected = p (p = 3.3820 for Triton OCT; p = 3.393 for Spectralis OCT) × 0.01306 × (AL (mm) – 1.82) × Measurement Circle Radius (1).

That is, 1.7 mm for the Triton OCT, and 1.75 for the Spectralis OCT.

This led to the corrected circumference of the measurement circle, shown in equation (2):

CircumferenceCorrected = RadiusCorrected × 2 × π (2).

The mean RNFLT was finally corrected using equation (3):

RNFLTCorrected = RNFLT × (CircumferenceCorrected / Circumference) (3).

with a radius of 1.7 mm for the Topcon OCT and 1.75 mm for the Spectralis OCT. This is equivalent to equation (4):

RNFLTCorrected = RNFLT × (2 × π × RadiusCorrected / 2 × π × Measurement Circle Radius) (4).

That is, 1.7 mm for the Triton OCT, and 1.75 for the Spectralis OCT, which is equivalent to equation (5):

RNFLTCorrected = RNFLT × (RadiusCorrected / Measurement Circle Radius) (5).

That is, 1.7 mm for the Triton OCT, and 1.75 for the Spectralis OCT.

The ocular magnification factor was determined by using the parameter of AL instead of refractive error and keratometric readings, because axial length has been discussed to be more accurate.17, 18, 19, 20, 21

Availability of measurements of RNFLT and AL was the inclusion criterion for the present study, with data from only 1 eye per individual taken for the statistical analysis. Using a statistical software program (SPSS for Windows version 31.0 [IBM-SPSS]), the mean values of the parameters were determined, expressed as mean ± SD for the biometric parameters. Because the 4 study cohorts included in the overall study differed in their ocular biometric parameters, the studies were numbered according to an increasing RNFLT, with “1” for UVOS, “2” for BES, “3” for UCES, and “4” for UEMS (Table 1). Differences between the study cohorts were analyzed applying an analysis of variance. Associations of AL with other ocular and general parameters were determined in linear regression analyses, first in a univariate manner, followed by a multivariable approach. For the latter, all parameters were included that were significantly associated with AL in the univariate analyses. In a step-by-step manner, all parameters that were no longer significantly related to the main outcome parameter of AL were dropped. The standardized regression coefficient β and the nonstandardized regression coefficient B and its 95% CIs were calculated. Results

The total study cohort consisted of 23 384 eyes of 11 785 individuals with a mean age of 49.7 ± 24.0 years (median: 56.0 years; range: 6–96.3 years) and mean AL of 23.4 ± 1.1 mm (median: 23.29 mm; range: 18.50–32.87 mm) (Table 1). It included the cohorts of the BES, UEMS, UVOS, and UCES, with significant differences (because of the differences in the inclusion criteria) between the studies in age, sex, ocular biometric parameters, and other variables (Table 1).

The mean uncorrected RNFLT was 107.1 ± 16.6 μm (median: 107.0 μm; range: 31–189 μm), and if corrected for the fundus image magnification, it was 102.0 ± 15.5 μm (median: 102 μm; range: 30–169 μm) (Table 1). The corrected RNFLT measurements were used for all further analyses.

In univariate analysis, a thicker RNFLT was associated with a lower IOP (corrected for central corneal thickness) (β: –0.17; B: –0.79; 95% CI: –0.87, –0.70; P < 0.001), and for the further analyses, eyes with an IOP measurement higher than 21 mmHg were excluded. The study cohort eventually consisted of 10 875 individuals with an age of 49.4 ± 23.8 years. In multivariable analysis, with adjustments made for the study cohort and with eyes with an IOP of >21 mmHg excluded, thicker RNFLT remained significantly associated with the systemic parameters of younger age, female sex, urban region of habitation, taller body height, higher level of education, lower systolic blood pressure, and higher eCSFP; and with the ocular parameters of longer AL, lower IOP, lower MMD stage, and lower glaucoma prevalence (Table 3A). Similar results were obtained if eyes with an IOP of >21 mmHg, diabetic retinopathy, retinal vein occlusions, and pseudoexfoliation were excluded (Table 3B). Discussion

In this population-based project including 4 cohorts of different ethnicities and generations, after the exclusion of eyes with an IOP of >21 mmHg, thicker RNFLT was associated with the systemic parameters of younger age, female sex, urban region of habitation, taller body height, higher level of education, lower systolic blood pressure, and higher eCSFP (or alternatively, higher BMI); and with the ocular parameters of lower IOP, lower MMD stage, and lower glaucoma prevalence. If eyes with glaucoma and MMD were excluded, similar results were obtained (Table 4A). In all studies on adults (BES, UEMS, UVOS), total RNFLT increased with AL up to an AL of 24.0 mm, plateaued, and decreased for an AL >26.5 mm (Figs 1, S1–S3). In the UCES, RNFLT linearly increased with longer AL, also in the highly myopic range (Fig 2). Differentiated sectorially, temporal RNFLT in the UCES linearly increased with AL (including the highly myopic range), whereas in the adult studies it plateaued beyond an AL of 26.0 mm (Figure 3, Figure 4). RNFLT in the superior sector and inferior sector remained constant in the highly myopic range in the UCES, and decreased in the adult studies (Figure 5, Figure 6). Nasal RNFL decreased in the highly myopic range in all study cohorts (Fig 7).

The observations made in our study cohorts agree with findings of previous studies and further expand into the field of associations of the RNFLT with AL. As in previous investigations, the RNFLT decreased with older age in the adult cohorts of our study, in agreement with a histomorphometrically examined age-related decrease in the optic nerve fiber count of approximately 0.3% per year, parallel to an age-related decrease in the counts of retinal pigment epithelium cells and photoreceptors of approximately 0.3% per year.22, 23, 24 It also corresponds with the age-related decrease in the light differential threshold examined in conventional perimetry.1 The correlation between lower systolic blood pressure and thicker RNFLT corresponds to the effect of arterial hypertension leading to a loss of optic nerve fibers, in particular in eyes with (previous) hypertensive retinopathy. The relationship between thicker RNFLT and higher eCSFP or higher BMI is of interest and leads to the discussion of whether a higher orbital CSFP. as trans-lamina cribrosa counterpressure against the IOP, is protective against an IOP-related loss of optic nerve fibers.13, 14, 15,25, 26, 27, 28, 29, 30, 31 Correspondingly, a lower eTLCPD (with its determinants of a lower IOP and a higher CSFP) correlated with a thicker RNFLT. Interestingly, the standardized correlation coefficient for the association with the RNFLT was lowest for IOP, and higher for eCSFP and for the eTLCPD (Tables 2, 3A,B, and Table 4A, Table 4B). The association between a thicker RNFLT and taller body height in the multivariable analysis corresponds to the reported associations in nonhighly myopic eyes between a higher count of RPE cells, photoreceptors, and optic nerve fibers, parallel to a larger optic disc area, with a larger retinal surface area in nonhighly myopic eyes and with a larger retinal surface area correlated with longer AL and taller body height.23,24,32, 33, 34, 35

The association between the RNFLT and AL was tripartite (Figure 1, Figure 2, Figure 3, Figure 4, Figure 5, Figure 6, Figure 7, S1–S3). Taking all cohorts together, RNFLT increased with longer AL up to an AL of 24.0 mm, beyond which it plateaued up to an AL of 26.5 mm, and then decreased for longer axial length values (Fig 1). The 4 cohorts differed in the highly myopic part of the relationship (i.e., for an AL of >26.5 mm). In all adult cohorts (BES, UEMS, and UVOS), RNFLT decreased with longer AL in highly myopic eyes, whereas in the cohort of the UCES, it remained constant or slightly increased further (Figs 2, S1–S3). The AL-related increase in the RNFLT for the lower AL range up to 24 mm may be explained by the AL-related increase in the retinal surface area, associated with a higher number of retinal ganglion cells. In previous histomorphometric studies, a larger retinal surface area in nonhighly myopic eyes was associated with a higher number of retinal pigment epithelium cells, retinal photoreceptors, and optic nerve fibers and a larger optic disc.22, 23, 24 These findings fit with the observation in our investigation that the RNFLT as a surrogate for the number of retinal nerve fibers and optic nerve fibers increased, with longer AL up to an AL of 24 mmHg. The independence of the RNFLT of AL for the AL range between 24 mm and 26.5 mm may be explained by the process of myopization. It starts in late childhood or adolescence at a time when the number of retinal cells, including the retinal ganglion cells (as postmitotic, no longer multiplying cells), no longer increases, so that the retinal nerve fiber number, and thus the RNFLT, is independent of AL. In the highly myopic eyes with an AL of more than 26.5 mm, the AL-related decrease in the RNFLT is consistent with reports of a marked AL-related increase in the prevalence of nonglaucomatous optic neuropathy and glaucoma-like optic neuropathy in highly myopic eyes of adults.4,5 It has been discussed that the axial elongation-related increase in the distance between the retinal ganglion cells in the retina and the optic disc leads to a lengthening, stretching, and possible damage of the retinal nerve fibers. It holds true in particular for the peripheral retinal ganglion cells close to the ora serrata and for the parafoveal ganglion cells.35,36 According to a recent histological study, the distance between the ora serrata and the optic disc increases by 0.7 mm for each mm increase in ocular AL.32 In the case of the parafoveal ganglion cells, their distance to the optic disc gets elongated because of the axial elongation-related development and enlargement of a temporal parapapillary myopic β zone and gamma zone.36 Besides this nonglaucomatous type of optic neuropathy, a glaucoma-like optic neuropathy has been described in highly myopic eyes, in particular in those with a secondarily enlarged optic disc in which the neuroretinal is abnormally small and abnormally shaped.1,4,37

It has remained unclear whether the total RNFLT remained constant or increased slightly with longer AL in the highly myopic range in the UCES cohort (Fig 2). An increase in the number of retinal ganglion cells with longer AL in the highly myopic range has not yet been examined histologically and might not be expected, because the retinal ganglion cells are postmitotic and myopic axial elongation into the highly myopic range occurs after the 10th year of life. In view of the relatively wide range of the 95% CIs of the RNFLT in the highly myopic range in the schoolchildren examined in the UCES and as shown in Figure 2, one may assume a constancy of the RNFLT in the highly myopic range. In that context, it may be noted that a study reported greater zones of spatial summation in myopic eyes.38 It may suggest a decrease in the density of retinal ganglion cells with longer AL in myopic eyes, supporting the notion that RNFLT may be independent of AL in the highly myopic range in school children.

The finding that the temporal RNFLT and the nasal RNFLT, as compared to the superior RNFLT and inferior RNFLT, differed in their relationship with AL can be explained by an axial elongation-related increase in the optic disc-fovea distance.36 It is caused by the development of the temporal parapapillary myopic β zone and gamma zone in myopic axially elongating eyes.16 Because the distance between the temporal superior vascular arcade and the temporal inferior vascular arcade, as measured on a vertical line running through the foveola, remains unaffected by axial elongation, the increase in the fovea-disc distance leads to a decrease in the angle κ between the temporal arterial arcades.16 Because the retinal nerve fiber bundles adhere to the large retinal vessels, the decrease in angle κ leads to a relative crowding of the retinal nerve fibers in the temporal sector, and geometrically corresponding to a decrease in RNFLT density in the nasal sector.39 In the UCES cohort, the temporal RNFL correspondingly increased with longer AL, and the nasal RNFLT decreased (Figure 4, Figure 7). In the total study population, the nasal RNFLT, as in the UCES, also decreased with longer AL, however, by a higher amount as compared with the UCES. The temporal RNFLT in the adult cohorts, in contrast to the UCES, did not increase in the highly myopic range but remained roughly constant or slightly decreased in the highly myopic range (Fig 3). Both findings combined suggest that in the highly myopic eyes in the adult cohorts (and in contrast to the Central India Eye and Medical Study cohort), the temporal RNFLT relatively decreased with longer AL, suggesting a high myopia-related optic neuropathy. This agrees with the observation of an increased prevalence of a nonglaucomatous optic neuropathy in association with paracentral deep scotomas in highly myopic eyes without marked myopic macular degeneration.

The physiological dependence of the sectorial RNFLT on AL, in addition to age, should be taken into account if normative values of the sectorial RNFLT are taken to diagnose optic nerve damage.

To further elaborate and clarify the hypothesis-driven statements made in this study, namely the decrease in the RNFLT with longer AL in highly myopic eyes, future studies may examine the prevalence and incidence of optic neuropathies in highly myopic eyes and potentially differentiate them between a nonglaucomatous type of optic neuropathy and a glaucomatous or glaucoma-like type.5,16 The differentiation between both types may be based on the appearance of the optic nerve head, with a normal configuration of the neuroretinal rim in nonglaucomatous optic neuropathy, and an abnormal shape and abnormally small size of the neuroretinal rim in the glaucomatous or glaucoma-like optic neuropathy.37,40 Additionally, the method of perimetry or other visual psychophysical technique may be refined to better detect and monitor high myopia-related optic neuropathies. Future studies may also explore whether the glaucoma-like optic neuropathy shows a dependence in its prevalence, incidence, and progression on the IOP. In that case, the glaucoma-like type may be termed glaucomatous, and the term “glaucoma-like” may be dropped. Future studies may also assess by which refined techniques the RNFLT may be measured more accurately in highly myopic eyes, taking into account the technological problems posed by the presence of a parapapillary retinoschisis and the extension of the parapapillary β zone and gamma zone into the region of the measurement circle of the RNFLT.

When the results of our study are discussed, its limitations should be considered. First, the RNFLT was measured by 2 different OCT devices, with a difference in the radius of the RNFLT measurement circle. Inclusion of OCT measurements made by different OCT devices was necessary because the underlying studies had used these different OCT machines. The methodological difference between the two OCT devices was taken into account by correcting the raw data of the RNFLT measurement for their dependence on the fundus image magnification by the optic media of the eyes. Second, the regression coefficients for some of the determinants of RNFLT were relatively low, indicating that only a fraction of the variability in RNFLT could be explained by these relationships. Third, because of differences in the inclusion criteria, the 5 underlying study cohorts varied in the parameters assessed in this investigation, potentially leading to a bias in our results. We adjusted, however, for the underlying study in the multivariable analysis, and additionally performed the analyses separately for the underlying studies. The inclusion of population-based studies on cohorts of different generations was necessary to assess the relationship between RNFLT and AL in individuals of different ages, including school children and very old individuals. Fourth, the discussion of the findings reported in this study has been based on hypothesis-driven statements, which strongly need further confirmation.

In conclusion, in all 5 cohorts and across all ages, the AL-related increase in RNFLT up to an AL of 24 mm, found in all ages, may correspond to an AL-related larger retinal surface area with a higher number of retinal ganglion cells per eye. The RNFLT decrease beyond an AL of 26.5 mm in adult eyes may correspond to an increase in the prevalence of high myopia-related optic neuropathies (nonglaucomatous type and glaucoma-like type) with longer AL in highly myopic eyes. The sectorial differences in the RNFLT/AL relationship correspond to the axial elongation-related RNFL shift to the macula, with a secondary RNFL crowding temporally and a geometrically explained RNFLT decrease nasally.

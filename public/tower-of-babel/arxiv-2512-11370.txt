A variational approach to nonlocal heat equations
Edoardo Mainini

Abstract
. We discuss a weighted variational integral approach for nonlocal linear diffusion models with forcing term, providing a selection principle for solutions of elliptic in time regularizations.



AMS Classification Numbers (2020):
35S10, 49J45, 70H30

Key Words:
Calculus of Variations, Linear diffusion, Nonlocal Heat Equation,
Weighted Variational Integrals, Weighted inertia energy approach

1.
Introduction

We consider the following initial value problem

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u^{\prime}+\mathcal{L}u=f&\mbox{in $(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}$}\\
u(0)=u_{0}&\mbox{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$}\end{array}\right.

where
\mathcal{L}:\mathrm{Dom}(\mathcal{L})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a self-adjoint Fourier multiplier with symbol
\hat{L}
, the prime
′
stands for time derivative, and
f
is a given space-time depending forcing term.

Besides the classical heat equation, (
1.1
) covers general nonlocal diffusion models like the fractional heat equation
[
8
,
26
,
27
]
,
obtained by letting

(1.2)

\mathcal{L}u(x):=p.v.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\kappa(x-y)(u(x)-u(y))\,dy

with

\kappa(x)=c_{N,s}|x|^{-N-2s},\qquad 0<s<1,

where
c_{N,s}>0
is a suitable normalization constant. That is,
\mathcal{L}=(-\Delta)^{s}
, the fractional power of the Laplacian operator, characterized in terms of Fourier transform as

\widehat{(-\Delta)^{s}v}(\xi)=\hat{L}(\xi)\hat{v}(\xi)=|\xi|^{2s}\hat{v}(\xi),\qquad v\in\mathrm{Dom}(\mathcal{L}),

with
\mathrm{Dom}(\mathcal{L})
given by the fractional Sobolev space
H^{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Among the main applications of nonlocal diffusion models, we might also consider
\mathcal{L}
in the form (
1.2
) with symmetric kernel
\kappa\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
leading to the zeroth order operator
\mathcal{L}u=Mu-\kappa\ast u
,
M=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u
. In this case,
\hat{L}(\xi)=M-\hat{\kappa}(\xi)
belongs to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\mathrm{Dom}(\mathcal{L})=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and in general
\mathcal{L}
is not a nonnegative operator. Nonlocal diffusion models of this form appear in biology, population dynamics and several more applications, see
[
3
,
4
,
9
,
10
,
13
]
and references therein.

To the main equation (
1.1
) we
associate the weighted variational integral

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|u^{\prime}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u\;\mathcal{L}u-fu\right)\,dx\,dt

which is naturally related to the elliptic in time regularization

(1.3)

-\varepsilon u^{\prime\prime}+u^{\prime}+\mathcal{L}u=f\qquad\mbox{in $(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}$}.

Indeed, the latter is the Euler-Lagrange equation of functional
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
. Our objective is to show that, under a natural growth assumption on
f
with respect to the time variable, and assuming that
\hat{L}
is bounded from below, functional
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
admits a unique minimizer
u_{\varepsilon}
among functions
u
satisfying the initial condition
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}(\cdot)
, and that
u_{\varepsilon}
converge a.e. in space-time to the solution of (
1.1
) as
\varepsilon\to 0
. This is rigorously stated in Theorem
3.2
below, which is the main result.

The exponentially weighted variational approach is typically used for hyperbolic problems. In such setting, it was introduced by De Giorgi
[
11
,
12
]
, who conjectured that a
solution to the initial value problem for the following nonlinear wave equation (with an integer
k>1
)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u^{\prime\prime}-\Delta u+ku^{2k-1}=0&\mbox{in $(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}$}\\
u(0)=u_{0}&\mbox{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$}\\
u^{\prime}(0)=u_{1}&\mbox{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$},\end{array}\right.

with smooth compactly supported initial data,
is obtained as space-time a.e. limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
of minimizers of the weighted inertia energy (WIE) functional

\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}|u^{\prime\prime}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2k}\right)\,dx\,dt

among functions satisfying the same initial conditions.
This conjecture has been proved in
[
20
]
, see also
[
22
]
for the case of a finite time interval, and then extended to more general hyperbolic problems with dissipation in
[
1
,
21
]
, of course also including the classical linear wave equation. The nontrivial extension to the nonhomogeneous case is treated in
[
17
]
, see also
[
24
,
25
]
. This approach has also been often applied to parabolic problems, see
[
2
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
18
]
, see also
[
19
]
where the authors treat a nonlocal problem like (
1.1
). For a general overview we refer to the survey
[
23
]
.

The WIE approach can be used for treating ODEs as well, see
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
, and here in
this paper we are going to discuss such a variational approach for the case of the linear first order equation (
1.1
), which we rephrase as a ODE in Fourier variables. This allows to obtain a new insight on this method and also on the role of the conditions on the forcing term. Indeed, the unique minimizer for
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
among functions having initial value
u_{0}
is shown to be the unique finite energy solution to (
1.3
) having initial value
u_{0}
. Therefore, this method provides a selection principle for solutions of the elliptic in time regularization (
1.3
), by posing a condition on their behavior for large time ensuring finiteness of the energy. Such a condition depends on the forcing term, which is required to satisfy a fairly general growth assumption.
Before stating and proving the main result in Section
3
, we devote Section
2
to the finite dimensional version, which is a basic example providing the main elements of the proof.

2.
The case of linear ODEs

In this section we discuss a toy problem, i.e., the general ODE

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}y^{\prime}+Ay=f(t)&\mbox{in $(0,+\infty)$}\\
y(0)=y_{0}\end{array}\right.

where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
is a symmetric matrix,
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
f:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a forcing term that is assumed to be measurable and square Laplace transformable, i.e., we assume that

(2.2)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-{t/\varepsilon}}|f(t)|^{2}\,dt<+\infty\qquad\mbox{if $\varepsilon>0$ is small enough}.

The goal is to check that the unique solution to problem (
2.1
) can be recovered as limit of minimizers of functional

\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(y):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|y^{\prime}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\langle Ay,y\rangle-\langle f,y\rangle\right)\,dt,

among functions
y
such that
y(0)=y_{0}
,
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. The proof of this fact will serve as a reference also for the case of nonlocal heat equations. Indeed, we will check that this approach consists in a selection principle for solutions
y_{\varepsilon}
of the second order approximation

(2.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varepsilon y^{\prime\prime}=y^{\prime}+Ay-f(t)&\mbox{in $(0,+\infty)$}\\
y(0)=y_{0},\end{array}\right.

ensuring convergence of
y_{\varepsilon}
to the solution of (
2.1
). On top of that, we shall check that there is a unique solution to (
2.3
) having finite
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
energy (for fixed
\varepsilon
). Finiteness of the energy is therefore a necessary and sufficient condition for solutions to (
2.3
) to approximate the solution to (
2.1
) as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Let us introduce the functional setting. We let

\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}:=\left\{y\in W^{1,1}_{loc}((0,+\infty);\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}|y^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\,dt<+\infty\right\}.

A function in
\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}
admits a continuous representative that is in
AC([0,T])
for every
T>0
, and integration by parts along with Young inequality entails

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{-t/\varepsilon}|y(t)|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle=\left[-\varepsilon e^{-t/\varepsilon}|y(t)|^{2}\right]_{0}^{T}+2\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\langle y(t),y^{\prime}(t)\rangle\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon|y(0)|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-t/\varepsilon}|y(t)|^{2}\,dt+2\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-t/\varepsilon}|y^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\,dt,

therefore the following estimate holds for every
y\in\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}

(2.4)

\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}|y(t)|^{2}\,dt\leq\varepsilon|y(0)|^{2}+2\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}|y^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\,dt.

In particular,
\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}
is the weighted Sobolev space of functions that are in
L^{2}_{w}((0,+\infty);\mathbb{R}^{N})
along with their distributional derivatives, where the weight
w
is
e^{-t/\varepsilon}\,dt
. In view of (
2.4
) and (
2.2
), functional
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
is well defined and finite on every
y\in\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}
if
\varepsilon
is small enough. Still by using (
2.2
) and (
2.4
), it is not difficult the check that functional
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
is bounded from below over the the set
\{y\in\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}:y(0)=y_{0}\}
if
\varepsilon
is small enough.

A critical point over
\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}
for functional
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
is naturally defined as a function
y\in\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}
such that

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(y+\delta\varphi)-\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(y)}{\delta}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\left(\varepsilon\langle y^{\prime}(t),\varphi^{\prime}(t)\rangle+\langle Ay(t),\varphi(t)\rangle-\langle f(t),\varphi(t)\rangle\right)\,dt

vanishes for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}
. If
y\in\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}
is a critical point, integrating by parts we get

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\left\langle-\varepsilon y^{\prime\prime}(t)+y^{\prime}(t)+Ay(t)-f(t)\right\rangle\varphi(t)\,dt=0\qquad\mbox{for every $\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}((0,+\infty))$.}

Therefore any critical point over
\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}
for functional
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}

is necessarily a solution to
\varepsilon y^{\prime\prime}=y^{\prime}+Ay-f
.
In particular, any minimizer of
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
over the class
\{y\in\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}:y(0)=y_{0}\}
is necessarily a solution to
\varepsilon y^{\prime\prime}=y^{\prime}+Ay-f
(noticing that perturbations with
C^{1}_{c}((0,+\infty))
functions preserve the initial condition).
However, we shall check that not every solution to the equation
\varepsilon y^{\prime\prime}=y^{\prime}+Ay-f
has finite energy, i.e., not every solution belongs to
\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}
.
We are lead to search for a solution to (
2.3
) that belongs to
\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}
, and we next check that it exists and is unique, thus being the unique minimizer of
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
over
\{y\in\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}:y(0)=y_{0}\}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume (
2.2
). Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
be symmetric. Let
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. For every small enough
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a unique minimizer
y_{\varepsilon}
for
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
in the class
\{y\in\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}:y(0)=y_{0}\}
. As
\varepsilon\to 0
,
y_{\varepsilon}
converge pointwise in
[0,+\infty)
and uniformly on bounded intervals to

y(t)=e^{-At}\left(y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{As}f(s)\,ds\right),

i.e., to the unique solution to (
2.1
).

Proof.

Introducing the variable
z:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{2N}
defined by
z(t):=(y(t),y^{\prime}(t))
, (
2.3
) is equivalent to

z^{\prime}=A_{\varepsilon}z+f_{\varepsilon},

where the
\mathbb{R}^{2N\times 2N}
block matrix
A_{\varepsilon}
and the
\mathbb{R}^{2N}
vector
f_{\varepsilon}
are defined by

(2.5)

A_{\varepsilon}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{N}\\
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}A&\frac{1}{\varepsilon}I_{N}\end{pmatrix},\qquad f_{\varepsilon}(t):=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}f(t)\end{pmatrix}

where
I_{N}
denotes the
N\times N
identity matrix.
Since

\det(A_{\varepsilon}-\lambda I_{2N})=\det\left(\lambda(\lambda-\frac{1}{\varepsilon})I_{N}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}A\right)=\varepsilon^{-N}\det(\lambda(\varepsilon\lambda-1)I_{N}-A),

we see that
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
A_{\varepsilon}
if and only if
\lambda(\varepsilon\lambda-1)
is an eigenvalue of
A
. Therefore, letting
\mu^{1}\leq\mu^{2}\leq\ldots\leq\mu^{N}
the eigenvalues of
A
and
4\varepsilon<1/|\mu_{1}|
, the eigenvalues of
A_{\varepsilon}
are real and given by

(2.6)

\mu_{\varepsilon}^{i}=:\frac{1-\sqrt{1+4\varepsilon\mu^{i}}}{2\varepsilon},\qquad\bar{\mu}^{i}_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4\varepsilon\mu^{i}}}{2\varepsilon},\qquad i=1,\ldots,N.

It is not difficult to check that, for every
i=1,\ldots,N
, the eigenvectors respectively associated to
\mu_{\varepsilon}^{i}
and to
\bar{\mu}^{i}_{\varepsilon}
are the
\mathbb{R}^{2N}
vectors

h_{\varepsilon}^{i}:=\begin{pmatrix}h^{i}\\
\mu_{\varepsilon}^{i}\,h^{i}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\bar{h}_{\varepsilon}^{i}:=\begin{pmatrix}h^{i}\\
\bar{\mu}^{i}_{\varepsilon}\,h^{i}\end{pmatrix}

where
h^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is the normalized eigenvector of
A
associated to the eigenvalue
\mu^{i}
, and we also define
P\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
as the orthogonal matrix whose columns are
h^{1},\ldots,h^{N}
.
Let now
P_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}^{2N\times 2N}
a matrix that diagonalizes
A_{\varepsilon}
, i.e., we let
P_{\varepsilon}
the matrix whose columns are
h_{\varepsilon}^{1},\ldots h_{\varepsilon}^{N},\bar{h}_{\varepsilon}^{1},\ldots\bar{h}_{\varepsilon}^{N}
.
It can be written as the block matrix

(2.7)

P_{\varepsilon}=\begin{pmatrix}P&P\\
Q_{\varepsilon}&\bar{Q}_{\varepsilon}\end{pmatrix},

where
Q_{\varepsilon}
is the matrix with columns
(\mu_{\varepsilon}^{1}h^{1},\ldots\mu_{\varepsilon}^{N}h^{N})
and
\bar{Q}_{\varepsilon}
is the matrix with columns
(\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{1}h^{1},\ldots,\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{N}h^{N})
. Since
\mu_{\varepsilon}^{i}\to-\mu^{i}
and
\varepsilon\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}\to 1
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we deduce that

(2.8)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon Q_{\varepsilon}=0,\qquad\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon\bar{Q}_{\varepsilon}=P.

The standard substitution
z=P_{\varepsilon}X
allows to rephrase
z^{\prime}=A_{\varepsilon}z+f_{\varepsilon}
as the linear decoupled system

(2.9)

X^{\prime}=D_{\varepsilon}X+q_{\varepsilon},

where
D_{\varepsilon}:=P_{\varepsilon}^{-1}A_{\varepsilon}P_{\varepsilon}
is the diagonal matrix with eigenvalues
\mu_{\varepsilon}^{1},\ldots\mu_{\varepsilon}^{N},\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{1},\ldots,\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{N}
and where
q_{\varepsilon}:=P_{\varepsilon}^{-1}f_{\varepsilon}
.
We will make use of the following notation for solutions
X_{\varepsilon}
to the system (
2.9
):
x_{\varepsilon}
is the vector containing the first
N
components of
X_{\varepsilon}
, while
\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}
includes the components of
X_{\varepsilon}
from
N+1
to
2N
, so that
X_{\varepsilon}=(x_{\varepsilon},\bar{x}_{\varepsilon})=(x_{\varepsilon}^{1},\ldots x_{\varepsilon}^{N},\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}^{1},\ldots\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}^{N})
, and similarly we let
q_{\varepsilon}=(g_{\varepsilon},\bar{g}_{\varepsilon})=(g_{\varepsilon}^{1},\ldots g_{\varepsilon}^{N},\bar{g}_{\varepsilon}^{1},\ldots\bar{g}_{\varepsilon}^{N})
.
In particular, the relation
q_{\varepsilon}:=P_{\varepsilon}^{-1}f_{\varepsilon}
and the definition of
f_{\varepsilon}
from (
2.5
) imply

-\begin{pmatrix}0\\
f\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon P&\varepsilon P\\
\varepsilon Q_{\varepsilon}&\varepsilon\bar{Q}_{\varepsilon}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}g_{\varepsilon}\\
\bar{g}_{\varepsilon}\end{pmatrix}

so that
\bar{g}_{\varepsilon}=-g_{\varepsilon}
and taking (
2.8
) into account we get

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}g_{\varepsilon}(t)=P^{-1}f(t)\quad\mbox{ for a.e. $t>0$}.

The general solution
X_{\varepsilon}
to the decoupled system (
2.9
), since
q_{\varepsilon}=(g_{\varepsilon},-g_{\varepsilon})
, is given by

(2.10)

\displaystyle x_{\varepsilon}^{i}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{\mu_{\varepsilon}^{i}t}\left(x^{i}_{\varepsilon}(0)+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}^{i}s}g_{\varepsilon}^{i}(s)\,ds\right)\qquad\mbox{ for $i=1,\ldots N$}

\displaystyle\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}^{i}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}t}\left(\bar{x}^{i}_{\varepsilon}(0)-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}s}g_{\varepsilon}^{i}(s)\,ds\right)\qquad\mbox{ for $i=1,\ldots N$}

As observed at the beginning of this section, if
y_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
over
\{y\in\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}:y(0)=y_{0}\}
then
y_{\varepsilon}
solves (
2.3
). We claim that there exists one and only one solution
y_{\varepsilon}
to (
2.3
) satisfying
y_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}
, thus trivially coinciding with the unique minimizer of
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
over
\{y\in\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}:y(0)=y_{0}\}
.
Indeed, suppose
y_{\varepsilon}
is a solution to (
2.3
). We let as before

z_{\varepsilon}=(y_{\varepsilon},y^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})
and
X_{\varepsilon}=P_{\varepsilon}^{-1}z_{\varepsilon}
, so that
X_{\varepsilon}
is indeed a solution to (
2.9
), thus being one of the functions from (
2.10
). We have from (
2.7
) that

(2.11)

y_{\varepsilon}=P(x_{\varepsilon}+\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}),

therefore
|y_{\varepsilon}|^{2}=|x_{\varepsilon}+\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}
since
P
is orthogonal.
On the other hand, the condition
y_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}
implies

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}|y_{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}\,dt<+\infty

in view of (
2.4
). Therefore, a necessary condition on

(x_{\varepsilon},\bar{x}_{\varepsilon})
in order to have
y_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}
is

(2.12)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon}^{i}(t)^{2}+\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}^{i}(t)^{2})\,dt<+\infty\qquad\mbox{for every $i=1,\ldots,N$},

where the condition involving
\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}
, recalling (
2.10
) and (
2.6
), requires that for every
i=1,\ldots,N

(2.13)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{\frac{\sqrt{1+4\varepsilon\mu^{i}}}{\varepsilon}t}\left|\bar{x}^{i}_{\varepsilon}(0)-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}s}g_{\varepsilon}^{i}(s)\,ds\right|^{2}\,dt<+\infty.

Since
q_{\varepsilon}=P_{\varepsilon}^{-1}f_{\varepsilon}
,
q_{\varepsilon}=(g_{\varepsilon},-g_{\varepsilon})
and
f_{\varepsilon}=-(0,f/\varepsilon)
, and since
\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}\geq 1/(2\varepsilon)
, condition (
2.2
) entails that the map
s\mapsto e^{-\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}s}g_{\varepsilon}^{i}(s)
is in
L^{1}(0,+\infty)
for every small enough
\varepsilon
, therefore we have the finite limit

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}s}g_{\varepsilon}^{i}(s)\,ds=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}s}g_{\varepsilon}^{i}(s)\,ds

and thus
a necessary condition for the validity of (
2.13
) is

(2.14)

\bar{x}^{i}_{\varepsilon}(0)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}s}g_{\varepsilon}^{i}(s)\,ds\qquad\mbox{for every $i=1,\ldots N$,}

and we next check that it is also a sufficient condition for obtaining (
2.13
).
Indeed, under the validity of such a condition, for every
i=1,\ldots,N
we have by Hölder inequality

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\bar{x}^{i}_{\varepsilon}(t)^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{\frac{\sqrt{1+4\varepsilon\mu^{i}}}{\varepsilon}t}\left|\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}s}g_{\varepsilon}^{i}(s)\,ds\right|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{\frac{\sqrt{1+4\varepsilon\mu^{i}}}{\varepsilon}t}\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}s}\,ds\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}s}|g_{\varepsilon}^{i}(s)|^{2}\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{\frac{\sqrt{1+4\varepsilon\mu^{i}}}{\varepsilon}t}e^{-\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}t}\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}s}|g_{\varepsilon}^{i}(s)|^{2}\,ds\,dt,

hence by recalling the definition of
\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}
from (
2.6
) and the property
\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}\geq 1/(2\varepsilon)
, and since
|g_{\varepsilon}^{i}(s)|\leq|P_{\varepsilon}^{-1}||f(s)|/\varepsilon
for a.e.
s>0
(here the matrix norm is the operator norm), we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\bar{x}^{i}_{\varepsilon}(t)^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/(2\varepsilon)}\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-s/(2\varepsilon)}|g_{\varepsilon}^{i}(s)|^{2}\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{2\varepsilon}{\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s/(2\varepsilon)}|g_{\varepsilon}^{i}(s)|^{2}\,ds\leq\frac{2|P_{\varepsilon}^{-1}|}{\varepsilon\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s/(2\varepsilon)}|f(s)|^{2}\,ds<+\infty

for every small enough
\varepsilon>0
,
thanks to (
2.2
). On the other hand, the validity of (
2.12
), for the part involving
x_{\varepsilon}
, is obtained from (
2.6
) and from the elementary estimate
\mu_{\varepsilon}^{i}\leq-2(\mu^{1}\wedge 0)
, since Hölder inequality yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}x^{i}_{\varepsilon}(t)^{2}\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}e^{2\mu_{\varepsilon}^{i}t}\left|x^{i}_{\varepsilon}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}^{i}s}g_{\varepsilon}^{i}(s)\,ds\right|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\varepsilon x^{i}(0)^{2}}{\sqrt{1+4\varepsilon\mu^{i}}}+2\int_{0}^{+\infty}te^{-t/\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}e^{2\mu_{\varepsilon}^{i}(t-s)}|g_{\varepsilon}^{i}(s)|^{2}\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\varepsilon x^{i}(0)^{2}}{\sqrt{1+4\varepsilon\mu^{i}}}+2\int_{0}^{+\infty}te^{-t/(2\varepsilon)}e^{-4(\mu^{1}\wedge 0)t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-s/(2\varepsilon)}|g_{\varepsilon}^{i}(s)|^{2}\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\varepsilon x^{i}(0)^{2}}{\sqrt{1+4\varepsilon\mu^{i}}}+\frac{8|P_{\varepsilon}^{-1}|}{(1+4(\mu_{1}\wedge 0)\varepsilon)^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s/(2\varepsilon)}|f(s)|^{2}\,ds\,dt<+\infty

for every small enough
\varepsilon>0
, where finiteness of the integral is again due to (
2.2
). We have shown that (
2.14
) is a necessary and sufficient condition for having
y_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}
, and it identifies a unique solution of (
2.3
), because once
\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}(0)
is assigned the relation (
2.11
) at
t=0
identifies uniquely the vector
x_{\varepsilon}(0)
as the solution to the linear system

(2.15)

Px_{\varepsilon}(0)=y_{0}-P\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}(0).

The claim is proven, and thus for every fixed small enough
\varepsilon>0
there exists a unique minimizer
y_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}
for
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
over
\{y\in\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}:y(0)=y_{0}\}
, and it is characterized as the unique solution of (
2.3
) having finite
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
energy. Its expression is (
2.11
), with

(2.16)

\displaystyle x_{\varepsilon}^{i}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{\mu_{\varepsilon}^{i}t}\left(x_{\varepsilon}^{i}(0)+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}^{i}s}g_{\varepsilon}^{i}(s)\,ds\right)\qquad\mbox{ for $i=1,\ldots N,$}

\displaystyle\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}^{i}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}t}\displaystyle\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}s}g_{\varepsilon}^{i}(s)\,ds\qquad\mbox{ for $i=1,\ldots N$}

and with
x_{\varepsilon}(0)=P^{-1}(y_{0}-P\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}(0))
and
\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}(0)
given by (
2.14
).

Let us eventually check that such solution
y_{\varepsilon}
converges uniformly on compact intervals to the unique solution of (
2.1
).
We first recall that due to
\mathbb{R}^{2N}\ni q_{\varepsilon}=P_{\varepsilon}^{-1}f_{\varepsilon}
, where
f_{\varepsilon}=-(0,f/\varepsilon)
and
q_{\varepsilon}=(g_{\varepsilon},-g_{\varepsilon})
, by (
2.7
) we have
g_{\varepsilon}=(\varepsilon\bar{Q}_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon Q_{\varepsilon})^{-1}f
, and since
\varepsilon Q_{\varepsilon}\to 0
,
\varepsilon\bar{Q}_{\varepsilon}\to P
, and
|P|=|P^{-1}|=1
, we have, for a.e.
t>0
,

|g_{\varepsilon}(t)|\leq|(\varepsilon\bar{Q}_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon Q_{\varepsilon})^{-1}||f(t)|\leq 2|f(t)|
(having chosen
\varepsilon
small enough).
We have as a consequence, similarly as before by Hölder inequality and since
\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}\geq 1/(2\varepsilon)

(2.17)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}^{i}(s-t)}g_{\varepsilon}^{i}(s)\,ds\right|^{2}\leq 4\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{(t-s)/(2\varepsilon)}\,ds\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{(t-s)/(2\varepsilon)}|f(s)|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq 8\varepsilon\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{t}^{2T}e^{(t-s)/(2\varepsilon)}|f(s)|^{2}\,ds+8\varepsilon\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{2T}^{+\infty}e^{(t-s)/(2\varepsilon)}|f(s)|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq 8\varepsilon\sup_{t\in[0,2T]}\int_{t}^{2T}e^{(t-s)/(2\varepsilon)}|f(s)|^{2}\,ds+8\varepsilon\int_{2T}^{+\infty}e^{-s/(4\varepsilon)}|f(s)|^{2}\,ds

where the second term the right hand side vanishes as
\varepsilon\to 0
by dominated convergence due to (
2.2
), and the first term in the right hand side vanishes as well by invoking Lemma
2.2
below. Therefore
\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}(t)\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, uniformly on bounded intervals. In particular
\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}(0)\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
so that indeed from the relation (
2.15
) we see that
x_{\varepsilon}(0)\to P^{-1}y_{0}
.
As a consequence, it is readily seen that
e^{\mu_{\varepsilon}^{i}t}x_{\varepsilon}^{i}(0)\to e^{-\mu^{i}}t(P^{-1}y_{0})^{i}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
for every
i=1,\ldots,N
, uniformly on bounded time intervals. On the other hand by Hölder inequality

(2.18)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}e^{\mu_{\varepsilon}^{i}(t-s)}|g_{\varepsilon}(s)-P^{-1}f(s)|\,ds\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Te^{-4(\mu^{1}\wedge 0)T}\int_{0}^{T}|g_{\varepsilon}(s)-P^{-1}f(s)|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq Te^{-4(\mu^{1}\wedge 0)T}|(\varepsilon\bar{Q}_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon Q_{\varepsilon})^{-1}-P^{-1}|\int_{0}^{T}|f(s)|^{2}\,ds

where the right hand side vanishes as
\varepsilon\to 0
since (
2.2
) implies
f\in L^{2}(0,T)
for every
T>0
and since
\varepsilon Q_{\varepsilon}\to 0
,
\varepsilon\bar{Q}_{\varepsilon}\to P
. Since the right hand sides of both (
2.17
) and (
2.18
) are vanishing as
\varepsilon\to 0
from (
2.16
) and (
2.15
) we see that uniformly on bounded time intervals
\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}\to 0
and, for every
i=1,\ldots,N
,
x_{\varepsilon}^{i}
converge uniformly on bounded time intervals to

x^{i}(t):=e^{-\mu^{i}t}\left(x_{0}^{i}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\mu^{i}s}(P^{-1}f(s))^{i}\,ds\right),\qquad x_{0}^{i}:=(P^{-1}y_{0})^{i}.

Thus the limit
y
of
y_{\varepsilon}
is given by
Px
, thanks to (
2.11
), where

x
is the solution to
x^{\prime}=Dx+P^{-1}f
with initial datum
x(0)=x_{0}=P^{-1}y_{0}
, where
D\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
is the diagonal matrix with eigenvalues
-\mu^{1},\ldots,-\mu^{N}
. Since the columns of the orthogonal matrix
P
are the eigenvectors of
-A
, there holds
D=-P^{-1}AP
.
Going back to the variable
y
, we see that

y^{\prime}=Px^{\prime}=P(Dx+P^{-1}f)=PDP^{-1}y+f=-Ay+f,\qquad y(0)=Px(0)=y_{0},

so
y
is indeed the solution to (
2.1
).

The following simple lemma has been used through the proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
T\in(0,+\infty)
and
g\in L^{1}(0,T)
. For
\varepsilon>0
and
t\in[0,T]
, let

G_{\varepsilon}(t):=e^{t/\varepsilon}\int_{t}^{T}e^{-s/\varepsilon}|g(s)|\,ds.

Then
G_{\varepsilon}\to 0
in
C^{0}([0,T])
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Proof.

The result is trivial if
|g|\equiv 1
. By possibly switching from
|g|
to
|g|+1
, it is therefore not restrictive to assume that
|g|\geq 1
.
Let
\varepsilon\in(0,e^{-4})
, let
p_{\varepsilon}:=\sqrt{-\log\varepsilon}
, let
p_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}:=p_{\varepsilon}/(p_{\varepsilon}-1)
and let moreover
g_{\varepsilon}:=(p_{\varepsilon}-1)\wedge|g|
. Clearly
g_{\varepsilon}\geq 1
and
g_{\varepsilon}\to|g|
in
L^{1}(0,T)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Therefore, after having noticed that

\displaystyle e^{t/\varepsilon}\int_{t}^{T}e^{-s/\varepsilon}|g(s)|\,ds

\displaystyle=e^{t/\varepsilon}\int_{t}^{T}e^{-s/\varepsilon}g_{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds+e^{t/\varepsilon}\int_{t}^{T}e^{-s/\varepsilon}(|g(s)|-g_{\varepsilon}(s))\,ds

\displaystyle\leq e^{t/\varepsilon}\int_{t}^{T}e^{-s/\varepsilon}g_{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds+\int_{0}^{T}(|g(s)|-g_{\varepsilon}(s))\,ds,

we are left to prove that

(2.19)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}e^{t/\varepsilon}\int_{t}^{T}e^{-s/\varepsilon}g_{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds\to 0

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. We have by Hölder inequality

e^{t/\varepsilon}\int_{t}^{T}e^{-s/\varepsilon}g_{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds\leq e^{t/\varepsilon}\left(\int_{t}^{T}e^{-sp_{\varepsilon}/\varepsilon}\,ds\right)^{1/p_{\varepsilon}}\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}(0,T)}\leq\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{p_{\varepsilon}}\right)^{1/p_{\varepsilon}}\,\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}(0,T)}

and we claim that the right hand side goes to
0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, thus proving (
2.19
). Since
(1/p_{\varepsilon})^{1/p_{\varepsilon}}\to 1
and
p_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\to 1
, and since

\frac{\varepsilon^{p_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}/p_{\varepsilon}}}{T}\int_{0}^{T}g_{\varepsilon}(s)^{p_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\,ds\leq\varepsilon^{p_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}/p_{\varepsilon}}(p_{\varepsilon}-1)^{p_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}=e^{-p_{\varepsilon}p_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}(p_{\varepsilon}-1)^{p_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}=\,e^{-p_{\varepsilon}^{2}/(p_{\varepsilon}-1)}\,(p_{\varepsilon}-1)^{p_{\varepsilon}/(p_{\varepsilon}-1)}\to 0,

the claim follows, so that (
2.19
) holds. The proof is concluded.

3.
Nonlocal heat equations

In this section, the operator
\mathcal{L}:\mathrm{Dom}(\mathcal{L})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
appearing in equation (
1.1
) is assumed to be a self-adjoint Fourier multiplier, with
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset\mathrm{Dom}(\mathcal{L})
. We also assume that its symbol
\hat{L}
is bounded from below over
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, i.e.

(3.1)

\mbox{there exists $K\leq 0$ such that }\quad\hat{L}(\xi)\geq K\;\mbox{ for a.e. $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}

We further assume that the forcing term
f\in L^{1}_{loc}((0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfies the following property: the map
t\mapsto\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}
is Laplace transformable, i.e.,

(3.2)

\mbox{for every small enough $\varepsilon>0$ there holds
}\;\;\;\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\,dt<+\infty.

We define the functional setting for the main result. Let

V_{\mathcal{L}}:=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\hat{L}(\xi)||\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi<+\infty\right\}.

The space
V_{\mathcal{L}}
is the domain of the quadratic form
(u,v)\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\,\mathcal{L}v\,dx
associated with
\mathcal{L}
and appearing in functional
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
. It is normed by

\|v\|_{V_{\mathcal{L}}}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|\hat{L}(\xi)|)|v(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\right)^{1/2}.

We further define the weighted Sobolev space

H_{\varepsilon}:=\left\{u\in W^{1,1}_{loc}((0,+\infty);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})):\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\|u^{\prime}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}<+\infty\right\}.

We notice that for every
u\in H_{\varepsilon}
there holds by Young inequality

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,dt\leq\left[-\varepsilon e^{-t/\varepsilon}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right]_{0}^{T}+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\|u(0,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\left(\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+2\varepsilon\|u^{\prime}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right)\,dt

so that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,dt\leq\varepsilon\|u(0,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+2\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\|u^{\prime}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,dt.

We finally define the linear space

H^{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}:=\left\{u\in H_{\varepsilon}:\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\hat{L}(\xi)||\hat{u}(t,\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\,dt<+\infty\right\}.

Note that here and in the following the hat
\hat{}
symbol denotes Fourier transform in the space variable.
For every
u\in H^{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}
we have by Plancherel theorem

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}(\hat{u}):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|\hat{u}(t,\xi)|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\,\hat{L}(\xi)|\hat{u}(t,\xi)|^{2}-\hat{f}(t,\xi)\overline{\hat{u}(t,\xi)}\right)\,d\xi\,dt.

The Gateaux derivative of
\mathcal{F}
at
u\in H^{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}
is defined as the linear operator

(3.4)

\displaystyle d\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u)[\varphi]:

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u+\delta\varphi)-\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u)}{\delta}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\varepsilon u^{\prime}\varphi^{\prime}+\varphi\mathcal{L}u-f\varphi\right)\,dx\,dt,\qquad\varphi\in H^{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon},

where we used the fact that
\mathcal{L}
is self-adjoint. Similarly as above, making use of Plancherel theorem, the Gateaux derivative of
\mathcal{F}
is related to the Gateaux derivative of
\mathcal{J}
by

d\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}(\hat{u})[\hat{\varphi}]=d\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u)[\varphi]\qquad\mbox{for every $u\in H_{\varepsilon}^{\mathcal{L}}$ and every $\varphi\in H_{\varepsilon}^{\mathcal{L}}.$}

A critical point of functional
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
over
H^{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}
is naturally defined
as a function

u\in H^{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}
such that
d\mathcal{F}(u)[\varphi]=0\;\;\mbox{ for every }\;\;\varphi\in H^{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}.

Before stating the main result, let us preliminarily obtain a basic estimate for the solution to (
1.1
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
u_{0}\in V_{\mathcal{L}}
.
There exists a unique
u\in C^{0}([0,+\infty);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
solving (
1.1
) and it is given by the inverse Fourier transform of

(3.5)

\hat{u}(t,\xi)=e^{-\hat{L}(\xi)t}\left(\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\hat{L}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right).

Moreover,
u\in C^{0}([0,+\infty);V_{\mathcal{L}})
and for every
T>0

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{V_{\mathcal{L}}}\leq 2(1-K)(T+1)e^{-2KT}\left(\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{V_{\mathcal{L}}}+\int_{0}^{T}\|f(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right),

where
K\leq 0
is the constant from (
3.1
).

Proof.

The Fourier transform in space of the equation in (
1.1
) is
\hat{u}^{\prime}+\hat{L}\,\hat{u}=\hat{f}
, with the initial condition
\hat{u}(0,\xi)=\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)
. Therefore,
\hat{u}
takes the form (
3.5
). Moreover, taking advantage of (
3.1
) we have
the following estimate for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}(1+|\hat{L}(\xi)|)\int_{0}^{t}e^{\hat{L}(\xi)(s-t)}\,ds\leq(1-K)(T+1)e^{-KT}.

Indeed, if
\hat{L}(\xi)\leq 0
this is immediately obtained by using
|\hat{L}(\xi)|=-\hat{L}(\xi)\leq-K
, else if
\hat{L}(\xi)>0
this is obtained by noticing that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}(1+|\hat{L}(\xi)|)\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\hat{L}(\xi)(t-s)}\,ds=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\frac{1-e^{-\hat{L}(\xi)t}}{\hat{L}(\xi)}+(1-e^{-\hat{L}(\xi)t})\right)\leq T+1

since
1-e^{-x}\leq x
for every
x>0
.
Therefore by Hölder inequality

(3.6)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}(1+|\hat{L}(\xi)|)\left|\int_{0}^{t}e^{\hat{L}(\xi)(s-t)}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\right|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}(1+|\hat{L}(\xi)|)\int_{0}^{t}e^{\hat{L}(\xi)(s-t)}\,ds\int_{0}^{t}e^{\hat{L}(\xi)(s-t)}|\hat{f}(s,\xi)|^{2}\,ds\,

\displaystyle\leq(1-K)(T+1)e^{-2KT}\int_{0}^{T}|\hat{f}(s,\xi)|^{2}\,ds\qquad\mbox{for a.e. $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}.

By using (
3.1
) again and by Fubini theorem we deduce

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\hat{u}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{V_{\mathcal{L}}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|\hat{L}(\xi)|)e^{-2\hat{L}(\xi)t}|\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle+2\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|\hat{L}(\xi)|)\left|\int_{0}^{t}e^{\hat{L}(\xi)(s-t)}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{-2KT}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{V_{\mathcal{L}}}+2(1-K)(T+1)e^{-2KT}\int_{0}^{T}\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,ds

thus concluding the proof.

We now state the main result, proving that the solution to problem (
1.1
), with
u_{0}\in V_{\mathcal{L}}
, is the limit of minimizers of functional
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
over
\{u\in H_{\varepsilon}^{\mathcal{L}}:u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\mbox{ for a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}\}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume (
3.1
) and (
3.2
). Let
u_{0}\in V_{\mathcal{L}}
. For every small enough
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a unique function
u_{\varepsilon}\in H_{\varepsilon}^{\mathcal{L}}
such that
u_{\varepsilon}
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
over
\{u\in H_{\varepsilon}^{\mathcal{L}}:u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\mbox{ for a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}\}
. Moreover, letting
u
denote the unique solution to problem (
1.1
), then as
\varepsilon\to 0
we have
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
a.e. in space-time, and for every
T>0

u_{\varepsilon}\to u\;\;\mbox{in $C^{0}([0,T];V_{\mathcal{L}})$}.

Proof.

We preliminarily observe that functional
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
, for every fixed small enough
\varepsilon
, is bounded from below over the set
\{u\in H^{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}:u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\mbox{ for a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}\}
. This is a direct consequence of (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), since Young inequality entails

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(t,x)u(t,x)\,dx\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq 4\varepsilon\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\|f(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{2}\,dt+\frac{1}{8}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\|u^{\prime}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\,dt,

with the shorthand
\|\cdot\|_{2}
for
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
, here and in the rest of the proof.
Moreover there holds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\,\mathcal{L}u\,dx\,dt\geq\frac{K}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\geq K\varepsilon\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{2}+2K\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\|u^{\prime}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\,dt.

As a consequence, for every small enough
\varepsilon>0
we have the following bound from below

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(u_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\|u^{\prime}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{2}\,dt-\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(t,x)u(t,x)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\,\mathcal{L}u\,dx\,dt\geq-4\varepsilon\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\|f(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{2}\,dt+\left(K\varepsilon-\frac{1}{8}\right)\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{2}.

We also preliminarily observe that if
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}
is a critical point of
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
, then for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
by (
3.4
) we have

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\varepsilon u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\varphi^{\prime}+\varphi\mathcal{L}u_{\varepsilon}-f\varphi\right)\,dx\,dt=0.

Integration by parts in time entails

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(-\varepsilon u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}+u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}+\mathcal{L}u_{\varepsilon}-f\right)\varphi\,dx\,dt=0.

This shows that, in Fourier variables, for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
the function
\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\xi)
satisfies the ODE

(3.7)

-\varepsilon\hat{u}^{\prime\prime}+\hat{u}^{\prime}+\hat{L}(\xi)\hat{u}=\hat{f},\quad t>0.

In particular, a minimizer of
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
over
\{u\in H_{\varepsilon}^{\mathcal{L}}:u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\mbox{ for a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}\}
has Fourier transform that necessarily satisfies (
3.7
).

Step 1: uniqueness.

Suppose that
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
over
\{u\in H_{\varepsilon}^{\mathcal{L}}:u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\mbox{ for a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}\}
. Thus it satisfies (
3.7
).
The characteristic polynomial of the operator in the right hand side of (
3.7
) is
-\varepsilon\lambda^{2}+\lambda+\hat{L}(\xi)
, therefore for every small enough
\varepsilon
(independently on
\xi
, since
\hat{L}
is bounded from below) its roots are real and given by

\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi):=\frac{1-Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}{2\varepsilon},\qquad\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi):=\frac{1+Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}{2\varepsilon},\quad\qquad\mbox{where}\quad Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi):=\sqrt{1+4\varepsilon\hat{L}(\xi)}.

Moreover, the general solution of (
3.7
) is given, for every fixed
\xi
, by

(3.8)

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=e^{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}\left(c_{1}(\xi)+\frac{1}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right)

\displaystyle+e^{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}\left(c_{2}(\xi)-\frac{1}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right),

and by recalling (
3.3
),
v_{\varepsilon}
belonging to
H_{\varepsilon}
requires by Fubini and Plancherel theorem that

(3.9)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}|\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)|^{2}\,dt<+\infty\quad\mbox{for a.e. $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$.}

We notice that by (
3.8
) we have

e^{-t/(2\varepsilon)}\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)=a_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)+b_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi),

where

a_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi):=e^{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t/(2\varepsilon)}\left(c_{2}(\xi)-\frac{1}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right),

b_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi):=e^{-Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t/(2\varepsilon)}\left(c_{1}(\xi)+\frac{1}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right)

As an immediate consequence of (
3.2
) we have, for every small enough
\varepsilon
,

\limsup_{t\to+\infty}|b_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)|\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s/(2\varepsilon)}|\hat{f}(s,\xi)|\,ds<+\infty\quad\mbox{ for a.e. $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}

and

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\,|\hat{f}(s,\xi)|\,ds=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\,|\hat{f}(s,\xi)|\,ds<+\infty\quad\mbox{ for a.e. $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}.

Therefore, a necessary condition for the validity of (
3.9
) is

c_{2}(\xi)=\frac{1}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\quad\mbox{ for a.e. $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}.

Inserting this in (
3.8
), we deduce that being
u_{\varepsilon}
a minimizer satisfying the initial condition
\hat{u}(0,\xi)=\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)
for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then its Fourier transform in space must take the form

(3.10)

\displaystyle\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=e^{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}\left(\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)-\frac{1}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{e^{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds+\frac{e^{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds.

Step 2: existence
.
Let us check that the inverse Fourier transform of the function from (
3.10
) belongs indeed to
H_{\varepsilon}^{\mathcal{L}}
, thus showing it is the unique minimizer of
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
over
\{u\in H_{\varepsilon}^{\mathcal{L}}:u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\mbox{ for a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}\}
, and in fact the only solution to (
3.7
) with initial datum
u_{0}
and with finite
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
energy.
We have

(3.11)

\displaystyle\hat{u}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\,e^{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}\left(\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)-\frac{1}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\,e^{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds+\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\,e^{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds.

We remark that (
3.1
) implies that for every small enough
\varepsilon>0
(such that
4\varepsilon|K|<1/2
) the following estimates hold for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

(3.12)

Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\qquad\frac{|\hat{L}(\xi)|}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{4\varepsilon},\qquad\left|\frac{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\right|\leq\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\leq\frac{1+\sqrt{2}}{2\varepsilon},\qquad\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\geq\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}

as well as

(3.13)

\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\leq-2K,\qquad|\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)|\leq\sqrt{\frac{|\hat{L}(\xi)|}{\varepsilon}},\qquad-\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\leq\hat{L}(\xi),

obtained by using that
\sqrt{1+x}\geq 1+x
for
x\in(-1,0)
, that
|1-\sqrt{1+y}|\leq\sqrt{|y|}
for
y\in(-1,+\infty)
, and that
\sqrt{1+z}-1\leq z/2
for
z\in(-1,+\infty)
, respectively.
The estimates (
3.12
) along with
Hölder inequality and Fubini theorem entail

(3.14)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\left|\frac{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\right|^{2}\,e^{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}\left|\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right|^{2}\,d\xi\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\left|\frac{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\right|^{2}\frac{1}{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\,e^{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}|\hat{f}(s,\xi)|^{2}\,ds\right)\,d\xi\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1+\sqrt{2})^{2}}{2\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/(\sqrt{2}\varepsilon)}\,dt\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s/(2\varepsilon)}\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(1+\sqrt{2})^{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s/(2\varepsilon)}\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\,ds<+\infty

for every small enough
\varepsilon>0
, where finiteness is due to (
3.2
), and similarly

(3.15)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\frac{|\hat{L}(\xi)|}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)^{2}}\,e^{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}\left|\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right|^{2}\,d\xi\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\,e^{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}\,dt\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s/(2\varepsilon)}\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,\varepsilon\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s/(2\varepsilon)}\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{2}\,ds<+\infty.

The same arguments using (
3.12
)-(
3.13
) also yield

(3.16)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\left|\frac{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\right|^{2}\,e^{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}\left|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right|^{2}\,d\xi\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{2}}{2\varepsilon}\right)^{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}te^{-t/\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}e^{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}(t-s)}|\hat{f}(s,\xi)|^{2}\,ds\,d\xi\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{2}}{2\varepsilon}\right)^{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}te^{-t/\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-4K(t-s)}\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{2}}{2\varepsilon}\right)^{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}te^{-4Kt}e^{-t/(2\varepsilon)}\,dt\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s/(2\varepsilon)}\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{2}}{1+8\varepsilon K}\right)^{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s/(2\varepsilon)}\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\,ds<+\infty

and similarly

(3.17)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\frac{|\hat{L}(\xi)|}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)^{2}}\,e^{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}\left|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right|^{2}\,d\xi\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{(1+8\varepsilon K)^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s/(2\varepsilon)}\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\,ds<+\infty.

Furthermore, by the same reasoning,

(3.18)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\left|\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\right|^{2}\,e^{2\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}\left|\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right|^{2}\,d\xi\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\left|\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\right|^{2}\,e^{2\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\,ds\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}|\hat{f}(s,\xi)|^{2}\,ds\,d\xi\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1+\sqrt{2})^{2}}{2\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(s-t)}|\hat{f}(s,\xi)|^{2}\,ds\,d\xi\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1+\sqrt{2})^{2}}{2\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/(2\varepsilon)}\,dt\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s/(2\varepsilon)}\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle=(1+\sqrt{2})^{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s/(2\varepsilon)}\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\,ds<+\infty

and

(3.19)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\frac{|\hat{L}(\xi)|}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)^{2}}\,e^{2\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}\left|\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right|^{2}\,d\xi\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s/(2\varepsilon)}\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\,ds<+\infty.

Eventually, again with (
3.12
)-(
3.13
) we have

(3.20)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}|\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)|^{2}\,e^{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}|\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\,e^{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}|\hat{L}(\xi)||\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\,dt\leq\sqrt{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\hat{L}(\xi)||\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi<+\infty

and

(3.21)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}|\hat{L}(\xi)|\,e^{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}|\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\,dt\leq\sqrt{2}\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\hat{L}(\xi)||\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi<+\infty

since
u_{0}\in V_{\mathcal{L}}
.
By recalling the expression of
\hat{u}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
from (
3.11
), and by taking into account (
3.14
), (
3.16
), (
3.18
), (
3.20
) we deduce that

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\|\hat{u}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,dt<+\infty

for every small enough
\varepsilon>0
. Similarly, from (
3.10
) and taking into account (
3.15
), (
3.17
), (
3.19
), (
3.21
) we infer

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\hat{L}(\xi)||\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\,dt<+\infty

for every small enough
\varepsilon>0
. By Plancherel theorem, we have therefore
u_{\varepsilon}\in H_{\varepsilon}^{\mathcal{L}}
for every small enough
\varepsilon>0
.

Step 3: convergence.

We prove that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in
C^{0}([0,T];V_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
for every
T>0
, i.e.,

(3.22)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|u_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)-u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{V_{\mathcal{L}}}=0.

The main estimates that follow are similar to the ones of the previous step.
We have by (
3.12
)-(
3.13
) and Hölder inequality

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\frac{e^{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{-4Kt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}|\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\,d\xi\leq 4\varepsilon e^{-4Kt}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s/(2\varepsilon)}\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\,ds,

therefore

(3.23)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\frac{e^{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}4\varepsilon e^{-4KT}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s/(2\varepsilon)}\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\,ds=0

where the integral in the right hand side is indeed vanishing as
\varepsilon\to 0
thanks to the dominated convergence theorem and to the assumption (
3.2
). Similarly, by also using the second estimate in (
3.12
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\hat{L}(\xi)|\left|\frac{e^{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right|^{2}\,d\xi\leq\frac{1}{2}e^{-4Kt}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s/(2\varepsilon)}\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\,ds

so that

(3.24)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\hat{L}(\xi)|\left|\frac{e^{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right|^{2}\,d\xi=0.

Moreover, (
3.12
)-(
3.13
) allow to deduce with analogous reasoning that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\frac{e^{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right|^{2}\,d\xi\leq 4\varepsilon\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-(s-t)/(2\varepsilon)}\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\,ds,

and that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\hat{L}(\xi)|\left|\frac{e^{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right|^{2}\,d\xi\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-(s-t)/(2\varepsilon)}\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\,ds.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-(s-t)/(2\varepsilon)}\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{t}^{2T}e^{-(s-t)/(2\varepsilon)}\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\,ds+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{2T}^{+\infty}e^{-(s-t)/(2\varepsilon)}\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in[0,2T]}\int_{t}^{2T}e^{-(s-t)/(2\varepsilon)}\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\,ds+\int_{2T}^{+\infty}e^{-s/(4\varepsilon)}\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\,ds

where the first term in right hand side vanishes as
\varepsilon\to 0
due to Lemma
2.2
, since (
3.2
) implies that the function
s\mapsto\|\hat{f}(s,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}
is in
L^{1}(0,2T)
, while the second term in the right hand side vanishes by assumption (
3.2
) and by dominated convergence. We conclude that

(3.25)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|\hat{L}(\xi)|)\left|\frac{e^{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right|^{2}\,d\xi=0

We also notice that thanks to (
3.13
) and (
3.1
)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|e^{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}-e^{-\hat{L}(\xi)t}\right|^{2}\leq T^{2}\,e^{-4KT}|\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)-\hat{L}(\xi)|^{2}

so that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|e^{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}-e^{-\hat{L}(\xi)t}\right|^{2}\to 0

as
\varepsilon\to 0
for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
and also

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|e^{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}-e^{-\hat{L}(\xi)t}\right|^{2}\leq 2\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(e^{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}+e^{-2\hat{L}(\xi)t}\right)\leq 4e^{-4KT}

for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Therefore by dominated convergence theorem and since
u_{0}\in V_{\mathcal{L}}
we deduce

(3.26)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|\hat{L}(\xi)|)\left|e^{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}-e^{-\hat{L}(\xi)t}\right|^{2}\,|\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi=0

We finally claim that as
\varepsilon\to 0

(3.27)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|\hat{L}(\xi)|)\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\frac{e^{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\hat{L}(\xi)(t-s)}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right|^{2}\,d\xi\to 0.

We will prove the claim by dominated convergence.
By (
3.1
), (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) we directly have that if
\hat{L}(\xi)\leq 0
, then

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\frac{1+|\hat{L}(\xi)|}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)(t-s)}\,ds\leq 2(1-K)Te^{-4KT},

whereas for
\hat{L}(\xi)>0
(so that
\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)<0
) there holds

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\frac{1+|\hat{L}(\xi)|}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)(t-s)}\,ds\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\frac{1+|\hat{L}(\xi)|}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)^{2}}\frac{e^{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}-1}{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\frac{\varepsilon\hat{L}(\xi)(1-e^{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t})}{(1+4\varepsilon\hat{L}(\xi))(\sqrt{1+4\varepsilon\hat{L}(\xi)}-1)}+\frac{e^{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}-1}{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}+2T,

having used the elementary inequalities
\frac{2x}{(1+4x)(\sqrt{1+4x}-1)}\leq 1
for every
x>0
and
\frac{1-e^{-y}}{y}\leq 1
for every
y>0
. Combining the last two estimates we see that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\frac{1+|\hat{L}(\xi)|}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)(t-s)}\leq 2(1-K)(T+1)e^{-4KT}\qquad\mbox{ for a.e. $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$},

thus Hölder inequality entails

(3.28)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\frac{1+|\hat{L}(\xi)|}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)^{2}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}e^{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)(t-s)}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\frac{1+|\hat{L}(\xi)|}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)(t-s)}\,ds\int_{0}^{t}|\hat{f}(s,\xi)|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq 2(1-K)(T+1)e^{-4KT}\int_{0}^{T}|\hat{f}(s,\xi)|^{2}\,ds\qquad\mbox{ for a.e. $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}.

By (
3.6
) and (
3.28
), we find that

h(\xi):=6(1-K)(T+1)e^{-4KT}\int_{0}^{T}|\hat{f}(s,\xi)|^{2}\,ds

is a dominating function for the integrand in (
3.27
), noticing that indeed
h\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as a consequence of assumption
3.2
. Let us next prove the pointwise a.e. convergence to
0
for the integrand in (
3.27
). Indeed, we have by (
3.12
)-(
3.13
) that for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

(3.29)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\frac{e^{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds-\frac{e^{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\hat{L}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\frac{e^{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}(e^{\hat{L}(\xi)s}-e^{-\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s})|\hat{f}(s,\xi)|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{-4KT}\int_{0}^{T}(e^{\hat{L}(\xi)s}-e^{-\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s})|\hat{f}(s,\xi)|\,ds.

For a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the above right hand side vanishes as
\varepsilon\to 0
, since we have
-\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\to\hat{L}(\xi)
and
(e^{\hat{L}(\xi)s}-e^{-\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s})|\hat{f}(s,\xi)|\leq e^{|\hat{L}(\xi)|T}|\hat{f}(s,\xi)|
for every
s\in[0,T]
, where
s\mapsto f(s,\xi)
is in
L^{1}((0,T))
thanks to Hölder inequality and (
3.2
). On the other hand, for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
we have

(3.30)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\frac{e^{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\hat{L}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds-{e^{-\hat{L}(\xi)t}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\hat{L}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(e^{|\hat{L}(\xi)|T}\int_{0}^{T}|\hat{f}(s,\xi)|\,ds\right)\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\frac{e^{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}-e^{-\hat{L}(\xi)t}\right|

and we notice that by (
3.1
), (
3.12
), (
3.13
)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\frac{e^{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}-e^{-\hat{L}(\xi)t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|e^{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}-e^{-\hat{L}(\xi)t}|+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\frac{e^{-\hat{L}(\xi)}t}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}-e^{-\hat{L}(\xi)t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}e^{-2KT}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t+\hat{L}(\xi)t|+e^{-KT}\left|\frac{1}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}-1\right|,

and the right hand side vanishes for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, since
\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\to-\hat{L}(\xi)
and
Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\to 1
.
Having shown that the right hand sides of (
3.29
) and (
3.30
) are both vanishing for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we infer that the same holds for the integrand in (
3.27
) so that (
3.27
) does hold and the claim is proved.

The combination of (
3.23
), (
3.24
), (
3.25
), (
3.26
) and (
3.27
) allows to obtain (
3.22
).
Since the right hand sides of of (
3.29
) and (
3.30
) are both vanishing for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we also deduce that

\frac{e^{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)t}}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\xi)s}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds\to\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\hat{L}(\xi)(t-s)}\hat{f}(s,\xi)\,ds

for a.e.
(t,\xi)\in(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, and similarly by taking into account the previous estimates we see that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
a.e. in space-time as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

We conclude with a simple remark. We verify that existence of a minimizer of
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
over
\{u\in H_{\varepsilon}^{\mathcal{L}}:u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\mbox{ for a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}\}
can be obtained via direct method under a positivity assumption on the operator
\mathcal{L}
. In order to do this, let
\hat{L}\geq 0
. If
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\{u\in H^{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}:u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\mbox{ for a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}\}
is a minimizing sequence for
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
, then by (
3.3
) we have that up to subsequences
u_{n}
is weakly converging to
u
in
L^{2}_{w}((0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where the weight
w
is
e^{-t/\varepsilon}\,dt\,dx
, and
u_{n}^{\prime}
is weakly converging to
u^{\prime}
in
L^{2}_{w}((0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
. The weak lower semicontinuity of the
L^{2}
norm, the continuity of the linear operator
u\mapsto\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,t)u(x,t)\,dx\,dt
, following from (
3.2
), and the weak lower semicontinuity of the nonnegative quadratic form
u\mapsto\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\,\mathcal{L}u\,dx\,dt
entail

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u)\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{n}).

Moreover, the uniform bounds of
(u_{n})
and
(u_{n}^{\prime})
in
L^{2}((0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
imply the a.e. identities
u(0,\cdot)=u_{n}(0,\cdot)=u_{0}(\cdot)
, for every
n
,
thus showing the minimality of
u

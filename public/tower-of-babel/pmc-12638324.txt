Machine learning-based fabrication of phytogenic NiO nanoparticles for anticancer activity in HepG2 Cell Culture
Authors: Amina Bibi, Afzaal Hussain, Tariq Munir, Muhammad Zeshan Yaqoob, Arslan Mahmood, Shoaib Nazir, Yasin Khan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Metal oxide nanomaterials play a central role in biomedical applications due to their unique physicochemical properties. In particular, various treatment methods such as drug delivery, hyperthermia therapy, radiation, and chemotherapy are used for the treatment of carcinoma. Current studies prefer to investigate the anticancer activity of nickel oxide nanoparticles were synthesized using a green synthesis approach. The X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis was used to investigate the cubic crystalline structure and crystallite size varies from 11.08 nm to 12.88 nm due to increased calcination temperature. The crystallite size has a significant impact on the cytotoxicity and toxicity of nanoparticles; smaller crystal sizes frequently result in higher toxicity, because of their larger surface area to volume ratio. The MTT (Tetrazolium salts) assay was performed to test the cytotoxicity of NiO nanoparticles (NPs) against HepG2 cell line. After that, machine learning was applied to connect the biomedical field with artificial intelligence. It can be seen from the results that the NiO NPs that were calcinated at 600 °C gave the average cell viability <40%. At last, the machine learning approach was used to calculate the cytotoxicity of NiO NPs and decision tree was generated by using Google Colab. A correlation matrix was generated using a machine learning approach, providing insights into the interdependence among all parameters.Graphical Abstract

Introduction

Cancer is second leading disease worldwide, which caused by the uncontrolled growth of abnormal cells. While as tumors has become a significant global health problem/ challenge, with such increasing incidence rates [1]. The increasing incidence rate of cancer may be due to various aspects including persistent infections, sedentary lifestyles and hereditary traits [2]. Despite the enormous advances in our knowledge of cancer biology and the development of innovative therapeutic treatments, cancer still significantly continues to be a major cause of morbidity and death worldwide [3, 4]. Traditional cancer treatments including radiation therapy, Chemotherapy, and surgery have been essential in managing this medical condition [5, 6]. However, these treatments often cause adverse effects and limited efficiency and these methods also damage healthy cells, especially when cancer is aggressive or at an advanced stage [7, 8].

To overcome these challenges, metal oxide nanoparticles can be used like silver oxide nanoparticles, copper oxide nanoparticles, and nickel oxide nanoparticles for the treatment of cancer [9, 10]. Their unique properties have the potential to improve the accuracy and efficiency of biomedical therapies and diagnostic techniques, unlocking vast possibilities for advancements in the medical field [11, 12]. Additionally, they are biocompatible, and their capacity to deliver anti-cancer drugs makes them appropriate for cancer treatment [13]. These metal oxide nanoparticles can be synthesized by using different approaches such as chemical and green [14]. Green synthesis has been appeared as a sustainable and eco-friendly strategy for the fabrication of metal oxide nanoparticles in cancer therapy [15–17]. In these techniques natural resources like plant extracts, microbes, or biomolecules as reducing agents are used, eliminating the need for hazardous chemicals [18]. Green synthesis enhances the nanoparticles biocompatibility and can reduce its adverse impact on healthy cells, making it more appropriate for use in biomedical applications [19, 20].

Nickel oxide nanoparticles have emerged as promising candidates in cancer treatments. Their unique qualities like high stability, surface modifiability, and the generation of ROS, which can induce cancer cell death, make them ideal for overcoming the challenges of conventional therapeutic treatment [13, 21]. Due to their distinctive and enhanced physicochemical properties, they are highly desirable for advanced biomedical applications including Drug delivery, imaging, and cancer therapy [22, 23]. Ongoing research in the field of medicine aims to develop biocompatible and environment-friendly nanoparticles for the cure of cancer.

Machine learning has gained considerable attention in the biomedical field due to the expanding accessibility of huge data, improvements in computational power, and the need to solve problems in healthcare [24, 25]. Machine learning algorithms can assess large and complicated datasets far beyond human ability, revolutionizing diagnostics and treatment approaches. Machine Learning played a vital role in diagnosing, drug delivery system and medical imaging by evaluating complex biological data and identifying critical patterns. By integrating data-driven approaches, ML continues to transform biomedical research, leading to more efficient and personalized healthcare solutions [26, 27].

In this study, the green synthesis approach was used to synthesize nickel oxide (NiO) nanoparticles. For Green synthesis, egg white was used because of its rich protein content, which acts as a natural reducing agent and stabilizing agent. It is easily available, less costly, and overall eco-friendly. After the synthesis, these Nanoparticles were characterized by using X-ray diffraction (XRD) to determine their structural and crystalline properties. After the characterization, these nanoparticles were tested by using an MTT assay on cell line HepG2 (Liver cancer cells) to check their toxicity against the carcinoma cells. After that machine learning was used to predict the cytotoxicity by which dose optimization can be done and at last the correlation matrix was generated to check the interdependence of all the parameters that were used in input file for the machine learning.

Green synthesis approach

Green synthesis approach was used to synthesize nickel oxide nanoparticles. For the green synthesis process the Egg white was used as the biological or natural agent. First of all, 50 mL of Egg white was dissolved in 30 mL of deionized water and it was stirred almost for 15–20 min. Another solution, 7.26 g of nickel nitrate Ni (NO3)2 was dissolved into 50 mL of de-ionized water and it was stirred for 15–20 min. After that, the Nickel nitrate solution was added to the egg white solution drop wise at the temperature of 70 °C. At last, the solution was dried in an oven at 400 °C and then calcinated in tube furnace at different temperature.

Material characterization technique

The X-ray diffraction (XRD) technique was used to study the structural and crystalline nature of NiO nanoparticles, providing valuable insights into their potential applications.

Bioassay

HepG2 cell line

HepG2 is a human cancer cell line derived from hepatocellular carcinoma, frequently employed for determining metabolic activity. This cell line is widely used in pharmacology and toxicological research for assessing hepatotoxicity, drug metabolism, and the processes driving disorders linked to the liver [28].

MTT assay

The MTT assay was used to test the HepG2 cell lines with NiO NPs. This assay measures the activity of living cells by detecting the functionality of active mitochondria, which reduces the MTT reagent to purple formazan crystals, indicating metabolic activity. The liver cancer cell line was cultivated using 96-well plates and a 25-cm tissue culture flask that contributes a 10% solution of fetal bovine serum and Hank salts to DMEM. Additionally, Glutamine 2 mM/L and a few amino acids were incubated for 24 h at 37 °C. These Cultured cells were treated with solutions of NiO NPs at different doses (0–400 µg/mL). MTT assay

The HepG2 liver cancer cell line was used to assess the anticancer potential of NiO nanoparticles that were calcinated at different temperatures like 400 °C, 500 °C and 600 °C. Remarkably, the results showed that the NiO nanoparticles that were calcinated at 600 °C significantly reduced the cell viability in contrast to the other NiO nanoparticles that were calcinated at 400 °C and 500 °C. The reduction of cell viability up to 40% was significantly enhanced as the concentration increased from 10 mg/mL to 400 mg/mL. The green synthesized NiO NP’s exceptional performance indicates their potential to be effective options for fighting liver cancer cell lines. In simple words, green synthesized NiO nanoparticles provide the reactive oxygen species that can be used to kill cancer cells [34]. Machine learning

In biomedical research, machine learning has become an efficient tool for pattern detection and data-driven predictions of complicated biological interactions. By offering data-driven insights and automating complicated analytical procedures, Machine learning has transformed many fields like healthcare, nanotechnology, and biomedical science. In the realm of cytotoxicity prediction by using machine-learning models, supervised machine learning models are very helpful because they enable the models to learn from labeled experimental data and categorize the cytotoxicity of different nanoparticles according to their biological and physiochemical characteristics [35].

In this study, supervised machine learning was utilized to predict the cytotoxicity of green synthesized NiO nanoparticles based on the experimental data. By training these models on Biological and physiochemical parameters, Machine learning assists in finding the correlation and trends that could be difficult to detect by conventional statistical techniques or manually. Decision tree

The following decision tree was generated by using Google Colab. The decision tree represents the classification model that was generated based on specific threshold values. Here, the tree starts with a node that represents the cytotoxicity with the threshold value of 40.015 with a Gini of 0.626. At this level, the cytotoxicity was split into four classes: moderate (351 samples), weak (90 samples), strong (189 samples), and non-cytotoxic (42 samples).With a Gini index value of 0.626, which reflects the dataset’s impurity, there is a comparatively high degree of classification variety. Following the first split, the samples with cell viability value of 40 or lower were categorized as the strong cytotoxicity with a Gini index of 0.0, which represents the pure classification. Further, in the next node, the split occurred at the threshold value of 60, where the samples were categorized as the moderate cytotoxicity with the Gini index of 0.43 that represents the lower impurity in classification. Further, the nodes were split according to the threshold values of 80 and above. The samples that have the cell viability of 80 were categorized as weak while the other remaining were categorized as the non cytotoxic with the Gini index of 0.0 which showed the pure classification.

Figure 4 represents the decision tree successfully classifies the cytotoxic levels according to the predetermined value. As the tree grows, the Gini index values gradually decrease along the branches, indicating a higher degree of categorization purity. This structure is a useful tool for decision-making in biological and pharmaceutical applications since it offers a systematic method to cytotoxicity prediction.Fig. 4Decision tree classifies the cytotoxic levels according to the predetermined value

This analysis played a vital in feature selection and also gave ideas about the highly correlated and less correlated parameters that can be eliminated. By utilizing these outcomes, the models were refined which led to the improved accuracy of the model for Cytotoxicity prediction and also for dose optimization.

Conclusion

The green synthesis approach was used for the synthesis of nickels oxide nanoparticles. The cubic crystal structure appeared in NiO nanoparticles was investigated by XRD analysis and all samples were calcinated at 400 °C, 500 °C, and 600 °C. After that, the anticancer potential of NiO NPs was investigated by using an MTT assay. Cell viability was calculated against the HepG2 cell line. It can be seen from the results that the NiO NPs that were calcinated at 600 °C give the average cell viability <40%. At last, the machine learning approach was used to calculate the cytotoxicity of NiO NPs and a correlation matrix was generated by using the machine learning approach, which gave the information about the interdependence of all parameters. In the future, the NiO NPs will be utilized for medication administration and remote control processes in cancer therapy.

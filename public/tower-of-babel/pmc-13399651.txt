Increasing Patient Interest in Meniscus Surgery During Last 15 Years With Pain and Recovery Being Most Frequently Searched Topics Online
Authors: Irfan A. Khan, Tarick Ahmad, Fahad Faheem, Badr Squali‐Houssaini, Dillon Synchef, Brian J. Cole, Paul Phillips
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo use Google Trends to study whether public interest in meniscus surgery has increased during the last 15 years and after the COVID‐19 pandemic, and to determine what specific topics regarding meniscus surgery the public is interested in.MethodsA longitudinal observational study was conducted between January 2010 and December 2024 on public interest in meniscus surgery utilizing Google Trends. Through establishing a focus group comprising two attending orthopaedic surgeons (sports‐medicine fellowship trained), one orthopaedic surgery resident, and three medical students, and using ChatGPT, the 50 most commonly searched questions regarding meniscus surgery were identified. The mean relative search volume (RSV) was identified and compared for 50 search terms. RSV extends from 0 to 100, with 0 representing no public interest, and 100 representing maximal public interest. Analysis of variance tests compared means. Statistical significance was set at a P value less than .05.ResultsBetween 2010 and 2024, the mean RSV for “meniscus surgery” increased significantly (2010: 40; 2024: 89; P < .001), after a transient decline during the COVID‐19 pandemic. Among 50 topics regarding meniscus surgery, “recovery” and “pain” had the highest mean RSVs, while other topics including “arthroscopic meniscus surgery”, “cost”, “how long does meniscus surgery take”, “therapy”, “brace”, “rehab”, “swelling”, and “arthritis” were also commonly searched (60 vs 25 vs 15 vs 9 vs 9 vs 6 vs 6 vs 5 vs 4 vs 2; P < .001).ConclusionsPatient interest in meniscus surgery has increased significantly during the past 15 years, and rebounded after the COVID‐19 pandemic. Patients have interest in many topics regarding meniscus surgery, including recovery, pain, surgical length, cost, postoperative therapy, braces, and post‐traumatic arthritis.Clinical RelevanceThis study showed that public interest in meniscus surgery has increased significantly in the last 15 years, and the various topics that are important to patients, as identified in this study, should be included in patient education materials.

Meniscus surgeries are among the most common elective orthopaedic procedures, as meniscal pathologies affect 12% of the general adult population worldwide. 1 , 2 In the United States, 850,000 meniscus surgeries are performed yearly, accounting for 10% to 20% of annual orthopaedic procedures. 3 During the COVID‐19 pandemic, there was a significant reduction in elective orthopaedic surgeries, 4 causing the number of arthroscopic knee surgeries being performed to decrease significantly. 5 , 6 In accordance, public interest in elective orthopaedic procedures including meniscus surgery, derived from the frequency of Google searches, also experienced a significant decline. 7 However, little is known about public interest in meniscus surgeries using internet‐based searches after the COVID‐19 pandemic.

The internet is a popular source of medical information for patients, as an estimated 80% of Americans report searching the internet for health‐related inquiries. 8 , 9 Sixty‐nine percent of American patients use the internet as their first source of health information, compared with only 15% who seek a health care provider first. 10 Additionally, there are over 70,000 health care‐related Google searches a minute, equating to over 1 billion searches a day and amounting to 7% of all Google searches. 9 As the public has increasingly turned to the internet, and specifically Google, for their health concerns, Google Trends provides an accessible and pertinent account of public interest by analyzing the relative search volume (RSV) of terms entered into Google. 11 The tool enables the tracking of search term trends over time, offering valuable insights into which topics are becoming more or less popular among patients. Google Trends has been used to study public interest in a variety of health‐related fields; however, there is a paucity of literature regarding public interest in meniscus surgery. 11

The purpose of this study was to use Google Trends to evaluate whether public interest in meniscus surgery has increased over time, especially after the COVID‐19 pandemic, and to highlight the most commonly searched topics related to meniscus surgery. We hypothesized that public interest in meniscus surgery has significantly increased over time and has peaked after the COVID‐19 pandemic, and that pain and recovery would be some of the most frequently searched perioperative topics related to meniscus surgery.

METHODS

Study Design and Setting

This study was a longitudinal observational study on public interest in meniscus surgery in the United States, which was performed from January 2010 to December 2024 using Google Trends. Google Trends was utilized to obtain the mean RSV (rated from 0, which represents minimal public interest, to 100, which represents maximal interest) of 5 search terms associated with meniscus surgery (“meniscus surgery”, “meniscus repair”, “meniscectomy”, “meniscus transplant”, and “meniscus procedure”). 11 Mean RSV between 2010 and 2024 was extracted for the most popular of these search terms.

Mean RSV reflects the proportion of all Google searches that a search term comprises. 12 Therefore, if the overall number of Google searches remains steady but the number of searches for a specific search term increases, the mean RSV for that search term will increase. If the number of Google searches increases over time but the number of searches for a specific search term remains steady or decreases, the mean RSV for that search term will decrease. Through the mean RSV being calculated in this fashion, this provides insight on the overall popularity of a search term over time, while accounting for more searches being conducted on Google due to increased access to technology over time. 13

After identifying the most popular search term, 50 perioperative topics about that search term were identified. This was performed through the creation of a focus group consisting of 2 orthopaedic sports‐medicine fellowship‐trained surgeons, 1 orthopaedic surgery resident, and 3 medical students, who identified topics that would come up perioperatively for individuals undergoing meniscus surgery. To ensure the most popular perioperative topics were included in this study, ChatGPT (OpenAI, 2025) was also used on April 15, 2025 in incognito mode by inputting “what are the most popular questions patients ask about meniscus surgery” and reviewing the top 300 responses for inclusion in this study. Incognito mode was used when searching on ChatGPT to remove any prior computational biases from prior ChatGPT searches, helping to standardize the responses that we obtained. After using those 2 methods, 50 meniscus surgery topics were included in this study. We obtained the mean RSV for the 50 topics, which were compared to determine the most popular ones.

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Inclusion criteria for this study comprised Google search topics regarding meniscus surgery which had enough search volume to have a mean RSV available through Google Trends from January 2010 to December 2024. Google search terms that did not have an RSV available for analysis, and search topics that were not related to meniscus surgery were excluded.

Study Outcomes

The primary study outcome was to compare the mean RSV between 5 meniscus surgery related search terms (meniscus surgery, meniscus repair, meniscectomy, meniscus transplant, and meniscus procedure). The secondary outcomes of this study were to compare the mean RSV for the 50 meniscus surgery perioperative topics to identify the ones with most public interest. Additionally, secondary study outcomes included obtaining the trend for the mean RSV over time for “meniscus surgery” and trending the mean RSV over time for the meniscus surgery topics with most public interest.

Statistical Analysis

When comparing the mean RSV between 5 search terms regarding meniscus surgery, when comparing the mean RSV for a specific search term over 15 years, or when comparing the mean RSVs for 50 different perioperative search terms, analysis of variance (ANOVA) tests were used, and the data was presented as mean and associated variance. To determine whether a trend existed over time for the mean RSV of a search term, the Pearson correlation coefficient and its corresponding P value were obtained. The Pearson correlation coefficients were interpreted in accordance with published literature, with coefficients higher than 0.90 representing very strong correlation, and coefficients ranging from 0.70 to 0.89 representing strong correlation. 14 Statistical significance was defined as a P value of < .05. To perform the analyses in this study, Microsoft Excel (Version 16.72) Data Analysis Package was used.

RESULTS

Evaluating the Popularity of Meniscus Surgery Over Time

From 2010 to 2024, between the 5 search terms (meniscus surgery, meniscus repair, meniscectomy, meniscus transplant, and meniscus procedure), “meniscus surgery” had the highest mean RSV (68 vs 21 vs 16 vs 1 vs 1; P < .001) (Table 1). Furthermore, from 2010 to 2024, the mean RSV for “meniscus surgery” significantly increased over time (2010: 40; 2024: 89; P < .001) (Figure 1), and the mean RSV for “meniscus surgery” was noted to increase significantly after a substantial decline in 2020. The Pearson correlation coefficient over time for “meniscus surgery” was 0.952, with a P value of <.001.

Evaluating Specific Meniscus Surgery Related Topics

Among the 50 meniscus surgery related topics that may arise perioperatively, “meniscus surgery recovery” had a significantly higher mean RSV than other terms, while “meniscus surgery pain”, “arthroscopic meniscus surgery”, “meniscus surgery cost”, “how long does meniscus surgery take”, “meniscus surgery therapy”, “meniscus surgery brace”, “meniscus surgery rehab”, “meniscus surgery swelling”, and “meniscus surgery arthritis” were also among the 10 meniscus surgery related topics with the highest mean RSV (60 vs 25 vs 15 vs 9 vs 9 vs 6 vs 6 vs 5 vs 4 vs 2; P < .001) (Table 2; Figure 4).

Evaluating Interest in Meniscus Surgery Topics Over Time

The 2 most popular meniscus surgery related topics had a significant increase in mean RSV over time: “meniscus surgery recovery” (2010: 48; 2024: 76; P < .001) (Figure 5) and “meniscus surgery pain” (2010: 10; 2024: 33; P < .001) (Figure 6). The Pearson correlation coefficient over time for “meniscus surgery recovery” was 0.574 with a P value of .025, while the Pearson correlation coefficient over time for “meniscus surgery pain” was 0.918 with a P value of <.001.

DISCUSSION

This study found that there was increasing public interest in meniscus surgery during the last 15 years, and that patients have varying interest in topics about meniscus surgery including recovery, pain, surgical length, cost, postoperative therapy, braces, and post‐traumatic arthritis. Additionally, among the 2 most popular topics about meniscus surgery, recovery and pain, public interest in those 2 topics increased significantly during the last 15 years.

Prior studies have been performed using Google Trends to evaluate the temporal trend of public interest in a variety of orthopaedic procedures, such as anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction, 15 , 16 Achilles tendon repair, 17 and robotic total joint arthroplasty, 18 finding increasing public interest in those procedures over time. Our study evaluated public interest in meniscus surgery using Google Trends and found there is increasing public interest in meniscus surgery, which is analogous to the findings of prior studies on other orthopaedic procedures.

When specifically evaluating the types of meniscus surgery, our study found that the increase in mean RSV for “meniscus repair” was significantly greater than the increase in mean RSV for “meniscectomy” during the last 15 years. This may highlight the changing orthopaedic landscape regarding meniscus injuries, whereby surgeons may be aiming to preserve and repair the meniscus rather than perform a meniscectomy. 19 Multiple publications highlight an increasing number of surgeons advocating for meniscus repair, owing to the risk of changing knee biomechanics with meniscectomy and possible chondral damage after meniscectomy. 20 , 21

Additionally, prior studies have been conducted using Google to evaluate the most asked questions regarding sports‐medicine procedures such as anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction, 22 and other orthopaedic procedures such as total joint arthroplasty. 23 Similar to the findings of our study, they found that many of the most popular questions were related to pain and recovery after surgery. Additionally, prior studies used Google search algorithms to identify the most asked associated questions about those topics, with questions appearing the most frequently being highlighted as the most popular, with no direct objective comparison in public search volume between the various questions. Using Google Trends allows our study to directly compare public search volume between different questions, allowing us to determine which questions have the highest search volume.

The questions with the highest search volumes related to meniscus surgery were “recovery” and “pain”, and these topics were increasingly searched as time progressed. These findings underscore 2 of the most pressing concerns patients have in the perioperative period and align with existing literature on the impact of patient expectations. 24 Additionally, Vimala et al. evaluated factors influencing anxiety in orthopaedic patients, and found that postoperative pain was the highest factor influencing anxiety, followed by the surgery itself and the outcome of the surgery. 25 The prominence of “recovery” and “pain” in online search behavior may serve as a proxy for patient expectations and highlight areas where patients seek clarity before undergoing surgery. These insights present an opportunity for surgeons to proactively address these topics during consultations to enhance outcomes. Orthopaedic surgeons can review how multimodal pain regimens are used perioperatively to help improve postoperative outcomes, 26 and they can also appropriately set preoperative expectations to help facilitate optimal outcomes. 27

With the growing reliance on digital resources for health information, 28 online search behavior has become a valuable indicator of patient concerns and informational gaps. Patient concerns are ever changing and using tools like Google Trends enables physicians to track what topics are most valuable to address during preoperative counseling. Dawod et al. showed that structured, checklist‐based preoperative counseling significantly reduces postoperative pain and improves patient satisfaction. 29 Additionally, a systematic review by Dekker et al. showed that web‐based resources were a time and cost‐effective method to deliver information to patients. 30 Despite these findings, studies have found that the readability of online resources about meniscus tears and arthroscopy are frequently written above the average American's reading level, and below levels recommended by the American Medical Association. 31 , 32 These findings stress the need for orthopaedic surgeons to continue to develop more patient‐facing online educational resources that are accessible and free from advanced medical jargon to promote patient‐centered education.

Limitations

This study is not without limitations. The results of this study are based on individuals who have access to the internet and use Google as their search engine. Due to this, we are unable to assess public interest in topics related to meniscus surgery for individuals who use other resources to obtain information about meniscus surgery. Moreover, while the searches did not have any demographic variables associated with them for analysis, they were likely conducted by a younger population from high‐income countries with high internet penetration, and may be subject to sampling bias. Lastly, confounders can cause spikes or drops in the search volume, without necessarily relating to the organic interest in the procedure. For instance, there was a sharp drop in interest in meniscus surgery during the COVID‐19 pandemic due to surgical volume for elective orthopaedic procedures being reduced. 4 Alternatively, when star athletes get injured, it may cause temporary increases in public interest for those injuries and procedures associated with those injuries. 33 However, due to the large timespan included in this study, this likely does not significantly impact the findings of this study.

CONCLUSIONS

Patient interest in meniscus surgery has increased significantly during the past 15 years, and rebounded after the COVID‐19 pandemic. Patients have interest in many topics regarding meniscus surgery, including recovery, pain, surgical length, cost, postoperative therapy, braces, and post‐traumatic arthritis.

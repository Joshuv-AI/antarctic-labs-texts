1.
Introduction

This paper contributes to the study of the information the upper and lower metric mean dimension with potential of multifractal irregular set carry.

In this paper, we focus on the following framework.
Let
(X,d,f)
be a topological dynamical system(abbr,
\rm{TDS}
), i.e. a compact space
(X,d)
and a continuous transformation
f:X\to X
. For any continuous function
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{R}
, the space
X
has a natural multifractal decomposition

X=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}K_{\alpha}\cup I_{\varphi}

where

\displaystyle K_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in X:\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\varphi(f^{i}x)=\alpha\right\},

\displaystyle I_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in X:\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\varphi(f^{i}x)\ \text{does not exist}\right\}.

In this paper, we take
I_{\varphi}
as our research object.
I_{\varphi}
was called irregular set, the set of divergence points or historic set by different researchers. In the early stages,
I_{\varphi}
has been considered of little interest in dynamical systems and geometric measure theory. However, the works of
[
CX99
,
FF00
,
FFW01
,
FLW02
]
have changed such attitudes. For a general dynamical system with the specification property, Chen, Küpper and Shu
[
CKS05
]
proved that the irregular set is either empty or carries full topological entropy. In 2010, Thompson
[
TH10
]
extended it to that the irregular set is either empty or carries full topological pressure for maps with the specification property.
Meanwhile, the irregular set
I_{\varphi}
is either empty or carrying full Bowen topological in systems with the almost specification property
[
TH12
]
and in systems with shadowing property
[
DOT18
]
.
Recently, using (metric) mean dimension to describe the complexity of infinite entropy systems has attracted people’s research interest. In 2021, Lima and Varandas
[
Lv21
]
proved that for a continuous map with the gluing orbit property on a compact metric space
I_{\varphi}
is either an empty set or carries full topological pressure and full metric mean dimension.
Liu and Liu
[
LL24
]
gave a contribution to the study of the metric mean dimension by proving that
I_{\varphi}
of a system with the weak specification property is empty or its Bowen (upper and lower) metric mean dimension coincides with the classical (upper and lower) metric mean dimension of
(X,d,f)
.
For a dynamical system with the shadowing property, Foryś-Krawiec and Oprocha give a similar result
[
FO24
]
.
Recently, Tsukamoto introduced the notion of upper mean dimension with potential
[
TSU20
]
. Cheng, Li and Selmi
[
CLS21
]
introduced the Bowen upper metric mean dimension with potential. This prompts us to use these quantities to measure the complexity of
I_{\varphi}
.

Now, we state our main result as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X,d,f)
be a
\mathrm{TDS}
satisfying the specification property,
I_{\varphi}
be the irreugular set of
\varphi
and
\psi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
. If
\overline{\rm{mdim}}_{M}(f,X,d,\psi)<\infty
(resp.
\underline{\rm{mdim}}_{M}(X,f,\psi)<\infty
), then either
I_{\varphi}=\emptyset
or

\displaystyle\overline{\rm{mdim}}_{M}^{B}(f,I_{\varphi},d,\psi)=\overline{\rm{mdim}}_{M}(f,X,d,\psi),

\displaystyle\underline{\rm{mdim}}_{M}^{B}(f,I_{\varphi},d,\psi)=\underline{\rm{mdim}}_{M}(f,X,d,\psi).

2.
Preliminary

In this section, we give some auxiliary quantities and introduce some lemmas that we will use in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
(X,d,f)
be a topological dynamical system (abbr.
\mathrm{TDS}
), i.e., a compact metric space
(X,d)
with a continuous transformation
f:X\to X
.
Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
x,y\in X
, the
n
-th Bowen metric
d_{n}
on
X
is defined by

d_{n}(x,y):=\max_{0\leq j\leq n-1}\limits d(f^{j}(x),f^{j}(y)).

Then
Bowen open ball
of radius
\epsilon
and order
n
in the metric
d_{n}
around
x
is given by

B_{n}(x,\epsilon)=\{y\in X:d_{n}(x,y)<\epsilon\}.

Therefore, we say that
E
is an
(n,\epsilon)
spanning set of
Z\subset X
if for any
y\in Z
there exist
x\in E
such that
d_{n}(x,y)\leq\epsilon
and
F
is an
(n,\epsilon)
-separated set of
Z\subset X
if for any
x\neq y\in F
,
d_{n}(x,y)>\epsilon.

Denote
\#A
to be the cardinality of set
A
,
\partial A
to be the boundary of set
A
and
C(X,\mathbb{R})
to be the set of all continuous functions.
Denote
\mathcal{M}_{f}(X)
to be the set that is consisted of all
f
-invariant Borel probability measures on
X
and denote
\mathcal{M}_{f}^{e}(X)
to be the set that is consisted of all
f
-ergodic invariant Borel probability measures on
X.

Let
S_{n}\psi(x):=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\psi(f^{i}x)
and

Var(\varphi,\epsilon):=\max\{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|:d(x,y)<\epsilon\}
.

2.1.
Specification Property

Let
(X,d,f)
be a
\mathrm{TDS}
.
f
satisfies the specification property means that for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists an integer
m=m(\epsilon)
such that for any finite integer intervals
\{[a_{j},b_{j}]_{j=1}^{k}\}
with
a_{j+1}-b_{j}\geq m
for
j\in\{1,\dots,k-1\}
and any
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
in
X
, there exists a point
x\in X
such that

d_{b_{j}-a_{j}}(f^{a_{j}}x,x_{j})<\epsilon\text{ for all }\ j=1,\dots,k.

2.2.
Bowen metric mean dimension with potential for subsets

Given a set
Z\subset X,N\in\mathbb{N},0<\epsilon<1,s\in\mathbb{R},
and a potential
\psi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
, we consider

m_{N,\epsilon}(f,Z,s,d,\psi)=\inf\left\{\sum_{i\in I}\exp\left(-sn_{i}+S_{n_{i}}\psi(x_{i})\cdot\Big(\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Big)\right)\right\},

where the infimum is taken over all finite or countable covers
\{B_{n_{i}}(x_{i},\epsilon)\}_{i\in I}
of
Z
with
n_{i}\geq N
. Obviously, the limit

m_{\epsilon}(f,Z,s,d,\psi)=\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}m_{N,\epsilon}(f,Z,s,d,\psi)

exists since
m_{N,\epsilon}(f,Z,s,d,\psi)
is non-increasing when
N
increases.

m_{\epsilon}(f,Z,s,d,\psi)
has a critical value of parameter
s
jumping from
\infty
to
0
and which is defined by

\displaystyle M_{\epsilon}(f,Z,d,\psi)

\displaystyle:=\inf\{s:m_{\epsilon}(f,Z,s,d,\psi)=0\}

\displaystyle:=\sup\{s:m_{\epsilon}(f,Z,s,d,\psi)=\infty\}.

The Bowen upper metric mean dimension of
f
on
Z
with potential
\psi
is then defined as the following limit.

\overline{\rm{midm}}_{M}^{B}(f,Z,d,\psi)=\limsup\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{M_{\epsilon}(f,Z,d,\psi)}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}.

Similarly, the Bowen lower metric mean dimension of
f
on
Z
with potential
\psi
is defined as

\underline{\rm{midm}}_{M}^{B}(f,Z,d,\psi)=\liminf\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{M_{\epsilon}(f,Z,d,\psi)}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}.

When
\psi=0
and
Z=X
,
\overline{\rm{midm}}_{M}^{B}(f,X,d,\psi)
is equal to the usual metric mean dimension introduced by Lindenstrauss and Weiss
[
LW00
]
.

2.3.
Metric mean dimension with potential for subsets

Given a set
Z\subset X,n\in\mathbb{N},0<\epsilon<1
and
\psi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
. We set

\displaystyle Q_{n}(Z,\psi,\epsilon):=\inf\left\{\sum_{x\in E}\exp\left\{\left(\log\dfrac{1}{\epsilon}\right)S_{n}\psi(x)\right\}:E\ \text{is an}\ (n,\epsilon)\ \text{spanning set for}\ Z\right\},

\displaystyle P_{n}(Z,\psi,\epsilon):=\sup\left\{\sum_{x\in E}\exp\left\{\left(\log\dfrac{1}{\epsilon}\right)S_{n}\psi(x)\right\}:E\ \text{is an}\ (n,\epsilon)\ \text{separated set for}\ Z\right\}.

The upper(lower) metric mean dimension of
f
on
Z
with potential
\psi
are then defined as the following limits.

\overline{\rm{midm}}_{M}(f,Z,d,\psi):=\limsup\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log Q_{n}(Z,\psi,\epsilon)}{n},

\underline{\rm{midm}}_{M}(f,Z,d,\psi):=\liminf\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log Q_{n}(Z,\psi,\epsilon)}{n}.

The upper(lower) metric mean dimension of
f
on
Z
with
\psi
can also be defined via separated sets by replacing
Q_{n}
by
P_{n}

[
YCZ22
]
.
Now, we give some properties for upper (lower) metric mean dimension of
f
on
Z
with potential
\psi
.

Proposition 2.1
.

For
\psi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
,
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
Z\subset X
, we have

\overline{\rm{midm}}_{M}(f,Z,d,\psi+c)=\overline{\rm{midm}}_{M}(f,Z,d,\psi)+c.

Proof.

Obviously,
S_{n}(\psi+c)(x)=S_{n}\psi(x)+nc
. Then,
Q_{n}(Z,\psi+c,\epsilon)=Q_{n}(Z,\psi,\epsilon)\cdot\exp\{nc(\log\frac{1}{\epsilon})\}
. Immediately,
\overline{\rm{midm}}_{M}(f,Z,d,\psi+c)=\overline{\rm{midm}}_{M}(f,Z,d,\psi)+c.

∎

For any

X

such that
\psi+c>0
. Combing Proposition
2.1

Let

Define the upper and lower Katok’s entropy of

\displaystyle\overline{h}_{\mu}(f,\delta,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log N_{n}^{\mu}(\delta,\epsilon),

\displaystyle\underline{h}_{\mu}(f,\delta,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log N_{n}^{\mu}(\delta,\epsilon).

where

3.
Proof of main result

In this section, we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We now only consider the situation of upper metric mean dimension with potential. The result of lower metric mean dimension with potential can be proved in the similar way. We may assume that
I_{\varphi}\neq\emptyset
and show that

\overline{\rm{mdim}}_{M}^{B}(f,I_{\varphi},d,\psi)=\overline{\rm{mdim}}_{M}(f,X,d,\psi).

Since
I_{\varphi}\subset X
, we have

\overline{\rm{mdim}}_{M}^{B}(f,I_{\varphi},d,\psi)\leq\overline{\rm{mdim}}_{M}^{B}(f,X,d,\psi).

[
YCZ22
, Proposition 3.4]
shows that

\overline{\rm{mdim}}_{M}(f,X,d,\psi)=\overline{\rm{mdim}}_{M}^{B}(f,X,d,\psi).

This implies

\overline{\rm{mdim}}_{M}^{B}(f,I_{\varphi},d,\psi)\leq\overline{\rm{mdim}}_{M}(f,X,d,\psi).

Thus, we only need to show

\overline{\rm{mdim}}_{M}^{B}(f,I_{\varphi},d,\psi)\geq\overline{\rm{mdim}}_{M}(f,X,d,\psi).

Let
S:=\overline{\rm{mdim}}_{M}(f,X,d,\psi)<\infty
. Without losing generality, we only need to consider the case that
S>0
.

.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
\overline{\rm{mdim}}_{M}(f,X,d,\psi)<\infty

\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{f}(X)

\epsilon>0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{P_{i}\cap\mathcal{M}_{f}^{e}(X)}\left\{\inf_{\xi\succ\mathcal{U}}h_{\tau}(f,\xi)+\left(\int\psi(x)\mathrm{d}{\tau(x)}\right)\log{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\right\}\mathrm{d}{\hat{\mu}(\tau)}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\left\{\inf_{\xi\succ\mathcal{U}}h_{\nu_{i}}(f,\xi)+\left(\int\psi(x)\mathrm{d}{\nu_{i}}\right)\log{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\right\}+\delta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\inf_{\xi\succ\mathcal{U}}h_{\nu}(f,\xi)+\Big(\log{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\Big)\int\psi\mathrm{d}{\nu}+\delta.

∎

3.1.
Construction of the Moran-like Fractal

The construction of the Moran-like Fractal is a standard process that inspired by Thompson
[
TH10
]
and Backes
[
BR23
]
. By
[
SHI22
, Lemma 6]
, there exists a finite open cover
\mathcal{U}
of
X
that satisfies

\text{diam}(\mathcal{U})\leq 5\epsilon_{0}\ ,\ \text{Leb}(\mathcal{U})\geq\frac{5\epsilon_{0}}{4}.

Fix
\delta\in(0,\gamma/2)
. By Lemma
3.3
, there exists
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{f}(X)
satisfying

\displaystyle(1)\ \nu=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\nu_{i},\ where\ \lambda_{i}>0,\ \sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}=1\ and\ \nu_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{f}^{e}(X);

\displaystyle(2)\ \inf_{\xi\succ\mathcal{U}}h_{\mu_{1}}(f,\xi)+\Big(\log{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\Big)\int\psi\mathrm{d}{\mu_{1}}\leq\inf_{\xi\succ\mathcal{U}}h_{\nu}(f,\xi)+\Big(\log{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\Big)\int\psi\mathrm{d}{\nu}+\delta;

\displaystyle(3)\ \left|\int\varphi\mathrm{d}{\nu}-\int\varphi\mathrm{d}{\mu_{1}}\right|<\delta.

Since
\nu_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{f}^{e}(X)
, there exist
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
large enough such that the set

Y_{i}(N_{0})=\left\{x\in X:\left|\frac{1}{n}S_{n}\varphi(x)-\int\varphi d{\nu_{i}}\right|<\delta,\forall\ n\geq N_{0}\right\}

has
\nu_{i}-
measure at least
1-\gamma
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
\epsilon_{0}
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, we can find a large enough
\hat{n}
and
\mathcal{S}_{1,i}
such that
\mathcal{S}_{1,i}
is an
\left([\lambda_{i}\hat{n}],\frac{5\epsilon_{0}}{4}\right)
separated set for
Y_{i}(N_{0})
and

\displaystyle M_{1,i}:

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{1,i}}\exp(S_{[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]}\psi(x)\cdot\log{\frac{1}{5\epsilon_{0}}})

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left\{[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]\left(\inf_{\xi\succ\mathcal{U}}h_{\nu_{i}}(f,\xi)+\Big(\log\frac{1}{5\epsilon_{0}}\Big)\int\psi d{\nu_{i}}-2\gamma\right)\right\}.

Furthermore,
[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]
can be chosen such that
[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]\geq N_{0}
and
\hat{n}\geq 2^{m}
where
m=m(\epsilon/16)
is as in the definition of the specification property and
\epsilon
will be determined later.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log{N_{n}^{\nu_{i}}}\left(\psi,\delta,\frac{5\epsilon_{0}}{4}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\underline{h}_{\nu_{i}}\left(f,\delta,\frac{5\epsilon_{0}}{4}\right)+\Big(\log\frac{1}{5\epsilon_{0}}\Big)\int\psi d{\nu_{i}}-\frac{\gamma}{2}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\xi\succ\mathcal{U}}h_{\nu_{i}}(f,\xi)+\Big(\log\frac{1}{5\epsilon_{0}}\Big)\int\psi d{\nu_{i}}-\frac{\gamma}{2}.

Since
Q_{n}(Z,\psi,\epsilon)\leq P_{n}(Z,\psi,\epsilon)
and
\nu_{i}(Y_{i}(N_{0}))>1-\gamma
, immediately, we have that

Q_{n}\left(Y_{i}(N_{0}),\psi,\frac{5\epsilon_{0}}{4}\right)\geq N_{n}^{\nu_{i}}\left(\psi,\delta,\frac{5\epsilon_{0}}{4}\right).

Let
M(1,n)=P_{n}(Y_{i}(N_{0}),\psi,\frac{5\epsilon_{0}}{4})
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log{M(1,n)}

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log{N_{n}^{\nu_{i}}\left(\psi,\delta,\frac{5\epsilon_{0}}{4}\right)}

\displaystyle\geq\underline{h}_{\nu_{i}}\left(f,\frac{5\epsilon_{0}}{4},\delta\right)+\Big(\log\frac{1}{5\epsilon_{0}}\Big)\int\psi d{\nu_{i}}-\frac{\gamma}{2}.

Thus, we can choose a sequence
[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]\to\infty
as
\hat{n}\to\infty
so that

\frac{1}{[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]}\log{M(1,n)}\geq\underline{h}_{\nu_{i}}\left(f,\frac{5\epsilon_{0}}{4},\delta\right)+\Big(\log\frac{1}{5\epsilon_{0}}\Big)\int\psi d{\nu_{i}}-\gamma.

Taking
\mathcal{S}_{i}
be a choice of
\left([\lambda_{i}\hat{n}],\frac{5\epsilon_{0}}{4}\right)
separated set for
Y_{i}(N_{0})
that satisfies

\frac{1}{[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]}\log{\sum_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}\exp\left(S_{[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]}(x)\log{\frac{1}{5\epsilon_{0}}}\right)}\geq\frac{1}{[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]}\log{M(1,n)}-\gamma.

Then, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]}\log{M_{1,i}}\geq\underline{h}_{\nu_{i}}\left(f,\frac{5\epsilon_{0}}{4},\delta\right)+\Big(\log\frac{1}{5\epsilon_{0}}\Big)\int\psi d{\nu_{i}}-2\gamma,

\displaystyle M_{1,i}\geq\exp\left\{[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]\left(\inf_{\xi\succ\mathcal{U}}h_{\nu_{i}}(f,\xi)+\Big(\log\frac{1}{5\epsilon_{0}}\Big)\int\psi d{\nu_{i}^{k}}-2\gamma\right)\right\}.

∎

We choose
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
satisfying
Var(\psi,\epsilon)<\gamma
, and fix all the ingredients provided by Lemma
3.4
. For every
y_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{i}
, by the specification property, there exists
x=x(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})\in X
that satisfies

d_{[\lambda_{l}\hat{n}]}(y_{l},f^{a_{l}}x)<\frac{\epsilon}{16}

for
l\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, where
a_{1}=0
and
a_{l}=\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]+(l-1)m
for
l\in\{2,\dots,k\}.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{1}
be the set that consist of such
x=x(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})
. Define
n_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]+(k-1)m
, and we have that
\frac{n_{1}}{\hat{n}}\to 1
as
\hat{n}\to\infty
.
We claim that
\mathcal{S}_{1}
is a
(n_{1},9\epsilon_{0}/8)
-separated set and if
(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})\neq(y_{1}^{\prime},\dots,y_{k}^{\prime})
then
x=x(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})\neq x^{\prime}=x^{\prime}(y_{1}^{\prime},\dots,y_{k}^{\prime})
. Actually, if
y_{l}\neq y_{l}^{\prime}
for some
l\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{5\epsilon_{0}}{4}

\displaystyle<d_{[\lambda_{l}\hat{n}]}(y_{l},y_{l}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq d_{[\lambda_{l}\hat{n}]}(y_{l},f^{a_{l}}x)+d_{[\lambda_{l}\hat{n}]}(y_{l}^{\prime},f^{a_{l}}x^{\prime})+d_{[\lambda_{l}\hat{n}]}(f^{a_{l}}x,f^{a_{l}}x^{\prime})

\displaystyle<2\frac{\epsilon}{16}+d_{[\lambda_{l}\hat{n}_{k}]}(f^{a_{l}}x,f^{a_{l}}x^{\prime}).

Thus,

d_{n_{k}}(x,x^{\prime})\geq d_{[\lambda_{l}\hat{n}]}(f^{a_{l}}x,f^{a_{l}}x^{\prime})>\frac{5\epsilon_{0}}{4}-\frac{\epsilon}{8}\geq\frac{9\epsilon_{0}}{8}.

We define
M_{1}:=M_{1,1}\cdots M_{1,k}.

Lemma 3.5
.

For
\hat{n}
sufficiently large such that

k\leq\frac{\delta\hat{n}}{4\|\varphi\|+1}\ \text{and}\ \frac{2(k-1)m\|\varphi\|}{n}<\frac{\delta}{2},

we have

\displaystyle(1)\ M_{1}\geq\exp\left\{n_{1}(\inf_{diam\xi<5\epsilon_{0}}h_{\mu_{1}}(f,\xi)+\Big(\log{\frac{1}{5\epsilon_{0}}}\Big)\int\psi d{\mu_{1}}-5\gamma)\right\},

\displaystyle(2)\ if\ x\in\mathcal{S}_{1},\ \left|\frac{1}{n_{1}}S_{n_{1}}\varphi(x)-\alpha_{1}\right|<4\delta.

Proof.

(1): For sufficiently large
\hat{n}
, according to Lemma
3.4
, we have that

\displaystyle M_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]\left(\inf_{\xi\succ\mathcal{U}}h_{\nu_{i}}(f,\xi)+\Big(\log{\frac{1}{5\epsilon_{0}}}\Big)\int\psi\mathrm{d}{\nu_{i}}-2\gamma\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]}{\lambda_{i}}\left(\lambda_{i}\inf_{\xi\succ\mathcal{U}}h_{\nu_{i}}(f,\xi)+\lambda_{i}\Big(\log{\frac{1}{5\epsilon_{0}}}\Big)\int\psi\mathrm{d}{\nu_{i}}-2\lambda_{i}\gamma\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\hat{n}\left(\lambda_{i}\inf_{\xi\succ\mathcal{U}}h_{\nu_{i}}(f,\xi)+\lambda_{i}\Big(\log{\frac{1}{5\epsilon_{0}}}\Big)\int\psi\mathrm{d}{\nu_{i}}-3\lambda_{i}\gamma\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left\{\hat{n}\left(\inf_{\xi\succ\mathcal{U}}h_{\mu_{1}}(f,\xi)+\Big(\log{\frac{1}{5\epsilon_{0}}}\Big)\int\psi\mathrm{d}{\mu}-3\gamma-\delta\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left\{\hat{n}\left(\inf_{\xi\succ\mathcal{U}}h_{\mu_{1}}(f,\xi)+\Big(\log{\frac{1}{5\epsilon_{0}}}\Big)\int\psi\mathrm{d}{\mu}-\frac{7}{2}\gamma\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{\frac{\hat{n}}{n_{1}}\left(n_{1}\left(\inf_{\xi\succ\mathcal{U}}h_{\mu}(f,\xi)+\Big(\log{\frac{1}{5\epsilon_{0}}}\Big)\int\psi\mathrm{d}{\mu}-\frac{7}{2}\gamma\right)\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left\{{n_{1}}\left(\inf_{\xi\succ\mathcal{U}}h_{\mu_{1}}(f,\xi)+\Big(\log{\frac{1}{5\epsilon_{0}}}\Big)\int\psi\mathrm{d}{\mu_{1}}-4\gamma\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left\{{n_{1}}\left(\inf_{diam\xi<5\epsilon_{0}}h_{\mu_{1}}(f,\xi)+\Big(\log{\frac{1}{5\epsilon_{0}}}\Big)\int\psi\mathrm{d}{\mu_{1}}-4\gamma\right)\right\}.

\displaystyle(2):

\displaystyle\left|S_{n_{1}}\varphi(x)-n_{1}\alpha_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\left|S_{[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]}\varphi(f^{a_{i}}x)-S_{[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]}\varphi(y_{i})\right|+\left|S_{[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]}\varphi(y_{i})-[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]\int\varphi\mathrm{d}{\nu_{i}}\right|\right)

\displaystyle+\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left([\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]\int\varphi\mathrm{d}{\nu_{i}}\right)-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]\right)\alpha_{1}\right|+2(k-1)m\|\varphi\|,

where we use the fact that
|\alpha_{1}|\leq\|\varphi\|.
We have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left|S_{[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]}\varphi(f^{a_{i}}x)-S_{[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]}\varphi(y_{i})\right|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]Var(\varphi,\frac{\epsilon}{16})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]\delta,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left|S_{[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]}\varphi(y_{i})-[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]\int\varphi\mathrm{d}{\nu_{i}}\right|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]\delta,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left([\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]\int\varphi\mathrm{d}{\nu_{i}}\right)-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]\right)\alpha\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\hat{n}\int\varphi\mathrm{d}{\nu}-\hat{n}\alpha_{1}\right|+2(\hat{n}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}])\|\varphi\|\leq\hat{n}\delta+2k\|\varphi\|

\displaystyle\leq\hat{n}\delta+\frac{\delta\hat{n}}{2}\leq\frac{3\delta\hat{n}}{2}.

Thus,

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i=1}^{k}[\lambda_{i}\hat{n}]\delta+2(k-1)m\|\varphi\|+\frac{3\delta\hat{n}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq n_{1}(2\delta+\frac{3\delta}{2})+\frac{\delta n_{1}}{2}=4n_{1}\delta.

Combining these inequalities , we have that
\left|\frac{1}{n_{1}}S_{n_{1}}\varphi(x)-\alpha_{1}\right|<4\delta.

∎

Now, we begin to construct the Moran-like fractal. Let
\rho:\mathbb{N}\to\{1,2\}
and
\rho(k)=(k+1(\text{mod}\ 2))+1.
Then let
\{\delta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a strictly decreasing sequence with the property that
\delta_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
and
\delta_{1}<\gamma/2.

Let
m_{k}=m(\epsilon_{0}/2^{k+5})
be the gap in the definition of specification property. Then, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, resembling the Lemma
3.5
, exists
n_{k}>2^{m_{k}}
and the set
\mathcal{S}_{k}
such that

M_{k}\geq\exp\left\{n_{k}(\inf_{diam\xi<5\epsilon_{0}}h_{\mu}(f,\xi)+\Big(\log{\frac{1}{5\epsilon_{0}}}\Big)\int\psi\mathrm{d}{\mu_{\rho(k)}}-5\gamma)\right\}

and for
x\in\mathcal{S}_{k}

\left|\frac{1}{n_{k}}S_{n_{k}}\varphi(x)-\alpha_{\rho(k)}\right|<4\delta_{k}.

We choose a sequence of positive integers
\{N_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
N_{1}=1
and

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{n_{k+1}+m_{k+1}}{N_{k}}=0,\ \lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{N_{1}(n_{1}+m_{1})+\dots+N_{k}(n_{k+1}+m_{k+1})}{N_{k+1}}=0.

\mathbf{Step\ 1.}
Constructions of intermediate sets
\{\mathcal{C}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}.

For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathcal{S}_{k}:=\{x_{i}^{k}:i=1,\dots,\#\mathcal{S}_{k}\}
, we consider
\underline{i}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{N_{k}})\in\{1,\dots,\#\mathcal{S}_{k}\}^{N_{k}}
. By the specification property, there exists a point
y:=y(i_{1},\dots,i_{N_{k}})
which satisfies

d_{n_{k}}(x_{i_{j}}^{k},f^{a_{j}}y)<\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+5}},\ \text{for}\ j\in\{1,\dots,N_{k}\},\ a_{j}=(j-1)(n_{k}+m_{k}).

We collect all the shadowing points into

\mathcal{C}_{k}=\{y(i_{1},\dots,i_{N_{k}})\in X:(i_{1},\dots,i_{N_{k}})\in\{1,\dots,\#\mathcal{S}_{k}\}^{N_{k}}\}.

Denote the amount of time for which the orbit of points in
\mathcal{C}_{k}
has been shadowed by
c_{k}=N_{k}n_{k}+(N_{k}-1)m_{k}
and we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\underline{i},\ \underline{j}
be the different words in
\{1,\dots,\#\mathcal{S}_{k}\}^{N_{k}}
. Then
y_{1}:=y(\underline{i})
and
y_{2}:=y(\underline{j})
are
(c_{k},\frac{17\epsilon_{0}}{16})
-separated points, i.e.
d_{c_{k}}(y_{1},y_{2})>{\frac{17\epsilon_{0}}{16}}
. As a consequence,
\#\mathcal{C}_{k}=(\#\mathcal{S}_{k})^{N_{k}}.

Proof.

Since
\underline{i}\neq\underline{j}
, there exists
l\in\{1,\dots,N_{k}\}
such that
i_{l}\neq j_{l}.
We have

\displaystyle d_{c_{k}}(y_{1},y_{2})

\displaystyle\geq d_{n_{k}}(f^{a_{l}}y_{1},f^{a_{l}}y_{2})

\displaystyle\geq d_{n_{k}}(x_{i_{l}}^{k},x_{j_{l}}^{k})-d_{n_{k}}(f^{a_{l}}y_{1},x_{i_{l}}^{k})-d_{n_{k}}(f^{a_{l}}y_{2},x_{j_{l}}^{k})

\displaystyle>\frac{9\epsilon_{0}}{8}-\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{(k+5)}}-\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{(k+5)}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{17\epsilon_{0}}{16}.

∎

\mathbf{Step\ 2.}
Constructions of
\{\mathcal{T}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, the
k
-th level of the Moran-like fractal.

We define
\mathcal{T}_{k}
inductively. Let
\mathcal{T}_{1}=\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
t_{1}=c_{1}
. We construct
\mathcal{T}_{k+1}
from
\mathcal{T}_{k}
as follows. Let
t_{k+1}:=t_{k}+m_{k+1}+c_{k+1}
and
x\in\mathcal{T}_{k},\ y\in\mathcal{C}_{k+1}
. By the specification property, we can find a point
z:=z(x,y)
that satisfies

d_{t_{k}}(x,z)<\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{(k+6)}}\ and\ d_{c_{k+1}}(y,f^{t_{k}+m_{k+1}}z)<\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{(k+6)}}.

Define
\mathcal{T}_{k+1}=\{z(x,y):x\in\mathcal{T}_{k},\ y\in\mathcal{C}_{k+1}\}
, and note that
t_{k+1}
is the amount of time for which the orbits of points in
\mathcal{T}_{k}
has been shadowed. Similarly, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.7
.

For every
x\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
and distinct points
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathcal{C}_{k+1}
,

d_{t_{k}}(z(x,y_{1}),z(x,y_{2}))<\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+5}},\ d_{t_{k+1}}(z(x,y_{1}),z(x,y_{2}))>\frac{33\epsilon_{0}}{32}.

Thus,
\mathcal{T}_{k}
is a
\left(t_{k},\frac{33\epsilon_{0}}{32}\right)
-separated set. In particular, if
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
, then

\overline{B}_{t_{k}}\left(z_{1},\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+5}}\right)\cap\overline{B}_{t_{k}}\left(z_{2},\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+5}}\right)=\emptyset.

Proof.

Let
z_{1}=z(x,y_{1}),\ z_{2}=(x,y_{2})
. Hence, we have

d_{t_{k}}(z_{1},z_{2})\leq d_{t_{k}}(z_{1},x)+d_{t_{k}}(z_{2},x)<\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{(k+6)}}+\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{(k+6)}}=\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{(k+5)}},

\displaystyle d_{t_{k+1}}(z_{1},z_{1})

\displaystyle\geq d_{c_{k+1}}(f^{t_{k}+m_{k+1}}z_{1},f^{t_{k}+m_{k+1}}z_{2})

\displaystyle>\frac{17\epsilon_{0}}{16}-\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{(k+6)}}-\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{(k+6)}}\geq\frac{33\epsilon_{0}}{32}.

The third statement is a straight consequence of the second inequality.
∎

As a direct result of the Lemma
3.7
, we have

\#\mathcal{T}_{k}=\#\mathcal{T}_{k-1}\#\mathcal{C}_{k}=\#\mathcal{C}_{1}\dots\#\mathcal{C}_{k}=\#{\mathcal{S}_{1}}^{N_{1}}\dots\#{\mathcal{S}_{k}}^{N_{k}}.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
z=z(x,y)\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
, then we have

\overline{B}_{t_{k+1}}\left(z,\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+6}}\right)\subset\overline{B}_{t_{k}}\left(x,\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+5}}\right).

Proof.

From the construction of
\mathcal{T}_{k}
,
d_{t_{k}}(z,x)<\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+6}}.
Thus, for any point
p\in\overline{B}_{t_{k+1}}(z,\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+6}})
, one has

d_{t_{k}}(p,x)\leq d_{t_{k}}(p,z)+d_{t_{k}}(z,x)\leq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+6}}\cdot 2\leq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+5}},

that implies
p\in\overline{B}_{t_{k}}(x,\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+5}})
. Therefore, the result has been proved.
∎

\mathbf{Step\ 3.}
Constructions of the Moran-like fractal and show that it is contained in
I_{\varphi}.

Let
F_{k}=\cup_{x\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}\overline{B}_{t_{k}}\left(x,\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+5}}\right)
. By Lemma
3.8
,
F_{k+1}\subset F_{k}
and we have a decreasing sequence of compact sets, the set
F=\cap_{k}F_{k}
is non-empty. Besides, every point
p\in F
can be uniquely represented by a sequence
\underline{p}=(\underline{p}_{1},\underline{p}_{2},\dots)
, where each
\underline{p}_{i}=(\underline{p}_{1}^{i},\dots,\underline{p}_{N_{i}}^{i})\in\{1,2,\dots,\#\mathcal{S}_{i}\}^{N_{i}}.
Thus, every point in
\mathcal{T}_{k}
can be uniquely represented by a finite word
\underline{p}=(\underline{p}_{1},\dots,\underline{p}_{k}).

Lemma 3.9
.

Given
z=z(\underline{p}_{1},\dots,\underline{p}_{k})\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
, for all
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
and all
l\in\{1,\dots,N_{i}\}
we have that

d_{n_{i}}(x_{p_{l}^{i}}^{i},f^{t_{i-1}+m_{i}+(l-1)(m_{i}+n_{i})}z)<\epsilon_{0}.

Proof.

Given
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
and
l\in\{1,\dots,N_{i}\}.
For
m\in\{1,\dots,k-1\}
, let
z_{m}=z(\underline{p}_{1},\dots,\underline{p}_{m})\in\mathcal{T}_{m}
. Let
a=t_{i-1}+m_{i},\ b=(l-1)(m_{i}+n_{i})
. Then,

\displaystyle d_{n_{i}}(x_{p_{l}^{i}}^{i},f^{a+b}z)

\displaystyle\leq d_{n_{i}}(x_{p_{l}^{i}}^{i},f^{b}y{\underline{p}_{i}})+d_{n_{i}}(f^{b}y_{\underline{p}_{i}}^{i},f^{a+b}z)+d_{n_{i}}(f^{a+b}z_{i},f^{a+b}z)

\displaystyle<\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{i+5}}+d_{c_{i}}(y_{\underline{p}_{i}}^{i},f^{a}z)+d_{t_{i}}(z_{i},z)

\displaystyle<\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{i+5}}+\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{i+6}}+d_{t_{i}}(z_{i},z_{i+1})+\cdots+d_{t_{i}}(z_{k-1},z_{k})

\displaystyle<\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{i+5}}+\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{i+6}}+\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{i+6}}+\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{i+7}}+\cdots+\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+5}}

\displaystyle<\sum_{m=1}^{k}\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{m+5}}+\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{i+6}}<\epsilon_{0}.

∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Under the above conditions,
F\subset I_{\varphi},
which means that the sequence
\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\varphi(f^{i}x)\}
diverges.

Proof.

For any
x\in F
, we only need to show
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left|\frac{1}{t_{k}}S_{t_{k}}\varphi(x)-\alpha_{\rho(k)}\right|=0.
Thus, we need to estimate
\left|S_{t_{k}}\varphi(x)-t_{k}\alpha_{\rho(k)}\right|
. We can divide the estimation into 3 steps.

\mathbf{Step\ 1.}
Estimation on
\mathcal{C}_{k}
for
k\geq 1.

Supposing
y\in\mathcal{C}_{k}
, let us estimate
\left|\sum_{p=0}^{c_{k}-1}\varphi(f^{p}y)-c_{k}\alpha_{\rho(k)}\right|
. By the construction of
\mathcal{C}_{k}
, there exists
(i_{1},\dots,i_{N_{k}})\in(1,\dots,\#\mathcal{S}_{k})^{N_{k}}
and
x_{i_{j}}^{k}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
satisfying

d_{n_{k}}(x_{i_{j}}^{k},f^{a_{j}}y)<\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+5}}\ for\ j=1,\dots,N_{k}.

Since

\displaystyle[0,c_{k}-1]

\displaystyle=[0,N_{k}n_{k}+(N_{k}-1)m_{k}-1]

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{j=1}^{N_{k}}[a_{j},a_{j}+n_{k}-1]\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{N_{k}-1}[a_{j}+n_{k},a_{j}+n_{k}+m_{k}-1],

on
[a_{j},a_{j}+n_{k}-1]
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{p=0}^{n_{k}-1}\varphi(f^{a_{j}+p}y)-n_{k}\alpha_{\rho(k)}\right|\leq

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{p=0}^{n_{k}-1}\varphi(f^{a_{j}+p}y)-\sum_{p=0}^{n_{k}-1}\varphi(f^{p}x_{i_{j}}^{k})\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\sum_{p=0}^{n_{k}-1}\varphi(f^{p}x_{i_{j}}^{k})-n_{k}\alpha_{\rho(k)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq n_{k}Var\left(\varphi,\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+5}}\right)+4n_{k}\delta_{k};

on
[a_{j}+n_{k},a_{j}+n_{k}+m_{k}-1]
, we have

\left|\sum_{p=0}^{m_{k}-1}\varphi(f^{a_{j}+n_{k}+p}y)-m_{k}\alpha_{\rho(k)}\right|\leq m_{k}(\|\varphi\|+\alpha_{\rho(k)})\leq 2m_{k}\|\varphi\|.

Combining these inequalities, we have

\left|\sum_{p=0}^{c_{k}-1}\varphi(f^{p}y)-c_{k}\alpha_{\rho(k)}\right|\leq N_{k}n_{k}\left(Var\left(\varphi,\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+5}}\right)+4\delta_{k}\right)+2(N_{k}-1)m_{k}\|\varphi\|.

\mathbf{Step\ 2.}
Estimation on
\mathcal{T}_{k}
for
k\geq 2.

For
k\geq 2
, let us estimate

A_{k}:=\max_{x\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}\left|\sum_{p=0}^{t_{k}-1}\varphi(f^{p}z)-t_{k}\alpha_{\rho(k)}\right|

For any
z\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
, there exists
x\in\mathcal{T}_{k-1}
and
y\in\mathcal{C}_{k}
satisfying

d_{t_{k-1}}(x,z)<\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+5}},\ d_{c_{k}}(y,f^{t_{k}+m_{k-1}}z)<\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+5}}.

On
[0,t_{k-1}+m_{k}-1]
, we have
|\varphi-\alpha_{\rho(k+1)}|\leq 2\|\varphi\|
, while on
[t_{k-1}+m_{k},t_{k}-1]
we use the specification property and estimation on
\mathcal{C}_{k}
to obtain

\displaystyle A_{k}\leq

\displaystyle 2(t_{k-1}+m_{k})\|\varphi\|+c_{k}\cdot Var(\varphi,\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+5}})

\displaystyle+N_{k}n_{k}\left(Var\left(\varphi,\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+5}}\right)+4\delta_{k}\right)+2(N_{k}-1)m_{k}\|\varphi\|.

By the choice of
N_{k}\ and\ n_{k}
, wh have

\frac{t_{k-1}+m_{k}}{N_{k}}\to 0,\ \frac{(N_{k}-1)m_{k}}{N_{k}n_{k}}\to 0\ as\ k\to\infty.

Therefore, taking
k\to\infty
we have

\frac{A_{k}}{t_{k}}\leq\frac{2(t_{k-1}+m_{k})\|\varphi\|}{N_{k}}+2Var\left(\varphi,\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+5}}\right)+4\delta_{k}+\frac{2(N_{k}-1)m_{k}\|\varphi\|}{N_{k}n_{k}}\to 0.

\mathbf{Step\ 3.}
Estimation on
F
.

For any
x\in F
,
x\in F_{k}=\cup_{x\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}\overline{B}_{t_{k}}\left(x,\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+5}}\right)
, there exists
z_{k}(x)\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
such that
x\in\overline{B}_{t_{k}}\left(x,\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+5}}\right)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\left|S_{t_{k}}\varphi(x)-t_{k}\alpha_{\rho(k)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|S_{t_{k}}\varphi(x)-S_{t_{k}}\varphi(z_{k}(x))\right|+\left|S_{t_{k}}\varphi(z_{k}(x))-t_{k}\alpha_{\rho(k)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq t_{k}\cdot Var\left(\varphi,\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2^{k+5}}\right)+A_{k}.

Dividing the both by
t_{k}
and taking
k\to\infty
, we have

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left|\frac{1}{t_{k}}S_{t_{k}}\varphi(x)-\alpha_{\rho(k)}\right|=0.

The proof has been completed.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

\kappa_{k}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}M_{i}^{N_{i}}.

Proof.

We note that

\displaystyle\kappa_{k}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}\mathcal{L}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\underline{p}_{1}\in\{1,\dots,\#\mathcal{S}_{1}\}^{N_{1}}}\dots\sum_{\underline{p}_{k}\in\{1,\dots,\#\mathcal{S}_{k}\}^{N_{k}}}(\mathcal{L}(\underline{p}_{1})\cdots\mathcal{L}(\underline{p}_{1}))

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\underline{p}_{1}\in\{1,\dots,\#\mathcal{S}_{1}\}^{N_{1}}}\mathcal{L}(\underline{p}_{1})\right)\cdots\left(\sum_{\underline{p}_{1}\in\{1,\dots,\#\mathcal{S}_{k}\}^{N_{k}}}\mathcal{L}(\underline{p}_{k})\right).

From the definition of
\mathcal{L}(\underline{p}_{i})

\left(\sum_{\underline{p}_{i}\in\{1,\dots,\#\mathcal{S}_{i}\}^{N_{i}}}\mathcal{L}(\underline{p}_{1})\right)=\prod_{l=1}^{N_{i}}\left\{\sum_{p^{i}_{l}=1}^{\#\mathcal{S}_{i}}\exp\left\{S_{n_{i}}\psi(x_{p_{l}^{i}}^{i})\log{\frac{1}{5\epsilon_{0}}}\right\}\right\}=M_{i}^{N_{i}}.

Proposition 3.18
.

Let

\epsilon>0

for every

such that
B_{n}\left(x,\epsilon/2\right)\cap Z\neq\emptyset
and
n\geq N
. Furthermore, assume that at least one accumulate point
\nu
of
\mu_{k}
satisfies
\nu(Z)>0
. Then
M_{\epsilon/2}\left(f,Z,d,\psi\right)\geq s.

Proof.

Let
\nu
and
\epsilon>0
satisfy the conditions, and
\mu_{k_{j}}
be the sequence of measures which converges to
\nu
. Let
\Gamma=\{B_{n_{i}}(x_{i},\epsilon/2)\}_{i\in I}

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\exp\left\{-sn_{i}+S_{n_{i}}\psi(x)\log{\frac{2}{\epsilon}}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\in I}\exp\left\{-sn_{i}+S_{n_{i}}\psi(x)\log{\frac{1}{5\epsilon}}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{C}\sum_{i\in I}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mu_{k}\left(B_{n_{i}}(x_{i},\epsilon)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{C}\sum_{i\in I}\liminf_{j\to\infty}\mu_{k_{j}}\left(B_{n_{i}}(x_{i},\epsilon)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{C}\sum_{i\in I}\nu\left(B_{n}(x_{i},\epsilon)\right)

\displaystyle\Big\{\textit{f}\in\rm{Hom}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{E}):\textit{f\ injective, }\exists\,{\rm surjective}\,\textit{g}\in\rm{Hom}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{B})\textit{ s.t. g}\circ\textit{f}=0\Big\},

the associated morphisms

\displaystyle\varphi:\rm{W}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B};\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\rm{Fil}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B};\mathcal{E});\qquad(\textit{f, g})\mapstochar\rightarrow(0\subset{\rm{Im}}\,(f)\subset\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle\psi:\rm{W}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B};\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\rm{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A});\qquad(\textit{f, g})\mapstochar\rightarrow(0\rightarrow\mathcal{A}\xrightarrow{\textit{f}}\mathcal{E}\xrightarrow{\textit{g}}\mathcal{B}\rightarrow 0)

\displaystyle\pi:\rm{W}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B};\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\rm{U}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B};\mathcal{E});\qquad(\textit{f, g})\mapstochar\rightarrow\textit{f}

and the following natural actions:

\displaystyle\chi:\{(\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{A})^{op}

\displaystyle\times\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{B}\}\times\rm{W}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B};\mathcal{E})\rightarrow\rm{W}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B};\mathcal{E});\ \ (\rho,\sigma,(\textit{f, g}))\mapstochar\rightarrow(\textit{f}\circ\rho,\sigma\circ\textit{g})

\displaystyle\mu:\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{E}\times\rm{W}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B};\mathcal{E})\rightarrow\rm{W}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B};\mathcal{E});\qquad(\tau,(\textit{f, g}))\mapstochar\rightarrow(\tau\circ\textit{f, g}\circ\tau^{-1})

Accordingly, we have

(i)
\varphi
is surjective, and

(ii) There is a natural bijection between the fiber of
\varphi
and
(\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{A})^{op}\times\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{B}
.

This is a direct consequence of the five lemma. Indeed, this follows from the facts that
\varphi(f,g)=\varphi(f^{\prime},g^{\prime})
if and only if there exists
(\rho,\sigma)\in(\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{A})^{op}\times\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{B}
such that
(f^{\prime},g^{\prime})=\chi(\rho,\sigma,(f,g))
, and that the action
\chi
is free. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\#\rm{W}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B};\mathcal{E})\big/\big(\#(\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{A})\cdot\#\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{B}\big)=\#\rm{Fil}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B};\mathcal{E}).

(31)

(iii) The image of
\psi
is exactly the set
\rm{Ext}_{\mathcal{E}}^{1}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A})
of isomorphism classes of extensions of
\mathcal{B}
by
\mathcal{A}
the middle term of which is isomorphic to
\mathcal{E}
.
Moreover, any fiber of
\psi
is an orbit of
\rm{W}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B};\mathcal{E})
under the action
\mu
.

Indeed, this follows from the fact that
\psi(f,g)=\psi(f^{\prime},g^{\prime})
if and only if there exists
\tau\in\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{E}
such that
(f^{\prime},g^{\prime})=\mu(\tau,(f,g))
. In addition,

(iv) The stabilizer group of
(f,g)\in\rm{W}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B};\mathcal{E})
under the action
\mu
is isomorphic to
\rm{Hom}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A}).

Indeed, for any element
\tau\in\mathrm{Aut}\,{\mathcal{E}}
, if we write it as
\tau=\begin{pmatrix}\tau_{\mathcal{A}}&\tau_{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}}\\
\tau_{\mathcal{B}\mathcal{A}}&\tau_{\mathcal{B}}\end{pmatrix}
with

\tau_{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathrm{Aut}\,\mathcal{A},\ \tau_{\mathcal{B}}\in\mathrm{Aut}\,\mathcal{B},\ \tau_{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}),\ \tau_{\mathcal{B}\mathcal{A}}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A}),

then a direct calculation shows that
f=\tau\circ f
if and only if

\tau=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{A}}&0\\
\tau_{\mathcal{B}\mathcal{A}}&\tau_{\mathcal{B}}\end{pmatrix}.

Here we have used the claim that
\rm{Hom}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
part
\tau_{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}}
must be 0 since we have the inclusion

\tau(f(\mathcal{A}))\subset f(\mathcal{A})
by condition. Similarly,

g=g\circ\tau^{-1}
if and only if
g\circ\tau=g
if and only if

\tau=\begin{pmatrix}\tau_{\mathcal{A}}&0\\
\tau_{\mathcal{B}\mathcal{A}}&\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{B}}\end{pmatrix}.

Thus,
(f,g)=\mu(\tau,(f,g))
if and only if

\tau=\begin{pmatrix}\rm{Id}_{\mathcal{A}}&0\\
\tau_{\mathcal{B}\mathcal{A}}&\rm{Id}_{\mathcal{B}}\end{pmatrix}\in\begin{pmatrix}\rm{Id}_{\mathcal{A}}&0\\
\mathrm{Hom}({\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A}})&\rm{Id}_{\mathcal{B}}\end{pmatrix}.

Consequently, the stabilizer group of
(f,g)\in\rm{W}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B};\mathcal{E})
under the action
\mu
is isomorphic to
\rm{Hom}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A}).

Therefore, we obtain the following relation

\displaystyle\#\rm{W}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B};\mathcal{E})\big/\big(\#\rm{Aut}\mathcal{E}\big/\#(\rm{Hom}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A}))\big)=\#\rm{Ext}_{\mathcal{E}}^{1}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A}).

(32)

By (
31
) and (
32
), we have

\displaystyle\#\rm{Fil}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B};\mathcal{E})=\frac{\#\rm{Ext}_{\mathcal{E}}^{1}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A})\cdot\#\rm{Aut}\mathcal{E}}{\#\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{A}\cdot\#\rm{Aut}\mathcal{B}\cdot\#(\rm{Hom}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A}))}.

(33)

We mention in passing that this relation is an analogue of the formula given by [Ri].

(v)
\pi
is surjective, and each fiber of
\pi
is isomorphic to
\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{B}
.

This follows from the fact that
\pi(f,g)=\pi(f^{\prime},g^{\prime})
if and only if
f=f^{\prime}
and there exists
\sigma\in\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{B}
, by the five lemma. Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\#\rm{W}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B};\mathcal{E})\big/\#\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{B}=\#\rm{U}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B};\mathcal{E}).

(34)

Thorem.
(Counting Miracle)
Let
\mathcal{E}_{0}
be a stable vector bundle of rank
m\ (<n)
and degree
0
.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{M}_{X,n}(0)}\frac{q^{\#\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{E}_{0},\mathcal{E})}-1}{\#\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{E}}=q^{m(n-m)(g-1)}\sum_{\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{M}_{X,n-m}(0)}\frac{1}{\#\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{F}}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n+1}(0)=q^{n(g-1)}\beta_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}(0).

This counting miracle was conjectured by Weng in
[
19
]
. It was first established by Weng and Zagier
[
21
]
for elliptic curves, based on basic properties of Atiyah bundles and heavy combinatorial techniques. Our method here is totally different.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the first relation, since the second comes directly by applying the first to
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
and
m=1
.
To prove the first, by the stability of
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{M}_{X,n}(0)}\frac{q^{\#\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{E}_{0},\mathcal{E})}-1}{\#\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{M}_{X,n}(0)}\frac{\#\{f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{E}_{0},\mathcal{E}):f\ {\textrm{injective}}\}}{\#\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{M}_{X,n}(0)}\frac{1}{\#\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{E}}\,\#\rm{U}(\mathcal{E}_{0},\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{E}_{0};\mathcal{E})

since the category of semi-stable bundles of degree 0 is abelian.
Hence, by (
34
) and (
33
), this latest quantity can be written as

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{M}_{X,n}(0)}\frac{1}{\#\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{E}}\cdot\frac{\#\rm{W}(\mathcal{E}_{0},\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{E}_{0};\mathcal{E})}{\#\rm{Aut}(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{E}_{0})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{M}_{X,n}(0)}\frac{1}{\#\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{E}}\cdot\frac{\#\rm{Ext}_{\mathcal{E}}^{1}(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{E}_{0},\mathcal{E}_{0})\cdot\#\rm{Aut}\mathcal{E}}{\#\rm{Aut}\,(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{E}_{0})\cdot\#\rm{Hom}(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{E}_{0},\mathcal{E}_{0})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{M}_{X,n-m}(0)}\frac{\#\rm{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E}_{0})}{\#\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{F}\cdot\#\rm{Hom}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E}_{0})}

by changing from
\mathcal{E}
to
\mathcal{F}
, since the category of semistable bundles of degree 0 is abelian.
Therefore, by the Riemann-Roch theorem

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{M}_{X,n}(0)}\frac{q^{\#\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{E}_{0},\mathcal{E})}-1}{\#\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{m(n-m)(g-1)}\sum_{\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{M}_{X,n-m}(0)}\frac{1}{\#\rm{Aut}\,\mathcal{F}}

as wanted.
∎

2
Riemann hypothesis for rank two zeta: Yoshida’s approach

Applying Theorem
1.6
to
n=2
, easily we conclude that, up to a constant fact depending only on the genus
g
of the curve
X/\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which certainly does not affect our discussion on zeta zeros,

\widehat{\zeta}^{\,\mathrm{SL}_{2}}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q}}(s)=\frac{\widehat{\zeta}^{~}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(2s)}{1-q^{2-2s}}-\frac{\widehat{\zeta}^{~}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(2s-1)}{q^{2s}-1}

(35)

We first use the functional equation to obtain

\widehat{\zeta}^{\,\mathrm{SL}_{2}}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q}}(s)=\frac{\widehat{\zeta}^{~}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(1-2s)}{1-q^{2-2s}}-\frac{\widehat{\zeta}_{X,/F_{q}}(2s-1)}{q^{2s}-1}=\frac{\widehat{\zeta}^{~}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(2\sigma)}{1-q^{1+2\sigma}}-\frac{\widehat{\zeta}^{~}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(-2\sigma)}{q^{1-2\sigma}-1}

(36)

where
s=:\frac{1}{2}-\sigma
.
Therefore,

\widehat{\zeta}^{\,\mathrm{SL}_{2}}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q}}(s)=0\qquad{\rm if\ and\ only\ if}\qquad\frac{\widehat{\zeta}^{~}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(2\sigma)}{1-q^{1+2\sigma}}=\frac{\widehat{\zeta}^{~}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(-2\sigma)}{q^{1-2\sigma}-1}.

(37)

Write now

\widehat{\zeta}^{~}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(s)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{g}(1-\omega_{i}q^{-s})(1-\overline{\omega}_{i}q^{-s})}{q^{-s(g-1)}(1-q^{-s})(1-q^{1-s})}

(38)

where
\omega_{i}\in\mathbb{C}\smallsetminus\mathbb{R}
and
|\omega_{i}|=\sqrt{q}
, guaranteed by the Hasse-Weil Theorem, or better, the Riemann hypothesis for the Artin zeta function of
X/\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In particular,

|\omega_{i}+\overline{\omega}_{i}|\leq 2\sqrt{q}<q+1\qquad(\forall 1\leq i\leq g).

(39)

Accordingly, (
37
) becomes

q^{4\sigma(g-1)}(q^{1-2\sigma}-1)\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{g}(1-\omega_{i}q^{-2\sigma})(1-\overline{\omega}_{i}q^{-2\sigma})}{(1-q^{-2\sigma})(1-q^{1-2\sigma})}=(1-q^{1+2\sigma})\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{g}(1-\omega_{i}q^{2\sigma})(1-\overline{\omega}_{i}q^{2\sigma})}{(1-q^{2\sigma})(1-q^{1+2\sigma})}

(40)

This is equivalent to

q^{4\sigma(g-1)}(1-q^{2\sigma})\prod_{i=1}^{g}(1-\omega_{i}q^{-2\sigma})(1-\overline{\omega}_{i}q^{-2\sigma})=(1-q^{-2\sigma})\prod_{i=1}^{g}(1-\omega_{i}q^{2\sigma})(1-\overline{\omega}_{i}q^{2\sigma})

(41)

In particular, we should have

|q^{4\sigma(g-1)}|\cdot|(1-q^{2\sigma})|\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{g}|(1-\omega_{i}q^{-2\sigma})(1-\overline{\omega}_{i}q^{-2\sigma})|=|(1-q^{-2\sigma})|\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{g}|(1-\omega_{i}q^{2\sigma})(1-\overline{\omega}_{i}q^{2\sigma})|

(42)

Lemma 2.1

(Yoshida)
.

Fix a real number
q>1
. Let
\alpha,\,{\beta}\in\mathbb{C}
and write
c=\alpha+{\beta}
. Assume that
\alpha{\beta}=q
and that
c\in\mathbb{R}
satisfies
|c|\leq q+1
. Then for
w\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

|w-\alpha|\cdot|w-{\beta}|\ \begin{cases}&\hskip-8.5359pt>|1-\alpha w|\cdot|1-{\beta}w|\qquad{\rm if}\quad|w|<1\\
&\hskip-8.5359pt<|1-\alpha w|\cdot|1-{\beta}w|\qquad{\rm if}\quad|w|>1.\end{cases}

(43)

When
\alpha=1
and
{\beta}=q
, this lemma degenerates to an estimate on the fractional transformation
T_{q}(w):=\displaystyle{\frac{w-q}{1-qw}}
. In this sense, Yoshida’s lemma is a natural degree 2 generalization of that for fractional transformations. Even an elementary proof can be given immediately, we delay the details till the proof of Lemma
4.5
below, which itself is a generalization of Yoshida’s lemma.

Therefore, for the left hand side of (
42
), by applying Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle|q^{4\sigma(g-1)}|\cdot|(1-q^{2\sigma})|\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{g}|(1-\omega_{i}q^{-2\sigma})(1-\overline{\omega}_{i}q^{-2\sigma})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|(1-q^{2\sigma})||q^{-4\sigma}|\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{g}|(q^{2\sigma}-\omega_{i})(q^{2\sigma}-\overline{\omega}_{i})|

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\hskip-8.5359pt>\prod_{i=1}^{g}|(1-\omega_{i}q^{2\sigma})(1-\overline{\omega}_{i}q^{2\sigma})|\cdot|(q^{-2\sigma}-1)||q^{(1-2g)2\sigma}|\qquad{\rm if}\quad\Re(\sigma)<0\\
&\hskip-8.5359pt<\prod_{i=1}^{g}|(1-\omega_{i}q^{2\sigma})(1-\overline{\omega}_{i}q^{2\sigma})|\cdot|(q^{-2\sigma}-1)||q^{(1-2g)2\sigma}|\qquad{\rm if}\quad\Re(\sigma)>0.\end{cases}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\hskip-8.5359pt>\prod_{i=1}^{g}|(1-\omega_{i}q^{2\sigma})(1-\overline{\omega}_{i}q^{2\sigma})|\cdot|(q^{-2\sigma}-1)|\qquad{\rm if}\quad\Re(\sigma)<0\\
&\hskip-8.5359pt<\prod_{i=1}^{g}|(1-\omega_{i}q^{2\sigma})(1-\overline{\omega}_{i}q^{2\sigma})|\cdot|(q^{-2\sigma}-1)|\qquad{\rm if}\quad\Re(\sigma)>0.\end{cases}

which is nothing but the right hand side of (
42
), provided that
g\geq 1
. This implies that unless
\Re(\sigma)=0
, i.e. unless
\sigma
is a pure imaginary complex number,
\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{\,\mathrm{SL}_{2}}(s)
cannot be zero. This then proves the following

Theorem 2.2
.

(Yoshida) Let
X
be an integral regular projective curve of genus
g
, then the
\mathrm{SL}_{2}

zeta function

\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{\,\mathrm{SL}_{2}}(s)
and hence the rank two zeta function
\widehat{\zeta}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q},2}(s)
satisfy the Riemann hypothesis.

This result is due to H. Yoshida
[
23
]
, during our intensive lectures on non-abelian zeta functions for number fields in Kyoto. Yoshida, motivated by our works on rank two zeta functions of number fields
[
7
,
11
,
20
]
, actually proves the Riemann hypothesis for a slight more general zeta function

\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q}}(s;C_{1}):=C_{1}(s)\frac{\widehat{\zeta}^{~}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(2s)}{1-q^{1-s}}-C_{2}(s)\frac{q^{-s}\,\widehat{\zeta}^{~}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(2s-1)}{1-q^{2s-1}},

(44)

where

takes the form

C_{1}(s)=q^{as}(1+q^{-s})q^{-hs}\prod_{j=1}^{h}(1-\gamma_{i}q^{s-1/2})(1-\delta_{i}q^{s-1/2})

(45)

with constants

and

(47)

Since our
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
-zeta function

3
Applications of the RH for higher rank zetas

3.1
First estimates

Before going further, we here deduce some natural upper and lower bounds for the non-abelian geo-arithmetic invariants
\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}(mn)\ (m=0,\ldots,g-1)
and
{\beta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}(0)
of a curve
X/\mathbb{F}_{q}
by assuming the Riemann hypothesis for rank
n
zeta function of
X/\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Indeed as we will see later, these bounds in lower ranks, in turn, play key roles in proving the Riemann Hypothesis for higher rank zeta functions.

Set now

\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}(mn):=\frac{\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}(mn)}{\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}(0)}\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad{\beta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}(0)=\frac{{\beta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}(0)}{\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}(0)}.

(48)

Assume the Riemann hypothesis for the rank
n
non-abelian zeta function of
X/\mathbb{F}_{q}
. By (
7
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}(0)}P_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}(s)={T^{g-1}(1-T)(1-QT)}\cdot\frac{\widehat{Z}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}(T)}{\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}(0)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Biggl(\left(\sum_{m=0}^{g-2}\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}(mn)\left(T^{m}+(QT)^{2(g-1)-m}\right)+\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}(n(g-1))T^{g-1}\right)(1-(Q+1)T+QT^{2})

\displaystyle+(Q-1)T^{g}\beta_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}(0)\Biggr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{g}(1-\omega^{~}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q},n;i}T)(1-\overline{\omega}^{~}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q},n;i}T).

where
\omega^{~}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q},n;i}
denotes the reciprocal roots of
P_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}(s)
. Indeed,
\frac{1}{\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}(0)}P_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}(s)\in\mathbb{R}[T]
is a degree
2g

T
of real coefficients with leading coefficient

and constant term 1. By the functional equation of
\widehat{Z}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}(T)
, we may regroup all
2g

into
g

Q
. Consequently, the Riemann hypothesis for rank
n
zeta function of
X/\mathbb{F}_{q}
is equivalent to the condition that each such a pair is of the form
\left\{\omega^{~}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q},n;i},\,\overline{\omega}^{~}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q},n;i}\right\}
. This is certainly equivalent to the condition that

\left|\omega^{~}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q},n;i}\right|=Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\qquad(i=1,\ldots g)

(49)

since
\omega^{~}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q},n;i}\cdot\overline{\omega}^{~}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q},n;i}=Q
.

Set now
a^{~}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q},n;i}=\omega^{~}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q},n;i}+\overline{\omega}^{~}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q},n;i}
. From Vieta’s theorem between the reciprocal roots and coefficients of polynomials, by comparing the coefficients of
T^{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,g
in both sides of the above identity, under the Riemann hypothesis (
49
), we conclude that the follows hold:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Big|\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}(n)-(Q+1)\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}(0)\Big|=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{g}a_{n,i}\right|\leq 2g\sqrt{Q},\\
\Big|\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}(mn)-(Q+1)\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}((m-1)n)+Q\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}((m-2)n)\Big|\hskip 48.36958pt(2\leq m\leq g-1)\\
\hskip 122.34692pt\leq\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq 2g}Q^{m/2}=\binom{2g}{m}Q^{m/2}\\
\Big|(Q-1)\beta_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}(0)-(Q+1)\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}((g-1)n)+Q\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}((g-2)n)\Big|\\
\hskip 122.34692pt\leq\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{g}\leq 2g}Q^{g/2}=\binom{2g}{g}Q^{g/2}\end{cases}

Now expand each absolute value inequality, say
|\kappa_{i}|<c_{i}
as
-c_{i}<\kappa_{i}<c_{i}
, first, and then add three consecutive relations together with their lower and upper bounds, we arrive at

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-2g\sqrt{Q}\leq\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}(n)-(Q+1)\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}(0)\leq 2g\sqrt{Q},\\
-\sum_{k=3}^{m+1}Q^{k-3}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{2g}{i-1}Q^{(i-1)/2}-2gQ^{m-1}\sqrt{Q}\\
\hskip 42.67912pt\leq\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}(mn)-\Big(Q^{m}+\ldots+Q^{2}+Q+1\Big)\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}(0)\hskip 42.67912pt(2\leq m\leq g-1)\\
\hskip 85.35826pt\leq\sum_{k=3}^{m+1}Q^{k-3}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{2g}{i-1}Q^{(i-1)/2}+2gQ^{m-1}\sqrt{Q},\\
-\sum_{k=3}^{g+1}Q^{k-3}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{2g}{i-1}Q^{(i-1)/2}-2gQ^{g-1}\sqrt{Q}\\
\hskip 42.67912pt\leq(Q-1)\beta_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}(0)-\Big(Q^{g}+\ldots+Q^{2}+Q+1\Big)\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}(0)\\
\hskip 85.35826pt\leq\sum_{k=3}^{g+1}Q^{k-3}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{2g}{i-1}Q^{(i-1)/2}+2gQ^{g-1}\sqrt{Q}.\end{cases}

But, by definition,
\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}(0)=1
. Therefore, we have proved the following:

Proposition 3.1

(Rough Bounds of non-abelian invariants)
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis for the rank
n
zeta functions of a projective regular integral curve
X
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of genus
g
, we have for the invariants
\beta_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}(0)
and
\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}(2m)\ (m=1,\ldots,g-1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(Q+1)-2g\sqrt{Q}\leq\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}(n)\leq(Q+1)+2g\sqrt{Q}\\
\\
\Big(Q^{m}+\ldots+Q^{2}+Q+1\Big)-\sum_{k=3}^{m+1}Q^{k-3}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{2g}{i-1}Q^{(i-1)/2}-2gQ^{m-1}\sqrt{Q}\\
\hskip 56.9055pt\leq\alpha_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\prime}(mn)\hskip 193.47882pt(2\leq m\leq g)\\
\hskip 56.9055pt\leq\Big(Q^{m}+\ldots+Q^{2}+Q+1\Big)+\sum_{k=3}^{m+1}Q^{k-3}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{2g}{i-1}Q^{(i-1)/2}+2gQ^{m-1}\sqrt{Q}\\

\hskip 56.9055pt\leq\Big(Q^{g}+\ldots+Q^{2}+Q+1\Big)+\sum_{k=3}^{g+1}Q^{k-3}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{2g}{i-1}Q^{(i-1)/2}+2gQ^{g-1}\sqrt{Q}\end{cases}

3.2
Counting Brill-Noether stratifications

In this subsection, we explain why the rank

.

Example 3.1

(Brill-Noether Loci)
.

Even when
n=1
, the above result exposes some intrinsic geo-arithmetic properties of the curve
X/\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Indeed, we may introduce the Brill-Noether loci within the degree Picard group
\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(X)
of
X
by setting

W_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{\geq i}(d):=\big\{L\in\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(X):h^{0}(X,L)\geq i\big\}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad W_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{=i}(d):=\big\{L\in\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(X):h^{0}(X,L)=i\big\}.

(50)

The
W_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{\geq i}(d)
’s induce a natural stratification structure on
\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(X)
since

W_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{\geq i}(d)=\bigsqcup_{j\geq i}W_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{=j}(d)\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad W_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{\geq 0}(d)=\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(X).

(51)

It is natural to ask what are the topological or better motivic properties of these refined structures.
Set accordingly

w_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{\geq i}(d)=\#W_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{\geq i}(d)\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad w_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{=i}(d)=\#W_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{=i}(d).

(52)

and

\alpha_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{\geq i}(d)=\sum_{L\in W_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{\geq i}(d)}\frac{q^{h^{0}(X,L)-1}}{q-1}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\alpha_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{=i}(d)=\sum_{L\in W_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{=i}(d)}\frac{q^{h^{0}(X,L)-1}}{q-1}=\frac{q^{i}-1}{q-1}w_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{=i}(d).

(53)

Then

w_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{\geq 0}(d)=\sum_{i\geq 0}w_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{=i}(d)=\#\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X)\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\alpha_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(d)=\alpha_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{\geq 0}(d)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{q^{i}-1}{q-1}w_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{=i}(d),

(54)

which by Theorem
3.1
, is controlled by

\begin{cases}-2g\sqrt{q}\leq\alpha_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(1)-(q+1)\leq 2g\sqrt{q}\\
-\sum_{k=3}^{m+1}q^{k-3}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{2g}{i-1}q^{(i-1)/2}-2gq^{m-1}\sqrt{q}\\
\hskip 56.9055pt\leq\alpha_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{\prime}(m)-\Big(q^{m}+\ldots+q^{2}+q+1\Big)\hskip 51.21504pt(2\leq m\leq g)\\
\hskip 113.81102pt\leq\sum_{k=3}^{m+1}q^{k-3}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{2g}{i-1}q^{(i-1)/2}+2gq^{m-1}\sqrt{q}\\
-\sum_{k=3}^{g+1}q^{k-3}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{2g}{i-1}q^{(i-1)/2}-2gq^{g-1}\sqrt{q}\\
\hskip 56.9055pt\leq(q-1)\beta_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(0)-\Big(q^{g}+\ldots+q^{2}+q+1\Big)\\
\hskip 113.81102pt\leq\sum_{k=3}^{g+1}q^{k-3}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{2g}{i-1}q^{(i-1)/2}+2gq^{g-1}\sqrt{q}\end{cases}

(55)

since
\alpha_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(0)=1

Recall that, by definition,

\beta_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(0)=\frac{1}{q-1}\#\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X)=\widehat{v}_{1}=\frac{1}{q-1}\prod_{i=1}^{g}(1-\omega_{X/F_{q},i})(1-\overline{\omega}_{X/F_{q},i}).

{\beta}
-invariant, (

Furthermore, we may introduce natural associated invariants

\alpha_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{\geq i}(d):=\sum_{V\in W_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{\geq i}(d)}\frac{q^{h^{0}(X,L)}-1}{q-1}=\sum_{j\geq i}w_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{=j}(d)\frac{q^{j}-1}{q-1}

Obviously, these structures admit a natural generalization to the setting of rank

where

.

u
and

, for which a modification can be made to result a rational function that satisfies the standard functional equation. For details, please refer to our work in preparation on motivic structures of curves over general base fields.

We end this discussion with the following application of the Riemann hypothesis.

Lemma 3.2
.

For a regular projective curve
X
of genus
g\geq 2
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we have

\left(q^{-\binom{n}{2}(g-1)}\cdot\frac{{\beta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};2}(0)}{\widehat{\nu}_{1}^{2}}-\frac{1}{q+1}\right)>0.\qquad{\rm In\ particular,}\qquad\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}>\frac{q}{q^{2}-1}

(63)

Slight differently,

\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}>\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{q-1}.

(64)

Proof.

Note that

\left(q^{-\binom{n}{2}(g-1)}\cdot\frac{{\beta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};2}(0)}{\widehat{\nu}_{1}^{2}}-\frac{1}{q+1}\right)=\frac{\widehat{\nu}_{2}+\frac{1}{1-q^{2}}\widehat{\nu}_{1}^{2}}{\widehat{\nu}_{1}^{2}}-\frac{1}{q+1}=\frac{\widehat{\nu}_{2}}{\widehat{\nu}_{1}^{2}}-\frac{q}{q^{2}-1}

Now

\displaystyle\frac{\widehat{\nu}_{2}}{\widehat{\nu}_{1}^{2}}=

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{i=1}^{g}\frac{(1-\omega_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};i}q^{-2})(1-\overline{\omega}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};i}q^{-2})}{(1-\omega_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};i}q^{-1})^{2}(1-\overline{\omega}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};i}q^{-1})^{2}}\right)\frac{q^{-2(g-1)}(q-1)^{2}}{q^{-2(g-1)}(1-q^{-}2)(1-qq^{-2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q^{g}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{g}\frac{(\overline{\omega}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};i}-q^{-1})(\omega_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};i}-q^{-1})}{(1-\omega_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};i}q^{-1})(1-\overline{\omega}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};i}q^{-1})}\frac{1}{(1-\omega_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};i}q^{-1})(1-\overline{\omega}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};i}q^{-1})}\right)\frac{q^{3}}{q+1}

\displaystyle({\rm since}\ \omega_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};i}\overline{\omega}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};i}=q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{i=1}^{g}\frac{(\overline{\omega}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};i}-q^{-1})(\omega_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};i}-q^{-1})}{(1-\omega_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};i}q^{-1})(1-\overline{\omega}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};i}q^{-1})}\right)\frac{q^{2}}{q^{2}-1}\cdot\widehat{\nu}_{1}\geq\left(\prod_{i=1}^{g}1\right)\frac{q^{2}}{q^{2}-1}\cdot\widehat{\nu}_{1}

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle by\ Yoshida^{\prime}s\ lemma,\ since\ }|q^{-1}|<1\ {\rm(and\ }\ \widehat{\nu}_{1}>0\ {\rm and}\ \frac{\widehat{\nu}_{2}}{\widehat{\nu}_{1}^{2}}>0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q^{2}}{q^{2}-1}\cdot\widehat{\nu}_{1}=\frac{q^{2}}{(q^{2}-1)(q-1)}\cdot\#\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X)>\frac{q}{q^{2}-1}

where in the last step, we have used the fact that there exists at least one
\mathbb{F}_{q}
rational point in

.
This verifies (

In addition, since

\frac{q^{2}}{q^{2}-1}\#\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X)>\frac{3}{2}

\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}>\frac{q^{2}}{(q^{2}-1)(q-1)}\#\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X)>\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{q-1},

3.3
Rank
n

Let

where

runs through all semi-stable

-rational vector bundles of rank

As a direct consequence of Siegel’s formula on the total mass of rank

Here

Furthermore, to understand the

\beta_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\mathrm{tot}}(d)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\geq 1\\

where

(71)

In particular,

\displaystyle\beta_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}(d)\;=

\displaystyle\beta_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}^{\mathrm{tot}}(d)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 2\\
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\geq 1\\
n_{1}+\ldots+n_{k}=n\end{subarray}}\nu_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{~}(1)^{k-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\delta_{i})\in\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{Z}/n_{i}\\

to obtain a finite closed form

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
X
be an integral regular projective curve of genus
g
ovcer a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then its rank
n

\beta_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}(d)=q^{\binom{n}{2}(g-1)}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\geq 1\\

(72)

In particular, when
n|d
, say
d=mn

\beta_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}(mn)=q^{\binom{n}{2}(g-1)}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\geq 1\\

Note that, induced from the map
{\mathcal{E}}\mapstochar\rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}(A)

(74)

Besides this, in general, it is quite complicated to estimate

However, after the introduction of the rank

in

3.4

Recall that

\displaystyle\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}(s)=

\displaystyle{\beta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};n}(0)=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}_{T=1}\Biggl(C_{n,g;q}\cdot\sum_{a=1}^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\ldots,k_{p}>0\\

The point is to apply the functional equation for the abelian zeta function

Accordingly, for

\displaystyle\frac{\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q}}(ns-n+a)\cdot q^{(1-ns+n-b)(g-1)}}{\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q}}(1-ns+n-b)\cdot q^{(ns-n+a)(g-1)}}

|w|<1
, then

w
should be contained inside the disc of radius
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2q\Big(\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}+\frac{q}{1-q^{2}}\Big)}}
centered at
\displaystyle{\frac{\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}+\frac{1}{1-q^{2}}}{q\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}+\frac{q^{2}}{1-q^{2}}}}
; and

(2)

When
|w|>1
, then
w
should be totally located outside the disc of radius
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2q\Big(\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}+\frac{q}{1-q^{2}}\Big)}}
centered at
\displaystyle{\frac{\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}+\frac{1}{1-q^{2}}}{q\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}+\frac{q^{2}}{1-q^{2}}}}
.

An elementary discussion implies that this would happen if the disc of radius
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2q\Big(\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}+\frac{q}{1-q^{2}}\Big)}}
centered at
\displaystyle{\frac{\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}+\frac{1}{1-q^{2}}}{q\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}+\frac{q^{2}}{1-q^{2}}}}
is totally contained in the unit disc
|w|<1
.
Since
\displaystyle{\frac{\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}+\frac{1}{1-q^{2}}}{q\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}+\frac{q^{2}}{1-q^{2}}}}\in\mathbb{R}
is a real number, this means that

\begin{cases}-\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2q\Big(\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}+\frac{q}{1-q^{2}}\Big)}}+\displaystyle{\frac{\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}+\frac{1}{1-q^{2}}}{q\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}+\frac{q^{2}}{1-q^{2}}}}>-1\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2q\Big(\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}+\frac{q}{1-q^{2}}\Big)}}+\displaystyle{\frac{\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}+\frac{1}{1-q^{2}}}{q\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}+\frac{q^{2}}{1-q^{2}}}}<1\end{cases}

(112)

That is to say,

-1+\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2q\Big(\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}+\frac{q}{1-q^{2}}\Big)}}<\displaystyle{\frac{\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}+\frac{1}{1-q^{2}}}{q\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}+\frac{q^{2}}{1-q^{2}}}}<1-\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2q\Big(\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}+\frac{q}{1-q^{2}}\Big)}}

(113)

or the same

\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}>\max\left\{\frac{1/2+1}{q-1},\frac{1/2+\frac{q^{2}+1}{q^{2}-1}}{q+1}\right\}=\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{q-1}

(114)

which is guaranteed by (
64
) in Corollary
3.2
.
∎

We are now ready to complete our proof of the proposition. Indeed,

\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\,\geq 2}(s)=\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{[2]}(s)+\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{[3]}(s)=\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{[3]}(s)\left(\frac{\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{[3]}(s)}{\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{[3]}(s)}+1\right)

(115)

By Lemma
4.9
just proved, all the zeros of the second factor lie on the line
\Re(s)=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{6}=\frac{1}{3}
. Accordingly, it suffices to show that the zeros of

\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{[3]}(s)
cannot be the zeros of
\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{[2]}(s)
.
Recall that

\displaystyle\widehat{\zeta}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{[2]}(s):=\frac{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}{(1-q^{3s})(1-q^{-3s+3})}\,\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q}}(3s-1)

\displaystyle\widehat{\zeta}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{[3]}(s):=\left(\frac{\widehat{v}_{2}}{(1-q^{-3s+3})}+\frac{\widehat{v}_{1}^{\,2}}{(1-q^{2})(1-q^{-3s+2})}\right)\,\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q}}(3s)

Obviously, the zeta zeros from the zeta factor
\widehat{\zeta}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(3s)
, which are on the line of
\Re(s)=\frac{1}{6}
, cannot be the zeros of
\widehat{\zeta}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(3s-1)
, which are on the line
\Re(s)=\frac{1}{2}
, by the Riemann hypothesis for the Artin zeta function
\zeta_{F/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(s)
. This then leaves the zeros of the rational function factor in
\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{[3]}(s)
, which is clearly not on the line of
\Re(s)=\frac{1}{2}
. Therefore, all zeros of
\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{[2]}(s)+\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{[3]}(s)
are coming from the second factor
\frac{\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{[3]}(s)}{\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{[3]}(s)}+1
in (
115
) and hence lie on the line
\Re(s)=\frac{1}{3}
, as wanted.
∎

4.5
Rank three Riemann hypothesis

Now we are finally ready to complete our proof of Theorem
4.2
and hence Theorem
4.3
. We start with the function

R_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};3}(s):=\left(1+\frac{\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\leq 2}(s)}{\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\,\geq 2}(s)}\right).

Recall that

\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\,\geq 2}(s)=\frac{1}{2}{\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{[2]}(s)}+\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{[3]}(s)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n+2(g-1)}(1-T\gamma_{i})}{T^{g-1}\prod_{\ell=0}^{n}(1-q^{\ell}T)}

(116)

Here, by the Riemann hypothesis for
\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\,\geq 2}(s)
established in the previous subsection, we have

|\gamma_{i}|=Q^{1/3}\qquad(\forall i=1,\ldots,n+2(g-1))

(117)

Then

\displaystyle\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\leq 2}(s)=

\displaystyle\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{[1]}(s)+\frac{1}{2}{\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{[2]}(s)}=\frac{1}{2}{\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{[2]}(1-s)}+\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{[3]}(1-s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n+2(g-1)}(1-\frac{\gamma_{i}}{QT})}{(QT)^{-g+1}\prod_{\ell=0}^{n}(1-\frac{q^{\ell}}{QT})}=\frac{1}{Q^{\frac{n-1}{2}+g-1}T^{g-2}}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n+2(g-1)}(QT-{\gamma_{i}})}{\prod_{\ell=0}^{n}({q^{\ell}T}-1)}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\leq[2]}(s)}{\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\,\geq 2}(s)}=

\displaystyle\frac{{\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{[1]}(s)}+\frac{1}{2}{\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{[2]}(s)}}{\frac{1}{2}{\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{[2]}(s)}+\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{[3]}(s)}=\frac{\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n+2(g-1)}(1-T\gamma_{i})}{T^{g-1}\prod_{\ell=0}^{n}(1-q^{\ell}T)}}{\frac{1}{Q^{\frac{n-1}{2}+g-1}T^{g-2}}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n+2(g-1)}(QT-{\gamma_{i}})}{\prod_{\ell=0}^{n}({q^{\ell}T}-1)}}=\frac{T}{\sqrt{Q}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n+2(g-1)}\frac{1-T\gamma_{i}}{\sqrt{Q}T-\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\sqrt{Q}}}}

We examine the factors
\displaystyle{\frac{1-T\gamma_{i}}{\sqrt{Q}T-\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\sqrt{Q}}}}
under the condition that
|\gamma_{i}|=Q^{1/3}
for all
1\leq i\leq n+2(g-1)
. Write then
\gamma_{i}=Q^{\frac{1}{3}}e^{i\theta_{i}}
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1-T\gamma_{i}}{\sqrt{Q}T-\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\sqrt{Q}}}\right|=\left|\frac{1-TQ^{1/3}e^{i\theta_{i}}}{\sqrt{Q}T-\frac{Q^{1/3}e^{-i\theta_{i}}}{\sqrt{Q}}}\right|=\left|\frac{1-\sqrt{Q}TQ^{-1/6}e^{i\theta_{i}}}{\sqrt{Q}T-{Q^{-1/6}e^{-i\theta_{i}}}}\right|

Note that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1-\sqrt{Q}TQ^{-1/6}e^{i\theta}}{\sqrt{Q}T-{Q^{-1/6}e^{-i\theta}}}\right|^{2}=1-\frac{(Q^{{1}/{3}}-1)(1+Q^{2/3})|T|^{2}}{Q|T|^{2}-Q^{1/3}(e^{-i\theta}\overline{T}+e^{i\theta}T)+Q^{-\frac{1}{3}}}<1

Obviously,

\left|\frac{T}{\sqrt{Q}}\right|\begin{cases}<1&\qquad|T|<\sqrt{Q}\\
>1&\qquad|T|>\sqrt{Q}\end{cases}

(118)

This implies that

R_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};3}(s)
has no zero in the region
\Re(s)<\frac{1}{2}
. Now note that, by
§
4.1
, we have

\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}(s)=\widehat{\zeta}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\leq{2}}(s)+\widehat{\zeta}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\geq{2}}(s)=\widehat{\zeta}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\geq 2}(s)\left(1+\frac{\widehat{\zeta}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\leq 2}(s)}{\widehat{\zeta}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\geq 2}(s)}\right)=\widehat{\zeta}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\geq 2}(s)\cdot R_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};3}(s)

(119)

Thus, to prove Theorem
4.2
, what is left to to show that the zeros of
\widehat{\zeta}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\geq 2}(s)
cannot be the zeros of
\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}(s)
. But this is clear, since all zeros of
\widehat{\zeta}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\leq 2}(s)
lie on the line
\Re(s)=1-\frac{1}{3}
by the functional equation.

\widehat{\zeta}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\leq 2}(1-s)=\widehat{\zeta}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\geq 2}(s).

(120)

In particular,
\widehat{\zeta}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\leq 2}(1-s)
and
\widehat{\zeta}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\geq 2}(s)
cannot have any common zero. Therefore, from (
119
),
the zeros of
\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}(s)
cannot come from the first factor
\widehat{\zeta}_{X,\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\,\geq 2}(s)
, but all come from the second factor
\left(1+\frac{\widehat{\zeta}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\leq 2}(s)}{\widehat{\zeta}_{X/\mathbb{F}_{q};3}^{\geq 2}(s)}\right)
. This proves Theorem
4.2
and hence also Theorem
4.3
.

We mention in passing that an analogue of this result holds for the rank
3
zeta function
\widehat{\zeta}_{\mathbb{Q};3}(s)
and hence the
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
zeta function
\widehat{\zeta}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{SL}_{3}}(s)
the field
\mathbb{Q}
of rationals has been proved by Suzuki
[
16
]
(See also
[
8
,
20
]
for general discussions).

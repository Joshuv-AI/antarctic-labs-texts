An Euler system for the adjoint of a modular form
David Loeffler, Sarah Livia Zerbes

Abstract
. We construct an Euler system for the adjoint Galois representation of a modular form, using motivic cohomology classes arising from Hilbert modular surfaces. We use this Euler system to give an upper bound for the Selmer group of the adjoint representation over the cyclotomic 𝐙 p \mathbf{Z}_{p} -extension, which agrees with the predictions of the Iwasawa main conjecture up to powers of p p .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Setting

Many conjectures – such as the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, the Bloch–Kato conjecture, and the Main Conjecture of Iwasawa theory – relate the special values of
L
-functions attached to algebraic varietes, or more generally to Galois representations, to the algebraic properties of those Galois representations. The method of
Euler systems
, originating in the work of Thaine, Kolyvagin, and Rubin, is among the most powerful tools known for attacking these conjectures, via giving upper bounds for Selmer groups.

One particularly important case for these conjectures is the 3-dimensional
adjoint
Galois representation attached to an elliptic curve over
\mathbf{Q}
(or more generally a modular form). As well as being a natural and interesting example of a Galois representation in its own right, this case is important because of its relation to modularity lifting, and thus to the proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem. The Bloch–Kato conjecture is known in this setting (by
[
DFG04
]
, building on the methods introduced by Taylor and Wiles
[
TW95
]
for the proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem); but the Iwasawa main conjecture remains open.

In an earlier work
[
LZ19
]
, we studied the arithmetic of the adjoint using the
Beilinson–Flach elements
, introduced in
[
LLZ14
]
. These give an Euler system for a twist of the 4-dimensional tensor square Galois representation, which is the direct sum of the adjoint representation and a 1-dimensional complementary piece. However, this does not work as one might hope, due to an unexpected obstacle: the Euler factors appearing in the norm relations for these classes contain an extra term which is the Euler factor of the 1-dimensional summand. Since the Galois group acts trivially on this summand, this degree 1 factor
always vanishes
at the trivial character, and these trivial zeroes render the Beilinson–Flach classes useless for bounding the adjoint Selmer group. (We were able to bound the Selmer group of the
twist
of the adjoint by any sufficiently non-trivial Dirichlet character, but not that of the adjoint itself.)

1.2.
Overview of the results

In this paper, we construct an Euler system for the adjoint via a different approach: as a “degenerate” case of the
Asai–Flach elements
, introduced in
[
LLZ18
]
. These give an Euler system for the
Asai
, or
twisted tensor
, Galois representation for a Hilbert modular form over a real quadratic field. We show that if the Hilbert modular form is the base-change (Doi–Nagunuma lift) of an elliptic modular form, the four-dimensional Asai representation decomposes – like the tensor square – into the direct sum of the adjoint representation and a 1-dimensional complement. However in contrast to the tensor square case, the Galois group acts non-trivially on the 1-dimensional complement: it acts via the quadratic character associated to the real quadratic field. This quadratic twist resolves all the problems previously experienced in bounding the Selmer group – we may choose all the auxiliary primes in the Euler-system argument to be inert in the real quadratic field, and then the extra Euler factor is a unit in the group ring.

Using these classes, together with a result of Byeon
[
Bye01
]
showing that we may choose the real quadratic field so its
p
-adic
L
-function is a unit, we obtain a bound for the Selmer group of the adjoint over the cyclotomic
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-extension (under various technical hypotheses on
f
and
p
, see Theorem
A
below for the precise statement).

The bound we give here coincides with the bound predicted by the Iwasawa main conjecture, up to controlling powers of
p
. We hope to refine the bounds to remove the indeterminacy, and hence prove the full Main Conjecture, in future work.

1.3.
Relation to other work

We note that another, very different parallel approach to constructing an Euler systems for the adjoint of a modular form has been announced by Marco Sangiovanni and Chris Skinner, in work developed in parallel with this one. Their methods are very different than ours (using the pullback to
\GL_{2}\times\GL_{2}
of an Eisenstein class for the larger group
\GSp_{4}
, rather than the pushforward of an Eisenstein class for a subgroup as in our approach).

We also remark that a
lower
bound for the Selmer group was already announced in an an unpublished preprint of Urban from 2006 (
Groupes de Selmer et fonctions
L

p
-adiques pour les representations modulaires adjointes
), using congruences between Klingen Eisenstein series and cusp forms on
\GSp_{4}
.

as the unique lifting of

which acts trivially on

Lemma 3.3.3
.

Suppose

and
\ell\mid m
is a prime. Then the element

is a unit in the Iwasawa algebra
\mathcal{O}_{v}[[\Gamma^{\mathrm{cy}}_{m}]]
.

Proof.

As

is a
p
-group, its Iwasawa algebra is a local ring whose maximal ideal is the kernel of the augmentation map to the residue field

. Since
\ell=1\bmod p
, and
\ell
is inert in

\varepsilon_{F}(\ell)=-1
), this map sends

to

∎

Proposition 3.3.4
.

There exists a compatible family of classes

{}_{c}\widehat{\AF}_{\mathrm{cy},m}(\Pi)\in H^{1}_{\Iw}\left(\mathbf{Q}^{\mathrm{cy}}(m),T\right),\quad m\in\mathcal{R},\quad(c,m)=1,

with the following properties:

(1)

If
\ell m\in\mathcal{R}
with
\ell
prime, the Euler system norm relation

\operatorname{norm}^{\ell m}_{m}({}_{c}\widehat{\AF}_{\mathrm{cy},\ell m}(\Pi))=P_{\ell}(\ad f,\ell^{-1}\sigma_{\ell})\cdot{}_{c}\widehat{\AF}_{\mathrm{cy},m}(\Pi)

holds.

(2)

If
\chi
is a Dirichlet character factoring through

and having conductor exactly

, then the images of
{}_{c}\widehat{\AF}_{\mathrm{cy},m}(\Pi)
and
{}_{c}\!\AF_{\mathrm{cy},m}(\Pi)
under the projection map

\pr_{\chi}\left({}_{c}\!\AF_{\mathrm{cy},m}(\Pi)\right)=\left(\prod_{\ell\mid m}-\ell^{k+1}(1+\ell\chi(\hat{\ell}))\right)\cdot\pr_{\chi}\left({}_{c}\widehat{\AF}_{\mathrm{cy},m}(\Pi)\right),

where
\hat{\ell}=1\bmod\ell
and
\hat{\ell}=\ell\bmod m/\ell
. In particular, for

we have
{}_{c}\widehat{\AF}_{\mathrm{cy},1}(\Pi)={}_{c}\!\AF_{\mathrm{cy},1}(\Pi)
.

Proof.

We build up

from

in three steps, all of which keep the

\mathbf{x}_{m}=\left(\prod_{\ell\mid m}-\ell^{k+2}\hat{\sigma}_{\ell}\right)^{-1}{}_{c}\!\AF_{\mathrm{cy},m}(\Pi)
, so that we have
\operatorname{norm}^{\ell m}_{m}(\mathbf{x}_{\ell m})=Q_{\ell}(\mathcal{F},\sigma_{\ell}^{-1})\mathbf{x}_{m}
. Secondly, since we have
Q_{\ell}(\mathcal{F},X)=P_{\ell}(\mathcal{F},\ell^{-(k+2)}X)\bmod{\ell-1}
, we can apply Lemma IX.6.1 of
[

]

from the

in their norm relations, and such that

for primitive characters

. Finally, using

∎

3.4.
Local factors at bad primes

As we shall see below, the Asai–Flach classes are related to the “imprimitive” Asai
L
-series
L_{\As}^{\mathrm{imp}}(\mathcal{F},s)
. This is a Dirichlet series whose coefficients are given by an explicit formula in terms of Hecke eigenvalues of
\mathcal{F}
, but whose Euler factors at primes
\ell\mid N_{f}
can differ by finitely many Euler factors from the “true” (primitive) Asai
L
-series, whose
L
-factors are defined via the local Langlands correspondence. Thus there are polynomials
C_{\ell}\in\mathcal{O}_{L}[X]
, for each
\ell\mid N_{f}
, such that

L_{\As}^{\mathrm{imp}}(\mathcal{F},s)=\left(\prod_{\ell}C_{\ell}(\ell^{-s})\right)\cdot L_{\As}(\mathcal{F},s).

Our next proposition shows that, after giving away a uniform power of
p
, we may refine our Asai–Flach classes to have “optimal” local Euler factors:

Proposition 3.4.1
.

We can find an integer
t\geqslant 0
, and a family of classes
\{{}_{c}\widehat{\AF}{}_{\mathrm{cy},m}^{\mathrm{pr}}(\Pi)\in H^{1}_{\Iw}(\mathbf{Q}^{\mathrm{cy}},T):m\in\mathcal{R}\}
(“primitive Asai–Flach elements”) which satisfy the Euler system norm relations, such that

p^{t}{}_{c}\widehat{\AF}_{\mathrm{cy},1}(\Pi)=\left(\prod_{\ell\mid N_{f}}C_{\ell}(\ell^{-(k+2)}\sigma_{\ell}^{-1})\right)\cdot{}_{c}\widehat{\AF}{}_{\mathrm{cy},1}^{\mathrm{pr}}(\Pi).

Proof.

Let
S
denote the set of primes dividing
N_{f}
. Exactly as in the
\operatorname{GSp}_{4}
case described in
[
LSZ22
]
and
[
LZ24
]
, for any choice of “test data” consisting of an
L
-valued Schwartz function
\Phi_{S}
on
\mathbf{Q}_{S}^{2}=\prod_{\ell}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}^{2}
, and an element
w_{S}
in the Whittaker model of
\Pi_{S}
(defined over
L
), we can construct a family of Iwasawa cohomology classes in which the local data (the choice of Eisenstein class, and the choice of map from the cohomology of the infinite-level Shimura variety to
V
) are determined by
(w_{S},\Phi_{S})
. By definition, the classes
{}_{c}\!\AF_{\mathrm{cy},m}(\Pi)
are the output of this construction if we take
(w_{S},\Phi_{S})
to be the “imprimitive test data” of
[
GLZ23
, §7.3.2]
, so
w_{S}
is the normalised new vector, and
\Phi_{S}
a suitable multiple of the indicator function of
(0,1)+N_{f}\mathbf{Z}_{S}^{2}
. However, we may repeat the construction with any choice of test data; and the resulting system of classes will have denominators bounded uniformly in
m
(depending only on the denominators of
w_{S}
and
\Phi_{S}
relative to some lattice), and will satisfy the same norm relations as
{}_{c}\!\AF_{\mathrm{cy},m}(\Pi)
as
m
varies, so we can modify them to obtain the exact Euler system norm relation as above. This construction defines a map from test data at the primes in
S
to Euler systems.

If we choose a character
\lambda
of
\Gamma^{\mathrm{cy}}
, then the image of the above class under evaluation at
\lambda
(giving an element of
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},V(\lambda^{-1}))
) will satisfy an equivariance property: for each
\ell\in S
it will transform by an unramified character under the action of
H(\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})
on
w_{\ell}
and
\Phi_{\ell}
. From the multiplicity-one results proved in
[
Loe21
]
, the maximal quotient of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{Q}_{S}^{2})\otimes\Pi_{S}
on which
H
acts via this character is 1-dimensional, and we can construct a non-zero linear functional on this one-dimensional quotient as the product of the local Asai zeta-integrals. By definition, evaluating
\prod_{\ell}C_{\ell}(\ell^{-k-2}\sigma_{\ell}^{-1})
at
\lambda
gives the ratio of the values of the Asai zeta-integral at the stanard imprimitive test data, and at an optimal test vector. So the cohomology classes arising from the standard imprimitive test data, and the optimal test data, must stand in this same ratio. Since this holds for every
\lambda
, the underlying Iwasawa cohomology classes are related by
\prod_{\ell}C_{\ell}(\ell^{-k-2}\sigma_{\ell}^{-1})
. So if we define
{}_{c}\widehat{\AF}{}_{\mathrm{cy},1}^{\mathrm{pr}}(\Pi)
as the Euler system arising from an optimal test vector, scaled by a power of
p
to cancel out its denominator, we obtain the stated result.
∎

Remark 3.4.2
.

We do not know if the (inexplicit) test data which give the primitive Asai Euler factors are contained in the lattice in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{Q}_{S}^{2})\otimes\Pi_{S}
which gives rise to integral cohomology classes. (See
[
Gro25
]
for a partial result in this direction, covering primes
\ell\in S
that split in
F
.) So we can construct Euler system classes without denominators, or Euler system classes with optimal local factors at the bad primes; but not both at once.

However, since the polynomials
C_{\ell}
all have constant term 1, the correction term
\prod_{\ell}C_{\ell}(\ell^{-(k+2)}\sigma_{\ell}^{-1})
is coprime to
p
in the Iwasawa algebra. Since our main theorem concerns characteristic ideals, which are insensitive to “codimension
>1
” information, the above result will be sufficient for our purposes.

4.

4.1.
Special elements in the image

Let

be a big image prime for
f
, and

any quadratic extension in which

is unramified. (In this section we do not

to be ordinary or
p

Lemma 4.1.1
.

There exists an element
\tau\in\Gal(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}/\mathbf{Q})
such that

•

\tau
acts trivially on
\mathbf{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})
,

•

\tau
maps to the nontrivial element of

•

\rho_{f,v}(\tau)
is conjugate in
\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
to
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\

Proof.

It suffices to note that

are linearly disjoint over

(since one is unramified at

For

, let
\mathcal{K}_{r}
denote the composite of

and the splitting field of the representation

; and let

. Note that

of

and

4.2.
Vanishing of some residual Galois cohomology groups

Proposition 4.2.1

(Hertzig)
.

Let
q
be an odd prime power. If
q\neq 5
, then we have
H^{1}\left(\SL_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{q}),\ad\right)=0
, where
\ad
denotes the adjoint representation on
\mathfrak{sl}_{2,\mathbf{F}_{q}}
. If

, then this space is 1-dimensional over
\mathbf{F}_{5}
, and
\GL_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{q})
acts on it by the character
\det^{2}
.

Proof.

Theorem 1 (i) of
[
Her69
]
shows that
H^{1}(\SL_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{q}),\ad)
vanishes for
q\neq 5
. The case

can be verified by a direct calculation.
∎

Now suppose
v
is a big-image prime for
f
, with residue field
\mathbf{F}_{q}
; and let
\mathcal{K}_{1}=\mathbf{Q}(\bar{\rho},\mu_{p})
, as above.

Proposition 4.2.2
.

Let

. If
q=5
, suppose that
j\neq 2\pmod{4}
. Then we have
H^{1}\left(\mathcal{K}_{1}/\mathbf{Q},\ad(\bar{\rho})(j)\right)=0
. In the exceptional case
(q,j)=(5,2)
this group has dimension 1 over
\mathbf{F}_{5}
.

Proof.

Let us temporarily write
\Gamma
for
\Gal(\mathcal{K}_{1}/\mathbf{Q})
, and
\Delta
for the normal subgroup
\Gal(\mathcal{K}_{1}/\mathbf{Q}(\mu_{p}))
. Evidently
\bar{\rho}
gives a map

. The restriction of this map to
\Delta
is injective, and takes values in
\SL_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{q})
. On the other hand, since
\Gamma/\Delta
is abelian,
\bar{\rho}(\Delta)
must contain the derived subgroup of
\operatorname{Im}(\bar{\rho})
. For

, the group
\SL_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{q})
is equal to its own derived subgroup, so we have
\bar{\rho}(\Delta)=\SL_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{q})
. For

this is not true (

is solvable), but since

is odd, our hypotheses force

to be all of

, which does have

as its derived subgroup, and hence
\bar{\rho}(\Delta)=\SL_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{q})

The inflation-restriction sequence for

now reads

0\to H^{1}(\Gamma/\Delta,M^{\Delta})\to H^{1}(\Gamma,M)\to H^{1}(\Delta,M)^{\Gamma/\Delta}.

The first term is evidently 0, since

has order coprime to

. We have just seen that

for

, we have seen that

, and

acts via

is the mod

for an even integer

, and accordingly the invariants are zero if (and only if)

.
∎

Remark 4.2.3
.

We will only use this lemma for

and

. The

) is

. The

We now lift to the

4.4.

From now on we assume

.

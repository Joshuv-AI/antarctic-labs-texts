Bivariate distributions on the unit square: Theoretical properties and applications
Roberto Vila, Narayanaswamy Balakrishnan, Helton Saulo, Peter Zörnig

Abstract
We introduce the bivariate unit-log-symmetric model based on the bivariate log-symmetric distribution (BLS) defined in Vila et al., 2022 as a flexible family of bivariate distributions over the unit square. We then study its mathematical properties such as stochastic representations, quantiles, conditional distributions, independence of the marginal distributions and moments. Maximum likelihood estimation method is discussed and examined through Monte Carlo simulation. Finally, the proposed model is used to analyze soccer data.

1
Introduction

Bivariate distributions over the unit-square have been discussed in detail in the literature. Many of them are based on beta distribution and its generalizations; see
Arnold and Ng, 2011
and
Nadarajah et al., 2017
. Models of this type have been studied since the 1980s. Some other distributions on the unit square are based on generalized arcsine and inverse Gaussian distributions. A recent model, the bivariate unit-sinh-normal distribution, is based on the bivariate Birnbaum-Saunders distribution; see
Martínez-Flórez et al., 2022
. Bivariate distributions over the unit square arise naturally in comparing indices, rates or proportions in the interval
(0,1)
.

In this paper, we study the bivariate unit-log-symmetric (BULS) distribution defined over the unit-square, obtained as a modification of the bivariate log-symmetric (BLS) distribution introduced by
Vila et al., 2022
. The definitions of BLS and BULS distributions are given in Section
2
, along with some special cases of BULS. In Section
3
, we discuss some properties of the new model, including a stochastic representation, marginal quantiles, and the conditional distributions of BULS. We derive more compact formulas for the conditional densities, using the distribution functions of normal, Student-
t
, hyperbolic, Laplace and slash distributions.
One of the uses of having closed formulas for the conditional densities (of the BULS model), for example, is in studying Heckman-type selection models
(
Heckman, 1979
)
when the selection variables have bounded support. In addition, we derive the distribution of the squared Mahalanobis distance of a random vector
\bm{W}=(W_{1},W_{2})^{\top}
with BULS distribution, and present a necessary condition for the independence of the components of
\bm{W}
and formulas for the moments of
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
. In Section
4
, the log-likelihood function and the likelihood equations for the BULS distribution are presented. In Section
5
, we carry out a Monte Carlo simulation study to evaluate the performance of the ML estimators by means of their bias, root mean square error and coverage probability. In Section
6
, we present two applications to soccer data. Specifically, in Section
6.1
, we model the vector
\bm{W}=(W_{1},W_{2})^{\top}
, where
W_{1}
represents the time elapsed until a first kick goal (of any team) and the time elapsed until a goal of any type of the home team, and show that specific BULS distributions are suitable for modelling
\bm{W}
. In Section
6.2
, we consider the data of 2022 FIFA World Cup wherein the components of the vector
\bm{W}
represent the pass completion proportions of medium passes (14 to 18 meters) and long passes (longer than 37 meters). We then demonstrate that these data can also be fitted well by BULS distributions.

2
Bivariate unit-log-symmetric model

In this section, we describe the bivariate unit-log-symmetric model (BULS). To define this model, we first need to describe the bivariate log-symmetric distribution (BLS) defined in
Vila et al., 2022
.

2.1
BLS distribution

Following
Vila et al., 2022
, a continuous random vector
\bm{T}=(T_{1},T_{2})^{\top}
is said to have a bivariate log-symmetric (BLS) distribution if its joint probability density function (PDF) is given by

\displaystyle f_{T_{1},T_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2};\bm{\theta})={1\over t_{1}t_{2}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}Z_{g_{c}}}\,g_{c}\Biggl({\widetilde{t_{1}}^{2}-2\rho\widetilde{t_{1}}\widetilde{t_{2}}+\widetilde{t_{2}}^{2}\over 1-\rho^{2}}\Biggr),\quad t_{1},t_{2}>0,

(2.1)

where

\widetilde{t_{i}}=\log[({t_{i}/\eta_{i}})^{1/\sigma_{i}}],\ \eta_{i}=\exp(\mu_{i}),\ i=1,2
,
with
\bm{\theta}=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\rho)^{\top}
being the parameter vector,
\mu_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\sigma_{i}>0
,
i=1,2
and
\rho\in(-1,1)
. Furthermore,
Z_{g_{c}}>0
is the partition function, that is,

\displaystyle Z_{g_{c}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}{1\over t_{1}t_{2}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}}\,g_{c}\Biggl({\widetilde{t_{1}}^{2}-2\rho\widetilde{t_{1}}\widetilde{t_{2}}+\widetilde{t_{2}}^{2}\over 1-\rho^{2}}\Biggr)\,{\rm d}t_{1}{\rm d}t_{2}=\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}g_{c}(u)\,{\rm d}u,

(2.2)

and
g_{c}
is a scalar function referred to as the density generator
(
Fang et al., 1990
, see)
. The second integral in (
2.2
) is consequence of a change of variables; for more details, see Proposition 3.1 of
Vila et al., 2022
.
When a random vector
\bm{T}
is BLS distributed, with parameter vector
\bm{\theta}
, we denote it by
\bm{T}\sim{\rm BLS}(\bm{\theta},g_{c})
.

2.2
BULS distribution

We say that a continuous random vector
\bm{W}=(W_{1},W_{2})^{\top}
has a bivariate unit-log-symmetric (BULS) distribution with parameter vector
\bm{\theta}=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\rho)^{\top}
, denoted by
\bm{W}\sim{\rm BULS}(\bm{\theta},g_{c})
, if its PDF is, for
0<w_{1},w_{2}<1
, given by

\displaystyle f_{W_{1},W_{2}}(w_{1},w_{2};\bm{\theta})={1\over(1-w_{1})t_{1}\sigma_{1}(1-w_{2})t_{2}\sigma_{2}\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}Z_{g_{c}}}\,{\displaystyle g_{c}\Biggl({\widetilde{w}_{1}^{2}-2\rho\widetilde{w}_{1}\widetilde{w}_{2}+\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2}\over 1-\rho^{2}}\Biggr)},

(2.3)

where
\widetilde{w}_{i}=\log[({t_{i}/\eta_{i}})^{1/\sigma_{i}}],\ t_{i}=-\log(1-w_{i}),\ \eta_{i}=\exp(\mu_{i}),\ i=1,2
,
with

\sigma_{i}>0
,
i=1,2
,

, and

and

3
Some basic properties of the model

In this section, some mathematical properties of the bivariate unit-log-symmetric distribution are established.

3.1
Stochastic representation

Proposition 3.1
.

The random vector
\bm{W}=(W_{1},W_{2})^{\top}
has a BULS distribution if

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lllll}&W_{1}=1-\exp\big[-\eta_{1}\exp(\sigma_{1}Z_{1})\big],\\[11.38092pt]
&W_{2}=1-\exp\big[-\eta_{2}\exp\big(\sigma_{2}{\rho}Z_{1}+\sigma_{2}\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}Z_{2}\big)\big],\end{array}

where
Z_{1}=RDU_{1}
and
Z_{2}=R\sqrt{1-D^{2}}U_{2}
with

U_{1}
,
U_{2}
,
R
, and
D
being mutually independent random variables,
\rho\in(-1,1)
,
\eta_{i}=\exp(\mu_{i})
, and
\mathbb{P}(U_{i}=-1)=\mathbb{P}(U_{i}=1)=1/2
,
i=1,2
. The random variable
D
is positive and has PDF

f_{D}(d)={2/(\pi\sqrt{1-d^{2}})},\ d\in(0,1).

Further, the positive random variable
R

has its PDF as

f_{R}(r)={2rg_{c}(r^{2})/\int_{0}^{\infty}g_{c}(u)\,{\rm d}{u}},\ r>0.

Proof.

It is well-known that
(
Vila et al., 2022
, see Proposition 3.2 of)
the random vector
\bm{T}=(T_{1},T_{2})^{\top}
has a BLS distribution if

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lllll}&T_{1}=\eta_{1}\exp(\sigma_{1}Z_{1}),\\[11.38092pt]
&T_{2}=\eta_{2}\exp\big(\sigma_{2}{\rho}Z_{1}+\sigma_{2}\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}Z_{2}\big).\end{array}

Moreover, from (
2.4
),
W_{i}=1-\exp(-T_{i})
,
i=1,2
. Hence, the result.
∎

The following lemma provides a slight simplification in the representation of Proposition
3.1
. This result plays a fundamental role in the next subsections, since all the probabilistic characteristics that depend on the distribution of
\rho Z_{1}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}Z_{2}
will be simplified since it has the same distribution as
Z_{2}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For a Borelian subset
B
of
(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\rho Z_{1}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}Z_{2}\in{B}\right)=\mathbb{P}(Z_{2}\in B).

In other words,
\rho Z_{1}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}Z_{2}
and
Z_{2}
have the same distribution.

Proof.

It is clear that the density of
\rho Z_{1}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}{Z_{2}}
is related to the joint density
f_{Z_{1},Z_{2}}
by

\displaystyle f_{\rho Z_{1}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}{Z_{2}}}(s_{2})={1\over\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{Z_{1},Z_{2}}\left(z,\dfrac{s_{2}-\rho z}{\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}}\right){\rm d}z.

(3.3)

From Eq. (13) of
Saulo et al., 2023
, the joint PDF of
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
is given by

\displaystyle f_{Z_{1},Z_{2}}(x,y)={1\over Z_{g_{c}}}\,g_{c}(x^{2}+y^{2}),\quad-\infty<x,y<\infty,

(3.4)

and so the integral in (
3.3
) is

\displaystyle={1\over\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}Z_{g_{c}}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g_{c}\left(z^{2}+\bigg(\dfrac{s_{2}-\rho z}{\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}}\bigg)^{2}\right){\rm d}z.

(3.5)

Using the identity

\displaystyle z^{2}+\bigg(\dfrac{s_{2}-\rho z}{\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}}\bigg)^{2}=\dfrac{z^{2}-2\rho zs_{2}+s_{2}^{2}}{1-\rho^{2}}=\bigg(\dfrac{z-\rho s_{2}}{\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}}\bigg)^{2}+s_{2}^{2}

the integral in (
3.5
) is written as

\displaystyle={1\over\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}Z_{g_{c}}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g_{c}\left(\bigg(\dfrac{z-\rho s_{2}}{\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}}\bigg)^{2}+s_{2}^{2}\right){\rm d}z.

Making the change of variables

s_{1}={(z-\rho s_{2})}/{\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}}
,
the above integral is

\displaystyle={1\over Z_{g_{c}}}\,\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g_{c}(s_{1}^{2}+s_{2}^{2})\,{\rm d}s_{1}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{Z_{1},Z_{2}}(s_{1},s_{2})\,{\rm d}s_{1},

where, in the last line, we have used (
3.4
). Hence,

\displaystyle f_{\rho Z_{1}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}{Z_{2}}}(s_{2})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{Z_{1},Z_{2}}(s_{1},s_{2})\,{\rm d}s_{1}=f_{Z_{2}}(s_{2}).

(3.6)

Now, from (
3.6
), it is clear that
\rho Z_{1}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}Z_{2}
and
Z_{2}
are equal in distribution.
∎

3.2
Marginal Quantiles

Given
p\in(0,1)
, let
Q_{W_{i}}(p)
be the
p
-quantile of
W_{i}
, for
i=1,2
.
By using the stochastic representation in Proposition
3.1
, for
\bm{W}=(W_{1},W_{2})^{\top}\sim{\rm BULS}(\bm{\theta},g_{c})
, we have

\displaystyle p=\mathbb{P}(W_{1}\leqslant Q_{W_{1}}(p))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\big(1-\exp\big[-\eta_{1}\exp(\sigma_{1}Z_{1})\big]\leqslant Q_{W_{1}}(p)\big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{1}\leqslant\log\biggl[\left(-{\log(1-Q_{W_{1}}(p))\over\eta_{1}}\right)^{1/\sigma_{1}}\biggl]\right)

and

\displaystyle p=\mathbb{P}(W_{2}\leqslant Q_{W_{2}}(p))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(1-\exp\left[-\eta_{2}\exp\left(\sigma_{2}{\rho}Z_{1}+\sigma_{2}\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}Z_{2}\right)\right]\leqslant Q_{W_{2}}(p)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left({\rho}Z_{1}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}Z_{2}\leqslant\log\biggl[\left(-{\log(1-Q_{W_{2}}(p))\over\eta_{2}}\right)^{1/\sigma_{2}}\biggl]\right).

Hence, the
p
-quantiles
Q_{Z_{1}}(p)
and
Q_{Z_{2}}(p)
of
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
, respectively, are such that

\displaystyle\log\biggl[\left(-{\log(1-Q_{W_{1}}(p))\over\eta_{1}}\right)^{1/\sigma_{1}}\biggl]=Q_{Z_{1}}(p)

and

\displaystyle\log\biggl[\left(-{\log(1-Q_{W_{2}}(p))\over\eta_{2}}\right)^{1/\sigma_{2}}\biggl]=Q_{{\rho}Z_{1}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}Z_{2}}(p)=Q_{Z_{2}}(p),

where in the last equality we used that
\rho Z_{1}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}Z_{2}
and
Z_{2}
have the same distribution (see Lemma
3.2
).
Hence, the
p
-quantiles
Q_{W_{1}}(p)
and
Q_{W_{2}}(p)
are given by

\displaystyle Q_{W_{1}}(p)

\displaystyle=1-\exp\big[-\eta_{1}\exp(\sigma_{1}Q_{Z_{1}}(p))\big],

\displaystyle Q_{W_{2}}(p)

\displaystyle=1-\exp\big[-\eta_{2}\exp\big(\sigma_{2}Q_{Z_{2}}(p)\big)\big],

respectively.

3.3
Conditional distributions

Before enunciating and proving the main result (Theorem
3.4
) of this subsection, we prove the following technical lemma which will be indispensable in the sequel.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
\bm{W}=(W_{1},W_{2})^{\top}\sim{\rm BULS}(\bm{\theta},g_{c})
, then
the PDF of
W_{2}\,|\,(W_{1}=w_{1})
is given by

\displaystyle f_{W_{2}}(w_{2}\,|\,W_{1}=w_{1})={1\over(1-w_{2})t_{2}\sigma_{2}\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}}\,{f_{Z_{2}}\biggl({1\over\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}}\,(\widetilde{w}_{2}-\rho\widetilde{w}_{1})\,\bigg|\,Z_{1}=\widetilde{w}_{1}\biggr)},

(3.7)

where
\widetilde{w}_{i}
,
i=1,2
, and
t_{2}
are as defined in (
2.3
), and
{Z_{1}}
and
{Z_{2}}
are as given in Proposition
3.1
.

Proof.

If
W_{1}=w_{1}
, then

Z_{1}=\log\big[\left(-{\log(1-w_{1})/\eta_{1}}\right)^{1/\sigma_{1}}\big]=\widetilde{w}_{1}
.
So, the conditional distribution of
W_{2}
, given
W_{1}=w_{1}
, is the same as the distribution of

\displaystyle 1-\exp\left[-\eta_{2}\exp\left(\sigma_{2}{\rho}\widetilde{w}_{1}+\sigma_{2}\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}Z_{2}\right)\right]\,\bigg|\,W_{1}=w_{1}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle F_{W_{2}}(w_{2}\,|\,W_{1}=w_{1})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(1-\exp\left[-\eta_{2}\exp\left(\sigma_{2}{\rho}\widetilde{w}_{1}+\sigma_{2}\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}Z_{2}\right)\right]\leqslant w_{2}\,\bigg|\,W_{1}=w_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\biggl(Z_{2}\leqslant{1\over\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}}\,(\widetilde{w}_{2}-{\rho}\widetilde{w}_{1})\,\bigg|\,Z_{1}=\widetilde{w}_{1}\biggr).

Then, by differentiating
F_{W_{2}}(w_{2}\,|\,W_{1}=w_{1})
with respect to
w_{2}
, (
3.7
) is readily obtained.
∎

The following result
provides a simple formula for determining the conditional distribution of
W_{1}
, given
W_{2}\in B
, whenever the marginal and conditional distributions of
\bm{W}=(W_{1},W_{2})^{\top}\sim{\rm BULS}(\bm{\theta},g_{c})
are known.
This result is essential for studying Heckman-type selection models
(

Theorem 3.4
.

For a Borelian subset
B
of
(0,1)
, let us define the following Borelian set:

\displaystyle{B}_{r}={1\over\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\,\log\biggl[\left(-{\log(1-B)\over\eta_{2}}\right)^{1/\sigma_{2}}\biggr]-{r\over\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\,\widetilde{w}_{1},\quad-1<r<1,

(3.8)

where
\widetilde{w}_{1}
is as in (
2.3
).
If
\bm{W}\sim{\rm BULS}(\bm{\theta},g_{c})
, then the PDF of
W_{1}\,|\,(W_{2}\in B)
is given by

\displaystyle f_{W_{1}}(w_{1}\,|\,W_{2}\in B)={1\over(1-w_{1})t_{1}\sigma_{1}}\,f_{Z_{1}}(\widetilde{w}_{1})\,{\mathbb{P}(Z_{2}\in{B}_{\rho}\,|\,Z_{1}=\widetilde{w}_{1})\over\mathbb{P}(Z_{2}\in B_{0})},

in which

t_{1}
is as in (
2.3
),

{B}_{r}
is as in (
3.8
), and
{Z_{1}}
and
{Z_{2}}
are as given in Proposition
3.1
.

Proof.

Let
B
be a Borelian subset of
(0,1)
. Note that

\displaystyle f_{W_{1}}(w_{1}\,|\,W_{2}\in B)=f_{W_{1}}(w_{1})\,{\int_{B}f_{W_{2}}(w_{2}\,|\,W_{1}=w_{1})\,{\rm d}w_{2}\over\mathbb{P}(W_{2}\in B)}.

As
f_{W_{1}}(w_{1})=f_{Z_{1}}(\widetilde{w}_{1})/[(1-w_{1})t_{1}\sigma_{1}]
and
\mathbb{P}(W_{2}\in B)=\mathbb{P}\big(\rho Z_{1}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}Z_{2}\in{B}_{0}\big)
, where
{B}_{0}
is as given in (
3.8
) with
r=0
, the term on the right-hand side of the above identity is

\displaystyle={1\over(1-w_{1})t_{1}\sigma_{1}}\,f_{Z_{1}}(\widetilde{w}_{1})\,{\int_{B}f_{W_{2}}(w_{2}\,|\,W_{1}=w_{1})\,{\rm d}w_{2}\over\mathbb{P}\big(\rho Z_{1}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}Z_{2}\in{B}_{0}\big)}.

By using the formula for
f_{W_{2}}(w_{2}|W_{1}=w_{1})
provided in Lemma
3.3
, the above expression is

\displaystyle={1\over(1-w_{1})t_{1}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}}\,f_{Z_{1}}(\widetilde{w}_{1})\,{\int_{B}{1\over(1-w_{2})t_{2}}\,f_{Z_{2}}\Big({1\over\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}}\,\widetilde{w}_{2}-{\rho\over\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}}\,\widetilde{w}_{1}\,\Big|\,Z_{1}=\widetilde{w}_{1}\Big)\,{\rm d}w_{2}\over\mathbb{P}\big(\rho Z_{1}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}Z_{2}\in{B}_{0}\big)},

where
\widetilde{w}_{i}
and
t_{i}
,
i=1,2
, are as in (
2.3
).
Finally, by applying the change of variable
z=(\widetilde{w}_{2}-{\rho}\,\widetilde{w}_{1})/\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}
, the above expression is

\displaystyle={1\over(1-w_{1})t_{1}\sigma_{1}}\,f_{Z_{1}}(\widetilde{w}_{1})\,{\int_{{B}_{\rho}}f_{Z_{2}}(z\,|\,Z_{1}=\widetilde{w}_{1})\,{\rm d}z\over\mathbb{P}\big(\rho Z_{1}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}Z_{2}\in{B}_{0}\big)}.

We have thus proved that

\displaystyle f_{W_{1}}(w_{1}\,|\,W_{2}\in B)={1\over(1-w_{1})t_{1}\sigma_{1}}\,f_{Z_{1}}(\widetilde{w}_{1})\,{\int_{{B}_{\rho}}f_{Z_{2}}(z\,|\,Z_{1}=\widetilde{w}_{1})\,{\rm d}z\over\mathbb{P}\big(\rho Z_{1}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}Z_{2}\in{B}_{0}\big)}.

Finally, by combining the above identity with Lemma
3.2
, the required result follows.
∎

3.5
Independence

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\bm{W}=(W_{1},W_{2})^{\top}\sim{\rm BULS}(\bm{\theta},g_{c})
. If
\rho=0
and the density generator
g_{c}
in (
2.3
) is such that

\displaystyle g_{c}\big(x^{2}+y^{2}\big)=g_{c_{1}}\big(x^{2}\big)g_{c_{2}}\big(y^{2}\big),\quad\forall(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

(3.9)

for some density generators
g_{c_{1}}
and
g_{c_{2}}
, then
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
are independent.

Proof.

The proof follows the same steps as the proof of Proposition 3.11 of
Vila et al., 2022
. For the sake of completeness, however, we present it here.

Let
\rho=0
. From (
3.9
), the joint density (
2.3
) of
(W_{1},W_{2})
is such that

\displaystyle f_{W_{1},W_{2}}(w_{1},w_{2};\bm{\theta})={Z_{g_{c_{1}}}Z_{g_{c_{2}}}\over Z_{g_{c}}}\,f_{1}(w_{1};\mu_{1},\sigma_{1})f_{2}(w_{2};\mu_{2},\sigma_{2}),\quad\forall(w_{1},w_{2})\in(0,1)\times(0,1),

(3.10)

where

f_{i}(w_{i};\mu_{i},\sigma_{i})=g_{c_{i}}\bigl(\widetilde{w_{i}}^{2}\big)/[(1-w_{i})t_{i}\sigma_{i}Z_{g_{c_{i}}}],\ 0<w_{i}<1

Z_{g_{c_{i}}}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g_{c_{i}}\big({z_{i}}^{2}\big)\,{\rm d}z_{i},\ i=1,2,

and
\widetilde{w_{i}}
and
t_{i}
are as in (
2.3
).
Integrating (
3.10
) in terms of
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle{Z_{g_{c_{1}}}Z_{g_{c_{2}}}\over Z_{g_{c}}}=1,

and consequently,
Z_{g_{c}}=Z_{g_{c_{1}}}Z_{g_{c_{2}}}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle f_{W_{1},W_{2}}(w_{1},w_{2};\bm{\theta})=f_{1}(w_{1};\mu_{1},\sigma_{1})f_{2}(w_{2};\mu_{2},\sigma_{2}),\quad\forall(w_{1},w_{2})\in(0,1)\times(0,1).

)

W_{1}
and

are statistically independent, and even more,
f_{i}=f_{W_{i}}

James, 2004

.

4
Maximum likelihood estimation

Let
\{(W_{1i},W_{2i})^{\top}:i=1,\ldots,n\}
be a bivariate random sample of size
n
from the
{\rm BULS}(\bm{\theta},g_{c})
distribution with PDF as in (
2.3
), and let
(w_{1i},w_{2i})^{\top}
be the corresponding observations of
(W_{1i},W_{2i})^{\top}
. Then, the log-likelihood function for
\bm{\theta}=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\rho)^{\top}
,
without the additive constant, is given by

\displaystyle\ell(\bm{\theta})=-n\sum_{i=1}^{2}\log(\sigma_{i})-{n\over 2}\,\log\big({1-\rho^{2}}\big)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log g_{c}\Biggl({\widetilde{w}_{1i}^{2}-2\rho\widetilde{w}_{1i}\widetilde{w}_{2i}+\widetilde{w}_{2i}^{2}\over 1-\rho^{2}}\Biggr),\quad 0<w_{1i},w_{2i}<1,

where
\widetilde{w}_{ki}=\log[({t_{ki}/\eta_{k}})^{1/\sigma_{k}}],\ t_{ki}=-\log(1-w_{ki})>0
and
\eta_{k}=\exp(\mu_{k}),\ k=1,2;\ i=1,\ldots,n.

In the case when a supremum
\widehat{\bm{\theta}}=(\widehat{\eta_{1}},\widehat{\eta_{2}},\widehat{\sigma_{1}},\widehat{\sigma_{2}},\widehat{\rho})^{\top}
exists, it must satisfy the following likelihood equations:

\displaystyle{\partial\ell({\bm{\theta}})\over\partial\eta_{1}}\bigg|_{{\bm{\theta}}=\widehat{\bm{\theta}}}=0,\quad{\partial\ell(\bm{\theta})\over\partial\eta_{2}}=0,\quad{\partial\ell(\bm{\theta})\over\partial\sigma_{1}}\bigg|_{{\bm{\theta}}=\widehat{\bm{\theta}}}=0,\quad{\partial\ell(\bm{\theta})\over\partial\sigma_{2}}\bigg|_{{\bm{\theta}}=\widehat{\bm{\theta}}}=0,\quad{\partial\ell(\bm{\theta})\over\partial\rho}\bigg|_{{\bm{\theta}}=\widehat{\bm{\theta}}}=0,

(4.1)

with

\displaystyle{\partial\ell(\bm{\theta})\over\partial\eta_{1}}=\frac{2}{\sigma_{1}\eta_{1}(1-\rho^{2})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(\rho\widetilde{w}_{2i}-\widetilde{w}_{1i}\big)G(\widetilde{w}_{1i},\widetilde{w}_{2i}),

\displaystyle{\partial\ell(\bm{\theta})\over\partial\eta_{2}}=\frac{2}{\sigma_{2}\eta_{2}(1-\rho^{2})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(\rho\widetilde{w}_{1i}-\widetilde{w}_{2i}\big)G(\widetilde{w}_{1i},\widetilde{w}_{2i}),

\displaystyle{\partial\ell(\bm{\theta})\over\partial\sigma_{1}}=-\frac{n}{\sigma_{1}}+{2\over\sigma_{1}(1-\rho^{2})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widetilde{w}_{1i}\big(\rho\widetilde{w}_{2i}-\widetilde{w}_{1i}\big)G(\widetilde{w}_{1i},\widetilde{w}_{2i}),

\displaystyle{\partial\ell(\bm{\theta})\over\partial\sigma_{2}}=-\frac{n}{\sigma_{2}}+{2\over\sigma_{2}(1-\rho^{2})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widetilde{w}_{2i}\big(\rho\widetilde{w}_{1i}-\widetilde{w}_{2i}\big)G(\widetilde{w}_{1i},\widetilde{w}_{2i}),

\displaystyle{\partial\ell(\bm{\theta})\over\partial\rho}={n\rho\over 1-\rho^{2}}-{2\over(1-\rho^{2})^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(\rho\widetilde{w}_{1i}-\widetilde{w}_{2i}\big)\big(\rho\widetilde{w}_{2i}-\widetilde{w}_{1i}\big)G(\widetilde{w}_{1i},\widetilde{w}_{2i}),

(4.2)

where we have used the notation

\displaystyle G(\widetilde{w}_{1i},\widetilde{w}_{2i})={g_{c}^{\prime}(x_{\rho,i})\over g_{c}(x_{\rho,i})},

(4.3)

with
x_{\rho,i}={(\widetilde{w}_{1i}^{2}-2\rho\widetilde{w}_{1i}\widetilde{w}_{2i}+\widetilde{w}_{2i}^{2})/(1-\rho^{2})},\ i=1,\ldots,n.

Observe that the likelihood equations in (
4.1
) can be written as

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widetilde{w}_{1i}\,G(\widetilde{w}_{1i},\widetilde{w}_{2i})\bigg|_{{\bm{\theta}}=\widehat{\bm{\theta}}}=0,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(\widetilde{w}_{1i}^{2}-\widetilde{w}_{2i}^{2}\big)\,G(\widetilde{w}_{1i},\widetilde{w}_{2i})\bigg|_{{\bm{\theta}}=\widehat{\bm{\theta}}}=0,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widetilde{w}_{2i}\left[2\rho\widetilde{w}_{2i}-(1+\rho^{2})\widetilde{w}_{1i}\right]G(\widetilde{w}_{1i},\widetilde{w}_{2i})\bigg|_{{\bm{\theta}}=\widehat{\bm{\theta}}}=-{n\widehat{\rho}(1-\widehat{\rho}^{2})\over 2}\,.

Any nontrivial root
\widehat{\bm{\theta}}
of the above likelihood equations is an ML estimator in the loose sense. When the parameter value provides the absolute maximum of the log-likelihood function, it becomes the ML estimator in the strict sense.

In the following proposition, we discuss the existence of the ML estimator
\widehat{\rho}
when all other parameters are known.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
g_{c}
be a density generator such that

\displaystyle g^{\prime}_{c}(x)=r(x)g_{c}(x),\quad-\infty<x<\infty,

(4.4)

for some real-valued function
r(x)
with
\lim_{\rho\to\pm 1}r(x_{\rho,i})=c\in(-\infty,0)
, where
x_{\rho,i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, are as in (
4.3
).
If the parameters
\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are all known, then (
4.2
) has at least one root in the interval
(-1,1)
.

Proof.

The proof of this result follows by direct application of Intermediate value theorem. For more details, see Proposition 5.1. of
Vila et al., 2022
.
∎

For the BULS model, no closed-form solution to the maximization problem is available, and an MLE can only be found by means of numerical optimization. Under mild regularity conditions
(
Cox and Hinkley, 1974
;
Davison, 2008
)
, the asymptotic distribution of the ML estimator
\widehat{\bm{\theta}}
of
\bm{\theta}
is as follows:
(\widehat{\bm{\theta}}-\bm{\theta})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{D}}}{{\longrightarrow}}N(\bm{0},I^{-1}(\bm{\theta}))
,
where

\bm{0}
is the zero mean vector and
I^{-1}(\bm{\theta})
is the inverse expected
Fisher information matrix.
The main use of the last convergence is to construct confidence regions and to perform
hypothesis testing for
\bm{\theta}

(
Davison, 2008
)
.

5
Simulation study

In this section, we carry out a Monte Carlo simulation study for evaluating the performance of the ML estimators of the parameters of BULS distributions. For illustration purposes, we only present results for the bivariate unit-log-normal model. The simulation scenario considers is as follows: 1,000 Monte Carlo replications, sample size
n\in(25,100,500,700)
, vector of true parameters
(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})=(1,1,0.5,0.5)
,
\rho\in\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,0.95\}
(negative values of
\rho
produce the same results and so are omitted). To study the performance of the ML estimators, we computed the bias, root mean square error (RMSE), and coverage probability (CP), defined by

\displaystyle\widehat{\textrm{Bias}}(\widehat{\theta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widehat{\theta}^{(i)}-\theta,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{RMSE}}(\widehat{\theta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\widehat{\theta}^{(i)}-\theta)^{2}}},

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{CP}}(\widehat{\theta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{I}(\theta\in[L^{(i)}_{\widehat{\theta}},U^{(i)}_{\widehat{\theta}}]),

where
\theta
and
\widehat{\theta}^{(i)}
are the true parameter value and its
i
-th ML estimate,
N
is the number of Monte Carlo replications,
\mathcal{I}
is an indicator function taking the value 1 if
\theta\in\left[L^{(i)}_{\widehat{\theta}},U^{(i)}_{\widehat{\theta}}\right]
, and 0 otherwise, where
L^{(i)}_{\widehat{\theta}}
and
U^{(i)}_{\widehat{\theta}}
are the
i
-th upper and lower limit estimates of the 95% confidence interval. We expect that, as the sample size increases, the bias and RMSE would decrease, and the CP would approach the 95% nominal level.

7
Conclusions

In this paper, we have proposed a family of bivariate distributions over the unit square. By suitably defining the density generator, we can transform any distribution over the real line into a bivariate distribution over the region

(0,1)\times(0,1)
. Such a model has several potential applications, since the simultaneous modeling of quantities like proportions, rates or indices frequently arises in applied sciences like economics, medicine, engineering and social sciences. We have discussed several theoretical properties like stochastic representation, quantiles, conditional distributions, independence and moments. We have also carried out a Monte Carlo simulation study and finally demonstrated some applications to soccer data. The present research can be extended in several possible directions. By changing the density generator, numerous special forms of the BULS distribution can be constructed. Furthermore, generalizations to higher dimensions can be studied. We are currently working in these directions and hope to report the findings in a future paper.

Appendix A
Some additional results

For the convenience of readers, we present here some complementary results relating to Section
3.3
.

Definition A.1
.

We say that a random variable
X
follows a univariate generalized hyperbolic (GH) distribution, denoted by
X\sim{\rm GH}(\lambda,\alpha,\delta)
, if its PDF is given by

\displaystyle f_{\rm GH}(x;\lambda,\alpha,\delta)=\dfrac{(\sqrt{\alpha^{2}}/\delta)^{\lambda}}{\sqrt{2\pi}K_{\lambda}(\delta\sqrt{\alpha^{2}}\,)}\,\,\dfrac{K_{\lambda-1/2}(\alpha\sqrt{\delta^{2}+x^{2}})}{(\sqrt{\delta^{2}+x^{2}}/\alpha)^{1/2-\lambda}},\quad-\infty<x<\infty.

Here,
K_{r}
is the modified Bessel function
of the third kind with index
r
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R},\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and

\delta>0
is a scale parameter.

The following result has appeared in a multivariated version in
Deng and Yao, 2018
.

Proposition A.1

(Hyperbolic generator)
.

Let
\bm{Z}=(Z_{1},Z_{2})^{\top}
be a random vector as in Proposition
3.1
.
If
g_{c}(x)=\exp(-\nu\sqrt{1+x}\,)
, then
the conditional distribution of
Z_{2}
, given
Z_{1}=x
, is
{\rm GH}(1,\nu,\sqrt{1+x^{2}}\,)
and both of its unconditional distributions are
{\rm GH}(3/2,\nu,1)
.

Proof.

By using (
3.4
), the joint PDF of
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
is (see Table
1
)

\displaystyle f_{Z_{1},Z_{2}}(x,y)={\nu^{2}\exp(\nu)\over 2\pi(\nu+1)}\,\exp\left(-\nu\sqrt{1+x^{2}+y^{2}}\,\right).

(A.1)

So, the marginal PDF of
Z_{1}
is given by

\displaystyle f_{Z_{1}}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{Z_{1},Z_{2}}(x,y)\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle={\nu^{2}\exp(\nu)\over 2\pi(\nu+1)}\,\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\nu\sqrt{1+x^{2}+y^{2}}\,\right)\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle={2\nu^{2}\exp(\nu)\over 2\pi(\nu+1)}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\nu\sqrt{1+x^{2}+y^{2}}\,\right)\,{\rm d}y.

By using Formula 6 of Section 3.46-3.48 of
Gradshteyn and Ryzhik, 2000
that
\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-a\sqrt{b^{2}+x^{2}}){\rm d}x=bK_{1}(ab)
, the above integral is

\displaystyle={2\nu^{2}\exp(\nu)\over 2\pi(\nu+1)}\,\sqrt{1+x^{2}}K_{1}(\nu\sqrt{1+x^{2}}\,).

(A.2)

Now, as
K_{3/2}(\nu)=2\sqrt{\pi}\exp(-\nu)(\nu+1)(\nu/2)^{3/2}/\nu^{3}
, the above espression becomes

\displaystyle={\nu^{3/2}\over\sqrt{2\pi}K_{3/2}(\nu)}\,{K_{1}(\nu\sqrt{1+x^{2}})\over(\sqrt{1+x^{2}}/\nu\,)^{-1}}=f_{\rm GH}(x;3/2,\nu,1)

which proves that
Z_{1}\sim{\rm GH}(3/2,\nu,1)
. Similarly, we can show that
Z_{2}\sim{\rm GH}(3/2,\nu,1)
, as well.

On the other hand, from (
A.1
) and (
A.2
), and by using the well-known identity
K_{1/2}(z)=\sqrt{\pi/(2z)}\exp(-z)
, the conditional PDF of
Z_{2}
, given
Z_{1}=x
, is obtained as

\displaystyle f_{Z_{2}\,|\,Z_{1}}(y\,|\,x)=\dfrac{\exp(-\nu\sqrt{1+x^{2}+y^{2}}\,)}{2\sqrt{1+x^{2}}K_{1}(\nu\sqrt{1+x^{2}}\,)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\nu/\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}K_{1}(\nu\sqrt{1+x^{2}}\,)}\,\dfrac{K_{1/2}(\nu\sqrt{1+x^{2}+y^{2}}\,)}{(\sqrt{1+x^{2}+y^{2}}/\nu)^{-1/2}}

\displaystyle=f_{\rm GH}(x;1,\nu,\sqrt{1+x^{2}}),

which complete the proof.
∎

The following result has also appeared in a multivariated version in
Kotz et al., 2001
.

Proposition A.2

(Laplace generator)
.

Let
\bm{Z}=(Z_{1},Z_{2})^{\top}
be a random vector as in Proposition
3.1
.
If
g_{c}(x)=K_{0}(\sqrt{2x}\,)
, then
the conditional distribution of
Z_{2}
, given
Z_{1}=x
, is
{\rm GH}(1/2,\sqrt{2},|x|)
and both of its unconditional distributions are
{\rm Laplace}(0,1/\sqrt{2})
.

Proof.

By (
3.4
) and using the definitions of
K_{0}
and
Z_{g_{c}}
in Table
1
, we have

\displaystyle f_{Z_{1},Z_{2}}(x,y)={1\over\pi}\,K_{0}\left(\sqrt{2(x^{2}+y^{2})}\,\right)={1\over 2\pi}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}{1\over t}\exp\left(-t-{x^{2}+y^{2}\over 2t}\right){\rm d}t.

(A.3)

We then find the marginal density of
Z_{1}
to be

\displaystyle f_{Z_{1}}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{Z_{1},Z_{2}}(x,y)\,{\rm d}y={1\over\sqrt{2}}\,\exp(-\sqrt{2}\,|x|)=f_{\rm L}(x),

(A.4)

where
f_{\rm L}(x)=\exp(-\sqrt{2}\,|x|)/\sqrt{2}
is the Laplace PDF with scale parameter
1/\sqrt{2}
;
that is,
Z_{1}\sim{\rm Laplace}(0,1/\sqrt{2})
. Similarly, we can show that
Z_{2}\sim{\rm Laplace}(0,1/\sqrt{2})
, as well.

On the other hand, by using (
A.3
), (
A.4
) and the well-known identity
K_{1/2}(z)=\sqrt{\pi/(2z)}\exp(-z)
, the conditional PDF of
Z_{2}
, given
Z_{1}=x
, is obtained as

\displaystyle f_{Z_{2}\,|\,Z_{1}}(y\,|\,x)=\dfrac{\displaystyle{1\over\pi}\,K_{0}\left(\sqrt{2(x^{2}+y^{2})}\,\right)}{\displaystyle{1\over\sqrt{2}}\exp(-\sqrt{2}\,|x|)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(\sqrt{2}/|x|)^{1/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}K_{1/2}(\sqrt{2}|x|)}\,K_{0}\left(\sqrt{2}\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}\,\right)

\displaystyle=f_{\rm GH}(x;1/2,\sqrt{2},|x|).

Then, from Definition
A.1
, we simply have
Z_{2}\,|\,(Z_{1}=x)\sim{\rm GH}(1/2,\sqrt{2},|x|)
.
∎

Definition A.2
.

We say that a random variable
X
follows an univariate extended slash (ESL) distribution, denoted by
X\sim{\rm ESL}(a,q)
, if its PDF is given by

\displaystyle f_{\rm ESL}(x;a,q)=\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}t^{q}\phi(ta)\phi(tx)\,{\rm d}t}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}u^{q-1}\phi(ua)\,{\rm d}u},\quad-\infty<x<\infty,

where
\phi
denotes the PDF of the standard normal distribution.

If we now choose
a=0
, the classical slash (SL) PDF is obtained, given by

\displaystyle f_{\rm SL}(x;q)

\displaystyle=q\int_{0}^{1}t^{q}\phi(tx)\,{\rm d}t,\quad-\infty<x<\infty

\displaystyle={q\,2^{{q\over 2}-1}\over\sqrt{\pi}}\,|x|^{-(q+1)}\,\gamma\left({q+1\over 2},{x^{2}\over 2}\right).

In this case, we denote it by
X\sim{\rm SL}(q)
.

Proposition A.3

(Slash generator)
.

Let
\bm{Z}=(Z_{1},Z_{2})^{\top}
be a random vector as in Proposition
3.1
.
If

g_{c}(x)=x^{-{(q+2)/2}}\gamma({(q+2)/2},{x/2})
,
then
the conditional distribution of
Z_{2}
, given
Z_{1}=x
, is
{\rm ESL}(x,q+1)
and both of its unconditional distributions are
{\rm SL}(q)
.

Proof.

By (
3.4
) and using the definition of
Z_{g_{c}}
in Table
1
, the joint PDF of
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
is given by

\displaystyle f_{Z_{1},Z_{2}}(x,y)

\displaystyle={q\,2^{{q\over 2}-1}\over\pi}\,(x^{2}+y^{2})^{-{q+2\over 2}}\,\gamma\left({q+2\over 2},{x^{2}+y^{2}\over 2}\right)

\displaystyle=q\int_{0}^{1}t^{q+1}\phi(tx)\phi(ty)\,{\rm d}t.

(A.5)

So, the marginal PDF of
Z_{1}
is

\displaystyle f_{Z_{1}}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{Z_{1},Z_{2}}(x,y)\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=q\int_{0}^{1}t^{q+1}\phi(tx)\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi(ty)\,{\rm d}y\right]{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=q\int_{0}^{1}t^{q}\phi(tx)\,{\rm d}t=f_{\rm SL}(x;q),

(A.6)

which proves that (see Definition
A.2
),
Z_{1}\sim{\rm SL}(q)
. Similarly, we can show that
Z_{2}\sim{\rm SL}(q)
.

On the other hand, by (
A
) and (
A
), the conditional PDF of
Z_{2}
, given
Z_{1}=x
, is obtained as

\displaystyle f_{Z_{2}\,|\,Z_{1}}(y\,|\,x)=\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}t^{q+1}\phi(tx)\phi(ty)\,{\rm d}t}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}u^{q}\phi(ux)\,{\rm d}u}=f_{\rm ESL}(y;x,q+1).

From Definition
A.2
, we then find that
Z_{2}\,|\,(Z_{1}=x)\sim{\rm ESL}(x,q+1)
.
∎

Completeness of Tableau Calculi for Two-Dimensional Hybrid Logics
Yuki Nishimura

Abstract
Hybrid logic is one of the extensions of modal logic. The many-dimensional product of hybrid logic is called hybrid product logic (HPL). We construct a sound and complete tableau calculus for two-dimensional HPL. Also, we made a tableau calculus for hybrid dependent product logic (HdPL), where one dimension depends on the other. In addition, we add a special rule to the tableau calculus for HdPL and show that it is still sound and complete. All of them lack termination, however.



1
Introduction

Hybrid logic is an extension of basic modal logic with additional propositional symbols called nominals and an operator called a satisfaction operator
@
. A nominal is a formula that is true only in a single possible world in a Kripke model. Simply put, we can assume that it is a syntactic name of a possible world. Also, with the satisfaction operator, we can create a formula of the form
@_{i}p
, which means “the proposition
p
holds at point
i
.” Hybrid logic was first invented by Prior
[
20
,
21
]
and has evolved in various ways since then (see
[
25
,
6
,
16
,
10
]
).

For example, consider the following sentence: “Alice publishes a book on September 1.” Using hybrid language, we can write it as
@_{i}p
, where
p=
“Alice will publish a book” and
i=
“It is September 1.” In this way, we can describe one particular point, such as a moment, a place, or a person.

Needless to say,
\square
and
\diamondsuit
are familiar from ordinary languages of modal logic (e.g.,
[
4
]
). On the other hand, nominals can be said to incorporate the idea of “individual constants” in predicate logic. In this respect, hybrid logic is a “hybrid” of modal logic and predicate logic. Furthermore, we can also reinterpret the word “hybrid” from a different perspective. In modal logic,
\square\varphi
and
\diamondsuit\varphi
are local descriptions from the viewpoint of where we are now
[
4
, Preface]
. On the other hand, satisfaction operators, which point a single world, enable us to write a global description from a bird’s-eye view of the model. In this respect, hybrid logic is a hybrid of local and global descriptions
[
5
,
2
]
.

The advantage of hybrid logic is that, when considering modal logic as a “logic of relational structures”
[
4
, Preface]
, hybrid logic is more expressive than modal logic in terms of definability of relational structures, i.e., frame definability. For example, in basic modal logic, there is no axiom corresponding to the irreflexivity of the model
[
12
]
. In hybrid logic, however, we have a simple axiom,
@_{i}\neg\diamondsuit i
, where
i
is a nominal
[
7
]
.

The expansion of hybrid logic to multiple dimensions is called many-dimensional hybrid logic. To illustrate what it is, let us examine one example of two-dimesional hybrid logic, borrowing an idea from
[
22
]
. Consider the following scenario:

It is 10 o’clock, and you are writing a paper in your room on the 1st floor. Then, you get an e-mail: we have an urgent matter to discuss, so please meet in room 1001 on the 10th floor at 12 o’clock. You think 12 o’clock is in the future, and room 1001 is above your room. Thus, the emergency meeting will be held upstairs in the future.

This deduction can be formalized as follows:

@_{i}@_{a}p\land\langle\textsf{Future}\rangle i\land\langle\textsf{Up}\rangle a\rightarrow\langle\textsf{Future}\rangle\langle\textsf{Up}\rangle p,

where
p=
“A meeting is held,”
i=
“It is 12 o’clock,” and
a=
“It is on 10th floor.” In this example, we regard one dimension as time and the other as place. The propositions “It is 12 o’clock” and “It is on the 10th floor” are treated as nominals since they point to one moment and one area, respectively.

The formula
@_{i}@_{a}p
in the example above, combining two kinds of satisfaction operators, describes some properties of a unique world. In this point of view, many-dimensional hybrid logics inherit the globality of orthodox (i.e., one-dimensional) hybrid logic.

However, note that there is a case in which we cannot keep the globality of hybrid logic. In the example above, nominal
i
points to the worlds that are at 12 o’clock, so
@_{i}p
holds only at 12 o’clock. In this case, it is a global expression about the dimension of time. However, this proposition says nothing about where you are. So the proposition
@_{i}p
holds on the 10th floor, but it does not hold in your room on the 1st floor, which loses the globality.

Another interesting example of two-dimesional hybrid logic is Facebook logic
[
24
]
, which multiplies two concepts, personal friendships and knowledge, and describes the influence of each other. For instance, consider this sentence: “I am a friend of Andy, and Andy knows that Alice published a book. Then, one of my friends knows that Alice had published a book.” This inference can be written using the language of Facebook logic, as follows:

\langle\textsf{Friend}\rangle i\land@_{i}[\textsf{Know}]p\rightarrow\langle\textsf{Friend}\rangle[\textsf{Know}]p,

where
p=
“Alice published a book” and
i=
“This is Andy.”

Facebook logic makes the most of the strong points of hybrid logic. Friendship has the property that no one is a friend of themself, and the corresponding property of the binary relation is irreflexivity. As mentioned earlier, this property can be expressed not in the modal language but in the hybrid language. Furthermore, mapping a nominal of the hybrid language to an agent makes it possible to express friendships of specific agents. For example, the formula
@_{i}\langle\textsf{Friend}\rangle j
has the meaning that “
i
has a friend satisfying
j
.” Since the proposition
j
holds for only one agent, i.e.,
j
is the agent’s name, we can interpret this formula as meaning that “
i
is a friend of
j
.”

In particular, when all the dimensions are independent of each other, it is called hybrid product logic (HPL). For two-dimensional HPL, a Hilbert-style axiomatization was made in
[
22
]
. Then, the sequent calculus of HPL was proposed in
[
23
]
. However, there is no tableau calculus for many-dimensional hybrid logic.

Tableau calculi for orthodox hybrid logic have been studied extensively in
[
8
]
(see also
[
9
,
17
,
3
]
). In the case of two-dimensional modal logic, tableau calculi typically use labeled formulae of the form
(x,y):\varphi
instead of ordinary formulae. However, the internalized tableau calculus we propose here treats the labels as part of the hybrid formulae (we write
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi
as the meaning of
(i,a):\varphi
), allowing us to create tableaux in which only non-labeled formulae appear. As this paper will show, this seems to be a natural way to explore HPL, hybrid dependent product logic (HdPL), and their extensions.

In this paper, we have constructed a sound and complete tableau calculus for two-dimensional HPL and also for HdPL, where one dimension depends on the other, based on the method of
[
8
]
. It lacks termination, however, so the decidability of HPL is still an open problem.

One of the main differences to
[
23
]
is how to treat completeness and cut-elimination. In
[
23
]
, the completeness of the sequent calculus was indirectly verified by showing that it is equivalent to the Hilbert system. Moreover, in this paper, the cut-elimination theorem is shown without proof, perhaps because the proof is too complex to write briefly. In contrast, we introduce the cut-free tableau calculi, and their completeness is directly demonstrated by constructing a model that satisfies the root formula from an arbitrary tableau. These properties are helpful since if a formula
\varphi
is not provable, then we can make a counterexample model of
\varphi
.

Besides, this tableau calculus and Sano’s sequent calculus differ for some points. For example, we directly create the proof system of HPL. This is different from
[
23
]
since it constructs two-dimensional hybrid logic whose dimensions depend on each other, and it treats HPL as an application. Another more technical difference is that our rules (specifically
[\mathit{Id}_{n}]
and
[\mathit{Id^{\prime}}_{n}]
) only allow us to change the “label” of nominals, though Equality Rules in
[
23
]
let us replace nominals in an arbitrary occurrence with equivalent ones.

We proceed as follows: Section
2
introduces the semantics of HPL according to
[
22
]
, and Section
3
introduces the tableau calculus of HPL. Then, we show soundness and completeness in Sections
4
and
5
, respectively. Furthermore, in Section
6
, we introduce the semantics and the tableau calculus of HdPL, a logic with dependencies on dimensions, and show that soundness and completeness hold. In particular, in Section
6.4
, we introduce the property “decreasing” of the HdPL model and show that it can be successfully incorporated into the tableau calculus of HdPL.

Kripke Semantics of Hybrid Product Logic

Definition 2.1
.

The vocabulary of HPL is the following:

i)

a countable set
\mathbf{Prop}=\{p,q,r,\dots\}
of propositional variables,

ii)

two disjoint countable sets
\mathbf{Nom}_{1}=\{i,j,k,\dots\}
and
\mathbf{Nom}_{2}=\{a,b,c,\dots\}
of nominals, which are disjoint from
\mathbf{Prop}
,

iii)

two boolean connectives:
\neg
and
\land
,

iv)

two modal operators:
\diamondsuit_{1}
and
\diamondsuit_{2}
,

v)

two kinds of satisfaction operators:
@_{i}(i\in\mathbf{Nom}_{1})
and
@_{a}(a\in\mathbf{Nom}_{2})
.

We denote the set of all nominals by
\mathbf{Nom}
, i.e.,
\mathbf{Nom}=\mathbf{Nom}_{1}\cup\mathbf{Nom}_{2}
.

Definition 2.2
.

The set of formulae of HPL

is defined inductively as follows:

\displaystyle\varphi\Coloneqq p\mid i\mid a\mid\neg\varphi\mid\varphi\land\varphi\mid\diamondsuit_{1}\varphi\mid\diamondsuit_{2}\varphi\mid@_{i}\varphi\mid@_{a}\varphi,

where

and

.

We define the following abbreviations:

\displaystyle\varphi\lor\psi\coloneqq\neg(\neg\varphi\land\neg\psi),\ \varphi\rightarrow\psi\coloneqq\neg(\varphi\land\neg\psi),\ \square_{k}\varphi\coloneqq\neg\diamondsuit_{k}\neg\varphi\ (k=1,2).

Also, we use the following abbreviations for a finite set of formulae
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\}
:

\displaystyle\bigwedge\Gamma

\displaystyle\coloneqq\gamma_{1}\land\cdots\land\gamma_{n},

\displaystyle\bigvee\Gamma

\displaystyle\coloneqq\gamma_{1}\lor\cdots\lor\gamma_{n}.

Definition 2.5
.

Given a product model

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},(x,y)\models p\quad

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad(x,y)\in V(p),\text{where}\ p\in\mathbf{Prop},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},(x,y)\models i\quad

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad x=i^{V},\text{where}\ i\in\mathbf{Nom}_{1},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},(x,y)\models a\quad

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad y=a^{V},\text{where}\ a\in\mathbf{Nom}_{2},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},(x,y)\models\neg\varphi\quad

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad\text{not}\ \mathfrak{M},(x,y)\models\varphi,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},(x,y)\models\varphi\land\psi\quad

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad\text{there is some}\ (x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\ \text{s.t.}\ (x,y)R_{h}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\ \text{and}\ \mathfrak{M},(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\models\varphi,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},(x,y)\models\diamondsuit_{2}\varphi\quad

Intuitively,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},(x,y)\models\diamondsuit_{1}\varphi\quad

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},(x,y)\models\diamondsuit_{2}\varphi\quad

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad\text{there is some}\ y^{\prime}\in W_{2}\ \text{s.t.}\ yR_{2}y^{\prime}\ \text{and}\ \mathfrak{M},(x,y^{\prime})\models\varphi.

We can derive the following satisfaction relation for

and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},(x,y)\models\varphi\lor\psi\quad

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad\mathfrak{M},(x,y)\models\varphi\ \text{or}\ \mathfrak{M},(x,y)\models\psi,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},(x,y)\models\varphi\rightarrow\psi\quad

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad\text{if}\ \mathfrak{M},(x,y)\models\varphi,\ \text{then}\ \mathfrak{M},(x,y)\models\psi,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},(x,y)\models\square_{1}\varphi\quad

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad\text{for all}\ (x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\ ,\text{if}\ (x,y)R_{h}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}),\ \text{then}\ \mathfrak{M},(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\models\varphi

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad\text{for all}\ x^{\prime}\in W_{1},\ \text{if}\ xR_{1}x^{\prime},\ \text{then}\ \mathfrak{M},(x^{\prime},y)\models\varphi,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},(x,y)\models\square_{2}\varphi\quad

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad\text{for all}\ (x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\ ,\text{if}\ (x,y)R_{v}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}),\ \text{then}\ \mathfrak{M},(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\models\varphi

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad\text{for all}\ y^{\prime}\in W_{2},\ \text{if}\ yR_{2}y^{\prime},\ \text{then}\ \mathfrak{M},(x,y^{\prime})\models\varphi.

3
Internalized Tableau

For modal logic, the most established tableau calculus is a prefixed tableau calculus
[
19
]
. In this system, each formula occurs with a prefix representing a possible world. In hybrid logic, however, we can remove all prefixes. First, an expression of the form “
i:\varphi
” that appears in the prefix tableau can be written as
@_{i}\varphi
. Also, the notation
irj
, which shows reachability, can be written as
@_{i}\diamondsuit j
. Finally, all symbolic sequences that appear in a tableau can be formulae. Such a system is called internalized tableau calculus.

Now, we will construct a tableau calculus for HPL based on an idea proposed in
[
8
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
tableau
is a well-founded tree constructed in the following way:

•

Start with a formula of the form
@_{i}@_{a}\neg\varphi
, where
\varphi
is a formula and both
i\in\mathbf{Nom}_{1}
and
a\in\mathbf{Nom}_{2}
do not occur in
\varphi
(this is called the
root formula
).

•

For each branch, extend it by applying rules (see Definition
2
) to all nodes as often as possible. However, we can no longer add any formula in a branch if at least one of the following conditions is satisfied:

i)

Every new formula generated by applying an arbitrary rule already exists in the branch.

ii)

The branch is closed (see Definition
3.2
).

Here, a
branch
means a maximal path of the tableau. If a formula
\varphi
occurs in a branch
\Theta
, then we write
\varphi\in\Theta
.

Definition 3.2
.

A branch of a tableau
\Theta
is
closed
if
\Theta
satisfies at least one of the following three conditions:

i)

There is some
\varphi
,
i\in\mathbf{Nom}_{1}
and
a\in\mathbf{Nom}_{2}
such that
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi,@_{i}@_{a}\neg\varphi\in\Theta
.

ii)

There is some
\varphi
and
i\in\mathbf{Nom}_{1}
such that
@_{i}\varphi,@_{i}\neg\varphi\in\Theta
.

iii)

There is some
\varphi
and
a\in\mathbf{Nom}_{2}
such that
@_{a}\varphi,@_{a}\neg\varphi\in\Theta
.

We say that
\Theta
is
open
if it is not closed. A tableau is called
closed
if all branches in the tableau are closed.

Definition 3.3
.

We provide the rules of the tableau calculus for HPL in Figure

.

Remark 3.4
.

Considering that

, the rule
[\neg\diamondsuit_{1}]
is equivalent to the following rule:

@_{j}@_{a}\varphi@_{i}\diamondsuit_{1}j@_{i}@_{a}\square_{1}\varphi.

We can say the same for

\varphi\rightarrow\psi\equiv\neg(\varphi\land\neg\psi)

Definition 3.5

(provability)
.

Given a formula
\varphi

\varphi
is

if there is a closed tableau whose root formula is

, where both

and

do not occur in

Also, given two finite sets of formulae
\Gamma
and
\Delta
, we write
\Gamma\vdash\Delta
if there is a closed tableau whose root formula is
@_{i}@_{a}\neg(\bigwedge\Gamma\rightarrow\bigvee\Delta)
, equivalent to
@_{i}@_{a}(\bigwedge\Gamma\land\neg(\bigvee\Delta))
, where both

and

do not occur in any formula in
\Gamma
or
\Delta
.

4
Soundness

In this section, we show that the tableau calculus of HPL is sound for the class of product frames, i.e., if
\vdash\varphi
, then
\models\varphi
.

First, we need to make some preparations. Let us denote by
\Theta^{n}
a fragment of a branch
\Theta
that we can get by using rules
n
times from the root formula.

Next, we define the concept
faithfulness
for a branch
\Theta
and a model
\mathfrak{M}
. This is named after
[
19
, p. 31, Definition 2.9.2]
.

Definition 4.1

(faithful model)
.

Given a branch (or its fragment)
\Theta
and a model
\mathfrak{M}=(\mathfrak{F},V)
, we say that
\mathfrak{M}
is
faithful
to
\Theta
if all of the following three conditions hold:

i)

If
@_{j}@_{b}\psi\in\Theta
, then
\mathfrak{M},(j^{V},b^{V})\models\psi
.

ii)

If
@_{j}s\in\Theta\ (s=k,\neg k,\diamondsuit_{1}k)
, then for all
y\in W_{2}
,
\mathfrak{M},(j^{V},y)\models s
.

iii)

If
@_{b}t\in\Theta\ (t=c,\neg c,\diamondsuit_{2}c)
, then for all
x\in W_{1}
,
\mathfrak{M},(x,b^{V})\models t
.

First, we prove this lemma. This is a “step case” of Lemma
4.3
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\Theta=\Theta^{m}
be a fragment of a branch and
\mathfrak{M}=(\mathfrak{F},V)
a model that is faithful to
\Theta^{m}
. Let
\Theta^{m+1}
be a new branch (possibly not the only one) that we acquire by applying one of the rules to
\Theta^{m}
. Then, there exists a model
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}=(\mathfrak{F},V^{\prime})
such that
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
is faithful to
\Theta^{m+1}
.

Then, there exists a model
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}=(\mathfrak{F},V^{\prime})
such that
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
is faithful to
\Theta^{m+1}
.

Proof.

This proof is divided into different cases depending on which rule is applied to
\Theta^{m}
. We give the proof only for the cases
[\diamondsuit_{1}]
and
[@_{1}]
. The other cases are left to the reader.

[\diamondsuit_{1}]

Suppose that we acquire two new formulae
@_{j}\diamondsuit_{1}k
and
@_{k}@_{b}\psi
by applying
[\diamondsuit_{1}]
to
\Theta^{m}
. Let
\Theta^{m+1}
be a branch that contains both formulae. Since we can apply
[\diamondsuit_{1}]
to
\Theta^{m}
,
@_{j}@_{b}\diamondsuit_{1}\psi\in\Theta^{m}
holds, and by assumption
\mathfrak{M},(j^{V},b^{V})\models\diamondsuit_{1}\psi
holds. This implies that there is some
x^{\prime}\in W_{1}
such that
j^{V}R_{1}x^{\prime}
and
\mathfrak{M},(x^{\prime},b^{V})\models\psi
. Now, we define a new valuation function
V^{\prime}
:

V^{\prime}(s)=\begin{cases}\{x^{\prime}\}\times W_{2}&(s=k)\\
V(s)&(\text{otherwise}).\end{cases}

Define
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}=(\mathfrak{F},V^{\prime})
. Then, we have
j^{V^{\prime}}R_{1}k^{V^{\prime}}
and
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},(k^{V^{\prime}},b^{V^{\prime}})\models\psi
. By the latter, Definition
4.1
(
i
) holds for
@_{k}@_{b}\psi\in\Theta^{m+1}
and
(k^{V^{\prime}},b^{V^{\prime}})
. By the former,
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},(j^{V^{\prime}},y)\models\diamondsuit_{1}k
holds for an arbitrary
y
. Then, Definition
4.1
(
ii
) holds for
@_{j}\diamondsuit_{1}k\in\Theta^{m+1}
and
j^{V^{\prime}}
. Moreover, since
k
does not occur in
\Theta^{m}
, the interpretation of any formula in
\Theta^{m}
does not change even if
V
is changed into
V^{\prime}
. Therefore,
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
is faithful to
\Theta^{m+1}
.

[@_{1}]

Suppose that we acquire a formula
@_{k}@_{b}\psi
by applying
[@_{1}]
to
\Theta^{m}
. Let
\Theta^{m+1}
be a branch that contains the formula. Since we can apply
[@_{1}]
to
\Theta^{m}
,
@_{j}@_{b}@_{k}\psi\in\Theta^{m}
holds. Then, by assumption,
\mathfrak{M},(j^{V},b^{V})\models@_{k}\psi
holds. By this fact, we straightforwardly acquire
\mathfrak{M},(k^{V},b^{V})\models\psi
, which implies that
\mathfrak{M}
is faithful to
\Theta^{m+1}
.

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a tableau whose root formula is
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi
and all of whose branches are finite, and let
\mathfrak{M}=(\mathfrak{F},V)
be a product model such that a possible world
(i^{V},a^{V})\in W
satisfies
\mathfrak{M},(i^{V},a^{V})\models\varphi
. Then, there exists a pair of a branch
\Theta
in
\mathcal{T}
and a model
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}=(\mathfrak{F},V^{\prime})
such that
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
is faithful to
\Theta
.

Proof.

By assumption,
\mathfrak{M}
is faithful to the fragment of the branch
\Theta^{0}
that we can get by applying the rules
0
times to the root formula (the fragment that contains only the root formula). Using this fact and Lemma
4.2
, we can construct a model
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}=(\mathfrak{F},V^{\prime})
that is faithful to at least one branch
\Theta
. This is the desired pair of a branch and a model.
∎

Lemma
4.3
holds only for a finite tableau. To prove soundness, we have to show that every closed tableau is finite.

Lemma 4.4
.

If a tableau is closed, all the branches of it are finite.

Proof.

Take an arbitrary branch
\Theta
of a closed tableau. Then, there are formulas
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi
and
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi
in
\Theta
(
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi
may have the form
@_{i}\varphi
or
@_{a}\varphi
, but this does not affect the proof.) By the construction of tableau (Definition
3.1
), one of the formulas should be at the end of the branch. Therefore,
\Theta
is finite.
∎

Theorem 4.5

(soundness)
.

The tableau calculus of HPL is sound for the class of product frames, i.e., if
\vdash\varphi
, then
\models\varphi
.

Proof.

By
reductio ad absurdum
. Assume that both
\vdash\varphi
and
\not\models\varphi
. From the latter, there are a model
\mathfrak{M}=(\mathfrak{F},V)
and its possible world
(x,y)
such that
\mathfrak{M},(x,y)\models\neg\varphi
. Now, take arbitrary nominals
i\in\mathbf{Nom}_{1}
and
a\in\mathbf{Nom}_{2}
both of which do not occur in
\varphi
. Also, define
V^{\prime}
as follows:

V^{\prime}(s)=\begin{cases}\{x\}\times W_{2}&(s=i)\\
W_{1}\times\{y\}&(s=a)\\
V(s)&(\text{otherwise}).\end{cases}

Thus, for
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}=(\mathfrak{F},V^{\prime})
, we have
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},(i^{V^{\prime}},a^{V^{\prime}})\models\neg\varphi
. Now, the assumption that
\vdash\varphi
implies that there is a tableau
\mathcal{T}
whose root formula is
@_{i}@_{a}\neg\varphi
. By Lemma
4.4
, all branches in
\mathcal{T}
are finite. Then, by Lemma
4.3
, there is a pair of a branch
\Theta
of the tableau and a model
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime\prime}=(\mathfrak{F},V^{\prime\prime})
such that
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime\prime}
is faithful to
\Theta
.

Since
\mathcal{T}
is closed, so is
\Theta
. Then, at least one of the following statements holds:

1.

There are a pair
(j,b)
of nominals and a formula
\psi
such that
@_{j}@_{b}\psi,@_{j}@_{b}\neg\psi\in\Theta
.

2.

There are a nominal
j\in\mathbf{Nom}_{1}
and a formula
\psi
such that
@_{j}\psi,@_{j}\neg\psi\in\Theta
.

3.

There are a nominal
b\in\mathbf{Nom}_{2}
and a formula
\psi
such that
@_{b}\psi,@_{b}\neg\psi\in\Theta
.

Suppose that case
1
holds. Then, by Definition
4.1
(
i
), both
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime\prime},(j^{V^{\prime\prime}},b^{V^{\prime\prime}})\models\psi
and
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime\prime},(j^{V^{\prime\prime}},b^{V^{\prime\prime}})\models\neg\psi
hold, but that is a contradiction. We can similarly show a contradiction in cases
2
and
3
.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

Given two finite sets of formulae
\Gamma
and
\Delta
, if
\Gamma\vdash\Delta
, then
\Gamma\models\Delta
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.5
and letting
\varphi=\bigwedge\Gamma\rightarrow\bigvee\Delta
.
∎

5
Completeness

In this section, we show that the tableau calculus of HPL is complete for the class of product frames, i.e., if
\models\varphi
, then
\vdash\varphi
.

First, we define an important concept,
quasi-subformula
.

Definition 5.1

(quasi-subformula)
.

Given two formulae
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi
and
@_{j}@_{b}\psi
, we say that
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi
is
a quasi-subformula
of
@_{j}@_{b}\psi
if one of the following statements holds:

•

\varphi
is a subformula of
\psi
.

•

\varphi
has the form
\neg\chi
, and
\chi
is a subformula of
\psi
.

Lemma 5.2

(quasi-subformula property)
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a tableau. If a formula in
\mathcal{T}
has the form
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi
, then it is a quasi-subformula of the root formula of
\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

By induction on the rules of tableau calculus, except these six rules that do not generate a formula of the form
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi
:
[Red_{1}],[Red_{2}],[\neg_{1}],[\neg_{2}],[Id^{\prime}_{1}],
and
[Id^{\prime}_{2}]
. We provide the proof only for the cases
[\neg\land]
and
[\diamondsuit_{1}]
. The other cases are left to the reader.

[\neg\land]

@_{i}@_{a}\neg\varphi
and
@_{i}@_{a}\neg\psi
are both quasi-subformulae of
@_{i}@_{a}\neg(\varphi\land\psi)
. Also,
@_{i}@_{a}\neg(\varphi\land\psi)
is a quasi-subformula of the root formula by the induction hypothesis. Therefore,
@_{i}@_{a}\neg\varphi
and
@_{i}@_{a}\neg\psi
are quasi-subformulae of the root formula.

[\diamondsuit_{1}]

It suffices to show that
@_{j}@_{a}\varphi
is a quasi-subformula of the root formula.
@_{j}@_{a}\varphi
is a quasi-subformula of
@_{i}@_{a}\diamondsuit_{1}\varphi
, and by the induction hypothesis,
@_{i}@_{a}\diamondsuit_{1}\varphi
is a quasi-subformula of the root formula.

∎

Next, we define the concept of saturation. Intuitively, a branch is saturated if we cannot add any new formula to it even if we can apply some rules to it. The following is the formal definition of that.

Definition 5.3
.

A branch
\Theta
of a tableau is
saturated
if
\Theta
satisfies all the conditions below:

i)

If
@_{i}@_{a}\neg\neg\varphi\in\Theta
, then
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi\in\Theta
.

ii)

If
@_{i}@_{a}(\varphi\land\psi)\in\Theta
, then
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi,@_{i}@_{a}\psi\in\Theta
.

iii)

If
@_{i}@_{a}\neg(\varphi\land\psi)\in\Theta
, then either
@_{i}@_{a}\neg\varphi\in\Theta
or
@_{i}@_{a}\neg\psi\in\Theta
.

iv)

If
@_{i}@_{a}\diamondsuit_{1}\varphi\in\Theta
, then there is some
j\in\textbf{Nom}_{1}
such that
@_{i}\diamondsuit_{1}j\in\Theta,@_{j}@_{a}\varphi\in\Theta
.

v)

If
@_{i}@_{a}\diamondsuit_{2}\varphi\in\Theta
, then there is some
b\in\textbf{Nom}_{2}
such that
@_{a}\diamondsuit_{2}b,@_{i}@_{b}\varphi\in\Theta
.

vi)

If
@_{i}@_{a}\neg\diamondsuit_{1}\varphi\in\Theta
and
@_{i}\diamondsuit_{1}j\in\Theta
, then
@_{j}@_{a}\neg\varphi\in\Theta
.

vii)

If
@_{i}@_{a}\neg\diamondsuit_{2}\varphi\in\Theta
and
@_{a}\diamondsuit_{2}b\in\Theta
, then
@_{i}@_{b}\neg\varphi\in\Theta
.

viii)

If
@_{i}@_{a}@_{j}\varphi\in\Theta
, then
@_{j}@_{a}\varphi\in\Theta
.

ix)

If
@_{i}@_{a}@_{b}\varphi\in\Theta
, then
@_{i}@_{b}\varphi\in\Theta
.

x)

If
@_{i}@_{a}\neg@_{j}\varphi\in\Theta
, then
@_{j}@_{a}\neg\varphi\in\Theta
.

xi)

If
@_{i}@_{a}\neg@_{b}\varphi\in\Theta
, then
@_{i}@_{b}\neg\varphi\in\Theta
.

xii)

If
s=k,\neg k\ (k\in\mathbf{Nom}_{1})
and
@_{i}@_{a}s\in\Theta
, then
@_{i}s\in\Theta
.

xiii)

If
t=c,\neg c\ (c\in\mathbf{Nom}_{2})
and
@_{i}@_{a}t\in\Theta
, then
@_{a}t\in\Theta
.

xiv)

If
@_{i}\neg j\in\Theta
, then
@_{j}j\in\Theta
.

xv)

If
@_{a}\neg b\in\Theta
, then
@_{b}b\in\Theta
.

xvi)

If
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi\in\Theta
and
@_{i}j\in\Theta
, then
@_{j}@_{a}\varphi\in\Theta
.

xvii)

If
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi\in\Theta
and
@_{a}b\in\Theta
, then
@_{i}@_{b}\varphi\in\Theta
.

xviii)

If
s=k,\neg k\ (k\in\mathbf{Nom}_{1})
, and
@_{i}s,@_{i}j\in\Theta
, then
@_{j}s\in\Theta
.

xix)

If
t=c,\neg c\ (c\in\mathbf{Nom}_{2})
, and
@_{a}t,@_{a}b\in\Theta
, then
@_{b}t\in\Theta
.

We call a tableau
saturated
if all branches of it are saturated.

The next lemma claims that for an arbitrary root formula, we can make any open branch saturated.

Lemma 5.4
.

Given an HPL formula
\varphi
, we can construct a tableau whose root formula is
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi
(
i
and
a
do not occur in
\varphi
) and of which all the open branches are saturated.

Proof.

Given an HPL formula
\varphi
, we construct a tableau
\mathcal{T}_{n},
as follows:

•

\mathcal{T}_{0}
is a tableau with only the node
@_{i}@_{a}\neg\varphi
(
i
and
a
do not occur in
\varphi
).

•

\mathcal{T}_{n+1}
is constructed by applying rules to all nodes in each branch of
\mathcal{T}_{n}
whenever it is possible. In constructing it, we have to observe the following rules:

i)

If the new formulae generated by applying some rules already exist in the same branch, they are not added to the branch.

ii)

If a branch becomes closed, we can no longer add any formula.

Now, we define
\mathcal{T}^{*}
as the closure of all
\mathcal{T}_{n}\ (n\in\mathbb{N})
. Then, all the open branches of
\mathcal{T}^{*}
are saturated; to prove that, suppose otherwise. Then, there exists a branch
\Theta
such that one of the conditions of Definition
5.3
does not hold. Let
@_{j}@_{b}\varphi
(it may be of the form
@_{j}\psi
or
@_{b}\psi
) be the formula that falsifies the condition of Definition
5.3
with respect to
\Theta
. Now, we can find an integer
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
@_{j}@_{b}\varphi
is in
\mathcal{T}_{k}
. Then,
\mathcal{T}_{k+1}
is a tableau of which all the open branches containing
@_{j}@_{b}\varphi
satisfy all the conditions of Definition
5.3
with respect to it. Thus, so is
\mathcal{T}^{*}
, but that is a contradiction.
∎

Next, we define the
right nominal
. Given a branch
\Theta
of tableau,
s\in\textbf{Nom}
is a right nominal of
\Theta
if there is a nominal
t
such that
@_{t}s\in\Theta
.

Right nominals have the following property.

Lemma 5.5
.

All the right nominals occurring in branch
\Theta
occur in the root formula.

Proof.

We prove the following proposition by induction on
n
, which straightforwardly derives the desired lemma:

For any nominal
s\in\mathbf{Nom}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and a fragment of branch
\Theta^{n}
, if
@_{t}s\in\Theta^{n}
or
@_{t}\neg s\in\Theta^{n}
, then
s
occurs in
\Theta
.

Since
@_{t}s
and
@_{t}\neg s
can be derived using
[Id^{\prime}_{1}]
,
[Red_{1}]
, or
[\neg_{1}]
, then we have to check only these three cases. We assume that
s,t\in\mathbf{Nom}_{1}
(the case in which
s,t\in\mathbf{Nom}_{2}
is the same).

i)

Suppose that
@_{t}s
is derived using
[Id^{\prime}_{1}]
(we can do it in the same way for
@_{t}\neg s
). Then, there is a nominal
u\in\mathbf{Nom}_{1}
such that
@_{u}s\in\Theta^{n-1}
and
@_{u}t\in\Theta^{n-1}
.

ii)

Suppose that
@_{t}s
is derived using
[Red_{1}]
(we can do it in the same way for
@_{t}\neg s
). Then, there is a nominal
a\in\mathbf{Nom}_{2}
such that
@_{t}@_{a}s\in\Theta^{n-1}
.

iii)

If
@_{t}s
is derived using
[\neg_{1}]
, then
t=s
, and there is a nominal
u\in\mathbf{Nom}_{1}
such that
@_{u}\neg s\in\Theta^{n-1}
.

∎

Definition 5.6
.

Let
\Theta
be a branch of a tableau. For any right nominals
i,j\in\mathbf{Nom}_{1}
and
a,b\in\mathbf{Nom}_{2}
occurring in
\Theta
, we define the binary relations
\sim_{\Theta}^{1}
and
\sim_{\Theta}^{2}
as follows:

\displaystyle i\sim_{\Theta}^{1}j\quad

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad@_{i}j\in\Theta,

\displaystyle a\sim_{\Theta}^{2}b\quad

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad@_{a}b\in\Theta.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\Theta
be a saturated branch of a tableau. Then,
\sim_{\Theta}^{k}(k=1,2)
of Definition
5.6
are equivalence relations of
\mathbf{Nom}_{1}
and
\mathbf{Nom}_{2}
, respectively.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\sim_{\Theta}^{1}
is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive.

First, we show reflexivity. Let
i
be a right nominal. Then, there is a nominal
j
such that
@_{j}i\in\Theta
. Since
\Theta
is saturated, by Definition
5.3
(
xviii
) we have
@_{i}i\in\Theta
. Therefore,
i\sim_{\Theta}^{1}i
.

Second, we show symmetry. Let
i\sim_{\Theta}^{1}j
. By definition,
@_{i}j\in\Theta
holds. Also, by reflexivity of
\sim_{\Theta}^{1}
, we have
@_{i}i\in\Theta
. Since
\Theta
is saturated, by Definition
5.3
(
xviii
) we have
@_{j}i\in\Theta
. Therefore,
j\sim_{\Theta}^{1}i
.

Lastly, we show transitivity. Let
i\sim_{\Theta}^{1}j
and
j\sim_{\Theta}^{1}k
. By definition,
@_{i}j\in\Theta
and
@_{j}k\in\Theta
hold. We have
@_{j}i\in\Theta
by the symmetry of
\sim_{\Theta}^{1}
, and since
\Theta
is saturated, by Definition
5.3
(
xviii
) we have
@_{i}k\in\Theta
. Therefore, we have
i\sim_{\Theta}^{1}k
.
∎

Definition 5.8
.

Let
\Theta
be a saturated branch of a tableau, and let
i\in\mathbf{Nom}_{1}
and
a\in\mathbf{Nom}_{2}
be right nominals occurring in
\Theta
. We denote by
[i]_{\Theta}^{1}
an equivalence class of a right nominal
i
by the relation
\sim_{\Theta}^{1}
. Similarly, we denote by
[a]_{\Theta}^{2}
an equivalence class of a right nominal
a
by the relation
\sim_{\Theta}^{2}
.

Note that since
\mathbf{Nom}_{1}
and
\mathbf{Nom}_{2}
are both countably infinite, we can insert well-order in each of them. For instance, Bolander and Blackburn (2007)
[
8
]
established an order that depends on when it was introduced
1
1

1

They did not mention the case that two or more nominals are introduced at the same formula (it will happen when we put root formula on.) In this case, however, we can define an easy and natural order: the more left, the earlier.
. For a non-empty set
A
of nominals, we write
\min(A)
as the minimal element in this order. We use this notation
\min(A)
in Definition
5.9
.

Definition 5.9
.

Let
\Theta
be a saturated branch of a tableau and
s
a nominal occurring in
\Theta
(note that
s
does not have to be a right nominal). We define an
urfather
of
s
on
\Theta
(denoted by

) as follows:

u_{\Theta}(s)=\begin{cases}\min([j]_{\Theta}^{1})&\text{if}\quad s\in\mathbf{Nom}_{1}\ \text{and}\ @_{s}j\in\Theta\\
\min([b]_{\Theta}^{2})&\text{if}\quad s\in\mathbf{Nom}_{2}\ \text{and}\ @_{s}b\in\Theta\\
s&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proposition 5.10
.

u_{\Theta}(s)
of Definition
5.9
is well-defined.

Proof.

Assume that
@_{s}i,@_{s}j\in\Theta
and
s,i,j\in\mathbf{Nom}_{1}
(we can do the case
\mathbf{Nom}_{2}
in the same way). Since
\Theta
is saturated, by Definition
5.3
(
xviii
) we have
@_{i}j\in\Theta
. Thus,
[i]_{\Theta}^{1}=[j]_{\Theta}^{1}
, and we have
\min([i]_{\Theta}^{1})=\min([j]_{\Theta}^{1})
. Therefore,
u_{\Theta}(s)
is unique.
∎

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
\Theta
be a saturated branch of a tableau. Then, we have the following properties:

i)

If
s\in\mathbf{Nom}
is a right nominal occuring in
\Theta
, then
@_{u_{\Theta}(s)}s\in\Theta
.

ii)

For all
s,t\in\mathbf{Nom}
, if
@_{s}t\in\Theta
, then
u_{\Theta}(s)=u_{\Theta}(t)
.

iii)

For all quasi-subformulae
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi\in\Theta
of the root formula in
\Theta
, we have
@_{u_{\Theta}(i)}@_{u_{\Theta}(a)}\varphi\in\Theta
.

Proof.

i)

If
s
is a right nominal, then by Lemma
5.7

@_{s}s\in\Theta
holds. Thus, by definition we have
u_{\Theta}(s)=\min([s]_{\Theta}^{k})\ (k=1,2)
. From this, we have
u_{\Theta}(s)\sim_{\Theta}^{k}s
. Therefore,
@_{u_{\Theta}(s)}s\in\Theta
.

ii)

First, by
@_{s}t\in\Theta
, we have
u_{\Theta}(s)=\min([t]_{\Theta}^{k})\ (k=1,2)
. Also, since
t
is a right nominal,
u_{\Theta}(t)=\min([t]_{\Theta}^{k})
holds by doing the same as in (
i
). Therefore,
u_{\Theta}(s)=u_{\Theta}(t)
.

iii)

Suppose that
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi\in\Theta
. Then, we have the following four cases:

a)

u_{\Theta}(i)=i,u_{\Theta}(a)=a
.

b)

u_{\Theta}(i)=i,u_{\Theta}(a)=\min([b]_{\Theta}^{2})
.

c)

Lemma 5.13

(model existence theorem)
.

Let
\Theta
be an open saturated branch of a tableau. Then, we have

\text{if }@_{i}@_{a}\varphi\in\Theta,\text{ then }\mathfrak{M}^{\Theta},(u_{\Theta}(i),u_{\Theta}(a))\models\varphi.

Proof.

By induction on the complexity of
\varphi
.

[\varphi=p]

Suppose that
@_{i}@_{a}p\in\Theta
. Then, by the definition of

,

\mathfrak{M}^{\Theta},(u_{\Theta}(i),u_{\Theta}(a))\models p

[\varphi=\neg p]

Suppose that
@_{i}@_{a}\neg p\in\Theta

\mathfrak{M}^{\Theta},(u_{\Theta}(i),u_{\Theta}(a))\models\neg p

[\varphi=j]

Suppose that

. Since
\Theta
is saturated, by Definition
5.3
(
xii
) we have
@_{i}j\in\Theta

5.11
(

\mathfrak{M}^{\Theta},(u_{\Theta}(i),u_{\Theta}(a))\models j
. We can do the same in the case
\varphi=b\in\mathbf{Nom}_{2}

[\varphi=\neg j]

Suppose that
@_{i}@_{a}\neg j\in\Theta\ (j\in\mathbf{Nom}_{1})

5.11
(

@_{u_{\Theta}(i)}@_{u_{\Theta}(a)}\neg j\in\Theta

\Theta
is saturated, by Definition
5.3
(
xii
) and (

[\varphi=(\psi_{1}\land\psi_{2})]

Suppose that

(

Theorem 5.14

(completeness)
.

The tableau calculus of HPL is complete for the class of product frames, i.e., if
\models\varphi
, then
\vdash\varphi
.

Proof.

We show the contraposition.

Suppose that
\not\vdash\varphi
. If we choose two arbitrary nominals
i\in\mathbf{Nom}_{1}
and
a\in\mathbf{Nom}_{2}
such that
i
and
a
do not occur in
\varphi
, then all tableaux whose root formula is
@_{i}@_{a}\neg\varphi
are not closed. Thus, a saturated tableau
\mathcal{T}
constructed by the method of Lemma
5.4
is not closed either. This implies that we have an open and saturated branch
\Theta
from
\mathcal{T}
. Then, by Lemma
5.13
, we have
\mathfrak{M}^{\Theta},(u_{\Theta}(i),u_{\Theta}(a))\models\neg\varphi
. By the definition of
\models
, we have
\mathfrak{M}^{\Theta},(u_{\Theta}(i),u_{\Theta}(a))\not\models\varphi
, and this implies that there is a model in which
\varphi
does not hold. Therefore,
\not\models\varphi
.
∎

Corollary 5.15
.

Given two finite sets of formulae
\Gamma
and
\Delta
, if
\Gamma\models\Delta
, then
\Gamma\vdash\Delta
.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.14
and letting
\varphi
be
\bigwedge\Gamma\rightarrow\bigvee\Delta
.

6
Hybrid Dependent Product Logic (HdPL)

A property of HPL is that two diamond operators are independent of each other. What if, for example, one diamond depends on the other? In the following, we deal with that logic, called hybrid dependent product logic (HdPL).

6.1
Frames and Models

A frame in HdPL is called a
dependent product Kripke frame
. This is defined as follows.

Definition 6.1
.

Given a Kripke frame
\mathfrak{F}_{1}=(W_{1},R_{1})
and a multi-modal Kripke frame
\mathfrak{F}_{2}=(W_{2},(R_{2}(x))_{x\in W_{1}})
, a
dependent product Kripke frame (d-product frame)

\mathfrak{F}=(W,R_{h},R_{v})
is defined as follows:

•

W=W_{1}\times W_{2}
.

•

(x,y)R_{h}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
iff
xR_{1}x^{\prime}
and
y=y^{\prime}
.

•

(x,y)R_{v}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
iff
x=x^{\prime}
and
yR_{2}(x)y^{\prime}
.

The definition of a
dependent product Kripke model (d-product model)
is similar to Definition
2.4
.

As can be seen, the second accessibility relation
R_{2}
depends on the world that we are in.

The satisfaction relation
\models
of a d-product model is the same as Definition
2.5
, using the notations
R_{h}
and
R_{v}
. If we rewrite the definition without them, then the case of
\diamondsuit_{2}
is different from that of HPL, as follows:

\mathfrak{M},(x,y)\models\diamondsuit_{2}\varphi\quad\text{iff}\quad\text{there is some}\ y^{\prime}\in W_{2}\ \text{s.t.}\ yR_{2}(x)y^{\prime}\ \text{and}\ \mathfrak{M},(x,y^{\prime})\models\varphi.

In d-product models, we lose some useful properties. For example, we cannot swap the order of diamonds freely.

The formula
\diamondsuit_{1}\diamondsuit_{2}p\leftrightarrow\diamondsuit_{2}\diamondsuit_{1}p

\displaystyle W_{1}

\displaystyle=\{x_{1},x_{2}\},\ W_{2}=\{y_{1},y_{2}\},

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle R_{2}(x_{1})

\displaystyle=\emptyset,R_{2}(x_{2})=\{(y_{1},y_{2})\},

\displaystyle V(p)

Remark 6.3

6.2
Tableau Calculus for HdPL

Next, let us define the tableau calculus for HdPL. We can obtain it by modifying the tableau calculus of HPL.
First, we replace two rules,
[\diamondsuit_{2}]
and
[\neg\diamondsuit_{2}]
, with the following two rules:

@_{i}@_{b}\varphi@_{i}@_{a}\diamondsuit_{2}b@_{i}@_{a}\diamondsuit_{2}\varphi,\qquad@_{i}@_{b}\neg\varphi@_{i}@_{a}\diamondsuit_{2}b@_{i}@_{a}\neg\diamondsuit_{2}\varphi.

The rule
[\diamondsuit_{2}^{d}]
has the same restrictions as
[\diamondsuit_{2}]
. That is,
[\diamondsuit_{2}^{d}]
can be applied only once per formula, and
b
is a new nominal in
\mathbf{Nom}_{2}
.

Moreover, we need further restrictions to obtain the HdPL tableau calculus. To describe these, we add a new concept called an
accessibility formula
.

By applying
[\diamondsuit_{2}^{d}]
, we can add two new formulae
@_{i}@_{a}\diamondsuit_{2}b
and
@_{i}@_{b}\varphi
to the branch. We call the former formula, containing a new nominal as a right nominal, an
accessibility formula
. Then, we are ready to write restrictions on the rules for constructing the tableau calculus of HdPL; any rule premise must not be an accessibility formula when we apply
[\diamondsuit_{2}^{d}]
,
[Id_{1}]
, and
[Id_{2}]
.

Remark 6.4
.

Consider, for example, the following branch (here
a,b\in\mathbf{Nom}_{2}
):

@_{i}@_{c}\diamondsuit_{2}b@_{i}@_{a}\diamondsuit_{2}c@_{i}@_{a}\diamondsuit_{2}\diamondsuit_{2}b.

Then,
@_{i}@_{a}\diamondsuit_{2}c
is an accessibility formula, but
@_{i}@_{c}\diamondsuit_{2}b
is not. This is because
b
is not a new nominal applying
[\diamondsuit_{2}^{d}]
.

In summary, the tableau calculus for HdPL is obtained by modifying the tableau calculus for HPL in the following way:

i)

Replacing
[\diamondsuit_{2}]
and
[\neg\diamondsuit_{2}]
with
[\diamondsuit^{d}_{2}]
and
[\neg\diamondsuit^{d}_{2}]
, respectively.

ii)

Adding a new rule when applying
[\diamondsuit_{2}^{d}]
,
[Id_{1}]
, and
[Id_{2}]
: none of the premises is an accessibility formula.

6.3
Soundness and Completeness

Theorem 6.5

(soundness)
.

The tableau calculus for HPL is sound for the class of product frames, i.e., if
\vdash\varphi
, then
\models\varphi
.

Proof.

The only differences between this proof and that of Theorem
4.5
are the cases of
[\diamondsuit_{2}^{d}]
,
[\neg\diamondsuit_{2}^{d}]
,
[Id_{1}]
, and
[Id_{2}]
in the proof of Lemma
4.2
. We only do the case of
[\diamondsuit_{2}^{d}]
. The other cases are left to the reader.

Suppose we acquire two new formulae
@_{j}@_{b}\diamondsuit_{2}c
and
@_{j}@_{c}\psi
by applying
[\diamondsuit_{2}^{d}]
to
\Theta^{m}
. Let
\Theta^{m+1}
be a branch that contains both formulae. Since we can apply
[\diamondsuit_{2}^{d}]
to
\Theta^{m}
,
@_{j}@_{b}\diamondsuit_{2}\psi\in\Theta^{m}
holds, and by assumption
\mathfrak{M},(j^{V},b^{V})\models\diamondsuit_{2}\psi
holds. This implies that there is some
y^{\prime}\in W_{2}
such that
b^{V}R_{2}(j^{V})y^{\prime}
and
\mathfrak{M},(j^{V},y^{\prime})\models\psi
. Now, we define a new valuation function
V^{\prime}
:

V^{\prime}(s)=\begin{cases}W_{1}\times\{y^{\prime}\}&(s=c)\\
V(s)&(\text{otherwise}).\end{cases}

Define
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}=(\mathfrak{F},V^{\prime})
. Then, we have
b^{V^{\prime}}R_{2}(j^{V^{\prime}})c^{V^{\prime}}
and
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},(j^{V^{\prime}},c^{V^{\prime}})\models\psi
. By the latter, Definition
4.1
(
i
) holds for
@_{j}@_{c}\psi\in\Theta^{m+1}
and
(j^{V^{\prime}},c^{V^{\prime}})
. By the former,
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},(j^{V^{\prime}},b^{V^{\prime}})\models\diamondsuit_{2}c
. Then, Definition
4.1
(
i
) holds for
@_{j}@_{b}\diamondsuit_{2}c\in\Theta^{m+1}
. Moreover, since
c
does not occur in
\Theta^{m}
, the interpretation of any formula in
\Theta^{m}
does not change even if
V
is changed into
V^{\prime}
. Therefore,
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
is faithful to
\Theta^{m+1}
.
∎

To show completeness, we first show that the quasi-subformula property holds even in the tableau calculus for HdPL.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a tableau. If a formula in
\mathcal{T}
has the form
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi
, then one of the following statements holds:

•

@_{i}@_{a}\varphi
is a quasi-subformula of the root formula of
\mathcal{T}
.

•

@_{i}@_{a}\varphi
is an accessibility formula.

Proof.

We only do the case of
[\diamondsuit_{2}^{d}]
. The other cases are left to the reader. Note that we can prove this lemma in the same way as Lemma
5.2
, except the cases of
[\diamondsuit_{2}^{d}]
,
[\neg\diamondsuit_{2}^{d}]
,
[Id_{1}]
, and
[Id_{2}]
.

Let us consider the case of
[\diamondsuit_{2}^{d}]
. First,
@_{i}@_{a}\diamondsuit_{2}b
is an accessibility formula. Also,
@_{i}@_{b}\varphi
is a quasi-subformula of
@_{i}@_{a}\diamondsuit_{2}\varphi
, and by the induction hypothesis,
@_{i}@_{a}\diamondsuit_{2}\varphi
is a quasi-subformula of the root formula.
∎

Next, we change the definition of saturation (Definition
5.3
) of the HdPL tableau calculus as follows.

Definition 6.7
.

A branch
\Theta
of an HdPL tableau is saturated if
\Theta
satisfies all the conditions below (we show only the differences from Definition
5.3
):

v
)

If
@_{i}@_{a}\diamondsuit_{2}\varphi\in\Theta
and it is not an accessibility formula, then there is some
b\in\textbf{Nom}_{2}
such that
@_{i}@_{a}\diamondsuit_{2}b,@_{i}@_{b}\varphi\in\Theta
.

vii
)

If
@_{i}@_{a}\neg\diamondsuit_{2}\varphi,@_{i}@_{a}\diamondsuit_{2}b\in\Theta
, then
@_{i}@_{b}\neg\varphi\in\Theta
.

xvi
)

If
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi,@_{i}j\in\Theta
and
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi
is not an accessibility formula, then
@_{j}@_{a}\varphi\in\Theta
.

xvii
)

If
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi,@_{a}b\in\Theta
and
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi
is not an accessibility formula, then
@_{i}@_{b}\varphi\in\Theta
.

The discussion about right nominals does not change in proving the completeness of the tableau calculus for HdPL. Thus, we can use the same definition of an urfather of a nominal as that for HPL. Moreover, the properties of urfathers (Lemma
5.11
) are preserved in the tableau calculus for HdPL.

Then, we are ready to construct a d-product model from a branch of an HdPL tableau. Note that the only difference is how we define the accessibility relations
R_{2}
.

Definition 6.8
.

Given an open branch
\Theta
of an HdPL tableau, a d-product model
\mathfrak{M}^{\Theta}=(W^{\Theta},R_{h}^{\Theta},R_{v}^{\Theta},V^{\Theta})
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle W_{1}^{\Theta}

\displaystyle=\{u_{\Theta}(i)\in\mathbf{Nom}_{1}\mid i\ \text{occurs in}\ \Theta\},

\displaystyle W_{2}^{\Theta}

\displaystyle=\{u_{\Theta}(a)\in\mathbf{Nom}_{2}\mid a\ \text{occurs in}\ \Theta\},

\displaystyle W^{\Theta}

\displaystyle=W_{1}^{\Theta}\times W_{2}^{\Theta},

\displaystyle R_{h}^{\Theta}

\displaystyle=\{((u_{\Theta}(i),u_{\Theta}(a)),(u_{\Theta}(j),u_{\Theta}(a)))\mid@_{i}\diamondsuit_{1}j\in\Theta\},

\displaystyle R_{v}^{\Theta}(u_{\Theta}(i))

\displaystyle=\{((u_{\Theta}(i),u_{\Theta}(a)),(u_{\Theta}(i),u_{\Theta}(b)))\mid@_{i}@_{a}\diamondsuit_{2}b\in\Theta\},

\displaystyle V^{\Theta}(p)

\displaystyle=\{(u_{\Theta}(i),u_{\Theta}(a))\mid@_{i}@_{a}p\in\Theta\},\quad\text{where}\quad p\in\mathbf{Prop},

\displaystyle V^{\Theta}(i)

\displaystyle=\{u_{\Theta}(i)\}\times W_{2},\quad\text{where}\quad i\in\mathbf{Nom}_{1},

\displaystyle V^{\Theta}(a)

\displaystyle=W_{1}\times\{u_{\Theta}(a)\},\quad\text{where}\quad a\in\mathbf{Nom}_{2}.

Lemma 6.9

(model existence theorem for HdPL)
.

Let
\Theta
be an open branch of an HdPL tableau and
@_{i}@_{a}\varphi
be a quasi-subformula of a root formula. Then, we have

\text{if }@_{i}@_{a}\varphi\in\Theta,\text{ then }\mathfrak{M}^{\Theta},(u_{\Theta}(i),u_{\Theta}(a))\models\varphi.

By induction on the complexity of
\varphi
. We only do the cases of
\varphi=\diamondsuit_{2}\psi
and
\varphi=\neg\diamondsuit_{2}\psi
; the other cases are same as in the proof of Lemma
5.13
.

[\varphi=\diamondsuit_{2}\psi]

Suppose that
@_{i}@_{a}\diamondsuit_{2}\psi\in\Theta
. Since
\Theta
is saturated, by Definition
6.7
(

Restrictions on Frames

By adding a special axiom to the HdPL, it is possible to place restrictions on the accessibility relation of the Kripke frame. We focus on the property “frame is decreasing” introduced in

An example in which this property is used is

Definition 6.11

(

7
Conclusions and Future Work

In this paper, we construct tableau calculi for HPL and HdPL and show the soundness and completeness of them. However, there is still room for further work on the tableau calculus of many-dimensional hybrid (dependent) product logic.

7.2
What if We Have More Dimensions?

The tableau calculus we have can be easily extended to
n

n\geq 3
). Soundness and completeness can be proved with a method similar to that of Sections
4
and
5

However, we cannot construct any tableau calculus with termination, since it is known that every product modal logic with more than 3 dimensions is undecidable (see
[
14
,
11
]
).

7.3
Adding More Operators

The system we have considered so far is a system containing only the most basic operator, @. The other operators in hybrid logic are the existential operator
E
and the downarrow operator
\downarrow

\varphi

\varphi

[

for more details.

Let

and

be existential operators and
\downarrow_{1}x
and
\downarrow_{2}y
be downarrow operators for the first and the second dimension, respectively. The rules for these operators will be as follows (we show only the rules for
E_{1}
and

7.4
Adding More Rules

Adding axioms to the hybrid tableau calculus makes them richer. With the proper addition of rules, we can construct a tableau calculus for
S4
.

In a previous work
[
9
]
, some axioms corresponding to reflexivity, irreflexivity, and transitivity were added to the tableau calculus for hybrid logic with preserving termination. Applying this method, we also acquire a many-dimensional tableau calculus corresponding to
S4
n
. In terms of automatic calculation, however, it will be better to add axioms in the form of an inference rule.

Moreover, for the HdPL tableau calculus, some rules corresponding to dependencies can be added as in Section
6.4
. These should also be added in the form of inference rules.
